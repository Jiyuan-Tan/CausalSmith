\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:positive-bounded-directed-lags]\label{ass:positive-bounded-directed-lags}
Every \((u,s)\to(v,t)\in E_G^{\to}\) satisfies \(1\le t-s\le L\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This paper deliberately restricts directed effects to delayed channels observable at the chosen sampling resolution, as in systems where actuation, transmission, or biological response cannot occur within one sampling interval. Contemporaneous latent dependence remains available through bidirected edges, so the restriction does not encode the target effect or remove hidden confounding.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:exact-summary-projection]\label{ass:exact-summary-projection}
The refinement satisfies \(\operatorname{proj}_H(G)=\mathcal S\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (full-time compatibility with a summary causal graph, \cite{FerreiraAssaad2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:structural-recursion]\label{ass:structural-recursion}
For every \(v\in\mathcal W_H\), the observed variable satisfies \(V_v=f_v(V_{\operatorname{pa}_G(v)},(U_a:a\ni v))\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (finite nonparametric structural-equation representation, \cite{DuarteEtAl2024}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:focal-positive-lag-parent]\label{ass:focal-positive-lag-parent}
The focal treatment satisfies \(X_{-\gamma}\in\operatorname{PP}_{\mathcal S,L}(Y_0)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (possible-parent controlled micro-direct-effect query, \cite{FerreiraAssaad2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:independent-edge-latents]\label{ass:independent-edge-latents}
The finite family \(\{U_a:a=\{v\},\ v\in\mathcal W_H,\text{ or }a\in E_G^{\leftrightarrow}\}\) is mutually independent.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (semi-Markovian independent pairwise latent-parent factorization, \cite{Evans2016}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:observed-law-compatibility]\label{ass:observed-law-compatibility}
The observational distribution induced by \(\mathcal M\) on \(\{0,1\}^{\mathcal W_H}\) equals \(p\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (SCM compatibility with an observed discrete law, \cite{DuarteEtAl2024}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:strict-observed-positivity]\label{ass:strict-observed-positivity}
Every observed-window cell satisfies \(p(w)>0\) for \(w\in\{0,1\}^{\mathcal W_H}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (positive observed law for summary-graph micro effects, \cite{FerreiraAssaad2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:iid-window-sampling]\label{ass:iid-window-sampling}
The \(n\) observed vectors are independent draws from the multinomial law \(p\) on \(\{0,1\}^{\mathcal W_H}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (multinomial sampling model, \cite{MalloyTripathyNowak2020}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{lemma}[lem:finite-refinements]\label{lem:finite-refinements}
Let \(m_{\to}=|E_{\mathcal S}^{\to}|\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}(H+1-\ell)\) and \(m_{\leftrightarrow}=|\mathcal B_H(\mathcal S)|\). Then \(\mathfrak G_{H,L}(\mathcal S)\) is finite and \(|\mathfrak G_{H,L}(\mathcal S)|\le 2^{m_{\to}+m_{\leftrightarrow}}\). A lexicographic edge-subset enumeration terminates and lists each exact-summary refinement once. The directed count depends on \(L\), whereas the bidirected count permits every summary-compatible pair of time points in the \(H\)-window.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For each \(u\to v\in E_{\mathcal S}^{\to}\), lag \(\ell\in\{1,\ldots,L\}\), and source time \(s\in\{-H,\ldots,-\ell\}\), there is exactly one candidate directed edge
\[
(u,s)\longrightarrow(v,s+\ell).
\]
There are \(H+1-\ell\) choices of \(s\), hence the set \(D_{H,L}(\mathcal S)\) of all candidate directed edges has cardinality
\[
|D_{H,L}(\mathcal S)|
=|E_{\mathcal S}^{\to}|\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}(H+1-\ell)
=m_{\to}.
\tag{4}
\]
The restriction \(L\le H\) makes every summand nonnegative.  By the definition of \(\mathcal B_H(\mathcal S)\), the bidirected candidate set has \(m_{\leftrightarrow}=|\mathcal B_H(\mathcal S)|\) elements.  Every member \(G\) of \(\mathfrak G_{H,L}(\mathcal S)\) is determined by the ordered pair
\[
(E_G^{\to},E_G^{\leftrightarrow})
\in 2^{D_{H,L}(\mathcal S)}\times2^{\mathcal B_H(\mathcal S)}.
\]
This assignment is injective because the vertex set \(\mathcal W_H\) is fixed.  Therefore
\[
|\mathfrak G_{H,L}(\mathcal S)|
\le 2^{|D_{H,L}(\mathcal S)|+|\mathcal B_H(\mathcal S)|}
=2^{m_{\to}+m_{\leftrightarrow}}.
\]
Every candidate directed edge strictly increases time by \(\ell\ge1\), so its directed part is acyclic.  Enumerate the two finite power sets in lexicographic order and retain precisely those pairs satisfying the exact-projection requirement in \(\text{ass:exact-summary-projection}\).  Every retained pair is visited once, and every exact-summary refinement occurs as one such pair.  Thus the enumeration terminates, has no duplicates, and treats the directed and bidirected counts as claimed.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The union over unknown lags must be a finite mathematical object before exact partial identification is meaningful. \textit{Gap.} JahnKarnikSchulman2025 reduces identification for one known periodic ADMG to a finite segment; it does not enumerate all summary-graph refinements inside a fixed segment. \textit{Consumer.} The raw endpoint compiler receives an explicit finite outer loop and a causal-parameter bound on its number of branches.

\begin{theorem}[thm:sharp-refinement-envelope]\label{thm:sharp-refinement-envelope}
For every nonempty fiber \(\mathfrak F(\mathcal S,L,H,p)\),
\[
[\underline q(p),\overline q(p)]
=\left[
\min_{\substack{G\in\mathfrak G_{H,L}(\mathcal S)\\ \{\lambda\in\Lambda_G:P_G(\lambda)=p\}\ne\varnothing}}\ \min_{\lambda\in\Lambda_G:P_G(\lambda)=p}q_G(\lambda),
\ \max_{\substack{G\in\mathfrak G_{H,L}(\mathcal S)\\ \{\lambda\in\Lambda_G:P_G(\lambda)=p\}\ne\varnothing}}\ \max_{\lambda\in\Lambda_G:P_G(\lambda)=p}q_G(\lambda)
\right].
\]
Both extrema are attained. Every feasible response-weight point reconstructs a semi-Markovian SCM with the displayed independent overlapping pairwise latent parents, and every compatible SCM has a raw canonical response-weight representative with the same \((p,q)\) image.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Assume that \(\mathfrak F(\mathcal S,L,H,p)\ne\varnothing\).  For each \(G\in\mathfrak G_{H,L}(\mathcal S)\), define the proof-local feasible set
\[
K_G(p)=\{\lambda\in\Lambda_G:P_G(\lambda)=p\}
\]
and the set of observationally feasible refinements
\[
\mathfrak G_p=\{G\in\mathfrak G_{H,L}(\mathcal S):K_G(p)\ne\varnothing\}.
\tag{1}
\]
These are abbreviations inside the proof, not additions to the causal target.

Fix \(G\in\mathfrak G_{H,L}(\mathcal S)\).  Apply \(\text{lem:finite-factor-support-reduction}\) with a singleton query index set whose sole query is the micro CDE in \(\text{def:micro-cde}\).  If \(\mathcal M\in\mathfrak M(G,p)\), the forward part of that lemma supplies a raw canonical branch and independent factor weights \(\lambda\in\Lambda_G\) for which
\[
P_G(\lambda)=p,
\qquad
q_G(\lambda)=q(\mathcal M).
\tag{2}
\]
The first equality in (2) uses \(\text{ass:observed-law-compatibility}\), and the second uses preservation of the specified interventional query by the finite-support reduction.  Thus \(\lambda\in K_G(p)\).

Conversely, let \(\lambda\in K_G(p)\).  By the converse part of \(\text{lem:finite-factor-support-reduction}\), the branch's deterministic tables and factor weights reconstruct a finite binary semi-Markovian SCM \(\mathcal M_\lambda\) on \(G\).  It satisfies \(\text{ass:structural-recursion}\), and drawing the private and pairwise edge parents from their separate simplex factors gives exactly \(\text{ass:independent-edge-latents}\).  The defining equality \(P_G(\lambda)=p\) supplies \(\text{ass:observed-law-compatibility}\).  Hence \(\mathcal M_\lambda\in\mathfrak M(G,p)\).  The observational and interventional recursions used by \(\text{def:raw-polynomial-compiler}\) are the recursions of this reconstructed SCM, so
\[
q(\mathcal M_\lambda)=q_G(\lambda).
\tag{3}
\]
Equations (2)--(3) prove, including when both sides are empty,
\[
\{q(\mathcal M):\mathcal M\in\mathfrak M(G,p)\}
=\{q_G(\lambda):\lambda\in K_G(p)\}.
\tag{4}
\]

Taking the union of (4) over all refinements and invoking \(\text{def:summary-fiber}\) and \(\text{def:sharp-envelope}\) yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Theta(\mathcal S,L,H,p)
&=\bigcup_{G\in\mathfrak G_{H,L}(\mathcal S)}
  \{q_G(\lambda):\lambda\in K_G(p)\}\\
&=\bigcup_{G\in\mathfrak G_p}
  \{q_G(\lambda):\lambda\in K_G(p)\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
The second equality deletes only empty sets.  Because the original SCM fiber is nonempty, its image under \(q\) is nonempty; (5) therefore shows that \(\mathfrak G_p\ne\varnothing\).

It remains to prove that all extrema written in the corrected display exist.  For a fixed branch \(r\in\mathcal R_G\), put
\[
\Lambda_{G,r}=\prod_a\Delta(\mathcal K_{G,r,a}).
\]
There are finitely many factors \(a\), and every \(\mathcal K_{G,r,a}\) is finite.  Each probability simplex is a closed and bounded subset of a finite-dimensional Euclidean space, hence compact; therefore the finite product \(\Lambda_{G,r}\) is compact. \tag{7}

\begin{lemma}[lem:refinement-embeds-complete-order]\label{lem:refinement-embeds-complete-order}

\begin{theorem}[thm:simultaneous-whole-set-inference]\label{thm:simultaneous-whole-set-inference}
Under \(\text{ass:iid-window-sampling}\), \(\inf_p\Pr_p\{p\in\mathcal C_{n,\alpha}(\widehat p)\}\ge1-\alpha\). On this single event, for every \(j\in\mathcal J\), the population envelope of \(q_j\) is contained in \(I_j^{\alpha}\), and for every distinct \(j,k\in\mathcal J\), the full same-SCM identified set of \(q_j-q_k\) is contained in \(I_{jk}^{\Delta,\alpha}\), simultaneously. Exact optimization over the closed confidence fiber remains valid at zero empirical cells. Declaring \(j\) and \(k\) tied exactly when \(0\in I_{jk}^{\Delta,\alpha}\) never asserts a strict ordering when zero is compatible with the covered observed law.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(K=2^N\) be the number of multinomial cells.  Fix a cell \(w\in\{0,1\}^{\mathcal W_H}\), and for sample \(i\) let \(R_i\) be the indicator that the observed window equals \(w\).  By \(\text{ass:iid-window-sampling}\), the variables \(R_1,\ldots,R_n\) are independent Bernoulli variables with \(\mathbb E_p R_i=p(w)\), and
\[
\widehat p(w)-p(w)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\bigl(R_i-p(w)\bigr).
\]
Set
\[
g(t)=\log\mathbb E_p\exp\{t(R_i-p(w))\}.
\]
We have \(g(0)=g'(0)=0\).  Under exponential tilting by \(t\), a Bernoulli variable remains Bernoulli, with some success probability \(r_t\in[0,1]\); hence
\[
g''(t)=r_t(1-r_t)\le\frac14.
\]
Taylor's formula with integral remainder therefore gives, for every real \(t\),
\[
g(t)=\int_0^t(t-s)g''(s)\,ds\le\frac{t^2}{8},
\]
where for \(t<0\) the same inequality follows by applying the displayed calculation to \(-t\) and \(-R_i\).  For \(t>0\), Markov's inequality and independence now imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr_p\{\widehat p(w)-p(w)\ge\varepsilon\}
&=\Pr_p\!\left\{\exp\!\left(t\sum_{i=1}^n(R_i-p(w))\right)
       \ge e^{tn\varepsilon}\right\}\\
&\le e^{-tn\varepsilon}\prod_{i=1}^n
       \mathbb E_p e^{t(R_i-p(w))}\\
&\le\exp\{-tn\varepsilon+nt^2/8\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Choosing \(t=4\varepsilon\) gives the upper-tail bound \(e^{-2n\varepsilon^2}\).  Applying the same argument to \(-R_i\) gives the lower-tail bound, and consequently
\[
\Pr_p\{|\widehat p(w)-p(w)|>\varepsilon\}
\le 2e^{-2n\varepsilon^2}.
\tag{1}
\]

Suppose first that the dyadic set in the definition of \(\varepsilon_{n,\alpha}\) is nonempty.  By a union bound over the \(K=2^N\) cells and by (1),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr_p\{p\notin\mathcal C_{n,\alpha}(\widehat p)\}
&\le\sum_{w\in\{0,1\}^{\mathcal W_H}}
  \Pr_p\{|\widehat p(w)-p(w)|>\varepsilon_{n,\alpha}\}\\
&\le 2^{N+1}e^{-2n\varepsilon_{n,\alpha}^2}\\
&\le\alpha.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
If that dyadic set is empty, then \(\varepsilon_{n,\alpha}=1\).  Because both \(p(w)\) and \(\widehat p(w)\) lie in \([0,1]\), their absolute difference is at most one for every \(w\), so \(p\in\mathcal C_{n,\alpha}(\widehat p)\) surely.  Thus (2) holds in both cases.  Its right-hand side is independent of \(p\), and therefore
\[
\inf_p\Pr_p\{p\in\mathcal C_{n,\alpha}(\widehat p)\}\ge 1-\alpha.
\tag{3}
\]

Let \(\mathcal E=\{p\in\mathcal C_{n,\alpha}(\widehat p)\}\).  On \(\mathcal E\), fix \(j\in\mathcal J\) and \((G,\mathcal M)\in\mathfrak F(\mathcal S,L,H,p)\).  In the set \(Q_j^\alpha\) from \(\text{def:confidence-endpoints}\), choose \(p'=p\) and this same pair \((G,\mathcal M)\).  This is feasible by the definition of \(\mathcal E\), and hence
\[
q_j(\mathcal M)\in Q_j^\alpha\subseteq
[\min Q_j^\alpha,\max Q_j^\alpha]=I_j^\alpha.
\tag{4}
\]
In particular, the presence of this model makes \(Q_j^\alpha\) nonempty, so the nonempty branch of the interval definition is the applicable one.  Taking (4) over all \((G,\mathcal M)\in\mathfrak F(\mathcal S,L,H,p)\) proves containment of the entire population envelope for query \(j\).  Since neither \(\mathcal E\) nor (3) depends on \(j\), the containment holds simultaneously for every \(j\in\mathcal J\), without a further union bound.

Now fix distinct \(j,k\in\mathcal J\).  Use \(p'=p\) and the same SCM \(\mathcal M\) for both coordinates in \(\text{def:contrast-confidence-interval}\).  Then
\[
q_j(\mathcal M)-q_k(\mathcal M)
\in Q_{jk}^{\Delta,\alpha}
\subseteq
[\min Q_{jk}^{\Delta,\alpha},\max Q_{jk}^{\Delta,\alpha}]
=I_{jk}^{\Delta,\alpha}.
\tag{5}
\]
Taking (5) over the full same-SCM identified set and over all distinct pairs proves the asserted simultaneous contrast containment on \(\mathcal E\).

It remains to justify the extrema and the zero-cell claim.  The empirical vector \(\widehat p\) has rational coordinates and \(\varepsilon_{n,\alpha}\) is rational.  Hence \(\mathcal C_{n,\alpha}(\widehat p)\) is the intersection of the probability simplex with finitely many rational closed half-spaces, and is compact even if \(\widehat p(w)=0\) for some cells.  Apply \(\text{lem:finite-factor-support-reduction}\) once to the full finite set \(\mathcal J\).  For each refinement \(G\) and raw branch \(r\), the portion of the confidence fiber represented on that branch is exactly
\[
\mathcal F_{G,r}
=\left\{\lambda\in\prod_a\Delta(\mathcal K_{G,r,a}):
-\varepsilon_{n,\alpha}
\le P_G(\lambda)(w)-\widehat p(w)
\le\varepsilon_{n,\alpha}
\text{ for every }w\right\}.
\tag{6}
\]
Every set in (6) is closed in a finite product of compact simplices and therefore compact.  The objectives \(q_j\) and \(q_j-q_k\) are multi-affine, hence continuous, on each branch.  The terminating finite refinement and raw-branch enumeration supplied by \(\text{thm:exact-algebraic-certification}\), together with both directions of \(\text{lem:finite-factor-support-reduction}\), shows that the confidence-fiber attainable sets are exactly finite unions of the corresponding continuous images of the sets (6).  They are therefore compact; when nonempty, their minima and maxima in the two interval definitions are attained.

Moreover, after splitting each two-sided inequality in (6), every branch is a compact rational polynomial program.  The branchwise real-algebraic elimination procedure used in \(\text{thm:exact-algebraic-certification}\) therefore decides emptiness and computes its exact attained extrema.  No step divides by \(\widehat p(w)\), so the same exact optimization applies when empirical cells vanish.

Finally, suppose zero is compatible with a covered observed law and a same-SCM contrast.  By definition, there then exist \(p'\in\mathcal C_{n,\alpha}(\widehat p)\) and \((G,\mathcal M)\in\mathfrak F(\mathcal S,L,H,p')\) such that \(q_j(\mathcal M)-q_k(\mathcal M)=0\).  Thus \(0\in Q_{jk}^{\Delta,\alpha}\), and (5) gives \(0\in I_{jk}^{\Delta,\alpha}\); the prescribed rule declares a tie and cannot assert a strict ordering.  The converse is not required: the interval hull may contain zero while a disconnected attainable set does not, so the rule may be conservative but remains valid.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} One multinomial event provides finite-sample protection for the whole vector of nonsmooth refinement-union endpoints and decisions based on their contrasts. \textit{Gap.} MalloyTripathyNowak2020 constructs optimal multinomial regions and DuarteEtAl2024 propagates uncertainty through one fixed-model program; EasyRCA2023 compares point direct effects without joint partial-identification or a zero-feasibility tie rule. \textit{Consumer.} EasyRCA-style root-cause ranking can retain statistically indistinguishable candidates instead of forcing an unsupported ordering under lag and graph uncertainty.

\begin{lemma}[lem:factorized-pushforward-fidelity]\label{lem:factorized-pushforward-fidelity}
For every \(G\) and finite \(\mathcal J\), \(\mathcal A_{G,\mathcal J}^{\mathrm{raw}}=\mathcal A_{G,\mathcal J}^{\mathrm{fac-q}}\subseteq\mathcal A_{G,\mathcal J}^{\mathrm{simp-q}}\). More precisely, for every \(\lambda\in\Lambda_G\), push-forward gives \(\mathbb E_{\mu_{\lambda}}[T_{G,\mathcal J}]=\mathbb E_{(\pi_{G,\mathcal J})_{\#}\mu_{\lambda}}[\overline T_{G,\mathcal J}]\), and every member of \(\overline{\mathcal P}_{G,\mathcal J}^{\mathrm{fac}}\) arises this way. Thus only replacement of the induced factorized quotient family by the unrestricted branchwise quotient simplex can enlarge the projected image.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(\lambda\in\Lambda_G\) and put \(\mu=\mu_\lambda\).  For every coordinate \(c\) of the vector \(T_{G,\mathcal J}\), the defining identity \(\overline T_{G,\mathcal J}\circ\pi_{G,\mathcal J}=T_{G,\mathcal J}\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{(\pi_{G,\mathcal J})_\#\mu}[\overline T_{G,\mathcal J,c}]
&=\sum_{C\in\overline\Omega_{G,\mathcal J}}\left(\sum_{\omega\in C}\mu(\omega)\right)\overline T_{G,\mathcal J,c}(C)\\
&=\sum_C\sum_{\omega\in C}\mu(\omega)T_{G,\mathcal J,c}(\omega)\\
&=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_{G,\mathcal J}}\mu(\omega)T_{G,\mathcal J,c}(\omega)\\
&=\mathbb E_\mu[T_{G,\mathcal J,c}].
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]
The quotient cells partition the finite raw domain, so every rearrangement in (26) is exact.  By the definition of \(\overline{\mathcal P}_{G,\mathcal J}^{\mathrm{fac}}\), every one of its members is \((\pi_{G,\mathcal J})_\#\mu_\lambda\) for some \(\lambda\), and every such push-forward is a member.  Taking vector expectations in (26) proves
\[
\mathcal A_{G,\mathcal J}^{\mathrm{raw}}
=\mathcal A_{G,\mathcal J}^{\mathrm{fac-q}}.
\]
Because \(\mu_\lambda\) is supported on the single branch selected by \(\lambda\), its push-forward is a probability mass on that branch's quotient cells.  It is therefore feasible for the unrestricted branchwise simplex in \(\mathcal A_{G,\mathcal J}^{\mathrm{simp-q}}\), which proves the inclusion.  No reverse inclusion was used: it is exactly the independent-factor restriction on quotient masses that the larger simplex discards.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This isolates the only dangerous step: push-forward itself is exact, whereas freeing quotient-cell masses can erase the nonlinear latent-factor constraints. \textit{Gap.} The unrestricted-simplex lifts in RossettoAntonucci2026 and ChoeEtAl2026 establish linear-program fidelity but do not establish membership in the induced independent-edge-latent quotient family. \textit{Consumer.} The quotient search can test the unrestricted reduced program against a precisely defined sound factorized benchmark.

\begin{theorem}[thm:factorization-safe-quotient-obstruction]\label{thm:factorization-safe-quotient-obstruction}
For \(G_{\mathrm{ABC}}\) and \(\mathcal J_{\mathrm{ABC}}\), the procedure \(\mathsf{QCan}\) returns alternative \({\rm(ii)}\) even at a strictly positive observed law.  More precisely, for every rational \(\theta\) with \(0<\theta<1\), there is a rational branchwise quotient mass \(\nu_\theta\) whose image \(x_\theta\) belongs to \(\mathcal A_{G_{\mathrm{ABC}},\mathcal J_{\mathrm{ABC}}}^{\mathrm{simp-q}}\) but not to \(\mathcal A_{G_{\mathrm{ABC}},\mathcal J_{\mathrm{ABC}}}^{\mathrm{raw}}\), whose query coordinate is zero, and whose observed coordinate is the full-support law
\[
p_\theta(w)=2^{-N}\bigl[1+\theta(-1)^{w_{A_0}\oplus w_{C_0}}\bigr],
\qquad w\in\{0,1\}^{\mathcal W_H}.
\]
Writing \(P_{ij}=\sum_{w:w_{A_0}=i,\,w_{C_0}=j}P(w)\), the observational polynomial
\[
D(P)=P_{00}P_{11}-P_{01}P_{10}
\]
vanishes throughout the raw image but satisfies \(D(p_\theta)=\theta/4\).  Thus the unrestricted quotient simplex is not factorization-safe even in the interior of the observed probability simplex: freeing quotient-cell masses erases the independent edge-factor incidence constraints, so the endpoint compiler must retain the raw product parameterization on this graph.

For general \(G\) and finite \(\mathcal J\), equality of the raw and unrestricted quotient images is decidable by the following necessary-and-sufficient factor-incidence test: for every branch \(r\) and every mass \(\nu\) on its quotient cells, there must exist a branch \(r'\) and factor weights \(\lambda\in\prod_a\Delta(\mathcal K_{G,r',a})\) such that
\[
\sum_C\nu(C)\overline T_{G,\mathcal J}(C)
=\sum_\kappa\left(\prod_a\lambda_a(\kappa_a)\right)T_{G,\mathcal J}(r',\kappa).
\]
This finite first-order real-algebraic condition is exact; when it holds, lexicographic minimization in its compact fibers gives a semialgebraic selector.  Moreover, the canonical catalogue may be chosen so that, with \(C=2^N+|\mathcal J|\),
\[
|\overline\Omega_{G,\mathcal J}|
\le
2^{N2^{N-1}C^{w_G}}
\min\left\{C^{N(w_G+1)/2},\,2^N3^{|\mathcal J|}\right\}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a rational number \(\theta\) with \(0<\theta<1\).  We first construct the quotient-simplex point.  In \(G_{\mathrm{ABC}}\), take the canonical branch \(r_\star\) in which every private exogenous variable is binary, every pairwise edge exogenous variable is constant, and, for each \(v\in\mathcal W_H\),
\[
f_v\bigl(x_{\operatorname{pa}_{G_{\mathrm{ABC}}}(v)},(u_a:a\ni v)\bigr)=u_{\{v\}}.
\tag{27}
\]
Thus every structural table ignores its observed-parent and pairwise-edge inputs.  This is one of the finite deterministic table systems enumerated by \(\text{def:raw-polynomial-compiler}\); equivalently, its exogenous supports have size at most \(C=2^N+|\mathcal J_{\mathrm{ABC}}|\), and the catalogue in \(\text{lem:finite-factor-support-reduction}\) contains its padded deterministic-table branch.

For each \(w\in\{0,1\}^{\mathcal W_H}\), let \(\omega_w\) be the raw signature in branch \(r_\star\) having \(u_{\{v\}}=w_v\) for every \(v\).  Equation (27) makes its observational recursion equal to \(w\).  It also makes the equation for \(C_0\) ignore the directed parent \(A_{-1}\).  Consequently the singleton query in \(\mathcal J_{\mathrm{ABC}}\) has deterministic contrast zero and
\[
T_{G_{\mathrm{ABC}},\mathcal J_{\mathrm{ABC}}}(\omega_w)=(e_w,0).
\tag{28}
\]
Let \(C_w=\pi_{G_{\mathrm{ABC}},\mathcal J_{\mathrm{ABC}}}(\omega_w)\).  If \(w\ne w'\), then \(e_w\ne e_{w'}\), so (28) and the definition of \(\sim_{G,\mathcal J}\) give \(C_w\ne C_{w'}\).  Define a mass on the quotient cells of \(r_\star\) by
\[
\nu_\theta(C_w)=p_\theta(w)
=2^{-N}\bigl[1+\theta(-1)^{w_{A_0}\oplus w_{C_0}}\bigr].
\tag{29}
\]
There are two assignments of \((w_{A_0},w_{C_0})\) with parity zero and two with parity one.  For each fixed assignment of the remaining \(N-2\) coordinates, their four contributions to the bracket in (29) sum to \(4\).  Hence
\[
\sum_w p_\theta(w)=1.
\]
Moreover, \(1-\theta>0\), so every \(p_\theta(w)\) is positive; and rationality of \(\theta\) makes every mass rational.  Thus \(\nu_\theta\) is a rational branchwise quotient probability mass and its observed law has full support.  By (28)--(29),
\[
\mathbb E_{\nu_\theta}[\overline T_{G_{\mathrm{ABC}},\mathcal J_{\mathrm{ABC}}}]
=(p_\theta,0)=x_\theta,
\]
so \(x_\theta\in\mathcal A_{G_{\mathrm{ABC}},\mathcal J_{\mathrm{ABC}}}^{\mathrm{simp-q}}\) by \(\text{def:factorized-quotient-images}\).

We next prove nonmembership in the raw image.  Consider an arbitrary raw canonical parameter point on \(G_{\mathrm{ABC}}\).  By \(\text{def:abc-kill-test-instance}\), \(A_0\) has no observed parent and its only incident pairwise latent is \(U_{A_0B_0}\); hence \(A_0\) is a deterministic function of
\[
\mathcal U_A=(U_{\{A_0\}},U_{A_0B_0}).
\]
The only observed parent of \(C_0\) is \(A_{-1}\), the latter has no observed parent and no incident pairwise latent, and the only pairwise latent incident to \(C_0\) is \(U_{B_0C_0}\).  Therefore \(C_0\) is a deterministic function of
\[
\mathcal U_C=(U_{\{A_{-1}\}},U_{\{C_0\}},U_{B_0C_0}).
\]
The two displayed latent collections are disjoint.  Mutual independence in \(\text{ass:independent-edge-latents}\) therefore makes \(\mathcal U_A\) independent of \(\mathcal U_C\), and summing their product mass over inverse images of the two structural functions gives
\[
\Pr(A_0=i,C_0=j)=\Pr(A_0=i)\Pr(C_0=j),
\qquad i,j\in\{0,1\}.
\tag{30}
\]
Thus every raw observational image obeys
\[
D(P)=P_{00}P_{11}-P_{01}P_{10}=0,
\tag{31}
\]
because substitution of (30) makes both products equal to
\(\Pr(A_0=0)\Pr(A_0=1)\Pr(C_0=0)\Pr(C_0=1)\).

For the law in (29), summing over the \(2^{N-2}\) assignments of the other coordinates gives
\[
(p_\theta)_{ij}
=\frac14\bigl[1+\theta(-1)^{i\oplus j}\bigr].
\tag{32}
\]
Consequently
\[
\begin{aligned}
D(p_\theta)
&=\left(\frac{1+\theta}{4}\right)^2
-\left(\frac{1-\theta}{4}\right)^2\\
&=\frac{\theta}{4}>0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{33}
\]
Equations (31)--(33) are an exact rational polynomial nonmembership certificate, so \(x_\theta\notin\mathcal A_{G_{\mathrm{ABC}},\mathcal J_{\mathrm{ABC}}}^{\mathrm{raw}}\).  In view of \(\text{lem:factorized-pushforward-fidelity}\), push-forward itself preserves the raw image exactly; the enlargement in (29) occurs only because the unrestricted quotient simplex discards the product-factorization restriction.  This proves the full-support obstruction and alternative \({\rm(ii)}\).

For the general decision statement, fix finite orderings of branches, quotient cells, factors, states, and output coordinates.  For a branch \(r\), let
\[
A_r\nu=\sum_C\nu(C)\overline T_{G,\mathcal J}(C),
\]
and for a branch \(r'\), let
\[
F_{r'}(\lambda)
=\sum_\kappa\left(\prod_a\lambda_a(\kappa_a)\right)
T_{G,\mathcal J}(r',\kappa).
\]
The first map is linear and the second is polynomial.  Simplex membership is given by coordinatewise weak inequalities and one linear equality.  Hence the displayed factor-incidence criterion is the finite first-order real sentence
\[
\bigwedge_r\ \forall\nu\in\Delta(\overline\Omega_r)\ 
\bigvee_{r'}\ \exists\lambda\in\prod_a\Delta(\mathcal K_{G,r',a}):
A_r\nu=F_{r'}(\lambda).
\tag{34}
\]
Here \(\overline\Omega_r\) denotes the quotient cells contained in branch \(r\).  By \(\text{def:factorized-quotient-images}\), the unrestricted quotient image is
\[
\bigcup_r A_r\Delta(\overline\Omega_r),
\]
whereas the raw image is
\[
\bigcup_{r'}F_{r'}\left(\prod_a\Delta(\mathcal K_{G,r',a})\right).
\]
The raw-to-quotient inclusion is \(\text{lem:factorized-pushforward-fidelity}\).  Therefore (34) is equivalent to the reverse inclusion and is necessary and sufficient for equality of the two images.  All coefficients in (34) are rational, indeed integral apart from the simplex normalizations.  Formula (34) has a universal quotient-mass block, a finite disjunction over raw branches, and an existential factor-weight block; it is therefore not a one-block feasibility instance.  Apply \(\text{lem:multiblock-real-algebraic-qe}\) directly to this finite alternating-block first-order sentence.  It decides the equality test exactly and returns a quantifier-free description whenever the displayed variables are retained as parameters.

Suppose (34) holds.  For fixed \((r,\nu)\), the feasible raw weights in each candidate branch \(r'\) form the inverse image of the singleton \(\{A_r\nu\}\) under the continuous polynomial map \(F_{r'}\), intersected with a product of finite simplices.  This set is closed and compact.  At least one such set is nonempty by (34).  Choose the first branch with a nonempty fiber and, within that fiber, successively minimize the coordinates of \(\lambda\) in the fixed order.  Compactness gives a minimizer at every step, and the final vector is unique.  The assertions that a branch is first feasible and that a feasible coordinate is no larger than every coordinate in the remaining fiber are finite first-order real formulas with free variables \(r,\nu,\lambda\).  Applying \(\text{lem:multiblock-real-algebraic-qe}\) to that graph formula yields an equivalent quantifier-free description.  Thus the lexicographic choice has a semialgebraic graph, is single-valued, and reconstructs factor weights for every quotient-simplex point whenever (34) holds.

It remains to prove the catalogue-size bound.  Put \(C=2^N+|\mathcal J|\).  By \(\text{lem:finite-factor-support-reduction}\), each exogenous factor may be padded to \(C\) states without changing the attainable image.  A vertex has at most \(N-1\) observed parents and at most \(w_G\) incident exogenous factors.  Its Boolean structural table consequently has at most
\[
2^{N-1}C^{w_G}
\]
input rows and at most \(2^{2^{N-1}C^{w_G}}\) possible values as a whole table.  Across the \(N\) vertices, a duplicate-free normalized catalogue thus has at most
\[
2^{N2^{N-1}C^{w_G}}
\tag{35}
\]
branches.

Let \(m\) be the number of exogenous factors.  There is one private factor per observed vertex and one factor per bidirected edge, so
\[
m=N+|E_G^{\leftrightarrow}|.
\]
Counting incidences of these factors at the observed vertices and using the definition of \(w_G\) gives
\[
N+2|E_G^{\leftrightarrow}|\le Nw_G.
\]
It follows that
\[
m\le\frac{N(w_G+1)}2.
\tag{36}
\]
Thus each branch has at most
\[
C^m\le C^{N(w_G+1)/2}
\]
raw signatures.  Independently, the observational part of \(T_{G,\mathcal J}\) is one of \(2^N\) one-hot vectors, while its query part is one of \(3^{|\mathcal J|}\) vectors in \(\{-1,0,1\}^{\mathcal J}\).  Hence a branch has at most \(2^N3^{|\mathcal J|}\) quotient cells.  The number of its quotient cells is bounded by the smaller of these two quantities.  Multiplication by the branch bound (35) proves
\[
|\overline\Omega_{G,\mathcal J}|
\le
2^{N2^{N-1}C^{w_G}}
\min\left\{C^{N(w_G+1)/2},2^N3^{|\mathcal J|}\right\}.
\]
This completes the obstruction, decision, selector, and size claims.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is a constructive negative result for a tempting computational shortcut: it separates the unrestricted response-signature and quotient-simplex lifts used in RossettoAntonucci2026 and ChoeEtAl2026 from the factorized semi-Markovian model class required here, rather than merely presenting an isolated incompatible graph. \textit{Gap.} RossettoAntonucci2026 optimizes arbitrary common-latent signature masses, and ChoeEtAl2026 preserves an unrestricted canonical-domain simplex by quotienting. Neither result retains independent, overlapping private and pairwise edge-latent factors, so neither establishes that its unrestricted quotient lift preserves this model class. \textit{Consumer.} The parity-tilt certificate is full-support and rational, so an implementation can reject the unsafe simplex lift with an auditable polynomial witness rather than attributing failure to boundary cells. The exact alternating-block factor-incidence test identifies the exceptional graphs where a quotient lift is safe, while the catalogue bound makes that diagnostic and raw fallback explicitly finite.

\begin{lemma}[lem:finite-factor-support-reduction]\label{lem:finite-factor-support-reduction}
Fix \(G\) and a finite query set \(\mathcal J\).  Put \(C=2^N+|\mathcal J|\).  Every finite binary semi-Markovian SCM on \(G\) has another SCM with the same vector \((P,(q_j)_{j\in\mathcal J})\), the same deterministic structural recursion, mutually independent private and pairwise exogenous parents, and \(|\mathcal U_a|\le C\) for every \(a\).  After padding smaller supports by zero-weight states, the resulting systems range over a finite catalogue of deterministic tables, and on every table branch the map from the independent latent-factor weights to \((P,(q_j)_{j\in\mathcal J})\) is multi-affine.  Conversely, every point of every such product-of-simplices branch reconstructs a finite semi-Markovian SCM on \(G\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let the exogenous factors be indexed by the finite set consisting of the \(N\) singleton factors and the factors indexed by \(E_G^{\leftrightarrow}\).  Finiteness follows because \(G\) has the finite vertex set \(\mathcal W_H\).  For a fixed factor \(U_a\), hold the deterministic tables and the laws of all other factors fixed.  For \(u\in\mathcal U_a\), let
\[
b_a(u)=\mathbb E\!\left[\left((e_w)_{w\in\{0,1\}^{\mathcal W_H}},(d_j)_{j\in\mathcal J}\right)\middle|U_a=u\right]
 \in\mathbb R^{2^N+|\mathcal J|}.
\]
The conditional expectation is well defined because all state spaces are finite.  Mutual independence in \(\text{ass:independent-edge-latents}\) gives
\[
\left(P,(q_j)_{j\in\mathcal J}\right)
=\sum_{u\in\mathcal U_a}\Pr(U_a=u)b_a(u).
\tag{1}
\]
For every \(u\), the first \(2^N\) coordinates of \(b_a(u)\) sum to one.  Hence the points \(b_a(u)\) lie in an affine subspace of dimension at most
\[
(2^N-1)+|\mathcal J|=C-1.
\]
Suppose more than \(C\) states have positive mass.  Their augmented vectors \((1,b_a(u))\) are linearly dependent, so there are real numbers \(c_u\), not all zero, satisfying
\[
\sum_u c_u=0,\qquad \sum_u c_u b_a(u)=0.
\tag{2}
\]
After replacing \(c\) by \(-c\) if necessary, it has both a positive and a negative coordinate because its coordinates sum to zero.  The closed interval
\[
I=\{t\in\mathbb R:\Pr(U_a=u)+t c_u\ge0\text{ for every }u\}
\]
contains zero and has a finite nonzero endpoint.  At either such endpoint at least one formerly positive weight is zero.  Equations (1)--(2) show that the new weights still sum to one and preserve the whole vector in (1).  Repeating this finite elimination leaves at most \(C\) positive states of \(U_a\).

Apply this operation successively to every exogenous factor.  At each step the current full vector \((P,(q_j)_j)\) is unchanged, so all factors end with support size at most \(C\).  Restricting each \(f_v\) from \(\text{ass:structural-recursion}\) to the retained states does not alter any recursion having positive probability.  Pad a smaller support to \(C\) states with zero weights.  There are finitely many factors, finitely many binary observed-parent assignments, and finitely many assignments of the padded exogenous inputs.  Therefore only finitely many Boolean structural-table systems occur.  With a table system fixed, each observational or interventional response is deterministic at a joint latent state \(\kappa\), and independence gives its mass as \(\prod_a\lambda_a(\kappa_a)\).  Summing these monomials proves multi-affinity.

Conversely, given a branch and weights \((\lambda_a)_a\), draw the \(U_a\)'s independently with these weights and use the displayed deterministic tables.  This construction satisfies \(\text{ass:structural-recursion}\) and \(\text{ass:independent-edge-latents}\), and its observational and interventional recursions are exactly those used to compile the branch.  This proves the converse and the claimed equality of response images.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:one-block-real-algebraic-elimination]\label{lem:one-block-real-algebraic-elimination}
Let \(s,D,\tau,k\) be positive integers.  For a rational polynomial system with at most \(s\) predicates, degree at most \(D\), input-coefficient bit length at most \(\tau\), and one block of \(k\) quantified variables, a verified well-behaved one-block real-quantifier-elimination execution decides feasibility and computes a projection with one free variable using \((sD)^{O(k)}\) exact scalar arithmetic and sign operations.  Its projection and sign-cell sample-point encodings have algebraic degree \(D^{O(k)}\); every integer coefficient in the output or the attested well-behaved intermediate execution, and every rational isolating datum in the output, has bit length \(\tau(sD)^{O(k)}\).  Every nonempty sign cell admits an exact real-algebraic sample-point encoding within these degree and height bounds.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[lem:multiblock-real-algebraic-qe]\label{lem:multiblock-real-algebraic-qe}
\texttt{Every finite first-\allowbreak{}order formula over a real closed field with rational polynomial atoms,\allowbreak{} including arbitrary finite Boolean combinations and alternating universal and existential variable blocks,\allowbreak{} admits effective quantifier elimination:\allowbreak{} its truth is decidable when it is a sentence,\allowbreak{} and when it has free variables its solution set has an effectively computable quantifier-\allowbreak{}free semialgebraic description. Consequently,\allowbreak{} if a first-\allowbreak{}order formula uniquely specifies a point in each nonempty compact semialgebraic fiber,\allowbreak{} its graph is semialgebraic and effectively describable.}
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[lem:tiv-summary-iv-representation]\label{lem:tiv-summary-iv-representation}
Assume \(H=L=\gamma=1\), \(\mathcal S=\mathcal S_{\mathrm{TIV}}\), and strict observed positivity.  For every \((G,\mathcal M)\in\mathfrak F(\mathcal S,L,H,p)\), write \(p_{zxy}=\Pr_p(A_{-1}=z,X_{-1}=x,Y_0=y)\) and \(\pi_z=\Pr_p(A_{-1}=z)\).  Then \(\pi_0,\pi_1>0\), and there is a probability mass \(\eta\) on \(t=(x_0,x_1,y_0,y_1)\in\{0,1\}^4\) such that
\[
p_{zxy}=\pi_z\sum_t\eta(t)\mathbf 1\{x=x_z,\ y=y_x\},
\qquad
q(\mathcal M)=\sum_t\eta(t)(y_1-y_0).
\]
This representation holds for every admissible full-time refinement, including refinements containing additional summary-compatible bidirected edges.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Positive directed lags and \(H=L=1\) imply that every directed edge goes from time \(-1\) to time \(0\).  Exact projection to \(\mathcal S_{\mathrm{TIV}}\) therefore forces \(X_{-1}\to Y_0\) and permits no other directed edge incident to \(A_{-1}\), \(X_{-1}\), or \(Y_0\).  Moreover, the absence of an \(A\leftrightarrow Y\) summary edge means that no pairwise exogenous parent is shared by \(A_{-1}\) and \(Y_0\).

Partition the mutually independent exogenous parents as follows.  Let \(R\) contain all factors entering \(A_{-1}\), and put every remaining factor into \(T\); a factor entering \(X_{-1}\) may occur in either block, whereas every factor entering \(Y_0\) belongs to \(T\).  Factors in \(R\) that do not enter \(X_{-1}\) are simply ignored by its structural table.  By \(\text{ass:independent-edge-latents}\), \(R\) and \(T\) are independent.  The structural recursion can consequently be written, after harmlessly adjoining unused coordinates, as
\[
A_{-1}=a(R),\qquad X_{-1}=x(R,T),\qquad Y_0=y(X_{-1},T).
\tag{3}
\]
Here the last equality uses the fact just established that \(Y_0\) has no exogenous input from the \(A\)-side block.  Any additional \(A\leftrightarrow X\) or \(X\leftrightarrow Y\) edges are already assigned to \(R\) or \(T\) and do not change (3).

Strict observed positivity implies
\[
\pi_z=\Pr\{a(R)=z\}>0,\qquad z=0,1,
\]
so the conditional laws \(\mathcal L(R\mid a(R)=z)\) exist.  On an auxiliary finite probability space, draw \(R_0\) and \(R_1\) with these two conditional laws and draw \(T\) independently of them.  Define
\[
x_z=x(R_z,T),\qquad y_x=y(x,T),\qquad
t=(x_0,x_1,y_0,y_1),\qquad \eta=\mathcal L(t).
\]
Conditioning (3) on \(A_{-1}=z\), and using the independence of \(R\) and \(T\), yields
\[
\Pr(X_{-1}=x,Y_0=y\mid A_{-1}=z)
=\sum_t\eta(t)\mathbf 1\{x=x_z,\ y=y_x\}.
\]
Multiplication by the positive \(\pi_z\) proves the first displayed formula.  Under \(\operatorname{do}(X_{-1}=x)\), (3) replaces the middle equation and gives \(Y_0=y(x,T)=y_x\).  Thus the two intervention means differ by
\[
\mathbb E[y_1-y_0]=\sum_t\eta(t)(y_1-y_0),
\]
which is the second formula by \(\text{def:micro-cde}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:positive-lag-cyclic-cde-adjustment]\label{lem:positive-lag-cyclic-cde-adjustment}
For every \((G,\mathcal M)\in\mathfrak F(\mathcal S,L,H,p)\), suppose that
\[
\bigl\{b\in\mathcal W_H:\{Y_0,b\}\in\mathcal B_H(\mathcal S)\bigr\}
\cap\operatorname{An}_{D_{H,L}(\mathcal S)}(W)=\varnothing.
\tag{MC}
\]
Then, without requiring the strongly connected component of \(Y\) in \(\mathcal S\) to be a singleton,
\[
\mathbb E_{\mathcal M}\!\left[Y_0\mid\operatorname{do}(X_{-\gamma}=x,Z=z)\right]
=\mathbb E_p\!\left[Y_0\mid X_{-\gamma}=x,Z=z\right],
\qquad x\in\{0,1\},
\]
and consequently \(q(\mathcal M)=A_{\mathrm{FA}}(p)\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write
\[
W=\operatorname{PP}_{\mathcal S,L}(Y_0)
=\{X_{-\gamma}\}\mathbin{\dot\cup}Z,
\qquad w_x=(x,z),\quad x\in\{0,1\}.
\tag{1}
\]
The disjoint union follows from \(\text{ass:focal-positive-lag-parent}\) and the definition of \(Z\).  By \(\text{ass:strict-observed-positivity}\), every cell of \(p\) on \(\{0,1\}^{\mathcal W_H}\) has positive mass.  Each assignment \(W=w_x\) has at least one full-window extension, and its probability is the sum of the positive probabilities of all such extensions.  Therefore
\[
p(W=w_x)>0,\qquad x\in\{0,1\}.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus every observational conditional expectation below is well defined; equation (2) is the division condition used in conditioning.

We first compare the directed part of a refinement with the supergraph.  If \((u,s)\to(v,t)\in E_G^{\to}\), then \(u\to v\in E_{\mathcal S}^{\to}\) by \(\text{ass:exact-summary-projection}\), and \(1\le t-s\le L\) by \(\text{ass:positive-bounded-directed-lags}\).  Hence the definition of \(D_{H,L}(\mathcal S)\) gives
\[
E_G^{\to}\subseteq E_D
\quad\text{and consequently}\quad
\operatorname{An}_G(A)\subseteq\operatorname{An}_{D_{H,L}(\mathcal S)}(A)
\quad(A\subseteq\mathcal W_H).
\tag{3}
\]
Every directed edge in \(D_{H,L}(\mathcal S)\) strictly increases its time coordinate, so this directed graph is acyclic.  The same positive-lag property makes \(Y_0\) terminal in the observed window: an edge \(Y_0\to(C,t)\) would require \(t\ge1\), whereas \(t\in\mathbb T_H\) implies \(t\le0\).  In particular,
\[
Y_0\notin\operatorname{An}_G(W).
\tag{4}
\]
Indeed, membership in the reflexive ancestral closure would give a directed path from \(Y_0\) to a vertex of \(W\), while \(W\) consists of vertices at strictly negative times.

If \((B,s)\in\operatorname{pa}_G(Y_0)\), then \((B,s)\to(Y,0)\in E_G^{\to}\).  The two structural assumptions just used give \(B\to Y\in E_{\mathcal S}^{\to}\) and \(1\le -s\le L\).  By the definition of the positive-lag possible-parent set,
\[
\operatorname{pa}_G(Y_0)\subseteq W.
\tag{5}
\]

We next isolate the exogenous blocks.  For \(v\in\mathcal W_H\), let
\[
\mathcal I_G(v)=\bigl\{\{v\}\bigr\}\cup
\bigl\{a\in E_G^{\leftrightarrow}:v\in a\bigr\},
\qquad
\mathcal E_G(A)=\bigcup_{v\in\operatorname{An}_G(A)}\mathcal I_G(v).
\tag{6}
\]
Because \(G\) is a finite ADMG, its directed part has a topological ordering.  Induction in that ordering using \(\text{ass:structural-recursion}\) shows that \(V_A\) is a deterministic function of \((U_a:a\in\mathcal E_G(A))\): at a vertex \(v\), the recursion introduces only factors indexed by \(\mathcal I_G(v)\), and the induction hypotheses for its directed parents introduce factors incident to directed ancestors of \(v\).  Apply this fact with \(A=W\).

We claim
\[
\mathcal I_G(Y_0)\cap\mathcal E_G(W)=\varnothing.
\tag{7}
\]
For the private index \(\{Y_0\}\), membership in \(\mathcal E_G(W)\) would force \(Y_0\in\operatorname{An}_G(W)\), contrary to (4).  Now let a pairwise index \(a=\{Y_0,b\}\in E_G^{\leftrightarrow}\) belong to \(\mathcal E_G(W)\).  By (6), at least one endpoint of \(a\) belongs to \(\operatorname{An}_G(W)\).  Equation (4) excludes \(Y_0\), hence \(b\in\operatorname{An}_G(W)\).  By (3), \(b\in\operatorname{An}_{D_{H,L}(\mathcal S)}(W)\).  Moreover, the refinement definition gives \(E_G^{\leftrightarrow}\subseteq\mathcal B_H(\mathcal S)\), so \(\{Y_0,b\}\in\mathcal B_H(\mathcal S)\).  This contradicts condition \(\mathrm{(MC)}\), proving (7).  Notice that this is the exact latent-block check: an edge latent incident to \(Y_0\) can enter the graphical recursive determination of \(W\) only through its other endpoint, and that endpoint must be a directed ancestor of \(W\).

Let \(U_Y=(U_a:a\in\mathcal I_G(Y_0))\) and \(U_W=(U_a:a\in\mathcal E_G(W))\).  The index sets are disjoint by (7).  The entire finite latent family is mutually independent by \(\text{ass:independent-edge-latents}\); factorization of its joint mass function therefore gives \(U_Y\perp U_W\).  Since \(V_W\) is a deterministic function of \(U_W\),
\[
U_Y\perp V_W.
\tag{8}
\]
This step allows several independent edge latents to overlap at an observed vertex; it uses independence of distinct latent factors, not disjointness of their observed children.

By \(\text{ass:structural-recursion}\) and (5), the terminal structural equation is
\[
Y_0=f_{Y_0}\!\left(V_{\operatorname{pa}_G(Y_0)},U_Y\right),
\qquad \operatorname{pa}_G(Y_0)\subseteq W.
\tag{9}
\]
For \(x\in\{0,1\}\), equations (2), (8), and (9), together with \(\text{ass:observed-law-compatibility}\), imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_p[Y_0\mid W=w_x]
&=\sum_u f_{Y_0}\!\left((w_x)_{\operatorname{pa}_G(Y_0)},u\right)
  \Pr_{\mathcal M}(U_Y=u\mid W=w_x)\\
&=\sum_u f_{Y_0}\!\left((w_x)_{\operatorname{pa}_G(Y_0)},u\right)
  \Pr_{\mathcal M}(U_Y=u).
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
Under \(\operatorname{do}(W=w_x)\), all actual parents in (5) are fixed at their entries in \(w_x\), while the intervention leaves the exogenous law unchanged.  Truncating the structural recursion at the intervened coordinates and using (9) therefore gives
\[
\mathbb E_{\mathcal M}[Y_0\mid\operatorname{do}(W=w_x)]
=\sum_u f_{Y_0}\!\left((w_x)_{\operatorname{pa}_G(Y_0)},u\right)
  \Pr_{\mathcal M}(U_Y=u).
\tag{11}
\]
Comparison of (10) and (11), followed by (1), proves the two conditional-mean equalities in the statement.  Subtracting the case \(x=0\) from the case \(x=1\), and using \(\text{def:micro-cde}\) and the definition of \(A_{\mathrm{FA}}(p)\), proves \(q(\mathcal M)=A_{\mathrm{FA}}(p)\).

Finally, condition \(\mathrm{(MC)}\) is load-bearing for this argument and cannot simply be omitted.  Take \(H=L=\gamma=1\), directed edge \(X_{-1}\to Y_0\), and bidirected edge \(X_{-1}\leftrightarrow Y_0\).  Then \(W=\{X_{-1}\}\), and the other endpoint \(X_{-1}\) belongs to the reflexive closure \(\operatorname{An}_{D_{H,L}(\mathcal S)}(W)\), so \(\mathrm{(MC)}\) fails.  Let the shared edge latent \(U\) be fair, let independent private noises satisfy \(E_X\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(e)\) and \(E_Y\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(\delta)\), where \(e,\delta\in(0,1/2)\), and set
\[
X_{-1}=U\mathbin\oplus E_X,
\qquad
Y_0=(X_{-1}\mathbin\lor U)\mathbin\oplus E_Y.
\tag{12}
\]
Give every remaining window vertex an independent private Bernoulli noise with parameter in \((0,1)\).  For each observed cell, choosing a value of \(U\) and then the uniquely required private-noise values gives a latent configuration of positive mass, so the induced observed law is strictly positive.  Directly,
\[
\begin{aligned}
q(\mathcal M)
&=(1-\delta)-\frac12=\frac{1-2\delta}{2},\\
\mathbb E[Y_0\mid X_{-1}=1]-\mathbb E[Y_0\mid X_{-1}=0]
&=(1-\delta)-\bigl(\delta+(1-2\delta)e\bigr)
=(1-2\delta)(1-e).
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
The two quantities differ because \(e\ne1/2\).  This exact two-node calculation verifies failure when an incident edge latent can enter the controlled parent itself.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The exact time-stamped ancestry test isolates whether a latent factor entering terminal \(Y_0\) can also enter the recursive determination of the controlled possible-parent vector. \textit{Gap.} A label-level bidirected-ancestor exclusion rejects harmless placements whose time indices cannot feed the controlled parent vector; the bounded-window criterion removes precisely that conservatism in the present positive-lag independent-edge-latent class. \textit{Consumer.} The envelope-collapse theorem invokes this identifying map on the maximally enlarged micro-ancestry regime.

\begin{theorem}[thm:positive-lag-fa-extension-collapse]\label{thm:positive-lag-fa-extension-collapse}
Suppose \(\mathfrak F(\mathcal S,L,H,p)\ne\varnothing\) and
\[
\bigl\{b\in\mathcal W_H:\{Y_0,b\}\in\mathcal B_H(\mathcal S)\bigr\}
\cap\operatorname{An}_{D_{H,L}(\mathcal S)}(W)=\varnothing.
\tag{MC}
\]
Then every \((G,\mathcal M)\in\mathfrak F(\mathcal S,L,H,p)\) satisfies \(q(\mathcal M)=A_{\mathrm{FA}}(p)\), and
\[
\Theta(\mathcal S,L,H,p)=\{A_{\mathrm{FA}}(p)\},
\qquad
\underline q(p)=\overline q(p)=A_{\mathrm{FA}}(p).
\]
Condition \(\mathrm{(MC)}\) is strictly weaker than excluding every summary bidirected edge between \(Y\) and a directed summary ancestor of \(Y\).  In particular, a strict-gain instance has \(H=L=\gamma=1\), three distinct component labels \(B,X,Y\), summary directed edges \(B\to X\to Y\), and summary bidirected edge \(Y\leftrightarrow B\): condition \(\mathrm{(MC)}\) holds and the fiber is nonempty for a strictly positive observed law, although \(B\) is a directed summary ancestor of \(Y\).  Directed cycles through \(Y\) also remain permitted.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By \(\text{ass:focal-positive-lag-parent}\),
\[
W=\{X_{-\gamma}\}\mathbin{\dot\cup}Z.
\tag{C1}
\]
For \(x\in\{0,1\}\), the probability \(p(W=(x,z))\) is a finite sum of full-window cell probabilities.  Every summand is positive by \(\text{ass:strict-observed-positivity}\), and at least one completion exists; hence
\[
p(W=(x,z))>0.
\tag{C2}
\]
Thus both conditionals in \(A_{\mathrm{FA}}(p)\) are well defined.

Fix \((G,\mathcal M)\in\mathfrak F(\mathcal S,L,H,p)\).  Its induced law is \(p\) by \(\text{ass:observed-law-compatibility}\), and all other hypotheses in the universal property \(\mathrm{(UA)}\) of \(\text{thm:positive-lag-fa-exact-frontier}\) follow from membership in \(\mathfrak F(\mathcal S,L,H,p)\).  The displayed hypothesis \(\mathrm{(MC)}\) and the forward implication of that theorem therefore give
\[
q(\mathcal M)=A_{\mathrm{FA}}(p).
\tag{C3}
\]
The fiber member was arbitrary, so \(\text{def:sharp-envelope}\) yields
\[
\Theta(\mathcal S,L,H,p)\subseteq\{A_{\mathrm{FA}}(p)\}.
\tag{C4}
\]
The assumed nonemptiness of the fiber supplies a member to which (C3) applies, proving the reverse inclusion in (C4).  Hence
\[
\Theta(\mathcal S,L,H,p)=\{A_{\mathrm{FA}}(p)\}.
\tag{C5}
\]
Taking the infimum and supremum of this nonempty singleton and using \(\text{def:sharp-envelope}\) proves
\[
\underline q(p)=\overline q(p)=A_{\mathrm{FA}}(p).
\tag{C6}
\]

We next compare \(\mathrm{(MC)}\) with the label-level exclusion in the statement.  Suppose that exclusion holds and, toward a contradiction, let \(b=(B,s)\) belong to the intersection in \(\mathrm{(MC)}\).  The relation \(\{Y_0,b\}\in\mathcal B_H(\mathcal S)\) implies \(Y\leftrightarrow B\in E_{\mathcal S}^{\leftrightarrow}\).  Ancestry supplies a directed path in \(D_{H,L}(\mathcal S)\) from \(b\) to some \(w=(C,-\ell)\in W\).  Projection of that path is a directed summary walk from \(B\) to \(C\), with the length-zero case giving \(B=C\).  Since \(w\in W\), the definition of \(W\) gives \(C\to Y\in E_{\mathcal S}^{\to}\).  Concatenation and deletion of repeated vertices give a directed summary path from \(B\) to \(Y\).  Thus \(B\) is a directed summary ancestor of \(Y\), contradicting the label-level exclusion.  Therefore that exclusion implies \(\mathrm{(MC)}\).

The implication is strict.  Let \(H=L=\gamma=1\), take distinct labels \(B,X,Y\), and give the summary graph precisely the directed edges
\[
B\to X,
\qquad
X\to Y,
\tag{C7}
\]
and the bidirected edge \(Y\leftrightarrow B\).  The directed supergraph has only
\[
B_{-1}\to X_0,
\qquad
X_{-1}\to Y_0.
\tag{C8}
\]
Therefore \(W=\{X_{-1}\}\),
\[
\operatorname{An}_{D_{1,1}(\mathcal S)}(W)=\{X_{-1}\},
\tag{C9}
\]
and the endpoints paired with \(Y_0\) in \(\mathcal B_H(\mathcal S)\), namely \(B_{-1}\) and \(B_0\), do not lie in (C9).  Thus \(\mathrm{(MC)}\) holds, even though \(B\to X\to Y\) makes \(B\) a directed summary ancestor of \(Y\).

To show that this strict gain has a nonempty positive fiber, let \(G^{\star}\) contain the two arrows in (C8) and \(Y_0\leftrightarrow B_{-1}\).  It obeys the positive-lag restriction, its bidirected edge is allowed, and its exact projection is the stated summary graph.  Let \(U\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/4)\) be the edge latent and let every vertex have a mutually independent private binary noise with both values of positive probability, all independent of \(U\).  Define
\[
\begin{array}{lll}
B_{-1}=U\mathbin\oplus E_{B,-1},
&B_0=E_{B,0},
&X_{-1}=E_{X,-1},\\
X_0=B_{-1}\mathbin\oplus E_{X,0},
&Y_{-1}=E_{Y,-1},
&Y_0=X_{-1}\mathbin\oplus U\mathbin\oplus E_{Y,0}.
\end{array}
\tag{C10}
\]
These equations satisfy structural recursion and independent-edge-latent factorization.  For any observed six-bit cell, fix \(U=0\) and solve (C10) for the six private noises.  The resulting finite latent configuration has positive probability, so the observed law \(p^{\star}\) is strictly positive.  Consequently
\[
(G^{\star},\mathcal M^{\star})\in\mathfrak F(\mathcal S,1,1,p^{\star}),
\tag{C11}
\]
which proves the advertised nonemptiness.

Finally, directed summary cycles through \(Y\) are compatible with the theorem.  For \(H=L=\gamma=1\), take the two-label summary with directed edges \(X\to Y\) and \(Y\to X\), and no bidirected edges.  The full-time graph with arrows \(X_{-1}\to Y_0\) and \(Y_{-1}\to X_0\) belongs to the refinement class: each arrow advances time, although its projection is cyclic.  Condition \(\mathrm{(MC)}\) is vacuous.  Equations
\[
X_{-1}=E_{X,-1},
\quad
Y_{-1}=E_{Y,-1},
\quad
X_0=Y_{-1}\mathbin\oplus E_{X,0},
\quad
Y_0=X_{-1}\mathbin\oplus E_{Y,0},
\tag{C12}
\]
with independent two-sided private noises generate a strictly positive law by the same solve-for-the-private-noises argument used for (C10).  Thus a cyclic summary has a nonempty positive fiber satisfying \(\mathrm{(MC)}\), while its positive-lag full-time refinement remains acyclic.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The sharp envelope collapses whenever the exact bounded-window ancestry test prevents any terminal outcome latent from entering the controlled parent recursion. \textit{Gap.} The result strictly enlarges the former summary-ancestor regime: a one-lag \(B\to X\to Y\), \(Y\leftrightarrow B\) instance is admitted because its only directed \(B\to X\) placement occurs too late to affect \(X_{-1}\). \textit{Consumer.} Users obtain the observed adjustment contrast on the graphically characterized micro-ancestry regime and sharp refinement bounds elsewhere.

\begin{theorem}[thm:positive-lag-fa-exact-frontier]\label{thm:positive-lag-fa-exact-frontier}
Put
\[
\mathrm{(MC)}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\bigl\{b\in\mathcal W_H:\{Y_0,b\}\in\mathcal B_H(\mathcal S)\bigr\}
\cap\operatorname{An}_{D_{H,L}(\mathcal S)}(W)=\varnothing.
\]
For the fixed query \((X_{-\gamma},Y_0,z)\), define the universal unadjusted-adjustment property
\[
\mathrm{(UA)}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
q(\mathcal M)=A_{\mathrm{FA}}(p_{\mathcal M})
\]
for every \(G\in\mathfrak G_{H,L}(\mathcal S)\) and every finite binary semi-Markovian model \(\mathcal M\) on \(G\) satisfying structural recursion and mutually independent private and pairwise-edge exogenous variables, whenever its induced observational law \(p_{\mathcal M}\) obeys \(p_{\mathcal M}(a)>0\) for every \(a\in\{0,1\}^{\mathcal W_H}\).  Then
\[
\mathrm{(MC)}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathrm{(UA)}.
\]
This equivalence is an exact frontier for universal equality with the unadjusted Ferreira--Assaad contrast, not a necessary-and-sufficient criterion for identification from the full observed law.  Indeed, with \(H=2\), \(L=\gamma=1\), summary edges \(B\to X\to Y\) and \(B\leftrightarrow Y\), the refinement having \(B_{-2}\to X_{-1}\to Y_0\) and \(B_{-2}\leftrightarrow Y_0\) violates \(\mathrm{(MC)}\), but every strictly positive model on that refinement satisfies
\[
q(\mathcal M)=
\sum_{b\in\{0,1\}}
\Bigl\{
\mathbb E_{p_{\mathcal M}}[Y_0\mid X_{-1}=1,B_{-2}=b]
-
\mathbb E_{p_{\mathcal M}}[Y_0\mid X_{-1}=0,B_{-2}=b]
\Bigr\}p_{\mathcal M}(B_{-2}=b).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We first prove sufficiency.  Assume \(\mathrm{(MC)}\), fix \(G\in\mathfrak G_{H,L}(\mathcal S)\), and fix a model \(\mathcal M\) quantified in \(\mathrm{(UA)}\).  Let \(p_{\mathcal M}\) be its induced observational law.  By its definition, \(\mathcal M\) satisfies \(\text{ass:observed-law-compatibility}\) with \(p=p_{\mathcal M}\); the hypotheses defining \(\mathfrak G_{H,L}(\mathcal S)\), together with structural recursion and independent edge latents, therefore give
\[
(G,\mathcal M)\in\mathfrak F(\mathcal S,L,H,p_{\mathcal M}).
\tag{F1}
\]
The stipulated positivity of \(p_{\mathcal M}\) is exactly \(\text{ass:strict-observed-positivity}\).  Applying \(\text{lem:positive-lag-cyclic-cde-adjustment}\) to (F1) yields
\[
q(\mathcal M)=A_{\mathrm{FA}}(p_{\mathcal M}).
\tag{F2}
\]
Since \(G\) and \(\mathcal M\) were arbitrary, (F2) proves \(\mathrm{(UA)}\).

For necessity, suppose that \(\mathrm{(MC)}\) fails.  By \(\text{def:bounded-window-micro-ancestry}\), there are a vertex \(b\in\mathcal W_H\), a vertex \(w\in W\), and a directed path
\[
v_0=b\to v_1\to\cdots\to v_m=w
\quad\text{in }D_{H,L}(\mathcal S),
\qquad
\{Y_0,b\}\in\mathcal B_H(\mathcal S).
\tag{F3}
\]
Choose such a path with the least possible number of edges.  Then
\[
v_i\notin W\quad(0\le i<m),
\tag{F4}
\]
because otherwise its prefix would be a shorter path from \(b\) to \(W\).  The case \(m=0\), where \(b=w\), is included.

We construct an admissible refinement.  Include in its directed edge set every edge of the path in (F3) and the focal edge \(X_{-\gamma}\to Y_0\), which is allowed by \(\text{ass:focal-positive-lag-parent}\).  For every directed summary edge not yet represented, add its lag-one placement \((u,-1)\to(v,0)\).  Include the bidirected edge \(Y_0\leftrightarrow b\), and for every bidirected summary edge not yet represented choose one pair of distinct time-indexed endpoints allowed by \(\mathcal B_H(\mathcal S)\).  Such padding is possible because \(H\ge L\ge1\).  Every directed edge just chosen advances time by between one and \(L\) steps, so the directed part is acyclic.  No edge projects outside \(\mathcal S\), and every summary edge has a representative.  Thus the resulting graph, denoted \(G^{\dagger}\), satisfies
\[
G^{\dagger}\in\mathfrak G_{H,L}(\mathcal S).
\tag{F5}
\]
The padded edges will be structurally inert.

Let \(U\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\) be the exogenous variable for the edge \(Y_0\leftrightarrow b\).  Give every other bidirected edge an independent fair binary exogenous variable.  Give every observed vertex \(a\) an independent binary private variable \(E_a\), independently of all edge variables.  Along the path take
\[
\Pr(E_{v_i}=1)=\frac14,
\qquad 0\le i\le m,
\tag{F6}
\]
and define
\[
V_{v_0}=U\mathbin\oplus E_{v_0},
\qquad
V_{v_i}=V_{v_{i-1}}\mathbin\oplus E_{v_i}\qquad(1\le i\le m).
\tag{F7}
\]
For each controlled vertex in \(W\setminus\{w\}\), set \(V_a=E_a\) with \(E_a\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\).  This prescription is consistent with (F7) by (F4).  At every remaining vertex other than \(Y_0\), use the same fair-private-noise equation unless (F7) already defines it.  All actual parents and nonspecial edge latents may be ignored by these structural tables, as announced when padding the graph.

Let \(E_0\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/4)\) be the private variable at \(Y_0\).  Because \(W=\{X_{-\gamma}\}\mathbin{\dot\cup}Z\), exactly one of the following cases holds.  Define the terminal equation by
\[
V_{Y_0}=
\begin{cases}
(V_{X_{-\gamma}}\mathbin\vee U)\mathbin\oplus E_0,
&w=X_{-\gamma},\\[2mm]
(V_{X_{-\gamma}}\mathbin\wedge U)\mathbin\oplus E_0,
&w\in Z.
\end{cases}
\tag{F8}
\]
The focal edge in \(G^{\dagger}\) and the special bidirected edge make every argument in (F8) a permitted structural argument.  Equations (F6)--(F8), the inert equations, and mutual independence give a finite binary model \(\mathcal M^{\dagger}\) satisfying \(\text{ass:structural-recursion}\) and \(\text{ass:independent-edge-latents}\).

This model has full observed support.  To see this, fix any observed cell \(a\in\{0,1\}^{\mathcal W_H}\) and fix every edge latent, including \(U\), at zero; that edge-latent configuration has positive probability.  For every vertex \(v\), its equation has the form
\[
V_v=h_v(V_{\operatorname{pa}_{G^{\dagger}}(v)},(U_e:e\ni v))\mathbin\oplus E_v.
\tag{F9}
\]
Set \(E_v=a_v\mathbin\oplus h_v(a_{\operatorname{pa}_{G^{\dagger}}(v)},0)\).  Every private variable takes either value with positive probability, and there are finitely many mutually independent exogenous variables.  The latent configuration just specified therefore has positive probability and generates \(a\).  Consequently
\[
p_{\mathcal M^{\dagger}}(a)>0
\quad\text{for every }a\in\{0,1\}^{\mathcal W_H}.
\tag{F10}
\]

It remains to calculate the two contrasts exactly.  Put
\[
R=E_{v_0}\mathbin\oplus\cdots\mathbin\oplus E_{v_m},
\qquad
\kappa=\prod_{i=0}^{m}\bigl(1-2\Pr(E_{v_i}=1)\bigr)=2^{-(m+1)}.
\tag{F11}
\]
Independence in \(\text{ass:independent-edge-latents}\) and (F6) give
\[
\Pr(R=1)=r:=\frac{1-\kappa}{2}<\frac12,
\qquad
V_w=U\mathbin\oplus R.
\tag{F12}
\]
Since \(U\) is fair and independent of \(R\), \(V_w\) is fair and Bayes' formula, whose denominators are \(1/2>0\), gives
\[
\Pr(U=1\mid V_w=0)=r,
\qquad
\Pr(U=1\mid V_w=1)=1-r.
\tag{F13}
\]
All coordinates of \(W\setminus\{w\}\) are independent fair private variables, so conditioning on them does not alter (F13).

If \(w=X_{-\gamma}\), intervention on \(W\) leaves \(U\) fair.  From the first equation in (F8),
\[
q(\mathcal M^{\dagger})
=\left(1-2\Pr(E_0=1)\right)\left(1-\Pr(U=1)\right)
=\frac14.
\tag{F14}
\]
Observationally, the OR input equals one throughout the arm \(X_{-\gamma}=1\), while (F13) applies in the arm \(X_{-\gamma}=0\).  Hence
\[
A_{\mathrm{FA}}(p_{\mathcal M^{\dagger}})
=\frac12(1-r)
=\frac14+\frac{\kappa}{4}
\ne q(\mathcal M^{\dagger}).
\tag{F15}
\]
If \(w\in Z\), write \(z_w\) for the fixed component of \(z\), and put \(\pi_{z_w}=r\) when \(z_w=0\) and \(\pi_{z_w}=1-r\) when \(z_w=1\).  The second equation in (F8) gives
\[
q(\mathcal M^{\dagger})=\frac14,
\qquad
A_{\mathrm{FA}}(p_{\mathcal M^{\dagger}})=\frac12\pi_{z_w},
\qquad
\left|A_{\mathrm{FA}}(p_{\mathcal M^{\dagger}})-q(\mathcal M^{\dagger})\right|=\frac{\kappa}{4}>0.
\tag{F16}
\]
Thus failure of \(\mathrm{(MC)}\) supplies an admissible full-support rational model violating \(\mathrm{(UA)}\).  This proves \(\mathrm{(UA)}\Rightarrow\mathrm{(MC)}\), and (F2) proves the converse.

Finally, we prove the stated delimitation from full-law identification.  Set \(H=2\), \(L=\gamma=1\), take distinct labels \(B,X,Y\), and let \(G^{\mathrm{alt}}\) have precisely the nonisolated edges
\[
B_{-2}\to X_{-1}\to Y_0,
\qquad
B_{-2}\leftrightarrow Y_0.
\tag{F17}
\]
Its projection has summary directed edges \(B\to X\to Y\) and bidirected edge \(B\leftrightarrow Y\), so \(G^{\mathrm{alt}}\in\mathfrak G_{2,1}(\mathcal S)\).  Here \(W=\{X_{-1}\}\), and \(B_{-2}\to X_{-1}\) in \(D_{2,1}(\mathcal S)\); the endpoint \(B_{-2}\) in (F17) therefore witnesses failure of \(\mathrm{(MC)}\).

Consider any strictly positive semi-Markovian model on \(G^{\mathrm{alt}}\).  Writing \(U_{BY}\) for the edge latent and \(U_B,U_X,U_Y\) for the relevant private latents, structural recursion has the form
\[
B_{-2}=f_B(U_B,U_{BY}),
\quad
X_{-1}=f_X(B_{-2},U_X),
\quad
Y_0=f_Y(X_{-1},U_Y,U_{BY}).
\tag{F18}
\]
Mutual independence of the four exogenous variables implies
\[
U_X\ \perp\ (B_{-2},U_Y,U_{BY}),
\quad\text{and hence}\quad
X_{-1}\ \perp\ (U_Y,U_{BY})\mid B_{-2}.
\tag{F19}
\]
For \(x\in\{0,1\}\), define the intervened response \(Y_0(x)=f_Y(x,U_Y,U_{BY})\).  Equation (F19) gives \(Y_0(x)\perp X_{-1}\mid B_{-2}\), and structural recursion gives \(Y_0(x)=Y_0\) on \(\{X_{-1}=x\}\).  Strict positivity makes every conditional below well defined.  Iterated finite summation, conditional independence, and this consistency identity yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[Y_0\mid\operatorname{do}(X_{-1}=x)]
&=\sum_{b\in\{0,1\}}\mathbb E[Y_0(x)\mid B_{-2}=b]p(B_{-2}=b)\\
&=\sum_{b\in\{0,1\}}\mathbb E[Y_0(x)\mid X_{-1}=x,B_{-2}=b]p(B_{-2}=b)\\
&=\sum_{b\in\{0,1\}}\mathbb E[Y_0\mid X_{-1}=x,B_{-2}=b]p(B_{-2}=b).
\end{aligned}
\tag{F20}
\]
Subtracting (F20) at \(x=0\) from its value at \(x=1\) proves the displayed alternative-adjustment formula.  Hence failure of \(\mathrm{(MC)}\) rules out universal equality with the unadjusted contrast, as (F3)--(F16) prove, but does not by itself rule out identification by another functional of the full observed law.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The bounded-window ancestry condition is exactly the graphical frontier for universal equality with the unadjusted Ferreira--Assaad contrast across all admissible positive refinements and models. \textit{Gap.} The converse is deliberately contrast-specific: failure permits a full-support model on which the unadjusted contrast is wrong, while a displayed two-lag graph shows that another observed-law adjustment may still identify the causal effect. \textit{Consumer.} The collapse theorem uses the forward direction, and analysts use the converse to distinguish maximality of this simple contrast from the stronger and generally false claim of a complete identification criterion.

\begin{lemma}[lem:well-behaved-arithmetic-to-bit-lift]\label{lem:well-behaved-arithmetic-to-bit-lift}
Let \(A_{\mathrm{op}},\ell,\Delta\ge 2\).  Consider a verified exact computation with at most \(A_{\mathrm{op}}\) integer or rational additions, subtractions, multiplications, sign tests, comparisons, and exact divisions.  Encode each rational in reduced signed-binary form, each real algebraic number by a square-free integer polynomial together with a rational isolating interval, and each algebraic sample point by such a primitive-element encoding and rational univariate coordinate polynomials.  Suppose every intermediate integer coefficient and every reduced numerator and denominator has bit length at most \(\ell\), the algebraic encodings and polynomial predicates used in decoding have degree at most \(\Delta\) and coefficient and isolating-data bit length at most \(\ell\), and the total number of encoded coefficient slots in the execution, its replay trace, and its sample-point output is at most \(A_{\mathrm{op}}\ell^{c_0}\) for an absolute constant \(c_0\), with trace addresses also of length at most \(\ell\).  Then the execution and exact replay, square-free factorization, Sturm--subresultant real-root isolation, algebraic sign determination and comparison, sample-point decoding, and endpoint comparison require
\[
A_{\mathrm{op}}\ell^{O(1)}+(\Delta\ell)^{O(1)}
\]
bit operations.  The resulting algebraic encodings have degree \(\Delta^{O(1)}\) and coefficient and isolating-data bit length \(\ell\Delta^{O(1)}\).  Consequently, for positive integers \(B,s,D,k\), the substitutions
\[
A_{\mathrm{op}}=(sD)^{O(k)},\qquad
\ell=B(sD)^{O(k)},\qquad
\Delta=D^{O(k)}
\]
imply a bit-operation bound \(B^{O(1)}(sD)^{O(k)}\), output degree \(D^{O(k)}\), and output bit length \(B(sD)^{O(k)}\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write the binary length of a nonzero integer \(a\) as \(1+\lceil\log_2(|a|+1)\rceil\), including one sign bit, and encode zero by one bit.  A rational number is stored as a pair \((a,b)\) with \(b>0\), \(\gcd(|a|,b)=1\), and represents \(a/b\).  Thus the hypothesis says that each stored scalar in the main computation uses \(O(\ell)\) bits.  Addition and multiplication of two \(m\)-bit integers can be carried out by the grade-school algorithms in \(O(m^2)\) bit operations.  Long division has the same polynomial cost.  The Euclidean algorithm makes at most \(O(m)\) long-division steps because each two steps reduce the larger operand by at least a factor of two, so greatest common divisors and exact divisibility checks cost \(m^{O(1)}\) bit operations.  Therefore reduced rational addition,
\[
\frac ab+\frac cd=\frac{ad+bc}{bd},
\]
reduced rational multiplication, exact rational division, comparison by the sign of \(ad-bc\), and normalization by a greatest common divisor each cost \(\ell^{O(1)}\) under the stated intermediate-length bound.  The same argument applies coefficientwise to polynomial additions, products, sign tests, and exact divisions.

Represent the replay trace as an ordered list of opcodes, operand addresses, branch signs, and produced reduced scalars.  An address and every stored scalar use \(O(\ell)\) bits by hypothesis.  If \(C\le A_{\mathrm{op}}\ell^{c_0}\) is the total number of coefficient slots, reading, verifying, and writing all trace items costs
\[
O(C)\ell^{O(1)}=A_{\mathrm{op}}\ell^{O(1)}.
\tag{14}
\]
This charges the size of the replay certificate itself: no coefficient can be output or replayed without one of the \(C\) slot visits.  It also charges the primitive-element and rational-univariate-representation coordinate lists used for a sample point.  The main execution has at most \(A_{\mathrm{op}}\) scalar operations, each of cost \(\ell^{O(1)}\), and hence obeys the same bound (14).

It remains to account for univariate algebraic postprocessing.  Let
\[
h(t)=a_mt^m+\cdots+a_0\in\mathbb Z[t],
\qquad m\le\Delta,
\qquad \max_i\operatorname{bitlen}(a_i)\le\ell.
\tag{15}
\]
Compute the primitive part of \(h\), the subresultant sequence of \(h\) and \(h'\), and \(g=\gcd(h,h')\); then \(h/g\) is square-free.  Indeed, polynomial pseudo-division replaces a pair \((u,v)\) by \((v,r)\) with \(r=c u-qv\) and nonzero integer \(c\), so the common roots are unchanged at every nonzero remainder; the last nonzero primitive remainder is therefore the greatest common divisor up to sign.  A root remains multiple in \(h/g\) exactly if its multiplicity in \(h\) exceeds its multiplicity in \(h'\) by more than one, which is impossible because differentiation lowers each positive multiplicity by exactly one.  Every subresultant coefficient is a determinant of a matrix of order at most \(2m\) whose entries are coefficients of \(h\) or \(h'\).  Expanding the determinant and using \(|a_i|<2^\ell\) gives
\[
\operatorname{bitlen}(\text{subresultant coefficient})
\le O\bigl(m(\ell+\log m)\bigr).
\tag{16}
\]
The same bound, enlarged by a polynomial factor in \(m\), applies to the exact quotients and primitive parts because the subresultant algorithm uses coefficientwise integer operations and exact divisions.  There are polynomially many coefficients and operations in \(m\).  The integer algorithms already analyzed therefore perform square-free factorization in \((\Delta\ell)^{O(1)}\) bit operations and produce coefficient lengths \(\ell\Delta^{O(1)}\).

For root isolation, suppose \(|z|>1+\max_{i<m}|a_i/a_m|\).  Dividing by \(|z|^m\) and summing the resulting geometric series shows \(|a_mz^m|>\sum_{i<m}|a_i z^i|\), so \(h(z)\ne0\).  Hence every complex root satisfies the Cauchy estimate
\[
|t|\le 1+\max_{i<m}|a_i/a_m|<2^{\ell+1}.
\tag{17}
\]
If \(h\) is square-free, its discriminant is a nonzero integer.  Writing it as the leading-coefficient power times the product of squared pairwise root differences, using that its absolute value is at least one, and bounding every factor other than a chosen separation by (17), yields
\[
\min_{\alpha\ne\beta:\ h(\alpha)=h(\beta)=0}|\alpha-\beta|
\ge 2^{-O(m^2(\ell+\log m))}.
\tag{18}
\]
After zero remainders are removed and signs are normalized, the signed subresultant remainders \(S_0=h,S_1=h',S_2,\ldots\) satisfy \(S_{i-1}=Q_iS_i-c_iS_{i+1}\) with \(c_i>0\).  Let \(V(x)\) be the number of sign variations after zero values are omitted.  Away from roots of the sequence, continuity makes \(V\) locally constant.  At a root of an interior \(S_i\), the displayed recurrence makes its two neighboring signs opposite, so deleting, inserting, or changing the middle sign leaves \(V\) unchanged.  At a simple root \(\alpha\) of \(S_0=h\), \(S_1(\alpha)=h'(\alpha)\ne0\), and the first-order expansion \(h(x)=h'(\alpha)(x-\alpha)+o(|x-\alpha|)\) shows that the first sign variation drops by exactly one as \(x\) crosses \(\alpha\) from left to right.  Therefore \(V(a)-V(b)\) equals the number of real roots in \((a,b)\) whenever the rational endpoints are not roots.  Starting with (17), dyadic bisection to the precision in (18) consequently produces disjoint rational isolating intervals after \((\Delta\ell)^{O(1)}\) sign evaluations.  Equations (16)--(18), rational clearing of denominators, and the integer-operation accounting above show that all these evaluations together use \((\Delta\ell)^{O(1)}\) bit operations and that interval endpoints have bit length \(\ell\Delta^{O(1)}\).

A real algebraic number is now represented exactly by a pair \((h,I)\), where \(h\) is square-free and \(I\) is a rational interval containing exactly one real root \(\alpha\) of \(h\).  For another integer polynomial \(b\), first compute \(c=\gcd(h,b)\).  Isolating the roots of \(c\) decides whether its unique root in \(I\) is \(\alpha\), which is exactly the case \(b(\alpha)=0\).  Otherwise isolate all roots of the square-free part of \(hb\) and refine \(I\) to an interval containing \(\alpha\) but no root of \(b\).  Continuity makes the sign of \(b\) constant on this interval, so evaluation at any rational interior point gives \(\operatorname{sign}b(\alpha)\).  To compare roots encoded by \((h,I)\) and \((\widetilde h,\widetilde I)\), isolate the real roots of the square-free part of \(h\widetilde h\); the common-factor test handles equality and the common ordered isolating intervals handle strict order.  These are again subresultant, greatest-common-divisor, rational evaluation, and isolation computations on degrees at most \(2\Delta\), so they cost \((\Delta\ell)^{O(1)}\) and satisfy the bounds following (18).

Finally, encode a real-algebraic sample point as
\[
\bigl(h,I;g_0,g_1,\ldots,g_m\bigr),
\qquad
x_i=\frac{g_i(\alpha)}{g_0(\alpha)},
\qquad g_0(\alpha)\ne0,
\tag{19}
\]
where \((h,I)\) encodes \(\alpha\) and the \(g_i\) are integer polynomials of degree below \(\deg h\).  Substituting (19) into a polynomial predicate and multiplying by a sufficiently large even power of \(g_0(\alpha)\) produces an integer polynomial \(b(\alpha)\) with the same equality status and, for an inequality, the same sign.  Its coefficient slots are charged to (14), while its degree and coefficient lengths grow only polynomially in the displayed degree and height bounds.  The sign procedure just proved therefore checks the nonzero denominator, simplex inequalities, and every polynomial equality or inequality at (19).  Decoding means retaining these exact coordinates together with the finite branch's structural tables; it does not expand an algebraic number into an infinite approximation.  Its coordinatewise work and output length were already charged in (14), and any normalization or comparison uses the polynomial postprocessing bound.  Combining (14)--(19) proves
\[
A_{\mathrm{op}}\ell^{O(1)}+(\Delta\ell)^{O(1)}.
\]
The determinant bounds show simultaneously that degrees become at most \(\Delta^{O(1)}\) and bit lengths at most \(\ell\Delta^{O(1)}\).

For the final substitution, take positive integers \(B,s,D,k\) as in the last clause of the statement.  Then \(D^{O(k)}\le(sD)^{O(k)}\), and multiplication or taking a fixed power of \((sD)^{O(k)}\) only changes the hidden absolute constant in its exponent.  Hence
\[
(sD)^{O(k)}\bigl(B(sD)^{O(k)}\bigr)^{O(1)}
+\bigl(D^{O(k)}B(sD)^{O(k)}\bigr)^{O(1)}
=B^{O(1)}(sD)^{O(k)}.
\]
Likewise \((D^{O(k)})^{O(1)}=D^{O(k)}\) and \(B(sD)^{O(k)}(D^{O(k)})^{O(1)}=B(sD)^{O(k)}\).  This proves all three asserted consequences.
\end{proof}

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

Exact push-forward to response-signature equivalence classes preserves the raw image when the induced factorized quotient family is retained, but this operation alone does not remove the latent product parameterization. The unrestricted branchwise quotient simplex is computationally smaller and is not sound in general. On the edge-latent-chain instance, the rational family \(p_\theta(w)=2^{-N}[1+\theta(-1)^{A_0\mathbin\oplus C_0}]\), \(0<\theta<1\), has every observed cell positive but violates by \(\theta/4\) the independence determinant that vanishes on the raw image. The exact alternating-block factor-incidence test decides graph by graph whether the unrestricted quotient happens to be safe, and the canonical catalogue has a derived finite quotient-size bound. Consequently the certified endpoint compiler retains raw product weights unless that test succeeds; the remaining limitation is worst-case combinatorial size, not unresolved fidelity, support, or quotient size.

\section{Honest scope}

The paper studies finite-window binary SCMs with mutually independent private and pairwise edge latents. It excludes instantaneous directed effects and directed lags beyond \(L\), but places no \(L\)-bound on bidirected lags inside the observed \(H\)-window. Ferreira--Assaad allow contemporaneous arrows and general joint exogenous laws, so the positive-lag theorem is an extension only within the present distinct scope. Its necessary-and-sufficient claim is exactly a frontier for uniform equality with the unadjusted contrast \(A_{\mathrm{FA}}\), not a necessary-and-sufficient criterion for identification from the full observed law. Indeed, with \(H=2\), \(L=\gamma=1\), summary arrows \(B\to X\to Y\), and \(B\leftrightarrow Y\), a refinement containing \(B_{-2}\to X_{-1}\to Y_0\) and \(B_{-2}\leftrightarrow Y_0\) fails the micro condition, yet the observed \(B_{-2}\) supplies an alternative adjustment route under the usual positivity needed for that conditional formula. The envelope is the smallest closed interval containing all attainable CDE values; the attainable set itself need not be connected. Its nested extrema range only over refinements whose canonical feasible set is nonempty. No stationarity or infinite-history reduction is assumed. Exact certification is worst-case singly exponential in the raw response-weight dimension and is a certificate engine, not a polynomial-time method. Its provenance split is: direct arithmetic/degree/height/sample-point bounds; paper-owned derived bit bound. The derived proof explicitly charges exact rational execution, replay-trace size, square-free factorization, Sturm--subresultant isolation, algebraic sign determination and comparison, sample-point decoding, and endpoint comparison. Empty fibers receive exact branchwise infeasibility certificates, while nonempty fibers receive attained algebraic endpoints and decoded SCMs. Every graph refinement embeds in the complete temporal-order common-latent comparator, but unrestricted comparator masses are not asserted to respect missing bidirected edges. Strict tightening is relative to positive laws with a nonempty summary-graph fiber. The quotient question is resolved: factorized push-forward is exact, a necessary-and-sufficient real-algebraic test characterizes when the unrestricted quotient simplex is also exact, a finite quotient-size bound is proved, and a full-support rational edge-latent-chain witness shows that unrestricted quotienting fails in general. Accordingly the main compiler remains unquotiented unless the exact test certifies a particular graph. When the refinement class is a singleton the construction reduces to a fixed-graph response program.
