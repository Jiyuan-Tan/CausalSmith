\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} In the unrestricted published bivariate complete-two-cycle SCM, every \(t\)-faithful stationary covariance admits full-support Hurwitz generators of both cycle-product polarities, so Gaussian equilibrium snapshots never identify that product sign. The analyst-imposed refinement instead bounds the scale-invariant raw ratios \(|B_{12}|/\operatorname{tr}(\widetilde D)\), \(|B_{21}|/\operatorname{tr}(\widetilde D)\), and \(\lVert B\rVert_F/\operatorname{tr}(\widetilde D)\), represented after the trace-one gauge by \(\eta\) and \(M\). On that refinement, a complete four-sector certificate characterizes the compatible polarity set, including every strict-diffusion and norm-attainment branch, equal-variance thresholds, an asymmetric contact, honest covariance-region inversion, uniform singleton recovery beyond root-\(n\) clearance, and a matching local Gaussian obstruction.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} The published bivariate complete-two-cycle class leaves a one-dimensional circulation fiber after covariance is fixed, but the consequence for the joint edge-product sign was not separated from individual-edge ambiguity. The first result closes that global question negatively: every \(t\)-faithful covariance admits both product polarities. Existing work also does not give the exact four-sector, open-versus-closed certificate obtained after imposing scale-invariant edge margins and a drift-norm cap, nor its contact-specific inference theory.

\paragraph{Niche.} The exactly bivariate feedback cycle is the smallest graph where the unrestricted published SCM is globally product-sign nonidentifying yet an analyst's scientifically bounded refinement can identify a joint causal sign while neither individual edge sign is identified. Common rescaling of the raw generator and diffusion changes time units but leaves the stationary covariance and product polarity fixed, so the restrictions are formulated as raw trace ratios and computed in their unique trace-one representative.

\paragraph{Fill.} The paper first constructs same-sign and opposite-sign full-support witnesses throughout the unrestricted \(t\)-faithful published class, proving that its compatible polarity set is always \(\{-1,+1\}\). It then derives the affine trace-normalized fiber and a complete finite semialgebraic certificate for the bounded-margin refinement, proves all strict-attainment branches, gives analytic equal-variance and asymmetric-contact reductions, exhibits an open \(t\)-faithful joint-only region, and propagates the population set through an honest Gaussian covariance confidence region with locally matching root-\(n\) limits.

\section{Related work}

Van Seeventer and Salehkaleybar (2026) formulate the published graphical continuous-Lyapunov class using a Hurwitz generator, strictly positive diagonal diffusion, \(t\)-faithfulness, and structural minimality, and their Theorem 4.2 and Remark 5.1 analyze one-edge sign ambiguity by linear feasibility. The present first theorem stays in that unrestricted class and proves a global negative result for the two-edge product: every \(t\)-faithful bivariate complete-cycle covariance admits both polarities. The later positive identification regions require the separately declared scale-invariant edge margins and drift-norm cap. Dettling, Homs, Améndola, Drton, and Hansen (2023) identify the directed two-cycle as the obstruction to global drift recovery with diagonal volatility; their Lyapunov rank analysis motivates, but does not provide, the polarity witnesses or strict-boundary certificate here. Hansen (2025) gives a trek expansion and acyclic variance inequalities. Dettling, Drton, and Kolar (2024) study penalized graph selection rather than inference on a known graph's compatible sign quotient. Chernozhukov, Hong, and Tamer (2007) inform confidence-region inversion; Fang and Santos (2019) and Kaji (2021) inform nonsmooth and weak-identification comparisons. Recke and Hansen (2026) use non-Gaussian higher cumulants to recover information that is absent from the Gaussian snapshot experiment studied here.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:covariance-conditioning]\label{ass:covariance-conditioning}
The stationary covariance satisfies \(\kappa I_2\preceq\Sigma\preceq\kappa^{-1}I_2\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This is this proposal's compact model-class restriction for uniform bounds, not a condition attributed to Dettling, Drton, and Kolar. It holds on any fixed compact subset of the positive-definite covariance cone, including standardized Gaussian equilibrium families whose covariance eigenvalues remain in a prespecified interval.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:common-time-scale-gauge]\label{ass:common-time-scale-gauge}
The selected common-time-scale representative satisfies \(\operatorname{tr}(D)=1\) and \(D(d)=\operatorname{diag}(d,1-d)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This proposal derives the normalization from the common time-scale orbit. If \(\widetilde A\Sigma+\Sigma\widetilde A^{\top}=\widetilde D\) and \(c=1/\operatorname{tr}(\widetilde D)\), then \((c\widetilde A)\Sigma+\Sigma(c\widetilde A)^{\top}=c\widetilde D\) and \(\operatorname{tr}(c\widetilde D)=1\). Equivalently, in generator-sign form the direct algebra is \((-\widetilde A)\Sigma+\Sigma(-\widetilde A)^{\top}+\widetilde D=0\Rightarrow(-c\widetilde A)\Sigma+\Sigma(-c\widetilde A)^{\top}+c\widetilde D=0\). The constants \(\eta\) and \(M\) are bounds in the normalized time unit selected by this gauge.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:strict-diffusion]\label{ass:strict-diffusion}
The diffusion allocation satisfies \(0<d<1\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (strictly positive diagonal volatility, \cite{vanSeeventerSalehkaleybar2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:lyapunov-stationarity]\label{ass:lyapunov-stationarity}
The mean-reversion matrix and covariance obey \(A\Sigma+\Sigma A^{\top}=D\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (continuous Lyapunov stationarity equation under the mean-reversion sign convention, \cite{DettlingHomsAmendolaDrtonHansen2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:edge12-margin]\label{ass:edge12-margin}
In the normalized time unit selected by \(\operatorname{tr}(D)=1\), the first directed coefficient satisfies \(|A_{12}|\ge\eta\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (beta-min separation for the first directed edge, \cite{DettlingDrtonKolar2024}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:edge21-margin]\label{ass:edge21-margin}
In the normalized time unit selected by \(\operatorname{tr}(D)=1\), the second directed coefficient satisfies \(|A_{21}|\ge\eta\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (beta-min separation for the second directed edge, \cite{DettlingDrtonKolar2024}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:drift-bound]\label{ass:drift-bound}
In the normalized time unit selected by \(\operatorname{tr}(D)=1\), the drift satisfies \(\lVert A\rVert_F\le M\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (bounded finite-dimensional parameter space, \cite{ChernozhukovHongTamer2007}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:model-compatibility]\label{ass:model-compatibility}
The covariance admits at least one legal parameter pair, \(\mathcal F(\Sigma)\ne\varnothing\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (observed law restricted to the possible set, \cite{vanSeeventerSalehkaleybar2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:gaussian-snapshots]\label{ass:gaussian-snapshots}
Conditional on \(\Sigma\), the observations \(X_1,\ldots,X_n\) are independent with common law \(\mathcal N(0,\Sigma)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (independent equilibrium snapshots from a Gaussian OU process, \cite{VarandoHansen2020}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{definition}[def:legal-fiber]\label{def:legal-fiber}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{scale-\allowbreak{}invariant bounded-\allowbreak{}margin legal drift-\allowbreak{}diffusion fiber}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\mathcal F(\Sigma)\) is the set
\[
\left\{(A,D):A\Sigma+\Sigma A^{\top}=D,\quad D=\operatorname{diag}(d,1-d),\quad 0<d<1,\quad |A_{12}|\ge\eta,\quad |A_{21}|\ge\eta,\quad \lVert A\rVert_F\le M\right\}.

Its normalized representative belongs to \(\mathcal F(\Sigma)\) exactly when \(\widetilde D\) is strictly positive diagonal and
\[
\frac{|B_{12}|}{\operatorname{tr}(\widetilde D)}\ge\eta,\qquad
\frac{|B_{21}|}{\operatorname{tr}(\widetilde D)}\ge\eta,\qquad
\frac{\lVert B\rVert_F}{\operatorname{tr}(\widetilde D)}\le M.
\]
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Member properties.} \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}common-\allowbreak{}time-\allowbreak{}scale-\allowbreak{}gauge}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}strict-\allowbreak{}diffusion}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}lyapunov-\allowbreak{}stationarity}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}edge12-\allowbreak{}margin}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}edge21-\allowbreak{}margin}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}drift-\allowbreak{}bound}.

\begin{lemma}[lem:time-scale-gauge]\label{lem:time-scale-gauge}
Suppose \(\widetilde D\) is diagonal and strictly positive and \(\widetilde A\Sigma+\Sigma\widetilde A^{\top}=\widetilde D\). With \(\lambda=\operatorname{tr}(\widetilde D)\), the common rescaling \(A=\widetilde A/\lambda\) and \(D=\widetilde D/\lambda\) preserves \(\Sigma\), satisfies \(A\Sigma+\Sigma A^{\top}=D\) and \(\operatorname{tr}(D)=1\), and preserves \(\operatorname{sign}(A_{12}A_{21})\). It is the unique representative of the positive common-scaling class with trace-one diffusion.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Because \(\widetilde D\) is diagonal and strictly positive, \(\lambda=\operatorname{tr}(\widetilde D)>0\).  Dividing the assumed Lyapunov equation by \(\lambda\) gives
\[
 (\widetilde A/\lambda)\Sigma+\Sigma(\widetilde A/\lambda)^{\top}
 =\widetilde D/\lambda.
\]
Thus the displayed \(A\) and \(D\) preserve \(\Sigma\), and
\[
 \operatorname{tr}(D)=\lambda^{-1}\operatorname{tr}(\widetilde D)=1.
\]
Moreover \(A_{12}A_{21}=\lambda^{-2}\widetilde A_{12}\widetilde A_{21}\); since \(\lambda^{-2}>0\), its sign is unchanged.  A positive common rescaling has the form \((c\widetilde A,c\widetilde D)\), and its diffusion trace is \(c\lambda\).  The equation \(c\lambda=1\) has the unique solution \(c=\lambda^{-1}\).  This proves uniqueness of the trace-one representative.  In particular, raw edge and norm bounds transform as \(|A_{ij}|=|\widetilde A_{ij}|/\lambda\) and \(\lVert A\rVert_F=\lVert\widetilde A\rVert_F/\lambda\), which explains why \(\eta\) and \(M\) are stated in the selected time unit.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The lemma separates an observationally irrelevant common time-unit choice from the substantive edge-margin and norm restrictions. \textit{Gap.} The trace-one restriction is a gauge choice for this bounded-fiber analysis, not a modeling condition attributed to the one-edge sign-identification literature. \textit{Consumer.} Every sector, threshold, contact, and inference result can be interpreted for an arbitrary strictly positive diagonal diffusion after converting \(\eta\) and \(M\) to the normalized time unit.

\begin{lemma}[lem:affine-fiber]\label{lem:affine-fiber}
Every solution of \(A\Sigma+\Sigma A^{\top}=D(d)\) has the unique representation \[A(d,q;\Sigma)=\frac1\Delta\begin{pmatrix}du/2+qt&-dt/2-qs\\-(1-d)t/2+qu&(1-d)s/2-qt\end{pmatrix}.\] Conversely, every \((d,q)\in(0,1)\times\mathbb R\) in this representation solves the Lyapunov equation and has eigenvalues with strictly positive real parts, so \(-A\) is Hurwitz.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The trace-one coordinate \(D(d)=\operatorname{diag}(d,1-d)\) is the representative supplied by the time-scale-gauge lemma. For a solution of the Lyapunov equation, set
\[
R=A\Sigma-D/2.
\]
By the Lyapunov-stationarity assumption,
\[
R+R^{\top}=A\Sigma+\Sigma A^{\top}-D=0.
\]
Every real skew-symmetric \(2\times2\) matrix is uniquely \(-Q(q)\) for one \(q\in\mathbb R\). Since \(\Sigma\) is invertible, this gives the unique representation
\[
A=(D/2-Q(q))\Sigma^{-1}.
\]
Using
\[
\Sigma^{-1}=\frac1\Delta\begin{pmatrix}u&-t\\-t&s\end{pmatrix}
\quad\text{and}\quad
D=\operatorname{diag}(d,1-d),
\]
matrix multiplication yields
\[
A(d,q;\Sigma)=\frac1\Delta
\begin{pmatrix}
du/2+qt&-dt/2-qs\\
-(1-d)t/2+qu&(1-d)s/2-qt
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Conversely, this formula gives \(A\Sigma=D/2-Q(q)\), and hence
\[
A\Sigma+\Sigma A^{\top}=D/2-Q(q)+D/2+Q(q)=D.
\]

It remains to derive stability. By the strict-diffusion assumption, \(0<d<1\), so \(D\succ0\). Define
\[
C=\Sigma^{-1/2}A\Sigma^{1/2}.
\]
The matrix \(C\) is similar to \(A\), and multiplying the Lyapunov identity on both sides by \(\Sigma^{-1/2}\) gives
\[
C+C^{\top}=\Sigma^{-1/2}D\Sigma^{-1/2}\succ0.
\]
If \(Cv=\mu v\) for a nonzero complex vector \(v\), then
\[
2\operatorname{Re}(\mu)\lVert v\rVert_2^2
=v^*(C+C^{\top})v>0.
\]
Thus every eigenvalue of \(C\), and hence of \(A\), has strictly positive real part. Every eigenvalue of \(-A\) therefore has strictly negative real part, so \(-A\) is Hurwitz.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The affine fiber supplies a common two-coordinate representation for both the unrestricted published-class witnesses and the bounded-margin sector programs. \textit{Gap.} Dettling, Homs, Améndola, Drton, and Hansen identify the two-cycle rank defect but do not use this trace-normalized unknown-diffusion parametrization to classify product polarity. \textit{Consumer.} The global nonidentification theorem, all four finite sector programs, and every explicit contact witness use this coordinate system.

\begin{theorem}[thm:sector-characterization]\label{thm:sector-characterization}
For each \(\sigma\), let \(z_{\sigma}\) be the unique minimizer of \(f\) on nonempty \(H_{\sigma}\), with \(v_{\sigma}=f(z_{\sigma})\). The sector contains a legal model if and only if \(H_{\sigma}\ne\varnothing\), \(J_{\sigma}\) holds, and \[v_{\sigma}<M^2\quad\text{or}\quad\bigl(v_{\sigma}=M^2\ \text{and}\ 0<(z_{\sigma})_d<1\bigr).\] Moreover, for \(\omega\in\{-1,+1\}\), \(H_{(\omega,\omega)}\ne\varnothing\) if and only if \(-\omega t\max(s,u)\ge2\eta\Delta(s+u)\), and \(J_{(\omega,\omega)}\) holds if and only if the inequality is strict or \(s=u\) at equality; both opposite-sign sectors always satisfy \(J_{\sigma}\). Hence \(+1\in\mathcal P(\Sigma)\) exactly when one same-sign sector passes the certificate, and \(-1\in\mathcal P(\Sigma)\) exactly when one opposite-sign sector passes it.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The certificate-completeness theorem proves that, whenever \(H_{\sigma}\ne\varnothing\), the retained equality-active family contains the unique minimizer \(z_{\sigma}\) of the strictly convex quadratic \(f\) over \(H_{\sigma}\). It also proves the exact strict-diffusion and norm-attainment criterion
\[
H_{\sigma}\ne\varnothing,\qquad
J_{\sigma},\qquad
v_{\sigma}<M^2
\quad\text{or}\quad
\left(v_{\sigma}=M^2\ \text{and}\ 0<(z_{\sigma})_d<1\right). \tag{1}
\]
The assumptions on the two edge margins and the drift norm are precisely the inequalities encoded in that program, while the legal-fiber definition identifies strict diffusion with \(0<d<1\). Thus (1) proves the first assertion. It remains to derive the advertised scalar rules for \(J_{\sigma}\) and then to aggregate the four sectors by product sign.

Write \(b=A_{12}\) and \(c=A_{21}\). The affine-fiber lemma gives
\[
b=\frac{-dt/2-qs}{\Delta},
\qquad
c=\frac{-(1-d)t/2+qu}{\Delta}.
\]
Consequently the circulation coordinate cancels from the weighted sum:
\[
ub+sc
=-\frac{t\{s+(u-s)d\}}{2\Delta}. \tag{2}
\]
The four signed edge-margin inequalities are, respectively,
\[
\begin{array}{lll}
b\ge\eta &\Longleftrightarrow& q\le(-\eta\Delta-dt/2)/s,\\
b\le-\eta&\Longleftrightarrow& q\ge(\eta\Delta-dt/2)/s,\\
c\ge\eta &\Longleftrightarrow& q\ge(\eta\Delta+(1-d)t/2)/u,\\
c\le-\eta&\Longleftrightarrow& q\le(-\eta\Delta+(1-d)t/2)/u.
\end{array} \tag{3}
\]
Every division in (3) is legitimate because positive definiteness of \(\Sigma\) gives \(s>0\), \(u>0\), and \(\Delta>0\).

Fix \(d\in[0,1]\). For the same-sign sector \((\omega,\omega)\), comparison of the relevant lower and upper endpoints in (3) shows that a feasible \(q\) exists if and only if
\[
-\omega t\{s+(u-s)d\}\ge2\eta\Delta(s+u). \tag{4}
\]
Indeed, for \(\omega=+1\), (4) is obtained by requiring the lower bound for \(c\ge\eta\) not to exceed the upper bound for \(b\ge\eta\); for \(\omega=-1\), it is obtained by requiring the lower bound for \(b\le-\eta\) not to exceed the upper bound for \(c\le-\eta\). Thus no implication is lost in passing from (3) to (4).

The bracket in (4) is the affine function \(s(1-d)+ud\), which is positive on \([0,1]\) and whose maximum is \(\max(s,u)\). Since the right-hand side of (4) is strictly positive, feasibility also forces \(-\omega t>0\). Maximizing the left-hand side over the closed interval therefore gives
\[
H_{(\omega,\omega)}\ne\varnothing
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
-\omega t\max(s,u)\ge2\eta\Delta(s+u). \tag{5}
\]
If the inequality in (5) is strict, continuity of the affine function in (4) gives a satisfying \(d\in(0,1)\). If equality holds and \(s=u\), the bracket is constant, so every \(d\in(0,1)\) satisfies (4) at equality. If equality holds and \(s\ne u\), the bracket reaches its maximum at exactly one of \(d=0\) and \(d=1\), so no interior \(d\) is feasible. This proves the complete stated rule for \(J_{(\omega,\omega)}\), including its open-versus-closed equality branch.

For the opposite-sign sector \((+1,-1)\), (3) imposes two upper bounds on \(q\). For any fixed \(d\in(0,1)\), a real \(q\) smaller than both bounds satisfies the two margins. For \((-1,+1)\), (3) imposes two lower bounds, and a \(q\) larger than both satisfies them. Hence both opposite-sign sectors always satisfy \(J_{\sigma}\).

Finally, the edge-margin assumptions exclude zero off-diagonal entries. A sector with equal edge signs has \(\operatorname{sign}(A_{12}A_{21})=+1\), and a sector with opposite edge signs has product sign \(-1\). By the polarity-set definition, taking the union over the legal sectors and applying (1) proves the two final equivalences.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem is the exact necessary-and-sufficient kernel for the bounded-margin refinement and records closure-only versus legally attained branches. \textit{Gap.} The unrestricted class is globally nonidentifying, while existing individual-edge feasibility results do not characterize the joint product under simultaneous scale-invariant magnitude and norm restrictions. \textit{Consumer.} A bivariate feedback analysis with declared scientific bounds can compute every compatible loop polarity from four finite certificates.

\begin{theorem}[thm:equal-variance]\label{thm:equal-variance}
Suppose \(\Sigma=\Sigma_r\) and \(|r|\le1-\kappa\). Then a positive-polarity legal model exists if and only if \(|r|\ge4\eta(1-r^2)\) and \(M^2\ge C_r\). A negative-polarity legal model exists if and only if \(M^2>V_-(r)\), or \(M^2=V_-(r)\) and \(|r|L_r<(1-r^2)/4\). At \(r=1/2\), \(\eta=3/5\), and \(M^2=67/45\), the negative-sector closed infimum is unattained under \(0<d<1\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(\Delta=1-r^2>0\), set \(x=d-1/2\in(-1/2,1/2)\), and set \(y=q+rx/2\).  Substitution in the affine-fiber formula gives
\[
 A_{12}=-\frac{r/4+y}{\Delta},\qquad
 A_{21}=-\frac{r/4-y}{\Delta},
\]
and
\[
 A_{11}=\frac1{4\Delta}+\frac x2+\frac{ry}{\Delta},\qquad
 A_{22}=\frac1{4\Delta}-\frac x2-\frac{ry}{\Delta}.
\]
Squaring and adding yields the exact identity
\[
 f=C_r+\frac{2y^2}{\Delta^2}
 +2\left(\frac x2+\frac{ry}{\Delta}\right)^2,\qquad
 C_r=\frac{1+r^2}{8\Delta^2}. \tag{5}
\]

The two off-diagonal entries have the same sign and magnitudes at least \(\eta\) exactly when
\[
 |y|\le |r|/4-\eta\Delta.
\]
This interval is nonempty exactly when \(|r|\ge4\eta\Delta\).  When it is nonempty, \(x=y=0\) is feasible, has \(d=1/2\), and attains the lower bound \(C_r\) in (5).  Therefore a positive-polarity legal model exists exactly when \(|r|\ge4\eta(1-r^2)\) and \(M^2\ge C_r\).  In particular equality in the edge threshold is attained with strict diffusion, which is the required attained boundary check.

The entries have opposite signs and both magnitudes at least \(\eta\) exactly when
\[
 |y|\ge L_r=\eta\Delta+|r|/4. \tag{6}
\]
For fixed \(y\), minimization of the last square in (5) over the closed interval \(-1/2\le x\le1/2\) gives
\[
 \inf_x f
 =\psi(y):=C_r+\frac{2y^2}{\Delta^2}
 +2\max\left\{\frac{|ry|}{\Delta}-\frac14,0\right\}^2. \tag{7}
\]
As a function of \(|y|>0\), the first nonconstant term in (7) is strictly increasing and the last is nondecreasing.  Hence the closed-sector minimum under (6) is \(\psi(L_r)=V_-(r)\).  Its minimizing \(x\) is the projection of \(-2ry/\Delta\) onto \([-1/2,1/2]\), and it is interior exactly when
\[
 |r|L_r<\Delta/4. \tag{8}
\]
The sector-characterization theorem now yields feasibility when \(M^2>V_-(r)\), and at equality precisely under (8).  If the inequality in the norm cap is strict, an interior \(x\) sufficiently close to the closed minimizer keeps (6) and makes the norm smaller than \(M\), including when the infimum itself occurs at an endpoint.

The eigenvalues of \(\Sigma_r\) are \(1\pm|r|\).  The lower covariance bound is therefore \(|r|\le1-\kappa\); this also implies the upper bound because \(2-\kappa\le\kappa^{-1}\), equivalently \((1-\kappa)^2\ge0\).

For the stated boundary example, \(r=1/2\) gives \(\Delta=3/4\), and \(\eta=3/5\) gives
\[
 L_r=\frac35\frac34+\frac18=\frac{23}{40},\qquad
 \frac{|r|L_r}{\Delta}=\frac{23}{60}>\frac14.
\]
Moreover
\[
 V_-(r)=\frac5{18}+\frac{529}{450}+\frac8{225}=\frac{67}{45}.
\]
Thus every closed minimizer has \(d=0\) or \(d=1\); the opposite-sign sector has \(J_{\sigma}=\mathrm{true}\), but its norm infimum at \(M^2=67/45\) is unattained under \(0<d<1\).  This certifies the asserted third boundary branch.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The slice yields analytic thresholds and separates attained margin equality from unattained norm equality. \textit{Gap.} Hansen2025Trek derives variance inequalities for acyclic models; no cited result gives cyclic product-sign thresholds or their strict-attainment branch. \textit{Consumer.} The formulas give exact calibration and regression tests for implementations of the general certificate.

\begin{theorem}[thm:asymmetric-contact]\label{thm:asymmetric-contact}

\begin{proof}
Write \(s_0=11/10\), \(u_0=9/10\), and \(p(t)=100t^2+275t-99\).  Since
\[

 \quad\text{and}\quad

direct substitution gives \(p(t_0)=0\).  The exact comparisons

\]
give

\]
These bounds imply \((1/2)I_2\prec\Sigma_0\prec2I_2\): for example
\[

\]
and
\[
 (2-s_0)(2-u_0)-t_0^2>\frac9{10}\frac{11}{10}-\left(\frac{3223}{10000}\right)^2>0.
\]

On the open region \(s>u\), \(t>0\), the \((+,+)\) sector is empty by the scalar sector rule.  For the \((-,-)\) sector, identity (1) implies that a closed feasible point must satisfy

\]
Conversely, (10) is exactly the compatibility condition between the two bounds on \(q\).  Thus strict diffusion is impossible when \(g\le0\).  At \(g=0\), (10) forces \(d=0\); equality in the weighted sum in (1), together with \(A_{12},A_{21}\le-\eta\), then forces

\]
This proves uniqueness of the closed same-sign witness and shows \(J_{(-1,-1)}=\mathrm{false}\) at the contact.

For \(g>0\), define
\[
 d=\frac{g}{2(s-u)t},\qquad
 q=\frac{\eta\Delta-dt/2}{s}. \tag{11}
\]
Then direct substitution gives
\[
 A_{12}=-\eta,\qquad
 A_{21}=-\eta-\frac{g}{4s\Delta}< -\eta. \tag{12}
\]
As \(\Sigma\to\Sigma_0\) from \(g>0\), (11) converges to the unique closed point \((d,q)=(0,\eta\Delta/s)\).  The limiting drift has
\[
 |A_{11}|=\eta t/s<3/100,\qquad
 |A_{22}|=s/(2\Delta)-\eta t/s<3/5,
\]
both displayed diagonal expressions are positive, and (9) gives the sharper rational bounds
\[
 |A_{11}|<\frac{293}{10000}<\frac3{100},\qquad
 |A_{22}|<\frac{262022330129}{443061355000}<\frac35.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \lVert A\rVert_F^2<(3/100)^2+2(1/10)^2+(3/5)^2
 =3809/10000<1. \tag{13}
\]

There is also a persistent opposite-sign witness: take \(d=1/2\), \(q=1/5\).  At \(\Sigma_0\), (9) and the affine formula give, by bounding each positive numerator and \(\Delta=99/100-t_0^2\) at the appropriate endpoint,
\[
 |A_{11}|<\frac{28946000}{88612271}<\frac{33}{100},
 \qquad
 \frac{33}{100}<\frac{7513750}{22154679}<|A_{12}|<
 \frac{2732500}{8055661}<\frac{34}{100},
\]
\[
 \frac{11}{100}<\frac{2485625}{22154679}<|A_{21}|<
 \frac{9945000}{88612271}<\frac{113}{1000},
 \qquad
 |A_{22}|<\frac{21056000}{88612271}<\frac{238}{1000}.
\]
In particular both margins are strict and
\[
 \lVert A\rVert_F^2
 <(33/100)^2+(34/100)^2+(113/1000)^2+(238/1000)^2
 =293913/10^6<1. \tag{14}
\]
All inequalities in (9), (13), and (14) are rational after clearing the positive denominators \(\Delta\); hence no decimal approximation is being used as a premise.

By continuity, shrink an open neighborhood \(U\) of \(\Sigma_0\) so that throughout \(U\): the covariance bounds are strict; \(s>u\) and \(t>0\); the fixed opposite-sign witness retains strict margins and norm below one; the limiting family (11)--(12), whenever \(g>0\), has \(0<d<1\) and norm below one.  The fixed witness proves \(U\subseteq\mathcal K\) and \(-1\in\mathcal P(\Sigma)\).  Equations (10)--(12) show that \(+1\) is absent for \(g\le0\) and present for \(g>0\), proving the displayed polarity sets.  Finally,
\[
 \frac{\partial g}{\partial t}=s+4\eta t(s+u)>0
\]
on \(U\), so the contact is regular and all remaining assertions follow.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem exposes a smooth statistical boundary generated by strict diffusion positivity rather than by a binding drift norm. \textit{Gap.} The cited Lyapunov identification papers analyze algebraic or generic failures and do not identify an explicit zero-diffusion polarity contact with open adjacent regimes. \textit{Consumer.} The contact supplies a transparent stress test for inference and a local coordinate for sharp recovery theory.

\begin{theorem}[thm:finite-sample-coverage]\label{thm:finite-sample-coverage}
Assume \(\mathcal K\ne\varnothing\). The random set \(\Gamma_n\) is measurable and nonempty, and for every \(n\ge1\), \[\inf_{\Sigma\in\mathcal K}\Pr_{\Sigma}\{\mathcal P(\Sigma)\subseteq\Gamma_n\}\ge1-\alpha.\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First establish measurability without closing the strict-diffusion model.  For \(a\in\{-1,+1\}\) and integers \(m\ge3\), let \(K_{a,m}\) be the projection onto \(\Theta\) of the parameter triples satisfying the covariance bounds, one of the finitely many sectors of product sign \(a\), the two closed edge-margin inequalities, the norm cap, and \(1/m\le d\le1-1/m\).  These triples form a compact set.  Indeed \(\Theta\) and \(d\) range over compact sets, and
\[
 Q=D/2-A\Theta,\qquad
 \lVert Q\rVert_F\le\frac12+M\kappa^{-1}
\]
bounds \(q\); all remaining constraints are closed and the affine formula is continuous under \(\Theta\succeq\kappa I_2\).  Hence every \(K_{a,m}\) is compact.  Strict diffusion and the nonzero margins imply
\[
 K_a=\bigcup_{m\ge3}K_{a,m},\qquad
 \mathcal K=K_{-1}\cup K_{+1}. \tag{15}
\]
Consequently the events that \(B_n\) is nonempty and that its union contains a fixed sign are countable unions of closed distance-sublevel events, hence Borel.

Because \(\mathcal K\) is a subset of a finite-dimensional Euclidean space, it has a countable dense sequence.  For nonempty \(\mathcal K\), the function \(\rho(S)=\inf_{\Theta\in\mathcal K}\lVert\Theta-S\rVert_F\) is one-Lipschitz.  Density implies that for every \(S\) at least one index satisfies
\[
 \lVert\Theta_j-S\rVert_F<\rho(S)+1/n.
\]
The event \(j_n(S)=j\) is the intersection of this strict Borel inequality with the failures of the finitely many preceding inequalities.  Thus \(j_n\) is Borel.  Together with (15), this proves measurability of membership of either sign in \(\Gamma_n\), which is measurability of this finite-valued random set.  It is nonempty because every member of \(\mathcal K\) has a nonempty polarity set; this applies either to a member of nonempty \(B_n\) or to the fallback \(\Theta_{j_n(S_n)}\).

It remains to prove coverage.  For one centered Gaussian observation, differentiating its moment generating function \(\exp(v^{\top}\Sigma v/2)\) four times at zero gives
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(X_jX_k,X_\ell X_m)
 =\Sigma_{j\ell}\Sigma_{km}+\Sigma_{jm}\Sigma_{k\ell}.
\]
Independence therefore gives
\[
 \mathbb E_{\Sigma}\lVert S_n-\Sigma\rVert_F^2
 =\frac{(\operatorname{tr}\Sigma)^2+\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma^2)}{n}
 \le\frac{4\kappa^{-2}+2\kappa^{-2}}n=\frac Vn. \tag{16}
\]
For \(Y=\lVert S_n-\Sigma\rVert_F^2\ge0\), the elementary inequality \(\mathbb E Y\ge r_n^2\Pr(Y>r_n^2)\), (16), and \(r_n^2=V/(\alpha n)\) yield
\[
 \Pr_{\Sigma}\{\lVert S_n-\Sigma\rVert_F>r_n\}\le\alpha. \tag{17}
\]
On the complementary event, the true \(\Sigma\in\mathcal K\) belongs to \(B_n\), so the nonempty-ball branch is used and \(\mathcal P(\Sigma)\subseteq\Gamma_n\).  The bound (17) is uniform over \(\Sigma\in\mathcal K\), proving the result for every \(n\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The result gives honest finite-sample coverage of the entire compatible sign set, including nonclosed model branches. \textit{Gap.} ChernozhukovHongTamer2007 provides general set-inversion theory, while vanSeeventerSalehkaleybar2026 has no sampling result; the measurable empty-intersection fallback is specific to this strict OU model. \textit{Consumer.} A reported bivariate feedback sign set has a finite-sample frequentist guarantee rather than a brittle plug-in label.

\begin{theorem}[thm:singleton-recovery]\label{thm:singleton-recovery}
For every sequence \(a_n\to\infty\), uniformly over \(\Sigma\in\mathcal K\) with \(\mathcal P(\Sigma)=\{a\}\) and \(\delta_a(\Sigma)\ge a_n/\sqrt n\), \(\Pr_{\Sigma}\{\Gamma_n=\{a\}\}\to1\). More precisely, when \(\delta_a(\Sigma)>2r_n\) and \(\delta_a(\Sigma)>2/n\), the failure probability is at most \(16V/(n\delta_a(\Sigma)^2)\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(\Sigma\in\mathcal K\) with \(\mathcal P(\Sigma)=\{a\}\), write \(\delta=\delta_a(\Sigma)\), and put \(e=\lVert S_n-\Sigma\rVert_F\).  Suppose first that \(0<\delta<\infty\), \(\delta>2r_n\), \(\delta>2/n\), and \(e<\delta/4\).  If \(B_n\ne\varnothing\), then every \(\Theta\in B_n\) satisfies
\[
 \lVert\Theta-\Sigma\rVert_F
 \le\lVert\Theta-S_n\rVert_F+e
 \le r_n+e<3\delta/4<\delta. \tag{18}
\]
Thus no such \(\Theta\) belongs to \(K_{-a}\).  If \(B_n=\varnothing\), the fallback and \(\rho(S_n)\le e\) give
\[
 \lVert\Theta_{j_n(S_n)}-\Sigma\rVert_F
 <\rho(S_n)+1/n+e\le2e+1/n<\delta. \tag{19}
\]
Again the selected model is not in \(K_{-a}\).  Every selected covariance is in \(\mathcal K\), so its polarity set is nonempty; absence of \(-a\) makes it exactly \(\{a\}\).  Equations (18)--(19) therefore imply \(\Gamma_n=\{a\}\).

By (16),
\[
 \Pr_{\Sigma}\{e\ge\delta/4\}
 \le\frac{16\mathbb E_{\Sigma}e^2}{\delta^2}
 \le\frac{16V}{n\delta^2}. \tag{20}
\]
This is the stated finite-sample failure bound.  If \(\delta=\infty\), then \(K_{-a}=\varnothing\); every covariance selected in either branch has polarity set \(\{a\}\), so recovery is certain.

Finally, if \(\delta\ge a_n/\sqrt n\) and \(a_n\to\infty\), then uniformly over the stated class the two strict conditions on \(\delta\) hold for all large \(n\), because \(a_n>2R\) and \(a_n\sqrt n>2\).  The right side of (20) is at most \(16V/a_n^2\to0\).  This proves uniform singleton recovery.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Coverage becomes informative away from the opposing-sign frontier at the regular covariance-estimation scale. \textit{Gap.} No cited OU paper proves uniform exact-set recovery; generic set inversion does not resolve the nearest-model branch when \(B_n\) is empty. \textit{Consumer.} Analysts obtain a transparent clearance condition under which the honest set collapses to the identified polarity.

\begin{theorem}[thm:local-contact-geometry]\label{thm:local-contact-geometry}
For every compact \(C\subseteq\{\Sigma\in U:g(\Sigma)=0\}\), there are a neighborhood \(W\) of \(C\) with \(\overline W\subset U\) and constants \(0<c<C_1<\infty\) such that, for \(\Sigma\in W\) with \(g(\Sigma)\le0\), \[\operatorname{dist}(\Sigma,K_{+1})=\operatorname{dist}(\Sigma,\{\Theta\in U:g(\Theta)=0\}),\qquad c|g(\Sigma)|\le\operatorname{dist}(\Sigma,K_{+1})\le C_1|g(\Sigma)|.\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The assertion is trivial for \(C=\varnothing\), so suppose \(C\ne\varnothing\).  By the asymmetric-contact theorem, \(g_t>0\) on \(U\), and
\[
 K_{+1}\cap U=\{\Theta\in U:g(\Theta)>0\}. \tag{21}
\]
Compactness of \(C\) and continuity permit nested open neighborhoods \(N_0\) and \(N\), with \(C\subset N_0\), \(\overline{N_0}\subset N\), and \(\overline N\subset U\), and constants \(0<m\le L<\infty\) such that
\[
 g_t\ge m\quad\text{and}\quad \lVert\nabla g\rVert_F\le L
 \qquad\text{on }\overline N. \tag{22}
\]
Here the gradient and norm use the Frobenius coordinates on symmetric matrices, so changing only the covariance entry \(t\) by \(e\) changes the matrix by Frobenius norm \(\sqrt2|e|\).

For \(\Sigma=(s,t,u)\in N_0\) sufficiently close to \(C\), monotonicity in \(t\) and (22) give a unique nearby \(\bar t\) with \(g(s,\bar t,u)=0\), and the entire vertical segment between \(t\) and \(\bar t\) lies in \(N\).  The mean-value identity gives
\[
 |g(\Sigma)|=g_t(s,\xi,u)|t-\bar t|\ge m|t-\bar t|,
\]
and therefore
\[
 \operatorname{dist}(\Sigma,\{\Theta\in U:g(\Theta)=0\})
 \le\sqrt2|t-\bar t|\le\frac{\sqrt2}{m}|g(\Sigma)|. \tag{23}
\]
Conversely, for every zero \(\Theta\in N_0\) sufficiently close to \(\Sigma\), the segment joining \(\Sigma\) and \(\Theta\) lies in \(N\), so the segment mean-value bound from (22) gives \(|g(\Sigma)|\le L\lVert\Sigma-\Theta\rVert_F\).  Zeros outside \(N_0\) have a positive buffer from a sufficiently small neighborhood of \(C\), because \(\operatorname{dist}(C,N_0^c)>0\).  Shrinking that neighborhood once more and using (23) therefore gives
\[
 L^{-1}|g(\Sigma)|
 \le\operatorname{dist}(\Sigma,\{\Theta\in U:g(\Theta)=0\})
 \le\frac{\sqrt2}{m}|g(\Sigma)|. \tag{24}
\]

It remains to compare with all of \(K_{+1}\).  Let \(b=\operatorname{dist}(C,U^c)>0\).  Choose \(W\) so small that \(\overline W\subset N_0\), every point of \(W\) is within \(b/4\) of \(C\), and the zero supplied in (23) is within \(b/4\).  Points of \(K_{+1}\setminus U\) are then farther from \(\Sigma\in W\) than this local zero.  By (21), every path inside the resulting \(b/2\)-ball from a point with \(g\le0\) to a point of \(K_{+1}\cap U\) crosses \(g=0\), so its length is at least the distance to the zero set.  Conversely, every local zero is approached by points with \(g>0\), because \(g_t>0\), and those points belong to \(K_{+1}\) by (21).  Taking infima proves
\[
 \operatorname{dist}(\Sigma,K_{+1})
 =\operatorname{dist}(\Sigma,\{\Theta\in U:g(\Theta)=0\}).
\]
Combining with (24) proves the theorem with \(c=L^{-1}\) and \(C_1=\sqrt2/m\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The statement connects the explicit algebraic certificate margin to the metric clearance used by the recovery theorem. \textit{Gap.} FangSantos2019 supplies general tangent-cone tools but not this one-sided OU boundary-distance equivalence or the buffer excluding remote model components. \textit{Consumer.} Certificate-based clearance can be interpreted and calibrated as local covariance distance on regular contact subcharts.

\begin{theorem}[thm:rootn-obstruction]\label{thm:rootn-obstruction}
For every \(\Sigma_*\in C\) and fixed \(h>0\), the alternatives \(\Sigma_n^{-}\) and \(\Sigma_n^{+}\) lie in \(U\cap\mathcal K\) for all large \(n\), satisfy \(\mathcal P(\Sigma_n^{-})=\{-1\}\) and \(\mathcal P(\Sigma_n^{+})=\{-1,+1\}\), and have \(\operatorname{dist}(\Sigma_n^{-},K_{+1})\asymp n^{-1/2}\). Their \(n\)-snapshot Gaussian experiments are mutually contiguous, uniformly for \(\Sigma_*\) on compact contact subcharts. Consequently, any procedure \(\widetilde\Gamma_n\) satisfying asymptotic uniform coverage of \(\mathcal P\) at level \(1-\alpha\) has \(\liminf\Pr_{\Sigma_n^{-}}\{+1\in\widetilde\Gamma_n\}>0\) along these sequences and cannot recover the singleton uniformly at root-\(n\) contact distance.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write \(\varepsilon_n=h/\sqrt n\), and, for \(\Sigma_*\in C\), write
\[
 \Sigma_*=\begin{pmatrix}s_*&t_*\\t_*&u_*\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The definition of the local alternatives gives
\(\Sigma_n^\pm=\begin{pmatrix}s_*&t_*\pm\varepsilon_n\\t_*\pm\varepsilon_n&u_*\end{pmatrix}\).  Since
\[
 g\!\left(\begin{pmatrix}s&t\\t&u\end{pmatrix}\right)
 =st-2\eta(su-t^2)(s+u),
\]
direct differentiation, with \(s\) and \(u\) fixed, gives
\[
 g_t=s+4\eta t(s+u),\qquad g_{tt}=4\eta(s+u).
\]
Because \(g(\Sigma_*)=0\), the quadratic Taylor formula is exact and yields
\[
 g(\Sigma_n^\pm)
 =\pm g_t(\Sigma_*)\varepsilon_n
   +2\eta(s_*+u_*)\varepsilon_n^2. \tag{1}
\]
By \(\mathrm{thm:asymmetric\text{-}contact}\), \(g_t>0\) on \(U\).  Continuity and compactness of \(C\subset U\) therefore imply
\[
 m_g:=\min_{\Sigma_*\in C}g_t(\Sigma_*)>0,
 \qquad
 B_g:=\max_{\Sigma_*\in C}(s_*+u_*)<\infty. \tag{2}
\]
Equations (1)--(2) show, uniformly over \(\Sigma_*\in C\), that for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 g(\Sigma_n^-)<0<g(\Sigma_n^+),
 \qquad
 \frac{m_g}{2}\varepsilon_n
 \le |g(\Sigma_n^-)|
 \le
 \left(\max_{\Sigma_*\in C}g_t(\Sigma_*)+1\right)\varepsilon_n. \tag{3}
\]
Here the harmless constant \(1\) in the upper bound absorbs the quadratic term for all sufficiently large \(n\).

Because \(C\) is compact and contained in the open set \(U\), and because
\(\sup_{\Sigma_*\in C}\|\Sigma_n^\pm-\Sigma_*\|_F
=\varepsilon_n\|H\|_F\to0\), both alternatives belong to \(U\) for all sufficiently large \(n\), uniformly over \(C\).  The inclusion \(U\subseteq\mathcal K\) and the polarity characterization on \(U\), both supplied by \(\mathrm{thm:asymmetric\text{-}contact}\), then give
\[
 \mathcal P(\Sigma_n^-)=\{-1\},
 \qquad
 \mathcal P(\Sigma_n^+)=\{-1,+1\}. \tag{4}
\]

Apply \(\mathrm{thm:local\text{-}contact\text{-}geometry}\) to the compact set \(C\).  Its neighborhood \(W\) contains \(\Sigma_n^-\) for all sufficiently large \(n\), uniformly over \(C\).  Combining its two-sided distance inequality with (3), there are constants \(0<c_h<C_h<\infty\), independent of \(\Sigma_*\in C\) and \(n\), such that
\[
 c_h n^{-1/2}
 \le \operatorname{dist}(\Sigma_n^-,K_{+1})
 \le C_h n^{-1/2}. \tag{5}
\]
This proves the asserted root-\(n\) contact-distance statement.

It remains to compare the experiments.  Under \(\mathrm{ass:gaussian\text{-}snapshots}\), let \(P_{n,*}^-\) and \(P_{n,*}^+\) denote the \(n\)-fold product laws with one-observation covariances \(\Sigma_n^-\) and \(\Sigma_n^+\), respectively.  Compactness of \(C\) in the positive-definite cone and the uniform convergence of the alternatives imply that there is \(m_0>0\) such that
\[
 \lambda_{\min}(\Sigma_n^\pm)\ge m_0
\]
for every sufficiently large \(n\) and every \(\Sigma_*\in C\).  Put
\[
 B_{n,*}=(\Sigma_n^-)^{-1/2}\Sigma_n^+(\Sigma_n^-)^{-1/2}
 =I_2+E_{n,*}.
\]
Since \(\Sigma_n^+-\Sigma_n^-=2\varepsilon_nH\),
\[
 \|E_{n,*}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le \frac{2\varepsilon_n\|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}}{m_0},
 \qquad
 \|E_{n,*}\|_F\le C_E n^{-1/2} \tag{6}
\]
uniformly over \(C\), for a finite constant \(C_E\).  In particular,
\(\|E_{n,*}\|_{\mathrm{op}}<1\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).

For the corresponding one-observation laws \(P^-\) and \(P^+\), let
\(L=dP^+/dP^-\).  Whitening a vector from \(P^-\), substituting the two Gaussian densities, and completing the Gaussian square give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_{P^-}L^2
 &=\det(B_{n,*})^{-1}
   \det(2B_{n,*}^{-1}-I_2)^{-1/2}\\
 &=\det\!\left(B_{n,*}(2I_2-B_{n,*})\right)^{-1/2}\\
 &=\det(I_2-E_{n,*}^2)^{-1/2}. 
 \end{aligned} \tag{7}
\]
The Gaussian integral in the first line is finite because (6) makes
\(2B_{n,*}^{-1}-I_2\) positive definite.  If \(e_1,e_2\) are the real eigenvalues of the symmetric matrix \(E_{n,*}\), then, for large \(n\),
\(-\log(1-e_j^2)\le2e_j^2\).  Hence (6)--(7) imply
\[
 \mathbb E_{P_{n,*}^-}
 \left(\frac{dP_{n,*}^+}{dP_{n,*}^-}\right)^2
 =\left\{\det(I_2-E_{n,*}^2)^{-1/2}\right\}^{n}
 \le \exp\!\left(n\|E_{n,*}\|_F^2\right)
 \le C_0<\infty, \tag{8}
\]
where \(C_0\) is uniform over \(\Sigma_*\in C\).  Repeating the same calculation with \(+\) and \(-\) interchanged gives another uniform finite bound, enlarged into the same \(C_0\), for the reverse likelihood ratio.  Thus, for every sequence of events \(A_n\), Cauchy--Schwarz and (8) give
\[
 P_{n,*}^+(A_n)\le C_0^{1/2}P_{n,*}^-(A_n)^{1/2},
 \qquad
 P_{n,*}^-(A_n)\le C_0^{1/2}P_{n,*}^+(A_n)^{1/2}. \tag{9}
\]
Consequently, vanishing probability under either sequence implies vanishing probability under the other.  This proves mutual contiguity, with constants uniform on the compact contact subchart.

Finally, spell out the coverage premise as
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{\Theta\in\mathcal K}
 P_\Theta^{\otimes n}
 \{\mathcal P(\Theta)\subseteq\widetilde\Gamma_n\}
 \ge1-\alpha. \tag{10}
\]
Let \(A_n=\{+1\in\widetilde\Gamma_n\}\).  By (4), the coverage event at \(\Sigma_n^+\) is contained in \(A_n\), so (10) implies
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}P_{n,*}^+(A_n)\ge1-\alpha. \tag{11}
\]
The first inequality in (9), rearranged and combined with (11), gives
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}P_{n,*}^-(A_n)
 \ge \frac{(1-\alpha)^2}{C_0}>0. \tag{12}
\]
The bound is uniform over \(\Sigma_*\in C\).  At the minus alternative, (4) says that the true polarity set is the singleton \(\{-1\}\), whereas the event \(A_n\) rules out returning exactly that singleton.  Equations (5) and (12) therefore preclude uniform singleton recovery at root-\(n\) contact distance, as claimed.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The lower bound shows that the clearance rate in the upper theorem is locally sharp for honest procedures. \textit{Gap.} Kaji2021 gives general weak-identification impossibility, but the reduced-form coordinate, contact geometry, and Gaussian covariance divergence are new to this OU target. \textit{Consumer.} The result prevents claims of uniformly faster singleton classification and identifies the appropriate reporting regime near contact.

\begin{theorem}[thm:certificate-completeness]\label{thm:certificate-completeness}
For every \(\Sigma\in\mathbb S_{++}^2\) and every sector \(\sigma\), if \(H_{\sigma}\ne\varnothing\), the retained finite equality-active family contains the unique minimizer of \(f\) over \(H_{\sigma}\).  This remains true when \(H_{\sigma}\) is a line or a point and requires no Slater condition.  For every sector, a legal strict-diffusion model exists if and only if \(H_{\sigma}\ne\varnothing\), \(J_{\sigma}\) holds, and \(\bigl[v<M^2\ \text{or}\ \bigl(v=M^2\ \text{and the corresponding minimizer has }d\in(0,1)\bigr)\bigr]\), where \(v\) is the smallest retained value.  The test uses at most eleven candidates, skipping every dependent active-row set.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write \(z=(d,q)^{\top}\).  From the affine-fiber lemma, the linear part of the map \(z\mapsto A(z;\Sigma)\) sends an increment \((e,p)\) to a matrix \(L(e,p)\) satisfying
\[
 L(e,p)\Sigma=\operatorname{diag}(e,-e)/2-Q(p).
\]
If \(L(e,p)=0\), the symmetric and skew-symmetric parts on the right vanish separately, so \(e=p=0\).  The map is therefore injective.  Hence the Hessian \(2G\) of \(f=\lVert A\rVert_F^2\) is positive definite.  In particular \(f\) is strictly convex and coercive.  Direct expansion gives the \(G\) and \(h\) in the sector-quadratic definition, with
\[
 c_0=\frac{s^2+t^2}{4\Delta^2},\qquad
 f(z)=f(z_0)+(z-z_0)^{\top}G(z-z_0),\qquad z_0=-G^{-1}h.
\]
Since \(H_{\sigma}\) is closed, every nonempty \(H_{\sigma}\) has a unique minimizer \(z_*\).

Let \(F_*\) be the unique minimal face of \(H_{\sigma}\) whose relative interior contains \(z_*\), and let \(I_*\) be the inequalities active on that face.  If \(v\) satisfies \(C_i v=0\) for every \(i\in I_*\), then both \(z_*+\epsilon v\) and \(z_*-\epsilon v\) remain in \(F_*\) for all sufficiently small \(\epsilon>0\).  First-order optimality along these two directions yields
\[
 v^{\top}G(z_*-z_0)=0.
\]
Choose independent rows \(C_I\) spanning the active-row space.  Its rank is \(0\), \(1\), or \(2\).  At rank zero the preceding orthogonality gives \(z_*=z_0\).  At positive rank it gives \(G(z_*-z_0)\in\operatorname{range}(C_I^{\top})\), while activity gives \(C_Iz_*=a_I\).  Solving these equations gives
\[
 z_*=z_0+G^{-1}C_I^{\top}
 (C_IG^{-1}C_I^{\top})^{-1}(a_I-C_Iz_0).
\]
The inverse exists because, for \(w\ne0\), independence of the rows and \(G^{-1}\succ0\) give
\[
 w^{\top}C_IG^{-1}C_I^{\top}w
 =(C_I^{\top}w)^{\top}G^{-1}(C_I^{\top}w)>0.
\]
Thus \(z_*\) is one of the retained candidates.  This argument also covers a line: a relative-interior minimum has active-row rank one, whereas an endpoint has rank two.  A singleton feasible set has active-row rank two, since rank at most one would leave a nonzero feasible tangent direction.  No inverse of a dependent row family is used.  There are at most \(1+4+\binom42=11\) tested candidates.

Now impose strict diffusion and the norm cap.  Necessity is immediate: a legal point belongs to \(H_{\sigma}\), has \(d\in(0,1)\), and has \(f\le M^2\), so \(J_{\sigma}\) holds and the closed minimum \(v=f(z_*)\) is at most \(M^2\).  If \(v=M^2\), strict convexity makes every feasible point of norm at most \(M\) equal to \(z_*\), so \((z_*)_d\in(0,1)\).

Conversely, if \(v=M^2\) and \((z_*)_d\in(0,1)\), then \(z_*\) is legal.  If \(v<M^2\) and \(J_{\sigma}\) holds, choose \(w\in H_{\sigma}\) with \(w_d\in(0,1)\).  For \(0<\lambda\le1\), convexity of the sector gives \(z_{\lambda}=(1-\lambda)z_*+\lambda w\in H_{\sigma}\), and \((z_{\lambda})_d\in(0,1)\).  Continuity gives \(f(z_{\lambda})<M^2\) for all sufficiently small \(\lambda>0\).  This proves sufficiency, including the case in which the closed minimum lies at \(d=0\) or \(d=1\).  The finite candidate comparison therefore supplies the asserted complete strict-attainment certificate on every rank stratum.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:published-class-polarity]\label{thm:published-class-polarity}
For every \(\Sigma=\begin{pmatrix}s&t\\ t&u\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb S_{++}^{2}\) that is \(t\)-faithful for the complete directed two-cycle \(\mathcal E\), meaning \(t\ne0\), the published legal fiber is nonempty and
\[
\mathcal P_{\mathrm{pub}}(\Sigma)=\{-1,+1\}.
\]
More precisely, after trace normalization, every fixed \(d\in(0,1)\) admits full-support witnesses of both product polarities. Thus removal of the analyst-imposed constants \(\eta\) and \(M\) makes same-sign witnesses reappear for every \(t\ne0\), and the published-class product-polarity set is never a singleton.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix such a covariance. Positive definiteness gives \(s>0\), \(u>0\), and \(\Delta=su-t^2>0\), while \(t\)-faithfulness gives \(t\ne0\). Fix any \(d\in(0,1)\) and let \(A(d,q;\Sigma)\) be the affine-fiber matrix. By the affine-fiber lemma,
\[
A\Sigma+\Sigma A^{\top}=D(d),
\qquad D(d)=\operatorname{diag}(d,1-d)\succ0,
\]
and \(-A\) is Hurwitz. Consequently \(B=-A\) and \(\widetilde D=D(d)\) obey
\[
B\Sigma+\Sigma B^{\top}=-\widetilde D,
\]
so only structural minimality and the desired product sign remain to be arranged.

The four entries of \(A\) are
\[
A_{11}=\frac{du/2+qt}{\Delta},\quad
A_{12}=\frac{-dt/2-qs}{\Delta},\quad
A_{21}=\frac{-(1-d)t/2+qu}{\Delta},\quad
A_{22}=\frac{(1-d)s/2-qt}{\Delta}.
\]
The off-diagonal zero values of \(q\) are
\[
q_b=-\frac{dt}{2s},
\qquad
q_c=\frac{(1-d)t}{2u},
\]
and the diagonal zero values are
\[
q_{11}=-\frac{du}{2t},
\qquad
q_{22}=\frac{(1-d)s}{2t}.
\]
All four values are finite. If \(t>0\), then \(q_b<0<q_c\); if \(t<0\), then \(q_c<0<q_b\). Hence the open interval
\[
I_d=\bigl(\min\{q_b,q_c\},\max\{q_b,q_c\}\bigr)
\]
is nonempty. Directly from the two affine formulas, for \(q\in I_d\) the entries \(A_{12}\) and \(A_{21}\) have the same nonzero sign, whereas for
\[
q<\min\{q_b,q_c\}
\quad\text{or}\quad
q>\max\{q_b,q_c\}
\]
they have opposite nonzero signs. Removing the two points \(q_{11}\) and \(q_{22}\) from the nonempty open interval leaves a point, and removing them from either exterior ray also leaves a point. At either selected point all four entries of \(A\), and therefore all four entries of \(B=-A\), are nonzero. Thus \(\operatorname{supp}(B)=\mathcal E\).

The interval choice supplies an element of \(\mathcal F_{\mathrm{pub}}(\Sigma)\) with \(B_{12}B_{21}>0\), and an exterior-ray choice supplies one with \(B_{12}B_{21}<0\). Structural minimality excludes a zero product, so \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{pub}}(\Sigma)\subseteq\{-1,+1\}\); the two witnesses prove the reverse inclusion. No magnitude lower bound or drift-norm upper bound was used, which proves the final assertion.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the first headline: in the exact unrestricted published complete-two-cycle class, the covariance-compatible product-polarity set is always \(\{-1,+1\}\) on the \(t\)-faithful region. \textit{Gap.} Published sign-identifiability results treat target edges individually and do not give full-support same-product and opposite-product witnesses for every \(t\)-faithful bivariate complete-cycle covariance. \textit{Consumer.} Analysts cannot attribute singleton product-sign conclusions to the unrestricted published SCM; any such conclusion must be tied to additional restrictions.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The equality-active certificate is complete on full-dimensional, line, point, dependent-row, strict-diffusion, and norm-equality branches, and the covariance inversion and local lower bound are proved. The remaining limitations are matters of declared scope rather than unresolved proof steps: the metric comparison and lower bound are local to compact regular contact subcharts.

\section{Honest scope}

The unrestricted headline applies only to the exactly bivariate, \(t\)-faithful, structurally minimal complete two-cycle with positive diagonal diffusion; it proves global nonidentification, not recovery. Singleton regions, equal-variance thresholds, contacts, and inference concern the analyst-imposed bounded-margin refinement expressed by raw trace ratios, with \(\kappa\) used for uniform covariance inference. The paper excludes larger-graph projection, marginalization, empirical-performance claims, and any global equivalence between certificate margin and covariance distance. Its distance equivalence and Gaussian lower bound are confined to compact regular subcharts of the explicit asymmetric contact.
