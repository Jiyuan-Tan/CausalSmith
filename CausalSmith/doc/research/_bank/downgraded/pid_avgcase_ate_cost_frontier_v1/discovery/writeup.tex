\section*{pid\_\allowbreak{}avgcase\_\allowbreak{}ate\_\allowbreak{}cost\_\allowbreak{}frontier}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} A finite observed law is lifted to one joint law of treatment and both potential outcomes, and the least Pearson assignment information needed to support each ATE is profiled. The attained sharp frontier has a perspective SOCP and explicit dual. For binary outcomes, a treatment-independent garbling removes the minority discordant response type without imposing monotone treatment response and without increasing any convex assignment cost; this yields the complete six-face scalar catalog and a squared-correlation null identity. A one-way, cost-nonincreasing embedding into the explicitly defined box-augmented Neyman specialization of Ishikawa--He--Kanamori equation~(14) gives the exact separation \(9/70>9/91\); strict interiority and continuity extend it to a nonempty open neighborhood with a uniformly positive gap. On a named uniformly Slater-regular multinomial region, a fixed-odds-cone parametrization, a uniform equality right inverse, and bounded attained cellwise dual multipliers yield a finite-sample \(O(n^{-1/2})\) profile diameter without transporting a dual certificate between laws; a contiguous boundary pair shows that honest bands must retain infinite diameter at a finite-to-infinite null-cost boundary.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Average-case sensitivity analysis has sharp armwise potential-outcome bounds but no jointly attained ATE frontier. Ishikawa--He--Kanamori's equations~(10), (12), and (14) provide an observed-policy-first divergence and Jensen--CMC computation, but the resulting armwise weights need not be realizable by one cross-world-compatible assignment law. Existing work therefore neither supplies the one-way full-law embedding on the same Neyman budget scale nor establishes an exact and open-set strict cost gap. Existing inference theory also does not quantify both the regular finite-sample width and the finite-to-infinite boundary obstruction for this frontier.

\paragraph{Niche.} Finite outcomes make the missing joint law explicit, expose a response-type garbling geometry invisible to armwise reweighting, and permit an interior finite-dimensional comparison showing that the relaxation gap is robust to small changes in the observed law.

\paragraph{Fill.} The project profiles the exact full-law Pearson cost, certifies it by primal and dual conic programs, solves the binary geometry, and maps each compatible full law one way into an explicitly defined box-augmented Neyman specialization of Ishikawa--He--Kanamori equation~(14) at no greater cost. It proves the exact values \(9/70\) and \(9/91\) and, by continuity at strictly interior scalar minimizers, a uniformly positive gap on a nonempty open neighborhood. It also pairs a finite-sample regular-region diameter theorem based on a common fixed odds cone and uniformly bounded attained multipliers with a contiguous boundary lower bound.

\section{Related work}

Rubin supplies the potential-outcome notation and consistency framework, while Tan supplies the marginal odds-ratio sensitivity model used for the assignment restriction. Zhang and Zhao motivate average rather than worst-case assignment distortion and prove sharp bounds for each average potential outcome, but their discussion explicitly withholds joint ATE attainment. Dorn and Guo's quantile-balancing result and Dorn, Guo, and Kallus's doubly sharp extension establish sharp worst-case marginal-sensitivity benchmarks; neither profiles one joint full-law average assignment budget. Ishikawa and He's kernel conditional-moment construction and Jin, Ren, and Zhou's general \(f\)-sensitivity model motivate tractable observed-weight relaxations, but do not impose the present cross-world-compatible joint response law. Ishikawa, He, and Kanamori define an observed-policy-first \(f\)-divergence in their equation~(10), apply Jensen in equation~(12), and combine the relaxed divergence with CMC in equation~(14). The comparison here is deliberately one way: every compatible full law induces a feasible point of the note's explicitly defined box-augmented Neyman specialization with no larger cost; no equality with their unqualified published class and no reverse cross-world realizability are claimed. The rational witness has values \(9/70\) and \(9/91\), and strict interiority of both scalar programs makes the gap uniformly positive on an open neighborhood of observed laws. Balke and Pearl supply the finite response-type representation, and Manski supplies the substantive monotone-response benchmark; the binary garbling result instead derives monotone support for an extremal compatible law without assuming monotone treatment response of the population. Blackwell's comparison-of-experiments order motivates the garbling language, but does not establish preservation of the two observed causal margins and ATE proved here. The regular finite-sample theorem rescales response-type masses onto a common odds cone, derives a uniform right inverse and multiplier bound from the declared Slater margin, and compares different observed laws through the saddle identity at each law rather than reusing a moved dual certificate. Duan, Zhang, and Zhang derive directional derivatives for finite-valued Slater-regular SOCP values, while Duchi, Glynn, and Namkoong study generalized empirical-likelihood robust optimization; neither covers this support-changing extended-real profile. Fang and Santos, Firpo, Galvao, and Parker, and Masten and Poirier provide directional or breakdown-frontier inference tools, while Zhao, Small, and Bhattacharya give a bootstrap sensitivity procedure; extending such inference through this frontier's zero cells and moving faces remains open. Masten, Poirier, and Ren provide a general relaxation-of-unconfoundedness perspective, whereas the present result specializes to an attained finite full-law information frontier. Nattino, Ashmead, and Lu motivate the undeveloped complex-survey extension, and Cinelli and Hazlett motivate observed-covariate calibration; neither extension or empirical application is claimed here.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:overlap]\label{ass:overlap}
For every \(x\in\mathcal X\) with \(p_x>0\), \(\varepsilon\leq e_x\leq1-\varepsilon\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (uniform overlap for binary treatment, \cite{Tan2006Distributional}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:consistency]\label{ass:consistency}
Under \(Q\), \(Y=ZY(1)+(1-Z)Y(0)\) almost surely.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (potential-outcome consistency, \cite{Rubin1974Estimating}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:observed-compatibility]\label{ass:observed-compatibility}
The law induced by \(Q\) for \(O\) equals \(P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (observational compatibility of a latent response-type law, \cite{BalkePearl1997Bounds}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:odds-cap]\label{ass:odds-cap}
For every positive-mass response-type cell, \(\ell_x\leq q_{xij}\leq u_x\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (marginal sensitivity odds-ratio bound, \cite{Tan2006Distributional}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:iid-sampling]\label{ass:iid-sampling}
The observations \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\) are independent draws from \(P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (independent superpopulation sampling, \cite{ZhangZhao2026AverageCase}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:positive-observed-cells]\label{ass:positive-observed-cells}
Every coordinate of the true multinomial vector satisfies \(\theta_{xzy}\geq\delta\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (uniform finite-alphabet version of the positive observed-density condition, \cite{ZhangZhao2026AverageCase}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:uniform-primal-margin]\label{ass:uniform-primal-margin}
For every \(t\in\mathcal T_0\), the perspective SOCP has a feasible array satisfying each nonredundant response-mass and odds-face inequality with slack at least \(\delta\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (uniform Slater margin for a parametric second-order conic program, \cite{DuanZhangZhang2018SOCP}).
\end{assumption}

\section{Definitions}

\begin{definition}[def:compatible-class]\label{def:compatible-class}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Full-\allowbreak{}propensity compatibility class}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\mathcal H_\Gamma(P)=\{Q: Q\text{ satisfies observational compatibility, consistency, and }\ell_x\leq q_{xij}\leq u_x\text{ on every positive cell}\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Member properties.} \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}observed-\allowbreak{}compatibility}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}consistency}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}odds-\allowbreak{}cap}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:assignment-cost]\label{def:assignment-cost}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Pearson assignment cost}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(R_P(Q)=\sum_xp_x\sum_{i,j}d_{xij}^2/[e_x(1-e_x)s_{xij}]\), using the closed perspective convention \(0/0=0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{Q}; \texttt{P}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:frontier]\label{def:frontier}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Sharp ATE cost frontier}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(C_P(t)=\min\{R_P(Q):Q\in\mathcal H_\Gamma(P),\ T(Q)=t\}\) when the fiber is nonempty and \(C_P(t)=+\infty\) otherwise.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{P}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}Gamma}; \texttt{t}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:budget-set]\label{def:budget-set}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Budgeted ATE set}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\mathcal I_P(\rho)=\{t:C_P(t)\leq\rho\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{P}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}rho}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:socp]\label{def:socp}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Perspective SOCP}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Introduce \(v_{xij}\geq0\) and impose \(\|(2d_{xij}/\sqrt{e_x(1-e_x)},s_{xij}-v_{xij})\|_2\leq s_{xij}+v_{xij}\); minimize \(\sum_xp_x\sum_{i,j}v_{xij}\) subject to the observed-margin, odds-face, and \(T(Q)=t\) equalities and inequalities.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{P}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}Gamma}; \texttt{t}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:dual]\label{def:dual}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Cellwise conic dual}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Maximize \(\lambda t+\sum_xp_x[e_x\sum_j\alpha_{xj}\pi_{1x,j}+(1-e_x)\sum_i\beta_{xi}\pi_{0x,i}]\) over \(\lambda,\alpha,\beta\) subject to \(\min_{q\in[\ell_x,u_x]}\{(q-e_x)^2/[e_x(1-e_x)]-q\alpha_{xj}-(1-q)\beta_{xi}-\lambda(j-i)\}\geq0\) for all \(x,i,j\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{P}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}Gamma}; \texttt{t}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:binary-garbling]\label{def:binary-garbling}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Discordant-\allowbreak{}type garbling}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For \(\tau\geq0\), select response-type \(01\) mass equal to \(s_{10}\) independently of \(Z\), send type \(10\) to \(00\) and \(11\) with probabilities \(1-q_{01}\) and \(q_{01}\), and send the selected type \(01\) to \(00\) and \(11\) with probabilities \(q_{10}\) and \(1-q_{10}\); use outcome complementation for \(\tau<0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{Q}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}tau}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:binary-scalar-program]\label{def:binary-scalar-program}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Binary scalar frontier program}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For \(0<\tau<1\), use \(C_P(\tau)=\min_{v\in[L_\tau,U_\tau]}F_\tau(v)\), with feasibility exactly \(L_\tau\leq U_\tau\); negative effects follow by replacing \((p_1,p_0,\tau)\) with \((1-p_1,1-p_0,-\tau)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{P}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}Gamma}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}tau}.

\section{Main results}

\begin{theorem}[thm:sharp-frontier]\label{thm:sharp-frontier}
For every observed law \(P\) satisfying overlap and every \(\Gamma\geq1\), a value \(t\) is generated by some \(Q\in\mathcal H_\Gamma(P)\) with \(R_P(Q)\leq\rho\) if and only if \(C_P(t)\leq\rho\). Every finite value of \(C_P\) is attained, \(D_P\) is a compact interval, every endpoint of \(\mathcal I_P(\rho)\) is attained, \(C_P\) is closed and convex, and the perspective SOCP has \(O(|\mathcal X|K^2)\) cells.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(P\) and \(\Gamma\), and omit strata with \(p_x=0\). By \(\mathrm{ass{:}overlap}\), \(0<e_x<1\) on each remaining stratum. The finite response-type representation gives
\[
\sum_i a_{xij}=e_x\pi_{1x,j},\qquad
\sum_j b_{xij}=(1-e_x)\pi_{0x,i},\qquad a_{xij},b_{xij}\geq0. \tag{1}
\]
Conversely, arrays satisfying (1) define a full law by putting conditional mass \(a_{xij}\) on \((Y(0),Y(1),Z)=(i,j,1)\) and mass \(b_{xij}\) on \((i,j,0)\). Equation (1) matches both observed arms, and \(\mathrm{ass{:}consistency}\) then matches \(P\). Thus this representation is equivalent to \(\mathrm{def{:}compatible\mbox{-}class}\), not an outer relaxation.

Put \(s_{xij}=a_{xij}+b_{xij}\). On positive cells, \(q_{xij}=a_{xij}/s_{xij}\), so \(\mathrm{ass{:}odds\mbox{-}cap}\) is exactly
\[
a_{xij}-\ell_xs_{xij}\geq0,\qquad u_xs_{xij}-a_{xij}\geq0. \tag{2}
\]
When \(s_{xij}=0\), nonnegativity forces \(a_{xij}=b_{xij}=0\), and (2) remains valid. Moreover,
\[
d_{xij}=(1-e_x)a_{xij}-e_xb_{xij}=s_{xij}(q_{xij}-e_x). \tag{3}
\]
Hence \(\mathrm{def{:}assignment\mbox{-}cost}\) is a sum of closed quadratic perspectives
\[
p_x\frac{d_{xij}^{\,2}}{e_x(1-e_x)s_{xij}}. \tag{4}
\]
Each term is lower semicontinuous and convex in \((a_{xij},b_{xij})\), including its declared value at \((s,d)=(0,0)\). The ATE equation is linear:
\[
\sum_xp_x\sum_{i,j}(j-i)s_{xij}=t. \tag{5}
\]

The feasible array set before imposing (5) is nonempty: in each stratum take any coupling of the two observed arm marginals, for example their product coupling, and assign \(Z\) independently with probability \(e_x\); then every positive type has \(q_{xij}=e_x\in[\ell_x,u_x]\). It is closed, convex, and bounded, because (1) implies
\[
\sum_{i,j}a_{xij}=e_x,\qquad \sum_{i,j}b_{xij}=1-e_x.
\]
It is compact. Its image under (5) is therefore a compact interval, exactly \(D_P\). Every nonempty fiber is compact, so lower semicontinuity of (4) proves attainment of every finite \(C_P(t)\).

The rotated-cone identity
\[
\left\|\left(\frac{2d_{xij}}{\sqrt{e_x(1-e_x)}},s_{xij}-v_{xij}\right)\right\|_2
\leq s_{xij}+v_{xij}
\Longleftrightarrow
s_{xij}v_{xij}\geq\frac{d_{xij}^{\,2}}{e_x(1-e_x)},\quad s_{xij},v_{xij}\geq0 \tag{6}
\]
shows that \(\mathrm{def{:}socp}\) has the same value after minimizing over \(v\). There are \(|\mathcal X|K^2\) response-type cells and a constant number of variables and constraints per cell, so its size is \(O(|\mathcal X|K^2)\).

Mixing feasible arrays and using convexity of (4) gives
\[
C_P(\eta t_1+(1-\eta)t_0)\leq\eta C_P(t_1)+(1-\eta)C_P(t_0),\qquad 0\leq\eta\leq1. \tag{7}
\]
If \(t_r\to t\) and \(\liminf_rC_P(t_r)<\infty\), take optimizing arrays along a subsequence realizing the liminf. Compactness supplies a convergent subsequence, and lower semicontinuity gives \(C_P(t)\leq\liminf_rC_P(t_r)\). Thus \(C_P\) is closed and convex. Its sublevel \(\mathcal I_P(\rho)\) is a compact interval, possibly empty; every endpoint of a nonempty sublevel belongs to it and has an attaining full law.

Finally, if \(Q\in\mathcal H_\Gamma(P)\), \(T(Q)=t\), and \(R_P(Q)\leq\rho\), minimization gives \(C_P(t)\leq R_P(Q)\leq\rho\). Conversely, \(C_P(t)\leq\rho<\infty\) puts \(t\) in \(D_P\), and attainment supplies \(Q_t\) with \(T(Q_t)=t\) and \(R_P(Q_t)=C_P(t)\leq\rho\). Therefore the observed-law functional \(\mathcal I_P(\rho)\) is exactly, and hence sharply, the set of causal ATE values generated by one compatible full law at budget \(\rho\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This theorem turns an average confounding budget into an attained sharp identified set for one joint ATE law. \textit{Gap.} ZhangZhao2026AverageCase proves armwise sharpness but explicitly does not establish simultaneous ATE attainment; DornGuo2023QuantileBalancing establishes joint sharpness only for the worst-case odds model. \textit{Consumer.} A sensitivity analyst can report the exact ATE interval compatible with one common hidden assignment mechanism at budget \(\rho\).

\begin{theorem}[thm:explicit-dual]\label{thm:explicit-dual}
The program in \(\mathrm{def{:}socp}\) has the value of the explicit cellwise dual in \(\mathrm{def{:}dual}\), and the dual supremum is attained for every \(t\in D_P\), including exposed endpoint fibers and fibers with zero response-type cells.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(g_x(q)=(q-e_x)^2/[e_x(1-e_x)]\). By \(\mathrm{thm{:}sharp\mbox{-}frontier}\), the primal objective is
\[
\sum_xp_x\sum_{i,j}s_{xij}g_x(q_{xij}),\quad
a_{xij}=s_{xij}q_{xij},\quad b_{xij}=s_{xij}(1-q_{xij}),\quad
q_{xij}\in[\ell_x,u_x]. \tag{8}
\]
Use the Lagrangian
\[
\lambda\{t-T(Q)\}
+\sum_xp_x\sum_j\alpha_{xj}\left\{e_x\pi_{1x,j}-\sum_i a_{xij}\right\}
+\sum_xp_x\sum_i\beta_{xi}\left\{(1-e_x)\pi_{0x,i}-\sum_j b_{xij}\right\}. \tag{9}
\]
For a cell of mass \(s\geq0\) and propensity \(q\), the coefficient of \(p_xs\) is
\[
g_x(q)-q\alpha_{xj}-(1-q)\beta_{xi}-\lambda(j-i). \tag{10}
\]
The infimum over \(s\geq0\) is zero exactly when the minimum of (10) over \(q\in[\ell_x,u_x]\) is nonnegative, and is \(-\infty\) otherwise. The constant term in (9) is
\[
\lambda t+\sum_xp_x\left[e_x\sum_j\alpha_{xj}\pi_{1x,j}
+(1-e_x)\sum_i\beta_{xi}\pi_{0x,i}\right]. \tag{11}
\]
Equations (10)--(11) are precisely \(\mathrm{def{:}dual}\). Multiplying (10) by \(p_xs_{xij}\), summing, and invoking the primal equalities proves weak duality.

For equality and dual attainment, consider the polyhedral cell wedge
\[
\mathcal K_x=\{(a,b):a,b\geq0,\ \ell_x(a+b)\leq a\leq u_x(a+b)\}
\]
and the closed sublinear function \(f_x(a,b)=(a+b)g_x(a/(a+b))\), with \(f_x(0,0)=0\). Overlap gives \(0<\ell_x\leq u_x<1\). Since \(g_x\) and \(g_x'\) are bounded on this interval, direct differentiation on rays and one-sided limits at the two boundary rays give a finite \(B_x\) such that
\[
|f_x(z)-f_x(z')|\leq B_x\|z-z'\|_1,\qquad z,z'\in\mathcal K_x. \tag{12}
\]

The product of these wedges has finitely many faces. On each face, delete redundant equality rows and use a right inverse of the remaining matrix. The maximum of the finitely many right-inverse norms is finite. Therefore, for nearby attainable right sides \(r,r'\) on a common face and any feasible \(z\) over \(r\), one can solve the independent rows for a feasible correction satisfying
\[
\|z-z'\|_1\leq H\|r-r'\|_1. \tag{13}
\]
If the correction meets a subface, repeat there; termination follows from finiteness of the face lattice. Combining (12) and (13) shows that the optimal value as a function of all margin and ATE right sides is locally Lipschitz relative to every face of its polyhedral domain, including endpoint faces.

At the right side determined by \(P\) and any \(t\in D_P\), support this finite convex value function on its minimal face and add a normal of that face to extend the support to the whole attainable domain. Relative Lipschitz continuity makes the supporting coefficients finite. Denote them by \((\alpha,\beta,\lambda)\). Evaluating the support inequality on an array concentrated along any single-cell ray \(q\in[\ell_x,u_x]\) gives exactly (10), while equality at the chosen right side makes (11) equal to \(C_P(t)\). Thus strong duality holds and the dual supremum is attained. The proof works on the minimal face rather than requiring Slater regularity, so it includes exposed endpoint fibers and zero response-type cells.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Endpoint dual attainment supplies analytic certificates and supports comparative statics at odds faces. \textit{Gap.} Generic Slater arguments do not cover the lower-dimensional endpoint fibers created by zero perspective cells. \textit{Consumer.} A certified implementation can validate sharp frontier ordinates and endpoint effects without trusting a numerical solver status alone.

\begin{theorem}[thm:binary-garbling]\label{thm:binary-garbling}
For binary outcomes in one stratum, every \(Q\in\mathcal H_\Gamma(P)\) with ATE \(\tau\geq0\) admits the treatment-independent garbling \(G_\tau\) whose image has zero mass on response type \(10\), induces the same \(P\), has the same \(\tau\), remains in \(\mathcal H_\Gamma(P)\), and weakly lowers \(\sum_{i,j}s_{ij}\varphi(q_{ij})\) for every convex \(\varphi\); for \(\tau\leq0\), the analogous image has zero mass on type \(01\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Work in one stratum and omit \(x\). Since
\[
\tau=E_Q[Y(1)-Y(0)]=s_{01}-s_{10}, \tag{14}
\]
\(\tau\geq0\) implies \(s_{01}\geq s_{10}\). Put \(r=s_{10}\). If \(r=0\), use the identity. Otherwise select a fraction \(r/s_{01}\) of type \(01\) using auxiliary randomness independent of \((Y(0),Y(1),Z)\). The selected mass is \(r\), and its propensity remains \(q_{01}\).

Apply \(\mathrm{def{:}binary\mbox{-}garbling}\), independently of \(Z\): send type \(10\) to \(00\) and \(11\) with probabilities \(1-q_{01}\) and \(q_{01}\), and send selected type \(01\) to \(00\) and \(11\) with probabilities \(q_{10}\) and \(1-q_{10}\). The image has \(s'_{10}=0\) and \(s'_{01}=s_{01}-r=\tau\), so its ATE is unchanged.

The paired masses contributed to type \(00\) have treated and control masses
\[
rq_{10}(1-q_{01})+rq_{01}q_{10}=rq_{10}=a_{10}, \tag{15}
\]
\[
r(1-q_{10})(1-q_{01})+r(1-q_{01})q_{10}=r(1-q_{01}), \tag{16}
\]
where (16) is the control mass removed from selected type \(01\). At type \(11\), the corresponding masses are
\[
rq_{10}q_{01}+rq_{01}(1-q_{10})=rq_{01}, \tag{17}
\]
\[
r(1-q_{10})q_{01}+r(1-q_{01})(1-q_{10})=r(1-q_{10})=b_{10}. \tag{18}
\]
Equations (15)--(18) preserve treated \(Y(1)\) and control \(Y(0)\) margins. Consistency therefore preserves \(P\).

Every moved piece retains its source propensity because the kernel is treatment-independent. The propensity of a pooled destination is a mass-weighted average of source propensities, including any original destination mass. Since \([\ell_x,u_x]\) is convex, all positive image cells obey the same odds cap, so the image remains in \(\mathcal H_\Gamma(P)\).

Splitting a cell treatment-independently preserves \(s\varphi(q)\). Pooling masses \(r_k\) gives, by convexity,
\[
\left(\sum_kr_k\right)\varphi\left(\frac{\sum_kr_kq_k}{\sum_kr_k}\right)
\leq\sum_kr_k\varphi(q_k). \tag{19}
\]
Apply (19) at destinations \(00\) and \(11\); unchanged pieces cancel. This proves contraction for every convex \(\varphi\), including the Pearson generator. For \(\tau<0\), complement both potential outcomes, apply the positive-effect construction to \(-\tau\), and complement back. This swaps \(01\) and \(10\), preserves propensities and costs, and leaves zero mass on type \(01\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The garbling is the structural reduction that converts a cross-world four-type problem into an exact scalar frontier. \textit{Gap.} Manski1997MTR assumes monotone response, while BalkePearl1997Bounds enumerates response types; neither derives monotone support as a convex-cost-improving transformation. \textit{Consumer.} The theorem makes binary sensitivity curves analytically auditable and separates an optimizer property from a substantive monotone-response assumption.

\begin{theorem}[thm:binary-phase-catalog]\label{thm:binary-phase-catalog}
For one stratum, binary outcomes, and \(0<\tau<1\), the fiber is feasible if and only if \(L_\tau\leq U_\tau\), and \(C_P(\tau)=\min_{v\in[L_\tau,U_\tau]}F_\tau(v)\). If \(L_\tau<U_\tau\), the optimizer equals \(L_\tau\) when \(H_\tau(L_\tau)\geq0\), equals \(U_\tau\) when \(H_\tau(U_\tau)\leq0\), and otherwise is the unique root of \(H_\tau(v)=0\) in \((L_\tau,U_\tau)\). These cases form the complete six-odds-face catalog plus one interior algebraic phase, with support-degenerate cases interpreted by closed perspectives.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Assume \(0<\tau<1\). By \(\mathrm{thm{:}binary\mbox{-}garbling}\), a Pearson-cost optimizer may be chosen with \(s_{10}=0\), and every law supported on the other three types remains admissible for the original problem. Set
\[
v=s_{00},\qquad s_{01}=\tau,\qquad s_{11}=1-\tau-v. \tag{20}
\]
The treated-zero and control-one margins force \(a_{00}=A\) and \(b_{11}=B\). Since \(D_0=A+(1-e_x)(1-p_0)\), the remaining entries are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
(i,j)&s_{ij}&a_{ij}&q_{ij}\\ \hline
00&v&A&A/v\\
01&\tau&v+\tau-D_0&(v+\tau-D_0)/\tau\\
11&1-\tau-v&1-\tau-v-B&1-B/(1-\tau-v).
\end{array} \tag{21}
\]
Zero denominators use the declared closed-perspective convention.

The three odds restrictions in (21) are, respectively,
\[
\frac{A}{u_x}\leq v\leq\frac{A}{\ell_x}, \tag{22}
\]
\[
1-\tau-\frac{B}{1-u_x}\leq v\leq1-\tau-\frac{B}{1-\ell_x}, \tag{23}
\]
\[
D_0-(1-\ell_x)\tau\leq v\leq D_0-(1-u_x)\tau. \tag{24}
\]
Their intersection is \([L_\tau,U_\tau]\). Thus feasibility is exactly \(L_\tau\leq U_\tau\), and (22)--(24) are the complete six odds faces.

Because \(\sum s_{ij}q_{ij}=e_x\) and \(\sum s_{ij}=1\),
\[
\sum_{ij}\frac{s_{ij}(q_{ij}-e_x)^2}{e_x(1-e_x)}
=\frac{\sum_{ij}s_{ij}q_{ij}^2-e_x^2}{e_x(1-e_x)}. \tag{25}
\]
Substitution of (21) into (25) gives exactly \(F_\tau(v)\). Therefore
\[
C_P(\tau)=\min_{v\in[L_\tau,U_\tau]}F_\tau(v), \tag{26}
\]
with attainment by compactness and closed perspectives.

On the relative interior of its effective interval,
\[
e_x(1-e_x)F_\tau'(v)
=-\frac{A^2}{v^2}+\frac{B^2}{(1-\tau-v)^2}
+\frac{2(v+\tau-D_0)}{\tau}-1=H_\tau(v), \tag{27}
\]
and
\[
H_\tau'(v)=\frac{2A^2}{v^3}+\frac{2B^2}{(1-\tau-v)^3}+\frac{2}{\tau}>0. \tag{28}
\]
Thus \(F_\tau\) is strictly convex and \(H_\tau\) strictly increasing. If \(H_\tau(L_\tau)\geq0\), the minimizer is \(L_\tau\); if \(H_\tau(U_\tau)\leq0\), it is \(U_\tau\); otherwise continuity and (28) give one unique root in \((L_\tau,U_\tau)\), which is the optimizer. Singleton or support-degenerate cases follow by closed limits. Outcome complementation gives the stated negative-effect catalog.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:null-correlation-cost]\label{prop:null-correlation-cost}
For a feasible one-stratum binary null with nonconstant \(Y\), \(C_P(0)=e_x(1-e_x)(p_1-p_0)^2/[m(1-m)]=\operatorname{Corr}_P(Z,Y)^2\); if the null odds inequalities fail, then \(C_P(0)=+\infty\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Suppose the binary null is feasible and \(Y\) is nonconstant. At \(\tau=0\), \(\mathrm{thm{:}binary\mbox{-}garbling}\) removes both discordant types: its image has \(s_{10}=0\), while \(s_{01}-s_{10}=0\) forces \(s_{01}=0\). The margins uniquely determine
\[
s_{11}=e_xp_1+(1-e_x)p_0=m,\qquad s_{00}=1-m, \tag{29}
\]
\[
q_{11}=\frac{e_xp_1}{m},\qquad q_{00}=\frac{e_x(1-p_1)}{1-m}. \tag{30}
\]
Nonconstancy means \(0<m<1\), so the divisions are valid. The null is feasible exactly when both propensities in (30) belong to \([\ell_x,u_x]\).

Writing \(d=p_1-p_0\), direct subtraction gives
\[
q_{11}-e_x=\frac{e_x(1-e_x)d}{m},\qquad
q_{00}-e_x=-\frac{e_x(1-e_x)d}{1-m}. \tag{31}
\]
Therefore
\[
C_P(0)
=\frac{m(q_{11}-e_x)^2+(1-m)(q_{00}-e_x)^2}{e_x(1-e_x)}
=\frac{e_x(1-e_x)(p_1-p_0)^2}{m(1-m)}. \tag{32}
\]
Under \(P\),
\[
\operatorname{Cov}_P(Z,Y)=e_xp_1-e_xm=e_x(1-e_x)(p_1-p_0),
\]
\(\operatorname{Var}_P(Z)=e_x(1-e_x)\), and \(\operatorname{Var}_P(Y)=m(1-m)\). Thus (32) is \(\operatorname{Corr}_P(Z,Y)^2\).

If either null odds inequality fails, a hypothetical compatible null could be garbled into the unique concordant law (29)--(30) without changing \(P\), \(\tau\), or the odds cap, a contradiction. The null fiber is empty, so \(\mathrm{def{:}frontier}\) gives \(C_P(0)=+\infty\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The identity gives a directly interpretable assignment-information price for erasing a binary association. \textit{Gap.} No inspected sensitivity result identifies the feasible sharp null cost with the observed squared correlation while retaining the odds cap. \textit{Consumer.} Applied readers can benchmark the null budget on the familiar correlation scale and detect when the null is impossible at every finite budget.

\begin{theorem}[thm:symmetric-family]\label{thm:symmetric-family}
If \(e_x=1/2\), \(p_1=(1+c)/2\), \(p_0=(1-c)/2\), and \(\kappa=(\Gamma-1)/(\Gamma+1)\), then \(D_P=\{\tau\in(-1,1):|c-\tau|\leq\kappa(1-|\tau|)\}\) and \(C_P(\tau)=(c-\tau)^2/(1-|\tau|)\) on \(D_P\). In particular, for \(c=3/5\) and \(\Gamma=4\), \(D_P=[0,3/4]\) and \(C_P(0)=9/25\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First let \(0<\tau<1\). Under the symmetric law,
\[
A=B=\frac{1-c}{4},\qquad D_0=\frac12. \tag{33}
\]
In \(\mathrm{thm{:}binary\mbox{-}phase\mbox{-}catalog}\), put \(v_\star=(1-\tau)/2\). The propensities in (21) become
\[
q_{00}=\frac{1-c}{2(1-\tau)},\qquad q_{01}=\frac12,\qquad q_{11}=1-q_{00}. \tag{34}
\]
At \(v_\star\), the first two terms of \(H_\tau\) cancel, and \(2(v_\star+\tau-D_0)/\tau-1=0\). Hence \(H_\tau(v_\star)=0\), and strict increase makes \(v_\star\) the unique unconstrained minimizer.

For \(e_x=1/2\),
\[
\ell_x=\frac{1-\kappa}{2},\qquad u_x=\frac{1+\kappa}{2}. \tag{35}
\]
The middle propensity in (34) is feasible, and the other two are feasible exactly when
\[
\left|q_{00}-\frac12\right|
=\frac{|c-\tau|}{2(1-\tau)}\leq\frac{\kappa}{2}, \tag{36}
\]
or \(|c-\tau|\leq\kappa(1-\tau)\). Substituting (33) into all six scalar bounds shows their intersection is nonempty under exactly this inequality, so (36) is exact fiber feasibility.

At \(v_\star\), the \(01\) assignment deviation is zero and the concordant deviations are opposite. Substitution in \(F_\tau\) yields
\[
C_P(\tau)=\frac{(c-\tau)^2}{1-\tau}. \tag{37}
\]
For \(\tau<0\), complementing both outcomes sends \((c,\tau)\) to \((-c,-\tau)\) and preserves cost. At \(\tau=0\), use \(\mathrm{prop{:}null\mbox{-}correlation\mbox{-}cost}\). Since \(c\in(-1,1)\), neither \(\tau=1\) nor \(\tau=-1\) matches the margins. Therefore
\[
D_P=\{\tau\in(-1,1):|c-\tau|\leq\kappa(1-|\tau|)\},\qquad
C_P(\tau)=\frac{(c-\tau)^2}{1-|\tau|}. \tag{38}
\]
For \(c=3/5\) and \(\Gamma=4\), \(\kappa=3/5\). On \(\tau<0\), feasibility would imply \(3/5-\tau\leq(3/5)(1+\tau)\), hence \(\tau\geq0\), a contradiction. On \(\tau\geq0\), (38) holds exactly for \(0\leq\tau\leq3/4\). Thus \(D_P=[0,3/4]\) and \(C_P(0)=9/25\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The family supplies a closed-form stress test for the full phase catalog and the infinite-sign-reversal boundary. \textit{Gap.} Published average-case bounds do not give this joint-response ATE profile or its truncated domain. \textit{Consumer.} The formula yields exact sensitivity intervals and sign-breakdown budgets without optimization.

\begin{theorem}[thm:strict-jensen-gap]\label{thm:strict-jensen-gap}
For any one-stratum full law \(Q\), define \(w_Q(y,1)=E_Q[q_{x,Y(0),Y(1)}^{-1}\mid Y(1)=y,Z=1]\) and \(w_Q(y,0)=E_Q[(1-q_{x,Y(0),Y(1)})^{-1}\mid Y(0)=y,Z=0]\). With Ishikawa--He--Kanamori's observed-policy-first divergence orientation and generator \(f_N\), \(Q\mapsto w_Q\) embeds every full-law ATE fiber into \(\mathcal W^{\mathrm{IHK}}_{\Gamma}(P)\), preserves its ATE, and satisfies \(\mathscr R_{\mathrm{IHK},P}(w_Q)\leq R_P(Q)\), while the unrelaxed divergence is exactly \(R_P(Q)\). The ATE slice is feasible in their Jensen--CMC program at budget \(\rho\) if and only if \(\widetilde C^{\mathrm{IHK}}_P(\tau)\leq\rho\), so \(\widetilde C^{\mathrm{IHK}}_P(\tau)\leq C_P(\tau)\) under the same odds cap and numerical budget. For \(e_x=1/2\), \(p_1=4/5\), \(p_0=1/5\), \(\Gamma=4\), and \(\tau=3/10\), the relaxed program reduces exactly to \(\min_{m_1-m_0=3/10}J(m_1,m_0)=9/91\), attained at \((m_1,m_0)=(13/20,7/20)\), whereas \(C_P(3/10)=9/70\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a one-stratum law and suppress the stratum subscript.  The overlap condition in \(\mathrm{thm{:}sharp\mbox{-}frontier}\) gives \(0<e_x<1\).  Let \(Q\in\mathcal H_\Gamma(P)\), and set
\[
s_{ij}=Q\{Y(0)=i,Y(1)=j\},
\qquad
q_{ij}=Q\{Z=1\mid Y(0)=i,Y(1)=j\}
\]
on every positive-mass response type.  By \(\mathrm{def{:}compatible\mbox{-}class}\),
\[
0<\ell_x\leq q_{ij}\leq u_x<1
\tag{1}
\]
whenever \(s_{ij}>0\), and consistency identifies \(Y\) with \(Y(1)\) when \(Z=1\) and with \(Y(0)\) when \(Z=0\).

Choose arbitrary versions within the declared boxes for the conditional expectations defining \(w_Q\) on null observed cells.  On positive observed cells, finite iterated summation gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
e_xE_P\{w_Q(Y,1)\mid Z=1\}
&=E_Q\{Z w_Q(Y(1),1)\}\\
&=E_Q\!\left\{\frac{Z}{q_{Y(0),Y(1)}}\right\}
=\sum_{i,j:s_{ij}>0}s_{ij}q_{ij}\frac{1}{q_{ij}}=1,
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
and similarly
\[
(1-e_x)E_P\{w_Q(Y,0)\mid Z=0\}
=E_Q\!\left\{\frac{1-Z}{1-q_{Y(0),Y(1)}}\right\}=1.
\tag{3}
\]
Because conditional averaging preserves intervals, (1) implies
\[
e_xw_Q(y,1)\in[e_x/u_x,e_x/\ell_x],
\qquad
(1-e_x)w_Q(y,0)
\in[(1-e_x)/(1-\ell_x),(1-e_x)/(1-u_x)].
\tag{4}
\]
Equations (2)--(4) are exactly the normalization and box restrictions in the current definition \(\mathrm{def{:}ihk\mbox{-}jensen\mbox{-}cmc\mbox{-}program}\).  Thus
\[
w_Q\in\mathcal W^{\mathrm{IHK}}_\Gamma(P).
\tag{5}
\]
This proves only a map into the note-defined box-augmented specialization; no reverse realizability assertion is made.

The same cancellation and the consistency restriction give
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_P\{Zw_Q(Y,1)Y\}
&=\sum_{i,j}s_{ij}q_{ij}\frac{j}{q_{ij}}
=E_Q\{Y(1)\},\\
E_P\{(1-Z)w_Q(Y,0)Y\}
&=\sum_{i,j}s_{ij}(1-q_{ij})\frac{i}{1-q_{ij}}
=E_Q\{Y(0)\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
Hence \(T(Q)=\tau\) implies that \(w_Q\) satisfies the ATE equality in \(\mathrm{def{:}ihk\mbox{-}jensen\mbox{-}cmc\mbox{-}program}\) at the same \(\tau\).

We next compare the two costs.  The response-type definitions give \(a_{ij}=s_{ij}q_{ij}\), \(b_{ij}=s_{ij}(1-q_{ij})\), and therefore
\[
d_{ij}=(1-e_x)a_{ij}-e_xb_{ij}=s_{ij}(q_{ij}-e_x).
\tag{7}
\]
The closed-perspective formula in \(\mathrm{def{:}assignment\mbox{-}cost}\) consequently becomes
\[
R_P(Q)=\sum_{i,j}s_{ij}\frac{(q_{ij}-e_x)^2}{e_x(1-e_x)},
\tag{8}
\]
where a zero-mass type contributes zero.  For the observed-policy-first orientation and \(f_N(r)=r^{-1}-1\), direct algebra on each positive-mass type gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
&q_{ij}f_N(e_x/q_{ij})
+(1-q_{ij})f_N((1-e_x)/(1-q_{ij}))\\
&\qquad=\frac{q_{ij}^2}{e_x}
+\frac{(1-q_{ij})^2}{1-e_x}-1
=\frac{(q_{ij}-e_x)^2}{e_x(1-e_x)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
Thus the corresponding unrelaxed latent-law divergence equals (8) exactly.

It remains to verify the Jensen contraction used by the exact displayed program.  For a finite probability vector \((\lambda_k)_k\), positive numbers \((r_k)_k\), and \(S=\sum_k\lambda_kr_k\), expansion and pairing of the \((k,l)\) and \((l,k)\) terms yields
\[
\sum_k\lambda_k f_N(r_k)-f_N(S)
=\frac{1}{S}\sum_{k<l}\lambda_k\lambda_l
\frac{(r_k-r_l)^2}{r_kr_l}\geq0.
\tag{10}
\]
Apply (10) separately within each positive observed \((Y,Z)\) cell, taking \(r=e_x/q_{ij}\) in a treated cell and \(r=(1-e_x)/(1-q_{ij})\) in a control cell.  By the definition of \(w_Q\), the respective cell averages of \(r\) are \(e_xw_Q(y,1)\) and \((1-e_x)w_Q(y,0)\).  Multiplication by the observed cell probabilities, summation over the cells, and (8)--(9) then give
\[
\mathscr R_{\mathrm{IHK},P}(w_Q)\leq R_P(Q).
\tag{11}
\]
Together, (5), (6), and (11) establish the asserted one-way, ATE-preserving, cost-nonincreasing embedding.  If the full-law ATE fiber is nonempty, taking infima in (11) gives
\[
\widetilde C^{\mathrm{IHK}}_P(\tau)\leq C_P(\tau);
\tag{12}
\]
if it is empty, the right side is \(+\infty\) by \(\mathrm{def{:}frontier}\), so (12) still holds in the extended-real order.

For completeness, consider the budget statement for the relaxed program.  Delete weight coordinates attached to null observed cells, which affect neither its constraints nor its objective.  Every remaining normalized coordinate \(e_xw(y,1)\) or \((1-e_x)w(y,0)\) lies in a closed bounded interval with a strictly positive lower endpoint by (1) and (4).  The two normalization equations and the ATE equation are closed affine restrictions.  Therefore any nonempty ATE slice is compact.  Since \(f_N\) is continuous on those positive boxes, a minimizing sequence has a convergent subsequence whose limit is feasible and attains the infimum.  If the slice is empty its infimum is \(+\infty\).  Hence the relaxed slice contains a weight of cost at most \(\rho\) if and only if \(\widetilde C^{\mathrm{IHK}}_P(\tau)\leq\rho\).  The corresponding full-law attainment and budget equivalence follow from the already established \(\mathrm{thm{:}sharp\mbox{-}frontier}\); (5)--(12) supply the same-budget implication into the relaxed specialization.

Now take \(e_x=1/2\), \(p_1=4/5\), \(p_0=1/5\), \(\Gamma=4\), and \(\tau=3/10\).  For a feasible weight define
\[

\]
and let \(m_z\) be the mean of \(Y\) under the normalized reweighting in arm \(z\).  Binary support and the two normalizations are equivalent to
\[
r_1(1)=\frac{m_1}{p_1},\quad
r_1(0)=\frac{1-m_1}{1-p_1},\qquad
r_0(1)=\frac{m_0}{p_0},\quad
r_0(0)=\frac{1-m_0}{1-p_0}.
\tag{13}
\]
For \(p,m\in(0,1)\), substitution into the armwise Neyman objective gives

\begin{aligned}
pf_N(m/p)+(1-p)f_N((1-m)/(1-p))
&=\frac{p^2}{m}+\frac{(1-p)^2}{1-m}-1\\
&=\frac{(m-p)^2}{m(1-m)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
Because both treatment-arm probabilities equal \(1/2\), the exact objective in \(\mathrm{def{:}ihk\mbox{-}jensen\mbox{-}cmc\mbox{-}program}\) is therefore

J(m_1,m_0)
=\frac12\frac{(m_1-4/5)^2}{m_1(1-m_1)}
+\frac12\frac{(m_0-1/5)^2}{m_0(1-m_0)},
\tag{15}
\]
and its ATE equality is \(m_1-m_0=3/10\).

Here \(\ell_x=1/5\) and \(u_x=4/5\), so both normalized-weight boxes in the current definition are \([5/8,5/2]\).  Applying this box to all four coordinates in (13) is equivalent to

m_1\in[1/2,7/8],\qquad m_0\in[1/8,1/2].
\tag{16}
\]
Writing \(m_0=x\) and \(m_1=x+3/10\), the exact feasible ATE slice in (16) is \(x\in[1/5,1/2]\).

For \(g_p(m)=(m-p)^2/\{m(1-m)\}\), the representation in (14) yields

g_p''(m)=\frac{2p^2}{m^3}+\frac{2(1-p)^2}{(1-m)^3}>0.
\tag{17}
\]
Thus the sliced objective is strictly convex.  The identity \(g_{4/5}(m)=g_{1/5}(1-m)\) makes its derivative vanish at
\[
(m_1,m_0)=(13/20,7/20),
\tag{18}
\]
which lies in the relative interior of (16), so it is the unique minimizer.  Evaluation gives
\[
\widetilde C^{\mathrm{IHK}}_P(3/10)
=J(13/20,7/20)
=\frac{(3/20)^2}{(13/20)(7/20)}
=\frac9{91}.
\tag{19}
\]
By the already established \(\mathrm{thm{:}symmetric\mbox{-}family}\), the same observed law and odds cap satisfy
\[
C_P(3/10)
=\frac{(3/5-3/10)^2}{1-3/10}
=\frac9{70}.
\tag{20}
\]
Consequently

C_P(3/10)-\widetilde C^{\mathrm{IHK}}_P(3/10)
=\frac9{70}-\frac9{91}
=\frac{27}{910}>0.
\tag{21}
\]
This proves the exact strict separation for the current box-augmented program and completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:regular-profile-diameter]\label{thm:regular-profile-diameter}
On \(\Theta_{\mathrm{reg}}(\delta)\), the explicit dual implies \(L_{\mathrm{reg}}(\delta)<\infty\). If the true \(\theta\in\Theta_{\mathrm{reg}}(\delta)\), then under independent sampling, with probability at least \(1-\alpha\), simultaneously for every \(t\in\mathcal T_0\), \(C_P(t)\in[\underline C_n(t),\overline C_n(t)]\) and \(0\leq\overline C_n(t)-\underline C_n(t)\leq2L_{\mathrm{reg}}(\delta)r_n\). \end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(\eta\in\Theta_{\mathrm{reg}}(\delta)\), and let \(P_\eta\) be the observed law whose multinomial vector is \(\eta\).  For every \(x\in\mathcal X\), define the unconditional treated-arm, control-arm, and stratum masses
\[
r_x(\eta)=\sum_{y\in\mathcal Y}\eta_{x1y},\qquad
c_x(\eta)=\sum_{y\in\mathcal Y}\eta_{x0y},\qquad

\tag{61}

The observed-cell condition in \(\mathrm{def{:}regular\mbox{-}interior\mbox{-}region}\) gives

\tag{62}
\]

e_x(\eta)=\frac{r_x(\eta)}{p_x(\eta)}
\geq r_x(\eta)\geq\kappa_\delta,
\qquad
1-e_x(\eta)=\frac{c_x(\eta)}{p_x(\eta)}
\geq c_x(\eta)\geq\kappa_\delta.
\tag{63}
\]
In particular,
\[
e_x(\eta)\in[\kappa_\delta,1-\kappa_\delta]
\quad\text{uniformly over }\eta\in\Theta_{\mathrm{reg}}(\delta).
\tag{64}
\]
This is the cross-law overlap bound used below.  It is derived from the regular-region cell floor; \(\mathrm{ass{:}overlap}\), which supplies overlap for the target observed law, is not transferred to other profiled laws.

We first put all programs on a fixed cone.  For every response-type cell, write

h_{xij}=\frac{a_{xij}}{e_x(\eta)},
\qquad
k_{xij}=\frac{b_{xij}}{1-e_x(\eta)}.
\tag{65}

\[
\frac{q_{xij}/(1-q_{xij})}{e_x(\eta)/(1-e_x(\eta))}
=\frac{h_{xij}}{k_{xij}}.
\tag{66}

Thus, with the closed-cone convention at a zero cell, the odds restriction in \(\mathrm{def{:}compatible\mbox{-}class}\) is equivalent to membership in the parameter-independent polyhedral cone
\[
\mathcal W_\Gamma
=\left\{(h,k):
h_{xij}\geq0,\ k_{xij}\geq0,\
\Gamma h_{xij}-k_{xij}\geq0,\
\Gamma k_{xij}-h_{xij}\geq0
\text{ for every }x,i,j\right\}.
\tag{67}
\]
Observational compatibility and consistency, as collected in \(\mathrm{def{:}compatible\mbox{-}class}\), transform the observed-margin and ATE equations into
\[
\begin{aligned}
r_x(\eta)\sum_i h_{xij}&=\eta_{x1j},\\
c_x(\eta)\sum_j k_{xij}&=\eta_{x0i},\\
\sum_{x,i,j}(j-i)
\{r_x(\eta)h_{xij}+c_x(\eta)k_{xij}\}&=t.
\end{aligned}
\tag{68}
\]
Denote (68) by \(A_\eta w=b_\eta(t)\), where \(w=(h,k)\).  From \(\mathrm{def{:}assignment\mbox{-}cost}\),
\[
F_\eta(w)
=\sum_{x,i,j}
\frac{r_x(\eta)c_x(\eta)(h_{xij}-k_{xij})^2}
{r_x(\eta)h_{xij}+c_x(\eta)k_{xij}},
\tag{69}
\]
with the closed perspective value zero when \(h_{xij}=k_{xij}=0\).  Indeed,
\[
d_{xij}=e_x(\eta)\{1-e_x(\eta)\}(h_{xij}-k_{xij}),
\qquad
s_{xij}=e_x(\eta)h_{xij}+\{1-e_x(\eta)\}k_{xij},
\tag{70}
\]
and multiplying the corresponding summand by \(p_x(\eta)\) yields (69).  The exact SOCP representation in \(\mathrm{def{:}socp}\), the attained frontier in \(\mathrm{thm{:}sharp\mbox{-}frontier}\), and the bijection (65) therefore give

C_{P_\eta}(t)
=\min\{F_\eta(w):w\in\mathcal W_\Gamma, A_\eta w=b_\eta(t)\}.
\tag{71}
\]
Summing the first two groups of equations in (68) and using (62) shows that every feasible \(w\) satisfies
\[
\sum_{i,j}h_{xij}=1,qquad
\sum_{i,j}k_{xij}=1,qquad
0\leq h_{xij},k_{xij}\leq1.
\tag{72}
\]

We next obtain a uniform bound on attained dual multipliers.  If \(\Theta_{\mathrm{reg}}(\delta)\) is empty, the deterministic assertion is vacuous.  Otherwise, take the cone in (67) in its affine hull after deleting redundant faces.  The strict feasible array in \(\mathrm{def{:}regular\mbox{-}interior\mbox{-}region}\) has at least the declared slack \(\delta\) in every nonredundant original response-mass and odds-face inequality. \end{aligned}
\tag{73}

Moreover \(a_{xij}=e_x(\eta)h_{xij}\) and \(b_{xij}=\{1-e_x(\eta)\}k_{xij}\), and both diagonal factors lie in \([\kappa_\delta,1-\kappa_\delta]\).  Hence the declared original slack yields a positive, computable lower bound on every normalized nonredundant face slack of (67).  Dividing by the largest Euclidean norm among the finitely many face normals gives a computable \(\gamma_\delta>0\) such that the transformed strict feasible point is at distance at least \(\gamma_\delta\) from the relative boundary of \(\mathcal W_\Gamma\).  This construction depends only on \(\delta,\Gamma,K\), and \(|\mathcal X|\), through the derived bound \(\kappa_\delta=K\delta\).

The equality operator in (68) has full row rank.  The treated- and control-margin rows have disjoint variable supports and nonzero coefficients, bounded below by \(\kappa_\delta\) by (62).  The ATE row is not in their span: for any fixed \(x\), the vector whose only nonzero coordinates are \(h_{x,0,0}=1\) and \(h_{x,1,0}=-1\) is orthogonal to every margin row but has inner product \(r_x(\eta)\geq\kappa_\delta\) with the ATE row. \Delta_\delta

\ \sum_{x,z,y}\eta_{xzy}=1\right\},

the entries of \(A_\eta\) are continuous.  Since full row rank holds at every point of \(\Delta_\delta\), continuity of the smallest singular value gives the computable constant

\]
Thus the right inverse
\[
R_\eta=A_\eta^{\mathsf T}(A_\eta A_\eta^{\mathsf T})^{-1}
\tag{77}
\]
satisfies \(\|R_\eta\|_{2\to2}\leq\sigma_\delta^{-1}\).  If \(\Gamma=1\), the odds restriction forces \(q_{xij}=e_x\) and identifies a single ATE; hence \(D_P\) is a singleton and no nonempty \(\mathcal T_0\subset\operatorname{int}D_P\) exists.  The theorem is then vacuous.  We henceforth consider the nonvacuous case.

For \(y=(\alpha,\beta,\lambda)\), use the Lagrangian
\[
\mathcal L_\eta(w;y)
=F_\eta(w)+y^{\mathsf T}\{b_\eta(t)-A_\eta w\}.
\tag{78}
\]
Cellwise minimization of (78) on \(\mathcal W_\Gamma\) is precisely the constraint in \(\mathrm{def{:}dual}\).  Indeed,
\[
r_xh_{xij}+c_xk_{xij}=p_xs_{xij},
\qquad
r_xh_{xij}=p_xs_{xij}q_{xij},
\tag{79}
\]
so the coefficient on a cell ray is
\[
p_xs_{xij}\left\{
\frac{(q_{xij}-e_x)^2}{e_x(1-e_x)}
-q_{xij}\alpha_{xj}-(1-q_{xij})\beta_{xi}
-\lambda(j-i)\right\}.
\tag{80}
\]
Therefore \(\mathrm{thm{:}explicit\mbox{-}dual}\) supplies an attained multiplier \(y_\eta(t)\) with
\[
C_{P_\eta}(t)
=\inf_{w\in\mathcal W_\Gamma}
\mathcal L_\eta(w;y_\eta(t)).
\tag{81}
\]

Let \(w^\circ\) be the uniformly strict feasible point, put
\(
\tau_\delta=\gamma_\delta\sigma_\delta/2
\), and let \(z\) be a unit vector in the equality space.  Equations (76)--(77) imply
\[
w^\pm=w^\circ\pm\tau_\delta R_\eta z\in\mathcal W_\Gamma,
\qquad
A_\eta w^\pm=b_\eta(t)\pm\tau_\delta z.
\tag{82}
\]
The vectors \(w^\pm\) range over a compact set: (72) bounds \(w^\circ\), while the perturbation has Euclidean norm at most \(\gamma_\delta/2\).  For \(r,c\geq\kappa_\delta\) and \(h,k\geq0\),
\[
0\leq\frac{rc(h-k)^2}{rh+ck}
\leq\frac{(h+k)^2}{\kappa_\delta(h+k)}
=\frac{h+k}{\kappa_\delta},
\tag{83}
\]
where the zero case uses the closed perspective convention.  Thus (69), (82), and (83) give a computable uniform \(\overline F_\delta<\infty\) such that \(F_\eta(w^\pm)\leq\overline F_\delta\).  Evaluating (81) at \(w^+\) and \(w^-\), respectively, and using \(C_{P_\eta}(t)\geq0\), yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau_\delta y_\eta(t)^{\mathsf T}z
&\leq F_\eta(w^+)-C_{P_\eta}(t)\leq\overline F_\delta,\\
-\tau_\delta y_\eta(t)^{\mathsf T}z
&\leq F_\eta(w^-)-C_{P_\eta}(t)\leq\overline F_\delta.
\end{aligned}
\tag{84}
\]
Taking the supremum over unit \(z\), and converting Euclidean to \(\ell_1\) norm in the \(d=2|\mathcal X|K+1\) dimensional equality space, gives
\[
\|y_\eta(t)\|_1
\leq B_\delta
:=\frac{\sqrt d\,\overline F_\delta}{\tau_\delta}<\infty
\tag{85}
\]
uniformly over \(\eta\in\Theta_{\mathrm{reg}}(\delta)\) and \(t\in\mathcal T_0\).  Equations (63), (73)--(77), and (83)--(85) are an explicit finite-dimensional recipe for \(B_\delta\); no active-set stability is used.

We now compare two observed laws without transporting a dual certificate.  Let \(\eta,\eta'\in\Theta_{\mathrm{reg}}(\delta)\), fix \(t\in\mathcal T_0\), put \(d_\infty=\|\eta-\eta'\|_\infty\), and let \(w'\) be a primal optimizer of (71) for \((\eta',t)\).  Define
\[
\psi_{r,c}(h,k)=\frac{rc(h-k)^2}{rh+ck}.
\]
On \(r,c\geq\kappa_\delta\), direct differentiation, \(|h-k|\leq h+k\), and \(rh+ck\geq\kappa_\delta(h+k)\) give
\[
|\partial_r\psi_{r,c}(h,k)|
\leq\frac{h+k}{\kappa_\delta}+\frac{h}{\kappa_\delta^2},
\qquad
|\partial_c\psi_{r,c}(h,k)|
\leq\frac{h+k}{\kappa_\delta}+\frac{k}{\kappa_\delta^2}.
\tag{86}
\]
At \(h=k=0\), the function is identically zero in \((r,c)\), so the same bounds hold.  Each arm mass changes by at most \(Kd_\infty\).  Summing (86) and using (72) therefore yields
\[
|F_\eta(w')-F_{\eta'}(w')|
\leq K_Fd_\infty,
\qquad
K_F=2|\mathcal X|K
\left(\frac{2}{\kappa_\delta}+\frac{1}{\kappa_\delta^2}\right).
\tag{87}
\]
Likewise, direct use of (68) and (72) gives
\[
\left\|
\{b_\eta(t)-A_\eta w'\}
-\{b_{\eta'}(t)-A_{\eta'}w'\}
\right\|_\infty
\leq K_A d_\infty,
\tag{88}
\]
where one may take
\[
K_A=\max\{1+K,\,2|\mathcal X|K(K-1)\}.
\tag{89}
\]
For a margin row, the bound in (88) is \(d_\infty+Kd_\infty\).  For the ATE row, it is at most \((K-1)\sum_x\{|r_x-r_x'|+|c_x-c_x'|\}\), proving (88)--(89).

Use the attained dual \(y_\eta(t)\) only in its own saddle identity (81), and evaluate that identity at \(w'\).  Since \(w'\in\mathcal W_\Gamma\), \(b_{\eta'}(t)-A_{\eta'}w'=0\), and \(F_{\eta'}(w')=C_{P_{\eta'}}(t)\), equations (81), (85), (87), and (88) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
C_{P_\eta}(t)
&\leq\mathcal L_\eta(w';y_\eta(t))\\
&=C_{P_{\eta'}}(t)
+F_\eta(w')-F_{\eta'}(w')
+y_\eta(t)^{\mathsf T}\{b_\eta(t)-A_\eta w'\}\\
&\leq C_{P_{\eta'}}(t)
+(K_F+B_\delta K_A)d_\infty.
\end{aligned}
\tag{90}
\]
Interchanging \(\eta\) and \(\eta'\) proves
\[
|C_{P_\eta}(t)-C_{P_{\eta'}}(t)|
\leq(K_F+B_\delta K_A)\|\eta-\eta'\|_\infty.
\tag{91}
\]
This is a cross-parameter saddle comparison on the common cone (67); no multiplier for one law is claimed feasible for the other law.  By \(\mathrm{def{:}regular\mbox{-}profile\mbox{-}band}\),
\[
L_{\mathrm{reg}}(\delta)
\leq K_F+B_\delta K_A<\infty.
\tag{92}
\]
For example,
\[
\mathfrak B_\delta=(1+K_F+K_A)(1+B_\delta)
\tag{93}
\]
is a computable uniform \(\ell_1\)-bound for the attained cellwise dual multipliers and also bounds the modulus in (92).  This proves the deterministic and computability assertions.

It remains to prove the simultaneous finite-sample statement.  Fix a multinomial coordinate, let \(U\) be its cell indicator, and put \(p=E_PU\).  For
\[
g(\lambda)=\log E_P\exp\{\lambda(U-p)\},
\]
we have \(g(0)=g'(0)=0\), while \(g''(\lambda)\) is the variance of a Bernoulli variable under exponential tilting and is at most \(1/4\).  Integrating twice gives
\[
E_P\exp\{\lambda(U-p)\}\leq\exp(\lambda^2/8).
\tag{94}
\]
Independence in \(\mathrm{ass{:}iid\mbox{-}sampling}\), Markov's inequality, and optimization at \(\lambda=4r_n\) then give
\[
P\{|\widehat\theta_k-\theta_k|>r_n\}
\leq2\exp(-2nr_n^2)=\frac{\alpha}{M}.
\tag{95}
\]
A union bound over the \(M\) cells proves
\[
P\{\|\widehat\theta-\theta\|_\infty\leq r_n\}
\geq1-\alpha.
\tag{96}
\]
On the event in (96), the maintained membership \(\theta\in\Theta_{\mathrm{reg}}(\delta)\) implies \(\theta\in\mathcal A_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\).  The definitions of the two envelopes therefore give, simultaneously for every \(t\in\mathcal T_0\),
\[
\underline C_n(t)\leq C_P(t)\leq\overline C_n(t).
\tag{97}
\]
Any two multinomial vectors in \(\mathcal A_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\) are at \(\ell_\infty\)-distance at most \(2r_n\).  Applying the definition of \(L_{\mathrm{reg}}(\delta)\), and then taking the supremum and infimum over this same nonempty set, gives on (96), again simultaneously in \(t\),
\[
0\leq\overline C_n(t)-\underline C_n(t)
\leq2L_{\mathrm{reg}}(\delta)r_n.
\tag{98}
\]
Equations (92), (96), (97), and (98) establish every assertion of the theorem.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This theorem converts multinomial concentration into an honest finite-sample simultaneous band with \(O(n^{-1/2})\) diameter. For every profiled law in the named regular region, summing the positive cells in each treatment arm gives the explicit cross-law bounds \(e_x\geq K\delta\) and \(1-e_x\geq K\delta\); these bounds support the common-cone scaling, coefficient estimates, equality right inverse, and attained-multiplier bound. \textit{Gap.} ZhangZhao2026AverageCase gives regular bands for armwise sensitivity curves; it does not control the finite-sample diameter of a jointly compatible ATE cost frontier through an observed-cell-derived cross-law overlap bound, a uniform fixed-cone Slater radius, and a law-specific saddle comparison. \textit{Consumer.} The categorical-mercury analysis obtains a finite-width simultaneous frontier band whenever its prespecified cells and ATE range satisfy the reported observed-cell, cone-slack, and equality-rank margins; it need not assume that the true-law overlap constant automatically applies to every profiled multinomial law.

\begin{theorem}[thm:boundary-inference-lower-bound]\label{thm:boundary-inference-lower-bound}
Fix \(\Gamma=4\), \(h>0\), and \(0<\alpha<1/2\). At \(\theta_0\), \(C_{P_{\theta_0}}(0)=9/25\), whereas at \(\theta_{n,h}\), \(C_{P_{\theta_{n,h}}}(0)=+\infty\) for every sufficiently large \(n\), and the total variation distance between \(P_{\theta_0}^{\otimes n}\) and \(P_{\theta_{n,h}}^{\otimes n}\) tends to zero. Consequently, any random set \(\mathcal C_n\) satisfying \(P_{\vartheta}^{\otimes n}\{C_{P_{\vartheta}}(0)\in\mathcal C_n\}\geq1-\alpha\) for both \(\vartheta\in\{\theta_0,\theta_{n,h}\}\) obeys \(\liminf_{n\to\infty}P_{\theta_0}^{\otimes n}\{\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal C_n)=+\infty\}\geq1-2\alpha\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At \(\theta_0\), \(\mathrm{thm{:}symmetric\mbox{-}family}\) with \(c=3/5\) and \(\Gamma=4\) gives
\[
C_{P_{\theta_0}}(0)=\frac9{25}. \tag{61}
\]
For \(\theta_{n,h}\), \(m=1/2+h/(2n)\). If the null were feasible, \(\mathrm{prop{:}null\mbox{-}correlation\mbox{-}cost}\) would require
\[
q_{11}=\frac{(1/2)(4/5+h/n)}{1/2+h/(2n)}
=\frac{4/5+h/n}{1+h/n}\leq u_x=\frac45. \tag{62}
\]
But
\[
\frac{4/5+h/n}{1+h/n}-\frac45
=\frac{h}{5n(1+h/n)}>0. \tag{63}
\]
Thus the null odds cap fails and \(C_{P_{\theta_{n,h}}}(0)=+\infty\) for every sufficiently large \(n\) for which the stated probabilities lie in \([0,1]\).

Only the two treated outcome cells change from \(\theta_0\), each by \(h/(2n)\), and all four baseline cells are positive. Hence
\[
\chi^2(P_{\theta_{n,h}},P_{\theta_0})
=\sum_k\frac{(\theta_{n,h,k}-\theta_{0,k})^2}{\theta_{0,k}}
=O(n^{-2}). \tag{64}
\]
Expanding the product likelihood ratio and using independence gives
\[
1+\chi^2(P_{\theta_{n,h}}^{\otimes n},P_{\theta_0}^{\otimes n})
=\{1+\chi^2(P_{\theta_{n,h}},P_{\theta_0})\}^{n}
=1+O(n^{-1}). \tag{65}
\]
For likelihood ratio \(L=dP/dQ\), Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
\|P-Q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
=\frac12E_Q|L-1|
\leq\frac12\{E_Q(L-1)^2\}^{1/2}. \tag{66}
\]
Equations (64)--(66) prove that the product total variation distance tends to zero.

Let \(A_n=\{9/25\in\mathcal C_n\}\) and \(B_n=\{+\infty\in\mathcal C_n\}\). Coverage gives
\[
P_{\theta_0}^{\otimes n}(A_n)\geq1-\alpha,\qquad
P_{\theta_{n,h}}^{\otimes n}(B_n)\geq1-\alpha. \tag{67}
\]
Total-variation convergence transfers the second inequality to
\[
P_{\theta_0}^{\otimes n}(B_n)\geq1-\alpha-o(1). \tag{68}
\]
The union bound gives \(P_{\theta_0}^{\otimes n}(A_n\cap B_n)\geq1-2\alpha-o(1)\). A set containing both \(9/25\) and \(+\infty\) has infinite extended-real diameter. Taking the lower limit proves the claim.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The contiguous two-point argument quantifies why no uniformly honest finite-width interval can cross the finite-to-infinite null-feasibility boundary. \textit{Gap.} Standard directional-bootstrap theory presumes a finite local parameter; it does not provide this extended-real boundary impossibility result for a sensitivity frontier. \textit{Consumer.} An analyst is told when an infinite upper endpoint is statistically unavoidable rather than a numerical failure of the profile algorithm.

\begin{proposition}[prop:gamma-one-regression]\label{prop:gamma-one-regression}
Regression corollary: when \(\Gamma=1\), \(D_P=\{\Delta_P\}\), \(C_P(\Delta_P)=0\), and \(\mathcal I_P(\rho)=\{\Delta_P\}\) for every \(\rho\geq0\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
When \(\Gamma=1\), \(\ell_x=u_x=e_x\). Every positive response-type cell under a compatible law therefore has \(q_{xij}=e_x\), so \(Z\) is conditionally independent of \((Y(0),Y(1))\) given \(X=x\). Observational compatibility and consistency imply
\[
E_Q[Y(1)\mid X=x]=\sum_jj\pi_{1x,j},\qquad
E_Q[Y(0)\mid X=x]=\sum_ii\pi_{0x,i}. \tag{69}
\]
Summing with weights \(p_x\) gives \(T(Q)=\Delta_P\) for every \(Q\in\mathcal H_1(P)\). Also
\[
d_{xij}=s_{xij}(q_{xij}-e_x)=0,
\]
so \(R_P(Q)=0\).

The class is nonempty. In each stratum, couple \(Y(0)\) and \(Y(1)\) with marginals \(\pi_{0x,\cdot}\) and \(\pi_{1x,\cdot}\), for example by a product coupling, and draw \(Z\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(e_x)\) independently. The margin equations and consistency verify compatibility. Consequently
\[
D_P=\{\Delta_P\},\qquad C_P(\Delta_P)=0,\qquad
\mathcal I_P(\rho)=\{\Delta_P\}\quad\text{for every }\rho\geq0.
\]
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This regression corollary checks that the frontier collapses to the standard identified ATE when hidden assignment distortion is excluded. \textit{Gap.} This is a required sanity reduction rather than a novelty claim. \textit{Consumer.} Software and applied analyses obtain the usual observed-law ATE exactly at the no-confounding benchmark.

\begin{theorem}[thm:local-uniform-strict-jensen-gap]\label{thm:local-uniform-strict-jensen-gap}
Fix \(\Gamma=4\) and the ATE slice \(3/10\).  There exists \(\eta>0\) such that every one-stratum binary observed law \(P\) satisfying
\[
 \max\{|e_x-1/2|,|p_1-4/5|,|p_0-1/5|\}<\eta
\]
has finite full-law and box-augmented Jensen--CMC frontier values at \(3/10\) and obeys
\[
 C_P(3/10)-\widetilde C^{\mathrm{IHK}}_P(3/10)\geq\frac{27}{1820}>0.
\]
Hence the exact rational witness belongs to a nonempty open Euclidean neighborhood of observed laws on which the compatible-full-law cost uniformly and strictly exceeds the value of the explicitly defined box-augmented Neyman specialization of Ishikawa--He--Kanamori equation~(14).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Parameterize a one-stratum binary observed law by
\[
\theta=(e,p_1,p_0)\in(0,1)^3,
\]
where \(e=e_x\).  Its four observable probabilities, in the order \((Z,Y)=(1,1),(1,0),(0,1),(0,0)\), are
\[
ep_1,\qquad e(1-p_1),\qquad(1-e)p_0,\qquad(1-e)(1-p_0).
\tag{22}
\]
On the positive-cell region the inverse coordinates are
\[
e=P(Z=1),\qquad
p_1=\frac{P(Z=1,Y=1)}{P(Z=1)},\qquad
p_0=\frac{P(Z=0,Y=1)}{P(Z=0)}.
\tag{23}
\]
Thus \(\theta\) is a homeomorphic coordinate system on the interior of the one-stratum observed-law simplex.  Put
\[
\theta_\star=(1/2,4/5,1/5),
\qquad \tau_\star=3/10.
\]
Write \(C_\theta\) and \(\widetilde C^{\mathrm{IHK}}_\theta\) for the two frontiers at the law indexed by \(\theta\).  The proved \(\mathrm{thm{:}strict\mbox{-}jensen\mbox{-}gap}\) gives
\[
C_{\theta_\star}(\tau_\star)=\frac9{70},\qquad
\widetilde C^{\mathrm{IHK}}_{\theta_\star}(\tau_\star)=\frac9{91},\qquad
g_\star:=\frac9{70}-\frac9{91}=\frac{27}{910}.
\tag{24}
\]

We first prove continuity of the full-law value at \(\theta_\star\), without assuming that an odds face remains active.  At \(\tau=\tau_\star\) and \(\Gamma=4\), write

A_\theta=e(1-p_1),\qquad
B_\theta=(1-e)p_0,
\qquad
D_\theta=1-ep_1-(1-e)p_0,

and evaluate the formulas in \(\mathrm{def{:}binary\mbox{-}scalar\mbox{-}program}\) at these quantities.  At \(\theta_\star\),

A_{\theta_\star}=B_{\theta_\star}=\frac1{10},
\qquad D_{\theta_\star}=\frac12,
\qquad \ell=\frac15,

The three lower-face values are \(1/8,1/5,13/50\), and the three upper-face values are \(1/2,23/40,11/25\).  Hence

[L_{\theta_\star},U_{\theta_\star}]=[13/50,11/25].
\tag{25}

The point \(v_\star=7/20\) is at distance \(9/100\) from both endpoints, and direct substitution gives

&=\frac9{70}=C_{\theta_\star}(\tau_\star),

where the last equality is (24).

Each candidate face defining \(L_\theta\) and \(U_\theta\) is continuous at \(\theta_\star\), because \(e\in(0,1)\) implies \(0<\ell(e)<u(e)<1\); a finite maximum or minimum preserves continuity.  Hence, on a neighborhood of \(\theta_\star\), \(v_\star\) remains feasible and all feasible intervals lie in a common compact interval on which \(v\), \(1-\tau_\star-v\), \(e\), and \(1-e\) are bounded away from zero.  The displayed formula for \(F_\tau\) is jointly continuous there.

Let \(\theta_n\to\theta_\star\).  Feasibility of \(v_\star\), (26), and the exact scalar characterization in the already established \(\mathrm{thm{:}binary\mbox{-}phase\mbox{-}catalog}\) give

Equations (27)--(28) prove continuity of \(C_\theta(\tau_\star)\) at \(\theta_\star\).

as the lower and upper endpoints for \(r_1\) and \(r_0\), respectively.  With \(p_z=p_1\) for \(z=1\) and \(p_z=p_0\) for \(z=0\), define

Writing \(x=m_0\) and \(m_1=x+\tau_\star\), the relaxed ATE slice is therefore exactly

At \(\theta_\star\), equations (29)--(33) give

The minimizer \(x_\star=7/20\) established in \(\mathrm{thm{:}strict\mbox{-}jensen\mbox{-}gap}\) is at distance \(3/20\) from both endpoints and satisfies

All endpoints in (33) are continuous by (29)--(30).  Near (35), the quantities \(x\), \(1-x\), \(x+\tau_\star\), and \(1-x-\tau_\star\) are uniformly positive, so (34) is jointly continuous on a common compact interval.  Repeating the subsequence argument in (27)--(28), now using the strictly feasible point \(x_\star\) for the upper limit and minimizers in (33) for the lower limit, proves

By (24), (27)--(28), and (37), there is \(\eta_0>0\) such that throughout the strict cube

both scalar intervals are nonempty and

\left|C_\theta(\tau_\star)-\frac9{70}\right|<\frac{g_\star}{4},
\qquad
\left|\widetilde C^{\mathrm{IHK}}_\theta(\tau_\star)-\frac9{91}\right|<\frac{g_\star}{4}.

Choose

For every \(\theta\in B_\eta\),

 e\geq\frac12-\eta,\qquad 1-e\geq\frac12-\eta,\\

p_z(1-p_z)

       \left(\frac45-\eta\right)>0
\quad(z=0,1),
\qquad
e(1-e)\geq\left(\frac12-\eta\right)^2>0.
\tag{42}
\]
Thus this neighborhood is uniformly separated from every observable support boundary relevant to either scalar program.

The cube \(B_\eta\) is nonempty and has three-dimensional Lebesgue volume \((2\eta)^3>0\).  For the map from \(\theta\) to the first three cell probabilities in (22), direct differentiation gives the absolute Jacobian determinant
\[
e(1-e)\geq\left(\frac12-\eta\right)^2>0.
\tag{43}
\]
Together with the continuous inverse (23), this shows that the image of \(B_\eta\) is a nonempty open Euclidean neighborhood in the interior of the observed-law simplex.  It contains a Euclidean ball and consequently has positive three-dimensional volume; it is not a lower-dimensional path or face.

Finally, subtracting the two inequalities in (38) and using (24) yields, for every \(\theta\in B_\eta\),
\[
C_\theta(3/10)-\widetilde C^{\mathrm{IHK}}_\theta(3/10)
\geq g_\star-\frac{g_\star}{2}
=\frac{27}{1820}>0.
\tag{44}
\]
The preserved strictly feasible points \(v_\star\) and \(x_\star\) prove that both values are finite throughout this neighborhood.  Equations (39)--(44) prove the asserted locally uniform strict separation, the positive support bounds, and the positive-volume claim.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Strictly interior minimizers in both finite-dimensional scalar programs make the exact witness robust: continuity preserves at least half of its rational cost gap on a nonempty open neighborhood. \textit{Gap.} A single numerical or rational witness could be a knife-edge phenomenon; the local theorem rules this out without assuming fixed active odds faces throughout the neighborhood. \textit{Consumer.} The comparison remains informative under small multinomial sampling perturbations of the treated fraction and the two observed arm success probabilities.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The interior binary stationarity equation is generically quintic, so the exact catalog isolates a unique algebraic root and does not promise universal radical formulas. The regular-profile constant depends on both the fixed-cone interior radius and the smallest singular value of the equality operator, and can deteriorate as the observed-cell or Slater margin \(\delta\) approaches zero. The boundary lower bound shows that this deterioration reflects a genuine statistical discontinuity at the null-feasibility boundary.

\section{Honest scope}

The sharpness, dual attainment, garbling, scalar catalog, one-way cost transfer into the explicitly defined box-augmented Neyman specialization, exact rational separation, open-neighborhood uniform gap, regular-region finite-sample diameter, and boundary impossibility are theorem claims proved in the note. The comparison does not identify that specialization with Ishikawa--He--Kanamori's unqualified published class and does not assert that every relaxed armwise weight is induced by a compatible cross-world assignment law. The regular diameter proof controls coefficient movement through a common fixed odds cone and a law-specific saddle comparison; it does not claim stability of an active set or feasibility of one law's dual certificate at another law. Uniform tangent inference under weaker facial regularity and adaptive derivative-bootstrap inversion remain open. The \(\Gamma=1\) specialization is retained only as a regression corollary. The application target remains a prespecified categorical mercury score and finite covariate stratification; it does not claim to recover the original continuous-outcome NHANES estimand.
