Fix \(\eta\in\Theta_{\mathrm{reg}}(\delta)\), and let \(P_\eta\) be the observed law whose multinomial vector is \(\eta\).  For every \(x\in\mathcal X\), define the unconditional treated-arm, control-arm, and stratum masses
\[
r_x(\eta)=\sum_{y\in\mathcal Y}\eta_{x1y},\qquad
c_x(\eta)=\sum_{y\in\mathcal Y}\eta_{x0y},\qquad
p_x(\eta)=r_x(\eta)+c_x(\eta).
\tag{61}
\]
The observed-cell condition in \(\mathrm{def{:}regular\mbox{-}interior\mbox{-}region}\) gives
\[
r_x(\eta)\geq K\delta,qquad
c_x(\eta)\geq K\delta,qquad
p_x(\eta)\geq2K\delta.
\tag{62}
\]
Set \(\kappa_\delta=K\delta\).  Since \(p_x(\eta)\leq1\), (62) implies, for every candidate law in the regular region,
\[
e_x(\eta)=\frac{r_x(\eta)}{p_x(\eta)}
\geq r_x(\eta)\geq\kappa_\delta,
\qquad
1-e_x(\eta)=\frac{c_x(\eta)}{p_x(\eta)}
\geq c_x(\eta)\geq\kappa_\delta.
\tag{63}
\]
In particular,
\[
e_x(\eta)\in[\kappa_\delta,1-\kappa_\delta]
\quad\text{uniformly over }\eta\in\Theta_{\mathrm{reg}}(\delta).
\tag{64}
\]
This is the cross-law overlap bound used below.  It is derived from the regular-region cell floor; \(\mathrm{ass{:}overlap}\), which supplies overlap for the target observed law, is not transferred to other profiled laws.

We first put all programs on a fixed cone.  For every response-type cell, write
\[
h_{xij}=\frac{a_{xij}}{e_x(\eta)},
\qquad
k_{xij}=\frac{b_{xij}}{1-e_x(\eta)}.
\tag{65}
\]
The divisions are valid by (64).  On a positive response-type cell,
\[
\frac{q_{xij}/(1-q_{xij})}{e_x(\eta)/(1-e_x(\eta))}
=\frac{h_{xij}}{k_{xij}}.
\tag{66}
\]
Thus, with the closed-cone convention at a zero cell, the odds restriction in \(\mathrm{def{:}compatible\mbox{-}class}\) is equivalent to membership in the parameter-independent polyhedral cone
\[
\mathcal W_\Gamma
=\left\{(h,k):
h_{xij}\geq0,\ k_{xij}\geq0,\
\Gamma h_{xij}-k_{xij}\geq0,\
\Gamma k_{xij}-h_{xij}\geq0
\text{ for every }x,i,j\right\}.
\tag{67}
\]
Observational compatibility and consistency, as collected in \(\mathrm{def{:}compatible\mbox{-}class}\), transform the observed-margin and ATE equations into
\[
\begin{aligned}
r_x(\eta)\sum_i h_{xij}&=\eta_{x1j},\\
c_x(\eta)\sum_j k_{xij}&=\eta_{x0i},\\
\sum_{x,i,j}(j-i)
\{r_x(\eta)h_{xij}+c_x(\eta)k_{xij}\}&=t.
\end{aligned}
\tag{68}
\]
Denote (68) by \(A_\eta w=b_\eta(t)\), where \(w=(h,k)\).  From \(\mathrm{def{:}assignment\mbox{-}cost}\),
\[
F_\eta(w)
=\sum_{x,i,j}
\frac{r_x(\eta)c_x(\eta)(h_{xij}-k_{xij})^2}
{r_x(\eta)h_{xij}+c_x(\eta)k_{xij}},
\tag{69}
\]
with the closed perspective value zero when \(h_{xij}=k_{xij}=0\).  Indeed,
\[
d_{xij}=e_x(\eta)\{1-e_x(\eta)\}(h_{xij}-k_{xij}),
\qquad
s_{xij}=e_x(\eta)h_{xij}+\{1-e_x(\eta)\}k_{xij},
\tag{70}
\]
and multiplying the corresponding summand by \(p_x(\eta)\) yields (69).  The exact SOCP representation in \(\mathrm{def{:}socp}\), the attained frontier in \(\mathrm{thm{:}sharp\mbox{-}frontier}\), and the bijection (65) therefore give
\[
C_{P_\eta}(t)
=\min\{F_\eta(w):w\in\mathcal W_\Gamma, A_\eta w=b_\eta(t)\}.
\tag{71}
\]
Summing the first two groups of equations in (68) and using (62) shows that every feasible \(w\) satisfies
\[
\sum_{i,j}h_{xij}=1,qquad
\sum_{i,j}k_{xij}=1,qquad
0\leq h_{xij},k_{xij}\leq1.
\tag{72}
\]

We next obtain a uniform bound on attained dual multipliers.  If \(\Theta_{\mathrm{reg}}(\delta)\) is empty, the deterministic assertion is vacuous.  Otherwise, take the cone in (67) in its affine hull after deleting redundant faces.  The strict feasible array in \(\mathrm{def{:}regular\mbox{-}interior\mbox{-}region}\) has at least the declared slack \(\delta\) in every nonredundant original response-mass and odds-face inequality.  The two odds-face slacks transform as
\[
\begin{aligned}
a_{xij}-\ell_x(a_{xij}+b_{xij})
&=\frac{e_x(\eta)\{1-e_x(\eta)\}}
{e_x(\eta)+\Gamma\{1-e_x(\eta)\}}
(\Gamma h_{xij}-k_{xij}),\\
u_x(a_{xij}+b_{xij})-a_{xij}
&=\frac{e_x(\eta)\{1-e_x(\eta)\}}
{1-e_x(\eta)+\Gamma e_x(\eta)}
(\Gamma k_{xij}-h_{xij}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{73}
\]
By (64) and \(\Gamma\geq1\), each scalar factor in (73) lies in the explicit interval
\[
\left[\frac{\kappa_\delta^2}{\Gamma},\frac14\right].
\tag{74}
\]
Moreover \(a_{xij}=e_x(\eta)h_{xij}\) and \(b_{xij}=\{1-e_x(\eta)\}k_{xij}\), and both diagonal factors lie in \([\kappa_\delta,1-\kappa_\delta]\).  Hence the declared original slack yields a positive, computable lower bound on every normalized nonredundant face slack of (67).  Dividing by the largest Euclidean norm among the finitely many face normals gives a computable \(\gamma_\delta>0\) such that the transformed strict feasible point is at distance at least \(\gamma_\delta\) from the relative boundary of \(\mathcal W_\Gamma\).  This construction depends only on \(\delta,\Gamma,K\), and \(|\mathcal X|\), through the derived bound \(\kappa_\delta=K\delta\).

The equality operator in (68) has full row rank.  The treated- and control-margin rows have disjoint variable supports and nonzero coefficients, bounded below by \(\kappa_\delta\) by (62).  The ATE row is not in their span: for any fixed \(x\), the vector whose only nonzero coordinates are \(h_{x,0,0}=1\) and \(h_{x,1,0}=-1\) is orthogonal to every margin row but has inner product \(r_x(\eta)\geq\kappa_\delta\) with the ATE row.  Here \(K\geq2\) makes both indicated response types available.  On the compact set
\[
\Delta_\delta
=\left\{\eta:\eta_{xzy}\geq\delta,
\ \sum_{x,z,y}\eta_{xzy}=1\right\},
\tag{75}
\]
the entries of \(A_\eta\) are continuous.  Since full row rank holds at every point of \(\Delta_\delta\), continuity of the smallest singular value gives the computable constant
\[
\sigma_\delta
=\min_{\eta\in\Delta_\delta}\sigma_{\min}(A_\eta)>0.
\tag{76}
\]
Thus the right inverse
\[
R_\eta=A_\eta^{\mathsf T}(A_\eta A_\eta^{\mathsf T})^{-1}
\tag{77}
\]
satisfies \(\|R_\eta\|_{2\to2}\leq\sigma_\delta^{-1}\).  If \(\Gamma=1\), the odds restriction forces \(q_{xij}=e_x\) and identifies a single ATE; hence \(D_P\) is a singleton and no nonempty \(\mathcal T_0\subset\operatorname{int}D_P\) exists.  The theorem is then vacuous.  We henceforth consider the nonvacuous case.

For \(y=(\alpha,\beta,\lambda)\), use the Lagrangian
\[
\mathcal L_\eta(w;y)
=F_\eta(w)+y^{\mathsf T}\{b_\eta(t)-A_\eta w\}.
\tag{78}
\]
Cellwise minimization of (78) on \(\mathcal W_\Gamma\) is precisely the constraint in \(\mathrm{def{:}dual}\).  Indeed,
\[
r_xh_{xij}+c_xk_{xij}=p_xs_{xij},
\qquad
r_xh_{xij}=p_xs_{xij}q_{xij},
\tag{79}
\]
so the coefficient on a cell ray is
\[
p_xs_{xij}\left\{
\frac{(q_{xij}-e_x)^2}{e_x(1-e_x)}
-q_{xij}\alpha_{xj}-(1-q_{xij})\beta_{xi}
-\lambda(j-i)\right\}.
\tag{80}
\]
Therefore \(\mathrm{thm{:}explicit\mbox{-}dual}\) supplies an attained multiplier \(y_\eta(t)\) with
\[
C_{P_\eta}(t)
=\inf_{w\in\mathcal W_\Gamma}
\mathcal L_\eta(w;y_\eta(t)).
\tag{81}
\]

Let \(w^\circ\) be the uniformly strict feasible point, put
\(
\tau_\delta=\gamma_\delta\sigma_\delta/2
\), and let \(z\) be a unit vector in the equality space.  Equations (76)--(77) imply
\[
w^\pm=w^\circ\pm\tau_\delta R_\eta z\in\mathcal W_\Gamma,
\qquad
A_\eta w^\pm=b_\eta(t)\pm\tau_\delta z.
\tag{82}
\]
The vectors \(w^\pm\) range over a compact set: (72) bounds \(w^\circ\), while the perturbation has Euclidean norm at most \(\gamma_\delta/2\).  For \(r,c\geq\kappa_\delta\) and \(h,k\geq0\),
\[
0\leq\frac{rc(h-k)^2}{rh+ck}
\leq\frac{(h+k)^2}{\kappa_\delta(h+k)}
=\frac{h+k}{\kappa_\delta},
\tag{83}
\]
where the zero case uses the closed perspective convention.  Thus (69), (82), and (83) give a computable uniform \(\overline F_\delta<\infty\) such that \(F_\eta(w^\pm)\leq\overline F_\delta\).  Evaluating (81) at \(w^+\) and \(w^-\), respectively, and using \(C_{P_\eta}(t)\geq0\), yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau_\delta y_\eta(t)^{\mathsf T}z
&\leq F_\eta(w^+)-C_{P_\eta}(t)\leq\overline F_\delta,\\
-\tau_\delta y_\eta(t)^{\mathsf T}z
&\leq F_\eta(w^-)-C_{P_\eta}(t)\leq\overline F_\delta.
\end{aligned}
\tag{84}
\]
Taking the supremum over unit \(z\), and converting Euclidean to \(\ell_1\) norm in the \(d=2|\mathcal X|K+1\) dimensional equality space, gives
\[
\|y_\eta(t)\|_1
\leq B_\delta
:=\frac{\sqrt d\,\overline F_\delta}{\tau_\delta}<\infty
\tag{85}
\]
uniformly over \(\eta\in\Theta_{\mathrm{reg}}(\delta)\) and \(t\in\mathcal T_0\).  Equations (63), (73)--(77), and (83)--(85) are an explicit finite-dimensional recipe for \(B_\delta\); no active-set stability is used.

We now compare two observed laws without transporting a dual certificate.  Let \(\eta,\eta'\in\Theta_{\mathrm{reg}}(\delta)\), fix \(t\in\mathcal T_0\), put \(d_\infty=\|\eta-\eta'\|_\infty\), and let \(w'\) be a primal optimizer of (71) for \((\eta',t)\).  Define
\[
\psi_{r,c}(h,k)=\frac{rc(h-k)^2}{rh+ck}.
\]
On \(r,c\geq\kappa_\delta\), direct differentiation, \(|h-k|\leq h+k\), and \(rh+ck\geq\kappa_\delta(h+k)\) give
\[
|\partial_r\psi_{r,c}(h,k)|
\leq\frac{h+k}{\kappa_\delta}+\frac{h}{\kappa_\delta^2},
\qquad
|\partial_c\psi_{r,c}(h,k)|
\leq\frac{h+k}{\kappa_\delta}+\frac{k}{\kappa_\delta^2}.
\tag{86}
\]
At \(h=k=0\), the function is identically zero in \((r,c)\), so the same bounds hold.  Each arm mass changes by at most \(Kd_\infty\).  Summing (86) and using (72) therefore yields
\[
|F_\eta(w')-F_{\eta'}(w')|
\leq K_Fd_\infty,
\qquad
K_F=2|\mathcal X|K
\left(\frac{2}{\kappa_\delta}+\frac{1}{\kappa_\delta^2}\right).
\tag{87}
\]
Likewise, direct use of (68) and (72) gives
\[
\left\|
\{b_\eta(t)-A_\eta w'\}
-\{b_{\eta'}(t)-A_{\eta'}w'\}
\right\|_\infty
\leq K_A d_\infty,
\tag{88}
\]
where one may take
\[
K_A=\max\{1+K,\,2|\mathcal X|K(K-1)\}.
\tag{89}
\]
For a margin row, the bound in (88) is \(d_\infty+Kd_\infty\).  For the ATE row, it is at most \((K-1)\sum_x\{|r_x-r_x'|+|c_x-c_x'|\}\), proving (88)--(89).

Use the attained dual \(y_\eta(t)\) only in its own saddle identity (81), and evaluate that identity at \(w'\).  Since \(w'\in\mathcal W_\Gamma\), \(b_{\eta'}(t)-A_{\eta'}w'=0\), and \(F_{\eta'}(w')=C_{P_{\eta'}}(t)\), equations (81), (85), (87), and (88) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
C_{P_\eta}(t)
&\leq\mathcal L_\eta(w';y_\eta(t))\\
&=C_{P_{\eta'}}(t)
+F_\eta(w')-F_{\eta'}(w')
+y_\eta(t)^{\mathsf T}\{b_\eta(t)-A_\eta w'\}\\
&\leq C_{P_{\eta'}}(t)
+(K_F+B_\delta K_A)d_\infty.
\end{aligned}
\tag{90}
\]
Interchanging \(\eta\) and \(\eta'\) proves
\[
|C_{P_\eta}(t)-C_{P_{\eta'}}(t)|
\leq(K_F+B_\delta K_A)\|\eta-\eta'\|_\infty.
\tag{91}
\]
This is a cross-parameter saddle comparison on the common cone (67); no multiplier for one law is claimed feasible for the other law.  By \(\mathrm{def{:}regular\mbox{-}profile\mbox{-}band}\),
\[
L_{\mathrm{reg}}(\delta)
\leq K_F+B_\delta K_A<\infty.
\tag{92}
\]
For example,
\[
\mathfrak B_\delta=(1+K_F+K_A)(1+B_\delta)
\tag{93}
\]
is a computable uniform \(\ell_1\)-bound for the attained cellwise dual multipliers and also bounds the modulus in (92).  This proves the deterministic and computability assertions.

It remains to prove the simultaneous finite-sample statement.  Fix a multinomial coordinate, let \(U\) be its cell indicator, and put \(p=E_PU\).  For
\[
g(\lambda)=\log E_P\exp\{\lambda(U-p)\},
\]
we have \(g(0)=g'(0)=0\), while \(g''(\lambda)\) is the variance of a Bernoulli variable under exponential tilting and is at most \(1/4\).  Integrating twice gives
\[
E_P\exp\{\lambda(U-p)\}\leq\exp(\lambda^2/8).
\tag{94}
\]
Independence in \(\mathrm{ass{:}iid\mbox{-}sampling}\), Markov's inequality, and optimization at \(\lambda=4r_n\) then give
\[
P\{|\widehat\theta_k-\theta_k|>r_n\}
\leq2\exp(-2nr_n^2)=\frac{\alpha}{M}.
\tag{95}
\]
A union bound over the \(M\) cells proves
\[
P\{\|\widehat\theta-\theta\|_\infty\leq r_n\}
\geq1-\alpha.
\tag{96}
\]
On the event in (96), the maintained membership \(\theta\in\Theta_{\mathrm{reg}}(\delta)\) implies \(\theta\in\mathcal A_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\).  The definitions of the two envelopes therefore give, simultaneously for every \(t\in\mathcal T_0\),
\[
\underline C_n(t)\leq C_P(t)\leq\overline C_n(t).
\tag{97}
\]
Any two multinomial vectors in \(\mathcal A_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\) are at \(\ell_\infty\)-distance at most \(2r_n\).  Applying the definition of \(L_{\mathrm{reg}}(\delta)\), and then taking the supremum and infimum over this same nonempty set, gives on (96), again simultaneously in \(t\),
\[
0\leq\overline C_n(t)-\underline C_n(t)
\leq2L_{\mathrm{reg}}(\delta)r_n.
\tag{98}
\]
Equations (92), (96), (97), and (98) establish every assertion of the theorem.
