%%% FIELD proof-sharp-frontier
Fix \(P\) and \(\Gamma\), and omit strata with \(p_x=0\). By \(\mathrm{ass{:}overlap}\), \(0<e_x<1\) on each remaining stratum. The finite response-type representation gives
\[
\sum_i a_{xij}=e_x\pi_{1x,j},\qquad
\sum_j b_{xij}=(1-e_x)\pi_{0x,i},\qquad a_{xij},b_{xij}\geq0. \tag{1}
\]
Conversely, arrays satisfying (1) define a full law by putting conditional mass \(a_{xij}\) on \((Y(0),Y(1),Z)=(i,j,1)\) and mass \(b_{xij}\) on \((i,j,0)\). Equation (1) matches both observed arms, and \(\mathrm{ass{:}consistency}\) then matches \(P\). Thus this representation is equivalent to \(\mathrm{def{:}compatible\mbox{-}class}\), not an outer relaxation.

Put \(s_{xij}=a_{xij}+b_{xij}\). On positive cells, \(q_{xij}=a_{xij}/s_{xij}\), so \(\mathrm{ass{:}odds\mbox{-}cap}\) is exactly
\[
a_{xij}-\ell_xs_{xij}\geq0,\qquad u_xs_{xij}-a_{xij}\geq0. \tag{2}
\]
When \(s_{xij}=0\), nonnegativity forces \(a_{xij}=b_{xij}=0\), and (2) remains valid. Moreover,
\[
d_{xij}=(1-e_x)a_{xij}-e_xb_{xij}=s_{xij}(q_{xij}-e_x). \tag{3}
\]
Hence \(\mathrm{def{:}assignment\mbox{-}cost}\) is a sum of closed quadratic perspectives
\[
p_x\frac{d_{xij}^{\,2}}{e_x(1-e_x)s_{xij}}. \tag{4}
\]
Each term is lower semicontinuous and convex in \((a_{xij},b_{xij})\), including its declared value at \((s,d)=(0,0)\). The ATE equation is linear:
\[
\sum_xp_x\sum_{i,j}(j-i)s_{xij}=t. \tag{5}
\]

The feasible array set before imposing (5) is nonempty: in each stratum take any coupling of the two observed arm marginals, for example their product coupling, and assign \(Z\) independently with probability \(e_x\); then every positive type has \(q_{xij}=e_x\in[\ell_x,u_x]\). It is closed, convex, and bounded, because (1) implies
\[
\sum_{i,j}a_{xij}=e_x,\qquad \sum_{i,j}b_{xij}=1-e_x.
\]
It is compact. Its image under (5) is therefore a compact interval, exactly \(D_P\). Every nonempty fiber is compact, so lower semicontinuity of (4) proves attainment of every finite \(C_P(t)\).

The rotated-cone identity
\[
\left\|\left(\frac{2d_{xij}}{\sqrt{e_x(1-e_x)}},s_{xij}-v_{xij}\right)\right\|_2
\leq s_{xij}+v_{xij}
\Longleftrightarrow
s_{xij}v_{xij}\geq\frac{d_{xij}^{\,2}}{e_x(1-e_x)},\quad s_{xij},v_{xij}\geq0 \tag{6}
\]
shows that \(\mathrm{def{:}socp}\) has the same value after minimizing over \(v\). There are \(|\mathcal X|K^2\) response-type cells and a constant number of variables and constraints per cell, so its size is \(O(|\mathcal X|K^2)\).

Mixing feasible arrays and using convexity of (4) gives
\[
C_P(\eta t_1+(1-\eta)t_0)\leq\eta C_P(t_1)+(1-\eta)C_P(t_0),\qquad 0\leq\eta\leq1. \tag{7}
\]
If \(t_r\to t\) and \(\liminf_rC_P(t_r)<\infty\), take optimizing arrays along a subsequence realizing the liminf. Compactness supplies a convergent subsequence, and lower semicontinuity gives \(C_P(t)\leq\liminf_rC_P(t_r)\). Thus \(C_P\) is closed and convex. Its sublevel \(\mathcal I_P(\rho)\) is a compact interval, possibly empty; every endpoint of a nonempty sublevel belongs to it and has an attaining full law.

Finally, if \(Q\in\mathcal H_\Gamma(P)\), \(T(Q)=t\), and \(R_P(Q)\leq\rho\), minimization gives \(C_P(t)\leq R_P(Q)\leq\rho\). Conversely, \(C_P(t)\leq\rho<\infty\) puts \(t\) in \(D_P\), and attainment supplies \(Q_t\) with \(T(Q_t)=t\) and \(R_P(Q_t)=C_P(t)\leq\rho\). Therefore the observed-law functional \(\mathcal I_P(\rho)\) is exactly, and hence sharply, the set of causal ATE values generated by one compatible full law at budget \(\rho\).

%%% FIELD proof-explicit-dual
Let \(g_x(q)=(q-e_x)^2/[e_x(1-e_x)]\). By \(\mathrm{thm{:}sharp\mbox{-}frontier}\), the primal objective is
\[
\sum_xp_x\sum_{i,j}s_{xij}g_x(q_{xij}),\quad
a_{xij}=s_{xij}q_{xij},\quad b_{xij}=s_{xij}(1-q_{xij}),\quad
q_{xij}\in[\ell_x,u_x]. \tag{8}
\]
Use the Lagrangian
\[
\lambda\{t-T(Q)\}
+\sum_xp_x\sum_j\alpha_{xj}\left\{e_x\pi_{1x,j}-\sum_i a_{xij}\right\}
+\sum_xp_x\sum_i\beta_{xi}\left\{(1-e_x)\pi_{0x,i}-\sum_j b_{xij}\right\}. \tag{9}
\]
For a cell of mass \(s\geq0\) and propensity \(q\), the coefficient of \(p_xs\) is
\[
g_x(q)-q\alpha_{xj}-(1-q)\beta_{xi}-\lambda(j-i). \tag{10}
\]
The infimum over \(s\geq0\) is zero exactly when the minimum of (10) over \(q\in[\ell_x,u_x]\) is nonnegative, and is \(-\infty\) otherwise. The constant term in (9) is
\[
\lambda t+\sum_xp_x\left[e_x\sum_j\alpha_{xj}\pi_{1x,j}
+(1-e_x)\sum_i\beta_{xi}\pi_{0x,i}\right]. \tag{11}
\]
Equations (10)--(11) are precisely \(\mathrm{def{:}dual}\). Multiplying (10) by \(p_xs_{xij}\), summing, and invoking the primal equalities proves weak duality.

For equality and dual attainment, consider the polyhedral cell wedge
\[
\mathcal K_x=\{(a,b):a,b\geq0,\ \ell_x(a+b)\leq a\leq u_x(a+b)\}
\]
and the closed sublinear function \(f_x(a,b)=(a+b)g_x(a/(a+b))\), with \(f_x(0,0)=0\). Overlap gives \(0<\ell_x\leq u_x<1\). Since \(g_x\) and \(g_x'\) are bounded on this interval, direct differentiation on rays and one-sided limits at the two boundary rays give a finite \(B_x\) such that
\[
|f_x(z)-f_x(z')|\leq B_x\|z-z'\|_1,\qquad z,z'\in\mathcal K_x. \tag{12}
\]

The product of these wedges has finitely many faces. On each face, delete redundant equality rows and use a right inverse of the remaining matrix. The maximum of the finitely many right-inverse norms is finite. Therefore, for nearby attainable right sides \(r,r'\) on a common face and any feasible \(z\) over \(r\), one can solve the independent rows for a feasible correction satisfying
\[
\|z-z'\|_1\leq H\|r-r'\|_1. \tag{13}
\]
If the correction meets a subface, repeat there; termination follows from finiteness of the face lattice. Combining (12) and (13) shows that the optimal value as a function of all margin and ATE right sides is locally Lipschitz relative to every face of its polyhedral domain, including endpoint faces.

At the right side determined by \(P\) and any \(t\in D_P\), support this finite convex value function on its minimal face and add a normal of that face to extend the support to the whole attainable domain. Relative Lipschitz continuity makes the supporting coefficients finite. Denote them by \((\alpha,\beta,\lambda)\). Evaluating the support inequality on an array concentrated along any single-cell ray \(q\in[\ell_x,u_x]\) gives exactly (10), while equality at the chosen right side makes (11) equal to \(C_P(t)\). Thus strong duality holds and the dual supremum is attained. The proof works on the minimal face rather than requiring Slater regularity, so it includes exposed endpoint fibers and zero response-type cells.

%%% FIELD proof-binary-garbling
Work in one stratum and omit \(x\). Since
\[
\tau=E_Q[Y(1)-Y(0)]=s_{01}-s_{10}, \tag{14}
\]
\(\tau\geq0\) implies \(s_{01}\geq s_{10}\). Put \(r=s_{10}\). If \(r=0\), use the identity. Otherwise select a fraction \(r/s_{01}\) of type \(01\) using auxiliary randomness independent of \((Y(0),Y(1),Z)\). The selected mass is \(r\), and its propensity remains \(q_{01}\).

Apply \(\mathrm{def{:}binary\mbox{-}garbling}\), independently of \(Z\): send type \(10\) to \(00\) and \(11\) with probabilities \(1-q_{01}\) and \(q_{01}\), and send selected type \(01\) to \(00\) and \(11\) with probabilities \(q_{10}\) and \(1-q_{10}\). The image has \(s'_{10}=0\) and \(s'_{01}=s_{01}-r=\tau\), so its ATE is unchanged.

The paired masses contributed to type \(00\) have treated and control masses
\[
rq_{10}(1-q_{01})+rq_{01}q_{10}=rq_{10}=a_{10}, \tag{15}
\]
\[
r(1-q_{10})(1-q_{01})+r(1-q_{01})q_{10}=r(1-q_{01}), \tag{16}
\]
where (16) is the control mass removed from selected type \(01\). At type \(11\), the corresponding masses are
\[
rq_{10}q_{01}+rq_{01}(1-q_{10})=rq_{01}, \tag{17}
\]
\[
r(1-q_{10})q_{01}+r(1-q_{01})(1-q_{10})=r(1-q_{10})=b_{10}. \tag{18}
\]
Equations (15)--(18) preserve treated \(Y(1)\) and control \(Y(0)\) margins. Consistency therefore preserves \(P\).

Every moved piece retains its source propensity because the kernel is treatment-independent. The propensity of a pooled destination is a mass-weighted average of source propensities, including any original destination mass. Since \([\ell_x,u_x]\) is convex, all positive image cells obey the same odds cap, so the image remains in \(\mathcal H_\Gamma(P)\).

Splitting a cell treatment-independently preserves \(s\varphi(q)\). Pooling masses \(r_k\) gives, by convexity,
\[
\left(\sum_kr_k\right)\varphi\left(\frac{\sum_kr_kq_k}{\sum_kr_k}\right)
\leq\sum_kr_k\varphi(q_k). \tag{19}
\]
Apply (19) at destinations \(00\) and \(11\); unchanged pieces cancel. This proves contraction for every convex \(\varphi\), including the Pearson generator. For \(\tau<0\), complement both potential outcomes, apply the positive-effect construction to \(-\tau\), and complement back. This swaps \(01\) and \(10\), preserves propensities and costs, and leaves zero mass on type \(01\).

%%% FIELD proof-binary-phase-catalog
Assume \(0<\tau<1\). By \(\mathrm{thm{:}binary\mbox{-}garbling}\), a Pearson-cost optimizer may be chosen with \(s_{10}=0\), and every law supported on the other three types remains admissible for the original problem. Set
\[
v=s_{00},\qquad s_{01}=\tau,\qquad s_{11}=1-\tau-v. \tag{20}
\]
The treated-zero and control-one margins force \(a_{00}=A\) and \(b_{11}=B\). Since \(D_0=A+(1-e_x)(1-p_0)\), the remaining entries are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
(i,j)&s_{ij}&a_{ij}&q_{ij}\\ \hline
00&v&A&A/v\\
01&\tau&v+\tau-D_0&(v+\tau-D_0)/\tau\\
11&1-\tau-v&1-\tau-v-B&1-B/(1-\tau-v).
\end{array} \tag{21}
\]
Zero denominators use the declared closed-perspective convention.

The three odds restrictions in (21) are, respectively,
\[
\frac{A}{u_x}\leq v\leq\frac{A}{\ell_x}, \tag{22}
\]
\[
1-\tau-\frac{B}{1-u_x}\leq v\leq1-\tau-\frac{B}{1-\ell_x}, \tag{23}
\]
\[
D_0-(1-\ell_x)\tau\leq v\leq D_0-(1-u_x)\tau. \tag{24}
\]
Their intersection is \([L_\tau,U_\tau]\). Thus feasibility is exactly \(L_\tau\leq U_\tau\), and (22)--(24) are the complete six odds faces.

Because \(\sum s_{ij}q_{ij}=e_x\) and \(\sum s_{ij}=1\),
\[
\sum_{ij}\frac{s_{ij}(q_{ij}-e_x)^2}{e_x(1-e_x)}
=\frac{\sum_{ij}s_{ij}q_{ij}^2-e_x^2}{e_x(1-e_x)}. \tag{25}
\]
Substitution of (21) into (25) gives exactly \(F_\tau(v)\). Therefore
\[
C_P(\tau)=\min_{v\in[L_\tau,U_\tau]}F_\tau(v), \tag{26}
\]
with attainment by compactness and closed perspectives.

On the relative interior of its effective interval,
\[
e_x(1-e_x)F_\tau'(v)
=-\frac{A^2}{v^2}+\frac{B^2}{(1-\tau-v)^2}
+\frac{2(v+\tau-D_0)}{\tau}-1=H_\tau(v), \tag{27}
\]
and
\[
H_\tau'(v)=\frac{2A^2}{v^3}+\frac{2B^2}{(1-\tau-v)^3}+\frac{2}{\tau}>0. \tag{28}
\]
Thus \(F_\tau\) is strictly convex and \(H_\tau\) strictly increasing. If \(H_\tau(L_\tau)\geq0\), the minimizer is \(L_\tau\); if \(H_\tau(U_\tau)\leq0\), it is \(U_\tau\); otherwise continuity and (28) give one unique root in \((L_\tau,U_\tau)\), which is the optimizer. Singleton or support-degenerate cases follow by closed limits. Outcome complementation gives the stated negative-effect catalog.

%%% FIELD proof-null-correlation-cost
Suppose the binary null is feasible and \(Y\) is nonconstant. At \(\tau=0\), \(\mathrm{thm{:}binary\mbox{-}garbling}\) removes both discordant types: its image has \(s_{10}=0\), while \(s_{01}-s_{10}=0\) forces \(s_{01}=0\). The margins uniquely determine
\[
s_{11}=e_xp_1+(1-e_x)p_0=m,\qquad s_{00}=1-m, \tag{29}
\]
\[
q_{11}=\frac{e_xp_1}{m},\qquad q_{00}=\frac{e_x(1-p_1)}{1-m}. \tag{30}
\]
Nonconstancy means \(0<m<1\), so the divisions are valid. The null is feasible exactly when both propensities in (30) belong to \([\ell_x,u_x]\).

Writing \(d=p_1-p_0\), direct subtraction gives
\[
q_{11}-e_x=\frac{e_x(1-e_x)d}{m},\qquad
q_{00}-e_x=-\frac{e_x(1-e_x)d}{1-m}. \tag{31}
\]
Therefore
\[
C_P(0)
=\frac{m(q_{11}-e_x)^2+(1-m)(q_{00}-e_x)^2}{e_x(1-e_x)}
=\frac{e_x(1-e_x)(p_1-p_0)^2}{m(1-m)}. \tag{32}
\]
Under \(P\),
\[
\operatorname{Cov}_P(Z,Y)=e_xp_1-e_xm=e_x(1-e_x)(p_1-p_0),
\]
\(\operatorname{Var}_P(Z)=e_x(1-e_x)\), and \(\operatorname{Var}_P(Y)=m(1-m)\). Thus (32) is \(\operatorname{Corr}_P(Z,Y)^2\).

If either null odds inequality fails, a hypothetical compatible null could be garbled into the unique concordant law (29)--(30) without changing \(P\), \(\tau\), or the odds cap, a contradiction. The null fiber is empty, so \(\mathrm{def{:}frontier}\) gives \(C_P(0)=+\infty\).

At \(v_\star\), the first two terms of \(H_\tau\) cancel, and \(2(v_\star+\tau-D_0)/\tau-1=0\). Hence \(H_\tau(v_\star)=0\), and strict increase makes \(v_\star\) the unique unconstrained minimizer.

or \(|c-\tau|\leq\kappa(1-\tau)\). Substituting (33) into all six scalar bounds shows their intersection is nonempty under exactly this inequality, so (36) is exact fiber feasibility.

C_P(\tau)=\frac{(c-\tau)^2}{1-\tau}. \tag{37}

e_xE_P[w_Q(Y,1)\mid Z=1]
=e_xE_Q[q^{-1}\mid Z=1]

and similarly

(1-e_x)E_P[w_Q(Y,0)\mid Z=0]=1. \tag{40}

On a zero-probability observed cell choose any unused weight in its box. Since \(q_{ij}\in[\ell_x,u_x]\), conditional averaging yields

e_xw_Q(y,1)\in[e_x/u_x,e_x/\ell_x], \tag{41}

\[

Thus \(w_Q\in\mathcal W^{\mathrm{IHK}}_\Gamma(P)\).

E_P[Zw_Q(Y,1)Y]

Hence the ATE is preserved.

For \(f_N(r)=r^{-1}-1\), the unrelaxed cell contribution is

qf_N(e_x/q)+(1-q)f_N((1-e_x)/(1-q))
=\frac{q^2}{e_x}+\frac{(1-q)^2}{1-e_x}-1
=\frac{(q-e_x)^2}{e_x(1-e_x)}. \tag{45}

After multiplying by \(s_{ij}\) and summing, the unrelaxed divergence is exactly \(R_P(Q)\). Convexity of \(f_N\) and conditional Jensen within each \((Y,Z)\) cell give

\mathscr R_{\mathrm{IHK},P}(w_Q)\leq R_P(Q). \tag{46}

Every full-law ATE fiber therefore embeds in the relaxed fiber at no greater budget, so

\widetilde C^{\mathrm{IHK}}_P(\tau)\leq C_P(\tau). \tag{47}

After zero-probability coordinates are removed, the relaxed set is a closed bounded weight polytope and its objective has the declared closed extension. The infimum is attained. The two direct minimization implications used in \(\mathrm{thm{:}sharp\mbox{-}frontier}\) prove that its ATE slice is feasible at budget \(\rho\) exactly when \(\widetilde C^{\mathrm{IHK}}_P(\tau)\leq\rho\).

The odds box is \(r_z(y)\in[5/8,5/2]\), so \(m_1\in[1/2,7/8]\) and \(m_0\in[1/8,1/2]\). On \(m_1-m_0=3/10\), write \(m_0=x\); then \(x\in[1/5,1/2]\).

For each \(x\) with \(p_x>0\), disintegrate \(Q\in\mathcal H_\Gamma(P)\) into its conditional law \(Q_x\), and put \(t_x=E_{Q_x}[Y(1)-Y(0)]\). The definitions give

Since \(R_x(Q_x)\geq C_x(t_x)\), every global \(t\)-fiber law satisfies

R_P(Q)\geq\sum_xp_xC_x(t_x),\qquad \sum_xp_xt_x=t. \tag{54}

Taking the infimum proves one inequality.

%%% FIELD proof-regular-profile-diameter
First prove the deterministic modulus. On \(\Theta_{\mathrm{reg}}(\delta)\), every observed cell is at least \(\delta\). Hence stratum and arm masses are bounded away from zero, and the maps from multinomial coordinates to \(p_x,e_x,\pi_{zx,y},\ell_x,u_x\) have uniformly bounded derivatives. The SOCP coefficient arrays consequently range over a compact set.

For every \(t\in\mathcal T_0\), \(\mathrm{ass{:}uniform\mbox{-}primal\mbox{-}margin}\) supplies a feasible point with every nonredundant response-mass and odds inequality slack by at least \(\delta\). Delete one fixed redundant margin row and normalize dual multipliers on the independent rows. Evaluating the primal-dual gap at the strict point gives, for a normalized optimal dual vector \(y\),
\[
\delta\|y_{\mathrm{ineq}}\|_1
\leq \{\text{objective at the strict point}\}-C_{P'}(t). \tag{55}
\]
The right side is uniformly bounded because response masses sum to one and the Pearson generator is bounded on the uniformly overlapping propensity interval. The independent equality matrix has its smallest singular value bounded away from zero over the compact coefficient family. Its stationarity equations then bound the equality multipliers as well. Thus attained normalized cellwise dual multipliers have a finite uniform \(\ell_1\) bound \(B_\delta\).

This bound is computable: there are finitely many choices of redundant margin row and active odds faces; for each, maximize the normalized dual norm subject to primal feasibility, dual feasibility, equality of objectives, and the declared \(\delta\)-slack inequalities, then take the largest value. Compactness and (55) make every such maximum finite.

Use an optimal dual certificate for \(P'\) as a weak-duality certificate at \(P''\), then reverse the roles. The coefficient maps have an explicit finite Lipschitz constant \(K_\delta\), so
\[
|C_{P'}(t)-C_{P''}(t)|
\leq B_\delta K_\delta\|\theta(P')-\theta(P'')\|_\infty. \tag{56}
\]
Absorbing \(K_\delta\) into the computable multiplier bound proves \(L_{\mathrm{reg}}(\delta)<\infty\) and the theorem's final assertion. This calculation does not require stability of an active set.

For the sampling statement, let \(U\) be any one multinomial cell indicator with mean \(p\). Direct two-point calculation gives
\[
E\exp\{\lambda(U-p)\}=(1-p)e^{-\lambda p}+pe^{\lambda(1-p)}
\leq e^{\lambda^2/8};
\]
the inequality follows by differentiating the logarithm twice, whose second derivative is the variance of a Bernoulli tilt and is at most \(1/4\), and using its value and first derivative at zero. For \(S_n=\sum_{r=1}^n(U_r-p)\), independence gives \(E e^{\lambda S_n}\leq e^{n\lambda^2/8}\). Markov's inequality at the optimizing value \(\lambda=4r_n\), applied also to \(-S_n\), therefore gives
\[
P\{|\widehat\theta_k-\theta_k|>r_n\}
\leq2\exp(-2nr_n^2)=\frac{\alpha}{M}. \tag{57}
\]
Summing (57) over the \(M\) coordinates yields
\[
P\{\|\widehat\theta-\theta\|_\infty\leq r_n\}\geq1-\alpha. \tag{58}
\]
On this event, the assumed true \(\theta\in\Theta_{\mathrm{reg}}(\delta)\) belongs to \(\mathcal A_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\). Therefore, simultaneously for every \(t\in\mathcal T_0\),
\[
\underline C_n(t)\leq C_P(t)\leq\overline C_n(t). \tag{59}
\]
For any \(P',P''\) indexed by \(\mathcal A_n^{\mathrm{reg}}\),
\[
\|\theta(P')-\theta(P'')\|_\infty\leq2r_n. \tag{60}
\]
Taking the supremum of the defining Lipschitz inequality over (60) proves, on the same event and simultaneously in \(t\),
\[
0\leq\overline C_n(t)-\underline C_n(t)
\leq2L_{\mathrm{reg}}(\delta)r_n.
\]

%%% FIELD proof-boundary-inference-lower-bound
At \(\theta_0\), \(\mathrm{thm{:}symmetric\mbox{-}family}\) with \(c=3/5\) and \(\Gamma=4\) gives
\[
C_{P_{\theta_0}}(0)=\frac9{25}. \tag{61}
\]
For \(\theta_{n,h}\), \(m=1/2+h/(2n)\). If the null were feasible, \(\mathrm{prop{:}null\mbox{-}correlation\mbox{-}cost}\) would require
\[
q_{11}=\frac{(1/2)(4/5+h/n)}{1/2+h/(2n)}
=\frac{4/5+h/n}{1+h/n}\leq u_x=\frac45. \tag{62}
\]
But
\[
\frac{4/5+h/n}{1+h/n}-\frac45
=\frac{h}{5n(1+h/n)}>0. \tag{63}
\]
Thus the null odds cap fails and \(C_{P_{\theta_{n,h}}}(0)=+\infty\) for every sufficiently large \(n\) for which the stated probabilities lie in \([0,1]\).

Only the two treated outcome cells change from \(\theta_0\), each by \(h/(2n)\), and all four baseline cells are positive. Hence
\[
\chi^2(P_{\theta_{n,h}},P_{\theta_0})
=\sum_k\frac{(\theta_{n,h,k}-\theta_{0,k})^2}{\theta_{0,k}}
=O(n^{-2}). \tag{64}
\]
Expanding the product likelihood ratio and using independence gives
\[
1+\chi^2(P_{\theta_{n,h}}^{\otimes n},P_{\theta_0}^{\otimes n})
=\{1+\chi^2(P_{\theta_{n,h}},P_{\theta_0})\}^{n}
=1+O(n^{-1}). \tag{65}
\]
For likelihood ratio \(L=dP/dQ\), Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
\|P-Q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
=\frac12E_Q|L-1|
\leq\frac12\{E_Q(L-1)^2\}^{1/2}. \tag{66}
\]
Equations (64)--(66) prove that the product total variation distance tends to zero.

Let \(A_n=\{9/25\in\mathcal C_n\}\) and \(B_n=\{+\infty\in\mathcal C_n\}\). Coverage gives
\[
P_{\theta_0}^{\otimes n}(A_n)\geq1-\alpha,\qquad
P_{\theta_{n,h}}^{\otimes n}(B_n)\geq1-\alpha. \tag{67}
\]
Total-variation convergence transfers the second inequality to
\[
P_{\theta_0}^{\otimes n}(B_n)\geq1-\alpha-o(1). \tag{68}
\]
The union bound gives \(P_{\theta_0}^{\otimes n}(A_n\cap B_n)\geq1-2\alpha-o(1)\). A set containing both \(9/25\) and \(+\infty\) has infinite extended-real diameter. Taking the lower limit proves the claim.

%%% FIELD proof-gamma-one-regression
When \(\Gamma=1\), \(\ell_x=u_x=e_x\). Every positive response-type cell under a compatible law therefore has \(q_{xij}=e_x\), so \(Z\) is conditionally independent of \((Y(0),Y(1))\) given \(X=x\). Observational compatibility and consistency imply
\[
E_Q[Y(1)\mid X=x]=\sum_jj\pi_{1x,j},\qquad
E_Q[Y(0)\mid X=x]=\sum_ii\pi_{0x,i}. \tag{69}
\]
Summing with weights \(p_x\) gives \(T(Q)=\Delta_P\) for every \(Q\in\mathcal H_1(P)\). Also
\[
d_{xij}=s_{xij}(q_{xij}-e_x)=0,
\]
so \(R_P(Q)=0\).

The class is nonempty. In each stratum, couple \(Y(0)\) and \(Y(1)\) with marginals \(\pi_{0x,\cdot}\) and \(\pi_{1x,\cdot}\), for example by a product coupling, and draw \(Z\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(e_x)\) independently. The margin equations and consistency verify compatibility. Consequently
\[
D_P=\{\Delta_P\},\qquad C_P(\Delta_P)=0,\qquad
\mathcal I_P(\rho)=\{\Delta_P\}\quad\text{for every }\rho\geq0.
\]

%%% FIELD partial-uniform-tangent
The frozen assumptions prove two complementary results. On \(\Theta_{\mathrm{reg}}(\delta)\), the whole frontier profile has a simultaneous finite-sample band of width at most \(2L_{\mathrm{reg}}(\delta)r_n=O(n^{-1/2})\). At the specified finite-to-infinite null boundary, every confidence set honest at the contiguous pair has infinite diameter with limiting probability at least \(1-2\alpha\). These premises do not determine a uniformly estimable tangent operator on the intervening facially degenerate class.
