%%% FIELD proof-strict-jensen-gap
Fix one stratum and suppress its \(x\)-subscript.  Let \(Q\in\mathcal H_\Gamma(P)\), write \(s_{ij}=Q(Y(0)=i,Y(1)=j)\), and write \(q_{ij}=Q(Z=1\mid Y(0)=i,Y(1)=j)\) on positive-mass types.  By overlap and the odds cap, \(0<\ell_x\leq q_{ij}\leq u_x<1\).  Consistency and observational compatibility identify \(Y\) with \(Y(1)\) in the treated arm and with \(Y(0)\) in the control arm.

For a positive-probability treated outcome cell, the definition of \(w_Q\) gives
\[
 e_x E_P\{w_Q(Y,1)\mid Z=1\}
 =E_Q\{Z w_Q(Y(1),1)\}
 =E_Q\!\left(\frac{Z}{q_{Y(0),Y(1)}}\right)
 =\sum_{i,j}s_{ij}q_{ij}\frac{1}{q_{ij}}=1. \tag{1}
\]
The same calculation in the control arm gives
\[
 (1-e_x)E_P\{w_Q(Y,0)\mid Z=0\}
 =E_Q\!\left(\frac{1-Z}{1-q_{Y(0),Y(1)}}\right)=1. \tag{2}
\]
Weights on null observed cells do not enter either equality and may be assigned any value in their declared boxes.  Conditional averaging of \(q_{ij}^{-1}\in[u_x^{-1},\ell_x^{-1}]\) and \((1-q_{ij})^{-1}\in[(1-\ell_x)^{-1},(1-u_x)^{-1}]\) yields
\[
 e_xw_Q(y,1)\in[e_x/u_x,e_x/\ell_x],\qquad
 (1-e_x)w_Q(y,0)\in[(1-e_x)/(1-\ell_x),(1-e_x)/(1-u_x)]. \tag{3}
\]
Equations (1)--(3) are exactly the normalization and box conditions in the explicitly defined box-augmented Neyman specialization \(\mathcal W^{\mathrm{IHK}}_\Gamma(P)\).  Thus \(w_Q\in\mathcal W^{\mathrm{IHK}}_\Gamma(P)\).

The same inverse-propensity cancellation recovers the two full-law potential-outcome means:
\[
 E_P\{Zw_Q(Y,1)Y\}
 =\sum_{i,j}s_{ij}q_{ij}\frac{j}{q_{ij}}
 =E_Q\{Y(1)\}, \tag{4}
\]
\[
 E_P\{(1-Z)w_Q(Y,0)Y\}
 =\sum_{i,j}s_{ij}(1-q_{ij})\frac{i}{1-q_{ij}}
 =E_Q\{Y(0)\}. \tag{5}
\]
Hence the map \(Q\mapsto w_Q\) preserves the ATE fiber.

It remains to compare costs in the same orientation and normalization.  Since \(a_{ij}=s_{ij}q_{ij}\) and \(b_{ij}=s_{ij}(1-q_{ij})\),
\[
 d_{ij}=(1-e_x)a_{ij}-e_xb_{ij}=s_{ij}(q_{ij}-e_x). \tag{6}
\]
Consequently the closed-perspective definition gives
\[
 R_P(Q)=\sum_{i,j}s_{ij}\frac{(q_{ij}-e_x)^2}{e_x(1-e_x)}, \tag{7}
\]
where a zero-mass type contributes zero.  In the observed-policy-first orientation, the unrelaxed objective evaluated at \(Q\) is the sum over types of \(s_{ij}\) times
\[
 q_{ij}f_N(e_x/q_{ij})+(1-q_{ij})f_N((1-e_x)/(1-q_{ij})).
\]
For \(f_N(r)=r^{-1}-1\), direct algebra on every positive-mass type gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &q_{ij}f_N(e_x/q_{ij})+(1-q_{ij})f_N((1-e_x)/(1-q_{ij}))\\
 &\qquad=\frac{q_{ij}^2}{e_x}+\frac{(1-q_{ij})^2}{1-e_x}-1
 =\frac{(q_{ij}-e_x)^2}{e_x(1-e_x)}.
 \end{aligned} \tag{8}
\]
Thus the unrelaxed latent-law Neyman divergence is exactly (7).  Moreover \(f_N''(r)=2r^{-3}>0\) for \(r>0\), and
\[
 e_xw_Q(y,1)=E_Q\{e_x/q_{Y(0),Y(1)}\mid Y(1)=y,Z=1\}, \tag{9}
\]
with the analogous identity in the control arm.  No external form of Jensen's inequality is needed here: for any finite probability vector \((\lambda_k)_k\), positive numbers \((r_k)_k\), and \(S=\sum_k\lambda_kr_k\), direct symmetrization gives
\[
 \sum_k\lambda_k f_N(r_k)-f_N(S)
 =\sum_k\frac{\lambda_k}{r_k}-\frac1S
 =\frac{\sum_{k<l}\lambda_k\lambda_l(r_k-r_l)^2/(r_kr_l)}{S}\geq0. \tag{9a}
\]
Apply (9a) to the finitely many response types in each observed \((Y,Z)\) cell and then sum over those cells.  This yields
\[
 \mathscr R_{\mathrm{IHK},P}(w_Q)\leq R_P(Q). \tag{10}
\]
This is a one-way inclusion and cost transfer: every compatible full-law fiber maps into the explicitly defined box-augmented Jensen--CMC fiber, but no reverse realizability claim is being made.  Taking infima in (10) gives
\[
 \widetilde C^{\mathrm{IHK}}_P(\tau)\leq C_P(\tau). \tag{11}
\]

For completeness, on a fixed ATE slice the effective coordinates of \(\mathcal W^{\mathrm{IHK}}_\Gamma(P)\) lie in a finite product of closed, bounded, strictly positive intervals; normalization and the ATE restriction are closed affine constraints.  After irrelevant null observed cells are deleted, the slice is therefore compact.  The objective is continuous there because \(f_N\) is continuous on the positive weight box, so a nonempty slice attains its infimum.  It follows directly that the relaxed slice contains a weight of cost at most \(\rho\) if and only if \(\widetilde C^{\mathrm{IHK}}_P(\tau)\leq\rho\).  Together with the attained full-law frontier from \(\mathrm{thm{:}sharp\mbox{-}frontier}\), (10) proves the asserted same-budget implication.

Now impose \(e_x=1/2\), \(p_1=4/5\), \(p_0=1/5\), \(\Gamma=4\), and \(\tau=3/10\).  Put \(r_1(y)=e_xw(y,1)\) and \(r_0(y)=(1-e_x)w(y,0)\), and let \(m_z\) be the mean of \(Y\) under the corresponding normalized reweighting.  Binary support and normalization uniquely imply
\[
 r_1(1)=\frac{m_1}{p_1},\quad r_1(0)=\frac{1-m_1}{1-p_1},\qquad
 r_0(1)=\frac{m_0}{p_0},\quad r_0(0)=\frac{1-m_0}{1-p_0}. \tag{12}
\]
Substituting (12) into the Neyman objective gives
\[
 \mathscr R_{\mathrm{IHK},P}(w)
 =\frac12\frac{(m_1-4/5)^2}{m_1(1-m_1)}
 +\frac12\frac{(m_0-1/5)^2}{m_0(1-m_0)}
 =J(m_1,m_0), \tag{13}
\]
and the weighted ATE equality is \(m_1-m_0=3/10\).  Here \(\ell_x=1/5\) and \(u_x=4/5\), so both armwise boxes for \(r_z(y)\) equal \([5/8,5/2]\).  Applying (12) to both outcome values shows
\[
 m_1\in[1/2,7/8],\qquad m_0\in[1/8,1/2]. \tag{14}
\]
Writing \(m_0=x\) and \(m_1=x+3/10\), (14) reduces the ATE slice exactly to \(x\in[1/5,1/2]\).

For \(g_p(m)=(m-p)^2/\{m(1-m)\}\),
\[
 g_p(m)=\frac{p^2}{m}+\frac{(1-p)^2}{1-m}-1,qquad
 g_p''(m)=\frac{2p^2}{m^3}+\frac{2(1-p)^2}{(1-m)^3}>0. \tag{15}
\]
Thus the sliced objective is strictly convex.  The identity \(g_{4/5}(m)=g_{1/5}(1-m)\) makes its derivative vanish at
\[
 (m_1,m_0)=(13/20,7/20), \tag{16}
\]
which lies in the relative interior of (14) and is therefore the unique minimizer.  Its value is
\[
 \widetilde C^{\mathrm{IHK}}_P(3/10)
 =J(13/20,7/20)
 =\frac{(3/20)^2}{(13/20)(7/20)}=\frac{9}{91}. \tag{17}
\]
By \(\mathrm{thm{:}symmetric\mbox{-}family}\), the same observed law and odds cap satisfy
\[
 C_P(3/10)=\frac{(3/5-3/10)^2}{1-3/10}=\frac{9}{70}. \tag{18}
\]
Therefore
\[
 C_P(3/10)-\widetilde C^{\mathrm{IHK}}_P(3/10)
 =\frac{9}{70}-\frac{9}{91}=\frac{27}{910}>0, \tag{19}
\]
which proves the exact strict separation without identifying the box-augmented relaxation with an unqualified published class.

%%% FIELD statement-local-uniform-gap
Fix \(\Gamma=4\) and the ATE slice \(3/10\).  There exists \(\eta>0\) such that every one-stratum binary observed law \(P\) satisfying
\[
 \max\{|e_x-1/2|,|p_1-4/5|,|p_0-1/5|\}<\eta
\]
has finite full-law and box-augmented Jensen--CMC frontier values at \(3/10\) and obeys
\[
 C_P(3/10)-\widetilde C^{\mathrm{IHK}}_P(3/10)\geq\frac{27}{1820}>0.
\]
Hence the exact rational witness belongs to a nonempty open Euclidean neighborhood of observed laws on which the compatible-full-law cost uniformly and strictly exceeds the value of the explicitly defined box-augmented Neyman specialization of Ishikawa--He--Kanamori equation~(14).

%%% FIELD proof-local-uniform-gap
Parameterize a one-stratum binary observed law by \(\theta=(e,p_1,p_0)\in(0,1)^3\), where \(e=e_x\).  This parameterization is exact because the observable cell probabilities for \((Z,Y)=(1,1),(1,0),(0,1),(0,0)\), respectively, are \(ep_1\), \(e(1-p_1)\), \((1-e)p_0\), and \((1-e)(1-p_0)\).  Its inverse is continuous on the positive-cell region because \(e=P(Z=1)\), \(p_1=P(Z=1,Y=1)/P(Z=1)\), and \(p_0=P(Z=0,Y=1)/P(Z=0)\), whose denominators are positive there.  Thus these coordinates give the natural Euclidean topology of the interior of the one-stratum observed-law simplex.  Put \(\theta_\star=(1/2,4/5,1/5)\) and \(\tau_\star=3/10\).  Write \(C_\theta\) and \(\widetilde C^{\mathrm{IHK}}_\theta\) for the two frontiers evaluated at the observed law indexed by \(\theta\).  The theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}strict\mbox{-}jensen\mbox{-}gap}\) gives
\[
 C_{\theta_\star}(\tau_\star)=\frac{9}{70},\qquad
 \widetilde C^{\mathrm{IHK}}_{\theta_\star}(\tau_\star)=\frac{9}{91},\qquad
 g_\star:=\frac{9}{70}-\frac{9}{91}=\frac{27}{910}. \tag{20}
\]
We prove continuity of both value functions at \(\theta_\star\) by transporting strictly interior feasible scalar points and by compactness of the minimizing intervals.

For the full-law value, let \(A_\theta=e(1-p_1)\), \(B_\theta=(1-e)p_0\), and \(D_\theta=1-ep_1-(1-e)p_0\), and form \(L_\theta,U_\theta,F_\theta\) from the formulas in \(\mathrm{def{:}binary\mbox{-}scalar\mbox{-}program}\) at \(\tau_\star\) and \(\Gamma=4\).  At \(\theta_\star\), \(A_{\theta_\star}=B_{\theta_\star}=1/10\), \(D_{\theta_\star}=1/2\), \(\ell=1/5\), and \(u=4/5\).  The three lower-face values are
\[
 \frac18,\quad\frac15,\quad\frac{13}{50},
\]
and the three upper-face values are
\[
 \frac12,\quad\frac{23}{40},\quad\frac{11}{25}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 [L_{\theta_\star},U_{\theta_\star}]=[13/50,11/25]. \tag{21}
\]
The point \(v_\star=7/20\) is strictly interior, with distance \(9/100\) from each endpoint, and direct substitution gives
\[
 F_{\theta_\star}(v_\star)
 =4\left(\frac1{35}+\frac5{28}+\frac3{40}-\frac14\right)
 =4\left(\frac9{280}\right)
 =\frac{9}{70}=C_{\theta_\star}(\tau_\star). \tag{22}
\]

Each candidate face in \(L_\theta\) and \(U_\theta\) is continuous for \(e\in(0,1)\); a finite maximum or minimum of continuous functions is continuous.  Hence (21) remains a nonempty interval and \(v_\star\) remains feasible for all \(\theta\) sufficiently close to \(\theta_\star\).  On a common compact rectangle around (21), the denominators \(v\), \(1-\tau_\star-v\), \(e\), and \(1-e\) are bounded away from zero, so \((\theta,v)\mapsto F_\theta(v)\) is continuous there.

To verify continuity of the minimum rather than merely assert it, take any sequence \(\theta_n\to\theta_\star\).  The feasible interior point \(v_\star\) and (22) give
\[
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}C_{\theta_n}(\tau_\star)
 \leq\lim_{n\to\infty}F_{\theta_n}(v_\star)
 =C_{\theta_\star}(\tau_\star). \tag{23}
\]
For each sufficiently large \(n\), \(\mathrm{thm{:}binary\mbox{-}phase\mbox{-}catalog}\) supplies a minimizer \(v_n\in[L_{\theta_n},U_{\theta_n}]\).  Along any subsequence realizing the lower limit, compactness supplies a further subsequence with \(v_n\to v\); continuity of the endpoints implies \(v\in[L_{\theta_\star},U_{\theta_\star}]\).  Therefore
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}C_{\theta_n}(\tau_\star)
 =F_{\theta_\star}(v)\geq C_{\theta_\star}(\tau_\star). \tag{24}
\]
Equations (23)--(24) prove \(C_\theta(\tau_\star)\to C_{\theta_\star}(\tau_\star)\).

For the relaxed value, write \(r_1=e w(\,\cdot\,,1)\) and \(r_0=(1-e)w(\,\cdot\,,0)\).  At \(\Gamma=4\), their lower and upper box endpoints are the continuous functions
\[
 a_1(e)=\frac{1+3e}{4},\quad b_1(e)=4-3e,qquad
 a_0(e)=\frac{4-3e}{4},\quad b_0(e)=1+3e. \tag{25}
\]
For \(z\in\{0,1\}\), let \(p_z=p_1\) when \(z=1\) and \(p_z=p_0\) when \(z=0\), and set
\[
 \lambda_z(\theta)=\max\{a_z(e)p_z,\,1-b_z(e)(1-p_z)\},\qquad
 \nu_z(\theta)=\min\{b_z(e)p_z,\,1-a_z(e)(1-p_z)\}. \tag{26}
\]
Binary support and armwise normalization show directly that the reweighted mean \(m_z\) ranges exactly over \([\lambda_z(\theta),\nu_z(\theta)]\): indeed \(r_z(1)=m_z/p_z\) and \(r_z(0)=(1-m_z)/(1-p_z)\), and imposing both box inequalities is equivalent to (26).  With \(x=m_0\) and \(m_1=x+\tau_\star\), the relaxed ATE slice is therefore the interval
\[
 \widetilde L_\theta=\max\{\lambda_0(\theta),\lambda_1(\theta)-\tau_\star\}
 \leq x\leq
 \min\{\nu_0(\theta),\nu_1(\theta)-\tau_\star\}=\widetilde U_\theta. \tag{27}
\]
For either arm, substitution of the normalized binary weights gives
\[
 p\,f_N(m/p)+(1-p)f_N((1-m)/(1-p))
 =\frac{p^2}{m}+\frac{(1-p)^2}{1-m}-1
 =\frac{(m-p)^2}{m(1-m)}.
\]
Multiplying these two armwise expressions by their observed treatment probabilities shows that the objective is exactly
\[
 \widetilde F_\theta(x)
 =e\,g_{p_1}(x+\tau_\star)+(1-e)g_{p_0}(x),qquad
 g_p(m)=\frac{(m-p)^2}{m(1-m)}. \tag{28}
\]
At \(\theta_\star\), (26)--(27) give
\[
 [\widetilde L_{\theta_\star},\widetilde U_{\theta_\star}]=[1/5,1/2], \tag{29}
\]
and \(x_\star=7/20\) is strictly interior, with distance \(3/20\) from each endpoint.  By (17),
\[
 \widetilde F_{\theta_\star}(x_\star)=\frac{9}{91}
 =\widetilde C^{\mathrm{IHK}}_{\theta_\star}(\tau_\star). \tag{30}
\]
The endpoints in (27) are continuous by (25)--(26).  Near (29), \(x\), \(1-x\), \(x+\tau_\star\), and \(1-x-\tau_\star\) are uniformly positive, so \(\widetilde F_\theta(x)\) is jointly continuous.  Repeating the sequence argument in (23)--(24), now using the fixed strictly feasible point \(x_\star\) for the upper limit and compactness of the intervals (27) for the lower limit, proves
\[
 \widetilde C^{\mathrm{IHK}}_\theta(\tau_\star)
 \longrightarrow
 \widetilde C^{\mathrm{IHK}}_{\theta_\star}(\tau_\star)
 \quad\text{as }\theta\to\theta_\star. \tag{31}
\]

By (20), (23)--(24), and (31), there exists \(\eta>0\), chosen also small enough that \(\theta\in(0,1)^3\) and both intervals (21) and (29) remain nonempty, such that \(\|\theta-\theta_\star\|_\infty<\eta\) implies
\[
 \left|C_\theta(\tau_\star)-\frac{9}{70}\right|<\frac{g_\star}{4},\qquad
 \left|\widetilde C^{\mathrm{IHK}}_\theta(\tau_\star)-\frac{9}{91}\right|<\frac{g_\star}{4}. \tag{32}
\]
Subtracting the two inequalities in (32) yields
\[
 C_\theta(\tau_\star)-\widetilde C^{\mathrm{IHK}}_\theta(\tau_\star)
 \geq g_\star-\frac{g_\star}{2}
 =\frac{27}{1820}>0. \tag{33}
\]
The strict ball in the statement is open and contains \(\theta_\star\), so it is a nonempty open neighborhood of observed laws.  Local nonemptiness of (21) and (27) also proves finiteness of both values throughout that neighborhood.
