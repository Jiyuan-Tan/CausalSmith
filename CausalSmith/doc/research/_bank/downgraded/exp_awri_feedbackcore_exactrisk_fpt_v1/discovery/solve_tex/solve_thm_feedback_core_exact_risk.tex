Because every priority variable has a density on \((0,1)\), ties have \(P_\ell\)-probability zero; the claim is therefore about a strict order.  Suppose first that the scan in \(\texttt{def:wri-law}\) returns \(S\).  Two adjacent vertices cannot both be in \(S\): of the two, the later one encounters the earlier selected neighbor and is rejected.  Thus \(S\) is independent.  If \(v\notin S\), then at the instant when \(v\) is scanned it is rejected, so there is a neighbor \(u\in S\) already scanned.  This \(u\) precedes \(v\).

Conversely, suppose that \(S\) is independent and every \(v\notin S\) has a neighbor in \(S\) preceding it.  If the greedy output differed from \(S\), let \(w\) be the first vertex in the order at which membership differs.  If \(w\in S\), then every earlier selected vertex agrees with \(S\); independence of \(S\) implies that none is adjacent to \(w\), so the scan selects \(w\), a contradiction.  If \(w\notin S\), choose an earlier neighbor \(u\in S\).  By minimality of \(w\), the scan has selected \(u\), so it rejects \(w\), again a contradiction.  Hence the scan output is exactly \(S\).

Fix a rule \(\ell\), a tag \(A\), and a realization \(x\in(0,1)^{\mathcal K}\) outside the null set of ties.  Let \(\mathcal U=S_\ell\cap\mathcal K\).  By \(\texttt{lem:greedy-domination-certificate}\), \(\mathcal U\) is independent, and the tag requires \(A\cap\mathcal K\subseteq\mathcal U\).  Conversely, fix any such \(\mathcal U\) and temporarily impose all greedy-certificate conditions except domination of vertices in \(\mathcal K\setminus\mathcal U\).

For \(f\in\mathcal K\setminus\mathcal U\), let \(E_f\) be the event that \(f\) has no selected neighbor of priority greater than \(x_f\).  The missing core conditions are exactly \(\bigcap_f E_f^{\mathsf c}\).  Finite inclusion--exclusion gives
\[
 \mathbf 1\!\left\{\bigcap_{f\in\mathcal K\setminus\mathcal U}E_f^{\mathsf c}\right\}
 =\sum_{\mathcal B\subseteq\mathcal K\setminus\mathcal U}(-1)^{|\mathcal B|}
   \mathbf 1\!\left\{\bigcap_{f\in\mathcal B}E_f\right\}.

On \(\bigcap_{f\in\mathcal B}E_f\), a selected core neighbor \(u\in\mathcal U\cap N_{C_G}(f)\) must satisfy \(x_u<x_f\); these inequalities are precisely \(\chi_{\mathcal U,\mathcal B}(x)=1\).  A selected forest vertex \(v\) adjacent to a member of \(\mathcal B\) must similarly have priority
\[
 y<\min\bigl(\{x_f:f\in\mathcal B\cap N_{C_G}(v)\}\cup\{1\}\bigr)=r_v(x).
\]
Independence from the selected core requires \(N_{C_G}(v)\cap\mathcal U=\varnothing\) whenever \(v\) is selected.  An unselected forest vertex lacks a higher-priority selected core neighbor exactly when
\[
 y\ge \max\bigl(\{x_u:u\in\mathcal U\cap N_{C_G}(v)\}\cup\{0\}\bigr)=p_v(x).
\]

It remains to verify the three forest messages.  This is by induction from the leaves toward the root in each component, which is legitimate by \(\texttt{ass:feedback-forest}\).  Conditional on the priority \(y\) of \(v\), different child subtrees contain disjoint independent priority variables.  If \(v\) is selected, a child \(w\) must either already be certified, contributing \(J_w=\int_0^1B_w(t,z)\,dt\), or require its selected parent and have \(t<y\), contributing \(\int_0^yC_w(t,z)\,dt\).  Multiplying these alternatives, the density \(f_v(y)\), the count marker \(z\), the independence indicator, and the cutoff indicator gives exactly \(A_v\).

If \(v\) is unselected and itself receives no certificate, then every child must either already be certified or be selected below \(v\); the child contribution is
\[
 E_w(y)=J_w+\int_0^yA_w(t,z)\,dt.
\]
The absence of a selected core certificate is \(y\ge p_v(x)\), so the product is exactly \(C_v\).  If \(v\) is unselected but valid without its parent, no child may be in its parent-needing state.  All such child configurations have total weight \(\prod_wD_w\), where \(D_w=\int_0^1(A_w+B_w)\), and subtracting the disjoint uncertified case \(C_v\) gives \(B_v\).  These cases are exhaustive and disjoint.  Setting \(B_v=C_v=0\) at a tagged forest vertex forces that vertex to be selected, as required by \(A\subseteq S_\ell\).

At a component root the parent-needing state is invalid, hence integration of \(A_{v_T}+B_{v_T}\) gives \(P_{T;\mathcal U,\mathcal B,A}\).  Conditional on \(x\), distinct forest components factor.  Multiplication by \(z^{|\mathcal U|}\), by the independent core density \(\prod_{f\in\mathcal K}h_{\ell f}x_f^{h_{\ell f}-1}\), summation over \(\mathcal U,\mathcal B\), and integration over \((0,1)^{\mathcal K}\) therefore gives exactly \(F_{\ell,A}(z)\).  This is the formula defining \(\mathcal R_{\ell,A}(z)\), so \(F_{\ell,A}=\mathcal R_{\ell,A}\).  Each core vertex is either in \(\mathcal U\), in \(\mathcal B\), or in neither; consequently there are at most \(3^k\) pairs before chamber decomposition.  Boundaries have probability zero because all priority laws are continuous.

Then \(X^0\succ0\), \(\operatorname{tr}(AX^0)=1\), and \(\operatorname{tr}(Q_\ell X^0)-s^0\ge1\) for every \(\ell\).  These seeds and their margins have encoding length polynomial in \(\Lambda\).  At an optimum one may restrict \(0\le t\le R\).  On \(\mathcal D\), \(\operatorname{tr}(X)\le1\), hence \(|X_{ij}|\le1\), and one may restrict \(-1\le s\le R\).  Both feasible regions are therefore rationally well bounded in their affine hulls.

All linear constraints have exact rational separation.  A rational symmetric matrix can be tested for positive semidefiniteness by fraction-free symmetric elimination: a positive diagonal pivot is removed by a rational Schur complement; a zero diagonal in a positive semidefinite matrix forces its row to be zero; a negative pivot or a nonzero row at a zero diagonal yields a rational vector of negative quadratic form.  This gives a polynomial-time rational separating hyperplane whenever the matrix is not positive semidefinite.  The cited weak-optimization theorem therefore returns rational weak primal and dual solutions with accuracy \(\delta\) in time polynomial in \(L,n,\Lambda,\log(1/\delta)\).

Weak feasibility is converted to exact feasibility as follows.  First enforce the rational affine equalities \(\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}q=1\) and \(\operatorname{tr}(AX)=1\) by rational elimination.  Mix the resulting approximants with \((q^0,t^0)\) and \((X^0,s^0)\) with rational weight \(\eta>0\).  The primal matrix slack is then at least \(\eta I\), the dual matrix slack is at least \(\eta\lambda_{\min}(X^0)I\), every simplex coordinate has margin \(\eta/L\), and every dual rule inequality has margin at least \(\eta\), before approximation error.  Choose \(\delta\) smaller than one half of each of these margins divided by the rational operator norms of the constraint maps.  Rational outward adjustments of \(t\) and downward adjustments of \(s\), each of size at most the remaining error bound, then give exact rational feasibility.  Because \(\lambda_{\min}(X^0)=1/\operatorname{tr}(A)\) and every displayed norm and margin has polynomial encoding length, one can choose \(\eta\) and \(\delta\) with
\[
 \log(1/\eta)+\log(1/\delta)
 =\operatorname{poly}(\Lambda)+O(\log(1/\varepsilon))
\]
so that the total objective loss is at most \(\varepsilon/B^2\).  This yields a rational tuple \((\widehat q,\widehat t,\widehat X,\widehat s)\) satisfying every condition in \(\texttt{def:rational-gap-certificate}\) and \(B^2(\widehat t-\widehat s)\le\varepsilon\).

Finally, for any feasible primal and dual tuples,
\[
 \widehat t
 =\operatorname{tr}(\widehat tA\widehat X)
 \ge\operatorname{tr}(Q(\widehat q)\widehat X)
 =\sum_\ell\widehat q_\ell\operatorname{tr}(Q_\ell\widehat X)
 \ge\widehat s.
\]
Together with equality of optimal values, this is weak duality and proves
\[
 B^2\widehat s\le V^\star\le B^2\widehat t,
 \qquad B^2(\widehat t-\widehat s)\le\varepsilon.
\]
The same fraction-free elimination verifies every positive-semidefinite constraint exactly; all remaining checks are rational linear arithmetic.  The running time has the asserted polynomial dependence after setting the requested weak-optimization accuracy as above.
