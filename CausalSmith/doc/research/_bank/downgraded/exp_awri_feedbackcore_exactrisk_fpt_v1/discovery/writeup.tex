\section*{exp\_\allowbreak{}awri\_\allowbreak{}feedbackcore\_\allowbreak{}exactrisk\_\allowbreak{}fpt}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} Weighted random isolation creates exposure-compatible focal units under network interference, but published AWRI rule selection relies on simulation. Conditioning on a supplied size-\(k\) feedback vertex set and applying a three-state forest recursion with \(3^k\) core inclusion--exclusion yields an exact, fully evaluated dense algorithm for every zero-, one-, and two-tagged isolated-count law in \(Ln^2k!3^k(nb+n+1)^{O(k)}\operatorname{poly}(\lambda)\) bit operations. The tagged laws reconstruct every dense RDIM risk matrix. Rational weak semidefinite optimization then returns an independently checkable \(\varepsilon\)-minimax certificate in time polynomial in \(L\), \(n\), \(\Lambda\), and the binary encoding length of \(\varepsilon\); for canonical \(\varepsilon=2^{-p}\), this dependence is polynomial in \(p=\log_2(1/\varepsilon)\). When the supplied core is a vertex cover, an entered-subset cumulative-exponent dynamic program evaluates every admissible chamber implicitly and computes all tagged laws, with no unevaluated integral, in \(L6^k\operatorname{poly}(n,b,\lambda)\) bit operations with polynomial intermediate rational bit length. An evaluated recurrence also covers identical replicated-star attachments, but fixed-parameter closure for arbitrary heterogeneous forest attachments remains open.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} AWRI fixes the weighted greedy design and balanced RDIM estimator but simulates a surrogate. Random sequential adsorption treats scalar occupation, while structural graph programs count static sets. General semidefinite design methods neither supply the exact WRI/RDIM risk matrices nor give an end-to-end rational certificate with complexity measured against the binary encoding of an arbitrary rational tolerance. None of these results gives the evaluated heterogeneous tagged integration used here. The entered-subset dynamic program closes the vertex-cover special case, without an integration gate, in \(L6^k\operatorname{poly}(n,b,\lambda)\) bit operations, but arbitrary heterogeneous forest attachments still lack a fixed-parameter representation.

\paragraph{Niche.} A supplied feedback set exposes a forest decomposition on which exact heterogeneous priority integration is possible. The proved general guarantee is XP in the feedback size. If the supplied core is a vertex cover, every exterior component is a singleton and the cumulative priority exponent is determined by the subset of core coordinates already entered; this yields an \(L6^k\operatorname{poly}(n,b,\lambda)\) dynamic program with polynomial rational height. Identical attachment profiles give a second evaluated special regime. Once the exact rational risk library is available, the finite ellipsoidal risk game admits a rational primal-dual certificate whose computation is polynomial in the encoded tolerance; for dyadic tolerance \(2^{-p}\), it is polynomial in \(p\).

\paragraph{Fill.} The paper proves core inclusion--exclusion, a fully evaluated dense chamber-polynomial algorithm with controlled rational bit height, exact tagged RDIM risk reconstruction, and a rational near-minimax certificate. In the vertex-cover subclass, it replaces factorial chamber enumeration by an entered-subset recurrence, obtaining all tagged laws exactly and without unevaluated integrals in \(L6^k\operatorname{poly}(n,b,\lambda)\) bit operations with polynomial intermediate rational height. The certificate algorithm is polynomial in \(L\), \(n\), \(\Lambda\), and the binary encoding length of \(\varepsilon\), specializing to polynomial dependence on \(p=\log_2(1/\varepsilon)\) for canonical dyadic input \(\varepsilon=2^{-p}\). The paper records arbitrary-forest fixed-parameter moment closure as an open problem rather than assuming it.

\section{Related work}

Shi, Yang, Qin, and Li supply the published directed-network FNI/TTE/WRI plus balanced-RDIM causal-design construction used here. This note first identifies WRI deletion with greedy maximal-independent-set selection on the neighborhood-overlap conflict graph, and then proves exact algorithms only for the positive-integer-weight subclass with a supplied feedback set. Positive rational priority weights transfer by multiplying all exponential, equivalently Beta, rates by a common denominator, which preserves their random order; arbitrary published real weights are not covered by the exact Turing-model algorithms. Shi, Yang, Qin, and Li derive a balance and inverse-size MSE surrogate and use simulation for candidate-weight selection. Pippenger's random-sequential-adsorption recursions, Gamarnik and Goldberg's rooted-tree bonus recursions, and the local-limit equations of Krivelevich, Meszaros, Michaeli, and Shikhelman do not compute exact finite heterogeneous tagged moments. Wan, Tu, Zhang, and Li count static independent sets on bounded-treewidth graphs, and Borie, Parker, and Tovey give finite-state decomposition methods, but neither weights maximal independent sets by heterogeneous priority orders. Cygan, Pilipczuk, Pilipczuk, and Wojtaszczyk establish a \(2^{O(k\log k)}\) scale for a different feedback-set problem, not an oracle for the present integration. The present \(L6^k\operatorname{poly}(n,b,\lambda)\) vertex-cover bound instead comes from a problem-specific entered-subset dynamic program for cumulative cutoff exponents; it contains no unevaluated integral and does not settle heterogeneous forest closure. Kandiros, Harshaw, and Savje study an unrestricted network-experiment minimax functional; no equality with that functional is claimed. Kallus motivates mixed minimax design, and Duarte, Sagnol, and Wong develop semidefinite minimax-design methods. Boyd and Vandenberghe supply the finite-dimensional separating-hyperplane theorem and its open-set converse used in the minimax interchange. Groetschel, Lovasz, and Schrijver's Theorem 4.3.13 supplies weak constrained convex minimization for centered convex bodies given by weak-membership and convex-function value oracles; the rational affine-SDP parametrization, radius bounds, membership tests, and pullback needed here are proved separately in this note. Aronow and Samii provide the design-based interference framework. Cai, de Janvry, and Sadoulet motivate the rural-China insurance application but provide no evidence that its measured conflict graph has small feedback number or a small vertex cover.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:full-neighborhood-interference]\label{ass:full-neighborhood-interference}
\(\forall i\in V,\ \forall \mathbf z,\mathbf z'\in\{0,1\}^n:\ \mathbf z_{D_i}=\mathbf z'_{D_i}\Rightarrow Y_i(\mathbf z)=Y_i(\mathbf z')\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Full-neighborhood interference, \cite{ShiYangQinLi2024}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:design-based-randomness]\label{ass:design-based-randomness}
\(\{Y_i(\mathbf z):i\in V,\mathbf z\in\{0,1\}^n\}\text{ is nonrandom under }P_{\ell}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Finite-population design-based randomness, \cite{AronowSamii2017GeneralInterference}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:feedback-forest]\label{ass:feedback-forest}
\(C_G-\mathcal K\) is a forest.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} A small set of brokers, market hubs, or cross-village connectors can contain every cycle while within-village residual contact structure is tree-like; the condition is directly checkable after the network is measured.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:nondegenerate-isolation]\label{ass:nondegenerate-isolation}
\(K_{\ell}(\pi)\ge 2\) for every \(\ell\in\{1,\ldots,L\}\) and every strict priority order \(\pi\) on \(V\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} The condition is equivalent to the conflict graph having no universal vertex and is satisfied by experiments spanning at least two disconnected villages or communities, since each component contributes a focal unit.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:endpoint-ellipsoid]\label{ass:endpoint-ellipsoid}
\(\vartheta^{\mathsf T}H^{-1}\vartheta\le B^2\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} The graph-Sobolev ellipsoid allows arbitrary levels while controlling variation across conflict edges, a transparent sensitivity class for bounded or smoothly varying village-network endpoints after choosing a conservative radius.
\end{assumption}

\section{Definitions}

\begin{definition}[def:conflict-graph]\label{def:conflict-graph}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{WRI unit-\allowbreak{}isolation conflict graph}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(C_G=(V,E_C)\), where \(\{i,j\}\in E_C\) if and only if \(i\ne j\) and \(D_i\cap D_j\ne\varnothing\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{G}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:wri-law]\label{def:wri-law}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Sequential weighted random isolation law}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Independently draw \(X_{\ell i}\) with density \(h_{\ell i}x^{h_{\ell i}-1}\) on \((0,1)\), scan vertices in decreasing \(X_{\ell i}\), and include a scanned vertex in \(S_{\ell}\) exactly when it has no previously included neighbor in \(C_G\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{conflictGraph}; \texttt{weights}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:tagged-count-polynomial]\label{def:tagged-count-polynomial}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Tagged isolated-\allowbreak{}count polynomial}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(F_{\ell,A}(z)=\mathbb E_{\ell}[\mathbf 1\{A\subseteq S_{\ell}\}z^{K_{\ell}}]\), for every \(A\subseteq V\) with \(|A|\le2\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{ruleLaw}; \texttt{isolatedSet}; \texttt{tagSet}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:feedback-core-input-class]\label{def:feedback-core-input-class}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Feedback-\allowbreak{}core WRI input class}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\mathfrak C_k=\{(n,C_G,\mathcal K,h):n\ge2,\ |\mathcal K|=k,\ C_G-\mathcal K\text{ is a forest},\ h\in\{1,\ldots,b\}^{L\times n}\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Member properties.} \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}feedback-\allowbreak{}forest}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:cutoff-forest-messages]\label{def:cutoff-forest-messages}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Three-\allowbreak{}state cutoff-\allowbreak{}forest messages}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Fix an independent \(\mathcal U\subseteq\mathcal K\), a blocker set \(\mathcal B\subseteq\mathcal K\setminus\mathcal U\), and core priorities \(x\in(0,1)^{\mathcal K}\). For \(v\notin\mathcal K\), put \(p_v(x)=\max(\{x_u:u\in\mathcal U\cap N_{C_G}(v)\}\cup\{0\})\) and \(r_v(x)=\min(\{x_f:f\in\mathcal B\cap N_{C_G}(v)\}\cup\{1\})\). Root each component of \(C_G-\mathcal K\). For a child \(w\) define \(J_w=\int_0^1B_w(t,z)\,dt\), \(D_w=\int_0^1(A_w(t,z)+B_w(t,z))\,dt\), and \(E_w(y)=J_w+\int_0^yA_w(t,z)\,dt\). With \(f_v(y)=h_{\ell v}y^{h_{\ell v}-1}\), set \(A_v(y,z)=zf_v(y)\mathbf 1\{N_{C_G}(v)\cap\mathcal U=\varnothing\}\mathbf 1\{y<r_v(x)\}\prod_w(J_w+\int_0^yC_w(t,z)\,dt)\), \(C_v(y,z)=f_v(y)\mathbf 1\{y\ge p_v(x)\}\prod_wE_w(y)\), and \(B_v(y,z)=f_v(y)\prod_wD_w-C_v(y,z)\); at a tagged forest vertex set \(B_v=C_v=0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{feedbackSet}; \texttt{conflictGraph}; \texttt{weights}; \texttt{tagSet}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:core-inclusion-exclusion]\label{def:core-inclusion-exclusion}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Feedback-\allowbreak{}core inclusion-\allowbreak{}exclusion functional}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For a rooted forest component \(T\) with root \(v_T\), put \(P_{T;\mathcal U,\mathcal B,A}(x,z)=\int_0^1(A_{v_T}(y,z)+B_{v_T}(y,z))\,dy\) and \(\chi_{\mathcal U,\mathcal B}(x)=\mathbf 1\{x_u<x_f\text{ for every }f\in\mathcal B,\ u\in\mathcal U\cap N_{C_G}(f)\}\). Define \(\mathcal R_{\ell,A}(z)=\sum_{\substack{\mathcal U\subseteq\mathcal K\text{ independent}\\A\cap\mathcal K\subseteq\mathcal U}}z^{|\mathcal U|}\sum_{\mathcal B\subseteq\mathcal K\setminus\mathcal U}(-1)^{|\mathcal B|}\int_{(0,1)^{\mathcal K}}\chi_{\mathcal U,\mathcal B}(x)\prod_{f\in\mathcal K}h_{\ell f}x_f^{h_{\ell f}-1}\prod_{T\in\operatorname{comp}(C_G-\mathcal K)}P_{T;\mathcal U,\mathcal B,A}(x,z)\,dx\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{forestMessages}; \texttt{feedbackSet}; \texttt{weights}; \texttt{tagSet}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:evaluated-moment-handle]\label{def:evaluated-moment-handle}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Ordered-\allowbreak{}ratio evaluated moment handle}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For each of the at most \(k!\) strict core-priority chambers, substitute ordered ratios \(t_r=x_r/x_{r+1}\) for \(r<k\) and \(t_k=x_k\). Starting from the nested-moment recurrence for identical attachments, construct a finite operator algebra indexed only by core order types, tag states, and shared future-prefix transformations; require direct rational routines for child multiplication, component multiplication, moving-endpoint integration, and coefficient extraction, with no stored vector of \(k\) independent degree totals and no unevaluated integration gate.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{forestMessages}; \texttt{feedbackSet}; \texttt{weights}; \texttt{tagSet}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:replicated-star-test]\label{def:replicated-star-test}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Directed-\allowbreak{}realizable replicated-\allowbreak{}star stress test}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Let \(\mathcal K=\{f_1,\ldots,f_k\}\). For each \(j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\) and \(i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\), add vertices \(a_{ji},b_{ji}\), add a center \(c_j\), and use directed arcs \(a_{ji}\to f_i\), \(a_{ji}\to b_{ji}\), and \(b_{ji}\to c_j\). Add \(M\) private in-neighbors of each \(f_i\). The resulting WRI conflict graph consists of triangles \(\{f_i,a_{ji},b_{ji}\}\), edges \(b_{ji}c_j\), and the private core leaves.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{feedbackSize}; \texttt{starMultiplicity}; \texttt{privateLeafCount}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:rdim-construction]\label{def:rdim-construction}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Balanced restricted difference in means}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Given \(S_{\ell}\) of size \(K_{\ell}\), draw \(T_{\ell}\) uniformly among its \(t(K_{\ell})\)-subsets, put \(U_{\ell}=S_{\ell}\setminus T_{\ell}\), assign exposure \(\mathbf 1_{D_i}\) to every \(i\in T_{\ell}\) and \(\mathbf 0_{D_i}\) to every \(i\in U_{\ell}\), and compute \(\widehat\tau_{\ell}=e_{\ell}^{\mathsf T}\theta\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{isolatedSet}; \texttt{potentialOutcomes}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:tagged-risk-functional]\label{def:tagged-risk-functional}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Tagged-\allowbreak{}polynomial risk reconstruction}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Put \(p_{\ell i}(r)=[z^r]F_{\ell,\{i\}}(z)\), \(p_{\ell ij}(r)=[z^r]F_{\ell,\{i,j\}}(z)\), \(\rho_{\ell i}=\sum_{r=2}^np_{\ell i}(r)/r\), and \(m_{\ell}=(\rho_{\ell}^{\mathsf T},-\rho_{\ell}^{\mathsf T})^{\mathsf T}\). Define \(M_{\ell}^{11}(i,i)=\sum_rp_{\ell i}(r)/(r t(r))\), \(M_{\ell}^{00}(i,i)=\sum_rp_{\ell i}(r)/(r c(r))\), and, for \(i\ne j\), \(M_{\ell}^{11}(i,j)=\sum_rp_{\ell ij}(r)(t(r)-1)/(t(r)r(r-1))\), \(M_{\ell}^{00}(i,j)=\sum_rp_{\ell ij}(r)(c(r)-1)/(c(r)r(r-1))\), \(M_{\ell}^{10}(i,j)=-\sum_rp_{\ell ij}(r)/(r(r-1))\), with \(M_{\ell}^{10}(i,i)=0\) and \(M_{\ell}^{01}=(M_{\ell}^{10})^{\mathsf T}\). Set \(M_{\ell}=\begin{psmallmatrix}M_{\ell}^{11}&M_{\ell}^{10}\\M_{\ell}^{01}&M_{\ell}^{00}\end{psmallmatrix}\) and \(Q_{\ell}=M_{\ell}-m_{\ell}d^{\mathsf T}-dm_{\ell}^{\mathsf T}+dd^{\mathsf T}\).

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:greedy-domination-certificate]\label{lem:greedy-domination-certificate}
For every strict priority order \(\pi_{\ell}\), a set \(S\subseteq V\) is the WRI greedy output if and only if \(S\) is independent in \(C_G\) and every \(v\in V\setminus S\) has a neighbor \(u\in S\) that precedes \(v\) in \(\pi_{\ell}\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Because every priority variable has a density on \((0,1)\), ties have \(P_\ell\)-probability zero; the claim is therefore about a strict order.  Suppose first that the scan in \(\texttt{def:wri-law}\) returns \(S\).  Two adjacent vertices cannot both be in \(S\): of the two, the later one encounters the earlier selected neighbor and is rejected.  Thus \(S\) is independent.  If \(v\notin S\), then at the instant when \(v\) is scanned it is rejected, so there is a neighbor \(u\in S\) already scanned.  This \(u\) precedes \(v\).

Conversely, suppose that \(S\) is independent and every \(v\notin S\) has a neighbor in \(S\) preceding it.  If the greedy output differed from \(S\), let \(w\) be the first vertex in the order at which membership differs.  If \(w\in S\), then every earlier selected vertex agrees with \(S\); independence of \(S\) implies that none is adjacent to \(w\), so the scan selects \(w\), a contradiction.  If \(w\notin S\), choose an earlier neighbor \(u\in S\).  By minimality of \(w\), the scan has selected \(u\), so it rejects \(w\), again a contradiction.  Hence the scan output is exactly \(S\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The certificate replaces the history-dependent scan by independence and earlier-neighbor domination constraints. \textit{Gap.} Random sequential adsorption uses the same greedy mechanism but does not formulate the certificate for heterogeneous tagged exact integration. \textit{Consumer.} The core inclusion--exclusion proof uses the certificate to separate forest components after conditioning on core priorities.

\begin{proposition}[prop:core-inclusion-exclusion]\label{prop:core-inclusion-exclusion}
For every feedback-core input and every tag \(A\) with \(|A|\le2\), the exact tagged polynomial satisfies \(F_{\ell,A}(z)=\mathcal R_{\ell,A}(z)\), where \(\mathcal R_{\ell,A}\) is the evaluated target specified in \(\texttt{def:core-inclusion-exclusion}\). The identity uses at most \(3^k\) pairs \((\mathcal U,\mathcal B)\) before core-order chamber decomposition.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix a rule \(\ell\), a tag \(A\), and a realization \(x\in(0,1)^{\mathcal K}\) outside the null set of ties.  Let \(\mathcal U=S_\ell\cap\mathcal K\).  By \(\texttt{lem:greedy-domination-certificate}\), \(\mathcal U\) is independent, and the tag requires \(A\cap\mathcal K\subseteq\mathcal U\).  Conversely, fix any such \(\mathcal U\) and temporarily impose all greedy-certificate conditions except domination of vertices in \(\mathcal K\setminus\mathcal U\).

For \(f\in\mathcal K\setminus\mathcal U\), let \(E_f\) be the event that \(f\) has no selected neighbor of priority greater than \(x_f\).  The missing core conditions are exactly \(\bigcap_f E_f^{\mathsf c}\).  Finite inclusion--exclusion gives
\[
 \mathbf 1\!\left\{\bigcap_{f\in\mathcal K\setminus\mathcal U}E_f^{\mathsf c}\right\}
 =\sum_{\mathcal B\subseteq\mathcal K\setminus\mathcal U}(-1)^{|\mathcal B|}
   \mathbf 1\!\left\{\bigcap_{f\in\mathcal B}E_f\right\}.
\]
On \(\bigcap_{f\in\mathcal B}E_f\), a selected core neighbor \(u\in\mathcal U\cap N_{C_G}(f)\) must satisfy \(x_u<x_f\); these inequalities are precisely \(\chi_{\mathcal U,\mathcal B}(x)=1\).  A selected forest vertex \(v\) adjacent to a member of \(\mathcal B\) must similarly have priority
\[
 y<\min\bigl(\{x_f:f\in\mathcal B\cap N_{C_G}(v)\}\cup\{1\}\bigr)=r_v(x).
\]
Independence from the selected core requires \(N_{C_G}(v)\cap\mathcal U=\varnothing\) whenever \(v\) is selected.  An unselected forest vertex lacks a higher-priority selected core neighbor exactly when
\[
 y\ge \max\bigl(\{x_u:u\in\mathcal U\cap N_{C_G}(v)\}\cup\{0\}\bigr)=p_v(x).
\]

It remains to verify the three forest messages.  This is by induction from the leaves toward the root in each component, which is legitimate by \(\texttt{ass:feedback-forest}\).  Conditional on the priority \(y\) of \(v\), different child subtrees contain disjoint independent priority variables.  If \(v\) is selected, a child \(w\) must either already be certified, contributing \(J_w=\int_0^1B_w(t,z)\,dt\), or require its selected parent and have \(t<y\), contributing \(\int_0^yC_w(t,z)\,dt\).  Multiplying these alternatives, the density \(f_v(y)\), the count marker \(z\), the independence indicator, and the cutoff indicator gives exactly \(A_v\).

If \(v\) is unselected and itself receives no certificate, then every child must either already be certified or be selected below \(v\); the child contribution is
\[
 E_w(y)=J_w+\int_0^yA_w(t,z)\,dt.
\]
The absence of a selected core certificate is \(y\ge p_v(x)\), so the product is exactly \(C_v\).  If \(v\) is unselected but valid without its parent, no child may be in its parent-needing state.  All such child configurations have total weight \(\prod_wD_w\), where \(D_w=\int_0^1(A_w+B_w)\), and subtracting the disjoint uncertified case \(C_v\) gives \(B_v\).  These cases are exhaustive and disjoint.  Setting \(B_v=C_v=0\) at a tagged forest vertex forces that vertex to be selected, as required by \(A\subseteq S_\ell\).

At a component root the parent-needing state is invalid, hence integration of \(A_{v_T}+B_{v_T}\) gives \(P_{T;\mathcal U,\mathcal B,A}\).  Conditional on \(x\), distinct forest components factor.  Multiplication by \(z^{|\mathcal U|}\), by the independent core density \(\prod_{f\in\mathcal K}h_{\ell f}x_f^{h_{\ell f}-1}\), summation over \(\mathcal U,\mathcal B\), and integration over \((0,1)^{\mathcal K}\) therefore gives exactly \(F_{\ell,A}(z)\).  This is the formula defining \(\mathcal R_{\ell,A}(z)\), so \(F_{\ell,A}=\mathcal R_{\ell,A}\).  Each core vertex is either in \(\mathcal U\), in \(\mathcal B\), or in neither; consequently there are at most \(3^k\) pairs before chamber decomposition.  Boundaries have probability zero because all priority laws are continuous.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The identity removes the need to carry already-satisfied core blockers through every forest message. \textit{Gap.} Scalar occupation recursions do not include core domination inclusion--exclusion or zero-, one-, and two-vertex tags. \textit{Consumer.} The proved dense XP oracle and both fixed-parameter subclasses start from this exact reduction.

\begin{theorem}[thm:vertex-cover-core-oracle]\label{thm:vertex-cover-core-oracle}
If the supplied \(\mathcal K\) is a vertex cover of \(C_G\), then all \(F_{\ell,A}(z)\), \(|A|\le2\), are computable exactly in \(L6^k\operatorname{poly}(n,b,\lambda)\) bit operations, with no unevaluated integral, and every intermediate rational has \(\operatorname{poly}(n,b,\lambda)\) bit length.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write \(N(v)=N_{C_G}(v)\) and \(N(H)=\bigcup_{u\in H}N(u)\).  Because \(\mathcal K\) is a vertex cover, \(V\setminus\mathcal K\) is edgeless.  If \(k=0\), then \(C_G\) itself is edgeless, so the greedy rule in \(\texttt{def:wri-law}\) selects every vertex for every strict priority order.  Consequently
\[
 F_{\ell,A}(z)=z^n
 \qquad (|A|\leq 2)
\]
by \(\texttt{def:tagged-count-polynomial}\).  This case takes polynomial output time and uses only integers.  Henceforth suppose \(k\geq1\).

Fix a rule \(\ell\), an independent set \(\mathcal U\subseteq\mathcal K\), and \(\mathcal B\subseteq\mathcal K\setminus\mathcal U\).  Define
\[
 W_{\mathcal U}=(V\setminus\mathcal K)\cap N(\mathcal U),
 \qquad
 T_{\mathcal U}=(V\setminus\mathcal K)\setminus N(\mathcal U),
 \qquad
 S_{\mathcal U}=\mathcal U\cup T_{\mathcal U}.
\]
If a greedy output has intersection \(\mathcal U\) with the core, independence excludes every vertex in \(W_{\mathcal U}\).  Conversely, each vertex in \(T_{\mathcal U}\) must be selected.  Indeed, if \(v\in T_{\mathcal U}\) were unselected, \(\texttt{lem:greedy-domination-certificate}\) would provide a previously selected neighbor of \(v\); there is no such neighbor in \(\mathcal U\) by the definition of \(T_{\mathcal U}\), and there is no such neighbor in \(V\setminus\mathcal K\) because that set is edgeless.  Thus \(S_{\mathcal U}\) is the unique possible greedy output whose intersection with \(\mathcal K\) is \(\mathcal U\).  In particular, the contribution indexed by \(\mathcal U\) is zero unless \(A\subseteq S_{\mathcal U}\).

The vertex-cover condition also makes every component of \(C_G-\mathcal K\) a singleton.  For an untagged \(v\in W_{\mathcal U}\), the selected message in \(\texttt{def:cutoff-forest-messages}\) is zero, while the singleton component factor is
\[
 \int_0^1 B_v(y,z)\,dy
 =\int_0^{p_v(x)}h_{\ell v}y^{h_{\ell v}-1}\,dy
 =p_v(x)^{h_{\ell v}},
 \qquad
 p_v(x)=\max_{u\in\mathcal U\cap N(v)}x_u.
\]
For \(v\in T_{\mathcal U}\), one has \(p_v(x)=0\).  Hence \(C_v(y,z)=h_{\ell v}y^{h_{\ell v}-1}\), \(B_v(y,z)=0\), and the component factor is
\[
 \int_0^1 A_v(y,z)\,dy
 =z\int_0^{r_v(x)}h_{\ell v}y^{h_{\ell v}-1}\,dy
 =z r_v(x)^{h_{\ell v}},
 \qquad
 r_v(x)=\min\bigl(\{x_f:f\in\mathcal B\cap N(v)\}\cup\{1\}\bigr).
\]
This formula is unchanged when \(v\) is tagged, because tagging sets \(B_v=C_v=0\).  If instead a tagged exterior vertex belongs to \(W_{\mathcal U}\), then \(A_v=0\) and tagging also sets \(B_v=C_v=0\), so its component factor is zero.  Combining this exterior-tag test with the core-tag condition in \(\texttt{def:core-inclusion-exclusion}\), and then invoking \(\texttt{prop:core-inclusion-exclusion}\), gives
\[
 F_{\ell,A}(z)
 =\sum_{\substack{\mathcal U\subseteq\mathcal K\text{ independent}\\ A\subseteq S_{\mathcal U}}}
 z^{|S_{\mathcal U}|}
 \sum_{\mathcal B\subseteq\mathcal K\setminus\mathcal U}
 (-1)^{|\mathcal B|} I_{\ell,\mathcal U,\mathcal B},                         \tag{1}
\]
where
\[
\begin{split}
 I_{\ell,\mathcal U,\mathcal B}
 ={}&\int_{(0,1)^{\mathcal K}}
 \chi_{\mathcal U,\mathcal B}(x)
 \prod_{f\in\mathcal K}h_{\ell f}x_f^{h_{\ell f}-1}
 \prod_{v\in W_{\mathcal U}}
 \left(\max_{u\in\mathcal U\cap N(v)}x_u\right)^{h_{\ell v}}\\
 &\quad\cdot
 \prod_{v\in T_{\mathcal U}}
 \left(\min\bigl(\{x_f:f\in\mathcal B\cap N(v)\}\cup\{1\}\bigr)\right)^{h_{\ell v}}\,dx .       \tag{2}
\end{split}
\]
All exponents in (2) are nonnegative integers, because \(\texttt{def:feedback-core-input-class}\) requires \(1\leq h_{\ell i}\leq b\).

We now evaluate (2) without enumerating the \(k!\) chambers.  For \(f\in\mathcal K\), put
\[
 \operatorname{Pred}(f)=
 \begin{cases}
 \mathcal U\cap N(f),&f\in\mathcal B,\\
 \varnothing,&f\notin\mathcal B,
 \end{cases}
\]
and, for every \(S\subseteq\mathcal K\), define
\[
\begin{split}
 E_{\mathcal U,\mathcal B}(S)={}&
 \sum_{f\in S}(h_{\ell f}-1)
 +\sum_{\substack{v\in W_{\mathcal U}\\
                    \mathcal U\cap N(v)\subseteq S}}h_{\ell v}
 +\sum_{\substack{v\in T_{\mathcal U}\\
                    \mathcal B\cap N(v)\cap S\neq\varnothing}}h_{\ell v}.                 \tag{3}
\end{split}
\]
This is a nonnegative integer.  Partition \((0,1)^{\mathcal K}\), apart from its tie set, into ascending chambers
\[
 0<x_{\sigma(1)}<\cdots<x_{\sigma(k)}<1.
\]
The tie set has probability zero because the densities in \(\texttt{def:wri-law}\) are continuous.  On such a chamber, \(\chi_{\mathcal U,\mathcal B}(x)=1\) exactly when every member of \(\operatorname{Pred}(f)\) occurs before \(f\).  This is precisely the family of strict inequalities \(x_u<x_f\) in \(\texttt{def:core-inclusion-exclusion}\).

Let \(S_r=\{\sigma(1),\ldots,\sigma(r)\}\).  After removing the constant \(\prod_{f\in\mathcal K}h_{\ell f}\), the integrand in (2) is a monomial on the chamber.  Its total exponent on the coordinates in \(S_r\) is exactly \(E_{\mathcal U,\mathcal B}(S_r)\).  The density exponent \(h_{\ell f}-1\) is included exactly when \(f\in S_r\).  A maximum over \(\mathcal U\cap N(v)\) is the last of its arguments in the ascending order, so its exponent is included exactly when all its arguments are in \(S_r\).  A nonconstant minimum over \(\mathcal B\cap N(v)\) is the first of its arguments, so its exponent is included exactly when at least one argument is in \(S_r\).  These are the three terms of (3).

If the resulting chamber monomial is \(\prod_{j=1}^k x_{\sigma(j)}^{a_j}\), successive integration of the smallest remaining coordinate, using \(\int_0^t x^a\,dx=t^{a+1}/(a+1)\) for \(a\geq0\), gives
\[
 \int_{0<x_{\sigma(1)}<\cdots<x_{\sigma(k)}<1}
 \prod_{j=1}^k x_{\sigma(j)}^{a_j}\,dx
 =\prod_{r=1}^k
 \frac{1}{r+\sum_{j=1}^r a_j}
 =\prod_{r=1}^k\frac{1}{r+E_{\mathcal U,\mathcal B}(S_r)}.                    \tag{4}
\]
Every division is valid because the denominator in the \(r\)-th factor is at least \(r\geq1\).

Define the entered-subset dynamic program by
\[
 D_{\mathcal U,\mathcal B}[\varnothing]=1,
\]
and, for each \(S\subsetneq\mathcal K\) and \(f\in\mathcal K\setminus S\) satisfying \(\operatorname{Pred}(f)\subseteq S\), make the update
\[
 D_{\mathcal U,\mathcal B}[S\cup\{f\}]
 \mathrel{+}=
 \frac{D_{\mathcal U,\mathcal B}[S]}
 {|S|+1+E_{\mathcal U,\mathcal B}(S\cup\{f\})}.                              \tag{5}
\]
Induction on \(|S|\) proves that \(D_{\mathcal U,\mathcal B}[S]\) is the sum over all admissible ascending orderings of the elements of \(S\) of the product of the factors in (4) along their successive entered subsets.  The base case is the empty product.  For the induction step, the last entered element is some \(f\in S\), its predecessors must already lie in \(S\setminus\{f\}\), and the last factor is \((|S|+E_{\mathcal U,\mathcal B}(S))^{-1}\), exactly as in (5).  At \(S=\mathcal K\), these admissible orderings are exactly the chambers where \(\chi_{\mathcal U,\mathcal B}=1\).  Thus (2)--(5) imply the evaluated identity
\[
 I_{\ell,\mathcal U,\mathcal B}
 =\left(\prod_{f\in\mathcal K}h_{\ell f}\right)
 D_{\mathcal U,\mathcal B}[\mathcal K].                                      \tag{6}
\]
Equations (1), (3), (5), and (6) therefore give every coefficient of every tagged polynomial by rational additions, multiplications, and divisions, with no remaining integral.

There are at most
\[
 \sum_{\mathcal U\subseteq\mathcal K}2^{k-|\mathcal U|}=3^k
\]
disjoint pairs \((\mathcal U,\mathcal B)\); the independence test on \(\mathcal U\) only decreases this number.  For a fixed pair, tabulating (3) and all transitions (5) costs \(2^k\operatorname{poly}(n,k,\lambda)\) elementary operations.  Formula (1) contributes a single monomial for that pair and can be accumulated for the at most \(1+n+\binom n2\) tags satisfying \(A\subseteq S_{\mathcal U}\).  Across the \(L\) rules, the arithmetic-operation count is therefore \(L6^k\operatorname{poly}(n,b,\lambda)\).

It remains to control exact bit cost.  Put \(D_0=nb\) and \(M=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,D_0)\).  A transition reaching a subset \(S\) of size \(r\geq1\) divides by
\[
 q(S)=r+E_{\mathcal U,\mathcal B}(S).
\]
The sets \(W_{\mathcal U}\) and \(T_{\mathcal U}\) partition \(V\setminus\mathcal K\).  Hence (3), \(h_{\ell i}\leq b\), and \(r\leq k\) give
\[
 1\leq q(S)
 \leq r+r(b-1)+(n-k)b
 =rb+(n-k)b
 \leq nb=D_0.                                                                 \tag{7}
\]
Thus \(q(S)\mid M\).  Store every layer-\(r\) dynamic-program value over the common denominator \(M^r\); an update from layer \(r-1\) multiplies the old numerator by the integer \(M/q(S)\).  There are at most \(r!\) paths to a layer-\(r\) state, and every factor in (4) is at most one, so the stored numerator has absolute value at most \(r!M^r\).  Moreover,
\[
 \log_2 M\leq\log_2(D_0!)\leq D_0\log_2 D_0.                                 \tag{8}
\]
After multiplication by \(\prod_{f\in\mathcal K}h_{\ell f}\), accumulation of at most \(3^k\) signed terms over denominator \(M^k\) increases numerator bit length by at most \(O(k\log b+k\log k+k+k\log M)\).  Equations (7)--(8), together with \(k\leq n\), bound the bit length of every numerator and denominator by \(\operatorname{poly}(n,b,\lambda)\).  The integer \(M\) can itself be formed by successive greatest-common-divisor and least-common-multiple operations within this bound.  Standard exact integer arithmetic therefore makes the bit cost of each of the \(L6^k\operatorname{poly}(n,b,\lambda)\) arithmetic steps polynomial in \(n,b,\lambda\), and this polynomial factor is absorbed in the displayed bound.

For an exact smallest-instance check, let \(n=2\), \(\mathcal K=\{f\}\), and let the exterior vertex \(v\) be adjacent to \(f\).  Equations (1)--(6) give
\[
 \Pr_{\ell}(S_{\ell}=\{f\})
 =\Pr(X_{\ell f}>X_{\ell v})
 =\int_0^1h_{\ell f}x^{h_{\ell f}-1}x^{h_{\ell v}}\,dx
 =\frac{h_{\ell f}}{h_{\ell f}+h_{\ell v}},
\]
and the complementary probability for \(S_{\ell}=\{v\}\).  Therefore
\[
 F_{\ell,\varnothing}(z)=z,
 \quad
 F_{\ell,\{f\}}(z)=\frac{zh_{\ell f}}{h_{\ell f}+h_{\ell v}},
 \quad
 F_{\ell,\{v\}}(z)=\frac{zh_{\ell v}}{h_{\ell f}+h_{\ell v}},
 \quad
 F_{\ell,\{f,v\}}(z)=0,
\]
which exhausts all admissible tags.  At the opposite boundary \(k=n\), the exterior sums in (3) vanish and every denominator in (7) remains positive, so the proof does not require a nonempty exterior.

If the supplied \(\mathcal K\) is a vertex cover of \(C_G\), then all \(F_{\ell,A}(z)\), \(|A|\le2\), are computable exactly in \(L6^k\operatorname{poly}(n,b,\lambda)\) bit operations, with no unevaluated integral, and every intermediate rational has \(\operatorname{poly}(n,b,\lambda)\) bit length.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The entered-subset cumulative-exponent dynamic program sums every admissible core-order chamber without factorial enumeration, yielding the exact \(6^k\) fixed-parameter oracle, no unevaluated integral, and polynomial intermediate rational height. \textit{Gap.} Static vertex-cover programs do not assign heterogeneous greedy-order probability. \textit{Consumer.} Experiments whose measured conflict cycles are concentrated on a small vertex-cover hub set obtain an exact fixed-parameter AWRI rule comparison.

\begin{proposition}[prop:replicated-star-support]\label{prop:replicated-star-support}
For \(\Gamma_{k,m,0}\) with unit noncore weights, conditional on every \(f_i\in\mathcal K\) being selected and on \(0<x_1<\cdots<x_k<1\), one component contributes \(z(\prod_{i=1}^kx_i)T_k(x)\), where \(T_k(x)=\frac1{k+1}+\sum_{r=1}^k\frac{x_r^{r+1}\prod_{i>r}x_i}{r(r+1)}\). The ordinary monomial expansion of the \(m\)-component conditional message has exactly \(\binom{m+k}{k}\) nonzero coefficients.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Condition on selection of every \(f_i\) and on \(0<x_1<\cdots<x_k<1\).  In component \(j\), independence forces every \(a_{ji}\) and \(b_{ji}\) to be unselected.  The center \(c_j\) must then be selected, because its only conflict neighbors are the vertices \(b_{ji}\), none of which is selected.  By \(\texttt{lem:greedy-domination-certificate}\), \(a_{ji}\) is certified precisely when its uniform priority is below \(x_i\), contributing \(x_i\).  Given center priority \(y\), vertex \(b_{ji}\) is certified precisely when its priority is below at least one of the selected neighbors \(f_i,c_j\), contributing \(\max(x_i,y)\).  Therefore one component contributes
\[
 z\left(\prod_{i=1}^kx_i\right)T_k(x),\qquad
 T_k(x)=\int_0^1\prod_{i=1}^k\max(x_i,y)\,dy.
\]
Splitting the last integral at \(x_1,\ldots,x_k\) yields
\[
 T_k(x)=x_1\prod_{i=1}^kx_i+
 \sum_{r=1}^{k-1}\frac{x_{r+1}^{r+1}-x_r^{r+1}}{r+1}
       \prod_{i>r}x_i+\frac{1-x_k^{k+1}}{k+1}.
\]
Collecting the coefficient of each boundary monomial telescopes to
\[
 T_k(x)=\frac1{k+1}+\sum_{r=1}^k
 \frac{x_r^{r+1}\prod_{i>r}x_i}{r(r+1)}.
\]

For the \(m\) independent components the conditional message is
\[
 z^m\left(\prod_{i=1}^kx_i^m\right)T_k(x)^m;
\]
the proposition's phrase ``one component'' accounts for the displayed single factor \(z(\prod_i x_i)T_k\).  Let \(v_r\in\mathbb N_0^k\) be the exponent vector of \(x_r^{r+1}\prod_{i>r}x_i\).  The matrix with columns \(v_1,ldots,v_k\) is triangular with diagonal \(2,3,\ldots,k+1\), hence the \(v_r\) are linearly independent.  Thus two choices of multiplicities \((q_0,q_1,\ldots,q_k)\), with \(\sum q_r=m\), give the same exponent in \(T_k^m\) only if \(q_1,\ldots,q_k\), and then \(q_0\), agree.  All coefficients are positive, so none cancels.  Stars and bars gives exactly \(\binom{m+k}{k}\) nonzero monomials; multiplication by \(\prod_i x_i^m\) only translates their exponent vectors.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The family proves that relative core order and dense monomial expansion are insufficient descriptions of numerical cutoff dependence. \textit{Gap.} Static set counting and scalar adsorption recurrences do not expose this future-exponent obstruction. \textit{Consumer.} The obstruction delineates the still-open fixed-parameter representation problem and prevents an invalid scalar-state proof.

\begin{theorem}[thm:identical-star-moment-recurrence]\label{thm:identical-star-moment-recurrence}
For \(\Gamma_{k,m,M}\) with unit noncore weights and arbitrary positive integer core weights, the probability that every core vertex is selected is computable in \(O(k!km^2)\) rational operations and \(2^{O(k\log(k+1))}\operatorname{poly}(n,b,\lambda)\) bit operations. On a chamber ordered by \(\sigma\), put \(H_r=\sum_{i=1}^r h_{\ell,\sigma(i)}\), \(\beta_r=H_r+r(M+m)-1\), \(c_0=1/(k+1)\), and \(c_r=1/(r(r+1))\). The ordered-ratio integral is evaluated by \(V_1(q)=c_1^q\), \(V_j(r)=\sum_{q=0}^r\binom rq c_j^{r-q}V_{j-1}(q)/(\beta_{j-1}+jq+1)\), and \(I_\sigma=\sum_{q=0}^m\binom mq c_0^{m-q}V_k(q)/(\beta_k+(k+1)q+1)\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If \(k=0\), the event that every core vertex is selected is vacuous and has probability one, which is returned in constant time.  Hence assume \(k\ge1\).  Fix an ascending core chamber and relabel its order as \(1,\ldots,k\).  By \(\texttt{prop:replicated-star-support}\), conditional on selection of every core vertex, the \(m\) components contribute \((\prod_i x_i^m)T_k(x)^m\).  Each of the \(M\) private leaves at \(f_i\) must have priority below \(x_i\), contributing \(x_i^M\); the core density contributes \(\prod_i h_{\ell i}x_i^{h_{\ell i}-1}\).  Hence, apart from \(\prod_i h_{\ell i}\), the chamber integrand is
\[
 \prod_{i=1}^kx_i^{h_{\ell i}-1+M+m}
 \left(c_0+\sum_{r=1}^kc_r x_r^{r+1}\prod_{i>r}x_i\right)^m,
 \qquad c_0=\frac1{k+1},\quad c_r=\frac1{r(r+1)}.
\]
Set \(t_r=x_r/x_{r+1}\) for \(r<k\) and \(t_k=x_k\).  Then \(x_i=\prod_{s=i}^kt_s\), the chamber becomes \((0,1)^k\), and the Jacobian is \(\prod_{r=1}^kt_r^{r-1}\).  If \(H_r=\sum_{i=1}^rh_{\ell i}\), the exponent of \(t_r\) outside the last power is
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^r(h_{\ell i}-1+M+m)+(r-1)=H_r+r(M+m)-1=\beta_r.
\]
Moreover
\[
 x_r^{r+1}\prod_{i>r}x_i=\prod_{s=r}^kt_s^{s+1}.
\]
Define \(R_1=c_1\) and \(R_j=c_j+t_{j-1}^jR_{j-1}\).  The bracket is \(c_0+t_k^{k+1}R_k\).  For \(0\le q\le m\), let
\[
 V_j(q)=\int_{(0,1)^{j-1}}R_j^q\prod_{s<j}t_s^{\beta_s}\,dt_1\cdots dt_{j-1}.
\]
The binomial theorem followed by \(\int_0^1t^a\,dt=(a+1)^{-1}\), valid because every \(\beta_s\ge0\), gives
\[
 V_1(q)=c_1^q,qquad
 V_j(r)=\sum_{q=0}^r\binom rq c_j^{r-q}
       \frac{V_{j-1}(q)}{\beta_{j-1}+jq+1}.
\]
One final binomial expansion and integration in \(t_k\) gives
\[
 I_\sigma=\sum_{q=0}^m\binom mq c_0^{m-q}
       \frac{V_k(q)}{\beta_k+(k+1)q+1}.
\]
Multiplication by \(\prod_i h_{\ell i}\) and summation over the \(k!\) chambers gives \(P_\ell(\mathcal K\subseteq S_\ell)\).  The arrays have \(m+1\) entries; evaluating each displayed convolution directly costs \(O(m^2)\) rational operations at each of \(k\) stages, hence \(O(k!km^2)\) overall.

All denominator arguments are positive integers bounded by \(W_0:=\sum_i h_{\ell i}+k(M+m)+km+k+1\), while every denominator \(r(r+1)\) is at most \(W_0^2\).  Along any expanded term there are at most \(O(k+m)\) such factors.  A common multiple formed from \(\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,W_0^2)^{O(k+m)}\) has polynomial bit length in \(n,b,\lambda\); binomial coefficients are integers, and summing \(k!\) chambers adds \(O(k\log(k+1))\) bits.  The bit height and bit-operation cost are therefore bounded by \(2^{O(k\log(k+1))}\operatorname{poly}(n,b,\lambda)\), as claimed.  Every integral has been replaced by a displayed rational recurrence.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The recurrence evaluates exponentially dense identical-component powers using only a one-dimensional moment index. \textit{Gap.} Existing weighted-greedy limit recursions do not give this exact finite core-conditioned rational coefficient. \textit{Consumer.} It is a proved fixed-parameter subclass and a positive control for any future heterogeneous representation.

\begin{proposition}[prop:linear-risk]\label{prop:linear-risk}
For every rule \(\ell\), \(\widehat\tau_{\ell}-\tau=a_{\ell}^{\mathsf T}\theta\) and \(\mathbb E_{\ell}[(\widehat\tau_{\ell}-\tau)^2]=\theta^{\mathsf T}Q_{\ell}\theta\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By \(\texttt{ass:nondegenerate-isolation}\), \(K_\ell\ge2\) for every assignment in the support.  Hence \(t(K_\ell)\ge1\) and \(c(K_\ell)\ge1\), so every division in \(e_\ell\) is defined.  Isolation in \(C_G\), \(\texttt{def:conflict-graph}\), and the assignment in \(\texttt{def:rdim-construction}\) make the realized exposure on \(D_i\) equal to \(\mathbf 1_{D_i}\) for \(i\in T_\ell\) and to \(\mathbf 0_{D_i}\) for \(i\in U_\ell\).  By \(\texttt{ass:full-neighborhood-interference}\), the corresponding observed outcomes are therefore the fixed endpoints \(Y_i(\mathbf 1)\) and \(Y_i(\mathbf 0)\).  Consequently the RDIM estimator is the stated linear form \(\widehat\tau_\ell=e_\ell^{\mathsf T}\theta\).  Since \(\tau=d^{\mathsf T}\theta\),
\[
 \widehat\tau_\ell-\tau=(e_\ell-d)^{\mathsf T}\theta=a_\ell^{\mathsf T}\theta.
\]
The endpoint vector is fixed under the design by \(\texttt{ass:design-based-randomness}\).  Taking the square and then design expectation yields
\[
 \mathbb E_\ell[(\widehat\tau_\ell-\tau)^2]
 =\theta^{\mathsf T}\mathbb E_\ell[a_\ell a_\ell^{\mathsf T}]\theta
 =\theta^{\mathsf T}Q_\ell\theta,
\]
using the definition of \(Q_\ell\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The quadratic retains selection bias, random denominators, and assignment variance in one exact design-computable object. \textit{Gap.} Published WRI analysis bounds RDIM MSE by a surrogate rather than states this endpoint quadratic. \textit{Consumer.} Exact rule comparison requires a loss matrix valid for heterogeneous fixed potential outcomes.

\begin{proposition}[prop:tagged-risk-reconstruction]\label{prop:tagged-risk-reconstruction}
Under nondegenerate isolation, the construction in \(\texttt{def:tagged-risk-functional}\) equals \(Q_{\ell}=\mathbb E_{\ell}[a_{\ell}a_{\ell}^{\mathsf T}]\) for every \(\ell\). Thus every dense rational RDIM risk matrix is determined by tagged polynomials with tag size at most two and can be reconstructed in \(O(n^3)\) rational operations with polynomial output bit length.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write \(r=|S_\ell|\), \(t=t(r)\), and \(c=c(r)\), and denote the treated and control blocks of \(e_\ell\) by \(e_\ell^1\) and \(e_\ell^0\), respectively.  The positivity of \(t,c\) follows from \(\texttt{ass:nondegenerate-isolation}\).  Conditional on a fixed \(S_\ell\) of size \(r\), uniform choice of a \(t\)-subset gives, for \(i\ne j\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P(i\in T_\ell\mid S_\ell)&=\frac tr,&
 P(i,j\in T_\ell\mid S_\ell)&=\frac{t(t-1)}{r(r-1)},\\
 P(i\in U_\ell\mid S_\ell)&=\frac cr,&
 P(i,j\in U_\ell\mid S_\ell)&=\frac{c(c-1)}{r(r-1)},\\
 P(i\in T_\ell,j\in U_\ell\mid S_\ell)&=\frac{tc}{r(r-1)}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Every probability is understood as zero unless the displayed vertices belong to \(S_\ell\).  Therefore
\[
 \mathbb E_\ell[e_{\ell,i}^{1}]=\sum_{r=2}^n\frac{p_{\ell i}(r)}r=\rho_{\ell i},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_\ell[e_{\ell,i}^{0}]=-\rho_{\ell i},
\]
so \(\mathbb E_\ell[e_\ell]=m_\ell\).  Dividing the five conditional probabilities by the corresponding coefficient denominators \(t^2,c^2,tc\) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E[(e_{\ell,i}^{1})^2]&=\sum_r\frac{p_{\ell i}(r)}{rt(r)},&
 \mathbb E[(e_{\ell,i}^{0})^2]&=\sum_r\frac{p_{\ell i}(r)}{rc(r)},\\
 \mathbb E[e_{\ell,i}^{1}e_{\ell,j}^{1}]&=\sum_rp_{\ell ij}(r)\frac{t(r)-1}{t(r)r(r-1)},&
 \mathbb E[e_{\ell,i}^{0}e_{\ell,j}^{0}]&=\sum_rp_{\ell ij}(r)\frac{c(r)-1}{c(r)r(r-1)},\\
 \mathbb E[e_{\ell,i}^{1}e_{\ell,j}^{0}]&=-\sum_r\frac{p_{\ell ij}(r)}{r(r-1)}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
For \(i=j\), the cross-arm product is zero.  These are exactly the blocks of \(M_\ell\) in \(\texttt{def:tagged-risk-functional}\).  Expanding the centered outer product now gives
\[
 \mathbb E[(e_\ell-d)(e_\ell-d)^{\mathsf T}]
 =M_\ell-m_\ell d^{\mathsf T}-d m_\ell^{\mathsf T}+dd^{\mathsf T},
\]
which is the constructed matrix \(Q_\ell\).  The fixed-potential-outcome assumptions used to interpret this as causal MSE are supplied by \(\texttt{prop:linear-risk}\); the reconstruction itself is design algebra.

There are \(O(n^2)\) entries, and each is a sum over at most \(n-1\) values of \(r\), so direct reconstruction uses \(O(n^3)\) rational operations.  The factors introduced here have denominators at most \(n^3\).  Each tagged coefficient is a probability and hence lies in \([0,1]\); termwise integration of the integer-density polynomials supplies a common denominator dividing a polynomial power of \(\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,nb+n)\).  This common denominator has polynomial bit length, and the numerator is no larger than it.  The additional sums and products above therefore preserve polynomial output bit length.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The proposition identifies exactly which one- and two-vertex marked moments determine all bias and covariance entries. \textit{Gap.} An unmarked isolated-set size law cannot recover pair-specific RDIM risk. \textit{Consumer.} The reconstructed rational matrices feed the finite-library minimax certificate.

\begin{openendedquestion}[oeq:heterogeneous-moment-closure]\label{oeq:heterogeneous-moment-closure}
Construct and characterize an evaluated representation implementing \(\mathfrak H_{\mathrm{mom}}\) such that every cutoff-forest message remains closed under arbitrary heterogeneous branching, forest-component products, moving endpoints at ordered core priorities, and zero-, one-, and two-tagged coefficient extraction, while its state count, arithmetic count, and intermediate rational bit height are each bounded by \(2^{O(k\log(k+1))}\operatorname{poly}(n,b,\lambda)\). The representation must match exact residual enumeration on every \(\Gamma_{k,m,M}\) instance with \(k\le3\), \(m\le2\), branch weights in \(\{1,2\}\), and all admissible tags, and it must have a symbolic arbitrary-\(k,m\) complexity proof.
\end{openendedquestion}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the isolated open question separating the proved dense XP algorithm from arbitrary-feedback-set fixed-parameter tractability. The new vertex-cover subset dynamic program sharpens a special case to \(L6^k\operatorname{poly}(n,b,\lambda)\) bit operations but does not resolve heterogeneous forest closure. \textit{Gap.} Heterogeneous branches preserve distinct future ordered-ratio exponent profiles. Unlike the vertex-cover case, those profiles are not determined by the entered core subset alone, and no fixed-parameter-size state algebra is proved to close them. \textit{Consumer.} A future solution would sharpen the general XP bound; current design recommendations may use the proved XP evaluator or the \(L6^k\operatorname{poly}(n,b,\lambda)\) vertex-cover oracle and do not rely on the unresolved general sharpening.

\begin{theorem}[thm:feedback-core-exact-risk]\label{thm:feedback-core-exact-risk}
There is a deterministic Turing-model algorithm that, for every input in \(\mathfrak C_k\), outputs every coefficient of every \(F_{\ell,A}(z)\), \(|A|\le2\), and, under nondegenerate isolation, every entry of every \(Q_\ell\), in \(Ln^2k!3^k(nb+n+1)^{O(k)}\operatorname{poly}(\lambda)\) bit operations, with no unevaluated integral and with intermediate rational bit height bounded by the same XP envelope.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(\ell,A,\mathcal U,\mathcal B\) in the exact expansion of \(\texttt{prop:core-inclusion-exclusion}\), and split \((0,1)^{\mathcal K}\) into its at most \(k!\) strict chambers.  On one chamber write the ordered core coordinates as
\[
 0=x_{\sigma(0)}<x_{\sigma(1)}<\cdots<x_{\sigma(k)}<x_{\sigma(k+1)}=1.
\]
For every forest vertex, each cutoff \(p_v(x)\) or \(r_v(x)\) is one of these coordinates (or \(0,1\)).  Split its priority variable \(y\) into the \(k+1\) open slabs between consecutive coordinates.  On each slab every cutoff indicator in \(\texttt{def:cutoff-forest-messages}\) is constant.

We now give the dense evaluated representation.  At each vertex and slab store every coefficient in \(z\) of \(A_v,B_v,C_v\) as a dense rational polynomial in \(y,x_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(k)}\).  For a child polynomial, compute \(J_w,D_w\), and the restriction of \(E_w(y)\) to a slab by termwise antiderivatives: the integral from \(0\) to \(y\) is the sum of evaluations over completed earlier slabs and the antiderivative over the current slab.  Thus every moving endpoint is explicitly substituted by either \(y\) or a named core coordinate.  Multiply child factors densely, multiply by \(h_{\ell v}y^{h_{\ell v}-1}\), and apply the displayed additions and subtraction defining the three messages.  At a root, integrate \(A+B\) slab by slab in the same manner.  Multiply the resulting component polynomials densely.  Finally multiply by the core density and integrate every monomial over the chamber using
\[
 \int_{0<x_{\sigma(1)}<\cdots<x_{\sigma(k)}<1}
    \prod_{j=1}^kx_{\sigma(j)}^{\alpha_j}\,dx
 =\prod_{r=1}^k\frac{1}{r+\sum_{j=1}^r\alpha_j}.
\]
All operations are rational additions, multiplications, divisions by positive integers, and endpoint substitutions.  Hence no integral remains unevaluated.

To bound the tables, put \(D=nb+n+1\).  An induction over the rooted forest shows that every term in a subtree message contains the density of each subtree vertex at most once and one antiderivative for each eliminated forest priority.  Consequently its degree in \((y,x_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(k)})\) is at most \(D\), and its degree in \(z\) is at most the subtree size.  The same bounds survive component products and multiplication by the core densities because the participating vertex sets are disjoint.  A dense total-degree table in \(k+1\) numerical variables has at most
\[
 \binom{D+k+1}{k+1}=D^{O(k)}
\]
entries, while storing all \(z\)-coefficients and all slabs costs only an additional \(\operatorname{poly}(n,k)\) factor.  Naive dense convolution, performed at each of \(O(n)\) vertices and components, is therefore \(D^{O(k)}\operatorname{poly}(\lambda)\) per fixed chamber and pair \((\mathcal U,\mathcal B)\).  There are at most \(3^k\) such pairs, \(k!\) chambers, \(O(n^2)\) tags, and \(L\) rules.  This proves the arithmetic-operation envelope in the proposed statement.

For the Turing bound, every antiderivative divides by an integer between \(1\) and \(D\).  Along a term at most \(n+k\) priority variables are integrated.  Taking \(\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,D)^{n+k}\) as a common denominator, and noting that products of input density coefficients have bit length \(O(n\log b)\), bounds the bit length before summation by \(\operatorname{poly}(n,b,\lambda)\).  Dense convolutions and the signed sum over at most \(k!3^kD^{O(k)}\operatorname{poly}(\lambda)\) stored terms increase height by at most the logarithm of the number of elementary summands under common-denominator arithmetic; the cruder bound by the full operation count is already the claimed XP envelope.  Standard integer addition, multiplication, gcd reduction, and exact division consequently convert the arithmetic count to
\[
 Ln^2k!3^kD^{O(k)}\operatorname{poly}(\lambda)
\]
bit operations after enlarging the absolute constants in \(O(k)\) and the polynomial.

By \(\texttt{prop:core-inclusion-exclusion}\), the resulting coefficients are exactly those of every \(F_{\ell,A}\).  Under nondegenerate isolation, \(\texttt{prop:tagged-risk-reconstruction}\) then obtains all entries of \(Q_\ell\) in an additional \(O(n^3)\) rational operations, absorbed by the displayed envelope.  This proves the corrected XP theorem without using \(\mathfrak H_{\mathrm{mom}}\) or the unresolved heterogeneous closure.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The corrected theorem gives a fully evaluated exact-risk algorithm for arbitrary supplied feedback forests with explicit XP complexity and bit height. \textit{Gap.} Prior work does not evaluate all heterogeneous tagged greedy-order probabilities, while the stronger general fixed-parameter bound remains open. \textit{Consumer.} Investigators can exactly compare prespecified AWRI rules when the displayed XP cost is feasible, with sharper guarantees in the two proved subclasses.

\begin{theorem}[thm:rational-epsilon-minimax]\label{thm:rational-epsilon-minimax}
Given the exact rational matrices \(Q_1,\ldots,Q_L\) and a positive rational \(\varepsilon\) supplied in binary, a Turing-model algorithm computes a rational certificate \((\widehat q,\widehat t,\widehat X,\widehat s)\) satisfying \(\texttt{def:rational-gap-certificate}\) in time polynomial in \(L\), \(n\), \(\Lambda\), and the binary encoding length of \(\varepsilon\). For a canonical dyadic input \(\varepsilon=2^{-p}\), where \(p\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\), the dependence is polynomial in \(p=\log_2(1/\varepsilon)\). Exact rational arithmetic verifies the simplex, trace, rule inequalities, and positive-semidefinite constraints, and weak duality certifies \(B^2\widehat s\le V^\star\le B^2\widehat t\) with gap at most \(\varepsilon\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(A=H^{-1}=\operatorname{diag}(I_n+\gamma L_C,I_n+\gamma L_C)\).  The combinatorial Laplacian satisfies \(L_C\succeq0\), and \(\gamma>0\); hence
\[
 A\succeq I_{2n}\succ0.
\]
By \(\texttt{ass:design-based-randomness}\), the endpoint vector is fixed under the randomization.  By \(\texttt{prop:linear-risk}\), its causal estimation error and design risk are
\[
 \widehat\tau_\ell-\tau=a_\ell^{\mathsf T}\theta,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_\ell[(\widehat\tau_\ell-\tau)^2]
 =\theta^{\mathsf T}Q_\ell\theta.
\]
Thus the causal estimand \(\tau\) is not defined by the optimization below: the optimization chooses a mixture of design laws to control the already identified squared-error functional.  The exact evaluation theorem \(\texttt{thm:feedback-core-exact-risk}\), followed by \(\texttt{prop:tagged-risk-reconstruction}\), supplies rational matrices
\[
 Q_\ell=\mathbb E_\ell[a_\ell a_\ell^{\mathsf T}]\succeq0,
 \qquad 1\le\ell\le L.
\]

We first prove that the primal program in \(\texttt{def:minimax-sdp}\) equals the stated minimax risk.  Fix \(q\in\Delta_L\).  By \(\texttt{def:endpoint-class}\) and \(\texttt{ass:endpoint-ellipsoid}\), every \(\vartheta\in\Theta_G(B)\) has the form \(\vartheta=BH^{1/2}u\) with \(\|u\|_2\le1\).  Diagonalizing the symmetric positive-semidefinite matrix \(H^{1/2}Q(q)H^{1/2}\) therefore gives
\[
 \sup_{\vartheta\in\Theta_G(B)}
 \vartheta^{\mathsf T}Q(q)\vartheta
 =B^2\sup_{\|u\|_2\le1}u^{\mathsf T}H^{1/2}Q(q)H^{1/2}u
 =B^2\lambda_{\max}\!\left(H^{1/2}Q(q)H^{1/2}\right).
\]
Congruence by \(H^{1/2}\) also gives the equivalence
\[
 Q(q)\preceq tA
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 H^{1/2}Q(q)H^{1/2}\preceq tI_{2n}.
\]
Consequently the least feasible \(t\) for a fixed \(q\) is the displayed largest eigenvalue, and minimizing over \(q\in\Delta_L\) proves that the primal value is exactly \(V^\star\).

We next certify equality with the dual value.  Define
\[
 \mathcal D=\{X\in\mathbb S_+^{2n}:\operatorname{tr}(AX)=1\}.
\]
This set is nonempty.  It is closed, and \(A\succeq I_{2n}\) implies \(\operatorname{tr}(X)\le1\), so it is compact.  Under the bijection \(Z=A^{1/2}XA^{1/2}\), its elements correspond exactly to matrices \(Z\succeq0\) with \(\operatorname{tr}(Z)=1\).  If \(W=A^{-1/2}Q(q)A^{-1/2}\), diagonalization of \(W\) shows
\[
 \max_{X\in\mathcal D}\operatorname{tr}(Q(q)X)
 =\max_{Z\succeq0,\ \operatorname{tr}(Z)=1}\operatorname{tr}(WZ)
 =\lambda_{\max}(W),
\]
because the trace is a convex combination of the eigenvalues of \(W\), and a rank-one projector onto a largest-eigenvalue eigenvector attains the maximum.

For clarity, the remaining minimax interchange follows from the cited separation result \(\texttt{lem:finite-dimensional-separation}\).  Let
\[
 \mathcal C=
 \{(\operatorname{tr}(Q_1X),\ldots,\operatorname{tr}(Q_LX)):X\in\mathcal D\}.
\]
The set \(\mathcal C\) is compact and convex.  Put \(\beta=\max_{c\in\mathcal C}\min_\ell c_\ell\).  For every \(a>\beta\), \(\mathcal C\) is disjoint from the open upper orthant \((a,\infty)^L\).  The cited theorem \(\texttt{lem:finite-dimensional-separation}\) supplies a nonzero separator \(q\) and a level \(b\). If \(d\in(a,\infty)^L\), then \(d+te_i\) remains in the open upper orthant for every \(t\ge0\). The separating lower bound on \(q^{\mathsf T}(d+te_i)\) would fail as \(t\to\infty\) if \(q_i<0\); hence \(q_i\ge0\) for every \(i\). Since \(q\ne0\), normalize \(\mathbf 1_L^{\mathsf T}q=1\). Taking the infimum over \(d\in(a,\infty)^L\) then yields
\[
 \max_{c\in\mathcal C}q^{\mathsf T}c\le a.
\]
Letting \(a\downarrow\beta\) proves
\[
 \min_{q\in\Delta_L}\max_{c\in\mathcal C}q^{\mathsf T}c\le\beta.
\]
Conversely, for every \(q\in\Delta_L\) and \(c\in\mathcal C\), \(q^{\mathsf T}c\ge\min_\ell c_\ell\); maximizing over \(c\) and then minimizing over \(q\) gives the reverse inequality.  Hence
\[
 \min_{q\in\Delta_L}\max_{X\in\mathcal D}
 \sum_{\ell=1}^Lq_\ell\operatorname{tr}(Q_\ell X)
 =\max_{X\in\mathcal D}\min_{1\le\ell\le L}
 \operatorname{tr}(Q_\ell X).
\]
Introducing \(s\le\operatorname{tr}(Q_\ell X)\) for all \(\ell\) gives exactly the dual in \(\texttt{def:minimax-sdp}\).  Denote the common unscaled optimal value by \(v\), so \(V^\star=B^2v\).

We now verify the concrete data used by the proved application bridge \(\texttt{lem:rational-sdp-gls-bridge}\).  Let \(\|\cdot\|_\infty\) denote the maximum absolute row-sum norm and set
\[
 R=1+\max_{1\le\ell\le L}\|Q_\ell\|_\infty,
 \qquad
 q^0=L^{-1}\mathbf 1_L,
 \qquad
 t^0=R.
\]
Since \(Q(q^0)\succeq0\) and
\[
 \lambda_{\max}(Q(q^0))
 \le\|Q(q^0)\|_\infty
 \le\max_\ell\|Q_\ell\|_\infty=R-1,
\]
the inequality \(A\succeq I_{2n}\) implies
\[
 t^0A-Q(q^0)\succeq I_{2n}.
\]
Thus \((q^0,t^0)\) is a rational relative-interior primal point, with every simplex coordinate equal to \(1/L\).  For the dual take
\[
 X^0=I_{2n}/\operatorname{tr}(A),
 \qquad s^0=-1.
\]
Then \(X^0\succ0\), \(\operatorname{tr}(AX^0)=1\), and, because \(Q_\ell\succeq0\),
\[
 \operatorname{tr}(Q_\ell X^0)-s^0\ge1
 \quad(1\le\ell\le L).
\]
All these points and margins are rational and have binary encoding length polynomial in \(\Lambda\).  Indeed, inversion of the rational positive-definite matrix \(H\), traces, row sums, and the displayed arithmetic are computable by fraction-free elimination with polynomial bit growth.

The optimum obeys \(0\le v\le R\): nonnegativity follows from \(Q(q)\succeq0\), and the primal point just constructed gives the upper bound.  On \(\mathcal D\), \(\operatorname{tr}(X)\le1\); positive semidefiniteness then gives \(|X_{ij}|\le\sqrt{X_{ii}X_{jj}}\le1\).  Thus we may intersect the primal with \(-1\le t\le2R\) and the dual with \(-2\le s\le R+1\) without removing an optimum.  The two feasible regions are rationally bounded in the rational affine subspaces
\[
 \mathbf 1_L^{\mathsf T}q=1,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{tr}(AX)=1,
\]
and the two strict rational points above give inner radii whose reciprocal binary lengths are polynomial in \(L,n,\Lambda\).

The exact rational membership tests required by \\(\\texttt{lem:rational-sdp-gls-bridge}\\) are polynomial-time and implement valid weak-membership oracles.  Linear inequalities are immediate.  For a rational symmetric matrix \(M\), recursively pivot on a positive diagonal entry and apply the rational Schur-complement identity
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}a&r^{\mathsf T}\\r&C\end{pmatrix}\succeq0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 a>0\ \text{ and }\ C-a^{-1}rr^{\mathsf T}\succeq0.
\]
If no positive diagonal exists, a negative diagonal is already a rational negative-quadratic-form witness; if every diagonal is zero but an off-diagonal entry is nonzero, one of the rational vectors \(e_i+e_j\) and \(e_i-e_j\) is such a witness.  Otherwise the residual matrix is zero.  Hence rejection produces a rational vector \(w\) with \(w^{\mathsf T}Mw<0\), and the rational linear inequality \(\langle ww^{\mathsf T},M\rangle\ge0\) separates the candidate from the positive-semidefinite cone.  Fraction-free implementation bounds all intermediate integers by minors of the rational input after clearing denominators, so Hadamard's determinant bound gives polynomial bit length and polynomial running time.

Apply \(\texttt{lem:rational-sdp-gls-bridge}\), which parametrizes and centers the two affine regions before invoking \(\texttt{lem:weak-rational-convex-optimization}\).  With rational accuracy \(\delta>0\), it returns rational points \((\bar q,\bar t)\) and \((\bar X,\bar s)\) for which, after rescaling the finitely many constraint residuals by their explicitly computed rational operator norms,
\[
 \bar q_i\ge-\delta,
 \qquad
 \bar tA-Q(\bar q)\succeq-\delta I_{2n},
 \qquad
 \bar X\succeq-\delta I_{2n},
\]
\[
 \operatorname{tr}(Q_\ell\bar X)-\bar s\ge-\delta,
 \qquad
 \bar t\le v+\delta,
 \qquad
 \bar s\ge v-\delta.
\]
The affine equalities hold exactly because optimization is performed in their rational affine subspaces.  Absorbing the constraint-map norm factors into \(\delta\) changes its reciprocal binary length by only a polynomial in \(L,n,\Lambda\).

It remains to turn weak feasibility into the exact rational certificate required by \(\texttt{def:rational-gap-certificate}\).  Set
\[
 \eta=
 \min\!\left\{\frac14,
 \frac{\varepsilon}{4B^2(2R+1)}\right\},
\]
which is a positive rational number, and set
\[
 \delta=
 \min\!\left\{
 \frac{\eta}{2L},
 \frac{\eta}{2\operatorname{tr}(A)},
 \frac{\eta}{2},
 \frac{\varepsilon}{8B^2}
 \right\}.
\]
Use this \(\delta\) in the preceding weak optimizations and define
\[
 (\widehat q,\widehat t)
 =(1-\eta)(\bar q,\bar t)+\eta(q^0,t^0),
 \qquad
 (\widehat X,\widehat s)
 =(1-\eta)(\bar X,\bar s)+\eta(X^0,s^0).
\]
The affine equalities are preserved.  For every coordinate,
\[
 \widehat q_i\ge-(1-\eta)\delta+\eta/L\ge0.
\]
Linearity of the primal matrix slack and the unit slack at \((q^0,t^0)\) give
\[
 \widehat tA-Q(\widehat q)
 \succeq\{\eta-(1-\eta)\delta\}I_{2n}\succeq0.
\]
Likewise, using \(\lambda_{\min}(X^0)=1/\operatorname{tr}(A)\),
\[
 \widehat X
 \succeq\left\{\frac{\eta}{\operatorname{tr}(A)}-(1-\eta)\delta\right\}I_{2n}
 \succeq0,
\]
and each dual rule slack satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{tr}(Q_\ell\widehat X)-\widehat s
 \ge-(1-\eta)\delta+\eta\ge0.
\]
Thus all primal and dual constraints hold exactly over the rationals.  Since \(0\le v\le R\), their objective gap is bounded by
\[
 \widehat t-\widehat s
 \le 2\delta+\eta(2R+1)
 \le\frac{\varepsilon}{B^2}.
\]

For any exactly feasible primal-dual pair, positivity and the trace normalization yield the weak-duality chain
\[
 \widehat t
 =\operatorname{tr}(\widehat tA\widehat X)
 \ge\operatorname{tr}(Q(\widehat q)\widehat X)
 =\sum_{\ell=1}^L\widehat q_\ell\operatorname{tr}(Q_\ell\widehat X)
 \ge\widehat s.
\]
Together with the established equality of the primal value, the dual value, and \(V^\star/B^2\), this proves
\[
 B^2\widehat s\le V^\star\le B^2\widehat t,
 \qquad
 B^2(\widehat t-\widehat s)\le\varepsilon.
\]
The same rational pivot procedure verifies the two positive-semidefinite constraints exactly, while the remaining certificate conditions are exact rational linear equalities or inequalities.

Finally, let the binary encoding of a positive rational \(\varepsilon\) contain the signed numerator and positive denominator in lowest terms.  The rational choices of \(\eta\) and \(\delta\) above have binary length polynomial in the binary encoding lengths of \(\varepsilon\), \(B\), \(R\), \(A\), and \(L\).  Therefore \(\log(1/\delta)\), every intermediate rational bit height, the weak-optimization running time, and the exact-verification running time are polynomial in \(L\), \(n\), \(\Lambda\), and the binary encoding length of \(\varepsilon\).  For the canonical dyadic input \(\varepsilon=2^{-p}\), with \(p\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\), that encoding length is \(O(p+1)\), so the dependence is polynomial in \(p=\log_2(1/\varepsilon)\), as claimed.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The rational primal-dual tuple makes near-optimal mixing independently checkable without asserting an exact algebraic optimizer. \textit{Gap.} General semidefinite design methods do not include the exact WRI/RDIM risk construction and its audit trail. \textit{Consumer.} A trial team can mix the exactly evaluated rule library and verify the ellipsoidal worst-case risk gap before assignment.

\begin{proposition}[prop:forest-and-empty-reduction]\label{prop:forest-and-empty-reduction}
When \(k=0\), the cutoff construction reduces extensionally to the ordinary three-state forest recurrence. When \(C_G\) has no edges, \(S_{\ell}=V\) for every rule and the RDIM construction reduces to balanced complete randomization on all \(n\) units.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If \(k=0\), then \(\mathcal K=\varnothing\), so the only pair in core inclusion--exclusion is \((\mathcal U,\mathcal B)=(\varnothing,\varnothing)\).  The definitions give \(p_v=0\), \(r_v=1\), and \(\chi=1\).  Substitution into \(\texttt{def:cutoff-forest-messages}\) removes every cutoff and yields exactly the selected, certified-unselected, and parent-needing three-state recurrence on each rooted forest.  The component product in \(\texttt{prop:core-inclusion-exclusion}\) is therefore the ordinary forest recurrence extensionally.

If \(C_G\) has no edges, the scan in \(\texttt{def:wri-law}\) never encounters a previously selected neighbor, so \(S_\ell=V\) and \(K_\ell=n\) for every rule and priority order.  Moreover, because \(i\in D_i\), any directed arc \((j,i)\) would put \(j\) in both \(D_j\) and \(D_i\), creating the conflict edge \(\{i,j\}\); hence an empty conflict graph implies \(D_i=\{i\}\) for all \(i\).  The construction then chooses \(T_\ell\) uniformly among all \(t(n)\)-subsets of \(V\), puts the remaining \(c(n)\) units in control, and forms their difference in means.  This is balanced complete randomization on all \(n\) units.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The reduction checks both the cutoff recursion's \(k=0\) boundary and the classical no-interference design boundary. \textit{Gap.} The dense feedback-core algorithm must recover the ordinary forest recurrence and cannot alter RDIM on an empty conflict graph. \textit{Consumer.} Implementers obtain exact boundary regression tests for the proved XP oracle.

\begin{lemma}[lem:finite-dimensional-separation]\label{lem:finite-dimensional-separation}
Suppose \(C,D\subseteq\mathbb R^m\) are nonempty disjoint convex sets. Then there are \(a\ne0\) and \(b\in\mathbb R\) such that \(a^{\mathsf T}x\le b\) for every \(x\in C\) and \(a^{\mathsf T}x\ge b\) for every \(x\in D\). Conversely, if \(C\) and \(D\) are convex, at least one is open, and such a separating hyperplane exists, then \(C\) and \(D\) are disjoint.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[lem:weak-rational-convex-optimization]\label{lem:weak-rational-convex-optimization}
There is an oracle-polynomial-time algorithm for the following weak constrained convex minimization problem: given a positive rational accuracy \(\epsilon\), a centered convex body \((K;n,R,r,a_0)\) through a weak-membership oracle, and a convex function \(f:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R\) through an oracle returning rational values to requested rational accuracy, output \(y\in S(K,\epsilon)\) such that \(f(y)\le f(x)+\epsilon\) for every \(x\in S(K,-\epsilon)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[lem:rational-sdp-gls-bridge]\label{lem:rational-sdp-gls-bridge}
For the bounded primal and dual semidefinite regions in \(\texttt{def:minimax-sdp}\), rational parametrization of their affine hulls and translation by explicit strict rational feasible points produce centered convex bodies whose inner- and outer-radius data have polynomial binary encoding length. Exact rational linear and positive-semidefinite tests implement weak-membership oracles, and the linear objectives implement exact value oracles. Applying \(\texttt{lem:weak-rational-convex-optimization}\) in these coordinates and pulling back yields rational weakly feasible primal and dual points with prescribed rational objective accuracy, polynomial output bit length, and oracle-polynomial running time.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(A=H^{-1}\) and \(R=1+\max_\ell\|Q_\ell\|_\infty\). Intersect the primal region with \(-1\le t\le2R\) and the dual region with \(-2\le s\le R+1\); the points \(q^0=L^{-1}\mathbf1_L,t^0=R\) and \(X^0=I_{2n}/\operatorname{tr}(A),s^0=-1\) show that these added bounds remove no optimum. They also give strict rational feasibility: every coordinate of \(q^0\) is \(1/L\), \(t^0A-Q(q^0)\succeq I_{2n}\), \(X^0\succ0\), and \(\operatorname{tr}(Q_\ell X^0)-s^0\ge1\).

Eliminate one simplex coordinate and one independent entry of \(X\) by rational Gaussian elimination in \(\mathbf1_L^{\mathsf T}q=1\) and \(\operatorname{tr}(AX)=1\). The resulting rational affine maps have polynomially bounded bit length. The box bounds, \(A\succeq I_{2n}\), and \(X\succeq0\Rightarrow |X_{ij}|\le1\) give rational outer balls. The strict margins above, divided by rational upper bounds for the operator norms of the affine constraint maps, give rational inner balls about the translated seeds. Fraction-free elimination and Hadamard's bound show that all radius and map encodings are polynomial in \(L,n,\Lambda\). Thus both coordinate images are centered convex bodies in the sense required by \(\texttt{lem:weak-rational-convex-optimization}\).

For a rational query point, test all linear constraints exactly. Rational symmetric elimination decides positive semidefiniteness; if it fails, the same elimination produces a rational \(u\) with \(u^{\mathsf T}Mu<0\), with polynomial bit growth. Exact membership is a valid weak-membership oracle: it accepts the inner parallel body and rejects every point outside the outer parallel body, while either answer is allowed in the boundary band. The objectives \(t\) and \(-s\) are rational linear functions and hence exact value oracles.

Apply the cited weak constrained minimization theorem at an accuracy rescaled by rational bounds for the affine and constraint maps. Pullback preserves the affine equalities exactly and gives \(q_i\ge-\delta\), \(tA-Q(q)\succeq-\delta I\), \(X\succeq-\delta I\), and \(\operatorname{tr}(Q_\ell X)-s\ge-\delta\), with objective errors at most \(\delta\). The rescaling adds only polynomially many bits. The pulled-back points are rational and are computed with polynomial bit length and in time polynomial in \(L,n,\Lambda\) and \(\log(1/\delta)\), proving the bridge.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This proved bridge isolates the application-specific affine-SDP encoding and oracle construction that is not stated by the cited convex-optimization theorem. \textit{Gap.} The cited centered-body theorem does not itself establish that these rational semidefinite regions meet its oracle and radius hypotheses. \textit{Consumer.} The minimax certificate proof can invoke GLS without attributing the application-specific reduction to the source.

\section{Interpretation}

The exact algorithm removes Monte Carlo error from comparison of prespecified WRI rules, but its worst-case general cost is XP rather than fixed-parameter in the feedback number. If a measured conflict graph comes with a small vertex-cover core, the entered-subset cumulative-exponent program gives exact tagged-law computation, with no integration gate, in \(L6^k\operatorname{poly}(n,b,\lambda)\) bit operations and with polynomial intermediate rational bit length. Identical repeated attachments admit a separate evaluated recurrence. Neither special case establishes fixed-parameter tractability for arbitrary heterogeneous forest attachments. The final semidefinite certificate concerns worst-case squared error of the fixed RDIM rule library over the stated endpoint ellipsoid; it is not a claim of optimality among all network designs or estimators.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The dense chamber-polynomial representation closes arbitrary heterogeneous branching, component products, moving endpoints, and tags, but uses \((nb+n+1)^{O(k)}\) states. It therefore proves an evaluated XP theorem, not the originally proposed fixed-parameter theorem. In the vertex-cover case, every exterior component is a singleton, so the entered core subset \(S\subseteq\mathcal K\) determines the scalar cumulative exponent \(E_{\mathcal U,\mathcal B}(S)\); this is exactly why the \(2^k\)-state recurrence closes. Branching forest components instead retain distinct future ordered-ratio exponent profiles. Two heterogeneous branches can have the same present activation count and exponent while leaving different exponents on future variables. The unresolved question is whether a shared operator representation can preserve those profiles in \(2^{O(k\log(k+1))}\operatorname{poly}(n,b,\lambda)\) time. A table indexed by \(k\) degree totals, a profile-count vector with an \(n\)-dependent exponent, or a circuit retaining an integration gate does not solve that question.

\section{Honest scope}

The general proved result assumes a supplied feedback set, positive integer priority weights, and permits XP dependence \((nb+n+1)^{O(k)}\); it neither finds a minimum feedback set nor proves arbitrary-feedback-set fixed-parameter tractability. The exact \(L6^k\operatorname{poly}(n,b,\lambda)\) guarantee, absence of an unevaluated integral, and polynomial rational height require that the supplied feedback core also be a vertex cover; the other evaluated special result concerns the stated identical replicated-star coefficient. Nondegenerate isolation is required only for RDIM denominators and risk reconstruction. The minimax result chooses within a prespecified WRI/RDIM library over a graph-Sobolev endpoint ellipsoid and does not equal the unrestricted design-and-estimator minimax functional. The rural-China follow-up is a prospective consumer, not evidence for a small empirical feedback number, a small vertex cover, a calibrated ellipsoid, or improved field performance.
