\section*{scm\_\allowbreak{}structsimp\_\allowbreak{}exogenous\_\allowbreak{}frontier}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} Beckers defines actual causation by counterfactual dependence in an actualized structural simplification, but the definition itself gives no exact algorithmic frontier. Under finite explicit local tables, the project proves a relevant-component frontier: the answer is immediately negative when \(X\) and \(Y\) lie in different weak full-graph components; otherwise arbitrary unrelated components are harmless, and an exact certificate calculus is polynomial whenever their common component has cycle rank at most one. A rank-zero component first yields a directed-reachability cut when \(X\) cannot reach \(Y\), and only a reachable instance invokes the linear-arithmetic value-path compiler; a rank-one component uses the exceptional-edge min-sum program. The backend returns a witness and exact optimum certificate or a separating cut. A shared-selector construction separately proves NP-completeness even for deterministic binary models whose endogenous graph is one directed chain, showing why the positive full-graph restriction is substantive.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Section 6, Proposition 20 on page 525, with proof on page 532, of Beckers2025Actual supplies a same-signature structural-simplification representative but no compiler, certificate system, minimum-cost result, or complexity classification. Existing causal-tree algorithms and SAT encodings concern Halpern--Pearl causation and do not enforce Beckers's full-graph reachability legality or actual-row freezing.

\paragraph{Niche.} The novel computational scope uses finite explicitly encoded tables and restricts only the weak full-graph component relevant to \(X\) and \(Y\): disconnected queries are rejected by a component cut, while a common component of cycle rank zero or one is tractable even if every other component is unrestricted. This is a full-graph condition on \(U\cup V\), not the published endogenous causal-tree condition for Halpern--Pearl causation.

\paragraph{Fill.} The project proves component restriction with cost preservation, an explicit three-way frontier functional, a linear-arithmetic value-path compiler after a directed-reachability test, and an exact exceptional-edge min-sum algorithm for a connected rank-one component. Independently checkable positive, optimality, and negative certificates accompany the result. The restricted chain reduction and shared-selector obstruction establish the complementary Beckers hardness phenomenon without claiming unrestricted counting or general treewidth tractability.

\section{Related work}

Beckers2025Actual, Section 5.1, Definitions 13, 15, and 16 on pages 523--524 and Section 6, Definitions 18 and 19 and Proposition 20 on page 525, supplies the structural-simplification and actual-causation semantics implemented here; the proof of Proposition 20 appears on page 532. It contains neither a compiler nor the relevant-component complexity frontier. Beckers2025NCM supplies set-valued equations and topological counterfactual semantics, while finite explicit encoding is this project's computational-scope restriction. The earlier UAI version of EiterLukasiewicz2006Tractable, Section 4.1 and Theorems 4.2--4.3, is the closest methodological comparator: its Halpern--Pearl causal-tree analysis propagates subsets of endogenous path-variable domains. The present positive theorem instead audits Beckers actualized projection and deletion legality on the full graph, first separating directed nonreachability in a forest component and then extending exact computation through one full-graph cycle by an exceptional-edge state. The shared-selector proposition shows only that transferring the unaugmented endogenous-only state type loses full-exogenous reachability information; it does not modify the published Halpern--Pearl theorem. EiterLukasiewicz2002Complexity and AleksandrowiczEtAl2014 show that complexity depends on the causation definition, but neither treats Beckers deletion. IbrahimRehwaldPretschner2019SAT and IbrahimPretschner2020Optimization address Boolean Halpern--Pearl encodings and objectives, and ReydDiaconescuDessalles2025Search gives approximate Halpern--Pearl-style search; none supplies the arbitrary-domain exact Beckers certificates proved here.

Primary functional / estimator / diagnostic: On the relevant-component cycle-rank-at-most-one class, let \(C_X,C_Y\) be the weak components of \(X,Y\).  Then \(C^\star(I)=+\infty\) if \(C_X\ne C_Y\), and, for \(C_X=C_Y=C\), \(C^\star(I)=+\infty\) when \(C\) has cycle rank zero and \(X\not\leadsto_{G[C]}Y\); \(C^\star(I)=\min_{s_i\in R(P_i)\setminus\{a_{P_i}\}}\sum_{i=1}^m C_i(s_{i-1},s_i)\) when \(C\) has cycle rank zero and \(X\leadsto_{G[C]}Y\); and \(C^\star(I)=\operatorname{MS}_1(I|_C)\) when \(C\) has cycle rank one. \section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:finite-signature]\label{ass:finite-signature}
\(U\cap V=\varnothing\), both sets are finite, and every \(R(Z)\) is finite and nonempty.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Finite explicitly enumerable signatures are a computational-scope restriction chosen for an auditable backend over categorical systems, finite-state controllers, and tabular causal simulators; this restriction is not attributed as a standard assumption of either Beckers or Eiter--Lukasiewicz.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:acyclic-full-graph]\label{ass:acyclic-full-graph}
\(G\) is acyclic.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Acyclic NSCM graph, \cite{Beckers2025NCM}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:exogenous-roots]\label{ass:exogenous-roots}
Every vertex in \(U\) has indegree zero in \(G\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Exogenous-root convention, \cite{Beckers2025NCM}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:total-local-tables]\label{ass:total-local-tables}
\(F_Z(r)\ne\varnothing\) for every \(Z\in V\) and every \(r\in R(\operatorname{Pa}_G(Z))\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Total nonempty set-valued NSCM mechanism, \cite{Beckers2025NCM}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:actual-solution]\label{ass:actual-solution}
\(a_Z\in F_Z(a_{\operatorname{Pa}_G(Z)})\) for every \(Z\in V\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Designated actual solution, \cite{Beckers2025NCM}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:distinct-singletons]\label{ass:distinct-singletons}
\(X\ne Y\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Singleton actual-cause query, \cite{Beckers2025Actual}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:nonnegative-rational-cost]\label{ass:nonnegative-rational-cost}
\(c(e)\in\mathbb Q_{\ge 0}\) for every \(e\in E(G)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Nonnegative additive edge cost, \cite{IbrahimPretschner2020Optimization}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:explicit-table-encoding]\label{ass:explicit-table-encoding}
Every value, table row, parent coordinate, allowed output, graph edge, actual value, and rational cost is explicitly indexed and contributes to \(L\) or \(B\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Complete local support tables are a realistic backend input for finite categorical systems, finite-state controllers, and tabular causal simulators; this novel encoding convention makes every allowed outcome auditable and is not attributed as a standard assumption of either Beckers or Eiter--Lukasiewicz.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:full-polyforest]\label{ass:full-polyforest}
The undirected graph underlying \(G\) on \(U\cup V\) is a forest.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Requiring the undirected graph on all exogenous and endogenous vertices to be a forest is a backend-motivated restriction that is satisfied by singly connected finite causal networks with unshared exogenous inputs; it is neither a Beckers assumption nor the endogenous causal-tree condition of EiterLukasiewicz2006Tractable.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:binary-domains]\label{ass:binary-domains}
\(R(Z)=\{0,1\}\) for every \(Z\in U\cup V\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Binary causal model, \cite{EiterLukasiewicz2002Complexity}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:deterministic-tables]\label{ass:deterministic-tables}
\(|F_Z(r)|=1\) for every \(Z\in V\) and every parent row \(r\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Deterministic SCM mechanisms, \cite{EiterLukasiewicz2002Complexity}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:endogenous-chain]\label{ass:endogenous-chain}
The subgraph of \(G\) induced by \(V\) is one directed chain from \(X\) to \(Y\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Directed endogenous-chain restriction, \cite{EiterLukasiewicz2006Tractable}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:actual-zero]\label{ass:actual-zero}
\(a_Z=0\) for every \(Z\in U\cup V\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} The all-zero convention is satisfied after relabeling the two values of every variable in binary reliability, fault-propagation, and Boolean-controller models; here it strengthens the lower bound rather than supplying algorithmic leverage.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:indegree-four]\label{ass:indegree-four}
Every vertex of \(G\) has total indegree at most four.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Bounded-indegree restriction, \cite{EiterLukasiewicz2006Tractable}).
\end{assumption}

\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:compiler-handle]\label{def:compiler-handle}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Explicit support compiler}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} At each path layer, encode off-path difference supports as masks, apply upward Boolean subset propagation to mark every deletion mask containing a bad support, scan nonactual table rows to form the finite transitions \(C_i\), and apply the min-plus recurrence \(d_i(t)=\min_s\{d_{i-1}(s)+C_i(s,t)\}\); retain predecessor rows and reachable-value cuts as certificate data.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:verifier-handle]\label{def:verifier-handle}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Independent replay verifier}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \texttt{Recompute actualized rows before projection,\allowbreak{} audit every deleted edge by full-\allowbreak{}graph reachability,\allowbreak{} replay each projected-\allowbreak{}table membership against an original supporting row,\allowbreak{} complete unassigned variables in topological order,\allowbreak{} and check either the min-\allowbreak{}plus inequalities plus an attaining path or the closure of the separating value sets.}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:selector-obstruction-family]\label{def:selector-obstruction-family}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \(\mathcal O_{\kappa}\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For each \(\kappa\ge2\), take binary exogenous roots \(T_i,F_i\) for \(1\le i\le\kappa\) and the endogenous chain \(X\to S_1\to\cdots\to S_{\kappa}\to C_1\to\cdots\to C_{\kappa}=Y\), all actually zero, with the unintervened table of \(X\) constantly zero. With \(p\) denoting the chain predecessor, for every \(1\le i\le\kappa\) set \(S_i(p,T_i,F_i)=T_i\wedge F_i\) when \(p=0\) and \(S_i(p,T_i,F_i)=T_i\mathbin{\oplus}F_i\) when \(p=1\); add the shared edge \(T_i\to C_i\) and set \(C_i(p,T_i)=p\wedge T_i\). These displayed edges are the full graph, every displayed parent is functionally genuine, and these equations are the total local tables.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{kappa}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:unicycle-handle]\label{def:unicycle-handle}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Relevant-\allowbreak{}component forest/\allowbreak{}unicycle handle}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Given an explicit instance, compute the weak components of the underlying undirected full graph and let \(C_X\) and \(C_Y\) contain \(X\) and \(Y\).  If \(C_X\ne C_Y\), \(\operatorname{UnicycleHandle}\) returns the weak-component separating cut.  If \(C_X=C_Y=C\) and \(C\) has cycle rank zero, it first computes directed reachability from \(X\) in \(G[C]\): when \(Y\) is unreachable it returns that directed-reachability cut and value \(+\infty\), and only when \(Y\) is reachable does it invoke the value-path compiler on the unique directed path.  If the common component has cycle rank one, it cuts the unique factor-graph cycle at a boundary variable and carries the exceptional-edge mode---shortcut retained, complementary arc already broken, or one specified repair edge---jointly with the local nonactual-value state.  Every other weak component is retained at its actual assignment.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:rank-one-min-sum-functional]\label{def:rank-one-min-sum-functional}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Connected rank-\allowbreak{}one normalized min-\allowbreak{}sum functional}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For a restricted instance \(J=I|_C\in\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{exp}}\) whose underlying undirected full graph is connected and has cycle rank one, let \(K\) be its unique simple cycle.  If there is an edge \(e^\star\in E(K)\) for which \(K\setminus\{e^\star\}\) is a directed path from the tail of \(e^\star\) to its head, use that edge; if no such edge exists, write \(e^\star=\bot\).  Put \(U_C=U\cap C\) and \(V_C=V\cap C\).  For \(w\in R(C)\) and rows \(\rho_Z\in R(\operatorname{Pa}_{G[C]}(Z))\), \(Z\in V_C\setminus\{X\}\), define
\[
 D^0(w,\rho)=\{p\to Z\in E(G[C]):Z\ne X,\ \rho_{Z,p}\ne w_p\}.
\]
The repair choice is \(f=\bot\) whenever \(e^\star=\bot\) or \(D^0(w,\rho)\) satisfies
\[
 e^\star\notin D^0(w,\rho)
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 D^0(w,\rho)\cap\bigl(E(K)\setminus\{e^\star\}\bigr)\ne\varnothing;
\]
otherwise \(f\) ranges over \(E(K)\setminus\{e^\star\}\).  Let \(D_f=D^0(w,\rho)\) for \(f=\bot\), and let \(D_f=D^0(w,\rho)\cup\{f\}\) otherwise.  For \(Z\in V_C\), put
\[
 S_Z(D_f)=\{p\in\operatorname{Pa}_{G[C]}(Z):p\to Z\notin D_f\}.
\]
Then \(\operatorname{MS}_1(J)\) is the minimum of \(\sum_{e\in D_f}c(e)\) over \((w,\rho,f)\) satisfying
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 w_{U_C}=a_{U_C},\qquad w_X\ne a_X,\qquad w_Y\ne a_Y,\\
 w_Z\in A_Z(\rho_Z)\quad(Z\in V_C\setminus\{X\}),\\
 S_Z(D_f)\cap\Delta\ne\varnothing
 \quad\bigl(Z\in V_C,\ \Delta\in\mathcal B_Z\bigr).
 \end{gathered}
\]
The empty minimum is \(+\infty\).  Thus the variables, objective, support constraints, actual-row hitting constraints, and exceptional-edge legality repair are all part of the definition.
\end{definition}

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:local-hitting-equivalence]\label{lem:local-hitting-equivalence}
For every \(Z\in V\) and \(H\subseteq G\), the actualized projection preserves the actual local row, \(Q_Z^{S_Z}(a_{S_Z})=\{a_Z\}\), if and only if \(S_Z\cap D\ne\varnothing\) for every \(D\in\mathcal B_Z\). This equivalence freezes the original actual row before any deleted coordinate is unioned.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(Z\in V\), put \(S=\operatorname{Pa}_H(Z)\), and let \(P=\operatorname{Pa}_G(Z)\).  By \texttt{def:actualized-projection},
\[
 Q_Z^S(a_S)=\bigcup_{r\in R(P):\,r|_S=a_S}A_Z(r).
\]
The row \(a_P\) belongs to the indexing set, and \(A_Z(a_P)=\{a_Z\}\).  Hence \(a_Z\in Q_Z^S(a_S)\).  Finiteness in \texttt{ass:finite-signature} makes this an ordinary finite union, while nonemptiness in \texttt{ass:total-local-tables} ensures that every displayed set is defined.

Suppose first that \(Q_Z^S(a_S)=\{a_Z\}\).  If some \(D\in\mathcal B_Z\) were disjoint from \(S\), then by \texttt{def:bad-support-family} there would be a row \(r\) and a value \(t\in A_Z(r)\setminus\{a_Z\}\) such that \(D=\Delta_Z(r)\).  Disjointness gives \(r_p=a_p\) for every \(p\in S\), so \(r|_S=a_S\).  The preceding union would then contain \(t\ne a_Z\), a contradiction.  Thus \(S\cap D\ne\varnothing\) for every \(D\in\mathcal B_Z\).

Conversely, suppose that \(S\) meets every member of \(\mathcal B_Z\).  If \(t\in Q_Z^S(a_S)\) and \(t\ne a_Z\), the definition of the union supplies a row \(r\) with \(r|_S=a_S\) and \(t\in A_Z(r)\setminus\{a_Z\}\).  Then \(\Delta_Z(r)\in\mathcal B_Z\), but \(r|_S=a_S\) implies \(S\cap\Delta_Z(r)=\varnothing\), again a contradiction.  Therefore the union contains no value other than \(a_Z\), and since it contains \(a_Z\), it equals \(\{a_Z\}\).

Finally, the actual full-parent row cannot itself generate a bad support: it is replaced by \(\{a_Z\}\) before projection.  This is precisely the actual-row freezing asserted in the last sentence of the lemma, and it uses the designated solution in \texttt{ass:actual-solution} to identify the row being actualized.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the local semantic invariant that turns Beckers legality into an explicit hitting condition without assuming the answer. \textit{Gap.} Beckers2025Actual defines actualization and generalized parent deletion separately; it does not derive their row-level iff characterization. \textit{Consumer.} The verifier can reject an invalid simplification by displaying one uncovered bad-row support.

\begin{lemma}[lem:canonical-replay]\label{lem:canonical-replay}
For every instance in \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{exp}}\) and retained graph \(H\in\mathcal H(G)\), a model is a legal full-setting Beckers structural simplification with retained graph \(H\) if and only if every node satisfies \(Q_Z^{S_Z}(a_{S_Z})=\{a_Z\}\); in that case the canonical pre-actualized projection has actualization exactly \(Q_Z^{S_Z}\), preserves \(H\), and preserves the deletion cost \(\sum_{e\in E(G)\setminus E(H)}c(e)\). Every accepted alternate partial state extends to a complete solution by topological completion.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix an instance in \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{exp}}\) and \(H\in\mathcal H(G)\).  Write \(S_Z=\operatorname{Pa}_H(Z)\).  Under the full-setting structural-simplification definition, deleting the other parent coordinates projects the actualized original relation, so the simplified actualized table at \(Z\) must be
\[
 Q_Z^{S_Z}(b)=\bigcup_{r:\,r|_{S_Z}=b}A_Z(r).
\]
An actualized model on the retained signature has the singleton table \(\{a_Z\}\) at \(a_{S_Z}\).  Consequently a legal simplification with graph \(H\) necessarily satisfies \(Q_Z^{S_Z}(a_{S_Z})=\{a_Z\}\) for every \(Z\).  The graphical part of legality is already supplied by \(H\in\mathcal H(G)\), as defined in \texttt{def:legal-retained-graph}.

For sufficiency, assume these singleton equalities and define a pre-actualized table on the retained signature by
\[
 B_Z(b)=\bigcup_{r:\,r|_{S_Z}=b}F_Z(r).
\]
It is total and nonempty by \texttt{ass:total-local-tables}.  Moreover, \(a_Z\in B_Z(a_{S_Z})\) by \texttt{ass:actual-solution}, so \(a\) is a solution of the model with tables \(B_Z\).  Let \(B_Z^a\) denote its actualization.  At \(b=a_{S_Z}\),
\[
 B_Z^a(a_{S_Z})=\{a_Z\}=Q_Z^{S_Z}(a_{S_Z}).
\]
If \(b\ne a_{S_Z}\), no full row extending \(b\) is the actual full-parent row.  Thus \(A_Z(r)=F_Z(r)\) for every such extension and
\[
 B_Z^a(b)=B_Z(b)=\bigcup_{r:\,r|_{S_Z}=b}A_Z(r)=Q_Z^{S_Z}(b).
\]
Hence the canonical model has actualization exactly \(Q_Z^{S_Z}\) at every row.  Giving this model the retained parent signature \(H\) preserves the graph by construction.  Since deletion cost is a function only of the deleted edge set, its cost is exactly
\[
 sum_{e\in E(G)\setminus E(H)}c(e),
\]
the cost of every representative having retained graph \(H\).  This proves the converse and cost preservation.

For completeness of replay, an accepted partial record means the following checkable object: exogenous variables have their fixed actual values; the intervention value is recorded; whenever a nonintervened endogenous variable is already assigned, all of its retained parents that can affect its projected row are assigned and the record supplies a full original row witnessing membership in the corresponding \(Q_Z^{S_Z}\).  Equivalently, its assigned portion is closed under retained parents, apart from coordinates deleted and hence quantified locally by the supporting row.  Process the remaining endogenous variables in a topological order of the acyclic graph \(H\).  When \(Z\) is reached, all retained parents have values, and \(Q_Z^{S_Z}\) is nonempty because it is a union of nonempty actualized rows; choose any member.  Previously checked memberships remain true because none of their retained-parent values changes.  Induction over the order produces a complete solution extending the record.  This is the topological completion used by the replay verifier.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The lemma closes the main extensional gap between finite compiler records and Beckers's quantified model semantics. \textit{Gap.} Section 6, Proposition 20 on page 525, with its proof on page 532, of Beckers2025Actual gives existence of a same-signature representative that is also a structural simplification of the original model, but not an explicit arbitrary-domain, cost-preserving replay theorem with certificate completion. \textit{Consumer.} An implementation can expose a small checkable record rather than asking users to trust an internal search over simplified models.

\begin{theorem}[thm:value-path-optimum]\label{thm:value-path-optimum}
For every instance in \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{forest}}\), \(X=a_X\) is a Beckers actual cause of \(Y=a_Y\) if and only if a value-path certificate exists. Moreover, the minimum deletion cost over all legal witnesses is exactly \(\operatorname{OPT}=\min_{s_i\ne a_{P_i}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}C_i(s_{i-1},s_i)\). If \(X\) does not reach \(Y\), the answer is negative; otherwise the unique full-graph path fixes every off-path retained parent at its actual value and makes the local safe relations complete under composition.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write \(a_i=a_{P_i}\).  We first record the propagation fact used in both directions.  In an admissible actualized simplification, if a nonintervened endogenous variable \(Z\) has a nonactual value in an alternate solution, then some retained parent of \(Z\) also has a nonactual value.  Indeed, if all retained parents were actual, its value would lie in \(Q_Z^{S_Z}(a_{S_Z})=\{a_Z\}\) by \texttt{lem:canonical-replay}, a contradiction.  Repeating this argument backwards terminates because \(G\) is acyclic.  It cannot terminate at an exogenous root, whose value is fixed at its actual value, or at a nonintervened endogenous root, whose actualized empty-parent row is a singleton.  It therefore terminates at the intervened variable \(X\).  Thus every changed variable is a retained descendant of \(X\).

If \(X\) does not reach \(Y\) in \(G\), no retained subgraph can contain such a path, so the propagation fact rules out a witness.  Now suppose that \(X\) reaches \(Y\).  By \texttt{ass:full-polyforest}, the undirected full graph has a unique simple path between them; since a directed path exists, it is \(P_0=X\to P_1\to\cdots\to P_m=Y\).

Consider any legal witness with retained graph \(H\) and alternate solution \(w\).  Backward propagation from changed \(Y\) must follow a retained \(X\)-to-\(Y\) path.  Uniqueness forces every edge \(P_{i-1}\to P_i\) to be retained and every \(P_i\) to be changed.  Put \(s_i=w_{P_i}\ne a_i\).  If an off-path parent \(q\in W_i\) were a descendant of \(X\) in \(G\), a directed \(X\)-to-\(q\) path followed by \(q\to P_i\) would give a second undirected route from \(X\) to \(P_i\), unless \(q=P_{i-1}\), which is excluded by the definition of \(W_i\).  This contradicts the forest condition.  Hence no member of \(W_i\) is a descendant of \(X\), and the propagation fact gives \(w_q=a_q\) for every \(q\in W_i\).

Membership of \(s_i\) in the projected table at \(P_i\) supplies a full supporting row \((s_{i-1},b_i)\) such that
\[
 s_i\in A_{P_i}(s_{i-1},b_i).
\]
If \((b_i)_q\ne a_q\), then \(q\to P_i\) cannot be retained, because a retained off-path parent has alternate value \(a_q\).  Therefore \(D_i(b_i)\) is contained in the witness's set \(E_i\) of deleted off-path incoming edges.  The actual-row equality for that witness says that projection over \(E_i\) has output exactly \(\{a_i\}\).  Reinstating every edge in \(E_i\setminus D_i(b_i)\) only removes rows from this union, while the actual full row still contributes \(a_i\).  Hence
\[
 \operatorname{Safe}_i(D_i(b_i))
\]
holds.  By the definition of \(C_i\),
\[
 C_i(s_{i-1},s_i)
 \le \sum_{q\in D_i(b_i)}c(q\to P_i)
 \le \sum_{q\in E_i}c(q\to P_i).
\]
The second inequality and the omission of deletions elsewhere use the nonnegativity in \texttt{ass:nonnegative-rational-cost}.  Summing over \(i\) shows that every legal witness costs at least the cost of a value-path certificate and hence at least the displayed minimum defining \(\operatorname{OPT}\).

Conversely, take a finite value-path certificate \((s_0,\ldots,s_m;b_1,\ldots,b_m)\).  Delete exactly the edges \(q\to P_i\) with \(q\in D_i(b_i)\), and retain every other edge.  Any retained alternative path between the endpoints of a deleted edge would combine with that edge to form an undirected cycle in \(G\); therefore the retained graph belongs to \(\mathcal H(G)\).  At a path node, its retained predecessor is actual in the local actual-row check and the deleted off-path set is \(D_i(b_i)\), so \(\operatorname{Safe}_i(D_i(b_i))\) is exactly the equality \(Q_{P_i}^{S_{P_i}}(a_{S_{P_i}})=\{a_i\}\).  Outside the path no incoming edge was deleted, and the same equality reduces to \(A_Z(a_{\operatorname{Pa}_G(Z)})=\{a_Z\}\).  Thus \texttt{lem:canonical-replay} supplies a legal structural simplification.

Intervene with \(X\leftarrow s_0\).  Give every nondescendant of \(X\) its actual value and set \(P_i=s_i\).  For each path node the supporting row agrees with the alternate values of all retained parents: the predecessor entry is \(s_{i-1}\), and every coordinate outside \(D_i(b_i)\) is actual.  Hence \(s_i\in Q_{P_i}^{S_{P_i}}\) at the alternate retained-parent row.  No off-path descendant can feed into a path node, by the preceding unique-path argument.  Process all still unassigned descendants topologically and choose an output from their nonempty projected tables.  This completes an alternate solution without changing a specified path value.  Since \(s_m\ne a_Y\), it is a causal witness.  Its deletion cost is exactly the certificate charge.

It remains only to identify the recurrence with the minimum.  By induction on \(i\), \(d_i(t)\) is the minimum charge of a length-\(i\) value path ending at \(t\): the assertion is the definition of \(d_0\), and adjoining the last transition gives
\[
 d_i(t)=\min_{s\ne a_{i-1}}\{d_{i-1}(s)+C_i(s,t)\}.
\]
All state sets are finite, so every finite minimum is attained.  Taking the minimum over \(t\ne a_Y\) proves both the formula for \(\operatorname{OPT}\) and the equivalence between its finiteness, existence of a certificate, and canonical Beckers causation.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem gives both a semantic characterization and an additive optimum rather than only a decision algorithm. \textit{Gap.} EiterLukasiewicz2006Tractable propagates value sets for Halpern--Pearl causes on an endogenous causal tree; it does not cover actualized deletion relations, full exogenous incidence, arbitrary rational deletion costs, or Beckers counterfactual witnesses. \textit{Consumer.} The result supplies the exact forest dispatch and minimum-abnormality witness required by an explicit-support backend for the \(\texttt{actualcauses}\) Python package.

\begin{theorem}[thm:compiler-verifier-equivalence]\label{thm:compiler-verifier-equivalence}
There exist total algorithms \(\operatorname{Compiler}\) and \(\operatorname{Verifier}\) such that, for every instance in \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{forest}}\), the compiler returns a certificate in \(\mathcal C_{+}\), \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{opt}}\), or \(\mathcal C_{-}\); the verifier accepts exactly the certificates whose decoded claim is true under canonical Beckers causation and deletion cost. A positive or optimality certificate is returned if and only if the cause exists, an optimality certificate reports exactly \(\operatorname{OPT}\), and a separating certificate is returned if and only if no witness exists. Compilation and verification use \(O(L)\) arithmetic and indexed-address operations and polynomial bit time; after common-denominator scaling, every intermediate integer has \(O(B+\log(L+1))\) bits.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Assume first that \(X\) reaches \(Y\), and use the unique path from \texttt{thm:value-path-optimum}.  At layer \(i\), discard off-path coordinates with singleton domains from the mask indexing: they can never differ from actuality, and deleting such an off-cycle edge neither changes a projection nor lowers a nonnegative cost.  If \(r_i\) coordinates remain, total explicit tabulation contains at least \(2^{r_i}\) parent rows and, for \(r_i>0\), at least \(r_i2^{r_i}\) indexed parent-coordinate incidences.

Initialize a Boolean array \(\operatorname{bad}_i[D]\) on the \(2^{r_i}\) masks.  Scan exactly the rows whose predecessor coordinate is \(a_{i-1}\).  If such a row has a nonactual output after actualization, mark its off-path difference support.  For each coordinate \(j\), perform
\[
 \operatorname{bad}_i[D]\leftarrow
 \operatorname{bad}_i[D]\lor\operatorname{bad}_i[D\setminus\{j\}]
 \qquad(j\in D).
\]
After processing the first \(k\) coordinates, induction on \(k\) shows that \(\operatorname{bad}_i[D]\) is true exactly when \(D\) contains a marked support after arbitrary removal of those \(k\) coordinates.  At the end it is true exactly when some bad support is contained in \(D\).  By \texttt{lem:local-hitting-equivalence},
\[
 \operatorname{Safe}_i(D)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \neg\operatorname{bad}_i[D].
\]

Compute \(w_i[\varnothing]=0\) and \(w_i[D]=w_i[D\setminus\{j\}]+c(j\to P_i)\) for one indexed \(j\in D\).  Scan every table row with predecessor value \(s\ne a_{i-1}\).  Let \(D\) be its off-path difference mask.  When \(\neg\operatorname{bad}_i[D]\), scan its explicitly listed outputs \(t\ne a_i\) and relax
\[
 d_i(t)\leftarrow\min\{d_i(t),d_{i-1}(s)+w_i[D]\}.
\]
Store the attaining predecessor and original row.  This scan considers exactly the feasible triples in the definition of \(C_i\), and no other triples; hence its relaxations implement exactly the min-plus recurrence for \(d_i\).  The induction in the final paragraph of \texttt{thm:value-path-optimum} proves that the final finite label is exactly \(\operatorname{OPT}\).  Backpointers give the rows of a value-path certificate.  The theorem's constructive sufficiency proof gives \(H\), the alternate value of \(X\), and a full alternate state, so the compiler returns an element of \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{opt}}\subseteq\mathcal C_{+}\cup\mathcal C_{\mathrm{opt}}\).  If only a positive record is requested, forgetting the labels gives an element of \(\mathcal C_{+}\).

If no final label is finite, define
\[
 T_0=R(X)\setminus\{a_X\},\qquad
 T_i=\{t\ne a_i:\exists s\in T_{i-1},\ C_i(s,t)<+\infty\}.
\]
The same induction says that \(T_i\) is exactly the set of endpoints of feasible length-\(i\) value paths.  Thus \(T_m=\varnothing\), and the arrays \((T_i)\) form the layerwise certificate in \(\mathcal C_{-}\).  If \(X\) does not reach \(Y\), a graph search returns the set of vertices reachable from \(X\); it contains \(X\), excludes \(Y\), and is closed under outgoing edges, giving the other form of \(\mathcal C_{-}\).  These cases make the compiler total and deterministic after fixing the indexed tie-breaking order.

Define the verifier by independent replay.  It first checks the explicit input, actualizes the original rows, and verifies that the supplied graph is a full forest.  In a forest, a deleted edge cannot have another retained path between its endpoints, so this single linear forest audit verifies the reachability condition for every deletion.  For a positive record it scans every actual projected row, applying \texttt{lem:local-hitting-equivalence}, and checks each claimed projected membership against its supplied original row.  It checks the intervention, the fixed exogenous values, every nonintervened equation, and the nonactual value of \(Y\); topological completion is justified by \texttt{lem:canonical-replay}.  Thus acceptance implies a canonical Beckers witness.  Conversely every witness normalizes by \texttt{thm:value-path-optimum} to a record passing these checks.

For an optimality record the verifier recomputes the subset flags and all feasible relaxations, checks the initial labels, every min-plus equality, the terminal minimum, and the attaining backpointer chain.  Induction over layers proves that accepted labels are the true \(d_i\), so the reported value is exactly \(\operatorname{OPT}\); the compiler-produced labels show completeness.  For a layerwise negative record it recomputes the displayed \(T_i\) equalities and checks \(T_m=\varnothing\); for a graph cut it checks outgoing-edge closure.  The recurrence induction and the no-reachability argument prove soundness, while the compiler constructions prove completeness.  Therefore, for each well-typed certificate, the decoded positive, optimum, or negative claim is accepted exactly when its displayed replay data establish the corresponding canonical claim, and one of the three complete records exists for every instance.

It remains to account for resources.  At layer \(i\), the subset transform takes \(O(r_i2^{r_i})\) Boolean/address operations and the weight array takes \(O(2^{r_i})\) arithmetic operations.  Both are bounded by the explicitly stored rows and parent-coordinate incidences for that table.  Every other step scans each vertex, edge, row, parent coordinate, or output incidence only a constant number of times.  The path search, forest audit, certificate reconstruction, and topological completion are also linear in those indexed records.  Summing over layers gives \(O(L)\) arithmetic and indexed-address operations and \(O(L)\) stored entries for both compilation and replay verification.

Write every nonzero cost as \(p_e/q_e\), with \(p_e\ge0\) and \(q_e>0\), and put \(Q=\prod_e q_e\).  The total input bit length gives
\[
 \operatorname{bits}(Q)\le B+1,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{bits}\bigl(p_eQ/q_e\bigr)=O(B).
\]
A finite label sums at most \(|E(G)|\le L\) scaled weights, so it has \(O(B+\log(L+1))\) bits.  Infinity is a separate marker.  Exact common-denominator scaling, addition, comparison, and certificate checking therefore take polynomial bit time; for example schoolbook arithmetic gives the conservative bound
\[
 O\!\left(L\bigl(B+\log(L+1)\bigr)^2\right)
\]
for cost arithmetic, in addition to indexed graph and table work.  This proves every semantic and resource assertion.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This theorem is the unresolved end-to-end obligation: it binds semantics, output records, optimal cost, and the explicit-input resource bound in one contract. \textit{Gap.} IbrahimRehwaldPretschner2019SAT proves Boolean HP encodings and ReydDiaconescuDessalles2025Search supplies adjustable approximate search; neither proves an exact arbitrary-domain Beckers compiler with independently replayable three-way certificates. \textit{Consumer.} Package maintainers obtain a reference checker for regression tests and users obtain auditable explanations of positive, optimal, and negative answers.

\begin{theorem}[thm:chain-np-completeness]\label{thm:chain-np-completeness}
Deciding whether \(X=0\) is a Beckers actual cause of \(Y=0\) is NP-complete on \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{chain}}\); the budgeted problem remains NP-complete when every edge has cost zero and the budget is zero. NP membership uses the retained graph, alternate full state, and supporting table rows. NP-hardness is by a polynomial reduction from distinct-literal \(3\)-SAT in which shared exogenous selector roots encode one Boolean choice per variable and clause gates lie on the endogenous chain; full-graph reachability legality forces every successful clause input to agree with the selector choice.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We first prove membership in \(\mathrm{NP}\), using only the explicit-input hypotheses and hence, in fact, for every singleton query in \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{exp}}\).  A certificate consists of a retained edge set \(E(H)\subseteq E(G)\), an alternate assignment \(w\in R(U\cup V)\), and, for each \(Z\in V\setminus\{X\}\), an index for a full parent row \(\rho_Z\in R(\operatorname{Pa}_G(Z))\).  The verifier checks
\[
 w_U=a_U,\qquad w_X\ne a_X,\qquad w_Y\ne a_Y,                                      \tag{1}
\]
and checks that \(\rho_Z\) agrees with \(w\) on every retained parent of \(Z\) and that
\[
 w_Z\in A_Z(\rho_Z)\qquad (Z\in V\setminus\{X\}).                                  \tag{2}
\]
Thus the row \(\rho_Z\) witnesses the required membership of \(w_Z\) in the projected table at the alternate retained-parent assignment.  The verifier also checks, for every \(Z\in V\),
\[
 Q_Z^{\operatorname{Pa}_H(Z)}(a_{\operatorname{Pa}_H(Z)})=\{a_Z\}.                  \tag{3}
\]
To check (3), it scans every explicitly listed row \(r\) whose retained coordinates equal their actual values, replaces the one full actual row by \(\{a_Z\}\), and rejects exactly when an allowed nonactual output occurs.  Finally, for every deleted edge \(p\to Z\), a graph search checks that there is no nonempty retained directed path from \(p\) to \(Z\).  By \(\mathrm{def:legal\mbox{-}retained\mbox{-}graph}\), this last test is equivalent to \(H\in\mathcal H(G)\).  By \(\mathrm{lem:canonical\mbox{-}replay}\), (2)--(3) and graphical legality are equivalent to a legal full-setting Beckers structural simplification in which the intervention \(X\leftarrow w_X\) has the complete alternate solution \(w\).  Conditions (1) therefore make the certificate accepting exactly when it witnesses the singleton causal query.  Actuality holds by the supplied actual solution, and minimality is automatic because the singleton candidate has no nonempty proper subcandidate.

Every guessed object is a list of indices into the explicit input.  The scans above take polynomially many operations in \(L\), and reachability can be checked once for each of at most \(L\) deleted edges.  For a budgeted instance the verifier additionally sums the costs of the deleted edges and compares the sum with the budget.  There are at most \(L\) summands, and elementary integer numerator--denominator arithmetic has bit length polynomial in \(B+L\).  Hence both the unbudgeted and budgeted problems are in \(\mathrm{NP}\).

For hardness, reduce from the problem in \(\mathrm{lem:three\mbox{-}sat\mbox{-}np\mbox{-}complete}\).  Delete repeated occurrences of a literal within a clause and discard every tautological clause.  These operations preserve satisfiability and leave at most three distinct literals in each remaining clause.  If an empty clause occurs, map to the construction below applied to the fixed unsatisfiable formula \((z)\wedge(\neg z)\); if no clause remains, map to the construction applied to the fixed satisfiable formula \((z)\).  We may therefore assume henceforth that the input \(\varphi=\bigwedge_{j=1}^m K_j\) has \(m\ge1\), variables \(z_1,\ldots,z_n\), and between one and three distinct, noncomplementary literals in every clause.

For every \(z_i\), create two binary exogenous roots \(T_i\) and \(F_i\).  Create binary endogenous variables in the chain
\[
 X\longrightarrow S_1\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow S_n
 \longrightarrow C_1\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow C_m=Y.                  \tag{4}
\]
All actual values are zero, and the structural table of \(X\) is constantly zero.  If \(p\) denotes the chain predecessor of \(S_i\), add the two root edges \(T_i\to S_i\) and \(F_i\to S_i\), and define the deterministic table
\[
 S_i(p,T_i,F_i)=
 \begin{cases}
 T_i\wedge F_i,&p=0,\\
 T_i\mathbin\oplus F_i,&p=1.
 \end{cases}                                                                       \tag{5}
\]
For a positive occurrence of \(z_i\) use the root \(T_i\), and for a negative occurrence use the root \(F_i\).  If the resulting distinct literal roots of \(K_j\) are \(\ell_{j1},\ldots,\ell_{jk_j}\), add their edges to \(C_j\) and set
\[
 C_j(p,\ell_{j1},\ldots,\ell_{jk_j})
 =p\wedge\bigl(\ell_{j1}\vee\cdots\vee\ell_{jk_j}\bigr).                       \tag{6}
\]
Equations (5)--(6) are total deterministic binary tables.  The all-zero assignment is a solution: (5) gives zero when all its arguments are zero, and (6) gives zero when \(p=0\).  The full graph is acyclic because every root edge enters a later vertex of (4), and its endogenous induced graph is exactly (4).  A selector has total indegree three and a clause has total indegree \(1+k_j\le4\).  Every displayed parent is genuine: in (5), fixing \((T_i,F_i)=(1,0)\) makes the output vary with \(p\), while fixing \(p=1\) and the other root at zero makes the output vary with either root; in (6), fixing one literal root at one makes the output vary with \(p\), and fixing \(p=1\) and all other literal roots at zero makes the output vary with the selected literal root.  Each table has at most \(2^4=16\) rows, and there are at most three literal incidences per clause, so the graph and all tables are constructed in time polynomial in \(|\varphi|\).  The constructed instance therefore belongs to \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{chain}}\).

We next establish the invariant forced by any accepting witness.  Let \(H\) and \(w\) be such a witness.  Since the domains and actual values are binary, (1) gives \(w_X=w_Y=1\), while every exogenous root remains at zero.  If a nonintervened endogenous variable \(Z\) has \(w_Z=1\), then at least one retained parent of \(Z\) has value one.  Indeed, if every retained parent had its actual value zero, alternate membership would put \(w_Z\) in the left side of (3), which is \(\{0\}\), a contradiction.  Starting at \(Y\) and iterating this implication backwards, the value-one parent cannot be exogenous.  Each vertex of (4) other than \(X\) has exactly one endogenous parent, so that parent and its incident chain edge must respectively have value one and be retained.  Induction back to \(X\) proves
\[
 w_{S_i}=w_{C_j}=1\quad\text{for all \(i,j\)},
 \qquad E((4))\subseteq E(H).                                                       \tag{7}
\]

Consider the two root edges entering \(S_i\).  If both were retained, their alternate values would be \((T_i,F_i)=(0,0)\); together with the retained predecessor value one from (7), equation (5) would force \(w_{S_i}=0\), contradicting (7).  If both were deleted, then in the actual retained-parent row the predecessor is zero and the deleted coordinates may be completed by \((T_i,F_i)=(1,1)\).  This is not the full actual row, so actualization leaves (5) unchanged there, and (5) outputs one.  Consequently the projection in (3) would contain one, again a contradiction.  Hence exactly one of
\[
 T_i\to S_i,\qquad F_i\to S_i                                                       \tag{8}
\]
is deleted.  Call its tail the selected root.  Define a Boolean assignment \(\alpha\) by
\[
 \alpha(z_i)=\mathrm{true}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad T_i\to S_i\text{ is deleted}. \tag{9}
\]
Equivalently, \(T_i\) is selected when \(z_i\) is true and \(F_i\) is selected when \(z_i\) is false.

Suppose first that \(\varphi\) has a satisfying assignment \(\alpha\).  Select roots according to (9), delete every outgoing edge of every selected root, and retain every other edge; call the resulting graph \(H_\alpha\).  Each deleted edge has a selected root as its tail, and that root has no retained outgoing edge.  Thus no deleted edge has a retained directed bypass, and \(H_\alpha\in\mathcal H(G)\) by \(\mathrm{def:legal\mbox{-}retained\mbox{-}graph}\).  At a selector, precisely one root coordinate is deleted and the other is retained at zero.  With the retained predecessor at its actual value zero, (5) is then zero for both completions of the deleted coordinate.  Hence (3) holds at every selector.  At a clause, the retained chain predecessor has actual value zero, so (6) is zero for every completion of all deleted literal coordinates; (3) holds there as well.  No edge enters \(X\), so its actualized empty-parent row is \(\{0\}\).  Therefore (3) holds at every endogenous vertex.

Intervene with \(X\leftarrow1\), fix all exogenous roots at zero, and assign value one to every vertex after \(X\) on (4).  At \(S_i\), choose the full support row having predecessor one, selected root coordinate one, and unselected root coordinate zero.  Equation (5) outputs one, and this row agrees with every retained parent.  Since \(\alpha\) satisfies \(K_j\), some literal of \(K_j\) has its selected root as parent.  Its incoming edge to \(C_j\) was deleted; set that coordinate to one, set every retained root coordinate to its exogenous value zero, and use predecessor one.  Equation (6) then outputs one.  These rows verify (2).  Thus \(\mathrm{lem:canonical\mbox{-}replay}\) yields a Beckers witness changing \(Y\) from zero to one, so \(X=0\) is a cause of \(Y=0\).

Conversely, suppose the constructed instance has a Beckers witness.  Recover \(\alpha\) from (8)--(9).  Fix a clause \(C_j\).  By (7), its retained chain predecessor has value one and \(w_{C_j}=1\).  A supporting row for its projected-table membership must therefore make at least one literal-root coordinate equal to one, by (6).  Let \(r\) be such a root.  Since the alternate exogenous value of \(r\) is zero, the edge \(r\to C_j\) must be deleted; otherwise a support row agreeing with retained-parent values would have that coordinate zero.  If \(r\) were unselected, its edge \(r\to S_i\) would be retained by (8), where \(i\) is the variable represented by \(r\).  Equation (7) retains the entire chain segment from \(S_i\) through \(S_n,C_1,\ldots,C_j\).  Hence
\[
 r\to S_i\to S_{i+1}\to\cdots\to S_n\to C_1\to\cdots\to C_j                    \tag{10}
\]
would be a retained directed path from the tail to the head of the deleted edge \(r\to C_j\), contradicting \(H\in\mathcal H(G)\).  Therefore \(r\) is selected.  If \(r=T_i\), the corresponding positive literal is true under (9); if \(r=F_i\), the corresponding negative literal is true.  Thus every clause contains a true literal, and \(\alpha\) satisfies \(\varphi\).

We have constructed a polynomial many-one reduction satisfying
\[
 \varphi\text{ is satisfiable}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 X=0\text{ is a Beckers actual cause of }Y=0.                                      \tag{11}
\]
Together with \(\mathrm{lem:three\mbox{-}sat\mbox{-}np\mbox{-}complete}\), (11) proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness, and the verifier above proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness.  Setting \(c(e)=0\) for every edge and setting the budget to zero satisfies the nonnegative rational cost restriction and makes the budget constraint equivalent to mere witness existence, so the same reduction proves the stated budgeted hardness.

As the smallest boundary check, the one-clause formula \((z)\) selects \(T\), deletes \(T\to S\) and \(T\to C\), and has the all-one endogenous alternate solution.  For \((z)\wedge(\neg z)\), (8) selects exactly one of \(T,F\); making the clause for the other literal equal to one would delete that unselected root's clause edge while retaining its path through \(S\), violating (10).  Thus the construction accepts the former and rejects the latter, in agreement with (11).  The proof concerns decision certificates only and makes no claim that unrestricted witnesses are counted parsimoniously.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The restriction shows that endogenous treewidth and domain size cannot parameterize an exact Beckers backend by themselves. \textit{Gap.} EiterLukasiewicz2002Complexity and AleksandrowiczEtAl2014 analyze Halpern--Pearl variants; their reductions do not establish hardness for Beckers deletion legality on a deterministic binary endogenous chain of total indegree four. \textit{Consumer.} The backend can refuse an endogenous-chain fast path when shared exogenous roots create nonforest full incidence.

\begin{proposition}[prop:full-endogenous-separation]\label{prop:full-endogenous-separation}
For every \(\kappa\ge2\), the endogenous graph of \(\mathcal O_{\kappa}\) is one directed causal tree, namely a chain. For each selector \(S_i\), the history deleting \(T_i\to S_i\) and the history deleting \(F_i\to S_i\) both satisfy local actual-row hitting and give the same singleton feasible nonactual endogenous-value set \(\{1\}\). Deleting \(T_i\to C_i\) is also locally safe and admits value \(1\) at the later gate \(C_i\) in both histories. It is nevertheless full-graph legal after deleting \(T_i\to S_i\) and illegal after deleting \(F_i\to S_i\), because the latter history retains the directed path \(T_i\to S_i\to\cdots\to C_i\). Thus a direct transfer that keeps only \(G[V]\) and the unaugmented layer state of families of subsets of the endogenous path-variable domains from the causal-tree recurrence of EiterLukasiewicz2006Tractable cannot audit Beckers deletion legality on this parameterized family.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(\kappa\ge2\) and \(i\in\{1,\ldots,\kappa\}\).  By \texttt{def:selector-obstruction-family}, the induced endogenous graph contains exactly the displayed edges
\[
 X\to S_1\to\cdots\to S_\kappa\to C_1\to\cdots\to C_\kappa=Y,
\]
so it is a directed chain and hence a causal tree.

At \(S_i\), delete \(T_i\to S_i\) and retain \(F_i\to S_i\).  At actual predecessor zero the retained root has value \(F_i=0\), and
\[
 S_i(0,T_i,0)=T_i\wedge0=0
\]
for both completions of the deleted coordinate.  Thus the local actual row is preserved.  With predecessor one, the supporting completion \(T_i=1,F_i=0\) gives \(S_i=1\).  Interchanging \(T_i\) and \(F_i\) proves the same two assertions when \(F_i\to S_i\) is deleted instead.  Because the domain is binary, the feasible nonactual output set in either history is exactly \(\{1\}\).  This also agrees with the hitting criterion in \texttt{lem:local-hitting-equivalence}: the sole bad actual-predecessor completion is \((T_i,F_i)=(1,1)\), whose support is hit by either retained root edge.

Now additionally delete \(T_i\to C_i\).  Since
\[
 C_i(0,T_i)=0
\]
for both values of the deleted coordinate, the deletion is locally safe.  At predecessor one, the supporting completion \(T_i=1\) gives \(C_i=1\) in both selector histories.

Retain all chain edges and all edges not just specified as deleted.  In the first history, \(T_i\to S_i\) is absent and the only other outgoing edge of \(T_i\), namely \(T_i\to C_i\), is also absent.  Hence there is no retained path from \(T_i\) to \(C_i\); neither deleted edge has an alternative retained path, so the history is in \(\mathcal H(G)\).  In the second history, \(T_i\to S_i\) is retained, as is the entire chain segment
\[
 S_i\to S_{i+1}\to\cdots\to S_\kappa\to C_1\to\cdots\to C_i.
\]
It is therefore a retained directed path from \(T_i\) to \(C_i\), making deletion of the direct edge \(T_i\to C_i\) illegal under \texttt{def:legal-retained-graph}.  The two histories have identical feasible nonactual singleton state at the selector and at the later gate, but different full-graph legality.  Any recurrence whose state records only \(G[V]\) and unaugmented families of endogenous path-value subsets identifies these histories and therefore cannot, by itself, certify the Beckers deletion condition on this family.  This is the asserted separation, without a claim about an augmented state or unrestricted treewidth.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The scalable selector family isolates exactly which full-graph reachability information an endogenous-value state omits. \textit{Gap.} The earlier UAI version of EiterLukasiewicz2006Tractable, Section 4.1 and Theorems 4.2--4.3, proves its Halpern--Pearl causal-tree result by recursively propagating families of subsets of each endogenous path-variable domain. This proposition leaves that theorem untouched and shows only that a direct \(G[V]\)-only transfer of that state type cannot certify Beckers edge-deletion legality. \textit{Consumer.} A parameterized regression batch over \(\mathcal O_{\kappa}\) detects backends that collapse two selector histories with identical endogenous value states but different full-graph legality.

\begin{lemma}[lem:three-sat-np-complete]\label{lem:three-sat-np-complete}
\texttt{Satisfiability of a conjunctive normal form in which every clause has at most three literals is NP-\allowbreak{}complete under polynomial-\allowbreak{}time many-\allowbreak{}one reductions.}
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[lem:component-restriction-normalization]\label{lem:component-restriction-normalization}
For an instance \(I=(M,a,X,Y,c,L,B)\in\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{exp}}\), let \(C_X\) and \(C_Y\) be the weak components of \(X\) and \(Y\) in the underlying undirected full graph. If \(C_X\ne C_Y\), then \(X=a_X\) is not a Beckers actual cause of \(Y=a_Y\). If \(C_X=C_Y=C\), restriction to \(C\) preserves existence of a Beckers witness and its exact minimum deletion cost. More precisely, every global witness restricts to a witness on \(C\) of no larger cost, and every witness on \(C\) extends, with the same cost, by retaining all edges and using the actual assignment on every other weak component.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(H\in\mathcal H(G)\) and let \(w\) be an alternate solution witnessing that \(X=a_X\) causes \(Y=a_Y\). By \(\texttt{lem:canonical-replay}\), for every endogenous vertex \(Z\),
\[
 Q_Z^{\operatorname{Pa}_H(Z)}(a_{\operatorname{Pa}_H(Z)})=\{a_Z\}. \tag{1}
\]
If a nonintervened endogenous vertex \(Z\) satisfies \(w_Z\ne a_Z\), then some retained parent \(p\in\operatorname{Pa}_H(Z)\) satisfies \(w_p\ne a_p\). Indeed, if every retained parent were actual, the structural membership of \(w_Z\) would place it in the left side of (1), which is the singleton \(\{a_Z\}\). Repeating this predecessor step terminates because \(G\) is acyclic. It cannot terminate at an exogenous root, because the context fixes every exogenous value at its actual value, and it cannot terminate at a nonintervened endogenous root, because (1) applies to its empty retained-parent row. Hence it terminates at the sole intervened vertex \(X\). Applied to \(w_Y\ne a_Y\), this gives a retained directed path from \(X\) to \(Y\). Since \(H\subseteq G\), the vertices \(X\) and \(Y\) must lie in the same weak component of \(G\). This proves the negative assertion when \(C_X\ne C_Y\).

Now suppose \(C_X=C_Y=C\). No edge of \(G\) has exactly one endpoint in \(C\). Consequently every parent of a vertex of \(C\) lies in \(C\), and restricting the domains and local tables to the vertices of \(C\) gives an explicit acyclic instance \(I|_C\) with the same query and the same costs on \(E(G[C])\).

First restrict a global witness \((H,w)\) to \(C\). If a deleted edge of \(G[C]\) had a retained alternative path in \(H[C]\), the same path would violate \(H\in\mathcal H(G)\); hence \(H[C]\in\mathcal H(G[C])\). All local actual-row equalities and all alternate memberships at vertices of \(C\) are unchanged, because these vertices have no parents outside \(C\). Thus \(\texttt{lem:canonical-replay}\) makes \((H[C],w|_C)\) a witness for \(I|_C\). Its cost is
\[
 \sum_{e\in E(G[C])\setminus E(H[C])}c(e)
 \le
 \sum_{e\in E(G)\setminus E(H)}c(e), \tag{2}
\]
where the inequality uses \(c(e)\ge0\) from \(\texttt{ass:nonnegative-rational-cost}\).

Conversely, let \((H_C,w_C)\) witness the restricted instance. Define
\[
 H'=H_C\cup\bigcup_{D\ne C}G[D],
 \qquad
 w'_q=
 \begin{cases}
 (w_C)_q,&q\in C,\\
 a_q,&q\notin C,
 \end{cases} \tag{3}
\]
where \(D\) ranges over the other weak components. Every deleted edge of \(H'\) lies in \(C\), and any path between its endpoints also lies in \(C\); legality of \(H_C\) therefore proves \(H'\in\mathcal H(G)\). At a vertex outside \(C\), all parents are retained and actual, so its projected actualized relation at both the actual and alternate input is
\[
 A_Z(a_{\operatorname{Pa}_G(Z)})=\{a_Z\}. \tag{4}
\]
At vertices in \(C\), the local checks are exactly those of the restricted witness. Hence canonical replay proves that (3) is a global witness. No edge outside \(C\) is deleted, so its cost equals that of \((H_C,w_C)\).

Taking minima in the two directions gives
\[
 \min_{\text{global witnesses}}\sum_{e\notin H}c(e)
 =
 \min_{\text{witnesses on }C}\sum_{e\in E(G[C])\setminus E(H_C)}c(e), \tag{5}
\]
with both empty minima equal to \(+\infty\). Equation (3) is the claimed cost-nonincreasing normalization outside the relevant component.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:connected-unicycle-min-sum]\label{lem:connected-unicycle-min-sum}
Let \(I=(M,a,X,Y,c,L,B)\in\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{exp}}\) have connected underlying undirected full graph of cycle rank one, with unique simple cycle \(K\). There is at most one directed edge \(e^\star\in E(K)\) whose complementary arc \(K\setminus\{e^\star\}\) is a directed path from the tail of \(e^\star\) to its head. Every Beckers witness has a cost-nonincreasing normalization consisting of an alternate assignment \(w\in R(U\cup V)\), one support row \(\rho_Z\in R(\operatorname{Pa}_G(Z))\) for each \(Z\in V\setminus\{X\}\), the row-difference deletion set
\[
 D^0(w,\rho)=\{p\to Z:Z\ne X,\ \rho_{Z,p}\ne w_p\},
\]
and, only when deleting \(e^\star\) would leave its complementary arc retained, one additional cycle edge. Conversely, every such normalized tuple satisfying the local actual-row hitting tests and \(w_Z\in A_Z(\rho_Z)\) for \(Z\ne X\) is a Beckers witness. Its exact minimum cost is the minimum of at most \(2|E(K)|-1\) explicit min-sum programs on a factor graph of cycle rank one. The recurrences in the proof evaluate them using \(O(L^7)\) arithmetic and indexed-address operations, every finite rational encountered has \(O(B+\log(L+1))\) bits, and the computation returns either a positive witness with an exact optimum certificate or polynomial-size infeasibility tables forming a separating cut.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(I=(M,a,X,Y,c,L,B)\in\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{exp}}\) as in the statement.  Thus the full directed graph \(G\) is acyclic, its underlying undirected graph is connected of cycle rank one, and that undirected graph has a unique simple cycle \(K\).  All domains and all local tables are finite and nonempty by \(\mathrm{ass:finite\mbox{-}signature}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:total\mbox{-}local\mbox{-}tables}\).  For a retained graph \(H\subseteq G\), write
\[
 D_H=E(G)\setminus E(H),
 \qquad
 S_Z(H)=\operatorname{Pa}_H(Z).
\]

We first reduce the causal semantics to finite constraints.  By \(\mathrm{lem:canonical\mbox{-}replay}\), a Beckers witness with retained graph \(H\in\mathcal H(G)\) is equivalent to the actual-row equalities
\[
 Q_Z^{S_Z(H)}(a_{S_Z(H)})=\{a_Z\}
 \qquad (Z\in V),                                                     \tag{1}
\]
together with an assignment \(w\in R(U\cup V)\) satisfying
\[
 w_U=a_U,\qquad w_X\ne a_X,qquad w_Y\ne a_Y,                         \tag{2}
\]
and
\[
 w_Z\in Q_Z^{S_Z(H)}(w_{S_Z(H)})
 \qquad (Z\in V\setminus\{X\}).                                     \tag{3}
\]
Here \(w_U=a_U\) keeps the exogenous context fixed, and the equation at \(X\) is absent from (3) because the alternate model intervenes on \(X\).  The singleton event \(X=a_X\), with \(X\ne Y\) by \(\mathrm{ass:distinct\mbox{-}singletons}\), has no nonempty proper subevent, so the minimality clause adds no further constraint.  Thus (1)--(3) are obtained from the Beckers query by canonical replay; they are not a definition of that query.

We next characterize graphical legality.  If a directed edge \(e=p\to Z\) is not on \(K\), then its underlying undirected edge is a bridge, so there is no path at all from \(p\) to \(Z\) after deleting \(e\).  If \(e\in E(K)\), the only undirected \(p\)-to-\(Z\) path not using \(e\) is the complementary arc \(K\setminus\{e\}\).  Consequently, for every \(e=p\to Z\in D_H\),
\[
 p\leadsto_H Z
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 K\setminus\{e\}\text{ is directed from \(p\) to \(Z\) and every edge of that arc is retained}.       \tag{4}
\]
Fix a cyclic orientation of the undirected cycle and label a directed cycle edge by the sign with which it is oriented relative to that cyclic orientation.  The complementary arc for \(e\) is directed from the tail of \(e\) to its head exactly when the sign of \(e\) is opposite to the sign of every other cycle edge.  Since a simple undirected cycle has at least three edges, two distinct edges cannot both have this property: a third edge would force the two distinguished edges to have the same sign, while either distinguished edge forces their signs to be opposite.  Hence there is at most one such edge; denote it by \(e^\star\) when it exists.

If \(e^\star\) does not exist, (4) shows that every retained subgraph is legal.  If it does exist, (4) and \(\mathrm{def:legal\mbox{-}retained\mbox{-}graph}\) give the exact equivalence
\[
 H\in\mathcal H(G)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 e^\star\notin D_H
 \ \text{or}\ 
 D_H\cap\bigl(E(K)\setminus\{e^\star\}\bigr)\ne\varnothing.          \tag{5}
\]
This is the only global constraint that will enter the finite programs.

We now prove the normalization.  Fix any witness \((H,w)\) satisfying (1)--(3).  For each \(Z\in V\setminus\{X\}\), membership (3) and the union in \(\mathrm{def:actualized\mbox{-}projection}\) supply a full parent row
\[
 \rho_Z\in R(\operatorname{Pa}_G(Z))
 \quad\text{such that}\quad
 w_Z\in A_Z(\rho_Z),
 \qquad
 \rho_Z|_{S_Z(H)}=w|_{S_Z(H)}.                                        \tag{6}
\]
Define the row-difference deletion set
\[
 D^0(w,\rho)
 =\{p\to Z:Z\ne X,\ \rho_{Z,p}\ne w_p\}.                             \tag{7}
\]
Equation (6) implies
\[
 D^0(w,\rho)\subseteq D_H.                                            \tag{8}
\]
If \(G\setminus D^0\) satisfies (5), set \(D=D^0\).  Otherwise \(e^\star\) exists and the failure of (5) says
\[
 e^\star\in D^0,
 \qquad
 D^0\cap\bigl(E(K)\setminus\{e^\star\}\bigr)=\varnothing.           \tag{9}
\]
The original graph \(H\) is legal and \(D^0\subseteq D_H\), so (5) and (9) imply that there is an edge
\[
 f\in D_H\cap\bigl(E(K)\setminus\{e^\star\}\bigr).                  \tag{10}
\]
In this case set \(D=D^0\cup\{f\}\).  In both cases
\[
 D\subseteq D_H
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 G\setminus D\in\mathcal H(G).                                      \tag{11}
\]
Thus at most one deletion beyond the row-difference set is used, and it is used only to repair the unique possible legality defect.

For each \(Z\in V\), put
\[
 S_Z(D)=\{p\in\operatorname{Pa}_G(Z):p\to Z\notin D\}.
\]
By (8), (10), and (11), \(D\subseteq D_H\), whence
\[
 S_Z(H)\subseteq S_Z(D).                                               \tag{12}
\]
Equation (1) and \(\mathrm{lem:local\mbox{-}hitting\mbox{-}equivalence}\) imply that \(S_Z(H)\) meets every \(\Delta\in\mathcal B_Z\).  Its superset in (12) therefore meets every such \(\Delta\), and the reverse implication of the same lemma yields
\[
 Q_Z^{S_Z(D)}(a_{S_Z(D)})=\{a_Z\}
 \qquad (Z\in V).                                                     \tag{13}
\]
For \(Z\ne X\), every parent retained under \(D\) agrees between \(\rho_Z\) and \(w\), by (7); adding the optional edge \(f\) to the deletion set only removes a matching requirement.  Hence (6) and the definition of the projected relation give
\[
 w_Z\in A_Z(\rho_Z)
 \subseteq Q_Z^{S_Z(D)}(w_{S_Z(D)})
 \qquad (Z\in V\setminus\{X\}).                                    \tag{14}
\]
Equations (2), (11), (13), and (14), followed by \(\mathrm{lem:canonical\mbox{-}replay}\), prove that \((G\setminus D,w)\) is again a Beckers witness.  Since costs are nonnegative by \(\mathrm{ass:nonnegative\mbox{-}rational\mbox{-}cost}\), (11) gives
\[
 \sum_{e\in D}c(e)\le \sum_{e\in D_H}c(e).                          \tag{15}
\]

Conversely, suppose that \(w\), rows \(\rho_Z\) for \(Z\ne X\), and a set \(D\) have the normalized form just described; suppose they obey (2), the legality condition (5),
\[
 w_Z\in A_Z(\rho_Z)\quad(Z\ne X),                                   \tag{16}
\]
and the local actual-row hitting tests
\[
 S_Z(D)\cap\Delta\ne\varnothing
 \quad(Z\in V,\ \Delta\in\mathcal B_Z).                            \tag{17}
\]
The hitting equivalence applied to (17) gives (13).  The row-difference definition (7), including the possible extra deletion \(f\), gives (14) from (16).  Condition (5) gives graphical legality.  Canonical replay therefore proves that this normalized tuple is a Beckers witness, and its deletion cost is exactly \(\sum_{e\in D}c(e)\).  This proves both directions of the normalized-certificate characterization.  Because all involved sets are finite, every nonempty feasible set of such tuples has a cost minimizer.

We expose the promised programs.  For an edge \(p\to Z\), define its base-deletion indicator by
\[
 \delta^0_{p\to Z}(\rho,w)
 =
 \begin{cases}
  \mathbf 1\{\rho_{Z,p}\ne w_p\},&Z\ne X,\\
  0,&Z=X.
 \end{cases}                                                          \tag{18}
\]
If \(e^\star\) is absent, use one unconstrained mode.  If it is present, use the following modes:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{mode \(0\):}&\delta^0_{e^\star}=0;\\[2pt]
\text{mode \(g\), \(g\in E(K)\setminus\{e^\star\}\):}
 &\delta^0_{e^\star}=\delta^0_g=1;\\[2pt]
\text{repair mode \(\bar f\), \(f\in E(K)\setminus\{e^\star\}\):}
 &\delta^0_{e^\star}=1,\quad
   \delta^0_h=0\ \text{for every }h\in E(K)\setminus\{e^\star\},
   \quad\text{and }f\text{ is additionally deleted.}
\end{array}                                                            \tag{19}
\]
There are \(1+(|E(K)|-1)+(|E(K)|-1)=2|E(K)|-1\) modes.  Mode \(0\) covers the first disjunct of (5).  If the second disjunct of (5) already holds for \(D^0\), at least one deleted complementary edge can be selected as \(g\).  The remaining case is exactly (9), and is covered by choosing its repair edge \(f\).  Thus (19) covers every normalized tuple; overlaps between modes do not affect a minimum.

For a mode \(b\), let \(f_b=f\) in repair mode \(\bar f\), and let \(f_b=\bot\) otherwise.  For an assignment of values to \(Z\) and all of its full-graph parents, define
\[
 \Phi_Z^b(w_Z,w_{\operatorname{Pa}_G(Z)})
 =
 \min_{\tau_Z}
 \left\{
  \sum_{p:p\to Z\in D_Z^b(\tau_Z,w)}c(p\to Z)
 \right\},                                                            \tag{20}
\]
where \(\tau_Z=\rho_Z\in R(\operatorname{Pa}_G(Z))\) and \(w_Z\in A_Z(\rho_Z)\) when \(Z\ne X\), while \(\tau_X=\star\) is the sole choice and no output constraint is imposed at \(X\).  The local deletion set in (20) is
\[
 D_Z^b(\tau_Z,w)
 =\{p\to Z:Z\ne X,\ \rho_{Z,p}\ne w_p\}
  \cup\{f_b:f_b=p\to Z\text{ for some \(p\)}\}.                     \tag{21}
\]
The minimum in (20) is also subject to the restrictions in (19) on indicators of edges entering \(Z\), and to the fully explicit hitting constraints
\[
 \left(\operatorname{Pa}_G(Z)
 \setminus\{p:p\to Z\in D_Z^b(\tau_Z,w)\}\right)
 \cap\Delta\ne\varnothing
 \qquad(\Delta\in\mathcal B_Z).                                     \tag{22}
\]
If these constraints admit no row, the value of (20) is \(+\infty\).  Each restriction in (19) concerns one edge indicator, so it is checked at the factor belonging to that edge's head.  The sets in (21), as \(Z\) varies, partition the deletion set by edge head; therefore the sum of the finite values in (20) counts each deleted edge exactly once.

For every \(q\in U\cup V\), define the unary potential
\[
 \psi_q(v)=
 \begin{cases}
  0,&q\in U\text{ and }v=a_q,\\
  0,&q=X\text{ and }v\ne a_X,\\
  0,&q=Y\text{ and }v\ne a_Y,\\
  0,&q\in V\setminus\{X,Y\},\\
  +\infty,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}                                                          \tag{23}
\]
The cases are disjoint because \(U\cap V=\varnothing\) and \(X\ne Y\).  Let \(C^\star(I)\) denote the minimum deletion cost among Beckers witnesses, with the empty minimum equal to \(+\infty\).  The cost-nonincreasing normalization (6)--(15) maps every witness to a feasible point of the following program, while (16)--(17) and canonical replay map every finite program point to a witness of exactly the displayed cost.  Both inequalities therefore hold, proving the identifying equality
\[
 C^\star(I)
 =
 \min_b\ \min_{w\in R(U\cup V)}
 \left\{
  \sum_{Z\in V}\Phi_Z^b(w_Z,w_{\operatorname{Pa}_G(Z)})
  +\sum_{q\in U\cup V}\psi_q(w_q)
 \right\}.                                                           \tag{24}
\]
In particular, the causal query has a positive witness if and only if the observed explicit-input functional on the right of (24) is finite.

It remains to evaluate (24) and certify the answer.  Form the bipartite factor graph with one variable vertex for every \(q\in U\cup V\), one factor vertex for every \(Z\in V\), and an edge from factor \(Z\) to each variable in \(\{Z\}\cup\operatorname{Pa}_G(Z)\).  Contracting every factor--child edge \(Z\text{--}Z\) recovers the underlying undirected graph of \(G\).  Edge contraction preserves cycle rank here, and connectedness is preserved, so this factor graph is connected of cycle rank one.  Choose a variable vertex \(q_0\) on its unique cycle.  For a fixed value \(v\in R(q_0)\), substitute \(q_0=v\) in every incident potential and delete \(q_0\); the remaining factor graph is a forest.

For any oriented edge of a rooted component of this forest, let \(N(\cdot)\) denote its neighbors and use the min-sum messages
\[
 m_{q\to Z}(x_q)
 =\psi_q(x_q)+
   \sum_{T\in N(q)\setminus\{Z\}}m_{T\to q}(x_q),                    \tag{25}
\]
and
\[
 m_{Z\to q}(x_q)
 =\min_{x_{N(Z)\setminus\{q\}}}
 \left\{
  \Phi_Z^b(x_{N(Z)})+
  \sum_{r\in N(Z)\setminus\{q\}}m_{r\to Z}(x_r)
 \right\}.                                                           \tag{26}
\]
An induction from the leaves proves that a message is exactly the minimum sum of all potentials on the sending side of the removed edge, conditional on its boundary value.  At a leaf this is (25) or (26) with an empty sum.  For the induction step, removing the receiving edge separates the sending side into the neighbor subtrees appearing in the relevant recurrence; the induction hypothesis gives each conditional subtree minimum, addition combines the disjoint subtrees, and the minimization in (26) eliminates precisely the newly internal boundary values.  Thus the component root expression is its exact conditional minimum.  Summing the component minima, adding \(\psi_{q_0}(v)\), minimizing over \(v\in R(q_0)\), and then minimizing over the modes in (19) evaluates exactly the right side of (24).

We now verify the stated resource bounds using \(\mathrm{ass:explicit\mbox{-}table\mbox{-}encoding}\).  Put \(d_Z=|R(Z)|\) and \(n_Z=|R(\operatorname{Pa}_G(Z))|\).  The encoding counts all domain entries, parent rows, parent-coordinate incidences, output incidences, edges, and vertices, so each of their totals is at most \(L\), and
\[
 \sum_{Z\in V}d_Zn_Z
 \le \left(\sum_{Z\in V}d_Z\right)
      \left(\sum_{Z\in V}n_Z\right)
 \le L^2.                                                             \tag{27}
\]
For each local assignment counted in (27), (20) enumerates at most \(L\) support rows.  For each candidate row, membership in its explicitly listed output set, all mode restrictions, and all intersections in (22) are checked by scanning at most \(L\) indexed incidences.  Thus all local potentials for one mode require at most \(O(L^4)\) elementary arithmetic or indexed-address operations, and all modes require at most \(O(L^5)\).  For a fixed mode and fixed \(q_0=v\), every message in (25)--(26) can be computed by enumerating the explicit local assignments of its factor; charging an assignment once per incident factor edge gives at most \(O(L^3)\) operations.  There are at most \(L\) values of \(q_0\) and at most \(2L\) modes.  Hence the entire computation is bounded by \(O(L^5)\), and therefore by the claimed conservative bound
\[
 O(L^7).                                                              \tag{28}
\]

Every finite local potential, message, and root value is a sum of costs of a subset of \(E(G)\); disjoint factor sides in (25)--(26) ensure that no edge cost acquires a coefficient other than zero or one.  Write the nonzero input costs as reduced fractions.  A common denominator may be chosen as the product of their denominators, whose binary length is at most the total input length \(B\).  After scaling, each summand has \(O(B)\) bits, and adding at most \(L\) summands adds at most \(\lceil\log_2(L+1)\rceil\) bits.  Reduction of a fraction cannot increase either length.  Consequently every finite rational encountered has
\[
 O(B+\log(L+1))                                                       \tag{29}
\]
bits, so comparisons and additions have polynomial bit complexity.

For a finite optimum, retain a minimizing mode, a minimizing value of \(q_0\), and one minimizing argument for every application of (20) and (26).  Backtracking supplies \(w\), every support row \(\rho_Z\), and the optional repair edge.  Equations (16), (17), and (5) verify the resulting positive witness, while the complete message tables together with (25)--(26) verify that its cost equals the minimum in (24); this is an exact optimum certificate of polynomial size.  If the value is \(+\infty\), output instead, for every oriented message edge, the finite-support set
\[
 T_{\nu\to\mu}=\{x:m_{\nu\to\mu}(x)<+\infty\}.                    \tag{30}
\]
The Boolean versions of (25)--(26) show locally that each set in (30) contains exactly the boundary values extendible through its sending-side subtree.  For every mode and every \(q_0=v\), the root combination is empty (or \(\psi_{q_0}(v)=+\infty\)); hence these tables separate every admissible boundary value from a complete feasible tuple.  Their entries and their local closure checks are indexed by the same polynomial-size tables used above, so (30) is the promised polynomial-size separating cut.

Finally, the exceptional full-graph constraint is genuinely load-bearing.  Consider the three-vertex full graph \(U\to X\to Y\) together with \(U\to Y\), binary domains, actual world zero, and deterministic tables \(X=U\) and \(Y=X\wedge U\).  The unique exceptional edge is \(e^\star=U\to Y\).  To obtain \(w_U=0\), \(w_X=1\), and \(w_Y=1\), the only supporting row for \(Y\) is \((U,X)=(1,1)\), so \(D^0\) deletes \(U\to Y\) and no complementary-cycle edge.  This violates (5).  Repair by deleting \(U\to X\) fails the hitting test at \(X\), while repair by deleting \(X\to Y\), together with the already deleted \(U\to Y\), fails the hitting test at \(Y\).  Thus every mode correctly returns infeasible.  Propagation on the endogenous chain alone would miss this obstruction, confirming that the full-graph legality state used above cannot be discarded.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:endogenous-tree-hardness-transfer]\label{prop:endogenous-tree-hardness-transfer}
The class \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{chain}}\) is contained in the broader Beckers class
\[
 \{I\in\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{exp}}:\text{the underlying undirected graph of \(G[V]\) is a tree}\}.
\]
Consequently, deciding whether \(X=a_X\) is a Beckers actual cause of \(Y=a_Y\) is NP-complete on this broader endogenous-causal-tree graph shape, and its zero-cost, zero-budget restriction is NP-hard. This transfer concerns Beckers actualized structural simplification and full-graph deletion legality; it does not identify Beckers causation with Halpern--Pearl causation or modify the published Halpern--Pearl causal-tree theorem.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By \(\texttt{ass:endogenous-chain}\), every instance of \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{chain}}\) has \(G[V]\) equal to one directed chain from \(X\) to \(Y\). Forgetting orientations gives a path, hence a tree. This proves the displayed class inclusion. The reduction in \(\texttt{thm:chain-np-completeness}\) is therefore already a reduction into the broader endogenous-tree graph-shape class, and it uses zero edge costs and budget zero. NP-hardness transfers upward by set inclusion.

It remains to prove membership for the broader class rather than assume it from the restricted theorem. For any explicit instance, guess a retained graph \(H\subseteq G\), a complete alternate assignment \(w\), and one original supporting row for every nonintervened projected-table membership. Reachability searches in \(H\) check, for each deleted \(p\to Z\), that \(p\not\leadsto_H Z\). A scan of the explicitly stored rows checks
\[
 Q_Z^{\operatorname{Pa}_H(Z)}(a_{\operatorname{Pa}_H(Z)})=\{a_Z\}
 \qquad(Z\in V),
\]
and the supplied rows check every alternate projected membership. The verifier also checks fixed exogenous values, \(w_X\ne a_X\), \(w_Y\ne a_Y\), and, when a budget is present, the exact rational deletion-cost sum. Each operation is polynomial in the explicit input length and rational bit length. By \(\texttt{lem:canonical-replay}\), acceptance is equivalent to a legal full-setting Beckers simplification and alternate solution; singleton minimality is automatic. Thus the problem is in NP on all of \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{exp}}\), and in particular on the displayed endogenous-tree subclass. Together with transferred NP-hardness this proves NP-completeness. The proof uses only the common graph shape when comparing with the published causal-tree setting; its semantic equivalence is nowhere assumed.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The corollary prevents an unsound endogenous-tree fast path: the chain reduction is already a subclass of that graph shape, while its shared exogenous selectors retain the Beckers hardness mechanism. \textit{Gap.} The transfer is a graph-class inclusion for Beckers semantics, not a reduction between Beckers and Halpern--Pearl definitions; prior Halpern--Pearl complexity results therefore do not themselves prove this corollary. \textit{Consumer.} The backend may use the relevant full component as its tractability test and must not infer tractability from the endogenous causal-tree shape alone.

\begin{proposition}[prop:endogenous-causal-tree-np-completeness]\label{prop:endogenous-causal-tree-np-completeness}
On the broader class of instances \(I\in\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{exp}}\) for which the endogenous induced graph \(G[V]\) is a directed causal tree containing a directed path from \(X\) to \(Y\), deciding whether the singleton event \(X=a_X\) is a Beckers actual cause of the singleton event \(Y=a_Y\) is \(\mathrm{NP}\)-complete.  In particular, \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{chain}}\) is a subclass of this endogenous causal-tree graph shape, the graph restriction studied by Eiter and Lukasiewicz~\cite{EiterLukasiewicz2006Tractable}.  This is a transfer of graph-shape complexity for Beckers structural-simplification semantics; it does not identify that causal predicate with any Halpern--Pearl predicate studied on the same graph shape.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By \(\mathrm{def:chain\mbox{-}class}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:endogenous\mbox{-}chain}\), every instance in \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{chain}}\) has \(G[V]\) equal to one directed chain from \(X\) to \(Y\).  The underlying undirected graph of a chain is a tree, and its displayed orientation supplies a directed path from \(X\) to \(Y\).  Hence
\[
 \mathfrak M_{\mathrm{chain}}
 \subseteq
 \{I\in\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{exp}}:G[V]\text{ is a directed causal tree containing a directed \(X\)-to-\(Y\) path}\}. \tag{12}
\]
The restriction theorem \(\mathrm{thm:chain\mbox{-}np\mbox{-}completeness}\) and (12) imply \(\mathrm{NP}\)-hardness on the class on the right: the identical instances and the identical Beckers yes/no predicate are used, so no semantic translation is involved.

For membership, use the retained-graph, alternate-assignment, and support-row certificate from the first part of the proof of \(\mathrm{thm:chain\mbox{-}np\mbox{-}completeness}\).  That verification uses only explicit finite tables, the actual-row tests, the reachability test in \(\mathrm{def:legal\mbox{-}retained\mbox{-}graph}\), and the equivalence in \(\mathrm{lem:canonical\mbox{-}replay}\); it does not use determinism, binarity, bounded indegree, or the chain restriction.  All of those inputs and tests are available for every member of \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{exp}}\), so the broader causal-tree restriction is in \(\mathrm{NP}\).  Combining membership with the transferred hardness proves \(\mathrm{NP}\)-completeness.  The reference to the endogenous causal-tree shape is therefore purely graph-theoretic, while the verified witness relation remains Beckers structural simplification throughout.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:relevant-component-polynomial-frontier]\label{thm:relevant-component-polynomial-frontier}
For every \(I=(M,a,X,Y,c,L,B)\in\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{exp}}\), let \(C_X\) and \(C_Y\) be the weak components of \(X\) and \(Y\) in the underlying undirected full graph.  Suppose either \(C_X\ne C_Y\), or \(C_X=C_Y=C\) and \(C\) has cycle rank at most one; no restriction is imposed on the other weak components.  Then \(\operatorname{UnicycleHandle}\) is a sound-and-complete polynomial-size certificate calculus and an exact polynomial-time algorithm, with the following explicit branches.

If \(C_X\ne C_Y\), then \(X=a_X\) is not a Beckers actual cause of \(Y=a_Y\), \(C^\star(I)=+\infty\), and the weak-component partition is a separating cut.  If \(C_X=C_Y=C\), the exact global minimum equals the minimum for \(I|_C\), and all other components may be retained at actuality.  When \(C\) has cycle rank zero and \(X\not\leadsto_{G[C]}Y\), the answer is again negative,
\[
 C^\star(I)=+\infty,
\]
and the set of vertices directed-reachable from \(X\) is a separating cut.  Only when \(X\leadsto_{G[C]}Y\) does the unique directed path \(P_0=X\to\cdots\to P_m=Y\) enter the functional, in which case
\[
 C^\star(I)
 =\min_{\substack{s_i\in R(P_i)\setminus\{a_{P_i}\}\\0\le i\le m}}
   \sum_{i=1}^{m} C_i(s_{i-1},s_i).
\]
The value-path compiler and verifier use \(O(L)\) arithmetic and indexed-address operations.  When \(C\) has cycle rank one,
\[
 C^\star(I)=\operatorname{MS}_1(I|_C),
\]
and the exceptional-edge min-sum algorithm uses \(O(L^7)\) arithmetic and indexed-address operations.  In every branch, \(X=a_X\) is a Beckers actual cause of \(Y=a_Y\) if and only if \(C^\star(I)<+\infty\); every finite rational has \(O(B+\log(L+1))\) bits, and the algorithm returns a positive witness with an exact optimum certificate or a polynomial-size separating cut.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The quantity \(C^\star(I)\) is defined independently of the algorithm as the minimum deletion cost among canonical Beckers witnesses, with the empty minimum equal to \(+\infty\).  Because the signature and edge set are finite and every edge cost is a finite nonnegative rational, a witness exists if and only if this minimum is finite.  Consequently
\[
 \mathbf 1\{X=a_X\text{ is a Beckers actual cause of }Y=a_Y\}
 =\mathbf 1\{C^\star(I)<+\infty\}.                                      \tag{1}
\]
This relates the causal query to the optimization functional; it does not define the causal query by the functional.

Let \(C_X\) and \(C_Y\) be as in the statement.  If \(C_X\ne C_Y\), \(\texttt{lem:component-restriction-normalization}\) says directly that no Beckers witness exists.  Equation (1) therefore gives
\[
 C^\star(I)=+\infty.                                                       \tag{2}
\]
By the definition of a weak component, no edge of the underlying undirected graph joins \(C_X\) to its complement.  Hence the partition \((C_X,(U\cup V)\setminus C_X)\), with \(X\in C_X\) and \(Y\notin C_X\), is an explicitly checkable separating cut.  This proves the first branch.

Now suppose \(C_X=C_Y=C\), and write \(I_C=I|_C\).  No vertex of \(C\) has a parent outside \(C\), since such an edge would join the two vertices in the same weak component.  Thus restricting the domains and tables to \(C\) preserves acyclicity, exogenous-root status, totality of every local table, the actual-solution equations, distinctness of \(X,Y\), nonnegative rational costs, and explicit encoding.  Therefore \(I_C\in\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{exp}}\), with input sizes \(L_C\le L\) and \(B_C\le B\).  The component-normalization lemma gives both existence equivalence and the exact equality
\[
 C^\star(I)=C^\star(I_C).                                                  \tag{3}
\]
It also gives the promised extension: retain every edge outside \(C\) and assign every vertex outside \(C\) its actual value.  Nonnegativity of the costs is already one of that lemma's hypotheses, so (3) includes the cost claim rather than merely the decision claim.

Assume first that \(C\) has cycle rank zero.  Its underlying undirected graph is connected by the definition of \(C\), and a connected graph of cycle rank zero is a tree: if an undirected cycle existed, deleting one edge of that cycle would preserve connectedness and contradict \(|E|=|C|-1\); conversely a connected acyclic graph has \(|E|=|C|-1\) by induction after removing a leaf.  Hence the full graph of \(I_C\) is a polyforest, so \(I_C\in\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{forest}}\).

Suppose \(X\not\leadsto_{G[C]}Y\).  Applying \(\texttt{thm:value-path-optimum}\) to \(I_C\) proves that no Beckers witness exists on \(C\).  Let
\[
 S=\{q\in C:X\leadsto_{G[C]}q\}\cup\{X\}.                               \tag{4}
\]
Then \(X\in S\), \(Y\notin S\), and there is no directed edge \(p\to q\) with \(p\in S\) and \(q\in C\setminus S\): such an edge would append to a directed path witnessing \(p\in S\) and would put \(q\) in \(S\).  Thus (4) is the claimed directed-reachability separating cut.  Combining the negative conclusion with (1) and (3) proves
\[
 C^\star(I)=C^\star(I_C)=+\infty.                                         \tag{5}
\]
This is the subcase that must be decided before any value-path object is formed.

Suppose instead that \(X\leadsto_{G[C]}Y\).  Since the underlying graph is a tree, there is only one undirected \(X\)-to-\(Y\) path; the directed path is therefore unique and may be written \(P_0=X\to\cdots\to P_m=Y\).  The exact-optimum part of \(\texttt{thm:value-path-optimum}\), followed by (3), gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 C^\star(I)
 &=C^\star(I_C)\\
 &=\min_{\substack{s_i\in R(P_i)\setminus\{a_{P_i}\}\\0\le i\le m}}
   \sum_{i=1}^{m}C_i(s_{i-1},s_i).                         \tag{6}
 \end{aligned}
\]
The same theorem proves that finiteness of (6) is equivalent to a value-path certificate and hence, by (1), to the causal query.  Applying \(\texttt{thm:compiler-verifier-equivalence}\) to \(I_C\) yields the positive, exact-optimum, or layerwise separating certificate, uses \(O(L_C)\subseteq O(L)\) arithmetic and indexed-address operations, and gives the stated bit bound.  A positive certificate is extended globally by the construction following (3).  A negative certificate remains globally separating because the component lemma rules out every global witness whenever it rules out a witness on \(C\).

Finally suppose that \(C\) has cycle rank one.  The restricted instance \(I_C\) meets every premise of \(\texttt{lem:connected-unicycle-min-sum}\).  The normalized tuples in that lemma are exactly the feasible tuples in \(\mathrm{def:rank\mbox{-}one\mbox{-}min\mbox{-}sum\mbox{-}functional}\): if their row-difference set is legal, the repair variable is \(\bot\); if it is illegal, the unique exceptional edge is deleted while its complementary directed arc is intact, and the repair variable chooses one edge on that arc.  The retained-parent hitting constraints are identical to those in the definition, and the support-row constraints are identical as well.  Therefore the normalization direction of the lemma gives
\[
 \operatorname{MS}_1(I_C)\le C^\star(I_C),                                 \tag{7}
\]
whereas its converse maps every feasible tuple defining \(\operatorname{MS}_1(I_C)\) to a Beckers witness of exactly the displayed deletion cost and gives
\[
 C^\star(I_C)\le\operatorname{MS}_1(I_C).                                  \tag{8}
\]
Equations (3), (7), and (8) prove the explicit identifying equality
\[
 C^\star(I)=\operatorname{MS}_1(I_C).                                       \tag{9}
\]
The same lemma partitions the legality alternatives into at most \(2|E(K)|-1\) min-sum programs, evaluates them in \(O(L_C^7)\subseteq O(L^7)\) arithmetic and indexed-address operations, bounds every finite rational by \(O(B_C+\log(L_C+1))\subseteq O(B+\log(L+1))\) bits, and returns either a witness with exact optimum data or infeasibility tables forming a separating cut.  Extension or separation for the global instance again follows from (3).

Computing weak components, cycle rank, and the reachable set (4) requires one scan of the explicitly indexed vertices and edges, hence \(O(L)\) address operations.  The forest branch is therefore linear-arithmetic, and the rank-one branch dominates the uniform bound by \(O(L^7)\).  All recorded assignments, support rows, predecessor choices, message tables, and cuts occur in the explicit finite tables considered by the cited compiler or min-sum lemma, so their lengths are polynomial in \(L+B\).  This proves soundness, completeness, exact optimality, and all certificate and complexity claims.

Two smallest exact checks isolate the repaired split.  In the deterministic binary tree \(Z\to X\), \(Z\to Y\), with actual world zero and equations \(X=Z\), \(Y=Z\), intervention \(X\leftarrow1\) leaves \(Y=0\).  Here the set (4) is \(\{X\}\), so (5) correctly returns \(+\infty\).  In the deterministic binary chain \(X\to Y\), with actual world zero and equation \(Y=X\), intervention \(X\leftarrow1\) gives \(Y=1\); the unique transition has \(C_1(1,1)=0\), so (6) returns zero.  Thus the no-directed-path and directed-path branches differ already on the smallest connected forests and cannot be merged.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem identifies the exact relevant-component frontier and exposes the directed-reachability failure branch before the path functional is formed. \textit{Gap.} Existing Halpern--Pearl causal-tree algorithms do not establish component restriction, full-graph reachability legality, actualized projection, or the rank-one exceptional-edge program for Beckers causation. \textit{Consumer.} The result gives the explicit-support backend a complete dispatcher returning a witness and exact optimum certificate or a separating cut whenever the relevant full-graph component has cycle rank at most one.

\begin{theorem}[thm:global-unicycle-polynomial-corollary]\label{thm:global-unicycle-polynomial-corollary}
Every explicit instance \(I\in\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{exp}}\) whose underlying full graph \(G\) has exactly one global simple cycle satisfies the hypotheses of \(\texttt{thm:relevant-component-polynomial-frontier}\); hence \(\operatorname{UnicycleHandle}\) gives it a sound-and-complete polynomial-size certificate calculus and a polynomial-time exact decision and optimization algorithm.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(K\) be any weak component of the underlying undirected full graph, and choose a spanning tree \(T_K\) of \(K\).  Each edge in \(E(K)\setminus E(T_K)\), together with the unique path in \(T_K\) between its endpoints, determines a distinct simple cycle.  Because \(G\) has exactly one global simple cycle, \(|E(K)\setminus E(T_K)|\le 1\), so the cycle rank of every weak component \(K\) is at most one.  In particular, the weak components containing \(X\) and \(Y\) are either distinct, or they coincide in a component of cycle rank at most one.  These are precisely the alternatives in \(\texttt{thm:relevant-component-polynomial-frontier}\), whose conclusion gives the asserted certificate calculus and exact polynomial-time decision and optimization algorithm.
\end{proof}

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The positive theorem requires finite explicitly indexed local tables and that the common weak full-graph component of \(X\) and \(Y\) have cycle rank at most one; it makes no claim for relevant components of larger cycle rank, general full-graph treewidth, succinct mechanisms, continuous domains, or negative deletion costs. The hardness theorem concerns a broader Beckers class described by an endogenous chain and shared exogenous selectors, not the positive relevant-component class. The selector reduction gives a bijection only for normalized certificates; unrestricted successful retained graphs need not be counted parsimoniously.

\section{Honest scope}

The exact tractability statement is component-local: different weak components give a negative answer, and when \(X\) and \(Y\) share a component, all other components may be retained at their actual assignments regardless of their cycle structure. A common rank-zero component is split again by full directed reachability; no path means value \(+\infty\), whereas a path activates the unique-path functional. A common rank-one component is handled by the proved normalized min-sum functional and exceptional-edge legality modes. These are restrictions on the full graph over \(U\cup V\), not assumptions inherited from Beckers or the endogenous causal-tree class of Eiter--Lukasiewicz. The explicit-table, finite-domain, acyclic, total-table, actual-solution, and nonnegative-rational-cost conditions remain in force. No general treewidth fixed-parameter algorithm or unrestricted parsimonious certificate-counting theorem is claimed.
