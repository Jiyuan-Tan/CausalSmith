The observational-rate-bracket theorem gives the complete proved result
\[
 c_{\mathrm{rate}}n^{-2\gamma/(4\gamma+d)}
 \le \rho_\star(n)
 \le C_{\mathrm{rate}}\left\{
 n^{-2\gamma/(4\gamma+d)}\vee
 n^{-4\gamma(\alpha+\beta)/[4\gamma(\alpha+\beta)+d(\alpha+\beta)+2d\gamma]}
 \right\}.
\]
For the lower inequality, every observed-data test is also a known-propensity test that ignores its propensity input, so the known-propensity minimax radius is no larger than \(\rho_\star(n)\); the known-propensity benchmark then gives the displayed lower endpoint.  For the upper inequality, the computable-upper-bound theorem supplies the terminating corrected-energy test uniformly over the same observed-law class.  Writing \(s=\alpha+\beta\), comparison of the two positive exponents gives
\[
 \frac{4\gamma s}{4\gamma s+ds+2d\gamma}
 \ge \frac{2\gamma}{4\gamma+d}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 s(4\gamma+d)\ge 2d\gamma.
\]
Hence the endpoints match when \(s\ge 2d\gamma/(4\gamma+d)\), including equality, where the two exact power sequences coincide and no logarithmic loss is present.  In the strict complementary regime the proved upper endpoint is \(r_{\mathrm{nu}}(n)\), but no matching legal same-class observational converse has been established.
%%% FIELD open-phase-partial
Let \(s=\alpha+\beta\).  The proved upper endpoint is oracle-sized exactly when
\[
 s\ge \frac{2d\gamma}{4\gamma+d},
\]
and in the strict complementary regime it has exponent
\[
 a_{\mathrm{nu}}=
 \frac{4\gamma s}{4\gamma s+ds+2d\gamma}.
\]
This characterizes the achieved bracket, not the unknown sharp frontier in that complementary regime.  For a sequence satisfying \(V(P_n)\asymp r_n^2\), the Dhawan--Guo--Shah implication additionally requires \(\mathsf R_{\mathrm{DGS},n}(P_n)\), the conditional correlation event with lower bound \(\kappa_n\), and \(n\kappa_n^2r_n^2\to\infty\); the score-translation lemma and overlap give energy equivalence but no uniform direction-correlation lower bound.  Yu's implication likewise remains conditional on both \(\mathsf R_{\mathrm{Yu},n}(P_n)\) and \(\mathsf Y_n\), with the separate signal requirement \(\sqrt n\,r_n^2\to\infty\).

At the proved oracle endpoint,
\[
 \sqrt n\,r_{\mathrm{or}}(n)^2
 =n^{1/2-4\gamma/(4\gamma+d)},
\]
which diverges exactly for \(\gamma<d/4\), is of constant order for \(\gamma=d/4\), and vanishes for \(\gamma>d/4\).  At the achieved nuisance endpoint,
\[
 \sqrt n\,r_{\mathrm{nu}}(n)^2
 =n^{1/2-8\gamma s/(4\gamma s+ds+2d\gamma)},
\]
which diverges exactly when \(ds+2d\gamma>12\gamma s\), is of constant order at equality, and vanishes when the inequality is reversed.  These are arithmetic consequences of the proved endpoints; they neither establish the learner predicates nor turn favorable-direction pointwise power into uniform omnibus power.
