Fix a known \(x_0\in[0,1]^d\).  Throughout, constants may depend on the fixed tuple \((d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L)\), but not on \(n\) or \(x_0\).

First consider achievability.  By \(\mathrm{lem:closed\mbox{-}regime\mbox{-}fixed\mbox{-}point\mbox{-}upper}\), the explicitly defined total finite Borel estimator \(\widehat\tau_n(x_0)\) satisfies, under \(\mathrm{ass:below\mbox{-}elbow}\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P(d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L)}
 \mathbb E_{P_O^{\otimes n}}
 \left|\widehat\tau_n(x_0)-\tau_P(x_0)\right|
 \le C\rho_n .                                                   \tag{1}
\]
The infimum in \(\mathrm{def:minimax\mbox{-}risk}\) is over all Borel data-only estimators and hence includes \(\widehat\tau_n(x_0)\).  Therefore
\[
 R_n
 \le
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P(d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L)}
 \mathbb E_{P_O^{\otimes n}}
 \left|\widehat\tau_n(x_0)-\tau_P(x_0)\right|
 \le C\rho_n .                                                   \tag{2}
\]

For the converse, apply \(\mathrm{thm:fixed\mbox{-}n\mbox{-}component\mbox{-}converse\mbox{-}resolution}\).  It supplies finite priors \(\Pi_+\) and \(\Pi_-\), both supported on the same class \(\mathcal P(d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L)\), such that \(\tau_P(x_0)=\theta_\varepsilon\) is constant for \(P\) in the support of \(\Pi_\varepsilon\),
\[
 \Delta:=|\theta_+-\theta_-|\ge c_0\rho_n,                       \tag{3}
\]
and the genuine fixed-\(n\) observed-data mixtures
\[
 \mathsf M_\varepsilon
 :=\int P_O^{\otimes n}\,d\Pi_\varepsilon(P),
 \qquad \varepsilon\in\{+,-\},                                  \tag{4}
\]
satisfy
\[
 H^2(\mathsf M_+,\mathsf M_-)\le\frac14.                         \tag{5}
\]
Thus (4) is already the required fixed-sample product experiment; no conditioning or Poissonization is used in the decision-theoretic reduction.

We now derive that reduction.  Let \(\nu=\mathsf M_++\mathsf M_-\), and let \(m_+\) and \(m_-\) denote the corresponding densities with respect to \(\nu\).  The identity
\[
 |m_+-m_-|=|\sqrt{m_+}-\sqrt{m_-}|(\sqrt{m_+}+\sqrt{m_-})
\]
and Cauchy--Schwarz give the standard inequality
\[
 \|\mathsf M_+-\mathsf M_-\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le \sqrt{2}\,H(\mathsf M_+,\mathsf M_-)
 \le\frac1{\sqrt2}.                                             \tag{6}
\]
Indeed, with the convention \(H^2=\tfrac12\int(\sqrt{m_+}-\sqrt{m_-})^2d\nu\), the first factor after Cauchy--Schwarz is \(\sqrt2 H\), while
\[
 \frac12\int(\sqrt{m_+}+\sqrt{m_-})^2d\nu\le2;
\]
if the unnormalized convention for squared Hellinger distance is used, the sharper inequality \(\|\mathsf M_+-\mathsf M_-\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le H\) follows by the same calculation.  Hence (6) holds under either convention, and its last inequality follows from (5).

Fix an arbitrary Borel estimator \(T\).  Define a Borel test \(\varphi\in\{+,-\}\) by choosing the value \(\theta_\varepsilon\) nearest to \(T\), with a fixed rule for ties.  If \(\varphi\ne\varepsilon\), nearest-value selection and the triangle inequality imply
\[
 \Delta
 \le |T-\theta_+|+|T-\theta_-|
 \le 2|T-\theta_\varepsilon|.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 |T-\theta_\varepsilon|\mathbf 1\{\varphi\ne\varepsilon\}
 \ge\frac{\Delta}{2}\mathbf 1\{\varphi\ne\varepsilon\}.     \tag{7}
\]
Because both priors are supported on the exact model, averaging the maximal risk over them and using (7) yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\sup_{P\in\mathcal P(d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L)}
 \mathbb E_{P_O^{\otimes n}}|T-\tau_P(x_0)|\\
 &\quad\ge
 \frac12\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{+,-\}}
 \int \mathbb E_{P_O^{\otimes n}}|T-\theta_\varepsilon|\,
 d\Pi_\varepsilon(P)\\
 &\quad\ge
 \frac{\Delta}{4}
 \{\mathsf M_+(\varphi=-)+\mathsf M_-(\varphi=+)\}.             \tag{8}
\end{aligned}
\]
Writing \(B=\{\varphi=+\}\), the variational definition of total variation and (6) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathsf M_+(\varphi=-)+\mathsf M_-(\varphi=+)
 &=1-\mathsf M_+(B)+\mathsf M_-(B)\\
 &\ge1-\|\mathsf M_+-\mathsf M_-\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\\
 &\ge1-2^{-1/2}.                                                  \tag{9}
\end{aligned}
\]
Combining (3), (8), and (9), and then taking the infimum over all Borel \(T\), proves
\[
 R_n\ge \frac{c_0}{4}(1-2^{-1/2})\rho_n.                        \tag{10}
\]
Equations (2) and (10) establish the claimed matched order for every \(n\ge2\).  The constants are uniform in \(x_0\) because both invoked all-point results have constants depending only on \((d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L)\).  Moreover, (4) consists of mixtures of actual fixed-\(n\) product laws in the exact-uniform, bounded-overlap Bernoulli class.

It remains to classify the oracle boundary.  By \(\mathrm{def:rate}\),
\[
 \eta=\left(1+\frac d{2\gamma}+\frac d{2S}\right)^{-1}.
\]
At \(S=S_\star=d\gamma/(2\gamma+d)\), positivity of \(d\) and \(\gamma\) permits substitution and inversion:
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \eta
 &=\left(1+\frac d{2\gamma}
          +\frac{2\gamma+d}{2\gamma}\right)^{-1}\\
 &=\left(2+\frac d\gamma\right)^{-1}
 =\frac\gamma{2\gamma+d}.                                      \tag{11}
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(\rho_n=n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}\), a pure power with no logarithmic multiplier.  If \(S<S_\star\), then positivity gives
\[
 \frac d{2S}>\frac d{2S_\star}
 =1+\frac d{2\gamma}.
\]
After adding the positive term \(1+d/(2\gamma)\) and taking reciprocals of positive quantities,
\[
 \eta<\left(2+\frac d\gamma\right)^{-1}
 =\frac\gamma{2\gamma+d}.                                      \tag{12}
\]
Consequently, (2) and (10) imply
\[
 \frac{R_n}{n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}}
 \asymp n^{\gamma/(2\gamma+d)-\eta}\longrightarrow\infty.      \tag{13}
\]
Hence, within the stipulated closed regime, the same-class oracle order is attained exactly at the elbow and is unattainable strictly below it.

Finally, let \(\mathcal P'\) be any model containing \(\mathcal P(d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L)\) and carrying the same point target.  For every Borel estimator \(T\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P'}\mathbb E_{P_O^{\otimes n}}|T-\tau_P(x_0)|
 \ge
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P(d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L)}
 \mathbb E_{P_O^{\otimes n}}|T-\tau_P(x_0)|.
\]
Taking infima over the same estimator class preserves the inequality, so (10) transfers to \(\mathcal P'\).  The upper bound (2) uses a guarantee only over the exact class and therefore yields no upper bound over \(\mathcal P'\).
