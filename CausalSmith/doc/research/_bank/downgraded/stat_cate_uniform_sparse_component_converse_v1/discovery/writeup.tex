\section*{stat\_\allowbreak{}cate\_\allowbreak{}uniform\_\allowbreak{}sparse\_\allowbreak{}component\_\allowbreak{}converse}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} For every fixed known \(x_0\in[0,1]^d\), the exact-uniform bounded-overlap Bernoulli model has sharp pointwise CATE risk \(R_n\asymp n^{-1/(1+d/(2\gamma)+d/(2(\alpha+\beta)))}\) throughout \(S=\alpha+\beta\le d\gamma/(2\gamma+d)\). A reflected inward local projection estimator attains the rate on every data set, and a translated correlated-sign component prior proves the genuine fixed-\(n\) same-class converse. At the elbow the rate is exactly \(n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}\), with no logarithmic multiplier; strictly below it, nuisance learning causes an unavoidable slower power.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} The published pointwise CATE theory gives this candidate power on a broader model, but its displayed lower laws delete design mass around nuisance-bump transitions. A lower bound on that broader class does not imply the same bound after fixing \(P_X\) to the exact uniform law, and it does not by itself certify arbitrary boundary or interior point targets on the singleton class.

\paragraph{Niche.} The note isolates the exact known-uniform, bounded-overlap Bernoulli/interiority subclass and proves its pointwise estimation and precision benchmark. The class is a strict subclass of the published general-design model, so a same-class converse is the missing ingredient. Causal identification is taken as given and recorded with the target estimand, rather than presented as a separate contribution.

\paragraph{Fill.} A reflected inward chart gives one total finite projection \(U\)-statistic estimator uniformly over fixed \(x_0\). For the converse, a translated compact smooth window and squared frame partition make singleton sign mixtures agree exactly; sparse component factorization, a mixed-amplitude Hellinger bound, and spanning-tree enumeration control the full fixed-\(n\) mixture. At equality, \(n\ell^d\) is a tunably small constant, which closes the elbow without a logarithm.

\section{Related work}

KennedyBalakrishnanRobinsWasserman2024MinimaxCATE is the closest comparator: its lower theorem covers a broader bounded-density class and its upper theorem requires additional design and nuisance-estimation conditions, whereas the present result closes the fixed-\(n\) converse and matching upper bound on the strict exact-uniform bounded-overlap Bernoulli/interiority subclass. The lower converse therefore transfers upward by subclass inclusion to any broader model that contains this subclass and uses the same point target; the exact-uniform upper proof does not transfer in that direction and is not claimed there. RobinsTchetgenLiVanderVaart2009SemiparametricMinimax supplies the product-mixture perspective, but the shared smooth frames here require random overlap-component factorization rather than a deterministic coordinate-local partition. ShenGaoWittenHan2020Variance is a random-design nonlinear-functional analogue for repeated local occupancy. Kennedy2023DRCATE and NieWager2021RLearner provide oracle-facing upper comparisons, while RobinsLiTchetgenVanderVaart2008HOIF motivates the finite projection correction. LiuMukherjeeRobinsTchetgen2021Adaptive studies adaptive nonlinear-functional estimation, whereas the present estimator uses known smoothness. LeeOkuiWhang2017CATEBands studies uniform CATE confidence bands, whereas this note proves pointwise mean-risk only. AtheyWager2021PolicyLearning studies policy-value and regret learning rather than pointwise CATE minimax precision. ChernozhukovWuthrichZhu2021Conformal studies conformal counterfactual and synthetic-control inference rather than this estimand or guarantee. WagerAthey2018CausalForests is an application-facing consumer: this is a precision benchmark for \(\texttt{causal\_forest}\) and R-learner workflows, not a claim that existing forests attain the bound.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:below-elbow]\label{ass:below-elbow}
\(S\le S_{\star}=d\gamma/(2\gamma+d)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (At-or-below-elbow H"older regime, \cite{KennedyBalakrishnanRobinsWasserman2024MinimaxCATE}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:iid-sampling]\label{ass:iid-sampling}
\(O_{1:n}\sim P_O^{\otimes n}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Independent and identically distributed sampling, \cite{KennedyBalakrishnanRobinsWasserman2024MinimaxCATE}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:uniform-design]\label{ass:uniform-design}
\(P_X=\operatorname{Unif}([0,1]^d)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Known uniform random design, \cite{KennedyBalakrishnanRobinsWasserman2024MinimaxCATE}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:propensity-normalization]\label{ass:propensity-normalization}
\(e_P(x)=g_P(x)/4\) for every \(x\in\mathcal X\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Smooth bounded propensity parametrization, \cite{KennedyBalakrishnanRobinsWasserman2024MinimaxCATE}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:propensity-envelope]\label{ass:propensity-envelope}
\(1\le g_P(x)\le2\) for every \(x\in\mathcal X\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform overlap envelope, \cite{KennedyBalakrishnanRobinsWasserman2024MinimaxCATE}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:propensity-holder]\label{ass:propensity-holder}
\(g_P\in\mathcal H_d^{\alpha}(L)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (H\"older-smooth propensity, \cite{KennedyBalakrishnanRobinsWasserman2024MinimaxCATE}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:control-holder]\label{ass:control-holder}
\(\mu_{0,P}\in\mathcal H_d^{\beta}(L)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (H\"older-smooth control regression, \cite{KennedyBalakrishnanRobinsWasserman2024MinimaxCATE}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:contrast-holder]\label{ass:contrast-holder}
\(\tau_P\in\mathcal H_d^{\gamma}(L)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Separately smooth H\"older treatment contrast, \cite{KennedyBalakrishnanRobinsWasserman2024MinimaxCATE}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:response-interiority]\label{ass:response-interiority}
\(1/8\le\mu_{a,P}(x)\le7/8\) for every \(a\in\{0,1\}\) and \(x\in\mathcal X\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Interior Bernoulli regression means, \cite{KennedyBalakrishnanRobinsWasserman2024MinimaxCATE}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:bernoulli-response]\label{ass:bernoulli-response}
\(Y\mid(X=x,A=a)\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(\mu_{a,P}(x))\) for every \(a\in\{0,1\}\) and \(P_X\)-almost every \(x\in\mathcal X\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Binary conditional-response experiment, \cite{KennedyBalakrishnanRobinsWasserman2024MinimaxCATE}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:consistency]\label{ass:consistency}
\(Y=AY(1)+(1-A)Y(0)\) almost surely under \(P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Potential-outcome consistency, \cite{KennedyBalakrishnanRobinsWasserman2024MinimaxCATE}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:conditional-exchangeability]\label{ass:conditional-exchangeability}
\((Y(0),Y(1))\perp A\mid X\) under \(P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Conditional exchangeability, \cite{KennedyBalakrishnanRobinsWasserman2024MinimaxCATE}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{definition}[def:minimax-risk]\label{def:minimax-risk}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Rn}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \[
R_n(x_0)=\inf_{T\ \mathrm{Borel}}\sup_{P\in\mathcal P(d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L)}
\mathbb E_{P_O^{\otimes n}}\!\left|T(O_{1:n})-\tau_P(x_0)\right|.
\]
Because \(x_0\) is fixed and known throughout the note, write this quantity as \(R_n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}exact-\allowbreak{}model}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}iid-\allowbreak{}sampling}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:rate]\label{def:rate}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{rhoN}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Under \(S\le S_\star\), define
\[
\rho_n=n^{-\eta}=n^{-1/(1+d/(2\gamma)+d/(2S))}.
\]
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}below-\allowbreak{}elbow}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:corrected-moments]\label{def:corrected-moments}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{corrected local moments}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Put \(h=h_n\), \(\bar h=h/2\), and, for \(k=1,\ldots,d\), set \(\sigma_k=1\) if \(x_{0k}\le1/2\) and \(\sigma_k=-1\) otherwise.  Define the inward affine chart
\[
\Phi_{x_0,h}(z)=x_0+\bar h\,\sigma\odot z,\qquad z\in[0,1]^d,
\]
whose image lies in \([0,1]^d\), and define
\[
Z_i=\bar h^{-1}\sigma\odot(X_i-x_0),\quad I_i=\mathbf1\{Z_i\in[0,1]^d\},\quad
w_i=\bar h^{-d}I_i,\quad v_i=v(Z_i)I_i,\quad b_i=b_{M_n}(Z_i)I_i.
\]
Every zero-indicator product is evaluated before an outside-domain basis value.  With \(p=3/8\), define
\[
\widetilde Q_n=\frac1n\sum_iw_iv_iA_i(A_i-p)v_i^{\mathsf T}
-\frac1{n(n-1)}\sum_{i\ne j}w_iw_jv_i(A_i-p)(b_i^{\mathsf T}b_j)A_jv_j^{\mathsf T},
\]
\[
\widetilde R_n=\frac1n\sum_iw_iv_i(Y_i-1/2)(A_i-p)
-\frac1{n(n-1)}\sum_{i\ne j}w_iw_jv_i(A_i-p)(b_i^{\mathsf T}b_j)(Y_j-1/2).
\]
The factor \(\bar h=h_n/2\) makes the full chart available at every boundary or interior location; reflection has unit absolute Jacobian and the remaining constant factor is absorbed in rate constants.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}exact-\allowbreak{}model}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}iid-\allowbreak{}sampling}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:total-estimator]\label{def:total-estimator}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{That}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} With \(\lambda_0=3/16\), define
\[
\widehat\tau_n(x_0)=
\begin{cases}
0,&s_{\min}(\widetilde Q_n)<\lambda_0/2,\\
\operatorname{clip}_{[-3/4,3/4]}\!\left(v(0)^{\mathsf T}\widetilde Q_n^{-1}\widetilde R_n\right),
&s_{\min}(\widetilde Q_n)\ge\lambda_0/2.
\end{cases}
\]
The inward chart, branch rule, half-open cells, fixed basis order, ceiling in \(M_n\), and clipping make this a finite total Borel algorithm on every data set.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}corrected-\allowbreak{}moments}; \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}rate}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:component-handle]\label{def:component-handle}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{ComponentHandle}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For the fixed known \(x_0\), construct \(\mathfrak H_{n,x_0}^{\mathrm{comp}}\) by multiplying a smooth squared partition of unity at fine scale \(\ell\) by a translated compact \(C^\infty\) localization bump that equals one at \(x_0\), using shared Rademacher coefficients and joint \((A,Y)\)-cell perturbations that cancel after one sign average.  Conditional on \(X_{1:n}\), connect observations whose active frames share a coefficient, factor the mixture over connected components, control each non-singleton component through a mixed amplitude derivative, and sum connected subsets by labeled spanning trees.  The localization is restricted to \([0,1]^d\); at a boundary point this only reduces its active volume and never changes the exact uniform design.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}exact-\allowbreak{}model}; \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}rate}.
\end{definition}

\section{Main results}

\begin{theorem}[thm:sharp-exact-uniform-rate]\label{thm:sharp-exact-uniform-rate}
Fix any known \(x_0\in[0,1]^d\).  There exist constants \(0<c\le C<\infty\), depending only on \((d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L)\) and uniform in \(x_0\), such that, for every \(n\ge2\) and \(S\le S_\star\),
\[
c\rho_n\le R_n\le C\rho_n,\qquad
\rho_n=n^{-1/(1+d/(2\gamma)+d/(2(\alpha+\beta)))}.
\]
The upper bound is attained by the total finite estimator \(\widehat\tau_n(x_0)\), and the lower bound uses genuine fixed-\(n\) product laws in the same exact-uniform, bounded-overlap Bernoulli class.  At \(S=S_\star\), the rate is exactly \(n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}\) with no logarithmic multiplier; for \(S<S_\star\), it is strictly slower than that oracle power.  Consequently, the lower bound transfers by set inclusion to any model containing this exact class and using the same point target; no corresponding upper bound is asserted.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix the known point \(x_0\).  The constants below depend only on the fixed tuple \((d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L)\) and are uniform in \(x_0\).

By \(\mathrm{lem:closed\mbox{-}regime\mbox{-}fixed\mbox{-}point\mbox{-}upper}\), the total finite Borel estimator in \(\mathrm{def:total\mbox{-}estimator}\) satisfies, under \(\mathrm{ass:below\mbox{-}elbow}\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P(d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L)}
 \mathbb E_{P_O^{\otimes n}}
 \left|\widehat\tau_n(x_0)-\tau_P(x_0)\right|
 \le C\rho_n.                                                    \tag{1}
\]
The infimum in \(\mathrm{def:minimax\mbox{-}risk}\) ranges over every Borel data-only estimator and therefore includes this estimator.  Hence
\[
 R_n\le
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P(d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L)}
 \mathbb E_{P_O^{\otimes n}}
 \left|\widehat\tau_n(x_0)-\tau_P(x_0)\right|
 \le C\rho_n.                                                    \tag{2}
\]

For the converse, \(\mathrm{thm:fixed\mbox{-}n\mbox{-}component\mbox{-}converse\mbox{-}resolution}\) supplies finite priors \(\Pi_+\) and \(\Pi_-\), both supported on the same class \(\mathcal P(d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L)\), such that \(\tau_P(x_0)=\theta_\varepsilon\) is constant on the support of \(\Pi_\varepsilon\),
\[
 \Delta:=|\theta_+-\theta_-|\ge c_0\rho_n,                     \tag{3}
\]
and the actual fixed-product mixtures
\[
 \mathsf M_\varepsilon
 :=\int P_O^{\otimes n}\,d\Pi_\varepsilon(P),
 \qquad \varepsilon\in\{+,-\},                                \tag{4}
\]
satisfy
\[
 H^2(\mathsf M_+,\mathsf M_-)\le\frac14.                       \tag{5}
\]
Thus the decision experiment in (4) uses neither a conditioning event nor Poissonization.

We derive the testing reduction explicitly.  Let \(\nu=\mathsf M_++\mathsf M_-\), and write \(m_+\) and \(m_-\) for the densities with respect to \(\nu\).  Cauchy--Schwarz, the definition \(H^2=\int(\sqrt{m_+}-\sqrt{m_-})^2d\nu\), and \(\int m_+d\nu=\int m_-d\nu=1\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \lVert\mathsf M_+-\mathsf M_-\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
 &=\frac12\int|\sqrt{m_+}-\sqrt{m_-}|(\sqrt{m_+}+\sqrt{m_-})\,d\nu\\
 &\le\frac12H(\mathsf M_+,\mathsf M_-)
 \left\{\int(\sqrt{m_+}+\sqrt{m_-})^2d\nu\right\}^{1/2}\\
 &\le H(\mathsf M_+,\mathsf M_-)
 \le\frac12.                                                     \tag{6}
\end{aligned}
\]
The penultimate inequality uses \(\int(\sqrt{m_+}+\sqrt{m_-})^2d\nu\le4\), and the final inequality is (5).

Fix an arbitrary Borel estimator \(T\), and define a Borel test \(\varphi\in\{+,-\}\) by selecting the value \(\theta_\varepsilon\) nearest to \(T\), with a fixed tie rule.  On \(\{\varphi\ne\varepsilon\}\), the triangle inequality and the nearest-value property imply
\[
 \Delta\le|T-\theta_+|+|T-\theta_-|
 \le2|T-\theta_\varepsilon|,
\]
so
\[
 |T-\theta_\varepsilon|\mathbf 1\{\varphi\ne\varepsilon\}
 \ge\frac\Delta2\mathbf 1\{\varphi\ne\varepsilon\}.         \tag{7}
\]
Prior support in the exact model, followed by (7), yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\sup_{P\in\mathcal P(d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L)}
 \mathbb E_{P_O^{\otimes n}}|T-\tau_P(x_0)|\\
 &\quad\ge\frac12\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{+,-\}}
 \int\mathbb E_{P_O^{\otimes n}}|T-\theta_\varepsilon|\,d\Pi_\varepsilon(P)\\
 &\quad\ge\frac\Delta4
 \{\mathsf M_+(\varphi=-)+\mathsf M_-(\varphi=+)\}.           \tag{8}
\end{aligned}
\]
Writing \(B=\{\varphi=+\}\), the variational definition of total variation and (6) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathsf M_+(\varphi=-)+\mathsf M_-(\varphi=+)
 &=1-\mathsf M_+(B)+\mathsf M_-(B)\\
 &\ge1-\lVert\mathsf M_+-\mathsf M_-\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \ge\frac12.                                                     \tag{9}
\end{aligned}
\]
Combining (3), (8), and (9), then taking the infimum over all Borel \(T\), proves
\[
 R_n\ge\frac{c_0}{8}\rho_n.                                   \tag{10}
\]
Equations (2) and (10) establish the matched order for every \(n\ge2\).  The lower priors in (4) consist of laws in the exact-uniform, bounded-overlap Bernoulli class, as asserted.

It remains to classify the oracle boundary.  From \(\mathrm{def:rate}\),
\[
 \eta=\left(1+\frac d{2\gamma}+\frac d{2S}\right)^{-1}.
\]
At \(S=S_\star=d\gamma/(2\gamma+d)\), substitution through positive denominators gives
\[
 \eta=\left(1+\frac d{2\gamma}+\frac{2\gamma+d}{2\gamma}\right)^{-1}
 =\frac\gamma{2\gamma+d}.                                      \tag{11}
\]
Thus \(\rho_n=n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}\), a pure power without a logarithmic multiplier.  If \(S<S_\star\), then
\[
 \frac d{2S}>1+\frac d{2\gamma},
\]
and taking reciprocals of positive quantities gives
\[
 \eta<\left(2+\frac d\gamma\right)^{-1}
 =\frac\gamma{2\gamma+d}.                                      \tag{12}
\]
Consequently, (2) and (10) imply
\[
 \frac{R_n}{n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}}
 \asymp n^{\gamma/(2\gamma+d)-\eta}\longrightarrow\infty.    \tag{13}
\]

Finally, suppose another model contains \(\mathcal P(d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L)\) and uses the same point target.  For every estimator, its supremum risk over the larger model is at least its supremum risk over this subclass.  Taking infima preserves this inequality, so (10) transfers.  Equation (2) is only an exact-class estimator guarantee and supplies no upper bound for the larger model.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem determines the exact-uniform minimax order at every fixed known point throughout the closed at-or-below-elbow regime. \textit{Gap.} The published general-design lower construction does not prove the converse after fixing the design to exact uniformity; the component prior supplies that missing same-class certificate. \textit{Consumer.} Users obtain an exact pointwise CATE precision benchmark, including the no-log oracle equality at the elbow.

\begin{proposition}[prop:oracle-attainment-classification]\label{prop:oracle-attainment-classification}
Under \(S<S_{\star}\), \(\eta<\gamma/(2\gamma+d)\), and therefore \(R_n/\rho_n^{\mathrm{or}}\asymp n^{\gamma/(2\gamma+d)-\eta}\to\infty\). Hence no parameter tuple in the stated strict below-elbow regime attains the same-class supplied-propensity benchmark. At the sanity-check boundary \(S=S_{\star}\), the two exponents coincide and reduce to \(n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}\) without a logarithmic multiplier.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By \(\mathrm{def:rate}\),
\[
 \eta=\left(1+\frac d{2\gamma}+\frac d{2S}\right)^{-1}.
\]
The declared oracle rate is
\[
 \rho_n^{\mathrm{or}}=n^{-q_{\mathrm{or}}},
 \qquad q_{\mathrm{or}}:=\frac\gamma{2\gamma+d}
 =\left(2+\frac d\gamma\right)^{-1}.                            \tag{1}
\]
The declared spaces give \(d,S,\gamma>0\).  All denominators are therefore positive, so cross-multiplication preserves inequality directions.  Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \eta<q_{\mathrm{or}}
 &\Longleftrightarrow
 1+\frac d{2\gamma}+\frac d{2S}>2+\frac d\gamma\\
 &\Longleftrightarrow \frac d{2S}>1+\frac d{2\gamma}\\
 &\Longleftrightarrow d\gamma>S(2\gamma+d)\\
 &\Longleftrightarrow
 S<\frac{d\gamma}{2\gamma+d}=S_\star.                         \tag{2}
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus in the strict regime \(S<S_\star\), the exponent \(\delta:=q_{\mathrm{or}}-\eta\) is positive.  By \(\mathrm{thm:sharp\mbox{-}exact\mbox{-}uniform\mbox{-}rate}\),
\[
 cn^{-\eta}\le R_n\le Cn^{-\eta},
 \qquad n\ge2.                                                   \tag{3}
\]
Since \(\rho_n^{\mathrm{or}}=n^{-q_{\mathrm{or}}}>0\), division in (3) gives
\[
 cn^{q_{\mathrm{or}}-\eta}
 \le\frac{R_n}{\rho_n^{\mathrm{or}}}
 \le Cn^{q_{\mathrm{or}}-\eta}.                               \tag{4}
\]
Because \(\delta>0\), equation (4) proves
\[
 \frac{R_n}{\rho_n^{\mathrm{or}}}\asymp n^\delta\longrightarrow\infty. \tag{5}
\]
Therefore no tuple in the strict below-elbow regime attains the same-class oracle order.

At the boundary \(S=S_\star\) permitted by \(\mathrm{ass:below\mbox{-}elbow}\), every equivalence in (2) holds with equality, and
\[
 \eta=q_{\mathrm{or}}=\frac\gamma{2\gamma+d}.                  \tag{6}
\]
Substitution of (6) into the sharp-rate theorem gives
\[
 cn^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}
 \le R_n\le Cn^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}.                          \tag{7}
\]
Both bounds in (7) are pure powers of \(n\); no logarithmic multiplier appears.  Thus the same-class oracle benchmark is attained exactly at the elbow and nowhere in the strict below-elbow regime.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The rate comparison turns the minimax theorem into an exact answer about when nuisance learning destroys oracle pointwise precision. \textit{Gap.} The oracle elbow appears in KennedyBalakrishnanRobinsWasserman2024MinimaxCATE, but its necessity on the exact-uniform subclass requires the new same-class converse. \textit{Consumer.} The classification supplies the decision-facing phase boundary for interpreting pointwise precision in R-learner and causal-forest workflows.

\begin{lemma}[lem:closed-regime-fixed-point-upper]\label{lem:closed-regime-fixed-point-upper}
For every fixed known \(x_0\in[0,1]^d\) and every parameter tuple satisfying \(S\le S_\star\), the estimator in \(\mathrm{def:total\mbox{-}estimator}\) obeys
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P(d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L)}
\mathbb E_{P_O^{\otimes n}}|\widehat\tau_n(x_0)-\tau_P(x_0)|
\le C\{h_n^\gamma+\ell_n^S+(nh_n^d)^{-1/2}+\sqrt{K_n}/(nh_n^d)\}
\le C\rho_n
\]
for all \(n\ge2\), with \(C\) depending only on \((d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L)\).  At \(S=S_\star\), the last quantity is \(Cn^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}\) and contains no logarithmic factor.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix the known point \(x_0\in[0,1]^d\), put \(D=[0,1]^d\), \(p=3/8\), \(h=h_n\), \(\bar h=h/2\), \(M=M_n\), and \(K=K_n\), and use the signs and coordinates from \(\mathrm{def:corrected\mbox{-}moments}\).  Because \(\sigma_k=1\) when \(x_{0k}\le 1/2\) and \(\sigma_k=-1\) otherwise,
\[
 \Phi_{x_0,h}(z)=x_0+\bar h\,\sigma\odot z\in D
 \quad (z\in D).                                                    \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, in the first case \(0\le \bar h z_k\le1/2\le1-x_{0k}\), and in the second \(0\le\bar h z_k\le1/2\le x_{0k}\).  Thus the local rectangle is never truncated, even when \(x_0\) lies on a face, edge, or corner.  Uniform design and the absolute Jacobian \(\bar h^d\) give, for every integrable \(u\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[\bar h^{-d}\mathbf1\{Z_i\in D\}u(Z_i)\right]
 =\int_Du(z)\,dz.                                                     \tag{2}
\]
Reflection does not change the sup norm, and composition with \(\Phi_{x_0,h}\) multiplies an order-\(r\) derivative by at most \(\bar h^r\).  Hence all H\"older estimates below are uniform in \(x_0\).

Let \(\Pi_M\) be Lebesgue orthogonal projection onto the piecewise-polynomial space generated by \(b_{M_n}\), applied coordinatewise to vectors.  Taylor's formula on each cell of side \(M^{-1}\), the H\"older assumptions, and the degree choice \(p_{\mathrm{deg}}\) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \|(I-\Pi_M)\{v_j(e\circ\Phi_{x_0,h}-p)\}\|_2&\le C \ell^\alpha,\\
 \|(I-\Pi_M)\{v_j(e\circ\Phi_{x_0,h})\}\|_2&\le C \ell^\alpha,\\
 \|(I-\Pi_M)\{\mu_0\circ\Phi_{x_0,h}-1/2\}\|_2&\le C \ell^\beta. \tag{3}
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(e=e_P\) and \(\mu_0=\mu_{0,P}\).  The compositions with \(\Phi_{x_0,h}\) have H\"older moduli bounded by \(C\bar h^\alpha\) and \(C\bar h^\beta\), and multiplication by a fixed-degree component of \(v\) preserves these bounds up to a fixed constant.  The cellwise errors are consequently \(C(\bar h/M)^\alpha\) and \(C(\bar h/M)^\beta\).  Constants of two are absorbed in (3), where \(\ell=h/M\).

Set \(e_h=e\circ\Phi_{x_0,h}\), \(\tau_h=\tau_P\circ\Phi_{x_0,h}\), and
\[
 Q=\int_D vv^{\mathsf T}e_h(1-e_h),\qquad
 R=\int_Dv e_h(1-e_h)\tau_h .                                       \tag{4}
\]
The propensity assumptions give \(1/4\le e_h\le1/2\).  Orthonormality of \(v\) therefore gives
\[
 u^{\mathsf T}Qu\ge\frac3{16}\int_D(u^{\mathsf T}v)^2
 =\lambda_0\|u\|_2^2,\qquad \lambda_0=3/16.                         \tag{5}
\]
Let \(\theta=Q^{-1}R\).  Equation (5), boundedness of the fixed vector \(v\), \(0\le e_h(1-e_h)\le1/4\), and \(|\tau_h|\le3/4\) imply \(\|\theta\|_2\le C\).  Taylor's formula of order \(\lceil\gamma\rceil-1\), followed by the normal equations in (4), yields
\[
 \sup_{z\in D}|v(z)^{\mathsf T}\theta-\tau_h(z)|\le C h^\gamma,
 \qquad |v(0)^{\mathsf T}\theta-\tau_P(x_0)|\le Ch^\gamma.          \tag{6}
\]
This step uses \(\Phi_{x_0,h}(0)=x_0\), and is therefore valid without assigning a conditional mean on a null event.

We next calculate the correction bias.  Conditional Bernoulli algebra gives
\[
 \mathbb E[A(A-p)\mid X]=e(1-p),                                    \tag{7}
\]
\[
 \mathbb E[(Y-1/2)(A-p)\mid X]
 =(e-p)(\mu_0-1/2)+e(1-p)\tau_P,                                   \tag{8}
\]
and \(\mathbb E[A-p\mid X]=e-p\), \(\mathbb E[A\mid X]=e\), and
\(\mathbb E[Y-1/2\mid X]=\mu_0-1/2+e\tau_P\).  Applying (2) to the linear term and independence to each ordered pair in the quadratic term shows, with bars denoting expectations, that
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \bar Q&=\int_Dvv^{\mathsf T}e_h(1-p)
 -\int_D\Pi_M\{v(e_h-p)\}e_hv^{\mathsf T},\\
 \bar R&=\int_Dv\{(e_h-p)(\mu_{0,h}-1/2)+e_h(1-p)\tau_h\}\\
 &\hspace{23mm}-\int_D\Pi_M\{v(e_h-p)\}\{\mu_{0,h}-1/2+e_h\tau_h\}. \tag{9}
\end{aligned}
\]
Projection orthogonality, (3), and Cauchy--Schwarz imply
\[
 \|\bar Q-Q\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le C\ell^{2\alpha}.                    \tag{10}
\]
Writing \(\Delta=\tau_h-v^{\mathsf T}\theta\), subtracting \(\bar Q\theta\) from \(\bar R\), and using \(Q\theta=R\) gives the exact residual identity
\[
 \bar R-\bar Q\theta
 =\int_D(I-\Pi_M)\{v(e_h-p)\}
          (I-\Pi_M)\{\mu_{0,h}-1/2+e_h\Delta\}.                    \tag{11}
\]
Equations (3), (6), projection contraction, and \(S=\alpha+\beta\) now yield
\[
 \|\bar R-\bar Q\theta\|_2\le C\{\ell^S+\ell^\alpha h^\gamma\}
 \le C(\ell^S+h^\gamma).                                           \tag{12}
\]

For completeness, the stochastic scale follows directly from the Hoeffding decomposition of the displayed ordered-pair statistics.  The linear projections are supported on a set of probability \(\bar h^d\), the weights are \(\bar h^{-d}\), and \(q\) is fixed.  Their coordinate variances are therefore at most \(C\bar h^{-d}\).  For the degenerate projections, the identity \(\int_D b_{M_n}b_{M_n}^{\mathsf T}=I_K\) gives the sum of coordinatewise second moments \(C K\bar h^{-2d}\).  Jensen's inequality and the finite-dimensional comparison between Euclidean and operator norms consequently give
\[
 \mathbb E\|\widetilde Q_n-\bar Q\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 +\mathbb E\|\widetilde R_n-\bar R\|_2
 \le C\left\{(nh^d)^{-1/2}+\frac{\sqrt K}{nh^d}\right\}.          \tag{13}
\]
All cell variables are bounded because \(A,Y\) are binary and the fixed-degree bases have finite sup norm; thus no unlisted moment or tail assumption is used in (13).

For sufficiently large \(n\), (10) is at most \(\lambda_0/4\).  On the event \(\|\widetilde Q_n-\bar Q\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\lambda_0/4\), (5) implies \(s_{\min}(\widetilde Q_n)\ge\lambda_0/2\), so the nonsingular branch is taken and \(\|\widetilde Q_n^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le2/\lambda_0\).  On its complement, Markov's inequality and (13), together with clipping and \(|\tau_P(x_0)|\le3/4\), bound the risk by the right side of (13).  On the good event, (6), (12), (13), and
\[
 \widetilde Q_n^{-1}\widetilde R_n-\theta
 =\widetilde Q_n^{-1}(\widetilde R_n-\widetilde Q_n\theta)           \tag{14}
\]
give the same bound.  The finitely many smaller \(n\) are absorbed by increasing a constant depending only on the fixed parameters.  Since clipping onto \([-3/4,3/4]\) cannot increase distance from \(\tau_P(x_0)\), we obtain, uniformly in \(P\) and \(x_0\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat\tau_n(x_0)-\tau_P(x_0)|
 \le C\left\{h^\gamma+\ell^S+(nh^d)^{-1/2}+\frac{\sqrt K}{nh^d}\right\}. \tag{15}
\]

It remains to evaluate the deterministic tuning in the closed regime.  Because \(S\le S_\star=d\gamma/(2\gamma+d)<\gamma\), \(M\le2n^{\eta(1/S-1/\gamma)}\), while its defining ceiling also gives the reverse inequality without the factor two.  Hence
\[
 h^\gamma=n^{-\eta},\qquad
 \ell^S\le n^{-\eta},                                               \tag{16}
\]
and \(S\le S_\star\) is algebraically equivalent to
\[
 \frac12-\frac{\eta d}{2\gamma}\ge\eta,
 \quad\text{so}\quad (nh^d)^{-1/2}\le n^{-\eta}.                  \tag{17}
\]
Finally, using \(K\le C M^d\) and the definition of \(\eta\),
\[
 \frac{\sqrt K}{nh^d}
 \le Cn^{-1+\eta d/(2\gamma)+\eta d/(2S)}
 =Cn^{-\eta}.                                                       \tag{18}
\]
Equations (15)--(18) prove the claim.  At \(S=S_\star\), \(\eta=\gamma/(2\gamma+d)\), and every equality above is between pure powers; in particular, no logarithmic factor is introduced.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:fixed-n-component-converse-resolution]\label{thm:fixed-n-component-converse-resolution}
For every fixed known \(x_0\in[0,1]^d\), every \(n\ge2\), and every fixed parameter tuple satisfying \(S\le S_\star\), the construction \(\mathfrak H_{n,x_0}^{\mathrm{comp}}\) supplies deterministic scales and amplitudes \(0<\ell\le h\le1\) and \(a,b>0\), constants \(0<c\le C<\infty\) depending only on \((d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L)\), and two finite priors \(\Pi_+\) and \(\Pi_-\) supported on \(\mathcal P(d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L)\) with the following properties.  The target \(\tau_P(x_0)\) is constant under each prior, with values \(\theta_+\) and \(\theta_-\) satisfying
\[
 |\theta_+-\theta_-|\ge c\rho_n.
\]
Writing
\[
 \mathsf M_\varepsilon=\int P_O^{\otimes n}\,d\Pi_\varepsilon(P),
 \qquad \varepsilon\in\{+,-\},
\]
for the actual fixed-\(n\) product mixtures, one has
\[
 H^2(\mathsf M_+,\mathsf M_-)\le
 Cn^2h^d\ell^da^2b^2\le\frac14.
\]
Conditional on the covariates, the shared-sign graph factorization has exactly equal singleton mixtures, \(Q_{1,+}=Q_{1,-}\), and every component of size \(r\ge2\) satisfies the uniform inequality
\[
 H^2(Q_{r,+},Q_{r,-})\le Ca^2b^2r^4(9/2)^r.
\]
Every law in both prior supports has the exact uniform covariate law on \([0,1]^d\).  Thus the certificate deletes no support and uses neither conditioning on a favorable occupancy event nor Poissonization.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(x_0\in[0,1]^d\), \(n\ge2\), and the parameter tuple.  All constants in this proof depend only on \((d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L)\), never on \(n\) or \(x_0\).  Write \(D=[0,1]^d\) and \(p=3/8\).

Choose a nonnegative \(C^\infty\) function \(k:\mathbb R^d\to[0,1]\), supported on \([-1,1]^d\), with \(k(0)=1\).  Such a function is obtained by normalizing the coordinatewise product of
\[
 u(t)=\exp\{-1/(1-t^2)\}\mathbf 1\{|t|<1\}.
\]
For \(0<h\le1\), set
\[
 k_{x_0,h}(x)=k\!\left(\frac{x-x_0}{h}\right),
 \qquad K_{x_0,h}(x)=k_{x_0,h}(x)^2.                                      \tag{1}
\]
The restriction of these functions to \(D\) is smooth, \(K_{x_0,h}(x_0)=1\), and
\[
 \operatorname{Leb}\{x\in D:k_{x_0,h}(x)\ne0\}\le(2h)^d.                \tag{2}
\]
This remains true at every boundary point because intersecting the ambient support cube with \(D\) can only reduce its volume.

We next construct a smooth squared partition at scale \(0<\ell\le1\).  Let
\[
 J_\ell=\{j\in\mathbb Z^d:\operatorname{dist}_\infty(\ell j,D)\le\ell\}
\]
and, on a neighborhood of \(D\), define
\[
 \phi_j(x)=\prod_{k=1}^d
 \exp\!\left\{-\frac{1}{1-(x_k/\ell-j_k)^2}\right\}
 \mathbf 1\{|x_k/\ell-j_k|<1\},
 \quad
 A_\ell(x)=\sum_{j\in J_\ell}\phi_j(x)^2,
 \quad
 \psi_j(x)=\frac{\phi_j(x)}{A_\ell(x)^{1/2}}.                    \tag{3}
\]
The set \(J_\ell\) is finite.  For each \(x\in D\), coordinatewise nearest-integer rounding produces \(j\in J_\ell\) with \(\lVert x/\ell-j\rVert_\infty\le1/2\).  Hence the corresponding term in \(A_\ell(x)\) is bounded below by a positive constant depending only on \(d\).  The same remains true on a fixed relative neighborhood of \(D\).  Differentiating (3), using this lower bound, and rescaling gives, for every multi-index \(\nu\),
\[
 \sum_{j\in J_\ell}\psi_j(x)^2=1,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{supp}(\psi_j)\subseteq
 \{x:\lVert x-\ell j\rVert_\infty\le\ell\},
 \qquad
 \lVert D^\nu\psi_j\rVert_\infty\le C_\nu\ell^{-|\nu|}.       \tag{4}
\]
At most \(3^d\) functions in (4) are nonzero at any fixed point.

Let \((\vartheta_j)_{j\in J_\ell}\) be independent Rademacher variables and put
\[
 F_\vartheta(x)=k_{x_0,h}(x)\sum_{j\in J_\ell}\vartheta_j\psi_j(x),
 \qquad K(x)=K_{x_0,h}(x).                                      \tag{5}
\]
Independence, (4), and Cauchy--Schwarz imply
\[
 \mathbb E_\vartheta F_\vartheta(x)=0,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_\vartheta F_\vartheta(x)^2=K(x),
 \qquad
 |F_\vartheta(x)|\le B_d:=3^{d/2}.                              \tag{6}
\]
The product rule and (4) show that, whenever \(0<\ell\le h\le1\),
\[
 \lVert D^\nu F_\vartheta\rVert_\infty\le C_\nu\ell^{-|\nu|},
 \qquad
 \lVert D^\nu K\rVert_\infty\le C_\nu h^{-|\nu|}.            \tag{7}
\]
These derivative bounds also give the precise H\"older estimates required by the definition of \(\mathcal H_d^s(L)\).  Indeed, let \(m=\lceil s\rceil-1\), let \(|\nu|=m\), and let \(r\) denote either \(\ell\) for \(F_\vartheta\) or \(h\) for \(K\).  The order-\(m\) bound in (7) and the mean-value bound obtained from the order-\(m+1\) derivative yield
\[
 |D^\nu f(x)-D^\nu f(y)|
 \le C\min\{r^{-m},r^{-m-1}\lVert x-y\rVert_\infty\}
 \le Cr^{-s}\lVert x-y\rVert_\infty^{s-m}.                    \tag{8}
\]
The last inequality follows separately for \(\lVert x-y\rVert_\infty\le r\) and for \(\lVert x-y\rVert_\infty>r\), and it also covers integral \(s\), when \(s-m=1\).  Thus, uniformly in \(\vartheta,x_0,h,\ell\),
\[
 \lVert F_\vartheta\rVert_{\mathcal H_d^s}\le C_s\ell^{-s},
 \qquad
 \lVert K\rVert_{\mathcal H_d^s}\le C_sh^{-s}.                \tag{9}
\]

By \(\mathrm{ass:below\mbox{-}elbow}\),
\[
 S\le S_\star=\frac{d\gamma}{2\gamma+d}<\gamma.               \tag{10}
\]
With \(\eta\) from \(\mathrm{def:rate}\), choose deterministic scales and amplitudes
\[
 \ell=c_\ell n^{-\eta/S},
 \qquad h=\ell^{S/\gamma},
 \qquad a=c_a\ell^\alpha,
 \qquad b=c_b\ell^\beta,                                      \tag{11}
\]
where \(0<c_\ell\le1\) and \(c_a,c_b>0\) will be fixed below.  Equation (10) gives \(0<\ell\le h\le1\).  Since \(S=\alpha+\beta\), (11) implies
\[
 a\ell^{-\alpha}=c_a,
 \qquad b\ell^{-\beta}=c_b,
 \qquad ab h^{-\gamma}=c_ac_b,
 \qquad ab h^{-\beta}
 =c_ac_b\ell^{S(1-\beta/\gamma)}\le c_ac_b.                    \tag{12}
\]

For \(\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}\), define
\[
\begin{aligned}
 e_\vartheta(x)&=p+aF_\vartheta(x),\\
 \mu_{0,\varepsilon,\vartheta}(x)
 &=\frac12+\varepsilon bF_\vartheta(x)
   +\frac{\varepsilon ab}{1-p}K(x),\\
 \tau_\varepsilon(x)&=-\frac{\varepsilon ab}{p(1-p)}K(x),\\
 \mu_{1,\varepsilon,\vartheta}(x)
 &=\frac12+\varepsilon bF_\vartheta(x)
   -\frac{\varepsilon ab}{p}K(x).
                                                                    \tag{13}
\end{aligned}
\]
Choose \(c_a,c_b\) sufficiently small.  Equations (6), (9), and (12), together with the positive constants \(p=3/8\), \(1-p=5/8\), and \(L\ge4\), then give
\[
 4e_\vartheta\in\mathcal H_d^\alpha(L),
 \quad 1\le4e_\vartheta\le2,
 \quad \mu_{0,\varepsilon,\vartheta}\in\mathcal H_d^\beta(L),
 \quad \tau_\varepsilon\in\mathcal H_d^\gamma(L),
 \quad \frac18\le\mu_{a,\varepsilon,\vartheta}\le\frac78.   \tag{14}
\]
For clarity, the two nonconstant terms in \(\mu_0\) have order-\(\beta\) H\"older bounds controlled by \(b\ell^{-\beta}=c_b\) and \(ab h^{-\beta}\le c_ac_b\); the same calculation applies to \(\mu_1\).  The order-\(\gamma\) bound for \(\tau_\varepsilon\) is controlled by \(ab h^{-\gamma}=c_ac_b\).  The value envelopes in (14) follow from (6), \(0\le K\le1\), and a further fixed decrease of \(c_a,c_b\).  Thus (14) verifies exactly \(\mathrm{ass:propensity\mbox{-}normalization}\), \(\mathrm{ass:propensity\mbox{-}envelope}\), \(\mathrm{ass:propensity\mbox{-}holder}\), \(\mathrm{ass:control\mbox{-}holder}\), \(\mathrm{ass:contrast\mbox{-}holder}\), and \(\mathrm{ass:response\mbox{-}interiority}\); no smoothness condition on a marginal outcome mean has been introduced.

For each \((\varepsilon,\vartheta)\), draw \(X\) exactly uniformly on \(D\), as required by \(\mathrm{ass:uniform\mbox{-}design}\), and draw \(A\mid X=x\) as \(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(e_\vartheta(x))\).  Conditional on \(X=x\), draw \(Y(0)\) and \(Y(1)\) independently as Bernoulli variables with respective means \(\mu_{0,\varepsilon,\vartheta}(x)\) and \(\mu_{1,\varepsilon,\vartheta}(x)\), independently of \(A\), and set \(Y=AY(1)+(1-A)Y(0)\).  This verifies \(\mathrm{ass:bernoulli\mbox{-}response}\), \(\mathrm{ass:consistency}\), and \(\mathrm{ass:conditional\mbox{-}exchangeability}\).  Hence (14) and \(\mathrm{def:exact\mbox{-}model}\) place every resulting law in \(\mathcal P(d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L)\).

Let \(\Pi_\varepsilon\) be the uniform prior on the finite set of these laws indexed by \(\vartheta\).  Because \(K(x_0)=1\), the target is constant on each prior support and
\[
 |\theta_+-\theta_-|
 =|\tau_+(x_0)-\tau_-(x_0)|
 =\frac{2ab}{p(1-p)}.                                           \tag{15}
\]

It remains to prove affinity in the genuine fixed-\(n\) experiment.  For fixed \(x\) and \(\vartheta\), replace \((a,\varepsilon b)\) in (13) by real variables \((s,t)\).  Thus
\[
 e_s=p+sF,
 \qquad \mu_{0,s,t}=\frac12+tF+\frac{st}{1-p}K,
 \qquad \mu_{1,s,t}=\frac12+tF-\frac{st}{p}K,                  \tag{16}
\]
where \(F=F_\vartheta(x)\).  Direct multiplication of the Bernoulli cell probabilities for \((A,Y)=(1,1),(1,0),(0,1),(0,0)\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 q_{11}&=p/2+sF/2+ptF+st(F^2-K)-s^2tKF/p,\\
 q_{10}&=p/2+sF/2-ptF-st(F^2-K)+s^2tKF/p,\\
 q_{01}&=(1-p)/2-sF/2+(1-p)tF-st(F^2-K)-s^2tKF/(1-p),\\
 q_{00}&=(1-p)/2-sF/2-(1-p)tF+st(F^2-K)+s^2tKF/(1-p).
                                                                    \tag{17}
\end{aligned}
\]
Taking Rademacher expectation in (17) and using (6) cancels every term: \(\mathbb E F=0\), \(\mathbb E(F^2-K)=0\), and \(\mathbb E(KF)=0\).  Therefore the one-observation sign mixture is, for both signs of \(t\),
\[
 q_0=(p/2,p/2,(1-p)/2,(1-p)/2).                                \tag{18}
\]
This proves the exact singleton identity and, in particular, rules out a deterministic macro-scale leakage term.

The fixed choice of sufficiently small \(c_a,c_b\), (6), and (17) ensure uniformly for \(|s|\le a\), \(|t|\le b\), and every cell \(z\) that
\[
 \frac12\le L_z(s,t;x,\vartheta):=\frac{q_z(s,t;x,\vartheta)}{q_{0,z}}
 \le\frac32,                                                     \tag{19}
\]
and that the first \(s\)- and \(t\)-derivatives and the mixed derivative of \(L_z\) are bounded by a fixed constant.  These assertions follow term by term from the polynomial formula (17), \(|F|\le B_d\), \(0\le K\le1\), and \(\min_zq_{0,z}=p/2>0\).

For fixed locations \(x_1,\ldots,x_r\) and cells \(z_1,\ldots,z_r\), let
\[
 M_r(s,t;z_{1:r})
 =\mathbb E_\vartheta\prod_{i=1}^r
 L_{z_i}(s,t;x_i,\vartheta).                                    \tag{20}
\]
The product rule expresses \(\partial_s\partial_t\) of the product in (20) as \(r\) terms having both derivatives on one factor and \(r(r-1)\) terms having the derivatives on distinct factors.  Equation (19) therefore gives
\[
 |\partial_s\partial_tM_r(s,t;z_{1:r})|
 \le Cr^2(3/2)^r.                                                \tag{21}
\]
At \(s=0\), changing every sign \(\vartheta_j\) to \(-\vartheta_j\) changes \(F\) to \(-F\) and shows \(M_r(0,b)=M_r(0,-b)\).  Two applications of the fundamental theorem of calculus, justified by the polynomial formula (17), now yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |M_r(a,b)-M_r(a,-b)|
 &\le\int_0^a\int_{-b}^b
 |\partial_s\partial_tM_r(u,t)|\,dt\,du\\
 &\le Cabr^2(3/2)^r.                                             \tag{22}
\end{aligned}
\]
Also (19) implies \(M_r(a,\pm b)\ge2^{-r}\).  The conditional component-mixture densities relative to \(q_0^{\otimes r}\) are precisely \(M_r(a,b)\) and \(M_r(a,-b)\).  Consequently, using
\[
 (\sqrt u-\sqrt v)^2=\frac{(u-v)^2}{(\sqrt u+\sqrt v)^2}
\]
together with (22) and the lower bound \((\sqrt u+\sqrt v)^2\ge 2^{2-r}\), we obtain, for every \(r\ge2\),
\[
 H^2(Q_{r,+},Q_{r,-})
 \le Ca^2b^2r^4(9/2)^r.                                        \tag{23}
\]
For \(r=1\), equation (18) gives \(Q_{1,+}=Q_{1,-}\) exactly.

Condition now on \(X_{1:n}\), which is legitimate under \(\mathrm{ass:iid\mbox{-}sampling}\).  Observations outside the support in (2) have \(F=K=0\) and hence have the common baseline conditional law.  Among the remaining observations, join \(i\) and \(j\) when \(\lVert X_i-X_j\rVert_\infty\le2\ell\).  If a frame coefficient \(\vartheta_j\) is active at both locations, the support assertion in (4) forces those locations to be adjacent.  Distinct graph components therefore involve disjoint collections of independent Rademacher coefficients.  It follows that, for each fixed \(\varepsilon\), the conditional mixture factors over graph components.

Let \(N_r\) be the number of components of size \(r\).  Multiplicativity of Hellinger affinity and \(1-\prod_j u_j\le\sum_j(1-u_j)\) for \(0\le u_j\le1\) imply from (23) that the conditional squared Hellinger distance is at most
\[
 Ca^2b^2\sum_{r=2}^n N_r r^4(9/2)^r.                            \tag{24}
\]
Every connected labeled \(r\)-set contains a spanning tree.  The standard leaf-deletion map sends a labeled tree bijectively to its Pr\"ufer sequence of length \(r-2\): repeatedly delete the least labeled leaf and record its neighbor; conversely, repeatedly attach the least label absent from the remaining sequence to the first sequence entry, then join the final two labels.  Hence there are exactly \(r^{r-2}\) labeled trees.  For any fixed tree, integrate first its root, which by (2) lies in a set of volume at most \((2h)^d\), and then integrate children in tree order.  Each child must lie in an \(L^\infty\)-ball of volume at most \((4\ell)^d\) around its parent.  Exact uniformity and independence from \(\mathrm{ass:uniform\mbox{-}design}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:iid\mbox{-}sampling}\) therefore give
\[
 \mathbb E N_r
 \le {n\choose r}r^{r-2}(2h)^d(4\ell)^{d(r-1)}.                 \tag{25}
\]
Using \({n\choose r}\le n^r/r!\), \(r!\ge(r/e)^r\), and (24)--(25), we obtain
\[
 \mathbb E_XH^2\{\mathsf M_+(\cdot\mid X_{1:n}),
                         \mathsf M_-(\cdot\mid X_{1:n})\}
 \le Ca^2b^2h^dn
 \sum_{r=2}^n r^2(C_0n\ell^d)^{r-1}.                            \tag{26}
\]
If \(C_0n\ell^d\le1/2\), the polynomial-geometric series in (26) is bounded by \(C n\ell^d\), and hence
\[
 \mathbb E_XH^2\{\mathsf M_+(\cdot\mid X_{1:n}),
                         \mathsf M_-(\cdot\mid X_{1:n})\}
 \le Cn^2h^d\ell^da^2b^2.                                     \tag{27}
\]
Both mixtures have the same exact-uniform \(X_{1:n}\)-marginal.  Integrating the conditional Hellinger integrand against that common marginal proves that the left side of (27) is exactly \(H^2(\mathsf M_+,\mathsf M_-)\) for the fixed-product mixtures \(\mathsf M_\varepsilon=\int P_O^{\otimes n}\,d\Pi_\varepsilon(P)\).  Thus no conditioning event and no Poissonization has changed the experiment.

Finally we verify both tuning inequalities.  Positivity of \(d,S,\gamma\) permits the equivalence
\[
 1-\frac{d\eta}{S}\le0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 S\le\frac{d\gamma}{2\gamma+d}=S_\star.                       \tag{28}
\]
Indeed, substituting \(\eta^{-1}=1+d/(2\gamma)+d/(2S)\) and multiplying by positive denominators reduces both sides to \(2S\gamma+Sd\le d\gamma\).  Thus (11) and \(\mathrm{ass:below\mbox{-}elbow}\) imply \(n\ell^d\le c_\ell^d\), including equality of the exponent at the elbow.  Choosing \(c_\ell\) small makes the series condition in (26) hold without a logarithm.  Moreover,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 n^2h^d\ell^da^2b^2
 &=c_a^2c_b^2n^2\ell^{2S+d+Sd/\gamma},\\
 \eta\left(2+\frac dS+\frac d\gamma\right)&=2,
                                                                    \tag{29}
\end{aligned}
\]
where the second identity is the definition of \(\eta\).  Hence the first line of (29) is a constant multiple of \(c_a^2c_b^2c_\ell^{2S+d+Sd/\gamma}\).  Decreasing \(c_\ell>0\) once more makes the right side of (27) at most \(1/4\).  From (11) and (15),
\[
 |\theta_+-\theta_-|
 =\frac{2c_ac_bc_\ell^S}{p(1-p)}n^{-\eta}
 =c\rho_n.                                                       \tag{30}
\]
Equations (14), (18), (23), (27), and (30) prove respectively same-class support, singleton cancellation, component affinity, fixed-product affinity, and target separation.  The constants and all preceding bounds are uniform in \(x_0\), completing the construction \(\mathfrak H_{n,x_0}^{\mathrm{comp}}\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This theorem records the affirmative all-\(x_0\) answer to the fixed-\(n\) component-mixture question as a direct consequence of the established component certificate. \textit{Gap.} One-observation mixture cancellation alone does not control a shared-prior product likelihood; the invoked component theorem supplies the exact singleton cancellation, component affinity estimate, and full graph sum in the stipulated experiment. \textit{Consumer.} The certificate is the fixed-product lower-bound input used by the sharp exact-uniform pointwise CATE minimax theorem.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The sparse-component summation uses \(n\ell^d\le C\) and therefore certifies only the at-or-below-elbow regime; above the elbow the present prior is not a converse certificate. The finite estimator also uses the known smoothness tuple in its deterministic bandwidth and projection dimension. Constants are not optimized, and the theorem concerns mean absolute pointwise risk rather than a confidence procedure.

\section{Honest scope}

The proved contribution is the same-class full-support fixed-\(n\) lower bound, paired with a total finite estimator, for every fixed known \(x_0\in[0,1]^d\) and \(S\le S_\star\). It includes boundary points through a reflected inward chart and proves the exact no-log oracle power at \(S=S_\star\). The exact-uniform class is a strict subclass of the published general-design class: only the lower bound transfers upward by subclass inclusion at matching point targets. The note does not claim its upper bound for unknown or nonuniform design, and it does not treat \(\kappa>0\) local overlap decay, adaptive tuning, confidence intervals, integrated loss, or performance guarantees for existing causal forests.
