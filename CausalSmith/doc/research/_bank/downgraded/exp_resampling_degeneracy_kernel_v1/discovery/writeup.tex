\section*{exp\_\allowbreak{}resampling\_\allowbreak{}degeneracy\_\allowbreak{}kernel}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} A finite conditional randomization law need not resample from a partition cell. Universal validity of an arbitrary legal row kernel is characterized exactly by the inequalities \(d_K(A)\geq\pi(A)\), with an equivalent peeling core, an \(O(m2^m)\) separator, and a finite union-of-polyhedra design region. A generic asymmetric-isolate support criterion constructs a valid nonpartition kernel with power \(\pi(x)=\alpha\) against zero for every legal partition kernel; every one-way three-cycle support is a corollary, and the original cyclic matrix remains an exact \(2/3\)-power stress test. On a predeclared network-exposure fiber, finite weak-order enumeration and positive-slack linear feasibility compute each worst-case bounded-null value. Projecting the finitely many cells onto the threshold line evaluates \(b_k(x_0;\alpha)\) from interval endpoints and gives \(\ell_k(e;\alpha)=b_k(X;\alpha)\) simultaneously for all effect quantiles. This exact network reduction does not reproduce the adjusted-outcome computations of Caughey--Dafoe--Li--Miratrix or Su--Li; a two-unit example gives frozen and comparator values \(1\) and \(1/2\), respectively.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Conditional randomization under interference has valid imputability-and-conditioning constructions, but those constructions do not characterize score-uniform validity for arbitrary overlapping deterministic rows. Conventional minimax permutation-power theory and design-based exposure-mapping estimators address different targets. No-interference quantile methods provide important inversion templates, but their adjusted-outcome reductions neither enforce one shared potential-outcome schedule across network assignments nor equal the frozen assignment-specific score.

\paragraph{Niche.} Finite directed legal supports permit overlapping row kernels whose validity and power can be decided before outcomes are observed, while finite weak-order cells preserve the shared potential-outcome constraints needed for exact network effect-quantile inference.

\paragraph{Fill.} The project gives exact row-kernel validity geometry, a generic asymmetric-isolate support certificate with a strict partition power gap, the uniform three-cycle as a finite stress-test corollary, certified catalogue-power design, and a terminating exact weak-order and linear-feasibility reduction for bounded-null schedules. Projected cell intervals and their endpoints explicitly evaluate the simultaneous within-exposure quantile bounds. The project also gives an obstruction to claiming exact recovery of the Caughey or Su--Li computational statistics.

\section{Related work}

Basse, Feller, and Toulis (2019) construct valid conditional randomization tests under interference through imputability and conditioning mechanisms. They do not give a necessary-and-sufficient score-uniform characterization of arbitrary overlapping deterministic rows or the present bounded-null effect-quantile schedule optimization. Zhang and Zhao (2023) formulate conditional randomization through assignment-space partitions and prove validity by a conditional probability-integral transform; their sufficient partition construction is distinct from the necessary-and-sufficient row-kernel geometry here. Ramdas et al. (2023) validate arbitrary nonuniform permutation laws after a random reference permutation restores exchangeability, rather than characterize an unmodified deterministic row kernel. Kim, Balakrishnan, and Wasserman (2022) study minimax power of conventional permutation tests for specified exchangeable testing problems, not validity geometry for state-dependent legal row kernels or network causal schedules. Aronow and Samii (2017) define exposure mappings and design-based estimators for average causal effects under interference, not an exact randomization-p-value row-kernel equivalence, a catalogue-power optimizer, or individual-effect-quantile inversion. Puelz et al. (2022) encode imputability through null-exposure graphs and bicliques, which supply legal network fibers, while Yanagi and Sei (2022) use an approximate model-based power expression to refine biclique selection. Caughey et al. (2023, Theorem 4 and Section 5.5) provide individual and simultaneous no-interference effect-quantile inference under exchangeable treatment, and Su and Li (2024) provide stratified quantile tests and a multiple-choice-knapsack computation. Their order-statistic null equivalence and inversion logic inform the present construction, but the frozen score evaluates assignment-specific imputed outcomes rather than a fixed adjusted-outcome vector; the explicit two-unit witness therefore rules out exact computational recovery of either method. Chen and Li's 2026 finite-sample improvements likewise concern no-interference complete, stratified, sampling-based, or matched designs. Owusu (2023) studies network heterogeneous-effect randomization tests with adaptive focal assignments; the Cai, de Janvry, and Sadoulet weather-insurance reanalysis is the intended prospective empirical consumer, not evidence for the theorem.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:positive-finite-design]\label{ass:positive-finite-design}
The conditional design law satisfies \(\pi(x)>0\) for every \(x\in\Omega\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Finite-support design positivity, \cite{ZhangZhao2023Randomization}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:row-stochastic]\label{ass:row-stochastic}
For every \(x\in\Omega\), \(\sum_{y\in\Omega}K(x,y)=1\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Markov-kernel normalization, \cite{RamdasBarberCandesTibshirani2023Arbitrary}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:legal-support]\label{ass:legal-support}
For every \(x,y\in\Omega\) with \((x,y)\notin L\), \(K(x,y)=0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Imputability support restriction, \cite{PuelzBasseFellerToulis2022Graph}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:outcome-free-kernel]\label{ass:outcome-free-kernel}
The pair \((L,K)\) is measurable with respect to design-stage information fixed before observing \(Y^{\mathrm{obs}}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Predeclared conditional randomization mechanism, \cite{ZhangZhao2023Randomization}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:prespecified-catalogue]\label{ass:prespecified-catalogue}
The pair \((s^{1:q},w)\) is a nonrandom design-stage input.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Design-stage power catalogue, \cite{YanagiSei2022Improving}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:fixed-potential-outcomes]\label{ass:fixed-potential-outcomes}
The schedule \(Y\) is fixed, and the only randomness is \(X\sim\pi\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Fisherian finite-population randomization, \cite{CaugheyDafoeLiMiratrix2023Bounded}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:fixed-peer-exposure]\label{ass:fixed-peer-exposure}
Within \(\Omega\), the peer exposure of every \(u\in U\) equals \(e\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Exposure-mapping restriction, \cite{AronowSamii2017GeneralInterference}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:own-treatment-variation]\label{ass:own-treatment-variation}
For every \(u\in U\), own treatment takes both values on at least one legal comparison in \(\Omega\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Exposure positivity on a conditioning fiber, \cite{PuelzBasseFellerToulis2022Graph}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:statistic-imputability]\label{ass:statistic-imputability}
Every outcome entering \(T(x,Y_{\theta}(x))\) is imputable for each legal comparison and each candidate sharp schedule.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Biclique-style conditional imputability, \cite{PuelzBasseFellerToulis2022Graph}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:fixed-rank-statistic]\label{ass:fixed-rank-statistic}
The map \(T\) is a deterministic rank statistic with a fixed weak-order tie rule on every assignment in \(\Omega\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Fixed rank-score randomization statistic, \cite{SuLi2024TreatmentEffectQuantiles}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{definition}[def:quantile-lower-bound]\label{def:quantile-lower-bound}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Projected quantile lower bound and finite-\allowbreak{}cell evaluator}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For \(1\leq k\leq N\), define
\[
\ell_k(e;\alpha)
=\inf_{\theta\in\mathcal C_{\alpha}}\theta_{(k)}(e),
\qquad
\inf\varnothing=+\infty.
\tag{1}
\]
The following clause evaluates (1).  For an observed \(x_0\in\Omega\), set
\(r=N-k\).  For each \(c\in\mathbb R\), enumerate binary labels
\(h\in\{0,1\}^{U}\) with \(\sum_{u\in U}h_u\leq r\) and ternary
assignment-wide weak-order labels
\[
\lambda_{xuv}\in\{-1,0,1\},
\qquad x\in\Omega,\quad 1\leq u<v\leq N.
\]
Writing \(z_{ua}=\theta_u(a,e)\), impose
\[
z_{u,Z_u(x_0)}=Y_u^{\mathrm{obs}},
\qquad
h_u=1\Longrightarrow z_{u1}-z_{u0}>c,
\qquad
h_u=0\Longrightarrow z_{u1}-z_{u0}\leq c,
\]
and let \(\lambda_{xuv}\) record the sign of
\(z_{u,Z_u(x)}-z_{v,Z_v(x)}\).  These are the observation, effect, and
weak-order constraints for the label.
After writing its strict rows as \(a_j^{\mathsf T}z<b_j\), its weak rows as
\(Cz\leq d\), and its equalities as \(Ez=f\), test a cell containing strict
rows with
\[
\max_{z,\varepsilon}\ \varepsilon
\quad\text{subject to}\quad
a_j^{\mathsf T}z\leq b_j-\varepsilon\ (j),
\quad Cz\leq d,
\quad Ez=f,
\quad 0\leq\varepsilon\leq1;
\tag{2}
\]
the cell is feasible exactly when the optimum in (2) is positive.  If there is
no strict row, use ordinary linear feasibility.  The fixed tie rule assigns to
each feasible \(\lambda\) the constants
\(t_{\lambda}(x)=T(x,Y_{\theta}(x))\), and
\[
p^{\mathrm{wc}}_{r,c}(x_0)
=\max_{(h,\lambda):\,\mathrm{feasible}}
\sum_{y\in\Omega}K(x_0,y)
\mathbf 1\{t_{\lambda}(y)\geq t_{\lambda}(x_0)\},
\qquad \max\varnothing=0.
\tag{3}
\]
Define
\[
b_k(x_0;\alpha)
=\sup\left\{c\in\mathbb R:
p^{\mathrm{wc}}_{N-k,c}(x_0)\leq\alpha\right\},
\qquad \sup\varnothing=-\infty.
\tag{4}
\]
The projected bound remains defined by (1), not by the computation below.  The
exact network schedule theorem proves the evaluation identity
\[
\ell_k(e;\alpha)=b_k(X;\alpha).
\tag{5}
\]
Treating \(c\) as a variable, projection of every feasible weak-order cell onto
the \(c\)-line gives an interval with its endpoint inclusions recorded by (2).
For rational or real-algebraic representations of the kernel weights, observed
outcomes, level, and finite rank-score table, the finitely many projected
intervals and endpoints give a terminating exact evaluation of (3)--(5).  With
arbitrary unspecified real inputs, (2)--(5) retain only their finite ideal
exact-real interpretation.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathcal\{C\}\allowbreak{}\_\allowbreak{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}alpha\}\allowbreak{}}; \texttt{p\textasciicircum{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathrm\{wc\}\allowbreak{}\}\allowbreak{}\_\allowbreak{}\{r,\allowbreak{}c\}\allowbreak{}}; \texttt{K}; \texttt{X}; \texttt{Y\textasciicircum{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathrm\{obs\}\allowbreak{}\}\allowbreak{}}; \texttt{T}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}alpha}.
\end{definition}

\section{Main results}

\begin{theorem}[thm:universal-validity-iff]\label{thm:universal-validity-iff}
For every row-stochastic \(K\), universal validity for \(\pi\) holds if and only if \(d_K(A)\geq\pi(A)\) for every nonempty \(A\subseteq\Omega\). This is a strict tightening of partition sufficiency: it is necessary over the full row-kernel class and admits legal nonpartition kernels.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(s\in\mathbb R^{\Omega}\) and \(\alpha\in[0,1]\), and define
\[
R=\{x\in\Omega:p_K(x;s)\leq\alpha\}.
\]
If \(R=\varnothing\), then
\(\Pr_{X\sim\pi}\{p_K(X;s)\leq\alpha\}=0\leq\alpha\).  Suppose
\(R\neq\varnothing\).  Because \(R\) is finite, it has an ordering
\(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_{|R|}\) satisfying
\[
s(\sigma_1)\geq\cdots\geq s(\sigma_{|R|});
\]
ties may be broken arbitrarily.  For
\(P_j=\{\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_j\}\), this ordering gives
\[
P_j\subseteq\{y\in\Omega:s(y)\geq s(\sigma_j)\}.
\tag{1}
\]
The candidate-kernel type gives \(K(x,y)\geq0\), while
\(\texttt{ass:row-stochastic}\) gives unit row sums.  Hence the row masses are
monotone in their set argument.  By (1) and
\(\texttt{def:kernel-tail-value}\), for every \(j\),
\[
K(\sigma_j,P_j)
\leq K\!\left(\sigma_j,\{y:s(y)\geq s(\sigma_j)\}\right)
=p_K(\sigma_j;s)
\leq\alpha.
\tag{2}
\]
Taking the maximum in (2) and then applying
\(\texttt{def:subset-degeneracy}\) yields
\[
d_K(R)
\leq\max_{1\leq j\leq |R|}K(\sigma_j,P_j)
\leq\alpha.
\tag{3}
\]
If \(d_K(A)\geq\pi(A)\) for every nonempty \(A\), then (3) and the design
law \(X\sim\pi\) give
\[
\Pr_{X\sim\pi}\{p_K(X;s)\leq\alpha\}
=\pi(R)
\leq d_K(R)
\leq\alpha.
\]
Since \(s\) and \(\alpha\) were arbitrary, this is universal validity by
\(\texttt{def:universal-validity}\).

Conversely, suppose that the subset inequality fails at a nonempty
\(A\subseteq\Omega\), so
\[
d_K(A)<\pi(A).
\tag{4}
\]
The set \(\operatorname{Ord}(A)\) is finite, so the minimum in
\(\texttt{def:subset-degeneracy}\) is attained by some ordering
\(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_{|A|}\).  Choose a score with
\[
s(\sigma_1)>\cdots>s(\sigma_{|A|})
\]
and assign every point in \(\Omega\setminus A\) a score strictly below
\(s(\sigma_{|A|})\).  For this score, the upper score set at \(\sigma_j\) is
exactly \(P_j\).  Therefore
\[
p_K(\sigma_j;s)
=K(\sigma_j,P_j)
\leq\max_{1\leq i\leq |A|}K(\sigma_i,P_i)
=d_K(A),
\qquad j=1,\ldots,|A|.
\tag{5}
\]
Row stochasticity ensures \(d_K(A)\in[0,1]\), so
\(\alpha=d_K(A)\) is an admissible threshold.  Equation (5) shows that the
rejection set at this threshold contains \(A\).  Using (4),
\[
\Pr_{X\sim\pi}\{p_K(X;s)\leq d_K(A)\}
\geq\pi(A)
>d_K(A),
\]
which violates \(\texttt{def:universal-validity}\).  Thus universal validity
implies every subset inequality.  The use of \(\leq\) in (5) also proves that
there is no boundary loss when a tail value equals the rejection threshold.

To make the tightening relative to partition sufficiency explicit, let
\(\mathcal G\) be any partition of \(\Omega\), write \(G_x\) for the cell
containing \(x\), and consider the conditional row kernel
\[
K(x,B)=\frac{\pi(B\cap G_x)}{\pi(G_x)}.
\tag{6}
\]
The denominator is positive by \(\texttt{ass:positive-finite-design}\).  Fix a
nonempty \(A\) and any ordering of it.  For each cell \(G\) meeting \(A\), look
at the last element of \(A\cap G\) in that ordering.  At its prefix, the row
mass is \(\pi(A\cap G)/\pi(G)\).  Consequently the maximum prefix mass is at
least
\[
\max_{G:\,A\cap G\neq\varnothing}
\frac{\pi(A\cap G)}{\pi(G)}
\geq
\sum_{G\in\mathcal G}\pi(G)
\frac{\pi(A\cap G)}{\pi(G)}
=\pi(A),
\tag{7}
\]
where cells disjoint from \(A\) contribute zero and the middle inequality says
that a maximum dominates a weighted average.  Since the ordering was arbitrary,
\(d_K(A)\geq\pi(A)\).  Thus every partition kernel is included in the
characterized class.

It remains to verify that the inclusion is strict.  Let
\(\Omega=\{1,2,3\}\), let \(\pi\) be uniform, and, with indices interpreted
cyclically, set
\[
K(i,i)=\frac23,
\qquad
K(i,i+1)=\frac13,
\qquad
K(i,j)=0\ \text{otherwise}.
\tag{8}
\]
The matrix in (8) is nonnegative and row-stochastic.  For a singleton,
\(d_K(A)=2/3\).  Any two-point set contains exactly one directed cycle edge.
Order its tail first and its head second.  The first prefix contains only the
tail, while the head's outgoing cycle edge leaves the set, so both prefix
masses are \(2/3\).  Every ordering has first-prefix mass \(2/3\).  Hence
\(d_K(A)=2/3\) for every two-point set.  Finally,
\(d_K(\Omega)=1\), because the last prefix in every ordering is \(\Omega\) and
has row mass one.  Thus
\[
d_K(A)\geq\pi(A)
\qquad(\varnothing\neq A\subseteq\Omega),
\]
and the equivalence just proved makes (8) universally valid.  Taking the legal
relation to contain the diagonal and the three directed cycle edges makes (8)
a legal kernel.  Its three two-point row supports overlap but are neither
identical nor disjoint.  By contrast, rows obtained by conditioning \(\pi\) on
partition cells have identical supports within a cell and disjoint supports
across cells.  Therefore (8) is not a partition kernel, proving that the
characterized class admits legal nonpartition kernels.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem converts validity for every unknown score into finitely many design-only inequalities. \textit{Gap.} Zhang and Zhao (2023, Theorem 1) give partition sufficiency, and Ramdas et al. (2023, Theorem 2) use auxiliary randomization. Basse--Feller--Toulis give valid imputability-and-conditioning tests, Kim--Balakrishnan--Wasserman study minimax power for conventional exchangeable permutation problems, and Aronow--Samii develop exposure mappings and average-effect estimators. None gives the present necessary-and-sufficient score-uniform geometry for arbitrary overlapping deterministic rows or its bounded-null network effect-quantile schedule optimization. \textit{Consumer.} A network-randomization analyst can screen any outcome-free legal kernel before observing outcomes.

\begin{theorem}[thm:subset-recursion]\label{thm:subset-recursion}
For every nonempty \(A\subseteq\Omega\), \(d_K(A)=\min_{i\in A}\max\{K(i,A),d_K(A\setminus\{i\})\}\), with \(d_K(\varnothing)=0\). Computing subsets by increasing cardinality takes \(O(m2^m)\) arithmetic operations, and stored minimizers return a violating subset and ordering whenever universal validity fails.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Set \(d_K(\varnothing)=0\) as in \(\texttt{def:subset-degeneracy}\).  For nonempty
\(A\), partition the finite set \(\operatorname{Ord}(A)\) according to the last
element \(i\).  If \(\sigma_{|A|}=i\), the last prefix is \(A\), so the last
contribution to the ordering maximum is \(K(i,A)\).  The preceding entries form an
arbitrary ordering \(\rho\) of \(A\setminus\{i\}\), and their smallest achievable
maximum is \(d_K(A\setminus\{i\})\).  Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
d_K(A)
&=\min_{i\in A}\min_{\rho\in\operatorname{Ord}(A\setminus\{i\})}
  \max\left\{K(i,A),\max_{1\leq j\leq |A|-1}
  K(\rho_j,P_j(\rho))\right\}\\
&=\min_{i\in A}\max\{K(i,A),d_K(A\setminus\{i\})\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The second equality uses the elementary identity
\(\min_{\rho}\max\{a,g(\rho)\}=\max\{a,\min_{\rho}g(\rho)\}\), valid because the
minimum is over a finite set.

For the operation count, first compute every row subset sum \(K(i,A)\) by the
recursion \(K(i,A)=K(i,A\setminus\{j\})+K(i,j)\).  This takes
\(O(m2^m)\) additions.  Evaluating the displayed recursion for every subset then
requires
\[
\sum_{A\subseteq\Omega}|A|=m2^{m-1}
\]
candidate evaluations, hence \(O(m2^m)\) arithmetic operations in total.  Store,
for every nonempty \(A\), a minimizing last element \(i_A\).  Recursively following
\(A,A\setminus\{i_A\},\ldots\) and reversing the stored deletion sequence produces
an ordering attaining \(d_K(A)\).  Consequently, after comparing the computed
values with the known masses \(\pi(A)\), any subset with
\(d_K(A)<\pi(A)\), together with its recovered ordering, is an exact violated-subset
certificate.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The recursion makes the iff characterization exactly separable and produces an auditable invalidity witness. \textit{Gap.} The opened conditional-randomization papers do not provide separation for arbitrary state-dependent rows. \textit{Consumer.} The separator is the cutting-plane oracle for globally optimizing legal kernels.

\begin{theorem}[thm:peeling-core]\label{thm:peeling-core}
For every nonempty \(A\subseteq\Omega\) and \(t\in[0,1]\), the following are equivalent: \(d_K(A)\geq t\); there exists nonempty \(C\subseteq A\) with \(K(i,C)\geq t\) for all \(i\in C\); and the threshold-peeling procedure does not delete all of \(A\). In particular, universal validity is equivalent to a nonempty \(\pi(A)\)-core in every nonempty \(A\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We first prove the equivalence of the degeneracy and core conditions.  Suppose that
\(C\subseteq A\) is nonempty and satisfies \(K(i,C)\geq t\) for every \(i\in C\).
For an arbitrary ordering \(\sigma\) of \(A\), let \(\sigma_j\) be the last member
of \(C\) to occur.  Then \(C\subseteq P_j(\sigma)\), and nonnegativity of \(K\)
gives
\[
K(\sigma_j,P_j(\sigma))\geq K(\sigma_j,C)\geq t.
\]
Thus every ordering maximum is at least \(t\), so \(d_K(A)\geq t\).

For the converse, assume a peeling run deletes all points, in deletion order
\(i_1,\ldots,i_{|A|}\).  Immediately before deleting \(i_j\), the current set is
\(B_j=\{i_j,\ldots,i_{|A|}\}\) and
\(K(i_j,B_j)<t\).  The reverse ordering
\(\sigma=(i_{|A|},\ldots,i_1)\) has \(B_j\) as the prefix ending at \(i_j\).
Consequently every prefix mass in this ordering is strictly below \(t\), and
\(d_K(A)<t\).  Contrapositively, \(d_K(A)\geq t\) prevents complete deletion.

The conclusion is independent of deletion choices.  If a core \(C\) exists and
the current set \(B\) contains \(C\), then for each \(i\in C\),
\(K(i,B)\geq K(i,C)\geq t\); hence no member of \(C\) can be deleted.  Conversely,
any nonempty terminal set produced by the procedure satisfies the defining core
inequalities.  Thus the procedure leaves a nonempty set if and only if a core
exists, and the preceding two paragraphs establish all three equivalences.  Taking
\(t=\pi(A)\) and invoking \(\texttt{thm:universal-validity-iff}\) gives the final
universal-validity assertion.  Positivity in
\(\texttt{ass:positive-finite-design}\) also shows at the singleton boundary that
\(K(i,i)\geq\pi(i)>0\) is necessary.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The core formulation exposes the combinatorial obstruction and yields smaller disjunctive formulations. \textit{Gap.} Biclique constructions certify one chosen conditioning set but do not give a core certificate for all row kernels. \textit{Consumer.} A solver can return a surviving core as a human-readable exactness certificate.

\begin{theorem}[thm:polyhedral-description]\label{thm:polyhedral-description}
The universally valid members of \(\mathcal{K}_L\) equal \[\mathcal{K}_L\cap\bigcap_{\varnothing\neq A\subseteq\Omega}\bigcap_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Ord}(A)}\bigcup_{j=1}^{|A|}\{K:K(\sigma_j,P_j(\sigma))\geq\pi(A)\}.\] Hence the feasible set is an exact finite union of closed polyhedra. The core formulation gives an equivalent finite mixed-integer representation, while the recursion gives finite exact separation.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By \(\texttt{thm:universal-validity-iff}\) and
\(\texttt{def:subset-degeneracy}\), validity is equivalent, for every nonempty
\(A\), to
\[
\min_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Ord}(A)}
\max_{1\leq j\leq |A|}K(\sigma_j,P_j(\sigma))\geq\pi(A).
\]
A minimum of finitely many quantities is at least \(\pi(A)\) exactly when each is,
and a finite maximum is at least \(\pi(A)\) exactly when at least one constituent
is.  Hence the condition is exactly
\[
\bigcap_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Ord}(A)}
\bigcup_{j=1}^{|A|}
\{K:K(\sigma_j,P_j(\sigma))\geq\pi(A)\}.
\]
Intersecting this expression over nonempty \(A\) and with
\(\mathcal K_L\) proves the displayed description in the statement.  Each set in
braces is a closed half-space, while \(\mathcal K_L\) is defined by finitely many
linear equalities and inequalities under \(\texttt{ass:row-stochastic}\) and
\(\texttt{ass:legal-support}\).  Distributing the finitely many intersections over
the finitely many unions therefore gives an exact finite union of closed
polyhedra.

For an explicit core mixed-integer representation, for each nonempty \(A\) introduce
\(z_{A i}\in\{0,1\}\) for \(i\in A\), require
\(\sum_{i\in A}z_{A i}\geq1\), and introduce continuous variables
\(v_{Aij}\) for \(i,j\in A\).  The linear constraints
\[
\begin{gathered}
0\leq v_{Aij}\leq K(i,j),\qquad v_{Aij}\leq z_{Aj},\qquad
v_{Aij}\geq K(i,j)+z_{Aj}-1,\\
\sum_{j\in A}v_{Aij}\geq \pi(A)z_{Ai}\qquad(i\in A)
\end{gathered}
\]
force \(v_{Aij}=z_{Aj}K(i,j)\), because \(0\leq K(i,j)\leq1\).  Thus the selected
set \(C_A=\{i:z_{Ai}=1\}\) is nonempty and every selected row obeys
\(K(i,C_A)\geq\pi(A)\).  Conversely any core supplies feasible \(z,v\).  The
equivalence in \(\texttt{thm:peeling-core}\) proves exactness.  Finally,
\(\texttt{thm:subset-recursion}\) computes a violated \(A\) and an attaining
ordering whenever one exists, so it is an exact finite separator.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The result turns a universal testing requirement into a finite certifiable design region. \textit{Gap.} Existing arbitrary-permutation and partition results provide sufficient constructions rather than the complete feasible geometry under directed support. \textit{Consumer.} Exact optimization packages can search the entire valid design class rather than a heuristic partition subclass.

\begin{proposition}[thm:partition-strict-inclusion]\label{thm:partition-strict-inclusion}
Every \(K\in\mathcal{P}_L\) is universally valid. The inclusion can be strict: for uniform \(\pi\) on three states and complete \(L\), the cyclic kernel \[K=\begin{pmatrix}2/3&1/3&0\\0&2/3&1/3\\1/3&0&2/3\end{pmatrix}\] is universally valid but belongs to no partition-kernel class.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let \(K\in\mathcal P_L\).  By the definition of \(\mathcal P_L\), there is a finite partition \(\mathcal G\) of \(\Omega\) such that, for the unique cell \(G_x\in\mathcal G\) containing \(x\),
\[
K(x,y)=\frac{\pi(y)\mathbf 1\{y\in G_x\}}{\pi(G_x)}.
\]
Every cell is nonempty, so \(\pi(G)>0\) for every \(G\in\mathcal G\) by \(\pi(x)>0\) from Assumption \(\texttt{ass:positive-finite-design}\); thus every displayed division is well defined.  Fix a nonempty set \(A\subseteq\Omega\) and an ordering \(\sigma\in\operatorname{Ord}(A)\).  For each cell \(G\) with \(A\cap G\neq\varnothing\), let \(i_G\) be the last member of \(A\cap G\) in the ordering \(\sigma\), and let \(P(i_G)\) be the prefix ending at \(i_G\).  Since \(A\cap G\subseteq P(i_G)\) and \(G_{i_G}=G\), Definition \(\texttt{def:kernel-tail-value}\)'s row-mass notation and the partition formula give
\[
K\bigl(i_G,P(i_G)\bigr)
=\frac{\pi\bigl(P(i_G)\cap G\bigr)}{\pi(G)}
\geq \frac{\pi(A\cap G)}{\pi(G)}.
\]
Put \(r_G=\pi(A\cap G)/\pi(G)\) for every \(G\in\mathcal G\), taking \(r_G=0\) when \(A\cap G=\varnothing\).  Because the cells partition \(\Omega\),
\[
\pi(A)=\sum_{G\in\mathcal G}\pi(A\cap G)
=\sum_{G\in\mathcal G}\pi(G)r_G
\leq \max_{G\in\mathcal G}r_G,
\]
where the last inequality uses \(\sum_{G\in\mathcal G}\pi(G)=\pi(\Omega)=1\).  Consequently,
\[
\max_{1\leq j\leq |A|}K\bigl(\sigma_j,P_j(\sigma)\bigr)
\geq \max_{G\in\mathcal G}r_G
\geq \pi(A).
\]
This holds for every ordering \(\sigma\).  Taking the minimum over orderings in Definition \(\texttt{def:subset-degeneracy}\) yields \(d_K(A)\geq\pi(A)\).  Since \(A\) was arbitrary, Theorem \(\texttt{thm:universal-validity-iff}\) proves that \(K\) is universally valid.

It remains to prove that the inclusion is strict.  Let \(\Omega=\{1,2,3\}\), let \(\pi(i)=1/3\) for every \(i\), let \(L=\Omega\times\Omega\), and set
\[
K=\begin{pmatrix}
2/3&1/3&0\\
0&2/3&1/3\\
1/3&0&2/3
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
All entries are nonnegative, every row sums to one, and complete \(L\) makes every positive entry legal; hence \(K\in\mathcal K_L\) by Definition \(\texttt{def:legal-kernel-class}\).  For a singleton \(\{i\}\), the sole ordering gives
\[
d_K(\{i\})=K(i,i)=\frac23\geq\frac13=\pi(\{i\}).
\]
For a two-point set \(A\), the first prefix under every ordering is a singleton and therefore has row mass \(2/3\), so \(d_K(A)\geq2/3\).  Conversely, write the directed cycle as \(1\to2\to3\to1\).  Exactly one of the two vertices of \(A\) has its off-diagonal mass \(1/3\) directed to the omitted vertex; place that vertex last.  Its full-prefix mass is then \(2/3\), while the first vertex's singleton-prefix mass is \(2/3\).  This ordering has maximum prefix mass \(2/3\), and hence
\[
d_K(A)=\frac23=\pi(A).
\]
Finally, if \(A=\Omega\), the last prefix equals \(\Omega\), and Assumption \(\texttt{ass:row-stochastic}\) gives row mass one.  Thus
\[
d_K(\Omega)=1=\pi(\Omega).
\]
Theorem \(\texttt{thm:universal-validity-iff}\) now proves universal validity of this cyclic kernel.

Suppose, toward a contradiction, that this kernel belongs to \(\mathcal P_L\), and let \(\mathcal G\) be a representing partition.  The strict inequality \(K(1,2)>0\) in the partition formula forces \(1\) and \(2\) into the same cell.  Likewise \(K(2,3)>0\) forces \(2\) and \(3\) into the same cell.  Transitivity of membership in a partition cell therefore puts all three states in one cell, namely \(\Omega\).  The partition formula and uniformity of \(\pi\) would then imply \(K(x,y)=\pi(y)/\pi(\Omega)=1/3\) for every \(x,y\), contradicting \(K(1,1)=2/3\).  Hence the cyclic kernel is not a partition kernel, which proves strict inclusion.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} A concrete cyclic witness establishes that the characterization enlarges the operational design space. \textit{Gap.} Puelz et al. (2022) and Zhang and Zhao (2023) build partition or biclique kernels and do not exhibit this overlapping-row class. \textit{Consumer.} The strict witness motivates searching beyond biclique partitions whenever legal support contains directed cycles.

\begin{theorem}[thm:catalogue-power-optimizer]\label{thm:catalogue-power-optimizer}
For every finite prespecified catalogue, the program defining \(K^{\star}\) has an exact finite mixed-integer linear formulation. A terminating branch-and-cut implementation using \(\texttt{thm:subset-recursion}\) either returns an infeasibility certificate or returns \(K^{\star}\), its exact rejection probability, active validity disjuncts, and a global upper-bound certificate. In the feasible case it also returns \(\Delta_{\mathrm{part}}=\operatorname{Pow}(K^{\star})-\max_{K\in\mathcal{P}_L}\operatorname{Pow}(K)\geq0\); whenever \(\Delta_{\mathrm{part}}>0\), the certificate proves strict power dominance over every partition kernel.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For each catalogue member \(h\) and observed state \(x\), define the fixed coefficients and the corresponding linear row-tail mass
\[
 a_{hxy}=\mathbf 1\{s^h(y)\geq s^h(x)\},
 \qquad
 Q_{hx}(K)=\sum_{y\in\Omega}a_{hxy}K(x,y).
\]
The catalogue is fixed before outcomes by \(\texttt{ass:prespecified-catalogue}\), and \(\texttt{def:kernel-tail-value}\), as used through \(\texttt{def:catalogue-power}\), gives \(Q_{hx}(K)=p_K(x;s^h)\).  In addition to the continuous entries \(K(x,y)\), introduce rejection variables \(z_{hx}\in\{0,1\}\).  Impose
\[
 Q_{hx}(K)\leq \alpha+1-z_{hx}
 \qquad(h=1,\ldots,q,\ x\in\Omega)
\]
and maximize
\[
 \sum_{h=1}^{q}w_h\sum_{x\in\Omega}\pi(x)z_{hx}.
\]
Because row stochasticity and nonnegativity imply \(0\leq Q_{hx}(K)\leq1\), the constraint is vacuous when \(z_{hx}=0\), while \(z_{hx}=1\) is feasible exactly when the closed rejection inequality \(Q_{hx}(K)\leq\alpha\) holds.  Hence every feasible choice satisfies
\[
 z_{hx}\leq\mathbf 1\{p_K(x;s^h)\leq\alpha\},
\]
and, for every fixed \(K\), equality can be chosen for all \((h,x)\).  This remains true at \(Q_{hx}(K)=\alpha\), so threshold equality is handled without a strict inequality.  Terms with \(w_h=0\) are immaterial to the objective and may also be set equal to their indicators.  The optimized linear objective is therefore exactly \(\max_K\operatorname{Pow}(K)\), not a relaxation.

We next encode universal validity.  For every nonempty \(A\subseteq\Omega\), every ordering \(\sigma\in\operatorname{Ord}(A)\), and every \(j\in\{1,\ldots,|A|\}\), introduce \(y_{A\sigma j}\in\{0,1\}\) and impose
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^{|A|}y_{A\sigma j}\geq1,
 \qquad
 K(\sigma_j,P_j(\sigma))
 \geq \pi(A)-\bigl(1-y_{A\sigma j}\bigr).
\]
If \(y_{A\sigma j}=0\), the latter inequality follows from nonnegativity because \(\pi(A)-1\leq0\).  If it equals one, the selected half-space is
\(K(\sigma_j,P_j(\sigma))\geq\pi(A)\).  Thus the first constraint selects at least one valid prefix for every pair \((A,\sigma)\).  By \(\texttt{thm:polyhedral-description}\), these finitely many constraints are equivalent to \(d_K(A)\geq\pi(A)\) for every nonempty \(A\).  Adding
\[
 0\leq K(x,y)\leq1,
 \qquad
 \sum_{y\in\Omega}K(x,y)=1,
 \qquad
 K(x,y)=0\quad\text{when }(x,y)\notin L
\]
therefore gives an exact finite mixed-integer linear formulation of the program in \(\texttt{def:power-optimal-kernel}\).  Finiteness follows from finiteness of \(\Omega\), its nonempty subsets, their orderings, and the catalogue.

For completeness, consider a branch-and-cut solution of this formulation.  At a proposed kernel, \(\texttt{thm:subset-recursion}\) computes all \(d_K(A)\) in \(O(m2^m)\) arithmetic operations.  If \(d_K(A)<\pi(A)\), a stored minimizing ordering \(\sigma\) satisfies
\[
 \max_{1\leq j\leq|A|}K(\sigma_j,P_j(\sigma))<\pi(A),
\]
so the disjunction indexed by \((A,\sigma)\) is violated and is added.  No indexed disjunction need be added twice, and only finitely many exist.  Consequently the separation phase terminates; exhaustive branching over the finitely many \(z\)- and \(y\)-variables is a finite fallback.

We spell out the promised exact certificate.  After fixing all binary variables in one branch, the remaining problem is a bounded system of linear inequalities in the finitely many entries of \(K\) and one objective variable.  Eliminating a variable is exact: every pair of bounds
\[
 a v\leq b\quad(a>0),
 \qquad
 -c v\leq d\quad(c>0)
\]
implies and is feasible over \(v\) exactly when the retained variables satisfy \(cb+ad\geq0\), together with the inequalities not containing \(v\).  Repeating this finite Fourier--Motzkin step either produces a contradictory constant inequality or projects the branch onto an objective interval with an exact upper endpoint.  The elimination trace is respectively an infeasibility certificate or an upper-bound certificate.  Taking the largest certified branch endpoint and exhibiting an incumbent attaining it certifies global optimality.  The chosen \(y\)-variables identify active validity disjuncts, and direct evaluation of the closed \(z\)-conditions returns the exact rejection probability.

For inputs represented by rational or real-algebraic numbers, all coefficient comparisons and elimination operations above are terminating exact computations.  If arbitrary real constants are admitted, the same finite derivation is an ideal exact-real-arithmetic procedure rather than a Turing-algorithm claim.  This distinction does not change the finite mixed-integer representation or its mathematical certificate.

It remains to certify the partition comparison.  First, every legal partition kernel satisfies the validity inequalities.  Indeed, fix a nonempty \(A\) and any ordering \(\sigma\).  For a partition \(\mathcal G\), positivity gives \(\pi(G)>0\) for each nonempty cell, and
\[
 \pi(A)=\sum_{G\in\mathcal G}\pi(G)\frac{\pi(A\cap G)}{\pi(G)}.
\]
Therefore some cell \(G\) meeting \(A\) obeys \(\pi(A\cap G)/\pi(G)\geq\pi(A)\).  Let \(i\) be the last element of \(A\cap G\) in \(\sigma\), at position \(j\).  Then \(P_j(\sigma)\cap G=A\cap G\), and the partition-kernel formula gives
\[
 K(i,P_j(\sigma))=\frac{\pi(A\cap G)}{\pi(G)}\geq\pi(A).
\]
This holds for every ordering, so \(d_K(A)\geq\pi(A)\).  Thus every member of \(\mathcal P_L\) is feasible for the unrestricted program.

If that program is feasible, its singleton constraint gives
\[
 K(i,i)=d_K(\{i\})\geq\pi(i)>0
\]
for every \(i\).  Legal support then forces every self-loop \((i,i)\) to lie in \(L\), so the singleton partition kernel belongs to \(\mathcal P_L\); the benchmark is nonempty.  There are only finitely many partitions of a finite set, and each legal one can be evaluated by the same exact arithmetic.  Since each is feasible,
\[
 \max_{K\in\mathcal P_L}\operatorname{Pow}(K)
 \leq \operatorname{Pow}(K^\star),
 \qquad
 \Delta_{\mathrm{part}}\geq0.
\]
The globally certified optimum of the full mixed-integer program and the finite certified maximum over legal partitions therefore certify strict dominance over every partition kernel exactly when \(\Delta_{\mathrm{part}}>0\).  Finally, \(\texttt{ass:outcome-free-kernel}\) ensures that optimizing this prespecified catalogue selects a design-stage kernel and does not adapt the test to the observed outcomes.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Power is treated as a finite experimental-design objective with a checkable optimality certificate. \textit{Gap.} Yanagi and Sei (2022) optimize an approximate criterion within biclique constructions, whereas the mixed-integer program optimizes exact rejection probability over all universally valid legal row kernels; the asymmetric-isolate family supplies an analytic instance with a matching global upper bound. \textit{Consumer.} The Cai weather-insurance reanalysis can first test for the analytic asymmetric-isolate certificate and then compare the globally optimized kernel with every partition kernel at the prespecified level.

\begin{proposition}[prop:worked-nonpartition-power-gap]\label{prop:worked-nonpartition-power-gap}
Let \(m=3\), \(\Omega=\{1,2,3\}\), \(\pi(i)=1/3\), and \(L=\{(1,1),(2,2),(3,3),(1,2),(2,3),(3,1)\}\). At \(\alpha=2/3\), take \(q=1\), \(w_1=1\), and \(s^1(1)>s^1(2)>s^1(3)\). The legal nonpartition kernel \[K=\begin{pmatrix}2/3&1/3&0\\0&2/3&1/3\\1/3&0&2/3\end{pmatrix}\] satisfies \(d_K(A)\geq\pi(A)\) for every nonempty \(A\subseteq\Omega\) and has \(\operatorname{Pow}(K)=2/3\). Every member of \(\mathcal{P}_L\) has singleton cells, hence \(K(x,y)=\mathbf{1}\{x=y\}\) and power zero. Consequently \(K\) is globally catalogue-power optimal and \(\Delta_{\mathrm{part}}=2/3\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The displayed matrix has nonnegative entries, every row sums to one, and its positive entries are exactly the six pairs listed in \(L\).  Hence it is a legal row-stochastic kernel.  We now inspect every nonempty subset, which is also the required smallest-instance stress test.  For a singleton,
\[
 d_K(\{i\})=K(i,i)=\frac23,\qquad i=1,2,3.
\]
For each two-point set \(A\), exactly one of its two directed cycle edges lies in \(L\).  If that edge is \((i,j)\), order the set as \((i,j)\).  The first prefix mass is \(K(i,\{i\})=2/3\), and the second is \(K(j,A)=2/3\), because the off-diagonal mass in row \(j\) points to the third state, outside \(A\).  Thus this ordering has maximum \(2/3\).  Conversely, the first prefix in either ordering is a diagonal mass \(2/3\), so
\[
 d_K(A)=\frac23\qquad (|A|=2).
\]
Finally, for \(A=\Omega\), the last prefix in every ordering is \(\Omega\), and row stochasticity gives \(K(i,\Omega)=1\); hence \(d_K(\Omega)=1\).  Since \(\pi(A)=|A|/3\), these calculations give
\[
 d_K(A)\geq \pi(A)\qquad\text{for every nonempty }A\subseteq\Omega.
\]
Theorem \(\texttt{thm:universal-validity-iff}\) therefore proves universal validity.

The strict score order gives the upper-tail sets \(\{1\}\), \(\{1,2\}\), and \(\Omega\) at states one, two, and three, respectively.  By \(\texttt{def:kernel-tail-value}\),
\[
 p_K(1;s^1)=K(1,\{1\})=\frac23,
 \qquad
 p_K(2;s^1)=K(2,\{1,2\})=\frac23,
 \qquad
 p_K(3;s^1)=K(3,\Omega)=1.
\]
Thus the rejection set at \(\alpha=2/3\) is exactly \(\{1,2\}\), and \(\texttt{def:catalogue-power}\) yields
\[
 \operatorname{Pow}(K)=\sum_{x\in\Omega}\pi(x)\mathbf 1\{p_K(x;s^1)\leq2/3\}=\frac23.
\]
For any universally valid legal kernel \(\widetilde K\), universal validity applied to this fixed score and level gives
\[
 \operatorname{Pow}(\widetilde K)
 =\Pr_{X\sim\pi}\{p_{\widetilde K}(X;s^1)\leq2/3\}
 \leq\frac23.
\]
The displayed \(K\) attains this upper bound, so it is globally catalogue-power optimal.

It remains to calculate the partition benchmark.  If two distinct states \(i\) and \(j\) belonged to the same partition cell, positivity of \(\pi\) would imply that its conditional-design kernel assigns both \(K(i,j)>0\) and \(K(j,i)>0\).  The directed three-cycle contains at most one direction for each unordered pair, contradicting legal support.  Consequently every legal partition has only singleton cells and its kernel is the identity.  All its upper-tail values equal one, so its power at \(\alpha=2/3<1\) is zero.  Therefore
\[
 \Delta_{\mathrm{part}}
 =\operatorname{Pow}(K)-\max_{\widetilde K\in\mathcal P_L}\operatorname{Pow}(\widetilde K)
 =\frac23-0=\frac23.
\]
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The uniform directed cycle is the smallest exhaustive stress test for the broader asymmetric-isolate support mechanism and also exhibits a sharper \(2/3\)-power cyclic kernel at its stated score and level. \textit{Gap.} Partition kernels cannot use any one-way edge on this support; the generic asymmetric-isolate construction needs only one such edge, while the displayed cyclic kernel uses all three to reach power \(2/3\). \textit{Consumer.} The Cai weather-insurance presolve can use the three-state calculation as a unit test and the asymmetric-isolate criterion as a support-level screening rule before full optimization.

\begin{theorem}[thm:composite-null-validity]\label{thm:composite-null-validity}
Suppose \(K\) is row-stochastic and satisfies \(d_K(A)\geq\pi(A)\) for every nonempty \(A\subseteq\Omega\). If the true fixed schedule belongs to \(\Theta_{r,c}(X,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})\), then for every \(\alpha\in[0,1]\), \(\Pr_{X\sim\pi}\{p^{\mathrm{wc}}_{r,c}(X)\leq\alpha\}\leq\alpha\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(Y\) be the true fixed potential-outcome schedule.  Under the bounded null in the theorem, for every possible realized assignment \(x\in\Omega\), the schedule \(Y\) is consistent with the outcomes that would be observed at \(x\) and satisfies
\[
 \sum_{u\in U}\mathbf 1\{Y_u(1,e)-Y_u(0,e)>c\}\leq r.
\]
Consequently, by \(\texttt{def:bounded-null-schedules}\),
\[
 Y\in\Theta_{r,c}\bigl(x,Y^{\mathrm{obs}}(x)\bigr)
 \qquad(x\in\Omega).
\tag{1}
\]
The fixed-potential-outcome assumption makes \(Y\) nonrandom under the design.  Fixed peer exposure and statistic imputability make
\[
 s_Y(y):=T\bigl(y,Y_Y(y)\bigr),\qquad y\in\Omega,
\tag{2}
\]
a well-defined score vector, and the fixed statistic rule implicit in \(\texttt{def:schedule-score}\) makes it deterministic.  The outcome-free-kernel assumption ensures that the same kernel \(K\) is used for every possible realization of \(X\).  Thus (2) is an admissible deterministic score in \(\texttt{def:universal-validity}\).

By (1) and the supremum in \(\texttt{def:worst-case-pvalue}\), pointwise in \(x\),
\[
 p^{\mathrm{wc}}_{r,c}(x)
 \geq p_K(x;s_Y).
\tag{3}
\]
The assumed subset inequalities and \(\texttt{thm:universal-validity-iff}\) give
\[
 \Pr_{X\sim\pi}\{p_K(X;s_Y)\leq\alpha\}\leq\alpha.
\tag{4}
\]
Since (3) implies
\[
 \{p^{\mathrm{wc}}_{r,c}(X)\leq\alpha\}
 \subseteq
 \{p_K(X;s_Y)\leq\alpha\},
\tag{5}
\]
taking design probabilities in (5) and applying (4) proves
\[
 \Pr_{X\sim\pi}\{p^{\mathrm{wc}}_{r,c}(X)\leq\alpha\}
 \leq\alpha.
\]
All probabilities here are with respect to \(X\sim\pi\) alone, as required by \(\texttt{ass:fixed-potential-outcomes}\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Pointwise maximization over null-consistent sharp schedules preserves finite-sample design validity. \textit{Gap.} Caughey et al. establish bounded-null validity without network coupling, while Owusu's network heterogeneity procedure is asymptotic. \textit{Consumer.} Applied researchers obtain exact finite-population statements about how many farmers have direct effects above a scientifically chosen threshold.

\begin{proposition}[prop:minimal-assignment-coupling]\label{prop:minimal-assignment-coupling}
Suppose \(K\) is row-stochastic and satisfies \(d_K(A)\geq\pi(A)\) for every
nonempty \(A\subseteq\Omega\).  The coupled-pattern handle is cardinality-minimal
among schedule problems having a nontrivial pairwise rank comparison,
own-treatment variation for every focal unit, and at least one potential-outcome
coordinate shared by two distinct assignment scores: necessarily \(N\geq2\) and
\(m\geq3\), and the handle attains \(N=2\) and \(m=3\).  Its repeated-coordinate
and effect-exceedance constraint hypergraph connects all three assignment-specific
rank comparisons.  It contains a genuine nonproduct compatible-schedule instance:
when state \(2\) is observed and \(r=N\), the common
\(\theta_1(0,e)\) coordinate couples the first and third scores.  This is an
existential structural certificate; it does not assert nonproductness for every
realized assignment and every effect-count null.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A nontrivial pairwise rank comparison contains outcomes from two distinct focal units, so necessarily \(N\geq2\).  A fiber with \(m=1\) cannot provide own-treatment variation.  Suppose instead that \(m=2\) and every focal unit has own-treatment variation between the two available states.  Then, for every \(u\in U\), one state uses \(\theta_u(0,e)\) and the other uses \(\theta_u(1,e)\).  Hence no coordinate \(\theta_u(a,e)\) occurs in both assignment-specific outcome vectors.  In particular, no potential-outcome coordinate can be shared by two distinct assignment scores.  This contradicts the shared-coordinate requirement and proves \(m\geq3\).

The handle in \(\texttt{def:minimal-coupled-pattern}\) attains both lower bounds.  Its three treatment vectors are
\[
 (0,0),\qquad(1,0),\qquad(0,1).
\]
Unit one varies along the legal comparison \(1\to2\), and unit two varies along \(2\to3\), as well as along \(3\to1\).  The three assignment-specific pairwise comparisons use, respectively,
\[
 \bigl(\theta_1(0,e),\theta_2(0,e)\bigr),\qquad
 \bigl(\theta_1(1,e),\theta_2(0,e)\bigr),\qquad
 \bigl(\theta_1(0,e),\theta_2(1,e)\bigr).
\tag{1}
\]
Thus the repeated coordinate \(\theta_2(0,e)\) joins the first and second comparisons, while \(\theta_1(0,e)\) joins the first and third.  Therefore the hypergraph whose vertices are the three comparisons and whose hyperedges record repeated coordinates is connected.  The two effect-count comparisons additionally use
\[
 \theta_1(1,e)-\theta_1(0,e)
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \theta_2(1,e)-\theta_2(0,e),
\]
so adjoining the effect-exceedance constraints preserves this connectedness.

To certify genuine nonproductness, take state \(2\) as the realized assignment and take \(r=N\).  The effect-count condition is then vacuous.  Observation consistency fixes \(\theta_1(1,e)\) and \(\theta_2(0,e)\), whereas \(\theta_1(0,e)\) and \(\theta_2(1,e)\) remain free.  In the product of the three assignment-wise marginal schedule images, the state-\(1\) component may use one real value \(a\) for \(\theta_1(0,e)\) while the state-\(3\) component uses a different real value \(a'\).  Each marginal value is feasible because the coordinate is unrestricted.  A globally compatible schedule, however, uses one common coordinate \(\theta_1(0,e)\) in both the first and third vectors in (1), and therefore requires \(a=a'\).  Choosing \(a\neq a'\) shows that the global compatible-schedule image is a proper subset of the product of its assignment-wise projections.  This gives the claimed existential coupled instance.

The conclusion is deliberately not universal over observations and nulls.  For example, if state \(1\) is observed and \(r=N\), both repeated baseline coordinates in (1) are fixed by observation consistency, and the remaining coordinates can separate.  Thus the proposition asserts exactly the minimal existential certificate proved above.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} A minimal nonseparable instance fixes the residual schedule question at the first fiber where partition and stratumwise reductions genuinely cease to apply. \textit{Gap.} The exchangeable corner and multiple-choice-knapsack comparators factor schedule choices by a single cell or by independent strata and contain no shared-coordinate three-score pattern. \textit{Consumer.} An exact solver can use the two-unit, three-assignment instance as its smallest coupled regression test.

\begin{proposition}[prop:no-interference-reduction]\label{prop:no-interference-reduction}
Assume that \(\pi(x)>0\) for every \(x\in\Omega\), that the potential-outcome schedule \(Y\) is fixed with \(X\sim\pi\) the only random object, that every focal unit has peer exposure \(e\) throughout \(\Omega\), that \(T(x,Y_{\theta}(x))\) is imputable and uses a fixed weak-order tie rule, and that \((L,K)\) is fixed before \(Y^{\mathrm{obs}}\) is observed.  Suppose also that \(K\) is row-stochastic, \(d_K(A)\geq\pi(A)\) for every nonempty \(A\subseteq\Omega\), and \(Y_u(a,e')\) is constant in \(e'\in\mathcal E\).  Then, for \(0\leq r<N\),
\[
 \sum_{u\in U}\mathbf 1\{Y_u(1,e)-Y_u(0,e)>c\}\leq r
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \tau_{(N-r)}(e)\leq c,
\]
while the null with \(r=N\) is vacuous.  For either the exchangeable fixed-treatment-count complete-randomization setup with one cell or the product of exchangeable fixed-treatment-count within-stratum randomizations, let \(\mathcal G\) be the resulting partition and use the partition kernel
\[
 K(x,B)=\frac{\pi(B\cap G_x)}{\pi(G_x)},\qquad B\subseteq\Omega.
\]
This kernel satisfies \(d_K(A)\geq\pi(A)\) for every nonempty \(A\), and, with respectively \(T=T_{\mathrm C}\) or \(T=T_{\mathrm S}\), the frozen schedule-supremum test is finite-sample valid for its own score:
\[
 Y\in\Theta_{r,c}(X,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \Pr_{X\sim\pi}\{p^{\mathrm{wc}}_{r,c}(X)\leq\alpha\}\leq\alpha.
\]
The individual and simultaneous confidence conclusions in \(\texttt{lem:caughey-quantile-specialization}\) and \(\texttt{lem:suli-knapsack-specialization}\) remain valid for the comparator procedures stated in those cited results.  They are not, however, exact reductions of the frozen score map.  In the two-unit, one-treated, uniform design, take \(\phi(j)=j\) and the no-interference schedule
\[
 \theta_1(0,e')=1,\quad \theta_1(1,e')=3,
 \qquad
 \theta_2(0,e')=0,\quad \theta_2(1,e')=2
 \quad(e'\in\mathcal E).
\]
At the assignment treating unit one, with \(r=0\) and \(c=2\), the frozen construction gives \(p^{\mathrm{wc}}_{0,2}=1\), whereas the Caughey adjusted-outcome upper-tail value based on holding \(Y^{\mathrm{obs}}-2Z(X)\) fixed is \(1/2\).  Taking \(S=1\), \(\mathcal U=\{U\}\), and \(\phi_1=\phi\) gives the same obstruction for the Su--Li statistic.  Thus neither equality with the Caughey adjusted-outcome test nor exact reuse of the Su--Li multiple-choice-knapsack optimization follows from the frozen definitions.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Because \(Y_u(a,e')\) is constant in \(e'\in\mathcal E\), the definition of \(\tau_u(e)\) gives the ordinary individual treatment effect, independent of the exposure label.  Write
\[
 \tau_{(1)}(e)\leq\cdots\leq\tau_{(N)}(e).
\]
Fix \(0\leq r<N\).  The inequality
\[
 \sum_{u\in U}\mathbf 1\{Y_u(1,e)-Y_u(0,e)>c\}\leq r
\tag{1}
\]
holds if and only if at least \(N-r\) coordinates of \(\tau(e)\) are at most \(c\).  By the definition of the \((N-r)\)-th order statistic, this is equivalent to
\[
 \tau_{(N-r)}(e)\leq c.
\tag{2}
\]
The strict inequality in the indicator ensures that an effect equal to \(c\) is correctly counted on the left side of (2).  When \(r=N\), the sum of the \(N\) indicators is always at most \(N\), so the null is vacuous.

We next establish the universal-validity certificate for the stated partition kernel.  Let \(\mathcal G\) be either partition in the proposition and define
\[
 K(x,B)=\frac{\pi(B\cap G_x)}{\pi(G_x)}.
\tag{3}
\]
Every cell \(H\in\mathcal G\) is nonempty.  Positivity of \(\pi\) therefore gives \(\pi(H)>0\), so (3) is well defined, nonnegative, and satisfies
\[
 \sum_{y\in\Omega}K(x,y)=\frac{\pi(G_x)}{\pi(G_x)}=1.
\tag{4}
\]
Fix nonempty \(A\subseteq\Omega\) and \(\sigma\in\operatorname{Ord}(A)\).  For each cell \(H\) meeting \(A\), let \(j_H\) be the position of the last element of \(A\cap H\) in \(\sigma\).  Then
\[
 P_{j_H}(\sigma)\cap H=A\cap H,
 \qquad G_{\sigma_{j_H}}=H,
\]
and hence
\[
 K\bigl(\sigma_{j_H},P_{j_H}(\sigma)\bigr)
 =\frac{\pi(A\cap H)}{\pi(H)}.
\tag{5}
\]
For a cell disjoint from \(A\), set the ratio in (5) to zero.  Since the positive weights \(\pi(H)\) sum to one, (5) yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \max_{1\leq j\leq |A|}K\bigl(\sigma_j,P_j(\sigma)\bigr)
 &\geq \max_{H\in\mathcal G}\frac{\pi(A\cap H)}{\pi(H)}\\
 &\geq \sum_{H\in\mathcal G}\pi(H)
       \frac{\pi(A\cap H)}{\pi(H)}\\
 &=\sum_{H\in\mathcal G}\pi(A\cap H)
 =\pi(A).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
Taking the minimum over \(\sigma\) in \(\texttt{def:subset-degeneracy}\) proves \(d_K(A)\geq\pi(A)\).  This argument applies both to the one-cell fixed-treatment-count design and to the partition induced by the product of the within-stratum fixed-treatment-count designs.

By \(\texttt{thm:universal-validity-iff}\), the kernel in (3) is universally valid.  The fixed schedule, fixed peer exposure, legal-comparison imputability, fixed weak-order tie rule, and pre-outcome choice of \((L,K)\) are exactly the hypotheses needed by \(\texttt{thm:composite-null-validity}\).  Therefore, with \(T=T_{\mathrm C}\) or \(T=T_{\mathrm S}\), respectively,
\[
 Y\in\Theta_{r,c}(X,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \Pr_{X\sim\pi}\{p^{\mathrm{wc}}_{r,c}(X)\leq\alpha\}
 \leq\alpha.
\tag{7}
\]
The comparator-specific individual and simultaneous confidence conclusions are separately supplied by the cited leaves \(\texttt{lem:caughey-quantile-specialization}\) and \(\texttt{lem:suli-knapsack-specialization}\).  Those leaves concern their own comparator constructions; they do not identify either construction with \(\texttt{def:schedule-score}\).

We now prove the claimed obstruction to such identification.  Let \(N=2\), let \(x_i\) be the assignment treating unit \(i\), and set
\[
 \Omega=\{x_1,x_2\},\qquad
 \pi(x_1)=\pi(x_2)=\frac12,
 \qquad K(x_i,x_j)=\frac12\quad(1\leq i,j\leq2).
\tag{8}
\]
This is the one-cell partition kernel.  For either singleton \(A=\{x_i\}\), \(d_K(A)=1/2=\pi(A)\); for \(A=\Omega\), either ordering has prefix masses \(1/2\) and \(1\), so \(d_K(\Omega)=1=\pi(\Omega)\).

Choose \(\phi(j)=j\), set \(T=T_{\mathrm C}\), and, for every \(e'\in\mathcal E\), take
\[
 \theta_1(0,e')=1,\quad \theta_1(1,e')=3,
 \qquad
 \theta_2(0,e')=0,\quad \theta_2(1,e')=2.
\tag{9}
\]
Suppose \(X=x_1\), so \(Y^{\mathrm{obs}}=(3,0)\).  Under \(x_1\), the schedule-imputed outcome vector is \((3,0)\), and under \(x_2\) it is \((1,2)\).  In both assignments the treated unit has ascending rank two, without ties.  Hence
\[
 s_\theta(x_1)=2=s_\theta(x_2),
 \qquad p_K(x_1;s_\theta)=1.
\tag{10}
\]
Both direct effects in (9) equal two, so \(\theta\in\Theta_{0,2}(x_1,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})\).  The supremum defining the worst-case value and (10) give \(p^{\mathrm{wc}}_{0,2}(x_1)\geq1\).  Row stochasticity gives the reverse inequality, and therefore
\[
 p^{\mathrm{wc}}_{0,2}(x_1)=1.
\tag{11}
\]

For the Caughey adjusted-outcome upper-tail construction at the boundary \(c=2\), the vector held fixed across reallocations is
\[
 a=Y^{\mathrm{obs}}-2\bigl(Z_1(x_1),Z_2(x_1)\bigr)=(1,0).
\]
The treated unit has ascending rank two under \(x_1\) and rank one under \(x_2\).  Thus its upper-tail randomization value at \(x_1\) is
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^{2}\pi(x_j)
 \mathbf 1\{T_{\mathrm C}(x_j,a)\geq T_{\mathrm C}(x_1,a)\}
 =\frac12.
\tag{12}
\]
Equations (11) and (12) give the asserted \(1\)-versus-\(1/2\) discrepancy.  Taking \(S=1\), \(\mathcal U=\{U\}\), and \(\phi_1=\phi\) makes the within-stratum ranks equal the full-sample ranks, so \(T_{\mathrm S}=T_{\mathrm C}\) and the same discrepancy applies.  Hence neither comparator algorithm is an exact reduction of the frozen score map, although (1)--(7) establish the null interpretation and finite-sample validity that do carry over.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The reduction checks that the new target respects the established no-interference boundary. \textit{Gap.} The comparator results cover precisely these special cases rather than assignment-coupled network fibers. \textit{Consumer.} Implementations may reuse the null interpretation and finite-sample validity theorem, but adjusted-outcome and knapsack routines cannot be reused for the frozen score without changing the score construction.

\begin{lemma}[lem:caughey-quantile-specialization]\label{lem:caughey-quantile-specialization}
Under an exchangeable complete-randomization law with a fixed treatment count and
a nondecreasing full-sample rank-score statistic, the bounded-null randomization
test yields an exact \(1-\alpha\) confidence set for each fixed ordered individual
effect \(\tau_{(k)}(e)\); the confidence-set construction obtained by inverting all
quantile nulls is simultaneously valid for all \(k\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[lem:suli-knapsack-specialization]\label{lem:suli-knapsack-specialization}
\texttt{Under a product of independent within-\allowbreak{}stratum exchangeable complete-\allowbreak{}randomization
laws with fixed treated counts and a nondecreasing stratified rank-\allowbreak{}score statistic,\allowbreak{}
the exact bounded-\allowbreak{}quantile-\allowbreak{}null optimization reduces to a multiple-\allowbreak{}choice-\allowbreak{}knapsack
problem,\allowbreak{} and inversion of these tests gives simultaneous randomization-\allowbreak{}based
confidence sets for all ordered individual effects.}
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[thm:exact-network-schedule-reduction]\label{thm:exact-network-schedule-reduction}
Assume that \(K\) is row-stochastic, that \((L,K)\) is fixed before observing \(Y^{\mathrm{obs}}\), and that
\[
d_K(A)\geq \pi(A)\qquad
(\varnothing\neq A\subseteq\Omega).
\]
Fix a realized assignment \(x_0\in\Omega\), a count \(r\in\{0,\ldots,N\}\), and a threshold \(c\in\mathbb R\).  Under fixed peer exposure, imputability, and the fixed rank-statistic rule, the following finite formulation is exact.  Introduce the \(2N\) variables \(z_{ua}=\theta_u(a,e)\).  Enumerate \(h\in\{0,1\}^{U}\) with \(\sum_u h_u\leq r\), and enumerate
\[
\lambda_{xuv}\in\{-1,0,1\},qquad
x\in\Omega,\quad 1\leq u<v\leq N,
\]
where \(h_u=1\) represents \(z_{u1}-z_{u0}>c\) and \(\lambda_{xuv}\) is the sign of \(z_{u,Z_u(x)}-z_{v,Z_v(x)}\).  For each of the at most
\(2^N3^{m\binom N2}\) labels, impose the observation equalities, the indicated strict or weak effect inequalities, and the indicated sign equalities or inequalities.  A label having strict rows \(a_j^{\mathsf T}z<b_j\) is feasible if and only if the auxiliary linear program
\[
\max_{z,\varepsilon}\ \varepsilon
\quad\text{subject to}\quad
a_j^{\mathsf T}z\leq b_j-\varepsilon\ (j),
\quad Cz\leq d,
\quad Ez=f,
\quad 0\leq\varepsilon\leq1
\]
has optimum strictly positive; if there is no strict row, ordinary feasibility is used.  The fixed tie rule turns each feasible \(\lambda\) into ranks and hence into constants \(t_\lambda(x)=T(x,Y_\theta(x))\).  Consequently
\[
p^{\mathrm{wc}}_{r,c}(x_0)
=max_{(h,\lambda)\ {
m feasible}}
\sum_{y\in\Omega}K(x_0,y)
\mathbf 1\{t_\lambda(y)\geq t_\lambda(x_0)\}.
\]
If \(\Theta_{r,c}(x_0,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})\neq\varnothing\), the maximum is attained and the feasibility program returns an optimizing compatible schedule; otherwise the formulation returns an infeasibility certificate.  With rational or real-algebraic representations of \(K\), \(Y^{\mathrm{obs}}\), \(c\), \(\alpha\), and the finite rank-score table, this is a terminating exact computation.  For arbitrary unspecified real inputs it is a finite formulation in ideal exact-real arithmetic, not a Turing-algorithm claim.  The same finite formulation applies to, and therefore exactly solves, the three-assignment, two-unit shared-coordinate pattern.

For \(1\leq k\leq N\), define in the extended real line
\[
b_k(x_0;\alpha)
=\sup\{c\in\mathbb R:
p^{\mathrm{wc}}_{N-k,c}(x_0)\leq\alpha\},
\qquad \sup\varnothing=-\infty.
\]
The nested finite-cell computations give the exact projection identity
\[
\ell_k(e;\alpha)=b_k(X;\alpha),
\qquad k=1,\ldots,N,
\]
where \(\inf\varnothing=+infty\).  In particular,
\[
\Pr_{X\sim\pi}\!\left\{
\tau_{(k)}(e)\geq\ell_k(e;\alpha)
\text{ for every }k=1,\ldots,N
\right\}\geq1-\alpha.
\]
If the inverted region is empty because rejected thresholds are unbounded, the finite set of attainable rank-score patterns stabilizes for large \(c\); this forces rejection at \(r=N\), and hence \(b_k=+\infty\) for every \(k\), so the displayed projection identity remains valid.

Under no interference, the order-statistic equivalence
\[
\sum_{u\in U}\mathbf 1\{\tau_u(e)>c\}\leq r
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\tau_{(N-r)}(e)\leq c
\qquad(r<N)
\]
is exact, and partition kernels retain finite-sample validity.  Nevertheless, the frozen assignment-specific schedule score does not exactly recover either the Caughey--Dafoe--Li--Miratrix adjusted-outcome statistic or the Su--Li multiple-choice-knapsack statistic.  In the two-unit, one-treated partition example with \(\phi(q)=q\), potential outcomes \((1,3)\) for unit one and \((0,2)\) for unit two, and observed assignment treating unit one, the frozen bounded-null value at \((r,c)=(0,2)\) is \(1\), whereas the Caughey adjusted-outcome upper-tail value is \(1/2\); the one-stratum Su--Li clause contains the same obstruction.  Thus the exact network schedule reduction and simultaneous projection are positive, but the requested exact recovery and strict-extension claims for those two comparator algorithms are false.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write \(x_0\) for the realized assignment.  By fixed peer exposure, every focal outcome used on the fiber has the form
\[
 Y_\theta(x)_u=z_{u,Z_u(x)},
 \qquad z_{ua}:=\theta_u(a,e),
\tag{1}
\]
and consistency with the observed data is precisely
\[
 z_{u,Z_u(x_0)}=Y^{\mathrm{obs}}_u,
 \qquad u\in U.
\tag{2}
\]
Coordinates of \(\theta\) outside exposure \(e\) enter neither (1) nor any fiber score and may be completed arbitrarily.  Statistic imputability ensures that every outcome in (1) needed for a legal comparison is available.

For each \(u\), split the parameter space into the disjoint cases
\[
 h_u=1:\ z_{u1}-z_{u0}>c,
 \qquad
 h_u=0:\ z_{u1}-z_{u0}\leq c.
\tag{3}
\]
The bounded-null restriction is exactly \(\sum_u h_u\leq r\); in particular, equality of an effect with \(c\) belongs to the \(h_u=0\) case.  For every \(x\in\Omega\) and \(u<v\), define
\[
 D_{xuv}=z_{u,Z_u(x)}-z_{v,Z_v(x)}
\]
and label it by one of the disjoint alternatives \(D_{xuv}<0\), \(D_{xuv}=0\), or \(D_{xuv}>0\).  Equations (2)--(3) and these ternary comparisons, over all binary labels \(h\) and ternary labels \(\lambda\), form a disjoint cover of \(\Theta_{r,c}(x_0,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})\).  The number of labels is at most
\[
 2^N3^{|\Omega|\binom N2}=2^N3^{m\binom N2}.
\tag{4}
\]
The same variables \(z_{ua}\) occur for every assignment, so this cover enforces the shared-potential-outcome equalities and is not an assignment-wise product relaxation.

Fix a label.  Write its strict inequalities, after reversing signs where necessary, as \(a_j^{\mathsf T}z<b_j\), its weak inequalities as \(Cz\leq d\), and its equalities as \(Ez=f\).  If \(z^\star\) belongs to this cell, the minimum of its finitely many strict slacks is some \(\delta>0\).  Then \((z^\star,\min\{1,\delta\})\) is feasible for
\[
 \max_{z,\varepsilon}\ \varepsilon
 \quad\text{subject to}\quad
 a_j^{\mathsf T}z\leq b_j-\varepsilon\ (j),\quad
 Cz\leq d,\quad Ez=f,\quad0\leq\varepsilon\leq1.
\tag{5}
\]
Conversely, any feasible pair in (5) with \(\varepsilon>0\) satisfies every original strict inequality.  Since the feasible set in (5) is a polyhedron and its objective is bounded above by one, a finite optimum, when feasible, is attained.  It follows that the original cell is nonempty if and only if the optimum in (5) is strictly positive.  If there is no strict row, ordinary feasibility of \(Cz\leq d,Ez=f\) is equivalent to nonemptiness.  This establishes the claimed feasibility test without bounds on \(z\) or a big-\(M\) constant.

The signs \(\lambda\), together with the deterministic predeclared tie rule, determine the rank vector at every assignment.  Because \(T\) is a fixed rank statistic, it is constant on the cell; write this value as \(t_\lambda(x)\).  The kernel tail value throughout the cell is therefore
\[
 W_\lambda(x_0)
 :=\sum_{y\in\Omega}K(x_0,y)
 \mathbf 1\{t_\lambda(y)\geq t_\lambda(x_0)\}.
\tag{6}
\]
The finite cells cover the compatible schedule set, and hence \(\texttt{def:worst-case-pvalue}\) gives
\[
 p^{\mathrm{wc}}_{r,c}(x_0)
 =\max_{(h,\lambda):\,\mathrm{cell\ nonempty}}W_\lambda(x_0),
\tag{7}
\]
with value zero when no cell is feasible.  If the compatible set is nonempty, a cell attaining the finite maximum in (7), together with a feasible solution of (5), supplies an optimizing \(z\); arbitrary completion outside exposure \(e\) supplies an optimizing schedule \(\theta\).  If no cell is feasible, the finite list of infeasibility conclusions certifies that \(\Theta_{r,c}(x_0,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})=\varnothing\).  Because (1)--(3) reuse every repeated coordinate, the same formulation exactly solves the three-assignment, two-unit pattern in \(\texttt{def:minimal-coupled-pattern}\).

For rational or real-algebraic inputs, feasibility and positivity in (5) are decidable exactly.  Indeed, substitute equality pivots and apply Fourier--Motzkin elimination: at each of finitely many variables, pair every lower bound with every upper bound, retain the resulting finite list of inequalities, decide the required rational or algebraic signs, and then back-substitute.  Applying the same elimination with \(\varepsilon\) retained decides whether a feasible value \(\varepsilon>0\) exists and constructs one.  Thus the finite enumeration in (4), the feasibility tests, and the finite maximum (7) terminate for the input representations stated in the theorem.  For arbitrary real constants with no finite representation, (1)--(7) are instead an exact finite formulation in the ideal ordered-real model and make no Turing-computability assertion.

Treating \(c\) as one additional variable in (3), elimination projects each relatively open cell onto an interval of the \(c\)-line.  The slack test (5) records whether each endpoint is included.  There are finitely many cells and finitely many values (6), so their projected endpoints partition the line into finitely many points and intervals on which \(p^{\mathrm{wc}}_{r,c}(x_0)\) is constant.  This proves the terminating exact evaluation clause in \(\texttt{def:quantile-lower-bound}\) for rational or real-algebraic inputs, and its ideal exact-real interpretation for arbitrary real inputs.

We next prove the projection identity, including its extended-real boundary cases.  For \(j=1,\ldots,N\), define
\[
 \mathcal R_j
 :=\{c\in\mathbb R:p^{\mathrm{wc}}_{N-j,c}(x_0)\leq\alpha\},
 \qquad b_j:=\sup\mathcal R_j,
\tag{8}
\]
where \(\sup\varnothing=-\infty\).  Increasing \(r\) enlarges \(\Theta_{r,c}\), so it cannot decrease the supremum defining \(p^{\mathrm{wc}}_{r,c}\).  Therefore, if \(j\leq k\), then
\[
 p^{\mathrm{wc}}_{N-j,c}(x_0)
 \geq p^{\mathrm{wc}}_{N-k,c}(x_0),
 \qquad \mathcal R_j\subseteq\mathcal R_k,
 \qquad b_j\leq b_k.
\tag{9}
\]
For any \(v\in\mathbb R^N\), with order statistics \(v_{(1)}\leq\cdots\leq v_{(N)}\), direct counting gives, for \(r<N\),
\[
 \sum_{u=1}^N\mathbf 1\{v_u>c\}>r
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 v_{(N-r)}>c.
\tag{10}
\]
Consequently, unless a rejected \(r=N\) null has already contributed the empty set, \(\texttt{def:joint-confidence-region}\), (8), and (10) imply
\[
 \theta\in\mathcal C_\alpha
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \theta_{(j)}(e)>c
 \text{ for every }c\in\mathcal R_j
 \text{ and every }j=1,\ldots,N.
\tag{11}
\]

Suppose first that \(\mathcal C_\alpha\neq\varnothing\).  Equation (11) implies \(\theta_{(k)}(e)\geq b_k\) for every \(\theta\in\mathcal C_\alpha\), hence \(\ell_k(e;\alpha)\geq b_k\).  Nonemptiness rules out \(b_j=+\infty\).  If \(b_k\in\mathbb R\), fix \(\eta>0\), set the first \(k\) ordered effects equal to \(b_k+\eta\), and recursively choose each later effect strictly larger than both its predecessor and \(b_j\).  By (9), the resulting vector satisfies (11).  It is realized by setting \(\theta_u(0,e)=0\) and assigning its ordered coordinates as the values \(\theta_u(1,e)\), with arbitrary completion at other exposures.  Letting \(\eta\downarrow0\) proves \(\ell_k(e;\alpha)\leq b_k\).  If \(b_k=-\infty\), (9) gives \(b_j=-\infty\) for \(j\leq k\); set the first \(k\) effects equal to \(-M\), choose the later effects recursively as above, and let \(M\to\infty\).  Thus
\[
 \ell_k(e;\alpha)=b_k
 \qquad\text{when }\mathcal C_\alpha\neq\varnothing.
\tag{12}
\]

It remains to handle an empty inverted region.  For fixed \(r\), let \(\mathcal Q_r(c)\) be the finite set of assignment-wide rank patterns attained by schedules in \(\Theta_{r,c}(x_0,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})\).  Increasing \(c\) weakens the effect-count restriction, so
\[
 c_1\leq c_2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \mathcal Q_r(c_1)\subseteq\mathcal Q_r(c_2).
\tag{13}
\]
The set \(\mathcal Q_N(c)\) is independent of \(c\), because the count restriction with \(r=N\) is vacuous.  For each of its finitely many patterns, choose one observation-compatible schedule attaining that pattern and let \(M\) be the maximum, over these representatives and units, of the finite direct effects.  For every \(c\geq M\), every chosen representative has zero effects strictly above \(c\), and hence belongs to \(\Theta_{r,c}\) for every \(r\).  Since always \(\Theta_{r,c}\subseteq\Theta_{N,c}\), we obtain the stabilization
\[
 \mathcal Q_r(c)=\mathcal Q_N(c)
 \qquad(c\geq M).
\tag{14}
\]

If some \(\mathcal R_j\) is unbounded above, monotonicity of \(p^{\mathrm{wc}}_{N-j,c}\) in \(c\) implies that it is at most \(\alpha\) for every \(c\).  By (6)--(7) and (14), its stabilized value equals the worst-case value for \(r=N\).  Hence
\[
 p^{\mathrm{wc}}_{N,c}(x_0)\leq\alpha
 \qquad(c\in\mathbb R).
\tag{15}
\]
Thus a rejected \(r=N\) constraint makes \(\mathcal C_\alpha\) empty.  Moreover, \(\Theta_{N-k,c}\subseteq\Theta_{N,c}\), so (15) gives \(\mathcal R_k=\mathbb R\) and \(b_k=+\infty\) for every \(k\).

Conversely, suppose there is no rejected \(r=N\) constraint and every \(b_j<+\infty\).  Choose a finite nondecreasing vector recursively so that its \(j\)-th coordinate is strictly greater than \(b_j\) and its predecessor.  Realize this vector as exposure-\(e\) direct effects as above.  It satisfies (11), proving \(\mathcal C_\alpha\neq\varnothing\).  Therefore every empty-region case forces (15), and then
\[
 \ell_k(e;\alpha)
 =\inf_{\theta\in\varnothing}\theta_{(k)}(e)
 =+\infty=b_k
 \qquad(k=1,\ldots,N).
\tag{16}
\]
Equations (12) and (16) prove \(\ell_k(e;\alpha)=b_k(X;\alpha)\) in all cases, with exactly the empty-set conventions in the definitions.

For joint coverage, let \(Y\) again denote the true fixed schedule and define the deterministic-score randomization value
\[
 p^Y(x):=p_K(x;s_Y).
\tag{17}
\]
The outcome-free kernel, fixed schedule, fixed statistic, subset inequalities, and \(\texttt{thm:universal-validity-iff}\) give
\[
 \Pr_{X\sim\pi}\{p^Y(X)\leq\alpha\}\leq\alpha.
\tag{18}
\]
If \(Y\notin\mathcal C_\alpha\), then for some rejected pair \((r,c)\),
\[
 \sum_{u\in U}\mathbf 1\{\tau_u(e)>c\}\leq r.
\tag{19}
\]
For every realized assignment, the true schedule is observation-consistent; hence (19) puts it in \(\Theta_{r,c}(X,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})\).  The defining supremum then gives
\[
 p^Y(X)\leq p^{\mathrm{wc}}_{r,c}(X)\leq\alpha.
\tag{20}
\]
It follows that \(\{Y\notin\mathcal C_\alpha\}\subseteq\{p^Y(X)\leq\alpha\}\).  By (18), \(\Pr\{Y\in\mathcal C_\alpha\}\geq1-\alpha\).  On this event, the definition of the infimum and the projection identity give, simultaneously for every \(k\),
\[
 \tau_{(k)}(e)
 \geq\inf_{\theta\in\mathcal C_\alpha}\theta_{(k)}(e)
 =\ell_k(e;\alpha).
\tag{21}
\]
This proves the asserted simultaneous coverage.

Finally, the no-interference order-statistic equivalence, the validity of the partition kernels, and the explicit \(1\)-versus-\(1/2\) comparator obstruction are proved in \(\texttt{prop:no-interference-reduction}\).  That proposition shows that the exact finite network reduction and simultaneous projection above are valid, but that equality with the Caughey adjusted-outcome statistic and exact reuse of the Su--Li multiple-choice-knapsack routine are false for the frozen assignment-specific score.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This theorem closes the finite assignment-coupled schedule optimization and simultaneous within-exposure projection while explicitly isolating the comparator mismatch. \textit{Gap.} Existing no-interference reductions do not enforce one shared potential-outcome schedule across network assignments, and their adjusted-outcome scores are not identical to the frozen assignment-specific score. \textit{Consumer.} On a predeclared imputable Cai--Owusu exposure fiber, the theorem yields exact worst-case bounded-null values and simultaneous lower bounds, subject to the separately required empirical fiber and power checks.

\begin{proposition}[prop:asymmetric-isolate-power-gap]\label{prop:asymmetric-isolate-power-gap}
Suppose every self-loop belongs to \(L\), and suppose there are distinct \(x,y\in\Omega\) such that \((x,y)\in L\) and, for every \(z\neq x\), at least one of \((x,z)\) and \((z,x)\) is absent from \(L\).  Take \(q=1\), \(w_1=1\), \(\alpha=\pi(x)\), and a score satisfying \(s^1(x)>s^1(z)=s^1(z')\) for all \(z,z'\in\Omega\setminus\{x\}\).  Define
\[
 K(x,x)=\pi(x),\qquad K(x,y)=1-\pi(x),\qquad K(z,z)=1\ (z\neq x),
\]
with all other entries zero.  Then \(K\in\mathcal K_L\) is a nonpartition kernel,
\[
 d_K(\{x\})=\pi(x),
 \qquad
 d_K(A)=1\quad\text{for every nonempty }A\neq\{x\},
\]
and it is universally valid.  It rejects exactly at \(x\), has \(\operatorname{Pow}(K)=\pi(x)=\alpha\), and is globally catalogue-power optimal.  Every legal partition kernel has power zero, so \(\Delta_{\mathrm{part}}=\pi(x)>0\).  In particular, the uniform three-state directed-cycle support in \(\texttt{prop:worked-nonpartition-power-gap}\) satisfies the premise at each vertex and is a corollary of this support criterion (with the kernel and level constructed here).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Because \(x\neq y\), positivity and \(\sum_{z\in\Omega}\pi(z)=1\) give \(0<\pi(x)<1\).  The displayed row at \(x\) sums to \(\pi(x)+1-\pi(x)=1\), and every other row is an identity row.  All entries are nonnegative.  Its only positive off-diagonal entry is the assumed legal pair \((x,y)\), while every positive diagonal entry uses an assumed legal self-loop.  Thus \(K\in\mathcal K_L\).

For \(A=\{x\}\), the only ordering has prefix mass \(K(x,\{x\})=\pi(x)\), so \(d_K(\{x\})=\pi(x)\).  Now let \(A\neq\{x\}\) be nonempty and fix any ordering \(\sigma\) of \(A\).  If \(x\notin A\), every \(i\in A\) is an identity row, and at the position of any such \(i\),
\[
 K(i,P_j(\sigma))=K(i,i)=1.
\]
If \(x\in A\), then \(A\) contains some \(i\neq x\), and the same equality holds at the position of that \(i\).  Since no row mass can exceed one, every ordering consequently satisfies
\[
 \max_j K(\sigma_j,P_j(\sigma))=1.
\]
Minimizing over orderings proves \(d_K(A)=1\).  Hence \(d_K(A)\geq\pi(A)\) for every nonempty \(A\), and \(\texttt{thm:universal-validity-iff}\) proves universal validity.

Under the stated tied score, the upper-tail set at \(x\) is \(\{x\}\), whereas the upper-tail set at every \(z\neq x\) is all of \(\Omega\).  Therefore
\[
 p_K(x;s^1)=K(x,\{x\})=\pi(x)=\alpha,
 \qquad
 p_K(z;s^1)=K(z,\Omega)=1>\alpha\quad(z\neq x).
\]
The rejection set is exactly \(\{x\}\), and its design probability and catalogue power are both \(\pi(x)=\alpha\).  Universal validity bounds the rejection probability of every universally valid competitor at this score and level by \(\alpha\), so this \(K\) attains the global optimum.

For any partition kernel supported on \(L\), the cell containing \(x\) cannot contain a state \(z\neq x\): if it did, positivity would make both conditional transitions \(x\to z\) and \(z\to x\) positive, contrary to the no-mutual-neighbor premise.  Thus \(x\) is a singleton cell.  The partition upper-tail value at \(x\) is consequently one.  At every other state the upper-tail set is \(\Omega\), so its value is also one.  Since \(\alpha<1\), every legal partition kernel has power zero.  Moreover, the constructed kernel has \(K(x,y)=1-\pi(x)>0\), while every legal partition kernel has zero at \((x,y)\); hence it is not a partition kernel.  It follows that \(\Delta_{\mathrm{part}}=\pi(x)>0\).

Finally, on the uniform three-state directed cycle every self-loop is legal, no distinct pair has both directions legal, and one may take, for example, \(x=1\) and \(y=2\).  Substitution into the displayed construction proves the stated corollary.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} A support-level condition, rather than a hand-picked matrix, certifies a universally valid nonpartition design with a matching global power upper bound. \textit{Gap.} A partition must isolate any vertex having no mutual off-diagonal neighbor, whereas a legal one-way outgoing edge can still carry resampling mass in a universally valid row kernel. \textit{Consumer.} An empirical legal fiber can be screened for the asymmetric-isolate pattern before running the full mixed-integer optimizer.

\begin{theorem}[thm:network-schedule-reduction-answer]\label{thm:network-schedule-reduction-answer}
For every row-stochastic, outcome-free kernel \(K\) satisfying \(d_K(A)\geq\pi(A)\) for every nonempty \(A\subseteq\Omega\), the weak-order schedule handle has an exact finite-cell formulation.  It computes \(p^{\mathrm{wc}}_{r,c}(X)\) and an optimizing compatible schedule, when one exists, by at most \(2^N3^{m\binom N2}\) slack-certified linear feasibility problems.  For rational or real-algebraic inputs this computation terminates exactly; arbitrary real inputs admit only the corresponding ideal exact-real formulation.  The formulation solves the three-assignment shared-coordinate pattern and its nested inversion satisfies
\[
 \ell_k(e;\alpha)=b_k(X;\alpha),
 \qquad
 \Pr_{X\sim\pi}\!\left\{\tau_{(k)}(e)\geq\ell_k(e;\alpha)
 \text{ for every }k=1,\ldots,N\right\}\geq1-\alpha.
\]
The no-interference order-statistic interpretation and finite-sample validity of the two partition comparators are retained.  Exact recovery of the Caughey adjusted-outcome statistic and of the Su--Li multiple-choice-knapsack routine is false for the frozen assignment-specific score, as witnessed by the two-unit \(1\)-versus-\(1/2\) example.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The exact finite-cell formulation, its bound of \(2^N3^{m\binom N2}\) labels, attainment and infeasibility alternatives, exact-input qualifications, solution of the shared-coordinate pattern, projection identity, and simultaneous coverage are precisely the conclusions proved in \(\texttt{thm:exact-network-schedule-reduction}\).  The final clauses of that theorem, proved through \(\texttt{prop:no-interference-reduction}\), establish the no-interference order-statistic equivalence and partition-kernel validity and exhibit the stated \(1\)-versus-\(1/2\) counterexample.  Hence the open-ended question has the asserted positive answer for exact network schedule reduction and simultaneous inversion, and the asserted negative answer for exact recovery of the two comparator algorithms.
\end{proof}

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The exact schedule evaluator is finite but exponential, with at most \(2^N3^{m\binom N2}\) raw effect and weak-order labels before pruning. Its threshold projections are intervals, but their number can still be exponential. Ordinary terminating computation requires rational or real-algebraic representations of kernel weights, outcomes, thresholds, levels, and the finite rank-score table; arbitrary real constants support only an ideal exact-real formulation. The universal-validity geometry does not guarantee useful power on every legal support. The prospective Cai application must stop if its imputable fiber is too small, its legal support forces partition kernels, or its certified partition power gap is zero.

\section{Honest scope}

The universal equivalence, recursion, peeling equivalence, polyhedral representation, finite kernel optimizer, generic asymmetric-isolate power certificate, strict three-state inclusion and power witness, minimal coupled pattern, and composite-null validity are theorem-level results in finite design-based randomization inference. The asymmetric-isolate family proves a strict partition gap whenever its stated support pattern occurs; it does not assert that an actual Cai--Owusu fiber has that pattern or a positive optimized gap. The network schedule problem is closed by finite weak-order enumeration and positive-slack linear feasibility; empty null schedule sets receive worst-case value zero. Projecting the finite cells onto the threshold line gives exact endpoint evaluation of \(b_k\), and nested inversion gives exact simultaneous lower bounds at one predeclared exposure, including \(\inf\varnothing=+\infty\) and \(\sup\varnothing=-\infty\). The no-interference count null is exactly an order-statistic null and partition kernels remain valid, but the frozen assignment-specific score does not equal the Caughey adjusted-outcome statistic or the Su--Li knapsack statistic. Cross-exposure simultaneity and an actual Cai--Owusu power gain remain outside the theorem and require, respectively, explicit level allocation and empirical fiber computation. Ordinary exact computation assumes rational or real-algebraic input representations; arbitrary real constants are covered only in an ideal exact-real-arithmetic model.
