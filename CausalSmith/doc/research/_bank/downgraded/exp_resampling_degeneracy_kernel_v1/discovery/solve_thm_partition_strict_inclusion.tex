Let \(K\in\mathcal P_L\).  By the definition of \(\mathcal P_L\), there is a finite partition \(\mathcal G\) of \(\Omega\) such that, for the unique cell \(G_x\in\mathcal G\) containing \(x\),
\[
K(x,y)=\frac{\pi(y)\mathbf 1\{y\in G_x\}}{\pi(G_x)}.
\]
Every cell is nonempty, so \(\pi(G)>0\) for every \(G\in\mathcal G\) by \(\pi(x)>0\) from Assumption \(\texttt{ass:positive-finite-design}\); thus every displayed division is well defined.  Fix a nonempty set \(A\subseteq\Omega\) and an ordering \(\sigma\in\operatorname{Ord}(A)\).  For each cell \(G\) with \(A\cap G\neq\varnothing\), let \(i_G\) be the last member of \(A\cap G\) in the ordering \(\sigma\), and let \(P(i_G)\) be the prefix ending at \(i_G\).  Since \(A\cap G\subseteq P(i_G)\) and \(G_{i_G}=G\), Definition \(\texttt{def:kernel-tail-value}\)'s row-mass notation and the partition formula give
\[
K\bigl(i_G,P(i_G)\bigr)
=\frac{\pi\bigl(P(i_G)\cap G\bigr)}{\pi(G)}
\geq \frac{\pi(A\cap G)}{\pi(G)}.
\]
Put \(r_G=\pi(A\cap G)/\pi(G)\) for every \(G\in\mathcal G\), taking \(r_G=0\) when \(A\cap G=\varnothing\).  Because the cells partition \(\Omega\),
\[
\pi(A)=\sum_{G\in\mathcal G}\pi(A\cap G)
=\sum_{G\in\mathcal G}\pi(G)r_G
\leq \max_{G\in\mathcal G}r_G,
\]
where the last inequality uses \(\sum_{G\in\mathcal G}\pi(G)=\pi(\Omega)=1\).  Consequently,
\[
\max_{1\leq j\leq |A|}K\bigl(\sigma_j,P_j(\sigma)\bigr)
\geq \max_{G\in\mathcal G}r_G
\geq \pi(A).
\]
This holds for every ordering \(\sigma\).  Taking the minimum over orderings in Definition \(\texttt{def:subset-degeneracy}\) yields \(d_K(A)\geq\pi(A)\).  Since \(A\) was arbitrary, Theorem \(\texttt{thm:universal-validity-iff}\) proves that \(K\) is universally valid.

It remains to prove that the inclusion is strict.  Let \(\Omega=\{1,2,3\}\), let \(\pi(i)=1/3\) for every \(i\), let \(L=\Omega\times\Omega\), and set
\[
K=\begin{pmatrix}
2/3&1/3&0\\
0&2/3&1/3\\
1/3&0&2/3
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
All entries are nonnegative, every row sums to one, and complete \(L\) makes every positive entry legal; hence \(K\in\mathcal K_L\) by Definition \(\texttt{def:legal-kernel-class}\).  For a singleton \(\{i\}\), the sole ordering gives
\[
d_K(\{i\})=K(i,i)=\frac23\geq\frac13=\pi(\{i\}).
\]
For a two-point set \(A\), the first prefix under every ordering is a singleton and therefore has row mass \(2/3\), so \(d_K(A)\geq2/3\).  Conversely, write the directed cycle as \(1\to2\to3\to1\).  Exactly one of the two vertices of \(A\) has its off-diagonal mass \(1/3\) directed to the omitted vertex; place that vertex last.  Its full-prefix mass is then \(2/3\), while the first vertex's singleton-prefix mass is \(2/3\).  This ordering has maximum prefix mass \(2/3\), and hence
\[
d_K(A)=\frac23=\pi(A).
\]
Finally, if \(A=\Omega\), the last prefix equals \(\Omega\), and Assumption \(\texttt{ass:row-stochastic}\) gives row mass one.  Thus
\[
d_K(\Omega)=1=\pi(\Omega).
\]
Theorem \(\texttt{thm:universal-validity-iff}\) now proves universal validity of this cyclic kernel.

Suppose, toward a contradiction, that this kernel belongs to \(\mathcal P_L\), and let \(\mathcal G\) be a representing partition.  The strict inequality \(K(1,2)>0\) in the partition formula forces \(1\) and \(2\) into the same cell.  Likewise \(K(2,3)>0\) forces \(2\) and \(3\) into the same cell.  Transitivity of membership in a partition cell therefore puts all three states in one cell, namely \(\Omega\).  The partition formula and uniformity of \(\pi\) would then imply \(K(x,y)=\pi(y)/\pi(\Omega)=1/3\) for every \(x,y\), contradicting \(K(1,1)=2/3\).  Hence the cyclic kernel is not a partition kernel, which proves strict inclusion.
