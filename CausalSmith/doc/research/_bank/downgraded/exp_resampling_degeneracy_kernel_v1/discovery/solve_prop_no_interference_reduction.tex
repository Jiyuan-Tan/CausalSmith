Because (Y_u(a,e')) is constant in (e'\in\mathcal E), the definition of (\tau_u(e)) gives the ordinary individual treatment effect, independent of the exposure label.  Write
\[
 \tau_{(1)}(e)\leq\cdots\leq\tau_{(N)}(e)
\]
for the ordered coordinates.  Fix (0\leq r<N).  Since (U=\{1,\ldots,N\}),
\[
 \sum_{u\in U}\mathbf 1\{Y_u(1,e)-Y_u(0,e)>c\}\leq r
\]
holds if and only if at least (N-r) coordinates of (\tau(e)) are at most (c).  By the definition of the ((N-r))-th order statistic, this is equivalent to
\[
 \tau_{(N-r)}(e)\leq c.
\]
This also verifies the boundary convention: an effect equal to (c) is not counted by the strict inequality.  If (r=N), each of the (N) indicators is at most one, so their sum is always at most (N); hence that null is vacuous.

We next verify the universal-validity certificate for the stated partition kernel.  Let (\mathcal G) be either of the partitions in the proposition, and, for each (x\in\Omega), define
\[
 K(x,B)=\frac{\pi(B\cap G_x)}{\pi(G_x)},\qquad B\subseteq\Omega.
\]
Every cell (H\in\mathcal G) is nonempty.  By \(\texttt{ass:positive-finite-design}\), (\pi(z)>0) for every (z\in\Omega), so (\pi(H)>0) and the quotient is well defined.  It is nonnegative, and
\[
 \sum_{y\in\Omega}K(x,y)
 =\frac{\pi(G_x)}{\pi(G_x)}=1.
\]
Thus it satisfies \(\texttt{ass:row-stochastic}\).

Fix a nonempty (A\subseteq\Omega) and an arbitrary ordering (\sigma\in\operatorname{Ord}(A)).  For each cell (H\in\mathcal G) with (A\cap H\neq\varnothing), let (j_H) be the position of the last element of (A\cap H) in (\sigma).  Every member of (A\cap H) has appeared by time (j_H), and every prefix is contained in (A), so
\[
 P_{j_H}(\sigma)\cap H=A\cap H.
\]
Moreover, (\sigma_{j_H}\in H), whence (G_{\sigma_{j_H}}=H), and therefore
\[
 K\bigl(\sigma_{j_H},P_{j_H}(\sigma)\bigr)
 =\frac{\pi(A\cap H)}{\pi(H)}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \max_{1\leq j\leq |A|}K\bigl(\sigma_j,P_j(\sigma)\bigr)
 &\geq \max_{H\in\mathcal G}\frac{\pi(A\cap H)}{\pi(H)}\\
 &\geq \sum_{H\in\mathcal G}\pi(H)
       \frac{\pi(A\cap H)}{\pi(H)}\\
 &=\sum_{H\in\mathcal G}\pi(A\cap H)
 =\pi(A).
 \end{aligned}
\]
For a cell disjoint from (A), the corresponding ratio in the maximum and sum is understood as zero.  The second inequality holds because the nonnegative weights (\pi(H)) sum to one, and the last equality holds because the cells partition (\Omega).  Since (\sigma) was arbitrary, \(\texttt{def:subset-degeneracy}\) yields
\[
 d_K(A)=\min_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Ord}(A)}
       \max_{1\leq j\leq |A|}K\bigl(\sigma_j,P_j(\sigma)\bigr)
 \geq\pi(A).
\]
This argument applies to both the one-cell exchangeable complete-randomization design and the partition induced by a product of exchangeable fixed-treatment-count within-stratum randomizations.

By \(\texttt{thm:universal-validity-iff}\), this certificate makes the partition kernel universally valid.  Under \(\texttt{ass:fixed-potential-outcomes}\), the schedule is fixed and (X\sim\pi) is the only random object.  The common peer exposure, legal-comparison imputability, fixed weak-order tie rule, and pre-outcome choice of ((L,K)) are precisely \(\texttt{ass:fixed-peer-exposure}\), \(\texttt{ass:statistic-imputability}\), \(\texttt{ass:fixed-rank-statistic}\), and \(\texttt{ass:outcome-free-kernel}\).  Therefore \(\texttt{thm:composite-null-validity}\), with (T=T_{\mathrm C}) or (T=T_{\mathrm S}), respectively, gives, for every (\alpha\in[0,1]),
\[
 Y\in\Theta_{r,c}(X,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \Pr_{X\sim\pi}\!\left\{p^{\mathrm{wc}}_{r,c}(X)\leq\alpha\right\}
 \leq\alpha.
\]
This is finite-sample validity of the frozen schedule-supremum test for its own score map.  Separately, the comparator-specific individual and simultaneous confidence conclusions are exactly the conclusions of the cited leaves \(\texttt{lem:caughey-quantile-specialization}\) and \(\texttt{lem:suli-knapsack-specialization}\).  Those cited results concern their own comparator constructions and do not identify either construction with \(\texttt{def:schedule-score}\).

It remains to prove the claimed obstruction to such an identification.  Take (N=2), let (x_i) denote the assignment treating unit (i) and not the other unit, and set
\[
 \Omega=\{x_1,x_2\},\qquad
 \pi(x_1)=\pi(x_2)=\frac12,
 \qquad K(x_i,x_j)=\frac12\quad(1\leq i,j\leq2).
\]
This is the partition kernel for the one-cell partition.  For a singleton (A=\{x_i\}), \(\texttt{def:subset-degeneracy}\) gives (d_K(A)=1/2=\pi(A)).  For (A=\Omega), either ordering has prefix masses (1/2) and (1), so (d_K(\Omega)=1=\pi(\Omega)).  Thus even this smallest complete-randomization kernel meets the universal-validity certificate exactly.

Choose (\phi(j)=j), set (T=T_{\mathrm C}), and, for every (e'\in\mathcal E), take the fixed no-interference schedule
\[
 \theta_1(0,e')=1,\quad \theta_1(1,e')=3,
 \qquad
 \theta_2(0,e')=0,\quad \theta_2(1,e')=2.
\]
Let this be the true schedule and suppose (X=x_1).  Then (Y^{\mathrm{obs}}=(3,0)).  Under (x_1), the schedule-imputed outcome vector is ((3,0)), whose treated unit has ascending rank two.  Under (x_2), it is ((1,2)), whose treated unit also has ascending rank two.  There are no ties.  Hence \(\texttt{def:caughey-rank-score}\) and \(\texttt{def:schedule-score}\) give
\[
 s_\theta(x_1)=2=s_\theta(x_2).
\]
Using \(\texttt{def:kernel-tail-value}\),
\[
 p_K(x_1;s_\theta)
 =\frac12\mathbf 1\{2\geq2\}
  +\frac12\mathbf 1\{2\geq2\}=1.
\]
Both direct effects equal two, so
\[
 \sum_{u=1}^{2}\mathbf 1\{\theta_u(1,e)-\theta_u(0,e)>2\}=0.
\]
The schedule is consistent with ((x_1,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})); thus \(\texttt{def:bounded-null-schedules}\) gives (\theta\in\Theta_{0,2}(x_1,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})).  By \(\texttt{def:worst-case-pvalue}\),
\[
 p^{\mathrm{wc}}_{0,2}(x_1)\geq p_K(x_1;s_\theta)=1.
\]
Every kernel tail value is at most one by nonnegativity and row-stochasticity, so the supremum is also at most one.  Therefore
\[
 p^{\mathrm{wc}}_{0,2}(x_1)=1.
\]

For the Caughey adjusted-outcome upper-tail construction at the boundary (c=2), instead hold fixed across reallocations
\[
 a=Y^{\mathrm{obs}}-2\bigl(Z_1(x_1),Z_2(x_1)\bigr)=(1,0).
\]
The treated unit has ascending rank two under (x_1) and rank one under (x_2).  Thus
\[
 T_{\mathrm C}(x_1,a)=2,
 \qquad T_{\mathrm C}(x_2,a)=1,
\]
and its upper-tail randomization value at (x_1) is
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^{2}\pi(x_j)
 \mathbf 1\!\left\{T_{\mathrm C}(x_j,a)\geq T_{\mathrm C}(x_1,a)\right\}
 =\frac12.
\]
This differs from the frozen worst-case value one.  Finally, take (S=1), (\mathcal U=\{U\}), and (\phi_1=\phi).  By \(\texttt{def:suli-stratified-rank-score}\), the within-stratum ranks then equal the full-sample ranks and (T_{\mathrm S}=T_{\mathrm C}); the same (1)-versus-(1/2) discrepancy follows.  The witness proves that neither exact equality with the Caughey adjusted-outcome test nor exact reuse of the Su--Li multiple-choice-knapsack optimization follows from the frozen score construction, while the order-statistic equivalence and both finite-sample validity conclusions proved above remain intact.
