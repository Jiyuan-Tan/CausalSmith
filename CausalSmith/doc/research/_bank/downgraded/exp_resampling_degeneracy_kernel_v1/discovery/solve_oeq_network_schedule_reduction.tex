%%% FIELD theorem-statement
Assume that \(K\) is row-stochastic, that \((L,K)\) is fixed before observing \(Y^{\mathrm{obs}}\), and that
\[
d_K(A)\geq \pi(A)\qquad
(\varnothing\neq A\subseteq\Omega).
\]
Fix a realized assignment \(x_0\in\Omega\), a count \(r\in\{0,\ldots,N\}\), and a threshold \(c\in\mathbb R\).  Under fixed peer exposure, imputability, and the fixed rank-statistic rule, the following finite formulation is exact.  Introduce the \(2N\) variables \(z_{ua}=\theta_u(a,e)\).  Enumerate \(h\in\{0,1\}^{U}\) with \(\sum_u h_u\leq r\), and enumerate
\[
\lambda_{xuv}\in\{-1,0,1\},qquad
x\in\Omega,\quad 1\leq u<v\leq N,
\]
where \(h_u=1\) represents \(z_{u1}-z_{u0}>c\) and \(\lambda_{xuv}\) is the sign of \(z_{u,Z_u(x)}-z_{v,Z_v(x)}\).  For each of the at most
\(2^N3^{m\binom N2}\) labels, impose the observation equalities, the indicated strict or weak effect inequalities, and the indicated sign equalities or inequalities.  A label having strict rows \(a_j^{\mathsf T}z<b_j\) is feasible if and only if the auxiliary linear program
\[
\max_{z,\varepsilon}\ \varepsilon
\quad\text{subject to}\quad
a_j^{\mathsf T}z\leq b_j-\varepsilon\ (j),
\quad Cz\leq d,
\quad Ez=f,
\quad 0\leq\varepsilon\leq1
\]
has optimum strictly positive; if there is no strict row, ordinary feasibility is used.  The fixed tie rule turns each feasible \(\lambda\) into ranks and hence into constants \(t_\lambda(x)=T(x,Y_\theta(x))\).  Consequently
\[
p^{\mathrm{wc}}_{r,c}(x_0)
=max_{(h,\lambda)\ {m feasible}}
\sum_{y\in\Omega}K(x_0,y)
\mathbf 1\{t_\lambda(y)\geq t_\lambda(x_0)\}.
\]
If \(\Theta_{r,c}(x_0,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})\neq\varnothing\), the maximum is attained and the feasibility program returns an optimizing compatible schedule; otherwise the formulation returns an infeasibility certificate.  With rational or real-algebraic representations of \(K\), \(Y^{\mathrm{obs}}\), \(c\), \(\alpha\), and the finite rank-score table, this is a terminating exact computation.  For arbitrary unspecified real inputs it is a finite formulation in ideal exact-real arithmetic, not a Turing-algorithm claim.  The same finite formulation applies to, and therefore exactly solves, the three-assignment, two-unit shared-coordinate pattern.

For \(1\leq k\leq N\), define in the extended real line
\[
b_k(x_0;\alpha)
=\sup\{c\in\mathbb R:
p^{\mathrm{wc}}_{N-k,c}(x_0)\leq\alpha\},
\qquad \sup\varnothing=-\infty.
\]
The nested finite-cell computations give the exact projection identity
\[
\ell_k(e;\alpha)=b_k(X;\alpha),
\qquad k=1,\ldots,N,
\]
where \(\inf\varnothing=+infty\).  In particular,
\[
\Pr_{X\sim\pi}\!\left\{
\tau_{(k)}(e)\geq\ell_k(e;\alpha)
\text{ for every }k=1,\ldots,N
\right\}\geq1-\alpha.
\]
If the inverted region is empty because rejected thresholds are unbounded, the finite set of attainable rank-score patterns stabilizes for large \(c\); this forces rejection at \(r=N\), and hence \(b_k=+\infty\) for every \(k\), so the displayed projection identity remains valid.

Under no interference, the order-statistic equivalence
\[
\sum_{u\in U}\mathbf 1\{\tau_u(e)>c\}\leq r
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\tau_{(N-r)}(e)\leq c
\qquad(r<N)
\]
is exact, and partition kernels retain finite-sample validity.  Nevertheless, the frozen assignment-specific schedule score does not exactly recover either the Caughey--Dafoe--Li--Miratrix adjusted-outcome statistic or the Su--Li multiple-choice-knapsack statistic.  In the two-unit, one-treated partition example with \(\phi(q)=q\), potential outcomes \((1,3)\) for unit one and \((0,2)\) for unit two, and observed assignment treating unit one, the frozen bounded-null value at \((r,c)=(0,2)\) is \(1\), whereas the Caughey adjusted-outcome upper-tail value is \(1/2\); the one-stratum Su--Li clause contains the same obstruction.  Thus the exact network schedule reduction and simultaneous projection are positive, but the requested exact recovery and strict-extension claims for those two comparator algorithms are false.

%%% FIELD theorem-proof
Write \(x_0\) for the realized value of \(X\).  By fixed peer exposure, every focal outcome used on the fiber has the form
\[
Y_\theta(x)_u=z_{u,Z_u(x)},
\qquad z_{ua}:=\theta_u(a,e),
\]
and observation consistency is precisely
\[
z_{u,Z_u(x_0)}=Y^{\mathrm{obs}}_u,
\qquad u\in U.
\tag{1}
\]
Coordinates of \(\theta\) outside exposure \(e\) do not enter any fiber score and may be completed arbitrarily.  This uses the fixed-exposure assumption, while legal imputability ensures that every coordinate displayed above is available in every comparison used by \(T\).

For each \(u\), split the real line into the two disjoint cases
\[
h_u=1:\ z_{u1}-z_{u0}>c,
\qquad
h_u=0:\ z_{u1}-z_{u0}\leq c.
\tag{2}
\]
Then the bounded null is exactly \(\sum_u h_u\leq r\), including equality at the threshold in the \(h_u=0\) case.  For each \(x\in\Omega\) and \(u<v\), split the difference
\[
D_{xuv}:=z_{u,Z_u(x)}-z_{v,Z_v(x)}
\tag{3}
\]
into \(D_{xuv}<0\), \(D_{xuv}=0\), or \(D_{xuv}>0\).  Equations (1)--(3), over every binary label \(h\) and ternary label \(\lambda\), form a disjoint cover of the compatible schedule set.  There are no more than
\[
2^N3^{|\Omega|\binom N2}=2^N3^{m\binom N2}
\tag{4}
\]
such labels.  Reusing the same \(z_{ua}\) in (1)--(3) across every assignment enforces all shared-potential-outcome equalities; no product relaxation has been made.

Fix one label.  After reversing the sign of each strict lower inequality if necessary, write its strict rows as \(a_j^{\mathsf T}z<b_j\), its weak rows as \(Cz\leq d\), and its equalities as \(Ez=f\).  If the cell contains \(z^\star\), the finitely many strict slacks have a positive minimum \(\delta\).  Hence \((z^\star,\min\{1,\delta\})\) is feasible for the auxiliary program and its optimum is positive.  Conversely, any feasible pair with \(\varepsilon>0\) obeys every original strict row.  Thus
\[
\{z:a_j^{\mathsf T}z<b_j, Cz\leq d, Ez=f\}\neq\varnothing
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\varepsilon^\star>0.
\tag{5}
\]
When the set of strict rows is empty, (5) is replaced by feasibility of the displayed closed system.  This proves the feasibility certificate without imposing artificial bounds or a big-\(M\) constant.

The ternary signs in (3), together with the predeclared deterministic tie order, fix the complete rank vector at every assignment.  Because \(T\) is a fixed rank statistic, it is constant on each such cell; denote its value at \(x\) by \(t_\lambda(x)\).  The kernel tail value on the cell is therefore the constant
\[
W_\lambda(x_0)
:=\sum_{y\in\Omega}K(x_0,y)
\mathbf 1\{t_\lambda(y)\geq t_\lambda(x_0)\}.
\tag{6}
\]
The cells cover \(\Theta_{r,c}(x_0,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})\), so taking the supremum in the definition of \(p^{\mathrm{wc}}_{r,c}\) gives
\[
\sup_{\theta\in\Theta_{r,c}(x_0,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})}p_K(x_0;s_\theta)
=\max_{(h,\lambda):\,\text{cell nonempty}}W_\lambda(x_0).
\tag{7}
\]
There are finitely many terms in (7).  A nonempty maximizing cell supplies a feasible \(z\) by (5), and arbitrary completion outside \(e\) supplies an optimizing schedule \(\theta\).  If no cell is feasible, the compatible set is empty and the enumeration certifies that fact.  In particular, (1)--(7) retain the repeated coordinates in the three-state, two-unit pattern, so that pattern is solved exactly; its claimed minimality and connected shared-coordinate structure are supplied by the established minimal-coupling proposition.

For rational or real-algebraic inputs, every comparison and arithmetic operation in (1)--(7) is exact.  A direct elimination algorithm terminates: substitute equality pivots, eliminate one inequality variable at a time by pairing every lower bound with every upper bound, decide the resulting finitely many algebraic signs, and back-substitute.  Applying the same elimination to \(\varepsilon\) decides whether a feasible value with \(\varepsilon>0\) exists and constructs one.  Thus finite enumeration plus elimination is a Turing procedure for exactly represented rational or algebraic data.  With arbitrary real constants lacking finite representations, equations (1)--(7) remain an exact finite reduction only in an ideal ordered-real computation model.

The same argument computes the continuum inversion with finitely many operations.  Treat \(c\) as one additional variable in (2).  For each fixed label, eliminating \(z\) projects its relatively open polyhedron onto an interval in the \(c\)-line, with open or closed endpoints recorded by the slack test (5).  Linear optimization or the just-described elimination computes its algebraic endpoints.  The finitely many interval endpoints and the finitely many values (6) partition the line into finitely many points and intervals on which \(p^{\mathrm{wc}}_{r,c}(x_0)\) is constant.  Exact endpoint evaluation therefore computes every \(b_k(x_0;\alpha)\).

We next prove that these computed endpoints are exactly the projections of \(\mathcal C_\alpha\), including the empty-region case.  For \(j=1,\ldots,N\), put
\[
\mathcal R_j:=\{c:p^{\mathrm{wc}}_{N-j,c}(x_0)\leq\alpha\},
\qquad b_j:=\sup\mathcal R_j.
\tag{8}
\]
Increasing \(r\) enlarges \(\Theta_{r,c}\), so it cannot decrease the worst-case value.  Hence, if \(j\leq k\), then
\[
p^{\mathrm{wc}}_{N-j,c}(x_0)
\geq p^{\mathrm{wc}}_{N-k,c}(x_0),
\quad
\mathcal R_j\subseteq\mathcal R_k,
\quad
b_j\leq b_k.
\tag{9}
\]
For any vector \(v\in\mathbb R^N\), written in increasing order as \(v_{(1)}\leq\cdots\leq v_{(N)}\), elementary counting gives, for \(r<N\),
\[
\sum_{u=1}^N\mathbf 1\{v_u>c\}>r
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
v_{(N-r)}>c.
\tag{10}
\]
Thus, unless a rejected \(r=N\) null has already made the intersection empty, the definition of \(\mathcal C_\alpha\), (8), and (10) give
\[
\theta\in\mathcal C_\alpha
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\theta_{(j)}(e)>c
\text{ for every }c\in\mathcal R_j
\text{ and every }j=1,\ldots,N.
\tag{11}
\]

Suppose first that \(\mathcal C_\alpha\neq\varnothing\).  Equation (11) implies \(\theta_{(k)}(e)\geq b_k\) for every \(\theta\in\mathcal C_\alpha\), so \(\ell_k(e;\alpha)\geq b_k\).  If \(b_k\) is finite, (9) permits a nondecreasing effect vector whose first \(k\) coordinates equal \(b_k+\eta\), with later coordinates chosen recursively above the corresponding finite \(b_j\).  It satisfies (11), and it is realized by setting \(\theta_u(0,e)=0\) and \(\theta_u(1,e)\) equal to its coordinates.  Letting \(\eta\downarrow0\) proves \(\ell_k(e;\alpha)\leq b_k\).  If \(b_k=-\infty\), then (9) makes \(\mathcal R_j\) empty for \(j\leq k\); choosing the first \(k\) effects equal to \(-M\) and letting \(M\to\infty\) proves the same equality.  Therefore
\[
\ell_k(e;\alpha)=b_k
\quad\text{whenever }\mathcal C_\alpha\neq\varnothing.
\tag{12}
\]

It remains to verify (12) when the confidence region is empty.  For fixed \(r\), let \(\mathcal Q_r(c)\) be the finite set of assignment-wide rank patterns attainable by schedules in \(\Theta_{r,c}(x_0,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})\).  Since increasing \(c\) weakens the exceedance-count restriction,
\[
c_1\leq c_2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathcal Q_r(c_1)\subseteq\mathcal Q_r(c_2).
\tag{13}
\]
Every observation-compatible finite schedule belongs to \(\Theta_{r,c}\) once \(c\) exceeds its largest direct effect.  Because the total set of rank patterns is finite, there is a finite \(c_0\) such that
\[
\mathcal Q_r(c)=\mathcal Q_N(c)
\quad\text{for every }c\geq c_0.
\tag{14}
\]
This is the required finite-cell stabilization.

If some \(\mathcal R_j\) is unbounded above, monotonicity in \(c\) implies \(p^{\mathrm{wc}}_{N-j,c}(x_0)\leq\alpha\) for every \(c\).  By (6), (7), and (14), its stabilized value is the worst-case value over all compatible schedules, which is exactly the value for \(r=N\).  Therefore
\[
p^{\mathrm{wc}}_{N,c}(x_0)\leq\alpha
\quad\text{for every }c.
\tag{15}
\]
The rejected \(r=N\) constraint in \(\mathcal C_\alpha\) is the empty set.  Moreover, every \(\Theta_{N-k,c}\) is contained in \(\Theta_{N,c}\), so (15) implies \(\mathcal R_k=\mathbb R\) and \(b_k=+\infty\) for all \(k\).  Conversely, if there is no rejected \(r=N\) constraint and every \(b_j<+\infty\), one can recursively choose a nondecreasing finite vector strictly above all thresholds in the sets \(\mathcal R_j\), realize it as a schedule, and satisfy (11); hence the region is nonempty.  Thus every empty-region case gives
\[
\ell_k(e;\alpha)=\inf_{\theta\in\varnothing}\theta_{(k)}(e)
=+\infty=b_k,
\qquad k=1,\ldots,N.
\tag{16}
\]
Equations (12) and (16) prove the projection identity in all cases.

For simultaneous coverage, let \(Y\) denote the true fixed schedule and set
\[
p^Y(x):=p_K(x;s_Y).
\tag{17}
\]
The outcome-free kernel, fixed schedule, and fixed statistic make (17) a deterministic score-vector randomization value.  The subset inequalities and the universal-validity theorem imply
\[
\Pr_{X\sim\pi}\{p^Y(X)\leq\alpha\}\leq\alpha.
\tag{18}
\]
If \(Y\notin\mathcal C_\alpha\), then some rejected pair \((r,c)\) satisfies
\[
\sum_{u\in U}\mathbf 1\{\tau_u(e)>c\}\leq r.
\tag{19}
\]
At every realized assignment, observation consistency and (19) put the true schedule in \(\Theta_{r,c}(X,Y^{\mathrm{obs}})\).  The defining supremum therefore gives
\[
p^Y(X)\leq p^{\mathrm{wc}}_{r,c}(X)\leq\alpha.
\tag{20}
\]
Thus \(\{Y\notin\mathcal C_\alpha\}\subseteq\{p^Y(X)\leq\alpha\}\), and (18) yields \(\Pr\{Y\in\mathcal C_\alpha\}\geq1-\alpha\).  On this event, the definition of an infimum gives
\[
\tau_{(k)}(e)=Y_{(k)}(e)
\geq\inf_{\theta\in\mathcal C_\alpha}\theta_{(k)}(e)
=\ell_k(e;\alpha)
\quad\text{for all }k,
\tag{21}
\]
which proves the asserted joint coverage.  This common-sharp-schedule domination also contains the established per-null composite validity as a special case.

Finally, (10) proves the stated no-interference order-statistic equivalence, and partition kernels remain valid by the universal-validity characterization.  Exact equality with the two cited comparator statistics is a different assertion.  The comparator-obstruction proposition supplies the two-unit, one-treated example stated in the theorem: the frozen score uses assignment-specific outcome vectors \((3,0)\) and \((1,2)\), giving treated rank two at both assignments and hence tail mass \(1\), while subtraction of the boundary value \(2\) fixes the adjusted vector \((1,0)\), giving treated ranks two and one and tail mass \(1/2\).  The one-stratum Su--Li case contains the same example.  Therefore the finite network reduction, order-statistic equivalence, validity, and simultaneous projection are proved, while exact recovery of those comparator algorithms, and hence the claimed strict extension of both, is refuted.
