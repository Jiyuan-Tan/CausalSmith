%%% FIELD proof-block-reduction
Fix \(m\in\mathcal M_n(B)\) and a block \(B_l\).  By
\(\mathrm{ass{:}partition}\), the block is nonempty and the blocks are
pairwise disjoint.  By \(\mathrm{ass{:}balanced\text{-}bernoulli}\), every
one of the \(2^{r_l}\) assignments on \(B_l\) has probability
\(2^{-r_l}=q_l\).  Exactly one such assignment is all treated, exactly one
is all controlled, and all remaining assignments are mixed.  Therefore
\[
 \mathbb P_Z(A_l=1)=q_l,\qquad
 \mathbb P_Z(C_l=1)=q_l,\qquad
 \mathbb P_Z(H_l=1)=1-2q_l,
 \tag{1}
\]
and \(A_lC_l=0\) for every assignment.  Each triple
\((A_l,C_l,H_l)\) is a function only of \((Z_i:i\in B_l)\).  These
coordinate subvectors are independent for distinct blocks by
\(\mathrm{ass{:}partition}\) and
\(\mathrm{ass{:}balanced\text{-}bernoulli}\); hence the triples are
independent across \(l\).

Write \(u_l=\mu_l(a)\) and \(v_l=\mu_l(b)\).  The schedule is fixed by
\(\mathrm{ass{:}fixed\text{-}schedule}\), so \(u_l\) and \(v_l\) are
constants under \(P_Z\).  By
\(\mathrm{lem{:}observed\text{-}block\text{-}means}\), the observed block
mean equals \(u_l\) on \(\{A_l=1\}\) and equals \(v_l\) on
\(\{C_l=1\}\).  On a mixed assignment both multipliers in
\(\mathrm{def{:}block\text{-}ht}\) vanish.  Thus, pointwise on the whole
assignment space,
\[
 X_l=\frac{w_l}{q_l}\{A_lu_l-C_lv_l\}
 =
 \begin{cases}
   (w_l/q_l)u_l,&A_l=1,\\
   -(w_l/q_l)v_l,&C_l=1,\\
   0,&H_l=1.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{2}
\]
Using (1), mutual exclusion, and (2) gives
\[
 \mathbb E_ZX_l
 =q_l\frac{w_l}{q_l}u_l
  +q_l\left(-\frac{w_l}{q_l}v_l\right)
 =w_l\{\mu_l(a)-\mu_l(b)\}.
 \tag{3}
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Var}_Z(X_l)
 &\le \mathbb E_Z(X_l^2)\\
 &=q_l\frac{w_l^2}{q_l^2}u_l^2
   +q_l\frac{w_l^2}{q_l^2}v_l^2\\
 &=\frac{w_l^2}{q_l}(u_l^2+v_l^2)
 \le \frac{w_l^2}{2q_l}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{4}
\]
Here the first inequality is the identity
\(\operatorname{Var}(X)=\mathbb E(X^2)-(\mathbb EX)^2\le\mathbb E(X^2)\),
and the last inequality follows from
\(\mathrm{ass{:}bounded\text{-}outcomes}\) and
\(\mathrm{def{:}block\text{-}means}\), which imply
\(|u_l|,|v_l|\le 1/2\).  Summing (4) and using the definition of \(S\)
yields
\[
 \sum_{l=1}^L\operatorname{Var}_Z(X_l)
 \le \frac12\sum_{l=1}^L\frac{w_l^2}{q_l}
 =\frac S2.
 \tag{5}
\]

It remains to establish the sharper centered-increment bound required for
the downstream Bernstein radius.  Subtracting (3) from each of the three
values in (2) gives
\[
 X_l-\mathbb E_ZX_l=
 \begin{cases}
 w_l\{(q_l^{-1}-1)u_l+v_l\},&A_l=1,\\
 -w_l\{u_l+(q_l^{-1}-1)v_l\},&C_l=1,\\
 -w_l(u_l-v_l),&H_l=1.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{6}
\]
Because \(B_l\ne\varnothing\), one has \(r_l\ge1\), so
\(0<q_l=2^{-r_l}\le1/2\).  Consequently \(q_l^{-1}-1\ge0\), and the
outcome bounds applied to (6) show, on the all-treated and all-control
events respectively,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \left|w_l\{(q_l^{-1}-1)u_l+v_l\}\right|
 &\le \frac{w_l}{2}\{q_l^{-1}-1+1\}
 =\frac{w_l}{2q_l},\\
 \left|w_l\{u_l+(q_l^{-1}-1)v_l\}\right|
 &\le \frac{w_l}{2}\{1+q_l^{-1}-1\}
 =\frac{w_l}{2q_l}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{7}
\]
On the mixed event,
\[
 |w_l(u_l-v_l)|\le w_l\le\frac{w_l}{2q_l}.
 \tag{8}
\]
Equations (6)--(8) therefore prove the exact uniform envelope
\[
 |X_l-\mathbb E_ZX_l|\le\frac{w_l}{2q_l}\le\frac M2,
 \tag{9}
\]
which in particular implies the weaker displayed bound
\(|X_l-\mathbb E_ZX_l|\le w_l/q_l\le M\) in the frozen claim.  The
constant in (9) cannot be improved uniformly: the admissible schedule
\(u_l=v_l=1/2\) makes the centered value on \(\{A_l=1\}\) equal to
\(w_l/(2q_l)\).

For the required smallest unequal-block stress test, take
\(B_1=\{1\}\) and \(B_2=\{2,3\}\).  Among the two assignments on
\(B_1\), the category counts \((A_1,C_1,H_1)\) are \((1,1,0)\), while
among the four assignments on \(B_2\) the corresponding counts are
\((1,1,2)\).  Hence exhaustive enumeration of the \(2\cdot4=8\) joint
assignments gives probabilities \((1/2,1/2,0)\) for block one and
\((1/4,1/4,1/2)\) for block two, independently.  On every one of those
eight assignments, (2) and (6) give the realized and centered summands;
direct averaging reproduces (3)--(5), and (7)--(9) hold assignment by
assignment.  With \(u_l=v_l=1/2\), an all-treated assignment attains
equality in both (4) and (9), confirming the variance and increment
constants at this finite boundary instance.
