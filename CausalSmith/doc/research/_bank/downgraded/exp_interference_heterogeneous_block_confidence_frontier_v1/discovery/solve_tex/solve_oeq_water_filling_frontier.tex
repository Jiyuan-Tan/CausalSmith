%%% FIELD capped-upper-statement
For every \(0<\alpha\le 1/4\),
\[
 \mathcal R_\alpha(w,q)
 \le 5\min\{1,\omega_\alpha(w,q)\}.
\]
The bound is attained by a deterministic observed-data interval and therefore remains valid when the competing class permits randomization by the independent external randomizer \(R\).  In addition, if \(x_\alpha<\sum_{l=1}^Lq_l\) and no coordinate of the water-filling optimizer is capped, meaning \(\lambda_\alpha w_l/q_l<1\) for every \(l\), then
\[
 \omega_\alpha(w,q)=\sqrt{Sx_\alpha}
 =\sqrt{S\log(1/\alpha)}.
\]
%%% FIELD capped-upper-proof
Fix \(0<\alpha\le1/4\).  By \(\mathrm{thm{:}trimmed\text{-}upper}\), there is a deterministic interval \(I_J(D)\in\mathfrak C_\alpha\) whose pointwise length, for every fixed potential-outcome schedule in \(\mathcal M_n(B)\), is at most
\[
 2\min\{1,\rho_\alpha(w,q)\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Because the infimum in \(\mathrm{def{:}length\text{-}risk}\) ranges over a class that contains this deterministic rule, taking first the expectation, then the supremum over fixed schedules, and finally the infimum over all rules gives
\[
 \mathcal R_\alpha(w,q)
 \le 2\min\{1,\rho_\alpha(w,q)\}.
\tag{2}
\]
The strengthened trimming duality in \(\mathrm{lem{:}capped\text{-}trimming\text{-}duality}\) gives
\[
 \rho_\alpha(w,q)\le\frac52\omega_\alpha(w,q).
\tag{3}
\]
For every \(u\ge0\) and every \(K\ge1\),
\[
 \min\{1,Ku\}\le K\min\{1,u\}.
\tag{4}
\]
Indeed, if \(u\le1\), the left side is at most \(Ku\), and if \(u>1\), it is at most \(1\le K\).  Combining (2)--(4) with \(K=5/2\) proves
\[
 \mathcal R_\alpha(w,q)
 \le5\min\{1,\omega_\alpha(w,q)\}.
\tag{5}
\]
This comparison uses a deterministic member of \(\mathfrak C_\alpha\); enlarging the decision class to procedures depending measurably on the independent \(R\) cannot invalidate the upper bound.

It remains to verify the no-cap formula.  Put \(x=x_\alpha\).  Under \(x<\sum_lq_l\), the definition of the water-filling multiplier and its optimality certificate in \(\mathrm{lem{:}capped\text{-}trimming\text{-}duality}\) give
\[
 \delta_l^*=\min\{1,\lambda_\alpha w_l/q_l\},
 \qquad
 \sum_lq_l(\delta_l^*)^2=x.
\tag{6}
\]
The asserted no-cap condition turns (6) into \(\delta_l^*=\lambda_\alpha w_l/q_l\) for every \(l\).  Since \(q_l>0\), \(w_l>0\), and
\(S=\sum_lw_l^2/q_l>0\), the constraint in (6) becomes
\[
 x=\lambda_\alpha^2\sum_l\frac{w_l^2}{q_l}
  =\lambda_\alpha^2S,
 \qquad
 \lambda_\alpha=\sqrt{x/S}.
\tag{7}
\]
Substitution into the objective of \(\mathrm{def{:}capped\text{-}modulus}\) yields
\[
 \omega_\alpha(w,q)
 =\sum_lw_l\delta_l^*
 =\lambda_\alpha\sum_l\frac{w_l^2}{q_l}
 =\lambda_\alpha S
 =\sqrt{Sx}.
\tag{8}
\]
As \(x=x_\alpha=\log(1/\alpha)\), (8) is the claimed identity.

\]
The lower bound must hold when the infimum defining \(\mathcal R_\alpha\) ranges over every measurable interval procedure, including procedures randomized by the independent external randomizer \(R\).  This universal linear lower bound is the sole residual question.

%%% FIELD residual-partial-result
The capped optimizer and both computable sides are closed.  The strengthened trimming duality gives
\[
 \omega_\alpha(w,q)\le\rho_\alpha(w,q)
 \le\frac52\omega_\alpha(w,q),
 \qquad
 \omega_1(w,q)\le\rho_0(w,q)\le2\omega_1(w,q).
\]
Together with the trimmed interval, \(\mathrm{thm{:}capped\text{-}modulus\text{-}upper}\) proves
\[
 \mathcal R_\alpha(w,q)
 \le5\min\{1,\omega_\alpha(w,q)\}.
\]
When no cap binds, the active quadratic constraint gives
\(\omega_\alpha(w,q)=\sqrt{S\log(1/\alpha)}\).  On the converse side, \(\mathrm{lem{:}capped\text{-}erasure\text{-}consistency\text{-}lower}\) proves
\[
 \mathcal R_\alpha(w,q)
 \ge
 \frac{\omega_\alpha(w,q)^3}
 {128\log(1/\alpha)+8\omega_\alpha(w,q)^2}
 \ge
 \frac{\omega_\alpha(w,q)^3}{136\log(1/\alpha)}.
\]
Thus the only unresolved step is to replace this cubic converse by a universal linear converse at nonzero finite confidence.
