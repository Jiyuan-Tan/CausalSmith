By \(\mathrm{ass{:}partition}\), the blocks are pairwise disjoint and their union is \(U\).  Under the hypothesis \(r_l=r\) for every \(l\), finite additivity of cardinality therefore gives
\[
n=|U|=\sum_{l=1}^L |B_l|=\sum_{l=1}^L r=Lr.
\]
Every block is nonempty by \(\mathrm{ass{:}partition}\), so \(r\ge 1\); hence division by \(r\) is valid and \(L=n/r\).  The definition of the block weights yields \(w_l=r_l/n=r/n\).  By \(\mathrm{ass{:}balanced\text{-}bernoulli}\), the assignments within a block are independent Bernoulli random variables with success probability \(1/2\).  Thus
\[
q_l=P_Z(Z_i=1\ \forall i\in B_l)
=\prod_{i\in B_l}\frac12
=2^{-|B_l|}=2^{-r};
\]
the same calculation applies to the all-control event.

Substituting \(L=n/r\), \(w_l=r/n\), and \(q_l=2^{-r}\) into the definitions of \(S\) and \(M\) gives
\[
S=\sum_{l=1}^L\frac{w_l^2}{q_l}
=\frac nr\frac{r^2}{n^2}2^r
=\frac{r2^r}{n},
\qquad
M=\max_{1\le l\le L}\frac{w_l}{q_l}
=\frac rn2^r
=\frac{r2^r}{n}=S.
\tag{1}
\]
In particular, \(S>0\).  Because \(0<\alpha\le 1/4\), one has \(\log(1/\alpha)>0\).  Consequently the no-dominance condition in \(\mathrm{thm{:}no\text{-}dominance\text{-}upper}\) is, using \(M=S\), equivalent to
\[
S\sqrt{\log(1/\alpha)}\le c_0\sqrt S
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\sqrt{S\log(1/\alpha)}\le c_0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
S\log(1/\alpha)\le c_0^2.
\]
Here the first equivalence divides by the strictly positive quantity \(\sqrt S\), and the second squares two nonnegative quantities.  Formula \((1)\) turns the last display into
\[
\frac{r2^r\log(1/\alpha)}{n}\le c_0^2.
\]
Whenever this condition holds, \(\mathrm{thm{:}no\text{-}dominance\text{-}upper}\) applies and, after one more substitution from \((1)\), gives
\[
\mathcal R_\alpha(w,q)
\le C_1\min\{1,\sqrt{S\log(1/\alpha)}\}
=C_1\min\left\{1,
\sqrt{\frac{r2^r\log(1/\alpha)}{n}}\right\}.
\]
The cited theorem requires only \(0<\alpha\le1/4\) and the displayed no-dominance inequality.  Therefore this reduction introduces no additional extreme-confidence cutoff.
