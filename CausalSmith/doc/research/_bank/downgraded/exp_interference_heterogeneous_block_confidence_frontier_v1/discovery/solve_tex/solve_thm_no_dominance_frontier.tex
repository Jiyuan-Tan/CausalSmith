%%% FIELD capped-lower-statement
For \(0<\alpha\le 1/4\), put \(x=\log(1/\alpha)\).  Then
\[
 \mathcal R_\alpha(w,q)
 \ge
 \frac{\omega_\alpha(w,q)^3}
      {128x+8\omega_\alpha(w,q)^2}
 \ge
 \frac{\omega_\alpha(w,q)^3}{136x}.
\]

%%% FIELD capped-lower-proof
Write \(\mathsf A=\omega_\alpha(w,q)\).  The partition assumption and
the definition \(w_l=r_l/n\) give
\[
 w_l>0,\qquad \sum_{l=1}^Lw_l=1.
\tag{1}
\]
Also \(q_l=2^{-r_l}\in(0,1/2]\).  The feasible set in
\(\mathrm{def{:}capped\text{-}modulus}\) is a nonempty compact subset
of \([0,1]^L\), and its objective is continuous.  It therefore has an
optimizer \(\delta^*\).  Since all \(w_l\) are positive and \(x>0\),
\(\mathsf A>0\).  Define
\[
 H=\{l:\delta_l^*\ge \mathsf A/2\},
 \qquad W_H=\sum_{l\in H}w_l,
 \qquad Q_H=\sum_{l\in H}q_l.
\]
Feasibility of \(\delta^*\) yields
\[
 Q_H\frac{\mathsf A^2}{4}
 \le \sum_{l\in H}q_l(\delta_l^*)^2
 \le x,
 \qquad	ext{hence}\qquad
 Q_H\le \frac{4x}{\mathsf A^2}.
\tag{2}
\]
On the other hand, (1), \(0\le\delta_l^*\le1\), and the definition of
\(H\) give
\[
 \mathsf A=\sum_lw_l\delta_l^*
 \le W_H+\frac{\mathsf A}{2}\sum_{l\notin H}w_l
 \le W_H+\frac{\mathsf A}{2},
\]
so
\[
 W_H\ge \frac{\mathsf A}{2}.
\tag{3}
\]

We next partition \(H\) into groups of reveal mass at most \(1/2\).
First make every coordinate with \(q_l>1/4\) a singleton group.  Then
order all remaining coordinates arbitrarily and accumulate them until
the running mass first reaches \(1/4\), close that group, and continue.
Each closed group has mass in \([1/4,1/2]\), and only the final group
formed from the remaining coordinates can have mass below \(1/4\).
Thus every group except possibly one has mass at least \(1/4\).  If
\(N\) is the number of groups, then
\[
 N\le4Q_H+1\le\frac{16x}{\mathsf A^2}+1
\tag{4}
\]
by (2).  Thus some group \(G\subseteq H\), with
\(Q_G=\sum_{l\in G}q_l\le1/2\), has weight
\[
 g:=\sum_{l\in G}w_l
 \ge\frac{W_H}{N}
 \ge\frac{\mathsf A^3}{32x+2\mathsf A^2}.
\tag{5}
\]

Fix any measurable \(\Gamma\in\mathfrak C_\alpha\).  For each
\(u\in[-1/2,1/2]\), construct a fixed schedule \(m_u\) by setting, for
\(i\in B_l\),
\[
 Y_i^u(d)=
 \begin{cases}
 u,&l\in G\text{ and }d=a,\\
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\tag{6}
\]
The schedule is nonrandom, respects the block-exposure map, and all
its outcomes lie in \([-1/2,1/2]\); hence the partition, balanced
randomization, fixed-schedule, block-exposure, and bounded-outcome
assumptions imply \(m_u\in\mathcal M_n(B)\).  By
\(\mathrm{def{:}block\text{-}means}\) and the definition of the causal
target,
\[
 \theta(m_u)=gu.
\tag{7}
\]

Let \(\mathsf U_0\sim\operatorname{Uniform}[-1/2,1/2]\) be independent
of \((Z,R)\), and mix the fixed schedules in (6) over \(u=\mathsf U_0\).
This mixing is only a proof device: honesty is applied first to every
fixed \(m_u\).  Let
\[
 \mathcal E_0=\bigcap_{l\in G}\{A_l=0\}.
\]
Disjointness of the blocks and balanced independent assignment imply
that the \(A_l\), \(l\in G\), are independent Bernoulli variables with
success probabilities \(q_l\).  In particular, the union bound gives
\[
 p_0:=P_Z(\mathcal E_0)
 =\prod_{l\in G}(1-q_l)
 \ge1-\sum_{l\in G}q_l
 \ge\frac12.
\tag{8}
\]
On \(\mathcal E_0\), no block in \(G\) attains exposure \(a\), so (6)
and the block-exposure assumption give \(Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}=0\) for
every unit.  Consequently the conditional law of \((D,R)\) on
\(\mathcal E_0\) is independent of \(\mathsf U_0\); this also uses the
declared independence of the external randomizer \(R\).

Uniform honesty and (7), integrated over \(\mathsf U_0\), imply
\[
 P\{g\mathsf U_0\in\Gamma(D,R)\}\ge1-\alpha.
\tag{9}
\]
Combining (8)--(9),
\[
 P\{\mathcal E_0,\ g\mathsf U_0\in\Gamma(D,R)\}
 \ge p_0-\alpha.
\tag{10}
\]
Conditionally on \((\mathcal E_0,D,R)\), the variable
\(g\mathsf U_0\) is uniform on \([-g/2,g/2]\).  For every interval
\(J\), including a random interval after conditioning,
\[
 P\{g\mathsf U_0\in J\mid J\}
 =\frac{\operatorname{len}(J\cap[-g/2,g/2])}{g}
 \le\frac{\operatorname{len}(J)}{g}.
\tag{11}
\]
Equations (10)--(11) therefore give
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\operatorname{len}\{\Gamma(D,R)\}\right]
 \ge g(p_0-\alpha)
 \ge\frac g4,
\tag{12}
\]
where the expectation on the left averages the fixed-schedule risks
over \(\mathsf U_0\).  Hence the supremum of those risks over
\(m\in\mathcal M_n(B)\) is at least the same quantity.  Taking the
infimum over \(\Gamma\in\mathfrak C_\alpha\), then applying (5), proves
\[
 \mathcal R_\alpha(w,q)
 \ge\frac{\mathsf A^3}{128x+8\mathsf A^2}.
\tag{13}
\]
Finally, (1) and \(0\le\delta_l^*\le1\) imply
\(\mathsf A\le1\), while \(x\ge\log4>1\).  Thus
\(128x+8\mathsf A^2\le136x\), and the second inequality follows from
(13).

%%% FIELD no-dominance-upper-statement
There exist universal constants \(c_0,C_1>0\) such that, for every
\(0<\alpha\le1/4\),
\[
 M\sqrt{\log(1/\alpha)}\le c_0\sqrt S
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \mathcal R_\alpha(w,q)
 \le C_1\min\{1,\sqrt{S\log(1/\alpha)}\}.
\]
The infimum defining \(\mathcal R_\alpha\) may range over every
measurable interval procedure randomized by \(R\).

%%% FIELD no-dominance-upper-proof
Put \(x=\log(1/\alpha)\) and \(y=\log(2/\alpha)\).  Since
\(\alpha\le1/4\), one has \(x\ge\log4\) and therefore
\[
 y=x+\log2\le\frac32x.
\tag{14}
\]
By \(\mathrm{thm{:}finite\text{-}upper}\), the deterministic
observed-data interval \(I_{\mathrm{HT},\alpha}\) is an element of
\(\mathfrak C_\alpha\) and has, under every fixed schedule, pointwise
length at most
\[
 2\min\left\{1,\sqrt{Sy}+\frac{My}{3}\right\}.
\tag{15}
\]
The definition of \(\mathcal R_\alpha\) permits this deterministic
rule even though it also permits rules using the independent
randomizer \(R\).  Hence (15) bounds the minimax risk from above.

Take \(c_0=1\).  Under the displayed no-dominance condition, (14)
implies
\[
 \sqrt{Sy}+\frac{My}{3}
 \le\sqrt{\frac32}\sqrt{Sx}+\frac12M x
 \le\left(\sqrt{\frac32}+\frac12\right)\sqrt{Sx}.
\tag{16}
\]
For \(K\ge1\) and \(z\ge0\),
\(\min\{1,Kz\}\le K\min\{1,z\}\).  Applying this elementary
inequality to (15)--(16), with
\(K=\sqrt{3/2}+1/2\), proves the result with
\[
 C_1=2\left(\sqrt{\frac32}+\frac12\right).
\]

%%% FIELD unequal-two-scale-statement
Let \(B^{(k)}\) contain \(k\) blocks of size two and \(k\) blocks of
size four, so \(n=6k\).  The two strata have
\((w_l,q_l)=(1/(3k),1/4)\) and
\((w_l,q_l)=(2/(3k),1/16)\), respectively, and
\[
 S=\frac{68}{9k},\qquad M=\frac{32}{3k},\qquad
 \frac{M\sqrt{\log(1/\alpha)}}{\sqrt S}
 =\frac{32\sqrt{\log(1/\alpha)}}{\sqrt{68k}}.
\]
Thus, for every fixed \(0<\alpha\le1/4\), the no-dominance condition
holds for all sufficiently large \(k\).  With \(C_1\) denoting the
universal constant in
\(\mathrm{thm{:}no\text{-}dominance\text{-}upper}\),
\[
 \mathcal R_\alpha(w,q)
 \le C_1\min\left\{1,
       \sqrt{\frac{68\log(1/\alpha)}{9k}}\right\}
 =O_\alpha(k^{-1/2}).
\]
The two water-filling breakpoints \(q_l/w_l\) are \(3k/4\) and
\(3k/32\).  No common block size can reproduce both reveal
probabilities, or both breakpoints.

%%% FIELD unequal-two-scale-proof
The total population size is \(2k+4k=6k\).  Therefore a size-two
block has
\[
 w_l=\frac2{6k}=\frac1{3k},\qquad q_l=2^{-2}=\frac14,
\]
and a size-four block has
\[
 w_l=\frac4{6k}=\frac2{3k},\qquad q_l=2^{-4}=\frac1{16},
\]
where the reveal probabilities follow from balanced independent
assignment.  Direct substitution in the definitions of \(S\) and
\(M\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 S
 &=k\frac{(1/(3k))^2}{1/4}
   +k\frac{(2/(3k))^2}{1/16}
   =\frac4{9k}+\frac{64}{9k}
   =\frac{68}{9k},\\
 M
 &=\max\left\{\frac{1/(3k)}{1/4},
                \frac{2/(3k)}{1/16}\right\}
   =\frac{32}{3k}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
Consequently
\[
 \frac{M\sqrt{\log(1/\alpha)}}{\sqrt S}
 =\frac{32\sqrt{\log(1/\alpha)}}{\sqrt{68k}}
 \longrightarrow0
\tag{18}
\]
for fixed \(\alpha\).  The hypothesis of
\(\mathrm{thm{:}no\text{-}dominance\text{-}upper}\) therefore holds
eventually, and substitution of (17) in that theorem gives the stated
upper bound.

Finally, the cap breakpoints in
\(\mathrm{def{:}capped\text{-}modulus}\) are \(q_l/w_l\).  They equal
\[
 \frac{1/4}{1/(3k)}=\frac{3k}{4},
 \qquad
 \frac{1/16}{2/(3k)}=\frac{3k}{32}.
\tag{19}
\]
Because \(r\mapsto2^{-r}\) is injective on positive integers, a
single common block size cannot yield both \(1/4\) and \(1/16\); (19)
also shows directly that it cannot yield both breakpoint values.

%%% FIELD equal-block-statement
If \(r_l=r\) for every \(l\), then
\[
 L=\frac nr,\qquad q_l=2^{-r},\qquad w_l=\frac rn,
 \qquad S=M=\frac{r2^r}{n}.
\]
With \(c_0,C_1\) denoting the universal constants in
\(\mathrm{thm{:}no\text{-}dominance\text{-}upper}\), the no-dominance
condition is equivalent to
\[
 \frac{r2^r\log(1/\alpha)}{n}\le c_0^2.
\]
Whenever this condition holds, for every \(0<\alpha\le1/4\),
\[
 \mathcal R_\alpha(w,q)
 \le C_1\min\left\{1,
       \sqrt{\frac{r2^r\log(1/\alpha)}{n}}\right\}.
\]
Thus the heterogeneous upper theorem recovers the familiar
equal-block effective-count upper rate without any additional
extreme-confidence cutoff.

%%% FIELD equal-block-proof
The blocks form a partition and each has cardinality \(r\), so
\(n=Lr\) and hence \(L=n/r\).  Balanced independent assignment gives
\(q_l=2^{-r}\), while the weight definition gives \(w_l=r/n\).
Therefore
\[
 S=\sum_{l=1}^L\frac{w_l^2}{q_l}
 =\frac nr\frac{r^2}{n^2}2^r
 =\frac{r2^r}{n},
 \qquad
 M=\frac{w_l}{q_l}=\frac{r2^r}{n}=S.
\tag{20}
\]
Since \(S>0\), the no-dominance inequality is equivalent, after
division by \(\sqrt S\) and squaring, to
\[
 S\log(1/\alpha)\le c_0^2,
\]
which becomes the displayed condition after (20).  Substitution of
(20) into \(\mathrm{thm{:}no\text{-}dominance\text{-}upper}\) proves
the asserted risk bound.  The only confidence restriction used is
\(0<\alpha\le1/4\), together with that displayed no-dominance
condition, so no further extreme-confidence cutoff is present.
