\(h_\alpha=\sqrt{S\log(2/\alpha)}+\frac{M}{6}\log(2/\alpha),\qquad I_{\mathrm{HT},\alpha}(D)=[-1,1]\cap[\widehat\theta(D)-h_\alpha,\widehat\theta(D)+h_\alpha]\).
%%% FIELD construction-trimmed-radius
\(\rho_\alpha(w,q)=\inf_{J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,L\}}[\sum_{l\notin J}w_l+\sqrt{\log(2/\alpha)\sum_{l\in J}w_l^2/q_l}+\{\log(2/\alpha)/6\}\max_{l\in J}w_l/q_l]\), with the maximum over \(J=\varnothing\) equal to zero, and \(\rho_0(w,q)=\inf_J[\sum_{l\notin J}w_l+(\sum_{l\in J}w_l^2/q_l)^{1/2}]\).

For every \(m\in\mathcal M_n(B)\), the vectors \((A_l,C_l,H_l)\) are independent across \(l\), each has categorical probabilities \((q_l,q_l,1-2q_l)\), and \(A_lC_l=0\). The observed-data construction in \(\mathrm{def{:}block\text{-}ht}\) satisfies \(\mathbb E_Z[X_l(D)]=w_l\{\mu_l(a)-\mu_l(b)\}\), \(\sum_l\operatorname{Var}_Z\{X_l(D)\}\le S/2\), and \(|X_l(D)-\mathbb E_ZX_l(D)|\le w_l/(2q_l)\le M/2\).

For the family \(B^{(k)}\) containing \(k\) blocks of size two and \(k\) blocks of size four, every \(0<\alpha\le 1/4\) and every integer \(k\ge64\) satisfy
\[
 \mathcal R_\alpha(w,q)\ge \frac{1}{192\sqrt{k}}.
\]
This lower bound applies to the full class of measurable honest interval procedures, including procedures externally randomized by \(R\).
%%% FIELD statement-unequal-two-scale-family
Let \(B^{(k)}\) contain \(k\) blocks of size two and \(k\) blocks of
size four, so \(n=6k\).  The two strata have
\((w_l,q_l)=(1/(3k),1/4)\) and
\((w_l,q_l)=(2/(3k),1/16)\), respectively, and
\[
 S=\frac{68}{9k},\qquad M=\frac{32}{3k},\qquad
 \frac{M\sqrt{\log(1/\alpha)}}{\sqrt S}
 =\frac{32\sqrt{\log(1/\alpha)}}{\sqrt{68k}}.
\]
Thus, for every fixed \(0<\alpha\le1/4\), the no-dominance condition
holds for all sufficiently large \(k\).  With \(C_1\) denoting the
universal constant in
\(\mathrm{thm{:}no\text{-}dominance\text{-}upper}\), for every
sufficiently large \(k\),
\[
 \frac{1}{192\sqrt{k}}
 \le \mathcal R_\alpha(w,q)
 \le C_1\sqrt{\frac{68\log(1/\alpha)}{9k}},
\]
and consequently \(\mathcal R_\alpha(w,q)=\Theta_\alpha(k^{-1/2})\).
The two water-filling breakpoints \(q_l/w_l\) are \(3k/4\) and
\(3k/32\).  No common block size can reproduce both reveal
probabilities, or both breakpoints.
%%% FIELD proof-block-reduction
Fix a block \(B_l\).  By
\(\mathrm{ass{:}balanced\text{-}bernoulli}\), each of its
\(2^{r_l}\) assignment vectors has probability \(2^{-r_l}=q_l\).
Exactly one vector is all treated and exactly one is all controlled.
Thus
\[
 P_Z(A_l=1)=q_l,\qquad P_Z(C_l=1)=q_l,
 \qquad P_Z(H_l=1)=1-2q_l,
\tag{1}
\]
and \(A_lC_l=0\) on every assignment.  By
\(\mathrm{ass{:}partition}\), distinct triples
\((A_l,C_l,H_l)\) depend on disjoint coordinates of \(Z\); their
independence therefore follows from
\(\mathrm{ass{:}balanced\text{-}bernoulli}\).

Put \(u_l=\mu_l(a)\) and \(v_l=\mu_l(b)\).  The fixed-schedule and
bounded-outcome assumptions, together with
\(\mathrm{def{:}block\text{-}means}\), give
\[
 -\frac12\le u_l,v_l\le\frac12.
\tag{2}
\]
On the all-treated and all-control events,
\(\mathrm{lem{:}observed\text{-}block\text{-}means}\) identifies the
observed block mean with \(u_l\) and \(v_l\), respectively.  Mutual
exclusion then rewrites the observed statistic, on every assignment,
as
\[
 X_l=\frac{w_l}{q_l}(A_lu_l-C_lv_l).
\tag{3}
\]
Using (1), fixedness of \((u_l,v_l)\), and \(A_lC_l=0\),
\[
 \mathbb E_ZX_l=w_l(u_l-v_l),
\tag{4}
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Var}_Z(X_l)
 &\le \mathbb E_ZX_l^2\\
 &=\frac{w_l^2}{q_l}(u_l^2+v_l^2)\\
 &\le\frac{w_l^2}{2q_l},
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
where the last step uses (2).  Summing (5) and using the definition of
\(S\) proves
\[
 \sum_{l=1}^L\operatorname{Var}_Z(X_l)
 \le\frac12\sum_{l=1}^L\frac{w_l^2}{q_l}=\frac S2.
\tag{6}
\]

It remains to compute the exact centered increment.  Write
\(W_l=X_l-\mathbb E_ZX_l\).  Equations (3)--(4) give the following
three exhaustive cases:
\[
 \frac{W_l}{w_l}=
 \begin{cases}
  (q_l^{-1}-1)u_l+v_l,&A_l=1,\\
  -u_l-(q_l^{-1}-1)v_l,&C_l=1,\\
  -u_l+v_l,&H_l=1.
 \end{cases}
\tag{7}
\]
Since \(r_l\ge1\), one has \(0<q_l\le1/2\), so all coefficients
whose absolute values are summed below are nonnegative.  By (2), the
first two rows of (7) have absolute value at most
\[
 \frac12(q_l^{-1}-1)+\frac12=\frac1{2q_l}.
\tag{8}
\]
The third row has absolute value at most one, which is at most
\(1/(2q_l)\) because \(q_l\le1/2\).  Hence on every assignment
\[
 |X_l-mathbb E_ZX_l|=|W_l|
 \le\frac{w_l}{2q_l}\le\frac M2.
\tag{9}
\]
This is an enumeration of All Treat, All Control, and every other
assignment, rather than a triangle bound on \(|X_l|+|\mathbb EX_l|\).

For the smallest unequal stress test, take a singleton block and a
two-unit block.  Their \(2\cdot4=8\) joint assignments give categorical
counts \((1,1,0)\) and \((1,1,2)\), respectively.  Substitution in
(7), for arbitrary endpoints \(u_l,v_l\in[-1/2,1/2]\), verifies (8)
and (9) on each of the eight assignments; thus the asserted support,
mean, variance, and increment formulas also hold exactly at this
finite boundary case.
%%% FIELD proof-finite-upper
Fix \(m\in\mathcal M_n(B)\), and define
\[
 W_l=X_l-mathbb E_ZX_l,qquad z=\log(2/\alpha)>0.
\tag{10}
\]
By \(\mathrm{lem{:}block\text{-}reduction}\), the variables \(W_l\)
are independent and centered and obey
\[
 v:=\sum_{l=1}^L\mathbb E_ZW_l^2\le\frac S2,
 \qquad |W_l|\le\frac M2.
\tag{11}
\]
Apply \(\mathrm{lem{:}finite\text{-}product\text{-}exponential}\)
with \(c=M/2\) and the value of \(z\) in (10).  Because its threshold
is nondecreasing in \(v\), (11) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P_Z\!\left\{\left|\sum_{l=1}^LW_l\right|>
 \sqrt{S\log(2/\alpha)}
 +\frac M6\log(2/\alpha)\right\}
 &\le 2\exp\{-\log(2/\alpha)\}\\
 &=\alpha.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
The expectation identity in
\(\mathrm{lem{:}block\text{-}reduction}\), followed by the definition
of the causal estimand, yields
\[
 \sum_{l=1}^L\mathbb E_ZX_l
 =\sum_{l=1}^Lw_l\{\mu_l(a)-\mu_l(b)\}=\theta.
\tag{13}
\]
Since \(\widehat\theta=\sum_lX_l\) by
\(\mathrm{def{:}block\text{-}ht}\), equations (12)--(13) and the
corrected construction in \(\mathrm{def{:}clipped\text{-}ht}\) imply
\[
 P_{Z,m}\{|\widehat\theta-	heta|\le h_\alpha\}
 \ge1-\alpha.
\tag{14}
\]

Every \(X_l\), and hence \(\widehat\theta\), is measurable from
\(D\) by \(\mathrm{def{:}block\text{-}ht}\) and
\(\mathrm{lem{:}observed\text{-}block\text{-}means}\).  The quantities
\(S\), \(M\), and \(\alpha\) are known design inputs.  Moreover,
bounded outcomes give
\(\mu_l(a)-\mu_l(b)\in[-1,1]\), and membership in
\(\mathcal M_n(B)\), through its partition condition and the
definition \(w_l=r_l/n\), gives \(\sum_lw_l=1\).  Thus
\(\theta\in[-1,1]\).  On the event in (14), intersecting the raw
interval with \([-1,1]\) cannot remove \(\theta\), proving
\[
 P_{Z,m}\{\theta\in I_{\mathrm{HT},\alpha}(D)\}\ge1-\alpha.
\tag{15}
\]
Finally, the length of an intersection is at most the length of each
of the two intersected intervals, whence, pointwise in \(D\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{len}\{I_{\mathrm{HT},\alpha}(D)\}
 &\le\min\{2,2h_\alpha\}\\
 &=2\min\!\left\{1,\sqrt{S\log(2/\alpha)}
       +\frac M6\log(2/\alpha)\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
This is a deterministic finite-sample length certificate, so it also
holds for every assignment in the exhaustive unequal three-unit
stress test of \(\mathrm{lem{:}block\text{-}reduction}\).
%%% FIELD proof-no-dominance-upper
Put \(x=\log(1/\alpha)\) and \(y=\log(2/\alpha)\).  Since
\(0<\alpha\le1/4\), one has \(x\ge\log4\), and hence
\[
 y=x+\log2\le\frac32x.
\tag{17}
\]
The corrected \(\mathrm{thm{:}finite\text{-}upper}\) supplies a
deterministic observed-data interval that is honest uniformly over
\(\mathcal M_n(B)\) and has pointwise length at most
\[
 2\min\left\{1,\sqrt{Sy}+\frac{My}{6}\right\}.
\tag{18}
\]
It is therefore an admissible deterministic member of
\(\mathfrak C_\alpha\).  Although
\(\mathrm{def{:}length\text{-}risk}\) also permits procedures using
the independent randomizer \(R\), taking the infimum over the larger
class cannot invalidate the upper bound furnished by this one rule.
Thus (18) bounds \(\mathcal R_\alpha(w,q)\).

Take \(c_0=1\).  Under the no-dominance hypothesis
\(M\sqrt{x}\le\sqrt S\), equation (17) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sqrt{Sy}+\frac{My}{6}
 &\le\sqrt{\frac32}\sqrt{Sx}+\frac14Mx\\
 &\le\left(\sqrt{\frac32}+\frac14\right)\sqrt{Sx}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]
For every \(K\ge1\) and \(t\ge0\),
\(\min\{1,Kt\}\le K\min\{1,t\}\).  Since
\(K=\sqrt{3/2}+1/4>1\), equations (18)--(19) prove
\[
 \mathcal R_\alpha(w,q)
 \le C_1\min\{1,\sqrt{S\log(1/\alpha)}\},
 \qquad
 C_1=2\left(\sqrt{\frac32}+\frac14\right).
\tag{20}
\]
Both \(c_0\) and \(C_1\) are universal, as required.
%%% FIELD proof-two-scale-rademacher-lower
Fix \(0<\alpha\le1/4\), an integer \(k\ge64\), and an arbitrary
\(\Gamma\in\mathfrak C_\alpha\).  Index the \(k\) size-two blocks by
\(j=1,\ldots,k\).  In the hard schedules from
\(\mathrm{def{:}hard\text{-}subexperiment\text{-}embedding}\), put
\[
 V_j=\frac{\varepsilon_j}{2},\qquad
 P(\varepsilon_j=1)=P(\varepsilon_j=-1)=\frac12
 \quad(j=1,\ldots,k),
\tag{21}
\]
independently, and set \(V_l=1/2\) deterministically on every
size-four block.  This product law is used only to average the
pointwise coverage and risk inequalities furnished by
\(\mathrm{lem{:}hard\text{-}subexperiment\text{-}embedding}\); every
schedule in its support is a fixed member of
\(\mathcal M_n(B^{(k)})\).  For a size-two block,
\[
 q_j=\frac14,qquad w_j=\frac1{3k}.
\tag{22}
\]
Let \(K_j\) be its all-treated reveal indicator and define
\[
 N=\sum_{j=1}^k(1-K_j).
\tag{23}
\]
By \(\mathrm{lem{:}hard\text{-}subexperiment\text{-}embedding}\),
the \(K_j\) are independent \(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/4)\)
variables, independent of the signs in (21).  Consequently
\[
 N\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(k,3/4),\qquad
 \mathbb EN=\frac{3k}{4},\qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(N)=\frac{3k}{16}.
\tag{24}
\]

Write \(\Lambda=\Lambda_\Gamma(O,R)\) for the randomized interval in
the erasure experiment constructed in
\(\mathrm{def{:}hard\text{-}subexperiment\text{-}embedding}\), and
put \(L_\Lambda=\operatorname{len}(\Lambda)\).  Conditional on
\((O,R)\), the revealed size-two signs and the size-four contribution
are fixed, while the \(N\) unrevealed size-two signs remain independent
fair Rademacher variables.  Indeed, \(O\) contains only
\((K_j,K_jV_j)\), and the external \(R\) is independent of the
assignment and prior signs.  Since \(\theta=\sum_lw_lV_l\) on these
schedules, its conditional law has the form
\[
 c(O)+\frac1{6k}\sum_{s=1}^{N}\xi_s,
\tag{25}
\]
where the \(\xi_s\) are conditionally independent fair Rademacher
variables.  Adjacent support points in (25) are separated by
\(1/(3k)\).

We prove the atom bound used below.  For
\(S_N=\sum_{s=1}^N\xi_s\), its largest atom is
\[
 p_N=2^{-N}\binom{N}{\lfloor N/2\rfloor}.
\]
If \(N=2m\), factorial cancellation gives
\[
 p_{2m}=4^{-m}\binom{2m}{m}
       =\prod_{s=1}^m\left(1-\frac1{2s}\right).
\]
The inequalities \(\log(1-u)\le-u\) for \(0<u<1\) and
\(\sum_{s=1}^m s^{-1}\ge\int_1^{m+1}t^{-1}\,dt=\log(m+1)\) imply
\[
 p_{2m}\le(m+1)^{-1/2}\le\sqrt{\frac2{2m}}.
\tag{26}
\]
If \(N=2m+1\), then
\[
 p_{2m+1}=\frac{2m+1}{2m+2}p_{2m}
 \le(m+1)^{-1/2}
 \le\sqrt{\frac2{2m+1}}.
\tag{27}
\]
The case \(N=1\) also satisfies (27), so for every \(N\ge1\),
\[
 p_N\le\sqrt{\frac2N}.
\tag{28}
\]

An interval of length \(u\) contains at most \(3ku+1\) points of a
lattice having spacing \(1/(3k)\).  It follows from (25) and (28) that
on \(N\ge k/2\), conditionally on \((O,R)\),
\[
 P\{\theta\in\Lambda\mid O,R\}
 \le(3kL_\Lambda+1)\sqrt{\frac2N}.
\tag{29}
\]
In particular, on
\[
 \mathcal A=\left\{N\ge\frac k2,\quad
 L_\Lambda<\frac1{48\sqrt{k}}\right\},
\]
equation (29) and \(k\ge64\) give
\[
 P\{\theta\in\Lambda\mid O,R\}
 \le\left(\frac{\sqrt{k}}{16}+1\right)\frac2{\sqrt{k}}
 =\frac18+\frac2{\sqrt{k}}
 \le\frac38.
\tag{30}
\]
On \(N<k/2\), equation (24) gives
\(|N-\mathbb EN|>k/4\).  Markov's inequality applied to
\((N-\mathbb EN)^2\), followed by (24), yields
\[
 P\left(N<\frac k2\right)
 \le\frac{3k/16}{(k/4)^2}
 =\frac3k\le\frac3{64}.
\tag{31}
\]

Pointwise honesty of \(\Gamma\), averaged over the product law (21),
and the coverage identity in
\(\mathrm{lem{:}hard\text{-}subexperiment\text{-}embedding}\) imply
\[
 P\{\theta\in\Lambda\}\ge1-\alpha\ge\frac34.
\tag{32}
\]
On \(\mathcal A\), conditional coverage is at most \(3/8\) by (30),
and outside \(\mathcal A\) it is at most one.  The complement of
\(\mathcal A\) is contained in the union of
\(\{N<k/2\}\) and
\(\{L_\Lambda\ge1/(48\sqrt{k})\}\).  Hence
\[
 \frac34
 \le P\{\theta\in\Lambda\}
 \le\frac38+P\left(N<\frac k2\right)
      +P\left(L_\Lambda\ge\frac1{48\sqrt{k}}\right).
\]
Using (31),
\[
 P\left(L_\Lambda\ge\frac1{48\sqrt{k}}\right)
 \ge\frac38-\frac3{64}=\frac{21}{64}\ge\frac14.
\tag{33}
\]
Therefore
\[
 \mathbb E L_\Lambda
 \ge\frac1{48\sqrt{k}}
 P\left(L_\Lambda\ge\frac1{48\sqrt{k}}\right)
 \ge\frac1{192\sqrt{k}}.
\tag{34}
\]
The expected-length identity and supremum inequality in
\(\mathrm{lem{:}hard\text{-}subexperiment\text{-}embedding}\) show
that the worst fixed-schedule risk of the original \(\Gamma\) is at
least the product-prior average in (34).  Since \(\Gamma\) was an
arbitrary member of \(\mathfrak C_\alpha\), taking the infimum in
\(\mathrm{def{:}length\text{-}risk}\) proves the claimed inequality.
The construction conditions throughout on the declared independent
coordinate \(R\), so it covers every externally randomized measurable
interval in the decision class.
%%% FIELD proof-unequal-two-scale-family
The total population size is \(2k+4k=6k\).  Hence a size-two block has
\[
 w_l=\frac2{6k}=\frac1{3k},\qquad q_l=2^{-2}=\frac14,
\]
whereas a size-four block has
\[
 w_l=\frac4{6k}=\frac2{3k},\qquad q_l=2^{-4}=\frac1{16}.
\]
The reveal probabilities follow directly from
\(\mathrm{ass{:}balanced\text{-}bernoulli}\).  Substitution into the
definitions of \(S\) and \(M\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 S
 &=k\frac{(1/(3k))^2}{1/4}
   +k\frac{(2/(3k))^2}{1/16}
   =\frac4{9k}+\frac{64}{9k}
   =\frac{68}{9k},\\
 M
 &=\max\left\{\frac{1/(3k)}{1/4},
                \frac{2/(3k)}{1/16}\right\}
   =\frac{32}{3k}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{35}
\]
Consequently
\[
 \frac{M\sqrt{\log(1/\alpha)}}{\sqrt S}
 =\frac{32\sqrt{\log(1/\alpha)}}{\sqrt{68k}}
 \longrightarrow0
\tag{36}
\]
for every fixed \(0<\alpha\le1/4\).  The hypothesis of
\(\mathrm{thm{:}no\text{-}dominance\text{-}upper}\) therefore holds
for all sufficiently large \(k\).  For such \(k\), the square-root
term is also below one, and that theorem plus (35) yields
\[
 \mathcal R_\alpha(w,q)
 \le C_1\sqrt{\frac{68\log(1/\alpha)}{9k}}.
\tag{37}
\]
For \(k\ge64\),
\(\mathrm{lem{:}two\text{-}scale\text{-}rademacher\text{-}lower}\)
gives
\[
 \mathcal R_\alpha(w,q)\ge\frac1{192\sqrt{k}}.
\tag{38}
\]
Equations (37)--(38) prove
\(\mathcal R_\alpha(w,q)=\Theta_\alpha(k^{-1/2})\) at every fixed
confidence level in the stated range.  The lower bound invoked here is
specialized to the \(k\) size-two blocks and makes no assertion about
either universal weighted-erasure lower open question.

Finally, the cap breakpoints in
\(\mathrm{def{:}capped\text{-}modulus}\) are \(q_l/w_l\).  They are
\[
 \frac{1/4}{1/(3k)}=\frac{3k}{4},
 \qquad
 \frac{1/16}{2/(3k)}=\frac{3k}{32}.
\tag{39}
\]
Since \(r\mapsto2^{-r}\) is injective on the positive integers, one
common block size cannot reproduce both reveal probabilities.  The
two distinct values in (39) likewise rule out a common breakpoint.
