%%% FIELD statement-two-scale-rademacher-lower
For the family \(B^{(k)}\) containing \(k\) blocks of size two and \(k\) blocks of size four, every \(0<\alpha\le 1/4\) and every integer \(k\ge64\) satisfy
\[
 \mathcal R_\alpha(w,q)\ge \frac{1}{192\sqrt{k}}.
\]
This lower bound applies to the full class of measurable honest interval procedures, including procedures externally randomized by \(R\).
%%% FIELD statement-unequal-two-scale-family
Let \(B^{(k)}\) contain \(k\) blocks of size two and \(k\) blocks of
size four, so \(n=6k\).  The two strata have
\((w_l,q_l)=(1/(3k),1/4)\) and
\((w_l,q_l)=(2/(3k),1/16)\), respectively, and
\[
 S=\frac{68}{9k},\qquad M=\frac{32}{3k},\qquad
 \frac{M\sqrt{\log(1/\alpha)}}{\sqrt S}
 =\frac{32\sqrt{\log(1/\alpha)}}{\sqrt{68k}}.
\]
Thus, for every fixed \(0<\alpha\le1/4\), the no-dominance condition
holds for all sufficiently large \(k\).  With \(C_1\) denoting the
universal constant in
\(\mathrm{thm{:}no\text{-}dominance\text{-}upper}\),
\[
 \mathcal R_\alpha(w,q)
 \le C_1\min\left\{1,
       \sqrt{\frac{68\log(1/\alpha)}{9k}}\right\}
 =O_\alpha(k^{-1/2}).
\]
The two water-filling breakpoints \(q_l/w_l\) are \(3k/4\) and
\(3k/32\).  No common block size can reproduce both reveal
probabilities, or both breakpoints.
%%% FIELD proof-two-scale-rademacher-lower
Fix \(0<\alpha\le1/4\), an integer \(k\ge64\), and an arbitrary
\(\Gamma\in\mathfrak C_\alpha\).  Index the \(k\) size-two blocks by
\(j=1,\ldots,k\).  In the hard schedules from
\(\mathrm{def{:}hard\text{-}subexperiment\text{-}embedding}\), put
\[
 V_j=\frac{\varepsilon_j}{2},\qquad
 P(\varepsilon_j=1)=P(\varepsilon_j=-1)=\frac12
 \quad(j=1,\ldots,k),
\tag{1}
\]
independently, and set \(V_l=1/2\) deterministically on every size-four
block.  This product law is used only to average the pointwise coverage
and risk inequalities furnished by
\(\mathrm{lem{:}hard\text{-}subexperiment\text{-}embedding}\); every
schedule in its support is a fixed member of \(\mathcal M_n(B^{(k)})\).
For a size-two block, balanced independent Bernoulli assignment gives
\[
 q_j=\frac14,\qquad w_j=\frac1{3k}.
\tag{2}
\]
Let \(K_j\) be its all-treated reveal indicator and let
\[
 N=\sum_{j=1}^k(1-K_j).
\tag{3}
\]
By the independent erasure reduction in
\(\mathrm{lem{:}hard\text{-}subexperiment\text{-}embedding}\), the
variables \(K_j\) are independent \(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/4)\)
variables, independent of the signs in (1).  Consequently
\[
 N\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(k,3/4),\qquad
 \mathbb EN=\frac{3k}{4},\qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(N)=\frac{3k}{16}.
\tag{4}
\]

Write \(\Lambda=\Lambda_\Gamma(O,R)\) for the randomized interval in
the erasure experiment constructed in
\(\mathrm{def{:}hard\text{-}subexperiment\text{-}embedding}\), and put
\(L_\Lambda=\operatorname{len}(\Lambda)\).  Conditional on \((O,R)\),
the revealed size-two signs are fixed, the size-four contribution is
fixed, and the \(N\) unrevealed size-two signs remain independent fair
Rademacher variables: the observation contains only
\((K_j,K_jV_j)\), while the declared external randomizer \(R\) is
independent of the assignment and of the prior signs.  Since
\(\theta=\sum_lw_lV_l\) on these schedules, its conditional law thus
has the form
\[
 c(O)+\frac1{6k}\sum_{s=1}^{N}\xi_s,
\tag{5}
\]
where, conditionally on \((O,R)\), the \(\xi_s\) are independent fair
Rademacher variables and \(c(O)\) is fixed.  Adjacent support points in
(5) are separated by \(1/(3k)\).

We next prove the elementary atom bound needed below.  If
\(S_N=\sum_{s=1}^N\xi_s\), then its largest atom is
\[
 p_N=2^{-N}\binom{N}{\lfloor N/2\rfloor}.
\]
For \(N=2m\), factorial cancellation gives
\[
 p_{2m}=4^{-m}\binom{2m}{m}
       =\prod_{s=1}^m\left(1-\frac1{2s}\right).
\]
The inequalities \(\log(1-u)\le-u\) for \(0<u<1\) and
\(\sum_{s=1}^m s^{-1}\ge\int_1^{m+1}t^{-1}\,dt=\log(m+1)\) imply
\[
 p_{2m}\le(m+1)^{-1/2}\le\sqrt{\frac2{2m}}.
\tag{6}
\]
For \(N=2m+1\),
\[
 p_{2m+1}=\frac{2m+1}{2m+2}p_{2m}
 \le(m+1)^{-1/2}
 \le\sqrt{\frac2{2m+1}}.
\tag{7}
\]
The case \(N=1\) also satisfies (7).  Hence, for every \(N\ge1\),
\[
 p_N\le\sqrt{\frac2N}.
\tag{8}
\]

An interval of length \(u\) contains at most \(3ku+1\) points of a
lattice with spacing \(1/(3k)\).  It follows from (5) and (8) that on
\(N\ge k/2\), conditionally on \((O,R)\),
\[
 P\{\theta\in\Lambda\mid O,R\}
 \le (3kL_\Lambda+1)\sqrt{\frac2N}.
\tag{9}
\]
In particular, on
\[
 \mathcal A=\left\{N\ge\frac k2,\quad
 L_\Lambda<\frac1{48\sqrt{k}}\right\},
\]
equation (9) and \(k\ge64\) give
\[
 P\{\theta\in\Lambda\mid O,R\}
 \le\left(\frac{\sqrt{k}}{16}+1\right)\frac2{\sqrt{k}}
 =\frac18+\frac2{\sqrt{k}}
 \le\frac38.
\tag{10}
\]
On the event \(N<k/2\), equation (4) gives
\(|N-\mathbb EN|>k/4\), and hence
\(\mathbf 1\{N<k/2\}\le16(N-\mathbb EN)^2/k^2\).  Taking
expectations and using the variance in (4) yields
\[
 P\left(N<\frac k2\right)
 \le \frac{3k/16}{(k/4)^2}
 =\frac3k\le\frac3{64}.
\tag{11}
\]

The pointwise honesty of \(\Gamma\), averaged over the product law
(1), and the coverage identity in
\(\mathrm{lem{:}hard\text{-}subexperiment\text{-}embedding}\) imply
\[
 P\{\theta\in\Lambda\}\ge1-\alpha\ge\frac34.
\tag{12}
\]
On \(\mathcal A\) the conditional coverage is at most \(3/8\) by
(10), and outside \(\mathcal A\) it is at most one.  Therefore
\[
 \frac34
 \le P\{\theta\in\Lambda\}
 \le\frac38+P\left(N<\frac k2\right)
      +P\left(L_\Lambda\ge\frac1{48\sqrt{k}}\right).
\]
Using (11),
\[
 P\left(L_\Lambda\ge\frac1{48\sqrt{k}}\right)
 \ge\frac38-\frac3{64}=\frac{21}{64}\ge\frac14.
\tag{13}
\]
Consequently
\[
 \mathbb E L_\Lambda
 \ge\frac1{48\sqrt{k}}\,
     P\left(L_\Lambda\ge\frac1{48\sqrt{k}}\right)
 \ge\frac1{192\sqrt{k}}.
\tag{14}
\]
The expected-length identity and supremum inequality in
\(\mathrm{lem{:}hard\text{-}subexperiment\text{-}embedding}\) show
that the worst fixed-schedule risk of the original \(\Gamma\) is at
least the product-prior average in (14).  Since \(\Gamma\) was an
arbitrary member of \(\mathfrak C_\alpha\), taking the infimum in
\(\mathrm{def{:}length\text{-}risk}\) proves
\[
 \mathcal R_\alpha(w,q)\ge\frac1{192\sqrt{k}}.
\]
The construction and conditioning retained the independent coordinate
\(R\) throughout, so the argument covers every externally randomized
measurable interval in the decision class.
%%% FIELD proof-unequal-two-scale-family
The total population size is \(2k+4k=6k\).  Therefore a size-two
block has
\[
 w_l=\frac2{6k}=\frac1{3k},\qquad q_l=2^{-2}=\frac14,
\]
and a size-four block has
\[
 w_l=\frac4{6k}=\frac2{3k},\qquad q_l=2^{-4}=\frac1{16},
\]
where the reveal probabilities follow from balanced independent
assignment.  Direct substitution in the definitions of \(S\) and
\(M\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 S
 &=k\frac{(1/(3k))^2}{1/4}
   +k\frac{(2/(3k))^2}{1/16}
   =\frac4{9k}+\frac{64}{9k}
   =\frac{68}{9k},\\
 M
 &=\max\left\{\frac{1/(3k)}{1/4},
                \frac{2/(3k)}{1/16}\right\}
   =\frac{32}{3k}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]
Consequently
\[
 \frac{M\sqrt{\log(1/\alpha)}}{\sqrt S}
 =\frac{32\sqrt{\log(1/\alpha)}}{\sqrt{68k}}
 \longrightarrow0
\tag{16}
\]
for fixed \(\alpha\).  The hypothesis of
\(\mathrm{thm{:}no\text{-}dominance\text{-}upper}\) therefore holds
eventually, and substitution of (15) in that theorem gives the stated
upper bound.  Together with
\(\mathrm{lem{:}two\text{-}scale\text{-}rademacher\text{-}lower}\),
the same calculation also yields the directive's specialized sharp
conclusion
\[
 \mathcal R_\alpha(w,q)=\Theta_\alpha(k^{-1/2})
\]
for every fixed \(0<\alpha\le1/4\).  This invocation is confined to
the present two-scale family and makes no assertion about either
universal linear lower-bound open question.

Finally, the cap breakpoints in
\(\mathrm{def{:}capped\text{-}modulus}\) are \(q_l/w_l\).  They equal
\[
 \frac{1/4}{1/(3k)}=\frac{3k}{4},
 \qquad
 \frac{1/16}{2/(3k)}=\frac{3k}{32}.
\tag{17}
\]
Because \(r\mapsto2^{-r}\) is injective on positive integers, a
single common block size cannot yield both \(1/4\) and \(1/16\); (17)
also shows directly that it cannot yield both breakpoint values.
