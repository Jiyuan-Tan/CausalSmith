\section*{exp\_\allowbreak{}interference\_\allowbreak{}heterogeneous\_\allowbreak{}block\_\allowbreak{}confidence\_\allowbreak{}frontier}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} Unequal clique sizes turn full-neighborhood All Treat and All Control exposure into independent nonidentical categorical reveals. The exact centered block Horvitz--Thompson increment is at most \(w_l/(2q_l)\), giving the clipped half-width \(\sqrt{S\log(2/\alpha)}+M\log(2/\alpha)/6\). Trimming is within a factor \(5/2\) of the capped water-filling modulus and yields \(\mathcal R_\alpha\le5\min\{1,\omega_\alpha\}\). A cubic common-location lower bound proves the exact fixed-confidence consistency boundary, and a separate product-Rademacher argument gives \(\Theta_\alpha(k^{-1/2})\) for the stated two-scale family. The sole unresolved sharp-frontier step is a universal linear weighted-erasure lower bound for all measurable externally randomized intervals. The pinned \(\texttt{szonszein/interference}\) version \(0.1.0\) diagnostic reports structural graph rejection separately from package-versus-reference exposure-label mismatch and releases the corrected interval only after both checks pass.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Existing partial-interference confidence procedures do not characterize worst-case expected length over every measurable externally randomized interval under fixed balanced Bernoulli assignment on unequal cliques. The exact consistency boundary is settled, the capped optimizer and trimming dual are certified, and the unrestricted upper bound is explicit. The sole remaining sharp-frontier gap is to strengthen the proved cubic capped-erasure converse to a universal linear lower bound.

\paragraph{Niche.} The paper treats the known assignment distribution as the sole inferential randomness and exposes three computable design scales: the quadratic rare-exposure scale \(S\), the largest centered-increment scale \(M/2\), and the capped water-filling or trimming scale. The capped scale is necessary and sufficient for fixed-confidence consistency and controls honest length from above even when a dominant rare block makes \(S\) misleading.

\paragraph{Fill.} The proved contribution consists of the exact independent categorical block reduction, the observed-data block Horvitz--Thompson estimator, the clipped Bernstein interval with linear term \(M\log(2/\alpha)/6\), the bias-protected trimmed interval, the certified capped water-filling optimizer, the factor-\(5/2\) trimming dual, the factor-five unrestricted upper bound, the cubic all-procedure lower bound, and the exact consistency equivalence. A dedicated product-Rademacher anti-concentration argument also proves the fixed-confidence \(\Theta_\alpha(k^{-1/2})\) rate for the specified two-scale family. The note leaves the universal linear converse open rather than promoting the special-family lower bound to the full class.

\section{Related work}

TchetgenTchetgenVanderWeele2012, Section 4.3, constructs Hoeffding intervals for binary allocation-strategy effects under two-stage Type-A partial interference, and RigdonHudgens2015 constructs exact confidence sets for those binary two-stage effects. Neither treats this unequal-clique Bernoulli All Treat versus All Control minimax expected-length problem or proves the open water-filling lower frontier. AronowSamii2017 supplies the design--exposure--estimand architecture and exposure-specific inverse weighting, while UganderKarrerBackstromKleinberg2013 shows that full-neighborhood exposure probabilities may be exponentially small in degree. BasseAiroldi2018 gives a nonconcentration warning, UganderYin2023 changes the design to mitigate small exposure probabilities, and KandirosHarshawSavje2026 studies design-based mean-squared-error criteria rather than all-interval confidence length. CaiLow2004 and ArmstrongKolesar2018 motivate modulus arguments in Gaussian experiments, but do not provide the heterogeneous weighted-erasure interval-spread theorem required here. The common-location construction is used only for the proved cubic consistency lower bound, and the product-Rademacher argument is confined to the specified two-scale family.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:partition]\label{ass:partition}
\(B_l\ne\varnothing,\ B_l\cap B_k=\varnothing\text{ for }l\ne k,\ \bigcup_{l=1}^LB_l=U\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (known partial-interference partition with unequal blocks, \cite{TchetgenTchetgenVanderWeele2012}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:balanced-bernoulli]\label{ass:balanced-bernoulli}
\(P_Z=\bigotimes_{i\in U}\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (independent balanced Bernoulli randomization, \cite{AronowSamii2017}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:fixed-schedule]\label{ass:fixed-schedule}
\(Y\text{ is nonrandom under }P_Z\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (design-based fixed potential outcomes, \cite{AronowSamii2017}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:block-exposure]\label{ass:block-exposure}
\(e_i(z)=a\iff z_j=1\ \forall j\in B_l,\quad e_i(z)=b\iff z_j=0\ \forall j\in B_l,\quad e_i(z)=m\text{ otherwise},\quad i\in B_l\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (full-neighborhood exposure mapping on disjoint cliques, \cite{UganderKarrerBackstromKleinberg2013}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:bounded-outcomes]\label{ass:bounded-outcomes}
\(-1/2\le Y_i(d)\le1/2\quad\forall i\in U,\ d\in E\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (uniformly bounded fixed potential outcomes, centered variant, \cite{AronowSamii2017}).
\end{assumption}

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:observed-block-means]\label{lem:observed-block-means}
For every \(m\in\mathcal M_n(B)\), \(\overline Y_l^{\mathrm{obs}}(D)\) is measurable with respect to \(D\), and \(A_l=1\) implies \(\overline Y_l^{\mathrm{obs}}(D)=\mu_l(a)\), while \(C_l=1\) implies \(\overline Y_l^{\mathrm{obs}}(D)=\mu_l(b)\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(m\in\mathcal M_n(B)\) and a block \(B_l\).  By
\(\mathrm{def{:}observed\text{-}block\text{-}means}\),
\[
 \overline Y_l^{\mathrm{obs}}(D)
 =\frac1{r_l}\sum_{i\in B_l}Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}.
\tag{11}
\]
The partition is known by \(\mathrm{ass{:}partition}\), and every
coordinate \(Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}\) is contained in \(D\); hence the
finite sum (11) is \(\sigma(D)\)-measurable.  If \(A_l=1\), then all
assignments in \(B_l\) equal one.  By
\(\mathrm{ass{:}block\text{-}exposure}\), \(e_i(Z)=a\) for every
\(i\in B_l\), and observed-outcome consistency in the definition of
\(Y^{\mathrm{obs}}\) gives \(Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}=Y_i(a)\).
Therefore, by \(\mathrm{def{:}block\text{-}means}\),
\[
 \overline Y_l^{\mathrm{obs}}(D)
 =\frac1{r_l}\sum_{i\in B_l}Y_i(a)=\mu_l(a).
\]
The same argument with all assignments equal to zero shows that
\(C_l=1\) implies
\(\overline Y_l^{\mathrm{obs}}(D)=\mu_l(b)\).
The fixed-schedule assumption ensures that the right sides are
nonrandom under \(P_Z\), and boundedness ensures all displayed finite
averages exist.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This measurability bridge licenses every occurrence of the fixed-schedule block target mean in the expectation calculation without placing it inside the reported statistic. \textit{Gap.} Exposure-weighted estimators are standard, but the clique reduction requires an explicit observed-data equality on each mutually exclusive unanimous event. \textit{Consumer.} The block estimator, clipped interval, and software diagnostic consume only assignment and observed outcomes.

\begin{lemma}[lem:block-reduction]\label{lem:block-reduction}
For every \(m\in\mathcal M_n(B)\), the vectors \((A_l,C_l,H_l)\) are independent across \(l\), each has categorical probabilities \((q_l,q_l,1-2q_l)\), and \(A_lC_l=0\). The observed-data construction in \(\mathrm{def{:}block\text{-}ht}\) satisfies \(\mathbb E_Z[X_l(D)]=w_l\{\mu_l(a)-\mu_l(b)\}\), \(\sum_l\operatorname{Var}_Z\{X_l(D)\}\le S/2\), and \(|X_l(D)-\mathbb E_ZX_l(D)|\le w_l/(2q_l)\le M/2\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a block \(B_l\). By \(\mathrm{ass{:}balanced\text{-}bernoulli}\), each of its \(2^{r_l}\) assignment vectors has probability \(2^{-r_l}=q_l\). Exactly one vector is all treated and exactly one is all controlled. Thus
\[
 P_Z(A_l=1)=q_l,\qquad P_Z(C_l=1)=q_l,
 \qquad P_Z(H_l=1)=1-2q_l,
\tag{1}
\]
and \(A_lC_l=0\) on every assignment. By \(\mathrm{ass{:}partition}\), distinct triples \((A_l,C_l,H_l)\) depend on disjoint coordinates of \(Z\); their independence follows from \(\mathrm{ass{:}balanced\text{-}bernoulli}\).

Put \(u_l=\mu_l(a)\) and \(v_l=\mu_l(b)\). The fixed-schedule and bounded-outcome assumptions, together with \(\mathrm{def{:}block\text{-}means}\), give
\[
 -\frac12\le u_l,v_l\le\frac12.
\tag{2}
\]
On the all-treated and all-control events, \(\mathrm{lem{:}observed\text{-}block\text{-}means}\) identifies the observed block mean with \(u_l\) and \(v_l\), respectively. Mutual exclusion then rewrites the observed statistic, on every assignment, as
\[
 X_l=\frac{w_l}{q_l}(A_lu_l-C_lv_l).
\tag{3}
\]
Using (1), fixedness of \((u_l,v_l)\), and \(A_lC_l=0\),
\[
 \mathbb E_ZX_l=w_l(u_l-v_l),
\tag{4}
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Var}_Z(X_l)
 &\le\mathbb E_ZX_l^2\\
 &=\frac{w_l^2}{q_l}(u_l^2+v_l^2)\\
 &\le\frac{w_l^2}{2q_l}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
The last step uses (2). Summing (5) and using the definition of \(S\) proves
\[
 \sum_{l=1}^L\operatorname{Var}_Z(X_l)
 \le\frac12\sum_{l=1}^L\frac{w_l^2}{q_l}=\frac S2.
\tag{6}
\]

It remains to compute the exact centered increment. Write \(W_l=X_l-\mathbb E_ZX_l\). Equations (3)--(4) give the following three exhaustive cases:
\[
 \frac{W_l}{w_l}=
 \begin{cases}
  (q_l^{-1}-1)u_l+v_l,&A_l=1,\\
  -u_l-(q_l^{-1}-1)v_l,&C_l=1,\\
  -u_l+v_l,&H_l=1.
 \end{cases}
\tag{7}
\]
Since \(r_l\ge1\), one has \(0<q_l\le1/2\). By (2), the first two rows of (7) have absolute value at most
\[
 \frac12(q_l^{-1}-1)+\frac12=\frac1{2q_l}.
\tag{8}
\]
The third row has absolute value at most one, which is at most \(1/(2q_l)\) because \(q_l\le1/2\). Hence on every assignment
\[
 |X_l-\mathbb E_ZX_l|=|W_l|
 \le\frac{w_l}{2q_l}\le\frac M2.
\tag{9}
\]
This is an enumeration of All Treat, All Control, and every other assignment, rather than a triangle bound on \(|X_l|+|\mathbb E_ZX_l|\).

For the smallest unequal stress test, take a singleton block and a two-unit block. Their \(2\cdot4=8\) joint assignments give categorical counts \((1,1,0)\) and \((1,1,2)\), respectively. Substitution in (7), for arbitrary \(u_l,v_l\in[-1/2,1/2]\), verifies (8)--(9) on each of the eight assignments. Thus the asserted support, mean, variance bound, and increment bound also hold exactly at this finite boundary case.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The exact categorical reduction preserves mutual exclusion and supplies the heterogeneous variance and increment scales for an implementable statistic. \textit{Gap.} AronowSamii2017 gives general exposure-probability Horvitz--Thompson algebra, while UganderKarrerBackstromKleinberg2013 studies full-neighborhood exposure; neither isolates this independent nonidentical weighted-erasure experiment. \textit{Consumer.} The upper interval, lower prior, and package diagnostic use the same exact block law and do not approximate dependence within blocks.

\begin{theorem}[thm:finite-upper]\label{thm:finite-upper}
For every \(0<\alpha\le1/4\) and every \(m\in\mathcal M_n(B)\), the interval \(I_{\mathrm{HT},\alpha}(D)\), which is a measurable function solely of \(D\) and known design quantities, obeys \(P_{Z,m}\{\theta\in I_{\mathrm{HT},\alpha}(D)\}\ge1-\alpha\) and \(\operatorname{len}\{I_{\mathrm{HT},\alpha}(D)\}\le2\min\{1,\sqrt{S\log(2/\alpha)}+(M/6)\log(2/\alpha)\}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(m\in\mathcal M_n(B)\), and define
\[
 W_l=X_l-\mathbb E_ZX_l,\qquad z=\log(2/\alpha)>0.
\tag{10}
\]
By \(\mathrm{lem{:}block\text{-}reduction}\), the variables \(W_l\) are independent and centered and obey
\[
 v:=\sum_{l=1}^L\mathbb E_ZW_l^2\le\frac S2,
 \qquad |W_l|\le\frac M2.
\tag{11}
\]
Apply \(\mathrm{lem{:}finite\text{-}product\text{-}exponential}\) with \(c=M/2\) and the value of \(z\) in (10). Because its threshold is nondecreasing in \(v\), (11) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P_Z\!\left\{\left|\sum_{l=1}^LW_l\right|>
 \sqrt{S\log(2/\alpha)}+\frac M6\log(2/\alpha)\right\}
 &\le2\exp\{-\log(2/\alpha)\}\\
 &=\alpha.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
The expectation identity in \(\mathrm{lem{:}block\text{-}reduction}\), followed by the definition of the causal estimand, yields
\[
 \sum_{l=1}^L\mathbb E_ZX_l
 =\sum_{l=1}^Lw_l\{\mu_l(a)-\mu_l(b)\}=\theta.
\tag{13}
\]
Since \(\widehat\theta=\sum_lX_l\) by \(\mathrm{def{:}block\text{-}ht}\), equations (12)--(13) and the corrected construction in \(\mathrm{def{:}clipped\text{-}ht}\) imply
\[
 P_{Z,m}\{|\widehat\theta-\theta|\le h_\alpha\}\ge1-\alpha.
\tag{14}
\]
Every \(X_l\), and hence \(\widehat\theta\), is measurable from \(D\) by \(\mathrm{def{:}block\text{-}ht}\) and \(\mathrm{lem{:}observed\text{-}block\text{-}means}\). The quantities \(S\), \(M\), and \(\alpha\) are known design inputs. Moreover, bounded outcomes give \(\mu_l(a)-\mu_l(b)\in[-1,1]\), and the partition together with \(w_l=r_l/n\) gives \(\sum_lw_l=1\). Thus \(\theta\in[-1,1]\). On the event in (14), intersecting the raw interval with \([-1,1]\) cannot remove \(\theta\), proving
\[
 P_{Z,m}\{\theta\in I_{\mathrm{HT},\alpha}(D)\}\ge1-\alpha.
\tag{15}
\]
Finally, the length of an intersection is at most the length of each intersected interval, whence, pointwise in \(D\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{len}\{I_{\mathrm{HT},\alpha}(D)\}
 &\le\min\{2,2h_\alpha\}\\
 &=2\min\!\left\{1,\sqrt{S\log(2/\alpha)}
       +\frac M6\log(2/\alpha)\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
This deterministic finite-sample certificate holds on every assignment, including all eight assignments in the unequal three-unit stress test of \(\mathrm{lem{:}block\text{-}reduction}\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is a genuinely observed-data finite-sample interval, and the exact centered-increment enumeration sharpens its linear Bernstein term to \(M\log(2/\alpha)/6\). \textit{Gap.} TchetgenTchetgenVanderWeele2012, Section 4.3, constructs Hoeffding intervals for binary allocation-strategy effects under two-stage Type-A partial interference, and RigdonHudgens2015 constructs exact confidence sets for those binary two-stage effects; neither treats this unequal-clique Bernoulli All Treat versus All Control minimax expected-length problem or proves the open water-filling lower frontier. \textit{Consumer.} An analyst using full-neighborhood exposures obtains a computable finite-sample interval and sees exactly when a single rare block controls the Bernstein correction.

\begin{lemma}[lem:hard-subexperiment-embedding]\label{lem:hard-subexperiment-embedding}
For every \(v=(v_1,\ldots,v_L)\in\{-1/2,1/2\}^L\), the fixed block-homogeneous schedule \(m_v\) in \(\mathrm{def{:}hard\text{-}subexperiment\text{-}embedding}\) belongs to \(\mathcal M_n(B)\), because it is fixed and all outcomes lie in \([-1/2,1/2]\); moreover, \(\mu_l^v(a)=v_l\), \(\mu_l^v(b)=0\), and \(\theta(m_v)=\sum_{l=1}^Lw_lv_l=T(v)\). Under \(m_v\), \(K_l=A_l=\mathbf 1\{Z_{B_l}=\mathbf 1_{B_l}\}\), the \(K_l\) are independent \(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(q_l)\), \(Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}=K_lv_l\) for \(i\in B_l\), and \(O_l=(K_l,K_lv_l)\) is measurable from \(D\). The remaining assignment information is retained exactly: \(\operatorname{Law}(Z\mid K=k)=\bigotimes_{l=1}^L\nu_{l,k_l}\), where \(\nu_{l,1}=\delta_{\mathbf 1_{B_l}}\) and \(\nu_{l,0}=\operatorname{Uniform}(\{0,1\}^{B_l}\setminus\{\mathbf 1_{B_l}\})\), so the \(K_l=0\) branch includes all-control and every mixed pattern with their original conditional probabilities. With the measure-preserving splitter \(\beta(R)=(R_0,R_1,\ldots,R_L)\), the samplers \(F_l\), and \(D^*\) of the same definition, \(\Lambda_\Gamma(O,R)=\Gamma(D^*(O,R_1,\ldots,R_L),R_0)\) is measurable in \((O,R)\) for every measurable externally randomized \(\Gamma\). For every fixed \(v\), \[\operatorname{Law}(D^*,R_0\mid V=v)=\operatorname{Law}_{Z,R_0,m_v}(D,R_0),\] and therefore \[P_{K,R}\{T(v)\in\Lambda_\Gamma(O,R)\mid V=v\}=P_{Z,R_0,m_v}\{\theta(m_v)\in\Gamma(D,R_0)\},\qquad \mathbb E_{K,R}[\operatorname{len}\{\Lambda_\Gamma(O,R)\}\mid V=v]=\mathbb E_{Z,R_0,m_v}[\operatorname{len}\{\Gamma(D,R_0)\}].\] Consequently, if \(\Gamma\in\mathfrak C_\alpha\), then \(\Lambda_\Gamma\) has coverage at least \(1-\alpha\) for every fixed \(v\), \(Q_t\{T(V)\in\Lambda_\Gamma(O,R)\}\ge1-\alpha\) for every \(t\in\mathcal T_{\mathrm{tilt}}\), and \[\sup_{m'\in\mathcal M_n(B)}\mathbb E_{Z,R,m'}[\operatorname{len}\{\Gamma(D,R)\}]\ge\sup_{v\in\{-1/2,1/2\}^L}\mathbb E_{K,R}[\operatorname{len}\{\Lambda_\Gamma(O,R)\}\mid V=v]\ge\mathbb E_{Q_t}[\operatorname{len}\{\Lambda_\Gamma(O,R)\}]\] for every admissible \(t\). Taking the supremum over admissible \(t\), applying a weighted-erasure spread result if and when it is proved, and then taking the infimum over \(\Gamma\in\mathfrak C_\alpha\) yields the minimax lower bound. Thus the bridge preserves the entire randomized decision experiment; it does not assert the weighted-erasure posterior-spread inequality.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For fixed \(v\), the schedule in
\(\mathrm{def{:}hard\text{-}subexperiment\text{-}embedding}\) is a
nonrandom array, so it satisfies
\(\mathrm{ass{:}fixed\text{-}schedule}\).  Its entries are either
\(v_l\in\{-1/2,1/2\}\) or zero, so it satisfies
\(\mathrm{ass{:}bounded\text{-}outcomes}\).  It uses the fixed design,
partition, and exposure map from the model class, hence \(m_v\in
\mathcal M_n(B)\).  Direct substitution in
\(\mathrm{def{:}block\text{-}means}\) gives
\[
 \mu_l^v(a)=v_l,\qquad \mu_l^v(b)=0,
\]
and therefore
\[
 \theta(m_v)=\sum_lw_l\{\mu_l^v(a)-\mu_l^v(b)\}
 =\sum_lw_lv_l=T(v).
\tag{22}
\]

By the block exposure rule, an outcome in \(B_l\) equals \(v_l\)
exactly on the all-treated pattern and equals zero on every other
pattern.  Thus
\[
 K_l=A_l=\mathbf1\{Z_{B_l}=\mathbf1_{B_l}\},\qquad
 Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}=K_lv_l\quad(i\in B_l),
\tag{23}
\]
and \(O_l=(K_l,K_lv_l)\) is measurable from \(D\).  The block
assignments are independent.  Within a block all \(2^{r_l}\) patterns
have equal probability, whence
\[
 \operatorname{Law}(Z_{B_l}\mid K_l=1)
 =\delta_{\mathbf1_{B_l}},\qquad
 \operatorname{Law}(Z_{B_l}\mid K_l=0)
 =\operatorname{Uniform}
 \bigl(\{0,1\}^{B_l}\setminus\{\mathbf1_{B_l}\}\bigr).
\tag{24}
\]
This proves both the stated Bernoulli law of \(K_l\) and the product
conditional kernel \(\bigotimes_l\nu_{l,k_l}\).  In particular, (24)
retains the all-control assignment together with all mixed patterns;
none is discarded as ancillary.

For completeness, the randomization devices in the construction may
be realized explicitly.  Outside the null set of dyadic rationals,
write the binary digits of \(R\), and distribute successive digits
cyclically among \(L+1\) coordinates.  Independent fair binary digits
show on every dyadic cylinder that the resulting coordinates
\((R_0,\ldots,R_L)\) have product Lebesgue law; extension from
cylinders gives the asserted pushforward, and arbitrary values on the
null exceptional set preserve it.  Because each \(\nu_{l,k}\) has
finite support, \(F_l(k,\cdot)\) can be its inverse-distribution
sampler and is measurable.

Under \(Q_t\), \(K_l\) are independent Bernoulli variables with
probabilities \(q_l\), and the split coordinates are independent
uniforms.  Equations (24) and the definition of \(F_l\) therefore give,
conditional on \(V=v\),
\[
 \operatorname{Law}(Z^*\mid K=k,V=v)
 =\bigotimes_l\nu_{l,k_l}
 =\operatorname{Law}_{Z,m_v}(Z\mid K=k).
\tag{25}
\]
Equations (23) and (25) imply
\[
 \operatorname{Law}(D^*,R_0\mid V=v)
 =\operatorname{Law}_{Z,R_0,m_v}(D,R_0).
\tag{26}
\]
Composition of the measurable samplers with a measurable
\(\Gamma\) proves measurability of \(\Lambda_\Gamma\).
Because (22) identifies the two fixed targets and (26) identifies the
entire randomized observations, it follows that
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P_{K,R}\{T(v)\in\Lambda_\Gamma(O,R)\mid V=v\}
 &=P_{Z,R_0,m_v}\{\theta(m_v)\in\Gamma(D,R_0)\},\\
 \mathbb E_{K,R}[\operatorname{len}\{\Lambda_\Gamma(O,R)\}\mid V=v]
 &=\mathbb E_{Z,R_0,m_v}[\operatorname{len}\{\Gamma(D,R_0)\}].
\end{aligned}
\tag{27}
\]

If \(\Gamma\in\mathfrak C_\alpha\), its pointwise honesty applied to
each \(m_v\) makes the first line of (27) at least \(1-\alpha\).
Integrating that inequality over \(V\sim\pi_t\), using nonnegativity
and Tonelli's theorem, gives
\[
 Q_t\{T(V)\in\Lambda_\Gamma(O,R)\}\ge1-\alpha.
\tag{28}
\]
Similarly, the second line of (27) and the fact that a prior average
does not exceed a supremum give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sup_{m'\in\mathcal M_n(B)}
 \mathbb E_{Z,R,m'}\operatorname{len}\{\Gamma(D,R)\}
 &\ge\sup_v\mathbb E_{K,R}
 [\operatorname{len}\{\Lambda_\Gamma(O,R)\}\mid V=v]\\
 &\ge\mathbb E_{Q_t}
 \operatorname{len}\{\Lambda_\Gamma(O,R)\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{29}
\]
This proves every asserted bridge statement.  Equations (28)--(29)
use no posterior-spread claim; such a claim would be a separate lower
bound in the weighted-erasure experiment.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This exact Markov-kernel embedding retains every assignment ancillary and transfers pointwise coverage and expected length for arbitrary externally randomized original-data intervals. \textit{Gap.} The weighted-erasure lower experiment is not automatically a subexperiment for arbitrary procedures unless the unrevealed assignment patterns and external randomization are reconstructed with their exact laws. \textit{Consumer.} The posterior-spread question and no-dominance minimax theorem can invoke a rigorous experiment-to-experiment reduction without assuming ancillary irrelevance.

\begin{openendedquestion}[oeq:weighted-erasure-spread]\label{oeq:weighted-erasure-spread}
Establish or refute the following assertion using \(H_{\mathrm{WE}}\). There exist universal \(c_0,c_1,c_2\in(0,1)\) such that, for every \(0<\alpha\le1/4\) and every \((w,q)\) satisfying \(\sum_{l=1}^Lq_ls_l^2=1\) and \(\|s\|_\infty\sqrt{x_\alpha}\le c_0\), the interval \([-c_2\sqrt{x_\alpha},c_2\sqrt{x_\alpha}]\) is contained in \(\mathcal T_{\mathrm{tilt}}\), and every measurable \(\Lambda(O,R)\) obeys \[\inf_{|t|\le c_2\sqrt{x_\alpha}}Q_t\{T(V)\in\Lambda(O,R)\}\ge1-\alpha\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\sup_{|t|\le c_2\sqrt{x_\alpha}}\mathbb E_{Q_t}[\operatorname{len}\{\Lambda(O,R)\}]\ge c_1\min\{1,\sqrt{Sx_\alpha}\}.\] Here \(Q_t=\pi_t\otimes P_K\otimes\operatorname{Uniform}[0,1]\): every probability and expectation integrates \(V\sim\pi_t\), \(K\sim P_K\) independently of \(V\), and the external \(R\) independently of \((V,K)\). The assertion is solely the posterior-spread inequality. The pointwise-coverage and worst-case-to-prior-average transfer for \(\Lambda_\Gamma\) is supplied by \(\mathrm{lem{:}hard\text{-}subexperiment\text{-}embedding}\), not assumed here.
\end{openendedquestion}

\begin{theorem}[thm:trimmed-upper]\label{thm:trimmed-upper}
For every \(0<\alpha\le1/4\), there exists an interval in \(\mathfrak C_\alpha\), measurable from \(D\), obtained by minimizing over \(J\), applying the observed-data block Horvitz--Thompson estimator on \(J\), and adding omitted contrast allowance \(\sum_{l\notin J}w_l\), whose length is at most \(2\min\{1,\rho_\alpha(w,q)\}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a deterministic subset \(J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,L\}\) and define
\[
 \widehat\theta_J=\sum_{l\in J}X_l,
 \qquad
 \theta_J=\sum_{l\in J}w_l\{\mu_l(a)-\mu_l(b)\},
\]
\[
 S_J=\sum_{l\in J}\frac{w_l^2}{q_l},\qquad
 M_J=\max_{l\in J}\frac{w_l}{q_l},\qquad
 b_J=\sum_{l\notin J}w_l,
\tag{17}
\]
with \(M_\varnothing=0\). For \(l\in J\), \(\mathrm{lem{:}block\text{-}reduction}\) gives independent centered variables \(W_l=X_l-\mathbb E_ZX_l\) satisfying
\[
 \sum_{l\in J}\mathbb E_ZW_l^2\le\frac{S_J}{2},
 \qquad
 |W_l|\le\frac{w_l}{2q_l}\le\frac{M_J}{2}.
\tag{18}
\]
If \(J\ne\varnothing\), apply \(\mathrm{lem{:}finite\text{-}product\text{-}exponential}\) with \(v\le S_J/2\), \(c=M_J/2\), and \(y=\log(2/\alpha)>0\). If \(J=\varnothing\), the same conclusion holds deterministically because both sums are zero. In either case,
\[
 P_Z\!\left\{|\widehat\theta_J-\theta_J|>
 \sqrt{S_Jy}+\frac{M_Jy}{6}\right\}\le\alpha.
\tag{19}
\]
Bounded outcomes imply \(|\mu_l(a)-\mu_l(b)|\le1\), so
\[
 |\theta-\theta_J|
 =\left|\sum_{l\notin J}w_l\{\mu_l(a)-\mu_l(b)\}\right|
 \le b_J.
\tag{20}
\]
Consequently the observed-data interval
\[
 I_J(D)=[-1,1]\cap
 \left[\widehat\theta_J-b_J-\sqrt{S_Jy}-\frac{M_Jy}{6},
       \widehat\theta_J+b_J+\sqrt{S_Jy}+\frac{M_Jy}{6}\right]
\tag{21}
\]
has coverage at least \(1-\alpha\) by (19)--(20). Each \(X_l\) is measurable from \(D\), and \(J\), \(S_J\), \(M_J\), and \(b_J\) are fixed known-design quantities, so \(I_J\) is measurable from \(D\). Its pointwise length is at most
\[
 2\min\!\left\{1,b_J+\sqrt{S_Jy}+\frac{M_Jy}{6}\right\}.
\tag{22}
\]
There are finitely many subsets. Choose a minimizer of the known expression in (22), using a fixed deterministic tie rule. The selected subset is nonrandom, so (19) retains its coverage. By the corrected construction in \(\mathrm{def{:}trimmed\text{-}radius}\), the expression after the leading one in (22) is exactly \(\rho_\alpha(w,q)\). Thus the selected interval lies in \(\mathfrak C_\alpha\) and has length at most \(2\min\{1,\rho_\alpha(w,q)\}\), as claimed.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Trimming prevents a low-weight, astronomically rare block from making the global interval inconsistent through inverse-probability amplification. \textit{Gap.} KandirosHarshawSavje2026 removes difficult conflict-graph vertices for mean-squared error; this fixed-design procedure protects omitted causal contrast and retains finite-sample coverage. \textit{Consumer.} The interval tells practitioners when to bias-protect a negligible rare block rather than report an explosive inverse-probability radius.

 \mathcal R_\alpha(w,q)\ge c\min\{1,\omega_\alpha(w,q)\}.
\]
The lower bound must hold when the infimum defining \(\mathcal R_\alpha\) ranges over every measurable interval procedure, including procedures randomized by the independent external randomizer \(R\). This universal linear lower bound is the sole residual question.

\begin{proposition}[prop:capped-modulus-exhibit]\label{prop:capped-modulus-exhibit}
For the unequal six-block population with \(r=(1,1,1,3,3,3)\), \(n=12\), and \(\alpha=1/4\), the three size-three coordinates cap and the three size-one coordinates do not. Explicitly, \(\lambda_\alpha=\sqrt{24(\log4-3/8)}\), \(\omega_{1/4}=3/4+\sqrt{(\log4-3/8)/24}\approx0.9553\), and the subset retaining only size-one blocks gives \(\rho_0\le3/4+1/\sqrt{24}\approx0.9541\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By the definitions of \(w_l\) and \(q_l\), each of the three blocks of size one has
\[
 (w_l,q_l)=\left(\frac{1}{12},\frac{1}{2}\right),
 \qquad \frac{w_l}{q_l}=\frac{1}{6},
\]
whereas each of the three blocks of size three has
\[
 (w_l,q_l)=\left(\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{8}\right),
 \qquad \frac{w_l}{q_l}=2.
\tag{1}
\]
At \(\alpha=1/4\), the quadratic budget in \(\mathrm{def{:}capped\text{-}modulus}\) is \(x_\alpha=\log 4\).  Moreover,
\[
 \sum_{l=1}^L q_l=3\left(\frac12\right)+3\left(\frac18\right)=\frac{15}{8}>\log4,
\]
where \(\log4<3/2\) follows, for example, from
\(e^{3/2}>1+3/2+(3/2)^2/2+(3/2)^3/6>4\).
Thus this is the finite-multiplier case of
\(\mathrm{def{:}capped\text{-}modulus}\).  By the water-filling characterization in
\(\mathrm{lem{:}capped\text{-}trimming\text{-}duality}\), a coordinate is capped exactly when
\(\lambda_\alpha w_l/q_l\ge 1\).  The breakpoints \(q_l/w_l\) are therefore \(1/2\) for a size-three block and \(6\) for a size-one block.

Suppose that \(1/2<\lambda_\alpha<6\).  Then the budget equation in
\(\mathrm{def{:}capped\text{-}modulus}\) becomes
\[
 3\left(\frac18\right)
 +3\left(\frac12\right)\left(\frac{\lambda_\alpha}{6}\right)^2
 =\frac38+\frac{\lambda_\alpha^2}{24}
 =\log4.
\]
Its unique positive solution is
\[
 \lambda_\alpha=\sqrt{24\left(\log4-\frac38\right)}.
\tag{2}
\]
This solution indeed lies in the supposed interval.  To see this without relying on decimal rounding, \(k!\ge 2^{k-1}\) for every \(k\ge2\), with strict inequality for some \(k\), and hence the exponential series gives
\[
 e=2+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac1{k!}
 <2+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}2^{-(k-1)}=3<4.
\]
Thus \(\log4>1\), while the preceding exponential-series lower bound gives \(\log4<3/2\).  Consequently
\[
 15<24\left(\log4-\frac38\right)<27,
\]
so in particular \(1/2<\lambda_\alpha<6\).  Thus the three size-three coordinates are capped and the three size-one coordinates are not, and uniqueness in
\(\mathrm{lem{:}capped\text{-}trimming\text{-}duality}\) confirms that (2) is the optimizer's multiplier.

Substituting this optimizer into the linear objective gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \omega_{1/4}(w,q)
 &=3\left(\frac14\right)
   +3\left(\frac1{12}\right)\frac{\lambda_\alpha}{6}\\
 &=\frac34+\frac{\lambda_\alpha}{24}
  =\frac34+\sqrt{\frac{\log4-3/8}{24}}
  \approx0.9553.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
Finally, in the infimum defining \(\rho_0(w,q)\), choose \(J\) to be the three size-one blocks.  The omitted weight and retained quadratic term are, respectively,
\[
 \sum_{l\notin J}w_l=3\left(\frac14\right)=\frac34,
 \qquad
 \sum_{l\in J}\frac{w_l^2}{q_l}
 =3\frac{(1/12)^2}{1/2}=\frac1{24}.
\]
This feasible choice in \(\mathrm{def{:}trimmed\text{-}radius}\) proves
\[
 \rho_0(w,q)\le \frac34+\frac1{\sqrt{24}}\approx0.9541,
\]
which completes every asserted calculation.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The numerical exhibit exposes the cap threshold and the near-matching trimming certificate in a reproducible unequal-block instance. \textit{Gap.} An equal-block substitution has only one breakpoint and therefore cannot reproduce simultaneous capped and uncapped coordinates. \textit{Consumer.} The software diagnostic uses this instance as a unit test for sorting, multiplier solution, and trimming output.

\begin{theorem}[thm:consistency-boundary]\label{thm:consistency-boundary}
For every triangular array \((B^{(n)},w^{(n)},q^{(n)})\) and every fixed \(0<\alpha\le1/4\), \[\mathcal R_\alpha(w^{(n)},q^{(n)})\to0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\omega_\alpha(w^{(n)},q^{(n)})\to0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\rho_0(w^{(n)},q^{(n)})\to0.\] In particular, \(S^{(n)}\to0\) is sufficient but not necessary.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(0<\alpha\le1/4\), put \(x=\log(1/\alpha)>1\), and write
\[
 \omega_z(w,q)=\max\left\{\sum_{l=1}^Lw_l\delta_l:
 0\le\delta_l\le1,\quad
 \sum_{l=1}^Lq_l\delta_l^2\le z\right\}.
\tag{63}
\]
Thus \(\omega_\alpha=\omega_x\), whereas \(\omega_1\) is the budget-one modulus. Since \(x>1\), nesting gives \(\omega_1\le\omega_x\). Conversely, if \(\delta\) is feasible at budget \(x\), then \(\delta/\sqrt{x}\in[0,1]^L\), its quadratic cost is at most one, and its objective is the original objective divided by \(\sqrt{x}\). Taking suprema proves
\[
 \omega_1\le\omega_\alpha\le\sqrt{x}\,\omega_1.
\tag{64}
\]
By \(\mathrm{lem{:}capped\text{-}trimming\text{-}duality}\),
\[
 \omega_1\le\rho_0\le2\omega_1.
\tag{65}
\]
Because \(x\) is fixed along the triangular array, (64)--(65) imply
\[
 \omega_\alpha\to0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\rho_0\to0.
\tag{66}
\]
The unrestricted upper theorem gives
\[
 \mathcal R_\alpha(w,q)\le5\min\{1,\omega_\alpha(w,q)\},
\tag{67}
\]
so \(\omega_\alpha\to0\) implies \(\mathcal R_\alpha\to0\). Conversely, \(\mathrm{lem{:}capped\text{-}erasure\text{-}consistency\text{-}lower}\) gives row by row
\[
 \mathcal R_\alpha(w,q)\ge\frac{\omega_\alpha(w,q)^3}{136x}.
\tag{68}
\]
Since \(136x\) is fixed, finite, and positive, \(\mathcal R_\alpha\to0\) forces \(\omega_\alpha\to0\). Equations (66)--(68) prove the three-way equivalence.

For sufficiency of \(S^{(n)}\to0\), positivity of every \(q_l\) and Cauchy--Schwarz give, for each vector feasible at budget \(x\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_{l=1}^Lw_l\delta_l
 &=\sum_{l=1}^L\frac{w_l}{\sqrt{q_l}}\sqrt{q_l}\delta_l\\
 &\le\left(\sum_{l=1}^L\frac{w_l^2}{q_l}\right)^{1/2}
       \left(\sum_{l=1}^Lq_l\delta_l^2\right)^{1/2}
 \le\sqrt{xS}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{69}
\]
Maximizing proves \(\omega_\alpha\le\sqrt{xS}\); (67) then shows that \(S^{(n)}\to0\) is sufficient.

For nonnecessity, for each integer \(k\ge1\) set \(n_k=2k\,2^k\). Partition the \(n_k\) units into one block of size \(2k\) and \(n_k-2k\) singleton blocks. These blocks are nonempty, disjoint, and exhaustive. Under balanced Bernoulli assignment, the large block has
\[
 w_{\mathrm{big}}=\frac{2k}{n_k}=2^{-k},
 \qquad q_{\mathrm{big}}=2^{-2k},
 \qquad \frac{w_{\mathrm{big}}^2}{q_{\mathrm{big}}}=1.
\tag{70}
\]
Every singleton has \(w_l=1/n_k\) and \(q_l=1/2\). Therefore
\[
 S^{(n_k)}=1+\frac{2(n_k-2k)}{n_k^2}\longrightarrow1.
\tag{71}
\]
Let \(J_k\) contain all singleton blocks and put \(y=\log(2/\alpha)\). The omitted weight is \(2^{-k}\), the retained quadratic sum is \(2(n_k-2k)/n_k^2\), and the largest retained ratio is \(2/n_k\). The corrected trimmed radius gives
\[
 \rho_\alpha(w^{(n_k)},q^{(n_k)})
 \le2^{-k}+\sqrt{\frac{2y(n_k-2k)}{n_k^2}}
       +\frac{y}{3n_k}
 \longrightarrow0.
\tag{72}
\]
The lower comparison \(\omega_\alpha\le\rho_\alpha\), followed by (67), gives \(\mathcal R_\alpha(w^{(n_k)},q^{(n_k)})\to0\), whereas (71) shows \(S^{(n_k)}\not\to0\). This explicit unequal-clique triangular array proves nonnecessity.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The cubic all-procedure lower bound and trimmed upper bound prove the full fixed-confidence equivalence R\_alpha->0 iff omega\_alpha->0 iff rho\_0->0. \textit{Gap.} Only the sharp nonzero finite-confidence relation R\_alpha comparable to min{1,omega\_alpha} remains open; no direction of the zero-versus-nonzero consistency boundary is unresolved. \textit{Consumer.} Designers obtain the intrinsic consistency boundary now; resolving the linear OEQs would add sharp expected-length rates and constants, not alter consistency.

\begin{proposition}[prop:unequal-two-scale-family]\label{prop:unequal-two-scale-family}
Let \(B^{(k)}\) contain \(k\) blocks of size two and \(k\) blocks of size four, so \(n=6k\). The two strata have \((w_l,q_l)=(1/(3k),1/4)\) and \((w_l,q_l)=(2/(3k),1/16)\), respectively, and
\[
 S=\frac{68}{9k},\qquad M=\frac{32}{3k},\qquad
 \frac{M\sqrt{\log(1/\alpha)}}{\sqrt S}
 =\frac{32\sqrt{\log(1/\alpha)}}{\sqrt{68k}}.
\]
Thus, for every fixed \(0<\alpha\le1/4\), the no-dominance condition holds for all sufficiently large \(k\). With \(C_1\) denoting the universal constant in \(\mathrm{thm{:}no\text{-}dominance\text{-}upper}\), for every sufficiently large \(k\),
\[
 \frac{1}{192\sqrt{k}}
 \le \mathcal R_\alpha(w,q)
 \le C_1\sqrt{\frac{68\log(1/\alpha)}{9k}},
\]
and consequently \(\mathcal R_\alpha(w,q)=\Theta_\alpha(k^{-1/2})\). The two water-filling breakpoints \(q_l/w_l\) are \(3k/4\) and \(3k/32\). No common block size can reproduce both reveal probabilities, or both breakpoints.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The population size is \(2k+4k=6k\). Hence a size-two block has
\[
 w_l=\frac2{6k}=\frac1{3k},\qquad q_l=2^{-2}=\frac14,
\]
whereas a size-four block has
\[
 w_l=\frac4{6k}=\frac2{3k},\qquad q_l=2^{-4}=\frac1{16}.
\]
The reveal probabilities follow from \(\mathrm{ass{:}balanced\text{-}bernoulli}\). Substitution into \(S\) and \(M\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 S
 &=k\frac{(1/(3k))^2}{1/4}
   +k\frac{(2/(3k))^2}{1/16}
   =\frac4{9k}+\frac{64}{9k}
   =\frac{68}{9k},\\
 M
 &=\max\left\{\frac{1/(3k)}{1/4},
                \frac{2/(3k)}{1/16}\right\}
   =\frac{32}{3k}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{58}
\]
Consequently
\[
 \frac{M\sqrt{\log(1/\alpha)}}{\sqrt S}
 =\frac{32\sqrt{\log(1/\alpha)}}{\sqrt{68k}}
 \longrightarrow0
\tag{59}
\]
for fixed \(\alpha\). Thus \(\mathrm{thm{:}no\text{-}dominance\text{-}upper}\) applies for all sufficiently large \(k\), and (58) yields
\[
 \mathcal R_\alpha(w,q)
 \le C_1\sqrt{\frac{68\log(1/\alpha)}{9k}}.
\tag{60}
\]
For \(k\ge64\), \(\mathrm{lem{:}two\text{-}scale\text{-}rademacher\text{-}lower}\) gives
\[
 \mathcal R_\alpha(w,q)\ge\frac1{192\sqrt{k}}.
\tag{61}
\]
Equations (60)--(61) prove \(\mathcal R_\alpha(w,q)=\Theta_\alpha(k^{-1/2})\). The lower bound is specialized to the repeated size-two stratum and makes no assertion about either universal weighted-erasure open question.

Finally, the cap breakpoints are \(q_l/w_l\), namely
\[
 \frac{1/4}{1/(3k)}=\frac{3k}{4},
 \qquad
 \frac{1/16}{2/(3k)}=\frac{3k}{32}.
\tag{62}
\]
Since \(r\mapsto2^{-r}\) is injective on the positive integers, one common block size cannot reproduce both reveal probabilities. The two distinct values in (62) likewise rule out a common breakpoint.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Exact design algebra, the observed-data upper interval, and product-Rademacher posterior anti-concentration on the \(k\) size-two blocks jointly give a refutable fixed-confidence \(\Theta_\alpha(k^{-1/2})\) prediction. \textit{Gap.} This family-specific lower bound uses its repeated size-two stratum and is not a proof of the universal weighted-erasure lower frontier. \textit{Consumer.} A simulation study and the package diagnostic can check both sides of the predicted \(k^{-1/2}\) confidence-length curve within the stated two-scale design.

\begin{proposition}[prop:equal-block-reduction]\label{prop:equal-block-reduction}
If \(r_l=r\) for every \(l\), then
\[
 L=\frac nr,\qquad q_l=2^{-r},\qquad w_l=\frac rn,
 \qquad S=M=\frac{r2^r}{n}.
\]
With \(c_0,C_1\) denoting the universal constants in
\(\mathrm{thm{:}no\text{-}dominance\text{-}upper}\), the no-dominance
condition is equivalent to
\[
 \frac{r2^r\log(1/\alpha)}{n}\le c_0^2.
\]
Whenever this condition holds, for every \(0<\alpha\le1/4\),
\[
 \mathcal R_\alpha(w,q)
 \le C_1\min\left\{1,
       \sqrt{\frac{r2^r\log(1/\alpha)}{n}}\right\}.
\]
Thus the heterogeneous upper theorem recovers the familiar
equal-block effective-count upper rate without any additional
extreme-confidence cutoff.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By \(\mathrm{ass{:}partition}\), the blocks are pairwise disjoint and their union is \(U\).  Under the hypothesis \(r_l=r\) for every \(l\), finite additivity of cardinality therefore gives
\[
n=|U|=\sum_{l=1}^L |B_l|=\sum_{l=1}^L r=Lr.
\]
Every block is nonempty by \(\mathrm{ass{:}partition}\), so \(r\ge 1\); hence division by \(r\) is valid and \(L=n/r\).  The definition of the block weights yields \(w_l=r_l/n=r/n\).  By \(\mathrm{ass{:}balanced\text{-}bernoulli}\), the assignments within a block are independent Bernoulli random variables with success probability \(1/2\).  Thus
\[
q_l=P_Z(Z_i=1\ \forall i\in B_l)
=\prod_{i\in B_l}\frac12
=2^{-|B_l|}=2^{-r};
\]
the same calculation applies to the all-control event.

Substituting \(L=n/r\), \(w_l=r/n\), and \(q_l=2^{-r}\) into the definitions of \(S\) and \(M\) gives
\[
S=\sum_{l=1}^L\frac{w_l^2}{q_l}
=\frac nr\frac{r^2}{n^2}2^r
=\frac{r2^r}{n},
\qquad
M=\max_{1\le l\le L}\frac{w_l}{q_l}
=\frac rn2^r
=\frac{r2^r}{n}=S.
\tag{1}
\]
In particular, \(S>0\).  Because \(0<\alpha\le 1/4\), one has \(\log(1/\alpha)>0\).  Consequently the no-dominance condition in \(\mathrm{thm{:}no\text{-}dominance\text{-}upper}\) is, using \(M=S\), equivalent to
\[
S\sqrt{\log(1/\alpha)}\le c_0\sqrt S
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\sqrt{S\log(1/\alpha)}\le c_0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
S\log(1/\alpha)\le c_0^2.
\]
Here the first equivalence divides by the strictly positive quantity \(\sqrt S\), and the second squares two nonnegative quantities.  Formula \((1)\) turns the last display into
\[
\frac{r2^r\log(1/\alpha)}{n}\le c_0^2.
\]
Whenever this condition holds, \(\mathrm{thm{:}no\text{-}dominance\text{-}upper}\) applies and, after one more substitution from \((1)\), gives
\[
\mathcal R_\alpha(w,q)
\le C_1\min\{1,\sqrt{S\log(1/\alpha)}\}
=C_1\min\left\{1,
\sqrt{\frac{r2^r\log(1/\alpha)}{n}}\right\}.
\]
The cited theorem requires only \(0<\alpha\le1/4\) and the displayed no-dominance inequality.  Therefore this reduction introduces no additional extreme-confidence cutoff.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is solely a reduction check showing that the heterogeneous formulas contain the established equal-block effective-count calculation. \textit{Gap.} No independent novelty is claimed for the equal-block identity; the contribution begins when weights, reveal probabilities, and cap thresholds differ. \textit{Consumer.} Users with regular partitions recover the familiar \(n/(r2^r)\) effective count as a check on the general implementation.

\(B_1,\ldots,B_L\).  By the definition of a clique component,
\(N_G[i]=B_l\) for every \(i\in B_l\), so the graph check in
\(\mathrm{def{:}software\text{-}diagnostic}\) passes.  Fix \(i\in B_l\).
Because assignments are binary, the package formulas in
\(\mathrm{def{:}package\text{-}label\text{-}maps}\), together with
\(N_G[i]=B_l\), give

 \mathsf E^{0.1.0}_{\mathrm{pkg},i,a}(G,Z)=A_l,
 \qquad
 \mathsf E^{0.1.0}_{\mathrm{pkg},i,b}(G,Z)=1-A_l.
\]
The independently specified reference formulas give
\[
 \mathsf E_{\mathrm{ref},i,a}(B,Z)=A_l,
 \qquad
 \mathsf E_{\mathrm{ref},i,b}(B,Z)=C_l.
\]
Since the matrices supplied in the proposition are the outputs of these two maps, their two columns on block \(B_l\) consequently have the table
\[
 \begin{array}{c|cc}
  &a&b\\ \hline
 L^{\mathrm{pkg}}_{i,\cdot}&A_l&1-A_l\\
 L^{\mathrm{ref}}_{i,\cdot}&A_l&C_l.
 \end{array}
\tag{4}
\]
The first columns in (4) agree identically.  The second columns agree for every unit if and only if
\(1-A_l=C_l\) for every block, which is equivalent to
\[
 A_l+C_l=1\qquad\text{for every }l.
\tag{5}
\]
By \(\mathrm{lem{:}block\text{-}reduction}\), \(A_lC_l=0\) and
\(H_l=1-A_l-C_l\).  Hence (5) fails exactly when at least one block is mixed.  In a mixed block, \(A_l=C_l=0\), so (4) gives the promised explicit mismatch: the package's second-column entry is one and the target All Control entry is zero.

It remains to verify the outputs and the rejection ordering.  On a valid clique graph, the first check in
\(\mathrm{def{:}software\text{-}diagnostic}\) passes.  Because
\(L^{\mathrm{pkg}}\) and \(L^{\mathrm{ref}}\) are, by hypothesis, the outputs of their displayed defining maps, the two map-reproduction checks pass as well.  The subsequent coordinatewise equality check passes exactly under (5), by (4).  When it passes,
\(\mathrm{def{:}software\text{-}diagnostic}\) computes
\(\overline Y_l^{\mathrm{obs}}(D)\) from
\(\mathrm{def{:}observed\text{-}block\text{-}means}\).  The partition and balanced Bernoulli design give
\[
 P_Z(A_l=1)=P_Z(C_l=1)=\left(\frac12\right)^{r_l}=2^{-r_l}=q_l,
\]
and the definitions of \(w_l\), \(S\), \(M\),
\(\mathrm{def{:}capped\text{-}modulus}\),
\(\mathrm{def{:}trimmed\text{-}radius}\), and
\(\mathrm{def{:}clipped\text{-}ht}\) then determine all the reported values exactly.  The validity of the reported clipped interval is supplied by
\(\mathrm{thm{:}finite\text{-}upper}\), while

\(\mathrm{prop{:}capped\text{-}modulus\text{-}exhibit}\).  If (5) fails, (4) identifies the first unequal coordinate sought by the diagnostic, which therefore returns

Finally, suppose that \(N_G[i]\ne B_l\) for some \(i\in B_l\).  Since

\(\mathrm{def{:}software\text{-}diagnostic}\), this graph check occurs before either label check.  Therefore the diagnostic returns

\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:finite-product-exponential]\label{lem:finite-product-exponential}
Let \(W_1,\ldots,W_{N_0}\) be independent, centered random variables with \(|W_j|\le c\), and put \(v=\sum_j\mathbb E W_j^2\). For every \(z>0\),
\[
 \Pr\!\left\{\left|\sum_jW_j\right|>
 \sqrt{2vz}+\frac{cz}{3}\right\}\le 2e^{-z}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If \(c=0\), then every \(W_j=0\) almost surely and the assertion is immediate.  Suppose \(c>0\).  For \(\lambda\ge0\) and \(u\le1\), the elementary Bennett inequality
\[
 e^{\lambda c u}\le
 1+\lambda c u+
 \{e^{\lambda c}-1-\lambda c\}u^2
\tag{1}
\]
holds when \(|u|\le1\).  Indeed, after the affine tangent at zero is subtracted, differentiation shows that
\[
 u\longmapsto\frac{e^{\lambda c u}-1-\lambda c u}{u^2},
\]
with its continuous value at zero, is maximized on \([-1,1]\) at \(u=1\).  Apply (1) with \(u=W_j/c\), use \(\mathbb EW_j=0\), and then \(1+a\le e^a\).  This gives
\[
 \log\mathbb E e^{\lambda W_j}
 \le \frac{\mathbb EW_j^2}{c^2}
       \{e^{\lambda c}-1-\lambda c\}.
\tag{2}
\]
Independence and Markov's inequality therefore imply, for every \(u>0\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \Pr\!\left\{\sum_jW_j\ge u\right\}
 &\le\inf_{\lambda>0}
 \exp\!\left[-\lambda u+\frac v{c^2}
       \{e^{\lambda c}-1-\lambda c\}\right]  \\
 &=\exp\!\left[-\frac v{c^2}
 h\!\left(\frac{cu}{v}\right)\right],
 \qquad h(y)=(1+y)\log(1+y)-y ,
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
where, when \(v>0\), equality follows by choosing
\(\lambda=c^{-1}\log(1+cu/v)\).  If \(v=0\), centering and
\(\sum_j\mathbb EW_j^2=0\) imply \(W_j=0\) almost surely for every
\(j\), so that case is already settled.

It remains to invert (3).  For \(a\ge0\), put
\(y(a)=\sqrt{2a}+a/3\).  Writing \(b=\sqrt{2a}\), differentiation gives
\[
 \frac{d}{db}\{h(b+b^2/6)-b^2/2\}
 =\left(1+\frac b3\right)\log(1+b+b^2/6)-b .
\]
The last expression is nonnegative because
\[
 \log(1+b+b^2/6)-\frac{3b}{3+b}\ge0,
\]
and the derivative of its left side equals
\[
 \frac{2(3+b)}{b^2+6b+6}-\frac9{(3+b)^2}
 =\frac{b^2(2b+9)}
 {(b^2+6b+6)(3+b)^2}\ge0,
\]
while its value at \(b=0\) is zero.  Hence \(h(y(a))\ge a\).
Taking \(a=c^2z/v\) and
\(u=\sqrt{2vz}+cz/3\) in (3) proves that the upper-tail probability
is at most \(e^{-z}\).  The same calculation applies to \(-W_j\);
the union bound proves the displayed two-sided inequality.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:capped-trimming-duality]\label{lem:capped-trimming-duality}
Let \(0<\alpha\le1/4\), \(x=\log(1/\alpha)\), and \(y=\log(2/\alpha)\). The optimizer in \(\mathrm{def{:}capped\text{-}modulus}\) is the stated water-filling vector. Moreover,
\[
 \omega_\alpha(w,q)\le\rho_\alpha(w,q)\le\frac52\omega_\alpha(w,q),
 \qquad
 \omega_1(w,q)\le\rho_0(w,q)\le2\omega_1(w,q),
\]
where \(\omega_1\) denotes the same capped program with quadratic budget one.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Because every block is nonempty, \(w_l>0\); and because \(q_l=2^{-r_l}\), also \(q_l>0\). For \(z>0\), define
\[
 \mathcal D_z=\left\{\delta\in[0,1]^L:
                 \sum_{l=1}^Lq_l\delta_l^2\le z\right\}.
\tag{23}
\]
This is a nonempty compact subset of \(\mathbb R^L\), and the linear objective is continuous, so a maximizer exists. If \(z\ge\sum_lq_l\), then \((1,\ldots,1)\in\mathcal D_z\); it is optimal because \(w_l>0\) and every feasible coordinate is at most one.

Suppose \(0<z<\sum_lq_l\). The function
\[
 f(\lambda)=\sum_{l=1}^Lq_l\min\{1,\lambda w_l/q_l\}^2,
 \qquad \lambda\ge0,
\tag{24}
\]
is continuous, equals zero at zero, and is strictly increasing until all coordinates reach their caps, after which it equals \(\sum_lq_l\). Hence there is a unique \(\lambda>0\), before the terminal plateau, such that \(f(\lambda)=z\). Put
\[
 \delta_l^*=\min\{1,\lambda w_l/q_l\}.
\tag{25}
\]
This vector belongs to \(\mathcal D_z\) and makes its quadratic constraint active.

For optimality, let \(C=\{l:\delta_l^*=1\}\), let \(J=C^c\), and take any \(\delta\in\mathcal D_z\). For \(l\in J\), the identity \(w_l=q_l\delta_l^*/\lambda\) and \((\delta_l-\delta_l^*)^2\ge0\) give
\[
 w_l(\delta_l-\delta_l^*)
 \le\frac{q_l}{2\lambda}\{\delta_l^2-(\delta_l^*)^2\}.
\tag{26}
\]
For \(l\in C\), the cap relation gives \(w_l\ge q_l/\lambda\). Since \(\delta_l-1\le0\) and \((\delta_l-1)^2\ge0\),
\[
 w_l(\delta_l-1)
 \le\frac{q_l}{\lambda}(\delta_l-1)
 \le\frac{q_l}{2\lambda}(\delta_l^2-1).
\tag{27}
\]
Summing (26)--(27), using feasibility of \(\delta\), and using the active constraint for \(\delta^*\), yields
\[
 \sum_{l=1}^Lw_l(\delta_l-\delta_l^*)
 \le\frac1{2\lambda}\sum_{l=1}^Lq_l\{\delta_l^2-(\delta_l^*)^2\}
 \le0.
\tag{28}
\]
Thus (25) is the global optimizer. Equations (26)--(28) are also a first-order certificate for the capped convex feasible program.

We next compare the moduli. Fix \(\delta\) feasible at budget \(x\), and fix \(J_0\subseteq\{1,\ldots,L\}\). Since \(0\le\delta_l\le1\), Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_{l=1}^Lw_l\delta_l
 &\le\sum_{l\notin J_0}w_l+
      \sum_{l\in J_0}w_l\delta_l\\
 &\le\sum_{l\notin J_0}w_l+
 \left(\sum_{l\in J_0}\frac{w_l^2}{q_l}\right)^{1/2}
 \left(\sum_{l\in J_0}q_l\delta_l^2\right)^{1/2}\\
 &\le\sum_{l\notin J_0}w_l+
 \sqrt{x\sum_{l\in J_0}w_l^2/q_l}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{29}
\]
The corrected construction of \(\rho_\alpha\) has square-root coefficient \(\sqrt y\), where \(y=x+\log2\ge x\), and a nonnegative linear term. Taking the supremum over \(\delta\) and then the infimum over \(J_0\) in (29) proves
\[
 \omega_\alpha(w,q)\le\rho_\alpha(w,q).
\tag{30}
\]
If \(x\ge\sum_lq_l\), the optimizer is all-capped and \(\omega_\alpha=\sum_lw_l\). Choosing \(J_0=\varnothing\) gives \(\rho_\alpha\le\sum_lw_l=\omega_\alpha\). Now suppose \(x<\sum_lq_l\). Apply (25) at budget \(x\), retain its capped set \(C\) and uncapped set \(J\), and define
\[
 A=\sum_{l\in J}\frac{w_l^2}{q_l},\qquad
 Q_C=\sum_{l\in C}q_l,\qquad
 B=\sum_{l\in C}w_l.
\tag{31}
\]
The active constraint and objective value are
\[
 x=Q_C+\lambda^2A,
 \qquad
 \omega_\alpha=B+\lambda A.
\tag{32}
\]
For \(l\in C\), \(\lambda w_l/q_l\ge1\), so \(B\ge Q_C/\lambda\). For \(l\in J\), \(w_l/q_l<1/\lambda\). Consequently (32) implies
\[
 \omega_\alpha\ge\frac{x}{\lambda},
 \qquad A\le\frac{x}{\lambda^2},
 \qquad \max_{l\in J}\frac{w_l}{q_l}<\frac1\lambda.
\tag{33}
\]
Because \(0<\alpha\le1/4\), \(x\ge\log4=2\log2\), and hence \(y=x+\log2\le3x/2\). Choosing \(J_0=J\) in the corrected definition, whose linear coefficient is \(y/6\), and applying (32)--(33), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \rho_\alpha
 &\le B+\sqrt{yA}+\frac y6\max_{l\in J}\frac{w_l}{q_l}\\
 &\le B+\sqrt{\frac32}\,\frac{x}{\lambda}
       +\frac14\frac{x}{\lambda}\\
 &\le\left(1+\sqrt{\frac32}+\frac14\right)\omega_\alpha
 <\frac52\omega_\alpha.
\end{aligned}
\tag{34}
\]
This proves the confidence-level comparison.

Finally, repeat (29) with quadratic budget one and without the linear term to obtain \(\omega_1\le\rho_0\). If \(\sum_lq_l\le1\), the budget-one optimizer is all-capped, so choosing the empty retained set gives \(\rho_0\le\sum_lw_l=\omega_1\). If \(\sum_lq_l>1\), apply (31)--(33) with \(x=1\). Choosing the uncapped set in the definition of \(\rho_0\) gives
\[
 \rho_0\le B+\sqrt A
 \le B+\lambda^{-1}
 \le2\omega_1.
\tag{35}
\]
Thus \(\omega_1\le\rho_0\le2\omega_1\), completing the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:capped-erasure-consistency-lower]\label{lem:capped-erasure-consistency-lower}
For \(0<\alpha\le 1/4\), put \(x=\log(1/\alpha)\).  Then
\[
 \mathcal R_\alpha(w,q)
 \ge
 \frac{\omega_\alpha(w,q)^3}
      {128x+8\omega_\alpha(w,q)^2}
 \ge
 \frac{\omega_\alpha(w,q)^3}{136x}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write \(\mathsf A=\omega_\alpha(w,q)\).  The partition assumption and
the definition \(w_l=r_l/n\) give
\[
 w_l>0,\qquad \sum_{l=1}^Lw_l=1.
\tag{1}
\]
Also \(q_l=2^{-r_l}\in(0,1/2]\).  The feasible set in
\(\mathrm{def{:}capped\text{-}modulus}\) is a nonempty compact subset
of \([0,1]^L\), and its objective is continuous.  It therefore has an
optimizer \(\delta^*\).  Since all \(w_l\) are positive and \(x>0\),
\(\mathsf A>0\).  Define
\[
 H=\{l:\delta_l^*\ge \mathsf A/2\},
 \qquad W_H=\sum_{l\in H}w_l,
 \qquad Q_H=\sum_{l\in H}q_l.
\]
Feasibility of \(\delta^*\) yields
\[
 Q_H\frac{\mathsf A^2}{4}
 \le \sum_{l\in H}q_l(\delta_l^*)^2
 \le x,
 \qquad \text{hence}\qquad
 Q_H\le \frac{4x}{\mathsf A^2}.
\tag{2}
\]
On the other hand, (1), \(0\le\delta_l^*\le1\), and the definition of
\(H\) give
\[
 \mathsf A=\sum_lw_l\delta_l^*
 \le W_H+\frac{\mathsf A}{2}\sum_{l\notin H}w_l
 \le W_H+\frac{\mathsf A}{2},
\]
so
\[
 W_H\ge \frac{\mathsf A}{2}.
\tag{3}
\]

We next partition \(H\) into groups of reveal mass at most \(1/2\).
First make every coordinate with \(q_l>1/4\) a singleton group.  Then
order all remaining coordinates arbitrarily and accumulate them until
the running mass first reaches \(1/4\), close that group, and continue.
Each closed group has mass in \([1/4,1/2]\), and only the final group
formed from the remaining coordinates can have mass below \(1/4\).
Thus every group except possibly one has mass at least \(1/4\).  If
\(N\) is the number of groups, then
\[
 N\le4Q_H+1\le\frac{16x}{\mathsf A^2}+1
\tag{4}
\]
by (2).  Thus some group \(G\subseteq H\), with
\(Q_G=\sum_{l\in G}q_l\le1/2\), has weight
\[
 g:=\sum_{l\in G}w_l
 \ge\frac{W_H}{N}
 \ge\frac{\mathsf A^3}{32x+2\mathsf A^2}.
\tag{5}
\]

Fix any measurable \(\Gamma\in\mathfrak C_\alpha\).  For each
\(u\in[-1/2,1/2]\), construct a fixed schedule \(m_u\) by setting, for
\(i\in B_l\),
\[
 Y_i^u(d)=
 \begin{cases}
 u,&l\in G\text{ and }d=a,\\
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\tag{6}
\]
The schedule is nonrandom, respects the block-exposure map, and all
its outcomes lie in \([-1/2,1/2]\); hence the partition, balanced
randomization, fixed-schedule, block-exposure, and bounded-outcome
assumptions imply \(m_u\in\mathcal M_n(B)\).  By
\(\mathrm{def{:}block\text{-}means}\) and the definition of the causal
target,
\[
 \theta(m_u)=gu.
\tag{7}
\]

Let \(\mathsf U_0\sim\operatorname{Uniform}[-1/2,1/2]\) be independent
of \((Z,R)\), and mix the fixed schedules in (6) over \(u=\mathsf U_0\).
This mixing is only a proof device: honesty is applied first to every
fixed \(m_u\).  Let
\[
 \mathcal E_0=\bigcap_{l\in G}\{A_l=0\}.
\]
Disjointness of the blocks and balanced independent assignment imply
that the \(A_l\), \(l\in G\), are independent Bernoulli variables with
success probabilities \(q_l\).  In particular, the union bound gives
\[
 p_0:=P_Z(\mathcal E_0)
 =\prod_{l\in G}(1-q_l)
 \ge1-\sum_{l\in G}q_l
 \ge\frac12.
\tag{8}
\]
On \(\mathcal E_0\), no block in \(G\) attains exposure \(a\), so (6)
and the block-exposure assumption give \(Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}=0\) for
every unit.  Consequently the conditional law of \((D,R)\) on
\(\mathcal E_0\) is independent of \(\mathsf U_0\); this also uses the
declared independence of the external randomizer \(R\).

Uniform honesty and (7), integrated over \(\mathsf U_0\), imply
\[
 P\{g\mathsf U_0\in\Gamma(D,R)\}\ge1-\alpha.
\tag{9}
\]
Combining (8)--(9),
\[
 P\{\mathcal E_0,\ g\mathsf U_0\in\Gamma(D,R)\}
 \ge p_0-\alpha.
\tag{10}
\]
Conditionally on \((\mathcal E_0,D,R)\), the variable
\(g\mathsf U_0\) is uniform on \([-g/2,g/2]\).  For every interval
\(J\), including a random interval after conditioning,
\[
 P\{g\mathsf U_0\in J\mid J\}
 =\frac{\operatorname{len}(J\cap[-g/2,g/2])}{g}
 \le\frac{\operatorname{len}(J)}{g}.
\tag{11}
\]
Equations (10)--(11) therefore give
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\operatorname{len}\{\Gamma(D,R)\}\right]
 \ge g(p_0-\alpha)
 \ge\frac g4,
\tag{12}
\]
where the expectation on the left averages the fixed-schedule risks
over \(\mathsf U_0\).  Hence the supremum of those risks over
\(m\in\mathcal M_n(B)\) is at least the same quantity.  Taking the
infimum over \(\Gamma\in\mathfrak C_\alpha\), then applying (5), proves
\[
 \mathcal R_\alpha(w,q)
 \ge\frac{\mathsf A^3}{128x+8\mathsf A^2}.
\tag{13}
\]
Finally, (1) and \(0\le\delta_l^*\le1\) imply
\(\mathsf A\le1\), while \(x\ge\log4>1\).  Thus
\(128x+8\mathsf A^2\le136x\), and the second inequality follows from
(13).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} A common-location prior on a greedily selected low-reveal group yields a fully finite-sample all-procedure converse strong enough to characterize consistency. \textit{Gap.} The cubic dependence on \(\omega_\alpha(w,q)\) is sufficient for the zero-versus-nonzero boundary but does not give the conjectured sharp linear confidence-length modulus. \textit{Consumer.} The consistency theorem uses this lower bound to prove that vanishing minimax expected length forces the capped modulus to vanish.

\begin{theorem}[thm:no-dominance-upper]\label{thm:no-dominance-upper}
There exist universal constants \(c_0,C_1>0\) such that, for every
\(0<\alpha\le1/4\),
\[
 M\sqrt{\log(1/\alpha)}\le c_0\sqrt S
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \mathcal R_\alpha(w,q)
 \le C_1\min\{1,\sqrt{S\log(1/\alpha)}\}.
\]
The infimum defining \(\mathcal R_\alpha\) may range over every
measurable interval procedure randomized by \(R\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(x=\log(1/\alpha)\) and \(y=\log(2/\alpha)\). Since \(0<\alpha\le1/4\), \(x\ge\log4\), and hence
\[
 y=x+\log2\le\frac32x.
\tag{42}
\]
The corrected \(\mathrm{thm{:}finite\text{-}upper}\) supplies a deterministic observed-data interval that is honest uniformly over \(\mathcal M_n(B)\) and has pointwise length at most
\[
 2\min\left\{1,\sqrt{Sy}+\frac{My}{6}\right\}.
\tag{43}
\]
It is an admissible deterministic member of \(\mathfrak C_\alpha\). Although \(\mathrm{def{:}length\text{-}risk}\) also permits procedures using \(R\), its infimum is bounded above by this rule. Thus (43) bounds \(\mathcal R_\alpha(w,q)\).

Take \(c_0=1\). Under \(M\sqrt{x}\le\sqrt S\), (42) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sqrt{Sy}+\frac{My}{6}
 &\le\sqrt{\frac32}\sqrt{Sx}+\frac14Mx\\
 &\le\left(\sqrt{\frac32}+\frac14\right)\sqrt{Sx}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{44}
\]
For \(K\ge1\) and \(t\ge0\), \(\min\{1,Kt\}\le K\min\{1,t\}\). Hence (43)--(44) prove the theorem with the universal constants
\[
 c_0=1,
 \qquad
 C_1=2\left(\sqrt{\frac32}+\frac14\right).
\tag{45}
\]
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This theorem records exactly the proved confidence-sensitive upper frontier under the no-dominant-block condition. \textit{Gap.} A matching lower bound of linear order in \(\min\{1,\sqrt{S\log(1/\alpha)}\}\) remains the weighted-erasure interval-spread question. \textit{Consumer.} The result supplies an honest attainable precision benchmark for heterogeneous school, village, and network partitions.

\begin{lemma}[lem:two-scale-rademacher-lower]\label{lem:two-scale-rademacher-lower}
For the family \(B^{(k)}\) containing \(k\) blocks of size two and \(k\) blocks of size four, every \(0<\alpha\le1/4\) and every integer \(k\ge64\) satisfy
\[
 \mathcal R_\alpha(w,q)\ge\frac{1}{192\sqrt{k}}.
\]
This lower bound applies to the full class of measurable honest interval procedures, including procedures externally randomized by \(R\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(0<\alpha\le1/4\), an integer \(k\ge64\), and \(\Gamma\in\mathfrak C_\alpha\). Index the \(k\) size-two blocks by \(j=1,\ldots,k\). In the hard schedules from \(\mathrm{def{:}hard\text{-}subexperiment\text{-}embedding}\), put
\[
 V_j=\frac{\varepsilon_j}{2},\qquad
 P(\varepsilon_j=1)=P(\varepsilon_j=-1)=\frac12,
 \quad j=1,\ldots,k,
\tag{46}
\]
independently, and set \(V_l=1/2\) deterministically on every size-four block. This product law is used only to average the pointwise coverage and risk identities in \(\mathrm{lem{:}hard\text{-}subexperiment\text{-}embedding}\); every schedule in its support is a fixed member of \(\mathcal M_n(B^{(k)})\). For a size-two block,
\[
 q_j=\frac14,\qquad w_j=\frac1{3k}.
\tag{47}
\]
Let \(K_j\) be its all-treated reveal indicator and define \(N=\sum_{j=1}^k(1-K_j)\). The embedding lemma gives independent \(K_j\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/4)\), independent of the signs, and hence
\[
 N\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(k,3/4),\qquad
 \mathbb EN=\frac{3k}{4},\qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(N)=\frac{3k}{16}.
\tag{48}
\]
Write \(\Lambda=\Lambda_\Gamma(O,R)\) for the corresponding interval in the erasure experiment and \(L_\Lambda=\operatorname{len}(\Lambda)\). Conditional on \((O,R)\), the revealed size-two signs and the size-four contribution are fixed, whereas the \(N\) unrevealed size-two signs remain independent fair Rademacher variables. The independence of \(R\) is part of \(\mathrm{def{:}weighted\text{-}erasure\text{-}law}\). Since the embedding gives \(\theta=\sum_lw_lV_l\), the conditional target law is
\[
 c(O)+\frac1{6k}\sum_{s=1}^{N}\xi_s,
\tag{49}
\]
where the \(\xi_s\) are conditionally independent fair Rademacher variables. Adjacent support points in (49) are separated by \(1/(3k)\).

For \(S_N=\sum_{s=1}^N\xi_s\), its largest atom is \(p_N=2^{-N}\binom{N}{\lfloor N/2\rfloor}\). If \(N=2m\), factorial cancellation gives
\[
 p_{2m}=4^{-m}\binom{2m}{m}
       =\prod_{s=1}^m\left(1-\frac1{2s}\right).
\]
Since \(\log(1-u)\le-u\) for \(0<u<1\) and \(\sum_{s=1}^m s^{-1}\ge\int_1^{m+1}t^{-1}\,dt=\log(m+1)\),
\[
 p_{2m}\le(m+1)^{-1/2}\le\sqrt{\frac2{2m}}.
\tag{50}
\]
If \(N=2m+1\), then
\[
 p_{2m+1}=\frac{2m+1}{2m+2}p_{2m}
 \le(m+1)^{-1/2}
 \le\sqrt{\frac2{2m+1}}.
\tag{51}
\]
Thus for every \(N\ge1\), \(p_N\le\sqrt{2/N}\).

An interval of length \(u\) contains at most \(3ku+1\) points of a lattice with spacing \(1/(3k)\). Therefore, on \(N\ge k/2\), conditionally on \((O,R)\),
\[
 P\{\theta\in\Lambda\mid O,R\}
 \le(3kL_\Lambda+1)\sqrt{\frac2N}.
\tag{52}
\]
On the event
\[
 \mathcal A=\left\{N\ge\frac k2,\quad
 L_\Lambda<\frac1{48\sqrt{k}}\right\},
\]
equation (52) and \(k\ge64\) give
\[
 P\{\theta\in\Lambda\mid O,R\}
 \le\left(\frac{\sqrt{k}}{16}+1\right)\frac2{\sqrt{k}}
 =\frac18+\frac2{\sqrt{k}}
 \le\frac38.
\tag{53}
\]
By Chebyshev's inequality and (48),
\[
 P\left(N<\frac k2\right)
 \le\frac{3k/16}{(k/4)^2}
 =\frac3k\le\frac3{64}.
\tag{54}
\]
Pointwise honesty of \(\Gamma\), averaged over (46), and the coverage identity in the embedding lemma imply
\[
 P\{\theta\in\Lambda\}\ge1-\alpha\ge\frac34.
\tag{55}
\]
On \(\mathcal A\), conditional coverage is at most \(3/8\), and outside it is at most one. Since \(\mathcal A^c\) is contained in the union of \(\{N<k/2\}\) and \(\{L_\Lambda\ge1/(48\sqrt{k})\}\), equations (53)--(55) give
\[
 P\left(L_\Lambda\ge\frac1{48\sqrt{k}}\right)
 \ge\frac38-\frac3{64}=\frac{21}{64}\ge\frac14.
\tag{56}
\]
Therefore
\[
 \mathbb E L_\Lambda
 \ge\frac1{48\sqrt{k}}
 P\left(L_\Lambda\ge\frac1{48\sqrt{k}}\right)
 \ge\frac1{192\sqrt{k}}.
\tag{57}
\]
The expected-length identity and supremum inequality in the embedding lemma show that the worst fixed-schedule risk of the original \(\Gamma\) is at least the prior average in (57). Since \(\Gamma\) was arbitrary, taking the infimum in \(\mathrm{def{:}length\text{-}risk}\) proves the result. Conditioning throughout on the declared independent \(R\) covers every externally randomized measurable interval.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} A product-Rademacher prior on the size-two stratum supplies a fully finite-sample lower bound for the concrete heterogeneous triangular array. \textit{Gap.} The argument is deliberately specialized to this two-scale family and does not settle either universal linear lower-bound open question. \textit{Consumer.} Together with the no-dominance upper bound, this lemma certifies the fixed-confidence order of minimax expected length in the two-scale example.

\begin{theorem}[thm:capped-modulus-upper]\label{thm:capped-modulus-upper}
For every \(0<\alpha\le1/4\),
\[
 \mathcal R_\alpha(w,q)\le5\min\{1,\omega_\alpha(w,q)\}.
\]
The bound is attained by a deterministic observed-data interval and therefore remains valid when the competing class permits randomization by the independent external randomizer \(R\). In addition, if \(x_\alpha<\sum_{l=1}^Lq_l\) and no coordinate of the water-filling optimizer is capped, meaning \(\lambda_\alpha w_l/q_l<1\) for every \(l\), then
\[
 \omega_\alpha(w,q)=\sqrt{Sx_\alpha}=\sqrt{S\log(1/\alpha)}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(0<\alpha\le1/4\). By \(\mathrm{thm{:}trimmed\text{-}upper}\), there is a deterministic interval in \(\mathfrak C_\alpha\) whose pointwise length, under every fixed schedule in \(\mathcal M_n(B)\), is at most
\[
 2\min\{1,\rho_\alpha(w,q)\}.
\tag{36}
\]
Because the infimum in \(\mathrm{def{:}length\text{-}risk}\) ranges over a class containing this rule, taking the expectation, the supremum over schedules, and then the infimum over procedures gives
\[
 \mathcal R_\alpha(w,q)\le2\min\{1,\rho_\alpha(w,q)\}.
\tag{37}
\]
By \(\mathrm{lem{:}capped\text{-}trimming\text{-}duality}\), \(\rho_\alpha(w,q)\le(5/2)\omega_\alpha(w,q)\). For \(u\ge0\) and \(K\ge1\), \(\min\{1,Ku\}\le K\min\{1,u\}\), by considering \(u\le1\) and \(u>1\). Therefore
\[
 \mathcal R_\alpha(w,q)\le5\min\{1,\omega_\alpha(w,q)\}.
\tag{38}
\]
The witness is deterministic, so allowing competitors to use the independent \(R\) does not affect this upper bound.

For the no-cap identity, put \(x=x_\alpha\). Under \(x<\sum_lq_l\), the optimizer certificate gives
\[
 \delta_l^*=\min\{1,\lambda_\alpha w_l/q_l\},
 \qquad
 \sum_lq_l(\delta_l^*)^2=x.
\tag{39}
\]
If no cap binds, \(\delta_l^*=\lambda_\alpha w_l/q_l\) for every \(l\). Positivity of \(q_l\), \(w_l\), and \(S=\sum_lw_l^2/q_l\) permits division, and (39) becomes
\[
 x=\lambda_\alpha^2S,
 \qquad
 \lambda_\alpha=\sqrt{x/S}.
\tag{40}
\]
Substitution into the objective gives
\[
 \omega_\alpha(w,q)
 =\lambda_\alpha\sum_l\frac{w_l^2}{q_l}
 =\lambda_\alpha S
 =\sqrt{Sx}
 =\sqrt{S\log(1/\alpha)}.
\tag{41}
\]
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The deterministic trimmed interval and strengthened trimming dual give the explicit unrestricted factor-five upper bound, while the active uncapped constraint gives the exact quadratic formula. \textit{Gap.} The matching universal linear lower bound for all measurable externally randomized intervals remains open. \textit{Consumer.} The residual water-filling question can now be stated solely as a lower-bound problem.

\section{Interpretation}

The computable quantity \(\rho_0(w,q)\), equivalently the unit-budget capped modulus \(\omega_1(w,q)\), is the intrinsic consistency diagnostic. A rare block may keep \(S\) bounded away from zero while carrying so little causal weight that trimming it still permits vanishing honest length. At nonzero finite confidence, the corrected trimmed interval has risk at most five times the capped modulus, while the general converse is presently cubic. The sharper \(k^{-1/2}\) lower rate is proved only for the explicit two-scale family and does not settle the universal linear frontier.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The common-location no-reveal construction yields \(\mathcal R_\alpha\gtrsim\omega_\alpha^3/\log(1/\alpha)\), which is sufficient for the fixed-confidence consistency equivalence but can be much smaller than \(\omega_\alpha\). The unrestricted sharp lower bound and the no-dominance sharp lower half remain open forms of the same universal linear weighted-erasure interval-spread problem. A resolution must control compact-support boundaries, random weighted reveal information, conditional target fluctuation, and external interval randomization; neither the exact embedding, the cubic argument, nor the family-specific product-Rademacher lemma supplies that universal step.

\section{Honest scope}

The proved results concern fixed outcomes in \([-1/2,1/2]\), known unequal disjoint clique blocks, and independent unit-level \(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\) assignment. The estimator and upper intervals are measurable from \(D\) and known design quantities. The capped optimizer, factor-\(5/2\) trimming comparison, factor-five unrestricted upper bound, exact consistency boundary, and cubic lower bound cover arbitrary independent external interval randomization. The fixed-confidence \(\Theta_\alpha(k^{-1/2})\) statement is confined to the specified two-scale family; it is not a universal posterior-spread theorem. The equal-block and general no-dominance results retain only their proved upper conclusions. Overlapping or nonclique neighborhoods are rejected before the software diagnostic reports an interval.
