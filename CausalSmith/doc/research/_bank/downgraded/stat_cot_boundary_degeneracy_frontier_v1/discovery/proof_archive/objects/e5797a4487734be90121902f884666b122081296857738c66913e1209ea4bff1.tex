Let \(d=0\).  For arm \(a\), write \(F_a\) for the distribution function, \(Q_a=F_a^{-1}\),
\[
D=Q_1-Q_0,
\qquad
\widehat D=\widehat Q_{1,n}-\widehat Q_{0,n},
\qquad
P_J=P^Q_{J_y}.
\]
The uncrossing calculation in the proof of
\(\mathrm{thm{:}conditional\text{-}minimax\text{-}frontier}\) gives the one-dimensional identity
\[
\theta_n=\int_0^1D(u)^2\,du=\|D\|_2^2,                     \tag{1}
\]
whereas
\(\mathrm{def{:}no\text{-}covariate\text{-}branch}\) defines the explicit sample map
\[
\widehat\theta^0_{J_y,n}=\|P_J\widehat D\|_2^2.            \tag{2}
\]

We first establish its uniform risk.  Since \(q_{a,n}\ge c\), every \(Q_a\) is \(c^{-1}\)-Lipschitz.  Under the probability integral transform, for almost every \(u\in(0,1)\),
\[
\widehat Q_{a,n}(u)=Q_a\{U_{a,(k)}\},
\qquad k=\lceil nu\rceil,
\]
where \(U_{a,(k)}\) is the \(k\)-th order statistic of \(n\) independent uniform variables.  Its density is proportional to
\(x^{k-1}(1-x)^{n-k}\).  Integrating this beta density gives, for \(j=1,2,3,4\),
\[
E\{U_{a,(k)}^j\}
=\prod_{r=0}^{j-1}\frac{k+r}{n+1+r}.                       \tag{3}
\]
Because \(|k/(n+1)-u|\le2/n\), expanding the centered powers in (3) gives universal constants such that
\[
\sup_{a,u}E|U_{a,(k)}-u|^2\le\frac{C_2}{n},
\qquad
\sup_{a,u}E|U_{a,(k)}-u|^4\le\frac{C_4}{n^2}.              \tag{4}
\]
The Lipschitz property, Tonelli's theorem, Jensen's inequality, and
\(|x-y|^j\le2^{j-1}(|x|^j+|y|^j)\) now yield
\[
E\|\widehat D-D\|_2^2\lesssim n^{-1},
\qquad
E\|\widehat D-D\|_2^4\lesssim n^{-2}.                    \tag{5}
\]

The identity \(Q_a'=1/(q_{a,n}\circ Q_a)\), the lower density bound, and the composition and product rules for H\"older differences show inductively that
\(Q_a\in\mathcal C^{s_y+1}([0,1])\), with norm bounded only by \((c,C,L,s_y)\).  The boundary wavelets in
\(\mathrm{ass{:}wavelet\text{-}regularity}\) have regularity beyond \(s_y+1\); Taylor expansion against their vanishing moments therefore bounds a level-\(j\) coefficient by a constant times
\(2^{-j(s_y+3/2)}\).  Summing the squared coefficients above \(J_y\) gives
\[
\|(I-P_J)D\|_2^2\lesssim2^{-2(s_y+1)J_y}.                  \tag{6}
\]
Let \(E_J=P_J(\widehat D-D)\) and \(B_J=(I-P_J)D\).  Orthogonality gives the exact decomposition
\[
\widehat\theta^0_{J_y,n}-\theta_n
=2\langle P_JD,E_J\rangle+\|E_J\|_2^2-\|B_J\|_2^2.        \tag{7}
\]
Since \(P_J\) is a contraction, (5)--(7) and Cauchy--Schwarz imply
\[
\|\widehat\theta^0_{J_y,n}-\theta_n\|_{L^2(P)}
\lesssim
\|D\|_2n^{-1/2}+n^{-1}+2^{-2(s_y+1)J_y}.                  \tag{8}
\]
The energy comparison (5) in the preceding proof applies also when \(d=0\), and hence
\(\|D\|_2^2=\theta_n\lesssim\rho_n^2\le\rho^2\).  At \(J_y=J_y^0\), the tuning condition in
\(\mathrm{def{:}no\text{-}covariate\text{-}branch}\) makes the last term in (8) \(o(n^{-1})\), so
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,0}(s_y,s_z,\rho)}
\|\widehat\theta^0_{J_y^0,n}-\theta_n\|_2
\lesssim\rho n^{-1/2}+n^{-1}.                              \tag{9}
\]
The zero estimator and the same energy comparison give the additional bound \(O(\rho^2)\).  Choosing the better deterministic guarantee proves the minimax upper bound.

For the converse, use the fixed-frequency laws in
\(\mathrm{def{:}no\text{-}covariate\text{-}witness}\).  First compare \(t=0\) with
\[
t'=c_0\min\{\rho,n^{-1/2}\}.                               \tag{10}
\]
For sufficiently small \(c_0\), both laws are in the class by
\(\mathrm{lem{:}explicit\text{-}mixture\text{-}checks}\), and their product Hellinger distance is bounded away from its maximal value.  The expansion in
\(\mathrm{lem{:}cosine\text{-}second\text{-}variation}\), with continuity of the second derivative obtained by implicit differentiation of the quantiles, gives endpoint separation
\[
|\Theta(t')-\Theta(0)|\gtrsim\min\{\rho^2,n^{-1}\}.        \tag{11}
\]
If \(\rho\ge K n^{-1/2}\), compare instead
\[
t=c_1\rho,
\qquad t'=c_1\rho+c_2n^{-1/2},                              \tag{12}
\]
where \(c_1,c_2\) are fixed and small and \(K\) is fixed and large.  The exact radius calculation
\(\rho(t)=|t|/(2\sqrt2\pi)\) puts both points in the class.  The derivative calculation (15) in the preceding proof and
\(\Theta''(0)=1/(4\pi^2)\) imply, after making \(c_1\) small, that
\[
|\Theta(t')-\Theta(t)|\gtrsim\rho n^{-1/2}.                 \tag{13}
\]
Thresholding any estimator halfway between the two endpoints, the testing affinity supplied by the Hellinger bound yields an root-mean-square lower bound by a fixed multiple of (11), and also of (13) in its stated regime.  Splitting at \(\rho=K n^{-1/2}\), exactly as in the three cases leading to (18) above but with \(r=n^{-1}\), gives
\[
\inf_{\widetilde\theta_n}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,0}(s_y,s_z,\rho)}
\{E(\widetilde\theta_n-\theta_n)^2\}^{1/2}
\gtrsim\min\{\rho^2,\rho n^{-1/2}+n^{-1}\}.              \tag{14}
\]
Together, (9), the zero-estimator bound, and (14) prove the minimax equivalence.

It remains to identify the exact equality limit.  Under the uniform density, define
\[
u_{a,n}(t)=\sqrt n\{t-\widehat Q_{a,n}(t)\},
\qquad
u_{a,n}^{\mathrm{tr}}(t)=u_{a,n}(t)\mathbf1\{1/n<t<1-1/n\}.
\]
By
\(\mathrm{lem{:}del\text{-}barrio\text{-}gine\text{-}utzet\text{-}uniform\text{-}quantile\text{-}limit}\),
\(u_{a,n}^{\mathrm{tr}}\Rightarrow\mathbb B_a\) in \(L^2(0,1)\).  Formula (3) for the first and last order statistics gives
\[
E\|u_{a,n}-u_{a,n}^{\mathrm{tr}}\|_2^2=O(n^{-2}),          \tag{15}
\]
so the same convergence holds for \(u_{a,n}\).  Independence of the arm samples in
\(\mathrm{ass{:}two\text{-}arm\text{-}sampling}\) gives joint convergence to independent bridges.  Consequently,
\[
X_n:=\sqrt n(\widehat Q_{1,n}-\widehat Q_{0,n})
=u_{0,n}-u_{1,n}
\Rightarrow\mathbb B_0-\mathbb B_1
\stackrel{d}=\sqrt2\,\mathbb B                              \tag{16}
\]
in \(L^2(0,1)\).

Because \(J_y^0\to\infty\), the orthogonal projectors
\(P^Q_{J_y^0}\) converge strongly to the identity.  Strong convergence of uniformly bounded operators is uniform on compact subsets of \(L^2\): this follows by covering a compact set by finitely many small balls and using the contraction property.  Tightness in (16) therefore gives
\[
\|(I-P^Q_{J_y^0})X_n\|_2=o_P(1).                           \tag{17}
\]
Equations (2), (16), and (17), followed by continuity of the squared norm, yield
\[
n\widehat\theta^0_{J_y^0,n}
=\|P^Q_{J_y^0}X_n\|_2^2
\Rightarrow2\int_0^1\mathbb B(t)^2\,dt.                   \tag{18}
\]
The covariance kernel \(s\wedge t-st\) is the Green kernel of the Dirichlet operator \(-d^2/dt^2\).  Direct differentiation verifies the normalized eigenfunctions
\(\sqrt2\sin(\pi kt)\) and eigenvalues \((\pi^2k^2)^{-1}\).  Expanding the Gaussian bridge in that orthonormal basis gives
\[
2\int_0^1\mathbb B(t)^2\,dt
=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{\pi^2k^2}\xi_{1,k}^2.        \tag{19}
\]
Moreover,
\(2\sum_{k\le2^{J_y^0}}(\pi^2k^2)^{-1}\to
2\sum_{k\ge1}(\pi^2k^2)^{-1}=1/3\).  Thus subtracting the stated finite sum divided by \(n\) from \(\widehat\theta^0_{J_y^0,n}\) changes the limit in (19) to
\[
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{\pi^2k^2}(\xi_{1,k}^2-1),
\]
the centered chaos.  Finally, equating \(\rho^2\), \(\rho n^{-1/2}\), and \(n^{-1}\) gives
\(\rho_{Q,n}=\rho_{L,n}=n^{-1/2}\), as claimed.
