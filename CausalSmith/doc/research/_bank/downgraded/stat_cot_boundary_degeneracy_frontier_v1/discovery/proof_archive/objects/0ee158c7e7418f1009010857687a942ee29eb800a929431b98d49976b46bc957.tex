Fix \(d\ge1\) and a triangular sequence in \(\mathcal M_{n,d}(s_y,s_z,\rho)\) satisfying the premises of \(\mathrm{thm{:}mixed\text{-}spectral\text{-}transition}\).  Put
\[
E_{R,n}=C_B\left[
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\{2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-s_zJ_z}\}
+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{2+a_y}
+\rho^{2a_y/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^2
\right].
\]
By \(\mathrm{thm{:}uniform\text{-}second\text{-}order\text{-}expansion}\), after choosing \(C_B\) at least as large as its uniform remainder constant, there is a deterministic sequence
\[
r_n^\circ=o\!\left\{\rho n^{-1/2}+A_{J_y,J_z,n}/n\right\}
\]
such that
\[
\|\mathbb R_{J_y,J_z,n}\|_2\le E_{R,n}+r_n^\circ.
\]
Define the population Gaussian--quadratic proxy
\[
Z_n^\circ=\sum_{e=1}^2\sum_k\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}
\{(\xi_{e,k}+\eta_{k,n})^2-(1+\eta_{k,n}^2)\},
\]
and let \(q_{n,1-\alpha/2}^\circ\) be the lower \((1-\alpha/2)\)-quantile of \(|Z_n^\circ|\).  If
\[
d_n^\circ=d_{\mathrm{BL}}\!\left(
\mathcal L\!\left[\frac{n(\Theta^{\mathrm{or}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n)}{S_n}\right],
\mathcal L\!\left[\frac{Z_n^\circ}{S_n}\right]\right),
\]
then \(\mathrm{thm{:}mixed\text{-}spectral\text{-}transition}\) gives \(d_n^\circ\to0\).  Choose deterministic \(\delta_n\downarrow0\) with \(d_n^\circ/\delta_n\to0\), and set
\[
\widetilde{\mathcal I}_n^\circ=
\left[\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-h_n^\circ,
\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}+h_n^\circ\right],
\]
\[
h_n^\circ=\frac{q_{n,1-\alpha/2}^\circ+\delta_nS_n}{n}
+\sqrt{\frac{2}{\alpha}}\,(E_{R,n}+r_n^\circ).
\]
Apply the definition of bounded--Lipschitz distance to the function which equals one on
\(\{|x|\le q_{n,1-\alpha/2}^\circ/S_n\}\), decreases linearly to zero over an exterior band of width \(\delta_n\), and vanishes outside that band.  Rescaling this function to bounded--Lipschitz norm at most one yields
\[
P\!\left(\left|n(\Theta^{\mathrm{or}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n)\right|
\le q_{n,1-\alpha/2}^\circ+\delta_nS_n\right)
\ge1-\frac{\alpha}{2}-\frac{2d_n^\circ}{\delta_n}.
\]
Markov's inequality applied to \(\mathbb R_{J_y,J_z,n}^2\) gives
\[
P\!\left(
|\mathbb R_{J_y,J_z,n}|>
\sqrt{2/\alpha}\,(E_{R,n}+r_n^\circ)\right)\le\frac{\alpha}{2}.
\]
The definitions of \(\Theta^{\mathrm{or}}_{J_y,J_z,n}\) and
\(\mathbb R_{J_y,J_z,n}\) give the exact identity
\[
\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n
=\Theta^{\mathrm{or}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n
+\mathbb R_{J_y,J_z,n}.
\]
The union bound therefore proves
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}P\{\theta_n\in\widetilde{\mathcal I}_n^\circ\}
\ge1-\alpha.
\]

For a standard Gaussian variable \(\xi\),
\(E\xi=E\xi^3=0\), \(E\xi^2=1\), and \(E\xi^4=3\).  Independence of the Gaussian coordinates consequently gives
\[
E Z_n^\circ=0,\qquad
E\{(Z_n^\circ)^2\}
=A_{J_y,J_z,n}^2+
8\sum_k\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}^2\eta_{k,n}^2.
\]
The score-approximation conclusion used in
\(\mathrm{thm{:}mixed\text{-}spectral\text{-}transition}\) identifies the last summand with
\(nV_{L,n}+o(S_n^2)\).  Hence
\[
E\{(Z_n^\circ)^2\}
=A_{J_y,J_z,n}^2+nV_{L,n}+o(S_n^2)=O(S_n^2).
\]
Markov's inequality gives
\(q_{n,1-\alpha/2}^\circ=O_\alpha(S_n)\).  Since
\(V_{L,n}\lesssim\rho_n^2\le\rho^2\), it follows that
\[
h_n^\circ=O_\alpha\!\left\{
E_{R,n}+\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}}n+\frac{\rho}{\sqrt n}
\right\}.
\]
At \((J_y^*,J_z^*)\),
\(\mathrm{prop{:}anisotropic\text{-}spectrum\text{-}bias}\) and
\(\mathrm{def{:}anisotropic\text{-}tuning}\) imply
\[
\frac{A_{J_y^*,J_z^*,n}}n
\asymp\frac{2^{dJ_z^*/2}}n
\asymp n^{-2s_z/(2s_z+d)}
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{d/(2s_z+d)}.
\]
The first two projection terms in \(E_{R,n}\) have this order as well.  Thus the population experiment admits an infeasible interval of the requested positive-dimensional order, without centering by \(B^y_{J_y,n}+B^z_{J_z,n}\).

This does not construct the requested observable interval.  Consider the admissible parameters
\(d=1\), \(s_y=1/10\), and \(s_z=1\).  Direct substitution into
\(\beta^{-1}=s_y^{-1}+ds_z^{-1}\) and the tuning definitions gives
\[
\beta=\frac1{11},\qquad a_y=\frac1{10},\qquad
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}
=\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/13},
\]
\[
2^{J_z^*}\asymp(n\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n})^{2/3},
\qquad
2^{J_y^*}\asymp(n\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n})^{20/33},
\qquad
D_{J_y^*,J_z^*}\asymp
(n\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n})^{14/11}.
\]
The uniform conditional-equality law is in the model class.  Its discrepancy is zero, and
\[
\frac{\sqrt n\{2^{-(s_y+1)J_y^*}+2^{-s_zJ_z^*}\}}
{A_{J_y^*,J_z^*,n}}
\asymp n^{-1/2}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{-1}\longrightarrow0,
\]
so it also satisfies the transition theorem's score-approximation premise.  Nevertheless,
\[
\frac{D_{J_y^*,J_z^*}\log n}{m}
\asymp n^{25/143}(\log n)^{157/143}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Therefore the sufficient covariance-consistency condition in
\(\mathrm{lem{:}observable\text{-}pilot\text{-}control}\) is unavailable.  Even if the population covariance were known, the frozen operator-norm Hessian estimate yields only
\[
\frac{\sqrt{D_{J_y^*,J_z^*}}\,
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{a_y}}
{2^{dJ_z^*/2}}
\asymp(n\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n})^{10/33}
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{1/10}
=n^{389/1430}(\log n)^{133/4290}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Thus the available bounds do not establish the relative Hilbert--Schmidt consistency required for observable spectral-quantile substitution.  They also furnish neither a common-multiplier coupling which estimates the local shifts while preserving linear--quadratic covariance nor a uniform anti-concentration inequality for the mixed triangular Gaussian quadratic form.  This admissible sequence disproves applicability of the available sufficient controls, not existence of some other honest calibration method.

For \(d=0\), the exact uniform-null limit does provide one observable submodel interval.  Put
\[
H_0=2\int_0^1\mathbb B(t)^2dt
=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{\pi^2k^2}\xi_{1,k}^2,
\]
and let \(q^0_{1-\alpha}\) be its lower \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile.  Its distribution is continuous because its first summand has an absolutely continuous law and is independent of the remaining almost surely finite nonnegative series.  By
\(\mathrm{thm{:}no\text{-}covariate\text{-}recovery}\),
\[
\mathcal I_n^0=
\left[\widehat\theta^0_{J_y^0,n}-\frac{q^0_{1-\alpha}}n,
\widehat\theta^0_{J_y^0,n}+\frac{q^0_{1-\alpha}}n\right]
\]
satisfies
\[
P\{0\in\mathcal I_n^0\}
=P\{n\widehat\theta^0_{J_y^0,n}\le q^0_{1-\alpha}\}
\longrightarrow1-\alpha.
\]
That theorem does not supply a single observable coupling or anti-concentration bound uniform over general equality laws, local alternatives, and fixed unequal laws.  Accordingly, the frozen results certify the positive-dimensional infeasible interval and this exact no-covariate uniform-null interval, but do not resolve the observable mixed-calibration question.
