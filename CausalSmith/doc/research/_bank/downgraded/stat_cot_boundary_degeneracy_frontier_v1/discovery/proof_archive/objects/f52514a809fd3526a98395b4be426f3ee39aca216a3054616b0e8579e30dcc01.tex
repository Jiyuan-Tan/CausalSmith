For \(d\ge1\), abbreviate the deterministic displayed remainder envelope by
\[
E_{R,n}=C_B\left[
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\{2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-s_zJ_z}\}
+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{2+a_y}
+\rho^{2a_y/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^2
\right].                                                     \tag{1}
\]
The following population-calibrated conclusion follows from the established expansion and transition theorem.  In addition to the score-approximation premise of \(\mathrm{thm{:}mixed\text{-}spectral\text{-}transition}\), suppose that a deterministic sequence \(r_n^\circ=o(S_n/n)\) dominates, uniformly in \(L^2\), the little-oh remainder in \(\mathrm{thm{:}uniform\text{-}second\text{-}order\text{-}expansion}\).  Define the population Gaussian--quadratic variable
\[
G_n^\circ=\sum_{e=1}^2\sum_k\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}
\{(\xi_{e,k}+\eta_{k,n})^2-(1+\eta_{k,n}^2)\},               \tag{2}
\]
and let \(q_{n,1-\alpha/2}^\circ\) be its \((1-\alpha/2)\)-quantile in absolute value.  Then, for a sufficiently large fixed \(C_\alpha\), the infeasible interval
\[
\mathcal I_n^\circ=
\left[
\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}
-\frac{q_{n,1-\alpha/2}^\circ}{n}-C_\alpha(E_{R,n}+r_n^\circ),
\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}
+\frac{q_{n,1-\alpha/2}^\circ}{n}+C_\alpha(E_{R,n}+r_n^\circ)
\right]                                                       \tag{3}
\]
has asymptotic coverage at least \(1-\alpha\), uniformly over every triangular collection on which the stated score-approximation premise holds.

Indeed, write \(B_n=\sqrt{nV_{L,n}}\).  By definition, \(S_n=A_{J_y,J_z,n}+B_n\).  The variance of the normal proxy in \(\mathrm{thm{:}mixed\text{-}spectral\text{-}transition}\) is
\[
\sigma_n^2=\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}^2+B_n^2}
{(A_{J_y,J_z,n}+B_n)^2}.                                      \tag{4}
\]
Because \(A_{J_y,J_z,n}>0\) and \(B_n\ge0\),
\[
\frac12\le\sigma_n^2\le1;                                   \tag{5}
\]
the lower inequality follows from \((A_{J_y,J_z,n}-B_n)^2\ge0\), and the upper one from \(2A_{J_y,J_z,n}B_n\ge0\).  Thus the normal anti-concentration modulus is bounded uniformly in \(n\).  The two bounded-Lipschitz approximations in \(\mathrm{thm{:}mixed\text{-}spectral\text{-}transition}\), followed by smoothing the indicator of a symmetric interval and using (5), give
\[
P\left(
\left|n\{\Theta^{\mathrm{or}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n\}\right|
\le q_{n,1-\alpha/2}^\circ
\right)\ge1-\frac\alpha2-o(1).                                \tag{6}
\]
The uniform expansion and the definition of \(r_n^\circ\) imply, for a fixed constant \(C_0<\infty\),
\[
\|\mathbb R_{J_y,J_z,n}\|_2
\le C_0(E_{R,n}+r_n^\circ)                                    \tag{7}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Markov's inequality applied to \(\mathbb R_{J_y,J_z,n}^2\) yields
\[
P\left\{
|\mathbb R_{J_y,J_z,n}|>
C_\alpha(E_{R,n}+r_n^\circ)
\right\}
\le \frac{C_0^2}{C_\alpha^2}.                                \tag{8}
\]
Choose \(C_\alpha\ge C_0\sqrt{2/\alpha}\).  Since
\[
\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n
=\Theta^{\mathrm{or}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n
+\mathbb R_{J_y,J_z,n},                                      \tag{9}
\]
the union bound applied to (6) and (8) proves the coverage assertion for (3).

The same Gaussian approximation and (5) show that
\[
q_{n,1-\alpha/2}^\circ=O(S_n).                                \tag{10}
\]
Therefore the half-width in (3) is
\[
O\!\left{
E_{R,n}+\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}}n
+\sqrt{\frac{V_{L,n}}n}
\right}
\le
O\!\left{
E_{R,n}+\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}}n+\frac{\rho_n}{\sqrt n}
\right},                                                     \tag{11}
\]
where the last inequality is the established bound \(V_{L,n}\lesssim\rho_n^2\).  At the corrected resolutions, \(A_{J_y,J_z,n}/n\) has the Gram-envelope order appearing in the question, by \(\mathrm{prop{:}anisotropic\text{-}spectrum\text{-}bias}\) and \(\mathrm{def{:}anisotropic\text{-}tuning}\).  Hence (3) proves the requested order for a population-calibrated interval, but not for an observable one.

The frozen observable-kernel control cannot simply replace the population quantile in all allowed smoothness regimes.  Its spectral plug-in conclusion assumes
\[
D_{J_y,J_z}\log n/m=o(1).                                     \tag{12}
\]
For the admissible choice \(d=1\), \(s_y=1/10\), and \(s_z=1\), the pilot exponent is
\[
e=\frac{\beta}{2\beta+1}
=\frac1{2+s_y^{-1}+ds_z^{-1}}=\frac1{13},                    \tag{13}
\]
while the corrected tuning gives
\[
D_{J_y^*,J_z^*}
=\{n\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\}^{14/11+o(1)}.
                                                                    \tag{14}
\]
Since \(n\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}
=n^{12/13}(\log n)^{1/13}\), equations (13)--(14) imply
\[
\frac{D_{J_y^*,J_z^*}\log n}{m}
=n^{25/143+o(1)}(\log n)^{1+14/143+o(1)}\longrightarrow\infty. \tag{15}
\]
Thus (12) is not a consequence of the primitive model and corrected tuning.  Even in subregimes where (12) happens to hold, the current receipts give neither a conditional common-multiplier approximation for the joint linear--quadratic statistic nor uniform control of the noncentral spectral coordinates and their covariance with the linear score.  The absence of a reverse inequality \(V_{L,n}\gtrsim\rho_n^2\) also prevents ordinary relative studentizer consistency from bridging conditional equality and regular alternatives.  For \(d=0\), \(\mathrm{thm{:}no\text{-}covariate\text{-}recovery}\) supplies the exact uniform-density equality law and the local minimax rate, but no observable quantile coupling uniform over arbitrary equal densities, local alternatives, and fixed unequal laws.  These are precisely the residual steps needed to replace (3) by \(\mathcal I_n^{\mathrm{mix}}\).
