For \(F_{a,z}(y)=\int_0^yq_{a,n}(v,z)dv\), let \(Q_{a,z}=F_{a,z}^{-1}\) and \(H_z=F_{1,z}-F_{0,z}\). The inverses exist because \(q_{a,n}\ge c>0\). If \(u_z=\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n(\cdot,z)\), integration of the Neumann equation gives
\[
-\bar q_n(y,z)u_z'(y)=H_z(y),
\qquad
\int_0^1\Delta_nu_z\,dy=\int_0^1\frac{H_z(y)^2}{\bar q_n(y,z)}dy. \tag{1}
\]
Since \(F_{0,z}\{Q_{0,z}(v)\}=F_{1,z}\{Q_{1,z}(v)\}=v\), the mean-value theorem gives
\[
H_z\{Q_{0,z}(v)\}
=q_{1,n}(\zeta_{v,z},z)\{Q_{0,z}(v)-Q_{1,z}(v)\}                \tag{2}
\]
for an intermediate point \(\zeta_{v,z}\). Changing variables \(v=F_{0,z}(y)\) in the quantile representation of \(W_2^2\), and using \(c\le q_{a,n},\bar q_n\le C\) in (1)--(2), gives constants \(0<c_E<C_E<\infty\) such that
\[
c_E\int\Delta_n\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n\,dy
\le W_2^2(Q_{0,n}(\cdot\mid z),Q_{1,n}(\cdot\mid z))
\le C_E\int\Delta_n\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n\,dy.              \tag{3}
\]
The shared marginal allows integration against one \(R_n\). By the definitions of \(\rho_n\), \(\theta_n\), and the local-radius assumption,
\[
c_E\rho_n^2\le\theta_n\le C_E\rho_n^2\le C_E\rho^2.           \tag{4}
\]
Thus the zero estimator has uniform root-mean-square risk at most \(C_E\rho^2\).

Put
\[
p_n=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{2+a_y}
+\rho^{2a_y/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^2.
\]
The proposed expansion and its \(L^2\) pilot bound, without imposing absorption, give at the corrected tuning
\[
\|\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y^*,J_z^*,n}-\theta_n\|_2
\lesssim\rho n^{-1/2}+\frac{A_{J_y^*,J_z^*,n}}n
+|B^y_{J_y^*,n}|+|B^z_{J_z^*,n}|+p_n.                           \tag{5}
\]
The corrected tuning and spectrum proposition imply
\[
\frac{A_{J_y^*,J_z^*,n}}n
+|B^y_{J_y^*,n}|+|B^z_{J_z^*,n}|lesssim r_{n,d}(s_z),            \tag{6}
\]
so the explicit Gram-corrected estimator has risk at most \(C\{\rho n^{-1/2}+r_{n,d}(s_z)+p_n\}\).

For fixed \(0<\tau<\min\{s_y,s_z\}\), the already proved \(\mathrm{lem{:}anisotropic\text{-}smoothcot\text{-}median\text{-}envelope}\) gives
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}
\{E_P(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{sm,med}}_{\tau,n}-\theta_n)^2\}^{1/2}
\le C_\tau n^{-a_\tau}(\log n)^{a_\tau}.                       \tag{7}
\]
When \(s_z\ge1\), the already proved \(\mathrm{lem{:}direct\text{-}cot\text{-}median\text{-}envelope}\) gives
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}
\{E_P(\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{dir,med}}-\theta_n)^2\}^{1/2}
\lesssim n^{-1/(d+2)}(\log n)^{1+1/(d+2)}.                      \tag{8}
\]
For fixed \((n,d,s_y,s_z,\rho)\), select before observing the data the statistic among the zero, Gram-corrected, SmoothCOT, and, when available, direct-COT estimators with the smallest deterministic bound. This is one data-driven sample map, not a law-specific oracle, and proves the upper minimum in the theorem.

For the zero and quadratic converse, use \(\mathrm{def{:}rademacher\text{-}family}\). The already proved \(\mathrm{lem{:}explicit\text{-}mixture\text{-}checks}\) puts every component in the same model class and, after reducing the fixed \(c_0\) if necessary, gives
\[
\chi^2(\overline{\mathbb P}_n,\mathbb P_n^0)\le\eta<1,
\qquad
\theta_n(\omega)-\theta_n(0)\ge c_M\min\{\rho^2,r_{n,d}(s_z)\}. \tag{9}
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz gives \(\|P-Q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le\chi^2(P,Q)^{1/2}/2\), so the testing affinity is bounded away from zero. Thresholding any estimator halfway between the null and alternative endpoints and applying Jensen's inequality yields
\[
\inf_{\widetilde\theta_n}\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}
\{E_P(\widetilde\theta_n-\theta_n)^2\}^{1/2}
\ge c\min\{\rho^2,r_{n,d}(s_z)\}.                               \tag{10}
\]
This sign mixture supplies only the zero and quadratic lower bounds.

The regular lower bound uses a separate two-point argument. Extend \(q_{a,t}^{0}\) constantly in \(z\). When \(\rho\ge K n^{-1/2}\), choose fixed small constants with \(t=c_7\rho\) and \(t'=t+c_8n^{-1/2}\), both of inverse-elliptic radius at most \(\rho\). The product Hellinger bound in the mixture-check lemma leaves positive testing affinity. If \(\Theta(t)\) is the endpoint, implicit differentiation gives
\[
\partial_tQ_{a,t}(v)
=-\frac{(2a-1)\sin\{2\pi Q_{a,t}(v)\}}
{4\pi q_{a,t}^{0}\{Q_{a,t}(v)\}}.                               \tag{11}
\]
The denominator is bounded away from zero. The cosine second-variation lemma gives \(\Theta'(0)=0\) and \(\Theta''(0)=1/(4\pi^2)\); continuity of \(\Theta''\) on a sufficiently small fixed neighborhood therefore gives
\[
|\Theta(t')-\Theta(t)|\ge c,t|t'-t|\ge c'\rho n^{-1/2}.        \tag{12}
\]
The same two-point testing reduction proves the regular lower bound. If \(\rho<K n^{-1/2}\), then \(r_{n,d}=n^{-b}\ge n^{-1}\gtrsim\rho^2\), where \(b=4s_z/(4s_z+d)<1\), and (10) has order \(\rho^2\). If \(\rho\ge K n^{-1/2}\), splitting according as \(\rho^2\le r_{n,d}\) or \(\rho^2>r_{n,d}\) gives
\[
\max\!\left\{\min(\rho^2,r_{n,d}),\rho n^{-1/2}\mathbf1\{\rho\ge Kn^{-1/2}\}\right\}
\asymp\min\{\rho^2,\rho n^{-1/2}+r_{n,d}\}.                   \tag{13}
\]
Equations (10)--(13) prove the lower frontier and explicitly separate its two constructions.

Combining (5)--(6) with (13) proves matching precisely when \(p_n=O\{\rho n^{-1/2}+r_{n,d}\}\). The supplemental envelopes cannot create another uniform matching region. Indeed, \(\tau<s_z\) implies
\[
a_\tau<\frac{s_z}{2s_z+d+1}<\frac{4s_z}{4s_z+d},
\qquad a_\tau<\frac12,                                          \tag{14}
\]
and, when \(s_z\ge1\),
\[
\frac1{d+2}<\frac12,
\qquad \frac1{d+2}<\frac{4s_z}{4s_z+d}.                         \tag{15}
\]
Finally, solving \(\rho_{Q,n}^2=r_{n,d}\) and \(\rho_{L,n}n^{-1/2}=r_{n,d}\) gives
\[
\rho_{Q,n}=n^{-2s_z/(4s_z+d)},
\qquad
\rho_{L,n}=n^{(d-4s_z)/\{2(4s_z+d)\}}.                          \tag{16}
\]
The latter tends to zero exactly when \(s_z>d/4\). When \(s_z\le d/4\), the equality quadratic lower bound is at least root-\(n\) order while the first projection vanishes at equality, excluding a uniformly positive-variance regular root-\(n\) description. This proves every assertion.
