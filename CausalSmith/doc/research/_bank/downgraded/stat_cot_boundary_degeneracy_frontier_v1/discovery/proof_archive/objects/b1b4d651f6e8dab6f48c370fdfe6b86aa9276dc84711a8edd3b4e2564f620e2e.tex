For \(d\ge1\), define
\[
E_{R,n}=C_B\left[
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\{2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-s_zJ_z}\}
+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{2+a_y}
+\rho^{2a_y/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^2
\right].
\]
Under the score-approximation premise of \(\mathrm{thm{:}mixed\text{-}spectral\text{-}transition}\), suppose a deterministic \(r_n^\circ=o(S_n/n)\) dominates in \(L^2\) the little-oh term in the corrected expansion.  If \(q_{n,1-\alpha/2}\) is the \((1-\alpha/2)\)-quantile of the absolute population Gaussian--quadratic proxy, then the infeasible interval centered at \(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}\) with half-width
\[
\frac{q_{n,1-\alpha/2}}n+C_\alpha(E_{R,n}+r_n^\circ)
\]
has asymptotic coverage at least \(1-\alpha\).  Markov's inequality allocates probability at most \(\alpha/2\) to the remainder, while the bounded-Lipschitz approximation, its diffuse Gaussian approximation, and Gaussian anti-concentration transfer the population-proxy quantile.  Its half-width is
\[
O\!\left(E_{R,n}+\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}}n+\sqrt{\frac{V_{L,n}}n}\right)
\le O\!\left(E_{R,n}+\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}}n+\frac{\rho_n}{\sqrt n}\right).
\]
This is only a population-calibrated partial result: its quantile depends on the population Hessian spectrum, local shifts, and \(V_{L,n}\).  The separate \(d=0\) theorem supplies the exact equality law and local minimax rate, but not an observable calibration uniform from equality through regular alternatives.
