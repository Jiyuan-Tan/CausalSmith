For \(F_{a,z}(y)=\int_0^yq_{a,n}(v,z)dv\), let \(Q_{a,z}=F_{a,z}^{-1}\) and \(H_z=F_{1,z}-F_{0,z}\). If \(u_z=\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n(\cdot,z)\), integration of the Neumann equation and then integration by parts give
\[
-\bar q_n\,u_z'=H_z,
\qquad \int_0^1\Delta_nu_z\,dy=\int_0^1\frac{H_z(y)^2}{\bar q_n(y,z)}\,dy. \tag{1}
\]
The inverse maps exist because \(q_{a,n}\ge c\). The mean-value theorem applied to \(F_{1,z}\) gives
\[
H_z\{Q_{0,z}(v)\}=q_{1,n}(\zeta_{v,z},z)\{Q_{0,z}(v)-Q_{1,z}(v)\}
\]
for an intermediate point \(\zeta_{v,z}\). Changing variables \(v=F_{0,z}(y)\) and using all density bounds in this identity and (1) proves
\[
c_3\int\Delta_n\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n\,dy
\le W_2^2(Q_{0,n},Q_{1,n}\mid z)
\le C_3\int\Delta_n\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n\,dy.                  \tag{2}
\]
The shared marginal permits integration against the same \(R_n\), so
\[
c_4\rho_n^2\le\theta_n\le C_4\rho_n^2\le C_4\rho^2.                \tag{3}
\]
Thus the zero estimator has uniform root-mean-square risk at most \(C_4\rho^2\).

Put
\[
p_n=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{2+a_y}
+\rho^{2a_y/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^2.
\]
Before imposing the absorption premise of \(\mathrm{thm{:}uniform\text{-}second\text{-}order\text{-}expansion}\), expand the statistic as in (10) of \(\mathrm{lem{:}observable\text{-}pilot\text{-}control}\). Its conditional mean is (13), its residual linear conditional variance is (16), and its canonical replacement variance is (17). Tail integration of (2) in that lemma therefore gives, without any smallness assumption,
\[
\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}-\theta_n=\mathbb L+\mathbb U+B^y+B^z+R,
\qquad
\|R\|_2\le Cp_n+o\!\left\{\rho n^{-1/2}+A_{J_y,J_z,n}/n\right\}.       \tag{4}
\]
The spectrum and bias bounds then give at \((J_y^*,J_z^*)\)
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}
\{E_P(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y^*,J_z^*,n}-\theta_n)^2\}^{1/2}
\le C\{\rho n^{-1/2}+r_{n,d}(s_z)+p_n\}.                            \tag{5}
\]
The minimax experiment fixes \(\rho\), so selecting the zero statistic or this single observable sample map according to the smaller deterministic guarantee proves the displayed upper bound. This selection uses no law-specific nuisance.

For the converse, use \(\mathrm{def{:}rademacher\text{-}family}\). The established \(\mathrm{lem{:}explicit\text{-}mixture\text{-}checks}\) puts every component in the same model class and supplies, for sufficiently small fixed \(c_0\),
\[
\chi^2(\overline{\mathbb P}_n,\mathbb P_n^0)\le\eta<1,
\qquad \theta_n(\omega)-\theta_n(0)\ge c_5\min\{\rho^2,r_{n,d}(s_z)\}. \tag{6}
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz applied to the likelihood ratio gives \(\|P-Q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le\chi^2(P,Q)^{1/2}/2\), so the testing affinity in (6) is bounded below. Thresholding an arbitrary estimator halfway between the endpoint values turns it into a test; on a testing error its absolute estimation error is at least half the separation. Averaging over the mixture proves
\[
\inf_{\widetilde\theta_n}\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}
\{E_P(\widetilde\theta_n-\theta_n)^2\}^{1/2}
\ge c_6\min\{\rho^2,r_{n,d}(s_z)\}.                                 \tag{7}
\]

For the regular direction, extend \(q_{a,t}^0\) constantly in \(z\). When \(\rho\ge Kn^{-1/2}\), choose fixed small constants so that \(t=c_7\rho\) and \(t'=t+c_8n^{-1/2}\) both have radius at most \(\rho\). The product Hellinger bound in the mixture-check lemma leaves positive testing affinity. Writing the endpoint as \(\Theta(t)\), implicit differentiation gives
\[
\partial_tQ_{a,t}(v)
=-\frac{(2a-1)\sin\{2\pi Q_{a,t}(v)\}}{4\pi q_{a,t}^0\{Q_{a,t}(v)\}}. \tag{8}
\]
The quotient and its derivative are uniformly bounded near zero. Hence \(\Theta''\) is continuous; \(\mathrm{lem{:}cosine\text{-}second\text{-}variation}\) gives \(\Theta'(0)=0\) and \(\Theta''(0)=1/(4\pi^2)\). Shrinking the fixed neighborhood and integrating twice yields
\[
|\Theta(t')-\Theta(t)|\ge c_9t|t'-t|\ge c_{10}\rho n^{-1/2}.         \tag{9}
\]
The same testing reduction supplies this regular lower bound. If \(\rho<Kn^{-1/2}\), then \(r_{n,d}=n^{-b}\ge n^{-1}\gtrsim\rho^2\), where \(b=4s_z/(4s_z+d)<1\), so (7) already has order \(\rho^2\). Splitting at \(\rho^2=r_{n,d}\) now gives the elementary comparison
\[
\max\!\left[\min\{\rho^2,r_{n,d}\},\rho n^{-1/2}\mathbf1\{\rho\ge Kn^{-1/2}\}\right]
\asymp\min\{\rho^2,\rho n^{-1/2}+r_{n,d}\}.                        \tag{10}
\]
Equations (5) and (10) prove the bracket and its matching condition. Solving \(\rho_{Q,n}^2=r_{n,d}\) and \(\rho_{L,n}n^{-1/2}=r_{n,d}\) gives the stated transition radii. The latter vanishes exactly when \(s_z>d/4\). If \(s_z\le d/4\), the equality quadratic lower bound is at least root-\(n\) order while the linear projection is identically zero, ruling out a uniformly positive-variance regular root-\(n\) description of the whole class.
