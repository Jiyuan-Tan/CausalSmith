For \(d\ge1\), abbreviate the deterministic remainder envelope by
\[
E_{R,n}=C_B\left[
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\{2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-s_zJ_z}\}
+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{2+a_y}
+\rho^{2a_y/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^2
\right].                                                     \tag{1}
\]
The uniform little-oh assertion in the established second-order expansion permits a deterministic sequence \(r_n^\circ\) such that its \(L^2\) norm is bounded by \(r_n^\circ\), uniformly over the triangular collection under consideration, and
\[
r_n^\circ=o\!\left\{\frac{\rho}{\sqrt n}+\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}}n\right\}. \tag{2}
\]
In addition to the score-approximation premises of the established mixed-spectral transition theorem, define the population Gaussian--quadratic variable
\[
G_n^\circ=\sum_{e=1}^2\sum_k\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}
\{(\xi_{e,k}+\eta_{k,n})^2-(1+\eta_{k,n}^2)\},               \tag{3}
\]
and let \(q_{n,1-\alpha/2}^\circ\) be the \((1-\alpha/2)\)-quantile of \(|G_n^\circ|\).  Consider the infeasible, population-calibrated interval
\[
\mathcal I_n^\circ=
\left[
\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}
-\frac{q_{n,1-\alpha/2}^\circ}{n}-C_\alpha(E_{R,n}+r_n^\circ),
\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}
+\frac{q_{n,1-\alpha/2}^\circ}{n}+C_\alpha(E_{R,n}+r_n^\circ)
\right].                                                       \tag{4}
\]
This interval has asymptotic coverage at least \(1-\alpha\), uniformly over every triangular collection on which the premises just stated hold.  To verify the claim, write
\[
B_n=\sqrt{nV_{L,n}},
\qquad S_n=A_{J_y,J_z,n}+B_n.                                  \tag{5}
\]
The variance of the normalized Gaussian proxy in the mixed-spectral transition theorem is
\[
\sigma_n^2=\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}^2+B_n^2}
{(A_{J_y,J_z,n}+B_n)^2}.                                      \tag{6}
\]
The spectral proposition gives \(A_{J_y,J_z,n}>0\), while \(B_n\ge0\) by the definition of a variance.  Hence
\[
\frac12\le\sigma_n^2\le1.                                   \tag{7}
\]
Indeed, the lower inequality is equivalent to \((A_{J_y,J_z,n}-B_n)^2\ge0\), and the upper inequality follows from \(2A_{J_y,J_z,n}B_n\ge0\).  Thus the centered normal comparison laws have a uniform anti-concentration modulus.  The two bounded-Lipschitz approximations in the mixed-spectral transition theorem, followed by a Lipschitz smoothing of the indicator of a symmetric interval and (7), imply
\[
P\!\left(
\left|n\{\Theta^{\mathrm{or}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n\}\right|
\le q_{n,1-\alpha/2}^\circ
\right)\ge1-\frac\alpha2-o(1).                                \tag{8}
\]
Because \(\rho_n\le\rho\), the established second-order expansion, (1), and (2) give a fixed \(C_0<\infty\) for which
\[
\|\mathbb R_{J_y,J_z,n}\|_2\le C_0(E_{R,n}+r_n^\circ)        \tag{9}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Markov's inequality applied to \(\mathbb R_{J_y,J_z,n}^2\) yields
\[
P\!\left\{
|\mathbb R_{J_y,J_z,n}|>C_\alpha(E_{R,n}+r_n^\circ)
\right\}\le\frac{C_0^2}{C_\alpha^2}.                         \tag{10}
\]
Choose \(C_\alpha\ge C_0\sqrt{2/\alpha}\).  The exact identity
\[
\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n
=\Theta^{\mathrm{or}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n
+\mathbb R_{J_y,J_z,n}                                      \tag{11}
\]
and the union bound applied to (8)--(10) prove the asserted coverage of (4).

The same Gaussian comparison and (7) show
\[
q_{n,1-\alpha/2}^\circ=O(S_n).                                \tag{12}
\]
Consequently, the half-width in (4) is
\[
O\!\left\{
E_{R,n}+r_n^\circ+\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}}n
+\sqrt{\frac{V_{L,n}}n}
\right\}
\le
O\!\left\{
E_{R,n}+\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}}n+\frac{\rho}{\sqrt n}
\right\},                                                     \tag{13}
\]
where (2) absorbs \(r_n^\circ\), and the final inequality uses the established bound \(V_{L,n}\lesssim\rho_n^2\le\rho^2\).  At the corrected resolutions, the spectral proposition and the anisotropic tuning identities give
\[
\frac{A_{J_y^*,J_z^*,n}}n
\asymp\frac{2^{dJ_z^*/2}}n
\asymp n^{-2s_z/(2s_z+d)}
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{d/(2s_z+d)}.                    \tag{14}
\]
Thus (13) is exactly the requested positive-dimensional width envelope, but for a population-calibrated rather than observable interval.

There is a concrete obstruction to replacing the population quantities by the currently certified observed spectral plug-in over the whole primitive model.  That plug-in conclusion assumes
\[
D_{J_y,J_z}\log n/m=o(1).                                     \tag{15}
\]
For the admissible values \(d=1\), \(s_y=1/10\), and \(s_z=1\), the pilot exponent equals
\[
\frac{\beta}{2\beta+1}
=\frac1{2+s_y^{-1}+ds_z^{-1}}=\frac1{13},                    \tag{16}
\]
whereas the corrected tuning gives
\[
D_{J_y^*,J_z^*}
=\{n\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\}^{14/11+o(1)}.             \tag{17}
\]
Since \(n\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}
=n^{12/13}(\log n)^{1/13}\), equations (16)--(17) imply
\[
\frac{D_{J_y^*,J_z^*}\log n}{m}
=n^{25/143+o(1)}(\log n)^{1+14/143+o(1)}\longrightarrow\infty. \tag{18}
\]
Therefore (15) is not a consequence of the primitive class and corrected tuning.  Even in subregimes satisfying (15), the established results do not supply a conditional common-multiplier approximation for the joint linear--quadratic statistic, uniform control of its noncentral spectral coordinates, or preservation of the covariance between its linear and quadratic parts.  Moreover, the proved failure of a uniform reverse inequality \(V_{L,n}\gtrsim\rho_n^2\) prevents ordinary relative studentization from bridging equality and regular alternatives.  When \(d=0\), the established no-covariate theorem gives the exact uniform-density equality law and the local minimax rate, but not an observable quantile coupling uniform over arbitrary equal densities, local alternatives, and fixed unequal laws.  These are the residual ingredients needed to replace (4) by the requested \(\mathcal I_n^{\mathrm{mix}}\).
