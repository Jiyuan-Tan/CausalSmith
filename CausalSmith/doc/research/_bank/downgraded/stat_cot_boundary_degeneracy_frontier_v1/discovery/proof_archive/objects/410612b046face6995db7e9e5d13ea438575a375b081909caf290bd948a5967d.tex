Under \(L>1\), the lower frontier and an honest loose upper frontier close under the same model class.  Uniform density bounds and the one-dimensional CDF representation give \(\theta_n\asymp\rho_n^2\), so the zero estimator has root mean-square risk \(O(\rho^2)\).  The proposed second-order expansion, its exact conditional variance identities, and bounded wavelet exponential moments give root mean-square risk
\[
 O\{\rho n^{-1/2}+r_{n,d}(s_z)+
 \varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^3+
 \rho^{\,2/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^2\}
\]
for the rate-balanced estimator.  Taking the better of these two sample maps gives the corresponding upper minimum.  The Rademacher mixture yields the lower term \(\min\{\rho^2,r_{n,d}(s_z)\}\).  The constant-in-\(z\) cosine pair at amplitudes \(c_0\rho\) and \(c_0\rho+c_0n^{-1/2}\) has bounded product Hellinger distance and endpoint gap of order \(\rho n^{-1/2}\) when \(\rho\gg n^{-1/2}\).  The maximum of these lower bounds is, within a factor two, \(\min\{\rho^2,\rho n^{-1/2}+r_{n,d}(s_z)\}\).  Thus the bracket matches whenever \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^3+\rho^{\,2/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^2=O\{\rho n^{-1/2}+r_{n,d}(s_z)\}\).  Solving \(\rho^2=r_{n,d}\) and \(\rho n^{-1/2}=r_{n,d}\) gives exactly \(\rho_{Q,n}\) and \(\rho_{L,n}\), and \(\rho_{L,n}\to0\) exactly when \(s_z>d/4\).
