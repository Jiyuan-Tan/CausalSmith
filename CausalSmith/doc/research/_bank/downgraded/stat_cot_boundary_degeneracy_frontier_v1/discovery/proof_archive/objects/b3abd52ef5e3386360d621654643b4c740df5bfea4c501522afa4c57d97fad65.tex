Write
\[
E_{R,n}=C_B\left[
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}
\{2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-s_zJ_z}\}
+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{2+a_y}
+\rho^{2a_y/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^2
\right].
\]
The uniform little-oh term in
\(\mathrm{thm{:}uniform\text{-}second\text{-}order\text{-}expansion}\)
admits, on every triangular collection covered by that theorem, a deterministic
sequence \(r_n^\circ\) satisfying
\[
r_n^\circ=o\!\left\{\rho n^{-1/2}+A_{J_y,J_z,n}/n\right\}
\]
and uniformly bounding the \(L^2\)-norm of that little-oh remainder.  Under the
score-approximation hypotheses of
\(\mathrm{thm{:}mixed\text{-}spectral\text{-}transition}\), put
\[
G_n^\circ=
\sum_{e=1}^2\sum_k\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}
\{(\xi_{e,k}+\eta_{k,n})^2-(1+\eta_{k,n}^2)\},
\]
and let \(q_{n,1-\alpha/2}^\circ\) denote the
\((1-\alpha/2)\)-quantile of \(|G_n^\circ|\).  For a sufficiently large fixed
constant \(C_\alpha\), the population-calibrated interval
\[
\mathcal I_n^\circ=
\left[
\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}
-\frac{q_{n,1-\alpha/2}^\circ}{n}
-C_\alpha(E_{R,n}+r_n^\circ),
\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}
+\frac{q_{n,1-\alpha/2}^\circ}{n}
+C_\alpha(E_{R,n}+r_n^\circ)
\right]
\]
has asymptotic coverage at least \(1-\alpha\), uniformly over that triangular
collection.  Indeed, with
\(B_n=\sqrt{nV_{L,n}}\) and
\(S_n=A_{J_y,J_z,n}+B_n\), the Gaussian comparison variance obeys
\[
\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}^2+B_n^2}{S_n^2}\in[1/2,1].
\]
Thus the population proxy has uniform Gaussian anti-concentration.  Smoothing
the bounded-Lipschitz comparison in
\(\mathrm{thm{:}mixed\text{-}spectral\text{-}transition}\) controls the proxy
quantile event, and Markov's inequality applied to
\(\mathrm{thm{:}uniform\text{-}second\text{-}order\text{-}expansion}\)
controls the remainder.  Consequently the half-width is
\[
O\!\left\{
E_{R,n}+r_n^\circ+A_{J_y,J_z,n}/n+\sqrt{V_{L,n}/n}
\right\}
=O\!\left\{E_{R,n}+A_{J_y,J_z,n}/n+\rho n^{-1/2}\right\}.
\]
At the corrected resolutions,
\[
A_{J_y^*,J_z^*,n}/n
\asymp n^{-2s_z/(2s_z+d)}
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{d/(2s_z+d)},
\]
so \(\mathcal I_n^\circ\) has the requested positive-dimensional width.  It
is infeasible because its quantile uses the population spectrum and population
local shifts.

The presently proved observable controls do not justify replacing that
quantile by a data-dependent common-multiplier quantile at the corrected
resolutions.  In fact,
\(\mathrm{lem{:}observable\text{-}pilot\text{-}control}\) gives only an
operator-norm comparison for the pilot Hessian.  The resulting generic
Hilbert--Schmidt estimate is
\[
\|\widehat B_{J_y,J_z,n}^{(-e)}-B_{J_y,J_z,n}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}
\lesssim_P
\sqrt{D_{J_y,J_z}}\,
(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{a_y}
+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}+\lambda_n),
\]
whereas
\(\mathrm{prop{:}anisotropic\text{-}spectrum\text{-}bias}\) gives
\(\|K_{J_y,J_z,n}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\asymp2^{dJ_z/2}\).  The uniform
population covariance bounds in that proposition make the same scale valid
for \(\|B_{J_y,J_z,n}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\).  Thus the displayed comparison is
not relatively small in general.  For the explicit admissible choice
\(d=1\), \(s_y=1/10\), and \(s_z=1\), one has
\(a_y=1/10\),
\(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}=(\log n/n)^{1/13}\), and
\[
D_{J_y^*,J_z^*,n}
=\{n\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\}^{14/11+o(1)},
\qquad
2^{dJ_z^*}
=\{n\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\}^{2/3+o(1)}.
\]
Hence even the leading ratio allowed by the frozen operator bound satisfies
\[
\frac{\sqrt{D_{J_y^*,J_z^*,n}}
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{a_y}}
{2^{dJ_z^*/2}}
=n^{5057/18590+o(1)}
(\log n)^{10/429+1/130+o(1)}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\frac{D_{J_y^*,J_z^*,n}\log n}{m}
=n^{25/143+o(1)}
(\log n)^{1+14/143+o(1)}\longrightarrow\infty,
\]
so the additional observable spectral condition in
\(\mathrm{lem{:}observable\text{-}pilot\text{-}control}\) also fails for
this admissible smoothness pair.

A direct scalar multiplier applied jointly to the observed linear summands and
diagonal-free quadratic kernel could avoid estimating the full covariance
matrix.  To certify it, however, one still must prove, uniformly over the
primitive model class, conditional trace and contraction bounds for the
pilot-dependent kernel, control its noncentral local coordinates, and establish
anti-concentration for its conditional quantiles on the common mixed scale.
Neither the population contraction lemma nor the displayed operator-norm pilot
bound implies those assertions, as the preceding divergence demonstrates.
For \(d=0\), the cited uniform-density quantile-process limit proves only the
stated null law; it does not provide a multiplier coupling uniform over all
equal densities, local alternatives, and fixed unequal laws.  Thus the
population interval above is the strongest result derivable from the frozen
core, while observable mixed calibration remains a genuine open problem rather
than a consequence of the existing covariance plug-in argument.
