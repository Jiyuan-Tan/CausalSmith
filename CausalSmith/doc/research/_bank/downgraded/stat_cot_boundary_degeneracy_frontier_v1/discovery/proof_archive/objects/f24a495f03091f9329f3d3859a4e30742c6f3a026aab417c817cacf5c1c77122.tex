For \(d\ge1\), define
\[
E_{R,n}=C_B\left[
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\{2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-s_zJ_z}\}
+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{2+a_y}
+\rho^{2a_y/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^2
\right].
\]
The proved exact-\(\theta_n\) expansion and the population spectral theorem do yield an infeasible benchmark. Suppose the score-approximation condition of \(\mathrm{thm{:}mixed\text{-}spectral\text{-}transition}\) holds and a deterministic sequence \(r_n^{\circ}=o(S_n/n)\) dominates in \(L^2\) the little-oh term in \(\mathrm{thm{:}uniform\text{-}second\text{-}order\text{-}expansion}\). Let \(q_{n,1-\alpha/2}\) be the \((1-\alpha/2)\)-quantile of the absolute population Gaussian-quadratic proxy in that theorem. Centering at \(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}\) and taking half-width
\[
\frac{q_{n,1-\alpha/2}}{n}+C_\alpha\{E_{R,n}+r_n^{\circ}\},
\]
where \(C_\alpha\) is chosen from the uniform \(L^2\) remainder constant, gives population-calibrated asymptotic coverage at least \(1-\alpha\). Indeed, Markov's inequality assigns probability at most \(\alpha/2\) to the remainder enlargement; the bounded-Lipschitz approximation, the diffuse normal approximation with variance in \([1/2,1]\), and normal anti-concentration transfer the proxy quantile with error \(o(1)\). Its half-width is
\[
O\!\left(E_{R,n}+\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}}n+\sqrt{\frac{V_{L,n}}n}\right),
\]
which at the corrected resolutions is the certified observable point-estimation scale. This interval is infeasible because its quantile uses the population Hessian, spectrum, local shifts, and linear variance. The separate \(d=0\) theorem supplies the exact equality law and local minimax rate, but not one uniform observable local-to-null calibration.
