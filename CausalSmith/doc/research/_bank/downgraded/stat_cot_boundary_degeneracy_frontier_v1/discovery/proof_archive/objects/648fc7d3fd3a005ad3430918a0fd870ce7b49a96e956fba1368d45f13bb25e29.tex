For \(F_{a,z}(y)=\int_0^yq_{a,n}(v,z)\,dv\), let \(Q_{a,z}=F_{a,z}^{-1}\) and \(H_z=F_{1,z}-F_{0,z}\).  The inverses exist because \(q_{a,n}\ge c>0\) by \(\mathrm{ass{:}conditional\text{-}density\text{-}bounds}\).  If \(u_z=\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n(\cdot,z)\), the zero conditional mass of \(\Delta_n\), the Neumann equation, and integration by parts give
\[
-\bar q_n(y,z)u_z'(y)=H_z(y),
\qquad
\int_0^1\Delta_n(y,z)u_z(y)\,dy
=\int_0^1\frac{H_z(y)^2}{\bar q_n(y,z)}\,dy.                                   \tag{1}
\]
Since \(F_{0,z}\{Q_{0,z}(v)\}=F_{1,z}\{Q_{1,z}(v)\}=v\), the mean-value theorem gives
\[
H_z\{Q_{0,z}(v)\}
=q_{1,n}(\zeta_{v,z},z)\{Q_{0,z}(v)-Q_{1,z}(v)\}                               \tag{2}
\]
for some \(\zeta_{v,z}\) between the two quantiles.  Changing variables \(v=F_{0,z}(y)\) in the quantile formula for \(W_2^2\), and using \(c\le q_{a,n},\bar q_n\le C\) in (1)--(2), gives constants \(0<c_E<C_E<\infty\), depending only on \(c\) and \(C\), such that
\[
c_E\int\Delta_n\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n\,dy
\le W_2^2\{Q_{0,n}(\cdot\mid z),Q_{1,n}(\cdot\mid z)\}
\le C_E\int\Delta_n\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n\,dy.                             \tag{3}
\]
The common-marginal assumption permits integration against the single law \(R_n\).  By the definitions of \(\rho_n\) and \(\theta_n\), followed by \(\mathrm{ass{:}local\text{-}equality\text{-}radius}\),
\[
c_E\rho_n^2\le\theta_n\le C_E\rho_n^2\le C_E\rho^2.                          \tag{4}
\]
Thus the zero estimator has uniform root-mean-square risk at most \(C_E\rho^2\).

Define
\[
g_n=n^{-2s_z/(2s_z+d)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{d/(2s_z+d)},
\qquad
p_n=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{2+a_y}
+\rho^{2a_y/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^2.
\]
The corrected \(\mathrm{thm{:}uniform\text{-}second\text{-}order\text{-}expansion}\) gives the exact identity \(\operatorname{sd}(\mathbb L)=\sqrt{V_{L,n}/n}\) and the one-sided uniform bound \(V_{L,n}\lesssim\rho_n^2\); it does not use a reverse bound.  Together with \(\rho_n\le\rho\) and \(\mathrm{def{:}anisotropic\text{-}tuning}\), this yields
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}(s_y,s_z,\rho)}
\|\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y^*,J_z^*,n}-\theta_n\|_2
\lesssim\rho n^{-1/2}+g_n+p_n.                                                  \tag{5}
\]
The estimator in (5) is the single observable sample map in \(\mathrm{def{:}anisotropic\text{-}estimator}\); it contains no law-specific oracle input.

For every fixed \(0<\tau<\min\{s_y,s_z\}\), \(\mathrm{lem{:}anisotropic\text{-}smoothcot\text{-}median\text{-}envelope}\) gives
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}(s_y,s_z,\rho)}
\|\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{sm,med}}_{\tau,n}-\theta_n\|_2
\le C_\tau n^{-a_\tau}(\log n)^{a_\tau}.                                      \tag{6}
\]
When \(s_z\ge1\), \(\mathrm{lem{:}direct\text{-}cot\text{-}median\text{-}envelope}\) gives
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}(s_y,s_z,\rho)}
\|\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{dir,med}}-\theta_n\|_2
\lesssim n^{-1/(d+2)}(\log n)^{1+1/(d+2)}.                                     \tag{7}
\]
For fixed class indices \((n,d,s_y,s_z,\rho)\), select before seeing the data whichever of the zero, Gram-corrected, SmoothCOT, and applicable direct-COT maps has the smallest displayed deterministic guarantee.  This is still one data-driven estimator.  Equations (4)--(7) prove the stated upper minimum.

For the zero and quadratic converse, use \(\mathrm{def{:}rademacher\text{-}family}\).  By \(\mathrm{lem{:}explicit\text{-}mixture\text{-}checks}\), every component belongs to the same class, and a sufficiently small fixed \(c_0\) ensures
\[
\chi^2(\overline{\mathbb P}_n,\mathbb P_n^0)\le\eta<1,
\qquad
\theta_n(\omega)-\theta_n(0)
\ge c_M\min\{\rho^2,r_{n,d}(s_z)\}.                                            \tag{8}
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz applied to the likelihood ratio gives
\(\|P-Q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le\chi^2(P,Q)^{1/2}/2\), so the testing affinity in (8) is bounded below.  Thresholding an arbitrary estimator halfway between the common null endpoint and the alternative endpoint, and using \(E|X|\le\{E(X^2)\}^{1/2}\), gives
\[
\inf_{\widetilde\theta_n}\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}(s_y,s_z,\rho)}
\{E_P(\widetilde\theta_n-\theta_n)^2\}^{1/2}
\ge c_2\min\{\rho^2,r_{n,d}(s_z)\}.                                           \tag{9}
\]

For the regular lower term, extend \(\mathrm{def{:}no\text{-}covariate\text{-}witness}\) constantly in \(z\).  When \(\rho\ge K n^{-1/2}\), choose
\[
t=c_7\min\{\rho,t_0\},
\qquad t'=t+c_8n^{-1/2},                                                        \tag{10}
\]
where \(t_0,c_7,c_8>0\) are fixed and sufficiently small and \(K\) is sufficiently large.  The exact inverse-elliptic radius of the symmetric cosine pair is \(|t|/(2\sqrt2\pi)\), so both radii in (10) are at most \(\rho\).  Positivity and H\"older membership follow from \(\mathrm{ass{:}holder\text{-}radius\text{-}interior}\) and \(\mathrm{lem{:}explicit\text{-}mixture\text{-}checks}\).  The product Hellinger bound in that lemma leaves a testing affinity bounded away from zero.

If \(\Theta(t)\) is the endpoint on this submodel, implicit differentiation of the conditional quantiles gives
\[
\partial_tQ_{a,t}(v)
=-\frac{(2a-1)\sin\{2\pi Q_{a,t}(v)\}}
{4\pi q_{a,t}^{0}\{Q_{a,t}(v)\}}.                                             \tag{11}
\]
The denominator is uniformly positive.  The second-variation calculation in \(\mathrm{lem{:}cosine\text{-}second\text{-}variation}\) gives \(\Theta'(0)=0\) and \(\Theta''(0)=1/(4\pi^2)\).  Equation (11), dominated differentiation, and the density lower bound make \(\Theta''\) continuous on a fixed neighborhood of zero.  Taylor's theorem and (10) therefore imply
\[
|\Theta(t')-\Theta(t)|
\ge c_3\min\{\rho,t_0\}n^{-1/2}
\ge c_3t_0\rho n^{-1/2},                                                       \tag{12}
\]
where the last inequality uses \(0\le\rho\le1\).  The two-point testing reduction used for (9) proves the regular lower term.

Put \(r=r_{n,d}(s_z)=n^{-b}\), where \(b=4s_z/(4s_z+d)<1\).  If \(\rho<K n^{-1/2}\), then \(r\gtrsim n^{-1}\gtrsim\rho^2\), so (9) is of order \(\rho^2\), which is the target minimum.  If \(\rho\ge K n^{-1/2}\), split according as \(\rho^2\le r\) or \(\rho^2>r\).  In the first case (9) supplies \(\rho^2\).  In the second, (9) and (12) supply \(\max\{r,\rho n^{-1/2}\}\), which is comparable to \(r+\rho n^{-1/2}\); moreover \(\rho n^{-1/2}\le K^{-1}\rho^2\) and \(r<\rho^2\).  Hence in all cases
\[
\max\!\left\{\min(\rho^2,r),
\rho n^{-1/2}\mathbf1\{\rho\ge Kn^{-1/2}\}\right\}
\asymp\min\{\rho^2,\rho n^{-1/2}+r\}.                                        \tag{13}
\]
This proves the lower frontier in \(\mathrm{def{:}conditional\text{-}minimax\text{-}rate}\).

It remains to compare the two sides without conflating them.  Write
\[
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}=(\log n/n)^e,
\qquad e=\frac1{2+s_y^{-1}+ds_z^{-1}}.
\]
Since \(d\ge1\) and \(s_y,s_z>0\),
\[
e<\frac{s_z}{2s_z+d}<\frac{2s_z}{4s_z+d}.                                      \tag{14}
\]
The polynomial exponent of \(g_n\) is \((2s_z+ed)/(2s_z+d)\), and direct subtraction gives
\[
\frac{4s_z}{4s_z+d}-\frac{2s_z+ed}{2s_z+d}
=\frac{d\{2s_z-e(4s_z+d)\}}{(4s_z+d)(2s_z+d)}>0.                              \tag{15}
\]
The logarithmic factor has positive power, so
\[
n^{-4s_z/(4s_z+d)}=o(g_n).                                                       \tag{16}
\]
Furthermore, \(\tau<s_z\) implies
\[
a_\tau<\frac{s_z}{2s_z+d+1}<\frac{4s_z}{4s_z+d},
\qquad a_\tau<\frac12,                                                         \tag{17}
\]
and, when \(s_z\ge1\),
\[
\frac1{d+2}<\min\!\left\{\frac12,\frac{4s_z}{4s_z+d}\right\}.               \tag{18}
\]
Thus neither fallback proves the intrinsic quadratic exponent, although either may sharpen the certified upper side.

Finally, solving \(\rho_{Q,n}^2=r_{n,d}(s_z)\) and \(\rho_{L,n}n^{-1/2}=r_{n,d}(s_z)\) gives
\[
\rho_{Q,n}=n^{-2s_z/(4s_z+d)},
\qquad
\rho_{L,n}=n^{(d-4s_z)/\{2(4s_z+d)\}}.                                        \tag{19}
\]
The second boundary tends to zero exactly when \(s_z>d/4\).  When \(s_z\le d/4\), the equality-local quadratic lower term is at least of root-\(n\) order while the first projection vanishes at equality, precluding a uniformly positive-variance root-\(n\) description.  Equations (4)--(19) prove the theorem's upper map, lower frontier, strict gap, and phase statements.  The only use of \(\rho_n/\sqrt n\) on the upper side is the one-sided variance envelope from the corrected expansion; no reverse inequality for \(V_{L,n}\) is used.
