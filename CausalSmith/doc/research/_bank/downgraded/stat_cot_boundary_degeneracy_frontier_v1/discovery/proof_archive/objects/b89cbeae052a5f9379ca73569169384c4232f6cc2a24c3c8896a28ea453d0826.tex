Write \(F_{a,z}(y)=\int_0^yq_{a,n}(v,z)dv\), let \(Q_{a,z}=F_{a,z}^{-1}\), and put
\[
H_z(y)=F_{1,z}(y)-F_{0,z}(y)=\int_0^y\Delta_n(v,z)dv.
\]
If \(u_z=\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n(\cdot,z)\), integration of the defining Neumann equation gives \(-\bar q_n\partial_yu_z=H_z\). Since \(H_z(0)=H_z(1)=0\), integration by parts yields
\[
\int_0^1\Delta_nu_zdy
=\int_0^1\bar q_n(\partial_yu_z)^2dy
=\int_0^1\frac{H_z(y)^2}{\bar q_n(y,z)}dy.            \tag{1}
\]
For \(u\in(0,1)\), the mean-value identity gives
\[
H_z\{Q_{0,z}(u)\}
=q_{1,n}(\zeta_{u,z},z)\{Q_{0,z}(u)-Q_{1,z}(u)\}
\]
for some \(\zeta_{u,z}\) between the quantiles. Changing variables \(u=F_{0,z}(y)\) and using \(c\le q_{a,n}\le C\) shows
\[
c_2\int_0^1H_z(y)^2dy
\le W_2^2(Q_{0,n}(\cdot\mid z),Q_{1,n}(\cdot\mid z))
\le C_2\int_0^1H_z(y)^2dy.                            \tag{2}
\]
Combining (1)--(2), integrating in \(R_n\), and using \(\rho_n\le\rho\), gives
\[
c_3\rho_n^2\le\theta_n\le C_3\rho_n^2\le C_3\rho^2.  \tag{3}
\]
Thus the zero estimator has root-mean-square risk at most \(C_3\rho^2\).

For the observable estimator put
\[
p_n=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^3+
\rho^{\,2/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^2.
\]
The exact decomposition preceding the negligibility specialization in \(\texttt{thm:uniform-second-order-expansion}\) is
\[
\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n
=\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}+\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}
+B^y_{J_y,n}+B^z_{J_z,n}+R_{J_y,J_z,n}.              \tag{4}
\]
We require a uniform \(L^2\), rather than only an in-probability, version of its bound. The number of pilot coefficients is
\[
\overline D_n\asymp2^{\bar J_y+d\bar J_z}
\asymp(n/\log n)^{1/(2\beta+1)}.
\]
The exponential-moment calculation in the pilot lemma gives, uniformly for fixed \(x\in[0,1]\),
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}
P\!\left(\max_e\left[\|\widehat r_n^{(-e)}-r_n\|_\infty+
\max_a\|\widehat q_{a,n}^{(-e)}-q_{a,n}\|_\infty\right]
>C\{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}+x\}\right)
\le C\exp\{-cnx^2/\overline D_n\}.                    \tag{5}
\]
The deterministic projection bias is \(O(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n})\); on the positivity event ratios and normalization are Lipschitz, and off that event fixed clipping keeps all quantities bounded. Integrating (5) gives each fixed moment at the corresponding power of \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\). The deterministic Taylor and interpolation inequalities in the pilot lemma therefore imply
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}\|R_{J_y,J_z,n}\|_{L^2(P)}
\le Cp_n+o\!\left(\frac{\rho}{\sqrt n}+\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}}n
+2^{-2(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-2s_zJ_z}\right).               \tag{6}
\]

Orthogonality and the variance identities in the expansion theorem, together with \(\rho_n\le\rho\), give
\[
\|\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}\|_2\le C\rho n^{-1/2},
\qquad
\|\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}\|_2\le C A_{J_y,J_z,n}/n.     \tag{7}
\]
At \((J_y^*,J_z^*)\), the spectrum and bias proposition gives
\[
A_{J_y^*,J_z^*,n}/n\asymp r_{n,d}(s_z),\qquad
|B^y_{J_y^*,n}|+|B^z_{J_z^*,n}|\le Cr_{n,d}(s_z).     \tag{8}
\]
Minkowski's inequality applied to (4), followed by (6)--(8), proves
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}
\{E_P(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y^*,J_z^*,n}-\theta_n)^2\}^{1/2}
\le C\{\rho n^{-1/2}+r_{n,d}(s_z)+p_n\}.             \tag{9}
\]
For the known class radius \(\rho\), select the better of this explicit sample map and the zero map. Equations (3) and (9) establish the asserted upper bound; in particular the zero map applies when \(\rho\lesssim\rho_{Q,n}\).

For the quadratic converse, choose the fixed \(c_0\) in \(\texttt{lem:explicit-mixture-checks}\) small enough that
\[
\chi^2(\overline{\mathbb P}_n,\mathbb P_n^0)\le\eta<1.
\]
The elementary inequality
\[
\|\overline{\mathbb P}_n-\mathbb P_n^0\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\le\frac12\{\chi^2(\overline{\mathbb P}_n,\mathbb P_n^0)\}^{1/2}
\]
leaves testing affinity bounded away from zero. The null endpoint is zero, and every mixture component has endpoint at least
\[
\delta_n=c_4\min\{\rho^2,r_{n,d}(s_z)\}.
\]
For any estimator, thresholding at \(\delta_n/2\) gives a test. On either testing error, its squared estimation loss is at least \(\delta_n^2/4\). Since for every test \(\phi\),
\[
\mathbb P_n^0\phi+\overline{\mathbb P}_n(1-\phi)
\ge1-\|\overline{\mathbb P}_n-\mathbb P_n^0\|_{\mathrm{TV}},
\]
we obtain
\[
\inf_{\widetilde\theta_n}\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}
\{E_P(\widetilde\theta_n-\theta_n)^2\}^{1/2}
\ge c_5\min\{\rho^2,r_{n,d}(s_z)\}.                  \tag{10}
\]

For the regular direction, extend \(q_{a,t}^{0}\) constantly in \(z\). When \(\rho\ge K n^{-1/2}\), compare \(t=c_6\rho\) with \(t'=t+c_7n^{-1/2}\), choosing fixed constants so both inverse-elliptic radii are at most \(\rho\). The Hellinger calculation in the mixture lemma leaves affinity bounded away from zero. Let \(\Theta(t)\) be the endpoint along this path. Its distribution function satisfies
\[
F_{a,t}(y)=y+(2a-1)t\sin(2\pi y)/(4\pi),
\]
and differentiation of \(F_{a,t}\{Q_{a,t}(u)\}=u\) gives
\[
\partial_tQ_{a,t}(u)=
-\frac{(2a-1)\sin\{2\pi Q_{a,t}(u)\}}
{4\pi q_{a,t}^0\{Q_{a,t}(u)\}}.                      \tag{11}
\]
The denominator is at least \(c\). A second differentiation of (11) shows that \(\Theta''\) is continuous near zero. By the cosine lemma, \(\Theta'(0)=0\) and \(\Theta''(0)=1/(4\pi^2)\). Shrinking the fixed neighborhood if necessary gives \(\Theta''(v)\ge1/(8\pi^2)\). Integrating twice yields
\[
|\Theta(t')-\Theta(t)|
\ge c_8t|t'-t|\ge c_9\rho n^{-1/2}.                  \tag{12}
\]
The same threshold-test argument therefore supplies a lower bound \(c_{10}\rho n^{-1/2}\). If \(\rho<Kn^{-1/2}\), then \(r_{n,d}(s_z)\ge n^{-1}\gtrsim\rho^2\), so (10) already gives order \(\rho^2\). The case split proves the elementary equivalence
\[
\max\!\left[\min\{\rho^2,r_{n,d}(s_z)\},
\rho n^{-1/2}\mathbf1\{\rho\ge Kn^{-1/2}\}\right]
\asymp
\min\{\rho^2,\rho n^{-1/2}+r_{n,d}(s_z)\}.            \tag{13}
\]
This proves the lower frontier.

If \(p_n=O\{\rho n^{-1/2}+r_{n,d}(s_z)\}\), (9)--(13) match; otherwise (9) and (13) are exactly the asserted honest bracket. Finally,
\[
\rho_{Q,n}^2=r_{n,d}(s_z)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\rho_{Q,n}=n^{-2s_z/(4s_z+d)},
\]
and
\[
\rho_{L,n}n^{-1/2}=r_{n,d}(s_z)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\rho_{L,n}=n^{(d-4s_z)/\{2(4s_z+d)\}}.
\]
Thus \(\rho_{L,n}\to0\) exactly when \(s_z>d/4\), giving the stated zones. If \(s_z\le d/4\), then \(r_{n,d}(s_z)\gtrsim n^{-1/2}\) and \(\rho_{L,n}\not\to0\). At equality the linear projection vanishes, while (10) keeps the quadratic difficulty at least at root-\(n\) order when \(s_z=d/4\), and larger below it. Hence a positive-variance regular root-\(n\) law cannot hold uniformly over the class.
