For \(e\in\{1,2\}\), let \(\mathscr F_{-e}\) be generated by the two pilot folds.  By \(\mathrm{ass{:}two\text{-}arm\text{-}sampling}\) and the deterministic split in the corrected estimator definition, the evaluation observations in fold \(e\) are independent of \(\mathscr F_{-e}\).  Conditional on \(\mathscr F_{-e}\), all pilots, scores, Gram matrices, and raw-moment Hessians are fixed.

Fix a fold, suppress its superscript, and put
\[
Z_{a,i}=\Phi(W_{a,i,n})-\mu_a,
\qquad d_a=\mu_a-\widehat\mu_a,
\qquad d=d_1-d_0.
\]
Then \(X_{a,i}=Z_{a,i}+d_a\).  Expanding every product in the diagonal-free correction gives the exact identity
\[
\begin{split}
\widehat U_e={}&U_e^{\mathrm{can}}(\widehat B)
+2\bar Z_{1,e}^{\top}\widehat Bd-2\bar Z_{0,e}^{\top}\widehat Bd
+d^{\top}\widehat Bd,\\
U_e^{\mathrm{can}}(\widehat B)={}&
\frac1{m(m-1)}\sum_{a=0}^1\sum_{i\ne j}Z_{a,i}^{\top}\widehat BZ_{a,j}
-\frac2{m^2}\sum_{i\in I_{1,e}}\sum_{j\in I_{0,e}}Z_{1,i}^{\top}\widehat BZ_{0,j}.
\end{split}\tag{1}
\]
Indeed, the two within-arm linear terms are \(2\bar Z_{a,e}^{\top}\widehat Bd_a\), while the cross-arm linear terms are \(-2\bar Z_{1,e}^{\top}\widehat Bd_0-2d_1^{\top}\widehat B\bar Z_{0,e}\).  The last term is exactly the quadratic Taylor correction by (14) in the pilot-control proof and the corrected Gram projection.

Apply Taylor's integral formula to the conditional transport functional about the opposite-fold pilot law.  Its empirical constant, first derivative, and quadratic derivative are respectively the displayed plug-in, the displayed score averages, and the last term of (1).  The deterministic projection complement is \(B^y_{J_y,n}+B^z_{J_z,n}\).  Consequently, after averaging the folds and grouping terms with exactly one and exactly two centered evaluation observations, one obtains the von Mises--Hoeffding decomposition
\[
\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n
=\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}+\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}
+B^y_{J_y,n}+B^z_{J_z,n}+R_{J_y,J_z,n}.                                  \tag{2}
\]
Here \(\mathbb L\) is the sum of the score projection and the linear terms in (1), \(\mathbb U\) is the average of \(U_e^{\mathrm{can}}(B)\), and \(R\) contains only Taylor, pilot, Gram, ridge, and kernel-replacement terms.  Thus (2) is obtained by product expansion and Taylor's identity, not by defining the desired rate.

Let \(F_{a,n}(y\mid z)=\int_0^yq_{a,n}(v,z)\,dv\).  Positivity makes each inverse distribution function \(c^{-1}\)-Lipschitz, so differentiating the inverse-cdf representation is valid.  If \(H_z(y)=\int_0^y\Delta_n(v,z)\,dv\) and \(u_z=\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n(\cdot,z)\), the Neumann equation and zero conditional mass give
\[
-\bar q_n(y,z)\partial_yu_z(y)=H_z(y),
\qquad
\int_0^1\Delta_nu_z\,dy=\int_0^1\frac{H_z(y)^2}{\bar q_n(y,z)}\,dy.       \tag{3}
\]
The centered oracle arm derivatives have squared two-sample \(L^2\)-norm comparable, by the density bounds and shared marginal, to the integral of (3) against \(R_n\).  Hence
\[
V_{L,n}=n\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n})\asymp\rho_n^2,
\qquad
\operatorname{sd}(\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n})\asymp\rho_n n^{-1/2}.          \tag{4}
\]
If \(\Delta_n=0\), the value and first derivative vanish, so \(\mathbb L=0\).

Abbreviate \(B=B_{J_y,J_z,n}\), \(\Gamma_a=\Gamma_{a,J_y,J_z,n}\), and \(\Gamma=\Gamma_0+\Gamma_1\).  Centering and independence make two canonical summands uncorrelated unless their ordered index pairs coincide or reverse.  Counting these pairs, then using independence of the folds and the factor \(1/2\) in their average, gives
\[
\begin{split}
\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb U)
={}&\frac{\operatorname{tr}\{(B\Gamma_0)^2\}+\operatorname{tr}\{(B\Gamma_1)^2\}}{m(m-1)}
+\frac{2\operatorname{tr}(B\Gamma_0B\Gamma_1)}{m^2}\\
={}&\frac{4\operatorname{tr}\{(\Gamma^{1/2}B\Gamma^{1/2})^2\}}{n^2}\{1+O(m^{-1})\}
=\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}^2}{n^2}\{1+o(1)\}.
\end{split}\tag{5}
\]
This calculation also shows why raw moments require \(B\), not \(H^{\mathrm{form}}\).

The spectrum proposition gives
\[
|B^y_{J_y,n}|\lesssim2^{-2(s_y+1)J_y},
\qquad |B^z_{J_z,n}|\lesssim2^{-2s_zJ_z}.                                \tag{6}
\]
The corrected pilot-control lemma gives
\[
R_{J_y,J_z,n}
=O_P\!\left\{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{2+a_y}+
\rho_n^{2a_y/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^2\right\}
+o_P(1)\left\{\frac{\rho_n}{\sqrt n}+\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}}n\right\}.   \tag{7}
\]
The theorem's premise makes (7) little-oh of the sum of the four displayed fluctuation and bias scales.  Substitution in (2), together with (4)--(6), proves the uniform expansion and both variance assertions.

At the declared balancing resolutions,
\[
\frac{A_{J_y^*,J_z^*,n}}n
\asymp\frac{2^{dJ_z^*/2}}n
\asymp2^{-2s_zJ_z^*}
\asymp2^{-2(s_y+1)J_y^*}
\asymp r_{n,d}(s_z).                                                       \tag{8}
\]
The \(L^2\) triangle inequality in (2), followed by (4)--(8), proves the root-mean-square statement and its absorption sub-regime.  The point estimator uses the pilot-built \(\widehat B\); the population covariance appears only in (5) and in the limit proxy, so covariance estimation is unnecessary for the point-estimator expansion.
