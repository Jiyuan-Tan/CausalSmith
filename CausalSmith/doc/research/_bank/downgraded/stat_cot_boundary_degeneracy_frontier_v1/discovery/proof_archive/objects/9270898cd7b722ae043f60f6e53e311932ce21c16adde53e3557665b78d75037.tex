Put \(p_{a,n}(y,z)=q_{a,n}(y,z)r_n(z)\).  Products preserve the declared anisotropic H\"older ball up to a constant depending only on \(C,L\).  At the pilot resolutions, the tensor wavelet approximation error in supremum norm is bounded by
\[
 C\{2^{-s_y\bar J_y}+2^{-s_z\bar J_z}\}\lesssim\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}. \tag{1}
\]
At any point only a fixed number of compactly supported wavelets at each terminal scale are nonzero.  Applying Bernstein's exponential-moment inequality coefficientwise and taking a union bound over \(O(2^{\bar J_y+d\bar J_z})\) coefficients gives
\[
 \max_{a,e}\|\widetilde p_{a,n}^{(-e)}-p_{a,n}\|_\infty
 +\max_e\|\widetilde r_n^{(-e)}-r_n\|_\infty
 =O_P(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n})                     \tag{2}
\]
uniformly over the model class.  The denominators \(r_n\ge c\), the ratio identity
\[
 \frac{\widetilde p}{\widetilde r}-\frac p r
 =\frac{\widetilde p-p}{\widetilde r}
   +p\frac{r-\widetilde r}{{r\widetilde r}},            \tag{3}
\]
and (2) prove the same rate for the raw conditional pilots.  Since the true densities lie in the strict interior of the clipping bounds \(c/2,2C\), (2) implies \(P(\cap_e\mathcal E_n^{(-e)})\to1\).  Clipping and normalization are locally Lipschitz on that event and enforce positivity and unit conditional mass on its complement, proving the first two conclusions.

Let \(A_z=P_{J_y}\{-\partial_y(\bar q_n\partial_y)\}P_{J_y}\) and let \(\widehat A_z\) be its pilot analogue.  On the zero-mean subspace, ellipticity and Poincar\'e give \(A_z\succeq c_PI\) uniformly.  The variational form gives \(\|\widehat A_z-A_z\|\lesssim\|\widehat{\bar q}-\bar q\|_\infty\).  The resolvent identity
\[
 (\widehat A_z+\lambda_nI)^{-1}-A_z^{-1}
 =(\widehat A_z+\lambda_nI)^{-1}
 \{A_z-\widehat A_z-\lambda_nI\}A_z^{-1}              \tag{4}
\]
therefore has operator norm \(O_P(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}+\lambda_n)\).  Integration against \(\widehat r_n\), using (2), proves the displayed Hessian bound.

It remains to track the population correction.  Parameterize a pair by its average density \(\bar q\) and discrepancy \(\Delta\).  Arm exchange sends \(\Delta\) to \(-\Delta\) without changing \(W_2^2\), so the functional is even in \(\Delta\).  The quantile implicit equation used in the cosine proof, twice polarized and integrated, shows on the density set \([c/2,2C]\) that the equality Hessian is Lipschitz in \(\bar q\), while its difference from the Hessian at \((\bar q,\Delta)\) is \(O(\|\Delta\|_\infty)\) as a bilinear form on the pilot error.  The anisotropic interpolation inequality implied by the declared H\"older ball and the zero outcome mass is
\[
 \|\Delta_n\|_\infty
 \lesssim \rho_n^{\,2/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}.                 \tag{5}
\]
Indeed, an amplitude \(A\) at a maximum persists on outcome and covariate rectangles of side lengths \(A^{1/s_y}\) and \(A^{1/s_z}\); the zero-mass outcome antiderivative then has squared \(L^2(dR_n;L^2_y)\) norm at least a constant times \(A^{2+3/s_y+d/s_z}\).  Its norm is comparable to \(\rho_n^2\) by ellipticity, proving (5).  Taylor's identity with integral remainder therefore gives
\[
 O_P\{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^3+
 \rho_n^{\,2/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}
 \varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^2\}.                       \tag{6}
\]
The first term is the equality third-order remainder; the second is the unequal-law Hessian mismatch.  Replacing the equality Hessian by its ridge pilot contributes \(O_P\{(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}+\lambda_n)\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^2\}\), which is absorbed by (5) because \(\lambda_n=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\).  Sample splitting makes the remaining centered operator terms \(o_P(1)\{\rho_n/\sqrt n+A/n\}\).  This proves the proposed local-quadratic remainder and its stated negligibility condition.

For the covariance assertion, the feature covariance is uniformly bounded and uniformly positive by the spectrum proposition, and \(\|\Phi(W)\|_2^2\lesssim D_{J_y,J_z}\).  The matrix exponential-moment argument yields
\[
 \max_e\|\widehat\Gamma^{(-e)}-\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 =O_P\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{D_{J_y,J_z}\log n}{m}}+
 \frac{D_{J_y,J_z}\log n}{m}\right)=o_P(1).            \tag{5}
\]
On the resulting fixed spectral interval, the matrix square-root map is Lipschitz.  Combining (4)--(5) in the definition of \(K\) proves \(\max_e\|\widehat K^{(-e)}-K\|_{\mathrm{op}}=o_P(1)\).
