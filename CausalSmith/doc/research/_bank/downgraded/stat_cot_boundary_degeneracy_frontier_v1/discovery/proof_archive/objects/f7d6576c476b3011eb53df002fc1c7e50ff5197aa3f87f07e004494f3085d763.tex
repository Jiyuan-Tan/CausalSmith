For \(d=0\), retain the notation \(F_a,Q_a\), and \(H=F_1-F_0\) from the frontier proof.  Its equations (1)--(3) use only the one-dimensional conditional-density bounds, so
\[
\theta_n=\|Q_1-Q_0\|_{L^2(0,1)}^2\asymp\rho_n^2\le\rho^2.                 \tag{1}
\]
Let \(D=Q_1-Q_0\), \(\widehat D=\widehat Q_{1,n}-\widehat Q_{0,n}\), and \(P_J=P^Q_J\).  Orthogonality gives the exact identity
\[
\widehat\theta^0_{J,n}-\theta_n
=2\langle P_JD,P_J(\widehat D-D)\rangle
+\|P_J(\widehat D-D)\|_2^2-\|(I-P_J)D\|_2^2.                             \tag{2}
\]

Represent an observation from arm \(a\) as \(Q_a(U_i)\), where the \(U_i\) are independent uniforms.  On \((k-1)/n<u\le k/n\), \(\widehat Q_{a,n}(u)=Q_a(U_{(k)})\).  Positivity makes \(Q_a\) \(c^{-1}\)-Lipschitz.  Direct integration of the beta density of \(U_{(k)}\) gives mean \(k/(n+1)\), variance \(k(n+1-k)/\{(n+1)^2(n+2)\}\), and fourth centered moment at most \(C/n^2\).  Because \(|k/(n+1)-u|\le2/n\), integration over all quantile cells yields
\[
E\|\widehat Q_{a,n}-Q_a\|_2^2\le Cn^{-1},
\qquad
E\|\widehat Q_{a,n}-Q_a\|_2^4\le Cn^{-2}.                                \tag{3}
\]
For the second inequality, use \((\int e^2)^2\le\int e^4\) on the unit interval.  Independence and the triangle inequality give the same orders for \(\widehat D-D\).

The density smoothness and positivity imply \(Q_a'=1/(q_{a,n}\circ Q_a)\).  Iterating the defining H\"older difference bound through composition and reciprocal operations shows that \(Q_a\in\mathcal C^{s_y+1}\) with a uniform norm bound.  Wavelet regularity and vanishing moments then give
\[
\|(I-P_J)D\|_2^2\le C2^{-2(s_y+1)J}.                                     \tag{4}
\]
Applying Cauchy--Schwarz and Minkowski's inequality to (2), followed by (1), (3), and (4), yields
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,0}}
\{E_P(\widehat\theta^0_{J_y^0,n}-\theta_n)^2\}^{1/2}
\le C\{\rho n^{-1/2}+n^{-1}+2^{-2(s_y+1)J_y^0}\}
\le C'\{\rho n^{-1/2}+n^{-1}\}.                                        \tag{5}
\]
The zero estimator has risk at most \(C\rho^2\) by (1).  Selecting the better explicit map proves the upper bound \(C\min\{\rho^2,\rho n^{-1/2}+n^{-1}\}\).

For the lower bound, compare \(t=0\) with \(t=c_0\min\{\rho,n^{-1/2}\}\) in the fixed-frequency family.  The mixture-check Hellinger calculation is bounded, and the cosine second variation gives endpoint separation
\[
c\min\{\rho^2,n^{-1}\}.                                                  \tag{6}
\]
For \(\rho\ge Kn^{-1/2}\), instead compare \(t=c_1\rho\) with \(t'=t+c_2n^{-1/2}\).  Small fixed constants keep both laws inside the radius-\(\rho\) class and their testing affinity bounded away from zero.  The twice-integrated cosine-path argument used in (11) of the frontier proof gives separation at least \(c_3\rho n^{-1/2}\).  Thresholding any estimator into a test, as in (10) of that proof, combines these separations into
\[
\inf_{\widetilde\theta_n}\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,0}}
\{E_P(\widetilde\theta_n-\theta_n)^2\}^{1/2}
\ge c\min\{\rho^2,\rho n^{-1/2}+n^{-1}\}.                               \tag{7}
\]
Together with (5), this proves the minimax assertion.

At the uniform null, apply the revalidated quantile-process lemma independently to the two arms.  The omitted lower-tail squared norm is bounded by \((U_{(1)}+n^{-1})^2\), and the upper-tail term by the analogous expression involving \(1-U_{(n)}\).  The beta moments used in (3) make both \(o_P(1)\).  Hence the full quantile processes have the same \(L^2\) limits as their truncated versions.  Independence and symmetry of the bridge imply
\[
Z_n:=\sqrt n(\widehat Q_{1,n}-\widehat Q_{0,n})
\Rightarrow\mathbb B_1-\mathbb B_0\stackrel d=\sqrt2\,\mathbb B
\quad\text{in }L^2(0,1).                                                  \tag{8}
\]
The projectors \(P^Q_{J_y^0}\) are contractions and converge strongly to the identity because \(J_y^0\to\infty\).  Strong convergence is uniform on compact subsets of \(L^2\), and tightness from (8) therefore gives \(\|(I-P^Q_{J_y^0})Z_n\|_2=o_P(1)\).  Continuity of the squared \(L^2\)-norm now yields
\[
n\widehat\theta^0_{J_y^0,n}
=\|P^Q_{J_y^0}Z_n\|_2^2
\Rightarrow2\int_0^1\mathbb B(t)^2dt.                                   \tag{9}
\]

The bridge covariance kernel is \(K(s,t)=s\wedge t-st\).  Differentiating its integral operator twice, with zero boundary values, shows that \(\sqrt2\sin(\pi kt)\) are orthonormal eigenfunctions with eigenvalues \((\pi^2k^2)^{-1}\).  Gaussian orthogonality makes their coordinates independent standard normals, so Parseval's identity gives
\[
2\int_0^1\mathbb B(t)^2dt
\stackrel d=\sum_{k=1}^\infty\frac{2}{\pi^2k^2}\xi_{1,k}^2.               \tag{10}
\]
Furthermore,
\[
2\sum_{k\le2^{J_y^0}}\frac1{\pi^2k^2}\longrightarrow
2\sum_{k=1}^\infty\frac1{\pi^2k^2}=\frac13
=E\left\{2\int_0^1\mathbb B(t)^2dt\right\}.
\]
Subtracting the corresponding finite sum divided by \(n\) from the estimator therefore centers the limit in (9)--(10).  Finally, both \(\rho^2=n^{-1}\) and \(\rho n^{-1/2}=n^{-1}\) give \(\rho=n^{-1/2}\), proving \(\rho_{Q,n}=\rho_{L,n}=n^{-1/2}\).
