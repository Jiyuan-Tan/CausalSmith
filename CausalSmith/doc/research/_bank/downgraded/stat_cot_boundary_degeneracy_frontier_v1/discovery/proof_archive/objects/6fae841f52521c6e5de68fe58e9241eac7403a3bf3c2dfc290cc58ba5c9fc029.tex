Write \(F_{a,z}(y)=\int_0^yq_{a,n}(v,z)dv\), let \(Q_{a,z}=F_{a,z}^{-1}\), and put
\[
H_z(y)=F_{1,z}(y)-F_{0,z}(y)=\int_0^y\Delta_n(v,z)dv.
\]
If \(u_z=\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n(\cdot,z)\), the defining Neumann equation for \(\mathcal G_{n,z}\) gives
\[
-\bar q_n(y,z)\partial_yu_z(y)=H_z(y).
\]
Indeed, this follows by integrating from \(0\) to \(y\) and using the Neumann condition at zero.  Since \(H_z(0)=H_z(1)=0\), integration by parts yields
\[
\int_0^1\Delta_n(y,z)u_z(y)dy
=\int_0^1\bar q_n(y,z)\{\partial_yu_z(y)\}^2dy
=\int_0^1\frac{H_z(y)^2}{\bar q_n(y,z)}dy. \tag{1}
\]
The inverses \(Q_{a,z}\) exist because the conditional-density bound gives \(\partial_yF_{a,z}=q_{a,n}\ge c>0\).  For each \(v\in(0,1)\), the mean-value theorem gives
\[
H_z\{Q_{0,z}(v)\}
=F_{1,z}\{Q_{0,z}(v)\}-F_{1,z}\{Q_{1,z}(v)\}
=q_{1,n}(\zeta_{v,z},z)\{Q_{0,z}(v)-Q_{1,z}(v)\}
\]
for a point \(\zeta_{v,z}\) between the two quantiles.  Changing variables \(v=F_{0,z}(y)\) and using \(c\le q_{a,n}\le C\) and \(c\le\bar q_n\le C\) in this identity and in (1), there are constants \(0<c_3<C_3<\infty\), depending only on \(c,C\), such that
\[
c_3\int_0^1\Delta_n(y,z)\{\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n(\cdot,z)\}(y)dy
\le W_2^2\{Q_{0,n}(\cdot\mid z),Q_{1,n}(\cdot\mid z)\}
\]
\[
\le C_3\int_0^1\Delta_n(y,z)\{\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n(\cdot,z)\}(y)dy. \tag{2}
\]
The shared-marginal assumption permits integration of (2) against the same \(R_n\) in both arms.  By the definition of \(\rho_n\) and the class restriction \(\rho_n\le\rho\),
\[
c_4\rho_n^2\le\theta_n\le C_4\rho_n^2\le C_4\rho^2. \tag{3}
\]
Thus the data-independent zero estimator has root-mean-square risk at most \(C_4\rho^2\), uniformly over the model class.

For the observable upper bound define
\[
p_n=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^3
+\rho^{\,2/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^2.
\]
The Hoeffding decomposition of the explicit estimator in \(\text{def:anisotropic-estimator}\), which is the decomposition used in \(\text{thm:uniform-second-order-expansion}\), is
\[
\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n
=\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}+\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}
+B^y_{J_y,n}+B^z_{J_z,n}+R_{J_y,J_z,n}. \tag{4}
\]
We next record the uniform second-moment version of the remainder control.  The number of pilot coefficients is, by \(\text{def:pilot-tuning-and-studentizer}\),
\[
\overline D_n\asymp2^{\bar J_y+d\bar J_z}
\asymp(n/\log n)^{1/(2\beta+1)}.
\]
For every individual bounded compactly supported wavelet coefficient, apply \(e^v\le1+v+v^2/\{2(1-|v|/3)\}\) to the centered coefficient and then apply exponential Markov bounds to its independent sample sum.  Taking a union bound over \(O(\overline D_n)\) pilot coefficients, applying the boundary-wavelet reconstruction inequality, and adding the anisotropic projection bias gives, for fixed \(x\in[0,1]\),
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}
P\!\left(\max_e\left[\|\widehat r_n^{(-e)}-r_n\|_\infty
+\max_a\|\widehat q_{a,n}^{(-e)}-q_{a,n}\|_\infty\right]
>C\{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}+x\}\right)
\le C\exp\{-cnx^2/\overline D_n\}. \tag{5}
\]
Here the ratio and normalization maps are Lipschitz on the clipping-inactive event because the density bounds are positive, while clipping makes the same quantities uniformly bounded on its complement.  Integrating (5) in \(x\) therefore gives fixed-moment versions of the uniform pilot bounds in \(\text{lem:observable-pilot-control}\).  Applying the deterministic third-order Taylor, interpolation, and resolvent inequalities stated there, now to those moments, yields
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}\|R_{J_y,J_z,n}\|_{L^2(P)}
\le Cp_n+o\!\left(\frac\rho{\sqrt n}+\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}}n
+2^{-2(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-2s_zJ_z}\right). \tag{6}
\]
The two Hoeffding projections are orthogonal in \(L^2(P)\).  Their variance formulas in \(\text{thm:uniform-second-order-expansion}\), together with \(\rho_n\le\rho\), give
\[
\|\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}\|_2\le C\rho n^{-1/2},
\qquad
\|\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}\|_2\le C A_{J_y,J_z,n}/n. \tag{7}
\]
At the resolutions in \(\text{def:anisotropic-tuning}\), the spectral and approximation bounds in \(\text{prop:anisotropic-spectrum-bias}\) imply
\[
A_{J_y^*,J_z^*,n}/n\asymp r_{n,d}(s_z),
\qquad
|B^y_{J_y^*,n}|+|B^z_{J_z^*,n}|\le Cr_{n,d}(s_z). \tag{8}
\]
Minkowski's inequality applied to (4), followed by (6)--(8), proves
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}
\{E_P(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y^*,J_z^*,n}-\theta_n)^2\}^{1/2}
\le C\{\rho n^{-1/2}+r_{n,d}(s_z)+p_n\}. \tag{9}
\]
Because \(\rho\) and all class constants are fixed features of the declared minimax problem, selecting between the zero map and this explicit sample map according to the smaller displayed upper guarantee is itself one data-driven estimator for that problem.  Equations (3) and (9) establish the stated upper bound.  In particular, \(\rho^2\lesssim r_{n,d}(s_z)\) precisely in the zero zone \(\rho\lesssim\rho_{Q,n}\).

For the converse use the family in \(\text{def:rademacher-family}\).  By \(\text{lem:explicit-mixture-checks}\), the small fixed constant \(c_0\) may be chosen so that every component belongs to the same model class and
\[
\chi^2(\overline{\mathbb P}_n,\mathbb P_n^0)\le\eta<1,
\qquad
\theta_n(\omega)-\theta_n(0)\ge
\delta_n:=c_5\min\{\rho^2,r_{n,d}(s_z)\}. \tag{10}
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz applied to the likelihood ratio gives
\(\|P-Q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le\{\chi^2(P,Q)\}^{1/2}/2\), so the testing affinity of the mixture and null is bounded away from zero.  For any estimator, thresholding it at \(\delta_n/2\) defines a test; on either corresponding testing error its absolute estimation error is at least \(\delta_n/2\).  Averaging the alternative risk over the Rademacher mixture and using the testing affinity therefore gives
\[
\inf_{\widetilde\theta_n}\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}
\{E_P(\widetilde\theta_n-\theta_n)^2\}^{1/2}
\ge c_6\min\{\rho^2,r_{n,d}(s_z)\}. \tag{11}
\]

It remains to obtain the regular local direction.  Extend \(q_{a,t}^{0}\) constantly in \(z\).  When \(\rho\ge K n^{-1/2}\), compare \(t=c_7\rho\) and \(t'=t+c_8n^{-1/2}\).  The constants \(c_7,c_8\) can be fixed sufficiently small that positivity and the H\"older bounds hold and both inverse-elliptic radii are at most \(\rho\).  The product Hellinger bound in \(\text{lem:explicit-mixture-checks}\) leaves their testing affinity bounded away from zero.  Let \(\Theta(t)\) denote the endpoint on this symmetric cosine path.  Its distribution functions satisfy
\[
F_{a,t}(y)=y+(2a-1)t\sin(2\pi y)/(4\pi),
\qquad \partial_yF_{a,t}=q_{a,t}^{0}\ge c.
\]
Implicit differentiation is therefore legitimate and gives
\[
\partial_tQ_{a,t}(v)
=-\frac{(2a-1)\sin\{2\pi Q_{a,t}(v)\}}
{4\pi q_{a,t}^{0}\{Q_{a,t}(v)\}}.
\]
The quotient and its first derivative in \(t\) are uniformly bounded on a fixed neighborhood of zero, by the positive conditional-density bound.  Hence \(\Theta''\) is continuous there.  The exact calculation in \(\text{lem:cosine-second-variation}\) gives \(\Theta'(0)=0\) and \(\Theta''(0)=1/(4\pi^2)\).  After shrinking the fixed neighborhood, \(\Theta''(v)\ge1/(8\pi^2)\), and two integrations give
\[
|\Theta(t')-\Theta(t)|\ge c_9t|t'-t|\ge c_{10}\rho n^{-1/2}. \tag{12}
\]
The same two-point testing reduction used for (11) gives a lower bound \(c_{11}\rho n^{-1/2}\).  If instead \(\rho<K n^{-1/2}\), then \(r_{n,d}(s_z)=n^{-b}\ge n^{-1}\gtrsim\rho^2\), because \(b=4s_z/(4s_z+d)<1\), and (11) already has order \(\rho^2\).  Splitting between these cases and using the elementary identity, up to constants,
\[
\max\!\left[\min\{\rho^2,r_{n,d}(s_z)\},
\rho n^{-1/2}\mathbf 1\{\rho\ge K n^{-1/2}\}\right]
\asymp\min\{\rho^2,\rho n^{-1/2}+r_{n,d}(s_z)\}, \tag{13}
\]
proves the asserted lower frontier.

When \(p_n=O\{\rho n^{-1/2}+r_{n,d}(s_z)\}\), (9) and (13) match.  Otherwise, (9) and (13) give exactly the stated honest two-sided bracket.  Finally,
\[
\rho_{Q,n}^2=r_{n,d}(s_z)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\rho_{Q,n}=n^{-2s_z/(4s_z+d)},
\]
and
\[
\rho_{L,n}n^{-1/2}=r_{n,d}(s_z)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\rho_{L,n}=n^{(d-4s_z)/\{2(4s_z+d)\}}.
\]
Thus \(\rho_{L,n}\to0\) exactly when \(s_z>d/4\), yielding the displayed zero, quadratic, mixed, and regular zones.  When \(s_z\le d/4\), \(r_{n,d}(s_z)\gtrsim n^{-1/2}\); at conditional equality the first projection is identically zero, while (11) retains quadratic difficulty at least at the root-\(n\) scale.  Hence no uniformly positive first-order regular variance can govern a root-\(n\) procedure over the whole class, as claimed.
