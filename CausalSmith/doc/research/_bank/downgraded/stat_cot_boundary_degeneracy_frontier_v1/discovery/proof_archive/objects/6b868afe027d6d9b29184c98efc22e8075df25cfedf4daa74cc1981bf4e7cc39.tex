There is a population-calibrated partial result for \(d\ge1\).  Abbreviate the deterministic remainder envelope by
\[
E_{R,n}=C_B\left[
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}
\{2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-s_zJ_z}\}
+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{2+a_y}
+\rho^{2a_y/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^2
\right].
\]
Uniformity of the little-oh term in
\(\mathrm{thm{:}uniform\text{-}second\text{-}order\text{-}expansion}\)
permits a deterministic \(r_n^\circ\) with
\[
r_n^\circ=o\!\left\{\rho n^{-1/2}+A_{J_y,J_z,n}/n\right\}
\]
that bounds its \(L^2\) norm over any triangular collection under consideration.  Under the score-approximation premises of
\(\mathrm{thm{:}mixed\text{-}spectral\text{-}transition}\), define
\[
G_n^\circ=\sum_{e=1}^2\sum_k\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}
\{(\xi_{e,k}+\eta_{k,n})^2-(1+\eta_{k,n}^2)\},
\]
and let \(q_{n,1-\alpha/2}^\circ\) be the
\((1-\alpha/2)\)-quantile of \(|G_n^\circ|\).  Then, for a sufficiently large fixed \(C_\alpha\), the infeasible interval
\[
\mathcal I_n^\circ=
\left[
\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}
-\frac{q_{n,1-\alpha/2}^\circ}{n}-C_\alpha(E_{R,n}+r_n^\circ),
\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}
+\frac{q_{n,1-\alpha/2}^\circ}{n}+C_\alpha(E_{R,n}+r_n^\circ)
\right]
\]
has asymptotic coverage at least \(1-\alpha\), uniformly over that triangular collection.  Indeed, with
\(B_n=\sqrt{nV_{L,n}}\) and \(S_n=A_{J_y,J_z,n}+B_n\), the Gaussian comparison variance is
\[
\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}^2+B_n^2}{S_n^2}\in[1/2,1].
\]
This supplies uniform anti-concentration for the population proxy.  Bounded-Lipschitz smoothing gives probability at least
\(1-\alpha/2-o(1)\) for its quantile event, while Markov's inequality and the exact expansion control the remainder failure by at most \(\alpha/2\).  Its half-width is
\[
O\!\left\{
E_{R,n}+r_n^\circ+A_{J_y,J_z,n}/n+\sqrt{V_{L,n}/n}
\right\}
=O\!\left\{E_{R,n}+A_{J_y,J_z,n}/n+\rho n^{-1/2}\right\}.
\]
At the corrected resolutions,
\[
A_{J_y^*,J_z^*,n}/n
\asymp n^{-2s_z/(2s_z+d)}
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{d/(2s_z+d)},
\]
so this is the requested positive-dimensional width, but it is not observable.  The frozen observable spectral control requires
\(D_{J_y,J_z}\log n/m=o(1)\), which need not hold at the corrected resolutions.  For example, at
\(d=1\), \(s_y=1/10\), and \(s_z=1\), one has
\(e=1/13\),
\[
D_{J_y^*,J_z^*}
=\{n\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\}^{14/11+o(1)},
\qquad
\frac{D_{J_y^*,J_z^*}\log n}{m}
=n^{25/143+o(1)}(\log n)^{1+14/143+o(1)}\to\infty.
\]
Even where this dimension condition holds, the frozen core supplies neither a conditional common-multiplier approximation preserving the covariance of the linear and quadratic summands nor uniform control of the noncentral spectral coordinates.  The absence of a uniform reverse bound
\(V_{L,n}\gtrsim\rho_n^2\) also rules out ordinary relative studentization as a bridge across equality.  For \(d=0\), the exact uniform-density null law does not itself give an observable quantile coupling uniform over all equal densities, local alternatives, and fixed unequal laws.
