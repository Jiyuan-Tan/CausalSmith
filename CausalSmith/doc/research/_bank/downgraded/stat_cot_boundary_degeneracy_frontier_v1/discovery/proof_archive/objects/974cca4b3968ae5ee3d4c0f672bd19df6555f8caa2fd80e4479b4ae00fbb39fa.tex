Put \(M_n=2^{dJ_z}\), abbreviate \(K=K_{J_y,J_z,n}\), \(A=A_{J_y,J_z,n}\), \(V=V_{L,n}\), and \(S=S_n=A+\sqrt{nV}\).  By \(d\ge1\), \(J_z\to\infty\), and \(\mathrm{prop{:}anisotropic\text{-}spectrum\text{-}bias}\),
\[
A\asymp M_n^{1/2}\longrightarrow\infty,
\qquad
\frac{\max_k|\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}|}{A}\lesssim M_n^{-1/2}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{24}
\]
In particular, \(S\to\infty\).  The expansion in \(\mathrm{thm{:}uniform\text{-}second\text{-}order\text{-}expansion}\), multiplied by \(n\), and the assumed deterministic-bias bound give
\[
n\{\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n-B^y_{J_y,n}-B^z_{J_z,n}\}
=n\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}+n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}+o_P(S).
\tag{25}
\]
Indeed, the cited expansion gives \(n\rho_n/\sqrt n\asymp\sqrt{nV}\le S\), its quadratic term has scale \(A\le S\), and the two displayed truncation terms, after multiplication by \(n\), are \(O(S)\).  All divisions by \(S\) below are therefore legitimate.

We next identify the local first projection, including the common-marginal component.  Define
\[
F_{a,n}(y\mid z)=\int_0^yq_{a,n}(u,z)\,du,
\quad
T_{n,z}=F_{1,n}^{-1}(\cdot\mid z)\circ F_{0,n}(\cdot\mid z),
\quad
H_n(y,z)=\int_0^y\Delta_n(u,z)\,du.
\]
The zero conditional mass of \(\Delta_n\) gives \(H_n(0,z)=H_n(1,z)=0\).  If \(g=T_{n,z}(y)-y\), the transport identity gives
\[
\int_y^{y+g}q_{1,n}(u,z)\,du=-H_n(y,z).
\]
The lower density bound in \(\mathrm{ass{:}conditional\text{-}density\text{-}bounds}\) first implies \(|g|\le c^{-1}|H_n(y,z)|\).  Put \(a_y=\min\{s_y,1\}\).  The outcome H\"older condition in \(\mathrm{ass{:}anisotropic\text{-}conditional\text{-}smoothness}\) implies
\[
\left|\int_y^{y+g}\{q_{1,n}(u,z)-q_{1,n}(y,z)\}\,du\right|
\le C_0|g|^{1+a_y}.
\]
Because \(|H_n(y,z)|\le\|\Delta_n\|_\infty\) and \(\|\Delta_n\|_\infty\to0\), solving the preceding display for \(g\), and then using \(|q_{1,n}-\bar q_n|\le\|\Delta_n\|_\infty/2\) together with the lower density bound, yields uniformly in \((y,z)\)
\[
T_{n,z}(y)-y
=-\frac{H_n(y,z)}{\bar q_n(y,z)}
+O\!\left(\|\Delta_n\|_\infty^{a_y}|H_n(y,z)|\right).
\tag{26}
\]
This is the required H\"older inverse-map remainder; when \(s_y<1\), it cannot be replaced by a remainder linear in \(\|\Delta_n\|_\infty\).

Let \(u_n(\cdot,z)=\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n(\cdot,z)\).  Integrating the defining differential equation once and using the Neumann boundary condition gives \(\partial_yu_n=-H_n/\bar q_n\).  Integration by parts, with the two boundary values of \(H_n\) equal to zero, gives the exact energy identity
\[
\rho_n^2
=\int_{\mathcal Z_d}\int_0^1\Delta_nu_n\,dy\,dR_n
=\int_{\mathcal Z_d}\int_0^1\frac{H_n(y,z)^2}{\bar q_n(y,z)}\,dy\,dR_n(z).
\tag{27}
\]
Thus the conditional density bounds imply \(\|H_n\|_{L^2(dy\,dR_n)}\asymp\rho_n\).

For completeness, let \(f_n(z)=W_2^2(Q_{0,n}(\cdot\mid z),Q_{1,n}(\cdot\mid z))\), and let \(\ell_{a,n}\) denote the exact one-dimensional Kantorovich derivative in arm \(a\).  Under the shared-marginal tangent restriction in \(\mathrm{ass{:}shared\text{-}covariate\text{-}marginal}\), the exact centered arm score is
\[
s_{a,n}(y,z)
=\frac12\{f_n(z)-\theta_n\}
+\ell_{a,n}(y,z)-\int_0^1\ell_{a,n}(u,z)q_{a,n}(u,z)\,du.
\tag{28}
\]
The first term in (28) is the common-marginal centering term and is not discarded.  From \(|T_{n,z}(y)-y|\le c^{-1}|H_n(y,z)|\), (27), and \(\sup_{y,z}|H_n(y,z)|\le\|\Delta_n\|_\infty\),
\[
\|f_n-\theta_n\|_{L^2(R_n)}
\le2\|f_n\|_{L^2(R_n)}
\lesssim\rho_n\|\Delta_n\|_\infty
\le\rho_n\|\Delta_n\|_\infty^{a_y}
\tag{29}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Thus this shared-marginal score is of the same or smaller order than the corrected inverse-map remainder.

For the conditional potentials, the one-dimensional optimality identities are
\[
\partial_y\ell_{0,n}(y,z)=2\{y-T_{n,z}(y)\},
\qquad
\partial_y\ell_{1,n}(y,z)=2\{y-T_{n,z}^{-1}(y)\}.
\]
Apply (26) once as written and once with the arms interchanged.  Since \(\partial_yu_n=-H_n/\bar q_n\), and since subtracting the conditional mean fixes the irrelevant additive constants, the Poincar\'e inequality on \([0,1]\), (27), and the uniform density bounds imply
\[
\max_{a\in\{0,1\}}
\left\|\ell_{a,n}-\int\ell_{a,n}q_{a,n}
-(2a-1)2\left(u_n-\int u_nq_{a,n}\right)\right\|_{L^2(P_{a,n})}
\lesssim \rho_n\|\Delta_n\|_\infty^{a_y}.
\tag{30}
\]

We now use the actual weighted covariate projector.  Let \(P_z^R\) be the \(L^2(R_n)\)-orthogonal projector onto the retained covariate wavelets through \(J_z\), and let \(P_z^0\) be the coefficient truncation in \(L^2(dz)\).  The identity \(\partial_yu_n=-H_n/\bar q_n\), the fixed density bounds, and anisotropic H\"older multiplication show that \(u_n\) has outcome regularity \(s_y+1\) and covariate regularity \(s_z\), uniformly in the declared class.  Hence, by best approximation, the bounds \(c\le r_n\le C\) from \(\mathrm{ass{:}covariate\text{-}density\text{-}bounds}\), and the wavelet coefficient estimates supplied by \(\mathrm{ass{:}anisotropic\text{-}conditional\text{-}smoothness}\) and \(\mathrm{ass{:}wavelet\text{-}regularity}\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|(I-P_{J_y})u_n\|_{L^2(dy\,dR_n)}&\lesssim2^{-(s_y+1)J_y},\\
\|(I-P_z^R)P_{J_y}u_n\|_{L^2(dy\,dR_n)}
&\le \|P_{J_y}u_n-P_z^0P_{J_y}u_n\|_{L^2(dy\,dR_n)}\\
&\le C^{1/2}\|P_{J_y}u_n-P_z^0P_{J_y}u_n\|_{L^2(dy\,dz)}
\lesssim2^{-s_zJ_z}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{31}
\]
This is precisely the weighted stability step underlying the covariate complement bound in \(\mathrm{prop{:}anisotropic\text{-}spectrum\text{-}bias}\); no unweighted projector is substituted for \(P_z^R\).

Write
\[
\mu_{a,J_y,J_z,n}=E_{P_{a,n}}\Phi_{J_y,J_z}(W_{a,1,n}),
\qquad
s^{(2)}_{a,J_y,J_z,n}(w)
=(2a-1)2\delta_{J_y,J_z,n}^{\top}B_{J_y,J_z,n}
\{\Phi_{J_y,J_z}(w)-\mu_{a,J_y,J_z,n}\}.
\]
Let \(\Pi=\Pi_{J_y,J_z}=P_{J_y}P_z^R\); this equality follows from the tensor feature construction and the Gram definition in \(\mathrm{def{:}anisotropic\text{-}projection}\).  Write \(\mathcal G_n\) for the fiberwise operator \((\mathcal G_nh)(y,z)=\{\mathcal G_{n,z}h(\cdot,z)\}(y)\).  If \(a=G_{R,J_y,J_z,n}^{-1}\delta_{J_y,J_z,n}\) is the coefficient vector of \(\Pi\Delta_n\), then
\[
\Pi\mathcal G_n\Pi\Delta_n
=\Phi_{J_y,J_z}^{\top}G_{R,J_y,J_z,n}^{-1}
H^{\mathrm{form}}_{J_y,J_z,n}a
=\Phi_{J_y,J_z}^{\top}B_{J_y,J_z,n}\delta_{J_y,J_z,n}.
\tag{32}
\]
Thus the Gram conjugations in \(\mathrm{def{:}whitened\text{-}spectral\text{-}array}\) make \(s^{(2)}_{a,J_y,J_z,n}\) exactly the raw-feature-coordinate derivative of the projected quadratic form.  Decomposing
\[
u_n-\Pi\mathcal G_n\Pi\Delta_n
=(I-\Pi)u_n+\Pi\mathcal G_n(I-\Pi)\Delta_n,
\]
the first term is controlled by (31), while the inverse-elliptic spectral comparison and weighted complement stability in \(\mathrm{prop{:}anisotropic\text{-}spectrum\text{-}bias}\) give the same two bounds for the second term.  Equations (29)--(32) and the conditional-potential estimate (30) therefore give
\[
\max_{a\in\{0,1\}}
\|s_{a,n}-s^{(2)}_{a,J_y,J_z,n}\|_{L^2(P_{a,n})}
\lesssim
\rho_n\|\Delta_n\|_\infty^{a_y}
+2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-s_zJ_z}.
\tag{33}
\]
The conditional centering in (28) and in \(s^{(2)}_{a,J_y,J_z,n}\) can only multiply these \(L^2\) bounds by a fixed constant.

For fold \(e\), put
\[
\bar\Phi_{a,e}=m^{-1}\sum_{i\in I_{a,e}}\Phi_{J_y,J_z}(W_{a,i,n}),
\qquad
Z_{e,n}=\Gamma_{J_y,J_z,n}^{-1/2}\sqrt m
\{\bar\Phi_{1,e}-\bar\Phi_{0,e}-\delta_{J_y,J_z,n}\}.
\tag{34}
\]
The covariance bounds in \(\mathrm{prop{:}anisotropic\text{-}spectrum\text{-}bias}\) justify the inverse square root.  The two vectors \(Z_{1,n},Z_{2,n}\) are independent, centered, and have identity covariance by \(\mathrm{ass{:}two\text{-}arm\text{-}sampling}\), the deterministic split, and the definition of \(\Gamma_{J_y,J_z,n}\).  Define
\[
G_n=2\sum_{e=1}^2\sqrt m\,
\delta_{J_y,J_z,n}^{\top}B_{J_y,J_z,n}
\Gamma_{J_y,J_z,n}^{1/2}Z_{e,n}.
\tag{35}
\]
An empirical average of a centered score error with \(L^2\)-norm \(r_n\), after multiplication by \(n\), has \(L^2\)-norm at most a fixed multiple of \(\sqrt n r_n\).  Hence (33), the definition of the oracle first Hoeffding projection, and the split identity \(n/2=m\) imply
\[
\|n\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}-G_n\|_2
\lesssim\sqrt n\{\rho_n\|\Delta_n\|_\infty^{a_y}
+2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-s_zJ_z}\}=o(S).
\tag{36}
\]
Here the first term is \(o(S)\) because \(\rho_n\sqrt n\asymp\sqrt{nV}\le S\) and \(\|\Delta_n\|_\infty^{a_y}\to0\).  For \(x_n=2^{-J_y}\), the bias premise gives
\[
\frac{n x_n^{2s_y+2}}{S^2}
=\frac{n x_n^{2s_y+1}}{S}\frac{x_n}{S}=o(1),
\]
because the first factor is bounded and \(x_n/S\to0\).  Similarly,
\[
\frac{n2^{-2s_zJ_z}}{S^2}=O(S^{-1})=o(1).
\]
This proves every assertion in (36), in particular the corrected requirement
\(\rho_n\sqrt n\,\|\Delta_n\|_\infty^{a_y}=o(S)\).

Let \((\nu_k,v_k)\) abbreviate the eigenpairs of \(K\), and put \(Z_{e,k}=v_k^{\top}Z_{e,n}\).  Since
\(\eta_{k,n}=v_k^{\top}\Gamma_{J_y,J_z,n}^{-1/2}\sqrt m\,\delta_{J_y,J_z,n}\), diagonalizing (33) gives the exact identities
\[
G_n=2\sum_{e=1}^2\sum_k\nu_k\eta_{k,n}Z_{e,k},
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(G_n)=8\sum_k\nu_k^2\eta_{k,n}^2.
\tag{37}
\]
Since \(n\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n})=V\), (36), (37), and the boundedness of both variances on the scale \(S^2\) yield
\[
8\sum_k\nu_k^2\eta_{k,n}^2=nV+o(S^2).
\tag{38}
\]

Put \(Q_n=n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}\).  The assumptions of \(\mathrm{lem{:}growing\text{-}kernel\text{-}contractions}\) hold: \(M_n\le D_{J_y,J_z}=o(n^2)\), the projected score summands in (35) are centered and uniformly bounded by the one-dimensional Green bound and the fixed density bounds, and (37)--(38) give \(EG_n^2=O(S^2)\).  That lemma represents \(G_n+Q_n\) as the sum of martingale differences \(\mathcal D_{n,r}\), proves conditional Lindeberg, and proves convergence of their predictable quadratic variation in \(L^2/S^2\).  Canonical-kernel orthogonality, its variance identity, and (38) give
\[
E(G_n+Q_n)^2=A^2+nV+o(S^2).
\tag{39}
\]
We record the short characteristic-function argument rather than invoke an undeclared limit theorem.  For fixed \(t\), condition successively on the ordered observations and use
\[
|e^{ix}-1-ix+x^2/2|\le Cx^2\min\{|x|,1\}.
\]
The conditional Lindeberg conclusion makes the sum of the Taylor remainders tend to zero.  The predictable-variance conclusion replaces the sum of conditional second moments by (37), because the exponential is Lipschitz and bounded.  Iteration of the conditional expectations therefore gives
\[
E\exp\{it(G_n+Q_n)/S\}
-\exp\!\left[-\frac{t^2}{2S^2}\{A^2+nV+o(S^2)\}\right]\longrightarrow0.
\tag{40}
\]

Now define the stated finite Gaussian-quadratic proxy
\[
\mathcal Q_n^G=\sum_{e=1}^2\sum_k\nu_k
\{(\xi_{e,k}+\eta_{k,n})^2-(1+\eta_{k,n}^2)\}.
\]
For \(c_{e,k}=\nu_k/S\) and \(l_{e,k}=2\nu_k\eta_{k,n}/S\), direct one-dimensional Gaussian integration, which is valid because \(1-2itc_{e,k}\ne0\) for real \(t\), gives
\[
\log E e^{it\mathcal Q_n^G/S}
=\sum_{e,k}\left[-itc_{e,k}-\frac12\log(1-2itc_{e,k})
-\frac{t^2l_{e,k}^2}{2(1-2itc_{e,k})}\right].
\tag{41}
\]
By (24) and (38),
\[
\max_k\frac{|\nu_k|}{S}\le\frac{\max_k|\nu_k|}{A}\to0,
\qquad
\sum_{e,k}l_{e,k}^2=\frac{nV+o(S^2)}{S^2},
\qquad
\sum_{e,k}c_{e,k}^2=\frac{A^2}{2S^2}.
\tag{42}
\]
Expanding (41), the total centered-quadratic remainder is at most
\(C_t\max_k|\nu_k|S^{-1}\sum_{e,k}c_{e,k}^2=o(1)\), and the total noncentral remainder is at most
\(C_t\max_k|\nu_k|S^{-1}\sum_{e,k}l_{e,k}^2=o(1)\).  Consequently
\[
\log E e^{it\mathcal Q_n^G/S}
=-\frac{t^2}{2S^2}(A^2+nV)+o(1).
\tag{43}
\]

Set \(\sigma_n^2=(A^2+nV)/S^2\).  Since \(A,\sqrt{nV}\ge0\),
\[
\frac12\le\sigma_n^2\le1.
\]
Every subsequence has a further subsequence on which \(\sigma_n^2\) converges.  Along that further subsequence, (40) and (43) show that both \((G_n+Q_n)/S\) and \(\mathcal Q_n^G/S\) converge to the same centered normal law.  Their second moments are uniformly bounded by (39), (42), and (43), so they are tight.  The subsequence criterion for the metric \(d_{\mathrm{BL}}\) therefore yields
\[
d_{\mathrm{BL}}\{\mathcal L((G_n+Q_n)/S),\mathcal L(\mathcal Q_n^G/S)\}\to0
\]
and
\[
d_{\mathrm{BL}}\{\mathcal L((G_n+Q_n)/S),\mathcal N(0,\sigma_n^2)\}\to0.
\]
Equations (25) and (36), followed by the triangle inequality for \(d_{\mathrm{BL}}\), replace \(G_n+Q_n\) by the normalized estimator and prove the first two conclusions.

At conditional equality, \(\Delta_n=0\), hence \(\delta_{J_y,J_z,n}=0\), every \(\eta_{k,n}=0\), \(V=0\), and \(\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}=0\).  Taking \(S=A\) in (41)--(43) gives
\[
\frac{n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}}{A_{J_y,J_z,n}}\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1).
\]
The decisive ratio is (24), so a fixed or subsequential non-Gaussian chaos cannot survive in the growing-covariate-resolution branch.

Finally, consider a fixed unequal law.  The exact centered scores (28) are bounded because the support is compact, the cost is bounded, and the densities are bounded away from zero.  The same conditional characteristic-function expansion applied to their independent sums gives
\[
\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}/\sqrt{V/n}\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1).
\]
The hypothesis \(\sqrt{nV}/A\to\infty\), together with the quadratic variance in the uniform expansion, gives
\(\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}=o_P(\sqrt{V/n})\).  The assumed corrected bias and remainder condition removes the remaining expansion terms, and \(\widehat V_{L,n}/V_{L,n}\to_P1\) permits studentization.  This proves the final displayed central limit theorem.
