For each fold \(e\), condition on the sigma-field generated by the opposite fold.  By \(\mathrm{ass{:}two\text{-}arm\text{-}sampling}\), all evaluation-fold observations are then independent of the fitted objects.  Let \(\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}\) be the equal-weight average of the exact population first Hoeffding projections and let \(\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}\) be the corresponding population-kernel canonical diagonal-free projection.  The definition of \(\mathbb R_{J_y,J_z,n}\) gives the algebraic identity
\[
\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n
=\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}+\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}+\mathbb R_{J_y,J_z,n}.                 \tag{1}
\]
Thus the statistical target in (1) is the identified endpoint \(\theta_n\), not a projected energy.

Write \(\zeta_e\) for the actual pilot-density error on fold \(e\), \(\widehat a_e^0\) for the pilot inverse-elliptic bilinear form, and \(\widehat\Pi_e\) for its declared tensor projection.  The conditional von Mises identity in \(\mathrm{lem{:}observable\text{-}pilot\text{-}control}\) states that the conditional-mean part of the remainder is
\[
-\left\{\widehat a_e^0(\zeta_e,\zeta_e)
-\widehat a_e^0(\widehat\Pi_e\zeta_e,\widehat\Pi_e\zeta_e)\right\}
+O\!\left(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{2+a_y}
+\|\Delta_n\|_\infty^{a_y}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{2}\right).             \tag{2}
\]
It is therefore the negative full-minus-projected energy of the pilot error and is zero for an oracle pilot.  In particular, the true-discrepancy gaps \(B^y_{J_y,n}\) and \(B^z_{J_z,n}\) cannot enter (1): for an oracle pilot the plug-in equals \(\theta_n\), both centered first-order averages have mean zero, and the diagonal-free canonical correction has mean zero.  The quantitative conclusion of \(\mathrm{lem{:}observable\text{-}pilot\text{-}control}\) is, uniformly over \(\mathcal M_{n,d}(s_y,s_z,\rho)\),
\[
\begin{split}
\|\mathbb R_{J_y,J_z,n}\|_2
\lesssim{}&\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}
\{2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-s_zJ_z}\}
+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{2+a_y}\\
&+\rho_n^{2a_y/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{2}
+o\!\left\{\frac{\rho_n}{\sqrt n}+\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}}n\right\}.              \tag{3}
\end{split}
\]

We next establish the corrected assertion about the linear projection.  For fixed \(z\), put
\[
F_{a,z}(y)=\int_0^yq_{a,n}(v,z)\,dv,
\qquad
H_z(y)=F_{1,z}(y)-F_{0,z}(y)=\int_0^y\Delta_n(v,z)\,dv,
\]
and let \(u_z=\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n(\cdot,z)\).  The zero conditional mass of \(\Delta_n\), the Neumann boundary conditions, and integration of the defining elliptic equation yield
\[
-\bar q_n(y,z)u_z'(y)=H_z(y),
\qquad
e_n(z):=\int_0^1\Delta_n(y,z)u_z(y)\,dy
=\int_0^1\frac{H_z(y)^2}{\bar q_n(y,z)}\,dy,                                    \tag{4}
\]
where the second identity follows by integration by parts; its boundary term is zero because \(H_z(0)=H_z(1)=0\).  Consequently \(\rho_n^2=\int e_n(z)\,dR_n(z)\).

Let \(f_n(z)=W_2^2\{Q_{0,n}(\cdot\mid z),Q_{1,n}(\cdot\mid z)\}\), and let \(\ell_{a,n}(\cdot,z)\) be the exact conditional Kantorovich derivative, normalized to have conditional mean zero under \(q_{a,n}(\cdot,z)\).  If \(T_{0,z}=F_{1,z}^{-1}\circ F_{0,z}\) and \(T_{1,z}=F_{0,z}^{-1}\circ F_{1,z}\), direct differentiation of the one-dimensional transport dual gives
\[
|\partial_y\ell_{a,n}(y,z)|=2|y-T_{a,z}(y)|.                                    \tag{5}
\]
The inverse maps exist because \(q_{a,n}\ge c>0\) by \(\mathrm{ass{:}conditional\text{-}density\text{-}bounds}\).  The mean-value theorem, (4), and \(c\le q_{a,n},\bar q_n\le C\) imply
\[
f_n(z)\le C_0 e_n(z)                                                            \tag{6}
\]
for a constant \(C_0\) depending only on \(c\) and \(C\).  Since the outcome diameter is one, \(0\le f_n(z)\le1\), and hence
\[
\int\{f_n(z)-\theta_n\}^2\,dR_n(z)
\le\int f_n(z)^2\,dR_n(z)
\le C_0\rho_n^2.                                                               \tag{7}
\]

For completeness, the weighted Poincar\'e bound needed below follows directly on \([0,1]\).  For every absolutely continuous \(g\), first minimize over constants, then use \(q_{a,n}\le C\), write \(g(y)-g(x)=\int_x^yg'(v)\,dv\), and apply Cauchy--Schwarz; finally use \(q_{a,n}\ge c\).  This gives
\[
\operatorname{Var}_{q_{a,n}(\cdot,z)}\{g(Y)\}
\le C_1\int_0^1|g'(y)|^2q_{a,n}(y,z)\,dy.                                      \tag{8}
\]
Applying (8) to \(g=\ell_{a,n}(\cdot,z)\), using (5), and changing variables along the monotone transport map give
\[
\operatorname{Var}_{q_{a,n}(\cdot,z)}\{\ell_{a,n}(Y,z)\}
\le4C_1 f_n(z).                                                                 \tag{9}
\]
The population arm score is
\[
s_{a,n}(y,z)=\frac12\{f_n(z)-\theta_n\}+\ell_{a,n}(y,z).
\]
Equations (7)--(9), the common marginal in \(\mathrm{ass{:}shared\text{-}covariate\text{-}marginal}\), and \((x+y)^2\le2x^2+2y^2\) therefore show
\[
\sum_{a=0}^1\operatorname{Var}_{P_{a,n}}\{s_{a,n}(W_{a,1,n})\}
\lesssim\rho_n^2.                                                              \tag{10}
\]
Because the two folds partition each arm and \(2m=n\), their equal-weight average is exactly
\[
\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}=\frac1n\sum_{a=0}^1\sum_{i=1}^ns_{a,n}(W_{a,i,n}).
\]
Independence within and between arms now gives
\[
\operatorname{sd}(\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n})
=\sqrt{\frac{V_{L,n}}n},
\qquad
V_{L,n}=\sum_{a=0}^1\operatorname{Var}_{P_{a,n}}\{s_{a,n}(W_{a,1,n})\}
\lesssim\rho_n^2.                                                              \tag{11}
\]
If \(\rho_n=0\), (4) implies \(H_z=0\) for \(R_n\)-almost every \(z\), hence \(\Delta_n=0\) almost everywhere.  Then \(f_n\), both normalized potentials, and \(\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}\) vanish.

When \(L>1\), there is no uniform reverse inequality in (11).  To see this within the declared class, take \(R_n\) uniform, choose integers \(k_n\to\infty\), put \(t_n=\kappa k_n^{-s_y}\) for a sufficiently small fixed \(\kappa>0\), and use the \(z\)-independent densities
\[
q_{a,n}(y,z)=1+(2a-1)\frac{t_n}{2}\cos(2\pi k_ny).
\]
The choice of \(\kappa\), \(c<1<C\), and \(L>1\) ensures the density bounds and a uniform \(\mathcal C^{s_y,s_z}\) norm.  Here \(\bar q_n=1\), and direct solution of the Neumann equation gives
\[
\rho_n^2=\frac{t_n^2}{8\pi^2k_n^2}.                                             \tag{12}
\]
Uniform Taylor expansion of the inverse distribution functions, justified by the fixed positive density lower bound, gives
\[
T_{0,z}(y)=y-\frac{t_n}{2\pi k_n}\sin(2\pi k_ny)
+O\!\left(\frac{t_n^2}{k_n}\right),
\]
uniformly in \(y\), with the opposite sign for \(T_{1,z}\).  Integrating (5) after centering therefore yields
\[
\sum_{a=0}^1\operatorname{Var}_{q_{a,n}}\{\ell_{a,n}(Y)\}
=O\!\left(\frac{t_n^2}{k_n^4}+\frac{t_n^4}{k_n^2}\right).                      \tag{13}
\]
The marginal value score is zero because \(f_n(z)\) is constant in \(z\).  Equations (11)--(13) imply \(V_{L,n}/\rho_n^2=O(k_n^{-2}+t_n^2)\to0\), proving the asserted failure of a uniform reverse bound.

It remains to calculate the canonical quadratic scale.  Independence, true centering, and diagonal deletion leave only identical or reversed observation pairs.  With \(B=B_{J_y,J_z,n}\), \(\Gamma_a=\Gamma_{a,J_y,J_z,n}\), and \(\Gamma=\Gamma_0+\Gamma_1\), direct pair counting across the two independent folds gives
\[
\begin{split}
\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n})
={}&\frac{\operatorname{tr}\{(B\Gamma_0)^2\}+\operatorname{tr}\{(B\Gamma_1)^2\}}{m(m-1)}
+\frac{2\operatorname{tr}(B\Gamma_0B\Gamma_1)}{m^2}\\
={}&\frac{4\operatorname{tr}\{(\Gamma^{1/2}B\Gamma^{1/2})^2\}}{n^2}\{1+o(1)\}
=\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}^2}{n^2}\{1+o(1)\}.                                      \tag{14}
\end{split}
\]
The last equality is \(\mathrm{def{:}whitened\text{-}spectral\text{-}array}\).  This proves the quadratic standard-deviation assertion.

At \((J_y^*,J_z^*)\), \(\mathrm{prop{:}anisotropic\text{-}spectrum\text{-}bias}\) and \(\mathrm{def{:}anisotropic\text{-}tuning}\) give
\[
\frac{A_{J_y^*,J_z^*,n}}n
\asymp\frac{2^{dJ_z^*/2}}n
\asymp\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}2^{-s_zJ_z^*}
\asymp\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}2^{-(s_y+1)J_y^*}
\asymp n^{-2s_z/(2s_z+d)}
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{d/(2s_z+d)}.                                    \tag{15}
\]
Moreover,
\[
D_{J_y^*,J_z^*}
=\{n\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\}^{\{2s_z/(s_y+1)+2d\}/(2s_z+d)+o(1)}
=o(n^2),                                                                        \tag{16}
\]
because \(2s_z/(s_y+1)+2d<2(2s_z+d)\) and \(n\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}<n\) eventually.  Substituting (15)--(16) into (3), using \(\rho_n\le\rho\), and applying Minkowski's inequality to (1), (11), and (14) proves the optimized root-mean-square display.  Thus \(\rho_n/\sqrt n\) is used only as the valid uniform upper envelope furnished by (11), not as the actual standard deviation.  No covariance estimator appears in this argument.
