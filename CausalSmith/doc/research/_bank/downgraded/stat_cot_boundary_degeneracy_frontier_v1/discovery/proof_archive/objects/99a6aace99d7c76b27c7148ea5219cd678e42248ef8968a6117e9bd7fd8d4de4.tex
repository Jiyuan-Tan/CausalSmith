Write \(M_n=2^{dJ_z}\), \(B=B_{J_y,J_z,n}\), \(K=K_{J_y,J_z,n}\), and \(A=A_{J_y,J_z,n}\).  All constants below depend only on the density bounds, the compactly supported boundary-wavelet bases, and the fixed support dimension.  They are therefore uniform over the declared model class.

We first prove the kernel bounds without pretending that the variable-coefficient Gram conjugations preserve compact support.  Replace the covariate wavelets spanning the levels through \(J_z\) by the orthonormal tensor boundary-scaling basis \(\{\varphi_{J_z,k}:k\in\mathcal K_{J_z}^{\mathrm{sc}}\}\) of the same space.  This orthogonal change of coordinates leaves the scalar kernel \(\Phi(w)^{\top}B\Phi(w')\), its moments, and the eigenvalues of the whitened matrix unchanged.  Its covariate Gram matrix is
\[
G_z(k,l)=\int\varphi_{J_z,k}(v)\varphi_{J_z,l}(v)r_n(v)\,dv.
\tag{1}
\]
By \(c\le r_n\le C\) from \(\mathrm{ass{:}covariate\text{-}density\text{-}bounds}\),
\[
cI\preceq G_z\preceq CI.
\tag{2}
\]
Compact support of the scaling functions from \(\mathrm{ass{:}wavelet\text{-}regularity}\) makes \(G_z(k,l)=0\) when the lattice distance between \(k\) and \(l\) exceeds a fixed integer \(R\).  Put \(a=(C+c)/2\) and \(E=I-G_z/a\).  Equation (2) gives \(\|E\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le q=(C-c)/(C+c)<1\), whereas bandedness gives \((E^j)_{kl}=0\) when \(\operatorname{dist}(k,l)>Rj\).  The convergent Neumann series therefore proves, rather than assumes, the inverse decay
\[
|G_z^{-1}(k,l)|
=\left|a^{-1}\sum_{j\ge0}(E^j)_{kl}\right|
\le \frac{a^{-1}}{1-q}q^{\lceil\operatorname{dist}(k,l)/R\rceil}.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus the weighted projection kernel
\[
\kappa_{J_z}(z,v)=\sum_{k,l}\varphi_{J_z,k}(z)G_z^{-1}(k,l)\varphi_{J_z,l}(v)
\]
is not compactly supported, but (3), the bound \(\|\varphi_{J_z,k}\|_\infty\lesssim2^{dJ_z/2}\), and bounded overlap give
\[
|\kappa_{J_z}(z,v)|
\le C M_n\exp\{-c_1 2^{J_z}\|z-v\|\}.
\tag{4}
\]

For the outcome coordinate, let \(R^y_{J_y}\) be the kernel of the orthogonal projector onto the retained zero-mean outcome space.  Compact support gives
\[
\sup_y\int|R^y_{J_y}(y,u)|\,du\le C.
\tag{5}
\]
The Green kernel of \(\mathcal G_{n,z}\) is uniformly bounded: indeed, for zero-mean \(h\), integration of
\(-\partial_y(\bar q_n\partial_yu)=h\) and the Neumann condition gives
\(u'(y)=-\bar q_n(y,z)^{-1}\int_0^yh(v)dv\); the lower density bound and the zero-mean normalization of \(u\) then bound the integral kernel by a constant depending only on \(c\).  Consequently (5) implies that the kernel \(g_{J_y}(y,y';z)\) of \(P_{J_y}\mathcal G_{n,z}P_{J_y}\) satisfies
\[
\sup_{J_y,y,y',z}|g_{J_y}(y,y';z)|\le C.
\tag{6}
\]
Because the full Gram matrix factorizes as the identity on the orthonormal outcome coordinates tensored with \(G_z\), expanding both Gram inverses in the definition of \(B\) gives the exact raw-kernel identity
\[
b_J\{(y,z),(y',z')\}
=\Phi(y,z)^{\top}B\Phi(y',z')
=\int\kappa_{J_z}(z,v)\kappa_{J_z}(z',v)
g_{J_y}(y,y';v)\,dR_n(v).
\tag{7}
\]
This is the required argument after both nonseparable, variable-coefficient Gram conjugations.  Equations (4), (6), the upper density bound, and the triangle inequality yield
\[
|b_J\{(y,z),(y',z')\}|
\le C M_n\exp\{-c_2 2^{J_z}\|z-z'\|\}.
\tag{8}
\]
The joint arm densities are bounded by \(C^2\), so integration of (8) over a tube of covariate volume \(O(M_n^{-1})\) gives, for \(p=2,4\),
\[
\max_{a,b}\iint|b_J(w,w')|^p\,dP_{a,n}(w)dP_{b,n}(w')
\le C M_n^{p-1},
\quad
\max_{a,b,w}\int|b_J(w,w')|^p\,dP_{b,n}(w')
\le C M_n^{p-1}.
\tag{9}
\]
Let \(\mu_a=E_{P_{a,n}}\Phi(W)\) and
\(b_{ab}^{\circ}(w,w')=\{\Phi(w)-\mu_a\}^{\top}B\{\Phi(w')-\mu_b\}\).
Conditional expectation is an \(L^p\)-contraction, so the four terms produced by centering preserve every order in (9).  In particular,
\[
E|b_{ab}^{\circ}(W_a,W_b)|^2\lesssim M_n,
\qquad
E|b_{ab}^{\circ}(W_a,W_b)|^4\lesssim M_n^3,
\tag{10}
\]
and the conditional second moment in either argument has \(L^2\)-norm \(O(M_n)\).

We also need the four-cycle contraction, which cannot be obtained merely by recounting supports.  If
\[
c_{abc}(w,w')=E_{P_{c,n}}
\{b_{ac}^{\circ}(w,W_c)b_{bc}^{\circ}(w',W_c)\},
\]
then covariance algebra gives its squared \(L^2(P_{a,n}\otimes P_{b,n})\)-norm as a cyclic trace containing two factors \(B\Gamma_cB\) and the arm covariances \(\Gamma_a,\Gamma_b\).  Since \(0\preceq\Gamma_a\preceq\Gamma=\Gamma_0+\Gamma_1\), insertion of the contractions \(\Gamma^{-1/2}\Gamma_a^{1/2}\) bounds every such cyclic trace by a fixed multiple of \(\operatorname{tr}(K^4)\).  The spectral conclusion of \(\mathrm{prop{:}anisotropic\text{-}spectrum\text{-}bias}\) gives
\[
\|c_{abc}\|_2^2\lesssim\operatorname{tr}(K^4)
\le\|K\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2\operatorname{tr}(K^2)lesssim M_n,
\qquad
\frac{\operatorname{tr}(K^4)}{\operatorname{tr}(K^2)^2}\lesssim M_n^{-1}.
\tag{11}
\]

Index an evaluation observation by \(r=(e,a,i)\), put \(Z_r=\Phi(W_r)-E\Phi(W_r)\), and order observations fold by fold.  Direct expansion of the diagonal-free statistic in \(\mathrm{def{:}anisotropic\text{-}estimator}\), using \(n=2m\), gives
\[
h_{n,rs}(x,y)=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{2}{m-1}x^{\top}By,&e_r=e_s,\ a_r=a_s,\\[4pt]
-\dfrac{2}{m}x^{\top}By,&e_r=e_s,\ a_r\ne a_s,\\[4pt]
0,&e_r\ne e_s,
\end{cases}
\qquad
n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}=\sum_{r<s}h_{n,rs}(Z_r,Z_s).
\tag{12}
\]
Independence in \(\mathrm{ass{:}two\text{-}arm\text{-}sampling}\) and \(EZ_r=0\) show that every edge is canonical in either argument.  There are \(O(m)\) edges at a vertex and \(O(m^2)\) nonzero edges.  Equations (10) and (12) therefore imply
\[
\max_r\sum_{s\ne r}Eh_{n,rs}^2\lesssim\frac{M_n}{m},
\qquad
\sum_{r<s}Eh_{n,rs}^4\lesssim\frac{M_n^3}{m^2}.
\tag{13}
\]
Canonicality makes two distinct edges orthogonal, even when they share one vertex.  Hence the variance is the sum of edge variances.  Separating the two within-arm edge sets from the cross-arm edge set gives
\[
\begin{split}
\operatorname{Var}(n\mathbb U)
&=\frac{4m}{m-1}\sum_{a=0}^1\operatorname{tr}(B\Gamma_aB\Gamma_a)
+8\operatorname{tr}(B\Gamma_0B\Gamma_1)\\
&=4\operatorname{tr}\{B(\Gamma_0+\Gamma_1)B(\Gamma_0+\Gamma_1)\}
+O(m^{-1})\operatorname{tr}(K^2)\\
&=A^2\{1+O(m^{-1})\},
\end{split}
\tag{14}
\]
where \(A^2=4\operatorname{tr}(K^2)\asymp M_n\) by \(\mathrm{prop{:}anisotropic\text{-}spectrum\text{-}bias}\).

It remains to prove the martingale assertions, including every contraction that enters them.  Let
\(q_r=\sum_{s<r}h_{n,sr}(Z_s,Z_r)\), so \(\mathcal D_{n,r}=\ell_{n,r}(W_r)+q_r\).  Canonicality gives
\(E(\mathcal D_{n,r}\mid\mathcal F_{r-1})=0\) and
\(\sum_r\mathcal D_{n,r}=G_n+n\mathbb U\).  Conditional on \(W_r\), the summands forming \(q_r\) are independent and centered.  Thus its fourth moment contains only a repeated-edge term and pairings of two edges:
\[
Eq_r^4
=\sum_{s<r}Eh_{n,sr}^4
+6\sum_{s<t<r}E\!left[E(h_{n,sr}^2\mid W_r)E(h_{n,tr}^2\mid W_r)\right].
\tag{15}
\]
The section bound following (10) makes the sum of the conditional second moments in (15) at most \(CM_n/m\).  Summing (15) over \(r\), and using (13), therefore gives
\[
\sum_rEq_r^4\lesssim \frac{M_n^2}{m}+\frac{M_n^3}{m^2}.
\tag{16}
\]
If the uniform bound on \(|\ell_{n,r}|\) is \(L_0\), independence and centering give
\(\sum_rE\ell_{n,r}^4\le L_0^2EG_n^2=O(S_n^2)\).  Since \(S_n\ge A\asymp M_n^{1/2}\), (16) and \((x+y)^4\le8(x^4+y^4)\) imply
\[
S_n^{-4}\sum_rE\mathcal D_{n,r}^4
\lesssim M_n^{-1}+m^{-1}+M_n/m^2\longrightarrow0.
\tag{17}
\]
Here \(J_z\to\infty\) gives \(M_n\to\infty\), and \(M_n\le D_{J_y,J_z}=o(n^2)=o(m^2)\).  The inequality
\(x^2\mathbf1\{|x|>\epsilon S_n\}\le x^4/(\epsilon^2S_n^2)\), followed by (17), proves the stated Lindeberg condition.

For completeness, expand the predictable variance rather than naming a changing-kernel theorem:
\[
\sum_rE(\mathcal D_{n,r}^2\mid\mathcal F_{r-1})
=EG_n^2+C_n+R_n+T_n,
\tag{18}
\]
where
\[
\begin{split}
C_n&=2\sum_r\sum_{s<r}E\{\ell_{n,r}(W_r)h_{n,sr}(Z_s,Z_r)\mid Z_s\},\\
R_n&=\sum_r\sum_{s<r}\{E(h_{n,sr}^2\mid Z_s)-Eh_{n,sr}^2\},\\
T_n&=2\sum_r\sum_{s<t<r}E\{h_{n,sr}h_{n,tr}\mid Z_s,Z_t\}.
\end{split}
\tag{19}
\]
The repeated-edge term \(R_n\) groups into independent centered functions of \(Z_s\).  The \(L^2\) conditional-second-moment bound after (10), the coefficient size \(O(m^{-1})\), and at most \(O(m)\) future edges yield
\[
ER_n^2\lesssim M_n^2/m.
\tag{20}
\]
For \(T_n\), regroup first over the unordered pair \((s,t)\).  The sum over their possible common terminal vertices has coefficient \(O(m^{-1})\).  Pair kernels with disjoint edge sets are independent, and pair kernels sharing just one vertex are orthogonal by canonicality.  The surviving repeated pair is exactly the four-cycle contraction in (11).  There are \(O(m^2)\) pairs, so
\[
ET_n^2\lesssim m^2m^{-2}\operatorname{tr}(K^4)\lesssim M_n.
\tag{21}
\]
Finally put \(u_r=E\{Z_r\ell_{n,r}(W_r)\}\).  Covariance Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\(\|\Gamma_{a_r}^{-1/2}u_r\|^2\le E\ell_{n,r}^2\), while
\(\|\Gamma_a^{1/2}B\Gamma_b^{1/2}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\|K\|_{\mathrm{op}}\lesssim1\).  Regrouping \(C_n\) over \(s\), applying Cauchy--Schwarz to the \(O(m)\) future indices, and then summing the independent centered functions of \(Z_s\) proves
\[
EC_n^2\lesssim EG_n^2=O(S_n^2).
\tag{22}
\]
This is the mixed linear--quadratic contraction.  Equations (20)--(22), \((x+y+z)^2\le3(x^2+y^2+z^2)\), \(S_n^4\ge A^4\asymp M_n^2\), and \(S_n^2\gtrsim M_n\) give
\[
\frac{E\left[\left\{\sum_rE(\mathcal D_{n,r}^2\mid\mathcal F_{r-1})
-E(G_n+n\mathbb U)^2\right\}^2\right]}{S_n^4}
\lesssim m^{-1}+\frac{M_n}{m^2}+M_n^{-1}+o(1)\longrightarrow0.
\tag{23}
\]
The centering in (23) is correct because the martingale differences are orthogonal and hence the expectation of (18) is \(E(G_n+n\mathbb U)^2\).  Equations (3)--(23) establish the claimed noncompact Gram-inverse localization, \(L^2\) and \(L^4\) bounds after both conjugations, maximal influence, repeated-edge, shared-vertex, four-cycle, mixed, Lindeberg, and predictable-variance assertions.
