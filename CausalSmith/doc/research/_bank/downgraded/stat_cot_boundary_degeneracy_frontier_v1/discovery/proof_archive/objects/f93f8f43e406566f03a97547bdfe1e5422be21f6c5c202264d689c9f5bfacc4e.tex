Write
\[
E_{R,n}(J_y,J_z,\rho)=C_B\left[
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\{2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}+\mathbf 1\{d\ge1\}2^{-s_zJ_z}\}
+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{2+a_y}
+\rho^{2a_y/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{2}
\right].
\]
Consider first \(d\ge1\) and any triangular collection satisfying the local score-approximation premises of \(\mathrm{thm{:}mixed\text{-}spectral\text{-}transition}\).  Define the population Gaussian--quadratic proxy
\[
G_n^\circ=\sum_{e=1}^2\sum_k\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}
\{(\xi_{e,k}+\eta_{k,n})^2-(1+\eta_{k,n}^2)\},
\]
and let \(q_{n,1-\alpha/2}^\circ\) be a \((1-\alpha/2)\)-quantile of \(|G_n^\circ|\).  By \(\mathrm{thm{:}mixed\text{-}spectral\text{-}transition}\), with
\[
X_n=n\{\Theta^{\mathrm{or}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n\},
\qquad
S_n=A_{J_y,J_z,n}+\sqrt{nV_{L,n}},
\]
one has
\[
d_{\mathrm{BL}}\{\mathcal L(X_n/S_n),\mathcal L(G_n^\circ/S_n)\}\longrightarrow0
\]
and both laws are asymptotically within bounded--Lipschitz distance zero of
\[
\mathcal N(0,\sigma_n^2),
\qquad
\sigma_n^2=\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}^2+nV_{L,n}}{S_n^2}.
\]
Since \(A_{J_y,J_z,n}>0\), \(V_{L,n}\ge0\), and
\[
\frac12\le
\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}^2+nV_{L,n}}
{(A_{J_y,J_z,n}+\sqrt{nV_{L,n}})^2}
\le1,
\]
the approximating normal laws have uniformly bounded densities and variances bounded away from zero.  Lipschitz upper and lower approximations to interval indicators therefore turn the two bounded--Lipschitz convergences into uniform convergence of the corresponding distribution functions.  Consequently,
\[
P\{|X_n|\le q_{n,1-\alpha/2}^\circ\}\ge1-\alpha/2-o(1).
\]

By \(\mathrm{thm{:}uniform\text{-}second\text{-}order\text{-}expansion}\) and the definition of \(\mathbb R_{J_y,J_z,n}\), there is a deterministic sequence
\[
r_n^\circ=o\!\left\{\rho n^{-1/2}+A_{J_y,J_z,n}/n\right\}
\]
such that, uniformly on the same triangular collection,
\[
\|\mathbb R_{J_y,J_z,n}\|_2\le C_0E_{R,n}(J_y,J_z,\rho)+r_n^\circ
\]
for a fixed \(C_0<\infty\).  Hence Markov's inequality gives, for
\(C_\alpha=\sqrt{2/\alpha}\max\{C_0,1\}\),
\[
P\!\left[
|\mathbb R_{J_y,J_z,n}|>
C_\alpha\{E_{R,n}(J_y,J_z,\rho)+r_n^\circ\}
\right]\le\alpha/2.
\]
Combining the last two displays with
\[
\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n
=X_n/n+\mathbb R_{J_y,J_z,n}
\]
proves that the infeasible population-calibrated interval centered at
\(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}\) and having half-width
\[
\frac{q_{n,1-\alpha/2}^\circ}{n}
+C_\alpha\{E_{R,n}(J_y,J_z,\rho)+r_n^\circ\}
\]
has asymptotic coverage at least \(1-\alpha\).  Normal tightness and the quantile comparison above imply
\[
q_{n,1-\alpha/2}^\circ=O(S_n).
\]
The variance bound \(V_{L,n}\lesssim\rho_n^2\le\rho^2\), supplied by
\(\mathrm{thm{:}uniform\text{-}second\text{-}order\text{-}expansion}\) and
\(\mathrm{ass{:}local\text{-}equality\text{-}radius}\), thus yields half-width
\[
O\!\left\{
E_{R,n}(J_y,J_z,\rho)+\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}}n+\frac{\rho}{\sqrt n}
\right\}.
\]
At the corrected resolutions, \(\mathrm{prop{:}anisotropic\text{-}spectrum\text{-}bias}\) and
\(\mathrm{def{:}anisotropic\text{-}tuning}\) give
\[
\frac{A_{J_y^*,J_z^*,n}}n
\asymp\frac{2^{dJ_z^*/2}}n
\asymp n^{-2s_z/(2s_z+d)}
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{d/(2s_z+d)},
\]
and each projection term in \(E_{R,n}\) is of this order.  The resulting infeasible half-width is therefore exactly of the order requested in the open-ended question.

The frozen observable controls do not justify replacement of
\(q_{n,1-\alpha/2}^\circ\) by an observable common-multiplier quantile at these resolutions.  Indeed, take
\(d=1\), \(s_y=1/10\), and \(s_z=1\).  Then \(\beta=1/11\),
\[
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}=(\log n/n)^{1/13},
\qquad
2^{J_z^*}\asymp(n\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n})^{2/3},
\qquad
2^{J_y^*}\asymp(n\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n})^{20/33},
\]
and
\[
D_{J_y^*,J_z^*}\asymp(n\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n})^{14/11}.
\]
Thus the sufficient covariance condition in \(\mathrm{lem{:}observable\text{-}pilot\text{-}control}\) fails because
\[
\frac{D_{J_y^*,J_z^*}\log n}{m}
\asymp n^{25/143}(\log n)^{157/143}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Moreover, converting the available operator-norm pilot-Hessian error
\(O_P(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{a_y})\) to Hilbert--Schmidt norm gives only the relative envelope
\[
\frac{\sqrt{D_{J_y^*,J_z^*}}\,
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{a_y}}
{\|K_{J_y^*,J_z^*,n}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}}
\asymp
\frac{\sqrt{D_{J_y^*,J_z^*}}\,
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{1/10}}
{2^{J_z^*/2}}
\asymp
n^{389/1430}(\log n)^{133/4290}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Hence neither relative spectral consistency nor the required observable Gaussian--quadratic coupling follows from the current primitive-model lemmas.  This is a failure of the available proof conditions, not an impossibility theorem for every conceivable confidence procedure.  For \(d=0\), \(\mathrm{thm{:}no\text{-}covariate\text{-}recovery}\) supplies the exact equality law, but it does not supply a single observable coupling and anti-concentration argument that is uniform from that null chaos through local alternatives to the regular Gaussian regime.  The observable mixed-calibration question therefore remains genuinely open.
