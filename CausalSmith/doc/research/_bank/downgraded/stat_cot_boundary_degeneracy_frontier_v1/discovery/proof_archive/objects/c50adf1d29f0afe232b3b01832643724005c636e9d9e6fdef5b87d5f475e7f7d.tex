Write \(M=2^{dJ_z}\), \(A=A_{J_y,J_z,n}\), \(V=V_{L,n}\), \(S=S_n=A+\sqrt{nV}\), and suppress \((J_y,J_z,n)\) from matrices and eigenvalues.

\emph{Step 1: reduction to the population linear and quadratic projections.}
The uniform second-order expansion and the corrected bias premise imply
\[
n\{\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n-B^y_{J_y,n}-B^z_{J_z,n}\}
=n\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}+n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}+o_P(S).        \tag{20}
\]
Indeed, the linear and quadratic stochastic scales become \(\rho_n\sqrt n\) and \(A\) after multiplication by \(n\), while the premise gives
\(n\{2^{-(2s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-2s_zJ_z}\}=O(S)\).  The variance assertion in the expansion gives \(\rho_n\sqrt n\asymp\sqrt{nV}\), so every term preceding its little-oh remainder is \(O(S)\).

Let \(s_{a,n}\) be the exact centered first derivative and let \(s^{(2)}_{a,J_y,J_z,n}\) be the projected inverse-elliptic derivative.  To compare them, write \(H_n(y,z)=\int_0^y\Delta_n(v,z)dv\).  The identity defining an inverse distribution function, expanded once with integral remainder, and the bounds \(c\le q_{a,n}\le C\), give
\[
q_{a,n}^{-1}\text{-remainder at }(y,z)\le C|H_n(y,z)|\|\Delta_n\|_\infty.
\]
Integrating its square and using the energy identity (3) in the contraction proof yields a nonlinear score remainder at most \(C\rho_n\|\Delta_n\|_\infty\) in \(L^2(P_{a,n})\).  The outcome antiderivative estimate and the weighted covariate projection estimate in the anisotropic spectrum-and-bias proposition give the two projection tails.  Therefore
\[
\max_a\|s_{a,n}-s^{(2)}_{a,J_y,J_z,n}\|_{L^2(P_{a,n})}
\le C\{\rho_n\|\Delta_n\|_\infty
+2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-s_zJ_z}\}.                               \tag{21}
\]

For fold \(e\), put
\[
Z_{e,n}=\Gamma^{-1/2}\sqrt m\{ar\Phi_{1,e}-\bar\Phi_{0,e}-\delta\},
\qquad
G_n=2\sum_{e=1}^2\sqrt m\,\delta^\top B\Gamma^{1/2}Z_{e,n}. \tag{22}
\]
The two vectors \(Z_{e,n}\) are independent, centered, and have covariance \(I\) by the two-arm sampling assumption and the definition of \(\Gamma\).  If \(Z_{e,k}=v_k^\top Z_{e,n}\) and \(\eta_k=v_k^\top\Gamma^{-1/2}\sqrt m\,\delta\), diagonalizing \(K\) in (22) gives
\[
G_n=2\sum_{e=1}^2\sum_k\nu_k\eta_kZ_{e,k},
\qquad
EG_n^2=8\sum_k\nu_k^2\eta_k^2.                               \tag{23}
\]
An empirical average of a centered score error with \(L^2\)-norm \(r_n\), multiplied by \(n\), has \(L^2\)-norm at most \(C\sqrt n r_n\).  Thus (21) gives
\[
\|n\mathbb L-G_n\|_2
\le C\sqrt n\{\rho_n\|\Delta_n\|_\infty
+2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-s_zJ_z}\}.                               \tag{24}
\]
The first term in (24) is \(o(S)\) because \(\|\Delta_n\|_\infty\to0\) and \(\rho_n\sqrt n\asymp\sqrt{nV}\le S\).  With \(x=2^{-J_y}\), the bias premise gives \(nx^{2s_y+1}=O(S)\), whence
\[
\left\{\frac{\sqrt n x^{s_y+1}}S\right\}^2
=\frac{nx^{2s_y+2}}{S^2}=O(x/S)=o(1).                         \tag{25}
\]
Similarly, \(n2^{-2s_zJ_z}=O(S)\) gives
\(n2^{-2s_zJ_z}/S^2=O(S^{-1})=o(1)\).  Here \(S\ge A\asymp M^{1/2}\to\infty\) by the spectral proposition.  Consequently
\[
\|n\mathbb L-G_n\|_2=o(S),
\qquad
8\sum_k\nu_k^2\eta_k^2=nV+o(S^2).                            \tag{26}
\]

\emph{Step 2: the data statistic is diffuse Gaussian.}
Put \(Q_n=n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}\).  The growing-kernel contraction lemma applies because \(M\le D=o(n^2)\), the projected summands in (22) are centered and uniformly bounded by the density and localized-kernel bounds, and (26) gives \(EG_n^2=O(S^2)\).  It gives a martingale-difference array \((\mathcal D_{n,r})\) summing to \(G_n+Q_n\), its Lindeberg condition, and convergence of its predictable variance.  Hoeffding orthogonality, (26), and the variance part of that lemma yield
\[
E(G_n+Q_n)^2=A^2+nV+o(S^2).                                   \tag{27}
\]
For fixed \(t\), the elementary expansion
\[
|e^{ix}-1-ix+x^2/2|\le Cx^2\min\{|x|,1\}                    \tag{28}
\]
applied conditionally to each \(t\mathcal D_{n,r}/S\) has a total remainder tending to zero by Lindeberg.  Replacing the sum of conditional variances by (27), which is valid in probability and in \(L^1\) by the contraction lemma, and iterating conditional expectations gives
\[
E\exp\{it(G_n+Q_n)/S\}
-\exp\!\left[-\frac{t^2}{2S^2}\{A^2+nV+o(S^2)\}\right]\to0. \tag{29}
\]

\emph{Step 3: the finite Gaussian-quadratic proxy has the same limit.}
Define
\[
\mathcal Q_n^G=\sum_{e=1}^2\sum_k\nu_k
\{(\xi_{e,k}+\eta_k)^2-(1+\eta_k^2)\}.
\]
For \(c_{e,k}=\nu_k/S\) and \(l_{e,k}=2\nu_k\eta_k/S\), direct completion of the square in the standard Gaussian density gives
\[
\log Ee^{it\mathcal Q_n^G/S}
=\sum_{e,k}\left[-itc_{e,k}-\frac12\log(1-2itc_{e,k})
-\frac{t^2l_{e,k}^2}{2(1-2itc_{e,k})}\right].                \tag{30}
\]
Equation (26) and the spectral proposition imply
\[
\sum_{e,k}l_{e,k}^2=\frac{nV+o(S^2)}{S^2},
\qquad
\max_k\frac{|\nu_k|}{S}\le\frac{\|K\|_{\rm op}}A
\lesssim M^{-1/2}\to0.                                      \tag{31}
\]
Taylor's formula in (30) has error bounded by a constant times
\[
\frac{\max_k|\nu_k|}{S}
\left\{\frac{\sum_{e,k}\nu_k^2}{S^2}+\sum_{e,k}l_{e,k}^2\right\}=o(1).
\]
Using \(A^2=4\sum_k\nu_k^2\) therefore gives
\[
\log Ee^{it\mathcal Q_n^G/S}
=-\frac{t^2}{2S^2}(A^2+nV)+o(1).                              \tag{32}
\]
The variance \(\sigma_n^2=(A^2+nV)/S^2\) lies in \([1/2,1]\).  Along every subsequence, choose a further subsequence on which \(\sigma_n^2\) converges.  Equations (29) and (32) then identify the same centered normal limit.  Their uniformly bounded second moments give tightness; approximation of bounded Lipschitz functions by smooth functions with integrable Fourier transform converts convergence of the characteristic functions into bounded-Lipschitz convergence.  Since this holds on every subsequence,
\[
d_{\rm BL}\{\mathcal L((G_n+Q_n)/S),\mathcal L(\mathcal Q_n^G/S)\}\to0,
\]
and both laws have bounded-Lipschitz distance tending to zero from \(\mathcal N(0,\sigma_n^2)\).  Equations (20) and (26) change the normalized estimator by \(o_P(1)\), proving the first two displayed conclusions.

At conditional equality, \(\Delta_n=0\), so \(V=0\), every \(\eta_k=0\), and \(\mathbb L=0\).  Taking \(S=A\) in (29), or in (30)--(32), gives
\[
n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}/A\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1).
\]
The decisive condition is the forced diffuseness \(\max_k|\nu_k|/(\sum_k\nu_k^2)^{1/2}\lesssim M^{-1/2}\to0\); hence no fixed non-Gaussian chaos survives.

\emph{Step 4: the separate regular branch.}
At a fixed unequal law, the exact centered Kantorovich scores are bounded by compact support and positivity.  Their independent triangular sum obeys Lindeberg because its maximal summand divided by \(\sqrt{nV_{L,n}}\) tends to zero, and the same conditional characteristic-function calculation as (28)--(29) gives
\[
\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}/\sqrt{V_{L,n}/n}\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1).
\]
The hypothesis \(\sqrt{nV_{L,n}}/A\to\infty\), together with the quadratic variance formula, makes \(\mathbb U=o_P(\sqrt{V_{L,n}/n})\).  The stated bias and remainder condition removes the deterministic and higher-order terms, and \(\widehat V_{L,n}/V_{L,n}\to_P1\) changes the normalization by a factor converging in probability to one.  This proves the studentized regular conclusion.

The omitted observed-kernel clause cannot be inferred from the frozen pilot result.  That result gives \(\|\widehat K-K\|_{\rm HS}=o_P(\|K\|_{\rm HS})\), which controls the centered Gaussian quadratic error, but the noncentral proxy also contains \(2\eta^\top(\widehat K-K)\xi\), whose conditional variance is \(4\|(\widehat K-K)\eta\|_2^2\).  No frozen premise bounds this quantity by \(o_P(S^2)\).  The proposed statement therefore removes only that optional assertion rather than silently assuming shift-weighted consistency.
