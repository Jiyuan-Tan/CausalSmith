Let
\[
\mathcal R_{n,d}(\rho)=\inf_{\widetilde\theta_n}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}(s_y,s_z,\rho)}
\{E_P(\widetilde\theta_n-\theta_n)^2\}^{1/2}.
\]
We first relate the causal endpoint to the radius.  For fixed \(z\), let \(F_{a,z}(y)=\int_0^yq_{a,n}(v,z)dv\), \(Q_{a,z}=F_{a,z}^{-1}\), and \(H_z=F_{1,z}-F_{0,z}\).  Positivity makes the inverses continuous.  The uncrossing inequality
\[
(x-y)^2+(x'-y')^2\le(x-y')^2+(x'-y)^2
\quad(x\le x',\ y\le y')
\]
proves that the increasing coupling minimizes squared cost, so
\[
W_2^2\{Q_{0,n}(\cdot\mid z),Q_{1,n}(\cdot\mid z)\}
=\int_0^1\{Q_{1,z}(v)-Q_{0,z}(v)\}^2dv. \tag{1}
\]
For \(u_z=\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n\), integration of the Neumann equation and then integration by parts give
\[
-\bar q_nu_z'=H_z,
\qquad
\int\Delta_nu_z\,dy=\int H_z^2/\bar q_n\,dy. \tag{2}
\]
For each \(v\), the mean-value theorem gives a point \(\zeta_{v,z}\) between the two quantiles such that
\[
H_z\{Q_{0,z}(v)\}=q_{1,n}(\zeta_{v,z},z)
\{Q_{0,z}(v)-Q_{1,z}(v)\}. \tag{3}
\]
Changing variables \(v=F_{0,z}(y)\) in (1) and using \(c\le q_{a,n},\bar q_n\le C\) in (2)--(3) prove two-sided comparability.  Integrating against the common marginal gives
\[
(c^2/C^2)\rho_n^2\le\theta_n\le(C^2/c^2)\rho_n^2
\le(C^2/c^2)\rho^2. \tag{4}
\]
Thus the zero estimator has root-mean-square risk \(O(\rho^2)\).

Put
\[
g_n=n^{-2s_z/(2s_z+d)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{d/(2s_z+d)},
\quad
p_n=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{2+a_y}
+\rho^{2a_y/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^2.
\]
The observable expansion theorem gives
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}
\|\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y^*,J_z^*,n}-\theta_n\|_2
\lesssim\rho n^{-1/2}+g_n+p_n. \tag{5}
\]
The established SmoothCOT median envelope gives, for every fixed \(0<\tau<\min\{s_y,s_z\}\),
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}
\|\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{sm,med}}_{\tau,n}-\theta_n\|_2
\le C_\tau n^{-a_\tau}(\log n)^{a_\tau}. \tag{6}
\]
When \(s_z\ge1\), the direct-COT median envelope gives
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}
\|\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{dir,med}}-\theta_n\|_2
\lesssim n^{-1/(d+2)}(\log n)^{1+1/(d+2)}. \tag{7}
\]
For the fixed class indices, choose before seeing the data the estimator with the smallest deterministic guarantee among (4)--(7).  This law-independent selection proves the asserted minimax upper bound.

For the converse, use \(2^{J_z}\asymp n^{2/(4s_z+d)}\).  The explicit mixture lemma places its null and alternatives in the class and, after reducing the fixed \(c_0\), gives constants \(\eta<1\) and \(c_M>0\) such that
\[
\chi^2(\overline{\mathbb P}_n,\mathbb P_n^0)\le\eta,
\qquad
\theta_n(\omega)-\theta_n(0)
\ge c_M\min\{\rho^2,r_{n,d}(s_z)\}. \tag{8}
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz applied to the mixture likelihood ratio yields
\[
\|\overline{\mathbb P}_n-\mathbb P_n^0\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\le\tfrac12\sqrt\eta<1. \tag{9}
\]
Thresholding any estimator halfway between the endpoints, and observing that either testing error forces absolute estimation error at least half the separation, gives
\[
\mathcal R_{n,d}(\rho)\gtrsim\min\{\rho^2,r_{n,d}(s_z)\}. \tag{10}
\]

For the regular term, extend the fixed-frequency cosine family constantly in \(z\), and denote its endpoint by \(\Theta(t)\).  Direct solution gives
\[
\rho(t)^2=t^2/(8\pi^2). \tag{11}
\]
For \(\rho\ge K n^{-1/2}\), take \(t=c_7\min\{\rho,t_0\}\) and \(t'=t+c_8n^{-1/2}\), with fixed constants sufficiently small.  Equation (11) puts both points in the class, and the mixture lemma gives bounded product Hellinger distance.  Implicit differentiation of \(F_{a,t}\{Q_{a,t}(v)\}=v\) gives
\[
\partial_tQ_{a,t}(v)
=-\frac{(2a-1)\sin\{2\pi Q_{a,t}(v)\}}
{4\pi q_{a,t}^{0}\{Q_{a,t}(v)\}}. \tag{12}
\]
The denominator is bounded below.  Dominated differentiation makes \(\Theta''\) continuous near zero; the cosine second-variation lemma gives \(\Theta'(0)=0\) and \(\Theta''(0)=1/(4\pi^2)\).  Two applications of the mean-value theorem therefore yield
\[
|\Theta(t')-\Theta(t)|\gtrsim\rho n^{-1/2}. \tag{13}
\]
The same two-point testing argument gives
\[
\mathcal R_{n,d}(\rho)\gtrsim\rho n^{-1/2}
\quad\text{for }\rho\ge K n^{-1/2}. \tag{14}
\]

Let \(r=r_{n,d}(s_z)\), whose polynomial exponent is less than one.  If \(\rho<K n^{-1/2}\), then \(\rho^2=o(r)\), so (10) equals the target minimum up to constants.  If \(\rho\ge K n^{-1/2}\) and \(\rho^2\le r\), (10) again supplies \(\rho^2\).  If \(\rho^2>r\), (10) and (14) supply \(\max\{r,\rho n^{-1/2}\}\ge(r+\rho n^{-1/2})/2\).  Therefore
\[
\mathcal R_{n,d}(\rho)\gtrsim
\min\{\rho^2,\rho n^{-1/2}+r_{n,d}(s_z)\}
=\mathfrak R_{n,d}(s_y,s_z,\rho). \tag{15}
\]

It remains to verify the strict gap and phase claims.  Write \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}=(\log n/n)^e\), where
\[
e=\{2+s_y^{-1}+ds_z^{-1}\}^{-1}
<\frac{s_z}{2s_z+d}<\frac{2s_z}{4s_z+d}. \tag{16}
\]
The polynomial exponent of \(g_n\) is \((2s_z+ed)/(2s_z+d)\), and
\[
\frac{4s_z}{4s_z+d}-\frac{2s_z+ed}{2s_z+d}
=\frac{d\{2s_z-e(4s_z+d)\}}{(4s_z+d)(2s_z+d)}>0. \tag{17}
\]
Together with the positive logarithmic power, (17) proves \(n^{-4s_z/(4s_z+d)}=o(g_n)\).  Monotonicity of \(x/(2x+d+1)\) and \(\tau<s_z\) imply
\[
a_\tau<\frac{s_z}{2s_z+d+1}<
\min\left\{\frac{4s_z}{4s_z+d},\frac12\right\}. \tag{18}
\]
When \(s_z\ge1\), direct cross-multiplication also gives
\[
1/(d+2)<\min\{4s_z/(4s_z+d),1/2\}. \tag{19}
\]
Thus neither global fallback establishes the intrinsic quadratic exponent.  Finally, solving \(\rho_{Q,n}^2=r_{n,d}(s_z)\) and \(\rho_{L,n}n^{-1/2}=r_{n,d}(s_z)\) gives the two displayed boundaries.  The second tends to zero exactly for \(s_z>d/4\); for \(s_z\le d/4\), the boundary quadratic scale is at least root-\(n\) while the first derivative vanishes, precluding a uniformly positive-variance root-\(n\) description.  This proves all claims.
