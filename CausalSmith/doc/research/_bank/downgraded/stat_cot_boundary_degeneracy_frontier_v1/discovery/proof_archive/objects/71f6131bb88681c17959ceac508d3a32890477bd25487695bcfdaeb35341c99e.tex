Put \(c_y(y)=\cos(2\pi y)\). If \(\rho=0\), then \(\gamma_n=0\), the construction is the null law, and every asserted property is immediate. Suppose \(\rho>0\).

Boundary-wavelet scaling and disjointness of the supports indexed by \(\mathcal K_{J_z}\) give constants depending only on the fixed basis such that
\[
\|b_{n,\omega}\|_\infty
\le C_1\gamma_n2^{dJ_z/2}
\le C_1c_0 2^{-s_zJ_z},
\qquad
\|b_{n,\omega}\|_{\mathcal C^{s_z}}
\le C_2\gamma_n2^{(s_z+d/2)J_z}
\le C_2c_0.                                           \tag{1}
\]
Since \(L>1\), choose \(c_0\) so small that the perturbation H\"older norm is at most \(L-1\). Shrink it further so that \(C_1c_0\le2\min\{1-c,C-1\}\). The fixed outcome cosine has bounded H\"older norm of every fixed order. Therefore
\[
c\le 1+(2a-1)b_{n,\omega}(z)c_y(y)/2\le C,
\qquad
\|q_{a,n,\omega}^{\mathrm{lb}}\|_{\mathcal C^{s_y,s_z}}\le L. \tag{2}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\int_0^1q_{a,n,\omega}^{\mathrm{lb}}(y,z)dy
=1+\frac{2a-1}{2}b_{n,\omega}(z)\int_0^1c_y(y)dy=1.   \tag{3}
\]
Thus the conditional laws are normalized. Taking \(r_n(z)=1\) gives the common covariate marginal and satisfies its density and smoothness conditions.

For this symmetric pair, \(\bar q_n=1\), \(\Delta_n=b_{n,\omega}c_y\), and
\[
\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n(\cdot,z)
=\frac{b_{n,\omega}(z)}{4\pi^2}c_y.
\]
Orthonormality of the selected covariate wavelets yields
\[
\int_{\mathcal Z_d}b_{n,\omega}(z)^2dz
=\gamma_n^2M_{J_z},\qquad
\rho_n^2=\frac{\gamma_n^2M_{J_z}}{8\pi^2}\le\rho^2.   \tag{4}
\]
The last inequality uses the radius branch in \(\gamma_n\) and the fixed small choice of \(c_0\). Hence every alternative belongs to \(\mathcal M_{n,d}(s_y,s_z,\rho)\). By the cosine lemma,
\[
\theta_{n,\omega}
=\frac{\gamma_n^2M_{J_z}}{8\pi^2}
+O(\|b_{n,\omega}\|_\infty^3).                        \tag{5}
\]
Since \(\|b_{n,\omega}\|_\infty=o(1)\) and \(\gamma_n^2M_{J_z}\asymp(\gamma_n2^{dJ_z/2})^2\), the remainder divided by the leading term tends to zero uniformly in \(\omega\).

Under \(\mathbb P_n^0\), let \(L_\omega\) be the likelihood ratio of the two-arm product alternative. Independence, \(\int_0^1c_y^2=1/2\), and wavelet orthonormality give
\[
E_0(L_\omega L_{\omega'})
=\left\{1+\frac{\gamma_n^2}{8}
\sum_{k\in\mathcal K_{J_z}}\omega_k\omega'_k\right\}^{2n}. \tag{6}
\]
The factor is positive by (2). Since \(\log(1+x)\le x\) for \(x>-1\), averaging over independent uniform signs and writing \(\epsilon_k=\omega_k\omega'_k\) gives
\[
\begin{split}
1+\chi^2(\overline{\mathbb P}_n,\mathbb P_n^0)
&\le E_\epsilon\exp\left\{\frac{n\gamma_n^2}{4}
\sum_{k=1}^{M_{J_z}}\epsilon_k\right\}\\
&=\left\{\cosh(n\gamma_n^2/4)\right\}^{M_{J_z}}
\le \exp\left\{\frac{n^2\gamma_n^4M_{J_z}}{32}\right\}.
\end{split}                                           \tag{7}
\]
At \(2^{J_z}\asymp n^{2/(4s_z+d)}\),
\[
n^2\gamma_n^4M_{J_z}
\le Cc_0^4n^2 2^{-(4s_z+d)J_z}=O(c_0^4),
\]
and
\[
\gamma_n^2M_{J_z}
\asymp c_0^2\min\{2^{-2s_zJ_z},\rho^2\}
\asymp c_0^2\min\{r_{n,d}(s_z),\rho^2\}.              \tag{8}
\]

Finally \(q_{a,t}^0=1+(2a-1)tc_y/2\) integrates to one. The same \(L>1\) slack and a sufficiently small fixed neighborhood give positivity, the upper bound, and the required H\"older norm. If \(|t'-t|\le Cn^{-1/2}\), positivity and \((\sqrt x-\sqrt y)^2\le(x-y)^2/y\) imply
\[
H^2(q_{a,t}^0,q_{a,t'}^0)
\le \int_0^1\frac{\{q_{a,t}^0(y)-q_{a,t'}^0(y)\}^2}{q_{a,t'}^0(y)}dy
\le C'(t-t')^2.                                      \tag{9}
\]
The product Hellinger affinity factorizes, so the two-arm product squared Hellinger distance is at most \(2nC'(t-t')^2=O(1)\). This completes all asserted checks.
