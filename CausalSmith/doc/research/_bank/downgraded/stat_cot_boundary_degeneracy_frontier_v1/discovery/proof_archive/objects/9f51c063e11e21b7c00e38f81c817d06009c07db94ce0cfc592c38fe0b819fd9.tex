Put \(c_y(y)=\cos(2\pi y)\).  We verify every property of the construction rather than treating it as part of the definition.

If \(\rho=0\), then \(\gamma_n=0\), the construction is the null law, and every asserted membership, separation, and divergence statement is immediate.  Hence suppose \(\rho>0\) below.

First, boundary-wavelet scaling and the disjointness of the supports indexed by \(\mathcal K_{J_z}\) give constants \(C_1,C_2<\infty\), depending only on the fixed wavelet basis, such that
\[
 \|b_{n,\omega}\|_\infty
 \le C_1\gamma_n2^{dJ_z/2}
 \le C_1c_0 2^{-s_zJ_z},
 \qquad
 \|b_{n,\omega}\|_{\mathcal C^{s_z}}
 \le C_2\gamma_n2^{(s_z+d/2)J_z}
 \le C_2c_0.
\]
The first inequality uses that at most a fixed number of the selected wavelets are nonzero at any \(z\), and the second follows by applying the defining H\"older difference quotient to an \(L^2\)-normalized level-\(J_z\) wavelet.  Since \(L>1\) by \(\mathrm{ass{:}holder\text{-}radius\text{-}interior}\), choose \(c_0\) so small that the perturbation H\"older norm is at most \(L-1\).  Shrinking the same fixed \(c_0\), if necessary, makes
\[
 C_1c_0\le 2\min\{1-c,C-1\}.
\]
It follows from \(\mathrm{ass{:}conditional\text{-}density\text{-}bounds}\) and \(\mathrm{ass{:}anisotropic\text{-}conditional\text{-}smoothness}\) that the displayed candidates have the required positivity, upper bound, and anisotropic H\"older radius:
\[
 c\le 1+(2a-1)b_{n,\omega}(z)c_y(y)/2\le C,
 \qquad
 \|q_{a,n,\omega}^{\mathrm{lb}}\|_{\mathcal C^{s_y,s_z}}\le L.
\]
The outcome frequency is fixed, so its \(\mathcal C^{s_y}\) contribution is absorbed into the fixed constants above.  Moreover,
\[
 \int_0^1q_{a,n,\omega}^{\mathrm{lb}}(y,z)dy
 =1+\frac{2a-1}{2}b_{n,\omega}(z)\int_0^1c_y(y)dy=1.
\]
Thus the conditional laws are normalized.  Taking \(r_n(z)=1\) gives the same covariate marginal in both arms, as required by \(\mathrm{ass{:}shared\text{-}covariate\text{-}marginal}\).  The constant marginal is positive and belongs to the required H\"older ball.

For this symmetric pair, \(\bar q_n=1\) and \(\Delta_n=b_{n,\omega}c_y\).  The Neumann solution in the definition of \(\mathcal G_{n,z}\) is therefore
\[
 \mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n(\cdot,z)
 =\frac{b_{n,\omega}(z)}{4\pi^2}c_y,
\]
which has zero integral and zero derivative at both endpoints.  Orthonormality of the selected covariate wavelets yields
\[
 \int_{\mathcal Z_d}b_{n,\omega}(z)^2dz
 =\gamma_n^2\sum_{k\in\mathcal K_{J_z}}\omega_k^2
 =\gamma_n^2M_{J_z}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \rho_n^2
 =\frac{1}{4\pi^2}\int b_{n,\omega}(z)^2dz
       \int_0^1c_y(y)^2dy
 =\frac{\gamma_n^2M_{J_z}}{8\pi^2}
 \le \frac{c_0^2}{8\pi^2}\rho^2
 \le \rho^2,
\]
where the penultimate inequality is the radius branch in the definition of \(\gamma_n\), and the last is ensured by the already fixed small choice of \(c_0\).  Thus every alternative belongs to \(\mathcal M_{n,d}(s_y,s_z,\rho)\).

By \(\mathrm{lem{:}cosine\text{-}second\text{-}variation}\), uniformly in \(\omega\),
\[
 \theta_{n,\omega}
 =\frac{gamma_n^2M_{J_z}}{8\pi^2}
   +O(\|b_{n,\omega}\|_\infty^3).
\]
Here \(\gamma_n^2M_{J_z}\asymp(\gamma_n2^{dJ_z/2})^2\), whereas \(\|b_{n,\omega}\|_\infty\lesssim\gamma_n2^{dJ_z/2}=o(1)\).  Hence the remainder divided by \(\gamma_n^2M_{J_z}\) tends to zero, proving the asserted endpoint separation.

It remains to compute the testing distance.  Under \(\mathbb P_n^0\), let \(L_\omega\) be the likelihood ratio of the two-arm product alternative.  Independence in \(\mathrm{ass{:}two\text{-}arm\text{-}sampling}\), \(\int_0^1c_y^2=1/2\), and wavelet orthonormality give, for two sign vectors \(\omega,\omega'\),
\[
 E_0(L_\omega L_{\omega'})
 =\left\{1+\frac18\int b_{n,\omega}b_{n,\omega'}dz\right\}^{2n}
 =\left\{1+\frac{\gamma_n^2}{8}
             \sum_{k\in\mathcal K_{J_z}}\omega_k\omega'_k\right\}^{2n}.
\]
The factor inside braces is positive by the positivity choice above.  Since \(\log(1+x)\le x\) for \(x>-1\), averaging over independent uniform sign vectors and writing \(\epsilon_k=\omega_k\omega'_k\) gives
\[
 1+\chi^2(\overline{\mathbb P}_n,\mathbb P_n^0)
 \le E_\epsilon\exp\left\{\frac{n\gamma_n^2}{4}
                     \sum_{k=1}^{M_{J_z}}\epsilon_k\right\}
 =\left\{\cosh(n\gamma_n^2/4)\right\}^{M_{J_z}}
 \le \exp\left\{\frac{n^2\gamma_n^4M_{J_z}}{32}\right\}.
\]
Thus the claimed inequality holds for any fixed \(C_\chi\ge1/32\).  At \(2^{J_z}\asymp n^{2/(4s_z+d)}\),
\[
 n^2\gamma_n^4M_{J_z}
 \le Cc_0^4n^2 2^{-(4s_z+d)J_z}=O(c_0^4),
\]
and, because \(M_{J_z}\asymp2^{dJ_z}\),
\[
 \gamma_n^2M_{J_z}
 \asymp c_0^2\min\{2^{-2s_zJ_z},\rho^2\}
 \asymp c_0^2\min\{r_{n,d}(s_z),\rho^2\}.
\]

Finally consider \(q_{a,t}^0=1+(2a-1)tc_y/2\).  Its integral is one.  The same \(L>1\) slack and a sufficiently small fixed neighborhood of zero give positivity, the upper density bound, and the required H\"older norm.  If \(|t'-t|\le Cn^{-1/2}\), then for one observation in either arm, positivity and \((\sqrt x-\sqrt y)^2\le(x-y)^2/y\) imply
\[
 H^2(q_{a,t}^0,q_{a,t'}^0)
 \le \int_0^1\frac{\{q_{a,t}^0(y)-q_{a,t'}^0(y)\}^2}{q_{a,t'}^0(y)}dy
 \le C'(t-t')^2.
\]
Tensorization over the two independent samples gives product squared Hellinger distance at most \(2nC'(t-t')^2=O(1)\).  This completes all asserted checks.
