Once the empirical-quantile projection is written as an explicit sample map and \(L>1\) is imposed, the standard argument gives the claimed branch.  Uniform density bounds identify \(W_2^2\) with the squared \(L^2\) distance between quantiles and make it comparable to \(\rho_n^2\).  A \(C^{s_y}\) density bounded below has a \(C^{s_y+1}\) quantile, so projection past \(J_y^0\) has squared bias \(O(2^{-2(s_y+1)J_y^0})=o(n^{-1})\).  The two independent empirical quantile processes satisfy \(\sqrt n(\widehat Q_1-\widehat Q_0)\Rightarrow\mathbb B_1-\mathbb B_0\stackrel{d}{=}\sqrt2\mathbb B\) at the uniform null.  Consequently \(n\widehat\theta^0\Rightarrow2\int_0^1\mathbb B^2\), and the Karhunen--Lo\`eve expansion of the bridge gives \(2\sum_{k\ge1}(\pi^2k^2)^{-1}\xi_k^2\).  The zero, quadratic two-point, and regular two-point arguments then give \(\min\{\rho^2,\rho n^{-1/2}+n^{-1}\}\).
