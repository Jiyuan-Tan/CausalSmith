Put \(s=\beta_{\mathrm{iso}}\) and \(a_\tau=\tau/(2\tau+d+1)\).  The theorem-level premise fixes compactly supported boundary-corrected bases \(\Psi_y\) and \(\Psi_z\), each with regularity strictly greater than \(s\).  Thus every boundary-wavelet approximation and H\"older characterization below is licensed for all fixed \(0<\tau<s\).  We first prove the upper inequality uniformly over the entire class \(\mathcal S^{\mathrm{sm}}_{n,d}(s,H,\gamma,\rho)\).

First dispose of the boundary value \(\rho=0\).  For any class member, integrating the Neumann equation exactly as in (5) below gives
\[
\rho_n^2=\int_{\mathcal Z_d}\int_0^1
\frac{\left\{\int_0^y\Delta_n(v,z)\,dv\right\}^2}{\bar q_n(y,z)}\,dy\,dR_n(z).
\]
The integrand is nonnegative and \(\bar q_n>0\).  Thus \(\rho_n=0\) implies \(\int_0^y\Delta_n(v,z)\,dv=0\) for Lebesgue-almost every \((y,z)\); continuity of the class densities and differentiation in \(y\) then imply \(\Delta_n=0\) everywhere.  Hence \(Q_{0,n}(\cdot\mid z)=Q_{1,n}(\cdot\mid z)\), \(\theta_n=0\), and the zero estimator has zero risk.  Both sides of the claimed bracket are zero.  We may therefore assume \(0<\rho\le\rho_{\mathrm{adm}}\) for the remaining construction.

For any member and either arm, integration of the joint-density bounds in \(\mathrm{def{:}positive\text{-}isotropic\text{-}smoothcot\text{-}local\text{-}slice}\) over the unit outcome interval gives
\[
\gamma^{-1}\le r_n(z)=\int_0^1p_{a,n}(y,z)\,dy\le\gamma. \tag{1}
\]
Consequently the conditional densities defined in that same definition satisfy
\[
\gamma^{-2}\le q_{a,n}(y,z)=\frac{p_{a,n}(y,z)}{r_n(z)}\le\gamma^2,
\qquad
\gamma^{-2}\le\bar q_n(y,z)\le\gamma^2. \tag{2}
\]
Every division in (2) is licensed by the positive lower bound in (1).

Let \(F_{a,z}(y)=\int_0^yq_{a,n}(v,z)\,dv\), let \(Q_{a,z}=F_{a,z}^{-1}\), and put \(H_z=F_{1,z}-F_{0,z}\).  Strict positivity in (2) makes both inverses well defined.  The scalar increasing-rearrangement identity used in \(\mathrm{thm{:}conditional\text{-}minimax\text{-}frontier}\) is
\[
W_2^2\{Q_{0,n}(\cdot\mid z),Q_{1,n}(\cdot\mid z)\}
=\int_0^1\{Q_{1,z}(u)-Q_{0,z}(u)\}^2\,du. \tag{3}
\]
If \(u=F_{0,z}(y)\), the mean-value theorem supplies a point \(\zeta_{y,z}\) between \(Q_{0,z}(u)=y\) and \(Q_{1,z}(u)\) such that
\[
H_z(y)=q_{1,n}(\zeta_{y,z},z)\{Q_{0,z}(u)-Q_{1,z}(u)\}. \tag{4}
\]
On the other hand, integrating the defining Neumann equation for \(\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n\), using zero conditional mass and the two Neumann boundary conditions, and then integrating by parts gives
\[
\int_0^1\Delta_n(y,z)\{\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n(\cdot,z)\}(y)\,dy
=\int_0^1\frac{H_z(y)^2}{\bar q_n(y,z)}\,dy. \tag{5}
\]
Changing variables in (3), then applying (2) to (4)--(5), yields
\[
W_2^2\{Q_{0,n}(\cdot\mid z),Q_{1,n}(\cdot\mid z)\}
\le\gamma^8\int_0^1\Delta_n(y,z)\{\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n(\cdot,z)\}(y)\,dy. \tag{6}
\]
The common marginal in the slice definition permits integration against the single measure \(R_n\).  Since \(\rho_n\le\rho\), (6) proves
\[
0\le\theta_n\le\gamma^8\rho_n^2\le\gamma^8\rho^2. \tag{7}
\]
Therefore the zero estimator has uniform root-mean-square error at most \(\gamma^8\rho^2\).

We next control the observable SmoothCOT estimator on the whole slice.  Fix \(0<\tau<s\) and set
\[
\epsilon_\tau=\frac{s-\tau}{2}>0,
\qquad
\alpha_\tau=\tau+\epsilon_\tau=\frac{s+\tau}{2}<s. \tag{8}
\]
On the fixed cube, the definition of the H\"older norm directly implies the continuous embedding from \(\mathcal C^s\) into \(\mathcal C^{\alpha_\tau}\): derivatives through order \(\lfloor\alpha_\tau\rfloor\) and the required difference quotients are among those controlled by the \(\mathcal C^s\) norm.  Hence the slice condition gives
\[
\sup_{a,n}\|p_{a,n}\|_{\mathcal C^{\alpha_\tau}([0,1]^{d+1})}
\le C_{s,d,\tau}H. \tag{9}
\]
Together, the joint positivity in the slice definition and (9) verify the hypotheses of \(\mathrm{lem{:}lin\text{-}gao\text{-}blanchet\text{-}glynn\text{-}smoothcot\text{-}rate}\), with source smoothness \(\tau\) and extra smoothness \(\epsilon_\tau\).  Its printed constant is law-dependent, so the desired uniformity does not follow from the citation alone; we now verify that every law-dependent input used by that result is uniformly bounded by the fixed \((\gamma,H)\) envelope and the fixed bases.

Let \(N=N_n^{\mathrm{sm}}\), let \(J=\lfloor\log_2N/(2\tau+d+1)\rfloor\), and let \(\widetilde p_{a,J}\) be the empirical boundary-wavelet projection in one paired block.  The theorem-level basis premise permits the boundary-wavelet characterization of the H\"older bound (9).  Orthonormality, the resulting coefficient decay, coefficient variances bounded by \(p_{a,n}\le\gamma\), and the fixed compact-support overlap number give
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal S^{\mathrm{sm}}_{n,d}}
E_P\|\widetilde p_{a,J}-p_{a,n}\|_2^2
\le C_{s,d,\tau,\gamma,H,\Psi_y,\Psi_z}
\left\{2^{-2\alpha_\tau J}+\frac{2^{(d+1)J}}{N}\right\}
\lesssim N^{-2a_\tau}. \tag{10}
\]
For the same reason, bounded-summand exponential Markov bounds applied coefficientwise, followed by a union bound over \(O\{2^{(d+1)J}\}\) coefficients and the compact-overlap inequality, give
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal S^{\mathrm{sm}}_{n,d}}
P\!\left(\|\widetilde p_{a,J}-p_{a,n}\|_\infty>\frac{1}{2\gamma}\right)
\le C_{s,d,\tau,\gamma,H,\Psi_y,\Psi_z}N^{-2} \tag{11}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Indeed, the deterministic sup-norm bias is \(O(2^{-\alpha_\tau J})\), while the stochastic scale is
\[
O\!\left[\left\{J2^{(d+1)J}/N\right\}^{1/2}\right]
=O\{J^{1/2}N^{-a_\tau}\}=o(1).
\]
Thus the positive-part normalization in \(\mathrm{def{:}smoothcot\text{-}median\text{-}estimator}\) is inactive with the uniform probability in (11), and every reciprocal-density constant on that event is bounded by a fixed power of \(\gamma\).

For clarity, the comparison of the estimated aligned marginal with the common true marginal also has a law-uniform form.  If \(f(z)=W_2^2\{Q_{0,n}(\cdot\mid z),Q_{1,n}(\cdot\mid z)\}\), if \(\widehat f\) is its block analogue, and if \(\widehat r\) is the pooled block marginal, then the outcome diameter is one and hence
\[
\left|\int\widehat f\,\widehat r-\int f r_n\right|
\le\|\widehat r-r_n\|_1
+2\sum_{a=0}^1\int W_1(\widehat Q_a^z,Q_a^z)\widehat r(z)\,dz. \tag{12}
\]
The first term is bounded by the two joint \(L^2\) errors in (10).  The second is the conditional negative-order term in the cited SmoothCOT argument.  The fixed reciprocal bound, (9)--(11), and (12) are precisely the fixed-ball wavelet-risk, positivity-event, reciprocal-density, and alternative-marginal ingredients tracked in \(\mathrm{lem{:}anisotropic\text{-}smoothcot\text{-}median\text{-}envelope}\).  Consequently the cited block conclusion is uniformized to
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal S^{\mathrm{sm}}_{n,d}(s,H,\gamma,\rho)}
E_P\left|\widehat V_{\ell,\tau,n}^{\mathrm{sm}}-\theta_n\right|
\le C_{s,d,\tau,\gamma,H,\Psi_y,\Psi_z}N^{-a_\tau}. \tag{13}
\]
This step applies to every member of the declared slice, not only to the lower-bound family.

Put \(\delta_N=C_{s,d,\tau,\gamma,H,\Psi_y,\Psi_z}N^{-a_\tau}\).  Markov's inequality applied to (13) shows that a block is bad at threshold \(8\delta_N\) with probability at most \(1/8\).  The product sampling experiment in the slice definition makes the paired blocks independent.  If their median is bad, at least half the blocks are bad, so
\[
P\!\left(
|\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{sm,med}}_{\tau,n}-\theta_n|>8\delta_N
\right)
\le2^{K_n^{\mathrm{sm}}}8^{-(K_n^{\mathrm{sm}}+1)/2}
\le2^{-K_n^{\mathrm{sm}}/2}. \tag{14}
\]
Choose the fixed multiplier in \(K_n^{\mathrm{sm}}\asymp\log n\) so that the last expression is at most \(\delta_N^2\).  Both the estimator and \(\theta_n\) lie in \([0,1]\); therefore
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal S^{\mathrm{sm}}_{n,d}}
E_P(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{sm,med}}_{\tau,n}-\theta_n)^2
\le64\delta_N^2+2^{-K_n^{\mathrm{sm}}/2}
\le65\delta_N^2. \tag{15}
\]
Since \(N_n^{\mathrm{sm}}\asymp n/\log n\), (15) proves the uniform SmoothCOT root-mean-square bound \(C_{\mathrm{sm},\tau}n^{-a_\tau}(\log n)^{a_\tau}\).  The constant depends only on the fixed quantities listed in the proposition, including the fixed boundary-wavelet constants, and is independent of \(n,\rho\), and the model element.  Selecting before observing the data whichever of the zero estimator and the median SmoothCOT estimator has the smaller deterministic guarantee in (7) and (15) proves the upper inequality.

We finally prove the lower inequality by embedding the explicit hard subclass.  In \(\mathrm{def{:}rademacher\text{-}family}\), set its covariate smoothness parameter to \(s_z=s=\beta_{\mathrm{iso}}\), choose
\[
2^{J_z}\asymp n^{2/(4s+d)},
\qquad
b_{n,\omega}(z)=\gamma_n\sum_{k\in\mathcal K_{J_z}}\omega_k\psi^z_{J_z,k}(z),
\]
and use \(r_n\equiv1\) and
\[
p_{a,n,\omega}(y,z)=q_{a,n,\omega}^{\mathrm{lb}}(y,z)
=1+(2a-1)b_{n,\omega}(z)\cos(2\pi y)/2. \tag{16}
\]
The support in (16) is the required scalar-outcome cube, and \(\int_0^1\cos(2\pi y)\,dy=0\), so both covariate marginals are the common uniform density.  Because \(\Psi_z\) has regularity strictly greater than \(s\), its level-\(J_z\) scaling law and the disjoint supports in \(\mathcal K_{J_z}\), together with the amplitude in \(\mathrm{def{:}rademacher\text{-}family}\), give exactly the calculation recorded in \(\mathrm{lem{:}explicit\text{-}mixture\text{-}checks}\):
\[
\|b_{n,\omega}\|_\infty\le C_1c_0 2^{-sJ_z},
\qquad
\|b_{n,\omega}\|_{\mathcal C^s([0,1]^d)}\le C_2c_0. \tag{17}
\]
Because the outcome factor in (16) is fixed and smooth, the product rule for isotropic H\"older norms and (17) imply
\[
\|p_{a,n,\omega}-1\|_{\mathcal C^s([0,1]^{d+1})}
\le C_3c_0. \tag{18}
\]
The slack \(H-\|1\|_{\mathcal C^s}>0\) is fixed.  Choose the single constant \(c_0>0\) so small that \(C_3c_0\le H-\|1\|_{\mathcal C^s}\) and that the pointwise perturbation in (16) is at most \(\min\{1-\gamma^{-1},\gamma-1\}\).  Equations (17)--(18) then give, uniformly in \(n,\rho\), and \(\omega\),
\[
\gamma^{-1}\le p_{a,n,\omega}\le\gamma,
\qquad
\|p_{a,n,\omega}\|_{\mathcal C^s}\le H. \tag{19}
\]
Thus positivity and the fixed isotropic radius are verified with constants independent of the model element.

For (16), \(\bar q_n=1\), \(\Delta_n=b_{n,\omega}\cos(2\pi y)\), and direct solution of the Neumann equation gives
\[
\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n(\cdot,z)
=\frac{b_{n,\omega}(z)}{4\pi^2}\cos(2\pi y).
\]
Orthonormality of the selected covariate wavelets and the radius branch of \(\gamma_n\) therefore yield
\[
\rho_n^2
=\frac{\gamma_n^2M_{J_z}}{8\pi^2}
\le\frac{c_0^2}{8\pi^2}\rho^2
\le\rho^2. \tag{20}
\]
Together, (16), (19), and (20) prove that the null and every sign-paired alternative lie in \(\mathcal S^{\mathrm{sm}}_{n,d}(s,H,\gamma,\rho)\).  This verifies class containment directly; it is not inferred merely from the hard-family definition.

The endpoint and mixture calculations in \(\mathrm{lem{:}explicit\text{-}mixture\text{-}checks}\), based on the second variation in \(\mathrm{lem{:}cosine\text{-}second\text{-}variation}\), give
\[
\theta_{n,\omega}-\theta_{n,0}
\ge c_4\gamma_n^2M_{J_z}
\asymp c_0^2\min\!\left\{\rho^2,n^{-4s/(4s+d)}\right\} \tag{21}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\), while
\[
\chi^2(\overline{\mathbb P}_n,\mathbb P_n^0)
\le\exp\{C_\chi n^2\gamma_n^4M_{J_z}\}-1
\le\eta \tag{22}
\]
for a fixed \(\eta<1\) after decreasing the same fixed \(c_0\).  This choice is uniform because, at the displayed resolution, \(n^2\gamma_n^4M_{J_z}=O(c_0^4)\).  Cauchy--Schwarz applied to the mixture likelihood ratio gives
\[
\|\overline{\mathbb P}_n-\mathbb P_n^0\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\le\frac12\sqrt\eta<1. \tag{23}
\]
Threshold any estimator halfway between the null endpoint and the common lower separation in (21).  On either testing error its absolute estimation error is at least half that separation.  Averaging over the Rademacher alternatives and applying (23), as in the mixture testing argument of \(\mathrm{thm{:}conditional\text{-}minimax\text{-}frontier}\), gives
\[
\inf_{\widetilde\theta_n}
\sup_{P\in\{\mathbb P_n^0\}\cup\{\mathbb P_{n,\omega}:\omega\}}
\{E_P(\widetilde\theta_n-\theta_n)^2\}^{1/2}
\ge c_5\min\!\left\{\rho^2,n^{-4s/(4s+d)}\right\}. \tag{24}
\]
The set in (24) is a subclass of the full slice by (16)--(20), so its minimax lower bound transfers upward.  Substituting \(s=\beta_{\mathrm{iso}}\) completes the lower inequality.  No fixed-frequency regular witness was used, so (24) justifies only the stated quadratic-and-zero lower benchmark and not a \(\rho n^{-1/2}\) term.  The upper and lower exponents need not agree, which proves the asserted honest unmatched bracket.
