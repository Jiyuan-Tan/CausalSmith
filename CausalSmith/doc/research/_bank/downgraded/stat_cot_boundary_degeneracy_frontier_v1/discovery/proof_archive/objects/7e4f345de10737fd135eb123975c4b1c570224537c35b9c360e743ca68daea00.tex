Index an evaluation observation by \(r=(e,a,i)\), and put \(Z_{a,i}=\Phi_{J_y,J_z}(W_{a,i,n})-E\Phi_{J_y,J_z}(W_{a,i,n})\). Because \(n=2m\) and the two fold estimators have weight one half, direct expansion of the canonical correction gives
\[
h_{n,rs}(x,y)=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\frac{2}{m-1}x^\top B_{J_y,J_z,n}y,
&e_r=e_s,\ a_r=a_s,\\[6pt]
\displaystyle-\frac{2}{m}x^\top B_{J_y,J_z,n}y,
&e_r=e_s,\ a_r\ne a_s,\\[6pt]
0,&e_r\ne e_s,
\end{cases}                                                       \tag{1}
\]
and
\[
n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}=\sum_{r<s}h_{n,rs}(Z_r,Z_s).              \tag{2}
\]
Every \(Z_r\) is centered, and the samples are independent by \(\mathrm{ass{:}two\text{-}arm\text{-}sampling}\), so (1) is canonical in either argument.

Write \(M_n=2^{dJ_z}\). The quadratic-form comparison in \(\mathrm{prop{:}anisotropic\text{-}spectrum\text{-}bias}\) factors the uncentered kernel into an outcome inverse-elliptic kernel and the weighted covariate projection kernel, up to uniformly bounded Gram conjugations. The one-dimensional Green kernel is uniformly bounded because \(c\le\bar q_n\le C\). At the finest covariate scaling level, compact support and bounded overlap give
\[
\iint |K_{z,J_z}(z,z')|^2dR_n(z)dR_n(z')\lesssim M_n,
\qquad
\iint |K_{z,J_z}(z,z')|^4dR_n(z)dR_n(z')\lesssim M_n^3.          \tag{3}
\]
Indeed, \(|K_{z,J_z}|\lesssim M_n\) on a set of product measure \(O(M_n^{-1})\), and it vanishes outside a fixed-neighbor enlargement of that set. Centering in either argument adds only one-variable and constant projections, so it does not enlarge the orders in (3). Hence, uniformly over arm choices,
\[
E(Z_r^\top BZ_s)^2\lesssim M_n,
\qquad
E(Z_r^\top BZ_s)^4\lesssim M_n^3.                                \tag{4}
\]

There are \(O(m^2)\) nonzero edges in (1), each with coefficient \(O(m^{-1})\), and \(O(m)\) edges incident to a fixed vertex. Equations (1) and (4) therefore give
\[
\max_r\sum_{s\ne r}Eh_{n,rs}^2\lesssim M_n/m,
\qquad
\sum_{r<s}Eh_{n,rs}^4\lesssim M_n^3/m^2.                         \tag{5}
\]
Pairing equal and reversed edges in (2) gives exactly the trace formula in equation (5) of \(\mathrm{thm{:}uniform\text{-}second\text{-}order\text{-}expansion}\), hence
\[
\operatorname{Var}(n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n})=A_{J_y,J_z,n}^2\{1+o(1)\}. \tag{6}
\]
The spectral proposition also gives
\[
\frac{\operatorname{tr}(K^4)}{\operatorname{tr}(K^2)^2}
\le\frac{\|K\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2}{\|K\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2}lesssim M_n^{-1}. \tag{7}
\]

Let \(G_n=\sum_r\ell_{n,r}(W_r)\) satisfy the lemma's hypotheses and define \(\mathcal D_{n,r}\) as stated. Conditional centering of every edge in (1) gives \(E(\mathcal D_{n,r}\mid\mathcal F_{r-1})=0\) and \(\sum_r\mathcal D_{n,r}=G_n+n\mathbb U\). By \(x^2\mathbf1\{|x|>\epsilon S_n\}\le x^4/(\epsilon^2S_n^2)\), expansion of fourth moments, (5), and (7),
\[
\sum_rE\left[\left(\frac{\mathcal D_{n,r}}{S_n}\right)^2
\mathbf1\{|\mathcal D_{n,r}|>\epsilon S_n\}\right]
\lesssim_\epsilon m^{-1}+\frac{M_n}{m^2}+M_n^{-1}.               \tag{8}
\]
Here the bounded linear summands contribute \(O(m^{-1})\); terms with one linear and one quadratic factor are bounded by Cauchy--Schwarz; two-edge contractions are controlled by (5); and the only four-edge cycle is (7). Since \(D_{J_y,J_z}=o(n^2)\) implies \(M_n=o(m^2)\), while \(J_z\to\infty\) gives \(M_n\to\infty\), the right side of (8) tends to zero.

For the predictable variance, expand
\[
\sum_rE(\mathcal D_{n,r}^2\mid\mathcal F_{r-1})                 \tag{9}
\]
and subtract its expectation. Terms with disjoint index sets vanish by independence and canonicality. A shared single vertex contributes at most \(CM_n/m^2\) after division by \(S_n^4\); repeated-edge terms contribute at most \(C/m\); the fourth-order cycle is bounded by (7); and mixed linear--quadratic contractions are bounded by the geometric mean of the corresponding linear variance and these quadratic contractions. Therefore
\[
\frac{E\left[\left\{(9)-E(G_n+n\mathbb U)^2\right\}^2\right]}{S_n^4}
\lesssim m^{-1}+\frac{M_n}{m^2}+M_n^{-1}+o(1)\to0.              \tag{10}
\]
Equations (1)--(10) explicitly establish the fourth moment, maximal influence, Lindeberg, and predictable conditional-variance bounds, rather than importing them from a changing-kernel theorem.
