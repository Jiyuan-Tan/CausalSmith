Work under the proposed local and scale conditions.  The expansion remainder is \(o_P(S_n/n)\), because each bias is \(O(S_n/n)\) and the stochastic part has scale \(S_n/n\).  The local condition \(\|\Delta_n\|_\infty\to0\), the quantile differentiation in the cosine lemma, and the wavelet approximation bound imply that the first Hoeffding projection differs in \(L^2\) by \(o(S_n/\sqrt n)\) from the linearization \(2H_{J_y,J_z,n}\delta_{J_y,J_z,n}\).  Thus it suffices to analyze the oracle quadratic statistic and this aligned linear term.

On fold \(e\), put
\[
 Z_{e,n}=\Gamma_{J_y,J_z,n}^{-1/2}\sqrt m
 \{(\overline\Phi_{1,e}-\overline\Phi_{0,e})-\delta_{J_y,J_z,n}\}.
\]
It is centered with identity covariance.  Diagonal removal changes the quadratic form only by its trace centering.  Hence the normalized oracle fold contribution equals, up to \(o_P(S_n)\),
\[
 Z_{e,n}^{\top}KZ_{e,n}-\operatorname{tr}K
 +2\sqrt m\,\delta^{\top}H\Gamma^{1/2}Z_{e,n}.          \tag{1}
\]
Diagonalize \(K=\sum_k\nu_kv_kv_k^\top\).  The definition of \(\eta_{k,n}\) turns (1), with a standard Gaussian vector in place of \(Z_{e,n}\), into
\[
 \sum_k\nu_k\{(\xi_{e,k}+\eta_{k,n})^2-(1+\eta_{k,n}^2)\}. \tag{2}
\]

To justify the replacement, truncate (1) to its first \(K_0\) eigenvectors.  For fixed \(K_0\), the multivariate Lindeberg argument applies because the observations are independent, the densities are bounded, and every retained wavelet coordinate has a finite uniform fourth moment.  The variance of the discarded centered quadratic part is \(2\sum_{k>K_0}\nu_k^2\), and that of its discarded linear part is \(4\sum_{k>K_0}\nu_k^2\eta_{k,n}^2\).  First let \(n\to\infty\), then \(K_0\to\infty\); the spectral count, \(D=o(n^2)\), and the normalization by \(S_n\) make both tails uniformly negligible.  The bounded-Lipschitz triangle inequality proves the displayed triangular approximation.  The two independent folds produce the repeated spectrum and \(A^2=4\sum_k\nu_k^2\).

At equality \(\eta_{k,n}=0\).  If \(J_z\to\infty\), the spectrum proposition gives
\[
 \frac{\max_k|\nu_k|}{(\sum_k\nu_k^2)^{1/2}}\to0.
\]
The Lyapunov calculation for the independent variables \(\nu_k(\xi_{e,k}^2-1)\) then yields the stated standard normal limit.  If instead the padded weight and shift arrays converge in \(\ell^2\), construct all proxies with the same Gaussian array.  Their \(L^2\) distance converges to zero by independence and the identity \(\operatorname{Var}(\xi^2-1)=2\), proving the subsequential chaos assertion.

Under \(D\log n/m=o(1)\), the pilot-control argument and the multiplicative quadratic-form comparison strengthen operator consistency to \(\|\widehat K-K\|_{\mathrm{HS}}=o_P(\|K\|_{\mathrm{HS}})\); Hoffman--Wielandt then permits the observable spectral replacement.  Finally, in the regular zone, linear dominance gives \(nV_{L,n}\to\infty\).  The centered scores are uniformly bounded on the compact support, so Lindeberg's condition holds.  The assumed variance consistency, bias condition, and Slutsky's lemma give the final studentized normal limit.  This regular argument does not identify its linear coefficient with the equality Hessian and therefore remains valid at fixed unequal laws.
