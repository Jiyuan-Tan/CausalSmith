For (e\in\{1,2\}), let \(\mathscr F_{-e}\) be the sigma-field generated by the two pilot folds \(I_{0,3-e}\) and \(I_{1,3-e}\).  By \(\mathrm{ass{:}two\text{-}arm\text{-}sampling}\) and the deterministic split in \(\mathrm{def{:}anisotropic\text{-}estimator}\), the observations indexed by \(I_{0,e}\cup I_{1,e}\) are independent of \(\mathscr F_{-e}\).  Hence, conditionally on \(\mathscr F_{-e}\), the pilot densities, score, and Hessian in fold \(e\) are fixed.

We first record the exact algebraic decomposition.  In every factor \(X_{a,i}^{(-e)}\) in the diagonal-free correction, add and subtract the true feature mean \(\mu_{a}=E_{P_{a,n}}\{\Phi_{J_y,J_z}(W_{a,1,n})\}\).  Collecting terms with exactly one centered evaluation observation gives the first Hoeffding projection, and collecting products of two centered observations gives the canonical second projection.  Taylor expansion of the conditional transport functional through second order, followed by projection onto \(\Lambda_{J_y,J_z}^{(d)}\), then gives
\[
\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n
=\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}+\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}
+B^y_{J_y,n}+B^z_{J_z,n}+R_{J_y,J_z,n}.                                  \tag{1}
\]
This step uses only the displayed estimator in \(\mathrm{def{:}anisotropic\text{-}estimator}\): the Hoeffding split is obtained by expanding products, and \(R_{J_y,J_z,n}\) is, by construction, the sum of the pilot-density, ridge, and operator-replacement terms.

We next determine the linear scale.  Replace the opposite-fold pilots in the first-order score by \(q_{0,n},q_{1,n},r_n\).  Positivity in \(\mathrm{ass{:}conditional\text{-}density\text{-}bounds}\) makes each conditional distribution function strictly increasing and its inverse \(c^{-1}\)-Lipschitz.  Differentiating the one-dimensional inverse-cdf representation of squared Wasserstein distance is therefore legitimate.  Centering the resulting two conditional Kantorovich derivatives gives the oracle first projection in (1).  If \(H_z(y)=\int_0^y\Delta_n(v,z)\,dv\) and \(u_z=\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n(\cdot,z)\), the Neumann equation and the zero conditional mass of \(\Delta_n\) imply
\[
-\bar q_n(y,z)\partial_yu_z(y)=H_z(y),
\qquad
\int_0^1\Delta_n(y,z)u_z(y)\,dy
=\int_0^1\frac{H_z(y)^2}{\bar q_n(y,z)}\,dy.                              \tag{2}
\]
The bounds \(c\le q_{a,n},\bar q_n\le C\), together with centering of the two arm scores, identify their two-sample squared \(L^2\) norm up to constants depending only on \(c,C\) with the integral of (2) against the common marginal \(R_n\) from \(\mathrm{ass{:}shared\text{-}covariate\text{-}marginal}\).  By the definition of \(\rho_n\),
\[
n\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n})\asymp \rho_n^2,
\qquad
\operatorname{sd}(\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n})\asymp\frac{\rho_n}{\sqrt n}.    \tag{3}
\]
At conditional equality, \(\Delta_n=0\); the conditional transport value and its centered first derivative are then zero, so \(\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}=0\).

For the quadratic scale, put
\[
Z_{a,i}=\Phi_{J_y,J_z}(W_{a,i,n})-E_{P_{a,n}}\Phi_{J_y,J_z}(W_{a,i,n}),
\quad H=H_{J_y,J_z,n},\quad \Gamma_a=\Gamma_{a,J_y,J_z,n},
\quad \Gamma=\Gamma_0+\Gamma_1.
\]
The oracle canonical term in one evaluation fold is
\[
\frac{1}{m(m-1)}\sum_{a=0}^1\sum_{i\ne j}Z_{a,i}^{\top}HZ_{a,j}
-\frac{2}{m^2}\sum_{i\in I_{1,e}}\sum_{j\in I_{0,e}}Z_{1,i}^{\top}HZ_{0,j}. \tag{4}
\]
Independence and centering make a covariance between two summands in (4) zero unless their ordered index pairs coincide or are reversed.  Counting those pairs, and then averaging the two oracle fold terms, which depend on disjoint independent folds, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n})
&=\frac{\operatorname{tr}\{(H\Gamma_0)^2\}+\operatorname{tr}\{(H\Gamma_1)^2\}}{m(m-1)}
+\frac{2\operatorname{tr}(H\Gamma_0H\Gamma_1)}{m^2}\\
&=\frac{4\operatorname{tr}\!\left[\{\Gamma^{1/2}H\Gamma^{1/2}\}^2\right]}{n^2}\{1+O(m^{-1})\}\\
&=\frac{4\sum_k\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}^2}{n^2}\{1+O(m^{-1})\}
=\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}^2}{n^2}\{1+o(1)\}.                                \tag{5}
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(n=2m\), the middle identity uses \(K_{J_y,J_z,n}=\Gamma^{1/2}H\Gamma^{1/2}\), and the only finite-sample discrepancy is the factor \(m/(m-1)\) in the within-arm contractions.  Thus the asymptotic second-order standard-deviation scale is \(A_{J_y,J_z,n}/n\), as asserted.  Compact wavelet support and the fixed density bounds make the fourth-moment contractions uniform; \(D_{J_y,J_z}=o(n^2)\) makes the corresponding diagonal-removal error negligible uniformly over the model class.

The deterministic terms in (1) are controlled by the already established \(\mathrm{prop{:}anisotropic\text{-}spectrum\text{-}bias}\):
\[
|B^y_{J_y,n}|\lesssim2^{-2(s_y+1)J_y},
\qquad
|B^z_{J_z,n}|\lesssim2^{-2s_zJ_z}.                                      \tag{6}
\]
The already established \(\mathrm{lem{:}observable\text{-}pilot\text{-}control}\), together with \(\lambda_n=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\) from \(\mathrm{def{:}pilot\text{-}tuning\text{-}and\text{-}studentizer}\), gives
\[
R_{J_y,J_z,n}
=O_P\!\left\{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^3
+\rho_n^{\,2/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^2\right\}
+o_P(1)\left\{\frac{\rho_n}{\sqrt n}+\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}}n\right\}.   \tag{7}
\]
By the hypothesis of the theorem, the first brace in (7) is little-oh of
\[
\frac{\rho_n}{\sqrt n}+\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}}n
+2^{-2(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-2s_zJ_z};                                         \tag{8}
\]
the second term in (7) is also little-oh of (8).  Substituting (6)--(7) into (1) proves the displayed uniform expansion.  Equations (3) and (5) give its linear and quadratic fluctuation scales, and the equality conclusion follows from the paragraph after (3).

Finally, at the resolutions in \(\mathrm{def{:}anisotropic\text{-}tuning}\), that definition and \(\mathrm{prop{:}anisotropic\text{-}spectrum\text{-}bias}\) yield
\[
\frac{A_{J_y^*,J_z^*,n}}n
\asymp\frac{2^{dJ_z^*/2}}n
\asymp2^{-2s_zJ_z^*}
\asymp2^{-2(s_y+1)J_y^*}
\asymp r_{n,d}(s_z).                                                      \tag{9}
\]
Applying the \(L^2\) triangle inequality to (1), and then using (3), (5)--(7), and (9), gives the root-mean-square scale
\[
\frac{\rho_n}{\sqrt n}+r_{n,d}(s_z)
+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^3
+\rho_n^{\,2/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^2.
\]
If the last two terms are no larger than the first two, this is of order \(\rho_n/\sqrt n+r_{n,d}(s_z)\).  The covariance \(\Gamma_{J_y,J_z,n}\) was used only to express the population variance and spectrum in (5) and (9); it is not an input to the point estimator in \(\mathrm{def{:}anisotropic\text{-}estimator}\).  Hence no covariance estimate enters the point-estimator expansion.
