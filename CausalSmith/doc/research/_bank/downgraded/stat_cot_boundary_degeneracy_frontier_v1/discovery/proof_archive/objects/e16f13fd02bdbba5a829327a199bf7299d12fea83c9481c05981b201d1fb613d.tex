Write \(\Phi=\Phi_{J_y,J_z}\), \(G=G_{R,J_y,J_z,n}\), \(H=H^{\mathrm{form}}_{J_y,J_z,n}\), \(B=B_{J_y,J_z,n}\), and \(K=K_{J_y,J_z,n}\).  By \(\mathrm{ass{:}wavelet\text{-}regularity}\), the tensor wavelets are Lebesgue-orthonormal.  Hence, for every coefficient vector \(x\),
\[
x^{\top}Gx=\int (x^{\top}\Phi)^2r_n\,dy\,dz,
\qquad c_G\|x\|_2^2\le x^{\top}Gx\le C_G\|x\|_2^2. \tag{1}
\]
For \(d\ge1\), (1) follows from \(\mathrm{ass{:}covariate\text{-}density\text{-}bounds}\); for \(d=0\), it follows from \(r_n\equiv1\).  Because the weight depends only on \(z\), outcome orthogonality gives \(G=I_y\otimes G_z\), so \(G^{-1}\) preserves each outcome level.

For \(g=x^{\top}\Phi\), put \(u_z=\mathcal G_{n,z}g(\cdot,z)\).  Every retained outcome feature has zero outcome integral.  The weak Neumann equation and its boundary conditions therefore give
\[
\int_0^1g(y,z)u_z(y)\,dy
=\int_0^1\bar q_n(y,z)|u_z'(y)|^2\,dy. \tag{2}
\]
If \(v\in H^1(0,1)\) has zero mean, the fundamental theorem of calculus and Cauchy--Schwarz give \(\|v\|_2\le\|v'\|_2\).  The variational characterization of (2), together with \(c\le\bar q_n\le C\) from \(\mathrm{ass{:}conditional\text{-}density\text{-}bounds}\), yields, uniformly in \(z\),
\[
\int_0^1g\,\mathcal G_{n,z}g\,dy
\asymp \|g(\cdot,z)\|_{H^{-1}(0,1)}^2. \tag{3}
\]
The antiderivative of a normalized boundary wavelet at level \(j_y\) has squared \(L^2\)-norm comparable to \(2^{-2j_y}\).  Compact support gives a uniformly bounded overlap number, including the finite boundary block.  Applying (3) level by level and integrating in \(z\) proves
\[
x^{\top}Hx\asymp
\sum_{j_y,k_y,\gamma}2^{-2j_y}x_{j_y,k_y,\gamma}^2. \tag{4}
\]
Equations (1), (4), and the fact that \(G^{-1}\) preserves outcome levels give
\[
x^{\top}Bx=(G^{-1}x)^{\top}H(G^{-1}x)
\asymp\sum_{j_y,k_y,\gamma}2^{-2j_y}x_{j_y,k_y,\gamma}^2. \tag{5}
\]

For \(f=x^{\top}\Phi\), the covariance identity is
\[
x^{\top}\Gamma_{a,J_y,J_z,n}x
=\inf_{v\in\mathbb R}\int(f-v)^2q_{a,n}r_n\,dy\,dz. \tag{6}
\]
Since \(\int f(y,z)dy=0\) for every \(z\), Lebesgue orthogonality gives \(\int(f-v)^2dy\,dz=\|x\|_2^2+v^2\).  Applying the density bounds in (6), and then summing over the two arms, proves \(c_\Gamma I\preceq\Gamma_{J_y,J_z,n}\preceq C_\Gamma I\).

Let \(w_\lambda=2^{-2j_y(\lambda)}\), ordered decreasingly.  Congruence by \(\Gamma^{1/2}\), (5), and the min--max characterization applied to the spans of the largest reference weights and their orthogonal complements give
\[
\underline c\,w_k^\downarrow\le\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}
\le\overline C\,w_k^\downarrow,
\qquad 1\le k\le D_{J_y,J_z}. \tag{7}
\]
Taking superlevel sets proves the two asserted counting inequalities.  There are \(\asymp2^{j_y+dJ_z}\) retained indices at outcome level \(j_y\); hence, on the threshold range in the proposition,
\[
N_{J_y,J_z,n}(u)
\asymp2^{dJ_z}\!\sum_{j_0\le j_y\le
\min\{J_y,\frac12\log_2(u^{-1})+O(1)\}}2^{j_y}
\asymp\min\{D_{J_y,J_z},2^{dJ_z}u^{-1/2}\}. \tag{8}
\]
Inverting (7)--(8), with the finite endpoint blocks handled directly, gives
\[
\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}\asymp\min\{1,(2^{dJ_z}/k)^2\}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\sum_{k=1}^{D_{J_y,J_z}}\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}^2
\asymp2^{dJ_z}+2^{4dJ_z}\sum_{k>2^{dJ_z}}k^{-4}
\asymp2^{dJ_z}. \tag{9}
\]
Equations (7)--(9) prove all stated operator, Hilbert--Schmidt, and maximal-eigenvalue conclusions.  When \(d=0\), the covariate multiplicity in (9) is one, so the Hilbert--Schmidt norm stays bounded above and away from zero.

It remains to control every retained--omitted energy cross term.  Let \(P_y\) be the outcome projector and \(P_z^R\) the \(L^2(dR_n)\)-orthogonal covariate projector.  The tensor Gram formula in \(\mathrm{def{:}anisotropic\text{-}projection}\) implies
\[
\Pi_{J_y,J_z}=P_yP_z^R=P_z^RP_y. \tag{10}
\]
Put
\[
p=P_yP_z^R\Delta_n,
\qquad r_y=(I-P_y)\Delta_n,
\qquad r_z=P_y(I-P_z^R)\Delta_n.
\]
Then \(\Delta_n=p+r_z+r_y\) and \(P_y\Delta_n=p+r_z\).

At resolution \(J_z\), write the covariate scaling-space Gram matrix as
\[
G_z=\bigl(\langle\phi_{J_z,k},r_n\phi_{J_z,l}\rangle\bigr)_{kl}.
\]
Compact support makes \(G_z\) banded in the finite-neighbor lattice metric, whereas the density bounds give \(cI\preceq G_z\preceq CI\).  With \(\alpha_0=2/(c+C)\) and \(R_z^0=I-\alpha_0G_z\),
\[
\|R_z^0\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\vartheta:=\frac{C-c}{C+c}<1,
\qquad
G_z^{-1}=\alpha_0\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(R_z^0)^j.
\]
Because \((R_z^0)^j_{kl}=0\) whenever the lattice distance between \(k\) and \(l\) exceeds a fixed multiple of \(j\), the geometric tail gives
\[
|(G_z^{-1})_{kl}|\le C_1\vartheta^{c_1\operatorname{dist}(k,l)}. \tag{11}
\]
Thus the row sums of \(G_z^{-1}\) are uniformly bounded.  Applying the weighted projector to a scaling-space approximant and using (11) yields, for every \(z\)-H\"older function in a fixed ball,
\[
\|f-P_z^Rf\|_{L^2(dR_n)}+\|f-P_z^Rf\|_\infty
\lesssim2^{-s_zJ_z},
\qquad
\|P_z^Rf\|_{\mathcal C^{s_z}}\lesssim1. \tag{12}
\]
The same localization argument is valid for Banach-valued coefficient functions and for boundary scaling functions.  The unweighted outcome projector has the analogous stability and approximation properties.

By \(\mathrm{ass{:}anisotropic\text{-}conditional\text{-}smoothness}\), (12) and the outcome wavelet approximation bounds imply
\[
\|r_y\|_2\lesssim2^{-s_yJ_y},
\qquad
\|r_y\|_{H_y^{-1}}\lesssim2^{-(s_y+1)J_y},
\qquad
\|r_z\|_2\lesssim2^{-s_zJ_z}. \tag{13}
\]
The localization in (11) also makes \(P_z^R\) uniformly bounded on the Banach space of \(y\)-sections with the \(\mathcal C^{s_y}\)-norm.  It commutes with outcome derivatives and outcome difference quotients, while \(P_y\) is uniformly bounded on the same outcome H\"older class.  Therefore
\[
\sup_{z\in\mathcal Z_d}\|r_z(\cdot,z)\|_{\mathcal C^{s_y}(\mathcal Y)}
\le C,
\qquad
\int_0^1r_z(y,z)dy=0. \tag{14}
\]
This is the required uniform outcome-H\"older stability of the covariate residual.

The same projector stability applied to \(p=P_yP_z^R\Delta_n\) gives a uniformly bounded anisotropic H\"older norm for \(p\).  Thus both \(p\) and \(r_z\) have uniformly bounded outcome \(\mathcal C^{s_y}\)-norm.  For \(f\in\{p,r_z\}\), let \(u_f=\mathcal G_nf\) and \(H_f(y,z)=\int_0^yf(v,z)dv\).  Integrating the Neumann equation and using the zero outcome mass gives
\[
\partial_yu_f(y,z)=-\frac{H_f(y,z)}{\bar q_n(y,z)}. \tag{15}
\]
The denominator is at least \(c\) by \(\mathrm{ass{:}conditional\text{-}density\text{-}bounds}\).  The antiderivative supplies one additional outcome order, and products and reciprocals preserve the declared H\"older order under the fixed positive bounds.  The outcome Jackson bound and (14)--(15) consequently yield, for both choices of \(f\),
\[
\|(I-P_y)\mathcal G_nf\|_2
\lesssim2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}. \tag{16}
\]
In particular, (16) with \(f=r_z\) is
\[
\|(I-P_y)\mathcal G_nr_z\|_2
\lesssim2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}. \tag{17}
\]
For \(f=p\), the same reciprocal stability in the covariate coordinate and (12) additionally give
\[
\|(I-P_z^R)P_y\mathcal G_np\|_2\lesssim2^{-s_zJ_z}. \tag{18}
\]

Define the symmetric bilinear form
\[
a(f,g)=\int_{\mathcal Z_d}\int_{\mathcal Y}
f(y,z)\{\mathcal G_{n,z}g(\cdot,z)\}(y)\,dy\,dR_n(z).
\]
Self-adjointness of the Neumann inverse and ordinary projection orthogonality give the exact identities
\[
a(p,r_y)=\langle(I-P_y)\mathcal G_np,r_y\rangle_2,
\qquad
a(r_z,r_y)=\langle(I-P_y)\mathcal G_nr_z,r_y\rangle_2,
\]
\[
a(p,r_z)=\langle(I-P_z^R)P_y\mathcal G_np,r_z\rangle_2. \tag{19}
\]
Equations (13), (16)--(19), and Cauchy--Schwarz prove
\[
|a(p,r_y)|\lesssim2^{-(2s_y+1)J_y},
\qquad
|a(r_z,r_y)|\lesssim2^{-(2s_y+1)J_y},
\qquad
|a(p,r_z)|\lesssim2^{-2s_zJ_z}. \tag{20}
\]
By (3) and (13),
\[
a(r_y,r_y)\lesssim2^{-2(s_y+1)J_y},
\qquad
a(r_z,r_z)\lesssim2^{-2s_zJ_z}. \tag{21}
\]

The sequential gaps have the exact decompositions
\[
\begin{aligned}
B^y_{J_y,n}
&=a(\Delta_n,\Delta_n)-a(P_y\Delta_n,P_y\Delta_n)\\
&=2a(p,r_y)+2a(r_z,r_y)+a(r_y,r_y),
\end{aligned} \tag{22}
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
B^z_{J_z,n}
&=a(P_y\Delta_n,P_y\Delta_n)-a(p,p)\\
&=2a(p,r_z)+a(r_z,r_z).
\end{aligned} \tag{23}
\]
The middle term in (22) is precisely the retained--omitted cross term omitted by the earlier decomposition.  Equations (20)--(23) show
\[
|B^y_{J_y,n}|\lesssim2^{-(2s_y+1)J_y},
\qquad
|B^z_{J_z,n}|\lesssim2^{-2s_zJ_z}. \tag{24}
\]
These identities also show directly that the two quantities are energy-approximation gaps, not terms in the mean of the exact-\(\theta_n\) estimator.  Every constant used above depends only on the fixed support, density, H\"older-radius, and wavelet constants, which proves the asserted uniformity.
