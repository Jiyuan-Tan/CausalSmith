Outcome smoothing and covariate rank play different roles. The inverse Neumann operator adds one outcome derivative, but the ordinary \(L^2\) projection is not energy-orthogonal, so its retained--omitted cross term sets the outcome bias at \(2^{-(2s_y+1)J_y}\), not the squared-tail order. Covariate smoothness controls both the quadratic minimax exponent and whether the growing spectrum becomes negligible relative to a regular first-order term. Equality for \(d\ge1\) Gaussianizes because covariate rank diverges; equality for \(d=0\) retains a trace-class quadratic-process law.
