\section*{stat\_\allowbreak{}cot\_\allowbreak{}boundary\_\allowbreak{}degeneracy\_\allowbreak{}frontier}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} For \(d\ge1\), conditional squared Wasserstein distance has an anisotropic boundary geometry: inverse ellipticity smooths outcome frequencies while covariate resolution replicates the ordered spectrum. The sign-paired family supplies the zero and quadratic lower components, while a separate fixed-frequency, constant-in-\(z\) two-point family supplies the regular \(\rho n^{-1/2}\) component; together they yield the intrinsic lower frontier \(\min\{\rho^2,\rho n^{-1/2}+n^{-4s_z/(4s_z+d)}\}\), while the zero estimator, the explicit Gram-corrected estimator, median SmoothCOT, and, when available, median direct-COT give a computable observable upper envelope; the nontrivial quadratic zone remains an honest unmatched bracket. Under the theorem's stated triangular conditions, the infeasible oracle-score, population-kernel statistic has Gaussian limits for \(d\ge1\); no observable-experiment Gaussianization or calibration is claimed. The separate \(d=0\) branch recovers the two-sample del Barrio--Gin\'e--Utzet law and \(n^{-1}\) scale. Uniform mixed-regime calibration remains open.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Published SmoothCOT theory supplies global estimation bounds and a regular Gaussian analysis, but it does not give the equality-local conditional minimax converse, a uniform fixed-radius transfer of its law-dependent constant, or one observable calibration valid through null, local, critical, and regular regimes. Unconditional Wasserstein laws and fixed-entropic causal transport do not resolve these conditional growing-rank questions.

\paragraph{Niche.} The missing object is a primitive smoothness-by-equality frontier separating inverse-elliptic outcome smoothing from covariate rank, together with a recognizable positive isotropic slice on which published SmoothCOT upper theory and the conditional sign-mixture lower construction can be compared under an explicit common wavelet basis condition.

\paragraph{Fill.} The project derives the inverse-elliptic spectrum and approximation bounds, constructs the foldwise clipped ridge-Galerkin statistic, proves an intrinsic sign-mixture and two-point lower frontier, and supplies a deterministic minimum of explicit Gram, SmoothCOT, direct-COT, and zero-estimator upper guarantees. These results form an honest two-sided bracket rather than a matched conditional rate in the nontrivial quadratic zone. It also proves the stated Gaussian limits for the infeasible oracle-score, population-kernel experiment, recovers the exact no-covariate quadratic-process law, and isolates observable all-regime calibration as unresolved.

\section{Related work}

Lin, Gao, Blanchet, and Glynn's SmoothCOT Section 3.3 gives quadratic stability, Theorem 3.2 gives the regular Gaussian law, Remark 3.2 records faster-than-root-\(n\) convergence at equality, and Appendix C.4 proves the regular CLT. Their aligned-wavelet result supplies the SmoothCOT upper candidate used here, and their direct-COT Theorem 4 supplies a separate global upper candidate under Wasserstein--1 Lipschitz conditional kernels; neither result supplies the equality-local conditional converse, the present anisotropic two-sided bracket, or an observable calibration uniform through degeneracy. Niles-Weed and Berthet provide negative-order wavelet geometry; Manole and Niles-Weed provide unconditional separation-sensitive rates. Target (estimand / structural parameter): \(\theta_n=\int_{\mathcal Z_d}W_2^2(Q_{0,n}(\cdot\mid z),Q_{1,n}(\cdot\mid z))dR_n(z)\).
Primary functional / estimator / diagnostic: Put \(a_y=\min\{s_y,1\}\). For \(d\ge1\), use the corrected pilot-error balancing resolutions
\[
2^{J_z^*}\asymp\{n\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\}^{2/(2s_z+d)},
\qquad
2^{J_y^*}\asymp\{n\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\}^{2s_z/\{(s_y+1)(2s_z+d)\}}.
\]
The explicit Gram-corrected estimator \(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y^*,J_z^*,n}\) has local RMSE at most
\[
\rho n^{-1/2}
+n^{-2s_z/(2s_z+d)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{d/(2s_z+d)}
+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{2+a_y}
+\rho^{2a_y/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^2.
\]
For every fixed \(0<\tau<\min\{s_y,s_z\}\), the explicit median-aggregated aligned wavelet estimator \(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{sm,med}}_{\tau,n}\) has RMSE \(O_\tau\{n^{-a_\tau}(\log n)^{a_\tau}\}\), where \(a_\tau=\tau/(2\tau+d+1)\). When \(s_z\ge1\), \(\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{dir,med}}\) additionally has RMSE \(O\{n^{-1/(d+2)}(\log n)^{1+1/(d+2)}\}\). The certified upper map is the deterministic minimum of these guarantees and the zero-estimator guarantee \(O(\rho^2)\). The sign mixture and fixed-frequency two-point construction give the intrinsic lower frontier
\[
\min\{\rho^2,\rho n^{-1/2}+n^{-4s_z/(4s_z+d)}\}.
\]
The optimized observable Gram scale is strictly slower than \(n^{-4s_z/(4s_z+d)}\), so the conditional result is an honest two-sided bracket rather than a matched rate in the nontrivial quadratic zone.  The quantities \(B^y_{J_y,n}\) and \(B^z_{J_z,n}\) are true-discrepancy energy-approximation gaps and are not estimator biases or centering terms. For \(d=0\), \(\widehat\theta^0_{J_y^0,n}\) has local RMSE \(\min\{\rho^2,\rho n^{-1/2}+n^{-1}\}\)

Fix the compactly supported boundary-corrected bases \(\Psi_y\) and \(\Psi_z\) used below with regularity strictly greater than \(\beta_{\mathrm{iso}}\). On the additional recognizable positive isotropic slice \(\mathcal S^{\mathrm{sm}}_{n,d}(\beta_{\mathrm{iso}},H,\gamma,\rho)\), for every fixed \(0<\tau<\beta_{\mathrm{iso}}\), the same causal endpoint obeys the transferred minimax root-mean-square bracket
\[
c_{\mathrm{sm}}\min\!\left\{\rho^2,n^{-4\beta_{\mathrm{iso}}/(4\beta_{\mathrm{iso}}+d)}\right\}
\le \inf_{\widetilde\theta_n}\sup_{P\in\mathcal S^{\mathrm{sm}}_{n,d}}
\{E_P(\widetilde\theta_n-\theta_n)^2\}^{1/2}
\le C_{\mathrm{sm},\tau}\min\!\left\{\rho^2,n^{-a_\tau}(\log n)^{a_\tau}\right\}.
\]
The upper side uses the existing zero and median-aggregated aligned wavelet SmoothCOT estimators; the quadratic-and-zero lower side uses the existing sign-paired family. This broader-class bracket is not asserted to be tight in its nontrivial quadratic zone..

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:two-arm-sampling]\label{ass:two-arm-sampling}
For every \(n\), the observations \(W_{a,1,n},\ldots,W_{a,n,n}\) are i.i.d. from \(P_{a,n}\) within each \(a\in\mathcal A\), and the two arm samples are independent.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Independent randomized-arm sampling, \cite{LinGaoBlanchetGlynn2026SmoothCOT}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:shared-covariate-marginal]\label{ass:shared-covariate-marginal}
The \(\mathcal Z_d\)-marginal of \(P_{a,n}\) equals \(R_n\) for every \(a\in\mathcal A\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Common-marginal conditional transport, \cite{LinGaoBlanchetGlynn2026SmoothCOT}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:covariate-density-bounds]\label{ass:covariate-density-bounds}
For \(d\ge1\), the common marginal density satisfies \(c\le r_n(z)\le C\) for every \(z\in\mathcal Z_d\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Bounded positive covariate density, \cite{LinGaoBlanchetGlynn2026SmoothCOT}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:conditional-density-bounds]\label{ass:conditional-density-bounds}
The conditional densities satisfy \(c\le q_{a,n}(y,z)\le C\) for every \(a\in\mathcal A\) and \((y,z)\in\mathcal Y\times\mathcal Z_d\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniformly positive bounded transport densities, \cite{LinGaoBlanchetGlynn2026SmoothCOT}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:covariate-holder-smoothness]\label{ass:covariate-holder-smoothness}
For \(d\ge1\), \(r_n\in\mathcal C^{s_z}(\mathcal Z_d;L)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Covariate H\"older density smoothness, \cite{NilesWeedBerthet2022}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:anisotropic-conditional-smoothness]\label{ass:anisotropic-conditional-smoothness}
Each \(q_{a,n}\) belongs to the tensor anisotropic ball \(\mathcal C^{s_y,s_z}(\mathcal Y\times\mathcal Z_d;L)\), with the \(z\)-condition omitted when \(d=0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Tensor anisotropic H\"older smoothness, \cite{CohenDaubechiesVial1993}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:local-equality-radius]\label{ass:local-equality-radius}
The inverse-elliptic discrepancy satisfies \(\rho_n\le\rho\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Shrinking local neighborhood, \cite{ChenFang2019}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:wavelet-regularity]\label{ass:wavelet-regularity}
The bases \(\Psi_y\) and \(\Psi_z\) are compactly supported boundary wavelets with regularities strictly greater than \(s_y+1\) and \(s_z\), respectively.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Boundary-corrected wavelet regularity, \cite{CohenDaubechiesVial1993}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:holder-radius-interior]\label{ass:holder-radius-interior}
The H\"older radius satisfies \(L>1\), so the uniform density is an interior point of the declared H\"older balls.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} standard
\end{assumption}

\section{Definitions}

\begin{definition}[def:whitened-spectral-array]\label{def:whitened-spectral-array}

B_{J_y,J_z,n}=G_{R,J_y,J_z,n}^{-1}H^{\mathrm{form}}_{J_y,J_z,n}G_{R,J_y,J_z,n}^{-1}.

A_{J_y,J_z,n}=\left\{4\sum_k\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}^2\right\}^{1/2}.

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \(P_{a,n}\); \(\Phi_{J_y,J_z}\); \(\mathcal G_{n,z}\); \(R_n\); \(\delta_{J_y,J_z,n}\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:anisotropic-estimator]\label{def:anisotropic-estimator}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Observable equal-\allowbreak{}weight two-\allowbreak{}fold anisotropic second-\allowbreak{}order estimator}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Partition each arm into deterministic folds \(I_{a,1}\) and \(I_{a,2}\), each of size \(m=n/2\). \widehat{\mathcal G}_{n,z}^{(-e)}=
\left[P_{J_y^{\mathrm{op}}}\{-\partial_y(\widehat{\bar q}_n^{(-e)}(\cdot,z)\partial_y)\}P_{J_y^{\mathrm{op}}}+\lambda_n I\right]^{-1}P_{J_y^{\mathrm{op}}}.

\widehat G_{R,J_y,J_z,n}^{(-e)}

\]
\[
\widehat H_{\mathrm{form},J_y,J_z,n;\lambda\mu}^{(-e)}

and the raw-moment-coordinate Hessian
\[
\widehat B_{J_y,J_z,n}^{(-e)}
=\{\widehat G_{R,J_y,J_z,n}^{(-e)}\}^{-1}
\widehat H_{\mathrm{form},J_y,J_z,n}^{(-e)}
\{\widehat G_{R,J_y,J_z,n}^{(-e)}\}^{-1}.

Let \(\widehat f_n^{(-e)}(z)=W_2^2(\widehat q_{0,n}^{(-e)}(\cdot,z)dy,\widehat q_{1,n}^{(-e)}(\cdot,z)dy)\), let \(\widehat\ell_{a,n}^{(-e)}\) be its observable one-dimensional Kantorovich derivative in arm \(a\), and let \(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{plug}}^{(-e)}=\int\widehat f_n^{(-e)}(z)\widehat r_n^{(-e)}(z)dz\). \[
\widehat\theta_{e,J_y,J_z,n}=\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{plug}}^{(-e)}+\sum_{a=0}^1\frac1m\sum_{i\in I_{a,e}}\widehat s_{a,n}^{(-e)}(W_{a,i,n})+\widehat U_{e,J_y,J_z,n},

\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}=\frac12\sum_{e=1}^2\widehat\theta_{e,J_y,J_z,n}.
\]
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \(W_{a,i,n}\); \(J_y\); \(J_y^{\mathrm{op}}\); \(J_z\); \(\Psi_y\); \(\Psi_z\).
\end{definition}

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Fix \(d\ge1\), \(\beta_{\mathrm{iso}}>0\), \(\gamma>1\), a finite constant \(H>\|1\|_{\mathcal C^{\beta_{\mathrm{iso}}}([0,1]^{d+1})}\) whose positive slack above the displayed norm is held fixed, and a fixed admissible ceiling \(\rho_{\mathrm{adm}}\in(0,1]\). \section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:cosine-second-variation]\label{lem:cosine-second-variation}
For \(q_{0,n}(y,z)=1-b(z)\cos(2\pi y)/2\) and \(q_{1,n}(y,z)=1+b(z)\cos(2\pi y)/2\), uniformly as \(\|b\|_{\infty}\to0\),
\[
\theta_n=\frac{1}{8\pi^2}\int_{\mathcal Z_d}b(z)^2dR_n(z)+O(\|b\|_{\infty}^3).
\]
More generally, let \(q(y,z)\) be a common conditional density satisfying \(c\le q(y,z)\le C\), and let \(h\) be a bounded admissible direction satisfying
\[
\int_{\mathcal Y}h(y,z)dy=0\quad\text{for }R_n\text{-almost every }z,
\qquad K_h:=\|h/q\|_{\infty}<\infty.
\]
For \(q_{0,t}=q-th/2\), \(q_{1,t}=q+th/2\), and \(|t|<2/K_h\), with \(2/K_h=\infty\) when \(K_h=0\), both paths are positive normalized conditional densities. If \(\theta_n(t)\) is the corresponding conditional quadratic transport endpoint, then
\[
\theta_n'(0)=0,
\qquad
\frac12\theta_n''(0)
=\int_{\mathcal Z_d}\int_{\mathcal Y}
h(y,z)\{\mathcal G_{n,z}h(\cdot,z)\}(y)dydR_n(z).
\]
The displayed quadratic form extends continuously from bounded admissible directions to \(L_0^2(\mathcal Y\times\mathcal Z_d,dy\,dR_n)\) in the \(L^2\) topology; no Fr\'echet derivative on an \(L^2\)-neighborhood of densities is asserted. In particular, the scalar path \(q_{0,n}=1\), \(q_{1,n}=1+t b(z)\cos(2\pi y)\) has second derivative \((4\pi^2)^{-1}\int b^2dR_n\) at \(t=0\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(K_h=\|h/q\|_{\infty}\).  If \(K_h=0\), then \(h=0\) almost everywhere, both paths coincide, and every asserted derivative is zero.  Hence suppose first that \(K_h>0\) and \(|t|K_h<2\).  The conditional-density lower bound in \(\mathrm{ass{:}conditional\text{-}density\text{-}bounds}\) gives, for either sign,
\[
q(y,z)\left\{1\pm\frac{t h(y,z)}{2q(y,z)}\right\}
\ge c\left(1-\frac{|t|K_h}{2}\right)>0.
\tag{1}
\]
Thus \(q_{0,t}=q-th/2\) and \(q_{1,t}=q+th/2\) are positive.  Since \(q(\cdot,z)\) is a conditional density and the locally quantified admissibility condition gives \(\int_{\mathcal Y}h(y,z)dy=0\),
\[
\int_{\mathcal Y}q_{a,t}(y,z)dy
=\int_{\mathcal Y}q(y,z)dy+\frac{(2a-1)t}{2}
  \int_{\mathcal Y}h(y,z)dy
=1
\tag{2}
\]
for \(a\in\{0,1\}\) and \(R_n\)-almost every \(z\).  Equations (1)--(2) prove the path assertion.  Together with \(\mathrm{ass{:}shared\text{-}covariate\text{-}marginal}\), they also ensure that its two arm laws retain the common marginal \(R_n\).  The convention \(2/K_h=\infty\) covers the already-settled case \(K_h=0\).

Fix a covariate value \(z\) for which (2) holds, suppress \(z\) temporarily, and define
\[
F(y)=\int_0^yq(x,z)dx,
\qquad
H_h(y)=\int_0^yh(x,z)dx.
\tag{3}
\]
Then \(H_h(0)=H_h(1)=0\), and the two perturbed distribution functions are
\[
F_{0,t}=F-\frac{t}{2}H_h,
\qquad
F_{1,t}=F+\frac{t}{2}H_h.
\tag{4}
\]
The bounds \(c\le q\le C\) in \(\mathrm{ass{:}conditional\text{-}density\text{-}bounds}\) imply
\[
c|y-y'|\le |F(y)-F(y')|\le C|y-y'|.
\tag{5}
\]
Consequently \(F\) is a continuous strictly increasing bijection of \([0,1]\), and its inverse \(Q=F^{-1}\) is \(c^{-1}\)-Lipschitz.  Let \(Q_{a,t}=F_{a,t}^{-1}\).  Applying (5) to the inverse identities from (4) yields
\[
\sup_{u\in[0,1]}|Q_{a,t}(u)-Q(u)|
\le \frac{|t|}{2c}\|H_h\|_{\infty}.
\tag{6}
\]

For the arm-one inverse, (4) gives
\[
F\{Q_{1,t}(u)\}-F\{Q(u)\}
=-\frac{t}{2}H_h\{Q_{1,t}(u)\}.
\tag{7}
\]
At every \(u\) for which \(Q(u)\) is a Lebesgue point of \(q(\cdot,z)\), (6), the absolute continuity of \(F\), and the defining property of a Lebesgue point show that the secant quotient of the left side of (7) converges to \(q\{Q(u),z\}\).  Dividing (7) by \(t\), and using the continuity of the absolutely continuous function \(H_h\), gives
\[
\frac{Q_{1,t}(u,z)-Q(u,z)}{t}
\longrightarrow
-\frac{H_h\{Q(u,z),z\}}{2q\{Q(u,z),z\}}.
\tag{8}
\]
The same calculation for arm zero gives
\[
\frac{Q_{0,t}(u,z)-Q(u,z)}{t}
\longrightarrow
\frac{H_h\{Q(u,z),z\}}{2q\{Q(u,z),z\}}.
\tag{9}
\]
The exceptional set in (8)--(9) has Lebesgue measure zero in \(u\): the non-Lebesgue points form a null set in \(y\), and its image under the absolutely continuous map \(F\) has measure \(\int q(y,z)\mathbf 1_N(y)dy=0\).  Moreover, (6) supplies the uniform domination
\[
\left|\frac{Q_{1,t}(u,z)-Q_{0,t}(u,z)}{t}\right|
\le \frac{\|H_h(\cdot,z)\|_{\infty}}{c}
\le \frac{\|h\|_{\infty}}{c}.
\tag{10}
\]

For completeness, the one-dimensional squared-transport formula used next follows by an elementary uncrossing argument.  Whenever \(x\le x'\) and \(y\le y'\), direct expansion gives
\[
(x-y)^2+(x'-y')^2
\le (x-y')^2+(x'-y)^2.
\tag{11}
\]
For finitely supported laws, repeatedly replacing crossed pairs by the ordered pairs in (11) produces the increasing coupling without increasing cost.  Approximating arbitrary laws on the compact interval by their equal-mass quantile discretizations, whose squared costs converge by bounded dominated convergence, proves
\[
W_2^2(Q_{0,t},Q_{1,t})
=\int_0^1\{Q_{1,t}(u)-Q_{0,t}(u)\}^2du.
\tag{12}
\]
Using (8)--(10) in (12), first applying dominated convergence in \(u\) and then in \(z\) (the latter is valid because \(R_n\) is a probability law), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\lim_{t\to0}\frac{\theta_n(t)}{t^2}
&=\int_{\mathcal Z_d}\int_0^1
  \frac{H_h\{Q(u,z),z\}^2}{q\{Q(u,z),z\}^2}
  \,du\,dR_n(z)\\
&=\int_{\mathcal Z_d}\int_0^1
  \frac{H_h(y,z)^2}{q(y,z)}\,dy\,dR_n(z),
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
where the second equality is the monotone change of variables \(u=F(y,z)\), \(du=q(y,z)dy\).  Since \(\theta_n(0)=0\), (13) yields the pathwise first and second directional derivatives
\[
\theta_n'(0)=0,
\qquad
\frac12\theta_n''(0)
=\int_{\mathcal Z_d}\int_0^1
  \frac{H_h(y,z)^2}{q(y,z)}\,dy\,dR_n(z).
\tag{14}
\]

It remains to identify (14) with the declared inverse-elliptic form.  Along the symmetric path, the reference density is exactly \(\bar q_n=(q_{0,t}+q_{1,t})/2=q\).  Define, up to its unique additive normalization,
\[
u_h(y,z)=-\int_0^y\frac{H_h(v,z)}{q(v,z)}dv.
\tag{15}
\]
Because \(q\ge c\) and \(H_h\) is bounded, \(u_h(\cdot,z)\in H^1(0,1)\).  Equations (3) and (15) imply in the weak sense
\[
q(y,z)\partial_yu_h(y,z)=-H_h(y,z),
\qquad
-\partial_y\{q(y,z)\partial_yu_h(y,z)\}=h(y,z).
\tag{16}
\]
The endpoint equalities for \(H_h\) give the two Neumann conditions.  Subtracting the Lebesgue mean of (15) therefore makes \(u_h=\mathcal G_{n,z}h\) by the definition and normalization of \(\mathcal G_{n,z}\).  Integration by parts in the weak equation, with the boundary term zero by (16), now gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^1h(y,z)\{\mathcal G_{n,z}h\}(y)dy
&=\int_0^1q(y,z)|\partial_yu_h(y,z)|^2dy\\
&=\int_0^1\frac{H_h(y,z)^2}{q(y,z)}dy.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
Integrating (17) against \(R_n\) proves the inverse-elliptic identity in the statement.

We next prove precisely the asserted extension, without asserting differentiability on an \(L^2\)-open density neighborhood.  On the conditional zero-mass subspace, let
\[
(Vg)(y,z)=\int_0^yg(x,z)dx.
\tag{18}
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz followed by Fubini gives
\[
\|Vg\|_{L^2(dy\,dR_n)}^2
\le \|g\|_{L^2(dy\,dR_n)}^2.
\tag{19}
\]
Therefore the bilinear form
\[
\mathcal B(g_1,g_2)
=\int_{\mathcal Z_d}\int_0^1
  \frac{(Vg_1)(y,z)(Vg_2)(y,z)}{q(y,z)}dy\,dR_n(z)
\tag{20}
\]
satisfies, by \(q\ge c\) and (19),
\[
|\mathcal B(g_1,g_2)|
\le c^{-1}\|g_1\|_{L^2(dy\,dR_n)}
              \|g_2\|_{L^2(dy\,dR_n)}.
\tag{21}
\]
Bounded conditional zero-mass directions are dense in this subspace.  Indeed, for \(g\) in the subspace let \(g_M=(-M)\vee(g\wedge M)\), let \(\bar g_M(z)=\int_0^1g_M(y,z)dy\), and put \(g_M^0=g_M-\bar g_M\).  Then \(g_M^0\) is bounded and has conditional zero mass, while Jensen's inequality and \(\int g(\cdot,z)=0\) give
\[
\|\bar g_M\|_{L^2(dR_n)}
\le\|g_M-g\|_{L^2(dy\,dR_n)},
\qquad
\|g_M^0-g\|_{L^2(dy\,dR_n)}
\le2\|g_M-g\|_{L^2(dy\,dR_n)}\to0.
\tag{22}
\]
Equations (20)--(22) uniquely and continuously extend the quadratic form \(\mathcal B(g,g)\), which equals (17) on bounded admissible directions, to all of \(L_0^2(\mathcal Y\times\mathcal Z_d,dy\,dR_n)\).  This establishes exactly the extension clause and no stronger density-cone differentiability claim.

Finally, compute the two announced constants.  For the symmetric cosine pair, fix \(z\), put \(e=b(z)/2\), and write
\[
F_{1,e}(y)=y+\frac{e}{2\pi}\sin(2\pi y),
\qquad
F_{0,e}(y)=y-\frac{e}{2\pi}\sin(2\pi y).
\tag{23}
\]
When \(\|b\|_{\infty}\) is sufficiently small, the derivatives in (23) are uniformly bounded away from zero.  The inverse identities imply
\[
F_{1,e}^{-1}(u)
=u-\frac{e}{2\pi}\sin(2\pi u)+O(e^2),
\qquad
F_{0,e}^{-1}(u)
=u+\frac{e}{2\pi}\sin(2\pi u)+O(e^2),
\tag{24}
\]
uniformly in \(u\) and \(z\).  To verify the remainder directly, substitute the inverse identity into the left side and use the unit Lipschitz bound for \(x\mapsto\sin(2\pi x)/(2\pi)\), together with \(|F_{a,e}^{-1}(u)-u|\le |e|/(2\pi)\).  Subtracting (24) yields
\[
F_{1,e}^{-1}(u)-F_{0,e}^{-1}(u)
=-\frac{b(z)}{2\pi}\sin(2\pi u)+O\{|b(z)|^2\}.
\tag{25}
\]
The left side is \(O\{|b(z)|\}\), so (12), (25), and \(\int_0^1\sin^2(2\pi u)du=1/2\) give, uniformly in \(z\),
\[
W_2^2\{Q_{0,n}(\cdot\mid z),Q_{1,n}(\cdot\mid z)\}
=\frac{b(z)^2}{8\pi^2}+O\{|b(z)|^3\}.
\tag{26}
\]
Since \(R_n\) is a probability law, integration of (26) proves
\[
\theta_n
=\frac{1}{8\pi^2}\int_{\mathcal Z_d}b(z)^2dR_n(z)
 +O(\|b\|_{\infty}^3).
\tag{27}
\]

For the one-sided path \(q_{0,n}=1\), \(q_{1,n}=1+t b(z)\cos(2\pi y)\), the arm-one distribution function is
\[
F_{1,t}(y,z)=y+\frac{t b(z)}{2\pi}\sin(2\pi y),
\tag{28}
\]
while the arm-zero quantile is \(u\).  The same direct inverse calculation as in (24) gives
\[
F_{1,t}^{-1}(u,z)-u
=-\frac{t b(z)}{2\pi}\sin(2\pi u)+O\{t^2b(z)^2\}.
\tag{29}
\]
Squaring and integrating as above yields
\[
\theta_n(t)
=\frac{t^2}{8\pi^2}\int_{\mathcal Z_d}b(z)^2dR_n(z)+o(t^2).
\tag{30}
\]
Therefore its second derivative at zero is
\[
\theta_n''(0)=\frac{1}{4\pi^2}
\int_{\mathcal Z_d}b(z)^2dR_n(z),
\tag{31}
\]
which is the final coefficient claimed.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The exact coefficient fixes the Hessian convention, the lower-bound separation, and the null-law constants. \textit{Gap.} LinGaoBlanchetGlynn2026SmoothCOT stops at a vanishing first derivative, while GoldmanHuesmannOtto2021 supplies deterministic Poisson linearization but not this conditional statistical second variation. \textit{Consumer.} The sign-paired lower-bound family and the \(\mathsf{STAR}\) simulation benchmark use this coefficient.

\begin{proposition}[prop:anisotropic-spectrum-bias]\label{prop:anisotropic-spectrum-bias}
Uniformly over \(\mathcal M_{n,d}(s_y,s_z,\rho)\), there are fixed constants \(0<c_H<C_H<\infty\) and \(0<c_\Gamma<C_\Gamma<\infty\) such that, if \(g=\sum_{(j_y,k_y,\gamma)\in\Lambda_{J_y,J_z}^{(d)}}x_{j_y,k_y,\gamma}\psi^y_{j_y,k_y}\psi^z_\gamma\), then
\[
c_H\sum_{j_y,k_y,\gamma}2^{-2j_y}x_{j_y,k_y,\gamma}^2
\le\int_{\mathcal Z_d}\int_{\mathcal Y}g(y,z)\{\mathcal G_{n,z}g(\cdot,z)\}(y)dydR_n(z)
\le C_H\sum_{j_y,k_y,\gamma}2^{-2j_y}x_{j_y,k_y,\gamma}^2,
\]
and \(c_\Gamma I\preceq\Gamma_{J_y,J_z,n}\preceq C_\Gamma I\). Consequently there are fixed \(0<\underline c<\overline C<\infty\) such that, for every \(u>0\),
\[
\left|\left\{(j_y,k_y,\gamma)\in\Lambda_{J_y,J_z}^{(d)}:\underline c\,2^{-2j_y}\ge u\right\}\right|
\le N_{J_y,J_z,n}(u)
\le
\left|\left\{(j_y,k_y,\gamma)\in\Lambda_{J_y,J_z}^{(d)}:\overline C\,2^{-2j_y}\ge u\right\}\right|.
\]
Writing \(j_0\) for the fixed coarsest retained outcome level, throughout the nonempty range \(\overline C2^{-2J_y}\le u\le\underline c2^{-2j_0}\),
\[
N_{J_y,J_z,n}(u)\asymp\min\{D_{J_y,J_z},2^{dJ_z}u^{-1/2}\},
\qquad
\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}\asymp\min\{1,(2^{dJ_z}/k)^2\},\quad 1\le k\le D_{J_y,J_z}.
\]
Consequently, for \(d\ge1\),
\[
\|K_{J_y,J_z,n}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\asymp1,
\qquad \|K_{J_y,J_z,n}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\asymp2^{dJ_z},
\qquad
\frac{\max_k|\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}|}{\{\sum_k\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}^2\}^{1/2}}
\lesssim2^{-dJ_z/2}.
\]
The sequential full-minus-projected inverse-elliptic energy gaps, including their retained--omitted cross terms, satisfy
\[
|B^y_{J_y,n}|\lesssim2^{-(2s_y+1)J_y},
\qquad
|B^z_{J_z,n}|\lesssim2^{-2s_zJ_z}.
\]
They compare the true discrepancy energy with its outcome and weighted-covariate projections; they are not biases of \(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}\). When \(d=0\), the Hilbert--Schmidt norm remains bounded above and away from zero as \(J_y\to\infty\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write \(\Phi=\Phi_{J_y,J_z}\), \(G=G_{R,J_y,J_z,n}\), \(H=H^{\mathrm{form}}_{J_y,J_z,n}\), \(B=B_{J_y,J_z,n}\), and \(K=K_{J_y,J_z,n}\).  The tensor wavelets are Lebesgue-orthonormal by \(\mathrm{ass{:}wavelet\text{-}regularity}\).  Hence, for every coefficient vector \(x\),
\[
x^{\top}Gx=\int (x^{\top}\Phi)^2r_n\,dy\,dz,
\qquad c_G\|x\|_2^2\le x^{\top}Gx\le C_G\|x\|_2^2. \tag{1}
\]
For \(d\ge1\), (1) follows from \(\mathrm{ass{:}covariate\text{-}density\text{-}bounds}\); for \(d=0\), it follows from \(r_n\equiv1\).  Since the weight depends only on \(z\), outcome orthogonality gives \(G=I_y\otimes G_z\), so \(G^{-1}\) preserves every outcome level.

For \(g=x^{\top}\Phi\), put \(u_z=\mathcal G_{n,z}g(\cdot,z)\).  Each retained outcome feature has zero outcome integral.  The weak Neumann equation and its boundary conditions give
\[
\int_0^1g(y,z)u_z(y)\,dy
=\int_0^1\bar q_n(y,z)|u_z'(y)|^2\,dy. \tag{2}
\]
For a zero-mean \(v\in H^1(0,1)\), the fundamental theorem of calculus and Cauchy--Schwarz give \(\|v\|_2\le\|v'\|_2\).  The variational characterization of (2), together with \(c\le\bar q_n\le C\) from \(\mathrm{ass{:}conditional\text{-}density\text{-}bounds}\), therefore yields, uniformly in \(z\),
\[
\int_0^1g\,\mathcal G_{n,z}g\,dy\asymp
\|g(\cdot,z)\|_{H^{-1}(0,1)}^2. \tag{3}
\]
The antiderivative of a normalized boundary wavelet at level \(j_y\) has squared \(L^2\)-norm comparable to \(2^{-2j_y}\).  Compact support gives a uniformly bounded overlap number, including the finite boundary block.  Applying (3) level by level and integrating in \(z\) proves
\[
x^{\top}Hx\asymp
\sum_{j_y,k_y,\gamma}2^{-2j_y}x_{j_y,k_y,\gamma}^2. \tag{4}
\]
Equations (1), (4), and preservation of outcome levels by \(G^{-1}\) give
\[
x^{\top}Bx=(G^{-1}x)^{\top}H(G^{-1}x)
\asymp\sum_{j_y,k_y,\gamma}2^{-2j_y}x_{j_y,k_y,\gamma}^2. \tag{5}
\]

For \(f=x^{\top}\Phi\), covariance admits the identity
\[
x^{\top}\Gamma_{a,J_y,J_z,n}x
=\inf_{v\in\mathbb R}\int(f-v)^2q_{a,n}r_n\,dy\,dz. \tag{6}
\]
Because \(\int f(y,z)dy=0\) for every \(z\), Lebesgue orthogonality gives \(\int(f-v)^2dy\,dz=\|x\|_2^2+v^2\).  The density bounds in (6), followed by summation over arms, prove \(c_\Gamma I\preceq\Gamma_{J_y,J_z,n}\preceq C_\Gamma I\).

Let \(w_\lambda=2^{-2j_y(\lambda)}\), ordered decreasingly.  Congruence by \(\Gamma^{1/2}\), (5), and the min--max characterization applied to the spans of the largest reference weights and their orthogonal complements give
\[
\underline c\,w_k^\downarrow\le\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}
\le\overline C\,w_k^\downarrow,
\qquad1\le k\le D_{J_y,J_z}. \tag{7}
\]
Taking superlevel sets proves the two counting inequalities.  There are \(\asymp2^{j_y+dJ_z}\) retained indices at outcome level \(j_y\); hence, throughout the asserted threshold range,
\[
N_{J_y,J_z,n}(u)
\asymp2^{dJ_z}\!\sum_{j_0\le j_y\le
\min\{J_y,\frac12\log_2(u^{-1})+O(1)\}}2^{j_y}
\asymp\min\{D_{J_y,J_z},2^{dJ_z}u^{-1/2}\}. \tag{8}
\]
Inverting (7)--(8), with the finite endpoint blocks handled directly, gives
\[
\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}\asymp\min\{1,(2^{dJ_z}/k)^2\}.
\]
Consequently
\[
\sum_{k=1}^{D_{J_y,J_z}}\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}^2
\asymp2^{dJ_z}+2^{4dJ_z}\sum_{k>2^{dJ_z}}k^{-4}
\asymp2^{dJ_z}. \tag{9}
\]
Equations (7)--(9) prove the operator, Hilbert--Schmidt, and maximal-eigenvalue assertions.  For \(d=0\), the covariate multiplicity in (9) is one, so the Hilbert--Schmidt norm remains bounded above and away from zero.

It remains to retain every cross term.  Let \(P_y\) be the outcome projector and \(P_z^R\) the \(L^2(dR_n)\)-orthogonal covariate projector.  The tensor Gram formula in \(\mathrm{def{:}anisotropic\text{-}projection}\) gives
\[
\Pi_{J_y,J_z}=P_yP_z^R=P_z^RP_y. \tag{10}
\]
Put \(p=P_yP_z^R\Delta_n\), \(r_y=(I-P_y)\Delta_n\), and \(r_z=P_y(I-P_z^R)\Delta_n\).  Thus \(\Delta_n=p+r_z+r_y\).  In the covariate scaling basis, the Gram matrix is banded by compact support and obeys \(cI\preceq G_z\preceq CI\).  With \(\alpha_0=2/(c+C)\) and \(R_z^0=I-\alpha_0G_z\),
\[
\|R_z^0\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\vartheta:=\frac{C-c}{C+c}<1,
\qquad G_z^{-1}=\alpha_0\sum_{j\ge0}(R_z^0)^j.
\]
Bandedness of \((R_z^0)^j\) and the geometric tail imply
\[
|(G_z^{-1})_{kl}|\le C_1\vartheta^{c_1\operatorname{dist}(k,l)}. \tag{11}
\]
Thus the weighted projector is uniformly local and bounded on the relevant scalar and Banach-valued H\"older classes.  Boundary-wavelet Jackson bounds consequently give
\[
\|r_y\|_2\lesssim2^{-s_yJ_y},\quad
\|r_y\|_{H_y^{-1}}\lesssim2^{-(s_y+1)J_y},\quad
\|r_z\|_2\lesssim2^{-s_zJ_z}, \tag{12}
\]
and \(p,r_z\) have uniformly bounded outcome \(\mathcal C^{s_y}\)-norms.  If \(f\in\{p,r_z\}\), then, writing \(H_f(y,z)=\int_0^yf(v,z)dv\), the Neumann equation gives
\[
\partial_y\mathcal G_nf(y,z)=-H_f(y,z)/\bar q_n(y,z). \tag{13}
\]
The lower density bound, reciprocal H\"older stability, and the extra order furnished by the antiderivative imply
\[
\|(I-P_y)\mathcal G_nf\|_2\lesssim2^{-(s_y+1)J_y},
\qquad
\|(I-P_z^R)P_y\mathcal G_np\|_2\lesssim2^{-s_zJ_z}. \tag{14}
\]

For the symmetric bilinear form \(a(f,g)=\int f\mathcal G_ng\,dy\,dR_n\), self-adjointness and projection orthogonality give
\[
a(p,r_y)=\langle(I-P_y)\mathcal G_np,r_y\rangle,quad
a(r_z,r_y)=\langle(I-P_y)\mathcal G_nr_z,r_y\rangle,
\]
\[
a(p,r_z)=\langle(I-P_z^R)P_y\mathcal G_np,r_z\rangle. \tag{15}
\]
Equations (12)--(15), Cauchy--Schwarz, and (3) give
\[
|a(p,r_y)|+|a(r_z,r_y)|\lesssim2^{-(2s_y+1)J_y},
\qquad |a(p,r_z)|+a(r_z,r_z)\lesssim2^{-2s_zJ_z}, \tag{16}
\]
and \(a(r_y,r_y)\lesssim2^{-2(s_y+1)J_y}\).  Finally,
\[
B^y_{J_y,n}=2a(p,r_y)+2a(r_z,r_y)+a(r_y,r_y),
\qquad
B^z_{J_z,n}=2a(p,r_z)+a(r_z,r_z). \tag{17}
\]
Equations (16)--(17) prove the two claimed envelopes, including the retained--omitted cross terms.  Since (17) compares full and projected energies, it also proves that these quantities are approximation gaps rather than biases of the exact-target estimator.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The proposition proves the ordered spectrum and both retained--omitted energy cross terms while explicitly classifying \(B^y_{J_y,n}\) and \(B^z_{J_z,n}\) as approximation gaps. \textit{Gap.} The variable-coefficient inverse prevents ordinary projection orthogonality from eliminating the energy cross terms. \textit{Consumer.} The pilot-error projection proof reuses this geometry; no exact-target expansion treats these gaps as biases.

\begin{lemma}[lem:observable-pilot-control]\label{lem:observable-pilot-control}
Let \(d\ge1\) and put \(a_y=\min\{s_y,1\}\). For the opposite-fold estimator and the declared pilot resolutions, uniformly over \(\mathcal M_{n,d}(s_y,s_z,\rho)\),
\[
\max_e\left[\|\widehat r_n^{(-e)}-r_n\|_\infty+\max_a\|\widehat q_{a,n}^{(-e)}-q_{a,n}\|_\infty\right]=O_P(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}),
\qquad
P\left(\bigcap_{e=1}^2\mathcal E_n^{(-e)}\right)\to1.
\]
The same bound holds in every fixed \(L^k\) norm.  The clipping is inactive on the displayed event, and conditional normalization is exact everywhere. The fixed density bounds imply
\[
\frac{c}{4C}I\preceq G_{R,J_y,J_z,n},\widehat G_{R,J_y,J_z,n}^{(-e)}\preceq\frac{4C}{c}I
\]
for every fold and every realization. Moreover,
\[
\max_e\left[
\|\widehat G_{R,J_y,J_z,n}^{(-e)}-G_{R,J_y,J_z,n}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
+\|\{\widehat G_{R,J_y,J_z,n}^{(-e)}\}^{-1}-G_{R,J_y,J_z,n}^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
+\|\widehat B_{J_y,J_z,n}^{(-e)}-B_{J_y,J_z,n}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\right]
=O_P(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{a_y}+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}+\lambda_n).
\]
Conditionally on an opposite fold, the exact von Mises identity has the following sign: the conditional mean of the diagonal-free quadratic correction is the observed projected ridge-Galerkin energy of the actual arm-versus-pilot discrepancy error, whereas the second-order Taylor term for \(\theta_n\) is its full exact pilot-density energy.  Consequently their leading contribution to \(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n\) is the negative of the full-minus-projected pilot-error energy, plus ridge-Galerkin and H\"older Hessian-variation remainders.  This contribution is zero for an oracle pilot.  Quantitatively, with \(\mathbb R_{J_y,J_z,n}=\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n-\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}-\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}\),
\[
\|\mathbb R_{J_y,J_z,n}\|_2
\lesssim
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\{2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-s_zJ_z}\}
+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{2+a_y}
+\rho_n^{2a_y/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^2
+o\!\left\{\frac{\rho_n}{\sqrt n}+\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}}n\right\}.
\]
In particular, no term \(B^y_{J_y,n}+B^z_{J_z,n}\) occurs in the exact-\(\theta_n\) expansion. If additionally \(D_{J_y,J_z}\log n/m=o(1)\), then
\[
\max_e\|\widehat\Gamma_{J_y,J_z,n}^{(-e)}-\Gamma_{J_y,J_z,n}\|_{\mathrm{op}}=o_P(1),
\qquad
\max_e\|\widehat K_{J_y,J_z,n}^{(-e)}-K_{J_y,J_z,n}\|_{\mathrm{op}}=o_P(1),
\]
and \(\max_e\|\widehat K_{J_y,J_z,n}^{(-e)}-K_{J_y,J_z,n}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}=o_P(\|K_{J_y,J_z,n}\|_{\mathrm{HS}})\). This extra covariance condition is required only when the population triangular proxy is replaced by its observed spectral plug-in, not for the point estimator.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(p_{a,n}=q_{a,n}r_n\), \(\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\), and \(\bar D_n=2^{\bar J_y+d\bar J_z}\).  Product stability of anisotropic H\"older classes and the pilot resolutions give
\[
\max_a\|p_{a,n}-p_{a,n}^{\mathrm{proj}}\|_\infty
+\|r_n-r_n^{\mathrm{proj}}\|_\infty
\lesssim2^{-s_y\bar J_y}+2^{-s_z\bar J_z}\lesssim\varepsilon. \tag{1}
\]
For every retained wavelet coefficient, the centered empirical summands are bounded.  Expanding their exponential moments and using \(e^v-1-v\le v^2e^{|v|}/2\), followed by a union bound and finite wavelet overlap, gives uniformly
\[
P\{E_n>C(\varepsilon+x)\}\le C\exp(-cnx^2/\bar D_n),\qquad0\le x\le1, \tag{2}
\]
where \(E_n\) is the maximum, over arms and folds, of the raw joint- and marginal-pilot sup-norm errors.  Since \(\bar D_n\asymp(n/\log n)^{1/(2\beta+1)}\), (2) gives \(E_n=O_P(\varepsilon)\), and tail integration gives \(\|E_n\|_{L^k}\lesssim_k\varepsilon\) for every fixed \(k\).

On \(\widetilde r\ge c/2\),
\[
\frac{\widetilde p}{\widetilde r}-\frac pr
=\frac{\widetilde p-p}{\widetilde r}
+p\frac{r-\widetilde r}{r\widetilde r}. \tag{3}
\]
Equations (2)--(3) and the density lower bounds prove the conditional-pilot rate and \(P(\cap_e\mathcal E_n^{(-e)})\to1\).  Conditional normalization is exact everywhere.  Since normalized clipping gives \(c/(4C)\le\widehat r_n^{(-e)}\le4C/c\), orthonormality yields the deterministic Gram bounds.  Moreover,
\[
\|\widehat G-G\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\|\widehat r-r\|_\infty,
\qquad \widehat G^{-1}-G^{-1}=\widehat G^{-1}(G-\widehat G)G^{-1}, \tag{4}
\]
which proves both Gram perturbation assertions.

Let \(A_z=-\partial_y(\bar q_n\partial_y)\), let \(A_z^{\mathrm{op}}=P_{J_y^{\mathrm{op}}}A_zP_{J_y^{\mathrm{op}}}\), and use hats for the pilot forms.  The true and clipped pilot bounds give uniform coercivity on the zero-mean space.  The weak forms and (2) imply
\[
\|\widehat A_z^{\mathrm{op}}-A_z^{\mathrm{op}}\|_{H^1/\mathbb R\to H^{-1}}
\lesssim E_n. \tag{5}
\]
The resolvent identity gives an \(O_P(E_n+\lambda_n)\) perturbation in energy norm.  If \(u=A_z^{-1}f\), then \(u'=-F/\bar q_n\), where \(F(y)=\int_0^yf(v)dv\).  Since \(\bar q_n^{-1}\) is uniformly \(a_y\)-H\"older, Galerkin orthogonality and the oversampling rule give, for retained \(f,g\),
\[
|\langle f,\{(A_z^{\mathrm{op}})^{-1}P_{J_y^{\mathrm{op}}}-A_z^{-1}\}g\rangle|
\lesssim2^{-2a_yJ_y^{\mathrm{op}}}\|f\|_2\|g\|_2
\le\varepsilon^{a_y}\|f\|_2\|g\|_2. \tag{6}
\]
Integrating against \(\widehat r\) and expanding both Gram inverses in (4) proves
\[
\|\widehat B^{(-e)}-B\|_{\mathrm{op}}
=O_P(\varepsilon^{a_y}+\varepsilon+\lambda_n). \tag{7}
\]

Fix a fold \(e\) and condition on its pilot sigma-field.  Define \(d\widehat P_{a,e}^{\dagger}=\widehat q_{a,n}^{(-e)}\widehat r_n^{(-e)}dy\,dz\), \(\dot P_{a,e}=P_{a,n}-\widehat P_{a,e}^{\dagger}\), and
\[
\zeta_e(y,z)=
\frac{r_n(z)\Delta_n(y,z)-\widehat r_n^{(-e)}(z)
(\widehat q_{1,n}^{(-e)}-\widehat q_{0,n}^{(-e)})(y,z)}
{\widehat r_n^{(-e)}(z)}. \tag{8}
\]
All divisions are valid by clipping, and \(\int\zeta_e(y,z)dy=0\).  If \(d_e\) is the retained-moment vector of \(\dot P_{1,e}-\dot P_{0,e}\), the two Gram conjugations give
\[
d_e^\top\widehat B^{(-e)}d_e
=\widehat a_e^{\mathrm{op}}(\widehat\Pi_e\zeta_e,widehat\Pi_e\zeta_e)
=E(\widehat U_{e,J_y,J_z,n}\mid\mathscr F_{-e}). \tag{9}
\]

Along the positive convex path \(\widehat P_e+t\dot P_e\), exact Taylor expansion of the one-dimensional quantile formula gives
\[
\theta_n=\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{plug}}^{(-e)}
+D\Theta_{\widehat P_e}[\dot P_e]
+2\int_0^1(1-t)\mathcal H_{e,t}(\dot P_e,\dot P_e)dt. \tag{10}
\]
The displayed score in \(\mathrm{def{:}anisotropic\text{-}estimator}\) represents the derivative in (10), including the shared-marginal component.  If a distribution has density at least \(c_*>0\), a distribution-function perturbation of size \(E\) moves its inverse by at most \(E/c_*\), and
\[
F(x+w)-F(x)-q(x)w
=\int_0^w\{q(x+t)-q(x)\}dt=O(|w|^{1+a_y}). \tag{11}
\]
Applying (11) to both conditional quantile maps in (10), and writing \(\widehat a_e^0\) for the exact unridged pilot inverse-elliptic form, yields
\[
\left|2\int_0^1(1-t)\mathcal H_{e,t}(\dot P_e,\dot P_e)dt
-\widehat a_e^0(\zeta_e,\zeta_e)\right|
\lesssim E_n^{2+a_y}+\|\Delta_n\|_\infty^{a_y}E_n^2. \tag{12}
\]
The resolvent and oversampling estimates (5)--(7) likewise give
\[
|\widehat a_e^{\mathrm{op}}(\widehat\Pi_e\zeta_e,widehat\Pi_e\zeta_e)
-\widehat a_e^0(\widehat\Pi_e\zeta_e,widehat\Pi_e\zeta_e)|
\lesssim E_n^{2+a_y}+E_n^3. \tag{13}
\]
Subtracting (10) from the conditional mean of the fold estimator and using (9)--(13) proves the sign identity
\[
E(\widehat\theta_{e,J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n\mid\mathscr F_{-e})
=-\{\widehat a_e^0(\zeta_e,\zeta_e)
-\widehat a_e^0(\widehat\Pi_e\zeta_e,widehat\Pi_e\zeta_e)\}+R_{0,e}, \tag{14}
\]
where \(|R_{0,e}|\lesssim E_n^{2+a_y}+\|\Delta_n\|_\infty^{a_y}E_n^2\).  If the pilot is oracle, \(\zeta_e=0\), and (14) vanishes.

On the clipping-inactive event, balanced pilot wavelet estimates have bounded anisotropic H\"older norms in every fixed \(L^k\), while \(\|\zeta_e\|_2=O_{L^k}(\varepsilon)\).  Applying the complement calculation of \(\mathrm{prop{:}anisotropic\text{-}spectrum\text{-}bias}\) to \(\zeta_e\), and using \(\min(x^2,y^2)\le xy\), gives
\[
|\widehat a_e^0(\zeta_e,\zeta_e)
-\widehat a_e^0(\widehat\Pi_e\zeta_e,widehat\Pi_e\zeta_e)|
\lesssim_{L^2}\varepsilon\{2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-s_zJ_z}\}. \tag{15}
\]
If \(A=\|\Delta_n\|_\infty\), the H\"older modulus preserves amplitude \(A\) on an outcome interval of length \(\asymp A^{1/s_y}\) and a covariate rectangle of volume \(\asymp A^{d/s_z}\); zero outcome mass makes the antiderivative have size \(\asymp A^{1+1/s_y}\).  Since \(\rho_n^2=\int H_n^2/\bar q_n\),
\[
\|\Delta_n\|_\infty\lesssim
\rho_n^{2/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}. \tag{16}
\]
Equations (2), (12)--(16) prove the three deterministic remainder terms.

After subtracting conditional means, the residual first-order coefficient has squared \(L^2(P_0)\oplus L^2(P_1)\) norm bounded by
\[
C\{E_n^{2+2a_y}+\|\Delta_n\|_\infty^{2a_y}E_n^2\}+o_P(\rho_n^2). \tag{17}
\]
Conditional independence divides its empirical variance by \(m\).  Pair counting for the canonical-kernel replacement gives
\[
E[\{U_e^{\mathrm{can}}(\widehat B)-U_e^{\mathrm{can}}(B)\}^2\mid\mathscr F_{-e}]
\le\frac C{m^2}\|\Gamma^{1/2}(\widehat B-B)\Gamma^{1/2}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2. \tag{18}
\]
Levelwise use of (6)--(7) makes (18) \(o_P(A_{J_y,J_z,n}^2/n^2)\).  Since \(\sqrt n\varepsilon\to\infty\), the square roots of (17)--(18) are absorbed by the displayed deterministic terms plus \(o\{\rho_n/\sqrt n+A_{J_y,J_z,n}/n\}\).  Averaging folds proves the asserted \(L^2\) remainder bound and, by (14), excludes \(B^y_{J_y,n}+B^z_{J_z,n}\).

Finally, compact support gives \(\|\Phi(W)\|_2^2\lesssim D_{J_y,J_z}\).  Exponential bounds for scalar quadratic forms on a \(1/4\)-net imply
\[
\max_e\|\widehat\Gamma^{(-e)}-\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{op}}
=O_P\!\left(\sqrt{D_{J_y,J_z}\log n/m}+D_{J_y,J_z}\log n/m\right)=o_P(1). \tag{19}
\]
Uniform positive definiteness makes the square-root map locally Lipschitz.  Equations (7), (18), and (19) prove operator and relative Hilbert--Schmidt consistency of \(\widehat K\).  Since (19) was unused before this last step, the covariance condition is needed only for the observed spectral proxy.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Conditioning on the pilot yields the exact sign of the second-order error and a uniform \(L^2\) envelope under the unchanged assumptions. \textit{Gap.} Generic pilot convergence alone does not identify the full-minus-projected Hessian term or its anisotropic balance. \textit{Consumer.} The corrected observable expansion, tuning, upper bracket, and calibration allowance use this result.

\begin{lemma}[lem:explicit-mixture-checks]\label{lem:explicit-mixture-checks}
Assume \(L>1\). For the sign-paired conditional wavelet construction and a sufficiently small fixed \(c_0\), every \(b_{n,\omega}\) has zero conditional mass after multiplication by \(\cos(2\pi y)\), \(q_{a,n,\omega}^{\mathrm{lb}}\ge c\), anisotropic H\"older norm at most \(L\), common marginal \(R_n(dz)=dz\), and inverse-elliptic radius at most \(\rho\). Its endpoint separation from the null equals
\[
\frac{\gamma_n^2M_{J_z}}{8\pi^2}\{1+o(1)\}.
\]
The uniform Rademacher mixture and the null product law satisfy
\[
\chi^2(\overline{\mathbb P}_n,\mathbb P_n^0)\le\exp\{C_{\chi}n^2\gamma_n^4M_{J_z}\}-1.
\]
At \(2^{J_z}\asymp n^{2/(4s_z+d)}\), the exponent is bounded and the separation is a fixed multiple of \(\min\{\rho^2,r_{n,d}(s_z)\}\). The fixed-frequency no-covariate construction is positive, normalized, H\"older, and has bounded product Hellinger distance for parameter increments of order \(n^{-1/2}\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(c_y(y)=\cos(2\pi y)\).  If \(\rho=0\), then \(\gamma_n=0\), so the construction is the null and every assertion is immediate.  Suppose \(\rho>0\).  Boundary-wavelet scaling and disjointness of the supports in \(\mathcal K_{J_z}\) give
\[
\|b_{n,\omega}\|_\infty
\le C_1\gamma_n2^{dJ_z/2}\le C_1c_0 2^{-s_zJ_z},
\qquad
\|b_{n,\omega}\|_{\mathcal C^{s_z}}
\le C_2\gamma_n2^{(s_z+d/2)J_z}\le C_2c_0. \tag{1}
\]
Because \(L>1\), choose \(c_0\) so that the perturbation norm is at most \(L-1\) and \(C_1c_0\le2\min\{1-c,C-1\}\).  The outcome frequency is fixed; hence
\[
c\le q_{a,n,\omega}^{\mathrm{lb}}\le C,
\qquad \|q_{a,n,\omega}^{\mathrm{lb}}\|_{\mathcal C^{s_y,s_z}}\le L. \tag{2}
\]
Moreover, \(\int_0^1c_y(y)dy=0\), so each conditional density integrates to one.  With \(r_n=1\), the marginal is common and satisfies its density and smoothness requirements.

For this symmetric pair, \(\bar q_n=1\), \(\Delta_n=b_{n,\omega}c_y\), and
\[
\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n(\cdot,z)
=\frac{b_{n,\omega}(z)}{4\pi^2}c_y.
\]
The solution has zero mean and satisfies both Neumann conditions.  Orthonormality gives \(\int b_{n,\omega}^2=\gamma_n^2M_{J_z}\), whence
\[
\rho_n^2=\frac{\gamma_n^2M_{J_z}}{8\pi^2}
\le\frac{c_0^2}{8\pi^2}\rho^2\le\rho^2. \tag{3}
\]
Thus all alternatives belong to the declared class.  The established cosine second-variation calculation gives
\[
\theta_{n,\omega}
=\frac{\gamma_n^2M_{J_z}}{8\pi^2}+O(\|b_{n,\omega}\|_\infty^3)
=\frac{\gamma_n^2M_{J_z}}{8\pi^2}\{1+o(1)\}, \tag{4}
\]
uniformly in \(\omega\), because (1) makes the cubic remainder negligible relative to \(\gamma_n^2M_{J_z}\).

Under \(\mathbb P_n^0\), let \(L_\omega\) be the likelihood ratio.  Arm and observation independence and \(\int c_y^2=1/2\) imply
\[
E_0(L_\omega L_{\omega'})
=\left\{1+\frac{\gamma_n^2}{8}\sum_{k\in\mathcal K_{J_z}}\omega_k\omega'_k\right\}^{2n}. \tag{5}
\]
The factor is positive by (2).  Since \(\log(1+x)\le x\), averaging (5) over independent sign vectors and putting \(\epsilon_k=\omega_k\omega'_k\) gives
\[
1+\chi^2(\overline{\mathbb P}_n,\mathbb P_n^0)
\le E_\epsilon\exp\!\left\{\frac{n\gamma_n^2}{4}\sum_{k=1}^{M_{J_z}}\epsilon_k\right\}
=\{\cosh(n\gamma_n^2/4)\}^{M_{J_z}}
\le\exp\{n^2\gamma_n^4M_{J_z}/32\}. \tag{6}
\]
At \(2^{J_z}\asymp n^{2/(4s_z+d)}\),
\[
n^2\gamma_n^4M_{J_z}=O(c_0^4),
\qquad
\gamma_n^2M_{J_z}\asymp c_0^2\min\{r_{n,d}(s_z),\rho^2\}. \tag{7}
\]
This proves the mixture claims.

For \(d=0\), \(q_{a,t}^0\) integrates to one.  The same fixed slack and a sufficiently small neighborhood of zero give positivity and H\"older membership.  If \(|t'-t|\lesssim n^{-1/2}\), positivity and \((\sqrt x-\sqrt y)^2\le(x-y)^2/y\) yield
\[
H^2(q_{a,t}^0,q_{a,t'}^0)\lesssim(t-t')^2.
\]
For products, expanding \(1-\prod_j(1-H_j^2/2)\) bounds squared Hellinger distance by the sum of component distances.  Across both independent arms it is therefore \(O\{n(t-t')^2\}=O(1)\), completing the proof.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The converse is tied to a finite sign family whose normalization, positivity, H\"older membership under the explicit \(L>1\) slack, shared marginal, local radius, endpoint gap, and mixture chi-square distance are all calculated. \textit{Gap.} BickelRitov1988 and IngsterSuslina2003 provide quadratic-functional mixture templates, but not this two-arm conditional-density construction preserving a common marginal and exact conditional normalization. \textit{Consumer.} The conditional minimax frontier uses the family in the zero and quadratic zones, and the no-covariate theorem reuses its fixed-frequency testing calculation.

\begin{theorem}[thm:uniform-second-order-expansion]\label{thm:uniform-second-order-expansion}
Let \(d\ge1\), put \(a_y=\min\{s_y,1\}\), let \(J_y,J_z\to\infty\), let \(D_{J_y,J_z}=o(n^2)\), and use the observable anisotropic estimator with the declared pilot tuning. Uniformly over \(\mathcal M_{n,d}(s_y,s_z,\rho)\), the exact-\(\theta_n\) Hoeffding expansion is
\[
\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n
=\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}+\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}+\mathbb R_{J_y,J_z,n},
\]
where
\[
\operatorname{sd}(\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n})=\sqrt{V_{L,n}/n},
\qquad
V_{L,n}\lesssim\rho_n^2,
\qquad
\operatorname{sd}(\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n})=\{1+o(1)\}\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}}n,
\qquad
\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}=0
\quad\text{at conditional equality},
\]
and
\[
\|\mathbb R_{J_y,J_z,n}\|_2
\lesssim
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\{2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-s_zJ_z}\}
+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{2+a_y}
+\rho_n^{2a_y/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^2
+o\!\left\{\frac{\rho_n}{\sqrt n}+\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}}n\right\}.
\]
If \(L>1\), there is no uniform reverse bound \(V_{L,n}\gtrsim\rho_n^2\): \(z\)-independent discrepancies at increasingly high outcome frequency can satisfy \(V_{L,n}/\rho_n^2\to0\). The leading deterministic component of \(\mathbb R_{J_y,J_z,n}\) is the negative full-minus-projected inverse-elliptic energy of the actual pilot discrepancy error; it vanishes for an oracle pilot.  The true-discrepancy energy gaps \(B^y_{J_y,n}\) and \(B^z_{J_z,n}\) do not occur in this expansion. At the corrected \((J_y^*,J_z^*)\) in \(\mathrm{def{:}anisotropic\text{-}tuning}\),
\[
\left\|\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y^*,J_z^*,n}-\theta_n\right\|_2
\lesssim
\frac{\rho_n}{\sqrt n}
+n^{-2s_z/(2s_z+d)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{d/(2s_z+d)}
+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{2+a_y}
+\rho_n^{2a_y/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^2.
\]
Here \(\rho_n/\sqrt n\) in the remainder and root-mean-square displays is a uniform upper envelope, not the actual linear standard deviation. No covariance estimate enters this point-estimator expansion.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For each fold \(e\), condition on the opposite-fold sigma-field.  By \(\mathrm{ass{:}two\text{-}arm\text{-}sampling}\), all evaluation observations are independent of the fitted objects.  The definitions of the oracle Hoeffding projections and of \(\mathbb R_{J_y,J_z,n}\) give the exact algebraic identity
\[
\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n
=\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}+\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}+\mathbb R_{J_y,J_z,n}. \tag{1}
\]
Thus the target in (1) is the identified endpoint \(\theta_n\), not a projected energy.  The conditional von Mises calculation in \(\mathrm{lem{:}observable\text{-}pilot\text{-}control}\) gives, uniformly over the class,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\|\mathbb R_{J_y,J_z,n}\|_2\lesssim{}&
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\{2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-s_zJ_z}\}
+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{2+a_y}\\
&+\rho_n^{2a_y/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{2}
+o\!\left\{\frac{\rho_n}{\sqrt n}+\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}}n\right\}.
\end{split}
\tag{2}\end{equation}
Its conditional-mean leading term is the negative full-minus-projected energy of the actual pilot error and vanishes for an oracle pilot.  Hence the true-discrepancy gaps \(B^y_{J_y,n}\) and \(B^z_{J_z,n}\) do not occur in (1).

We establish the precise linear scale.  For fixed \(z\), set
\[
H_z(y)=\int_0^y\Delta_n(v,z)dv,
\qquad u_z=\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n(\cdot,z).
\]
Zero conditional mass, the Neumann boundary conditions, and integration by parts give
\[
-\bar q_nu_z'=H_z,
\qquad
e_n(z):=\int\Delta_nu_z,dy
=\int\frac{H_z^2}{\bar q_n}\,dy,
\qquad \rho_n^2=\int e_n,dR_n. \tag{3}
\]
Let \(f_n(z)=W_2^2\{Q_{0,n}(\cdot\mid z),Q_{1,n}(\cdot\mid z)\}\), and let \(\ell_{a,n}\) be the conditionally centered exact Kantorovich derivative.  The density lower bound makes the conditional quantile maps invertible, and their one-dimensional derivative identity is
\[
|\partial_y\ell_{a,n}(y,z)|=2|y-T_{a,z}(y)|. \tag{4}
\]
The mean-value theorem applied to the conditional distribution functions, together with (3) and \(c\le q_{a,n},\bar q_n\le C\), gives \(f_n(z)\lesssim e_n(z)\).  Since \(0\le f_n\le1\),
\[
\int\{f_n(z)-\theta_n\}^2dR_n(z)\le\int f_n(z)^2dR_n(z)\lesssim\rho_n^2. \tag{5}
\]
On \([0,1]\), the fundamental theorem of calculus and Cauchy--Schwarz imply the weighted Poincar\'e inequality
\[
\operatorname{Var}_{q_{a,n}(\cdot,z)}\{g(Y)\}
\lesssim\int|g'(y)|^2q_{a,n}(y,z)dy. \tag{6}
\]
Apply (6) to \(g=\ell_{a,n}\), use (4), and change variables along the monotone transport map to obtain
\[
\operatorname{Var}_{q_{a,n}(\cdot,z)}\{\ell_{a,n}(Y,z)\}\lesssim f_n(z). \tag{7}
\]
The exact arm score is
\[
s_{a,n}(y,z)=\frac12\{f_n(z)-\theta_n\}+\ell_{a,n}(y,z).
\]
Equations (5)--(7), the shared marginal, and arm independence show
\[
V_{L,n}=\sum_{a=0}^1\operatorname{Var}_{P_{a,n}}\{s_{a,n}(W_{a,1,n})\}
\lesssim\rho_n^2,
\qquad
\operatorname{sd}(\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n})=\sqrt{V_{L,n}/n}. \tag{8}
\]
If \(\rho_n=0\), (3) gives \(H_z=0\) almost everywhere and hence \(\Delta_n=0\); then \(f_n\), the centered potentials, and \(\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}\) vanish.

There is no uniform reverse inequality.  With uniform \(R_n\), choose \(k_n\to\infty\), \(t_n=\kappa k_n^{-s_y}\), and
\[
q_{a,n}(y,z)=1+(2a-1)t_n\cos(2\pi k_ny)/2.
\]
For sufficiently small fixed \(\kappa\), \(L>1\) and the density bounds put these laws in the class.  Direct inversion of the Neumann operator gives
\[
\rho_n^2=t_n^2/(8\pi^2k_n^2). \tag{9}
\]
Uniform Taylor expansion of the inverse distribution functions, licensed by the density lower bound, and then (4) give
\[
V_{L,n}=O(t_n^2k_n^{-4}+t_n^4k_n^{-2}),
\qquad
V_{L,n}/\rho_n^2=O(k_n^{-2}+t_n^2)\to0. \tag{10}
\]

For the canonical quadratic term, true centering and diagonal deletion leave only identical or reversed observation pairs.  Writing \(B=B_{J_y,J_z,n}\), \(\Gamma_a=\Gamma_{a,J_y,J_z,n}\), and \(\Gamma=\Gamma_0+\Gamma_1\), direct pair counting gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n})={}&
\frac{\operatorname{tr}\{(B\Gamma_0)^2\}+\operatorname{tr}\{(B\Gamma_1)^2\}}{m(m-1)}
+\frac{2\operatorname{tr}(B\Gamma_0B\Gamma_1)}{m^2}\\
={}&\frac{4\operatorname{tr}\{(\Gamma^{1/2}B\Gamma^{1/2})^2\}}{n^2}\{1+o(1)\}
=\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}^2}{n^2}\{1+o(1)\}.
\end{split}
\tag{11}\end{equation}
This is valid because \(m=n/2\), and the final identity is \(\mathrm{def{:}whitened\text{-}spectral\text{-}array}\).

At \((J_y^*,J_z^*)\), \(\mathrm{prop{:}anisotropic\text{-}spectrum\text{-}bias}\) and \(\mathrm{def{:}anisotropic\text{-}tuning}\) give
\[
\frac{A_{J_y^*,J_z^*,n}}n
\asymp\frac{2^{dJ_z^*/2}}n
\asymp n^{-2s_z/(2s_z+d)}
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{d/(2s_z+d)}. \tag{12}
\]
They also give
\[
D_{J_y^*,J_z^*}
=\{n\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\}^{\{2s_z/(s_y+1)+2d\}/(2s_z+d)+o(1)}
=o(n^2), \tag{13}
\]
because \(2s_z/(s_y+1)+2d<2(2s_z+d)\).  Substitute (12)--(13) into (2), use \(\rho_n\le\rho\), and apply Minkowski's inequality to (1), (8), and (11).  This proves the optimized root-mean-square bound and every remaining assertion.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The corrected expansion identifies the actual linear standard deviation as \(\sqrt{V_{L,n}/n}\), proves only the valid uniform envelope \(V_{L,n}\lesssim\rho_n^2\), and retains the exact-target pilot remainder. \textit{Gap.} A reverse comparison fails on covariate-constant discrepancies with increasing outcome frequency, so \(\rho_n/\sqrt n\) can be used only as a uniform upper envelope. \textit{Consumer.} The minimax upper proof uses the one-sided envelope, while the population transition and the calibration OEQ retain \(V_{L,n}\) exactly.

\begin{theorem}[thm:mixed-spectral-transition]\label{thm:mixed-spectral-transition}
Let \(d\ge1\), \(J_y,J_z\to\infty\), and \(D_{J_y,J_z}=o(n^2)\).  Define the infeasible oracle-score, population-kernel statistic
\[
\Theta^{\mathrm{or}}_{J_y,J_z,n}
=\theta_n+\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}+\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}
\]
and put \(S_n=A_{J_y,J_z,n}+\sqrt{nV_{L,n}}\).  Suppose that \(\|\Delta_n\|_\infty\to0\) and
\[
\sqrt n\left\{
\rho_n\|\Delta_n\|_\infty^{a_y}
+2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-s_zJ_z}
\right\}=o(S_n).
\]
Then the finite-\(n\) Gaussian-quadratic proxy based on the population raw-moment kernel satisfies
\[
d_{\mathrm{BL}}\!\left(
\mathcal L\left[\frac{n\{\Theta^{\mathrm{or}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n\}}{S_n}\right],
\mathcal L\left[\frac1{S_n}\sum_{e=1}^2\sum_k\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}
\{(\xi_{e,k}+\eta_{k,n})^2-(1+\eta_{k,n}^2)\}\right]
\right)\to0,
\]
where
\[
K_{J_y,J_z,n}=\Gamma_{J_y,J_z,n}^{1/2}B_{J_y,J_z,n}\Gamma_{J_y,J_z,n}^{1/2},
\qquad
A_{J_y,J_z,n}^2=4\sum_k\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}^2,
\]
\[
\eta_{k,n}=2^{-1/2}v_{k,J_y,J_z,n}^{\top}\Gamma_{J_y,J_z,n}^{-1/2}\sqrt n\,\delta_{J_y,J_z,n}.
\]
The same proxy and the normalized oracle statistic are diffuse locally Gaussian:
\[
d_{\mathrm{BL}}\!\left(
\mathcal L\left[\frac{n\{\Theta^{\mathrm{or}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n\}}{S_n}\right],
\mathcal N\!\left(0,\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}^2+nV_{L,n}}{S_n^2}\right)
\right)\to0.
\]
At conditional equality all shifts and the linear projection vanish, so
\[
\frac{n\{\Theta^{\mathrm{or}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n\}}{A_{J_y,J_z,n}}
=\frac{n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}}{A_{J_y,J_z,n}}
\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1).
\]
Separately, without the preceding local-to-equality and score-approximation premises, at a fixed unequal law, if \(\sqrt{nV_{L,n}}/A_{J_y,J_z,n}\to\infty\), then
\[
\frac{\Theta^{\mathrm{or}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n}{\sqrt{V_{L,n}/n}}
\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1).
\]
No fixed or subsequential non-Gaussian chaos survives in this growing-covariate-resolution regime.  This theorem concerns the infeasible population experiment; transferring it to one observable multiplier interval is not asserted.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(M_n=2^{dJ_z}\), \(A=A_{J_y,J_z,n}\), \(V=V_{L,n}\), and \(S=A+\sqrt{nV}\).  By \(d\ge1\), \(J_z\to\infty\), and \(\mathrm{prop{:}anisotropic\text{-}spectrum\text{-}bias}\),
\[
A\asymp M_n^{1/2}\to\infty,
\qquad \max_k|\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}|/A\lesssim M_n^{-1/2}\to0. \tag{1}
\]
Thus \(S>0\) eventually.

Let \(H_n(y,z)=\int_0^y\Delta_n(u,z)du\) and \(u_n=\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n\).  The shared-marginal and density assumptions imply
\[
\partial_yu_n=-H_n/\bar q_n,
\qquad \rho_n^2=\int H_n^2/\bar q_n\,dy\,dR_n. \tag{2}
\]
If \(T_{n,z}=F_{1,n}^{-1}\circ F_{0,n}\), then
\[
\int_y^{T_{n,z}(y)}q_{1,n}(u,z)du=-H_n(y,z).
\]
The density lower bound and outcome H\"older modulus therefore give, uniformly as \(\|\Delta_n\|_\infty\to0\),
\[
T_{n,z}(y)-y=-H_n(y,z)/\bar q_n(y,z)
+O\{\|\Delta_n\|_\infty^{a_y}|H_n(y,z)|\}. \tag{3}
\]

Let \(s_{a,n}\) be the exact centered arm score and \(s^{(2)}_{a,J_y,J_z,n}\) its projected quadratic-score linearization.  The derivative of the conditional transport potential is twice the transport displacement.  Equations (2)--(3), weighted Poincar\'e, and the outcome and covariate projection bounds from \(\mathrm{prop{:}anisotropic\text{-}spectrum\text{-}bias}\) give
\[
\max_a\|s_{a,n}-s^{(2)}_{a,J_y,J_z,n}\|_{L^2(P_{a,n})}
\lesssim\rho_n\|\Delta_n\|_\infty^{a_y}
+2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-s_zJ_z}. \tag{4}
\]
The two Gram conjugations give the exact coordinate identity behind (4):
\[
\Pi\mathcal G_n\Pi\Delta_n
=\Phi_{J_y,J_z}^{\top}B_{J_y,J_z,n}\delta_{J_y,J_z,n}. \tag{5}
\]

For fold \(e\), define
\[
Z_{e,n}=\Gamma^{-1/2}\sqrt m
\{\bar\Phi_{1,e}-\bar\Phi_{0,e}-\delta_{J_y,J_z,n}\}.
\]
The covariance bounds justify the inverse; the two \(Z_{e,n}\) are independent, centered, and have identity covariance.  Put
\[
G_n=2\sum_{e=1}^2\sqrt m\,
\delta_{J_y,J_z,n}^{\top}B\Gamma^{1/2}Z_{e,n}. \tag{6}
\]
An empirical average of a centered score error of \(L^2\)-norm \(r\), after multiplication by \(n\), has \(L^2\)-norm \(O(\sqrt n r)\).  Hence (4) and the theorem's approximation premise give
\[
\|n\mathbb L_{J_y,J_z,n}-G_n\|_2=o(S). \tag{7}
\]
Diagonalization in the eigenbasis of \(K\) yields
\[
G_n=2\sum_{e=1}^2\sum_k\nu_k\eta_{k,n}Z_{e,k},
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(G_n)=8\sum_k\nu_k^2\eta_{k,n}^2
=nV+o(S^2). \tag{8}
\]

Put \(Q_n=n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}\).  The hypotheses of \(\mathrm{lem{:}growing\text{-}kernel\text{-}contractions}\) hold because \(M_n\le D_{J_y,J_z}=o(n^2)\), the projected score summands are uniformly bounded by localization and the density bounds, and (8) gives \(EG_n^2=O(S^2)\).  That lemma supplies conditional Lindeberg and predictable-variance convergence for \(G_n+Q_n\), while canonical orthogonality and (8) give
\[
E(G_n+Q_n)^2=A^2+nV+o(S^2). \tag{9}
\]
Iterated conditional characteristic-function expansion, using
\(|e^{ix}-1-ix+x^2/2|\le Cx^2\min\{|x|,1\}\), now shows that along every subsequence on which \((A^2+nV)/S^2\) converges,
\[
\frac{G_n+Q_n}{S}\Rightarrow
\mathcal N\!\left(0,\frac{A^2+nV}{S^2}\right). \tag{10}
\]

For the Gaussian-quadratic proxy
\[
\mathcal Q_n^G=\sum_{e=1}^2\sum_k\nu_k
\{(\xi_{e,k}+\eta_{k,n})^2-(1+\eta_{k,n}^2)\},
\]
one-dimensional Gaussian integration gives, with \(c_{e,k}=\nu_k/S\) and \(l_{e,k}=2\nu_k\eta_{k,n}/S\),
\[
\log Ee^{it\mathcal Q_n^G/S}
=\sum_{e,k}\left[-itc_{e,k}-\frac12\log(1-2itc_{e,k})
-\frac{t^2l_{e,k}^2}{2(1-2itc_{e,k})}\right]. \tag{11}
\]
Equations (1) and (8) imply \(\max_k|\nu_k|/S\to0\), \(\sum l_{e,k}^2=(nV+o(S^2))/S^2\), and \(\sum c_{e,k}^2=A^2/(2S^2)\).  Expanding (11), with the total remainder bounded by \(O(\max_k|\nu_k|/S)\), gives the same normal limit as (10).  Since
\[
1/2\le(A^2+nV)/(A+\sqrt{nV})^2\le1, \tag{12}
\]
the subsequence criterion and the fact that bounded-Lipschitz distance metrizes weak convergence on \(\mathbb R\) prove both displayed bounded-Lipschitz approximations.  Equation (7) then replaces \(G_n+Q_n\) by \(n(\Theta^{\mathrm{or}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n)\).

At equality, \(\Delta_n=0\), so \(\delta=0\), every \(\eta_{k,n}=0\), \(V=0\), and \(\mathbb L=0\).  Taking \(S=A\) proves the standard-normal equality limit.  The maximal spectral ratio in (1) excludes every non-Gaussian subsequential chaos.

At a fixed unequal law, the exact score is bounded by compact support, bounded cost, and the positive density bound.  Unequal conditional laws on a set of positive \(R_n\)-measure make a conditional potential nonconstant, so positivity gives \(V>0\).  The usual characteristic-function expansion of the independent centered score summands, whose maximal normalized size is \(O(n^{-1/2})\), yields \(\mathbb L/\sqrt{V/n}\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1)\).  By the quadratic variance identity,
\[
\operatorname{sd}(\mathbb U)/\sqrt{V/n}
=\{1+o(1)\}A/\sqrt{nV}\to0
\]
under the stated dominance premise.  This proves the separate regular limit and completes the theorem.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem supplies a population Gaussian--quadratic array for the unregularized conditional, Gram-conjugated growing-rank kernel, its noncentral linear representation, and forced Gaussianization without false bias centering. RobinsLiTchetgenVanderVaart2016 motivates changing-kernel Gaussian approximation but does not supply this conditional inverse-elliptic spectral geometry or the required raw-moment Gram-contraction verification; AgarwalLuedtke2026 instead studies a fixed-entropic causal transport functional with a fixed-entropic equality law. \textit{Gap.} Existing changing-kernel quadratic theory does not establish the conditional score approximation, inverse-Gram localization, or common-marginal growing-rank contractions used here, while fixed-entropic causal-transport laws are for a different regularized target. This theorem deliberately stops at the infeasible population-score, population-kernel experiment; observable local-shift estimation, joint multiplier coupling, and conditional quantile calibration do not follow from the contraction proof. \textit{Consumer.} The theorem is the population limit experiment underlying the remaining honest-calibration question.

\begin{theorem}[thm:conditional-minimax-frontier]\label{thm:conditional-minimax-frontier}
Assume \(L>1\), put \(a_y=\min\{s_y,1\}\), and fix \(d\ge1\). For every fixed \(0<\tau<\min\{s_y,s_z\}\), put \(a_\tau=\tau/(2\tau+d+1)\). There are constants \(c_1>0\) and \(C_{1,\tau}<\infty\), depending only on the fixed class constants and, for the upper constant, on \(\tau\), such that for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
c_1\mathfrak R_{n,d}(s_y,s_z,\rho)\le\inf_{\widetilde\theta_n}\sup_{(P_{0,n},P_{1,n})\in\mathcal M_{n,d}(s_y,s_z,\rho)}\{E(\widetilde\theta_n-\theta_n)^2\}^{1/2}
\]
\[
\le C_{1,\tau}\min\!\left\{
\rho^2,
\rho n^{-1/2}
+n^{-2s_z/(2s_z+d)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{d/(2s_z+d)}
+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{2+a_y}
+\rho^{2a_y/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^2,
n^{-a_\tau}(\log n)^{a_\tau}
\right\}.
\]
When \(s_z\ge1\), the additional candidate \(n^{-1/(d+2)}(\log n)^{1+1/(d+2)}\) may be inserted in the last minimum. Here
\[
\mathfrak R_{n,d}(s_y,s_z,\rho)=\min\{\rho^2,\rho n^{-1/2}+n^{-4s_z/(4s_z+d)}\}.
\]
The nonzero upper candidates are attained, respectively, by the observable Gram-corrected estimator \(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y^*,J_z^*,n}\) at the corrected pilot-error balancing resolutions, the median-aggregated SmoothCOT estimator \(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{sm,med}}_{\tau,n}\), and, when \(s_z\ge1\), the median-aggregated direct-COT estimator \(\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{dir,med}}\); the first entry is attained by the zero estimator. The quadratic and zero-zone lower bounds hold on the explicit sign-paired family. In the zone \(\rho\gg n^{-1/2}\), the constant-in-\(z\) fixed-frequency two-point family supplies the regular \(\rho n^{-1/2}\) lower bound.

The corrected Gram scale is strictly slower than the intrinsic quadratic scale:
\[
n^{-4s_z/(4s_z+d)}
=o\!\left\{n^{-2s_z/(2s_z+d)}
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{d/(2s_z+d)}\right\}.
\]
Thus the present primitive-model analysis gives an honest two-sided bracket rather than a matched conditional rate in the nontrivial quadratic zone; the SmoothCOT and direct-COT envelopes can sharpen the upper side but do not by themselves close the intrinsic exponent.  The lower frontier has the intrinsic elbow \(s_z=d/4\): when \(s_z>d/4\), \(\rho_{Q,n}=n^{-2s_z/(4s_z+d)}\) and \(\rho_{L,n}=n^{(d-4s_z)/(2(4s_z+d))}\) delimit its zero, quadratic, mixed, and regular terms; when \(s_z\le d/4\), its quadratic term prevents a uniformly positive-variance root-\(n\) description. Tightness of the displayed bracket outside the zero and any separately regular subregimes is not asserted.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let
\[
\mathcal R_{n,d}(\rho)=\inf_{\widetilde\theta_n}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}(s_y,s_z,\rho)}
\{E_P(\widetilde\theta_n-\theta_n)^2\}^{1/2}.
\]
We first relate the causal endpoint to the radius.  For fixed \(z\), let \(F_{a,z}(y)=\int_0^yq_{a,n}(v,z)dv\), \(Q_{a,z}=F_{a,z}^{-1}\), and \(H_z=F_{1,z}-F_{0,z}\).  Positivity makes the inverses continuous.  The uncrossing inequality
\[
(x-y)^2+(x'-y')^2\le(x-y')^2+(x'-y)^2
\quad(x\le x',\ y\le y')
\]
proves that the increasing coupling minimizes squared cost, so
\[
W_2^2\{Q_{0,n}(\cdot\mid z),Q_{1,n}(\cdot\mid z)\}
=\int_0^1\{Q_{1,z}(v)-Q_{0,z}(v)\}^2dv. \tag{1}
\]
For \(u_z=\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n\), integration of the Neumann equation and then integration by parts give
\[
-\bar q_nu_z'=H_z,
\qquad
\int\Delta_nu_z\,dy=\int H_z^2/\bar q_n\,dy. \tag{2}
\]
For each \(v\), the mean-value theorem gives a point \(\zeta_{v,z}\) between the two quantiles such that
\[
H_z\{Q_{0,z}(v)\}=q_{1,n}(\zeta_{v,z},z)
\{Q_{0,z}(v)-Q_{1,z}(v)\}. \tag{3}
\]
Changing variables \(v=F_{0,z}(y)\) in (1) and using \(c\le q_{a,n},\bar q_n\le C\) in (2)--(3) prove two-sided comparability.  Integrating against the common marginal gives
\[
(c^2/C^2)\rho_n^2\le\theta_n\le(C^2/c^2)\rho_n^2
\le(C^2/c^2)\rho^2. \tag{4}
\]
Thus the zero estimator has root-mean-square risk \(O(\rho^2)\).

Put
\[
g_n=n^{-2s_z/(2s_z+d)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{d/(2s_z+d)},
\quad
p_n=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{2+a_y}
+\rho^{2a_y/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^2.
\]
The observable expansion theorem gives
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}
\|\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y^*,J_z^*,n}-\theta_n\|_2
\lesssim\rho n^{-1/2}+g_n+p_n. \tag{5}
\]
The established SmoothCOT median envelope gives, for every fixed \(0<\tau<\min\{s_y,s_z\}\),
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}
\|\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{sm,med}}_{\tau,n}-\theta_n\|_2
\le C_\tau n^{-a_\tau}(\log n)^{a_\tau}. \tag{6}
\]
When \(s_z\ge1\), the direct-COT median envelope gives
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}
\|\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{dir,med}}-\theta_n\|_2
\lesssim n^{-1/(d+2)}(\log n)^{1+1/(d+2)}. \tag{7}
\]
For the fixed class indices, choose before seeing the data the estimator with the smallest deterministic guarantee among (4)--(7).  This law-independent selection proves the asserted minimax upper bound.

For the converse, use \(2^{J_z}\asymp n^{2/(4s_z+d)}\).  The explicit mixture lemma places its null and alternatives in the class and, after reducing the fixed \(c_0\), gives constants \(\eta<1\) and \(c_M>0\) such that
\[
\chi^2(\overline{\mathbb P}_n,\mathbb P_n^0)\le\eta,
\qquad
\theta_n(\omega)-\theta_n(0)
\ge c_M\min\{\rho^2,r_{n,d}(s_z)\}. \tag{8}
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz applied to the mixture likelihood ratio yields
\[
\|\overline{\mathbb P}_n-\mathbb P_n^0\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\le\tfrac12\sqrt\eta<1. \tag{9}
\]
Thresholding any estimator halfway between the endpoints, and observing that either testing error forces absolute estimation error at least half the separation, gives
\[
\mathcal R_{n,d}(\rho)\gtrsim\min\{\rho^2,r_{n,d}(s_z)\}. \tag{10}
\]

For the regular term, extend the fixed-frequency cosine family constantly in \(z\), and denote its endpoint by \(\Theta(t)\).  Direct solution gives
\[
\rho(t)^2=t^2/(8\pi^2). \tag{11}
\]
For \(\rho\ge K n^{-1/2}\), take \(t=c_7\min\{\rho,t_0\}\) and \(t'=t+c_8n^{-1/2}\), with fixed constants sufficiently small.  Equation (11) puts both points in the class, and the mixture lemma gives bounded product Hellinger distance.  Implicit differentiation of \(F_{a,t}\{Q_{a,t}(v)\}=v\) gives
\[
\partial_tQ_{a,t}(v)
=-\frac{(2a-1)\sin\{2\pi Q_{a,t}(v)\}}
{4\pi q_{a,t}^{0}\{Q_{a,t}(v)\}}. \tag{12}
\]
The denominator is bounded below.  Dominated differentiation makes \(\Theta''\) continuous near zero; the cosine second-variation lemma gives \(\Theta'(0)=0\) and \(\Theta''(0)=1/(4\pi^2)\).  Two applications of the mean-value theorem therefore yield
\[
|\Theta(t')-\Theta(t)|\gtrsim\rho n^{-1/2}. \tag{13}
\]
The same two-point testing argument gives
\[
\mathcal R_{n,d}(\rho)\gtrsim\rho n^{-1/2}
\quad\text{for }\rho\ge K n^{-1/2}. \tag{14}
\]

Let \(r=r_{n,d}(s_z)\), whose polynomial exponent is less than one.  If \(\rho<K n^{-1/2}\), then \(\rho^2=o(r)\), so (10) equals the target minimum up to constants.  If \(\rho\ge K n^{-1/2}\) and \(\rho^2\le r\), (10) again supplies \(\rho^2\).  If \(\rho^2>r\), (10) and (14) supply \(\max\{r,\rho n^{-1/2}\}\ge(r+\rho n^{-1/2})/2\).  Therefore
\[
\mathcal R_{n,d}(\rho)\gtrsim
\min\{\rho^2,\rho n^{-1/2}+r_{n,d}(s_z)\}
=\mathfrak R_{n,d}(s_y,s_z,\rho). \tag{15}
\]

It remains to verify the strict gap and phase claims.  Write \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}=(\log n/n)^e\), where
\[
e=\{2+s_y^{-1}+ds_z^{-1}\}^{-1}
<\frac{s_z}{2s_z+d}<\frac{2s_z}{4s_z+d}. \tag{16}
\]
The polynomial exponent of \(g_n\) is \((2s_z+ed)/(2s_z+d)\), and
\[
\frac{4s_z}{4s_z+d}-\frac{2s_z+ed}{2s_z+d}
=\frac{d\{2s_z-e(4s_z+d)\}}{(4s_z+d)(2s_z+d)}>0. \tag{17}
\]
Together with the positive logarithmic power, (17) proves \(n^{-4s_z/(4s_z+d)}=o(g_n)\).  Monotonicity of \(x/(2x+d+1)\) and \(\tau<s_z\) imply
\[
a_\tau<\frac{s_z}{2s_z+d+1}<
\min\left\{\frac{4s_z}{4s_z+d},\frac12\right\}. \tag{18}
\]
When \(s_z\ge1\), direct cross-multiplication also gives
\[
1/(d+2)<\min\{4s_z/(4s_z+d),1/2\}. \tag{19}
\]
Thus neither global fallback establishes the intrinsic quadratic exponent.  Finally, solving \(\rho_{Q,n}^2=r_{n,d}(s_z)\) and \(\rho_{L,n}n^{-1/2}=r_{n,d}(s_z)\) gives the two displayed boundaries.  The second tends to zero exactly for \(s_z>d/4\); for \(s_z\le d/4\), the boundary quadratic scale is at least root-\(n\) while the first derivative vanishes, precluding a uniformly positive-variance root-\(n\) description.  This proves all claims.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem separates the intrinsic sign-mixture and regular two-point lower frontier from the optimized observable Gram, SmoothCOT, direct-COT, and zero-estimator upper map; its sign-mixture component also supports the broader fixed-radius isotropic transfer. \textit{Gap.} The actual pilot-error projection scale is slower than the intrinsic quadratic exponent, so the primitive class and the transferred isotropic slice both support rigorous brackets but not matched quadratic rates. \textit{Consumer.} Analysts can select the smallest certified explicit estimator bound and can reuse the quadratic-and-zero lower component on the recognizable SmoothCOT slice without adding a regular term there.

\begin{theorem}[thm:no-covariate-recovery]\label{thm:no-covariate-recovery}
Assume \(L>1\). When \(d=0\), use the no-covariate growing-outcome-resolution construction rather than setting \(d=0\) in the conditional tuning rule. Uniformly over \(\mathcal M_{n,0}(s_y,s_z,\rho)\),
\[
\inf_{\widetilde\theta_n}\sup_{(P_{0,n},P_{1,n})\in\mathcal M_{n,0}(s_y,s_z,\rho)}\{E(\widetilde\theta_n-\theta_n)^2\}^{1/2}\asymp\mathfrak R_{n,0}(s_y,s_z,\rho)=\min\{\rho^2,\rho n^{-1/2}+n^{-1}\}.
\]
At equality under the uniform density, the full-sample bias-retaining estimator has the exact benchmark
\[
n\widehat\theta^0_{J_y^0,n}\Rightarrow2\int_0^1\mathbb B(t)^2dt=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{\pi^2k^2}\xi_{1,k}^2,
\]
where \(J_y^0\to\infty\), \(2^{J_y^0}=o(n)\), and \(2^{-2(s_y+1)J_y^0}=o(n^{-1})\). Put \(X_n=\sqrt n(\widehat Q_{1,n}-\widehat Q_{0,n})\), \(P_n=P^Q_{J_y^0}\), and define the exact finite-sample center
\[
b_n:=n^{-1}E\|P_nX_n\|_2^2=E\widehat\theta^0_{J_y^0,n}.
\]
If \(\mathcal C_{\mathbb B}\) denotes the Brownian-bridge covariance operator, then
\[
b_n=2n^{-1}\operatorname{tr}(P_n\mathcal C_{\mathbb B})+o(n^{-1}),
\]
and
\[
n\{\widehat\theta^0_{J_y^0,n}-b_n\}
\Rightarrow2\int_0^1\mathbb B(t)^2dt-\frac13
=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{\pi^2k^2}(\xi_{1,k}^2-1).
\]
Thus the outcome resolution genuinely grows, the quadratic scale is \(n^{-1}\), and \(\rho_{Q,n}=\rho_{L,n}=n^{-1/2}\). This is the two-sample specialization of the del Barrio--Gin\'e--Utzet weighted quantile-process law.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(d=0\). Write \(F_a\) for the distribution function induced by \(q_{a,n}\), \(Q_a=F_a^{-1}\), \(D=Q_1-Q_0\), \(\widehat D=\widehat Q_{1,n}-\widehat Q_{0,n}\), and \(P_J=P^Q_{J_y}\). The one-dimensional increasing-coupling representation of the given Wasserstein functional and the definition of the estimator in \(\mathrm{def{:}no\text{-}covariate\text{-}branch}\) give
\[
\theta_n=\|D\|_2^2,
\qquad
\widehat\theta^0_{J_y,n}=\|P_J\widehat D\|_2^2. \tag{1}
\]
The support is \([0,1]\) by the model definition. By \(\mathrm{ass{:}conditional\text{-}density\text{-}bounds}\), \(q_{a,n}\ge c>0\), so each \(F_a\) is strictly increasing and every \(Q_a\) is \(c^{-1}\)-Lipschitz. Under the probability-integral transform,
\[
\widehat Q_{a,n}(u)=Q_a(U_{a,(k)}),
\qquad k=\lceil nu\rceil, \tag{2}
\]
where \(U_{a,(k)}\) is the \(k\)-th order statistic from \(n\) independent uniform variables; independence and the sample size here are from \(\mathrm{ass{:}two\text{-}arm\text{-}sampling}\). Integrating the beta density of \(U_{a,(k)}\) gives, for \(j\in\{1,2,3,4\}\),
\[
E(U_{a,(k)}^j)=\prod_{r=0}^{j-1}\frac{k+r}{n+1+r}. \tag{3}
\]
Expanding the centered second and fourth powers in (3), and using \(|k/(n+1)-u|\le2/n\), yields
\[
\sup_{a,u}E|U_{a,(k)}-u|^2\lesssim n^{-1},
\qquad
\sup_{a,u}E|U_{a,(k)}-u|^4\lesssim n^{-2}. \tag{4}
\]
Equations (2)--(4), the \(c^{-1}\)-Lipschitz property, Tonelli's theorem, and \((\int_0^1 f^2)^2\le\int_0^1 f^4\) imply
\[
E\|\widehat D-D\|_2^2\lesssim n^{-1},
\qquad
E\|\widehat D-D\|_2^4\lesssim n^{-2}. \tag{5}
\]

The identity \(Q_a'=1/(q_{a,n}\circ Q_a)\), the positive lower bound, and \(\mathrm{ass{:}anisotropic\text{-}conditional\text{-}smoothness}\) imply uniformly that \(Q_a\in\mathcal C^{s_y+1}([0,1])\). The regularity condition in \(\mathrm{ass{:}wavelet\text{-}regularity}\) and boundary-wavelet coefficient decay therefore give
\[
\|(I-P_J)D\|_2^2\lesssim2^{-2(s_y+1)J_y}. \tag{6}
\]
Set \(E_J=P_J(\widehat D-D)\) and \(B_J=(I-P_J)D\). Since \(P_J\) is an orthogonal projector, (1) gives the exact decomposition
\[
\widehat\theta^0_{J_y,n}-\theta_n
=2\langle P_JD,E_J\rangle+\|E_J\|_2^2-\|B_J\|_2^2. \tag{7}
\]
By (5)--(7), Cauchy--Schwarz, and \(\|P_Jf\|_2\le\|f\|_2\),
\[
\|\widehat\theta^0_{J_y,n}-\theta_n\|_{L^2(P)}
\lesssim\|D\|_2n^{-1/2}+n^{-1}+2^{-2(s_y+1)J_y}. \tag{8}
\]

For completeness, the local-radius condition controls the first term in (8) without identifying the two quantities. Put \(H_F=F_1-F_0\) and \(\bar q_n=(q_{0,n}+q_{1,n})/2\). The Neumann equation defining \(\mathcal G_{n,z}\), now with no \(z\), gives
\[
\rho_n^2=\int_0^1\frac{H_F(y)^2}{\bar q_n(y)}dy. \tag{9}
\]
Indeed, if \(u=\mathcal G_n(q_{1,n}-q_{0,n})\), integration from \(0\) to \(y\) and the Neumann boundary condition give \(u'(y)=-H_F(y)/\bar q_n(y)\), after which integration by parts gives (9). If \(T=Q_1\circ F_0\) is the increasing transport, then \(F_1(T(y))=F_0(y)\). The mean-value theorem and \(c\le q_{1,n},q_{0,n},\bar q_n\le C\) give
\[
c|T(y)-y|\le|H_F(y)|\le C|T(y)-y|,
\]
and hence
\[
\theta_n=\int_0^1|T(y)-y|^2q_{0,n}(y)dy
\le \frac{C^2}{c^2}\rho_n^2. \tag{10}
\]
Thus \(\|D\|_2=\theta_n^{1/2}\lesssim\rho_n\le\rho\), where the last inequality is \(\mathrm{ass{:}local\text{-}equality\text{-}radius}\) as incorporated in \(\mathrm{def{:}model\text{-}class}\). At \(J_y=J_y^0\), the tuning condition in \(\mathrm{def{:}no\text{-}covariate\text{-}branch}\) makes the last term in (8) \(o(n^{-1})\). Therefore the displayed estimator has root-mean-square error \(O(\rho n^{-1/2}+n^{-1})\). The zero estimator has error \(\theta_n=O(\rho^2)\) by (10), so the smaller of these two explicit sample maps proves the minimax upper bound.

We next prove the converse. Let \(\Theta(t)\) denote the causal endpoint and let \(\rho(t)\) denote the inverse-elliptic radius under the pair indexed by \(t\) in the fixed-frequency family of \(\mathrm{def{:}no\text{-}covariate\text{-}witness}\). Compare \(t=0\) with
\[
t'=c_0\min\{\rho,n^{-1/2}\}
\]
using a sufficiently small fixed \(c_0\). By \(\mathrm{lem{:}explicit\text{-}mixture\text{-}checks}\), both laws are positive, normalized, inside the declared H\"older ball because \(L>1\), and their product Hellinger distance is bounded away from its maximal value. The exact calculation behind \(\mathrm{lem{:}cosine\text{-}second\text{-}variation}\) gives
\[
\rho(t)^2=\frac{t^2}{8\pi^2},
\qquad
\Theta(t)-\Theta(0)=\frac{t^2}{8\pi^2}+O(|t|^3). \tag{11}
\]
Consequently the causal endpoints at these two experiments are separated by a fixed multiple of \(\min\{\rho^2,n^{-1}\}\).

For the regular part, take \(\rho\ge K n^{-1/2}\), where \(K\) is a sufficiently large fixed constant, and compare \(t=c_1\rho\) with \(t'=c_1\rho+c_2n^{-1/2}\). Choose the positive constants \(c_1,c_2\) small enough that (11) puts both radii below \(\rho\) and all densities inside the class; this is possible uniformly for \(0\le\rho\le1\) by the fixed interior slack \(L>1\). The fixed-frequency Hellinger assertion in \(\mathrm{lem{:}explicit\text{-}mixture\text{-}checks}\) again bounds the product distance. Here
\[
F_{a,t}(y)=y+(2a-1)\frac{t}{4\pi}\sin(2\pi y).
\]
On the chosen compact interval, \(\partial_yF_{a,t}=q_{a,t}^0\ge c\). The implicit-function theorem applied to \(F_{a,t}\bigl(Q_{a,t}(u)\bigr)=u\), followed by differentiation under the bounded integral defining \(\Theta(t)\), shows that \(\Theta\) is twice continuously differentiable there. By \(\mathrm{lem{:}cosine\text{-}second\text{-}variation}\), \(\Theta'(0)=0\) and \(\Theta''(0)=1/(4\pi^2)\). Shrinking \(c_1,c_2\) if necessary therefore ensures \(\Theta''(v)\ge1/(8\pi^2)\) throughout the interval. Two integrations then give
\[
\Theta(t')-\Theta(t)
=\int_t^{t'}\int_0^v\Theta''(w)\,dw\,dv
\gtrsim t(t'-t)
\asymp\rho n^{-1/2}. \tag{12}
\]
For either pair, thresholding an arbitrary estimator at the midpoint of the two endpoint values turns it into a test. The Hellinger affinity bound then implies that the larger of its two root-mean-square errors is at least a fixed multiple of the corresponding separation. Equation (11) covers \(\rho\lesssim n^{-1/2}\); (12) covers \(\rho\gtrsim n^{-1/2}\), with the overlap constants absorbed. Hence
\[
\inf_{\widetilde\theta_n}\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,0}(s_y,s_z,\rho)}
\{E_P(\widetilde\theta_n-\theta_n)^2\}^{1/2}
\gtrsim\min\{\rho^2,\rho n^{-1/2}+n^{-1}\}. \tag{13}
\]
Together with the upper bound, this proves the minimax equivalence.

It remains to prove the two limit statements and the corrected centering. Under conditional equality at the uniform density, define
\[
u_{a,n}(t)=\sqrt n\{t-\widehat Q_{a,n}(t)\},
\qquad
u_{a,n}^{\mathrm{tr}}(t)=u_{a,n}(t)\mathbf1\{1/n<t<1-1/n\}.
\]
The established \(\mathrm{lem{:}del\text{-}barrio\text{-}gine\text{-}utzet\text{-}uniform\text{-}quantile\text{-}limit}\) yields \(u_{a,n}^{\mathrm{tr}}\Rightarrow\mathbb B_a\) in \(L^2(0,1)\). Applying (3) to the two extreme order statistics gives
\[
E\|u_{a,n}-u_{a,n}^{\mathrm{tr}}\|_2^2=O(n^{-2}), \tag{14}
\]
so Slutsky's theorem removes the truncation. Independence of the arms from \(\mathrm{ass{:}two\text{-}arm\text{-}sampling}\) therefore gives
\[
X_n:=\sqrt n(\widehat Q_{1,n}-\widehat Q_{0,n})
\Rightarrow\mathbb B_0-\mathbb B_1\stackrel d=\sqrt2\,\mathbb B
\quad\text{in }L^2(0,1). \tag{15}
\]
Put \(P_n=P^Q_{J_y^0}\). Because \(J_y^0\to\infty\) and the nested boundary-wavelet spaces are dense, \(P_n\to I\) strongly. The \(P_n\) are contractions, so strong convergence is uniform on compact subsets of \(L^2\). Tightness in (15) then implies
\[
\|(I-P_n)X_n\|_2=o_P(1). \tag{16}
\]
Equations (15)--(16) and the continuous mapping theorem yield
\[
Z_n:=\|P_nX_n\|_2^2=n\widehat\theta^0_{J_y^0,n}
\Rightarrow2\int_0^1\mathbb B(t)^2dt. \tag{17}
\]

The centering requires expectations, not merely weak convergence. At equality \(D=0\), the fourth-moment bound (5) and projector contraction give
\[
\sup_n E Z_n^2
=\sup_nE\|P_nX_n\|_2^4
\le\sup_n n^2E\|\widehat D\|_2^4<\infty. \tag{18}
\]
Thus \((Z_n)_n\) is uniformly integrable. From (17)--(18),
\[
EZ_n\longrightarrow2E\|\mathbb B\|_2^2. \tag{19}
\]
Let \(\mathcal C_{\mathbb B}\) be the Brownian-bridge covariance operator, whose kernel is \(K(s,t)=\min(s,t)-st\). It is positive and trace class, and
\[
\operatorname{tr}(\mathcal C_{\mathbb B})
=\int_0^1K(t,t)dt
=\int_0^1t(1-t)dt=\frac16. \tag{20}
\]
Strong convergence \(P_n\to I\) and positivity of \(\mathcal C_{\mathbb B}\) imply
\[
\operatorname{tr}(P_n\mathcal C_{\mathbb B})\to
\operatorname{tr}(\mathcal C_{\mathbb B}). \tag{21}
\]
Indeed, for an eigenbasis \((e_j)\) of the positive trace-class operator with eigenvalues \((\lambda_j)\),
\[
0\le\operatorname{tr}\{(I-P_n)\mathcal C_{\mathbb B}\}
=\sum_j\lambda_j\|(I-P_n)e_j\|_2^2\to0
\]
by dominated convergence and \(\sum_j\lambda_j<\infty\). Since \(E\|\mathbb B\|_2^2=\operatorname{tr}(\mathcal C_{\mathbb B})\), (19)--(21) show
\[
b_n:=n^{-1}EZ_n=E\widehat\theta^0_{J_y^0,n}
=2n^{-1}\operatorname{tr}(P_n\mathcal C_{\mathbb B})+o(n^{-1}). \tag{22}
\]
In particular, \(EZ_n\to1/3\). Slutsky's theorem applied to (17) and (22) now gives
\[
n\{\widehat\theta^0_{J_y^0,n}-b_n\}
=Z_n-EZ_n
\Rightarrow2\int_0^1\mathbb B(t)^2dt-\frac13. \tag{23}
\]
Finally, direct differentiation of the covariance kernel shows that \(\sqrt2\sin(\pi kt)\) are the eigenfunctions of \(\mathcal C_{\mathbb B}\), with eigenvalues \((\pi^2k^2)^{-1}\). Expanding the centered Gaussian element in this covariance eigenbasis gives
\[
2\int_0^1\mathbb B(t)^2dt
=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{\pi^2k^2}\xi_{1,k}^2,
\qquad
2\int_0^1\mathbb B(t)^2dt-\frac13
=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{\pi^2k^2}(\xi_{1,k}^2-1), \tag{24}
\]
where both series converge in \(L^1\), and the centered series also converges in \(L^2\), because \(\sum_k k^{-2}<\infty\) and \(\sum_k k^{-4}<\infty\). Balancing the three proved scales \(\rho^2\), \(\rho n^{-1/2}\), and \(n^{-1}\) yields \(\rho_{Q,n}=\rho_{L,n}=n^{-1/2}\), as claimed.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:anisotropic-smoothcot-median-envelope]\label{lem:anisotropic-smoothcot-median-envelope}
Let \(d\ge1\) and fix \(0<\tau<\min\{s_y,s_z\}\). Put \(\epsilon_\tau=\{\min(s_y,s_z)-\tau\}/2\), \(a_\tau=\tau/(2\tau+d+1)\), and \(p_{a,n}(y,z)=q_{a,n}(y,z)r_n(z)\). Uniformly over \(\mathcal M_{n,d}(s_y,s_z,\rho)\),
\[
c^2\le p_{a,n}(y,z)\le C^2,
\qquad
p_{a,n}\in\mathcal C^{\tau+\epsilon_\tau}([0,1]^{d+1}),
\qquad
\sup_{a,n}\|p_{a,n}\|_{\mathcal C^{\tau+\epsilon_\tau}}\le H_\tau<\infty,
\]
where \(H_\tau\) depends only on \((c,C,L,s_y,s_z,d,\tau)\). The explicit estimator in \(\mathrm{def{:}smoothcot\text{-}median\text{-}estimator}\) then satisfies
\[
\sup_{(P_{0,n},P_{1,n})\in\mathcal M_{n,d}(s_y,s_z,\rho)}
\left\{E(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{sm,med}}_{\tau,n}-\theta_n)^2\right\}^{1/2}
\le C_\tau n^{-a_\tau}(\log n)^{a_\tau}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\), with \(C_\tau\) depending only on the same fixed quantities.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(s_*=\min\{s_y,s_z\}\) and \(\alpha=\tau+\epsilon_\tau=(s_*+\tau)/2<s_*\). By the common-marginal factorization and the density bounds,
\[
p_{a,n}(y,z)=q_{a,n}(y,z)r_n(z),
\qquad c^2\le p_{a,n}(y,z)\le C^2.                                 \tag{1}
\]
We verify the isotropic H\"older inclusion. Let \(k=\lfloor\alpha\rfloor\), write a multi-index on \([0,1]^{d+1}\) as \(\mu=(\mu_y,\mu_z)\), and take \(|\mu|\le k\). Since \(k<\alpha<s_*\), every derivative below is licensed by \(\mathrm{ass{:}anisotropic\text{-}conditional\text{-}smoothness}\) and \(\mathrm{ass{:}covariate\text{-}holder\text{-}smoothness}\). The product rule gives
\[
D^\mu p_{a,n}(y,z)
=\sum_{\nu_z\le\mu_z}{\mu_z\choose\nu_z}
\{D_y^{\mu_y}D_z^{\nu_z}q_{a,n}(y,z)\}
\{D_z^{\mu_z-\nu_z}r_n(z)\}.                                    \tag{2}
\]
Every factor on the right of (2) is uniformly bounded by the radius \(L\), so \(\|D^\mu p_{a,n}\|_\infty\le H_{0,\tau}\), where the constant depends only on \((L,d,k)\). For \(|\mu|=k\), apply the identity \(fg-f'g'=f(g-g')+g'(f-f')\) to each summand in (2), and change the coordinates from \((y,z)\) to \((y',z')\) one at a time. The anisotropic H\"older bounds and \(\alpha<s_y,s_z\) give
\[
|D^\mu p_{a,n}(y,z)-D^\mu p_{a,n}(y',z')|
\le H_{1,\tau}\left(|y-y'|^{\alpha-k}+\sum_{j=1}^d|z_j-z'_j|^{\alpha-k}\right)
\le H_{2,\tau}\|(y,z)-(y',z')\|_2^{\alpha-k}.                    \tag{3}
\]
For integer \(\alpha\), the same coordinate telescoping gives the source's integer-order modulus convention; alternatively one may reduce \(\epsilon_\tau\) by a fixed factor without changing any rate. Equations (2)--(3) are precisely the isotropic \(\mathcal C^\alpha\) norm bound, proving
\[
\sup_{a,n}\|p_{a,n}\|_{\mathcal C^\alpha}\le H_\tau.            \tag{4}
\]

Apply the cited \(\mathrm{lem{:}lin\text{-}gao\text{-}blanchet\text{-}glynn\text{-}smoothcot\text{-}rate}\) with outcome dimension one, covariate dimension \(d\), smoothness parameter \(\tau\), extra smoothness \(\epsilon_\tau\), and squared cost. On \([0,1]^2\),
\[
|(y_1-y_0)^2-(y_1'-y_0')^2|
\le2(|y_0-y_0'|+|y_1-y_1'|),                                      \tag{5}
\]
so its Lipschitz constant is fixed. The theorem's printed constant is law-dependent, so uniformity must be checked rather than assumed. Its proof has three law-dependent inputs: a reciprocal density bound, the joint wavelet \(L^2\) constant, and the high-probability positivity constant. By (1), the first is bounded by \(\max\{c^{-2},C^2\}\). At a generic resolution \(J\), orthogonality, the wavelet characterization of (4), and bounded variance of empirical coefficients give, uniformly in the law,
\[
E\|\widetilde p_{a,J}-p_{a,n}\|_2^2
\le C_\tau\left\{2^{-2\tau J}+\frac{2^{(d+1)J}}{N_n^{\mathrm{sm}}}\right\}. \tag{6}
\]
At \(J=J_{\tau,n}^{\mathrm{sm}}\), both terms in (6) are at most a constant times \((N_n^{\mathrm{sm}})^{-2a_\tau}\). The positive-part normalization is inactive on the event \(\|\widetilde p_{a,J}-p_{a,n}\|_\infty<c^2/2\). Compact wavelet support, coefficientwise Bernstein inequalities, and a union bound over \(O(2^{(d+1)J})\) coefficients give
\[
P\{\|\widetilde p_{a,J}-p_{a,n}\|_\infty\ge c^2/2\}
\le C_\tau(N_n^{\mathrm{sm}})^{-2},                               \tag{7}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\): the deterministic bias is \(O(2^{-\alpha J})\), while the stochastic sup-norm scale is \(O\{(J2^{(d+1)J}/N_n^{\mathrm{sm}})^{1/2}\}=o(1)\) because \(\alpha>\tau\). Thus the constants in (6)--(7), and hence the Besov and conditional-density bounds in the cited theorem's proof, are controlled only by \((c,C,H_\tau,\tau,d)\).

For completeness, the only derivative-dependent marginal comparison in the source proof is unnecessary here. If \(f(z)=W_2^2(Q_{0,n}(\cdot\mid z),Q_{1,n}(\cdot\mid z))\), \(\widehat f\) is its block analogue, and \(\widehat r\) is the aligned block marginal, then \(0\le f,\widehat f\le1\) and
\[
\left|\int\widehat f\,\widehat r-\int f r_n\right|
\le\|\widehat r-r_n\|_1+
2\int\{W_1(\widehat Q_0^z,Q_0^z)+W_1(\widehat Q_1^z,Q_1^z)\}\widehat r(z)\,dz. \tag{8}
\]
The first term is bounded by the joint \(L^2\) error in (6); the second is the conditional negative-order term bounded in the cited proof. Equations (6)--(8) therefore uniformize its conclusion:
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}
E_P|\widehat V_{\ell,\tau,n}^{\mathrm{sm}}-\theta_n|
\le C_\tau(N_n^{\mathrm{sm}})^{-a_\tau}.                           \tag{9}
\]

Let \(\delta_n=C_\tau(N_n^{\mathrm{sm}})^{-a_\tau}\). Markov's inequality applied to (9) makes a block bad at threshold \(8\delta_n\) with probability at most \(1/8\). Disjoint blocks are independent by \(\mathrm{ass{:}two\text{-}arm\text{-}sampling}\). If the median is bad, at least \((K_n^{\mathrm{sm}}+1)/2\) blocks are bad; hence
\[
P\{|\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{sm,med}}_{\tau,n}-\theta_n|>8\delta_n\}
\le2^{K_n^{\mathrm{sm}}}8^{-(K_n^{\mathrm{sm}}+1)/2}
\le2^{-K_n^{\mathrm{sm}}/2}.                                      \tag{10}
\]
Choose the fixed multiplier in \(K_n^{\mathrm{sm}}\asymp\log n\) so that the last quantity is at most \(\delta_n^2\). Both the estimator and \(\theta_n\) lie in \([0,1]\), so
\[
E(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{sm,med}}_{\tau,n}-\theta_n)^2
\le64\delta_n^2+2^{-K_n^{\mathrm{sm}}/2}
\le65\delta_n^2.                                                   \tag{11}
\]
Finally, \(N_n^{\mathrm{sm}}\asymp n/\log n\), and (11) is the asserted root-mean-square rate.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:direct-cot-median-envelope]\label{lem:direct-cot-median-envelope}
Let \(d\ge1\) and \(s_z\ge1\). Choose an odd \(K_n\asymp\log n\), split each arm into \(K_n\) disjoint blocks of size \(N_n=\lfloor n/K_n\rfloor\), and on each block use covariate cells of side \(N_n^{-1/(d+2)}\). Project covariates to cell centers, form the empirical conditional outcome law at each occupied center, optimally couple the two projected empirical covariate marginals in Euclidean distance, integrate the squared Wasserstein distance between paired empirical conditional outcome laws, and take the median of the block values. The resulting explicit estimator satisfies
\[
\sup_{(P_{0,n},P_{1,n})\in\mathcal M_{n,d}(s_y,s_z,\rho)}
\left\{E(\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{dir,med}}-\theta_n)^2\right\}^{1/2}
\lesssim n^{-1/(d+2)}(\log n)^{1+1/(d+2)}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The squared cost obeys
\[
|(y_1-y_0)^2-(y_1'-y_0')^2|
\le2(|y_0-y_0'|+|y_1-y_1'|).                                      \tag{1}
\]
Because \(s_z\ge1\), \(\mathrm{ass{:}anisotropic\text{-}conditional\text{-}smoothness}\) gives
\[
\int_0^1|q_{a,n}(y,z)-q_{a,n}(y,z')|\,dy
\le C_L\|z-z'\|_2.                                                \tag{2}
\]
Coupling common conditional mass identically and residual mass arbitrarily on the unit interval yields
\[
W_1(Q_{a,n}(\cdot\mid z),Q_{a,n}(\cdot\mid z'))
\le\frac12\int_0^1|q_{a,n}(y,z)-q_{a,n}(y,z')|\,dy
\le C_L\|z-z'\|_2.                                                \tag{3}
\]
Thus the cost and conditional-kernel hypotheses of \(\mathrm{lem{:}lin\text{-}gao\text{-}blanchet\text{-}glynn\text{-}direct\text{-}cot\text{-}rate}\) hold uniformly.

On a block of size \(N\), let \(h_N=N^{-1/(d+2)}\). There are \(M_N\lesssim h_N^{-d}\) cells. If \(p_j\) is the common marginal mass of cell \(j\), binomial variance and Cauchy--Schwarz give, for each arm,
\[
E\|\widehat R_{a,N}^{\varphi}-R_n^\varphi\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\le\frac12\sum_{j=1}^{M_N}\sqrt{\frac{p_j(1-p_j)}N}
\le\frac12\sqrt{\frac{M_N}{N}}
\lesssim N^{-1/(d+2)}.                                             \tag{4}
\]
Projection to cell centers costs at most \(\sqrt d\,h_N\). The triangle inequality through the common projected marginal and the bound \(W_1\le\sqrt d\,\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\) on the unit cube therefore construct a coupling satisfying the cited theorem's coupling-gap premise with constant times \(N^{-1/(d+2)}\). The cited direct-COT theorem gives for each bounded block value
\[
E|\widehat\theta_N^{\mathrm{dir}}-\theta_n|
\le\delta_N:=C N^{-1/(d+2)}\log N.                                \tag{5}
\]

Markov's inequality makes a block bad at threshold \(8\delta_N\) with probability at most \(1/8\). The blocks are independent by the two-arm sampling assumption. If the median is bad, at least half the blocks are bad, and therefore
\[
P\{|\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{dir,med}}-\theta_n|>8\delta_{N_n}\}
\le2^{K_n}8^{-K_n/2}=2^{-K_n/2}.                                  \tag{6}
\]
Choose the fixed multiplier in \(K_n\asymp\log n\) so that the last probability is \(O(\delta_{N_n}^2)\). Since both the block median and the endpoint lie in \([0,1]\),
\[
\{E(\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{dir,med}}-\theta_n)^2\}^{1/2}
\lesssim\delta_{N_n}
\lesssim n^{-1/(d+2)}(\log n)^{1+1/(d+2)}.                         \tag{7}
\]
All constants in (1)--(7) are uniform over the declared class, proving the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:growing-kernel-contractions]\label{lem:growing-kernel-contractions}
Let \(d\ge1\), \(J_y,J_z\to\infty\), \(D_{J_y,J_z}=o(n^2)\), and let \(n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}\) be the canonical quadratic projection obtained from the raw-moment Hessian. Put \(M_n=2^{dJ_z}\). After indexing the independent evaluation observations by \(r\), it has an explicit representation \(n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}=\sum_{r<s}h_{n,rs}(W_r,W_s)\), with each kernel canonical in either argument, such that
\[
\operatorname{Var}(n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n})=A_{J_y,J_z,n}^2\{1+o(1)\},
\]
\[
\max_r\sum_{s\ne r}E h_{n,rs}^2\lesssim \frac{M_n}{m},
\qquad
\sum_{r<s}E h_{n,rs}^4\lesssim\frac{M_n^3}{m^2},
\qquad
\frac{\operatorname{tr}(K_{J_y,J_z,n}^4)}{\operatorname{tr}(K_{J_y,J_z,n}^2)^2}\lesssim M_n^{-1}.
\]
If \(G_n=\sum_r\ell_{n,r}(W_r)\) is the projected linear term, its summands are centered and uniformly bounded, \(E G_n^2=O(S_n^2)\), and \(S_n\ge A_{J_y,J_z,n}\), then for the filtration generated by the ordered observations and
\[
\mathcal D_{n,r}=\ell_{n,r}(W_r)+\sum_{s<r}h_{n,sr}(W_s,W_r),
\]
the array is a martingale-difference array and, for every \(\epsilon>0\),
\[
\sum_rE\left[\left(\frac{\mathcal D_{n,r}}{S_n}\right)^2
\mathbf 1\{|\mathcal D_{n,r}|>\epsilon S_n\}\right]\to0,
\]
\[
\frac{E\left[\left\{\sum_rE(\mathcal D_{n,r}^2\mid\mathcal F_{r-1})-E(G_n+n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n})^2\right\}^2\right]}{S_n^4}
\lesssim m^{-1}+\frac{M_n}{m^2}+M_n^{-1}+o(1)\to0.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(M_n=2^{dJ_z}\), \(B=B_{J_y,J_z,n}\), \(K=K_{J_y,J_z,n}\), and \(A=A_{J_y,J_z,n}\).  Replace the covariate wavelets through \(J_z\) by the orthonormal boundary-scaling basis of the same space.  This orthogonal change does not alter the scalar kernel or the eigenvalues of \(K\).  Its covariate Gram matrix \(G_z\) is banded by compact support and satisfies \(cI\preceq G_z\preceq CI\).  With \(a=(C+c)/2\) and \(E=I-G_z/a\),
\[
\|E\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le q:=\frac{C-c}{C+c}<1.
\]
The norm-convergent Neumann series and bandedness of \(E^j\) give
\[
|G_z^{-1}(k,l)|\le Cq^{c\operatorname{dist}(k,l)}. \tag{1}
\]
Consequently the weighted projection kernel satisfies
\[
|\kappa_{J_z}(z,v)|\le CM_n\exp\{-c2^{J_z}\|z-v\|_2\}. \tag{2}
\]
The Green kernel of \(P_{J_y}\mathcal G_{n,z}P_{J_y}\) is uniformly bounded: integrate the Neumann equation to obtain \(u'=-\bar q_n^{-1}\int_0^\cdot h\), use \(\bar q_n\ge c\), normalize \(\int u=0\), and use the uniformly bounded \(L^1\)-norm of the boundary-wavelet projector kernel.  Thus the raw-moment kernel, including both Gram conjugations, obeys
\[
|b_J\{(y,z),(y',z')\}|
\le CM_n\exp\{-c2^{J_z}\|z-z'\|_2\}. \tag{3}
\]
Integrating over a covariate tube of volume \(O(M_n^{-1})\), and using the joint-density upper bound, gives for \(p=2,4\)
\[
\max_{a,b}\iint|b_J(w,w')|^p,dP_a(w)dP_b(w')\lesssim M_n^{p-1},
\quad
\max_{a,b,w}\int|b_J(w,w')|^p,dP_b(w')\lesssim M_n^{p-1}. \tag{4}
\]
Centering in both arguments preserves (4) by Jensen's inequality.  Hence the centered kernel \(b_{ab}^{\circ}\) satisfies
\[
E|b_{ab}^{\circ}(W_a,W_b)|^2\lesssim M_n,qquad
E|b_{ab}^{\circ}(W_a,W_b)|^4\lesssim M_n^3. \tag{5}
\]

For the four-cycle contraction, covariance algebra and \(0\preceq\Gamma_a\preceq\Gamma\) identify its squared norm with a cyclic trace bounded by \(\operatorname{tr}(K^4)\).  The spectral proposition gives
\[
\operatorname{tr}(K^4)\le\|K\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2\operatorname{tr}(K^2)lesssim M_n,
\qquad
\frac{\operatorname{tr}(K^4)}{\operatorname{tr}(K^2)^2}\lesssim M_n^{-1}. \tag{6}
\]

Index an evaluation observation by \(r=(e,a,i)\), put \(Z_r=\Phi(W_r)-E\Phi(W_r)\), and order observations foldwise.  Direct expansion of the diagonal-free statistic, using \(n=2m\), gives
\[
h_{n,rs}(x,y)=
\begin{cases}
2x^\top By/(m-1),&e_r=e_s,\ a_r=a_s,\\
-2x^\top By/m,&e_r=e_s,\ a_r\ne a_s,\\
0,&e_r\ne e_s,
\end{cases}
\quad
n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n}=\sum_{r<s}h_{n,rs}(Z_r,Z_s). \tag{7}
\]
Independence and \(EZ_r=0\) make every kernel canonical.  There are \(O(m)\) edges incident to a vertex and \(O(m^2)\) edges in total.  Equations (5)--(7) give
\[
\max_r\sum_{s\ne r}Eh_{n,rs}^2\lesssim M_n/m,qquad
\sum_{r<s}Eh_{n,rs}^4\lesssim M_n^3/m^2. \tag{8}
\]
Distinct canonical edges are orthogonal, including edges sharing one vertex.  Summing edge variances yields
\[
\operatorname{Var}(n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n})
=4\operatorname{tr}\{B(\Gamma_0+\Gamma_1)B(\Gamma_0+\Gamma_1)\}
+O(m^{-1})\operatorname{tr}(K^2)
=A^2\{1+O(m^{-1})\}. \tag{9}
\]

Let \(q_r=\sum_{s<r}h_{n,sr}\) and \(\mathcal D_{n,r}=\ell_{n,r}+q_r\).  Canonicality makes this a martingale-difference array.  Conditional on \(W_r\), the summands in \(q_r\) are independent and centered.  Expanding the fourth power, and using (5) and (8), gives
\[
\sum_rEq_r^4\lesssim M_n^2/m+M_n^3/m^2. \tag{10}
\]
Uniform boundedness of \(\ell_{n,r}\) and \(EG_n^2=O(S_n^2)\) give \(\sum_rE\ell_{n,r}^4=O(S_n^2)\).  Since \(S_n\ge A\asymp M_n^{1/2}\),
\[
S_n^{-4}\sum_rE\mathcal D_{n,r}^4
\lesssim M_n^{-1}+m^{-1}+M_n/m^2\to0. \tag{11}
\]
Here \(M_n\to\infty\) because \(J_z\to\infty\), and \(M_n\le D_{J_y,J_z}=o(m^2)\).  The inequality \(x^2\mathbf1\{|x|>\epsilon S_n\}\le x^4/(\epsilon^2S_n^2)\) proves Lindeberg.

Expanding the predictable quadratic variation separates a mixed linear--quadratic contraction \(C_n\), a repeated-edge term \(R_n\), and a shared-terminal-vertex term \(T_n\).  Independence, (5), and the edge coefficients give
\[
ER_n^2\lesssim M_n^2/m. \tag{12}
\]
Regrouping \(T_n\) over unordered initial pairs and applying the four-cycle bound (6) gives
\[
ET_n^2\lesssim M_n. \tag{13}
\]
For \(u_r=E(Z_r\ell_{n,r})\), covariance Cauchy--Schwarz gives \(\|\Gamma_{a_r}^{-1/2}u_r\|_2^2\le E\ell_{n,r}^2\), while \(\|\Gamma_a^{1/2}B\Gamma_b^{1/2}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\|K\|_{\mathrm{op}}\lesssim1\).  Regrouping over the initial vertex therefore gives
\[
EC_n^2\lesssim EG_n^2=O(S_n^2). \tag{14}
\]
Equations (12)--(14), \(S_n^4\gtrsim M_n^2\), and \(S_n^2\gtrsim M_n\) prove
\[
\frac{E\left[\left\{\sum_rE(\mathcal D_{n,r}^2\mid\mathcal F_{r-1})
-E(G_n+n\mathbb U_{J_y,J_z,n})^2\right\}^2\right]}{S_n^4}
\lesssim m^{-1}+M_n/m^2+M_n^{-1}+o(1)\to0. \tag{15}
\]
Together, (1)--(15) prove all asserted contractions and limits.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The finite Gaussian--quadratic proxy needs moment and influence control after both nonlocal raw-moment Gram conjugations. \textit{Gap.} The proof now closes exponential inverse-Gram localization, fourth moments, repeated-edge and four-cycle contractions, the mixed contraction, Lindeberg, and predictable-variance concentration from the primitive assumptions. \textit{Consumer.} The mixed spectral-transition theorem uses the lemma to compare the population quadratic statistic with its Gaussian--quadratic array and to prove diffuse Gaussianization.

\begin{proposition}[prop:observable-pilot-feasibility-frontier]\label{prop:observable-pilot-feasibility-frontier}
Put \(a_y=\min\{s_y,1\}\) and \(e=\beta/(2\beta+1)=\{2+s_y^{-1}+ds_z^{-1}\}^{-1}\). For \(d\ge1\), the corrected full-minus-projected pilot-error term is bounded by
\[
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\{2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-s_zJ_z}\}.
\]
Balancing its covariate component with \(A_{J_y,J_z,n}/n\asymp2^{dJ_z/2}/n\), and then making the outcome component of the same order, gives exactly
\[
2^{J_z^*}\asymp\{n\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\}^{2/(2s_z+d)},
\qquad
2^{J_y^*}\asymp\{n\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\}^{2s_z/\{(s_y+1)(2s_z+d)\}},
\]
with optimized scale
\[
n^{-2s_z/(2s_z+d)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{d/(2s_z+d)}.
\]
The corresponding projection dimension is \(o(n^2)\).  Moreover,
\[
e<\frac{s_z}{2s_z+d}<\frac{2s_z}{4s_z+d},
\]
and hence, including the positive logarithmic power in \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\),
\[
\frac{n^{-2s_z/(2s_z+d)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{d/(2s_z+d)}}{n^{-4s_z/(4s_z+d)}}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Thus there is no smoothness region in which the corrected certified Gram envelope is uniformly absorbed into \(\rho n^{-1/2}+n^{-4s_z/(4s_z+d)}\); the earlier pilot-feasibility region disappears once the actual pilot-error Hessian gap is retained.

For every fixed \(0<\tau<\min\{s_y,s_z\}\), the explicit median-aggregated SmoothCOT estimator satisfies, uniformly over \(\mathcal M_{n,d}(s_y,s_z,\rho)\),
\[
\left\{E(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{sm,med}}_{\tau,n}-\theta_n)^2\right\}^{1/2}
\lesssim_\tau n^{-a_\tau}(\log n)^{a_\tau},
\qquad a_\tau=\frac{\tau}{2\tau+d+1}.
\]
When \(s_z\ge1\), the median-aggregated direct-COT estimator satisfies
\[
\left\{E(\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{dir,med}}-\theta_n)^2\right\}^{1/2}
\lesssim n^{-1/(d+2)}(\log n)^{1+1/(d+2)}.
\]
Accordingly, the certified upper bracket is the deterministic minimum of the zero-estimator, corrected Gram, SmoothCOT, and applicable direct-COT envelopes.  The two global envelopes may sharpen the upper side, but \(a_\tau<\min\{4s_z/(4s_z+d),1/2\}\) and, when \(s_z\ge1\), \(1/(d+2)<\min\{4s_z/(4s_z+d),1/2\}\), so neither proves the intrinsic quadratic exponent.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put
\[
e=\frac{\beta}{2\beta+1}=\{2+s_y^{-1}+ds_z^{-1}\}^{-1},
\qquad \varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}=(\log n/n)^e.
\]
The exact conditional von Mises identity in \(\mathrm{lem{:}observable\text{-}pilot\text{-}control}\) bounds the full-minus-projected pilot-error contribution by
\[
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}
\{2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-s_zJ_z}\}. \tag{1}
\]
In particular, this is an error of the pilot discrepancy, not either true-discrepancy energy gap.  The spectral proposition gives
\[
A_{J_y,J_z,n}/n\asymp2^{dJ_z/2}/n. \tag{2}
\]
Writing \(x=2^{J_z}\), balance the covariate term in (1) with (2):
\[
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}x^{-s_z}=n^{-1}x^{d/2}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
2^{J_z^*}\asymp\{n\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\}^{2/(2s_z+d)}. \tag{3}
\]
Equating the outcome term to the same scale gives
\[
2^{-(s_y+1)J_y^*}=2^{-s_zJ_z^*},
\qquad
2^{J_y^*}\asymp
\{n\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\}^{2s_z/\{(s_y+1)(2s_z+d)\}}. \tag{4}
\]
Substitution proves the optimized scale
\[
g_n=n^{-2s_z/(2s_z+d)}
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{d/(2s_z+d)}. \tag{5}
\]
Moreover,
\[
D_{J_y^*,J_z^*}
=\{n\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\}^{\kappa+o(1)},
\qquad
\kappa=\frac{2s_z/(s_y+1)+2d}{2s_z+d}<2, \tag{6}
\]
so \(D_{J_y^*,J_z^*}=o(n^2)\).

Since
\[
e<\frac{s_z}{2s_z+d}<\frac{2s_z}{4s_z+d}, \tag{7}
\]
direct subtraction gives
\[
\frac{4s_z}{4s_z+d}-\frac{2s_z+ed}{2s_z+d}
=\frac{d\{2s_z-e(4s_z+d)\}}{(4s_z+d)(2s_z+d)}>0. \tag{8}
\]
The logarithmic power in (5) is positive, so
\[
g_n/n^{-4s_z/(4s_z+d)}\to\infty. \tag{9}
\]
Thus no positive finite smoothness pair permits uniform absorption of the certified Gram scale into the intrinsic quadratic scale.

The established SmoothCOT median lemma gives, for fixed \(0<\tau<\min\{s_y,s_z\}\),
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}
\|\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{sm,med}}_{\tau,n}-\theta_n\|_2
\lesssim_\tau n^{-a_\tau}(\log n)^{a_\tau}. \tag{10}
\]
When \(s_z\ge1\), the established direct-COT median lemma gives
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{n,d}}
\|\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm{dir,med}}-\theta_n\|_2
\lesssim n^{-1/(d+2)}(\log n)^{1+1/(d+2)}. \tag{11}
\]
The zero-estimator guarantee follows from the energy comparison in \(\mathrm{thm{:}conditional\text{-}minimax\text{-}frontier}\), while the Gram guarantee follows from the uniform expansion and (3)--(5).  Selecting, before observing data, the smallest deterministic guarantee among these explicit sample maps proves the certified upper minimum.

Finally, monotonicity of \(x/(2x+d+1)\) and \(\tau<s_z\) imply
\[
a_\tau<\frac{s_z}{2s_z+d+1}<
\min\left\{\frac{4s_z}{4s_z+d},\frac12\right\}. \tag{12}
\]
If \(s_z\ge1\), direct cross-multiplication gives
\[
1/(d+2)<\min\{4s_z/(4s_z+d),1/2\}. \tag{13}
\]
Equations (1)--(13) prove all claims.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The proposition solves the corrected tuning optimization and proves that the former nonempty absorption region disappears. \textit{Gap.} No available primitive-model control reduces the pilot-error projection gap to the intrinsic covariate quadratic scale. \textit{Consumer.} The upper bracket, estimand functional, and calibration width use the corrected resolutions.

\begin{proposition}[prop:scalar-smoothcot-local-minimax-bracket-transfer]\label{prop:scalar-smoothcot-local-minimax-bracket-transfer}
Fix \(d\ge1\), \(\beta_{\mathrm{iso}}>0\), \(\gamma>1\), \(H>\|1\|_{\mathcal C^{\beta_{\mathrm{iso}}}([0,1]^{d+1})}\) with fixed positive slack, the fixed ceiling \(\rho_{\mathrm{adm}}\), and \(0\le\rho\le\rho_{\mathrm{adm}}\). Fix the compactly supported boundary-corrected wavelet bases \(\Psi_y\) and \(\Psi_z\) used in the median SmoothCOT estimator and the sign-paired family, and assume that both bases have regularity strictly greater than \(\beta_{\mathrm{iso}}\). For every fixed \(0<\tau<\beta_{\mathrm{iso}}\), put \(a_\tau=\tau/(2\tau+d+1)\). There are constants \(0<c_{\mathrm{sm}}\le C_{\mathrm{sm},\tau}<\infty\), depending only on \(\beta_{\mathrm{iso}},d,\gamma,H,\tau\), the fixed ceiling, and the boundary-wavelet constants, but independent of \(n,\rho\), and the model element, such that, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
c_{\mathrm{sm}}\min\!\left\{\rho^2,n^{-4\beta_{\mathrm{iso}}/(4\beta_{\mathrm{iso}}+d)}\right\}
\le
\inf_{\widetilde\theta_n}\sup_{(P_{0,n},P_{1,n})\in\mathcal S^{\mathrm{sm}}_{n,d}(\beta_{\mathrm{iso}},H,\gamma,\rho)}
\left\{E(\widetilde\theta_n-\theta_n)^2\right\}^{1/2}
\le
C_{\mathrm{sm},\tau}\min\!\left\{\rho^2,n^{-a_\tau}(\log n)^{a_\tau}\right\}.
\]
The two upper entries are attained by the zero estimator and by the existing median-aggregated aligned wavelet SmoothCOT estimator \(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{sm,med}}_{\tau,n}\), respectively. The lower bound is attained as a testing separation by the existing sign-paired cosine and covariate-wavelet family with its covariate smoothness index set to \(\beta_{\mathrm{iso}}\). This is a quadratic-and-zero-zone bracket; it neither includes the regular term \(\rho n^{-1/2}\) nor asserts that the bracket is tight in the nontrivial quadratic zone.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put \(s=\beta_{\mathrm{iso}}\).  At \(\rho=0\), the identity
\[
\rho_n^2=\int\frac{\{\int_0^y\Delta_n(v,z)dv\}^2}{\bar q_n(y,z)}dy\,dR_n(z) \tag{1}
\]
and positivity imply \(\Delta_n=0\), so \(\theta_n=0\) and both sides of the bracket vanish.  Assume \(0<\rho\le\rho_{\mathrm{adm}}\).

For any member of \(\mathcal S^{\mathrm{sm}}_{n,d}(s,H,\gamma,\rho)\), integration of the joint-density bounds over \(y\) gives
\[
\gamma^{-1}\le r_n(z)\le\gamma,
\qquad
\gamma^{-2}\le q_{a,n}(y,z),\bar q_n(y,z)\le\gamma^2. \tag{2}
\]
Every division is licensed by the first bound.  The increasing-coupling formula, the mean-value theorem for the conditional distribution functions, and (1) give
\[
W_2^2\{Q_{0,n}(\cdot\mid z),Q_{1,n}(\cdot\mid z)\}
\le\gamma^8\int\Delta_n\mathcal G_{n,z}\Delta_n\,dy. \tag{3}
\]
Integration against the common marginal and \(\rho_n\le\rho\) yield
\[
0\le\theta_n\le\gamma^8\rho^2. \tag{4}
\]
Thus the zero estimator gives the \(O(\rho^2)\) upper entry.

Fix \(0<\tau<s\), put \(\epsilon_\tau=(s-\tau)/2\), and \(\alpha_\tau=\tau+\epsilon_\tau<s\).  On the fixed cube, the continuous embedding of H\"older balls gives
\[
\sup_{a,n}\|p_{a,n}\|_{\mathcal C^{\alpha_\tau}}
\le C_{s,d,\tau}H. \tag{5}
\]
This and joint positivity verify the premises of the established SmoothCOT rate lemma.  Its law-dependent constant is uniform here because every input is uniformly controlled, as follows.  For one block of size \(N=N_n^{\mathrm{sm}}\) and \(J=\lfloor\log_2N/(2\tau+d+1)\rfloor\), wavelet coefficient decay, variance bounded by \(p_{a,n}\le\gamma\), and finite overlap give
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal S^{\mathrm{sm}}_{n,d}}
E_P\|\widetilde p_{a,J}-p_{a,n}\|_2^2
\lesssim2^{-2\alpha_\tau J}+2^{(d+1)J}/N
\lesssim N^{-2a_\tau}. \tag{6}
\]
Coefficientwise exponential bounds and a union bound likewise give
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal S^{\mathrm{sm}}_{n,d}}
P\{\|\widetilde p_{a,J}-p_{a,n}\|_\infty>(2\gamma)^{-1}\}
\lesssim N^{-2}. \tag{7}
\]
Thus positive-part normalization is inactive with the uniform probability in (7), and reciprocal-density constants are fixed powers of \(\gamma\).  The aligned marginal comparison obeys
\[
\left|\int\widehat f\widehat r-\int fr_n\right|
\le\|\widehat r-r_n\|_1
+2\sum_{a=0}^1\int W_1(\widehat Q_a^z,Q_a^z)\widehat r(z)dz. \tag{8}
\]
The first term is bounded by (6), while the second is exactly the conditional negative-order term in the cited SmoothCOT proof; (2), (5)--(7) bound every constant in that argument uniformly.  Hence
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal S^{\mathrm{sm}}_{n,d}}
E_P|\widehat V_{\ell,\tau,n}^{\mathrm{sm}}-\theta_n|
\le C_{s,d,\tau,\gamma,H,\Psi_y,\Psi_z}N^{-a_\tau}. \tag{9}
\]

Let \(\delta_N\) denote the right side.  Markov's inequality makes one block bad at threshold \(8\delta_N\) with probability at most \(1/8\).  Independent product sampling makes paired blocks independent.  If the median is bad, at least half the blocks are bad, whence
\[
P\{|\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{sm,med}}_{\tau,n}-\theta_n|>8\delta_N\}
\le2^{K_n^{\mathrm{sm}}}8^{-(K_n^{\mathrm{sm}}+1)/2}
\le2^{-K_n^{\mathrm{sm}}/2}. \tag{10}
\]
Choose the fixed multiplier in \(K_n^{\mathrm{sm}}\asymp\log n\) so that the last term is at most \(\delta_N^2\).  Both values lie in \([0,1]\), so
\[
\sup_PE_P(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{sm,med}}_{\tau,n}-\theta_n)^2
\le65\delta_N^2. \tag{11}
\]
Since \(N_n^{\mathrm{sm}}\asymp n/\log n\), (11), together with (4), proves the upper inequality by deterministic selection.

For the lower inequality, use the sign family with \(s_z=s\),
\[
2^{J_z}\asymp n^{2/(4s+d)},
\qquad
p_{a,n,\omega}(y,z)=1+(2a-1)b_{n,\omega}(z)\cos(2\pi y)/2. \tag{12}
\]
The wavelet regularity premise and disjoint supports give
\[
\|b_{n,\omega}\|_\infty\lesssim c_0 2^{-sJ_z},
\qquad
\|b_{n,\omega}\|_{\mathcal C^s}\lesssim c_0. \tag{13}
\]
The outcome factor is fixed and smooth.  Choose the single \(c_0\) so small that the product perturbation is no greater than both the fixed H\"older slack \(H-\|1\|_{\mathcal C^s}\) and \(\min\{1-\gamma^{-1},\gamma-1\}\).  Then every law in (12) satisfies the joint density and norm constraints, uniformly in \(n,\rho,\omega\).  Its covariate marginal is one because the cosine integrates to zero.  Moreover,
\[
\rho_n^2=\frac{\gamma_n^2M_{J_z}}{8\pi^2}\le\rho^2, \tag{14}
\]
so the null and alternatives lie in the announced slice.

The explicit mixture lemma and cosine second variation give, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
\theta_{n,\omega}-\theta_{n,0}
\gtrsim\min\{\rho^2,n^{-4s/(4s+d)}\},
\qquad
\chi^2(\overline{\mathbb P}_n,\mathbb P_n^0)\le\eta<1, \tag{15}
\]
after reducing the same fixed \(c_0\).  Cauchy--Schwarz gives total variation at most \(\sqrt\eta/2\).  Thresholding an arbitrary estimator halfway between the null endpoint and the common alternative separation therefore gives
\[
\inf_{\widetilde\theta_n}\sup_{P\in\mathcal S^{\mathrm{sm}}_{n,d}}
\{E_P(\widetilde\theta_n-\theta_n)^2\}^{1/2}
\gtrsim\min\{\rho^2,n^{-4s/(4s+d)}\}. \tag{16}
\]
Substituting \(s=\beta_{\mathrm{iso}}\) proves the result.  The construction contains no regular fixed-frequency comparison, so it proves only the stated zero and quadratic lower entries and no tightness claim.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Under explicitly fixed boundary-wavelet bases of regularity greater than \(\beta_{\mathrm{iso}}\), this transfer corollary makes the existing sign-mixture converse and observable SmoothCOT envelope available on a recognizable fixed-radius positive isotropic scalar class. \textit{Gap.} The published SmoothCOT bound has a law-dependent constant and supplies no equality-local minimax converse; the transfer uniformizes that constant on the fixed \((\gamma,H)\) slice and adds the quadratic-and-zero sign-mixture lower benchmark, with the required wavelet regularity now explicit. \textit{Consumer.} Applied scalar SmoothCOT analyses with fixed boundary wavelets of regularity greater than \(\beta_{\mathrm{iso}}\) can use the displayed bracket without claiming a matched quadratic rate or an observable mixed-regime confidence procedure.

\section{Interpretation}

The same boundary mechanism survives two presentations. On the primitive anisotropic conditional class, outcome inversion smooths high outcome frequencies while covariate resolution creates growing effective rank; this yields a sharp intrinsic lower phase map but only an observable upper envelope. On the recognizable positive isotropic slice, the sign-paired family is a valid subclass and the median SmoothCOT estimator controls every member of the full fixed-radius slice, so they produce a genuine two-sided transfer bracket. The lower and upper powers differ in the nontrivial quadratic zone, and this mismatch is a result to be reported, not evidence for a matched rate.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The cosine witness reduces one lower-bound channel to a covariate quadratic functional, but the optimized observable Gram upper expansion still contains a density-pilot projection remainder and is strictly slower than the intrinsic quadratic lower scale. Replacing the population mixed Gaussian--quadratic proxy by an observed spectral or multiplier proxy additionally requires relative covariance and Hessian control that the primitive model does not ensure at every corrected resolution. The population transition theorem and exact no-covariate null law therefore do not by themselves deliver uniform observable calibration.

\section{Honest scope}

The model has scalar compactly supported outcomes, equal randomized arm sizes, a shared covariate marginal, positive anisotropic H\"older densities, and known smoothness. For \(d\ge1\), the note gives an intrinsic lower frontier and an explicit observable upper envelope; it does not assert that the bracket is tight in the nontrivial quadratic zone. The positive isotropic SmoothCOT slice likewise carries an unmatched transferred bracket. The Gaussian transition concerns the infeasible oracle-score, population-kernel statistic under its stated triangular conditions, not the observable experiment. The \(d=0\) result uses a separate full-sample empirical-quantile branch. Adaptation, multivariate outcomes, observational propensity estimation, high-dimensional observed spectral plug-in, closure of the conditional minimax bracket, and uniform mixed calibration remain open.
