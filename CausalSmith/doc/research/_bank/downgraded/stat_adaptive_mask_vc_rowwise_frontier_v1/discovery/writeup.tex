\section*{stat\_\allowbreak{}adaptive\_\allowbreak{}mask\_\allowbreak{}vc\_\allowbreak{}rowwise\_\allowbreak{}frontier}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} Recorded propensities identify the causal effect matrix and make the entrywise IPW errors a bounded row-major martingale difference array.  On the frozen predictable class the strongest common-risk statement is the honest bracket \(c b_{nm}\le\inf_{\widetilde U}\sup_P E_P[\mathcal L(\widetilde U,U)]\le\sup_P E_P[\mathcal L(\widehat U^{\mathrm I},U)]\le C a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm}\).  Thus \(b_{nm}\) is a minimax lower/reference scale, not a matched frontier.  The endogenous-target construction rules out uniform \(b_{nm}\)-attainment by recorded IPW--SVD only; whether another observed-data estimator closes the bracket remains open.  Blind row scaling is certified only at the explicit \(d_{nm}\) resolution: both its causal loss and its observed disagreement differ from total drift by \(O_P(d_{nm})\).  With fixed \(r,\mu,K,q\), bounded aspect ratio, and \(b_{nm}=o(1)\), one has \(d_{nm}/b_{nm}\asymp\sqrt n\), so no \(b_{nm}\)-scale iff is claimed.  The rank-one \(0.25/0.75\) Markov law remains an explicit failure witness for blind scaling and a HeartSteps II stress design.  For predeclared dense families, direct finite-sample bands have a separate \(\sqrt{\log|\mathcal G_n|}\) multiplicity cost and make no feasible-multiplier exactness claim.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Published independent-mask rowwise matrix results do not settle causal effect-subspace risk under history-predictable assignment.  On the present effect-only low-rank class, a constant-propensity packing yields the lower/reference scale \(b_{nm}\), while a matrix-Freedman and deterministic subspace argument yields only \(a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm}\) for recorded IPW--SVD.  The accepted endogenous-target example prevents labeling the latter estimator as \(b_{nm}\)-optimal, but it does not prove an estimator-independent lower bound above \(b_{nm}\).

\paragraph{Niche.} The contribution is an explicit same-loss minimax bracket for a causal row subspace under predictable masks, together with an estimator-specific obstruction, a calibrated blind-normalization resolution theorem, and conservative simultaneous causal-contrast bands whose entropy cost is kept separate from the subspace scales.

\paragraph{Fill.} The project proves an expected normalized maximum-row upper bound for recorded IPW--SVD, combines it with the constant-propensity minimax lower bound in one risk criterion, and records the remaining rate gap.  It proves two unconditional \(d_{nm}\)-resolution inequalities for blind scaling and shows algebraically that the former \(d_{nm}=O(b_{nm})\) premise is unavailable in the fixed-complexity \(b_{nm}=o(1)\) regime.  It also supplies the explicit Markov witness and a direct finite-sample union-bound band with separate family-size inflation.

\section{Related work}

Mehrotra, Tran, Vu, and Zampetakis are the closest rowwise estimator comparator: their public Algorithm 1, Theorem 3.2, and Corollary 3.3 analyze separate empirical-row-scaled SVD fits for two low-rank arm means under independent Bernoulli assignment.  Their geometric rowwise matrix-error scale motivates \(b^0_{nm}\), but their theorem does not cover history-predictable masks, effect-only low rank, the effect matrix's left singular space, or the present minimax criterion.  Cai, Li, Chi, Poor, and Chen prove two-to-infinity upper and lower bounds for an independently sampled low-rank matrix with independent noise; those results inform the deterministic perturbation and local packing architecture but do not make the causal target predictable under the row-major filtration.  The present result is therefore an honest bracket rather than a claimed matched extension of either paper.

Hadad, Hirshberg, Zhan, Wager, and Athey support the recorded-propensity martingale-score reduction, and Tropp supplies the bounded-increment matrix Freedman inequality used for the upper moment bound.  Dwivedi, Tian, Tomkins, Klasnja, Murphy, and Shah study adaptive nearest-neighbor estimation of unit-by-time arm means under an exogenous factor model, a different target and structural class.  Athey, Bayati, Doudchenko, Imbens, and Khosravi; Sun, Zhu, and Dubin; Bai and Ng; Agarwal, Dahleh, Shah, and Shen; Choi and Yuan; Mao, Wong, and Chen; and Wang, Wong, Mao, and Chan address outcome completion, treatment effects on treated cells, entrywise or Frobenius loss, or fixed missingness structures.  None supplies the missing estimator-independent upper rate for the present predictable effect-subspace class, and the current effect-only rank condition does not imply their outcome-matrix assumptions.

Khan and Ugander analyze scalar affine normalization under independent indicators; the blind row-fraction SVD and its \(0.25/0.75\) Markov witness are new objects here and do not refute that published procedure.  Belloni and Oliveira and Ma, Niu, Zhang, and Zhu motivate high-dimensional martingale or multiplier inference, but the retained simultaneous theorem uses direct finite-sample Freedman radii and a union bound only.  Its \(\sqrt{\log|\mathcal G_n|}\) factor is a query-family entropy cost, not part of either subspace scale.  Spruijt-Metz et al. make saved propensities and omission sensitivity operationally relevant for the HeartSteps II consumer.

  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Conditionally independent \(N(0,1)\) multipliers \(\xi_{ij}\).
  \item \texttt{c} --- \texttt{random positive scalar}; \(\mathbb R_+\); \texttt{Simultaneous critical value.}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Conditional \(1-\alpha\) quantile \(c_{1-\alpha}\) of the standardized Gaussian multiplier maximum over \(\mathcal G_n\).
  \item \texttt{theta} --- \texttt{positive constant}; \((0,\infty)\); \texttt{Witness signal.}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \texttt{Effect amplitude in the Markov witness.}
  \item \texttt{h} --- \texttt{positive constant}; \((0,\infty)\); \texttt{Witness distortion.}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \texttt{Common-\allowbreak{}arm amplitude in the Markov witness.}
  \item \texttt{uD} --- \texttt{random factor vector}; \((0,1)^n\); \texttt{Dwivedi-\allowbreak{}class separation witness.}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Independent unit factors \(u^{\mathrm D}_i\sim\operatorname{Uniform}(0,1)\), which are almost surely distinct.
  \item \texttt{vD} --- \texttt{random factor vector}; \((0,1)^m\); \texttt{Dwivedi-\allowbreak{}class separation witness.}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Independent time factors \(v^{\mathrm D}_j\sim\operatorname{Uniform}(0,1)\), independent of \(u^{\mathrm D}\), which are almost surely distinct.
  \item \texttt{fD0} --- \texttt{bounded Lipschitz function}; \([0,1]^2\to\mathbb R\); \texttt{Dwivedi-\allowbreak{}class separation witness.}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Control comparator kernel \(f^{\mathrm D}_0(u,v)=0\).
  \item \texttt{fD1} --- \texttt{bounded Lipschitz function}; \([0,1]^2\to\mathbb R\); \texttt{Dwivedi-\allowbreak{}class separation witness.}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Treatment comparator kernel \(f^{\mathrm D}_1(u,v)=\exp(uv)\).
  \item \texttt{MD} --- \texttt{random matrix}; \(\mathbb R^{n\times m}\); \texttt{Full-\allowbreak{}rank nonlinear-\allowbreak{}factor separation witness.}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Comparator effect matrix \(M^{\mathrm D}_{ij}=f^{\mathrm D}_1(u^{\mathrm D}_i,v^{\mathrm D}_j)-f^{\mathrm D}_0(u^{\mathrm D}_i,v^{\mathrm D}_j)=\exp(u^{\mathrm D}_iv^{\mathrm D}_j)\).
  \item \texttt{W} --- \texttt{rank-\allowbreak{}one witness law}; \(\prod_{\ell\ge1}\mathcal P_{n_\ell,m_\ell}\); \texttt{Necessity witness.}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} The explicit bounded-aspect sequence of \(0.25/0.75\) Markov-mask laws in \(\mathrm{def:markov\mbox{-}witness}\).
  \item \texttt{Oobs} --- \texttt{sigma-\allowbreak{}field}; \texttt{Information available to estimators.}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Observed-experiment sigma-field \(\mathcal O_{n,m}=\sigma(A,Y,p)\).
  \item \texttt{Eobs} --- \texttt{estimator class}; \texttt{Admissible data-\allowbreak{}based estimators.}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Class \(\mathfrak E_{n,m}=\{\widetilde U:\widetilde U\text{ is }\mathcal O_{n,m}\text{-measurable and takes values in }\mathbb O_{n,r}\}\).
  \item \texttt{Cstar} --- \texttt{nonnegative extended-\allowbreak{}real number}; \([0,\infty]\); \texttt{Open sharp-\allowbreak{}efficiency object.}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Asymptotic minimax constant along \((n_\ell,m_\ell)\), as constructed in \(\mathrm{def:sharp\mbox{-}experiment}\).
  \item \texttt{aloose} --- \texttt{nonnegative deterministic sequence}; \(\mathbb R_+\); \texttt{Recorded-\allowbreak{}IPW spectral upper radius and hybrid switching scale.}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Loose spectral radius \(a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm}=\sqrt{n/(\mu r)}[1\wedge(B/\lambda_{nm})\{\sqrt{(n\vee m)\log(e(n+m))/q}+\log(e(n+m))/q\}]\).
  \item \texttt{S} --- \texttt{deterministic orthonormal matrix}; \(\mathbb O_{n,r}\); \texttt{Deterministic incoherent fallback.}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Sine fallback frame \(S^{\mathrm{sin}}_{ik}=\sqrt{2/(n+1)}\sin(\pi ik/(n+1))\) for \(1\le i\le n\) and \(1\le k\le r\).
  \item \texttt{UhatH} --- \texttt{orthonormal random matrix}; \(\mathbb O_{n,r}\); \texttt{Uniform constant-\allowbreak{}risk observed-\allowbreak{}data estimator.}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Hybrid estimator \(\widehat U^{\mathrm H}=\widehat U^{\mathrm I}\) when \(a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm}\le1\), and \(\widehat U^{\mathrm H}=S^{\mathrm{sin}}\) otherwise.
  \item \texttt{H} --- \texttt{construction handle}; \texttt{Rate-\allowbreak{}gap handle preceding any sharp-\allowbreak{}constant calculation.}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Rate-gap handle \(H\): combine the hybrid \(\widehat U^{\mathrm H}\) and its \(1\wedge a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm}\) bound in loss \(\mathcal L\) with the \(b_{nm}\) packing, then search over \(\mathfrak E_{n,m}\) under the row-major filtration \(\mathbb G\) before evaluating \(C_\star\).
\end{itemize}

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:sequential-randomization]\label{ass:sequential-randomization}
For every \(i\in I\) and \(j\in J\), \(P(A_{ij}=1\mid\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1},Y_{ij}(0),Y_{ij}(1))=p_{ij}\) almost surely.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Sequential randomization with recorded propensities, \cite{HadadHirshbergZhanWagerAthey2021}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:overlap]\label{ass:overlap}
For every \(i\in I\) and \(j\in J\), \(q\le p_{ij}\le1-q\) almost surely.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform sequential overlap, \cite{HadadHirshbergZhanWagerAthey2021}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:bounded-outcomes]\label{ass:bounded-outcomes}
For every \(i\in I\), \(j\in J\), and \(a\in\{0,1\}\), \(|Y_{ij}(a)|\le B\) almost surely.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniformly bounded adaptive-experiment outcomes, \cite{HadadHirshbergZhanWagerAthey2021}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:exact-rank]\label{ass:exact-rank}
The effect matrix satisfies \(\operatorname{rank}(M)=r\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Exact low-rank signal model, \cite{CaiLiChiPoorChen2021Subspace}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:left-incoherence]\label{ass:left-incoherence}
The left singular space satisfies \(\lVert U\rVert_{2,\infty}\le\sqrt{\mu r/n}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Left singular-space incoherence, \cite{CaiLiChiPoorChen2021Subspace}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:right-incoherence]\label{ass:right-incoherence}
The right singular space satisfies \(\lVert V\rVert_{2,\infty}\le\sqrt{\mu r/m}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Right singular-space incoherence, \cite{CaiLiChiPoorChen2021Subspace}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:condition-number]\label{ass:condition-number}
The signal condition number satisfies \(\sigma_1(M)/\sigma_r(M)\le K\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Bounded low-rank condition number, \cite{CaiLiChiPoorChen2021Subspace}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:signal-floor]\label{ass:signal-floor}
The smallest nonzero singular value satisfies \(\sigma_r(M)\ge\lambda_{nm}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Explicit spectral signal floor, \cite{CaiLiChiPoorChen2021Subspace}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:aspect-ratio]\label{ass:aspect-ratio}
The dimensions satisfy \(c_-\le m/n\le c_+\) for fixed \(0<c_-<c_+<\infty\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Bounded rectangular aspect ratio, \cite{CaiLiChiPoorChen2021Subspace}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:propensity-path-stability]\label{ass:propensity-path-stability}
The predictable propensity path satisfies \(\max_{i\in I}|m^{-1}\sum_{j\in J}(p_{ij}-E_P[p_{ij}])|=O_P(\sqrt{\log(en)/m})\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This law-level stability condition holds for deterministic schedules and for bounded finite-state geometrically ergodic personalization rules, including the exhibited two-state Markov witness; it excludes persistent random policy regimes for which a single empirical row fraction has no stable design target.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:oracle-blind-gap]\label{ass:oracle-blind-gap}
The primitive oracle blind center satisfies \(\sigma_r(M^{\mathrm B})-\sigma_{r+1}(M^{\mathrm B})\ge c_{\mathrm B}\lambda_{nm}\) almost surely for a fixed \(c_{\mathrm B}>0\), with \(\sigma_{r+1}(M^{\mathrm B})=0\) when \(r=\min(n,m)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This is a law-level separation condition on the oracle pseudo-outcome before empirical normalization. It is directly checkable for deterministic schedules, block-randomized designs, and the displayed Markov law, where the two oracle singular values are explicit; it does not impose any property on the realized row denominator or on a fitted matrix.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:polynomial-family]\label{ass:polynomial-family}
The family satisfies \(2\le|\mathcal G_n|\le n^{\kappa}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Polynomial high-dimensional index growth, \cite{BelloniOliveira2018MartingaleCLT}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:average-loadings]\label{ass:average-loadings}
Every \(g\in\mathcal G_n\) satisfies \(c_g/(nm)\le\lVert g\rVert_{\mathrm F}^2\le C_g/(nm)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Grand means, time-window means, and subgroup-by-window means encoded with deterministic weights have squared Frobenius norm of order \(1/(nm)\); the two-sided scale restriction keeps the causal queries nonvanishing without referring to an estimated singular space.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:dense-loadings]\label{ass:dense-loadings}
Every \(g\in\mathcal G_n\) satisfies \(\max_{i\in I,j\in J}|g_{ij}|\le C_\infty/(nm)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Predeclared averages over a fixed positive fraction of unit-time cells have entry weights of order \(1/(nm)\). This excludes localized entry queries, for which a dense-average central limit theorem is not the relevant inferential object.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:variance-nondegeneracy]\label{ass:variance-nondegeneracy}
For every \(g\in\mathcal G_n\), \(s_g^2=\sum_{i\in I,j\in J}E_P[g_{ij}^2E_{ij}^2]\ge c_sB^2/(nm)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Nondegenerate coordinate variance, \cite{BelloniOliveira2018MartingaleCLT}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:predictable-covariance-mean]\label{ass:predictable-covariance-mean}
The normalized predictable covariance satisfies \(E_P[\lVert\Omega^{\mathrm P}_{nm}-\Omega_{nm}\rVert_{\max}]\le (nm)^{-1/16}/\log^2(enm|\mathcal G_n|)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Belloni--Oliveira predictable-covariance stabilization, expectation part, \cite{BelloniOliveira2018MartingaleCLT}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:predictable-covariance-order]\label{ass:predictable-covariance-order}
With probability at least \(1-(nm)^{-1/16}\), \(\Omega^{\mathrm P}_{nm}\preceq[1+(nm)^{-1/16}/\log^2|\mathcal G_n|]\Omega_{nm}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Belloni--Oliveira predictable-covariance Loewner control, \cite{BelloniOliveira2018MartingaleCLT}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:predictable-covariance-tail]\label{ass:predictable-covariance-tail}
With probability at least \(1-(nm)^{-1/16}\), \(\lVert\Omega^{\mathrm P}_{nm}-\Omega_{nm}\rVert_{\max}\le(nm)^{-1/16}/\log^2(enm|\mathcal G_n|)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Belloni--Oliveira predictable-covariance high-probability control, \cite{BelloniOliveira2018MartingaleCLT}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:inference-rank-margin]\label{ass:inference-rank-margin}
The inference branch satisfies \(r<\min(n,m)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Nonsaturated effect surfaces are natural in long mobile-health panels with a small latent response dimension. This restriction is imposed only for feasible variance estimation, because a saturated rank interpolates every score and makes residual studentization identically zero.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:inference-low-rank-growth]\label{ass:inference-low-rank-growth}
The inference branch satisfies \((nm)^{1/8}r(n+m)\log^6(enm)/(nmq)+(nm)^{1/8}B^2(n+m)\log^4(enm)/(q\lambda_{nm}^2)=o(1)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This primitive effective-dimension and signal margin permits fixed or slowly growing rank with singular values above the adaptive score-noise scale in bounded-aspect longitudinal panels. It makes aggregate leave-one-cell-out prediction error negligible for dense averages without assuming residual consistency itself.
\end{assumption}

\section{Definitions}

\begin{definition}[def:global-filtration]\label{def:global-filtration}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Row-\allowbreak{}major global experiment filtration}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Order \(\mathcal C_{n,m}=I\times J\) by \(o(i,j)=(i-1)m+j\). Let \(\mathbb G_0\) be the recorded baseline field and let \(\mathbb G_k\) be the field after recording the assignment and outcome at the unique cell \(o^{-1}(k)\), together with all earlier cells. Define \(\mathcal F_{i,0}=\mathbb G_{(i-1)m}\) and \(\mathcal F_{i,j}=\mathbb G_{o(i,j)}\) for \(j\in J\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{The row and time indices and the recorded cell sequence.}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:predictable-class]\label{def:predictable-class}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Predictable low-\allowbreak{}rank experiment class}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\mathfrak P^{\mathrm{pred}}_{n,m}(r,\mu,K,q,B,\lambda)=\{P\in\mathcal P_{n,m}:\text{ assumptions }\mathrm{ass:sequential\mbox{-}randomization},\mathrm{ass:overlap},\mathrm{ass:bounded\mbox{-}outcomes},\mathrm{ass:exact\mbox{-}rank},\mathrm{ass:left\mbox{-}incoherence},\mathrm{ass:right\mbox{-}incoherence},\mathrm{ass:condition\mbox{-}number},\mathrm{ass:signal\mbox{-}floor},\mathrm{ass:aspect\mbox{-}ratio}\text{ hold for the }(n,m)\text{ array}\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Member properties.} \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}sequential-\allowbreak{}randomization}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}overlap}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}bounded-\allowbreak{}outcomes}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}exact-\allowbreak{}rank}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}left-\allowbreak{}incoherence}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}right-\allowbreak{}incoherence}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}condition-\allowbreak{}number}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}signal-\allowbreak{}floor}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}aspect-\allowbreak{}ratio}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:rowwise-loss]\label{def:rowwise-loss}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Normalized maximum-\allowbreak{}row loss}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\mathcal L(\widetilde U,U)=\sqrt{n/(\mu r)}\min_{R\in\mathbb O_r}\lVert\widetilde U R-U\rVert_{2,\infty}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{A candidate and target row-\allowbreak{}factor matrix.}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:ipw-score]\label{def:ipw-score}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Recorded-\allowbreak{}propensity IPW score}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Set \(Z^{\mathrm{ipw}}_{ij}=A_{ij}Y_{ij}/p_{ij}-(1-A_{ij})Y_{ij}/(1-p_{ij})\) and \(E_{ij}=Z^{\mathrm{ipw}}_{ij}-M_{ij}\) for every \(i\in I\) and \(j\in J\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{Assignments,\allowbreak{} outcomes,\allowbreak{} and recorded propensities.}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:ipw-svd]\label{def:ipw-svd}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Recorded-\allowbreak{}propensity IPW-\allowbreak{}SVD}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Compute \(\widehat M^{\mathrm I}=\mathcal P_r(Z^{\mathrm{ipw}})\) and report its leading left singular matrix \(\widehat U^{\mathrm I}\) under the fixed tie rule.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{The recorded score and rank.}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:geometric-rate]\label{def:geometric-rate}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Uncalibrated geometric term}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(b^0_{nm}=K r^{3/2}\mu q^{-1/2}\sqrt{n^{-1}+nm^{-2}}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{Structural envelopes,\allowbreak{} overlap,\allowbreak{} and dimensions.}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:frontier-rate]\label{def:frontier-rate}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Maximum-\allowbreak{}row lower/\allowbreak{}reference scale}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(b_{nm}=1\wedge\{(B\sqrt{nm}/\lambda_{nm})\sqrt{\log(en)}\,b^0_{nm}\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{The geometric term,\allowbreak{} outcome scale,\allowbreak{} signal floor,\allowbreak{} and row cardinality.}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:blind-tangent]\label{def:blind-tangent}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Blind ratio target and total drift}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Set \(\bar p_i=m^{-1}\sum_{j\in J}E_P[p_{ij}]=m^{-1}\sum_{j\in J}E_P[A_{ij}]\), \(\widehat p_i=\Pi_{[q/2,1-q/2]}(m^{-1}\sum_{j\in J}A_{ij})\), \(M^{\mathrm B}_{ij}=p_{ij}\mu^1_{ij}/\bar p_i-(1-p_{ij})\mu^0_{ij}/(1-\bar p_i)\), \(H^{\mathrm B}_{ij}=p_{ij}\mu^1_{ij}/\bar p_i^2+(1-p_{ij})\mu^0_{ij}/(1-\bar p_i)^2\), \(D^{\mathrm B}_{ij}=-(\widehat p_i-\bar p_i)H^{\mathrm B}_{ij}\), and \(T^{\mathrm B}=M^{\mathrm B}+D^{\mathrm B}\). Let \(U^{\mathrm O}\) and \(U^{\mathrm B}\) be the leading \(r\) left singular matrices of \(M^{\mathrm B}\) and \(T^{\mathrm B}\), respectively, under the fixed tie rule, and set \(\rho_{nm}=\mathcal L(U^{\mathrm B},U)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{The law,\allowbreak{} propensity path,\allowbreak{} assignments,\allowbreak{} and conditional arm means.}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:blind-svd]\label{def:blind-svd}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Blind row-\allowbreak{}scaled SVD}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Set \(Z^{\mathrm B}_{ij}=A_{ij}Y_{ij}/\widehat p_i-(1-A_{ij})Y_{ij}/(1-\widehat p_i)\), compute \(\widehat M^{\mathrm B}=\mathcal P_r(Z^{\mathrm B})\), and report its leading left singular matrix \(\widehat U^{\mathrm B}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{Assignments and outcomes,\allowbreak{} discarding entrywise propensities.}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:blind-diagnostic]\label{def:blind-diagnostic}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Observable blind-\allowbreak{}versus-\allowbreak{}recorded diagnostic}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Compute both row-factor fits and set \(\widehat\rho_{nm}=\mathcal L(\widehat U^{\mathrm B},\widehat U^{\mathrm I})\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{The blind and recorded-\allowbreak{}propensity row-\allowbreak{}factor fits.}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:blind-expansion-residual]\label{def:blind-expansion-residual}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Blind ratio expansion residual}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Set \(Z^{\bar p}_{ij}=A_{ij}Y_{ij}/\bar p_i-(1-A_{ij})Y_{ij}/(1-\bar p_i)\) and \(R^{\mathrm B}=Z^{\mathrm B}-Z^{\bar p}-D^{\mathrm B}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{The oracle row propensity and the realized blind score.}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:contrast-process]\label{def:contrast-process}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Leave-\allowbreak{}one-\allowbreak{}cell-\allowbreak{}out direct IPW multiplier process}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For every cell \( (i,j)\), replace only \(Z^{\mathrm{ipw}}_{ij}\) by zero, compute \(\widehat M^{(-ij)}=\mathcal P_r(Z^{\mathrm{ipw}}-Z^{\mathrm{ipw}}_{ij}e_ie_j^\top)\), and set \(\widehat e_{ij}=Z^{\mathrm{ipw}}_{ij}-\widehat M^{(-ij)}_{ij}\). For every \(g\in\mathcal G_n\), set \(\widehat\tau_g=\sum_{i,j}g_{ij}Z^{\mathrm{ipw}}_{ij}\) and \(\widehat s_g^2=\sum_{i,j}g_{ij}^2\widehat e_{ij}^2\). Draw conditionally independent \(\xi_{ij}\sim N(0,1)\), and define \(c_{1-\alpha}\) as the conditional \(1-\alpha\) quantile of \(\max_{g\in\mathcal G_n}|\sum_{i,j}\xi_{ij}g_{ij}\widehat e_{ij}/\widehat s_g|\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{The IPW score,\allowbreak{} rank,\allowbreak{} and deterministic contrast family.}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:markov-witness]\label{def:markov-witness}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} Rank-one \(0.25/0.75\) Markov-mask witness
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Along \(n_\ell=2\ell\) and \(m_\ell=2\lceil\gamma\ell\rceil\), define \(W^{\theta,h}_\ell\) using independent baseline states \(A^{\mathrm{init}}_i=A_{i0}\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\), recorded in \(\mathbb G_0\), and fixed \(0<\theta<h\) with \(h+\theta/2\le B\). For \(j\ge1\), set \(p_{ij}=1/2+(1/4)(-1)^{i+j}(2A_{i,j-1}-1)\), \(C_{ij}=h(-1)^{i+j}(2A_{i,j-1}-1)\), \(Y_{ij}(1)=\mu^1_{ij}=C_{ij}+(\theta/2)(-1)^{i+j}\), and \(Y_{ij}(0)=\mu^0_{ij}=C_{ij}-(\theta/2)(-1)^{i+j}\), where \(A_{i,j-1}=A^{\mathrm{init}}_i\) at \(j=1\). Hence \(M_{ij}=\theta(-1)^{i+j}\), \(\bar p_i=1/2\), and \(M^{\mathrm B}_{ij}=h+\theta(-1)^{i+j}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} The explicit bounded-aspect path and amplitudes \(0<\theta<h\) bounded by \(B\)..
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:dwivedi-full-rank-witness]\label{def:dwivedi-full-rank-witness}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Nonlinear-\allowbreak{}factor separation witness}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Draw independent \(u^{\mathrm D}_i,v^{\mathrm D}_j\sim\operatorname{Uniform}(0,1)\), set \(f^{\mathrm D}_0(u,v)=0\) and \(f^{\mathrm D}_1(u,v)=\exp(uv)\), and form \(M^{\mathrm D}_{ij}=\exp(u^{\mathrm D}_iv^{\mathrm D}_j)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{Independent unit and time factors on a compact positive interval.}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:observed-estimators]\label{def:observed-estimators}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Observed-\allowbreak{}experiment estimators and deterministic hybrid}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Set \(\mathcal O_{n,m}=\sigma(A,Y,p)\) and
\[
 \mathfrak E_{n,m}=\{\widetilde U:\widetilde U\text{ is }\mathcal O_{n,m}\text{-measurable and }\widetilde U\in\mathbb O_{n,r}\}.
\]
Define the deterministic sine frame \(S^{\mathrm{sin}}\in\mathbb R^{n\times r}\) by
\[
 S^{\mathrm{sin}}_{ik}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{n+1}}\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi ik}{n+1}\right),
 \qquad 1\le i\le n,\quad 1\le k\le r,
\]
and set
\[
 a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm}=\sqrt{\frac n{\mu r}}\left[1\wedge
 \frac B{\lambda_{nm}}\left\{
 \sqrt{\frac{(n\vee m)\log(e(n+m))}{q}}+
 \frac{\log(e(n+m))}{q}\right\}\right].
\]
The hybrid estimator is the sample map
\[
 \widehat U^{\mathrm H}=\begin{cases}
 \widehat U^{\mathrm I},&a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm}\le1,\\
 S^{\mathrm{sin}},&a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm}>1.
 \end{cases}
\]
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{The observed assignments,\allowbreak{} outcomes,\allowbreak{} and recorded propensities,\allowbreak{} together with the declared class parameters.}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:sharp-experiment]\label{def:sharp-experiment}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Bounded-\allowbreak{}aspect sharp-\allowbreak{}constant experiment}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Fix \(\gamma\in(c_-,c_+)\), set \(n_\ell=2\ell\) and \(m_\ell=2\lceil\gamma\ell\rceil\), and define \(C_\star=\liminf_{\ell\to\infty}\inf_{\widetilde U\in\mathfrak E_{n_\ell,m_\ell}}\sup_{P\in\mathfrak P^{\mathrm{pred}}_{n_\ell,m_\ell}(r,\mu,K,q,B,\lambda)}E_P[\mathcal L(\widetilde U,U)]/b_{n_\ell m_\ell}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{A fixed aspect limit and the predictable experiment class.}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:sharp-constant-handle]\label{def:sharp-constant-handle}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Minimax-\allowbreak{}rate-\allowbreak{}gap handle}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} The handle \(H\) uses \(\widehat U^{\mathrm H}\) and \(1\wedge a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm}\) for the constant upper endpoint in loss \(\mathcal L\), and the constant-propensity row packing for the \(b_{nm}\) lower endpoint.  It searches over \(\mathfrak E_{n,m}\) for either a smaller uniform risk or an estimator-independent adaptive lower construction preserving \(\mathbb G\).  Only after this rate gap closes does it derive a local likelihood experiment for \(C_\star\).

\end{definition}

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:ipw-identification]\label{lem:ipw-identification}
For every \(i\in I\) and \(j\in J\), \(E_P[Z^{\mathrm{ipw}}_{ij}\mid\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1}]=M_{ij}\). Hence the row-major sequence \((E_{o^{-1}(k)},\mathbb G_k)_{k=1}^{nm}\) is a matrix martingale-difference array, with conditional variance bounded by a constant times \(B^2/q\) and increment magnitude bounded by a constant times \(B/q\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a cell \((i,j)\) and write \(\mathcal H=\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1}\).  Consistency gives
\[
 A_{ij}Y_{ij}=A_{ij}Y_{ij}(1),\qquad
 (1-A_{ij})Y_{ij}=(1-A_{ij})Y_{ij}(0).
\]
Sequential randomization and the tower property therefore give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 E_P\!\left[\frac{A_{ij}Y_{ij}}{p_{ij}}\middle|\mathcal H\right]
 &=E_P\!\left[
 \frac{Y_{ij}(1)}{p_{ij}}
 E_P[A_{ij}\mid\mathcal H,Y_{ij}(0),Y_{ij}(1)]
 \middle|\mathcal H\right]
 =\mu^1_{ij},\\
 E_P\!\left[\frac{(1-A_{ij})Y_{ij}}{1-p_{ij}}\middle|\mathcal H\right]
 &=\mu^0_{ij}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The divisions are valid because overlap gives \(p_{ij},1-p_{ij}\ge q>0\).  Subtracting proves
\[
 E_P[Z^{\mathrm{ipw}}_{ij}\mid\mathcal H]
 =\mu^1_{ij}-\mu^0_{ij}=M_{ij},
 \qquad E_P[E_{ij}\mid\mathcal H]=0.
\]
Thus the row-major sequence is adapted and is a matrix martingale-difference array.  Since only one arm contributes to the score and the potential outcomes are bounded,
\[
 |Z^{\mathrm{ipw}}_{ij}|\le \frac Bq,
 \qquad |M_{ij}|\le 2B,
 \qquad |E_{ij}|\le \frac{3B}{q}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 E_P[(Z^{\mathrm{ipw}}_{ij})^2\mid\mathcal H]
 &=E_P\!\left[\frac{Y_{ij}(1)^2}{p_{ij}}+
 \frac{Y_{ij}(0)^2}{1-p_{ij}}\middle|\mathcal H\right]\\
 &\le B^2\left(\frac1{p_{ij}}+\frac1{1-p_{ij}}\right)
 \le \frac{2B^2}{q}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Because \(M_{ij}\) is the conditional mean of the score, conditional centering can only reduce the second moment, and hence
\[
 E_P[E_{ij}^2\mid\mathcal H]
 =\operatorname{Var}_P(Z^{\mathrm{ipw}}_{ij}\mid\mathcal H)
 \le \frac{2B^2}{q}.
\]
This proves every asserted identification, support, increment, and conditional-variance claim.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This identifies the effect matrix and exposes the conditional variance and increment scales needed by rowwise perturbation. \textit{Gap.} Hadad et al. identify scalar adaptive IPW scores, not their rowwise spectral use. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies a correctly scaled effect surface for micro-randomized trials with saved propensities.

\begin{proposition}[prop:fixed-propensity-sanity]\label{prop:fixed-propensity-sanity}
Let \(p_{ij}=q_0\in(q,1-q)\), \(n/m\to c_0\in(0,\infty)\), and \(r=\mu=K=1\).  If \(\lambda_{nm}\asymp B\sqrt{n\log n}\), then \(b_{nm}\asymp1\); there exists a bounded rank-one law with a deterministic nonzero common control-arm mean for which \(\sqrt n\lVert EV/\sigma_1(M)\rVert_\infty=\Theta_P(1)\).  If \(\lambda_{nm}\asymp B\sqrt{nm}\), then \(b_{nm}\asymp\sqrt{\log n/n}\).  For every constant-propensity law,
\[
 Z^{\mathrm B}=Z^{\mathrm{ipw}}+D^{\mathrm B}+R^{\mathrm B},\qquad
 D^{\mathrm B}_{ij}=-(\widehat p_i-q_0)
 \left\{\frac{\mu^1_{ij}}{q_0}+\frac{\mu^0_{ij}}{1-q_0}\right\}.
\]
Thus empirical row normalization has a first-order denominator tangent whenever the displayed derivative loading is nonzero; the identity does not assert that the realized tangent is nonzero on every sample.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(q_0\in(q,1-q)\), and suppose throughout the first two calculations that \(n/m\to c_0\in(0,\infty)\) and \(r=\mu=K=1\).  By the definition of \(b^0_{nm}\),
\[
 b^0_{nm}
 =q^{-1/2}\sqrt{\frac1n+\frac{n}{m^2}}
 =q^{-1/2}n^{-1/2}\sqrt{1+\left(\frac nm\right)^2}
 \asymp n^{-1/2}.
\]
Here and below, the two constants implicit in \(\asymp\) may depend on \(q\) and \(c_0\), but not on \(n\) or \(m\).  This use of two-sided boundedness is justified by the assumed limit \(n/m\to c_0\); in particular, it is the relevant specialization of \(\mathrm{ass:aspect\mbox{-}ratio}\).

Suppose first that \(\lambda_{nm}\asymp B\sqrt{n\log n}\).  Since \(m/n\to c_0^{-1}\) and \(\log(en)/\log n\to1\), the untruncated quantity in \(\mathrm{def:frontier\mbox{-}rate}\) satisfies
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \frac{B\sqrt{nm}}{\lambda_{nm}}\sqrt{\log(en)}\,b^0_{nm}
 &\asymp
 \frac{\sqrt{nm}}{\sqrt{n\log n}}
 \sqrt{\log(en)}\,n^{-1/2}\\
 &=\sqrt{\frac mn}\sqrt{\frac{\log(en)}{\log n}}
 \asymp1.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The quantity on the left is therefore bounded above and bounded away from zero.  Taking its minimum with one, as required by \(\mathrm{def:frontier\mbox{-}rate}\), preserves those bounds, and hence \(b_{nm}\asymp1\).  If instead \(\lambda_{nm}\asymp B\sqrt{nm}\), the same definition gives
\[
 \frac{B\sqrt{nm}}{\lambda_{nm}}\sqrt{\log(en)}\,b^0_{nm}
 \asymp\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}.
\]
The last expression tends to zero, so the truncation is inactive for all sufficiently large \(n\), proving
\[
 b_{nm}\asymp\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}.
\]

We next construct the asserted law in the first signal regime.  Put
\[
 U=n^{-1/2}{\bf 1}_n,
 \qquad
 V=m^{-1/2}{\bf 1}_m,
 \qquad
 M=\lambda_{nm}UV^\top,
 \qquad
 d=\frac B8.
\]
Thus \(M_{ij}=\lambda_{nm}/\sqrt{nm}\) for all cells.  Define deterministic potential outcomes by
\[
 Y_{ij}(0)=d,
 \qquad
 Y_{ij}(1)=d+M_{ij},
\]
and draw all \(A_{ij}\) independently as \({\rm Bernoulli}(q_0)\), independently of these potential outcomes.  With the row-major filtration in \(\mathrm{def:global\mbox{-}filtration}\), this gives the recorded propensity \(p_{ij}=q_0\) at every cell and satisfies sequential randomization.  The strict inequalities \(q<q_0<1-q\) give overlap.  Moreover, bounded aspect ratio and \(\lambda_{nm}\asymp B\sqrt{n\log n}\) imply
\[
 \max_{i,j}|M_{ij}|
 =\frac{\lambda_{nm}}{\sqrt{nm}}
 =O\!\left(B\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n}}\right)
 =o(B).
\]
Consequently, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 |Y_{ij}(0)|=\frac B8\le B,
 \qquad
 |Y_{ij}(1)|\le\frac B8+o(B)\le B.
\]
Thus the law has bounded potential outcomes, and its control-arm mean is the deterministic nonzero constant \(d\).  Because its conditional arm means equal its deterministic potential outcomes, its effect matrix is exactly \(M\).  The displayed factorization is a singular-value decomposition: \(U\) and \(V\) are unit vectors, \(M\) has rank one, and
\[
 \sigma_1(M)=\lambda_{nm}.
\]

We now compute the stochastic scale rather than infer it from the rate formula.  By \(\mathrm{def:ipw\mbox{-}score}\), or equivalently by direct substitution in the score identified in \(\mathrm{lem:ipw\mbox{-}identification}\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_{ij}
 &=\frac{A_{ij}(d+M_{ij})}{q_0}
   -\frac{(1-A_{ij})d}{1-q_0}-M_{ij}\\
 &=(A_{ij}-q_0)c_{nm},
 \end{aligned}
\]
where the common deterministic coefficient is
\[
 c_{nm}
 =\frac{d+M_{ij}}{q_0}+\frac d{1-q_0}.
\]
Since \(d=B/8\), \(M_{ij}=o(B)\), and \(q_0\in(0,1)\) is fixed, there are constants \(0<c<C<\infty\), depending only on \(q_0\), such that
\[
 cB\le c_{nm}\le CB
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Therefore
\[
 (EV)_i
 =\frac{c_{nm}}{\sqrt m}\sum_{j=1}^m(A_{ij}-q_0).
\]

For completeness, we prove the needed two-sided maximum order.  Let
\[
 S_i=m^{-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^m(A_{ij}-q_0).
\]
The variables \(S_1,\ldots,S_n\) are independent.  For a single Bernoulli variable \(A\) of mean \(q_0\), set
\[
 \psi(s)=\log E\exp\{s(A-q_0)\}.
\]
Under the exponentially tilted two-point law, \(\psi''(s)\) is the variance of a random variable supported on \(\{0,1\}\), and hence \(0\le\psi''(s)\le1/4\).  Since \(\psi(0)=\psi'(0)=0\), two integrations give
\[
 \psi(s)=\int_0^s(s-u)\psi''(u)\,du\le\frac{s^2}{8}
\]
for \(s\ge0\), and the same bound follows for \(s<0\) by integrating over the reversed interval.  Independence within a row and exponential Markov inequality now give, for \(t>0\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P(S_i\ge t)
 &\le\inf_{s>0}
 \exp\left\{-st\sqrt m+\frac{ms^2}{8}\right\}
 =e^{-2t^2},\\
 P(S_i\le-t)&\le e^{-2t^2}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The union bound therefore yields
\[
 P\!\left(\max_{1\le i\le n}|S_i|>C_1\sqrt{\log n}\right)
 \le2n^{1-2C_1^2}\longrightarrow0
\]
for every fixed \(C_1>1\).  Hence \(\max_i|S_i|=O_P(\sqrt{\log n})\).

For the matching lower order, let \(X\sim{\rm Binomial}(m,q_0)\), and write \(p_k=P(X=k)\).  The exact adjacent-mass identity is
\[
 \frac{p_{k+1}}{p_k}
 =\frac{m-k}{k+1}\frac{q_0}{1-q_0}.
\]
It follows directly from whether this ratio is at least or at most one that a mode \(k_0\) may be chosen with \(|k_0-mq_0|\le1\).  Also,
\[
 E(X-mq_0)^2=mq_0(1-q_0)\le\frac m4.
\]
Applying Markov's inequality to \((X-mq_0)^2\) gives
\[
 P(|X-mq_0|\le\sqrt m)\ge\frac34.
\]
The interval in this event contains at most \(2\sqrt m+2\) integers.  Since a mode has mass at least that of every integer, there is a constant \(c_1>0\) such that
\[
 p_{k_0}\ge c_1m^{-1/2}.
\]

We next compare a moderate-deviation mass to the modal mass using only the adjacent-mass identity.  Because \(q_0\) is fixed in \((0,1)\) and \(|k_0-mq_0|\le1\), there exist constants \(c_2,C_2>0\), depending only on \(q_0\), such that whenever \(1\le s\le c_2m\) and \(0\le l<s\),
\[
 \log\frac{p_{k_0+l+1}}{p_{k_0+l}}
 \ge-C_2\frac{l+1}{m}.
\]
Indeed, after substituting \(k=k_0+l\) in the exact ratio, both its numerator and denominator are positive fixed fractions of \(m\); subtracting its value at \(l=0\) changes each logarithm by at most a fixed multiple of \((l+1)/m\), while the possible unit displacement of \(k_0\) from \(mq_0\) contributes only another fixed multiple of \(m^{-1}\).  Summing this displayed inequality from \(l=0\) to \(s-1\) gives
\[
 p_{k_0+s}
 \ge c_1m^{-1/2}
 \exp\!\left\{-\frac{C_2s(s+1)}{2m}\right\}.
\]
Choose a fixed \(\eta>0\) sufficiently small that \(C_2\eta^2<1/4\), and put
\[
 s_n=\left\lceil\eta\sqrt{m\log n}\right\rceil.
\]
Because \(m\asymp n\), one has \(s_n=o(m)\), so the preceding bound applies for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Moreover,
\[
 \frac{k_0+s_n-mq_0}{\sqrt m}
 \ge\frac{\eta}{2}\sqrt{\log n}
\]
eventually.  Thus, after increasing \(C_2\) harmlessly to absorb the ceiling term, a generic row obeys
\[
 \pi_n
 :=P\!\left(S_i\ge\frac{\eta}{2}\sqrt{\log n}\right)
 \ge c_3m^{-1/2}n^{-C_2\eta^2}.
\]
Since \(m\asymp n\) and \(C_2\eta^2<1/4\),
\[
 n\pi_n\ge c_4 n^{1/2-C_2\eta^2}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Independence across rows then implies
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P\!\left(\max_{1\le i\le n}|S_i|
 <\frac{\eta}{2}\sqrt{\log n}\right)
 &\le(1-\pi_n)^n\\
 &\le e^{-n\pi_n}\longrightarrow0.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Together with the upper bound, this proves
\[
 \max_{1\le i\le n}|S_i|=\Theta_P(\sqrt{\log n}).
\]
The coefficient bounds and \(\sigma_1(M)=\lambda_{nm}\asymp B\sqrt{n\log n}\) consequently give
\[
 \sqrt n\left\lVert\frac{EV}{\sigma_1(M)}\right\rVert_\infty
 =\frac{\sqrt n\,c_{nm}}{\lambda_{nm}}max_i|S_i|
 =\Theta_P(1),
\]
which is the asserted stochastic sanity check.

It remains to verify the algebraic blind-score statement.  For an arbitrary constant-propensity law with \(p_{ij}=q_0\), the definition in \(\mathrm{def:blind\mbox{-}tangent}\) gives
\[
 \bar p_i=\frac1m\sum_{j\in J}E_P[p_{ij}]=q_0.
\]
Therefore \(Z^{\bar p}=Z^{\mathrm{ipw}}\) cell by cell.  The defining identity in \(\mathrm{def:blind\mbox{-}expansion\mbox{-}residual}\),
\[
 R^{\mathrm B}=Z^{\mathrm B}-Z^{\bar p}-D^{\mathrm B},
\]
can then be rearranged without approximation to yield
\[
 Z^{\mathrm B}=Z^{\mathrm{ipw}}+D^{\mathrm B}+R^{\mathrm B}.
\]
Finally, substituting \(p_{ij}=\bar p_i=q_0\) into the derivative loading in \(\mathrm{def:blind\mbox{-}tangent}\) gives
\[
 H^{\mathrm B}_{ij}
 =\frac{q_0\mu^1_{ij}}{q_0^2}
  +\frac{(1-q_0)\mu^0_{ij}}{(1-q_0)^2}
 =\frac{\mu^1_{ij}}{q_0}+\frac{\mu^0_{ij}}{1-q_0},
\]
and hence
\[
 D^{\mathrm B}_{ij}
 =-(\widehat p_i-q_0)
 \left\{\frac{\mu^1_{ij}}{q_0}+\frac{\mu^0_{ij}}{1-q_0}\right\}.
\]
This displays a nonzero first derivative whenever the loading in braces is nonzero.  It does not force the realized tangent to be nonzero: on any sample for which \(\widehat p_i=q_0\), the displayed product vanishes.  This proves precisely the final qualification in the proposition.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This pins down the signal regimes and shows why empirical row normalization has its own first-order tangent. \textit{Gap.} Mehrotra et al.'s public Algorithm 1 gives the independent-assignment empirical-row-scaling benchmark for separate arm fits; the signed recorded-score decomposition here isolates the denominator tangent relevant to the new blind procedure. \textit{Consumer.} Analysts can distinguish a fixed known denominator from a random row-total normalization.

\begin{theorem}[thm:dwivedi-separation]\label{thm:dwivedi-separation}
Fix \(q<1/4\) and the amplitudes in \(\mathrm{def:markov\mbox{-}witness}\). For all sufficiently large \(\ell\), the bounded Markov law \(W_\ell\) lies in \(\mathfrak P^{\mathrm{pred}}_{n_\ell,m_\ell}(1,1,1,q,B,\theta\sqrt{n_\ell m_\ell})\), but its arm means depend on \(A_{i,j-1}\), so it is not generated by the exogenous unit and time factors in Dwivedi et al.'s published equation (4) on page 2383. Conversely, their published Assumption 6 on page 2397 and Theorem 4.1 on page 2398 contain the bounded Lipschitz nonlinear-factor law generated by \(f^{\mathrm D}_0\), \(f^{\mathrm D}_1\), \(u^{\mathrm D}\), and \(v^{\mathrm D}\), for which \(\operatorname{rank}(M^{\mathrm D})=\min(n,m)\) almost surely; this law is excluded by \(\operatorname{rank}(M)=r\) for fixed \(r\). Thus their unit-by-time nearest-neighbor bound in published Theorem 3.1 and equations (17)--(18) on page 2389 and the normalized maximum-row IPW-SVD frontier are non-representable in either direction.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(n=n_\ell=2\ell\) and \(m=m_\ell=2\lceil\gamma\ell\rceil\).  We first verify membership of the Markov witness in the predictable class, condition by condition.  Since
\[
 \frac{m_\ell}{n_\ell}=\frac{\lceil\gamma\ell\rceil}{\ell}\longrightarrow\gamma
 \quad\text{and}\quad c_-<\gamma<c_+,
\]
there is \(\ell_0\) such that \(c_-\le m_\ell/n_\ell\le c_+\) whenever \(\ell\ge\ell_0\).  This proves the condition in \(\mathrm{ass:aspect\mbox{-}ratio}\) along the witness path.

Fix a cell \((i,j)\).  If \(j=1\), then \(A_{i0}=A_i^{\mathrm{init}}\) belongs to \(\mathbb G_0\); if \(j\ge2\), then \(A_{i,j-1}\) has been recorded before global step \(o(i,j)\).  Hence \(A_{i,j-1}\), \(p_{ij}\), and both \(Y_{ij}(0)\) and \(Y_{ij}(1)\) in \(\mathrm{def:markov\mbox{-}witness}\) are \(\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1}\)-measurable.  Consequently,
\[
 \sigma\!\left(\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1},Y_{ij}(0),Y_{ij}(1)\right)
 =\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1}
 \quad\text{up to completion}.
\]
By the definition of the recorded propensity and by the witness construction,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P\!\left(A_{ij}=1\mid\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1},Y_{ij}(0),Y_{ij}(1)\right)
 &=P\!\left(A_{ij}=1\mid\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1}\right)\\
 &=p_{ij}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(\mathrm{ass:sequential\mbox{-}randomization}\) holds.  Because each product \((-1)^{i+j}(2A_{i,j-1}-1)\) is either \(-1\) or \(1\),
\[
 p_{ij}\in\left\{\frac14,\frac34\right\}.
\]
The strict inequality \(q<1/4\) therefore gives \(q\le p_{ij}\le1-q\), which is exactly \(\mathrm{ass:overlap}\).  Moreover, for \(a\in\{0,1\}\), the triangle inequality and the amplitude restriction in \(\mathrm{def:markov\mbox{-}witness}\) give
\[
 |Y_{ij}(a)|\le |C_{ij}|+\frac{\theta}{2}
 =h+\frac{\theta}{2}\le B.
\]
This verifies \(\mathrm{ass:bounded\mbox{-}outcomes}\).

Since the two potential outcomes are measurable with respect to the conditioning field, their conditional means equal the outcomes themselves.  Subtracting the two displayed formulas in \(\mathrm{def:markov\mbox{-}witness}\) yields
\[
 M_{ij}=\mu^1_{ij}-\mu^0_{ij}=\theta(-1)^{i+j}.
\]
Let \(s\in\mathbb R^n\) and \(t\in\mathbb R^m\) have coordinates \(s_i=(-1)^i\) and \(t_j=(-1)^j\).  Then
\[
 M=\theta st^\top
   =\left(\frac{s}{\sqrt n}\right)
      \bigl(\theta\sqrt{nm}\bigr)
      \left(\frac{t}{\sqrt m}\right)^\top .
\]
Because \(\theta>0\), this is a singular-value decomposition with rank one, sole singular value \(\theta\sqrt{nm}\), and singular vectors \(U=s/\sqrt n\) and \(V=t/\sqrt m\), up to their common harmless sign.  It follows equation by equation that
\[
 \lVert U\rVert_{2,\infty}=n^{-1/2}=\sqrt{\frac{1\cdot1}{n}},
 \qquad
 \lVert V\rVert_{2,\infty}=m^{-1/2}=\sqrt{\frac{1\cdot1}{m}},
\]
\[
 \frac{\sigma_1(M)}{\sigma_1(M)}=1,
 \qquad
 \sigma_1(M)=\theta\sqrt{nm}.
\]
Thus \(\mathrm{ass:exact\mbox{-}rank}\), \(\mathrm{ass:left\mbox{-}incoherence}\), \(\mathrm{ass:right\mbox{-}incoherence}\), \(\mathrm{ass:condition\mbox{-}number}\), and \(\mathrm{ass:signal\mbox{-}floor}\) hold with \((r,\mu,K,\lambda_{nm})=(1,1,1,\theta\sqrt{nm})\).  Together with the design, boundedness, and aspect checks above, the membership definition \(\mathrm{def:predictable\mbox{-}class}\) gives
\[
 W_\ell\in
 \mathfrak P^{\mathrm{pred}}_{n_\ell,m_\ell}
 \left(1,1,1,q,B,\theta\sqrt{n_\ell m_\ell}\right)
 \quad\text{for every }\ell\ge\ell_0.
\]

We next prove that this member is outside the published Dwivedi model.  For every \(j\ge2\), \(\mathrm{def:markov\mbox{-}witness}\) gives
\[
 \mu^1_{ij}
 =h(-1)^{i+j}(2A_{i,j-1}-1)+\frac{\theta}{2}(-1)^{i+j}.
\]
Changing \(A_{i,j-1}\) from zero to one changes this mean by \(2h(-1)^{i+j}\ne0\).  Both values of \(A_{i,j-1}\) have positive conditional probability because its recorded propensity lies in \(\{1/4,3/4\}\).  Hence this is genuine, rather than null-set, dependence of the current arm mean on a past treatment.  The published model restriction recorded in \(\mathrm{lem:dwivedi\mbox{-}exogenous\mbox{-}model\mbox{-}scope}\) requires the current counterfactual to be generated from exogenous unit and time factors and exogenous noise and expressly excludes dependence on past treatment.  The Markov witness therefore cannot be generated by that model.

For the converse separation, consider \(\mathrm{def:dwivedi\mbox{-}full\mbox{-}rank\mbox{-}witness}\).  On \([0,1]^2\),
\[
 0\le f^{\mathrm D}_1(u,v)=e^{uv}\le e,
 \qquad
 \left|\frac{\partial}{\partial u}e^{uv}\right|=ve^{uv}\le e,
 \qquad
 \left|\frac{\partial}{\partial v}e^{uv}\right|=ue^{uv}\le e.
\]
The one-dimensional mean-value theorem, applied once in each coordinate, therefore shows that \(f^{\mathrm D}_1\) is bounded and coordinatewise \(e\)-Lipschitz; \(f^{\mathrm D}_0=0\) is bounded and coordinatewise Lipschitz with constant zero.  The independent uniform factors have compact support and density one.  Thus these functions and factors satisfy the nonlinear-factor and factor-distribution conditions recorded in \(\mathrm{lem:dwivedi\mbox{-}nonlinear\mbox{-}extension\mbox{-}scope}\).  The remaining conditions of that cited theorem are simultaneously satisfiable: take an independent constant one-half treatment policy, independent bounded centered noise, and independent uniform factors for the constant control arm.  These choices preserve the two arm means and hence preserve
\[
 M^{\mathrm D}_{ij}=e^{u_i^{\mathrm D}v_j^{\mathrm D}}.
\]
This constructs a law in the published nonlinear-factor setting whose effect matrix is \(M^{\mathrm D}\).

It remains to compute its rank.  Independent variables with continuous uniform laws have no ties with probability one; a finite union bound over pairs shows that all \(u_i^{\mathrm D}\) are distinct and all \(v_j^{\mathrm D}\) are distinct almost surely.  We use the following elementary zero-count argument on this probability-one event.  If \(x_1<\cdots<x_d\) are distinct real numbers, then every nonzero function
\[
 F(y)=\sum_{k=1}^d c_k e^{x_k y}
\]
has at most \(d-1\) distinct real zeros.  Indeed, the assertion is immediate for \(d=1\).  For the induction step, suppose \(F\) had \(d\) distinct zeros and put
\[
 H(y)=e^{-x_1y}F(y)=c_1+\sum_{k=2}^d c_k e^{(x_k-x_1)y}.
\]
Rolle's theorem would give at least \(d-1\) distinct zeros of
\[
 H'(y)=\sum_{k=2}^d c_k(x_k-x_1)e^{(x_k-x_1)y}.
\]
The induction hypothesis forces \(c_k(x_k-x_1)=0\) for every \(k\ge2\).  Distinctness gives \(x_k-x_1\ne0\), so \(c_k=0\) for \(k\ge2\); the zeros of the constant \(H=c_1\) then force \(c_1=0\), contradicting nontriviality.  This proves the zero-count assertion.

Let \(d=\min(n,m)\), choose any \(d\) rows and \(d\) columns of \(M^{\mathrm D}\), and permute them so that the selected \(u\)'s and \(v\)'s are increasing.  If this square submatrix were singular, there would be coefficients \(c_1,\ldots,c_d\), not all zero, for which
\[
 \sum_{k=1}^d c_k e^{u_k^{\mathrm D}v_j^{\mathrm D}}=0,
 \qquad j=1,\ldots,d.
\]
The corresponding exponential sum would have the \(d\) distinct zeros \(v_1^{\mathrm D},\ldots,v_d^{\mathrm D}\), contradicting the zero-count assertion.  The submatrix is therefore invertible, so
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(M^{\mathrm D})\ge d.
\]
The reverse inequality holds for every \(n\)-by-\(m\) matrix, and hence
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(M^{\mathrm D})=\min(n,m)
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
For a fixed \(r\), eventually \(\min(n_\ell,m_\ell)>r\), so this source-compatible law violates \(\mathrm{ass:exact\mbox{-}rank}\) and is excluded from \(\mathfrak P^{\mathrm{pred}}_{n_\ell,m_\ell}(r,\mu,K,q,B,\lambda)\), independently of the other class restrictions.

We have exhibited a member of the predictable low-rank class outside the published factor class and a member of the published nonlinear-factor class outside every fixed-rank predictable class along the dimension path.  Thus neither model class contains the other.  Finally, \(\mathrm{lem:dwivedi\mbox{-}nearest\mbox{-}neighbor\mbox{-}scope}\) records that the cited Theorem 3.1 and equations (17)--(18) bound the squared error of a scalar nearest-neighbor estimate at a fixed unit and time, whereas the present frontier concerns normalized maximum-row loss for a left singular space.  The differing targets together with the two explicit class witnesses prove that neither uniform guarantee is a specialization of the other, which is the claimed non-representability in both directions.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This gives the exact model-theoretic separation from the closest adaptive pooled unit-by-time factor result. \textit{Gap.} Dwivedi et al., published Theorem 3.1 and equations (17)--(18) on page 2389, control a nearest-neighbor unit-by-time arm-mean error under the factor structure in equation (4) on page 2383, not a row-subspace loss under treatment-path-dependent arm means. \textit{Consumer.} Referees can distinguish the row-factor target and predictable-mask domain from published adaptive counterfactual imputation.

\begin{theorem}[thm:ipw-rowwise-upper]\label{thm:ipw-rowwise-upper}
Uniformly over \(P\in\mathfrak P^{\mathrm{pred}}_{n,m}(r,\mu,K,q,B,\lambda)\), define
\[
 a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm}=
 \sqrt{\frac n{\mu r}}\left[1\wedge
 \frac B{\lambda_{nm}}\left\{
 \sqrt{\frac{(n\vee m)\log(e(n+m))}{q}}+
 \frac{\log(e(n+m))}{q}\right\}\right].
\]
Then recorded-propensity IPW--SVD satisfies
\(\mathcal L(\widehat U^{\mathrm I},U)=O_P(a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm})\).  This maximum-row assertion is pointwise in the target and contains no factor depending on \(|\mathcal G_n|\).  In general the displayed IPW--SVD conclusion cannot replace \(a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm}\) by the lower/reference scale \(b_{nm}\), as witnessed by Proposition \(\mathrm{prop:adaptive\mbox{-}endogenous\mbox{-}target\mbox{-}obstruction}\).  That witness concerns this estimator only and does not assert that every observed-data estimator has risk larger than order \(b_{nm}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Proposition \(\mathrm{prop:ipw\mbox{-}expected\mbox{-}risk\mbox{-}upper}\) gives
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P^{\mathrm{pred}}_{n,m}(r,\mu,K,q,B,\lambda)}
 E_P\!\left[\mathcal L(\widehat U^{\mathrm I},U)\right]
 \le C a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm}.
\]
The radius is positive because \(B,q,\lambda_{nm}>0\) and \(n,m\ge2\).  For the nonnegative loss \(X=\mathcal L(\widehat U^{\mathrm I},U)\), the pointwise inequality \(t a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm}{\bf1}\{X>t a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm}\}\le X\) and expectation give, for every \(t>0\),
\[
 \sup_P P\{\mathcal L(\widehat U^{\mathrm I},U)>t a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm}\}
 \le C/t,
\]
which is the uniform \(O_P(a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm})\) assertion.  Neither the radius nor the moment bound uses \(\mathcal G_n\), so no factor depending on \(|\mathcal G_n|\) occurs.

Proposition \(\mathrm{prop:adaptive\mbox{-}endogenous\mbox{-}target\mbox{-}obstruction}\) supplies a same-class sequence \(P_n\), constants \(c_0,c_1>0\), and \(b_{nn}\to0\) such that
\[
 \liminf_nP_n\{\mathcal L(\widehat U^{\mathrm I},U)>c_0\}\ge c_1.
\]
If the IPW--SVD loss were uniformly \(O_P(b_{nm})\), then for \(\varepsilon=c_1/2\) a fixed \(T\) would make the probability at threshold \(Tb_{nn}\) at most \(\varepsilon\) eventually.  Since \(Tb_{nn}<c_0\) eventually, this contradicts the last display.  The witness evaluates \(\widehat U^{\mathrm I}\) only, and therefore proves no impossibility for another estimator in \(\mathfrak E_{n,m}\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the explicit achievable side for the specified recorded-propensity IPW--SVD estimator; the companion moment proposition places it in the expected-loss criterion used by the converse. \textit{Gap.} The rate is \(a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm}\), not the lower/reference scale \(b_{nm}\).  The endogenous-target witness rules out replacing it by \(b_{nm}\) for this estimator but says nothing conclusive about all observed-data estimators. \textit{Consumer.} Designers obtain a conservative, fully explicit row-subspace error radius under recorded predictable randomization.

\begin{theorem}[thm:adaptive-minimax-lower]\label{thm:adaptive-minimax-lower}
Fix \(r\), \(K\), and an incoherence envelope \(\mu>1\).  Positive constants \(c_0,c_1,c_2\), depending only on these fixed quantities, \(q\), and the aspect bounds, exist such that, for all sufficiently large \(n,m\), whenever \(b_{nm}\le c_0\) and \(\lambda_{nm}\le c_1B\sqrt{nm}\),
\[
 \inf_{\widetilde U\in\mathfrak E_{n,m}}
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P^{\mathrm{pred}}_{n,m}(r,\mu,K,q,B,\lambda)}
 E_P[\mathcal L(\widetilde U,U)]\ge c_2b_{nm}.
\]
The bound already holds on an independent constant-propensity submodel with \(p_{ij}=q\), so observing \(p\) does not weaken it.  The strict slack \(\mu>1\) is needed by the continuous local row packing used here.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(t=m/n\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:aspect\mbox{-}ratio}\) gives \(t\in[c_-,c_+]\).  Choose the constant \(c_0\) in the theorem smaller than one.  On \(b_{nm}\le c_0\), the truncation in \(\mathrm{def:frontier\mbox{-}rate}\) is inactive, and hence
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 b_{nm}
 &=\frac{B}{\lambda_{nm}}K r^{3/2}\mu q^{-1/2}
   \sqrt{\log(en)}\sqrt{nm}\sqrt{n^{-1}+nm^{-2}}\\
 &=K r^{3/2}\mu\sqrt{t+t^{-1}}
   \frac{B}{\lambda_{nm}}\sqrt{\frac{n\log(en)}q}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus, for
\[
 s_{nm}:=\frac{B}{\lambda_{nm}}\sqrt{\frac{n\log(en)}q},
\]
there are constants \(C_-,C_+>0\), depending only on \(r,K,\mu,c_-,c_+\), such that
\[
 C_-s_{nm}\le b_{nm}\le C_+s_{nm}.
 \tag{1}
\]

We construct a finite constant-propensity subexperiment.  Since \(r\) is fixed, partition \(I\) into balanced nonempty blocks \(I_1,\ldots,I_r\) and \(J\) into balanced nonempty blocks \(J_1,\ldots,J_r\).  Write \(N_s=|I_s|\), \(M_s=|J_s|\),
\[
 u_0=N_1^{-1/2}{\bf1}_{I_1},\qquad
 u_s=N_s^{-1/2}{\bf1}_{I_s}\quad(2\le s\le r),
 \qquad v_s=M_s^{-1/2}{\bf1}_{J_s}\quad(1\le s\le r).
\]
Fix \(k_0\in I_1\), put \(\mathcal K=I_1\setminus\{k_0\}\), and, for \(k\in\mathcal K\), define
\[
 w_k=\frac{e_k-e_{k_0}}{\sqrt2},\qquad
 \delta=a\frac{B}{\lambda_{nm}}\sqrt{\frac{\log(en)}q},
 \qquad u_k=\frac{u_0+\delta w_k}{\sqrt{1+\delta^2}},
 \tag{2}
\]
where \(a>0\) is fixed below.  Since \(u_0\perp w_k\) and both are unit vectors, \(u_k\) is a unit vector.  Disjoint block supports show that
\[
 U_k=(u_k,u_2,\ldots,u_r)\in\mathbb O_{n,r},
 \qquad U_0=(u_0,u_2,\ldots,u_r)\in\mathbb O_{n,r},
 \qquad V=(v_1,\ldots,v_r)\in\mathbb O_{m,r}.
\]
Set
\[
 M_k=\lambda_{nm}U_kV^\top\quad(k\in\mathcal K),
 \qquad M_0=\lambda_{nm}U_0V^\top.
 \tag{3}
\]

We verify membership rather than building it into the construction.  Equations (1)--(2) imply
\[
 \delta\sqrt n=a s_{nm}\le aC_-^{-1}b_{nm}\le aC_-^{-1}c_0.
 \tag{4}
\]
For a row in \(I_1\),
\[
 \|e_i^\top U_k\|_2\le N_1^{-1/2}+\delta/\sqrt2,
 \tag{5}
\]
whereas a row in \(I_s\), \(s\ge2\), has norm \(N_s^{-1/2}\).  Balanced blocks give \(\max_s\sqrt{n/N_s}\to\sqrt r\).  The strict slack \(\mu>1\) places \(\sqrt r\) strictly below \(\sqrt{\mu r}\).  Choose \(ac_0\) small enough in (4), depending only on this slack and the constants in (1), and then take \(n\) sufficiently large.  Equations (4)--(5) give
\[
 \|U_k\|_{2,\infty}\le\sqrt{\mu r/n}.
 \tag{6}
\]
The same balanced-block calculation, without the perturbation, yields
\[
 \|V\|_{2,\infty}\le\sqrt{\mu r/m}
 \tag{7}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(m\).  Thus the two incoherence assumptions hold.  By (3), all \(r\) nonzero singular values of \(M_k\) equal \(\lambda_{nm}\).  Hence exact rank, the signal floor, and the condition-number bound hold, the last because \(1\le K\).

For sufficiently large dimensions, balanced blocks satisfy \(N_s\ge n/(2r)\) and \(M_s\ge m/(2r)\).  Equations (2)--(3) therefore give
\[
 \max_{i,j}|(M_k)_{ij}|
 \le \frac{2r\lambda_{nm}}{\sqrt{nm}}
     +aB\sqrt{\frac{2r\log(en)}{qm}}.
 \tag{8}
\]
Choose \(c_1>0\) so that \(2rc_1\le1/4\).  Under \(\lambda_{nm}\le c_1B\sqrt{nm}\), the first term in (8) is at most \(B/4\); bounded aspect ratio makes the second at most \(B/4\) for all sufficiently large \(n,m\).  Consequently
\[
 \max_{i,j}|(M_k)_{ij}|\le B/2.
 \tag{9}
\]

For \(k\in\mathcal K\cup\{0\}\), define \(P_k\) by setting \(Y_{ij}(0)=0\), drawing the \(Y_{ij}(1)\)'s independently with values in \(\{-B,B\}\) and
\[
 P_k\{Y_{ij}(1)=B\}
 =h_{k,ij}:=\frac12\left(1+\frac{(M_k)_{ij}}B\right),
 \tag{10}
\]
and, independently of all potential outcomes and independently across cells, drawing \(A_{ij}\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(q)\) and recording \(p_{ij}=q\).  Equation (9) puts \(h_{k,ij}\in[1/4,3/4]\).  Independence from the pre-cell field in \(\mathrm{def:global\mbox{-}filtration}\) and (10) give
\[
 \mu^1_{ij}=E_{P_k}[Y_{ij}(1)\mid\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1}]=(M_k)_{ij},
 \qquad \mu^0_{ij}=0.
 \tag{11}
\]
Thus the causal matrix \(M=\mu^1-\mu^0\) is \(M_k\); it has not been defined through an observed-law score.  Independence of \(A_{ij}\) from the current potential outcomes after conditioning on the recorded past verifies sequential randomization.  Since \(q<1/2\), \(p_{ij}=q\) verifies overlap.  The support in (10), together with \(Y_{ij}(0)=0\), verifies bounded outcomes.  Equations (6)--(7), the singular-value calculation, and the assumed aspect ratio verify the remaining conditions in \(\mathrm{def:predictable\mbox{-}class}\).  Therefore
\[
 P_k\in\mathfrak P^{\mathrm{pred}}_{n,m}(r,\mu,K,q,B,\lambda)
 \quad(k\in\mathcal K\cup\{0\}).
 \tag{12}
\]

We next control the information in \(\mathcal O_{n,m}=\sigma(A,Y,p)\).  If \(x,y\in[-B/2,B/2]\), put \(s=(1+x/B)/2\) and \(t'=(1+y/B)/2\).  For fixed \(t'\in[1/4,3/4]\), the Bernoulli relative entropy
\[
 f(s)=s\log(s/t')+(1-s)\log\{(1-s)/(1-t')\}
\]
satisfies \(f(t')=f'(t')=0\) and \(f''(s)=1/\{s(1-s)\}\le16/3\) on \([1/4,3/4]\).  Twice integrating \(f''\) gives \(f(s)=\int_{t'}^s(s-u)f''(u)\,du\), with the usual oriented-integral interpretation when \(s<t'\).  Hence
\[
 \operatorname{kl}\left(\frac{1+x/B}{2},\frac{1+y/B}{2}\right)
 \le \frac{8}{3}\left(\frac{x-y}{2B}\right)^2
 =\frac{2}{3}\frac{(x-y)^2}{B^2}.
 \tag{13}
\]
The assignment law and recorded propensity agree under \(P_k\) and \(P_0\).  Conditional on \(A_{ij}=0\), the observed outcome is the common value zero; conditional on \(A_{ij}=1\), it has Bernoulli sign parameter \(h_{k,ij}\).  Product likelihood factorization, (13), and the chain rule for relative entropy give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{KL}(P_k^{\mathcal O},P_0^{\mathcal O})
 &=q\sum_{i,j}\operatorname{kl}(h_{k,ij},h_{0,ij})\\
 &\le \frac{Cq}{B^2}\|M_k-M_0\|_F^2.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{14}
\]
Only the first singular component differs, so
\[
 \|M_k-M_0\|_F^2
 =\lambda_{nm}^2\|u_k-u_0\|_2^2.
\]
Orthogonality in (2) gives
\[
 \|u_k-u_0\|_2^2
 =2\left(1-\frac1{\sqrt{1+\delta^2}}\right)\le\delta^2.
\]
Substitution of (2) into (14) proves
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_k^{\mathcal O},P_0^{\mathcal O})
 \le Ca^2\log(en).
 \tag{15}
\]

For distinct \(k,l\in\mathcal K\), inspect row \(k\).  Right orthogonal multiplication preserves a row's Euclidean norm, while (2) gives
\[
 \|e_k^\top U_k\|_2-\|e_k^\top U_l\|_2
 =\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{2(1+\delta^2)}}.
\]
The reverse triangle inequality therefore yields, for every \(R\in\mathbb O_r\),
\[
 \|U_kR-U_l\|_{2,\infty}
 \ge\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{2(1+\delta^2)}}.
\]
By \(\mathrm{def:rowwise\mbox{-}loss}\),
\[
 \mathcal L(U_k,U_l)
 \ge\Delta_{nm}:=\sqrt{\frac n{\mu r}}
       \frac{\delta}{\sqrt{2(1+\delta^2)}}.
 \tag{16}
\]
Equation (4) implies \(\delta\to0\).  Combining (1), (2), and (16), and using \(t\in[c_-,c_+]\), gives a constant \(c>0\) such that
\[
 \Delta_{nm}\ge c b_{nm}.
 \tag{17}
\]
Indeed, before the bounded factor \(\{2(1+\delta^2)\}^{-1/2}\), the exact ratio is
\[
 \frac{\sqrt{n/(\mu r)}\,\delta}{b_{nm}}
 =\frac{a}{K r^2\mu^{3/2}\sqrt{t+t^{-1}}}.
\]

It remains to convert (15)--(17) into risk.  Let \(D=|\mathcal K|=N_1-1\), let \(\Theta\) be uniform on \(\mathcal K\), and conditionally on \(\Theta=k\) draw the observed experiment from \(P_k^{\mathcal O}\).  With \(\overline P=D^{-1}\sum_kP_k^{\mathcal O}\), direct expansion of the logarithms gives
\[
 I(\Theta;\mathcal O)
 =\frac1D\sum_k\operatorname{KL}(P_k^{\mathcal O},P_0^{\mathcal O})
  -\operatorname{KL}(\overline P,P_0^{\mathcal O})
 \le Ca^2\log(en),
 \tag{18}
\]
where the inequality uses nonnegativity of relative entropy and (15).  For any decoder \(\widehat\Theta\), conditioning the entropy of \(\Theta\) on its error indicator gives
\[
 P(\widehat\Theta\ne\Theta)
 \ge1-\frac{I(\Theta;\mathcal O)+\log2}{\log D}.
 \tag{19}
\]
Balanced blocks imply \(D\asymp n\).  Choose \(a>0\) sufficiently small that (18)--(19) make the error probability at least \(1/2\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).

For any \(\widetilde U\in\mathfrak E_{n,m}\), decode by a nearest member of \(\{U_k:k\in\mathcal K\}\) in the orthogonal-quotient \(2,\infty\) distance, with a deterministic rule for ties.  This quotient distance satisfies the triangle inequality: compose two orthogonal alignments and use invariance of row norms under right orthogonal multiplication.  Hence a decoding error under \(P_k\), together with (16), implies
\[
 \mathcal L(\widetilde U,U_k)\ge\Delta_{nm}/2.
\]
Averaging over the uniform index, then using (17)--(19), gives
\[
 \sup_{k\in\mathcal K}E_{P_k}[\mathcal L(\widetilde U,U_k)]
 \ge\Delta_{nm}/4\ge c_2b_{nm}.
\]
By (12), the finite family is contained in the frozen predictable class and has \(p_{ij}=q\) at every cell.  Taking the supremum over the full class and then the infimum over \(\widetilde U\in\mathfrak E_{n,m}\) proves the theorem.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The converse tests whether the corrected adaptive scale is information-limited. \textit{Gap.} Cai et al., published Theorem 2 and equation (22b) on page 953, give a two-to-infinity lower bound for independent Gaussian noise observed through independent Bernoulli sampling; arXiv v5 page 10 corroborates the locator. Mehrotra et al.'s public Theorem 3.2 and Corollary 3.3, Athey et al.'s published Theorem 2, equation (14), on page 1724, and Sun et al.'s published Theorem 3 in Section 4 of article e70002 are upper-error results and contain no normalized effect-subspace minimax experiment. The present converse instead embeds a bounded causal score with observed propensities and permits predictable masks; Wang et al. study Frobenius risk under fixed heterogeneous sampling. \textit{Consumer.} Trial designers obtain a lower benchmark linking decision budget, outcome scale, and signal strength.

\begin{lemma}[lem:blind-tangent-control]\label{lem:blind-tangent-control}
Under sequential randomization, overlap, bounded outcomes, bounded aspect ratio, propensity-path stability, and \(b_{nm}=o(1)\), \(\max_{i\in I}|\widehat p_i-\bar p_i|=O_P(\sqrt{\log(en)/m})\) and \(\lVert D^{\mathrm B}\rVert/\lambda_{nm}=O_P(Bq^{-2}\sqrt{n\log(en)}/\lambda_{nm})=O_P(b_{nm})=o_P(1)\). If the primitive oracle gap holds, then \(P\{\sigma_r(T^{\mathrm B})-\sigma_{r+1}(T^{\mathrm B})\ge(c_{\mathrm B}/2)\lambda_{nm}\}\to1\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let
\[
 \widetilde p_i=\frac1m\sum_{j\in J}A_{ij},\qquad
 \delta_{nm}=\max_{i\in I}|\widehat p_i-\bar p_i|.
\]
Overlap implies \(q\le E_P[p_{ij}]\le1-q\), and hence \(\bar p_i\in[q,1-q]\).  Projection onto the closed interval \([q/2,1-q/2]\) fixes \(\bar p_i\) and is one-Lipschitz.  Therefore
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |\widehat p_i-\bar p_i|
 &\le |\widetilde p_i-\bar p_i|\\
 &\le \left|\frac1m\sum_{j\in J}(A_{ij}-p_{ij})\right|
 +\left|\frac1m\sum_{j\in J}(p_{ij}-E_P[p_{ij}])\right|.
 \end{aligned}
\]
For fixed \(i\), the variables \(A_{ij}-p_{ij}\), in increasing \(j\), are consecutive martingale differences for the global filtration: this follows from the definition of \(p_{ij}\) and the row-major ordering.  If \(X=A_{ij}-p_{ij}\), then, conditionally on \(\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1}\), \(X\) has mean zero and lies in an interval of length one.  The elementary convexity bound
\[
 E_P[e^{tX}\mid\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1}]\le e^{t^2/8}
\]
follows by maximizing \(p e^{t(1-p)}+(1-p)e^{-tp}\) over \(p\in[0,1]\).  Iterated conditioning and Chernoff's inequality consequently give
\[
 P\!\left(\left|\frac1m\sum_{j\in J}(A_{ij}-p_{ij})\right|>x\right)
 \le 2e^{-2mx^2}.
\]
A union bound over the \(n\) rows, followed by the propensity-path-stability assumption, yields
\[
 \delta_{nm}=O_P\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log(en)}m}\right).
\]

Bounded outcomes imply \(|\mu^a_{ij}|\le B\) for \(a\in\{0,1\}\), because conditional expectation contracts the almost-sure supremum norm.  Since \(\bar p_i\in[q,1-q]\),
\[
 |H^{\mathrm B}_{ij}|
 \le \frac{|\mu^1_{ij}|}{\bar p_i^2}
      +\frac{|\mu^0_{ij}|}{(1-\bar p_i)^2}
 \le \frac{2B}{q^2}.
\]
The definition \(D^{\mathrm B}_{ij}=-(\widehat p_i-\bar p_i)H^{\mathrm B}_{ij}\), the inequality \(\lVert C\rVert\le\lVert C\rVert_F\), and the preceding concentration bound now imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \lVert D^{\mathrm B}\rVert
 &\le \frac{2B}{q^2}\delta_{nm}\sqrt{nm}\\
 &=O_P\!\left(Bq^{-2}\sqrt{n\log(en)}\right).
 \end{aligned}
\]

It remains to compare this expression with the declared lower/reference scale.  Because \(b_{nm}=o(1)\), the truncation in \(\mathrm{def:frontier\mbox{-}rate}\) is inactive eventually.  Hence
\[
 b_{nm}=\frac{B\sqrt{nm}}{\lambda_{nm}}\sqrt{\log(en)}
 K r^{3/2}\mu q^{-1/2}\sqrt{n^{-1}+nm^{-2}}.
\]
The aspect lower bound \(m\ge c_-n\) and \(K,r,\mu\ge1\) give
\[
 b_{nm}\ge
 \sqrt{c_-}\,\frac{B}{\lambda_{nm}}q^{-1/2}
 \sqrt{n\log(en)}.
\]
The parameters \(q,c_-,c_+\) are fixed along the asymptotic sequence.  Dividing the operator bound by the positive signal floor therefore gives
\[
 \frac{\lVert D^{\mathrm B}\rVert}{\lambda_{nm}}
 =O_P\!\left(\frac{Bq^{-2}\sqrt{n\log(en)}}{\lambda_{nm}}\right)
 =O_P(b_{nm})=o_P(1).
\]

For completeness, the singular-value perturbation used for the gap is elementary.  The min--max characterization and the triangle inequality imply, for every admissible \(k\),
\[
 |\sigma_k(C+Q)-\sigma_k(C)|\le\lVert Q\rVert.
\]
Apply this once to \(\sigma_r\) and once to \(\sigma_{r+1}\), with \(C=M^{\mathrm B}\) and \(Q=D^{\mathrm B}\).  On using the primitive oracle gap,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sigma_r(T^{\mathrm B})-\sigma_{r+1}(T^{\mathrm B})
 &\ge \sigma_r(M^{\mathrm B})-\sigma_{r+1}(M^{\mathrm B})
      -2\lVert D^{\mathrm B}\rVert\\
 &\ge c_{\mathrm B}\lambda_{nm}-2\lVert D^{\mathrm B}\rVert.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Because \(\lVert D^{\mathrm B}\rVert/\lambda_{nm}=o_P(1)\), the last member is at least \((c_{\mathrm B}/2)\lambda_{nm}\) with probability tending to one.  When \(r=\min(n,m)\), the declared convention sets the absent \((r+1)\)-st singular value to zero, and the same lower bound remains valid.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This derives the realized tangent gap from a primitive oracle gap and path concentration instead of assuming a property of the random normalized target. \textit{Gap.} Cai et al. perturb a fixed signal under independent entries, while Khan and Ugander linearize scalar ratios; neither transfers an oracle singular gap through a same-path adaptive row denominator. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies a checkable sufficient condition for stable blind SVD in archived experiments.

\begin{lemma}[lem:blind-ratio-expansion]\label{lem:blind-ratio-expansion}
Under overlap, bounded outcomes, bounded aspect ratio, propensity-path stability, the primitive oracle gap, and \(b_{nm}=o(1)\),
\[
 Z^{\mathrm B}=Z^{\bar p}+D^{\mathrm B}+R^{\mathrm B},\qquad
 E_P[Z^{\bar p}_{ij}\mid\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1}]=M^{\mathrm B}_{ij}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \lVert R^{\mathrm B}\rVert=O_P(Bq^{-3}\log(en)).
\]
Writing
\[
 a^{\mathrm B,loose}_{nm}=\sqrt{\frac n{\mu r}}\left[1\wedge
 \frac B{\lambda_{nm}}\left\{
 q^{-1}\sqrt{(n\vee m)\log(e(n+m))}+q^{-3}\log(en)
 \right\}\right],
\]
the blind fit obeys \(\mathcal L(\widehat U^{\mathrm B},U^{\mathrm B})=O_P(a^{\mathrm B,loose}_{nm})\).  The frozen assumptions do not imply the sharper claim that the residual contributes \(o_P(b_{nm})\) to maximum-row loss.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(t_i=\widehat p_i-\bar p_i\) and \(s_i=1-\bar p_i\).  By overlap and the definition of clipping,
\[
 \bar p_i,s_i\ge q,
 \quad
 \widehat p_i,1-\widehat p_i\ge q/2,
\]
so every division below is valid.  The exact reciprocal identities
\[
 \frac1{\bar p_i+t_i}
 =\frac1{\bar p_i}-\frac{t_i}{\bar p_i^2}
 +\frac{t_i^2}{\bar p_i^2(\bar p_i+t_i)}
\]
and
\[
 \frac1{s_i-t_i}
 =\frac1{s_i}+\frac{t_i}{s_i^2}
 +\frac{t_i^2}{s_i^2(s_i-t_i)}
\]
give, cell by cell,
\[
 Z^{\mathrm B}=Z^{\bar p}+D^{\mathrm B}+R^{\mathrm B},
\]
where
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 R^{\mathrm B}_{ij}
 &=-t_i\{Q_{ij}-H^{\mathrm B}_{ij}\}+t_i^2S_{ij},\\
 Q_{ij}
 &=\frac{A_{ij}Y_{ij}}{\bar p_i^2}
   +\frac{(1-A_{ij})Y_{ij}}{(1-\bar p_i)^2},\\
 S_{ij}
 &=\frac{A_{ij}Y_{ij}}{\bar p_i^2\widehat p_i}
   -\frac{(1-A_{ij})Y_{ij}}
   {(1-\bar p_i)^2(1-\widehat p_i)}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Indeed, the two linear reciprocal terms equal \(-t_iQ_{ij}\), while \(D^{\mathrm B}_{ij}=-t_iH^{\mathrm B}_{ij}\); subtracting the latter leaves the displayed centered-linear term.

Sequential randomization and consistency of the observed outcome give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_P[A_{ij}Y_{ij}\mid\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1}]
 &=p_{ij}\mu^1_{ij},\\
 E_P[(1-A_{ij})Y_{ij}\mid\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1}]
 &=(1-p_{ij})\mu^0_{ij}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Consequently
\[
 E_P[Q_{ij}\mid\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1}]=H^{\mathrm B}_{ij}
\]
and, separately,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_P[Z^{\bar p}_{ij}\mid\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1}]
 &=\frac{p_{ij}\mu^1_{ij}}{\bar p_i}
   -\frac{(1-p_{ij})\mu^0_{ij}}{1-\bar p_i}\\
 &=M^{\mathrm B}_{ij}.
 \end{aligned}
\]

We next bound the centered-linear matrix.  Bounded outcomes and the displayed denominator bounds imply
\[
 |Q_{ij}-H^{\mathrm B}_{ij}|\le C Bq^{-2},
 \quad
 E_P[(Q_{ij}-H^{\mathrm B}_{ij})^2
       \mid\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1}]\le C B^2q^{-4}.
\]
Apply the self-adjoint dilation to the row-major sequence of single-cell matrices.  Its predictable quadratic variation is block diagonal; the row block has diagonal entries bounded by \(CmB^2q^{-4}\), and the column block has diagonal entries bounded by \(CnB^2q^{-4}\).  Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:tropp\mbox{-}matrix\mbox{-}freedman}\), applied to both signs, therefore yields
\[
 \lVert Q-H^{\mathrm B}\rVert
 =O_P\!\left(
 Bq^{-2}\sqrt{(n\vee m)\log(e(n+m))}
 +Bq^{-2}\log(e(n+m))
 \right).
\]
Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:blind\mbox{-}tangent\mbox{-}control}\) gives
\[
 \max_i|t_i|=O_P\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log(en)}m}\right),
\]
and the denominator bounds give \(\max_{i,j}|S_{ij}|\le CBq^{-3}\).  Since left multiplication by \(\operatorname{diag}(t_i)\) has operator norm \(\max_i|t_i|\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \lVert R^{\mathrm B}\rVert
 &\le \max_i|t_i|\,\lVert Q-H^{\mathrm B}\rVert
 +CBq^{-3}\sqrt{nm}\max_i t_i^2\\
 &=O_P(Bq^{-3}\log(en)).
 \end{aligned}
\]
The last equality uses \(c_-\le m/n\le c_+\), which implies \(n\vee m\asymp m\) and \(\log(e(n+m))\asymp\log(en)\); it also uses \(q<1/2\), so a factor \(q^{-2}\) is absorbed by \(q^{-3}\).

Similarly, \(Z^{\bar p}_{ij}-M^{\mathrm B}_{ij}\) is a row-major matrix martingale difference with
\[
 |Z^{\bar p}_{ij}-M^{\mathrm B}_{ij}|\le CBq^{-1},
 \quad
 E_P[(Z^{\bar p}_{ij}-M^{\mathrm B}_{ij})^2
       \mid\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1}]\le CB^2q^{-2}.
\]
The same dilation and matrix-Freedman calculation gives
\[
 \lVert Z^{\bar p}-M^{\mathrm B}\rVert
 =O_P\!\left(
 Bq^{-1}\sqrt{(n\vee m)\log(e(n+m))}
 +Bq^{-1}\log(e(n+m))
 \right).
\]
Because \(T^{\mathrm B}=M^{\mathrm B}+D^{\mathrm B}\), the exact expansion cancels the tangent and gives
\[
 Z^{\mathrm B}-T^{\mathrm B}
 =(Z^{\bar p}-M^{\mathrm B})+R^{\mathrm B}.
\]
Thus, after absorbing the smaller linear logarithm into the \(q^{-3}\log(en)\) term,
\[
 \lVert Z^{\mathrm B}-T^{\mathrm B}\rVert
 =O_P\!\left(B\left\{
 q^{-1}\sqrt{(n\vee m)\log(e(n+m))}
 +q^{-3}\log(en)\right\}\right).
\]

We spell out the deterministic perturbation step because \(T^{\mathrm B}\) need not have rank \(r\).  Let a rectangular matrix \(S\) have a gap \(g=\sigma_r(S)-\sigma_{r+1}(S)>0\), and let \(\widetilde U\) and \(U_S\) be the leading left singular matrices of \(S+Q\) and \(S\).  Form the symmetric dilations \(\mathscr S=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&S\\S^\top&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) and \(\mathscr Q=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&Q\\Q^\top&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\).  Their operator norms satisfy \(\lVert\mathscr Q\rVert=\lVert Q\rVert\), and the selected positive eigenspace of \(\mathscr S\) is separated from its complement by \(g\).  If \(\lVert Q\rVert<g/2\), project the eigen-equation for \(\mathscr S+\mathscr Q\) onto the orthogonal complement of the selected eigenspace.  In an eigenbasis of \(\mathscr S\), every resulting coefficient is divided by a spectral difference at least \(g-\lVert Q\rVert\).  Hence
\[
 \lVert\sin\Theta(\widetilde U,U_S)\rVert
 \le \frac{\lVert Q\rVert}{g-\lVert Q\rVert}
 \le\frac{2\lVert Q\rVert}{g}.
\]
This coefficient calculation uses only the eigen-equation and the min--max bound \(|\widetilde\sigma_k-\sigma_k|\le\lVert Q\rVert\).  Aligning the two bases by the polar factor gives
\[
 \min_{R\in\mathbb O_r}\lVert\widetilde U R-U_S\rVert
 \le\sqrt2\lVert\sin\Theta(\widetilde U,U_S)\rVert.
\]
If \(\lVert Q\rVert\ge g/2\), the left side is at most two.  Since \(\lVert C\rVert_{2,\infty}\le\lVert C\rVert\), both cases yield
\[
 \mathcal L(\widetilde U,U_S)
 \le C\sqrt{\frac n{\mu r}}
 \left(1\wedge\frac{\lVert Q\rVert}{g}\right).
\]

By Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:blind\mbox{-}tangent\mbox{-}control}\), the gap of \(T^{\mathrm B}\) is at least \((c_{\mathrm B}/2)\lambda_{nm}\) with probability tending to one.  Apply the preceding deterministic inequality with \(S=T^{\mathrm B}\), \(Q=Z^{\mathrm B}-T^{\mathrm B}\), and \(U_S=U^{\mathrm B}\).  The fixed tie rule makes the leading left singular vectors of \(\mathcal P_r(Z^{\mathrm B})\) equal to the selected leading vectors of \(Z^{\mathrm B}\).  We obtain
\[
 \mathcal L(\widehat U^{\mathrm B},U^{\mathrm B})
 =O_P\!\left(
 \sqrt{\frac n{\mu r}}\left[1\wedge
 \frac B{\lambda_{nm}}\left\{
 q^{-1}\sqrt{(n\vee m)\log(e(n+m))}
 +q^{-3}\log(en)\right\}\right]\right),
\]
which is the asserted \(O_P(a^{\mathrm B,loose}_{nm})\) conclusion.

Finally, the claim about the residual is a genuine non-implication, not merely a limitation of the preceding upper bound.  In the deterministic perturbation inequality just proved, the operator contribution of the residual is
\[
 c^{R}_{nm}=\sqrt{\frac n{\mu r}}\frac{\lVert R^{\mathrm B}\rVert}{\lambda_{nm}}
\]
whenever the truncation is inactive.  Consider the square path \(m=n\), let \(r=\mu=K=1\), and fix \(q<1/2\).  At baseline draw independent Rademacher variables \(R_i\), set
\[
 \delta_n=c\sqrt{\frac{\log(en)}n},\qquad
 p_{ij}=\frac12+\delta_nR_i,\qquad
 Y_{ij}(1)=\frac B2,\qquad Y_{ij}(0)=-\frac B2,
\]
and, conditionally on the baseline, draw the assignments independently with these recorded propensities.  For all sufficiently large \(n\), overlap holds.  The effect matrix and oracle blind center both equal \(B\mathbf1_n\mathbf1_n^\top\), so exact rank, incoherence, condition number, signal floor with \(\lambda_{nn}=Bn\), and the primitive oracle gap all hold.  Moreover \(\bar p_i=1/2\), and
\[
 \max_i\left|\frac1n\sum_j(p_{ij}-E_Pp_{ij})\right|=\delta_n,
\]
so propensity-path stability holds.  The frontier formula gives \(b_{nn}\asymp\sqrt{\log(en)/n}=o(1)\).

Write \(X_{ij}=2A_{ij}-1\) and \(t_i=\widehat p_i-1/2\).  For a fixed row, conditional independence and boundedness give
\[
 E_P\!\left[\left\{\frac1{2n}\sum_j(X_{ij}-2\delta_nR_i)\right\}^2
 \mathrel{\Big|}R_i\right]\le\frac1{4n}.
\]
Markov's inequality therefore shows that clipping is inactive with probability tending to one and that
\[
 t_i=\delta_nR_i+O_P(n^{-1/2})
\]
for any fixed row.  Moreover, direct expansion and the same variance calculation give
\[
 \frac1n\sum_j(X_{ij}-2\delta_nR_i)^2
 =1-4\delta_nR_i\frac1n\sum_jX_{ij}+4\delta_n^2
 \longrightarrow1
\]
in probability.  Direct substitution in the exact residual formula above gives
\[
 Q_{ij}-H^{\mathrm B}_{ij}=2B(X_{ij}-2\delta_nR_i),
 \quad
 S_{ij}=2B\left\{\frac{A_{ij}}{\widehat p_i}
 +\frac{1-A_{ij}}{1-\widehat p_i}\right\}.
\]
On the clipping-inactive event \(\max_j|S_{ij}|\le C_qB\).  For the first row, the reverse triangle inequality therefore yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \lVert e_1^\top R^{\mathrm B}\rVert_2
 &\ge 2B|t_1|
 \left\{\sum_j(X_{1j}-2\delta_nR_1)^2\right\}^{1/2}
 -C_qBt_1^2\sqrt n\\
 &=(2c+o_P(1))B\sqrt{\log(en)}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Since the operator norm dominates every row Euclidean norm,
\[
 c^R_{nn}\ge(2c+o_P(1))\sqrt{\frac{\log(en)}n}\asymp b_{nn}.
\]
Thus the frozen assumptions do not imply that the residual's operator perturbation contribution to normalized maximum-row loss is \(o_P(b_{nm})\), as claimed.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:blind-iff]\label{thm:blind-iff}
Uniformly over sequences \(P\in\mathfrak P^{\mathrm{pred}}_{n,m}(r,\mu,K,q,B,\lambda)\) that satisfy propensity-path stability, the primitive oracle blind gap, and \(b_{nm}=o(1)\), define
\[
 d_{nm}=\sqrt{\frac n{\mu r}}\left[1\wedge
 \frac B{\lambda_{nm}}\left\{
 q^{-1}\sqrt{(n\vee m)\log(e(n+m))}+q^{-3}\log(en)
 \right\}\right].
\]
Then
\[
 \left|\mathcal L(\widehat U^{\mathrm B},U)-\rho_{nm}\right|=O_P(d_{nm}),
 \qquad
 |\widehat\rho_{nm}-\rho_{nm}|=O_P(d_{nm}).
\]
Thus the observed blind-versus-recorded discrepancy is certified at the explicit \(d_{nm}\) resolution; no \(b_{nm}\)-scale equivalence is asserted.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For \(Q_1,Q_2\in\mathbb O_{n,r}\), set
\[
 \delta(Q_1,Q_2)=\min_{R\in\mathbb O_r}
 \lVert Q_1R-Q_2\rVert_{2,\infty}.
\]
The minimum exists because \(\mathbb O_r\) is compact.  Right multiplication by an orthogonal matrix preserves every row Euclidean norm.  Consequently \(\delta\) is symmetric.  If \(R_{12}\) and \(R_{23}\) are minimizers for \((Q_1,Q_2)\) and \((Q_2,Q_3)\), respectively, then
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \delta(Q_1,Q_3)
 &\le\lVert Q_1R_{12}R_{23}-Q_3\rVert_{2,\infty}\\
 &\le\lVert(Q_1R_{12}-Q_2)R_{23}\rVert_{2,\infty}
 +\lVert Q_2R_{23}-Q_3\rVert_{2,\infty}\\
 &=\delta(Q_1,Q_2)+\delta(Q_2,Q_3).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Multiplication by the positive constant \(\sqrt{n/(\mu r)}\) proves the triangle and reverse-triangle inequalities for \(\mathcal L\).

Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:blind\mbox{-}ratio\mbox{-}expansion}\), under exactly the hypotheses in the statement, gives
\[
 \mathcal L(\widehat U^{\mathrm B},U^{\mathrm B})=O_P(d_{nm}).
\]
Let \(a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm}\) denote the radius in Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:ipw\mbox{-}rowwise\mbox{-}upper}\).  Since \(q<1/2\),
\(q^{-1/2}\le q^{-1}\).  Bounded aspect ratio also gives a constant \(C\), depending only on \(c_-\) and \(c_+\), for which
\(\log(e(n+m))\le C\log(en)\).  Hence the expression inside the truncation defining \(a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm}\) is at most a constant times the one defining \(d_{nm}\).  Because
\(1\wedge(Cx)\le(1\vee C)(1\wedge x)\) for \(x\ge0\),
\[
 a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm}=O(d_{nm}).
\]
The recorded-IPW theorem therefore implies
\[
 \mathcal L(\widehat U^{\mathrm I},U)=O_P(d_{nm}).
\]

By the reverse-triangle inequality and the definitions of \(\rho_{nm}\) and \(\widehat\rho_{nm}\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \left|\mathcal L(\widehat U^{\mathrm B},U)-\rho_{nm}\right|
 &\le\mathcal L(\widehat U^{\mathrm B},U^{\mathrm B}),\\
 |\widehat\rho_{nm}-\rho_{nm}|
 &=\left|\mathcal L(\widehat U^{\mathrm B},\widehat U^{\mathrm I})
 -\mathcal L(U^{\mathrm B},U)\right|\\
 &\le\mathcal L(\widehat U^{\mathrm B},U^{\mathrm B})
 +\mathcal L(\widehat U^{\mathrm I},U).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Substitution of the two stochastic bounds proves both conclusions.  No comparison between \(d_{nm}\) and \(b_{nm}\) is used.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The retained theorem calibrates the causal blind-fit error and the observable blind-versus-recorded discrepancy against total drift at the actually proved stochastic resolution. \textit{Gap.} The resolution is \(d_{nm}\).  With fixed complexity and \(b_{nm}=o(1)\), \(d_{nm}/b_{nm}\asymp\sqrt n\), so the previous conditional \(b_{nm}\)-scale iff is not advertised. \textit{Consumer.} HeartSteps-style analyses may use the discrepancy as a coarse warning statistic, with its \(d_{nm}\) resolution stated explicitly.

\begin{proposition}[prop:markov-witness]\label{prop:markov-witness}
Fix \(\gamma\in(c_-,c_+)\), \(q<1/4\), \(r=1\), \(\mu=K=1\), and \(0<\theta<h\) with \(h+\theta/2\le B\). Along \(n_\ell=2\ell\) and \(m_\ell=2\lceil\gamma\ell\rceil\), for all sufficiently large \(\ell\) the law \(W_\ell\) belongs to \(\mathfrak P^{\mathrm{pred}}_{n_\ell,m_\ell}(1,1,1,q,B,\theta\sqrt{n_\ell m_\ell})\), satisfies propensity-path stability, and satisfies the primitive oracle gap with \(c_{\mathrm B}=(h-\theta)/\theta\). It has \(b_{n_\ell m_\ell}\asymp\sqrt{\log n_\ell/n_\ell}\). Its marginal row propensity is \(\bar p_i=1/2\), and Markov concentration gives \(\max_i|\widehat p_i-1/2|=O_P(\sqrt{\log n_\ell/m_\ell})\). Consequently \(T^{\mathrm B}=h\mathbf1_{n_\ell}\mathbf1_{m_\ell}^\top+\theta((-1)^{i+j})_{ij}+o_P(\sqrt{n_\ell m_\ell})\) in operator norm, so \(\rho_{n_\ell m_\ell}\to2\), \(\widehat\rho_{n_\ell m_\ell}\to2\), \(\mathcal L(\widehat U^{\mathrm I},U)=O_P(b_{n_\ell m_\ell})\), and \(\mathcal L(\widehat U^{\mathrm B},U)\to2\). This is a boundary example for \(\mathrm{def:blind\mbox{-}svd}\), not a claim about any published normalization procedure.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write
\[
 s_{ij}=(-1)^{i+j},\qquad X_{i,j}=2A_{ij}-1,
 \qquad X_{i,0}=2A_i^{\mathrm{init}}-1.
\]
The construction gives
\[
 2p_{ij}-1=\frac12s_{ij}X_{i,j-1},
 \qquad
 E_P[X_{i,j}\mid\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1}]
 =\frac12s_{ij}X_{i,j-1}.
\]
Thus \(p_{ij}\in\{1/4,3/4\}\), and the assumption \(q<1/4\) verifies overlap.  Both potential outcomes at cell \((i,j)\) are measurable with respect to the pre-cell field, whereas the current assignment is drawn with the recorded probability \(p_{ij}\).  Conditioning additionally on those potential outcomes therefore leaves this probability unchanged, proving sequential randomization.  Also
\[
 |Y_{ij}(a)|\le h+\theta/2\le B,
\]
so bounded outcomes hold.  Direct subtraction gives
\[
 M_{ij}=\theta s_{ij}=\theta(-1)^i(-1)^j.
\]
Because \(n_\ell\) and \(m_\ell\) are even, the normalized alternating vectors
\[
 u_1=n_\ell^{-1/2}((-1)^i)_i,
 \qquad
 v_1=m_\ell^{-1/2}((-1)^j)_j
\]
are unit vectors, and \(M=\theta\sqrt{n_\ell m_\ell},u_1v_1^\top\).  Hence \(M\) has rank one, singular value \(\theta\sqrt{n_\ell m_\ell}\), left and right incoherence equal to one, and condition number one.  The signal floor holds with equality for \(\lambda_{n_\ell m_\ell}=\theta\sqrt{n_\ell m_\ell}\); the prescribed dimension path satisfies the aspect condition for all sufficiently large \(\ell\).

The baseline state is fair.  The recursion above preserves fairness, because \(E_PX_{i,0}=0\) and
\[
 E_PX_{i,j}=\frac12s_{ij}E_PX_{i,j-1}=0.
\]
It follows that \(E_Pp_{ij}=1/2\) and \(\bar p_i=1/2\).  With \(C_{ij}=hs_{ij}X_{i,j-1}\), direct algebra gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 M^{\mathrm B}_{ij}
 &=2\left[p_{ij}\left(C_{ij}+\frac\theta2s_{ij}\right)
 -(1-p_{ij})\left(C_{ij}-\frac\theta2s_{ij}\right)\right]\\
 &=2\left[(2p_{ij}-1)C_{ij}+\frac\theta2s_{ij}\right]\\
 &=h+\theta s_{ij}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Let \(u_0=n_\ell^{-1/2}\mathbf1_{n_\ell}\) and \(v_0=m_\ell^{-1/2}\mathbf1_{m_\ell}\).  Evenness makes \(u_0\perp u_1\) and \(v_0\perp v_1\), so
\[
 M^{\mathrm B}=h\sqrt{n_\ell m_\ell},u_0v_0^\top
 +\theta\sqrt{n_\ell m_\ell},u_1v_1^\top.
\]
Its two nonzero singular values are \(h\sqrt{n_\ell m_\ell}\) and \(\theta\sqrt{n_\ell m_\ell}\).  Since \(h>\theta\), the primitive oracle gap is
\[
 (h-\theta)\sqrt{n_\ell m_\ell}
 =\frac{h-\theta}{\theta}\lambda_{n_\ell m_\ell},
\]
which proves the asserted value of \(c_{\mathrm B}\).

We next verify both path concentration claims.  Put
\[
 \varepsilon_{i,j}=X_{i,j}-\frac12s_{ij}X_{i,j-1}.
\]
These are conditionally mean-zero variables with conditional range of length two.  Recursively substituting
\(X_{i,j}=2^{-1}s_{ij}X_{i,j-1}+\varepsilon_{i,j}\) expresses any deterministic linear form \(\sum_j a_jX_{i,j}\) as a martingale transform of \((\varepsilon_{i,j})_j\) plus a bounded multiple of \(X_{i,0}\).  The transform coefficients are obtained by convolution with a kernel whose absolute values are \((1,2^{-1},2^{-2},\ldots)\).  The triangle inequality in \(\ell_2\), applied to the shifted copies of \(a\), gives directly
\[
 \lVert d\rVert_2
 \le\sum_{k\ge0}2^{-k}\lVert a\rVert_2
 =2\lVert a\rVert_2.
\]
Applying the same conditional exponential calculation used in the tangent-control proof at each innovation yields
\[
 E_P\exp\!\left(t\sum_ja_jX_{i,j}\right)
 \le \exp(Ct^2\lVert a\rVert_2^2)
\]
after enlarging \(C\) to absorb the bounded initial term.  The same bound applies when \(X_{i,j}\) is replaced by \(s_{ij}X_{i,j-1}\).

Take \(a_j=m_\ell^{-1}\), use
\[
 p_{ij}-E_Pp_{ij}=\frac14s_{ij}X_{i,j-1},
 \qquad
 \frac1{m_\ell}\sum_jA_{ij}-\frac12
 =\frac1{2m_\ell}\sum_jX_{i,j},
\]
and apply a union bound over the \(n_\ell\) rows.  We obtain
\[
 \max_i\left|\frac1{m_\ell}\sum_j(p_{ij}-E_Pp_{ij})\right|
 =O_P\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log n_\ell}{m_\ell}}\right)
\]
and, because projection onto the clipping interval is one-Lipschitz and fixes \(1/2\),
\[
 \max_i|\widehat p_i-1/2|
 =O_P\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log n_\ell}{m_\ell}}\right).
\]
This proves propensity-path stability and the asserted Markov concentration.

Substitution into the tangent gives the useful exact identity
\[
 H^{\mathrm B}_{ij}=X_{i,j-1}(4hs_{ij}+\theta).
\]
Thus, with \(\delta_\ell=\max_i|\widehat p_i-1/2|\),
\[
 \lVert D^{\mathrm B}\rVert
 \le \lVert D^{\mathrm B}\rVert_F
 \le C B\delta_\ell\sqrt{n_\ell m_\ell}
 =O_P(B\sqrt{n_\ell\log n_\ell}).
\]
Bounded aspect ratio makes the last expression \(o_P(\sqrt{n_\ell m_\ell})\).  Therefore
\[
 T^{\mathrm B}
 =h\mathbf1_{n_\ell}\mathbf1_{m_\ell}^\top
 +\theta(s_{ij})_{ij}+o_P(\sqrt{n_\ell m_\ell})
\]
in operator norm.

We now prove the rowwise conclusions.  We first record an elementary deterministic calculation specialized to the two matrices used here.  Suppose
\[
 S=s_0u_0v_0^\top+s_1u_1v_1^\top,
 \qquad s_0>s_1\ge0,
\]
where the second term may be absent, and let \((\widehat s,\widehat u,\widehat v)\) be the leading singular triple of \(S+Q\).  Put \(g=s_0-s_1\).  The symmetric-dilation eigen-equation, projected orthogonally to \((u_0^\top,v_0^\top)^\top/\sqrt2\), gives, whenever \(\lVert Q\rVert<g/2\),
\[
 \lVert\widehat u-u_0\rVert_2+
 \lVert\widehat v-v_0\rVert_2
 \le C\frac{\lVert Q\rVert}{g}
\]
after a common sign alignment.  Indeed, the inverse on that orthogonal complement has norm at most \((g-\lVert Q\rVert)^{-1}\), by the singular-value min--max inequality.  Expanding the row singular equation as
\[
 \widehat s(\widehat u-u_0)
 =u_0(s_0v_0^\top\widehat v-\widehat s)
 +s_1u_1v_1^\top\widehat v+Qv_0+Q(\widehat v-v_0)
\]
and using \(|\widehat s-s_0|\le\lVert Q\rVert\) consequently yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \lVert\widehat u-u_0\rVert_\infty
 \le C\bigg\{&\frac{\lVert Qv_0\rVert_\infty}{s_0}
 +(\lVert u_0\rVert_\infty+\lVert u_1\rVert_\infty)
   \frac{\lVert Q\rVert}{g}\\
 &+\frac{\max_i\lVert e_i^\top Q\rVert_2}{s_0}
   \frac{\lVert Q\rVert}{g}\bigg\}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
When the second spike is absent, its row-norm term is omitted.  Every denominator is positive on the displayed event; outside it we will use only statements for which \(\lVert Q\rVert/g=o_P(1)\).

For the recorded score let \(E=Z^{\mathrm{ipw}}-M\).  The conditional assignment calculation already used above gives
\[
 E_P[Z^{\mathrm{ipw}}_{ij}\mid\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1}]
 =\mu^1_{ij}-\mu^0_{ij}=M_{ij},
\]
so its row-major cells are martingale differences; here \(|E_{ij}|\le C B\) because the only propensities are \(1/4\) and \(3/4\).  For a fixed row, the scalar variables \(E_{ij}(v_1)_j\) are martingale differences bounded by \(CB/\sqrt{m_\ell}\), with total conditional variance at most \(CB^2\).  Conditional exponential iteration and a union bound over rows give
\[
 \lVert Ev_1\rVert_\infty=O_P(B\sqrt{\log n_\ell}).
\]
For deterministic unit vectors \(x\in\mathbb R^{n_\ell}\) and \(y\in\mathbb R^{m_\ell}\), the same iteration applied in row-major order gives
\[
 P\{|x^\top Ey|>t\}\le2\exp(-ct^2/B^2),
\]
because \(\sum_{i,j}x_i^2y_j^2=1\).  Choose maximal \(1/4\)-separated subsets of the two unit spheres.  Maximality makes them \(1/4\)-nets.  Comparing the disjoint radius-\(1/8\) Euclidean balls around their points with the radius-\(9/8\) ball containing them proves cardinalities at most \(9^{n_\ell}\) and \(9^{m_\ell}\).  Approximating arbitrary unit vectors by net points and moving the two approximation errors to the left gives
\[
 \lVert E\rVert
 \le2\max_{x,y\ \mathrm{in\ the\ product\ net}}|x^\top Ey|.
\]
A union bound therefore gives
\[
 \lVert E\rVert=O_P(B\sqrt{n_\ell+m_\ell}),
\]
while boundedness gives \(\max_i\lVert e_i^\top E\rVert_2\le CB\sqrt{m_\ell}\).  Apply the deterministic row calculation with \(s_0=g=\theta\sqrt{n_\ell m_\ell}\).  Bounded aspect ratio yields
\[
 \sqrt{n_\ell}\min_{\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}}
 \lVert\epsilon\widehat U^{\mathrm I}-u_1\rVert_\infty
 =O_P\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log n_\ell}{n_\ell}}\right).
\]
Substitution into the frontier definition gives
\[
 b_{n_\ell m_\ell}
 =1\wedge\left\{
 \frac B\theta\sqrt{\log(en_\ell)}\,
 q^{-1/2}\sqrt{n_\ell^{-1}+n_\ell m_\ell^{-2}}
 \right\}
 \asymp\sqrt{\frac{\log n_\ell}{n_\ell}},
\]
so \(\mathcal L(\widehat U^{\mathrm I},U)=O_P(b_{n_\ell m_\ell})\).

For the blind score, the ratio expansion gives
\[
 Z^{\mathrm B}-M^{\mathrm B}
 =(Z^{\bar p}-M^{\mathrm B})+D^{\mathrm B}+R^{\mathrm B}.
\]
The first summand is a bounded matrix martingale difference array.  Repeating the bilinear-form exponential iteration and product-net argument from the recorded score, and then using the tangent bound above and Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:blind\mbox{-}ratio\mbox{-}expansion}\), gives
\[
 \lVert Z^{\mathrm B}-M^{\mathrm B}\rVert
 =O_P(B\sqrt{n_\ell}+B\sqrt{n_\ell\log n_\ell}+B\log n_\ell)
 =O_P(B\sqrt{n_\ell\log n_\ell}).
\]
For the constant vector \(v_0\), scalar martingale concentration gives
\(\lVert(Z^{\bar p}-M^{\mathrm B})v_0\rVert_\infty
=O_P(B\sqrt{\log n_\ell})\).  The entrywise residual formula in the ratio-expansion proof, together with \(\delta_\ell=O_P(\sqrt{\log n_\ell/m_\ell})\), gives
\[
 \max_i\lVert e_i^\top D^{\mathrm B}\rVert_2
 +\max_i\lVert e_i^\top R^{\mathrm B}\rVert_2
 =O_P(B\sqrt{\log n_\ell}).
\]
Hence
\[
 \lVert(Z^{\mathrm B}-M^{\mathrm B})v_0\rVert_\infty
 =O_P(B\sqrt{\log n_\ell}),
 \qquad
 \max_i\lVert e_i^\top(Z^{\mathrm B}-M^{\mathrm B})\rVert_2
 =O_P(B\sqrt{m_\ell}).
\]
Apply the deterministic row calculation with
\[
 s_0=h\sqrt{n_\ell m_\ell},\quad
 s_1=\theta\sqrt{n_\ell m_\ell},\quad
 g=(h-\theta)\sqrt{n_\ell m_\ell}.
\]
Since \(h-\theta>0\) is fixed, all three rowwise terms vanish after multiplication by \(\sqrt{n_\ell}\), and in fact
\[
 \sqrt{n_\ell}\min_{\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}}
 \lVert\epsilon\widehat U^{\mathrm B}-u_0\rVert_\infty
 =O_P\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log n_\ell}{n_\ell}}\right)=o_P(1).
\]
The identical calculation with \(Q=D^{\mathrm B}\) shows
\[
 \sqrt{n_\ell}\min_{\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}}
 \lVert\epsilon U^{\mathrm B}-u_0\rVert_\infty=o_P(1).
\]

Finally, the normalized quotient distance between \(u_0\) and \(u_1\) is exactly two.  For either global sign, half of the even number of rows differ by \(2/\sqrt{n_\ell}\), so
\[
 \sqrt{n_\ell}\min_{\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}}
 \lVert\epsilon u_0-u_1\rVert_\infty=2.
\]
The quotient maximum-row distance obeys the triangle and reverse-triangle inequalities: compose the two optimizing signs and use the ordinary \(\ell_\infty\) triangle inequality.  Combining the last display with the three rowwise approximations proves
\[
 \rho_{n_\ell m_\ell}\longrightarrow2,
 \qquad
 \widehat\rho_{n_\ell m_\ell}\longrightarrow2,
 \qquad
 \mathcal L(\widehat U^{\mathrm B},U)\longrightarrow2
\]
in probability.  All assertions are about the explicitly defined blind procedure and the displayed witness law; no property of a published normalization method is used.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:simultaneous-bands]\label{thm:simultaneous-bands}
Let \(N=nm\) and \(d=|\mathcal G_n|\).  Under the predictable experiment class, polynomial family growth, and deterministic dense average-scale loadings, define
\[
 R_{g,\alpha}=2B\sqrt{\frac{\lVert g\rVert_F^2}{q}\log\!\frac{2d}{\alpha}}
 +\frac{2BC_\infty}{qN}\log\!\frac{2d}{\alpha}.
\]
Then, for every finite \(n,m\),
\[
 P\!\left(\tau_g\in[\widehat\tau_g-R_{g,\alpha},
 \widehat\tau_g+R_{g,\alpha}]\text{ for every }g\in\mathcal G_n\right)
 \ge1-\alpha.
\]
Uniformly in \(g\),
\[
 R_{g,\alpha}=O\!\left(
 B\sqrt{\frac{\log(2|\mathcal G_n|/\alpha)}{nmq}}
 +\frac{B\log(2|\mathcal G_n|/\alpha)}{qnm}\right).
\]
Thus the simultaneous stochastic radius has the separate \(\sqrt{\log|\mathcal G_n|}\) inflation and this factor is not part of \(b_{nm}\).  This conservative finite-sample result makes no claim that the frozen leave-one-cell-out multiplier critical value is asymptotically exact.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(N=nm\) and \(d=|\mathcal G_n|\), fix \(P\in\mathfrak P^{\mathrm{pred}}_{n,m}(r,\mu,K,q,B,\lambda)\), and fix a deterministic \(g\in\mathcal G_n\).  By \(\mathrm{def:contrast\mbox{-}process}\), the definition of \(\tau_g\), and \(\mathrm{def:ipw\mbox{-}score}\),
\[
 \widehat\tau_g-\tau_g
 =\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{j\in J}g_{ij}
   \bigl(Z^{\mathrm{ipw}}_{ij}-M_{ij}\bigr)
 =\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{j\in J}g_{ij}E_{ij}.
\]
For the cell \((i,j)\), write \(\mathcal H_{ij}=\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1}\).  The loading \(g\) is deterministic, while \(\mathrm{lem:ipw\mbox{-}identification}\) gives
\[
 E_P[g_{ij}E_{ij}\mid\mathcal H_{ij}]
 =g_{ij}E_P[E_{ij}\mid\mathcal H_{ij}]=0.
\]
Consequently, in the row-major order from \(\mathrm{def:global\mbox{-}filtration}\), the summands \(g_{ij}E_{ij}\) form a scalar martingale-difference sequence.

We next bound its predictable quadratic variation and increments.  The observed-outcome identity gives
\[
 Z^{\mathrm{ipw}}_{ij}
 =\frac{A_{ij}Y_{ij}(1)}{p_{ij}}
  -\frac{(1-A_{ij})Y_{ij}(0)}{1-p_{ij}}.
\]
The denominators are strictly positive by \(\mathrm{ass:overlap}\), and \(p_{ij}\) is \(\mathcal H_{ij}\)-measurable by the definition of the recorded propensity.  Since \(A_{ij}(1-A_{ij})=0\), iterated conditional expectation and \(\mathrm{ass:sequential\mbox{-}randomization}\) yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
 E_P\!\left[(Z^{\mathrm{ipw}}_{ij})^2\mid\mathcal H_{ij}\right]
 &=E_P\!\left[
   \frac{A_{ij}Y_{ij}(1)^2}{p_{ij}^2}
   +\frac{(1-A_{ij})Y_{ij}(0)^2}{(1-p_{ij})^2}
   \mathrel{\Big|}\mathcal H_{ij}\right]\\
 &=E_P\!\left[
   \frac{Y_{ij}(1)^2}{p_{ij}}
   +\frac{Y_{ij}(0)^2}{1-p_{ij}}
   \mathrel{\Big|}\mathcal H_{ij}\right].
\end{aligned}
\]
Indeed, the first term in the first conditional expectation can be conditioned once more on \(\sigma(\mathcal H_{ij},Y_{ij}(0),Y_{ij}(1))\), on which \(\mathrm{ass:sequential\mbox{-}randomization}\) makes the conditional mean of \(A_{ij}\) equal to \(p_{ij}\); the second term is identical with \(1-A_{ij}\) and \(1-p_{ij}\).

The variable \(M_{ij}=E_P[Z^{\mathrm{ipw}}_{ij}\mid\mathcal H_{ij}]\) is \(\mathcal H_{ij}\)-measurable by \(\mathrm{lem:ipw\mbox{-}identification}\).  Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
 E_P[E_{ij}^2\mid\mathcal H_{ij}]
 &=E_P[(Z^{\mathrm{ipw}}_{ij})^2\mid\mathcal H_{ij}]-M_{ij}^2\\
 &\le E_P[(Z^{\mathrm{ipw}}_{ij})^2\mid\mathcal H_{ij}]\\
 &\le \frac{2B^2}{q}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last line uses \(\mathrm{ass:bounded\mbox{-}outcomes}\) and both inequalities in \(\mathrm{ass:overlap}\).  The same assumptions give
\[
 |Z^{\mathrm{ipw}}_{ij}|\le \frac{B}{q}
\]
and, from the definition \(M_{ij}=\mu^1_{ij}-\mu^0_{ij}\) and conditional Jensen's inequality,
\[
 |M_{ij}|
 \le E_P[|Y_{ij}(1)|\mid\mathcal H_{ij}]
     +E_P[|Y_{ij}(0)|\mid\mathcal H_{ij}]
 \le 2B.
\]
Because \(q<1\), it follows that
\[
 |g_{ij}E_{ij}|
 \le |g_{ij}|\left(\frac{B}{q}+2B\right)
 \le \frac{3B}{q}|g_{ij}|
 \le R_g:=\frac{3BC_\infty}{qN},
\]
where the final inequality is \(\mathrm{ass:dense\mbox{-}loadings}\).  Moreover, for every row-major partial sum ending at step \(k\),
\[
 \sum_{o(i,j)\le k}E_P[g_{ij}^2E_{ij}^2\mid\mathcal H_{ij}]
 \le \sigma_g^2:=\frac{2B^2}{q}\lVert g\rVert_F^2.
\]
The lower bound in \(\mathrm{ass:average\mbox{-}loadings}\), together with \(B>0\) and \(q>0\), ensures \(\sigma_g^2>0\).

Apply \(\mathrm{lem:tropp\mbox{-}matrix\mbox{-}freedman}\) to the \(1\)-dimensional self-adjoint martingale of partial sums and then to its negative.  The deterministic bounds just obtained verify respectively its predictable-variation and increment hypotheses.  A union bound over the two signs gives, for every \(t\ge0\),
\[
 P\!\left(\left|\widehat\tau_g-\tau_g\right|\ge t\right)
 \le 2\exp\!\left\{-\frac{t^2}{2(\sigma_g^2+R_gt/3)}\right\}.
\]
For \(x>0\), define
\[
 t_g(x)=\sqrt{2\sigma_g^2x}+\frac{2R_gx}{3}.
\]
A direct expansion shows
\[
 t_g(x)^2-2x\{\sigma_g^2+R_gt_g(x)/3\}
 =\frac{2R_gx}{3}\sqrt{2\sigma_g^2x}\ge0.
\]
Thus the preceding tail inequality and the displayed values of \(\sigma_g^2\) and \(R_g\) imply
\[
 P\!\left(
 |\widehat\tau_g-\tau_g|>
 2B\sqrt{\frac{\lVert g\rVert_F^2}{q}x}
 +\frac{2BC_\infty}{qN}x
 \right)\le2e^{-x}.
\]

Take \(x=\log(2d/\alpha)>0\).  Positivity follows from \(d\ge2\) in \(\mathrm{ass:polynomial\mbox{-}family}\) and \(\alpha\in(0,1)\).  A union bound over the deterministic family \(\mathcal G_n\) makes the probability of any failure at most
\[
 2d\exp\{-\log(2d/\alpha)\}=\alpha.
\]
On the complementary event, \(|\widehat\tau_g-\tau_g|\le R_{g,\alpha}\) simultaneously for every \(g\in\mathcal G_n\), which is the asserted finite-sample inclusion.

Finally, \(\mathrm{ass:dense\mbox{-}loadings}\) implies uniformly in \(g\) that
\[
 \lVert g\rVert_F^2
 \le N\left(\frac{C_\infty}{N}\right)^2
 =\frac{C_\infty^2}{N}.
\]
Substitution into \(R_{g,\alpha}\), with \(N=nm\), proves
\[
 R_{g,\alpha}
 =O\!\left(
 B\sqrt{\frac{\log(2|\mathcal G_n|/\alpha)}{nmq}}
 +\frac{B\log(2|\mathcal G_n|/\alpha)}{qnm}
 \right)
\]
uniformly in \(g\), with constants depending only on \(C_\infty\).  The family size enters the leading stochastic term through the separate factor \(\sqrt{\log|\mathcal G_n|}\); the upper bound \(|\mathcal G_n|\le n^\kappa\) in \(\mathrm{ass:polynomial\mbox{-}family}\) makes this at most order \(\sqrt{\log n}\).  By \(\mathrm{def:frontier\mbox{-}rate}\), \(b_{nm}\) contains no dependence on \(\mathcal G_n\), so this entropy multiplier is not absorbed into that lower/reference scale.  No step uses \(\widehat s_g\), its positivity or consistency, or the multiplier critical value \(c_{1-\alpha}\).  Hence the proved bands are the stated conservative direct-IPW bands and do not assert feasible multiplier exactness.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:adaptive-endogenous-target-obstruction]\label{prop:adaptive-endogenous-target-obstruction}
On the even square path \(m=n\), fix \(r=1\), \(\mu=2\), \(K=1\), \(q<1/2\), and \(\lambda_{nn}=cBn\) for a sufficiently small fixed \(c>0\).  There is a sequence in \(\mathfrak P^{\mathrm{pred}}_{n,n}(1,2,1,q,B,\lambda)\) with constant recorded propensity \(p_{ij}=1/2\) and \(b_{nn}\to0\), but
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}P\{\mathcal L(\widehat U^{\mathrm I},U)>c_0\}>0
\]
for a fixed \(c_0>0\).  Thus sequential randomization alone does not make the future singular factors predictable with respect to earlier score increments, and the \(O_P(b_{nm})\) IPW--SVD conclusion is false on the frozen predictable class.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Restrict attention to even integers \(n\) tending to infinity and put \(m=n\), \(r=1\), \(\mu=2\), \(K=1\), and \(\lambda_{nn}=cBn\), where \(0<c\le 1/2\) is fixed.  Let \(C=B/4\).  We construct one law for each such \(n\).  All assignments have support \(\{0,1\}\), are mutually independent, and satisfy \(A_{ij}\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\).  Thus the recorded propensities are \(p_{ij}=1/2\), which obey the overlap inequalities \(q\le p_{ij}\le1-q\) because \(q<1/2\).

In the first row set
\[
Y_{1j}(1)=Y_{1j}(0)=C,
\qquad
S_j=2A_{1j}-1.
\]
After the first row has been recorded, define
\[
v=n^{-1/2}(S_1,\ldots,S_n)^\top,
\qquad
u=(0,(n-1)^{-1/2},\ldots,(n-1)^{-1/2})^\top.
\]
For every \(i\ge2\), set
\[
Y_{ij}(0)=0,
\qquad
Y_{ij}(1)=\lambda_{nn}u_iv_j.
\]
These later potential outcomes are measurable with respect to \(\mathbb G_n\), hence with respect to every pre-cell field \(\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1}\) for \(i\ge2\), by the row-major filtration definition.  At a first-row cell the two current potential outcomes are constant, and at every later cell they are functions only of assignments already recorded.  Since the current assignment is an independent fair coin conditional on the pre-cell field, conditioning additionally on the current potential outcomes does not change its law.  Therefore the sequential-randomization assumption holds with \(p_{ij}=1/2\).

The bounded-outcome assumption also holds.  The first-row magnitude is \(C\le B\), the control outcomes below the first row vanish, and, for \(i\ge2\),
\[
|Y_{ij}(1)|
=\frac{cBn}{\sqrt{n(n-1)}}
=cB\sqrt{\frac n{n-1}}
\le B,
\]
where the last inequality follows from \(c\le1/2\) and \(n\ge2\).  The predictable conditional means are \(\mu^1_{1j}=\mu^0_{1j}=C\), while for \(i\ge2\) they are \(\mu^0_{ij}=0\) and \(\mu^1_{ij}=\lambda_{nn}u_iv_j\).  Consequently, by the definition \(M=\mu^1-\mu^0\),
\[
M=\lambda_{nn}uv^\top.
\]
Both \(u\) and \(v\) have Euclidean norm one.  Thus \(M\) has exact rank one, its sole nonzero singular value equals \(\lambda_{nn}\), and its condition number is one.  Moreover,
\[
\lVert u\rVert_\infty=(n-1)^{-1/2}\le\sqrt{2/n},
\qquad
\lVert v\rVert_\infty=n^{-1/2}\le\sqrt{2/n}.
\]
Hence the left- and right-incoherence conditions hold with \(\mu=2\), the signal-floor condition holds with equality, and the aspect-ratio condition holds because \(m/n=1\) and \(c_-<1<c_+\).  We have therefore verified every member property in the definition of \(\mathfrak P^{\mathrm{pred}}_{n,n}(1,2,1,q,B,\lambda)\).

It remains to analyze the estimator.  By the recorded-score definition, the first score row is
\[
Z^{\mathrm{ipw}}_{1j}=2CS_j=a_nv_j,
\qquad
a_n=2C\sqrt n.
\]
For \(i\ge2\), let \(\xi_{ij}=2A_{ij}-1\).  Since the control outcome is zero,
\[
Z^{\mathrm{ipw}}_{ij}=2A_{ij}M_{ij}=M_{ij}+\xi_{ij}M_{ij}.
\]
It follows that
\[
Z^{\mathrm{ipw}}=(\lambda_{nn}u+a_ne_1)v^\top+F,
\]
where \(F_{1j}=0\) and \(F_{ij}=\xi_{ij}\lambda_{nn}u_iv_j\) for \(i\ge2\).  Conditional on the first row, the variables \((\xi_{ij}:i\ge2,1\le j\le n)\) are independent Rademacher variables and \(u,v\) are fixed.

We next prove the required operator-norm control rather than assuming it.  For deterministic unit vectors \(x,y\in\mathbb R^n\), conditional independence and the Rademacher exponential bound give
\[
P\!\left(\left.|x^\top Fy|>t\,\right|\mathbb G_n\right)
\le
2\exp\!\left{-\frac{t^2}{2\sum_{i=2}^n\sum_{j=1}^n
\lambda_{nn}^2u_i^2v_j^2x_i^2y_j^2}\right}
\le
2\exp\!\left{-\frac{n(n-1)t^2}{2\lambda_{nn}^2}\right}.
\]
Indeed, the coefficient-square sum in the denominator is at most
\[
\frac{\lambda_{nn}^2}{n(n-1)}
\left(\sum_{i=2}^n x_i^2\right)
\left(\sum_{j=1}^n y_j^2\right)
\le\frac{\lambda_{nn}^2}{n(n-1)}.
\]
A maximal \(1/4\)-separated subset of the Euclidean unit sphere is a \(1/4\)-net and has cardinality at most \(9^n\), by comparing the disjoint radius-\(1/8\) balls around its points with the radius-\(9/8\) ball.  If \(\mathcal N\) is such a net, two applications of the triangle inequality yield
\[
\lVert F\rVert\le2\max_{x,y\in\mathcal N}|x^\top Fy|.
\]
Therefore, for every fixed \(D>0\), the preceding tail bound and a union bound imply
\[
P\!\left(\left.\lVert F\rVert>D\frac{\lambda_{nn}}{\sqrt n}\,\right|\mathbb G_n\right)
\le
2\exp\!\left\{2n\log 9-\frac{D^2(n-1)}8\right\}.
\]
Choosing \(D\) so that \(D^2/8>2\log9\) and then integrating the conditional probability proves
\[
\delta_n:=\lVert F\rVert=O_P(\lambda_{nn}/\sqrt n)=o_P(\lambda_{nn}).
\]

Put \(w_n=\lambda_{nn}u+a_ne_1\) and \(s_n=\lVert w_n\rVert_2=(\lambda_{nn}^2+a_n^2)^{1/2}\), where the equality uses \(u_1=0\).  Let \((\widehat u,\widehat v,\widehat\sigma)\) be a leading singular triple of \(Z^{\mathrm{ipw}}\), reorienting both singular vectors so that \(v^\top\widehat v\ge0\).  This reorientation does not change the reported one-dimensional singular subspace or the loss.  The variational definition of the operator norm and the triangle inequality give
\[
\widehat\sigma
\ge\lVert Z^{\mathrm{ipw}}v\rVert_2
\ge s_n-\delta_n,
\qquad
\widehat\sigma
\le s_n+\delta_n.
\]
On the other hand,
\[
\widehat\sigma
=\lVert Z^{\mathrm{ipw}}\widehat v\rVert_2
\le s_n|v^\top\widehat v|+\delta_n.
\]
Combining these inequalities yields
\[
v^\top\widehat v\ge1-2\delta_n/s_n\longrightarrow1
\quad\text{in probability}.
\]
Also \(a_n/\lambda_{nn}=2C/(cB\sqrt n)\to0\), so \(s_n/\lambda_{nn}\to1\), and the two-sided bound on \(\widehat\sigma\) gives
\[
\widehat\sigma/\lambda_{nn}\longrightarrow1
\quad\text{in probability}.
\]

The first row of \(F\) is identically zero.  Hence the first coordinate of the singular-vector equation \(Z^{\mathrm{ipw}}\widehat v=\widehat\sigma\widehat u\) is the exact identity
\[
\widehat\sigma\widehat u_1=a_n v^\top\widehat v.
\]
Because the target vector has \(u_1=0\), the preceding limits imply
\[
\sqrt n\,|\widehat u_1-u_1|
=\frac{\sqrt n\,a_n}{\widehat\sigma}|v^\top\widehat v|
\longrightarrow\frac{2C}{cB}
\quad\text{in probability}.
\]
For rank one, the orthogonal alignment set is \(\mathbb O_1=\{-1,1\}\).  Since \(u_1=0\), either alignment has first-row error \(|\widehat u_1|\).  The rowwise-loss definition therefore gives
\[
\mathcal L(\widehat U^{\mathrm I},U)
\ge Q_n,
\qquad
Q_n:=\sqrt{n/2}\,|\widehat u_1|
\longrightarrow d:=\frac{\sqrt2 C}{cB}>0
\quad\text{in probability}.
\]
Taking \(c_0=d/2\), the inclusion \(\{Q_n>c_0\}\subseteq\{\mathcal L(\widehat U^{\mathrm I},U)>c_0\}\) yields
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}P\{\mathcal L(\widehat U^{\mathrm I},U)>c_0\}=1>0.
\]

Finally, the geometric-term and reference-scale definitions give, on this square rank-one path,
\[
b^0_{nn}=2q^{-1/2}\sqrt{2/n}
\]
and hence
\[
b_{nn}
=1\wedge\left\{
\frac{Bn}{cBn}\sqrt{\log(en)}\,
2q^{-1/2}\sqrt{2/n}
\right\}
=1\wedge\left\{\frac{2\sqrt2}{c\sqrt q}
\sqrt{\frac{\log(en)}n}\right\}
\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus the same law sequence belongs to the frozen predictable class, has vanishing lower/reference scale, and gives a nonvanishing normalized maximum-row error for recorded-propensity IPW--SVD.  The obstruction is specifically the lack of predictability of the future singular factors relative to earlier score increments; it does not assert a lower bound for every estimator.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:ipw-expected-risk-upper]\label{prop:ipw-expected-risk-upper}
Put
\[
 a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm}=
 \sqrt{\frac n{\mu r}}\left[1\wedge
 \frac B{\lambda_{nm}}\left\{
 \sqrt{\frac{(n\vee m)\log(e(n+m))}{q}}+
 \frac{\log(e(n+m))}{q}\right\}\right].
\]
There is a universal constant \(C<\infty\) such that, for every \(n,m\ge2\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P^{\mathrm{pred}}_{n,m}(r,\mu,K,q,B,\lambda)}

 \le C a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm}.
\]
Thus the loose IPW--SVD upper bound holds in the same expected normalized maximum-row loss used by the minimax lower benchmark.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(P\in\mathfrak P^{\mathrm{pred}}_{n,m}(r,\mu,K,q,B,\lambda)\) and expose cells in the row-major order from \(\mathrm{def:global\mbox{-}filtration}\).  Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:ipw\mbox{-}identification}\) gives
\[
 E_P[E_{ij}\mid\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1}]=0.
 \tag{20}
\]
Sequential randomization, iterated conditional expectation, bounded outcomes, and overlap give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_P[(Z^{\mathrm{ipw}}_{ij})^2\mid\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1}]
 &=E_P\!\left[
 \frac{Y_{ij}(1)^2}{p_{ij}}+
 \frac{Y_{ij}(0)^2}{1-p_{ij}}
 \mathrel{\Big|}\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1}\right]\\
 &\le\frac{2B^2}{q}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{21}
\]
Both divisions are valid by overlap.  Since \(M_{ij}\) is the conditional mean of the score, conditional variance is at most (21).  Also \(|Z^{\mathrm{ipw}}_{ij}|\le B/q\) and \(|M_{ij}|\le2B\); because \(q<1/2\),
\[
 |E_{ij}|\le2B/q.
 \tag{22}
\]

Introduce the self-adjoint dilation
\[
 \Delta_{ij}=\begin{pmatrix}
 0&E_{ij}e_ie_j^\top\\ E_{ij}e_je_i^\top&0
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its norm is \(|E_{ij}|\), and its square has diagonal blocks \(E_{ij}^2e_ie_i^\top\) and \(E_{ij}^2e_je_j^\top\).  Thus (21) implies
\[
 \left\|\sum_{i,j}E_P[\Delta_{ij}^2\mid\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1}]\right\|
 \le v_{nm}:=\frac{2B^2(n\vee m)}q.
 \tag{23}
\]
Apply Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:tropp\mbox{-}matrix\mbox{-}freedman}\) to both signs of the \((n+m)\)-dimensional dilation martingale, using (20), (22), and (23).  Since the terminal dilation has norm \(\|E\|\),
\[
 P\{\|E\|\ge t\}
 \le2(n+m)\exp\!\left\{-\frac{t^2/2}{v_{nm}+R_{nm}t/3}\right\},
 \qquad R_{nm}=2B/q.
 \tag{24}
\]
With \(L_{nm}=\log(2(n+m))\), solving the quadratic inequality in (24) gives, for \(x\ge0\),
\[
 P\!\left\{\|E\|>
 \sqrt{2v_{nm}(L_{nm}+x)}+\frac{2R_{nm}}3(L_{nm}+x)\right\}\le e^{-x}.
 \tag{25}
\]
Let \(u(x)\) denote the threshold in (25).  Tail integration and the substitution \(t=u(x)\) give
\[
 E_P\|E\|\le u(0)+\int_0^\infty e^{-x}u'(x)\,dx.
\]
Here \(u'(x)=\sqrt{v_{nm}/\{2(L_{nm}+x)\}}+2R_{nm}/3\); hence the last integral is bounded by a universal constant times \(\sqrt{v_{nm}}+R_{nm}\).  Using the definitions of \(v_{nm},R_{nm},L_{nm}\) yields
\[
 E_P\|E\|
 \le CB\left\{
 \sqrt{\frac{(n\vee m)\log(e(n+m))}{q}}+
 \frac{\log(e(n+m))}{q}\right\}
 =:C\eta_{nm}.
 \tag{26}
\]
The bound is uniform in \(P\).

Let \(P_U=UU^\top\), and let \((\widehat U,\widehat\Sigma,\widehat V)\) be the leading rank-\(r\) singular triple of \(M+E\).  Exact rank gives \((I-P_U)M=0\).  The variational identity
\[
 \sigma_r(X)=\max_{\dim(S)=r}\ \min_{x\in S,\ \|x\|_2=1}\|Xx\|_2
\]
applied to \(X=M+E\) and the particular subspace \(S=\operatorname{span}(V)\) gives \(\sigma_r(M+E)\ge\sigma_r(M)-\|E\|\), by the ordinary triangle inequality.  Thus, on \(\{\|E\|\le\lambda_{nm}/2\}\), the signal floor gives
\[
 \sigma_r(\widehat\Sigma)\ge\sigma_r(M)-\|E\|
 \ge\lambda_{nm}-\|E\|>0.
\]
Projecting \((M+E)\widehat V=\widehat U\widehat\Sigma\) onto the orthogonal complement of \(U\) gives
\[
 \|(I-P_U)\widehat U\|
 \le\frac{\|E\|}{\lambda_{nm}-\|E\|}
 \le\frac{2\|E\|}{\lambda_{nm}}.
 \tag{27}
\]
Write a singular-value decomposition \(U^\top\widehat U=W\operatorname{diag}(\cos\theta_1,\ldots,\cos\theta_r)Q^\top\), with \(0\le\theta_j\le\pi/2\), and take \(R=QW^\top\).  Direct multiplication gives
\[
 \|\widehat UR-U\|^2=\max_j2(1-\cos\theta_j),
 \qquad
 \|(I-P_U)\widehat U\|^2=\max_j\sin^2\theta_j.
\]
Since \(2(1-\cos x)=2\sin^2x/(1+\cos x)\le2\sin^2x\),
\[
 \|\widehat UR-U\|\le\sqrt2\|(I-P_U)\widehat U\|.
 \tag{28}
\]
Off the event in (27), the left side of (28) is at most two.  Since the maximum row norm of any matrix is at most its operator norm, the two cases imply the deterministic bound
\[
 \mathcal L(\widehat U^{\mathrm I},U)
 \le C\sqrt{\frac n{\mu r}}
 \left(1\wedge\frac{\|E\|}{\lambda_{nm}}\right).
 \tag{29}
\]
The fixed tie rule makes the leading left singular vectors of \(\mathcal P_r(M+E)\) the selected leading vectors of \(M+E\).  Finally, \(x\mapsto1\wedge x\) is concave on \(\mathbb R_+\).  Jensen's inequality, (26), and (29) yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_P\mathcal L(\widehat U^{\mathrm I},U)
 &\le C\sqrt{\frac n{\mu r}}
 E_P\left[1\wedge\frac{\|E\|}{\lambda_{nm}}\right]\\
 &\le C\sqrt{\frac n{\mu r}}
 \left(1\wedge\frac{\eta_{nm}}{\lambda_{nm}}\right)
 \le C a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Taking the supremum over the class proves the proposition.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:fixed-complexity-resolution-gap]\label{prop:fixed-complexity-resolution-gap}
Suppose \(n\to\infty\), \(c_-\le m/n\le c_+\), and \(r,\mu,K,q\) are fixed, and define
\[
 d_{nm}=\sqrt{\frac n{\mu r}}\left[1\wedge
 \frac B{\lambda_{nm}}\left\{
 q^{-1}\sqrt{(n\vee m)\log(e(n+m))}+q^{-3}\log(en)
 \right\}\right].
\]
If \(b_{nm}=o(1)\), then the truncations in both \(b_{nm}\) and \(d_{nm}\) are eventually inactive and
\[
 b_{nm}\asymp\frac{B\sqrt{n\log(en)}}{\lambda_{nm}},
 \qquad
 d_{nm}\asymp\sqrt{\frac n{\mu r}}\,b_{nm}.
\]
In particular \(d_{nm}/b_{nm}\asymp\sqrt n\to\infty\), so \(d_{nm}=O(b_{nm})\) is not a nonempty fixed-complexity subregime under \(b_{nm}=o(1)\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write
\[
 s_{nm}=\sqrt{n^{-1}+nm^{-2}},\qquad
 x_{nm}=\frac{B\sqrt{nm}}{\lambda_{nm}}\sqrt{\log(en)}\,b^0_{nm}.
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:aspect\mbox{-}ratio}\) gives
\[
 s_{nm}=n^{-1/2}\sqrt{1+(n/m)^2}\asymp n^{-1/2},
 \qquad
 \sqrt{nm}\,s_{nm}\asymp\sqrt n.
\]
All constants implicit in this proof may depend on the fixed quantities
\(c_-\), \(c_+\), \(r\), \(\mu\), \(K\), and \(q\), but not on
\(n\), \(m\), \(B\), or \(\lambda_{nm}\).  Substitution of Definition
\(\mathrm{def:geometric\mbox{-}rate}\) into \(x_{nm}\) therefore yields
\[
 x_{nm}
 =\frac{B\sqrt{nm}}{\lambda_{nm}}\sqrt{\log(en)}
 K r^{3/2}\mu q^{-1/2}s_{nm}
 \asymp \frac{B\sqrt{n\log(en)}}{\lambda_{nm}}.
\]
Definition \(\mathrm{def:frontier\mbox{-}rate}\) says
\(b_{nm}=1\wedge x_{nm}\).  If \(b_{nm}=o(1)\), then
\(b_{nm}<1\) eventually.  For every such index, \(x_{nm}<1\), since
\(x_{nm}\ge1\) would imply \(1\wedge x_{nm}=1\).  Hence
\(b_{nm}=x_{nm}\) eventually, and consequently
\[
 b_{nm}\asymp\frac{B\sqrt{n\log(en)}}{\lambda_{nm}}.
\]

Let
\[
 y_{nm}=\frac B{\lambda_{nm}}
 \left\{q^{-1}\sqrt{(n\vee m)\log(e(n+m))}
 +q^{-3}\log(en)\right\}.
\]
The aspect-ratio bounds imply
\[
 \log(e(n+m))\asymp\log(en),
 \qquad n\vee m\asymp n.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \frac{\log(en)}{\sqrt{n\log(en)}}
 =\sqrt{\frac{\log(en)}n}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Because \(q\) is fixed and positive, these relations give
\[
 y_{nm}\asymp
 \frac{B\sqrt{n\log(en)}}{\lambda_{nm}}
 \asymp b_{nm}=o(1).
\]
Thus \(y_{nm}<1\) eventually, so the truncation in the definition of
\(d_{nm}\) is also inactive.  It follows that
\[
 d_{nm}=\sqrt{\frac n{\mu r}}\,y_{nm}
 \asymp\sqrt{\frac n{\mu r}}\,b_{nm}.
\]
All quantities divided by here are strictly positive because
\(B>0\), \(\lambda_{nm}>0\), \(q>0\), and \(n,m,r,\mu,K>0\).
Therefore
\[
 \frac{d_{nm}}{b_{nm}}\asymp
 \sqrt{\frac n{\mu r}}\asymp\sqrt n\longrightarrow\infty,
\]
which excludes \(d_{nm}=O(b_{nm})\) on every fixed-complexity sequence
with \(b_{nm}=o(1)\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Nonemptiness and bounded outcomes expose the scale floor that governs whether the stated uniform radii can shrink. \textit{Gap.} At fixed complexity, \(a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm}\) and \(d_{nm}\) are at least order \(\sqrt{\log n}\), so neither is a shrinking uniform resolution. \textit{Consumer.} Blind-diagnostic and minimax claims are interpreted only at resolutions compatible with the bounded-outcome class.

\begin{lemma}[lem:deterministic-sine-frame-loss]\label{lem:deterministic-sine-frame-loss}
The sine frame in \(\mathrm{def:observed\mbox{-}estimators}\) has orthonormal columns.  For every target \(U\in\mathbb O_{n,r}\) satisfying \(\|U\|_{2,\infty}\le\sqrt{\mu r/n}\), it obeys the pathwise bound
\[
 \mathcal L(S^{\mathrm{sin}},U)\le1+\sqrt{2/\mu}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(\vartheta=\pi/(n+1)\), and for \(1\le k\le n\) let \(s_k\in\mathbb R^n\) have entries \((s_k)_i=\sin(ik\vartheta)\).  Let \(T\) be the symmetric tridiagonal matrix with ones immediately above and below the diagonal.  With the boundary convention \((s_k)_0=(s_k)_{n+1}=0\), the addition formula gives, for every \(1\le i\le n\),
\[
 (Ts_k)_i=\sin((i-1)k\vartheta)+\sin((i+1)k\vartheta)
 =2\cos(k\vartheta)(s_k)_i.
\]
The eigenvalues \(2\cos(k\vartheta)\), \(1\le k\le n\), are distinct because cosine is strictly decreasing on \((0,\pi)\).  Since \(T\) is symmetric, for \(k\ne l\),
\[
 \{2\cos(k\vartheta)-2\cos(l\vartheta)\}\langle s_k,s_l\rangle
 =\langle Ts_k,s_l\rangle-\langle s_k,Ts_l\rangle=0,
\]
so \(s_k\perp s_l\).  To compute the norm, let \(z=\exp(2\mathrm i k\vartheta)\).  Then \(z\ne1\), \(z^{n+1}=1\), and the finite geometric-series identity gives \(\sum_{i=0}^nz^i=0\).  Taking real parts and using \(\sin^2x=(1-\cos(2x))/2\),
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^n\sin^2(ik\vartheta)
 =\frac n2-\frac12\sum_{i=1}^n\cos(2ik\vartheta)
 =\frac n2+\frac12=\frac{n+1}{2}.
\]
Because the symbol domain has \(r\le n\), the first \(r\) normalized vectors are therefore orthonormal, proving \(S^{\mathrm{sin}}\in\mathbb O_{n,r}\).

For each row, \(|\sin(ik\vartheta)|\le1\) gives
\[
 \|e_i^\top S^{\mathrm{sin}}\|_2^2
 =\frac2{n+1}\sum_{k=1}^r\sin^2(ik\vartheta)
 \le\frac{2r}{n+1}\le\frac{2r}{n}.
\]
Choose \(R=I_r\) in \(\mathrm{def:rowwise\mbox{-}loss}\).  The rowwise triangle inequality and the assumed left incoherence of \(U\) imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal L(S^{\mathrm{sin}},U)
 &\le\sqrt{\frac n{\mu r}}
 \bigl(\|S^{\mathrm{sin}}\|_{2,\infty}+\|U\|_{2,\infty}\bigr)\\
 &\le\sqrt{\frac n{\mu r}}
 \left(\sqrt{\frac{2r}{n}}+\sqrt{\frac{\mu r}{n}}\right)
 =1+\sqrt{2/\mu}.
\end{aligned}
\]
This is deterministic and hence holds on every sample path.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:hybrid-expected-risk-upper]\label{prop:hybrid-expected-risk-upper}
For every \(n,m\ge2\), the hybrid rule in \(\mathrm{def:observed\mbox{-}estimators}\) belongs to \(\mathfrak E_{n,m}\).  A universal constant \(C_{\mathrm H}<\infty\) exists such that
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P^{\mathrm{pred}}_{n,m}(r,\mu,K,q,B,\lambda)}
 E_P[\mathcal L(\widehat U^{\mathrm H},U)]
 \le C_{\mathrm H}\bigl(1\wedge a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm}\bigr).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The radius \(a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm}\) depends only on the fixed dimensions and class parameters, so the branch in \(\mathrm{def:observed\mbox{-}estimators}\) is deterministic.  The recorded score is a measurable function of \((A,Y,p)\); rank projection followed by the fixed singular-vector tie rule is a measurable finite-dimensional map.  Hence \(\widehat U^{\mathrm I}\) is \(\mathcal O_{n,m}\)-measurable.  Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:deterministic\mbox{-}sine\mbox{-}frame\mbox{-}loss}\) shows that \(S^{\mathrm{sin}}\in\mathbb O_{n,r}\), and it is deterministic.  Each branch is therefore \(\mathcal O_{n,m}\)-measurable and orthonormal, proving \(\widehat U^{\mathrm H}\in\mathfrak E_{n,m}\).

If \(a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm}\le1\), then \(\widehat U^{\mathrm H}=\widehat U^{\mathrm I}\), and Proposition \(\mathrm{prop:ipw\mbox{-}expected\mbox{-}risk\mbox{-}upper}\) gives
\[
 \sup_PE_P[\mathcal L(\widehat U^{\mathrm H},U)]
 \le C_{\mathrm I}a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm}
 =C_{\mathrm I}(1\wedge a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm}).
\]
If \(a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm}>1\), then \(\widehat U^{\mathrm H}=S^{\mathrm{sin}}\).  Every law in \(\mathrm{def:predictable\mbox{-}class}\) satisfies left incoherence, so the pathwise sine-frame lemma gives
\[
 \sup_PE_P[\mathcal L(\widehat U^{\mathrm H},U)]
 \le1+\sqrt{2/\mu}
 \le1+\sqrt2
 =(1+\sqrt2)(1\wedge a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm}),
\]
where \(\mu\ge1\) is part of the symbol domain.  Taking \(C_{\mathrm H}=\max(C_{\mathrm I},1+\sqrt2)\) proves the assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:adaptive-minimax-hybrid-risk-bracket]\label{prop:adaptive-minimax-hybrid-risk-bracket}
Fix \(r\), \(K\), and \(\mu>1\).  There are constants \(c_0,c_1,c,C>0\), depending only on these fixed quantities, \(q\), and the aspect bounds, such that, for all sufficiently large \(n,m\), whenever \(b_{nm}\le c_0\) and \(\lambda_{nm}\le c_1B\sqrt{nm}\),
\[
 c b_{nm}\le
 \inf_{\widetilde U\in\mathfrak E_{n,m}}
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P^{\mathrm{pred}}_{n,m}(r,\mu,K,q,B,\lambda)}
 E_P[\mathcal L(\widetilde U,U)]
 \le
 \sup_{P\in\mathfrak P^{\mathrm{pred}}_{n,m}(r,\mu,K,q,B,\lambda)}
 E_P[\mathcal L(\widehat U^{\mathrm H},U)]
 \le C(1\wedge a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm}).
\]
Separately,
\[
 \sup_PE_P[\mathcal L(\widehat U^{\mathrm I},U)]\le C a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm}.
\]
The endogenous-target obstruction rules out uniform \(b_{nm}\)-attainment by \(\widehat U^{\mathrm I}\) only; it supplies no estimator-independent lower bound above order \(b_{nm}\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The first inequality is Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:adaptive\mbox{-}minimax\mbox{-}lower}\), whose finite lower-bound family lies in the same predictable class.  Proposition \(\mathrm{prop:hybrid\mbox{-}expected\mbox{-}risk\mbox{-}upper}\) proves both \(\widehat U^{\mathrm H}\in\mathfrak E_{n,m}\) and its displayed risk bound.  Evaluating the infimum over \(\mathfrak E_{n,m}\) at this one data-driven estimator proves the middle inequality, and the same proposition proves the final hybrid inequality.  Proposition \(\mathrm{prop:ipw\mbox{-}expected\mbox{-}risk\mbox{-}upper}\) gives the separate IPW--SVD display; increasing \(C\) to the maximum of the two universal upper constants makes one \(C\) valid in both displays.  Finally, Proposition \(\mathrm{prop:adaptive\mbox{-}endogenous\mbox{-}target\mbox{-}obstruction}\) evaluates only \(\widehat U^{\mathrm I}\); it therefore cannot be used to strengthen the minimax lower bound for every member of \(\mathfrak E_{n,m}\).  This proves the bracket and its stated scope.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:nonempty-class-resolution-floors]\label{prop:nonempty-class-resolution-floors}
If
\(\mathfrak P^{\mathrm{pred}}_{n,m}(r,\mu,K,q,B,\lambda)\ne\varnothing\),
then
\[
 \lambda_{nm}\le 2B\sqrt{nm}.
\]
Consequently, suppose \(n\to\infty\),
\(c_-\le m/n\le c_+\), the quantities \(r,\mu,K,q\) are fixed, and
the predictable class is nonempty for every sufficiently large index.  Define
\[
 a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm}=\sqrt{\frac n{\mu r}}
 \left[1\wedge\frac B{\lambda_{nm}}
 \left\{\sqrt{\frac{(n\vee m)\log(e(n+m))}{q}}
 +\frac{\log(e(n+m))}{q}\right\}\right]
\]
and
\[
 d_{nm}=\sqrt{\frac n{\mu r}}
 \left[1\wedge\frac B{\lambda_{nm}}
 \left\{q^{-1}\sqrt{(n\vee m)\log(e(n+m))}
 +q^{-3}\log(en)\right\}\right].
\]
Then, for every sufficiently large index,
\[
 b_{nm}\ge
 \frac{K r^{3/2}\mu}{2\sqrt q}\sqrt{\frac{\log(en)}n},
 \qquad
 a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm}\ge
 \frac{1}{2\sqrt{\mu r q c_+}}\sqrt{\log(en)},
 \qquad
 d_{nm}\ge
 \frac{1}{2q\sqrt{\mu r c_+}}\sqrt{\log(en)}.
\]
In particular, the lower/reference scale is bounded below by order
\(\sqrt{\log n/n}\), whereas both displayed uniform resolution radii are
bounded below by order \(\sqrt{\log n}\) and hence do not shrink.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose any law \(P\) in the nonempty class.  By Assumption
\(\mathrm{ass:bounded\mbox{-}outcomes}\),
\(-B\le Y_{ij}(a)\le B\) almost surely for every cell and arm.  We first
derive, rather than assume, the corresponding conditional-mean bound.  Put
\(X=E_P[Y_{ij}(a)\mid\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1}]\).  For every event
\(H\in\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1}\), the defining integral identity for conditional
expectation gives
\[
 \int_H(B-X)\,dP=\int_H(B-Y_{ij}(a))\,dP\ge0.
\]
If \(P(X>B)>0\), then for some positive integer \(k\), the event
\(H_k=\{X\ge B+1/k\}\) has positive probability; choosing \(H=H_k\)
would make the left side at most \(-P(H_k)/k<0\), a contradiction.  Thus
\(X\le B\) almost surely.  Applying the same argument to \(-Y_{ij}(a)\)
gives \(X\ge-B\).  By the definitions of \(\mu^0\), \(\mu^1\), and \(M\),
\[
 |M_{ij}|=|\mu^1_{ij}-\mu^0_{ij}|\le2B
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \lVert M\rVert_{\mathrm F}^2
 =\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{j=1}^m M_{ij}^2\le4B^2nm.
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:exact\mbox{-}rank}\) gives exactly \(r\) positive
singular values.  Hence
\[
 \sigma_r(M)^2\le\sum_{k=1}^r\sigma_k(M)^2
 =\lVert M\rVert_{\mathrm F}^2\le4B^2nm.
\]
Combining this pathwise inequality with Assumption
\(\mathrm{ass:signal\mbox{-}floor}\) proves
\[
 \lambda_{nm}\le\sigma_r(M)\le2B\sqrt{nm}.
\]

It remains to derive the three rate floors.  Write \(L_n=\log(en)\) and
\(L_{nm}=\log(e(n+m))\).  The signal inequality just proved implies
\[
 \frac{B\sqrt{nm}}{\lambda_{nm}}\ge\frac12,
 \qquad
 \frac B{\lambda_{nm}}\ge\frac1{2\sqrt{nm}}.
\]
Definitions \(\mathrm{def:geometric\mbox{-}rate}\) and
\(\mathrm{def:frontier\mbox{-}rate}\), together with
\(\sqrt{n^{-1}+nm^{-2}}\ge n^{-1/2}\), therefore give
\[
 b_{nm}\ge 1\wedge
 \left\{\frac{K r^{3/2}\mu}{2\sqrt q}
 \sqrt{\frac{L_n}{n}}\right\}.
\]
The expression inside braces converges to zero because the displayed
structural quantities are fixed.  This proves the asserted lower bound for
all sufficiently large indices.

Next, \(n\vee m\ge n\), \(L_{nm}\ge L_n\), and the upper aspect bound
\(m\le c_+n\) imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \frac B{\lambda_{nm}}
 \sqrt{\frac{(n\vee m)L_{nm}}q}
 &\ge \frac1{2\sqrt{nm}}\sqrt{\frac{nL_{nm}}q}
 =\frac1{2\sqrt q}\sqrt{\frac{L_{nm}}m}
 \ge\frac1{2\sqrt{q c_+}}\sqrt{\frac{L_n}n},\\
 \frac B{\lambda_{nm}}q^{-1}
 \sqrt{(n\vee m)L_{nm}}
 &\ge\frac1{2q\sqrt{c_+}}\sqrt{\frac{L_n}n}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The omitted addends in the definitions of \(a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm}\) and
\(d_{nm}\) are nonnegative.  Each lower bound on the right tends to zero, so
for all sufficiently large indices monotonicity of \(x\mapsto1\wedge x\)
and multiplication by the positive factor \(\sqrt{n/(\mu r)}\) yield
\[
 a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm}\ge
 \frac1{2\sqrt{\mu r q c_+}}\sqrt{L_n},
 \qquad
 d_{nm}\ge
 \frac1{2q\sqrt{\mu r c_+}}\sqrt{L_n}.
\]
This proves all three claims and, since \(L_n\to\infty\), the nonshrinking
conclusion.
\end{proof}

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

Operator-norm martingale concentration followed by a deterministic subspace inequality pays the factor \(\sqrt{n/(\mu r)}\), producing \(a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm}\).  The frozen class permits the target singular factors to depend on earlier outcomes, so the usual leave-one-row argument cannot simply treat those factors as predictable; the explicit obstruction shows that this is substantive for IPW--SVD.  The minimax lower scale \(b_{nm}\) therefore remains separated from the available upper rate.  For blind normalization, the ratio residual and stochastic spectral perturbation give resolution \(d_{nm}\); at fixed complexity and bounded aspect ratio, \(d_{nm}/b_{nm}\asymp\sqrt n\) whenever \(b_{nm}=o(1)\).  The separate arm means in the denominator derivative are not estimable at a claimed frontier rate under the current effect-only low-rank assumptions.  No residual covariance consistency, positive random studentizer, or feasible multiplier exactness is assumed or claimed.

\section{Honest scope}

Identification is given by the recorded IPW conditional mean.  The proved statistical statement is the expected-risk bracket between the lower/reference scale \(b_{nm}\) and the loose IPW--SVD radius \(a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm}\); these rates are not claimed to match.  The adaptive endogenous-target construction refutes only uniform \(b_{nm}\)-attainment by the specified IPW--SVD estimator.  It does not show that every estimator measurable from \(A,Y,p\) fails at that scale, so minimax tightness and the sharp constant remain open.  The blind procedure is introduced here and is certified only up to \(d_{nm}\); no unconditional \(b_{nm}\)-scale validity criterion or feasible tangent correction is claimed.  The \(0.25/0.75\) Markov sequence is a boundary example for that procedure, not a refutation of a published method.  Simultaneous inference is supplied by conservative finite-sample direct-IPW radii, not by asymptotic exactness of the frozen leave-one-cell-out multiplier statistic.
