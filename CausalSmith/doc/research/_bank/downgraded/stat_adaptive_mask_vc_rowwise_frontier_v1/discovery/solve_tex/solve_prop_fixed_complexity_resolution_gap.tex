%%% FIELD target-proof
Put
\[
 t_{nm}=\frac{B\sqrt{nm}}{\lambda_{nm}}\sqrt{\log(en)}\,b^0_{nm}.
\]
By \(\mathrm{ass:aspect\mbox{-}ratio}\),
\[
 n^{-1}\le n^{-1}+nm^{-2}
 \le \bigl(1+c_-^{-2}\bigr)n^{-1},
\]
and hence, by \(\mathrm{def:geometric\mbox{-}rate}\),
\[
 b^0_{nm}
 =K r^{3/2}\mu q^{-1/2}\sqrt{n^{-1}+nm^{-2}}
 \asymp n^{-1/2}.
\]
Here and below the comparison constants may depend on the fixed positive quantities \(r,\mu,K,q,c_-,c_+\), but not on \(n,m,B,\lambda_{nm}\).  The same aspect-ratio bounds give
\[
 c_-n^2\le nm\le c_+n^2,
\]
so
\[
 t_{nm}\asymp
 \frac{B\sqrt{n\log(en)}}{\lambda_{nm}}.
\]
By \(\mathrm{def:frontier\mbox{-}rate}\), \(b_{nm}=1\wedge t_{nm}\).  The hypothesis \(b_{nm}=o(1)\) implies that \(b_{nm}<1\) eventually.  For every such index, \(1\wedge t_{nm}<1\) forces \(t_{nm}<1\) and therefore \(b_{nm}=t_{nm}\).  Thus the truncation in \(b_{nm}\) is eventually inactive and
\[
 b_{nm}\asymp
 \frac{B\sqrt{n\log(en)}}{\lambda_{nm}}.
\]

For the second radius, define its untruncated inner term by
\[
 x_{nm}=\frac B{\lambda_{nm}}
 \left\{q^{-1}\sqrt{(n\vee m)\log(e(n+m))}
 +q^{-3}\log(en)\right\}.
\]
The aspect-ratio condition implies
\[
 n\le n\vee m\le (1\vee c_+)n
\]
and
\[
 (1+c_-)n\le n+m\le(1+c_+)n.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \sqrt{(n\vee m)\log(e(n+m))}
 \asymp\sqrt{n\log(en)}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \frac{\log(en)}{\sqrt{n\log(en)}}
 =\sqrt{\frac{\log(en)}n}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Since \(q\) is fixed in \((0,1/2)\), these two displays yield
\[
 x_{nm}\asymp
 \frac{B\sqrt{n\log(en)}}{\lambda_{nm}}
 \asymp b_{nm}=o(1).
\]
Hence \(x_{nm}<1\) eventually, so the truncation in the definition of \(d_{nm}\) is also eventually inactive.  It follows that
\[
 d_{nm}=\sqrt{\frac n{\mu r}}\,x_{nm}
 \asymp\sqrt{\frac n{\mu r}}\,b_{nm}.
\]
All factors defining \(b_{nm}\) are strictly positive, so division is legitimate.  Because \(r\) and \(\mu\) are fixed,
\[
 \frac{d_{nm}}{b_{nm}}\asymp\sqrt n\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Thus no path satisfying the proposition's hypotheses can also satisfy \(d_{nm}=O(b_{nm})\), which proves the claim.

%%% FIELD scale-floor-statement
For every \(n,m\) such that
\(\mathfrak P^{\mathrm{pred}}_{n,m}(r,\mu,K,q,B,\lambda)\neq\varnothing\),
\[
 \lambda_{nm}\le 2B\sqrt{nm}.
\]
Suppose further that \(n\to\infty\), \(c_-\le m/n\le c_+\), the quantities \(r,\mu,K,q\) are fixed, and the displayed class is nonempty eventually.  Define
\[
 a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm}=
 \sqrt{\frac n{\mu r}}\left[1\wedge
 \frac B{\lambda_{nm}}\left\{
 \sqrt{\frac{(n\vee m)\log(e(n+m))}{q}}
 +\frac{\log(e(n+m))}{q}
 \right\}\right]
\]
and
\[
 d_{nm}=\sqrt{\frac n{\mu r}}\left[1\wedge
 \frac B{\lambda_{nm}}\left\{
 q^{-1}\sqrt{(n\vee m)\log(e(n+m))}
 +q^{-3}\log(en)
 \right\}\right].
\]
Then there are constants \(c_b,c_a,c_d>0\), depending only on the fixed quantities and the aspect bounds, such that eventually
\[
 b_{nm}\ge c_b\sqrt{\frac{\log(en)}n},\qquad
 a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm}\ge c_a\sqrt{\log(en)},\qquad
 d_{nm}\ge c_d\sqrt{\log(en)}.
\]
In particular, the last two uniform resolution radii do not shrink; they diverge at least as fast as \(\sqrt{\log n}\).

%%% FIELD scale-floor-proof
Fix \(n,m\) for which the class is nonempty, and choose
\(P\in\mathfrak P^{\mathrm{pred}}_{n,m}(r,\mu,K,q,B,\lambda)\).
For a cell \((i,j)\), an arm \(a\in\{0,1\}\), and
\(\mathcal H_{ij}=\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1}\), assumption
\(\mathrm{ass:bounded\mbox{-}outcomes}\) gives
\[
 -B\le Y_{ij}(a)\le B\qquad P\text{-almost surely}.
\]
We verify the corresponding conditional-mean bound directly.  If
\(D_+=\{E_P[Y_{ij}(a)\mid\mathcal H_{ij}]>B\}\) had positive probability, then \(D_+\in\mathcal H_{ij}\) and the defining integral identity for conditional expectation would give
\[
 \int_{D_+}E_P[Y_{ij}(a)\mid\mathcal H_{ij}]\,dP
 =\int_{D_+}Y_{ij}(a)\,dP
 \le B P(D_+),
\]
whereas the definition of \(D_+\) makes the left side strictly larger than \(BP(D_+)\), a contradiction.  Applying the same argument to \(-Y_{ij}(a)\) proves
\[
 \left|E_P[Y_{ij}(a)\mid\mathcal H_{ij}]\right|\le B
 \qquad P\text{-almost surely}.
\]
The conditional expectation exists because boundedness implies integrability.  By the definitions of \(\mu^a_{ij}\) and \(M\), the triangle inequality therefore yields, simultaneously over the finite cell set,
\[
 |M_{ij}|=|\mu^1_{ij}-\mu^0_{ij}|
 \le |\mu^1_{ij}|+|\mu^0_{ij}|\le2B
 \qquad P\text{-almost surely}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \lVert M\rVert_F^2=\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{j=1}^mM_{ij}^2
 \le4B^2nm,
 \qquad
 \lVert M\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le\lVert M\rVert_F\le2B\sqrt{nm}.
\]
The operator-norm inequality follows from Cauchy--Schwarz applied row by row to \(Mx\) for every \(x\) with \(\lVert x\rVert_2=1\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:exact\mbox{-}rank}\) makes \(\sigma_r(M)\) a nonzero singular value, and assumption \(\mathrm{ass:signal\mbox{-}floor}\) gives \(\lambda_{nm}\le\sigma_r(M)\).  Since every singular value is at most the operator norm,
\[
 \lambda_{nm}\le\sigma_r(M)\le\lVert M\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le2B\sqrt{nm}.
\]
Both endpoints are deterministic, so their almost-sure comparison proves the first assertion for every nonempty class.

Now work along the stated fixed-complexity path, at indices where the class is nonempty.  The preceding bound and positivity of \(B\) and \(\lambda_{nm}\) imply
\[
 \frac{B\sqrt{nm}}{\lambda_{nm}}\ge\frac12,
 \qquad
 \frac B{\lambda_{nm}}\ge\frac1{2\sqrt{nm}}.
\]
Let \(s_n=\sqrt{\log(en)/n}\).  By
\(\mathrm{def:geometric\mbox{-}rate}\),
\[
 b^0_{nm}
 =K r^{3/2}\mu q^{-1/2}\sqrt{n^{-1}+nm^{-2}}
 \ge K r^{3/2}\mu q^{-1/2}n^{-1/2}.
\]
Thus the untruncated expression in
\(\mathrm{def:frontier\mbox{-}rate}\) is at least
\[
 \frac{K r^{3/2}\mu}{2\sqrt q}\,s_n.
\]
Because \(s_n\to0\), this lower benchmark is at most one eventually.  For nonnegative \(x\ge y\) with \(y\le1\), one has \(1\wedge x\ge y\).  Applying this elementary inequality to the preceding display proves
\[
 b_{nm}\ge
 \frac{K r^{3/2}\mu}{2\sqrt q}\,s_n
\]
eventually.

For the inner untruncated term of \(a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm}\), discard its nonnegative second summand and use \(n\vee m\ge n\), \(\log(e(n+m))\ge\log(en)\), and \(m\le c_+n\).  This gives
\[
 \frac B{\lambda_{nm}}
 \left\{
 \sqrt{\frac{(n\vee m)\log(e(n+m))}{q}}
 +\frac{\log(e(n+m))}{q}
 \right\}
 \ge
 \frac1{2\sqrt{nm}}
 \sqrt{\frac{n\log(en)}q}
 \ge\frac1{2\sqrt{c_+q}}s_n.
\]
The right side tends to zero, so the same minimum inequality and the positive normalization yield eventually
\[
 a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm}
 \ge\sqrt{\frac n{\mu r}}\,\frac1{2\sqrt{c_+q}}s_n
 =\frac1{2\sqrt{c_+q\mu r}}\sqrt{\log(en)}.
\]
Likewise, after discarding the nonnegative second summand in the inner term of \(d_{nm}\),
\[
 \frac B{\lambda_{nm}}
 \left\{
 q^{-1}\sqrt{(n\vee m)\log(e(n+m))}
 +q^{-3}\log(en)
 \right\}
 \ge\frac1{2q\sqrt{c_+}}s_n.
\]
Therefore, eventually,
\[
 d_{nm}\ge
 \frac1{2q\sqrt{c_+\mu r}}\sqrt{\log(en)}.
\]
Taking the three displayed constants as \(c_b,c_a,c_d\), respectively, proves the asserted lower bounds.  Finally \(\log(en)\to\infty\), so both \(a^{\mathrm{loose}}_{nm}\) and \(d_{nm}\) diverge and in particular cannot be shrinking resolutions.
