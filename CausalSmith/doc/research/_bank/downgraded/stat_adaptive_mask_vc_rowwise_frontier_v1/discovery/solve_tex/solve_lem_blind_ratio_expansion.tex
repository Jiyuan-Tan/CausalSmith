Let
\[
 \widetilde p_i=\frac1m\sum_{j\in J}A_{ij},\qquad
 \delta_{nm}=\max_{i\in I}|\widehat p_i-\bar p_i|.
\]
Overlap implies \(q\le E_P[p_{ij}]\le1-q\), and hence \(\bar p_i\in[q,1-q]\).  Projection onto the closed interval \([q/2,1-q/2]\) fixes \(\bar p_i\) and is one-Lipschitz.  Therefore
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |\widehat p_i-\bar p_i|
 &\le |\widetilde p_i-\bar p_i|\\
 &\le \left|\frac1m\sum_{j\in J}(A_{ij}-p_{ij})\right|
 +\left|\frac1m\sum_{j\in J}(p_{ij}-E_P[p_{ij}])\right|.
 \end{aligned}
\]
For fixed \(i\), the variables \(A_{ij}-p_{ij}\), in increasing \(j\), are consecutive martingale differences for the global filtration: this follows from the definition of \(p_{ij}\) and the row-major ordering.  If \(X=A_{ij}-p_{ij}\), then, conditionally on \(\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1}\), \(X\) has mean zero and lies in an interval of length one.  The elementary convexity bound
\[
 E_P[e^{tX}\mid\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1}]\le e^{t^2/8}
\]
follows by maximizing \(p e^{t(1-p)}+(1-p)e^{-tp}\) over \(p\in[0,1]\).  Iterated conditioning and Chernoff's inequality consequently give
\[
 P\!\left(\left|\frac1m\sum_{j\in J}(A_{ij}-p_{ij})\right|>x\right)
 \le 2e^{-2mx^2}.
\]
A union bound over the \(n\) rows, followed by the propensity-path-stability assumption, yields
\[
 \delta_{nm}=O_P\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log(en)}m}\right).
\]

Bounded outcomes imply \(|\mu^a_{ij}|\le B\) for \(a\in\{0,1\}\), because conditional expectation contracts the almost-sure supremum norm.  Since \(\bar p_i\in[q,1-q]\),
\[
 |H^{\mathrm B}_{ij}|
 \le \frac{|\mu^1_{ij}|}{\bar p_i^2}
      +\frac{|\mu^0_{ij}|}{(1-\bar p_i)^2}
 \le \frac{2B}{q^2}.
\]
The definition \(D^{\mathrm B}_{ij}=-(\widehat p_i-\bar p_i)H^{\mathrm B}_{ij}\), the inequality \(\lVert C\rVert\le\lVert C\rVert_F\), and the preceding concentration bound now imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \lVert D^{\mathrm B}\rVert
 &\le \frac{2B}{q^2}\delta_{nm}\sqrt{nm}\\
 &=O_P\!\left(Bq^{-2}\sqrt{n\log(en)}\right).
 \end{aligned}
\]

It remains to compare this expression with the declared lower/reference scale.  Because \(b_{nm}=o(1)\), the truncation in \(\mathrm{def:frontier\mbox{-}rate}\) is inactive eventually.  Hence
\[
 b_{nm}=\frac{B\sqrt{nm}}{\lambda_{nm}}\sqrt{\log(en)}
 K r^{3/2}\mu q^{-1/2}\sqrt{n^{-1}+nm^{-2}}.
\]
The aspect lower bound \(m\ge c_-n\) and \(K,r,\mu\ge1\) give
\[
 b_{nm}\ge
 \sqrt{c_-}\,\frac{B}{\lambda_{nm}}q^{-1/2}
 \sqrt{n\log(en)}.
\]
The parameters \(q,c_-,c_+\) are fixed along the asymptotic sequence.  Dividing the operator bound by the positive signal floor therefore gives
\[
 \frac{\lVert D^{\mathrm B}\rVert}{\lambda_{nm}}
 =O_P\!\left(\frac{Bq^{-2}\sqrt{n\log(en)}}{\lambda_{nm}}\right)
 =O_P(b_{nm})=o_P(1).
\]

For completeness, the singular-value perturbation used for the gap is elementary.  The min--max characterization and the triangle inequality imply, for every admissible \(k\),
\[
 |\sigma_k(C+Q)-\sigma_k(C)|\le\lVert Q\rVert.
\]
Apply this once to \(\sigma_r\) and once to \(\sigma_{r+1}\), with \(C=M^{\mathrm B}\) and \(Q=D^{\mathrm B}\).  On using the primitive oracle gap,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sigma_r(T^{\mathrm B})-\sigma_{r+1}(T^{\mathrm B})
 &\ge \sigma_r(M^{\mathrm B})-\sigma_{r+1}(M^{\mathrm B})
      -2\lVert D^{\mathrm B}\rVert\\
 &\ge c_{\mathrm B}\lambda_{nm}-2\lVert D^{\mathrm B}\rVert.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Because \(\lVert D^{\mathrm B}\rVert/\lambda_{nm}=o_P(1)\), the last member is at least \((c_{\mathrm B}/2)\lambda_{nm}\) with probability tending to one.  When \(r=\min(n,m)\), the declared convention sets the absent \((r+1)\)-st singular value to zero, and the same lower bound remains valid.

Put \(t_i=\widehat p_i-\bar p_i\) and \(s_i=1-\bar p_i\).  By overlap and the definition of clipping,
\[
 \bar p_i,s_i\ge q,
 \quad
 \widehat p_i,1-\widehat p_i\ge q/2,
\]
so every division below is valid.  The exact reciprocal identities
\[
 \frac1{\bar p_i+t_i}
 =\frac1{\bar p_i}-\frac{t_i}{\bar p_i^2}
 +\frac{t_i^2}{\bar p_i^2(\bar p_i+t_i)}
\]
and
\[
 \frac1{s_i-t_i}
 =\frac1{s_i}+\frac{t_i}{s_i^2}
 +\frac{t_i^2}{s_i^2(s_i-t_i)}
\]
give, cell by cell,
\[
 Z^{\mathrm B}=Z^{\bar p}+D^{\mathrm B}+R^{\mathrm B},
\]
where
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 R^{\mathrm B}_{ij}
 &=-t_i\{Q_{ij}-H^{\mathrm B}_{ij}\}+t_i^2S_{ij},\\
 Q_{ij}
 &=\frac{A_{ij}Y_{ij}}{\bar p_i^2}
   +\frac{(1-A_{ij})Y_{ij}}{(1-\bar p_i)^2},\\
 S_{ij}
 &=\frac{A_{ij}Y_{ij}}{\bar p_i^2\widehat p_i}
   -\frac{(1-A_{ij})Y_{ij}}
   {(1-\bar p_i)^2(1-\widehat p_i)}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Indeed, the two linear reciprocal terms equal \(-t_iQ_{ij}\), while \(D^{\mathrm B}_{ij}=-t_iH^{\mathrm B}_{ij}\); subtracting the latter leaves the displayed centered-linear term.

Sequential randomization and consistency of the observed outcome give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_P[A_{ij}Y_{ij}\mid\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1}]
 &=p_{ij}\mu^1_{ij},\\
 E_P[(1-A_{ij})Y_{ij}\mid\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1}]
 &=(1-p_{ij})\mu^0_{ij}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Consequently
\[
 E_P[Q_{ij}\mid\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1}]=H^{\mathrm B}_{ij}
\]
and, separately,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_P[Z^{\bar p}_{ij}\mid\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1}]
 &=\frac{p_{ij}\mu^1_{ij}}{\bar p_i}
   -\frac{(1-p_{ij})\mu^0_{ij}}{1-\bar p_i}\\
 &=M^{\mathrm B}_{ij}.
 \end{aligned}
\]

We next bound the centered-linear matrix.  Bounded outcomes and the displayed denominator bounds imply
\[
 |Q_{ij}-H^{\mathrm B}_{ij}|\le C Bq^{-2},
 \quad
 E_P[(Q_{ij}-H^{\mathrm B}_{ij})^2
       \mid\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1}]\le C B^2q^{-4}.
\]
Apply the self-adjoint dilation to the row-major sequence of single-cell matrices.  Its predictable quadratic variation is block diagonal; the row block has diagonal entries bounded by \(CmB^2q^{-4}\), and the column block has diagonal entries bounded by \(CnB^2q^{-4}\).  Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:tropp\mbox{-}matrix\mbox{-}freedman}\), applied to both signs, therefore yields
\[
 \lVert Q-H^{\mathrm B}\rVert
 =O_P\!\left(
 Bq^{-2}\sqrt{(n\vee m)\log(e(n+m))}
 +Bq^{-2}\log(e(n+m))
 \right).
\]
Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:blind\mbox{-}tangent\mbox{-}control}\) gives
\[
 \max_i|t_i|=O_P\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log(en)}m}\right),
\]
and the denominator bounds give \(\max_{i,j}|S_{ij}|\le CBq^{-3}\).  Since left multiplication by \(\operatorname{diag}(t_i)\) has operator norm \(\max_i|t_i|\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \lVert R^{\mathrm B}\rVert
 &\le \max_i|t_i|\,\lVert Q-H^{\mathrm B}\rVert
 +CBq^{-3}\sqrt{nm}\max_i t_i^2\\
 &=O_P(Bq^{-3}\log(en)).
 \end{aligned}
\]
The last equality uses \(c_-\le m/n\le c_+\), which implies \(n\vee m\asymp m\) and \(\log(e(n+m))\asymp\log(en)\); it also uses \(q<1/2\), so a factor \(q^{-2}\) is absorbed by \(q^{-3}\).

Similarly, \(Z^{\bar p}_{ij}-M^{\mathrm B}_{ij}\) is a row-major matrix martingale difference with
\[
 |Z^{\bar p}_{ij}-M^{\mathrm B}_{ij}|\le CBq^{-1},
 \quad
 E_P[(Z^{\bar p}_{ij}-M^{\mathrm B}_{ij})^2
       \mid\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1}]\le CB^2q^{-2}.
\]
The same dilation and matrix-Freedman calculation gives
\[
 \lVert Z^{\bar p}-M^{\mathrm B}\rVert
 =O_P\!\left(
 Bq^{-1}\sqrt{(n\vee m)\log(e(n+m))}
 +Bq^{-1}\log(e(n+m))
 \right).
\]
Because \(T^{\mathrm B}=M^{\mathrm B}+D^{\mathrm B}\), the exact expansion cancels the tangent and gives
\[
 Z^{\mathrm B}-T^{\mathrm B}
 =(Z^{\bar p}-M^{\mathrm B})+R^{\mathrm B}.
\]
Thus, after absorbing the smaller linear logarithm into the \(q^{-3}\log(en)\) term,
\[
 \lVert Z^{\mathrm B}-T^{\mathrm B}\rVert
 =O_P\!\left(B\left\{
 q^{-1}\sqrt{(n\vee m)\log(e(n+m))}
 +q^{-3}\log(en)\right\}\right).
\]

We spell out the deterministic perturbation step because \(T^{\mathrm B}\) need not have rank \(r\).  Let a rectangular matrix \(S\) have a gap \(g=\sigma_r(S)-\sigma_{r+1}(S)>0\), and let \(\widetilde U\) and \(U_S\) be the leading left singular matrices of \(S+Q\) and \(S\).  Form the symmetric dilations \(\mathscr S=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&S\\S^\top&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) and \(\mathscr Q=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&Q\\Q^\top&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\).  Their operator norms satisfy \(\lVert\mathscr Q\rVert=\lVert Q\rVert\), and the selected positive eigenspace of \(\mathscr S\) is separated from its complement by \(g\).  If \(\lVert Q\rVert<g/2\), project the eigen-equation for \(\mathscr S+\mathscr Q\) onto the orthogonal complement of the selected eigenspace.  In an eigenbasis of \(\mathscr S\), every resulting coefficient is divided by a spectral difference at least \(g-\lVert Q\rVert\).  Hence
\[
 \lVert\sin\Theta(\widetilde U,U_S)\rVert
 \le \frac{\lVert Q\rVert}{g-\lVert Q\rVert}
 \le\frac{2\lVert Q\rVert}{g}.
\]
This coefficient calculation uses only the eigen-equation and the min--max bound \(|\widetilde\sigma_k-\sigma_k|\le\lVert Q\rVert\).  Aligning the two bases by the polar factor gives
\[
 \min_{R\in\mathbb O_r}\lVert\widetilde U R-U_S\rVert
 \le\sqrt2\lVert\sin\Theta(\widetilde U,U_S)\rVert.
\]
If \(\lVert Q\rVert\ge g/2\), the left side is at most two.  Since \(\lVert C\rVert_{2,\infty}\le\lVert C\rVert\), both cases yield
\[
 \mathcal L(\widetilde U,U_S)
 \le C\sqrt{\frac n{\mu r}}
 \left(1\wedge\frac{\lVert Q\rVert}{g}\right).
\]

By Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:blind\mbox{-}tangent\mbox{-}control}\), the gap of \(T^{\mathrm B}\) is at least \((c_{\mathrm B}/2)\lambda_{nm}\) with probability tending to one.  Apply the preceding deterministic inequality with \(S=T^{\mathrm B}\), \(Q=Z^{\mathrm B}-T^{\mathrm B}\), and \(U_S=U^{\mathrm B}\).  The fixed tie rule makes the leading left singular vectors of \(\mathcal P_r(Z^{\mathrm B})\) equal to the selected leading vectors of \(Z^{\mathrm B}\).  We obtain
\[
 \mathcal L(\widehat U^{\mathrm B},U^{\mathrm B})
 =O_P\!\left(
 \sqrt{\frac n{\mu r}}\left[1\wedge
 \frac B{\lambda_{nm}}\left\{
 q^{-1}\sqrt{(n\vee m)\log(e(n+m))}
 +q^{-3}\log(en)\right\}\right]\right),
\]
which is the asserted \(O_P(a^{\mathrm B,loose}_{nm})\) conclusion.

Finally, the claim about the residual is a genuine non-implication, not merely a limitation of the preceding upper bound.  In the deterministic perturbation inequality just proved, the operator contribution of the residual is
\[
 c^{R}_{nm}=\sqrt{\frac n{\mu r}}\frac{\lVert R^{\mathrm B}\rVert}{\lambda_{nm}}
\]
whenever the truncation is inactive.  Consider the square path \(m=n\), let \(r=\mu=K=1\), and fix \(q<1/2\).  At baseline draw independent Rademacher variables \(R_i\), set
\[
 \delta_n=c\sqrt{\frac{\log(en)}n},\qquad
 p_{ij}=\frac12+\delta_nR_i,\qquad
 Y_{ij}(1)=\frac B2,\qquad Y_{ij}(0)=-\frac B2,
\]
and, conditionally on the baseline, draw the assignments independently with these recorded propensities.  For all sufficiently large \(n\), overlap holds.  The effect matrix and oracle blind center both equal \(B\mathbf1_n\mathbf1_n^\top\), so exact rank, incoherence, condition number, signal floor with \(\lambda_{nn}=Bn\), and the primitive oracle gap all hold.  Moreover \(\bar p_i=1/2\), and
\[
 \max_i\left|\frac1n\sum_j(p_{ij}-E_Pp_{ij})\right|=\delta_n,
\]
so propensity-path stability holds.  The frontier formula gives \(b_{nn}\asymp\sqrt{\log(en)/n}=o(1)\).

Write \(X_{ij}=2A_{ij}-1\) and \(t_i=\widehat p_i-1/2\).  For a fixed row, conditional independence and boundedness give
\[
 E_P\!\left[\left\{\frac1{2n}\sum_j(X_{ij}-2\delta_nR_i)\right\}^2
 \mathrel{\Big|}R_i\right]\le\frac1{4n}.
\]
Markov's inequality therefore shows that clipping is inactive with probability tending to one and that
\[
 t_i=\delta_nR_i+O_P(n^{-1/2})
\]
for any fixed row.  Moreover, direct expansion and the same variance calculation give
\[
 \frac1n\sum_j(X_{ij}-2\delta_nR_i)^2
 =1-4\delta_nR_i\frac1n\sum_jX_{ij}+4\delta_n^2
 \longrightarrow1
\]
in probability.  Direct substitution in the exact residual formula above gives
\[
 Q_{ij}-H^{\mathrm B}_{ij}=2B(X_{ij}-2\delta_nR_i),
 \quad
 S_{ij}=2B\left\{\frac{A_{ij}}{\widehat p_i}
 +\frac{1-A_{ij}}{1-\widehat p_i}\right\}.
\]
On the clipping-inactive event \(\max_j|S_{ij}|\le C_qB\).  For the first row, the reverse triangle inequality therefore yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \lVert e_1^\top R^{\mathrm B}\rVert_2
 &\ge 2B|t_1|
 \left\{\sum_j(X_{1j}-2\delta_nR_1)^2\right\}^{1/2}
 -C_qBt_1^2\sqrt n\\
 &=(2c+o_P(1))B\sqrt{\log(en)}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Since the operator norm dominates every row Euclidean norm,
\[
 c^R_{nn}\ge(2c+o_P(1))\sqrt{\frac{\log(en)}n}\asymp b_{nn}.
\]
Thus the frozen assumptions do not imply that the residual's operator perturbation contribution to normalized maximum-row loss is \(o_P(b_{nm})\), as claimed.

Write
\[
 s_{ij}=(-1)^{i+j},\qquad X_{i,j}=2A_{ij}-1,
 \qquad X_{i,0}=2A_i^{\mathrm{init}}-1.
\]
The construction gives
\[
 2p_{ij}-1=\frac12s_{ij}X_{i,j-1},
 \qquad
 E_P[X_{i,j}\mid\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1}]
 =\frac12s_{ij}X_{i,j-1}.
\]
Thus \(p_{ij}\in\{1/4,3/4\}\), and the assumption \(q<1/4\) verifies overlap.  Both potential outcomes at cell \((i,j)\) are measurable with respect to the pre-cell field, whereas the current assignment is drawn with the recorded probability \(p_{ij}\).  Conditioning additionally on those potential outcomes therefore leaves this probability unchanged, proving sequential randomization.  Also
\[
 |Y_{ij}(a)|\le h+\theta/2\le B,
\]
so bounded outcomes hold.  Direct subtraction gives
\[
 M_{ij}=\theta s_{ij}=\theta(-1)^i(-1)^j.
\]
Because \(n_\ell\) and \(m_\ell\) are even, the normalized alternating vectors
\[
 u_1=n_\ell^{-1/2}((-1)^i)_i,
 \qquad
 v_1=m_\ell^{-1/2}((-1)^j)_j
\]
are unit vectors, and \(M=\theta\sqrt{n_\ell m_\ell},u_1v_1^\top\).  Hence \(M\) has rank one, singular value \(\theta\sqrt{n_\ell m_\ell}\), left and right incoherence equal to one, and condition number one.  The signal floor holds with equality for \(\lambda_{n_\ell m_\ell}=\theta\sqrt{n_\ell m_\ell}\); the prescribed dimension path satisfies the aspect condition for all sufficiently large \(\ell\).

The baseline state is fair.  The recursion above preserves fairness, because \(E_PX_{i,0}=0\) and
\[
 E_PX_{i,j}=\frac12s_{ij}E_PX_{i,j-1}=0.
\]
It follows that \(E_Pp_{ij}=1/2\) and \(\bar p_i=1/2\).  With \(C_{ij}=hs_{ij}X_{i,j-1}\), direct algebra gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 M^{\mathrm B}_{ij}
 &=2\left[p_{ij}\left(C_{ij}+\frac\theta2s_{ij}\right)
 -(1-p_{ij})\left(C_{ij}-\frac\theta2s_{ij}\right)\right]\\
 &=2\left[(2p_{ij}-1)C_{ij}+\frac\theta2s_{ij}\right]\\
 &=h+\theta s_{ij}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Let \(u_0=n_\ell^{-1/2}\mathbf1_{n_\ell}\) and \(v_0=m_\ell^{-1/2}\mathbf1_{m_\ell}\).  Evenness makes \(u_0\perp u_1\) and \(v_0\perp v_1\), so
\[
 M^{\mathrm B}=h\sqrt{n_\ell m_\ell},u_0v_0^\top
 +\theta\sqrt{n_\ell m_\ell},u_1v_1^\top.
\]
Its two nonzero singular values are \(h\sqrt{n_\ell m_\ell}\) and \(\theta\sqrt{n_\ell m_\ell}\).  Since \(h>\theta\), the primitive oracle gap is
\[
 (h-\theta)\sqrt{n_\ell m_\ell}
 =\frac{h-\theta}{\theta}\lambda_{n_\ell m_\ell},
\]
which proves the asserted value of \(c_{\mathrm B}\).

We next verify both path concentration claims.  Put
\[
 \varepsilon_{i,j}=X_{i,j}-\frac12s_{ij}X_{i,j-1}.
\]
These are conditionally mean-zero variables with conditional range of length two.  Recursively substituting
\(X_{i,j}=2^{-1}s_{ij}X_{i,j-1}+\varepsilon_{i,j}\) expresses any deterministic linear form \(\sum_j a_jX_{i,j}\) as a martingale transform of \((\varepsilon_{i,j})_j\) plus a bounded multiple of \(X_{i,0}\).  The transform coefficients are obtained by convolution with a kernel whose absolute values are \((1,2^{-1},2^{-2},\ldots)\).  The triangle inequality in \(\ell_2\), applied to the shifted copies of \(a\), gives directly
\[
 \lVert d\rVert_2
 \le\sum_{k\ge0}2^{-k}\lVert a\rVert_2
 =2\lVert a\rVert_2.
\]
Applying the same conditional exponential calculation used in the tangent-control proof at each innovation yields
\[
 E_P\exp\!\left(t\sum_ja_jX_{i,j}\right)
 \le \exp(Ct^2\lVert a\rVert_2^2)
\]
after enlarging \(C\) to absorb the bounded initial term.  The same bound applies when \(X_{i,j}\) is replaced by \(s_{ij}X_{i,j-1}\).

Take \(a_j=m_\ell^{-1}\), use
\[
 p_{ij}-E_Pp_{ij}=\frac14s_{ij}X_{i,j-1},
 \qquad
 \frac1{m_\ell}\sum_jA_{ij}-\frac12
 =\frac1{2m_\ell}\sum_jX_{i,j},
\]
and apply a union bound over the \(n_\ell\) rows.  We obtain
\[
 \max_i\left|\frac1{m_\ell}\sum_j(p_{ij}-E_Pp_{ij})\right|
 =O_P\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log n_\ell}{m_\ell}}\right)
\]
and, because projection onto the clipping interval is one-Lipschitz and fixes \(1/2\),
\[
 \max_i|\widehat p_i-1/2|
 =O_P\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log n_\ell}{m_\ell}}\right).
\]
This proves propensity-path stability and the asserted Markov concentration.

Substitution into the tangent gives the useful exact identity
\[
 H^{\mathrm B}_{ij}=X_{i,j-1}(4hs_{ij}+\theta).
\]
Thus, with \(\delta_\ell=\max_i|\widehat p_i-1/2|\),
\[
 \lVert D^{\mathrm B}\rVert
 \le \lVert D^{\mathrm B}\rVert_F
 \le C B\delta_\ell\sqrt{n_\ell m_\ell}
 =O_P(B\sqrt{n_\ell\log n_\ell}).
\]
Bounded aspect ratio makes the last expression \(o_P(\sqrt{n_\ell m_\ell})\).  Therefore
\[
 T^{\mathrm B}
 =h\mathbf1_{n_\ell}\mathbf1_{m_\ell}^\top
 +\theta(s_{ij})_{ij}+o_P(\sqrt{n_\ell m_\ell})
\]
in operator norm.

We now prove the rowwise conclusions.  We first record an elementary deterministic calculation specialized to the two matrices used here.  Suppose
\[
 S=s_0u_0v_0^\top+s_1u_1v_1^\top,
 \qquad s_0>s_1\ge0,
\]
where the second term may be absent, and let \((\widehat s,\widehat u,\widehat v)\) be the leading singular triple of \(S+Q\).  Put \(g=s_0-s_1\).  The symmetric-dilation eigen-equation, projected orthogonally to \((u_0^\top,v_0^\top)^\top/\sqrt2\), gives, whenever \(\lVert Q\rVert<g/2\),
\[
 \lVert\widehat u-u_0\rVert_2+
 \lVert\widehat v-v_0\rVert_2
 \le C\frac{\lVert Q\rVert}{g}
\]
after a common sign alignment.  Indeed, the inverse on that orthogonal complement has norm at most \((g-\lVert Q\rVert)^{-1}\), by the singular-value min--max inequality.  Expanding the row singular equation as
\[
 \widehat s(\widehat u-u_0)
 =u_0(s_0v_0^\top\widehat v-\widehat s)
 +s_1u_1v_1^\top\widehat v+Qv_0+Q(\widehat v-v_0)
\]
and using \(|\widehat s-s_0|\le\lVert Q\rVert\) consequently yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \lVert\widehat u-u_0\rVert_\infty
 \le C\bigg\{&\frac{\lVert Qv_0\rVert_\infty}{s_0}
 +(\lVert u_0\rVert_\infty+\lVert u_1\rVert_\infty)
   \frac{\lVert Q\rVert}{g}\\
 &+\frac{\max_i\lVert e_i^\top Q\rVert_2}{s_0}
   \frac{\lVert Q\rVert}{g}\bigg\}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
When the second spike is absent, its row-norm term is omitted.  Every denominator is positive on the displayed event; outside it we will use only statements for which \(\lVert Q\rVert/g=o_P(1)\).

For the recorded score let \(E=Z^{\mathrm{ipw}}-M\).  The conditional assignment calculation already used above gives
\[
 E_P[Z^{\mathrm{ipw}}_{ij}\mid\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1}]
 =\mu^1_{ij}-\mu^0_{ij}=M_{ij},
\]
so its row-major cells are martingale differences; here \(|E_{ij}|\le C B\) because the only propensities are \(1/4\) and \(3/4\).  For a fixed row, the scalar variables \(E_{ij}(v_1)_j\) are martingale differences bounded by \(CB/\sqrt{m_\ell}\), with total conditional variance at most \(CB^2\).  Conditional exponential iteration and a union bound over rows give
\[
 \lVert Ev_1\rVert_\infty=O_P(B\sqrt{\log n_\ell}).
\]
For deterministic unit vectors \(x\in\mathbb R^{n_\ell}\) and \(y\in\mathbb R^{m_\ell}\), the same iteration applied in row-major order gives
\[
 P\{|x^\top Ey|>t\}\le2\exp(-ct^2/B^2),
\]
because \(\sum_{i,j}x_i^2y_j^2=1\).  Choose maximal \(1/4\)-separated subsets of the two unit spheres.  Maximality makes them \(1/4\)-nets.  Comparing the disjoint radius-\(1/8\) Euclidean balls around their points with the radius-\(9/8\) ball containing them proves cardinalities at most \(9^{n_\ell}\) and \(9^{m_\ell}\).  Approximating arbitrary unit vectors by net points and moving the two approximation errors to the left gives
\[
 \lVert E\rVert
 \le2\max_{x,y\ \mathrm{in\ the\ product\ net}}|x^\top Ey|.
\]
A union bound therefore gives
\[
 \lVert E\rVert=O_P(B\sqrt{n_\ell+m_\ell}),
\]
while boundedness gives \(\max_i\lVert e_i^\top E\rVert_2\le CB\sqrt{m_\ell}\).  Apply the deterministic row calculation with \(s_0=g=\theta\sqrt{n_\ell m_\ell}\).  Bounded aspect ratio yields
\[
 \sqrt{n_\ell}\min_{\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}}
 \lVert\epsilon\widehat U^{\mathrm I}-u_1\rVert_\infty
 =O_P\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log n_\ell}{n_\ell}}\right).
\]
Substitution into the frontier definition gives
\[
 b_{n_\ell m_\ell}
 =1\wedge\left\{
 \frac B\theta\sqrt{\log(en_\ell)}\,
 q^{-1/2}\sqrt{n_\ell^{-1}+n_\ell m_\ell^{-2}}
 \right\}
 \asymp\sqrt{\frac{\log n_\ell}{n_\ell}},
\]
so \(\mathcal L(\widehat U^{\mathrm I},U)=O_P(b_{n_\ell m_\ell})\).

For the blind score, the ratio expansion gives
\[
 Z^{\mathrm B}-M^{\mathrm B}
 =(Z^{\bar p}-M^{\mathrm B})+D^{\mathrm B}+R^{\mathrm B}.
\]
The first summand is a bounded matrix martingale difference array.  Repeating the bilinear-form exponential iteration and product-net argument from the recorded score, and then using the tangent bound above and Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:blind\mbox{-}ratio\mbox{-}expansion}\), gives
\[
 \lVert Z^{\mathrm B}-M^{\mathrm B}\rVert
 =O_P(B\sqrt{n_\ell}+B\sqrt{n_\ell\log n_\ell}+B\log n_\ell)
 =O_P(B\sqrt{n_\ell\log n_\ell}).
\]
For the constant vector \(v_0\), scalar martingale concentration gives
\(\lVert(Z^{\bar p}-M^{\mathrm B})v_0\rVert_\infty
=O_P(B\sqrt{\log n_\ell})\).  The entrywise residual formula in the ratio-expansion proof, together with \(\delta_\ell=O_P(\sqrt{\log n_\ell/m_\ell})\), gives
\[
 \max_i\lVert e_i^\top D^{\mathrm B}\rVert_2
 +\max_i\lVert e_i^\top R^{\mathrm B}\rVert_2
 =O_P(B\sqrt{\log n_\ell}).
\]
Hence
\[
 \lVert(Z^{\mathrm B}-M^{\mathrm B})v_0\rVert_\infty
 =O_P(B\sqrt{\log n_\ell}),
 \qquad
 \max_i\lVert e_i^\top(Z^{\mathrm B}-M^{\mathrm B})\rVert_2
 =O_P(B\sqrt{m_\ell}).
\]
Apply the deterministic row calculation with
\[
 s_0=h\sqrt{n_\ell m_\ell},\quad
 s_1=\theta\sqrt{n_\ell m_\ell},\quad
 g=(h-\theta)\sqrt{n_\ell m_\ell}.
\]
Since \(h-\theta>0\) is fixed, all three rowwise terms vanish after multiplication by \(\sqrt{n_\ell}\), and in fact
\[
 \sqrt{n_\ell}\min_{\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}}
 \lVert\epsilon\widehat U^{\mathrm B}-u_0\rVert_\infty
 =O_P\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log n_\ell}{n_\ell}}\right)=o_P(1).
\]
The identical calculation with \(Q=D^{\mathrm B}\) shows
\[
 \sqrt{n_\ell}\min_{\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}}
 \lVert\epsilon U^{\mathrm B}-u_0\rVert_\infty=o_P(1).
\]

Finally, the normalized quotient distance between \(u_0\) and \(u_1\) is exactly two.  For either global sign, half of the even number of rows differ by \(2/\sqrt{n_\ell}\), so
\[
 \sqrt{n_\ell}\min_{\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}}
 \lVert\epsilon u_0-u_1\rVert_\infty=2.
\]
The quotient maximum-row distance obeys the triangle and reverse-triangle inequalities: compose the two optimizing signs and use the ordinary \(\ell_\infty\) triangle inequality.  Combining the last display with the three rowwise approximations proves
\[
 \rho_{n_\ell m_\ell}\longrightarrow2,
 \qquad
 \widehat\rho_{n_\ell m_\ell}\longrightarrow2,
 \qquad
 \mathcal L(\widehat U^{\mathrm B},U)\longrightarrow2
\]
in probability.  All assertions are about the explicitly defined blind procedure and the displayed witness law; no property of a published normalization method is used.
