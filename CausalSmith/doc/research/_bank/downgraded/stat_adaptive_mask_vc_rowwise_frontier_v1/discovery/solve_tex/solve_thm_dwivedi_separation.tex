Put \(n=n_\ell=2\ell\) and \(m=m_\ell=2\lceil\gamma\ell\rceil\).  We first verify membership of the Markov witness in the predictable class, condition by condition.  Since
\[
 \frac{m_\ell}{n_\ell}=\frac{\lceil\gamma\ell\rceil}{\ell}\longrightarrow\gamma
 \quad\text{and}\quad c_-<\gamma<c_+,
\]
there is \(\ell_0\) such that \(c_-\le m_\ell/n_\ell\le c_+\) whenever \(\ell\ge\ell_0\).  This proves the condition in \(\mathrm{ass:aspect\mbox{-}ratio}\) along the witness path.

Fix a cell \((i,j)\).  If \(j=1\), then \(A_{i0}=A_i^{\mathrm{init}}\) belongs to \(\mathbb G_0\); if \(j\ge2\), then \(A_{i,j-1}\) has been recorded before global step \(o(i,j)\).  Hence \(A_{i,j-1}\), \(p_{ij}\), and both \(Y_{ij}(0)\) and \(Y_{ij}(1)\) in \(\mathrm{def:markov\mbox{-}witness}\) are \(\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1}\)-measurable.  Consequently,
\[
 \sigma\!\left(\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1},Y_{ij}(0),Y_{ij}(1)\right)
 =\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1}
 \quad\text{up to completion}.
\]
By the definition of the recorded propensity and by the witness construction,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P\!\left(A_{ij}=1\mid\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1},Y_{ij}(0),Y_{ij}(1)\right)
 &=P\!\left(A_{ij}=1\mid\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1}\right)\\
 &=p_{ij}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(\mathrm{ass:sequential\mbox{-}randomization}\) holds.  Because each product \((-1)^{i+j}(2A_{i,j-1}-1)\) is either \(-1\) or \(1\),
\[
 p_{ij}\in\left\{\frac14,\frac34\right\}.
\]
The strict inequality \(q<1/4\) therefore gives \(q\le p_{ij}\le1-q\), which is exactly \(\mathrm{ass:overlap}\).  Moreover, for \(a\in\{0,1\}\), the triangle inequality and the amplitude restriction in \(\mathrm{def:markov\mbox{-}witness}\) give
\[
 |Y_{ij}(a)|\le |C_{ij}|+\frac{\theta}{2}
 =h+\frac{\theta}{2}\le B.
\]
This verifies \(\mathrm{ass:bounded\mbox{-}outcomes}\).

Since the two potential outcomes are measurable with respect to the conditioning field, their conditional means equal the outcomes themselves.  Subtracting the two displayed formulas in \(\mathrm{def:markov\mbox{-}witness}\) yields
\[
 M_{ij}=\mu^1_{ij}-\mu^0_{ij}=\theta(-1)^{i+j}.
\]
Let \(s\in\mathbb R^n\) and \(t\in\mathbb R^m\) have coordinates \(s_i=(-1)^i\) and \(t_j=(-1)^j\).  Then
\[
 M=\theta st^\top
   =\left(\frac{s}{\sqrt n}\right)
      \bigl(\theta\sqrt{nm}\bigr)
      \left(\frac{t}{\sqrt m}\right)^\top .
\]
Because \(\theta>0\), this is a singular-value decomposition with rank one, sole singular value \(\theta\sqrt{nm}\), and singular vectors \(U=s/\sqrt n\) and \(V=t/\sqrt m\), up to their common harmless sign.  It follows equation by equation that
\[
 \lVert U\rVert_{2,\infty}=n^{-1/2}=\sqrt{\frac{1\cdot1}{n}},
 \qquad
 \lVert V\rVert_{2,\infty}=m^{-1/2}=\sqrt{\frac{1\cdot1}{m}},
\]
\[
 \frac{\sigma_1(M)}{\sigma_1(M)}=1,
 \qquad
 \sigma_1(M)=\theta\sqrt{nm}.
\]
Thus \(\mathrm{ass:exact\mbox{-}rank}\), \(\mathrm{ass:left\mbox{-}incoherence}\), \(\mathrm{ass:right\mbox{-}incoherence}\), \(\mathrm{ass:condition\mbox{-}number}\), and \(\mathrm{ass:signal\mbox{-}floor}\) hold with \((r,\mu,K,\lambda_{nm})=(1,1,1,\theta\sqrt{nm})\).  Together with the design, boundedness, and aspect checks above, the membership definition \(\mathrm{def:predictable\mbox{-}class}\) gives
\[
 W_\ell\in
 \mathfrak P^{\mathrm{pred}}_{n_\ell,m_\ell}
 \left(1,1,1,q,B,\theta\sqrt{n_\ell m_\ell}\right)
 \quad\text{for every }\ell\ge\ell_0.
\]

We next prove that this member is outside the published Dwivedi model.  For every \(j\ge2\), \(\mathrm{def:markov\mbox{-}witness}\) gives
\[
 \mu^1_{ij}
 =h(-1)^{i+j}(2A_{i,j-1}-1)+\frac{\theta}{2}(-1)^{i+j}.
\]
Changing \(A_{i,j-1}\) from zero to one changes this mean by \(2h(-1)^{i+j}\ne0\).  Both values of \(A_{i,j-1}\) have positive conditional probability because its recorded propensity lies in \(\{1/4,3/4\}\).  Hence this is genuine, rather than null-set, dependence of the current arm mean on a past treatment.  The published model restriction recorded in \(\mathrm{lem:dwivedi\mbox{-}exogenous\mbox{-}model\mbox{-}scope}\) requires the current counterfactual to be generated from exogenous unit and time factors and exogenous noise and expressly excludes dependence on past treatment.  The Markov witness therefore cannot be generated by that model.

For the converse separation, consider \(\mathrm{def:dwivedi\mbox{-}full\mbox{-}rank\mbox{-}witness}\).  On \([0,1]^2\),
\[
 0\le f^{\mathrm D}_1(u,v)=e^{uv}\le e,
 \qquad
 \left|\frac{\partial}{\partial u}e^{uv}\right|=ve^{uv}\le e,
 \qquad
 \left|\frac{\partial}{\partial v}e^{uv}\right|=ue^{uv}\le e.
\]
The one-dimensional mean-value theorem, applied once in each coordinate, therefore shows that \(f^{\mathrm D}_1\) is bounded and coordinatewise \(e\)-Lipschitz; \(f^{\mathrm D}_0=0\) is bounded and coordinatewise Lipschitz with constant zero.  The independent uniform factors have compact support and density one.  Thus these functions and factors satisfy the nonlinear-factor and factor-distribution conditions recorded in \(\mathrm{lem:dwivedi\mbox{-}nonlinear\mbox{-}extension\mbox{-}scope}\).  The remaining conditions of that cited theorem are simultaneously satisfiable: take an independent constant one-half treatment policy, independent bounded centered noise, and independent uniform factors for the constant control arm.  These choices preserve the two arm means and hence preserve
\[
 M^{\mathrm D}_{ij}=e^{u_i^{\mathrm D}v_j^{\mathrm D}}.
\]
This constructs a law in the published nonlinear-factor setting whose effect matrix is \(M^{\mathrm D}\).

It remains to compute its rank.  Independent variables with continuous uniform laws have no ties with probability one; a finite union bound over pairs shows that all \(u_i^{\mathrm D}\) are distinct and all \(v_j^{\mathrm D}\) are distinct almost surely.  We use the following elementary zero-count argument on this probability-one event.  If \(x_1<\cdots<x_d\) are distinct real numbers, then every nonzero function
\[
 F(y)=\sum_{k=1}^d c_k e^{x_k y}
\]
has at most \(d-1\) distinct real zeros.  Indeed, the assertion is immediate for \(d=1\).  For the induction step, suppose \(F\) had \(d\) distinct zeros and put
\[
 H(y)=e^{-x_1y}F(y)=c_1+\sum_{k=2}^d c_k e^{(x_k-x_1)y}.
\]
Rolle's theorem would give at least \(d-1\) distinct zeros of
\[
 H'(y)=\sum_{k=2}^d c_k(x_k-x_1)e^{(x_k-x_1)y}.
\]
The induction hypothesis forces \(c_k(x_k-x_1)=0\) for every \(k\ge2\).  Distinctness gives \(x_k-x_1\ne0\), so \(c_k=0\) for \(k\ge2\); the zeros of the constant \(H=c_1\) then force \(c_1=0\), contradicting nontriviality.  This proves the zero-count assertion.

Let \(d=\min(n,m)\), choose any \(d\) rows and \(d\) columns of \(M^{\mathrm D}\), and permute them so that the selected \(u\)'s and \(v\)'s are increasing.  If this square submatrix were singular, there would be coefficients \(c_1,\ldots,c_d\), not all zero, for which
\[
 \sum_{k=1}^d c_k e^{u_k^{\mathrm D}v_j^{\mathrm D}}=0,
 \qquad j=1,\ldots,d.
\]
The corresponding exponential sum would have the \(d\) distinct zeros \(v_1^{\mathrm D},\ldots,v_d^{\mathrm D}\), contradicting the zero-count assertion.  The submatrix is therefore invertible, so
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(M^{\mathrm D})\ge d.
\]
The reverse inequality holds for every \(n\)-by-\(m\) matrix, and hence
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(M^{\mathrm D})=\min(n,m)
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
For a fixed \(r\), eventually \(\min(n_\ell,m_\ell)>r\), so this source-compatible law violates \(\mathrm{ass:exact\mbox{-}rank}\) and is excluded from \(\mathfrak P^{\mathrm{pred}}_{n_\ell,m_\ell}(r,\mu,K,q,B,\lambda)\), independently of the other class restrictions.

We have exhibited a member of the predictable low-rank class outside the published factor class and a member of the published nonlinear-factor class outside every fixed-rank predictable class along the dimension path.  Thus neither model class contains the other.  Finally, \(\mathrm{lem:dwivedi\mbox{-}nearest\mbox{-}neighbor\mbox{-}scope}\) records that the cited Theorem 3.1 and equations (17)--(18) bound the squared error of a scalar nearest-neighbor estimate at a fixed unit and time, whereas the present frontier concerns normalized maximum-row loss for a left singular space.  The differing targets together with the two explicit class witnesses prove that neither uniform guarantee is a specialization of the other, which is the claimed non-representability in both directions.
