Restrict attention to even integers \(n\) tending to infinity and put \(m=n\), \(r=1\), \(\mu=2\), \(K=1\), and \(\lambda_{nn}=cBn\), where \(0<c\le 1/2\) is fixed.  Let \(C=B/4\).  We construct one law for each such \(n\).  All assignments have support \(\{0,1\}\), are mutually independent, and satisfy \(A_{ij}\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\).  Thus the recorded propensities are \(p_{ij}=1/2\), which obey the overlap inequalities \(q\le p_{ij}\le1-q\) because \(q<1/2\).

In the first row set
\[
Y_{1j}(1)=Y_{1j}(0)=C,
\qquad
S_j=2A_{1j}-1.
\]
After the first row has been recorded, define
\[
v=n^{-1/2}(S_1,\ldots,S_n)^\top,
\qquad
u=(0,(n-1)^{-1/2},\ldots,(n-1)^{-1/2})^\top.
\]
For every \(i\ge2\), set
\[
Y_{ij}(0)=0,
\qquad
Y_{ij}(1)=\lambda_{nn}u_iv_j.
\]
These later potential outcomes are measurable with respect to \(\mathbb G_n\), hence with respect to every pre-cell field \(\mathbb G_{o(i,j)-1}\) for \(i\ge2\), by the row-major filtration definition.  At a first-row cell the two current potential outcomes are constant, and at every later cell they are functions only of assignments already recorded.  Since the current assignment is an independent fair coin conditional on the pre-cell field, conditioning additionally on the current potential outcomes does not change its law.  Therefore the sequential-randomization assumption holds with \(p_{ij}=1/2\).

The bounded-outcome assumption also holds.  The first-row magnitude is \(C\le B\), the control outcomes below the first row vanish, and, for \(i\ge2\),
\[
|Y_{ij}(1)|
=\frac{cBn}{\sqrt{n(n-1)}}
=cB\sqrt{\frac n{n-1}}
\le B,
\]
where the last inequality follows from \(c\le1/2\) and \(n\ge2\).  The predictable conditional means are \(\mu^1_{1j}=\mu^0_{1j}=C\), while for \(i\ge2\) they are \(\mu^0_{ij}=0\) and \(\mu^1_{ij}=\lambda_{nn}u_iv_j\).  Consequently, by the definition \(M=\mu^1-\mu^0\),
\[
M=\lambda_{nn}uv^\top.
\]
Both \(u\) and \(v\) have Euclidean norm one.  Thus \(M\) has exact rank one, its sole nonzero singular value equals \(\lambda_{nn}\), and its condition number is one.  Moreover,
\[
\lVert u\rVert_\infty=(n-1)^{-1/2}\le\sqrt{2/n},
\qquad
\lVert v\rVert_\infty=n^{-1/2}\le\sqrt{2/n}.
\]
Hence the left- and right-incoherence conditions hold with \(\mu=2\), the signal-floor condition holds with equality, and the aspect-ratio condition holds because \(m/n=1\) and \(c_-<1<c_+\).  We have therefore verified every member property in the definition of \(\mathfrak P^{\mathrm{pred}}_{n,n}(1,2,1,q,B,\lambda)\).

It remains to analyze the estimator.  By the recorded-score definition, the first score row is
\[
Z^{\mathrm{ipw}}_{1j}=2CS_j=a_nv_j,
\qquad
a_n=2C\sqrt n.
\]
For \(i\ge2\), let \(\xi_{ij}=2A_{ij}-1\).  Since the control outcome is zero,
\[
Z^{\mathrm{ipw}}_{ij}=2A_{ij}M_{ij}=M_{ij}+\xi_{ij}M_{ij}.
\]
It follows that
\[
Z^{\mathrm{ipw}}=(\lambda_{nn}u+a_ne_1)v^\top+F,
\]
where \(F_{1j}=0\) and \(F_{ij}=\xi_{ij}\lambda_{nn}u_iv_j\) for \(i\ge2\).  Conditional on the first row, the variables \((\xi_{ij}:i\ge2,1\le j\le n)\) are independent Rademacher variables and \(u,v\) are fixed.

We next prove the required operator-norm control rather than assuming it.  For deterministic unit vectors \(x,y\in\mathbb R^n\), conditional independence and the Rademacher exponential bound give
\[
P\!\left(\left.|x^\top Fy|>t\,\right|\mathbb G_n\right)
\le
2\exp\!\left{-\frac{t^2}{2\sum_{i=2}^n\sum_{j=1}^n
\lambda_{nn}^2u_i^2v_j^2x_i^2y_j^2}\right}
\le
2\exp\!\left{-\frac{n(n-1)t^2}{2\lambda_{nn}^2}\right}.
\]
Indeed, the coefficient-square sum in the denominator is at most
\[
\frac{\lambda_{nn}^2}{n(n-1)}
\left(\sum_{i=2}^n x_i^2\right)
\left(\sum_{j=1}^n y_j^2\right)
\le\frac{\lambda_{nn}^2}{n(n-1)}.
\]
A maximal \(1/4\)-separated subset of the Euclidean unit sphere is a \(1/4\)-net and has cardinality at most \(9^n\), by comparing the disjoint radius-\(1/8\) balls around its points with the radius-\(9/8\) ball.  If \(\mathcal N\) is such a net, two applications of the triangle inequality yield
\[
\lVert F\rVert\le2\max_{x,y\in\mathcal N}|x^\top Fy|.
\]
Therefore, for every fixed \(D>0\), the preceding tail bound and a union bound imply
\[
P\!\left(\left.\lVert F\rVert>D\frac{\lambda_{nn}}{\sqrt n}\,\right|\mathbb G_n\right)
\le
2\exp\!\left\{2n\log 9-\frac{D^2(n-1)}8\right\}.
\]
Choosing \(D\) so that \(D^2/8>2\log9\) and then integrating the conditional probability proves
\[
\delta_n:=\lVert F\rVert=O_P(\lambda_{nn}/\sqrt n)=o_P(\lambda_{nn}).
\]

Put \(w_n=\lambda_{nn}u+a_ne_1\) and \(s_n=\lVert w_n\rVert_2=(\lambda_{nn}^2+a_n^2)^{1/2}\), where the equality uses \(u_1=0\).  Let \((\widehat u,\widehat v,\widehat\sigma)\) be a leading singular triple of \(Z^{\mathrm{ipw}}\), reorienting both singular vectors so that \(v^\top\widehat v\ge0\).  This reorientation does not change the reported one-dimensional singular subspace or the loss.  The variational definition of the operator norm and the triangle inequality give
\[
\widehat\sigma
\ge\lVert Z^{\mathrm{ipw}}v\rVert_2
\ge s_n-\delta_n,
\qquad
\widehat\sigma
\le s_n+\delta_n.
\]
On the other hand,
\[
\widehat\sigma
=\lVert Z^{\mathrm{ipw}}\widehat v\rVert_2
\le s_n|v^\top\widehat v|+\delta_n.
\]
Combining these inequalities yields
\[
v^\top\widehat v\ge1-2\delta_n/s_n\longrightarrow1
\quad\text{in probability}.
\]
Also \(a_n/\lambda_{nn}=2C/(cB\sqrt n)\to0\), so \(s_n/\lambda_{nn}\to1\), and the two-sided bound on \(\widehat\sigma\) gives
\[
\widehat\sigma/\lambda_{nn}\longrightarrow1
\quad\text{in probability}.
\]

The first row of \(F\) is identically zero.  Hence the first coordinate of the singular-vector equation \(Z^{\mathrm{ipw}}\widehat v=\widehat\sigma\widehat u\) is the exact identity
\[
\widehat\sigma\widehat u_1=a_n v^\top\widehat v.
\]
Because the target vector has \(u_1=0\), the preceding limits imply
\[
\sqrt n\,|\widehat u_1-u_1|
=\frac{\sqrt n\,a_n}{\widehat\sigma}|v^\top\widehat v|
\longrightarrow\frac{2C}{cB}
\quad\text{in probability}.
\]
For rank one, the orthogonal alignment set is \(\mathbb O_1=\{-1,1\}\).  Since \(u_1=0\), either alignment has first-row error \(|\widehat u_1|\).  The rowwise-loss definition therefore gives
\[
\mathcal L(\widehat U^{\mathrm I},U)
\ge Q_n,
\qquad
Q_n:=\sqrt{n/2}\,|\widehat u_1|
\longrightarrow d:=\frac{\sqrt2 C}{cB}>0
\quad\text{in probability}.
\]
Taking \(c_0=d/2\), the inclusion \(\{Q_n>c_0\}\subseteq\{\mathcal L(\widehat U^{\mathrm I},U)>c_0\}\) yields
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}P\{\mathcal L(\widehat U^{\mathrm I},U)>c_0\}=1>0.
\]

Finally, the geometric-term and reference-scale definitions give, on this square rank-one path,
\[
b^0_{nn}=2q^{-1/2}\sqrt{2/n}
\]
and hence
\[
b_{nn}
=1\wedge\left\{
\frac{Bn}{cBn}\sqrt{\log(en)}\,
2q^{-1/2}\sqrt{2/n}
\right\}
=1\wedge\left\{\frac{2\sqrt2}{c\sqrt q}
\sqrt{\frac{\log(en)}n}\right\}
\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus the same law sequence belongs to the frozen predictable class, has vanishing lower/reference scale, and gives a nonvanishing normalized maximum-row error for recorded-propensity IPW--SVD.  The obstruction is specifically the lack of predictability of the future singular factors relative to earlier score increments; it does not assert a lower bound for every estimator.
