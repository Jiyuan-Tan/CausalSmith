%%% FIELD padding-statement
Let \((G,\Sigma,e)\) be a triple in the promise domain underlying \(\mathsf{NUMEDGE}_2\), and suppose that every directed weak component of \(G\) is an isolated vertex, a single directed edge, or a two-parent in-star.  There is a deterministic polynomial-bit transformation producing \((\widetilde G,\widetilde\Sigma,e)\) such that \(\widetilde G\) is acyclic of depth one, has maximum directed indegree three, maximum directed outdegree one, directed component size at most four, and \(K_{\widetilde G}=0\); moreover, \(\widetilde\Sigma\) is rational and positive definite, and it is strictly diagonally dominant whenever \(\Sigma\) is.  Restriction to the original vertices is a bijection \(\mathcal F_{\widetilde G}(\widetilde\Sigma)\to\mathcal F_G(\Sigma)\), and hence
\[
\mathcal A_e(\widetilde G,\widetilde\Sigma)=\mathcal A_e(G,\Sigma).
\]

%%% FIELD padding-proof
Put
\[
S=\{v\in V:|\operatorname{pa}_D(v)|=2\},\qquad h=|S|,
\]
and introduce pairwise distinct new vertices \(R=\{r_v:v\in S\}\), disjoint from \(V\).  Define
\[
\widetilde V=V\mathbin{\dot\cup}R,
\qquad
\widetilde D=D\cup\{(r_v,v):v\in S\},
\qquad
\widetilde B=B.
\]
Here the last equality regards \(B\) as a set of pairs contained in \(V\), so every pair involving a new vertex is absent from \(\widetilde B\).  Finally, in the vertex order \(V,R\), set
\[
\widetilde\Sigma=
\begin{pmatrix}
\Sigma&0\\
0&I_h
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
These operations append at most \(n\) vertices and entries from \(\{0,1\}\), so they have deterministic polynomial bit complexity in the binary input length.  Rationality is immediate from rationality of \(\Sigma\).  For every nonzero \((a,b)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^h\), positive definiteness of the promised \(\Sigma\) gives
\[
\begin{pmatrix}a\\b\end{pmatrix}^{\!\mathsf T}
\widetilde\Sigma
\begin{pmatrix}a\\b\end{pmatrix}
=a^{\mathsf T}\Sigma a+b^{\mathsf T}b>0.
\]
Thus \(\widetilde\Sigma\succ0\).  If \(\Sigma\) is strictly diagonally dominant, every old row of \(\widetilde\Sigma\) has the same diagonal and off-diagonal absolute row sum as before, while every new row has diagonal one and off-diagonal absolute row sum zero.  Hence strict diagonal dominance is also preserved.

Every supported coefficient matrix on \(\widetilde G\) has a unique block representation
\[
\widetilde L=
\begin{pmatrix}
L&0\\
U&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
U_{r_v,j}=
\begin{cases}
u_v,&j=v,\\
0,&j\ne v,
\end{cases}
\]
where \(L\) is supported on \(D\).  Direct block multiplication using the definition of the induced disturbance covariance gives
\[
I_{n+h}-\widetilde L
=
\begin{pmatrix}
I_n-L&0\\
-U&I_h
\end{pmatrix}
\]
and therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Omega_{\widetilde G}(\widetilde L)
&=(I_{n+h}-\widetilde L)^{\mathsf T}
  \widetilde\Sigma
  (I_{n+h}-\widetilde L)\\
&=
\begin{pmatrix}
(I_n-L)^{\mathsf T}\Sigma(I_n-L)+U^{\mathsf T}U&-U^{\mathsf T}\\
-U&I_h
\end{pmatrix}\\
&=
\begin{pmatrix}
\Omega(L)+U^{\mathsf T}U&-U^{\mathsf T}\\
-U&I_h
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{aligned}
\]
For each \(v\in S\), the pair \(\{r_v,v\}\) is absent from \(\widetilde B\).  The corresponding defining equation of \(\mathcal F_{\widetilde G}(\widetilde\Sigma)\) is, by the lower-left block in the preceding display,
\[
0=[\Omega_{\widetilde G}(\widetilde L)]_{r_v,v}=-U_{r_v,v}=-u_v.
\]
Consequently every point of the enlarged fiber has \(U=0\).  Its old-old disturbance block then equals \(\Omega(L)\), while every off-diagonal entry involving a new vertex is zero.  By the definition of the coefficient fiber, it follows that
\[
\widetilde L\in\mathcal F_{\widetilde G}(\widetilde\Sigma)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
U=0\ \text{ and }\ L\in\mathcal F_G(\Sigma).
\]
Thus restriction \(\widetilde L\mapsto L\) is a bijection, with inverse \(L\mapsto\left(\begin{smallmatrix}L&0\\0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\).  In particular, nonemptiness of the original promised fiber implies nonemptiness of the enlarged fiber.  The queried edge \(e\) is an old edge and its coefficient is unchanged by both maps; the definition of the attainable set therefore yields
\[
\mathcal A_e(\widetilde G,\widetilde\Sigma)
=\mathcal A_e(G,\Sigma).
\]

It remains to verify the graph restrictions.  Each new vertex is a root with one outgoing edge, so adding it cannot create a directed cycle and preserves directed depth one.  Every old vertex of indegree two acquires exactly one new parent and hence has indegree three; vertices of indegree zero or one are unchanged, and new vertices have indegree zero.  Therefore no vertex has indegree exactly two, so \(K_{\widetilde G}=0\), and the maximum indegree is three.  A two-parent in-star becomes a three-parent in-star on four vertices; the other components are unchanged.  Private roots imply maximum directed outdegree one.  This proves all asserted properties without deleting or selecting any branch of the original fiber.

%%% FIELD resolution-statement
For every fixed \(k\in\mathbb N_0\), pointwise numerical edge identification on the literal class \(K_G\le k\) is promise \(\forall\mathbb R\)-complete.  Hardness holds already for the restriction of \(\mathsf{NUMEDGE}_3\) having \(K_G=0\), rational strictly diagonally dominant positive-definite covariance, and a depth-one directed graph of maximum directed outdegree one whose directed weak components are isolated vertices, single directed edges, or three-parent in-stars.  Consequently, if \(N\) denotes total binary input length, an algorithm of bit complexity \(f(k)\operatorname{poly}(N)\) for the class posed in the question would imply \(\mathsf P=\forall\mathbb R\).  Thus \(\mathsf{ParamHandle}\), as presently defined, does not yield a fixed-parameter algorithm for that literal class: \(K_G\) does not count vertices of indegree at least three.  The completeness and obstruction hold for exact fibers, including singular fibers, and require no genericity assumption.

%%% FIELD resolution-proof
The fan-in-two completeness theorem supplies a promise-preserving polynomial-time many-one reduction from the \(\forall\mathbb R\)-complete complemented planted-selector language to \(\mathsf{NUMEDGE}_2\).  Every output of that reduction has rational strictly diagonally dominant positive-definite covariance, and its directed weak components are isolated vertices, single directed edges, or two-parent in-stars; it also has directed depth one and maximum directed outdegree one.

Apply the padding lemma to each output \((G_Q,\Sigma_Q,e_Q)\).  It produces \((\widetilde G_Q,\widetilde\Sigma_Q,e_Q)\) in deterministic polynomial bit complexity, with maximum directed indegree three and \(K_{\widetilde G_Q}=0\), while preserving strict diagonal dominance, positive definiteness, acyclicity, and feasibility.  The exact fiber bijection in that lemma preserves the queried projection, so
\[
\mathcal A_{e_Q}(\widetilde G_Q,\widetilde\Sigma_Q)
=\mathcal A_{e_Q}(G_Q,\Sigma_Q).
\]
By the promise-problem definition, the two triples have the same YES/NO answer because each is a YES instance exactly when its displayed attainable set is a singleton.  Composing the compiler reduction with the padding transformation is therefore a promise-preserving many-one reduction.  The fan-in-two theorem and the equality above prove \(\forall\mathbb R\)-hardness even on the stated subclass with \(K_G=0\).  Notice that this is equality of complete fibers followed by equality of their queried projections; it does not discard poles, singular components, or exceptional solutions.

The published unrestricted numerical edge-identification membership result places the unrestricted promise problem in \(\forall\mathbb R\).  Restricting its promise domain by the polynomial-time decidable conditions \(K_G\le k\) and, for the smaller hard subclass, maximum directed indegree at most three, preserves membership: the same universal real formula decides the singleton-fiber predicate on the restricted promise.  Hence the literal class \(K_G\le k\), and also its maximum-indegree-three restriction, belong to promise \(\forall\mathbb R\).  Since the \(K_G=0\) hard family lies in every class \(K_G\le k\) for \(k\ge0\), each fixed-\(k\) restriction is promise \(\forall\mathbb R\)-complete.

Finally, suppose an exact algorithm had running time \(f(k)\operatorname{poly}(N)\), where \(N\) is total encoded bit length.  On the hard subfamily, \(k=0\), so its running time would be the polynomial \(f(0)\operatorname{poly}(N)\).  Together with \(\forall\mathbb R\)-hardness this would place every problem in \(\forall\mathbb R\) in \(\mathsf P\), and the standard inclusion \(\mathsf P\subseteq\forall\mathbb R\) would give \(\mathsf P=\forall\mathbb R\).  The obstruction to using \(\mathsf{ParamHandle}\) is visible directly at \(K_G=0\): it exposes no coefficient belonging to a two-parent vertex, while the padded hard instance retains three-parent vertices and an exactly bijective hard fiber.  This resolves the question in favor of fixed-parameter hardness for the parameter and class as stated.
