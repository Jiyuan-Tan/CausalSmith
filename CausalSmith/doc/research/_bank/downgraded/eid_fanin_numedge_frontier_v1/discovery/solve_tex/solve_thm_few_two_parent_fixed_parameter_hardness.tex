Let \((G,\Sigma,e)\) be a triple in the promise domain underlying \(\mathsf{NUMEDGE}_2\), and suppose that every directed weak component of \(G\) is an isolated vertex, a single directed edge, or a two-parent in-star. There is a deterministic polynomial-bit transformation producing \((\widetilde G,\widetilde\Sigma,e)\) such that \(\widetilde G\) is acyclic of depth one, has maximum directed indegree at most three, maximum directed outdegree one, directed component size at most four, and \(K_{\widetilde G}=0\); moreover, \(\widetilde\Sigma\) is rational and positive definite, and it is strictly diagonally dominant whenever \(\Sigma\) is. Restriction to the original vertices is a bijection \(\mathcal F_{\widetilde G}(\widetilde\Sigma)\to\mathcal F_G(\Sigma)\), and hence
\[
\mathcal A_e(\widetilde G,\widetilde\Sigma)=\mathcal A_e(G,\Sigma).
\]
%%% FIELD padding-proof
Let
\[
S=\{v\in V:|\operatorname{pa}_D(v)|=2\},
\qquad h=|S|,
\]
and introduce pairwise distinct vertices \(R=\{r_v:v\in S\}\) disjoint from \(V\). Define
\[
\widetilde V=V\mathbin{\dot\cup}R,
\qquad
\widetilde D=D\cup\{(r_v,v):v\in S\},
\qquad
\widetilde B=B.
\]
In the last equality, \(B\) is viewed as a set of pairs contained in \(V\), so every pair containing a new vertex is absent from \(\widetilde B\). In the vertex order \(V,R\), define
\[
\widetilde\Sigma=
\begin{pmatrix}
\Sigma&0\\
0&I_h
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Because the input lies in the promise domain of \(\mathsf{NUMEDGE}_2\), \(\Sigma\) is rational, \(\Sigma\succ0\), and \(\mathcal F_G(\Sigma)\ne\varnothing\), by \(\mathrm{def:promise\mbox{-}problem}\). The construction appends at most \(n\) vertices and uses only entries from \(\{0,1\}\) outside the supplied matrix, so its output size and running time are polynomial in the binary input length. Rationality of \(\widetilde\Sigma\) follows immediately.

For any nonzero \((a,b)\in\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^h\), positive definiteness of \(\Sigma\) gives
\[
\begin{pmatrix}a\\b\end{pmatrix}^{\!\mathsf T}
\widetilde\Sigma
\begin{pmatrix}a\\b\end{pmatrix}
=a^{\mathsf T}\Sigma a+b^{\mathsf T}b>0.
\]
Thus \(\widetilde\Sigma\succ0\). If \(\Sigma\) is strictly diagonally dominant, each old row of \(\widetilde\Sigma\) has the same diagonal entry and the same sum of absolute off-diagonal entries as before, while each new row has diagonal entry one and off-diagonal absolute row sum zero. Hence strict diagonal dominance is preserved.

Every matrix supported on \(\widetilde D\) has a unique block representation
\[
\widetilde L=
\begin{pmatrix}
L&0\\
U&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
U_{r_v,j}=
\begin{cases}
u_v,&j=v,\\
0,&j\ne v,
\end{cases}
\]
where \(L\) is supported on \(D\). Direct block multiplication using \(\mathrm{def:fiber}\) yields
\[
I_{n+h}-\widetilde L=
\begin{pmatrix}
I_n-L&0\\
-U&I_h
\end{pmatrix}
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
(I_{n+h}-\widetilde L)^{\mathsf T}
\widetilde\Sigma
(I_{n+h}-\widetilde L)
&=
\begin{pmatrix}
(I_n-L)^{\mathsf T}\Sigma(I_n-L)+U^{\mathsf T}U&-U^{\mathsf T}\\
-U&I_h
\end{pmatrix}\\
&=
\begin{pmatrix}
\Omega(L)+U^{\mathsf T}U&-U^{\mathsf T}\\
-U&I_h
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{aligned}
\]
For each \(v\in S\), the pair \(\{r_v,v\}\) is absent from \(\widetilde B\). The corresponding missing-bidirected-edge equation is therefore
\[
0=\bigl[(I_{n+h}-\widetilde L)^{\mathsf T}
\widetilde\Sigma(I_{n+h}-\widetilde L)\bigr]_{r_v,v}
=-U_{r_v,v}=-u_v.
\]
Hence every \(\widetilde L\in\mathcal F_{\widetilde G}(\widetilde\Sigma)\) has \(U=0\). Conversely, if \(U=0\), the old--old block in the displayed product is \(\Omega(L)\), every new--old off-diagonal entry is zero, and the new--new block is \(I_h\), whose off-diagonal entries are zero. The fiber definition consequently gives the exact equivalence
\[
\widetilde L\in\mathcal F_{\widetilde G}(\widetilde\Sigma)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
U=0\ \text{ and }\ L\in\mathcal F_G(\Sigma).
\]
Thus restriction \(\widetilde L\mapsto L\) is a bijection with inverse
\[
L\longmapsto
\begin{pmatrix}
L&0\\
0&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
In particular, the enlarged fiber is nonempty. Since the queried edge \(e\) is an old edge and its coefficient is unchanged under the two inverse maps, \(\mathrm{def:attainable\mbox{-}set}\) implies
\[
\mathcal A_e(\widetilde G,\widetilde\Sigma)
=\mathcal A_e(G,\Sigma).
\]

It remains to check the graph restrictions. The component hypothesis implies that \(G\) is acyclic of depth one and has maximum directed outdegree one. Each new vertex is a root having exactly one outgoing edge, so the construction preserves all three properties. Every old vertex of indegree two receives exactly one new parent and therefore has indegree three; vertices of indegree zero or one are unchanged; and new vertices have indegree zero. Consequently
\[
\max_{v\in\widetilde V}|\operatorname{pa}_{\widetilde D}(v)|\le3,
\qquad
K_{\widetilde G}
=|\{v\in\widetilde V:|\operatorname{pa}_{\widetilde D}(v)|=2\}|=0.
\]
The inequality, rather than equality, is necessary when \(S=\varnothing\). Each two-parent in-star becomes a three-parent in-star on four vertices, while isolated vertices and single directed edges are unchanged. Hence every directed weak component has at most four vertices. All asserted properties follow, and the exact equivalence of fibers shows that no singular or exceptional branch has been discarded.

By \(\mathrm{thm:fanin\mbox{-}two\mbox{-}completeness}\), there is a promise-preserving polynomial-time many-one reduction from the \(\forall\mathbb R\)-complete complemented planted-selector language to \(\mathsf{NUMEDGE}_2\). For every promised source instance \(Q\), its output \((G_Q,\Sigma_Q,e_Q)\) has rational strictly diagonally dominant positive-definite covariance, a nonempty fiber containing the supplied rational witness, directed depth one, maximum directed outdegree one, and directed weak components that are isolated vertices, single directed edges, or two-parent in-stars.

Apply \(\mathrm{lem:fanin\mbox{-}two\mbox{-}to\mbox{-}three\mbox{-}zero\mbox{-}parameter\mbox{-}padding}\) to this output. It produces \((\widetilde G_Q,\widetilde\Sigma_Q,e_Q)\) in deterministic polynomial bit complexity. The corrected conclusion of that lemma gives
\[
\max_{v\in V(\widetilde G_Q)}
|\operatorname{pa}_{\widetilde D_Q}(v)|\le3,
\qquad K_{\widetilde G_Q}=0,
\]
and preserves acyclicity, directed depth one, maximum directed outdegree one, rationality, positive definiteness, strict diagonal dominance, and feasibility; in particular, the rational feasible witness supplied by the compiler extends by setting every new coefficient to zero. Its component analysis shows that every directed weak component of \(\widetilde G_Q\) is an isolated vertex, a single directed edge, or a three-parent in-star. The exact restriction bijection preserves the queried coefficient and therefore gives
\[
\mathcal A_{e_Q}(\widetilde G_Q,\widetilde\Sigma_Q)
=\mathcal A_{e_Q}(G_Q,\Sigma_Q).
\]
By \(\mathrm{def:promise\mbox{-}problem}\), the padded triple lies in the promise domain of \(\mathsf{NUMEDGE}_3\), and either triple is a YES instance precisely when its displayed attainable set is a singleton. Thus the composition of the compiler with the padding map is a promise-preserving many-one reduction to the stated \(K_G=0\) subclass of \(\mathsf{NUMEDGE}_3\). The source equivalences in \(\mathrm{thm:fanin\mbox{-}two\mbox{-}completeness}\) and the equality of attainable sets show explicitly that
\[
Q\in\mathsf{coSEL\mbox{-}PNQF}
\Longleftrightarrow
\mathcal A_{e_Q}(\widetilde G_Q,\widetilde\Sigma_Q)=\{0\},
\]
whereas
\[
Q\in\mathsf{SEL\mbox{-}PNQF}
\Longleftrightarrow
\mathcal A_{e_Q}(\widetilde G_Q,\widetilde\Sigma_Q)=\{0,1\}.
\]
This proves \(\forall\mathbb R\)-hardness on the stated subclass. Because the padding establishes equality of complete fibers before taking the queried projection, the reduction preserves singular fibers and uses no genericity, rank, division, or limiting condition.

For membership, \(\mathrm{lem:published\mbox{-}numedge\mbox{-}bounds}\) places unrestricted promised numerical edge identification in \(\forall\mathbb R\). For a fixed \(k\in\mathbb N_0\), the additional condition \(K_G\le k\) is decidable from the directed adjacency matrix in polynomial time: compute every directed indegree and count those equal to two. Restricting a promise problem in \(\forall\mathbb R\) by such a polynomial-time decidable promise condition leaves it in \(\forall\mathbb R\). Hence numerical edge identification on the literal class \(K_G\le k\) belongs to promise \(\forall\mathbb R\). Since the hard family just constructed has \(K_G=0\), it belongs to \(K_G\le k\) for every \(k\in\mathbb N_0\). Therefore the restriction is promise \(\forall\mathbb R\)-complete for every fixed \(k\).

Finally, suppose there were an exact algorithm on the literal parameterized class with bit complexity \(f(k)\operatorname{poly}(N)\), where \(N\) is the total binary input length. On the hard family, \(k=0\), so the running time would be \(f(0)\operatorname{poly}(N)\), a polynomial because \(f(0)\) is constant. Promise \(\forall\mathbb R\)-hardness would then give \(\forall\mathbb R\subseteq\mathsf P\). The reverse inclusion follows directly from the universal-real decision model, since a polynomial-time decision may be performed before outputting a fixed true or fixed false quantifier-free sentence. Thus \(\mathsf P=\forall\mathbb R\).

The obstruction in \(\mathrm{def:parameterized\mbox{-}handle}\) is exact: when \(K_G=0\), the handle exposes no coefficient entering a two-parent vertex, but the padded instance can still contain three-parent vertices and retains a fiber bijective to the hard source fiber. The separately displayed zero-two-parent hardness statement is precisely the \(k=0\) special case of the present all-fixed-\(k\) result, rather than an independent hardness phenomenon.
