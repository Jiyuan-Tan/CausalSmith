We first verify the five-node pair directly from \(\texttt{def:five-node-rational-instance}\).  Write
\[
 a_u=P(X=0\mid U=u),\qquad (a_0,a_1)=\left(\frac34,\frac14\right).
\]
The latent roots \(U\) and \(T\) are independent and fair.  The equality
\[
 P(Z=0\mid X=0,T=0)=P(Z=0\mid X=0,T=1)=\frac12
\]
is exactly the asserted whole-row CSI; complementation gives the same equality for \(Z=1\).  Its activation variable \(X\) is observed, but the arrow \(U\to X\) makes \(X\) a nonroot.  Thus this input explicitly witnesses the permitted endogenous observed-activation syntax and is not an input whose activation variable is required to be an observed root.

For either \(z\in\{0,1\}\), finite marginalization and the preceding CSI give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P(X=0,Z=z)
 &=\sum_{u,t}P(U=u)P(T=t)P(X=0\mid U=u)P(Z=z\mid X=0,T=t)\\
 &=\sum_{u,t}\frac14 a_u\frac12
 =\frac14.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Put \(s_t=P(Z=0\mid X=1,T=t)\), so \((s_0,s_1)=(3/4,1/4)\).  Independence of \(U\) and \(T\) yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P(X=1,Z=0)
 &=\frac14\sum_{u,t}(1-a_u)s_t
 =\frac14\left(\sum_u(1-a_u)\right)\left(\sum_t s_t\right)
 =\frac14,\\
 P(X=1,Z=1)
 &=\frac14\left(\sum_u(1-a_u)\right)\left(\sum_t(1-s_t)\right)
 =\frac14.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Hence \((X,Z)\) is uniform under both displayed models.  Every outcome row of \(\rho^+_5\) is \(1/2\), so
\[
 P_{\rho^+_5}(X=x,Z=z,Y=y)=\frac14\frac12=\frac18
 \quad\text{for all }x,z,y\in\{0,1\}.
\]

Only the four \(Z=0\) probabilities of \(Y=1\) change in \(\rho^-_5\).  In the order \((U,T)=(0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1)\), let
\[
 \Delta=\left(\frac3{100},\frac3{100},\frac7{100},-\frac{25}{100}\right)
\]
be the vector \(P_{\rho^-_5}(Y=1\mid U,T,Z=0)-P_{\rho^+_5}(Y=1\mid U,T,Z=0)\).  For \((X,Z)=(0,0)\), the four unnormalized latent weights are
\[
 \left(P(U=u,T=t,X=0,Z=0)\right)_{u,t}
 =\frac1{32}(3,3,1,1).
\]
Therefore the increment of the corresponding observed \(Y=1\) cell is
\[
 \frac1{32}(3,3,1,1)\Delta^{\mathsf T}
 =\frac{9+9+7-25}{3200}=0.
\]
For \((X,Z)=(1,0)\), the four weights are
\[
 \left(P(U=u,T=t,X=1,Z=0)\right)_{u,t}
 =\frac1{64}(3,1,9,3),
\]
and the observed-cell increment is
\[
 \frac1{64}(3,1,9,3)\Delta^{\mathsf T}
 =\frac{9+3+63-75}{6400}=0.
\]
The outcome rows at \(Z=1\) are unchanged.  Thus every observed \(Y=1\) cell agrees.  Since the \((X,Z)\)-marginals also agree and each binary outcome row is normalized, every observed \(Y=0\) cell agrees as well.  Consequently all eight cells under \(\rho^-_5\) also equal \(1/8\).

Under \(\operatorname{do}(X=0)\), intervention replaces only the mechanism for \(X\); the two latent roots remain independent and fair, and the CSI row gives \(P(Z=0\mid X=0,T=t)=1/2\).  The change in the interventional cell is therefore
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P_{X=0}^{\rho^-_5}(Y=1)-P_{X=0}^{\rho^+_5}(Y=1)
 &=\sum_{u,t}P(U=u)P(T=t)P(Z=0\mid X=0,T=t)\Delta_{ut}\\
 &=\frac18\left(\frac{3+3+7-25}{100}\right)
 =-\frac3{200}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The first query equals \(1/2\), because all of its outcome rows equal \(1/2\).  Hence
\[
 P_{X=0}^{\rho^+_5}(Y=1)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 P_{X=0}^{\rho^-_5}(Y=1)=\frac12-\frac3{200}=\frac{97}{200}.
\]
Every displayed primitive probability and every binary complement lies strictly between zero and one; all tables are normalized, the roots are independent, and each latent root has exactly the two children displayed in the definition.  Thus the calculation uses two positive semi-Markovian models satisfying the input CSI, and their query gap is exactly \(3/200\).

We next verify the trace assertions.  In the treatment state \(X=0\), the CSI authorizes deletion of \(T\to Z\).  After this deletion, latent projection has directed edges \(X\to Z\to Y\) and the single bidirected edge \(X\leftrightarrow Y\): the edge \(Z\leftrightarrow Y\) induced by the common latent parent \(T\) disappears when \(T\to Z\) is deleted.  Consider any pair satisfying \(\texttt{def:rooted-forest-obstruction}\) in this pruned graph.  Its larger forest must meet the treatment set.  If its vertex set is \(\{X\}\), its childless set contains \(X\), which is not a vertex of \(\operatorname{An}_{H\setminus X}(Y)\).  If it also contains \(Y\), bidirected connectedness forces its bidirected component to be contained in \(\{X,Y\}\); it cannot contain \(Z\), because \(Z\) is incident to no bidirected edge.  But the induced subgraph on \(\{X,Y\}\) has no directed edge, so its childless set is \(\{X,Y\}\), again not contained in \(\operatorname{An}_{H\setminus X}(Y)\).  These exhaust the bidirected-connected possibilities meeting \(X\).  Hence the pruned graph has no rooted C-forest pair of the required kind.

For the empty trace in the original latent projection, use the activation-refining cell \(Z=0\) and take
\[
 F=\{Y\},\qquad F'=\{X,Z,Y\}.
\]
The bidirected edges \(X\leftrightarrow Y\) and \(Z\leftrightarrow Y\) make \(F'\) one district.  The directed forest edges \(X\to Z\to Y\) give each vertex at most one directed child, and both \(F\) and \(F'\) have the common childless set \(R_0=\{Y\}\).  Moreover,
\[
 F\cap X=\varnothing,
 \qquad
 F'\cap X\ne\varnothing,
 \qquad
 R_0\subseteq\operatorname{An}_{G\setminus X}(Y).
\]
Thus this is a rooted C-forest pair under \(\texttt{def:rooted-forest-obstruction}\).

Regard \(\Delta\) as a perturbation of the four outcome-row classes with \(Z=0\), and extend it by zero on every outcome-row class with \(Z=1\).  It is then supported on the row classes exposed by the displayed \(Z=0\) forest.  The two observed \(Y=1\) increments computed above are the only potentially nonzero observed increments.  They vanish; the \(Z=1\) increments vanish by the zero extension, and the corresponding \(Y=0\) increments are their negatives by normalization.  By the contraction identity in \(\texttt{thm:fixed-sink-affine-map}\), equivalently by the defining contraction in \(\texttt{def:fixed-sink-linearization}\), this proves
\[
 A_{\eta,t,F}\Delta=0.
\]
The intervention calculation gives
\[
 b_{\eta,t,F}^{\mathsf T}\Delta=-\frac3{200}\ne0.
\]
Thus the displayed outcome-row difference lies in the relevant observed kernel while changing the query, as claimed.

Finally consider \(\mathsf E_{\mathrm{bow}}\).  By \(\texttt{def:reference-context-bow-instance}\), \(A\) and \(U\) are independent and fair, and the \(A=0\) CSI gives
\[
 P(X=x\mid A=0,U=0)=P(X=x\mid A=0,U=1)=\frac12
 \quad (x\in\{0,1\}).
\]
The conditioning event has probability
\[
 P(A=0,X=x)=\sum_u\frac12\frac12\frac12=\frac14>0.
\]
Bayes' rule therefore gives \(P(U=u\mid A=0,X=x)=1/2\).  Intervention on \(X\) leaves the fair latent root \(U\) unchanged, and \(Y\) has no parent \(A\).  Using the four outcome rows from the definition,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P_{X=0}(Y=1)&=\frac12\left(\frac15+\frac35\right)=\frac25,\\
 P_{X=1}(Y=1)&=\frac12\left(\frac25+\frac45\right)=\frac35.
 \end{aligned}
\]
On the other hand, for either \(x\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P(A=0,X=x,Y=1)
 &=\sum_u P(A=0)P(U=u)P(X=x\mid A=0,U=u)P(Y=1\mid X=x,U=u)\\
 &=\frac18\sum_u P(Y=1\mid X=x,U=u)
 =\frac14 P_{X=x}(Y=1).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Consequently
\[
 P_{X=0}(Y=1)=4P(A=0,X=0,Y=1)=\frac25,
 \qquad
 P_{X=1}(Y=1)=4P(A=0,X=1,Y=1)=\frac35,
\]
in agreement with the displayed observed cells \(1/10\) and \(3/20\).

For the structural rejection, order the outcome-row coordinates as
\[
 (p_{00},p_{01},p_{10},p_{11})
 =\bigl(P(Y=1\mid X=x,U=u): (x,u)=(0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1)\bigr).
\]
The two intervention coefficient vectors are
\[
 b_0^{\mathsf T}=\left(\frac12,\frac12,0,0\right),
 \qquad
 b_1^{\mathsf T}=\left(0,0,\frac12,\frac12\right).
\]
The rows of the full observed-cell coefficient matrix belonging to \((A,X,Y)=(0,0,1)\) and \((0,1,1)\) are, respectively,
\[
 \left(\frac18,\frac18,0,0\right)=\frac14 b_0^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad
 \left(0,0,\frac18,\frac18\right)=\frac14 b_1^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Thus both query vectors belong to the row space of the full observed-cell matrix.  The same equality remains true after applying the common forest-support column restriction prescribed by \(\texttt{def:fixed-sink-linearization}\).  Therefore the applicable vector \(b_{\eta,t,F}^{\mathsf T}\) lies in \(\operatorname{row}(A_{\eta,t,F})\).  The row-space alternative of \(\texttt{lem:forest-to-kernel-lift}\), together with the rejection rule in \(\texttt{def:binary-sink-verifier}\), makes \(\mathsf{Verify}_{\mathrm{sink}}\) reject the local \(A=1\) bow forest before a kernel direction or a countermodel pair is formed.
