We first separate the causal object from the observable quantities.  The
kernel \(K_M\) is defined by the intervention distribution, whereas
\(e_z\), \(m_{zx}\), \(\rho_z\), and \(\mathcal K_\pi(p)\) are
functionals of the observed law.  We prove their relationship from the
three causal assumptions and the displayed support conditions.

Because \(p(z)>0\) for every \(z\in\mathcal Z\), every division defining
\(e_z\) is valid, and
\[
 e_z(x)=\frac{p(z,x)}{p(z)}\geq 0,
 \qquad
 \sum_{x\in\mathcal X}e_z(x)=1.
 \tag{1}
\]
Whenever \(p(z,x)>0\), the division defining \(m_{zx}\) is likewise
valid, and
\[
 m_{zx}(y)=\frac{p(z,x,y)}{p(z,x)}\geq 0,
 \qquad
 \sum_{y\in\mathcal Y}m_{zx}(y)=1.
 \tag{2}
\]
Thus \(e_z\in\Delta(\mathcal X)\), and every reached response row
\(m_{zx}\) belongs to \(\Delta(\mathcal Y)\).  The other stipulated
coordinate-positivity condition is \(p_X(x)>0\) for every \(x\); it is
not used to infer joint positivity.

Let \(M\in\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{sep}}(G,\mathcal A,V,W,T,Y;\iota)\)
induce \(p\).  By qualitative functionality there is a map
\(f:\mathcal Z\to\mathcal B\) such that \(i_A=f(i_Z)\) almost surely.
Write the other conditional rows as
\[
 \lambda(z)=\theta_{i_Z}(z),
 \qquad
 r_a(x)=\theta_{i_X}(x\mid a),
 \qquad
 g_{a,x}(y)=\theta_{i_Y}(y\mid a,x).
\]
The causal-factorization assumption, followed by summation over the hidden
point mass at \(f(z)\), gives, for every \((z,x,y)\),
\[
 p(z,x,y)=\lambda(z)r_{f(z)}(x)g_{f(z),x}(y).
 \tag{3}
\]
Summing (3) first over \((x,y)\), and then only over \(y\), gives
\[
 p(z)=\lambda(z),
 \qquad
 p(z,x)=p(z)r_{f(z)}(x).
 \tag{4}
\]
The guard \(p(z)>0\) permits division in (4), hence
\[
 e_z=r_{f(z)}.
 \tag{5}
\]
If \(p(z,x)>0\), then (4) implies \(r_{f(z)}(x)>0\), and division of
(3) by the positive number \(p(z,x)\) gives
\[
 m_{zx}=g_{f(z),x}.
 \tag{6}
\]

Suppose \(f(z)=f(z')\).  Equation (5) gives \(e_z=e_{z'}\), so the two
positive supports agree; equation (6) then gives equality of all response
rows appearing in the two guarded profiles.  Consequently
\(\rho_z=\rho_{z'}\).  Every nonempty fiber of \(f\) is therefore
contained in one observed-profile class.  Its fiber partition has at most
\(B=\lvert\mathcal B\rvert\) blocks, so it is an element of
\(\Pi_B(p)\), and in particular
\[
 R=\lvert\mathcal R(p)\rvert\leq B.
 \tag{7}
\]
This proves necessity in item 1 and proves that a functional-map fiber
cannot merge distinct profiles.

Conversely, fix \(\pi\in\Pi_B(p)\).  Since \(\pi\) has at most \(B\)
blocks, choose distinct states \(a_C\in\mathcal B\), one for each
\(C\in\pi\), and define \(f(z)=a_C\) for \(z\in C\).  Set
\(\lambda(z)=p(z)\).  Because each block lies within a single profile
class, the following rows do not depend on the representative
\(z\in C\):
\[
 r_{a_C}=e_z,
 \qquad
 g_{a_C,x}=m_{zx}\quad\text{when }e_z(x)>0.
 \tag{8}
\]
For \(x\) with \(e_z(x)=0\), choose any row of
\(\Delta(\mathcal Y)\); also choose arbitrary valid rows for unused
hidden states.  Equations (1)--(2) show that every assigned row is a
conditional distribution.  For a cell with \(p(z,x)=0\), equation (1)
and the guard \(p(z)>0\) imply \(e_z(x)=0\), so the constructed cell
probability and \(p(z,x,y)\) are both zero.  For a cell with
\(p(z,x)>0\), both denominators below are positive and
\[
 p(z)e_z(x)m_{zx}(y)
 =p(z)\frac{p(z,x)}{p(z)}
       \frac{p(z,x,y)}{p(z,x)}
 =p(z,x,y).
 \tag{9}
\]
Thus the construction induces exactly \(p\).  Its marginals inherit the
assumed strict inequalities \(p(z)>0\) and \(p_X(x)>0\), so it belongs
to \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{sep}}\).  If \(R\leq B\), the partition into
the \(R\) profile classes lies in \(\Pi_B(p)\), proving sufficiency.
Since every \(\pi\in\Pi_B(p)\) was arbitrary, the set of fiber
partitions across all models in the observed-law fiber is exactly
\(\Pi_B(p)\).  This completes item 1.

We next derive the full causal response kernel.  Under
\(do(i_Z=z,i_X=x)\), truncated factorization replaces the rows of
\(i_Z\) and \(i_X\) by point masses, qualitative functionality fixes
\(i_A=f(z)\), and summation over that hidden point mass gives
\[
 K_M(z,x,\cdot)=g_{f(z),x}.
 \tag{10}
\]
For the fiber partition \(\pi_f\), equation (6) fixes (10) to
\(m_{zx}\) at every reached pair.  If \(e_z(x)=0\), then every member of
the block containing \(z\) has the same zero coordinate by (5), and the
single mechanism row \(g_{f(z),x}\) is a common, observationally
unrestricted vector \(s_{C,x}\in\Delta(\mathcal Y)\).  Hence
\[
 \left\{K_M:M\in\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{sep}},\ p_M=p\right\}
 \subseteq\bigcup_{\pi\in\Pi_B(p)}\mathcal K_\pi(p).
 \tag{11}
\]

For the reverse containment, fix \(\pi\in\Pi_B(p)\) and
\(k\in\mathcal K_\pi(p)\).  Use the construction (8), and for every
block--treatment pair on which the profile has zero treatment probability
set
\[
 g_{a_C,x}=s_{C,x},
 \tag{12}
\]
where \(s_{C,x}\) is the common row supplied by the definition of
\(k\in\mathcal K_\pi(p)\).  These choices do not alter (9), because the
corresponding observational multiplier is \(e_z(x)=0\).  Equation (10)
now gives \(K_M=k\) simultaneously for all \((z,x)\).  This proves the
reverse inclusion in (11), the asserted sharpness, simultaneous
attainment, and preservation of every prescribed alphabet.  If \(p\)
and \(k\) are rational, then the guarded ratios in (8), the rows chosen
from \(k\) in (12), and the root probabilities are rational; unused rows
may be chosen as point masses.  The attaining model then has rational
conditional tables.  Item 2 follows.

For item 3, write
\(C_\rho=\{z\in\mathcal Z:\rho_z=\rho\}\).  Every admissible partition
has at least one block in each of the \(R\) distinct profile classes.
At \(B=R\), it must therefore have exactly one block \(C_\rho\) per
profile, so every \(x\in H_\rho\) indexes one row shared by the whole
class \(C_\rho\).

Now split \(C_\rho\) into singleton blocks whenever
\(H_\rho\ne\varnothing\), and retain \(C_\rho\) as one block whenever
\(H_\rho=\varnothing\).  The resulting partition has
\[
 \sum_{\rho:H_\rho=\varnothing}1
 +\sum_{\rho:H_\rho\ne\varnothing}\lvert C_\rho\rvert
 =R+\sum_{\rho:H_\rho\ne\varnothing}
       (\lvert C_\rho\rvert-1)
 =B_{\mathrm{sat}}(p)
 \tag{13}
\]
blocks.  For this partition, every missing pair \((z,x)\) has its own
row \(s_{\{z\},x}\), so (12) realizes every member of
\(\mathcal K_{\mathrm{ind}}(p)\).  Conversely, every kernel in any
\(\mathcal K_\pi(p)\) has the fixed reached rows and simplex-valued
missing rows required by \(\mathcal K_{\mathrm{ind}}(p)\).  Hence the
region equals \(\mathcal K_{\mathrm{ind}}(p)\) for hidden cardinality
\(b\geq B_{\mathrm{sat}}(p)\), because the partition just constructed
uses at most \(b\) blocks.

For minimality, assume \(\lvert\mathcal Y\rvert\geq2\).  Then
\(\Delta(\mathcal Y)\) contains infinitely many distinct points.  For
each profile with \(H_\rho\ne\varnothing\), choose pairwise distinct
tuples
\[
 u_z\in\prod_{x\in H_\rho}\Delta(\mathcal Y),
 \qquad z\in C_\rho.
 \tag{14}
\]
Define a kernel in \(\mathcal K_{\mathrm{ind}}(p)\) by assigning
\(u_z\) to the missing rows of \(z\), independently over all such
profiles, and retaining the fixed reached rows.  If two distinct elements
of one hole-bearing \(C_\rho\) lay in the same block of a representing
partition, the common-row equalities defining \(\mathcal K_\pi(p)\)
would make their entire tuples equal, contradicting (14).  Thus every
hole-bearing profile requires \(\lvert C_\rho\rvert\) blocks, and every
other profile requires at least one.  Equation (13) shows that this
kernel needs at least \(B_{\mathrm{sat}}(p)\) blocks.  Therefore the
region for \(R\leq b<B_{\mathrm{sat}}(p)\) is a strict subset of
\(\mathcal K_{\mathrm{ind}}(p)\).  Since the region at any intermediate
\(b\) is, by item 2 with \(B=b\), precisely the union over
\(\Pi_b(p)\), item 3 is proved.

For item 4, if \(\lvert\mathcal Y\rvert=1\), every response simplex is
a singleton.  If \(p(z,x)>0\) for every pair, equations (6) and (10)
fix the entire kernel.  Conversely, suppose
\(\lvert\mathcal Y\rvert\geq2\), feasibility holds, and
\(p(z_0,x_0)=0\).  Since \(p(z_0)>0\), equation (1) gives
\(e_{z_0}(x_0)=0\).  In any admissible partition, all members of the
block containing \(z_0\) have that same zero coordinate, so its row at
\(x_0\) is unrestricted.  Choosing two distinct points of
\(\Delta(\mathcal Y)\) for this row in construction (12) produces two
models with the same observed law and different values of
\(K_M(z_0,x_0,\cdot)\).  The kernel is therefore not point identified.
This proves the equivalence.

Under the single intervention \(do(i_X=x)\), truncated factorization
retains the root law \(p(z)\), qualitative functionality sets
\(i_A=f(z)\), and the outcome mechanism is the row in (10).  Hence
\[
 Q_M^X(x,\cdot)
 =\sum_{z\in\mathcal Z}p(z)g_{f(z),x}
 =\sum_{z\in\mathcal Z}p(z)K_M(z,x,\cdot).
 \tag{15}
\]
For the fiber partition \(\pi_f\), split this sum into reached and
missing pairs to obtain
\[
 Q_M^X(x,\cdot)
 =L_x+
 \sum_{C\in\pi_f:\,e_z(x)=0\ {
m for}\ z\in C}
 \left(\sum_{z\in C}p(z)\right)s_{C,x}.
 \tag{16}
\]
The weights in the second term are nonnegative and sum to
\(M_x=\sum_{z:p(z,x)=0}p(z)\).  If \(M_x>0\), their normalized convex
combination belongs to \(\Delta(\mathcal Y)\); if \(M_x=0\), the term
vanishes.  Thus (16) belongs to
\(L_x+M_x\Delta(\mathcal Y)\).  Conversely, for any
\(q_x=L_x+M_xs_x\) with \(s_x\in\Delta(\mathcal Y)\), use any
admissible partition and set \(s_{C,x}=s_x\) for every block missing
\(x\); when \(M_x=0\), no choice is needed.  These mechanism-row choices
are independent across \(x\), and (16) then equals \(q_x\)
simultaneously for every \(x\).  This proves the sharp product region.
If joint positivity also holds, every denominator \(p(z,x)\) is
strictly positive, and (6) and (10) yield the guarded identifying circuit
\[
 K_M(z,x,y)=m_{zx}(y)=\frac{p(z,x,y)}{p(z,x)}.
 \tag{17}
\]
Thus item 5 holds, with the causal target identified with the observed
conditional only after the equality has been proved.

Finally, each partition with at most \(B\) blocks is represented by at
least one map from \(\mathcal Z\) to a labeled set of \(B\) labels.
There are exactly \(B^{\lvert\mathcal Z\rvert}\) such maps.  Enumerating
them and retaining precisely those whose nonempty fibers lie within
profile classes therefore enumerates \(\Pi_B(p)\) within the claimed
bound.  For fixed \(\pi\), there is one simplex constraint per response
row, reached-row equalities, and common-row equalities; after scalarizing
normalization and nonnegativity, their number is
\(O(\lvert\mathcal Z\rvert\lvert\mathcal X\rvert
\lvert\mathcal Y\rvert)\).  Equations (1), (2), (6), (9), and (17)
exhibit every division together with its tested strict denominator.
This proves item 6 and completes the theorem.
