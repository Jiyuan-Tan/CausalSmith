Write
\[
q=\bigl(Q_{X,M}(0,1),Q_{X,M}(1,1)\bigr)^{\mathsf T},
\qquad
\widehat q=(\widehat q_0,\widehat q_1)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Let the eight observed cells be indexed by \(v=(z,x,y)\), and define the
indicator vector \(R_k=(\mathbf 1\{O_k=v\})_v\).  By the independent
multinomial-sampling assumption,
\[
\widehat p=\frac1n\sum_{k=1}^nR_k,
\qquad
\mathbb E[R_k]=p,
\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}(R_k)=\operatorname{diag}(p)-pp^{\mathsf T}=\Sigma(p).
\tag{10}
\]
The vectors are bounded and hence have finite second moments.  Applying
Lemma~\texttt{lem:multivariate-central-limit} to (10) yields
\[
\sqrt n(\widehat p-p)\Rightarrow
\mathcal N\bigl(0,\Sigma(p)\bigr).
\tag{11}
\]

Let \(S=\{z:p_z>0\}\).  On the point-identified branch,
Theorem~\texttt{thm:binary-rectangle} implies
\(p_{zx}>0\) for every \(z\in S\) and \(x\in\{0,1\}\); the fixed-support
margin strengthens these inequalities to \(p_{zx}\ge\epsilon\).  For a
real cell vector \(r=(r_{zxy})\), write
\(r_z=\sum_{x,y}r_{zxy}\), \(r_{zx}=\sum_yr_{zxy}\), and define, on
\[
\mathcal U=\{r:r_{zx}>\epsilon/2
\text{ for all }z\in S,\ x\in\{0,1\}\},
\]
the guarded standardization map
\[
g_x(r)=\sum_{z\in S}r_z\frac{r_{zx1}}{r_{zx}},
\qquad
g(r)=(g_0(r),g_1(r))^{\mathsf T}.
\tag{12}
\]
Every denominator in (12) is positive on \(\mathcal U\), so \(g\) is a
rational, continuously differentiable map there.  Theorem
\texttt{thm:binary-rectangle} gives \(g(p)=q\).  Its ambient Jacobian
\(J=Dg(p)\) is computable cell by cell: with
\(m_{ax}=p_{ax1}/p_{ax}\),
\[
J_{x,(a,b,c)}
=\mathbf 1\{a\in S\}
\left[
m_{ax}
+\mathbf 1\{b=x\}p_a
\frac{\mathbf 1\{c=1\}p_{ax}-p_{ax1}}{p_{ax}^2}
\right].
\tag{13}
\]
The guards \(p_{ax}\ge\epsilon\) justify both divisions in (13).

For completeness, \(\widehat p\to p\) in probability follows directly from
the sampling assumption: each cell frequency has variance at most \(1/(4n)\),
so Chebyshev's inequality followed by a union bound over eight cells makes
\(\max_v|\widehat p_v-p_v|\to0\) in probability.  Hence
\(P(\widehat p\in\mathcal U)\to1\).  If \(z\notin S\), then \(P(Z=z)=0\),
so its empirical cells are zero almost surely.  Consequently, on
\(\{\widehat p\in\mathcal U\}\), Definition
\texttt{def:binary-estimator} agrees exactly with (12):
\[
\widehat q=g(\widehat p).
\tag{14}
\]
The arbitrary bounded convention on the complementary zero-denominator
event is therefore used with probability tending to zero.

Apply Lemma~\texttt{lem:finite-dimensional-delta-method} to (11)--(12).
Together with (14), which can alter expectations of a bounded continuous
test function by at most twice its supremum times
\(P(\widehat p\notin\mathcal U)\), this gives
\[
\sqrt n(\widehat q-q)
\Rightarrow
\mathcal N\bigl(0,J\Sigma(p)J^{\mathsf T}\bigr).
\tag{15}
\]

To make the covariance assertion explicit, let \(J(r)=Dg(r)\) on
\(\mathcal U\), and define the plug-in matrix
\[
\widehat V=J(\widehat p)\Sigma(\widehat p)J(\widehat p)^{\mathsf T}
\tag{16}
\]
there, and set \(\widehat V=0\) off \(\mathcal U\).  Both \(J(r)\) and
\(\Sigma(r)=\operatorname{diag}(r)-rr^{\mathsf T}\) are continuous at
\(p\).  The elementary epsilon--delta definition of continuity and
\(\widehat p\to p\), together with
\(P(\widehat p\in\mathcal U)\to1\), therefore imply
\[
\widehat V\mathrel{\mathop{\longrightarrow}^{p}}
V:=J\Sigma(p)J^{\mathsf T}.
\tag{17}
\]

Finally fix any nonrandom contrast \(c\in\mathbb R^2\) whose limiting
variance \(\sigma_c^2=c^{\mathsf T}Vc\) is strictly positive.  By (17),
\(\widehat\sigma_c^2=c^{\mathsf T}\widehat Vc\to_p\sigma_c^2\), so its
nonnegative square root is also consistent and is positive with probability
tending to one.  Lemma~\texttt{lem:studentized-wald-coverage}, applied to
(15) and (17), gives
\[
\frac{\sqrt n\,c^{\mathsf T}(\widehat q-q)}{\widehat\sigma_c}
\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1).
\tag{18}
\]
If \(z_{1-\alpha/2}\) is the \((1-\alpha/2)\)-quantile of the standard
normal law, its continuity and \(\alpha\in(0,1)\) yield
\[
P\!\left(
c^{\mathsf T}q\in
\left[c^{\mathsf T}\widehat q
\mathbin{-}z_{1-\alpha/2}\frac{\widehat\sigma_c}{\sqrt n},
c^{\mathsf T}\widehat q
\mathbin{+}z_{1-\alpha/2}\frac{\widehat\sigma_c}{\sqrt n}
\right]
\right)\longrightarrow1-\alpha.
\tag{19}
\]
The strict inequality \(\sigma_c^2>0\) is exactly the nondegeneracy
condition required for the division in (18); no Wald assertion is made for
a zero-variance contrast.
