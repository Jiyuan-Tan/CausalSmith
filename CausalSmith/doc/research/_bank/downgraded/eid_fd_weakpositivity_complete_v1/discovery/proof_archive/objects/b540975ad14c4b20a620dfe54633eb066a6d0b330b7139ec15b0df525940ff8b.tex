Fix a model \(M=(G,\mathcal A,\theta)\in\mathfrak M(G,\mathcal A,V,W,T)\).  All sums below are finite because every component of \(\mathcal A\) is finite.  Since \(A\in W\), Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:qualitative\text{-}functionality}\) says separately for every \(z\in\mathcal A_Z\) that the row \(\theta_A(\cdot\mid z)\) is a point mass.  A point mass on the nonempty set \(\mathcal A_A\) has a unique support point, so there is a function \(f:\mathcal A_Z\to\mathcal A_A\) satisfying
\[
 \theta_A(a\mid z)=\mathbf 1\{a=f(z)\}
 \qquad
 (a\in\mathcal A_A,\ z\in\mathcal A_Z).
\]

The stated edge set gives
\[
 \operatorname{pa}_G(Z)=\varnothing,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{pa}_G(A)=\{Z\},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{pa}_G(X)=\{A\},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{pa}_G(Y)=\{A,X\}.
\]
Therefore Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:causal\text{-}factorization}\) and the preceding point-mass identity imply, for every \((z,a,x,y)\in\mathcal A_Z\times\mathcal A_A\times\mathcal A_X\times\mathcal A_Y\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P_M(z,a,x,y)
 &=\theta_Z(z)\theta_A(a\mid z)\theta_X(x\mid a)\theta_Y(y\mid a,x)\\
 &=\theta_Z(z)\mathbf 1\{a=f(z)\}\theta_X(x\mid a)\theta_Y(y\mid a,x).
\end{aligned}
\]
Because \(V=\{Z,X,Y\}\) and \(H=\{A\}\), the definition of the observed marginal and summation over \(a\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 p_M(z,x,y)
 &=\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_A}P_M(z,a,x,y)\\
 &=\theta_Z(z)\theta_X(x\mid f(z))\theta_Y(y\mid f(z),x).
\end{aligned}
\]
Summing this equality over \(y'\in\mathcal A_Y\), and using normalization of the conditional-probability row \(\theta_Y(\cdot\mid f(z),x)\), yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
 p_M(z,x)
 &=\sum_{y'\in\mathcal A_Y}p_M(z,x,y')\\
 &=\theta_Z(z)\theta_X(x\mid f(z))
   \sum_{y'\in\mathcal A_Y}\theta_Y(y'\mid f(z),x)\\
 &=\theta_Z(z)\theta_X(x\mid f(z)).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(T=\{Z,X\}\).  Hence Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:joint\text{-}treatment\text{-}positivity}\), applied to the joint assignment \((z,x)\in\mathcal A_Z\times\mathcal A_X\), gives
\[
 p_M(z,x)=P_M(T=(z,x))>0.
\]
This is the required mechanically entailed support guard.  Division by it is therefore valid, and the last two displays imply
\[
 \frac{p_M(z,x,y)}{p_M(z,x)}
 =\theta_Y(y\mid f(z),x)
 =p_M(Y=y\mid Z=z,X=x).
\]
The ratio on the left is an observed-law functional; it has not been defined to be the causal target.

To relate it to that target, apply Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:truncated\text{-}intervention}\).  Under \(do(Z=z,X=x)\), the rows at \(Z\) and \(X\) are replaced by point masses while the mechanisms at \(A\) and \(Y\) are retained.  Marginalizing the remaining non-outcome variable \(A\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P_M(Y=y\mid do(Z=z,X=x))
 &=\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_A}\theta_A(a\mid z)\theta_Y(y\mid a,x)\\
 &=\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_A}\mathbf 1\{a=f(z)\}\theta_Y(y\mid a,x)\\
 &=\theta_Y(y\mid f(z),x).
\end{aligned}
\]
Combining the last two calculations proves the displayed identifying equality for every \(z,x,y\).

Finally, if \(M,M'\in\mathfrak M(G,\mathcal A,V,W,T)\) induce the same observed law \(p\), applying the proved identity to each model yields
\[
 P_M(Y=y\mid do(Z=z,X=x))
 =\frac{p(z,x,y)}{p(z,x)}
 =P_{M'}(Y=y\mid do(Z=z,X=x))
\]
for all \(z,x,y\).  Thus the entire intervention vector is constant on each observed-law fiber, which is graphwide identification.  The only division in the circuit is guarded by the strict inequality derived above.
