The binary witness is an instance of \(\mathrm{thm:categorical\text{-}joint\text{-}identification}\): take \(D=\{Z,A,X,Y\}\), take every alphabet to be \(\{0,1\}\), use the edge set of \(G_{\mathrm w}\), and set \(V=V_{\mathrm w}\), \(H=H_{\mathrm w}\), \(W=W_{\mathrm w}\), \(T=T_{ZX}\), and the outcome set to \(Y_{\mathrm w}^{\star}\).  By the definition of \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm w,ZX}\), every \(M\in\mathfrak M_{\mathrm w,ZX}\) is then a model in the categorical theorem's class, and its observed marginal on \((Z,X,Y)\) is \(p_{\mathrm w}\).

Consequently, for every \(z,x,y\in\{0,1\}\), that theorem supplies the strict guard
\[
 p_{\mathrm w}(Z=z,X=x)>0
\]
and the equation-by-equation identification
\[
\begin{aligned}
 Q_{ZX,M}(z,x,y)
 &=P_M(Y=y\mid do(Z=z,X=x))\\
 &=\frac{p_{\mathrm w}(z,x,y)}{p_{\mathrm w}(z,x)}\\
 &=p_{\mathrm w}(Y=y\mid Z=z,X=x).
\end{aligned}
\]
The first equality is the definition of \(Q_{ZX,M}\); the second is the categorical theorem; and the third is the definition of conditional probability under the displayed positive-denominator guard.  The theorem's explicit support premise is therefore satisfied (and, in this class, redundant with joint-treatment positivity).

If two binary models \(M,M'\in\mathfrak M_{\mathrm w,ZX}\) have the same observed law \(p_{\mathrm w}\), the middle ratio is common to both, so
\[
 Q_{ZX,M}(z,x,y)
 =\frac{p_{\mathrm w}(z,x,y)}{p_{\mathrm w}(z,x)}
 =Q_{ZX,M'}(z,x,y)
\]
for every \(z,x,y\).  Hence the full binary joint-intervention distribution is graphwide identified, as claimed.
