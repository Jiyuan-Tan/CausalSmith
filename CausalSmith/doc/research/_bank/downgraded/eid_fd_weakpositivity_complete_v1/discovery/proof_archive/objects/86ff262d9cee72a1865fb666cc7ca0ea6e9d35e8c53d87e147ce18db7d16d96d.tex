We first separate the causal mechanisms from the observable quantities.
Qualitative functionality at \(i_A\) and nonemptiness of
\(\mathcal B\) give a map \(f:\mathcal Z\to\mathcal B\) such that
\[
 \theta_{i_A}(a\mid z)=\mathbf 1\{a=f(z)\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Write
\[
 \lambda(z)=\theta_{i_Z}(z),\qquad
 r_b(x)=\theta_{i_X}(x\mid b),\qquad
 g_{b,x}(y)=\theta_{i_Y}(y\mid b,x).
\]
The four displayed edges determine all parent sets.  Substitution of
(1) into the recursive factorization, followed by summation over the
hidden state, therefore gives
\[
 p_M(z,x,y)=\lambda(z)r_{f(z)}(x)g_{f(z),x}(y).
\tag{2}
\]
Every row of \(r\) and \(g\) is a probability vector.  Summing (2) over
\((x,y)\) gives
\[
 p_M(z)=\lambda(z),
\tag{3}
\]
and summing only over \(y\) gives
\[
 p_M(z,x)=\lambda(z)r_{f(z)}(x).
\tag{4}
\]
For \(z\in\mathcal Z_+\), division of (4) by the strict guard
\(p(z)=\lambda(z)>0\) yields
\[
 e_z(x)=r_{f(z)}(x).
\tag{5}
\]
If in addition \(p(z,x)>0\), division of (2) by (4), whose common value
is then strictly positive, yields
\[
 m_{zx}(y)=g_{f(z),x}(y).
\tag{6}
\]
Equations (5)--(6) show that two positive-mass roots having the same
functional value have the same profile.  Thus the nonempty fibers of
\(f\) form a partition in \(\Pi_B^0(p)\), and distinct members of
\(\mathcal R_+(p)\) require distinct values of \(f\).  This proves the
necessity of
\[
 R\leq B.
\tag{7}
\]

We next give a construction that proves feasibility, sharpness, and
simultaneous attainment rather than defining them into the candidate
region.  Fix \(\pi\in\Pi_B^0(p)\), fix
\(k\in\mathcal K_\pi^0(p)\), and choose an injection
\(\ell:\pi\to\mathcal B\).  Put
\[
 \lambda(z)=p(z),\qquad f(z)=\ell(C)\quad(z\in C).
\tag{8}
\]
If \(C\cap\mathcal Z_+\ne\varnothing\), select
\(z_C\in C\cap\mathcal Z_+\) and set
\[
 r_{\ell(C)}=e_{z_C}.
\tag{9}
\]
This does not depend on the selection at the level of its value, because
all positive members of \(C\) have one profile.  If
\(C\cap\mathcal Z_+=\varnothing\), choose any probability vector for
\(r_{\ell(C)}\).  For every block and every \(x\), set
\[
 g_{\ell(C),x}=k(z,x,\cdot)\quad(z\in C).
\tag{10}
\]
The first defining equality of \(\mathcal K_\pi^0(p)\) makes (10)
well defined.  Assign arbitrary probability rows to unused hidden states,
and use (1) for the functional row.

For \(z\in\mathcal Z_+\) and \(p(z,x)>0\), equations (8)--(10) and the
second defining equality of \(\mathcal K_\pi^0(p)\) give
\[
 \lambda(z)r_{f(z)}(x)g_{f(z),x}(y)
 =p(z)e_z(x)m_{zx}(y)=p(z,x,y),
\tag{11}
\]
where the two ratios defining \(e_z\) and \(m_{zx}\) are guarded by
\(p(z)>0\) and \(p(z,x)>0\).  If \(z\in\mathcal Z_+\) but
\(p(z,x)=0\), then \(e_z(x)=0\), so both sides of (11) are zero without
division.  If \(z\in\mathcal Z_0\), then \(\lambda(z)=p(z)=0\), and
again both sides are zero.  Hence (2) for the constructed mechanism is
exactly the prescribed observed law \(p\).

Under \(do(i_Z=z,i_X=x)\), truncated factorization replaces the two
intervened rows by point masses and retains (1) and the outcome row.
Summing over the hidden state gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 K_M(z,x,y)
 &=\sum_{a\in\mathcal B}
   \mathbf 1\{a=f(z)\}g_{a,x}(y)\\
 &=g_{f(z),x}(y)\\
 &=k(z,x,y).
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
Thus every member of every displayed polytope is genuinely attained by a
causal mechanism on the original alphabets.  Conversely, (5), (6), and
(12) show that any compatible mechanism has a functional-fiber partition
\(\pi\in\Pi_B^0(p)\), is constant on each block at every \(x\), and
equals \(m_{zx}\) on every reached positive-root row.  Its kernel
therefore belongs to \(\mathcal K_\pi^0(p)\).  We have proved
\[
 \{K_M:M\in\mathfrak F_0(G,\mathcal A,V,W;\iota),\ p_M=p\}
 =\mathcal K_B^0(p).
\tag{13}
\]

Because \(p\) has total mass one, \(\mathcal Z_+\) and hence
\(\mathcal R_+(p)\) are nonempty.  If (7) holds, partition
\(\mathcal Z_+\) into its \(R\) profile classes and attach every member
of \(\mathcal Z_0\), if any, to any one of those classes.  This is an
element of \(\Pi_B^0(p)\), and its kernel set is nonempty because all
unfixed rows may be chosen as point masses.  The preceding construction
then realizes \(p\), proving sufficiency in item 1.  It also proves that
every member of \(\Pi_B^0(p)\) is the exact nonempty-fiber partition of
some realizing functional map, while the necessity argument proved the
reverse inclusion.  If \(p\) and \(k\) are rational, every guarded ratio
in (9) and (11) is rational; the other rows can be chosen as rational
point masses, and (10) is rational.  This proves the rational-attainment
claim.

We now prove the saturation formula.  The inclusion
\[
 \mathcal K_b^0(p)\subseteq\mathcal K_{\mathrm{ind}}^0(p)
\tag{14}
\]
follows immediately from the reached-row constraint in the definition of
each \(\mathcal K_\pi^0(p)\).  Conversely, fix
\(k\in\mathcal K_{\mathrm{ind}}^0(p)\).  Make every positive root in a
profile with \(H_\rho\ne\varnothing\) a singleton block, keep all of
\(\mathcal Z_\rho\) in one block when \(H_\rho=\varnothing\), and make
every zero-root state a singleton block.  For a no-hole profile all rows
of \(k\) are the profile's observed rows, so they are identical within
its block.  The resulting augmented partition has exactly
\[
 \sum_{\rho:H_\rho=\varnothing}1
 +\sum_{\rho:H_\rho\ne\varnothing}|\mathcal Z_\rho|
 +|\mathcal Z_0|
 =B_{\mathrm{sat}}^0(p)
\tag{15}
\]
blocks and places \(k\) in its kernel polytope.  Hence equality holds in
(14) whenever \(b\geq B_{\mathrm{sat}}^0(p)\).

For the reverse implication, assume \(|\mathcal Y|\geq2\).  Then
\(\Delta(\mathcal Y)\) contains infinitely many distinct probability
vectors.  For each hole-bearing profile \(\rho\), choose pairwise
distinct missing-row tuples
\[
 u_{\rho,z}\in
 \prod_{x\in H_\rho}\Delta(\mathcal Y),
 \qquad z\in\mathcal Z_\rho,
\tag{16}
\]
and use them for the missing rows of the corresponding positive roots,
while using \(m_{zx}\) on reached rows.  This defines finitely many full
response tuples for the positive roots.  For the zero roots, choose
pairwise distinct full response tuples in
\(\prod_{x\in\mathcal X}\Delta(\mathcal Y)\), also distinct from every
positive-root full tuple just constructed.  Such a finite choice exists
because that product is infinite.  Call the resulting kernel \(k^\star\).
It belongs to \(\mathcal K_{\mathrm{ind}}^0(p)\).

If \(k^\star\in\mathcal K_\pi^0(p)\), block constancy and (16) force a
separate block for every member of every hole-bearing positive profile.
Profile compatibility forces at least one distinct block for each
no-hole positive profile.  The pairwise-distinct full tuples force every
zero root into its own block and prevent it from joining a positive-root
block.  Therefore \(\pi\) has at least the number in (15) of blocks.
For \(R\leq b<B_{\mathrm{sat}}^0(p)\), this proves
\(k^\star\notin\mathcal K_b^0(p)\), so the inclusion (14) is strict.
Item 3 follows.

We next characterize point identification.  If
\(|\mathcal Y|=1\), every probability simplex used above is a singleton,
so (13) is a singleton.  Suppose henceforth that
\(|\mathcal Y|\geq2\).  If some positive profile has
\(H_\rho\ne\varnothing\), use the partition into positive profile
classes with zero roots attached arbitrarily.  At any
\(x\in H_\rho\), two distinct choices in \(\Delta(\mathcal Y)\) for the
free common row of that block give two members of (13) that differ at a
positive root.  Thus point identification requires
\[
 H_\rho=\varnothing\quad\text{for every }\rho.
\tag{17}
\]
Under (17), every positive-root response row is fixed by (6).  If
\(\mathcal Z_0=\varnothing\), these are all rows of the kernel, so (13)
is a singleton.

Suppose instead that \(\mathcal Z_0\ne\varnothing\).  If \(B>R\), take
the \(R\) positive profile blocks and add a singleton block containing
one fixed \(z_0\in\mathcal Z_0\), attaching any remaining zero roots
arbitrarily.  The singleton's full response tuple is unconstrained, so
two choices give distinct kernels in (13).  Hence point identification
requires \(B=R\).  At \(B=R\), every augmented partition must use one
block for each of the \(R\) distinct positive profiles.  There is no
remaining block with which to split a positive class or form a zero-only
block.  Consequently every zero root must join a positive profile block,
and its response tuple is the complete array \(g_\rho\) of that profile.
If all arrays \(g_\rho\) agree, this fixes every zero-root row to their
common value and proves point identification.  If
\(g_{\rho_1}\ne g_{\rho_2}\), attaching a fixed zero root first to the
\(\rho_1\)-block and then to the \(\rho_2\)-block gives two admissible
partitions and two kernels that differ at that root.  This proves both
necessity and sufficiency in item 4.

For the single-coordinate intervention, truncated factorization gives
the causal equality
\[
\begin{aligned}
 Q_M^X(x,y)
 &=\sum_{z\in\mathcal Z}\sum_{a\in\mathcal B}
   \lambda(z)\mathbf 1\{a=f(z)\}g_{a,x}(y)\\
 &=\sum_{z\in\mathcal Z}p(z)K_M(z,x,y).
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]
The first line follows by replacing only the \(i_X\)-row by a point mass;
the second uses (3) and (12).  Split the last sum into reached and missing
positive-root pairs; zero roots have weight zero.  This yields
\[
 Q_M^X(x,\cdot)
 =L_x+\sum_{z\in\mathcal Z_+:p(z,x)=0}p(z)K_M(z,x,\cdot).
\tag{19}
\]
The second term lies in \(M_x\Delta(\mathcal Y)\): when \(M_x>0\), its
division by \(M_x\) is a convex combination of probability vectors, and
when \(M_x=0\) it is zero.  Hence every projection lies in the displayed
product region.

Conversely, given \(q_x\in L_x+M_x\Delta(\mathcal Y)\), define under the
strict guard
\[
 s_x=\frac{q_x-L_x}{M_x}\in\Delta(\mathcal Y)
 \quad\text{when }M_x>0,
\tag{20}
\]
and choose any \(s_x\in\Delta(\mathcal Y)\) when \(M_x=0\).  Use the
positive profile-class partition, attach zero roots arbitrarily, and set
every block row missing \(x\) to the common value \(s_x\).  These choices
are independent for different \(x\), so they define one member of
\(\mathcal K_B^0(p)\).  Equations (19)--(20) give
\(Q_M^X(x,\cdot)=q_x\) for every \(x\), proving sharp simultaneous
attainment.  Rationality follows from the already-proved construction and
the guarded rational ratio in (20).

If \(p(z,x)>0\) for all \((z,x)\), then in particular
\(p(z)=\sum_xp(z,x)>0\), so \(\mathcal Z_0=\varnothing\), and every
\(e_z(x)>0\).  Equation (6) fixes every response row, while (12) proves
the causal, rather than definitional, equality
\[
 K_M(z,x,y)=m_{zx}(y)=\frac{p(z,x,y)}{p(z,x)},
\]
with division licensed by the assumed strict cell guard.  If only
\(\mathcal Z_0=\varnothing\), the definitions of the augmented objects
literally remove the zero-root allowance and the term
\(|\mathcal Z_0|\), giving the stated root-positive specialization.

Finally, one pass through the finite observed table computes all
marginals and guarded ratios.  Once exact comparison of the resulting
finite profiles has supplied their class labels, accumulation of the
class sizes and hole sets requires
\(O(|\mathcal Z||\mathcal X||\mathcal Y|)\) arithmetic operations.
Each map from \(\mathcal Z\) to \(B\) labels induces at most one labeled
partition, so there are at most \(B^{|\mathcal Z|}\) candidates.  For a
fixed partition, block equalities, reached-row equalities, simplex
normalizations, and nonnegativity inequalities are all linear and number
\(O(|\mathcal Z||\mathcal X||\mathcal Y|)\).  This proves item 6 and
completes the theorem.
