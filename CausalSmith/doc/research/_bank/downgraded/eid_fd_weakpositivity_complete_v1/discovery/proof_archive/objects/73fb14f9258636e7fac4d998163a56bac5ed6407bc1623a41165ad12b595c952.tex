For a tuple \(f=(f_i)_{i\in W}\) of deterministic maps and a treatment assignment \(t\in\mathcal A_T\), define
\[
 \Gamma_f(t)
 =\left\{a_D\in\Omega:
 a_T=t\ \text{ and }\
 a_i=f_i(a_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)})\ \text{for every }i\in W
 \right\}.
\]
There are exactly
\[
 \prod_{i\in W}
 |\mathcal A_i|^{|\mathcal A_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)}|}
 =\prod_{i\in W}|\mathcal A_i|^{r_i}=F
\]
such tuples, by the definitions of \(r_i\) and \(F\).

First suppose that \(M\in\mathfrak M(G,\mathcal A,V,W,T)\).  By qualitative functionality, each functional row selects a unique value, and hence determines one tuple \(f\).  For any \(t\in\mathcal A_T\), causal factorization gives
\[
 P_M(T=t)
 =\sum_{a_{D\setminus T}\in\mathcal A_{D\setminus T}}
   \prod_{i\in D}
   \theta_i(a_i\mid a_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)}).
\]
Every summand is nonnegative because every conditional-table entry is a probability.  Joint-treatment positivity says that the left-hand side is strictly positive.  A finite sum of nonnegative numbers is strictly positive only if one summand is strictly positive.  In such a summand every factor at a vertex \(i\in W\) must equal the selected point-mass entry, so
\(a_i=f_i(a_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)})\).  Consequently \(\Gamma_f(t)\ne\varnothing\) for every \(t\).

Conversely, suppose that a tuple \(f\) satisfies \(\Gamma_f(t)\ne\varnothing\) for every \(t\).  Define a CPT family \(\theta^f\) by
\[
 \theta^f_i(a\mid u)
 =\begin{cases}
  1, & i\in W\ \text{and }a=f_i(u),\\
  0, & i\in W\ \text{and }a\ne f_i(u),\\
  |\mathcal A_i|^{-1}, & i\notin W.
 \end{cases}
\]
Every row is normalized and nonnegative, every declared functional row is a point mass, and every entry is rational.  Let \(M_f=(G,\mathcal A,\theta^f)\), with the factorization and truncated-intervention semantics stipulated in the model-class definition.  If \(a_D\in\Gamma_f(t)\), then
\[
 P_{M_f}(a_D)
 =\prod_{i\in W}1
  \prod_{i\in D\setminus W}|\mathcal A_i|^{-1}
 >0.
\]
Hence, choosing one \(a_D\in\Gamma_f(t)\),
\[
 P_{M_f}(T=t)
 \ge P_{M_f}(a_D)>0.
\]
This holds for every \(t\), so \(M_f\in\mathfrak M(G,\mathcal A,V,W,T)\).  This proves both directions and also proves that the feasible witness preserves all prescribed alphabets.

For the operation count, enumerate the \(F\) map tuples.  For a fixed pair \((f,t)\), scan at most \(h=|\Omega|\) complete assignments.  Comparing \(a_T\) with \(t\) costs at most \(d\) operations, and checking all functional equations costs at most one vertex operation per functional vertex plus one parent-coordinate access per incident edge, hence \(O(d+m)\).  The scans therefore cost
\[
 O\!\left(FJ_T(d+m)h\right).
\]
Generating or reading all functional row choices and, on success, writing uniform rows costs at most \(O(FR)\) row operations.  Thus the claimed total is
\[
 O\!\left(FJ_T(d+m)h+FR\right).
\]
All loops range over finite sets, so the enumerator terminates.

It remains to justify the exact infeasibility certificate.  If no tuple succeeds, then for every \(f\) the scan returns an assignment \(t_f\) with \(\Gamma_f(t_f)=\varnothing\).  On the branch selecting \(f\), expand
\[
 P_M(T=t_f)
 =\sum_{a_{D\setminus T}}
   \prod_{i\in D}
   \theta_i(a_i\mid a_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)}).
\]
Every extension of \(t_f\) violates at least one functional equation, by emptiness of \(\Gamma_f(t_f)\); the corresponding monomial therefore contains an entry declared zero on that branch.  Thus the displayed network polynomial is identically zero on the branch, with a recorded zero factor for every monomial.

For a single global algebraic check, let
\[
 E_f=\prod_{i\in W}\prod_{u\in
 \mathcal A_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)}}
 \theta_i(f_i(u)\mid u).
\]
Point-mass rows obey normalization and the valid orthogonality relations
\[
 \theta_i(a\mid u)\theta_i(b\mid u)=0
 \quad(a\ne b).
\]
Expanding the product of the row normalizations gives the exact identity
\[
 \sum_f E_f
 =\prod_{i\in W}\prod_u\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_i}
   \theta_i(a\mid u)=1.
\]
Because \(\Gamma_f(t_f)=\varnothing\), every monomial of
\(E_fP_M(T=t_f)\) contains two distinct entries from some functional row and is therefore zero by orthogonality.  Hence
\[
 E_fP_M(T=t_f)=0
 \quad\text{for every }f.
\]
Strict positivity of a nonnegative treatment probability is equivalently encoded by the existence of a real auxiliary variable \(s_t\) satisfying
\[
 P_M(T=t)s_t^2=1:
\]
if the probability is positive take \(s_t=P_M(T=t)^{-1/2}\), while the equation and nonnegativity imply strict positivity in the reverse direction.  Multiplying the equation for \(t_f\) by \(E_f\) yields
\[
 E_f=E_fP_M(T=t_f)s_{t_f}^2=0.
\]
Summing over \(f\) contradicts \(\sum_fE_f=1\).  The enumerator can output the finite list \((f,t_f)\), every expanded network monomial with its functional zero factor, the normalization identity, and these displayed polynomial reductions.  They use rational coefficients and constitute the asserted exact infeasibility certificate.
