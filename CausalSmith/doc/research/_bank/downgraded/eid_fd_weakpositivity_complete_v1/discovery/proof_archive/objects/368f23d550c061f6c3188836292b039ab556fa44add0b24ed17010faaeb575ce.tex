Fix a strictly positive observed law \(p\), meaning
\[
 p(v)>0\qquad\text{for every }v\in\mathcal A_V,
\]
and suppose \(W=\varnothing\).  Let \(M\in\mathfrak F(p)\).  Causal factorization and truncated intervention semantics make \(M\) a recursive latent-variable causal model compatible with the latent projection of \(G\) onto \(V\).  The cited soundness theorem for \(\mathsf{ID}\) therefore gives, coordinate by coordinate, whenever the algorithm succeeds,
\[
 Q_M(t,y)=\phi_{\mathrm{ID}}(p)(t,y).
\]
Thus, if \(M_1,M_2\in\mathfrak F(p)\),
\[
 q_{M_1}=\phi_{\mathrm{ID}}(p)=q_{M_2}.
\]
This proves constancy on the strictly positive observed-law fiber.  It proves only the stated sound implication from successful classical identification; no failure-to-nonidentification converse is used.

It remains to build the support-guarded representation.  The successful \(\mathsf{ID}\) recursion described in the cited result uses finitely many marginalizations, products, and conditional-probability divisions.  Strict positivity of all joint observed cells implies positivity of every observed marginal, since for \(B\subseteq V\),
\[
 p(B=b)=\sum_{v:v_B=b}p(v)>0.
\]
Starting with the positive cells \(p(v)\), an induction over the successful recursion shows that every kernel entry being normalized is positive: finite products of positive entries are positive, and any nonempty finite marginal sum of positive entries is positive.  Hence each conditional-probability divisor is strictly positive.

For a mechanical polynomial guard trace, place the finitely many rational-coefficient guards
\[
 p(v)>0,\qquad v\in\mathcal A_V,
\]
at the root.  At any later division, clear the already licensed positive denominators from its divisor.  The result is a rational-coefficient polynomial that is a nonempty sum of products of the guarded positive cells, and is therefore strictly positive.  Add that polynomial sign test immediately above the division.  Induction over the finite expression proves that every divisor has an ancestor guard whose conjunction entails its strict positivity.  The resulting finite decision DAG is a support-guarded rational success certificate and returns \(\phi_{\mathrm{ID}}(p)=q_M\).

Finally suppose \(H=\varnothing\), so \(V=D\).  Strict positivity of \(p\) implies \(p(a_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)})>0\) for every parent assignment.  Sum the causal product first over the descendants of \(i\) in reverse topological order; row normalization removes all their factors.  The factor at \(i\), which depends only on the retained values of \(i\) and its parents, can then be taken outside the remaining sum over nondescendants.  That remaining sum is the parent marginal, and therefore
\[
 p(a_i,a_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)})
 =\theta_i(a_i\mid a_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)})
  p(a_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)}).
\]
Division is licensed by the positive parent marginal, and hence
\[
 \theta_i(a_i\mid a_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)})
 =p(a_i\mid a_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)}).
\]
Substituting this equality into the truncated factorization and marginalizing the nonoutcome, nonintervened coordinates proves
\[
 Q_M(t,y)
 =\sum_{a_{D\setminus(T\cup Y)}}
   \prod_{i\in D\setminus T}
   p(a_i\mid a_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)}),
\]
with \(a_T=t\) and \(a_Y=y\) in every summand.  Every displayed conditional denominator is again licensed by strict observational positivity.
