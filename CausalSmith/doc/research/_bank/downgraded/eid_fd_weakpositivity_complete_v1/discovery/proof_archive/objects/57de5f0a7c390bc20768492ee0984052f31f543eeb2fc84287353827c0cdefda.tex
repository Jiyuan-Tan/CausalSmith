The target in this theorem is the single-coordinate causal distribution
\(Q_M(x,y)=P_M(i_Y=y\mid do(i_X=x))\), not an observed conditional.  We
derive its observed-law region from the arbitrary-law response-kernel
theorem and the truncated intervention assumption.

Put \(\mathcal Z_+=\{z:p(z)>0\}\).  Because \(p\) has total mass one,
\(\mathcal Z_+\ne\varnothing\).  The positive-root profiles in
Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm:categorical\text{-}arbitrary\text{-}law\text{-}response\text{-}kernel}\)
are exactly the profiles \(\mathcal R(p)\) in this statement.  A mechanism
in the present model class has the same four-node factorization and the
same qualitative functional map as a mechanism in that theorem's
family-local class.  Conversely, any realizing mechanism constructed by
that theorem induces the prescribed \(p\); the hypothesis
\[
 p_X(x)=P_M(i_X=x)>0\qquad(x\in\mathcal X)
\tag{1}
\]
then supplies precisely the joint-treatment positivity condition for the
singleton treatment set \(T=\{i_X\}\).  Thus the compatible mechanism
fibers for the present target are the theorem's mechanism fibers restricted
by (1), and (1) is constant over the observed-law fiber.

Theorem
\(\mathrm{thm:categorical\text{-}arbitrary\text{-}law\text{-}response\text{-}kernel}\),
item 1, now gives
\[
 p\text{ is feasible}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 |\mathcal R(p)|\leq|\mathcal B|.
\tag{2}
\]
For completeness, choose the augmented partition whose positive parts are
the profile classes and attach every zero-mass root state to an arbitrary
existing block.  Label its positive profile blocks by the prescribed
injection \(\kappa:\mathcal R(p)\to\mathcal B\).  The constructive part
of that theorem sets
\[
 \theta_{i_Z}(z)=p(z),\qquad
 f(z)=\kappa(\rho_z),\qquad
 \theta_{i_X}(\cdot\mid\kappa(\rho_z))=e_z
\tag{3}
\]
for positive-mass \(z\), and sets
\[
 \theta_{i_Y}(\cdot\mid\kappa(\rho_z),x)=m_{zx}
 \quad\text{when }p(z,x)>0.
\tag{4}
\]
The guarded identities
\[
 p(z)e_z(x)m_{zx}(y)
 =p(z)\frac{p(z,x)}{p(z)}
       \frac{p(z,x,y)}{p(z,x)}
 =p(z,x,y)
\tag{5}
\]
verify every reached positive-root cell; the denominators in (5) are
strictly positive by the conditions under which the ratios are defined.
An unreached cell is zero because \(e_z(x)=0\), and a zero-root cell is
zero because \(\theta_{i_Z}(z)=p(z)=0\).  Zero-root functional values,
unused hidden-state rows, and unreached outcome rows therefore may be
filled arbitrarily without changing \(p\).  Rational \(p\) makes every
ratio in (3)--(5) rational, and arbitrary rows may be rational point
masses.  This proves all assertions in item 1.

To identify the sharp region of the causal target, write as in the proof
of the arbitrary-law theorem
\[
 g_{b,x}(y)=\theta_{i_Y}(y\mid b,x).
\]
Truncated factorization for \(do(i_X=x)\), qualitative functionality, and
the equality \(\theta_{i_Z}(z)=p(z)\) give, equation by equation,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 Q_M(x,y)
 &=\sum_{z\in\mathcal Z}\sum_{b\in\mathcal B}
   \theta_{i_Z}(z)\mathbf 1\{b=f(z)\}g_{b,x}(y)\\
 &=\sum_{z\in\mathcal Z}p(z)g_{f(z),x}(y).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
For \(z\in\mathcal Z_+\) with \(p(z,x)>0\), the observed factorization
and guarded division give \(g_{f(z),x}=m_{zx}\).  Splitting (6) into
reached and missing positive-root pairs, while noting that zero roots have
weight zero, yields
\[
 Q_M(x,\cdot)
 =L_x+
 \sum_{z\in\mathcal Z_+:p(z,x)=0}p(z)g_{f(z),x}.
\tag{7}
\]
The weights in the second term are nonnegative and sum to \(M_x\).  If
\(M_x>0\), division of that term by the strict guard \(M_x>0\) gives a
convex combination of elements of \(\Delta(\mathcal Y)\); if
\(M_x=0\), the term is zero.  Hence every compatible target lies in
\[
 \prod_{x\in\mathcal X}\bigl(L_x+M_x\Delta(\mathcal Y)\bigr).
\tag{8}
\]

Conversely, fix any \(q_x\in L_x+M_x\Delta(\mathcal Y)\) for every
\(x\).  When \(M_x>0\), put
\[
 s_x=\frac{q_x-L_x}{M_x}.
\tag{9}
\]
Membership in the translated simplex implies
\[
 q_x-L_x\geq0,
 \qquad
 \sum_y(q_x(y)-L_x(y))=M_x,
\]
so the guarded division in (9) gives
\(s_x\in\Delta(\mathcal Y)\).  When \(M_x=0\), choose any
\(s_x\in\Delta(\mathcal Y)\).  In the construction (3)--(4), set the
common outcome row of every assigned positive profile missing \(x\) to
\(s_x\).  Different values of \(x\) index different conditional-table
rows, so these choices are simultaneous.  Substitution into (7) gives
\[
 Q_M(x,\cdot)=L_x+M_xs_x=q_x
\]
for every \(x\).  This proves the reverse inclusion in (8), hence the
sharp equality in item 2.  If \(p\) and \(q\) are rational, (9) and the
construction use only guarded rational divisions and rational point-mass
fillers, proving rational simultaneous attainment on the original
alphabets.

If \(|\mathcal Y|=1\), each simplex in (8) is a singleton.  Suppose
\(|\mathcal Y|\geq2\).  When every \(M_x=0\), (8) is a singleton.  If
some \(M_{x_0}>0\), two distinct probability vectors in
\(\Delta(\mathcal Y)\), used as the value of \(s_{x_0}\) in (9), give
two compatible original-alphabet models with the same observed law and
different causal vectors.  Thus point identification is equivalent to
\(M_x=0\) for every \(x\).  Each summand defining \(M_x\) is strictly
positive, so
\[
 M_x=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 p(z,x)>0\text{ for every }z\in\mathcal Z_+.
\tag{10}
\]
Requiring (10) for every \(x\) proves item 3.  Notice that the additional
zero-root condition required for point identification of the full
response kernel is absent here for the proved reason in (6): every such
row has coefficient \(p(z)=0\) in the single-intervention target.

Finally, normalization of every reached \(m_{zx}\) gives
\[
 \sum_yL_x(y)
 =\sum_{z:p(z,x)>0}p(z)=1-M_x.
\tag{11}
\]
It follows that
\[
 q_x\in L_x+M_x\Delta(\mathcal Y)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 q_x(y)\geq L_x(y)\text{ for all }y,
 \quad\sum_yq_x(y)=1.
\tag{12}
\]
For the reverse direction of (12), \(q_x-L_x\) is nonnegative and has
sum \(M_x\); divide by \(M_x\) when it is positive, while for
\(M_x=0\) nonnegativity and zero sum force \(q_x=L_x\).  Thus (12) is
the claimed linear-size half-space representation.

One pass through the observed table computes all marginals in
\(O(|\mathcal Z||\mathcal X||\mathcal Y|)\) operations.  Once exact
profile comparison has supplied the profile classes, accumulation of
\(L_x(y)\) and \(M_x\) has the same order, and the collapse test uses
(10).  Every division is performed only under its tested positive
denominator.  This proves item 4 and completes the theorem.
