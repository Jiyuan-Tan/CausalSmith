Let the row in the statement belong to vertex \(i\notin T\), and write its parent configuration as \(u\).  For \(r\in\Delta(\mathcal A_i)\), let \(M^r\) be obtained from \(M\) by replacing only \(\theta_i(\cdot\mid u)\) by \(r\).  When \(i\in W\), only vertex choices \(r=\delta_a\) are asserted to belong to the qualitative-functional class; when \(i\notin W\), every \(r\) is allowed.

First, the joint law of the parents of \(i\) does not depend on the conditional row of \(i\).  Indeed, no descendant of \(i\) can be a parent of \(i\), by acyclicity.  In the causal product, first sum all descendants of \(i\) in reverse topological order.  Once the descendants of such a vertex have been summed, its own conditional row is the only remaining factor involving its state, so its sum is \(1\).  After all proper descendants have been removed in this way, summing over the state of \(i\) also replaces its conditional row by \(1\).  The remaining finite sum computes the parent marginal and contains no entry from the row at \(i\).  Therefore
\[
 P_{M^r}(\operatorname{pa}_G(i)=u)
 =P_M(\operatorname{pa}_G(i)=u)=0.
\]
All full-assignment probabilities are nonnegative.  Hence every full assignment satisfying \(a_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)}=u\) has probability zero under both \(M\) and \(M^r\).  If the parent configuration is different from \(u\), the two causal products use identical rows.  It follows cell by cell that
\[
 P_{M^r}(a_D)=P_M(a_D)
 \quad\text{for every }a_D\in\Omega,
\]
and therefore
\[
 p_{M^r}=p_M.
\]
In particular all treatment marginals are unchanged, so the joint-treatment positivity already possessed by \(M\) is preserved.  Row normalization, nonnegativity, the graph, and the alphabets are also preserved; for vertex choices the functionality declaration is preserved as well.

Because \(i\notin T\), the truncated product for the fixed target coordinate still contains exactly one entry from the row of vertex \(i\).  Grouping the terms according to the value of \(a_i\) when the parents equal \(u\) gives constants \(c\) and \(c_a\), independent of \(r\), such that
\[
 q_{M^r}(t,y)=c+\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_i}c_a r(a).
\]
Thus this coordinate is affine in \(r\).  Since every
\(r\in\Delta(\mathcal A_i)\) has the representation
\[
 r=\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_i}r(a)\delta_a,
\]
affineness yields
\[
 q_{M^r}(t,y)
 =\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_i}r(a)
   q_{M^{\delta_a}}(t,y).
\]
If all vertex values on the right were equal, the function would be constant on the simplex.  The assumed nonconstancy therefore supplies \(a_0,a_1\in\mathcal A_i\) with
\[
 q_{M^{\delta_{a_0}}}(t,y)
 \ne q_{M^{\delta_{a_1}}}(t,y).
\]
Set \(M_0=M^{\delta_{a_0}}\) and \(M_1=M^{\delta_{a_1}}\).  The preceding invariance proves \(p_{M_0}=p_{M_1}\), while the last display separates the target coordinate.  Both models use the original alphabets and belong to \(\mathfrak M\).  Finally, if every unchanged row is rational, the only new entries are zeros and ones, so both CPT families are rational.
