The causal target in this theorem is the single-intervention distribution
\(Q_M(x,y)=P_M(i_Y=y\mid do(i_X=x))\).  The quantities \(e_z\),
\(m_{zx}\), \(L_x\), and \(M_x\) are instead functionals of the observed
law \(p\).  We prove their relationship rather than defining the causal
target to equal them.

Put
\[
 \mathcal Z_+=\{z\in\mathcal Z:p(z)>0\}.
\]
This set is nonempty because \(p\) has total mass one.  Fix first a model
\(M\in\mathfrak M(G,\mathcal A,V,W,T)\) with \(p_M=p\).  By
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:qualitative\text{-}functionality}\), each row of
the mechanism at \(i_A\) is a point mass.  Since \(\mathcal B\) is finite
and nonempty, there is a map \(f:\mathcal Z\to\mathcal B\) such that
\[
 \theta_{i_A}(a\mid z)=\mathbf 1\{a=f(z)\}.
\]
Define the normalized rows
\[
 \lambda(z)=\theta_{i_Z}(z),\qquad
 r_b(x)=\theta_{i_X}(x\mid b),\qquad
 g_{b,x}(y)=\theta_{i_Y}(y\mid b,x).
\]
The displayed edge set and Assumption
\(\mathrm{ass:causal\text{-}factorization}\), followed by summation over
the hidden vertex \(i_A\), give, for every \(z,x,y\),
\[
 p(z,x,y)=\lambda(z)r_{f(z)}(x)g_{f(z),x}(y).
\tag{1}
\]
Summing (1) over \(x,y\), and using normalization of the rows \(r_b\) and
\(g_{b,x}\), gives
\[
 \lambda(z)=p(z).
\tag{2}
\]
For \(z\in\mathcal Z_+\), division of the \(y\)-marginal of (1) by the
strictly positive guard \(p(z)>0\) yields
\[
 e_z(x)=\frac{p(z,x)}{p(z)}=r_{f(z)}(x).
\tag{3}
\]
If in addition \(p(z,x)>0\), division of (1) by that strictly positive
guard yields
\[
 m_{zx}(y)=\frac{p(z,x,y)}{p(z,x)}=g_{f(z),x}(y).
\tag{4}
\]
Consequently, if two positive-mass root states have the same value under
\(f\), then (3) gives equality of their treatment rows and (4) gives
equality of their outcome rows on the common positive support.  Their
observed profiles are therefore equal.  Thus distinct elements of
\(\mathcal R(p)\) require distinct values of \(f\), and necessity of the
feasibility condition follows:
\[
 |\mathcal R(p)|\leq|\mathcal B|.
\tag{5}
\]

Conversely, suppose (5) holds and choose an injection
\(\kappa:\mathcal R(p)\to\mathcal B\).  For every
\(z\in\mathcal Z_+\), set \(f(z)=\kappa(\rho_z)\), and extend \(f\) to
\(\mathcal Z\setminus\mathcal Z_+\) arbitrarily; this extension exists
because \(\mathcal B\) is nonempty.  Set
\[
 \theta_{i_Z}(z)=p(z),
 \qquad
 r_{\kappa(\rho_z)}=e_z
 \quad(z\in\mathcal Z_+).
\tag{6}
\]
The second assignment is well defined because equal profiles have equal
\(e\)-rows.  Whenever \(p(z,x)>0\), set
\[
 g_{\kappa(\rho_z),x}=m_{zx}.
\tag{7}
\]
Again this is well defined: equality of profiles gives equality of all
reached \(m\)-rows.  Fill unreached outcome rows and all rows indexed by
unused hidden states with arbitrary probability vectors, and use the
point-mass mechanism determined by \(f\) at \(i_A\).  If \(p(z)>0\) and
\(p(z,x)>0\), equations (6)--(7) give
\[
 p(z)e_z(x)m_{zx}(y)
 =p(z)\frac{p(z,x)}{p(z)}
       \frac{p(z,x,y)}{p(z,x)}
 =p(z,x,y),
\tag{8}
\]
where both divisions are licensed by their displayed strict guards.  If
\(p(z)>0\) and \(p(z,x)=0\), the generated cell is zero because
\(e_z(x)=0\); if \(p(z)=0\), it is zero because the root factor in (6)
is zero.  Thus the constructed model has observed marginal exactly
\(p\).  Moreover,
\[
 P_M(i_X=x)=\sum_zp(z)e_z(x)=p_X(x)>0
\tag{9}
\]
for every \(x\), by the hypothesis of the theorem.  Hence singleton
joint-treatment positivity holds.  The construction obeys Assumptions
\(\mathrm{ass:causal\text{-}factorization}\),
\(\mathrm{ass:truncated\text{-}intervention}\), and
\(\mathrm{ass:qualitative\text{-}functionality}\), so Definition
\(\mathrm{def:model\text{-}class}\) places it in
\(\mathfrak M(G,\mathcal A,V,W,T)\).  This proves sufficiency and the
explicit realization in item 1.  If \(p\) is rational, all guarded ratios
in (6)--(7) are rational, and the arbitrary rows may be chosen as point
masses; the resulting realizing model is rational.

We next calculate the causal fiber.  Assumption
\(\mathrm{ass:truncated\text{-}intervention}\) replaces the row at
\(i_X\) by a point mass at \(x\), while leaving the root, functional, and
outcome mechanisms unchanged.  Marginalizing the non-outcome vertices
therefore gives
\[
 Q_M(x,\cdot)
 =\sum_{z\in\mathcal Z}\lambda(z)g_{f(z),x}
 =\sum_{z\in\mathcal Z_+}p(z)g_{f(z),x},
\tag{10}
\]
where the second equality uses (2), so zero-mass roots contribute nothing.
Split (10) according to whether \(p(z,x)>0\).  Equation (4) identifies
the reached part as \(L_x\), whence
\[
 Q_M(x,\cdot)
 =L_x+\sum_{z\in\mathcal Z_+:,p(z,x)=0}
       p(z)g_{f(z),x}.
\tag{11}
\]
The coefficients in the final sum are nonnegative and sum to \(M_x\).
If \(M_x>0\), their normalized weighted average is an element of
\(\Delta(\mathcal Y)\); if \(M_x=0\), the final sum is the zero vector.
Thus every compatible causal target satisfies
\[
 Q_M(x,\cdot)\in L_x+M_x\Delta(\mathcal Y)
\quad(x\in\mathcal X).
\tag{12}
\]

For the reverse inclusion, choose any vector
\((q_x)_{x\in\mathcal X}\) in the product on the right-hand side of the
claimed equality and use the realizing construction (6)--(7).  When
\(M_x>0\), define
\[
 s_x=\frac{q_x-L_x}{M_x}.
\tag{13}
\]
Membership of \(q_x\) in \(L_x+M_x\Delta(\mathcal Y)\) and the strict
guard \(M_x>0\) show that (13) belongs to
\(\Delta(\mathcal Y)\).  For every observed profile whose treatment row
misses \(x\), set its common row
\(g_{\kappa(\rho),x}=s_x\).  This is consistent because profile equality
makes the missing status common within a profile class.  When \(M_x=0\),
the corresponding missing rows have total causal weight zero and may be
arbitrary.  Choices for different \(x\)'s affect different conditional
rows and can therefore be made simultaneously.  Substitution in (11)
gives
\[
 Q_M(x,\cdot)=L_x+M_xs_x=q_x
\]
for every \(x\).  This proves the reverse inclusion and hence the sharp
product formula in item 2, using only the prescribed alphabets.  If
\(p\) and \(q\) are rational, then \(L_x\), \(M_x\), and every guarded
row (13) are rational, so the attaining model is rational.

If \(|\mathcal Y|=1\), the simplex \(\Delta(\mathcal Y)\) is a
singleton and so is the product in item 2.  Suppose
\(|\mathcal Y|\geq2\).  If \(M_x=0\) for every \(x\), each factor is a
singleton.  If \(M_{x_0}>0\) for some \(x_0\), choose two distinct point
masses in \(\Delta(\mathcal Y)\) as the two values of \(s_{x_0}\) in
the simultaneous construction above.  The resulting original-alphabet
models have the same observed law but their \(x_0\)-intervention vectors
differ by \(M_{x_0}\) times a nonzero vector.  Hence point identification
holds exactly when every \(M_x=0\).  Since the summands defining \(M_x\)
are strictly positive, this is equivalent to
\[
 p(z,x)>0\quad\text{for every }z\in\mathcal Z_+
 \text{ and every }x\in\mathcal X,
\]
which proves item 3.

Finally, normalization of the observed conditional rows gives
\[
 \sum_{y\in\mathcal Y}L_x(y)
 =\sum_{z:p(z,x)>0}p(z)=1-M_x.
\tag{14}
\]
It follows that
\[
 q_x\in L_x+M_x\Delta(\mathcal Y)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 q_x(y)\geq L_x(y)\ \text{for every }y,
 \qquad \sum_yq_x(y)=1.
\tag{15}
\]
Indeed, the forward implication is immediate.  Conversely,
\(q_x-L_x\) is coordinatewise nonnegative and, by (14), has coordinate
sum \(M_x\); division by the strict guard \(M_x>0\) gives a simplex row,
while \(M_x=0\) forces \(q_x=L_x\).  This proves the half-space
representation.

One pass over the observed table computes all marginals using
\(O(|\mathcal Z||\mathcal X||\mathcal Y|)\) arithmetic operations.
Once the profiles have been compared exactly, counting their distinct
values, computing every \(L_x(y)\) and \(M_x\), and testing all collapse
conditions require another
\(O(|\mathcal Z||\mathcal X||\mathcal Y|)\) guarded operations.  The
representation (15) has \(|\mathcal X||\mathcal Y|\) inequalities and
\(|\mathcal X|\) equalities.  This establishes item 4 and completes the
proof.
