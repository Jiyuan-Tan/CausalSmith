The causal target in this theorem is the post-intervention vector
\(\bigl(Q_M(x,\cdot)\bigr)_{x\in\mathcal X}\), whereas \(p\),
\(e_z\), \(m_{zx}\), \(L_x\), and \(M_x\) are observational
quantities.  We prove the asserted equality between the causal fiber and
the observable product region rather than defining the target by that
region.

Because the displayed graph has root \(i_Z\), has sole parent \(i_Z\)
for \(i_A\), sole parent \(i_A\) for \(i_X\), and parents
\((i_A,i_X)\) for \(i_Y\), qualitative functionality gives a map
\(f:\mathcal Z\to\mathcal B\).  Introduce the mechanism rows
\[
 \lambda(z)=\theta_{i_Z}(z),\qquad
 r_b(x)=\theta_{i_X}(x\mid b),\qquad
 g_{b,x}(y)=\theta_{i_Y}(y\mid b,x).
\]
The recursive factorization assumption and the point-mass row at
\(i_A\) then give, for every \((z,x,y)\),
\[
 p_M(z,x,y)=\lambda(z)r_{f(z)}(x)g_{f(z),x}(y).
\tag{1}
\]
All three factors are nonnegative, each relevant row sums to one, and
summation in (1) yields
\[
 p_M(z)=\lambda(z),\qquad
 p_M(z,x)=\lambda(z)r_{f(z)}(x).
\tag{2}
\]

Suppose first that \(p_M=p\).  If \(p(z)>0\), division of the second
identity in (2) by its strictly positive denominator gives
\[
 e_z(x)=\frac{p(z,x)}{p(z)}=r_{f(z)}(x).
\tag{3}
\]
If also \(p(z,x)>0\), then (1)--(2), divided by that strictly positive
cell marginal, give
\[
 m_{zx}(y)=\frac{p(z,x,y)}{p(z,x)}=g_{f(z),x}(y).
\tag{4}
\]
Consequently, whenever two positive-mass root values satisfy
\(f(z)=f(z')\), (3) makes their \(e\)-vectors equal and (4) makes all
their conditional outcome rows equal on the common positive support.
Thus \(\rho_z=\rho_{z'}\).  The fibers of \(f\) therefore cannot merge
two distinct observed profiles, and hence
\[
 \lvert\mathcal R(p)\rvert\leq\lvert\mathcal B\rvert.
\tag{5}
\]

Conversely, suppose (5) holds and fix an injection
\(\kappa:\mathcal R(p)\to\mathcal B\).  For every \(z\) with
\(p(z)>0\), set \(f(z)=\kappa(\rho_z)\); assign \(f(z)\) arbitrarily
when \(p(z)=0\).  Set
\[
 \lambda(z)=p(z).
\tag{6}
\]
For a hidden state \(b=\kappa(\rho_z)\) used by a positive-mass root
value, set
\[
 r_b=e_z,
 \qquad
 g_{b,x}=m_{zx}\quad\hbox{if }e_z(x)>0.
\tag{7}
\]
These assignments are well defined: equality of profiles gives equality
of the full \(e\)-vectors and of every displayed \(m\)-row.  Each
\(e_z\) is a probability vector by its guarded definition, and each
\(m_{zx}\) is a probability vector whenever it is used.  Complete every
unreached \(g_{b,x}\), every unused \(r_b\), and every row associated
only with zero-root-mass values by an arbitrary probability vector.
Together with the point-mass conditional row implementing \(f\), these
are valid conditional probability tables on the prescribed alphabets.

For \(p(z)>0\) and \(p(z,x)>0\), (6)--(7) give
\[
 \lambda(z)r_{f(z)}(x)g_{f(z),x}(y)
 =p(z)\frac{p(z,x)}{p(z)}
       \frac{p(z,x,y)}{p(z,x)}
 =p(z,x,y),
\tag{8}
\]
where both divisions are licensed by the displayed strict inequalities.
If \(p(z)>0\) but \(p(z,x)=0\), then
\(r_{f(z)}(x)=e_z(x)=0\), so both sides of (8) are zero without any
division by \(p(z,x)\).  If \(p(z)=0\), the factor \(\lambda(z)\) is
zero and again both sides are zero.  Thus the constructed model has
observed marginal \(p\).  Moreover,
\[
 P_M(i_X=x)=\sum_{z,y}p_M(z,x,y)=p_X(x)>0
\]
for every \(x\), by the positivity hypothesis in the statement.  Hence
the singleton-treatment instance of joint-treatment positivity holds.
The construction also satisfies recursive factorization, truncated
intervention semantics, and qualitative functionality, so it belongs to
\(\mathfrak M(G,\mathcal A,V,W,T)\).  This proves both directions of
item 1 and verifies the announced realizing model.  If \(p\) is
rational, every guarded ratio in (7) is rational and all arbitrary rows
may be chosen as point masses, proving the rationality assertion.

We next calculate the causal target.  Under \(do(i_X=x)\), truncated
intervention semantics deletes the factor \(r_{f(z)}(x)\), while the
root and functional mechanisms remain unchanged.  Therefore
\[
 Q_M(x,y)=\sum_{z\in\mathcal Z}\lambda(z)g_{f(z),x}(y)
         =\sum_{z:p(z)>0}p(z)g_{f(z),x}(y).
\tag{9}
\]
For every reached pair \((z,x)\), equation (4) fixes the summand in (9)
at \(p(z)m_{zx}(y)\).  For a missing pair, \(g_{f(z),x}\) is an element
of \(\Delta(\mathcal Y)\).  Thus
\[
 Q_M(x,\cdot)
 =L_x+\sum_{z:p(z)>0,\ p(z,x)=0}p(z)g_{f(z),x}.
\tag{10}
\]
If \(M_x>0\), the second term divided by \(M_x\) is a convex
combination of probability vectors and hence lies in
\(\Delta(\mathcal Y)\); if \(M_x=0\), that term is zero.  Equation
(10) proves the inclusion
\[
 \mathcal Q(p)\subseteq
 \prod_{x\in\mathcal X}\bigl(L_x+M_x\Delta(\mathcal Y)\bigr).
\tag{11}
\]

For the reverse inclusion, choose an arbitrary vector in the product and
write its \(x\)-coordinate as \(q_x=L_x+M_xs_x\), with
\(s_x\in\Delta(\mathcal Y)\) when \(M_x>0\).  Equivalently, under that
strict guard,
\[
 s_x=\frac{q_x-L_x}{M_x}.
\tag{12}
\]
In the realizing construction above, set every used hidden-state outcome
row \(g_{b,x}\) that is unreached observationally at treatment value
\(x\) equal to this common \(s_x\).  If \(M_x=0\), choose those rows
arbitrarily.  This does not change the observed law, since every altered
row is multiplied in (1) either by a zero root mass or by a zero
\(r_b(x)\).  Different values of \(x\) index different conditional rows,
so these choices can be made simultaneously.  Substitution into (10)
gives
\[
 Q_M(x,\cdot)=L_x+M_xs_x=q_x
\]
for every \(x\).  This proves reverse inclusion in (11), simultaneous
attainment, and item 2.  If both \(p\) and \(q\) are rational, then
\(L_x\) and \(M_x\) are rational; (12) is rational under its strict
guard, and all remaining arbitrary rows may again be point masses.

If \(\lvert\mathcal Y\rvert=1\), every simplex in the product is a
singleton.  Suppose \(\lvert\mathcal Y\rvert\geq2\).  When every
\(M_x=0\), the product region is the singleton \((L_x)_x\).  If
\(M_{x_0}>0\) for some \(x_0\), choose two distinct point masses in
\(\Delta(\mathcal Y)\) for \(s_{x_0}\) in the simultaneous construction.
The resulting two original-alphabet models have the same observed law
but intervention vectors differing by \(M_{x_0}\) times a nonzero
vector.  Hence point identification fails.  Since each summand in
\[
 M_x=\sum_{z:p(z)>0,\ p(z,x)=0}p(z)
\]
is strictly positive, \(M_x=0\) holds exactly when
\(p(z,x)>0\) for every \(z\) with \(p(z)>0\).  This proves item 3,
including the stated support characterization.

It remains to verify the representation and operation bound.  For each
\(x\), partitioning the positive-mass root values according to whether
\(p(z,x)\) is positive gives
\[
 \sum_{y\in\mathcal Y}L_x(y)
 =\sum_{z:p(z,x)>0}p(z)=1-M_x.
\tag{13}
\]
Therefore
\[
 q_x\in L_x+M_x\Delta(\mathcal Y)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 q_x(y)\geq L_x(y)\ \text{for all }y,
 \qquad \sum_yq_x(y)=1.
\tag{14}
\]
Indeed, the forward implication is immediate.  Conversely,
\(q_x-L_x\) is coordinatewise nonnegative and has total mass \(M_x\)
by (13).  Division by \(M_x\) gives a probability vector when
\(M_x>0\); if \(M_x=0\), a nonnegative vector of total mass zero is the
zero vector.  Thus (14) holds in both boundary cases.

One pass through the
\(\lvert\mathcal Z\rvert\lvert\mathcal X\rvert
\lvert\mathcal Y\rvert\) cells computes the required marginals.
Conditional on the exact profile comparison specified in the statement,
one further pass computes every guarded ratio, every \(L_x(y)\), every
\(M_x\), the number of distinct profiles, and the collapse test, using
\(O(\lvert\mathcal Z\rvert\lvert\mathcal X\rvert
\lvert\mathcal Y\rvert)\) arithmetic operations.  Formula (14) has
\(\lvert\mathcal X\rvert\lvert\mathcal Y\rvert\) inequalities and
\(\lvert\mathcal X\rvert\) equalities.  This proves item 4 and completes
the theorem.
