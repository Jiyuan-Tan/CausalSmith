We first distinguish the causal object from the observed quantities.  The
array \(K_M\) is defined through a joint intervention.  In contrast,
\(e_z\), \(m_{zx}\), \(\rho_z\), and every set
\(\mathcal K_\pi(p)\) are computed from \(p\).  The proof below establishes
their asserted relationship.

Every division in the definitions is licensed.  Separate root positivity
gives \(p(z)>0\) for every \(z\), and hence
\[
 e_z(x)=\frac{p(z,x)}{p(z)}\geq0,
 \qquad
 \sum_x e_z(x)=1.
\tag{1}
\]
Whenever \(p(z,x)>0\), marginalization gives \(p(z)>0\), and
\[
 m_{zx}(y)=\frac{p(z,x,y)}{p(z,x)}\geq0,
 \qquad
 \sum_y m_{zx}(y)=1.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus \(e_z\in\Delta(\mathcal X)\) and every reached response row belongs
to \(\Delta(\mathcal Y)\).  The additional inequalities \(p_X(x)>0\)
are the second, separate coordinate-positivity condition; they do not
assert \(p(z,x)>0\).

Let \(M\) in the separate-positive class induce \(p\).  Qualitative
functionality supplies a map \(f:\mathcal Z\to\mathcal B\) with
\(A=f(Z)\).  Denote the remaining conditional rows by
\[
 \lambda(z)=\theta_{i_Z}(z),\qquad
 r_a(x)=\theta_{i_X}(x\mid a),\qquad
 g_{a,x}(y)=\theta_{i_Y}(y\mid a,x).
\]
The recursive factorization and summation over the hidden point mass give
the cell identity
\[
 p(z,x,y)=\lambda(z)r_{f(z)}(x)g_{f(z),x}(y).
\tag{3}
\]
Summing (3) over \(x,y\), and over \(y\), respectively, yields
\[
 p(z)=\lambda(z),
 \qquad p(z,x)=p(z)r_{f(z)}(x).
\tag{4}
\]
By root positivity, division of the second identity in (4) by \(p(z)\)
is valid and proves
\[
 e_z=r_{f(z)}.
\tag{5}
\]
If \(p(z,x)>0\), equations (3)--(4) also give
\(r_{f(z)}(x)>0\); dividing (3) by \(p(z,x)\) therefore gives
\[
 m_{zx}=g_{f(z),x}.
\tag{6}
\]
If \(f(z)=f(z')\), equation (5) makes the two \(e\)-vectors equal, so
their positive supports agree, and equation (6) makes every reached
\(m\)-row equal.  Hence \(\rho_z=\rho_{z'}\).  It follows that every
nonempty fiber of \(f\) lies within one observed-profile class.  The
fiber partition has at most \(B\) blocks, so it belongs to
\(\Pi_B(p)\).  In particular, the number \(R\) of profile classes cannot
exceed \(B\).  This proves necessity and the ``never merge'' direction.

Conversely, take any \(\pi\in\Pi_B(p)\).  Assign a distinct state
\(a_C\in\mathcal B\) to each block \(C\in\pi\), and set
\(f(z)=a_C\) for \(z\in C\).  Put \(\lambda(z)=p(z)\).  Since every block
lies within a profile class, the following definitions do not depend on
the representative \(z\in C\):
\[
 r_{a_C}=e_z,
 \qquad
 g_{a_C,x}=m_{zx}\quad\text{if }e_z(x)>0.
\tag{7}
\]
Choose arbitrary simplex rows for an \(x\) with \(e_z(x)=0\), and for
unused hidden states.  Equations (1)--(2) show that all rows in (7) are
valid conditional distributions.  For every observed cell, the induced
law equals \(p\): when \(p(z,x)=0\), equation (1) gives \(e_z(x)=0\) and
both sides are zero; when \(p(z,x)>0\),
\[
 p(z)e_z(x)m_{zx}(y)
 =p(z)\frac{p(z,x)}{p(z)}
       \frac{p(z,x,y)}{p(z,x)}
 =p(z,x,y).
\tag{8}
\]
The denominators in (8) are positive in the stated case.  The resulting
root and \(X\) marginals are the prescribed positive ones, so the model is
in \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{sep}}\).  Taking \(\pi\) to be the partition
into profile classes proves feasibility whenever \(R\leq B\); taking an
arbitrary \(\pi\) proves that every allowed refinement is realizable.
Thus item 1 holds.

We next identify the response kernel.  Under \(do(i_Z=z,i_X=x)\), the
truncated-intervention rule replaces the root and \(X\) rows by point
masses, while the functional equation sets \(A=f(z)\).  Summation over
the resulting hidden point mass gives
\[
 K_M(z,x,\cdot)=g_{f(z),x}.
\tag{9}
\]
For the fiber partition \(\pi_f\), equation (6) fixes (9) to \(m_{zx}\)
on every reached pair.  If \(e_z(x)=0\), then this equality holds for all
members of the block containing \(z\), by equality of profiles, and the
single mechanism row \(g_{f(z),x}\) is a common arbitrary vector
\(s_{C,x}\in\Delta(\mathcal Y)\).  Thus
\(K_M\in\mathcal K_{\pi_f}(p)\), proving
\[
 \{K_M:p_M=p\}\subseteq
 \bigcup_{\pi\in\Pi_B(p)}\mathcal K_\pi(p).
\tag{10}
\]

For the reverse inclusion, fix \(\pi\in\Pi_B(p)\) and
\(k\in\mathcal K_\pi(p)\).  Repeat construction (7), and for every
missing block--treatment pair set
\[
 g_{a_C,x}=s_{C,x},
\tag{11}
\]
where \(s_{C,x}\) is the common row supplied by membership of \(k\) in
\(\mathcal K_\pi(p)\).  Changing these rows does not alter (8), because
their observational multiplier is \(e_z(x)=0\).  Equation (9) now gives
\(K_M=k\) simultaneously at every \((z,x)\).  This proves the reverse
inclusion in (10), sharpness, simultaneous attainment, and preservation of
the original alphabets.  If \(p\) and \(k\) are rational, all guarded
ratios in (7), all rows in (11), and the root row are rational; unused
rows may be chosen as point masses.  The realizing model is therefore
rational.  Item 2 follows.

For the cardinality elbow, let \(C_\rho=\{z:\rho_z=\rho\}\).  Every
admissible partition has at least one block inside each of the \(R\)
distinct profile classes.  At \(B=R\), it consequently has exactly one
block \(C_\rho\) per profile, proving the first assertion of item 3.

For every profile with \(H_\rho\ne\varnothing\), split \(C_\rho\) into
singletons; for every profile with \(H_\rho=\varnothing\), retain the one
block \(C_\rho\).  This partition has exactly
\[
 \sum_{\rho:H_\rho=\varnothing}1
 +\sum_{\rho:H_\rho\ne\varnothing}|C_\rho|
 =R+\sum_{\rho:H_\rho\ne\varnothing}(|C_\rho|-1)
 =B_{\mathrm{sat}}(p)
\tag{12}
\]
blocks.  With this partition, every missing pair \((z,x)\) indexes its own
row \(s_{\{z\},x}\), so construction (11) attains every element of
\(\mathcal K_{\mathrm{ind}}(p)\).  The reverse containment is immediate
from the definition of that set.  Hence equality holds whenever
\(b\geq B_{\mathrm{sat}}(p)\).

To prove minimality, assume \(|\mathcal Y|\geq2\).  Then
\(\Delta(\mathcal Y)\) contains a nondegenerate line segment, so for every
finite \(C_\rho\) with \(H_\rho\ne\varnothing\) one can choose pairwise
distinct tuples
\[
 u_z\in\prod_{x\in H_\rho}\Delta(\mathcal Y),
 \qquad z\in C_\rho.
\tag{13}
\]
Define a kernel in \(\mathcal K_{\mathrm{ind}}(p)\) by assigning the
tuple \(u_z\) to the missing rows of each such \(z\), and using the fixed
reached rows elsewhere.  If two distinct elements of \(C_\rho\) belonged
to one block of a representing partition, the definition of
\(\mathcal K_\pi(p)\) would force their complete missing-row tuples to be
equal, contrary to (13).  Therefore every hole-bearing profile requires
\(|C_\rho|\) blocks, and every other profile requires at least one.  By
(12), any representation of this kernel needs at least
\(B_{\mathrm{sat}}(p)\) blocks.  Thus for
\(R\leq b<B_{\mathrm{sat}}(p)\) the inclusion in the independent-row set
is strict.  Item 3 is proved.

If \(|\mathcal Y|=1\), every response simplex is a singleton.  If every
\(p(z,x)>0\), there are no missing rows, so equations (6) and (9) fix the
entire kernel.  Conversely, suppose \(|\mathcal Y|\geq2\) and some
\(p(z_0,x_0)=0\).  Feasibility supplies an admissible partition; replacing
the free row of the block containing \(z_0\) at \(x_0\) by two distinct
points of \(\Delta(\mathcal Y)\) in construction (11) produces two models
with the same \(p\) and different kernels.  This proves item 4.

Under the single intervention \(do(i_X=x)\), the root retains law
\(p(z)\), the functional equation gives \(A=f(z)\), and the outcome row is
the same row as in (9).  The truncated factorization therefore yields the
projection identity
\[
 Q_M^X(x,\cdot)=\sum_zp(z)g_{f(z),x}
               =\sum_zp(z)K_M(z,x,\cdot).
\tag{14}
\]
Splitting the sum into reached and missing pairs gives
\[
 Q_M^X(x,\cdot)
 =L_x+\sum_{C\in\pi_f:\,x\in H_{\rho(C)}}
       \left(\sum_{z\in C}p(z)\right)s_{C,x}.
\tag{15}
\]
The nonnegative weights in the second term sum to \(M_x\), so (15)
belongs to \(L_x+M_x\Delta(\mathcal Y)\).  Conversely, for any
\(q_x=L_x+M_xs_x\), use one admissible partition and put
\(s_{C,x}=s_x\) for every block missing \(x\).  These row choices are
independent across \(x\), and (15) then equals \(q_x\) simultaneously for
all \(x\).  This proves the product region.  Under joint positivity every
pair is reached, so (6) and (9) give
\[
 K_M(z,x,y)=m_{zx}(y)=\frac{p(z,x,y)}{p(z,x)},
\tag{16}
\]
where the joint-positivity guard licenses the final division.  Item 5
follows.

Finally, a map from \(\mathcal Z\) to a labeled set of \(B\) possible
blocks has \(B^{|\mathcal Z|}\) possibilities, so filtering these maps by
profile compatibility enumerates every admissible partition within the
claimed bound.  For fixed \(\pi\), the reached-row equalities and the
simplex nonnegativity, normalization, and common-row equalities use at
most a constant multiple of
\(|\mathcal Z||\mathcal X||\mathcal Y|\) scalar constraints.  Equations
(1), (2), (6), (8), and (16) list every division and its strict support
guard.  This proves item 6 and completes the theorem.
