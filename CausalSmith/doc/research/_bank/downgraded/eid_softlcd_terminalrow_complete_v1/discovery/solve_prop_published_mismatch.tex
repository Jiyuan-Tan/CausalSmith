Order the vertices as \(1<2<3<4\), which is a topological order for both displayed graphs.  Exact baseline support on \(G_{\times}\) gives
\[
\Lambda^{(0)}=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&0&0\\
\alpha&0&0&0\\
d&b&0&0\\
0&0&c&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \alpha b c d\ne0.
\]
Since this matrix is nilpotent, the finite geometric identity for \(P=(I_4-\Lambda^{(0)})^{-1}\) gives
\[
P=I_4+\Lambda^{(0)}+(\Lambda^{(0)})^2+(\Lambda^{(0)})^3
=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&0&0\\
\alpha&1&0&0\\
d+\alpha b&b&1&0\\
c(d+\alpha b)&cb&c&1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Both \(G_{\times}\) and \(G_{\mathrm{ch}}\) have a directed path from \(i\) to \(j\) exactly when \(i<j\), so \(\overline{G_{\times}}=\overline{G_{\mathrm{ch}}}\).

We first verify every rank test in the definition of \(\mathsf H_{\mathrm{obs}}\), rather than only the test that will fail for the actual rows.  For the candidate \(G_{\mathrm{ch}}\), at \(j=1\) one has \(C_1=\{2\}\) and \(R_1=\{3,4\}\), and hence
\[
P[R_1,C_1]=\begin{pmatrix}b\\cb\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
P[R_1,C_1\cup\{1\}]
=\begin{pmatrix}
b&d+\alpha b\\
cb&c(d+\alpha b)
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The second row of the augmented matrix is \(c\) times its first row.  Because \(b\ne0\), both matrices have rank one.  At \(j=2\), \(C_2=\{3\}\) and \(R_2=\{4\}\), so
\[
P[R_2,C_2]=(c),
\qquad
P[R_2,C_2\cup\{2\}]=(c\;cb),
\]
and both ranks are one because \(c\ne0\).  At \(j=3\) and \(j=4\), the set \(R_j=\operatorname{de}_{G_{\mathrm{ch}}}(j)\setminus C_j\) is empty, so both sides of each rank equality are zero.  These calculations use only the exact nonzero baseline support and therefore hold for every parameter point in \(\mathcal M^{\circ}_{\mathrm{term},p,4}(G_{\times},G_{\mathrm{ch}})\).  The definition of the published class now yields
\[
G_{\mathrm{ch}}\in\mathsf H_{\mathrm{obs}}(G_{\times}).
\]

We next compute the rows that the soft interventions actually produce.  Acyclicity and the support of \(\delta_k\) imply \(\delta_k^{\mathsf T}Pe_k=0\): every coordinate in the support of \(\delta_k\) is a parent of \(k\), whereas \(P_{ik}=0\) for every such parent \(i\).  Consequently, direct multiplication shows
\[
\bigl(I_4-\Lambda^{(0)}-e_k\delta_k^{\mathsf T}\bigr)
\bigl(P+Pe_k\delta_k^{\mathsf T}P\bigr)=I_4,
\]
and therefore
\[
\Delta^{(k)}=Pe_k\delta_k^{\mathsf T}P=Pe_kh_k^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Thus the row factor in the inverse-path update is precisely the actual row \(h_k^{\mathsf T}=\delta_k^{\mathsf T}P\).  Exact change support gives \(\delta_3^{\mathsf T}=(\delta,\beta,0,0)\) and \(\delta_4^{\mathsf T}=(0,0,\gamma,0)\), whence
\[
h_3^{\mathsf T}=(\delta+\alpha\beta,\beta,0,0),
\qquad
h_4^{\mathsf T}=\bigl(\gamma(d+\alpha b),\gamma b,\gamma,0\bigr).
\]
It follows from the definition of the affine block that
\[
M_1=H[\{3,4\},\{2\}]
=\begin{pmatrix}\beta\\\gamma b\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
(M_1\mid b_1)=H[\{3,4\},\{2,1\}]
=\begin{pmatrix}
\beta&\delta+\alpha\beta\\
\gamma b&\gamma(d+\alpha b)
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The determinant of the augmented matrix is
\[
\det(M_1\mid b_1)
=\gamma\bigl(\beta(d+\alpha b)-b(\delta+\alpha\beta)\bigr)
=\gamma(\beta d-b\delta).
\]
This is not the zero polynomial.  Indeed, at
\[
\alpha=b=c=d=\beta=\gamma=1,
\qquad \delta=2,
\qquad \delta_{21}=1,
\]
it equals \(-1\).  At the same point all four baseline edge coefficients and all four required changes are nonzero, and the post-change coefficients on \(1\to2\), \(2\to3\), \(1\to3\), and \(3\to4\) are respectively \(2,2,3,2\); hence the exact-support and retained-edge conditions hold.

For completeness, this numerical point certifies a nonempty admissible generic set, not merely an isolated specialization.  Parameterize the structural-support stratum by its free edge and change coordinates.  The preceding strict nonzero conditions persist on a nonempty relatively Euclidean-open neighborhood \(\mathcal O\).  By the definition of \(\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{term}}\), after identically zero polynomials have been deleted, the product of its members with \(\gamma(\beta d-b\delta)\) is a nonzero polynomial in these free coordinates.  A nonzero real polynomial cannot vanish on a nonempty Euclidean-open set (an induction on the number of variables reduces this assertion to the fact that a nonzero univariate polynomial has finitely many roots).  Hence \(\mathcal O\) meets the simultaneous nonvanishing locus defining \(\mathcal M^{\circ}_{\mathrm{term},p,4}(G_{\times},G_{\mathrm{ch}})\).  The remaining model coordinates may be chosen admissibly: for \(p=2,3,4\), respectively, one may take loading columns \((1,r)^{\mathsf T}\) with four distinct \(r\)'s, the columns \(e_1,e_2,e_3,(1,1,1)^{\mathsf T}\), or the four standard basis vectors, and then perturb within the open full-row-rank and pairwise-noncollinearity conditions.  The variance vectors \(v,t\) may be any positive vectors, including choices with \(t_k=v_k\), because neither the displayed ranks nor the terminal exceptional conditions impose variance activity.

At every point of this nonempty relative Zariski-open intersection where \(\gamma(\beta d-b\delta)\ne0\), exact change support gives \(\beta\ne0\), so \(\operatorname{rank}M_1=1\), while the nonzero determinant gives \(\operatorname{rank}(M_1\mid b_1)=2\).  Thus \(M_1x_1=b_1\) is inconsistent and \(\mathcal A_1=\varnothing\).  The sound-and-complete terminal certificate then implies
\[
G_{\mathrm{ch}}\notin\mathsf H_{\mathrm{soft}}(G_{\times}).
\]

Finally, under the observational-path specialization \((\beta,\delta)=(b,d)\), the actual augmented matrix becomes
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
b&d+\alpha b\\
\gamma b&\gamma(d+\alpha b)
\end{pmatrix},
\]
whose second row is \(\gamma\) times its first; its rank is one because \(b\ne0\).  Equivalently, the determinant has been forced to \(\gamma(bd-bd)=0\).  The coordinates \((\beta,\delta)\) describe intervention changes, whereas \((b,d)\) describe baseline edges; exact support and retention require nonvanishing but do not impose either equality.  The substitution therefore restricts the model to a proper algebraic subset and is not a valid generic-rank argument.  We have exhibited a same-closure candidate that belongs to Definition 3.14's class but not to the corrected actual-row class on an admissible relative Zariski-open set.  This contradicts the asserted equivalence in Theorem 3.15 as written, and the calculation is unchanged over the entire positive-variance ambient domain.
