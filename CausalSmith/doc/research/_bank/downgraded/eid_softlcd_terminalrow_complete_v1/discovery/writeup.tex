\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Equation (20) of Leyes Carreno, Meroni, and Seigal uses the actual inverse-update rows, while Definition 3.14 deliberately records a baseline inverse-path class. The gap is in Theorem 3.15's identification of those two rank systems: specializing independent intervention changes to baseline coefficients can lower an augmented rank. Problem 5.2 poses a separate covariance-versus-higher-cumulant question, but neither the fork chamberwise density alternative nor a graph-specific non-quantifier-elimination criterion is established.

\paragraph{Niche.} The contribution separates three levels that had been conflated: exact actual-row graph compatibility, quotient-invariant pointwise observable covariance separation, and robust chamberwise covariance density. Terminal-copy linkage plus one batched affine reduction per candidate column completely solves the first. The rational fork family and its \(-2\) affine inconsistency solve a concrete pointwise instance of the second. The third remains an explicitly delimited projection problem.

\paragraph{Fill.} The paper delivers the corrected \(O(q^4)\) terminal algorithm, an exact finite comparison with the unchanged Definition 3.14 class, an anchor-domain counterexample to Theorem 3.15, and the pointwise fork covariance-minus-soft graph witness modulo common sign, scale, and permutation action. Covariance factor branches and the eight-system fork reduction remain secondary diagnostics. No chamberwise dense cover, full-dimensional separator, all-pair compiler, pathwidth guarantee, coefficient-height bound, or infinite generic-strictness family is claimed.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:finite-dimensions]\label{ass:finite-dimensions}
\(2\le p<q\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This is the paper's deliberate noninjective measurement regime. It covers applications with more latent mechanisms than observed coordinates and isolates the positive-real projection problem that injective recovery theorems do not address.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:dag]\label{ass:dag}
\(G\) is acyclic.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Acyclic linear structural equation model, \cite{LeyesCarrenoMeroniSeigal2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:topological-order]\label{ass:topological-order}
The labels in \(V\) form a topological order of \(G\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Topologically ordered DAG coordinates, \cite{SquiresSeigalBhateUhler2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:candidate-dag]\label{ass:candidate-dag}
\(G'\) is acyclic.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Candidate DAG, \cite{LeyesCarrenoMeroniSeigal2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:candidate-closure]\label{ass:candidate-closure}
\(\overline{G'}=\overline G\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Same-transitive-closure comparison, \cite{LeyesCarrenoMeroniSeigal2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:baseline-support]\label{ass:baseline-support}
\(\Lambda^{(0)}_{ij}\ne0\) if and only if \(j\to i\) is an edge of \(G\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Exact nonzero DAG support, \cite{LeyesCarrenoMeroniSeigal2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:mixing-rank]\label{ass:mixing-rank}
\(\operatorname{rank}(F)=p\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Full-row-rank linear mixing, \cite{LeyesCarrenoMeroniSeigal2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:mixing-nonzero]\label{ass:mixing-nonzero}
Every column of \(F\) is nonzero.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Nonzero LCD factors, \cite{LeyesCarrenoMeroniSeigal2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:mixing-columns]\label{ass:mixing-columns}
No two columns of \(F\) are collinear.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Noncollinear LCD factors, \cite{LeyesCarrenoMeroniSeigal2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:baseline-variance]\label{ass:baseline-variance}
\(v_i>0\) for every \(i\in V\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Positive structural error variances, \cite{SquiresSeigalBhateUhler2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:change-support]\label{ass:change-support}
\(\delta_{kj}\ne0\) if and only if \(j\in\operatorname{pa}_{G}(k)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Single-node soft intervention changing every incoming edge, \cite{LeyesCarrenoMeroniSeigal2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:retained-edges]\label{ass:retained-edges}
\(\Lambda^{(0)}_{kj}+\delta_{kj}\ne0\) for every \(j\in\operatorname{pa}_{G}(k)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This retained-edge soft-intervention condition describes perturbations that modify rather than delete active regulatory inputs; any continuously distributed perturbation away from the finitely many deletion values satisfies it almost surely.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:intervention-variance]\label{ass:intervention-variance}
\(t_k>0\) for every \(k\in V\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Positive intervened error variance, \cite{SquiresSeigalBhateUhler2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:error-independence]\label{ass:error-independence}
The coordinates of \(\varepsilon^{(k)}\) are mutually independent in every context \(k\in K\).

\section{Main results}

\begin{theorem}[thm:terminal-certificate]\label{thm:terminal-certificate}
For every \(G'\) with \(\overline{G'}=\overline G\) and every \(\theta\in\mathcal M^{\circ}_{\mathrm{term},p,q}(G,G')\), \(G'\in\mathsf H_{\mathrm{soft}}(G)\) if and only if, for every \(j\in V\), the affine space \(\mathcal A_j\) is nonempty and none of \(x_{ji}\), \(d_{ji}(x_j)\), or \(x_{ji}+d_{ji}(x_j)\), for \(i\in C_j\), vanishes identically on \(\mathcal A_j\). For all \(R,C\subseteq V\), \(\operatorname{rank}H[R,C]\) equals the maximum number of vertex-disjoint paths from \(C\) to terminal copies \(R^{\star}\) in \(G^{\mathrm{term}}\). These conditions give sound and complete membership and nonmembership certificates in \(O(q^5)\) rational-function field operations. The baseline-path class in Definition 3.14 agrees with corrected actual-row compatibility exactly when these tests agree with its baseline inverse-path rank tests for every same-closure candidate \(G'\); this is an exact finite graph-input decision procedure, and the four-node counterexample below proves that the equivalence asserted by Theorem 3.15 is false without this agreement condition. This structural result holds throughout the positive-variance ambient model, including \(t_k=v_k\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a candidate \(G'\) with \(\overline{G'}=\overline G\).  Work first over the rational-function field \(\mathbb K\) generated by the baseline edge coefficients and the intervention-change coefficients having the exact supports prescribed by \(G\).  Since \(G\) is acyclic, \(\Lambda^{(0)}\) is nilpotent in a topological order, and therefore
\[
 P=(I_q-\Lambda^{(0)})^{-1}
   =I_q+\Lambda^{(0)}+\cdots+(\Lambda^{(0)})^{q-1}.
\]
For \(c,k\in V\), the definition of the actual intervention row gives
\[
 H_{kc}=h_{kc}
 =\sum_{i\in\operatorname{pa}_{G}(k)}\delta_{ki}P_{ic}.
\]
Every entry \(P_{ic}\) is the sum of the weights of all directed baseline paths from \(c\) to \(i\).  Appending the edge \(i\to k^\star\), labelled \(\delta_{ki}\), is a bijection from the terms in the last display to directed paths from \(c\) to the terminal copy \(k^\star\) in \(G^{\mathrm{term}}\).  Thus \(H\) is the path matrix from original vertices to terminal copies.

Let \(C_0\subseteq C\), \(R_0\subseteq R\), and \(|C_0|=|R_0|=m\).  Expand \(\det H[R_0,C_0]\), then expand every entry as its path sum.  If a path family has an intersecting pair, choose the first common original vertex of the lexicographically first such pair and exchange the two tails after that vertex.  The exchange preserves the edge monomial and reverses the endpoint permutation, so it is a sign-reversing involution on intersecting families.  Its survivors are precisely the vertex-disjoint path families.  A surviving edge union determines its constituent paths and endpoint permutation: every selected internal vertex has one selected entering and one selected leaving edge, every source in \(C_0\) begins one path, and every terminal copy in \(R_0^\star\) ends one path.  Hence two distinct surviving families cannot produce the same monomial with opposite signs.  Consequently,
\[
 \det H[R_0,C_0]\ne0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \text{there are \(m\) vertex-disjoint paths from \(C_0\) to \(R_0^\star\)}.
\]
Taking the largest nonzero minor yields
\[
 \operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb K}H[R,C]
 =\max\bigl\{m:\text{there are \(m\) vertex-disjoint paths from \(C\) to \(R^\star\)}\bigr\}.
\]
Vertex splitting with capacity one at every original vertex converts the maximum on the right into an integral maximum-flow problem; a linkage certifies the lower bound and a vertex cut certifies the upper bound.

Every structurally nonzero path minor needed above is included in \(\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{term}}\).  Therefore no such minor vanishes at a point of \(\mathcal M^{\circ}_{\mathrm{term},p,q}(G,G')\), while structurally zero minors remain zero under every specialization.  The displayed rank identity consequently holds at every stated parameter point, not merely over \(\mathbb K\).

We next derive the affine conditions.  Write \(U=I_q-\Lambda'^{(0)}\), and for each edge \(j\to i\) of \(G'\) put \(x_{ji}=\Lambda'^{(0)}_{ij}\).  Direct multiplication gives
\[
 (HU)_{kj}=H_{kj}-\sum_{i\in C_j}H_{ki}x_{ji}.
\]
If \(k=j\), then \(H_{jj}=0\), and no child of \(j\) can be an ancestor of \(j\), so the displayed entry is zero.  If \(k\notin\operatorname{de}_{G'}(j)\), same closure implies that neither \(j\) nor a child of \(j\) is an ancestor of \(k\); every term is again zero.  For the remaining nonchildren \(k\in R_j\), the required zero is exactly
\[
 H[R_j,C_j]x_j=H[R_j,j],
 \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad M_jx_j=b_j.
\]
For a child \(i\in C_j\), the candidate baseline coefficient, the candidate intervention change, and the candidate post-change coefficient are respectively
\[
 x_{ji},\qquad
 (HU)_{ij}=H[i,j]-H[i,C_j]x_j=d_{ji}(x_j),\qquad
 x_{ji}+d_{ji}(x_j).
\]
Thus exact baseline support, exact change support, and edge retention are exactly the three asserted nonvanishing conditions.  No support condition has been omitted: all nonparent entries of \(HU\) are either among the equations indexed by \(R_j\) or are the automatic zeros just proved.

By the batched affine-support lemma, one row reduction of each \((M_j\mid b_j)\) decides feasibility, and the products \(L_jx_j^0+c_j\) and \(L_jN_j\) decide all forbidden forms simultaneously.  If a system is inconsistent or a forbidden form is identically zero, every candidate \(U\) violates a required condition.  If all systems are feasible and no forbidden form vanishes identically, the finite-hyperplane argument in that lemma gives one \(x_j\in\mathcal A_j\) avoiding all forbidden forms for every \(j\).  The column blocks are independent, so these choices assemble into one matrix \(U=I_q-\Lambda'^{(0)}\) with exact support \(G'\), exact intervention-change support, and retained changed edges.  Conversely, any admissible \(U\) provides such a point in every affine block.  This proves the sound-and-complete equivalence with the corrected actual-row class \(\mathsf H_{\mathrm{soft}}(G)\).  The augmented affine-support minors in \(\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{term}}\) make feasibility and identical-vanishing decisions constant on the stated generic locus, so the pointwise test is equivalent to the universal generic quantifier in the definition of that class.

Computing \(P\) and \(H\) costs at most \(O(q^3)\) field operations.  The batched lemma costs \(O(q^4)\) over all columns.  Hence the complete per-candidate test actually satisfies the sharper bound
\[
 O(q^4),
\]
which implies the printed \(O(q^5)\) bound.  An inconsistent reduced row or an identically-zero stacked row is a nonmembership certificate; a linkage or cut certifies each symbolic rank used, and points \(x_j\) avoiding all forbidden forms certify membership.  This is a rational-function field-operation count only; it asserts no expanded bit complexity or coefficient-height bound.

 &=I_q+e_kh_k^{\mathsf T}-e_k\delta_k^{\mathsf T}P

 &=I_q.
 \end{aligned}

\[
 (I_q-\Lambda^{(k)})^{-1}=P+Pe_kh_k^{\mathsf T},
 \quad
 \Delta^{(k)}=Pe_kh_k^{\mathsf T},
 \quad

 \quad

\]
where the third identity uses \(P_{kk}=1\).  Also \(A^{(k)}e_k=a_k\).  Expanding the covariance and using \(S^{(k)}=S+(t_k-v_k)e_ke_k^{\mathsf T}\) therefore yields

 &=(A+a_kh_k^{\mathsf T})S(A+a_kh_k^{\mathsf T})^{\mathsf T}-ASA^{\mathsf T}

 \end{aligned}

one has \(D_k=a_kw_k^{\mathsf T}+w_ka_k^{\mathsf T}\).

\[

 =\ell m^{\mathsf T}+m\ell^{\mathsf T}.
\]
For every indeterminate vector \(y\), equality of quadratic forms gives
\[
 (y^{\mathsf T}a_k)(y^{\mathsf T}w_k)
 =(y^{\mathsf T}\ell)(y^{\mathsf T}m).
\]
The ranges on both sides show that \(\ell,m\in\operatorname{span}(a_k,w_k)\).  In the ordered basis \((a_k,w_k)\), write \(\ell=(r,s)^{\mathsf T}\) and \(m=(u,v)^{\mathsf T}\).  Comparing the two diagonal coefficients and the cross coefficient gives
\[
 ru=0,\qquad sv=0,\qquad rv+su\ne0.
\]
The last inequality also says that \(\ell,m\) are independent.  Hence either \(r=v=0\) or \(s=u=0\), so the unordered pair of lines \(\{\mathbb Ra_k,\mathbb Rw_k\}\) equals \(\{\mathbb R\ell,\mathbb Rm\}\). \end{proof}

\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:fork-eight-branch-classification]\label{thm:fork-eight-branch-classification}
For \(p=2\) and \((G_{\mathrm{fork}},G'_{\mathrm{fork}})\), the regular covariance-twin problem on \(\mathcal M^{\circ}_{\mathrm{fork},2,4}(G_{\mathrm{fork}},G'_{\mathrm{fork}})\) is exactly the union of eight rational systems in two candidate scalars \((s,x)\).  On each system, \(B_{\ell}v'=\operatorname{vech}(\Sigma^{(0)})\) has the form \(v'=v'_0+sn\), \(m_2=\zeta\ell_1+\rho_2\ell_2\), and \(g=v'_1x\ell_1+v'_2\ell_2\).  For \(k\in\{3,4\}\),
\[
 \beta_k=\frac{\det[m_k,\ell_k]}{\det[g,\ell_k]},\qquad
 m_k-\beta_kg=\rho_k\ell_k,\qquad

and

 t'_k=v'_k+2\rho_k-\beta_k^2(v'_1x^2+v'_2),
 \qquad

This result is a sound-and-complete regular reduction; it makes no claim that the projection of the eight systems is dense, or has full-dimensional complement, in every full-dimensional true-parameter chamber.

\begin{proof}
On the fork generic locus, vertex \(1\) is the only source. By the covariance-branch lemma, \(D_1\) has its unique regular factor line, whereas each of \(D_2,D_3,D_4\) has rank two and has exactly two real factor lines. Hence the regular factorization has exactly
\[
 1\cdot 2^3=8
\]
line branches. The rational projective gauge in the factor-branch definition fixes the scale on every selected line, so no additional scaling branch remains.

The exceptional polynomials used here are not identically zero. Indeed, substitution of the worked true point with \(T=2\) into the covariance-difference formula of \(\mathrm{lem:covariance\text{-}branches}\) gives the following unnormalized factor choices:
\[
 \ell_1=(1,2)^{\mathsf T},\qquad
 \ell_2\in\{(2,3)^{\mathsf T},(5,8)^{\mathsf T}\},\qquad
 \ell_3\in\{(7,11)^{\mathsf T},(87/2,139/2)^{\mathsf T}\},
\]
and
\[
 \ell_4\in\{(4,7)^{\mathsf T},(13,45/2)^{\mathsf T}\}.
\]
For the four choices of \((\ell_2,\ell_3)\), direct expansion of the determinant of the first three columns of \(B_{\ell}\) gives, respectively,
\[
 3,\qquad \frac{595}{4},\qquad -6,\qquad -\frac{35}{2}.
\]
These values do not depend on the choice of \(\ell_4\). The endpoint determinants are
\[
 \det[\ell_1,\ell_2]\in\{-1,-2\},\qquad
 \det[\ell_3,\ell_4]
 \in\left\{5,\frac{29}{2},\frac{53}{2},\frac{301}{4}\right\}.
\]
Thus every displayed value is nonzero. Each relevant minor is therefore a nonzero polynomial in the true parameters. By the definitions of \(\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{fork}}\) and \(\mathcal M^{\circ}_{\mathrm{fork},2,4}\), the selected chart denominators and these rank minors do not vanish on the stated generic locus.

Fix one of the eight branches. Because \(p=2\), equality of the three distinct entries of the baseline covariance is exactly
\[
 B_{\ell}v'=\operatorname{vech}(\Sigma^{(0)}),\qquad
 B_{\ell}
 =\left[
   (\ell_{k1}^{2},\ell_{k1}\ell_{k2},\ell_{k2}^{2})^{\mathsf T}
  \right]_{k=1}^{4}.
\]
The preceding nonvanishing minor and the fact that \(B_{\ell}\) has three rows imply
\[
 \operatorname{rank}B_{\ell}=3.
\]
Consequently the displayed linear map is onto \(\mathbb R^3\), and its complete solution set is the affine line
\[
 v'=v'_0+s n,\qquad n\in\ker B_{\ell}\setminus\{0\}.
\]
Candidate baseline positivity is exactly the four inequalities \(v'_i>0\). In particular, every division by a component of \(v'\) below is justified on the legal region.

The nonzero determinant \(\det[\ell_1,\ell_2]\) makes \((\ell_1,\ell_2)\) a basis of \(\mathbb R^2\). There are therefore unique scalars \(\zeta,\rho_2\) satisfying
\[
 m_2=\zeta\ell_1+\rho_2\ell_2.
\]
For the candidate graph, vertex \(2\) has the sole ancestor and parent \(1\). Thus the branch equation \(Lz_2+\rho_2\ell_2=m_2\) forces
\[
 z_2=(\zeta,0,0,0)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Since \(z_2=\operatorname{diag}(v')h'_2\), it follows that
\[
 h'_2=(\zeta/v'_1,0,0,0),\qquad
 t'_2=v'_2+2\rho_2-\frac{\zeta^2}{v'_1}.
\]
The exact-change condition is \(\zeta\ne0\), intervention-variance positivity is \(t'_2>0\), and if \(x=\Lambda'^{(0)}_{21}\), retention of the edge \(1\to2\) is
\[
 x+\frac{\zeta}{v'_1}\ne0.
\]

For \(k\in\{3,4\}\), the candidate graph gives \(\operatorname{pa}_{G'_{\mathrm{fork}}}(k)=\{2\}\) and \(\operatorname{an}_{G'_{\mathrm{fork}}}(k)=\{1,2\}\). Write a possible actual row as \(h'_k=(r_1,r_2,0,0)\). Multiplication by \(U=I_4-\Lambda'^{(0)}\) gives
\[
 \delta'_{k1}=r_1-xr_2,\qquad \delta'_{k2}=r_2.
\]
Exact support \(\operatorname{supp}(\delta'_k)=\{2\}\) is therefore equivalent to
\[
 h'_k=\beta_k(x,1,0,0),\qquad \beta_k\ne0.
\]
It follows from \(z_k=\operatorname{diag}(v')h'_k\) that
\[
 z_k=\beta_k(v'_1x,v'_2,0,0)^{\mathsf T},\qquad
 Lz_k=\beta_k g,\qquad
 g=v'_1x\ell_1+v'_2\ell_2.
\]
The factor equation becomes
\[
 m_k-\beta_kg=\rho_k\ell_k.
\]
Taking determinants with \(\ell_k\) gives
\[
 \det[m_k,\ell_k]-\beta_k\det[g,\ell_k]=0.
\]
Since \(D_k=\ell_km_k^{\mathsf T}+m_k\ell_k^{\mathsf T}\) has rank two, the vectors \(\ell_k,m_k\) are independent, and hence \(\det[m_k,\ell_k]\ne0\). A feasible solution therefore cannot have \(\det[g,\ell_k]=0\), and division yields
\[
 \beta_k=\frac{\det[m_k,\ell_k]}{\det[g,\ell_k]}.
\]
Substitution in the variance identity from the complete covariance branch gives
\[
 t'_k
 =v'_k+2\rho_k-\sum_{i=1}^{4}\frac{z_{ki}^{2}}{v'_i}
 =v'_k+2\rho_k-\beta_k^2(v'_1x^2+v'_2),
\]
and legality is exactly \(t'_k>0\). No argument divides by \(t'_k-v'_k\), so the equality \(t'_k=v'_k\) remains admissible. At the source \(1\), one has \(h'_1=z_1=0\), \(m_1=\rho_1\ell_1\), and \(t'_1=v'_1+2\rho_1>0\), as required by the inherited branch constraints.

It remains to recover the baseline structural mixing. Put
\[
 b=\Lambda'^{(0)}_{32},\qquad c=\Lambda'^{(0)}_{42}.
\]
Then
\[
 F'=LU
 =\bigl[\ell_1-x\ell_2,\ \ell_2-b\ell_3-c\ell_4,\ \ell_3,\ \ell_4\bigr].
\]
The explicit branch inequations require the first, third, and fourth columns here to be nonzero and pairwise noncollinear. Because \(\det[\ell_3,\ell_4]\ne0\), the affine map
\[
 (b,c)\longmapsto\ell_2-b\ell_3-c\ell_4
\]
is onto \(\mathbb R^2\). Requiring its image to be nonzero and not collinear with any of the other three columns excludes only finitely many proper affine subsets of the \((b,c)\)-plane. The conditions
\[
 b\ne0,\qquad c\ne0,\qquad b+\beta_3\ne0,\qquad c+\beta_4\ne0
\]
exclude four additional proper affine lines and express exact baseline support and retained edges. A finite union of proper affine lines and points cannot cover \(\mathbb R^2\), so legal \(b,c\) exist.

Necessity of all displayed equations and inequations follows from \(\mathrm{lem:covariance\text{-}branches}\). Conversely, start with a solution of one of the eight systems, choose \(b,c\) outside the preceding finite forbidden union, set \(F'=LU\), and set \(\delta'_k{}^{\mathsf T}=h'_k{}^{\mathsf T}U\). The baseline equation gives
\[
 \Sigma'^{(0)}=L\operatorname{diag}(v')L^{\mathsf T}=\Sigma^{(0)}.
\]
For every \(k\), write \(z_k=\operatorname{diag}(v')h'_k\). The constructed identities imply
\[
 m_k=Lz_k+\rho_k\ell_k,\qquad
 h'_k{}^{\mathsf T}\operatorname{diag}(v')h'_k+t'_k-v'_k=2\rho_k.
\]
Applying the covariance-difference identity to the candidate therefore gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 D'_k
 &=\ell_k(Lz_k)^{\mathsf T}+(Lz_k)\ell_k^{\mathsf T}
   +2\rho_k\ell_k\ell_k^{\mathsf T}\\
 &=\ell_km_k^{\mathsf T}+m_k\ell_k^{\mathsf T}
 =D_k.
\end{aligned}
\]
Together with the baseline equality, this proves \(\Sigma'^{(k)}=\Sigma^{(k)}\) for all five contexts \(k\in K\). Thus every legal twin enters one of the eight systems, and every legal solution reconstructs a twin satisfying the original covariance equations. Finally, \(\mathrm{prop:fork\text{-}rational\text{-}collision}\) supplies such a legal twin for every \(T>0\), \(T\ne1\), so completeness places each worked point on one of the eight systems. This proves exactly the asserted regular two-scalar reduction and no chamberwise density conclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:information-nesting]\label{prop:information-nesting}
For every \(2\le p<q\) and every DAG \(G\), \(\mathsf H_{\mathrm{soft}}(G)\subseteq\mathsf{Cov}_p(G)\) for the generic chamberwise covariance functional. The claim covers both signs of every true variance change, makes no pointwise assertion on \(t_k=v_k\), and allows candidate twins with \(t'_k=v'_k\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(G'\in\mathsf H_{\mathrm{soft}}(G)\), and let \(C\) be an arbitrary full-dimensional connected semialgebraic chamber of \(\mathcal M^{\circ}_{\mathrm{branch},p,q}(G,G')\).  We prove that \(\mathcal E_p(G,G')\cap C\) is relatively dense in \(C\), which is stronger than the condition required in the definition of \(\mathsf{Cov}_p(G)\).

First, the terminal-generic locus is relatively dense in \(C\).  Indeed, by the definitions of the exceptional-polynomial families and generic loci, its complement in \(C\) is contained in the union of the zero sets of finitely many polynomials that are nonzero on the ambient coordinate space for the fixed graph pair.  A nonzero real polynomial cannot vanish on a nonempty Euclidean open set: this follows by induction on the number of variables from the fact that a nonzero univariate polynomial has only finitely many roots.  Since \(C\) is full-dimensional in the ambient semialgebraic model, each such zero set has empty relative interior in \(C\), as does their finite union.  Consequently,
\[
 C\cap\mathcal M^{\circ}_{\mathrm{term},p,q}(G,G')
 \quad\text{is relatively dense in }C.
\]

Let \(O\subseteq C\) be a nonempty relatively open set.  Choose
\[
 \theta_0=(F_0,\Lambda^{(0)}_0,\delta_0,v_0,t_0)
 \in O\cap\mathcal M^{\circ}_{\mathrm{term},p,q}(G,G').
\]
Write \(P_0=(I_q-\Lambda^{(0)}_0)^{-1}\), let \(H_0\) be its actual-row matrix, and abbreviate the structural coordinates other than the mixing matrix by
\(
 \eta_0=(\Lambda^{(0)}_0,\delta_0,v_0,t_0).
\)
Because \(G'\in\mathsf H_{\mathrm{soft}}(G)\), the corrected-class definition, equivalently the terminal certificate, supplies a unit lower-triangular matrix
\[
 U_0=I_q-\Lambda'^{(0)}
\]
with exact baseline support \(G'\) such that, for every \(k\in V\),
\[
 \delta'_k{}^{\mathsf T}:=h_{0k}^{\mathsf T}U_0
\]
has exact support \(\operatorname{pa}_{G'}(k)\), and
\(
 \Lambda'^{(0)}_{kj}+\delta'_{kj}\ne0
\)
for every \(j\in\operatorname{pa}_{G'}(k)\).  Thus all candidate structural support and retained-edge conditions already hold at the fixed structural coordinates.

It remains to arrange the candidate mixing conditions without leaving \(O\).  Put \(B=P_0U_0\).  Both factors are invertible, so the columns \(b_1,\ldots,b_q\) of \(B\) are nonzero and every pair \(b_i,b_j\), \(i\ne j\), is linearly independent.  For a variable matrix \(\widetilde F\in\mathbb R^{p\times q}\), define the bad set
\[
 \mathcal Z_B=
 \bigcup_{j=1}^q\{\widetilde F:\widetilde F b_j=0\}
 \;\cup\!
 \bigcup_{1\le i<j\le q}
 \{\widetilde F:\operatorname{rank}[\widetilde F b_i\;\widetilde F b_j]<2\}.
\]
Every component in the first union is the zero set of the nonzero polynomial
\(
 \|\widetilde F b_j\|_2^2.
\)
Every component in the second union is the zero set of the sum of squares of the \(2\times2\) minors of
\([
\widetilde F b_i\;\widetilde F b_j
]\).  That polynomial is nonzero: because \(b_i,b_j\) are linearly independent and \(p\ge2\), there is a linear map \(T:\mathbb R^q\to\mathbb R^p\) with \(Tb_i=e_1\) and \(Tb_j=e_2\).  Hence \(\mathcal Z_B\) is a finite union of proper real algebraic sets and has empty Euclidean interior by the polynomial argument above.

Full row rank and the nonzero, pairwise-noncollinear-column conditions for \(\widetilde F\) are open and hold at \(F_0\).  Moreover, because \(O\) is relatively open and the structural coordinate \(\eta_0\) is fixed, every sufficiently small admissible perturbation of \(F_0\) remains in \(O\).  We may therefore choose \(\widetilde F\) arbitrarily close to \(F_0\) such that
\[
 \widetilde\theta=(\widetilde F,\eta_0)\in O,
 \qquad \widetilde F\notin\mathcal Z_B.
\]
In particular, \(\widetilde F\) is a legal true mixing matrix, and \(\widetilde F B\) has nonzero pairwise-noncollinear columns.

We now construct a covariance twin for \(\widetilde\theta\).  Let
\[
 P'=U_0^{-1},\qquad
 F'=\widetilde F P_0U_0=\widetilde F B,
 \qquad v'=v_0,\qquad t'=t_0,
\]
and use the candidate coefficients \(\Lambda'^{(0)}=I_q-U_0\) and \(\delta'_k{}^{\mathsf T}=h_{0k}^{\mathsf T}U_0\) fixed above.  Since right multiplication by the invertible matrix \(B\) preserves row rank,
\(
 \operatorname{rank}(F')=\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde F)=p.
\)
The choice \(\widetilde F\notin\mathcal Z_B\) gives the remaining candidate mixing conditions.  Positivity follows from \(v'=v_0>0\) and \(t'=t_0>0\).  Exact baseline support, exact intervention support, and retained edges were supplied by \(U_0\).  Finally, equality of transitive closures implies that the displayed topological order for \(G\) is also a topological order for \(G'\).  Hence the constructed parameter \(\theta'\) belongs to \(\mathcal M_{p,q}(G')\).

Let \(\widetilde A=\widetilde F P_0\) and \(\widetilde a_k=\widetilde A e_k\).  The candidate baseline reduced mixing is
\[
 A'=F'P'=\widetilde F P_0U_0U_0^{-1}=\widetilde A,
\]
and its actual intervention row is
\[
 h'_k{}^{\mathsf T}
 =\delta'_k{}^{\mathsf T}P'
 =h_{0k}^{\mathsf T}U_0U_0^{-1}
 =h_{0k}^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Applying the inverse-update identity proved in the covariance-branches lemma first to \(\widetilde\theta\) and then to \(\theta'\) gives, for every \(k\in V\),
\[
 F'(I_q-\Lambda'^{(k)})^{-1}
 =A'+A'e_kh'_k{}^{\mathsf T}
 =\widetilde A+\widetilde a_kh_{0k}^{\mathsf T}
 =\widetilde F(I_q-\Lambda^{(k)}_0)^{-1}.
\]
The same equality holds at \(k=0\) by the displayed baseline calculation.  Since \(v'=v_0\) and \(t'=t_0\), the positive diagonal error-covariance matrices also agree context by context.  Substitution into the defining covariance formula therefore yields
\[
 \Sigma_{\theta'}^{(k)}=\Sigma_{\widetilde\theta}^{(k)}
 \qquad\text{for every }k\in K.
\]
Thus \(\widetilde\theta\in\mathcal E_p(G,G')\cap O\).

Because every nonempty relatively open \(O\subseteq C\) contains such a point,
\[
 \operatorname{cl}_{C}\bigl(\mathcal E_p(G,G')\cap C\bigr)=C,
\]
and hence
\[
 C\setminus\operatorname{cl}_{C}\bigl(\mathcal E_p(G,G')\cap C\bigr)=\varnothing.
\]
This holds for every full-dimensional chamber \(C\), so the definition of the generic positive-real covariance class gives \(G'\in\mathsf{Cov}_p(G)\).

The argument is chamberwise for either sign of every nonzero true variance change.  It never divides by \(t_k-v_k\), and it makes no assertion on the true variance-neutral hyperplanes excluded from the covariance-generic locus.  The definition permits candidate variance neutrality; the particular dense witness above simply takes \(v'=v_0\) and \(t'=t_0\).  Since \(G'\in\mathsf H_{\mathrm{soft}}(G)\) was arbitrary, we conclude
\[
 \mathsf H_{\mathrm{soft}}(G)\subseteq\mathsf{Cov}_p(G).
\]
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The nesting gives the information-order direction needed before equality or strict covariance surplus can be meaningful. \textit{Gap.} Problem 5.2 motivates an information comparison for the published classes. Corrected actual-row compatibility and the chamberwise robust covariance class change both objects, so their nesting requires a separate result and is not an asserted identification with the published covariance-compatible class. \textit{Consumer.} This fixes the information-order direction needed to interpret robust covariance-only alternatives in the companion LCD workflow.

\begin{proposition}[prop:observable-quotient]\label{prop:observable-quotient}
On \(\mathcal M^{\circ}_{\mathrm{term},p,q}(G,G)\cap\mathcal M^{\circ}_{\mathrm{branch},p,q}(G,G)\), the relation recovered from the aligned factorization datum satisfies \(\mathcal C(\mathcal R)=\overline G\) up to the common latent permutation.  The maps \(\mathcal R\mapsto\mathcal G_{\mathrm{soft}}(\mathcal R)\) and \((\mathcal R,\{\Sigma^{(k)}\}_{k\in K})\mapsto\mathcal G_{\mathrm{cov}}(\mathcal R,\{\Sigma^{(k)}\}_{k\in K})\) are invariant to the representative of \(\mathcal R\) under \(\mathfrak M_q\).  The rational projective gauge removes every nonzero diagonal scale, and enumeration of the remaining common permutation orbit preserves terminal ranks, exact-support conditions, covariance feasibility, realization witnesses, and separators.  Consequently, \(\mathcal G_{\mathrm{cov}}(\mathcal R,\{\Sigma^{(k)}\}_{k\in K})\setminus\mathcal G_{\mathrm{soft}}(\mathcal R)\) is a well-defined quotient-valued pointwise population functional.  Without an additional generic-to-pointwise constancy theorem, this proposition does not identify that pointwise difference with the chamberwise graph functional \(\mathsf{Cov}_p(G)\setminus\mathsf H_{\mathrm{soft}}(G)\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(\theta\in\mathcal M^{\circ}_{\mathrm{term},p,q}(G,G)\cap
\mathcal M^{\circ}_{\mathrm{branch},p,q}(G,G)\), and first use the representative of
\(\mathcal R\) generated by \(\theta\).  By \texttt{lem:covariance-branches}, for
each \(k\in V\),
\[
 A^{(k)}-A^{(0)}=a_kh_k^{\mathsf T}.
\]
The nonzero rank minors and factor-line discriminants defining the branch-generic
locus make \(D_k\) nonzero.  Indeed, at a source the displayed formula in
\texttt{lem:covariance-branches} reduces to
\(D_k=(t_k-v_k)a_ka_k^{\mathsf T}\), where \(t_k-v_k\ne0\) on that locus, and at
a non-source the same lemma gives two nonproportional rank-one directions.  Since
every term in the covariance-difference formula contains \(a_k\), in either case
\(D_k\ne0\) implies \(a_k\ne0\).  Choose an observed coordinate \(r(k)\) with
\((a_k)_{r(k)}\ne0\).  Then the preceding rank-one identity determines the row
entry by entry:
\[
 h_{ki}=\frac{(A^{(k)}-A^{(0)})_{r(k),i}}{(a_k)_{r(k)}}
 \qquad(i\in V).
\]
Thus the row recovered in \texttt{def:recovered-closure} is the actual row in
\texttt{def:actual-rows}.  It also satisfies the imposed normalization
\(h_{kk}=0\): by topological ordering, \(P_{jk}=0\) for
\(j\in\operatorname{pa}_G(k)\), and hence
\[
 h_{kk}=\sum_{j\in\operatorname{pa}_G(k)}\delta_{kj}P_{jk}=0.
\]

It remains to determine the support of the recovered row without assuming away
path cancellation.  Apply the terminal-linkage conclusion of
\texttt{thm:terminal-certificate} with \(R=\{k\}\) and \(C=\{i\}\).  The
one-by-one matrix \(H[R,C]\) has rank one exactly when there is a directed path
from \(i\) to \(k^{\star}\) in \(G^{\mathrm{term}}\).  By
\texttt{def:terminal-copy}, such a path exists exactly when there is a
positive-length directed path from \(i\) to \(k\) in \(G\): a path to
\(k^{\star}\) ends with \(j\to k^{\star}\) for a parent \(j\) of \(k\), and
replacing that last edge by \(j\to k\) gives the path in \(G\), while the reverse
replacement gives the converse.  Consequently,
\[
 (H_{\mathcal R})_{ki}\ne0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 i\to k\in\overline G.
\]
The definition of \(\mathcal C(\mathcal R)\) therefore yields
\(\mathcal C(\mathcal R)=\overline G\) for this representative.

We next prove representative invariance.  The monomial group is generated by
nonsingular diagonal matrices and permutation matrices.  Let
\(C=\operatorname{diag}(c_1,\ldots,c_q)\), with every \(c_i\ne0\).  Under the
diagonal action in \texttt{def:aligned-factor-input},
\[
 A^{(k)}C-A^{(0)}C
 =a_kh_k^{\mathsf T}C
 =(c_ka_k)\bigl(c_k^{-1}h_k^{\mathsf T}C\bigr).
\]
Uniqueness of the row just proved shows that the recovered matrices before and
after rescaling satisfy
\[
 \widetilde H=C^{-1}HC.
\]
It follows entrywise that \(\widetilde H_{ki}=c_k^{-1}H_{ki}c_i\), so the zero
pattern is unchanged.  If \(U'=I_q-\Lambda'^{(0)}\) is a candidate structural
witness, put \(\widetilde U=C^{-1}U'C\).  Then
\[
 (\widetilde H\widetilde U)_{ki}
 =c_k^{-1}(HU')_{ki}c_i.
\]
Thus every exact-support and noncancellation condition is preserved.  Likewise,
for all index sets \(R_0,C_0\subseteq V\),
\[
 \operatorname{rank}\widetilde H[R_0,C_0]
 =\operatorname{rank}\bigl(C[R_0,R_0]^{-1}H[R_0,C_0]C[C_0,C_0]\bigr)
 =\operatorname{rank}H[R_0,C_0],
\]
because both diagonal submatrices are invertible.  These equalities transport the
terminal-rank, affine-feasibility, and exact-support certificates of
\texttt{thm:terminal-certificate} in both directions.

For completeness, consider any model witness
\((F',\Lambda'^{(0)},\delta',v',t')\in\mathcal M_{p,q}(D)\) for a candidate DAG
\(D\).  Define, in every context,
\[
 \widetilde F=F'C,
 \qquad
 \widetilde\Lambda^{(k)}=C^{-1}\Lambda'^{(k)}C,
 \qquad
 \widetilde\varepsilon^{(k)}=C^{-1}\varepsilon'^{(k)}.
\]
Since \(C^{-1}e_k=c_k^{-1}e_k\), the intervention remains a single-node
intervention and its row, baseline variances, and target variances are
\[
 \widetilde\delta_k^{\mathsf T}=c_k^{-1}\delta_k'{}^{\mathsf T}C,
 \qquad
 \widetilde v_i=c_i^{-2}v'_i,
 \qquad
 \widetilde t_i=c_i^{-2}t'_i.
\]
Hence all variances remain strictly positive, and
\[
 \widetilde\Lambda^{(0)}_{ki}+\widetilde\delta_{ki}
 =c_k^{-1}\bigl(\Lambda'^{(0)}_{ki}+\delta'_{ki}\bigr)c_i.
\]
The exact edge support and retained-edge inequalities are therefore preserved.
Right multiplication of \(F'\) by an invertible diagonal matrix preserves its
rank, its nonzero columns, and its pairwise noncollinear column lines.  Nonzero
coordinatewise scaling also preserves centering, independence, and
non-Gaussianity of the error coordinates.  Finally,
\[
 (I_q-\widetilde\Lambda^{(k)})^{-1}
 =C^{-1}(I_q-\Lambda'^{(k)})^{-1}C,
\]
and therefore
\[
 \widetilde F(I_q-\widetilde\Lambda^{(k)})^{-1}
 \widetilde\varepsilon^{(k)}
 =F'(I_q-\Lambda'^{(k)})^{-1}\varepsilon'^{(k)}.
\]
In particular the full observed laws, and hence every observed covariance
\(\Sigma^{(k)}\), are unchanged.  The construction with \(C^{-1}\) is its
inverse, so this is a bijection of realization witnesses and of positive-real
covariance-feasibility fibers.

Now let \(\Pi\) be a permutation matrix and define \(\sigma\) by
\(\Pi e_j=e_{\sigma(j)}\).  Keep the baseline label \(0\) fixed and relabel each
target context \(j\in V\) as the old context \(\sigma(j)\).  Set
\[
 \widetilde\Lambda^{(0)}=\Pi^{\mathsf T}\Lambda'^{(0)}\Pi,
 \qquad
 \widetilde\Lambda^{(j)}=\Pi^{\mathsf T}\Lambda'^{(\sigma(j))}\Pi,
 \qquad
 \widetilde F=F'\Pi,
\]
and transform the corresponding errors to \(\Pi^{\mathsf T}\varepsilon'^{(0)}\)
and \(\Pi^{\mathsf T}\varepsilon'^{(\sigma(j))}\), respectively.  Direct
substitution gives the same observed law in the relabeled context and gives
\[
 \widetilde H=\Pi^{\mathsf T}H\Pi.
\]
Thus a common permutation merely relabels the graph, targets, supports, terminal
paths, and all feasibility systems.  Combining this permutation transport with
the diagonal transport proves invariance under every element of
\(\mathfrak M_q\).  A realization witness is carried to the displayed witness;

\begin{theorem}[thm:fork-all-chamber-projection]\label{thm:fork-all-chamber-projection}
For \(p=2\) and the pair \((G_{\mathrm{fork}},G'_{\mathrm{fork}})\), index the eight systems of \(\mathfrak B_2(G_{\mathrm{fork}},G'_{\mathrm{fork}})\) by \(\eta\in\{0,1\}^3\). For every full-dimensional connected chamber \(C\) of \(\mathcal M^{\circ}_{\mathrm{fork},2,4}(G_{\mathrm{fork}},G'_{\mathrm{fork}})\), let \(\mathscr S_{\eta,C}\subseteq C\times\mathbb R^2\) consist of the triples \((\theta,s,x)\) satisfying all baseline-variance, intervention-variance, denominator, exact-support, retained-edge, mixing, and exceptional-polynomial equations and inequations of branch \(\eta\), with the reconstructed \(\theta'\in\mathcal M_{2,4}(G'_{\mathrm{fork}})\) required to satisfy \(\Sigma_{\theta'}^{(k)}=\Sigma_{\theta}^{(k)}\) for every \(k\in K\). If \(\pi_C(\theta,s,x)=\theta\), then
\[
 \mathcal E_2(G_{\mathrm{fork}},G'_{\mathrm{fork}})\cap C
 =\bigcup_{\eta\in\{0,1\}^3}\pi_C(\mathscr S_{\eta,C}).
\]
Since \(G'_{\mathrm{fork}}\notin\mathsf H_{\mathrm{soft}}(G_{\mathrm{fork}})\), membership in \(\mathsf{Frontier}_2(G_{\mathrm{fork}})\) is equivalent to the condition that, for every such \(C\),
\[
 \operatorname{relint}_C\!\left(
 C\setminus\operatorname{cl}_C
 \left(\bigcup_{\eta\in\{0,1\}^3}\pi_C(\mathscr S_{\eta,C})\right)
 \right)=\varnothing.
\]
Let \(\theta_{\mathrm{true}}(T)\) denote the true-coordinate projection of the pair \(\mathcal W_{\mathrm{fork}}(T)\). This one-parameter family, even together with regular open neighborhoods of all its audited points, is insufficient to decide the density condition: there are relatively semialgebraic subsets of a full-dimensional chamber containing the same true-coordinate family and neighborhoods but having opposite answers to the displayed density test. Thus the eight-branch reduction gives an exact projection problem, but neither a chamberwise dense cover nor a full-dimensional separator follows without an additional global projection argument.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a full-dimensional connected chamber \(C\) of
\(\mathcal M^{\circ}_{\mathrm{fork},2,4}(G_{\mathrm{fork}},G'_{\mathrm{fork}})\).
We first prove the asserted equality of sets.

Let
\(\theta\in\mathcal E_2(G_{\mathrm{fork}},G'_{\mathrm{fork}})\cap C\).
By the definition of \(\mathcal E_2\), there is a legal point
\(\theta'\in\mathcal M_{2,4}(G'_{\mathrm{fork}})\) such that
\[
 \Sigma_{\theta'}^{(k)}=\Sigma_{\theta}^{(k)}
 \qquad\text{for every }k\in K.
\]
Because \(\theta\in C\) lies in the stated regular fork locus,
\(\mathrm{thm:fork\text{-}eight\text{-}branch\text{-}classification}\) applies.  Its
completeness direction selects one of the eight factor-line indices
\(\eta\in\{0,1\}^3\) and returns scalars \((s,x)\in\mathbb R^2\) satisfying
the branch's baseline-variance, intervention-variance, denominator,
exact-support, retained-edge, mixing, and exceptional-polynomial conditions.
The same result verifies the five displayed covariance equations.  Hence
\((\theta,s,x)\in\mathscr S_{\eta,C}\), and therefore
\[
 \theta\in
 \bigcup_{\eta\in\{0,1\}^3}\pi_C(\mathscr S_{\eta,C}).
\]

Conversely, suppose that
\(\theta\in\pi_C(\mathscr S_{\eta,C})\) for some \(\eta\).  Then there are
\((s,x)\in\mathbb R^2\) with \((\theta,s,x)\in\mathscr S_{\eta,C}\).
By the soundness direction of
\(\mathrm{thm:fork\text{-}eight\text{-}branch\text{-}classification}\), the
branch solution reconstructs a point
\(\theta'\in\mathcal M_{2,4}(G'_{\mathrm{fork}})\): all four baseline
variances and all four intervention variances are positive, every required
coefficient and change is nonzero, every changed edge is retained, and the
mixing columns are nonzero and pairwise noncollinear.  That theorem's final
calculation gives
\[
 \Sigma_{\theta'}^{(k)}-\Sigma_{\theta'}^{(0)}
 =D_k
 =\Sigma_{\theta}^{(k)}-\Sigma_{\theta}^{(0)}
 \qquad(k\in V),
\]
while its baseline equation gives
\(\Sigma_{\theta'}^{(0)}=\Sigma_{\theta}^{(0)}\).  Adding the baseline
equality to each difference equality proves
\[
 \Sigma_{\theta'}^{(k)}=\Sigma_{\theta}^{(k)}
 \qquad(k\in K).
\]
Thus \(\theta\in\mathcal E_2(G_{\mathrm{fork}},G'_{\mathrm{fork}})\cap C\).
The two inclusions prove
\[
 \mathcal E_2(G_{\mathrm{fork}},G'_{\mathrm{fork}})\cap C
 =\bigcup_{\eta\in\{0,1\}^3}\pi_C(\mathscr S_{\eta,C}).
\]

We next translate this equality into the information-frontier criterion.
For an arbitrary subset \(A\subseteq C\), the complement
\(C\setminus\operatorname{cl}_C(A)\) is relatively open in \(C\).  It is
therefore nonempty if and only if its relative interior is nonempty.  Hence
\[
 \operatorname{relint}_C\bigl(C\setminus\operatorname{cl}_C(A)\bigr)
 =\varnothing
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{cl}_C(A)=C.
\]
In words, the displayed relative-interior condition is exactly density in
\(C\).

The fork locus is obtained from the branch locus by deleting the zero sets
of the finitely many additional nonzero polynomials in
\(\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{fork}}\).  For completeness, a nonzero real polynomial
cannot vanish on a nonempty open set: induction on the number of variables
reduces the assertion to the fact that a nonzero univariate polynomial has
only finitely many roots, after viewing the polynomial as a univariate
polynomial whose coefficients are polynomials in the remaining variables.
Consequently each deleted zero set has empty relative interior in every
full-dimensional branch chamber.  The fork locus is thus relatively open
and dense there, and its full-dimensional connected chambers form an open
dense refinement.

If a set is dense in a branch chamber, its intersection with every member
of this open refinement is dense in that member: every nonempty relatively
open subset of the member is also relatively open in the branch chamber.
Conversely, if the intersections are dense in all refining chambers, their
closures contain their union, and the closure of that union is the whole
branch chamber because the refinement is dense.  Therefore the density test
in the definition of \(\mathsf{Cov}_2(G_{\mathrm{fork}})\) is equivalent to
the same test on every chamber \(C\) appearing in the theorem.  By
\(\mathrm{prop:fork\text{-}rational\text{-}collision}\),
\[
 G'_{\mathrm{fork}}\notin
 \mathsf H_{\mathrm{soft}}(G_{\mathrm{fork}}).
\]
Using the definition
\(\mathsf{Frontier}_2=\mathsf{Cov}_2\setminus\mathsf H_{\mathrm{soft}}\),
the just-proved set equality, and the density equivalence yields
\[
 G'_{\mathrm{fork}}\in\mathsf{Frontier}_2(G_{\mathrm{fork}})
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{relint}_C\!\left(
 C\setminus\operatorname{cl}_C
 \left(\bigcup_{\eta\in\{0,1\}^3}
 \pi_C(\mathscr S_{\eta,C})\right)
 \right)=\varnothing
\]
for every full-dimensional connected fork chamber \(C\).

It remains to prove the stated limitation of the worked family.  Its true
coordinates obey fixed affine equalities, including
\[
 v_1=v_2=v_3=v_4=1,
\]
and fixed equalities for the displayed baseline coefficient and loading
coordinates.  Its image is consequently contained in a proper affine
algebraic subset \(Z\) of the full-dimensional true-parameter domain.  Let
\(C\) be a full-dimensional chamber meeting this family.  The relatively
open semialgebraic set \(C\setminus Z\) is nonempty, so it contains a point
\(\theta_0\) and a sufficiently small relative open ball \(B\) whose closure
in \(C\) is disjoint from \(Z\).  Put
\[
 P_0=C\setminus\overline B,
 \qquad
 P_1=C.
\]
Both \(P_0\) and \(P_1\) are relatively semialgebraic.  Since
\(Z\cap\overline B=\varnothing\), both contain the same worked true-coordinate
family and a relative neighborhood of each of its points.  Nevertheless,
\[
 B\subseteq C\setminus\operatorname{cl}_C(P_0),
 \qquad
 C\setminus\operatorname{cl}_C(P_1)=\varnothing.
\]
Because \(B\) is nonempty and relatively open, it follows that
\[
 \operatorname{relint}_C
 \bigl(C\setminus\operatorname{cl}_C(P_0)\bigr)\ne\varnothing,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{relint}_C
 \bigl(C\setminus\operatorname{cl}_C(P_1)\bigr)=\varnothing.
\]
Thus the rational family, even with its audited regular neighborhoods, is
consistent with relatively semialgebraic supersets having opposite density
answers.  It cannot by itself determine the answer for the actual projected
union.  Only a global argument applied to all eight projections can produce
the chamberwise dense realization cover or a full-dimensional separator,
which proves the final assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:batched-affine-support-test]\label{lem:batched-affine-support-test}
For the affine blocks associated with a fixed candidate DAG \(G'\), feasibility of all \(q\) systems \(M_jx_j=b_j\) and identical-vanishing of all \(3|C_j|\) forbidden forms \(x_{ji}\), \(d_{ji}(x_j)\), and \(x_{ji}+d_{ji}(x_j)\) can be decided over any infinite coefficient field by row-reducing each augmented matrix \((M_j\mid b_j)\) once.  If a block is feasible, extracting one particular solution and one kernel basis and then performing two batched matrix products decides every forbidden form.  Over all columns this uses \(O(q^4)\) field operations and yields either an inconsistency or identically-zero-form certificate, or a point in every \(\mathcal A_j\) avoiding every forbidden hyperplane.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(n_j=|C_j|\), \(r_j=|R_j|\), and \(B_j=(M_j\mid b_j)\).  Gaussian elimination on \(B_j\) decides
\[
 \operatorname{rank}M_j=\operatorname{rank}B_j.
\]
If the ranks differ, a row of the reduced augmented matrix of the form \((0,\ldots,0\mid 1)\), after rescaling, certifies inconsistency.  Suppose that the ranks agree.  The same reduction, with no second elimination, returns a particular solution \(x_j^0\) and a matrix \(N_j\) whose columns form a basis of \(\ker M_j\).  Hence
\[
 \mathcal A_j=x_j^0+\operatorname{im}N_j.
\]

Stack the forbidden affine forms in the order
\[
 \bigl(x_{ji},\ d_{ji}(x_j),\ x_{ji}+d_{ji}(x_j)\bigr)_{i\in C_j}
 =L_jx_j+c_j,
\]
where the three rows corresponding to \(i\in C_j\) have linear parts
\[
 e_i^{\mathsf T},\qquad -H[i,C_j],\qquad e_i^{\mathsf T}-H[i,C_j]
\]
and respective constants
\[
 0,\qquad H[i,j],\qquad H[i,j].
\]
For a row \(\lambda^{\mathsf T}x+c\) of this stack and an arbitrary point \(x=x_j^0+N_jy\) of \(\mathcal A_j\),
\[
 \lambda^{\mathsf T}x+c
 =\lambda^{\mathsf T}x_j^0+c+\lambda^{\mathsf T}N_jy.
\]
It follows directly that this form vanishes identically on \(\mathcal A_j\) if and only if its entry in \(L_jx_j^0+c_j\) is zero and its entire row in \(L_jN_j\) is zero.  Thus the two displayed batched products decide all \(3n_j\) forms.

If no forbidden form is identically zero, each of its zero sets is a proper affine hyperplane of \(\mathcal A_j\).  Their finite union cannot cover \(\mathcal A_j\) over an infinite field.  Indeed, choose affine coordinates \(y\) on \(\mathcal A_j\), choose a direction successively outside the finitely many kernels of the nonconstant forms, and then choose the scalar coordinate outside the finitely many resulting roots; constant nonzero forms have no roots.  The resulting point avoids every forbidden hyperplane.  Conversely, any point avoiding the forbidden forms certifies feasibility and proves that no one of them vanishes identically.

The matrix \(B_j\) has at most \(q\) rows and \(q+1\) columns, so its reduction costs \(O(q^3)\) field operations.  The matrices \(L_j\), \(N_j\) have sizes at most \(3q\times q\) and \(q\times q\), respectively; constructing the stack and computing \(L_jx_j^0+c_j\) and \(L_jN_j\) also costs \(O(q^3)\).  Summing over \(j\in V\) gives \(O(q^4)\).  This counts arithmetic in the coefficient field and makes no expanded bit-complexity or coefficient-height claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:anchor-domain-transfer]\label{prop:anchor-domain-transfer}

 F_0=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&1&1\\0&1&1&2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The determinants of its six pairs of columns are \(1,1,2,-1,-1,1\).  Hence every column is nonzero, every two columns are noncollinear, and \(\operatorname{rank}F_0=2\).  \qquad

These choices meet the mixing and source-law clauses without changing the coefficient point or its nonzero determinant. Thus the argument yields no membership assertion for \(G'_{\mathrm{fork}}\) in \(\mathsf{Cov}_2(G_{\mathrm{fork}})\), and hence none in \(\mathsf{Frontier}_2(G_{\mathrm{fork}})\), as claimed.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:terminal-and-finite-branch-reduction]\label{prop:terminal-and-finite-branch-reduction}
Let \(2\le p<q\), and let \(G,G'\) be directed acyclic graphs in a common topological order with \(\overline{G'}=\overline G\).  The following two reductions are sound and complete.  First, corrected actual-row membership \(G'\in\mathsf H_{\mathrm{soft}}(G)\) is decided from \(H\) by the terminal-copy linkage and batched affine-support certificates in \(O(q^4)\) rational-function field operations per candidate \(G'\).  Second, for every \(\theta\in\mathcal M^{\circ}_{\mathrm{branch},p,q}(G,G')\), the set of \(\theta'\in\mathcal M_{p,q}(G')\) satisfying \(\Sigma_{\theta'}^{(k)}=\Sigma_{\theta}^{(k)}\) for every \(k\in K\) is represented exactly by the candidate-parameter projections of at most \(2^{q-r}\) systems in \(\mathfrak B_p(G,G')\).  For \(p=2\) and \((G,G')=(G_{\mathrm{fork}},G'_{\mathrm{fork}})\), these are the eight rational systems in the two candidate scalars \((s,x)\), and on every full-dimensional connected true-parameter chamber their projected union equals \(\mathcal E_2(G_{\mathrm{fork}},G'_{\mathrm{fork}})\) intersected with that chamber.  These reductions do not decide chamberwise density of the projected union and do not supply a realization-or-separator compiler, a bit-operation or coefficient-height bound, or a pathwidth guarantee.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(p,q,G,G'\) as in the statement.  By the proved batched terminal bound \(\mathrm{prop:terminal\text{-}o\text{-}q4}\), the terminal-certificate theorem is implemented by constructing \(H\), row-reducing each augmented affine block \((M_j\mid b_j)\) once, extracting a particular solution and a kernel basis, and evaluating all forbidden affine forms by two batched matrix products.  That proposition proves both soundness and completeness and gives
\[
 O(q^3)+\sum_{j\in V}O(q^3)=O(q^4)
\]
rational-function field operations.  This establishes the first reduction, including its stated arithmetic complexity.

For the second reduction, fix \(\theta\in\mathcal M^{\circ}_{\mathrm{branch},p,q}(G,G')\).  Let \(\mathcal I\) index the factor-line choices in \(\mathfrak B_p(G,G')\).  The covariance-branch lemma states that a source contributes its unique real factor line, whereas each of the \(q-r\) nonsources contributes at most two real factor lines.  Hence
\[
 |\mathcal I|\le \prod_{k\,\mathrm{nonsource}}2=2^{q-r}.
\]
For \(\eta\in\mathcal I\), let \(\mathcal X_\eta(\theta)\) be the solution set of the corresponding branch system after the true parameter is fixed at \(\theta\), and let \(\operatorname{Rec}_\eta\mathcal X_\eta(\theta)\) denote the candidate parameter points reconstructed by that system.  The completeness direction of the covariance-branch lemma sends every legal covariance twin to one factor-line choice and a solution of its branch system.  Therefore
\[
 \left\{\theta'\in\mathcal M_{p,q}(G'):
   \Sigma_{\theta'}^{(k)}=\Sigma_{\theta}^{(k)}\ \text{for all }k\in K\right\}
 \subseteq
 \bigcup_{\eta\in\mathcal I}\operatorname{Rec}_\eta\mathcal X_\eta(\theta).
\]
Conversely, the soundness direction of the same lemma reconstructs from every branch solution a candidate in \(\mathcal M_{p,q}(G')\) with positive baseline and intervention variances, exact structural and intervention support, retained changed edges, admissible mixing columns, and all context covariance equations.  Thus
\[
 \bigcup_{\eta\in\mathcal I}\operatorname{Rec}_\eta\mathcal X_\eta(\theta)
 \subseteq
 \left\{\theta'\in\mathcal M_{p,q}(G'):
   \Sigma_{\theta'}^{(k)}=\Sigma_{\theta}^{(k)}\ \text{for all }k\in K\right\}.
\]
The two inclusions prove the exact finite representation.  In particular, positivity and every sign, denominator, exact-support, retained-edge, mixing, and candidate variance-neutral branch required by the model are retained rather than discarded.

When \(p=2\) and the graph pair is \((G_{\mathrm{fork}},G'_{\mathrm{fork}})\), the fork eight-branch classification identifies \(\mathcal I\) with the eight rational systems in \((s,x)\).  The fork all-chamber projection theorem then gives, on each full-dimensional connected chamber, equality between the covariance fiber and the union of those eight projected solution sets, with the reconstructed points checked against the original five covariance equations.  That theorem expressly leaves the chamberwise density alternative undecided.  Since neither it nor either preceding reduction supplies projection-density certificates, elimination bit bounds, coefficient-height control, or a pathwidth guarantee, none of those stronger compiler conclusions follows.  This proves exactly the proposition.
\end{proof}

\section{Interpretation}

At population level the companion LCD workflow can now return the corrected actual-row compatible DAG set and, for each accepted or rejected candidate, a terminal linkage or cut, an affine inconsistency, an identically-zero support form, or an avoiding-point certificate. For the worked fork datum it can additionally report a genuine pointwise covariance alternative: the candidate reproduces all five covariances but cannot reproduce the aligned soft-intervention factor datum, and this verdict is invariant to sign, scale, and permutation representatives. It must label that conclusion pointwise. Higher cumulants remove this displayed covariance alternative, but whether they remove covariance alternatives generically across each full-dimensional chamber remains unresolved.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The complete regular fork fiber is reduced to eight legal two-scalar positive systems, but their projected union has not been classified over the full true parameter domain. The prescribed fixed resultant and subresultant attempt retained all true coordinates as parameters and eliminated only the two candidate scalars; it failed before emitting projection polynomials, sign cells, or Thom-encoded sample certificates. The rational \(T\)-family, positive candidate variances \(T+144/121\) and \(19/4\), exact five-covariance verification, the obstruction \(-2\), and audited Jacobian regularity establish pointwise feasibility and local facts only. They cannot certify chamberwise density or a full-dimensional separator.

\section{Honest scope}

The corrected terminal theorem is exact on its declared generic coefficient locus, independent of variance activity, and has an \(O(q^4)\) arithmetic-field bound with no expanded bit-complexity claim. Its finite Definition 3.14 agreement procedure may enumerate same-closure DAGs, so no polynomial total graph-enumeration bound is asserted. The anchor transfer is one-way: the exact-change retained-edge counterexample lies inside the published generic soft domain and therefore refutes a superclass theorem, while positive subclass results do not automatically extend upward. The fork corollary is quotient-invariant and pointwise; it explicitly does not establish \(G'_{\mathrm{fork}}\in\mathsf{Frontier}_2(G_{\mathrm{fork}})\). The all-chamber fork density test, dense-cover or separator certificates, all-pair non-quantifier-elimination compiler, complexity and pathwidth bounds, and infinite equality or generic-strictness families remain open.
