%%% FIELD def-effective-construction
Put
\[
q_0=4+\delta,
\qquad
b_\lambda=\left(M_{\mathrm{mom}}^{2/q_0}-2v_\star\right)_+^{1/2}.
\]
The number \(b_\lambda\) is the effective loading-mean and untreated-mean coordinate radius determined by the primitive moment and loading-covariance radii.

%%% FIELD helper-effective-statement
For every \(P\in\mathcal M\) and every \(c\in[C]\),
\[
\|m_c\|_2^2+\operatorname{tr}(H_c)\le M_{\mathrm{mom}}^{2/q_0},
\qquad
\|m_c\|_2\le b_\lambda,
\qquad
\max_{t\in[T]}|\mu_{ct}(P)|\le b_\lambda.
\]

%%% FIELD helper-effective-proof
Fix \(P\in\mathcal M\) and \(c\in[C]\).  The exponent \(q_0=4+\delta\) in \(\operatorname{def:effective\text{-}loading\text{-}mean\text{-}radius}\) is larger than two because \(\delta>0\).  By the definition of the conditional mean and covariance,
\[
\mathbb E_P\!\left(\|\lambda_i\|_2^2\mid C_i=c\right)
=\|m_c\|_2^2+\operatorname{tr}(H_c).
\tag{1}
\]
Lyapunov's inequality, followed by the nonnegativity of the error moment and \(\operatorname{ass:moment\text{-}radius}\), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left\{\|m_c\|_2^2+\operatorname{tr}(H_c)\right\}^{q_0/2}
&=\left\{\mathbb E_P(\|\lambda_i\|_2^2\mid C_i=c)\right\}^{q_0/2}\\
&\le \mathbb E_P(\|\lambda_i\|_2^{q_0}\mid C_i=c)\\
&\le \mathbb E_P(\|\lambda_i\|_2^{q_0}+\|\varepsilon_i\|_2^{q_0}\mid C_i=c)\\
&\le M_{\mathrm{mom}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
Taking the power \(2/q_0>0\) in (2) proves the first displayed inequality in the statement.  By \(\operatorname{ass:loading\text{-}covariance}\), both eigenvalues of \(H_c\) are at least \(v_\star\), and therefore
\[
\operatorname{tr}(H_c)\ge 2v_\star.
\tag{3}
\]
Combining (2)--(3) yields
\[
\|m_c\|_2^2
\le M_{\mathrm{mom}}^{2/q_0}-2v_\star.
\tag{4}
\]
The left side of (4) is nonnegative.  Hence the right side is nonnegative for every nonempty model, and the definition of \(b_\lambda\) gives \(\|m_c\|_2\le b_\lambda\).  Finally, \(\operatorname{ass:factor\text{-}normalization}\) implies \(\|\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{op}}=1\).  Since \(\mu_c(P)=\Gamma m_c\), for each \(t\in[T]\),
\[
|\mu_{ct}(P)|
=|e_t^{\prime}\Gamma m_c|
\le \|e_t^{\prime}\Gamma\|_2\|m_c\|_2
\le \|\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|m_c\|_2
\le b_\lambda.
\]
This proves all three assertions.

%%% FIELD pca-proof
Put \(q_0=4+\delta\), and let \(K\) denote a finite positive constant that may change from line to line and depends only on the fixed primitive radii and \((T,C)\).  Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_N(s_N)\).  Membership in \(\mathcal P_N(s_N)\) implies all conditions of \(\mathcal M\) and \(s(P)\ge s_N>0\).  Because \(\Gamma^{\prime}\Gamma=I_2\), \(\|\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{op}}=1\).  Hence, for \(X_{ic}=J_cY_i(0)\) conditional on \(C_i=c\),
\[
\|X_{ic}\|_2
\le \|\Gamma\lambda_i\|_2+\|\varepsilon_i\|_2
\le \|\lambda_i\|_2+\|\varepsilon_i\|_2.
\]
Since \(q_0>4\), Lyapunov's inequality, \((x+y)^4\le8(x^4+y^4)\), and \(\operatorname{ass:moment\text{-}radius}\) yield
\[
\max_{c\in[C]}
\mathbb E_P(\|X_{ic}\|_2^4\mid C_i=c)\le K.
\tag{1}
\]

Let \(N_c=\sum_{i=1}^N1\{C_i=c\}\) and
\[
G_N=\bigcap_{c=1}^C\{N_c\ge Np_\star/2\}.
\]
Because \(N_c\) is binomial with mean \(Np_c\), \(p_c\ge p_\star\), and \(\operatorname{Var}_P(N_c)\le N\), Chebyshev's inequality and a union bound give
\[
\Pr_P(G_N^c)
\le \sum_{c=1}^C
\frac{4\operatorname{Var}_P(N_c)}{N^2p_\star^2}
\le \frac{K}{N}.
\tag{2}
\]
For all \(N\) above a fixed constant, \(G_N\) ensures \(N_c\ge2\) for every cohort. Conditional on the cohort counts, the observations within cohort \(c\) are independent with mean \(\nu_c\), covariance \(V_c\), and fourth moment bounded by (1).  The sample-mean identity gives
\[
\mathbb E_P(\|\widehat\nu_c-\nu_c\|_2^2\mid N_c)
=\frac{\operatorname{tr}(V_c)}{N_c}\le\frac K{N_c}.
\]
Writing the centered sample covariance as the average of
\((X_{ic}-\nu_c)(X_{ic}-\nu_c)^{\prime}\) minus
\((\widehat\nu_c-\nu_c)(\widehat\nu_c-\nu_c)^{\prime}\), and using
\(\|xx^{\prime}\|_{\mathrm F}=\|x\|_2^2\), (1), and Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
\mathbb E_P(\|\widehat V_c-V_c\|_{\mathrm F}^2\mid N_c)
\le\frac K{N_c}.
\]
Also \(\mathbb E_P\|\widehat p-p\|_2^2\le K/N\).  Consequently,
\[
\mathbb E_P\!\left[
\sum_{c=1}^C\{\|\widehat\nu_c-\nu_c\|_2^2
+\|\widehat V_c-V_c\|_{\mathrm F}^2\}
+\|\widehat p-p\|_2^2;G_N\right]
\le\frac K N.
\tag{3}
\]

Let \(P_c=I-Q_c=\Pi_2(V_c)\) and \(\widehat P_c=I-\widehat Q_c=\Pi_2(\widehat V_c)\).  The factor law, loading--error orthogonality, and spherical error covariance give
\[
V_c=\Gamma_{S_c}H_c\Gamma_{S_c}^{\prime}+\sigma_c^2I_{|S_c|}.
\tag{4}
\]
The first term in (4) has rank two and second eigenvalue at least \(g_\star\).  Therefore, for every rank-two orthogonal projector \(P_0\), spectral decomposition gives
\[
\operatorname{tr}\{V_c(P_c-P_0)\}
\ge\frac{g_\star}{2}\|P_c-P_0\|_{\mathrm F}^2.
\tag{5}
\]
The projector \(\widehat P_c\) maximizes \(P_0\mapsto\operatorname{tr}(\widehat V_cP_0)\) over rank-two projectors. Thus
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{tr}\{V_c(P_c-\widehat P_c)\}
&\le \operatorname{tr}\{(V_c-\widehat V_c)(P_c-\widehat P_c)\}\\
&\le \|V_c-\widehat V_c\|_{\mathrm F}
       \|P_c-\widehat P_c\|_{\mathrm F}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Combining this display with (5), including the trivial case \(P_c=\widehat P_c\), proves
\[
\|\widehat Q_c-Q_c\|_{\mathrm F}
\le \frac{2}{g_\star}\|\widehat V_c-V_c\|_{\mathrm F}.
\tag{6}
\]

Use the padded matrices
\[
\mathcal R=\operatorname{stack}_{c=1}^C\sqrt{p_c}\,Q_cJ_c,
\qquad
\widehat{\mathcal R}
=\operatorname{stack}_{c=1}^C\sqrt{\widehat p_c}\,\widehat Q_cJ_c.
\]
Their Gram matrices are \(A(P)\) and \(\widehat A\).  On \(G_N\), both \(p_c\) and \(\widehat p_c\) are bounded below by a positive constant.  The identity
\[
\sqrt{\widehat p_c}\widehat Q_c-\sqrt{p_c}Q_c
=\sqrt{\widehat p_c}(\widehat Q_c-Q_c)
+(\sqrt{\widehat p_c}-\sqrt{p_c})Q_c,
\]
the bound
\(|\sqrt x-\sqrt y|=|x-y|/(\sqrt x+\sqrt y)\), (3), and (6) imply
\[
\mathbb E_P\!\left(
\|\widehat{\mathcal R}-\mathcal R\|_{\mathrm F}^2;G_N
\right)\le\frac K N.
\tag{7}
\]

Equation (4) implies \(Q_c\Gamma_{S_c}=0\) for every \(c\), so
\(\operatorname{col}(\Gamma)\subseteq\ker\mathcal R\).  Because
\(s(P)=\sigma_{T-2}(\mathcal R)\ge s_N>0\) and \(\Gamma\) has rank two, \(\mathcal R\) has rank \(T-2\) and
\[
\ker\mathcal R=\operatorname{col}(\Gamma).
\tag{8}
\]
Let \(U\) be an orthonormal basis of this kernel.  Since \(\widehat U\) consists of the bottom two right singular vectors of \(\widehat{\mathcal R}\), its variational optimality, with \(U\) as competitor, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\mathcal R\widehat U\|_{\mathrm F}
&\le \|(\mathcal R-\widehat{\mathcal R})\widehat U\|_{\mathrm F}
     +\|\widehat{\mathcal R}\widehat U\|_{\mathrm F}\\
&\le \sqrt2\|\mathcal R-\widehat{\mathcal R}\|_{\mathrm F}
     +\|\widehat{\mathcal R}U\|_{\mathrm F}\\
&\le2\sqrt2\|\widehat{\mathcal R}-\mathcal R\|_{\mathrm F}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The restriction of \(\mathcal R\) to \(\operatorname{col}(U)^\perp\) has smallest singular value at least \(s_N\). Hence
\[
E_U:=\|(I-UU^{\prime})\widehat U\|_{\mathrm F}
\le\frac{2\sqrt2}{s_N}
\|\widehat{\mathcal R}-\mathcal R\|_{\mathrm F}.
\tag{9}
\]

The covariance upper bound and local signal gap imply
\[
g_\star
\le\lambda_2(\Gamma_{S_c}H_c\Gamma_{S_c}^{\prime})
\le v^\star\sigma_2(\Gamma_{S_c})^2,
\qquad
\sigma_2(U_{S_c})\ge a:=\sqrt{g_\star/v^\star}>0.
\tag{10}
\]
The principal-angle singular-value decomposition supplies an orthogonal \(2\)-by-\(2\) matrix \(W\) such that, for \(\widetilde U=\widehat UW\),
\[
\|\widetilde U-U\|_{\mathrm F}^2
=2\sum_{j=1}^2(1-\cos\phi_j)
\le2\sum_{j=1}^2\sin^2\phi_j
=2E_U^2.
\tag{11}
\]
If \(E_U\le a/(4\sqrt2)\), Weyl's singular-value inequality and (11) give
\(\sigma_2(\widetilde U_{S_c})\ge a/2\) for every \(c\).  Define
\[
K_c(X)=X(X_{S_c}^{\prime}X_{S_c})^{-1}X_{S_c}^{\prime}.
\]
Whenever the inverse exists, right-orthogonal invariance gives
\(K_c(\widehat U)=K_c(\widehat UW)=K_c(\widetilde U)\).
For \(X=U\) and \(X=\widetilde U\), (10)--(11) bound the displayed inverses uniformly.  Applying
\[
G^{-1}-\widetilde G^{-1}=G^{-1}(\widetilde G-G)\widetilde G^{-1}
\]
to \(G=U_{S_c}^{\prime}U_{S_c}\) and
\(\widetilde G=\widetilde U_{S_c}^{\prime}\widetilde U_{S_c}\), then expanding the three factors in \(K_c\), yields
\[
\|K_c(\widetilde U)-K_c(U)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le K\|\widetilde U-U\|_{\mathrm F}
\le K E_U.
\tag{12}
\]
By the factor law and conditional mean-zero error,
\(\nu_c=\Gamma_{S_c}m_c\).  Since \(U=\Gamma W_0\) for an orthogonal \(W_0\), this identity and full column rank of \(U_{S_c}\) imply
\[
K_c(U)\nu_c=\Gamma m_c=\mu_c(P).
\tag{13}
\]
The effective-radius lemma gives \(\|m_c\|_2\le b_\lambda\), so \(\|\nu_c\|_2\le b_\lambda\), which is bounded by the fixed primitive radii.  On \(G_N\cap\{E_U\le a/(4\sqrt2)\}\), the raw least-squares branch exists, and (9), (12), and (13) give
\[
\|K_c(\widetilde U)\widehat\nu_c-\mu_c(P)\|_2
\le K\left\{
\|\widehat\nu_c-\nu_c\|_2
+\frac{\|\widehat{\mathcal R}-\mathcal R\|_{\mathrm F}}{s_N}
\right\}.
\tag{14}
\]
Clipping to \([-b_\lambda,b_\lambda]^T\) cannot increase coordinatewise distance from \(\mu_c(P)\), because the effective-radius lemma places every target coordinate in that interval.  Thus (14), (3), and (7) show that the expected squared sup-norm error on this event is at most \(K/(Ns_N^2)\).  By (2), (7), (9), and Markov's inequality, the complement has probability at most
\[
\Pr_P(G_N^c)
+\Pr_P\{E_U>a/(4\sqrt2),G_N\}
\le\frac K N+\frac{K}{Ns_N^2}
\le\frac{K}{Ns_N^2},
\tag{15}
\]
where the last inequality uses \(s_N\le1\).  The loss is at most one on the complement, including every prescribed zero-fallback sample.  Combining (14)--(15) proves
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_N(s_N)}
\mathbb E_PL(\widehat\mu,\mu(P))
\le\frac{K}{Ns_N^2}
\tag{16}
\]
for all \(N\) above a fixed constant.  Enlarging \(K\) proves (16) for the finitely many smaller \(N\), because \(s_N\le1\) and \(L\le1\).

Finally, both branches of the corrected estimator lie in \([-b_\lambda,b_\lambda]^{CT}\), and the effective-radius lemma puts \(\mu(P)\) in the same box.  Therefore, deterministically,
\[
L(\widehat\mu,\mu(P))
\le\min\{1,4b_\lambda^2\}.
\tag{17}
\]
Taking the minimum of (16) and (17) proves the stated bound, uniformly over the shell.

%%% FIELD minimax-proof
Fix \(N\in\mathbb N\) and \(s_N\in(0,1]\) such that \(\mathcal P_N(s_N)\ne\varnothing\).  Let \(\widehat\mu\) be the corrected covariance--PCA and APM least-squares estimator in \(\operatorname{def:pca\text{-}estimator}\).  Its deterministic tie-breaking and prescribed zero fallback make it an everywhere-defined measurable estimator.  By \(\operatorname{lem:pca\text{-}upper}\),
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_N(s_N)}
\mathbb E_P L\{\widehat\mu,\mu(P)\}
\le \min\!\left\{1,4b_\lambda^2,\frac{K}{Ns_N^2}\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]
The infimum in the theorem is over all measurable estimators based on the observed sample and therefore includes \(\widehat\mu\).  Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\inf_{\widetilde\mu}\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_N(s_N)}
\mathbb E_P L\{\widetilde\mu,\mu(P)\}
&\le
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_N(s_N)}
\mathbb E_P L\{\widehat\mu,\mu(P)\}\\
&\le \min\!\left\{1,4b_\lambda^2,\frac{K}{Ns_N^2}\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Taking \(C_0=K\) proves the claimed upper bound and the attainment statement.  The dependence of \(C_0\) is exactly that established in \(\operatorname{lem:pca\text{-}upper}\).

%%% FIELD consistency-proof
For each \(N\), define
\[
R_N=
\inf_{\widetilde\mu}\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_N(s_N)}
\mathbb E_P L\{\widetilde\mu,\mu(P)\}.
\]
Nonnegativity of the loss and \(\operatorname{thm:minimax\text{-}frontier}\) give
\[
0\le R_N
\le \min\!\left\{1,4b_\lambda^2,\frac{C_0}{Ns_N^2}\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]
If \(Ns_N^2\to\infty\), the last entry of the minimum in (1) tends to zero, so the squeeze theorem yields \(R_N\to0\).  This proves sufficiency.

To prove that the condition is not necessary, invoke \(\operatorname{thm:four\text{-}mask\text{-}early\text{-}kill\text{-}negative}\).  It supplies allowed fixed primitive radii, a legal equal-share four-mask family \((P_\eta)\), and constants \(a_0,a_1,\eta_0>0\) such that
\[
a_0|\eta|\le s(P_\eta)\le a_1|\eta|
\quad\text{for }0<|\eta|\le\eta_0,
\qquad
\mu(P)=0
\quad\text{for every law in that model}.
\tag{2}
\]
Set
\[
\eta_N=\frac{\eta_0}{N+1},
\qquad
s_N=s(P_{\eta_N}).
\tag{3}
\]
The first inequality in (2) gives \(s_N>0\).  To verify \(s_N\le1\), take any \(x\in\mathbb R^T\).  By \(\operatorname{def:relation\text{-}operator}\), \(0\preceq Q_c\preceq I_{|S_c|}\), and \(\sum_cp_c=1\),
\[
x^{\prime}A(P)x
=\sum_{c=1}^Cp_c\|Q_cJ_cx\|_2^2
\le\sum_{c=1}^Cp_c\|J_cx\|_2^2
\le\|x\|_2^2.
\tag{4}
\]
Thus \(A(P)\preceq I_T\), and \(\operatorname{def:relation\text{-}margin}\) implies \(s(P)\le1\).  By (3), \(s_N\in(0,1]\), and
\[
s_N\le s(P_{\eta_N})=s_N\le2s_N,
\]
so \(P_{\eta_N}\in\mathcal P_N(s_N)\).  Every shell in the sequence is therefore nonempty.  The upper inequality in (2) gives
\[
Ns_N^2
\le \frac{Na_1^2\eta_0^2}{(N+1)^2}
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{5}
\]
Every law in \(\mathcal P_N(s_N)\) belongs to the same zero-target ambient model, so the identically zero estimator satisfies
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_N(s_N)}
\mathbb E_PL\{0,\mu(P)\}=0.
\tag{6}
\]
By nonnegativity, (6) implies \(R_N=0\) for every \(N\), whereas (5) gives \(Ns_N^2\to0\).  Hence the sufficient condition is not necessary under the frozen assumptions.

%%% FIELD consistency-replacement-statement
For every sequence of nonempty shells, \(Ns_N^2\to\infty\) implies
\[
\inf_{\widetilde\mu}\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_N(s_N)}
\mathbb E_P L\{\widetilde\mu,\mu(P)\}\longrightarrow0.
\]
This condition is not necessary under the frozen assumptions: there are allowed fixed primitive radii and a sequence of nonempty shells with \(Ns_N^2\to0\) for which \(\mu(P)=0\) for every \(P\in\mathcal P_N(s_N)\), and hence the identically zero estimator has zero risk for every \(N\).
