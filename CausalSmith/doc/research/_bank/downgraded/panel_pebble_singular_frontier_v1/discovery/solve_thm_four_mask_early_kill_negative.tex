Fix \(P\in\mathcal M\), and put \(\mathcal L=\operatorname{col}(\Gamma)\). The conditional moments used below exist by \(\texttt{ass:moment-radius}\), and conditioning on cohort \(c\) is well defined because \(p_c\geq p_\star>0\) by \(\texttt{ass:positive-shares}\). By \(\texttt{ass:rank-two-factor-law}\) and \(\texttt{ass:error-mean}\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\nu_c
&=\mathbb E_P[J_cY_i(0)\mid C_i=c]\\
&=\mathbb E_P[\Gamma_{S_c}\lambda_i+\varepsilon_{i,S_c}\mid C_i=c]\\
&=\Gamma_{S_c}m_c.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
Likewise, \(\texttt{ass:loading-error-orthogonality}\) makes the two cross-covariance terms vanish, and \(\texttt{ass:conditional-homoskedasticity}\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
V_c
&=\operatorname{Cov}_P(\Gamma_{S_c}\lambda_i+\varepsilon_{i,S_c}\mid C_i=c)\\
&=\Gamma_{S_c}H_c\Gamma_{S_c}^{\prime}+\sigma_c^2I_{|S_c|}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
By \(\texttt{ass:local-signal-gap}\), the first summand in (2) has rank two and second eigenvalue at least \(g_\star>0\). Hence exactly the two largest eigenvalues of \(V_c\) exceed \(\sigma_c^2\), and
\[
\Pi_2(V_c)=\Pi_{\operatorname{col}(\Gamma_{S_c})},
\qquad
Q_c=I_{|S_c|}-\Pi_{\operatorname{col}(\Gamma_{S_c})}.
\tag{3}
\]
The entire observed law \(P_O\) determines \(p_c\), \(\nu_c\), and \(V_c\) for every \(c\), because \(p_c>0\). It therefore determines every local plane in (3).

Choose \(B_c\) as in \(\operatorname{def:relation\text{-}operator}\). Since \(B_c^{\prime}B_c=Q_c\),
\[
\|B_cy\|_2^2=y^{\prime}Q_cy
\quad\text{for every }y\in\mathbb R^{|S_c|},
\]
so \(\ker B_c=\ker Q_c=\operatorname{col}(\Gamma_{S_c})\). Positivity of the cohort shares implies the basis-independent identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal K(P):=\ker\overline R(P)
&=\bigcap_{c=1}^C\ker(B_cJ_c)\\
&=\left\{x\in\mathbb R^T:x_{S_c}\in
\operatorname{col}(\Gamma_{S_c})\text{ for every }c\in[C]\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
In particular, \(\mathcal L\subseteq\mathcal K(P)\), and (4) shows that \(\mathcal K(P)\) is a functional of \(P_O\), even though the individual row bases \(B_c\) are not unique.

By \(\texttt{lem:generic-rank-two-coordinate-projection-certificate}\), under \(\texttt{ass:fixed-dimensions}\), \(\texttt{ass:deterministic-masks}\), and \(\texttt{ass:generic-factor-rows}\), the local planes determine \(\mathcal L\) uniquely if and only if the anchor multihypergraph from \(\operatorname{def:anchor\text{-}hypergraph}\) contains a family \(F\) of \(T-2\) triples satisfying
\[
|F'|\leq |V(F')|-2
\quad\text{for every nonempty }F'\subseteq F.
\tag{5}
\]
By \(\operatorname{def:pebble\text{-}certificate}\), this is equivalent to \(r_{1,2}(H_3)=T-2\). Such an \(F\) spans \([T]\), because applying (5) to \(F\) gives
\[
T-2=|F|\leq |V(F)|-2\leq T-2.
\]

We next connect uniqueness to (4). If \(\mathcal K(P)=\mathcal L\) and a two-dimensional subspace \(\widetilde{\mathcal L}\) has \((\widetilde{\mathcal L})_{S_c}=\operatorname{col}(\Gamma_{S_c})\) for every \(c\), then (4) gives \(\widetilde{\mathcal L}\subseteq\mathcal K(P)=\mathcal L\); equality follows from the dimensions. Conversely, suppose \(\mathcal K(P)\supsetneq\mathcal L\). Choose \(h\in\mathcal K(P)\setminus\mathcal L\) and a basis \(u_1,u_2\) of \(\mathcal L\), and, for \(z\neq0\), set
\[
\mathcal L_z=\operatorname{span}(u_1+zh,u_2).
\tag{6}
\]
The two generators are independent, since any dependence would imply \(h\in\mathcal L\); the same observation gives \(\mathcal L_z\neq\mathcal L\). Equation (4) gives \((\mathcal L_z)_{S_c}\subseteq\operatorname{col}(\Gamma_{S_c})\). At \(z=0\), every restricted pair has rank two because the local signal gap implies \(\operatorname{rank}(\Gamma_{S_c})=2\). For each cohort choose a nonzero two-by-two minor of that restricted pair. Each selected minor is continuous in \(z\), and there are finitely many cohorts. Therefore all remain nonzero for sufficiently small \(|z|>0\), and then
\[
(\mathcal L_z)_{S_c}=\operatorname{col}(\Gamma_{S_c})
\quad\text{for every }c\in[C].
\tag{7}
\]
Thus strict inclusion in (4) produces a distinct compatible plane. The generic coordinate-projection certificate consequently yields
\[
r_{1,2}(H_3)=T-2
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathcal K(P)=\operatorname{col}(\Gamma).
\tag{8}
\]
In particular, certificate failure gives \(\ker\overline R(P)\supsetneq\operatorname{col}(\Gamma)\), and (6)--(7) construct the asserted \(\widetilde{\mathcal L}\).

Now suppose \(r_{1,2}(H_3)=T-2\). Let \(U\) be any orthonormal basis of the observed-law kernel \(\mathcal K(P)\). By (8) and \(\Gamma^{\prime}\Gamma=I_2\), there is an orthogonal matrix \(W\in\mathbb R^{2\times2}\) such that \(U=\Gamma W\). The local signal gap makes \(U_{S_c}\) full column rank. With \(G_c=\Gamma_{S_c}^{\prime}\Gamma_{S_c}\succ0\), (1) and \((W^{\prime}G_cW)^{-1}=W^{\prime}G_c^{-1}W\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
U(U_{S_c}^{\prime}U_{S_c})^{-1}U_{S_c}^{\prime}\nu_c
&=\Gamma W(W^{\prime}G_cW)^{-1}W^{\prime}G_cm_c\\
&=\Gamma m_c.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
The right side is the causal mean rather than its definition as an observed-law functional: the factor law and conditional mean-zero error imply
\[
\mathbb E_P[Y_i(0)\mid C_i=c]
=\Gamma\mathbb E_P[\lambda_i\mid C_i=c]
+\mathbb E_P[\varepsilon_i\mid C_i=c]
=\Gamma m_c=\mu_c(P).
\tag{10}
\]
The left side of (9) is invariant under replacing \(U\) by any other orthonormal basis of its column space. Since \(P_O\) determines \(\mathcal K(P)\) and \(\nu_c\), (9) is precisely the construction in \(\operatorname{def:counterfactual\text{-}functional}\). Hence two model laws with the same entire observed law have the same value of that map, and (9)--(10) prove that every coordinate of \(\mu\) is identified when the certificate passes.

M_{\mathrm{mom}}=(2v)^{(4+\delta)/2},

\tag{14}

The right side defining the gap is positive because \(\Gamma_{0,S_1}\) has rank two. m_c=0,

\qquad

\tag{15}
\]
Thus the causal target on this nonempty certificate-failing model is the constant observed-law functional zero, although (8) still gives generic nonuniqueness of the factor plane. Certificate failure therefore entails the asserted factor-plane ambiguity, but not, without further restrictions, observed-law-equivalent laws with different causal means. This proves every assertion.

Put \(q_0=4+\delta\), and let \(K\) denote a finite positive constant, allowed to change from line to line, that depends only on the fixed primitive radii and \((T,C)\). Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_N(s_N)\). Shell membership gives every condition of \(\mathcal M\) and \(s(P)\geq s_N>0\). Since \(\Gamma^{\prime}\Gamma=I_2\), \(\|\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{op}}=1\). Thus, conditional on \(C_i=c\), for \(X_{ic}=J_cY_i(0)\),
\[
\|X_{ic}\|_2
\leq\|\Gamma\lambda_i\|_2+\|\varepsilon_i\|_2
\leq\|\lambda_i\|_2+\|\varepsilon_i\|_2.
\]
Because \(q_0>4\), the elementary power-mean inequality, \((x+y)^4\leq8(x^4+y^4)\), and \(\texttt{ass:moment-radius}\) imply
\[
\max_{c\in[C]}\mathbb E_P(\|X_{ic}\|_2^4\mid C_i=c)\leq K.
\tag{1}
\]

Let \(N_c=\sum_{i=1}^N1\{C_i=c\}\) and define
\[
G_N=\bigcap_{c=1}^C\{N_c\geq Np_\star/2\}.
\]
Since \(N_c\) is binomial with mean \(Np_c\), \(p_c\geq p_\star\), and \(\operatorname{Var}(N_c)\leq N\), Chebyshev's inequality and a union bound give
\[
\Pr_P(G_N^c)
\leq\sum_{c=1}^C\frac{4\operatorname{Var}_P(N_c)}{N^2p_\star^2}
\leq\frac K N.
\tag{2}
\]
For all \(N\) above a fixed constant, \(G_N\) gives \(N_c\geq2\) for every cohort. Conditional on the counts, observations within cohort \(c\) are independent with mean \(\nu_c\), covariance \(V_c\), and uniformly bounded fourth moment. The sample-mean variance identity gives
\[
\mathbb E_P(\|\widehat\nu_c-\nu_c\|_2^2\mid N_c)
=\frac{\operatorname{tr}(V_c)}{N_c}\leq\frac K{N_c}.
\]
Writing the centered empirical covariance as the empirical average of \((X_{ic}-\nu_c)(X_{ic}-\nu_c)^{\prime}\) minus the outer product of the sample-mean error, using \(\|xx^{\prime}\|_{\mathrm F}=\|x\|_2^2\), and expanding independent centered sums gives
\[
\mathbb E_P(\|\widehat V_c-V_c\|_{\mathrm F}^2\mid N_c)
\leq\frac K{N_c}.
\]
Also \(\mathbb E_P\|\widehat p-p\|_2^2\leq K/N\). Consequently,
\[
\mathbb E_P\!\left[
\sum_{c=1}^C\{\|\widehat\nu_c-\nu_c\|_2^2
+\|\widehat V_c-V_c\|_{\mathrm F}^2\}
+\|\widehat p-p\|_2^2;G_N\right]
\leq\frac K N.
\tag{3}
\]

Put \(P_c=I-Q_c=\Pi_2(V_c)\) and \(\widehat P_c=I-\widehat Q_c=\Pi_2(\widehat V_c)\). The factor law, loading--error orthogonality, and spherical error covariance give
\[
V_c=\Gamma_{S_c}H_c\Gamma_{S_c}^{\prime}+\sigma_c^2I_{|S_c|}.
\tag{4}
\]
The first term has rank two and second eigenvalue at least \(g_\star\). For any rank-two orthogonal projector \(P_0\), spectral decomposition of that term therefore gives
\[
\operatorname{tr}\{V_c(P_c-P_0)\}
\geq\frac{g_\star}{2}\|P_c-P_0\|_{\mathrm F}^2.
\tag{5}
\]
The empirical top-two projector \(\widehat P_c\) maximizes \(P_0\mapsto\operatorname{tr}(\widehat V_cP_0)\). Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{tr}\{V_c(P_c-\widehat P_c)\}
&\leq\operatorname{tr}\{(V_c-\widehat V_c)(P_c-\widehat P_c)\}\\
&\leq\|V_c-\widehat V_c\|_{\mathrm F}\|P_c-\widehat P_c\|_{\mathrm F}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Combining this with (5), including the zero-distance case, proves
\[
\|\widehat Q_c-Q_c\|_{\mathrm F}
\leq\frac{2}{g_\star}\|\widehat V_c-V_c\|_{\mathrm F}.
\tag{6}
\]

Introduce padded relation matrices
\[
\mathcal R=\operatorname{stack}_{c=1}^C\sqrt{p_c}\,Q_cJ_c,
\qquad
\widehat{\mathcal R}=\operatorname{stack}_{c=1}^C
\sqrt{\widehat p_c}\,\widehat Q_cJ_c.
\]
Their Gram matrices are \(A(P)\) and \(\widehat A\); padding changes no positive singular value relative to \(\overline R(P)\). On \(G_N\), \(p_c\geq p_\star\) and \(\widehat p_c\geq p_\star/2\). From
\[
\sqrt{\widehat p_c}\widehat Q_c-\sqrt{p_c}Q_c
=\sqrt{\widehat p_c}(\widehat Q_c-Q_c)
+(\sqrt{\widehat p_c}-\sqrt{p_c})Q_c,
\]
the identity \(|\sqrt x-\sqrt y|=|x-y|/(\sqrt x+\sqrt y)\), (3), and (6), we obtain
\[
\mathbb E_P[\|\widehat{\mathcal R}-\mathcal R\|_{\mathrm F}^2;G_N]
\leq\frac K N.
\tag{7}
\]

Equation (4) implies \(Q_c\Gamma_{S_c}=0\), so \(\operatorname{col}(\Gamma)\subseteq\ker\mathcal R\). Since \(s(P)=\sigma_{T-2}(\mathcal R)\geq s_N>0\) while \(\operatorname{rank}(\Gamma)=2\),
\[
\ker\mathcal R=\operatorname{col}(\Gamma).
\tag{8}
\]
Let \(U\) be an orthonormal basis of this kernel. The bottom-two eigenspace optimality defining \(\widehat U\) gives
\[
\|\widehat{\mathcal R}\widehat U\|_{\mathrm F}
\leq\|\widehat{\mathcal R}U\|_{\mathrm F}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\|\mathcal R\widehat U\|_{\mathrm F}
\leq2\|\widehat{\mathcal R}-\mathcal R\|_{\mathrm F}.
\]
The restriction of \(\mathcal R\) to \(\operatorname{col}(U)^\perp\) has smallest singular value at least \(s_N\). Thus
\[
E_U:=\|(I-UU^{\prime})\widehat U\|_{\mathrm F}
\leq\frac{2}{s_N}\|\widehat{\mathcal R}-\mathcal R\|_{\mathrm F}.
\tag{9}
\]

The covariance upper bound and local signal gap imply
\[
g_\star
\leq\lambda_2(\Gamma_{S_c}H_c\Gamma_{S_c}^{\prime})
\leq v^{\star}\sigma_2(\Gamma_{S_c})^2.
\]
Since \(U=\Gamma W_0\) for an orthogonal \(W_0\),
\[
\sigma_2(U_{S_c})\geq a:=\sqrt{g_\star/v^{\star}}>0.
\tag{10}
\]
The elementary principal-angle decomposition supplies an orthogonal two-by-two matrix \(W\) such that, with \(\widetilde U=\widehat UW\),
\[
\|\widetilde U-U\|_{\mathrm F}^2
=2\sum_{j=1}^2(1-\cos\phi_j)
\leq2\sum_{j=1}^2\sin^2\phi_j
=2E_U^2.
\tag{11}
\]
If \(E_U\leq a/(4\sqrt2)\), singular-value perturbation and (11) give \(\sigma_2(\widetilde U_{S_c})\geq a/2\) for every \(c\). Define, whenever the inverse exists,
\[
K_c(X)=X(X_{S_c}^{\prime}X_{S_c})^{-1}X_{S_c}^{\prime}.
\]
This map is right-orthogonally invariant. For \(X=U\) and \(X=\widetilde U\), (10)--(11) uniformly bound the inverse Gram matrices. The identity
\[
G^{-1}-\widetilde G^{-1}=G^{-1}(\widetilde G-G)\widetilde G^{-1}
\]
and a three-term expansion of \(K_c(\widetilde U)-K_c(U)\) then yield
\[
\|K_c(\widetilde U)-K_c(U)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq K\|\widetilde U-U\|_{\mathrm F}
\leq KE_U.
\tag{12}
\]

The factor law and conditional error mean zero give \(\nu_c=\Gamma_{S_c}m_c\). Hence full local column rank and \(U=\Gamma W_0\) imply
\[
K_c(U)\nu_c=\Gamma m_c=\mu_c(P).
\tag{13}
\]
By \(\texttt{lem:effective-loading-mean-bound}\), \(\|m_c\|_2\leq b_\lambda\), so \(\|\nu_c\|_2\leq b_\lambda\). On \(G_N\cap\{E_U\leq a/(4\sqrt2)\}\), every displayed inverse in the least-squares branch exists, and (9), (12), and (13) give
\[
\|K_c(\widetilde U)\widehat\nu_c-\mu_c(P)\|_2
\leq K\left\{\|\widehat\nu_c-\nu_c\|_2
+\frac{\|\widehat{\mathcal R}-\mathcal R\|_{\mathrm F}}{s_N}\right\}.
\tag{14}
\]
Coordinatewise clipping to \([-b_\lambda,b_\lambda]^T\) cannot increase the distance from \(\mu_c(P)\), because the same lemma puts every target coordinate in that interval. Equations (3), (7), and (14) bound the expected squared sup-norm error on this event by \(K/(Ns_N^2)\). By (2), (7), (9), and Markov's inequality,
\[
\Pr_P(G_N^c)+\Pr_P\{E_U>a/(4\sqrt2),G_N\}
\leq\frac K N+\frac{K}{Ns_N^2}
\leq\frac{K}{Ns_N^2},
\tag{15}
\]
where \(s_N\leq1\). The loss is at most one on the complement, including every sample on which the prescribed zero fallback is used. Thus, for all sufficiently large \(N\),
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_N(s_N)}
\mathbb E_PL(\widehat\mu,\mu(P))
\leq\frac{K}{Ns_N^2}.
\tag{16}
\]
Increasing \(K\) handles the finitely many smaller sample sizes because \(s_N\leq1\) and \(L\leq1\).

Finally, both estimator branches lie in \([-b_\lambda,b_\lambda]^{CT}\), and the target lies in that box. Deterministically,
\[
L(\widehat\mu,\mu(P))\leq\min\{1,4b_\lambda^2\}.
\tag{17}
\]
Taking the minimum of (16) and (17) proves the claimed uniform bound.

%%% FIELD proof-consistency-frontier
For each \(N\), define
\[
R_N=\inf_{\widetilde\mu}\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_N(s_N)}
\mathbb E_PL\{\widetilde\mu,\mu(P)\}.
\]
Nonnegativity of the loss and \(\texttt{thm:minimax-frontier}\) give
\[
0\leq R_N\leq\min\!\left\{1,4b_\lambda^2,\frac{C_0}{Ns_N^2}\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]
If \(Ns_N^2\to\infty\), the last term in (1) tends to zero, and therefore \(R_N\to0\).

For nonnecessity, use the legal equal-share family from \(\texttt{thm:four-mask-early-kill-negative}\). There are constants \(a_0,a_1,\eta_0>0\) such that
\[
a_0|\eta|\leq s(P_\eta)\leq a_1|\eta|
\quad\text{for }0<|\eta|\leq\eta_0,
\qquad
\mu(P)=0\quad\text{for every law in that fixed model}.
\tag{2}
\]
Set
\[
\eta_N=\frac{\eta_0}{N+1},
\qquad
s_N=s(P_{\eta_N}).
\tag{3}
\]
The lower inequality in (2) makes \(s_N>0\). Moreover, for every \(x\in\mathbb R^T\), \(0\preceq Q_c\preceq I\) and \(\sum_cp_c=1\) imply
\[
x^{\prime}A(P)x
=\sum_{c=1}^Cp_c\|Q_cJ_cx\|_2^2
\leq\sum_{c=1}^Cp_c\|J_cx\|_2^2
\leq\|x\|_2^2.
\tag{4}
\]
Thus \(A(P)\preceq I_T\), so \(s(P)\leq1\) by \(\operatorname{def:relation\text{-}margin}\). Hence \(s_N\in(0,1]\), and \(P_{\eta_N}\in\mathcal P_N(s_N)\); every shell is nonempty. The upper inequality in (2) gives
\[
Ns_N^2\leq\frac{Na_1^2\eta_0^2}{(N+1)^2}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{5}
\]
Every member of these shells belongs to the same zero-target ambient model. Therefore the identically zero estimator satisfies
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_N(s_N)}
\mathbb E_PL\{0,\mu(P)\}=0.
\tag{6}
\]
By nonnegativity, \(R_N=0\) for every \(N\), while (5) gives \(Ns_N^2\to0\). Thus divergence of \(Ns_N^2\) is sufficient but not necessary under the frozen assumptions.

%%% FIELD proof-four-mask-negative
Put \(q_0=4+\delta\). Choose allowed fixed constants \(v_\star=v>0\), \(v^{\star}=2v\), and
\[
M_{\mathrm{mom}}=(2v)^{q_0/2},
\]
and take \(p_c=1/4\) with \(0<p_\star<1/4\). For the four masks in \(\operatorname{def:four\text{-}mask\text{-}witness}\), let \(X_\eta\) have rows \((1,x_t)\), where
\[
(x_1,\ldots,x_6)=(1,2,3,4,5,19/5+\eta),
\]
and put
\[
\Gamma_\eta=X_\eta(X_\eta^{\prime}X_\eta)^{-1/2}.
\tag{1}
\]
The two columns of \(X_\eta\) are independent, so (1) is defined and \(\Gamma_\eta^{\prime}\Gamma_\eta=I_2\). For all sufficiently small \(|\eta|\), each mask contains three distinct slopes; hence every \(\Gamma_{\eta,S_c}\) has rank two. On a sufficiently small closed neighborhood of zero, continuity and finiteness of the cohort set give
\[
\underline g:=v\min_{c,\,|\eta|\leq\eta_0}
\sigma_2(\Gamma_{\eta,S_c})^2>0.
\]

\{\sqrt{2v}e_1,-\sqrt{2v}e_1,
\sqrt{2v}e_2,-\sqrt{2v}e_2\},

and set \(\varepsilon_i=0\) almost surely. Then, for every cohort,
\[
m_c=0,
\qquad
H_c=vI_2,
\qquad
\mathbb E\|\lambda_i\|_2^{q_0}=(2v)^{q_0/2}=M_{\mathrm{mom}}.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus the covariance bounds, conditional error mean, loading--error covariance orthogonality, spherical error covariance with \(\sigma_c=0\), local eigengap, and common moment bound all hold. Independent sampling supplies the sampling assumption.

For every sufficiently small nonzero \(\eta\), the minor calculation in \(\texttt{prop:o3-strict-inclusion}\) gives relation rank four, the generic maximum \(T-2\); hence \(\Gamma_\eta\notin\mathcal Z_S\). The same proposition gives constants \(a_0,a_1>0\), independent of \(\eta\), such that

a_0|\eta|\leq s(P_\eta)\leq a_1|\eta|.
\tag{3}
\]
Equations (1)--(3) verify that \((P_\eta)\) is a legal equal-share family in the frozen class and that every local restriction has rank two.

The boundary equality in (2) is \(M_{\mathrm{mom}}=(2v_\star)^{q_0/2}\). By the corrected \(\texttt{lem:tight-moment-radius-forces-zero-means}\), it forces
\[
\mu(P)=0

for these fixed primitive radii, not merely for the displayed family. For any sufficiently small nonzero \(\eta\), put \(s_N=s(P_\eta)\). Since a padded projector relation has operator norm at most one, \(s_N\leq1\), and plainly \(P_\eta\in\mathcal P_N(s_N)\). Thus the shell is nonempty. But (4) implies, for every pair of laws in this shell and every \(d\neq0\),

\|\mu(P_{\eta,d})-\mu(P_{\eta,0})\|_\infty=0

The target-separation conjunct in \(\operatorname{oeq:four\text{-}mask\text{-}early\text{-}kill}\) therefore fails on a legal nonempty family. This proves the asserted negative answer independently of whether moment inversion has an upper width bound.

%%% FIELD proof-nonempty-shell-negative
Use the legal fixed-radius family constructed in \(\texttt{thm:four-mask-early-kill-negative}\). Choose \(\eta_\star>0\) small enough that every nonzero \(|\eta|\leq\eta_\star\) belongs to the legal punctured neighborhood in that theorem, put \(\eta_N=\eta_\star/(N+1)\), and set

s_N=s(P_{\eta_N}).
\]
For every \(N\), \(s_N\in(0,1]\) and \(P_{\eta_N}\in\mathcal P_N(s_N)\), so these shells are nonempty. The corrected \(\texttt{lem:tight-moment-radius-forces-zero-means}\) applies to every law in their ambient model and gives \(\mu(P)=0\). Consequently, for any two same-shell laws,
\[
\|\mu(P_d)-\mu(P_0)\|_\infty=0.
\tag{1}
\]
For \(c_1>0\) and \(d\neq0\), (1) contradicts the required inequality \(\|\mu(P_d)-\mu(P_0)\|_\infty\geq c_1|d|\). Nonemptiness therefore does not imply the proposed legal target-separating deformation.

There is also a separate scale obstruction. Write
\[
r_N=\min\{1,(\sqrt N s_N)^{-1}\}.
\]
The condition in the question requires only \(0<|d|\leq c_0r_N\). It permits, for example, \(|d_N|=r_N/N\), for which \(d_N^2=o(r_N^2)\). Put \(q_0=4+\delta\). If \(\mathcal M\) is empty, the universal assertion is vacuous. Otherwise, the assumed equality

M_{\mathrm{mom}}=(2v_\star)^{q_0/2}

implies directly from \(\operatorname{def:effective\text{-}loading\text{-}mean\text{-}radius}\) that \(b_\lambda=0\). The zero-radius boundary conclusion of \(\texttt{lem:effective-loading-mean-radius-bound}\), which uses no equality between \(v^{\star}\) and \(v_\star\), applies to every \(P\in\mathcal M\) and every \(c\in[C]\). It yields

%%% FIELD proof-effective-radius-bound
Fix \(P\in\mathcal M\) and \(c\in[C]\). The positive-share condition makes the conditional law well defined. Put \(q_0=4+\delta>2\). By the definitions of \(m_c\) and \(H_c\),
\[
\mathbb E_P[\|\lambda_i\|_2^2\mid C_i=c]
=\operatorname{tr}(H_c)+\|m_c\|_2^2.
\tag{1}
\]
To justify the moment comparison without importing an undeclared theorem, let \(r=q_0/2>1\). For every \(x\geq0\), convexity of \(x^r\), or direct differentiation, gives
\[
x\leq\frac{r-1}{r}+\frac{x^r}{r}.
\]
Apply this inequality to a nonnegative random variable divided by its \(r\)-th moment scale and then take expectations; the zero-moment case is immediate. This proves \((\mathbb EX)^r\leq\mathbb E(X^r)\). Conditional application with \(X=\|\lambda_i\|_2^2\), followed by the primitive moment restriction, gives
\[
\left\{\operatorname{tr}(H_c)+\|m_c\|_2^2\right\}^{q_0/2}
\leq\mathbb E_P[\|\lambda_i\|_2^{q_0}\mid C_i=c]
\leq M_{\mathrm{mom}}.
\tag{2}
\]
Since \(H_c\succeq v_\star I_2\),
\[
\operatorname{tr}(H_c)\geq2v_\star.
\tag{3}
\]
Taking the positive power \(2/q_0\) in (2), using (3), and subtracting gives
\[
0\leq\|m_c\|_2^2
\leq M_{\mathrm{mom}}^{2/q_0}-\operatorname{tr}(H_c)
\leq M_{\mathrm{mom}}^{2/q_0}-2v_\star.
\tag{4}
\]
Thus \(\|m_c\|_2\leq b_\lambda\). Also \(\Gamma^{\prime}\Gamma=I_2\) gives \(\|\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{op}}=1\), while the factor equation and conditional mean-zero error give \(\mu_c(P)=\Gamma m_c\). Hence
\[
|\mu_{ct}(P)|
\leq\|\Gamma m_c\|_2
\leq\|m_c\|_2
\leq b_\lambda.
\tag{5}
\]

Suppose now that \(\mathcal M\) is nonempty. For any \(P\in\mathcal M\), (2)--(3) imply
\[
M_{\mathrm{mom}}\geq(2v_\star)^{q_0/2}.
\tag{6}
\]
By the positive-part definition, \(b_\lambda=0\) is equivalent to \(M_{\mathrm{mom}}^{2/q_0}\leq2v_\star\). Combining this with (6) proves
\[
b_\lambda=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
M_{\mathrm{mom}}=(2v_\star)^{q_0/2}.
\tag{7}
\]

Assume the equivalent boundary conditions in (7). For arbitrary \(P\in\mathcal M\) and \(c\in[C]\), retain every nonnegative term in the moment restriction to obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
(2v_\star)^{q_0/2}=M_{\mathrm{mom}}
&\geq\mathbb E_P[\|\lambda_i\|_2^{q_0}+\|\varepsilon_i\|_2^{q_0}\mid C_i=c]\\
&\geq\mathbb E_P[\|\lambda_i\|_2^{q_0}\mid C_i=c]\\
&\geq\{\operatorname{tr}(H_c)+\|m_c\|_2^2\}^{q_0/2}\\
&\geq(2v_\star)^{q_0/2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
The endpoints coincide, so equality holds throughout. Strict monotonicity gives
\[
\operatorname{tr}(H_c)+\|m_c\|_2^2=2v_\star.
\]
Both summands \(\operatorname{tr}(H_c)-2v_\star\) and \(\|m_c\|_2^2\) are nonnegative; hence
\[
\operatorname{tr}(H_c)=2v_\star,
\qquad
m_c=0.
\tag{9}
\]
The matrix \(H_c-v_\star I_2\) is positive semidefinite and has trace zero, so both of its eigenvalues vanish and
\[
H_c=v_\star I_2.
\tag{10}
\]
Equality between the first two expectation terms in (8) also gives
\[
\mathbb E_P[\|\varepsilon_i\|_2^{q_0}\mid C_i=c]=0.
\]
The integrand is nonnegative, so \(\varepsilon_i=0\) almost surely conditional on \(C_i=c\). Finally, \(\mu_c(P)=\Gamma m_c=0\) by (9). Since \(P\) and \(c\) were arbitrary, all boundary conclusions hold throughout \(\mathcal M\).

%%% FIELD statement-four-mask-resolution
The answer to \(\operatorname{oeq:four\text{-}mask\text{-}early\text{-}kill}\) is negative under the frozen assumptions. There are allowed fixed primitive radii and a legal equal-share four-mask family with \(s(P_\eta)\asymp|\eta|\), nonempty shells, and full-rank local factor restrictions, but \(\mu(P)=0\) for every law in the ambient model. Hence no two same-shell laws can satisfy positive order-\(|d|\) target separation, and the conjunction posed by the open-ended question fails regardless of the performance of observed-moment inversion.

%%% FIELD proof-four-mask-resolution
The family and fixed radii constructed in \(\texttt{thm:four-mask-early-kill-negative}\) satisfy every asserted rank, shell, and moment condition, while that theorem proves \(\mu(P)=0\) throughout the ambient model. Therefore every same-shell target difference is zero, so it cannot be at least \(c_1|d|\) when \(c_1>0\) and \(d\neq0\). Since the question asks for this separation jointly with the other properties, failure of this conjunct proves the stated negative answer.

%%% FIELD statement-shell-resolution
The answer to \(\operatorname{oeq:shell\text{-}deformation}\) is negative. Nonemptiness of \(\mathcal P_N(s_N)\) does not imply a positive target-separating deformation under the frozen primitive restrictions: there are allowed fixed radii and nonempty four-mask shells for which \(\mu(P)=0\) for every law in the ambient model. Moreover, an upper restriction \(0<|d|\leq c_0\min\{1,(\sqrt N s_N)^{-1}\}\), without a comparable lower restriction on \(|d|\), is insufficient by itself to deliver a frontier-scale squared-risk lower bound.

%%% FIELD proof-shell-resolution
The explicit family in \(\texttt{thm:nonempty-shell-does-not-imply-deformation}\) gives nonempty shells on which every target equals zero, so no two laws have a positive target difference. That theorem also shows that the stated one-sided restriction permits \(|d|\) to be little-oh of the frontier scale, making its squared separation little-oh of \(\min\{1,(Ns_N^2)^{-1}\}\). These two facts prove both parts of the negative answer.
