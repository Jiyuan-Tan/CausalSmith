%%% FIELD statement-low-intensity-frontier-dependency-fix
If \(0<\kappa\le-\log(1-\alpha)/\overline m_*\), then \(D_{\mathrm{det}}(\kappa)=L\exp(-\kappa m_*)\). At each fixed \(s\), the conditional value is \(L\exp\{-\kappa m(s)\}\). Every fixed-share optimizer reports \(\Theta\) at the zero table and a singleton at every nonzero table. A globally optimal observed-share policy is characterized exactly by \(C(s,0)=\Theta\) and \(\sum_{x\ne0}P_{\eta,\kappa}(x)\ell(C(s,x))\le L\exp(-\kappa m_*)-L\exp\{-\kappa M(\eta)\}\) for every \(\eta\). The share \(s^*\) is the unique minimizer of \(m(s)\), the associated lower-endpoint nuisance is least favorable for every optimizer, and additional least-favorable configurations occur exactly when the displayed allowance binds.

%%% FIELD proof-low-intensity-frontier
Put \(\bar g=T+1\) for the never-treated cohort.  First, \(B(\eta)>0\): the set \(\mathcal D\) is nonempty because \((2,2)\in\mathcal D\), and every summand \(s_gA_gb_t\) in \(B(\eta)\) is positive by \(\mathrm{ass{:}observed\text{-}share\text{-}overlap}\), \(\mathrm{ass{:}exposure\text{-}box}\), and \(\mathrm{ass{:}time\text{-}box}\).  Hence
\[
 \theta(\eta)
 =\frac{\sum_{(g,t)\in\mathcal D}w^0_{gt}v_{gt}}
         {B(\eta)}-1
 =\sum_{(g,t)\in\mathcal D}
   \frac{w^0_{gt}}{B(\eta)}(v_{gt}-1).
\]
The coefficients in the last sum are positive and sum to one.  Thus \(\theta(\eta)\in[-1/2,2]=\Theta\) under \(\mathrm{ass{:}treated\text{-}multiplier\text{-}box}\).  Conversely, for every fixed \(s\in\mathcal S_T\) and every \(r\in\Theta\), the admissible choice \(A_g=1\), \(b_t=1\), and \(v_{gt}=1+r\) has \(\theta(\eta)=r\).  This establishes target attainability at each observed share rather than defining it by the observed law.

We next calculate the exposure extrema.  For a finite adoption cohort \(g\le T\), its contribution to \(M(\eta)\) is
\[
 M_g=s_gA_g\left(\sum_{t<g}b_t+
                         \sum_{t\ge g}b_tv_{gt}\right),
\]
whereas for \(g=\bar g\) the second sum is absent.  Every factor multiplying a free coordinate is strictly positive.  Therefore the simultaneous minimum permitted by \(\mathrm{ass{:}exposure\text{-}box}\), \(\mathrm{ass{:}time\text{-}normalization}\), \(\mathrm{ass{:}time\text{-}box}\), and \(\mathrm{ass{:}treated\text{-}multiplier\text{-}box}\) has
\[
 A_g=\frac12,\qquad b_1=1,\qquad b_t=\frac12\ (t>1),
 \qquad v_{gt}=\frac12\ ((g,t)\in\mathcal D).
\]
For \(g\le T\), this makes the cohort contribution
\[
 s_g\frac12\left{1+\frac{g-2}{2}
              +\frac{T-g+1}{4}\right}
 =s_g\frac{T+g+1}{8}.
\]
For \(g=\bar g\), the contribution is
\(s_{\bar g}(T+1)/4=s_{\bar g}(T+\bar g+1)/8\).  Consequently
\[
 \min_{A,b,v}M(s,A,b,v)
 =m(s)=\sum_{g=2}^{T+1}s_g\frac{T+g+1}{8}.
\tag{1}
\]
The same strict monotonicity shows that this minimizing nuisance is unique at each fixed \(s\).

At the simultaneous upper endpoints \(A_g=2\), \(b_1=1\), \(b_t=2\) for \(t>1\), and \(v_{gt}=3\), the contribution of cohort \(g\le T\) is
\[
 2s_g\{1+2(g-2)+6(T-g+1)\}
 =s_g(12T-8g+6).
\]
The same expression at \(g=T+1\) equals \(s_g(4T-2)\), which is the never-treated contribution.  Hence
\[
 \max_{A,b,v}M(s,A,b,v)
 =\overline m(s):=\sum_{g=2}^{T+1}s_g(12T-8g+6).
\tag{2}
\]

There are \(T\) cohorts.  Write \(s_g=(2T)^{-1}+r_g\), where \(r_g\ge0\) and \(\sum_gr_g=1/2\).  The coefficient in (1) is strictly increasing in \(g\), while that in (2) is strictly decreasing in \(g\).  Thus both the minimum of \(m(s)\) and the maximum of \(\overline m(s)\) uniquely put all excess mass on \(g=2\), giving \(s=s^*\).  Direct summation yields
\[
 \min_{s\in\mathcal S_T}m(s)
 =\frac{1}{2T}\sum_{g=2}^{T+1}\frac{T+g+1}{8}
   +\frac12\frac{T+3}{8}
 =\frac{5T+11}{32}=m_*,
\tag{3}
\]
and
\[
 \max_{s\in\mathcal S_T}\overline m(s)
 =\frac{1}{2T}\sum_{g=2}^{T+1}(12T-8g+6)
   +\frac12(12T-10)
 =10T-8=\overline m_*.
\tag{4}
\]

Fix \(0<\kappa\le-\log(1-\alpha)/\overline m_*\).  By \(\mathrm{ass{:}exact\text{-}product\text{-}poisson}\), independence and the Poisson zero probabilities give
\[
 p_{\eta,0}:=P_{\eta,\kappa}(X=0)
 =\prod_{g,t}\exp(-\kappa w_{gt})
 =\exp\{-\kappa M(\eta)\}.
\tag{5}
\]
Equations (2)--(4) imply, uniformly over the experiment class,
\[
 p_{\eta,0}\ge \exp(-\kappa\overline m_*)\ge1-\alpha,
 \qquad P_{\eta,\kappa}(X\ne0)\le\alpha.
\tag{6}
\]

Let \(C\) be any honest deterministic interval policy and fix \(s\).  If some \(r\in\Theta\) were absent from \(C(s,0)\), choose the admissible nuisance constructed above with \(\theta(\eta)=r\).  Then (6) would give
\[
 P_{\eta,\kappa}\{r\in C(s,X)\}
 \le P_{\eta,\kappa}(X\ne0)
 \le\alpha<1-\alpha,
\]
contradicting honesty.  Hence \(C(s,0)\) contains every point of \(\Theta\).  Since its action is a subinterval of \(\Theta\), necessarily
\[
 C(s,0)=\Theta.
\tag{7}
\]

Let \(d_s(\kappa)\) denote the fixed-share version of the minimax problem, with only \((A,b,v)\) varying.  At the nuisance attaining (1), (5) and (7) imply
\[
 \sup_{A,b,v}E_{\eta,\kappa}\ell(C(s,X))
 \ge L\exp\{-\kappa m(s)\}.
\tag{8}
\]
For the reverse inequality, use the Borel rule \(C^\circ(s,0)=\Theta\) and \(C^\circ(s,x)=\{-1/2\}\) for \(x\ne0\).  By (6) its zero-table action alone gives coverage at least \(1-\alpha\), and
\[
 E_{\eta,\kappa}\ell(C^\circ(s,X))
 =L\exp\{-\kappa M(\eta)\}.
\]
Taking the nuisance supremum and using (1) proves
\[
 d_s(\kappa)=L\exp\{-\kappa m(s)\}.
\tag{9}
\]
Moreover, at the minimizing nuisance every count array has strictly positive probability because all cell means are positive.  For any fixed-share optimizer, (7) gives
\[
 E_{\eta_s^-,\kappa}\ell(C(s,X))
 =L e^{-\kappa m(s)}+
   \sum_{x\ne0}P_{\eta_s^-,\kappa}(x)\ell(C(s,x)),
\tag{10}
\]
where \(\eta_s^-\) denotes that minimizing nuisance.  Equality with (9) forces every nonnegative summand in (10) to vanish.  Full support then implies \(\ell(C(s,x))=0\) for every \(x\ne0\), so every such action is a singleton.  Conversely, (7) together with arbitrary singleton actions off zero gives exactly the risk in (9), so these and only these are the fixed-share optimizers.

Any globally honest rule is honest after fixing a share, so (9) gives a global lower bound \(\sup_s d_s(\kappa)\).  The single jointly Borel rule \(C^\circ\) above attains that bound.  Equation (3) therefore proves
\[
 D_{\mathrm{det}}(\kappa)
 =\sup_{s\in\mathcal S_T}L e^{-\kappa m(s)}
 =L e^{-\kappa m_*}=:R_*.
\tag{11}
\]

It remains to verify the claimed complete global characterization.  By (7), every honest global policy has zero-table risk contribution \(L e^{-\kappa M(\eta)}\).  Therefore its risk at \(\eta\) is at most \(R_*\) if and only if
\[
 \sum_{x\ne0}P_{\eta,\kappa}(x)\ell(C(s,x))
 \le R_*-L e^{-\kappa M(\eta)}.
\tag{12}
\]
Thus every globally optimal Borel policy must satisfy (7) and (12).  Conversely, (7) gives honesty by (6), while (12) bounds every nuisance risk by \(R_*\); the lower bound (11) then makes the policy optimal.  This proves the asserted if-and-only-if description.

Finally, (1)--(3) show that the configuration \(\eta_*^-\) consisting of \(s^*\) and all the stated lower-endpoint nuisance coordinates is the unique global minimizer of \(M\).  At that configuration the right side of (12) is zero, so every optimizer has risk \(R_*\); hence \(\eta_*^-\) is least favorable for every optimizer.  At an arbitrary configuration, its risk equals \(R_*\) if and only if (12) binds.  Consequently all additional least-favorable configurations, and only those configurations, are exactly the equality cases in the displayed allowance.

%%% FIELD proof-randomized-gap
Retain the low-intensity condition.  The coordinatewise upper bounds on \(A\), \(b\), and \(v\) give
\[
 M(\eta)\le\sum_{g=2}^{T+1}s_g(12T-8g+6)
 \le10T-8=\overline m_*;
\tag{13}
\]
the first inequality follows by summing \(2\{1+2(g-2)+6(T-g+1)\}=12T-8g+6\) for \(g\le T\), with the same formula giving \(4T-2\) at \(g=T+1\), and the second follows by assigning the overlap simplex's excess mass to the largest coefficient, at \(g=2\).  Also, for every fixed \(s\) and every \(r\in\Theta\), the admissible choice \(A_g=1\), \(b_t=1\), and \(v_{gt}=1+r\) attains \(\theta(\eta)=r\).

A data-independent Markov kernel that reports \(\Theta\) with probability \(1-\alpha\) and the fixed singleton \(\{-1/2\}\) with probability \(\alpha\) covers every \(r\in\Theta\) with probability at least \(1-\alpha\).  Its expected length is \((1-\alpha)L\), and hence
\[
 D_{\mathrm{rand}}(\kappa)\le(1-\alpha)L.
\tag{14}
\]

For the lower bound, fix an arbitrary honest kernel \(K\) and define its inclusion probability at a table \(x\) by
\[
 q_{s,x}(r):=K(s,x,\{J\in\mathfrak I:r\in J\}),
 \qquad r\in\Theta.
\]
For every \(s\) and \(r\), use the admissible target-attaining nuisance just constructed.  Writing \(p_{\eta,0}=e^{-\kappa M(\eta)}\), honesty and \(0\le q_{s,x}(r)\le1\) give
\[
 1-\alpha
 \le \sum_xP_{\eta,\kappa}(x)q_{s,x}(r)
 \le p_{\eta,0}q_{s,0}(r)+1-p_{\eta,0}.
\]
Rearranging, and using \(M(\eta)\le\overline m_*\), yields
\[
 q_{s,0}(r)\ge1-\alpha e^{\kappa M(\eta)}
 \ge1-\alpha e^{\kappa\overline m_*}.
\tag{15}
\]
Together with the trivial lower bound zero, (15) holds with its right side replaced by
\(c_\kappa:=[1-\alpha e^{\kappa\overline m_*}]_+\), where \([a]_+=\max\{a,0\}\).

For every closed interval \(J\subseteq\Theta\), its length is its Lebesgue measure, so
\[
 \ell(J)=\int_\Theta \mathbf 1\{r\in J\}\,dr.
\]
Integrating this nonnegative identity first with respect to \(K(s,0,dJ)\) and then over \(r\) gives
\[
 \int_{\mathfrak I}\ell(J)K(s,0,dJ)
 =\int_\Theta q_{s,0}(r)\,dr
 \ge Lc_\kappa.
\tag{16}
\]
Evaluate the risk of \(K\) at the lower-endpoint configuration \(\eta_*^-\) from \(\mathrm{thm{:}low\text{-}intensity\text{-}frontier}\), for which \(s=s^*\), \(M(\eta_*^-)=m_*\), and \(P_{\eta_*^-,\kappa}(X=0)=e^{-\kappa m_*}\).  Dropping the nonnegative risk from nonzero tables and applying (16) gives
\[
 \sup_\eta E_{P_{\eta,\kappa}}
       \int_{\mathfrak I}\ell(J)K(s,X,dJ)
 \ge e^{-\kappa m_*}Lc_\kappa.
\]
Taking the infimum over honest kernels proves
\[
 e^{-\kappa m_*}L[1-\alpha e^{\kappa\overline m_*}]_+
 \le D_{\mathrm{rand}}(\kappa)\le(1-\alpha)L.
\tag{17}
\]

The low-intensity restriction implies
\(e^{-\kappa m_*}>1-\alpha\): indeed \(m_*<\overline m_*\) for \(T\ge3\), and
\[
 \kappa m_*\le-\log(1-\alpha)\frac{m_*}{\overline m_*}
 <-\log(1-\alpha).
\]
Using the exact deterministic value from \(\mathrm{thm{:}low\text{-}intensity\text{-}frontier}\), (14) is consequently strict relative to it:
\[
 D_{\mathrm{rand}}(\kappa)\le(1-\alpha)L
 <Le^{-\kappa m_*}=D_{\mathrm{det}}(\kappa).
\]
Finally, as \(\kappa\downarrow0\), the lower and upper bounds in (17) both converge to \((1-\alpha)L\).  Hence
\[
 D_{\mathrm{rand}}(\kappa)\longrightarrow(1-\alpha)L,
 \qquad
 D_{\mathrm{det}}(\kappa)=Le^{-\kappa m_*}\longrightarrow L,
\]
and subtraction gives
\(D_{\mathrm{det}}(\kappa)-D_{\mathrm{rand}}(\kappa)\to\alpha L\), as claimed.

%%% FIELD proof-untreated-zero-penalty
Let
\[
 Z_{\mathcal U}:=\left\{x:\sum_{(g,t)\in\mathcal U}x_{gt}=0\right\},
 \qquad
 U_0(\eta):=\sum_{(g,t)\in\mathcal U}w_{gt}.
\]
Because untreated cells have \(w_{gt}=s_gA_gb_t\), the contribution of cohort \(g\le T\) to \(U_0\) is \(s_gA_g\sum_{t<g}b_t\), and the same formula includes all \(T\) periods when \(g=T+1\).  Every coefficient is positive.  The exposure and time restrictions therefore imply that the simultaneous minimum has
\[
 A_g=\frac12\quad(g\in\mathcal G),
 \qquad b_1=1,
 \qquad b_t=\frac12\quad(t>1),
\]
and its cohort contribution is
\[
 s_g\frac12\left\{1+\frac{g-2}{2}\right\}
 =s_g\frac g4.
\]
Thus
\[
 \min_{A,b,v}U_0(s,A,b,v)
 =u(s)=\sum_{g=2}^{T+1}s_g\frac g4.
\tag{17}
\]
The multipliers \(v_{gt}\) do not occur in \(U_0\) and are unrestricted within their maintained box.  Since the coefficients \(g/4\) are strictly increasing, the overlap-simplex calculation used in \(\mathrm{thm{:}low\text{-}intensity\text{-}frontier}\) uniquely selects \(s^*\), and
\[
 \min_{s\in\mathcal S_T}u(s)
 =\frac{1}{2T}\sum_{g=2}^{T+1}\frac g4
  +\frac12\frac24
 =\frac{T+7}{16}=u_*.
\tag{18}
\]

Under the exact product-Poisson law, summing the untreated zero-count exponents gives
\[
 P_{\eta,\kappa}(X\in Z_{\mathcal U})
 =e^{-\kappa U_0(\eta)}.
\tag{19}
\]
Every policy admitted by \(D_{\mathcal U}\) has length \(L\) throughout \(Z_{\mathcal U}\).  Evaluating at a nuisance attaining (17)--(18) therefore gives the lower bound
\[
 D_{\mathcal U}(\kappa)\ge Le^{-\kappa u_*}.
\tag{20}
\]

For the reverse bound, take the Borel policy that reports \(\Theta\) on \(Z_{\mathcal U}\) and \(\{-1/2\}\) otherwise.  The coordinatewise upper bounds give
\[
 M(\eta)\le\sum_{g=2}^{T+1}s_g(12T-8g+6)
 \le10T-8=\overline m_*.
\]
Since \(U_0(\eta)\le M(\eta)\), the low-intensity restriction yields
\[
 P_{\eta,\kappa}(X\in Z_{\mathcal U})
 =e^{-\kappa U_0(\eta)}
 \ge e^{-\kappa\overline m_*}
 \ge1-\alpha.
\]
Thus the policy is uniformly honest, and its risk is \(Le^{-\kappa U_0(\eta)}\).  Equations (17)--(18) show that its nuisance supremum is \(Le^{-\kappa u_*}\), which with (20) proves
\[
 D_{\mathcal U}(\kappa)=Le^{-\kappa u_*}.
\tag{21}
\]

The extremal formulas give
\[
 m_*-u_*=\frac{5T+11}{32}-\frac{T+7}{16}
 =\frac{3(T-1)}{32}>0.
\]
Therefore, for every \(\kappa>0\) in the stated regime,
\[
 D_{\mathcal U}(\kappa)=Le^{-\kappa u_*}
 >Le^{-\kappa m_*}=D_{\mathrm{det}}(\kappa).
\]
Finally, (17) is minimized uniquely in \(A\) and in every free time coordinate \(b_t\), because each appears with a strictly positive coefficient; \(b_1=1\) remains fixed by normalization.  Equation (18) makes \(s^*\) the unique least-favorable share, while all treated multipliers remain free.  Indeed, at any such minimizing configuration the forced event in (19) already contributes the value in (21); any optimal restricted policy can add no positive expected length off that event.  Hence precisely these lower-exposure configurations, with arbitrary admissible treated multipliers, are least favorable.
