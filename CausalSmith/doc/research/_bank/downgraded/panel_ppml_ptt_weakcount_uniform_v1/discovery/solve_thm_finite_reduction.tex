%%% FIELD proof-finite-reduction
Fix \(\kappa\in\mathcal K\).  There are \(|\mathcal G|T=T^2\) count cells.  The overlap and box assumptions imply \(s_g\le 1\), \(A_g\le2\), \(b_t\le2\), and the observed multiplier is at most \(3\).  Consequently
\[
  0<w_{gt}\le 12,
  \qquad
  0<\kappa w_{gt}\le12\kappa_+
  \quad(g\in\mathcal G,\ t\le T).
\]
If \(0\le\lambda\le\mu\), a \(\operatorname{Pois}(\mu)\) variable can be written as the sum of independent \(\operatorname{Pois}(\lambda)\) and \(\operatorname{Pois}(\mu-\lambda)\) variables.  Thus its upper tail is nondecreasing in its mean.  The exact product-Poisson assumption and the union bound therefore give, uniformly in the nuisance parameter and in \(\kappa\in\mathcal K\),
\[
 \begin{split}
 P_{\eta,\kappa}(\mathcal B_K^c)
 &\le \sum_{g,t}P_{\eta,\kappa}(X_{gt}>K)\\
 &\le T^2\Pr\{\operatorname{Pois}(12\kappa_+)>K\}
 \le \tau_K .
 \end{split}
\]
The last inequality also covers a union bound larger than one because \(\tau_K\) is truncated at one.

Let \(C\) be any honest deterministic policy at this \(\kappa\), and define
\[
 C^K(s,x)=
 \begin{cases}
 C(s,x),&x\in\mathcal B_K,\\
 \Theta,&x\notin\mathcal B_K.
 \end{cases}
\]
Because \(C^K(s,x)\supseteq C(s,x)\), its coverage is no smaller.  Since every reported interval is contained in \(\Theta\) and hence has length between zero and \(L\),
\[
 \mathbb E_{\eta,\kappa}\ell(C^K(s,X))
 \le
 \mathbb E_{\eta,\kappa}\ell(C(s,X))
   +L P_{\eta,\kappa}(\mathcal B_K^c)
 \le
 \mathbb E_{\eta,\kappa}\ell(C(s,X))+L\tau_K .
\]

For an in-box action \(C^K(s,x)=[a,c]\), let
\[
 a_h=\max\{q\in\mathcal G_h:q\le a\},
 \qquad
 c_h=\min\{q\in\mathcal G_h:q\ge c\}.
\]
These quantities exist because the grid contains both endpoints of \(\Theta\).  The maximum-spacing condition yields
\[
 0\le a-a_h\le h,
 \qquad
 0\le c_h-c\le h.
\]
Define \(C^{K,h}(s,x)=[a_h,c_h]\) in the box and retain \(\Theta\) outside it.  Then
\[
 C^{K,h}(s,x)\supseteq C^K(s,x),
 \qquad
 \ell(C^{K,h}(s,x))\le\ell(C^K(s,x))+2h.
\]
The rounding maps are finite-valued Borel maps, so \(C^{K,h}\) is an admissible Borel policy.  It preserves honesty and satisfies
\[
 \sup_\eta\mathbb E_{\eta,\kappa}\ell(C^{K,h}(s,X))
 \le
 \sup_\eta\mathbb E_{\eta,\kappa}\ell(C(s,X))+L\tau_K+2h.
\]
Write \(d_{K,h}(\kappa)\) for the restricted value at fixed \(\kappa\).  Restricting the action class can only increase an infimum, whereas applying the preceding construction to a policy within \(\delta>0\) of \(D_{\mathrm{det}}(\kappa)\) gives
\[
 D_{\mathrm{det}}(\kappa)
 \le d_{K,h}(\kappa)
 \le D_{\mathrm{det}}(\kappa)+\delta+L\tau_K+2h.
\]
Letting \(\delta\downarrow0\) and taking the supremum over \(\kappa\in\mathcal K\) proves
\[
 \overline D_{\mathrm{det}}(\mathcal K)
 \le D_{K,h}(\mathcal K)
 \le \overline D_{\mathrm{det}}(\mathcal K)+L\tau_K+2h.
\]

It remains to prove the finite-witness assertion.  Fix an observed share \(s\) and an intensity \(\kappa\).  The nuisance set
\[
 \mathcal N_s=[1/2,2]^{\mathcal G}
 \times\bigl(\{1\}\times[1/2,2]^{T-1}\bigr)
 \times[1/2,3]^{\mathcal D}
\]
is compact.  Moreover, because the \((2,t)\) cells with \(2\le t\le T\) are treated,
\[
 B(\eta)\ge \sum_{t=2}^T
 \frac1{2T}\frac12\frac12
 =\frac{T-1}{8T}>0.
\]
Thus \(\theta=H/B-1\) is continuous on \(\mathcal N_s\).  Let \(\mathcal J_h\) be the finite set of grid intervals and let
\[
 \mathcal A=\mathcal J_h^{\mathcal B_K}
\]
be the finite set of complete in-box action profiles.  For \(a\in\mathcal A\), denote its coverage and expected length at nuisance value \(\nu=(A,b,v)\) by \(q_a(\nu)\) and \(r_a(\nu)\), respectively, with \(\Theta\) used outside the box.

Every finite-box Poisson probability is continuous in \(\nu\).  The indicator of the closed condition \(\theta(\nu)\in I\) is upper semicontinuous, so \(q_a\) is upper semicontinuous.  The function \(r_a\) is a finite linear combination of continuous probabilities, plus the continuous out-of-box probability, and is therefore continuous.  If \(a\) is dishonest, by definition there is \(\nu_a^{\mathrm{bad}}\in\mathcal N_s\) such that
\[
 q_a(\nu_a^{\mathrm{bad}})<1-\alpha.
\]
If \(a\) is honest, compactness and continuity give \(\nu_a^{\mathrm{risk}}\in\mathcal N_s\) such that
\[
 r_a(\nu_a^{\mathrm{risk}})=\max_{\nu\in\mathcal N_s}r_a(\nu).
\]
Let \(W\) be the union of these selected points over the finite set \(\mathcal A\).  In the program retaining only nuisance constraints indexed by \(W\), every dishonest profile is still excluded by its own strict coverage witness, every honest profile remains feasible, and for every honest profile
\[
 \max_{\nu\in W}r_a(\nu)
 =\max_{\nu\in\mathcal N_s}r_a(\nu),
\]
because \(W\subseteq\mathcal N_s\) and \(W\) contains \(\nu_a^{\mathrm{risk}}\).  Hence the finite-witness integer optimum equals the continuum-nuisance integer optimum exactly.

Explicitly, introduce binary variables \(z_{xI}\) for \(x\in\mathcal B_K\), \(I\in\mathcal J_h\), impose
\[
 z_{xI}\in\{0,1\},
 \qquad
 \sum_{I\in\mathcal J_h}z_{xI}=1,
\]
and impose the coverage and risk inequalities at every \(\nu\in W\).  Those inequalities are linear in \(z\) and in the risk epigraph variable.  Branching on every unresolved binary variable terminates after finitely many branches and covers every integer action profile.  At each leaf, an integral incumbent or a valid dual lower bound computed with directed outward rounding yields a lower bound for that entire leaf; taking the least surviving leaf bound is therefore a lower bound for the integer optimum.  Directed rounding must enlarge feasible relaxations and underestimate their minimized objectives, which preserves the lower-bound direction.  A root relaxation does not enumerate the integer profiles and can have an integrality gap, so it cannot by itself certify equality with the integer optimum.  Finally, if \(b\) is any such lower bound for the fixed-design grid value \(d_{K,h}(s,\kappa)\), let \(d_{\mathrm{det}}(s,\kappa)\) denote the analogous unrestricted value at that observed share.  Restricting any globally honest policy to the fixed share shows \(D_{\mathrm{det}}(\kappa)\ge d_{\mathrm{det}}(s,\kappa)\), and the preceding truncation-and-rounding construction applied at that fixed share gives \(d_{K,h}(s,\kappa)\le d_{\mathrm{det}}(s,\kappa)+L\tau_K+2h\).  Hence the outward correction is
\[
 D_{\mathrm{det}}(\kappa)\ge b-L\tau_K-2h.
\]
This proves the asserted integrality-preserving lower-witness conclusion and displays its full truncation and endpoint error budget.

%%% FIELD proof-fixed-design-separation
Fix algebraic \(s\), positive algebraic \(\kappa\), an integer \(K\), and a finite algebraic endpoint grid.  Let \(a\) be a complete in-box action profile.  For \(x\in\mathcal B_K\), exact product-Poisson sampling gives
\[
 P_{\eta,\kappa}(X=x)
 =\exp\{-\kappa M(\eta)\}
   \frac{\kappa^{|x|}}{\prod_{g,t}x_{gt}!}
   \prod_{g,t}w_{gt}(\eta)^{x_{gt}},
 \qquad
 |x|=\sum_{g,t}x_{gt}.
\]
At fixed \(s\), each \(w_{gt}\), and hence \(M=\sum_{g,t}w_{gt}\), is a polynomial in \((A,b,v)\).  The factor following the exponential is also a polynomial with algebraic coefficients.

The denominator of the target is strictly positive.  Indeed, as in the finite-reduction proof,
\[
 B(\eta)\ge\frac{T-1}{8T}>0.
\]
Consequently, for algebraic endpoints \(u\le v\), the target-membership condition
\[
 u\le\theta(\eta)\le v
\]
is equivalent to the pair of polynomial inequalities
\[
 (1+u)B(\eta)\le H(\eta),
 \qquad
 H(\eta)\le(1+v)B(\eta).
\]
The nuisance box, its boundary faces, and all target-membership cells are therefore semialgebraic over the real algebraic numbers.

Because the policy reports \(\Theta\) outside \(\mathcal B_K\), failure can occur only in the box.  On a fixed target-membership stratum the set of failing tables is fixed, and hence
\[
 \begin{split}
 1-\operatorname{Coverage}_a(\eta)
 &=\exp\{-\kappa M(\eta)\}p_a^{\mathrm{fail}}(\eta),\\
 p_a^{\mathrm{fail}}(\eta)
 &=\sum_{\substack{x\in\mathcal B_K:\
             \theta(\eta)\notin a(x)}}
   \frac{\kappa^{|x|}}{\prod_{g,t}x_{gt}!}
   \prod_{g,t}w_{gt}(\eta)^{x_{gt}}.
 \end{split}
\]
Here the index set in the sum is constant on the stratum, so \(p_a^{\mathrm{fail}}\) is a polynomial.  Similarly, because the saved length \(L-\ell(\Theta)\) is zero outside the box,
\[
 \begin{split}
 L-\operatorname{Risk}_a(\eta)
 &=\exp\{-\kappa M(\eta)\}p_a^{\mathrm{save}}(\eta),\\
 p_a^{\mathrm{save}}(\eta)
 &=\sum_{x\in\mathcal B_K}
   \bigl[L-\ell(a(x))\bigr]
   \frac{\kappa^{|x|}}{\prod_{g,t}x_{gt}!}
   \prod_{g,t}w_{gt}(\eta)^{x_{gt}}.
 \end{split}
\]
This proves the claimed exponential--polynomial representation.  It also shows why the frozen wording for total coverage was incorrect: total coverage is one minus such a surface, not such a surface itself.

Apply the cited semialgebraic stratification result to the finite family of nuisance faces and target-membership sets.  On any resulting smooth stratum \(S\), write either relevant surface as
\[
 F(\eta)=\exp\{-\kappa M(\eta)\}p_a(\eta).
\]
For every tangent vector \(u\in T_\eta S\), the chain and product rules give
\[
 dF_\eta(u)
 =\exp\{-\kappa M(\eta)\}
   \bigl(dp_{a,\eta}(u)-\kappa p_a(\eta)dM_\eta(u)\bigr).
\]
The exponential factor is strictly positive.  Therefore, at every local extremum relative to \(S\),
\[
 \bigl(dp_a-\kappa p_a\,dM\bigr)_\eta(u)=0
 \quad\text{for all }u\in T_\eta S.
\]
This is precisely the stated tangential critical equation; it is vacuous, and hence valid, on zero-dimensional strata.

The critical locus on a stratum is semialgebraic: in local polynomial defining equations, tangential annihilation is equivalent to the existence of Lagrange multipliers, and projection preserves semialgebraicity.  There are finitely many strata, and the cited finite-component result gives finitely many connected critical components.  Since the nuisance domain is compact and \(F\) is continuous, each global extremum is attained.  Its stratum-wise tangential derivative vanishes, so an extremizer belongs to one of those critical components.  On a connected critical component, the cited piecewise continuously differentiable semialgebraic path property joins any two points.  Along every differentiable piece the preceding display makes the derivative of \(F\) zero.  The fundamental theorem of calculus then shows that \(F\) is constant on the component.

All critical loci are defined over the real algebraic numbers.  We next derive the existence of an algebraic representative.  In one dimension, the signs of finitely many polynomials with real-algebraic coefficients are constant between their finitely many real-algebraic roots.  Hence every nonempty one-dimensional semialgebraic set defined over the real algebraic numbers contains either one of those roots or a rational point in one of the intervening intervals.  In higher dimension, project onto the first coordinate, use the cited projection theorem and the one-dimensional argument to choose an algebraic first coordinate, then apply the same argument recursively to the nonempty fiber.  This induction gives an algebraic point \(\eta_C\) in every nonempty critical component.  At that representative, \(M(\eta_C)\) and \(p_a(\eta_C)\) are algebraic.  Isolating intervals for algebraic numbers, rational arithmetic with directed rounding, and the power series
\[
 \exp(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{z^j}{j!}+R_{n+1}(z),
 \qquad
 |R_{n+1}(z)|\le
 \exp(|z|)\frac{|z|^{n+1}}{(n+1)!},
\]
produce nested rational outward enclosures whose widths tend to zero for every component value \(\exp\{-\kappa M(\eta_C)\}p_a(\eta_C)\).  There are only finitely many such values.  If a rational threshold is strictly separated from every relevant extremal value, the minimum separation is positive, so sufficiently refined outward intervals decide the comparison after finitely many refinements.  Applying the affine transformations \(q\mapsto1-q\) for coverage and \(q\mapsto L-q\) for risk preserves this conclusion.  Equality contacts are deliberately not claimed decidable.  This proves the narrowed lemma.

For fixed \(K\) and \(h\), let \(\mathcal A\) be the finite set of complete endpoint-grid profiles and, for \(a\in\mathcal A\), define
\[
 \mathcal R_a(r)=\left\{(s,\kappa)\in\mathcal S_T\times\mathcal K:
 \inf_{\nu}q_a(s,\kappa,\nu)\ge1-\alpha,
 \quad
 \sup_{\nu}r_a(s,\kappa,\nu)\le r
 \right\}.
\]
Upper semicontinuity of coverage, continuity of risk, and compactness of the nuisance box imply that every \(\mathcal R_a(r)\) is closed.  A pointwise endpoint-grid policy of risk at most \(r\) exists exactly when
\[
 \mathcal S_T\times\mathcal K=\bigcup_{a\in\mathcal A}\mathcal R_a(r),
\]
and choosing the first valid profile gives a Borel selector.  At each fixed algebraic \((s,\kappa)\), strict coverage and risk comparisons reduce to the finitely many exponential--polynomial critical components proved above, while the finite-profile argument supplies an exact finite nuisance witness set for the integer optimum.

For the mandatory early-kill values, the part of the outward budget forced solely by count truncation and endpoint rounding is already strictly below the requested tolerance:
\[
 L\tau_2+2h
 =\frac52\,9\left[1-e^{-3/25}
 \left(1+\frac3{25}+\frac12\left(\frac3{25}\right)^2\right)\right]
 +\frac1{50}
 <0.026<\frac1{20}.
\]
Thus more than \(0.024\) of the tolerance remains for the primal--integer gap and outward enclosures.  What is not proved is that the closed cover above has a finite rational-polyhedral refinement carrying uniformly valid profiles at all zero-margin contacts, nor has a primal table and complete integer branch-and-bound witness for this early-kill instance been constructed.
