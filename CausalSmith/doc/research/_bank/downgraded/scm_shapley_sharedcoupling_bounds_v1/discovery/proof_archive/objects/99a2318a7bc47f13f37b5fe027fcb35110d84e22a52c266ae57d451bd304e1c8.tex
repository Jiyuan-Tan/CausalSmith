In \(\mathfrak G_\varepsilon\), every factual probability is \(p_t=1/2\).  For a binary nonfactual row whose observed marginal is also \((1/2,1/2)\), \(\text{def:posterior-row-fiber}\) imposes only \(0\le q(1)\le1\).  Since the fiber is a product, \(u,w,z,v\) vary independently over \([0,1]\), and the factual rows equal \(\delta_0\).

The first-stage reward is zero and the second-stage reward is the terminal next state.  Under the empty coalition, both factual rows are queried, so \(g_\varnothing=0\).  Under \(\{1\}\), baseline action one is selected at time one with probability \(\varepsilon\); it reaches state one with probability \(u\), after which the retained policy uses action zero and succeeds with probability \(z\).  Hence \(g_{\{1\}}=\varepsilon uz\).  Under \(\{2\}\), time one remains factual and time two takes action one at state zero, so \(g_{\{2\}}=w\).  Finally, under \(\{1,2\}\), the time-one baseline reaches state one with probability \(\varepsilon u\); the time-two baseline success probabilities are \(w\) from state zero and \(v\) from state one.  Therefore
\[
 g_{\{1,2\}}=(1-\varepsilon u)w+\varepsilon uv.
\]
For \(H=2\), both Shapley weights are \(1/2\).  Substitution in \(\text{def:shapley-coordinate}\) gives
\[
 \Phi_1=\frac12(g_\varnothing-g_{\{1\}})
       +\frac12(g_{\{2\}}-g_{\{1,2\}})
 =\frac{\varepsilon u}{2}(w-z-v).
\]
Because \(u,w,z,v\in[0,1]\), its maximum is \(\varepsilon/2\), attained at \((1,1,0,0)\), and its minimum is \(-\varepsilon\), attained at \((1,0,1,1)\).  The equality with \([L_1,U_1]\) and SCM attainment follow from \(\text{thm:sharp-shared-interval}\).

If each distinct coalition return is instead assigned its own posterior kernel, the preceding formulas show independently that
\[
 g_\varnothing\in\{0\},\qquad
 g_{\{1\}}\in[0,\varepsilon],\qquad
 g_{\{2\}}\in[0,1],\qquad
 g_{\{1,2\}}\in[0,1].
\]
Interval arithmetic with their signs in the Shapley formula therefore gives
\[
 \mathcal I_1^{\mathrm{ind}}
 =\left[-\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2},\frac12\right].
\]
Its excess below \(-\varepsilon\) and above \(\varepsilon/2\) is in each case \((1-\varepsilon)/2>0\).

We finally justify openness rather than infer it from the single tuple.  Near the tuple at \(\varepsilon=1/4\), all factual transition cells remain bounded away from zero.  For one alternate row, truncate a feasible old row at the perturbed upper caps and redistribute the removed mass into the unused perturbed cap.  The removed mass is bounded by the sum of the marginal-row perturbation and the factual-cell perturbation, divided by a common positive lower bound on the two factual cells.  Reversing old and new kernels gives the other Hausdorff direction.  The finite product fibers therefore converge in Hausdorff distance.  Markov returns are finite polynomials in posterior rows, rewards, transition marginals, and policy probabilities, hence are uniformly continuous on the surrounding compact product.  Matching feasible points in both directions proves continuity of each shared extremum.  The independent endpoints are finite signed sums of extrema of the same continuous coalition-return polynomials and are continuous by the identical argument.  At \(\varepsilon=1/4\), both slack functions equal \(3/8\); continuity supplies a relative neighborhood on which they remain positive.  The relative interior of every finite probability simplex is dense, so this neighborhood contains policy tuples with all action probabilities interior, while factual transition cells can remain positive.  This proves the final neighborhood claim.
