We give the circuit and then prove the mixed-elimination invariant.  A nonfactual row factor of \(\mathcal B(K,\tau)\) has the rational description
\[
 q(y)\ge0,\qquad \sum_yq(y)=1,
 \qquad q(y)\le \frac{K_t(y\mid x)}{p_t}.
\]
Its vertices are rational and can be enumerated by making enough of these inequalities tight; equivalently, all but at most one coordinate are zero or at their upper cap, and the last coordinate is fixed by the sum constraint.  This list can be produced output-sensitively.  Start from the vertex obtained by filling coordinates successively to their caps until the remaining mass fits in one coordinate.  At any vertex every feasible edge is an exchange direction \(e_y-e_{y'}\); for each ordered coordinate pair, move to the first endpoint at which the moving coordinates hit the appropriate bounds and retain the endpoints that satisfy the vertex criterion.  Testing all pairs lists its adjacent vertices in \(O(|\mathcal S|^2)\) rational operations.  To see connectivity directly, fix a current vertex \(v\).  Every feasible zero-sum direction is a nonnegative sum of transfers from coordinates that can decrease to coordinates that can increase.  If \(v\) has a unique coordinate strictly between its bounds, transfers not involving that coordinate split into two locally feasible transfers through it; hence the feasible tangent cone is generated by incident exchange-edge directions.  If an affine functional is chosen to have a given target vertex \(v^*\) as its unique maximizer, \(v^*-v\) is a feasible direction from any \(v\ne v^*\), so at least one incident edge direction increases the functional.  Following improving edges terminates at \(v^*\) because the vertex set is finite.  Graph traversal with duplicate suppression therefore enumerates all row vertices in \(O(|\mathcal S|^2d)\) operations per row.  The factual factor is the singleton \(\delta_{y_t}\).  By the separate-affinity argument in \(\text{thm:sharp-shared-interval}\), each maximum of \(F_i^\sigma=C_\nu+\sigma\Phi_i/R\) has a rowwise-vertex optimizer.  It is therefore exact to let \(Z_{t,x}\) range over the finite vertex list and to write \(q^{Z}_{t,x}\) for its selected row.

Exactness for the constant polynomial in \(\text{prop:exact-coalition-compression}\) gives \(\sum_j\nu_j=1\), hence \(C_\nu\ge1\).  Put
\[
 \rho_{jb}=\frac{|a_{jb}^\sigma|}{2C_\nu},
 \qquad
 \eta_{jb}=\begin{cases}+,&a_{jb}^\sigma\ge0,\\-,&a_{jb}^\sigma<0.\end{cases}
\]
Then \(\sum_{j,b}\rho_{jb}=1\).  Introduce one global chance selector \(W=(J,B,T)\) with probability \(\rho_{jb}/H\) at \((j,b,t)\).  Conditional on \((J,B)=(j,b)\), use the policy \((1-\zeta_j)\alpha_t^0+\zeta_j\alpha_t^1\) at \(t\ne i\), and use \(\alpha_i^b\) at \(t=i\).  The initial-state factor is \(\mathbf 1\{S_1=s_1\}\).  For every \((t,x)\), include the nonnegative transition factor
\[
 \psi_{t,x}(S_t,A_t,S_{t+1},Z_{t,x})
 =\begin{cases}
 q^{Z}_{t,x}(S_{t+1}),&(S_t,A_t)=x,\\
 1,&(S_t,A_t)\ne x.
 \end{cases}
\]
The product over \(x\) is exactly the selected transition probability for the single row visited at time \(t\).  Finally, for every \(t\), include the local nonnegative reward-selector factor
\[
 \chi_t(W,S_t,A_t,S_{t+1})
 =\begin{cases}
 Hh_t^{\eta_{JB}}((S_t,A_t),S_{t+1}),&T=t,\\
 1,&T\ne t.
 \end{cases}
\]
Their product is \(Hh_T^{\eta_{JB}}((S_T,A_T),S_{T+1})\).
All displayed factors are rational and nonnegative.  Summing the product over \(W\), the actions, and the states, while holding one joint selector assignment \(Z=z\) fixed, gives a circuit value \(T_\sigma(z)\) satisfying
\[
\begin{aligned}
 2C_\nu T_\sigma(z)
 &=\sum_{j,b}|a_{jb}^\sigma|
   \sum_{t=1}^H
   \mathbb E_{q^z}\!left[h_t^{\eta_{jb}}((S_t,A_t),S_{t+1})
       \mid J=j,B=b\right]\\
 &=F_i^\sigma(q^z).
\end{aligned}
\]
The last equality uses the pathwise complement identity in \(\text{prop:affine-nonnegative-lift}\).  This factorization has one occurrence of each variable \(Z_{t,x}\); its factors may mention that variable in many selector states, but there is no separately optimizable copy.  It is the required \(\mathfrak F_i^\sigma\), with the final deterministic scale \(2C_\nu\).

We next prove that any legal order preserves the quantifier value
\[
 \max_z\sum_{\text{all chance variables}}\mathfrak F_i^\sigma(z,\text{chance}).
\]
Maintain the ordinary bucket-elimination invariant: the product of the current factors, followed by the still-unperformed sums and maxima, equals the corresponding partially eliminated expression.  Summing a chance variable is exact by distributivity over the factors in its bucket.  Suppose a decision variable \(Z\) is next.  Legality says that no uneliminated chance variable occurs in the scope of the current bucket product \(A(Z,Y)\).  The remaining factor product can thus be written \(A(Z,Y)B(X,Y)\), where \(X\) collects the uneliminated chance variables and \(B\) does not involve \(Z\).  Since all factors are nonnegative,
\[
 \max_Z\{A(Z,Y)B(X,Y)\}=B(X,Y)\max_ZA(Z,Y).
\]
Thus this maximization commutes past every still-uneliminated chance sum on which its bucket does not depend.  Repeating this exchange transforms the legal order into the canonical order that sums all chance variables before jointly maximizing all decisions, without changing the value.  Induction over buckets proves the invariant and hence returns \(\max_zF_i^\sigma(q^z)=V_\sigma\).

At most \(w_{\mathrm c}(\preceq)+1\) nonglobal live variables occur in any bucket table.  Each has alphabet at most \(d\), while the sole global selector has \(2H^2\) states.  Forming and eliminating one bucket therefore costs \(O(H^2d^{w_{\mathrm c}(\preceq)+1})\) rational operations, and at most \(N\) variables or factors are processed.  The vertex enumeration just described is absorbed by this bound: \(d\ge|\mathcal S|\), there are at most \(N\) row factors, and every nonconstant row selector occurs with the action and next-state variables in an initial transition-factor scope, so \(w_{\mathrm c}+1\ge3\); singleton cases take constant work.  This proves the stated \(O(H^2N d^{w_{\mathrm c}(\preceq)+1})\) bound; buckets not containing \(W\) only reduce the cost.

Let \(B_0\) be the total bit length of the original rational arrays and quadrature data.  Every vertex coordinate is obtained from upper caps by rational division and one finite sum or difference, so its individual bit length is polynomial in \(B_0\).  Every table entry during elimination is a sum of products of disjoint original factors: bucket multiplication never duplicates a factor, and eliminating a decision selects rather than sums over its values.  For any fixed remaining decision context, a product therefore has denominator bit length polynomial in \(B_0+N\), and summing at most \((2H^2)d^N\) chance assignments adds at most \(O(N\log d+\log H)\) numerator bits after passage to a common denominator.  A maximization only compares and selects existing rationals.  Thus reduced intermediate rationals have polynomial bit length in \(B_0+N+\log d+\log H\); rounding an exact output to requested precision \(P\) adds \(O(P)\) work and bits.

At a maximization bucket, store an optimizing value of \(Z\) as a function of the later decision variables in its scope, breaking ties by a fixed order.  Reverse-order evaluation of these records is \(\mathsf{BP}_\sigma\)-backtracking and yields one joint selector assignment, independent of \(W\) and every path realization.  The compact construction in \(\text{lem:posterior-row-projection}\) glues the selected rows into a compatible SCM.  The affine identities in \(\text{prop:affine-nonnegative-lift}\) then show that the \(+1\) assignment attains \(U_i=R(V_{+1}-C_\nu)\) and the \(-1\) assignment attains \(L_i=R(C_\nu-V_{-1})\).
