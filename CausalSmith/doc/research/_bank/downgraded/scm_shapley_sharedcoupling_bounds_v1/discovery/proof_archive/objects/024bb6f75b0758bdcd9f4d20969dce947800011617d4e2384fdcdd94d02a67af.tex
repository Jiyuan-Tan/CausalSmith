The array with factual rows \(q_{t,x_t}=\delta_{y_t}\) and nonfactual rows \(q_{t,x}=K_t(\cdot\mid x)\) is feasible, because \(0<p_t\le1\) and
\[
 p_tq_{t,x}(y)=p_tK_t(y\mid x)\le K_t(y\mid x).
\]
Thus \(\mathcal B(K,\tau)\ne\varnothing\).  Every nonfactual factor is a simplex intersected with finitely many closed half-spaces, and every factual factor is a singleton.  Finiteness of \(\mathcal S\), \(\mathcal X\), and \([H]\) therefore makes \(\mathcal B(K,\tau)\) a compact convex polytope.

For a fixed coalition \(C\), expansion over the finite state-action paths gives
\[
 g_C(q)=\sum_{s_2,\ldots,s_{H+1}}\sum_{a_1,\ldots,a_H}
 \left\{\prod_{t=1}^H\alpha_t^C(a_t\mid s_t)
 q_{t,(s_t,a_t)}(s_{t+1})\right\}
 \left\{\sum_{t=1}^Hr_t((s_t,a_t),s_{t+1})\right\}. \tag{1}
\]
The external coalition coins in \(\texttt{ass:factual-policy-exogeneity}\) justify the policy factors in (1), and the deterministic rewards justify the last factor.  Formula (1) is a finite polynomial in \(q\), and a path uses at most one row at any fixed time; hence it is affine in each row separately.  By \(\texttt{def:shapley-coordinate}\), \(\Phi_i\) is also continuous and separately affine.

The two directions of \(\texttt{lem:posterior-row-projection}\), whose likelihood cancellation uses the fixed initial state and external policy coins, give
\[
 \Theta_i(K,\tau)=\{\Phi_i(q):q\in\mathcal B(K,\tau)\}. \tag{2}
\]
Compactness and continuity give a minimizer \(q^-\) and maximizer \(q^+\), whose values are \(L_i(K,\tau)\) and \(U_i(K,\tau)\) by \(\texttt{def:shared-endpoints}\).  For \(s\in[0,1]\), convexity gives \(q(s)=(1-s)q^-+sq^+\in\mathcal B(K,\tau)\), and \(s\mapsto\Phi_i(q(s))\) is continuous.  The intermediate-value property therefore fills every value between the endpoint values.  Conversely no value in (2) can lie outside the defining extrema.  Hence
\[
 \Theta_i(K,\tau)=[L_i(K,\tau),U_i(K,\tau)]. \tag{3}
\]
Applying the converse gluing in \(\texttt{lem:posterior-row-projection}\) to \(q^-\), \(q^+\), and any interpolating \(q(s)\) gives models in the repaired \(\mathfrak M(K)\); their fixed initial state and factual and coalition action coins are supplied exactly as in that lemma.  Thus (3) is a causal identified-set equality and both endpoints are attained by one compatible transition SCM shared across coalition worlds.

For the row-vertex assertion, start with either endpoint optimizer and one row factor \(P\).  With all other rows fixed, separate affineness gives an affine objective \(f\) on \(P\).  If an optimizing row lies in the relative interior of a nontrivial segment \([v_0,v_1]\subseteq P\), then its value is a strict convex combination of \(f(v_0)\) and \(f(v_1)\).  Optimality forces both endpoint values to equal the optimum.  Replace the row by an endpoint lying in a proper face and repeat.  The face dimension strictly decreases, so after finitely many replacements the row is a vertex without changing the global objective.  Repeating over the finitely many rows produces an optimizer all of whose rows are vertices; factual singleton rows already are vertices.

Finally, the gluing proof is a compact sampler.  It stores \(K_t(\cdot\mid x_t)\), the selected \(q_{t,x}\), and, when \(p_t<1\), the residual rows in equation (7) of that proof.  It samples the factual coordinate first and the remaining coordinates conditionally from the posterior or residual rows.  The storage is \(O(H|\mathcal X||\mathcal S|)\) probabilities, and it does not list the generally exponential mass table on \(\mathcal S^{\mathcal X}\).  Adding the independent finite action-coin samplers requires only the displayed policy tables and does not change this response-table storage bound.
