Write the realized product region as
\(\mathcal C_{1-\alpha}(\widehat K)=\prod_{t,x}\mathcal C_{t,x}\),
where the displayed coordinate-box construction gives
\[
 \mathcal C_{t,x}
 =\left\{k\in\Delta(\mathcal S):
       \ell_{t,x,y}\le k(y)\le u_{t,x,y}
       \text{ for every }y\in\mathcal S\right\}.
\]
These polytopes are nonempty: if \(n_{t,x}>0\), the empirical row
\(\widehat K_t(\cdot\mid x)\) belongs to \(\mathcal C_{t,x}\), and if
\(n_{t,x}=0\), then \(\mathcal C_{t,x}=\Delta(\mathcal S)\).  The fixed-row
multinomial assumption specifies the sampling law.  The probability argument
below uses exactly the theorem's additional premise that this product has
simultaneous coverage at least \(1-\alpha\); it does not require independence
between different sampled rows.

First we prove the projected-polytope characterization in the corrected
\(\texttt{def:confidence-fiber-endpoints}\).  Fix \(t\), and abbreviate
\(p^-=p_t^-\) and \(p^+=p_t^+\).  Minimizing or maximizing the
\(y_t\)-coordinate over a box intersected with the simplex gives
\[
 p^-=\max\!\left\{\ell_{t,x_t,y_t},
        1-\sum_{y\ne y_t}u_{t,x_t,y}\right\},
 \qquad
 p^+=\min\!\left\{u_{t,x_t,y_t},
        1-\sum_{y\ne y_t}\ell_{t,x_t,y}\right\}.
\]
Indeed, the first lower bound follows from the lower bound on the
\(y_t\)-cell and from
\(k(y_t)=1-\sum_{y\ne y_t}k(y)\); the larger of the two bounds is attainable
because the row box is nonempty.  The maximum identity is proved by the same
argument with lower and upper bounds interchanged.  Thus both numbers are
obtained by finite arithmetic from \(\widehat K\), the counts, and
\(\alpha\).

Suppose first that \(p^->0\).  If a posterior row \(q_x\) belongs to
\(\mathcal B(K',\tau)\) for some \(K'\in\mathcal C_{1-\alpha}(\widehat K)\),
then, with \(p=K'_t(y_t\mid x_t)\), the definition of
\(\mathcal B(K',\tau)\) gives
\[
 p^-q_x(y)\le p q_x(y)\le K'_t(y\mid x)
 \quad\text{for every }y\in\mathcal S,
\]
because \(p\ge p^-\).  Taking \(k_x=K'_t(\cdot\mid x)\) proves
\(q_x\in\mathcal Q_{t,x}^{\alpha}\).  Conversely, choose the single factual
row \(k_{x_t}^-\in\mathcal C_{t,x_t}\) attaining \(p^-\).  For every
\(x\ne x_t\), choose a witness \(k_x\in\mathcal C_{t,x}\) from the extended
constraints defining \(\mathcal Q_{t,x}^{\alpha}\).  Product structure of
the confidence region combines these choices into one transition kernel
\(K'\), and the same scalar \(p^-\) then appears in every constraint
\[
 p^-q_x(y)\le K'_t(y\mid x).
\]
Hence the time-\(t\) union of posterior-row fibers is exactly the product of
the local projected polytopes.  This step is where optimizing every alternate
row against one common factual confidence probability is enforced.

Now suppose \(p^-=0<p^+\).  For an alternate row \(x\), the largest attainable
value of coordinate \(y\) in \(\mathcal C_{t,x}\) is
\[
 \min\!\left\{u_{t,x,y},1-\sum_{z\ne y}\ell_{t,x,z}\right\}.
\]
Consequently this value is positive exactly when \(y\in J_{t,x}\).  If
\(q_x\) is feasible at a positive factual value \(p\), then
\(p q_x(y)\le k_x(y)\) for some \(k_x\in\mathcal C_{t,x}\).  When
\(y\notin J_{t,x}\), every such \(k_x(y)\) is zero, so positivity of \(p\)
forces \(q_x(y)=0\).  Every limit row is therefore in \(\Delta(J_{t,x})\).

For the reverse inclusion, for each \(y\in J_{t,x}\) choose
\(k_x^y\in\mathcal C_{t,x}\) with \(k_x^y(y)>0\).  Since \(J_{t,x}\) is
finite and \(\mathcal C_{t,x}\) is convex, their uniform average
\(k_x^*\) lies in \(\mathcal C_{t,x}\) and is strictly positive on all of
\(J_{t,x}\).  Given \(q_x\in\Delta(J_{t,x})\), there is therefore a number
\(c_x>0\) such that
\[
 p q_x(y)\le k_x^*(y)
 \quad\text{for every }y\in\mathcal S
 \quad\text{whenever }0<p\le c_x.
\]
There are finitely many alternate rows, so one positive \(p\) works for all
of them.  The projection of the convex factual-row polytope on its
\(y_t\)-coordinate is the interval \([0,p^+]\); hence every sufficiently
small such \(p\) is attained by one factual row.  Letting \(p\downarrow0\)
proves that the closure of the whole time block is
\[
 \{q_{t,x_t}=\delta_{y_t}\}
 \times\prod_{x\ne x_t}\Delta(J_{t,x}).
\]
The confidence region is also a product over times.  Thus the preceding
positive-minimum and zero-minimum arguments can be combined independently at
each time, and they prove
\[
 \overline{\bigcup_{K'\in\mathcal C_{1-\alpha}(\widehat K):\,
             K'_t(y_t\mid x_t)>0\ \forall t}
             \mathcal B(K',\tau)}
 =\overline{\mathcal Q}_{\alpha}(\widehat K,\tau)
\]
whenever \(p_t^+>0\) for every \(t\).  No division by a zero factual
probability has been used.  If some \(p_t^+=0\), the positive-evidence union
is empty at that time, which is precisely the case sent to the logical
fallback in the corrected definition.

That fallback contains every logically possible coordinate.  By the definition
of \(D\), any two complete-return expectations differ in absolute value by at
most \(D\).  Moreover, grouping coalitions by \(k=|C|\) gives
\[
 \sum_{C\subseteq[H]\setminus\{i\}}\omega_H(C)
 =\sum_{k=0}^{H-1}\binom{H-1}{k}
   \frac{k!(H-k-1)!}{H!}
 =\sum_{k=0}^{H-1}\frac1H=1.
\]
The triangle inequality in \(\texttt{def:shapley-coordinate}\) therefore
implies \(|\Phi_i(q)|\le D\) for every posterior kernel \(q\).

For completeness, this characterization is a finite endpoint algorithm.
When \(p_t^->0\), \(\mathcal Q_{t,x}^{\alpha}\) is the projection of the
displayed bounded linear system in \((q_x,k_x)\); when \(p_t^-=0\), it is a
face of a simplex.  Hence every local factor is a nonempty compact polytope.
For a coalition \(C\), direct expansion of the finite Markov expectation is
\[
 g_C(q)=
 \sum_{s_2,\ldots,s_{H+1}}\sum_{a_1,\ldots,a_H}
 \left\{\prod_{t=1}^H
   \alpha_t^C(a_t\mid s_t)
   q_{t,(s_t,a_t)}(s_{t+1})\right\}
 \left\{\sum_{t=1}^H r_t((s_t,a_t),s_{t+1})\right\},
\]
with \(s_1\) fixed.  It follows that every \(g_C\), and hence
\(\Phi_i\), is continuous and affine in each individual row factor while
the other rows are held fixed.  A minimum or maximum over a product of
polytopes is therefore attained with every factor at a vertex: successively
replace a nonvertex row by a vertex minimizing or maximizing its current
affine section.  An exact finite implementation enumerates basic feasible
solutions of each positive-minimum extended system, projects their
\(q_x\)-coordinates, adds the point-mass vertices of the support simplexes,
and evaluates the single shared objective \(\Phi_i\) on their Cartesian
product.  Images of the extended vertices contain every vertex of each
projected polytope, so this enumeration returns the exact two endpoints and
one shared posterior-row optimizer for each endpoint.

It remains to prove coverage.  Let
\[
 \mathcal E=\{K\in\mathcal C_{1-\alpha}(\widehat K)\}.
\]
By the simultaneous-coverage premise,
\(\Pr_K(\mathcal E)\ge1-\alpha\).  Fix any positive-probability factual
trajectory.  Assumption \(\text{ass:factual-positivity}\) gives
\(K_t(y_t\mid x_t)>0\) for every \(t\).  On \(\mathcal E\), the true kernel
is consequently one of the positive-evidence kernels in the union above, so
\[
 \mathcal B(K,\tau)
 \subseteq\overline{\mathcal Q}_{\alpha}(\widehat K,\tau).
\]
Using \(\texttt{def:shared-endpoints}\), the corrected confidence-endpoint
definition, and minimization and maximization over nested feasible sets gives
\[
 L_i^{\alpha}
 \le \min_{q\in\mathcal B(K,\tau)}\Phi_i(q)
 =L_i(K,\tau)
 \le U_i(K,\tau)
 =\max_{q\in\mathcal B(K,\tau)}\Phi_i(q)
 \le U_i^{\alpha}.
\]
The event \(\mathcal E\) depends only on the transition kernel and its
simultaneous confidence region, not on \(i\), the trajectory, or the specified
policy pair.  The same event therefore yields these inequalities
simultaneously for every asserted query; no union bound over coordinates,
trajectories, or policies is needed.  Finally,
\(\texttt{thm:sharp-shared-interval}\) identifies
\([L_i(K,\tau),U_i(K,\tau)]\) with the entire compatible-SCM identified set,
so the inclusion covers the whole causal set and not merely a selected
counterfactual value.
