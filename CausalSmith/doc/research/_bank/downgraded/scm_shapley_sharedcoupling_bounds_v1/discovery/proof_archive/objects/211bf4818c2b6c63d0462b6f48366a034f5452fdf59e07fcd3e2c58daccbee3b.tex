Work with symbolic factor scopes, where eliminating a variable multiplies all factors containing it and produces a factor on the union of their scopes with that variable removed.  Constant or singleton factors have already been removed by hypothesis, so none of the stated chain incidences disappears through the permitted simplification.

Let the consecutive chance vertices be \(X_0,\ldots,X_k\) and the active row selectors be \(Z_1,\ldots,Z_k\), with a surviving scope containing \(\{X_{j-1},X_j,Z_j\}\) for every \(j\).  These names are local to this proof.  Consider the first variable among \(Z_1,\ldots,Z_k\) that a legal order attempts to maximize.  Before that step no selector of the chain minor has been eliminated.

We record the fill created by eliminating chance vertices.  For any connected block of already eliminated vertices of the chance path, multiplication of the factors meeting that block and summation over its vertices produces a factor whose symbolic scope contains every uneliminated chance endpoint adjacent to the block and every selector \(Z_j\) whose incident edge meets the block.  This follows by induction on the size of the block: it is immediate for one eliminated chance vertex, and adjoining a neighboring chance vertex multiplies the two factors sharing their boundary, unions their scopes, and removes only the newly eliminated chance variable.

Suppose some chance vertex remains when the first chain selector, say \(Z_j\), is to be maximized.  Initially \(Z_j\) is adjacent to \(X_{j-1}\) and \(X_j\).  If either remains, legality directly forbids the maximization.  If both have been eliminated, take the eliminated chance block containing them.  Unless that block is the whole path, the preceding fill invariant puts \(Z_j\) in a factor with an uneliminated chance endpoint of the block, again contradicting legality.  Hence every \(X_0,\ldots,X_k\) must be summed before the first chain selector can be maximized.

Apply the same fill invariant after the whole connected chance path has been eliminated.  Its boundary selectors are exactly \(Z_1,\ldots,Z_k\), so the induced decision graph contains a clique on all \(k\) selectors.  The bucket of the first one maximized therefore contains at least these \(k\) nonglobal variables.  By the definition of constrained width,
\[
 w_{\mathrm c}(\preceq)\ge k-1=|J|-1.
\]
For \(|J|\ge2\), this is at least \(|J|/2\), which proves the asserted \(\Omega(|J|)\) bound.  Setting \(J=[H]\) gives \(w_{\mathrm c}(\preceq)\ge H-1\), so no bounded- or logarithmic-width conclusion follows for the fully active family.
