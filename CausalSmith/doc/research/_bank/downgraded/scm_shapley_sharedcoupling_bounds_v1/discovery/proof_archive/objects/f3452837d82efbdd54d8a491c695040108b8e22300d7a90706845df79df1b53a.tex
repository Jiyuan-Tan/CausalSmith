Write \(\mathcal C_{1-\alpha}(\widehat K)=\prod_{t,x}\mathcal C_{t,x}\) as in \(\texttt{def:simultaneous-row-product-confidence-region}\).  Each row polytope is nonempty: if \(n_{t,x}>0\), its center \(\widehat K_t(\cdot\mid x)\) belongs to it, and if \(n_{t,x}=0\), it is the full simplex.

We first derive simultaneous coverage.  Fix a cell \((t,x,y)\) with \(n_{t,x}>0\).  Apply \(\texttt{lem:hoeffding-bounded-mean}\) to the independent Bernoulli indicators of observing next state \(y\) in that row.  Their common mean is \(K_t(y\mid x)\), and their average is \(\widehat K_t(y\mid x)\).  The chosen outward-rounded radius satisfies
\[
 e_{t,x}^2\ge \frac{\log(2M_0/\alpha)}{2n_{t,x}},
\]
and therefore
\[
 \Pr_K\left\{\left|\widehat K_t(y\mid x)-K_t(y\mid x)\right|>e_{t,x}\right\}
 \le 2\exp\{-2n_{t,x}e_{t,x}^2\}
 \le \frac{\alpha}{M_0}. \tag{1}
\]
For a zero-count row the true row belongs to its full-simplex confidence set surely.  There are at most \(M_0=H|\mathcal X||\mathcal S|\) positive-count cells.  The union bound applied to (1), which requires no independence between different sampled rows, yields
\[
 \inf_K\Pr_K\{K\in\mathcal C_{1-\alpha}(\widehat K)\}\ge1-\alpha. \tag{2}
\]

We next identify the projected confidence fiber.  Fix \(t\) and put
\[
 p_t^- =\min_{k\in\mathcal C_{t,x_t}}k(y_t),
 \qquad p_t^+=\max_{k\in\mathcal C_{t,x_t}}k(y_t). \tag{3}
\]
These extrema exist because the row polytope is nonempty and compact, and they are computed by two linear programs.  Suppose \(p_t^->0\).  If \(q_x\) is the nonfactual posterior row of some \(K'\in\mathcal C_{1-\alpha}(\widehat K)\), then with \(p=K'_t(y_t\mid x_t)\), \(\texttt{def:posterior-row-fiber}\) gives
\[
 p_t^-q_x(y)\le p q_x(y)\le K'_t(y\mid x), \tag{4}
\]
so \(q_x\in\mathcal Q_{t,x}\).  Conversely, select one factual row \(k_{x_t}^-\in\mathcal C_{t,x_t}\) attaining \(p_t^-\), and for each \(x\ne x_t\) select one witness \(k_x\in\mathcal C_{t,x}\) from the extended system defining \(\mathcal Q_{t,x}\).  Product structure combines them into one kernel \(K'\); the same scalar \(p_t^-\) is then used in every inequality.  Thus the union of time-\(t\) posterior fibers is exactly the product of these projected polytopes when \(p_t^->0\).

Suppose now \(p_t^-=0<p_t^+\).  If \(q_x\) is feasible at a positive factual value \(p\), then \(p q_x(y)\le k_x(y)\) for some \(k_x\in\mathcal C_{t,x}\).  If \(y\notin J_{t,x}\), every such \(k_x(y)\) is zero, and hence \(q_x(y)=0\).  Every limit row is therefore supported on \(J_{t,x}\).

Conversely, for each \(y\in J_{t,x}\) choose \(k_x^y\in\mathcal C_{t,x}\) with \(k_x^y(y)>0\).  Convexity makes their average \(k_x^*\) a confidence row strictly positive on every element of the finite set \(J_{t,x}\).  Let \(q_x\in\Delta(J_{t,x})\).  Choose any \(\bar q_x\in\Delta(J_{t,x})\) that is strictly positive on \(J_{t,x}\), and set
\[
 q_{x,n}=(1-n^{-1})q_x+n^{-1}\bar q_x. \tag{5}
\]
For each of the finitely many alternate rows, every positive coordinate of \(q_{x,n}\) is backed by a positive coordinate of \(k_x^*\).  Hence there is \(c_t>0\), common to all alternate rows, such that \(p q_{x,n}(y)\le k_x^*(y)\) whenever \(0<p\le c_t\).  Choose factual rows \(k_{x_t}^0,k_{x_t}^+\in\mathcal C_{t,x_t}\) attaining respectively zero and \(p_t^+\), and put
\[
 k_{x_t,n}=(1-\gamma_{t,n})k_{x_t}^0+\gamma_{t,n}k_{x_t}^+,
 \qquad
 \gamma_{t,n}=\min\left\{\frac1n,\frac{c_t}{2p_t^+}\right\}. \tag{6}
\]
Its factual cell \(p_{t,n}=\gamma_{t,n}p_t^+\) is positive, tends to zero, and is at most \(c_t/2\).  Equations (5)--(6), the fixed alternate witnesses \(k_x^*\), and product structure give positive-evidence kernels whose posterior rows converge to the prescribed product of support-simplex rows.  There are finitely many times; using the same index \(n\) at every zero-minimum time and the exact witnesses from (4) at every positive-minimum time is an explicit diagonal sequence.  This proves
\[
 \overline{\bigcup_{K'\in\mathcal C_{1-\alpha}(\widehat K):\,
 K'_t(y_t\mid x_t)>0\ \forall t}\mathcal B(K',\tau)}
 =\prod_{t=1}^H\left(
 \{\delta_{y_t}\}\times\prod_{x\ne x_t}\mathcal Q_{t,x}
 \right), \tag{7}
\]
with \(\mathcal Q_{t,x}\) defined by the two cases in the proposed statement.  If some \(p_t^+=0\), no confidence kernel has positive factual evidence at that time, so the union on the left of (7) is empty.

We justify the logical fallback.  Every complete return lies in \([\underline G,\overline G]\), so any difference of two expected returns lies in \([-D,D]\).  Moreover
\[
 \sum_{C\subseteq[H]\setminus\{i\}}\omega_H(C)
 =\sum_{k=0}^{H-1}{H-1\choose k}\frac{k!(H-k-1)!}{H!}=1. \tag{8}
\]
The Shapley definition and (8) give \(|\Phi_i(q)|\le D\).  Therefore \([-D,D]\) contains every logically possible coordinate when the positive-evidence confidence fiber is empty.

If every \(p_t^+>0\), continuity of \(\Phi_i\) and (7) show
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 L_i^\alpha
 &=\min_{q\in\overline{\cup_{K'}\mathcal B(K',\tau)}}\Phi_i(q),\\
 U_i^\alpha
 &=\max_{q\in\overline{\cup_{K'}\mathcal B(K',\tau)}}\Phi_i(q),
 \end{aligned} \tag{9}
\]
where the unions range over positive-evidence confidence kernels.  Indeed, \(\inf_{K'}\min_{q\in\mathcal B(K',\tau)}\Phi_i(q)\) is the infimum over the union, which equals the minimum over its compact closure; the supremum identity is identical.

Equation (9) gives the advertised finite algorithm.  For each positive-minimum block, enumerate all basic feasible solutions of the bounded extended polytope in \((q_x,k_x)\), retain the \(k_x\)-witness, project its \(q_x\)-coordinate, and deduplicate.  Every vertex of a polytope projection is the projection of a vertex of the original polytope: an affine functional uniquely exposing the projected vertex exposes a nonempty face upstairs, and any vertex of that face projects to it.  For a zero-minimum block, enumerate the point masses on \(J_{t,x}\).  The path expansion in the proof of \(\texttt{thm:sharp-shared-interval}\) shows that \(\Phi_i\) is affine in each row separately.  Successively moving each row of an optimizer to a vertex of its local factor preserves the optimum.  Hence evaluation on the Cartesian product of the enumerated row vertices is exact.  Each evaluation uses \(\texttt{prop:exact-coalition-compression}\), and the signed objective may be passed through \(\texttt{prop:affine-nonnegative-lift}\) and the factor-circuit backend of \(\texttt{thm:optimization-preserving-compiler}\), always with one selector assignment shared across all branches.  With rational outward-rounded radii, all systems and evaluations are rational.  Basic-feasible-solution enumeration followed by finite Cartesian evaluation is finite and output-sensitive; no stronger polynomial bound is claimed.

If every \(p_t^->0\), the retained witnesses and the common factual minimizers in (4) assemble one \(K'\) for the selected posterior optimizer.  Applying \(\texttt{lem:posterior-row-projection}\) and adding its independent policy coins produces a compatible endpoint SCM.  If some \(p_t^-=0<p_t^+\), use the optimizer in the compact right side of (7), equations (5)--(6), and one common diagonal index across all such times.  Continuity makes the resulting shared objectives converge to the exact values in (9), and the projection lemma supplies a compatible SCM at every positive-evidence term.  This proves the promised approximating sequence without asserting a single confidence-kernel witness at the boundary.

Finally, let \(\mathcal E=\{K\in\mathcal C_{1-\alpha}(\widehat K)\}\).  On \(\mathcal E\), every trajectory that has positive probability under the repaired externally randomized model has \(K_t(y_t\mid x_t)>0\) for all \(t\), so the true \(K\) is a positive-evidence confidence kernel.  The definitions give
\[
 L_i^\alpha\le L_i(K,\tau)\le U_i(K,\tau)\le U_i^\alpha. \tag{10}
\]
The event \(\mathcal E\) depends only on the transition data, not on \(i\), the chosen trajectory, or the prespecified externally randomized policy pair.  By (2), the same event proves (10) for all such queries simultaneously.  By \(\texttt{thm:sharp-shared-interval}\), (10) covers each entire compatible-SCM identified interval, not merely a selected point.
