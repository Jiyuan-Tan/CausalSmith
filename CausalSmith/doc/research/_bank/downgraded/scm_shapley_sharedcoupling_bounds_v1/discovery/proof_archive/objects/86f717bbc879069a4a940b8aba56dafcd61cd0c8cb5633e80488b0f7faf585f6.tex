Fix \(i\in[H]\) and \(\sigma\in\{-1,+1\}\).  The sign is fixed and is not an eliminated variable.  For \(x\ne x_t\), put
\[
 u_{t,x}(y)=\min\left\{1,\frac{K_t(y\mid x)}{p_t}\right\}.
\]
Division is valid by \(\texttt{ass:factual-positivity}\).  The corresponding factor of \(\mathcal B(K,\tau)\) is
\[
 \mathcal P_{t,x}=\left\{v\in\mathbb R^{\mathcal S}:
 0\le v(y)\le u_{t,x}(y),\quad \sum_yv(y)=1\right\}. \tag{1}
\]
It is nonempty because \(\sum_yK_t(y\mid x)/p_t=1/p_t\ge1\), so the truncated caps have total at least one.  The factual row is the singleton \(\{\delta_{y_t}\}\).

A point of (1) is a vertex exactly when all but at most one coordinate are at zero or their upper cap.  If two coordinates are strictly between their bounds, a sufficiently small mass transfer between them writes the point as the midpoint of two distinct feasible points.  Conversely, in any convex decomposition of a point with all but at most one coordinate at bounds, the bound coordinates agree in both summands and the sum-to-one equality fixes the remaining coordinate.  The vertices can therefore be listed exactly by choosing a possible non-bound coordinate, assigning zero or the upper cap to every other coordinate, solving the remaining coordinate from the equality, and retaining feasible candidates.  This is finite and output-sensitive after duplicate suppression; every coordinate is zero, a rational cap, or one minus a sum of rational caps, so its bit length is polynomial in the rational input length.

Let \(Z_{t,x}\) select one listed vertex \(q^Z_{t,x}\), with a one-point alphabet for a singleton factor.  The vertex conclusion of \(\texttt{thm:sharp-shared-interval}\) and the affine identity in \(\texttt{prop:affine-nonnegative-lift}\) imply
\[
 V_\sigma=\max_zF_i^\sigma(q^z), \tag{2}
\]
where \(z\) is one joint assignment of all row selectors.  Thus (2) already enforces one row assignment across every coalition world.

Exact quadrature applied to the constant polynomial gives \(\sum_j\nu_j=1\), so \(C_\nu=\sum_j|\nu_j|\ge1\).  Define
\[
 \rho_{jb}=\frac{|a_{jb}^\sigma|}{2C_\nu},
 \qquad
 \eta_{jb}=\begin{cases}+,&a_{jb}^\sigma\ge0,\\-,&a_{jb}^\sigma<0.
 \end{cases} \tag{3}
\]
Since \(|a_{j0}^\sigma|=|a_{j1}^\sigma|=|\nu_j|\), \(\sum_{j,b}\rho_{jb}=1\).  Give the single global selector \(W\in[H]\times\{0,1\}\times[H]\) the law
\[
 \Pr\{W=(j,b,\ell)\}=\frac{\rho_{jb}}H. \tag{4}
\]
Conditional on \((j,b)\), use the action kernel
\[
 \gamma_t^{jb}(a\mid s)=
 \begin{cases}
 \alpha_i^b(a\mid s),&t=i,\\
 (1-\zeta_j)\alpha_t^0(a\mid s)+\zeta_j\alpha_t^1(a\mid s),&t\ne i.
 \end{cases} \tag{5}
\]
These are external action randomizations, as required by \(\texttt{ass:factual-policy-exogeneity}\).  For every row insert the factor
\[
 \psi_{t,x}(S_t,A_t,S_{t+1},Z_{t,x})=
 \begin{cases}
 q^Z_{t,x}(S_{t+1}),&(S_t,A_t)=x,\\
 1,&(S_t,A_t)\ne x,
 \end{cases} \tag{6}
\]
and insert the fixed-initial-state factor \(\mathbf1\{S_1=s_1\}\).  Finally insert
\[
 \chi_t(W,S_t,A_t,S_{t+1})=
 \begin{cases}
 Hh_t^{\eta_{jb}}((S_t,A_t),S_{t+1}),&W=(j,b,t),\\
 1,&W=(j,b,\ell),\ \ell\ne t.
 \end{cases} \tag{7}
\]
All factors are rational and nonnegative by the rational-input hypothesis and \(\texttt{prop:affine-nonnegative-lift}\).  Equation (6) uses exactly one variable \(Z_{t,x}\) for a row, without copies depending on \(W\), paths, or reward times.

Fix \(z\) and sum the product of (4)--(7), the policy factors, and the initial factor over \(W\), the actions, and \(S_2,\ldots,S_{H+1}\).  For a realized \((S_t,A_t)\), the product of (6) over \(x\) is \(q^z_{t,(S_t,A_t)}(S_{t+1})\).  Thus the path sum is a Markov expectation under the one kernel \(q^z\).  Averaging over reward time and then \((j,b)\) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 T_\sigma(z)
 &=\sum_{j,b}\rho_{jb}\,
 \mathbb E_{q^z}\left[
 \sum_{t=1}^Hh_t^{\eta_{jb}}((S_t,A_t),S_{t+1})
 \mid S_1=s_1,\gamma^{jb}\right]\\
 &=\frac{F_i^\sigma(q^z)}{2C_\nu}.
 \end{aligned} \tag{8}
\]
For \(\eta_{jb}=+\), the expectation is \(h_{jb}(q^z)\); for \(\eta_{jb}=-\), the pathwise complementary-leaf identity in \(\texttt{prop:affine-nonnegative-lift}\) makes it \(1-h_{jb}(q^z)\).  Multiplication of the circuit output by \(2C_\nu\) therefore realizes \(F_i^\sigma\) exactly.

We prove the mixed-elimination invariant.  Chance elimination is exact by distributivity.  Suppose a decision variable \(Z\) is next.  Multiply its current bucket factors into \(A(Z,Y)\), where \(Y\) consists of the other uneliminated decisions in the bucket.  Legality of \(\preceq\) says that every chance variable sharing a current factor with \(Z\) has already been summed, so \(A\) has no uneliminated chance argument.  The factors outside the bucket multiply to a nonnegative function \(B(X,Y,Y')\) that does not contain \(Z\).  Pointwise,
\[
 \max_Z A(Z,Y)B(X,Y,Y')=B(X,Y,Y')\max_ZA(Z,Y). \tag{9}
\]
Thus the maximizing choice can depend on later decisions but not on a chance realization, and (9) commutes it outward past all unperformed chance summations.  Finite maximizations over different decisions commute.  Induction over the buckets, using distributivity at chance buckets and (9) at decision buckets, gives
\[
 2C_\nu\max_z\sum_{W,S,A}\mathfrak F_i^\sigma(z,W,S,A)
 =\max_zF_i^\sigma(q^z)=V_\sigma. \tag{10}
\]
This establishes optimization preservation without allowing a coalition-, quadrature-, or path-specific row assignment.

A bucket contains at most \(w_{\mathrm c}(\preceq)+1\) nonglobal live variables, each with alphabet at most \(d\).  The only global variable \(W\) has \(2H^2\) values.  Hence its table has at most
\[
 2H^2d^{w_{\mathrm c}(\preceq)+1} \tag{11}
\]
entries, and the same expression bounds buckets not containing \(W\).  There is a constant number of rational operations per entry per consumed factor.  Every initial factor is consumed once, and every eliminated variable creates at most one later-consumed factor.  Since \(N\) bounds variables plus factors, the total is
\[
 O\!\left(H^2N d^{w_{\mathrm c}(\preceq)+1}\right). \tag{12}
\]
The finite vertex listing is absorbed by this output-sensitive bound because \(d\) bounds each selector alphabet and \(N\) includes every selector and transition factor.

Let \(B_0\) be the maximum binary length of a rational input.  Primitive circuit entries and row vertices have bit length polynomial in \(B_0\).  For a fixed remaining decision context, any intermediate entry expands as a sum of products in which each primitive factor occurs at most once.  A common denominator therefore has bit length \(O(NB_0)\).  There are at most \(2H^2d^N\) chance assignments, contributing only \(O(\log H+N\log d)\) numerator bits.  Maximization selects an existing rational and creates no denominator.  Moreover a row has at most \(|\mathcal S|2^{|\mathcal S|-1}\) vertices by the proved characterization, so \(\log d\) is polynomial in the explicit input length.  Exact intermediate and output bit lengths are therefore polynomial in \(B_0\) and \(N\); rounding to a requested binary precision adds only that precision.

At each maximization bucket store a maximizing selector value for every later-decision context, breaking ties deterministically.  Reverse backtracking fixes that context before consulting the stored value.  Since legality excludes chance variables from decision buckets, the recovered assignment \(z_\sigma\) is one joint assignment independent of \(W\), states, and actions.

Apply the converse sampler of \(\texttt{lem:posterior-row-projection}\) to \(q^{z_\sigma}\).  Its capped-row inequalities make every residual distribution nonnegative and normalized, its independently sampled tables give the required transition disturbances and marginals, and its additional construction supplies \(S_1=s_1\), factual equations \(A_t=G_t(S_t,V_t)\), and fresh external coalition coins.  Hence this is a member of the repaired \(\mathfrak M(K)\), not merely a transition-table fragment.  The same response-table draw is used across every circuit branch and coalition world.  Finally \(\texttt{prop:affine-nonnegative-lift}\) gives
\[
 U_i(K,\tau)=R(V_{+1}-C_\nu),
 \qquad L_i(K,\tau)=R(C_\nu-V_{-1}),
\]
so the two backtracked compatible SCMs attain the claimed endpoints.
