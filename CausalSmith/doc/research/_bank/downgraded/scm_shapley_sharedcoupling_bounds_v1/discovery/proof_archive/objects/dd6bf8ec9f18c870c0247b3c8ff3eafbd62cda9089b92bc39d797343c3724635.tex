Fix \(i\in[H]\) and \(\sigma\in\{-1,+1\}\).  The sign is fixed throughout, so it is neither summed nor maximized.

We first make the row decisions finite.  For \(x\ne x_t\), define
\[
 u_{t,x}(y)=\min\!\left\{1,\frac{K_t(y\mid x)}{p_t}\right\},
 \qquad y\in\mathcal S.
\]
The division is valid by \(\texttt{ass:factual-positivity}\).  The corresponding factor of \(\mathcal B(K,\tau)\) in \(\texttt{def:posterior-row-fiber}\) is the capped simplex
\[
 \mathcal P_{t,x}=\left\{v\in\mathbb R^{\mathcal S}:
 0\le v(y)\le u_{t,x}(y)\ \text{for all }y,\quad
 \sum_{y\in\mathcal S}v(y)=1\right\}.
 \tag{1}
\]
It is nonempty.  Indeed, \(p_t\le1\), because \(p_t\) is a transition probability, and
\(\sum_y K_t(y\mid x)/p_t=1/p_t\ge1\).  If an untruncated cap is at least one, the sum of the truncated caps is at least one; otherwise truncation changes no cap.  Hence \(\sum_yu_{t,x}(y)\ge1\), and mass one can be allocated beneath the caps.  The factual-row factor is the singleton \(\{\delta_{y_t}\}\).

A point \(v\in\mathcal P_{t,x}\) is a vertex if and only if all but at most one coordinate belongs to \(\{0,u_{t,x}(y)\}\).  If two coordinates are strictly between their respective bounds, transferring sufficiently small equal masses in the two opposite directions writes \(v\) as the midpoint of two distinct feasible points.  Conversely, in a convex decomposition of a point with all but at most one coordinate at a bound, every bound coordinate must agree in both summands, and the sum-to-one equality fixes the remaining coordinate.  This proves the characterization.

The vertices can be enumerated exactly in output-sensitive time.  Obtain one vertex by filling coordinates in a fixed order up to their caps until the remaining mass fits in one coordinate.  From a listed vertex, test every ordered transfer direction \(e_y-e_{y'}\) that is locally feasible, move maximally in that direction, and retain the endpoint if it satisfies the vertex characterization.  Such an endpoint is again a vertex, and every edge incident to a vertex has this form because an edge of the box intersected with the sum hyperplane frees at most two box constraints.

This traversal reaches every vertex.  To prove that claim directly, fix a target vertex \(v^\star\).  Give coefficient \(1\) to each coordinate at its upper cap in \(v^\star\), coefficient \(-1\) to each zero coordinate, and coefficient zero to the possible unique non-bound coordinate.  The resulting linear functional has unique maximizer \(v^\star\): all bound coordinates are forced at equality, and the sum constraint fixes the possible remaining coordinate.  At any other vertex \(v\), the feasible direction \(v^\star-v\) is a sum of transfers from coordinates that decrease to coordinates that increase.  At least one such locally feasible transfer strictly increases the functional.  Moving maximally reaches another vertex with a strictly larger value.  Finiteness forces eventual arrival at \(v^\star\).  With exact hashing for duplicate suppression, testing \(O(|\mathcal S|^2)\) transfers per output vertex lists a row with \(v_{t,x}\) vertices in \(O(|\mathcal S|^2v_{t,x})\) rational operations.  Each coordinate is zero, a cap, or one minus a sum of caps, so rational inputs produce vertices of bit length polynomial in the rational input length.

Let \(Z_{t,x}\) select a listed vertex \(q^Z_{t,x}\), using a one-point alphabet for a singleton row.  By the row-vertex assertion of \(\texttt{thm:sharp-shared-interval}\), both extrema have an optimizer among joint selector assignments.  By \(\texttt{prop:affine-nonnegative-lift}\),
\[
 V_\sigma=\max_z F_i^\sigma(q^z),
 \tag{2}
\]
where \(z\) is one joint assignment of all row selectors.  In particular, the same \(z\) is used in every coalition world.

We next construct the promised nonnegative circuit.  Exact quadrature in \(\texttt{prop:exact-coalition-compression}\), applied to the constant polynomial, gives \(\sum_{j=1}^H\nu_j=1\); hence \(C_\nu=\sum_j|\nu_j|\ge1\).  Put
\[
 \rho_{jb}=\frac{|a_{jb}^\sigma|}{2C_\nu},
 \qquad
 \eta_{jb}=\begin{cases}
 +,&a_{jb}^\sigma\ge0,\\
 -,&a_{jb}^\sigma<0.
 \end{cases}
\]
Because \(|a_{j0}^\sigma|=|a_{j1}^\sigma|=|\nu_j|\), one has \(\sum_{j,b}\rho_{jb}=1\).  Introduce the single bundled chance variable \(W\) with alphabet \([H]\times\{0,1\}\times[H]\) and law
\[
 \Pr\{W=(j,b,t)\}=\frac{\rho_{jb}}{H}.
 \tag{3}
\]
Conditional on the first two coordinates \((j,b)\), use the action kernel
\[
 \gamma_u^{jb}(a\mid s)=
 \begin{cases}
 \alpha_i^b(a\mid s),&u=i,\\
 (1-\zeta_j)\alpha_u^0(a\mid s)+\zeta_j\alpha_u^1(a\mid s),&u\ne i.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{4}
\]
For every transition row insert
\[
 \psi_{u,x}(S_u,A_u,S_{u+1},Z_{u,x})=
 \begin{cases}
 q^{Z}_{u,x}(S_{u+1}),&(S_u,A_u)=x,\\
 1,&(S_u,A_u)\ne x,
 \end{cases}
 \tag{5}
\]
and insert \(\mathbf 1\{S_1=s_1\}\).  For each reward time insert
\[
 \chi_u(W,S_u,A_u,S_{u+1})=
 \begin{cases}
 Hh_u^{\eta_{jb}}((S_u,A_u),S_{u+1}),&W=(j,b,u),\\
 1,&W=(j,b,t)\ \text{with }t\ne u.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{6}
\]
All factors are rational and nonnegative.  Rationality follows from the hypotheses and the vertex construction, while nonnegativity of the local reward leaves follows from \(R>0\), the definitions of \(m_u,M_u\), and \(\texttt{prop:affine-nonnegative-lift}\).  Equation (5) uses exactly one variable \(Z_{u,x}\) for a row and does not copy it across values of \(W\), paths, or reward times.

For fixed \(z\), sum the product of (3)--(6), the policy factors (4), and the initial factor over \(W\), all actions, and \(S_2,\ldots,S_{H+1}\); denote the result by \(T_\sigma(z)\).  On a realized row \((S_u,A_u)\), the product over \(x\) in (5) is exactly \(q^z_{u,(S_u,A_u)}(S_{u+1})\).  Therefore the state-action sum is the Markov expectation under the single kernel \(q^z\).  Averaging first over the reward-time coordinate and then over \((j,b)\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 T_\sigma(z)
 &=\sum_{j=1}^H\sum_{b=0}^1\rho_{jb}
   \mathbb E_{q^z}\!\left[
    \sum_{u=1}^H h_u^{\eta_{jb}}((S_u,A_u),S_{u+1})
    \mathrel{\Big|}S_1=s_1,\gamma^{jb}
   \right]\\
 &=\frac{1}{2C_\nu}F_i^\sigma(q^z).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
For \(\eta_{jb}=+\), the expectation is \(h_{jb}(q^z)\) by \(\texttt{def:nonnegative-endpoint-objectives}\).  For \(\eta_{jb}=-\), the pathwise complementary-leaf identity in \(\texttt{prop:affine-nonnegative-lift}\) makes it \(1-h_{jb}(q^z)\).  Thus the circuit value, followed by deterministic multiplication by \(2C_\nu\), is exactly \(F_i^\sigma(q^z)\).  This is an explicit realization of the circuit required by \(\texttt{def:compiler-handle}\).

We prove optimization preservation.  Bucket elimination maintains nonnegative factors, because both summation and maximization preserve nonnegativity.  Summing a chance variable is exact by distributivity: factors outside its bucket do not contain that variable.  Suppose a decision variable \(Z\) is next.  Multiply all current factors containing \(Z\) into \(A(Z,Y)\), with \(Y\) the other live decision variables in that bucket.  Legality of \(\preceq\) says that no uneliminated chance variable occurs in this scope.  Write the product of factors outside the bucket as \(B(X,Y,Y')\), where \(X\) are remaining chance variables, \(Y'\) are remaining decisions outside the bucket, and \(B\) does not involve \(Z\).  Pointwise in \((X,Y,Y')\),
\[
 \max_Z\{A(Z,Y)B(X,Y,Y')\}
 =B(X,Y,Y')\max_ZA(Z,Y),
 \tag{8}
\]
because \(B\ge0\).  Thus this maximization commutes outward past all later chance sums without allowing the maximizing value of \(Z\) to depend on a chance realization.  Finite maxima over decision variables commute with one another.  Induction over the buckets, using distributivity at chance buckets and (8) at decision buckets, converts the returned value to
\[
 2C_\nu\max_z
 \sum_{W,S_2,\ldots,S_{H+1},A_1,\ldots,A_H}
 \mathfrak F_i^\sigma(z,W,S,A)
 =\max_zF_i^\sigma(q^z)=V_\sigma,
 \tag{9}
\]
where (7) proves the first equality and (2) the second.  This establishes the mixed-elimination invariant for every legal order.

For the arithmetic count, a bucket contains at most \(w_{\mathrm c}(\preceq)+1\) nonglobal live variables, each of alphabet at most \(d\).  The bundled global variable \(W\) has \(2H^2\) values.  Consequently every bucket table has at most
\[
 2H^2d^{w_{\mathrm c}(\preceq)+1}
 \tag{10}
\]
entries, whether or not \(W\) actually occurs in that bucket.  Each initial factor is consumed once, and each eliminated variable creates at most one new factor, which is later consumed once.  Since \(N\) bounds the number of variables plus initial factors, the total number of factor consumptions is \(O(N)\).  A constant number of rational operations per table entry therefore gives
\[
 O\!\left(H^2N d^{w_{\mathrm c}(\preceq)+1}\right)
 \tag{11}
\]
rational arithmetic operations.  The output-sensitive row traversal costs \(O(N|\mathcal S|^2d)\), because each selector has at most \(d\) listed values.  If a non-singleton selector occurs, its transition factor contains that selector, the current state, the action, and the next state.  Hence some bucket has at least four nonglobal live variables, so \(w_{\mathrm c}(\preceq)+1\ge4\); since \(|\mathcal S|\le d\), the traversal cost is absorbed by (11).  If every row is a singleton, there is no nontrivial vertex enumeration.

Let \(B_0\) be the binary length of the rational input arrays and quadrature data.  Each cap in (1) has bit length polynomial in \(B_0\), and every vertex coordinate is zero, a cap, or one minus a sum of at most \(|\mathcal S|\) caps.  Hence initial circuit entries have polynomial bit length.  For a fixed remaining decision context, every intermediate entry expands as a sum of products of initial entries, with each initial factor used at most once in a product.  A common denominator uses the primitive denominators with exponent at most the number of factors, so its bit length is polynomial in \(B_0+N\).  The number of summed chance assignments is at most \((2H^2)d^N\), contributing at most \(O(\log H+N\log d)\) numerator bits.  A maximization selects one candidate rational and introduces no new denominator.  Since \(\log d=O(|\mathcal S|)\) for the row-vertex alphabet and \(|\mathcal S|\) is represented in the explicit input, every reduced intermediate and exact output rational has bit length polynomial in \(B_0\) and \(N\).  Rounding the exact answer to a requested binary precision adds only that requested number of output bits.

At every maximization bucket, store an optimizing value of the eliminated selector for each assignment of the later decision variables in the output scope, breaking ties by a fixed order.  Reverse-order backtracking fixes those later variables before consulting the stored value.  Legality ensures that no stored choice is indexed by \(W\), a state, or an action.  Thus \(\mathsf{BP}_\sigma\) returns one joint selector assignment \(z_\sigma\), shared across all branches and paths.

It remains to construct the endpoint SCM compactly.  At time \(t\), draw \(E_t\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p_t)\).  Conditional on \(E_t=1\), set the factual response coordinate to \(y_t\) and draw every nonfactual coordinate independently from \(q^{z_\sigma}_{t,x}\).  If \(p_t<1\), conditional on \(E_t=0\), draw the factual coordinate from
\[
 \frac{K_t(y\mid x_t)-p_t\mathbf 1\{y=y_t\}}{1-p_t}
\]
and every nonfactual coordinate \(x\ne x_t\) from
\[
 \frac{K_t(y\mid x)-p_tq^{z_\sigma}_{t,x}(y)}{1-p_t}.
 \tag{12}
\]
The numerators are nonnegative by \(q^{z_\sigma}\in\mathcal B(K,\tau)\), and each displayed array sums to one.  The residual factual law puts zero mass on \(y_t\), so conditioning on the factual event forces \(E_t=1\) and yields exactly the selected posterior rows.  Mixing the branches gives marginal \(K_t(\cdot\mid x)\) for every row.  If \(p_t=1\), the residual branch is absent; coordinatewise feasibility gives \(q^{z_\sigma}_{t,x}\le K_t(\cdot\mid x)\), and equality of the two total masses forces equality coordinatewise.  Sampling these response tables independently over \(t\) satisfies the structural equations, transition marginals, and independent-time requirement in \(\texttt{def:compatible-scm-class}\), exactly as in the converse direction of \(\texttt{lem:posterior-row-projection}\).  Formula (12), its branch probabilities, and the selected row distributions form a compact conditional sampler rather than an exponential response-table mass array.

Finally, (9) returns \(V_{+1}\) and \(V_{-1}\) in separate fixed-sign runs.  By \(\texttt{prop:affine-nonnegative-lift}\),
\[
 U_i(K,\tau)=R(V_{+1}-C_\nu),
 \qquad
 L_i(K,\tau)=R(C_\nu-V_{-1}).
\]
The two backtracked compatible SCMs attain these endpoints and each uses one joint row assignment in every coalition world.  This proves all assertions.
