Fix \(\mathcal M\in\mathfrak M(K)\), with the fixed ambient \((\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{base}},\tau)\) understood as in \(\texttt{def:compatible-scm-class}\).  For \(t\in[H]\), set
\[
 E_t=\{R_t(x_t)=y_t\},\qquad B_t=\{A_t=a_t\},
 \qquad E=\bigcap_{t=1}^H E_t,\qquad B=\bigcap_{t=1}^H B_t.
 \tag{1}
\]
For \(t\ge1\), write \(E_{<t}=\bigcap_{u<t}E_u\) and \(B_{<t}=\bigcap_{u<t}B_u\), with empty intersections equal to the sure event.  By \(\texttt{ass:fixed-initial-state}\), \(S_1=s_1\) almost surely.  Inductively, on \(B_{<t}\cap E_{<t}\) one has \(S_t=s_t\): if this holds at \(t\), then \(B_t\) gives \((S_t,A_t)=x_t\), and \(E_t\), together with \(S_{t+1}=R_t(S_t,A_t)\) from \(\texttt{ass:markov-structural-equations}\), gives \(S_{t+1}=R_t(x_t)=y_t=s_{t+1}\).  Consequently,
\[
 \{\tau\}=B\cap E. \tag{2}
\]
This induction uses the prefix action and preceding transition events together; the transition events alone do not fix the realized state path.

Because \(R_t\) is a function of \(U_t\), \(\texttt{ass:independent-time-disturbances}\) makes \(R_1,\ldots,R_H\) mutually independent.  Let \(\lambda_t\) denote the law of \(R_t\).  Fix a response-table tuple \((r_1,\ldots,r_H)\in E\) having positive probability.  On \(B_{<t}\cap E_{<t}\), the preceding induction gives \(S_t=s_t\).  The full product-extension condition in \(\texttt{ass:factual-policy-exogeneity}\) makes \(V_t\) independent of the complete table tuple, all the events \(E_u\), and \(V_1,\ldots,V_{t-1}\).  Hence the structural lookup and its coordinate marginal give
\[
 \Pr\!\left(B_t\mid R_1=r_1,\ldots,R_H=r_H,E,B_{<t}\right)
 =\Pr\{G_t(s_t,V_t)=a_t\}=\pi_t(a_t\mid s_t). \tag{3}
\]
The chain rule applied sequentially to (3) yields
\[
 \Pr\!\left(B\mid R_1=r_1,\ldots,R_H=r_H,E\right)
 =c_\tau,
 \qquad c_\tau:=\prod_{t=1}^H\pi_t(a_t\mid s_t). \tag{4}
\]
Every factor in \(c_\tau\) is positive by \(\texttt{ass:factual-action-positivity}\).  Thus (4) is a positive likelihood constant that does not depend on the response-table tuple.  For every set \(D\) of table tuples, equations (2)--(4) give
\[
 \Pr\{(R_1,\ldots,R_H)\in D,\tau\}
 =c_\tau\Pr\{(R_1,\ldots,R_H)\in D,E\}. \tag{5}
\]
By \(\texttt{ass:transition-marginals}\) and \(\texttt{ass:factual-positivity}\),
\[
 \Pr(E_t)=K_t(y_t\mid x_t)=p_t>0. \tag{6}
\]
Independence of the response tables gives \(\Pr(E)=\prod_t p_t\).  Dividing (5) by \(\Pr(\tau)=c_\tau\prod_t p_t>0\) proves
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Law}(R_1,\ldots,R_H\mid\tau)
 &=\operatorname{Law}(R_1,\ldots,R_H\mid E)\\
 &=\bigotimes_{t=1}^H\operatorname{Law}(R_t\mid E_t).
 \end{aligned} \tag{7}
\]

Let \(\mathscr V_{\mathrm{coal}}=(V_t^C:t\in[H],C\subseteq[H])\).  This entire finite family is jointly independent of \((S_1,R_1,\ldots,R_H,V_1,\ldots,V_H)\) by \(\texttt{ass:factual-policy-exogeneity}\), while \(\{\tau\}\) is measurable with respect to the latter variables.  Therefore, for events \(A\) of coalition coins and \(D\) of response tables,
\[
 \Pr\{\mathscr V_{\mathrm{coal}}\in A,(R_1,\ldots,R_H)\in D\mid\tau\}
 =\Pr\{\mathscr V_{\mathrm{coal}}\in A\}\Pr\{(R_1,\ldots,R_H)\in D\mid\tau\}. \tag{8}
\]
Thus factual conditioning neither changes the coalition policy laws nor destroys their mutual independence or their independence from the posterior transition tables.

Define
\[
 q_{t,x}(y)=\Pr\{R_t(x)=y\mid E_t\}.
\]
Then \(q_{t,x_t}=\delta_{y_t}\).  For \(x\ne x_t\), the multiplication rule, event inclusion, and \(\texttt{ass:transition-marginals}\) imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 p_tq_{t,x}(y)
 &=\Pr\{R_t(x_t)=y_t,R_t(x)=y\}\\
 &\le \Pr\{R_t(x)=y\}=K_t(y\mid x).
 \end{aligned} \tag{9}
\]
Conditioning is valid by (6), so every \(q_{t,x}\) is a probability mass function.  Equation (9) and the factual point mass are precisely the constraints in \(\texttt{def:posterior-row-fiber}\); hence \(q\in\mathcal B(K,\tau)\).  Equations (7)--(8) show that every coalition world uses its fixed kernel \(\alpha^C\), independent of the tables, and queries the same posterior transition kernel \(q\).

Conversely, fix \(q\in\mathcal B(K,\tau)\).  At time \(t\), draw \(Z_t\) with law \(K_t(\cdot\mid x_t)\) and set \(R_t(x_t)=Z_t\).  If \(p_t<1\), define, for \(x\ne x_t\),
\[
 h_{t,x}(y)=\frac{K_t(y\mid x)-p_tq_{t,x}(y)}{1-p_t}. \tag{10}
\]
The numerator is nonnegative by the fiber inequality, and
\[
 \sum_{y\in\mathcal S}h_{t,x}(y)=\frac{1-p_t}{1-p_t}=1, \tag{11}
\]
so \(h_{t,x}\in\Delta(\mathcal S)\).  Conditional on \(Z_t=y_t\), draw the nonfactual coordinates with respective laws \(q_{t,x}\); conditional on \(Z_t\ne y_t\), draw them with respective laws \(h_{t,x}\).  These conditional draws may be made independent across nonfactual rows.  This is one joint table law \(\lambda_t\), not a coalition-indexed law, and
\[
 \Pr\{R_t(x)=y\}=p_tq_{t,x}(y)+(1-p_t)h_{t,x}(y)=K_t(y\mid x). \tag{12}
\]
The factual coordinate has marginal \(K_t(\cdot\mid x_t)\) by construction.  If \(p_t=1\), the fiber inequalities \(q_{t,x}(y)\le K_t(y\mid x)\) and equality of the two total masses force \(q_{t,x}=K_t(\cdot\mid x)\); use that row in the sole branch and omit (10).

Draw the constructed response tables independently over \(t\), put \(U_t=R_t\), and set \(F_t(x,U_t)=U_t(x)\).  This establishes the structural equations, timewise disturbance independence, and transition marginals.  Set \(S_1=s_1\).  It remains to append the policy mechanisms.  For each \(t\), draw \(V_t\in\mathcal A^{\mathcal S}\) with coordinate marginals \(\pi_t(\cdot\mid s)\), for example by taking its state coordinates independently; for each of the finitely many pairs \((t,C)\), draw \(V_t^C\in\mathcal A^{\mathcal S}\) with coordinate marginals \(\alpha_t^C(\cdot\mid s)\), again using a product over state coordinates.  Take the product of all these finite laws and the transition-table law, and define \(G_t(s,v)=v(s)\).  This realizes exactly the joint independence and all action-kernel marginals in \(\texttt{ass:factual-policy-exogeneity}\).  Because it is an external product extension, it exists for every transition-table law and therefore does not shrink the admissible transition-response class.  Although the product construction proves simultaneous existence for all \(2^H\) coalitions, a sampler stores only \(\pi\), \(\pi_{\mathrm{base}}\), and the rule defining \(\alpha^C\), and generates the queried coin coordinates lazily; it need not store \(2^H\) mechanisms.

The resulting model satisfies every member property in \(\texttt{def:compatible-scm-class}\), and its factual trajectory has probability \(c_\tau\prod_t p_t>0\).  Applying (7) gives precisely the prescribed posterior rows.  The one law \(\lambda_t\) is shared by all nonfactual rows and every coalition world, completing the converse.
