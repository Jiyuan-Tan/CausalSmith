Write \(\delta=\delta(K,K')\), \(p=K_t(y_t\mid x_t)\), and \(p'=K'_t(y_t\mid x_t)\).  The definition of \(\delta\) gives \(|p-p'|\le\delta\), and the first hypothesis gives \(p,p'\ge\kappa>0\).  Fix a nonfactual row \(x\ne x_t\), abbreviate \(k=K_t(\cdot\mid x)\) and \(k'=K'_t(\cdot\mid x)\), and take a feasible posterior row \(q\) from its \(K\)-fiber.  Define
\[
 a(y)=\min\{q(y),k'(y)/p'\},
 \qquad
 d_0=1-\sum_{y\in\mathcal S}a(y).
\]
Division by \(p'\) is valid because \(p'\ge\kappa>0\).  Since \(pq(y)\le k(y)\) by \(\texttt{def:posterior-row-fiber}\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 d_0
 &=\frac1{p'}\sum_{y\in\mathcal S}\bigl(p'q(y)-k'(y)\bigr)_+\\
 &\le \frac{|p'-p|+\|k-k'\|_1}{p'}\\
 &\le \frac{2\delta}{\kappa}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
To justify the middle inequality coordinatewise, write
\[
 p'q-k'=(p'-p)q+(pq-k)+(k-k').
\]
The middle summand is nonpositive.  The positive parts of the first summand total at most \(|p'-p|\sum_yq(y)=|p'-p|\), and the positive parts of the last total at most \(\|k-k'\|_1\).  The last inequality in (2) uses the two rowwise bounds supplied by \(\delta\).

The unused upper-cap capacity equals
\[
 \sum_y\{k'(y)/p'-a(y)\}
 =1/p'-1+d_0
 \ge d_0,
\]
where \(p'\le1\) because it is a probability.  Since \(\mathcal S\) is finite, distribute mass \(d_0\) within these nonnegative capacities and call the added vector \(b\).  Then \(q'=a+b\) belongs to \(\Delta(\mathcal S)\) and satisfies \(p'q'(y)\le k'(y)\) for every \(y\).  Moreover,
\[
 \|q-q'\|_1
 \le \|q-a\|_1+\|b\|_1
 =2d_0
 \le\frac{4\delta}{\kappa},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{TV}(q,q')\le\frac{2\delta}{\kappa}.
 \tag{3}
\]
The factual posterior rows agree exactly at \(\delta_{y_t}\).  Applying this construction separately to every nonfactual row and time maps each \(q\in\mathcal B(K,\tau)\) to a \(q'\in\mathcal B(K',\tau)\) satisfying
\[
 \max_{t,x}\operatorname{TV}(q_{t,x},q'_{t,x})
 \le\frac{2\delta}{\kappa}.
 \tag{4}
\]
Interchanging \(K\) and \(K'\) gives the reverse fiber approximation with the same bound.

Fix a coalition policy.  Couple the two induced Markov paths sequentially.  They start at the same fixed state.  Whenever their histories agree through the current state, use the same action draw from their common known policy kernel.  For two next-state mass functions \(\mu\) and \(\nu\), put mass \(\min\{\mu(y),\nu(y)\}\) on each diagonal pair \((y,y)\) and couple the two residual mass functions arbitrarily.  The mismatch probability is
\[
 1-\sum_y\min\{\mu(y),\nu(y)\}
 =\frac12\sum_y|\mu(y)-\nu(y)|
 =\operatorname{TV}(\mu,\nu).
\]
Use this construction for the two posterior next-state rows.  By (4), the conditional probability of a first mismatch at that transition is at most \(2\delta/\kappa\).  A union bound over the \(H\) transitions therefore produces a coupling of the complete paths whose mismatch probability is at most \(2H\delta/\kappa\).  When the paths agree their complete returns agree, and otherwise the absolute return difference is at most \(D\).  Hence
\[
 |g_C(q)-g_C(q')|\le\frac{2DH\delta}{\kappa}.
 \tag{5}
\]
The Shapley weights are nonnegative and sum to one, because
\[
 \sum_{C\subseteq[H]\setminus\{i\}}\omega_H(C)
 =\sum_{k=0}^{H-1}{H-1\choose k}
   \frac{k!(H-k-1)!}{H!}
 =1.
 \tag{6}
\]
Combining (5), (6), and \(\texttt{def:shapley-coordinate}\) yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\Phi_i(q)-\Phi_i(q')|
 &\le\sum_C\omega_H(C)
 \left\{|g_C(q)-g_C(q')|
       +|g_{C\cup\{i\}}(q)-g_{C\cup\{i\}}(q')|\right\}\\
 &\le\frac{4DH\delta}{\kappa}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]

By \(\texttt{thm:sharp-shared-interval}\), the maxima and minima over both compact fibers are attained and have the asserted causal interpretation.  Map a maximizer over the \(K\)-fiber into the \(K'\)-fiber using (4).  Equation (7) gives
\[
 U_i(K,\tau)\le U_i(K',\tau)+4DH\delta/\kappa.
\]
Reverse the kernels to obtain the opposite inequality.  Applying the same two comparisons to minimizers proves
\[
 \max\!\left\{|L_i(K,\tau)-L_i(K',\tau)|,
                 |U_i(K,\tau)-U_i(K',\tau)|\right\}
 \le\frac{4DH\delta}{\kappa}.
 \tag{8}
\]

It remains to derive the evaluable corollaries.  By definition, \(r_{\mathrm{pos}}(\kappa)=\kappa/2\).  If \(K_t(y_t\mid x_t)\ge\kappa\) and \(\delta(K,K')<\kappa/2\), then
\[
 K'_t(y_t\mid x_t)
 \ge K_t(y_t\mid x_t)-\delta(K,K')
 >\kappa/2
\]
for every \(t\).  Thus both kernels have factual cells at least \(\kappa/2\).  Applying (8) with the common positivity constant \(\kappa/2\) gives, for \(E_i\in\{L_i,U_i\}\),
\[
 |E_i(K',\tau)-E_i(K,\tau)|
 \le\frac{8DH}{\kappa}\delta(K,K'),
\]
which is equivalent to the asserted two-sided trajectory bracket.  This certifies the stated radius; no maximality claim is made.

On the coverage event \(K\in\mathcal C_{1-\alpha}(\widehat K)\), the confidence region is nonempty and contains the true kernel.  If \(r_{\mathcal C}(K,\widehat K)<\kappa/2\), its definition implies that every \(K'\) in the region satisfies \(\delta(K,K')<\kappa/2\).  The preceding bracket therefore holds uniformly over the region, and all its factual cells are positive.  Taking the infimum of the lower endpoints and using feasibility of \(K\) in \(\texttt{def:confidence-fiber-endpoints}\) gives
\[
 L_i(K,\tau)-\frac{8DH}{\kappa}r_{\mathcal C}(K,\widehat K)
 \le L_i^\alpha\le L_i(K,\tau).
\]
Taking the supremum of the upper endpoints gives
\[
 U_i(K,\tau)
 \le U_i^\alpha
 \le U_i(K,\tau)+\frac{8DH}{\kappa}r_{\mathcal C}(K,\widehat K).
\]
Finally, on the event \(\delta(\widehat K,K)<\kappa/2\), the same corollary with \(K'=\widehat K\) bounds each plug-in endpoint error by \(8DH\delta(\widehat K,K)/\kappa\).  Uniform convergence of the discrepancy in probability therefore implies uniform plug-in consistency.  The two confidence-fiber displays give the stated conditional uniform convergence whenever the realized fiber radius converges uniformly to zero on the coverage events.
