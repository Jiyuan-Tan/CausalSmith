By \(\text{def:posterior-row-fiber}\), the choice \(q_{t,x}=K_t(\cdot\mid x)\) for \(x\ne x_t\), together with \(q_{t,x_t}=\delta_{y_t}\), is feasible because \(0<p_t\le1\).  Hence \(\mathcal B(K,\tau)\) is nonempty.  Each of its nonfactual row factors is the intersection of a finite-dimensional simplex with finitely many closed half-spaces, so it is compact and convex; the full finite product has the same properties.

For a fixed coalition, finite-state Markov expansion expresses \(g_C(q)\) as a finite sum of path terms, each term being a known reward times policy probabilities and one coordinate of \(q\) at each time.  Consequently \(g_C\), and hence \(\Phi_i\) by \(\text{def:shapley-coordinate}\), is a continuous polynomial.  The two directions of \(\text{lem:posterior-row-projection}\) now give the equality of sets
\[
 \Theta_i(K,\tau)=\{\Phi_i(q):q\in\mathcal B(K,\tau)\}.
\]
Compactness and continuity give minimizers \(q^-\) and maximizers \(q^+\), whose values are \(L_i(K,\tau)\) and \(U_i(K,\tau)\) by \(\text{def:shared-endpoints}\).

For \(s\in[0,1]\), convexity makes \(q(s)=(1-s)q^-+sq^+\) feasible, and \(f(s)=\Phi_i(q(s))\) is continuous.  For completeness, the intermediate-value step follows by bisection: for any \(c\) between \(f(0)\) and \(f(1)\), repeatedly retain a half-interval whose endpoint values bracket \(c\).  The nested intervals have lengths tending to zero and hence a common point \(s_*\); continuity excludes \(f(s_*)<c\) and \(f(s_*)>c\), so \(f(s_*)=c\).  Therefore the displayed image is the whole interval \([L_i,U_i]\), not merely a set with those extrema.  Applying the converse construction in \(\text{lem:posterior-row-projection}\) to \(q^-\), \(q^+\), or \(q(s_*)\) produces compatible SCMs.  In particular both endpoints are attained without introducing a time-common mixture variable.

It remains to obtain vertex witnesses.  In every path monomial, at a fixed time \(t\) exactly one transition row is queried.  Thus, with all other rows fixed, every \(g_C(q)\), and consequently \(\Phi_i(q)\), is affine in any one row \(q_{t,x}\).  Start from an endpoint optimizer and consider one row factor.  Its current row must optimize the resulting affine functional conditionally on all other rows, since otherwise replacing that row would improve the global endpoint.  An affine functional on a polytope has a vertex optimizer: if an optimizing point is not extreme, write it as a nontrivial convex combination; at least one component is no worse, and repeating inside the proper optimizing face terminates after finitely many dimension reductions at a vertex.  Replace the row by such a vertex.  Iterating over the finitely many \((t,x)\) preserves the endpoint value and makes every row a vertex; the factual singleton rows are already vertices.

Finally, the construction in \(\text{lem:posterior-row-projection}\) is a compact sampler.  At each time it stores \(K_t(\cdot\mid x_t)\), the selected conditional rows \(q_{t,x}\), and, when \(p_t<1\), the residual rows \(h_{t,x}\).  It then samples the factual coordinate followed by conditionally independent remaining coordinates.  This uses \(O(H|\mathcal X||\mathcal S|)\) displayed probabilities and need not list the generally exponential mass table on \(\mathcal S^{\mathcal X}\).
