At \(\theta^\star\), every factual transition probability is \(1/2\).  A binary nonfactual posterior success probability \(a\) is constrained only by \(a\in[0,1]\), so the fiber coordinates \(u,w,z,v\) range independently over \([0,1]\), while the factual rows are \(\delta_0\).

Put \(\eta=1/64\), \(\beta=63/64\), and \(\epsilon=1/4\).  The fixed initial state, external action coins, and deterministic terminal reward give the direct recursions
\[
 g_\varnothing=0,
 \qquad g_{\{1\}}=\epsilon u\{(1-\eta)z+\eta v\},
 \qquad g_{\{2\}}=\beta w,
\]
and
\[
 g_{\{1,2\}}=(1-\epsilon u)\beta w
 +\epsilon u\{(1-\beta)z+\beta v\}. \tag{1}
\]
For \(H=2\), both Shapley weights are \(1/2\), and \(\beta+\eta=1\).  Therefore
\[
 \Phi_1(q)=\frac{\epsilon u}{2}
 \left[\beta w-\{2-\beta-\eta\}z-(\beta+\eta)v\right]
 =\frac{u}{8}\left(\frac{63}{64}w-z-v\right). \tag{2}
\]
Its minimum is \(-1/4\), attained at \((u,w,z,v)=(1,0,1,1)\), and its maximum is \(63/512\), attained at \((1,1,0,0)\).  By the shared-endpoint definition and the repaired projection theorem, these are compatible-SCM endpoints with independent policy coins.

The separate coalition extrema from (1) are
\[
 \inf g_\varnothing=\sup g_\varnothing=0,
 \quad \inf g_{\{1\}}=0,
 \quad \sup g_{\{1\}}=\frac14,
 \quad \inf g_{\{2\}}=0,
 \quad \sup g_{\{2\}}=\frac{63}{64}.
\]
Also \(\inf g_{\{1,2\}}=0\), and setting all four row coordinates equal to one gives
\[
 \sup g_{\{1,2\}}=\beta+\epsilon(1-\beta)=\frac{253}{256}.
\]
The formula in \(\texttt{def:independent-coalition-outer-bound}\) consequently yields
\[
 \inf\mathcal I_1^{\mathrm{ind}}=-\frac12\left(\frac14+\frac{253}{256}\right)
 =-\frac{317}{512},
 \qquad
 \sup\mathcal I_1^{\mathrm{ind}}=\frac12\frac{63}{64}=\frac{63}{128}.
\]
Both excess widths are \(189/512\).

Now fix \(\theta\in\mathcal N_\rho(\theta^\star)\), where \(\rho=1/1024\) and \(\kappa=511/1024\).  The metric definition gives
\[
 \delta(K,K^\star)<\rho,
 \qquad K_t(0\mid(0,0))>\frac12-\rho=\kappa. \tag{3}
\]
With center rewards and policies held fixed, complete returns lie in \([0,1]\).  The local modulus with \(H=2\) moves either shared endpoint by at most \(8\rho/\kappa\) when \(K^\star\) is replaced by \(K\).

Next hold \(K\), and hence its posterior fiber, fixed while changing rewards and policies.  For any coalition, maximally couple each external action draw.  The rowwise policy \(\ell^1\) discrepancies are below \(\rho\), so each action mismatch probability is below \(\rho/2\); a union bound over two actions gives path-law total variation below \(\rho\).  For fixed paths the two rewards differ by less than \(2\rho\).  Comparing first rewards on a common path and then center rewards across the coupled paths gives, uniformly in feasible \(q\),
\[
 |g_C(q;\theta)-g_C(q;K,r^\star,\pi^\star,\pi_{\mathrm{base}}^\star)|<3\rho. \tag{4}
\]
Because Shapley weights sum to one and every contribution is a difference of two returns, (4) moves \(\Phi_1\), and hence either shared extremum over the unchanged fiber, by less than \(6\rho\).  Thus each shared endpoint moves from its center value by at most
\[
 A=\frac{8\rho}{\kappa}+6\rho. \tag{5}
\]

The same \(A\) bounds either endpoint of the internal relaxation.  Indeed, the row-matching construction in the proof of \(\texttt{thm:local-positivity-modulus}\), applied between \(K^\star\) and \(K\), moves each posterior row by total variation below \(2\rho/\kappa\).  Sequential coupling over two transitions moves an extremum of any center-reward coalition return by at most \(4\rho/\kappa\); equation (4) adds \(3\rho\).  An endpoint of the internal interval has total absolute coefficient two, giving the bound \(2(4\rho/\kappa+3\rho)=A\).  Consequently
\[
 \min\{\Delta_1^-(\theta),\Delta_1^+(\theta)\}
 \ge\frac{189}{512}-2A
 =\frac{183}{512}-\frac{16}{511}>\frac14, \tag{6}
\]
where the last inequality is equivalent to \(55\cdot511>16\cdot512\).

The remaining certificates follow directly from the metric ball.  Coordinate differences are bounded by rowwise \(\ell^1\) differences, so
\[
 \mu_\tau(\theta)>left(\frac{63}{64}-\rho\right)^2
 \left(\frac12-\rho\right)^2>\frac15,
\]
\[
 s_\pi(\theta)>\frac1{64}-\rho=\frac{15}{1024}>\frac1{100},
 \qquad
 s_K(\theta)>\frac12-\rho=\frac{511}{1024}>\frac25.
\]
For a time-two row put \(a=K_2(1\mid x)\).  Then \(|a-1/2|<\rho\), while the reward contrast exceeds \(1-2\rho\).  The binary variance identity gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_{Y\sim K_2(\cdot\mid x)}\{r_2(x,Y)\}
 =a(1-a)\{r_2(x,1)-r_2(x,0)\}^2
 >\left(\frac14-\rho^2\right)(1-2\rho)^2>\frac15.
\]
The strict metric ball is relative-open, and every policy and transition cell at its center is strictly positive.  Finite inverse-transform action equations with independent coins realize all its policy kernels, while \(\texttt{lem:posterior-row-projection}\) realizes all transition posterior rows.  Thus the ball is an open family in the repaired compatible class and every displayed inequality is uniform on it.
