Consider a fully active chain minor with summed chance variables \(Y_0,\ldots,Y_k\) and distinct non-singleton decisions \(Z_1,\ldots,Z_k\), where the stage-\(j\) factor has genuine scope \(\{Y_{j-1},Y_j,Z_j\}\).  Additional factors can only enlarge scopes.

Let \(Z_r\) be the first of these decisions maximized by a legal order.  Before that maximization, both adjacent chance variables in every current factor containing \(Z_r\) must have been summed.  Summing \(Y_r\) combines its incident factors and creates a factor containing \(Z_r,Z_{r+1},Y_{r+1}\) when \(r<k\).  Legality then forces \(Y_{r+1}\) to be summed before \(Z_r\); iterating reaches the right endpoint.  The same argument through \(Y_{r-1}\) reaches the left endpoint.  Hence all chain chance variables are summed before the first decision.  Successive chance elimination takes unions of incident scopes, and genuine dependence rules out numerical scope deletion.  The resulting factor contains \(Z_1,\ldots,Z_k\), so the first decision bucket has at least \(k\) nonglobal live variables.  Therefore
\[
 w_{\mathrm c}(\preceq)\ge k-1. \tag{1}
\]
Taking \(k=|J|\) proves the first assertion.

For every \(H\), take binary states and actions, fixed initial state zero, the all-zero factual state-action trajectory, and
\[
 K_t(y\mid x)=\frac12
 \qquad(t\in[H],\ x\in\mathcal X,\ y\in\mathcal S). \tag{2}
\]
Choose factual and baseline policies with both actions having positive probability at every state and time, implement the factual policy by independent coins \(V_t\), and use fresh independent coins for coalition actions.  Then the observed factual action cells and all transition evidence cells are positive, \(p_t=1/2<1\), and \(J=[H]\).  Every nonfactual posterior row is the full binary simplex, with vertices \(\delta_0,\delta_1\).  The converse in \(\texttt{lem:posterior-row-projection}\) supplies independent response tables over time, so together with these action equations and \(S_1=0\) the example belongs to the repaired compatible class.

At each stage select the row \(x=(0,1)\).  On the positive-probability action-one branch, the transition factor in the compiler genuinely depends on the current state, the next state, and that row selector.  These factors form a fully active chain minor of length \(H\).  Equation (1) gives \(w_{\mathrm c}(\preceq)\ge H-1\) for every legal order.  This property persists on a relative-open neighborhood: strict action and transition positivity persists; \(p_t<1\) persists; and the two binary caps have sum \(1/p_t>1\), so each selected row polytope retains positive length and genuine selector dependence.  Thus fully stochastic growing-horizon classes have \(w_{\mathrm c}(\preceq)=\Omega(H)\), and no bounded or logarithmic constrained-width guarantee holds uniformly.  This is consistent with the sparse theorem, which assumes only \(O(\log H)\) active stages.
