Consider first the stated chain minor with summed chance variables \(Y_0,\ldots,Y_k\) and distinct active decisions \(Z_1,\ldots,Z_k\), where the stage-\(j\) factor has structural scope \(\{Y_{j-1},Y_j,Z_j\}\).  Additional factors or variables can only add scopes, so it suffices to work in the minor.

Let \(Z_r\) be the first of these decisions maximized by a legal order.  Before that maximization, both adjacent chance variables in every current factor containing \(Z_r\) must have been summed.  Summing \(Y_r\) combines the factors on the two incident chain edges and creates a factor whose scope contains \(Z_r\), \(Z_{r+1}\), and \(Y_{r+1}\) when \(r<k\).  Legality now forces \(Y_{r+1}\) to be summed before \(Z_r\); repeating the argument propagates to the right endpoint.  The identical argument through \(Y_{r-1}\) propagates to the left endpoint.  Thus all chain chance variables are summed before the first decision is maximized.  Successive chance elimination takes unions of the incident scopes, so, because the displayed dependences are genuine and the definition of the minor excludes numerical scope deletion, the resulting factor contains every \(Z_1,\ldots,Z_k\).  Equivalently, these decisions form an induced clique.  The bucket of the first decision therefore contains at least \(k\) nonglobal live variables, and the definition of width gives
\[
 w_{\mathrm c}(\preceq)\ge k-1.
 \tag{1}
\]
For \(k=|J|\), this is the first assertion.

To show that the obstruction is realized inside the model rather than only by an abstract graph, fix any \(H\) and take \(\mathcal S=\mathcal A=\{0,1\}\), the all-zero factual state-action trajectory, and
\[
 K_t(y\mid x)=\frac12
 \qquad(t\in[H],\ x\in\mathcal X,\ y\in\mathcal S).
 \tag{2}
\]
Choose factual and baseline policies with both actions having positive probability at every state and time.  Then \(p_t=1/2<1\), so \(J=[H]\).  For every nonfactual row, the posterior constraints are
\[
 \frac12q_{t,x}(y)\le\frac12,
 \qquad q_{t,x}(0)+q_{t,x}(1)=1,
\]
and hence the row polytope is the full binary simplex with distinct vertices \(\delta_0\) and \(\delta_1\).  In particular the fiber is nonempty, and the converse direction of \(\texttt{lem:posterior-row-projection}\) supplies compatible response-table laws, independently sampled over time, so the example belongs to the stated Markov SCM class.  Select the row \(x=(0,1)\) at each stage.  In the factor circuit proved in \(\texttt{thm:optimization-preserving-compiler}\), after restricting the positive-probability action branch to \(A_t=1\), its transition factor genuinely depends on \(S_t\), \(S_{t+1}\), and that stage's row selector.  These factors form a fully active state-chain minor of length \(H\).  Equation (1) yields \(w_{\mathrm c}(\preceq)\ge H-1\) for every legal order.  This obstruction persists under sufficiently small perturbations: strict positivity of both entries of each binary transition row and the inequality \(p_t<1\) imply that the two caps have sum \(1/p_t>1\), so their intersection with the binary simplex has positive length; strict action positivity and genuine factor dependence also persist.  The primitives in (2) and the policies are strictly interior, and hence they have a relative-open neighborhood of fully active chain instances.  Thus no bounded- or logarithmic-width guarantee holds uniformly over fully stochastic growing horizons.  This does not contradict the sparse result, whose premise requires only \(O(\log H)\) stages with \(p_t<1\).
