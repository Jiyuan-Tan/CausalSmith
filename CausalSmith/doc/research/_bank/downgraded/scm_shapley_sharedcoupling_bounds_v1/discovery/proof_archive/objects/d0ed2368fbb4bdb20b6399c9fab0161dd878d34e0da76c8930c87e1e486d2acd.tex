By \(\texttt{def:posterior-row-fiber}\), choose \(q_{t,x_t}=\delta_{y_t}\) and \(q_{t,x}=K_t(\cdot\mid x)\) for \(x\ne x_t\).  Since \(K_t(\cdot\mid x)\) is a probability mass function and \(0<p_t\le 1\) by \(\texttt{ass:factual-positivity}\),
\[
 p_tq_{t,x}(y)=p_tK_t(y\mid x)\le K_t(y\mid x),
\]
so this choice belongs to \(\mathcal B(K,\tau)\).  Thus the fiber is nonempty.  Each nonfactual row factor is the intersection of the finite-dimensional simplex \(\Delta(\mathcal S)\) with the finitely many closed half-spaces \(p_tq_{t,x}(y)\le K_t(y\mid x)\); each factual row factor is the singleton \(\{\delta_{y_t}\}\).  Because \(\mathcal S\), \(\mathcal X\), and \([H]\) are finite, \(\mathcal B(K,\tau)\) is a compact convex polytope.

Fix a coalition \(C\).  Expanding the finite-state Markov recursion under the known policy \(\alpha^C\) expresses \(g_C(q)\) as a finite sum over state-action paths.  Each summand is a known reward multiplied by known policy probabilities and by exactly one coordinate of a posterior transition row at each time.  Therefore \(g_C(q)\) is a polynomial in the coordinates of \(q\), and it is affine when all but any one row \(q_{t,x}\) are held fixed.  By \(\texttt{def:shapley-coordinate}\), the finite weighted sum \(\Phi_i(q)\) is likewise continuous on the fiber and affine in each row separately.

The two directions of \(\texttt{lem:posterior-row-projection}\) give the equality
\[
 \Theta_i(K,\tau)
 =\{\Phi_i(q):q\in\mathcal B(K,\tau)\}.
 \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, the forward direction maps the posterior of every compatible SCM to a point of the displayed fiber, and the converse direction realizes every point of that fiber by timewise independent response-table laws shared across all coalition worlds.  Compactness of the fiber and continuity of \(\Phi_i\) give minimizers \(q^-\) and maximizers \(q^+\).  By \(\texttt{def:shared-endpoints}\), their values are \(L_i(K,\tau)\) and \(U_i(K,\tau)\).

For \(s\in[0,1]\), convexity makes \(q(s)=(1-s)q^-+sq^+\) feasible, and \(f(s)=\Phi_i(q(s))\) is continuous.  If \(c\) lies between \(f(0)\) and \(f(1)\), repeatedly retain a closed half-interval whose two endpoint values bracket \(c\).  The resulting nested intervals have lengths tending to zero and therefore have a common point \(s_*\).  Continuity rules out both \(f(s_*)<c\) and \(f(s_*)>c\), so \(f(s_*)=c\).  Hence the right side of (1) contains every point of \([L_i(K,\tau),U_i(K,\tau)]\); the reverse containment follows from the definitions of the extrema.  This proves
\[
 \Theta_i(K,\tau)=[L_i(K,\tau),U_i(K,\tau)].
\]
Applying the converse part of \(\texttt{lem:posterior-row-projection}\) to \(q^-\), \(q^+\), or \(q(s_*)\) gives compatible SCMs realizing the corresponding values.  In particular, both endpoints are attained without introducing a disturbance shared across times.

It remains to prove the vertex assertion.  Start with an endpoint optimizer and fix one row factor.  Since \(\Phi_i\) is affine in this row when all other rows are fixed, the optimizer's current row minimizes, or respectively maximizes, the resulting affine functional on that factor.  If an optimizing row is not extreme, take a line segment in the factor having that row in its relative interior and extend it maximally within the factor.  Both endpoints are feasible and lie in proper faces.  Affineness and optimality force both endpoints to have the same endpoint value, because their weighted average has the optimal value and neither can improve it.  Replace the row by either endpoint and repeat within its proper face.  The face dimension strictly decreases at each repetition, so finite dimensionality makes the process end at a vertex with the same value.  Iterating this replacement through the finitely many pairs \((t,x)\) preserves all vertices already selected and ends with an endpoint optimizer whose every nonfactual row is a vertex; every factual singleton row is already a vertex.

Finally, the converse construction in \(\texttt{lem:posterior-row-projection}\) is a compact conditional sampler.  At time \(t\), store the factual marginal \(K_t(\cdot\mid x_t)\), the selected posterior rows \(q_{t,x}\), and, when \(p_t<1\), the residual rows
\[
 h_{t,x}(y)=\frac{K_t(y\mid x)-p_tq_{t,x}(y)}{1-p_t},
 \qquad x\ne x_t.
\]
The fiber inequalities make every numerator nonnegative, and summing over \(y\) gives one, so each residual row is a probability mass function.  Sample the factual coordinate first; conditionally on its equaling \(y_t\), sample the other coordinates from the stored \(q_{t,x}\), and otherwise sample them from the residual rows.  When \(p_t=1\), the fiber constraints and equality of total masses force \(q_{t,x}=K_t(\cdot\mid x)\), so no residual row is needed.  Sampling these response tables independently over \(t\), as licensed by \(\texttt{lem:posterior-row-projection}\), returns a member of \(\mathfrak M(K)\).  The sampler stores \(O(H|\mathcal X||\mathcal S|)\) probabilities and never lists the generally exponential mass table on \(\mathcal S^{\mathcal X}\).
