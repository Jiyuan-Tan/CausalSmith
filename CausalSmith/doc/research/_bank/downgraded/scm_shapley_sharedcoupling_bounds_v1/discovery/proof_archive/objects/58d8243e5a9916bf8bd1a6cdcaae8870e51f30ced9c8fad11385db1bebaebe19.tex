In \(\mathfrak G_\varepsilon\), \(p_1=p_2=1/2\).  For a binary nonfactual row with marginal \((1/2,1/2)\), \(\texttt{def:posterior-row-fiber}\) imposes only
\[
 \tfrac12q(0)\le\tfrac12,\qquad \tfrac12q(1)\le\tfrac12,
 \qquad q(0)+q(1)=1,
\]
so its success coordinate ranges over \([0,1]\).  Product structure makes \(u,w,z,v\) independent coordinates of \([0,1]^4\), while both factual rows are \(\delta_0\).

The first reward is zero and the second is the terminal next state.  Under the externally randomized policies in the repaired model class, direct recursion gives
\[
 g_\varnothing=0,
 \qquad g_{\{1\}}=\varepsilon uz,
 \qquad g_{\{2\}}=w,
 \qquad g_{\{1,2\}}=(1-\varepsilon u)w+\varepsilon uv. \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, the time-one baseline chooses action one with probability \(\varepsilon\); conditional on that action it reaches state one with probability \(u\).  The retained time-two action is zero, with success probability \(z\), whereas a time-two switch uses action one, with success probability \(w\) from state zero and \(v\) from state one.  For \(H=2\), both Shapley weights equal \(1/2\), so (1) and \(\texttt{def:shapley-coordinate}\) yield
\[
 \Phi_1(q)=\frac12\{g_\varnothing-g_{\{1\}}+g_{\{2\}}-g_{\{1,2\}}\}
 =\frac{\varepsilon u}{2}(w-z-v). \tag{2}
\]
The maximum of (2) is \(\varepsilon/2\), attained at \((u,w,z,v)=(1,1,0,0)\), and its minimum is \(-\varepsilon\), attained at \((1,0,1,1)\).  By \(\texttt{thm:sharp-shared-interval}\), the gluing sampler, completed by the external policy coins, gives compatible shared-SCM witnesses for both assignments.

The internal relaxation gives each distinct coalition return its own posterior-kernel copy.  Equations (1) imply
\[
 g_\varnothing\in\{0\},\qquad g_{\{1\}}\in[0,\varepsilon],
 \qquad g_{\{2\}}\in[0,1],\qquad g_{\{1,2\}}\in[0,1],
\]
and every displayed endpoint is attainable in its respective copy.  Substitution into \(\texttt{def:independent-coalition-outer-bound}\) gives
\[
 \mathcal I_1^{\mathrm{ind}}(K,\tau)
 =\left[-\frac{1+\varepsilon}{2},\frac12\right].
\]
Both excess widths relative to \([-\varepsilon,\varepsilon/2]\) equal \((1-\varepsilon)/2>0\).

At \(\varepsilon=1/4\), the positive-gap set contains policy-interior points arbitrarily close to the displayed boundary tuple.  Keep \(K,r\), and the time-one baseline fixed, and, for \(0\le c\le1/64\), set \(\pi_t(1\mid s)=c\) and \(\pi_{\mathrm{base},2}(1\mid s)=1-c\) for every \(t,s\).  Repeating the recursion with \(\eta=c\) and \(\beta=1-c\) gives
\[
 [L_1,U_1]=\left[-\frac14,\frac{1-c}{8}\right],
 \qquad
 \mathcal I_1^{\mathrm{ind}}
 =\left[-\frac58+\frac{3c}{8},\frac{1-c}{2}\right]. \tag{3}
\]
Both excess widths in (3) equal \(3(1-c)/8>0\), and every policy cell is positive when \(c>0\).

The two excess-width maps are continuous on a relative neighborhood with positive factual transition cells.  To see this directly, \(\texttt{thm:local-positivity-modulus}\) controls the shared endpoints under changes of \(K\).  With a posterior fiber fixed, every coalition return is a finite polynomial in rewards, policies, and posterior rows, hence uniformly continuous on the finite compact product.  The row-matching construction in the modulus proof gives Hausdorff continuity of the posterior fibers, and matching an optimizer in both directions transfers uniform continuity to every coalition-return infimum and supremum.  Finite sums and differences preserve continuity.  Since the two slacks at the boundary tuple equal \(3/8\), their positivity set contains a relative-open neighborhood of that tuple; equation (3) shows that this neighborhood may be chosen to meet the policy interior.

The explicit interior center \(\theta^\star\) in \(\texttt{def:quantitative-gap-neighborhood}\) is the point \(c=1/64\) on this segment.  The theorem \(\texttt{thm:quantitative-gap-neighborhood}\) further proves that every primitive tuple in the relative-open ball \(\mathcal N_\rho(\theta^\star)\) has positive factual mass and both excess widths greater than \(1/4\).  Finite inverse-transform action maps and independent coins realize every policy in that ball, and the transition gluing theorem realizes every posterior optimizer.  Thus the strict separation persists on the claimed open family inside the repaired SCM class, without varying policy-response or reward-noise coupling.
