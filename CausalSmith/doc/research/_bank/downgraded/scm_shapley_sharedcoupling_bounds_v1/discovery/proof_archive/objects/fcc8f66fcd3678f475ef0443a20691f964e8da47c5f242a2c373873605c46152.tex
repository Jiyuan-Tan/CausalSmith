Fix \(i\in[H]\) and \(\sigma\in\{-1,+1\}\).  The sign is fixed throughout the construction; in particular, it is not a chance or decision variable.

We first reduce each row factor of \(\mathcal B(K,\tau)\) to a finite decision alphabet.  For \(x\ne x_t\), define
\[
 u_{t,x}(y)=\min\!\left\{1,\frac{K_t(y\mid x)}{p_t}\right\},
 \qquad y\in\mathcal S.
\]
The division is valid by \(\texttt{ass:factual-positivity}\).  The row factor in \(\texttt{def:posterior-row-fiber}\) is exactly the capped simplex
\[
 \mathcal P_{t,x}=\left\{v\in\mathbb R^{\mathcal S}:
 0\le v(y)\le u_{t,x}(y),\quad
 \sum_{y\in\mathcal S}v(y)=1\right\}.
 \tag{1}
\]
It is nonempty.  Indeed, \(\sum_yK_t(y\mid x)/p_t=1/p_t\ge1\); if one untruncated cap is at least one then the sum of the truncated caps is at least one, while if none is at least one truncation changes no cap.  Thus \(\sum_yu_{t,x}(y)\ge1\), which permits mass one to be filled beneath the caps.  The factual-row factor is the singleton \(\{\delta_{y_t}\}\).

A point \(v\) of (1) is a vertex if and only if all but at most one of its coordinates are in \(\{0,u_{t,x}(y)\}\).  If two coordinates are strictly between their bounds, a sufficiently small transfer of mass between them gives two distinct feasible points having midpoint \(v\), so \(v\) is not a vertex.  Conversely, in any convex decomposition of a point having all but at most one coordinate at a bound, every bound coordinate is fixed in both summands, and the sum-to-one constraint fixes the possible remaining coordinate.  This proves the characterization.

The vertices can be listed exactly in output-sensitive time.  A first vertex is obtained by filling coordinates in a fixed order up to their caps until the residual mass fits in one coordinate.  From a listed vertex, test every ordered transfer direction \(e_y-e_{y'}\) that is locally feasible, move maximally in that direction, and retain the endpoint when it satisfies the preceding vertex characterization.  These tests include every incident edge: at a vertex, an edge can free at most two box constraints inside the codimension-one sum hyperplane and hence has such a transfer direction.  The traversal reaches every vertex.  To see this without invoking an external connectivity result, fix a target vertex \(v^*\), discard zero-cap coordinates, and give coefficient \(1\) to a coordinate at its upper bound in \(v^*\), coefficient \(-1\) to a coordinate equal to zero in \(v^*\), and coefficient zero to the possible unique non-bound coordinate.  The resulting linear functional has unique maximizer \(v^*\).  At any other vertex \(v\), the feasible direction \(v^*-v\) decomposes into transfers from coordinates that decrease to coordinates that increase.  If \(v\) has a unique non-bound coordinate, a transfer between two bound coordinates is split into two transfers through that non-bound coordinate; both split directions are locally feasible.  Since the functional increases strictly along \(v^*-v\), it increases along at least one of these locally feasible transfer directions.  Moving maximally along that direction reaches another vertex and strictly increases the functional.  Finiteness therefore forces arrival at \(v^*\).  With duplicate suppression, the listing uses \(O(|\mathcal S|^2v_{t,x})\) rational operations when the row has \(v_{t,x}\) vertices.  Every listed coordinate is zero, a cap, or one minus a sum of caps, and consequently has rational bit length polynomial in the rational input length.

Let \(Z_{t,x}\) select one listed vertex, denoted \(q^{Z}_{t,x}\), including a one-point alphabet for every singleton row.  By \(\texttt{thm:sharp-shared-interval}\), the relevant extremum has an optimizer that is a vertex in every row.  Together with the affine identity in \(\texttt{prop:affine-nonnegative-lift}\), this gives
\[
 V_\sigma=\max_z F_i^\sigma(q^z),
 \tag{2}
\]
where \(z\) is one joint assignment of all row selectors.  Thus (2) is a finite outer maximization, and its single joint assignment already enforces sharing across every coalition world.

We next give the promised nonnegative circuit.  Exact quadrature applied to the constant polynomial gives \(\sum_{j=1}^H\nu_j=1\), so \(C_\nu=\sum_j|\nu_j|\ge1\).  Put
\[
 \rho_{jb}=\frac{|a_{jb}^\sigma|}{2C_\nu},
 \qquad
 \eta_{jb}=
 \begin{cases}
 +,&a_{jb}^\sigma\ge0,\\
 -,&a_{jb}^\sigma<0.
 \end{cases}
\]
Since \(|a_{j0}^\sigma|=|a_{j1}^\sigma|=|\nu_j|\), one has \(\sum_{j,b}\rho_{jb}=1\).  Introduce one global chance variable \(W=(J,B,T)\) with alphabet \([H]\times\{0,1\}\times[H]\) and law
\[
 \Pr\{W=(j,b,t)\}=\frac{\rho_{jb}}{H}.
 \tag{3}
\]
Conditional on \((J,B)=(j,b)\), use at time \(t\) the action kernel
\[
 \gamma_t^{jb}(a\mid s)=
 \begin{cases}
 \alpha_i^b(a\mid s),&t=i,\\
 (1-\zeta_j)\alpha_t^0(a\mid s)+\zeta_j\alpha_t^1(a\mid s),&t\ne i.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{4}
\]
For every row insert the transition factor
\[
 \psi_{t,x}(S_t,A_t,S_{t+1},Z_{t,x})=
 \begin{cases}
 q^{Z}_{t,x}(S_{t+1}),&(S_t,A_t)=x,\\
 1,&(S_t,A_t)\ne x,
 \end{cases}
 \tag{5}
\]
and insert the initial factor \(\mathbf 1\{S_1=s_1\}\).  Finally, for each reward time insert
\[
 \chi_t(W,S_t,A_t,S_{t+1})=
 \begin{cases}
 Hh_t^{\eta_{JB}}((S_t,A_t),S_{t+1}),&T=t,\\
 1,&T\ne t.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{6}
\]
All factors in (3)--(6) are rational and nonnegative: rationality follows from the theorem's input conditions, and nonnegativity of the reward leaves follows from \(R>0\), the definitions of \(m_t,M_t\), and \(\texttt{prop:affine-nonnegative-lift}\).  There is exactly one variable \(Z_{t,x}\) in (5) for each row.  It is not copied when \(W\), a state path, or a reward time varies.

Fix a selector assignment \(z\), and sum the product of (3)--(6), the policy factors (4), and the initial factor over \(W\), all action variables, and \(S_2,\ldots,S_{H+1}\).  Call the result \(T_\sigma(z)\).  For a realized \((S_t,A_t)\), the product of (5) over \(x\in\mathcal X\) is exactly \(q^z_{t,(S_t,A_t)}(S_{t+1})\).  Hence the state-action sum is the Markov expectation under the single kernel \(q^z\).  Averaging (6) over \(T\), and then (3) over \((J,B)\), yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 T_\sigma(z)
 &=\sum_{j=1}^H\sum_{b=0}^1\rho_{jb}
   \mathbb E_{q^z}\!\left[
    \sum_{t=1}^Hh_t^{\eta_{jb}}((S_t,A_t),S_{t+1})
    \mathrel{\big|}S_1=s_1,\gamma^{jb}
   \right]\\
 &= \frac{1}{2C_\nu}F_i^\sigma(q^z).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{7}
\]
For \(\eta_{jb}=+\), the expectation in (7) is \(h_{jb}(q^z)\) by \(\texttt{def:nonnegative-endpoint-objectives}\).  For \(\eta_{jb}=-\), the pathwise complementary-leaf identity in \(\texttt{prop:affine-nonnegative-lift}\) makes it \(1-h_{jb}(q^z)\).  Thus the displayed factor circuit followed by deterministic multiplication by \(2C_\nu\) realizes \(F_i^\sigma\) exactly.  This is the circuit \(\mathfrak F_i^\sigma\) required by \(\texttt{def:compiler-handle}\).

We now prove optimization preservation for every legal order \(\preceq\).  Bucket elimination maintains a multiset of nonnegative factors; summation and maximization preserve nonnegativity.  Summing a chance variable is exact by distributivity, because the factors outside its bucket do not contain that variable.  Suppose next that a decision variable \(Z\) is maximized.  Multiply all current factors containing \(Z\) into \(A(Z,Y)\), where \(Y\) contains the other uneliminated decision variables in that bucket.  By legality, no uneliminated chance variable occurs in the scope of \(A\).  The product of all factors outside the bucket can be denoted \(B(X,Y,Y')\), where \(X\) collects the remaining chance variables, \(Y'\) the remaining decisions not in the bucket, and \(B\) does not involve \(Z\).  Pointwise in \((X,Y,Y')\), nonnegativity gives
\[
 \max_Z\{A(Z,Y)B(X,Y,Y')\}
 =B(X,Y,Y')\max_ZA(Z,Y).
 \tag{8}
\]
Equation (8) shows that this decision maximization commutes outward past every still-unperformed chance summation: the choice of \(Z\) cannot depend on \(X\), and the nonnegative multiplier \(B\) cannot reverse the ordering of the values of \(A\).  Maxima over finite decision alphabets commute with one another.  Induction over the buckets, using distributivity at chance buckets and (8) at decision buckets, therefore transforms the value returned by any legal order into the canonical value
\[
 2C_\nu\max_z\sum_{W,S_2,\ldots,S_{H+1},A_1,\ldots,A_H}
       \mathfrak F_i^\sigma(z,W,S,A)
 =\max_zF_i^\sigma(q^z)=V_\sigma,
 \tag{9}
\]
where the first equality uses (7) and the second uses (2).  This proves the mixed-elimination invariant and, importantly, retains one outer row assignment rather than a chance-realization-dependent assignment.

We next count arithmetic operations.  A bucket contains at most \(w_{\mathrm c}(\preceq)+1\) nonglobal live variables by definition, each with alphabet at most \(d\).  The only global variable is \(W\), with \(2H^2\) values.  Thus a full bucket table has at most
\[
 2H^2d^{w_{\mathrm c}(\preceq)+1}
 \tag{10}
\]
entries; if \(W\) is absent, this remains an upper bound.  Multiplication and elimination use a constant number of rational operations per entry per factor consumed.  Every initial factor is consumed once, and every eliminated variable creates at most one new factor, which is itself consumed once.  Since \(N\) bounds the number of variables plus initial factors, summing bucket-factor counts over the run is \(O(N)\).  Equations (9)--(10) consequently give total elimination cost
\[
 O\!\left(H^2N d^{w_{\mathrm c}(\preceq)+1}\right).
 \tag{11}
\]
The vertex-listing preprocessing costs \(O(N|\mathcal S|^2d)\).  If there is a non-singleton selector, its transition factor (5) contains that selector together with the current state, action, and next state, so the maximum bucket scope gives \(w_{\mathrm c}(\preceq)+1\ge3\); because \(|\mathcal S|\le d\), the preprocessing is absorbed by (11).  If all row factors are singletons, no nontrivial vertex listing is required.  Construction of the remaining factors is also absorbed by (11).

For completeness, let \(B_0\) be the total binary length of the rational input arrays and rational quadrature data.  Every cap in (1) has bit length polynomial in \(B_0\), and every vertex coordinate is zero, a cap, or one minus a sum of at most \(|\mathcal S|\) caps.  Hence all entries of the initial circuit have polynomial bit length.  More generally, for each fixed remaining decision context, every intermediate entry is a sum over assignments of eliminated chance variables of products of initial factor entries, with each initial factor used at most once in each product; a maximization selects one of the finitely many candidate rationals and does not add their denominators.  A common denominator for all such products may be formed from the denominators of the primitive rational inputs and row caps, with exponent at most the number of factors, so its bit length is polynomial in \(B_0+N\).  The number of chance assignments in an expanded sum is at most \((2H^2)d^N\), which adds only \(O(\log H+N\log d)\) numerator bits.  Finally, the vertex characterization gives at most \(|\mathcal S|2^{|\mathcal S|-1}\) vertices per row, so \(\log d=O(|\mathcal S|)\), itself bounded by the explicit input length.  Therefore every reduced intermediate rational and the exact output rational have bit length polynomial in \(B_0\) and \(N\).  Rounding an exact output to a requested binary precision adds only the requested number of output bits.

At a maximization bucket, store a maximizing value of the eliminated selector for each assignment of the later decision variables in the output scope, breaking ties in a fixed order.  Reverse-order backtracking fixes those later variables before consulting the stored value.  Because legality removed all chance variables from a maximization bucket, no stored choice depends on \(W\), a state, or an action realization.  Thus \(\mathsf{BP}_\sigma\) recovers one joint row assignment \(z_\sigma\), shared across all quadrature and forced-membership branches and all paths.

It remains to turn this compact assignment into an endpoint SCM.  The construction can be written without an exponential response-table mass array.  At time \(t\), draw \(E_t\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p_t)\).  Conditional on \(E_t=1\), set the factual coordinate of \(R_t\) equal to \(y_t\) and draw each nonfactual coordinate from \(q^{z_\sigma}_{t,x}\).  If \(p_t<1\), conditional on \(E_t=0\), draw the factual coordinate from
\[
 \frac{K_t(y\mid x_t)-p_t\mathbf 1\{y=y_t\}}{1-p_t}
\]
and each nonfactual coordinate \(x\ne x_t\) from
\[
 \frac{K_t(y\mid x)-p_tq^{z_\sigma}_{t,x}(y)}{1-p_t}.
 \tag{12}
\]
Every numerator in (12) is nonnegative by membership in \(\mathcal B(K,\tau)\), and each displayed distribution sums to one.  The residual factual distribution assigns zero mass to \(y_t\), so conditioning on the factual event makes \(E_t=1\) and gives precisely the selected posterior rows.  Mixing the two branches gives unconditional marginal \(K_t(\cdot\mid x)\) for every row.  If \(p_t=1\), the residual branch is absent; the coordinatewise fiber inequalities and equality of total masses force \(q^{z_\sigma}_{t,x}=K_t(\cdot\mid x)\) for every nonfactual row.  Sample these response tables independently across \(t\).  The structural equations, transition marginals, and independent-time condition then hold, so this is a member of \(\mathfrak M(K)\), consistently with the converse construction in \(\texttt{lem:posterior-row-projection}\).  It is stored by the branch probabilities and row distributions in (12), requiring only a compact conditional sampler.

Finally, (9) gives \(V_{+1}\) and \(V_{-1}\) for the two separate fixed-sign runs.  By \(\texttt{prop:affine-nonnegative-lift}\),
\[
 U_i(K,\tau)=R(V_{+1}-C_\nu),
 \qquad
 L_i(K,\tau)=R(C_\nu-V_{-1}).
\]
The two backtracked SCMs attain these endpoints, respectively, and each uses one joint row-vertex assignment shared by every coalition world.  This proves every assertion.
