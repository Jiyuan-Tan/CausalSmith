Let \(P_n\in\mathcal P_n\) satisfy, in addition, conditions (i)--(v) of Choi and Yuan (2024), Theorem 3.1, and specialize to \(q=0\), \(H=1\), and \(W=\{t_0\}\). Then \(B_2=o_{\mathbb P}(s)\); the complete score \(L\) differs by \(o_{\mathbb P}(s)\) from the two-component Choi--Yuan score; the ratio of \(s^2\) to their displayed variance converges to one; and the resulting standardized leading score converges in distribution to \(\mathcal N(0,1)\). This is a reduction on the explicit intersection of the two sets of conditions, not an assertion that the Choi--Yuan model class and \(\mathcal P_n\) coincide. In this intersection-reduction sense, the construction is an extension of the balanced, fixed-rank, common-start, singleton-subgroup Choi--Yuan regime along the serial-dependence and growing-window axes, under a strengthened uniform class.

%%% FIELD reduction-proof
Fix a sequence \(P_n\in\mathcal P_n\) that also satisfies conditions (i)--(v) of Choi and Yuan (2024), Theorem 3.1.  Put \(W=\{t_0\}\), abbreviate \(A=A_{t_0}\) and \(B=B_S\), and define
\[
 \bar x_G=\frac1g\sum_{i\in G}x_i.
\]
The donor and pretreatment restricted-Gram assumptions give \(A\succ0\) and \(B\succ0\), so every inverse below exists.  By the definition of the oracle score and the complete-tangent lemma,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 L
 &=\frac1g\sum_{i\in G}\ell_{i t_0}(R_{i t_0}\epsilon)\\
 &=\frac1g\sum_{i\in G}\sum_{j\in C(t_0)}
       x_i^{\top}A^{-1}x_j\epsilon_{j,t_0}
   +\frac1g\sum_{i\in G}\sum_{u\in S}
       f_{t_0}^{\top}B^{-1}f_u\epsilon_{iu}\\
 &\quad
   -\frac1g\sum_{i\in G}\sum_{j\in C(t_0)}\sum_{u\in S}
       x_i^{\top}A^{-1}x_j
       f_{t_0}^{\top}B^{-1}f_u\epsilon_{ju}\\
 &=\mathcal D_n+\mathcal T_n-\mathcal O_n,
 \end{aligned}
\]
where
\[
 \mathcal D_n
 =\sum_{j\in C(t_0)}\bar x_G^{\top}A^{-1}x_j\epsilon_{j,t_0},
 \qquad
 \mathcal T_n
 =\frac1g\sum_{i\in G}\sum_{u\in S}
       f_{t_0}^{\top}B^{-1}f_u\epsilon_{iu},
\]
and \(\mathcal O_n\) is the donor--pretreatment overlap score in the one-date curvature-and-overlap lemma.  In particular, the negative overlap is an exact part of the differential, rather than a term suppressed by definition.

Because \(q=0\), conditional block dependence says that distinct dates within a row are conditionally independent given \(M\); conditional row independence gives the same conclusion across distinct rows.  The common-start mask makes the three coordinate sets
\[
 C(t_0)\times\{t_0\},
 \qquad G\times S,
 \qquad C(t_0)\times S
\]
pairwise disjoint.  Conditional centering and common stationary covariance therefore imply that \(\mathcal D_n\), \(\mathcal T_n\), and \(\mathcal O_n\) are conditionally centered and mutually conditionally independent.  From \(A=\sum_{j\in C(t_0)}x_jx_j^{\top}\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Var}(\mathcal D_n\mid M,\Omega)
 &=\gamma(0)\sum_{j\in C(t_0)}
    \bigl(\bar x_G^{\top}A^{-1}x_j\bigr)^2\\
 &=\gamma(0)\bar x_G^{\top}A^{-1}
    \left(\sum_{j\in C(t_0)}x_jx_j^{\top}\right)
    A^{-1}\bar x_G\\
 &=\gamma(0)\bar x_G^{\top}A^{-1}\bar x_G.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Likewise, using \(B=\sum_{u\in S}f_uf_u^{\top}\) and independence of the \(g\) treated rows,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Var}(\mathcal T_n\mid M,\Omega)
 &=\frac{\gamma(0)}{g^2}\sum_{i\in G}\sum_{u\in S}
   \bigl(f_{t_0}^{\top}B^{-1}f_u\bigr)^2\\
 &=\frac{\gamma(0)}g f_{t_0}^{\top}B^{-1}
   \left(\sum_{u\in S}f_uf_u^{\top}\right)
   B^{-1}f_{t_0}\\
 &=\frac{\gamma(0)}g f_{t_0}^{\top}B^{-1}f_{t_0}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Consequently the variance of the two-component score is
\[
 V_{\mathrm{CY}}
 :=\operatorname{Var}(\mathcal D_n+\mathcal T_n\mid M,\Omega)
 =\gamma(0)\left\{
    \bar x_G^{\top}A^{-1}\bar x_G
    +\frac1g f_{t_0}^{\top}B^{-1}f_{t_0}
  \right\}.
\]

This is the variance displayed by Choi and Yuan, expressed in the present factor normalization.  To verify the equality, let
\[
 \bar U_G=\frac1g\sum_{i\in G}U_{i\cdot}^{\top}.
\]
The definitions \(X=UD^{1/2}\) and \(F=VD^{1/2}\) give
\[
 A=D^{1/2}\left(\sum_{j\in C(t_0)}
 U_{j\cdot}^{\top}U_{j\cdot}\right)D^{1/2},
 \qquad \bar x_G=D^{1/2}\bar U_G,
\]
and hence
\[
 \bar x_G^{\top}A^{-1}\bar x_G
 =\bar U_G^{\top}
  \left(\sum_{j\in C(t_0)}U_{j\cdot}^{\top}U_{j\cdot}\right)^{-1}
  \bar U_G.
\]
Similarly,
\[
 f_{t_0}^{\top}B^{-1}f_{t_0}
 =V_{t_0\cdot}
  \left(\sum_{u\in S}V_{u\cdot}^{\top}V_{u\cdot}\right)^{-1}
  V_{t_0\cdot}^{\top}.
\]
Thus \(V_{\mathrm{CY}}\) is exactly the sum of the donor-loading quadratic form and the group-size-scaled pretreatment-factor quadratic form appearing in the cited theorem.

The oracle-variance definition and the preceding conditional independence yield
\[
 s^2
 =\operatorname{Var}(L\mid M,\Omega)
 =V_{\mathrm{CY}}+operatorname{Var}(\mathcal O_n\mid M,\Omega).
\]
The one-date curvature-and-overlap lemma gives, uniformly on \(\mathcal P_n\),
\[
 \mathcal O_n=o_{\mathbb P}(s),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(\mathcal O_n\mid M,\Omega)=O(n^{-2}),
 \qquad
 B_2=o_{\mathbb P}(s).
\]
Its variance comparison uses the declared nondegeneracy bound \(s^2\ge c_s/n\) at \(H=1\).  Therefore
\[
 0\le
 \frac{\operatorname{Var}(\mathcal O_n\mid M,\Omega)}{s^2}
 \le \frac{C n^{-2}}{c_s n^{-1}}
 =O(n^{-1})\longrightarrow0,
\]
and hence
\[
 \frac{V_{\mathrm{CY}}}{s^2}\longrightarrow1,
 \qquad
 \frac{s^2}{V_{\mathrm{CY}}}\longrightarrow1.
\]

On the imposed intersection, the cited Choi--Yuan theorem applies and gives
\[
 \frac{\mathcal D_n+\mathcal T_n}{\sqrt{V_{\mathrm{CY}}}}
 \ \Rightarrow\ N(0,1).
\]
Combining this result with the exact score decomposition gives
\[
 \frac{L}{s}
 =\frac{\mathcal D_n+\mathcal T_n}{\sqrt{V_{\mathrm{CY}}}}
   \sqrt{\frac{V_{\mathrm{CY}}}{s^2}}
   -\frac{\mathcal O_n}{s}
 =Z_n+R_n,
\]
where
\[
 Z_n=\frac{\mathcal D_n+\mathcal T_n}{\sqrt{V_{\mathrm{CY}}}}
 \Rightarrow \mathcal N(0,1)
\]
and
\[
 R_n=
 \left(\sqrt{\frac{V_{\mathrm{CY}}}{s^2}}-1\right)Z_n
 -\frac{\mathcal O_n}{s}
 =o_{\mathbb P}(1).
\]
Indeed, convergence of \(Z_n\) to a proper distribution makes \(Z_n=O_{\mathbb P}(1)\), while the multiplier in parentheses converges to zero.  To verify the last distributional step without adding any further premise, let \(\Phi\) be the standard-normal distribution function.  For every \(x\in\mathbb R\) and \(\delta>0\),
\[
 \mathbb P(Z_n\le x-\delta)-\mathbb P(|R_n|>\delta)
 \le \mathbb P(Z_n+R_n\le x)
 \le \mathbb P(Z_n\le x+\delta)+\mathbb P(|R_n|>\delta).
\]
Taking lower and upper limits and then letting \(\delta\downarrow0\), continuity of \(\Phi\) gives \(L/s\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1)\).  Since \(B_2/s=o_{\mathbb P}(1)\), the same sandwich argument shows that adding or subtracting the curvature drift does not change this limit.  This proves the estimator-and-limit reduction on the explicit intersection.  The argument has nowhere identified the two assumption classes: the final extension language refers only to retaining this intersection benchmark while allowing the present construction's serial-dependence and growing-window axes under its strengthened uniform assumptions.
