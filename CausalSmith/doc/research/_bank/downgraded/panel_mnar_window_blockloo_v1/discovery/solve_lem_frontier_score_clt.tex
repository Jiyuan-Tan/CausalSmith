Fix a compact set \(\mathcal K\subset [1/2,1]\times(0,1]\times[0,1]\), and condition throughout on \((M,\Omega)\).  By \(\mathrm{ass:deterministic\mbox{-}mask}\), conditioning on \(\Omega\) adds no randomness after conditioning on \(M\).  The oracle definition gives
\[
 \frac{L}{s}=\sum_{j\in I}\sum_{u\in J}X_{ju},
 \qquad
 X_{ju}:=\frac{w_{ju}\epsilon_{ju}}{s}.
\]
The weights are fixed under the conditional law.  By \(\mathrm{ass:centered\mbox{-}noise}\), \(\mathbb E[X_{ju}\mid M,\Omega]=0\).  By \(\mathrm{ass:independent\mbox{-}rows}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:conditional\mbox{-}block\mbox{-}dependence}\), two vertices \((j,u)\) and \((k,v)\) can be conditionally dependent only if \(j=k\) and \(|u-v|\le q\).  Thus the variables have a dependency graph of maximum degree at most \(2q\), where \(q\) is fixed by \(\mathrm{ass:fixed\mbox{-}known\mbox{-}range}\).

The added member property \(\mathrm{ass:conditional\mbox{-}subgaussian\mbox{-}noise}\) states that, almost surely,
\[
 \sup_{j,u}\mathbb E\!\left[\exp\!\left(\epsilon_{ju}^{2}/K^{2}\right)\middle|M\right]\le 2.
\]
Because \(\Omega\) is deterministic, the same inequality holds conditional on \((M,\Omega)\).  Markov's inequality and the tail integral formula therefore give, for every \(p>0\),
\[
 \mathbb P(|\epsilon_{ju}|>x\mid M,\Omega)
 \le 2e^{-x^{2}/K^{2}},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E(|\epsilon_{ju}|^{p}\mid M,\Omega)
 \le pK^{p}\Gamma(p/2)=:C_pK^p.
\tag{1}
\]
The exact value of \(C_p\) is immaterial and is uniform in the law and the exponent triple.

Set
\[
 A_n:=\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_n(a,b,c),\,(a,b,c)\in\mathcal K}
       \frac{\sum_{j,u}w_{ju}^{2}}{s^{2}},
 \qquad
 \delta_n:=\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_n(a,b,c),\,(a,b,c)\in\mathcal K}
       \frac{\max_{j,u}|w_{ju}|}{s}.
\]
By \(\mathrm{lem:frontier\mbox{-}score\mbox{-}diffuseness}\), \(\sup_n A_n<\infty\) and \(\delta_n\to0\).  Put \(\bar\delta_n=\max\{\delta_n,n^{-1}\}\) and \(k_n=\lfloor\bar\delta_n^{-1}\rfloor\).  Then \(k_n\to\infty\) and \(k_n\delta_n^2\le\bar\delta_n\to0\).

For each row, partition the ordered times into retained consecutive blocks of length at most \(k_n\), separated by gaps of \(q\) deleted times.  The origin of this periodic partition may be chosen separately for each row.  Averaging the squared deleted weights over the \(k_n+q\) possible origins shows that one origin satisfies
\[
 sum_{(j,u)\in D_n}w_{ju}^{2}
 \le \frac{q}{k_n+q}\sum_{j,u}w_{ju}^{2}.
\tag{2}
\]
Indeed, for a fixed row and a uniform origin, each time index belongs to a gap for at most \(q\) of the \(k_n+q\) origins; summing this incidence identity against \(w_{ju}^{2}\) proves (2), with endpoint indices only reducing the incidence count.  Let
\[
 R_n:=\sum_{(j,u)\in D_n}X_{ju}.
\]
For any deterministic coefficients \(a_{ju}\), conditional centering, the dependency graph, Cauchy--Schwarz, and (1) with \(p=2\) imply
\[
 \operatorname{Var}\!\left(\sum_{j,u}a_{ju}\epsilon_{ju}\middle|M,\Omega\right)
 \le C_2K^2(2q+1)\sum_{j,u}a_{ju}^{2}.
\tag{3}
\]
Applying (3) to the deleted coefficients and using (2) yields
\[
 \sup_{P,(a,b,c)\in\mathcal K}
 \operatorname{Var}(R_n\mid M,\Omega)
 \le C_qK^2\frac{q}{k_n+q}A_n=o(1).
\tag{4}
\]

Write \(Z_B:=\sum_{(j,u)\in B}X_{ju}\) for a retained block \(B\).  Distinct retained blocks are conditionally independent: different rows are independent, and consecutive retained blocks in one row are separated by \(q\) deleted indices, so their closest indices differ by \(q+1\).  To bound a block's fourth moment, split its time indices into their \(q+1\) residue classes.  Variables within each residue class are conditionally independent.  For centered independent variables \(Y_k\), expansion of the fourth power and (1) give
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\left(\sum_kY_k\right)^4\middle|M,\Omega\right]
 =\sum_k\mathbb E(Y_k^4\mid M,\Omega)
  +6\sum_{k<l}\mathbb E(Y_k^2\mid M,\Omega)
                    \mathbb E(Y_l^2\mid M,\Omega)
 \le C K^4\left(\sum_k a_k^2\right)^2
\]
when \(Y_k=a_k\epsilon_k\).  Combining the residue classes with
\( |x_0+\cdots+x_q|^4\le(q+1)^3\sum_{r=0}^q|x_r|^4 \) gives
\[
 \mathbb E(|Z_B|^4\mid M,\Omega)
 \le C_qK^4\left(\sum_{(j,u)\in B}\frac{w_{ju}^2}{s^2}\right)^2.
\tag{5}
\]
By Lyapunov's inequality, (5), the fact that every block contains at most \(k_n\) indices, and the definition of \(A_n,\delta_n\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_B\mathbb E(|Z_B|^3\mid M,\Omega)
 &\le C_qK^3
   \sum_B\left(\sum_{(j,u)\in B}\frac{w_{ju}^2}{s^2}\right)^{3/2}\\
 &\le C_qK^3
   \left(\max_B\sum_{(j,u)\in B}\frac{w_{ju}^2}{s^2}\right)^{1/2}A_n\\
 &\le C_qK^3\sqrt{k_n}\,\delta_n A_n=o(1).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]

It remains to identify the normalization.  Conditional row independence, conditional \(q\)-dependence, conditional centering, and \(\mathrm{ass:common\mbox{-}stationary\mbox{-}covariance}\) imply, directly from the displayed definition of \(s^2\),
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(L\mid M,\Omega)
 =\sum_{j=1}^N\sum_{h=-q}^{q}\gamma(h)
    \sum_{u=1}^T w_{ju}w_{j,u+h}=s^2.
\tag{7}
\]
Here weights outside \(J\) are zero, as stipulated in \(\mathrm{def:oracle\mbox{-}score\mbox{-}variance}\), and \(s>0\) follows from \(\mathrm{ass:frontier\mbox{-}variance\mbox{-}scale}\).  If
\[
 v_n^2:=\operatorname{Var}\!\left(\sum_BZ_B\middle|M,\Omega\right),
\]
then \(L/s=\sum_BZ_B+R_n\), (4), (7), and conditional Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
 |v_n^2-1|
 \le \operatorname{Var}(R_n\mid M,\Omega)
 +2\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(R_n\mid M,\Omega)}=o(1)
\tag{8}
\]
uniformly over the indicated laws and exponent triples.

For completeness, we now derive the independent-block normal approximation rather than invoke an external central limit theorem.  Put \(U_B=Z_B/v_n\), and let \(G_B\), conditionally independently over blocks, be centered Gaussian variables with \(\operatorname{Var}(G_B\mid M,\Omega)=\operatorname{Var}(U_B\mid M,\Omega)\).  Then \(\sum_BG_B\) is exactly standard normal.  For any three-times continuously differentiable \(f\) with bounded third derivative, replace the blocks one at a time and expand around the sum of all other terms.  The constant, linear, and quadratic terms cancel because \(U_B\) and \(G_B\) have the same first two moments; Taylor's remainder gives
\[
 \left|\mathbb Ef\!\left(\sum_BU_B\right)-\mathbb Ef\!\left(\sum_BG_B\right)\right|
 \le \frac{\|f'''\|_\infty}{6}
       \sum_B\{\mathbb E|U_B|^3+\mathbb E|G_B|^3\}.
\tag{9}
\]
Moreover, \(\mathbb E|G_B|^3=C\operatorname{Var}(U_B)^{3/2}\le C\mathbb E|U_B|^3\).  Equations (6) and (8) therefore make the right-hand side of (9) tend to zero uniformly.

Choose a fixed nonincreasing \(C^3\) cutoff \(\chi\) with \(\chi(x)=1\) for \(x\le0\) and \(\chi(x)=0\) for \(x\ge1\), and put \(f_{z,h}(x)=\chi((x-z)/h)\).  Its third derivative is bounded by \(C h^{-3}\), uniformly in \(z\).  Applying (9) to the upper and lower cutoff sandwiches gives, for each fixed \(h>0\),
\[
 \sup_{z\in\mathbb R}
 \left|\mathbb P\!\left(\sum_BU_B\le z\middle|M,\Omega\right)-\Phi(z)\right|
 \le \sup_z\{\Phi(z+h)-\Phi(z-h)\}+o(1)
 \le \frac{2h}{\sqrt{2\pi}}+o(1).
\]
First let \(n\to\infty\), and then let \(h\downarrow0\).  This proves the compact-uniform conditional normal approximation for \(\sum_BZ_B/v_n\).  By (8), rescaling by \(v_n\to1\) does not change the conclusion.  Finally, (4) and conditional Chebyshev imply \(R_n=o_{\mathbb P}(1)\) uniformly.  The inequalities
\[
 \mathbb P\!\left(\sum_BZ_B\le z-\varepsilon\middle|M,\Omega\right)
 -\mathbb P(|R_n|>\varepsilon\mid M,\Omega)
 \le \mathbb P(L/s\le z\mid M,\Omega)
\]
and the analogous upper bound with \(z+\varepsilon\), followed by \(n\to\infty\) and \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), establish
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_n(a,b,c)}\sup_{z\in\mathbb R}
 \left|\mathbb P_P(L/s\le z\mid M,\Omega)-\Phi(z)\right|\longrightarrow0
\]
uniformly for \((a,b,c)\in\mathcal K\), as claimed.

Conditional on \((M,\Omega)\), fixed \(q\), uniform sub-Gaussian moments, row-law factorization, conditional within-row block dependence, frontier variance nondegeneracy, and frontier score diffuseness imply \(\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_n(a,b,c)}\sup_{z\in\mathbb R}|\mathbb P_P(L/s\le z\mid M,\Omega)-\Phi(z)|\to0\) uniformly when \((a,b,c)\) ranges over a compact subset of \([1/2,1]\times(0,1]\times[0,1]\).
