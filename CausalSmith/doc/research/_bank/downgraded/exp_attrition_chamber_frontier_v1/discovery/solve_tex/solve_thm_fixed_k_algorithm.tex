%%% FIELD proof-fixed-k-algorithm
Let the observed coordinates induce the ordered nonempty equality blocks
\(B_1\prec\cdots\prec B_g\).  Process the \(k\) missing labels in a fixed
label order, as prescribed by \(\mathsf{ChamberMax}\) in
Definition \(\text{def:block-insertion-algorithm}\).  If the current ordered
partition has \(h\) blocks, the next missing label either forms a singleton
in one of the \(h+1\) gaps or joins one of the \(h\) existing blocks.  Thus
there are exactly \(2h+1\) branches at that node.

These branches enumerate the compatible weak orders without omission or
duplication.  Indeed, start with any compatible final weak order and delete
the processed missing labels in reverse order.  At a deletion step, if the
label shares its block with a label that has not yet been deleted, then its
unique predecessor is obtained by deleting it from that block and the forward
operation was attachment.  Otherwise it is a singleton, its unique predecessor
is obtained by deleting that singleton block, and the forward operation was
insertion into the resulting unique gap.  Reversing all deletions yields the
observed ordered blocks.  This gives a two-sided inverse to the insertion path.
After \(j\) labels have been inserted there are at most \(g+j\) blocks, so the
number \(C(g,k)\) of leaves satisfies
\[
 C(g,k)\le \prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\{2(g+j)+1\}
 \le (2n+1)^k.
\]
Here the empty product is one when \(k=0\), and the last inequality uses
\(g+j\le n\).

Fix a leaf and scan its ordered blocks.  By the midrank formula in
Definition \(\text{def:midrank-tail}\), if a block has size \(d\) and exactly
\(c\) labels precede it, every member of that block has midrank
\[
 r=c+\frac{d+1}{2},
 \qquad q:=2r=2c+d+1\in\{2,\ldots,2n\}.
\]
Consequently, after one \(O(n)\) scan has assigned every doubled rank
\(q_i=2r_i\), each explicitly listed row \(z\in\Omega\) is evaluated by the
integer sum
\[
 2T(z,b)=\sum_{i\in I}z_iq_i.
\]
Comparing this sum with \(2T(a,b)\) and accumulating the corresponding
\(w_z\) costs \(O(nL)\) arithmetic operations at the leaf.  By
Theorem \(\text{thm:weak-order-attainment}\), the tail is constant on each
enumerated cell and its maximum over these leaves is exactly
\(P_\delta(a)\).  Retaining a maximizing ordered partition therefore supplies
the asserted optimizing weak-order certificate.

It remains to account for exact bit complexity.  By
Assumption \(\text{ass:known-fixed-count-design}\), the \(L\) rows are finite
and explicitly available.  Sorting the rational observed coordinates and all
subsequent comparisons have cost polynomial in \(\ell\).  By
Assumption \(\text{ass:rational-positive-weights}\), all tail masses are exact
rational sums.  Placing their denominators over their product gives a common
denominator whose binary length is at most the sum of the input denominator
lengths, hence \(O(\ell)\); the accumulated numerator also has polynomial
binary length.  The doubled rank sums have \(O(\log n)\) bits.  Thus the total
number of bit operations, including sorting, is
\[
 O\!\left((2n+1)^k nL\,\operatorname{poly}(\ell)\right).
\]
For fixed \(k\) this bound is polynomial in the explicit input size.  Because
the exponent of \(n\) in the displayed guarantee depends on \(k\), it is an
\(\mathrm{XP}\) guarantee and does not itself give an \(\mathrm{FPT}\)
guarantee.

For the sharper \(k=1\) bound, write \(u\) for the missing label and traverse
the \(2g+1\) positions consisting of the bottom gap and, for each observed
block from bottom to top, its equality face followed by the gap above it.  For
each \(z\in\Omega\), define
\[
 d_i(z)=z_i-a_i,
 \qquad D_z= T(z,b)-T(a,b)=\sum_{i\in I}d_i(z)r_i(b).
\]
Consider an observed block \(B\) of size \(t\).  On moving \(u\) from the gap
immediately below \(B\) to the equality face of \(B\), the rank of \(u\)
increases by \(t/2\), while the rank of every member of \(B\) decreases by
\(1/2\).  Therefore
\[
 D_z(\text{face})-D_z(\text{lower gap})
 =\frac{t\,d_u(z)-\sum_{i\in B}d_i(z)}{2}.
\]
The same rank changes, and hence the same increment, occur from that equality
face to the gap immediately above \(B\).  For every row \(z\), all block sums
\(\sum_{i\in B_j}d_i(z)\), together with the value of \(D_z\) at the bottom
gap, are computed in \(O(n)\) operations.  Across all rows this initialization
costs \(O(nL)\).  Each of the \(2g\) half-steps updates all \(D_z\) and sums
the weights with \(D_z\ge0\) in \(O(L\operatorname{poly}(\ell))\) bit
operations.  Since \(g\le n\), the complete equality-inclusive sweep costs
\(O(nL\operatorname{poly}(\ell))\) after sorting and examines every one of the
\(2g+1\) compatible positions.

%%% FIELD statement-cre-midrank-fixed-k
Suppose \(\Omega=\mathcal Z_m\) and
\(w_z=\binom{n}{m}^{-1}\) for every \(z\in\mathcal Z_m\), with the completely
randomized design supplied by the succinct encoding of length
\(\ell_{\mathrm{CRE}}\).  Then the ordinary-midrank completion maximum
\(P_\delta(a)\) is computable in
\[
 \left[\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\{2(g+j)+1\}\right]
 \operatorname{poly}(n,\ell_{\mathrm{CRE}})
\]
bit operations without listing the \(\binom{n}{m}\) assignments.  Hence exact
maximization is polynomial for every fixed \(k\), including \(k=1\), over
completely randomized designs with an arbitrary fixed treatment-indexed
observation schedule \(O\), while retaining ordinary midranks and equality
faces.

%%% FIELD proof-cre-midrank-fixed-k
Begin with the \(g\) observed equality blocks and insert the \(k\) missing
labels in a fixed order according to
Definition \(\text{def:block-insertion-algorithm}\).  With \(h\) current
blocks, the next label has \(h+1\) singleton-gap choices and \(h\) attachment
choices.  Reverse deletion of the last inserted label is unique: delete it
from a shared block if another undeleted label remains there, and otherwise
delete its singleton block.  Thus the leaves are in bijection with all
compatible complete weak orders.  After \(j\) insertions there are at most
\(g+j\) blocks, so their number is at most
\[
 C(g,k):=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\{2(g+j)+1\}.
\]
Attachment branches are precisely the equality faces, so none are discarded.

Fix one leaf.  A block of size \(d\) preceded by \(c\) labels gives each of its
members the ordinary doubled midrank
\[
 q_i:=2r_i=2c+d+1,
 \qquad 2\le q_i\le2n.
\]
Form the bivariate integer polynomial
\[
 F(u,x)=\prod_{i=1}^n(1+u x^{q_i})
       =\sum_{t=0}^n\sum_s C_{t,s}u^t x^s.
\]
Expanding the product proves, term by term, that
\[
 C_{t,s}
 =\#\left\{J\subseteq I:|J|=t,
                    \ \sum_{i\in J}q_i=s\right\}.
\]
Under the completely randomized design, assignment rows are in bijection with
the \(m\)-subsets \(J=\{i:z_i=1\}\), each with probability
\(\binom{n}{m}^{-1}\).  Writing \(Q_a=2T(a,b)=\sum_i a_iq_i\), Definition
\(\text{def:midrank-tail}\) therefore gives the exact leaf tail
\[
 p(a,b)=\binom{n}{m}^{-1}\sum_{s\ge Q_a}C_{m,s}.
\]
The declared range \(1\le m\le n-1\) implies \(\binom{n}{m}>0\), so this
division is well defined.
This identity is valid with ties because the \(q_i\) are the doubled ordinary
midranks of the equality blocks at the current leaf.

The coefficients need not be obtained by symbolic multiplication.  Let
\(C^{(i)}_{t,s}\) be the coefficient after the first \(i\) factors.  Initialize
\(C^{(0)}_{0,0}=1\), set all other initial and out-of-range entries to zero,
and use the recurrence
\[
 C^{(i)}_{t,s}
 =C^{(i-1)}_{t,s}+C^{(i-1)}_{t-1,s-q_i}.
\]
Because \(0\le t\le m\), \(2\le q_i\le2n\), and an \(m\)-subset has sum at
most \(2mn\le2n^2\), this table has polynomially many entries and is filled
with polynomially many integer additions.  Each coefficient is at most
\(2^n\), so it has at most \(n+1\) bits; \(\binom{n}{m}\), the threshold
\(Q_a\), and the numerator tail sum likewise have polynomial bit length.
Hence one leaf tail is evaluated in
\(\operatorname{poly}(n,\ell_{\mathrm{CRE}})\) bit operations, without
constructing any assignment row.

The midrank vector and all tail indicators depend only on the leaf's weak
order by Definitions \(\text{def:midrank-tail}\) and
\(\text{def:weak-order-complex}\).  Every legal completion induces one of the
enumerated leaves, and every enumerated leaf is realizable: assign an attached
missing label in an observed block that block's fixed value; for each finite
run of missing-only blocks between two observed blocks choose distinct real
values strictly between the two fixed values; and choose distinct values below
or above the observed range for the two possible extreme runs.  Assign every
label in a missing-only equality block its block's chosen value.  Therefore the
supremum in
Definition \(\text{def:robust-pvalue}\) is the maximum of the computed leaf
tails.  Multiplying the per-leaf cost by \(C(g,k)\) gives
\[
 \left[\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\{2(g+j)+1\}\right]
 \operatorname{poly}(n,\ell_{\mathrm{CRE}}).
\]
For each fixed \(k\), the product is bounded by \((2n+1)^k\), so this is
polynomial in the succinct completely randomized-design input.  The argument
uses only that the treatment-indexed schedule \(O\) is fixed, as stated in
Assumption \(\text{ass:fixed-observation-schedule}\); it imposes no further
restriction on which outcomes are missing.
