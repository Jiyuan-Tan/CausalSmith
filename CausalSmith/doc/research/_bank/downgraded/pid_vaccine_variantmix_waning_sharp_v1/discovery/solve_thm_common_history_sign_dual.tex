\sum_s\mathbf 1\{h_1^0=u,r_{1au}=0\}
 \mathbf 1\{r^{[u]}_{2av}\ne r^{[0]}_{2av}\}x_s
\leq
\delta\sum_s\mathbf 1\{h_1^0=u,r_{1au}=0\}x_s
\]
for every \((a,u,v)\).  Thus \(x\in\mathcal P_\delta(b,q,\omega)\).

P(J_t=v\mid A=a)=\sum_s\widetilde g_{tav}(s)x_s=b_{tav},

The placebo-stability equality is its designated equality row, and every priming-fraction inequality is its designated inequality row. \quad\text{subject to}\quad
E_\omega^\top\lambda+F_\delta^\top\mu\geq c_\omega,
\quad \mu\geq0.

Weak duality is visible without any theorem: for feasible \(x,\lambda,\mu\),
\[

\leq\lambda^\top E_\omega x+\mu^\top F_\delta x
\leq d(b,q)^\top\lambda.

Strong duality and attainment therefore show that the primal maximum is negative if and only if a feasible \((\lambda,\mu)\) has \(d(b,q)^\top\lambda<0\), which is the first certificate.

\[
\max_{\eta,\kappa}\ d(b,q)^\top\eta
\quad\text{subject to}\quad
E_\omega^\top\eta-F_\delta^\top\kappa\leq c_\omega,
\quad \kappa\geq0.
\]
Indeed, for feasible variables,
\[
d(b,q)^\top\eta
=x^\top E_\omega^\top\eta
\leq c_\omega^\top x+x^\top F_\delta^\top\kappa
\leq c_\omega^\top x.
\]
Strong duality gives the claimed equivalence with a strictly positive dual value.

If the first certificate fails, attainment gives \(x^\star\) with \(c_\omega^\top x^\star\geq0\); if the second fails, it gives \(x_\star\) with \(c_\omega^\top x_\star\leq0\).  Theorem \(\text{thm:exact-history-compression}\) lifts either vector to a compatible full law.  When the denominator is positive, the sign of \(c_\omega^\top x\) is exactly the sign of \(\Psi_\omega(x)-1\).  If the denominator is zero, nonnegativity implies that \(c<0\) is impossible; \(c>0\) gives \(+\infty\), and \(c=0\) gives the full \([0,+\infty]\) branch. is strictly positive.  The primal optimizer certifies nonemptiness.  When it is not positive, the finite linear-program duality and Farkas lemma provide a finite separating multiplier; if the base fiber itself is empty, its ordinary Farkas ray does so.  This proves the empty-face certificate.

Now suppose \(F_t\ne\varnothing\).  A relative-interior point in \(F_t\) exists: take any point of \(F_t\), any point in the relative interior of the fiber, and a sufficiently small convex combination toward the former.  The affine hull and a basis \(N\) of its direction space are computable by finitely many linear programs identifying the inequalities active at every feasible point.  For any positive constant \(C\), define
\[
\phi_C(x)=p_{11}(x)p_{02}(x)-Cp_{01}(x)p_{12}(x)=x^\top Q_Cx.
\]
The positive denominators show that \(\operatorname{CSE}^{\mathrm P}_t(x)=C\) exactly when \(\phi_C(x)=0\).  Parameterize the affine hull as \(x=x^\circ+Nu\).  Direct expansion gives
\[
\phi_C(x^\circ+Nu)
=u^\top N^\top Q_CN u+2u^\top N^\top Q_Cx^\circ
+(x^\circ)^\top Q_Cx^\circ.
\]
Thus this polynomial vanishes for every \(u\) exactly when the three displayed coefficient identities hold.

The face \(F_t\) contains a relative neighborhood of \(x^\circ\).  A polynomial that vanishes on that neighborhood has all displayed coefficients zero; conversely the coefficient identities make it vanish on the entire affine hull.  Taking \(C=C^\circ=\operatorname{CSE}^{\mathrm P}_t(x^\circ)\) proves the necessary-and-sufficient constancy criterion.  Equality to the observed functional then requires and is implied by the additional scalar equality \(C^\circ=\operatorname{CSE}^{\mathrm{obs}}_t(b)\).

If the coefficient test fails, the rational function cannot be constant on any relative neighborhood of \(x^\circ\).  Choose a direction \(h\) in the span of \(N\) on which its one-dimensional restriction varies.  Relative interior and strict positivity provide \(\varepsilon>0\) such that \(x^\circ+sh\in F_t\) for \(|s|<\varepsilon\).  The equality of its value at two parameters is a nonzero polynomial equation, so two parameters outside its finite root set give two explicit points with different values.  The exact-history compression theorem lifts them to compatible full laws.  If the coefficient test holds but the observable equality fails, the lift of \(x^\circ\) has the four displayed controlled risks and value \(C^\circ\ne\operatorname{CSE}^{\mathrm{obs}}_t(b)\).  This is the required observable-mismatch certificate.  The three stated branches are mutually exclusive and exhaustive.

Fix \(\delta\in\mathcal D\), keep \(x\in\mathcal P_\delta(b,q,\omega)\), and define \(d_v(x)=\sum_s\{r_{11v}(s)-r^{[0]}_{21v}(s)\}x_s\).  If \(U_\delta(b,q,\omega)<1\), then \(r_\star(\delta)\) is the value of the finite-dimensional program
\[
\min_{x,w,t}\sum_vt_v
\quad\text{subject to}\quad x\in\mathcal P_\delta(b,q,\omega),\quad
w\in\Delta(\mathcal V),\quad -t_v\leq w_v-\omega_v\leq t_v,\quad t_v\geq0,
\quad\sum_vw_vd_v(x)\geq0.
\]
For \(L_\delta>1\), reverse the last inequality.  An empty feasible set has value \(+\infty\).  Every finite value is attained and its \((x,w)\) lifts to a compatible full law at the first loss of the strict baseline sign.

For any \(r<r_\star(\delta)\), let \(W_r=\{w\in\Delta(\mathcal V):\lVert w-\omega\rVert_1\leq r\}\) and enumerate its finitely many vertices \(w^j\).  In the below-unity case, sign preservation is certified by multipliers \(\lambda_j\) and \(\mu_j\geq0\) satisfying
\[
E_\omega^\top\lambda_j+F_\delta^\top\mu_j\geq c_{w^j},
\qquad d(b,q)^\top\lambda_j<0
\]
for every vertex, where \(c_w(s)=\sum_vw_v\{r_{11v}(s)-r^{[0]}_{21v}(s)\}\).  In the above-unity case the symmetric certificates are \(E_\omega^\top\eta_j-F_\delta^\top\kappa_j\leq c_{w^j}\), \(\kappa_j\geq0\), and \(d(b,q)^\top\eta_j>0\).  Finally, \(r_\star=+\infty\) exactly when the same strict certificate holds at every point-mass vertex \(e^{(v)}\) of the full simplex.  This is a finite dual infeasibility certificate for reversal.

%%% FIELD proof-mixture-radius
For every \((x,w)\), direct expansion gives
\[
\rho_{11}(x,w)-\rho_{21}(x,w)=\sum_vw_vd_v(x)=c_w^\top x.
\]
Thus the last constraint in the displayed program is exactly failure of the below-unity strict sign; reversing it is exactly failure of the above-unity strict sign.  The auxiliary inequalities imply \(t_v\geq|w_v-\omega_v|\), and minimization makes their sum equal \(\lVert w-\omega\rVert_1\).  Hence this program is precisely the definition of \(r_\star(\delta)\), with the observed-data fiber and its placebo-stability row still indexed by \(\omega\).

The product of the fixed fiber and the simplex is compact, the sign-failure constraint is closed, and the norm is continuous.  Therefore a nonempty reversal set has an attaining minimizer.  Theorem \(\text{thm:exact-history-compression}\) lifts its \(x\) to a compatible full law, while \(w\) changes only the evaluated policy mixture.  This proves the boundary witness.

Now fix \(r<r_\star\).  In the below-unity case, compactness and the definition of the radius imply
\[
\max_{w\in W_r}\max_{x\in\mathcal P_\delta}c_w^\top x<0.
\]
For fixed \(w\), the inner maximum is a convex, piecewise-linear function of \(w\), being a support function.  If \(w=\sum_j\gamma_jw^j\), then
\[
\max_xc_w^\top x
\leq\sum_j\gamma_j\max_xc_{w^j}^\top x
\leq\max_j\max_xc_{w^j}^\top x.
\]
Consequently it suffices to check the finitely many vertices.  Applying finite linear-program duality to each inner maximum gives exactly the displayed \((\lambda_j,\mu_j)\) certificate, by the same weak-duality calculation as in the common-history sign theorem.  It certifies every \(x\) and every mixture in the entire radius ball, not a selected law.  For an above-unity baseline, the inner minimum is concave in \(w\); its value at a convex combination is at least the corresponding combination of vertex values, so its global minimum is also achieved at a vertex.  The symmetric lower dual yields the stated \((\eta_j,\kappa_j)\) certificates.

If no reversal exists for any \(w\in\Delta(\mathcal V)\), the same argument uses the simplex vertices \(e^{(v)}\).  Conversely, strict certificates at all those vertices imply the strict sign for every convex combination and every compatible \(x\), so reversal is infeasible.  The finite Farkas/dual certificates therefore establish \(r_\star=+\infty\).  This proves all finite, sub-boundary, and infinity branches.

%%% FIELD proof-exact-projection
We first verify the finite-sample coverage of one exact inversion.  Fix a candidate multinomial law \(p\) and let \(Z\sim\operatorname{Mult}_N(p)\).  The statistic \(T_p(Z)\) has finite support.  Order its distinct values from largest to smallest.  At an observed value, \(\operatorname{pv}_N(p;Z)\) is the probability under \(p\) of the corresponding upper tail.  Therefore the set of values whose upper-tail probability is less than \(\gamma\) has total probability at most \(\gamma\).  Equivalently,
\[
P_p\{p\notin\Gamma_N(Z;\gamma)\}
=P_p\{\operatorname{pv}_N(p;Z)<\gamma\}\leq\gamma.
\]
This argument uses only a finite sum and remains valid when observed or candidate probabilities are zero, with the conventions in \(\text{def:exact-whole-set-projection}\).

Conditional on realized arm sizes, the two arm count vectors have their stated multinomial laws.  Each of the two external period count vectors also has its stated multinomial marginal law; no independence across the four vectors is needed.  Applying the preceding inequality with \(\gamma=\alpha/4\) and the union bound gives
\[
P\{(b,q)\in\mathcal G_{n,m}(\alpha)\mid\text{realized arm sizes}\}
\geq1-4(\alpha/4)=1-\alpha.
\]
If the trial law lies in \(\mathfrak M_\delta(b,q,\omega)\), exact history compression implies \(\mathcal P_\delta(b,q,\omega)\ne\varnothing\).  On the event \((b,q)\in\mathcal G_{n,m}(\alpha)\), the true candidate therefore occurs in the union defining \(\mathcal C_{n,m,\delta}^{\mathrm{exact}}\).  Pointwise sharpness then gives
\[
\Theta_{I,\delta}(b,q,\omega)
\subseteq\bigcup_{(b',q')\in\mathcal G:\mathcal P_\delta(b',q',\omega)\ne\varnothing}
\Theta_{I,\delta}(b',q',\omega)
\subseteq\mathcal C_{n,m,\delta}^{\mathrm{exact}}.
\]
Taking probabilities proves the coverage claim.  Since the inclusion is of the entire true set on one primitive-probability coverage event, it is whole-set coverage and involves no optimizer selection.  The argument invokes the full extended set, so both zero-denominator branches are included.

For computation, each multinomial sample space contains finitely many count vectors.  Thus each exact p-value is the finite tail sum displayed in the definition, and the four acceptance conditions have a finite enumeration.  For a retained \((b',q')\), the compatible fiber is the finite linear system defining \(\mathcal P_\delta\). This proves the finite representation and the claimed boundary behavior.
