%%% FIELD theorem-statement
Fix \(K=2\), \(t\in\mathcal T\), and a reported \(\delta\in\mathcal D\).  After deleting the coordinates outside \(\overline{\mathcal S}\), write the finite system in the reduced-polytope definition as
\[
 \mathcal P:=\mathcal P_\delta(b,q,\omega)
   =\{x\in\mathbb R^p:E x=d,\ Gx\leq u\},
 \qquad p=|\overline{\mathcal S}|,
\]
where the rows of \(Gx\leq u\) include nonnegativity and all priming inequalities.  For \(a\in\{0,1\}\) and \(v\in\{1,2\}\), let \(\ell_{av}\in\mathbb R^p\) be the coefficient vector satisfying
\[
 \ell_{av}^{\mathsf T}x=\rho_{ta}(x,e^{(v)}).
\]
Define the controlled-risk comparison face
\[
 \mathcal F_t=\{x\in\mathcal P:\ell_{av}^{\mathsf T}x>0
                    \text{ for every }a\in\{0,1\},\ v\in\{1,2\}\}.
\]
The observed-positive condition is
\[
 \mathcal O_t:\quad
 s_{ta}:=1-\sum_{r<t}\sum_{w\in\mathcal V}b_{raw}>0
 \quad(a=0,1),
 \qquad b_{t,0,1}>0,\quad b_{t,0,2}>0,\quad b_{t,1,2}>0.
\]
Under \(\mathcal O_t\), the observed comparator is defined and equals
\[
 k_{\rm obs}:=\operatorname{CSE}^{\rm obs}_t(b)
 =\frac{b_{t,1,1}b_{t,0,2}}{b_{t,0,1}b_{t,1,2}}.
\]

The following finite procedure gives necessary and sufficient collapse restrictions.  First test feasibility of \(\mathcal P\).  When \(\mathcal P\neq\varnothing\), compute
\[
 \varepsilon_\star=\max_{x,\varepsilon}\left\{\varepsilon:
 Ex=d,\ Gx\leq u,\ \ell_{av}^{\mathsf T}x\geq\varepsilon
 \text{ for all }(a,v)\right\}.
\]
Then \(\mathcal F_t\neq\varnothing\) if and only if \(\varepsilon_\star>0\).  When this holds, let
\[
 I_0=\left\{j:\max_{x\in\mathcal P}
       (u_j-G_j^{\mathsf T}x)=0\right\},
\]
let \(Hx=h\) stack \(Ex=d\) and \(G_j^{\mathsf T}x=u_j\) for \(j\in I_0\), and let the columns of \(N\) be any basis of \(\ker H\).  Obtain \(y\) from the finite linear program
\[
 \max_{x,\tau}\left\{\tau:x\in\mathcal P,\ 
 u_j-G_j^{\mathsf T}x\geq\tau\ (j\notin I_0),\ 
 \ell_{av}^{\mathsf T}x\geq\tau\ \text{for all }(a,v)\right\};
\]
its optimum is positive.  Put \(a_1=\ell_{11}\), \(p_1=\ell_{01}\), \(a_2=\ell_{12}\), \(p_2=\ell_{02}\), and define
\[
 k_0=\frac{(a_1^{\mathsf T}y)(p_2^{\mathsf T}y)}
 {(p_1^{\mathsf T}y)(a_2^{\mathsf T}y)},
 \qquad
 Q(k)=\frac12\{a_1p_2^{\mathsf T}+p_2a_1^{\mathsf T}
 -k(p_1a_2^{\mathsf T}+a_2p_1^{\mathsf T})\}.
\]
Then \(\operatorname{CSE}^{\rm P}_t(x)\) is constant on \(\mathcal F_t\) if and only if the quadratic affine-hull criterion
\[
 y^{\mathsf T}Q(k_0)y=0,\qquad
 N^{\mathsf T}Q(k_0)y=0,\qquad
 N^{\mathsf T}Q(k_0)N=0                                      \tag{C}
\]
holds.  The criterion is independent of the admissible choices of \(y\) and \(N\); when it holds, the constant is \(k_0\).  Consequently, on a nonempty comparison face and under \(\mathcal O_t\),
\[
 \operatorname{CSE}^{\rm P}_t(x)=\operatorname{CSE}^{\rm obs}_t(b)
 \quad\hbox{for every }x\in\mathcal F_t
\]
if and only if \((\mathrm C)\) holds and \(k_0=k_{\rm obs}\).

The failure certificates are exhaustive after observed positivity is checked.  If \(\mathcal O_t\) fails, the stipulated observed comparison is undefined; this does not imply that \(\mathcal F_t\) is empty.  Suppose \(\mathcal O_t\) holds.  If \(\mathcal F_t=\varnothing\), there is a finite certificate: either \(\mathcal P=\varnothing\) and there are \(\mu\geq0\), \(\nu\) such that
\[
 G^{\mathsf T}\mu+E^{\mathsf T}\nu=0,
 \qquad u^{\mathsf T}\mu+d^{\mathsf T}\nu<0,
\]
or \(\mathcal P\neq\varnothing\), \(\varepsilon_\star=0\), and there are \(\mu\geq0\), \(\lambda_{av}\geq0\), and \(\nu\) such that
\[
 \sum_{a,v}\lambda_{av}=1,\qquad
 G^{\mathsf T}\mu-\sum_{a,v}\lambda_{av}\ell_{av}
       +E^{\mathsf T}\nu=0,\qquad
 u^{\mathsf T}\mu+d^{\mathsf T}\nu=0.
\]
If \(\mathcal F_t\neq\varnothing\) but \((\mathrm C)\) fails, the coefficient test in the proof constructs \(x_1,x_2\in\mathcal F_t\) with different values of \(\operatorname{CSE}^{\rm P}_t\), and exact history compression lifts both vectors to compatible laws in \(\mathfrak M_\delta(b,q,\omega)\).  If \((\mathrm C)\) holds but \(k_0\neq k_{\rm obs}\), the lift of \(y\) is a one-law mismatch certificate, because its four controlled risks give \(\operatorname{CSE}^{\rm P}_t(y)=k_0\neq k_{\rm obs}\).
%%% FIELD theorem-proof
All vectors below use only the common-history coordinates.  The reduced-polytope definition has finitely many equality and inequality constraints, so it has the displayed representation \(Ex=d\), \(Gx\leq u\).  Nonnegativity is included among the rows of \(G\).  For \(t=1\), the entries of \(\ell_{av}\) are \(r_{1av}(s)\); for \(t=2\), they are \(r^{[0]}_{2av}(s)\).  Thus the definition of \(\rho\) gives, equation by equation,
\[
 \ell_{av}^{\mathsf T}x
 =\sum_{s\in\overline{\mathcal S}}r_{1av}(s)x_s
 =\rho_{1a}(x,e^{(v)})
\]
in period one, and
\[
 \ell_{av}^{\mathsf T}x
 =\sum_{s\in\overline{\mathcal S}}r^{[0]}_{2av}(s)x_s
 =\rho_{2a}(x,e^{(v)})
\]
in period two.  Each entry of every \(\ell_{av}\) is zero or one.  Hence \(x\geq0\) implies \(\ell_{av}^{\mathsf T}x\geq0\).

We first keep the observed functional separate from the controlled-risk target.  Under \(\mathcal O_t\), the observed hazard definition yields
\[
 h^{\rm obs}_{tav}(b)=\frac{b_{tav}}{s_{ta}}>0.
\]
Cancellation of the two arm-specific at-risk factors, which is legal because \(s_{t0},s_{t1}>0\), gives
\[
 \operatorname{CSE}^{\rm obs}_t(b)
 =\frac{(b_{t,1,1}/s_{t1})/(b_{t,0,1}/s_{t0})}
 {(b_{t,1,2}/s_{t1})/(b_{t,0,2}/s_{t0})}
 =\frac{b_{t,1,1}b_{t,0,2}}{b_{t,0,1}b_{t,1,2}}
 =k_{\rm obs}.
\]
The three cell probabilities appearing in denominators are positive under \(\mathcal O_t\), while \(b_{t,1,1}\geq0\), so every division is valid and \(k_{\rm obs}\in[0,+\infty)\).  If \(\mathcal O_t\) fails, at least one required at-risk or component-denominator positivity condition is absent; therefore the observed comparison used in the question is not defined.  This is a failure of the comparison premise, not a logical statement about feasibility of \(\mathcal F_t\).

Assume next that \(\mathcal P\neq\varnothing\).  Because every controlled risk is nonnegative, any \(x\in\mathcal P\) together with \(\varepsilon=0\) is feasible in the program defining \(\varepsilon_\star\).  Also \(\ell_{av}^{\mathsf T}x\leq\sum_sx_s=1\), so its optimal value is finite and attained.  If \(\varepsilon_\star>0\), an optimizer belongs to \(\mathcal F_t\).  Conversely, if \(x\in\mathcal F_t\), finiteness of the four indices gives
\[
 \varepsilon:=\min_{a\in\{0,1\},\,v\in\{1,2\}}
       \ell_{av}^{\mathsf T}x>0,
\]
so \(\varepsilon_\star\geq\varepsilon>0\).  This proves
\[
 \mathcal F_t\neq\varnothing\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \varepsilon_\star>0.                                      \tag{1}
\]

The advertised empty-face certificates follow from finite linear-program duality.  First suppose \(\mathcal P=\varnothing\).  Consider the phase-one program
\[
 \min_{x,r}\ r
 \quad\text{subject to}\quad
 Gx-r\mathbf 1\leq u,
 \ Ex-r\mathbf 1\leq d,
 \ -Ex-r\mathbf 1\leq-d,
 \ -r\leq0.
\]
It is feasible for sufficiently large \(r\), and its objective is bounded below by zero.  Its attained value \(r_\star\) is strictly positive, since a feasible point with \(r=0\) would belong to \(\mathcal P\).  Applying finite linear-program strong duality to this program gives nonnegative multipliers \(\mu,\alpha,\beta,\gamma\) satisfying
\[
 G^{\mathsf T}\mu+E^{\mathsf T}\alpha-E^{\mathsf T}\beta=0,
 \qquad
 \mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}\mu+\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}\alpha
 +\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}\beta+\gamma=1,
\]
and
\[
 -u^{\mathsf T}\mu-d^{\mathsf T}\alpha+d^{\mathsf T}\beta=r_\star>0.
\]
With \(\nu=\alpha-\beta\), these equations imply
\[
 G^{\mathsf T}\mu+E^{\mathsf T}\nu=0,
 \qquad u^{\mathsf T}\mu+d^{\mathsf T}\nu=-r_\star<0.       \tag{2}
\]
Conversely, multiplying the constraints of any putative feasible \(x\) by a pair satisfying (2) would give
\[
 0=x^{\mathsf T}(G^{\mathsf T}\mu+E^{\mathsf T}\nu)
   =\mu^{\mathsf T}Gx+\nu^{\mathsf T}Ex
   \leq\mu^{\mathsf T}u+\nu^{\mathsf T}d<0,
\]
a contradiction.  Thus (2) is a separating infeasibility certificate.

Now suppose \(\mathcal P\neq\varnothing\).  Rewrite the maximization defining \(\varepsilon_\star\) as minimization of \(-\varepsilon\).  Finite linear-program strong duality gives the dual
\[
 \min_{\mu,\lambda,\nu}\ u^{\mathsf T}\mu+d^{\mathsf T}\nu
\]
subject to
\[
 \mu\geq0,\quad\lambda_{av}\geq0,\quad
 \sum_{a,v}\lambda_{av}=1,\quad
 G^{\mathsf T}\mu-\sum_{a,v}\lambda_{av}\ell_{av}
       +E^{\mathsf T}\nu=0.                                \tag{3}
\]
The primal value is \(\varepsilon_\star\), so the dual value in (3) is also \(\varepsilon_\star\).  In view of (1), a nonempty \(\mathcal P\) has an empty \(\mathcal F_t\) exactly when the primal value is zero, and then an attained dual solution with
\(u^{\mathsf T}\mu+d^{\mathsf T}\nu=0\) is the asserted finite certificate.  Weak duality also directly verifies the certificate: for any \(x\in\mathcal P\), (3) gives
\[
 \sum_{a,v}\lambda_{av}\ell_{av}^{\mathsf T}x
 =\mu^{\mathsf T}Gx+\nu^{\mathsf T}Ex
 \leq\mu^{\mathsf T}u+\nu^{\mathsf T}d=0.
\]
Every summand on the left is nonnegative and the \(\lambda_{av}\)'s sum to one, so not all four risks can be strictly positive.

It remains to prove the exact constancy criterion.  A row \(j\) belongs to \(I_0\) precisely when its inequality is an equality at every point of \(\mathcal P\).  Hence
\[
 \operatorname{aff}(\mathcal P)\subseteq\{x:Hx=h\}.          \tag{4}
\]
For every \(j\notin I_0\), the defining maximum for \(I_0\) is positive, so there is \(x^{(j)}\in\mathcal P\) with
\(u_j-G_j^{\mathsf T}x^{(j)}>0\).  Averaging these finitely many witnesses gives a point strictly satisfying every inequality not in \(I_0\).  Averaging that point with any member of \(\mathcal F_t\) gives a point that additionally has all four controlled risks strictly positive.  Thus the linear program defining \(y\) has a positive optimum, and its optimizer satisfies
\[
 u_j-G_j^{\mathsf T}y>0\quad(j\notin I_0),
 \qquad \ell_{av}^{\mathsf T}y>0\quad(a=0,1;\ v=1,2).       \tag{5}
\]
Because there are finitely many inequalities, (5) supplies a relative neighborhood of \(y\) in \(\{x:Hx=h\}\) contained in \(\mathcal P\).  Therefore the reverse inclusion in (4) holds after taking affine hulls, and
\[
 \operatorname{aff}(\mathcal P)=\{x:Hx=h\}=y+\ker H
 =\{y+Nz:z\in\mathbb R^{\dim\ker H}\}.                     \tag{6}
\]
This also proves that the construction of the affine hull is computable using finitely many linear programs.

On \(\mathcal F_t\), every denominator below is positive, and the controlled-risk definition gives
\[
 \operatorname{CSE}^{\rm P}_t(x)
 =\frac{(a_1^{\mathsf T}x)(p_2^{\mathsf T}x)}
 {(p_1^{\mathsf T}x)(a_2^{\mathsf T}x)}.                    \tag{7}
\]
For every real \(k\), direct multiplication shows
\[
 x^{\mathsf T}Q(k)x
 =(a_1^{\mathsf T}x)(p_2^{\mathsf T}x)
 -k(p_1^{\mathsf T}x)(a_2^{\mathsf T}x).                   \tag{8}
\]
In particular, the definition of \(k_0\) and positivity in (5) imply
\[
 y^{\mathsf T}Q(k_0)y=0.                                    \tag{9}
\]
For \(x=y+Nz\), symmetry of \(Q(k_0)\) yields the exact expansion
\[
 x^{\mathsf T}Q(k_0)x
 =y^{\mathsf T}Q(k_0)y
 +2z^{\mathsf T}N^{\mathsf T}Q(k_0)y
 +z^{\mathsf T}N^{\mathsf T}Q(k_0)Nz.                      \tag{10}
\]
If criterion \((\mathrm C)\) holds, (10) is zero throughout the affine hull and hence throughout \(\mathcal F_t\).  Equations (7)--(8), with positive denominator, then give \(\operatorname{CSE}^{\rm P}_t(x)=k_0\) for every \(x\in\mathcal F_t\).

Conversely, suppose the controlled-risk ratio is constant on \(\mathcal F_t\).  Its value must be its value at \(y\), namely \(k_0\).  By (5)--(6), \(\mathcal F_t\) contains a relative open neighborhood of \(y\) in \(y+\ker H\).  Hence the quadratic polynomial in (10) vanishes for all sufficiently small \(z\).  Setting \(z=se_i\) and subtracting the identities at \(s\) and \(-s\) gives
\[
 4s\,e_i^{\mathsf T}N^{\mathsf T}Q(k_0)y=0,
\]
so \(N^{\mathsf T}Q(k_0)y=0\).  The remaining quadratic form vanishes near zero.  Taking \(z=se_i\) shows that every diagonal entry of \(N^{\mathsf T}Q(k_0)N\) is zero; taking \(z=s(e_i+e_j)\) then shows every off-diagonal entry is zero.  Together with (9), this is exactly \((\mathrm C)\).  This proves necessity, sufficiency, and invariance to the chosen anchor and null-space basis, because each version is equivalent to constancy on the same set \(\mathcal F_t\).

Under \(\mathcal O_t\), equality with the observed comparator holds precisely when the just-proved constancy criterion holds and its constant \(k_0\) equals \(k_{\rm obs}\).  This proves the claimed collapse boundary without defining the causal target to equal the observed functional.

For completeness, the nonconstant branch is constructive.  If \((\mathrm C)\) fails, (9) shows that either \(N^{\mathsf T}Q(k_0)y\neq0\), in which case choose a coordinate vector \(z\) with
\(z^{\mathsf T}N^{\mathsf T}Q(k_0)y\neq0\), or that vector is zero and \(N^{\mathsf T}Q(k_0)N\neq0\), in which case choose \(z\) with
\(z^{\mathsf T}N^{\mathsf T}Q(k_0)Nz\neq0\).  In the latter case such a \(z\) is found by testing the coordinate vectors and their pairwise sums.  Set \(n=Nz\).  By (5),
\[
 \bar e=\min\left(1,
 \min_{\substack{j\notin I_0\\G_j^{\mathsf T}n\neq0}}
 \frac{u_j-G_j^{\mathsf T}y}{2|G_j^{\mathsf T}n|},
 \min_{\substack{a,v\\\ell_{av}^{\mathsf T}n\neq0}}
 \frac{\ell_{av}^{\mathsf T}y}{2|\ell_{av}^{\mathsf T}n|}
 \right)>0,                                                  \tag{11}
\]
where an empty inner minimum is omitted.  Universal equalities are preserved because \(Hn=0\), and (11) implies \(y+en\in\mathcal F_t\) for every \(0<e<\bar e\).  The polynomial
\[
 \phi(e)=(y+en)^{\mathsf T}Q(k_0)(y+en)
 =2e\,n^{\mathsf T}Q(k_0)y+e^2n^{\mathsf T}Q(k_0)n
\]
is nonzero by the rule selecting \(z\).  It has at most one nonzero root.  Choose any \(e\in(0,\bar e)\) other than that root and set
\(x_1=y\), \(x_2=y+en\).  Equations (7)--(9) imply
\[
 \operatorname{CSE}^{\rm P}_t(x_1)=k_0,
 \qquad \operatorname{CSE}^{\rm P}_t(x_2)\neq k_0.
\]
Exact history compression lifts \(x_1\) and \(x_2\) to laws in \(\mathfrak M_\delta(b,q,\omega)\), so these are compatible-law certificates rather than merely algebraic points.  Finally, if \((\mathrm C)\) holds but \(k_0\neq k_{\rm obs}\), exact history compression lifts \(y\), and its four risks are the four positive numbers in (7); (7) calculates its value as \(k_0\neq k_{\rm obs}\).  This supplies the required one-law observable-mismatch certificate and completes all branches.
