%%% FIELD proof-selector-control
Condition throughout on \(E_0\) and on the complementary-fold center \(\widetilde z_{sk}=z\).  By \texttt{ass:donor-independence}, this additional conditioning does not change the conditional law of the historical calendar process, and hence does not change the geometric bound in \texttt{ass:temporal-mixing}.  For \(z\in[0,1]^d\) and \(0<h\le1\), define
\[
 \ell_{z,h}(x)=K((x-z)/h),
 \qquad
 \Phi_{z,h}(x)=K((x-z)/h)b_r((x-z)/h)b_r((x-z)/h)^{\top}.
\]
The support and boundedness conditions on \(K\), the density bounds in \texttt{ass:design-density}, and the change of variables \(x=z+hu\) give a constant \(C_0<\infty\) such that
\[
 \mathbb E_P[\ell_{z,h}(X_{st})\mid E_0]
 \le C_0h^d.
\tag{1}
\]
There is also a uniform lower bound.  Indeed, choose \(\eta>0\) so that \(K\) is bounded below by a positive constant on \([-\eta,\eta]^d\).  For every \(z\) and every coordinate, at least one of the two coordinate directions from \(z\) remains in \([0,1]\) for distance \(1/2\).  Consequently, after the preceding change of variables, the integration region contains one of the \(2^d\) orthant boxes of side \(\eta\wedge(1/2)\).  On each such box a nonzero polynomial \(v^{\top}b_r(u)\) cannot vanish almost everywhere.  Since there are finitely many boxes and the unit sphere in the fixed-dimensional space \(\mathbb R^{q_r}\) is compact,
\[
 \min_{\|v\|_2=1}\int_{O}
       \{v^{\top}b_r(u)\}^2\,du>0
\]
uniformly over those boxes \(O\).  The lower density bound therefore yields constants \(c_0,C_0>0\), depending only on the fixed class and procedural constants, for which
\[
 c_0h^d
 \le \mathbb E_P[\ell_{z,h}(X_{st})\mid E_0]
 \le C_0h^d,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_P[v^{\top}\Phi_{z,h}(X_{st})v\mid E_0]
 \ge c_0h^d\|v\|_2^2.
\tag{2}
\]
This argument covers centers on the boundary of the cube and hence, a fortiori, the interior centers required by \texttt{ass:interior-event-path}.  Fix
\[
 0<c_G<\frac{c_0}{4C_0}.
\tag{3}
\]

We next establish the needed concentration bound directly from absolute regularity.  Let \(A_0>2\lambda/d+2\) be fixed and put
\[
 b_T=\left\lceil C_\beta\log(T\vee2)\right\rceil,
\]
where \(C_\beta\) is sufficiently large that
\[
 T\beta_0\rho_0^{b_T}\le T^{-A_0}.
\tag{4}
\]
Partition \(\{1,\ldots,T\}\) into consecutive blocks of length at most \(b_T\), and separate the blocks into their odd and even subfamilies.  Successive blocks in either subfamily are separated by at least \(b_T\) calendar indices.  The definition of the conditional absolute-regularity coefficient implies, by replacing the blocks successively by independent copies and telescoping total-variation distances, that the joint law of either subfamily differs from the product of its block marginals by at most
\[
 C(T/b_T)\beta_P(b_T\mid E_0)
 \le CT^{-A_0}.
\tag{5}
\]
For completeness, the telescoping step follows because replacing one conditional block law by its marginal changes the integral of every indicator by at most the corresponding total-variation distance; summing these changes over the blocks gives the first expression in (5).  Thus no independence assertion beyond \texttt{ass:temporal-mixing} is being imposed.

Let \(F\) be either \(\ell_{z,h}\) or \(v^{\top}\Phi_{z,h}v\) with \(\|v\|_2=1\), and, for one parity subfamily, let \(W_g\) be the sum of \(F(X_{st})\) over block \(g\).  Boundedness of \(K\) and of \(b_r\) on the support of \(K\) gives
\[
 0\le W_g\le Cb_T,
 \qquad
 W_g^2\le Cb_TW_g.
\]
For the product law in (5), the block variables are independent, and (1)--(2) imply
\[
 \sum_g\operatorname{Var}(W_g)
 \le \sum_g\mathbb E[W_g^2]
 \le Cb_T\sum_g\mathbb E[W_g]
 \le Cb_TTh^d.
\tag{6}
\]
If \(V_g=W_g-\mathbb EW_g\), expansion of the exponential series and the bounds \(|V_g|\le Cb_T\) and \(\mathbb E[V_g^2]=\operatorname{Var}(W_g)\) give, for \(0<\theta<(Cb_T)^{-1}\),
\[
 \mathbb E e^{\theta V_g}
 \le
 \exp\!\left\{
   \frac{\theta^2\operatorname{Var}(W_g)}
        {2(1-C\theta b_T)}
 \right\}.
\tag{7}
\]
Multiplying (7) over the independent blocks, applying Chernoff's inequality, and choosing \(\theta\asymp b_T^{-1}\) for a deviation of size \(cTh^d\) yields
\[
 P\!\left(
  \left|\sum_g\{W_g-\mathbb EW_g\}\right|>cTh^d
 \right)
 \le C\exp\!\left(-c\frac{Th^d}{b_T}\right).
\tag{8}
\]
Applying (8) separately to the two parity subfamilies, using a union bound, and then using (5) gives the same right-hand side plus \(CT^{-A_0}\) for the original dependent calendar sample.

Apply this conclusion first to \(\ell_{z,h}\), and then to \(v^{\top}\Phi_{z,h}v\) on a fixed \(1/4\)-net \(\mathcal V\) of the unit sphere in \(\mathbb R^{q_r}\).  The cardinality of \(\mathcal V\) is a fixed constant.  For every symmetric matrix \(A\), the elementary net inequality
\[
 \|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le 2\max_{v\in\mathcal V}|v^{\top}Av|
\tag{9}
\]
follows by approximating a maximizing unit vector by an element of \(\mathcal V\).  Equations (2), (8), and (9) therefore show that, except on an event of probability at most
\[
 C\exp\!\left(-c\frac{Th^d}{b_T}\right)+CT^{-A_0},
\tag{10}
\]
the calendar-level mass and Gram matrix satisfy
\[
 \sum_{t=1}^T\ell_{z,h}(X_{st})\le2C_0Th^d,
 \qquad
 \sum_{t=1}^T\Phi_{z,h}(X_{st})\succeq\frac{c_0}{2}Th^d I_{q_r}.
\tag{11}
\]
Weights and local coordinates do not depend on the replicated-unit index \(i\).  Hence the quantities in \texttt{def:feasible-estimator} obey
\[
 L_{sk}(h)=n_s\sum_{t=1}^T\ell_{z,h}(X_{st}),
 \qquad
 G_{sk}(h)=n_s\sum_{t=1}^T\Phi_{z,h}(X_{st}).
\tag{12}
\]
It follows from (3), (11), and (12) that the candidate passes the normalized-shape rule outside the event in (10).  Thus
\[
 P\!\left\{
 L_{sk}(h)=0\ \text{or}\
 \lambda_{\min}\!\left(\frac{G_{sk}(h)}{L_{sk}(h)}\right)<c_G
 \;\middle|\;E_0,\widetilde z_{sk}
 \right\}
 \le C\exp\!\left(-c\frac{Th^d}{b_T}\right)+CT^{-A_0}.
\tag{13}
\]
Notice that replication cancels from the ratio in (12); an upper local-mass condition was not used.

Write \(h_0=h_{s,\beta}\).  If \(h_0<1\), its definition gives
\[
 \frac{T(2^vh_0)^d}{b_T}
 \ge c2^{vd}
\tag{14}
\]
for every dyadic candidate \(2^vh_0\le1\), after choosing the fixed constant \(C_h\) sufficiently large.  If the selector has not stopped by that candidate, the candidate itself fails.  Substitution of (14) into (13), with \(A=A_0\), proves
\[
 P\{h_{sk}>2^vh_{s,\beta}\mid E_0,\widetilde z_{sk}\}
 \le C\exp(-c2^{vd})+CT^{-A}.
\tag{15}
\]
The assertion is immediate when \(h_0=1\).  At the terminal candidate \(h=1\), (13) gives
\[
 P\{\text{terminal-grid failure}\mid E_0,\widetilde z_{sk}\}
 \le C\exp\{-cT/\log(T\vee2)\}+CT^{-A}.
\tag{16}
\]

Fix \(a>0\).  The same proof of (13) may be run with an arbitrarily large exponent \(A_a>a/d+2\) in place of \(A_0\), merely by increasing the auxiliary blocking constant \(C_\beta\); this does not alter the estimator or its bandwidth grid.  The dyadic tail-sum inequality and (15), with this stronger remainder, yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_P[h_{sk}^a\mathbf1_{\mathrm{good}}\mid E_0,\widetilde z_{sk}]
 &\le h_0^a
 +\sum_{v:\,2^vh_0<1}(2^{v+1}h_0)^a
 P\{h_{sk}>2^vh_0\mid E_0,\widetilde z_{sk}\}\\
 &\le Ch_0^a+CT^{-A_a}\log(T\vee2).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
Here \(\sum_{v\ge0}2^{av}\exp(-c2^{vd})<\infty\).  The definition of \(h_0\), including its truncation at one, and \(A_a>a/d+2\) now imply
\[
 \mathbb E_P[h_{sk}^a\mathbf1_{\mathrm{good}}\mid E_0,\widetilde z_{sk}]
 \le C\left\{
 (Tn_s)^{-a/(2\lambda+d)}
 +\left(\frac{\log(T\vee2)}T\right)^{a/d}
 +N^{-a/2}
 \right\}.
\tag{18}
\]

It remains to verify the asserted control of the complete bad-sample event.  Because \(S\) and \(K_D\) are fixed, a union bound in (16) controls all cohort-fold fitting failures.  A successful Gram matrix is invertible by the normalized-shape rule, so all successful-fit weights are finite.  The sample covariance of finite donor observations is finite almost surely under \texttt{ass:donor-fourth-moment}, and spectral clipping to \([c_\Sigma/2,2C_\Sigma]\) is then a defined measurable operation by \texttt{ass:donor-ellipticity}.  Moreover, for every nonempty balanced donor complement, fixed \(D=Sd\), fixed \(K_D\), and fixed \(\alpha_{\mathrm{don}}>0\), the displayed formulas in \texttt{def:honest-interval} imply
\[
 M_4(m_k)\le C,
 \qquad q_k\le C,
 \qquad \delta_k\le Cm_k^{-1/2}.
\tag{19}
\]
Since \(m_k\asymp N\), (19) makes \(\max_k\delta_k\le\delta_0\) for all sufficiently large \(N\).  For the finitely many smaller \(N\), including values producing an empty complement, \(R_{\mathrm{don}}=N^{-\kappa}\) is bounded below by a positive constant, so their deterministic bad-event indicators are at most \(CR_{\mathrm{don}}\).  Finally, because \(A>2\lambda/d+2\),
\[
 \exp\{-cT/\log(T\vee2)\}+T^{-A}
 \le C\left\{\frac{\log(T\vee2)}T\right\}^{2\lambda/d}
 =CR_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}.
\tag{20}
\]
Equations (16), (19), and (20), together with the definitions in \texttt{def:rate-frontier}, prove uniformly along all stated arrays that
\[
 P\{\mathrm{bad}\mid E_0\}\le CR_{\star,\beta}.
\tag{21}
\]
The uninflated smoothing-scale local count may satisfy
\[
 T\{(Tn_s)^{-1/(2\lambda+d)}\}^d=O(1),
\]
but the log-adjusted definition of \(h_{s,\beta}\) gives, whether or not its truncation at one binds,
\[
 Th_{s,\beta}^d\ge c\log(T\vee2).
\tag{22}
\]
Equation (22), rather than a dense-local-site assumption, is exactly the concentration input in (14).  No exogenous dense-local-site or upper-local-mass condition has been imposed.
