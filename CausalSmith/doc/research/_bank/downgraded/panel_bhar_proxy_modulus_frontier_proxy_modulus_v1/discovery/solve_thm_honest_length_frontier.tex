Put \(D=Sd\).  The compatibility calculation in
\texttt{lem:joint-law-embedding} gives
\[
D^2\le
\mathbb E_P\!\left[
\left\|\Sigma^{-1/2}(Z_1-z_0)\right\|_2^4
\,\middle|\,E_0\right]
\le B.
\tag{66}
\]
Thus the class is empty when \(B<D^2\), and there is no substantive
minimax assertion in that case.  Fix henceforth \(B\ge D^2\),
\(P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger}(\lambda)\), and an admissible
event state outside a conditional null set.  Let
\[
\mathscr X=\sigma\{X_{st}:1\le s\le S,\ 1\le t\le T\}.
\]
Because \(z_0\) is measurable with respect to \(E_0\), conditioning on
\((z_0,E_0)\) is the same as conditioning on \(E_0\) for the quantities
below.

We first prove finite-sample conditional coverage.  On each successful fit,
polynomial reproduction from \texttt{lem:full-data-local-polynomial-control},
applied to the degree-\(r\) Taylor polynomial of \(m_s\) at
\(\widetilde z_s\), gives
\[
\left|
e_0^\top\widehat\gamma_s-m_s(\widetilde z_s)
-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}a_{sti}\varepsilon_{sti}
\right|
\le
C_{r,d}L\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}|a_{sti}|
\|X_{st}-\widetilde z_s\|_2^\lambda.
\tag{67}
\]
After multiplication by the cohort weights and summation over \(s\), the
right-hand side is exactly \(A_L\).

Define the historical unit-cluster variables
\[
\mathsf H_{si}=\pi_s\sum_{t=1}^{T}a_{sti}\varepsilon_{sti}.
\]
Assumption \texttt{ass:historical-full-design-centering} centers these
variables given \((\mathscr X,E_0)\).  Assumption
\texttt{ass:donor-independence} preserves the centering after adjoining all
donors, and \texttt{ass:cross-unit-independence} gives independence over
\((s,i)\) under this conditioning.  By
\texttt{ass:bounded-historical-wealth}, conditional on the complete design
the range length of \(\mathsf H_{si}\) is at most
\[
c_{si}=B\pi_s\sum_{t=1}^{T}|a_{sti}|.
\]
For completeness, if a conditionally centered random variable \(U\) lies in
an interval of length \(c\), convexity of \(x\mapsto e^{vx}\) bounds its
conditional exponential moment by the endpoint mixture with the same mean;
differentiating the logarithm of that two-point mixture yields
\[
\mathbb E[e^{vU}\mid\mathscr X,E_0,(Z_j)_{j=1}^{N}]
\le e^{v^2c^2/8}.
\tag{68}
\]
Multiplying (68) over the conditionally independent unit clusters, applying
the exponential Markov inequality, optimizing over \(v>0\), and repeating
the argument for \(-U\) give
\[
P_P\!\left\{
\left|\sum_{s=1}^{S}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}\mathsf H_{si}\right|
>R_{\mathrm{hist}}
\,\middle|\,\mathscr X,E_0,(Z_j)_{j=1}^{N}
\right\}
\le\alpha_{\mathrm{hist}}.
\tag{69}
\]
Thus a unit's whole calendar history is one bounded cluster; no temporal
outcome mixing is used.

Similarly, define
\[
\mathsf E_{si}=\frac{\pi_s}{n_s}
\left\{B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}
-\mathbb E_P[B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid E_0]\right\}.
\]
Assumption \texttt{ass:event-full-design-mean-stability} centers
\(\mathsf E_{si}\) given \((\mathscr X,E_0)\); donor independence preserves
this centering after donors are adjoined, and cross-unit independence again
gives conditional independence.  By \texttt{ass:bounded-event-wealth}, their
conditional range lengths are at most \(B\pi_s/n_s\).  The same exponential
moment calculation therefore gives
\[
P_P\!\left\{
\left|\sum_{s=1}^{S}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}\mathsf E_{si}\right|
>R_{\mathrm{evt}}
\,\middle|\,\mathscr X,E_0,(Z_j)_{j=1}^{N}
\right\}
\le\alpha_{\mathrm{evt}}.
\tag{70}
\]
A union bound combines (69) and (70); independence between one unit's
historical path and event outcome is not required.

For donor calibration, define only in this proof
\[
Y_j=\Sigma^{-1/2}(Z_j-z_0),
\qquad
W_N=N^{-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}Y_j.
\]
By the definition of \(z_0\), \(\mathbb E_P[Y_j\mid E_0]=0\), and by the
definition of \(\Sigma\) and \texttt{ass:donor-ellipticity},
\(\mathbb E_P[Y_jY_j^\top\mid E_0]=I_D\).  Conditional independent donor
replication and expansion of \(\|\sum_jY_j\|_2^4\) show that the terms whose
indices occur once vanish, while the pairings contribute
\(D^2+2D\).  Using \texttt{ass:donor-fourth-moment},
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_P[\|W_N\|_2^4\mid E_0]
&=\frac1N\mathbb E_P[\|Y_1\|_2^4\mid E_0]
+\left(1-\frac1N\right)(D^2+2D)\\
&\le \frac BN+\left(1-\frac1N\right)(D^2+2D)
=M_4(N).
\end{aligned}
\tag{71}
\]
Let \(\mathcal D_N=\{\|W_N\|_2\le q_N\}\).  Markov's inequality and the
definition of \(q_N\) imply
\[
P_P(\mathcal D_N^c\mid E_0)
\le \frac{M_4(N)}{q_N^4}=\alpha_{\mathrm{don}}.
\tag{72}
\]
Projection onto the closed convex cube \(([0,1]^d)^S\) cannot increase
Euclidean distance from \(z_0\), since \(z_0\) belongs to that cube.  The
covariance eigenvalue bounds therefore give, on \(\mathcal D_N\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\Sigma^{-1/2}(\widetilde z-z_0)\|_2
&\le c_\Sigma^{-1/2}\|\widetilde z-z_0\|_2\\
&\le c_\Sigma^{-1/2}\|\bar Z-z_0\|_2\\
&\le \sqrt{C_\Sigma/c_\Sigma}\,q_N/\sqrt N
=\delta_N.
\end{aligned}
\tag{73}
\]
On the good sample in \texttt{def:honest-interval},
\(\delta_N\le\delta_0\).  Hence the upper half of
\texttt{ass:local-two-sided-modulus} and (73) imply
\[
\left|
\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s
\{m_s(\widetilde z_s)-m_s(z_{0s})\}
\right|
\le C_{\mathrm{mod}}\delta_N^\kappa=A_{\mathrm{don}}.
\tag{74}
\]

Let \(\mathcal G\) be the selector-and-radius good event from
\texttt{def:honest-interval}; it is measurable with respect to
\((\mathscr X,E_0,(Z_j)_{j=1}^{N})\).  On \(\mathcal G^c\), the interval is
the fallback \([-B,B]\).  Assumptions \texttt{ass:bounded-event-wealth} and
the positive weights summing to one imply \(\tau_n(z_0)\in[-B,B]\).  On
\(\mathcal G\cap\mathcal D_N\), \texttt{lem:identification-fwl}, (67), and
(74) decompose \(\widehat\tau-\tau_n(z_0)\) into the event fluctuation, the
negative of the historical fluctuation, and two remainders bounded in
absolute value by \(A_L\) and \(A_{\mathrm{don}}\).  Thus noncoverage there
implies failure of (69) or (70).  Iterated conditioning and Bonferroni's
inequality give, for every positive \(N,T,n_1,\ldots,n_S\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_P\{\tau_n(z_0)\notin C_\alpha\mid E_0\}
&\le P_P(\mathcal D_N^c\mid E_0)\\
&\quad+
\mathbb E_P\!\left[
\mathbf 1_{\mathcal G\cap\mathcal D_N}
P_P\!\left\{
\text{historical failure or event failure}
\,\middle|\,\mathscr X,E_0,(Z_j)_{j=1}^{N}
\right\}
\,\middle|\,E_0\right]\\
&\le \alpha_{\mathrm{don}}+\alpha_{\mathrm{hist}}
+\alpha_{\mathrm{evt}}=\alpha.
\end{aligned}
\tag{75}
\]
No selector-tail probability enters (75), so coverage is exact and
finite-sample, including at \(B=D^2\).  Intersecting the untruncated interval
with \([-B,B]\) cannot remove the target.

We next prove the expected-length upper bound.  The deterministic bounds in
\texttt{lem:full-data-local-polynomial-control} yield on every successful
fit
\[
R_{\mathrm{hist}}
\le B\left\{
\frac12\log(2/\alpha_{\mathrm{hist}})c_G^{-1}R_{\mathrm{tr}}
\right\}^{1/2},
\qquad
R_{\mathrm{evt}}
=B\left\{
\frac12\log(2/\alpha_{\mathrm{evt}})R_{\mathrm{tr}}
\right\}^{1/2}.
\tag{76}
\]
Compact support of \(K\) and the same deterministic bound on
\(\sum_{t,i}|a_{sti}|\) give
\[
A_L\le C\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_sh_s^\lambda
\quad\text{on }\mathcal G.
\tag{77}
\]
Apply \texttt{lem:geometric-mixing-selector-control} with exponent
\(a=\lambda\), conditionally on the projected donor center.  Since \(S\) is
fixed, Cauchy--Schwarz across cohorts, \(\sum_s\pi_s=1\), and
\(\kappa=\min\{\lambda,1\}\) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sup_P\mathbb E_P[A_L\mathbf 1_{\mathcal G}\mid E_0]
&\le C\left\{
R_{\mathrm{sm}}^{1/2}
+R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}^{1/2}+N^{-\lambda/2}
\right\}\\
&\le CR_{\star,\beta}^{1/2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{78}
\]
Because \(D,B,\alpha\) are fixed, (71) implies \(q_N\le C\), whence
\[
A_{\mathrm{don}}
\le CN^{-\kappa/2}=CR_{\mathrm{don}}^{1/2}.
\tag{79}
\]
The selector lemma also gives
\(P_P(\mathcal G^c\mid E_0)\le CR_{\star,\beta}\) along every asserted
array.  On \(\mathcal G\), interval length is at most \(2H_\alpha\); on
\(\mathcal G^c\), it is at most \(2B\).  Since
\(R_{\star,\beta}\to0\) along those arrays, eventually
\(R_{\star,\beta}\le R_{\star,\beta}^{1/2}\).  Combining (76)--(79) proves,
with a constant independently quantified in each compatible envelope
regime,
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger}(\lambda)}
\mathbb E_P[\operatorname{length}(C_\alpha)\mid E_0]
\le C_\alpha^{\mathrm{up}}R_{\star,\beta}^{1/2}.
\tag{80}
\]

It remains to establish the expected-length converse.  The treated,
smoothing, and donor constructions in \texttt{lem:joint-law-embedding} have
perturbation constants that may be decreased while remaining fixed along the
array.  Their displayed divergence calculations then make the testing
affinity arbitrarily close to one while retaining separation by a positive
fixed multiple of \(R_q^{1/2}\).  In the spacing construction in
\texttt{lem:geometric-mixing-spacing-obstruction}, decreasing its fixed
rare-block probability constant makes the no-active-block probability, and
hence the testing affinity, arbitrarily close to one while retaining a
positive fixed multiple of \(R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}^{1/2}\).  Therefore, for
the fixed \(\alpha<1/2\), the construction constants may be selected so that
every available channel affinity
\[
A_q=\int\min\{dP_{0,q},dP_{1,q}\}
\]
exceeds \(2\alpha+a_0\) for some fixed \(a_0>0\).  In the donor channel the
choice, and hence the resulting lower-bound constant, may depend on the
fixed slack \(B-D^2\).

Let \(I\) be any finite-sample conditionally honest observed-data interval,
write the two targets in a channel pair as \(\theta_0,\theta_1\), and let
\(\mu_q=P_{0,q}\wedge P_{1,q}\) denote the conditional overlap measure.
Uniform conditional honesty implies
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu_q\{\theta_0,\theta_1\in I\}
&\ge \mu_q(\Omega)
-P_{0,q}\{\theta_0\notin I\mid E_0\}
-P_{1,q}\{\theta_1\notin I\mid E_0\}\\
&\ge A_q-2\alpha\ge a_0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{81}
\]
On this event, \(\operatorname{length}(I)\ge|\theta_1-\theta_0|\).
Consequently,
\[
\max_{\ell\in\{0,1\}}
\mathbb E_{P_{\ell,q}}[\operatorname{length}(I)\mid E_0]
\ge \int\operatorname{length}(I)\,d\mu_q
\ge a_0|\theta_1-\theta_0|
\ge c_\alpha R_q^{1/2}.
\tag{82}
\]
For a fixed interior-compatible envelope \(B>D^2\), all four pairs in
\texttt{lem:joint-law-embedding} are available.  Maximizing (82) over
\(q\in\{\mathrm{tr},\mathrm{sm},\mathrm{sp},\mathrm{don}\}\) gives
\(c_{\alpha,>}R_{\star,\beta}^{1/2}\), with dependence on the fixed slack
allowed.  At \(B=D^2\), only the treated, smoothing, and spacing pairs are
available, so (82) gives
\[
c_{\alpha,=}
\{R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sm}}\vee
R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}\}^{1/2};
\tag{83}
\]
no donor converse is used or asserted at equality.  The constants in these
two cases are independently quantified.

Finally, if an interval sequence is only uniformly asymptotically
conditionally honest, then for every \(\epsilon>0\) both noncoverage
probabilities in (81) are eventually at most \(\alpha+\epsilon\).  Taking
\(\epsilon<a_0/4\) leaves overlap probability at least \(a_0/2\), so (82)
and the same rates continue to hold.  Equations (75), (80), and (82)--(83)
prove all asserted coverage and expected-length conclusions.
