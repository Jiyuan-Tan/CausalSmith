Fix a cohort \(s\) for which the normalized-shape fit succeeds.  Suppress the cohort subscript temporarily and put \(\mathsf L=L_s\), \(G=G_s\), and
\[
v_{ti}=w_{st}^{1/2}b_r(u_{st}).
\]
The kernel is nonnegative by \texttt{def:feasible-estimator}, so this square root is defined.  The definitions of \(G_s\), \(L_s\), and \(a_{sti}\) give
\[
G=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}v_{ti}v_{ti}^{\top},
\qquad
\mathsf L=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}w_{st},
\qquad
a_{sti}=w_{st}^{1/2}e_0^{\top}G^{-1}v_{ti}.
\tag{1}
\]
Success of the selector means both \(\mathsf L>0\) and
\[
G/\mathsf L\succeq c_G I_{q_r}.
\tag{2}
\]
In particular, \(G\) is invertible, as required in (1).  Applying Cauchy--Schwarz to (1), and then using the first identity in (1), yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}|a_{sti}|
&\le
\left\{\sum_{t,i}w_{st}\right\}^{1/2}
\left\{\sum_{t,i}
       (e_0^{\top}G^{-1}v_{ti})^2\right\}^{1/2}\\
&=\mathsf L^{1/2}
\{e_0^{\top}G^{-1}GG^{-1}e_0\}^{1/2}\\
&=\{\mathsf L e_0^{\top}G^{-1}e_0\}^{1/2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
By (2), inversion reverses the Loewner order and hence
\[
G^{-1}\preceq(c_G\mathsf L)^{-1}I_{q_r}.
\tag{4}
\]
The monomial vector \(b_r\) contains the constant monomial and every nonconstant monomial vanishes at zero.  Thus the definition \(e_0=b_r(0)\) gives \(\|e_0\|_2=1\).  Combining (3) and (4) proves
\[
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}|a_{sti}|
\le \{L_s e_0^{\top}G_s^{-1}e_0\}^{1/2}
\le c_G^{-1/2}.
\tag{5}
\]

For fixed \((s,t)\), the definitions of \(u_{st}\) and \(w_{st}\) do not involve \(i\).  Consequently
\[
G_s=n_s\sum_{t=1}^{T}w_{st}b_r(u_{st})b_r(u_{st})^{\top},
\]
and the definition of \(a_{sti}\) shows that \(a_{sti}\) is constant in \(i\).  Therefore (5) implies, for every \(i\in\{1,\ldots,n_s\}\),
\[
\sum_{t=1}^{T}|a_{sti}|
=\frac1{n_s}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i'=1}^{n_s}|a_{sti'}|
\le \frac{c_G^{-1/2}}{n_s}.
\tag{6}
\]
Using (6), the positivity of the cohort weights, and the definition of \(R_{\mathrm{tr}}\) in \texttt{def:rate-frontier}, we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{s=1}^{S}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}
\left[\pi_s\sum_{t=1}^{T}|a_{sti}|\right]^2
&\le \sum_{s=1}^{S}n_s
\left(\frac{\pi_s c_G^{-1/2}}{n_s}\right)^2\\
&=c_G^{-1}\sum_{s=1}^{S}\frac{\pi_s^2}{n_s}
=c_G^{-1}R_{\mathrm{tr}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]

It remains to establish the Taylor-bias assertion.  If \(p(u)=c^{\top}b_r(u)\) is any polynomial of total degree at most \(r\), then the definitions of \(a_{sti}\) and \(G_s\) imply the exact reproduction identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}a_{sti}p(u_{st})
&=e_0^{\top}G_s^{-1}
  \sum_{t,i}w_{st}b_r(u_{st})b_r(u_{st})^{\top}c\\
&=e_0^{\top}G_s^{-1}G_sc
=e_0^{\top}c
=p(0).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
For each cohort, let \(P_s\) be the degree-\(r\) Taylor polynomial of \(m_s\) about \(\widetilde z_s\).  The function \(u\mapsto P_s(\widetilde z_s+h_su)\) has total degree at most \(r\), so (8) gives
\[
\sum_{t,i}a_{sti}P_s(X_{st})=P_s(\widetilde z_s)=m_s(\widetilde z_s).
\tag{9}
\]
Under \texttt{ass:holder-smoothness}, with \(r=\lceil\lambda\rceil-1\), the declared H\"older-ball Taylor remainder convention gives, for \(x,\widetilde z_s\in[0,1]^d\),
\[
|m_s(x)-P_s(x)|
\le C_{r,d}L\|x-\widetilde z_s\|_2^{\lambda}.
\tag{10}
\]
The cube projection in \texttt{def:feasible-estimator} ensures \(\widetilde z_s\in[0,1]^d\), while the support declaration gives \(X_{st}\in[0,1]^d\), so (10) applies to every summand.  From (9)--(10) and the triangle inequality,
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\left|
\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s
\left\{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}
a_{sti}m_s(X_{st})-m_s(\widetilde z_s)\right\}
\right|\\
&\qquad\le
C_{r,d}L\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}|a_{sti}|
\|X_{st}-\widetilde z_s\|_2^{\lambda}
=A_L,
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
where the last equality is the definition in \texttt{def:honest-interval}.  Equations (5), (6), (7), and (11) establish all claimed conclusions.  The argument used only the lower normalized-shape condition (2); at no point was an upper bound on \(L_s\) imposed.
