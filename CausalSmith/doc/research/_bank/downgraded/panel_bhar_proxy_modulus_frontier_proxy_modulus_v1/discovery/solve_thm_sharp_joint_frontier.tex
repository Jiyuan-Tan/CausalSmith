Put \(D=Sd\).  By \texttt{lem:joint-law-embedding}, the standardized fourth-moment and covariance requirements force \(B\ge D^2\), so the class is empty when \(B<D^2\).  We therefore prove the two nonempty cases separately.

First suppose \(B>D^2\).  For each \(q\in\{\mathrm{tr},\mathrm{sm},\mathrm{sp},\mathrm{don}\}\), let \((P_{0,q},P_{1,q})\) be the conditional two-point experiment supplied by \texttt{lem:joint-law-embedding}.  Write
\[
\theta_{\ell,q}=\tau_{n,P_{\ell,q}}(z_0),
\qquad
\Delta_q=|\theta_{1,q}-\theta_{0,q}|.
\]
That lemma gives constants \(a_{q,>}>0\) and \(c_{q,>}>0\), independent of \(N,T,\mathbf n\), such that, for almost every admissible \(z_0\in\mathcal Z_+\), the conditional overlap of the two observed-data laws is at least \(a_{q,>}\) and
\[
\Delta_q^2\ge c_{q,>}R_q.
\tag{1}
\]
For an arbitrary observed-data estimator \(\widetilde\tau\), define
\[
A_{0,q}=\{|\widetilde\tau-\theta_{0,q}|\ge\Delta_q/2\},
\qquad
A_{1,q}=\{|\widetilde\tau-\theta_{1,q}|\ge\Delta_q/2\}.
\]
The triangle inequality implies \(A_{0,q}\cup A_{1,q}\) is the entire sample space.  Integrating its indicator against the overlap measure therefore yields
\[
P_{0,q}(A_{0,q}\mid E_0)+P_{1,q}(A_{1,q}\mid E_0)
\ge a_{q,>}.
\tag{2}
\]
It follows from (1)--(2) that
\[
\begin{aligned}
\max_{\ell\in\{0,1\}}
\mathbb E_{P_{\ell,q}}
 [ (\widetilde\tau-\theta_{\ell,q})^2\mid E_0]
&\ge {\Delta_q^2\over4}
 \max_{\ell\in\{0,1\}}P_{\ell,q}(A_{\ell,q}\mid E_0)\\
&\ge {a_{q,>}\Delta_q^2\over8}
\ge {a_{q,>}c_{q,>}\over8}R_q.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
There are only four channels.  Taking their maximum in (3), and using the definition
\(R_{\star,\beta}=R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sm}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}\vee R_{\mathrm{don}}\), proves the strict-slack minimax lower bound with a constant \(c_{>}>0\).  The donor-channel constants, and hence \(c_{>}\), may depend on the fixed slack \(B-D^2\).

If \(B=D^2\), the boundary clause of \texttt{lem:joint-law-embedding} supplies the same argument with independently quantified overlap and separation constants for precisely \(q\in\{\mathrm{tr},\mathrm{sm},\mathrm{sp}\}\).  Maximizing (3) over those three channels gives
\[
\inf_{\widetilde\tau}\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger}(\lambda)}
\mathbb E_P[(\widetilde\tau-\tau_n(z_0))^2\mid E_0]
\ge c_{=}
\{R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sm}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}\}
\tag{4}
\]
for a separately chosen \(c_{=}>0\).  No donor lower experiment is used at equality.

It remains to prove the common upper bound.  Let
\[
\mathscr X=\sigma\{X_{st}:1\le s\le S,\ 1\le t\le T\},
\qquad
\mathscr G=\sigma\{\mathscr X,E_0,Z_1,\ldots,Z_N\}.
\]
Because \(z_0=\mathbb E_P[Z_j\mid E_0]\) is \(E_0\)-measurable, conditioning additionally on \(z_0\) does not alter an \(E_0\)-conditional mean.  On the good sample, \texttt{lem:identification-fwl}, the linear representation in \texttt{def:honest-interval}, and \(Q_{sti}=m_s(X_{st})+\varepsilon_{sti}\) give the exact identity
\[
\widehat\tau-\tau_n(z_0)
=U_{\mathrm{evt}}-U_{\mathrm{hist}}-B_{\mathrm{LP}}-B_{\mathrm{don}},
\tag{5}
\]
where
\[
U_{\mathrm{evt}}
=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}{\pi_s\over n_s}
\{B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}-\mathbb E_P[B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid E_0]\},
\tag{6}
\]
\[
U_{\mathrm{hist}}
=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}\pi_s
\sum_{t=1}^{T}a_{sti}\varepsilon_{sti},
\tag{7}
\]
\[
B_{\mathrm{LP}}
=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s
\left\{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}a_{sti}m_s(X_{st})
-m_s(\widetilde z_s)\right\},
\tag{8}
\]
and
\[
B_{\mathrm{don}}
=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s\{m_s(\widetilde z_s)-m_s(z_{0s})\}.
\tag{9}
\]
The event full-design mean-stability assumption centers every summand in (6) conditionally on \(\mathscr X\) and \(E_0\); the historical full-design centering assumption does the same for every error in (7).  Donor independence permits adjoining \((Z_j)_{j=1}^{N}\) to that conditioning.  Conditional cross-unit independence then makes the unit-level terms in each of (6) and (7) independent across distinct \((s,i)\).  Since every event wealth lies in \([0,B]\),
\[
\mathbb E_P[U_{\mathrm{evt}}^2\mid\mathscr G]
\le B^2\sum_{s=1}^{S}{\pi_s^2\over n_s}
=B^2R_{\mathrm{tr}}.
\tag{10}
\]
Likewise, conditional on \(\mathscr G\), the range of the unit-level random variable \(\sum_t a_{sti}\varepsilon_{sti}\) has length at most \(B\sum_t|a_{sti}|\), because \(0\le Q_{sti}\le B\) and the regression means are then fixed.  Hence \texttt{lem:full-data-local-polynomial-control} gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_P[U_{\mathrm{hist}}^2\mid\mathscr G]
&\le B^2\sum_{s=1}^{S}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}
 \left[\pi_s\sum_{t=1}^{T}|a_{sti}|\right]^2\\
&\le B^2c_G^{-1}R_{\mathrm{tr}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
Thus neither calculation requires identical unit response laws or temporal independence of the outcomes.

We next control (8).  On a successful fit, the definition of \(a_{sti}\) and invertibility of \(G_s\) imply polynomial reproduction:
\[
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}a_{sti}b_r(u_{st})
=G_sG_s^{-1}e_0=e_0.
\tag{12}
\]
Let \(C_K<\infty\) be a fixed Euclidean radius of the compact support of \(K\).  A nonzero weight entails
\(\|X_{st}-\widetilde z_s\|_2\le C_Kh_s\).  Applying the degree-\(r\) Taylor remainder in the declared H\"older ball, using (12) to reproduce the Taylor polynomial at \(\widetilde z_s\), and then invoking the absolute-weight bound in \texttt{lem:full-data-local-polynomial-control}, we obtain
\[
\left|\sum_{t,i}a_{sti}m_s(X_{st})-m_s(\widetilde z_s)\right|
\le C L h_s^\lambda\sum_{t,i}|a_{sti}|
\le C h_s^\lambda.
\tag{13}
\]
Since \(S\) is fixed, Cauchy--Schwarz and \texttt{lem:geometric-mixing-selector-control} with exponent \(2\lambda\), first conditionally on \((E_0,\widetilde z_s)\) and then integrated over the donor sample, show that
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_P[B_{\mathrm{LP}}^2\mathbf1_{\mathrm{good}}\mid E_0]
&\le C\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s^2
 \mathbb E_P[h_s^{2\lambda}\mathbf1_{\mathrm{good}}\mid E_0]\\
&\le C\left\{
\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s^2(Tn_s)^{-2\lambda/(2\lambda+d)}
+\left({\log(T\vee2)\over T}\right)^{2\lambda/d}
+N^{-\lambda}\right\}\\
&\le C R_{\star,\beta},
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
where the last inequality uses \(\kappa=\min\{\lambda,1\}\), and hence \(N^{-\lambda}\le N^{-\kappa}\).

For (9), define the proof-only standardized variables
\[
Y_j=\Sigma^{-1/2}(Z_j-z_0),
\qquad
W_N=N^{-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}Y_j.
\]
Conditional donor replication, the definition of \(z_0\), and the definition of \(\Sigma\) imply
\[
\mathbb E_P[Y_j\mid E_0]=0,
\qquad
\mathbb E_P[Y_jY_j^\top\mid E_0]=I_D,
\]
with the \(Y_j\)'s conditionally independent.  Expanding the fourth power of the Euclidean norm, all products containing a singleton sample index vanish.  The all-equal terms and the three pairings therefore give exactly
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_P[\|W_N\|_2^4\mid E_0]
&={1\over N}\mathbb E_P[\|Y_1\|_2^4\mid E_0]\\
&\quad+\left(1-{1\over N}\right)
\{(\operatorname{tr}I_D)^2+2\operatorname{tr}(I_D^2)\}\\
&\le {B\over N}+\left(1-{1\over N}\right)(D^2+2D)
=M_4(N).
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\mathbb E_P\!\left[
\|\Sigma^{-1/2}(\bar Z-z_0)\|_2^4\mid E_0
\right]
\le C N^{-2}.
\tag{16}
\]
Euclidean projection on the closed cube cannot increase distance from the cube point \(z_0\).  Combining this fact with
\(c_\Sigma I_D\preceq\Sigma\preceq C_\Sigma I_D\) yields
\[
V_N:=\|\Sigma^{-1/2}(\widetilde z-z_0)\|_2
\le \sqrt{C_\Sigma/c_\Sigma}\,
\|\Sigma^{-1/2}(\bar Z-z_0)\|_2.
\tag{17}
\]
On \(\{V_N\le\delta_0\}\), the displacement \(\widetilde z-z_0\) is feasible in \texttt{def:local-modulus}; its upper modulus bound gives
\[
|B_{\mathrm{don}}|\le C_{\mathrm{mod}}V_N^\kappa.
\tag{18}
\]
By Lyapunov's inequality, (16)--(18), and \(0<\kappa\le1\),
\[
\mathbb E_P[B_{\mathrm{don}}^2
 \mathbf1\{V_N\le\delta_0\}\mid E_0]
\le C\{\mathbb E_P[V_N^4\mid E_0]\}^{\kappa/2}
\le C N^{-\kappa}.
\tag{19}
\]
Because \(m_s\) takes values in \([0,B]\) and the positive cohort weights sum to one, \(|B_{\mathrm{don}}|\le B\) everywhere.  Markov's inequality and (16)--(17) thus imply
\[
\mathbb E_P[B_{\mathrm{don}}^2
 \mathbf1\{V_N>\delta_0\}\mid E_0]
\le B^2P(V_N>\delta_0\mid E_0)
\le C N^{-2}
\le C N^{-\kappa}.
\tag{20}
\]
Equations (19)--(20) prove
\[
\mathbb E_P[B_{\mathrm{don}}^2\mid E_0]
\le C R_{\mathrm{don}}.
\tag{21}
\]
Only the covariance eigenvalue bounds and standardized fourth-moment envelope were used; in particular, neither Gaussianity, coordinate homoskedasticity, nor estimation of \(\Sigma\) is required.

It remains to account for the fallback event.  On a successful fit,
\[
|\widehat m_s|
\le B\sum_{t,i}|a_{sti}|
\le Bc_G^{-1/2}
\tag{22}
\]
by \texttt{lem:full-data-local-polynomial-control}, whereas the failed-fit rule sets the intercept contribution to zero.  Event averages and the causal target are bounded by the declared wealth envelope.  Hence \((\widehat\tau-\tau_n(z_0))^2\) is bounded by a fixed constant on the bad sample.  The total bad-sample probability bound in \texttt{lem:geometric-mixing-selector-control} makes its conditional risk contribution at most \(CR_{\star,\beta}\) along every array in the theorem.

Finally, apply \((x_1+x_2+x_3+x_4)^2\le4\sum_{j=1}^{4}x_j^2\) to (5), integrate (10)--(11), and combine the result with (14), (21), and the bad-sample bound.  Uniformly over the full-design-centered class,
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger}(\lambda)}
\mathbb E_P[(\widehat\tau-\tau_n(z_0))^2\mid E_0]
\le C R_{\star,\beta}.
\tag{23}
\]
The constants in (23) depend only on fixed class and procedural constants.  They may be named \(C_{>}\) in the strict-slack regime and, independently, \(C_{=}\) in the equality-boundary regime.  Because \(\widehat\tau\) is an observed-data estimator, the minimax infimum is no larger than its worst-case risk.  Combining (23) with (3) for \(B>D^2\), or with (4) for \(B=D^2\), proves all displayed bounds.  The selector result used in (14) assumes only the declared geometric beta-mixing envelope, so the construction requires no sequential-overlap or anti-clustering condition.
