%%% FIELD proof-identification-fwl
Let \(\mathscr E=\sigma(E_0)\).  By the definition of \(z_0\),
\(z_0=\mathbb E_P[Z_j\mid E_0]\) is \(\mathscr E\)-measurable.  Consequently,
adjoining \(z_0\) to the conditioning field generated by \(E_0\) does not
change any conditional expectation below.  By \texttt{def:target} and
linearity of conditional expectation,
\[
\tau_n(z_0)=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s\left\{
\frac1{n_s}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}\mathbb E_P[B_{si}(1)\mid z_0,E_0]
-\frac1{n_s}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}\mathbb E_P[B_{si}(0)\mid z_0,E_0]
\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Assumption \texttt{ass:consistency} and the definition of
\(\bar B_s^{\mathrm{obs}}\) give
\[
\frac1{n_s}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}\mathbb E_P[B_{si}(1)\mid z_0,E_0]
=\mathbb E_P[\bar B_s^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid z_0,E_0].
\tag{2}
\]
Assumption \texttt{ass:event-transport} gives, for each cohort,
\[
\frac1{n_s}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}\mathbb E_P[B_{si}(0)\mid z_0,E_0]
=m_s(z_{0s}).
\tag{3}
\]
Substitution of (2)--(3) into (1) proves
\[
\tau_n(z_0)=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s
\{\mathbb E_P[\bar B_s^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid z_0,E_0]-m_s(z_{0s})\}.
\]
Assumption \texttt{ass:historical-regression} separately identifies
\(m_s(x)\) as the observed-law conditional mean of an untreated
pseudo-event response at \(X_{st}=x\).  Thus consistency and event transport
prove the equality between the causal contrast and the observed-law
functional; that equality is not introduced as a definition of the causal
target.

Fix a successful cohort \(s\), abbreviate
\(\bar B_s^{\mathrm{obs}}\) by \(\bar B_s\), and hold
\(\gamma\in\mathbb R^{q_r}\) fixed.  The event part of the criterion in
\texttt{def:fwl-regression} satisfies the exact identity
\[
\frac1{n_s}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}
(B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}-\theta-e_0^\top\gamma)^2
=\frac1{n_s}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}(B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}-\bar B_s)^2
+(\bar B_s-\theta-e_0^\top\gamma)^2.
\tag{4}
\]
Consequently, for fixed \(\gamma\), the unique minimizing event coefficient is
\[
\theta=\bar B_s^{\mathrm{obs}}-e_0^\top\gamma.
\tag{5}
\]
At (5), the event criterion is independent of \(\gamma\).  Up to the first,
constant term in (4), the profiled criterion is therefore
\[
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}
w_{st}\{Q_{sti}-b_r(u_{st})^\top\gamma\}^2,
\tag{6}
\]
which is exactly the historical weighted least-squares problem in
\texttt{def:feasible-estimator}.  Passage of the normalized-shape test implies
\(L_s>0\) and
\[
G_s\succeq c_GL_sI_{q_r}\succ0.
\]
Hence (6) has the unique minimizer \(\widehat\gamma_s\), and profiling gives
\[
\widehat\theta_s
=\bar B_s^{\mathrm{obs}}-e_0^\top\widehat\gamma_s.
\tag{7}
\]
Using the definitions of \(\widehat m_s\), \(\theta_{\mathrm{FWL}}\), and
\(\widehat\tau\), on every sample for which all selected fits exist,
\[
\theta_{\mathrm{FWL}}
=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s
(\bar B_s^{\mathrm{obs}}-e_0^\top\widehat\gamma_s)
=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s(\bar B_s^{\mathrm{obs}}-\widehat m_s)
=\widehat\tau.
\]

%%% FIELD proof-canonical-reductions
Under the first specialization, \(S=1\) and \(\sum_s\pi_s=1\) imply
\(\pi_1=1\).  The identifying equality in
\texttt{lem:identification-fwl} and the restriction
\(m_1(x)=x+\Delta_0\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau_n(z_0)
&=\mathbb E_P[\bar B_1^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid z_0,E_0]-m_1(z_{01})\\
&=\mathbb E_P[\bar B_1^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid z_0,E_0]-z_{01}-\Delta_0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
When the scalar proxy is the event cohort's baseline outcome, \(z_{01}\) is
its conditional baseline mean.  When \(\Delta_0\) is the untreated
cohort-average post-minus-baseline mean change, (8) is treated post minus
treated baseline minus the untreated post-minus-baseline change.  This is the
canonical conditional finite-cohort two-period difference-in-differences
contrast.

For the second specialization define the cohort effect
\[
\tau_s=\frac1{n_s}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}
\mathbb E_P[B_{si}(1)-B_{si}(0)\mid z_0,E_0].
\]
Homogeneity across cohorts means that \(\tau_s=\tau^{\mathrm{com}}\) for every
\(s\).  By \texttt{def:target} and \(\sum_s\pi_s=1\),
\[
\tau_n(z_0)=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s\tau_s
=\tau^{\mathrm{com}}\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s
=\tau^{\mathrm{com}}.
\tag{9}
\]
The stipulated absence of carry-over beyond \(h=0\) makes this common
terminal-level contrast the contemporaneous finite-cohort treatment effect on
the level-wealth path in \texttt{def:buyhold-level}.  This causal
interpretation uses the stated specialization; it does not follow from the
aggregation identity alone.

%%% FIELD proof-geometric-mixing-spacing-obstruction
Put \(D=Sd\).  For any class member define, only for this proof,
\[
Y=\Sigma^{-1/2}(Z_1-z_0).
\]
The definitions of \(z_0\) and \(\Sigma\), together with positive
definiteness from \texttt{ass:donor-ellipticity}, imply
\(\mathbb E_P[YY^\top\mid E_0]=I_D\).  Taking traces, applying conditional
Jensen's inequality to the nonnegative random variable \(\lVert Y\rVert_2^2\),
and using \texttt{ass:donor-fourth-moment} give
\[
D^2
=\{\mathbb E_P[\lVert Y\rVert_2^2\mid E_0]\}^2
\le \mathbb E_P[\lVert Y\rVert_2^4\mid E_0]
\le B.
\tag{10}
\]
Thus \(B<D^2\) gives an empty class.  Fix henceforth \(B\ge D^2\), an
admissible interior query, and one cohort \(s_*\) having fixed positive
weight; hold all other cohorts fixed across the two laws.

For sufficiently small fixed constants \(c_p,c_L>0\), set
\[
L_T=\lfloor c_L\log(T\vee2)\rfloor\vee1,
\qquad
p_T=c_p\frac{L_T}{T},
\]
where \(c_L\lvert\log\rho_0\rvert<1\).  Since
\(p_T\asymp \log(T)/T\), whereas
\(\rho_0^{L_T}\asymp T^{-c_L\lvert\log\rho_0\rvert}\), decreasing
\(c_p\), if necessary, gives
\[
2p_T\le \beta_0\rho_0^{L_T-1}
\tag{11}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(T\).  Let \(A_T\) be a Euclidean ball centered
at \(z_{0s_*}\), contained in \([0,1]^d\), and having Lebesgue measure
\(p_T\).  The interior-path condition permits this choice for large \(T\),
and its radius satisfies
\[
h_T\asymp p_T^{1/d}
\asymp\left\{\frac{\log(T\vee2)}{T}\right\}^{1/d}.
\tag{12}
\]

Partition the calendar into deterministic consecutive blocks of length
\(L_T\), apart from a final shorter block.  Independently across blocks draw
\(G_b\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p_T)\).  Conditional on \(G_b=1\), draw the
selected cohort's design points in block \(b\) independently and uniformly on
\(A_T\); conditional on \(G_b=0\), draw them independently and uniformly on
\([0,1]^d\setminus A_T\).  At each time the marginal density is
\[
p_T\frac{\mathbf 1_{A_T}(x)}{p_T}
+(1-p_T)\frac{\mathbf 1_{A_T^c}(x)}{1-p_T}=1.
\tag{13}
\]
The other cohorts may have independent uniform designs.  Hence
\texttt{ass:design-density} holds because \(c_X\le1\le C_X\).  At lags at
least \(L_T\), the two relevant sigma-fields involve independent block
variables.  At a smaller lag, only the block crossing the separating cut can
be shared.  Conditional on that shared Bernoulli variable, the two sides are
independent; expanding the two-component mixture bounds its
absolute-regularity distance from the product law by
\(2p_T(1-p_T)\le2p_T\).  Since
\(\rho_0^\ell\ge\rho_0^{L_T-1}\) for \(1\le\ell<L_T\), (11) implies
\[
\beta_P(\ell_{\mathrm{mix}}\mid E_0)
\le
\begin{cases}
2p_T,&1\le\ell_{\mathrm{mix}}<L_T,\\
0,&\ell_{\mathrm{mix}}\ge L_T,
\end{cases}
\le\beta_0\rho_0^{\ell_{\mathrm{mix}}}.
\tag{14}
\]

Choose a bounded H\"older carrier \(g\) lying in the interior of the response
range.  Give its weighted cohort surface a nonzero affine component at
\(z_0\) when \(\lambda>1\), and an asymmetric \(\lambda\)-cusp there when
\(0<\lambda\le1\); fixed scaling places the carrier inside the H\"older and
two-sided-modulus envelopes.  Let \(\psi\) be a fixed smooth bump supported on
the unit ball with \(\psi(0)=1\), and define in cohort \(s_*\)
\[
m_0(x)=g(x),
\qquad
m_1(x)=g(x)+c_bh_T^\lambda
\psi\left(\frac{x-z_{0s_*}}{h_T}\right).
\tag{15}
\]
Take \(c_b>0\) small enough that both surfaces remain in
\(\mathcal H_d(\lambda,L)\) and in the response interior.  When
\(\lambda>1\), the bump increment over a displacement of norm \(\delta\) is
at most \(Cc_bh_T^{\lambda-1}\delta\) for \(\delta\le h_T\), and at most
\(Cc_bh_T^\lambda\le Cc_bh_T^{\lambda-1}\delta\) for \(\delta>h_T\).
It therefore cannot cancel the fixed affine lower modulus.  When
\(0<\lambda\le1\), the increment is at most \(Cc_b\delta^\lambda\), so it
cannot cancel the carrier's cusp lower modulus when \(c_b\) is small.  The
H\"older upper inequality and the covariance eigenvalue bounds give the upper
standardized modulus.  Thus both laws obey
\texttt{ass:local-two-sided-modulus} with
\(\kappa=\min\{\lambda,1\}\).

Conditional on the complete design
\(\mathscr X=\sigma\{X_{st}:s\le S,t\le T\}\), draw bounded historical
response paths independently across units from kernels having mean
\(m_\ell(X_{st})\); these kernels may vary by unit.  Draw bounded event
outcomes independently of \(\mathscr X\) with a common mean under the two
laws, and complete untreated event potential outcomes so that their
cohort-average conditional means are \(m_\ell(z_{0s})\).  Then
\[
\mathbb E[B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid\mathscr X,E_0]
=\mathbb E[B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid E_0],
\qquad
\mathbb E[\varepsilon_{sti}\mid\mathscr X,E_0]=0.
\tag{16}
\]
For the donors use, under both laws, independent copies of
\[
Z_j=z_0+U_j,
\qquad
U_j\sim\operatorname{Unif}\{\pm\sqrt D\,e_k:1\le k\le D\},
\]
independently of the panel.  Symmetry gives
\[
\mathbb E[U_j\mid E_0]=0,
\qquad
\mathbb E[U_jU_j^\top\mid E_0]=I_D,
\qquad
\mathbb E[\lVert U_j\rVert_2^4\mid E_0]=D^2\le B.
\tag{17}
\]
Thus \(\Sigma=I_D\), including at \(B=D^2\), and the donor data carry no
information distinguishing the two laws.  Take
\(X_{st}^{\mathrm{obs}}=X_{st}\).  Set observed and historical potential
outcomes according to consistency, make historical potential outcomes
invariant to event treatment, and make every potential outcome invariant to
other units' assignments.  Choose all random wealth values in a compact
subinterval of \((0,B)\), so that the primitive buy-and-hold representation
can be realized with a positive common initial wealth and returns strictly
greater than \(-1\).  Equations (13)--(17) and these completions verify every
member property of
\(\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger}(\lambda)\), including support, exact
coordinates, limited anticipation, no spillovers, event transport, and both
full-design centering restrictions.

Let \(\mathcal A_T\) be the event that no calendar block is active.  Since the
number of blocks is \(T/L_T+O(1)\),
\[
P(\mathcal A_T\mid E_0)
=(1-p_T)^{T/L_T+O(1)}\longrightarrow e^{-c_p}>0.
\tag{18}
\]
On \(\mathcal A_T\), every selected-cohort design point lies outside \(A_T\),
where the surfaces in (15) agree.  All remaining observed kernels are common,
so the complete observed-data laws coincide on this event.  Therefore
\[
\int\min\{dP_{0,T},dP_{1,T}\}
\ge P(\mathcal A_T\mid E_0)\ge c>0.
\tag{19}
\]
The observed event mean is common, whereas event transport changes the
selected cohort's untreated event mean by \(c_bh_T^\lambda\).  The
identification equality in \texttt{lem:identification-fwl}, the fixed positive
weight \(\pi_{s_*}\), and (12) give
\[
\lvert\tau_{n,P_{1,T}}(z_0)-\tau_{n,P_{0,T}}(z_0)\rvert
\ge ch_T^\lambda
\asymp\left\{\frac{\log(T\vee2)}{T}\right\}^{\lambda/d}.
\tag{20}
\]
For the finitely many \(T\) preceding (11), use a fixed interior ball and two
sufficiently close bounded response kernels with positive affinity and fixed
nonzero target separation; decreasing the common constant makes (19)--(20)
valid for every positive \(T\).

Write the two target values as \(\tau_0,\tau_1\) and their separation as
\(\Delta=\lvert\tau_1-\tau_0\rvert\).  For any estimator
\(\widehat\tau\), the triangle inequality gives
\[
\mathbf 1\{\lvert\widehat\tau-\tau_0\rvert\ge\Delta/2\}
+\mathbf 1\{\lvert\widehat\tau-\tau_1\rvert\ge\Delta/2\}\ge1.
\]
Integrating this inequality against the overlap measure in (19) yields
\[
\max_{\ell\in\{0,1\}}
\mathbb E_{P_{\ell,T}}
[\{\widehat\tau-\tau_\ell\}^2\mid E_0]
\ge \frac{\Delta^2}{8}\int\min\{dP_{0,T},dP_{1,T}\}
\ge cR_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}.
\tag{21}
\]
This holds for every estimator, so it gives the stated conditional minimax
mean squared error lower bound.

Finally, decrease \(c_p\), if needed, so that the affinity in (19) is larger
than \(2\alpha+a_0\) for some \(a_0>0\).  If \(C\) is a uniformly
conditionally \((1-\alpha)\)-honest interval, then
\[
\int \mathbf 1\{\tau_0\in C,\ \tau_1\in C\}
\min\{dP_{0,T},dP_{1,T}\}
\ge \int\min\{dP_{0,T},dP_{1,T}\}-2\alpha
\ge a_0.
\]
On the integrand's event, \(\operatorname{length}(C)\ge\Delta\).  Hence its
worst-case conditional expected length is at least
\(a_0\Delta\ge c_\alpha R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}^{1/2}\).  The chosen event
path lies in \(\mathcal Z_+\), so its conditional query probability is one
and hence at least \(c_X\).  This proves the lemma for every compatible
envelope without an anti-clustering premise.
