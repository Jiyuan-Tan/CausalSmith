Put
\[
a:=\frac{2\lambda}{2\lambda+d}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
q:=\frac{2\lambda}{d}.
\]
The declared restrictions \(\lambda>0\) and \(d>0\) imply \(a\in(0,1)\) and \(q>0\).  We first establish the weighted arithmetic--geometric mean inequality used below.  For \(u>0\), define
\[
\phi(u):=au+(1-a)-u^a.
\]
Then \(\phi(1)=0\), while
\[
\phi'(u)=a\bigl(1-u^{a-1}\bigr).
\]
Because \(a-1<0\), this derivative is negative for \(0<u<1\), vanishes at \(u=1\), and is positive for \(u>1\).  Thus \(u=1\) is a global minimizer and \(\phi(u)\geq0\).  Substituting \(u=x/y\) and multiplying by \(y>0\) proves
\[
x^a y^{1-a}\leq ax+(1-a)y,
\qquad x>0,quad y>0.
\tag{1}
\]
The definitions of \(a\) and \(q\) give
\[
q(1-a)
=\frac{2\lambda}{d}\frac{d}{2\lambda+d}
=\frac{2\lambda}{2\lambda+d}
=a.
\tag{2}
\]
For every cohort \(s\), positivity of the integers \(T\) and \(n_s\) permits applying (1) with \(x=n_s^{-1}\) and \(y=T^{-q}\).  Using (2),
\[
\begin{aligned}
(Tn_s)^{-2\lambda/(2\lambda+d)}
&=n_s^{-a}T^{-a}\\
&=(n_s^{-1})^a(T^{-q})^{1-a}\\
&\leq a n_s^{-1}+(1-a)T^{-q}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
The declared cohort weights satisfy \(\pi_s>0\) and \(\sum_{s=1}^S\pi_s=1\).  Hence
\[
\sum_{s=1}^S\pi_s^2
\leq\left(\sum_{s=1}^S\pi_s\right)^2
=1.
\tag{4}
\]
Multiplying (3) by \(\pi_s^2\), summing over \(s\), applying (4), and invoking the definitions of \(R_{\mathrm{sm}}\) and \(R_{\mathrm{tr}}\) in \(\texttt{def:rate-frontier}\), we obtain
\[
R_{\mathrm{sm}}
\leq aR_{\mathrm{tr}}+(1-a)T^{-q}.
\tag{5}
\]

For every positive integer \(T\), one has \(\log(T\vee2)\geq\log2>0\).  Therefore, because \(q>0\), the definition of \(R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}\) in \(\texttt{def:rate-frontier}\) yields
\[
T^{-q}
\leq(\log2)^{-q}
\left\{\frac{\log(T\vee2)}{T}\right\}^{q}
=(\log2)^{-q}R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}.
\tag{6}
\]
Combining (5) and (6), and using \(cx+ey\leq(c+e)(x\vee y)\) for nonnegative \(c,e,x,y\), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
R_{\mathrm{sm}}
&\leq aR_{\mathrm{tr}}+(1-a)(\log2)^{-q}R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}\\
&\leq\bigl\{a+(1-a)(\log2)^{-q}\bigr\}
\bigl(R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}\bigr)\\
&=C_{\lambda,d}
\bigl(R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}\bigr),
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
where the last equality follows from \(1-a=d/(2\lambda+d)\) and \(q=2\lambda/d\).  Since \(0<\log2<1\) and \(q>0\),
\[
C_{\lambda,d}=a+(1-a)(\log2)^{-q}
\geq a+(1-a)=1.
\tag{8}
\]
Define \(A:=R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}\) and \(G:=A\vee R_{\mathrm{don}}\).  Every rate component is positive by \(\texttt{def:rate-frontier}\).  Its definition of \(R_{\star,\beta}\), together with (7)--(8), gives
\[
G
\leq R_{\star,\beta}
=G\vee R_{\mathrm{sm}}
\leq G\vee(C_{\lambda,d}A)
\leq C_{\lambda,d}G.
\tag{9}
\]
Consequently,
\[
R_{\star,\beta}
\asymp_{\lambda,d}
R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}\vee R_{\mathrm{don}},
\]
with comparison constants \(1\) and \(C_{\lambda,d}\), uniformly in \(N,T,S\) and all positive replication counts.

Suppose next that \(T\geq3\).  Then \(T\vee2=T\) and \(\log T\geq\log3>1\), so
\[
T^{-q}
\leq\left(\frac{\log T}{T}\right)^q
=R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}.
\tag{10}
\]
Substitution of (10) into (5), followed by \(a\in(0,1)\), proves
\[
R_{\mathrm{sm}}
\leq aR_{\mathrm{tr}}+(1-a)R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}
\leq R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}.
\tag{11}
\]
Taking the maximum with the remaining components in \(\texttt{def:rate-frontier}\) therefore gives
\[
R_{\star,\beta}
=R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}\vee R_{\mathrm{don}}.
\tag{12}
\]

By \(\texttt{def:rate-frontier}\), \(R_{\mathrm{don}}=N^{-\kappa}\).  Along any joint growth array, this channel is negligible relative to \(A\) precisely when
\[
N^{-\kappa}=o(A),
\]
and it dominates \(A\) precisely when
\[
A=o(N^{-\kappa}).
\]
Because \(\kappa>0\) and \(A>0\), the boundary relation \(N^{-\kappa}\asymp A\) is equivalent, after applying the decreasing map \(x\mapsto x^{-1/\kappa}\) and absorbing fixed comparison constants, to
\[
N\asymp A^{-1/\kappa}.
\]
These comparisons have the asserted oracle/generated-center interpretation whenever a separate statistical result establishes \(R_{\star,\beta}\) as the sharp risk of the experiment; equations (1)--(12) do not assert that statistical premise.

For the independent-calendar class, or under the separately imposed fixed-query local-small-ball or anti-clustering condition specified in \(\texttt{def:rate-frontier}\), the delivered spacing component is
\[
R_{\mathrm{sp}}=T^{-q}.
\]
Equation (5) consequently implies
\[
R_{\mathrm{sm}}
\leq aR_{\mathrm{tr}}+(1-a)R_{\mathrm{sp}}
\leq R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp}}.
\tag{13}
\]
The corresponding definition of \(R_\star\) in \(\texttt{def:rate-frontier}\) and (13) then give the exact identity
\[
R_\star
=R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sm}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp}}\vee R_{\mathrm{don}}
=R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp}}\vee R_{\mathrm{don}}.
\]

Finally, \(\texttt{ass:exact-training-coordinates}\) states \(X_{st}^{\mathrm{obs}}=X_{st}\).  Thus the historical regression coordinate is observed exactly, so there is no measurement-error convolution to invert.  Consistently, the exhaustive maxima defining \(R_{\star,\beta}\) and \(R_\star\) in \(\texttt{def:rate-frontier}\) contain neither a deconvolution component nor a separate moving-Gram component.  The same definition uses \(\kappa=\min\{\lambda,1\}\).  If \(\lambda>1\), then \(\kappa=1\), and hence
\[
R_{\mathrm{don}}=N^{-\kappa}=N^{-1}.
\]
This proves every asserted algebraic conclusion and preserves the theorem's stated distinction between those conclusions and statistical sharpness.
