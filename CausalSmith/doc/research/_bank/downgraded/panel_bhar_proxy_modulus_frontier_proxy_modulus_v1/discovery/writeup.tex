\section*{panel\_\allowbreak{}bhar\_\allowbreak{}proxy\_\allowbreak{}modulus\_\allowbreak{}frontier}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} proxy\_\allowbreak{}modulus\_\allowbreak{}v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} The target is the cohort-weighted finite-cohort causal buy-and-hold level-wealth effect at a realized event path.  Consistency and cohort-average event transport identify it through historical conditional-mean surfaces.  The original class nevertheless permits one design-shared event shock to survive arbitrarily many unit replicates: an explicit two-completion witness gives fixed positive minimax-risk and uniformly honest expected-length lower bounds while \(R_{\star,\beta}\to0\).  The witness satisfies historical full-design centering and violates event full-design mean stability, so at least the event-side restriction is rate-essential; it does not prove separate necessity of the historical restriction.  On the full-design-centered class, nonemptiness requires \(B\ge D^2\).  Under design-only geometric beta mixing, matching strict-slack bounds give conditional mean-squared-error rate \(R_{\star,\beta}\) and honest expected-length rate \(R_{\star,\beta}^{1/2}\), including the unavoidable log-spacing term.  On the named conditionally independent-calendar subclass, the iid-tuned estimator and interval replace that term by \(R_{\mathrm{sp}}=T^{-2\lambda/d}\), giving the sharp no-log rates \(R_\star\) and \(R_\star^{1/2}\).  Exact finite-sample conditional coverage holds in both restricted regimes.  At \(B=D^2\), the same upper bounds remain valid but no universal donor converse is asserted.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Causal financial event-study theory mainly treats linear return contrasts; repeated-measurement regression fixes the query; generated-regressor theory typically moves training coordinates; and synthetic-control inference does not deliver a generated-event-proxy minimax frontier for compounded wealth across both weakly dependent and independent calendar designs.  More basically, conditional cross-unit independence alone does not ensure that unit replication averages an event outcome component measurable with respect to the shared historical design.

\paragraph{Niche.} The panel architecture observes historical proxy paths exactly, replicates untreated wealth blocks at each calendar window, and estimates only the event-date query from an independent donor sample.  It separates unit replication, calendar path coverage, generated-center uncertainty, and shared-design event shocks.  The resulting maximality witness shows why event full-design mean stability is rate-essential, while the restricted positive theory exposes a dependence elbow: design-only geometric beta mixing costs a logarithm in uniform spacing, whereas conditional calendar independence removes it.

\paragraph{Fill.} The project proves event-transport identification, a universal impossibility over the original class, exact weighted-panel algebra on the full-design-centered class, feasible full-donor local-polynomial estimators, and exact finite-sample conditionally honest intervals.  The impossibility uses two admissible causal completions with fixed observed-data overlap and target separation; it proves constant minimax-risk and honest-length lower bounds even when the candidate frontier vanishes.  The maintained beta-mixing restricted class has frontier \(R_{\star,\beta}\); the named independent-calendar full-design-centered subclass has the sharp no-log frontier \(R_\star\) for every \(\lambda>0\).  For fixed \(B>D^2\), all four lower channels match in both restricted regimes.  At \(B=D^2\), the upper bounds persist and the universal converse contains treated, smoothing, and the dependence-appropriate spacing channel only.  No donor splitting, covariance estimator, deconvolution, dense-site assumption, upper local-mass condition, or sequential overlap is used.

\section{Related work}

Goldsmith-Pinkham and Lyu (2025) motivate the causal financial-panel setup and explain why compounded buy-and-hold wealth requires more than linear return matching. Barber and Lyon (1997) and Mitchell and Stafford (2000) likewise motivate attention to dependence and compounding in long-horizon returns. Matching only marginal or mean return paths generally discards within-path dependence needed for compounded wealth; this is motivation, not a theorem or an observational-equivalence claim under the maintained full wealth panel. Yan, Yao, and Zhou (2023, revised 2025) provide repeated-measurement Holder rates, while Gaiffas (2007) provides a pointwise random-design local-mass template. Mammen, Rothe, and Schienle (2012), Delaigle, Hall, and Meister (2008), and Akhavan, Gogolashvili, and Tsybakov (2024) study different generated-coordinate or measurement-error experiments. Cattaneo, Feng, and Titiunik (2021) provide the closest beta-mixing block-coupling template, and Armstrong and Kolesar (2018) provide ordered-modulus language for honest expected length. The banked predecessor proves a no-log proxy-modulus result only in its fixed-query conditional experiment. The new result lifts that phenomenon to the present complete causal panel class, supplies a feasible estimator and honest interval, and proves the independent-calendar frontier for every \(\lambda>0\), alongside the distinct log-spacing frontier under design-only geometric beta mixing.

  \item \texttt{A\_\allowbreak{}don} --- \texttt{donor-\allowbreak{}modulus allowance}; \([0,\infty)\); \texttt{interval donor radius}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(A_{\mathrm{don}}=C_{\mathrm{mod}}\delta_N^\kappa\).
  \item \texttt{M\_\allowbreak{}dagger} --- \texttt{restricted joint panel-\allowbreak{}law class}; \(2^{\mathcal P}\); \texttt{restricted parameter class}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger}(\lambda)\), the base class subject to exactly the event full-design mean-stability and historical full-design centering restrictions.
  \item \texttt{R\_\allowbreak{}sp\_\allowbreak{}beta} --- \texttt{geometric-\allowbreak{}mixing path-\allowbreak{}spacing risk term}; \((0,\infty)\); \texttt{uniform spacing component}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}=\{\log(T\vee2)/T\}^{2\lambda/d}\).
  \item \texttt{R\_\allowbreak{}q} --- \texttt{geometric-\allowbreak{}mixing channelwise rate}; \((0,\infty)\); \texttt{generic uniform rate component}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(R_q\) equals respectively \(R_{\mathrm{tr}},R_{\mathrm{sm}},R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta},R_{\mathrm{don}}\) for \(q=\mathrm{tr},\mathrm{sm},\mathrm{sp},\mathrm{don}\).
  \item \texttt{R\_\allowbreak{}star\_\allowbreak{}beta} --- \texttt{geometric-\allowbreak{}mixing joint minimax rate}; \((0,\infty)\); \texttt{uniform focal frontier}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(R_{\star,\beta}=R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sm}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}\vee R_{\mathrm{don}}\).
  \item \texttt{C\_\allowbreak{}h} --- \texttt{fixed positive procedural constant}; \((0,\infty)\); \texttt{bandwidth calibration constant}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \texttt{A sufficiently large initial-\allowbreak{}bandwidth multiplier depending only on fixed mixing,\allowbreak{} design,\allowbreak{} kernel,\allowbreak{} dimension,\allowbreak{} and smoothness constants.}
  \item \texttt{h\_\allowbreak{}s\_\allowbreak{}beta} --- \texttt{log-\allowbreak{}adjusted initial bandwidth}; \((0,1]\); \texttt{uniform geometric-\allowbreak{}mixing smoothing scale}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(h_{s,\beta}=1\wedge C_h[(Tn_s)^{-1/(2\lambda+d)}\vee\{\log(T\vee2)/T\}^{1/d}\vee N^{-1/2}]\).
  \item \texttt{h\_\allowbreak{}s} --- \texttt{selected cohort bandwidth}; \((0,1]\); \texttt{selected smoothing scale}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} First successful bandwidth on the finite dyadic grid beginning at \(h_{s,\beta}\).
  \item \texttt{u\_\allowbreak{}st} --- \texttt{standardized local coordinate}; \(\mathbb R^d\); \texttt{local-\allowbreak{}polynomial coordinate}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(u_{st}=(X_{st}-\widetilde z_s)/h_s\).
  \item \texttt{w\_\allowbreak{}st} --- \texttt{kernel weight}; \([0,\infty)\); \texttt{weighted least-\allowbreak{}squares weight}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(w_{st}=K(u_{st})\).
  \item \texttt{L\_\allowbreak{}s} --- \texttt{selected full-\allowbreak{}data local mass}; \([0,\infty)\); \texttt{normalized-\allowbreak{}shape denominator}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(L_s=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}w_{st}\).
  \item \texttt{G\_\allowbreak{}s} --- \texttt{selected full-\allowbreak{}data polynomial Gram matrix}; \(\mathbb S_+^{q_r}\); \texttt{selected-\allowbreak{}fit Gram matrix}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(G_s=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}w_{st}b_r(u_{st})b_r(u_{st})^\top\).
  \item \texttt{a\_\allowbreak{}sti} --- \texttt{full-\allowbreak{}data local-\allowbreak{}polynomial linear weight}; \(\mathbb R\); \texttt{observable smoothing weight}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(a_{sti}=e_0^\top G_s^{-1}w_{st}b_r(u_{st})\).
  \item \texttt{hat\_\allowbreak{}m\_\allowbreak{}s} --- \texttt{raw local-\allowbreak{}polynomial value}; \(\mathbb R\); \texttt{counterfactual estimator}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Selected full-data local-polynomial intercept for cohort \(s\), equivalently \(\sum_{t,i}a_{sti}Q_{sti}\) on success.
  \item \texttt{hat\_\allowbreak{}tau} --- \texttt{raw causal wealth estimator}; \(\mathbb R\); \texttt{estimator}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \texttt{Cohort-\allowbreak{}weighted event average minus the single-\allowbreak{}center fitted untreated mean.}
  \item \texttt{A\_\allowbreak{}L} --- \texttt{observable full-\allowbreak{}data smoothing-\allowbreak{}bias allowance}; \([0,\infty)\); \texttt{interval bias radius}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(A_L=C_{r,d}L\sum_s\pi_s\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_i|a_{sti}|\,\|X_{st}-\widetilde z_s\|_2^\lambda\).
  \item \texttt{c\_\allowbreak{}G} --- \texttt{fixed positive procedural constant}; \((0,\infty)\); \texttt{selector stability threshold}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \texttt{Normalized local-\allowbreak{}polynomial design-\allowbreak{}shape threshold chosen from the fixed kernel,\allowbreak{} basis,\allowbreak{} density bounds,\allowbreak{} and cube geometry.}
  \item \texttt{alpha\_\allowbreak{}hist} --- \texttt{historical-\allowbreak{}component error allocation}; \((0,1/6)\); \texttt{inference constant}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\alpha_{\mathrm{hist}}=\alpha/3\).
  \item \texttt{R\_\allowbreak{}hist} --- \texttt{historical Hoeffding radius}; \([0,\infty)\); \texttt{historical stochastic radius}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(R_{\mathrm{hist}}=B\{\tfrac12\log(2/\alpha_{\mathrm{hist}})\sum_{s,i}[\pi_s\sum_t|a_{sti}|]^2\}^{1/2}\).
  \item \texttt{alpha\_\allowbreak{}evt} --- \texttt{event-\allowbreak{}component error allocation}; \((0,1/6)\); \texttt{inference constant}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\alpha_{\mathrm{evt}}=\alpha/3\).
  \item \texttt{R\_\allowbreak{}evt} --- \texttt{event Hoeffding radius}; \([0,\infty)\); \texttt{event stochastic radius}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(R_{\mathrm{evt}}=B\{\tfrac12\log(2/\alpha_{\mathrm{evt}})\sum_s\pi_s^2/n_s\}^{1/2}\).
  \item \texttt{H\_\allowbreak{}alpha} --- \texttt{total honest-\allowbreak{}interval radius}; \([0,\infty)\); \texttt{confidence radius}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(H_\alpha=R_{\mathrm{hist}}+R_{\mathrm{evt}}+A_L+A_{\mathrm{don}}\).
  \item \texttt{C\_\allowbreak{}alpha} --- \texttt{confidence interval}; \(2^{[-B,B]}\); \texttt{inference object}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(C_\alpha=[-B,B]\cap[\widehat\tau-H_\alpha,\widehat\tau+H_\alpha]\) on the declared good sample and \([-B,B]\) otherwise.
  \item \texttt{M\_\allowbreak{}dagger\_\allowbreak{}iid} --- \texttt{independent-\allowbreak{}calendar restricted joint panel-\allowbreak{}law class}; \(2^{\mathcal P}\); \texttt{independent-\allowbreak{}calendar parameter class}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger,\mathrm{iid}}(\lambda)\), the full-design-centered class whose stacked historical design vectors are conditionally independent over calendar indices given \(E_0\).
  \item \texttt{h\_\allowbreak{}s\_\allowbreak{}iid} --- \texttt{independent-\allowbreak{}calendar initial bandwidth}; \((0,1]\); \texttt{iid bandwidth calibration}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(h_{s,\mathrm{iid}}=1\wedge C_h[(Tn_s)^{-1/(2\lambda+d)}\vee T^{-1/d}\vee N^{-1/2}]\).
  \item \texttt{hsel\_\allowbreak{}s\_\allowbreak{}iid} --- \texttt{selected independent-\allowbreak{}calendar cohort bandwidth}; \((0,1]\); \texttt{iid selected smoothing scale}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} First successful bandwidth on the finite dyadic grid beginning at \(h_{s,\mathrm{iid}}\).
  \item \texttt{hat\_\allowbreak{}tau\_\allowbreak{}iid} --- \texttt{iid-\allowbreak{}tuned causal wealth estimator}; \(\mathbb R\); \texttt{independent-\allowbreak{}calendar estimator}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{iid}}\), the full-donor local-polynomial estimator using the grid beginning at \(h_{s,\mathrm{iid}}\).
  \item \texttt{C\_\allowbreak{}alpha\_\allowbreak{}iid} --- \texttt{iid-\allowbreak{}tuned confidence interval}; \(2^{[-B,B]}\); \texttt{independent-\allowbreak{}calendar inference object}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(C_{\alpha,\mathrm{iid}}\), the Hoeffding--Taylor--donor interval computed from the iid-tuned local-polynomial fit.
\end{itemize}

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:consistency]\label{ass:consistency}
\(B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}=B_{si}(1)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Potential-outcome consistency, \cite{GoldsmithPinkhamLyu2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:historical-consistency]\label{ass:historical-consistency}
\(Q_{sti}=Q_{sti}(0)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Historical potential-outcome consistency, \cite{GoldsmithPinkhamLyu2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:no-spillovers]\label{ass:no-spillovers}
\(B_{si}(a,\mathbf a_{-si})=B_{si}(a,\mathbf a'_{-si})\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (No cross-unit spillovers, \cite{GoldsmithPinkhamLyu2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:limited-anticipation]\label{ass:limited-anticipation}
\(Q_{sti}(a)=Q_{sti}(0)\quad(t\ge1)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Limited anticipation in event studies, \cite{GoldsmithPinkhamLyu2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:historical-regression]\label{ass:historical-regression}
\(\mathbb E_P[Q_{sti}\mid X_{st}=x,E_0]=m_s(x)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Nonparametric conditional-mean regression, \cite{Gaiffas2007}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:event-transport]\label{ass:event-transport}
\(n_s^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}\mathbb E_P[B_{si}(0)\mid z_0,E_0]=m_s(z_{0s})\quad(s=1,\ldots,S)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This cohort-average cross-date path-law stability condition is plausible for short event windows whose untreated wealth mechanism is stable after conditioning on the realized market path; rolling pseudo-events make its pre-event analogue diagnostically accessible without requiring event-date exchangeability.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:bounded-historical-wealth]\label{ass:bounded-historical-wealth}
\(0\le Q_{sti}\le B\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Finite-horizon wealth is naturally bounded after the same pre-specified return filters or winsorization rules used to protect long-horizon event studies from single-security price errors.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:bounded-event-wealth]\label{ass:bounded-event-wealth}
\(0\le B_{si}(a)\le B\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Finite-horizon wealth is naturally bounded after the same pre-specified return filters or winsorization rules used to protect long-horizon event studies from single-security price errors.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:holder-smoothness]\label{ass:holder-smoothness}
\(m_s\in\mathcal H_d(\lambda,L)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Isotropic Holder smoothness, \cite{Gaiffas2007}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:design-density]\label{ass:design-density}
\(c_X\le f_{s,E_0}(x)\le C_X\quad(x\in[0,1]^d)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Interior random-design density bounds, \cite{Gaiffas2007}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:interior-event-path]\label{ass:interior-event-path}
\(\min_{s\le S}\operatorname{dist}(z_{0s},\partial[0,1]^d)\ge\delta_0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Interior point overlap, \cite{Gaiffas2007}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:cross-unit-independence]\label{ass:cross-unit-independence}
\(((Q_{sti})_{t=1}^{T},B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}})\perp((Q_{s'ti'})_{t=1}^{T},B_{s'i'}^{\mathrm{obs}})\mid(X_{uv})_{u,v},E_0\quad\text{for }(s,i)\ne(s',i')\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Conditional cross-unit independence, \cite{YanYao2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:temporal-mixing]\label{ass:temporal-mixing}
\[
\beta_P(\ell_{\mathrm{mix}}\mid E_0)
\le \beta_0\rho_0^{\ell_{\mathrm{mix}}}
\quad(\ell_{\mathrm{mix}}\ge1),
\]
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Geometric conditional beta mixing of the design, \cite{CattaneoFengTitiunik2021}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:donor-independence]\label{ass:donor-independence}
\((Z_j)_{j=1}^{N}\perp((X_{st},Q_{sti},B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}})_{s,t,i})\mid E_0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Separate donor universes or pre-registered sample splitting produce this conditional independence while retaining the same realized market event state.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:donor-replication]\label{ass:donor-replication}
\((Z_j)_{j=1}^{N}\mid E_0\ \overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\ \mathcal L_P(Z_1\mid E_0)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Independent donor replication, \cite{DelaigleHallMeister2008}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:donor-ellipticity]\label{ass:donor-ellipticity}
\(c_\Sigma I_{Sd}\preceq\Sigma\preceq C_\Sigma I_{Sd}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform covariance eigenvalue bounds, \cite{CattaneoFengTitiunik2021}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:donor-fourth-moment]\label{ass:donor-fourth-moment}
\(\mathbb E_P[\|\Sigma^{-1/2}(Z_j-z_0)\|_2^4\mid E_0]\le B\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform standardized fourth-moment envelope; class compatibility is derived separately, \cite{Ferman2021}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:exact-training-coordinates]\label{ass:exact-training-coordinates}
\(X_{st}^{\mathrm{obs}}=X_{st}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Administrative panels record the historical proxy coordinates used to index pseudo-event windows, so classical measurement error is absent from the training design.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:local-two-sided-modulus]\label{ass:local-two-sided-modulus}
\(c_{\mathrm{mod}}\delta^\kappa\le\omega_P(\delta;z_0)\le C_{\mathrm{mod}}\delta^\kappa\quad(0<\delta\le\delta_0)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} The condition holds for regular surfaces with a nonzero covariance-standardized gradient when \(\lambda>1\), and for asymmetric Holder cusps with a coefficient bounded away from zero when \(0<\lambda\le1\); both describe economically meaningful path sensitivity rather than an oracle estimator property.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:positive-query-region]\label{ass:positive-query-region}
\(P\{z_0\in\mathcal Z_+\}\ge c_X\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} A positive-probability interior event-state region is natural when market states have a density and prevents a conditional lower bound from resting on a null query.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:event-full-design-mean-stability]\label{ass:event-full-design-mean-stability}
\[
\mathbb E_P\!\left[
B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}\,\middle|\,
(X_{s't'})_{1\le s'\le S,\,1\le t'\le T},E_0
\right]
=\mathbb E_P[B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid E_0]
\quad\text{for every }s,i.
\]
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This is an explicit strong full-design strict-exogeneity restriction preventing design-measurable event components from surviving cross-unit averaging; conditional cross-unit independence does not imply it.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:historical-full-design-centering]\label{ass:historical-full-design-centering}
\[
\mathbb E_P\!\left[
\varepsilon_{sti}\,\middle|\,
(X_{s't'})_{1\le s'\le S,\,1\le t'\le T},E_0
\right]=0
\quad\text{for every }s,t,i.
\]
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This is an explicit strong full-design centering restriction ensuring that replicated unit histories average after conditioning on the complete design; contemporaneous regression centering does not imply it.
\end{assumption}

\section{Definitions}

\begin{definition}[def:panel-law-class]\label{def:panel-law-class}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \(\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(\lambda)\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \[
\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(\lambda)
=\left\{P_{N,T,(n_s)_{s=1}^{S}}:
P_{N,T,(n_s)_{s=1}^{S}}\text{ satisfies every member property listed below}\right\}.
\]
The member properties are \texttt{ass:consistency}, \texttt{ass:historical-consistency}, \texttt{ass:no-spillovers}, \texttt{ass:limited-anticipation}, \texttt{ass:historical-regression}, \texttt{ass:event-transport}, \texttt{ass:bounded-historical-wealth}, \texttt{ass:bounded-event-wealth}, \texttt{ass:holder-smoothness}, \texttt{ass:design-density}, \texttt{ass:interior-event-path}, \texttt{ass:cross-unit-independence}, \texttt{ass:temporal-mixing}, \texttt{ass:donor-independence}, \texttt{ass:donor-replication}, \texttt{ass:donor-ellipticity}, \texttt{ass:donor-fourth-moment}, \texttt{ass:exact-training-coordinates}, \texttt{ass:local-two-sided-modulus}, and \texttt{ass:positive-query-region}.  With \(D=Sd\), these donor conditions imply the necessary class-constant compatibility relation \(B\ge D^2\).  Thus the displayed class is empty when \(B<D^2\); among otherwise nonempty configurations, \(B=D^2\) is called donor-envelope boundary-compatible and \(B>D^2\) is called donor-envelope interior-compatible.  These labels record a consequence of the listed member properties and add no law-level condition.

In addition, define the independent-calendar, full-design-centered subclass by
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger,\mathrm{iid}}(\lambda)
=\bigl\{P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(\lambda):{}&
\mathbb E_P[B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid (X_{s't'})_{s',t'},E_0]
=\mathbb E_P[B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid E_0],\\
&\mathbb E_P[\varepsilon_{sti}\mid (X_{s't'})_{s',t'},E_0]=0,
\text{ for every }s,t,i,\\
&((X_{st})_{s=1}^{S})_{t=1}^{T}
\text{ are mutually conditionally independent given }E_0\bigr\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the displayed class retains exactly the base member properties and the two full-design centering restrictions, and adds only conditional calendar independence.  Calendar independence is a named subclass restriction and is not imposed on the maintained geometrically beta-mixing class.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Member properties.} \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}consistency}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}historical-\allowbreak{}consistency}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}no-\allowbreak{}spillovers}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}limited-\allowbreak{}anticipation}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}historical-\allowbreak{}regression}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}event-\allowbreak{}transport}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}bounded-\allowbreak{}historical-\allowbreak{}wealth}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}bounded-\allowbreak{}event-\allowbreak{}wealth}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}holder-\allowbreak{}smoothness}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}design-\allowbreak{}density}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}interior-\allowbreak{}event-\allowbreak{}path}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}cross-\allowbreak{}unit-\allowbreak{}independence}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}temporal-\allowbreak{}mixing}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}donor-\allowbreak{}independence}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}donor-\allowbreak{}replication}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}donor-\allowbreak{}ellipticity}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}donor-\allowbreak{}fourth-\allowbreak{}moment}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}exact-\allowbreak{}training-\allowbreak{}coordinates}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}local-\allowbreak{}two-\allowbreak{}sided-\allowbreak{}modulus}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}positive-\allowbreak{}query-\allowbreak{}region}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:buyhold-level]\label{def:buyhold-level}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Buy-\allowbreak{}and-\allowbreak{}hold terminal wealth}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(B_{si}(a)=W_{si,-1}\prod_{h=0}^{H}\{1+R_{sih}(a)\}\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:target]\label{def:target}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Conditional finite-\allowbreak{}cohort causal buy-\allowbreak{}and-\allowbreak{}hold effect}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\tau_n(z_0)=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s n_s^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}\mathbb E_P[B_{si}(1)-B_{si}(0)\mid z_0,E_0]\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:local-modulus]\label{def:local-modulus}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Full-\allowbreak{}covariance local modulus}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\omega_P(\delta;z_0)=\sup\{|\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s[m_s(z_{0s}+u_s)-m_s(z_{0s})]|:\|\Sigma^{-1/2}u\|_2\le\delta,\ z_0+u\in([0,1]^d)^S\}\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:rate-frontier]\label{def:rate-frontier}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Dependence-\allowbreak{}specific four-\allowbreak{}term frontiers}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For the maintained geometrically beta-mixing class, define
\[
R_{\mathrm{tr}}=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\frac{\pi_s^2}{n_s},
\qquad
R_{\mathrm{sm}}=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s^2(Tn_s)^{-2\lambda/(2\lambda+d)},
\]
\[
R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}=\left\{\frac{\log(T\vee2)}T\right\}^{2\lambda/d},
\qquad
R_{\mathrm{don}}=N^{-\kappa},
\qquad
R_{\star,\beta}=R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sm}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}\vee R_{\mathrm{don}}.
\]
For \(q\in\{\mathrm{tr},\mathrm{sm},\mathrm{sp},\mathrm{don}\}\), \(R_q\) denotes respectively \(R_{\mathrm{tr}},R_{\mathrm{sm}},R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta},R_{\mathrm{don}}\) when the maintained beta-mixing class is under discussion.  For the independent-calendar subclass \(\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger,\mathrm{iid}}(\lambda)\), define
\[
R_{\mathrm{sp}}=T^{-2\lambda/d},
\qquad
R_\star=R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sm}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp}}\vee R_{\mathrm{don}}.
\]
The same no-log pair is also a comparator under a separately imposed fixed-query local-small-ball or anti-clustering condition that delivers \(R_{\mathrm{sp}}=T^{-2\lambda/d}\), but here it is a proved rate pair for the named independent-calendar joint panel class.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:feasible-estimator]\label{def:feasible-estimator}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Dependence-\allowbreak{}tuned generated-\allowbreak{}center replicated local-\allowbreak{}polynomial estimators}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Let
\[
\bar Z=N^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{N}Z_j,
\qquad
\widetilde z=\Pi_{([0,1]^d)^S}(\bar Z),
\]
and write \(\widetilde z_s\) for cohort \(s\)'s block.  For a dyadic candidate \(h\) from
\[
h_{s,\beta}
=1\wedge C_h\left[
(Tn_s)^{-1/(2\lambda+d)}
\vee\left\{\frac{\log(T\vee2)}T\right\}^{1/d}
\vee N^{-1/2}
\right]
\]
through \(1\), where \(C_h\) is a sufficiently large fixed constant depending only on \(\beta_0,\rho_0,d,\lambda\) and the fixed design and kernel constants, set
\[
u_{st}(h)=\frac{X_{st}-\widetilde z_s}{h},
\qquad
w_{st}(h)=K(u_{st}(h)),
\]
\[
L_s(h)=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}w_{st}(h),
\qquad
G_s(h)=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}
w_{st}(h)b_r(u_{st}(h))b_r(u_{st}(h))^\top.
\]
Select the first candidate satisfying
\[
L_s(h)>0,
\qquad
\lambda_{\min}\{G_s(h)/L_s(h)\}\ge c_G.
\]
Failure means that no grid value through \(1\) passes; no upper local-mass condition is imposed.  On success set \(h_s=h\), \(u_{st}=u_{st}(h)\), \(w_{st}=w_{st}(h)\), and let \(\widehat\gamma_s\) minimize
\[
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}
w_{st}\{Q_{sti}-b_r(u_{st})^\top\gamma\}^2.
\]
Define
\[
\widehat m_s=e_0^\top\widehat\gamma_s,
\qquad
\widehat\tau=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s
\{\bar B_s^{\mathrm{obs}}-\widehat m_s\}.
\]
On a failed cohort fit set its intercept contribution to zero.  This beta-mixing estimator uses all donors once and does not estimate \(\Sigma\).

For \(P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger,\mathrm{iid}}(\lambda)\), define the independent-calendar initial scale
\[
h_{s,\mathrm{iid}}
=1\wedge C_h\left[
(Tn_s)^{-1/(2\lambda+d)}\vee T^{-1/d}\vee N^{-1/2}
\right].
\]
Run exactly the preceding dyadic normalized-shape selector and local-polynomial fit with \(h_{s,\mathrm{iid}}\) in place of \(h_{s,\beta}\), keeping the full donor mean, cube projection, kernel, basis, response data, and failed-fit rule unchanged.  Denote its selected bandwidth by \(h_s^{\mathrm{iid}}\) and its final scalar estimator by \(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{iid}}\).  This is the iid-tuned estimator; it too uses all donors once and never estimates \(\Sigma\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}panel-\allowbreak{}law-\allowbreak{}class}; \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}rate-\allowbreak{}frontier}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:fwl-regression]\label{def:fwl-regression}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Algebraic cohortwise weighted least-\allowbreak{}squares panel regression}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For each cohort \(s\), set \(u_{st}=(X_{st}-\widetilde z_s)/h_s\), \(w_{st}=K(u_{st})\), and \(e_0=b_r(0)\).  On a successful selected fit define \((\widehat\theta_s,\widehat\gamma_s)\) as a minimizer of
\[
\frac1{n_s}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}
[B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}-\theta-e_0^\top\gamma]^2
+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}
w_{st}[Q_{sti}-b_r(u_{st})^\top\gamma]^2.
\]
Equivalently, use one block-diagonal stacked regression: event rows have \(D_{s0}=1\), polynomial row \(e_0\), and weight \(1/n_s\); historical rows have \(D_{st}=0\), polynomial row \(b_r(u_{st})\), and weight \(w_{st}\); outer cohort weights are \(\pi_s\).  Define
\[
\theta_{\mathrm{FWL}}=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s\widehat\theta_s.
\]
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}feasible-\allowbreak{}estimator}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:honest-interval]\label{def:honest-interval}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Dependence-\allowbreak{}tuned full-\allowbreak{}data Hoeffding-\allowbreak{}-\allowbreak{}Taylor-\allowbreak{}-\allowbreak{}donor intervals}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Set
\[
\alpha_{\mathrm{hist}}=\alpha_{\mathrm{evt}}
=\alpha_{\mathrm{don}}=\alpha/3.
\]
Use the full donor mean, projected center, log-adjusted bandwidth grid, normalized-shape selector, and raw local-polynomial fits in \texttt{def:feasible-estimator}.  On every successful fit define
\[
G_s=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}
w_{st}b_r(u_{st})b_r(u_{st})^\top,
\qquad
a_{sti}=e_0^\top G_s^{-1}w_{st}b_r(u_{st}),
\]
\[
\widehat m_s=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}a_{sti}Q_{sti}.
\]
Define
\[
A_L=C_{r,d}L\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}
|a_{sti}|\,\|X_{st}-\widetilde z_s\|_2^\lambda,
\]
\[
R_{\mathrm{hist}}
=B\left\{
\frac12\log\left(\frac2{\alpha_{\mathrm{hist}}}\right)
\sum_{s=1}^{S}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}
\left[\pi_s\sum_{t=1}^{T}|a_{sti}|\right]^2
\right\}^{1/2},
\]
\[
R_{\mathrm{evt}}
=B\left\{
\frac12\log\left(\frac2{\alpha_{\mathrm{evt}}}\right)
\sum_{s=1}^{S}\frac{\pi_s^2}{n_s}
\right\}^{1/2}.
\]
Put \(D=Sd\) and define
\[
M_4(N)=\frac BN+\left(1-\frac1N\right)(D^2+2D),
\]
\[
q_N=\left\{\frac{M_4(N)}{\alpha_{\mathrm{don}}}\right\}^{1/4},
\qquad
\delta_N=\sqrt{\frac{C_\Sigma}{c_\Sigma}}\frac{q_N}{\sqrt N},
\qquad
A_{\mathrm{don}}=C_{\mathrm{mod}}\delta_N^\kappa.
\]
On the good sample set
\[
H_\alpha=R_{\mathrm{hist}}+R_{\mathrm{evt}}+A_L+A_{\mathrm{don}},
\qquad
C_\alpha=[-B,B]\cap
[\widehat\tau-H_\alpha,\widehat\tau+H_\alpha].
\]
The bad-sample event is the union of failure of any selected Gram matrix under the normalized-shape rule, nonfiniteness of a required weight or radius, and \(\delta_N>\delta_0\).  On that event set \(C_\alpha=[-B,B]\).

For the independent-calendar subclass, define \(C_{\alpha,\mathrm{iid}}\) by the identical formulas and error allocation, but use \(h_{s,\mathrm{iid}}\), \(h_s^{\mathrm{iid}}\), and \(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{iid}}\) from \texttt{def:feasible-estimator}, and recompute the displayed weights, Taylor allowance, historical radius, and good event from that iid-tuned fit.  The full donor calibration and projection are unchanged.  If any iid-tuned cohort fit fails, a required quantity is nonfinite, or \(\delta_N>\delta_0\), set \(C_{\alpha,\mathrm{iid}}=[-B,B]\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}feasible-\allowbreak{}estimator}; \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}local-\allowbreak{}modulus}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:full-design-centered-class]\label{def:full-design-centered-class}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \(\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger}(\lambda)\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \[
\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger}(\lambda)
=\left\{P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(\lambda):
P\text{ satisfies \texttt{ass:event-full-design-mean-stability} and }

\]
These are exactly the two additional restrictions defining this subclass.  The shared-design replication witness in \(\texttt{prop:shared-design-replication-obstruction}\) belongs to the original class, satisfies historical full-design centering, and has a nonzero-effect completion that violates event full-design mean stability; over the original class its minimax risk and honest expected length remain bounded away from zero while the proposed rate tends to zero.  Thus the witness proves that the event-side restriction is rate-essential for the vanishing frontier.  It does not prove separate necessity of the historical restriction.

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:identification-fwl]\label{lem:identification-fwl}
For every \(P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(\lambda)\),
\[
\tau_n(z_0)=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s
\{\mathbb E_P[\bar B_s^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid z_0,E_0]-m_s(z_{0s})\}.
\]
On every realized sample for which all selected Gram matrices pass and the cohortwise minimizers exist, profiling the event coefficient gives
\[
\widehat\theta_s=\bar B_s^{\mathrm{obs}}-e_0^\top\widehat\gamma_s.
\]
The event term then has no \(\gamma\)-dependent component, \(\widehat\gamma_s\) is exactly the historical local-polynomial fit in \texttt{def:feasible-estimator}, and \(\theta_{\mathrm{FWL}}=\widehat\tau\) exactly.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(\mathscr E=\sigma(E_0)\).  By the definition of \(z_0\),
\(z_0=\mathbb E_P[Z_j\mid E_0]\) is \(\mathscr E\)-measurable.  Consequently,
adjoining \(z_0\) to the conditioning field generated by \(E_0\) does not
change any conditional expectation below.  By \texttt{def:target} and
linearity of conditional expectation,
\[
\tau_n(z_0)=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s\left\{
\frac1{n_s}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}\mathbb E_P[B_{si}(1)\mid z_0,E_0]
-\frac1{n_s}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}\mathbb E_P[B_{si}(0)\mid z_0,E_0]
\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Assumption \texttt{ass:consistency} and the definition of
\(\bar B_s^{\mathrm{obs}}\) give
\[
\frac1{n_s}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}\mathbb E_P[B_{si}(1)\mid z_0,E_0]
=\mathbb E_P[\bar B_s^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid z_0,E_0].
\tag{2}
\]
Assumption \texttt{ass:event-transport} gives, for each cohort,
\[
\frac1{n_s}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}\mathbb E_P[B_{si}(0)\mid z_0,E_0]
=m_s(z_{0s}).
\tag{3}
\]
Substitution of (2)--(3) into (1) proves
\[
\tau_n(z_0)=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s
\{\mathbb E_P[\bar B_s^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid z_0,E_0]-m_s(z_{0s})\}.
\]
Assumption \texttt{ass:historical-regression} separately identifies
\(m_s(x)\) as the observed-law conditional mean of an untreated
pseudo-event response at \(X_{st}=x\).  Thus consistency and event transport
prove the equality between the causal contrast and the observed-law
functional; that equality is not introduced as a definition of the causal
target.

Fix a successful cohort \(s\), abbreviate
\(\bar B_s^{\mathrm{obs}}\) by \(\bar B_s\), and hold
\(\gamma\in\mathbb R^{q_r}\) fixed.  The event part of the criterion in
\texttt{def:fwl-regression} satisfies the exact identity
\[
\frac1{n_s}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}
(B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}-\theta-e_0^\top\gamma)^2
=\frac1{n_s}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}(B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}-\bar B_s)^2
+(\bar B_s-\theta-e_0^\top\gamma)^2.
\tag{4}
\]
Consequently, for fixed \(\gamma\), the unique minimizing event coefficient is
\[
\theta=\bar B_s^{\mathrm{obs}}-e_0^\top\gamma.
\tag{5}
\]
At (5), the event criterion is independent of \(\gamma\).  Up to the first,
constant term in (4), the profiled criterion is therefore
\[
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}
w_{st}\{Q_{sti}-b_r(u_{st})^\top\gamma\}^2,
\tag{6}
\]
which is exactly the historical weighted least-squares problem in
\texttt{def:feasible-estimator}.  Passage of the normalized-shape test implies
\(L_s>0\) and
\[
G_s\succeq c_GL_sI_{q_r}\succ0.
\]
Hence (6) has the unique minimizer \(\widehat\gamma_s\), and profiling gives
\[
\widehat\theta_s
=\bar B_s^{\mathrm{obs}}-e_0^\top\widehat\gamma_s.
\tag{7}
\]
Using the definitions of \(\widehat m_s\), \(\theta_{\mathrm{FWL}}\), and
\(\widehat\tau\), on every sample for which all selected fits exist,
\[
\theta_{\mathrm{FWL}}
=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s
(\bar B_s^{\mathrm{obs}}-e_0^\top\widehat\gamma_s)
=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s(\bar B_s^{\mathrm{obs}}-\widehat m_s)
=\widehat\tau.
\]
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This result ties the statistical regression target to a causal level-wealth estimand and to the implementable full-donor panel coefficient. \textit{Gap.} The path-conditioned transport relation identifies compounded wealth rather than defining it through a generated benchmark. \textit{Consumer.} Applied event studies obtain a declared coefficient and causal estimand from one cohort fit centered at the full donor mean.

\begin{lemma}[lem:joint-law-embedding]\label{lem:joint-law-embedding}
Put \(D=Sd\).  The class \(\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger}(\lambda)\) is empty when \(B<D^2\).  For each fixed nonempty class with donor-envelope interior compatibility \(B>D^2\), and each \(q\in\{\mathrm{tr},\mathrm{sm},\mathrm{sp},\mathrm{don}\}\), there are pairs
\[
(P_{0,q},P_{1,q})\subset
\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger}(\lambda)
\]
such that \(P_{\ell,q}(z_0\in\mathcal Z_+\mid E_0)\ge c_X\), their conditional joint observed-data testing affinity is uniformly bounded away from zero,
\[
\inf_{N,T,\mathbf n}\int\min\{dP_{0,q},dP_{1,q}\}>0,
\]
and
\[
|\tau_{n,P_{1,q}}(z_0)-\tau_{n,P_{0,q}}(z_0)|^2
\ge cR_q
\quad\text{on }\mathcal Z_+,
\]
where \(R_q\) uses \(R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}\) in the spacing channel.  The constants in the donor pair may depend on the fixed slack \(B-D^2\).  The treated-mean, replicated-smoothing, and donor pairs may additionally be chosen with uniformly bounded Kullback--Leibler divergence; for the random-spacing pair only positive testing affinity is asserted.  For each fixed otherwise nonempty boundary class with \(B=D^2\), the same assertions hold for \(q\in\{\mathrm{tr},\mathrm{sm},\mathrm{sp}\}\), using one common donor marginal, but no universal donor-channel pair or \(R_{\mathrm{don}}\) converse is asserted.  Every asserted pair is induced by a regular conditional law of the complete causal panel; in particular, its event means are stable given the complete historical design and its historical errors are centered given that design.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(D=Sd\) and work with a regular conditional experiment given \(E_0\).  For any member of the class define, only in this proof,
\[
Y=\Sigma^{-1/2}(Z_1-z_0).
\]
The definitions of \(z_0\) and \(\Sigma\), together with \texttt{ass:donor-ellipticity}, give
\[
\mathbb E_P[Y\mid E_0]=0,
\qquad
\mathbb E_P[YY^\top\mid E_0]=I_D.
\]
Taking traces and applying conditional Jensen's inequality to \(\|Y\|_2^2\) therefore yields
\[
\mathbb E_P[\|Y\|_2^2\mid E_0]=D,
\qquad
D^2\leq \mathbb E_P[\|Y\|_2^4\mid E_0]\leq B,
\tag{1}
\]
where the last inequality is \texttt{ass:donor-fourth-moment}.  Thus \(B\geq D^2\) is necessary for nonemptiness, and the class is empty when \(B<D^2\).

We next record a causal completion used by every pair below.  Write
\[
\mathscr X=\sigma\{X_{st}:1\leq s\leq S,\ 1\leq t\leq T\}.
\]
Choose the query in the admissible part of \(\mathcal Z_+\), with the fixed interior margin required by \texttt{ass:interior-event-path}.  The response kernels below are supported in a fixed compact subinterval of \((0,B)\), and their binary mixing probabilities are kept in \([\eta,1-\eta]\) for a fixed \(\eta\in(0,1/2)\).  If \(p,p+v\in[\eta,1-\eta]\), two differentiations of binary relative entropy give
\[
\operatorname{kl}(p+v,p)\leq C_\eta v^2:
\tag{2}
\]
the first derivative in \(v\) vanishes at zero and the second derivative is uniformly bounded on that compact interval.

Draw event outcomes independently of \(\mathscr X\).  Conditional on \((\mathscr X,E_0)\), draw bounded historical response paths independently across \((s,i)\), using kernels, possibly heterogeneous in \((s,i)\), satisfying
\[
\mathbb E_P[Q_{sti}\mid\mathscr X,E_0]=m_s(X_{st}).
\]
It follows from the definition \(\varepsilon_{sti}=Q_{sti}-m_s(X_{st})\) that
\[
\mathbb E_P[B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid\mathscr X,E_0]
=\mathbb E_P[B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid E_0],
\qquad
\mathbb E_P[\varepsilon_{sti}\mid\mathscr X,E_0]=0.
\tag{3}
\]
Complete the unobserved event-date untreated outcomes so that their cohort-average conditional means are \(m_s(z_{0s})\).  Set observed and historical potential outcomes according to \texttt{ass:consistency} and \texttt{ass:historical-consistency}; make historical potential outcomes invariant to the event treatment and all potential outcomes invariant to other units' assignments.  Set \(X_{st}^{\mathrm{obs}}=X_{st}\).  These choices verify event transport, limited anticipation, no spillovers, exact training coordinates, and the two restrictions in \texttt{def:full-design-centered-class}.  Conditional independence across \((s,i)\) verifies \texttt{ass:cross-unit-independence}.  Every wealth value can be kept strictly between zero and \(B\), and then represented as in \texttt{def:buyhold-level} by choosing positive initial wealth and returns strictly above \(-1\).  In every channel the donor sample is conditionally independent of the panel and conditionally independent and identically distributed.

For all three non-donor channels and every \(B\geq D^2\), use the common donor marginal
\[
U\sim\operatorname{Unif}\{\pm\sqrt D\,e_k:1\leq k\leq D\},
\qquad Z_j=z_0+U_j,
\]
where the \(U_j\)'s are independent.  Symmetry gives
\[
\mathbb E[U\mid E_0]=0,
\qquad
\mathbb E[UU^\top\mid E_0]=I_D,
\qquad
\mathbb E[\|U\|_2^4\mid E_0]=D^2\leq B.
\tag{4}
\]
Since \(c_\Sigma\leq1\leq C_\Sigma\), this verifies the covariance and fourth-moment envelopes, including at \(B=D^2\).

For the treated channel let
\[
V_{\mathrm{tr}}=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\frac{\pi_s^2}{n_s}=R_{\mathrm{tr}},
\qquad
v_s=c_0\frac{\pi_s}{n_sV_{\mathrm{tr}}^{1/2}}.
\]
Because \(V_{\mathrm{tr}}\geq\pi_s^2/n_s\),
\[
|v_s|\leq c_0n_s^{-1/2}\leq c_0.
\]
Choose \(c_0\) so that all response probabilities stay in \([\eta,1-\eta]\), change only the treated event-response probability in cohort \(s\) by \(v_s\), and keep the untreated surfaces and all other observed laws fixed.  Absorbing the fixed separation of the two response-support points into constants, \texttt{lem:identification-fwl} and (2) give
\[
\left|\tau_{n,P_{1,\mathrm{tr}}}(z_0)-
\tau_{n,P_{0,\mathrm{tr}}}(z_0)\right|
=cV_{\mathrm{tr}}^{1/2},
\qquad
\operatorname{KL}(P_{1,\mathrm{tr}},P_{0,\mathrm{tr}})
\leq C\sum_s n_sv_s^2\leq Cc_0^2.
\tag{5}
\]

For the smoothing channel choose \(s_*\) maximizing
\(\pi_s^2(Tn_s)^{-2\lambda/(2\lambda+d)}\), and put
\[
b=c_b(Tn_{s_*})^{-1/(2\lambda+d)}.
\]
Take \(c_b>0\) small enough that the bump support is in the cube, and perturb only cohort \(s_*\) by
\[
m_{s_*,1}(x)-m_{s_*,0}(x)
=c_1b^\lambda\psi\!\left(\frac{x-z_{0s_*}}b\right),
\tag{6}
\]
where \(\psi\) is a fixed smooth compactly supported bump with \(\psi(0)=1\).  Use a bounded carrier with a nonzero covariance-standardized affine slope when \(\lambda>1\), and a one-sided asymmetric \(\lambda\)-cusp when \(0<\lambda\leq1\).  The carrier is scaled within the response and H\"older envelopes and so that its modulus lies between the fixed two bounds.  For \(\delta\leq b\), the increment of (6) over a displacement of standardized norm \(\delta\) is at most \(Cc_1\delta^\kappa\); for \(\delta>b\), it is at most \(Cc_1b^\lambda\leq Cc_1\delta^\kappa\).  Choosing \(c_1\) sufficiently small, and choosing the sign along the carrier's increasing direction, preserves both sides of \texttt{ass:local-two-sided-modulus}; the H\"older upper bound supplies the upper side and the carrier displacement supplies the lower side.  Thus both perturbed laws are members of the fixed nonempty class.

The event observed mean is held fixed and event transport adjusts the unobserved untreated mean.  Since \(S\) is fixed, \texttt{lem:identification-fwl} and maximality of \(s_*\) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|\tau_{n,P_{1,\mathrm{sm}}}(z_0)-
\tau_{n,P_{0,\mathrm{sm}}}(z_0)\right|^2
&=c\pi_{s_*}^2b^{2\lambda}\\
&\geq c\sum_s\pi_s^2(Tn_s)^{-2\lambda/(2\lambda+d)}
=cR_{\mathrm{sm}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
Use an independent uniform historical design.  The probability that a design point meets the bump support is at most \(Cb^d\).  Conditional product structure, (2), and integration over the design give
\[
\operatorname{KL}(P_{1,\mathrm{sm}},P_{0,\mathrm{sm}})
\leq CTn_{s_*}b^{2\lambda+d}\leq C.
\tag{8}
\]
Decreasing the fixed perturbation constant makes this divergence as small as desired while retaining separation by a fixed positive multiple of \(R_{\mathrm{sm}}\).

For the spacing channel, \texttt{lem:geometric-mixing-spacing-obstruction} supplies laws with the common donor marginal (4) such that
\[
\int\min\{dP_{0,\mathrm{sp}},dP_{1,\mathrm{sp}}\}
\geq a_{\mathrm{sp}}>0,
\qquad
\left|\tau_{n,P_{1,\mathrm{sp}}}(z_0)-
\tau_{n,P_{0,\mathrm{sp}}}(z_0)\right|^2
\geq cR_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}.
\tag{9}
\]
Only positive affinity is asserted or needed for this channel.

It remains to construct the donor channel.  Fix the strict slack \(B-D^2>0\).  Choose \(0<\xi<D\) such that
\[
D^2+\xi^2<B,
\]
put \(r_-=(D-\xi)^{1/2}\), \(r_+=(D+\xi)^{1/2}\), and choose two sufficiently close admissible interior centers
\[
z^{(1)}=z^{(0)}+\mu e_1,
\qquad
\mu=c_2N^{-1/2}.
\]
Use the common support
\[
\mathcal A=\{z^{(0)}\pm r_-e_k, z^{(0)}\pm r_+e_k:1\leq k\leq D\}.
\]
Under law zero, assign every atom probability \(1/(4D)\).  Its mean is \(z^{(0)}\), its covariance is \(I_D\), and
\[
\mathbb E_0[\|Z_j-z^{(0)}\|_2^4\mid E_0]
=\frac{r_-^4+r_+^4}{2}=D^2+\xi^2<B.
\tag{10}
\]
Under law one, leave all probabilities except those at the four atoms in direction \(e_1\) unchanged.  With
\[
a_N=\frac{\mu}{2r_-},
\qquad
b_N=\frac{\mu^2}{2\xi},
\]
replace the four probabilities, in the order
\(z^{(0)}+r_-e_1,z^{(0)}-r_-e_1,z^{(0)}+r_+e_1,z^{(0)}-r_+e_1\), by
\[
\frac1{4D}-\frac{b_N}{2}+a_N,
\quad
\frac1{4D}-\frac{b_N}{2}-a_N,
\quad
\frac1{4D}+\frac{b_N}{2},
\quad
\frac1{4D}+\frac{b_N}{2}.
\tag{11}
\]
For small fixed \(c_2\), depending only on the fixed slack through \(\xi\), these probabilities are positive for every \(N\) and sum to the baseline total.  Direct calculation gives
\[
\mathbb E_1[Z_j-z^{(0)}\mid E_0]=2a_Nr_-e_1=\mu e_1
\]
and
\[
\mathbb E_1[(Z_j-z^{(0)})(Z_j-z^{(0)})^\top\mid E_0]
=I_D+b_N(r_+^2-r_-^2)e_1e_1^\top
=I_D+\mu^2e_1e_1^\top.
\]
Consequently \(\mathbb E_1Z_j=z^{(1)}\) and
\(\operatorname{Var}_1(Z_j\mid E_0)=I_D\) exactly.  The centered standardized fourth moment is a continuous function of this finite probability vector and \(\mu\); by (10), a further fixed reduction of \(c_2\) keeps it below \(B\) under both laws.  This is the only step that uses strict slack.

The baseline probabilities are bounded below and the changes in (11) are \(O(\mu)\).  A second-order Taylor bound for \(x\log(x/y)\) on a compact subinterval of \((0,1)\) gives
\[
\operatorname{KL}(P_{1,\mathrm{don}},P_{0,\mathrm{don}})
\leq CN(a_N^2+b_N^2)
\leq CN\mu^2\leq Cc_2^2.
\tag{12}
\]
For \(\kappa=1\), choose the weighted regression carrier to have a nonzero linear slope in direction \(e_1\).  For \(0<\kappa=\lambda<1\), use in the selected cohort the common surface
\[
x\longmapsto c_3+c_4|e_1^\top(x-z^{(0)})|^\lambda
+c_5|e_1^\top(x-z^{(1)})|^\lambda,
\qquad c_4,c_5>0,quad c_4\ne c_5.
\tag{13}
\]
Fixed rescaling places the surface within the response and H\"older envelopes.  At either center, an outward one-coordinate displacement increases the relevant cusp contribution, which supplies the lower local modulus; the H\"older inequality supplies the upper modulus.  The fixed interior margin makes these displacements feasible for every \(0<\delta\leq\delta_0\).  Hence both centers obey the maintained two-sided condition, and evaluation at them yields
\[
\left|\sum_s\pi_s\{m_s(z_s^{(1)})-m_s(z_s^{(0)})\}\right|
\geq c\mu^\kappa=cN^{-\kappa/2}.
\tag{14}
\]
Keep the historical observed law, the regression surface, and the observed event mean fixed across the donor laws.  Under law \(\ell\), complete untreated event outcomes with cohort means \(m_s(z_s^{(\ell)})\).  The identification equality in \texttt{lem:identification-fwl} turns (14) into squared target separation at least \(cN^{-\kappa}=cR_{\mathrm{don}}\).

We finally convert the three bounded-divergence calculations to positive affinity without invoking an undeclared result.  Let \(A=\{dP_1/dP_0\geq1\}\), \(p=P_1(A)\), and \(q=P_0(A)\).  Convexity of \(x\log x\), applied on \(A\) and \(A^c\), gives the log-sum inequality
\[
\operatorname{KL}(P_1,P_0)\geq\operatorname{kl}(p,q).
\]
Twice differentiating \(\operatorname{kl}(q+u,q)\), and using \(q(1-q)\leq1/4\), gives
\(\operatorname{kl}(p,q)\geq2(p-q)^2\).  Because
\(p-q=\|P_1-P_0\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\),
\[
\int\min\{dP_0,dP_1\}
=1-\|P_1-P_0\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\geq1-\{\operatorname{KL}(P_1,P_0)/2\}^{1/2}.
\tag{15}
\]
Decreasing \(c_0,c_1,c_2\) makes (15) uniformly positive for the treated, smoothing, and, under strict slack, donor pairs; (9) handles spacing.  The completion following (3) verifies that every asserted pair is a regular conditional law of the complete causal panel.  Taking the admissible query deterministic makes its conditional probability one, hence at least \(c_X\).  Equations (1)--(15) prove all four assertions for \(B>D^2\).  For \(B=D^2\), (4)--(9) prove exactly the treated, smoothing, and spacing assertions with a common donor marginal, and no donor converse has been used.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:sharp-joint-frontier]\label{thm:sharp-joint-frontier}
Put \(D=Sd\).  For each fixed nonempty class with donor-envelope interior compatibility \(B>D^2\), there are constants \(0<c_{>}<C_{>}<\infty\), allowed to depend on the fixed slack \(B-D^2\) and otherwise only on fixed class and procedural constants, such that along every array with \(N\wedge T\wedge\min_s n_s\to\infty\), uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger}(\lambda)\) and for almost every admissible realized path \(z_0\in\mathcal Z_+\),
\[
c_{>}R_{\star,\beta}
\le\inf_{\widetilde\tau}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger}(\lambda)}
\mathbb E_P[(\widetilde\tau-\tau_n(z_0))^2\mid E_0]
\le\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger}(\lambda)}
\mathbb E_P[(\widehat\tau-\tau_n(z_0))^2\mid E_0]
\le C_{>}R_{\star,\beta}.
\]
For each fixed otherwise nonempty boundary class with \(B=D^2\), there are independently quantified constants \(0<c_{=}<C_{=}<\infty\) such that the same arrays obey
\[
c_{=}\{R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sm}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}\}
\le\inf_{\widetilde\tau}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger}(\lambda)}
\mathbb E_P[(\widetilde\tau-\tau_n(z_0))^2\mid E_0]
\le\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger}(\lambda)}
\mathbb E_P[(\widehat\tau-\tau_n(z_0))^2\mid E_0]
\le C_{=}R_{\star,\beta};
\]
no universal \(R_{\mathrm{don}}\) lower bound is asserted at equality.  If \(B<D^2\), the class is empty and no substantive minimax assertion is made.  In both nonempty regimes, the raw estimator in \texttt{def:feasible-estimator}, using all donors once, the normalized-shape selector, and the log-adjusted initial bandwidth, attains the displayed upper bound with nonidentically distributed bounded event and historical unit responses and an unknown non-Gaussian donor law with unrestricted coordinate heteroskedasticity inside the covariance envelope.  The estimator does not use or estimate \(\Sigma\).  No sequential-overlap or other anti-clustering assumption is imposed.

\begin{proof}
Put \(D=Sd\).  By \texttt{lem:joint-law-embedding}, the covariance and standardized fourth-moment requirements force \(B\geq D^2\), so the class is empty when \(B<D^2\).  We treat the two nonempty regimes separately.

Suppose first that \(B>D^2\).  For each
\(q\in\{\mathrm{tr},\mathrm{sm},\mathrm{sp},\mathrm{don}\}\), let
\((P_{0,q},P_{1,q})\) be the conditional two-law experiment furnished by \texttt{lem:joint-law-embedding}.  Write
\[
\theta_{\ell,q}=\tau_{n,P_{\ell,q}}(z_0),
\qquad
\Delta_q=|\theta_{1,q}-\theta_{0,q}|.
\]
There are constants \(a_{q,>},c_{q,>}>0\), uniform in \(N,T,\mathbf n\), such that
\[
\int\min\{dP_{0,q},dP_{1,q}\}\geq a_{q,>},
\qquad
\Delta_q^2\geq c_{q,>}R_q.
\tag{16}
\]
For any observed-data estimator \(\widetilde\tau\), define
\[
A_{\ell,q}=\{|\widetilde\tau-\theta_{\ell,q}|\geq\Delta_q/2\}.
\]
The triangle inequality implies \(A_{0,q}\cup A_{1,q}=\Omega\).  Integrating against the overlap measure gives
\[
P_{0,q}(A_{0,q}\mid E_0)+P_{1,q}(A_{1,q}\mid E_0)
\geq a_{q,>}.
\tag{17}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\max_{\ell\in\{0,1\}}
\mathbb E_{P_{\ell,q}}[(\widetilde\tau-\theta_{\ell,q})^2\mid E_0]
&\geq\frac{\Delta_q^2}{4}
\max_\ell P_{\ell,q}(A_{\ell,q}\mid E_0)\\
&\geq\frac{a_{q,>}\Delta_q^2}{8}
\geq\frac{a_{q,>}c_{q,>}}8R_q.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]
Maximizing (18) over the four channels and using \texttt{def:rate-frontier} proves the strict-slack minimax lower bound.  Its constant may depend on the fixed slack \(B-D^2\) through the donor pair.

When \(B=D^2\), the boundary clause of \texttt{lem:joint-law-embedding} supplies independently quantified overlap and separation constants for precisely
\(q\in\{\mathrm{tr},\mathrm{sm},\mathrm{sp}\}\).  The same calculation gives
\[
\inf_{\widetilde\tau}\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger}(\lambda)}
\mathbb E_P[(\widetilde\tau-\tau_n(z_0))^2\mid E_0]
\geq c_{=}
\{R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sm}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}\},
\tag{19}
\]
where \(c_{=}>0\) is chosen independently of the strict-slack constant.  No donor pair is used at equality.

We now prove the common upper bound.  Let
\[
\mathscr X=\sigma\{X_{st}:1\leq s\leq S,\ 1\leq t\leq T\},
\qquad
\mathscr G=\sigma\{\mathscr X,E_0,Z_1,\ldots,Z_N\}.
\]
The variable \(z_0=\mathbb E_P[Z_j\mid E_0]\) is \(E_0\)-measurable.  On the good sample, \texttt{lem:identification-fwl}, the linear representation in \texttt{def:honest-interval}, and \(Q_{sti}=m_s(X_{st})+\varepsilon_{sti}\) give the exact decomposition
\[
\widehat\tau-\tau_n(z_0)
=U_{\mathrm{evt}}-U_{\mathrm{hist}}-B_{\mathrm{LP}}-B_{\mathrm{don}},
\tag{20}
\]
where
\[
U_{\mathrm{evt}}
=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}\frac{\pi_s}{n_s}
\{B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}-\mathbb E_P[B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid E_0]\},
\tag{21}
\]
\[
U_{\mathrm{hist}}
=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}\pi_s
\sum_{t=1}^{T}a_{sti}\varepsilon_{sti},
\tag{22}
\]
\[
B_{\mathrm{LP}}
=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s
\left\{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}a_{sti}m_s(X_{st})
-m_s(\widetilde z_s)\right\},
\tag{23}
\]
and
\[
B_{\mathrm{don}}
=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s\{m_s(\widetilde z_s)-m_s(z_{0s})\}.
\tag{24}
\]
Assumption \texttt{ass:event-full-design-mean-stability} centers the summands in (21) given \((\mathscr X,E_0)\), and \texttt{ass:historical-full-design-centering} centers those in (22).  Assumption \texttt{ass:donor-independence} permits adjoining all donors to the conditioning.  Assumption \texttt{ass:cross-unit-independence} then makes the unit-level variables independent across distinct \((s,i)\).  Since event wealth lies in \([0,B]\),
\[
\mathbb E_P[U_{\mathrm{evt}}^2\mid\mathscr G]
\leq B^2\sum_{s=1}^{S}\frac{\pi_s^2}{n_s}
=B^2R_{\mathrm{tr}}.
\tag{25}
\]
Conditional on \(\mathscr G\), the range length of
\(\sum_ta_{sti}\varepsilon_{sti}\) is at most
\(B\sum_t|a_{sti}|\).  Therefore \texttt{lem:full-data-local-polynomial-control} gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_P[U_{\mathrm{hist}}^2\mid\mathscr G]
&\leq B^2\sum_{s=1}^{S}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}
\left[\pi_s\sum_{t=1}^{T}|a_{sti}|\right]^2\\
&\leq B^2c_G^{-1}R_{\mathrm{tr}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]
Thus neither bound requires identical unit laws or temporal independence within a unit.

On a successful fit, the definition of \(a_{sti}\) gives polynomial reproduction:
\[
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}a_{sti}b_r(u_{st})
=\left\{\sum_{t,i}w_{st}b_r(u_{st})b_r(u_{st})^\top\right\}G_s^{-1}e_0
=e_0.
\tag{27}
\]
Let \(C_K\) bound the Euclidean radius of the support of \(K\).  A nonzero weight implies
\(\|X_{st}-\widetilde z_s\|_2\leq C_Kh_s\).  The degree-\(r\) Taylor remainder in \texttt{ass:holder-smoothness}, (27), and \texttt{lem:full-data-local-polynomial-control} imply
\[
\left|\sum_{t,i}a_{sti}m_s(X_{st})-m_s(\widetilde z_s)\right|
\leq CLh_s^\lambda\sum_{t,i}|a_{sti}|
\leq Ch_s^\lambda.
\tag{28}
\]
Since \(S\) is fixed, the selector moment bound with exponent \(2\lambda\), first conditional on \((E_0,\widetilde z_s)\) and then integrated over donors, yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_P[B_{\mathrm{LP}}^2\mathbf1_{\mathrm{good}}\mid E_0]
&\leq C\sum_s\pi_s^2
\mathbb E_P[h_s^{2\lambda}\mathbf1_{\mathrm{good}}\mid E_0]\\
&\leq C\left\{
R_{\mathrm{sm}}+R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}+N^{-\lambda}\right\}
\leq CR_{\star,\beta},
\end{aligned}
\tag{29}
\]
because \(\kappa=\min\{\lambda,1\}\) implies \(N^{-\lambda}\leq N^{-\kappa}\).

For the donor term, define
\[
Y_j=\Sigma^{-1/2}(Z_j-z_0),
\qquad
W_N=N^{-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}Y_j.
\]
Conditional donor replication and the definitions of \(z_0\) and \(\Sigma\) give independent, centered \(Y_j\)'s with conditional covariance \(I_D\).  Expanding the fourth power, terms containing a singleton sample index vanish; the all-equal terms and the three pairings give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_P[\|W_N\|_2^4\mid E_0]
&=\frac1N\mathbb E_P[\|Y_1\|_2^4\mid E_0]\\
&\quad+\left(1-\frac1N\right)
\{(\operatorname{tr}I_D)^2+2\operatorname{tr}(I_D^2)\}\\
&\leq\frac BN+\left(1-\frac1N\right)(D^2+2D)=M_4(N).
\end{aligned}
\tag{30}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\mathbb E_P[\|\Sigma^{-1/2}(\bar Z-z_0)\|_2^4\mid E_0]
\leq CN^{-2}.
\tag{31}
\]
Euclidean projection on the closed cube cannot increase distance from the cube point \(z_0\).  Ellipticity therefore gives
\[
V_N:=\|\Sigma^{-1/2}(\widetilde z-z_0)\|_2
\leq\sqrt{C_\Sigma/c_\Sigma}\,
\|\Sigma^{-1/2}(\bar Z-z_0)\|_2.
\tag{32}
\]
On \(\{V_N\leq\delta_0\}\), the displacement in (24) is feasible in \texttt{def:local-modulus}, so its upper bound gives
\[
|B_{\mathrm{don}}|\leq C_{\mathrm{mod}}V_N^\kappa.
\]
Lyapunov's inequality, (31)--(32), and \(0<\kappa\leq1\) show
\[
\mathbb E_P[B_{\mathrm{don}}^2\mathbf1\{V_N\leq\delta_0\}\mid E_0]
\leq C\{\mathbb E_P[V_N^4\mid E_0]\}^{\kappa/2}
\leq CN^{-\kappa}.
\tag{33}
\]
Every \(m_s\) takes values in \([0,B]\), so \(|B_{\mathrm{don}}|\leq B\).  Markov's inequality and (31)--(32) yield
\[
\mathbb E_P[B_{\mathrm{don}}^2\mathbf1\{V_N>\delta_0\}\mid E_0]
\leq B^2P(V_N>\delta_0\mid E_0)
\leq CN^{-2}\leq CN^{-\kappa}.
\tag{34}
\]
Thus
\[
\mathbb E_P[B_{\mathrm{don}}^2\mid E_0]\leq CR_{\mathrm{don}}.
\tag{35}
\]
This calculation uses the full covariance but the estimator neither knows nor estimates it.

On a successful fit,
\[
|\widehat m_s|\leq B\sum_{t,i}|a_{sti}|\leq Bc_G^{-1/2},
\tag{36}
\]
whereas a failed cohort contributes zero.  Event averages and the target are bounded by the wealth envelope.  Hence the squared error is uniformly bounded on the bad event.  The bad-event bound in \texttt{lem:geometric-mixing-selector-control} makes its conditional contribution at most \(CR_{\star,\beta}\) along the arrays in the theorem.

Applying \((x_1+x_2+x_3+x_4)^2\leq4\sum_{j=1}^4x_j^2\) to (20), integrating (25)--(26), and combining (29), (35), and the bad-event bound gives
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger}(\lambda)}
\mathbb E_P[(\widehat\tau-\tau_n(z_0))^2\mid E_0]
\leq CR_{\star,\beta}.
\tag{37}
\]
The estimator is observed-data measurable, so the minimax infimum is no larger than its worst-case risk.  Name the constant in (37) \(C_{>}\) in the strict regime and, independently, \(C_{=}\) at equality.  Combining (37) with (18) or (19) proves the two displayed minimax brackets and all their qualifications.  The selector theorem used only the declared geometric beta-mixing envelope, so no sequential-overlap or anti-clustering premise has entered.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:oracle-generated-phase]\label{thm:oracle-generated-phase}
Uniformly over fixed \(S\) and all positive sample sizes, let
\[
C_{\lambda,d}:=\frac{2\lambda}{2\lambda+d}
+\frac{d}{2\lambda+d}(\log 2)^{-2\lambda/d}.
\]
Then
\[
R_{\mathrm{sm}}
\leq C_{\lambda,d}
\bigl(R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}\bigr),
\qquad
R_{\star,\beta}
\asymp_{\lambda,d}
R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}\vee R_{\mathrm{don}}.
\]
Writing
\[
a:=\frac{2\lambda}{2\lambda+d},
\]
for every integer \(T\geq 3\) one has the sharper exact relations
\[
R_{\mathrm{sm}}
\leq aR_{\mathrm{tr}}+(1-a)R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}
\leq R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta},
\qquad
R_{\star,\beta}
=R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}\vee R_{\mathrm{don}}.
\]
Consequently, in any experiment for which \(R_{\star,\beta}\) is established as the sharp risk, the oracle region is
\[
N^{-\kappa}=o\bigl(R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}\bigr),
\]
the generated-center region is
\[
R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}=o(N^{-\kappa}),
\]
and the boundary is
\[
N\asymp
\bigl(R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}\bigr)^{-1/\kappa}.
\]
Under independent design, or under a separately imposed fixed-query local-small-ball/anti-clustering condition that delivers \(R_{\mathrm{sp}}=T^{-2\lambda/d}\), one instead has the exact relations
\[
R_{\mathrm{sm}}\leq R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp}},
\qquad
R_\star=R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp}}\vee R_{\mathrm{don}}.
\]
Assumption \(\texttt{ass:exact-training-coordinates}\) removes a classical measurement-error inverse problem from the historical design; accordingly the defined bookkeeping contains neither a deconvolution term nor a separate moving-Gram rate. Finally, \(R_{\mathrm{don}}=N^{-1}\) whenever \(\lambda>1\). These algebraic conclusions do not by themselves assert that either frontier is sharp for a statistical experiment.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put
\[
a:=\frac{2\lambda}{2\lambda+d}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
q:=\frac{2\lambda}{d}.
\]
The declared restrictions \(\lambda>0\) and \(d>0\) imply \(a\in(0,1)\) and \(q>0\).  We first establish the weighted arithmetic--geometric mean inequality used below.  For \(u>0\), define
\[
\phi(u):=au+(1-a)-u^a.
\]
Then \(\phi(1)=0\), while
\[
\phi'(u)=a\bigl(1-u^{a-1}\bigr).
\]
Because \(a-1<0\), this derivative is negative for \(0<u<1\), vanishes at \(u=1\), and is positive for \(u>1\).  Thus \(u=1\) is a global minimizer and \(\phi(u)\geq0\).  Substituting \(u=x/y\) and multiplying by \(y>0\) proves
\[
x^a y^{1-a}\leq ax+(1-a)y,
\qquad x>0,quad y>0.
\tag{1}
\]
The definitions of \(a\) and \(q\) give
\[
q(1-a)
=\frac{2\lambda}{d}\frac{d}{2\lambda+d}
=\frac{2\lambda}{2\lambda+d}
=a.
\tag{2}
\]
For every cohort \(s\), positivity of the integers \(T\) and \(n_s\) permits applying (1) with \(x=n_s^{-1}\) and \(y=T^{-q}\).  Using (2),
\[
\begin{aligned}
(Tn_s)^{-2\lambda/(2\lambda+d)}
&=n_s^{-a}T^{-a}\\
&=(n_s^{-1})^a(T^{-q})^{1-a}\\
&\leq a n_s^{-1}+(1-a)T^{-q}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
The declared cohort weights satisfy \(\pi_s>0\) and \(\sum_{s=1}^S\pi_s=1\).  Hence
\[
\sum_{s=1}^S\pi_s^2
\leq\left(\sum_{s=1}^S\pi_s\right)^2
=1.
\tag{4}
\]
Multiplying (3) by \(\pi_s^2\), summing over \(s\), applying (4), and invoking the definitions of \(R_{\mathrm{sm}}\) and \(R_{\mathrm{tr}}\) in \(\texttt{def:rate-frontier}\), we obtain
\[
R_{\mathrm{sm}}
\leq aR_{\mathrm{tr}}+(1-a)T^{-q}.
\tag{5}
\]

For every positive integer \(T\), one has \(\log(T\vee2)\geq\log2>0\).  Therefore, because \(q>0\), the definition of \(R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}\) in \(\texttt{def:rate-frontier}\) yields
\[
T^{-q}
\leq(\log2)^{-q}
\left\{\frac{\log(T\vee2)}{T}\right\}^{q}
=(\log2)^{-q}R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}.
\tag{6}
\]
Combining (5) and (6), and using \(cx+ey\leq(c+e)(x\vee y)\) for nonnegative \(c,e,x,y\), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
R_{\mathrm{sm}}
&\leq aR_{\mathrm{tr}}+(1-a)(\log2)^{-q}R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}\\
&\leq\bigl\{a+(1-a)(\log2)^{-q}\bigr\}
\bigl(R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}\bigr)\\
&=C_{\lambda,d}
\bigl(R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}\bigr),
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
where the last equality follows from \(1-a=d/(2\lambda+d)\) and \(q=2\lambda/d\).  Since \(0<\log2<1\) and \(q>0\),
\[
C_{\lambda,d}=a+(1-a)(\log2)^{-q}
\geq a+(1-a)=1.
\tag{8}
\]
Define \(A:=R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}\) and \(G:=A\vee R_{\mathrm{don}}\).  Every rate component is positive by \(\texttt{def:rate-frontier}\).  Its definition of \(R_{\star,\beta}\), together with (7)--(8), gives
\[
G
\leq R_{\star,\beta}
=G\vee R_{\mathrm{sm}}
\leq G\vee(C_{\lambda,d}A)
\leq C_{\lambda,d}G.
\tag{9}
\]
Consequently,
\[
R_{\star,\beta}
\asymp_{\lambda,d}
R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}\vee R_{\mathrm{don}},
\]
with comparison constants \(1\) and \(C_{\lambda,d}\), uniformly in \(N,T,S\) and all positive replication counts.

Suppose next that \(T\geq3\).  Then \(T\vee2=T\) and \(\log T\geq\log3>1\), so
\[
T^{-q}
\leq\left(\frac{\log T}{T}\right)^q
=R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}.
\tag{10}
\]
Substitution of (10) into (5), followed by \(a\in(0,1)\), proves
\[
R_{\mathrm{sm}}
\leq aR_{\mathrm{tr}}+(1-a)R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}
\leq R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}.
\tag{11}
\]
Taking the maximum with the remaining components in \(\texttt{def:rate-frontier}\) therefore gives
\[
R_{\star,\beta}
=R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}\vee R_{\mathrm{don}}.
\tag{12}
\]

By \(\texttt{def:rate-frontier}\), \(R_{\mathrm{don}}=N^{-\kappa}\).  Along any joint growth array, this channel is negligible relative to \(A\) precisely when
\[
N^{-\kappa}=o(A),
\]
and it dominates \(A\) precisely when
\[
A=o(N^{-\kappa}).
\]
Because \(\kappa>0\) and \(A>0\), the boundary relation \(N^{-\kappa}\asymp A\) is equivalent, after applying the decreasing map \(x\mapsto x^{-1/\kappa}\) and absorbing fixed comparison constants, to
\[
N\asymp A^{-1/\kappa}.
\]
These comparisons have the asserted oracle/generated-center interpretation whenever a separate statistical result establishes \(R_{\star,\beta}\) as the sharp risk of the experiment; equations (1)--(12) do not assert that statistical premise.

For the independent-calendar class, or under the separately imposed fixed-query local-small-ball or anti-clustering condition specified in \(\texttt{def:rate-frontier}\), the delivered spacing component is
\[
R_{\mathrm{sp}}=T^{-q}.
\]
Equation (5) consequently implies
\[
R_{\mathrm{sm}}
\leq aR_{\mathrm{tr}}+(1-a)R_{\mathrm{sp}}
\leq R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp}}.
\tag{13}
\]
The corresponding definition of \(R_\star\) in \(\texttt{def:rate-frontier}\) and (13) then give the exact identity
\[
R_\star
=R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sm}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp}}\vee R_{\mathrm{don}}
=R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp}}\vee R_{\mathrm{don}}.
\]

Finally, \(\texttt{ass:exact-training-coordinates}\) states \(X_{st}^{\mathrm{obs}}=X_{st}\).  Thus the historical regression coordinate is observed exactly, so there is no measurement-error convolution to invert.  Consistently, the exhaustive maxima defining \(R_{\star,\beta}\) and \(R_\star\) in \(\texttt{def:rate-frontier}\) contain neither a deconvolution component nor a separate moving-Gram component.  The same definition uses \(\kappa=\min\{\lambda,1\}\).  If \(\lambda>1\), then \(\kappa=1\), and hence
\[
R_{\mathrm{don}}=N^{-\kappa}=N^{-1}.
\]
This proves every asserted algebraic conclusion and preserves the theorem's stated distinction between those conclusions and statistical sharpness.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:honest-length-frontier]\label{thm:honest-length-frontier}
Put \(D=Sd\).  For every positive \(N,T,n_1,\ldots,n_S\), every compatible fixed envelope \(B\ge D^2\), every \(P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger}(\lambda)\), and \(P\)-almost every admissible event state,
\[
P_P\{\tau_n(z_0)\in C_\alpha\mid E_0\}\ge1-\alpha.
\]
For fixed sample sizes and a fixed compatible envelope, call an interval \(I\) finite-sample conditionally honest if it is observed-data measurable and satisfies this same inequality with \(I\) in place of \(C_\alpha\), uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger}(\lambda)\).  For each fixed nonempty class with donor-envelope interior compatibility \(B>D^2\), along every array with \(N\wedge T\wedge\min_s n_s\to\infty\), there are constants \(0<c_{\alpha,>}<C_{\alpha,>}^{\mathrm{up}}<\infty\), allowed to depend on the fixed slack \(B-D^2\), such that
\[
c_{\alpha,>}R_{\star,\beta}^{1/2}
\le
\inf_{I:\,\text{finite-sample conditionally honest}}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger}(\lambda)}
\mathbb E_P[\operatorname{length}(I)\mid E_0]
\le
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger}(\lambda)}
\mathbb E_P[\operatorname{length}(C_\alpha)\mid E_0]
\le C_{\alpha,>}^{\mathrm{up}}R_{\star,\beta}^{1/2}.
\]
For each fixed otherwise nonempty boundary class with \(B=D^2\), there are independently quantified constants \(0<c_{\alpha,=}<C_{\alpha,=}^{\mathrm{up}}<\infty\) such that
\[
c_{\alpha,=}\{R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sm}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}\}^{1/2}
\le
\inf_{I:\,\text{finite-sample conditionally honest}}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger}(\lambda)}
\mathbb E_P[\operatorname{length}(I)\mid E_0]
\le
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger}(\lambda)}
\mathbb E_P[\operatorname{length}(C_\alpha)\mid E_0]
\le C_{\alpha,=}^{\mathrm{up}}R_{\star,\beta}^{1/2};
\]
no universal donor-channel expected-length converse is asserted at equality.  Both lower bounds also hold for uniformly asymptotically conditionally honest interval sequences by an \(\epsilon\)-slack argument.  If \(B<D^2\), the class is empty and no substantive minimax assertion is made.  The historical and event concentration steps condition on the complete historical design, \(E_0\), and all donors; the donor-deviation probability is then integrated out conditional on \(E_0\), and the three failure probabilities are combined by Bonferroni.  The result requires neither temporal outcome mixing within a unit, identical unit response laws, studentization, a dense-local-site condition, nor sequential overlap.  The log-adjusted spacing term is uniform and unavoidable under design-only geometric beta mixing and the maintained marginal-density assumptions.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(D=Sd\).  The compatibility calculation in \texttt{lem:joint-law-embedding} gives
\[
D^2\leq
\mathbb E_P\!\left[
\|\Sigma^{-1/2}(Z_1-z_0)\|_2^4
\,\middle|\,E_0\right]
\leq B.
\tag{38}
\]
Thus the class is empty when \(B<D^2\).  Fix \(B\geq D^2\), a law
\(P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger}(\lambda)\), and an admissible event state outside a conditional null set.  Let
\[
\mathscr X=\sigma\{X_{st}:1\leq s\leq S,\ 1\leq t\leq T\}.
\]
Because \(z_0\) is \(E_0\)-measurable, adjoining it does not alter any conditional mean below.

We first prove exact finite-sample coverage.  On each successful fit, polynomial reproduction from \texttt{lem:full-data-local-polynomial-control}, applied to the degree-\(r\) Taylor polynomial of \(m_s\) at \(\widetilde z_s\), gives
\[
\left|
e_0^\top\widehat\gamma_s-m_s(\widetilde z_s)
-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}a_{sti}\varepsilon_{sti}
\right|
\leq C_{r,d}L\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}|a_{sti}|
\|X_{st}-\widetilde z_s\|_2^\lambda.
\tag{39}
\]
After multiplication by \(\pi_s\) and summation over \(s\), the right-hand side is exactly \(A_L\).

Define the historical unit-cluster variables
\[
\mathsf H_{si}=\pi_s\sum_{t=1}^{T}a_{sti}\varepsilon_{sti}.
\]
Assumption \texttt{ass:historical-full-design-centering} centers these variables given \((\mathscr X,E_0)\).  Assumption \texttt{ass:donor-independence} preserves the centering when all donors are adjoined, and \texttt{ass:cross-unit-independence} gives conditional independence over \((s,i)\).  By \texttt{ass:bounded-historical-wealth}, the conditional range length of \(\mathsf H_{si}\) is at most
\[
c_{si}=B\pi_s\sum_{t=1}^{T}|a_{sti}|.
\]
For completeness, if a conditionally centered variable \(U\) lies in an interval of length \(c\), convexity of \(x\mapsto e^{vx}\) bounds its conditional exponential moment by that of the endpoint mixture with the same mean.  Differentiating the logarithm of the two-point mixture yields
\[
\mathbb E[e^{vU}\mid\mathscr X,E_0,(Z_j)_{j=1}^{N}]
\leq e^{v^2c^2/8}.
\tag{40}
\]
Multiplying (40) over conditionally independent unit clusters, applying the exponential Markov inequality, optimizing in \(v>0\), and repeating for \(-U\) give
\[
P_P\!\left\{
\left|\sum_{s=1}^{S}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}\mathsf H_{si}\right|
>R_{\mathrm{hist}}
\,\middle|\,\mathscr X,E_0,(Z_j)_{j=1}^{N}
\right\}
\leq\alpha_{\mathrm{hist}}.
\tag{41}
\]
Thus the whole calendar path of a unit is one bounded cluster; no temporal outcome mixing is used.

Similarly define
\[
\mathsf E_{si}=\frac{\pi_s}{n_s}
\{B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}-\mathbb E_P[B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid E_0]\}.
\]
Assumption \texttt{ass:event-full-design-mean-stability} centers \(\mathsf E_{si}\) given \((\mathscr X,E_0)\), donor independence preserves this after donors are adjoined, and cross-unit independence again gives conditional independence.  By \texttt{ass:bounded-event-wealth}, the range lengths are at most \(B\pi_s/n_s\).  The calculation leading to (41) gives
\[
P_P\!\left\{
\left|\sum_{s=1}^{S}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}\mathsf E_{si}\right|
>R_{\mathrm{evt}}
\,\middle|\,\mathscr X,E_0,(Z_j)_{j=1}^{N}
\right\}
\leq\alpha_{\mathrm{evt}}.
\tag{42}
\]
A union bound combines (41) and (42); independence between a unit's historical path and its event outcome is unnecessary.

For donor calibration define, only in this proof,
\[
Y_j=\Sigma^{-1/2}(Z_j-z_0),
\qquad
W_N=N^{-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}Y_j.
\]
The definitions of \(z_0\) and \(\Sigma\), ellipticity, and conditional donor replication imply
\[
\mathbb E_P[Y_j\mid E_0]=0,
\qquad
\mathbb E_P[Y_jY_j^\top\mid E_0]=I_D,
\]
with conditional independence across \(j\).  Expansion of the fourth power makes every term with a singleton sample index vanish.  The all-equal terms and the three pairings therefore give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_P[\|W_N\|_2^4\mid E_0]
&=\frac1N\mathbb E_P[\|Y_1\|_2^4\mid E_0]
+\left(1-\frac1N\right)(D^2+2D)\\
&\leq\frac BN+\left(1-\frac1N\right)(D^2+2D)
=M_4(N).
\end{aligned}
\tag{43}
\]
Let \(\mathcal D_N=\{\|W_N\|_2\leq q_N\}\).  Markov's inequality and the definition of \(q_N\) imply
\[
P_P(\mathcal D_N^c\mid E_0)
\leq\frac{M_4(N)}{q_N^4}=\alpha_{\mathrm{don}}.
\tag{44}
\]
Projection on the closed convex cube cannot increase Euclidean distance from \(z_0\), which belongs to that cube.  The covariance eigenvalue bounds therefore give, on \(\mathcal D_N\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\Sigma^{-1/2}(\widetilde z-z_0)\|_2
&\leq c_\Sigma^{-1/2}\|\widetilde z-z_0\|_2\\
&\leq c_\Sigma^{-1/2}\|\bar Z-z_0\|_2\\
&\leq\sqrt{C_\Sigma/c_\Sigma}\,q_N/\sqrt N
=\delta_N.
\end{aligned}
\tag{45}
\]
On the good sample, \(\delta_N\leq\delta_0\).  The upper half of \texttt{ass:local-two-sided-modulus} and (45) then imply
\[
\left|\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s
\{m_s(\widetilde z_s)-m_s(z_{0s})\}\right|
\leq C_{\mathrm{mod}}\delta_N^\kappa=A_{\mathrm{don}}.
\tag{46}
\]

Let \(\mathcal G\) denote the selector-and-radius good event in \texttt{def:honest-interval}.  It is measurable with respect to
\((\mathscr X,E_0,(Z_j)_{j=1}^{N})\).  On \(\mathcal G^c\), the interval is \([-B,B]\).  Bounded event potential wealth and the positive weights summing to one imply \(\tau_n(z_0)\in[-B,B]\).  On \(\mathcal G\cap\mathcal D_N\), \texttt{lem:identification-fwl}, (39), and (46) decompose \(\widehat\tau-\tau_n(z_0)\) into the event fluctuation, minus the historical fluctuation, and two remainders bounded by \(A_L\) and \(A_{\mathrm{don}}\).  Hence noncoverage there implies failure of (41) or (42).  Iterated conditioning and Bonferroni's inequality yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_P\{\tau_n(z_0)\notin C_\alpha\mid E_0\}
&\leq P_P(\mathcal D_N^c\mid E_0)\\
&\quad+\mathbb E_P\!\left[
\mathbf1_{\mathcal G\cap\mathcal D_N}
P_P\!\left\{
\text{historical failure or event failure}
\,\middle|\,\mathscr X,E_0,(Z_j)_{j=1}^{N}
\right\}
\,\middle|\,E_0\right]\\
&\leq\alpha_{\mathrm{don}}+\alpha_{\mathrm{hist}}+\alpha_{\mathrm{evt}}
=\alpha.
\end{aligned}
\tag{47}
\]
No selector-tail probability appears in (47), so coverage is exact for every positive sample size, including when \(B=D^2\).  Intersecting the untruncated interval with \([-B,B]\) cannot remove the target.

For the expected-length upper bound, \texttt{lem:full-data-local-polynomial-control} gives, on every successful fit,
\[
R_{\mathrm{hist}}
\leq B\left\{\frac12\log(2/\alpha_{\mathrm{hist}})c_G^{-1}R_{\mathrm{tr}}\right\}^{1/2},
\qquad
R_{\mathrm{evt}}
=B\left\{\frac12\log(2/\alpha_{\mathrm{evt}})R_{\mathrm{tr}}\right\}^{1/2}.
\tag{48}
\]
Compact support of \(K\) and the same absolute-weight bound imply
\[
A_L\leq C\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_sh_s^\lambda
\quad\text{on }\mathcal G.
\tag{49}
\]
Apply \texttt{lem:geometric-mixing-selector-control} with exponent \(\lambda\), conditionally on each projected donor center and then integrate over donors.  Since \(S\) is fixed, Cauchy--Schwarz across cohorts and \(\kappa=\min\{\lambda,1\}\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sup_P\mathbb E_P[A_L\mathbf1_{\mathcal G}\mid E_0]
&\leq C\{R_{\mathrm{sm}}^{1/2}
+R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}^{1/2}+N^{-\lambda/2}\}\\
&\leq CR_{\star,\beta}^{1/2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{50}
\]
Since \(D,B,\alpha\) are fixed, (43) implies \(q_N\leq C\), and hence
\[
A_{\mathrm{don}}\leq CN^{-\kappa/2}=CR_{\mathrm{don}}^{1/2}.
\tag{51}
\]
The selector lemma also gives
\(P_P(\mathcal G^c\mid E_0)\leq CR_{\star,\beta}\) along every asserted array.  On \(\mathcal G\), interval length is at most \(2H_\alpha\); on \(\mathcal G^c\), it is at most \(2B\).  Since \(R_{\star,\beta}\to0\), eventually \(R_{\star,\beta}\leq R_{\star,\beta}^{1/2}\).  Combining (48)--(51) proves
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger}(\lambda)}
\mathbb E_P[\operatorname{length}(C_\alpha)\mid E_0]
\leq C_\alpha^{\mathrm{up}}R_{\star,\beta}^{1/2},
\tag{52}
\]
with independently quantified constants in the two compatible envelope regimes.

It remains to prove the expected-length converse.  The treated, smoothing, and donor perturbation constants in \texttt{lem:joint-law-embedding} can be decreased, while remaining fixed along the array, so that their affinities are arbitrarily close to one and their target separations remain fixed positive multiples of \(R_q^{1/2}\).  In \texttt{lem:geometric-mixing-spacing-obstruction}, decreasing the fixed active-block constant likewise makes the no-active-block probability, and hence the affinity, arbitrarily close to one while preserving a fixed positive multiple of \(R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}^{1/2}\).  Because \(\alpha<1/2\), choose these constants so every available affinity
\[
A_q=\int\min\{dP_{0,q},dP_{1,q}\}
\]
exceeds \(2\alpha+a_0\) for a fixed \(a_0>0\).  The donor choice may depend on the strict slack.

Let \(I\) be a finite-sample conditionally honest observed-data interval, write the two targets in one channel as \(\theta_0,\theta_1\), and let \(\mu_q=P_{0,q}\wedge P_{1,q}\) be the conditional overlap measure.  Uniform honesty gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu_q\{\theta_0,\theta_1\in I\}
&\geq\mu_q(\Omega)
-P_{0,q}\{\theta_0\notin I\mid E_0\}
-P_{1,q}\{\theta_1\notin I\mid E_0\}\\
&\geq A_q-2\alpha\geq a_0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{53}
\]
On this event \(\operatorname{length}(I)\geq|\theta_1-\theta_0|\), so
\[
\max_{\ell\in\{0,1\}}
\mathbb E_{P_{\ell,q}}[\operatorname{length}(I)\mid E_0]
\geq\int\operatorname{length}(I)\,d\mu_q
\geq a_0|\theta_1-\theta_0|
\geq c_\alpha R_q^{1/2}.
\tag{54}
\]
When \(B>D^2\), maximizing (54) over all four channels gives the lower bound
\(c_{\alpha,>}R_{\star,\beta}^{1/2}\), with allowed dependence on the fixed slack.  When \(B=D^2\), independently quantified constants and the three available channels give
\[
c_{\alpha,=}
\{R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sm}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}\}^{1/2};
\tag{55}
\]
no donor converse is invoked.  Finally, for a uniformly asymptotically conditionally honest sequence, every \(\epsilon>0\) makes each noncoverage probability in (53) eventually at most \(\alpha+\epsilon\).  Choosing \(\epsilon<a_0/4\) leaves overlap at least \(a_0/2\), proving the same rates.  Equations (47), (52), and (54)--(55) establish every assertion.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This theorem retains exact coverage and the sharp beta-mixing expected-length frontier. \textit{Gap.} At the donor-envelope boundary its lower bound excludes the donor channel. \textit{Consumer.} It is the weak-dependence side of the honest-length dependence elbow.

\begin{proposition}[prop:canonical-reductions]\label{prop:canonical-reductions}
If \(H=0\), \(S=1\), the proxy is the baseline outcome, and \(m_1(x)=x+\Delta_0\) with \(\Delta_0\) equal to the untreated two-period cohort-average mean change, then the functional in \texttt{lem:identification-fwl} equals the canonical finite-cohort two-period difference-in-differences estimand. If treatment effects are homogeneous across cohorts and there is no carry-over beyond \(h=0\), then \(\tau_n(z_0)\) reduces to the common contemporaneous finite-cohort average treatment effect on level wealth.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Under the first specialization, \(S=1\) and \(\sum_s\pi_s=1\) imply
\(\pi_1=1\).  The identifying equality in
\texttt{lem:identification-fwl} and the restriction
\(m_1(x)=x+\Delta_0\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau_n(z_0)
&=\mathbb E_P[\bar B_1^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid z_0,E_0]-m_1(z_{01})\\
&=\mathbb E_P[\bar B_1^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid z_0,E_0]-z_{01}-\Delta_0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
When the scalar proxy is the event cohort's baseline outcome, \(z_{01}\) is
its conditional baseline mean.  When \(\Delta_0\) is the untreated
cohort-average post-minus-baseline mean change, (8) is treated post minus
treated baseline minus the untreated post-minus-baseline change.  This is the
canonical conditional finite-cohort two-period difference-in-differences
contrast.

For the second specialization define the cohort effect
\[
\tau_s=\frac1{n_s}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}
\mathbb E_P[B_{si}(1)-B_{si}(0)\mid z_0,E_0].
\]
Homogeneity across cohorts means that \(\tau_s=\tau^{\mathrm{com}}\) for every
\(s\).  By \texttt{def:target} and \(\sum_s\pi_s=1\),
\[
\tau_n(z_0)=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s\tau_s
=\tau^{\mathrm{com}}\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s
=\tau^{\mathrm{com}}.
\tag{9}
\]
The stipulated absence of carry-over beyond \(h=0\) makes this common
terminal-level contrast the contemporaneous finite-cohort treatment effect on
the level-wealth path in \texttt{def:buyhold-level}.  This causal
interpretation uses the stated specialization; it does not follow from the
aggregation identity alone.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Named reductions anchor the new compounded-path object to familiar panel estimands. \textit{Gap.} The banked predecessor did not provide a canonical DID or homogeneous-panel reduction. \textit{Consumer.} Readers can audit the identifying functional against a standard two-period benchmark before using the general event-path result.

\begin{proposition}[prop:shared-design-replication-obstruction]\label{prop:shared-design-replication-obstruction}
There is an admissible fixed choice of class constants with \(H=0\), \(p=S=d=1\), \(\lambda=\kappa=1\), and the uniform order-zero kernel \(K\) for which the following assertions hold along \(n_1=N=T^2\).  For every \(T\), the original class \(\mathcal M_{T^2,T,(T^2)}(1)\) contains two laws whose conditional observed-data overlap mass is \(3/4\) and whose values of \(\tau_n(z_0)\) are respectively \(0\) and \(1/4\).  Consequently,
\[
\inf_{\widetilde\tau}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{T^2,T,(T^2)}(1)}
\mathbb E_P\!\left[
\{\widetilde\tau-\tau_n(z_0)\}^2\,\middle|\,E_0
\right]
\ge \frac{3}{256},
\]
where the infimum is over all observed-data-measurable estimators, although, for all sufficiently large \(T\),
\[
R_{\star,\beta}
=\left\{\frac{\log(T\vee2)}{T}\right\}^2\longrightarrow0.
\]
For every fixed \(\alpha\in(0,1/2)\), put \(\eta_\alpha=(1-2\alpha)/2\).  A second pair in the same original class has target values \(0\) and \(\eta_\alpha\) and conditional observed-data overlap mass \(1-\eta_\alpha>2\alpha\).  Hence
\[
\inf_{I:\,\inf_{P\in\mathcal M_{T^2,T,(T^2)}(1)}
P\{\tau_n(z_0)\in I\mid E_0\}\ge1-\alpha}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{T^2,T,(T^2)}(1)}
\mathbb E_P[\operatorname{length}(I)\mid E_0]
\ge \eta_\alpha\{1-\eta_\alpha-2\alpha\}
=\eta_\alpha^2>0,
\]
where the infimum is over observed-data-measurable intervals.  In each pair the nonzero-effect completion violates \(\texttt{ass:event-full-design-mean-stability}\), whereas both completions satisfy \(\texttt{ass:historical-full-design-centering}\).  Thus the event-side full-design restriction is rate-essential for the stated vanishing frontiers; this witness does not establish separate necessity of the historical restriction.

For the nonzero-effect law in the first pair, the particular objects in \(\texttt{def:feasible-estimator}\) and \(\texttt{def:honest-interval}\) retain the sharper corollaries
\[
R_\star=T^{-2},
\qquad
\lim_{T\to\infty}\mathbb E_P\!\left[
\{\widehat\tau-\tau_n(z_0)\}^2\,\middle|\,E_0
\right]=\frac{3}{16},
\]
and, for every fixed \(\alpha\in(0,1/2)\),
\[
P\{\tau_n(z_0)\in C_\alpha\mid E_0\}\longrightarrow0.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix
\[
H=0,
\qquad p=S=d=1,
\qquad \lambda=\kappa=1,
\qquad \pi_1=1.
\tag{1}
\]
Choose
\[
B=16,
\quad c_X=C_X=c_\Sigma=C_\Sigma=1,
\quad \delta_0=\frac14,
\quad c_{\mathrm{mod}}=C_{\mathrm{mod}}=1,
\tag{2}
\]
take any fixed \(\beta_0>0\) and \(\rho_0\in(0,1)\), choose \(L\) at least the \(\mathcal H_1(1,\cdot)\)-radius of the affine function introduced below, and let
\[
K(u)=\mathbf 1\{|u|\le 1\}.
\tag{3}
\]
This kernel is nonnegative, bounded, compactly supported, and equal to one on a neighborhood of zero.  Choose \(0<c_G\le 1\).  By the definitions of \(r\), \(b_r\), and \(e_0\),
\[
r=\lceil 1\rceil-1=0,
\qquad b_0(u)=e_0=1.
\tag{4}
\]
Let \(E_0\) be constant, set \(z_0=1/2\) and \(\mathcal Z_+=\{1/2\}\), and put \(n_1=N=T^2\).

We construct a pair for every \(\eta\in(0,1)\).  Write \(X_t=X_{1t}\), and let \(X_1,\ldots,X_T\) be independent uniform random variables on \([0,1]\).  Set
\[
m(x)=8+x-\frac12,
\qquad
Q_{1ti}=m(X_t)+\frac12U_{ti},
\tag{5}
\]
where the \(U_{ti}\) are mutually independent Rademacher random variables, independent of the complete design.  Since \(m([0,1])=[15/2,17/2]\),
\[
7\le Q_{1ti}\le 9\le B,
\qquad
\mathbb E[Q_{1ti}\mid X_t=x,E_0]=m(x).
\tag{6}
\]
Thus (6) verifies \(\texttt{ass:bounded-historical-wealth}\) and \(\texttt{ass:historical-regression}\).  The choice of \(L\) and the affine form in (5) verify \(\texttt{ass:holder-smoothness}\).  The density of every \(X_t\) is one on \([0,1]\), so \(\texttt{ass:design-density}\) holds.  Calendar independence gives, for every \(\ell_{\mathrm{mix}}\ge1\),
\[
\beta_P(\ell_{\mathrm{mix}}\mid E_0)=0
\le \beta_0\rho_0^{\ell_{\mathrm{mix}}},
\tag{7}
\]
which is \(\texttt{ass:temporal-mixing}\).  Finally set \(X_{1t}^{\mathrm{obs}}=X_t\), as required by \(\texttt{ass:exact-training-coordinates}\).

Independently of the historical sample, let
\[
Z_j=\frac12+V_j,
\tag{8}
\]
where \(V_1,\ldots,V_N\) are independent Rademacher random variables.  Equations (2) and (8) give
\[
\mathbb E[Z_j\mid E_0]=z_0,
\qquad
\Sigma=\operatorname{Var}(Z_j\mid E_0)=1,
\tag{9}
\]
and
\[
\mathbb E\!\left[
\left|\Sigma^{-1/2}(Z_j-z_0)\right|^4\,\middle|\,E_0
\right]=1\le B.
\tag{10}
\]
Thus (8)--(10) verify \(\texttt{ass:donor-replication}\), \(\texttt{ass:donor-ellipticity}\), and \(\texttt{ass:donor-fourth-moment}\); the stipulated independence verifies \(\texttt{ass:donor-independence}\).  Since \(D=Sd=1\), (2) also gives the strictly compatible envelope \(B>D^2\).

For \(0<\delta\le\delta_0\), every \(z_0+u\) with \(|u|\le\delta\) belongs to \([0,1]\).  From \(\Sigma=1\), \(\pi_1=1\), \(\texttt{def:local-modulus}\), and (5),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\omega_P(\delta;z_0)
&=\sup_{|u|\le\delta}|m(z_0+u)-m(z_0)|\\
&=\sup_{|u|\le\delta}|u|
=\delta.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
Equation (11) verifies both inequalities in \(\texttt{ass:local-two-sided-modulus}\).  Moreover,
\[
\operatorname{dist}(z_0,\partial[0,1])=\frac12\ge\delta_0,
\qquad
P\{z_0\in\mathcal Z_+\}=1\ge c_X,
\tag{12}
\]
so \(\texttt{ass:interior-event-path}\) and \(\texttt{ass:positive-query-region}\) hold.

Define
\[
\Gamma_\eta=\mathbf 1\{X_1\le\eta\}.
\tag{13}
\]
The two causal completions have the same historical and donor variables.  Under the zero-effect completion \(P_{0,\eta}\), set
\[
B_{1i}(0)=B_{1i}(1)=B_{1i}^{\mathrm{obs}}=8.
\tag{14}
\]
Under the nonzero-effect completion \(P_{1,\eta}\), set
\[
B_{1i}(0)=8,
\qquad
B_{1i}(1)=B_{1i}^{\mathrm{obs}}=8+\Gamma_\eta.
\tag{15}
\]
For both completions define \(B_{1i}(a,\mathbf a_{-1i})=B_{1i}(a)\) for every other-unit assignment and define \(Q_{1ti}(a)=Q_{1ti}\) for \(a\in\{0,1\}\).  Equations (14)--(15) and these extensions verify, respectively, \(\texttt{ass:consistency}\), \(\texttt{ass:historical-consistency}\), \(\texttt{ass:no-spillovers}\), and \(\texttt{ass:limited-anticipation}\).  They also give
\[
0\le B_{1i}(a)\le9<B,
\tag{16}
\]
which verifies \(\texttt{ass:bounded-event-wealth}\).  To realize \(\texttt{def:buyhold-level}\), take
\[
W_{1i,-1}=1,
\qquad
R_{1i0}(a)=B_{1i}(a)-1>-1.
\tag{17}
\]
Because \(H=0\), equation (17) gives \(W_{1i,-1}\{1+R_{1i0}(a)\}=B_{1i}(a)\).

The untreated event potential outcome is eight under both completions.  Since \(m(z_0)=8\),
\[
\frac1{n_1}\sum_{i=1}^{n_1}
\mathbb E_{P_{\ell,\eta}}[B_{1i}(0)\mid z_0,E_0]
=8=m(z_0),
\qquad \ell\in\{0,1\},
\tag{18}
\]
so \(\texttt{ass:event-transport}\) holds.  Conditional on the complete design and \(E_0\), the event outcomes in (14) or (15) are degenerate, while unit \(i\)'s historical path is a function only of the independent vector \((U_{ti})_{t=1}^{T}\).  Hence the complete outcome vectors are independent across \(i\) under this conditioning, which verifies \(\texttt{ass:cross-unit-independence}\).  Equations (5)--(18) therefore verify every member property in \(\texttt{def:panel-law-class}\), and
\[
P_{0,\eta},P_{1,\eta}
\in\mathcal M_{T^2,T,(T^2)}(1).
\tag{19}
\]

The construction also identifies which extra centering condition fails.  From \(\varepsilon_{1ti}=U_{ti}/2\) and independence of the Rademacher errors from the design,
\[
\mathbb E_{P_{\ell,\eta}}\!\left[
\varepsilon_{1ti}\,\middle|\,(X_t)_{t=1}^{T},E_0
\right]=0,
\qquad \ell\in\{0,1\},
\tag{20}
\]
so both laws satisfy \(\texttt{ass:historical-full-design-centering}\).  In contrast, under \(P_{1,\eta}\),
\[
\mathbb E[B_{1i}^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid(X_t)_{t=1}^{T},E_0]
=8+\Gamma_\eta,
\qquad
\mathbb E[B_{1i}^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid E_0]=8+\eta.
\tag{21}
\]
Because \(0<\eta<1\), \(\Gamma_\eta\ne\eta\) almost surely, and (21) violates \(\texttt{ass:event-full-design-mean-stability}\).  The zero-effect completion satisfies that condition because both conditional means equal eight.

We now separate the causal targets from the realized observed event means.  Since \(z_0\) and \(E_0\) are constant, \(\pi_1=1\), and \(P(X_1\le\eta)=\eta\), \(\texttt{def:target}\) and (14)--(15) give
\[
\tau_{0,\eta}:=\tau_n^{P_{0,\eta}}(z_0)=0,
\qquad
\tau_{1,\eta}:=\tau_n^{P_{1,\eta}}(z_0)
=\mathbb E[\Gamma_\eta]=\eta.
\tag{22}
\]
The realized event mean under \(P_{1,\eta}\) is instead \(8+\Gamma_\eta\), a single design-shared Bernoulli shock which replication over \(i\) does not average.

Let \(\mathcal O\) be the sigma-field generated by all observed variables, and let \(\nu_{\ell,\eta}\) be its conditional law given \(E_0\) under \(P_{\ell,\eta}\).  On
\[
A_\eta^c=\{X_1>\eta\},
\tag{23}
\]
the two observed-data maps coincide.  On \(A_\eta\), their event vectors are respectively the all-eight and all-nine vectors, and hence have disjoint supports.  Therefore the greatest measure dominated by both observed-data laws is their common restriction to (23), and its mass, the conditional observed-data overlap, is exactly
\[
\|\nu_{0,\eta}\wedge\nu_{1,\eta}\|
=P(A_\eta^c\mid E_0)=1-\eta.
\tag{24}
\]

Let \(\widetilde\tau\) be any observed-data-measurable estimator and put \(\mu_\eta=\nu_{0,\eta}\wedge\nu_{1,\eta}\).  Pointwise, for every \(x\in\mathbb R\),
\[
x^2+(x-\eta)^2
=2(x-\eta/2)^2+\frac{\eta^2}{2}
\ge\frac{\eta^2}{2}.
\tag{25}
\]
Since \(\mu_\eta\le\nu_{\ell,\eta}\), equations (22)--(25) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbb E_{P_{0,\eta}}[(\widetilde\tau-\tau_{0,\eta})^2\mid E_0]
+\mathbb E_{P_{1,\eta}}[(\widetilde\tau-\tau_{1,\eta})^2\mid E_0]\\
&\qquad\ge
\int\{\widetilde\tau^2+(\widetilde\tau-\eta)^2\}\,d\mu_\eta
\ge(1-\eta)\frac{\eta^2}{2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]
At least one summand in (26) is at least one half of its right-hand side.  By (19),
\[
\inf_{\widetilde\tau}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{T^2,T,(T^2)}(1)}
\mathbb E_P[\{\widetilde\tau-\tau_n(z_0)\}^2\mid E_0]
\ge\frac{(1-\eta)\eta^2}{4}.
\tag{27}
\]
Taking \(\eta=1/4\) in (27) yields \(3/256\).

For this array, \(\texttt{def:rate-frontier}\) gives
\[
R_{\mathrm{tr}}=T^{-2},
\qquad
R_{\mathrm{sm}}=(T^3)^{-2/3}=T^{-2},
\qquad
R_{\mathrm{don}}=T^{-2},
\tag{28}
\]
and
\[
R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}
=\left\{\frac{\log(T\vee2)}T\right\}^{2}.
\tag{29}
\]
Thus, for all sufficiently large \(T\),
\[
R_{\star,\beta}=R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}\longrightarrow0,
\tag{30}
\]
while the lower bound in (27) is fixed and positive.  This proves the universal risk obstruction over the original class.

For the interval obstruction, fix \(\alpha\in(0,1/2)\) and define
\[
\eta_\alpha=\frac{1-2\alpha}{2}.
\tag{31}
\]
Then \(\eta_\alpha\in(0,1/2)\) and
\[
1-\eta_\alpha-2\alpha=\eta_\alpha>0,
\qquad
1-\eta_\alpha>2\alpha.
\tag{32}
\]
Let \(I\) be an observed-data-measurable interval that is conditionally honest uniformly over the original class.  Applied to the pair with \(\eta=\eta_\alpha\), honesty and (22) give
\[
P_{0,\eta_\alpha}\{0\notin I\mid E_0\}\le\alpha,
\qquad
P_{1,\eta_\alpha}\{\eta_\alpha\notin I\mid E_0\}\le\alpha.
\tag{33}
\]
Using \(\mu_{\eta_\alpha}\le\nu_{0,\eta_\alpha}\), \(\mu_{\eta_\alpha}\le\nu_{1,\eta_\alpha}\), (24), (33), and subadditivity,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu_{\eta_\alpha}\{0,\eta_\alpha\in I\}
&\ge \mu_{\eta_\alpha}(\Omega)
-\mu_{\eta_\alpha}\{0\notin I\}
-\mu_{\eta_\alpha}\{\eta_\alpha\notin I\}\\
&\ge1-\eta_\alpha-2\alpha
=\eta_\alpha.
\end{aligned}
\tag{34}
\]
Whenever an interval contains both zero and \(\eta_\alpha\), its length is at least \(\eta_\alpha\).  Since \(\mu_{\eta_\alpha}\le\nu_{0,\eta_\alpha}\), (34) implies
\[
\mathbb E_{P_{0,\eta_\alpha}}[\operatorname{length}(I)\mid E_0]
\ge\int\operatorname{length}(I)\,d\mu_{\eta_\alpha}
\ge\eta_\alpha^2.
\tag{35}
\]
Taking the supremum over the original class and then the infimum over all uniformly honest intervals proves the asserted honest-length lower bound.  Equations (20)--(21) show precisely that this impossibility proves rate-essentiality of the event-side restriction, but not separate necessity of the historical restriction.

It remains to prove the estimator-specific corollaries for \(\eta=1/4\).  Work henceforth under \(P_{1,1/4}\), abbreviate \(\Gamma=\Gamma_{1/4}\), and note from (15) and (22) that
\[
\bar B_1^{\mathrm{obs}}=8+\Gamma,
\qquad
\tau_n(z_0)=\frac14.
\tag{36}
\]
The generated and projected centers in \(\texttt{def:feasible-estimator}\) satisfy
\[
\bar Z=\frac1N\sum_{j=1}^{N}Z_j,
\qquad
\widetilde z=\Pi_{[0,1]}(\bar Z).
\tag{37}
\]
Projection fixes \(z_0\) and is nonexpansive.  Using (8)--(9) and \(N=T^2\),
\[
|\widetilde z-z_0|
\le\left|\frac1N\sum_{j=1}^{N}V_j\right|,
\qquad
\mathbb E[(\widetilde z-z_0)^2\mid E_0]
\le N^{-1}=T^{-2}.
\tag{38}
\]
For all sufficiently large \(T\), the truncation at one in the beta-mixing initial bandwidth is inactive, and (28)--(29) imply
\[
\underline h_T
=C_h\left[T^{-1}\vee\frac{\log T}{T}\vee T^{-1}\right]
=C_h\frac{\log T}{T}.
\tag{39}
\]
Define
\[
I_T=\{t:|X_t-\widetilde z|\le\underline h_T\},
\qquad
\mathcal A_T=\{|I_T|>0\}.
\tag{40}
\]
For every \(\widetilde z\in[0,1]\), the intersection of \([\widetilde z-\underline h_T,\widetilde z+\underline h_T]\) with \([0,1]\) has Lebesgue measure at least \(\underline h_T\).  Donor independence and the independent uniform design therefore give
\[
P(\mathcal A_T^c\mid E_0,\widetilde z)
\le(1-\underline h_T)^T
\le e^{-T\underline h_T}
=T^{-C_h},
\tag{41}
\]
and hence \(P(\mathcal A_T^c\mid E_0)\to0\).

On \(\mathcal A_T\), the first candidate has
\[
L_1=n_1|I_T|,
\qquad
G_1=n_1|I_T|,
\qquad
\lambda_{\min}(G_1/L_1)=1\ge c_G.
\tag{42}
\]
Thus the selector accepts that candidate, and the local-constant fit is
\[
\widehat m_1
=\frac1{n_1|I_T|}
\sum_{t\in I_T}\sum_{i=1}^{n_1}Q_{1ti}.
\tag{43}
\]
The conditional-mean part of (43) differs from \(m(z_0)=8\) by at most
\[
\frac1{|I_T|}\sum_{t\in I_T}|m(X_t)-m(z_0)|
\le\underline h_T+|\widetilde z-z_0|.
\tag{44}
\]
Given the design and donors, the noise part has mean zero and variance
\[
\operatorname{Var}\!\left(
\frac1{2n_1|I_T|}\sum_{t\in I_T}\sum_{i=1}^{n_1}U_{ti}
\,\middle|\,(X_t)_{t=1}^{T},(Z_j)_{j=1}^{N},E_0
\right)
=\frac1{4n_1|I_T|}
\le\frac1{4n_1}.
\tag{45}
\]
Every successful local-constant intercept is an average of values in \([7,9]\), while the failed-fit rule sets it to zero.  Hence \((\widehat m_1-8)^2\le64\) on \(\mathcal A_T^c\).  Splitting over \(\mathcal A_T\), using \((x+y)^2\le2x^2+2y^2\), and applying (38), (39), (41), (44), and (45), there is a fixed \(C<\infty\) such that
\[
\mathbb E[(\widehat m_1-8)^2\mid E_0]
\le C\{\underline h_T^2+N^{-1}+n_1^{-1}
+P(\mathcal A_T^c\mid E_0)\}
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{46}
\]
Put \(D_T=\widehat m_1-8\).  By (36) and \(\texttt{def:feasible-estimator}\),
\[
\widehat\tau-\tau_n(z_0)=\Gamma-\frac14-D_T.
\tag{47}
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz and (46) yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\left|
\mathbb E[(\widehat\tau-\tau_n(z_0))^2\mid E_0]
-\mathbb E[(\Gamma-1/4)^2\mid E_0]
\right|\\
&\quad\le
2\{\mathbb E[(\Gamma-1/4)^2\mid E_0]\}^{1/2}
\{\mathbb E[D_T^2\mid E_0]\}^{1/2}
+\mathbb E[D_T^2\mid E_0]
\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{48}
\]
Since \(\Gamma\) is Bernoulli with success probability \(1/4\),
\[
\lim_{T\to\infty}
\mathbb E[(\widehat\tau-\tau_n(z_0))^2\mid E_0]
=\operatorname{Var}(\Gamma)
=\frac14\left(1-\frac14\right)
=\frac3{16}.
\tag{49}
\]
The no-log spacing term is \(R_{\mathrm{sp}}=T^{-2}\); together with (28), this gives \(R_\star=T^{-2}\).

Finally consider \(C_\alpha\).  On \(\mathcal A_T\), (42)--(43) give
\[
a_{1ti}=\frac{\mathbf1\{t\in I_T\}}{n_1|I_T|},
\qquad
\sum_t|a_{1ti}|=\frac1{n_1},
\qquad
\sum_{t,i}|a_{1ti}|=1.
\tag{50}
\]
For fixed \(\alpha\), substitution into \(\texttt{def:honest-interval}\) yields
\[
R_{\mathrm{hist}}
=B\left\{\frac12\log\left(\frac2{\alpha_{\mathrm{hist}}}\right)n_1^{-1}\right\}^{1/2}
=O(T^{-1}),
\tag{51}
\]
and
\[
R_{\mathrm{evt}}
=B\left\{\frac12\log\left(\frac2{\alpha_{\mathrm{evt}}}\right)n_1^{-1}\right\}^{1/2}
=O(T^{-1}).
\tag{52}
\]
The support of \(K\), (39), and (50) give
\[
A_L\le C_{0,1}L\underline h_T
=O\!\left(\frac{\log T}{T}\right).
\tag{53}
\]
Here \(D=1\), so the exact donor calibration satisfies
\[
M_4(N)=\frac{16}{N}+3\left(1-\frac1N\right)\le16,
\qquad
q_N=\{M_4(N)/\alpha_{\mathrm{don}}\}^{1/4}=O(1).
\tag{54}
\]
By (2), \(N=T^2\), and \(\kappa=1\),
\[
\delta_N=\frac{q_N}{\sqrt N}=O(T^{-1}),
\qquad
A_{\mathrm{don}}=\delta_N=O(T^{-1}).
\tag{55}
\]
Thus \(\delta_N\le\delta_0\) eventually.  On \(\mathcal A_T\), the selected Gram matrix is positive and all required quantities are finite.  Consequently the fallback event is contained in \(\mathcal A_T^c\) for all sufficiently large \(T\), and, throughout \(\mathcal A_T\),
\[
H_\alpha
=R_{\mathrm{hist}}+R_{\mathrm{evt}}+A_L+A_{\mathrm{don}}
=O\!\left(\frac{\log T}{T}\right)
=o(1).
\tag{56}
\]
In particular, \(H_\alpha<1/8\) eventually on \(\mathcal A_T\).

If the nonfallback interval contains \(\tau_n(z_0)\), then \(|\widehat\tau-\tau_n(z_0)|\le H_\alpha\).  Yet \(|\Gamma-1/4|\ge1/4\) surely, so (47) and the reverse triangle inequality imply on \(\{|D_T|<1/16\}\) that
\[
|\widehat\tau-\tau_n(z_0)|
\ge\frac14-\frac1{16}
=\frac3{16}>\frac18.
\tag{57}
\]
Equations (56)--(57) show that, eventually,
\[
\{\tau_n(z_0)\in C_\alpha\}
\subseteq \mathcal A_T^c\cup\{|D_T|\ge1/16\}.
\tag{58}
\]
Markov's inequality and (46) now give
\[
\begin{aligned}
P\{\tau_n(z_0)\in C_\alpha\mid E_0\}
&\le P(\mathcal A_T^c\mid E_0)
+P(|D_T|\ge1/16\mid E_0)\\
&\le P(\mathcal A_T^c\mid E_0)
+256\,\mathbb E[D_T^2\mid E_0]
\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{59}
\]
This proves both estimator-specific corollaries and completes the proof.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The two causal completions turn failure of one estimator into a universal observed-data lower bound over the original class. \textit{Gap.} The witness proves event-side rate-essentiality because historical full-design centering still holds; it does not establish separate necessity of the historical restriction. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies the maximality boundary for both the risk and honest-length frontiers and explains why their positive results are stated on the full-design-centered class.

\begin{lemma}[lem:full-data-local-polynomial-control]\label{lem:full-data-local-polynomial-control}
On every successful normalized-shape fit,
\[
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}|a_{sti}|
\le\{L_se_0^\top G_s^{-1}e_0\}^{1/2}
\le c_G^{-1/2}.
\]
Because the weights and covariates do not depend on the replicated-unit index,
\[
\sum_t|a_{sti}|\le\frac{c_G^{-1/2}}{n_s},
\qquad
\sum_{s=1}^{S}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}
\left[\pi_s\sum_t|a_{sti}|\right]^2
\le c_G^{-1}R_{\mathrm{tr}}.
\]
The exact Taylor bias is bounded by \(A_L\).  These conclusions are deterministic on successful fits and require no upper local-mass event.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a cohort \(s\) for which the normalized-shape fit succeeds.  Suppress the cohort subscript temporarily and put \(\mathsf L=L_s\), \(G=G_s\), and
\[
v_{ti}=w_{st}^{1/2}b_r(u_{st}).
\]
The kernel is nonnegative by \texttt{def:feasible-estimator}, so this square root is defined.  The definitions of \(G_s\), \(L_s\), and \(a_{sti}\) give
\[
G=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}v_{ti}v_{ti}^{\top},
\qquad
\mathsf L=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}w_{st},
\qquad
a_{sti}=w_{st}^{1/2}e_0^{\top}G^{-1}v_{ti}.
\tag{1}
\]
Success of the selector means both \(\mathsf L>0\) and
\[
G/\mathsf L\succeq c_G I_{q_r}.
\tag{2}
\]
In particular, \(G\) is invertible, as required in (1).  Applying Cauchy--Schwarz to (1), and then using the first identity in (1), yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}|a_{sti}|
&\le
\left\{\sum_{t,i}w_{st}\right\}^{1/2}
\left\{\sum_{t,i}
       (e_0^{\top}G^{-1}v_{ti})^2\right\}^{1/2}\\
&=\mathsf L^{1/2}
\{e_0^{\top}G^{-1}GG^{-1}e_0\}^{1/2}\\
&=\{\mathsf L e_0^{\top}G^{-1}e_0\}^{1/2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
By (2), inversion reverses the Loewner order and hence
\[
G^{-1}\preceq(c_G\mathsf L)^{-1}I_{q_r}.
\tag{4}
\]
The monomial vector \(b_r\) contains the constant monomial and every nonconstant monomial vanishes at zero.  Thus the definition \(e_0=b_r(0)\) gives \(\|e_0\|_2=1\).  Combining (3) and (4) proves
\[
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}|a_{sti}|
\le \{L_s e_0^{\top}G_s^{-1}e_0\}^{1/2}
\le c_G^{-1/2}.
\tag{5}
\]

For fixed \((s,t)\), the definitions of \(u_{st}\) and \(w_{st}\) do not involve \(i\).  Consequently
\[
G_s=n_s\sum_{t=1}^{T}w_{st}b_r(u_{st})b_r(u_{st})^{\top},
\]
and the definition of \(a_{sti}\) shows that \(a_{sti}\) is constant in \(i\).  Therefore (5) implies, for every \(i\in\{1,\ldots,n_s\}\),
\[
\sum_{t=1}^{T}|a_{sti}|
=\frac1{n_s}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i'=1}^{n_s}|a_{sti'}|
\le \frac{c_G^{-1/2}}{n_s}.
\tag{6}
\]
Using (6), the positivity of the cohort weights, and the definition of \(R_{\mathrm{tr}}\) in \texttt{def:rate-frontier}, we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{s=1}^{S}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}
\left[\pi_s\sum_{t=1}^{T}|a_{sti}|\right]^2
&\le \sum_{s=1}^{S}n_s
\left(\frac{\pi_s c_G^{-1/2}}{n_s}\right)^2\\
&=c_G^{-1}\sum_{s=1}^{S}\frac{\pi_s^2}{n_s}
=c_G^{-1}R_{\mathrm{tr}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]

It remains to establish the Taylor-bias assertion.  If \(p(u)=c^{\top}b_r(u)\) is any polynomial of total degree at most \(r\), then the definitions of \(a_{sti}\) and \(G_s\) imply the exact reproduction identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}a_{sti}p(u_{st})
&=e_0^{\top}G_s^{-1}
  \sum_{t,i}w_{st}b_r(u_{st})b_r(u_{st})^{\top}c\\
&=e_0^{\top}G_s^{-1}G_sc
=e_0^{\top}c
=p(0).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
For each cohort, let \(P_s\) be the degree-\(r\) Taylor polynomial of \(m_s\) about \(\widetilde z_s\).  The function \(u\mapsto P_s(\widetilde z_s+h_su)\) has total degree at most \(r\), so (8) gives
\[
\sum_{t,i}a_{sti}P_s(X_{st})=P_s(\widetilde z_s)=m_s(\widetilde z_s).
\tag{9}
\]
Under \texttt{ass:holder-smoothness}, with \(r=\lceil\lambda\rceil-1\), the declared H\"older-ball Taylor remainder convention gives, for \(x,\widetilde z_s\in[0,1]^d\),
\[
|m_s(x)-P_s(x)|
\le C_{r,d}L\|x-\widetilde z_s\|_2^{\lambda}.
\tag{10}
\]
The cube projection in \texttt{def:feasible-estimator} ensures \(\widetilde z_s\in[0,1]^d\), while the support declaration gives \(X_{st}\in[0,1]^d\), so (10) applies to every summand.  From (9)--(10) and the triangle inequality,
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\left|
\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s
\left\{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}
a_{sti}m_s(X_{st})-m_s(\widetilde z_s)\right\}
\right|\\
&\qquad\le
C_{r,d}L\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}|a_{sti}|
\|X_{st}-\widetilde z_s\|_2^{\lambda}
=A_L,
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
where the last equality is the definition in \texttt{def:honest-interval}.  Equations (5), (6), (7), and (11) establish all claimed conclusions.  The argument used only the lower normalized-shape condition (2); at no point was an upper bound on \(L_s\) imposed.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The normalized Gram-shape rule yields the exact deterministic cluster-weight bound for the single fit per cohort. \textit{Gap.} No fold averaging or upper local-mass event is used. \textit{Consumer.} Historical noise is controlled at the replicated-unit rate even when nearby calendar sites are sparse.

\begin{lemma}[lem:geometric-mixing-selector-control]\label{lem:geometric-mixing-selector-control}
Let \(h_s\) be selected by the normalized-shape rule in \texttt{def:feasible-estimator}.  For a sufficiently large fixed initial-scale constant and some fixed \(A>2\lambda/d+2\), uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger}(\lambda)\), every cohort and every integer \(v\ge0\) with \(2^vh_{s,\beta}\le1\) satisfy
\[
P\{h_s>2^vh_{s,\beta}\mid E_0,\widetilde z_s\}
\le C\exp(-c2^{vd})+CT^{-A}.
\]
Consequently, for every fixed \(a>0\),
\[
\mathbb E_P[h_s^a\mathbf1_{\mathrm{good}}\mid E_0,\widetilde z_s]
\le C\left\{(Tn_s)^{-a/(2\lambda+d)}
+\left(\frac{\log(T\vee2)}T\right)^{a/d}
+N^{-a/2}\right\}.
\]
The terminal-grid failure probability is at most \(C\exp\{-cT/\log(T\vee2)\}+CT^{-A}\), and the total bad-sample probability in \texttt{def:honest-interval} is at most \(CR_{\star,\beta}\) along the stated arrays.  These bounds remain valid when the uninflated smoothing-scale local count \(T\{(Tn_s)^{-1/(2\lambda+d)}\}^d\) is \(O(1)\); the selected log-adjusted initial scale itself satisfies \(Th_{s,\beta}^d\ge c\log(T\vee2)\).  No exogenous dense-local-site or upper-local-mass condition is imposed.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a cohort \(s\) and condition throughout on \((E_0,\widetilde z_s=z)\).  By \texttt{ass:donor-independence}, adjoining \(\widetilde z_s\), which is measurable with respect to the donor sample and \(E_0\), does not change the conditional historical-design law.  Assumption \texttt{ass:temporal-mixing} applies to the stacked design process; no outcome process is used in this proof.

For \(z\in[0,1]^d\) and \(0<h\leq1\), define
\[
\ell_{z,h}(x)=K((x-z)/h),
\qquad
\Phi_{z,h}(x)=K((x-z)/h)b_r((x-z)/h)b_r((x-z)/h)^\top.
\]
Bounded support of \(K\), the upper density bound, and the substitution \(x=z+hu\) give a fixed \(C_0<\infty\) such that
\[
\mathbb E_P[\ell_{z,h}(X_{st})\mid E_0]\leq C_0h^d.
\tag{56}
\]
There is a uniform lower bound as well.  Choose \(\eta,k_0>0\) such that \(K(u)\geq k_0\) on \([-\eta,\eta]^d\).  For every cube point \(z\) and \(h\leq1\), the set
\(h^{-1}([0,1]^d-z)\cap[-\eta,\eta]^d\) contains one of finitely many orthant boxes whose side lengths are bounded below.  On each such box a nonzero polynomial \(v^\top b_r(u)\) cannot vanish almost everywhere.  Continuity in \(v\), compactness of the unit sphere in the fixed-dimensional space \(\mathbb R^{q_r}\), and the lower density bound therefore give \(c_0>0\) such that, uniformly in \(s,t,z,h\),
\[
c_0h^d\leq\mathbb E_P[\ell_{z,h}(X_{st})\mid E_0],
\qquad
\mathbb E_P[v^\top\Phi_{z,h}(X_{st})v\mid E_0]
\geq c_0h^d\|v\|_2^2.
\tag{57}
\]
Choose the fixed procedural threshold so that \(0<c_G<c_0/(4C_0)\).

Fix \(A_0>2\lambda/d+2\) and put
\[
b_T=\lceil C_\beta\log(T\vee2)\rceil.
\]
The fixed constant \(C_\beta\) can be chosen so that geometric mixing implies
\[
T\beta_0\rho_0^{b_T}\leq T^{-A_0}.
\tag{58}
\]
Partition \(\{1,\ldots,T\}\) into consecutive blocks of length at most \(b_T\), and split the blocks into odd and even subfamilies.  Within either subfamily, successive blocks are separated by one whole block.  By the definition of absolute regularity, replacing the conditional law of the next separated block by its marginal changes the joint law in total variation by at most \(\beta_P(b_T\mid E_0)\).  Successive replacement and the triangle inequality therefore show that either parity subfamily is within
\[
C(T/b_T)\beta_P(b_T\mid E_0)\leq CT^{-A_0}
\tag{59}
\]
in total variation of the product of its conditional block marginals.

Let \(F\) be either \(\ell_{z,h}\) or
\(v^\top\Phi_{z,h}v\) with \(\|v\|_2=1\), and let \(W_g\) be the sum of \(F(X_{st})\) over block \(g\) in one parity.  Boundedness of \(K\) and of \(b_r\) on the support of \(K\) gives
\[
0\leq W_g\leq Cb_T,
\qquad
W_g^2\leq Cb_TW_g.
\]
Under the product law in (59), the \(W_g\)'s are independent, and (56)--(57) imply
\[
\sum_g\operatorname{Var}(W_g)
\leq\sum_g\mathbb E[W_g^2]
\leq Cb_TTh^d.
\tag{60}
\]
For \(V_g=W_g-\mathbb EW_g\), expansion of the exponential series and \(|V_g|\leq Cb_T\) imply, whenever \(0<\theta<(Cb_T)^{-1}\),
\[
\mathbb Ee^{\theta V_g}
\leq\exp\!\left\{
\frac{\theta^2\operatorname{Var}(W_g)}{2(1-C\theta b_T)}
\right\}.
\tag{61}
\]
Multiplication of (61), exponential Markov inequality, and optimization in \(\theta\) show that a deviation by a sufficiently small fixed multiple of \(Th^d\) has probability at most
\[
C\exp\!\left(-c\frac{Th^d}{b_T}\right)
\tag{62}
\]
under the product law.  Adding the two parity bounds and the total-variation error (59) gives (62) plus \(CT^{-A_0}\) for the original calendar law.

For the Gram matrix, apply this bound to \(v^\top\Phi_{z,h}v\) on a fixed \(1/4\)-net \(\mathcal V\) of the unit sphere.  Its cardinality is fixed because \(q_r\) is fixed.  If \(A\) is symmetric, approximation of a maximizing unit eigenvector gives
\[
\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\leq2\max_{v\in\mathcal V}|v^\top Av|.
\tag{63}
\]
Equations (56)--(63) imply that, outside an event of conditional probability at most
\[
C\exp\!\left(-c\frac{Th^d}{b_T}\right)+CT^{-A_0},
\tag{64}
\]
the calendar mass is at most \(2C_0Th^d\), while the calendar Gram matrix is at least \((c_0/2)Th^dI_{q_r}\).  Replication multiplies both quantities by \(n_s\):
\[
L_s(h)=n_s\sum_{t=1}^{T}\ell_{z,h}(X_{st}),
\qquad
G_s(h)=n_s\sum_{t=1}^{T}\Phi_{z,h}(X_{st}).
\tag{65}
\]
Hence \(L_s(h)>0\) and
\[
\lambda_{\min}\{G_s(h)/L_s(h)\}
\geq c_0/(4C_0)>c_G
\]
outside (64).  Thus
\[
P\!\left\{L_s(h)=0\ \text{or}\ 
\lambda_{\min}\{G_s(h)/L_s(h)\}<c_G
\,\middle|\,E_0,\widetilde z_s\right\}
\leq C\exp\!\left(-c\frac{Th^d}{b_T}\right)+CT^{-A_0}.
\tag{66}
\]
The finitely many small values of \(T\) are absorbed by increasing \(C\).  Notice that no response and no upper-local-mass event occurs in (66).

Write \(h_0=h_{s,\beta}\).  If \(h_0<1\), its definition and a sufficiently large fixed \(C_h\) give, for every dyadic candidate \(2^vh_0\leq1\),
\[
\frac{T(2^vh_0)^d}{b_T}\geq c2^{vd}.
\tag{67}
\]
If selection has not stopped by that candidate, that candidate failed.  Substitution of (67) into (66) yields
\[
P\{h_s>2^vh_{s,\beta}\mid E_0,\widetilde z_s\}
\leq C\exp(-c2^{vd})+CT^{-A_0}.
\tag{68}
\]
When \(h_0=1\), the assertion is immediate.  At the terminal candidate \(h=1\), (66) gives
\[
P\{\text{terminal-grid failure}\mid E_0,\widetilde z_s\}
\leq C\exp\{-cT/\log(T\vee2)\}+CT^{-A_0}.
\tag{69}
\]

Fix \(a>0\).  For this moment calculation, repeat the total-variation comparison with a larger fixed block multiplier so its remainder is \(T^{-A_a}\) for some \(A_a>a/d+2\).  This is only a proof coupling and does not alter the estimator or its grid.  The exponential dyadic tail remains summable.  Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_P[h_s^a\mathbf1_{\mathrm{good}}\mid E_0,\widetilde z_s]
&\leq h_0^a+
\sum_{v:\,2^vh_0<1}(2^{v+1}h_0)^a
P\{h_s>2^vh_0\mid E_0,\widetilde z_s\}\\
&\leq Ch_0^a+CT^{-A_a}\\
&\leq C\left\{(Tn_s)^{-a/(2\lambda+d)}
+\left(\frac{\log(T\vee2)}T\right)^{a/d}
+N^{-a/2}\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{70}
\]
Here \(\sum_{v\geq0}2^{av}e^{-c2^{vd}}<\infty\), the grid is capped at one, and \(h_0\geq c\{\log(T\vee2)/T\}^{1/d}\) absorbs \(T^{-A_a}\).

A union bound over fixed \(S\) in (69) controls all selector failures.  The donor fourth moment makes \(\bar Z\) and its projection finite almost surely; a successful normalized Gram matrix is invertible; and all displayed deterministic calibration quantities are finite.  Since fixed \(D\) and \(\alpha_{\mathrm{don}}>0\) imply \(q_N\leq C\), one has \(\delta_N\leq CN^{-1/2}\), and hence \(\delta_N\leq\delta_0\) eventually.  Along the asserted arrays,
\[
e^{-cT/\log(T\vee2)}+T^{-A_0}
\leq C\left\{\frac{\log(T\vee2)}T\right\}^{2\lambda/d}
=CR_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}.
\tag{71}
\]
Consequently the total bad-sample probability is at most \(CR_{\star,\beta}\).  Finally, the initial-scale definition directly gives
\[
T\{(Tn_s)^{-1/(2\lambda+d)}\}^d\ \text{may be }O(1),
\qquad
Th_{s,\beta}^d\geq c\log(T\vee2).
\tag{72}
\]
The second relation, rather than any dense-local-site condition, supplies (67) and completes the proof.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Alternating calendar-block coupling gives the maintained log-adjusted selector bound; the direct independent-calendar companion removes the block length. \textit{Gap.} The beta-mixing result remains unchanged and requires no dense-site or upper-mass premise. \textit{Consumer.} The two dependence-specific estimators use the selector lemma appropriate to their class.

\begin{lemma}[lem:geometric-mixing-spacing-obstruction]\label{lem:geometric-mixing-spacing-obstruction}
Put \(D=Sd\).  For every fixed nonempty class with a compatible donor envelope \(B\ge D^2\), fixed \(d\), and fixed \(\lambda\), there are pairs
\[
(P_{0,T},P_{1,T})\subset
\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger}(\lambda)
\]
with the declared marginal design-density bounds and geometric conditional beta-mixing envelope such that
\[
\inf_{N,T,\mathbf n}\int\min\{dP_{0,T},dP_{1,T}\}>0,
\qquad
|\tau_{n,P_{1,T}}(z_0)-\tau_{n,P_{0,T}}(z_0)|^2
\ge cR_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}.
\]
The event means are stable given the complete historical design, the historical errors are centered given that design, and both laws satisfy the maintained two-sided local modulus.  The donor marginal is common to the two laws and may be chosen so that \(\Sigma=I_D\) and the standardized fourth moment is exactly \(D^2\), including when \(B=D^2\).  Consequently the spacing contribution to conditional minimax mean squared error is at least \(cR_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}\), and every uniformly conditionally \((1-\alpha)\)-honest interval has worst-case expected length at least \(c_\alpha R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}^{1/2}\).  Hence the logarithm in the uniform geometric-mixing spacing term is unavoidable without an additional anti-clustering restriction.  When \(B<D^2\), the class is empty and no substantive minimax assertion is made.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(D=Sd\).  For a member of the class define
\[
Y=\Sigma^{-1/2}(Z_1-z_0).
\]
The definitions of \(z_0\) and \(\Sigma\), ellipticity, conditional Jensen's inequality, and \texttt{ass:donor-fourth-moment} give
\[
D^2
=\{\mathbb E_P[\|Y\|_2^2\mid E_0]\}^2
\leq\mathbb E_P[\|Y\|_2^4\mid E_0]
\leq B.
\tag{73}
\]
Thus the class is empty if \(B<D^2\).  Fix \(B\geq D^2\), an admissible interior query, and one cohort \(s_*\) of fixed positive weight; keep the remaining cohorts common across the two laws.

For small fixed \(c_p,c_L>0\), set
\[
L_T=\lfloor c_L\log(T\vee2)\rfloor\vee1,
\qquad
p_T=c_p\frac{L_T}{T},
\]
where \(c_L|\log\rho_0|<1\).  Since \(p_T\asymp\log(T)/T\), while
\(\rho_0^{L_T}\asymp T^{-c_L|\log\rho_0|}\), decreasing \(c_p\), if necessary, gives
\[
2p_T\leq\beta_0\rho_0^{L_T-1}
\tag{74}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(T\).  Let \(A_T\) be a Euclidean ball centered at \(z_{0s_*}\), contained in the cube, and having Lebesgue measure \(p_T\).  The interior margin permits this choice for large \(T\); its radius obeys
\[
h_T\asymp p_T^{1/d}
\asymp\left\{\frac{\log(T\vee2)}T\right\}^{1/d}.
\tag{75}
\]

Partition the calendar into deterministic consecutive blocks of length \(L_T\), except possibly the last.  Independently across blocks draw \(G_b\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p_T)\).  Conditional on \(G_b=1\), draw the selected cohort's points in block \(b\) independently and uniformly on \(A_T\); conditional on \(G_b=0\), draw them independently and uniformly on its complement.  At each date the marginal density is
\[
p_T\frac{\mathbf1_{A_T}(x)}{p_T}
+(1-p_T)\frac{\mathbf1_{A_T^c}(x)}{1-p_T}=1.
\tag{76}
\]
Other cohorts may have independent uniform designs, so \texttt{ass:design-density} holds.  At lags at least \(L_T\), the relevant sigma-fields involve independent block variables.  At a smaller lag, only the block crossing the separating cut can be shared.  Conditional on its Bernoulli variable, the two sides are independent, and direct expansion of the two-component mixture bounds its absolute-regularity distance from the product law by \(2p_T(1-p_T)\leq2p_T\).  Hence (74) gives
\[
\beta_P(\ell_{\mathrm{mix}}\mid E_0)
\leq
\begin{cases}
2p_T,&1\leq\ell_{\mathrm{mix}}<L_T,\\
0,&\ell_{\mathrm{mix}}\geq L_T,
\end{cases}
\leq\beta_0\rho_0^{\ell_{\mathrm{mix}}}.
\tag{77}
\]

Choose a bounded H\"older carrier \(g\) in the interior of the response range.  Give its weighted cohort surface a nonzero affine component at \(z_0\) when \(\lambda>1\), and an asymmetric \(\lambda\)-cusp there when \(0<\lambda\leq1\); scale it to satisfy the fixed H\"older and two-sided-modulus envelopes.  Let \(\psi\) be a fixed smooth bump supported on the unit ball with \(\psi(0)=1\), and define in cohort \(s_*\)
\[
m_0(x)=g(x),
\qquad
m_1(x)=g(x)+c_bh_T^\lambda
\psi\!\left(\frac{x-z_{0s_*}}{h_T}\right).
\tag{78}
\]
For small fixed \(c_b\), both surfaces remain in \(\mathcal H_d(\lambda,L)\) and in the response interior.  If \(\lambda>1\), the bump increment over a displacement of norm \(\delta\) is at most
\(Cc_bh_T^{\lambda-1}\delta\) for \(\delta\leq h_T\), and at most
\(Cc_bh_T^\lambda\leq Cc_bh_T^{\lambda-1}\delta\) for \(\delta>h_T\); it cannot cancel the fixed affine lower modulus when \(c_b\) is small.  If \(0<\lambda\leq1\), its increment is at most \(Cc_b\delta^\lambda\), so it cannot cancel the carrier cusp.  The H\"older inequality and covariance eigenvalue bounds supply the upper standardized modulus.  Both laws therefore satisfy \texttt{ass:local-two-sided-modulus} with \(\kappa=\min\{\lambda,1\}\).

Conditional on the complete design \(\mathscr X\), draw bounded historical response paths independently across units from kernels with mean \(m_\ell(X_{st})\).  Draw bounded event outcomes independently of \(\mathscr X\), with a common observed mean under the two laws, and complete untreated event potential outcomes so their cohort-average conditional means are \(m_\ell(z_{0s})\).  Then
\[
\mathbb E[B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid\mathscr X,E_0]
=\mathbb E[B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid E_0],
\qquad
\mathbb E[\varepsilon_{sti}\mid\mathscr X,E_0]=0.
\tag{79}
\]
Under both laws use independent donors
\[
Z_j=z_0+U_j,
\qquad
U_j\sim\operatorname{Unif}\{\pm\sqrt D\,e_k:1\leq k\leq D\},
\]
independent of the panel.  Symmetry gives
\[
\mathbb E[U_j\mid E_0]=0,
\quad
\mathbb E[U_jU_j^\top\mid E_0]=I_D,
\quad
\mathbb E[\|U_j\|_2^4\mid E_0]=D^2\leq B.
\tag{80}
\]
Thus \(\Sigma=I_D\), including at \(B=D^2\), and donors carry no information about the law index.  Set exact training coordinates and complete the potential outcomes using consistency, limited anticipation, no spillovers, and the buy-and-hold representation exactly as in \texttt{def:panel-law-class}.  Equations (76)--(80) verify every member property of \(\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger}(\lambda)\), including both full-design restrictions.

Let \(\mathcal A_T\) be the event that no calendar block is active.  The number of blocks is \(T/L_T+O(1)\), so
\[
P(\mathcal A_T\mid E_0)
=(1-p_T)^{T/L_T+O(1)}\longrightarrow e^{-c_p}>0.
\tag{81}
\]
On \(\mathcal A_T\), all selected-cohort design points lie outside \(A_T\), where the two surfaces agree.  Every other observed kernel is common.  Hence the complete observed-data laws coincide on this event and
\[
\int\min\{dP_{0,T},dP_{1,T}\}
\geq P(\mathcal A_T\mid E_0)\geq c>0.
\tag{82}
\]
The observed event mean is common, whereas event transport changes the selected cohort's untreated event mean by \(c_bh_T^\lambda\).  The identification equality in \texttt{lem:identification-fwl}, the positive weight \(\pi_{s_*}\), and (75) imply
\[
|\tau_{n,P_{1,T}}(z_0)-\tau_{n,P_{0,T}}(z_0)|
\geq ch_T^\lambda
\asymp\left\{\frac{\log(T\vee2)}T\right\}^{\lambda/d}.
\tag{83}
\]
For the finitely many \(T\) preceding (74), use a fixed interior ball and two sufficiently close bounded kernels of positive affinity and fixed nonzero target separation; reducing the common constant makes (82)--(83) valid for every positive \(T\).

Let the targets be \(\tau_0,\tau_1\) and put \(\Delta=|\tau_1-\tau_0|\).  For any estimator \(\widehat\tau\), the triangle inequality gives
\[
\mathbf1\{|\widehat\tau-\tau_0|\geq\Delta/2\}
+\mathbf1\{|\widehat\tau-\tau_1|\geq\Delta/2\}\geq1.
\]
Integrating against the overlap measure in (82) gives
\[
\max_{\ell\in\{0,1\}}
\mathbb E_{P_{\ell,T}}[(\widehat\tau-\tau_\ell)^2\mid E_0]
\geq\frac{\Delta^2}{8}\int\min\{dP_{0,T},dP_{1,T}\}
\geq cR_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}.
\tag{84}
\]
This holds for every estimator and proves the conditional minimax lower bound.

Finally, decrease \(c_p\), if necessary, so the affinity in (82) exceeds \(2\alpha+a_0\) for some fixed \(a_0>0\).  For any uniformly conditionally \((1-\alpha)\)-honest interval \(C\),
\[
\int\mathbf1\{\tau_0\in C,\ \tau_1\in C\}
\min\{dP_{0,T},dP_{1,T}\}
\geq\int\min\{dP_{0,T},dP_{1,T}\}-2\alpha
\geq a_0.
\]
On this event \(\operatorname{length}(C)\geq\Delta\), so the worst-case conditional expected length is at least
\(a_0\Delta\geq c_\alpha R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}^{1/2}\).  The query was chosen in \(\mathcal Z_+\) and is deterministic conditional on \(E_0\), so its conditional probability is one, at least \(c_X\).  This proves every assertion for all compatible envelopes without an anti-clustering premise.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The explicit rare-block design array certifies the uniform logarithm for every compatible envelope, including \(B=D^2\). \textit{Gap.} Marginal density and geometric design mixing allow local states to occur in blocks of logarithmic length; the common donor marginal has covariance \(I_D\) and fourth moment \(D^2\). \textit{Consumer.} The iid spacing expression remains only a stricter-design comparator, and the boundary converse retains the spacing channel.

\begin{lemma}[lem:independent-calendar-selector-control]\label{lem:independent-calendar-selector-control}
Let \(h_s^{\mathrm{iid}}\) be selected from the dyadic grid starting at \(h_{s,\mathrm{iid}}\) in \texttt{def:feasible-estimator}.  For a sufficiently large fixed \(C_h\), uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger,\mathrm{iid}}(\lambda)\), every cohort and every integer \(v\ge0\) with \(2^vh_{s,\mathrm{iid}}\le1\) satisfy
\[
P\{h_s^{\mathrm{iid}}>2^vh_{s,\mathrm{iid}}\mid E_0,\widetilde z_s\}
\le C\exp(-c2^{vd}).
\]
For every fixed \(a>0\),
\[
\mathbb E_P[(h_s^{\mathrm{iid}})^a\mathbf1_{\mathrm{good,iid}}\mid E_0,\widetilde z_s]
\le C\{(Tn_s)^{-a/(2\lambda+d)}+T^{-a/d}+N^{-a/2}\}.
\]
The terminal-grid failure probability is at most \(C\exp(-cT)\), and the iid bad-sample probability is at most \(CR_\star\) along arrays with \(N\wedge T\wedge\min_s n_s\to\infty\).  No logarithmic block length, dense-local-site condition, or upper local-mass condition is used.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a cohort \(s\) and condition on \((E_0,\widetilde z_s=z)\).  Donor independence preserves the conditional historical-design law, and the definition of \(\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger,\mathrm{iid}}(\lambda)\) makes the stacked calendar vectors independent over \(t\).  For
\[
\ell_{z,h}(x)=K((x-z)/h),
\qquad
\Phi_{z,h}(x)=K((x-z)/h)b_r((x-z)/h)b_r((x-z)/h)^\top,
\]
the density, kernel, cube-geometry, and polynomial arguments in \texttt{lem:geometric-mixing-selector-control} give fixed \(0<c_0<C_0<\infty\) such that
\[
c_0h^d\leq\mathbb E[\ell_{z,h}(X_{st})\mid E_0]
\leq C_0h^d,
\qquad
\mathbb E[v^\top\Phi_{z,h}(X_{st})v\mid E_0]
\geq c_0h^d
\tag{85}
\]
uniformly over unit vectors \(v\).  Each summand is bounded by a fixed constant and has variance at most \(Ch^d\).

If \(V\) is a centered summand, expansion of its exponential series and boundedness give, for sufficiently small \(|\theta|\),
\[
\mathbb Ee^{\theta V}
\leq\exp\!\left\{
\frac{\theta^2\operatorname{Var}(V)}{2(1-C|\theta|)}
\right\}.
\tag{86}
\]
Multiplying (86) over the independent calendar indices, using exponential Markov inequality, and optimizing in \(\theta\) imply, for \(F=\ell_{z,h}\) and for each fixed quadratic form of \(\Phi_{z,h}\),
\[
P\!\left\{
\left|\sum_{t=1}^{T}\{F(X_{st})-\mathbb EF(X_{st})\}\right|>cTh^d
\,\middle|\,E_0,z\right\}
\leq C\exp(-cTh^d).
\tag{87}
\]
A fixed \(1/4\)-net of the unit sphere and
\(\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq2\max_v|v^\top Av|\) turn (87) into simultaneous mass and Gram bounds.  Replication multiplies both \(G_s\) and \(L_s\) by \(n_s\), so it cancels from their normalized ratio.  With the fixed threshold \(c_G<c_0/(4C_0)\),
\[
P\{\text{candidate }h\text{ fails}\mid E_0,z\}
\leq C\exp(-cTh^d).
\tag{88}
\]

Put \(h_0=h_{s,\mathrm{iid}}\).  The term \(T^{-1/d}\) in its definition ensures
\[
T(2^vh_0)^d\geq c2^{vd}
\tag{89}
\]
for every dyadic candidate \(2^vh_0\leq1\).  If the selector has not stopped by that candidate, the candidate has failed.  Equations (88)--(89) therefore give
\[
P\{h_s^{\mathrm{iid}}>2^vh_{s,\mathrm{iid}}\mid E_0,\widetilde z_s\}
\leq C\exp(-c2^{vd}).
\tag{90}
\]
At \(h=1\), (88) gives terminal failure probability at most \(C\exp(-cT)\).

For every fixed \(a>0\), tail summation using
\(\sum_{v\geq0}2^{av}e^{-c2^{vd}}<\infty\) yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[(h_s^{\mathrm{iid}})^a\mathbf1_{\mathrm{good,iid}}
\mid E_0,\widetilde z_s]
&\leq Ch_0^a\\
&\leq C\{(Tn_s)^{-a/(2\lambda+d)}+T^{-a/d}+N^{-a/2}\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{91}
\]
A union bound over fixed \(S\) controls all cohort failures.  Also
\(e^{-cT}\leq CT^{-2\lambda/d}\), while the donor moment bound makes \(\delta_N\leq CN^{-1/2}\), so the deterministic condition \(\delta_N\leq\delta_0\) holds eventually.  Along every array in the statement, the entire iid bad-sample probability is therefore at most
\[
C\{T^{-2\lambda/d}\vee N^{-\kappa}\}\leq CR_\star.
\tag{92}
\]
No block length, dense-site condition, or upper local-mass condition was used, proving the lemma.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Direct bounded concentration over independent calendar vectors gives the no-log dyadic tail and bandwidth moments. \textit{Gap.} It applies only to the named conditional independent-calendar subclass. \textit{Consumer.} Both iid upper-rate corollaries use it.

\begin{lemma}[lem:iid-joint-law-embedding]\label{lem:iid-joint-law-embedding}
Put \(D=Sd\).  For every fixed nonempty independent-calendar class with \(B>D^2\), each of the four rates \(R_{\mathrm{tr}},R_{\mathrm{sm}},R_{\mathrm{sp}},R_{\mathrm{don}}\) has a two-law subexperiment contained in \(\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger,\mathrm{iid}}(\lambda)\), with conditional testing affinity uniformly bounded away from zero and squared target separation bounded below by a fixed positive multiple of that rate.  The treated, smoothing, and donor pairs may be chosen with bounded Kullback--Leibler divergence.  The iid spacing pair uses independent uniform calendar designs and a query neighborhood of volume proportional to \(T^{-1}\); its no-hit event has probability bounded away from zero and its target separation is of order \(T^{-\lambda/d}\).  At \(B=D^2\), the same assertions hold only for \(R_{\mathrm{tr}},R_{\mathrm{sm}},R_{\mathrm{sp}}\), using a common donor marginal; no universal donor converse is asserted.  If \(B<D^2\), the class is empty.  Every pair is completed as a regular conditional law of the complete causal panel and obeys both full-design centering restrictions.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The compatibility calculation in \texttt{lem:joint-law-embedding} proves that \(B<D^2\) makes the class empty.  That lemma also constructs bounded causal response kernels, admissible surfaces, event completions, and donor laws for the treated, smoothing, and donor perturbations.  In each of those three constructions, choose the stacked historical calendar vectors independently with uniform density on \([0,1]^{Sd}\).  This density satisfies \(c_X\leq1\leq C_X\), has conditional beta-mixing coefficients zero, and is conditionally independent over calendar dates.  Draw historical responses conditionally on the complete design, independently across units, with means \(m_s(X_{st})\), and draw event outcomes independently of the design.  Hence both full-design centering restrictions continue to hold.

The treated and donor likelihood ratios do not involve the historical design.  For the smoothing bump of radius
\[
b=(Tn_s)^{-1/(2\lambda+d)},
\]
the uniform design has hit probability \(O(b^d)\), so the binary likelihood calculation in \texttt{lem:joint-law-embedding} remains
\[
\operatorname{KL}(P_1,P_0)
\leq CTn_sb^{2\lambda+d}\leq C.
\tag{93}
\]
Thus the treated, smoothing, and, when \(B>D^2\), donor pairs lie in the iid subclass with their stated target separations.  The common spherical donor law from that lemma proves the treated and smoothing assertions also at \(B=D^2\).

It remains to construct the iid spacing pair.  Fix an admissible interior query \(z_0\in\mathcal Z_+\), choose a cohort \(s_*\) with positive weight, put
\[
b=c_bT^{-1/d},
\]
and let \(A_b\) be an interior cube centered at \(z_{0s_*}\), of side proportional to \(b\), with volume \(|A_b|=c/T\).  Under both laws draw all calendar vectors independently and uniformly.  Then
\[
P\{X_{s_*t}\notin A_b\text{ for every }t\mid E_0\}
=(1-c/T)^T\geq a>0,
\tag{94}
\]
after choosing fixed \(c>0\) small enough; finitely many small \(T\) are absorbed into \(a\).

Start from an admissible affine carrier when \(\lambda>1\), or an asymmetric H\"older-cusp carrier when \(0<\lambda\leq1\), and perturb only cohort \(s_*\) by
\[
c_1b^\lambda\psi\!\left(\frac{x-z_{0s_*}}b\right),
\]
where \(\psi\) is supported inside \(A_b\) and \(\psi(0)=1\).  The increment bounds used in \texttt{lem:joint-law-embedding} show that sufficiently small fixed \(c_1\) preserves boundedness, the H\"older ball, and both sides of the local modulus.  Complete untreated event outcomes by event transport while keeping the observed event mean fixed.  The identification lemma then gives the genuine target-separation inequality
\[
|\tau_{n,P_1}(z_0)-\tau_{n,P_0}(z_0)|
\geq cb^\lambda=cT^{-\lambda/d}.
\tag{95}
\]
On the no-hit event in (94), every historical response kernel is identical under the two laws; event and donor observed laws were chosen identical.  The restrictions of the two complete observed-data laws to that event consequently coincide, and their conditional testing affinity is at least \(a\).  Equations (94)--(95) prove the iid spacing assertion.  The common spherical donor distribution has covariance \(I_D\) and fourth moment \(D^2\), so this spacing construction remains valid at the boundary.

For squared-error lower bounds, the triangle-inequality calculation in \texttt{lem:joint-law-embedding} converts positive affinity and target separation \(\Delta_q\) into risk at least a fixed multiple of \(\Delta_q^2\).  For honest-length bounds, decrease the fixed perturbation constants so affinity exceeds \(2\alpha+a_0\); the common-overlap event on which both target points belong to an honest interval then has mass at least \(a_0\), and interval length there is at least \(\Delta_q\).  These observations apply to all four channels for fixed \(B>D^2\), and to exactly the treated, smoothing, and spacing channels at \(B=D^2\).  Every construction uses the causal completion of \texttt{lem:joint-law-embedding}, so it is a regular conditional law of the complete panel and obeys both full-design restrictions.  This proves the exact envelope split and every assertion of the lemma.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Independent uniform calendar designs retain the treated, smoothing, and donor witnesses and add a no-hit spacing witness of volume \(c/T\). \textit{Gap.} The equality-envelope boundary again excludes a universal donor witness. \textit{Consumer.} The iid risk and honest-length lower bounds use its four or three channels.

\begin{theorem}[thm:iid-sharp-joint-frontier]\label{thm:iid-sharp-joint-frontier}
Put \(D=Sd\).  For each fixed nonempty independent-calendar class \(\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger,\mathrm{iid}}(\lambda)\) with \(B>D^2\), along every array with \(N\wedge T\wedge\min_s n_s\to\infty\), uniformly for almost every admissible realized path \(z_0\in\mathcal Z_+\), there are constants \(0<c_{>}<C_{>}<\infty\), allowed to depend on the fixed slack \(B-D^2\), such that
\[
c_{>}R_\star\le
\inf_{\widetilde\tau}\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger,\mathrm{iid}}(\lambda)}
\mathbb E_P[(\widetilde\tau-\tau_n(z_0))^2\mid E_0]
\le
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger,\mathrm{iid}}(\lambda)}
\mathbb E_P[(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{iid}}-\tau_n(z_0))^2\mid E_0]
\le C_{>}R_\star.
\]
At \(B=D^2\), there are independently quantified constants \(0<c_{=}<C_{=}<\infty\) such that the same iid-tuned estimator has risk at most \(C_{=}R_\star\), while the universal lower bound is \(c_{=}(R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sm}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp}})\); no donor converse is asserted.  At \(B<D^2\) the class is empty.  Thus conditional calendar independence removes the logarithm from the spacing channel without changing the treated, smoothing, or generated-donor terms.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(D=Sd\).  Under \(B>D^2\), \texttt{lem:iid-joint-law-embedding} supplies, for every
\(q\in\{\mathrm{tr},\mathrm{sm},\mathrm{sp},\mathrm{don}\}\), two laws with conditional affinity at least \(a_q>0\) and target separation \(\Delta_q\) satisfying
\[
\Delta_q^2\geq c_qR_q.
\tag{96}
\]
For an arbitrary estimator \(\widetilde\tau\), the two events
\(\{|\widetilde\tau-\tau_0|\geq\Delta_q/2\}\) and
\(\{|\widetilde\tau-\tau_1|\geq\Delta_q/2\}\) cover the sample space.  Integration against the overlap measure therefore gives
\[
\max_{\ell\in\{0,1\}}
\mathbb E_{P_{\ell,q}}[(\widetilde\tau-\tau_\ell)^2\mid E_0]
\geq\frac{a_q\Delta_q^2}{8}
\geq cR_q.
\tag{97}
\]
Maximizing (97) over four channels gives \(c_{>}R_\star\).  At \(B=D^2\), the same argument uses the independently quantified treated, smoothing, and spacing pairs and gives
\[
c_{=}\{R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sm}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp}}\}.
\tag{98}
\]
No donor lower experiment is used at equality.  The compatibility part of the embedding also proves emptiness for \(B<D^2\).

For the upper bound, let \(\mathcal G_{\mathrm{iid}}\) be the good event for the iid-tuned selector, and let \(a_{sti}^{\mathrm{iid}}\) denote the linear-weight formula in \texttt{def:honest-interval} evaluated at that fit.  On this event the identification lemma and the local-polynomial linear representation give
\[
\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{iid}}-\tau_n(z_0)
=U_{\mathrm{evt}}-U_{\mathrm{hist}}
-B_{\mathrm{LP,iid}}-B_{\mathrm{don}},
\tag{99}
\]
where
\[
U_{\mathrm{evt}}
=\sum_{s,i}\frac{\pi_s}{n_s}
\{B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}-\mathbb E_P[B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid E_0]\},
\]
\[
U_{\mathrm{hist}}
=\sum_{s,i}\pi_s\sum_ta_{sti}^{\mathrm{iid}}\varepsilon_{sti},
\]
\[
B_{\mathrm{LP,iid}}
=\sum_s\pi_s\left\{\sum_{t,i}a_{sti}^{\mathrm{iid}}m_s(X_{st})
-m_s(\widetilde z_s)\right\},
\qquad
B_{\mathrm{don}}
=\sum_s\pi_s\{m_s(\widetilde z_s)-m_s(z_{0s})\}.
\]
Let \(\mathscr G\) be the sigma-field generated by the complete historical design, \(E_0\), and all donors.  Conditional on \(\mathscr G\), the two full-design centering assumptions center the first two sums.  Cross-unit independence and boundedness, followed by \texttt{lem:full-data-local-polynomial-control}, imply
\[
\mathbb E_P[U_{\mathrm{evt}}^2\mid\mathscr G]
\leq B^2R_{\mathrm{tr}},
\qquad
\mathbb E_P[U_{\mathrm{hist}}^2\mid\mathscr G]
\leq B^2c_G^{-1}R_{\mathrm{tr}},
\tag{100}
\]
where \(\mathscr G\) is the sigma-field generated by that design, \(E_0\), and all donors.

Polynomial reproduction and the degree-\(r\) H\"older Taylor remainder give, deterministically on a successful iid fit,
\[
\left|\sum_{t,i}a_{sti}^{\mathrm{iid}}m_s(X_{st})
-m_s(\widetilde z_s)\right|
\leq C(h_s^{\mathrm{iid}})^\lambda.
\tag{101}
\]
Applying \texttt{lem:independent-calendar-selector-control} with exponent \(2\lambda\), and then using fixed \(S\), yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_P[B_{\mathrm{LP,iid}}^2\mathbf1_{\mathcal G_{\mathrm{iid}}}\mid E_0]
&\leq C\sum_s\pi_s^2
\mathbb E_P[(h_s^{\mathrm{iid}})^{2\lambda}
\mathbf1_{\mathcal G_{\mathrm{iid}}}\mid E_0]\\
&\leq C\{R_{\mathrm{sm}}+T^{-2\lambda/d}+N^{-\lambda}\}
\leq CR_\star.
\end{aligned}
\tag{102}
\]

The donor calculation is unchanged because the iid estimator uses the same full donor mean and projection.  Specifically, for
\(Y_j=\Sigma^{-1/2}(Z_j-z_0)\), conditional replication gives
\[
\mathbb E_P\!\left[
\left\|N^{-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}Y_j\right\|_2^4
\,\middle|\,E_0\right]
\leq M_4(N).
\tag{103}
\]
Projection contraction and ellipticity then imply
\[
\mathbb E_P[B_{\mathrm{don}}^2\mid E_0]
\leq CN^{-\kappa}=CR_{\mathrm{don}},
\tag{104}
\]
by applying the upper local modulus on standardized displacements at most \(\delta_0\), and boundedness plus fourth-moment Markov control on the complement.

The iid selector lemma gives
\(P(\mathcal G_{\mathrm{iid}}^c\mid E_0)\leq CR_\star\) along the asserted arrays.  On this bad event the estimator's squared error is uniformly bounded: failed cohorts contribute zero, while successful local-polynomial intercepts are at most \(Bc_G^{-1/2}\) in absolute value by the deterministic weight bound.  Hence the bad-event risk is \(O(R_\star)\).  Squaring (99), using
\((x_1+x_2+x_3+x_4)^2\leq4\sum_jx_j^2\), and combining (100), (102), and (104) gives
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger,\mathrm{iid}}(\lambda)}
\mathbb E_P[(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{iid}}-\tau_n(z_0))^2\mid E_0]
\leq CR_\star.
\tag{105}
\]
Because \(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{iid}}\) is observed-data measurable, (105) bounds the minimax infimum from above.  Combine it with (97) under strict slack and with (98) at equality.  Quantify the strict-regime constants \(c_{>},C_{>}\) from the four-channel construction, allowing dependence on \(B-D^2\); quantify \(c_{=},C_{=}\) separately from the three boundary pairs and the common upper calculation.  Thus no constant depending on positive slack is reused at equality, and all claims follow.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The iid-tuned full-donor estimator attains \(R_\star\), and channelwise causal embeddings match it under strict slack. \textit{Gap.} Conditional calendar independence is stronger than geometric beta mixing. \textit{Consumer.} It turns the former no-log comparator into a joint-panel statistical result.

\begin{theorem}[thm:iid-honest-length-frontier]\label{thm:iid-honest-length-frontier}
For every positive sample size, every compatible \(B\ge D^2\), and every \(P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger,\mathrm{iid}}(\lambda)\), the iid-tuned interval satisfies
\[
P_P\{\tau_n(z_0)\in C_{\alpha,\mathrm{iid}}\mid E_0\}\ge1-\alpha.
\]
For fixed sample sizes, call an observed-data measurable interval \(I\) conditionally honest on this subclass if the preceding coverage inequality holds with \(I\) in place of \(C_{\alpha,\mathrm{iid}}\), uniformly in \(P\).  For each fixed nonempty class with \(B>D^2\), along every joint growth array there are constants \(0<c<C<\infty\), allowed to depend on the fixed slack \(B-D^2\), such that
\[
cR_\star^{1/2}
\le \inf_{I:\,\mathrm{conditionally\ honest}}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger,\mathrm{iid}}(\lambda)}
\mathbb E_P[\operatorname{length}(I)\mid E_0]
\le \sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger,\mathrm{iid}}(\lambda)}
\mathbb E_P[\operatorname{length}(C_{\alpha,\mathrm{iid}})\mid E_0]
\le CR_\star^{1/2}.
\]
At \(B=D^2\), independently quantified positive constants satisfy
\[
c(R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sm}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp}})^{1/2}
\le \inf_{I:\,\mathrm{conditionally\ honest}}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger,\mathrm{iid}}(\lambda)}
\mathbb E_P[\operatorname{length}(I)\mid E_0]
\le \sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger,\mathrm{iid}}(\lambda)}
\mathbb E_P[\operatorname{length}(C_{\alpha,\mathrm{iid}})\mid E_0]
\le CR_\star^{1/2};
\]
no donor expected-length converse is asserted.  The same lower rates hold for uniformly asymptotically conditionally honest interval sequences.  At \(B<D^2\) the class is empty.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix positive sample sizes, \(B\geq D^2\), a law
\(P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger,\mathrm{iid}}(\lambda)\), and an admissible event state outside a conditional null set.  Let \(a_{sti}^{\mathrm{iid}}\), \(\widehat m_s^{\mathrm{iid}}\), \(A_L^{\mathrm{iid}}\), and \(R_{\mathrm{hist}}^{\mathrm{iid}}\) denote, respectively, the weight, fitted intercept, Taylor allowance, and historical radius obtained by applying the formulas in \texttt{def:honest-interval} to the iid-selected fit.  On an iid-successful fit, polynomial reproduction and the degree-\(r\) H\"older remainder imply
\[
\left|
\widehat m_s^{\mathrm{iid}}-m_s(\widetilde z_s)
-\sum_{t,i}a_{sti}^{\mathrm{iid}}\varepsilon_{sti}
\right|
\leq C_{r,d}L\sum_{t,i}|a_{sti}^{\mathrm{iid}}|
\|X_{st}-\widetilde z_s\|_2^\lambda.
\tag{106}
\]
After cohort weighting and summation, the right side is the iid-computed Taylor allowance in \(C_{\alpha,\mathrm{iid}}\).

Condition on the complete historical design, \(E_0\), and all donors.  Historical full-design centering, event full-design mean stability, and donor independence center, respectively,
\[
\mathsf H_{si}^{\mathrm{iid}}
=\pi_s\sum_ta_{sti}^{\mathrm{iid}}\varepsilon_{sti},
\qquad
\mathsf E_{si}
=\frac{\pi_s}{n_s}
\{B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}-\mathbb E_P[B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid E_0]\}.
\]
Cross-unit independence makes each collection independent over \((s,i)\).  Their conditional range lengths are at most
\[
B\pi_s\sum_t|a_{sti}^{\mathrm{iid}}|
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
B\pi_s/n_s,
\]
respectively.  The endpoint-mixture exponential-moment bound
\(\mathbb E[e^{vU}\mid\cdot]\leq e^{v^2c^2/8}\) for a centered variable of range length \(c\), followed by exponential Markov optimization, therefore gives
\[
P\!\left\{
\left|\sum_{s,i}\mathsf H_{si}^{\mathrm{iid}}\right|>R_{\mathrm{hist}}^{\mathrm{iid}}
\,\middle|\,\mathscr X,E_0,(Z_j)_{j=1}^{N}\right\}
\leq\alpha_{\mathrm{hist}},
\tag{107}
\]
\[
P\!\left\{
\left|\sum_{s,i}\mathsf E_{si}\right|>R_{\mathrm{evt}}
\,\middle|\,\mathscr X,E_0,(Z_j)_{j=1}^{N}\right\}
\leq\alpha_{\mathrm{evt}}.
\tag{108}
\]
For donors, conditional replication, the covariance definition, and the standardized fourth-moment envelope give
\[
\mathbb E_P\!\left[
\left\|N^{-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Sigma^{-1/2}(Z_j-z_0)\right\|_2^4
\,\middle|\,E_0\right]
\leq M_4(N).
\tag{109}
\]
Thus the event \(\mathcal D_N\) that the displayed norm is at most \(q_N\) has conditional complement probability at most \(\alpha_{\mathrm{don}}\).  Projection contraction and ellipticity imply on \(\mathcal D_N\)
\[
\|\Sigma^{-1/2}(\widetilde z-z_0)\|_2\leq\delta_N.
\tag{110}
\]
On the iid good event, \(\delta_N\leq\delta_0\), so the upper local-modulus bound gives donor remainder at most
\[
C_{\mathrm{mod}}\delta_N^\kappa=A_{\mathrm{don}}.
\tag{111}
\]

On a bad iid fit, \(C_{\alpha,\mathrm{iid}}=[-B,B]\), which contains the target by bounded potential wealth and \(\sum_s\pi_s=1\).  On the intersection of the iid good event and \(\mathcal D_N\), identification, (106), and (111) show that noncoverage implies one of the deviations in (107)--(108).  Iterated conditioning and the union bound therefore give
\[
P_P\{\tau_n(z_0)\notin C_{\alpha,\mathrm{iid}}\mid E_0\}
\leq\alpha_{\mathrm{hist}}+\alpha_{\mathrm{evt}}+\alpha_{\mathrm{don}}
=\alpha.
\tag{112}
\]
This argument uses no selector-tail approximation, so (112) is exact at every positive sample size and at the envelope boundary.

For expected length, the deterministic weight control gives
\[
R_{\mathrm{hist}}^{\mathrm{iid}}+R_{\mathrm{evt}}
\leq CR_{\mathrm{tr}}^{1/2}
\tag{113}
\]
on successful fits.  Compact kernel support gives
\[
A_L^{\mathrm{iid}}leq C\sum_s\pi_s(h_s^{\mathrm{iid}})^\lambda.
\]
Using the iid selector moment with exponent \(\lambda\), integrating over donors, and using fixed \(S\),
\[
\mathbb E_P[A_L^{\mathrm{iid}}\mathbf1_{\mathrm{good,iid}}\mid E_0]
\leq C\{R_{\mathrm{sm}}^{1/2}+T^{-\lambda/d}+N^{-\lambda/2}\}
\leq CR_\star^{1/2}.
\tag{114}
\]
The fixed fourth-moment calibration makes \(q_N\leq C\), and hence
\[
A_{\mathrm{don}}\leq CN^{-\kappa/2}=CR_{\mathrm{don}}^{1/2}.
\tag{115}
\]
The iid bad-event probability is \(O(R_\star)\); the fallback interval has length at most \(2B\).  Since \(R_\star\leq R_\star^{1/2}\) eventually, (113)--(115) yield
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}^{\dagger,\mathrm{iid}}(\lambda)}
\mathbb E_P[\operatorname{length}(C_{\alpha,\mathrm{iid}})\mid E_0]
\leq CR_\star^{1/2}.
\tag{116}
\]

For the converse, take any conditionally honest observed-data interval \(I\) and a channel pair from \texttt{lem:iid-joint-law-embedding}.  Decrease its fixed perturbation constant so its affinity
\(A_q=\int\min\{dP_{0,q},dP_{1,q}\}\) exceeds \(2\alpha+a_0\), while its target separation remains \(\Delta_q\geq cR_q^{1/2}\).  With overlap measure \(\mu_q=P_{0,q}\wedge P_{1,q}\), honesty implies
\[
\mu_q\{\tau_0,\tau_1\in I\}
\geq A_q-P_{0,q}\{\tau_0\notin I\mid E_0\}
-P_{1,q}\{\tau_1\notin I\mid E_0\}
\geq a_0.
\tag{117}
\]
On this event \(\operatorname{length}(I)\geq\Delta_q\), whence
\[
\max_{\ell\in\{0,1\}}
\mathbb E_{P_{\ell,q}}[\operatorname{length}(I)\mid E_0]
\geq\int\operatorname{length}(I)\,d\mu_q
\geq a_0\Delta_q
\geq cR_q^{1/2}.
\tag{118}
\]
Maximizing (118) over four channels for fixed \(B>D^2\) proves the strict-slack lower bound; maximizing over the independently constructed treated, smoothing, and spacing pairs at \(B=D^2\) proves the stated three-channel lower bound, without a donor converse.  For uniformly asymptotically honest intervals, replace \(\alpha\) in (117) by \(\alpha+\epsilon\) and choose \(\epsilon<a_0/4\), leaving overlap at least \(a_0/2\).  Equations (112), (116), and (118), together with the compatibility split, prove the theorem.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The iid-tuned interval retains exact conditional coverage and attains the no-log honest-length rate. \textit{Gap.} No donor expected-length converse is asserted at \(B=D^2\). \textit{Consumer.} It supplies inference on the independent-calendar side of the dependence elbow.

\begin{theorem}[thm:dependence-elbow]\label{thm:dependence-elbow}
For \(T\ge3\), write
\[
A_\beta=R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta},
\qquad
A_{\mathrm{iid}}=R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp}}.
\]
Then \(R_{\star,\beta}=A_\beta\vee R_{\mathrm{don}}\) and \(R_\star=A_{\mathrm{iid}}\vee R_{\mathrm{don}}\).  On every fixed nonempty class with \(B>D^2\), these are respectively the sharp conditional mean-squared-error rates under design-only geometric beta mixing and conditional calendar independence, and their square roots are the sharp uniformly honest expected-length rates.  Thus the oracle/generated-center boundaries are respectively
\[
N\asymp A_\beta^{-1/\kappa}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
N\asymp A_{\mathrm{iid}}^{-1/\kappa}.
\]
At \(B=D^2\), both expressions remain valid upper phase maps, but neither theorem asserts a universal donor converse.  Conditional calendar independence therefore removes exactly the logarithm in the spacing component; it introduces no deconvolution or moving-Gram term.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For \(T\geq3\), define
\[
A_\beta=R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta},
\qquad
A_{\mathrm{iid}}=R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp}}.
\]
The exact algebra proved in \texttt{thm:oracle-generated-phase} gives
\[
R_{\star,\beta}=R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}\vee R_{\mathrm{don}}
=A_\beta\vee R_{\mathrm{don}},
\tag{119}
\]
and
\[
R_\star=R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp}}\vee R_{\mathrm{don}}
=A_{\mathrm{iid}}\vee R_{\mathrm{don}}.
\tag{120}
\]
On every fixed nonempty class with \(B>D^2\), \texttt{thm:sharp-joint-frontier} identifies (119) as the sharp conditional mean-squared-error rate under design-only geometric beta mixing, while \texttt{thm:honest-length-frontier} identifies its square root as the sharp uniformly honest expected-length rate.  Directly, \texttt{thm:iid-sharp-joint-frontier} and \texttt{thm:iid-honest-length-frontier} give the corresponding two conclusions for (120) on the conditional independent-calendar subclass.  Thus these are statistical conclusions, not merely bookkeeping equalities.

By \texttt{def:rate-frontier}, \(R_{\mathrm{don}}=N^{-\kappa}\).  Since \(A_\beta,A_{\mathrm{iid}}>0\), equality between donor and oracle scales is equivalent, respectively, to
\[
N^{-\kappa}\asymp A_\beta
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
N\asymp A_\beta^{-1/\kappa},
\tag{121}
\]
and
\[
N^{-\kappa}\asymp A_{\mathrm{iid}}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
N\asymp A_{\mathrm{iid}}^{-1/\kappa}.
\tag{122}
\]
At \(B=D^2\), both upper bounds and hence both upper phase maps remain valid, whereas the two frontier theorems expressly omit a universal donor lower bound; this is exactly the boundary qualification in the claim.  Finally,
\[
R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}
=\{\log(T\vee2)/T\}^{2\lambda/d},
\qquad
R_{\mathrm{sp}}=T^{-2\lambda/d},
\]
so conditional calendar independence removes precisely the logarithm from the spacing term.  Assumption \texttt{ass:exact-training-coordinates} rules out a training-coordinate measurement-error convolution, and the exhaustive maxima (119)--(120) contain no moving-Gram or deconvolution term.  This proves every asserted phase and dependence-elbow conclusion.
\end{proof}

\section{Interpretation}

For \(T\ge3\), smoothing is algebraically dominated. Under design-only geometric beta mixing, \(R_{\star,\beta}=R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta}\vee R_{\mathrm{don}}\), so the strict-slack donor boundary is \(N\asymp(R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp},\beta})^{-1/\kappa}\). Under conditional calendar independence, \(R_\star=R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp}}\vee R_{\mathrm{don}}\), and the boundary becomes \(N\asymp(R_{\mathrm{tr}}\vee R_{\mathrm{sp}})^{-1/\kappa}\). Thus independence removes exactly the log-spacing penalty and no other channel. The donor exponent remains \(\kappa=\min\{\lambda,1\}\). At \(B=D^2\), both expressions remain valid upper phase maps, but donor domination is not claimed minimax-sharp because a universal donor lower witness is unavailable.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

Causal identification remains conditional on event transport.  The vanishing risk and honest-length frontiers require the two explicit full-design centering restrictions; the universal witness proves rate-essentiality of the event-side restriction but does not separately establish necessity of historical full-design centering.  The no-log corollary additionally requires conditional independence of the stacked historical design vectors across calendar indices.  The broader restricted theorem assumes only geometric beta mixing of the design and necessarily pays the uniform log-spacing term.  Nonemptiness requires \(B\ge D^2\), while the donor converse requires fixed strict slack \(B-D^2>0\).  The two-sided local modulus is assumed rather than derived over the full Holder ball, \(S\) and \(p(H+1)\) are fixed, and the results do not provide smoothness adaptation or sharp constants.

\section{Honest scope}

The proved contribution consists of event-transport identification; a universal shared-design minimax-risk and honest-length obstruction on the original class; the exact full-donor coefficient identity; deterministic local-polynomial control; dependence-specific selector bounds; causal-panel lower embeddings; sharp conditional-risk frontiers; and exact finite-sample conditionally honest intervals on the full-design-centered classes.  The obstruction satisfies historical full-design centering and violates event full-design mean stability, establishing rate-essentiality of the event-side restriction only.  Design-only geometric beta mixing yields the unavoidable log-spacing rate; the named conditional independent-calendar subclass yields the no-log rate without importing block lengths.  For fixed \(B>D^2\), four lower channels match both risk and expected length in each restricted regime.  At \(B=D^2\), exact coverage and upper bounds remain, but only treated, smoothing, and the appropriate spacing channels are universally lower-bounded.  No donor splitting, covariance estimation, response-variance lower bound, sequential overlap, dense-local-site, upper-local-mass, studentization, temporal-outcome mixing, deconvolution, or moving-Gram term is used.
