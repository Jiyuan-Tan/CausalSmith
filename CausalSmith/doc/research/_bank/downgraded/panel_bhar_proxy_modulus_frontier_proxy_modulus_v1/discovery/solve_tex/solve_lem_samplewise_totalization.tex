Let \(\mathcal C=([0,1]^d)^S\).  Coordinatewise clipping is the Euclidean projection \(\Pi_{\mathcal C}\): for each coordinate, the closest point in \([0,1]\) is \(x\mapsto 0\vee(x\wedge1)\).  This scalar map is continuous and one-Lipschitz, so \(\Pi_{\mathcal C}\) is continuous, Borel measurable, and nonexpansive in Euclidean norm.  Since \(z_0\in\mathcal C\), \(\Pi_{\mathcal C}(z_0)=z_0\), and therefore
\[
 \|\widetilde z_\ell-z_0\|_2
 =\|\Pi_{\mathcal C}(\overline Z_\ell)-\Pi_{\mathcal C}(z_0)\|_2
 \le \|\overline Z_\ell-z_0\|_2.
\]
The two inequalities in \(\texttt{ass:donor-ellipticity}\) imply
\[
 c_\Sigma\|v\|_2^2\le v^\top\Sigma v\le C_\Sigma\|v\|_2^2
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 C_\Sigma^{-1}\|v\|_2^2\le v^\top\Sigma^{-1}v
 \le c_\Sigma^{-1}\|v\|_2^2
\]
for every \(v\in\mathbb R^{Sd}\).  Consequently,
\[
 \|\Sigma^{-1/2}v\|_2\le c_\Sigma^{-1/2}\|v\|_2,
 \qquad
 \|v\|_2\le C_\Sigma^{1/2}\|\Sigma^{-1/2}v\|_2.
\]
Apply the first inequality to \(v=\widetilde z_\ell-z_0\), use the Euclidean contraction just proved, and then apply the second inequality to \(v=\overline Z_\ell-z_0\).  This gives
\[
 \|\Sigma^{-1/2}(\widetilde z_\ell-z_0)\|_2
 \le (C_\Sigma/c_\Sigma)^{1/2}
 \|\Sigma^{-1/2}(\overline Z_\ell-z_0)\|_2.
\]

We next verify totality and measurability of the estimator in \(\texttt{def:frontier-estimator}\).  Since \(T,n_s,N\ge1\),
\[
 0<(Tn_s)^{-1/(2\lambda+d)}\le1,
 \qquad 0<N^{-1/2}\le1,
\]
and hence \(0<b_{0s}\le1\).  Thus
\[
 M_s=\lfloor\log_2(b_{0s}^{-1})\rfloor\in\mathbb N_0,
 \qquad
 \mathcal B_s=\{2^{-m}:0\le m\le M_s\}
\]
is finite and nonempty, contains \(1\), and satisfies \(b\ge b_{0s}\) for every \(b\in\mathcal B_s\), because \(m\le\log_2(b_{0s}^{-1})\) implies \(2^{-m}\ge b_{0s}\).  Moreover, \(K_N=1\) when \(N=1\) and \(K_N=2\) when \(N\ge2\).  The balanced residue convention therefore makes every fold \(I_\ell\) nonempty, so every fold mean \(\overline Z_\ell\) exists and is Borel measurable.

For a fixed center, the event \(\lambda_{\min}\{G_{sT}(u,b)\}\ge c_G\) is Borel for every \(b\in\mathcal B_s\), by the continuity argument below.  Consequently, inspecting the finite ordered grid and returning its first coercive element, with the stipulated value \(1\) if none exists, is a finite composition of Borel comparisons and is itself Borel and total.

Fix \((s,\ell)\), and denote by \(\vartheta\) the finite-dimensional array of design and donor inputs entering its search.  Put \(E=[0,1]^d\times\mathcal B_s\), with the deterministic order on the finite second coordinate.  For \(e=(u,b)\in E\), define
\[
 h(\vartheta,e)=\lambda_{\min}\{G_{sT}(u,b)\}-c_G,
 \qquad
 \gamma(\vartheta,e)=\Gamma_{s\ell}(u,b).
\]
The kernel is continuous, the monomial basis is polynomial, and \(b>0\).  Hence every entry of \(G_{sT}(u,b)\) is continuous in \((\vartheta,u)\).  The variational identity
\[
 \lambda_{\min}(A)=\min_{\|v\|_2=1}v^\top Av
\]
shows directly that the smallest eigenvalue is continuous in a symmetric matrix \(A\): the displayed minimum is over the compact unit sphere, and the quadratic form changes uniformly when the entries of \(A\) change.  Thus \(h\) is continuous.  The continuity of clipping and of \(x\mapsto x^\kappa\) for \(\kappa>0\) also makes \(\gamma\) continuous.  It follows that
\[
 \mathcal A_{s\ell}(\vartheta)=\{e\in E:h(\vartheta,e)\ge0\}
\]
is a closed subset of the compact set \(E\), hence compact.  If it is nonempty, \(\gamma\) attains its minimum on it.

For completeness, the lexicographic minimizer is Borel rather than merely existent.  For \(m\ge1\), set
\[
 v_m(\vartheta)=\min_{e\in E}
 \bigl[\gamma(\vartheta,e)+m\{-h(\vartheta,e)\}_+\bigr].
\]
Each \(v_m\) is continuous.  Indeed, joint continuity on a compact neighborhood gives uniform continuity in \(e\), so taking a minimum changes its value by no more than the uniform change of the minimized function.  On the nonempty-candidate branch, \(v_m\) is nondecreasing and
\[
 \lim_{m\to\infty}v_m(\vartheta)
 =\min_{e\in\mathcal A_{s\ell}(\vartheta)}\gamma(\vartheta,e).
\]
To prove the equality, comparison with any feasible minimizer bounds \(v_m\) above by the constrained minimum.  If \(e_m\) minimizes the penalized criterion, compactness gives a convergent subsequence.  Along it the penalty bound forces \(\{-h(\vartheta,e_m)\}_+\to0\), so the limit is feasible; continuity of \(\gamma\) supplies the reverse inequality.  Therefore the constrained value is Borel as a pointwise limit of continuous functions.  The same argument with \(b\) fixed makes the constrained value for each \(b\in\mathcal B_s\) Borel.  Because the grid is finite, selecting the first \(b\) whose feasible value equals the overall constrained value is Borel.

It remains only to select the coordinates of \(u\).  On a fixed selected \(b\), suppose the first \(q-1\) selected coordinates have already been shown Borel.  For \(m\ge1\), minimize over \(u\in[0,1]^d\) the continuous-in-\(u\) penalty
\[
 u_q+m\{-h(\vartheta,(u,b))\}_+
 +m\left|\gamma(\vartheta,(u,b))-v(\vartheta)\right|
 +m\sum_{j<q}|u_j-u_j^\star(\vartheta)|,
\]
where \(v\) is the constrained minimum.  Its minimum is Borel: for a fixed countable dense subset \(D\subset[0,1]^d\), continuity in \(u\) makes the minimum equal to the infimum over \(D\), an infimum of countably many Borel functions.  As \(m\to\infty\), compactness and the same penalty argument show that these minima increase to the least possible \(q\)-th coordinate among constrained minimizers having the preceding selected coordinates.  The limit is Borel.  Induction over \(q=1,\ldots,d\) proves that the lexicographically first exact minimizer \((\widehat u_{s\ell},\widehat h_{s\ell})\) is Borel.  The event that the candidate set is empty is also Borel, since it is
\[
 \bigcap_{b\in\mathcal B_s}
 \left\{\max_{u\in[0,1]^d}h(\vartheta,(u,b))<0\right\},
\]
and each displayed maximum is continuous by the same compactness argument.

On a nonempty candidate set, coercivity gives
\[
 \lambda_{\min}\{G_{sT}(\widehat u_{s\ell},\widehat h_{s\ell})\}
 \ge c_G>0.
\]
The local-polynomial Gram matrix is therefore invertible, its inverse is continuous on this positive-definite domain, and the fitted intercept and its response weights are Borel and finite.  On the complementary Borel event, the pooled mean exists because \(Tn_s\ge1\).  Thus the deterministic fallback, clipping to \([-B,B]\), and finite donor-fold average produce a Borel, everywhere-defined \(\widehat m_s\), and the finite cohort sum produces a Borel, everywhere-defined \(\widehat\tau\).

We finally check the interval in \(\texttt{def:honest-interval}\).  Spectral clipping is the continuous functional-calculus map
\[
 A\longmapsto U\operatorname{diag}
 \bigl((c_\Sigma/2)\vee(\lambda_j(A)\wedge2C_\Sigma)\bigr)U^\top
\]
for a symmetric matrix \(A=U\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_j(A))U^\top\); the result is independent of the eigenbasis chosen inside a repeated eigenspace.  It is therefore Borel and always has eigenvalues in \([c_\Sigma/2,2C_\Sigma]\).  The convention at \(N=1\) is deterministic.  All weight norms, block bounds, bias terms, finite sums, maxima, minima, logarithms with declared positive arguments, and interval endpoints are consequently Borel on their declared branches.  More explicitly, with
\[
 A=\sum_{k=1}^{K_T}L_k^2,
 \qquad
 \eta=\alpha_{\mathrm{st}}/2-2SK_T\beta_0\rho_0^{a_T},
\]
the calendar defining set is empty when \(\eta\le0\); when \(\eta>0\), its infimum is, under the stated zero-block convention,
\[
 x_{\mathrm{cal}}=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&A=0,\\
 \{(A/2)\log(4/\eta)\}^{1/2},&A>0.
 \end{cases}
\]
Here \(0<\eta<4\), because \(0<\alpha_{\mathrm{st}}<1/4\).  Thus this scalar optimization is Borel and finite exactly on its usable branch.  Every condition declaring a bad branch is a Borel comparison among these finite-dimensional quantities.  The regular formula is used only when all its inputs are defined and finite; otherwise \(C_\alpha=[-2B,2B]\).  Hence \(C_\alpha\) is Borel measurable and samplewise defined, without division by any estimated variance.

It remains to prove the asserted range.  By \(\texttt{ass:consistency}\), \(B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}=B_{si}(1)\), and \(\texttt{ass:bounded-wealth}\) therefore yields \(|B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}|\le B\).  Each treated sample mean lies in \([-B,B]\).  Each selected fit and fallback is clipped to \([-B,B]\), and hence so is its donor-fold average \(\widehat m_s\).  Since \(\pi_s>0\) and \(\sum_{s=1}^S\pi_s=1\),
\[
 -2B
 \le\sum_{s=1}^S\pi_s
 \left\{n_s^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}-\widehat m_s\right\}
 =\widehat\tau
 \le2B.
\]
On the regular branch, \(\texttt{def:honest-interval}\) gives
\[
 C_\alpha=[\widehat\tau-R_\alpha,\widehat\tau+R_\alpha]\cap[-2B,2B],
\]
whereas every declared bad branch gives \(C_\alpha=[-2B,2B]\).  These are precisely the two alternatives in the claim.
