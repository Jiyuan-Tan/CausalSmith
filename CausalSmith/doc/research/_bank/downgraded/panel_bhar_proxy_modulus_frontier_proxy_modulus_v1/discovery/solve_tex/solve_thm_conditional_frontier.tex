%%% FIELD causal-identification-proof
Fix \(P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}\).  By \texttt{def:target}, at every query where the relevant conditional expectations exist,
\[
 \tau(z_0)=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s\left\{
 \mathbb E_P[B_{si}(1)\mid z_0]-
 \mathbb E_P[B_{si}(0)\mid z_0]\right\}.                 \tag{1}
\]
Assumption \texttt{ass:design-conditional-centering} gives, outside a marginally null set of queries, a value \(\mu_s(z_0)\) independent of the replica index such that
\[
 \mathbb E_P[B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid z_0]=\mu_s(z_0)
 \quad\text{for every }i.                                  \tag{2}
\]
The almost-sure equality in \texttt{ass:consistency} disintegrates to
\[
 \mathbb E_P[B_{si}(1)\mid z_0]
 =\mathbb E_P[B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid z_0],               \tag{3}
\]
and \texttt{ass:event-date-transport} gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P[B_{si}(0)\mid z_0]=m_s(z_{0s}).                \tag{4}
\]
All expectations are finite by the bounded-support clause incorporated in \texttt{def:panel-model-class}.  At a fixed triangular-array index there are finitely many cohorts and replicas.  Intersecting the conull sets on which (2)--(4) hold and substituting into (1) proves
\[
 \tau(z_0)=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s\left\{
 \mathbb E_P[B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid z_0]-m_s(z_{0s})
 \right\}                                                   \tag{5}
\]
for marginal-almost-every admissible query, and also proves that the right-hand side is independent of \(i\).

Now fix an admissible query and a labelled point
\(\xi=(\mathbb K_\xi;\mu,m,\Sigma)\in
\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)\).  Equations (2)--(3) of
\texttt{def:fixed-query-kernel-class} state, for every replica,
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb K_\xi}B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}=\mu_s,
 \qquad B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}=B_{si}(1)quad
 \mathbb K_\xi\text{-almost surely},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb K_\xi}B_{si}(0)=m_s(z_{0s}).           \tag{6}
\]
Applying \texttt{def:target} to (6) yields
\[
 \tau(z_0)=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s\{\mu_s-m_s(z_{0s})\}
 =\theta_{\mathrm{ker}}(\xi;z_0),                           \tag{7}
\]
where the last equality is exactly \texttt{def:fixed-query-functional}.  Thus the causal target is related to the observed-law functional by consistency and event-date transport; the equality is not created by the definition of the observed-law functional.  No density, mixing, donor-moment, rank, inversion, or division condition is used.

%%% FIELD conditional-frontier-proof
Put \(q=Sd\) and abbreviate
\[
 A=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\frac{\pi_s^2}{n_s},
 \qquad
 \rho_s=\rho_{sX}(z_{0s}),
 \qquad
 D_X=\sum_{s=1}^{S}\pi_s\rho_s^\lambda.                   \tag{1}
\]
Every \(n_s\) and \(\pi_s\) is positive, so \(A>0\).  We first work in the deterministic experiment \(\mathfrak E_X(z_0,\Sigma)\) from \texttt{def:fixed-design-induced-experiment}.  Here \(X,z_0,\Sigma\) are known specifications, but the decision rule is selected before the unknown label \((\mu,f,z)\).

For the estimator in \texttt{def:nearest-design-estimator}, condition on the donor sample and define
\[
 U_{si}=E_{si}-\mu_s-K_N^{-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{K_N}
 \{Q_{s,t_{s\ell},i}-f_s(X_{s,t_{s\ell}})\}.               \tag{2}
\]
Donor--history independence makes the indices \(t_{s\ell}\) fixed under this conditioning.  The mean restrictions in the experiment center every \(U_{si}\), and history independence makes the collection independent across \((s,i)\).  The support restrictions imply \(|E_{si}-\mu_s|\le2B\) and \(|Q_{sti}-f_s(X_{st})|\le2B\), hence \(|U_{si}|\le4B\).  Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_{\mathbb Q_\xi}\!\left(
 \sum_{s=1}^{S}\frac{\pi_s}{n_s}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}U_{si}
 \mathrel{\Big|}(Z_j)_{j=1}^{N}\right)
 \le16B^2A.                                                 \tag{3}
\]

Let \(t_s^0\) be the smallest nearest design index to \(z_{0s}\).  Nearest-site optimality, followed by two triangle inequalities, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \|X_{s,t_{s\ell}}-z_s\|_2
 &\le \|X_{s,t_s^0}-\widetilde z_{\ell s}\|_2
       +\|\widetilde z_{\ell s}-z_s\|_2\\
 &\le \rho_s+\|z_s-z_{0s}\|_2
       +2\|\widetilde z_{\ell s}-z_s\|_2.
\end{aligned}                                               \tag{4}
\]
Because \(0<\lambda\le1\), the map \(x\mapsto x^\lambda\) is subadditive.  The Holder restriction consequently yields
\[
 |f_s(X_{s,t_{s\ell}})-f_s(z_s)|
 \le C\left\{\rho_s^\lambda+
 \|z_s-z_{0s}\|_2^\lambda+
 \|\widetilde z_{\ell s}-z_s\|_2^\lambda\right\}.        \tag{5}
\]
Projection onto the closed cube is nonexpansive relative to every \(z\) in that cube.  Every balanced fold has size at least \(N/3\), including the cases \(N=1,2\).  Donor unbiasedness and \(\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma)\le qC_\Sigma\) thus imply
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb Q_\xi}\|\widetilde z_\ell-z\|_2^2
 \le\mathbb E\|\overline Z_\ell-z\|_2^2
 =\frac{\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma)}{|I_\ell|}
 \le\frac{3qC_\Sigma}{N}.                                  \tag{6}
\]
The local experiment also imposes
\[
 \|z-z_0\|_2^2
 \le C_\Sigma\|\Sigma^{-1/2}(z-z_0)\|_2^2
 \le C_\Sigma N^{-1}.                                      \tag{7}
\]
Expanding \(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{opt}}-\theta_{\mathfrak E}(\xi)\) gives the centered sum in (3) plus the weighted approximation errors in (5).  Jensen's inequality applied to (6)--(7), followed by Cauchy--Schwarz and \((x+y+z)^2\le3(x^2+y^2+z^2)\), therefore gives
\[
 \sup_{\xi\in\mathfrak E_X(z_0,\Sigma)}
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb Q_\xi}
 \left[\{\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{opt}}-
 \theta_{\mathfrak E}(\xi)\}^2\right]
 \le C\{A+D_X^2+N^{-\lambda}\}.                           \tag{8}
\]
The estimator uses only the observations and the known specifications, so it is an admissible witness for the infimum.  If \(\delta_0<1/2\), \texttt{lem:rough-holder-optimal-recovery-geometry} identifies the right side of (8), up to fixed constants, with \(R_{X,\mathrm{opt}}\).

We next prove the minimax converse.  We repeatedly use the following elementary two-point calculation.  If two laws \(P_0,P_1\) have targets \(\theta_0,\theta_1\), separation \(\Delta=|\theta_1-\theta_0|\), and total variation at most \(1-\eta\), then, with \(A_0=\{|\widetilde\theta-\theta_0|<\Delta/2\}\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \max_{v=0,1}\mathbb E_v(\widetilde\theta-\theta_v)^2
 &\ge\frac{\Delta^2}{8}\{P_0(A_0^c)+P_1(A_0)\}\\
 &\ge\frac{\eta\Delta^2}{8}.
\end{aligned}                                               \tag{9}
\]
The first inequality follows because the two open balls of radius \(\Delta/2\) are disjoint; the second is the defining testing characterization of total variation.

For the treated-replication channel, take \(f_s=0\), \(Q_{sti}=0\), \(z=z_0\), and a common donor law with mean \(z_0\) and covariance \(\Sigma\), for example \(z_0+\Sigma^{1/2}R\) with independent Rademacher coordinates in \(R\).  Fix \(0<b<B\).  Under \(v\in\{-1,1\}\), let all \(E_{si}\) be independent, supported on \(\{-b,b\}\), and satisfy
\[
 \Pr_v(E_{si}=b)=\frac12+v\varepsilon_s,
 \qquad
 \varepsilon_s=\frac{\gamma\pi_s}{bn_s\sqrt A}.            \tag{10}
\]
Since \(A\ge\pi_s^2/n_s\), a sufficiently small fixed \(\gamma>0\) puts every probability in \([1/4,3/4]\).  The two targets differ by
\[
 4b\sum_s\pi_s\varepsilon_s=4\gamma\sqrt A.                \tag{11}
\]
The Hellinger affinity of the observed products is
\[
 \prod_{s=1}^{S}(1-4\varepsilon_s^2)^{n_s/2}
 \ge \exp(-C\gamma^2/b^2)>0,                                \tag{12}
\]
because \(\sum_sn_s\varepsilon_s^2=\gamma^2/b^2\).  For any dominated laws with affinity \(a\), Cauchy--Schwarz applied to
\(|p-q|=|\sqrt p-\sqrt q|(\sqrt p+\sqrt q)\) gives total variation at most \(\sqrt{1-a^2}\).  Hence (9)--(12) give a lower bound \(cA\).

For the fixed-design channel, let
\[
 q_s(x)=a_0\min_{1\le t\le T}\|x-X_{st}\|_2^\lambda,
 \qquad f_s=q_s/2,
 \qquad g_s=-q_s/2,                                         \tag{13}
\]
where \(a_0>0\) is small enough for the Holder and support radii.  The distance to a nonempty finite set is one-Lipschitz, and
\(|u^\lambda-v^\lambda|\le|u-v|^\lambda\) for nonnegative \(u,v\).  Thus the two surfaces are admissible, they agree at every design site, and their values at \(z_{0s}\) differ by \(a_0\rho_s^\lambda\).  Taking deterministic zero responses, identical event laws, identical donors, and \(z=z_0\) makes the entire observation laws identical while separating the targets by \(a_0D_X\).  Equation (9) with \(\eta=1\) yields the lower bound \(cD_X^2\).

For the donor channel, fix \(\eta\in(0,1)\) and invoke \texttt{lem:full-covariance-location-pair}, reducing its displacement constant if necessary so that its Mahalanobis budget is at most one.  Choose the displaced coordinate in a cohort \(s_0\) and write it as coordinate \(k\).  Use the same regression under both alternatives,
\[
 f_{s_0}(x)=a_1|x_k-z_{0,s_0k}|^\lambda,
 \qquad f_s(x)=0\quad(s\ne s_0),                            \tag{14}
\]
with \(a_1>0\) small enough for the Holder and support bounds.  Keep all response and event laws identical and vary only the donor law.  If \(e\) is the signed displaced coordinate, the lemma and ellipticity give
\[
 |z_{1,e}-z_{0,e}|=
 \frac{c_\eta N^{-1/2}}{\|\Sigma^{-1/2}e\|_2}
 \ge c_\eta c_\Sigma^{1/2}N^{-1/2}.                       \tag{15}
\]
The donor products have total variation at most \(1-\eta\), whereas (14)--(15) separate the targets by at least \(cN^{-\lambda/2}\).  Equation (9) yields the lower bound \(cN^{-\lambda}\).  Combining the three subexperiments gives
\[
 \inf_{\widetilde\tau}\sup_{\xi\in\mathfrak E_X(z_0,\Sigma)}
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb Q_\xi}
 \left[\{\widetilde\tau-\theta_{\mathfrak E}(\xi)\}^2\right]
 \ge c\{A\vee D_X^2\vee N^{-\lambda}\}.                  \tag{16}
\]
Under \(\delta_0<1/2\), \texttt{lem:rough-holder-optimal-recovery-geometry} says that the last maximum is equivalent to \(R_{X,\mathrm{opt}}\), proving the asserted lower bound.  Notice that no donor moment beyond the specified covariance was imposed on the experiment; the finite-support laws in \texttt{lem:full-covariance-location-pair} are only witnesses.

Now fix \(\xi=(\mathbb K_\xi;\mu,m,\Sigma)\in
\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)\) and condition on the exact design sigma-field \(\mathcal X\).  Definition \texttt{def:fixed-query-kernel-class} gives donor--panel independence, conditional independence of histories across \((s,i)\), and
\[
 \mathbb E[Q_{sti}\mid\mathcal X]=m_s(X_{st}),
 \qquad
 \mathbb E[B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid\mathcal X]=\mu_s.     \tag{17}
\]
Thus, for almost every design, the conditional observed law with its labels is the central point \(z=z_0\) of \(\mathfrak E_X(z_0,\Sigma)\).  Because \(|Q_{sti}|\le B\), (17) gives \(|m_s(X_{st})|\le B\) almost surely.  The positive design density makes this true Lebesgue-almost everywhere, and Holder continuity extends it to every \(x\in[0,1]^d\); hence the experiment's sup-norm condition holds.  Moreover,
\[
 \theta_{\mathfrak E}
 =\sum_s\pi_s\{\mu_s-m_s(z_{0s})\}
 =\theta_{\mathrm{ker}}(\xi;z_0).                           \tag{18}
\]
Applying (8) conditionally proves the nearest-design upper bound.  If every cohort design contains \(z_{0s}\), then \(D_X=0\), and (8) and \texttt{lem:rough-holder-optimal-recovery-geometry} give both exact-site conclusions.

Suppose instead that \(g_{sT}(z_0)<1\) for every cohort.  Since \(0<\lambda\le1\), the order convention gives \(r=0\).  The pair \((z_{0s},h_s^\star)\) is then feasible in every foldwise anchor search.  Optimality of the selected certificate gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &C_H\|\widehat u_{s\ell}-\widetilde z_{\ell s}\|_2^\lambda
 +C_H\widehat h_{s\ell}^\lambda
 +C_V(Tn_s\widehat h_{s\ell}^d)^{-1/2}\\
 &\quad\le C_H\|z_{0s}-\widetilde z_{\ell s}\|_2^\lambda
 +C_H(h_s^\star)^\lambda
 +C_V\{Tn_s(h_s^\star)^d\}^{-1/2}.
\end{aligned}                                               \tag{19}
\]
The bandwidth floor in \(\mathcal B_s\) implies
\[
 \{Tn_s(h_s^\star)^d\}^{-1/2}\le(h_s^\star)^\lambda.      \tag{20}
\]
Scalar Gram coercivity makes every selected local-constant denominator positive.  Hence its weights are nonnegative and sum to one, and clipping is inactive for responses in \([-B,B]\).  Holder continuity bounds its approximation bias by the first two terms on the left of (19).  After averaging folds, the centered response and treated terms can again be grouped into one bounded variable per unit history; (17) and cross-unit independence bound their total variance by \(CA\), without any response-calendar independence.  Finally, donor unbiasedness, projection contraction, and ellipticity give
\[
 \mathbb E\|\widetilde z_\ell-z_0\|_2^2
 \le\frac{3qC_\Sigma}{N}.                                  \tag{21}
\]
Using (19)--(21), Jensen's inequality for the exponent \(\lambda\le1\), and the definition of \(R_D\), we obtain
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb K_\xi}\!\left[
 \{\widehat\tau-\theta_{\mathrm{ker}}(\xi;z_0)\}^2
 \mathrel{\Big|}\mathcal X\right]\le CR_D.               \tag{22}
\]
The last assertion of \texttt{lem:rough-holder-optimal-recovery-geometry} also gives \(R_{X,\mathrm{opt}}\le CR_D\).  This is only an upper certificate and proves no converse in terms of \(R_D\).

Finally take \(P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}\).  Lemma \texttt{lem:panel-fixed-query-kernel-transfer} supplies, for marginal-almost-every admissible query, the labelled point
\[
 \xi_P(z_0)=\bigl(\mathbb K_{P,z_0};\mu_P(z_0),m,
 \Sigma_P(z_0)\bigr)\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0),    \tag{23}
\]
not membership of the bare conditional kernel.  Lemma \texttt{lem:causal-identification} gives
\(\theta_{\mathrm{ker}}(\xi_P(z_0);z_0)=\tau(z_0)\) on the same conull set.  Equations (18), (22), and the nearest-site bound therefore transfer to the causal conditional problem at those query values and at conditional-almost-every exact design.  No regular conditional version is selected at a query-null value, and none of these one-sided upper transfers asserts a panel-induced conditional converse.

%%% FIELD honest-length-frontier-proof
Fix \(z_0\in\mathcal Z_{\delta_0}\) and a labelled point
\(\xi=(\mathbb K_\xi;\mu,m,\Sigma)\in
\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)\).  Since both the treated mean and the untreated regression value lie in \([-B,B]\),
\[
 \theta_{\mathrm{ker}}(\xi;z_0)\in[-2B,2B].                 \tag{1}
\]
We first prove coverage.  On the regular branch, \(r=0\), and selected Gram coercivity makes each local-constant denominator positive.  Its weights are nonnegative and sum to one, so clipping is inactive.  Conditional on the exact design and donors, all selected weights are response-independent.  Let \(w_{st}\) denote the actual fold-averaged weight and define one centered variable per complete unit history by
\[
 \zeta_{si}=\frac{\pi_s}{n_s}\left[
 B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}-\mu_s-
 \sum_{t=1}^{T}w_{st}\{Q_{sti}-m_s(X_{st})\}\right].       \tag{2}
\]
Donor--panel independence and the two equalities in \texttt{ass:design-conditional-centering} center (2).  Assumption \texttt{ass:cross-unit-independence} makes these variables independent across \((s,i)\) under the conditioning; no independence across dates within a history is used.  Their range lengths are at most
\[
 d_{si}=\frac{2B\pi_s}{n_s}
 \left(1+\sum_{t=1}^{T}|w_{st}|\right).                    \tag{3}
\]
For a random variable \(V\in[a,b]\), convexity of the exponential and integration of the chord bound give
\(\mathbb E\exp\{u(V-\mathbb EV)\}\le
\exp\{u^2(b-a)^2/8\}\).  Multiplying this inequality over the independent histories, applying exponential Markov to both tails, and optimizing over \(u>0\) yields
\[
 \Pr_{\mathbb K_\xi}\!\left\{
 \left|\sum_{s,i}\zeta_{si}\right|>r_{\mathrm{unit}}
 \mathrel{\Big|}\mathcal X,(Z_j)_{j=1}^{N}\right\}
 \le\alpha_{\mathrm{st}},                                  \tag{4}
\]
because \(\sum_{s,i}d_{si}^2=4B^2A_w\).  The positive linear term in the definition of \(r_{\mathrm{unit}}\) only enlarges the usual Hoeffding radius.

Write \(q=Sd\).  Spectral clipping gives
\(\widehat\Sigma\succeq(c_\Sigma/2)I_q\).  Each donor fold has size at least \(N/3\), and therefore the deterministic spectral inequality, donor unbiasedness, and \(\Sigma\preceq C_\Sigma I_q\) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb K_\xi}\!\left[
 N\|\widehat\Sigma^{-1/2}(\overline Z_\ell-z_0)\|_2^2
 \right]
 &\le \frac{2N}{c_\Sigma}
       \mathbb E\|\overline Z_\ell-z_0\|_2^2\\
 &\le\frac{6qC_\Sigma}{c_\Sigma}.
\end{aligned}                                               \tag{5}
\]
No independence between the sample mean and sample covariance is used.  Markov's inequality, a union bound over \(K_N\le2\), and the definition
\(q_{\mathrm{don}}^2=32qC_\Sigma/(c_\Sigma\alpha_{\mathrm{don}})\) give
\[
 \Pr\left\{
 \max_{\ell\le K_N}\|\widehat\Sigma^{-1/2}
 (\overline Z_\ell-z_0)\|_2>
 q_{\mathrm{don}}N^{-1/2}\right\}
 \le\alpha_{\mathrm{don}}.                                 \tag{6}
\]
On the complementary event, \(\widehat\Sigma\preceq2C_\Sigma I_q\).  Projection onto the cube is nonexpansive relative to \(z_0\), so
\[
 \max_{\ell\le K_N}\|\widetilde z_\ell-z_0\|_2
 \le(2C_\Sigma)^{1/2}q_{\mathrm{don}}N^{-1/2}.             \tag{7}
\]

For a selected local-constant fit, nonnegative unit-mass weights and the support of \(K\) imply
\[
 \left|\sum_tw_{st}^{(\ell)}m_s(X_{st})-
 m_s(\widetilde z_{\ell s})\right|
 \le C\left\{
 \|\widehat u_{s\ell}-\widetilde z_{\ell s}\|_2^\lambda
 +\widehat h_{s\ell}^\lambda\right\}.                    \tag{8}
\]
Indeed, every observation receiving positive weight is within \(\widehat h_{s\ell}\) of \(\widehat u_{s\ell}\), and Holder continuity plus subadditivity gives (8).  Holder continuity from \(\widetilde z_{\ell s}\) to \(z_{0s}\), equation (7), and the declared calibration of \(C_H\) bound the entire nonstochastic approximation error by \(r_{\mathrm{bias}}+r_{\mathrm{don}}\).  Equations (4), (6), and
\(\alpha_{\mathrm{st}}+\alpha_{\mathrm{don}}=\alpha\) prove coverage on the regular branch.  Every declared exceptional branch returns \([-2B,2B]\), which contains the target by (1); intersecting a covering interval with this known range preserves coverage.  Consequently the asserted liminf coverage holds uniformly.  This argument uses only second moments of donors and bounded whole histories.

We next bound expected length.  On the regular branch,
\(\sum_t|w_{st}|=1\).  Thus, with \(A=\sum_s\pi_s^2/n_s\),
\[
 r_{\mathrm{samp}}\le C(\alpha,B)A^{1/2},                  \tag{9}
\]
where \(\max_s\pi_s/n_s\le A^{1/2}\).  Let
\(\mathcal G=\{h_s^\star<1\text{ for every }s\}\).  On \(\mathcal G\), the pairs \((z_{0s},h_s^\star)\) are feasible.  Certificate optimality and the bandwidth floor therefore give
\[
 r_{\mathrm{bias}}
 \le C\sum_s\pi_s\left\{
 (h_s^\star)^\lambda+K_N^{-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{K_N}
 \|\widetilde z_\ell-z_0\|_2^\lambda\right\}.            \tag{10}
\]
Projection contraction, donor unbiasedness, covariance ellipticity, and Jensen's inequality give
\[
 \mathbb E\|\widetilde z_\ell-z_0\|_2^\lambda
 \le\left(3qC_\Sigma/N\right)^{\lambda/2}.                 \tag{11}
\]
Moreover, since \(0<h_s^\star\le1\),
\[
 \Pr(\mathcal G^c)
 \le\sum_s\Pr(h_s^\star=1)
 \le\sum_s\mathbb E(h_s^\star)^{2\lambda}.               \tag{12}
\]
The interval length is always at most \(4B\).  Apply
\texttt{lem:geometric-mixing-anchor-moment} to (10)--(12), and add the explicit term
\(r_{\mathrm{don}}=C_H(q_{\mathrm{don}}/\sqrt N)^\lambda\).  Uniformly in the fixed query and labelled point,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E\operatorname{length}(C_\alpha)
 \le C\bigg[&A^{1/2}+
 \sum_s\pi_s(Tn_s)^{-\lambda/(2\lambda+d)}+
 \left\{\frac{\log(T\vee2)}{T}\right\}^{\lambda/d}
 +N^{-\lambda/2}\bigg]+CR_\star.
\end{aligned}                                               \tag{13}
\]
To see directly why the smoothing term is redundant, for each \(s\),
\[
 (Tn_s)^{-\lambda/(2\lambda+d)}
 =(n_s^{-1/2})^{2\lambda/(2\lambda+d)}
   (T^{-\lambda/d})^{d/(2\lambda+d)}
 \le n_s^{-1/2}\vee T^{-\lambda/d}.                       \tag{14}
\]
Since \(S\) is fixed,
\(\sum_s\pi_sn_s^{-1/2}\le S^{1/2}A^{1/2}\), and
\(T^{-\lambda/d}\le\{\log(T\vee2)/T\}^{\lambda/d}\).  Hence (13)--(14), together with \(R_\star\le R_\star^{1/2}\) for all sufficiently large indices, prove
\[
 \sup_{z_0\in\mathcal Z_{\delta_0}}
 \sup_{\xi\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)}
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb K_\xi}\operatorname{length}(C_\alpha)
 \le CR_\star^{1/2}.                                       \tag{15}
\]
On the independent-calendar subclass, the anchor lemma replaces the spacing term by \(T^{-\lambda/d}\), and the same algebra applies.

It remains to prove the expected-length converse.  We first record an elementary coverage-to-testing inequality.  Suppose two observed laws \(P_0,P_1\), with targets \(\theta_0,\theta_1\), possess a common subprobability measure \(\nu\) of mass \(\eta>2\alpha\).  If an interval has coverage at least \(1-\alpha-o(1)\) under each law, then
\[
 \nu\{\theta_0,\theta_1\in I_\alpha\}
 \ge\eta-2\alpha-o(1),                                    \tag{16}
\]
because each noncoverage set has \(\nu\)-mass no larger than its probability under the corresponding law.  Every interval on this event has length at least \(|\theta_1-\theta_0|\), whence
\[
 \max_{v=0,1}\mathbb E_v\operatorname{length}(I_\alpha)
 \ge |\theta_1-\theta_0|\{\eta-2\alpha-o(1)\}.           \tag{17}
\]
For arbitrary laws a common measure of mass
\(1-\|P_1-P_0\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\) is obtained by taking
\(\vartheta=P_0+P_1\) and
\[
 d\nu=\min\left\{\frac{dP_0}{d\vartheta},
                    \frac{dP_1}{d\vartheta}\right\}
 d\vartheta.                                                \tag{18}
\]

For the treated channel, fix a query and common design, donor, and untreated laws.  Choose constants \(0<b<b_0<B-b\), put all untreated responses and potential untreated event wealth equal to \(b_0\), and under \(v\in\{-1,1\}\) let the treated wealth take values \(b_0-b,b_0+b\) with upper probability
\[
 \frac12+v\varepsilon_s,
 \qquad
 \varepsilon_s=\frac{\gamma\pi_s}{bn_s\sqrt A}.            \tag{19}
\]
Since \(A\ge\pi_s^2/n_s\), sufficiently small \(\gamma\) makes all probabilities lie in \([1/4,3/4]\).  All wealth levels are positive and can be realized by \texttt{def:buyhold-level} using baseline one, the horizon-zero return equal to wealth minus one, and later returns zero.  The target separation equals
\(4b\sum_s\pi_s\varepsilon_s=4\gamma A^{1/2}\), while the product Hellinger affinity is at least
\(\exp(-C\gamma^2/b^2)\).  Taking \(\gamma\) sufficiently small makes the corresponding common-measure mass exceed \(2\alpha\).  Equation (17) yields the treated lower channel.

For the donor channel, choose \(\eta\in(2\alpha,1)\) and apply \texttt{lem:full-covariance-location-pair}.  Under the two alternatives use a common independent uniform exact design and a common regression in the displaced cohort,
\[
 m_{s_0}(x)=b_0+a_0|x_k-z_{0,s_0k}|^\lambda,               \tag{20}
\]
with constant regressions elsewhere and small positive \(a_0,b_0\).  Set \(Q_{sti}=m_s(X_{st})\), use one common positive treated wealth, and set untreated event wealth to \(m_s(z_s^{(v)})\).  These choices verify support, Holder smoothness, centering, consistency, and transport.  The observed training and treated coordinates coincide; only the donor law changes.  Ellipticity and the root-\(N\) one-coordinate displacement separate the targets by \(cN^{-\lambda/2}\), while the common measure has mass at least \(\eta\).  Equation (17) gives the donor lower channel.  The finite fourth moment supplied by the lemma is a property of this witness and is not used as a model restriction.

For the geometric-mixing spacing channel,

\(z_0=\tfrac12\mathbf1_q\), which is admissible because \(\delta_0\le1/2\).  Use the common donor law
\[
 Z_j=z_0+U_j,                                                \tag{1}

 \mathbb EZ_j=z_0,
 \qquad \operatorname{Var}(Z_j)=I_q,

The covariance is elliptic because \(c_\Sigma\le1\le C_\Sigma\), and the last equality is a construction fact rather than a class assumption.  Take the donors independent of the panel.

\[
 L_T=\left\lfloor
 \frac{\log T-2\log\log(T\vee3)}{|\log\rho_0|}
 \right\rfloor\vee1,
 \qquad
 p_T=\frac{\gamma L_T}{T}.                                 \tag{3}
\]
For all sufficiently large \(T\), \(L_T\asymp\log T\), \(p_T\to0\), and
\[
 2p_T\le\beta_0\rho_0^{L_T-1}.                             \tag{4}
\]

Partition the first cohort's dates into complete consecutive blocks of length \(L_T\), plus one possible incomplete block.  Independently label every complete block by
\(C_b\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p_T)\).  Let \(A_T\) be a cube centered at \(z_{01}\) with Lebesgue volume \(p_T\).  Conditional on \(C_b=1\), draw all design points in block \(b\) independently and uniformly on \(A_T\); conditional on \(C_b=0\), draw them independently and uniformly on \(A_T^c\).  Every date in a complete block has density
\[
 p_T\frac{\mathbf1_{A_T}(x)}{p_T}
 +(1-p_T)\frac{\mathbf1_{A_T^c}(x)}{1-p_T}=1               \tag{5}
\]
for Lebesgue-almost every \(x\).  Draw the incomplete block and every other cohort independently and uniformly on the cube.

For a lag \(k<L_T\), the past and future sigma-fields can share at most one block label.  Replacing that future-side label by an independent copy changes their joint law with probability at most \(2p_T(1-p_T)\le2p_T\).  For \(k\ge L_T\), no block label is shared and the fields are independent.  By the total-variation definition of absolute regularity and (4),
\[
 \beta_1(k\mid z_0)\le\beta_0\rho_0^k
 \quad(k\ge1),                                             \tag{6}
\]
and the other cohorts have zero beta-mixing coefficients.  Thus the frozen geometric envelope holds.

Let \(A_T'\) be a fixed-ratio smaller concentric cube and let \(E_T\) be the event that every complete-block label is zero and that no incomplete-block point falls in \(A_T'\).  The incomplete-block failure probability is at most \(L_T|A_T'|=O(L_Tp_T)=o(1)\), while
\[
 \Pr(E_T)=(1-p_T)^{\lfloor T/L_T\rfloor}-o(1)
 \longrightarrow e^{-\gamma}.                              \tag{7}
\]
Hence, with
\(\eta_\alpha=(e^{-\gamma}+2\alpha)/2>2\alpha\), the event has probability at least \(\eta_\alpha\) for all sufficiently large \(T\).

\[
 m_{10}(x)=b_0,
 \qquad
 m_{11}(x)=b_0+ah_T^\lambda
 \phi\!\left(\frac{x-z_{01}}{h_T}\right),                 \tag{8}
\]
and keep every other regression equal to \(b_0\).  A derivative of order \(j\le\lfloor\lambda\rfloor\) in the added term is bounded by a constant times \(h_T^{\lambda-j}\), and its top derivative has a uniformly bounded Holder seminorm.  Thus both surfaces belong to \(\mathcal H_d(\lambda,L)\); reducing \(a,b_0\) enforces the wealth bound and positivity.

Under alternative \(v\), set \(Q_{1ti}=m_{1v}(X_{1t})\), use the common constant response in other cohorts, and use the same positive constant treated observation for every replica.  Set treated potential wealth equal to that observation and untreated event wealth equal to \(m_{sv}(z_{0s})\).  Baseline one, horizon-zero return equal to wealth minus one, and later returns zero realize these levels with returns above \(-1\).  All support, density, Holder, mixing, centering, cross-unit independence, consistency, transport, and donor clauses have therefore been verified, so both laws belong to \(\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}\).

 |\tau_1(z_0)-\tau_0(z_0)|
 =\pi_1ah_T^\lambda
 \asymp\left\{\frac{\log(T\vee2)}{T}\right\}^{\lambda/d}. \tag{9}

This proves the required clustered-design witness and its common-measure certificate.

Fix \(s\), \(z_0\in\mathcal Z_{\delta_0}\), and
\(\xi\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)\).  All probabilities in this proof are under \(\mathbb K_\xi\).  Since \(0<\lambda\le1\), the order convention gives \(r=0\), so \(G_{sT}\) is scalar.  For \(b\in(0,1]\), let
\[
 M_b=\sum_{t=1}^{T}
 \mathbf1\{\|X_{st}-z_{0s}\|_2\le\eta_Kb\}.               \tag{1}
\]
The ball in (1) is contained in \([0,1]^d\) because
\(\eta_K\le\delta_0\) and the query is \(\delta_0\)-interior.  The density lower bound therefore gives, uniformly in \(t\),
\[
 p_{tb}:=\Pr\{\|X_{st}-z_{0s}\|_2\le\eta_Kb\}
 \ge c_Xv_d\eta_K^db^d=:c_pb^d.                            \tag{2}

The declared kernel lower bound implies

 G_{sT}(z_{0s},b)
 \ge\frac{k_KM_b}{Tb^d}.                                   \tag{3}
\]
In particular, on \(M_b\ge c_pTb^d/4\),

 G_{sT}(z_{0s},b)\ge\frac14k_Kc_Xv_d\eta_K^d>c_G,          \tag{4}
\]
where the strict final inequality is exactly the frozen compatibility condition.  This is the step for which that precise threshold is needed.

\[
 a_T=\left\lceil\frac{3\log(T\vee2)}{|\log\rho_0|}\right\rceil. \tag{5}
\]
Partition the dates into consecutive blocks of length \(a_T\), and, for each parity, retain alternating complete blocks.  Retained blocks of a fixed parity are separated by at least one complete block.  By the total-variation definition of beta mixing, successively replacing each retained block's conditional law by its marginal law changes the joint law by at most the sum of the intervening beta coefficients.  Hence, simultaneously for all statistics of the retained blocks, its total variation distance from the corresponding independent product-block law is at most
\[
 \left\lceil\frac{T}{a_T}\right\rceil
 \beta_0\rho_0^{a_T}\le CT^{-2}.                           \tag{6}
\]
This comparison involves only the design sequence.

\[
 \mathbb Ee^{-Y_j/a_T}
 \le1-\frac{1-e^{-1}}{a_T}\mathbb EY_j
 \le\exp(-c b^d).                                          \tag{7}
\]
Exponential Markov applied to each parity under the product law, followed by (6), yields constants \(c_1,C_1>0\) such that

 \Pr\{M_b<c_pTb^d/4\}
 \le C_1\exp(-c_1Tb^d/a_T)+C_1T^{-2}                       \tag{8}
\]
whenever \(Tb^d\ge C_1a_T\).  Enlarging \(C_1\) makes (8) trivially valid below that scale.  Combining (4) and (8) controls Gram failure.

\[
 b_\circ=(Tn_s)^{-1/(2\lambda+d)}\vee N^{-1/2}
 \vee(a_T/T)^{1/d}.                                        \tag{9}
\]
For every scale between \(b_\circ\) and one, the least dyadic grid point not below that scale is at most twice the scale.  Couple the retained-block design once to its independent comparison array, and on that single coupling evaluate all dyadic visit counts.  \Pr\{h_s^\star>C2^jb_\circ\}
 \le C\exp(-c2^{jd})+CT^{-2}                               \tag{10}
\]
until the candidate scale reaches one.  Summation of the dyadic tail, using \(0<h_s^\star\le1\), gives
\[
 \mathbb E(h_s^\star)^{2\lambda}
 \le Cb_\circ^{2\lambda}+CT^{-2}.                         \tag{11}
\]
If \(b_\circ\) is bounded away from zero, (11) follows directly after increasing \(C\).  Since \(a_T\le C\log(T\vee2)\) and \(2\lambda/d\le2\), the last term in (11) is absorbed by the spacing term, and (9)--(11) imply
\[
 \mathbb E(h_s^\star)^{2\lambda}
 \le C\left\{(Tn_s)^{-2\lambda/(2\lambda+d)}+N^{-\lambda}
 +\left(\frac{\log(T\vee2)}{T}\right)^{2\lambda/d}\right\}. \tag{12}
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz gives the corresponding first-moment bound
\[
 \mathbb E(h_s^\star)^\lambda
 \le C\left\{(Tn_s)^{-\lambda/(2\lambda+d)}+N^{-\lambda/2}
 +\left(\frac{\log(T\vee2)}{T}\right)^{\lambda/d}\right\}. \tag{13}

\(\Pr\{G_{sT}(z_{0s},1)<c_G\}\le CT^{-2}\).  If this failure occurs for no cohort, then \((z_{0s},1)\) is in every fold--cohort anchor set, so fallback is impossible.  A union bound over fixed \(S\) proves

 \sup_{\xi\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)}
 \Pr_{\mathbb K_\xi}(\mathsf F_{\mathrm{fb}})\le CT^{-2}. \tag{14}
\]
All constants are uniform because the conditional density and mixing envelopes are uniform.  On
\(\mathcal M^{\mathrm{iid}}_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)\), use single-date blocks, omit (6), and replace \((a_T/T)^{1/d}\) in (9) by \(T^{-1/d}\). Finally, \texttt{lem:panel-fixed-query-kernel-transfer} places \(\xi_P(z_0)\) in the fixed-query class for marginal-almost-every admissible query of each panel law, proving the stated one-sided transfer.

Put \(q=Sd\), fix \(\eta\in(0,1)\) and \(\epsilon_4>0\), and choose a signed coordinate vector \(e\) pointing toward the center of its coordinate interval.  Define
\[
 w=\frac{\Sigma^{-1/2}e}{\|\Sigma^{-1/2}e\|_2},            \tag{1}
\]
and let \(O\) be an orthogonal matrix whose first column is \(w\).  Choose
\(\varepsilon\in(0,\epsilon_4/2)\), put
\(a=(1+\varepsilon)^{1/2}>1\), and, for \(|u|\) in a sufficiently small fixed neighborhood of zero, define a scalar variable \(A_u\in\{-a,0,a\}\) by
\[
\begin{aligned}
 p_+(u)&=\frac{1+u^2}{2a^2}+\frac{u}{2a},\\
 p_-(u)&=\frac{1+u^2}{2a^2}-\frac{u}{2a},\\
 p_0(u)&=1-\frac{1+u^2}{a^2}.
\end{aligned}                                               \tag{2}
\]
Because \(a>1\), all three expressions are positive after the neighborhood is reduced.  Direct summation gives
\[
 \mathbb EA_u=u,
 \qquad \mathbb EA_u^2=1+u^2,
 \qquad \operatorname{Var}(A_u)=1.                         \tag{3}
\]
Let \(R_2,\ldots,R_q\) be independent Rademacher variables, independent of \(A_u\), with the empty collection understood when \(q=1\), and put
\[
 V_u=O(A_u,R_2,\ldots,R_q)^\top.                           \tag{4}
\]
Equations (1), (3), and orthogonality show
\[
 \mathbb EV_u=uw,
 \qquad \operatorname{Var}(V_u)=I_q.                       \tag{5}
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \mathbb E\|V_u-uw\|_2^4
 =\mathbb E(A_u-u)^4+2(q-1)+(q-1)^2.                       \tag{6}
\]
At \(u=0\), the right side is
\[
 a^2+2(q-1)+(q-1)^2=q^2+\varepsilon<q^2+\epsilon_4.        \tag{7}
\]
It is continuous in \(u\), so after a further fixed reduction of the neighborhood,
\[
 \mathbb E\|V_u-uw\|_2^4\le q^2+\epsilon_4.               \tag{8}
\]

Let \(G_u\) be the law of
\[
 Z_u=z_0+\Sigma^{1/2}V_u.                                  \tag{9}
\]
Its support is finite.  By (1), (5), and (8),
\[
 z(u):=\mathbb EZ_u
 =z_0+\frac{u}{\|\Sigma^{-1/2}e\|_2}e,
 \qquad \operatorname{Var}(Z_u)=\Sigma,                   \tag{10}
\]
and
\[
 \mathbb E\|\Sigma^{-1/2}(Z_u-z(u))\|_2^4
 \le q^2+\epsilon_4.                                      \tag{11}
\]

It remains to control testing distance.  Because the same finite support labels occur in (2), the Hellinger affinity between \(G_0\) and \(G_u\) is
\[
 a_H(u)=\sum_{r\in\{-,0,+\}}\{p_r(0)p_r(u)\}^{1/2}.       \tag{12}
\]
All probabilities are bounded away from zero on the chosen neighborhood, so \(a_H''\) is bounded there.  Also
\(a_H(0)=1\) and
\(a_H'(0)=\tfrac12\sum_rp_r'(0)=0\).  Taylor's theorem therefore gives
\[
 a_H(u)\ge1-Cu^2.                                          \tag{13}
\]
Affinity factorizes under products.  If \(CNu^2\le1/2\), then
\[
 a_H(u)^N\ge(1-Cu^2)^N\ge e^{-2CNu^2}.                    \tag{14}
\]
For dominated probability laws \(P,Q\), Cauchy--Schwarz applied to
\(|p-q|=|\sqrt p-\sqrt q|(\sqrt p+\sqrt q)\) proves
\[
 \|P-Q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le\left\{1-\left(\int\sqrt{dP,dQ}\right)^2\right\}^{1/2}. \tag{15}
\]
Choose \(c_\eta>0\) sufficiently small that (14)--(15), with
\(u=c_\eta N^{-1/2}\), give
\[
 \|G_u^{\otimes N}-G_0^{\otimes N}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le1-\eta.                                                 \tag{16}
\]
Increase \(N_\eta\) so that this \(u\) lies in the probability and moment neighborhood for all \(N\ge N_\eta\).  Equation (10) gives the exact identity
\[
 N\|\Sigma^{-1/2}\{z(u)-z_0\}\|_2^2=c_\eta^2.           \tag{17}
\]
Finally,
\(\|\Sigma^{-1/2}e\|_2^{-1}\le C_\Sigma^{1/2}\).  Because \(\delta_0<1/2\), moving the selected coordinate toward \(1/2\) by at most \(1/2-\delta_0\) preserves the full interior restriction.  Reduce \(c_\eta\), and if necessary enlarge \(N_\eta\), so this holds.  Taking \(G_0=G_{u=0}\) and \(G_1=G_{u=c_\eta N^{-1/2}}\) proves every assertion, including exact full covariance and the arbitrary positive fourth-moment slack.

Fix \(P\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}\), choose a regular conditional law
\(\mathbb K_{P,z_0}=P(\,\cdot\mid z_0)\), and define
\[
 \mu_{P,s}(z_0)=\mathbb E_P[B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid z_0],
 \qquad
 \Sigma_P(z_0)=\operatorname{Var}_P(Z_j\mid z_0).           \tag{1}
\]
Bounded wealth makes the first quantity finite.  Assumption
\texttt{ass:design-conditional-centering} states that its value is common across replicas \(i\), and disintegration gives
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb K_{P,z_0}}B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}
 =\mu_{P,s}(z_0).                                           \tag{2}
\]
Assumption \texttt{ass:consistency} disintegrates to
\(B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}=B_{si}(1)\) almost surely under the kernel, while \texttt{ass:event-date-transport} gives
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb K_{P,z_0}}B_{si}(0)=m_s(z_{0s}).       \tag{3}
\]
Thus (2)--(3) verify the mean-label, consistency, and transport clauses of \texttt{def:fixed-query-kernel-class}.

Let \(\mathcal X=\sigma((X_{uv})_{u,v})\).  The conditional-mean restrictions in \texttt{ass:design-conditional-centering} become, under the regular conditional law,
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb K_{P,z_0}}[Q_{sti}\mid\mathcal X]
 =m_s(X_{st}),
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb K_{P,z_0}}
 [B_{si}^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid\mathcal X]
 =\mu_{P,s}(z_0).                                           \tag{4}
\]
These are mean restrictions and impose neither identical replica laws nor independence between the event and training coordinates within a unit history.  Assumption \texttt{ass:cross-unit-independence} supplies conditional independence of the histories across \((s,i)\) given \(\mathcal X\) under the kernel.

The boundedness, exact-observation, and interior assertions disintegrate to kernel-almost-sure assertions outside a marginally null set of queries.  Holder membership is a property of the retained regression labels.  Assumptions \texttt{ass:design-density} and \texttt{ass:temporal-mixing} give, on a marginally conull set,
\[
 c_X\le f_{st\mid z_0}(x)\le C_X
 \quad\text{for Lebesgue-almost every }x,
 \qquad
 \beta_s(k\mid z_0)\le\beta_0\rho_0^k                    \tag{5}
\]
for every cohort, date, and integer \(k\ge1\).

Assumptions \texttt{ass:donor-independence} and
\texttt{ass:donor-training-separation} give, under the kernel, independent and identically distributed donors independent of the complete joint panel.  Assumptions \texttt{ass:donor-unbiasedness} and \texttt{ass:donor-ellipticity}, together with (1), give
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb K_{P,z_0}}Z_j=z_0,
 \qquad
 c_\Sigma I_{Sd}\preceq\Sigma_P(z_0)
 \preceq C_\Sigma I_{Sd}.                                  \tag{6}
\]
No donor fourth-moment statement is required by, or inferred for, the class.

There are finitely many cohorts, dates, and replicas and countably many lags.  Intersecting the conull query sets supporting (2)--(6) and the disintegrated support assertions therefore leaves a marginally conull set.  At every query in that set, the labelled tuple
\[
 \xi_P(z_0)=\bigl(\mathbb K_{P,z_0};\mu_P(z_0),m,
 \Sigma_P(z_0)\bigr)                                       \tag{7}
\]
satisfies every member property in \texttt{def:fixed-query-kernel-class}.  Hence
\[
 \xi_P(z_0)\in\mathcal M_{N,T,\mathbf n}(z_0)              \tag{8}
\]
for marginal-almost-every admissible query.  Equation (8) concerns the labelled tuple, not the bare conditional law, and nothing is asserted about an arbitrarily selected version on the complementary query-null set.
