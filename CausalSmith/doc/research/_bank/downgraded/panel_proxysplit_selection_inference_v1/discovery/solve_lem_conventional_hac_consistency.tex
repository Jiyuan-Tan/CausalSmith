Fix \(s\in\mathcal S\).  Because the two partitions are predeclared, \(d_s\) is fixed.  We first record the moment and covariance bounds that will be used for both score sequences.  By \(\texttt{ass:bridge-rank}\),
\[
 \lVert M_s^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq \kappa^{-1}.
\]
The Cauchy--Schwarz inequality and \(\texttt{ass:moment-bound}\) therefore give, uniformly in the row law,
\[
 \lVert\alpha_s\rVert_2
 \leq \kappa^{-1}\lVert\mathbb E_n[Z_{s,t}Y_t(0)]\rVert_2\leq C,
 \qquad
 \lVert\mu_s\rVert_2\leq C,
 \qquad
 \lVert H_s\rVert_2\leq C.
\]
Consequently, for some \(\epsilon>0\), which may be chosen no larger than \(\delta_0/2\),
\[
 \sup_n\mathbb E_n|\psi_{s,t}|^{4+\epsilon}leq C,
 \qquad
 \sup_n\mathbb E_n|v_{s,t}|^{4+\epsilon}\leq C.                                      \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, \(\psi_{s,t}=H_sZ_{s,t}\{Y_t(0)-W_{D_s,t}^{\prime}\alpha_s\}\) is a product of two linear functions of \(X_t\), while \(v_{s,t}\) is a linear function of \(X_t\) plus the bounded constants \(\alpha_s\) and \(\tau=\mathbb E_n\Delta_t\); the asserted bounds thus follow from the \((8+\delta_0)\)-moment bound and H\"older's inequality.

Both scores are centered.  For the pre-period score, the definition of \(\alpha_s\) and invertibility of \(M_s\) imply
\[
 \mathbb E_n u_{s,t}
 =\mathbb E_n[Z_{s,t}Y_t(0)]-M_s\alpha_s=0,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_n\psi_{s,t}=H_s\mathbb E_nu_{s,t}=0.                                    \tag{2}
\]
For the post-period score, \(\texttt{ass:bridge-centering}\) and the definition of \(\tau\) imply
\[
 \mathbb E_nv_{s,t}
 =\mathbb E_n\{Y_t(0)-W_{D_s,t}^{\prime}\alpha_s\}
  +\mathbb E_n\Delta_t-\tau=0.                                                       \tag{3}
\]

We next give a self-contained covariance consequence of the mixing and moment assumptions.  If two random variables measurable with respect to time blocks separated by \(h\) have uniformly bounded \((2+\eta)\)-moments, truncate both at a level \(K\).  The definition of the absolute-regularity coefficient bounds the covariance of the bounded truncated variables by \(4K^2\beta_n(h)\); H\"older's and Markov's inequalities bound all truncation terms by \(CK^{-\eta}\).  Choosing \(K\) as a suitable positive exponential function of \(h\), and using \(\texttt{ass:geometric-beta-mixing}\), yields constants \(C,c>0\), independent of \(n\), such that
\[
 |\operatorname{Cov}_{P_n}(U,V)|\leq Ce^{-ch}.                                      \tag{4}
\]
Applying (4) with (1) first to either \(w_t=\psi_{s,t}\) or \(w_t=v_{s,t}\), and then to products of two such scores, gives
\[
 |\gamma_{w,n}(j)|
 :=|\operatorname{Cov}_{P_n}(w_t,w_{t-j})|\leq Ce^{-c|j|}.                           \tag{5}
\]
Strict stationarity in \(\texttt{ass:stationarity}\) makes this covariance independent of \(t\).

Consider a consecutive block of length \(N\), and define, for \(0\leq j<N\),
\[
 \widetilde\gamma_N(j)=\frac1N\sum_{t=j+1}^{N}w_tw_{t-j}.
\]
For fixed \(j\), put \(U_{t,j}=w_tw_{t-j}\).  By (1), \(\sup_{n,t,j}\mathbb E_n|U_{t,j}|^{2+\epsilon/2}<\infty\).  Among the ordered pairs \((t,u)\), at most \(C N(j+1)\) have overlapping or adjacent supports \(\{t-j,t\}\) and \(\{u-j,u\}\); their covariances are bounded by Cauchy--Schwarz and (1).  For every other pair, (4) bounds the covariance by \(Ce^{-c(|t-u|-j)}\).  Summing by the separation \(|t-u|-j\) gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_{P_n}\{\widetilde\gamma_N(j)\}
 \leq \frac{C(j+1)}{N}.                                                             \tag{6}
\]
Also, (2)--(5) imply \(\operatorname{Var}_{P_n}(N^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^Nw_t)\leq C/N\), and hence
\[
 \overline w_N:=N^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^Nw_t=O_{P_n}(N^{-1/2}).                            \tag{7}
\]

Let \(q<N\) and let \(\widetilde\Omega_{w,N}(q)\) be the uncentered Bartlett sum formed from the \(\widetilde\gamma_N(j)\)'s.  From (6), the triangle inequality, and Cauchy--Schwarz,
\[
 \mathbb E_n\left|
 \widetilde\Omega_{w,N}(q)-\mathbb E_n\widetilde\Omega_{w,N}(q)
 \right|
 \leq C\sum_{j=0}^q\sqrt{\frac{j+1}{N}}
 \leq C\frac{q^{3/2}}{\sqrt N}.                                                     \tag{8}
\]
Because \(\mathbb E_n\widetilde\gamma_N(j)=(1-j/N)\gamma_{w,n}(j)\), (5) gives the deterministic bound
\[
 \left|
 \mathbb E_n\widetilde\Omega_{w,N}(q)
 -\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}\gamma_{w,n}(j)
 \right|
 \leq C\left(e^{-cq}+\frac1q+\frac1N\right).                                      \tag{9}
\]
To account for empirical centering, write every Bartlett sum as \(N^{-1}x^{\prime}A_qx\), where the symmetric band matrix \(A_q\) has absolute row sums at most \(2q+1\).  Replacing \(x=(w_1,\ldots,w_N)^{\prime}\) by \(x-\overline w_N\mathbf 1\), using (7), \(N^{-1}\lVert x\rVert_2^2=O_{P_n}(1)\), and \(\lVert A_q\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq2q+1\), changes the quadratic form by \(O_{P_n}(qN^{-1/2})\).  Combining this fact with (8)--(9), the oracle centered Bartlett estimator obeys
\[
 \widehat\Omega^{\mathrm{or}}_{w,N}(q)
 -\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}\gamma_{w,n}(j)
 =O_{P_n}\left(
   \frac{q^{3/2}}{\sqrt N}+\frac q{\sqrt N}
   +\frac1q+e^{-cq}+\frac1N
 \right).                                                                           \tag{10}
\]

Take \(q=q_n=\lfloor\min(n,m)^{1/5}\rfloor\).  By \(\texttt{ass:sample-ratio}\), \(n\) and \(m\) are asymptotically comparable, so every term on the right of (10) tends to zero both for \((w,N)=(\psi_{s,\cdot},n)\) and for \((w,N)=(v_{s,\cdot},m)\).  The corresponding entries of the long-run covariance matrix in \(\texttt{ass:covariance-limit}\), together with the definitions of \(\Omega_{\psi,s}\) and \(\Omega_{v,s}\), therefore give
\[
 \widehat\Omega^{\mathrm{or}}_{\psi,s}\to_{P_n}\Omega_{\psi,s},
 \qquad
 \widehat\Omega^{\mathrm{or}}_{v,s}\to_{P_n}\Omega_{v,s}.                        \tag{11}
\]

It remains to show that fitting the scores in \(\texttt{def:conventional-selected-att}\) does not alter (11).  The same covariance summation used for (7), applied coordinatewise in the fixed-dimensional moment matrices and means, yields
\[
 \widehat M_s-M_s=O_{P_n}(n^{-1/2}),
 \qquad
 \widehat\mu_s^{\mathrm{post}}-\mu_s=O_{P_n}(m^{-1/2}).                            \tag{12}
\]
On the event \(\lVert\widehat M_s-M_s\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\kappa/2\), Weyl's inequality gives \(\sigma_{\min}(\widehat M_s)\geq\kappa/2\).  This event has probability tending to one by (12), and on it the resolvent identity gives
\[
 \widehat M_s^{-1}-M_s^{-1}
 =-\widehat M_s^{-1}(\widehat M_s-M_s)M_s^{-1}
 =O_{P_n}(n^{-1/2}).                                                                 \tag{13}
\]
Thus all inverse fallback events in the definition have probability tending to zero.  Equations (12)--(13), \(n/m\to r\), and the definitions of \(H_s\) and \(\widehat H_s\) imply
\[
 \widehat H_s-H_s
 =-(\widehat\mu_s^{\mathrm{post}}-\mu_s)^{\prime}\widehat M_s^{-1}
   -\mu_s^{\prime}(\widehat M_s^{-1}-M_s^{-1})
 =O_{P_n}(n^{-1/2}).                                                                 \tag{14}
\]
Moreover, \(\texttt{lem:fixed-role-linearization}\) gives
\[
 \widehat\alpha_s-\alpha_s=O_{P_n}(n^{-1/2}),
 \qquad
 \widehat\tau_s-\tau=O_{P_n}(n^{-1/2}),                                            \tag{15}
\]
where the second rate uses \(n/m\to r\) and tightness of the two normalized score sums in that lemma.

On the probability-tending-to-one inverse event, expand
\[
 \widehat\psi_{s,t}-\psi_{s,t}
 =(\widehat H_s-H_s)u_{s,t}
  -\widehat H_sZ_{s,t}W_{D_s,t}^{\prime}(\widehat\alpha_s-\alpha_s).                \tag{16}
\]
The empirical second moments of \(u_{s,t}\) and \(Z_{s,t}W_{D_s,t}^{\prime}\) are tight by \(\texttt{ass:moment-bound}\), stationarity, and the variance argument leading to (7).  Hence (14)--(16) yield
\[
 \left\{\frac1n\sum_{t=1}^n
   (\widehat\psi_{s,t}-\psi_{s,t})^2\right\}^{1/2}
 =O_{P_n}(n^{-1/2}).                                                                 \tag{17}
\]
Similarly, on the post block,
\[
 \widehat v_{s,t}-v_{s,t}
 =-W_{D_s,t}^{\prime}(\widehat\alpha_s-\alpha_s)
   -(\widehat\tau_s-\tau),
\]
so (15) and the tight empirical second moment of \(W_{D_s,t}\) give
\[
 \left\{\frac1m\sum_{t=n+1}^{n+m}
   (\widehat v_{s,t}-v_{s,t})^2\right\}^{1/2}
 =O_{P_n}(n^{-1/2}).                                                                 \tag{18}
\]

For any two length-\(N\) score vectors \(w\) and \(w+e\), the same Bartlett-matrix representation and the contraction property of empirical centering imply
\[
 |\widehat\Omega_{w+e,N}(q)-\widehat\Omega_{w,N}(q)|
 \leq(2q+1)\left[
  2\left\{N^{-1}\sum_tw_t^2\right\}^{1/2}
    \left\{N^{-1}\sum_te_t^2\right\}^{1/2}
  +N^{-1}\sum_te_t^2
 \right].                                                                           \tag{19}
\]
Apply (19) with (17) and (18).  Because \(q_n n^{-1/2}\to0\), and because \(n/m\to r\), fitted-score replacement is \(o_{P_n}(1)\) on both blocks.  The vanishing-probability fallback events do not affect convergence in probability.  Combining this conclusion with (11) proves
\[
 \widehat\Omega_{\psi,s}\to_{P_n}\Omega_{\psi,s},
 \qquad
 \widehat\Omega_{v,s}\to_{P_n}\Omega_{v,s}.                                       \tag{20}
\]

Finally, \(\texttt{ass:sample-ratio}\), (20), and the definition of \(V_s\) imply
\[
 \widehat V_s^{\mathrm{conv}}
 =\widehat\Omega_{\psi,s}+\frac nm\widehat\Omega_{v,s}
 \to_{P_n}\Omega_{\psi,s}+r\Omega_{v,s}=V_s.                                     \tag{21}
\]
The argument holds for each of the two roles and does not invert, or require nonsingularity of, their stacked covariance matrix.  Since \(\texttt{ass:att-variance-margin}\) states \(V_s\geq\nu\), the union bound and (21) give
\[
 \Pr_{P_n}\left\{\min_{s\in\mathcal S}\widehat V_s^{\mathrm{conv}}\geq\nu/2\right\}
 \geq 1-\sum_{s=1}^2
 \Pr_{P_n}\left\{|\widehat V_s^{\mathrm{conv}}-V_s|>\nu/2\right\}
 \longrightarrow1.
\]
This proves all three asserted consistency statements and the asserted asymptotic positivity.
