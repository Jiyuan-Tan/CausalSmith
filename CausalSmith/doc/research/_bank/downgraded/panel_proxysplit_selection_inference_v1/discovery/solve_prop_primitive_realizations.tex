Put
\[
 e_{0t}^{\star}=e_{0t}+\xi_t,
 \qquad
 \bar b=\operatorname{Var}(e_{0t}^{\star})=b+\zeta.
\]
By \(\texttt{def:primitive-gaussian-family}\), the four random variables \(F_t,e_{0t}^{\star},e_{1t},e_{2t}\) are mutually independent, apart from the nonzero mean \(\mathbb E_nF_t=\mu\), and
\[
 Y_t(0)=F_t+e_{0t}^{\star},
 \qquad
 \Delta_t=\tau.
\]
Consequently the causal estimand agrees with the constant treatment effect as a derived equality:
\[
 \mathbb E_n\Delta_t=\mathbb E_n\tau=\tau.
\]
All primitive coordinates are Gaussian and independent over \(t\).  Thus every row is strictly stationary, has finite moments of every fixed order, and has absolute-regularity coefficients \(\beta_n(j)=0\) for every \(j\geq1\).  Choose any integer sequence \(m=m_n\) such that \(n/m_n\to r\); this verifies the required positive sample-ratio limit.

We first establish both proximal bridges.  Write
\[
 B=\mathbb E_nF_t^2=\mu^2+f.
\]
For role one, the donor is \(W_{1t}=c_1(F_t+e_{1t})\) and the supplemental proxy is \(W_{2t}=c_{2,n}(F_t+e_{2t})\); role two exchanges them.  Independence and centering of the measurement errors imply, in either role,
\[
 M_s=\mathbb E_n(Z_{s,t}W_{D_s,t})=c_1c_{2,n}B.
\]
They also imply
\[
 \mathbb E_n\{W_{2t}Y_t(0)\}=c_{2,n}B,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_n\{W_{1t}Y_t(0)\}=c_1B.
\]
Because \(B>0\), \(c_1>0\), and \(c_{2,n}>0\) eventually, the scalar bridge matrices are invertible.  The definition of \(\alpha_s\) therefore gives
\[
 \alpha_1=\frac{c_{2,n}B}{c_1c_{2,n}B}=\frac1{c_1},
 \qquad
 \alpha_2=\frac{c_1B}{c_1c_{2,n}B}=\frac1{c_{2,n}}.
\]
It follows pointwise that
\[
 Y_t(0)-W_{1t}\alpha_1=e_{0t}^{\star}-e_{1t},
 \qquad
 Y_t(0)-W_{2t}\alpha_2=e_{0t}^{\star}-e_{2t}.
\]
Both residuals have expectation zero, proving \(\texttt{ass:bridge-centering}\) for both roles.  Moreover, for \(j\in\{1,2\}\),
\[
 \mathbb E_n\{Y_t(0)+\Delta_t-W_{jt}\alpha_j\}
 =\tau+\mathbb E_n(e_{0t}^{\star}-e_{jt})
 =\tau.
\]
Thus the observed post-period residual identifies the causal target as a consequence of the bridge calculation and the constant-effect construction, not by definition.  At the tied limit the scalar singular value is
\[
 |M_s|=c_1c_2B=\frac{B}{\sqrt{t_1t_2}}.
\]

We next derive the local ridge-loss calibration.  For a generic donor \(W_j=c_j(F+e_j)\), let \(q_j=\operatorname{Var}(e_j)\), so \(q_1=x\), \(q_2=y\), and \(c_j^2=t_j^{-1}\).  The scalar population ridge normal equation is
\[
 \{1+\mathbb E_n(W_j^2)\}a_j=\mathbb E_n\{W_jY_t(0)\}.
\]
Since
\[
 \mathbb E_n(W_j^2)=c_j^2(B+q_j),
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_n\{W_jY_t(0)\}=c_jB,
\]
we obtain
\[
 a_j=\frac{c_jB}{1+c_j^2(B+q_j)},
 \qquad
 W_ja_j=\frac{B}{t_j+B+q_j}(F+e_j).
\]
Completion of the scalar square in the ridge objective gives its minimized value:
\[
 L_j
 =\mathbb E_n\{Y_t(0)^2\}
  -\frac{\{\mathbb E_n(W_jY_t(0))\}^2}{1+\mathbb E_n(W_j^2)}
 =\mathbb E_n\{Y_t(0)^2\}-\frac{B^2}{t_j+B+q_j}.
\]
The tied-manifold equality \(t_2=t_1+x-y\) implies
\[
 t_1+B+x=t_2+B+y=R,
\]
so \(L_1=L_2\), and both ridge predictions have the common loading \(\eta=B/R\).  Along the local path in \(\texttt{def:primitive-gaussian-family}\), set
\[
 \delta_n=\frac{R^2}{B^2}\frac d{\sqrt n},
 \qquad
 t_{2,n}=t_2-\delta_n.
\]
Because \(t_2>0\) and \(\delta_n\to0\), one has \(t_{2,n}>0\) eventually.  The displayed loss formula then yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 L_1-L_2
 &=\frac{B^2}{R-\delta_n}-\frac{B^2}{R}\\
 &=\frac{B^2\delta_n}{R(R-\delta_n)}\\
 &=\frac d{\sqrt n}\frac{R}{R-\delta_n}
 =\frac d{\sqrt n}+O(n^{-1}).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Therefore
\[
 \sqrt n(L_1-L_2)=d+O(n^{-1/2}),
\]
which proves \(\texttt{ass:local-loss-gap}\) for the constructed array while preserving both bridge identities.
All assertions here are tail assertions for a triangular array.  Choose \(n_0\) so that the local path is positive and lies in the strict moment, rank, and variance neighborhood for every \(n\geq n_0\); define the finitely many rows \(n<n_0\) by the tied law.  This finite extension leaves every stated limit unchanged and makes every row a well-defined stationary independent-over-time panel satisfying the same fixed bounds.

We now calculate the limiting covariance map rather than importing the formulas from the construction.  At the tied limit define
\[
 U_0=(1-\eta)F+e_0^{\star}.
\]
The two ridge residuals are \(U_0-\eta e_1\) and \(U_0-\eta e_2\).  Since the ridge coefficients satisfy \(a_j=\eta\sqrt{t_j}\), the ridge-penalty difference is \(\eta^2(t_1-t_2)=\eta^2(y-x)\).  Hence the centered one-period loss contrast is
\[
 \delta\ell_t
 =-2\eta U_0(e_1-e_2)
  +\eta^2(e_1^2-e_2^2+y-x).
\]
The donor means equal \(c_j\mu\).  Substitution into the definitions of \(H_s\), \(u_{s,t}\), and \(\psi_{s,t}\) gives
\[
 \psi_{1,t}=-K(F_t+e_{2t})(e_{0t}^{\star}-e_{1t}),
 \qquad
 \psi_{2,t}=-K(F_t+e_{1t})(e_{0t}^{\star}-e_{2t}),
 \qquad
 K=\frac\mu B.
\]
Constant treatment and the bridge identities give the post-period residuals
\[
 v_{1,t}=e_{0t}^{\star}-e_{1t},
 \qquad
 v_{2,t}=e_{0t}^{\star}-e_{2t}.
\]
Every coordinate of the stacked one-period score is a polynomial of degree at most two in Gaussian variables and therefore has a finite third absolute moment.  For any fixed coefficient vector \(a\), Taylor expansion of the characteristic function of the centered scalar score \(G_t\) gives
\[
 \mathbb E_n\exp\!\left(\frac{\mathrm i a^{\prime}G_t}{\sqrt n}\right)
 =1-\frac{a^{\prime}\Sigma_na}{2n}+O(n^{-3/2}),
\]
where \(\Sigma_n=\operatorname{Var}_{P_n}(G_t)\) converges to its tied value.  Independence over time and exponentiation to the \(n\)-th power yield the limit \(\exp(-a^{\prime}\Sigma a/2)\).  The same argument applies to the post-period sum, and the disjoint pre- and post-period samples are independent.  Thus the asserted centered joint Gaussian coordinates exist, their long-run covariances equal their one-period covariances, and the post-period limit is independent of the pre-period limit.  This argument remains valid when the stacked covariance matrix \(\Sigma\) is singular because no inverse of \(\Sigma\) is taken.

The two summands of \(\delta\ell_t\) are uncorrelated: expansion of their product leaves only odd centered Gaussian moments.  Furthermore,
\[
 \mathbb E_n(U_0^2)=(1-\eta)^2B+\bar b,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_n\{(e_1-e_2)^2\}=x+y,
\]
and
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(e_1^2-e_2^2)=2(x^2+y^2).
\]
It follows that
\[
 \sigma_D^2
 =4\eta^2\{(1-\eta)^2B+\bar b\}(x+y)
  +2\eta^4(x^2+y^2).
\]
For the role-one selector covariance, conditioning on \((F,e_0^{\star})\) gives
\[
 \mathbb E_n\!\left[(e_1-e_2)(F+e_2)(e_0^{\star}-e_1)
       \mid F,e_0^{\star}\right]
 =-xF-ye_0^{\star}.
\]
The quadratic part of \(\delta\ell_t\) has zero covariance with \(\psi_{1,t}\), because each term in its expansion has either a centered odd Gaussian moment or a centered independent factor.  Therefore
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 c_{D1}
 &=\operatorname{Cov}(D,A_1)\\
 &=-2\eta K\{(1-\eta)Bx+\bar b y\}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Exchanging the two roles gives
\[
 c_{D2}=\operatorname{Cov}(D,A_2)
 =2\eta K\{(1-\eta)By+\bar b x\}.
\]
In each pre-score product, the two displayed factors are independent and the residual factor has mean zero.  Hence
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(A_1)=K^2(B+y)(\bar b+x),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(A_2)=K^2(B+x)(\bar b+y).
\]
The post-period variances are
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(R_1)=\bar b+x,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(R_2)=\bar b+y.
\]
By the definition of \(V_s\),
\[
 V_1=K^2(B+y)(\bar b+x)+r(\bar b+x),
 \qquad
 V_2=K^2(B+x)(\bar b+y)+r(\bar b+y).
\]
The independence of the post-period Gaussian coordinates from \(D\) implies
\[
 \rho_s
 =\operatorname{Corr}\!\left(\frac{A_s+\sqrt rR_s}{\sqrt{V_s}},\frac D{\sigma_D}\right)
 =\frac{c_{Ds}}{\sqrt{\sigma_D^2V_s}}
\]
whenever \(\sigma_D^2>0\) and \(V_s>0\), as holds at both points below.

At the symmetric point \(\theta_0\), one has \(\bar b=1\), \(B=2\), \(R=4\), and \(\eta=K=1/2\).  Direct substitution into the formulas just proved gives
\[
 \sigma_D^2=\frac{13}{4},
 \qquad
 c_{D1}=-1,
 \qquad
 c_{D2}=1,
 \qquad
 V_1=V_2=\frac32+2r.
\]
Thus \(\rho_1=-\rho_2\).  To verify the asserted nonzero selected mean, put \(Z_s=A_s+\sqrt rR_s\).  Bivariate Gaussian conditioning and post-period independence give
\[
 \mathbb E(Z_s\mid X)=\frac{c_{Ds}}{\sigma_D}X.
\]
Integration of \(x\phi(x)=-\phi'(x)\) gives
\[
 \mathbb E[X\mathbf 1\{X\leq k\}]=-\phi(k),
 \qquad
 \mathbb E[X\mathbf 1\{X>k\}]=\phi(k).
\]
Consequently, for every finite \(k\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E\{Z_1\mathbf 1(X\leq k)+Z_2\mathbf 1(X>k)\}
 &=\frac{c_{D2}-c_{D1}}{\sigma_D}\phi(k)\\
 &=\frac2{\sigma_D}\phi(k)>0.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus the selected limiting ATT coordinate has nonzero mean at every finite local gap.  On the other hand, \(\rho_1^2=\rho_2^2\), so the already established \(\texttt{thm:wald-symmetry-frontier}\) yields
\[
 C_z(k)=2\Phi(z)-1
\]
for every \(k\in\mathbb R\) and every \(z>0\).  The tied bridge singular value is \(2\), the selector variance is \(13/4\), and \(V_s=3/2+2r\).  Hence \(0<\kappa<2\) and \(0<\nu<63/200\) imply all rank and variance inequalities at \(\theta_0\).  The stipulated strict Gaussian moment bound supplies the remaining defining inequality of \(\Theta_{\mathcal G}^{\mathrm{leg}}\).  Thus \(\theta_0\) is legal.

At the asymmetric point \(\theta_a\),
\[
 \bar b=\frac1{10},
 \qquad
 B=2,
 \qquad
 R=4,
 \qquad
 \eta=K=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \frac{B}{\sqrt{t_1t_2}}=\frac{10}{3}.
\]
The selector-variance calculation is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sigma_D^2
 &=\left(\frac12+\frac1{10}\right)
   \left(\frac15+\frac95\right)
   +\frac18\left(\frac1{25}+\frac{81}{25}\right)\\
 &=\frac{161}{100}.
 \end{aligned}

The selector--ATT covariances are
\[
 c_{D1}=-\frac12\left(\frac15+\frac9{50}\right)=-\frac{19}{100},
 \qquad
 c_{D2}=\frac12\left(\frac95+\frac1{50}\right)=\frac{91}{100}.
\]
With \(r=1/10\), the ATT variances are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 V_1
 &=\frac14\frac{19}{5}\frac3{10}+\frac1{10}\frac3{10}
 =\frac{63}{200},\\
 V_2
 &=\frac14\frac{11}{5}\frac{19}{10}+\frac1{10}\frac{19}{10}

 \end{aligned}

Therefore
\[
 \rho_1=-\frac{19\sqrt2}{\sqrt{10143}}
 =-0.2667997301850519\ldots,
 \qquad
 \rho_2=\frac{91\sqrt2}{\sqrt{39767}}
 =0.6453494964281337\ldots,
\]
and
\[
 \rho_1^2=\frac{722}{10143}
 <\frac{16562}{39767}=\rho_2^2.
\]
The bridge singular value is \(10/3>\kappa\), the selector variance is \(161/100>\nu\), and the smaller ATT variance is \(63/200>\nu\).  The stipulated moment inequality therefore makes \(\theta_a\) legal as well.

If \(d/\sigma_D=-1\), then the definition \(k=-d/\sigma_D\) gives \(k=1\).  Substitution of the exact correlations above and \(z=\Phi^{-1}(0.95)\) into \(\texttt{def:coverage-functional}\) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 C_z(1)
 &=\int_{-\infty}^{1}\phi(x)
   g_{-19\sqrt2/\sqrt{10143}}(x;z)\,dx\\
 &\quad+\int_{1}^{\infty}\phi(x)
   g_{91\sqrt2/\sqrt{39767}}(x;z)\,dx\\

For completeness, this numerical display has a deterministic certificate: directed-rounding interval evaluation of the defining expressions for \(\phi\) and \(\Phi\), after subdividing \([-12,1]\) and \([1,12]\), encloses the two integrals respectively in
\[
 [0.7601411914468526,0.7601411914468528]
 \quad\text{and}\quad

Taylor interval remainders for \(\exp\) and \(\operatorname{erf}\) bound the error on each finite subinterval, while \(0\leq g_\rho\leq1\) and the Gaussian tail bound give omitted mass at most

Their sum therefore encloses \(C_z(1)\) in
\[
 [0.8857757910650705,0.8857757910650709],
\]
which certifies the displayed digits.  Independently of this numerical evaluation, strict undercoverage follows exactly from \(k>0\), \(\rho_1^2-\rho_2^2<0\), and the sign conclusion of \(\texttt{thm:wald-symmetry-frontier}\):
\[
 C_z(1)-\{2\Phi(z)-1\}<0.
\]
Since \(2\Phi\{\Phi^{-1}(0.95)\}-1=0.9\), the coverage is strictly below the nominal level.

At \(\theta_a\), \(R^2/B^2=4\), so the local path is
\[
 t_{2,n}=\frac15-\frac{4d}{\sqrt n}.
\]
It is positive eventually for every finite \(d\).  All bridge moments, Gaussian moments, covariance formulas, and the map \(\Gamma(\theta,d)\) are continuous where \(t_{2,n}>0\), \(\sigma_D^2>0\), and \(V_1,V_2>0\). These inequalities persist on a relatively open neighborhood in the tied parameter manifold crossed with the local-drift coordinate.  The sign part of \(\texttt{thm:wald-symmetry-frontier}\) proves strict undercoverage throughout that neighborhood.  Exchanging role labels exchanges the two squared correlations, and, with roles held fixed, replacing \(d\) by \(-d\) reverses the sign of \(k\); the same theorem then yields strict overcoverage.

It remains to verify the claimed relationship to the published proximal class.  Conditional on \(F_t\), the treated idiosyncratic error \(e_{0t}^{\star}\), the donor measurement error, and the supplemental-proxy measurement error are mutually independent for either role.  The synthetic-control loading equality is \(1=c_j\alpha_j\), the fixed-role rank matrix is the nonzero scalar \(M_s=c_1c_2B\), treatment is the constant \(\Delta_t=\tau\), and all stochastic coordinates are independent and identically distributed over time.  The already established \(\texttt{prop:shi-anchor-transfer}\) proves that, on a compact neighborhood on which the bridge and fixed-role variance inequalities are strict, these facts verify Shi et al.'s Assumptions 1--5, supplementary conditions D.1--D.10, and the fixed-role full-rank condition for either predeclared role.  The strict margins established above allow such compact neighborhoods around both \(\theta_0\) and \(\theta_a\).  Applying that proposition completes the published-class inclusion and proves every assertion.
