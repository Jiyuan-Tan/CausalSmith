\section*{panel\_\allowbreak{}proxysplit\_\allowbreak{}selection\_\allowbreak{}inference}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} Two predeclared valid proximal synthetic-control role assignments can identify the same ATT yet yield a nonregular conventional interval when the smaller full-pre-period ridge loss selects the reported role. The exact selected-Gaussian coverage map protects every symmetric Wald level at an exact tie and protects every local gap exactly when the two standardized ATT coordinates have equal squared selector correlations; otherwise the strict coverage-error sign is the gap sign times their squared-correlation difference. A constant-treatment one-factor Gaussian chart realizes both regimes inside a stationary square subclass of the Shi et al. model, including an open asymmetric region with coverage \(0.8857757910650707\) for a nominal \(90\%\) interval. For correction, an early selector, logarithm-squared gap, late bridge fit, post average, and future-only Bartlett standard error are uniformly selector-conditionally normal on the strengthened class with common finite-row horizon and scalar variance bounds. The same claim fails maximally on the broader pointwise-limit class, where expected conditional Kolmogorov distance can converge to one.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Published proximal synthetic-control theory predetermines donor and supplemental-proxy roles, while general post-selection results do not characterize exact ordinary symmetric-Wald coverage when a loss comparison switches between two valid proximal ATT coordinates. Pointwise horizon and limiting-variance conditions also do not support a supremum over whole triangular arrays.

\paragraph{Niche.} The binary ridge selector admits an exact squared-correlation coverage frontier. A future-only split construction admits uniform coordinatewise studentization after imposing common finite-row horizon comparability and scalar influence-variance bounds, while singular covariance across the stacked roles remains allowed.

\paragraph{Fill.} The paper gives the selected limit, evaluable coverage functional, all-gap if-and-only-if frontier, strict error sign, primitive open miscoverage family, and a positive/negative uniformity boundary. The corrected estimator is uniformly selector-conditionally normal on \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{stab}}(D_{\mathrm{gap}})\); a diagonal common-subclass witness proves maximal failure on \(\mathcal M(D_{\mathrm{gap}})\).

\section{Related work}

Shi et al. (2026) supply the fixed-role structural proximal anchor; the common-subclass proposition, not a full-class inclusion claim, transfers the present phenomena to legal stationary square panels. Leeb and Pötscher (2005) motivate local post-selection nonregularity, whereas Rakshit, Shi, and Tchetgen Tchetgen (2025) address the distinct problem of adaptive selection with potentially invalid proxies. Berk et al. (2013) obtain post-selection protection by simultaneous inference, not by characterizing when an ordinary selected Wald interval is already exact. Lee et al. (2016), Leeb, Pötscher, and Ewald (2015), and Azzalini and Regoli (2012) provide related Gaussian conditioning, coverage, and symmetry tools but not the switched proximal squared-correlation frontier. Lunde (2019) and Andrews (1991) motivate separated-block dependence control and Bartlett estimation. Here those ingredients are derived for the precise future-only proximal statistic on a stabilized class, while the diagonal theorem shows why pointwise array limits alone are insufficient.

  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{stab}}(D_{\mathrm{gap}})\) is the subclass of \(\mathcal M(D_{\mathrm{gap}})\) defined in \(\texttt{def:panel-stabilized-class}\)
  \item \texttt{alphahatBS} --- \texttt{late-\allowbreak{}block bridge coefficient}; \(\mathbb R^{d_s}\)
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat\alpha_{B,s}=\widehat M_{B,s}^{-1}N_{B,n}^{-1}\sum_{t\in\mathcal B_n}Z_{s,t}Y_t\) on invertibility and zero otherwise
  \item \texttt{muhatCS} --- \texttt{post-\allowbreak{}block donor mean}; \(\mathbb R^{d_s}\)
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat\mu_{C,s}=m^{-1}\sum_{t\in\mathcal C_n}W_{D_s,t}\)
  \item \texttt{HhatBCS} --- \texttt{future-\allowbreak{}only bridge-\allowbreak{}sensitivity row}; \(\mathbb R^{1\times d_s}\)
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat H_{B,C,s}=-\widehat\mu_{C,s}^{\prime}\widehat M_{B,s}^{-1}\) on invertibility and zero otherwise
  \item \texttt{uhatBst} --- \texttt{late-\allowbreak{}block fitted bridge score}; \(\mathbb R^{d_s}\)
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat u_{B,s,t}=Z_{s,t}(Y_t-W_{D_s,t}^{\prime}\widehat\alpha_{B,s})\) for \(t\in\mathcal B_n\)
  \item \texttt{psihatBst} --- \texttt{future-\allowbreak{}only fitted pre influence score}; \(\mathbb R\)
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat\psi_{B,s,t}=\widehat H_{B,C,s}\widehat u_{B,s,t}\) for \(t\in\mathcal B_n\)
  \item \texttt{tauhatBCS} --- \texttt{fixed-\allowbreak{}role late-\allowbreak{}fit post-\allowbreak{}average ATT estimator}; \(\mathbb R\)
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat\tau_{B,C,s}=m^{-1}\sum_{t\in\mathcal C_n}(Y_t-W_{D_s,t}^{\prime}\widehat\alpha_{B,s})\)
  \item \texttt{taugap} --- \texttt{gap-\allowbreak{}split selected proximal ATT estimator}; \(\mathbb R\)
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{gap}}=\widehat\tau_{B,C,\widehat J_A}\)
  \item \texttt{vhatCst} --- \texttt{centered fitted post residual}; \(\mathbb R\)
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat v_{C,s,t}=Y_t-W_{D_s,t}^{\prime}\widehat\alpha_{B,s}-\widehat\tau_{B,C,s}\) for \(t\in\mathcal C_n\)
  \item \texttt{gammahatPsiBS} --- \texttt{late-\allowbreak{}score fitted autocovariance}; \(\mathbb R\)
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat\gamma_{\psi,B,s}(j)\) is the centered lag-\(j\) sample autocovariance of \(\widehat\psi_{B,s,t}\) on \(\mathcal B_n\)
  \item \texttt{gammahatVCS} --- \texttt{post-\allowbreak{}score fitted autocovariance}; \(\mathbb R\)
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat\gamma_{v,C,s}(j)\) is the lag-\(j\) sample autocovariance of \(\widehat v_{C,s,t}\) on \(\mathcal C_n\)
  \item \texttt{OmegaHatPsiBS} --- \texttt{late-\allowbreak{}score Bartlett long-\allowbreak{}run variance estimate}; \([0,\infty)\)
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat\Omega_{\psi,B,s}=\widehat\gamma_{\psi,B,s}(0)+2\sum_{j=1}^{h_n}(1-j/(h_n+1))\widehat\gamma_{\psi,B,s}(j)\)
  \item \texttt{OmegaHatVCS} --- \texttt{post-\allowbreak{}score Bartlett long-\allowbreak{}run variance estimate}; \([0,\infty)\)
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat\Omega_{v,C,s}=\widehat\gamma_{v,C,s}(0)+2\sum_{j=1}^{h_n}(1-j/(h_n+1))\widehat\gamma_{v,C,s}(j)\)
  \item \texttt{ShatGapS} --- \texttt{raw gap-\allowbreak{}split variance estimate}; \(\mathbb R\)
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat S_{s,n}^2=\widehat\Omega_{\psi,B,s}/N_{B,n}+\widehat\Omega_{v,C,s}/m\)
  \item \texttt{sehats} --- \texttt{future-\allowbreak{}only Bartlett standard error}; \((0,\infty)\)
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat{\operatorname{se}}_s=\sqrt{\widehat S_{s,n}^2}\) when \(\widehat S_{s,n}^2>0\), and \(\widehat{\operatorname{se}}_s=1\) otherwise
  \item \texttt{Wsn} --- \texttt{finite-\allowbreak{}row oracle future variance}; \((0,\infty)\)
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(W_{s,n}=\operatorname{Var}_{P_n}[\sqrt n\{N_{B,n}^{-1}\sum_{t\in\mathcal B_n}\psi_{s,t}+m^{-1}\sum_{t\in\mathcal C_n}v_{s,t}\}]\)
  \item \texttt{cm} --- \texttt{positive horizon lower bound}; \((0,\infty)\)
  \item \texttt{Cm} --- \texttt{finite horizon upper bound}; \((0,\infty)\)
  \item \texttt{wlower} --- \texttt{positive finite-\allowbreak{}row variance lower bound}; \((0,\infty)\)
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\underline w>0\)
  \item \texttt{wupper} --- \texttt{finite-\allowbreak{}row variance upper bound}; \((0,\infty)\)
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\overline w<\infty\)
  \item \texttt{nstar} --- \texttt{common stabilization threshold}; \(\mathbb N\)
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(n_\star\)
\end{itemize}

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:stationarity]\label{ass:stationarity}
For every \(n\), \(\{X_t:t\in\mathbb Z\}\) is strictly stationary under \(P_n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (strict stationarity for long-panel time-series inference, \cite{ShiLiYuMiaoKuchibhotlaHuTchetgen2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:geometric-beta-mixing]\label{ass:geometric-beta-mixing}
The absolute-regularity coefficients satisfy \(\beta_n(j)\leq C_{\beta}\exp(-c_{\beta}j)\) for every \(j\geq1\) and every \(n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (uniform geometric beta mixing, \cite{Lunde2019SampleSplitting}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:moment-bound]\label{ass:moment-bound}
The primitive process obeys \(\sup_n\mathbb E_n\lVert X_t\rVert^{8+\delta_0}\leq C_{\mathrm{mom}}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (uniform fixed high-moment condition for dependent GMM, \cite{QiuShiMiaoDobribanTchetgen2024}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:sample-ratio]\label{ass:sample-ratio}
The ratio satisfies \(n/m\to r\in(0,\infty)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (comparable long pre- and post-treatment horizons, \cite{ShiLiYuMiaoKuchibhotlaHuTchetgen2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:bridge-rank]\label{ass:bridge-rank}
For each \(s\in\mathcal S\), \(\sigma_{\min}(M_s)\geq\kappa\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (square proximal bridge full-rank condition with a fixed margin, \cite{ShiLiYuMiaoKuchibhotlaHuTchetgen2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:bridge-centering]\label{ass:bridge-centering}
For each \(s\in\mathcal S\), \(\mathbb E_n[Y_t(0)-W_{D_s,t}^{\prime}\alpha_s]=0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (valid proximal synthetic-control bridge for each predeclared role, \cite{ShiLiYuMiaoKuchibhotlaHuTchetgen2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:local-loss-gap]\label{ass:local-loss-gap}
The population criterion difference satisfies \(\sqrt n(L_1-L_2)\to d\in\mathbb R\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (local-to-selection-boundary sequence, \cite{LeebPotscher2005}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:covariance-limit]\label{ass:covariance-limit}
The long-run covariance matrix of \((\ell_{1,t}-L_1,\ell_{2,t}-L_2,\psi_{1,t},\psi_{2,t},v_{1,t},v_{2,t})\) converges to a finite positive-semidefinite matrix.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (finite-dimensional long-run covariance convergence, \cite{Lunde2019SampleSplitting}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:att-variance-margin]\label{ass:att-variance-margin}
For each \(s\in\mathcal S\), \(V_s\geq\nu\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (fixed positive marginal asymptotic-variance condition for studentized GMM, \cite{QiuShiMiaoDobribanTchetgen2024}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:bounded-selector-gap-array]\label{ass:bounded-selector-gap-array}
For the fixed finite envelope \(D_{\mathrm{gap}}\), the whole triangular array satisfies \(\sup_{n\geq1}|\sqrt n(L_{1,n}-L_{2,n})|\leq D_{\mathrm{gap}}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (uniformly bounded local-to-selection-boundary array, \cite{LeebPotscher2005}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:selector-variance-margin]\label{ass:selector-variance-margin}
The selector contrast obeys \(\operatorname{Var}(D)\geq\nu\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (nondegenerate local selection statistic with a fixed margin, \cite{LeebPotscher2005}).
\end{assumption}

\section{Definitions}

\begin{definition}[def:panel-class]\label{def:panel-class}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{bounded-\allowbreak{}gap triangular-\allowbreak{}array proximal role-\allowbreak{}selection class}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For each fixed \(D_{\mathrm{gap}}<\infty\),
\[
 \mathcal M(D_{\mathrm{gap}})
 =\{(P_n)_{n\geq1}:(P_n)_{n\geq1}\text{ satisfies }
 \texttt{ass:stationarity},\texttt{ass:geometric-beta-mixing},
 \texttt{ass:moment-bound},\texttt{ass:sample-ratio},
 \texttt{ass:bridge-rank},\texttt{ass:bridge-centering},
 \texttt{ass:bounded-selector-gap-array},
 \texttt{ass:covariance-limit},\texttt{ass:att-variance-margin},
 \text{ and }\texttt{ass:selector-variance-margin}\}.
\]
The ratio and covariance conditions in this broad class are arraywise limiting conditions.  In particular, \(\mathcal M(D_{\mathrm{gap}})\) does not impose horizon-comparability constants and a threshold common to every array in a rowwise supremum, nor does it impose common finite-row lower and upper bounds on the scalar late/post influence variance.  Those additional restrictions define \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{stab}}(D_{\mathrm{gap}})\) in \(\texttt{def:panel-stabilized-class}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Member properties.} \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}stationarity}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}geometric-\allowbreak{}beta-\allowbreak{}mixing}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}moment-\allowbreak{}bound}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}sample-\allowbreak{}ratio}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}bridge-\allowbreak{}rank}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}bridge-\allowbreak{}centering}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}bounded-\allowbreak{}selector-\allowbreak{}gap-\allowbreak{}array}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}covariance-\allowbreak{}limit}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}att-\allowbreak{}variance-\allowbreak{}margin}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}selector-\allowbreak{}variance-\allowbreak{}margin}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:ridge-selector]\label{def:ridge-selector}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{same-\allowbreak{}pre-\allowbreak{}period ridge role selector}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Compute \(\widehat L_s=\min_a n^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^n\{Y_t-W_{D_s,t}^{\prime}a\}^2+\lVert a\rVert_2^2\) for \(s\in\{1,2\}\), and set \(\widehat J=1\) when \(\widehat L_1\leq\widehat L_2\) and \(\widehat J=2\) otherwise.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{pre-\allowbreak{}treatment observations}; \texttt{two predeclared partitions}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:conventional-selected-att]\label{def:conventional-selected-att}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{conventional role-\allowbreak{}selected proximal ATT}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For each role, compute \(\widehat M_s\), \(\widehat\mu_s^{\mathrm{post}}\), \(\widehat H_s\), the fitted scores \(\widehat\psi_{s,t}\) and \(\widehat v_{s,t}\), their centered sample autocovariances through \(q_n=\lfloor\min(n,m)^{1/5}\rfloor\), and the Bartlett estimates \(\widehat\Omega_{\psi,s}\) and \(\widehat\Omega_{v,s}\). Set \(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{sel}}=\widehat\tau_{\widehat J}\), \(\widehat{\operatorname{se}}^{\mathrm{conv}}_s=\{\widehat\Omega_{\psi,s}/n+\widehat\Omega_{v,s}/m\}^{1/2}\), and report \([\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{sel}}\pm z\widehat{\operatorname{se}}^{\mathrm{conv}}_{\widehat J}]\). This construction uses the full pre-period sample both to select the role and to fit its bridge, unlike \(\text{def:gap-split-estimator}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}ridge-\allowbreak{}selector}; \texttt{fixed-\allowbreak{}role proximal bridge estimators}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:coverage-functional]\label{def:coverage-functional}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{binary selected-\allowbreak{}Gaussian coverage functional}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(C_z(k)=\int_{-\infty}^{k}\phi(x)g_{\rho_1}(x;z)\,dx+\int_k^{\infty}\phi(x)g_{\rho_2}(x;z)\,dx\), where \(g_{\rho}\) is the conditional probability that a standard normal coordinate correlated by \(\rho\) with \(X=x\) lies in \([-z,z]\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{joint Gaussian limit}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:symmetry-frontier]\label{def:symmetry-frontier}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Wald-\allowbreak{}symmetry frontier}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\mathfrak F=\{\operatorname{Law}(Q_1,Q_2,A_1,A_2,R_1,R_2):\rho_1^2=\rho_2^2\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{standardized Gaussian limit coordinates}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:primitive-gaussian-family]\label{def:primitive-gaussian-family}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{primitive Gaussian proximal family}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For \(\theta=(\mu,f,b,x,y,\zeta,r,t_1,t_2)\in\Theta_{\mathcal G}^{\mathrm{leg}}\) and \(d\in\mathbb R\), put
\[
 \bar b=b+\zeta,\qquad B=\mu^2+f,\qquad
 R=t_1+B+x,\qquad \eta=B/R,\qquad K=\mu/B,
\]
\[
 c_1=t_1^{-1/2},\qquad
 t_{2,n}=t_2-\frac{R^2}{B^2}\frac d{\sqrt n},
 \qquad c_{2,n}=t_{2,n}^{-1/2}.
\]
Generate mutually independent coordinates
\[
 F_t\sim N(\mu,f),\quad e_{0t}\sim N(0,b),\quad
 e_{1t}\sim N(0,x),\quad e_{2t}\sim N(0,y),\quad
 \xi_t\sim N(0,\zeta),
\]
independently over \(t\), and define
\[
 Y_t(0)=F_t+e_{0t}+\xi_t,\qquad
 \Delta_t=\tau,\qquad
 W_{1t}=c_1(F_t+e_{1t}),\qquad
 W_{2t}=c_{2,n}(F_t+e_{2t}).
\]
Role one uses \(W_{1t}\) as donor and \(W_{2t}\) as supplemental proxy, while role two exchanges those assignments.  Thus, conditional on \(F_t\), the treated idiosyncratic error \(e_{0t}+\xi_t\) and the two factor-measurement errors are mutually independent, and the causal target satisfies \(\mathbb E_n\Delta_t=\tau\).

The tied manifold is \(t_2=t_1+x-y>0\).  Along the displayed local calibration, \(t_{2,n}>0\) eventually, both bridge matrices equal \(c_1c_{2,n}B\), the bridge coefficients are \(1/c_1\) and \(1/c_{2,n}\), and
\[
 \sqrt n(L_1-L_2)=d+O(n^{-1/2}).
\]
At the tied limit,
\[
 \sigma_D^2
 =4\eta^2\{(1-\eta)^2B+\bar b\}(x+y)
  +2\eta^4(x^2+y^2),
\]
\[
 c_{D1}=\operatorname{Cov}(D,A_1)
 =-2\eta K\{(1-\eta)Bx+\bar b y\},
\qquad
 c_{D2}=\operatorname{Cov}(D,A_2)
 =2\eta K\{(1-\eta)By+\bar b x\},
\]
and, because treatment is constant,
\[
 V_1=K^2(B+y)(\bar b+x)+r(\bar b+x),
\qquad
 V_2=K^2(B+x)(\bar b+y)+r(\bar b+y).
\]
Consequently
\[
 \Gamma(\theta,d)
 =\left(
 -\frac d{\sigma_D},
 \frac{c_{D1}}{\sqrt{\sigma_D^2V_1}},
 \frac{c_{D2}}{\sqrt{\sigma_D^2V_2}},
 V_1,V_2
 \right).
\]
The corrected family \(\mathcal G\) is the collection of these constant-treatment local Gaussian arrays.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} finite-dimensional legal parameter \(\theta\in\Theta_{\mathcal G}^{\mathrm{leg}}\); local drift \(d\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:gap-split-estimator]\label{def:gap-split-estimator}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{early-\allowbreak{}select,\allowbreak{} late-\allowbreak{}fit proximal ATT}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Put \(a_n=\lfloor\varrho n\rfloor\), \(b_n=\lceil(\log n)^2\rceil\), \(\mathcal B_n=\{a_n+b_n+1,\ldots,n\}\), \(N_{B,n}=|\mathcal B_n|\), and \(\mathcal C_n=\{n+1,\ldots,n+m\}\).  For each fixed role \(s\in\mathcal S\), define
\[
 \widehat M_{B,s}=N_{B,n}^{-1}\sum_{t\in\mathcal B_n}Z_{s,t}W_{D_s,t}^{\prime}.
\]
If \(\widehat M_{B,s}\) is invertible, let
\[
 \widehat\alpha_{B,s}
 =\widehat M_{B,s}^{-1}N_{B,n}^{-1}
   \sum_{t\in\mathcal B_n}Z_{s,t}Y_t;
\]
otherwise set \(\widehat\alpha_{B,s}=0\).  Define
\[
 \widehat\mu_{C,s}=m^{-1}\sum_{t\in\mathcal C_n}W_{D_s,t},
 \qquad
 \widehat H_{B,C,s}=-\widehat\mu_{C,s}^{\prime}\widehat M_{B,s}^{-1}
\]
when \(\widehat M_{B,s}\) is invertible, and set \(\widehat H_{B,C,s}=0\) otherwise.  For \(t\in\mathcal B_n\), put
\[
 \widehat u_{B,s,t}=Z_{s,t}
  (Y_t-W_{D_s,t}^{\prime}\widehat\alpha_{B,s}),
 \qquad
 \widehat\psi_{B,s,t}=\widehat H_{B,C,s}\widehat u_{B,s,t}.
\]
The counterfactual fixed-role estimate and the selected estimate are
\[
 \widehat\tau_{B,C,s}=m^{-1}\sum_{t\in\mathcal C_n}
 (Y_t-W_{D_s,t}^{\prime}\widehat\alpha_{B,s}),
 \qquad
 \widehat\tau_{\mathrm{gap}}=\widehat\tau_{B,C,\widehat J_A}.
\]
For \(t\in\mathcal C_n\), put
\[
 \widehat v_{C,s,t}=Y_t-W_{D_s,t}^{\prime}\widehat\alpha_{B,s}
 -\widehat\tau_{B,C,s}.
\]
Let \(\overline{\widehat\psi}_{B,s}=N_{B,n}^{-1}\sum_{t\in\mathcal B_n}\widehat\psi_{B,s,t}\).  For \(0\leq j\leq h_n\), define
\[
 \widehat\gamma_{\psi,B,s}(j)=N_{B,n}^{-1}
 \sum_{\substack{t,t-j\in\mathcal B_n}}
 (\widehat\psi_{B,s,t}-\overline{\widehat\psi}_{B,s})
 (\widehat\psi_{B,s,t-j}-\overline{\widehat\psi}_{B,s}),
\]
\[
 \widehat\gamma_{v,C,s}(j)=m^{-1}
 \sum_{t=n+j+1}^{n+m}\widehat v_{C,s,t}\widehat v_{C,s,t-j}.
\]
The Bartlett estimates are
\[
 \widehat\Omega_{\psi,B,s}=\widehat\gamma_{\psi,B,s}(0)
 +2\sum_{j=1}^{h_n}\left(1-\frac{j}{h_n+1}\right)
 \widehat\gamma_{\psi,B,s}(j),
\]
\[
 \widehat\Omega_{v,C,s}=\widehat\gamma_{v,C,s}(0)
 +2\sum_{j=1}^{h_n}\left(1-\frac{j}{h_n+1}\right)
 \widehat\gamma_{v,C,s}(j).
\]
Set
\[
 \widehat S_{s,n}^2
 =\frac{\widehat\Omega_{\psi,B,s}}{N_{B,n}}
  +\frac{\widehat\Omega_{v,C,s}}m,
 \qquad
 \widehat{\operatorname{se}}_s=
 \begin{cases}
  \sqrt{\widehat S_{s,n}^2},&\widehat S_{s,n}^2>0,\\
  1,&\widehat S_{s,n}^2\leq0
 \end{cases}
\]
(with the second branch also used for any nonfinite arithmetic value), and report
\[
 \frac{\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{gap}}-\tau}
 {\widehat{\operatorname{se}}_{\widehat J_A}}.
\]
Thus no full-pre-period fitted sensitivity or score enters the gap-split statistic, and every inverse and nonpositive-variance case has a fixed measurable fallback.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{two predeclared partitions}; \texttt{ordered panel}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:singular-mapping-handle]\label{def:singular-mapping-handle}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{singular joint Gaussian and HAC mapping handle}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Couple logarithmically separated beta-mixing big blocks to independent blocks, prove Cramér--Wold approximation along covariance-convergent subsequences, control Gaussian selector-boundary strips using only \(\operatorname{Var}(D)\geq\nu\), and studentize each selected ATT direction using its own positive scalar variance rather than inverting the stacked covariance.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}gap-\allowbreak{}split-\allowbreak{}estimator}; \texttt{row-\allowbreak{}dependent positive-\allowbreak{}semidefinite long-\allowbreak{}run covariance}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:panel-stabilized-class]\label{def:panel-stabilized-class}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{finite-\allowbreak{}row stabilized bounded-\allowbreak{}gap panel class}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Fix constants \(0<c_m\leq C_m<\infty\), \(0<\underline w\leq\overline w<\infty\), and an integer \(n_\star\), all common to the class.  For row \(n\) and role \(s\), define the oracle future linear statistic and its finite-row variance by
\[
 G_{s,n}=\sqrt n\left\{
  N_{B,n}^{-1}\sum_{t\in\mathcal B_n}\psi_{s,t}
  +m^{-1}\sum_{t\in\mathcal C_n}v_{s,t}
 \right\},
 \qquad
 W_{s,n}=\operatorname{Var}_{P_n}(G_{s,n}).
\]
Then \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{stab}}(D_{\mathrm{gap}})\) is the subclass of \(\mathcal M(D_{\mathrm{gap}})\) consisting of arrays for which, for every \(n\geq n_\star\) and both \(s\in\mathcal S\),
\[
 c_m\leq \frac mn\leq C_m,
 \qquad
 \underline w\leq W_{s,n}\leq\overline w.
\]
The constants and threshold are fixed before the supremum over arrays is taken.  These are primitive horizon and scalar variance restrictions; no Gaussian approximation, covariance nonsingularity for the stacked roles, or HAC consistency is included in the definition.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{common horizon constants}; \texttt{common scalar finite-\allowbreak{}row variance bounds}; \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}panel-\allowbreak{}class}.
\end{definition}

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:fixed-role-linearization]\label{lem:fixed-role-linearization}
Uniformly over \(s\in\mathcal S\), \(\widehat\alpha_s-\alpha_s=M_s^{-1}n^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^n u_{s,t}+o_{P_n}(n^{-1/2})\), and \(\sqrt n(\widehat\tau_s-\tau)=n^{-1/2}\sum_{t=1}^n\psi_{s,t}+\sqrt{n/m}\,m^{-1/2}\sum_{t=n+1}^{n+m}v_{s,t}+o_{P_n}(1)\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(\overline f_{mathrm{pre}}=n^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^n f_t\) and \(\overline f_{\mathrm{post}}=m^{-1}\sum_{t=n+1}^{n+m}f_t\).  We first record a consequence of \(\texttt{ass:geometric-beta-mixing}\) and \(\texttt{ass:moment-bound}\) that will be used repeatedly.  If two scalar functions of the primitive process, separated by \(j\) periods, have uniformly bounded \((2+\epsilon)\)-moments, truncating both functions at level \(\beta_n(j)^{-1/(2+\epsilon)}\), applying the total-variation definition of absolute regularity to the bounded parts, and applying H\"older's inequality to the tails gives
\[
 \left|\operatorname{Cov}_{P_n}(G_t,H_{t-j})\right|
 \leq C\beta_n(j)^{\epsilon/(2+\epsilon)}
 \leq C\exp(-c j).
\]
Here and below \(C,c\in(0,\infty)\) depend only on the fixed moment and mixing bounds.  All coordinates and products used below have such moments because \(u_{s,t}\), \(\psi_{s,t}\), and the entries of \(Z_{s,t}W_{D_s,t}^{\prime}\) are polynomials of degree at most two in \(X_t\), while \(\texttt{ass:moment-bound}\) supplies a uniform \((8+\delta_0)\)-moment.  Summing the preceding covariance bound therefore gives, for every centered such process \(G_t\),
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_{P_n}\!\left(N^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^N G_t\right)
 \leq \frac{1}{N}\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}
       \left|\operatorname{Cov}_{P_n}(G_0,G_j)\right|
 \leq \frac{C}{N}.
\]
Consequently all empirical first and second moments used below differ from their expectations by \(O_{P_n}(N^{-1/2})\).  Strict stationarity in \(\texttt{ass:stationarity}\) makes the expectations independent of the location of the block.

Fix \(s\in\mathcal S\).  By the definition of \(\alpha_s\),
\[
 \mathbb E_n u_{s,t}
 =\mathbb E_n[Z_{s,t}Y_t(0)]-M_s\alpha_s=0.
\]
On the event that \(\widehat M_s\) is invertible, the empirical bridge equation gives the exact identity
\[
 \widehat M_s(\widehat\alpha_s-\alpha_s)
 =\overline u_{s,\mathrm{pre}},
 \qquad
 \widehat\alpha_s-\alpha_s
 =\widehat M_s^{-1}\overline u_{s,\mathrm{pre}}.
\]
The preceding moment bound gives
\[
 \widehat M_s-M_s=O_{P_n}(n^{-1/2}),
 \qquad
 \overline u_{s,\mathrm{pre}}=O_{P_n}(n^{-1/2}).
\]
By \(\texttt{ass:bridge-rank}\), \(\lVert M_s^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\kappa^{-1}\).  Hence
\[
 \Pr_{P_n}\!\left\{\lVert\widehat M_s-M_s\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}<\kappa/2\right\}\longrightarrow1,
\]
and on this event \(\lVert\widehat M_s^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq2/\kappa\).  The resolvent identity then yields
\[
 \widehat M_s^{-1}-M_s^{-1}
 =-M_s^{-1}(\widehat M_s-M_s)\widehat M_s^{-1}
 =O_{P_n}(n^{-1/2}).
\]
It follows that the fixed measurable fallback is used with probability tending to zero and
\[
 \widehat\alpha_s-\alpha_s
 =M_s^{-1}\overline u_{s,\mathrm{pre}}+O_{P_n}(n^{-1})
 =M_s^{-1}n^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^n u_{s,t}+o_{P_n}(n^{-1/2}).
\]

Before treatment \(Y_t=Y_t(0)\), and after treatment \(Y_t=Y_t(0)+\Delta_t\).  Thus the definition of \(\widehat\tau_s\) gives the exact decomposition
\[
 \widehat\tau_s-\tau
 =\overline v_{s,\mathrm{post}}
  -(\overline W_{D_s,\mathrm{post}})^{\prime}
       (\widehat\alpha_s-\alpha_s).
\]
The process \(v_{s,t}\) is centered: by \(\texttt{ass:bridge-centering}\) and the definition \(\tau=\mathbb E_n\Delta_t\),
\[
 \mathbb E_n v_{s,t}
 =\mathbb E_n[Y_t(0)-W_{D_s,t}^{\prime}\alpha_s]
  +\mathbb E_n\Delta_t-\tau=0.
\]
Moreover, \(\overline W_{D_s,\mathrm{post}}-\mu_s=O_{P_n}(m^{-1/2})\).  Since \(n/m\to r\in(0,\infty)\) by \(\texttt{ass:sample-ratio}\), multiplication of the two root-sample-size errors gives
\[
 (\overline W_{D_s,\mathrm{post}}-\mu_s)^{\prime}
       (\widehat\alpha_s-\alpha_s)=O_{P_n}(n^{-1})=o_{P_n}(n^{-1/2}).
\]
Using \(H_s=-\mu_s^{\prime}M_s^{-1}\) and \(\psi_{s,t}=H_su_{s,t}\), we therefore obtain
\[
 \sqrt n(\widehat\tau_s-\tau)
 =n^{-1/2}\sum_{t=1}^n\psi_{s,t}
  +\sqrt{\frac nm}\,m^{-1/2}\sum_{t=n+1}^{n+m}v_{s,t}
  +o_{P_n}(1).
\]
Because \(\mathcal S=\{1,2\}\) is finite, taking the maximum of the two remainders proves both expansions uniformly over \(s\in\mathcal S\).  The covariance-limit assumption ensures that every variance named in these expansions has the finite limit used in the statement.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:conventional-hac-consistency]\label{lem:conventional-hac-consistency}
For each \(s\in\mathcal S\), under \(q_n=\lfloor\min(n,m)^{1/5}\rfloor\), replacement of \(M_s\), \(\mu_s\), \(\alpha_s\), and \(\tau\) by \(\widehat M_s\), \(\widehat\mu_s^{\mathrm{post}}\), \(\widehat\alpha_s\), and \(\widehat\tau_s\) in the fitted influence scores is asymptotically negligible for Bartlett estimation: \(\widehat\Omega_{\psi,s}\to_{P_n}\Omega_{\psi,s}\), \(\widehat\Omega_{v,s}\to_{P_n}\Omega_{v,s}\), and \(\widehat V_s^{\mathrm{conv}}\to_{P_n}V_s\). Moreover, \(\Pr_{P_n}\{\min_{s\in\mathcal S}\widehat V_s^{\mathrm{conv}}\geq\nu/2\}\to1\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(s\in\mathcal S\).  Because the two partitions are predeclared, \(d_s\) is fixed.  We first record the moment and covariance bounds that will be used for both score sequences.  By \(\texttt{ass:bridge-rank}\),
\[
 \lVert M_s^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq \kappa^{-1}.
\]
The Cauchy--Schwarz inequality and \(\texttt{ass:moment-bound}\) therefore give, uniformly in the row law,
\[
 \lVert\alpha_s\rVert_2
 \leq \kappa^{-1}\lVert\mathbb E_n[Z_{s,t}Y_t(0)]\rVert_2\leq C,
 \qquad
 \lVert\mu_s\rVert_2\leq C,
 \qquad
 \lVert H_s\rVert_2\leq C.
\]
Consequently, for some \(\epsilon>0\), which may be chosen no larger than \(\delta_0/2\),
\[
 \sup_n\mathbb E_n|\psi_{s,t}|^{4+\epsilon}leq C,
 \qquad
 \sup_n\mathbb E_n|v_{s,t}|^{4+\epsilon}\leq C.                                      \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, \(\psi_{s,t}=H_sZ_{s,t}\{Y_t(0)-W_{D_s,t}^{\prime}\alpha_s\}\) is a product of two linear functions of \(X_t\), while \(v_{s,t}\) is a linear function of \(X_t\) plus the bounded constants \(\alpha_s\) and \(\tau=\mathbb E_n\Delta_t\); the asserted bounds thus follow from the \((8+\delta_0)\)-moment bound and H\"older's inequality.

Both scores are centered.  For the pre-period score, the definition of \(\alpha_s\) and invertibility of \(M_s\) imply
\[
 \mathbb E_n u_{s,t}
 =\mathbb E_n[Z_{s,t}Y_t(0)]-M_s\alpha_s=0,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_n\psi_{s,t}=H_s\mathbb E_nu_{s,t}=0.                                    \tag{2}
\]
For the post-period score, \(\texttt{ass:bridge-centering}\) and the definition of \(\tau\) imply
\[
 \mathbb E_nv_{s,t}
 =\mathbb E_n\{Y_t(0)-W_{D_s,t}^{\prime}\alpha_s\}
  +\mathbb E_n\Delta_t-\tau=0.                                                       \tag{3}
\]

We next give a self-contained covariance consequence of the mixing and moment assumptions.  If two random variables measurable with respect to time blocks separated by \(h\) have uniformly bounded \((2+\eta)\)-moments, truncate both at a level \(K\).  The definition of the absolute-regularity coefficient bounds the covariance of the bounded truncated variables by \(4K^2\beta_n(h)\); H\"older's and Markov's inequalities bound all truncation terms by \(CK^{-\eta}\).  Choosing \(K\) as a suitable positive exponential function of \(h\), and using \(\texttt{ass:geometric-beta-mixing}\), yields constants \(C,c>0\), independent of \(n\), such that
\[
 |\operatorname{Cov}_{P_n}(U,V)|\leq Ce^{-ch}.                                      \tag{4}
\]
Applying (4) with (1) first to either \(w_t=\psi_{s,t}\) or \(w_t=v_{s,t}\), and then to products of two such scores, gives
\[
 |\gamma_{w,n}(j)|
 :=|\operatorname{Cov}_{P_n}(w_t,w_{t-j})|\leq Ce^{-c|j|}.                           \tag{5}
\]
Strict stationarity in \(\texttt{ass:stationarity}\) makes this covariance independent of \(t\).

Consider a consecutive block of length \(N\), and define, for \(0\leq j<N\),
\[
 \widetilde\gamma_N(j)=\frac1N\sum_{t=j+1}^{N}w_tw_{t-j}.
\]
For fixed \(j\), put \(U_{t,j}=w_tw_{t-j}\).  By (1), \(\sup_{n,t,j}\mathbb E_n|U_{t,j}|^{2+\epsilon/2}<\infty\).  Among the ordered pairs \((t,u)\), at most \(C N(j+1)\) have overlapping or adjacent supports \(\{t-j,t\}\) and \(\{u-j,u\}\); their covariances are bounded by Cauchy--Schwarz and (1).  For every other pair, (4) bounds the covariance by \(Ce^{-c(|t-u|-j)}\).  Summing by the separation \(|t-u|-j\) gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_{P_n}\{\widetilde\gamma_N(j)\}
 \leq \frac{C(j+1)}{N}.                                                             \tag{6}
\]
Also, (2)--(5) imply \(\operatorname{Var}_{P_n}(N^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^Nw_t)\leq C/N\), and hence
\[
 \overline w_N:=N^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^Nw_t=O_{P_n}(N^{-1/2}).                            \tag{7}
\]

Let \(q<N\) and let \(\widetilde\Omega_{w,N}(q)\) be the uncentered Bartlett sum formed from the \(\widetilde\gamma_N(j)\)'s.  From (6), the triangle inequality, and Cauchy--Schwarz,
\[
 \mathbb E_n\left|
 \widetilde\Omega_{w,N}(q)-\mathbb E_n\widetilde\Omega_{w,N}(q)
 \right|
 \leq C\sum_{j=0}^q\sqrt{\frac{j+1}{N}}
 \leq C\frac{q^{3/2}}{\sqrt N}.                                                     \tag{8}
\]
Because \(\mathbb E_n\widetilde\gamma_N(j)=(1-j/N)\gamma_{w,n}(j)\), (5) gives the deterministic bound
\[
 \left|
 \mathbb E_n\widetilde\Omega_{w,N}(q)
 -\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}\gamma_{w,n}(j)
 \right|
 \leq C\left(e^{-cq}+\frac1q+\frac1N\right).                                      \tag{9}
\]
To account for empirical centering, write every Bartlett sum as \(N^{-1}x^{\prime}A_qx\), where the symmetric band matrix \(A_q\) has absolute row sums at most \(2q+1\).  Replacing \(x=(w_1,\ldots,w_N)^{\prime}\) by \(x-\overline w_N\mathbf 1\), using (7), \(N^{-1}\lVert x\rVert_2^2=O_{P_n}(1)\), and \(\lVert A_q\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq2q+1\), changes the quadratic form by \(O_{P_n}(qN^{-1/2})\).  Combining this fact with (8)--(9), the oracle centered Bartlett estimator obeys
\[
 \widehat\Omega^{\mathrm{or}}_{w,N}(q)
 -\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}\gamma_{w,n}(j)
 =O_{P_n}\left(
   \frac{q^{3/2}}{\sqrt N}+\frac q{\sqrt N}
   +\frac1q+e^{-cq}+\frac1N
 \right).                                                                           \tag{10}
\]

Take \(q=q_n=\lfloor\min(n,m)^{1/5}\rfloor\).  By \(\texttt{ass:sample-ratio}\), \(n\) and \(m\) are asymptotically comparable, so every term on the right of (10) tends to zero both for \((w,N)=(\psi_{s,\cdot},n)\) and for \((w,N)=(v_{s,\cdot},m)\).  The corresponding entries of the long-run covariance matrix in \(\texttt{ass:covariance-limit}\), together with the definitions of \(\Omega_{\psi,s}\) and \(\Omega_{v,s}\), therefore give
\[
 \widehat\Omega^{\mathrm{or}}_{\psi,s}\to_{P_n}\Omega_{\psi,s},
 \qquad
 \widehat\Omega^{\mathrm{or}}_{v,s}\to_{P_n}\Omega_{v,s}.                        \tag{11}
\]

It remains to show that fitting the scores in \(\texttt{def:conventional-selected-att}\) does not alter (11).  The same covariance summation used for (7), applied coordinatewise in the fixed-dimensional moment matrices and means, yields
\[
 \widehat M_s-M_s=O_{P_n}(n^{-1/2}),
 \qquad
 \widehat\mu_s^{\mathrm{post}}-\mu_s=O_{P_n}(m^{-1/2}).                            \tag{12}
\]
On the event \(\lVert\widehat M_s-M_s\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\kappa/2\), Weyl's inequality gives \(\sigma_{\min}(\widehat M_s)\geq\kappa/2\).  This event has probability tending to one by (12), and on it the resolvent identity gives
\[
 \widehat M_s^{-1}-M_s^{-1}
 =-\widehat M_s^{-1}(\widehat M_s-M_s)M_s^{-1}
 =O_{P_n}(n^{-1/2}).                                                                 \tag{13}
\]
Thus all inverse fallback events in the definition have probability tending to zero.  Equations (12)--(13), \(n/m\to r\), and the definitions of \(H_s\) and \(\widehat H_s\) imply
\[
 \widehat H_s-H_s
 =-(\widehat\mu_s^{\mathrm{post}}-\mu_s)^{\prime}\widehat M_s^{-1}
   -\mu_s^{\prime}(\widehat M_s^{-1}-M_s^{-1})
 =O_{P_n}(n^{-1/2}).                                                                 \tag{14}
\]
Moreover, \(\texttt{lem:fixed-role-linearization}\) gives
\[
 \widehat\alpha_s-\alpha_s=O_{P_n}(n^{-1/2}),
 \qquad
 \widehat\tau_s-\tau=O_{P_n}(n^{-1/2}),                                            \tag{15}
\]
where the second rate uses \(n/m\to r\) and tightness of the two normalized score sums in that lemma.

On the probability-tending-to-one inverse event, expand
\[
 \widehat\psi_{s,t}-\psi_{s,t}
 =(\widehat H_s-H_s)u_{s,t}
  -\widehat H_sZ_{s,t}W_{D_s,t}^{\prime}(\widehat\alpha_s-\alpha_s).                \tag{16}
\]
The empirical second moments of \(u_{s,t}\) and \(Z_{s,t}W_{D_s,t}^{\prime}\) are tight by \(\texttt{ass:moment-bound}\), stationarity, and the variance argument leading to (7).  Hence (14)--(16) yield
\[
 \left\{\frac1n\sum_{t=1}^n
   (\widehat\psi_{s,t}-\psi_{s,t})^2\right\}^{1/2}
 =O_{P_n}(n^{-1/2}).                                                                 \tag{17}
\]
Similarly, on the post block,
\[
 \widehat v_{s,t}-v_{s,t}
 =-W_{D_s,t}^{\prime}(\widehat\alpha_s-\alpha_s)
   -(\widehat\tau_s-\tau),
\]
so (15) and the tight empirical second moment of \(W_{D_s,t}\) give
\[
 \left\{\frac1m\sum_{t=n+1}^{n+m}
   (\widehat v_{s,t}-v_{s,t})^2\right\}^{1/2}
 =O_{P_n}(n^{-1/2}).                                                                 \tag{18}
\]

For any two length-\(N\) score vectors \(w\) and \(w+e\), the same Bartlett-matrix representation and the contraction property of empirical centering imply
\[
 |\widehat\Omega_{w+e,N}(q)-\widehat\Omega_{w,N}(q)|
 \leq(2q+1)\left[
  2\left\{N^{-1}\sum_tw_t^2\right\}^{1/2}
    \left\{N^{-1}\sum_te_t^2\right\}^{1/2}
  +N^{-1}\sum_te_t^2
 \right].                                                                           \tag{19}
\]
Apply (19) with (17) and (18).  Because \(q_n n^{-1/2}\to0\), and because \(n/m\to r\), fitted-score replacement is \(o_{P_n}(1)\) on both blocks.  The vanishing-probability fallback events do not affect convergence in probability.  Combining this conclusion with (11) proves
\[
 \widehat\Omega_{\psi,s}\to_{P_n}\Omega_{\psi,s},
 \qquad
 \widehat\Omega_{v,s}\to_{P_n}\Omega_{v,s}.                                       \tag{20}
\]

Finally, \(\texttt{ass:sample-ratio}\), (20), and the definition of \(V_s\) imply
\[
 \widehat V_s^{\mathrm{conv}}
 =\widehat\Omega_{\psi,s}+\frac nm\widehat\Omega_{v,s}
 \to_{P_n}\Omega_{\psi,s}+r\Omega_{v,s}=V_s.                                     \tag{21}
\]
The argument holds for each of the two roles and does not invert, or require nonsingularity of, their stacked covariance matrix.  Since \(\texttt{ass:att-variance-margin}\) states \(V_s\geq\nu\), the union bound and (21) give
\[
 \Pr_{P_n}\left\{\min_{s\in\mathcal S}\widehat V_s^{\mathrm{conv}}\geq\nu/2\right\}
 \geq 1-\sum_{s=1}^2
 \Pr_{P_n}\left\{|\widehat V_s^{\mathrm{conv}}-V_s|>\nu/2\right\}
 \longrightarrow1.
\]
This proves all three asserted consistency statements and the asserted asymptotic positivity.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This lemma certifies that the concrete same-sample Bartlett standard error consistently studentizes each role coordinate without requiring inversion of the stacked covariance. \textit{Gap.} Andrews gives kernel HAC consistency for observed score arrays, while the conventional proximal interval requires uniform control of fitted bridge-Jacobian and ATT replacement in two role-specific score arrays. \textit{Consumer.} It makes the conventional selected-Gaussian coverage functional operational from the observed panel.

\begin{theorem}[thm:selected-gaussian-limit]\label{thm:selected-gaussian-limit}
Let \((Q_1,Q_2,A_1,A_2)\) be the centered Gaussian limit of the pre-period loss and ATT coordinates, and let \((R_1,R_2)\) be the centered Gaussian post-period limit, independent of the pre-period limit.  For each \(s\in\mathcal S\), the fitted-score Bartlett estimator with \(q_n=\lfloor\min(n,m)^{1/5}\rfloor\) satisfies \(\widehat V_s^{\mathrm{conv}}\to_{P_n}V_s\) and \(\Pr_{P_n}\{\widehat V_s^{\mathrm{conv}}>0\}\to1\).  Consequently,
\[
 \sqrt n(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{sel}}-\tau)
 \Rightarrow A_{J(d)}+\sqrt rR_{J(d)},
\]
and
\[
 \frac{\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{sel}}-\tau}
 {\widehat{\operatorname{se}}^{\mathrm{conv}}_{\widehat J}}
 \Rightarrow
 T_1\mathbf 1\{X\leq k\}+T_2\mathbf 1\{X>k\}.
\]
On the explicit common stationary square subclass certified by \(\texttt{prop:shi-anchor-transfer}\), fixing a predeclared role, or considering a separate sequence for which \(|\sqrt n(L_1-L_2)|\to\infty\), reduces these displays to the corresponding fixed-role centered normal law.  This is a common-subclass reduction, not a set-inclusion claim for the full published class or a claim about its rectangular application procedure.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By \(\texttt{lem:ridge-loss-expansion}\), jointly for the two roles,
\[
 \sqrt n\{(\widehat L_1-\widehat L_2)-(L_1-L_2)\}
 =n^{-1/2}\sum_{t=1}^n\{(\ell_{1,t}-L_1)-(\ell_{2,t}-L_2)\}+o_{P_n}(1).
\]
The stipulated centered Gaussian limit, \(\texttt{ass:local-loss-gap}\), and \(D=Q_1-Q_2\) yield
\[
 \sqrt n(\widehat L_1-\widehat L_2)\Rightarrow d+D.
\]
By \(\texttt{ass:selector-variance-margin}\), \(\operatorname{Var}(D)\geq\nu>0\).  Hence \(d+D\) has no atom at zero, even if the complete stacked covariance is singular, and the tie rule implies
\[
 \widehat J\Rightarrow J(d),\qquad
 J(d)=1\text{ on }\{d+D\leq0\},\quad J(d)=2\text{ otherwise}.
\]
More explicitly, the probability of the discontinuity strip is bounded by \(2\epsilon/\sqrt{2\pi\nu}\), so continuous upper and lower interpolations followed by \(\epsilon\downarrow0\) justify this indicator mapping.

For each role, \(\texttt{lem:fixed-role-linearization}\) gives
\[
 \sqrt n(\widehat\tau_s-\tau)
 =n^{-1/2}\sum_{t=1}^n\psi_{s,t}
 +\sqrt{n/m}\,m^{-1/2}\sum_{t=n+1}^{n+m}v_{s,t}+o_{P_n}(1).
\]
The joint Gaussian premise, independence of the post limit from the pre limit, and \(n/m\to r\) from \(\texttt{ass:sample-ratio}\) therefore imply
\[
 \left(\sqrt n(\widehat\tau_1-\tau),\sqrt n(\widehat\tau_2-\tau),
 \sqrt n(\widehat L_1-\widehat L_2)\right)
 \Rightarrow(A_1+\sqrt rR_1,A_2+\sqrt rR_2,d+D).
\]
The switching map is discontinuous only when \(d+D=0\), a null event.  Thus
\[
 \sqrt n(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{sel}}-\tau)
 \Rightarrow A_{J(d)}+\sqrt rR_{J(d)}.
\]

By \(\texttt{lem:conventional-hac-consistency}\),
\[
 \max_{s\in\mathcal S}|\widehat V_s^{\mathrm{conv}}-V_s|\to_{P_n}0,
 \qquad
 \Pr_{P_n}\{\min_s\widehat V_s^{\mathrm{conv}}\geq\nu/2\}\to1.
\]
Selection cannot enlarge the maximum error.  On this event the inverse square-root map is Lipschitz with a constant depending only on \(\nu\); tightness of the selected numerator makes replacement of the true inverse standard deviation by the estimated one an \(o_{P_n}(1)\) perturbation, and continuous upper and lower approximations therefore give
\[
 \frac{\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{sel}}-\tau}
 {\widehat{\operatorname{se}}^{\mathrm{conv}}_{\widehat J}}
 \Rightarrow
 \frac{A_1+\sqrt rR_1}{\sqrt{V_1}}\mathbf1\{d+D\leq0\}
 +\frac{A_2+\sqrt rR_2}{\sqrt{V_2}}\mathbf1\{d+D>0\}.
\]
Since \(X=D/\sigma_D\) and \(k=-d/\sigma_D\), this is exactly
\[
 T_1\mathbf1\{X\leq k\}+T_2\mathbf1\{X>k\}.
\]
If a role is fixed, the indicators disappear and \(T_s\sim N(0,1)\), because \(V_s=\operatorname{Var}(A_s+\sqrt rR_s)>0\).  On a separate sequence with \(|\sqrt n(L_1-L_2)|\to\infty\), the centered loss fluctuation is tight and the population sign selects one role with probability tending to one, giving the same reduction.  Proposition \(\texttt{prop:shi-anchor-transfer}\) places the explicit stationary square family in the intersection with Shi et al.'s fixed-role class.  This proves only the stated common-subclass comparison, not inclusion of either full class in the other and not a claim about the rectangular application rule.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem identifies the first-order law reported after outcome-based donor/proxy role choice and isolates the local selected-coordinate nonregularity. \textit{Gap.} Leeb and Pötscher provide the generic local post-selection perspective, Rakshit et al. study selection with potentially invalid proxies, and Berk et al. use simultaneous protection. None gives this switched valid-role proximal ATT law; the Shi comparison is restricted to the proved common subclass. \textit{Consumer.} An analyst comparing two scientifically credible West-German role assignments can calculate the conventional selected limit while recognizing that the published rectangular application rule is outside the theorem.

\begin{theorem}[thm:wald-symmetry-frontier]\label{thm:wald-symmetry-frontier}
For every \(z>0\), the limiting coverage of the conventional selected interval is \(C_z(k)\). At an exact selector tie, \(C_z(0)=2\Phi(z)-1\) for all \(\rho_1,\rho_2\in[-1,1]\). For any fixed \(z>0\), \(C_z(k)=2\Phi(z)-1\) for every \(k\in\mathbb R\) if and only if \(\rho_1^2=\rho_2^2\). If \(k\neq0\) and \(\rho_1^2\neq\rho_2^2\), then \(\operatorname{sign}\{C_z(k)-[2\Phi(z)-1]\}=\operatorname{sign}\{k(\rho_1^2-\rho_2^2)\}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each \(T_s\) is marginally standard normal and \(X\) is standard normal.  The boundary of the selected coverage event is contained in \(\{T_1=\pm z\}\cup\{T_2=\pm z\}\cup\{X=k\}\), which has probability zero.  Hence \(\texttt{thm:selected-gaussian-limit}\) transfers finite-sample coverage to the selected Gaussian probability.  For \(|\rho_s|<1\), completing the square in the bivariate Gaussian density gives
\[
 T_s\mid X=x\sim N(\rho_sx,1-\rho_s^2),
\]
and therefore \(\Pr(|T_s|\leq z\mid X=x)=g_{\rho_s}(x;z)\).  Conditioning on \(X\) proves
\[
 \Pr(|T_1|\leq z,X\leq k)+\Pr(|T_2|\leq z,X>k)=C_z(k).
\]
The endpoint cases \(|\rho_s|=1\) follow from the degenerate conditional identity \(T_s=\rho_sX\), equivalently by continuity.

Symmetry of \([-z,z]\) gives
\[
 g_\rho(-x;z)=g_\rho(x;z),
 \qquad
 \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\phi(x)g_\rho(x;z)\,dx=2\Phi(z)-1.
\]
Thus each half-line through zero contributes half of the unselected coverage, and \(C_z(0)=2\Phi(z)-1\) for arbitrary \(\rho_1,\rho_2\).

For \(a,z>0\) and \(q\in[0,1]\), let \(R_z(a,q)=\Pr_q(|X|\leq a,|T|\leq z)\), where the bivariate standard normal pair has correlation \(q\).  Symmetric rectangles make this quantity depend on a correlation only through its absolute value.  For \(0<q<1\), direct differentiation of the density \(f_q\) gives \(\partial_qf_q=\partial_x\partial_tf_q\).  Integration over \([-a,a]\times[-z,z]\) yields
\[
 \frac{\partial R_z(a,q)}{\partial q}
 =2\{f_q(a,z)-f_q(a,-z)\}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \frac{f_q(a,z)}{f_q(a,-z)}
 =\exp\left(\frac{2qaz}{1-q^2}\right)>1.
\]
Hence \(R_z(a,q)\) is strictly increasing in \(q\in[0,1]\), with endpoints obtained by continuity.  Evenness now gives, for \(k\ne0\),
\[
 C_z(k)-\{2\Phi(z)-1\}
 =\frac{\operatorname{sign}(k)}2
 \{R_z(|k|,|\rho_1|)-R_z(|k|,|\rho_2|)\}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[lem:gap-variance-inheritance]\label{lem:gap-variance-inheritance}
For every \(s\in\mathcal S\), \(V_s^{\mathrm{gap}}=(1-\varrho)^{-1}\operatorname{Var}(A_s)+r\operatorname{Var}(R_s)=V_s+\{\varrho/(1-\varrho)\}\operatorname{Var}(A_s)\geq V_s\geq\nu\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By the definition of \(V_s^{\mathrm{gap}}\) and the definition of \(V_s\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
V_s^{\mathrm{gap}}-V_s
&=\left\{(1-\varrho)^{-1}-1\right\}\operatorname{Var}(A_s)
  +r\operatorname{Var}(R_s)-r\operatorname{Var}(R_s)\\
&=\frac{\varrho}{1-\varrho}\operatorname{Var}(A_s).
\end{aligned}
\]
The declared restriction \(0<\varrho<1\) makes \(\varrho/(1-\varrho)>0\), and every variance is nonnegative.  Consequently
\[
V_s^{\mathrm{gap}}=V_s+\frac{\varrho}{1-\varrho}\operatorname{Var}(A_s)\geq V_s.
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}att\mbox{-}variance\mbox{-}margin}\) states, separately for each \(s\in\mathcal S\), that \(V_s\geq\nu\).  Combining the last two displays proves the claim for both roles.  No rank or joint-covariance inversion is used.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:known-case-reduction]\label{prop:known-case-reduction}
If one role is fixed in advance, or if \(|d|\to\infty\) so that one role is selected with probability tending to one, the selected limit and its symmetric-Wald coverage reduce to the fixed-role normal and nominal-coverage result; the gap-split estimator reduces as a construction to fixed-role late-block proximal inference, with its declared sample-splitting variance rather than the full-pre-period efficiency bound.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}

 \sqrt n(\widehat\tau_s-\tau)\Rightarrow A_s+\sqrt rR_s,
 \qquad
 \frac{\widehat\tau_s-\tau}{\widehat{\operatorname{se}}_s^{\mathrm{conv}}}
 \Rightarrow T_s\sim N(0,1).

\[
 N_{B,n}=n-\lfloor\varrho n\rfloor-\lceil(\log n)^2\rceil
 =(1-\varrho)n+o(n),
\]
the fixed-role late/post linearization has root-\(n\) variance

 =V_s+\frac{\varrho}{1-\varrho}\Omega_{\psi,s}\geq V_s.

\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:shi-anchor-transfer]\label{prop:shi-anchor-transfer}
The corrected Gaussian family in \(\texttt{def:primitive-gaussian-family}\), restricted to any compact neighborhood on which its bridge and fixed-role variance inequalities are strict, is a stationary independent-over-time, one-factor, square proximal synthetic-control subclass satisfying Shi et al.'s Assumptions 1--5, supplementary conditions D.1--D.10, and the fixed-role full-rank condition for either predeclared role.  \qquad

\[

which derives identification from the structural bridge.

The observations are independent and identically distributed over time, hence stationary, ergodic, and absolutely regular with zero positive-lag coefficients.  Gaussian rows have all moments and a Lebesgue density.  On a compact parameter neighborhood bounded away from zero factor, error, scaling, rank, and fixed-role variance coordinates, the moments have a common integrable envelope.  V_s=V_s^{\mathrm{gap}}=1>\nu.
\]
All defining conditions of \(\mathcal M(0)\) now hold.  The factor representation, constant effect, conditional error independence, nonzero square bridge, and rowwise domination established in \(\texttt{prop:shi-anchor-transfer}\) also place these arrays in the stated common published subclass.

Analyze row \(q\).  Let \(\mathcal N_q\) be the event that \(I_{q,t}=0\) for every \(t\in\mathcal B_q\cup\mathcal C_q\).  It is independent of \(\mathcal F_A\), and, because the two future blocks contain at most \(2q\) observations,
\[
 \Pr(\mathcal N_q)\geq(1-q^{-2})^{2q}\to1.
\]
The early label is \(\mathcal F_A\)-measurable.  Fix either possible selected donor \(j\), with opposite proxy \(k\).  On \(\mathcal N_q\), the true bridge residual is
\[
 r_{j,t}^0=-p_q+a_q(Z_{0t}-Z_{jt}).
\]
The exact future-only bridge definition gives, except on the null event \(\widehat M_{B,j}=0\),
\[
 \widehat\alpha_{B,j}-\alpha_j
 =\widehat M_{B,j}^{-1}N_{B,q}^{-1}
  \sum_{t\in\mathcal B_q}W_{kt}r_{j,t}^0.
\]
Conditional on \(\mathcal N_q\), Gaussian laws and Chebyshev's inequality give
\[
 \widehat M_{B,j}=c_0^2B_0+O_P(q^{-1/2}),quad
 \overline W_{k,B}=O_P(q^{-1/2}),quad
 \widehat\alpha_{B,j}-\alpha_j
 =O_P\{(p_q+a_q)q^{-1/2}\},
\]
uniformly over \(j=1,2\).  Similarly \(\overline W_{j,C}=O_P(q^{-1/2})\).  Hence the exact post average satisfies
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \widehat\tau_{B,C,j}-\tau
 &=\overline r_{j,C}^0-(\widehat\alpha_{B,j}-\alpha_j)\overline W_{j,C}\\
 &=-p_q+O_P(a_qq^{-1/2})+O_P\{(p_q+a_q)q^{-1}\}
 =-p_q\{1+o_P(1)\}.
 \end{aligned}
\]

For any centered scalar observations \(g_1,\ldots,g_N\), the Bartlett quadratic form obeys
\[
 0\leq\widehat\Omega_g\leq(2h+1)N^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^Ng_t^2,
\]
by \(2|g_tg_{t-j}|\leq g_t^2+g_{t-j}^2\).  The centered fitted post residual differs from \(a_q(Z_{0t}-Z_{jt})\) by the centered version of \(-(\widehat\alpha_{B,j}-\alpha_j)W_{jt}\).  Therefore
\[
 \widehat\Omega_{v,C,j}
 =O_P\left[h_q\left\{a_q^2+\frac{(p_q+a_q)^2}{q}\right\}\right].
\]
The precise future-only sensitivity is \(\widehat H_{B,C,j}=-\widehat\mu_{C,j}^{\prime}\widehat M_{B,j}^{-1}=O_P(q^{-1/2})\).  Before its multiplication, the fitted bridge score has conditional second moment \(O_P(p_q^2+a_q^2)\), so
\[
 \widehat\Omega_{\psi,B,j}=O_P\{h_q(p_q^2+a_q^2)/q\}.
\]
Since \(N_{B,q}\asymp q\), \(m_q=q\), \(h_q=O(q^{1/5})\), and \(a_q\leq p_q\),
\[
 \widehat S_{j,q}^2
 =O_P\left\{\frac{h_qa_q^2}{q}+\frac{h_qp_q^2}{q^2}\right\},
 \qquad
 \frac{\widehat S_{j,q}}{p_q}=o_P(1).
\]
Because \(a_q>0\), the centered post residual vector contains a nonconstant continuous Gaussian component almost surely.  The Bartlett quadratic form is nonnegative and its zero set is a proper algebraic subset of that continuous vector; hence \(\widehat S_{j,q}>0\) almost surely for large \(q\), so the nonpositive fallback is not invoked.  Combining numerator and denominator bounds yields, on \(\mathcal N_q\),
\[
 T_q=\frac{\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{gap}}-\tau}
 {\widehat{\operatorname{se}}_{\widehat J_A}}\to-\infty
\]
in probability, uniformly over both possible early labels.  Since \(\Pr(\mathcal N_q)\to1\), the same divergence holds unconditionally.

Choose deterministic \(b_q\to\infty\) so slowly that \(\Pr(T_q>-b_q)\to0\), and put
\[
 K_q=\sup_x\left|\Pr(T_q\leq x\mid\mathcal F_A)-\Phi(x)\right|.
\]
Evaluation at \(-b_q\) and Jensen's inequality give
\[
 \mathbb EK_q\geq
 \left|\Pr(T_q\leq-b_q)-\Phi(-b_q)\right|\to1.
\]
Since every Kolmogorov distance is at most one, \(\mathbb EK_q\to1\).  This is the asserted maximal failure.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The diagonal counterexample identifies the exact whole-array quantifier failure and sharpens the conditional Gaussian-approximation error to its maximal possible value. \textit{Gap.} Pointwise covariance limits and limiting variance margins do not control the finite row at which a supremum over whole arrays is evaluated. \textit{Consumer.} Together with the stabilized-class theorem, this establishes a positive/negative design boundary based on common finite-row horizon comparability and scalar variance stabilization for the proposed correction.

\begin{theorem}[thm:uniform-singular-mapping-fails]\label{thm:uniform-singular-mapping-fails}
The answer is negative.  Fix any \(D_{\mathrm{gap}}<\infty\), and suppose
\[
 0<\kappa<\frac{6373}{15369},
 \qquad
 0<\nu<\frac{39}{625},
\]
with a finite moment envelope \(C_{\mathrm{mom}}\) large enough to contain the bounded-moment counterexample in \(\texttt{thm:maximal-uniform-singular-mapping-failure}\).  Then
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}
 \sup_{(P_j)_{j\geq1}\in\mathcal M(D_{\mathrm{gap}})}
 \mathbb E_{P_n}\!\left[
  \sup_{x\in\mathbb R}
  \left|
   \Pr_{P_n}\!\left\{
    \frac{\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{gap}}-\tau}
         {\widehat{\operatorname{se}}_{\widehat J_A}}
    \leq x\,\middle|\,\mathcal F_A
   \right\}-\Phi(x)
  \right|
 \right]
 =1.
\]
The failure is witnessed by stationary independent-over-time square proximal arrays having constant treatment effect, singular stacked limiting covariance,
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(D)=\frac{39}{625},
 \qquad
 V_s^{\mathrm{gap}}=V_s=1
 \quad(s\in\mathcal S).
\]
Consequently the singular Gaussian and scalar-HAC mapping handle cannot establish the proposed whole-array uniform selector-conditional normal approximation from the frozen assumptions; a uniform finite-row variance and distributional-stabilization condition is necessary for that conclusion.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For a whole array \(P_\bullet\), define
\[
 \mathcal K_n(P_\bullet)=
 \mathbb E_{P_n}\left[
  \sup_x\left|
   \Pr_{P_n}\left\{
    \frac{\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{gap}}-\tau}
         {\widehat{\operatorname{se}}_{\widehat J_A}}
    \leq x\,\middle|\,\mathcal F_A
   \right\}-\Phi(x)
  \right|
 \right].
\]
It lies in \([0,1]\).  By \(\texttt{thm:maximal-uniform-singular-mapping-failure}\), for every sufficiently large \(q\) there is \(P_\bullet^{(q)}\in\mathcal M(0)\) with \(\mathcal K_q(P_\bullet^{(q)})\to1\), together with all structural and numerical witness properties in the theorem.

The only member restriction in \(\mathcal M(D_{\mathrm{gap}})\) depending on \(D_{\mathrm{gap}}\) is
\[
 \sup_{j\geq1}\sqrt j\,|L_{1,j}-L_{2,j}|\leq D_{\mathrm{gap}}.
\]
Every array in \(\mathcal M(0)\) satisfies it for any \(D_{\mathrm{gap}}\geq0\), so \(\mathcal M(0)\subseteq\mathcal M(D_{\mathrm{gap}})\).  Thus, with
\[
 S_n(D_{\mathrm{gap}})=\sup_{P_\bullet\in\mathcal M(D_{\mathrm{gap}})}\mathcal K_n(P_\bullet),
\]
we have
\[
 1\geq S_q(D_{\mathrm{gap}})\geq\mathcal K_q(P_\bullet^{(q)})\to1.
\]
Because \(q\) ranges over every sufficiently large integer, \(S_n(D_{\mathrm{gap}})\to1\).  The witness concerns this paper's gap-split procedure over its broad reduced-form class.  The common-subclass certificate does not assert failure of a published fixed-role estimator or guarantee.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:ridge-loss-expansion]\label{lem:ridge-loss-expansion}
Jointly for \(s\in\mathcal S\),
\[
 \sqrt n(\widehat L_s-L_s)
 =n^{-1/2}\sum_{t=1}^n(\ell_{s,t}-L_s)+o_{P_n}(1).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(s\in\mathcal S\).  Before treatment, \(Y_t=Y_t(0)\).  Define
\[
 \widehat K_s=I_{d_s}+n^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^nW_{D_s,t}W_{D_s,t}^{\prime},
 \qquad
 \widehat b_s=n^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^nW_{D_s,t}Y_t(0).
\]
The empirical ridge criterion has Hessian \(2\widehat K_s\succeq2I_{d_s}\), so its unique minimizer is \(\widehat a_s=\widehat K_s^{-1}\widehat b_s\).  If \(U_t,V_t\) are centered scalar functions of observations separated by \(j\) periods and have uniformly bounded \((2+\epsilon)\)-moments, truncation at a common level, the total-variation definition of absolute regularity, H\"older's inequality, and optimization over the truncation level give
\[
 |\operatorname{Cov}_{P_n}(U_t,V_{t-j})|
 \leq C\beta_n(j)^{\epsilon/(2+\epsilon)}.
\]
By \(\texttt{ass:geometric-beta-mixing}\) this is bounded by \(C_1e^{-c_1j}\), uniformly in \(n\).  Fixed dimension and \(\texttt{ass:moment-bound}\), applied to the quadratic entries of \(W_{D_s,t}W_{D_s,t}^{\prime}\) and \(W_{D_s,t}Y_t(0)\), imply
\[
 \widehat K_s-K_s=O_{P_n}(n^{-1/2}),
 \qquad \widehat b_s-b_s=O_{P_n}(n^{-1/2}).
\]
Indeed the variance of each centered sample mean is at most \(n^{-1}\{\gamma(0)+2\sum_{j\geq1}|\gamma(j)|\}\leq C/n\), so Chebyshev's inequality applies.  Since \(K_s\succeq I_{d_s}\), \(\|K_s^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq1\), and Cauchy--Schwarz gives \(\|b_s\|_2\leq C\).  Therefore \(\|a_s\|_2\leq C\), and the exact normal-equation identity
\[
 \widehat a_s-a_s
 =\widehat K_s^{-1}\{(\widehat b_s-b_s)
  -(\widehat K_s-K_s)a_s\}
\]
shows \(\widehat a_s-a_s=O_{P_n}(n^{-1/2})\).  Completing the empirical quadratic about its minimizer gives
\[
 \widehat L_s
 =\widehat Q_s(a_s)
  -(\widehat a_s-a_s)^{\prime}\widehat K_s(\widehat a_s-a_s).
\]
The last term is \(O_{P_n}(n^{-1})\), while the definition of \(\ell_{s,t}\) gives
\[
 \widehat Q_s(a_s)-L_s=n^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^n(\ell_{s,t}-L_s).
\]
Multiplication by \(\sqrt n\) proves the claimed expansion.  Since \(\mathcal S=\{1,2\}\), taking the maximum of the two remainder probabilities proves joint validity.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:gap-future-measurability]\label{lem:gap-future-measurability}
For every fixed \(s\in\mathcal S\), the counterfactual statistic
\[
 T_{s,n}^{\mathrm{gap}}
 =\frac{\widehat\tau_{B,C,s}-\tau}
        {\widehat{\operatorname{se}}_s}
\]
and every fitted object entering it are measurable with respect to \(\sigma(X_t:t\in\mathcal B_n\cup\mathcal C_n)\).  The selected statistic equals \(T_{\widehat J_A,n}^{\mathrm{gap}}\), while \(\widehat J_A\) is \(\mathcal F_A\)-measurable.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The sets \(\mathcal B_n\) and \(\mathcal C_n\) are deterministic.  For fixed \(s\), \(\widehat M_{B,s}\), its invertibility indicator, the inverse on that event, the fixed zero fallback, and \(\widehat\alpha_{B,s}\) are Borel functions of \((X_t:t\in\mathcal B_n)\).  The vector \(\widehat\mu_{C,s}\) is a Borel function of \((X_t:t\in\mathcal C_n)\).  Hence \(\widehat H_{B,C,s}\), including its fixed fallback, is measurable with respect to \(\sigma(X_t:t\in\mathcal B_n\cup\mathcal C_n)\).  The displayed finite sums defining \(\widehat u_{B,s,t}\), \(\widehat\psi_{B,s,t}\), \(\widehat\tau_{B,C,s}\), \(\widehat v_{C,s,t}\), their sample means and lag products, the two Bartlett estimates, and the piecewise square-root fallback are compositions of Borel maps of the same future observations.  Thus \(T_{s,n}^{\mathrm{gap}}\) is future-measurable.  Finally \(\widehat J_A\) is defined from the observations generating \(\mathcal F_A\), and substitution into the fixed-role formula gives \(T_{\widehat J_A,n}^{\mathrm{gap}}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:uniform-gap-split-validity]\label{thm:uniform-gap-split-validity}
For fixed common constants in \(\texttt{def:panel-stabilized-class}\), the future-only gap-split statistic satisfies
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}
 \sup_{(P_j)_{j\geq1}\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{stab}}(D_{\mathrm{gap}})}
 \mathbb E_{P_n}\!\left[
  \sup_{x\in\mathbb R}
  \left|
   \Pr_{P_n}\!\left\{
    \frac{\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{gap}}-\tau}
         {\widehat{\operatorname{se}}_{\widehat J_A}}
    \leq x\,\middle|\,\mathcal F_A
   \right\}-\Phi(x)
  \right|
 \right]=0.
\]
The conclusion uses only coordinatewise scalar normalization and therefore permits a singular covariance matrix after the two roles are stacked.  The class is nonempty: with \(m_n=n\), the symmetric Gaussian point \(\theta_0\) in \(\texttt{prop:primitive-realizations}\) belongs to such a class for common horizon constants satisfying \(c_m\leq1\leq C_m\), a sufficiently large common moment envelope, and finite positive variance bounds chosen around \(\frac{3}{2(1-\varrho)}+2\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write \(N_B=N_{B,n}\).  The common horizon inequalities and the definition of \(\mathcal B_n\) give, uniformly over the class,
\[
 N_B/n\to1-\varrho,
 \qquad c_m\leq m/n\leq C_m
\]
for all \(n\geq n_\star\).  The moment bound, Cauchy--Schwarz, and \(\|M_s^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\kappa^{-1}\) imply common bounds on \(\alpha_s,\mu_s,H_s\).  Consequently there is \(\epsilon>0\) such that both \(\psi_{s,t}\) and \(v_{s,t}\) have uniformly bounded \((2+\epsilon)\)-moments.  Because they are measurable functions of \(X_t\), their absolute-regularity coefficients are bounded by those of \(X_t\).

The covariance truncation argument used in \(\texttt{lem:ridge-loss-expansion}\) yields, uniformly over rows, roles, and lags,
\[
 |\operatorname{Cov}(g_t,g_{t-j})|\leq C e^{-cj}
\]
for any centered scalar coordinate \(g_t\) among the oracle scores and the finite collection of moment coordinates used below.  It follows by summing covariances and applying Chebyshev's inequality that
\[
 \widehat M_{B,s}-M_s=O_P(N_B^{-1/2}),quad
 \widehat\mu_{C,s}-\mu_s=O_P(m^{-1/2}),quad
 N_B^{-1}\sum_{t\in\mathcal B_n}u_{s,t}=O_P(N_B^{-1/2}),
\]
uniformly for \(s=1,2\).  The rank margin implies that the probability of the inverse fallback tends uniformly to zero.  On the invertibility event the bridge equation gives exactly
\[
 \widehat\alpha_{B,s}-\alpha_s
 =\widehat M_{B,s}^{-1}N_B^{-1}\sum_{t\in\mathcal B_n}u_{s,t},
\]
so \(\widehat\alpha_{B,s}-\alpha_s=O_P(N_B^{-1/2})\), and matrix inversion gives
\[
 \widehat H_{B,C,s}-H_s=O_P(N_B^{-1/2}+m^{-1/2}).
\]
Using bridge centering and \(\tau=\mathbb E_n\Delta_t\), the post identity is
\[
 \widehat\tau_{B,C,s}-\tau
 =m^{-1}\sum_{t\in\mathcal C_n}v_{s,t}
 -\widehat\mu_{C,s}^{\prime}(\widehat\alpha_{B,s}-\alpha_s).
\]
Substituting the exact bridge equation and \(H_s=-\mu_s^{\prime}M_s^{-1}\) shows
\[
 \sqrt n(\widehat\tau_{B,C,s}-\tau)=G_{s,n}+r_{s,n},
 \qquad
 \max_{s\in\mathcal S}|r_{s,n}|=o_P(1)
\]
uniformly over \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{stab}}(D_{\mathrm{gap}})\).  Every product in the remainder contains one \(O_P(n^{-1/2})\) moment-estimation error and one \(O_P(n^{-1/2})\) score mean before multiplication by \(\sqrt n\).

We next prove a uniform scalar Gaussian approximation for \(G_{s,n}\).  Its summands have deterministic weights bounded by \(C n^{-1/2}\).  Partition \(\mathcal B_n\cup\mathcal C_n\) into consecutive large blocks of length \(\ell_n=\lfloor n^{1/3}\rfloor\), separated by gaps of length \(g_n=\lceil(\log n)^2\rceil\), allowing one block boundary at the change from the pre-score to the post-score.  Exponential covariance decay makes the variance of the omitted normalized gaps \(O(g_n/\ell_n)=o(1)\).  For bounded complex functions of successive retained blocks, the total-variation definition of \(\beta\) gives recursively
\[
 \left|\mathbb E\prod_{j=1}^{K_n}f_j-prod_{j=1}^{K_n}\mathbb Ef_j\right|
 \leq2(K_n-1)\beta_n(g_n)
 \leq Cn e^{-c(\log n)^2}=o(1).
\]
Applied to characteristic functions, this compares the retained sum with a sum of independent variables having the retained block marginals.

For completeness, truncation at \(\ell_n^{1/(2+\epsilon)}\), expansion of products, and the same covariance inequality give the block moment bound
\[
 \mathbb E|B_{j,n}|^{2+\epsilon}\leq
 C(\ell_n/n)^{1+\epsilon/2}
\]
for each normalized retained block sum \(B_{j,n}\).  Hence
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^{K_n}\mathbb E|B_{j,n}|^{2+\epsilon}
 \leq C(\ell_n/n)^{\epsilon/2}\to0.
\]
Taylor's formula for the characteristic function of each independent centered block, followed by multiplication, proves a normal limit after division by the independent-block standard deviation.  The variance removed with the gaps is \(o(1)\), and the sum of all cross-block covariances discarded by independence is bounded by
\[
 C K_n\sum_{h\geq g_n}e^{-ch}=o(1).
\]
Therefore the independent-block variance differs from \(W_{s,n}\) by \(o(1)\), uniformly.  Since \(\underline w\leq W_{s,n}\leq\overline w\), division is uniformly safe.  The characteristic-function conclusion plus continuity of \(\Phi\), applied along an arbitrary allegedly failing sequence of rows and laws, yields the uniform Kolmogorov conclusion
\[
 \max_{s\in\mathcal S}\sup_{P_\bullet\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{stab}}}
 \sup_x\left|\Pr_{P_n}\{G_{s,n}/\sqrt{W_{s,n}}\leq x\}-\Phi(x)\right|\to0.
\]
This subsequence argument requires no convergence, and no inverse, of the covariance obtained by stacking the roles.

It remains to justify fitted Bartlett studentization.  Let \(\widetilde\Omega_{\psi,B,s}\) and \(\widetilde\Omega_{v,C,s}\) denote the same Bartlett formulas computed from centered oracle \(\psi_{s,t}\) and \(v_{s,t}\).  Exponential covariance decay gives the uniform bias bound
\[
 \left|\mathbb E\widetilde\Omega_{g}-\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}\operatorname{Cov}(g_t,g_{t-j})\right|
 \leq C\{h_n^{-1}+h_n/N+e^{-ch_n}\},
\]
where \(N=N_B\) or \(m\).  Expanding the covariance of the lag-product averages and applying the same truncation inequality to fourfold products gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(\widetilde\Omega_g)\leq C h_n^2/N=o(1),
\]
because \(h_n\asymp n^{1/5}\).  Thus each oracle Bartlett estimate is uniformly consistent for its row long-run variance.

The exact finite-block variance satisfies
\[
 W_{s,n}=n\operatorname{Var}\left(N_B^{-1}\sum_{\mathcal B_n}\psi_{s,t}\right)
 +n\operatorname{Var}\left(m^{-1}\sum_{\mathcal C_n}v_{s,t}\right)+o(1).
\]
Indeed the omitted cross covariance is bounded by
\[
 \frac{Cn}{N_Bm}\sum_{a\geq0}\sum_{b\geq1}e^{-c(a+b)}=O(n^{-1}),
\]
and the difference between either finite-block variance and its Bartlett-weighted long-run expression is \(o(1)\) by the preceding bias calculation.  Consequently
\[
 n\left\{\frac{\widetilde\Omega_{\psi,B,s}}{N_B}
 +\frac{\widetilde\Omega_{v,C,s}}m\right\}-W_{s,n}=o_P(1)
\]
uniformly.

The already proved parameter rates and Cauchy--Schwarz imply
\[
 N_B^{-1}\sum_{\mathcal B_n}(\widehat\psi_{B,s,t}-\psi_{s,t})^2=o_P(1),
 \qquad
 m^{-1}\sum_{\mathcal C_n}(\widehat v_{C,s,t}-v_{s,t})^2=o_P(1)
\]
uniformly.  For two centered sequences \(a_t,b_t\), summing \(|a_tb_{t-j}|\leq(a_t^2+b_{t-j}^2)/2\) over the \(h_n\) lags bounds the Bartlett replacement error by \(C h_n\) times the root mean-square score error times a uniformly tight score norm.  The parameter errors above are \(O_P(n^{-1/2})\), so \(h_n=O(n^{1/5})\) makes both fitted-score replacement errors \(o_P(1)\).  Therefore
\[
 \max_s|n\widehat S_{s,n}^2-W_{s,n}|=o_P(1).
\]
The lower bound \(W_{s,n}\geq\underline w\) makes the nonpositive fallback probability tend uniformly to zero.

Let \(U_{s,n}=G_{s,n}/\sqrt{W_{s,n}}\).  The linearization and variance conclusion imply that the fitted statistic differs from \(U_{s,n}\) by \(o_P(1)\), uniformly.  For every \(\delta>0\), normal anti-concentration gives
\[
 \sup_x|\Pr(T_{s,n}^{\mathrm{gap}}\leq x)-\Phi(x)|
 \leq \sup_x|\Pr(U_{s,n}\leq x)-\Phi(x)|
 +\Pr(|T_{s,n}^{\mathrm{gap}}-U_{s,n}|>\delta)
 +\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{2\pi}}.
\]
First sending \(n\to\infty\), then \(\delta\downarrow0\), proves uniform unconditional studentized normality.

By \(\texttt{lem:gap-future-measurability}\), the fixed-role statistic is measurable with respect to the future sigma-field beginning after the discarded gap.  Absolute regularity therefore gives, uniformly in \(x\),
\[
 \mathbb E_{P_n}\left|
 \Pr_{P_n}(T_{s,n}^{\mathrm{gap}}\leq x\mid\mathcal F_A)
 -\Pr_{P_n}(T_{s,n}^{\mathrm{gap}}\leq x)
 \right|\leq2\beta_n(b_n)=o(1).
\]
Monotonicity of distribution functions lets the same bound transfer to the supremum over \(x\) by evaluating first on a finite normal-quantile grid and controlling the intervening normal increments.  Thus the expected conditional Kolmogorov distance tends uniformly to zero for each role.  Since \(\widehat J_A\) is \(\mathcal F_A\)-measurable,
\[
 \Pr(T_{\widehat J_A,n}^{\mathrm{gap}}\leq x\mid\mathcal F_A)
 =\sum_{s=1}^2\mathbf1\{\widehat J_A=s\}
  \Pr(T_{s,n}^{\mathrm{gap}}\leq x\mid\mathcal F_A),
\]
so its distance is bounded by the maximum of the two fixed-role distances.  This proves the asserted selected result.

For nonemptiness, take the symmetric primitive Gaussian law \(\theta_0\), \(m_n=n\), and the precise future-only definitions above.  The sample inverse fallback and variance fallback have probabilities tending to zero by the scalar positive bridge moment and positive Gaussian score variance.  Independence makes all cross-block covariances zero, and direct substitution from \(\texttt{prop:primitive-realizations}\) gives
\[
 W_{s,n}\to\frac{3}{2(1-\varrho)}+2>0,
 \qquad s=1,2.
\]
Choose \(n_\star\) and common \(\underline w,\overline w\) around this limit and take \(c_m\leq1\leq C_m\).  All other class conditions hold by the primitive and anchor propositions.  Hence the strengthened class is nonempty.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem supplies a complete positive counterpart to the broad-class converse using a precisely future-measurable estimator and coordinatewise normalization that remains valid under singular stacked covariance. \textit{Gap.} Generic splitting and HAC results do not by themselves compose the proximal inverse, fitted-score replacement, random early label, and whole-array supremum; the proof closes those steps under explicit primitive stabilization. \textit{Consumer.} An analyst can predeclare two roles, select early, fit and studentize only on future blocks, and report a uniformly justified interval after verifying the common horizon and finite-row scalar variance margins.

\section{Interpretation}

For a predeclared pair of scientifically credible donor/proxy roles, conventional two-sided Wald inference is robust to local selection exactly on the squared-correlation frontier, even though the selected statistic can have nonzero mean. Off that frontier the sign formula diagnoses under- or overcoverage. If that covariance diagnostic is not defensible, the gap-split interval has a uniform justification only after the design fixes common horizon comparability and verifies finite-row scalar influence-variance margins.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The positive theorem is uniform only over \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{stab}}(D_{\mathrm{gap}})\), whose horizon constants, threshold, and finite-row scalar variance bounds are common before the supremum is taken. It does not require nonsingularity of the stacked role covariance, but it does require fixed dimension, uniform geometric beta mixing, the declared high moment and bridge-rank margins, and future-only fitted scores. The broad class \(\mathcal M(D_{\mathrm{gap}})\) retains only arraywise limiting horizon and covariance conditions; the diagonal counterexample exploits that difference and makes the conditional approximation error maximal.

\section{Honest scope}

The exact coverage result concerns two predeclared square bridges, ridge penalty one, bounded root-\(n\) selector gaps, and positive marginal selector and ATT variances. The conventional interval reuses the full pre period. The corrected interval uses a fixed early fraction, a logarithm-squared gap, late bridge fitting, post residual averaging, and bandwidth exponent one fifth; its uniform theorem additionally fixes common finite-row horizon and scalar oracle-variance bounds. The Gaussian chart and diagonal witness lie in an explicit stationary constant-effect intersection with Shi et al.'s fixed-role class, but no wholesale class extension or refutation of a published fixed-role guarantee is claimed. The analysis does not cover the rectangular simplex-constrained West-German rule, estimate the local drift, restore full-pre-period efficiency, or handle more than two roles. Its actionable use is a predeclared two-candidate robustness analysis before retaining the negative GDP conclusion.
