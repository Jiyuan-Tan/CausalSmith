%%% FIELD pilot-regret-proof
Fix \(\mathcal C\in\mathfrak C_K(G)\).  For every ordered pair \((i,i')\in U\times U\), the definition of the exposure-label sets gives \(L_i(\mathcal C),L_{i'}(\mathcal C)\subseteq\{1,\ldots,K\}\).  Hence, by the definition of \(m_{ii'}(\mathcal C)\),
\[
0\le m_{ii'}(\mathcal C)\le K.
\]
Consequently the coefficient appearing in \(\mathrm{def:exact-risk}\) satisfies, in the two cases \(m_{ii'}(\mathcal C)=0\) and \(m_{ii'}(\mathcal C)>0\), respectively,
\[
a_{ii'}(\mathcal C)=0
\quad\text{or}\quad
a_{ii'}(\mathcal C)=2^{m_{ii'}(\mathcal C)+1}\le 2^{K+1}.
\]
Thus \(0\le a_{ii'}(\mathcal C)\le 2^{K+1}\) on the full ordered-pair support \(U\times U\).  Since \(N_{\exp}>0\), the definition of the exact risk, the triangle inequality, this coefficient bound, and \(\mathrm{def:ordered-norm}\), in that order, yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|V(\mathcal C;\widehat\Sigma)-V(\mathcal C;\Sigma)\right|
&=N_{\exp}^{-1}\left|\sum_{i,i'\in U}a_{ii'}(\mathcal C)
       \bigl(\widehat\Sigma_{ii'}-\Sigma_{ii'}\bigr)\right|\\
&\le N_{\exp}^{-1}\sum_{i,i'\in U}a_{ii'}(\mathcal C)
       \left|\widehat\Sigma_{ii'}-\Sigma_{ii'}\right|\\
&\le 2^{K+1}N_{\exp}^{-1}\sum_{i,i'\in U}
       \left|\widehat\Sigma_{ii'}-\Sigma_{ii'}\right|\\
&=2^{K+1}N_{\exp}^{-1}
       \left\|\widehat\Sigma-\Sigma\right\|_{1,\mathrm{ord}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves the first assertion.  Put
\[
\varepsilon=2^{K+1}N_{\exp}^{-1}
       \left\|\widehat\Sigma-\Sigma\right\|_{1,\mathrm{ord}}.
\]
By the declared definitions of \(\widehat{\mathcal C}\) and \(\mathcal C^\star\), both designs belong to the same feasible class \(\mathfrak C_K(G)\), and their two optimizer inequalities are
\[
V(\widehat{\mathcal C};\widehat\Sigma)
 \le V(\mathcal C^\star;\widehat\Sigma)
\quad\text{and}\quad
V(\mathcal C^\star;\Sigma)
 \le V(\widehat{\mathcal C};\Sigma).
\]
The second inequality immediately proves the nonnegative lower bound on true-design regret.  For the upper bound, add and subtract the two plug-in risks and use the first optimizer inequality:
\[
\begin{aligned}
V(\widehat{\mathcal C};\Sigma)-V(\mathcal C^\star;\Sigma)
&=\bigl[V(\widehat{\mathcal C};\Sigma)
          -V(\widehat{\mathcal C};\widehat\Sigma)\bigr]\\
&\quad+\bigl[V(\widehat{\mathcal C};\widehat\Sigma)
          -V(\mathcal C^\star;\widehat\Sigma)\bigr]\\
&\quad+\bigl[V(\mathcal C^\star;\widehat\Sigma)
          -V(\mathcal C^\star;\Sigma)\bigr]\\
&\le
 \left|V(\widehat{\mathcal C};\Sigma)
          -V(\widehat{\mathcal C};\widehat\Sigma)\right|
 +\left|V(\mathcal C^\star;\widehat\Sigma)
          -V(\mathcal C^\star;\Sigma)\right|\\
&\le 2\varepsilon
=2^{K+2}N_{\exp}^{-1}
       \left\|\widehat\Sigma-\Sigma\right\|_{1,\mathrm{ord}},
\end{aligned}
\]
where the final inequality applies the first assertion to each of the two feasible designs.  The argument is finite and deterministic; it uses no division other than by the declared positive integer \(N_{\exp}\), no matrix inversion, conditioning, rank condition, optimizer uniqueness, boundary limit, or asymptotic passage.
