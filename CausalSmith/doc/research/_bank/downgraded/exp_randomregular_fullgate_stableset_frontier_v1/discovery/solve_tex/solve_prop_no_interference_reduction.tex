Fix an index \(n\) and suppose \(d=0\).  Because \(G\) is a simple \(0\)-regular graph, it has no edges.  Hence \(N_G[i]=\{i\}\), \(\operatorname{dist}_G(i,j)=\infty\) for distinct \(i,j\in V\), and
\[
F_G(A)=V\setminus A. \tag{1}
\]
In particular, \(|F_G(A)|=n-k\ge k\), since \(k\le\lfloor n/4\rfloor\).  Thus the first capsule trial is accepted under \(\text{def:reservoir-capsule}\).

Conditional on \(A\), the set \(C\) is uniform among the \(k\)-subsets of \(V\setminus A\).  Moreover, for each fixed \(k\)-subset \(S\subseteq V\),
\[
\Pr(C=S\mid G)
=\sum_{\substack{A\subseteq V:\ |A|=k,\ A\cap S=\varnothing}}
 \frac{1}{\binom nk}\frac{1}{\binom{n-k}{k}}
=\frac{\binom{n-k}{k}}{\binom nk\binom{n-k}{k}}
=\frac1{\binom nk}. \tag{2}
\]
Thus both \(A\) and \(C\) are marginally uniform \(k\)-subsets, and they are disjoint.  The independent fair arm-swap coin therefore makes each of \(T\) and \(C_0\) marginally uniform among the \(k\)-subsets of \(V\), while \(T\cap C_0=\varnothing\).  By \(\text{def:reservoir-capsule}\), the units outside \(T\cup C_0\) receive conditionally independent fair assignments.  This proves the asserted description of the design.

For each \(i\in V\) and \(a\in\{0,1\}\), (2) and the arm swap give
\[
\pi_{(i,a)}=\frac{k}{n}>0. \tag{3}
\]
Since the accepted capsule reveals the appropriate endpoint by \(\text{prop:capsule-realization}\), substitution of (3) into \(\text{def:raw-estimator}\) yields the well-defined raw estimator
\[
\widetilde\tau_G
=\frac1k\sum_{i\in T}y_{i1}-\frac1k\sum_{i\in C_0}y_{i0}. \tag{4}
\]
Each term in (4) is a simple-random-sample mean of its fixed endpoint array.  Indeed, if \(S\) is a uniform \(k\)-subset, \(I_i=\mathbf 1\{i\in S\}\), \(\bar x=n^{-1}\sum_i x_i\), and \(\bar x_S=k^{-1}\sum_i I_i x_i\), then
\[
\mathbb E I_i=\frac{k}{n},\qquad
\mathbb E(I_iI_j)=\frac{k(k-1)}{n(n-1)}\quad(i\ne j). \tag{5}
\]
Expanding the variance with (5) gives
\[
\operatorname{Var}(\bar x_S)
=\frac{n-k}{k(n-1)}\left\{\frac1n\sum_{i\in V}(x_i-\bar x)^2\right\}. \tag{6}
\]
In particular, (5) also shows directly that the two sample means in (4) are unbiased for the corresponding population endpoint means, so (4) is unbiased for \(\tau_G\).

For every \(y\in\mathcal M_2(G)\), the two endpoint moment restrictions in \(\text{def:endpoint-class}\) imply
\[
\frac1n\sum_i(y_{ia}-\bar y_a)^2
=\frac1n\sum_i y_{ia}^2-\bar y_a^2\le 1,
\qquad a\in\{0,1\}. \tag{7}
\]
Writing the two sample means in (4) as \(U\) and \(W\), respectively, the elementary inequality
\(\operatorname{Var}(U-W)\le 2\operatorname{Var}(U)+2\operatorname{Var}(W)\), together with (6)--(7), gives
\[
\sup_{y\in\mathcal M_2(G)}
\mathbb E[(\widetilde\tau_G-\tau_G)^2\mid G]
\le \frac{4(n-k)}{k(n-1)}. \tag{8}
\]
Condition (3) permits application of \(\text{lem:exact-risk-certificate}\), which proves that \(\widehat\tau_G\) is unbiased and that Rao--Blackwellization cannot increase the mean squared error.  Consequently the left side of (8) remains an upper bound with \(\widehat\tau_G\) in place of \(\widetilde\tau_G\).  Finally,
\[
\frac{k}{n}\longrightarrow \frac{\log 2}{32}, \tag{9}
\]
because \(0<\log 2/32<1/4\).  Equations (8)--(9) show that the capsule and its Rao--Blackwell estimator constitute an admissible outcome-independent design--estimator pair with worst-case risk \(O(n^{-1})\).  By \(\text{def:minimax-risk}\),
\[
R_2(G)\le \frac{C}{n} \tag{10}
\]
for an absolute constant \(C\) and all sufficiently large \(n\).

It remains to show that no other outcome-independent design and estimator can improve the order in (10).  Introduce a prior consisting of mutually independent Rademacher variables \(\{\varepsilon_{ia}:i\in V,\ a\in\{0,1\}\}\), and, for every prior realization, define the entire potential-outcome schedule by
\[
y_i(z)=\varepsilon_{i,z_i}. \tag{11}
\]
Because \(N_G[i]=\{i\}\), equality of \(z\) and \(z'\) on \(N_G[i]\) implies \(z_i=z_i'\), so (11) satisfies \(\text{ass:ani}\).  Its endpoints obey
\[
\frac1n\sum_i y_{ia}^2=1,
\qquad a\in\{0,1\}, \tag{12}
\]
so every schedule in the support of this prior belongs to \(\mathcal M_2(G)\) by \(\text{def:endpoint-class}\).

Now fix any outcome-independent assignment law \(\mathsf D\).  Independence of the design from the schedule implies that, conditional on a realized assignment \(Z=z\) and observed outcomes \(y(z)=(\varepsilon_{i,z_i})_{i\in V}\), the unobserved variables \(\varepsilon_{i,1-z_i}\) remain mutually independent Rademacher variables.  The target decomposes as
\[
\tau_G
=\frac1n\sum_{i\in V}
\left\{(2z_i-1)\varepsilon_{i,z_i}
 +(1-2z_i)\varepsilon_{i,1-z_i}\right\}. \tag{13}
\]
The first summand inside the braces is observed, whereas each term in the second summand has conditional variance one and the terms are conditionally independent.  Therefore, for the observed-data sigma-field \(\mathscr O=\sigma(G,Z,y(Z))\),
\[
\operatorname{Var}(\tau_G\mid\mathscr O)
=\frac1{n^2}\sum_{i\in V}(1-2Z_i)^2
=\frac1n \quad\text{almost surely}. \tag{14}
\]
For any possibly randomized estimator \(T_*\) based on \(\mathscr O\), adjoin its independent randomization seed to \(\mathscr O\).  Conditional expectation is the squared-error minimizer, and the seed contains no information about the unobserved endpoint signs; hence
\[
\mathbb E[(T_*-\tau_G)^2]
\ge \mathbb E[\operatorname{Var}(\tau_G\mid\mathscr O)]
=\frac1n. \tag{15}
\]
The prior is supported on \(\mathcal M_2(G)\) by (11)--(12), so the supremum risk of this fixed pair \((\mathsf D,T_*)\) is at least its prior-average risk in (15).  Taking the infimum over all eligible pairs in \(\text{def:minimax-risk}\) gives
\[
R_2(G)\ge \frac1n. \tag{16}
\]
Combining (10) and (16) proves \(R_2(G)=\Theta(n^{-1})\) when \(d=0\).

For the required smallest-instance audit, take \(n=4\).  Then \(k=1\).  There are twelve ordered choices \((T,C_0)\) with distinct entries, each equally likely, and the two remaining units have four equiprobable fair-coin assignments conditional on that pair.  On every such design history (4) equals \(y_{T,1}-y_{C_0,0}\), and direct averaging over the twelve ordered pairs gives
\[
\frac1{12}\sum_{t\ne c}(y_{t1}-y_{c0})
=\frac14\sum_{i=1}^4(y_{i1}-y_{i0})
=\tau_G. \tag{17}
\]
The treated count is \(1+\operatorname{Binomial}(2,1/2)\), so it takes the values \(1,2,3\); thus the audit also confirms that the capsule is the stated two-stage design rather than a fixed balanced complete randomization.  Under the supported prior (11), (14) equals \(1/4\) for every assignment in this enumeration, confirming the lower-bound calculation at the boundary instance.
