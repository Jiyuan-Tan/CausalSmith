If \(N<b_{\beta,D}\log(40m)\), set \(I_{\pi}=G_{\tau}\) for every \(\pi\in\Pi_m\). Otherwise use \(T^{\mathrm{ci}}_{n,m}=\{N/\log(40m)\}^{1/(2\beta+4)}\). At \(z=T^{\mathrm{ci}}_{n,m},eT^{\mathrm{ci}}_{n,m}\), set \(J_{xa}(z)=[0,1]\cap 2m\operatorname{CP}_{\gamma_0/(4m)}\{K_{xa}(z);n\}\) when this intersection is nonempty, and set \(J_{xa}(z)=\{0\}\) otherwise. Let the raw region be the intersection of \([1,4]\times[-\log2,0]\) with the two induced Hall strips displayed in the symbol definition of \(\Theta_{xa}\). If this raw region is nonempty, set \(\Theta_{xa}\) equal to it; if it is empty, set \(\Theta_{xa}:=\{(1,-\log2)\}\). For every \(\pi\in\Pi_m\), minimize and maximize the unique root of \(m^{-1}\sum_x\exp(\widetilde v_x-\widetilde\alpha_xt)=\tau\) over \((\widetilde\alpha_x,\widetilde v_x)\in\Theta_{x,\pi(x)}\), obtaining \(\underline t_{\pi}\) and \(\overline t_{\pi}\), and put \(J^*_{\pi}:=[\underline t_{\pi}-\varepsilon_{\tau},\overline t_{\pi}+\varepsilon_{\tau}]\). If \(J^*_{\pi}\cap G_{\tau}\neq\varnothing\), set \(I_{\pi}:=J^*_{\pi}\cap G_{\tau}\). If the intersection is empty, set \(I_{\pi}\) equal to the singleton containing the metric projection of the midpoint of \(J^*_{\pi}\) onto \(G_{\tau}\). All set comparisons and lexicographic tie resolutions are exact; Clopper--Pearson endpoints and the scalar roots may equivalently be computed to arbitrary prescribed accuracy by monotone bisection, and the finite policy class may be enumerated.

We prove the claim for the corrected construction proposed together with this proof.  Fix admissible \(n,m,\tau\) and \(P\in\mathcal H_{\beta,D}(m)\).  Write \(T=T^{\mathrm{ci}}_{n,m}\), \(\rho=\gamma _0/(4m)\), and, for the moment, fix \(x,a\) and one of the two thresholds \(z\in\{T,eT\}\).

\emph{Totality and computability.}  The binomial upper and lower tails are continuous and strictly monotone in their success probability away from the two boundary cases in \(\mathrm{def{:}clopper\text{-}pearson}\); consequently every Clopper--Pearson endpoint is uniquely defined and can be obtained by scalar bisection.  The correction makes \(J_{xa}(z)\) a nonempty closed interval even when the preliminary intersection with \([0,1]\) is empty.  After constraints containing \(\log0=-\infty\) are interpreted as stated in the definition, the raw set defining \(\Theta_{xa}\) is an intersection of a compact rectangle with finitely many closed half-spaces.  It is therefore compact when nonempty, and its replacement is a compact singleton otherwise.

For any \((\widetilde\alpha_x,\widetilde v_x)_{x=1}^m\) in a product of these regions, the function
\[
 t\longmapsto \frac1m\sum_{x=1}^m
       \exp(\widetilde v_x-\widetilde\alpha_xt)
\]
is continuous and strictly decreasing from \(+\infty\) to \(0\), because \(1\leq\widetilde\alpha_x\leq4\).  It consequently has exactly one root at level \(\tau>0\).  The root is continuous in the finitely many parameters: indeed the comparison argument in the width proof below bounds a change of the root by the largest change of a component log survival.  Compactness therefore gives the stated minimum and maximum.  The class \(\Pi_m\) is finite by \(\mathrm{def{:}policy\text{-}class}\), and \(\mathrm{prop{:}finite\text{-}capacity\text{-}inversion}\) supplies the finite policy-level inversion.  Thus finite enumeration, finite-dimensional linear optimization at each trial value of \(t\), and scalar bisection compute every endpoint.  The projection branch is also defined because \(G_\tau\) is a nonempty compact interval.  Hence the confidence system is total and computable.

\emph{Exact simultaneous coverage.}  By \(\mathrm{ass{:}uniform\text{-}cells}\), \(\mathrm{ass{:}treatment\text{-}probability}\), \(\mathrm{ass{:}conditional\text{-}randomization}\), and \(\mathrm{ass{:}consistency}\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 p_{xa}(z)
 &:=P\{X=x,A=a,L>z\}\\
 &=P(X=x)P(A=a\mid X=x)
   P\{L(a)>z\mid X=x,A=a\}\\
 &=\frac{S_{xa}(z)}{2m}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The product sampling law in \(\mathrm{def{:}sample\text{-}experiment}\) therefore gives
\[
 K_{xa}(z)\sim\operatorname{Bin}\{n,p_{xa}(z)\}.
\]
For completeness, let \([q_-(k),q_+(k)]=\operatorname{CP}_{\rho}(k;n)\).  Monotonicity of the binomial upper tail in its success probability shows that
\[
 \{p_{xa}(z)<q_-(K_{xa}(z))\}
 \subseteq
 \left\{P_{p_{xa}(z)}\{\operatorname{Bin}(n,p_{xa}(z))\geq K_{xa}(z)\}<\rho/2\right\}.
\]
The possible values of \(K_{xa}(z)\) on the right form an upper tail, whose probability is at most \(\rho/2\).  The analogous argument with the lower binomial tail gives
\[
 P\{p_{xa}(z)>q_+(K_{xa}(z))\}\leq\rho/2.
\]
Thus
\[
 P\{p_{xa}(z)\in\operatorname{CP}_{\rho}(K_{xa}(z);n)\}\geq1-\rho.
\]
No independence among the \(4m\) counts is needed.  A union bound over \(2m\) cell--arm pairs and two thresholds yields an event \(\mathcal E\) satisfying
\[
 \inf_{P\in\mathcal H_{\beta,D}(m)}P(\mathcal E)
 \geq 1-4m\rho=1-\gamma _0,
\]
on which \(S_{xa}(z)\in J_{xa}(z)\) for every indexed \(x,a,z\).  Indeed \(p_{xa}(z)\leq1/(2m)\), so on \(\mathcal E\) the preliminary intersection defining \(J_{xa}(z)\) is nonempty and contains \(2mp_{xa}(z)=S_{xa}(z)\).

Put \(v_{xa}=\log c_{xa}\).  By \(\mathrm{ass{:}scale\text{-}range}\), \(v_{xa}\in[-\log2,0]\), and by \(\mathrm{ass{:}index\text{-}range}\), \(\alpha_{xa}\in[1,4]\).  The Hall identity and \(\mathrm{ass{:}hall\text{-}envelope}\) give, at each of the two thresholds,
\[
 S_{xa}(z)=\exp(v_{xa}-\alpha_{xa}\log z)\{1+r_{xa}(z)\},
 \qquad -Dz^{-\beta}\leq r_{xa}(z)\leq Dz^{-\beta}.
\]
Because \(Dz^{-\beta}\leq D\leq1/4\), both divisors below are positive.  On \(\mathcal E\), division by \(1+r_{xa}(z)\) gives
\[
 \frac{\underline S_{xa}(z)}{1+Dz^{-\beta}}
 \leq \exp(v_{xa}-\alpha_{xa}\log z)
 \leq \frac{\overline S_{xa}(z)}{1-Dz^{-\beta}}.
\]
Taking logarithms, with the stipulated convention at a zero lower endpoint, proves \((\alpha_{xa},v_{xa})\in\Theta_{xa}\).  Hence for every \(\pi\in\Pi_m\), the true Pareto-core root obeys
\[
 \underline t_\pi\leq\bar t_P^\pi\leq\overline t_\pi.
\]
The already established \(\mathrm{lem{:}uniform\text{-}hall\text{-}core\text{-}inversion}\) and the definition of \(\varepsilon_\tau\) then imply
\[
 \log V_{P,\tau}(\pi)
 \in[\underline t_\pi-\varepsilon_\tau,
      \overline t_\pi+\varepsilon_\tau]
 =J^*_{\pi}.
\]

It remains to verify that intersection with \(G_\tau\) cannot remove the target.  For every \(y\geq1\), the support, index, scale, and Hall conditions imply
\[
 \frac{1-D}{2}y^{-4}
 \leq S_P^\pi(y)
 \leq (1+D)y^{-1}.
\]
Let
\[
 y_-:=\left\{\frac{1-D}{2\tau}\right\}^{1/4},
 \qquad y_+:=\frac{1+D}{\tau}.
\]
The restriction \(\tau\leq1/(8n)\) with \(n\geq2\) ensures \(y_->1\).  For every \(y<y_-\), the first displayed bound is strictly larger than \(\tau\), whereas the second gives \(S_P^\pi(y_+)\leq\tau\).  The generalized-inverse definition in \(\mathrm{def{:}policy\text{-}value}\), which is valid also at jumps and plateaus, therefore gives
\[
 y_-\leq V_{P,\tau}(\pi)\leq y_+,
\]
or equivalently \(\log V_{P,\tau}(\pi)\in G_\tau\).  On \(\mathcal E\), the ordinary intersection branch in the definition of \(I_\pi\) is consequently nonempty and contains \(\log V_{P,\tau}(\pi)\) for every \(\pi\).  We have proved
\[
 \inf_{P\in\mathcal H_{\beta,D}(m)}
 \mathbb P_{P,n}\!\left\{\log V_{P,\tau}(\pi)\in I_\pi
 \text{ for every }\pi\in\Pi_m\right\}
 \geq1-\gamma_0.
\]

\emph{Uniform stochastic width.}  Set
\[
 h:=\log(40m),\qquad q:=\frac hN=\frac{m\log(40m)}n,
 \qquad \eta:=q^{\varkappa}.
\]
We first derive the endpoint inequalities needed below directly from the binomial tail equations.  For \(r=k/n\), let
\[
 d(r\Vert u):=r\log\frac ru+(1-r)\log\frac{1-r}{1-u}
\]
denote Bernoulli relative entropy.  Exponential Markov inequality applied to
\[
 E_u e^{tB}=(1-u+ue^t)^n
\]
and optimized over \(t>0\), or over \(t<0\), gives respectively
\[
 P_u(B\geq nr)\leq e^{-nd(r\Vert u)}\quad(u<r),
 \qquad
 P_u(B\leq nr)\leq e^{-nd(r\Vert u)}\quad(u>r).
\]
Writing \(\lambda:=\log(2/\rho)=\log(8m/\gamma_0)\), the lower-endpoint equality implies \(d(r\Vert q_-(k))\leq\lambda/n\) whenever \(q_-(k)<r\), and the upper-endpoint equality implies \(d(r\Vert q_+(k))\leq\lambda/n\) whenever \(q_+(k)>r\).  The nontrivial endpoint in each boundary case is included.  Differentiating relative entropy in its second argument gives
\[
 d(r\Vert u)=
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\int_u^r\frac{r-s}{s(1-s)}\,ds,&u<r,\\[6pt]
 \displaystyle\int_r^u\frac{s-r}{s(1-s)}\,ds,&u>r.
 \end{cases}
\]
Since \(s(1-s)\leq s\leq\max(r,u)\) on the corresponding interval,
\[
 d(r\Vert u)\geq\frac{(r-u)^2}{2\max(r,u)}.
\]
Solving the resulting quadratic inequalities gives
\[
 r-q_-(k)\leq\sqrt{\frac{2r\lambda}{n}}
 \quad\text{when }q_-(k)<r,
 \qquad
 q_+(k)-r\leq C\left\{\sqrt{\frac{r\lambda}{n}}+\frac\lambda n\right\}
 \quad\text{when }q_+(k)>r.
 \tag{1}
\]

We next localize the observed proportions uniformly.  The same exponential-moment calculation, using
\[
 E_p e^{t(K-np)}
 \leq\exp\{np(e^t-1-t)\},
\]
and its negative-\(t\) counterpart, gives for every \(u>0\)
\[
 P\!\left(\left|\frac Kn-p\right|>
       \sqrt{\frac{2pu}{n}}+\frac{2u}{3n}\right)
 \leq2e^{-u}.
 \tag{2}
\]
This follows by exponential Markov inequality and the elementary bound
\(e^t-1-t\leq t^2/\{2(1-t/3)\}\) for \(0<t<3\); applying the same calculation to \(-t\) proves the lower-tail part.

Fix \(0<\epsilon<1\) and take \(u=\log(8m/\epsilon)\) in (2).  A union bound makes (2) simultaneous for the \(4m\) counts with probability at least \(1-\epsilon\).  Suppose first that \(N\geq b_{\beta,D}h\), so that \(T=q^{-1/(2\beta+4)}\).  At either \(z=T\) or \(z=eT\), the Hall, scale, and index restrictions give
\[
 S_{xa}(z)\geq\frac{1-D}{2e^4}T^{-4},
 \qquad np_{xa}(z)=\frac N2S_{xa}(z)
 \geq\frac{1-D}{4e^4}NT^{-4}.
 \tag{3}
\]
Because \(m\geq2\) and \(\gamma_0=0.05\), both \(u/h\) and \(\lambda/h\) are bounded by constants depending at most on \(\epsilon\), while
\[
 \frac{h}{NT^{-4}}=qT^4
 =q^{2\beta/(2\beta+4)}=\eta^2,
 \qquad T^{-\beta}=q^{\beta/(2\beta+4)}=\eta.
 \tag{4}
\]
Equations (2)--(4) show that, on the probability-\(1-\epsilon\) event and whenever \(\eta\) is smaller than a sufficiently small constant depending on \(\epsilon,\beta,D\),
\[
 \frac12p_{xa}(z)\leq\frac{K_{xa}(z)}n\leq\frac32p_{xa}(z)
 \quad\text{for every }x,a,z.
 \tag{5}
\]
For the lower endpoint, either \(q_-(k)\geq r\), in which case (5) already supplies the required lower bound, or \(q_-(k)<r\), in which case (1) supplies it.  For the upper endpoint, either \(q_+(k)\leq r\), in which case (5) supplies the required upper bound, or \(q_+(k)>r\), in which case (1) supplies it.  Thus (1) and (3)--(5) yield, for every nonempty preliminary scaled intersection,
\[
 S_{xa}(z)(1-C_\epsilon\eta)
 \leq\underline S_{xa}(z)
 \leq\overline S_{xa}(z)
 \leq S_{xa}(z)(1+C_\epsilon\eta).
 \tag{6}
\]
For the stipulated smallness of \(\eta\), the lower endpoint in (6) is positive.  If the preliminary intersection is empty, the corrected \(J_{xa}(z)=\{0\}\) forces the corresponding raw Hall region to be empty through its upper strip, and hence \(\Theta_{xa}\) is a singleton.  Such a cell has parameter diameter zero.

Consider a nonsingleton \(\Theta_{xa}\) and two of its points \((\alpha,v)\) and \((\alpha',v')\).  At threshold \(z\), the common Hall strip containing both linear forms has width
\[
 w_z:=\log\frac{\overline S_{xa}(z)}{\underline S_{xa}(z)}
      +\log\frac{1+Dz^{-\beta}}{1-Dz^{-\beta}}.
\]
By (4), (6), \(D\leq1/4\), and
\(\log\{(1+s)/(1-s)\}\leq2s/(1-s)\) for \(0\leq s<1\),
\[
 w_T+w_{eT}\leq C_\epsilon\eta.
 \tag{7}
\]
Since \(\log(eT)-\log T=1\), subtracting the two strip inequalities gives
\[
 |\alpha-\alpha'|\leq w_T+w_{eT},
 \qquad
 |v-v'|\leq w_T+\log T\,(w_T+w_{eT}).
 \tag{8}
\]
The same bounds hold trivially for a singleton region.

For any feasible choice of parameters from these regions, its core root \(t\) lies in
\[
 \frac{\ell-\log2}{4}\leq t\leq\ell.
 \tag{9}
\]
Indeed, at the root the scale and index restrictions give
\(\tfrac12e^{-4t}\leq\tau\leq e^{-t}\).  Moreover
\(\log T\leq\log n\leq\ell\), using \(N\leq n\) and \(\tau\leq1/(8n)\).  If two parameter choices for the same policy produce cores \(M_1,M_2\), the log-sum-exp inequality and (7)--(9) give, throughout the root range,
\[
 |\log M_1(t)-\log M_2(t)|
 \leq\max_x\left|(v_x-v'_x)-(\alpha_x-\alpha'_x)t\right|
 \leq C_\epsilon\ell\eta.
 \tag{10}
\]
For \(t_2>t_1\), every admissible core satisfies
\[
 M(t_2)\leq e^{-(t_2-t_1)}M(t_1),
\]
because every index is at least one.  Applying this inequality to the two roots and then using (10) proves that their distance is at most \(C_\epsilon\ell\eta\).  Taking extrema over the product regions therefore yields, simultaneously for all policies,
\[
 \overline t_\pi-\underline t_\pi\leq C_\epsilon\ell\eta.
 \tag{11}
\]
Intersection with \(G_\tau\) cannot increase length, and the empty-intersection branch has length zero.  Equations (11) and \(\varepsilon_\tau=C_\beta D\tau^{\beta/4}\) consequently give
\[
 W_{n,m,\tau}
 \leq C_\epsilon\ell\eta+2C_\beta D\tau^{\beta/4}
 \tag{12}
\]
on the event of probability at least \(1-\epsilon\), in the localized nonfallback regime.

The remaining regimes follow from a deterministic bound.  Every reported interval is a subinterval or a singleton of \(G_\tau\), so, since \(\ell\geq\log16\),
\[
 W_{n,m,\tau}\leq\operatorname{length}(G_\tau)
 =\frac34\ell+\log(1+D)-\frac14\log\frac{1-D}{2}
 \leq C_D\ell.
 \tag{13}
\]
If \(N<b_{\beta,D}h\), then
\[
 \min(1,\eta)\geq b_{\beta,D}^{-\varkappa},
\]
so (13) has the asserted order.  If \(N\geq b_{\beta,D}h\) but \(\eta\) is not below the localization constant used above, (13) is again at most a constant, depending on \(\epsilon,\beta,D\), times \(\ell\eta\).  Since \(\epsilon>0\) was arbitrary, (12)--(13) prove, uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal H_{\beta,D}(m)\) and along every admissible joint sequence,
\[
 W_{n,m,\tau}
 =O_{\mathbb P_{P,n}}\!\left(
 \ell\min\left\{1,\left[\frac{m\log(40m)}n\right]^{\varkappa}\right\}
 +D\tau^{\beta/4}\right).
\]
If \(n\to\infty\) and \(\ell[m\log(40m)/n]^{\varkappa}\to0\), then the minimum equals its second argument eventually, while \(D\tau^{\beta/4}\leq D(8n)^{-\beta/4}\to0\).  The displayed stochastic bound thus gives \(W_{n,m,\tau}\to0\) in probability.

Finally, on \(\mathcal E\) the inclusion holds for every element of \(\Pi_m\) simultaneously.  Conditional on any observed sample and any auxiliary randomization, every \(\widehat\pi\in\mathfrak A_{n,m}\) takes a value in \(\Pi_m\).  Substituting that realized value into the simultaneous inclusion gives
\[
 \log V_{P,\tau}(\widehat\pi)\in I_{\widehat\pi}.
\]
This applies in particular to \(\widehat\pi_{\mathrm{tail}}\) and proves selected-policy coverage without sample splitting.
