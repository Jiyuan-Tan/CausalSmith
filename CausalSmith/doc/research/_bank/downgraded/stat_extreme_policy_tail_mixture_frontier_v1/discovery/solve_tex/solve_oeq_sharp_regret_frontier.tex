%%% FIELD sat-subfamily-statement
Fix \(\beta>0\) and \(D\in(0,1/4]\). Put
\[
A_{n,m,\tau}:=\ell\min\{1,N^{-\varkappa}\},
\qquad L_m:=\log(2m).
\]
There are constants \(0<c_{\beta,D}\le C_{\beta,D}<\infty\), depending only on \(\beta,D\), such that for every \(n\ge2\), every even \(m\ge2\), and every \(0<\tau\le1/(8n)\) satisfying
\[
A_{n,m,\tau}<L_m^{1-\varkappa},
\qquad
A_{n,m,\tau}\left(1+A_{n,m,\tau}^{\varkappa/(1-\varkappa)}\right)\ge\ell,
\]
one has
\[
c_{\beta,D}\ell
\le \mathcal R(n,m,\tau)
\le C_{\beta,D}\ell.
\]
Moreover,
\[
\frac12\ell\le \mathcal B_{\mathrm{CK}}(n,m,\tau)\le\ell,
\]
so \(\mathcal R(n,m,\tau)\asymp_{\beta,D}\ell\asymp\mathcal B_{\mathrm{CK}}(n,m,\tau)\) throughout this saturated sub-familywise phase. Every case with \(N<1\) that satisfies the first, sub-familywise, inequality automatically satisfies the second inequality and hence lies in this phase.
%%% FIELD sat-subfamily-proof
Because \(\beta>0\), the definition \(\varkappa=\beta/(2\beta+4)\) gives
\[
0<\varkappa<\frac12,
\qquad
p:=\frac{\varkappa}{1-\varkappa}>0.
\]
Admissibility gives \(\tau<1\), hence \(\ell=\log(1/\tau)>0\), and \(m\ge2\) gives \(L_m=\log(2m)>1\). Since the map \(z\mapsto z^p\) is strictly increasing on \((0,\infty)\), the first displayed hypothesis implies
\[
A_{n,m,\tau}^{p}
<\left(L_m^{1-\varkappa}\right)^p
=L_m^{\varkappa}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\min\left\{A_{n,m,\tau}^{\varkappa/(1-\varkappa)},L_m^{\varkappa}\right\}
=A_{n,m,\tau}^{\varkappa/(1-\varkappa)}.
\]
Applying \(\mathrm{prop{:}multiscale\text{-}paired\text{-}splice\text{-}lower\text{-}envelope}\) and then the second displayed hypothesis therefore yields, equation by equation,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal R(n,m,\tau)
&\ge c_{\beta,D}\min\!\left\{\ell,
A_{n,m,\tau}\left[1+
\min\!\left\{A_{n,m,\tau}^{\varkappa/(1-\varkappa)},L_m^{\varkappa}\right\}\right]\right\}\\
&=c_{\beta,D}\min\!\left\{\ell,
A_{n,m,\tau}\left[1+A_{n,m,\tau}^{\varkappa/(1-\varkappa)}\right]\right\}\\
&=c_{\beta,D}\ell.
\end{aligned}
\]
On the other hand, \(\mathrm{thm{:}generic\text{-}regret\text{-}sandwich}\) gives
\[
\mathcal R(n,m,\tau)
\le C_{\beta,D}\ell
\min\left\{1,\left(\frac{mL_m}{n}\right)^{\varkappa}\right\}
=C_{\beta,D}\mathcal B_{\mathrm{CK}}(n,m,\tau)
\le C_{\beta,D}\ell,
\]
where the middle equality is the construction in \(\mathrm{def{:}coordinatewise\text{-}hall\text{-}baseline}\).

For completeness, that construction and \(N=n/m\) imply the useful identity
\[
\mathcal B_{\mathrm{CK}}(n,m,\tau)
=\min\{\ell,A_{n,m,\tau}L_m^{\varkappa}\}.
\]
Indeed, if \(N\ge1\), then \(A_{n,m,\tau}=\ell N^{-\varkappa}\), whereas if \(N<1\), both sides equal \(\ell\) because \(L_m>1\). The two regime hypotheses further give
\[
\ell
\le A_{n,m,\tau}\left(1+A_{n,m,\tau}^{p}\right)
<A_{n,m,\tau}(1+L_m^{\varkappa})
\le2A_{n,m,\tau}L_m^{\varkappa},
\]
where the last inequality uses \(L_m^{\varkappa}>1\). Hence
\[
\frac12\ell
\le\min\{\ell,A_{n,m,\tau}L_m^{\varkappa}\}
=\mathcal B_{\mathrm{CK}}(n,m,\tau)
\le\ell.
\]
This proves both comparisons.

Finally, if \(N<1\), then \(N^{-\varkappa}>1\), and therefore
\[
A_{n,m,\tau}=\ell,
\qquad
A_{n,m,\tau}(1+A_{n,m,\tau}^{p})
=\ell(1+\ell^p)\ge\ell.
\]
Thus the saturation inequality is automatic whenever \(N<1\), as claimed.

Put \(A_{n,m,\tau}:=\ell\min\{1,N^{-\varkappa}\}\) and \(L_m:=\log(2m)\). Restrict the unresolved regret problem to the unsaturated strict sub-familywise region
\[
A_{n,m,\tau}<L_m^{1-\varkappa},
\qquad
A_{n,m,\tau}\left[1+A_{n,m,\tau}^{\varkappa/(1-\varkappa)}\right]<\ell.
\]
No case with \(N<1\) lies in this region. The proved bounds there are
\[
c_{\beta,D}A_{n,m,\tau}
\left[1+A_{n,m,\tau}^{\varkappa/(1-\varkappa)}\right]
\le\mathcal R(n,m,\tau)
\le C_{\beta,D}\min\{\ell,A_{n,m,\tau}L_m^{\varkappa}\}
=C_{\beta,D}\mathcal B_{\mathrm{CK}}(n,m,\tau).
\]
Determine \(\mathcal R(n,m,\tau)\) up to constants depending only on \(\beta,D\) within this remaining region. In particular, construct one computable capacity-aware learner over the full heterogeneous class \(\mathcal H_{\beta,D}(m)\) that attains the lower expression, including sparse-dominance, collective-contribution, and local-to-tied mixtures, or prove that another same-class configuration raises the lower bound. Determine whether \(A_{n,m,\tau}\to0\), which is necessary for vanishing regret, is sufficient, or whether the stronger currently sufficient condition \(A_{n,m,\tau}L_m^{\varkappa}\to0\) is necessary. Equivalently, identify the maximal subregimes of this unsaturated phase on which capacity averaging yields \(\mathcal R(n,m,\tau)=o\{\mathcal B_{\mathrm{CK}}(n,m,\tau)\}\). The existing coordinatewise learner \(\widehat\pi_{\mathrm{tail}}\) remains only the upper witness; no nonzero policy-selection price is assumed.

Tensorize two-arm Hall splices across paired cells at an arbitrary admissible amplitude, retain the exact logarithmic mixture loss, condition on the pair sample counts, factor the product posterior, and compare every feasible zero-, one-, or two-cell capacity allocation with one posterior-MAP action per pair by concavity. A binomial lower-tail calculation followed by amplitude optimization gives the multiscale same-class lower envelope. Put \(A_{n,m,\tau}:=\ell\min\{1,N^{-\varkappa}\}\) and \(L_m:=\log(2m)\). Comparing that envelope with the separate coordinatewise sorting-and-inversion upper witness closes the familywise phase \(A_{n,m,\tau}\ge L_m^{1-\varkappa}\), including saturation. In the strict sub-familywise phase, the envelope and the generic upper bound also give \(\mathcal R(n,m,\tau)\asymp_{\beta,D}\ell\asymp\mathcal B_{\mathrm{CK}}(n,m,\tau)\) whenever \(A_{n,m,\tau}[1+A_{n,m,\tau}^{\varkappa/(1-\varkappa)}]\ge\ell\); this includes every \(N<1\) case satisfying the sub-familywise inequality. The only unresolved region is therefore
\[
A_{n,m,\tau}<L_m^{1-\varkappa},
\qquad
A_{n,m,\tau}\left[1+A_{n,m,\tau}^{\varkappa/(1-\varkappa)}\right]<\ell.
\]
There the construction supplies the lower rate \(A_{n,m,\tau}[1+A_{n,m,\tau}^{\varkappa/(1-\varkappa)}]\), while the known coordinatewise upper witness supplies \(\min\{\ell,A_{n,m,\tau}L_m^{\varkappa}\}\); no localized selected-mixture upper witness or harder same-class lower configuration is built into this handle.

The paired product-prior converse, fixed-sample posterior factorization, capacity exchange, binomial missed-cell lower bound, and amplitude optimization close the lower side. Direct algebra from that lower envelope also closes the newly separated saturated sub-familywise region. A natural-threshold empirical minimizer and an average multiplicative core comparison were tried in the remaining region, but rare severe underestimation can be selected adaptively; restoring simultaneous control reproduces the coordinatewise logarithmic factor.
