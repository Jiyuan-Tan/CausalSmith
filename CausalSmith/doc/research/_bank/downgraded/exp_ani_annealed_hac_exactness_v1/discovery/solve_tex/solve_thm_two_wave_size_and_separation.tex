%%% FIELD proof-buffered-coupling
Fix \(P\in\mathcal M_{2,\mathrm{loc}}\), \(n>4r\), \(i\in I_n\), and \(w\in\{1,2\}\).  Couple the finite-volume assignment to the restriction \(D^{(w)}|_{\Lambda_n}\) of the infinite assignment.  Since \(i\in I_n\),
\[
 [i-r,i+r]\cap\mathbb Z\subseteq\Lambda_n.
\]
By the radius-\(r\) score-locality condition, the exposure and outcome entering the score at \(i\) depend only on \((U_j,D_j^{(w)})\) in this interval.  By the graph edge-range condition, restriction deletes no graph neighbor used there.  Moreover, conditional on \(\mathcal F_U\), the assignments in this interval have the same product \(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p)\) law in the finite and infinite constructions.  Hence the conditional exposure probabilities agree, so every factor in the IPW formula agrees.  Thus \(Z_{n,i}^{(w)}=Z_i^{(w)}\) almost surely.

The complement of \(I_n=\{r+1,\ldots,n-r\}\) in \(\Lambda_n\) consists of the \(r\) sites at each endpoint.  Consequently
\[
 \frac{|\Lambda_n\setminus I_n|}{|\Lambda_n|}=\frac{2r}{n}\longrightarrow0,
\]
because \(r\) is fixed.

%%% FIELD proof-exact-score-split
Fix \(P\in\mathcal M_{2,\mathrm{loc}}\).  The definitions give, for either wave,
\[
 \xi_i^{(w)}+h_i=(Z_i^{(w)}-m_i)+(m_i-\tau_{\mathrm P})
 =Z_i^{(w)}-\tau_{\mathrm P}.
\]
Also \(\mathbb E_P[\xi_i^{(w)}\mid\mathcal F_U]=0\) by the definition of \(m_i\).  Since \(h_j\) is \(\mathcal F_U\)-measurable and the variables are integrable, the conditional-expectation identity yields
\[
 \mathbb E_P[\xi_i^{(w)}h_j]
 =\mathbb E_P\!\left[h_j\mathbb E_P(\xi_i^{(w)}\mid\mathcal F_U)\right]=0.
\]

For the envelope, if \(e_1\ne e_0\), at most one of the two exposure indicators is one.  Exposure positivity and the outcome envelope therefore imply
\[
 |Z_i^{(w)}|
 \le \frac{|Y_i(D^{(w)};U,G)|}{\min\{\pi_i(e_1),\pi_i(e_0)\}}
 \le \frac B\varepsilon=K
\]
almost surely.  If \(e_1=e_0\), the bracket in the score is zero and the same bound is immediate.  In particular all conditional expectations used above are square-integrable.

%%% FIELD proof-cross-wave-identification
Fix \(P\in\mathcal M_{2,\mathrm{loc}}\).  Conditional on \(\mathcal F_U\), the graph, potential-outcome schedule, and exposure maps are fixed, while the two entire assignment fields are independent by cross-wave independence.  Hence \(Z_i^{(1)}\) and \(Z_j^{(2)}\) are conditionally independent for every \(i,j\), even when their within-wave assignment neighborhoods overlap.  The definition of \(m_i\) and conditional independence give
\[
 \mathbb E_P[Z_i^{(1)}Z_j^{(2)}\mid\mathcal F_U]
 =\mathbb E_P[Z_i^{(1)}\mid\mathcal F_U]\,
  \mathbb E_P[Z_j^{(2)}\mid\mathcal F_U]
 =m_im_j.
\]
The integrability needed here follows from \(|Z_i^{(w)}|\le K\).  Taking expectations, using \(m_i=\tau_{\mathrm P}+h_i\), stationarity, and \(\mathbb E_P h_i=0\), gives for every \(k\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P[Z_0^{(1)}Z_k^{(2)}]-\tau_{\mathrm P}^2
 =\mathbb E_P[m_0m_k]-\tau_{\mathrm P}^2
 =\mathbb E_P[h_0h_k]=\gamma_h(k).
\]
Summing this equality over \(-2r\le k\le2r\) proves the displayed identification formula for \(\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}\).  This establishes a relationship between the causal Palm-score functional and the observable cross-wave moment; it does not define the former by the latter.

%%% FIELD proof-janson-boundary
By radius-\(r\) score locality, conditional expectation integrates a function of \(D_{i-r}^{(w)},\ldots,D_{i+r}^{(w)}\) against its finite product Bernoulli law.  It follows that \(m_i\), and hence \(h_i\), is measurable with respect to \(\sigma(U_{i-r},\ldots,U_{i+r})\).  The equivariance assumptions and the iid innovation law make \((h_i)_{i\in\mathbb Z}\) stationary, while its definition gives \(\mathbb E_P h_i=0\).  The score envelope gives square integrability.  Thus \(h_i\) is a centered \((2r+1)\)-block factor and \(h_i\) and \(h_j\) are independent whenever \(|i-j|>2r\).

For \(M>2r\), stationarity and this finite dependence give the exact variance identity
\[
 \frac1M\operatorname{Var}_P\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^M h_i\right)
 =\gamma_h(0)+2\sum_{k=1}^{2r}\left(1-\frac{k}{M}\right)\gamma_h(k).
\]
Letting \(M\to\infty\) shows that the left side converges to
\[
 \gamma_h(0)+2\sum_{k=1}^{2r}\gamma_h(k)
 =\sum_{k=-2r}^{2r}\gamma_h(k)=\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}.
\]
Every finite-\(M\) variance is nonnegative, so \(\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}\ge0\).

Apply the cited finite-block coboundary theorem with block length \(2r+1\) after reindexing the input block \((U_{i-r},\ldots,U_{i+r})\).  Because \(h_i\) is centered, that theorem says precisely that \(\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}=0\) if and only if there is a square-integrable \(2r\)-block factor
\[
 g_i=g(U_{i-r+1},\ldots,U_{i+r})
 \quad\text{such that}\quad h_i=g_i-g_{i-1}\quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
In that case telescoping, with no limiting argument, gives
\[
 \sum_{i\in I_n}h_i
 =\sum_{i=r+1}^{n-r}(g_i-g_{i-1})=g_{n-r}-g_r,
 \quad
 \frac1{N_n}\sum_{i\in I_n}h_i
 =\frac{g_{n-r}-g_r}{N_n}.
\]
For \(n>4r\), \(g_r\) is a function of \((U_1,\ldots,U_{2r})\), whereas \(g_{n-r}\) is a function of \((U_{n-2r+1},\ldots,U_n)\).  These blocks are disjoint, so iid innovations make the endpoints independent; stationarity makes each distributed as \(g_0\).  Therefore \(g_{n-r}-g_r\) already has, for every \(n>4r\), the law of \(g^\star-g_0\), and in particular
\[
 \sum_{i\in I_n}h_i\ \Rightarrow\ g^\star-g_0.
\]

%%% FIELD proof-uniform-concentration
Write \(L=4r+1\), \(N=N_n=n-2r\), and
\[
 c_n=\frac{(2r+1)LK^2}{n-4r}.
\]
By the buffered coupling, the statistic can be evaluated from the \(3n\) coordinates
\[
 U_1,\ldots,U_n,\quad D_1^{(1)},\ldots,D_n^{(1)},\quad
 D_1^{(2)},\ldots,D_n^{(2)}.
\]
These coordinates are mutually independent.  Indeed, the conditional law of each assignment wave given \(\mathcal F_U\) is the fixed product Bernoulli law, so each wave is independent of \(\mathcal F_U\); cross-wave conditional independence then factors the joint conditional law of the two waves.

First compute the bias.  Put \(\overline m=N^{-1}\sum_{i\in I_n}m_i\).  Cross-wave identification and stationarity give
\[
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[N_{n,k}^{-1}\sum_{i\in J_{n,k}}
 Z_i^{(1)}Z_{i+k}^{(2)}\right]
 =\tau_{\mathrm P}^2+\gamma_h(k).
\]
Conditional independence of the two waves also gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P[\widehat\tau_n^{(1)}\widehat\tau_n^{(2)}]
 =\mathbb E_P[\overline m^{\,2}]
 =\tau_{\mathrm P}^2+\operatorname{Var}_P(\overline m).
\]
Consequently
\[
 \mathbb E_P\widehat\Omega_n^{12}
 =\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}-L\operatorname{Var}_P(\overline m).
\]
The preceding locality argument makes \((h_i)\) \(2r\)-dependent.  Moreover \(|m_i|\le K\), \(|\tau_{\mathrm P}|\le K\), and hence \(|h_i|\le2K\).  There are at most \(NL\) ordered pairs \((i,j)\in I_n^2\) with \(|i-j|\le2r\); all other covariances vanish.  Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_P(\overline m)
 =\frac1{N^2}\sum_{i,j\in I_n}\mathbb E_P[h_ih_j]
 \le\frac{4LK^2}{N},
\]
and thus
\[
 \left|\mathbb E_P\widehat\Omega_n^{12}-\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}\right|
 \le\frac{4L^2K^2}{N}=b_n. \tag{1}
\]

We next calculate coordinate oscillations.  Decompose \(\widehat\Omega_n^{12}=A_n-C_n\), where \(A_n\) is the sum of the \(L\) lagged product averages and
\(C_n=L\widehat\tau_n^{(1)}\widehat\tau_n^{(2)}\).  Changing one coordinate \(U_\ell\) can change at most \(2r+1\) scores in each wave.  At a fixed lag, at most \(2(2r+1)\) products in \(A_n\) can change, each by at most \(2K^2\).  Since \(N_{n,k}\ge n-4r\), the oscillation of \(A_n\) is at most \(4c_n\).  Each sample mean changes by at most \(2(2r+1)K/N\), both means are bounded by \(K\), and hence the oscillation of \(C_n\) is at most \(4c_n\).  Thus an environment coordinate has total oscillation at most \(8c_n\).

Changing one coordinate \(D_\ell^{(1)}\) affects at most \(2r+1\) first-wave scores.  The same counting bounds the oscillations of \(A_n\) and \(C_n\) by \(2c_n\) each, so its total oscillation is at most \(4c_n\).  The identical bound holds for a second-wave assignment coordinate.  Hence the sum of the squared coordinate oscillations is bounded by
\[
 n(8c_n)^2+2n(4c_n)^2=96n c_n^2. \tag{2}
\]

For completeness, the required exponential inequality follows directly from the independent-coordinate filtration.  If \(F\) is a function of independent coordinates with oscillations \(a_1,\ldots,a_M\), let \(M_j=\mathbb E_P[F\mid X_1,\ldots,X_j]\).  Conditional on the first \(j-1\) coordinates, \(M_j-M_{j-1}\) has mean zero and lies in an interval of length at most \(a_j\).  Convexity of the exponential gives, for every such centered variable \(W\) in an interval of length \(a\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P[e^{\lambda W}]
 \le \exp(\lambda^2a^2/8).
\]
Indeed, the chord bound for \(e^{\lambda x}\) on the containing interval reduces the left side to the moment generating function of a two-point variable with the same mean, and differentiating its logarithm twice bounds the second derivative by \(a^2/4\); its value and first derivative at zero are zero.  Iterating conditional expectations therefore yields
\[
 \mathbb E_P\exp\{\lambda(F-\mathbb E_PF)\}
 \le\exp\!\left(\frac{\lambda^2}{8}\sum_{j=1}^M a_j^2\right).
\]
Exponential Markov inequality, optimized at \(\lambda=4t/\sum_j a_j^2\), and the same calculation for \(-F\), imply
\[
 P\{|F-\mathbb E_PF|\ge t\}
 \le2\exp\!\left\{-\frac{2t^2}{\sum_j a_j^2}\right\}. \tag{3}
\]
Apply (3) to \(F=\widehat\Omega_n^{12}\), use (2), and choose
\[
 t=\sqrt{48n}\,c_n\sqrt{\log(2/\delta)}=q_n(\delta).
\]
This gives \(P\{|\widehat\Omega_n^{12}-\mathbb E_P\widehat\Omega_n^{12}|\ge q_n(\delta)\}\le\delta\).  Combining this inequality with (1) and the triangle inequality proves
\[
 P\{|\widehat\Omega_n^{12}-\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}|>b_n+q_n(\delta)\}\le\delta.
\]
Every bound used only the common constants \(r\), \(K=B/\varepsilon\), and \(n\), so taking the supremum over \(P\in\mathcal M_{2,\mathrm{loc}}\) preserves the inequality.

%%% FIELD proof-size-separation
Let \(F_n=\widehat\Omega_n^{12}\).  Under any \(P\in\mathcal M_{2,\mathrm{loc}}\) satisfying \(\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}=0\), rejection entails
\[
 |F_n-\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}|=|F_n|>b_n+q_n(\alpha)=t_n(\alpha).
\]
The uniform concentration theorem with \(\delta=\alpha\) therefore gives \(P(\phi_{n,\alpha}=1)\le\alpha\), uniformly over the null.

Now suppose \(\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}\ge\rho_n(\alpha,\beta)=2b_n+q_n(\alpha)+q_n(\beta)\).  On nonrejection,
\[
 \Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}-F_n
 \ge \Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}-b_n-q_n(\alpha)
 \ge b_n+q_n(\beta). \tag{1}
\]
For any \(\beta'\in(\beta,1)\), the function \(q_n(\cdot)\) is strictly decreasing, so (1) implies
\[
 |F_n-\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}|>b_n+q_n(\beta').
\]
The concentration theorem with \(\delta=\beta'\) gives \(P(\phi_{n,\alpha}=0)\le\beta'\).  Letting \(\beta'\downarrow\beta\) yields \(P(\phi_{n,\alpha}=0)\le\beta\), proving the asserted uniform power bound, including the non-strict boundary \(\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}=\rho_n(\alpha,\beta)\).

Finally, with \(r,B,\varepsilon,\alpha,\beta\) fixed, \(K=B/\varepsilon\) is fixed and \(N_n=n-2r\).  Thus
\[
 b_n=O(n^{-1}),\qquad
 q_n(\delta)=
 \frac{\sqrt{48n}(2r+1)(4r+1)K^2}{n-4r}
 \sqrt{\log(2/\delta)}=O(n^{-1/2})
\]
for each fixed \(\delta\in(0,1)\).  Hence \(\rho_n(\alpha,\beta)=O(n^{-1/2})\).

%%% FIELD proof-nonhomogeneous-witnesses
Take \(r=1\).  In both laws the nearest-neighbor line is deterministic, connected, translation equivariant, has edge range one, and is measurable with respect to every local innovation sigma-field.  The exposure \(E_i(d,G)=d_i\) is shift equivariant and radius-one local.  Each outcome schedule is shift equivariant and depends only on \((U_{i-1},U_i,d_i)\).  Since \(|U_i-U_{i-1}|\le2\) and \(|U_i|=1\), the common outcome envelope holds when \(B\ge2\).  The stipulated assignment construction gives two conditionally independent iid \(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p)\) waves, and
\[
 \pi_i(e_1)=p,\quad \pi_i(e_0)=1-p.
\]
Thus exposure positivity follows from \(\varepsilon\le\min\{p,1-p\}\).  This verifies every membership condition in \(\mathcal M_{2,\mathrm{loc}}\) for both \(P^{\Delta}\) and \(P^+\).

For \(P^{\Delta}\), direct substitution into the IPW score gives
\[
 Z_i^{(w)}=(U_i-U_{i-1})\frac{D_i^{(w)}}p,
 \quad
 m_i=U_i-U_{i-1},
 \quad
 \tau_{\mathrm P}=0,
\]
so \(h_i=U_i-U_{i-1}\).  Independence and the Rademacher moments give
\[
 \gamma_h(0)=2,\quad \gamma_h(1)=\gamma_h(-1)=-1,\quad
 \gamma_h(2)=\gamma_h(-2)=0.
\]
Therefore \(\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}=2-1-1=0\), even though \(h_i\) is nonconstant and has variance two.

For \(P^+\), the corresponding calculation is
\[
 Z_i^{(w)}=U_i\frac{D_i^{(w)}}p,
 \quad m_i=U_i,\quad \tau_{\mathrm P}=0,\quad h_i=U_i.
\]
Here \(\gamma_h(0)=1\) and \(\gamma_h(k)=0\) for every nonzero \(k\), whence \(\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}=1\).  The paired laws thus exhibit both a heterogeneous coboundary null and a positive-variance alternative inside the same bounded radius-one class.

%%% FIELD minimax-partial-result
The proved test supplies the uniform achievability statement
\[
 \sup_{P:\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}=0}P(\phi_{n,\alpha}=1)\le\alpha,
 \qquad
 \inf_{P:\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}\ge\rho_n(\alpha,\beta)}
 P(\phi_{n,\alpha}=1)\ge1-\beta,
 \qquad
 \rho_n(\alpha,\beta)=O(n^{-1/2}).
\]
Thus an order \(n^{-1/2}\) separation is uniformly attainable from the declared two-wave statistic.  No matching minimax lower bound, and no uniformly valid faster test, follows from the frozen primitives.
