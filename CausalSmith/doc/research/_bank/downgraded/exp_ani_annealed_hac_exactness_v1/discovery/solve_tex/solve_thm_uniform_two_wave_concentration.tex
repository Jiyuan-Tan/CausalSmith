%%% FIELD uniform-two-wave-concentration-proof
Fix \(n>4r\), \(\delta\in(0,1)\), and \(P\in\mathcal M_{2,\mathrm{loc}}\).  We first derive the common local score rule required by \texttt{thm:folner-fixed-footprint-two-wave-concentration}.  Put
\[
 A=\{-r,\ldots,r\},
 \qquad |A|=2r+1,
\]
and write the site-zero cylinder map supplied by \texttt{ass:score-locality} as \(\Psi_0=(\Psi_0^E,\Psi_0^Y)\).  For \(a\in\{e_1,e_0\}\), define
\[
 \bar\pi_a(u_A)
 =\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^{A}}
   \mathbf 1\{\Psi_0^E(u_A,x)=a\}
   p^{\sum_{s\in A}x_s}(1-p)^{|A|-\sum_{s\in A}x_s}. \tag{1}
\]
This is a Borel function because the sum is finite.  By \texttt{ass:wavewise-bernoulli}, (1) is the conditional probability of exposure \(a\) at the origin when the local environment is \(u_A\).

Define the raw Borel map
\[
 \widetilde z(u_A,x)=
 \Psi_0^Y(u_A,x)
 \left\{
  \frac{\mathbf 1\{\Psi_0^E(u_A,x)=e_1\}}{\bar\pi_{e_1}(u_A)}
  -\frac{\mathbf 1\{\Psi_0^E(u_A,x)=e_0\}}{\bar\pi_{e_0}(u_A)}
 \right\} \tag{2}
\]
when both denominators in (2) are positive, and set \(\widetilde z(u_A,x)=0\) otherwise.  Thus no division by zero occurs.  With \(K=B/\varepsilon\), let
\[
 T_K(y)=\max\{-K,\min\{y,K\}\},
 \qquad z=T_K\circ\widetilde z. \tag{3}
\]
The map \(z:\mathcal U^A\times\{0,1\}^A\to[-K,K]\) is Borel and is defined on the entire stated domain, including environment blocks lying in null sets.

We next prove that this one map represents every site and both waves.  Let \(\mathcal N\) be the assignment-independent null set in \texttt{ass:score-locality}.  The iid law in \texttt{ass:iid-innovations} is invariant under each left shift \(\theta^i\), so
\[
 \mathcal N_\infty=\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb Z}\theta^{-i}\mathcal N
 \quad\text{satisfies}\quad
 P\{U\in\mathcal N_\infty\}=0. \tag{4}
\]
Indeed, every set in the countable union has probability zero.  If \(U\notin\mathcal N_\infty\), then \(\theta^iU\notin\mathcal N\) for every \(i\).  Apply the site-zero cylinder equality to \((\theta^iU,\theta^id)\).  By \texttt{ass:graph-equivariance},
\[
 G(\theta^iU)=\theta^iG(U), \tag{5}
\]
and the orientations in \texttt{ass:exposure-equivariance} and \texttt{ass:outcome-equivariance} give
\[
 \bigl(E_i(d,G(U)),Y_i(d;U,G(U))\bigr)
 =\Psi_0\bigl((U_{i+s})_{s\in A},(d_{i+s})_{s\in A}\bigr) \tag{6}
\]
for every \(i\in\mathbb Z\) and every assignment \(d\).  Applying the conditional product-Bernoulli law of \texttt{ass:wavewise-bernoulli} to (6) yields
\[
 \pi_i(a)=\bar\pi_a\bigl((U_{i+s})_{s\in A}\bigr)
 \quad\text{almost surely},
 \qquad a\in\{e_1,e_0\}. \tag{7}
\]
The exposure-positivity assumption makes both quantities in (7) at least \(\varepsilon>0\) almost surely, so (2), evaluated at the realized local coordinates, is exactly \(Z_i^{(w)}\).  By \texttt{lem:exact-score-split}, \(|Z_i^{(w)}|\le K\) almost surely.  Hence the clipping in (3) leaves the realized value unchanged, and, simultaneously for \(w\in\{1,2\}\),
\[
 Z_i^{(w)}
 =z\bigl((U_{i+s})_{s\in A},(D_{i+s}^{(w)})_{s\in A}\bigr)
 \quad\text{almost surely for every }i\in\mathbb Z. \tag{8}
\]
Thus (1)--(8) derive the site- and wave-independent bounded score map required by the fixed-footprint theorem; it is not an additional hypothesis.

Apply \texttt{thm:folner-fixed-footprint-two-wave-concentration} to the additive group \(\Gamma=\mathbb Z\), footprint \(A\), and covariance-support set
\[
 C=AA^{-1}=A-A=\{-2r,\ldots,2r\}. \tag{9}
\]
The iid innovation premise follows from \texttt{ass:iid-innovations}; the equivariance premises follow from \texttt{ass:graph-equivariance}, \texttt{ass:exposure-equivariance}, and \texttt{ass:outcome-equivariance}; the two conditional product designs and their conditional independence follow from \texttt{ass:wavewise-bernoulli} and \texttt{ass:cross-wave-independence}; positivity is \texttt{ass:exposure-positivity}; and the required bounded common cylinder representation is (8).  For \(\Lambda=\Lambda_n\), the theorem's interior and lag-pair sets equal \(I_n\) and \(J_{n,k}\), respectively.  Therefore
\[
 |C|=4r+1,
 \qquad N=N_n=n-2r,
 \qquad
 \nu=\min_{|k|\le 2r}|J_{n,k}|=n-4r>0. \tag{10}
\]
The strict positivity in (10) is exactly where \(n>4r\) is used.  By \texttt{prop:buffered-coupling}, the restricted scores in that theorem equal \(Z_{n,i}^{(w)}\) almost surely.  By \texttt{lem:cross-wave-identification}, its covariance functional is
\[
 \sum_{k=-2r}^{2r}
 \left\{\mathbb E_P[Z_0^{(1)}Z_k^{(2)}]-\tau_{\mathrm P}^2\right\}
 =\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}. \tag{11}
\]
Its statistic is consequently the statistic \(\widehat\Omega_n^{12}\) from \texttt{def:two-wave-statistic}.

Substitution of (10) into the finite-sample bias bound of the fixed-footprint theorem gives
\[
 \frac{4|C|^2K^2}{N}
 =\frac{4(4r+1)^2K^2}{N_n}
 =b_n. \tag{12}
\]
Substitution into its bounded-differences radius gives
\[
 \frac{\sqrt{48|\Lambda_n|}\,|A|\,|C|K^2}{\nu}
 \sqrt{\log(2/\delta)}
 =\frac{\sqrt{48n}(2r+1)(4r+1)K^2}{n-4r}
 \sqrt{\log(2/\delta)}
 =q_n(\delta). \tag{13}
\]
Equations (11)--(13) and the conclusion of \texttt{thm:folner-fixed-footprint-two-wave-concentration} therefore imply
\[
 P\!\left\{
  \left|\widehat\Omega_n^{12}-\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}\right|
  >b_n+q_n(\delta)
 \right\}\le\delta. \tag{14}
\]
The right side of (14), and the two allowances in (12)--(13), are independent of the particular \(P\in\mathcal M_{2,\mathrm{loc}}\): they use only the common class constants \(r\), \(B\), and \(\varepsilon\), through \(K=B/\varepsilon\).  Taking the supremum over \(P\in\mathcal M_{2,\mathrm{loc}}\) in (14), and using \(t_n(\delta)=b_n+q_n(\delta)\) from \texttt{def:diagnostic-test}, proves the claimed uniform inequality.

For the required smallest-volume stress test, take \(r=1\), \(n=5\), the deterministic line graph, \(p=1/2\), \(E_i(d,G)=e_1\) when \(d_i=1\) and \(E_i(d,G)=e_0\) when \(d_i=0\), and \(Y_i(d;U,G)=Bd_i\).  Then \(I_n=\{2,3,4\}\), \(N_n=3\),
\[
 Z_i^{(w)}=2BD_i^{(w)},
 \qquad m_i=B,
 \qquad \Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}=0. \tag{15}
\]
For each of the \(2^{10}\) two-wave assignments on \(\Lambda_n\), every lag-product average and every product of wave means belongs to \([0,4B^2]\).  There are five lags, so the triangle inequality gives
\[
 |\widehat\Omega_n^{12}|\le 20B^2. \tag{16}
\]
Because the class fixes \(\varepsilon<1/2\), one has \(K=B/\varepsilon>2B\), and hence
\[
 b_n=\frac{100K^2}{3}>\frac{400B^2}{3}>20B^2. \tag{17}
\]
Thus no assignment rejects in this smallest admissible instance.  Moreover, conditional independence of the two waves gives, for every lag, \(\mathbb E_P[Z_i^{(1)}Z_j^{(2)}]=B^2\) and \(\mathbb E_P[\widehat\tau_n^{(1)}\widehat\tau_n^{(2)}]=B^2\), so \(\mathbb E_P[\widehat\Omega_n^{12}]=0\), confirming the absence of bias in this instance.  At the opposite boundary, the denominator \(n-4r\) in (13) tends to zero as \(n\downarrow4r\); the theorem accordingly requires \(n>4r\) and makes no Gaussian or growing-range claim.
