For \(n>4r\), let
\[
 W_n=\bigl((Z_{n,i}^{(1)},Z_{n,i}^{(2)})\bigr)_{i\in I_n}
 \in\mathbb R^{2N_n},
 \qquad
 \Psi_n=\{\psi_n:\mathbb R^{2N_n}\to[0,1]:\psi_n\text{ is Borel measurable}\}.
\]
The observation sigma-field is the Borel sigma-field on \(\mathbb R^{2N_n}\), and every risk is computed under the induced score law as \(\mathbb E_P[\psi_n(W_n)]\) or \(\mathbb E_P[1-\psi_n(W_n)]\).  Define the score-vector \((\alpha,\beta)\)-minimax separation radius by
\[
 s_{n,\alpha,\beta}^{\mathrm{score}}
 :=\inf\Bigl\{s\ge0:\ \exists\psi_n\in\Psi_n\text{ such that }
 \mathbb E_P[\psi_n(W_n)]\le\alpha
 \text{ for every }P\in\mathcal M_{2,\mathrm{loc}}
 \text{ with }\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}=0,
\]
\[
 \hspace{54mm}
 \mathbb E_P[1-\psi_n(W_n)]\le\beta
 \text{ for every }P\in\mathcal M_{2,\mathrm{loc}}
 \text{ with }\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}\ge s\Bigr\}.
\]
This experiment deliberately excludes tests that use the innovations, graph, assignments, exposures, or outcomes beyond the displayed score vector.

%%% FIELD definition-unbiased-statistic
For \(n>4r+1\), put \(\ell=2r+1\) and define
\[
 \widehat\Omega_n^{\mathrm u}
 =\sum_{k=-2r}^{2r}
 \frac1{N_{n,k}}\sum_{i\in J_{n,k}}
 Z_{n,i}^{(1)}Z_{n,i+k}^{(2)}
 -(4r+1)\frac1{N_{n,\ell}}\sum_{i\in J_{n,\ell}}
 Z_{n,i}^{(1)}Z_{n,i+\ell}^{(2)}.
\]
Also define the deterministic deviation allowance
\[
 q_n^{\mathrm u}(\delta)
 =\frac{\sqrt{48n}(2r+1)(4r+1)K^2}{n-4r-1}
 \sqrt{\log(2/\delta)},
 \qquad 0<\delta<1.
\]
%%% FIELD statement-unbiased-proposition
For every \(P\in\mathcal M_{2,\mathrm{loc}}\), \(n>4r+1\), and \(\delta\in(0,1)\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P[\widehat\Omega_n^{\mathrm u}]=\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}},
 \qquad
 P\!\left\{\left|\widehat\Omega_n^{\mathrm u}-\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}\right|>
 q_n^{\mathrm u}(\delta)\right\}\le\delta.
\]
Consequently the common score-vector test
\(\mathbf 1\{\widehat\Omega_n^{\mathrm u}>q_n^{\mathrm u}(\alpha)\}\)
has level at most \(\alpha\) on \(\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}=0\), and has power at least
\(1-\beta\) whenever
\(\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}\ge q_n^{\mathrm u}(\alpha)+q_n^{\mathrm u}(\beta)\).
For fixed \(r,B,\varepsilon,\alpha,\beta\), this separation is \(O(n^{-1/2})\) and contains no sample-centering bias allowance.
%%% FIELD proof-unbiased-proposition
Fix \(P\in\mathcal M_{2,\mathrm{loc}}\), write \(L=4r+1\), \(a_r=2r+1\), \(\ell=2r+1\), and set \(M=N_{n,\ell}=n-4r-1>0\).  By the block-factor boundary proposition, \(h_i\) is measurable with respect to \(\sigma(U_{i-r},\ldots,U_{i+r})\).  Thus \(h_i\) and \(h_j\) are independent whenever \(|i-j|>2r\).  They are centered by the definitions of \(h_i\) and \(\tau_{\mathrm P}\), so
\[
 \mathbb E_P[h_i h_{i+\ell}]=0. \tag{1}
\]
The cross-wave identification lemma, stationarity, and the buffered coupling give, for every \(|k|\le2r\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[
 \frac1{N_{n,k}}\sum_{i\in J_{n,k}}Z_{n,i}^{(1)}Z_{n,i+k}^{(2)}
 \right]
 =\mathbb E_P[m_0m_k]
 =\tau_{\mathrm P}^2+\gamma_h(k). \tag{2}
\]
The same argument at lag \(\ell\), followed by (1), yields
\[
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[
 \frac1{N_{n,\ell}}\sum_{i\in J_{n,\ell}}Z_{n,i}^{(1)}Z_{n,i+\ell}^{(2)}
 \right]=\tau_{\mathrm P}^2. \tag{3}
\]
Subtracting \(L\) copies of (3) from the sum of (2) over \(-2r\le k\le2r\) proves
\[
 \mathbb E_P[\widehat\Omega_n^{\mathrm u}]
 =\sum_{k=-2r}^{2r}\gamma_h(k)=\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}. \tag{4}
\]

It remains to prove the deviation bound from the primitive independent-coordinate design.  Conditional on \(\mathcal F_U\), the two assignment waves have the fixed product law \(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p)^{\otimes\mathbb Z}\) and are conditionally independent.  Hence, for measurable cylinder events \(A,B,C\),
\[
 P\{U\in A,D^{(1)}\in B,D^{(2)}\in C\}
 =P\{U\in A\}\,P\{D^{(1)}\in B\}\,P\{D^{(2)}\in C\}.
\]
Together with the iid-innovation assumption, this shows that the \(3n\) coordinates
\(U_1,\ldots,U_n,D_1^{(1)},\ldots,D_n^{(1)},D_1^{(2)},\ldots,D_n^{(2)}\)
are mutually independent.  By score locality, changing one environmental coordinate can change at most \(a_r\) retained scores in each wave, while changing one assignment coordinate can change at most \(a_r\) retained scores in its own wave.  The score-envelope lemma gives \(|Z_{n,i}^{(w)}|\le K\), so changing either factor changes a product by at most \(2K^2\).

At any lag \(|k|\le2r\), changing one environmental coordinate changes at most \(2a_r\) products.  Since \(N_{n,k}\ge n-4r>M\), the sum of the \(L\) short-lag averages changes by at most \(4a_rLK^2/(n-4r)\).  The lag-\(\ell\) average, after multiplication by \(L\), changes by at most \(4a_rLK^2/M\).  Therefore the total environmental-coordinate oscillation is at most
\[
 4a_rLK^2\left\{\frac1{n-4r}+\frac1M\right\}
 \le \frac{8a_rLK^2}{M}. \tag{5}
\]
For an assignment coordinate, only one factor in each affected product can change.  The same count gives total oscillation at most
\[
 2a_rLK^2\left\{\frac1{n-4r}+\frac1M\right\}
 \le \frac{4a_rLK^2}{M}. \tag{6}
\]
Put \(c=a_rLK^2/M\).  Summing the squared oscillations in (5)--(6) over \(n\) environmental and \(2n\) assignment coordinates gives
\[
 n(8c)^2+2n(4c)^2=96nc^2. \tag{7}
\]
McDiarmid's bounded-differences lemma, applied first with arbitrarily enlarged versions of the weak oscillation bounds in (5)--(6) and then with the enlargement decreased to zero, implies
\[
 P\!\left\{\left|\widehat\Omega_n^{\mathrm u}
 -\mathbb E_P\widehat\Omega_n^{\mathrm u}\right|>t\right\}
 \le2\exp\!\left\{-\frac{t^2}{48nc^2}\right\}. \tag{8}
\]
Taking \(t=\sqrt{48n}\,c\sqrt{\log(2/\delta)}=q_n^{\mathrm u}(\delta)\) in (8) and using (4) proves the asserted concentration inequality uniformly in \(P\).

Under \(\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}=0\), rejection implies
\(|\widehat\Omega_n^{\mathrm u}-\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}|>q_n^{\mathrm u}(\alpha)\), so (8) gives size at most \(\alpha\).  If
\(\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}\ge q_n^{\mathrm u}(\alpha)+q_n^{\mathrm u}(\beta)\), nonrejection implies
\(\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}-\widehat\Omega_n^{\mathrm u}\ge q_n^{\mathrm u}(\beta)\).  For every \(\beta'>\beta\), strict monotonicity of \(q_n^{\mathrm u}(\cdot)\) turns this into a deviation strictly exceeding \(q_n^{\mathrm u}(\beta')\); (8) bounds its probability by \(\beta'\).  Letting \(\beta'\downarrow\beta\) proves power at least \(1-\beta\).  Finally, with \(r,B,\varepsilon,\alpha,\beta\) fixed, \(K=B/\varepsilon\) is fixed and the displayed formula for \(q_n^{\mathrm u}\) is \(O(n^{-1/2})\).
%%% FIELD statement-minimax-theorem
In the canonical score-vector experiment, suppose \(p=1/2\) and \(\alpha+\beta<1\).  For every fixed \(a\) satisfying \(0<a\le2B\) and every integer \(n>4r+1\),
\[
 \frac{a^2(1-\alpha-\beta)}{\sqrt{2N_n}}
 \le s_{n,\alpha,\beta}^{\mathrm{score}}
 \le \rho_n(\alpha,\beta).
\]
Consequently, for fixed \(r,B,\varepsilon,\alpha,\beta\) with \(\alpha+\beta<1\) and \(p=1/2\),
\[
 s_{n,\alpha,\beta}^{\mathrm{score}}=\Theta(n^{-1/2}).
\]
The upper bound is attained by the unchanged sample-mean-centered test \(\phi_{n,\alpha}\).  The exactly unbiased statistic \(\widehat\Omega_n^{\mathrm u}\) additionally gives a common score-vector test with level at most \(\alpha\), power at least \(1-\beta\) at separation \(q_n^{\mathrm u}(\alpha)+q_n^{\mathrm u}(\beta)=O(n^{-1/2})\), and no sample-centering bias term.  The lower bound concerns only common Borel tests of \(W_n\); it makes no claim for tests using richer raw data.
%%% FIELD proof-minimax-theorem
The upper bound is immediate only after checking that the existing test belongs to the declared experiment.  The statistic \(\widehat\Omega_n^{12}\) is a finite sum of products and averages of the coordinates of \(W_n\); hence
\(\phi_{n,\alpha}=\mathbf 1\{\widehat\Omega_n^{12}>t_n(\alpha)\}\)
is a common Borel map from \(\mathbb R^{2N_n}\) to \([0,1]\), independent of \(P\).  The size--separation theorem gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P[\phi_{n,\alpha}]
 \le\alpha\quad\text{if }\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}=0,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_P[1-\phi_{n,\alpha}]
 \le\beta\quad\text{if }\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}\ge\rho_n(\alpha,\beta),
\]
uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal M_{2,\mathrm{loc}}\).  The definition of the minimax radius therefore yields
\[
 s_{n,\alpha,\beta}^{\mathrm{score}}\le\rho_n(\alpha,\beta). \tag{1}
\]
The exact-unbiased proposition proves the additional test and separation statement in the theorem.

We now prove the converse by an explicit common-support submodel.  Fix \(a\in(0,2B]\) and \(s\in[0,1)\).  Let the graph be the deterministic nearest-neighbor line, let \(U_i\) be iid \(\operatorname{Uniform}[0,1]\), take \(E_i(d,G)=d_i\), and draw the two assignment waves independently with \(p=1/2\).  Partition the innovation at site \(i\) into the three types
\[
 T_i=+\quad\text{if }0\le U_i<s/2,\qquad
 T_i=-\quad\text{if }s/2\le U_i<s,\qquad
 T_i=0\quad\text{if }s\le U_i\le1.
\]
Thus the type probabilities are \(s/2,s/2,1-s\).  Define the common no-carryover potential-outcome schedule by
\[
 Y_i(d;U,G)=
 \begin{cases}
 a/2,&T_i=0,\\
 a(2d_i-1)/2,&T_i=+,\\
 -a(2d_i-1)/2,&T_i=-.
 \end{cases} \tag{2}
\]
This schedule and the exposure map are shift equivariant and depend only on \((U_i,d_i)\), the line graph is connected, shift equivariant, local, and has edge range one, and \(|Y_i|=a/2\le B\).  Since \(r\ge1\), all radius-one objects are also radius-\(r\) local.  Moreover \(\pi_i(1)=\pi_i(0)=1/2\ge\varepsilon\).  The iid innovations and the two independent Bernoulli waves verify the remaining defining conditions of \(\mathcal M_{2,\mathrm{loc}}\).  Denote this law by \(P_s\).

For \(S_i^{(w)}=2D_i^{(w)}-1\), direct substitution into the exposure-IPW formula gives
\[
 (Z_i^{(1)},Z_i^{(2)})=
 \begin{cases}
 (aS_i^{(1)},aS_i^{(2)}),&T_i=0,\\
 (a,a),&T_i=+,\\
 (-a,-a),&T_i=-.
 \end{cases} \tag{3}
\]
Consequently the one-site score-pair law \(Q_s\), listed exhaustively on its four-point support, is
\[
 \begin{array}{c|cccc}
 (z^{(1)},z^{(2)})&(a,a)&(a,-a)&(-a,a)&(-a,-a)\\ \hline
 Q_s&(1+s)/4&(1-s)/4&(1-s)/4&(1+s)/4.
 \end{array} \tag{4}
\]
For \(s<1\), this support is exactly the same as the support of \(Q_0\); thus ``common support'' here is a statement about the induced score laws.  Equation (4) is also the smallest-instance exhaustive check: all four assignment pairs have been enumerated and none produces an unaccounted score value.

Taking the conditional expectation of (3) given \(\mathcal F_U\) shows that
\[
 m_i=0\ \text{on }\{T_i=0\},\qquad
 m_i=a\ \text{on }\{T_i=+\},\qquad
 m_i=-a\ \text{on }\{T_i=-\}.
\]
Hence \(\tau_{\mathrm P}=\mathbb E_{P_s}m_i=0\), \(h_i=m_i\), and the \(h_i\) are iid with
\[
 \gamma_h(0)=\mathbb E_{P_s}[h_i^2]=a^2s,
 \qquad \gamma_h(k)=0\quad(k\ne0).
\]
It follows from the definition of the causal Palm population functional that
\[
 \Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}(P_s)=a^2s, \qquad
 \Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}(P_0)=0. \tag{5}
\]

Under the buffered restriction, (3) depends at site \(i\) only on the mutually independent triple \((U_i,D_i^{(1)},D_i^{(2)})\).  Therefore the induced law of \(W_n\) is \(Q_s^{\otimes N_n}\).  From (4),
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_s\Vert Q_0)
 =\frac12\{(1+s)\log(1+s)+(1-s)\log(1-s)\}. \tag{6}
\]
The elementary inequalities \(\log(1+s)\le s\) and \(\log(1-s)\le-s\), valid for \(0\le s<1\), imply from (6) that
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_s\Vert Q_0)
 \le\frac12\{(1+s)s-(1-s)s\}=s^2. \tag{7}
\]
Expanding the logarithm of the product likelihood ratio and taking expectation coordinate by coordinate gives, without an additional theorem,
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_s^{\otimes N_n}\Vert Q_0^{\otimes N_n})
 =N_n\operatorname{KL}(Q_s\Vert Q_0)\le N_ns^2. \tag{8}
\]
Pinsker's inequality and (8) yield
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(Q_s^{\otimes N_n},Q_0^{\otimes N_n})
 \le s\sqrt{N_n/2}. \tag{9}
\]

Let \(\psi_n\in\Psi_n\) be any randomized test with null risk
\(\mathbb E_{P_0}[\psi_n(W_n)]\le\alpha\).  To apply the binary two-point testing lemma exactly as stated, enlarge both score experiments by one common independent \(V\sim\operatorname{Uniform}[0,1]\) and use the binary test
\(\varphi_n(W_n,V)=\mathbf 1\{V\le\psi_n(W_n)\}\).  Its rejection probability equals \(\mathbb E_P[\psi_n(W_n)]\).  Taking products with the same uniform law leaves total variation unchanged: one inequality follows by integrating sections of a measurable event, and the reverse inequality follows by using cylinder events \(A\times[0,1]\).  The two-point testing lemma and (9) therefore give
\[
 \mathbb E_{P_0}[\psi_n(W_n)]
 +\mathbb E_{P_s}[1-\psi_n(W_n)]
 \ge1-s\sqrt{N_n/2}. \tag{10}
\]

Set
\[
 s_0=\frac{1-\alpha-\beta}{\sqrt{2N_n}}.
\]
Because \(\alpha+\beta<1\) and \(N_n\ge1\), one has \(0<s_0<1\), so the common-support calculation applies.  Equations (9)--(10) imply
\[
 \mathbb E_{P_{s_0}}[1-\psi_n(W_n)]
 \ge1-\alpha-\frac{1-\alpha-\beta}{2}
 =\beta+\frac{1-\alpha-\beta}{2}>\beta. \tag{11}
\]
By (5), this alternative has
\(\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}(P_{s_0})=a^2(1-\alpha-\beta)/\sqrt{2N_n}\).
For every candidate separation not exceeding this value, the same \(P_{s_0}\) belongs to the alternative set and violates the required type-II bound in (11).  Taking the infimum in the definition of the minimax radius proves
\[
 s_{n,\alpha,\beta}^{\mathrm{score}}
 \ge\frac{a^2(1-\alpha-\beta)}{\sqrt{2N_n}}. \tag{12}
\]
Combining (1) and (12) gives the displayed finite-sample sandwich.  Choosing, for example, \(a=B>0\), using \(N_n=n-2r\), and invoking the established bound \(\rho_n(\alpha,\beta)=O(n^{-1/2})\) shows that the lower and upper bounds are positive constant multiples of \(n^{-1/2}\) for fixed \(r,B,\varepsilon,\alpha,\beta\).  This proves the asserted \(\Theta(n^{-1/2})\) score-vector minimax rate.  Since every comparison above is between the induced laws of \(W_n\), no step supplies a lower bound for a test that observes richer raw data.
