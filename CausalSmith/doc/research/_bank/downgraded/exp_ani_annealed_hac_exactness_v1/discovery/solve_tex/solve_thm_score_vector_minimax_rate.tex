%%% FIELD proof-exact-unbiased-lagged-statistic
Fix \(P\in\mathcal M_{2,\mathrm{loc}}\), \(n>4r+1\), and put
\[
 L=4r+1,\qquad a_r=2r+1,\qquad \ell=2r+1,
 \qquad M=N_{n,\ell}=n-4r-1>0. \tag{1}
\]
The buffered-coupling proposition and cross-wave-identification lemma imply, for every \(|k|\le 2r\) and every \(i\in J_{n,k}\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P[Z_{n,i}^{(1)}Z_{n,i+k}^{(2)}]
 =\mathbb E_P[m_i m_{i+k}]. \tag{2}
\]
The class equivariance conditions and iid innovations make \((m_i)_{i\in\mathbb Z}\) stationary.  Moreover, the Janson-boundary proposition gives \(m_i=\tau_{\mathrm P}+h_i\), where \(h_i\) is centered and is measurable with respect to \(\sigma(U_{i-r},\ldots,U_{i+r})\).  Consequently, stationarity and the definition of \(\gamma_h(k)\) turn (2) into
\[
 \mathbb E_P[Z_{n,i}^{(1)}Z_{n,i+k}^{(2)}]
 =\tau_{\mathrm P}^{,2}+\gamma_h(k). \tag{3}
\]
At lag \(\ell=2r+1\), the innovation blocks \(\{i-r,\ldots,i+r\}\) and \(\{i+r+1,\ldots,i+3r+1\}\) are disjoint.  The iid-innovation condition therefore makes \(h_i\) and \(h_{i+\ell}\) independent.  Since both are centered,
\[
 \gamma_h(\ell)=\mathbb E_P[h_i h_{i+\ell}]=0. \tag{4}
\]
Thus (3), also valid at lag \(\ell\), yields
\[
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[
  \frac{1}{N_{n,k}}\sum_{i\in J_{n,k}}
  Z_{n,i}^{(1)}Z_{n,i+k}^{(2)}
 \right]
 =\tau_{\mathrm P}^{,2}+\gamma_h(k),
 \qquad |k|\le 2r, \tag{5}
\]
and
\[
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[
  \frac{1}{N_{n,\ell}}\sum_{i\in J_{n,\ell}}
  Z_{n,i}^{(1)}Z_{n,i+\ell}^{(2)}
 \right]
 =\tau_{\mathrm P}^{,2}. \tag{6}
\]
There are exactly \(L\) integers in \([-2r,2r]\).  Subtracting \(L\) times (6) from the sum of (5) over these lags proves
\[
 \mathbb E_P[\widehat\Omega_n^{\mathrm u}]
 =\sum_{k=-2r}^{2r}\gamma_h(k)
 =\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}. \tag{7}
\]

We next construct the pointwise-bounded version needed for bounded differences.  For a local innovation block \(u=(u_s)_{s=-r}^r\), let
\[
 q_i(u;a)=\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^{\{-r,\ldots,r\}}}
 \mathbf 1\{\Psi_i^E(u,x)=a\}
 p^{\sum_s x_s}(1-p)^{2r+1-\sum_s x_s},
 \qquad a\in\{e_1,e_0\}. \tag{8}
\]
Define the guarded raw local score by first setting it to zero at every bad denominator:
\[
 R_i^0(u,x)=
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle
 \Psi_i^Y(u,x)\left\{
  \frac{\mathbf 1\{\Psi_i^E(u,x)=e_1\}}{q_i(u;e_1)}
  -\frac{\mathbf 1\{\Psi_i^E(u,x)=e_0\}}{q_i(u;e_0)}
 \right\},
 &\min_{a\in\{e_1,e_0\}}q_i(u;a)\ge\varepsilon,\\[3mm]
 0,&\min_{a\in\{e_1,e_0\}}q_i(u;a)<\varepsilon.
 \end{cases} \tag{9}
\]
Now clip the entire guarded raw score, not merely its denominators.  Namely, with
\[
 T_K(y)=\max\{-K,\min\{K,y\}\},
 \qquad \widetilde z_i(u,x)=T_K(R_i^0(u,x)), \tag{10}
\]
one has \(|\widetilde z_i(u,x)|\le K\) for every \((u,x)\), including innovation blocks lying in outcome-envelope null sets.  The finite sum in (8), the Borel cylinder maps, the piecewise definition (9), and continuity of \(T_K\) show that \(\widetilde z_i\) is Borel measurable.

For \(i\in I_n\) and \(w\in\{1,2\}\), evaluate (10) at
\[
 u=(U_{i+s})_{s=-r}^r,
 \qquad x=(D_{i+s}^{(w)})_{s=-r}^r,
\]
and denote the result by \(\widetilde Z_{n,i}^{(w)}\).  Exposure positivity makes the first branch of (9) apply almost surely.  On that branch the raw expression is the finite score from the buffered-restriction definition; the exact-score-split lemma gives \(|Z_{n,i}^{(w)}|\le K\) almost surely, using the buffered coupling.  Hence clipping acts as the identity almost surely.  Taking the intersection over the finitely many \(i\in I_n\) and the two waves gives
\[
 \widetilde Z_{n,i}^{(w)}=Z_{n,i}^{(w)}
 \quad\text{simultaneously for all }i\in I_n, w\in\{1,2\},
 \quad P\text{-almost surely}. \tag{11}
\]
Let \(\widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm u}\) be the formula defining \(\widehat\Omega_n^{\mathrm u}\) with every \(Z_{n,i}^{(w)}\) replaced by \(\widetilde Z_{n,i}^{(w)}\).  Equation (11) yields
\[
 \widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm u}=\widehat\Omega_n^{\mathrm u}
 \quad P\text{-almost surely}. \tag{12}
\]

The wavewise Bernoulli condition has a conditional law that is the constant product \(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p)\) law, and the cross-wave-independence condition makes the two such product laws conditionally independent given \(\mathcal F_U\).  Therefore, for finite cylinder events \(A,B,C\),
\[
 P\{U\in A,D^{(1)}\in B,D^{(2)}\in C\}
 =P\{U\in A\}P\{D^{(1)}\in B\}P\{D^{(2)}\in C\}. \tag{13}
\]
Together with iid innovations, (13) proves that the \(3n\) coordinates
\[
 U_1,\ldots,U_n,
 \quad D_1^{(1)},\ldots,D_n^{(1)},
 \quad D_1^{(2)},\ldots,D_n^{(2)} \tag{14}
\]
are mutually independent.  By joint-cylinder locality and the buffered restriction, every \(\widetilde Z_{n,i}^{(w)}\) depends only on the radius-\(r\) coordinates about \(i\).  Thus one environmental coordinate in (14) can affect at most \(2a_r\) products at a fixed lag: at most \(a_r\) through the wave-one factor and at most \(a_r\) through the wave-two factor.  One assignment coordinate can affect only its own wave and hence at most \(a_r\) products at a fixed lag.  Since both factors lie in \([-K,K]\), changing either or both changes a product by at most \(2K^2\).

For \(|k|\le2r\),
\[
 N_{n,k}=n-2r-|k|\ge n-4r>M. \tag{15}
\]
Consequently, changing one environmental coordinate changes the sum of the \(L\) short-lag averages by at most \(4a_rLK^2/(n-4r)\), and changes the lag-\(\ell\) average after multiplication by \(L\) by at most \(4a_rLK^2/M\).  Its total oscillation is therefore at most
\[
 4a_rLK^2\left\{\frac{1}{n-4r}+\frac{1}{M}\right\}
 \le \frac{8a_rLK^2}{M}. \tag{16}
\]
The corresponding count for one assignment coordinate is half as large, so its total oscillation is at most
\[
 2a_rLK^2\left\{\frac{1}{n-4r}+\frac{1}{M}\right\}
 \le \frac{4a_rLK^2}{M}. \tag{17}
\]
Set \(c=a_rLK^2/M\).  Summing the squared oscillation bounds over the \(n\) environmental coordinates and \(2n\) assignment coordinates gives
\[
 n(8c)^2+2n(4c)^2=96nc^2. \tag{18}
\]
Apply the cited McDiarmid bounded-differences lemma to the Borel function \(\widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm u}\) of the independent coordinates (14).  If the lemma's oscillation bounds are required to be strict, use arbitrarily enlarged bounds in (16)--(17) and then let the enlargement decrease to zero.  For every \(t>0\),
\[
 P\!\left\{
  \left|\widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm u}
  -\mathbb E_P\widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm u}\right|>t
 \right\}
 \le 2\exp\!\left\{-\frac{t^2}{48nc^2}\right\}. \tag{19}
\]
Taking
\[
 t=\sqrt{48n}\,c\sqrt{\log(2/\delta)}
 =\frac{\sqrt{48n}(2r+1)(4r+1)K^2}{n-4r-1}
   \sqrt{\log(2/\delta)}
 =q_n^{\mathrm u}(\delta) \tag{20}
\]
in (19), then using (7) and (12), proves
\[
 P\!\left\{
  \left|\widehat\Omega_n^{\mathrm u}-\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}\right|
  >q_n^{\mathrm u}(\delta)
 \right\}\le\delta. \tag{21}
\]

When \(\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}=0\), rejection by
\(\mathbf 1\{\widehat\Omega_n^{\mathrm u}>q_n^{\mathrm u}(\alpha)\}\)
implies the event in (21) with \(\delta=\alpha\), proving size at most \(\alpha\).  If
\[
 \Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}
 \ge q_n^{\mathrm u}(\alpha)+q_n^{\mathrm u}(\beta), \tag{22}
\]
then nonrejection implies
\(\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}-\widehat\Omega_n^{\mathrm u}\ge q_n^{\mathrm u}(\beta)\).
For every \(\beta'\in(\beta,1)\), strict monotonicity of \(q_n^{\mathrm u}(\cdot)\) makes this a deviation strictly larger than \(q_n^{\mathrm u}(\beta')\).  Equation (21) bounds the nonrejection probability by \(\beta'\); letting \(\beta'\downarrow\beta\) proves power at least \(1-\beta\).  Finally, with \(r,B,\varepsilon,\alpha,\beta\) fixed, \(K=B/\varepsilon\) is fixed and (20) is \(O(n^{-1/2})\).  Equation (7) proves that no sample-centering bias allowance occurs.

%%% FIELD proof-score-vector-minimax-rate
Because exposure positivity keeps the observed propensity coordinates away from zero on every law in \(\mathcal M_{2,\mathrm{loc}}\), the exposure-IPW formula defines the score vector from the full record.  More explicitly, extend reciprocal division to all Euclidean inputs by assigning an arbitrary value, say zero, when a displayed denominator is zero.  The resulting coordinatewise formula is Borel measurable and law independent, and it agrees on the model support with a map \(T_n\) satisfying
\[
 W_n=T_n(O_n^{\mathrm{obs}})
 \quad P\text{-almost surely for every }P\in\mathcal M_{2,\mathrm{loc}}. \tag{1}
\]
Therefore every score-vector test \(\psi_n\in\Psi_n\) induces the full-observation test \(\psi_n\circ T_n\in\Phi_n^{\mathrm{obs}}\), with exactly the same rejection probability under every model law.  Hence every separation feasible in the score experiment is feasible in the full-observation experiment.  Comparing the feasible sets in the two definitions gives
\[
 s_{n,\alpha,\beta}^{\mathrm{obs}}
 \le s_{n,\alpha,\beta}^{\mathrm{score}}. \tag{2}
\]
Under \(e_1\ne e_0\), \(p=1/2\), \(\alpha+\beta<1\), \(0<a\le2B\), and \(n>4r+1\), the already-established full-observable minimax theorem and (2) imply
\[
 s_{n,\alpha,\beta}^{\mathrm{score}}
 \ge s_{n,\alpha,\beta}^{\mathrm{obs}}
 \ge \frac{a^2(1-\alpha-\beta)}{\sqrt{2N_n}}. \tag{3}
\]

For the other direction, the statistic \(\widehat\Omega_n^{12}\), its threshold, and therefore \(\phi_{n,\alpha}\) are finite sums, products, and an indicator applied to the coordinates of \(W_n\).  Thus \(\phi_{n,\alpha}\) is a common Borel member of \(\Psi_n\).  The full-observable minimax theorem, through its cited size-and-separation result, certifies for this same test
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{2,\mathrm{loc}}:\,\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}=0}
 \mathbb E_P[\phi_{n,\alpha}]\le\alpha,
 \qquad
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{2,\mathrm{loc}}:\,\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}\ge\rho_n(\alpha,\beta)}
 \mathbb E_P[1-\phi_{n,\alpha}]\le\beta. \tag{4}
\]
Thus \(\rho_n(\alpha,\beta)\) is feasible in the definition of the score radius, and
\[
 s_{n,\alpha,\beta}^{\mathrm{score}}
 \le\rho_n(\alpha,\beta). \tag{5}
\]
Equations (3) and (5) prove the two asserted finite-sample inequalities.

The exactly unbiased lagged-statistic proposition proved above supplies a second common Borel score-vector test,
\[
 \mathbf 1\{\widehat\Omega_n^{\mathrm u}>q_n^{\mathrm u}(\alpha)\}. \tag{6}
\]
Its size is at most \(\alpha\), and its type-II error is at most \(\beta\) uniformly over
\[
 \Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}
 \ge q_n^{\mathrm u}(\alpha)+q_n^{\mathrm u}(\beta). \tag{7}
\]
Its expectation is exactly \(\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}\), so this operational alternative contains no sample-centering bias term.

It remains to compare orders.  Fix any \(a\in(0,2B]\).  Since \(N_n=n-2r\le n\) and \(1-\alpha-\beta>0\), (3) gives the positive lower bound
\[
 s_{n,\alpha,\beta}^{\mathrm{score}}
 \ge \frac{a^2(1-\alpha-\beta)}{\sqrt{2}}\,n^{-1/2}. \tag{8}
\]
For fixed \(r,B,\varepsilon,\alpha,\beta\), one has \(K=B/\varepsilon\),
\[
 b_n=\frac{4(4r+1)^2K^2}{n-2r}=O(n^{-1}), \tag{9}
\]
and, for each fixed \(\delta\in(0,1)\),
\[
 q_n(\delta)
 =\frac{\sqrt{48n}(2r+1)(4r+1)K^2}{n-4r}
   \sqrt{\log(2/\delta)}
 =O(n^{-1/2}). \tag{10}
\]
Therefore
\[
 \rho_n(\alpha,\beta)
 =2b_n+q_n(\alpha)+q_n(\beta)
 =O(n^{-1/2}). \tag{11}
\]
Combining (5), (8), and (11) proves
\[
 s_{n,\alpha,\beta}^{\mathrm{score}}=\Theta(n^{-1/2}). \tag{12}
\]
The formula defining \(q_n^{\mathrm u}\) likewise shows that the separation in (7) is \(O(n^{-1/2})\), completing every assertion.
