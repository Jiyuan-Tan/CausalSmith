Fix \(P\in\mathcal M_{2,\mathrm{loc}}\), \(i,j\in\mathbb Z\), and \(w\in\{1,2\}\).  We first establish the envelope needed for every conditional-expectation step.  If \(e_1\ne e_0\), at most one of the two exposure indicators in the definition of \(Z_i^{(w)}\) is one.  The outcome-envelope and exposure-positivity conditions therefore give
\[
 |Z_i^{(w)}|
 \le
 \frac{|Y_i(D^{(w)};U,G)|}
      {\min\{\pi_i(e_1),\pi_i(e_0)\}}
 \le \frac B\varepsilon=K
 \qquad P\text{-almost surely}. \tag{1}
\]
If \(e_1=e_0\), the two terms in the score bracket cancel identically, so (1) again holds.  Conditional Jensen's inequality applied to the definition of \(m_i\) gives \(|m_i|\le K\) almost surely.  Also \(|\tau_{\mathrm P}|=|\mathbb E_Pm_0|\le K\); hence \(|\xi_i^{(w)}|\le2K\) and \(|h_j|\le2K\).  Thus all variables and products below are integrable, and in fact square-integrable.

The score-decomposition definitions now give the pointwise algebraic identity
\[
 \xi_i^{(w)}+h_i
 =(Z_i^{(w)}-m_i)+(m_i-\tau_{\mathrm P})
 =Z_i^{(w)}-\tau_{\mathrm P}. \tag{2}
\]
Moreover, the defining conditional expectation for \(m_i\) implies
\[
 \mathbb E_P[\xi_i^{(w)}\mid\mathcal F_U]
 =\mathbb E_P[Z_i^{(w)}-m_i\mid\mathcal F_U]=0
 \qquad P\text{-almost surely}. \tag{3}
\]
Because \(h_j=m_j-\tau_{\mathrm P}\) is \(\mathcal F_U\)-measurable, the pull-out and tower properties of conditional expectation, justified by the bounds above, yield
\[
 \mathbb E_P[\xi_i^{(w)}h_j]
 =\mathbb E_P\!\left[
   h_j\mathbb E_P(\xi_i^{(w)}\mid\mathcal F_U)
 \right]
 =0. \tag{4}
\]
Equations (1), (2), and (4) prove every assertion.

Fix \(P\in\mathcal M_{2,\mathrm{loc}}\) and \(i,j\in\mathbb Z\).  The exact score-split lemma gives \(|Z_i^{(w)}|\le K\), so all products and conditional expectations below are integrable.  Outside the single assignment-independent null set in the joint cylinder-locality condition, \(Z_i^{(w)}\), conditional on \(\mathcal F_U\), is a measurable function only of the assignment field \(D^{(w)}\): the graph, the common no-carryover outcome schedule, the exposure map, and the known propensities are then fixed.  Conditional independence of the two assignment waves therefore implies conditional independence of \(Z_i^{(1)}\) and \(Z_j^{(2)}\), irrespective of overlap between their within-wave neighborhoods.  Since the two waves have the same conditional Bernoulli design, the definition of \(m_i\) gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P[Z_i^{(1)}Z_j^{(2)}\mid\mathcal F_U]
 =\mathbb E_P[Z_i^{(1)}\mid\mathcal F_U]
  \mathbb E_P[Z_j^{(2)}\mid\mathcal F_U]
 =m_im_j
 \qquad P\text{-almost surely}. \tag{1}
\]

We next justify the stationarity used to identify the displayed target.  The graph, exposure, and outcome equivariance conditions, with the declared left-shift orientation, imply that shifting site and arguments together carries the score at site \(i\) to the score at site \(i+k\).  Integrating its local assignment coordinates against the same product \(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p)\) law therefore gives
\[
 m_{i+k}(U)=m_i(\theta^kU)
 \qquad P\text{-almost surely}. \tag{2}
\]
The iid innovation condition makes the law of \(U\) invariant under every \(\theta^k\).  Hence \((m_i)_{i\in\mathbb Z}\) is stationary, so
\[
 \mathbb E_Pm_i=\mathbb E_Pm_0=\tau_{\mathrm P},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_Ph_i=0. \tag{3}
\]
Taking expectations in (1), setting \(i=0\) and \(j=k\), and using \(m_i=\tau_{\mathrm P}+h_i\) and (3), we obtain
\[
 \mathbb E_P[Z_0^{(1)}Z_k^{(2)}]-\tau_{\mathrm P}^2
 =\mathbb E_P[m_0m_k]-\tau_{\mathrm P}^2
 =\mathbb E_P[h_0h_k]
 =\gamma_h(k). \tag{4}
\]
The causal target was defined independently as \(\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}=\sum_{k=-2r}^{2r}\gamma_h(k)\).  Summing (4) over that fixed support gives
\[
 \Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}
 =\sum_{k=-2r}^{2r}
 \left\{\mathbb E_P[Z_0^{(1)}Z_k^{(2)}]-\tau_{\mathrm P}^2\right\}.
\]
Thus the observed-law cross-wave moment is proved to equal the pre-existing causal functional; the equality is not imposed by definition.

Fix \(P\in\mathcal M_{2,\mathrm{loc}}\).  We first verify the finite-block premise of the cited coboundary theorem from the corrected joint cylinder condition.  At the origin, write the class-supplied cylinder map as \(\Psi_0=(\Psi_0^E,\Psi_0^Y)\).  For a local innovation block \(u=(u_s)_{s=-r}^r\) and \(a\in\{e_1,e_0\}\), put
\[
 q_a(u)=
 \sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^{\{-r,\ldots,r\}}}
 \mathbf 1\{\Psi_0^E(u,x)=a\}
 p^{\sum_sx_s}(1-p)^{2r+1-\sum_sx_s}. \tag{1}
\]
The wavewise Bernoulli condition identifies (1) with the conditional exposure propensity at the origin; exposure positivity makes both denominators at least \(\varepsilon\) almost surely.  Integrating the exposure-IPW formula over the same finite product law shows that \(m_0\), and hence \(h_0=m_0-\tau_{\mathrm P}\), is a Borel function of \((U_{-r},\ldots,U_r)\).  Equivariance gives the same translated Borel function at every site.  Consequently
\[
 h_i=f(U_{i-r},\ldots,U_{i+r}) \tag{2}
\]
for one Borel \(f\).  The exact score-split envelope implies square integrability, and the definition of \(\tau_{\mathrm P}\), together with stationarity established from iid innovations and equivariance, gives \(\mathbb E_Ph_i=0\).  Thus \((h_i)\) is a centered square-integrable \((2r+1)\)-block factor.  In particular, the innovation blocks in (2) are disjoint when \(|i-j|>2r\), so iid innovations imply
\[
 \gamma_h(k)=0\qquad(|k|>2r). \tag{3}
\]

For every integer \(M>2r\), stationarity and (3) give the exact covariance expansion
\[
 \frac1M\operatorname{Var}_P\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^Mh_i\right)
 =\gamma_h(0)+2\sum_{k=1}^{2r}
 \left(1-\frac{k}{M}\right)\gamma_h(k). \tag{4}
\]
Letting \(M\to\infty\) in this finite sum and using \(\gamma_h(-k)=\gamma_h(k)\) yields
\[
 \lim_{M\to\infty}\frac1M
 \operatorname{Var}_P\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^Mh_i\right)
 =\sum_{k=-2r}^{2r}\gamma_h(k)
 =\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}. \tag{5}
\]
Every quantity on the left of (5) is nonnegative, proving \(\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}\ge0\).

Apply the cited Janson block-factor coboundary lemma to \(X_j=h_{j+r}\), which by (2) is a block factor of \((U_j,\ldots,U_{j+2r})\) of length \(2r+1\).  Its square-integrability and centering were proved above, and (5) identifies its limiting normalized variance with \(\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}\).  The cited result therefore gives \(\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}=0\) if and only if there is a square-integrable \(2r\)-block factor
\[
 g_i=g(U_{i-r+1},\ldots,U_{i+r})
 \quad\text{such that}\quad
 h_i=g_i-g_{i-1}
 \qquad P\text{-almost surely}. \tag{6}
\]
Under (6), telescoping over the buffered interval is exact:
\[
 \sum_{i\in I_n}h_i
 =\sum_{i=r+1}^{n-r}(g_i-g_{i-1})
 =g_{n-r}-g_r,
 \qquad
 \frac1{N_n}\sum_{i\in I_n}h_i
 =\frac{g_{n-r}-g_r}{N_n}. \tag{7}
\]
For \(n>4r\), \(g_r\) depends on \((U_1,\ldots,U_{2r})\), whereas \(g_{n-r}\) depends on \((U_{n-2r+1},\ldots,U_n)\).  These blocks are disjoint.  Iid innovations make the endpoints independent, and stationarity gives each the law of \(g_0\).  Hence the first quantity in (7) has, for every such \(n\), the law of \(g^\star-g_0\), where \(g^\star\) is an independent copy of \(g_0\).  In particular,
\[
 \sum_{i\in I_n}h_i\Rightarrow g^\star-g_0.
\]
This proves the proposition.

Fix \(P\in\mathcal M_{2,\mathrm{loc}}\), let

 \mathbb E_P\!\left[

 \right]
 =\mathbb E_P[m_0m_k]
 =\tau_{\mathrm P}^2+\gamma_h(k). \tag{3}

The same argument at lag \(\ell\), followed by (2), gives

 \mathbb E_P\!\left[

 \right]
 =\tau_{\mathrm P}^2. \tag{4}

There are exactly \(L\) integers in \([-2r,2r]\).  Subtracting \(L\) copies of (4) from the sum of (3) over those lags proves

 \mathbb E_P[\widehat\Omega_n^{\mathrm u}]
 =\sum_{k=-2r}^{2r}\gamma_h(k)
 =\Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}. \tag{5}

 P\{U\in A,D^{(1)}\in B,D^{(2)}\in C\}

 U_1,\ldots,U_n,

are mutually independent.  The revised buffered-restriction definition constructs every retained exposure, outcome, and propensity coordinate from its common radius-\(r\) cylinder map. The exact score envelope and buffered coupling give \(|Z_{n,i}^{(w)}|\le K\) almost surely.

For pointwise application of the cited bounded-differences lemma, choose the following harmless Borel version.  On a local innovation block for which either cylinder propensity is smaller than \(\varepsilon\), set the corresponding local score map equal to zero; elsewhere use the displayed IPW formula.  Exposure positivity says that this modification occurs on a null event for every retained site, so the resulting finite statistic equals \(\widehat\Omega_n^{\mathrm u}\) almost surely.  The modified score is bounded by \(K\) on every input block, not merely almost surely.  Consequently changing either or both factors changes a product by at most \(2K^2\).  N_{n,k}=n-2r-|k|\ge n-4r>M, \tag{7}

 4a_rLK^2\left\{\frac1{n-4r}+\frac1M\right\}
 \le \frac{8a_rLK^2}{M}. \tag{8}

For one assignment coordinate only one wave's factor can change.  The identical count with \(a_r\), rather than \(2a_r\), affected products gives total oscillation at most

 2a_rLK^2\left\{\frac1{n-4r}+\frac1M\right\}
 \le \frac{4a_rLK^2}{M}. \tag{9}

 n(8c)^2+2n(4c)^2=96nc^2. \tag{10}

 P\!\left\{

 \right\}
 \le2\exp\!\left\{-\frac{t^2}{48nc^2}\right\}. \tag{11}

 t=\sqrt{48n}\,c\sqrt{\log(2/\delta)}

  \sqrt{\log(2/\delta)}
 =q_n^{\mathrm u}(\delta) \tag{12}
\]
in (11), and using (5), proves the asserted concentration inequality.

 \Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}

For every \(\beta'\in(\beta,1)\), strict decrease of \(q_n^{\mathrm u}(\cdot)\) turns this into a deviation strictly exceeding \(q_n^{\mathrm u}(\beta')\); (11) bounds its probability by \(\beta'\).  Letting \(\beta'\downarrow\beta\) proves power at least \(1-\beta\).  Finally, for fixed \(r,B,\varepsilon,\alpha,\beta\), (12) is \(O(n^{-1/2})\), and (5) confirms that no sample-centering bias allowance is present.

c=c'&(1/2,0,0,1/2)&(1/2,0,0,1/2)\\
c\ne c'\text{ within one exposure group}
 &(1/6,1/3,1/3,1/6)&(1/2,0,0,1/2)\\
c,c'\text{ in opposite exposure groups}
 &(1/3,1/6,1/6,1/3)&(0,1/2,1/2,0).
\end{array} \tag{13}
\]
These three cases cover all sixteen ordered pairs in \(\mathcal C^2\), furnishing the exact finite-cell stress test.  In every row, every atom charged by \(R_1^{c,c'}\) has positive \(R_0^{c,c'}\)-mass.  Hence the induced observed two-wave outcome laws have common support, although the latent four-cell schedule laws need not.

Let \(R_t^{c,c'}=(1-t)R_0^{c,c'}+tR_1^{c,c'}\).  Direct substitution in (13) yields
\[
 \sum_{x:R_0^{c,c'}(x)>0}
 \frac{\{R_1^{c,c'}(x)-R_0^{c,c'}(x)\}^2}
      {R_0^{c,c'}(x)}
 =\begin{cases}
   0,&c=c',\\
   2,&c\ne c'.
  \end{cases} \tag{14}
\]
For probability masses \(R\ll S\), the elementary inequality \(\log u\le u-1\) gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(R\Vert S)
 =\sum_xR(x)\log\frac{R(x)}{S(x)}
 \le\sum_x\frac{(R(x)-S(x))^2}{S(x)}. \tag{15}
\]
Applying (15) to \(R_t^{c,c'}\) and \(R_0^{c,c'}\), and then using (14), proves
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(R_t^{c,c'}\Vert R_0^{c,c'})\le2t^2. \tag{16}
\]

Conditional on the full assignments, the selected outcome pairs are independent over \(i\in I_n\), because pair \(i\) uses only \(V_i\).  The graph and assignment laws coincide under \(P_t\) and \(P_0\), while all exposures and propensity coordinates are common deterministic functions of those assignments.  The revised restriction definition also ensures that these finite coordinates are induced by the same cylinder maps used in the membership check.  Conditional on the assignments, the outcome likelihood ratio is therefore the product of the \(N_n\) sitewise likelihood ratios.  Taking its logarithm and conditional expectation, then averaging over the common assignment law, gives directly
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\operatorname{KL}\!\left(
   \mathcal L_{P_t}(O_n^{\mathrm{obs}})
   \middle\Vert
   \mathcal L_{P_0}(O_n^{\mathrm{obs}})
  \right)\\
 &\quad=
 \mathbb E_D\sum_{i\in I_n}
 \operatorname{KL}\!\left(
  R_t^{C_i(D^{(1)}),C_i(D^{(2)})}
  \middle\Vert
  R_0^{C_i(D^{(1)}),C_i(D^{(2)})}
 \right)
 \le2N_nt^2.
 \end{aligned} \tag{17}
\]
Thus (17) controls the complete declared record, not only its score-vector image.

Put \(\eta=1-\alpha-\beta>0\) and
\[
 t_0=\frac{\eta}{\sqrt{2N_n}}. \tag{18}
\]
Since \(0<\eta<1\) and \(N_n\ge1\), one has \(0<t_0<1\), so \(P_{t_0}\) belongs to the submodel.  The cited Pinsker lemma and (17) imply
\[
 \operatorname{TV}\!\left(
  \mathcal L_{P_{t_0}}(O_n^{\mathrm{obs}}),
  \mathcal L_{P_0}(O_n^{\mathrm{obs}})
 \right)
 \le\sqrt{N_nt_0^2}
 =\frac{\eta}{\sqrt2}. \tag{19}
\]
Let \(\varphi_n\in\Phi_n^{\mathrm{obs}}\) have type-I error at most \(\alpha\) under every null law.  To apply the cited binary Le Cam lemma to this randomized test, adjoin under both laws the same independent \(A\sim\operatorname{Uniform}[0,1]\) and reject exactly when \(A\le\varphi_n(O_n^{\mathrm{obs}})\).  If \(\mu,\nu\) are the two laws of \(O_n^{\mathrm{obs}}\) and \(\lambda\) is the uniform law, then sectioning any measurable \(H\) gives
\[
 |(\mu\times\lambda)(H)-(\nu\times\lambda)(H)|
 \le\int|\mu(H_u)-\nu(H_u)|\,d\lambda(u)
 \le\operatorname{TV}(\mu,\nu).
\]
The reverse inequality follows by taking \(H=C\times[0,1]\) and then the supremum over \(C\).  Thus product with the common uniform variable preserves total variation.  The Le Cam lemma and (19) now yield
\[
 \mathbb E_{P_{t_0}}[1-\varphi_n(O_n^{\mathrm{obs}})]
 \ge1-\alpha-\frac{\eta}{\sqrt2}
 =\beta+\eta\left(1-\frac1{\sqrt2}\right)
 >\beta. \tag{20}
\]
By (12) and (18),
\[
 \Omega_{\mathrm{pop}}(P_{t_0})
 =\frac{a^2(1-\alpha-\beta)}{\sqrt{2N_n}}. \tag{21}
\]
For every proposed separation not exceeding (21), the alternative class contains \(P_{t_0}\), whereas (20) rules out the required type-II guarantee for every test satisfying the uniform null guarantee.  The definition of the minimax radius therefore implies
\[
 s_{n,\alpha,\beta}^{\mathrm{obs}}
 \ge\frac{a^2(1-\alpha-\beta)}{\sqrt{2N_n}}. \tag{22}
\]
Combining (3) and (22) proves the displayed finite-sample bounds.  Finally, choose any fixed \(a\in(0,2B]\), for example \(a=B\).  Since \(N_n=n-2r\) and \(1-\alpha-\beta>0\), (22) is bounded below by a positive constant times \(n^{-1/2}\).  The two-wave size-and-separation theorem gives \(\rho_n(\alpha,\beta)=O(n^{-1/2})\), so (3) supplies the matching upper order.  Hence
\[
 s_{n,\alpha,\beta}^{\mathrm{obs}}=\Theta(n^{-1/2}).
\]
Distinct focal labels are necessary for this witness: if \(e_1=e_0\), the two terms in every exposure-IPW score cancel identically.

For each \(n>4r\), let \(\Lambda_n=\{1,\ldots,n\}\) and \(I_n=\{r+1,\ldots,n-r\}\).  Couple the finite record to the common infinite construction by retaining the same coordinates of \(U\), \(G(U)\), \(D^{(1)}\), and \(D^{(2)}\) on \(\Lambda_n\).  For \(i\in I_n\), write the joint cylinder map supplied by the locality condition as \(\Psi_i=(\Psi_i^E,\Psi_i^Y)\), put
\[
 u_i=(U_{i+s})_{s=-r}^{r},
 \qquad
 x_i^{(w)}=(D_{i+s}^{(w)})_{s=-r}^{r},
\]
and define the finite exposure and realized-outcome coordinates entering the retained score to be, respectively,
\[
 \Psi_i^E(u_i,x_i^{(w)})
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \Psi_i^Y(u_i,x_i^{(w)}).
\]
For a label \(a\in\{e_1,e_0\}\), define its finite known propensity by the induced product-Bernoulli cylinder probability
\[
 q_{n,i}(a)
 =\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^{\{-r,\ldots,r\}}}
 \mathbf 1\{\Psi_i^E(u_i,x)=a\}
 p^{\sum_sx_s}(1-p)^{2r+1-\sum_sx_s}. \tag{1}
\]
Then define, for \(w\in\{1,2\}\),

 Z_{n,i}^{(w)}
 =\Psi_i^Y(u_i,x_i^{(w)})
 \left\{
  \frac{\mathbf 1\{\Psi_i^E(u_i,x_i^{(w)})=e_1\}}{q_{n,i}(e_1)}

 \right\}. \tag{2}
