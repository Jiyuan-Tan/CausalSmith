Let \(\mu_{p_0,A}\) be the product \(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p_0)\) law on \(\{0,1\}^A\), and define
\[
 \bar z(u_A)
 :=\sum_{d_A\in\{0,1\}^A}
 z(u_A,d_A)\mu_{p_0,A}(\{d_A\}). \tag{3}
\]
This is a Borel function and \(|\bar z|\le K_0\), because \(A\) is finite, \(z\) is Borel, and \(|z|\le K_0\).  The conditional law of \((D_{vx}^{(w)})_{x\in A}\) given \(\sigma(U)\) is \(\mu_{p_0,A}\) and does not depend on \(U\).  Therefore the common local-score representation and the defining property of conditional expectation give, simultaneously for both waves,
\[
 m_v
 =\mathbb E[Z_v^{(w)}\mid\sigma(U)]
 =\bar z((U_{vx})_{x\in A}),
 \qquad |m_v|\le K_0. \tag{4}
\]
Thus the conditional mean is well defined independently of \(w\).  Moreover, for every \(t\in\Gamma\), the iid innovation assumption and the bijection \(y\mapsto ty\) imply
\[
 \bigl((U_{tvx})_{x\in A}\bigr)_{v\in\Gamma}
 \ \stackrel{d}{=}\ 
 \bigl((U_{vx})_{x\in A}\bigr)_{v\in\Gamma}.
\]
Because the same function \(\bar z\) occurs at every site, (4) proves that \((m_v)_{v\in\Gamma}\) is stationary under left translation.  In particular,
\[
 \mathbb E[m_v]=\mathbb E[m_e]=\tau
 \qquad(v\in\Gamma). \tag{5}
\]

We also record the finite-coordinate independence that will be used in the concentration argument.  If \(f_0,f_1,f_2\) are bounded Borel functions of finitely many coordinates of \(U,D^{(1)},D^{(2)}\), respectively, conditional independence of the waves and their fixed product conditional laws give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\mathbb E[f_0(U)f_1(D^{(1)})f_2(D^{(2)})]\\
 &\quad=\mathbb E\!\left[
 f_0(U)\mathbb E[f_1(D^{(1)})\mid\sigma(U)]
          \mathbb E[f_2(D^{(2)})\mid\sigma(U)]\right]\\
 &\quad=\mathbb E[f_0(U)]
       \left(\int f_1\,d\mu_{p_0}\right)
       \left(\int f_2\,d\mu_{p_0}\right),
 \end{aligned} \tag{6}
\]
where each integral is with respect to the appropriate finite-dimensional Bernoulli product marginal.  Hence every finite collection of innovation coordinates, first-wave assignment coordinates, and second-wave assignment coordinates used below is mutually independent, with independence also holding within each of the three collections.

The footprint determines the covariance support.  If \(vA\cap vgA\ne\varnothing\), then \(vx=vgy\) for some \(x,y\in A\), and cancellation in the group gives \(g=xy^{-1}\in AA^{-1}=C\).  Consequently, when \(g\notin C\), the two innovation blocks in (4) are disjoint.  The iid innovation assumption, (4), and (5) then yield
\[
 \mathbb E[h_vh_{vg}]
 =\mathbb E[(m_v-\tau)(m_{vg}-\tau)]=0
 \qquad(g\notin C). \tag{7}
\]
Thus the finite set \(C\) is indeed a covariance-support set.  Notice also that \(e\in C\) and \(C^{-1}=C\), directly from \(e\in A\) and \(C=AA^{-1}\).

Conditional on \(\sigma(U)\), \(Z_u^{(1)}\) is a bounded function of the first assignment field and \(Z_v^{(2)}\) is a bounded function of the second assignment field.  Conditional wave independence and (4) therefore imply, for arbitrary \(u,v\in\Gamma\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E[Z_u^{(1)}Z_v^{(2)}\mid\sigma(U)]
 &=\mathbb E[Z_u^{(1)}\mid\sigma(U)]
   \mathbb E[Z_v^{(2)}\mid\sigma(U)]\\
 &=m_um_v.
 \end{aligned} \tag{8}
\]
Taking \(u=v\) and then replacing the second site by \(vg\) proves the theorem's first displayed identity, including overlapping footprints.

For \(g\in C\) and \(v\in J_g\), equations (5), (8), and left stationarity give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E[Z_v^{(1)}Z_{vg}^{(2)}]
 &=\mathbb E[m_vm_{vg}]\\
 &=\tau^2+\mathbb E[h_vh_{vg}]\\
 &=\tau^2+\mathbb E[h_eh_g]
 =\tau^2+\gamma(g).
 \end{aligned} \tag{9}
\]
Since \(|J_g|>0\) by \(\nu>0\), averaging (9) gives
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[
 |J_g|^{-1}\sum_{v\in J_g}Z_v^{(1)}Z_{vg}^{(2)}
 \right]=\tau^2+\gamma(g). \tag{10}
\]
Set \(\overline m=N^{-1}\sum_{v\in I}m_v\).  Applying (8) term by term and then using (5) yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E[\overline Z^{(1)}\overline Z^{(2)}]
 &=\mathbb E[\overline m^2]\\
 &=(\mathbb E\overline m)^2+\operatorname{Var}(\overline m)\\
 &=\tau^2+\operatorname{Var}(\overline m).
 \end{aligned} \tag{11}
\]
This is the corrected square \(\overline m^2\).  Subtracting \(q=|C|\) copies of (11) from the sum of (10) over \(g\in C\) gives the exact identity
\[
 \mathbb E[\widehat\Omega]
 =\sum_{g\in C}\gamma(g)-q\operatorname{Var}(\overline m)
 =\Omega-q\operatorname{Var}(\overline m). \tag{12}
\]

It remains to bound the centering term.  Equations (4)--(5) imply \(|h_v|\le2K_0\).  For each fixed \(u\), (7) shows that \(\mathbb E[h_uh_v]\) can be nonzero only if \(u^{-1}v\in C\), so there are at most \(q\) such sites \(v\).  Since \(|\mathbb E[h_uh_v]|\le4K_0^2\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 0\le\operatorname{Var}(\overline m)
 &=\frac1{N^2}\sum_{u,v\in I}\mathbb E[h_uh_v]\\
 &\le\frac1{N^2}\sum_{u\in I}
       \sum_{\substack{v\in I:\ u^{-1}v\in C}}
       |\mathbb E[h_uh_v]|\\
 &\le\frac{Nq\,4K_0^2}{N^2}
 =\frac{4qK_0^2}{N}. 
 \end{aligned} \tag{13}
\]
Combining (12)--(13) proves
\[
 0\le\Omega-\mathbb E[\widehat\Omega]
 =q\operatorname{Var}(\overline m)
 \le\frac{4q^2K_0^2}{N},
\]
which is (1).

We next derive (2) from the frozen bounded-differences lemma.  Write
\[
 T:=\sum_{g\in C}|J_g|^{-1}\sum_{v\in J_g}
 Z_v^{(1)}Z_{vg}^{(2)},
 \qquad
 R:=q\overline Z^{(1)}\overline Z^{(2)},
 \qquad
 c:=\frac{aqK_0^2}{\nu}, \tag{14}
\]
so \(\widehat\Omega=T-R\).  Fix \(y\in\Lambda\).  The coordinate \(U_y\) belongs to at most \(a\) footprints \(vA\), because for a fixed \(x\in A\), the equation \(y=vx\) has the unique solution \(v=yx^{-1}\).  At a fixed \(g\), changing only \(U_y\) therefore changes at most \(2a\) summands of \(T\), allowing for either endpoint.  Each product belongs to \([-K_0^2,K_0^2]\), so
\[
 |\Delta_yT|
 \le\sum_{g\in C}\frac{2a(2K_0^2)}{|J_g|}
 \le\frac{4aqK_0^2}{\nu}=4c. \tag{15}
\]
The same change modifies at most \(a\) scores in each wave.  Each wave mean thus changes by at most \(2aK_0/N\), and both its old and new values belong to \([-K_0,K_0]\).  Using \(x'y'-xy=(x'-x)y'+x(y'-y)\) and \(\nu\le N\), because \(J_g\subseteq I\), gives
\[
 |\Delta_yR|
 \le q\left(\frac{2aK_0}{N}K_0
             +K_0\frac{2aK_0}{N}\right)
 =\frac{4aqK_0^2}{N}
 \le4c. \tag{16}
\]
Thus the oscillation of \(\widehat\Omega\) under a change of one environment coordinate is at most \(8c\).

A coordinate \(D_y^{(w)}\) belongs to at most \(a\) scores in its wave.  At every fixed \(g\), changing it can affect at most \(a\) products, because it can occur at only the wave-\(w\) endpoint.  The same calculations now give
\[
 |\Delta_yT|\le2c,
 \qquad
 |\Delta_yR|\le2c,
 \qquad
 |\Delta_y\widehat\Omega|\le4c. \tag{17}
\]
Every retained score uses only coordinates in \(vA\subseteq\Lambda\), by the definition of \(I\).  For every product, both \(v\) and \(vg\) lie in \(I\), by the definition of \(J_g\).  Hence \(\widehat\Omega\) is a Borel function of no more than \(|\Lambda|\) innovation coordinates and \(|\Lambda|\) coordinates from each assignment wave.  These finite coordinates are independent by (6).  From (15)--(17), the sum of squared oscillation bounds is at most
\[
 |\Lambda|(8c)^2+2|\Lambda|(4c)^2
 =96|\Lambda|c^2. \tag{18}
\]
Apply the frozen McDiarmid bounded-differences lemma, with every displayed oscillation bound first multiplied by \(1+\eta\) and then with \(\eta\downarrow0\) to meet that lemma's strict-inequality convention.  For every \(t>0\),
\[
 \Pr\{ |\widehat\Omega-\mathbb E\widehat\Omega|>t\}
 \le2\exp\!\left\{-\frac{t^2}{48|\Lambda|c^2}\right\}. \tag{19}
\]
For \(0<\delta<1\), choose
\[
 t=\sqrt{48|\Lambda|}\,c\sqrt{\log(2/\delta)}. \tag{20}
\]
Then the right-hand side of (19) is \(\delta\).  On the complementary event, (1), the triangle inequality, (14), and (20) imply
\[
 |\widehat\Omega-\Omega|
 \le \frac{4q^2K_0^2}{N}
 +\frac{\sqrt{48|\Lambda|}\,aqK_0^2}{\nu}
   \sqrt{\log(2/\delta)}.
\]
This is precisely (2).

Finally, let \((\Lambda_j)\) be a right F\o lner sequence.  Since \(e\in A\),
\[
 I_j=\Lambda_j\cap\bigcap_{x\in A}\Lambda_jx^{-1}.
\]
The union bound and the right F\o lner property, applied to each fixed \(x^{-1}\), give
\[
 \frac{|\Lambda_j\setminus I_j|}{|\Lambda_j|}
 \le\sum_{x\in A}
 \frac{|\Lambda_j\mathbin\triangle\Lambda_jx^{-1}|}{|\Lambda_j|}
 \longrightarrow0. \tag{21}
\]
Therefore \(N_j/|\Lambda_j|\to1\).  For fixed \(g\in C\), translation preserves cardinality, and the triangle inequality for symmetric differences gives
\[
 |I_j\mathbin\triangle I_jg^{-1}|
 \le2|\Lambda_j\setminus I_j|
   +|\Lambda_j\mathbin\triangle\Lambda_jg^{-1}|. \tag{22}
\]
The right-hand side is \(o(|\Lambda_j|)\) by (21) and the right F\o lner property.  Since
\[
 J_{j,g}=I_j\cap I_jg^{-1},
 \qquad
 |J_{j,g}|=|I_j|-\frac12|I_j\mathbin\triangle I_jg^{-1}|,
\]
we obtain \(|J_{j,g}|/|\Lambda_j|\to1\).  The set \(C\) is fixed and finite, so the convergence is uniform over \(g\in C\), and consequently \(\nu_j/|\Lambda_j|\to1\).  Substitution into (1)--(2), with fixed \(A\), \(a\), and \(q\), gives the claimed \(O(|\Lambda_j|^{-1})\) bias and \(O(\sqrt{\log(2/\delta)/|\Lambda_j|})\) deviation radius.

For the exact smallest-instance stress test, take \(A=\{e\}\) and \(\Lambda=\{e\}\).  Then \(C=I=J_e=\{e\}\) and \(a=q=N=\nu=1\).  For every realization of both assignments,
\[
 \widehat\Omega
 =Z_e^{(1)}Z_e^{(2)}
  -Z_e^{(1)}Z_e^{(2)}=0,
\]
whereas \(\Omega=\gamma(e)=\operatorname{Var}(m_e)\); hence (12) becomes the exact equality \(0=\Omega-\operatorname{Var}(m_e)\).  Thus the expectation identity survives exhaustive assignment enumeration in the smallest volume.  At the asymptotic boundary, the proof uses fixed \(A\) and \(\nu_j/|\Lambda_j|\to1\); a growing footprint or collapsing pair interior is outside the theorem and need not retain the root-volume radius.
