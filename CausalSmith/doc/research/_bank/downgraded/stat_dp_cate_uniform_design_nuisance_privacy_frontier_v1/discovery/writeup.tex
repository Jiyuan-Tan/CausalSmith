\section*{stat\_\allowbreak{}dp\_\allowbreak{}cate\_\allowbreak{}uniform\_\allowbreak{}design\_\allowbreak{}nuisance\_\allowbreak{}privacy\_\allowbreak{}frontier}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} On the deliberately restricted class with a known exact-uniform covariate law and bounded observed outcomes, pointwise CATE is a regression-functional hybrid whose pure-central privacy cost has the two scales \(\rho_n^{\mathrm{priv}}=\max\{(n\epsilon_n)^{-\gamma/(\gamma+d)},(n^2\epsilon_n)^{-1/(1+d/\gamma+d/(\alpha+\beta))}\}\). One second-order local estimator and a positive fuzzy Hamming-transport converse yield the matched frontier whenever privacy leads or nuisance smoothness is above the nonprivate elbow. Separately, the exact-uniform class is a subclass of the aligned unknown bounded-density class because its density is one, so both privacy lower barriers also hold on that broader Kennedy-style model. This is a lower-bound transfer only: neither the occupancy-normalized estimator nor the matched phase diagram is claimed beyond exact uniformity. The remaining exact-uniform question is a stability transfer from the attained benchmark to the symbolic nonprivate minimax risk in the privacy-inactive below-elbow region.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Kennedy et al. establish nonprivate pointwise-CATE minimax theory under bounded-density random design and density-qualified attainability conditions, while private CATE papers establish algorithmic privacy guarantees without a pure-central Hölder pointwise minimax frontier. None determines the additional central-private price generated by the product of propensity and outcome-regression errors on a known exact-uniform causal law.

\paragraph{Niche.} Exact uniformity is narrower than a bounded-density design and is load-bearing: it makes the local projection geometry deterministic and the probability of at least two observations in each equal-volume microcell public. The theorem applies directly to randomized benchmark studies that generate covariate locations from a public uniform law; deterministic uniform-grid studies are a fixed-design analogue, not a claim covered by the iid theorem.

\paragraph{Fill.} On the known exact-uniform class, a single \(p^2+3p\)-coordinate Laplace release contains the score block, the upper-triangular Gram block, and their two exact occupancy-normalized corrections; every subsequent symmetry and spectral operation is postprocessing. Boundedness is imposed only on observed outcomes, with causal and kernel bounds derived from consistency, armwise exchangeability, and overlap. A smooth positive exact-uniform fuzzy family supplies singleton cancellation and a componentwise Hamming coupling. Because the uniform density equals one, subclass inclusion and conditional Jensen additionally transfer both privacy lower barriers, but no upper bound, to the aligned unknown bounded-density class. One open symbolic-risk transfer question remains confined to the exact-uniform model and separate from the queued nonprivate converse.

\section{Related work}

Kennedy et al. (2024), Theorem 1 and Remark 2, analyze a bounded-density random-design Hölder model; Theorem 2 and Appendix B.8 add design-estimation conditions for attainability. Their localized fuzzy hypotheses and higher-order local R-moment architecture inform this proposal. The exact-uniform class is narrower than their design setting, while the new bounded-density corollary uses only inclusion of the uniform subclass to transfer the two privacy lower barriers; it does not transfer the exact-uniform estimator or claim Kennedy et al.'s nonprivate result settles the symbolic exact-uniform risk. Kennedy (2023), Theorems 1--3 and Sections 3--5, gives pseudo-outcome oracle inequalities and local-polynomial error bounds, but risk control alone supplies no neighboring-record stability. Niu et al. (2022), Theorem 6, proves \(f\)-DP composition for a sample-split multi-stage CATE meta-algorithm without a Hölder minimax utility theorem. Schröder, Melnychuk, and Feuerriegel (2025), page 3 and Theorems 1 and 3, assume bounded observed outcomes and develop approximate-DP orthogonal two-stage CATE releases for finite or functional queries; they do not provide a pure-central Hölder pointwise minimax rate or the \(n^2\epsilon_n\) coupled-nuisance branch. Chaudhuri et al. (2024), Algorithm 1 and Theorem 2, stabilize bounded-kernel U-statistics using local Hájek projections; the exact-uniform binomial normalization here is a causal-specific construction. Cai, Chakraborty, and Vuursteen (2024), Theorems 2.1, 3.4, and 4.4, establish pointwise Besov-regression rates under heterogeneous distributed privacy rather than one-database pure central privacy with causal nuisances. Acharya, Sun, and Zhang (2021), Theorems 1 and 3, and Lalanne, Garivier, and Gribonval (2023), the main transport reduction, supply private testing inequalities but no smooth causal shared-prior coupling. Nissim, Raskhodnikova, and Smith (2007), Sections 1.2--1.3, explain why typical occupancy cannot replace a worst-neighbor sensitivity certificate. Every comparator remains methodological input and informed-by positioning.

  \item \texttt{Pi\_\allowbreak{}s\_\allowbreak{}n} --- Probability law on \(\mathcal O^n\); \texttt{shared-\allowbreak{}prior sample mixture}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\Pi_s^n=\mathbb E_\lambda[P_{s,\lambda}^{\otimes n}]\).
  \item \texttt{W\_\allowbreak{}H} --- \([0,n]\); \texttt{private testing transport cost}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Minimum expected record Hamming distance over couplings of \(\Pi_+^n\) and \(\Pi_-^n\).
  \item \texttt{H\_\allowbreak{}transfer} --- \texttt{Proof handle}; \texttt{open privacy-\allowbreak{}transfer route}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \texttt{Stabilize a nonprivate nearly minimax pointwise-\allowbreak{}CATE procedure by rewriting its local score into bounded first-\allowbreak{}order and occupancy-\allowbreak{}normalized second-\allowbreak{}order blocks,\allowbreak{} then privatize one joint release and prove an oracle inequality relative to its original risk.}
  \item \texttt{M\_\allowbreak{}bd} --- \texttt{Class of causal laws}; Laws on \(Y_0,Y_1,A,X\); \texttt{unknown bounded-\allowbreak{}density coupled-\allowbreak{}nuisance Hölder class}
  \item \texttt{R\_\allowbreak{}NP\_\allowbreak{}bd} --- \([0,2]\); \texttt{bounded-\allowbreak{}density nonprivate minimax absolute risk}
  \item \texttt{R\_\allowbreak{}DP\_\allowbreak{}bd} --- \([0,2]\); \texttt{bounded-\allowbreak{}density pure-\allowbreak{}central-\allowbreak{}private minimax absolute risk}
\end{itemize}

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:dimension]\label{ass:dimension}
\(d\ge 1\) is fixed.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Fixed-dimensional Hölder model in Theorem 1, \cite{kennedy2024minimax}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:interior-point]\label{ass:interior-point}
\(x_0\in[1/4,3/4]^d\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Fixed interior point, specialized from Theorem 1 to a compact interior cube, \cite{kennedy2024minimax}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:low-nuisance]\label{ass:low-nuisance}
\(q<\gamma\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} The ordering \(\alpha+\beta<\gamma\) deliberately isolates the coupled-nuisance privacy branch. It is compatible with settings where a simple treatment-effect surface is substantially smoother than treatment selection and baseline outcome response, including the audit class \(\alpha=\beta=1/8\) and \(\gamma=1\).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:iid]\label{ass:iid}
\(O_1,\ldots,O_n\) are independent draws from the observed-data marginal induced by \(P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Independent observed-record sampling in Section 1, \cite{kennedy2024minimax}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:consistency]\label{ass:consistency}
\(Y=AY_1+(1-A)Y_0\) almost surely.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Consistency in Section 1, \cite{kennedy2024minimax}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:exchangeability]\label{ass:exchangeability}
\(Y_a\perp A\mid X\) for each \(a\in\{0,1\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Armwise conditional exchangeability in Section 1, \cite{kennedy2024minimax}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:uniform-design]\label{ass:uniform-design}
\(P_X=\operatorname{Unif}([0,1]^d)\) is known.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Known exact uniformity is a deliberate load-bearing restriction, directly applicable to randomized benchmark experiments whose covariate locations are generated by a public uniform randomization. A deterministic uniform grid is a useful fixed-design analogue but is not covered by the iid theorem. The restriction is strictly narrower than the unknown bounded-density random-design model in Kennedy et al. and makes both projection geometry and microcell occupancy probabilities public.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:overlap]\label{ass:overlap}
\(e_0\le\pi(x)\le 1-e_0\) for every \(x\in[0,1]^d\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform positivity in Section 1 and Proposition S5, \cite{kennedy2024minimax}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:propensity-holder]\label{ass:propensity-holder}
\(\pi\in\mathcal H^\alpha(L)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Hölder propensity smoothness in Theorem 1, \cite{kennedy2024minimax}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:arm-holder]\label{ass:arm-holder}
\(\mu_0\in\mathcal H^\beta(L)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Hölder control-regression smoothness in Theorem 1, \cite{kennedy2024minimax}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:effect-holder]\label{ass:effect-holder}
\(\tau\in\mathcal H^\gamma(L)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Hölder CATE smoothness in Theorem 1, \cite{kennedy2024minimax}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:privacy-range]\label{ass:privacy-range}
\(n^{-1}\le\epsilon_n\le 1\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This chosen pure-central asymptotic sequence class includes constant privacy budgets and polynomially decaying budgets down to \(n^{-1}\), regimes commonly used to separate sampling and privacy effects without importing a distributed-privacy model.
\end{assumption}

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:causal-identification-and-bounds]\label{lem:causal-identification-and-bounds}
On the bounded observed-record domain \(\mathcal O\), consistency, armwise conditional exchangeability, and overlap imply \(\mu_a(x)=\mathbb E_P[Y_a\mid X=x]\) for \(a\in\{0,1\}\) and \(P_X\)-almost every \(x\). Hence \(\tau(x)=\mathbb E_P[Y_1-Y_0\mid X=x]\), \(m=\mu_0+\pi\tau\), \(\lvert Y_a\rvert\le1\) almost surely, \(\lvert\mu_a\rvert\le1\), \(\lvert m\rvert\le1\), and \(\lvert\tau\rvert\le2\). Operationally, the released observed-data factors obey \(\lvert B_l(X)A Y\rvert\le K_B\), \(\lVert F_{lj}(O)\rVert_2\le K_BK_\Phi\), and \(\lVert M_j(O)\rVert_2\le K_\Phi\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(a\in\{0,1\}\).  By consistency, on \(\{A=a\}\) one has \(Y=Y_a\).  By armwise conditional exchangeability, for \(P_X\)-almost every \(x\) and every Borel set \(E\),
\[
 P(Y_a\in E\mid A=a,X=x)=P(Y_a\in E\mid X=x).
\]
Overlap makes the conditioning event non-null because \(P(A=a\mid X=x)\ge e_0\).  Therefore
\[
 \mu_a(x)=\mathbb E[Y\mid A=a,X=x]
 =\mathbb E[Y_a\mid A=a,X=x]
 =\mathbb E[Y_a\mid X=x]
\]
for \(P_X\)-almost every \(x\).  Applying the same argument to \(E=\mathbb R\setminus[-1,1]\), and using that the observed record belongs to \(\mathcal O\), gives
\[
 P(|Y_a|>1\mid X=x)=P(|Y|>1\mid A=a,X=x)=0.
\]
Integrating over \(P_X\) proves \(|Y_a|\le1\) almost surely.  Conditional Jensen then gives \(|\mu_a(x)|\le1\), while iterated expectation gives
\[
 m(x)=\pi(x)\mu_1(x)+(1-\pi(x))\mu_0(x)
 =\mu_0(x)+\pi(x)\tau(x).
\]
Thus \(|m(x)|\le1\), \(|\tau(x)|=|\mu_1(x)-\mu_0(x)|\le2\), and subtraction of the two identified arm means identifies \(\tau(x)=\mathbb E[Y_1-Y_0\mid X=x]\), all for \(P_X\)-almost every \(x\).  Finally, the definitions of \(K_B\) and \(K_\Phi\), together with \(|A|\le1\) and \(|Y|\le1\), imply
\[
 |B_l(X)AY|\le K_B,
 \qquad \|F_{lj}(O)\|_2\le K_BK_\Phi,
 \qquad \|M_j(O)\|_2\le K_\Phi.
\]
These are bounds on every record in the operational privacy domain, not merely almost-sure bounds under one member of the model.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This separates the operational privacy support restriction on observed records from its causal identification and bounded-kernel consequences. \textit{Gap.} Schröder, Melnychuk, and Feuerriegel assume a bounded observed outcome domain for private CATE learning; the present lemma records exactly how that observed-data restriction propagates through the causal model and this query. \textit{Consumer.} It licenses the complete sensitivity ledger without assuming bounded unobserved potential outcomes as a primitive condition.

\begin{lemma}[lem:projection-product-identity]\label{lem:projection-product-identity}
Let \(t_\gamma(x)=B(x)^\top\vartheta\) be the degree-\(\lceil\gamma\rceil-1\) Taylor polynomial of \(\tau\) at \(x_0\), and let \(r=\tau-t_\gamma\). Then \(W_{lr}=\langle B_lB_r,\pi(1-\pi)\rangle_h+\langle Q_{h,\ell}(B_l\pi),Q_{h,\ell}(B_r\pi)\rangle_h\), and \((V-W\vartheta)_l=\langle Q_{h,\ell}(B_l\pi),Q_{h,\ell}\mu_0\rangle_h+\langle B_l\pi,r\rangle_h-\langle P_{h,\ell}(B_l\pi),P_{h,\ell}(\pi r)\rangle_h\). Consequently \(\lambda_{\min}(W)\ge c>0\) and \(|e_1^\top W^{-1}V-\tau(x_0)|\le C(h^\gamma+\ell^q)\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write \(\langle f,g\rangle_h=H^{-1}\int_{Q_h}fg\), put \(P=P_{h,\ell}\), \(Q=I-P\), and set \(f_l=B_l\pi\).  Exact uniform design and the definitions of the conditional moments give
\[
 H^{-1}\mathbb E[\mathbf 1\{X\in Q_h\}B_lB_rA]
 =\langle B_lB_r,\pi\rangle_h.
\]
Since \(P\) is an orthogonal projection,
\[
 \langle Pf_l,Pf_r\rangle_h
 =\langle f_l,f_r\rangle_h-\langle Qf_l,Qf_r\rangle_h.
\]
As \(\langle f_l,f_r\rangle_h=\langle B_lB_r,\pi^2\rangle_h\), subtraction yields the exact identity
\[
 W_{lr}=\langle B_lB_r,\pi(1-\pi)\rangle_h
       +\langle Qf_l,Qf_r\rangle_h.                                      \tag{1}
\]
For any \(u\in\mathbb R^p\), with \(g_u=B^\top u\), overlap and (1) imply
\[
 u^\top Wu\ge e_0(1-e_0)\langle g_u,g_u\rangle_h
 =e_0(1-e_0)u^\top G_Bu.
\]
The fixed scaled-monomial Gram matrix \(G_B\) is positive definite: a polynomial having zero squared integral on a cube is the zero polynomial, hence has all coefficients zero.  Thus \(\lambda_{\min}(W)\ge e_0(1-e_0)\lambda_{\min}(G_B)>0\).  Moreover, from the original definition of \(W\),
\(0\preceq W\preceq G_B\), so its upper spectral norm is also bounded by a class constant.

Next, conditional identification gives
\[
 \mathbb E[AY\mid X]=\pi\mu_1=\pi m+\pi(1-\pi)\tau.
\]
Therefore
\[
 V_l=\langle Qf_l,Qm\rangle_h+\langle B_l\pi(1-\pi),\tau\rangle_h.       \tag{2}
\]
Let \(t_\gamma=B^\top\vartheta\) and \(r=\tau-t_\gamma\).  Multiplying (1) by \(\vartheta_r\), summing over \(r\), using \(m=\mu_0+\pi\tau\), and subtracting gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 (V-W\vartheta)_l
 &=\langle Qf_l,Q\mu_0\rangle_h
   +\langle Qf_l,Q(\pi r)\rangle_h
   +\langle B_l\pi(1-\pi),r\rangle_h\\
 &=\langle Qf_l,Q\mu_0\rangle_h
   +\langle B_l\pi,r\rangle_h
   -\langle Pf_l,P(\pi r)\rangle_h.                                    \tag{3}
 \end{aligned}
\]
The second equality follows from \(\langle Qf_l,Q(\pi r)\rangle_h=\langle f_l,\pi r\rangle_h-\langle Pf_l,P(\pi r)\rangle_h\).

On each microcube, multiply the degree-\((\lceil\alpha\rceil-1)\) Taylor polynomial of \(\pi\) by the exact polynomial \(B_l\).  Its degree is at most \(J\), and its uniform error is at most \(CK_B\ell^\alpha\).  The best-approximation property of \(P\) consequently gives
\[
 \|Q(B_l\pi)\|_{2,h}\le C\ell^\alpha.
\]
The analogous cellwise Taylor approximation for \(\mu_0\) gives \(\|Q\mu_0\|_{2,h}\le C\ell^\beta\).  The degree choice in the definition of \(J\) is exactly what licenses both polynomial inclusions.  Hölder smoothness of \(\tau\), and the fact that \(Q_h\) has diameter of order \(h\), give
\[
 \|r\|_\infty\le Ch^\gamma,
 \qquad t_\gamma(x_0)=\tau(x_0).
\]
Projection contraction, Cauchy--Schwarz, boundedness of \(B_l\) and \(\pi\), and (3) now yield
\[
 \|V-W\vartheta\|_2\le C(\ell^{\alpha+\beta}+h^\gamma)
 =C(\ell^q+h^\gamma).
\]
Since \(p\) is fixed and \(\|W^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le C\), while \(e_1^\top\vartheta=t_\gamma(x_0)=\tau(x_0)\),
\[
 |e_1^\top W^{-1}V-\tau(x_0)|
 =|e_1^\top W^{-1}(V-W\vartheta)|
 \le C(h^\gamma+\ell^q).
\]
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The identity converts two rough nuisance approximations into their product while preserving the smoother CATE localization bias. \textit{Gap.} Kennedy et al. derive a localized higher-order product bias with nuisance pilots; the exact fixed-uniform projection identity without pilots is not in their construction. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies the bias calculation for a single protected higher-order release rather than separately privatized nuisance fits.

\begin{lemma}[lem:pair-expectation-sensitivity]\label{lem:pair-expectation-sensitivity}
Suppose \(n\ge2\) and \(n\ell^d\le c_0\). For every bounded pair of cell features, \(\mathbb E[U_j(f,g)]=p_2\,\mathbb E[f(O)\mid X\in C_j]^\top\mathbb E[g(O)\mid X\in C_j]\), so \(q^V\) and \(q^W\) are exactly unbiased for the two projection-product blocks in \(V\) and \(W\). If \(K_B=\max_l\lVert B_l\rVert_\infty\) and \(K_\Phi^2=\sup_{j,x\in C_j}\lVert\Phi_j(x)\rVert_2^2\), replacement changes the complete first-order block by at most \(C_1/(nH)\), where \(C_1=2pK_B+p(p+1)K_B^2\). At most two cells change, every normalized \(U_j\) is bounded, and replacement changes the complete pair block by at most \(C_2/(kp_2)\), where \(C_2=4pK_BK_\Phi^2+2p(p+1)K_B^2K_\Phi^2\). Hence \(\Delta_1(S_{h,\ell})\le C_1/(nH)+C_2/(kp_2)\le C/(n^2H\ell^d)\), using \(p_2\asymp n^2\ell^{2d}\), \(k=H/\ell^d\), and \(n\ell^d\le c_0\). Each pair coordinate has variance at most \(C/(n^2H\ell^d)\). Counts, symmetric duplication, spectral projection, inversion, intercept extraction, and final clipping are unreleased postprocessing and contribute no sensitivity.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(v=\ell^d\).  By exact uniform design, \(N_j\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,v)\).  Conditional on \(N_j=r\), the \(r\) records in \(C_j\) are independent draws from the cell-conditional law.  Hence, for \(r\ge2\), ordered-pair independence gives
\[
 \mathbb E[U_j(f,g)\mid N_j=r]
 =\mathbb E[f(O)\mid X\in C_j]^\top
  \mathbb E[g(O)\mid X\in C_j].
\]
Multiplication by \(P(N_j\ge2)=p_2\) proves the claimed expectation.  Orthonormality of \(\Phi_j\) under \(v^{-1}dx\) shows, for example,
\[
 \frac1k\sum_{j=1}^k
 \mathbb E[F_{lj}(O)\mid X\in C_j]^\top
 \mathbb E[M_j(O)\mid X\in C_j]
 =\langle P_{h,\ell}(B_l\pi),P_{h,\ell}m\rangle_h,
\]
and the same calculation with \(M_j\) replaced by \(F_{rj}\) gives the Gram projection product.  Thus division by \(p_2\) makes both pair blocks exactly unbiased; there is no occupancy-normalization bias.

For \(nv\le c_0\), the event that exactly two observations enter the cell gives
\[
 p_2\ge {n\choose2}v^2(1-v)^{n-2}\ge c n^2v^2,
\]
where the final constant is positive because \((1-v)^{n-2}\ge\exp(-Cnv)\).  The union bound over ordered pairs gives \(p_2\le {n\choose2}v^2\le Cn^2v^2\).  Thus \(p_2\asymp n^2v^2\), uniformly under the stated cap.

For a replacement, each first-order coordinate changes by at most twice its summand envelope.  Summing over all released coordinates gives
\[
 \|\Delta s\|_1\le
 \frac{2pK_B+p(p+1)K_B^2}{nH}=\frac{C_1}{nH}.             \tag{4}
\]
Every \(U_j(f,g)\) is an average of numbers bounded by the product of the two feature envelopes.  A replacement affects only its origin and destination cells, even when a count crosses \(2\).  The bounds proved in the causal lemma therefore give
\[
 \|\Delta q\|_1\le
 \frac{4pK_BK_\Phi^2+2p(p+1)K_B^2K_\Phi^2}{kp_2}
 =\frac{C_2}{kp_2}.                                      \tag{5}
\]
Because \(k=H/v\), (4)--(5), \(p_2\asymp n^2v^2\), and \(nv\le c_0\le1\) imply
\[
 \Delta_1(S_{h,\ell})
 \le \frac{C_1}{nH}+\frac{C_2}{kp_2}
 \le \frac{C}{n^2Hv}
 =\frac{C}{n^2H\ell^d}.                                 \tag{6}
\]
This is a supremum over all bounded datasets; no random occupancy event has been inserted into global sensitivity.

For completeness, let \(Q(D_n)=(kp_2)^{-1}\sum_jU_j(f,g)\) be one pair coordinate and let \(D_n^{(i)}\) replace record \(i\) by an independent copy.  The difference is zero unless the old or new record lies in \(Q_h\) and has another observation in its cell.  A union bound and exact uniformity give this event probability at most \(CHnv\), while on the event \(|Q(D_n)-Q(D_n^{(i)})|\le C/(kp_2)\).  The elementary leave-one-out variance inequality, obtained by successively conditioning on the independent records, gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(Q)\le\frac12\sum_{i=1}^n
 \mathbb E\{Q(D_n)-Q(D_n^{(i)})\}^2
 \le \frac{CnHnv}{(kp_2)^2}
 \le \frac{C}{n^2Hv}.                                   \tag{7}
\]
Finally, counts are used only internally to evaluate the fixed query.  Symmetric duplication, spectral projection, inversion, intercept extraction, and clipping are deterministic postprocessing of the one release, so none adds to (6).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the early kill test: it protects counts, normalization, first-order moments, and every pair correction on every bounded dataset. \textit{Gap.} An ordinary all-pairs statistic has sensitivity of order \(1/(nh^d\ell^d)\); Chaudhuri et al. require projection-aware stabilization, whereas the exact design makes a different normalization available here. \textit{Consumer.} The lemma removes occupancy logarithms and unprotected nuisance pilots from the private CATE estimator.

\begin{theorem}[thm:private-upper-envelope]\label{thm:private-upper-envelope}
On the known exact-uniform class \(\mathcal M_H\), for \(n^{-1}\le\epsilon_n\le1\), the displayed unique joint-release mechanism admits a deterministic finite-grid choice of \((h,\ell)\), depending only on public parameters and using a fixed tie-break, for which \(T_{\mathrm{priv}}(h,\ell)\) is \(\epsilon_n\)-differentially private under replacement adjacency and \(\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_H}\mathbb E_P|T_{\mathrm{priv}}(h,\ell)-\tau_P(x_0)|\le C\max\{b_n,\rho_n^{\mathrm{priv}}\}\). Before optimization its risk is bounded by \(C\{h^\gamma+\ell^q+(nh^d)^{-1/2}+(n^2h^d\ell^d)^{-1/2}+(n^2\epsilon_nh^d\ell^d)^{-1}\}\). No conclusion is asserted for an unknown bounded design density or for \(\epsilon_n<n^{-1}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For adjacent datasets \(D,D'\), the joint Laplace density at output \(z\) has likelihood ratio bounded by
\[
 \exp\left\{\frac{\epsilon_n}{\Delta_1(S_{h,\ell})}
 \bigl(\|z-S_{h,\ell}(D')\|_1-\|z-S_{h,\ell}(D)\|_1\bigr)\right\}
 \le \exp(\epsilon_n).
\]
Thus the single vector release is pure \(\epsilon_n\)-differentially private.  All remaining operations in the definition of \(T_{\mathrm{priv}}\) are postprocessing.

Let \(\widehat V=s^V-q^V\) and define \(\widehat W\) analogously before privacy noise.  Exact unbiasedness and the variance bounds for the first- and second-order blocks imply, because the released dimension is fixed,
\[
 \mathbb E\|\widehat V-V\|_2+
 \mathbb E\|\widehat W-W\|_F
 \le C\left\{(nH)^{-1/2}+(n^2H\ell^d)^{-1/2}\right\}.    \tag{8}
\]
Equation (6) and the mean absolute deviation of a Laplace variable give
\[
 \mathbb E\|\bar V-\widehat V\|_2+
 \mathbb E\|\bar W-\widehat W\|_F
 \le \frac{C}{n^2\epsilon_nH\ell^d}.                    \tag{9}
\]
The population matrix satisfies \(2\lambda_-I_p\preceq W\preceq G_B\preceq(\lambda_+/2)I_p\).  Since \(W\) belongs to the clipping set, the defining variational inequality for Euclidean projection gives
\(\|\widetilde W-W\|_F\le\|\bar W-W\|_F\), and clipping gives \(\|\widetilde W^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\lambda_-^{-1}\).  The inverse identity yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |e_1^\top\widetilde W^{-1}\bar V-e_1^\top W^{-1}V|
 &\le \lambda_-^{-1}\|\bar V-V\|_2\\
 &\quad+\lambda_-^{-1}\|W^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|V\|_2
       \|\widetilde W-W\|_F.
 \end{aligned}                                           \tag{10}
\]
All displayed matrix and vector envelopes are class constants.  Final clipping cannot increase distance to \(\tau(x_0)\in[-2,2]\).  Combining (8)--(10) with the projection-product bias lemma and \(H=h^d\) proves the pre-optimization bound
\[
 C\left\{h^\gamma+\ell^q+(nh^d)^{-1/2}
 +(n^2h^d\ell^d)^{-1/2}
 +(n^2\epsilon_nh^d\ell^d)^{-1}\right\}.                \tag{11}
\]

It remains to exhibit a public tuning pair.  Let
\[
 \eta=\max\{b_n,r_G,r_F\}.
\]
Choose, up to fixed multiplicative constants and downward dyadic rounding,
\[
 h=\eta^{1/\gamma},
 \qquad \ell^d=\min\{\eta^{d/q},c_0/(2n)\}.              \tag{12}
\]
Because \(q<\gamma\) and \(0<\eta\le1\), (12) satisfies \(\ell\le h\), and it satisfies the occupancy cap.  A finite dyadic grid of depth \(C\log(n+1)\), with fixed tie-breaking, contains a pair within a factor two of (12); such rounding changes only constants.

The two bias terms in (11) are at most \(C\eta\).  Since \(\eta\ge n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}\), the first-order deviation is at most \(C\eta\).  If the first argument in the minimum in (12) is active, then
\[
 (n^2h^d\ell^d)^{-1/2}le C\eta
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \eta\ge n^{-2/(2+d/\gamma+d/q)}=n^{-\kappa},
\]
and
\[
 (n^2\epsilon_nh^d\ell^d)^{-1}\le C\eta
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \eta\ge r_F.
\]
If the occupancy cap is active, those two terms reduce, up to constants, to \((nh^d)^{-1/2}\) and \((n\epsilon_nh^d)^{-1}\), respectively; they are bounded by \(C\eta\) because \(\eta\ge n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}\) and \(\eta\ge r_G\).  Substitution in (11) proves
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M_H}\mathbb E_P|T_{\mathrm{priv}}-\tau_P(x_0)|
 \le C\max\{b_n,\rho_n^{\mathrm{priv}}\}.
\]
For fixed small \(n\), the zero or clipped estimator and an enlargement of \(C\) give the same statement.  Every choice in (12) depends only on public class parameters, \(n\), and \(\epsilon_n\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} A total one-shot release is the constructive half of the incremental privacy frontier. \textit{Gap.} Niu et al. prove privacy for modular meta-learners but no Hölder minimax utility bound; Chaudhuri et al. treat generic U-statistics rather than localized causal moments. \textit{Consumer.} It gives private heterogeneous-effect learners a sharp benchmark in privacy-leading regimes and a fully auditable sensitivity ledger.

\begin{lemma}[lem:fuzzy-membership-singletons]\label{lem:fuzzy-membership-singletons}
For sufficiently small fixed amplitude constants and scales satisfying \(ab\le c h^\gamma\), every \(P_{s,\lambda}\) belongs to \(\mathcal M_H\), its observed cell probabilities are uniformly positive, \(\tau_s(x_0)=-4sab+O(a^3b)\), and the one-record mixtures coincide exactly: \(\mathbb E_\lambda[P_{+,\lambda}]=\mathbb E_\lambda[P_{-,\lambda}]\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We prove the narrowed statement, including the necessary scale condition.  The smooth frame has bounded overlap and scale-\(\ell\) derivative bounds, so uniformly over all sign vectors
\[
 \|F_\lambda\|_\infty\le C,
 \qquad \|F_\lambda\|_{\mathcal H^r}\le C_r\ell^{-r},
 \qquad \mathbb E_\lambda F_\lambda=0,
 \qquad \mathbb E_\lambda F_\lambda^2=K
\]
on the support of \(\zeta_h\).  With \(a=c_a\ell^\alpha\), \(b=c_b\ell^\beta\), sufficiently small fixed \(c_a,c_b\) give \(e_0\le\pi_\lambda\le1-e_0\) and
\[
 D_{\mathrm{fuz}}=\tfrac14-a^2K\ge\tfrac18.
\]
The scale bounds imply \(\|aF_\lambda\|_{\mathcal H^\alpha}\le C\) and \(\|bF_\lambda\|_{\mathcal H^\beta}\le C\).  Since \(K\) is a scale-\(h\) smooth cutoff and its denominator is bounded away from zero, the ordinary product and reciprocal difference quotients give
\[
 \|\tau_s\|_{\mathcal H^\gamma}\le Cabh^{-\gamma}\le C
\]
under \(ab\le c h^\gamma\).  Moreover, \(\gamma>q>\beta\), so the \(\gamma\)-smooth part is also uniformly \(\beta\)-smooth.  For the only potentially rough product in \(\mu_{0,s,\lambda}=m_{s,\lambda}-\pi_\lambda\tau_s\),
\[
 \|aF_\lambda\tau_s\|_{\mathcal H^\beta}
 \le C\{a\|\tau_s\|_{\mathcal H^\beta}
          +a\ell^{-\beta}\|\tau_s\|_\infty\}
 \le C\{a+a^2b\ell^{-\beta}\}
 =C\{a+\ell^{2\alpha}\}.
\]
Thus \(\mu_0\in\mathcal H^\beta(L)\) after decreasing the fixed amplitudes; the same calculation bounds the displayed treated-arm mean.  Small amplitudes also put both arm means in a fixed subinterval of \((0,1)\).  Drawing independent Bernoulli potential outcomes with these arm means conditional on \(X\), before drawing treatment, supplies consistency and exchangeability.  Exact uniformity is built into the family.  Hence every component law is in \(\mathcal M_H\), and every conditional \((A,Y)\) cell has probability bounded below by a fixed positive constant.

At \(x_0\), \(K(x_0)=1\), and therefore
\[
 \tau_s(x_0)=-\frac{sab}{1/4-a^2}=-4sab+O(a^3b).
\]
It remains to prove singleton cancellation.  Put \(p=m_0=1/2\).  The four binary mark probabilities are determined by \(\pi_\lambda\), the marginal mean \(m_{s,\lambda}\), and
\[
 P(A=1,Y=1\mid X=x)
 =\pi_\lambda m_{s,\lambda}
   +\pi_\lambda(1-\pi_\lambda)\tau_s.
\]
Using the two frame identities above gives
\[
 \mathbb E_\lambda\pi_\lambda=p,
 \qquad \mathbb E_\lambda m_{s,\lambda}=m_0,
\]
and
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_\lambda\{\pi_\lambda m_{s,\lambda}
 +\pi_\lambda(1-\pi_\lambda)\tau_s\}
 &=pm_0+sabK+(p(1-p)-a^2K)\tau_s\\
 &=pm_0.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The analogous identity for \(P(A=0,Y=1\mid X=x)\) follows by subtraction from \(m_{s,\lambda}\).  Hence all four one-record mark probabilities, and the common uniform covariate law, are independent of \(s\) after averaging over \(\lambda\).  This proves exact equality of the one-record mixtures.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The legal witness must combine target separation, exact-uniform positive design, Hölder membership, and singleton cancellation. \textit{Gap.} Kennedy et al.'s fuzzy family uses a support geometry tailored to nonprivate Hellinger affinity; the banked private microcell witness failed CATE smoothness when \(q<\gamma\). \textit{Consumer.} It supplies a same-class causal hypothesis family for a privacy lower bound without support holes.

\begin{lemma}[lem:component-hamming-transport]\label{lem:component-hamming-transport}
Conditioning on the covariates and coupling latent-sign connected components independently yields \(W_H(\Pi_+^n,\Pi_-^n)\le Cabn^2h^d\ell^d\) whenever \(n\ell^d\le c_0\). More precisely, singleton components have zero cost, a conditional size-\(r\) component has total variation at most \(Cabr^2C_*^r\) for a fixed \(C_*<\infty\), and multiplying its maximal-coupling Hamming cost by the spanning-tree bound on the probability of covariate connectivity gives a series convergent uniformly in \(n\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We prove the corrected conditional-component statement.  Condition on \(X_1,\ldots,X_n\) and join two indices when their active frame functions involve a common latent sign.  Distinct connected components involve disjoint sign sets, so averaging the independent Rademacher signs factorizes the conditional mark law across components.  A singleton component has identical laws for \(s=+1\) and \(s=-1\) by the singleton identity.

Consider a component of size \(r\ge2\), and temporarily replace \((a,b)\) by amplitudes \((u,v)\).  For a mark vector \(z\in\{0,1\}^{2r}\), let \(f_s(z;u,v)\) be its sign-mixed conditional mass.  When \(v=0\), the outer sign disappears, so \(f_+=f_-\).  When \(u=0\), the transformation \(\lambda\mapsto-\lambda\) changes the law with outer sign \(+1\) to that with outer sign \(-1\), so equality again holds.  The conditional cells stay in a compact subinterval of \((0,1)\) throughout the amplitude rectangle.  Differentiating the product of the \(r\) one-record masses therefore gives, after summing over all marks,
\[
 \sum_z\left|\partial_u\partial_v(f_+-f_-)(z;u,v)\right|
 \le Cr^2C_*^r,                                           \tag{13}
\]
where \(C_*\) is fixed.  Indeed, the product rule contains \(r\) same-factor mixed derivatives and at most \(r(r-1)\) products of two first derivatives; bounded cell probabilities and bounded derivative-to-probability ratios bound each resulting mark sum.  Applying the fundamental theorem of calculus first in \(u\) and then in \(v\) yields
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(f_+(\cdot;a,b),f_-(\cdot;a,b))
 \le Cabr^2C_*^r.                                        \tag{14}
\]
On the finite mark space, matching the common mass \(\min(f_+,f_-)\) constructs a maximal coupling.  It changes no records on success and at most \(r\) on failure, so its expected Hamming cost is at most \(Cabr^3C_*^r\).

It remains to average connectivity over the covariates.  A connected labeled \(r\)-set admits a spanning tree.  The root must be in a set of volume at most \(Ch^d\), and, conditional on its parent, every child joined by a tree edge lies in a set of volume at most \(C\ell^d\).  Successively deleting the smallest labeled leaf records one neighboring label at each of \(r-2\) steps; the usual reverse insertion reconstructs the tree.  Thus the number of labeled trees is \(r^{r-2}\).  Consequently, if \(N_r\) is the number of size-\(r\) components,
\[
 \mathbb E N_r\le C{n\choose r}h^d r^{r-2}(C\ell^d)^{r-1}. \tag{15}
\]
Coupling components independently and using (14)--(15) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 W_H(\Pi_+^n,\Pi_-^n)
 &\le Cab\sum_{r\ge2}r^3C_*^r\mathbb E N_r\\
 &\le Cabn^2h^d\ell^d
 \sum_{r\ge2}\frac{r^{r+1}}{r!}
       (C_*'n\ell^d)^{r-2}\\
 &\le Cabn^2h^d\ell^d.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The last series converges uniformly after choosing the fixed cap \(c_0\) small enough, since \(r^{r+1}/r!\le C r^{3/2}e^r\).  Thus every component size is included.  The spanning-tree probabilities enter through (15), after the conditional total-variation estimate, which is the mathematically valid factorization.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Expected Hamming transport, rather than one-law total variation, is the missing object needed by central-private composite testing. \textit{Gap.} Acharya et al. and Lalanne et al. provide general coupling-to-testing reductions but no coupling for a smooth shared-prior CATE mixture. \textit{Consumer.} It permits a nuisance-sensitive private converse beyond the two-point total-variation barrier.

\begin{theorem}[thm:private-lower-envelope]\label{thm:private-lower-envelope}
On the known exact-uniform class \(\mathcal M_H\), for \(n^{-1}\le\epsilon_n\le1\), every \(\epsilon_n\)-differentially private estimator satisfies \(R_n^{\mathrm{DP}}\ge c\max\{R_n^{\mathrm{NP}},r_G,r_F\}=c\max\{R_n^{\mathrm{NP}},\rho_n^{\mathrm{priv}}\}\). The \(r_F\) branch follows by setting \(ab\asymp r_F\), \(h\asymp r_F^{1/\gamma}\), and \(\ell\asymp r_F^{1/q}\) in the component coupling before applying a central-private two-prior testing inequality.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First, randomization and privacy cannot improve the nonprivate minimax risk.  Fix an \(\epsilon_n\)-differentially private mechanism with output \(T\in[-2,2]\), and define the deterministic measurable map
\[
 \overline T(D)=\mathbb E[T\mid D].
\]
For every \(P\in\mathcal M_H\), conditional Jensen's inequality gives, dataset by dataset,
\[
 \bigl|\overline T(D)-\tau_P(x_0)\bigr|
 \le \mathbb E\!\left[\left.|T-\tau_P(x_0)|\,\right|D\right].                 \tag{16}
\]
Taking expectation under \(P^{\otimes n}\), then the supremum over \(P\in\mathcal M_H\), and finally the infimum over private mechanisms proves
\[
 R_n^{\mathrm{DP}}\ge R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}.                                      \tag{17}
\]

We next derive the private testing inequality used for both privacy branches.  Write \(M_D\) for the output law conditional on dataset \(D\), and, for an input law \(Q\), write \(MQ=\int M_D\,Q(dD)\) for the induced output law.  If \(D\sim_1D'\), pure differential privacy in both adjacency directions implies, for every measurable event \(E\),
\[
 M_D(E)-M_{D'}(E)\le (e^{\epsilon_n}-1)M_{D'}(E)\le e^{\epsilon_n}-1.
\]
The same inequality with \(D,D'\) interchanged and the variational characterization of total variation therefore yield
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(M_D,M_{D'})\le e^{\epsilon_n}-1\le2\epsilon_n,             \tag{18}
\]
where the last inequality uses \(\epsilon_n\le1\) from \(\text{ass:privacy-range}\).  Join arbitrary datasets by a shortest replacement path.  The triangle inequality and (18) give
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(M_D,M_{D'})\le2\epsilon_n d_{\mathrm H}(D,D').
\]
Consequently, for any input laws \(Q_+,Q_-\), any coupling \(\Gamma\) of them, and any output event \(E\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \left|(MQ_+)(E)-(MQ_-)(E)\right|
 &=\left|\int\{M_D(E)-M_{D'}(E)\}\,d\Gamma(D,D')\right|\\
 &\le2\epsilon_n\int d_{\mathrm H}(D,D')\,d\Gamma(D,D').
\end{aligned}
\]
Taking the supremum over \(E\) gives
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(MQ_+,MQ_-)
 \le2\epsilon_n\mathbb E_\Gamma d_{\mathrm H}(D,D').                         \tag{19}
\]
Suppose now that every law in the support of \(Q_s\) has target value \(\vartheta_s\), with \(\delta_0=|\vartheta_+-\vartheta_-|\), and define its Bayes absolute risk by \(\mathcal R_s(M)=\mathbb E_{Q_s,M}|T-\vartheta_s|\).  Classify an output according to the nearer of \(\vartheta_+\) and \(\vartheta_-\), resolving ties deterministically, and let \(E\) be the event that the classifier selects \(+\).  A classification error under sign \(s\) implies \(|T-\vartheta_s|\ge\delta_0/2\), while
\[
 (MQ_+)(E^c)+(MQ_-)(E)
 =1-\{(MQ_+)(E)-(MQ_-)(E)\}
 \ge1-\operatorname{TV}(MQ_+,MQ_-).
\]
Hence
\[
 \mathcal R_+(M)+\mathcal R_-(M)
 \ge \frac{\delta_0}{2}
       \{1-\operatorname{TV}(MQ_+,MQ_-)\}.                                  \tag{20}
\]

For the direct localization branch, fix a nonnegative \(C^\infty\) function \(g_0\), supported in \([-1/2,1/2]^d\), bounded by one, and satisfying \(g_0(0)=1\), and put \(g_h(x)=g_0((x-x_0)/h)\).  By \(\text{ass:interior-point}\), its support lies in \([0,1]^d\) whenever \(h\le1/4\).  For \(s\in\{-1,1\}\), construct a causal law by taking \(X\) uniform and, conditionally on \(X\), drawing \(A\), \(Y_0\), and \(Y_1\) independently with \(A\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\) and
\[
 Y_0\mid X\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2),
 \qquad
 Y_1\mid X\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2+s\Delta g_h(X)),
\]
followed by \(Y=AY_1+(1-A)Y_0\).  For \(\Delta\le1/4\), the observed outcome is in \(\{0,1\}\subset[-1,1]\), overlap holds because \(e_0\le1/4\), and consistency and conditional exchangeability hold by construction.  The propensity and control regression are constant, while
\[
 \tau_s(x)=s\Delta g_h(x).
\]
Scale differentiation gives \(\|\Delta g_h\|_{\mathcal H^\gamma}\le C\Delta h^{-\gamma}\).  Thus, after choosing a class-dependent constant small enough, \(\Delta\le c h^\gamma\) implies that both laws belong to \(\mathcal M_H\); all other defining restrictions are the frozen assumptions in \(\text{def:holder-class}\).  Their targets are separated by \(2\Delta\).

Denote these laws by \(P_+\) and \(P_-\), and set \(Q_s=P_s^{\otimes n}\).  Couple the two one-record laws by using the same \(X\), \(A\), and a common uniform random variable to generate the relevant Bernoulli outcome.  Conditional on \(X=x\), disagreement can occur only when \(A=1\), and its probability is \(\Delta g_h(x)\).  Uniform design and the support of \(g_h\) therefore give
\[
 \mathbb P(O_+\ne O_-)
 =\Delta\int_{[0,1]^d}g_h(x)\,dx
 \le C\Delta h^d.                                                               \tag{21}
\]
Applying this coupling independently to the \(n\) records gives expected Hamming distance at most \(Cn\Delta h^d\).  Since \(n\epsilon_n\ge1\), the truncation in \(r_G\) is inactive.  Choose
\[
 \Delta=c_G r_G,
 \qquad h=c_h\Delta^{1/\gamma},                                                  \tag{22}
\]
with fixed constants satisfying the support and Hölder-radius restrictions above.  Equations (19)--(22) yield
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(MQ_+,MQ_-)
 \le Cn\epsilon_n\Delta^{1+d/\gamma}\le\tfrac12
\]
after decreasing \(c_G\), because \(n\epsilon_n r_G^{1+d/\gamma}=1\).  Equation (20), followed by the fact that a Bayes risk is at most the worst-case risk over its support, proves
\[
 R_n^{\mathrm{DP}}\ge c r_G.                                                     \tag{23}
\]

It remains to obtain the fuzzy branch.  If \(r_F\le C_0r_G\) for a fixed constant \(C_0\), (23) already implies \(R_n^{\mathrm{DP}}\ge c r_F\).  Otherwise put
\[
 \Delta=c_Fr_F,\qquad
 h=c_h\Delta^{1/\gamma},\qquad
 \ell=c_\ell\Delta^{1/q},\qquad
 a=c_a\ell^\alpha,\qquad b=c_b\ell^\beta.                  \tag{24}
\]
Because \(q=\alpha+\beta\), (24) gives \(ab\asymp\Delta\).  The fixed constants can be chosen so that \(ab\le c h^\gamma\).  The result \(\text{lem:fuzzy-membership-singletons}\) then shows that every component law \(P_{s,\lambda}\) belongs to \(\mathcal M_H\), with target independent of \(\lambda\).  Since \(n^2\epsilon_n\ge1\), the truncation in \(r_F\) is also inactive, and
\[
 n^2\epsilon_n r_F^{\,1+d/\gamma+d/q}=1.                                        \tag{25}
\]
Moreover, \(r_F>C_0r_G\), with \(C_0\) chosen large enough to absorb fixed scale constants, implies
\[
 n\epsilon_n r_F^{\,1+d/\gamma}\ge c,
 \qquad
 n r_F^{d/q}=\{n\epsilon_n r_F^{\,1+d/\gamma}\}^{-1}\le C.                  \tag{26}
\]
After decreasing \(c_\ell\), (26) gives \(n\ell^d\le c_0\), so \(\text{lem:component-hamming-transport}\) applies.  Combining that lemma with (19) yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{TV}(M\Pi_+^n,M\Pi_-^n)
 &\le C\epsilon_nabn^2h^d\ell^d\\
 &\le Cn^2\epsilon_n\Delta^{\,1+d/\gamma+d/q}
 \le\tfrac12,                                                                  \tag{27}
 \end{aligned}
\]
where the last inequality follows from (25) after decreasing \(c_F\).  Since \(K(x_0)=1\), the definition of the fuzzy family gives the exact separation
\[
 \left|\tau_+(x_0)-\tau_-(x_0)\right|
 =\frac{2ab}{1/4-a^2}\asymp ab\asymp\Delta.                                  \tag{28}
\]
The denominator in (28) is positive because the fixed propensity amplitude was chosen smaller than \(1/2\), as required by the membership lemma.  Applying (20) to the shared-prior mixtures in (27), and then bounding each Bayes risk by the worst-case risk over its component laws, proves
\[
 R_n^{\mathrm{DP}}\ge c r_F.                                                     \tag{29}
\]
Together, (17), (23), and (29) imply, after taking the smallest of their fixed constants,
\[
 R_n^{\mathrm{DP}}
 \ge c\max\{R_n^{\mathrm{NP}},r_G,r_F\}
 =c\max\{R_n^{\mathrm{NP}},\rho_n^{\mathrm{priv}}\},
\]
where the final equality is \(\text{def:rates}\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The lower bound certifies that both privacy balances are intrinsic to the causal class. \textit{Gap.} Cai et al. establish the direct pointwise-regression privacy barrier, while no published result adds the coupled-nuisance \(n^2\epsilon_n\) branch for CATE. \textit{Consumer.} It prevents private CATE algorithms from being benchmarked only against ordinary private regression.

\begin{theorem}[thm:resolved-private-regimes]\label{thm:resolved-private-regimes}
On the known exact-uniform class \(\mathcal M_H\) and within \(n^{-1}\le\epsilon_n\le1\), the bounds match as \(R_n^{\mathrm{DP}}\asymp\max\{R_n^{\mathrm{NP}},\rho_n^{\mathrm{priv}}\}\) whenever either \(q\ge q_0\) or \(\rho_n^{\mathrm{priv}}\ge c b_n\). Under \(\epsilon_n=n^{-t}\), \(r_F=n^{-(2-t)/D_F}\) whenever the truncation at one is inactive, and the complete benchmark partition is as follows. If \(q\ge q_0\), privacy is hidden by \(b_n\) for \(0\le t\le\nu\), and \(r_G\) is the leading private branch for \(t>\nu\); at \(q=q_0\) and \(t=\nu\), \(b_n=r_F=r_G\). If \(q<q_0\), privacy is hidden by \(b_n\) for \(0\le t\le\kappa\), \(r_F\) is the leading private branch for \(\kappa<t<t_s\), \(r_F=r_G\) at \(t=t_s=1-q/d-q/\gamma\), and \(r_G\) is the leading private branch for \(t>t_s\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The private lower envelope and private upper envelope give
\[
 c\max\{R_n^{\mathrm{NP}},\rho_n^{\mathrm{priv}}\}
 \le R_n^{\mathrm{DP}}
 \le C\max\{b_n,\rho_n^{\mathrm{priv}}\}.                 \tag{19}
\]
If \(\rho_n^{\mathrm{priv}}\ge c_1b_n\), the right side of (19) is at most a constant times \(\rho_n^{\mathrm{priv}}\), so the bounds match.  If \(q\ge q_0=d\gamma/(2\gamma+d)\), then
\[
 \frac{d}{q}\le2+\frac d\gamma,
 \qquad
 \kappa=\frac{2}{2+d/\gamma+d/q}\ge\frac{\gamma}{2\gamma+d}=\nu.
\]
Thus \(b_n=n^{-\nu}\), and the direct nonprivate bump lemma gives \(b_n\le C R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\).  Equation (19) again matches, proving the first assertion.

Now write \(\epsilon_n=n^{-t}\).  The privacy range gives \(0\le t\le1\), and
\[
 r_G=n^{-(1-t)\gamma/(\gamma+d)},
 \qquad r_F=n^{-(2-t)/D_F}.                              \tag{20}
\]
The two private exponents in (20) agree precisely when
\[
 \frac{2-t}{1+d/\gamma+d/q}
 =\frac{1-t}{1+d/\gamma},
\]
which rearranges to
\[
 t=t_s=1-\frac qd-\frac q\gamma.                         \tag{21}
\]
Also \(r_G=n^{-\nu}\) at \(t=\nu\), while \(r_F=n^{-\kappa}\) at \(t=\kappa\), because \(\kappa=2/(D_F+1)\).

When \(q=q_0\), direct substitution gives \(\kappa=t_s=\nu\).  When \(q>q_0\), monotonicity in \(q\) gives \(\kappa>\nu\) and \(t_s<\nu\); hence both privacy terms are no larger than \(b_n=n^{-\nu}\) for \(t\le\nu\), and \(r_G\) exceeds both \(b_n\) and \(r_F\) for \(t>\nu\).  This proves the first phase partition and its equality point.

When \(q<q_0\), the inequalities reverse: \(\kappa<\nu\) and \(t_s>\kappa\).  Here \(b_n=n^{-\kappa}\).  Equation (20) shows that both privacy terms are at most \(b_n\) for \(t\le\kappa\); \(r_F\) is largest for \(\kappa<t<t_s\); (21) gives equality at \(t_s\); and \(r_G\) is largest for \(t>t_s\).  Since \(q>0\), one has \(0<\kappa<t_s<1\), so the listed intervals exhaust the privacy range.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The explicit phase map states exactly where the new nuisance privacy price changes the attainable risk. \textit{Gap.} The cited CATE and private-regression papers each have elbows, but neither derives this causal nuisance-by-privacy phase diagram. \textit{Consumer.} It tells analysts when privacy, rather than the unresolved nonprivate exact-uniform difficulty, determines sample size.

\begin{proposition}[prop:d1-kill-test]\label{prop:d1-kill-test}
If \(d=1\), \(\alpha=\beta=1/8\), \(\gamma=1\), and \(\epsilon_n=n^{-2/5}\), then \(q=1/4\), \(D_F=6\), \(\kappa=2/7\), and \(t_s=1/2\). At \(t=2/5\), \(b_n=n^{-2/7}\), \(r_F=n^{-4/15}\), and \(r_G=n^{-3/10}\), so \(r_F\) leads. The choices \(h\asymp n^{-4/15}\) and \(\ell\asymp n^{-16/15}\) give \(\Delta_1(S_{h,\ell})\asymp n^{-2}h^{-1}\ell^{-1}=n^{-2/3}\) and \(W_H(\Pi_+^n,\Pi_-^n)\lesssim abn^2h\ell\), yielding the matched scale \(R_n^{\mathrm{DP}}\asymp n^{-4/15}\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Substitution gives
\[
 q=\alpha+\beta=\tfrac14,
 \qquad D_F=1+1+4=6,
 \qquad \kappa=\frac{2}{7},
 \qquad t_s=1-\tfrac14-\tfrac14=\tfrac12.
\]
With \(t=2/5\),
\[
 b_n=\max\{n^{-1/3},n^{-2/7}\}=n^{-2/7},
 \quad r_F=n^{-(2-2/5)/6}=n^{-4/15},
 \quad r_G=n^{-(1-2/5)/2}=n^{-3/10}.
\]
Thus \(r_F\) is the largest term.  The lower-bound calibration and the upper-bound tuning both take
\[
 h\asymp r_F=n^{-4/15},
 \qquad \ell\asymp r_F^4=n^{-16/15}.
\]
In particular \(n\ell=n^{-1/15}\to0\), so the exact binomial normalization is in its sparse regime.  The complete-query sensitivity lemma gives the upper sensitivity bound.  For the reverse bound, place one record with \(A=Y=1\) in a microcell and all other records outside \(Q_h\), then replace one outside record by a second record at the same covariate in that microcell, again with \(A=Y=1\).  Since the constant polynomial belongs to the cell space and has conditional \(L_2\)-norm one, the evaluation norm of any orthonormal basis satisfies \(\|\Phi_j(x)\|_2^2\ge1\).  Thus this replacement changes the corresponding \(q^V\) coordinate from zero by at least \(c/(kp_2)\).  Hence
\[
 \Delta_1(S_{h,\ell})\asymp n^{-2}h^{-1}\ell^{-1}
 =n^{-2+4/15+16/15}=n^{-2/3}.
\]
After division by \(\epsilon_n=n^{-2/5}\), the Laplace error is \(n^{-4/15}\).  Also \(ab\asymp\ell^{1/4}=n^{-4/15}\), and the component coupling gives
\[
 W_H(\Pi_+^n,\Pi_-^n)\le Cabn^2h\ell.
\]
Multiplication by \(\epsilon_n\) makes the right side a constant, since
\[
 \epsilon_nabn^2h\ell
 \asymp n^{-2/5-4/15+2-4/15-16/15}=1.
\]
Choosing the fixed scale constants small makes the output total variation below \(1/2\), while singleton components cost exactly zero.  Finally \(r_F/b_n=n^{2/105}\to\infty\) and \(r_F/r_G=n^{1/30}\to\infty\), so the matching privacy-leading regime theorem applies and yields \(R_n^{\mathrm{DP}}\asymp n^{-4/15}\).  The calculation audits both the factor \(n^{-2}\) in sensitivity and the factor \(n^2\) in Hamming transport.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This specialization exposes any missing factor of \(n\), normalization bias, or surviving singleton before the general theorem is attempted. \textit{Gap.} A direct \(n\epsilon_n\) nuisance balance would give the unsupported slower scale \(n^{-3/25}\); this proposition audits the \(n^{-4/15}\) scale implied by the coupled fuzzy balance. \textit{Consumer.} It is a reproducible audit case for sensitivity and transport calculations.

\begin{openendedquestion}[oeq:symbolic-private-transfer]\label{oeq:symbolic-private-transfer}
Can the handle \(H_{\mathrm{transfer}}\) be completed, without using a formula for \(R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\), so that for \(q<q_0\) and \(\rho_n^{\mathrm{priv}}<c b_n\) there exists one \(\epsilon_n\)-differentially private estimator satisfying \(\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_H}\mathbb E_P|T_{\mathrm{DP}}-\tau_P(x_0)|\le C\max\{R_n^{\mathrm{NP}},\rho_n^{\mathrm{priv}}\}\)?
\end{openendedquestion}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the only remaining step from the matched privacy-leading theorem to a global incremental-price decomposition with a symbolic nonprivate benchmark. \textit{Gap.} Kennedy's oracle inequalities do not imply neighboring-dataset stability, and Niu et al.'s sample-splitting guarantee does not transfer arbitrary nonprivate minimax risk. \textit{Consumer.} A positive answer makes any future exact-uniform nonprivate improvement automatically compatible with the sharp privacy price; a negative answer identifies the necessary stability information beyond minimax risk.

\begin{lemma}[lem:nonprivate-oracle-bump-lower]\label{lem:nonprivate-oracle-bump-lower}
There is a constant \(c>0\), depending only on the fixed class parameters, such that \(R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\ge c n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}\) for every \(n\).  This is only the direct \(\gamma\)-smooth bump lower bound and makes no assertion about the queued sparse-component nonprivate converse.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use the two direct bump laws from the proof of the private lower envelope, with \(h=c_h\Delta^{1/\gamma}\) and target separation \(2\Delta\).  They belong to \(\mathcal M_H\) for small fixed constants.  Their one-record laws differ only in the treated binary-outcome cell on a set of volume \(O(h^d)\); all cell probabilities are bounded away from zero.  Direct expansion of the finite-cell chi-square divergence therefore gives
\[
 \chi^2(P_+,P_-)\le C\Delta^2h^d.
\]
Independence gives
\[
 1+\chi^2(P_+^{\otimes n},P_-^{\otimes n})
 =\{1+\chi^2(P_+,P_-)\}^n
 \le\exp(Cn\Delta^2h^d).
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz applied to the likelihood ratio yields \(\operatorname{TV}\le\tfrac12\sqrt{\chi^2}\).  Taking \(\Delta=c n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}\) makes \(n\Delta^2h^d\le Cc^{2+d/\gamma}\), so a small fixed \(c\) makes product total variation at most \(1/2\).  The nearest-target testing argument then gives maximum absolute risk at least \(c\Delta\), and infimizing over all nonprivate estimators proves the claim.  No nuisance-varying nonprivate construction is used.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:bounded-density-private-lower-envelope]\label{thm:bounded-density-private-lower-envelope}
Every law in \(\mathcal M_H\) has covariate density equal to one, and hence \(\mathcal M_H\subseteq\mathcal M_{\mathrm{bd}}\) because \(L^{-1}\le1\le L\).  On \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{bd}}\), for \(n^{-1}\le\epsilon_n\le1\),
\[
 R_{n,\mathrm{bd}}^{\mathrm{DP}}
 \ge c\max\{R_{n,\mathrm{bd}}^{\mathrm{NP}},r_G,r_F\}
 =c\max\{R_{n,\mathrm{bd}}^{\mathrm{NP}},\rho_n^{\mathrm{priv}}\}.
\]
This is a lower-bound transfer only; it asserts neither validity of the exact-uniform estimator nor a matched phase diagram on \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{bd}}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If \(P\in\mathcal M_H\), then \(\text{ass:uniform-design}\) gives \(dP_X/dx=1\) almost everywhere.  Since the declared class constant satisfies \(L\ge2\), one has \(L^{-1}\le1\le L\).  Every other member condition in \(\text{def:bounded-density-class}\) is also a member condition of \(\text{def:holder-class}\).  Thus
\[
 \mathcal M_H\subseteq\mathcal M_{\mathrm{bd}}.                                \tag{30}
\]

We use only the two comparisons stated in the corollary.  First, for any private randomized output \(T\), let \(\overline T(D)=\mathbb E[T\mid D]\).  Conditional Jensen's inequality, now for every \(P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{bd}}\), gives
\[
 \mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}\left|\overline T(D_n)-\tau_P(x_0)\right|
 \le
 \mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n},M_{\varepsilon_n}}
 \left|T-\tau_P(x_0)\right|.
\]
After taking the supremum over \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{bd}}\) and the infimum over private mechanisms,
\[
 R_{n,\mathrm{bd}}^{\mathrm{DP}}
 \ge R_{n,\mathrm{bd}}^{\mathrm{NP}}.                                           \tag{31}
\]
Second, the private mechanisms in the two minimax problems range over the same set of kernels on \(\mathcal O^n\).  Therefore minimax monotonicity and (30) imply
\[
 R_{n,\mathrm{bd}}^{\mathrm{DP}}
 \ge R_n^{\mathrm{DP}}
 \ge c_{\mathrm{lb}}\max\{r_G,r_F\},                                           \tag{32}
\]
where the last inequality is the privacy part of \(\text{thm:private-lower-envelope}\).  If \(x=R_{n,\mathrm{bd}}^{\mathrm{NP}}\) and \(y=\max\{r_G,r_F\}\), (31)--(32) give
\[
 R_{n,\mathrm{bd}}^{\mathrm{DP}}
 \ge \min\{1,c_{\mathrm{lb}}\}\max\{x,y\}.
\]
Renaming the positive fixed constant and using \(\rho_n^{\mathrm{priv}}=\max\{r_G,r_F\}\) from \(\text{def:rates}\) proves the claim.  No upper bound or exact-uniform construction is used.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The exact-uniform class is a subclass of the bounded-density causal class, so both intrinsic privacy barriers remain valid on the broader model even though the design-specific estimator need not transfer. \textit{Gap.} The exact-uniform construction does not establish achievability under an unknown density, but subclass monotonicity still yields a sharp lower-side comparison with the Kennedy-style bounded-density model. \textit{Consumer.} The corollary separates a broadly applicable privacy impossibility from the design-specific matched upper frontier.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The exact occupancy normalization and deterministic projection geometry require a known exact-uniform random design with equal public cell volumes. Thus the upper theorem, matched frontier, and phase diagram do not extend here to an unknown or merely bounded covariate density, and the iid theorem does not automatically cover a deterministic uniform grid. Only the privacy lower barriers transfer to the broader bounded-density class, by inclusion of the uniform subclass and conditional Jensen. On the exact-uniform class, the upper theorem still contains the explicit benchmark \(b_n\); replacing it by \(R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\) below the nuisance elbow and outside privacy-leading regimes is the stated open-ended question, not an assumed consequence of generic output perturbation.

\section{Honest scope}

The causal boundedness consequence, projection identity, pair sensitivity, fuzzy membership, component transport, upper envelope, lower envelope, and phase diagram concern the known exact-uniform class with bounded observed outcomes. The full symbolic decomposition is settled there for \(q\ge q_0\) and whenever \(\rho_n^{\mathrm{priv}}\gtrsim b_n\), and it remains open for \(q<q_0\) when privacy is inactive relative to \(b_n\). The queued exact-uniform nonprivate converse is neither cited as established nor duplicated. On the aligned unknown bounded-density class, this note proves only \(R_{n,\mathrm{bd}}^{\mathrm{DP}}\gtrsim\max\{R_{n,\mathrm{bd}}^{\mathrm{NP}},r_G,r_F\}\) by conditional Jensen and subclass monotonicity; it asserts no estimator, upper rate, matched phase diagram, or symbolic transfer there. Privacy sequences with \(\epsilon_n<n^{-1}\) and deterministic fixed designs remain outside the stated theorems.
