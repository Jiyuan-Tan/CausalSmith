For a fixed law, \(\text{lem:bin-moment-limit}\) gives \(\|\widehat\Gamma_{1,n}-\Gamma_1\|_{\mathrm{op}}=O_P(n^{-1/2})\).  Weyl's inequality follows from the variational definition of singular values and gives \(|\sigma_s(\widehat\Gamma_{1,n})-\sigma_s(\Gamma_1)|\leq\|\widehat\Gamma_{1,n}-\Gamma_1\|_{\mathrm{op}}\).  The \(r\)-th population singular value is fixed and positive, while all later singular values are zero.  Since \(t_n\to0\) and \(\sqrt n t_n\to\infty\), these inequalities imply \(P(\widehat r_n=r)\to1\).  The two-lag inverse is continuous on the fixed-rank stratum at the true moments: \(RR^{\mathsf T}\), \(F-I_r\), \(q\), and every \(\lambda_j\) are bounded away from their singular boundaries at that fixed law.  Basis invariance, proved in \(\text{thm:two-lag-exact-recovery}\), makes this continuity independent of the chosen singular vectors.  Thus the continuous mapping theorem gives \(\widehat\theta_n\to\theta\).  The positive-part operation is continuous, although generally not differentiable at a missing edge.  At a fixed finite graph the smallest nonzero amplitude is positive; therefore \(a_n\to0\), \(\sqrt n a_n\to\infty\), and root-\(n\) tightness of the locally Lipschitz inverse imply \(P(\widehat G_n=G(A))\to1\).

Uniformly on \(\mathcal M_{\Delta,p}(\kappa)\), Weyl's inequality and the rank gap give \(P(\widehat r_n\neq r)\to0\).  A basis-free description makes the conditioning explicit.  The projector \(P_S=\Gamma_1\Gamma_1^\dagger\) and the operator \(U=\Gamma_2\Gamma_1^\dagger\), restricted to \(S\), are locally Lipschitz under \(\sigma_r(\Gamma_1)\geq\kappa\).  The restricted trace gives \(q\in[\kappa,1-\kappa]\).  The parameter envelope and fixed \(p,\Delta\) bound \(\|U\|_{\mathrm{op}}\).  Since \(|\det(U|_S-I_S)|=(1-q)^r\geq\kappa^r\), the inequality \(\sigma_{\min}(M)\geq|\det M|/\|M\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{r-1}\) supplies a uniform inverse bound for \(U|_S-I_S\).  The finite nilpotent logarithm has degree at most \(p-1\), so its derivative is uniformly bounded on this compact envelope.  Finally \(\lambda_i\geq\kappa\) permits every edge division.  These facts show that the complete inverse, including the Lipschitz positive part, has a common local Lipschitz constant.  Combining it with the uniform root-\(n\) tightness in \(\text{lem:bin-moment-limit}\) proves
\[
 \lim_{M\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{\theta:P_\theta\in\mathcal M_{\Delta,p}(\kappa)}
 P_\theta\!\left[\sqrt n\{|\widehat\beta_n-\beta|+\|\widehat A_n-A\|_{\mathrm F}+\|\widehat\mu_n-\mu\|_2\}>M\right]=0.
\]
The HAC conclusion is exactly the last conclusion of \(\text{lem:bin-moment-limit}\).  This proves the proposed claim.  It deliberately does not assert a Gaussian law for the full positive-part amplitude estimator: at \(Z_{ij}=0\), \(z\mapsto\max(z,0)\) is not Fr\'{e}chet differentiable.
