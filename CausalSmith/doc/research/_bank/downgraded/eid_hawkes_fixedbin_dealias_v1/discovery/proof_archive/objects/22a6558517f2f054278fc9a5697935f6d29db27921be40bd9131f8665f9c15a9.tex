Fix \(\eta\in\mathfrak I_{\Delta,p}^{\mathrm{ib}}\), and choose an arbitrary representative \((\beta,A,\mu,I,E_I,A_I)\) supplied by \(\text{def:isolated-invertible-block-stable-moment-image}\).  Put \(r=|I|\), \(L_I=A_I-\beta I_r\), and let \(E_0\) be the ordered selector of the complementary labels.  Since \([E_I\ E_0]\) is a permutation matrix, the identities \(A=E_IA_IE_I^{\mathsf T}\) and \(D=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda)\) give, in these coordinates,
\[
 A=\begin{pmatrix}A_I&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 B=\begin{pmatrix}L_I&0\\0&-\beta I_{p-r}\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 D=\begin{pmatrix}D_I&0\\0&D_0\end{pmatrix},
\tag{1}
\]
where \(D_I=E_I^{\mathsf T}DE_I\succ0\) and \(D_0=E_0^{\mathsf T}DE_0\succ0\).  Positivity follows because every stationary intensity coordinate is strictly positive in \(\mathcal M_{\Delta,p}^{\mathrm{stab}}\).

The restriction \(\rho(A_I)<\beta\) implies that every eigenvalue of \(L_I\) has negative real part; thus \(L_I\) is Hurwitz and invertible.  The block form (1) and the definition of \(Q\) yield
\[
 Q=E_IQ_IE_I^{\mathsf T}+E_0Q_0E_0^{\mathsf T},\qquad
 Q_I=\int_0^\infty e^{L_Iu}D_Ie^{L_I^{\mathsf T}u}\,du,\qquad
 Q_0=(2\beta)^{-1}D_0.
\tag{2}
\]
The integral for \(Q_I\) converges because \(L_I\) is Hurwitz.  For every nonzero \(x\in\mathbb R^r\), its integrand is continuous and at \(u=0\) equals \(x^{\mathsf T}D_Ix>0\); hence \(x^{\mathsf T}Q_Ix>0\), proving \(Q_I\succ0\).

By \(\text{lem:stable-covariance-semigroup}\) and (1)--(2),
\[
 K=E_IK_IE_I^{\mathsf T},\qquad
 K_I=A_I(2\beta I_r-A_I)Q_I.
\tag{3}
\]
The matrices \(A_I\) and \(Q_I\) are invertible by the image definition and (2).  If \(z\in\operatorname{spec}(A_I)\), then \(|z|\leq\rho(A_I)<\beta\), so \(2\beta-z\neq0\); hence \(2\beta I_r-A_I\) is invertible.  Equation (3) therefore makes \(K_I\) invertible.

Let
\[
 J_I=\int_0^\Delta e^{L_Iu}\,du=L_I^{-1}(e^{L_I\Delta}-I_r).
\tag{4}
\]
Every eigenvalue of \(e^{L_I\Delta}\) has modulus strictly less than one, so \(e^{L_I\Delta}-I_r\) and hence \(J_I\) are invertible.  The same block calculation applied to \(J\), followed by the covariance identity in \(\text{lem:stable-covariance-semigroup}\), gives
\[
 \Gamma_1=E_IJ_I^2K_IE_I^{\mathsf T},\qquad
 \Gamma_2=E_Ie^{L_I\Delta}J_I^2K_IE_I^{\mathsf T}.
\tag{5}
\]
Because \(J_I^2K_I\) is invertible, (5) implies
\[
 S=\operatorname{im}(\Gamma_1)=\operatorname{im}(E_I)
 =\operatorname{im}(A)=\operatorname{span}\{e_i:i\in I\},\qquad
 \operatorname{rank}(\Gamma_1)=r.
\tag{6}
\]
Consequently \(I=\{i:e_i\in S\}\).  This set is determined by \(\Gamma_1\), and ordering its labels increasingly makes \(E_I\) unique.  In particular, two structural representatives of the same moment triple must use the same active set and ordered selector.

Put \(H_I=J_I^2K_I\).  From (5), \(\Gamma_1=E_IH_IE_I^{\mathsf T}\) with \(H_I\) invertible.  Direct verification of the four Moore--Penrose equations gives
\[
 \Gamma_1^\dagger=E_IH_I^{-1}E_I^{\mathsf T}.
\tag{7}
\]
Equations (5)--(7) now give the observable restricted transition
\[
 F^\circ=E_I^{\mathsf T}\Gamma_2\Gamma_1^\dagger E_I
 =e^{L_I\Delta}H_IH_I^{-1}=e^{L_I\Delta}.
\tag{8}
\]
The image condition places \(\operatorname{spec}(\Delta L_I)\) in the open principal strip.  Thus \(\text{lem:principal-log-strip}\) applied to (8) proves that the principal logarithm exists and
\[
 L^\circ=\Delta^{-1}\operatorname{Log}(F^\circ)=L_I.
\tag{9}
\]
Equations (4), (5), and (9) show that \(J^\circ=J_I\) is invertible and, crucially,
\[
 K^\circ=(J^\circ)^{-2}E_I^{\mathsf T}\Gamma_1E_I
 =J_I^{-2}J_I^2K_I=K_I.
\tag{10}
\]
Thus the two-lag inverse is total.  Notice that (10) uses two left factors of \(J_I^{-1}\); a congruence with \(J_I^{-\mathsf T}\) would not follow from \(\Gamma_1=J^2K\) and would generally fail when \(J_I\) is nonsymmetric.

Stationarity and \(\text{def:population-moments}\) give \(m=\Delta\lambda\), so \(\lambda^\circ=\lambda\) and \(D_I^\circ=D_I\).  Combining this with (2) and (9) proves
\[
 Q^\circ=Q_I\succ0.
\tag{11}
\]
It is also the unique solution of \(L_IQ+QL_I^{\mathsf T}=-D_I\): differentiating the integrand in (2) proves the equation, while the difference \(H\) of two solutions satisfies \(e^{L_Iu}He^{L_I^{\mathsf T}u}=H\) and hence is zero after letting \(u\to\infty\).

Since \(A_I=L_I+\beta I_r\), (3) becomes
\[
 K_I=(L_I+\beta I_r)(\beta I_r-L_I)Q_I
     =(\beta^2I_r-L_I^2)Q_I.
\tag{12}
\]
Equations (9)--(12) imply
\[
 K^\circ(Q^\circ)^{-1}+(L^\circ)^2=\beta^2I_r,\qquad
 s^\circ=r^{-1}\operatorname{tr}(\beta^2I_r)=\beta^2>0.
\tag{13}
\]
Because \(r\geq1\), division by \(r\) is valid; because \(\beta>0\), the positive square root in the definition gives \(\beta^\circ=\beta\).  It follows from (6), (9), and (13) that
\[
 A^\circ=E_I(L_I+\beta I_r)E_I^{\mathsf T}=E_IA_IE_I^{\mathsf T}=A.
\tag{14}
\]
Taking expectations in the nonnegative Hawkes intensity, using monotone convergence, stationarity, and \(\beta>0\), gives
\[
 \lambda=\mu+\int_0^\infty Ae^{-\beta u}\lambda\,du
 =\mu+(A/\beta)\lambda.
\tag{15}
\]
Therefore (14)--(15) yield \(\mu^\circ=(I_p-A/\beta)\lambda=\mu\).

For basis invariance, let \(O=E_IU\), where \(U\in\mathbb R^{r\times r}\) is orthogonal, and perform the same construction with \(O\) in place of \(E_I\).  Similarity covariance of the principal logarithm and direct substitution give
\[
 F_O=U^{\mathsf T}F^\circ U,\quad L_O=U^{\mathsf T}L_IU,\quad
 J_O=U^{\mathsf T}J_IU,\quad K_O=U^{\mathsf T}K_IU,\quad
 D_O=U^{\mathsf T}D_IU,\quad Q_O=U^{\mathsf T}Q_IU.
\tag{16}
\]
The trace in (13) is similarity invariant, and
\[
 O(L_O+\beta I_r)O^{\mathsf T}=E_I(L_I+\beta I_r)E_I^{\mathsf T}.
\tag{17}
\]
Thus the recovered scalar and ambient matrices do not depend on active-space coordinates.  Since all recovered quantities are functions only of \(\eta\), \(\Delta\), and an inessential coordinate choice, while the initial representative was arbitrary, equations (6)--(17) also prove representative independence.

It remains to verify that the displayed strict witnesses really lie in the image.  For the first, take \(p=2\), \(I=\{1\}\), \(A_I=(1/2)\), \(\beta=1\), and \(\mu=\mathbf1_2\).  Then \(A=\operatorname{diag}(1/2,0)\), \(A_I\) is nonnegative and invertible, \(\rho(A_I)=1/2<1\), and \(\operatorname{spec}\{\Delta(A_I-I_1)\}=\{-\Delta/2\}\) is real for every \(\Delta>0\).  For the second, take \(p=3\), \(I=\{1,2\}\), \(\beta=1\), \(\mu=\mathbf1_3\), and
\[
 A_I=\begin{pmatrix}1/4&1/4\\1/4&1/2\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{18}
\]
This matrix is entrywise positive, has determinant \(1/16\), and has eigenvalues \((3-\sqrt5)/8\) and \((3+\sqrt5)/8\).  Both are real and the larger is less than one, so the stability and principal-strip conditions hold strictly for every \(\Delta>0\).  It has two positive diagonal entries and both reciprocal off-diagonal entries, while the third label is isolated from excitation.

For completeness, both parameter triples induce stationary simple Hawkes laws.  In the immigration--birth construction, label-\(i\) immigrants form independent Poisson processes of rate \(\mu_i\), and a label-\(j\) event has label-\(i\) children with Poisson intensity \(A_{ij}e^{-u}\,du\).  The mean offspring matrix is \(A/\beta\).  In both witnesses its spectral radius is below one, so the entrywise nonnegative Neumann series \(\sum_{h\geq0}(A/\beta)^h\) is finite.  Hence the expected total size of each immigrant cluster is finite, which implies that every cluster is finite almost surely.  The stationary superposition over immigrants has finite expected count on every bounded interval and is therefore locally finite almost surely; continuous offspring delays make simultaneous points a null event, and summing the offspring hazards gives the stated Hawkes conditional intensity.  Thus these are laws in \(\mathcal M_{\Delta,p}^{\mathrm{stab}}\), and their moment triples belong to \(\mathfrak I_{\Delta,p}^{\mathrm{ib}}\).
