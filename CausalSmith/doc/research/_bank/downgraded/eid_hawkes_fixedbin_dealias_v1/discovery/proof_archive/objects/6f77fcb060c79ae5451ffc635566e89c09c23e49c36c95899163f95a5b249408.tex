Fix a law in \(\mathcal M_{\Delta,p}^{\mathrm{DAG},+}\).  By \(\text{thm:two-lag-exact-recovery}\), \(r=\operatorname{rank}(\Gamma_1)\geq1\), the positive-lag part of \(\Phi_{\Delta,r}\) is well defined and basis invariant, and it recovers \(q=e^{-\beta\Delta}\).  We verify directly that the positive-lag covariance curve used in \(\text{def:covariance-only-inverse}\) is the true one.

By \(\text{lem:covariance-semigroup}\),
\[
 S=\operatorname{im}(\Gamma_1)=\operatorname{im}(A)=\operatorname{im}(K),
 \qquad \Gamma_1=J^2K,
 \qquad \Gamma_2=T\Gamma_1.
\tag{1}
\]
The space \(S\) is invariant under \(A\), hence under \(B=A-\beta I_p\), \(T=e^{B\Delta}\), and \(J=\int_0^\Delta e^{Bu}\,du\).  Since \(R=O^{\mathsf T}\Gamma_1\) has full row rank and \(\Gamma_1=OR\), (1) and the definition of \(F\) give
\[
 F=O^{\mathsf T}TO.
\tag{2}
\]
Let \(A_S=O^{\mathsf T}AO\).  Acyclicity implies \(A^p=0\), and the restriction \(A|_S:S\to S\) is nilpotent on the \(r\)-dimensional space \(S\), so \(A_S^r=0\).  Equation (2) becomes
\[
 F=e^{-\beta\Delta}e^{A_S\Delta}=q e^{A_S\Delta},
 \qquad E=q^{-1}F-I_r=e^{A_S\Delta}-I_r.
\tag{3}
\]
For a nilpotent matrix, the scalar formal-power-series identity \(\log(e^x)=x\) may be evaluated in the quotient ring \(\mathbb R[x]/(x^r)\); all sums are finite there.  Hence the finite polynomial in \(\text{def:two-lag-inverse}\) satisfies
\[
 \sum_{h=1}^{r-1}\frac{(-1)^{h+1}}{h}E^h=\Delta A_S,
 \qquad L^\star=-\beta I_r+A_S=O^{\mathsf T}BO.
\tag{4}
\]
All eigenvalues of \(L^\star\) equal \(-\beta<0\); therefore \(L^\star\) and \(F-I_r=e^{L^\star\Delta}-I_r\) are invertible.  If \(J_S=O^{\mathsf T}JO\), then invariance of \(S\) and (4) give
\[
 J_S=\int_0^\Delta e^{L^\star u}\,du
 =(L^\star)^{-1}(F-I_r),
 \qquad H^\star=L^\star(F-I_r)^{-1}=J_S^{-1}.
\tag{5}
\]
Here \(F=e^{L^\star\Delta}\), so \(F-I_r\) commutes with \(L^\star\), which justifies the displayed order in the inverse of \(J_S\).
Since the columns of \(K\) lie in \(S\), equations (1) and (5) yield
\[
 R=O^{\mathsf T}J^2K=J_S^2O^{\mathsf T}K,
 \qquad K^\star=O(H^\star)^2R=OO^{\mathsf T}K=K.
\tag{6}
\]
Consequently, for every \(t\in(0,\Delta]\),
\[
 \widetilde C_{+}(t)
 =Oe^{L^\star t}(H^\star)^2R
 =e^{Bt}K
 =C_{+}(t),
\tag{7}
\]
where the last equality is \(\text{lem:covariance-semigroup}\).  Equality (7) also proves that this reconstructed curve is independent of the chosen orthonormal basis \(O\).

Apply \(\text{lem:zero-lag-bin-covariance}\), which is applicable because every acyclic law belongs to the stable class.  Substitution of (7) into its inverse identity gives
\[
 D^\star=\Delta^{-1}\!\left[\Gamma_0-
 \int_0^\Delta(\Delta-t)
 \{\widetilde C_{+}(t)+\widetilde C_{+}(t)^{\mathsf T}\}\,dt\right]
 =D.
\tag{8}
\]
Thus \(\lambda^\star=\operatorname{diag}(D^\star)=\lambda\in(0,\infty)^p\), and the reconstructed mean is \(\Delta\lambda^\star=m\).  Every division and inverse used above is therefore valid, and the integral in (8) is finite by (7) and continuity of \(e^{Bt}K\) on \([0,\Delta]\).

The remaining edge and baseline operations in \(\Psi_{\Delta,r}\) are now exactly those in \(\Phi_{\Delta,r}(m,\Gamma_1,\Gamma_2)\).  By \(\text{thm:two-lag-exact-recovery}\), they return \((\beta,A,\mu)\).  Hence
\[
 \Psi_{\Delta,r}(\Gamma_0,\Gamma_1,\Gamma_2)=(\beta,A,\mu).
\]
This proves both exact recovery and redundancy of the separately supplied mean.
