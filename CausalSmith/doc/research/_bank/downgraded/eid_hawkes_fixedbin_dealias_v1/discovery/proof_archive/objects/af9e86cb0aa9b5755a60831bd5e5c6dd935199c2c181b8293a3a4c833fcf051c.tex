Fix \(\eta=(m,\Gamma_1,\Gamma_2)\in\mathfrak I_{\Delta,p}^{\mathrm{fr}}\).  By \(\text{def:full-rank-stable-moment-image}\), choose an arbitrary admissible structural representative \((\beta,A,\mu)\), put \(B=A-\beta I_p\), and note that
\[
 \operatorname{spec}(\Delta B)\subset
 \{z\in\mathbb C:|\operatorname{Im}z|<\pi\}.
\tag{1}
\]
By \(\text{lem:stable-covariance-semigroup}\), \(B\) is real Hurwitz, \(Q\succ0\), \(J\) is invertible, and
\[
 \Gamma_1=J^2K,\quad \Gamma_2=TJ^2K,\quad
 T=e^{B\Delta},\quad K=A(2\beta I_p-A)Q.
\tag{2}
\]
Membership in \(\mathcal M_{\Delta,p}^{\mathrm{stab}}\) gives \(\rho(A/\beta)<1\) and \(\beta>0\).  Hence every \(z\in\operatorname{spec}(A)\) satisfies \(|z|<\beta\), so \(2\beta-z\neq0\).  Thus \(2\beta I_p-A\) is invertible.  The definition of \(\mathfrak I_{\Delta,p}^{\mathrm{fr}}\) gives \(\det(A)\neq0\), while \(Q\succ0\) gives invertibility of \(Q\).  The product in (2) therefore makes \(K\) invertible.  Since \(J\) is invertible, \(\Gamma_1=J^2K\) is invertible.  Consequently
\[
 T^\dagger=\Gamma_2\Gamma_1^{-1}
 =TJ^2K(J^2K)^{-1}=T=e^{B\Delta}.
\tag{3}
\]
By (1), (3), and \(\text{lem:principal-log-strip}\), the principal logarithm exists and
\[
 B^\dagger=\Delta^{-1}\operatorname{Log}(T^\dagger)
 =\Delta^{-1}\operatorname{Log}(e^{B\Delta})=B.
\tag{4}
\]
Division by \(\Delta\) is valid because \(\Delta>0\).  Thus \(B^\dagger\) is real Hurwitz and in particular invertible.  Its exponential has all eigenvalues strictly inside the unit disk, so \(T^\dagger-I_p\) is invertible.  The definition of \(J^\dagger\), (3), and (4) give
\[
 J^\dagger=(B^\dagger)^{-1}(T^\dagger-I_p)
 =B^{-1}(T-I_p)=J,
\quad
 K^\dagger=(J^\dagger)^{-2}\Gamma_1=J^{-2}J^2K=K.
\tag{5}
\]
In particular, \(J^\dagger\) and \(K^\dagger\) are invertible.

Stationarity and \(\text{def:population-moments}\) yield
\[
 m=\mathbb E\{N(\Delta)-N(0)\}=\Delta\lambda.
\tag{6}
\]
Hence \(\lambda^\dagger=m/\Delta=\lambda\) and \(D^\dagger=D\succ0\).  Because \(B^\dagger=B\) is Hurwitz, there are \(c>0\) and \(M<\infty\) such that \(\|e^{Bu}\|\leq Me^{-cu}\) for \(u\geq0\).  Therefore the integral defining \(Q^\dagger\) converges absolutely, and
\[
 Q^\dagger=\int_0^\infty e^{Bu}De^{B^{\mathsf T}u}\,du=Q\succ0.
\tag{7}
\]
Differentiating its integrand and using the Hurwitz limit gives
\[
 BQ^\dagger+Q^\dagger B^{\mathsf T}
 =\int_0^\infty\frac{d}{du}
   \{e^{Bu}De^{B^{\mathsf T}u}\}\,du=-D.
\tag{8}
\]
This Lyapunov solution is unique: if \(H_0\) is the difference of two solutions, then \(BH_0+H_0B^{\mathsf T}=0\), so \(e^{Bu}H_0e^{B^{\mathsf T}u}=H_0\) for every \(u\geq0\); the Hurwitz limit of the left side is zero, and hence \(H_0=0\).  Equations (4), (6), and (8) give the asserted Lyapunov characterization with \(B^\dagger\) and \(D^\dagger\).

Since \(A=B+\beta I_p\), the identity for \(K\) in (2) becomes
\[
 K=(B+\beta I_p)(\beta I_p-B)Q=(\beta^2I_p-B^2)Q.
\tag{9}
\]
Using (4), (5), (7), and (9),
\[
 K^\dagger(Q^\dagger)^{-1}+(B^\dagger)^2=\beta^2I_p.
\tag{10}
\]
Taking traces and using \(p\geq2\) gives
\[
 s^\dagger=p^{-1}\operatorname{tr}(\beta^2I_p)=\beta^2>0.
\tag{11}
\]
The inverse selects the positive real square root and \(\beta>0\); consequently \(\beta^\dagger=\beta\), and then
\[
 A^\dagger=B^\dagger+\beta^\dagger I_p=B+\beta I_p=A.
\tag{12}
\]

Finally, taking expectations in the common-exponential Hawkes intensity is justified componentwise by monotone convergence, because the kernel and point measures are nonnegative.  Stationarity, \(\beta>0\), and finiteness of \(\lambda\) give
\[
 \lambda=\mu+\int_0^\infty Ae^{-\beta u}\lambda\,du
 =\mu+(A/\beta)\lambda.
\tag{13}
\]
Thus, by (6), (11), and (12),
\[
 \mu^\dagger=(I_p-A^\dagger/\beta^\dagger)\lambda^\dagger
 =(I_p-A/\beta)\lambda=\mu.
\tag{14}
\]
The representative was arbitrary, whereas every daggered quantity is a function only of \(\eta\) and the known \(\Delta\).  Equations (3)--(14) therefore hold for every admissible representative and force all such representatives to coincide.  They prove every claimed inverse, positivity, well-definedness, representative-independence, and exact-recovery assertion.  Since \(\text{def:stable-common-exponential-class}\) imposes no acyclicity, zero-diagonal, orientation, reciprocity, or self-loop restriction, the conclusion covers the stated full-rank cyclic, reciprocal, and self-exciting members of the image domain.
