Acyclicity implies that a genealogical line uses at most \(p-1\) edges: each child follows an edge of \(G(A)\), and a line of length at least \(p\) repeats a label and contains a directed cycle.  For a parent of label \(j\), the total number of children is Poisson with mean \(\sum_iA_{ij}/\beta\).  The parameter envelope and the column norm inequality give
\[
 \sum_iA_{ij}\leq\sqrt p\|Ae_j\|_2\leq\sqrt p\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\overline a,
\]
while the decay margin gives \(\beta\geq\underline\beta\).  Thus every total offspring mean is at most \(\overline\nu=\overline a/\underline\beta\).  Conditional on a child occurring, its delay has density \(\beta e^{-\beta u}\).  Also \(\sum_i\mu_i\leq\sqrt p\|\mu\|_2\leq\overline\mu\).

Let \(c=c_{\mathrm{mix}}=\underline\beta/2\).  A descendant at generation \(d\) has a path delay equal in law, conditional on its path, to a sum \(S_d\) of \(d\) independent exponential variables of rate \(\beta\).  Exponential Markov inequality gives
\[
 P(S_d>u)\leq e^{-cu}\left(\frac{\beta}{\beta-c}\right)^d\leq2^de^{-cu}.
\]
The expected number of generation-\(d\) descendants is at most \(\overline\nu^d\).  A union bound through expected descendant counts therefore yields
\[
 P(D_{\mathrm{cl}}>u)\leq e^{-cu}\sum_{d=1}^{p-1}(2\overline\nu)^d
 \leq C_{\mathrm{mix}}e^{-cu}.
\]

For the strong-mixing bound, couple the past bins and future bins by using independent copies of every immigrant cluster that intersects both sides of the gap.  If the past ends at time \(\Delta\) and the future begins at \(h\Delta\), such a cluster must have immigrant time \(s\leq\Delta\) and duration exceeding \(h\Delta-s\).  The Poisson union bound and the preceding sharper tail with constant \(C_0=\sum_{d=1}^{p-1}(2\overline a/\underline\beta)^d\) give
\[
 P(\text{a crossing cluster})
 \leq\overline\mu\int_{-\infty}^{\Delta}C_0e^{-c(h\Delta-s)}ds
 =\overline\mu c^{-1}e^{c\Delta}C_0e^{-ch\Delta}
 \leq C_{\mathrm{mix}}e^{-ch\Delta}.
\]
On the complement, the two sigma-fields are functions of disjoint restrictions of the immigrant Poisson measure and independent offspring marks.  The coupling inequality thus proves \(\alpha_{X,\theta}(h)\leq C_{\mathrm{mix}}e^{-c_{\mathrm{mix}}h\Delta}\).  The process \(Y_k\) depends only on \(X_k,X_{k+1},X_{k+2}\), so its two sigma-fields have an \(X\)-bin gap of \((h-2)_+\), yielding the stated bound for \(\alpha_{Y,\theta}(h)\).  With parameters fixed, the same calculation uses finite law-specific constants and proves geometric mixing on the whole nonempty class.
