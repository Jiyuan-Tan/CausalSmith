By \(\text{lem:covariance-semigroup}\), nonemptiness gives \(r=\operatorname{rank}(A)\geq1\) and \(S=\operatorname{im}(A)=\operatorname{im}(\Gamma_1)\).  Since \(BA=AB\), the space \(S\) is invariant under \(B\), and therefore under \(T=e^{B\Delta}\) and \(J\).  For an orthonormal basis \(O\) of \(S\), put \(R=O^{\mathsf T}\Gamma_1\).  Then \(\Gamma_1=OR\), and \(R\) has row rank \(r\), so \(RR^{\mathsf T}\) is positive definite and invertible.

There is a unique matrix \(F_0\) satisfying \(TO=OF_0\).  The semigroup identity gives \(\Gamma_2=T\Gamma_1=OF_0R\); multiplication on the left by \(O^{\mathsf T}\) and on the right by the right inverse \(R^{\mathsf T}(RR^{\mathsf T})^{-1}\) yields
\[
 F=O^{\mathsf T}\Gamma_2R^{\mathsf T}(RR^{\mathsf T})^{-1}=F_0.
\]
Thus \(F\) represents \(T|_S\).  If \(A_S=O^{\mathsf T}AO\) denotes the coordinate matrix of \(A|_S\), then \(A_S^r=0\) and
\[
 F=e^{(-\beta I_r+A_S)\Delta}=q_0e^{A_S\Delta},\qquad q_0=e^{-\beta\Delta}\in(0,1).
\]
All eigenvalues of \(e^{A_S\Delta}\) equal one, so \(\operatorname{tr}(F)=rq_0\).  Hence the definition's scalar satisfies \(q=q_0\), and \(\beta^\star=-\Delta^{-1}\log q=\beta\).  Furthermore
\[
 E=q^{-1}F-I_r=e^{A_S\Delta}-I_r
\]
is a polynomial in \(A_S\) with zero constant term.  It is nilpotent and \(E^r=0\).  The mutually inverse exponential and logarithm polynomials on nilpotent matrices give
\[
 \sum_{h=1}^{r-1}\frac{(-1)^{h+1}}hE^h=A_S\Delta,
\]
so \(L^\star=-\beta I_r+A_S\) is exactly the coordinate matrix of \(B|_S\).  Since its only eigenvalue is \(-\beta\), it is invertible.  Since the only eigenvalue of \(F-I_r\) is \(q-1\neq0\), that matrix is also invertible.  On \(S\),
\[
 J_S=(L^\star)^{-1}(F-I_r),\qquad H^\star=L^\star(F-I_r)^{-1}=J_S^{-1},
\]
where commutativity follows because all matrices are polynomials or rational functions of \(A_S\).

The columns of \(K\) lie in \(S\).  Writing \(K=OK_S\), the identity \(\Gamma_1=J^2K\) becomes \(R=J_S^2K_S\).  Therefore
\[
 K^\star=O(H^\star)^2R=OK_S=K.
\]
It follows from \(K-K^{\mathsf T}=AD-DA^{\mathsf T}\) that
\[
 Z_{ij}=A_{ij}\lambda_j-A_{ji}\lambda_i.
\]
Acyclicity forbids a self-loop and forbids the two-cycle consisting of both \(i\to j\) and \(j\to i\).  Nonnegativity and \(\lambda_i>0\) consequently imply, for \(i\neq j\),
\[
 A_{ij}=\frac{\max\{Z_{ij},0\}}{\lambda_j}.
\]
The mean identity gives \(\lambda=m/\Delta\).  Integrating the Hawkes kernel in the stationary mean equation gives \(\lambda=\mu+(A/\beta)\lambda\), and hence \(\mu=(I_p-A/\beta)\lambda\).  Thus every component produced by \(\Phi_{\Delta,r}\) equals the structural component.

Finally, replace \(O\) by another orthonormal basis \(O'=OU\), where \(U\) is orthogonal.  Then \(R'=U^{\mathsf T}R\), \(F'=U^{\mathsf T}FU\), \(E'=U^{\mathsf T}EU\), \(L'=U^{\mathsf T}LU\), and \(H'=U^{\mathsf T}HU\).  Hence \(O'(H')^2R'=OH^2R\).  All later operations are basis free, proving basis invariance.  This argument uses the actual image and actual rank only, so singularity, disconnection, proportional rows, and repeated Jordan poles cause no exception.
