Let \(d=p(p-1)/2\), and let \(g_r\) map the raw moment vector to the \(d\) upper-triangular entries of the recovered signed jump \(Z^\star=K^\star-(K^\star)^{\mathsf T}\), using the rank-\(r\) image construction.  On \(\mathcal V_n\), the definitions give, for every \(i<j\),
\[
 \lambda_j^\star A_{ij}^\star-\lambda_i^\star A_{ji}^\star
 =\max\{Z_{ij}^\star,0\}-\max\{-Z_{ij}^\star,0\}
 =Z_{ij}^\star.
\tag{1}
\]
Moreover, \(\mu^\star=(I_p-A^\star/\beta^\star)\lambda^\star\), and invertibility of \(I_p-A^\star/\beta^\star\) is part of \(\mathcal V_n\); hence the intensity computed inside \(\zeta(\widehat\theta_n)\) is exactly \(\lambda^\star\).  On \(\mathcal V_n^c\), both \(\widehat z\) and \(\zeta(1,0,\mathbf1_p)\) are zero.  Therefore the total definitions satisfy
\[
 \widehat z=\zeta(\widehat\theta_n)
 =g_{\widehat r_n}(\widehat\xi_n)\quad\text{on }\mathcal V_n,
 \qquad
 \widehat z=\zeta(\widehat\theta_n)=0\quad\text{on }\mathcal V_n^c.
\tag{2}
\]

The contour-projector and polar-frame formulas (4)--(6) in the proof of \(\text{thm:rank-adaptive-estimation}\) show that the truncated active image has a differentiable local frame whenever the positive singular values are separated from zero.  In such a frame, \(F,q,\beta^\star,E,L^\star,H^\star,K^\star\), and \(Z^\star\) are compositions of multiplication, inversion at nonsingular matrices, the scalar logarithm on \((0,1)\), and a finite matrix polynomial.  Thus \(g_r\) is Fr\'{e}chet differentiable at each population point.  Identity (1) says that, on the open validity neighborhood, \(g_r=\zeta\circ\Phi_{\Delta,r}\circ\mathcal C\), including when a population signed-jump coordinate is zero.  This proves the differentiability claimed in the theorem; it neither requires nor asserts differentiability of either positive part separately.

The conditioning bounds (13)--(16) in \(\text{thm:rank-adaptive-estimation}\), \(\text{ass:rank-gap}\), \(\text{ass:decay-margin}\), \(\text{ass:parameter-envelope}\), and \(\text{ass:intensity-gap}\) give a common derivative bound and a common modulus of continuity.  There are only finitely many ranks.  Hence, on the uniformly likely event \(\{\widehat r_n=r(\theta)\}\cap\mathcal V_n\), Taylor expansion gives
\[
 \sqrt n\{\widehat z-\zeta(\theta)\}
 =J_z(\theta)\sqrt n\{\widehat\xi_n-\xi(\theta)\}+o_{P_\theta}(1),
 \qquad
 J_z(\theta)=D(\zeta\circ\Phi_{\Delta,r(\theta)}\circ\mathcal C)_{\xi(\theta)},
\tag{3}
\]
with a remainder uniform over \(\mathcal M_{\Delta,p}(\kappa)\).  Uniform rank and validity consistency follow from \(\text{thm:rank-adaptive-estimation}\).  Applying the bounded linear maps \(J_z(\theta)\) to the uniform Gaussian approximation in \(\text{lem:bin-moment-limit}\), then using (2)--(3), proves
\[
 \sup_{\theta:P_\theta\in\mathcal M_{\Delta,p}(\kappa)}
 d_{\mathrm{BL}}\!\left(
 \mathcal L_\theta[\sqrt n\{\zeta(\widehat\theta_n)-\zeta(\theta)\}],
 \mathcal N\{0,W(\theta)\}\right)\longrightarrow0,
\tag{4}
\]
where \(W(\theta)=J_z(\theta)\Omega(\theta)J_z(\theta)^{\mathsf T}\).

We next verify the covariance used for calibration.  Write \(v_s=Y_s-\overline Y_n\) and \(N_n=n-2\).  The Bartlett estimator in \(\text{def:hac-estimator}\) is exactly
\[
 \widehat\Omega_n=\frac1{N_n}\sum_{s,t=0}^{N_n-1}
 \left(1-\frac{|s-t|}{b_n}\right)_+v_sv_t^{\mathsf T}.
\tag{5}
\]
For integer \(b_n\geq1\),
\[
 \left(1-\frac{|s-t|}{b_n}\right)_+
 =\frac1{b_n}\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb Z}
 \mathbf1_{\{s\leq\ell<s+b_n\}}
 \mathbf1_{\{t\leq\ell<t+b_n\}},
\tag{6}
\]
a Gram representation.  Hence \(\widehat\Omega_n\succeq0\).  Its signed-score sandwich is positive semidefinite on \(\mathcal V_n\), and it is zero, hence positive semidefinite, on \(\mathcal V_n^c\).  By \(\text{lem:bin-moment-limit}\), \(\widehat\Omega_n\to\Omega(\theta)\) uniformly.  Derivative continuity, (3), and uniform validity then give, for every \(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
 \sup_{\theta:P_\theta\in\mathcal M_{\Delta,p}(\kappa)}
 P_\theta\{\lVert\widehat W_n-W(\theta)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}>\varepsilon\}
 \longrightarrow0.
\tag{7}
\]

The accepted optional full-parameter object is also fully explicit.  The event
\[
 \mathcal D_n=\mathcal V_n\cap
 \{Z_{ij}^\star\neq0\text{ for every }1\leq i<j\leq p\}
\tag{8}
\]
is measurable: \(\mathcal V_n\) is measurable by \(\text{def:rank-adaptive-estimator}\), every starred map is Borel on it, and (8) is a finite intersection of inverse images of \(\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}\).  On \(\mathcal D_n\), every positive-part coordinate is differentiable and the full sandwich \(\widehat V_n\) is defined; on \(\mathcal D_n^c\) it is the declared zero placeholder and the optional parameter set uses its full-space fallback.  Neither object enters (4) or graph calibration.

It remains to justify calibration when \(W(\theta)\) is singular.  Define the raw-moment direction
\[
 v_\theta=\{0,\operatorname{vec}(\Gamma_1),\operatorname{vec}(\Gamma_2)\}.
\]
Along \(\xi+s v_\theta\), the mean is fixed and both centered lag covariances are multiplied by \(1+s\).  Thus the active image is fixed, \(R\) is multiplied by \(1+s\), and direct cancellation in the formula for \(F\) gives \(F(s)=F\).  Consequently \(q,E,L^\star,H^\star\) are fixed while \(K^\star,Z^\star\), and \(g_r\) are multiplied by \(1+s\).  Therefore
\[
 J_z(\theta)v_\theta=\zeta(\theta).
\tag{9}
\]
The moment envelope gives \(\lVert v_\theta\rVert_2\leq C_0\).  Since the support is nonempty and \(\text{ass:edge-gap}\) holds, some coordinate \(a=a(\theta)\) has \(|\zeta_a(\theta)|\geq\kappa\).  If \(j_a\) is the corresponding row of \(J_z(\theta)\), (9) implies \(\lVert j_a\rVert_2\geq\kappa/C_0\).  By \(\text{ass:variance-gap}\),
\[
 W_{aa}(\theta)=j_a\Omega(\theta)j_a^{\mathsf T}
 \geq\kappa\lVert j_a\rVert_2^2
 \geq c_\star:=\kappa^3/C_0^2>0.
\tag{10}
\]
The uniform derivative and long-run-variance bounds also give \(\lVert W(\theta)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq C_1\).

Let \(G\sim\mathcal N(0,W(\theta))\) and \(M=\max_{1\leq a\leq d}|G_a|\).  For \(0<\varepsilon<1\) and \(x\geq0\), if \(x\leq\sqrt\varepsilon\), (10) and the bounded density of its nondegenerate coordinate give
\[
 P(x<M\leq x+\varepsilon)
 \leq P\{|G_a|\leq\sqrt\varepsilon+\varepsilon\}
 \leq C_2\sqrt\varepsilon.
\]
If \(x>\sqrt\varepsilon\), the event requires at least one coordinate absolute value to lie in \((x,x+\varepsilon]\).  For a centered normal coordinate of variance \(s^2>0\), its absolute-value density at \(y>0\), maximized over \(s\), is at most \(C/y\); a zero-variance coordinate contributes zero.  A union bound gives
\[
 \sup_{\theta}\sup_{x\geq0}P_\theta(x<M\leq x+\varepsilon)
 \leq C_3d\sqrt\varepsilon.
\tag{11}
\]
Thus the Gaussian-maximum laws have a uniform continuous modulus despite possible singular covariance.

For a positive semidefinite matrix \(V\), set
\[
 F_V(x)=P\!\left\{\max_{a\leq d}|(V^{1/2}U)_a|\leq x\right\},
 \qquad U\sim\mathcal N(0,I_d),
\]
and use the generalized-inverse convention
\[
 c(V;1-\alpha)=\inf\{x\in[0,\infty):F_V(x)\geq1-\alpha\},
 \qquad \inf\varnothing=+\infty.
\tag{12}
\]
This defines the conditional critical value even for singular \(V\).  On the operator-norm bounded positive-semidefinite set, the square-root map is uniformly continuous: this follows by diagonalizing two matrices, approximating \(x\mapsto\sqrt x\) uniformly by polynomials on the common spectral interval, and using continuity of each polynomial.  Coupling \(\widehat W_n^{1/2}U\) and \(W(\theta)^{1/2}U\), then applying (7), (11), and Markov's inequality to \(\lVert U\rVert_2\), proves
\[
 \sup_{x\geq0}|F_{\widehat W_n}(x)-F_{W(\theta)}(x)|=o_{P_\theta}(1)
\tag{13}
\]
uniformly on \(\mathcal V_n\).

Let \(c_{n,1-\alpha}=c(\widehat W_n;1-\alpha)\), exactly as in the corrected \(\text{def:graph-confidence-set}\).  On the event that the left side of (13) is at most \(\delta\), the generalized-inverse definition implies
\[
 c_{n,1-\alpha}\geq
 \inf\{x:F_{W(\theta)}(x)\geq1-\alpha-\delta\}.
\tag{14}
\]
The vector bounded-Lipschitz approximation (4), with upper and lower Lipschitz approximations to the maximum rectangle and boundary error bounded by (11), applies at the deterministic threshold on the right of (14).  Letting first \(n\to\infty\) and then \(\delta\downarrow0\) yields
\[
 \inf_{\theta:P_\theta\in\mathcal M_{\Delta,p}(\kappa)}
 P_\theta\!\left[
 \max_{i<j}\left|\sqrt n\{\widehat z_{ij}-\zeta_{ij}(\theta)\}\right|
 \leq c_{n,1-\alpha},\ \mathcal V_n\right]
 \geq1-\alpha-o(1)-\sup_\theta P_\theta(\mathcal V_n^c).
\tag{15}
\]
On \(\mathcal V_n^c\), every interval is \(\mathbb R\), so simultaneous coordinate coverage is automatic.  Combining this fallback with (15) and uniform validity proves
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{\theta:P_\theta\in\mathcal M_{\Delta,p}(\kappa)}
 P_\theta\{\zeta_{ij}(\theta)\in I_{ij,n}\text{ for all }i<j\}
 \geq1-\alpha.
\tag{16}
\]

Finally, nonnegative excitation and acyclicity imply that at most one of \(A_{ij}\) and \(A_{ji}\) is positive.  Since every \(\lambda_i>0\), \(\zeta_{ij}>0\) means \(j\to i\), \(\zeta_{ij}<0\) means \(i\to j\), and \(\zeta_{ij}=0\) means neither edge.  On the event in (16), every true pairwise choice is admitted by the sign-inversion rules, and the true graph is acyclic; hence it belongs to \(\mathcal C_n(1-\alpha)\).  On \(\mathcal V_n^c\), that set contains every labeled acyclic graph.  Therefore
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{\theta:P_\theta\in\mathcal M_{\Delta,p}(\kappa)}
 P_\theta\{G(A)\in\mathcal C_n(1-\alpha)\}\geq1-\alpha.
\]
