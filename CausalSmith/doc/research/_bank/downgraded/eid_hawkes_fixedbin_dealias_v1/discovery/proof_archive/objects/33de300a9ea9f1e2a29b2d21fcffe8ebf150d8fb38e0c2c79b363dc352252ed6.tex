Fix \(\gamma\in\mathfrak J_{\Delta,p}^{\mathrm{WH}}\) and choose an arbitrary admissible representative \((\beta,A,\mu)\), with population mean \(m\).  By the two image definitions,
\[
 \eta=(m,\Gamma_1,\Gamma_2)\in\mathfrak I_{\Delta,p}^{\mathrm{WH}}.
\tag{1}
\]
Apply \(\text{thm:stable-nonzero-two-lag-wiener-hopf-recovery}\) to (1).  Its proof through equations (2)--(8) uses only \((\Gamma_1,\Gamma_2)\), not \(m\), to establish totality of the operations
\(r,S,O,R,F,L^{\mathrm{WH}},H^{\mathrm{WH}},K^{\mathrm{WH}}\) and to prove
\[
 r=\operatorname{rank}(A),\quad S=\operatorname{im}(A),\quad
 F=[T|_S]_O,\quad L^{\mathrm{WH}}=[B|_S]_O,\quad
 H^{\mathrm{WH}}=[J|_S]_O^{-1},\quad K^{\mathrm{WH}}=K,
\tag{2}
\]
\[
 C_{+}^{\mathrm{WH}}(t)=C_{+}(t),\qquad t>0.
\tag{3}
\]
The same theorem proves that (2)--(3) are basis invariant.

The curve in (3) extends continuously to zero, so the integral in \(\text{def:wiener-hopf-covariance-only-inverse}\) is finite.  Substituting (3) into \(\text{lem:zero-lag-bin-covariance}\) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 D^{\mathrm{WH},0}
 &=\Delta^{-1}\!\left[
 \Gamma_0-\int_0^\Delta(\Delta-t)
 \{C_{+}(t)+C_{+}(t)^{\mathsf T}\}\,dt\right]\\
 &=D\succ0.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
Therefore \(\lambda^{\mathrm{WH},0}=\operatorname{diag}(D)=\lambda\) and, using the mean identity in that lemma,
\[
 m^{\mathrm{WH},0}=\Delta\lambda=m.
\tag{5}
\]
The argument of \(\Phi_{\Delta,p}^{\mathrm{WH}}\) in the covariance-only definition is consequently exactly the triple in (1).  The mean-plus-two-lag theorem now yields
\[
 \Psi_{\Delta,p}^{\mathrm{WH},0}(\gamma)
 =\Phi_{\Delta,p}^{\mathrm{WH}}(m,\Gamma_1,\Gamma_2)
 =(\beta,A,\mu).
\tag{6}
\]
Equations (2)--(6) show that every operation is defined.  Their left sides depend only on \(\gamma\), the known \(\Delta\), and an inessential basis; because the representative was arbitrary, they also prove representative independence and structural uniqueness.
