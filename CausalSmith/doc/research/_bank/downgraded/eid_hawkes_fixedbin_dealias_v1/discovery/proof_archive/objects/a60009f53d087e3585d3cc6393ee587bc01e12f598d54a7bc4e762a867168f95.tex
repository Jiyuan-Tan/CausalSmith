Write \(d_0=p+2p^2\).  We first record the stochastic rate that is needed for the fixed-law assertion.  By \(\text{lem:uniform-bin-eighth-moment}\), under any fixed law every coordinate of \(Y_0\) has a finite fourth moment, and by \(\text{lem:finite-depth-geometric-mixing}\) its strong-mixing coefficients decrease geometrically.  These two facts imply a summable covariance bound without requiring nonsingularity of the long-run covariance.  Indeed, for centered scalar coordinates \(U=Y_{0,a}-\mathbb E Y_{0,a}\) and \(V=Y_{h,b}-\mathbb E Y_{h,b}\), let \(U_M\) and \(V_M\) denote clipping to \([-M,M]\).  The definition of the strong-mixing coefficient gives
\[
 |\operatorname{Cov}(U_M,V_M)|\leq 4M^2\alpha_Y(h).
\]
Moreover, by Cauchy--Schwarz and Markov's inequality,
\[
 \lVert U-U_M\rVert_2
 \leq \{\mathbb E(U^2\mathbf 1_{\{|U|>M\}})\}^{1/2}
 \leq \frac{\lVert U\rVert_4^2}{M},
\]
and the analogous inequality holds for \(V\).  Expanding the covariance after adding and subtracting \(U_M,V_M\), and applying Cauchy--Schwarz to every remainder, therefore yields
\[
 |\operatorname{Cov}(U,V)|
 \leq 4M^2\alpha_Y(h)+\frac{C}{M}.
\]
When \(\alpha_Y(h)>0\), taking \(M=\max\{1,\alpha_Y(h)^{-1/6}\}\) gives
\[
 |\operatorname{Cov}(U,V)|\leq C'\alpha_Y(h)^{1/6}.
\]
When \(\alpha_Y(h)=0\), first applying the bounded covariance inequality to the clipped variables and then sending \(M\to\infty\) gives the same bound with both sides zero.
The geometric bound in \(\text{lem:finite-depth-geometric-mixing}\) makes the right-hand side summable in \(h\).  Consequently, componentwise expansion of the variance of the sample mean gives
\[
 \mathbb E\left\lVert\sqrt n(\widehat\xi_n-\xi)\right\rVert_2^2
 \leq C''<\infty,
\]
with a law-dependent constant.  Thus \(\sqrt n(\widehat\xi_n-\xi)=O_P(1)\).  Since the centering map in \(\text{def:centering-map}\) is a quadratic polynomial and \(\widehat\xi_n\to\xi\) by \(\text{lem:bin-moment-limit}\),
\[
 \sqrt n\lVert\widehat\eta_n-\eta\rVert=O_P(1).
\tag{1}
\]

Let \(r=\operatorname{rank}(\Gamma_1)\).  By \(\text{thm:two-lag-exact-recovery}\) and \(A\neq0\) in \(\text{def:model-class}\), \(r\geq1\).  The singular-value min--max formula and the triangle inequality imply, for every \(s\),
\[
 \left|\sigma_s(\widehat\Gamma_{1,n})-\sigma_s(\Gamma_1)\right|
 \leq\lVert\widehat\Gamma_{1,n}-\Gamma_1\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}.
\tag{2}
\]
At a fixed law, \(\sigma_r(\Gamma_1)>0\) and \(\sigma_s(\Gamma_1)=0\) for \(s>r\).  Combining (1), (2), \(t_n\downarrow0\), and \(\sqrt n\,t_n\to\infty\) proves
\[
 P(\widehat r_n=r)\longrightarrow1.
\tag{3}
\]

We next justify the local regularity of the inverse in a basis-free way.  Put \(M_1=\Gamma_1\Gamma_1^{\mathsf T}\).  Its positive eigenvalues are \(\sigma_1(\Gamma_1)^2,\ldots,\sigma_r(\Gamma_1)^2\), and its remaining eigenvalues are zero.  Choose a closed contour \(\mathfrak c\) separating these two finite spectral sets.  Diagonalizing the real symmetric matrix \(M_1\) verifies directly that its active projector is
\[
 P_S=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm i}\int_{\mathfrak c}(zI_p-M_1)^{-1}\,dz.
\tag{4}
\]
For a perturbation \(H\), the resolvent identity is
\[
 (zI_p-M_1-H)^{-1}-(zI_p-M_1)^{-1}
 =(zI_p-M_1-H)^{-1}H(zI_p-M_1)^{-1}.
\tag{5}
\]
The distance from \(\mathfrak c\) to the spectrum is positive.  Applying (5) once gives local Lipschitz continuity of (4); applying it a second time shows that the remainder after the linear term is \(O(\lVert H\rVert_{mathrm{op}}^2)\).  Thus the rank-\(r\) spectral projector is Fr\'{e}chet differentiable.  If \(O_0\) is any fixed orthonormal basis of the population space, then
\[
 O(P)=P O_0\{O_0^{\mathsf T}P O_0\}^{-1/2}
\tag{6}
\]
is a differentiable orthonormal frame for every projector \(P\) sufficiently close to \(P_S\).  Formula (6) proves local differentiability without making a globally meaningful choice of singular-vector signs.

Changing a frame from \(O\) to \(O U\), where \(U\) is orthogonal, changes
\[
 R\mapsto U^{\mathsf T}R,\quad
 F\mapsto U^{\mathsf T}FU,\quad
 E\mapsto U^{\mathsf T}EU,\quad
 L^\star\mapsto U^{\mathsf T}L^\star U,\quad
 H^\star\mapsto U^{\mathsf T}H^\star U.
\]
It follows equation by equation that \(O(H^\star)^2R\), its skew part, \(A^\star\), and \(\mu^\star\) are unchanged.  Hence the empirical construction in \(\text{def:rank-adaptive-estimator}\) is basis invariant wherever it is valid.

For a fixed law, all denominators and inverses in \(\text{def:two-lag-inverse}\) are separated from their boundaries: \(RR^{\mathsf T}\) is positive definite, \(q=e^{-\beta\Delta}\in(0,1)\), \(F-I_r\) is invertible, \(\lambda_j=m_j/\Delta>0\), and
\[
 (I_p-A/\beta)^{-1}=\sum_{d=0}^{p-1}(A/\beta)^d
\tag{7}
\]
because acyclicity makes \(A^p=0\).  Equations (4)--(7), continuity of inversion, smoothness of \(-\Delta^{-1}\log q\), and the finite polynomial defining \(L^\star\) show that the inverse before positive parts is locally Lipschitz.  Since \(x\mapsto\max\{x,0\}\) is globally one-Lipschitz, the complete inverse is locally Lipschitz as well.  Therefore (1), (3), and the sentinel definition imply
\[
 \widehat\theta_n\longrightarrow(\beta,A,\mu)\quad\text{in probability},
\quad
 \sqrt n\lVert\widehat A_n-A\rVert_{\mathrm F}=O_P(1).
\tag{8}
\]
The probability of the invalid-input event tends to zero because every strict condition defining \(\mathcal V_n\) holds at the population point and is preserved in a neighborhood of that point.

For support recovery at a fixed law, let \(a_{\min}=\min\{A_{ij}:A_{ij}>0\}>0\), which exists because the graph is finite and nonempty.  For all sufficiently large \(n\), \(a_n<a_{\min}/2\).  On
\[
 \lVert\widehat A_n-A\rVert_{\max}<\min\{a_n,a_{\min}/2\},
\]
no absent edge is selected and no present edge is deleted.  By (8) and \(\sqrt n,a_n\to\infty\), the probability of the complementary event tends to zero.  Thus \(P\{\widehat G_n=G(A)\}\to1\).

It remains to establish the uniform assertions.  On \(\mathcal M_{\Delta,p}(\kappa)\), \(\text{lem:bin-moment-limit}\) gives uniform root-\(n\) tightness of \(\widehat\xi_n-\xi(\theta)\), and the eighth-moment bound makes \(m,\Gamma_1,\Gamma_2\) uniformly bounded.  The centering derivative is consequently uniformly bounded, so
\[
 \sqrt n\lVert\widehat\eta_n-\eta(\theta)\rVert=O_P(1)
\quad\text{uniformly in }\theta.
\tag{9}
\]
By \(\text{ass:rank-gap}\), the positive singular values of \(\Gamma_1\) are at least \(\kappa\).  Equation (2) and (9) then give
\[
 \sup_{\theta:P_\theta\in\mathcal M_{\Delta,p}(\kappa)}
 P_\theta(\widehat r_n\neq r(\theta))\longrightarrow0.
\tag{10}
\]
Indeed, true singular values remain above \(t_n\) because \(t_n\to0\), whereas every population zero singular value remains below \(t_n\) with probability tending uniformly to one because \(\sqrt n,t_n\to\infty\).

The conditioning constants in the inverse are also uniform.  First,
\[
 \lambda_{\min}(RR^{\mathsf T})=\sigma_r(\Gamma_1)^2\geq\kappa^2.
\tag{11}
\]
Second, \(\text{ass:decay-margin}\) gives \(q\in[\kappa,1-\kappa]\).  Third, the moment bound and (11) bound \(\lVert F\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\).  By \(\text{thm:two-lag-exact-recovery}\), every eigenvalue of \(F\) equals \(q\), including repeated-pole and singular cases, whence
\[
 |\det(F-I_r)|=(1-q)^r\geq\kappa^r.
\tag{12}
\]
For an \(r\times r\) matrix \(M\), the product formula for singular values gives
\[
 \sigma_{\min}(M)
 \geq\frac{|\det M|}{\lVert M\rVert_{mathrm{op}}^{r-1}}.
\]
Thus (12) and the bound on \(F\) give a uniform bound for \((F-I_r)^{-1}\).  The finite logarithm has degree at most \(p-1\), so its derivative is uniformly bounded when \(q^{-1}\) and \(F\) are bounded.  Assumption \(\text{ass:intensity-gap}\) gives \(\lambda_j\geq\kappa\) for every edge division.  Finally, \(\beta\geq-\Delta^{-1}\log(1-\kappa)>0\) and \(\text{ass:parameter-envelope}\), together with (7), uniformly bound the inverse of \(I_p-A/\beta\).  Hence every strict validity condition in \(\mathcal V_n\) persists on one common neighborhood, and (4)--(6) give a common local Lipschitz constant for the basis-invariant inverse.  Combining this fact with (9), (10), and the sentinel fallback proves
\[
 \lim_{M\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}
 \sup_{\theta:P_\theta\in\mathcal M_{\Delta,p}(\kappa)}
 P_\theta\!\left[
 \sqrt n\left\{|\widehat\beta_n-\beta|+
 \lVert\widehat A_n-A\rVert_{\mathrm F}+
 \lVert\widehat\mu_n-\mu\rVert_2\right\}>M\right]=0.
\]
The exceptional sentinel event contributes at most the probability in (10) plus the probability that (9) leaves the common validity neighborhood, both of which tend uniformly to zero.  The asserted uniform consistency of \(\widehat\Omega_n\) is the last conclusion of \(\text{lem:bin-moment-limit}\), applied to the Bartlett kernel in the corrected \(\text{def:hac-estimator}\).  This completes all fixed-law and uniform conclusions.  No Gaussian limit for the full positive-part amplitude map has been used or asserted.
