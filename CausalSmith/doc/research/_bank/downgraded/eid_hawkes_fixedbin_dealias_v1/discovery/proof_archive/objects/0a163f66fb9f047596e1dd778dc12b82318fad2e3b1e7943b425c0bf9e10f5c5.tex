Rank is unchanged by relabeling, so choose canonical labels.  A directed chain has positive amplitudes in positions \((2,1)\) and \((3,2)\); its first two columns are linearly independent, and hence its excitation matrix has rank two.  A transitive triangle has positive entries in positions \((2,1),(3,1),(3,2)\); again its first two columns are linearly independent, so its rank is two.  A fork may be written
\[
 A_{21}=a>0,\qquad A_{31}=b>0,
\]
with all other entries zero, and its two nonzero rows are proportional; hence its rank is one.  By \(\text{lem:covariance-semigroup}\), \(\operatorname{rank}(\Gamma_1)=\operatorname{rank}(A)\).  This proves every rank-stratum assertion.

For the fork, \(A^2=0\).  Put
\[
 x=a/\beta,qquad y=b/\beta,qquad
 c(\beta)=\frac{1-e^{-\beta\Delta}}{\beta}.
\tag{1}
\]
The Lyapunov integral from \(\text{lem:covariance-semigroup}\), using \(e^{Bu}=e^{-\beta u}(I_p+Au)\), gives
\[
 Q=\frac{D}{2\beta}+\frac{AD+DA^{\mathsf T}}{4\beta^2}
   +\frac{ADA^{\mathsf T}}{4\beta^3}.
\tag{2}
\]
Substitution into \(K=A(2\beta I_p-A)Q\), or equivalently into \(K=AD+AQA^{\mathsf T}\), yields
\[
 K_{21}=a\lambda_1,\quad K_{31}=b\lambda_1,quad
 K_{22}=\frac{a^2\lambda_1}{2\beta},quad
 K_{23}=K_{32}=\frac{ab\lambda_1}{2\beta},quad
 K_{33}=\frac{b^2\lambda_1}{2\beta},
\tag{3}
\]
and all other entries are zero.  In particular \(AK=0\).  Since
\[
 J=\int_0^\Delta e^{-\beta u}(I_p+Au)\,du=c(\beta)I_p+d(\beta)A
\]
for a scalar \(d(\beta)\), equations \(A^2=0\) and \(AK=0\) imply
\[
 \Gamma_1=J^2K=c(\beta)^2K.
\tag{4}
\]

The one-lag observable contains \(m\), so it identifies \(\lambda=m/\Delta\); every \(\lambda_i\) is positive.  From (3)--(4),
\[
 x=\frac{2(\Gamma_1)_{22}}{(\Gamma_1)_{21}},qquad
 y=\frac{2(\Gamma_1)_{33}}{(\Gamma_1)_{31}},qquad
 h(\beta):=\frac{(1-e^{-\beta\Delta})^2}{\beta}
 =\frac{(\Gamma_1)_{21}}{x\lambda_1}.
\tag{5}
\]
All divisions in (5) are valid because \(a,b,\lambda_1,c(\beta)>0\).  Conversely, \((\lambda,x,y,h(\beta))\) reconstructs every entry in (3)--(4).  It also fixes
\[
 \mu=(I_p-A/\beta)\lambda
 =(\lambda_1,\lambda_2-x\lambda_1,\lambda_3-y\lambda_1)^{\mathsf T}.
\tag{6}
\]
Thus a candidate decay \(\widetilde\beta>0\) gives a legal point in the same one-lag fiber exactly when \(h(\widetilde\beta)=h(\beta)\), with amplitudes \(\widetilde a=x\widetilde\beta\), \(\widetilde b=y\widetilde\beta\), and the same \(\lambda,\mu\).  Positivity in (6) is preserved because its right side does not depend on the candidate decay.

Set \(z=\beta\Delta\).  Since
\[
 h(\beta)=\Delta\frac{(1-e^{-z})^2}{z},
\]
logarithmic differentiation gives
\[
 \frac{d}{dz}\log\frac{(1-e^{-z})^2}{z}
 =\frac{2}{e^z-1}-\frac1z.
\tag{7}
\]
The derivative in (7) vanishes exactly when \(e^z=1+2z\).  The function \(e^z-1-2z\) is strictly convex, is zero at \(z=0\), has derivative \(-1\) there, and tends to infinity.  It therefore has exactly one further zero \(z_*>0\).  Equation (7) is positive on \((0,z_*)\) and negative on \((z_*,\infty)\).  Moreover \(h(\beta)\to0\) at both endpoints \(0\) and \(\infty\).  Hence every value strictly below \(h(\beta_*)\) has exactly one preimage on each side of \(\beta_*=z_*/\Delta\), whereas the maximum has the single preimage \(\beta_*\).  Together with (5)--(6), this proves the asserted fiber cardinalities and injectivity locus.

For any alias \(\theta'\neq\theta\), (4) and \(A\Gamma_1=0\) give
\[
 \Gamma_2=T\Gamma_1=e^{-\beta\Delta}(I_p+A\Delta)\Gamma_1
 =e^{-\beta\Delta}\Gamma_1.
\tag{8}
\]
Because \(\Gamma_1\neq0\) and \(\beta'\neq\beta\), the two laws have different second-lag covariances by (8).  Their finite-block binned distributions therefore cannot all agree, so the definition of \(\rho_{\mathrm{law}}\) gives \(\rho_{\mathrm{law}}(P_\theta,P_{\theta'})>0\).

Finally, \(\beta\mapsto\beta\) is continuous in \(\rho_\Theta\), so the two strict-inequality subsets are relatively open.  They are nonempty: for any \(x,y>0\) and any decay on the desired side of \(\beta_*\), choose \(\lambda_1>0\), \(\lambda_2>x\lambda_1\), and \(\lambda_3>y\lambda_1\), then define \(a=x\beta\), \(b=y\beta\), and \(\mu\) by (6).  This produces a positive-baseline stationary acyclic Hawkes parameter in the required subset.
