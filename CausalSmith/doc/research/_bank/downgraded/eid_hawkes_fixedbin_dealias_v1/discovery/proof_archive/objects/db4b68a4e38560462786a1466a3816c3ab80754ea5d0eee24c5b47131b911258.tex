Fix \(\eta\in\mathfrak I_{\Delta,p}^{\mathrm{WH}}\) and choose an arbitrary admissible representative \((\beta,A,\mu)\).  By \(\text{def:stable-nonzero-restricted-strip-moment-image}\),
\[
 A\neq0,\quad
 \operatorname{spec}\!\left(\left.\Delta B\right|_{\operatorname{im}(A)}\right)
 \subset\{z\in\mathbb C:|\operatorname{Im}z|<\pi\}.
\tag{1}
\]
By \(\text{lem:stable-covariance-semigroup}\),
\[
 S=\operatorname{im}(\Gamma_1)=\operatorname{im}(A)=\operatorname{im}(K),\quad
 r=\operatorname{rank}(A)\geq1,\quad
 \Gamma_1=J^2K,\quad \Gamma_2=T\Gamma_1,
\tag{2}
\]
and \(B\) is Hurwitz, \(D\succ0\), and \(J\) is invertible.  The inequality \(r\geq1\) follows from \(A\neq0\).  Since \(A\operatorname{im}(A)\subseteq\operatorname{im}(A)\), the space \(S\) is invariant under \(A\), hence under \(B=A-\beta I_p\), \(T=e^{B\Delta}\), and \(J=\int_0^\Delta e^{Bu}\,du\).

Choose an admissible orthonormal basis \(O\) and put \(R=O^{\mathsf T}\Gamma_1\).  From (2), \(\Gamma_1=OR\).  Because both \(O\) and \(\Gamma_1\) have rank \(r\), \(R\) has full row rank, so \(RR^{\mathsf T}\succ0\).  There is a unique matrix \(F_0\) with \(TO=OF_0\).  Using \(\Gamma_2=T\Gamma_1\),
\[
 O^{\mathsf T}\Gamma_2R^{\mathsf T}(RR^{\mathsf T})^{-1}
 =F_0RR^{\mathsf T}(RR^{\mathsf T})^{-1}=F_0.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus \(F=F_0=[T|_S]_O\).  Put \(L_0=O^{\mathsf T}BO\); invariance gives \(BO=OL_0\), whence
\[
 F=e^{\Delta L_0}.
\tag{4}
\]
The restricted strip (1), (4), and \(\text{lem:principal-log-strip}\), applied in dimension \(r\), imply that the principal logarithm exists and
\[
 L^{\mathrm{WH}}=\Delta^{-1}\operatorname{Log}(F)=L_0=[B|_S]_O.
\tag{5}
\]
Because \(B|_S\) is Hurwitz, \(L^{\mathrm{WH}}\) is invertible, and every eigenvalue of \(F\) has modulus less than one; hence \(F-I_r\) is invertible.

Let \(J_S=O^{\mathsf T}JO\).  Integrating \(e^{Bu}O=Oe^{L_0u}\) and using (5) gives
\[
 J_S=\int_0^\Delta e^{L_0u}\,du
 =L_0^{-1}(F-I_r),\quad
 H^{\mathrm{WH}}=L^{\mathrm{WH}}(F-I_r)^{-1}=J_S^{-1}.
\tag{6}
\]
By (2), write \(K=OK_S\).  Since \(J^2O=OJ_S^2\), equations (2) and (6) yield
\[
 R=O^{\mathsf T}\Gamma_1=J_S^2K_S,\quad
 K^{\mathrm{WH}}=O(H^{\mathrm{WH}})^2R
 =OJ_S^{-2}J_S^2K_S=K.
\tag{7}
\]
For \(t>0\), (5), (7), and \(\text{lem:stable-covariance-semigroup}\) therefore prove
\[
 C_{+}^{\mathrm{WH}}(t)
 =Oe^{L^{\mathrm{WH}}t}(H^{\mathrm{WH}})^2R
 =e^{Bt}K=C_{+}(t).
\tag{8}
\]
This proves, using only \((\Gamma_1,\Gamma_2)\), exact reconstruction of the entire positive-lag covariance density, not merely its values inside one bin.

Stationarity and the compensator identity give
\[
 m=\mathbb E\{N(\Delta)-N(0)\}
 =\mathbb E\int_0^\Delta\ell(u)\,du=\Delta\lambda.
\tag{9}
\]
Because \(\Delta>0\), \(\lambda^{\mathrm{WH}}=m/\Delta=\lambda\), and hence \(D^{\mathrm{WH}}=D\succ0\).  Thus the inverse used in the definition of \(g^{\mathrm{WH}}\) exists.  For \(t>0\), the response orientation in \(\text{def:complete-hawkes-response}\) and (8) give
\[
 g^{\mathrm{WH}}(t)=C_{+}(t)D^{-1}=g(t).
\tag{10}
\]
For \(t<0\), stationarity gives
\(\operatorname{Cov}(dN(t),dN(0))=C_{+}(-t)^{\mathsf T}dt^2\); hence the second branch of the definition also equals \(g(t)\).  The chosen values at zero agree and are irrelevant to the convolution.  Therefore \(g^{\mathrm{WH}}=g\) almost everywhere on \(\mathbb R\).

The true kernel \(\phi(t)=Ae^{-\beta t}\mathbf 1_{\{t>0\}}\) is causal and entrywise integrable, and
\[
 \left(\int_0^\infty\phi^{ij}(t)\,dt\right)_{i,j}=A/\beta,\quad
 \rho(A/\beta)<1
\tag{11}
\]
by stable-class membership.  Therefore \(\text{lem:complete-response-orientation}\) and \(\text{lem:bacry-muzy-wiener-hopf-uniqueness}\), with the orientation verified in (10), say that \(\phi\) is the unique causal entrywise-\(L^1\) solution of the Wiener--Hopf equation driven by \(g^{\mathrm{WH}}\).  Existence and uniqueness of the defining operation follow, and
\[
 \chi^{\mathrm{WH}}(t)=\phi(t)=Ae^{-\beta t}\mathbf 1_{\{t>0\}}.
\tag{12}
\]
This citation is used only for uniqueness from the already reconstructed complete response; it supplies no fixed-bin reconstruction step.

Since \(\beta>0\), entrywise integration of (12) gives
\[
 M_0^{\mathrm{WH}}=\int_0^\infty Ae^{-\beta t}\,dt=A/\beta,\quad
 M_1^{\mathrm{WH}}=\int_0^\infty tAe^{-\beta t}\,dt=A/\beta^2.
\tag{13}
\]
Put \(a_+=\mathbf 1_p^{\mathsf T}A\mathbf 1_p\).  Because \(A\geq0\) and \(A\neq0\), \(a_+>0\).  Hence
\[
 u_0^{\mathrm{WH}}=a_+/\beta>0,\quad
 u_1^{\mathrm{WH}}=a_+/\beta^2>0,\quad
 \beta^{\mathrm{WH}}=u_0^{\mathrm{WH}}/u_1^{\mathrm{WH}}=\beta,
\tag{14}
\]
so every division in the inverse is valid and
\[
 A^{\mathrm{WH}}=\beta^{\mathrm{WH}}M_0^{\mathrm{WH}}=A.
\tag{15}
\]
Taking expectations in the intensity equation, as in the proof of \(\text{lem:stable-covariance-semigroup}\), gives
\[
 \lambda=\mu+(A/\beta)\lambda=\mu+M_0^{\mathrm{WH}}\lambda.
\tag{16}
\]
Equations (9), (13), and (16) prove
\[
 \mu^{\mathrm{WH}}=(I_p-M_0^{\mathrm{WH}})\lambda^{\mathrm{WH}}=\mu.
\tag{17}
\]
Thus the displayed recovery map returns the structural representative exactly.

For basis invariance, let \(O'=OU\) be another orthonormal basis of \(S\), where \(U\) is orthogonal.  Direct substitution and uniqueness of the principal logarithm give
\[
 R'=U^{\mathsf T}R,\quad F'=U^{\mathsf T}FU,\quad
 L^{\mathrm{WH}\prime}=U^{\mathsf T}L^{\mathrm{WH}}U,\quad
 H^{\mathrm{WH}\prime}=U^{\mathsf T}H^{\mathrm{WH}}U.
\tag{18}
\]
It follows that
\[
 O'e^{L^{\mathrm{WH}\prime}t}(H^{\mathrm{WH}\prime})^2R'
 =Oe^{L^{\mathrm{WH}}t}(H^{\mathrm{WH}})^2R.
\tag{19}
\]
Thus \(K^{\mathrm{WH}}\), \(C_{+}^{\mathrm{WH}}\), \(g^{\mathrm{WH}}\), the unique \(\chi^{\mathrm{WH}}\), its moments, and the output do not depend on \(O\).  Every reconstructed quantity is consequently a function only of \(\eta\) and the known \(\Delta\).  Since the structural representative was arbitrary, (3)--(17) hold for every admissible representative and force all representatives to equal the same recovered triple.  Exact recovery of the labeled graph follows from \(G(A)=\{j\to i:A_{ij}>0\}\).

Finally, fix \(p=2\), \(\Delta>0\), \(\beta=1\), \(\mu=\mathbf 1_2\), and \(A=\tfrac14\mathbf 1_2\mathbf 1_2^{\mathsf T}\).  The eigenvalues of \(A\) are \(1/2\) and \(0\), so \(\rho(A/\beta)=1/2<1\), while \(A\geq0\), \(A\neq0\), and \(\mu>0\).  The standard immigration--birth construction is locally finite here directly: the expected number of generation-\(d\) descendants is governed by \((A/\beta)^d\), and \(\sum_{d\geq0}(A/\beta)^d=(I_2-A/\beta)^{-1}\) is finite entrywise.  Thus total cluster size has finite expectation and is finite almost surely; continuous exponential offspring delays give simplicity, and stationary Poisson immigration with rate \(\mu\) gives the stated Hawkes intensity.  Hence this triple induces a law in \(\mathcal M_{\Delta,2}^{\mathrm{stab}}\).  Its image is \(S=\operatorname{span}(\mathbf 1_2)\), and \(B|_S=-\tfrac12 I_S\), so the restricted spectrum is real and satisfies (1) for every \(\Delta>0\).  The matrix has determinant zero, both diagonal entries are positive, and both off-diagonal entries are positive.  Therefore it is simultaneously singular, self-exciting, reciprocal, and non-coordinate-image, as claimed.
