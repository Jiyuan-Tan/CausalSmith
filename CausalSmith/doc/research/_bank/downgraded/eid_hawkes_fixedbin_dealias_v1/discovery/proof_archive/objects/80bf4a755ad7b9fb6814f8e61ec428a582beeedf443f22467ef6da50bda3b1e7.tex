We separate the fixed-law and uniform arguments.  Under a fixed law in \(\mathcal M_{\Delta,p}^{\mathrm{DAG},+}\), the fixed-law moment and geometric-mixing conclusions of \(\text{lem:uniform-bin-eighth-moment}\) and \(\text{lem:finite-depth-geometric-mixing}\), together with the clipping calculation (2)--(3) in \(\text{lem:bin-moment-limit}\), imply
\[
 \sqrt n(\widehat\xi_n-\xi)=O_P(1),
 \qquad
 \sqrt n(\widehat\eta_n-\eta)=O_P(1),
\tag{1}
\]
the second relation following from the quadratic centering map in \(\text{def:centering-map}\).

Let \(r=\operatorname{rank}(\Gamma_1)\).  By \(\text{thm:two-lag-exact-recovery}\) and \(A\neq0\) in \(\text{def:model-class}\), \(r\geq1\).  The singular-value min--max formula and the triangle inequality give, for every \(s\),
\[
 |\sigma_s(\widehat\Gamma_{1,n})-\sigma_s(\Gamma_1)|
 \leq\lVert\widehat\Gamma_{1,n}-\Gamma_1\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}.
\tag{2}
\]
At a fixed law \(\sigma_r(\Gamma_1)>0\), while \(\sigma_s(\Gamma_1)=0\) for \(s>r\).  Thus (1)--(2), \(t_n\downarrow0\), and \(\sqrt n\,t_n\to\infty\) imply
\[
 P(\widehat r_n=r)\longrightarrow1.
\tag{3}
\]

We next establish the required local regularity without choosing singular-vector signs.  Put \(M_1=\Gamma_1\Gamma_1^{\mathsf T}\).  Choose a closed contour \(\mathfrak c\) separating its \(r\) positive eigenvalues from zero.  Diagonalization of the real symmetric matrix \(M_1\) verifies that the active projector equals
\[
 P_S=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm i}\int_{\mathfrak c}(zI_p-M_1)^{-1}\,dz.
\tag{4}
\]
For a perturbation \(H\), the algebraic resolvent identity
\[
 (zI_p-M_1-H)^{-1}-(zI_p-M_1)^{-1}
 =(zI_p-M_1-H)^{-1}H(zI_p-M_1)^{-1}
\tag{5}
\]
shows first local Lipschitz continuity and, after one further substitution, a quadratic remainder after the linear term.  Hence the rank-\(r\) spectral projector is Fr\'{e}chet differentiable.  If \(O_0\) is any fixed orthonormal frame of \(S\), then
\[
 O(P)=PO_0\{O_0^{\mathsf T}PO_0\}^{-1/2}
\tag{6}
\]
is a differentiable orthonormal frame for every projector \(P\) close enough to \(P_S\).

If a frame is replaced by \(OU\) for an orthogonal \(U\), the quantities in \(\text{def:two-lag-inverse}\) transform as
\[
 R\mapsto U^{\mathsf T}R,\quad F\mapsto U^{\mathsf T}FU,\quad
 E\mapsto U^{\mathsf T}EU,\quad L^\star\mapsto U^{\mathsf T}L^\star U,\quad
 H^\star\mapsto U^{\mathsf T}H^\star U.
\tag{7}
\]
Therefore \(O(H^\star)^2R\), its skew part, \(A^\star\), and \(\mu^\star\) do not change.  Thus (6) may be used to differentiate the actual basis-invariant empirical inverse even if the measurable singular-value-decomposition rule in \(\text{def:rank-adaptive-estimator}\) chooses another frame.

At the population point every denominator is interior.  Indeed, \(RR^{\mathsf T}\succ0\), \(q=e^{-\beta\Delta}\in(0,1)\), and every eigenvalue of \(F\) is \(q\), so \(F-I_r\) is invertible.  Positive baselines imply \(\lambda_j=m_j/\Delta>0\).  Acyclicity gives \(A^p=0\), and hence
\[
 (I_p-A/\beta)^{-1}=\sum_{d=0}^{p-1}(A/\beta)^d.
\tag{8}
\]
Equations (4)--(8), continuity of matrix inversion, smoothness of \(-\Delta^{-1}\log q\) on \((0,1)\), and the finite polynomial defining \(L^\star\) show that the inverse before positive parts is locally Lipschitz.  The scalar map \(x\mapsto\max\{x,0\}\) is globally one-Lipschitz, so the entire recovery map is locally Lipschitz.  All strict conditions defining \(\mathcal V_n\) hold at the population point and persist in a neighborhood.  Equations (1) and (3), followed by \(\text{thm:two-lag-exact-recovery}\), therefore yield
\[
 \widehat\theta_n\longrightarrow(\beta,A,\mu),
 \qquad
 \sqrt n\lVert\widehat A_n-A\rVert_{\mathrm F}=O_P(1),
 \qquad
 P(\mathcal V_n^c)\longrightarrow0.
\tag{9}
\]
The sentinel is used only on the last event and hence does not affect these limits.

Let \(a_{\min}=\min\{A_{ij}:A_{ij}>0\}>0\); this minimum exists because \(p\) is finite and \(A\neq0\).  For all sufficiently large \(n\), \(a_n<a_{\min}/2\).  On
\[
 \lVert\widehat A_n-A\rVert_{\max}<\min\{a_n,a_{\min}/2\},
\tag{10}
\]
no absent edge is selected and no present edge is deleted.  By (9) and the stated condition \(\sqrt n\,a_n\to\infty\), the probability of the complement of (10) tends to zero.  This proves all fixed-law assertions.

Now fix \(p\), \(\Delta\), and \(\kappa\).  By \(\text{lem:bin-moment-limit}\), \(\sqrt n(\widehat\xi_n-\xi(\theta))\) is uniformly tight.  The uniform moment bound controls \(m,\Gamma_1,\Gamma_2\), so the derivative of the centering map is uniformly bounded and
\[
 \sqrt n\lVert\widehat\eta_n-\eta(\theta)\rVert=O_{P_\theta}(1)
 \quad\text{uniformly in }\theta.
\tag{11}
\]
By \(\text{ass:rank-gap}\), every positive singular value is at least \(\kappa\).  Combining (2), (11), \(t_n\to0\), and \(\sqrt n\,t_n\to\infty\) gives
\[
 \sup_{\theta:P_\theta\in\mathcal M_{\Delta,p}(\kappa)}
 P_\theta\{\widehat r_n\neq r(\theta)\}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{12}
\]

We expose all remaining uniform conditioning margins.  First,
\[
 \lambda_{\min}(RR^{\mathsf T})=\sigma_r(\Gamma_1)^2\geq\kappa^2.
\tag{13}
\]
Second, \(\text{ass:decay-margin}\) gives \(q\in[\kappa,1-\kappa]\).  The moment bound and (13) bound \(\lVert F\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\).  Since every eigenvalue of \(F\) is \(q\), including for repeated poles and singular excitation matrices,
\[
 |\det(F-I_r)|=(1-q)^r\geq\kappa^r.
\tag{14}
\]
For any \(r\times r\) matrix \(M\), the product of its singular values equals \(|\det M|\), so
\[
 \sigma_{\min}(M)\geq |\det M|\lVert M\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}^{-(r-1)}.
\tag{15}
\]
Equations (14)--(15) uniformly bound \((F-I_r)^{-1}\).  The finite logarithm has degree at most \(p-1\), and its derivative is uniformly bounded because both \(q^{-1}\) and \(F\) are bounded.  Assumption \(\text{ass:intensity-gap}\) gives \(\lambda_j\geq\kappa\) in every edge division.  Finally, \(\beta\geq\underline\beta=-\Delta^{-1}\log(1-\kappa)>0\), and \(\text{ass:parameter-envelope}\) with (8) gives
\[
 \lVert(I_p-A/\beta)^{-1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
 \leq\sum_{d=0}^{p-1}(\kappa^{-1}/\underline\beta)^d.
\tag{16}
\]
Thus every strict validity condition persists on a neighborhood of a common radius, while (4)--(6) have a common derivative bound because the positive and zero spectra are separated by \(\kappa^2\).  Taking the maximum over the finitely many ranks \(1,\ldots,p\) gives a common local Lipschitz constant for the basis-invariant inverse.

Combining this common Lipschitz bound with (11)--(12) proves
\[
 \lim_{M\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}
 \sup_{\theta:P_\theta\in\mathcal M_{\Delta,p}(\kappa)}
 P_\theta\!\left[
 \sqrt n\{ |\widehat\beta_n-\beta|+
 \lVert\widehat A_n-A\rVert_{\mathrm F}+
 \lVert\widehat\mu_n-\mu\rVert_2\}>M\right]=0.
\tag{17}
\]
The exceptional sentinel contribution is bounded by the probability in (12) plus the probability that (11) leaves the common validity neighborhood, both uniformly negligible.  The final assertion about \(\widehat\Omega_n\) is exactly the Bartlett-HAC conclusion of \(\text{lem:bin-moment-limit}\) under \(\text{ass:hac-bandwidth}\).  No Gaussian law for the nonsmooth positive-part amplitude map has been used.
