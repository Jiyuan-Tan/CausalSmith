Fix \(\eta\in\mathfrak I_{\Delta,p}^{\mathrm{fr}}\) and choose any structural representative \((\beta,A,\mu)\) whose existence is asserted by \(\text{def:full-rank-stable-moment-image}\).  Put \(B=A-\beta I_p\).  By \(\text{lem:stable-covariance-semigroup}\), \(B\) is real Hurwitz, \(Q\succ0\), \(J\) is invertible, and
\[
 \Gamma_1=J^2K,\qquad \Gamma_2=TJ^2K,\qquad
 K=A(2\beta I_p-A)Q.
\tag{1}
\]
The image definition gives \(\det(A)\neq0\).  Stability gives \(|z|\leq\rho(A)<\beta\) for every \(z\in\sigma(A)\), so \(2\beta I_p-A\) is invertible.  Since \(Q\) is invertible, the last identity in (1) shows that \(K\) is invertible.  Hence \(\Gamma_1=J^2K\) is invertible, and the observable transition is
\[
 T^\dagger=\Gamma_2\Gamma_1^{-1}
 =TJ^2K(J^2K)^{-1}=T=e^{B\Delta}.
\tag{2}
\]

Every eigenvalue of \(B\Delta\) lies in the open strip specified in \(\text{def:full-rank-stable-moment-image}\).  The principal-log identity \(\text{lem:principal-log-strip}\), applied to the real matrix \(B\Delta\), therefore gives
\[
 B^\dagger=\Delta^{-1}\operatorname{Log}(T^\dagger)
 =\Delta^{-1}\operatorname{Log}(e^{B\Delta})=B.
\tag{3}
\]
In particular \(B^\dagger\) is real Hurwitz.  Thus it is invertible, every eigenvalue of \(T^\dagger\) has modulus below one, and \(T^\dagger-I_p\) is invertible.  Consequently
\[
 J^\dagger=(B^\dagger)^{-1}(T^\dagger-I_p)
 =B^{-1}(T-I_p)=J
\tag{4}
\]
is invertible.  Equations (1) and (4) then yield
\[
 K^\dagger=(J^\dagger)^{-2}\Gamma_1=J^{-2}J^2K=K.
\tag{5}
\]

The population mean gives \(\lambda^\dagger=m/\Delta=\lambda\), where division is valid because \(\Delta>0\); hence \(D^\dagger=D\succ0\).  Since \(B^\dagger=B\) is Hurwitz, the integral defining \(Q^\dagger\) converges absolutely.  By its definition and that of \(Q\),
\[
 Q^\dagger=\int_0^\infty e^{Bu}De^{B^{\mathsf T}u}\,du=Q\succ0.
\tag{6}
\]
It is also the unique solution of \(BX+XB^{\mathsf T}=-D\): if \(H\) solves the homogeneous equation, then
\[
 \frac{d}{du}\{e^{Bu}He^{B^{\mathsf T}u}\}=0,
\]
so the bracket equals \(H\) for every \(u\), while Hurwitz decay forces it to converge to zero; hence \(H=0\).

Using \(A=B+\beta I_p\), the last identity in (1) becomes
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 K
 &=A(2\beta I_p-A)Q\\
 &=(B+\beta I_p)(\beta I_p-B)Q\\
 &=(\beta^2I_p-B^2)Q.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
Equations (3), (5)--(7) imply
\[
 K^\dagger(Q^\dagger)^{-1}+(B^\dagger)^2=\beta^2I_p,
 \qquad
 s^\dagger=p^{-1}\operatorname{tr}(\beta^2I_p)=\beta^2>0.
\tag{8}
\]
Because the inverse definition takes the positive scalar square root and \(\beta>0\), (8) gives \(\beta^\dagger=\beta\).  Therefore
\[
 A^\dagger=B^\dagger+\beta^\dagger I_p=B+\beta I_p=A.
\tag{9}
\]
Finally, the stationary mean equation (1) in the proof of \(\text{lem:stable-covariance-semigroup}\) gives
\[
 \mu=(I_p-A/\beta)\lambda
 =(I_p-A^\dagger/\beta^\dagger)\lambda^\dagger=\mu^\dagger.
\tag{10}
\]
Every quantity on the left sides of (2)--(10) is a function only of \(\eta\).  Thus two structural representatives of the same point in \(\mathfrak I_{\Delta,p}^{\mathrm{fr}}\) both equal the same recovered triple.  This proves representative independence, all asserted invertibility and uniqueness claims, positivity of the radicand, and exact recovery.  The proof used \(\text{lem:stable-covariance-semigroup}\), never the DAG specialization.
