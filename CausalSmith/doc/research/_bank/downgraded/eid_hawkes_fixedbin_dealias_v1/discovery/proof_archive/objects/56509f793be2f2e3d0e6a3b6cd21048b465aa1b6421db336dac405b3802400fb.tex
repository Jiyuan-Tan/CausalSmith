Write
\[
 \phi(u)=Ae^{-\beta u}\mathbf 1_{\{u>0\}},\qquad
 \widetilde N(dt)=N(dt)-\lambda\,dt,\qquad
 M(dt)=N(dt)-\ell(t)\,dt.
\]
Membership in \(\mathcal M_{\Delta,p}^{\mathrm{stab}}\) gives \(A\geq0\) entrywise and \(\rho(A/\beta)<1\).  Taking expectations in the intensity equation and using stationarity gives
\[
 \lambda=\mu+\int_0^\infty Ae^{-\beta u}\lambda\,du
 =\mu+(A/\beta)\lambda.
\tag{1}
\]
Since \(A\lambda\geq0\) and \(\min_i\mu_i>0\), (1) implies \(\lambda_i\geq\mu_i>0\) for every \(i\); hence \(D=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda)\succ0\).

The eigenvalues of \(B=A-\beta I_p\) are \(z-\beta\), where \(z\in\sigma(A)\).  The spectral-radius condition gives
\[
 \operatorname{Re}(z-\beta)\leq |z|-\beta\leq\rho(A)-\beta<0,
\tag{2}
\]
so \(B\) is a real Hurwitz matrix.  In particular all matrix integrals below converge absolutely.

Subtracting (1) from the intensity equation yields the centered innovation equation
\[
 \widetilde N=M+\phi*\widetilde N.
\tag{3}
\]
For \(d\geq1\), direct induction using the scalar gamma integral gives
\[
 \phi^{*d}(u)=A^d\frac{u^{d-1}}{(d-1)!}e^{-\beta u}\mathbf1_{\{u>0\}}.
\tag{4}
\]
Because \(A\geq0\) and \(\rho(A/\beta)<1\), the entrywise integrals of \(\sum_{d\geq1}\phi^{*d}\) converge.  Summing (4) therefore gives the causal resolvent
\[
 \psi(u)=\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}\phi^{*d}(u)
 =e^{-\beta u}e^{Au}A=e^{Bu}A,\qquad u>0.
\tag{5}
\]
Iteration of (3), followed by the same entrywise \(L^1\) limit, yields
\[
 \widetilde N=M+\psi*M.
\tag{6}
\]

By the defining compensator property of the conditional intensity, increments of \(M\) on disjoint time intervals are orthogonal in second mean.  Simplicity of the marked process rules out simultaneous jumps of distinct labels, and its predictable covariance measure is \(D\,dt\).  Expanding (6) first over bounded disjoint intervals and then differentiating their covariance with respect to their endpoints gives, for \(t>0\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 C_+(t)
 &=\psi(t)D+\int_0^\infty\psi(t+u)D\psi(u)^{\mathsf T}\,du\\
 &=e^{Bt}\left\{AD+\int_0^\infty e^{Bu}ADA^{\mathsf T}e^{B^{\mathsf T}u}\,du\right\}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
The passage to the limit is justified by (2), which bounds every entry of the integrands by an exponentially decaying function times a polynomial arising from a Jordan form.

Define
\[
 Q=\int_0^\infty e^{Bu}De^{B^{\mathsf T}u}\,du.
\]
For every nonzero \(x\in\mathbb R^p\), continuity at zero and \(D\succ0\) give
\[
 x^{\mathsf T}Qx
 =\int_0^\infty\left\|D^{1/2}e^{B^{\mathsf T}u}x\right\|_2^2du>0;
\tag{8}
\]
hence \(Q\succ0\).  Differentiating the integrand, integrating over \([0,\infty)\), and using (2) at the upper endpoint yields the Lyapunov identity
\[
 BQ+QB^{\mathsf T}=-D,\qquad
 D=2\beta Q-AQ-QA^{\mathsf T}.
\tag{9}
\]
Because \(A\) commutes with \(B\) and therefore with \(e^{Bu}\), (7)--(9) imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 K
 &:=AD+AQA^{\mathsf T}\\
 &=A(2\beta Q-AQ-QA^{\mathsf T})+AQA^{\mathsf T}\\
 &=A(2\beta I_p-A)Q,
 \end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
and \(C_+(t)=e^{Bt}K\).  Since \(AQA^{\mathsf T}\) is symmetric, (10) also gives
\[
 K-K^{\mathsf T}=AD-DA^{\mathsf T}.
\tag{11}
\]

For \(k\geq1\), the two bins are disjoint except for one endpoint, which has zero covariance measure.  Put the time in the later bin equal to \(k\Delta+u\) and that in the earlier bin equal to \(v\), with \(u,v\in[0,\Delta]\).  Stationarity, (7), and Fubini's theorem give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Gamma_k
 &=\int_0^\Delta\int_0^\Delta e^{B(k\Delta+u-v)}K\,du\,dv\\
 &=e^{B(k-1)\Delta}
 \left(\int_0^\Delta e^{Bu}\,du\right)
 \left(\int_0^\Delta e^{B(\Delta-v)}\,dv\right)K\\
 &=T^{k-1}J^2K.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]

It remains to prove the rank claim rather than assume it.  By (2), \(B\) is invertible.  Every eigenvalue of \(T=e^{B\Delta}\) has modulus strictly below one, so \(T-I_p\) is invertible.  Therefore
\[
 J=\int_0^\Delta e^{Bu}\,du=B^{-1}(T-I_p)
\tag{13}
\]
is invertible.  Also \(2\beta I_p-A\) is invertible, since every \(z\in\sigma(A)\) satisfies \(|z|<\beta<2\beta\), and \(Q\) is invertible by (8).  Equation (10) consequently gives
\[
 \operatorname{im}(K)=\operatorname{im}(A).
\tag{14}
\]
Both \(J\) and \(J^{-1}\) are analytic functions of \(B=A-\beta I_p\) and hence commute with \(A\).  They therefore map \(\operatorname{im}(A)\) bijectively onto itself.  Using (12) at \(k=1\) and (14),
\[
 \operatorname{im}(\Gamma_1)=J^2\operatorname{im}(K)
 =J^2\operatorname{im}(A)=\operatorname{im}(A),
\]
which also proves the equality of ranks.
