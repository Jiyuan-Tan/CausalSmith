Put \(N_n=n-2\) and \(U_k=Y_k-\xi\).  We first derive the moment and covariance bounds used below.  By \(\text{lem:uniform-bin-eighth-moment}\), uniformly on \(\mathcal M_{\Delta,p}(\kappa)\),
\[
 \mathbb E_\theta\lVert Y_0\rVert_2^4\leq C_Y<\infty,
\tag{1}
\]
because each quadratic coordinate of \(Y_0\) is bounded in absolute value by a constant depending only on \(p\) times \(\sum_{a=0}^2\lVert X_a\rVert_2^2\), and stationarity gives the same eighth-moment bound for every \(X_a\).  The fixed-law version of (1) follows from the first sentence of that lemma.  For centered scalar coordinates \(U=Y_{0,a}-\mathbb EY_{0,a}\) and \(V=Y_{h,b}-\mathbb EY_{h,b}\), clip at level \(M\geq1\).  The definition of strong mixing, followed by adding and subtracting the clipped variables and applying Cauchy--Schwarz and Markov inequalities, gives
\[
 |\operatorname{Cov}(U,V)|
 \leq4M^2\alpha_Y(h)+C M^{-1}.
\tag{2}
\]
Taking \(M=\max\{1,\alpha_Y(h)^{-1/6}\}\), with the zero-mixing case obtained by first fixing \(M\) and then sending \(M\) to infinity, yields
\[
 |\operatorname{Cov}(U,V)|\leq C'\alpha_Y(h)^{1/6}.
\tag{3}
\]
The exponential envelope in \(\text{lem:finite-depth-geometric-mixing}\) makes the right side summable uniformly.  Since the dimension \(d_0=p+2p^2\) is fixed,
\[
 \sup_{\theta:P_\theta\in\mathcal M_{\Delta,p}(\kappa)}
 \sum_{h\in\mathbb Z}\lVert\operatorname{Cov}_\theta(Y_0,Y_h)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}<\infty.
\tag{4}
\]
This proves both that \(\Omega(\theta)\) is well defined and uniformly bounded and that
\[
 \sup_\theta\mathbb E_\theta
 \left\lVert N_n^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{N_n-1}U_k\right\rVert_2^2
 \leq C N_n^{-1}.
\tag{5}
\]
Consequently \(\widehat\xi_n\to\xi\) in probability, pointwise and uniformly on the margin class.  The map \(\mathcal C\) in \(\text{def:centering-map}\) is a quadratic polynomial, so it is continuous; hence \(\widehat\eta_n=\mathcal C(\widehat\xi_n)\to\mathcal C(\xi)=\eta=(m,\Gamma_1,\Gamma_2)\).  This proves the two asserted probability limits, including for a fixed law outside the margin class by using the law-dependent versions of (1)--(5).

We shall also need a higher derived moment, not a new hypothesis.  The finite recursion displayed in \(\text{lem:uniform-bin-eighth-moment}\) is finite in a neighborhood of zero at every depth at most \(p-1\).  Repeating that lemma's Poisson-cluster cumulant calculation through order sixteen, using \(g_{p-1}^{(32)}(0)<\infty\) in its Cauchy--Schwarz duration-tail step, gives
\[
 \sup_{\theta:P_\theta\in\mathcal M_{\Delta,p}(\kappa)}
 \mathbb E_\theta\lVert X_0\rVert_2^{16}<\infty,
 \qquad
 \sup_\theta\mathbb E_\theta\lVert Y_0\rVert_2^8<\infty.
\tag{6}
\]
Only the same bounded depth, offspring envelope, immigrant envelope, and exponential cluster-duration bound are used.

We next prove the uniform Gaussian assertion rather than importing a mixing central limit theorem.  Fix a unit vector \(a\in\mathbb R^{d_0}\).  Divide \(0,\ldots,N_n-1\) into large blocks of length \(\ell_n=\lfloor N_n^{1/2}\rfloor\), gaps of length \(g_n=\lceil c_0\log N_n\rceil\), and a terminal remainder, where \(c_0>(2c_{\mathrm{mix}}\Delta)^{-1}\).  There are \(v_n\leq N_n/\ell_n\) large blocks.  By (4), the discarded gaps and remainder have variance at most \(C(v_ng_n+\ell_n)=o(N_n)\).  Telescoping the product of the bounded large-block characteristic functions and applying the bounded strong-mixing covariance inequality at each gap changes that product to the corresponding product for independent block copies by at most
\[
 v_n\alpha_Y(g_n-2)\leq C N_n^{1/2}e^{-c_{\mathrm{mix}}\Delta g_n}=o(1)
\tag{7}
\]
uniformly in \(\theta\).  This uses only strong mixing; no absolute-regularity coupling is invoked.

Expanding a fourth power and ordering its four time indices, repeated clipping applied to the two groups separated by the largest adjacent gap, with the eighth moment in (6), gives a positive power of the geometric mixing coefficient.  Summing that envelope and bounding coincident indices by (6) yields, uniformly for every interval \(I\) of length \(\ell\),
\[
 \mathbb E_\theta\left|\sum_{k\in I}a^{\mathsf T}U_k\right|^4\leq C_1\ell^2.
\tag{8}
\]
If \(B_{b,n}\) are the independent copies of the large-block sums, then
\[
 N_n^{-2}\sum_{b=1}^{v_n}\mathbb E_\theta|B_{b,n}|^4
 \leq C_1v_n\ell_n^2N_n^{-2}
 =O(\ell_n/N_n)=o(1).
\tag{9}
\]
Taylor expansion of each independent-block characteristic function through second order, with (9) controlling the summed remainder, shows that the characteristic function of \(N_n^{-1/2}\sum_bB_{b,n}\) differs uniformly from that of a centered normal variable with the same variance by \(o(1)\).  By (4), that variance differs from \(a^{\mathsf T}\Omega(\theta)a\) by the covariance tail and by \(O(g_n/\ell_n+\ell_n/N_n)=o(1)\).  Equations (7)--(9) and the negligible gaps therefore give the scalar Gaussian approximation for every \(a\), uniformly in \(\theta\).

To pass to vectors without a hidden uniformity assumption, suppose the displayed bounded-Lipschitz conclusion failed.  There would be a sequence \(\theta_n\) and a positive lower bound on its discrepancy.  The uniformly bounded matrices \(\Omega(\theta_n)\) have a convergent subsequence.  Along a further subsequence, the preceding scalar characteristic-function calculation applied to each vector in a countable dense subset of the unit sphere, together with the uniform second-moment bound (5), gives convergence of the vector characteristic functions to those of the centered normal law with the limiting covariance.  Fourier approximation of bounded Lipschitz functions on a large ball, and (5) outside that ball, then gives bounded-Lipschitz convergence, a contradiction.  Thus
\[
 \sup_{\theta:P_\theta\in\mathcal M_{\Delta,p}(\kappa)}
 d_{\mathrm{BL}}\!\left(
 \mathcal L_\theta\{\sqrt n(\widehat\xi_n-\xi(\theta))\},
 \mathcal N\{0,\Omega(\theta)\}\right)\longrightarrow0,
\tag{10}
\]
where replacing \(N_n\) by \(n\) is harmless because \(N_n/n\to1\).

The derivative of \(\mathcal C\) is uniformly bounded on the moment set supplied by (1), and its exact quadratic expansion has remainder bounded by \(C\lVert\widehat\xi_n-\xi\rVert_2^2\).  Equations (5) and (10) therefore give, uniformly,
\[
 \sqrt n\{\widehat\eta_n-\eta(\theta)\}
 =D\mathcal C_{\xi(\theta)}\sqrt n\{\widehat\xi_n-\xi(\theta)\}+o_{P_\theta}(1),
\tag{11}
\]
which proves the asserted centered-moment Gaussian approximation with covariance \(D\mathcal C_{\xi(\theta)}\Omega(\theta)D\mathcal C_{\xi(\theta)}^{\mathsf T}\).

It remains to prove the Bartlett heteroskedasticity-and-autocorrelation assertion.  Let
\[
 \widetilde\Xi_h=N_n^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{N_n-h-1}U_kU_{k+h}^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Repeated clipping as in (2), now for products of at most four coordinates and using (6), bounds each joint fourth cumulant by a constant times a positive power of the mixing coefficient across the largest separating gap.  After ordering the four time arguments, the geometric mixing envelope makes these cumulants absolutely summable, uniformly in every free translation and in the remaining lag indices.  Expanding a covariance of two products gives explicitly
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(U_0^aU_h^b,U_t^cU_{t+h'}^d)
 =\operatorname{cum}(U_0^a,U_h^b,U_t^c,U_{t+h'}^d)
 +\operatorname{Cov}(U_0^a,U_t^c)\operatorname{Cov}(U_h^b,U_{t+h'}^d)
 +\operatorname{Cov}(U_0^a,U_{t+h'}^d)\operatorname{Cov}(U_h^b,U_t^c).
\tag{12}
\]
The cumulant sum and both convolution sums in (12) are bounded uniformly by (3), (6), and the preceding largest-gap bound.  Consequently, for all \(0\leq h,h'\leq b_n\),
\[
 \left|\operatorname{Cov}\{(\widetilde\Xi_h)_{ab},(\widetilde\Xi_{h'})_{cd}\}\right|
 \leq C_2N_n^{-1}.
\tag{13}
\]
Summing (13) over the finitely many coordinates and the \((b_n+1)^2\) lag pairs yields
\[
 \mathbb E_\theta\left\lVert
 \sum_{h=0}^{b_n}w(h/b_n)
 \{\widetilde\Xi_h-\mathbb E_\theta\widetilde\Xi_h\}
 \right\rVert_{\mathrm F}^2
 \leq C_3b_n^2/N_n=o(1).
\tag{14}
\]
For the expectation, \(\mathbb E\widetilde\Xi_h=(1-h/N_n)\operatorname{Cov}(Y_0,Y_h)\).  Since \(w(0)=1\), \(w(h/b_n)\to1\) for fixed \(h\), and (4) supplies a uniform summable envelope, splitting at a fixed lag and then sending that lag to infinity proves that the expectation of the symmetrized lag-window sum converges uniformly to \(\Omega(\theta)\).

Finally, expanding \((Y_k-\overline Y_n)(Y_{k+h}-\overline Y_n)^{\mathsf T}\) around \(U_kU_{k+h}^{\mathsf T}\), using (5) and the boundary pieces of length at most \(h\), shows that replacing \(\xi\) by \(\overline Y_n\) changes the complete lag-window sum by
\[
 O_{P_\theta}\!\left(b_n\lVert\overline Y_n-\xi\rVert_2
 +b_n\lVert\overline Y_n-\xi\rVert_2^2\right)=o_{P_\theta}(1)
\tag{15}
\]
uniformly, because \(b_n=o(n^{1/2})\).  The empty-sum convention in \(\text{def:hac-estimator}\) makes this expansion valid for every finite sample.  Combining (4), (14), and (15), and using \(\lVert M\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\lVert M\rVert_{\mathrm F}\), proves the stated uniform operator-norm consistency of \(\widehat\Omega_n\).
