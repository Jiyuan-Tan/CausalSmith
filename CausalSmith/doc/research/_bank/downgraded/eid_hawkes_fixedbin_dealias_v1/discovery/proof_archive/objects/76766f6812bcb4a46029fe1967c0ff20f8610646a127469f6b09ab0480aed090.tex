For each \(s\in\{0,1\}\), the excitation matrix in \(\theta_s\) is zero. By \(\text{prop:empty-graph-equivalence}\), under \(P_{\theta_s}\) the coordinates \(N_1,\ldots,N_p\) are independent homogeneous Poisson processes with respective rates \(\mu_{0,1},\ldots,\mu_{0,p}\), and their observable law is independent of \(\beta_s\). The common baseline therefore gives
\[
 P_{\theta_0}=P_{\theta_1}=:P_0.
\]

Fix \(n\geq3\) and \(\mathfrak U_n=(\mathcal G_n,\mathcal T_n)\in\mathfrak H_{n,\kappa_0}(\alpha)\). For \(s\in\{0,1\}\), define
\[
 B_s=\{G(0)\in\mathcal G_n,\ \theta_s\in\mathcal T_n\}.
\]
The definition of \(\mathfrak H_{n,\kappa_0}(\alpha)\), applied separately to the two admitted parameter points \(\theta_s\), yields
\[
 P_0(B_s)=P_{\theta_s}(B_s)\geq1-\alpha,
 \qquad s\in\{0,1\}.
\]
The elementary union bound and nonnegativity of probability give
\[
 P_0(B_0\cap B_1)
 \geq P_0(B_0)+P_0(B_1)-1
 \geq1-2\alpha,
\]
and hence \(P_0(B_0\cap B_1)\geq(1-2\alpha)_+\). On \(B_0\cap B_1\), both \(\theta_0\) and \(\theta_1\) belong to \(\mathcal T_n\). By the definitions of \(\rho_{\Theta}\) and \(\operatorname{diam}_{\Theta}\),
\[
 \operatorname{diam}_{\Theta}(\mathcal T_n)
 \geq \rho_{\Theta}(\theta_0,\theta_1)
 =1\wedge\left\{|\beta_1-\beta_0|+\lVert0-0\rVert_{\mathrm F}+\lVert\mu_0-\mu_0\rVert_2\right\}
 =d_{01}.
\]
This proves the probability assertion. Since the diameter is nonnegative,
\[
 \mathbb E_0\!\left[\operatorname{diam}_{\Theta}(\mathcal T_n)\right]
 \geq d_{01}P_0\!\left\{\operatorname{diam}_{\Theta}(\mathcal T_n)\geq d_{01}\right\}
 \geq d_{01}(1-2\alpha)_+.
\]
Finally, \(A=0\) implies \(\delta(\theta_s)=0\) by \(\text{def:boundary-risks}\), while the assumed memberships place the common law in \(\mathcal M_{\kappa_0}(0)\). Thus, for every fixed honest \(\mathfrak U_n\), the supremum defining \(R_{\Theta,n,\kappa_0}(0)\) is at least the last display. Taking the infimum over \(\mathfrak U_n\in\mathfrak H_{n,\kappa_0}(\alpha)\) preserves this lower bound and proves the conclusion.
