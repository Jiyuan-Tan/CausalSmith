Put \(\widetilde N(dt)=N(dt)-\lambda\,dt\) and \(M_i(dt)=N_i(dt)-\ell_i(t)\,dt\).  By \(\text{ass:hawkes-intensity}\), after taking expectations and subtracting the mean-intensity equation,
\[
 \widetilde N(dt)=M(dt)+\int_0^\infty Ae^{-\beta u}\widetilde N(d(t-u))\,du .
\]
The compensators are absolutely continuous.  Hence distinct coordinates have no simultaneous jumps almost surely, and the martingale innovations are orthogonal with predictable covariance \(D\,dt\), where \(D=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda)\).  Acyclicity, nonnegativity, and the loop-inclusive graph convention imply \(A^p=0\): otherwise a positive entry of some \(A^d\), \(d\geq p\), would exhibit a directed walk that repeats a vertex and hence contains a directed cycle.  Iterating the preceding convolution equation therefore terminates and gives the causal resolvent
\[
 R(t)=\sum_{d=1}^{p-1}A^d\frac{t^{d-1}}{(d-1)!}e^{-\beta t}=e^{(A-\beta I_p)t}A=e^{Bt}A,\qquad t>0.
\]
Consequently \(\widetilde N=M+R*M\) in second mean, and expansion against the orthogonal innovation measure gives, for \(t>0\),
\[
 C_+(t)=R(t)D+\int_0^\infty R(t+u)DR(u)^{\mathsf T}\,du
 =e^{Bt}\left\{AD+\int_0^\infty e^{Bu}ADA^{\mathsf T}e^{B^{\mathsf T}u}\,du\right\}.
\]
This calculation follows directly first for finite unions of bounded intervals and then for covariance densities by differentiation; all integrals are finite because every matrix exponential here is \(e^{-\beta u}\) times a polynomial of degree at most \(p-1\), using \(\text{ass:positive-decay}\).

By \(\text{ass:positive-baseline}\) and the stationary mean equation \(\lambda=\mu+(A/\beta)\lambda\), every \(\lambda_i\) is strictly positive.  Thus, for \(x\neq0\),
\[
 x^{\mathsf T}Qx=\int_0^\infty\left\|D^{1/2}e^{B^{\mathsf T}u}x\right\|_2^2du>0,
\]
because the integrand is continuous and equals \(x^{\mathsf T}Dx>0\) at \(u=0\).  Hence \(Q\succ0\).  Differentiating \(e^{Bu}De^{B^{\mathsf T}u}\), integrating from zero to infinity, and using its exponential-polynomial decay yields
\[
 BQ+QB^{\mathsf T}=-D,\qquad D=2\beta Q-AQ-QA^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Since \(A\) commutes with \(e^{Bu}\), the integral in braces above is \(AQA^{\mathsf T}\).  Therefore
\[
 K=AD+AQA^{\mathsf T}=A(D+QA^{\mathsf T})=A(2\beta I_p-A)Q,
\]
and \(C_+(t)=e^{Bt}K\).  The second term \(AQA^{\mathsf T}\) is symmetric, so
\[
 K-K^{\mathsf T}=AD-DA^{\mathsf T}.
\]

For \(k\geq1\), the two bins are disjoint except for a deterministic endpoint of zero covariance measure.  With \(s=k\Delta+u\) and \(v\in[0,\Delta]\), stationarity and the positive-lag density give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \Gamma_k
 &=\int_0^\Delta\int_0^\Delta e^{B(k\Delta+u-v)}K\,du\,dv\\
 &=e^{B(k-1)\Delta}\left(\int_0^\Delta e^{Bu}du\right)
   \left(\int_0^\Delta e^{B(\Delta-v)}dv\right)K
 =T^{k-1}J^2K.
\end{aligned}
\]
The eigenvalues of \(B\) are all \(-\beta\), because \(A\) is nilpotent.  Thus \(B\) is invertible.  Moreover
\[
 J=\int_0^\Delta e^{Bu}du=B^{-1}(T-I_p)
\]
is a polynomial in \(A\) with constant coefficient \((1-e^{-\beta\Delta})/\beta>0\), and hence is invertible.  Both \(J\) and \(J^{-1}\) commute with \(A\), so they preserve \(\operatorname{im}(A)\).  Also \((2\beta I_p-A)Q\) is invertible, whence
\[
 \operatorname{im}(K)=\operatorname{im}(A),\qquad
 \operatorname{im}(\Gamma_1)=J^2\operatorname{im}(K)=\operatorname{im}(A),
\]
and the ranks are equal.  No step divided by \(A\), selected an eigenvector, or assumed distinct poles or structural rank.  The same argument therefore covers singular matrices, disconnected graphs, repeated poles, diamonds, and proportional rows.
