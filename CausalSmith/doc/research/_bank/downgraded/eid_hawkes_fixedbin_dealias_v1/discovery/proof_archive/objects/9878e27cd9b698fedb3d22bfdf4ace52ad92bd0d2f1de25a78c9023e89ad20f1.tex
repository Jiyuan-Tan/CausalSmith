Simultaneous permutation of coordinates preserves matrix rank, the equality of observable moments, and the binned-law metric.  It therefore suffices to use one canonical labeling of each motif.  For the fork \(1\to2\), \(1\to3\),
\[
 A=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\a&0&0\\b&0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad a,b>0,
\]
which has rank one.  A directed chain has two nonzero columns with pivots in distinct rows, hence rank two.  Adding the transitive edge to form the frozen three-node diamond leaves those two pivot columns linearly independent, hence also has rank two.  The image identity in \(\text{lem:covariance-semigroup}\) gives \(\operatorname{rank}(\Gamma_1)=\operatorname{rank}(A)\).  This proves the rank-stratum assertions.

Now fix the displayed fork.  Then \(A^2=0\).  Write \(\lambda=m/\Delta\), which is observed because \(\Delta>0\) is known.  From the integral defining \(Q\) and \(e^{Bu}=e^{-\beta u}(I_3+uA)\), direct integration gives
\[
 Q=\frac{D}{2\beta}+\frac{AD+DA^{\mathsf T}}{4\beta^2}
   +\frac{ADA^{\mathsf T}}{4\beta^3}.                            \tag{1}
\]
Because \(A^2=0\), the covariance jump in \(\text{lem:covariance-semigroup}\) reduces to
\[
 K=A(2\beta I_3-A)Q=2\beta AQ
 =AD+\frac{ADA^{\mathsf T}}{2\beta}.                             \tag{2}
\]
Thus its only nonzero entries are
\[
\begin{gathered}
 K_{21}=a\lambda_1,\qquad K_{31}=b\lambda_1,\\
 K_{22}=\frac{a^2\lambda_1}{2\beta},\qquad
 K_{23}=K_{32}=\frac{ab\lambda_1}{2\beta},\qquad
 K_{33}=\frac{b^2\lambda_1}{2\beta}.                            \tag{3}
\end{gathered}
\]
In particular, the first row of \(K\) is zero, so \(AK=0\).  Put
\[
 c(\beta)=\frac{1-e^{-\beta\Delta}}{\beta}>0,\qquad
 d(\beta)=\int_0^\Delta ue^{-\beta u}\,du .
\]
Then \(J=c(\beta)I_3+d(\beta)A\), and \(A^2=AK=0\) imply
\[
 \Gamma_1=J^2K=c(\beta)^2K.                                     \tag{4}
\]
Since \(a,b,\lambda_1,c(\beta)>0\), equations (3)--(4) show that the observable pair identifies
\[
 x=\frac a\beta=\frac{2(\Gamma_1)_{22}}{(\Gamma_1)_{21}},\qquad
 y=\frac b\beta=\frac{2(\Gamma_1)_{33}}{(\Gamma_1)_{31}},        \tag{5}
\]
and then identifies
\[
 h(\beta)=\beta c(\beta)^2
 =\frac{(\Gamma_1)_{21}}{x\lambda_1}
 =\frac{(1-e^{-\beta\Delta})^2}{\beta}.                         \tag{6}
\]

Conversely, (3)--(4) show entry by entry that \(\Gamma_1\) is determined by \(\lambda,x,y,h(\beta)\).  For example,
\[
 (\Gamma_1)_{21}=x\lambda_1h(\beta),\quad
 (\Gamma_1)_{22}=\frac{x^2\lambda_1h(\beta)}2,\quad
 (\Gamma_1)_{23}=\frac{xy\lambda_1h(\beta)}2,
\]
and the remaining nonzero entries have the analogous formulas.  If \(\beta'>0\) has \(h(\beta')=h(\beta)\), set \(a'=x\beta'\), \(b'=y\beta'\).  The stationary mean equation gives
\[
 \mu=(I_3-A/\beta)\lambda
 =\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_1\\ \lambda_2-x\lambda_1\\ \lambda_3-y\lambda_1\end{pmatrix}. \tag{7}
\]
It is independent of \(\beta\), so the primed parameter has the same strictly positive baseline as the original parameter, remains a legal fork, and has the same \(m\) and \(\Gamma_1\).  Equations (5)--(7) also prove the converse: every point in the same within-support one-lag fiber must arise from a positive solution of \(h(\beta')=h(\beta)\), and is then uniquely determined by that solution.

Set \(z=\beta\Delta\).  Then \(h(\beta)=\Delta g(z)\), where \(g(z)=(1-e^{-z})^2/z\).  Differentiation gives
\[
 g'(z)=\frac{(1-e^{-z})e^{-z}}{z^2}\{1+2z-e^z\}.                \tag{8}
\]
The function \(f(z)=1+2z-e^z\) satisfies \(f(0)=0\), \(f'(0)=1\), and \(f''(z)=-e^z<0\).  Its derivative vanishes once, at \(\log2\); thereafter \(f\) decreases strictly to \(-\infty\).  Hence \(f\) has exactly one positive zero \(z_\star\), characterized by \(e^{z_\star}=1+2z_\star\).  Equation (8) shows that \(h\) increases strictly on \((0,z_\star/\Delta)\) and decreases strictly on \((z_\star/\Delta,\infty)\).  Moreover,
\[
 \lim_{\beta\downarrow0}h(\beta)=0,\qquad
 \lim_{\beta\to\infty}h(\beta)=0.                              \tag{9}
\]
Thus a level in the range of \(h\) has one positive preimage exactly at the maximum and has exactly two positive preimages otherwise.  The fiber characterization follows from (5)--(9).

Finally, \(T=e^{-\beta\Delta}(I_3+\Delta A)\), and (4) gives \(A\Gamma_1=0\).  Therefore
\[
 \Gamma_2=T\Gamma_1=e^{-\beta\Delta}\Gamma_1.                  \tag{10}
\]
The two distinct roots of a nonmaximal level of \(h\) have distinct \(e^{-\beta\Delta}\), while \(\Gamma_1\neq0\); hence their \(\Gamma_2\)'s differ.  Their three-bin distributions cannot be equal, because equal distributions with finite second moments would have equal lag-two covariances.  The summand of \(\rho_{\mathrm{law}}\) corresponding to three consecutive bins is therefore positive, so the laws are distinct.

The sets cut out by \(\beta<\beta_\star\) and \(\beta>\beta_\star\) are relatively open in \(\Theta_{1,G_0}\).  They are nonempty: for example, choose \(x=y=1/4\), \(\lambda=(1,1,1)^{\mathsf T}\), so (7) is strictly positive, and choose \(\beta\) on either desired side.  Every point in either set has the unique alias on the opposite side by (8)--(9).  Permuting labels proves the assertion for every labeled fork.
