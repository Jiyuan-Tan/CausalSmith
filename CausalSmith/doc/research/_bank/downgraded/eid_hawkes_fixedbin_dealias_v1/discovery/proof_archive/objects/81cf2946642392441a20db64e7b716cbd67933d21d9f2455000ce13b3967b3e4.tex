Write \(N_n=n-2\) and \(U_k=Y_k-\xi\).  By \(\text{lem:uniform-bin-eighth-moment}\), \(Y_0\) has a uniformly bounded fourth moment, because each quadratic coordinate is bounded by a constant times \(\sum_{a=0}^2\|X_a\|_2^2\).  By \(\text{lem:finite-depth-geometric-mixing}\), its strong-mixing coefficients have a common exponential envelope.  Davydov's covariance inequality, proved here by truncating each centered scalar coordinate at level \(L\), bounding the truncated covariance by \(4L^2\alpha_Y(h)\), bounding the two tails by H\"older's inequality, and optimizing \(L\), gives
\[
 |\operatorname{Cov}(u^{\mathsf T}U_0,v^{\mathsf T}U_h)|
 \leq C\|u\|_2\|v\|_2\alpha_Y(h)^{1/2}
\]
with a uniform \(C\).  Hence \(\sum_{h\in\mathbb Z}\|\operatorname{Cov}(Y_0,Y_h)\|_{\mathrm{op}}<\infty\) uniformly, so \(\Omega(\theta)\) is finite, and the variance of \(N_n^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{N_n-1}U_k\) is \(O(N_n^{-1})\) uniformly.  This proves the stated probability limits and, by continuity of \(\mathcal C\), the centered-moment limit.

For completeness, fix a unit vector \(a\) and apply a Bernstein blocking argument to \(a^{\mathsf T}U_k\).  Take large-block length \(\ell_n=\lfloor N_n^{1/2}\rfloor\), gap length \(g_n=\lceil c_0\log N_n\rceil\), where \(c_0>(2c_{\mathrm{mix}}\Delta)^{-1}\), and \(v_n=\lfloor N_n/(\ell_n+g_n)\rfloor\).  The discarded gaps have variance \(O(v_ng_n)=o(N_n)\).  Successive large blocks can be coupled to independent copies with total error at most
\[
 v_n\alpha_Y(g_n-2)\leq C N_n^{1/2}e^{-c_{\mathrm{mix}}\Delta g_n}=o(1).
\]
Expanding a fourth power and applying the preceding truncation bound to the first separated pair in each ordered quadruple gives uniformly
\[
 E\left|\sum_{k=1}^{\ell_n}a^{\mathsf T}U_k\right|^4\leq C_1\ell_n^2.
\]
Therefore the independent-block Lyapunov ratio is at most
\[
 N_n^{-2}\sum_{b=1}^{v_n}E|B_{b,n}|^4
 \leq C_1v_n\ell_n^2/N_n^2=O(\ell_n/N_n)=o(1).
\]
The covariance of the normalized block sum differs from \(a^{\mathsf T}\Omega(\theta)a\) by the uniformly summable covariance tail plus \(O(g_n/\ell_n+\ell_n/N_n)\), hence tends to zero uniformly.  The independent triangular-array characteristic-function proof of Lindeberg--Feller, using the displayed Lyapunov bound in the Taylor remainder, now yields the scalar Gaussian approximation uniformly in \(\theta\).  A finite net of the unit sphere, Cram\'er--Wold, and tightness from the uniform second moment upgrade this to
\[
 \sup_\theta d_{\mathrm{BL}}\!\left(\mathcal L_\theta\{\sqrt n(\widehat\xi_n-\xi(\theta))\},
 \mathcal N\{0,\Omega(\theta)\}\right)\to0.
\]
The replacement of \(N_n\) by \(n\) is harmless because \(N_n/n\to1\).  Taylor's formula for the polynomial map \(\mathcal C\), whose quadratic remainder is \(O(\|\widehat\xi_n-\xi\|^2)\), gives the asserted centered-moment Gaussian law.

It remains to verify the HAC assertion under the corrected normalization \(w(0)=1\).  The cluster argument in \(\text{lem:uniform-bin-eighth-moment}\) was stated through order eight, but its displayed recursion is finite for every order.  Repeating the same cumulant calculation for \(1\leq u\leq16\), with \(g_{p-1}^{(32)}(0)\) in the Cauchy--Schwarz bound, gives a uniform sixteenth moment for \(X_0\), and hence a uniform eighth moment for \(Y_0\).  Thus products of two coordinates of \(Y\) have a uniform fourth moment, so the truncation covariance inequality applies to them with a summable power of the exponential mixing coefficient.

Let \(\widetilde\Xi_h=N_n^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{N_n-h-1}U_kU_{k+h}^{\mathsf T}\).  Absolute covariance summability and dominated convergence show that the expectation of the lag-window sum converges uniformly to \(\Omega\): the factor \(1-h/N_n\) tends to one, \(w(h/b_n)\to w(0)=1\) for fixed \(h\), and the omitted tail is uniformly summable.  Expanding products of four coordinates, separating the earliest large gap, and applying the same truncation inequality gives
\[
 E\left\|\sum_{h=0}^{b_n}w(h/b_n)\{\widetilde\Xi_h-E\widetilde\Xi_h\}\right\|_{\mathrm F}^2
 \leq C_2b_n^2/N_n=o(1).
\]
Replacing \(\xi\) by \(\overline Y_n\) changes the sum by
\(O_P(b_n\|\overline Y_n-\xi\|+b_n\|\overline Y_n-\xi\|^2)=o_P(1)\) uniformly, since \(b_n=o(n^{1/2})\).  These three bounds prove uniform operator-norm consistency of \(\widehat\Omega_n\).
