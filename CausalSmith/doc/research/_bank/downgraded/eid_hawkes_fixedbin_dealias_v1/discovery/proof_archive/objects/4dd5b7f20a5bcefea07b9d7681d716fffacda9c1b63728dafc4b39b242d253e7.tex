The positive-lag part of \(\Psi_{\Delta,r}\) is the same restricted-transition calculation as in \(\text{thm:two-lag-exact-recovery}\); it uses neither \(m\) nor \(D\).  It therefore recovers \(\beta\), \(L=B|_S\), \(H=J_S^{-1}\), and \(K=OH^2R\).  Because the columns of \(K\) lie in the \(B\)-invariant space \(S\), for every \(t>0\),
\[
 \widetilde C_+(t)=Oe^{Lt}H^2R=e^{Bt}K=C_+(t),
\]
where the last equality is \(\text{lem:covariance-semigroup}\).

For the count in one bin, the covariance measure has the diagonal atom \(D\,\delta_0\) and continuous density \(C_+(u-v)\) off the diagonal.  Splitting the square \([0,\Delta]^2\) into its diagonal and the two open triangles, using stationarity and \(C_+(-t)=C_+(t)^{\mathsf T}\), gives
\[
 \Gamma_0=\Delta D+\int_0^\Delta(\Delta-t)\{C_+(t)+C_+(t)^{\mathsf T}\}\,dt.
\]
Here \(\Delta>0\) by \(\text{ass:fixed-bin}\), so division yields exactly the matrix \(D^\star=D\) in \(\text{def:covariance-only-inverse}\).  Its diagonal is the strictly positive vector \(\lambda\).  The antisymmetric-jump formula then gives every \(A_{ij}\), and the stationary mean equation gives \(\mu=(I_p-A/\beta)\lambda\), exactly as in the last part of \(\text{thm:two-lag-exact-recovery}\).  Hence \(\Psi_{\Delta,r}(\Gamma_0,\Gamma_1,\Gamma_2)=(\beta,A,\mu)\), proving that the mean is redundant once \(\Gamma_0\) is retained.
