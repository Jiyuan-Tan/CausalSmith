Fix a law in \(\mathcal M_{\Delta,p}^{\mathrm{stab}}\), and put \(I=[0,\Delta)\).  By \(\text{def:stable-common-exponential-class}\), the labeled point process is simple, has an absolutely continuous predictable compensator \(\ell(t)\,dt\), is stationary, and has finite stationary intensity \(\lambda=\mathbb E\{\ell(t)\}\).  For coordinate indices \(a,b\), define the centered covariance measure on \(I^2\) by
\[
 \mathcal V_{ab}(du,dv)
 =\mathbb E\!\left[
   \{N_a(du)-\lambda_a\,du\}
   \{N_b(dv)-\lambda_b\,dv\}
 \right].
\tag{1}
\]
Simplicity and the absolutely continuous compensator imply that the only diagonal mass in (1) is
\(\mathbf 1\{a=b\}\lambda_a\,du\,\delta_u(dv)\): indeed, on the diagonal a jump indicator squares to itself, distinct labels do not jump simultaneously in a simple labeled process, and the compensator has no atoms.  Off the diagonal, stationarity and the definition of \(C_{+}\) give the matrix-valued decomposition
\[
 \mathcal V(du,dv)
 =D\,du\,\delta_u(dv)
  +\mathbf 1\{u>v\}C_{+}(u-v)\,du\,dv
  +\mathbf 1\{v>u\}C_{+}(v-u)^{\mathsf T}\,du\,dv.
\tag{2}
\]
The density in (2) is integrable on \(I^2\): by \(\text{lem:stable-covariance-semigroup}\), it equals \(e^{Bt}K\) for \(t>0\), hence is continuous on the compact interval \((0,\Delta]\) and has a finite right limit at zero.

By \(\text{def:population-moments}\), \(\Gamma_0=\mathcal V(I,I)\).  Integrating the diagonal term in (2) gives \(\Delta D\).  On the triangle \(u>v\), the change of variables \(t=u-v\) gives
\[
 \int_0^\Delta\!\int_0^u C_{+}(u-v)\,dv\,du
 =\int_0^\Delta(\Delta-t)C_{+}(t)\,dt.
\tag{3}
\]
The other triangle similarly gives the transpose of (3).  Thus (2)--(3) prove
\[
 \Gamma_0=\Delta D+\int_0^\Delta(\Delta-t)
 \{C_{+}(t)+C_{+}(t)^{\mathsf T}\}\,dt.
\tag{4}
\]
Moreover, the compensator identity and stationarity yield
\[
 m=\mathbb E\{N(\Delta)-N(0)\}
   =\mathbb E\!\int_0^\Delta\ell(u)\,du
   =\Delta\lambda.
\tag{5}
\]
Membership in the stable class gives \(\Delta>0\), so division in (4)--(5) is valid.  Nonnegative excitation and the strictly positive baseline give \(\ell_i(t)\geq\mu_i>0\) almost surely, whence \(\lambda_i\geq\mu_i>0\).  Therefore \(D=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda)\succ0\), and the displayed inverse for \(D\) follows from (4).

It remains to justify the asserted inclusion of the acyclic closure.  For a finite acyclic labeled graph, repeatedly removing a source produces a topological ordering.  In that ordering the nonnegative matrix \(A\) is strictly triangular, because a diagonal entry would be a directed cycle of length one.  Hence \(A^p=0\), so \(\rho(A/\beta)=0<1\) because \(\beta>0\).  Every law in \(\overline{\mathcal M}_{\Delta,p}^{\mathrm{DAG}}\) therefore satisfies \(\text{def:stable-common-exponential-class}\), and (1)--(5) apply to it as well.
