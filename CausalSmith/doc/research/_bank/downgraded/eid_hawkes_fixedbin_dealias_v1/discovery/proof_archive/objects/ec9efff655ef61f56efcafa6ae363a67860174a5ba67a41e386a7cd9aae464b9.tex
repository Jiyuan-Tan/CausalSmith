Fix a law satisfying the theorem's hypotheses.  By \(\text{lem:covariance-semigroup}\), \(B\) is Hurwitz, \(Q\succ0\), \(J\) is invertible, and
\[
 \Gamma_1=J^2K,\qquad \Gamma_2=T\Gamma_1,\qquad
 K=A(2\beta I_p-A)Q.                                             \tag{1}
\]
The hypothesis makes \(A\) invertible.  Stability gives \(|z|<\beta\) for every eigenvalue \(z\) of \(A\), so \(2\beta I_p-A\) is invertible.  All four factors on the right of \(J^2A(2\beta I_p-A)Q\) are therefore invertible, and (1) proves that \(\Gamma_1\) is invertible.  Hence
\[
 T^{\mathrm{fr}}=\Gamma_2\Gamma_1^{-1}=T=e^{B\Delta}.             \tag{2}
\]
The strip hypothesis and \(\text{lem:principal-log-strip}\) give
\[
 B^{\mathrm{fr}}=\Delta^{-1}\operatorname{Log}(T^{\mathrm{fr}})=B. \tag{3}
\]
Since \(B\) is Hurwitz, it is invertible; substituting (2)--(3) into the definition of the full-rank inverse and using \(J=B^{-1}(T-I_p)\) gives
\[
 J^{\mathrm{fr}}=J,\qquad K^{\mathrm{fr}}=J^{-2}\Gamma_1=K.     \tag{4}
\]

The mean identity proved in equation (1) of \(\text{lem:covariance-semigroup}\) gives \(m=\Delta\lambda\).  Therefore
\[
 D^{\mathrm{fr}}=\operatorname{diag}(m/\Delta)=D.                \tag{5}
\]
The same lemma gives \(BQ+QB^{\mathsf T}=-D\), so \(Q\) is a symmetric solution of the recovered Lyapunov equation.  It is the unique solution: if a matrix \(X\) satisfies \(BX+XB^{\mathsf T}=0\), then
\[
 \frac{d}{dt}\{e^{Bt}Xe^{B^{\mathsf T}t}\}=0.
\]
The displayed matrix is therefore constant in \(t\), while it tends to zero as \(t\to\infty\) because \(B\) is Hurwitz.  Thus \(X=0\).  Hence
\[
 Q^{\mathrm{fr}}=Q\succ0.                                       \tag{6}
\]

Multiplying the last identity in (1) by \(Q^{-1}\) and using \(B=A-\beta I_p\) now gives the exact cancellation
\[
\begin{aligned}
 KQ^{-1}+B^2
 &=A(2\beta I_p-A)+(A-\beta I_p)^2\\
 &=2\beta A-A^2+A^2-2\beta A+\beta^2I_p\\
 &=\beta^2I_p.                                                    \tag{7}
\end{aligned}
\]
Taking traces in (7), dividing by \(p\geq2\), and taking the positive square root, justified by \(\beta>0\), yields \(\beta^{\mathrm{fr}}=\beta\).  Equations (3) and (7) then yield
\[
 A^{\mathrm{fr}}=B+\beta I_p=A.                                 \tag{8}
\]
Finally, rearranging the stationary mean equation \(\lambda=\mu+(A/\beta)\lambda\) and using (5), (7), and (8) gives
\[
 \mu^{\mathrm{fr}}=(I_p-A/\beta)(m/\Delta)=\mu.                 \tag{9}
\]
Equations (2)--(9) prove the asserted recovery.  The proof nowhere uses acyclicity, a zero diagonal, or a one-way orientation; therefore cyclic, reciprocal, and self-exciting members are included whenever the explicit stability, full-rank, and principal-log strip hypotheses hold.
