Write
\[
 \phi(t)=Ae^{-\beta t}\mathbf 1_{\{t>0\}},\qquad
 \widetilde N(dt)=N(dt)-\lambda\,dt,
 \qquad M(dt)=N(dt)-\ell(t)\,dt .
\]
Membership in \(\mathcal M_{\Delta,p}^{\mathrm{exp,st}}\) supplies stationarity, the intensity equation, \(\mu_i>0\), \(\beta>0\), \(A_{ij}\geq0\), and \(\operatorname{spr}(A/\beta)<1\).  Taking expectations in the intensity equation, justified by nonnegativity and stationarity, gives
\[
 \lambda=\mu+\left(\int_0^\infty Ae^{-\beta u}\,du\right)\lambda
 =\mu+(A/\beta)\lambda .                                      \tag{1}
\]
The spectral-radius condition makes the Neumann series converge in finite dimension, so
\[
 \lambda=(I_p-A/\beta)^{-1}\mu
 =\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}(A/\beta)^d\mu .                            \tag{2}
\]
Every summand in (2) is nonnegative and the zeroth is strictly positive.  Hence every \(\lambda_i>0\) and \(D=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda)\succ0\).

Subtracting (1) from the intensity equation yields the centered convolution equation
\[
 \widetilde N=M+\phi*\widetilde N .                              \tag{3}
\]
For \(d\geq1\), direct induction using the beta-integral gives
\[
 \phi^{*d}(t)=A^d e^{-\beta t}\frac{t^{d-1}}{(d-1)!}\mathbf 1_{\{t>0\}}.
\]
Consequently the causal resolvent is
\[
 R(t)=\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}\phi^{*d}(t)
 =e^{-\beta t}e^{At}A=e^{Bt}A,\qquad t>0.                       \tag{4}
\]
The series converges absolutely on compact time intervals, and its \(L^1\) matrix norm is finite because \(\operatorname{spr}(A/\beta)<1\).  Iterating (3), with the remainder converging to zero in second mean, therefore gives \(\widetilde N=M+R*M\).

The compensators in the intensity model are absolutely continuous.  Thus two labeled coordinates have no common jump almost surely, and the martingale innovation measure has predictable covariance \(D\,dt\).  Expanding \(\widetilde N=M+R*M\) against this orthogonal innovation measure gives, for \(t>0\),
\[
 C_+(t)=R(t)D+\int_0^\infty R(t+u)D R(u)^{\mathsf T}\,du .      \tag{5}
\]
This equality first holds after integration over bounded rectangles; absolute integrability of (4) then permits differentiation away from lag zero.

If \(z\) is an eigenvalue of \(A\), then \(|z|\leq\operatorname{spr}(A)<\beta\).  Hence every eigenvalue of \(B=A-\beta I_p\) has strictly negative real part, so \(B\) is Hurwitz.  It follows that
\[
 Q=\int_0^\infty e^{Bu}D e^{B^{\mathsf T}u}\,du                \tag{6}
\]
is finite.  It is symmetric, and for \(v\neq0\),
\[
 v^{\mathsf T}Qv
 =\int_0^\infty\lVert D^{1/2}e^{B^{\mathsf T}u}v\rVert_2^2,du>0, \tag{7}
\]
because the continuous integrand at \(u=0\) equals \(v^{\mathsf T}Dv>0\).  Thus \(Q\succ0\).  Integrating the derivative of the integrand in (6), whose limit at infinity is zero because \(B\) is Hurwitz, yields
\[
 BQ+QB^{\mathsf T}=-D,\qquad
 D=2\beta Q-AQ-QA^{\mathsf T}.                                  \tag{8}
\]

Since \(A\) commutes with \(B\) and its exponential, (4)--(6) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
 C_+(t)
 &=e^{Bt}\left\{AD+AQA^{\mathsf T}\right\},\\
 AD+AQA^{\mathsf T}
 &=A\{2\beta Q-AQ-QA^{\mathsf T}\}+AQA^{\mathsf T}\\
 &=A(2\beta I_p-A)Q.
\end{aligned}                                                     \tag{9}
\]
Thus (9) is \(C_+(t)=e^{Bt}K\) with the asserted \(K\).  Because \(Q\) is symmetric, \(AQA^{\mathsf T}\) is symmetric, and the first line of (9) also gives
\[
 K-K^{\mathsf T}=AD-DA^{\mathsf T}.                              \tag{10}
\]

For \(k\geq1\), the two bins meet at most at a deterministic endpoint, which has zero covariance measure.  With the future and past within-bin coordinates denoted by \(u,v\in[0,\Delta]\), stationarity, (9), and Fubini's theorem give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \Gamma_k
 &=\int_0^\Delta\int_0^\Delta
 e^{B(k\Delta+u-v)}K\,du,dv\\
 &=e^{B(k-1)\Delta}
 \left(\int_0^\Delta e^{Bu}\,du\right)
 \left(\int_0^\Delta e^{B(\Delta-v)}\,dv\right)K\\
 &=T^{k-1}J^2K .
\end{aligned}                                                     \tag{11}
\]

It remains to prove the image assertion without an invertibility assumption on \(A\).  Since \(B\) is Hurwitz, it is invertible and
\[
 J=\int_0^\Delta e^{Bu}\,du=B^{-1}(T-I_p).                      \tag{12}
\]
For every eigenvalue \(w\) of \(B\), \(\operatorname{Re}(w)<0\), so
\[
 f(w)=\frac{e^{w\Delta}-1}{w}\neq0 .
\]
Triangularizing \(B\) shows that the diagonal entries of \(J=f(B)\) are precisely the corresponding \(f(w)\); hence \(J\) is invertible.  Moreover, \(J\) and \(J^{-1}\) commute with \(A\), so \(J\operatorname{im}(A)=\operatorname{im}(A)\).  The matrix \((2\beta I_p-A)Q\) is invertible: \(Q\) is invertible by (7), and \(|z|<\beta\) for every eigenvalue \(z\) of \(A\), so \(2\beta I_p-A\) is invertible.  Equations (9) and (11) now give
\[
 \operatorname{im}(K)=\operatorname{im}(A),\qquad
 \operatorname{im}(\Gamma_1)=J^2\operatorname{im}(K)
 =\operatorname{im}(A),                                         \tag{13}
\]
and therefore \(\operatorname{rank}(\Gamma_1)=\operatorname{rank}(A)\).

Finally, if the support of \(A\) is acyclic under the loop-inclusive convention, a simultaneous permutation of rows and columns makes \(A\) strictly triangular.  Hence \(A^p=0\), \(\operatorname{spr}(A/\beta)=0<1\), and every law in \(\overline{\mathcal M}_{\Delta,p}^{\mathrm{DAG}}\) belongs to \(\mathcal M_{\Delta,p}^{\mathrm{exp,st}}\).  This proves the stated acyclic special cases without any division by \(A\), eigenvector selection, distinct-pole condition, connectivity condition, or structural-rank condition.
