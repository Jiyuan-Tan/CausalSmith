For every three-node chain and transitive-triangle (the three-node ``diamond'' motif in the frozen family), \(\operatorname{rank}(A)=2\); for every three-node fork, \(\operatorname{rank}(A)=1\).  Since \(\operatorname{rank}(\Gamma_1)=\operatorname{rank}(A)\), all other named rank-support strata are empty.  On a fork relabeled as \(1\to2\) and \(1\to3\), write \(A_{21}=a\), \(A_{31}=b\), \(x=a/\beta\), and \(y=b/\beta\).  Direct substitution in the covariance formula gives \(AK=0\) and
\[
 \Gamma_1=c(\beta)^2K,\qquad c(\beta)=\frac{1-e^{-\beta\Delta}}{\beta},
\]
with \(K_{21}=a\lambda_1\), \(K_{31}=b\lambda_1\), \(K_{22}=a^2\lambda_1/(2\beta)\), \(K_{23}=K_{32}=ab\lambda_1/(2\beta)\), and \(K_{33}=b^2\lambda_1/(2\beta)\).  Thus the one-lag observables identify \(x,y\) and \(h(\beta)=(1-e^{-\beta\Delta})^2/\beta\).  The function \(h\) increases and then decreases, with unique maximizer \(\beta_*\Delta=x_*\), where \(e^{x_*}=1+2x_*\).  Hence the within-fork fiber is a singleton exactly at \(\beta=\beta_*\), and otherwise contains the unique parameter on the opposite side of \(\beta_*\) having the same \(h\), with \(a'=x\beta'\), \(b'=y\beta'\), and the same \(\mu=(I-A/\beta)\lambda\).  The two laws differ because \(\Gamma_2=e^{-\beta\Delta}\Gamma_1\).  Each side of \(\beta_*\) therefore supplies the requested nonempty relatively open alias region for the fork stratum.
