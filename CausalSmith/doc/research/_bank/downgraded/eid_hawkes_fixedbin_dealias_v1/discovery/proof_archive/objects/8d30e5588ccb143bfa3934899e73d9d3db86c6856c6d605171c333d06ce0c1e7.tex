Fix \(\eta\in\mathfrak I_{\Delta,p}^{\mathrm{WH}}\) and an arbitrary admissible representative \((\beta,A,\mu)\).  By \(\text{def:stable-nonzero-restricted-strip-moment-image}\),
\[
 A\neq0,\qquad \rho(A/\beta)<1,\qquad
 \operatorname{spec}\!\left(\left.\Delta B\right|_{\operatorname{im}(A)}\right)
 \subset\{z\in\mathbb C:|\operatorname{Im}z|<\pi\}.
\tag{1}
\]
The positive decay, positive baseline, nonnegative excitation, stationarity, and stability conditions used below are all part of membership in \(\mathcal M_{\Delta,p}^{\mathrm{stab}}\).  In particular, \(\lambda\in(0,\infty)^p\), so \(D=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda)\succ0\).

By \(\text{lem:stable-covariance-semigroup}\),
\[
 S=\operatorname{im}(\Gamma_1)=\operatorname{im}(A)=\operatorname{im}(K),\quad
 r=\operatorname{rank}(A)\geq1,\quad
 \Gamma_1=J^2K,\quad \Gamma_2=T\Gamma_1,
\tag{2}
\]
and \(B\) is Hurwitz while \(J\) is invertible.  The inequality \(r\geq1\) follows from \(A\neq0\).  Since \(A\operatorname{im}(A)\subseteq\operatorname{im}(A)\), the space \(S\) is invariant under \(A\), hence under \(B=A-\beta I_p\), \(T=e^{B\Delta}\), and \(J=\int_0^\Delta e^{Bu}\,du\).

Choose an admissible orthonormal basis \(O\), and put \(R=O^{\mathsf T}\Gamma_1\).  Then \(\Gamma_1=OR\).  Since \(O\) and \(\Gamma_1\) both have rank \(r\), \(R\) has full row rank and \(RR^{\mathsf T}\succ0\).  There is a unique \(F_0\) with \(TO=OF_0\).  Equation (2) yields
\[
 O^{\mathsf T}\Gamma_2R^{\mathsf T}(RR^{\mathsf T})^{-1}
 =F_0RR^{\mathsf T}(RR^{\mathsf T})^{-1}=F_0,
\tag{3}
\]
so \(F=F_0=[T|_S]_O\).  If \(L_0=O^{\mathsf T}BO\), invariance gives \(BO=OL_0\) and hence \(F=e^{\Delta L_0}\).  The strip condition in (1) and \(\text{lem:principal-log-strip}\), applied on the \(r\)-dimensional invariant space, imply
\[
 L^{\mathrm{WH}}=\Delta^{-1}\operatorname{Log}(F)=L_0=[B|_S]_O.
\tag{4}
\]
Because \(B|_S\) is Hurwitz, \(L^{\mathrm{WH}}\) is invertible and every eigenvalue of \(F\) has modulus less than one; thus \(F-I_r\) is invertible.  Moreover, \(F=e^{\Delta L^{\mathrm{WH}}}\), so \(F-I_r\) commutes with \(L^{\mathrm{WH}}\).  Writing \(J_S=O^{\mathsf T}JO\), integration of \(e^{Bu}O=Oe^{L_0u}\) gives
\[
 J_S=\int_0^\Delta e^{L_0u}\,du
 =L_0^{-1}(F-I_r),\qquad
 H^{\mathrm{WH}}=L^{\mathrm{WH}}(F-I_r)^{-1}=J_S^{-1}.
\tag{5}
\]
Write \(K=OK_S\), which is possible by (2).  Since \(J^2O=OJ_S^2\), equations (2) and (5) give
\[
 R=O^{\mathsf T}\Gamma_1=J_S^2K_S,\qquad
 P^{\mathrm{WH}}=(H^{\mathrm{WH}})^2R=K_S,\qquad
 K^{\mathrm{WH}}=OP^{\mathrm{WH}}=K.
\tag{6}
\]
Therefore, for every \(t>0\),
\[
 C_{+}^{\mathrm{WH}}(t)=Oe^{L^{\mathrm{WH}}t}P^{\mathrm{WH}}
 =e^{Bt}K=C_{+}(t)
\tag{7}
\]
by \(\text{lem:stable-covariance-semigroup}\).  This proves exact recovery of the entire positive-lag covariance density from \((\Gamma_1,\Gamma_2)\).

Stationarity and the compensator identity give
\[
 m=\mathbb E\{N(\Delta)-N(0)\}
 =\mathbb E\int_0^\Delta\ell(u)\,du=\Delta\lambda.
\tag{8}
\]
Because \(\Delta>0\), \(\lambda^{\mathrm{WH}}=m/\Delta=\lambda\) and \(D^{\mathrm{WH}}=D\succ0\), so all displayed inverses involving \(D^{\mathrm{WH}}\) exist.  Equations (7), (8), \(\text{def:complete-hawkes-response}\), and \(\text{lem:complete-response-orientation}\) show that \(g^{\mathrm{WH}}=g\) almost everywhere on \(\mathbb R\), including the transpose and right normalization on negative lags.

We next verify the advertised finite transform.  For \(x>0\), all eigenvalues of \((L^{\mathrm{WH}})^{\mathsf T}-xI_r\) have negative real part.  Hence its exponential tends to zero, and the fundamental theorem of calculus gives
\[
 \int_0^\infty e^{((L^{\mathrm{WH}})^{\mathsf T}-xI_r)t}\,dt
 =(xI_r-(L^{\mathrm{WH}})^{\mathsf T})^{-1}.
\tag{9}
\]
Transposing (7) and using (9) yields
\[
 \mathcal R^{\mathrm{WH}}(x)
 =\int_0^\infty e^{-xt}(P^{\mathrm{WH}})^{\mathsf T}
 e^{(L^{\mathrm{WH}})^{\mathsf T}t}O^{\mathsf T}\,dt
 =(P^{\mathrm{WH}})^{\mathsf T}(xI_r-(L^{\mathrm{WH}})^{\mathsf T})^{-1}O^{\mathsf T}.
\tag{10}
\]
Thus evaluating \(\mathcal R^{\mathrm{WH}}\) requires only an \(r\)-dimensional matrix resolvent.

We prove existence of the true root-pair.  From (7), \(B=A-\beta I_p\), and the Hurwitz property,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal R^{\mathrm{WH}}(\beta)
 &=\int_0^\infty e^{-\beta t}K^{\mathsf T}e^{B^{\mathsf T}t}\,dt\\
 &=K^{\mathsf T}(2\beta I_p-A^{\mathsf T})^{-1}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
The matrix \(2\beta I_p-A^{\mathsf T}\) is invertible because \(\rho(A/\beta)<1\).  Since \(Q\) is symmetric and \(K=A(2\beta I_p-A)Q\), while \(A^{\mathsf T}\) commutes with \(2\beta I_p-A^{\mathsf T}\), equation (11) becomes
\[
 \mathcal R^{\mathrm{WH}}(\beta)
 =Q(2\beta I_p-A^{\mathsf T})A^{\mathsf T}
   (2\beta I_p-A^{\mathsf T})^{-1}=QA^{\mathsf T}.
\tag{12}
\]
For completeness, differentiate the integrand defining \(Q\).  Since \(B\) is Hurwitz, its boundary value at infinity is zero, and therefore
\[
 BQ+QB^{\mathsf T}
 =\int_0^\infty\frac{d}{du}
   \{e^{Bu}De^{B^{\mathsf T}u}\}\,du=-D.
\tag{13}
\]
Substitution of \(B=A-\beta I_p\) into (13) gives
\[
 D+QA^{\mathsf T}=(2\beta I_p-A)Q.
\tag{14}
\]
Equations (12)--(14) and the definition of \(K\) imply
\[
 D+\mathcal R^{\mathrm{WH}}(\beta)=(2\beta I_p-A)Q,\qquad
 A[D+\mathcal R^{\mathrm{WH}}(\beta)]=K.
\tag{15}
\]
Both \(Q\) and \(2\beta I_p-A\) are invertible, the first because \(Q\succ0\) and the second by stability.  Thus \(\beta\in\Omega^{\mathrm{WH}}\).  Finally, (4) and \(B=A-\beta I_p\) give
\[
 AO=O(L^{\mathrm{WH}}+\beta I_r).
\tag{16}
\]
Hence \((\beta,A)\) satisfies both candidate equations.

It remains to prove uniqueness without leaving an infinite-dimensional operation in the evaluator.  Let \((x,M)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb R^{p\times p}\) satisfy
\[
 MO=O(L^{\mathrm{WH}}+xI_r),\qquad
 M[D+\mathcal R^{\mathrm{WH}}(x)]=K.
\tag{17}
\]
Define only for this proof the causal, entrywise-integrable comparison kernel
\[
 \chi_x(s)=Me^{-xs}\mathbf 1_{\{s>0\}}.
\tag{18}
\]
Fix \(t>0\), and split \((\chi_x*g)(t)\) at \(s=t\).  On \(0<s<t\), equations (6)--(7), the first equation in (17), and \(BO=OL^{\mathrm{WH}}\) imply that \(M\) acts as \(B+xI_p\) on every column of \(e^{B(t-s)}K\).  Therefore
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \int_0^t Me^{-xs}g(t-s)\,ds
 &=\int_0^t Me^{-xs}e^{B(t-s)}K D^{-1}\,ds\\
 &=-\int_0^t\frac{d}{ds}
   \{e^{-xs}e^{B(t-s)}K\}\,ds\,D^{-1}\\
 &=g(t)-e^{-xt}KD^{-1}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]
On \(s>t\), the negative-lag branch of \(g\) from \(\text{def:complete-hawkes-response}\) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \int_t^\infty Me^{-xs}g(t-s)\,ds
 &=\int_t^\infty Me^{-xs}K^{\mathsf T}e^{B^{\mathsf T}(s-t)}D^{-1}\,ds\\
 &=e^{-xt}M\mathcal R^{\mathrm{WH}}(x)D^{-1}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]
Adding (18)--(20), and then using the second equation in (17), proves
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \chi_x(t)+(\chi_x*g)(t)
 &=g(t)+e^{-xt}\{M+M\mathcal R^{\mathrm{WH}}(x)D^{-1}-KD^{-1}\}\\
 &=g(t)+e^{-xt}\{M[D+\mathcal R^{\mathrm{WH}}(x)]-K\}D^{-1}=g(t).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]
Thus every finite-dimensional candidate pair produces a causal entrywise-\(L^1\) solution of the original complete-response Wiener--Hopf equation.

The true Hawkes kernel \(\phi(t)=Ae^{-\beta t}\mathbf 1_{\{t>0\}}\) is causal and entrywise integrable because \(\beta>0\), and its integrated kernel is \(A/\beta\), whose spectral radius is strictly below one by (1).  By \(\text{lem:complete-response-orientation}\) and \(\text{lem:bacry-muzy-wiener-hopf-uniqueness}\), (21) forces
\[
 Me^{-xt}=Ae^{-\beta t}\quad\text{for almost every }t>0.
\tag{22}
\]
Both sides are continuous on \((0,\infty)\), so (22) holds everywhere there.  Letting \(t\downarrow0\) gives \(M=A\).  Since \(A\neq0\), some entry of \(A\) is nonzero; equality of that entry at any two distinct positive times then gives \(x=\beta\).  Thus \((\beta,A)\) is the unique candidate pair.  On \(\Omega^{\mathrm{WH}}\), the second equation in (17) is equivalent to \(M=\mathcal A^{\mathrm{WH}}(x)\), so the scalar equation in the definition has the unique root \(\beta^{\#}=\beta\), and \(A^{\#}=A\).

Taking expectations in the Hawkes intensity equation and using stationarity yields
\[
 \lambda=\mu+(A/\beta)\lambda.
\tag{23}
\]
Together with (8), (23) proves
\[
 \mu^{\#}=(I_p-A^{\#}/\beta^{\#})\lambda^{\mathrm{WH}}
 =(I_p-A/\beta)\lambda=\mu.
\tag{24}
\]
This proves exact recovery of the structural triple and of \(G(A)=\{j\to i:A_{ij}>0\}\); the latter equality is the graph definition, not an identification assumption.

For basis invariance, let \(O'=OU\), where \(U\) is orthogonal.  Direct substitution and uniqueness of the principal logarithm yield
\[
 R'=U^{\mathsf T}R,\quad F'=U^{\mathsf T}FU,\quad
 L^{\mathrm{WH}\prime}=U^{\mathsf T}L^{\mathrm{WH}}U,\quad
 H^{\mathrm{WH}\prime}=U^{\mathsf T}H^{\mathrm{WH}}U,\quad
 P^{\mathrm{WH}\prime}=U^{\mathsf T}P^{\mathrm{WH}}.
\tag{25}
\]
Consequently \(K^{\mathrm{WH}}\), \(C_{+}^{\mathrm{WH}}\), \(\mathcal R^{\mathrm{WH}}\), and \(g^{\mathrm{WH}}\) are unchanged.  The first equation in (17) after the basis change is obtained by right-multiplying its original version by \(U\), so the candidate set and its unique pair are unchanged.  Every operation and the final output are therefore basis invariant.  Since the admissible representative at the start was arbitrary, equations (1)--(24) force every representative of the same observed moment triple to equal this uniquely recovered triple; this proves representative invariance.

Finally, consider \(p=2\), arbitrary \(\Delta>0\), \(\beta=1\), \(\mu=\mathbf 1_2\), and \(A=\tfrac14\mathbf 1_2\mathbf 1_2^{\mathsf T}\).  Its eigenvalues are \(1/2\) and \(0\), so \(\rho(A/\beta)=1/2<1\).  A direct stationary immigration--birth construction is available: immigrants of each type form homogeneous Poisson processes of rate one, and every type-\(j\) point produces independent type-\(i\) Poisson offspring at delay density \(A_{ij}e^{-t}\mathbf 1_{\{t>0\}}\).  The expected total descendants are bounded by the entrywise-finite series \(\sum_{d\geq0}A^d=(I_2-A)^{-1}\); hence every cluster is finite almost surely, and Campbell's formula gives finite expected counts on bounded intervals for their stationary superposition, hence local finiteness almost surely.  Translation invariance gives stationarity, continuous offspring delays give simplicity, and summing the independent offspring hazards gives the stated conditional intensity.  Thus the construction is a member of \(\mathcal M_{\Delta,2}^{\mathrm{stab}}\).  Its image is \(\operatorname{span}(\mathbf 1_2)\), and \(B\) restricts there to multiplication by \(-1/2\), so the restricted spectrum is real and satisfies the strip condition for every \(\Delta>0\).  The matrix \(A\) has determinant zero, positive diagonal entries, positive reciprocal off-diagonal entries, and non-coordinate image, which proves the final strictness example.
