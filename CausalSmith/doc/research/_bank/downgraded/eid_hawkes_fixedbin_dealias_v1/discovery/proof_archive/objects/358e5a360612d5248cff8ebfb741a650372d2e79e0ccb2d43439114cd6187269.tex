At the empty graph, choose any positive \(\mu_0\) and any two distinct decays \(\beta_0,\beta_1\) for which \(\theta_j=(\beta_j,0,\mu_0)\) both belong to \(\overline{\mathcal M}_{\Delta,p}(\kappa_0)\).  The two observable laws are identical by \(\text{prop:empty-graph-equivalence}\).  For every honest procedure, each event \(\{\theta_j\in\mathcal T_n\}\) has probability at least \(1-\alpha\) under this common law, so their intersection has probability at least \((1-2\alpha)_+\).  On that intersection,
\[
 \operatorname{diam}_\Theta(\mathcal T_n)\geq1\wedge|\beta_1-\beta_0|.
\]
Consequently \(R_{\Theta,n,\kappa_0}(0)\geq(1-2\alpha)_+\{1\wedge|\beta_1-\beta_0|\}\) for every \(n\).  This proves a constant-diameter obstruction at the empty boundary, but it does not determine the sharp local frontier for positive \(\delta_n\), where contiguity between weak nonempty graphs and the empty graph must be quantified.
