Fix \(\eta=(m,\Gamma_1,\Gamma_2)\in\mathfrak I_{\Delta,p}^{\mathrm{fr}}\).  By \(\text{def:full-rank-stable-moment-image}\), there is a structural representative \((\beta,A,\mu)\) inducing these moments, with \(\det(A)\neq0\) and
\[
 \operatorname{spec}\{\Delta(A-\beta I_p)\}
 \subset\{z\in\mathbb C:|\operatorname{Im}z|<\pi\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Choose an arbitrary such representative and put \(B=A-\beta I_p\).  By \(\text{lem:stable-covariance-semigroup}\), \(B\) is real Hurwitz, \(Q\succ0\), \(J\) is invertible, and
\[
 \Gamma_1=J^2K,\qquad
 \Gamma_2=TJ^2K,\qquad
 T=e^{B\Delta},\qquad
 K=A(2\beta I_p-A)Q.
\tag{2}
\]

Membership in \(\mathcal M_{\Delta,p}^{\mathrm{stab}}\) gives \(\rho(A/\beta)<1\) and \(\beta>0\), hence \(\rho(A)<\beta\).  Thus every \(z\in\operatorname{spec}(A)\) satisfies \(|z|\leq\rho(A)<\beta\), so \(2\beta-z\neq0\).  Therefore \(2\beta I_p-A\) is invertible.  The matrix \(A\) is invertible by the full-rank condition in \(\text{def:full-rank-stable-moment-image}\), and \(Q\) is invertible because \(Q\succ0\); consequently the last product in (2) shows that \(K\) is invertible.  Since \(J\) is invertible, \(\Gamma_1=J^2K\) is invertible as well.  The first observable reconstruction in \(\text{def:full-rank-two-lag-inverse}\) is therefore well defined, and (2) gives
\[
 T^\dagger
 =\Gamma_2\Gamma_1^{-1}
 =TJ^2K(J^2K)^{-1}
 =T=e^{B\Delta}.
\tag{3}
\]

The strip condition (1) and \(\text{lem:principal-log-strip}\) imply that the principal logarithm in \(\text{def:full-rank-two-lag-inverse}\) exists and satisfies
\[
 B^\dagger
 =\Delta^{-1}\operatorname{Log}(T^\dagger)
 =\Delta^{-1}\operatorname{Log}(e^{B\Delta})
 =B.
\tag{4}
\]
Here division by \(\Delta\) is valid because \(\Delta>0\).  In particular, \(B^\dagger\) is real Hurwitz and hence invertible.  By spectral mapping, every eigenvalue of \(T^\dagger=e^{B\Delta}\) has modulus strictly less than one, so \(T^\dagger-I_p\) is invertible.  Thus \(J^\dagger\) is well defined, and its defining formula together with (3)--(4) yields
\[
 J^\dagger
 =(B^\dagger)^{-1}(T^\dagger-I_p)
 =B^{-1}(T-I_p)
 =J.
\tag{5}
\]
It follows from (2) and (5) that
\[
 K^\dagger=(J^\dagger)^{-2}\Gamma_1
 =J^{-2}J^2K=K.
\tag{6}
\]
In particular, both \(J^\dagger\) and \(K^\dagger\) are invertible.

Stationarity and \(\text{def:population-moments}\) give
\[
 m=\mathbb E[N(\Delta)-N(0)]=\Delta\lambda.
\tag{7}
\]
Hence \(\lambda^\dagger=m/\Delta=\lambda\) and \(D^\dagger=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda)=D\succ0\); positivity follows from the positive stationary intensities in the stable Hawkes class.  Since \(B^\dagger=B\) is Hurwitz, there are constants \(c>0\) and \(M<\infty\) such that \(\lVert e^{Bu}\rVert\leq Me^{-cu}\) for \(u\geq0\).  Therefore the integral defining \(Q^\dagger\) converges absolutely, and its definition gives
\[
 Q^\dagger
 =\int_0^\infty e^{Bu}De^{B^{\mathsf T}u}\,du
 =Q\succ0.
\tag{8}
\]
Thus \(Q^\dagger\) is invertible.  Moreover, differentiating the integrand, using absolute convergence, and using Hurwitz decay give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 BQ^\dagger+Q^\dagger B^{\mathsf T}
 &=\int_0^\infty \frac{d}{du}
   \bigl(e^{Bu}De^{B^{\mathsf T}u}\bigr)\,du\\
 &=\lim_{v\to\infty}e^{Bv}De^{B^{\mathsf T}v}-D
 =-D.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
This solution is unique.  Indeed, if \(H\) is the difference of two solutions, then \(BH+HB^{\mathsf T}=0\), whence
\[
 \frac{d}{du}\bigl(e^{Bu}He^{B^{\mathsf T}u}\bigr)=0.
\]
The displayed matrix is consequently equal to \(H\) for every \(u\geq0\), whereas Hurwitz decay makes it converge to the zero matrix as \(u\to\infty\).  Hence \(H=0\).  Equations (4), (7), and (9) prove the asserted Lyapunov characterization with \(B^\dagger\) and \(D^\dagger\).

It remains to identify the scalar shift separating \(A\) from \(B\).  Since \(A=B+\beta I_p\), the last identity in (2) becomes
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 K
 &=A(2\beta I_p-A)Q\\
 &=(B+\beta I_p)(\beta I_p-B)Q\\
 &=(\beta^2I_p-B^2)Q.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
Using (4), (6), (8), and (10),
\[
 K^\dagger(Q^\dagger)^{-1}+(B^\dagger)^2
 =\beta^2I_p.
\tag{11}
\]
Taking traces and using \(p\geq2\) gives
\[
 s^\dagger
 =p^{-1}\operatorname{tr}(\beta^2I_p)
 =\beta^2>0.
\tag{12}
\]
The inverse uses the positive real square root, and \(\beta>0\), so \(\beta^\dagger=\beta\).  Equation (4) then yields
\[
 A^\dagger=B^\dagger+\beta^\dagger I_p
 =B+\beta I_p=A.
\tag{13}
\]

For completeness, the baseline identity follows from the defining common-exponential Hawkes intensity.  Taking expectations in
\[
 \ell(t)=\mu+\int_{(-\infty,t)}Ae^{-\beta(t-s)}\,dN(s)
\]
is justified componentwise by monotone approximation of the nonnegative integrand; stationarity and finiteness of \(\lambda\) then give
\[
 \lambda
 =\mu+\int_0^\infty Ae^{-\beta u}\lambda\,du
 =\mu+(A/\beta)\lambda.
\tag{14}
\]
The integral is finite because \(\beta>0\).  Rearranging (14) and applying (7), (12), and (13) gives
\[
 \mu^\dagger
 =(I_p-A^\dagger/\beta^\dagger)\lambda^\dagger
 =(I_p-A/\beta)\lambda
 =\mu.
\tag{15}
\]

The representative chosen after (1) was arbitrary, while every daggered quantity is constructed solely from \(\eta\) and the known \(\Delta\).  Equations (3)--(15) therefore hold for every representative and force every representative to equal the same recovered triple \((\beta^\dagger,A^\dagger,\mu^\dagger)\).  This proves representative independence, all asserted well-definedness, invertibility, positivity, and exact-recovery conclusions.  Finally, \(\text{def:stable-common-exponential-class}\) imposes no acyclicity, zero-diagonal, orientation, or reciprocity restriction; hence the same inverse covers the full-rank cyclic, reciprocal, and self-exciting members of the image domain subject to the stated principal-log strip.
