The proof uses an exact product-mixture reduction for necessity and an explicit diagonal causal completion for sufficiency. The observed law \(P\) is an arbitrary point of the four-cell simplex \(\mathcal P(\{0,1\}^2)\), and \(\kappa\in[0,\log2]\). No positivity, overlap, rank, division by an observed probability, boundary exclusion, or limiting condition is imposed. Observed cells and latent masses may vanish, and \(0\log0=0\).

Suppose first that \((0,0)\in\Theta_\kappa(P)\). By \(\texttt{def:identified-set}\), there is a law \(Q\in\mathfrak M_\kappa(P)\) with \(B(Q)=H(Q)=0\). Because the two off-diagonal response events are disjoint and exhaust the event of unequal binary potential outcomes,
\[
\begin{aligned}
Q\{Y(0)\neq Y(1)\}
&=Q\{Y(0)=0,Y(1)=1\}+Q\{Y(0)=1,Y(1)=0\}\\
&=B(Q)+H(Q)=0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
Thus \(V:=Y(0)\) satisfies \(V=Y(0)=Y(1)\), \(Q\)-almost surely. For every \(u\) with \(Q(U=u)>0\), \(\texttt{ass:latent-exchangeability}\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
Q(A=a,V=y\mid U=u)
&=\sum_{j=0}^1 Q(A=a,Y(0)=y,Y(1)=j\mid U=u)\\
&=Q(A=a\mid U=u)
  \sum_{j=0}^1Q(Y(0)=y,Y(1)=j\mid U=u)\\
&=Q(A=a\mid U=u)Q(V=y\mid U=u).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
By \(\texttt{ass:consistency}\) and (1),
\[
Y=Y(A)=V\qquad Q\text{-almost surely}.
\tag{3}
\]
Substituting (3) into (2) proves \(A\perp Y\mid U\) under the marginal extension \(\widetilde P:=Q_{A,Y,U}\). By \(\texttt{ass:factual-compatibility}\), \(\widetilde P_{A,Y}=P\), and by \(\texttt{ass:entropy-budget}\),
\[
\operatorname{Ent}_{\widetilde P}(U)=\operatorname{Ent}_Q(U)\leq\kappa.
\tag{4}
\]
The alphabet of \(U\) is finite by \(\texttt{def:model-fiber}\). Hence \(\widetilde P\) is feasible in \(\texttt{def:common-entropy}\), and
\[
G_P(A;Y)\leq\operatorname{Ent}_{\widetilde P}(U)
=\operatorname{Ent}_Q(U)\leq\kappa.
\tag{5}
\]
This establishes necessity by mapping a causal origin witness to an observed-law product mixture; it does not equate the causal target with the observed functional by definition.

Conversely, assume \(G_P(A;Y)\leq\kappa\). Apply \(\texttt{lem:binary-common-entropy-two-state}\) to the binary pair \((A,Y)\). There is an extension \(\widetilde P\) of \(P\) such that
\[
A\perp_{\widetilde P}Y\mid U,
\qquad
|\operatorname{supp}_{\widetilde P}(U)|\leq2,
\qquad
\operatorname{Ent}_{\widetilde P}(U)=G_P(A;Y)\leq\kappa.
\tag{6}
\]
Here support means positive-mass support. Equation (6) is the sole published product-mixture input.

Define a full-data probability mass function by the diagonal completion
\[
Q(a,i,j,y,u)
:=\widetilde P(a,y,u)\mathbf 1\{i=y\}\mathbf 1\{j=y\},
\qquad a,i,j,y\in\{0,1\}.
\tag{7}
\]
The right-hand side is nonnegative, and summing (7) over all coordinates gives one. Summing over \(i,j,u\) gives, for every observed cell,
\[
Q(A=a,Y=y)=\widetilde P(A=a,Y=y)=p_{ay},
\tag{8}
\]
so \(\texttt{ass:factual-compatibility}\) holds, including at zero cells. The indicators in (7) imply
\[
Q\{Y(0)=Y(1)=Y\}=1,
\tag{9}
\]
and therefore \(Y=Y(A)\), which is \(\texttt{ass:consistency}\).

For every positive-mass state \(u\), conditional independence in (6) and (7) yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
&Q(A=a,Y(0)=i,Y(1)=j\mid U=u)\\
&\quad=\mathbf 1\{i=j\}\widetilde P(A=a,Y=i\mid U=u)\\
&\quad=\widetilde P(A=a\mid U=u)
  \mathbf 1\{i=j\}\widetilde P(Y=i\mid U=u)\\
&\quad=Q(A=a\mid U=u)
  Q(Y(0)=i,Y(1)=j\mid U=u).
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
Thus \(\texttt{ass:latent-exchangeability}\) holds. Conditional kernels at zero-mass labels are irrelevant and may be chosen arbitrarily. The construction preserves both the positive support and the marginal of \(U\), so
\[
|\operatorname{supp}_{Q}(U)|
=|\operatorname{supp}_{\widetilde P}(U)|\leq2,
\qquad
\operatorname{Ent}_{Q}(U)=G_P(A;Y)\leq\kappa.
\tag{11}
\]
Consequently \(\texttt{ass:entropy-budget}\) and every member condition in \(\texttt{def:model-fiber}\) hold, whence \(Q\in\mathfrak M_\kappa(P)\). Finally, (9) gives
\[
B(Q)=Q\{Y(0)=0,Y(1)=1\}=0,
\qquad
H(Q)=Q\{Y(0)=1,Y(1)=0\}=0.
\tag{12}
\]
By \(\texttt{def:identified-set}\), (12) implies \((0,0)\in\Theta_\kappa(P)\). Equations (5) and (12) prove the equivalence, while (6)--(11) prove constructively that both the minimizing observed factor and the no-effect causal witness use at most two positive latent states.
