The technique is an extremal conditional-moment inequality with a matching two-component product mixture.  Under \(P^\star\), both observed marginals are uniform and the crossover probability is \(1/10\).  Direct substitution in the definition of mutual information gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 I_{P^\star}(A;Y)
 &=\frac9{10}\log\frac{9/20}{1/4}+\frac1{10}\log\frac{1/20}{1/4}\\
 &=\frac9{10}\log\frac95+\frac1{10}\log\frac15
 =\log2-h(1/10).
 \end{aligned}
\]
We next compute the common entropy without assuming its advertised value.  Put \(X=(-1)^A\) and \(Z=(-1)^Y\).  Then
\[
 E(X)=E(Z)=0,\qquad E(XZ)=\frac9{10}-\frac1{10}=\frac45.
\]
Consider any finite product-mixture representation of \(P^\star\), write \(\pi_u=P(U=u)\), \(x_u=E(X\mid U=u)\), and \(z_u=E(Z\mid U=u)\).  Conditional independence gives
\[
 \frac45=\sum_u\pi_u x_uz_u,
 \qquad \sum_u\pi_u x_u=\sum_u\pi_u z_u=0.
\]
Fix a state of positive mass \(w=\pi_u\).  Since \(|x_v|,|z_v|\leq1\), the two zero-mean identities imply
\[
 |x_u|\leq\frac{1-w}{w},\qquad
 |z_u|\leq\frac{1-w}{w}.
\]
It follows, using the triangle inequality and \(|x_vz_v|\leq1\), that
\[
 \frac45
 \leq w|x_uz_u|+\sum_{v\neq u}\pi_v|x_vz_v|
 \leq \frac{(1-w)^2}{w}+(1-w)
 =\frac{1-w}{w}.
\]
Thus every atom satisfies \(w\leq5/9\).  If \(q=\max_u\pi_u\leq1/2\), then
\[
 H(U)=\sum_u\pi_u\log(1/\pi_u)\geq\log(1/q)\geq\log2>h(4/9).
\]
If \(1/2\leq q\leq5/9\), grouping all atoms other than a maximizer and using nonnegativity of conditional entropy gives
\[
 H(U)=h(q)+(1-q)H(U\mid U\neq u_{\max})
 \geq h(q)\geq h(5/9)=h(4/9),
\]
where the middle inequality uses that \(h\) decreases on \([1/2,1]\).  This proves the lower bound for every finite auxiliary.

For the matching construction, take two auxiliary states with masses \(5/9\) and \(4/9\).  In the first state let \(A\) and \(Y\) be independent with both success probabilities equal to \(1/10\); in the second state let \(A=Y=1\) deterministically.  The four resulting probabilities are
\[
 \frac59\frac{81}{100}=\frac9{20},\quad
 \frac59\frac9{100}=\frac1{20},\quad
 \frac59\frac9{100}=\frac1{20},\quad
 \frac59\frac1{100}+\frac49=\frac9{20}.
\]
Hence this is a product-mixture representation of \(P^\star\) and its auxiliary entropy is \(h(4/9)\).  We have proved
\[
 G_{P^\star}(A;Y)=h(4/9).
\]

For completeness, the numerical inequalities can be certified using, for every rational \(x>0\),
\[
 \log x=2\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{z^{2k+1}}{2k+1}+R_n,qquad
 z=\frac{x-1}{x+1},\qquad
 |R_n|\leq\frac{2|z|^{2n+3}}{(2n+3)(1-z^2)}.
\]
Substitution of the rational arguments in the two entropy expressions with \(n=60\) yields the rational interval certificates
\[
 0.3680642<I_{P^\star}(A;Y)<0.3680643<\frac12
 <0.6869615<G_{P^\star}(A;Y)<0.6869617.
\]
These intervals imply the displayed decimal expansions and both strict inequalities.

It remains to justify persistence.  A four-state bound sufficient for this purpose follows directly: hold the conditional product components in any finite representation fixed and vary only their weights subject to the four factual-cell equations.  Shannon entropy is concave.  If an entropy minimizer in this compact weight polytope is not extreme, write it as a nontrivial convex combination of two feasible weights; concavity shows that at least one endpoint has no larger entropy, and iteration reaches an extreme minimizer.  If an extreme weight has more positive entries than the rank of the four cell equations, the corresponding columns are linearly dependent and a small signed perturbation in both directions preserves feasibility, contradicting extremality.  It therefore has at most four positive weights.  Repeating if necessary shows that the infimum defining \(G_P(A;Y)\) is attained in the compact four-state parameter space.  If \(P_n\to P\), take four-state minimizers and a convergent subsequence along which the entropies attain the lower limit.  Continuity of the product-mixture map and entropy gives a feasible limit at \(P\), and hence
\[
 G_P(A;Y)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}G_{P_n}(A;Y).
\]
Thus \(G_P(A;Y)\) is lower semicontinuous.  Mutual information is continuous on the positive binary-law simplex.  Since both inequalities at \(P^\star\) are strict, a relative neighborhood of \(P^\star\) satisfies \(I_P(A;Y)<1/2<G_P(A;Y)\).  The origin equivalence \(\texttt{thm:origin-common-entropy-frontier}\) then excludes \((0,0)\) from \(\Theta_{1/2}(P)\) throughout that neighborhood, while the mutual-information rule includes it.
