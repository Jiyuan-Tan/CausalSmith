Fix a binary observed law \(P\), a budget \(\kappa\in[0,\log 2]\), and
\(\theta\in\Theta_\kappa(P)\).  The first technique is exact finite-mixture
compression at a fixed joint law.  By
\(\texttt{lem:factor-program-equivalence}\), there are an integer \(m\geq 1\)
and \((\pi,e,r)\in\mathcal F_{m,\kappa}(P)\) such that
\(\Phi_m(\pi,e,r)=\theta\).  Put
\[
 \mathcal R_0=\{0,1\}^2
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 J\bigl(a,(i,j)\bigr)
 =\sum_{u=1}^m\pi_u e_u^a(1-e_u)^{1-a}r_{ij\mid u}.
 \tag{1}
\]
The right-hand side is nonnegative, and summing it over \(a,i,j\) gives
\[
 \sum_{u=1}^m\pi_u\{(1-e_u)+e_u\}
       \sum_{i,j=0}^1r_{ij\mid u}=\sum_{u=1}^m\pi_u=1,
\]
so \(J\) is a probability mass function on
\(\mathcal A\times\mathcal R_0\).  Equation (1) itself supplies an extension
of \(J\) by the original state index: conditional on state \(u\), treatment
is Bernoulli with success probability \(e_u\), the response type has mass
function \(r_{\cdot\cdot\mid u}\), and the two variables are independent.
Its auxiliary entropy is
\[
 -\sum_{u=1}^m\pi_u\log\pi_u\leq\kappa,
 \tag{2}
\]
where (2) is the entropy constraint in
\(\texttt{def:finite-factor-program}\).  Therefore, for the fixed joint law
\(J\),
\[
 G_J(A;R)\leq-\sum_{u=1}^m\pi_u\log\pi_u\leq\kappa,
 \qquad R=(Y(0),Y(1)).
 \tag{3}
\]

Apply the published finite-alphabet common-entropy theorem recorded as
\(\texttt{lem:binary-common-entropy-cardinality}\) with
\(\mathcal X=\mathcal A\) and \(\mathcal Z=\mathcal R_0\).  Since these
alphabets have sizes two and four, respectively, it gives an extension
\(\widetilde J\) of \(J\) by a finite variable \(W\) such that
\[
 A\perp_{\widetilde J}R\mid W,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Ent}_{\widetilde J}(W)=G_J(A;R),
 \qquad
 |\operatorname{supp}(W)|\leq 2^{\min\{2,4\}}-1=3.
 \tag{4}
\]
This is the only borrowed compression step.

Enumerate the positive-mass states in (4).  For each such state define
\[
 \pi'_w=\widetilde J(W=w),\qquad
 e'_w=\widetilde J(A=1\mid W=w),\qquad
 r'_{ij\mid w}=\widetilde J(R=(i,j)\mid W=w).
 \tag{5}
\]
All conditionals in (5) are taken only at states with \(\pi'_w>0\), so no
division by zero occurs.  If fewer than three states have positive mass, pad
the list with zero-mass states and choose their propensity and response
probabilities arbitrarily in the declared simplices.  The convention
\(0\log0=0\) makes the padded entropy equal to the entropy in (4).
Conditional independence in (4) and preservation of the \((A,R)\)-marginal
give, for every \(a,y\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\sum_w\pi'_w(e'_w)^a(1-e'_w)^{1-a}
   \sum_{i,j=0}^1r'_{ij\mid w}
      \mathbf 1\{(1-a)i+aj=y\}\\
 &\quad=\sum_{i,j=0}^1J\bigl(a,(i,j)\bigr)
      \mathbf 1\{(1-a)i+aj=y\}\\
 &\quad=\sum_{u=1}^m\pi_u e_u^a(1-e_u)^{1-a}
   \sum_{i,j=0}^1r_{ij\mid u}
      \mathbf 1\{(1-a)i+aj=y\}
 =p_{ay}.
 \tag{6}
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus (6) verifies every factual cell, including when an observed treatment
arm has probability zero.  In particular, no treatment positivity or
arm-specific conditional risk is used.  Equations (3)--(5) also verify the
entropy constraint.

The second technique is preservation of linear marginals under fixed-joint
compression.  Summing (1) over \(a\) and using
\((1-e_u)+e_u=1\) yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_w\pi'_w r'_{01\mid w}
 &=\sum_{a=0}^1J(a,(0,1))
 =\sum_{u=1}^m\pi_u r_{01\mid u},\\
 \sum_w\pi'_w r'_{10\mid w}
 &=\sum_{a=0}^1J(a,(1,0))
 =\sum_{u=1}^m\pi_u r_{10\mid u}.
 \tag{7}
\end{aligned}
\]
By (5)--(7), the padded tuple belongs to
\(\mathcal F_{3,\kappa}(P)\) and its \(\Phi_3\)-image is \(\theta\).  Hence
\[
 \Theta_\kappa(P)\subseteq\Phi_3(\mathcal F_{3,\kappa}(P)).
 \tag{8}
\]
Conversely, \(\texttt{lem:factor-program-equivalence}\) constructs from every
tuple in \(\mathcal F_{3,\kappa}(P)\) a factual-compatible full-data law with
the same benefit--harm coordinates, conditional exchangeability given its
at-most-three-state factor, consistency, and no larger entropy.  Therefore
\[
 \Phi_3(\mathcal F_{3,\kappa}(P))\subseteq\Theta_\kappa(P).
 \tag{9}
\]
Equations (8) and (9) prove the asserted equality and the stated constructive
witness conclusion for every binary observed law, including boundary laws.
