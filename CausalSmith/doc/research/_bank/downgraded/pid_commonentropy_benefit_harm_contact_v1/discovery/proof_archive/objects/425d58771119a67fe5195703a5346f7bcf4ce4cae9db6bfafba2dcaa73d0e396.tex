Fix any observed law \(P\) on \(\mathcal A\times\mathcal Y\) and any
\(\kappa\in[0,\log 2]\).  The argument below does not use treatment
positivity; in particular, it also applies to boundary laws.  Let
\(\theta\in\Theta_\kappa(P)\).  By
\(\texttt{lem:factor-program-equivalence}\), there exist an integer
\(m\geq 1\) and a tuple \((\pi,e,r)\in\mathcal F_{m,\kappa}(P)\) such that
\(\Phi_m(\pi,e,r)=\theta\).

The first technique is exact finite-mixture compression at a fixed joint
law.  Introduce the four-point response-type alphabet
\(\mathcal R_0=\{0,1\}^2\), write \(R=(Y(0),Y(1))\), and define a mass
function on \(\mathcal A\times\mathcal R_0\) by
\[
 J\bigl(a,(i,j)\bigr)
 =\sum_{u=1}^m \pi_u e_u^a(1-e_u)^{1-a}r_{ij\mid u}.
 \tag{1}
\]
Every summand in (1) is nonnegative.  Using the simplex constraints in
\(\texttt{def:finite-factor-program}\), its total mass is
\[
 \sum_{a=0}^1\sum_{i,j=0}^1J\bigl(a,(i,j)\bigr)
 =\sum_{u=1}^m\pi_u\bigl((1-e_u)+e_u\bigr)
       \sum_{i,j=0}^1r_{ij\mid u}
 =\sum_{u=1}^m\pi_u=1.
 \tag{2}
\]
Thus \(J\) is a joint probability mass function.  Moreover, (1) is itself
an exact product-mixture extension of \(J\): conditional on the state
\(u\), let \(A\) be Bernoulli with success probability \(e_u\), let
\(R\) have mass function \(r_{\cdot\cdot\mid u}\), and generate these two
variables independently.  This extension has auxiliary entropy
\[
 -\sum_{u=1}^m\pi_u\log\pi_u\leq\kappa,
 \tag{3}
\]
where the inequality is the entropy constraint in
\(\texttt{def:finite-factor-program}\).  Consequently, for the fixed law
\(J\),
\[
 G_J(A;R)\leq-\sum_{u=1}^m\pi_u\log\pi_u\leq\kappa.
 \tag{4}
\]

Apply the published exact-common-entropy compression result recorded in
\(\texttt{lem:binary-common-entropy-cardinality}\) with
\(\mathcal X=\mathcal A\) and \(\mathcal Z=\mathcal R_0\).  Since these
alphabets have sizes two and four, respectively, there is an extension
\(\widetilde J\) of \(J\) by a finite random variable \(W\) satisfying
\[
 A\perp_{\widetilde J}R\mid W,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Ent}_{\widetilde J}(W)=G_J(A;R),
 \qquad
 |\operatorname{supp}(W)|
 \leq 2^{\min\{2,4\}}-1=3.
 \tag{5}
\]
This is the only borrowed compression step.  Combining (4) and (5) also
shows that the replacement factor has entropy no greater than that of the
chosen original factor.

Enumerate only the positive-mass states in (5).  For each such state set
\[
 \pi'_w=\widetilde J(W=w),\qquad
 e'_w=\widetilde J(A=1\mid W=w),\qquad
 r'_{ij\mid w}=\widetilde J(R=(i,j)\mid W=w).
 \tag{6}
\]
The conditionals in (6) are taken only when \(\pi'_w>0\), so no division by
zero occurs.  If there are fewer than three positive states, pad the list
with zero-mass states and choose their propensities and response-type mass
functions arbitrarily in \([0,1]\) and \(\Delta_4\), respectively.  The
convention \(0\log 0=0\) makes the entropy invariant under this padding.
Therefore \(\pi'\in\Delta_3\), \(e'\in[0,1]^3\), and every
\(r'_{\cdot\cdot\mid w}\in\Delta_4\).

Conditional independence in (5), preservation of the \((A,R)\)-marginal,
and (1) give, for every \(a,y\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\sum_w\pi'_w(e'_w)^a(1-e'_w)^{1-a}
   \sum_{i,j=0}^1r'_{ij\mid w}
      \mathbf 1\{(1-a)i+aj=y\}\\
 &\quad=\sum_{i,j=0}^1J\bigl(a,(i,j)\bigr)
      \mathbf 1\{(1-a)i+aj=y\}\\
 &\quad=\sum_{u=1}^m\pi_u e_u^a(1-e_u)^{1-a}
   \sum_{i,j=0}^1r_{ij\mid u}
      \mathbf 1\{(1-a)i+aj=y\}
 =p_{ay}.
 \tag{7}
\end{aligned}
\]
The final equality is exactly the factual-cell constraint in
\(\texttt{def:finite-factor-program}\).  Equation (7) verifies the cells
directly and never divides by an arm probability, so it remains valid if
one treatment arm has probability zero.  Equations (4)--(6) verify the
entropy constraint.

The second technique is preservation of linear response-type marginals
under fixed-joint compression.  For each \((i,j)\in\{0,1\}^2\), summing
(1) over \(a\) yields
\[
 \sum_w\pi'_w r'_{ij\mid w}
 =\sum_{a=0}^1J\bigl(a,(i,j)\bigr)
 =\sum_{u=1}^m\pi_u r_{ij\mid u}
      \bigl((1-e_u)+e_u\bigr)
 =\sum_{u=1}^m\pi_u r_{ij\mid u}.
 \tag{8}
\]
Taking \((i,j)=(0,1)\) and \((1,0)\) in (8) proves that the padded tuple
has exactly the same benefit and harm coordinates as \((\pi,e,r)\).
Together with (4)--(7), this shows
\((\pi',e',r')\in\mathcal F_{3,\kappa}(P)\) and
\(\Phi_3(\pi',e',r')=\theta\).  Hence
\[
 \Theta_\kappa(P)\subseteq\Phi_3(\mathcal F_{3,\kappa}(P)).
 \tag{9}
\]

Conversely, \(\texttt{lem:factor-program-equivalence}\) constructs from
every tuple in \(\mathcal F_{3,\kappa}(P)\) a factual-compatible full-data
law satisfying consistency, conditional exchangeability given its
at-most-three-state factor, and the entropy budget, with benefit--harm
coordinates equal to its \(\Phi_3\)-image.  Therefore
\[
 \Phi_3(\mathcal F_{3,\kappa}(P))\subseteq\Theta_\kappa(P).
 \tag{10}
\]
Combining (9) and (10) proves the stated equality and gives the asserted
causal witness.  The construction also proves the stronger boundary-law
version because positivity was nowhere invoked.
