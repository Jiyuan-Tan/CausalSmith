The proof uses elementary Fr\'echet response-type algebra at zero budget and an explicit factual-cell split at high budget.  Throughout, treatment positivity in \(\texttt{ass:treatment-positivity}\) gives \(P(A=a)>0\) for both \(a\in\{0,1\}\), so each \(\mu_a=P(Y=1\mid A=a)\) is well defined.

First set \(\kappa=0\) and take \(Q\in\mathfrak M_0(P)\).  By \(\texttt{ass:entropy-budget}\),
\[
0\leq \operatorname{Ent}_Q(U)=-\sum_{u\in\mathcal U}Q(U=u)\log Q(U=u)\leq 0.
\]
For \(x\in[0,1]\), \(-x\log x\geq0\), with equality only at \(x=0\) or \(x=1\).  Since the finitely many masses \(Q(U=u)\) sum to one, there is therefore exactly one positive-probability state, and it has mass one.  Thus \(U\) is constant almost surely.  Conditional exchangeability from \(\texttt{ass:latent-exchangeability}\) consequently reduces to
\[
A\perp (Y(0),Y(1)).
\]
For each \(a\in\{0,1\}\), this independence, consistency from \(\texttt{ass:consistency}\), and factual compatibility from \(\texttt{ass:factual-compatibility}\) give
\[
Q\{Y(a)=1\}=Q\{Y(a)=1\mid A=a\}=Q(Y=1\mid A=a)=\mu_a.
\]
Write
\[
x=Q\{Y(0)=1,Y(1)=1\}.
\]
The four response-type probabilities are then forced to be
\[
r_{11}=x,\qquad r_{01}=\mu_1-x,\qquad r_{10}=\mu_0-x,\qquad
r_{00}=1-\mu_0-\mu_1+x.
\]
Their nonnegativity is equivalent to the Fr\'echet inequalities
\[
\max\{0,\mu_0+\mu_1-1\}\leq x\leq\min\{\mu_0,\mu_1\}.
\]
Since \(B(Q)=r_{01}\) and \(H(Q)=r_{10}\), subtraction and the four nonnegativity inequalities yield
\[
B(Q)-H(Q)=\mu_1-\mu_0,
\]
\[
0\leq B(Q)\leq\min\{\mu_1,1-\mu_0\},\qquad
0\leq H(Q)\leq\min\{\mu_0,1-\mu_1\}.
\]
By \(\texttt{def:zero-budget-segment}\), this proves
\[
\Theta_0(P)\subseteq\mathcal S_0(P).
\]

Conversely, fix \((b,h)\in\mathcal S_0(P)\) and define a response-type law by
\[
r_{01}=b,\qquad r_{10}=h,\qquad
r_{11}=\mu_1-b=\mu_0-h,\qquad
r_{00}=1-\mu_0-b=1-\mu_1-h.
\]
The two displayed equalities follow from \(b-h=\mu_1-\mu_0\).  The upper bounds in \(\texttt{def:zero-budget-segment}\) imply that all four masses are nonnegative, and direct summation gives
\[
r_{00}+r_{01}+r_{10}+r_{11}=1.
\]
Let \(U\) be constant, draw \(A\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p_1)\) independently of the response type, and set \(Y=Y(A)\).  Then latent exchangeability and consistency hold by construction, while
\[
Q(Y=1\mid A=0)=r_{10}+r_{11}=\mu_0,
\qquad
Q(Y=1\mid A=1)=r_{01}+r_{11}=\mu_1.
\]
Together with \(Q(A=1)=p_1\), these identities reproduce all four cells of the binary factual law \(P\).  Moreover, \(\operatorname{Ent}_Q(U)=0\), \(B(Q)=b\), and \(H(Q)=h\).  Hence \(Q\in\mathfrak M_0(P)\) and
\[
\mathcal S_0(P)\subseteq\Theta_0(P).
\]
The two inclusions prove the sharp equality \(\Theta_0(P)=\mathcal S_0(P)\), with every point attained by the displayed Fr\'echet coupling.

We next prove the high-budget endpoint.  For any \(\kappa\) and any \(Q\in\mathfrak M_\kappa(P)\), split the benefit event by treatment status.  Consistency and factual compatibility imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
B(Q)
&=Q\{A=0,Y(0)=0,Y(1)=1\}+Q\{A=1,Y(0)=0,Y(1)=1\}\\
&\leq Q(A=0,Y=0)+Q(A=1,Y=1)\\
&=p_{00}+p_{11}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The same argument for the harm event gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
H(Q)
&=Q\{A=0,Y(0)=1,Y(1)=0\}+Q\{A=1,Y(0)=1,Y(1)=0\}\\
&\leq Q(A=0,Y=1)+Q(A=1,Y=0)\\
&=p_{01}+p_{10}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Because both coordinates are probabilities, \(\texttt{def:factual-rectangle}\) now gives the outer bound
\[
\Theta_\kappa(P)\subseteq\mathcal R(P)
\quad\text{for every }\kappa.
\]

For reverse inclusion at \(\kappa\geq h(p_1)\), fix \((b,h)\in\mathcal R(P)\) and set
\[
b_0=\min\{b,p_{00}\},\qquad b_1=b-b_0,
\qquad
h_0=\min\{h,p_{01}\},\qquad h_1=h-h_0.
\]
The rectangle inequalities \(b\leq p_{00}+p_{11}\) and \(h\leq p_{01}+p_{10}\) imply
\[
0\leq b_0\leq p_{00},\quad 0\leq b_1\leq p_{11},\quad
0\leq h_0\leq p_{01},\quad 0\leq h_1\leq p_{10}.
\]
Define the joint masses of \((A,Y(0),Y(1))\), with columns indexed by response type, by
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
 &00&01&10&11\\ \hline
A=0&p_{00}-b_0&b_0&h_0&p_{01}-h_0\\
A=1&p_{10}-h_1&b_1&h_1&p_{11}-b_1.
\end{array}
\]
All entries are nonnegative.  The first row sums to \(p_{00}+p_{01}=P(A=0)\), the second to \(p_{10}+p_{11}=P(A=1)\), and hence the entire table sums to one.  Under consistency, the \(A=0\) row yields factual masses \(p_{00}\) and \(p_{01}\), while the \(A=1\) row yields \(p_{10}\) and \(p_{11}\).  Thus its \((A,Y)\)-marginal is exactly \(P\).  Its type-\(01\) and type-\(10\) masses are, respectively,
\[
b_0+b_1=b,\qquad h_0+h_1=h.
\]

Finally set \(U=A\).  Conditional on \(U=u\), \(A=u\) almost surely; therefore, for every treatment value \(a\) and response type \(z\),
\[
Q(A=a,(Y(0),Y(1))=z\mid U=u)
=Q(A=a\mid U=u)Q((Y(0),Y(1))=z\mid U=u),
\]
so latent exchangeability holds, including when some response-type masses vanish.  Since \(U\) is Bernoulli with success probability \(p_1\),
\[
\operatorname{Ent}_Q(U)=h(p_1)\leq\kappa.
\]
Thus the construction lies in \(\mathfrak M_\kappa(P)\), proving
\[
\mathcal R(P)\subseteq\Theta_\kappa(P)
\quad\text{whenever }\kappa\geq h(p_1).
\]
Combining the inclusions proves \(\Theta_\kappa(P)=\mathcal R(P)\) in that regime.  Since every point of the rectangle is attained by one full-data law, the result is jointly sharp; projecting the equality onto either coordinate gives the sharp factual benefit and harm intervals.
