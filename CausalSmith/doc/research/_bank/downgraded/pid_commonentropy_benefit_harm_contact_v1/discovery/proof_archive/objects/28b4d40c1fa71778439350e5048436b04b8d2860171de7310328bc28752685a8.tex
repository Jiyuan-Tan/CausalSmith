Fix an observed law \(P\) satisfying \(0<p_1<1\), a budget \(\kappa\in[0,\log 2]\), and a point \(\theta=(b,h)\in\Theta_\kappa(P)\).  By \(\texttt{def:identified-set}\) there is a law \(Q\in\mathfrak M_\kappa(P)\) with \((B(Q),H(Q))=(b,h)\).  Define the four-valued response type
\[
R=(Y(0),Y(1))\in\{0,1\}^2
\]
and let \(J\) be the \((A,R)\)-marginal of \(Q\).  By \(\texttt{ass:latent-exchangeability}\),
\[
A\perp R\mid U
\]
under \(Q\).  Thus the joint extension of \(J\) by the original finite \(U\) is an admissible exact product-mixture representation of \(J\).  If \(G_J(A;R)\) denotes the common-entropy infimum for this fixed joint law, the definition of an infimum and \(\texttt{ass:entropy-budget}\) give
\[
G_J(A;R)\leq \operatorname{Ent}_Q(U)\leq\kappa. \tag{1}
\]

The technique in the next step is the published finite-alphabet common-entropy cardinality theorem, recorded in \(\texttt{lem:binary-common-entropy-cardinality}\).  Its finite-attainment statement and its bound
\[
|\operatorname{supp}(W)|
\leq 2^{\min\{|\mathcal X|,|\mathcal Z|\}}-1
\]
applied with \(\mathcal X=\mathcal A\) and \(\mathcal Z=\{0,1\}^2\) produce a joint law \(\widetilde J\) of \((A,R,W)\) such that
\[
\widetilde J_{A,R}=J,
\qquad A\perp_{\widetilde J}R\mid W,
\qquad \operatorname{Ent}_{\widetilde J}(W)=G_J(A;R),
\qquad |\operatorname{supp}(W)|\leq 2^{\min\{2,4\}}-1=3. \tag{2}
\]
This is the only borrowed cardinality step.  The remaining fixed-joint causal reconstruction is proved directly.

Define a full-data law \(Q'\) by
\[
\begin{split}
&Q'\bigl(A=a,Y(0)=i,Y(1)=j,Y=y,W=w\bigr)\\
&\hspace{2cm}=
\widetilde J\bigl(A=a,R=(i,j),W=w\bigr)
\mathbf 1\{y=(1-a)i+aj\}.
\end{split} \tag{3}
\]
This is a probability law because for each \((a,i,j,w)\) exactly one \(y\in\{0,1\}\) makes the indicator in (3) equal to one.  Equation (3) also gives \(Y=Y(A)\) \(Q'\)-almost surely, which is \(\texttt{ass:consistency}\).  For every \(a,y\in\{0,1\}\), preservation of the \((A,R)\)-joint law in (2), followed by consistency and factual compatibility of the original \(Q\), yields
\[
\begin{split}
Q'(A=a,Y=y)
&=\sum_{i,j,w}\widetilde J(a,(i,j),w)
  \mathbf 1\{(1-a)i+aj=y\}\\
&=J\bigl(A=a,R_a=y\bigr)\\
&=Q\bigl(A=a,Y(a)=y\bigr)\\
&=Q(A=a,Y=y)\\
&=p_{ay}.
\end{split} \tag{4}
\]
Thus (4) verifies \(\texttt{ass:factual-compatibility}\) cell by cell; no division by an arm probability is used.

For every \(w\) with \(\widetilde J(W=w)>0\), conditional independence in (2) says
\[
\widetilde J(A=a,R=(i,j)\mid W=w)
=\widetilde J(A=a\mid W=w)
 \widetilde J(R=(i,j)\mid W=w).
\]
Because (3) leaves the \((A,R,W)\)-law unchanged, this is exactly
\[
A\perp_{Q'}(Y(0),Y(1))\mid W, \tag{5}
\]
so \(\texttt{ass:latent-exchangeability}\) holds for the reconstructed law.  Equations (1)--(2) give the budget check
\[
\operatorname{Ent}_{Q'}(W)
=\operatorname{Ent}_{\widetilde J}(W)
=G_J(A;R)
\leq\operatorname{Ent}_Q(U)
\leq\kappa. \tag{6}
\]
Finally, the marginal law of \(R\) is also preserved, so
\[
B(Q')=Q'(R=(0,1))=J(R=(0,1))=B(Q)=b, \tag{7}
\]
and
\[
H(Q')=Q'(R=(1,0))=J(R=(1,0))=H(Q)=h. \tag{8}
\]
Consequently, (3)--(8) give a law in \(\mathfrak M_\kappa(P)\) attaining \(\theta\) with at most three positive latent masses.

For completeness, its factor-program witness is explicit.  On each positive-mass state set
\[
\pi_w=\widetilde J(W=w),\qquad
e_w=\widetilde J(A=1\mid W=w),\qquad
r_{ij\mid w}=\widetilde J(R=(i,j)\mid W=w).
\]
If fewer than three states have positive mass, pad the list to three with \(\pi_w=0\), choosing the corresponding \(e_w\) and \(r_{\cdot\cdot\mid w}\) arbitrarily in their stated simplices.  The convention \(0\log0=0\) preserves (6).  Conditional independence and (4) verify all constraints in \(\mathcal F_{3,\kappa}(P)\), while (7)--(8) verify that \(\Phi_3(\pi,e,r)=\theta\).  Hence
\[
\Theta_\kappa(P)\subseteq\Phi_3(\mathcal F_{3,\kappa}(P)). \tag{9}
\]
Conversely, \(\texttt{lem:factor-program-equivalence}\) states constructively that every point of \(\Phi_3(\mathcal F_{3,\kappa}(P))\) is generated by a law in \(\mathfrak M_\kappa(P)\) with at most three latent states.  By \(\texttt{def:identified-set}\),
\[
\Phi_3(\mathcal F_{3,\kappa}(P))\subseteq\Theta_\kappa(P). \tag{10}
\]
Combining (9) and (10) proves the asserted equality and both constructive directions.  The positivity assumption fixes the theorem's declared domain; the compression itself does not require conditioning on \(A=a\).
