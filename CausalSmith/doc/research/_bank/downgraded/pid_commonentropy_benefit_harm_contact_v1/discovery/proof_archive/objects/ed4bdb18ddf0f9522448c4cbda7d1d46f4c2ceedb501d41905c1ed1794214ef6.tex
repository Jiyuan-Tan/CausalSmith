First consider an arbitrary \(Q\in\mathfrak M_\kappa(P)\) with \(B(Q)=H(Q)=0\).  The first part of the proof of \(\texttt{thm:origin-common-entropy-frontier}\) gives \(Y(0)=Y(1)=Y\) almost surely and therefore the conditional Markov relation \(A\perp Y\mid U\).  Since \(Q_{A,Y}=P\), finite-alphabet entropy identities give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 I_P(A;Y)&=I_Q(A;Y)\\
 &\leq I_Q(A;U)\\
 &=\operatorname{Ent}_Q(U)-\operatorname{Ent}_Q(U\mid A)\\
 &\leq\operatorname{Ent}_Q(U)\\
 &\leq\kappa.
\end{aligned}
\]
For completeness, the data-processing inequality in the second line follows here directly from
\[
 I_Q(A;U,Y)=I_Q(A;U)+I_Q(A;Y\mid U)=I_Q(A;U)
\]
and
\[
 I_Q(A;U,Y)=I_Q(A;Y)+I_Q(A;U\mid Y)\geq I_Q(A;Y).
\]
The two conditional mutual informations are finite sums of conditional Kullback--Leibler divergences; their nonnegativity follows termwise from \(\log x\leq x-1\).  Also \(\operatorname{Ent}_Q(U\mid A)\geq0\) because every summand is a nonnegative multiple of \(-\log Q(U=u\mid A=a)\).  Thus \(I_P(A;Y)\leq\kappa\) is indeed necessary for origin feasibility.

We next calculate the two information quantities for \(P^\star\).  Its two margins are uniform, and its crossover probability is \(1/10\).  Therefore
\[
 I_{P^\star}(A;Y)=\operatorname{Ent}_{P^\star}(Y)-
 \operatorname{Ent}_{P^\star}(Y\mid A)=\log2-h(1/10).
\]
It remains to calculate the common entropy.  By \(\texttt{lem:binary-common-entropy-two-state}\), choose an entropy-minimizing product-mixture factor with at most two positive states.  One state cannot represent \(P^\star\), since under \(P^\star\)
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(A,Y)=P^\star(A=1,Y=1)-P^\star(A=1)P^\star(Y=1)
 =\frac9{20}-\frac14=\frac15\ne0.
\]
Consequently there are exactly two positive weights.  Relabel them as \(q\) and \(1-q\), where \(0<q\leq1/2\), and write
\[
 \alpha_i=P(A=1\mid U=i),\qquad
 \beta_i=P(Y=1\mid U=i),\qquad i\in\{0,1\},
\]
with state \(1\) having weight \(q\).  Conditional independence and the uniform margins imply
\[
 \frac15=q(1-q)(\alpha_1-\alpha_0)(\beta_1-\beta_0),
 \qquad q\alpha_1+(1-q)\alpha_0=q\beta_1+(1-q)\beta_0=\frac12.
\]
Let \(d_\alpha=\alpha_1-\alpha_0\).  Solving the first marginal equation for the component probabilities gives
\[
 \alpha_1=\frac12+(1-q)d_\alpha,
 \qquad \alpha_0=\frac12-qd_\alpha.
\]
Since \(0\leq\alpha_1\leq1\), one has \(|d_\alpha|\leq\{2(1-q)\}^{-1}\).  The identical argument gives \(|\beta_1-\beta_0|\leq\{2(1-q)\}^{-1}\).  The covariance product is positive, and hence
\[
 \frac15\leq q(1-q)\frac1{4(1-q)^2}=\frac{q}{4(1-q)}.
\]
Solving this inequality yields \(q\geq4/9\).  Since \(h'(q)=\log\{(1-q)/q\}\geq0\) for \(0<q\leq1/2\), every minimizing two-state factor has entropy
\[
 h(q)\geq h(4/9).
\]
This lower bound is attained.  Give state \(1\) weight \(4/9\) and set \(\alpha_1=\beta_1=1\); give state \(0\) weight \(5/9\) and set \(\alpha_0=\beta_0=1/10\).  The resulting product mixture has cells
\[
 \frac59\left(\frac9{10}\right)^2=\frac9{20},\quad
 \frac59\left(\frac9{10}\right)\left(\frac1{10}\right)=\frac1{20},\quad
 \frac1{20},\quad
 \frac49+\frac59\left(\frac1{10}\right)^2=\frac9{20},
\]
in the order \((0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1)\).  Thus it represents \(P^\star\) exactly and proves
\[
 G_{P^\star}(A;Y)=h(4/9).
\]

The displayed decimals and strict comparisons can be certified without floating-point assumptions.  For \(x>1\), put \(z=(x-1)/(x+1)\).  Integrating the finite geometric expansion of \((1-s^2)^{-1}\) gives, for every integer \(M\geq0\),
\[
 \log x=2\sum_{j=0}^{M}\frac{z^{2j+1}}{2j+1}+R_M,
 \qquad 0<R_M<\frac{2z^{2M+3}}{(2M+3)(1-z^2)}.
\]
Use \(M=10\), together with
\[
 \log10=3\log2+\log(5/4),\qquad
 \log(9/4)=\log2+\log(9/8),
\]
and \(h(1/10)=\tfrac1{10}\log10+\tfrac9{10}\log(10/9)\), \(h(4/9)=\tfrac49\log(9/4)+\tfrac59\log(9/5)\).  Substitution of the rational values \(z=1/3,1/9,1/19,1/17,2/7\), respectively, and rational interval arithmetic yields
\[
 0.3680642071<I_{P^\star}(A;Y)<0.3680642072
 <\frac12
 <0.6869615765<G_{P^\star}(A;Y)<0.6869615767.
\]
This proves the asserted decimal expansions and strict inequalities.  At \(\kappa^\star=1/2\), the necessary observed-law inequality therefore holds, whereas \(G_{P^\star}(A;Y)>\kappa^\star\); \(\texttt{thm:origin-common-entropy-frontier}\) then gives \((0,0)\notin\Theta_{\kappa^\star}(P^\star)\).  Hence the necessary inequality is not sufficient.

Finally, parameterize every two-state product mixture by the compact cube
\[
 D=[0,1]^5\ni(q,\alpha_0,\alpha_1,\beta_0,\beta_1)
\]
and let \(F:D\to\mathcal P(\{0,1\}^2)\) be its continuously induced cell-probability map.  By \(\texttt{lem:binary-common-entropy-two-state}\),
\[
 G_P(A;Y)=\min\{h(q):d\in D,\ F(d)=P\}.
\]
If \(P_n\to P\), choose minimizers \(d_n\).  Along a subsequence realizing \(\liminf_nG_{P_n}(A;Y)\), compactness gives a further subsequence with \(d_n\to d\in D\).  Continuity gives \(F(d)=P\) and
\[
 G_P(A;Y)\leq h(q)=\liminf_nG_{P_n}(A;Y).
\]
Thus \(P\mapsto G_P(A;Y)\) is lower semicontinuous.  On the positive binary-law simplex, \(P\mapsto I_P(A;Y)\) is continuous because it is a finite sum of continuous logarithmic cell expressions with positive cells and margins.  Since at \(P^\star\) one has \(I_P(A;Y)<1/2<G_P(A;Y)\), continuity and lower semicontinuity provide a relative neighborhood on which both strict inequalities persist.  The exact frontier theorem excludes the origin throughout that neighborhood at budget \(1/2\).
