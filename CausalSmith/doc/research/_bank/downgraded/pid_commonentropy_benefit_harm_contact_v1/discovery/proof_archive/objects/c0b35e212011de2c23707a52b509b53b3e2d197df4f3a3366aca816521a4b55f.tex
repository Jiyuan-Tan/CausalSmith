The technique is statewise convex interpolation of response kernels while holding the latent distribution and treatment propensities fixed.  Fix \(\theta\in\Theta_\kappa(P)\).  By the constructive forward direction of \(\texttt{lem:factor-program-equivalence}\), there are a finite \(m\geq1\) and \((\pi,e,r)\in\mathcal F_{m,\kappa}(P)\) such that
\[
\Phi_m(\pi,e,r)=\theta.
\]
Fix \(\theta_0\in\mathcal S_0(P)\).  The constructive zero-budget equality in \(\texttt{thm:endpoint-geometry}\) supplies a single response-type probability vector \(s=(s_{ij})_{i,j\in\{0,1\}}\in\Delta_4\) satisfying
\[
\sum_{j=0}^1s_{1j}=\mu_0,qquad
\sum_{i=0}^1s_{i1}=\mu_1,qquad
(s_{01},s_{10})=\theta_0.
\]
For \(t\in[0,1]\), retain \(\pi\) and \(e\), and for every state \(u\) define
\[
r^{(t)}_{ij\mid u}=t r_{ij\mid u}+(1-t)s_{ij}.
\]
Convexity of the probability simplex gives \(r^{(t)}_{\cdot\cdot\mid u}\in\Delta_4\) for every \(u\).

It remains to verify the factual constraints rather than assume that ordinary mixture closure is available.  Put
\[
w_a(e_u)=e_u^a(1-e_u)^{1-a}
\]
and sum the factual equations in \(\texttt{def:finite-factor-program}\) over \(y\).  Because each response kernel has total mass one, this yields
\[
\sum_{u=1}^m\pi_u w_a(e_u)=\sum_{y=0}^1p_{ay}=P(A=a).
\]
For \(a,y\in\{0,1\}\), define the \(a\)-th margin of \(s\) by
\[
s_{a,y}=\sum_{i,j=0}^1s_{ij}\mathbf 1\{(1-a)i+aj=y\}.
\]
The displayed margins of \(s\), treatment positivity, and the definition of \(\mu_a\) imply
\[
s_{a,y}=P(Y=y\mid A=a).
\]
Therefore the factual cell generated by the interpolated kernels is
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\sum_{u=1}^m\pi_u w_a(e_u)
 \sum_{i,j=0}^1r^{(t)}_{ij\mid u}\mathbf 1\{(1-a)i+aj=y\}\\
&\quad=t\sum_{u=1}^m\pi_u w_a(e_u)
 \sum_{i,j=0}^1r_{ij\mid u}\mathbf 1\{(1-a)i+aj=y\}
 +(1-t)s_{a,y}\sum_{u=1}^m\pi_u w_a(e_u)\\
&\quad=t p_{ay}+(1-t)P(Y=y\mid A=a)P(A=a)\\
&\quad=t p_{ay}+(1-t)p_{ay}=p_{ay}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus every factual equality is preserved.  The latent masses have not changed, so
\[
-\sum_{u=1}^m\pi_u\log\pi_u\leq\kappa
\]
continues to hold.  Hence \((\pi,e,r^{(t)})\in\mathcal F_{m,\kappa}(P)\); in particular, no mixture indicator has been adjoined and there is no additional entropy cost.

Finally, linearity of \(\Phi_m\) in the response probabilities gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Phi_m(\pi,e,r^{(t)})
&=\left(\sum_u\pi_u r^{(t)}_{01\mid u},
          \sum_u\pi_u r^{(t)}_{10\mid u}\right)\\
&=t\Phi_m(\pi,e,r)+(1-t)(s_{01},s_{10})\\
&=t\theta+(1-t)\theta_0.
\end{aligned}
\]
The constructive reverse direction of \(\texttt{lem:factor-program-equivalence}\) now supplies a law in \(\mathfrak M_\kappa(P)\) attaining this pair.  Consequently,
\[
(1-t)\theta_0+t\theta\in\Theta_\kappa(P)
\]
for every admissible \(\theta\), \(\theta_0\), and \(t\).  The zero-budget construction in \(\texttt{thm:endpoint-geometry}\) also shows that \(\mathcal S_0(P)\) is nonempty.  Choosing any \(\theta_0\in\mathcal S_0(P)\), the displayed line segment joins \(\theta_0\) continuously to every point of \(\Theta_\kappa(P)\); concatenating two such segments joins any two points.  Therefore \(\mathcal S_0(P)\) is contained in the star kernel and \(\Theta_\kappa(P)\) is path connected.
