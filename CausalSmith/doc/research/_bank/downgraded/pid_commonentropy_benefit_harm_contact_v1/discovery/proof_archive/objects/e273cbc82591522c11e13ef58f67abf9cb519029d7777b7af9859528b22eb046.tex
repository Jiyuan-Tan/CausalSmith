For every \(\theta=(b,h)\in\Theta_\kappa(P)\), there is a witness with at most six latent states and entropy no larger than that of the original witness.  Indeed, from any finite factorization define
\[
 z_u=\bigl(f_{00}(e_u,r_u),f_{01}(e_u,r_u),f_{10}(e_u,r_u),f_{11}(e_u,r_u),r_{01\mid u},r_{10\mid u}\bigr),
\]
where \(f_{ay}\) is the conditional factual-cell expression in \(\texttt{def:finite-factor-program}\).  The target vector is \(\sum_u\pi_u z_u=(p_{00},p_{01},p_{10},p_{11},b,h)\).  Minimize Shannon entropy over the compact polytope of weights on these fixed \(z_u\)'s having this barycenter.  Concavity permits an extreme minimizer: if a minimizer is a nontrivial convex combination, one endpoint has no larger entropy.  An extreme feasible weight cannot have more than six positive entries, because otherwise its six-coordinate columns are linearly dependent and a small signed perturbation in both directions would preserve the barycenter and nonnegativity.  Keeping the associated \((e_u,r_u)\)'s preserves every factual and benefit--harm moment and cannot increase entropy.  Hence the frozen primitives prove \(\Theta_\kappa(P)=\Phi_6(\mathcal F_{6,\kappa}(P))\).  They do not yet prove the asserted reduction from six to three.
