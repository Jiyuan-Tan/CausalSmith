The technique is statewise convex interpolation of response kernels, which leaves the latent distribution fixed.  Fix \(\theta\in\Theta_\kappa(P)\).  By \(\texttt{lem:factor-program-equivalence}\), choose some finite \(m\) and \((\pi,e,r)\in\mathcal F_{m,\kappa}(P)\) with \(\Phi_m(\pi,e,r)=\theta\).  Fix \(\theta_0\in\mathcal S_0(P)\).  The zero-budget construction in \(\texttt{thm:endpoint-geometry}\) supplies one response-type law \(s=(s_{ij})\in\Delta_4\), common to every latent state, whose margins are
\[
 \sum_j s_{1j}=\mu_0,qquad \sum_i s_{i1}=\mu_1,
\]
and whose benefit--harm pair is \(\theta_0=(s_{01},s_{10})\).

For \(t\in[0,1]\), retain \(\pi\) and \(e\) and define, state by state,
\[
 r^{(t)}_{ij\mid u}=t r_{ij\mid u}+(1-t)s_{ij}.
\]
These are probability vectors.  Summing the original factual equations over \(y\) gives
\[
 \sum_u\pi_u e_u=p_1,qquad
 \sum_u\pi_u(1-e_u)=1-p_1.
\]
Let \(s_{a,y}=\sum_{i,j}s_{ij}\mathbf 1\{(1-a)i+aj=y\}\).  Its specified margins imply
\[
 s_{a,y}=P(Y=y\mid A=a),
\]
where treatment positivity permits the conditional probability.  Consequently, for every \(a,y\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\sum_u\pi_u e_u^a(1-e_u)^{1-a}
 \sum_{i,j}r^{(t)}_{ij\mid u}\mathbf 1\{(1-a)i+aj=y\}\\
 &\qquad=t p_{ay}+(1-t)P(A=a)s_{a,y}=p_{ay}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus the factual law is unchanged.  The latent masses are also unchanged, so their entropy remains at most \(\kappa\); no mixture indicator and no extra entropy are introduced.  Finally, linearity of \(\Phi_m\) in the response probabilities gives
\[
 \Phi_m(\pi,e,r^{(t)})=t\theta+(1-t)\theta_0.
\]
The reverse construction in \(\texttt{lem:factor-program-equivalence}\) therefore proves that this point lies in \(\Theta_\kappa(P)\).  Since \(t\mapsto(1-t)\theta_0+t\theta\) is a continuous path, every point is joined to every \(\theta_0\in\mathcal S_0(P)\), so \(\mathcal S_0(P)\) is contained in the star kernel and \(\Theta_\kappa(P)\) is path connected.
