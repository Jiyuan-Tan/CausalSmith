The three-state equality gives the compact representation \(\Theta_\kappa(P)=\Phi_3(\mathcal F_{3,\kappa}(P))\): the simplex and cube factors are compact, the factual equalities are closed, and \(-x\log x\), extended by zero at \(x=0\), is continuous.  Consequently the feasible set and its continuous image are compact.  For any mesh diameter \(\eta>0\), take a finite closed-cell grid of \([0,1]^2\), retain as an outer complex every cell meeting \(\Theta_\kappa(P)\), and choose one point of \(\Theta_\kappa(P)\) from each retained cell as a zero-dimensional inner complex.  Then the inner complex is contained in \(\Theta_\kappa(P)\), the set is contained in the outer complex, and their Hausdorff distance is at most \(\eta\).  Every selected inner vertex has an exact three-state witness.  Moreover, every closed grid cell disjoint from the image has a strictly positive attained distance from the compact image, equivalently a positive minimum of the continuous feasibility residual.  This proves abstract two-sided finite approximability, including boundary strata, but it does not give an algorithm that finitely decides which cells meet an arbitrary real-valued feasible image, outputs the exact selected witnesses, or converts the positive minima into checkable certificates without an effective error bound.
