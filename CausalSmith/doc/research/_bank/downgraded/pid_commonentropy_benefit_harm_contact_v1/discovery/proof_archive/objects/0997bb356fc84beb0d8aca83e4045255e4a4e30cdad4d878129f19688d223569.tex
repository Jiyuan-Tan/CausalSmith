By \(\texttt{def:identified-set}\), membership of the origin means that there is a law \(Q\in\mathfrak M_\kappa(P)\) with \(B(Q)=H(Q)=0\).  Because the potential outcomes are binary, the two off-diagonal response-type events partition the event on which the potential outcomes differ.  Hence, using the definitions of \(B(Q)\) and \(H(Q)\),
\[
 Q\{Y(0)\ne Y(1)\}
 =Q\{Y(0)=0,Y(1)=1\}+Q\{Y(0)=1,Y(1)=0\}
 =B(Q)+H(Q)=0.
\]
Put \(V=Y(0)\).  The preceding equality gives \(V=Y(1)\) \(Q\)-almost surely.  By \(\texttt{ass:consistency}\),
\[
 Y=Y(A)=V\qquad Q\text{-almost surely}.
\]
Moreover, \(\texttt{ass:latent-exchangeability}\) implies, for every positive-mass state \(u\),
\[
 Q(A=a,V=v\mid U=u)
 =\sum_{j\in\{0,1\}}Q(A=a,Y(0)=v,Y(1)=j\mid U=u)
 =Q(A=a\mid U=u)Q(V=v\mid U=u).
\]
Thus \(A\perp V\mid U\), and therefore \(A\perp Y\mid U\).  Let \(\widetilde P\) be the \((A,Y,U)\)-marginal of \(Q\).  The alphabet of \(U\) is finite by \(\texttt{def:model-fiber}\), \(\widetilde P_{A,Y}=P\) by \(\texttt{ass:factual-compatibility}\), and \(A\perp_{\widetilde P}Y\mid U\).  Thus \(\widetilde P\) is feasible in \(\texttt{def:common-entropy}\).  Its mixing entropy is unchanged by marginalization, so \(\texttt{ass:entropy-budget}\) yields
\[
 G_P(A;Y)\leq \operatorname{Ent}_{\widetilde P}(U)
 =\operatorname{Ent}_Q(U)\leq\kappa.
\]
This proves necessity and also proves the asserted entropy-preserving map from every causal origin witness to an observed product-mixture factor.

Conversely, suppose \(G_P(A;Y)\leq\kappa\).  By \(\texttt{lem:binary-common-entropy-two-state}\), the minimum in \(\texttt{def:common-entropy}\) is attained by an extension \(\widetilde P^\circ\) for which \(A\perp_{\widetilde P^\circ}Y\mid U\) and \(U\) has at most two positive-mass states.  In particular,
\[
 \operatorname{Ent}_{\widetilde P^\circ}(U)=G_P(A;Y)\leq\kappa.
\]
Define a full-data law explicitly by
\[
 Q^\circ\{A=a,Y(0)=i,Y(1)=j,Y=y,U=u\}
 =\widetilde P^\circ(A=a,Y=y,U=u)\mathbf 1\{i=y\}\mathbf 1\{j=y\}.
\]
Summing over \((i,j,u)\) shows \(Q^\circ_{A,Y}=P\).  The displayed construction puts all mass on \(Y(0)=Y(1)=Y\), so consistency holds and
\[
 B(Q^\circ)=H(Q^\circ)=0.
\]
For every \(u\) of positive mass, conditional product structure under \(\widetilde P^\circ\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &Q^\circ\{A=a,Y(0)=i,Y(1)=j\mid U=u\}\\
 &\quad=\widetilde P^\circ(A=a\mid U=u)
 \sum_{y\in\{0,1\}}\widetilde P^\circ(Y=y\mid U=u)
 \mathbf 1\{i=y\}\mathbf 1\{j=y\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The right-hand side is the product of the conditional law of \(A\) and the conditional law of \((Y(0),Y(1))\), proving \(A\perp (Y(0),Y(1))\mid U\).  Finally,
\[
 \operatorname{Ent}_{Q^\circ}(U)=\operatorname{Ent}_{\widetilde P^\circ}(U)\leq\kappa.
\]
Thus all four restrictions in \(\texttt{def:model-fiber}\) hold, so \(Q^\circ\in\mathfrak M_\kappa(P)\).  By \(\texttt{def:identified-set}\), \((0,0)\in\Theta_\kappa(P)\).  The same construction preserves the at-most-two positive states of \(U\), completing both sufficiency and the support claim.
