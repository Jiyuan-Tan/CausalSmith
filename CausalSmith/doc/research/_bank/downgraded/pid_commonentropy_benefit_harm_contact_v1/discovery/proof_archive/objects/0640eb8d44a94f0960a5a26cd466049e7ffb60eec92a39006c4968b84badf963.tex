The technique is exact inversion on a finite sample space followed by deterministic image containment.  Fix \(N\geq1\), \(\alpha\in(0,1)\), \(\kappa\in[0,\log2]\), and let \(p=(p_{ay})_{a,y\in\{0,1\}}\) be the true cell vector of \(P\).  The multinomial sample space
\[
\mathscr X_N=\left\{c\in\mathbb N^4:\sum_{a,y}c_{ay}=N\right\}
\tag{1}
\]
is finite.  Under \(P\), the probability of \(C_N=c\) is exactly \(L_p(c)\), including boundary cell vectors because the convention \(0^0=1\) is used.  Define
\[
\psi_p(s)=\sum_{c\in\mathscr X_N:L_p(c)\geq s}L_p(c),
\qquad
S_p=\{s\in[0,1]:\psi_p(s)\geq1-\alpha\}.
\tag{2}
\]
Since \(\psi_p(0)=1\), the set \(S_p\) is nonempty.  Since \(\mathscr X_N\) is finite, \(\psi_p\) changes only at the finitely many values \(\{L_p(c):c\in\mathscr X_N\}\).  It follows directly that \(S_p\) is a closed initial interval and its supremum is attained.  Thus the threshold in \(\texttt{def:exact-multinomial-region}\) satisfies
\[
u(p,\alpha)=\max S_p,\qquad
\psi_p(u(p,\alpha))\geq1-\alpha.
\tag{3}
\]
By that definition and (3),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr_P\{p\in\mathcal C_{N,\alpha}(C_N)\}
&=\Pr_P\{L_p(C_N)\geq u(p,\alpha)\}\\
&=\sum_{c\in\mathscr X_N:L_p(c)\geq u(p,\alpha)}L_p(c)\\
&=\psi_p(u(p,\alpha))\geq1-\alpha.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
On the event in (4), the candidate \(q=p\) occurs in the union defining \(\widehat{\mathcal T}_{N,\alpha,\kappa}\).  The induced factual law \(P_p\) equals \(P\) cell by cell.  Hence, deterministically on this event,
\[
\Theta_\kappa(P)=\Theta_\kappa(P_p)
\subseteq\bigcup_{q\in\mathcal C_{N,\alpha}(C_N)}\Theta_\kappa(P_q)
=\widehat{\mathcal T}_{N,\alpha,\kappa}.
\tag{5}
\]
Therefore the event in (4) is contained in the whole-set coverage event.  Taking probabilities in (5) and applying (4) proves
\[
\Pr_P\left\{\Theta_\kappa(P)\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal T}_{N,\alpha,\kappa}\right\}\geq1-\alpha.
\]
This is an outer whole-set coverage statement; it does not assert optimality, small area, or equality of the random envelope with the identified set.
