The proof combines an exact conditional-moment lower bound with a matching product-mixture construction. Under \(P^\star\), both observed marginals are uniform and the crossover probability is \(1/10\). Direct substitution in the definition of mutual information gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
I_{P^\star}(A;Y)
&=\frac9{10}\log\frac{9/20}{1/4}
  +\frac1{10}\log\frac{1/20}{1/4}\\
&=\frac9{10}\log\frac95+\frac1{10}\log\frac15
=\log2-h(1/10).
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]

We next derive the common entropy rather than creating its value by definition. Put \(X=(-1)^A\) and \(Z=(-1)^Y\). The balanced channel gives
\[
\mathbb E(X)=\mathbb E(Z)=0,
\qquad
\mathbb E(XZ)=P^\star(A=Y)-P^\star(A\neq Y)=\frac45.
\tag{2}
\]
Consider any finite exact product-mixture representation of \(P^\star\). Write \(\pi_u=P(U=u)\), \(x_u=\mathbb E(X\mid U=u)\), and \(z_u=\mathbb E(Z\mid U=u)\). Conditional independence and (2) imply
\[
\sum_u\pi_u x_u=0,
\qquad
\sum_u\pi_u z_u=0,
\qquad
\sum_u\pi_u x_uz_u=\frac45.
\tag{3}
\]
Fix a positive-mass state and write \(w=\pi_u\). Since \(|x_v|,|z_v|\leq1\), the two zero-mean equalities in (3) give
\[
w|x_u|
=\left|\sum_{v\neq u}\pi_vx_v\right|\leq1-w,
\qquad
w|z_u|
=\left|\sum_{v\neq u}\pi_vz_v\right|\leq1-w.
\tag{4}
\]
Using (4), the triangle inequality, and \(|x_vz_v|\leq1\),
\[
\frac45
\leq w|x_uz_u|+\sum_{v\neq u}\pi_v|x_vz_v|
\leq\frac{(1-w)^2}{w}+(1-w)
=\frac{1-w}{w}.
\tag{5}
\]
Thus every atom obeys \(w\leq5/9\). Let \(q=\max_u\pi_u\). If \(q\leq1/2\), then
\[
\operatorname{Ent}(U)
=\sum_u\pi_u\log(1/\pi_u)
\geq\log(1/q)\geq\log2>h(4/9).
\tag{6}
\]
The last inequality is strict because \(h'(s)=\log((1-s)/s)\) changes sign only at \(s=1/2\), so \(h\) is uniquely maximized there.
If \(1/2\leq q\leq5/9\), group all states other than a maximizing atom. Writing their normalized masses as \(\bar\pi_v=\pi_v/(1-q)\), direct expansion of the entropy gives
\[
\operatorname{Ent}(U)
=h(q)+(1-q)\sum_{v\neq u_{\max}}\bar\pi_v\log(1/\bar\pi_v)
\geq h(q)\geq h(5/9)=h(4/9),
\tag{7}
\]
where nonnegativity gives the first inequality and \(h'(s)=\log((1-s)/s)\leq0\) on \([1/2,1]\) gives the second. Equations (6)--(7) prove \(G_{P^\star}(A;Y)\geq h(4/9)\) for every finite auxiliary.

For equality, take two auxiliary states with masses \(5/9\) and \(4/9\). In the first state let \(A\) and \(Y\) be independent Bernoulli variables with success probabilities \(1/10\); in the second state let \(A=Y=1\) deterministically. In the order \((0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1)\), the induced cell probabilities are
\[
\frac59\frac{81}{100}=\frac9{20},
\quad
\frac59\frac9{100}=\frac1{20},
\quad
\frac59\frac9{100}=\frac1{20},
\quad
\frac59\frac1{100}+\frac49=\frac9{20}.
\tag{8}
\]
Thus (8) is a factual-compatible exact product mixture, and its auxiliary entropy is \(h(4/9)\). Together with (6)--(7),
\[
G_{P^\star}(A;Y)=h(4/9).
\tag{9}
\]

We certify the displayed decimals and strict inequalities without a floating-point premise. For \(x>0\), put \(z=(x-1)/(x+1)\), so \(|z|<1\). Integrating the finite geometric identity
\[
\frac1{1-t^2}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}t^{2k}+\frac{t^{2n+2}}{1-t^2}
\]
from \(0\) to \(z\) yields
\[
\log x=2\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{z^{2k+1}}{2k+1}+R_n,
\qquad
|R_n|\leq
\frac{2|z|^{2n+3}}{(2n+3)(1-z^2)}.
\tag{10}
\]
All quantities in the truncated sums and remainder bounds are rational for the rational arguments occurring in (1) and (9). Exact rational arithmetic with \(n=60\) gives
\[
0.3680642<I_{P^\star}(A;Y)<0.3680643<\frac12
<0.6869615<G_{P^\star}(A;Y)<0.6869617.
\tag{11}
\]
Therefore the six-decimal expansions in the claim and both strict inequalities follow.

It remains to prove neighborhood persistence. By \(\texttt{lem:binary-common-entropy-two-state}\), for every binary law the minimum defining common entropy is attained on the compact parameter space
\[
\Delta_2\times[0,1]^2\times[0,1]^2,
\tag{12}
\]
where the last two factors are the Bernoulli success probabilities of \(A\) and \(Y\) in each state. The product-mixture map and entropy, with \(0\log0=0\), are continuous.

Let \(P_n\to P\). Choose two-state minimizers for \(P_n\) and pass, along a subsequence attaining \(\liminf_nG_{P_n}(A;Y)\), to a convergent subsequence in (12). Continuity of the mixture map makes the limit feasible for \(P\), and continuity of entropy gives
\[
G_P(A;Y)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}G_{P_n}(A;Y).
\tag{13}
\]
Hence \(G_P(A;Y)\) is lower semicontinuous. Mutual information is continuous on the finite positive-law simplex because it is a finite sum of continuous \(x\log x\) terms and the marginals stay positive. By (11), there is therefore a relative neighborhood \(\mathcal N\) of \(P^\star\) in the positive binary-law simplex such that
\[
I_P(A;Y)<\frac12<G_P(A;Y)
\qquad(P\in\mathcal N).
\tag{14}
\]
At \((P^\star,\kappa^\star)\), and throughout \(\mathcal N\) at budget \(1/2\), the rule \(I_P(A;Y)\leq\kappa\) therefore accepts the origin. In contrast, \(\texttt{thm:origin-common-entropy-frontier}\) and (14) give \((0,0)\notin\Theta_{1/2}(P)\). This proves the claimed strict estimand-level false inclusion and its persistence.
