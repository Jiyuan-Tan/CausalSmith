The technique is statewise convex interpolation of response kernels, which preserves the latent distribution and therefore incurs no additional entropy. Fix \(\theta\in\Theta_\kappa(P)\). By the constructive direction of \(\texttt{lem:factor-program-equivalence}\), there are a finite \(m\) and \((\pi,e,r)\in\mathcal F_{m,\kappa}(P)\) such that
\[
\Phi_m(\pi,e,r)=\theta.
\]
Fix \(\theta_0\in\mathcal S_0(P)\). By the constructive zero-budget part of \(\texttt{thm:endpoint-geometry}\), there is a response-type distribution \(s=(s_{ij})_{i,j\in\{0,1\}}\in\Delta_4\), common to all latent states, satisfying
\[
\sum_j s_{1j}=\mu_0,
\qquad
\sum_i s_{i1}=\mu_1,
\qquad
(s_{01},s_{10})=\theta_0.
\]
For \(t\in[0,1]\), retain \(\pi\) and \(e\) and define, for every state \(u\),
\[
r^{(t)}_{ij\mid u}=t r_{ij\mid u}+(1-t)s_{ij}.
\]
Convexity of \(\Delta_4\) gives \(r^{(t)}_{\cdot\cdot\mid u}\in\Delta_4\).

It remains to verify the factual equations rather than assume that interpolation preserves them. Summing the original factual equations over \(y\) yields
\[
\sum_u\pi_u e_u=p_1,
\qquad
\sum_u\pi_u(1-e_u)=1-p_1.
\]
For \(a,y\in\{0,1\}\), define the \(a\)-margin of \(s\) by
\[
s_{a,y}=\sum_{i,j}s_{ij}\mathbf 1\{(1-a)i+aj=y\}.
\]
The displayed response margins and their complements imply
\[
s_{a,y}=P(Y=y\mid A=a).
\]
Treatment positivity makes these conditional probabilities well defined. Therefore, for every \(a,y\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\sum_u\pi_u e_u^a(1-e_u)^{1-a}
 \sum_{i,j}r^{(t)}_{ij\mid u}
 \mathbf 1\{(1-a)i+aj=y\}\\
&\quad=t p_{ay}
 +(1-t)s_{a,y}\sum_u\pi_u e_u^a(1-e_u)^{1-a}\\
&\quad=t p_{ay}+(1-t)P(Y=y\mid A=a)P(A=a)\\
&\quad=p_{ay}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus \((\pi,e,r^{(t)})\) satisfies every factual constraint. Its latent masses are exactly \(\pi\), so
\[
-\sum_u\pi_u\log\pi_u\leq\kappa
\]
is unchanged. In particular, no mixture indicator is adjoined. Hence \((\pi,e,r^{(t)})\in\mathcal F_{m,\kappa}(P)\).

Finally, linearity of the benefit--harm projection gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Phi_m(\pi,e,r^{(t)})
&=t\Phi_m(\pi,e,r)
 +(1-t)\left(\sum_u\pi_u s_{01},\sum_u\pi_u s_{10}\right)\\
&=t\theta+(1-t)\theta_0,
\end{aligned}
\]
where \(\sum_u\pi_u=1\) was used in the second equality. The reverse constructive direction of \(\texttt{lem:factor-program-equivalence}\) now yields
\[
(1-t)\theta_0+t\theta\in\Theta_\kappa(P).
\]
Thus every \(\theta_0\in\mathcal S_0(P)\) is a star center. The set \(\mathcal S_0(P)=\Theta_0(P)\) is nonempty by \(\texttt{thm:endpoint-geometry}\); choosing any one of its points joins every \(\theta\in\Theta_\kappa(P)\) to that common center by a line segment. Concatenating two such segments gives a continuous path between any two points of \(\Theta_\kappa(P)\), so \(\Theta_\kappa(P)\) is path connected.
