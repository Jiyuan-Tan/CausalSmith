The techniques are Fr\'echet response-type algebra at zero budget and an explicit factual-cell split at high budget.  We first treat the zero budget.  For a finite discrete variable, every term \(-\pi_u\log\pi_u\) is nonnegative and vanishes only when \(\pi_u\in\{0,1\}\).  Thus \(\operatorname{Ent}_Q(U)=0\) forces exactly one state to have mass one.  Conditional exchangeability then reduces to
\[
 A\perp(Y(0),Y(1)).
\]
Treatment positivity makes both observed conditional risks well defined, and consistency and factual compatibility give
\[
 Q\{Y(0)=1\}=\mu_0,qquad Q\{Y(1)=1\}=\mu_1.
\]
Writing \(x=Q\{Y(0)=1,Y(1)=1\}\), the four response-type masses are
\[
 r_{11}=x,quad r_{01}=\mu_1-x,quad r_{10}=\mu_0-x,quad
 r_{00}=1-\mu_0-\mu_1+x,
\]
and nonnegativity is equivalent to
\[
 \max\{0,\mu_0+\mu_1-1\}\leq x\leq\min\{\mu_0,\mu_1\}.
\]
Therefore \(B-H=\mu_1-\mu_0\), \(0\leq B\leq\min\{\mu_1,1-\mu_0\}\), and \(0\leq H\leq\min\{\mu_0,1-\mu_1\}\), proving \(\Theta_0(P)\subseteq\mathcal S_0(P)\).

Conversely, for \((b,h)\in\mathcal S_0(P)\), set
\[
 r_{01}=b,qquad r_{10}=h,qquad
 r_{11}=\mu_1-b=\mu_0-h,qquad
 r_{00}=1-\mu_0-b=1-\mu_1-h.
\]
The defining bounds of \(\mathcal S_0(P)\) make all four numbers nonnegative, and they sum to one.  Take \(U\) constant, take \(A\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p_1)\) independently of this response type, and impose consistency.  The arm-specific margins are \(\mu_0,\mu_1\), so the factual law is \(P\), the entropy is zero, and the benefit--harm pair is \((b,h)\).  This proves the sharp equality \(\Theta_0(P)=\mathcal S_0(P)\).

For the high-budget endpoint, first take any admissible \(Q\).  Splitting the benefit event according to \(A\) and using consistency gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 B(Q)
 &=Q(A=0,Y(0)=0,Y(1)=1)+Q(A=1,Y(0)=0,Y(1)=1)\\
 &\leq Q(A=0,Y=0)+Q(A=1,Y=1)=p_{00}+p_{11}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Similarly,
\[
 H(Q)\leq Q(A=0,Y=1)+Q(A=1,Y=0)=p_{01}+p_{10}.
\]
Nonnegativity therefore yields \(\Theta_\kappa(P)\subseteq\mathcal R(P)\) for every budget.

To prove simultaneous attainability, fix \((b,h)\in\mathcal R(P)\) and choose
\[
 b_0=\min\{b,p_{00}\},\quad b_1=b-b_0,qquad
 h_0=\min\{h,p_{01}\},\quad h_1=h-h_0.
\]
The rectangle inequalities imply \(0\leq b_0\leq p_{00}\), \(0\leq b_1\leq p_{11}\), \(0\leq h_0\leq p_{01}\), and \(0\leq h_1\leq p_{10}\).  Define the joint masses of \((A,Y(0),Y(1))\) by the following nonzero allocations:
\[
 \begin{array}{c|cccc}
 &00&01&10&11\\ \hline
 A=0&p_{00}-b_0&b_0&h_0&p_{01}-h_0\\
 A=1&p_{10}-h_1&b_1&h_1&p_{11}-b_1.
 \end{array}
\]
Each row reproduces its observed factual cells under consistency, and the total masses of types \(01\) and \(10\) are \(b\) and \(h\), respectively.  Set \(U=A\).  Given \(U\), treatment is deterministic, so \(A\perp(Y(0),Y(1))\mid U\) holds even on boundary response distributions.  Finally,
\[
 \operatorname{Ent}_Q(U)=h(P(A=1))=h(p_1).
\]
Thus the displayed construction belongs to \(\mathfrak M_\kappa(P)\) whenever \(\kappa\geq h(p_1)\), proving \(\mathcal R(P)\subseteq\Theta_\kappa(P)\).  Both coordinate intervals are therefore jointly sharp, not merely coordinatewise outer bounds.
