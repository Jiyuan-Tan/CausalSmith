\section*{pid\_\allowbreak{}commonentropy\_\allowbreak{}benefit\_\allowbreak{}harm\_\allowbreak{}contact}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} Average effects conceal simultaneous benefit and harm, while existing low-entropy-confounding work studies scalar causal restrictions. The focal object is the exact joint set \(\Theta_\kappa(P)\) of benefit and harm over factual-compatible common-latent SCMs. The proved population kernel is a global exact three-state representation, an iff common-entropy threshold for universal no individual effect, a full-support law showing that the entropy-implied observed mutual-information inequality is necessary but not sufficient for origin feasibility, and, under treatment positivity, the zero-budget Fréchet segment, high-budget factual rectangle, and a star kernel at every intermediate budget. A finite-sample proposition maps an exact multinomial region into conservative whole-set coverage. Rank-free BH-CERT termination and the regular-versus-contact Hausdorff law remain explicit open questions.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} JiangWeiKocaoglu2023 uses mutual-information consequences of low latent entropy for scalar causal bounds; JiangKocaoglu2024 studies graph-specific conditional common entropy in an instrumental-variable graph; Zhang2024 conditions on a specified full marginal law \(P(U)\); and CiocaneaTeodorescuGabrielSjolander2025 studies probability of benefit in randomized trials under a cross-world sensitivity parameter. None identifies the joint benefit--harm plane for a no-instrument binary factual law with unknown finite \(U\) constrained only by \(\operatorname{Ent}_Q(U)\leq\kappa\). Existing interval and Pareto methods do not provide the proposed exact witness/exclusion contract at zero masses and entropy contact.

\paragraph{Niche.} At the no-effect origin, exact feasibility is a common-entropy factorization problem. The observed-law inequality \(I_P(A;Y)\leq\kappa\) follows necessarily by data processing from any origin witness, but the balanced law shows that it is not sufficient. Away from the origin, factual compatibility, cross-world response types, and the nonconvex Shannon sublevel set determine a two-dimensional sharp object with singular support changes.

\paragraph{Fill.} The project proves a global support-three equality and the exact origin frontier, separates that frontier from its necessary mutual-information relaxation without attributing a sufficient test to any published workflow, and establishes endpoint and star-kernel geometry on the positive-arm domain. It also proves conservative whole-set coverage by deterministic inversion of an exact multinomial region, while retaining BH-CERT and contact asymptotics as open strengthening paths.

\section{Related work}

KumarLiElGamal2014 defines one-shot common entropy over arbitrary finite auxiliaries and supplies the finite-alphabet cardinality result used to compress the joint law of treatment and the four-valued response type; the present theorem adds the causal preservation argument for all factual cells and both benefit--harm coordinates. KocaogluShakkottaiDimakisCaramanisVishwanath2020 uses common entropy for causal-graph discovery, not joint counterfactual benefit and harm. JiangWeiKocaoglu2023 motivates low-entropy confounding and derives scalar causal outer programs from mutual-information consequences. The present strict-separation proposition only proves that the entropy-implied observed-law inequality \(I_P(A;Y)\leq\kappa\) can hold while the causal origin is infeasible; it neither evaluates nor refutes a published estimator or origin-feasibility workflow in JiangWeiKocaoglu2023. JiangKocaoglu2024 develops graph-specific conditional common entropy for instrumental-variable testing and scalar partial identification in an instrumental-variable graph. The support-three theorem instead concerns a no-instrument binary factual law and the joint response-type image \((B,H)\) under an unconditional entropy budget; neither result implies the other. CiocaneaTeodorescuGabrielSjolander2025 studies probability of benefit as one coordinate in randomized trials under a cross-world sensitivity parameter and supplies confidence intervals at fixed sensitivity values. This paper gives a population joint benefit--harm set under latent-entropy confounding and does not supply that sampling-inference result. Zhang2024 gives tight binary causal partial identification conditional on a specified full marginal law \(P(U)\), so neither its results nor the present unknown-\(U\) theorem subsume the other. TianPearl2000 supplies the unrestricted probabilities-of-causation benchmark; Pena2023 and GabrielSachsJensen2024 study benefit or harm under different restrictions. JonzonSachsGabriel2023 supplies causaloptim's polyhedral response-function workflow as a computational contrast, not a method claimed to be extended here. ArayaReyes2016 surveys generic interval branch-and-bound without exact singular causal witnesses; FernandezGoth2009 gives an outer efficient-set method rather than full causal-image certification; and AudetMessineNinin2022 certifies Pareto proximity rather than exact SCM witness and exclusion sections. None settles the rank-free BH-CERT question. ChafaiConcordet2009 informs the exact multinomial coverage event; the whole-set proposition is its deterministic image-containment consequence. ChernozhukovHongTamer2007, FangSantos2019, and MastenPoirier2020 motivate, without settling, the moving-center Hausdorff derivative and entropy-contact law.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:factual-compatibility]\label{ass:factual-compatibility}
The \((A,Y)\)-marginal of \(Q\) equals \(P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Observed-law compatibility for a discrete SCM, \cite{DuarteFinkelsteinKnoxMummoloShpitser2024}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:consistency]\label{ass:consistency}
Under \(Q\), \(Y=Y(A)\) almost surely.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Counterfactual consistency, \cite{TianPearl2000}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:latent-exchangeability]\label{ass:latent-exchangeability}
Under \(Q\), \(A\perp (Y(0),Y(1))\mid U\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Latent conditional exchangeability in the common-cause SCM, \cite{JiangWeiKocaoglu2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:entropy-budget]\label{ass:entropy-budget}
The latent factor satisfies \(\operatorname{Ent}_Q(U)\leq\kappa\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Shannon low-entropy confounding restriction, \cite{JiangWeiKocaoglu2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:treatment-positivity]\label{ass:treatment-positivity}
The observed treatment probability satisfies \(0<p_1<1\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Binary treatment positivity, \cite{TianPearl2000}).
\end{assumption}

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Member properties.} \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}factual-\allowbreak{}compatibility}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}consistency}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}latent-\allowbreak{}exchangeability}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}entropy-\allowbreak{}budget}.

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Sharp joint benefit-\allowbreak{}-\allowbreak{}harm set}

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}model-\allowbreak{}fiber}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:finite-factor-program]\label{def:finite-factor-program}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Finite latent-\allowbreak{}factor program}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \[\mathcal F_{m,\kappa}(P)=\left\{(\pi,e,r):\pi\in\Delta_m,\ e\in[0,1]^m,\ r_{\cdot\cdot\mid u}\in\Delta_4,\ -\sum_{u=1}^m\pi_u\log\pi_u\leq\kappa,\ p_{ay}=\sum_{u=1}^m\pi_u e_u^a(1-e_u)^{1-a}\sum_{i,j=0}^1r_{ij\mid u}\mathbf 1\{(1-a)i+aj=y\}\text{ for all }a,y\right\}.\]
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \(P\); \(\kappa\); \(m\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:common-entropy]\label{def:common-entropy}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Observed common entropy}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Let the auxiliary range over every finite alphabet. Define
\[
G_P(A;Y)=\inf_{\substack{m\geq1,\ \widetilde P\in\mathcal P(\mathcal A\times\mathcal Y\times\{1,\ldots,m\}):\\ \widetilde P_{A,Y}=P,\ A\perp_{\widetilde P}Y\mid U}}
\left\{-\sum_{u=1}^m\widetilde P(U=u)\log\widetilde P(U=u)\right\}.
\]
For binary \(A\) and \(Y\), the binary specialization of the finite-cardinality theorem permits the convention \(1\leq m\leq2\) without changing the value; the resulting finite union of compact feasible sets attains the infimum, so \(G_P(A;Y)\) is a minimum. Zero-mass auxiliary states are allowed, with \(0\log0=0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \(P\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:zero-budget-segment]\label{def:zero-budget-segment}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Zero-\allowbreak{}budget Fréchet segment}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \[\mathcal S_0(P)=\left\{(b,h)\in[0,1]^2:b-h=\mu_1-\mu_0,\ 0\leq b\leq\min\{\mu_1,1-\mu_0\},\ 0\leq h\leq\min\{\mu_0,1-\mu_1\}\right\}.\]
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \(P\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:factual-rectangle]\label{def:factual-rectangle}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Unrestricted factual rectangle}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \[\mathcal R(P)=[0,p_{00}+p_{11}]\times[0,p_{01}+p_{10}].\]
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \(P\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:balanced-channel]\label{def:balanced-channel}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Balanced full-\allowbreak{}support witness law}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \[P^\star(A=0)=P^\star(A=1)=\tfrac12,\qquad P^\star(Y=A\mid A)=\tfrac9{10},\] so \(p^\star_{00}=p^\star_{11}=9/20\) and \(p^\star_{01}=p^\star_{10}=1/20\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \(\mathcal A\); \(\mathcal Y\).
\end{definition}

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:factor-program-equivalence]\label{lem:factor-program-equivalence}
For every \(m\geq1\), the image \(\Phi_m(\mathcal F_{m,\kappa}(P))\) is exactly the set of benefit--harm pairs generated by laws in \(\mathfrak M_\kappa(P)\) whose latent factor has at most \(m\) states. Consequently, \(\Theta_\kappa(P)=\bigcup_{m\geq1}\Phi_m(\mathcal F_{m,\kappa}(P))\), and both directions are constructive.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(R=(Y(0),Y(1))\).  The technique is direct finite-kernel factorization: we prove the two inclusions by explicit constructions.

First let \(Q\in\mathfrak M_\kappa(P)\) and suppose that the support of \(U\) has size at most \(m\).  Relabel its positive-mass states by a subset of \(\{1,\ldots,m\}\), pad with zero-mass states if necessary, and define, for every positive-mass state,
\[
 \pi_u=Q(U=u),\qquad e_u=Q(A=1\mid U=u),\qquad
 r_{ij\mid u}=Q(R=(i,j)\mid U=u).
\]
At a zero-mass state choose \(e_u\in[0,1]\) and \(r_{\cdot\cdot\mid u}\in\Delta_4\) arbitrarily.  Conditional exchangeability, namely \(\texttt{ass:latent-exchangeability}\), gives, for every positive-mass state,
\[
 Q(A=a,R=(i,j),U=u)
 =\pi_u e_u^a(1-e_u)^{1-a}r_{ij\mid u}.
\]
By consistency \(\texttt{ass:consistency}\) and factual compatibility \(\texttt{ass:factual-compatibility}\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 p_{ay}
 &=Q(A=a,Y=y)\\
 &=\sum_{u=1}^m\sum_{i,j=0}^1
 \pi_u e_u^a(1-e_u)^{1-a}r_{ij\mid u}
 \mathbf 1\{(1-a)i+aj=y\}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Moreover, the entropy restriction \(\texttt{ass:entropy-budget}\) is exactly
\[
 -\sum_{u=1}^m\pi_u\log\pi_u=\operatorname{Ent}_Q(U)\leq\kappa,
\]
with the stipulated convention at zero.  Hence \((\pi,e,r)\in\mathcal F_{m,\kappa}(P)\).  Direct summation also gives
\[
 B(Q)=\sum_{u=1}^m\pi_u r_{01\mid u},\qquad
 H(Q)=\sum_{u=1}^m\pi_u r_{10\mid u},
\]
so the pair generated by \(Q\) is \(\Phi_m(\pi,e,r)\).

Conversely, let \((\pi,e,r)\in\mathcal F_{m,\kappa}(P)\).  On the finite space \(\{1,\ldots,m\}\times\{0,1\}\times\{0,1\}^2\), put
\[
 Q\{U=u,A=a,R=(i,j)\}
 =\pi_u e_u^a(1-e_u)^{1-a}r_{ij\mid u},
\]
and define \(Y=(1-A)Y(0)+AY(1)\).  The displayed masses are nonnegative and sum to one.  Their factorization proves \(A\perp R\mid U\), the definition of \(Y\) proves consistency, and the factual equations in \(\texttt{def:finite-factor-program}\) prove \(Q_{A,Y}=P\).  The entropy is \(-\sum_u\pi_u\log\pi_u\leq\kappa\), and the two response-type sums are precisely \(\Phi_m(\pi,e,r)\).  Thus \(Q\in\mathfrak M_\kappa(P)\) and realizes the proposed pair.  Taking the union over all finite \(m\), as required by \(\texttt{def:model-fiber}\) and \(\texttt{def:identified-set}\), proves the final equality and the asserted constructive equivalence.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This lemma makes sharpness causal: every numerical factor is completed to an SCM, and every admissible SCM is represented by the program. \textit{Gap.} DuarteFinkelsteinKnoxMummoloShpitser2024 gives a general response-function polynomialization, while JiangWeiKocaoglu2023 uses mutual-information consequences; neither gives this exact Shannon-entropy factor fiber for the joint cross-world coordinates. \textit{Consumer.} The equivalence is the soundness contract for every subsequent geometric statement and for BH-CERT witnesses.

\begin{theorem}[thm:three-state-reduction]\label{thm:three-state-reduction}
For every binary observed law \(P\) and every \(\kappa\in[0,\log2]\), \[\Theta_\kappa(P)=\Phi_3(\mathcal F_{3,\kappa}(P)).\] Thus every feasible \((B,H)\) has a factual-compatible causal witness with at most three latent states and no larger Shannon entropy.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a binary observed law \(P\), a budget \(\kappa\in[0,\log2]\), and \(\theta\in\Theta_\kappa(P)\).  The first technique is exact finite-mixture compression at a fixed joint law.  By \(\texttt{lem:factor-program-equivalence}\), there are an integer \(m\geq1\) and \((\pi,e,r)\in\mathcal F_{m,\kappa}(P)\) such that \(\Phi_m(\pi,e,r)=\theta\).  Let \(\mathcal R_0=\{0,1\}^2\), write \(R=(Y(0),Y(1))\), and define a mass function on \(\mathcal A\times\mathcal R_0\) by
\[
J\bigl(a,(i,j)\bigr)=\sum_{u=1}^m\pi_u e_u^a(1-e_u)^{1-a}r_{ij\mid u}.
\tag{1}
\]
The right side is nonnegative, and
\[
\sum_{a,i,j}J\bigl(a,(i,j)\bigr)
=\sum_{u=1}^m\pi_u\{(1-e_u)+e_u\}
 \sum_{i,j=0}^1r_{ij\mid u}
=\sum_{u=1}^m\pi_u=1,
\tag{2}
\]
so \(J\) is a probability mass function.  Equation (1) itself gives an extension of \(J\) by the original state index: conditional on \(U=u\), \(A\) is Bernoulli with success probability \(e_u\), \(R\) has mass function \(r_{\cdot\cdot\mid u}\), and \(A\perp R\mid U\).  Its entropy satisfies
\[
\operatorname{Ent}(U)=-\sum_{u=1}^m\pi_u\log\pi_u\leq\kappa
\tag{3}
\]
by \(\texttt{def:finite-factor-program}\).  Consequently the common entropy of this fixed joint law obeys
\[
G_J(A;R)\leq-\sum_{u=1}^m\pi_u\log\pi_u\leq\kappa.
\tag{4}
\]

Apply the published finite-alphabet common-entropy result recorded as \(\texttt{lem:binary-common-entropy-cardinality}\) with \(\mathcal X=\mathcal A\) and \(\mathcal Z=\mathcal R_0\).  Since their cardinalities are two and four, respectively, it supplies an entropy-minimizing exact product-mixture extension \(\widetilde J\) with auxiliary \(W\) satisfying
\[
A\perp_{\widetilde J}R\mid W,\qquad
\operatorname{Ent}_{\widetilde J}(W)=G_J(A;R),\qquad
|\operatorname{supp}(W)|\leq2^{\min\{2,4\}}-1=3.
\tag{5}
\]
This is the sole borrowed compression step.

Enumerate only the positive-mass states in (5), and for each define
\[
\pi'_w=\widetilde J(W=w),\qquad
e'_w=\widetilde J(A=1\mid W=w),\qquad
r'_{ij\mid w}=\widetilde J(R=(i,j)\mid W=w).
\tag{6}
\]
All conditionals in (6) are taken at \(\pi'_w>0\), so no division by zero occurs.  If fewer than three states have positive mass, pad with zero-mass states and choose their propensity and response distribution arbitrarily in the declared cube and simplices.  Because \(0\log0=0\), padding does not alter entropy.  Conditional independence in (5), preservation of the \((A,R)\)-marginal, and (1) imply, for every \(a,y\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\sum_w\pi'_w(e'_w)^a(1-e'_w)^{1-a}
 \sum_{i,j=0}^1r'_{ij\mid w}\mathbf 1\{(1-a)i+aj=y\}\\
&\quad=\sum_{i,j=0}^1J\bigl(a,(i,j)\bigr)\mathbf 1\{(1-a)i+aj=y\}\\
&\quad=\sum_{u=1}^m\pi_u e_u^a(1-e_u)^{1-a}
 \sum_{i,j=0}^1r_{ij\mid u}\mathbf 1\{(1-a)i+aj=y\}
=p_{ay}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
Thus all factual cells are preserved, including when an observed arm has probability zero; treatment positivity is not used.  Equations (4)--(6) also give the entropy bound.

The second technique is preservation of linear response-type marginals under fixed-joint compression.  Summing (1) over \(a\) and using \((1-e_u)+e_u=1\) yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_w\pi'_wr'_{01\mid w}
&=\sum_{a=0}^1J(a,(0,1))
=\sum_{u=1}^m\pi_ur_{01\mid u},\\
\sum_w\pi'_wr'_{10\mid w}
&=\sum_{a=0}^1J(a,(1,0))
=\sum_{u=1}^m\pi_ur_{10\mid u}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
By (5)--(8), the padded tuple belongs to \(\mathcal F_{3,\kappa}(P)\) and has \(\Phi_3\)-image \(\theta\).  Hence
\[
\Theta_\kappa(P)\subseteq\Phi_3(\mathcal F_{3,\kappa}(P)).
\tag{9}
\]
Conversely, \(\texttt{lem:factor-program-equivalence}\) constructs from every tuple in \(\mathcal F_{3,\kappa}(P)\) a factual-compatible full-data law satisfying consistency, conditional exchangeability, and the same entropy bound, with the same benefit--harm coordinates.  Therefore
\[
\Phi_3(\mathcal F_{3,\kappa}(P))\subseteq\Theta_\kappa(P).
\tag{10}
\]
Equations (9) and (10) prove the asserted equality and the constructive at-most-three-state conclusion for every binary observed law, including boundary laws.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem converts the infinite union of latent-factor programs into one fixed three-state sharp representation for every binary factual law, including boundary laws. \textit{Gap.} KumarLiElGamal2014 supplies finite attainment and the general support bound for an exact common-entropy factor of a fixed finite joint law. The new causal step preserves factual cells and both benefit--harm coordinates when compressing treatment jointly with the response type. JiangKocaoglu2024 instead concerns graph-specific conditional common entropy in an instrumental-variable graph, and CiocaneaTeodorescuGabrielSjolander2025 concerns one benefit coordinate under cross-world sensitivity in randomized trials with fixed-sensitivity confidence intervals; neither comparator implies this population joint unknown-\(U\) theorem, and this theorem implies neither comparator's result. \textit{Consumer.} The global fixed three-state domain yields one sharp finite-dimensional representation for exact population set inversion; it does not itself provide the open BH-CERT algorithm or fixed-sensitivity confidence intervals.

\begin{theorem}[thm:origin-common-entropy-frontier]\label{thm:origin-common-entropy-frontier}
The no-individual-effect point obeys the exact equivalence
\[
(0,0)\in\Theta_\kappa(P)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad G_P(A;Y)\leq\kappa.
\]
Here \(G_P(A;Y)\) optimizes over all finite auxiliary alphabets. When these equivalent conditions hold, its minimum is attained by an exact product-mixture factor with at most two positive latent states, and the explicit diagonal completion gives a causal witness \(Q\in\mathfrak M_\kappa(P)\) whose \(U\) also has at most two positive states and for which \(B(Q)=H(Q)=0\) and \(Y(0)=Y(1)\) almost surely. Conversely, every causal origin witness induces a feasible product-mixture factor with the same latent entropy.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By \(\texttt{def:identified-set}\), membership of the origin means that there is a law \(Q\in\mathfrak M_\kappa(P)\) with \(B(Q)=H(Q)=0\).  Because the potential outcomes are binary, the two off-diagonal response-type events partition the event on which the potential outcomes differ.  Hence, using the definitions of \(B(Q)\) and \(H(Q)\),
\[
 Q\{Y(0)\ne Y(1)\}
 =Q\{Y(0)=0,Y(1)=1\}+Q\{Y(0)=1,Y(1)=0\}
 =B(Q)+H(Q)=0.
\]
Put \(V=Y(0)\).  The preceding equality gives \(V=Y(1)\) \(Q\)-almost surely.  By \(\texttt{ass:consistency}\),
\[
 Y=Y(A)=V\qquad Q\text{-almost surely}.
\]
Moreover, \(\texttt{ass:latent-exchangeability}\) implies, for every positive-mass state \(u\),
\[
 Q(A=a,V=v\mid U=u)
 =\sum_{j\in\{0,1\}}Q(A=a,Y(0)=v,Y(1)=j\mid U=u)
 =Q(A=a\mid U=u)Q(V=v\mid U=u).
\]
Thus \(A\perp V\mid U\), and therefore \(A\perp Y\mid U\).  Let \(\widetilde P\) be the \((A,Y,U)\)-marginal of \(Q\).  The alphabet of \(U\) is finite by \(\texttt{def:model-fiber}\), \(\widetilde P_{A,Y}=P\) by \(\texttt{ass:factual-compatibility}\), and \(A\perp_{\widetilde P}Y\mid U\).  Thus \(\widetilde P\) is feasible in \(\texttt{def:common-entropy}\).  Its mixing entropy is unchanged by marginalization, so \(\texttt{ass:entropy-budget}\) yields
\[
 G_P(A;Y)\leq \operatorname{Ent}_{\widetilde P}(U)
 =\operatorname{Ent}_Q(U)\leq\kappa.
\]
This proves necessity and also proves the asserted entropy-preserving map from every causal origin witness to an observed product-mixture factor.

Conversely, suppose \(G_P(A;Y)\leq\kappa\).  By \(\texttt{lem:binary-common-entropy-two-state}\), the minimum in \(\texttt{def:common-entropy}\) is attained by an extension \(\widetilde P^\circ\) for which \(A\perp_{\widetilde P^\circ}Y\mid U\) and \(U\) has at most two positive-mass states.  In particular,
\[
 \operatorname{Ent}_{\widetilde P^\circ}(U)=G_P(A;Y)\leq\kappa.
\]
Define a full-data law explicitly by
\[
 Q^\circ\{A=a,Y(0)=i,Y(1)=j,Y=y,U=u\}
 =\widetilde P^\circ(A=a,Y=y,U=u)\mathbf 1\{i=y\}\mathbf 1\{j=y\}.
\]
Summing over \((i,j,u)\) shows \(Q^\circ_{A,Y}=P\).  The displayed construction puts all mass on \(Y(0)=Y(1)=Y\), so consistency holds and
\[
 B(Q^\circ)=H(Q^\circ)=0.
\]
For every \(u\) of positive mass, conditional product structure under \(\widetilde P^\circ\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &Q^\circ\{A=a,Y(0)=i,Y(1)=j\mid U=u\}\\
 &\quad=\widetilde P^\circ(A=a\mid U=u)
 \sum_{y\in\{0,1\}}\widetilde P^\circ(Y=y\mid U=u)
 \mathbf 1\{i=y\}\mathbf 1\{j=y\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The right-hand side is the product of the conditional law of \(A\) and the conditional law of \((Y(0),Y(1))\), proving \(A\perp (Y(0),Y(1))\mid U\).  Finally,
\[
 \operatorname{Ent}_{Q^\circ}(U)=\operatorname{Ent}_{\widetilde P^\circ}(U)\leq\kappa.
\]
Thus all four restrictions in \(\texttt{def:model-fiber}\) hold, so \(Q^\circ\in\mathfrak M_\kappa(P)\).  By \(\texttt{def:identified-set}\), \((0,0)\in\Theta_\kappa(P)\).  The same construction preserves the at-most-two positive states of \(U\), completing both sufficiency and the support claim.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The equivalence gives the exact entropy breakdown budget and constructively realizes the origin with at most two positive latent states. \textit{Gap.} Proposition 8 of KumarLiElGamal2014 supplies binary product-mixture compression, not the necessity map from a causal origin witness or the diagonal full-data completion proved here. \textit{Consumer.} The balanced contact test and BH-CERT origin face use the exact support-two witness rather than the full image's three-state domain.

\begin{proposition}[prop:strict-mi-false-inclusion]\label{prop:strict-mi-false-inclusion}
For every binary observed law \(P\) and entropy budget \(\kappa\), \((0,0)\in\Theta_\kappa(P)\) implies the entropy-induced observed-law inequality \(I_P(A;Y)\leq\kappa\); this inequality is necessary, but it is not sufficient. For the balanced law \(P^\star\), using natural logarithms, \[I_{P^\star}(A;Y)=\log2-h(1/10)=0.368064\ldots<\kappa^\star=1/2<h(4/9)=0.686962\ldots=G_{P^\star}(A;Y).\] Thus \(I_{P^\star}(A;Y)\leq\kappa^\star\), while \((0,0)\notin\Theta_{\kappa^\star}(P^\star)\). The strict inequalities persist on a relative neighborhood of \(P^\star\) in the positive binary-law simplex.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
First consider an arbitrary \(Q\in\mathfrak M_\kappa(P)\) with \(B(Q)=H(Q)=0\).  The first part of the proof of \(\texttt{thm:origin-common-entropy-frontier}\) gives \(Y(0)=Y(1)=Y\) almost surely and therefore the conditional Markov relation \(A\perp Y\mid U\).  Since \(Q_{A,Y}=P\), finite-alphabet entropy identities give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 I_P(A;Y)&=I_Q(A;Y)\\
 &\leq I_Q(A;U)\\
 &=\operatorname{Ent}_Q(U)-\operatorname{Ent}_Q(U\mid A)\\
 &\leq\operatorname{Ent}_Q(U)\\
 &\leq\kappa.
\end{aligned}
\]
For completeness, the data-processing inequality in the second line follows here directly from
\[
 I_Q(A;U,Y)=I_Q(A;U)+I_Q(A;Y\mid U)=I_Q(A;U)
\]
and
\[
 I_Q(A;U,Y)=I_Q(A;Y)+I_Q(A;U\mid Y)\geq I_Q(A;Y).
\]
The two conditional mutual informations are finite sums of conditional Kullback--Leibler divergences; their nonnegativity follows termwise from \(\log x\leq x-1\).  Also \(\operatorname{Ent}_Q(U\mid A)\geq0\) because every summand is a nonnegative multiple of \(-\log Q(U=u\mid A=a)\).  Thus \(I_P(A;Y)\leq\kappa\) is indeed necessary for origin feasibility.

We next calculate the two information quantities for \(P^\star\).  Its two margins are uniform, and its crossover probability is \(1/10\).  Therefore
\[
 I_{P^\star}(A;Y)=\operatorname{Ent}_{P^\star}(Y)-
 \operatorname{Ent}_{P^\star}(Y\mid A)=\log2-h(1/10).
\]
It remains to calculate the common entropy.  By \(\texttt{lem:binary-common-entropy-two-state}\), choose an entropy-minimizing product-mixture factor with at most two positive states.  One state cannot represent \(P^\star\), since under \(P^\star\)
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(A,Y)=P^\star(A=1,Y=1)-P^\star(A=1)P^\star(Y=1)
 =\frac9{20}-\frac14=\frac15\ne0.
\]
Consequently there are exactly two positive weights.  Relabel them as \(q\) and \(1-q\), where \(0<q\leq1/2\), and write
\[
 \alpha_i=P(A=1\mid U=i),\qquad
 \beta_i=P(Y=1\mid U=i),\qquad i\in\{0,1\},
\]
with state \(1\) having weight \(q\).  Conditional independence and the uniform margins imply
\[
 \frac15=q(1-q)(\alpha_1-\alpha_0)(\beta_1-\beta_0),
 \qquad q\alpha_1+(1-q)\alpha_0=q\beta_1+(1-q)\beta_0=\frac12.
\]
Let \(d_\alpha=\alpha_1-\alpha_0\).  Solving the first marginal equation for the component probabilities gives
\[
 \alpha_1=\frac12+(1-q)d_\alpha,
 \qquad \alpha_0=\frac12-qd_\alpha.
\]
Since \(0\leq\alpha_1\leq1\), one has \(|d_\alpha|\leq\{2(1-q)\}^{-1}\).  The identical argument gives \(|\beta_1-\beta_0|\leq\{2(1-q)\}^{-1}\).  The covariance product is positive, and hence
\[
 \frac15\leq q(1-q)\frac1{4(1-q)^2}=\frac{q}{4(1-q)}.
\]
Solving this inequality yields \(q\geq4/9\).  Since \(h'(q)=\log\{(1-q)/q\}\geq0\) for \(0<q\leq1/2\), every minimizing two-state factor has entropy
\[
 h(q)\geq h(4/9).
\]
This lower bound is attained.  Give state \(1\) weight \(4/9\) and set \(\alpha_1=\beta_1=1\); give state \(0\) weight \(5/9\) and set \(\alpha_0=\beta_0=1/10\).  The resulting product mixture has cells
\[
 \frac59\left(\frac9{10}\right)^2=\frac9{20},\quad
 \frac59\left(\frac9{10}\right)\left(\frac1{10}\right)=\frac1{20},\quad
 \frac1{20},\quad
 \frac49+\frac59\left(\frac1{10}\right)^2=\frac9{20},
\]
in the order \((0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1)\).  Thus it represents \(P^\star\) exactly and proves
\[
 G_{P^\star}(A;Y)=h(4/9).
\]

The displayed decimals and strict comparisons can be certified without floating-point assumptions.  For \(x>1\), put \(z=(x-1)/(x+1)\).  Integrating the finite geometric expansion of \((1-s^2)^{-1}\) gives, for every integer \(M\geq0\),
\[
 \log x=2\sum_{j=0}^{M}\frac{z^{2j+1}}{2j+1}+R_M,
 \qquad 0<R_M<\frac{2z^{2M+3}}{(2M+3)(1-z^2)}.
\]
Use \(M=10\), together with
\[
 \log10=3\log2+\log(5/4),\qquad
 \log(9/4)=\log2+\log(9/8),
\]
and \(h(1/10)=\tfrac1{10}\log10+\tfrac9{10}\log(10/9)\), \(h(4/9)=\tfrac49\log(9/4)+\tfrac59\log(9/5)\).  Substitution of the rational values \(z=1/3,1/9,1/19,1/17,2/7\), respectively, and rational interval arithmetic yields
\[
 0.3680642071<I_{P^\star}(A;Y)<0.3680642072
 <\frac12
 <0.6869615765<G_{P^\star}(A;Y)<0.6869615767.
\]
This proves the asserted decimal expansions and strict inequalities.  At \(\kappa^\star=1/2\), the necessary observed-law inequality therefore holds, whereas \(G_{P^\star}(A;Y)>\kappa^\star\); \(\texttt{thm:origin-common-entropy-frontier}\) then gives \((0,0)\notin\Theta_{\kappa^\star}(P^\star)\).  Hence the necessary inequality is not sufficient.

Finally, parameterize every two-state product mixture by the compact cube
\[
 D=[0,1]^5\ni(q,\alpha_0,\alpha_1,\beta_0,\beta_1)
\]
and let \(F:D\to\mathcal P(\{0,1\}^2)\) be its continuously induced cell-probability map.  By \(\texttt{lem:binary-common-entropy-two-state}\),
\[
 G_P(A;Y)=\min\{h(q):d\in D,\ F(d)=P\}.
\]
If \(P_n\to P\), choose minimizers \(d_n\).  Along a subsequence realizing \(\liminf_nG_{P_n}(A;Y)\), compactness gives a further subsequence with \(d_n\to d\in D\).  Continuity gives \(F(d)=P\) and
\[
 G_P(A;Y)\leq h(q)=\liminf_nG_{P_n}(A;Y).
\]
Thus \(P\mapsto G_P(A;Y)\) is lower semicontinuous.  On the positive binary-law simplex, \(P\mapsto I_P(A;Y)\) is continuous because it is a finite sum of continuous logarithmic cell expressions with positive cells and margins.  Since at \(P^\star\) one has \(I_P(A;Y)<1/2<G_P(A;Y)\), continuity and lower semicontinuity provide a relative neighborhood on which both strict inequalities persist.  The exact frontier theorem excludes the origin throughout that neighborhood at budget \(1/2\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The full-support witness separates exact origin feasibility from an entropy-implied necessary observed-information inequality. \textit{Gap.} The proposition uses the Markov chain induced by an origin witness to prove \(I_P(A;Y)\leq I_Q(A;U)\leq\operatorname{Ent}_Q(U)\leq\kappa\), then shows on an open positive-law neighborhood that this necessary inequality is not sufficient. It does not attribute a sufficient origin test to JiangWeiKocaoglu2023 or any other published workflow. \textit{Consumer.} Users can distinguish a cheap necessary observed-information screen from the exact common-entropy origin frontier without interpreting the example as a refutation of a published estimator.

\begin{theorem}[thm:endpoint-geometry]\label{thm:endpoint-geometry}
Under treatment positivity, the sensitivity path has the exact endpoints \[\Theta_0(P)=\mathcal S_0(P),\qquad \Theta_\kappa(P)=\mathcal R(P)\quad\text{for every }\kappa\geq h(p_1).\] The first equality is attained by every Fréchet coupling of Bernoulli margins \(\mu_0\) and \(\mu_1\); the second is attained by a witness with \(U=A\). In particular, the high-budget coordinate projections recover the sharp factual benefit and harm bounds.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The proof uses Fr\'echet response-type algebra at zero budget and an explicit factual-cell split at high budget.

First set \(\kappa=0\) and take \(Q\in\mathfrak M_0(P)\). By \(\texttt{ass:entropy-budget}\), \(\operatorname{Ent}_Q(U)\leq0\). Every summand \(-Q(U=u)\log Q(U=u)\) is nonnegative, so the finite-alphabet entropy can vanish only when exactly one latent state has probability one. Hence \(U\) is \(Q\)-almost surely constant. The conditional independence in \(\texttt{ass:latent-exchangeability}\) therefore becomes
\[
A\perp (Y(0),Y(1)).
\]
Treatment positivity ensures that both \(\mu_0\) and \(\mu_1\) are defined. For each \(a\in\{0,1\}\), independence, consistency, and factual compatibility give
\[
Q\{Y(a)=1\}
=Q\{Y(a)=1\mid A=a\}
=Q(Y=1\mid A=a)
=\mu_a.
\]
Write \(x=Q\{Y(0)=1,Y(1)=1\}\). The four response-type probabilities must then be
\[
r_{11}=x,\qquad
r_{01}=\mu_1-x,\qquad
r_{10}=\mu_0-x,\qquad
r_{00}=1-\mu_0-\mu_1+x.
\]
Their nonnegativity is equivalent to
\[
\max\{0,\mu_0+\mu_1-1\}\leq x\leq\min\{\mu_0,\mu_1\}.
\]
Since \(B(Q)=r_{01}\) and \(H(Q)=r_{10}\), it follows that
\[
B(Q)-H(Q)=\mu_1-\mu_0,
\]
\[
0\leq B(Q)\leq\min\{\mu_1,1-\mu_0\},\qquad
0\leq H(Q)\leq\min\{\mu_0,1-\mu_1\}.
\]
By \(\texttt{def:zero-budget-segment}\), this proves \(\Theta_0(P)\subseteq\mathcal S_0(P)\).

Conversely, fix \((b,h)\in\mathcal S_0(P)\) and define a response-type law by
\[
r_{01}=b,\qquad r_{10}=h,\qquad
r_{11}=\mu_1-b=\mu_0-h,\qquad
r_{00}=1-\mu_0-b=1-\mu_1-h.
\]
The equalities follow from \(b-h=\mu_1-\mu_0\). The coordinate bounds in \(\texttt{def:zero-budget-segment}\) make every displayed probability nonnegative, and direct summation gives \(\sum_{i,j}r_{ij}=1\). Let \(U\) be constant, draw \((Y(0),Y(1))\) from this law, draw \(A\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p_1)\) independently of the response type, and set \(Y=Y(A)\). Then \(A\perp(Y(0),Y(1))\mid U\), \(\operatorname{Ent}_Q(U)=0\), and the two response margins are
\[
Q\{Y(0)=1\}=r_{10}+r_{11}=\mu_0,
\qquad
Q\{Y(1)=1\}=r_{01}+r_{11}=\mu_1.
\]
Independence and consistency therefore reproduce \(P(A=a,Y=y)=p_{ay}\) for all \(a,y\). This constructs a member of \(\mathfrak M_0(P)\) with \((B(Q),H(Q))=(b,h)\). Thus \(\mathcal S_0(P)\subseteq\Theta_0(P)\), and
\[
\Theta_0(P)=\mathcal S_0(P).
\]
This is sharp because every point of the segment has just been attained by a Fr\'echet coupling of the two Bernoulli margins.

We next prove the high-budget assertion. For any \(Q\in\mathfrak M_\kappa(P)\), split the benefit event according to treatment and use consistency and factual compatibility:
\[
\begin{aligned}
B(Q)
&=Q\{A=0,Y(0)=0,Y(1)=1\}
  +Q\{A=1,Y(0)=0,Y(1)=1\}\\
&\leq Q(A=0,Y=0)+Q(A=1,Y=1)\\
&=p_{00}+p_{11}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The same argument for harm gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
H(Q)
&=Q\{A=0,Y(0)=1,Y(1)=0\}
  +Q\{A=1,Y(0)=1,Y(1)=0\}\\
&\leq Q(A=0,Y=1)+Q(A=1,Y=0)\\
&=p_{01}+p_{10}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Because both coordinates are probabilities, \(\Theta_\kappa(P)\subseteq\mathcal R(P)\) for every \(\kappa\).

For the reverse inclusion, fix \((b,h)\in\mathcal R(P)\) and put
\[
b_0=\min\{b,p_{00}\},\qquad b_1=b-b_0,
\qquad
h_0=\min\{h,p_{01}\},\qquad h_1=h-h_0.
\]
The rectangle restrictions imply
\[
0\leq b_0\leq p_{00},\quad 0\leq b_1\leq p_{11},
\qquad
0\leq h_0\leq p_{01},\quad 0\leq h_1\leq p_{10}.
\]
Indeed, if \(b>p_{00}\), then \(b_1=b-p_{00}\leq p_{11}\), while otherwise \(b_1=0\); the argument for \(h_1\) is identical. Define the joint masses of \((A,Y(0),Y(1))\), with columns indexed by the response types \(00,01,10,11\), by
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
 &00&01&10&11\\ \hline
A=0&p_{00}-b_0&b_0&h_0&p_{01}-h_0\\
A=1&p_{10}-h_1&b_1&h_1&p_{11}-b_1.
\end{array}
\]
All entries are nonnegative, and their sum is \(\sum_{a,y}p_{ay}=1\). Set \(Y=Y(A)\) and \(U=A\). In the row \(A=0\), the masses of response types with \(Y(0)=0\) and \(Y(0)=1\) are respectively \(p_{00}\) and \(p_{01}\); in the row \(A=1\), the masses with \(Y(1)=0\) and \(Y(1)=1\) are respectively \(p_{10}\) and \(p_{11}\). Thus consistency produces exactly the factual law \(P\). Moreover,
\[
B(Q)=b_0+b_1=b,
\qquad
H(Q)=h_0+h_1=h.
\]
Conditional on \(U\), treatment is deterministic, so \(A\perp(Y(0),Y(1))\mid U\), including when some displayed masses vanish. Finally,
\[
\operatorname{Ent}_Q(U)=h\bigl(Q(A=1)\bigr)=h(p_1).
\]
Hence this law belongs to \(\mathfrak M_\kappa(P)\) whenever \(\kappa\geq h(p_1)\). It follows that \(\mathcal R(P)\subseteq\Theta_\kappa(P)\) in that regime. Combining the inclusions proves
\[
\Theta_\kappa(P)=\mathcal R(P)\qquad\text{for every }\kappa\geq h(p_1).
\]
The construction attains every pair in the rectangle simultaneously, so this is a sharp joint set and its coordinate projections are the sharp factual benefit and harm intervals.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} These reductions anchor the new sensitivity set to randomized Fréchet geometry and unrestricted probability-of-causation bounds. \textit{Gap.} TianPearl2000 gives sharp scalar probability-of-causation bounds, and Pena2023 studies separate benefit and harm bounds. Neither identifies the full joint rectangle as the high-common-entropy limit or the zero-budget segment of one sensitivity path. \textit{Consumer.} The endpoint formulas provide exact regression tests for implementations and interpretable anchors for sensitivity plots.

\begin{theorem}[thm:star-kernel]\label{thm:star-kernel}
Under treatment positivity \(0<p_1<1\), for every \(\theta\in\Theta_\kappa(P)\), every \(\theta_0\in\mathcal S_0(P)\), and every \(t\in[0,1]\), \[(1-t)\theta_0+t\theta\in\Theta_\kappa(P).\] A witness is obtained without adjoining a mixture indicator: retain the latent masses and propensities of a witness for \(\theta\), and replace each latent-specific response-type law by its common convex mixture with one no-confounding response law attaining \(\theta_0\). Therefore \(\mathcal S_0(P)\) lies in the star kernel of \(\Theta_\kappa(P)\), and \(\Theta_\kappa(P)\) is path connected.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The technique is statewise convex interpolation of response kernels.  It preserves the latent law, and hence the Shannon-entropy budget, rather than introducing an additional mixture indicator.  Assume treatment positivity \(0<p_1<1\), as declared in \(\texttt{ass:treatment-positivity}\).  Fix \(\theta\in\Theta_\kappa(P)\), \(\theta_0\in\mathcal S_0(P)\), and \(t\in[0,1]\).  By the constructive direction of \(\texttt{lem:factor-program-equivalence}\), there are a finite \(m\geq1\) and \((\pi,e,r)\in\mathcal F_{m,\kappa}(P)\) such that \(\Phi_m(\pi,e,r)=\theta\).  By the zero-budget equality and constructive Fréchet-coupling assertion in \(\texttt{thm:endpoint-geometry}\), there is a response-type mass function \(s=(s_{ij})_{i,j\in\{0,1\}}\in\Delta_4\), common across latent states, such that
\[
\sum_{j=0}^1s_{1j}=\mu_0,\qquad
\sum_{i=0}^1s_{i1}=\mu_1,\qquad
(s_{01},s_{10})=\theta_0.
\tag{1}
\]
Define, for every \(u\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\),
\[
r^{(t)}_{ij\mid u}=t r_{ij\mid u}+(1-t)s_{ij},
\qquad i,j\in\{0,1\}.
\tag{2}
\]
Since \(t\in[0,1]\) and both terms in (2) are probability mass functions, convexity of \(\Delta_4\) gives \(r^{(t)}_{\cdot\cdot\mid u}\in\Delta_4\).

We next verify every factual equality in \(\texttt{def:finite-factor-program}\).  Summing the original equations for \(a=1\) over \(y\) and using \(\sum_{i,j}r_{ij\mid u}=1\) gives
\[
\sum_{u=1}^m\pi_u e_u=\sum_{y=0}^1p_{1y}=p_1.
\tag{3}
\]
The corresponding calculation for \(a=0\) gives
\[
\sum_{u=1}^m\pi_u(1-e_u)=1-p_1.
\tag{4}
\]
For \(a,y\in\{0,1\}\), set
\[
s_{a,y}=\sum_{i,j=0}^1s_{ij}\mathbf 1\{(1-a)i+aj=y\}.
\tag{5}
\]
Equations (1) and the complementary binary margins imply
\[
s_{a,y}=P(Y=y\mid A=a).
\tag{6}
\]
The divisions implicit in (6) are legitimate because \(0<p_1<1\).  Combining (2)--(6), for every \(a,y\), yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\sum_{u=1}^m\pi_u e_u^a(1-e_u)^{1-a}
 \sum_{i,j=0}^1r^{(t)}_{ij\mid u}\mathbf 1\{(1-a)i+aj=y\}\\
&\quad=t p_{ay}+(1-t)s_{a,y}
 \sum_{u=1}^m\pi_u e_u^a(1-e_u)^{1-a}\\
&\quad=t p_{ay}+(1-t)P(Y=y\mid A=a)P(A=a)\\
&\quad=p_{ay}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
Thus the interpolated tuple satisfies the factual constraints.  Its latent masses remain \(\pi\), so the entropy is unchanged:
\[
-\sum_{u=1}^m\pi_u\log\pi_u\leq\kappa.
\tag{8}
\]
Equations (2), (7), and (8) prove \((\pi,e,r^{(t)})\in\mathcal F_{m,\kappa}(P)\).

Finally, linearity of the benefit--harm projection and \(\sum_u\pi_u=1\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Phi_m(\pi,e,r^{(t)})
&=t\Phi_m(\pi,e,r)
 +(1-t)\left(\sum_u\pi_us_{01},\sum_u\pi_us_{10}\right)\\
&=t\theta+(1-t)\theta_0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
The reverse constructive direction of \(\texttt{lem:factor-program-equivalence}\) turns (9) into a law in \(\mathfrak M_\kappa(P)\); hence \((1-t)\theta_0+t\theta\in\Theta_\kappa(P)\).  Thus every point of \(\mathcal S_0(P)\) is a star center.  By \(\texttt{thm:endpoint-geometry}\), \(\mathcal S_0(P)=\Theta_0(P)\) is nonempty.  Every zero-budget witness is also feasible at budget \(\kappa\), because \(0\leq\kappa\); therefore \(\mathcal S_0(P)\subseteq\Theta_\kappa(P)\).  Fixing one \(\theta_0\in\mathcal S_0(P)\), the displayed segments join every pair of points in \(\Theta_\kappa(P)\) through \(\theta_0\); their concatenation is a continuous path.  Therefore \(\Theta_\kappa(P)\) is path connected.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Under positive treatment arms, the theorem supplies global topology despite the nonconvex entropy sublevel set and avoids the entropy cost of an external mixture indicator. \textit{Gap.} Neither the scalar programs of JiangWeiKocaoglu2023 nor the coordinatewise bounds of Pena2023 address joint-set topology. The proof instead interpolates response kernels while holding the entropy-bearing latent law fixed. \textit{Consumer.} On the positive-arm domain, the star kernel gives BH-CERT an inner-set generator and guarantees that the sensitivity region cannot split into disconnected islands.

\begin{proposition}[prop:three-state-sharpness]\label{prop:three-state-sharpness}
For the balanced reverse-channel witness \(P^\dagger\), benefit--harm point \(\theta^\dagger\), and entropy budget \(\kappa^\dagger\), one has \(0<\kappa^\dagger<\log2\) and \[\theta^\dagger\in\Phi_3(\mathcal F_{3,\kappa^\dagger}(P^\dagger))\setminus\Phi_2(\mathcal F_{2,\kappa^\dagger}(P^\dagger)).\] Consequently, \[\Phi_2(\mathcal F_{2,\kappa^\dagger}(P^\dagger))\subsetneq\Phi_3(\mathcal F_{3,\kappa^\dagger}(P^\dagger)),\] so three latent states are sometimes necessary for the full benefit--harm image.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let \(R=(Y(0),Y(1))\).  We first verify the entropy range.  By the definition of \(h\) and the definition of \(\kappa^\dagger\),
\[
10\kappa^\dagger
=9\log\frac{10}{9}+\log 20
=\log\!\left(20\left(\frac{10}{9}\right)^9\right).
\]
Both terms defining \(\kappa^\dagger\) are nonnegative and \(h(9/10)>0\), so \(\kappa^\dagger>0\).  Moreover,
\[
20\cdot 10^9=20{,}000{,}000{,}000
<396{,}718{,}580{,}736=2^{10}9^9.
\]
Strict monotonicity of the logarithm therefore gives \(10\kappa^\dagger<10\log 2\), and hence
\[
0<\kappa^\dagger<\log 2.
\tag{1}
\]

We next give a constructive feasible three-state factorization.  Label the states \(00,10,11\), and set
\[
(\pi_{00},\pi_{10},\pi_{11})
=\left(\frac1{20},\frac9{10},\frac1{20}\right),
\qquad
(e_{00},e_{10},e_{11})=\left(0,\frac12,1\right).
\tag{2}
\]
At state \(ij\), let \(r_{ij\mid ij}=1\) and let every other response-type probability be zero.  Thus each \(r_{\cdot\cdot\mid u}\) belongs to \(\Delta_4\), \(\pi\in\Delta_3\), and \(e\in[0,1]^3\).  Substitution into the factual constraints in \(\mathcal F_{3,\kappa^\dagger}(P^\dagger)\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_u\pi_u(1-e_u)\sum_{i,j}r_{ij\mid u}\mathbf 1\{i=0\}
&=\frac1{20}=p^\dagger_{00},\\
\sum_u\pi_u(1-e_u)\sum_{i,j}r_{ij\mid u}\mathbf 1\{i=1\}
&=\frac9{20}=p^\dagger_{01},\\
\sum_u\pi_u e_u\sum_{i,j}r_{ij\mid u}\mathbf 1\{j=0\}
&=\frac9{20}=p^\dagger_{10},\\
\sum_u\pi_u e_u\sum_{i,j}r_{ij\mid u}\mathbf 1\{j=1\}
&=\frac1{20}=p^\dagger_{11}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
The entropy of the masses in (2) is
\[
-2\frac1{20}\log\frac1{20}-\frac9{10}\log\frac9{10}
=h\!\left(\frac9{10}\right)+\frac1{10}\log2
=\kappa^\dagger.
\tag{4}
\]
Finally, the definition of \(\Phi_3\) and (2) give
\[
\Phi_3(\pi,e,r)
=\left(\sum_u\pi_u r_{01\mid u},\sum_u\pi_u r_{10\mid u}\right)
=\left(0,\frac9{10}\right)
=\theta^\dagger.
\tag{5}
\]
Equations (2)--(5) prove
\[
\theta^\dagger\in\Phi_3\bigl(\mathcal F_{3,\kappa^\dagger}(P^\dagger)\bigr).
\tag{6}
\]
Equivalently, the constructive direction of \(\texttt{lem:factor-program-equivalence}\) turns this tuple into a factual-compatible law satisfying consistency, latent exchangeability, and the entropy budget.

It remains to rule out two positive latent states.  Consider any factual-compatible law having \((B,H)=\theta^\dagger\), and write \(J\) for its joint law of \((A,R)\).  Since response-type probabilities are nonnegative, the definitions of \(B\) and \(H\) imply
\[
J(R=01)=0,
\qquad
J(R=10)=\frac9{10},
\qquad
J(R=00)+J(R=11)=\frac1{10}.
\tag{7}
\]
By consistency, when \(A=0\) the observed outcome is the first component of \(R\), and when \(A=1\) it is the second component.  Factual compatibility with \(P^\dagger\), together with \(J(R=01)=0\), therefore yields
\[
J(A=0,R=00)=p^\dagger_{00}=\frac1{20},
\qquad
J(A=1,R=11)=p^\dagger_{11}=\frac1{20}.
\tag{8}
\]
The two displayed masses exhaust the total diagonal-type mass \(1/10\) in (7).  Nonnegativity consequently forces
\[
J(A=1,R=00)=J(A=0,R=11)=0.
\tag{9}
\]
Applying factual compatibility to the other two observed cells and using (9) gives
\[
J(A=0,R=10)=p^\dagger_{01}=\frac9{20},
\qquad
J(A=1,R=10)=p^\dagger_{10}=\frac9{20}.
\tag{10}
\]
Thus (7)--(10) force the complete joint table
\[
J(0,00)=\frac1{20},\quad
J(0,10)=\frac9{20},\quad
J(1,10)=\frac9{20},\quad
J(1,11)=\frac1{20},
\tag{11}
\]
with every other cell equal to zero.

Suppose for contradiction that a tuple in \(\mathcal F_{2,\kappa^\dagger}(P^\dagger)\) has image \(\theta^\dagger\).  By the constructive equivalence in \(\texttt{lem:factor-program-equivalence}\), its positive-mass latent states give a product-mixture representation
\[
J(a,r)=\sum_w\lambda_w e_w^a(1-e_w)^{1-a}s_w(r),
\tag{12}
\]
where \(\lambda_w>0\) on the retained support, \(e_w\in[0,1]\), \(s_w\in\Delta_4\), and there are at most two retained states.  Positivity of \(J(0,00)\) in (11) provides a state \(w_0\) for which \(\lambda_{w_0}s_{w_0}(00)>0\) and \(1-e_{w_0}>0\).  But
\[
0=J(1,00)=\sum_w\lambda_w e_ws_w(00),
\]
and all summands are nonnegative, so \(e_{w_0}=0\).  Similarly, positivity of \(J(1,11)\) provides a state \(w_1\) with \(\lambda_{w_1}s_{w_1}(11)>0\), whereas
\[
0=J(0,11)=\sum_w\lambda_w(1-e_w)s_w(11)
\]
forces \(e_{w_1}=1\).  Hence \(w_0\ne w_1\).  Because (12) has at most two positive-mass states, these are all of them, and the latent state determines \(A\).  The marginal treatment law under \(P^\dagger\) is balanced, since
\[
P^\dagger(A=0)=p^\dagger_{00}+p^\dagger_{01}=\frac12,
\qquad
P^\dagger(A=1)=p^\dagger_{10}+p^\dagger_{11}=\frac12.
\]
It follows that \(\lambda_{w_0}=\lambda_{w_1}=1/2\), so the entropy of the factor in (12) is
\[
-\frac12\log\frac12-\frac12\log\frac12=\log2>\kappa^\dagger
\tag{13}
\]
by (1).  This contradicts the entropy constraint in \(\mathcal F_{2,\kappa^\dagger}(P^\dagger)\).  Therefore
\[
\theta^\dagger\notin\Phi_2\bigl(\mathcal F_{2,\kappa^\dagger}(P^\dagger)\bigr).
\tag{14}
\]
This zero-pattern product-mixture argument is the response-type embedding of the nonrepresentability technique in \citet[Section V, Example 2 and Appendix A]{KumarLiElGamal2014}; all properties needed here have been derived directly in (7)--(13).

Finally, any element \((\pi,e,r)\in\mathcal F_{2,\kappa^\dagger}(P^\dagger)\) can be padded by a third state with mass zero and arbitrary admissible propensity and response distribution.  The convention \(0\log0=0\) shows that padding preserves entropy, and the zero mass shows that it preserves every factual constraint and \(\Phi_m\).  Hence
\[
\Phi_2\bigl(\mathcal F_{2,\kappa^\dagger}(P^\dagger)\bigr)
\subseteq
\Phi_3\bigl(\mathcal F_{3,\kappa^\dagger}(P^\dagger)\bigr).
\]
Together with (6) and (14), this inclusion is strict, proving every assertion of the proposition.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The explicit balanced reverse-channel witness certifies that the full image's support-three bound is sharp. \textit{Gap.} The information-theoretic zero-pattern obstruction is classical; the proposition supplies the exact causal response-type embedding, factual table, benefit--harm coordinates, and entropy comparison. \textit{Consumer.} It completes the support-cardinality elbow and prevents the origin's two-state reduction from being overgeneralized to the entire joint set.

\begin{proposition}[prop:whole-set-confidence-coverage]\label{prop:whole-set-confidence-coverage}
Suppose \(N\geq1\) and \(C_N\) is the multinomial count vector from \(N\) independent draws from \(P\). For every \(\alpha\in(0,1)\) and every fixed \(\kappa\in[0,\log2]\), \[\Pr_P\left\{\Theta_\kappa(P)\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal T}_{N,\alpha,\kappa}\right\}\geq1-\alpha.\] This is a whole-set outer-coverage guarantee obtained by exact multinomial inversion and deterministic image containment.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The technique is exact inversion on a finite sample space followed by deterministic image containment.  Fix \(N\geq1\), \(\alpha\in(0,1)\), \(\kappa\in[0,\log2]\), and let \(p=(p_{ay})_{a,y\in\{0,1\}}\) be the true cell vector of \(P\).  The multinomial sample space
\[
\mathscr X_N=\left\{c\in\mathbb N^4:\sum_{a,y}c_{ay}=N\right\}
\tag{1}
\]
is finite.  Under \(P\), the probability of \(C_N=c\) is exactly \(L_p(c)\), including boundary cell vectors because the convention \(0^0=1\) is used.  Define
\[
\psi_p(s)=\sum_{c\in\mathscr X_N:L_p(c)\geq s}L_p(c),
\qquad
S_p=\{s\in[0,1]:\psi_p(s)\geq1-\alpha\}.
\tag{2}
\]
Since \(\psi_p(0)=1\), the set \(S_p\) is nonempty.  Since \(\mathscr X_N\) is finite, \(\psi_p\) changes only at the finitely many values \(\{L_p(c):c\in\mathscr X_N\}\).  It follows directly that \(S_p\) is a closed initial interval and its supremum is attained.  Thus the threshold in \(\texttt{def:exact-multinomial-region}\) satisfies
\[
u(p,\alpha)=\max S_p,\qquad
\psi_p(u(p,\alpha))\geq1-\alpha.
\tag{3}
\]
By that definition and (3),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr_P\{p\in\mathcal C_{N,\alpha}(C_N)\}
&=\Pr_P\{L_p(C_N)\geq u(p,\alpha)\}\\
&=\sum_{c\in\mathscr X_N:L_p(c)\geq u(p,\alpha)}L_p(c)\\
&=\psi_p(u(p,\alpha))\geq1-\alpha.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
On the event in (4), the candidate \(q=p\) occurs in the union defining \(\widehat{\mathcal T}_{N,\alpha,\kappa}\).  The induced factual law \(P_p\) equals \(P\) cell by cell.  Hence, deterministically on this event,
\[
\Theta_\kappa(P)=\Theta_\kappa(P_p)
\subseteq\bigcup_{q\in\mathcal C_{N,\alpha}(C_N)}\Theta_\kappa(P_q)
=\widehat{\mathcal T}_{N,\alpha,\kappa}.
\tag{5}
\]
Therefore the event in (4) is contained in the whole-set coverage event.  Taking probabilities in (5) and applying (4) proves
\[
\Pr_P\left\{\Theta_\kappa(P)\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal T}_{N,\alpha,\kappa}\right\}\geq1-\alpha.
\]
This is an outer whole-set coverage statement; it does not assert optimality, small area, or equality of the random envelope with the identified set.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The proposition supplies the finite-sample conclusion required to turn the confidence-envelope definition into an auditable coverage statement. \textit{Gap.} ChafaiConcordet2009 informs the likelihood-ordered exact multinomial region; the contribution here is only the deterministic mapping of that covered factual law into the entire entropy-indexed identified set. \textit{Consumer.} Users obtain conservative finite-sample coverage of the full benefit--harm set without relying on the unresolved contact asymptotics.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The support-three equality uses a published finite-alphabet common-entropy compression for the fixed joint law of treatment and the four-valued response type, but its causal glue is proved here by showing that preservation of that joint law preserves every factual cell and both response-type coordinates. The result makes the feasible image compact even at zero masses because \(-x\log x\) extends continuously to zero. Compactness yields non-effective Hausdorff approximations and positive separation margins, not a rank-free terminating certification algorithm with exact singular witnesses. For inference, the boundary perturbation includes \(\dot\theta_c(P;Z)+\dot\rho_{P,\kappa}(v;Z)v\); the derivative of the full radial reconstruction, the contact normalization, and uniform resampling remain unproved.

\section{Honest scope}

The three-state and origin theorems are global over binary factual laws. Endpoint and star-kernel geometry are asserted only under treatment positivity. The balanced example shows insufficiency of a necessary observed mutual-information relaxation and makes no claim about a published estimator or sufficient origin test. The whole-set confidence envelope has finite-sample outer coverage by exact multinomial inversion and deterministic set mapping, but no claim of optimal area or novel confidence-region ordering is made. BH-CERT termination at zero weights and active entropy equality remains open, as do the exact regular, active-entropy, and support-contact Hausdorff laws and a uniformly valid resampling rule.
