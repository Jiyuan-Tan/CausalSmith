%%% FIELD thm-simultaneous-bands-statement
Under Assumptions \(\ref{ass:iid-sampling}\), \(\ref{ass:holder-regressions}\), \(\ref{ass:design-density}\), \(\ref{ass:characteristic-kernels}\), and \(\ref{ass:residual-kernel-lipschitz}\), put \(t_n=\log(M_d/\alpha)/n\). There are finite constants \(K_R,K_H\), depending only on the fixed regularity constants, such that, with probability at least \(1-\alpha\), all \(d2^{d-1}\) risks and all score-relevant and pruning residual-HSIC values lie in their cross-fitted bands and
\[
w_R\le K_R\{t_n^{1/2}+t_n^{6/(d+5)}\},
\qquad
w_H\le K_H\{t_n^{1/2}+t_n^{3/(d+5)}\}.
\]
On this simultaneous event, whenever the propagated dependence labels are singleton and correct and \(w_R<\underline v/2\),
\[
w_S\le \frac{4d}{\underline v}w_R.
\]
If \((G_0,f,p_N,P)\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{pair}}(d)\) and every queried HSIC value belongs to \(\{0\}\cup[\lambda,\infty)\), then, without requiring either width inequality,
\[
\Pr_P\{G_0\in\widehat{\mathcal G}_n\}\ge 1-\alpha.
\]
On the simultaneous event, the additional inequalities \(2w_S<\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}\) and \(2w_H<\lambda\) imply \(\widehat{\mathcal G}_n=\{G_0\}\). More directly, for constants \(K_1,K_2<\infty\) depending only on the fixed regularity constants, the inequalities
\[
K_1d\{t_n^{1/2}+t_n^{6/(d+5)}\}<\delta_{\mathrm{pair}},
\qquad
K_2\{t_n^{1/2}+t_n^{3/(d+5)}\}<\lambda
\]
are sufficient for singleton recovery. For fixed regularity constants and \(0<\delta_{\mathrm{pair}},\lambda\le1\), it is sufficient that
\[
n\ge K\log(M_d/\alpha)
\max\left\{
\left(\frac d{\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}}\right)^{\max\{2,(d+5)/6\}},
\lambda^{-(d+5)/3}
\right\}.
\]
The score-margin exponent is two for \(d\le7\), the two powers coincide at \(d=7\), and \((d+5)/6\) governs for \(d>7\), giving an elbow at \(d=7\) without a minimax claim. Without the two separation inequalities, interval propagation never forces a binary label for an HSIC interval meeting both \(0\) and \([\lambda,\infty)\): it retains both labels and returns the union of all compatible terminal DAGs, which may be a singleton or may contain multiple graphs.

Put \(t_n=\log(M_d/\alpha)/n\), and let \(\underline v\) be the constant in Assumption \(\ref{ass:variance-nondegeneracy}\). Compactness of \(\mathcal X\) supplies a finite bound \(B\) for every coordinate of \(V\) and every clipped residual used below. Constants denoted by \(K\), \(K_R\), or \(K_H\) may depend on \(B\), the fixed fold proportions, the Holder radius, the design-density bounds, the kernel bounds, and \(L_k\), but not on \(n\), \(\alpha\), or the indices \((v,A,u)\).

Fix \(v\in I\), \(A\subseteq I\setminus\{v\}\), and put \(m=|A|\). Partition \(\mathcal X_A\) into rectangles of side at most \(h\), use polynomials of total degree at most two on each nonempty rectangle, and clip their values to the coordinate range of \(V_v\). Taylor's formula and Assumption \(\ref{ass:holder-regressions}\) give a sieve element \(g_h\) satisfying
\[
\lVert g_h-\mu_{v,A}\rVert_\infty\le Kh^3. \tag{1}
\]
There are at most \(Kh^{-m}\) polynomial coefficients. We record the required bounded least-squares inequality. The empirical least-squares basic inequality uses \(g_h\) as comparison element. For every clipped competitor, the variance of its squared-loss difference from the loss of the conditional mean is at most a fixed constant times its excess squared-error risk. Exponential Markov's inequality on each finite-dimensional cell, followed by peeling over dyadic excess-risk shells, therefore gives, for a training fold of size proportional to \(n\),
\[
\Pr\!\left\{
\lVert\widehat\mu_{v,A}-\mu_{v,A}\rVert_{L^2(P_A)}^2
>K\left(h^6+\frac{h^{-m}+\log(1/\delta)}{n}\right)
\right\}\le\delta. \tag{2}
\]
Indeed, on a shell with excess risk \(r\), the exponential bound is at most \(\exp\{-cnr+Kh^{-m}\}\); summing the shells is finite once \(r\ge K\{h^{-m}+\log(1/\delta)\}/n\). Thus (2) uses only boundedness, (1), and the independent training sample supplied by Assumption \(\ref{ass:iid-sampling}\).

For \(t_n\le1\), take \(h=t_n^{1/(m+6)}\) and allocate failure probability proportional to \(\alpha/M_d\). Since \(m\le d-1\),
\[
t_n^{3/(m+6)}\le t_n^{3/(d+5)}=a_n.
\]
A union bound over the \(d2^{d-1}\) regressions and the fixed number of folds yields
\[
\max_{v,A}\lVert\widehat\mu_{v,A}-\mu_{v,A}\rVert_{L^2(P_A)}
\le Ka_n. \tag{3}
\]
When \(t_n>1\), clipping and enlargement of \(K\) give the same inequality because the response and the fitted regressions are bounded.

On an evaluation fold independent of the fitted regression, let \(\widehat R_{v,A}\) be the empirical mean of the squared fitted residual. By the defining orthogonality of conditional expectation in Definition \(\ref{def:conditional-risk}\), conditionally on the training fold,
\[
\mathbb E_P\!\left[(V_v-\widehat\mu_{v,A}(V_A))^2\mid\widehat\mu_{v,A}\right]
=R_{v,A}+\lVert\widehat\mu_{v,A}-\mu_{v,A}\rVert_{L^2(P_A)}^2. \tag{4}
\]
The held-out losses are conditionally independent and bounded. If \(W\) is centered and \(|W|\le b\), convexity of the exponential on \([-b,b]\) gives \(\mathbb E e^{sW}\le e^{s^2b^2/2}\); exponential Markov optimization and a union bound thus bound all held-out empirical fluctuations by \(K\sqrt{t_n}\). Combining this bound with (3)--(4) constructs intervals containing every \(R_{v,A}\) whose largest half-width satisfies
\[
w_R\le K_R\{t_n^{1/2}+a_n^2\}
=K_R\{t_n^{1/2}+t_n^{6/(d+5)}\}. \tag{5}
\]

For an HSIC coordinate, use the bounded centered order-four V-statistic on an evaluation fold and the off-fold residuals. Conditional on the training fold, changing one evaluation observation changes the statistic by at most \(K/n\). Exposing the observations successively and applying the preceding exponential argument to the resulting bounded martingale differences gives fluctuation at most \(K\sqrt{t_n}\); repeated-index terms contribute \(K/n\), which is absorbed by this bound. To compare fitted-residual and true-residual population HSIC, expand HSIC as its fixed finite sum of expectations of products of bounded kernel factors. Telescope every product at its residual-kernel factor and invoke Assumption \(\ref{ass:residual-kernel-lipschitz}\). Cauchy--Schwarz and (3) give
\[
\left|H_{\widehat\mu}(v,A,u)-H(v,A,u)\right|
\le K\mathbb E_P\left|\widehat\mu_{v,A}(V_A)-\mu_{v,A}(V_A)\right|
\le Ka_n. \tag{6}
\]
Hence the intervals contain all \(d(d-1)2^{d-2}\) score-relevant residual-HSIC values and obey
\[
w_H\le K_H\{t_n^{1/2}+a_n\}
=K_H\{t_n^{1/2}+t_n^{3/(d+5)}\}. \tag{7}
\]
There are at most another \(d(d-1)2^{d-2}\) pruning coordinates \(H(v,A,u)\) with \(u\notin A\). The same union bound therefore covers every possibly adaptive pruning query. Replacing \(M_d\) by at most \(2M_d\) changes only the constants because \(M_d/\alpha\ge2\) and \(\log(2M_d/\alpha)\le2\log(M_d/\alpha)\). Allocating the failure probabilities to sum to at most \(\alpha\) proves the simultaneous coverage event, denoted below by \(E_n\), and (5)--(7).

We next propagate the risk intervals into score intervals. Work on \(E_n\), and suppose first that all dependence labels used in a gain are singleton and correct. Assume \(w_R<\underline v/2\). Set
\[
y_v=R_{v,\varnothing}=\operatorname{Var}_P(V_v),
\qquad x_{v,A}=R_{v,A}.
\]
Conditional expectation is the \(L^2(P)\) projection onto functions of \(V_A\), so Definition \(\ref{def:conditional-risk}\) and Assumption \(\ref{ass:variance-nondegeneracy}\) imply
\[
0\le x_{v,A}\le y_v,
\qquad y_v\ge\underline v. \tag{8}
\]
Let \(\widehat y_v\) and \(\widehat x_{v,A}\) be risk-band centers, and project \(\widehat x_{v,A}\) onto \([0,\widehat y_v]\), calling the result \(\widetilde x_{v,A}\). Coverage and \(w_R<\underline v/2\) imply \(\widehat y_v>0\). Since \(x_{v,A}\in[0,y_v]\), this projection and displacement of its upper endpoint give
\[
|\widetilde x_{v,A}-x_{v,A}|\le w_R,
\qquad |\widehat y_v-y_v|\le w_R. \tag{9}
\]
Define \(\widetilde q(v,A)=1-\widetilde x_{v,A}/\widehat y_v\). Equations (8)--(9) and \(0\le\widetilde x_{v,A}/\widehat y_v\le1\) yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
|\widetilde q(v,A)-q(v,A)|
&=\left|\frac{x_{v,A}}{y_v}-\frac{\widetilde x_{v,A}}{\widehat y_v}\right|\\
&\le\frac{|x_{v,A}-\widetilde x_{v,A}|}{y_v}
+\frac{\widetilde x_{v,A}}{\widehat y_v}
  \frac{|\widehat y_v-y_v|}{y_v}
\le\frac{2w_R}{\underline v}. \tag{10}
\end{aligned}
\]
With singleton labels, the multiplier \(1-D(v,A)/(|A|+1)\) in Definition \(\ref{def:gene-score}\) is fixed and lies in \([0,1]\). An insertion changes predecessor sets only for the moved node and nodes in its traversed block, hence for at most \(d\) nodes. Its gain is the difference of the before and after local-score sums over precisely those nodes. Applying (10) to the two sums proves
\[
w_S\le\frac{4d}{\underline v}w_R. \tag{11}
\]
This step does not use continuity of the independence indicator: it applies only after the labels have been fixed.

We now prove the confidence-set assertion, which does not require small widths. Suppose \((G_0,f,p_N,P)\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{pair}}(d)\) and every queried residual-HSIC value lies in \(\{0\}\cup[\lambda,\infty)\). On \(E_n\), each true risk and HSIC coordinate lies in its interval. Its intersection with \(\{0\}\cup[\lambda,\infty)\) therefore contains the true HSIC value and, consequently, Definition \(\ref{def:simultaneous-bands}\) retains the true independence or dependence label. Thus the exact population score table, every exact best-improvement choice, and every exact pruning choice form a compatible execution in the interval-propagation construction. Theorem \(\ref{thm:oracle-recovery}\) says that this population execution terminates at \(G_0\), from every initial order. Hence
\[
E_n\subseteq\{G_0\in\widehat{\mathcal G}_n\}.
\]
Since \(\Pr_P(E_n)\ge1-\alpha\),
\[
\Pr_P\{G_0\in\widehat{\mathcal G}_n\}\ge1-\alpha. \tag{12}
\]
No inequality involving \(w_R\), \(w_H\), or \(w_S\) was used in this inclusion.

For singleton recovery, continue on \(E_n\) and assume \(2w_H<\lambda\). If \(H=0\), a covering interval centered within its half-width of zero has upper endpoint at most \(2w_H<\lambda\); if \(H\ge\lambda\), its lower endpoint is at least \(H-2w_H>0\). Thus Definition \(\ref{def:simultaneous-bands}\) returns the correct singleton label for every queried dependence indicator. If also \(2w_S<\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}\), Theorem \(\ref{thm:pairwise-navigation}\) supplies at every non-topological order a correcting insertion of true gain at least \(\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}\), whose entire propagated interval is positive. Strict ascent cannot revisit an order in the finite set \(\mathfrak S_I\), so every compatible terminal order is topological. The singleton pruning labels then equal the population labels in Theorem \(\ref{thm:oracle-recovery}\), which gives \(G_0\) for every compatible terminal path. Therefore
\[
\widehat{\mathcal G}_n=\{G_0\}. \tag{13}
\]

It remains to express conditions (11) and (13) directly in \(n\). Choose constants
\[
K_1\ge\frac{8K_R}{\underline v},
\qquad K_2\ge2K_H, \tag{14}
\]
and enlarge \(K_1\), if needed, by a fixed regularity-dependent factor. Because \(d\ge2\) and \(0<\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}\le1\), the inequalities
\[
K_1d\{t_n^{1/2}+t_n^{6/(d+5)}\}<\delta_{\mathrm{pair}},
\qquad
K_2\{t_n^{1/2}+t_n^{3/(d+5)}\}<\lambda \tag{15}
\]
imply, respectively, \(w_R<\underline v/2\) together with \(2w_S<\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}\), and \(2w_H<\lambda\). Equations (11) and (13) then prove that (15) is sufficient for singleton recovery.

For fixed regularity constants and \(0<\delta_{\mathrm{pair}},\lambda\le1\), set
\[
\gamma_d=\max\left\{2,\frac{d+5}{6}\right\},
\qquad \beta_d=\frac{d+5}{3}.
\]
Because \(\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}/d\le1\), taking \(t_n\) at most a sufficiently small fixed multiple of \((\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}/d)^{\gamma_d}\) controls both terms in the first inequality of (15). Since \(\beta_d>2\) for \(d\ge2\), taking \(t_n\) at most a sufficiently small fixed multiple of \(\lambda^{\beta_d}\) controls both terms in the second. Consequently it suffices that
\[
n\ge K\log(M_d/\alpha)
\max\left\{
\left(\frac d{\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}}\right)^{\max\{2,(d+5)/6\}},
\lambda^{-(d+5)/3}
\right\}. \tag{16}
\]
The first exponent is two for \(d\le7\), equals \((d+5)/6\) at \(d=7\), and is \((d+5)/6\) for \(d>7\). This is the asserted score-rate elbow, and (16) is only a sufficient bound.

Without the two separation inequalities, interval propagation never forces a binary label for an HSIC interval meeting both \(0\) and \([\lambda,\infty)\): it retains both labels and returns the union of all compatible terminal DAGs, which may be a singleton or may contain multiple graphs. This is precisely the set-valued rule in Definition \(\ref{def:simultaneous-bands}\), and completes the proof.
