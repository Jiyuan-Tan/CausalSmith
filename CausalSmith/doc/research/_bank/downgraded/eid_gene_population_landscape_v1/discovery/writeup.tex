\section*{eid\_\allowbreak{}gene\_\allowbreak{}population\_\allowbreak{}landscape}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} For the exact \(\alpha=1\) population-oracle analogue of GENE Equation (6), a primitive edgewise inequality on support-closed matching-component additive-noise models forces a positive reversal-correcting insertion at every wrong order, so best improvement and independence pruning recover the labelled DAG with a finite oracle-query budget. This is a certified success region, not a theorem for Li et al.'s full nonlinear class. For every \(\eta\in(0,1/2)\), an explicitly parameterized relatively open bivariate family, including a nonempty relatively open non-affine subfamily, makes the identifiable causal model \(X\to Y\) have the wrong order as its unique global and hence insertion-local maximizer with score advantage strictly greater than \(\eta\); the universal sharp supremum is \(1/2\). Cross-fitted simultaneous bands abstain at unresolved independence labels. The generic ambient-dimensional branch retains its sufficient sample size with the score-rate elbow at \(d=7\). On \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{pair}}(d)\), a pairing-agnostic union of constant and univariate fits exploits structural regression dimension one, giving \(a_{n,\mathrm{pair}}=t_n^{3/7}\), \(w_R\lesssim t_n^{1/2}+t_n^{6/7}\), \(w_H\lesssim t_n^{1/2}+t_n^{3/7}\), and the sufficient singleton order \(n\gtrsim\log(M_d/\alpha)\max\{(d/\delta_{\mathrm{pair}})^2,\lambda^{-7/3}\}\), with no dimensional elbow and no minimax claim. HSIC separation only on the exact population execution suffices for \(1-\alpha\) coverage of \(G_0\) without width conditions; singleton recovery additionally needs all-compatible-query separation and the score and HSIC width inequalities.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Li et al. (2025, Equation (6)) use finite-sample neural-network fits and residual-independence p-values, while their Theorem 4.3 assumes a global score ranking across orders with different ancestral-reversal counts. Peters et al. (2014) identify restricted additive-noise models through Condition 19 configurations, but observational identifiability alone supplies neither local ascent nor protection against a wrong population-score optimum. Existing work also does not provide simultaneous learned-risk and residual-HSIC uncertainty propagation for adaptive GENE search.

\paragraph{Niche.} The useful boundary has three auditable pieces: a structural success certificate with finite population-query complexity, a genuinely open set of identifiable mechanisms on which the exact population score fails as sharply as its range permits, and finite-sample confidence logic that returns a singleton only when navigation and pruning margins are resolved.

\paragraph{Fill.} On the matching-component subclass, the forward-versus-reverse edge signal inequality gives a strict correcting-gain bound and labelled-DAG oracle recovery. A six-node nonlinear witness separates this certificate from Li et al.'s global ranking premise, while a rare-component bivariate construction and its non-affine perturbations produce, for every \(\eta<1/2\), an open trapped-order family with advantage greater than \(\eta\). Simultaneous bands retain a generic ambient-dimensional branch but also adapt, without knowing the pairing, over constant and univariate regressions. The matching-adaptive branch yields \(w_S\le4d w_R/\underline v\), the sufficient singleton order \(n\gtrsim\log(M_d/\alpha)\max\{(d/\delta_{\mathrm{pair}})^2,\lambda^{-7/3}\}\), and set-valued abstention near independence.

\section{Related work}

Li et al. (2025, Equation (6), Definition 4.1, and Theorem 4.3 with its proof on page 5) define the released finite-sample score using neural-network prediction and residual-independence test p-values and prove greedy recovery under a global reversal-monotone fitness premise. The present score is its exact \(\alpha=1\) population-oracle analogue. The matching-class navigation theorem is therefore not transferred to their full nonlinear class. The included nonlinear separation and trapped-order witnesses are nevertheless members of that broader class and hence transfer only as existential examples; the trap violates the premise needed by Theorem 4.3 and does not refute that conditional theorem. Peters et al. (2014, Condition 19, Definition 27, and Theorem 28 on pages 13--16) prove identification under their positive-density restricted-ANM configuration requirement. Their Proposition 17 gives a pointwise nonconstancy characterization of causal minimality, whereas the compact-support model here uses positive-parent-law-measure nonconstancy and a separate relative-interior identification bridge. Reisach et al. (2023, Sections 3 and 5) analyze scale-normalized explained variance, informing the primitive ratios but not the GENE insertion landscape. Solus, Wang, and Uhler (2021, Proposition 8 and Theorem 9) study covered-arrow walks for a sparsity score rather than GENE insertion ascent. Pfister et al. (2018, Theorem E.2 and Proposition E.3) and Shekhar, Kim, and Ramdas (2023, Theorems 6 and 7) provide kernel-independence and sample-splitting ingredients but not simultaneous adaptive graph sets with learned normalized risks. Buhlmann, Peters, and Ernest (2014, Theorems 1 and 2) analyze likelihood order estimation and pruning under a different wrong-order variance margin.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:anm-independence]\label{ass:anm-independence}
The coordinates \(N_j\), \(j\in I\), are mutually independent.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (additive-noise exogeneity, \cite{peters14a}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:smooth-mechanisms]\label{ass:smooth-mechanisms}
Each \(f_j\) is continuous on its compact parent domain and three times continuously differentiable on its relative interior.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (three-times differentiable nonlinear ANM mechanism condition, \cite{peters14a}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:smooth-positive-densities]\label{ass:smooth-positive-densities}
Each \(p_{N_j}\) is three times continuously differentiable and strictly positive on the relative interior of its compact interval support.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This is a compact-support adaptation of the positive-density regime used by Peters et al., not their published premise. Smooth compact bump innovations satisfy it and model measurements with hard feasibility limits while allowing exact density decay only at the boundary.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:support-closure]\label{ass:support-closure}
For every \(j\in I\), \(f_j(\prod_{u\in\operatorname{pa}_0(j)}[a_u,b_u])+\operatorname{supp}(N_j)\subseteq[a_j,b_j]\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Bounded physical measurements and bounded innovations satisfy this compatibility condition after choosing the recorded node ranges to contain every feasible structural output.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:causal-minimality]\label{ass:causal-minimality}
For every edge \(u\to v\) of \(G_0\), the map \(x_u\mapsto f_v(x_u,x_{\operatorname{pa}_0(v)\setminus\{u\}})\) is nonconstant on a set of positive parent-law measure.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This strengthens the pointwise nonconstancy characterization in Peters et al., Proposition 17, for the compact-support setting. Requiring each parent effect to be nonconstant on a set of positive parent-law measure ensures, under the relative-interior density and smoothness conditions, that the effect is nonconstant on a positive-law interior set rather than only at boundary or null configurations, which is the form needed by the compact identification bridge.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:condition19-configurations]\label{ass:condition19-configurations}
For every \(v\in I\), every \(u\in\operatorname{pa}_0(v)\), and every \(A\) satisfying \(\operatorname{pa}_0(v)\setminus\{u\}\subseteq A\subseteq\operatorname{nd}_0(v)\setminus\{u,v\}\), there is an interior \(x_A\) of positive density such that the section \(x_u\mapsto f_v(x_{\operatorname{pa}_0(v)\setminus\{u\}},x_u)\), the conditional law \(\mathcal L(V_u\mid V_A=x_A)\), and \(\mathcal L(N_v)\) satisfy Peters et al.'s Condition 19 by failing their differential equation (7) on some open part of its domain.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This is the compact-section analogue of Peters et al.'s Condition 19 configuration requirement. It is directly checkable for smooth compact mechanisms and bump innovations by evaluating their differential equation on an interior section; failure on an open section is stable under small three-times continuously differentiable perturbations and includes the quadratic and cosine mechanisms used here.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:connected-support-fibers]\label{ass:connected-support-fibers}
Every nonempty conditional support fiber appearing in Assumption \(\ref{ass:condition19-configurations}\) has connected relative interior.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Convex innovation supports combined with monotone triangular mechanisms give connected interval fibers, covering compact measurement systems while excluding disconnected-support reversals.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:variance-nondegeneracy]\label{ass:variance-nondegeneracy}
There is a constant \(\underline v>0\) such that \(\min_{j\in I}\operatorname{Var}_P(V_j)\ge\underline v\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (uniform variance nondegeneracy, \cite{buehlmann2014cam}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:matching-components]\label{ass:matching-components}
Every weakly connected component of \(G_0\) is either an isolated vertex or one directed edge.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Independent cause--effect modules arise in separately engineered subsystems and controlled benchmark suites; this graph regime isolates a directly checkable mechanism-level certificate.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:pair-signal-dominance]\label{ass:pair-signal-dominance}
For every edge \(u\to v\) of \(G_0\), \(\rho^{\to}_{uv}>(d-1)\rho^{\leftarrow}_{uv}/d\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} The inequality is an integral condition computed from a structural function, a root density, and an innovation density; small-noise monotone mechanisms and symmetric many-to-one mechanisms satisfy it without comparing permutation scores.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:iid-sampling]\label{ass:iid-sampling}
The observations \(V^{(1)},\ldots,V^{(n)}\) are independent draws from \(P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (independent sampling, \cite{pfister2018dhsic}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:holder-regressions]\label{ass:holder-regressions}
For every \(v\in I\) and \(A\subseteq I\setminus\{v\}\), \(\mu_{v,A}\) belongs to a radius-bounded Holder ball of smoothness three on \(\mathcal X_A\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Holder smoothness for nonparametric regression, \cite{stone1982optimal}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:design-density]\label{ass:design-density}
For each \(A\subseteq I\), the density of \(P_A\) is bounded above and bounded away from zero on the relative interior of its support.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (regular random-design density condition for Holder regression, \cite{stone1982optimal}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:characteristic-kernels]\label{ass:characteristic-kernels}
Every kernel in \(k\) is bounded, continuous, and characteristic.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (bounded continuous characteristic-kernel premise, \cite{pfister2018dhsic}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:residual-kernel-lipschitz]\label{ass:residual-kernel-lipschitz}
There is \(L_k<\infty\) such that every residual-coordinate factor satisfies \(|k^{\varepsilon}_{v,A}(e,e')-k^{\varepsilon}_{v,A}(\tilde e,\tilde e')|\le L_k(|e-\tilde e|+|e'-\tilde e'|)\) on the compact residual range.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This explicitly nonstandard perturbation premise is satisfied by Gaussian and Laplace residual kernels restricted to compact residual ranges, covering the usual smooth kernels used for residual-independence screening.
\end{assumption}

\section{Definitions}

\begin{definition}[def:base-model]\label{def:base-model}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \(\mathcal M_0(d)\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\mathcal M_0(d)=\{(G_0,f,p_N,P):j\in\{1,\ldots,d\},\ V_j=f_j(V_{\operatorname{pa}_0(j)})+N_j,\ P=\mathcal L(V),\text{ and the eight member properties below hold}\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Member properties.} \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}anm-\allowbreak{}independence}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}smooth-\allowbreak{}mechanisms}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}smooth-\allowbreak{}positive-\allowbreak{}densities}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}support-\allowbreak{}closure}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}causal-\allowbreak{}minimality}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}condition19-\allowbreak{}configurations}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}connected-\allowbreak{}support-\allowbreak{}fibers}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}variance-\allowbreak{}nondegeneracy}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:structural-density]\label{def:structural-density}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{structural density}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(p_{f,p_N}(v)=\prod_{j\in I}p_{N_j}(v_j-f_j(v_{\operatorname{pa}_0(j)}))\mathbf 1\{v\in\mathcal X\}\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:predecessors]\label{def:predecessors}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{predecessor set}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\operatorname{Pred}_{\pi}(v)=\{u\in I:\pi^{-1}(u)<\pi^{-1}(v)\}\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:conditional-risk]\label{def:conditional-risk}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{conditional mean and risk}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\mu_{v,A}(x_A)=\mathbb E_P[V_v\mid V_A=x_A]\) and \(R_{v,A}=\inf_{g\in L^2(P_A)}\mathbb E_P[(V_v-g(V_A))^2]=\mathbb E_P[(V_v-\mu_{v,A}(V_A))^2]\), with \(R_{v,\varnothing}=\operatorname{Var}_P(V_v)\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:correlation-ratio]\label{def:correlation-ratio}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{conditional correlation ratio}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(q(v,A)=1-R_{v,A}/\operatorname{Var}_P(V_v)=\operatorname{Var}_P\{\mu_{v,A}(V_A)\}/\operatorname{Var}_P(V_v)\), with \(q(v,\varnothing)=0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}conditional-\allowbreak{}risk}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}variance-\allowbreak{}nondegeneracy}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:hsic]\label{def:hsic}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{residual HSIC}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(H(v,A,u)=\|\mu_{P_{\varepsilon_{v,A},V_u}}-\mu_{P_{\varepsilon_{v,A}}\otimes P_{V_u}}\|_{\mathcal H_{k_{v,A,u}}}^2\), where \(\varepsilon_{v,A}=V_v-\mu_{v,A}(V_A)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}conditional-\allowbreak{}risk}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}characteristic-\allowbreak{}kernels}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:dependence-count]\label{def:dependence-count}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{exact dependence count}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(D(v,A)=\sum_{u\in A}\mathbf 1\{H(v,A,u)>0\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}hsic}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}characteristic-\allowbreak{}kernels}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:gene-score]\label{def:gene-score}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{population GENE score}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\ell_P(v,A)=q(v,A)\{1-D(v,A)/(|A|+1)\}\) and \(S_P(\pi)=\sum_{v\in I}\ell_P(v,\operatorname{Pred}_{\pi}(v))\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}predecessors}; \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}correlation-\allowbreak{}ratio}; \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}dependence-\allowbreak{}count}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:insertion-gain]\label{def:insertion-gain}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{insertion and score gain}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\operatorname{Ins}_{r\to s}(\pi)\) removes the node at rank \(r\), shifts the traversed block by one rank, and reinserts that node at rank \(s\); \(C_P(\pi;r,s)=S_P(\operatorname{Ins}_{r\to s}(\pi))-S_P(\pi)\) for \(r\ne s\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}gene-\allowbreak{}score}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:correcting-moves]\label{def:correcting-moves}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{ancestral reversal potential and correcting moves}

Define \(\rho^{\to}_{uv}=\operatorname{Var}_{p_u}(f_v(X_u))/[\operatorname{Var}_{p_u}(f_v(X_u))+\operatorname{Var}(N_v)]\) and \(\rho^{\leftarrow}_{uv}=\operatorname{Var}_{p_v}(m_{uv}(V_v))/\operatorname{Var}_{p_u}(X_u)\).

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{cross-\allowbreak{}fitted bands and separated-\allowbreak{}model decisions}

The generic and matching-adaptive branches are propagated separately.

\end{definition}

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:compact-identifiability]\label{lem:compact-identifiability}
Every law in \(\mathcal M_0(d)\) identifies \(G_0\). Support closure first makes the structural recursion a law on \(\mathcal X\). On every nonempty conditional-support fiber, the induced density is positive and three times continuously differentiable on a connected relative interior. The conditional-independence intersection argument therefore applies on that open set, connectedness rules out patching distinct reverse factorizations across disjoint interior components, and the support boundary is null. Assumption \(\ref{ass:condition19-configurations}\) then supplies an interior open set on which Peters et al.'s differential identity fails, excluding every reversed bivariate conditional ANM. These steps extend their positive-density Condition 19 argument to the stated compact-support regime without assuming uniqueness of the DAG.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write \(\mathcal O\) for the relative interior of the attainable support.  In a topological ordering of \(G_0\), the triangular change of variables
\[
 T(v)_j=v_j-f_j(v_{\operatorname{pa}_0(j)})
\]
has triangular Jacobian with diagonal entries one.  Hence Assumption \(\ref{ass:anm-independence}\), the structural equations in Definition \(\ref{def:base-model}\), and the change-of-variables formula give, almost everywhere on \(\mathcal X\),
\[
 p_P(v)=\prod_{j\in I}p_{N_j}\!\left(v_j-f_j(v_{\operatorname{pa}_0(j)})\right)
 =p_{f,p_N}(v).
\]
Assumption \(\ref{ass:support-closure}\) makes this a probability density supported on \(\mathcal X\), rather than a formal recursion which can leave the recorded node ranges.  Because every innovation has a density, every boundary face of an innovation interval, and hence its inverse image under \(T\), has \(P\)-measure zero.

Fix one of the conditioning configurations in Assumption \(\ref{ass:condition19-configurations}\).  Conditioning the displayed product density and cancelling its normalizing constant shows that the conditional density on a nonempty fiber is a quotient of finite products of the factors in Definition \(\ref{def:structural-density}\).  Assumptions \(\ref{ass:smooth-mechanisms}\) and \(\ref{ass:smooth-positive-densities}\) therefore make that density strictly positive and three times continuously differentiable on the fiber's relative interior.  Assumption \(\ref{ass:connected-support-fibers}\) makes that relative interior connected.  In particular, an almost-everywhere conditional factorization on the fiber extends, by continuity, throughout each open coordinate patch.  On two overlapping patches the two extensions agree on a nonempty open overlap; connectedness then propagates the same factorization along a finite chain of overlapping patches.  Thus distinct reverse factorizations cannot be pasted on disconnected pieces.  Since the omitted boundary is null, all conditional-independence equalities required by the restricted-ANM argument hold under the original conditional law.

We now invoke the finite-graph reduction and bivariate differential obstruction in Lemma \(\ref{lem:peters-restricted-anm-identification}\).  Its proof uses positivity only to obtain the preceding intersection-and-localization step.  We have just established that step on every relevant relative interior.  If a second causally minimal additive-noise DAG generated \(P\), the reduction would produce an edge \(u\to v\), a set \(A\) with
\[
 \operatorname{pa}_0(v)\setminus\{u\}\subseteq A
 \subseteq\operatorname{nd}_0(v)\setminus\{u,v\},
\]
and a positive-density conditional section on which both causal directions admitted additive-noise representations.  The bivariate part of the cited lemma then forces Peters et al.'s differential equation (7) on every open subpatch on which its denominators are nonzero.  Assumption \(\ref{ass:condition19-configurations}\) supplies, for this same \((u,v,A)\), an interior open subpatch on which that equation fails.  This is a contradiction.  Assumption \(\ref{ass:causal-minimality}\) rules out removal of a true edge before the reduction is applied.  Therefore no distinct causally minimal additive-noise DAG generates \(P\), so the observed law \(P\) identifies the labelled graph \(G_0\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The compact-domain model needs a genuine identification bridge because positivity only on relative interiors is weaker than full-support positivity. \textit{Gap.} Peters et al. (2014, Condition 19, Definition 27, and Theorem 28) do not state the support-closed compact-fiber extension. \textit{Consumer.} The score analysis can target the labelled DAG using a proved compact restricted-ANM identification result.

\begin{theorem}[thm:pairwise-navigation]\label{thm:pairwise-navigation}
Let \((G_0,f,p_N,P)\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{pair}}(d)\). For every \(\pi\notin\Pi(G_0)\), choose a component edge \(u\to v\) with \(v\) before \(u\) and insert \(u\) immediately before \(v\). This move belongs to \(\operatorname{Corr}_{G_0}(\pi)\) and satisfies \(C_P(\pi;r,s)\ge\rho^{\to}_{uv}-(d-1)\rho^{\leftarrow}_{uv}/d\ge\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}>0\). The bound follows from the structural product law: before the move the reversed root contributes at most \((d-1)\rho^{\leftarrow}_{uv}/d\), after the move the effect contributes \(\rho^{\to}_{uv}\), and every traversed node from another independent component has unchanged explained risk and a weakly larger GENE penalty factor. Consequently every insertion-local maximizer is topological.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \((G_0,f,p_N,P)\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{pair}}(d)\).  Independence of the innovations and the matching-component condition imply that the random vectors belonging to distinct weak components of \(G_0\) are mutually independent.  Consider an edge component \(u\to v\).  For any set \(B\) made only of nodes in other components, conditional expectation and independence give
\[
 q(v,B)=q(u,B)=0,
\]
\[
 q(v,B\cup\{u\})
 =\frac{\operatorname{Var}\{f_v(V_u)\}}
 {\operatorname{Var}\{f_v(V_u)\}+\operatorname{Var}(N_v)}
 =\rho^{\to}_{uv},
\]
and
\[
 q(u,B\cup\{v\})
 =\frac{\operatorname{Var}\{\mathbb E[V_u\mid V_v]\}}
 {\operatorname{Var}(V_u)}
 =\rho^{\leftarrow}_{uv}.
\]
The divisions are valid by Assumption \(\ref{ass:variance-nondegeneracy}\), which is a member property of \(\mathcal M_0(d)\).

When \(u\) precedes \(v\), the residual after regressing \(V_v\) on \(B\cup\{u\}\) is \(N_v-\mathbb E N_v\).  It is independent of every predecessor by Assumption \(\ref{ass:anm-independence}\), so characteristicness in Definition \(\ref{def:hsic}\) gives \(D(v,B\cup\{u\})=0\).  Thus the edge component contributes \(\rho^{\to}_{uv}\) to \(S_P\).  When \(v\) precedes \(u\), regression on \(B\cup\{v\}\) is the same as regression on \(V_v\).  If its residual were independent of \(V_v\), it would give the reverse additive-noise representation
\[
 V_u=\mathbb E[V_u\mid V_v]+E,
 \qquad E\perp V_v.
\]
The Condition 19 configuration and Lemma \(\ref{lem:compact-identifiability}\) exclude that representation.  The residual is independent of every member of \(B\), however, because \((V_u,V_v)\) is independent of the other components.  Therefore, if \(a=|B|+1\) is the number of predecessors of \(u\),
\[
 D(u,B\cup\{v\})=1,
 \qquad
 \ell_P(u,B\cup\{v\})
 =\rho^{\leftarrow}_{uv}\frac{a}{a+1}
 \le \frac{d-1}{d}\rho^{\leftarrow}_{uv}.
\]

Let \(\pi\notin\Pi(G_0)\).  Some edge component \(u\to v\) is reversed in \(\pi\).  Move \(u\) from its rank to the rank immediately before \(v\).  Since \(u\) has no descendant other than \(v\), this removes the ancestral reversal \((u,v)\) and creates none, so the move belongs to \(\operatorname{Corr}_{G_0}(\pi)\).  The contribution of this component changes from at most \((d-1)\rho^{\leftarrow}_{uv}/d\) to \(\rho^{\to}_{uv}\).  For each node \(w\) strictly traversed by the insertion, the new predecessor set is its old predecessor set with the independent variable \(V_u\) added.  Its conditional mean, explained risk, and dependence count are unchanged, while
\[
 1-\frac{D(w,A)}{|A|+2}\ge 1-\frac{D(w,A)}{|A|+1}.
\]
All other local terms are unchanged.  Definitions \(\ref{def:gene-score}\) and \(\ref{def:insertion-gain}\) consequently yield
\[
 C_P(\pi;r,s)
 \ge \rho^{\to}_{uv}-\frac{d-1}{d}\rho^{\leftarrow}_{uv}
 \ge\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}>0,
\]
where strict positivity follows from Assumption \(\ref{ass:pair-signal-dominance}\) and finiteness of the edge set.  (If the graph has no edges, every order is topological and the assertion about non-topological orders is vacuous.)  Finally, an insertion-local maximizer cannot be non-topological, because the construction above would supply a strictly improving insertion.  Hence every insertion-local maximizer is topological.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the field-level root result: a check on mechanisms and noise laws yields strict local ascent without assuming any ordering of permutation scores. \textit{Gap.} Li et al. (2025, Theorem 4.3) assume a global ranking for every pair of orders with different ancestral-reversal counts and give no structural-function condition implying a correcting insertion. \textit{Consumer.} Benchmark designers can certify population success of insertion search directly from each cause--effect module.

\begin{theorem}[thm:li-strict-separation]\label{thm:li-strict-separation}
Li et al.'s global reversal-monotone premise implies the positive correcting-move property on the matching-component graph class, but the converse fails even for smooth identifiable ANMs. For all sufficiently small \(\sigma>0\), \(P_{\mathrm{sep},\sigma}\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{pair}}(6)\): symmetry gives \(\rho^{\leftarrow}_{12}=0\), while \(\rho^{\to}_{12}\to1\) and both directional ratios on edges \(3\to4\) and \(5\to6\) converge to \(1\). Nevertheless, for \(\pi_1=(2,3,4,5,6,1)\) and \(\pi_2=(1,2,4,6,3,5)\), \(\operatorname{Rev}_{G_0}(\pi_1)=1<2=\operatorname{Rev}_{G_0}(\pi_2)\), whereas \(S_{P_{\mathrm{sep},\sigma}}(\pi_1)\to2\) and \(S_{P_{\mathrm{sep},\sigma}}(\pi_2)\to1+4/5+5/6>2\). Continuity of the correlation ratios, together with forward residual independence and Condition 19 reverse dependence for every \(\sigma>0\), makes the reversed ranking strict for small positive \(\sigma\). Thus Theorem \(\ref{thm:pairwise-navigation}\) guarantees local recovery for a support-closed smooth law excluded by Li et al.'s premise.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First write the global reversal-monotone premise used by Li et al. in the form
\[
 \operatorname{Rev}_{G_0}(\pi)<\operatorname{Rev}_{G_0}(\pi')
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad S_P(\pi)>S_P(\pi').
\]
On a matching graph, every non-topological order has the correcting insertion constructed in Theorem \(\ref{thm:pairwise-navigation}\), and that insertion strictly decreases \(\operatorname{Rev}_{G_0}\).  The displayed premise therefore implies the positive correcting-move property.

We prove failure of the converse using Definition \(\ref{def:li-separation-witness}\).  The three edge components are independent.  All displayed root and innovation densities are positive and \(C^3\) on their relative interiors; the mechanisms are \(C^3\), nonconstant, and their stated node intervals contain the structural outputs.  Variances are positive.  The relevant conditional fibers for the root given variables from the other components have connected interiors.  It remains to verify the differential obstruction.  If \(\xi=\log p_X\), \(\nu=\log p_N\), \(t=y-f(x)\), and
\[
 L(x,y)=\xi(x)+\nu(t),
\]
then a reverse additive-noise representation implies, wherever \(L_{xx}\ne0\),
\[
 \partial_x\!\left(\frac{L_{xy}}{L_{xx}}\right)=0. \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, in a reverse representation \(L(x,y)=\widetilde\nu(x-g(y))+\widetilde\xi(y)\), and the ratio is \(-g'(y)\).  For the symmetric bump innovation, at \(t=0\) one has \(\nu'(0)=\nu'''(0)=0\) and \(\nu''(0)<0\).  For the cosine edge, at \(x=1/2\), \(f'(x)=0\), \(f''(x)=4\pi^2\), and \(\xi''(x)\ne0\), so the left side of (1) equals
\[
 -\frac{f''(x)\nu''(0)}{\xi''(x)}\ne0.
\]
For either quadratic edge, substitution at \(t=0\) reduces the numerator in (1), up to the nonzero factor \(\nu''(0)\), to
\[
 f'(x)\xi'''(x)-f''(x)\xi''(x)+2f'(x)^2f''(x)\nu''(0). \tag{2}
\]
For \(f(x)=x+x^2\) and \(\xi(x)=-1/[x(1-x)]+\mathrm{constant}\), the quotient
\[
 \frac{f'(x)\xi'''(x)-f''(x)\xi''(x)}{2f'(x)^2f''(x)}
\]
is not constant on \((0,1)\) (direct differentiation gives a nonzero rational function).  Hence (2) cannot vanish for every \(x\) for any fixed value \(\nu''(0)=-2/\sigma^2\).  For every \(\sigma>0\) there is therefore an interior point where (1) fails, and continuity makes the failure hold on an open patch.  Thus Assumption \(\ref{ass:condition19-configurations}\) holds; Lemma \(\ref{lem:compact-identifiability}\) gives identifiability, and the witness belongs to \(\mathcal M_0(6)\).

For edge \(1\to2\), symmetry under \(x\mapsto1-x\) gives
\[
 \mathbb E[V_1\mid V_2]=\frac12,
 \qquad \rho^{\leftarrow}_{12}=0,
\]
whereas
\[
 \rho^{\to}_{12}
 =\frac{\operatorname{Var}\{\cos(2\pi V_1)\}}
 {\operatorname{Var}\{\cos(2\pi V_1)\}+\sigma^2\operatorname{Var}(Z_2)}
 \longrightarrow1.
\]
For \(h(x)=x+x^2\), the inverse of \(h\) on \([0,1]\) is one-Lipschitz.  Extending it by clipping to \([0,1]\) gives an estimator \(\widetilde X(V)=h^{-1}_{\mathrm{clip}}(V)\) satisfying
\[
 \mathbb E\{(X-\widetilde X(h(X)+\sigma Z))^2\}
 \le \sigma^2\mathbb E[Z^2].
\]
The conditional mean minimizes squared risk, so the conditional-variance identity gives
\[
 1-\rho^{\leftarrow}
 =\frac{\mathbb E\operatorname{Var}(X\mid h(X)+\sigma Z)}{\operatorname{Var}(X)}
 \le\frac{\sigma^2\mathbb E[Z^2]}{\operatorname{Var}(X)}\longrightarrow0.
\]
The forward ratio is \(\operatorname{Var}(h(X))/\{\operatorname{Var}(h(X))+\sigma^2\operatorname{Var}(Z)\}\to1\).  Consequently both directional ratios on edges \(3\to4\) and \(5\to6\) tend to one.  Since their pairwise gaps tend to \(1-5/6=1/6\), and the cosine gap tends to one, Assumption \(\ref{ass:pair-signal-dominance}\) holds for all sufficiently small positive \(\sigma\).  Hence \(P_{\mathrm{sep},\sigma}\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{pair}}(6)\).

The local score calculation in the proof of Theorem \(\ref{thm:pairwise-navigation}\) now gives the score limits exactly.  In \(\pi_1=(2,3,4,5,6,1)\), the cosine edge is reversed but has reverse ratio zero, while the other two edges are forward, so
\[
 S_{P_{\mathrm{sep},\sigma}}(\pi_1)\longrightarrow 0+1+1=2.
\]
In \(\pi_2=(1,2,4,6,3,5)\), the cosine edge is forward.  The roots of the two reversed quadratic edges have respectively four and five predecessors; their reverse residuals are dependent on their effects by (1), and independent of all variables in the other components.  Thus
\[
 S_{P_{\mathrm{sep},\sigma}}(\pi_2)\longrightarrow
 1+\frac45+\frac56>2.
\]
Direct counting gives \(\operatorname{Rev}_{G_0}(\pi_1)=1<2=\operatorname{Rev}_{G_0}(\pi_2)\).  The ratios above are continuous in \(\sigma>0\), and the dependence indicators stay equal to one because (1) fails for every sufficiently small positive \(\sigma\).  The reverse score inequality is therefore strict for all sufficiently small \(\sigma\), contradicting global reversal monotonicity.  Theorem \(\ref{thm:pairwise-navigation}\) nevertheless supplies the local recovery guarantee.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} An explicit score-limit calculation certifies a strict reduction in the recovery hypothesis, rather than a verbal comparison between differently named conditions. \textit{Gap.} Li et al. (2025, Theorem 4.3 and its proof on page 5) establish sufficiency of global reversal monotonicity but do not separate it from the local property their optimizer needs. \textit{Consumer.} Users obtain a guarantee for heterogeneous modular systems whose inversion costs cannot be ranked by an unweighted reversal count.

\begin{proposition}[prop:positive-witness]\label{prop:positive-witness}
For \(P_{\mathrm w,\sigma}\), the forward residual is independent of \(X\), the reverse conditional-mean residual is dependent on \(Y\), and, with \(m_2=\mathbb E[Z^2]\), \(C_{P_{\mathrm w,\sigma}}((Y,X);2,1)\ge1/2-\sigma^2m_2/\operatorname{Var}(X+X^2)\). Hence the correcting gain is positive for sufficiently small \(\sigma\) and converges to \(1/2\). The support-closure relation holds on \([0,1]\times[-\sigma,2+\sigma]\), and the Condition 19 differential identity fails on an interior open set.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let \(h(x)=x+x^2\) and \(m_2=\mathbb E[Z^2]\).  Since \(Z\) is symmetric, \(\mathbb E Z=0\), and Definition \(\ref{def:positive-witness}\) gives
\[
 \mathbb E[Y\mid X]=h(X),\qquad
 \varepsilon_{Y,\{X\}}=\sigma Z,
\]
so the forward residual is independent of \(X\).  Moreover
\[
 q(Y,\{X\})
 =\frac{\operatorname{Var}(h(X))}
 {\operatorname{Var}(h(X))+\sigma^2m_2}
 \ge 1-\frac{\sigma^2m_2}{\operatorname{Var}(h(X))}. \tag{2}
\]
The denominator is positive because \(h\) is nonconstant under the uniform law.

We next show that \(\varepsilon_{X,\{Y\}}=X-\mathbb E[X\mid Y]\) is not independent of \(Y\).  Positivity of both densities on their support interiors gives, for every \(y\in(-\sigma,\sigma)\),
\[
 \operatorname{supp}(X\mid Y=y)^{\circ}
 =\bigl(0,h^{-1}(y+\sigma)\bigr).
\]
Translation by \(-\mathbb E[X\mid Y=y]\) does not change the diameter of this interval.  Its diameter \(h^{-1}(y+\sigma)\) is strictly increasing in \(y\) on an interval having positive \(P_Y\)-density.  If the residual were independent of \(Y\), its conditional support would agree with one fixed marginal support for almost every such \(y\), which is impossible because these diameters differ.  Hence characteristicness of the kernel implies \(H(X,\{Y\},Y)>0\).

For two nodes, the correct order has score \(q(Y,\{X\})\), while the wrong order has score \(q(X,\{Y\})/2\): the factor is one half because the reverse residual has exactly one dependent predecessor.  Therefore
\[
 C_{P_{\mathrm w,\sigma}}((Y,X);2,1)
 =q(Y,\{X\})-\frac12q(X,\{Y\})
 \ge q(Y,\{X\})-\frac12
 \ge\frac12-\frac{\sigma^2m_2}{\operatorname{Var}(X+X^2)},
\]
where \(q(X,\{Y\})\le1\) follows from the conditional-variance decomposition.  This lower bound is positive for sufficiently small \(\sigma\).  The one-Lipschitz inverse argument used in the proof of Theorem \(\ref{thm:li-strict-separation}\) gives \(q(X,\{Y\})\to1\), while (2) gives \(q(Y,\{X\})\to1\), so the gain converges to \(1/2\).

Finally, \(h([0,1])+[-\sigma,\sigma]\subseteq[-\sigma,2+\sigma]\), proving support closure.  To check the differential premise explicitly, put \(L(x,y)=\log p_X(x)+\log p_{\sigma Z}(y-h(x))\).  At any interior point with \(y=h(x)\), the uniform root has zero log-density derivatives, while the symmetric bump has \(\nu'(0)=\nu'''(0)=0\) and \(\nu''(0)<0\).  Direct differentiation yields
\[
 \partial_x\!\left(\frac{L_{xy}}{L_{xx}}\right)
 =\frac{2h''(x)}{h'(x)^2}=\frac{4}{(1+2x)^2}\ne0.
\]
A reverse additive-noise representation would make this ratio a function of \(y\) alone.  Thus Peters et al.'s differential identity fails on an interior open set by continuity, verifying the stated Condition 19 requirement.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The witness supplies a quantitative, scale-invariant calibration of the primitive certificate in the smallest graph. \textit{Gap.} Reisach et al. (2023, Sections 3 and 5) analyze normalized explained variance but not its combination with exact residual dependence in GENE Equation (6). \textit{Consumer.} Implementers obtain an analytic two-node test case whose population gain approaches one half.

\begin{theorem}[thm:oracle-recovery]\label{thm:oracle-recovery}
If \((G_0,f,p_N,P)\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{pair}}(d)\), then \(\mathfrak R_{\mathrm{or}}(P,\pi)=G_0\) for every \(\pi\in\mathfrak S_I\). Strict ascent prevents revisiting an order, and Theorem \(\ref{thm:pairwise-navigation}\) makes every terminal order topological; Condition 19 configurations and causal minimality then make residual-independence pruning delete exactly the nonedges. The search uses at most \(d!-1\) insertions and \(d(d-1)d!\) gain comparisons. Memoization uses at most \(d2^{d-1}\) conditional-risk queries and \(d(d-1)2^{d-2}\) score-relevant residual-HSIC queries, followed by at most \(d(d-1)/2\) pruning checks.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Run the map in Definition \(\ref{def:oracle-recovery}\) from an arbitrary \(\pi\in\mathfrak S_I\).  Every accepted insertion has strictly positive gain, so \(S_P\) strictly increases.  An order can therefore never be revisited.  Since \(|\mathfrak S_I|=d!\), at most \(d!-1\) insertions are accepted.  If the terminal order were non-topological, Theorem \(\ref{thm:pairwise-navigation}\) would provide a positive correcting insertion, contradicting termination.  Thus the terminal order is topological.

It remains to verify pruning.  In a topological order on a matching graph, a nonparent predecessor \(u\) of \(v\) lies in another independent component.  As long as the current ordered parent set \(A\) contains the true parent when one exists,
\[
 \mathbb E[V_v\mid V_{A\setminus\{u\}}]
 =\mathbb E[V_v\mid V_{\operatorname{pa}_0(v)}],
\]
and its residual is a centered innovation, independent of \(V_u\).  Hence \(H(v,A\setminus\{u\},u)=0\), and every nonedge is deleted.  If \(u\) is the true parent of \(v\), all variables in \(A\setminus\{u\}\) are from independent components, so the tested residual is \(V_v-\mathbb E V_v\).  Were it independent of \(V_u\), the pair would admit both the stated nonconstant forward ANM and an independence factorization, contrary to causal minimality and the Condition 19 identification conclusion in Lemma \(\ref{lem:compact-identifiability}\).  Characteristicness therefore gives \(H(v,A\setminus\{u\},u)>0\), so no true edge is deleted.  This invariant also shows that the conclusion is independent of the deletion order.  The output is exactly \(G_0\).

There are \(d(d-1)\) ordered insertions at each visited order, including the terminal order.  At most \(d!\) orders are visited, giving at most \(d(d-1)d!\) gain comparisons.  Every score is determined by the table
\[
 \{R_{v,A}:v\in I,\ A\subseteq I\setminus\{v\}\},
\]
which has \(d2^{d-1}\) entries, and the score-relevant table
\[
 \{H(v,A,u):v\in I,\ A\subseteq I\setminus\{v\},\ u\in A\},
\]
which has
\[
 d(d-1)2^{d-2}
\]
entries.  Memoization therefore gives the asserted risk and score-HSIC query bounds.  A complete ordered DAG has at most \(d(d-1)/2\) arrows, so pruning requires at most that many additional HSIC checks.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The primitive certificate is useful only if it yields a complete labelled-DAG recovery theorem and a finite oracle budget. \textit{Gap.} Solus, Wang, and Uhler (2021, Proposition 8 and Theorem 9) study covered-arrow walks for sparsity, while Li et al. (2025) do not give primitive risk and dependence query counts for GENE. \textit{Consumer.} An implementation can cache the finite local-score table and know its worst-case population-query budget.

\begin{theorem}[thm:simultaneous-bands]\label{thm:simultaneous-bands}
Under Assumptions \(\ref{ass:iid-sampling}\), \(\ref{ass:holder-regressions}\), \(\ref{ass:design-density}\), \(\ref{ass:characteristic-kernels}\), and \(\ref{ass:residual-kernel-lipschitz}\), write \(t_n=\log(M_d/\alpha)/n\). There are finite constants \(K,K_1,K_2\), depending only on the fixed regularity and fold constants, for which the following assertions hold. The generic ambient-dimensional branch has \(a_n=t_n^{3/(d+5)}\) and, with probability at least \(1-\alpha\), simultaneously covers all \(d2^{d-1}\) conditional risks, all \(d(d-1)2^{d-2}\) score-relevant residual-HSIC coordinates, and every residual-HSIC coordinate that can be queried by pruning, with
\[
 w_R\le K\{t_n^{1/2}+t_n^{6/(d+5)}\},
 \qquad
 w_H\le K\{t_n^{1/2}+t_n^{3/(d+5)}\}.
\]
If additionally \((G_0,f,p_N,P)\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{pair}}(d)\), the pairing-agnostic matching-adaptive branch simultaneously covers the same quantities with
\[
 a_{n,\mathrm{pair}}=t_n^{3/7},
 \qquad
 w_R\le K\{t_n^{1/2}+t_n^{6/7}\},
 \qquad
 w_H\le K\{t_n^{1/2}+t_n^{3/7}\}.
\]
Indeed, independence of weak components makes every \(\mu_{v,A}\) either constant or a function only of the unique node paired with \(v\), when that node belongs to \(A\); the adaptive fit selects over the constant and all \(|A|\) univariate degree-two local-polynomial candidates without knowing the pairing. Across all \((v,A)\), these candidates number
\[
 \sum_{v\in I}\sum_{A\subseteq I\setminus\{v\}}(|A|+1)
 =d2^{d-1}+d(d-1)2^{d-2}=M_d,
\]
so their selection is covered by the existing \(\log(M_d/\alpha)\) factor. In either branch, bounded kernels control held-out HSIC fluctuation, while Assumption \(\ref{ass:residual-kernel-lipschitz}\) is used only to convert cross-fitted residual error into the additive regression-error term. On the corresponding simultaneous event, whenever the score-relevant dependence labels under consideration are fixed at their true singleton values and \(w_R<\underline v/2\),
\[
 w_S\le \frac{4d}{\underline v}w_R.
\]
If \((G_0,f,p_N,P)\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{pair}}(d)\) and every score or pruning HSIC coordinate queried along the exact population execution belongs to \(\{0\}\cup[\lambda,\infty)\), then \(\Pr_P\{G_0\in\widehat{\mathcal G}_n\}\ge1-\alpha\), without any width requirement. Singleton recovery requires the stronger promise that every score and pruning HSIC coordinate queryable by any execution compatible with the realized bands belongs to \(\{0\}\cup[\lambda,\infty)\); under that promise, \(2w_S<\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}\) and \(2w_H<\lambda\) imply \(\widehat{\mathcal G}_n=\{G_0\}\). For the generic branch, the direct inequalities
\[
 K_1d\{t_n^{1/2}+t_n^{6/(d+5)}\}<\delta_{\mathrm{pair}},
 \qquad
 K_2\{t_n^{1/2}+t_n^{3/(d+5)}\}<\lambda
\]
imply those width conditions, and, for fixed regularity constants and \(0<\delta_{\mathrm{pair}},\lambda\le1\), the previously displayed sufficient order
\[
 n\ge K\log(M_d/\alpha)
 \max\left\{
 \left(\frac d{\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}}\right)^{\max\{2,(d+5)/6\}},
 \lambda^{-(d+5)/3}
 \right\}
\]
remains valid. For the matching-adaptive branch, it is enough that
\[
 K_1d\{t_n^{1/2}+t_n^{6/7}\}<\delta_{\mathrm{pair}},
 \qquad
 K_2\{t_n^{1/2}+t_n^{3/7}\}<\lambda,
\]
and hence it is sufficient that
\[
 n\ge K\log(M_d/\alpha)
 \max\left\{
 \left(\frac d{\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}}\right)^2,
 \lambda^{-7/3}
 \right\}.
\]
Thus the generic branch's score-rate elbow at \(d=7\) is an artifact of unrestricted \((d-1)\)-dimensional smoothing; the matching recovery class has structural regression dimension one and no such elbow. These orders are sufficient and are not claimed to be minimax optimal. If the singleton width conditions and the all-compatible-query separation promise are not imposed, the set-valued rule retains both labels whenever warranted and the compatible-DAG union may be a singleton or may contain multiple graphs.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(t_n=\log(M_d/\alpha)/n\), and let \(\underline v\) be the constant in Assumption \(\ref{ass:variance-nondegeneracy}\). Compactness of \(\mathcal X\) supplies a finite bound \(B\) for every coordinate of \(V\) and for every clipped residual below. Constants denoted by \(K\), \(K_R\), and \(K_H\) may depend on \(B\), the fixed fold proportions, the Holder radius, the two design-density bounds, the kernel bounds, and \(L_k\), but not on \(n\), \(\alpha\), or the indices \((v,A,u)\).

We first record the regression inequality used by both branches. Fix \(v\in I\), let \(\mathcal F_1,\ldots,\mathcal F_J\) be clipped degree-two piecewise-polynomial sieves, each having at most \(D\) coefficients, and let \(\widehat\mu\) minimize training-fold squared loss over their union. For squared loss, the defining projection property of \(\mu_{v,A}\) in Definition \(\ref{def:conditional-risk}\) gives, for every bounded candidate \(g\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P\{(V_v-g(V_A))^2-(V_v-\mu_{v,A}(V_A))^2\}
 =\lVert g-\mu_{v,A}\rVert_{L^2(P_A)}^2. \tag{1}
\]
Moreover, boundedness by \(B\) gives the Bernstein relation
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_P\{(V_v-g(V_A))^2-(V_v-\mu_{v,A}(V_A))^2\}
 \le K\lVert g-\mu_{v,A}\rVert_{L^2(P_A)}^2. \tag{2}
\]
To see the resulting fast rate directly, intersect each sieve with an \(L^2(P_A)\)-shell of squared radius \(r\). Symmetrization followed by Cauchy--Schwarz in its at most \(D\)-dimensional coefficient space bounds the expected centered empirical supremum on that shell by \(K\sqrt{Dr/n}\). Exponential Markov's inequality applied using (2), followed by a union bound over \(J\) sieves, therefore bounds the probability that this supremum exceeds \(r/2\) by
\[
 2J\exp\{-c n r+K D\}. \tag{3}
\]
Applying (3) to dyadic shells and using the empirical least-squares basic inequality yields
\[
 \Pr\!\left[
 \lVert\widehat\mu-\mu_{v,A}\rVert_{L^2(P_A)}^2
 >K\left\{
 \inf_{g\in\cup_{j=1}^J\mathcal F_j}
 \lVert g-\mu_{v,A}\rVert_{L^2(P_A)}^2
 +\frac{D+\log(J/\delta)}{n}
 \right\}
 \right]\le\delta . \tag{4}
\]
All variables in (1)--(4) are evaluated on the regression fold, whose size is a fixed positive fraction of \(n\); replacing that size by \(n\) only changes \(K\). This establishes (4) from boundedness, independence in Assumption \(\ref{ass:iid-sampling}\), and the least-squares projection identity, without a further tail condition.

For the generic branch, put \(m=|A|\). Taylor's formula under Assumption \(\ref{ass:holder-regressions}\), together with the regular support and density in Assumption \(\ref{ass:design-density}\), gives a degree-two piecewise-polynomial \(g_h\) on cells of side at most \(h\) such that
\[
 \lVert g_h-\mu_{v,A}\rVert_\infty\le K h^3,
 \qquad D\le K h^{-m}. \tag{5}
\]
Take \(J=1\), \(h=\min\{1,t_n^{1/(m+6)}\}\), and allocate failure probability at most \(\alpha/M_d\). Equations (4)--(5) give, for \(t_n\le1\),
\[
 \lVert\widehat\mu^{\mathrm{gen}}_{v,A}-\mu_{v,A}\rVert_{L^2(P_A)}
 \le K t_n^{3/(m+6)}
 \le K t_n^{3/(d+5)}=K a_n, \tag{6}
\]
because \(m\le d-1\). When \(t_n>1\), clipping and enlargement of \(K\) give the same conclusion. A union bound over all \(d2^{d-1}\) pairs \((v,A)\), and then over the fixed number of folds, proves (6) simultaneously.

We next prove the structural reduction for the matching-adaptive branch rather than assuming it. Suppose \((G_0,f,p_N,P)\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{pair}}(d)\). By Assumption \(\ref{ass:matching-components}\), each weak component is a singleton or an edge. The structural recursion makes the variables in a component measurable functions of precisely the noises indexed by that component. Assumption \(\ref{ass:anm-independence}\) therefore implies that the observed subvectors belonging to distinct weak components are mutually independent. Fix \(v\) and write \(c(v)\) for its unique component-mate when it exists. If \(c(v)\notin A\), then \(V_A\) is independent of \(V_v\), and hence
\[
 \mu_{v,A}(V_A)=\mathbb E_P[V_v]. \tag{7}
\]
If \(c(v)\in A\), then \(V_{A\setminus\{c(v)\}}\) is independent of \((V_v,V_{c(v)})\). Conditional expectation against bounded product test functions consequently gives
\[
 \mu_{v,A}(V_A)
 =\mathbb E_P[V_v\mid V_{c(v)}]
 =\mu_{v,\{c(v)\}}(V_{c(v)})
 \quad P\text{-almost surely}. \tag{8}
\]
Assumption \(\ref{ass:holder-regressions}\), applied to the singleton conditioning set \(\{c(v)\}\), makes the last function in (8) Holder-smooth of order three. Thus the true regression is in the closure, to approximation error \(K h^3\), of the constant candidate or one of the \(|A|\) univariate candidates in Definition \(\ref{def:simultaneous-bands}\); the estimator does not need to know which coordinate is \(c(v)\).

For each such univariate candidate, (5) holds with \(m=1\), hence \(D\le K h^{-1}\). Take \(h=\min\{1,t_n^{1/7}\}\) in (4). The number of candidate sieves over the entire regression table is exactly
\[
 \sum_{v\in I}\sum_{A\subseteq I\setminus\{v\}}(|A|+1)
 =d\left\{2^{d-1}+(d-1)2^{d-2}\right\}=M_d. \tag{9}
\]
Indeed, the first term counts one constant candidate for each \((v,A)\), and the second counts incidences \((v,A,u)\) with \(u\in A\). Therefore allocating error probability across all candidates in (9) uses only \(\log(M_d/\alpha)\). Equations (4), (7), and (8) now give simultaneously
\[
 \max_{v,A}
 \lVert\widehat\mu^{\mathrm{pair}}_{v,A}-\mu_{v,A}\rVert_{L^2(P_A)}
 \le K t_n^{3/7}=K a_{n,\mathrm{pair}}. \tag{10}
\]
As in (6), bounded clipping covers \(t_n>1\), and a fixed number of cross-fitting folds changes only \(K\).

We next translate either regression bound into the advertised bands. Write \(b_n=a_n\) for the generic branch and \(b_n=a_{n,\mathrm{pair}}\) for the matching-adaptive branch. On an evaluation fold independent of its fitted regression, let \(\widehat R_{v,A}\) be the empirical mean of the squared fitted residual. The conditional-expectation orthogonality in Definition \(\ref{def:conditional-risk}\) gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[(V_v-\widehat\mu_{v,A}(V_A))^2\mid\widehat\mu_{v,A}\right]
 =R_{v,A}+\lVert\widehat\mu_{v,A}-\mu_{v,A}\rVert_{L^2(P_A)}^2. \tag{11}
\]
The evaluation losses are conditionally independent and bounded. If a centered random variable \(W\) satisfies \(|W|\le b\), convexity of the exponential on \([-b,b]\) gives \(\mathbb E e^{sW}\le e^{s^2b^2/2}\); exponential Markov optimization and a union bound thus give simultaneous evaluation error at most \(K t_n^{1/2}\). Combining this fact with (6), (10), and (11) produces intervals containing every \(R_{v,A}\), with
\[
 w_R\le K_R\{t_n^{1/2}+b_n^2\}. \tag{12}
\]

For an HSIC coordinate, use the bounded centered order-four V-statistic on an evaluation fold with off-fold residuals. Conditional on the training fold, changing one evaluation observation changes this statistic by at most \(K/n\); exposing the observations one at a time and applying the same exponential argument gives deviation \(K t_n^{1/2}\). Repeated-index terms contribute at most \(K/n\), which is absorbed by this bound. To compare fitted- and true-residual population HSIC, expand HSIC as its finite sum of expectations of products of bounded kernel factors and telescope each product at its residual-kernel factor. Assumption \(\ref{ass:residual-kernel-lipschitz}\) and Cauchy--Schwarz then give
\[
 \left|H_{\widehat\mu}(v,A,u)-H(v,A,u)\right|
 \le K\mathbb E_P
 \left|\widehat\mu_{v,A}(V_A)-\mu_{v,A}(V_A)\right|
 \le K b_n. \tag{13}
\]
There are \(d(d-1)2^{d-2}\) score-relevant coordinates with \(u\in A\), and at most the same number of pruning coordinates with \(u\notin A\). Thus
\[
 w_H\le K_H\{t_n^{1/2}+b_n\}. \tag{14}
\]
Adding the pruning table changes the controlled count from \(M_d\) to at most \(2M_d\). Since \(M_d/\alpha\ge2\), \(\log(2M_d/\alpha)\le2\log(M_d/\alpha)\), which is absorbed in \(K_H\). Allocating all regression, risk, and HSIC failure probabilities to sum to at most \(\alpha\) defines a simultaneous event \(\mathcal E_n\) with \(P^n(\mathcal E_n)\ge1-\alpha\). Substituting \(b_n=t_n^{3/(d+5)}\) in (12)--(14) proves the generic widths; substituting \(b_n=t_n^{3/7}\) proves the matching-adaptive widths.

We now propagate risk uncertainty to scores. Work on \(\mathcal E_n\), suppose every score-relevant dependence indicator under consideration has been fixed at its true singleton value, and assume \(w_R<\underline v/2\). Put
\[
 y_v=R_{v,\varnothing}=\operatorname{Var}_P(V_v),
 \qquad x_{v,A}=R_{v,A}.
\]
Conditional expectation is an \(L^2(P)\) projection, so Definition \(\ref{def:conditional-risk}\) and Assumption \(\ref{ass:variance-nondegeneracy}\) imply
\[
 0\le x_{v,A}\le y_v,
 \qquad y_v\ge\underline v. \tag{15}
\]
Let \(\widehat y_v\) and \(\widehat x_{v,A}\) be band centers and project \(\widehat x_{v,A}\) onto \([0,\widehat y_v]\), obtaining \(\widetilde x_{v,A}\). Coverage and \(w_R<\underline v/2\) imply \(\widehat y_v>0\). If \(x_{v,A}\le\widehat y_v\), projection onto an interval containing \(x_{v,A}\) cannot increase error; if \(x_{v,A}>\widehat y_v\), then \(0<x_{v,A}-\widehat y_v\le y_v-\widehat y_v\le w_R\). Hence
\[
 |\widetilde x_{v,A}-x_{v,A}|\le w_R,
 \qquad |\widehat y_v-y_v|\le w_R. \tag{16}
\]
Define \(\widetilde q(v,A)=1-\widetilde x_{v,A}/\widehat y_v\). Equations (15)--(16) and Definition \(\ref{def:correlation-ratio}\) yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\widetilde q(v,A)-q(v,A)|
 &=\left|\frac{x_{v,A}}{y_v}
       -\frac{\widetilde x_{v,A}}{\widehat y_v}\right|\\
 &\le\frac{|x_{v,A}-\widetilde x_{v,A}|}{y_v}
 +\frac{\widetilde x_{v,A}}{\widehat y_v}
  \frac{|\widehat y_v-y_v|}{y_v}
 \le\frac{2w_R}{\underline v}. \tag{17}
\end{aligned}
\]
With singleton labels, the multiplier \(1-D(v,A)/(|A|+1)\) in Definition \(\ref{def:gene-score}\) lies in \([0,1]\). An insertion changes predecessor sets only for the moved node and the traversed block, hence for at most \(d\) nodes. Its gain is the difference between the before and after local contributions of those nodes. Applying (17) to both contributions for every affected node proves
\[
 w_S\le\frac{4d}{\underline v}w_R. \tag{18}
\]
No continuity of the independence indicator is asserted: (18) is conditional on the relevant labels being true singletons.

It remains to prove the graph conclusions and margins. Let \(\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{ex}}\) be the finite set of score and pruning HSIC coordinates actually queried along the exact population execution of Definition \(\ref{def:oracle-recovery}\), and let \(\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{all}}(\mathcal B)\) be the union of those coordinates queryable along any execution compatible with realized bands \(\mathcal B\). Suppose first that
\[
 H(\xi)\in\{0\}\cup[\lambda,\infty)
 \quad\text{for every }\xi\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{ex}}. \tag{19}
\]
On \(\mathcal E_n\), a true value zero makes its intersected interval contain zero, and a true value at least \(\lambda\) makes its intersection meet \([\lambda,\infty)\). Therefore the set-valued rule retains the true label at every exact-path query, irrespective of \(w_H\). The risk intervals retain every true risk, and interval propagation through Definition \(\ref{def:correlation-ratio}\) retains every exact correlation ratio and gain; if a denominator interval meets zero, replacing its image by the known range \([0,1]\) still retains the truth. The exact population execution is consequently compatible. Theorem \(\ref{thm:oracle-recovery}\) identifies its terminal graph as \(G_0\), and Definition \(\ref{def:simultaneous-bands}\) unions all compatible terminal DAGs. Thus
\[
 P^n\{G_0\in\widehat{\mathcal G}_n\}\ge P^n(\mathcal E_n)\ge1-\alpha. \tag{20}
\]

For singleton recovery, impose instead the stronger promise
\[
 H(\xi)\in\{0\}\cup[\lambda,\infty)
 \quad\text{for every }\xi\in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{all}}(\mathcal B). \tag{21}
\]
Continue on \(\mathcal E_n\) and suppose \(2w_H<\lambda\). If \(H=0\), the band intersected with the nonnegative line has upper endpoint at most \(2w_H<\lambda\); if \(H\ge\lambda\), its lower endpoint is at least \(H-2w_H>0\). Hence every label on every compatible path is the correct singleton label. If also \(2w_S<\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}\), Theorem \(\ref{thm:pairwise-navigation}\) supplies at every compatible non-topological order a correcting insertion with true gain at least \(\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}\). Its propagated lower endpoint exceeds \(\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}-2w_S>0\), so no compatible execution can stop there. Strict score ascent on the finite permutation set makes every compatible terminal order topological. By (21), pruning uses the population labels, and Theorem \(\ref{thm:oracle-recovery}\) makes every compatible terminal graph equal to \(G_0\). Therefore
\[
 \widehat{\mathcal G}_n=\{G_0\}. \tag{22}
\]
The weaker exact-path promise (19) gives coverage but cannot replace (21), because it does not constrain a coordinate reached only by another compatible execution.

Choose \(K_1\ge8K_R/\underline v\) and \(K_2\ge2K_H\). For either branch, if its displayed risk expression is denoted by \(r_n\) and its HSIC expression by \(h_n\), then
\[
 K_1d r_n<\delta_{\mathrm{pair}},
 \qquad K_2h_n<\lambda \tag{23}
\]
imply \(w_R<\underline v/2\), \(2w_S<\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}\) by (18), and \(2w_H<\lambda\). For the generic branch, substituting \(r_n=t_n^{1/2}+t_n^{6/(d+5)}\) and \(h_n=t_n^{1/2}+t_n^{3/(d+5)}\) into (23) reproduces
\[
 n\ge K\log(M_d/\alpha)
 \max\left\{
 \left(\frac d{\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}}\right)^{\max\{2,(d+5)/6\}},
 \lambda^{-(d+5)/3}
 \right\}. \tag{24}
\]
For the matching-adaptive branch, substitute \(r_n=t_n^{1/2}+t_n^{6/7}\) and \(h_n=t_n^{1/2}+t_n^{3/7}\). Since \(0<\delta_{\mathrm{pair}},\lambda\le1\) and \(d\ge2\), the implications
\[
 t_n\le c(\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}/d)^2
 \Longrightarrow d\{t_n^{1/2}+t_n^{6/7}\}<\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}/K_1,
\]
and
\[
 t_n\le c\lambda^{7/3}
 \Longrightarrow t_n^{1/2}+t_n^{3/7}<\lambda/K_2 \tag{25}
\]
hold for a sufficiently small constant \(c>0\): the powers \(2\) and \(7/3\) are respectively the more restrictive powers on \((0,1]\). Consequently
\[
 n\ge K\log(M_d/\alpha)
 \max\left\{
 \left(\frac d{\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}}\right)^2,
 \lambda^{-7/3}
 \right\} \tag{26}
\]
is sufficient for (22) under (21). The generic exponent \(\max\{2,(d+5)/6\}\) changes at \(d=7\) only because that branch smooths in as many as \(d-1\) coordinates. Equations (7)--(10) show that the matching class has regression dimension one, so (26) has no dimensional elbow. Both (24) and (26) are sufficient guarantees, not minimax claims. Without (21) and the singleton width conditions, the rule keeps both labels whenever warranted, and the compatible-DAG union may be a singleton or may contain multiple graphs; this does not affect the coverage conclusion (20) under the weaker promise (19).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The finite-sample theorem exploits the matching class's structural regression dimension one while retaining generic bands, and ties singleton recovery to the primitive gain slack and an explicit pruning resolution with abstention near independence. \textit{Gap.} Existing kernel-independence and sample-splitting results do not jointly cover pairing-agnostic structural adaptation, learned normalized risks, simultaneous residual perturbations, adaptive insertions, and separated-model graph confidence sets. \textit{Consumer.} Matching-class users obtain a dimension-adaptive labelled-DAG certificate when navigation and pruning margins dominate propagated uncertainty, while the generic branch remains available outside that structural reduction.

\begin{theorem}[thm:smooth-open-trap]\label{thm:smooth-open-trap}
Equip each fixed-graph stratum of \(\mathcal M_0(2)\) with the product structural topology given by Euclidean convergence of support endpoints, uniform \(C^1\) and compact-open \(C^3\) convergence of mechanisms after affine transport to fixed reference intervals, and \(L^1\) plus compact-open \(C^3\) convergence of innovation densities, and give the graph strata the discrete topology. For every \(\eta\in(0,1/2)\), there is an explicitly parameterized nonempty relatively open set \(\mathcal U_\eta\subset\mathcal M_0(2)\), all of whose members have generating graph \(X\to Y\), such that every law in \(\mathcal U_\eta\) satisfies
\[
 \frac12q(X,\{Y\})-q(Y,\{X\})>\eta,
 \qquad H(X,\{Y\},Y)>0.
\]
Thus the wrong order \((Y,X)\) is the unique global maximizer, and hence an insertion-local maximizer, of the exact \(\alpha=1\) population GENE score, although the causal order \(X\to Y\) is identifiable. Moreover, \(\mathcal U_\eta\) contains a nonempty relatively open subfamily having a non-affine child mechanism, and
\[
 \sup\left\{\frac12q(X,\{Y\})-q(Y,\{X\}):(G_0,f,p_N,P)\in\mathcal M_0(2),\ G_0=(X\to Y)\right\}=\frac12.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(\eta\in(0,1/2)\) and put \(\delta=1/2-\eta\).  We first construct one interior point of the asserted family.  Let
\[
 b(z)=c_b\exp\{-1/(1-z^2)\}\mathbf 1\{|z|<1\},
 \qquad m_2=\int_{-1}^1z^2b(z)\,dz,
\]
where \(c_b\) normalizes the density.  Symmetry gives \(\mathbb E Z=0\), and strict positivity on \((-1,1)\) gives \(0<m_2<1\).  Let
\[
 \beta(t)=c_\beta\exp\{-1/[t(1-t)]\}\mathbf 1\{0<t<1\},
 \qquad L=3,
\]
with \(c_\beta\) again chosen to give integral one.  Define the strictly positive numbers
\[
 e_\eta=\frac{\delta m_2}{64L^2},\qquad
 v_\eta=L^2e_\eta(1-e_\eta),\qquad
 B_\eta=\frac{\delta v_\eta}{256},
\]
and then
\[
 h_\eta=\sqrt{B_\eta/8},
 \qquad \tau_\eta=\frac{B_\eta}{2L^2}.
\]
Because \(0<\delta<1/2\), \(0<m_2<1\), and \(L=3\), these choices satisfy \(0<e_\eta<1/2\), \(0<h_\eta<1/4\), and \(0<\tau_\eta<1\).  On \([0,L]\), set
\[
 p_\eta(x)=(1-\tau_\eta)
 \left\{(1-e_\eta)h_\eta^{-1}\beta(x/h_\eta)
 +e_\eta h_\eta^{-1}\beta((L-x)/h_\eta)\right\}
 +\frac{\tau_\eta}{L}. \tag{15}
\]
The bump terms in (15) are zero off their indicated endpoint bands.  Let \(X\) have density \(p_\eta\), let \(N\) have density \(b\), take \(X\perp N\), and set
\[
 Y=X+N. \tag{16}
\]
The node domains are \([0,L]\) and \([-1,L+1]\), and the generating graph is \(X\to Y\).

We verify membership in \(\mathcal M_0(2)\), including its identification restriction.  The functions \(b\), \(\beta\), and \(p_\eta\) are \(C^\infty\) on their relative interiors; \(b\) is positive on \((-1,1)\), and the bridge term makes \(p_\eta\) positive on all of \((0,L)\).  Equation (16) has a nonconstant \(C^\infty\) mechanism, independent innovations, and
\[
 [0,L]+[-1,1]=[-1,L+1],
\]
so Assumptions \(\ref{ass:anm-independence}\), \(\ref{ass:smooth-mechanisms}\), \(\ref{ass:smooth-positive-densities}\), \(\ref{ass:support-closure}\), and \(\ref{ass:causal-minimality}\) hold.  The root support and every bivariate conditional-support fiber have connected relative interior.  Both variances are positive, so Assumption \(\ref{ass:variance-nondegeneracy}\) holds for this model with any positive lower bound smaller than their minimum.

It remains to check the Condition 19 configuration.  Write \(\xi=\log p_\eta\), \(\nu=\log b\), and
\[
 \Lambda(x,y)=\xi(x)+\nu(y-x)
\]
on the interior of the joint support.  If a reverse additive-noise representation existed locally, then for some functions \(g\), \(r\), and \(s\) its log-density would have the form
\[
 \Lambda(x,y)=r(x-g(y))+s(y).
\]
At points where \(\Lambda_{xx}\ne0\), direct differentiation would give
\[
 \frac{\Lambda_{xy}}{\Lambda_{xx}}=-g'(y),
 \qquad
 \partial_x\left(\frac{\Lambda_{xy}}{\Lambda_{xx}}\right)=0. \tag{17}
\]
Thus failure of (17) is precisely the required local differential obstruction.  For the forward density,
\[
 \Lambda_{xx}=\xi''(x)+\nu''(y-x),
 \qquad \Lambda_{xy}=-\nu''(y-x).
\]
Since \(b\) is symmetric, \(\nu'''(0)=0\), while direct differentiation of \(\nu(z)=\log c_b-1/(1-z^2)\) gives \(\nu''(0)=-2\ne0\).  Hence at \(y=x\), whenever the denominator is nonzero,
\[
 \partial_x\left(\frac{\Lambda_{xy}}{\Lambda_{xx}}\right)
 =\frac{\nu''(0)\xi'''(x)}{\{\xi''(x)+\nu''(0)\}^2}. \tag{18}
\]
There exists \(x\in(0,h_\eta)\) for which the right side of (18) is nonzero.  Indeed, if \(\xi'''\) vanished throughout \((0,h_\eta)\), then \(\xi\) would be quadratic there.  Flatness of the bump at \(h_\eta\) gives \(\xi'(h_\eta-)=\xi''(h_\eta-)=0\), forcing that quadratic to be constant, contrary to the nonconstant bump in (15).  Thus the open set \(\{x:\xi'''(x)\ne0\}\) is nonempty.  The identity \(\xi''(x)+\nu''(0)=0\) cannot hold throughout any component of that open set, because differentiating it would give \(\xi'''=0\).  This proves the assertion.  Continuity makes (18) nonzero on an interior open patch.  Assumption \(\ref{ass:condition19-configurations}\) therefore holds, as does Assumption \(\ref{ass:connected-support-fibers}\).  The constructed law belongs to \(\mathcal M_0(2)\), and Lemma \(\ref{lem:compact-identifiability}\) identifies its labelled graph as \(X\to Y\).

We next bound the two correlation ratios.  Couple \(X\) to \(X_0\in\{0,L\}\) as follows.  On the two bump components use their corresponding endpoint; on the bridge component draw \(X_0=L\) with probability \(e_\eta\), independently of the bridge draw, and otherwise take \(X_0=0\).  Then \(\mathbb P(X_0=L)=e_\eta\), \(\operatorname{Var}(X_0)=v_\eta\), and
\[
 \mathbb E(X-X_0)^2
 \le h_\eta^2+L^2\tau_\eta
 =\frac{5B_\eta}{8}<B_\eta. \tag{19}
\]
The reverse triangle inequality in \(L^2\), followed by Cauchy--Schwarz, yields
\[
 \left|\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(X)}-\sqrt{v_\eta}\right|
 \le \left\|(X-\mathbb EX)-(X_0-\mathbb EX_0)\right\|_2
 \le2\|X-X_0\|_2<2\sqrt{B_\eta}. \tag{20}
\]
Since \(B_\eta/v_\eta=\delta/256<1/512\), (20) implies the convenient bounds
\[
 \frac12v_\eta<\operatorname{Var}(X)<2v_\eta. \tag{21}
\]
Independence and \(\mathbb EN=0\) give \(\mathbb E[Y\mid X]=X\) and \(\operatorname{Var}(Y)=\operatorname{Var}(X)+m_2\).  Definitions \(\ref{def:conditional-risk}\) and \(\ref{def:correlation-ratio}\), followed by (21), therefore give
\[
 q(Y,\{X\})
 =\frac{\operatorname{Var}(X)}{\operatorname{Var}(X)+m_2}
 \le\frac{\operatorname{Var}(X)}{m_2}
 <\frac{2v_\eta}{m_2}
 \le\frac{2L^2e_\eta}{m_2}
 =\frac{\delta}{32}. \tag{22}
\]

For the reverse prediction problem, use
\[
 \widehat X(Y)=0\quad\text{if }Y<L/2,
 \qquad
 \widehat X(Y)=L\quad\text{if }Y\ge L/2.
\]
Because \(h_\eta<1/4\) and the noise support is \([-1,1]\), this rule classifies both bump components correctly.  Its squared error is at most \(h_\eta^2\) on them and at most \(L^2\) on the bridge.  Conditional-mean optimality in Definition \(\ref{def:conditional-risk}\), (19), and (21) imply
\[
 R_{X,\{Y\}}=\mathbb E\operatorname{Var}(X\mid Y)
 \le h_\eta^2+L^2\tau_\eta<B_\eta,
\]
and consequently
\[
 1-q(X,\{Y\})
 =\frac{R_{X,\{Y\}}}{\operatorname{Var}(X)}
 <\frac{2B_\eta}{v_\eta}
 =\frac{\delta}{128}. \tag{23}
\]
Combining (22) and (23),
\[
 \frac12q(X,\{Y\})-q(Y,\{X\})
 >\frac12-\frac{\delta}{256}-\frac{\delta}{32}
 =\frac12-\frac{9\delta}{256}
 >\frac12-\delta=\eta. \tag{24}
\]

We now thicken the construction by primitive strict inequalities.  This also makes the meaning of relative openness explicit.  On each fixed graph stratum, transport every compact interval affinely to a reference interval and use the product of: Euclidean convergence of support endpoints, uniform \(C^1\) and compact-open \(C^3\) convergence of mechanisms, and \(L^1\) plus compact-open \(C^3\) convergence of innovation densities.  Give the finitely many graph strata the discrete topology.  This is the structural topology used below.

For a two-node model in the \(X\to Y\) stratum, write the root and child-noise support intervals as \([a,b]\) and \([c,d]\), write the mechanism as \(g\), and put \(r_\eta=2h_\eta\).  Define the endpoint bands
\[
 A_0=[a,a+r_\eta],\qquad A_1=[b-r_\eta,b],
\]
whenever \(b-a>2r_\eta\), and define
\[
 B_{\mathrm{cl}}=r_\eta^2+(b-a)^2
 \mathbb P\{X\notin A_0\cup A_1\}. \tag{25}
\]
Let \(\mathcal U_\eta\) consist of the members of \(\mathcal M_0(2)\) in this stratum for which
\[
 \inf_{x\in[a,b]}g'(x)>0, \tag{26}
\]
\[
 \sup_{x\in A_0}g(x)+d
 <\inf_{x\in A_1}g(x)+c, \tag{27}
\]
\[
 \frac{B_{\mathrm{cl}}}{\operatorname{Var}(X)}<\frac{\delta}{16}, \tag{28}
\]
and
\[
 \frac{\operatorname{Var}\{g(X)\}}
 {\operatorname{Var}\{g(X)\}+\operatorname{Var}(N)}
 <\frac{\delta}{16}. \tag{29}
\]
These conditions expose the variables and all divisions: the denominators in (28) and (29) are positive by Assumption \(\ref{ass:variance-nondegeneracy}\), and child-noise variance is positive because its density is positive on a nondegenerate interval.

The set \(\mathcal U_\eta\) is relatively open.  Conditions (26)--(29) are strict; extrema in (26)--(27), probabilities and variances in (25), (28), and (29), and their positive-denominator ratios are continuous in the stated topology.  At the reference model (15)--(16), the mass outside \([0,2h_\eta]\cup[L-2h_\eta,L]\) is at most \(\tau_\eta\), so
\[
 B_{\mathrm{cl}}\le4h_\eta^2+L^2\tau_\eta=B_\eta.
\]
Equations (21)--(22) imply (28)--(29).  Moreover, (26) holds with derivative one, and (27) follows from
\[
 2h_\eta+1<L-2h_\eta-1,
\]
which is equivalent to \(h_\eta<1/4\).  Thus the reference law lies in \(\mathcal U_\eta\), proving that this relatively open set is nonempty.

Every law in \(\mathcal U_\eta\) obeys the desired strict margin by the same two elementary risk bounds, without appealing to continuity of the score.  Choose a threshold strictly between the two sides of (27), predict \(a\) below it and \(b\) above it.  On \(A_0\cup A_1\) the squared error is at most \(r_\eta^2\), and outside it the squared error is at most \((b-a)^2\).  Hence (25), conditional-mean optimality, and (28) give
\[
 1-q(X,\{Y\})
 =\frac{R_{X,\{Y\}}}{\operatorname{Var}(X)}
 \le\frac{B_{\mathrm{cl}}}{\operatorname{Var}(X)}
 <\frac{\delta}{16}. \tag{30}
\]
By independent additive noise,
\[
 q(Y,\{X\})
 =\frac{\operatorname{Var}\{g(X)\}}
 {\operatorname{Var}\{g(X)\}+\operatorname{Var}(N)}
 <\frac{\delta}{16}. \tag{31}
\]
Equations (30)--(31) yield
\[
 \frac12q(X,\{Y\})-q(Y,\{X\})
 >\frac12-\frac{\delta}{32}-\frac{\delta}{16}
 =\frac12-\frac{3\delta}{32}>\eta. \tag{32}
\]

Reverse residual dependence holds at every point of \(\mathcal U_\eta\) by support geometry, not by continuity of an independence indicator.  Positivity of the two densities on their interval interiors and strict increase in (26) imply that, for
\[
 y\in\bigl(g(a)+c,\,\min\{g(a)+d,g(b)+c\}\bigr),
\]
an interval of positive \(P_Y\)-measure, the relative interior of the conditional support is
\[
 \operatorname{supp}(X\mid Y=y)^\circ
 =\bigl(a,g^{-1}(y-c)\bigr). \tag{33}
\]
Its diameter \(g^{-1}(y-c)-a\) is strictly increasing in \(y\).  Subtracting \(\mathbb E[X\mid Y=y]\) translates (33) and does not alter its diameter.  If \(X-\mathbb E[X\mid Y]\) were independent of \(Y\), its regular conditional distribution, hence its closed support and support diameter, would be the same for \(P_Y\)-almost every \(y\), contradicting (33).  Assumption \(\ref{ass:characteristic-kernels}\) and Definition \(\ref{def:hsic}\) therefore give
\[
 H(X,\{Y\},Y)>0 \tag{34}
\]
throughout \(\mathcal U_\eta\).  No continuity at the independence boundary is asserted or needed.

The family genuinely lies in the broad nonlinear-ANM regime.  For example, replace the identity mechanism at the reference point by
\[
 g_s(x)=x+s x(L-x).
\]
For every sufficiently small nonzero \(s\), this mechanism is non-affine and \(C^3\), maps \([0,L]\) monotonically onto itself, preserves (26)--(29), and preserves the strict differential failure (18) by compact-open \(C^3\) continuity.  Hence these perturbations remain in \(\mathcal U_\eta\cap\mathcal M_0(2)\).  The affine mechanisms form a closed subset in the declared structural topology: if \(g_m(x)=a_mx+b_m\) converges in uniform \(C^1\), then \(g_m'\) converges uniformly to \(g'\), and because every \(g_m'\) is constant, \(g'\) is constant, so \(g\) is affine.  Therefore the complement of the affine mechanisms is relatively open.  Intersecting that complement with \(\mathcal U_\eta\) gives the asserted nonempty relatively open non-affine subfamily.

It remains to translate the inequalities into the GENE landscape.  In the causal order \((X,Y)\), the residual of \(Y\) on \(X\) is \(N-\mathbb EN\), independent of \(X\), so Definitions \(\ref{def:hsic}\)--\(\ref{def:gene-score}\) give
\[
 S_P(X,Y)=q(Y,\{X\}). \tag{35}
\]
In the reverse order, (34) makes the dependence count equal to one, and therefore
\[
 S_P(Y,X)=\frac12q(X,\{Y\}). \tag{36}
\]
By (32), (36) is strictly larger than (35).  There are only two labelled orders, so \((Y,X)\) is the unique global maximizer and hence the unique insertion-local maximizer, even though Lemma \(\ref{lem:compact-identifiability}\) identifies \(X\to Y\) for every member of \(\mathcal U_\eta\).

Finally, Definition \(\ref{def:correlation-ratio}\) gives \(0\le q\le1\).  Consequently every nondegenerate bivariate law satisfies
\[
 \frac12q(X,\{Y\})-q(Y,\{X\})\le\frac12. \tag{37}
\]
For every \(\eta<1/2\), (32) supplies a member of \(\mathcal M_0(2)\) with generating graph \(X\to Y\) for which the difference is larger than \(\eta\).  Thus the displayed supremum equals \(1/2\), proving sharpness.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This theorem certifies that observational ANM identifiability does not by itself control the exact GENE population landscape: for every desired margin below the universal one-half boundary, a structurally open family makes the wrong order uniquely optimal. \textit{Gap.} A fixed-margin witness would not establish the sharp \(1/2\) boundary, a margin-indexed relatively open family, or a relatively open non-affine subfamily; continuity of nonzero HSIC cannot be used because exact independence is discontinuous. \textit{Consumer.} Users of population or high-precision GENE optimization obtain an explicit sharp adversarial family and know that the matching-class success certificate cannot be omitted merely because the underlying ANM is identifiable.

\begin{lemma}[lem:peters-restricted-anm-identification]\label{lem:peters-restricted-anm-identification}
\texttt{For a distribution generated by a causally minimal restricted additive-\allowbreak{}noise model satisfying the Condition 19 configurations,\allowbreak{} the generating DAG is identifiable from the observational distribution.}
\end{lemma}

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The success certificate is sufficient only on matching-component DAGs and is not an observable necessary-and-sufficient boundary for general GENE landscapes. The sharp bivariate construction proves a margin-indexed relatively open failure region, including a relatively open non-affine subfamily, inside the identifiable nonlinear-ANM class and attains the universal one-half supremum only as a limit; it does not classify the intervening general class. Exact independence remains discontinuous: the population trap handles it pointwise by support geometry throughout its open structural family, while finite-sample inference uses set-valued labels. Under the matching class, separation only for score and pruning coordinates on the exact population execution gives \(\mathbb P\{G_0\in\widehat{\mathcal G}_n\}\ge1-\alpha\) even when widths are large, but does not separate coordinates reached only by alternative compatible paths. Singleton recovery therefore requires the stronger all-compatible-query separation promise as well as \(2w_S<\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}\) and \(2w_H<\lambda\). The generic branch's \(d=7\) elbow is caused by unrestricted \((d-1)\)-dimensional smoothing; it is not intrinsic to the matching recovery class, whose pairing-agnostic branch has structural regression dimension one. The residual-HSIC bound still uses the stated nonstandard residual-coordinate Lipschitz premise. Both sample-size orders suppress fixed regularity constants and are sufficient, not minimax optimal; no full-class recovery guarantee is made.

\section{Honest scope}

The delivered population results are a compact-support identification bridge, a matching-subclass insertion-navigation theorem, a six-node strict separation witness, finite-query oracle recovery, a quadratic positive-gain witness, and, for every \(\eta\in(0,1/2)\), a sharp relatively open bivariate trapped-order family with a nonempty relatively open non-affine subfamily. The wrong order is the unique global and hence insertion-local maximizer by more than \(\eta\), while the universal supremum of this score advantage is \(1/2\). The matching-subclass theorem does not establish success on Li et al.'s full nonlinear class; the nonlinear witnesses transfer to that class only existentially, and the trap does not contradict Theorem 4.3 because its global-ranking premise fails there. The simultaneous-band theorem retains generic ambient-dimensional rates, whose \(d=7\) elbow is a smoothing artifact, and proves a matching-adaptive, pairing-agnostic branch with \(a_{n,\mathrm{pair}}=t_n^{3/7}\), \(w_R\le K\{t_n^{1/2}+t_n^{6/7}\}\), \(w_H\le K\{t_n^{1/2}+t_n^{3/7}\}\), and \(w_S\le4d w_R/\underline v\). Under the stronger all-compatible-query separation promise, \(n\ge K\log(M_d/\alpha)\max\{(d/\delta_{\mathrm{pair}})^2,\lambda^{-7/3}\}\) is sufficient for singleton recovery on \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{pair}}(d)\). Exact-path separation alone still yields \(\mathbb P\{G_0\in\widehat{\mathcal G}_n\}\ge1-\alpha\) without width inequalities. Otherwise the compatible-DAG union may be singleton or multiple. No minimax rate, observable general-class boundary, full-class recovery claim, or claim against Li et al. Theorem 4.3 is made. The released Sachs workflow remains an audit case: a fixed chi-square threshold does not certify a population margin, and intervention-informed reference directions do not follow from an observational acyclic ANM alone.
