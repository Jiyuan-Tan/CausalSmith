Fix \(a\in\{0,1\}\), and define \(p_1(X)=\pi(X)\) and \(p_0(X)=1-\pi(X)\). Since \(Y_a\in[-1,1]\), the conditional mean \(m_a(X):=\mathbb E_P[Y_a\mid X]\) exists. By consistency, on the event \(\{A=a\}\) one has \(Y=Y_a\), and therefore
\[
 \mathbb E_P[\mathbf 1\{A=a\}Y\mid X]
 =\mathbb E_P[\mathbf 1\{A=a\}Y_a\mid X].
\]
Conditional exchangeability gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P[\mathbf 1\{A=a\}Y_a\mid X]
 =\mathbb E_P[Y_a\mid X]P(A=a\mid X)
 =m_a(X)p_a(X)
 \quad P\text{-almost surely}.
\]
On the other hand, the defining conditional-regression property of \(\mu_a\), followed by the tower property, gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P[\mathbf 1\{A=a\}Y\mid X]=p_a(X)\mu_a(X)
 \quad P\text{-almost surely}.
\]
Combining the last three displays yields
\[
 p_a(X)\{\mu_a(X)-m_a(X)\}=0
 \quad P\text{-almost surely}.
\]
The overlap assumption implies \(p_a(X)\ge e_0>0\) almost surely for both \(a=0\) and \(a=1\); division is therefore legitimate and proves
\[
 \mu_a(X)=\mathbb E_P[Y_a\mid X]
 \quad P\text{-almost surely}.
\]
By linearity of conditional expectation,
\[
 \mu_1(X)-\mu_0(X)
 =\mathbb E_P[Y_1-Y_0\mid X]
 \quad P\text{-almost surely}.
\]

It remains to justify pointwise evaluation. By the definition of \(\tau\), it is a representative of \(\mu_1-\mu_0\), and the effect-H\"older assumption makes it continuous on \([0,1]^d\). Suppose that \(f\) and \(g\) are two continuous representatives of the same \(P_X\)-almost-sure equivalence class. If \(f(x)\ne g(x)\) at some \(x\in[0,1]^d\), continuity supplies a nonempty relatively open neighborhood \(U\) of \(x\) on which \(|f-g|>0\). Under \(P_X=\operatorname{Unif}([0,1]^d)\), every such \(U\) has strictly positive \(P_X\)-probability, contradicting \(f=g\) \(P_X\)-almost surely. Thus the continuous representative is unique on all of \([0,1]^d\).

The observed law determines \(\mu_1-\mu_0\) as a \(P_X\)-almost-sure equivalence class by the displayed regression identities, and the uniform-design support property determines its unique continuous representative \(\tau\), hence its value at the fixed interior point \(x_0\). Consequently \(\theta(P)=\tau_P(x_0)\) is identified, and the notation \(\mathbb E_P[Y_1-Y_0\mid X=x_0]\) denotes this unique continuous representative evaluated at \(x_0\).
