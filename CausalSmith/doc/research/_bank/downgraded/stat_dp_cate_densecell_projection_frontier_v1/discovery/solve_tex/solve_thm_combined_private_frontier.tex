%%% FIELD proof-two-fiber
Fix a sufficiently small \(\delta>0\).  All constants below depend only on \(d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L,e_0\).  Choose
\[
 h=c_h\delta^{1/\gamma},\qquad \ell=c_\ell\delta^{1/q}.
\]
Because \(q<\gamma\), after decreasing \(\delta\) we have \(0<\ell\le h\le1/4\).  Let \(g\) be a nonnegative smooth bump supported on \([-1,1]^d\), with \(g(0)=1\), and define
\[
 g_h(x)=g\!\left(\frac{x-x_0}{h}\right),\qquad G_h(x)=g_h(x)^2.
\]
The support of \(G_h\) lies in \([0,1]^d\), because \(x_0\in[1/4,3/4]^d\) and \(h\le1/4\).  Choose a smooth \(\eta\) that is strictly positive on \([-1/2,1/2]^d\) and vanishes outside \([-1,1]^d\), and translate it on the lattice \(\ell\mathbb Z^d\).  These translates cover \(\mathbb R^d\), at most \(3^d\) are nonzero at any point, and their squared sum is bounded below by a positive constant.  For the lattice points \(z_j\), put
\[
 w_j(x)=\frac{\eta((x-z_j)/\ell)}{\left\{\sum_s\eta((x-z_s)/\ell)^2\right\}^{1/2}},
 \qquad \phi_j(x)=g_h(x)w_j(x).
\]
Only finitely many \(\phi_j\)'s meet the support of \(g_h\).  The denominator is bounded away from zero, the overlap multiplicity is bounded by a constant \(M=M(d)\), and direct differentiation gives, for every fixed \(s>0\),
\[
 \sum_j\phi_j(x)^2=G_h(x),\qquad
 \sup_j\|\phi_j\|_{\mathcal H^s}\le C_s\ell^{-s}.
 \tag{1}
\]
For independent Rademacher variables \(\lambda_j\), define
\[
 f_\lambda(x)=\sum_j\lambda_j\phi_j(x).
\]
Bounded overlap and (1) imply
\[
 |f_\lambda(x)|\le C,\qquad
 \|f_\lambda\|_{\mathcal H^s}\le C_s\ell^{-s},\qquad
 \mathbb E_\lambda f_\lambda(x)=0,\qquad
 \mathbb E_\lambda f_\lambda(x)^2=G_h(x).
 \tag{2}
\]

Choose positive constants \(c_a,c_b,c_\ell\) so that
\[
 a=c_a\ell^\alpha,\qquad b=c_b\ell^\beta,\qquad 4ab=\delta.
 \tag{3}
\]
This is possible because \(q=\alpha+\beta\): first take \(c_\ell\) sufficiently large, and then take \(c_a,c_b\) sufficiently small with \(4c_ac_bc_\ell^q=1\).  If \(\alpha\ge\beta\), define two laws, indexed by \(\lambda\), through
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 &\pi&\mu_0&\tau\\ \hline
 P_\lambda&1/2&1/2+b f_\lambda-\delta G_h/2&\delta G_h\\
 Q_\lambda&1/2+a f_\lambda&1/2+b f_\lambda&0.
\end{array}
\tag{4}
\]
If \(\beta>\alpha\), use instead
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 &\pi&\mu_0&\tau\\ \hline
 P_\lambda&1/2+a f_\lambda&1/2-\delta G_h/2&\delta G_h\\
 Q_\lambda&1/2+a f_\lambda&1/2+b f_\lambda&0.
\end{array}
\tag{5}
\]
In every law, let \(X\) be uniform on \([0,1]^d\).  Conditional on \(X=x\), draw \(A\) with the displayed propensity; draw \(Y_0,Y_1\in\{0,1\}\), conditionally independently of \(A\), with respective means \(\mu_0(x)\) and \(\mu_0(x)+\tau(x)\); and set \(Y=AY_1+(1-A)Y_0\).  Thus consistency and conditional exchangeability hold.  Equations (1)--(3) give
\[
 \|a f_\lambda\|_{\mathcal H^\alpha}\le Cc_a,\qquad
 \|b f_\lambda\|_{\mathcal H^\beta}\le Cc_b,\qquad
 \|\delta G_h\|_{\mathcal H^\gamma}\le C\delta h^{-\gamma}=Cc_h^{-\gamma},
 \qquad
 \|\delta G_h\|_{\mathcal H^\beta}\le C\delta h^{-\beta}
 =C\delta^{1-\beta/\gamma}.
 \tag{6}
\]
Here \(\beta<\gamma\) follows from \(\beta<q<\gamma\), so the final exponent is positive.  The fixed constants and then the upper threshold for \(\delta\) can be chosen so that the displayed bounds, including the fixed constant parts of the Hölder norms, are at most \(L\).  The same choices keep every displayed propensity and regression in \([1/4,3/4]\), hence give the required overlap and bounded outcomes.  Therefore every law in (4) and (5) belongs to \(\mathcal M_H\).  Since \(G_h(x_0)=1\),
\[
 \theta(P_\lambda)=\delta,\qquad \theta(Q_\lambda)=0.
 \tag{7}
\]

We next prove the two predictive-distance assertions.  Write \(s=2A-1\) and \(z=2Y-1\).  Conditional on \(X=x\), let \(F^P_\lambda(s,z\mid x)\) and \(F^Q_\lambda(s,z\mid x)\) denote four times the corresponding conditional atom masses.  From (4), when \(\alpha\ge\beta\), direct multiplication gives
\[
 F^P_\lambda=1+2zb f_\lambda+sz\delta G_h,
 \qquad
 F^Q_\lambda=1+2sa f_\lambda+2zb f_\lambda+4szab f_\lambda^2.
 \tag{8}
\]
From (5), when \(\beta>\alpha\), it gives
\[
 F^P_\lambda=1+2sa f_\lambda+sz\delta G_h+2za\delta f_\lambda G_h,
 \qquad
 F^Q_\lambda=1+2sa f_\lambda+2zb f_\lambda+4szab f_\lambda^2.
 \tag{9}
\]
By (2) and \(4ab=\delta\), both cases satisfy the exact identity
\[
 \mathbb E_\lambda F^P_\lambda(s,z\mid x)
 =\mathbb E_\lambda F^Q_\lambda(s,z\mid x)
 =1+sz\delta G_h(x).
 \tag{10}
\]
Since the covariate law is also common, (10) proves equality of the one-record prior predictives.

Condition now on \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\).  Define the active-sign set
\[
 J_i=\{j:\phi_j(x_i)\ne0\},
\]
and join \(i\) and \(r\) when \(J_i\cap J_r\ne\varnothing\).  If \(C\) and \(C'\) are distinct connected components, then \(\bigcup_{i\in C}J_i\) and \(\bigcup_{r\in C'}J_r\) are disjoint.  Independence of the Rademacher signs therefore yields the conditional factorization
\[
 \mathbb E_\lambda\prod_{i=1}^nF^P_\lambda(s_i,z_i\mid x_i)
 =\prod_C\mathbb E_\lambda\prod_{i\in C}F^P_\lambda(s_i,z_i\mid x_i),
 \tag{11}
\]
and the identical formula for \(Q\).

We give the component calculation explicitly.  For a record \(i\), let
\[
 \overline F_i=1+s_i z_i\delta G_h(x_i),\qquad
 V_i^P=F_i^P-\overline F_i,\qquad V_i^Q=F_i^Q-\overline F_i.
\]
Then \(\mathbb E_\lambda V_i^P=\mathbb E_\lambda V_i^Q=0\) by (10).  Put
\[
 u=a\vee b,\qquad w=a\wedge b.
\]
If \(\alpha\ge\beta\), then \(u=b,w=a\); if \(\beta>\alpha\), then \(u=a,w=b\).  Equations (2), (8), and (9), together with \(\delta=4uw\) and the smallness of \(u\), imply in both cases
\[
 |V_i^P|\vee|V_i^Q|\le C u,\qquad
 |V_i^P-V_i^Q|\le Cw,\qquad
 |\overline F_i|\le1+\delta.
 \tag{12}
\]
Indeed, in (8) the difference is \(-2s_i a f_i-s_i z_i\delta(f_i^2-G_i)\), which is \(O(a)\), while in (9) it is \(2z_i a\delta f_iG_i-2z_i b f_i-s_i z_i\delta(f_i^2-G_i)\), which is \(O(b)\).

Let \(C\) have size \(m\), and fix its \(4^m\) possible vectors \(\omega_C=((s_i,z_i):i\in C)\).  Expanding the product around its common one-record mean gives
\[
 4^m\overline p_C(\omega_C\mid x_C)
 =\prod_{i\in C}\overline F_i+
 \sum_{\substack{I\subseteq C\\ |I|\ge2}}
 \left(\prod_{i\in C\setminus I}\overline F_i\right)
 \mathbb E_\lambda\prod_{i\in I}V_i^P,
 \tag{13}
\]
and the same expansion with \(P\) replaced by \(Q\).  There are no terms with \(|I|=1\) because each centered factor has mean zero.  For \(|I|=r\ge2\), telescoping the two products and applying (12) yields
\[
 \left|\mathbb E_\lambda\prod_{i\in I}V_i^P-
 \mathbb E_\lambda\prod_{i\in I}V_i^Q\right|
 \le r C^r w u^{r-1}
 =\frac{\delta}{4}r C^r u^{r-2}.
 \tag{14}
\]
Thus every surviving difference is charged to at least two distinct records and contains the factor \(uw=\delta/4\).  Summing (14) in (13) gives, for a fixed constant \(K_1\),
\[
 4^m|\overline p_C(\omega_C\mid x_C)-\overline q_C(\omega_C\mid x_C)|
 \le C\delta(m)_2K_1^m.
 \tag{15}
\]
Summation over the \(4^m\) atoms therefore proves
\[
 \|\overline p_C-\overline q_C\|_1
 \le C\delta(m)_2K_1^m,\qquad
 d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\overline p_C,\overline q_C)
 \le C\delta(m)_2K_1^m.
 \tag{16}
\]

The fixed constants in (3) may also be chosen so that every factor in (8) and (9) is at least \(1/2\).  Consequently every conditional \(Q\)-mixture atom obeys
\[
 \overline q_C(\omega_C\mid x_C)\ge 8^{-m}.
 \tag{17}
\]
Combining the atomwise bound (15) with (17), and summing over \(4^m\) atoms, gives a fixed \(K_2\) such that
\[
 \chi^2(\overline p_C,\overline q_C)
 =\sum_{\omega_C}\frac{(\overline p_C-\overline q_C)^2}{\overline q_C}
 \le C\delta^2m^4K_2^m.
 \tag{18}
\]
The elementary inequality \((\sqrt{x}-\sqrt{y})^2\le(x-y)^2/y\) also gives \(H^2(\overline p_C,\overline q_C)\le\chi^2(\overline p_C,\overline q_C)\).  This is the required componentwise quadratic, rather than linear, bound.

It remains to average the component sizes.  A nonempty \(J_i\) requires \(x_i\) to lie in a set of volume at most \(Ch^d\).  Moreover, bounded support of \(\eta\) implies that, for any fixed \(x_i\), the set of \(x_r\) for which \(J_i\cap J_r\ne\varnothing\) has volume at most \(C\ell^d\).  Put \(p_\ell=C\ell^d\).  Conditional on \(X_i=x_i\), the number \(Z_i\) of other records in this overlap neighborhood is stochastically bounded by \(\operatorname{Bin}(n-1,p_\ell)\).  Consequently, for every fixed \(b>1\),
\[
 \mathbb E[b^{Z_i}\mid X_i=x_i]
 \le(1-p_\ell+p_\ell b)^{n-1}
 \le\exp\{(b-1)(n-1)p_\ell\}.
 \tag{19}
\]
Thus the required direct-neighborhood exponential moments are uniform when \(n\ell^d\) is sufficiently small.

We also control transitive overlap rather than replacing it by direct overlap.  Let \(M_i\) be the size of the connected component containing record \(i\).  On the event \(J_i\ne\varnothing\) and \(M_i\ge m\), there are \(m-1\) other record indices and a rooted spanning tree on these \(m\) indices all of whose edges are overlap edges.  There are at most \(m^{m-2}\) labeled trees: repeatedly delete the smallest labeled leaf and record its neighbor; the resulting word of length \(m-2\) on an \(m\)-letter alphabet reconstructs the tree, so the encoding is injective.  Orienting a fixed tree away from its root and integrating successively over its leaves shows
\[
 \Pr\!\left(J_i\ne\varnothing,\ \text{all edges of the fixed tree occur}\right)
 \le Ch^d p_\ell^{m-1}.
 \tag{20}
\]
Indeed, the root must be in the macro support, of mass at most \(Ch^d\), and each child must lie in its parent's overlap neighborhood, of conditional mass at most \(p_\ell\).  A union bound over the index sets and trees therefore gives
\[
\begin{split}
 \Pr(J_i\ne\varnothing,M_i\ge m)
 &\le Ch^d\binom{n-1}{m-1}m^{m-2}p_\ell^{m-1}\\
 &\le Ch^d(C_0n\ell^d)^{m-1},\qquad m\ge2,
\end{split}
 \tag{21}
\]
where the second line uses \(\binom{n-1}{m-1}\le n^{m-1}/(m-1)!\) and \(m^{m-2}/(m-1)!\le C_0^{m-1}\).  Equation (21) is the iterated counterpart of the binomial exponential-moment bound (19).

Fix \(K\in\{K_1,K_2\}\) and a nonnegative integer \(a\).  Since \(\Pr(M_i=m,J_i\ne\varnothing)\le\Pr(M_i\ge m,J_i\ne\varnothing)\), (21) yields
\[
\begin{split}
 &\mathbb E\!\left[
 \mathbf 1\{J_i\ne\varnothing,M_i\ge2\}M_i^aK^{M_i}
 \right]\\
 &\qquad\le Ch^d\sum_{m\ge2}m^aK^m(C_0n\ell^d)^{m-1}
 \le Ch^d n\ell^d,
\end{split}
 \tag{22}
\]
provided \(n\ell^d\le c_0\), where \(c_0\) is chosen so that \(C_0Kc_0<1/2\).  This choice depends only on the fixed model constants and proves the needed occupancy-weighted exponential-moment estimate.

Given the covariates, couple the two mixture laws separately on each component by placing the pointwise minimum of their atom masses on the diagonal and coupling the two residual measures arbitrarily.  The residual mass is exactly the component total variation.  On the diagonal all records agree; off the diagonal, at most all \(m\) component records change.  By (16),
\[
 \mathbb E[d_{\mathrm{Ham}}(D_n,D_n')\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n]
 \le C\delta\sum_C\mathbf 1\{|C|\ge2\}|C|^3K_1^{|C|}.
 \tag{24}
\]
The sum on the right of (24) equals
\(\sum_i\mathbf 1\{J_i\ne\varnothing,M_i\ge2\}M_i^2K_1^{M_i}\).  Applying (22) to each record therefore gives
\[
 \mathbb E d_{\mathrm{Ham}}(D_n,D_n')
 \le C\delta\,n h^d\,(n\ell^d)
 =C\delta n^2h^d\ell^d.
 \tag{25}
\]

Finally, (11) makes the conditional laws products over components.  If \(A_C\) is the Hellinger affinity on component \(C\), then
\(1-\prod_CA_C\le\sum_C(1-A_C)\); hence squared Hellinger distance tensorizes subadditively.  Using (18), then writing the component sum as a sum over its records as above, yields
\[
 H^2(\overline P_n(\cdot\mid X),\overline Q_n(\cdot\mid X))
 \le C\delta^2\sum_C\mathbf 1\{|C|\ge2\}|C|^4K_2^{|C|}.
 \tag{26}
\]
The covariate distribution is the same under the two mixtures, so unconditional squared Hellinger distance is the expectation of the conditional squared Hellinger distance.  Applying (22) with the corresponding third power of \(M_i\) gives
\[
 H^2(\overline P_n,\overline Q_n)
 \le C\delta^2 n h^d(n\ell^d)
 =C n^2\delta^2h^d\ell^d.
 \tag{27}
\]
Equations (7), (10), (25), and (27), together with the verified class membership, prove all assertions of the lemma.

%%% FIELD proof-full-lower
Write \(m_G=1+d/\gamma\) and \(D_F=1+d/\gamma+d/q\).  All constants below are positive, depend only on \(d,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,L,e_0\), and may change from line to line.

First, privacy cannot reduce the nonprivate minimax risk.  For any pure \(\epsilon\)-differentially private Markov kernel \(T\) from \(\mathcal O^n\) to \([-2,2]\), define its conditional barycenter
\[
 \overline T(D_n)=\int_{[-2,2]}t\,T(dt\mid D_n).
\]
The bounded output support makes \(\overline T\) a nonprivate estimator taking values in \([-2,2]\).  Conditional Jensen's inequality gives, for every \(P\in\mathcal M_H\),
\[
 |\overline T(D_n)-\theta(P)|
 \le\int_{[-2,2]}|t-\theta(P)|\,T(dt\mid D_n).
\]
After expectation and supremization over \(P\), the definition of \(R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\) implies that the risk of \(T\) is at least \(R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\).  Infimizing over the private kernels in the definition of \(R_{n,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{DP}}\) gives
\[
 R_{n,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{DP}}\ge R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}.
 \tag{28}
\]

For the dense branch, fix a nonnegative smooth bump \(g\), supported on \([-1,1]^d\), with \(g(0)=1\).  For \(0<\Delta\le\Delta_0\), let
\[
 h=c_h\Delta^{1/\gamma},\qquad
 g_h(x)=g\!\left(\frac{x-x_0}{h}\right).
\]
Choose \(c_h\) so that \(\|\Delta g_h\|_{\mathcal H^\gamma}\le L\), and choose \(\Delta_0\) so that \(h\le1/4\) and \(0\le\Delta g_h\le1\).  Since \(x_0\) is interior, the bump is supported in \([0,1]^d\).  Let \(P_0,P_1\) have uniform \(X\), constant propensity \(1/2\), control regression zero, and effects
\[
 \tau_0=0,\qquad \tau_1=\Delta g_h.
\]
Given \(X\), draw binary potential outcomes in \(\{-1,1\}\), independently of \(A\), with the required conditional means, and impose consistency.  Constant propensity, zero control regression, the Hölder scaling, uniform design, and \(e_0\le1/4\) verify every defining property of \(\mathcal M_H\).  Moreover,
\[
 \theta(P_0)=0,\qquad \theta(P_1)=\Delta.
 \tag{29}
\]
Couple one record by using the same \(X,A\) and the monotone coupling of the two binary outcomes.  A mismatch is possible only for \(A=1\), and its conditional probability is \(\Delta g_h(X)/2\).  Therefore
\[
 \Pr(O_0\ne O_1)
 =\frac{\Delta}{4}\int g_h(x)\,dx
 \le C\Delta h^d.
\]
The coordinatewise product coupling has expected Hamming distance
\[
 D\le Cn\Delta h^d\le Cn\Delta^{m_G}.
 \tag{30}
\]
Under \(n\epsilon\ge1\), \(\rho_G=(n\epsilon)^{-1/m_G}\).  Set \(\Delta=c_G\rho_G\), with \(c_G\) small enough that \(\Delta\le\Delta_0\) and \(10\epsilon D\le1\).  Thresholding any private estimator at \(\Delta/2\) is post-processing and gives a pure \(\epsilon\)-private test.  The private Le Cam coupling lemma, with privacy slack zero, gives testing error at least \(0.45e^{-1}\).  By (29), every testing error entails absolute estimation loss at least \(\Delta/2\).  Hence
\[
 R_{n,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{DP}}\ge c\rho_G.
 \tag{31}
\]

For the sparse branch, first suppose \(\rho_F\ge\rho_G\).  Since \(n\epsilon\ge1\), \(n\ge1\), and \(\epsilon\le1\), both caps in the radius definitions are inactive, and
\[
 \rho_F^{D_F}=(n^2\epsilon)^{-1},
 \qquad \rho_G^{m_G}=(n\epsilon)^{-1}.
 \tag{32}
\]
We claim that \(n\rho_F^{d/q}\le1\).  Otherwise, (32) would imply
\[
 \rho_F^{m_G}
 =\frac{\rho_F^{D_F}}{\rho_F^{d/q}}
 <\frac1{n\epsilon}
 =\rho_G^{m_G},
\]
contradicting \(\rho_F\ge\rho_G\), since \(m_G>0\).  This proves the claim.

Set \(\Delta=c_F\rho_F\), with \(c_F>0\) sufficiently small.  The two-fiber distance certificate gives two priors on laws in \(\mathcal M_H\), supported respectively on \(\theta(P)=\Delta\) and \(\theta(P)=0\), at scales
\[
 h\asymp\Delta^{1/\gamma},\qquad
 \ell\asymp\Delta^{1/q}.
\]
The proved inequality \(n\rho_F^{d/q}\le1\) and a sufficiently small \(c_F\) ensure \(n\ell^d\le c_0\), so the certificate supplies a coupling of the prior-predictive samples with
\[
 D\le C\Delta n^2h^d\ell^d
 \le Cn^2\Delta^{D_F}.
 \tag{33}
\]
By (32), \(\epsilon D\le Cc_F^{D_F}\).  Choose \(c_F\) so that \(10\epsilon D\le1\).  Applying the private Le Cam coupling lemma to the two prior predictives and thresholding at \(\Delta/2\) then gives
\[
 R_{n,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{DP}}\ge c\Delta\ge c\rho_F.
 \tag{34}
\]
The construction in the certificate is precisely the asserted smooth shared-sign two-fiber witness and proves the stated Hamming, scale, and occupancy properties.  If \(\rho_F<\rho_G\), (31) already implies a constant multiple of \(\rho_F\).

Taking the smallest of the constants in (28), (31), and (34) proves
\[
 R_{n,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{DP}}
 \ge c_{\mathrm{lower}}\left(R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\vee\rho_G\vee\rho_F\right).
\]

Assume the affirmative premise in the proposed statement.  At \(r=C_{\mathrm{scale}}\rho_\star\), that premise supplies a finite score family whose members have replacement sensitivity at most one and whose weighted-shell functional satisfies \(\mathfrak W_r(Q)\le C_{\mathrm{shell}}\).  The shell-risk proposition therefore gives an explicitly data-dependent pure \(\epsilon\)-differentially private selector satisfying
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M_H}\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}
 |T_{\mathrm{shell}}-\theta(P)|
 \le(C_{\mathrm{shell}}+3)C_{\mathrm{scale}}\rho_\star.
 \tag{35}
\]
Thus one may take \(C_{\mathrm{upper}}=(C_{\mathrm{shell}}+3)C_{\mathrm{scale}}\).  Since this selector is one admissible kernel in the definition of private minimax risk, (35) gives
\[
 R_{n,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{DP}}\le C_{\mathrm{upper}}\rho_\star.
 \tag{36}
\]
The full-class lower-frontier theorem gives the reverse inequality
\[
 R_{n,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{DP}}\ge c_{\mathrm{lower}}\rho_\star.
 \tag{37}
\]
Substituting \(\rho_\star=R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\vee\rho_G\vee\rho_F\) into (36)--(37) proves the claimed conditional equivalence.  The only upper-bound input is the affirmative premise.  Because the shell-risk proposition depends on the target-shell sum rather than the grid or sieve cardinality, an assumed nuisance-sieve-independent value of \(C_{\mathrm{shell}}\) creates no nuisance-sieve logarithm.  Nothing in this reduction constructs the required scores or addresses adaptation.

For fixed \(\epsilon\in(0,1]\) and \(q<\gamma\),

 \rho_G\vee\rho_F=O(R_n^{\mathrm{NP}})
\]
unconditionally as \(n\to\infty\), where the implicit constant may depend on the fixed privacy budget.  If Open-ended Question~1 has an affirmative answer, the conditional frontier theorem consequently reduces to

No unconditional private upper bound is asserted by this proposition.

%%% FIELD proof-fixed
The nonprivate CATE rate floor gives a constant \(c>0\) such that
\[
 R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}
 \ge c\left\{n^{-1/(2+d/\gamma)}
 \vee n^{-2/(2+d/\gamma+d/q)}\right\}.
 \tag{38}
\]
Fix \(\epsilon\in(0,1]\), independently of \(n\).  The radius definitions and \(1\wedge x\le x\) give
\[
 \rho_G\le\epsilon^{-1/(1+d/\gamma)}n^{-1/(1+d/\gamma)},
 \qquad
 \rho_F\le\epsilon^{-1/(1+d/\gamma+d/q)}n^{-2/(1+d/\gamma+d/q)}.
 \tag{39}
\]
Since \(q>0\) and \(\gamma>0\),
\[
 \frac1{1+d/\gamma}>\frac1{2+d/\gamma},
 \qquad
 \frac2{1+d/\gamma+d/q}>\frac2{2+d/\gamma+d/q}.
 \tag{40}
\]
For \(n\ge1\), (39)--(40) bound each privacy radius by a fixed-budget constant times the corresponding term on the right of (38).  Hence \(\rho_G\vee\rho_F=O(R_n^{\mathrm{NP}})\) unconditionally.  The condition \(q<\gamma\) ensures that this comparison is made on the declared two-scale model class; the exponent inequalities themselves use only positivity.

Now additionally assume an affirmative answer to Open-ended Question~1.  The conditional frontier theorem and the just-proved comparison give
\[
 R_{n,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{DP}}
 \asymp R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\vee\rho_G\vee\rho_F
 \asymp R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}.
\]
This last step uses the open-ended premise, whereas the radius comparison does not.

%%% FIELD proof-kennedy-transfer
Let \(\mathfrak T_\epsilon\) be the set of Markov kernels from \(\mathcal O^n\) to \([-2,2]\) that are pure \(\epsilon\)-differentially private under replacement adjacency.  This is the estimator set in both private-risk definitions.  The exact-uniform subclass lemma gives \(\mathcal M_H\subseteq\mathcal P_K\).  Therefore, for every \(T\in\mathfrak T_\epsilon\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_K}\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n},T}|T(D_n)-\theta(P)|
 \ge
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M_H}\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n},T}|T(D_n)-\theta(P)|.
\]
Taking the infimum over the same set \(\mathfrak T_\epsilon\) preserves the inequality, so
\[
 R_{n,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{DP},K}\ge R_{n,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{DP}}.
 \tag{41}
\]
Define
\[
 r_{\mathrm{NP}}=n^{-1/(2+d/\gamma)}\vee n^{-2/(2+d/\gamma+d/q)}.
\]
The nonprivate CATE rate floor gives \(R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\ge c_{\mathrm{NP}}r_{\mathrm{NP}}\).  Throughout \(n\epsilon\ge1\), the full-class lower-frontier theorem and (41) consequently give
\[
\begin{split}
 R_{n,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{DP},K}
 &\ge c_{\mathrm{lower}}
 \left(R_n^{\mathrm{NP}}\vee\rho_G\vee\rho_F\right)\\
 &\ge c_{\mathrm{lower}}\min\{c_{\mathrm{NP}},1\}
 \left(r_{\mathrm{NP}}\vee\rho_G\vee\rho_F\right).
\end{split}
\]
Taking \(c_K=c_{\mathrm{lower}}\min\{c_{\mathrm{NP}},1\}>0\) proves the theorem.  The argument uses only class inclusion and a common estimator set, so it transfers no upper bound and invokes no attainability condition from Kennedy et al.'s Theorem~2.
