Fix the certified input \(\mathfrak u\), the returned class \(C\), its canonical representative \(R_C\), and \(P\in\mathcal M_{\mathcal I}^{\circ}\) as in the statement.  By the definition of the quotient returned in \(\mathrm{thm{:}sparse\text{-}simid\text{-}completeness}\), for every \(D\in C\), every outcome cell \(y\), and every \(p\in\mathcal M_{\mathcal I}^{\circ}\),
\[
 T_{D,y}(p)=T_{R_C,y}(p). \tag{45}
\]
By \(\mathrm{lem{:}common\text{-}law\text{-}redundancy}\), \(P\) is Markov to every \(D\in C\).  Also, \(P\) is strictly positive by \(\mathrm{def{:}exact\text{-}oracle\text{-}model}\).  Hence \(\mathrm{lem{:}dag\text{-}intervention\text{-}functional}\) applies to every recursive Markovian structural causal model compatible with \(D\) and having observational law \(P\), and gives
\[
 P_x^D(Y=y)=T_{D,y}(P). \tag{46}
\]
Taking \(p=P\) in (45), using (46), and using the definition \(\tau_{C,y}(p)=T_{R_C,y}(p)\) from \(\mathrm{def{:}plugin\text{-}estimator}\), we obtain
\[
 P_x^D(Y=y)=T_{D,y}(P)=T_{R_C,y}(P)=\tau_{C,y}(P),
 \qquad D\in C. \tag{47}
\]
Because this holds coordinatewise, \(P_x^D(Y)=\tau_C(P)\).  Thus the causal target is related to the observed-law functional through the DAG intervention formula and the certified quotient, rather than by definition.

By \(\mathrm{lem{:}truncated\text{-}vector\text{-}normalization}\),
\[
 B_{R_C,y}(P)>0\qquad\text{for every outcome cell }y. \tag{48}
\]
There are finitely many outcome cells because every \(\Omega_v\) is finite.  Therefore
\[
 b_\star:=\min_y B_{R_C,y}(P)>0. \tag{49}
\]
Each \(B_{R_C,y}\) is a polynomial by \(\mathrm{def{:}truncated\text{-}functional}\), hence is continuous.  The finite intersection of the corresponding continuity neighborhoods gives an open neighborhood \(U\) of \(P\) such that
\[
 \min_y B_{R_C,y}(p)>b_\star/2>0
 \qquad\text{for every }p\in U. \tag{50}
\]
On \(U\), every coordinate of \(\tau_C\) is a quotient of polynomials with nonzero denominator.  Consequently \(\tau_C\) is continuously differentiable on \(U\), with continuous Jacobian \(J_{\tau_C}\).

Let \(m=|\Omega|\), identify probability tables with vectors in \(\mathbb R^m\), let \(e_z\) be the coordinate vector associated with \(z\in\Omega\), and define
\[
 W_i=e_{Z_i}-P.
\]
By \(\mathrm{ass{:}iid\text{-}multinomial}\) and the definition of \(\widehat p_n\), the vectors \(W_i\) are independent and identically distributed and satisfy
\[
 \mathbb E_P W_i=0,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_P(W_iW_i^{\mathsf T})
 =\operatorname{diag}(P)-PP^{\mathsf T}=:\Sigma_P, \tag{51}
\]
as follows by computing the \((z,z')\) entry from
\(\mathbb E_P[\mathbf 1\{Z_i=z\}\mathbf 1\{Z_i=z'\}]\).  Moreover,
\[
 \sqrt n(\widehat p_n-P)=n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^n W_i. \tag{52}
\]
Since \(W_i\) has finite support, \(\mathbb E_P\|W_i\|_2^2<\infty\).  Applying \(\mathrm{lem{:}multivariate\text{-}iid\text{-}clt}\) to (51)--(52) yields
\[
 \sqrt n(\widehat p_n-P)\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,\Sigma_P), \tag{53}
\]
where singularity of \(\Sigma_P\), caused in particular by probability normalization, is allowed by that lemma.  Independence and (51) also imply
\[
 \mathbb E_P\|\widehat p_n-P\|_2^2
 =\frac{\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma_P)}{n}\longrightarrow0. \tag{54}
\]
Thus \(\widehat p_n\to P\) in probability.  Since \(U\) is a neighborhood of \(P\), (50) and (54) give
\[
 \Pr_P\!\left\{\min_yB_{R_C,y}(\widehat p_n)>0\right\}
 \geq \Pr_P\{\widehat p_n\in U\}\longrightarrow1. \tag{55}
\]
This proves the asserted high-probability well-definedness of the representative circuit.

On \(U\), differentiability gives the expansion
\[
 \tau_C(P+h)-\tau_C(P)=J_{\tau_C}(P)h+r(h),
 \qquad
 \frac{\|r(h)\|_2}{\|h\|_2}\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{as }h\to0. \tag{56}
\]
Equation (53) implies \(\sqrt n\|\widehat p_n-P\|_2=O_P(1)\), while (54) implies \(\widehat p_n-P=o_P(1)\).  Substitution into (56), on the event \(\{\widehat p_n\in U\}\), therefore gives
\[
 \sqrt n\,r(\widehat p_n-P)=o_P(1). \tag{57}
\]
Equivalently, this is the conclusion of \(\mathrm{lem{:}multivariate\text{-}delta\text{-}method}\) applied to (53) and the differentiable map \(\tau_C\) on \(U\).  On \(\{\widehat p_n\in U\}\), the estimator in \(\mathrm{def{:}plugin\text{-}estimator}\) equals \(\tau_C(\widehat p_n)\); by (55), its stipulated value off the denominator-positive event affects a set whose probability tends to zero.  Combining (53), (56), and (57) thus yields
\[
 \sqrt n\{\widehat\tau_{C,n}-\tau_C(P)\}
 \Rightarrow
 \mathcal N\!\left(0,V_C(P)\right),
 \qquad
 V_C(P):=J_{\tau_C}(P)\Sigma_PJ_{\tau_C}(P)^{\mathsf T}. \tag{58}
\]

The map \(p\mapsto\operatorname{diag}(p)-pp^{\mathsf T}\) is continuous, so (54) implies
\[
 \widehat\Sigma_n
 =\operatorname{diag}(\widehat p_n)-\widehat p_n\widehat p_n^{\mathsf T}
 \xrightarrow{P}\Sigma_P. \tag{59}
\]
Continuity of \(J_{\tau_C}\) on \(U\), (54), and \(\Pr_P(\widehat p_n\in U)\to1\) imply
\[
 J_{\tau_C}(\widehat p_n)\xrightarrow{P}J_{\tau_C}(P) \tag{60}
\]
on the denominator-positive branch.  By continuity of finite-dimensional matrix multiplication, (59)--(60), and the off-event convention in \(\mathrm{def{:}plugin\text{-}estimator}\),
\[
 \widehat V_{C,n}
 \xrightarrow{P}
 J_{\tau_C}(P)\Sigma_PJ_{\tau_C}(P)^{\mathsf T}
 =V_C(P). \tag{61}
\]

Now fix a contrast vector \(a\) and \(\alpha\in(0,1)\), and suppose, exactly as required in the theorem, that
\[
 v_{C,a}:=a^{\mathsf T}V_C(P)a>0. \tag{62}
\]
Taking the linear contrast in (58) and using (61) gives
\[
 \sqrt n\,a^{\mathsf T}\{\widehat\tau_{C,n}-\tau_C(P)\}
 \Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,v_{C,a}),
 \qquad
 a^{\mathsf T}\widehat V_{C,n}a\xrightarrow{P}v_{C,a}. \tag{63}
\]
The matrix \(\widehat\Sigma_n\) is positive semidefinite, so \(\widehat V_{C,n}\) is positive semidefinite on its defining event.  In addition, (62)--(63) show that \(a^{\mathsf T}\widehat V_{C,n}a>0\) with probability tending to one.  Applying \(\mathrm{lem{:}slutsky\text{-}scalar}\) to (63) therefore gives
\[
 \frac{\sqrt n\,a^{\mathsf T}\{\widehat\tau_{C,n}-\tau_C(P)\}}
 {\sqrt{a^{\mathsf T}\widehat V_{C,n}a}}
 \Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1). \tag{64}
\]
Let \(z_{1-\alpha/2}=\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha/2)\).  Since the standard normal distribution function is continuous and symmetric, (64) implies
\[
 \Pr_P\!\left(
 \left|
 \frac{\sqrt n\,a^{\mathsf T}\{\widehat\tau_{C,n}-\tau_C(P)\}}
 {\sqrt{a^{\mathsf T}\widehat V_{C,n}a}}
 \right|
 \leq z_{1-\alpha/2}
 \right)
 \longrightarrow 1-\alpha. \tag{65}
\]
Rearranging (65) proves the asserted Wald interval coverage.  Equation (47) shows that its center targets the common causal contrast \(a^{\mathsf T}P_x^D(Y)\) for every fixed \(D\in C\).

For the coordinate contrast \(a=e_y\), condition (62) is precisely
\[
 \sigma_{C,y}^2
 =e_y^{\mathsf T}J_{\tau_C}(P)\Sigma_PJ_{\tau_C}(P)^{\mathsf T}e_y>0. \tag{66}
\]
Using the fixed critical value \(1.96\) in (64) gives limiting coverage
\[
 \Pr\{-1.96\leq Z\leq1.96\}
 =2\Phi(1.96)-1
 =0.9500042097\ldots,
 \qquad Z\sim\mathcal N(0,1), \tag{67}
\]
which is the stated numerical corollary.

Finally, throughout the argument \(\mathcal I\), the returned quotient, \(C\), and \(R_C\) were fixed before observing \(Z_1,\ldots,Z_n\), or externally certified independently of that sample, exactly as required by the theorem.  If any of them is instead selected from the inference sample, the representative rational map may change across selection events; none of (53)--(67) accounts for that selection.  Accordingly, the proof establishes no post-selection coverage claim.
