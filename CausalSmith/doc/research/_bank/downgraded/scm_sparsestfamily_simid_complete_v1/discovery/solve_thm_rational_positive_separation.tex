%%% FIELD pairwise-positive-chamber-statement
Theorem 1 (pairwise positive-chamber decision).  For every finite input \(\mathfrak u\) accepted by \(\mathrm{def{:}certified\text{-}input}\), every \(D,D'\in\mathfrak D\), and every outcome cell \(y\), define, for each bit-zero conditional-independence statement \(c\),
\[
 h_c(p)=\sum_{g\in\mathcal C_0(\mathcal I,c)}g(p)^2.
\]
There is a terminating exact real-closed-field decision procedure for the sentence
\[
 \exists p\in\mathbb R^\Omega\left[
 \sum_{z\in\Omega}p_z=1
 \ \wedge\ 
 \bigwedge_{g\in\mathcal C_1(\mathcal I)}g(p)=0
 \ \wedge\ 
 \bigwedge_{z\in\Omega}p_z>0
 \ \wedge\ 
 \bigwedge_{c:\mathcal I(c)=0}h_c(p)>0
 \ \wedge\ 
 F_{D,D',y}(p)\neq0
 \right].
\]
Exactly one of the following certificates is returned.  If the sentence is feasible, the procedure returns an exact real-algebraic table \(p^\dagger\in\mathcal M_{\mathcal I}^{\circ}\cap\mathbb R_{\mathrm{alg}}^\Omega\), encoded by a rational univariate representation together with coordinate minimal polynomials and rational isolating or Thom data, such that
\[
 T_{D,y}(p^\dagger)\neq T_{D',y}(p^\dagger).
\]
If the sentence is infeasible, it returns a finite quantifier-elimination trace or Positivstellensatz identity proving
\[
 T_{D,y}(p)=T_{D',y}(p)
 \qquad\text{for every }p\in\mathcal M_{\mathcal I}^{\circ}.
\]
Thus feasibility is the complete pairwise separation criterion on the positive exact-oracle chamber, and infeasibility is the complete universal-equality criterion on that chamber.
%%% FIELD pairwise-positive-chamber-proof
Fix an input \(\mathfrak u\) accepted by \(\mathrm{def{:}certified\text{-}input}\), graphs \(D,D'\in\mathfrak D\), and an outcome cell \(y\).  For every conditional-independence statement \(c\) with \(\mathcal I(c)=0\), put
\[
 h_c(p)=\sum_{g\in\mathcal C_0(\mathcal I,c)}g(p)^2.
\tag{1}
\]
Define finite subsets of \(\mathbb Q[p_z:z\in\Omega]\) by
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E&=\mathcal C_1(\mathcal I)\cup
 \left\{\sum_{z\in\Omega}p_z-1\right\},\\
 G&=\{p_z:z\in\Omega\}\cup
 \{h_c:\mathcal I(c)=0\},\\
 H&=\{p_z:z\in\Omega\}\cup
 \{h_c:\mathcal I(c)=0\}\cup\{F_{D,D',y}\}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
These sets are finite because \(V\), each \(\Omega_v\), and the collection of conditional-independence statements on \(V\) are finite by \(\mathrm{def{:}certified\text{-}input}\).  They have rational coefficients: the minors in \(\mathcal C_1(\mathcal I)\) and \(\mathcal C_0(\mathcal I,c)\) have integer coefficients by their determinantal definitions, the normalization polynomial has integer coefficients, (1) is a finite sum of squares, and \(F_{D,D',y}\) is an integer polynomial by \(\mathrm{def{:}pairwise\text{-}cross\text{-}signature}\).

Use the notation \(S(E,G,H)\) of \(\mathrm{lem{:}semialgebraic\text{-}decision\text{-}with\text{-}certificates}\).  For every cell \(z\), the two conditions \(p_z\geq0\) from \(G\) and \(p_z\neq0\) from \(H\) are equivalent over \(\mathbb R\) to \(p_z>0\).  For every bit-zero statement \(c\), equation (1) implies \(h_c(p)\geq0\) for every real table \(p\), so the conditions \(h_c(p)\geq0\) and \(h_c(p)\neq0\) are equivalent to \(h_c(p)>0\).  The equalities indexed by \(E\) are exactly normalization and all bit-one oracle equalities, while the remaining inequation in \(H\) is \(F_{D,D',y}(p)\neq0\).  Therefore \(\mathrm{def{:}exact\text{-}oracle\text{-}model}\) gives the set identity
\[
 S(E,G,H)
 =\left\{p\in\mathcal M_{\mathcal I}^{\circ}:
 F_{D,D',y}(p)\neq0\right\}.
\tag{3}
\]

Apply \(\mathrm{lem{:}semialgebraic\text{-}decision\text{-}with\text{-}certificates}\) to the sets in (2), with \(k=|\Omega|\).  Its exact algorithm terminates and decides whether the set in (3) is empty.  The nonempty and empty cases are mutually exclusive and exhaustive.

Suppose first that the set in (3) is nonempty.  The decision lemma returns an exactly encoded point
\[
 p^\dagger\in S(E,G,H)\cap\mathbb R_{\mathrm{alg}}^\Omega,
\tag{4}
\]
represented by a rational univariate representation together with coordinate minimal polynomials and rational isolating or Thom data.  Combining (3) and (4) yields
\[
 p^\dagger\in\mathcal M_{\mathcal I}^{\circ}
 \cap\mathbb R_{\mathrm{alg}}^\Omega,
 \qquad
 F_{D,D',y}(p^\dagger)\neq0.
\tag{5}
\]
Every observed-table marginal occurring as a denominator factor in the truncated factorization is a sum over a nonempty collection of cells.  Strict positivity in \(\mathcal M_{\mathcal I}^{\circ}\), used in (5), makes each such marginal strictly positive.  By the denominator-recording rule in \(\mathrm{def{:}truncated\text{-}functional}\), the reduced denominators are products of the uncancelled positive marginal factors; hence
\[
 B_{D,y}(p^\dagger)>0,
 \qquad
 B_{D',y}(p^\dagger)>0.
\tag{6}
\]
The identity in \(\mathrm{def{:}pairwise\text{-}cross\text{-}signature}\), with division justified by (6), now gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 T_{D,y}(p^\dagger)-T_{D',y}(p^\dagger)
 &=\frac{A_{D,y}(p^\dagger)}{B_{D,y}(p^\dagger)}
   -\frac{A_{D',y}(p^\dagger)}{B_{D',y}(p^\dagger)}\\
 &=\frac{A_{D,y}(p^\dagger)B_{D',y}(p^\dagger)
          -A_{D',y}(p^\dagger)B_{D,y}(p^\dagger)}
         {B_{D,y}(p^\dagger)B_{D',y}(p^\dagger)}\\
 &=\frac{F_{D,D',y}(p^\dagger)}
         {B_{D,y}(p^\dagger)B_{D',y}(p^\dagger)}\neq0.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
Thus the feasible branch returns the required exact real-algebraic positive-chamber separator, and its encoding is checkable by the exact algebraic-number arithmetic guaranteed by the decision lemma.

Suppose instead that the set in (3) is empty.  The same decision lemma returns a finite identity
\[
 a_0+b_0+c_0^2=0,
 \qquad
 a_0\in\langle E\rangle,
 \quad b_0\in\operatorname{PO}(G),
 \quad c_0\in\operatorname{Mon}(H),
\tag{8}
\]
over a finite real algebraic extension of \(\mathbb Q\); equivalently, a finite quantifier-elimination trace may certify the same infeasibility conclusion.  By (3), exact infeasibility implies
\[
 F_{D,D',y}(p)=0
 \qquad\text{for every }p\in\mathcal M_{\mathcal I}^{\circ}.
\tag{9}
\]
Fix any \(p\in\mathcal M_{\mathcal I}^{\circ}\).  The positivity argument establishing (6), now applied to \(p\), gives
\[
 B_{D,y}(p)B_{D',y}(p)>0.
\tag{10}
\]
Equations (9)--(10) and the pairwise cross-signature identity imply
\[
 T_{D,y}(p)-T_{D',y}(p)
 =\frac{F_{D,D',y}(p)}{B_{D,y}(p)B_{D',y}(p)}=0.
\tag{11}
\]
Since \(p\) was arbitrary, the two functionals are universally equal on \(\mathcal M_{\mathcal I}^{\circ}\).

Conversely, if the functionals differ at some \(p\in\mathcal M_{\mathcal I}^{\circ}\), denominator positivity as in (10) and the pairwise identity force \(F_{D,D',y}(p)\neq0\), so (3) is nonempty.  Hence feasibility is exactly pairwise separation on the declared positive exact-oracle chamber, whereas infeasibility is exactly universal equality there.  No conclusion is drawn from real-radical nonmembership alone.
