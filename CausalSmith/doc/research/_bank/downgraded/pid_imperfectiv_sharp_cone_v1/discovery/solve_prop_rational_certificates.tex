Put \(x=\mathbb E_Q[Y_1\mid(D,Z)=(0,0)]\) and \(y=\mathbb E_Q[Y_1\mid(D,Z)=(0,1)]\).  The cell probabilities in \(\text{def:strict-example}\) give
\[
 \mu_D=\frac{9}{25}+\frac{11}{100}=\frac{47}{100},
 \qquad
 \mu_Z=\frac{9}{20}+\frac{11}{100}=\frac{14}{25}.
\]
The two factual treated-cell means are \(79/100\) and \(17/20\).  Indeed, from the beta density definition, the mean is
\(B(\alpha+1,\beta)/B(\alpha,\beta)=\alpha/(\alpha+\beta)\).  Therefore the factual contribution to the arm-one mean is
\[
 c_1=\frac{9}{25}\frac{79}{100}+\frac{11}{100}\frac{17}{20}
     =\frac{3779}{10000},
\]
and consistency and the finite-cell decomposition in \(\text{lem:branch-reduction}\) give
\[
 \theta_1=\frac{3779}{10000}+\frac{2}{25}x+\frac{9}{20}y. \tag{1}
\]
Define the two SDC score moments
\[
 C_D=\operatorname{Cov}_Q(Y_1,D),
 \qquad C_Z=\operatorname{Cov}_Q(Y_1,Z).
\]
Since \(\mathbb E_Q[Y_1D]=c_1\) and
\(\mathbb E_Q[Y_1Z]=(9/20)y+(11/100)(17/20)\), direct substitution into the covariance definitions yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 C_D
   &=\frac{200287}{1000000}-\frac{47}{1250}x-\frac{423}{2000}y,\\
 C_Z
   &=-\frac{29531}{250000}-\frac{28}{625}x+\frac{99}{500}y.
 \end{aligned} \tag{2}
\]
Thus the positive branch is characterized by \(C_D\geq0,C_Z\geq0\), and the negative branch by \(C_D\leq0,C_Z\leq0\), exactly as in \(\text{def:branch-polytope}\).

Substitution into (1)--(2), with no rounding, gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
(x,y)&\theta_1&C_D&C_Z\\ \hline
\left(0,\frac{29531}{49500}\right)&\frac{711}{1100}&\frac{1019}{13750}&0\\[2pt]
\left(0,\frac{200287}{211500}\right)&\frac{3779}{4700}&0&\frac{2038}{29375}\\[2pt]
\left(1,\frac{54229}{70500}\right)&\frac{3779}{4700}&0&-\frac{312}{29375}\\[2pt]
\left(1,\frac{40731}{49500}\right)&\frac{911}{1100}&-\frac{78}{6875}&0
\end{array}. \tag{3}
\]
Consequently the first two vectors are feasible for the positive branch and the last two are feasible for the negative branch.

It remains to certify that the four feasible values in (3) are endpoints.  The two missing-cell SDC scores have common treatment coordinate \(-47/100\) and instrument coordinates \(-14/25\) and \(11/25\).  For
\[
 \lambda_Z=\left(0,\frac{25}{11}\right)'
\]
the two lower-dual box coefficients for the positive branch are
\[
 1-\lambda_Z's_{(0,0)}^{\mathrm{SDC}}=\frac{25}{11}>0,
 \qquad
 1-\lambda_Z's_{(0,1)}^{\mathrm{SDC}}=0. \tag{4}
\]
Hence the box minimization chooses \(x=0\) and is indifferent to \(y\).  At the first row of (3), the instrument score moment is zero, so the primal objective and the lower-dual objective at \(\lambda_Z\) coincide.  The equality \(L=V_L\) from \(\text{thm:cone-endpoint-sharpness}\) therefore proves that the first row attains \(L_{1,+}^{\mathrm{SDC}}(P^\star)=711/1100\).  For the negative branch, the upper-dual box coefficients are again those in (4); box maximization chooses \(x=1\), and the fourth row has zero instrument score moment.  The equality \(U=V_U\) therefore proves that the fourth row attains \(U_{1,-}^{\mathrm{SDC}}(P^\star)=911/1100\).

For the remaining two endpoints take
\[
 \lambda_D=\left(\frac{100}{47},0\right)'.
\]
Because both missing cells have treatment score \(-47/100\), the relevant upper-dual coefficients for the positive branch and lower-dual coefficients for the negative branch are, respectively,
\[
 1+\lambda_D's_j^{\mathrm{SDC}}=0,
 \qquad
 1+\lambda_D's_j^{\mathrm{SDC}}=0. \tag{5}
\]
The second and third rows of (3) have zero treatment score moment.  Primal and dual objectives hence coincide in (5), and \(\text{thm:cone-endpoint-sharpness}\) gives
\[
 U_{1,+}^{\mathrm{SDC}}(P^\star)
 =L_{1,-}^{\mathrm{SDC}}(P^\star)
 =\frac{3779}{4700}.
\]

Every displayed second coordinate lies strictly between zero and one.  Indeed, their respective distances to the nearer support endpoint are
\[
 \frac{19969}{49500},\qquad
 \frac{11213}{211500},\qquad
 \frac{16271}{70500},\qquad
 \frac{2923}{16500},
\]
all of which are positive.  Taking the corresponding slack coordinate equal to any of these positive distances verifies positive total interior slack under \(\text{def:interior-slack}\).  Finally,
\[
 \|\lambda_Z\|_1=\frac{25}{11}>1,
\]
so the two unrestricted-cone certificates for the outer sharp endpoints lie strictly outside the published normalized multiplier simplex.  This proves all assertions.

Write

 q(\rho,s)=\frac{9}{25}\rho+\frac{11}{100}s.
\]
At \((\rho,s)=(79/100,17/20)\), the two treated-cell laws in \(\text{def:parametric-strict-family}\) are \(\operatorname{Beta}(79,21)\) and \(\operatorname{Beta}(17,3)\).  The remaining conditional laws and all cell masses are fixed to those in \(\text{def:strict-example}\).  Hence
\[
 P^{79/100,17/20}=P^\star.
\]
The four cell masses, \(\mu_D=47/100\), \(\mu_Z=14/25\), and both missing-cell score vectors are constant throughout the family.  For missing means \((x,y)\in[0,1]^2\), the finite-cell moment decomposition gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \theta_1(\rho,s;x,y)
   &=q(\rho,s)+\frac{2}{25}x+\frac{9}{20}y,\\
 C_D(\rho,s;x,y)
   &=\frac{53}{100}q(\rho,s)
     -\frac{47}{100}\left(\frac{2}{25}x+\frac{9}{20}y\right),\\
 C_Z(\rho,s;x,y)
   &=\frac{11}{100}s-\frac{14}{25}q(\rho,s)
     -\frac{28}{625}x+\frac{99}{500}y.
 \end{aligned} \tag{6}
\]
These identities follow by expanding \(\mathbb E[Y_1D]-\mu_D\mathbb E[Y_1]\) and \(\mathbb E[Y_1Z]-\mu_Z\mathbb E[Y_1]\), respectively.

First record quantitative margins at the center.  The first and fourth endpoint certificates in \(\text{prop:rational-certificates}\) permit the feasible slack choices

 (t_x,t_y)=\left(0,\frac{19969}{49500}\right)
 \quad\hbox{and}\quad

so the asserted lower bounds on \(\tau_{1,+}^{\mathrm{SDC}}\) and \(\tau_{1,-}^{\mathrm{SDC}}\) follow directly from \(\text{def:interior-slack}\).  Substitution into (6) gives

 \begin{array}{c|cc}
 (x,y)&C_D&C_Z\\ \hline
 (1/2,3/4)&11431/500000&997/125000\\
 (19/20,4/5)&-4633/1000000&-571/250000
 \end{array}, \tag{7}

which proves strict score feasibility with positive margins.  The multiplier margins are the exact calculations

 \left\|\left(0,\frac{25}{11}\right)\right\|_1-1=\frac{14}{11},
 \qquad
 \left\|\left(\frac{100}{47},0\right)\right\|_1-1=\frac{53}{47}.
\]
Finally, \(\text{def:published-outer}\) and the center equality \(P^{79/100,17/20}=P^\star\), together with the exact endpoints from \(\text{prop:rational-certificates}\), give the two displayed positive outer-endpoint gaps.

 z(\rho,s)=\frac{56}{55}\rho-\frac{11}{45}s,
 \qquad

The four candidate endpoint vectors are

At the center, (9) is exactly the four-vector list in \(\text{prop:rational-certificates}\).  Equation (6) shows that the rows labeled \(L_+\) and \(U_-\) have \(C_Z=0\), whereas the rows labeled \(U_+\) and \(L_-\) have \(C_D=0\).  At the center, the nonbinding score coordinates, in row order, are

              \frac{16271}{70500},\frac{2923}{16500}\right\}
 =\frac{11213}{211500}>0 \tag{11}

from the relevant nearest box boundary.  Let \(\varepsilon=\max\{|\rho-79/100|,|s-17/20|\}\).  The center value of \(-27\rho+121s\) is \(2038/25\), its distance from \(94\) is \(312/25\), and its perturbation is at most \(148\varepsilon\).  Hence both signs in (10) persist when \(\varepsilon<1/100\).  The perturbations of \(z(\rho,s)\) and \(d(\rho,s)\) are at most \((125/99)\varepsilon\) and \((53/45)\varepsilon\), respectively. Thus all four rows in (9) remain feasible throughout the asserted neighborhood, and each branch remains aggregate-support eligible by \(\text{thm:support-eligibility}\).

The fixed multipliers \(\lambda_Z=(0,25/11)'\) and \(\lambda_D=(100/47,0)'\) used in the proof of \(\text{prop:rational-certificates}\) have coefficients depending only on the fixed missing-cell scores.  \mathcal I_1^{\mathrm{SDC}}(P^{\rho,s})
 =\left[\frac{9\rho}{11},\frac{9\rho}{11}+\frac{2}{11}\right] \tag{13}
\]
throughout the asserted max-norm neighborhood.  That neighborhood lies inside \((0,1)^2\); there all four Beta shape parameters are positive, and their densities are positive on \((0,1)\), so the observed conditional laws have support \([0,1]\), as required by the factual-support restriction.

It remains to prove that strict containment in the normalized outer union is stable.  For fixed \(b\), denote the lower and upper objective functions in \(\text{def:parametric-normalized-outer}\) by \(G_{L,b}(\lambda;\rho,s)\) and \(G_{U,b}(\lambda;\rho,s)\).  The box terms in these functions are independent of \((\rho,s)\), while \(c_1\) and \(h_1^{\mathrm{SDC}}\) are affine in \((\rho,s)\).  Hence, for either objective and center \((\rho_0,s_0)=(79/100,17/20)\),
\[
 \sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda_1}
 \left|G_{\cdot,b}(\lambda;\rho,s)-G_{\cdot,b}(\lambda;\rho_0,s_0)\right|
 \leq |c_1(\rho,s)-c_1(\rho_0,s_0)|
      +\|h_1^{\mathrm{SDC}}(\rho,s)-h_1^{\mathrm{SDC}}(\rho_0,s_0)\|_\infty,
 \tag{14}
\]
because \(\|\lambda\|_1\leq1\) on \(\Lambda_1\).  The right-hand side tends to zero.  The elementary inequalities
\[
 \left|\sup f-\sup g\right|\leq\sup|f-g|,
 \qquad
 \left|\inf f-\inf g\right|\leq\sup|f-g| \tag{15}
\]
therefore prove continuity of every normalized branch endpoint.  This is the required compact-parameter, or Berge-type, continuity conclusion, derived here directly from the finite dual formulas.  Finite minima and maxima of these four endpoints are continuous as well.

Restricting the dual multiplier domain from \(\mathbb R_+^2\) to \(\Lambda_1\) can only decrease a lower dual supremum and increase an upper dual infimum.  Therefore each normalized branch interval contains its exact branch interval in (12).  At the center, the equality just proved and the constructions in \(\text{def:parametric-normalized-outer}\) and \(\text{def:published-outer}\) show that the normalized union is \(\mathcal I_{BK}^\star\).  Thus the distance from the exact lower endpoint to the lower edge of the normalized union is \(206717/1375000\), and the distance from the exact upper endpoint to the upper edge is \(61383/1375000\).  More quantitatively, (6) implies
\[
 |c_1(\rho,s)-c_1(\rho_0,s_0)|\leq\frac{47}{100}\varepsilon,
 \qquad
 \|h_1^{\mathrm{SDC}}(\rho,s)-h_1^{\mathrm{SDC}}(\rho_0,s_0)\|_\infty
 \leq\frac14\varepsilon.
\]
Thus every normalized branch endpoint, and hence the minimum lower and maximum upper normalized endpoints, moves by at most \((18/25)\varepsilon\).  Either outer exact endpoint in (13) moves by at most \((9/11)\varepsilon\).  Each strict gap can consequently decrease by at most
\[
 \left(\frac{18}{25}+\frac{9}{11}\right)\varepsilon
 =\frac{423}{275}\varepsilon.
\]
For \(\varepsilon<1/100\), the smaller center gap remains at least
\[
 \frac{61383}{1375000}-\frac{423}{27500}
 =\frac{40233}{1375000}>0.
\]
Therefore (13) is contained branchwise in \(\mathcal I_{BK}^{\rho,s}\), while the normalized union still contains a point strictly below the left endpoint of (13) and a point strictly above its right endpoint.  This proves the strict inclusion on the stated neighborhood.  Substitution of \(\rho=79/100\) in (13) gives \([711/1100,911/1100]\), completing the proof.
