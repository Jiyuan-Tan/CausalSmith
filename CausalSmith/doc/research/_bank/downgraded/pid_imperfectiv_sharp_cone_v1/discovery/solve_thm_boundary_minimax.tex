\[
 \max_{i=0,1}\mathbb E_{Q_i}\rho_H(\widehat I_n,I_i)
 \ge\frac{\rho_H(I_0,I_1)}{2}
       \{1-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\}.                         \tag{34b}
\]
Indeed, integrate \(\rho_H(\widehat I_n,I_0)+\rho_H(\widehat I_n,I_1)\ge\rho_H(I_0,I_1)\) against that common submeasure, and bound the two integrals by their respective risks.  Taking \((Q_0,Q_1)=(P_0^n,P_{\varepsilon_n}^n)\), (31), (34a), and (34b) yield the lower limit \(3/20\).

For the matching upper bound, we construct a deterministic Hausdorff midpoint.  For nonempty compact \(A,B\subset\mathbb R\), set \(\delta=d_H(A,B)\) and
\[
 M(A,B)=\left\{\frac{a+b}{2}:a\in A,\ b\in B,\ |a-b|\le\delta\right\}.
\]
The set is nonempty and compact.  Every point of \(M(A,B)\) is within \(\delta/2\) of both \(A\) and \(B\).  Conversely, the definition of Hausdorff distance and compactness give, for every \(a\in A\), a \(b\in B\) with \(|a-b|\le\delta\), and conversely for every \(b\in B\); the corresponding midpoint is within \(\delta/2\).  Hence
\[
 d_H(A,M(A,B))=d_H(B,M(A,B))=\frac\delta2.                       \tag{34c}
\]
where equality follows from the triangle inequality after the two upper bounds.  In the known two-point experiment, the constant estimator \(M(A_n,B_n)\) is measurable and has maximal risk \(\delta_n/2\).  Thus the minimax risk is at most \(\delta_n/2\), which tends to \(3/20\) by (31).  Together with the lower bound this proves the asserted limit.  Finally, (25) shows that the minimum positive slack along the alternative is \(\kappa_n=3\varepsilon_n/2=o(n^{-1/2})\), yet the minimax risk does not shrink.  Every calculation used SDC alone; no LEI conclusion follows.
