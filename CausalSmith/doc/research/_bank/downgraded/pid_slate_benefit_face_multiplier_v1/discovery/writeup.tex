\section*{pid\_\allowbreak{}slate\_\allowbreak{}benefit\_\allowbreak{}face\_\allowbreak{}multiplier}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} The completed \(\texttt{pid\_slate\_benefit\_partialtransport}\) theorem supplies the exact selected-complier capacities and closed sharp benefit endpoints as an established input. Every empirical conditional proportion, projected capacity, influence component, multiplier quantile, and confidence interval is totalized on all finite samples by an explicit count-denominator convention, whose activation probability vanishes uniformly. Because the projected piecewise linear-fractional map can change active cuts and survivor support at the \(n^{-1/2}\) scale, the procedure keeps every empirically near-active projection, gap, survivor-minimum, cut, and mass-cap face and calibrates the largest of the two signed endpoint errors with one conditional multiplier process. The main theorem gives uniform root-\(n\) coverage of the entire sharp interval without deterministic padding, oracle equivalence recovers ordinary precision on separated faces, the formulas reduce to ordinary ordinal-benefit bounds under perfect compliance and universal selection, and a complete finite atom table supplies a legal changing-support stress test.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Existing ordinal-benefit work supplies sharp symbolic bounds and studies ordinary-bootstrap boundary undercoverage with a conservative subsampling remedy, but does not prove uniform no-padding coverage of the entire projected survivor-complier quotient interval. Generic nonsmooth and intersection-bound methods do not directly handle the interacting orthant projection, changing cell support, nested min-cut branches, two signed endpoints, and common denominator.

\paragraph{Niche.} Fixed finite covariate and ordered-outcome supports make every relevant face and gradient explicit, while still permitting the economically important configurations that defeat smooth delta-method inference.

\paragraph{Fill.} Taking the completed exact-capacity and closed-endpoint mapping as fixed, a finite pathwise secant envelope reduces its sampling problem to a common finite Gaussian maximum. Explicit count-based totalization makes the procedure a well-defined map on every sample, while the uniform empty-cell bound shows that this convention is asymptotically invisible. Near-face retention then delivers honest whole-set coverage and adapts to the oracle on separated configurations.

\section{Related work}

The completed \(\texttt{pid\_slate\_benefit\_partialtransport}\) theorem establishes the exact capacities and closed sharp endpoints used here; those identification results are imported rather than claimed as a contribution. Chen and Flores (2015) motivate the always-employed Job Corps complier stratum and bound its mean wage effect, but their inferential discussion excludes bounding functions with nested minimum and maximum operators. Lu, Ding, and Dasgupta (2018) give ordinary sharp ordinal-benefit bounds, recovered here under perfect compliance and universal selection. Gabriel, Sachs, and Jensen (2024) give sharp symbolic ordinal-benefit bounds, document \(n\)-out-of-\(n\) bootstrap undercoverage at boundary laws, and use a conservative \(m\)-out-of-\(n\) remedy; the narrower missing result is uniform no-padding coverage of the whole projected survivor-complier quotient interval. Dong and Heiler (2026) derive efficient orthogonal inference for selected-complier mean bounds, but their smooth trimming and relevant-support conditions do not cover discrete ordered atoms or disappearing cell survivor masses. Fang and Santos (2019) explain why ordinary resampling fails for nonlinear directional derivatives and motivate resampling an estimated local derivative. Chernozhukov, Lee, and Rosen (2013), Andrews and Soares (2010), and Andrews and Barwick (2012) motivate retaining near-binding faces. Firpo, Galvao, and Parker (2023) show how localized optimizer sets enter value-function inference. Hsieh, Shi, and Shum (2022) and Cho and Russell (2024) provide optimization-based alternatives, but their procedures do not yield the explicit no-latent-program critical value for this exact whole identified set. Standard multinomial asymptotics are usually written on the event of nonempty conditioning cells; the finite-cell and instrument-overlap conditions here additionally yield an explicit uniform exponential bound for the complement, making all reported empirical and multiplier objects total on finite samples.

  \item \(\widehat g^L_{n,r}\) --- \texttt{random vector}; \(\mathbb R^{2JK}\); \texttt{estimated lower face gradient}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat g^L_{n,r}=[A^L_r-\widehat\theta_{L,n}B_r]/\widehat M_n^{\dagger}\).
  \item \(\widehat g^U_{n,r}\) --- \texttt{random vector}; \(\mathbb R^{2JK}\); \texttt{estimated upper face gradient}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat g^U_{n,r}=-[A^U_r-\widehat\theta_{U,n}B_r]/\widehat M_n^{\dagger}\).
  \item \(\xi_i\) --- \texttt{random variable}; \(N(0,1)\); \texttt{multiplier weight}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \texttt{Independent standard Gaussian multipliers,\allowbreak{} independent of the data.}
  \item \(G_n^*\) --- \texttt{conditionally Gaussian vector}; \(\mathbb R^{2JK}\); \texttt{common multiplier first-\allowbreak{}stage process}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(G_n^*=n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^n\xi_i\widehat\psi_{w,n}(O_{ni})\), with the totalized influence vector used even if some empirical \((X,Z)\) cell is empty.
  \item \(c_{n,1-\alpha}\) --- \texttt{random real number}; \texttt{least-\allowbreak{}favourable critical value}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(c_{n,1-\alpha}=\inf\{t\in\mathbb R:P_{\xi}[\max_{r\in\mathcal R_n^{\mathrm{ret}}}\{\widehat g^L_{n,r}{}^{\prime}G_n^*,\widehat g^U_{n,r}{}^{\prime}G_n^*\}\le t\mid O_{n1},\ldots,O_{nn}]\ge1-\alpha\}\). The retained Cartesian family is nonempty on every sample, so this generalized-inverse quantile is defined even when \(G_n^*\) is degenerate.
  \item \(\mathrm{CI}_n\) --- \texttt{random closed interval}; subsets of \([0,1]\); \texttt{confidence interval for the entire sharp identified set}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\mathrm{CI}_n=[\max\{0,\widehat\theta_{L,n}-c_{n,1-\alpha}/\sqrt n\},\min\{1,\max\{0,\widehat\theta_{U,n}+c_{n,1-\alpha}/\sqrt n\}\}]\); if the displayed lower endpoint exceeds the upper endpoint under an off-support finite-sample realization, set \(\mathrm{CI}_n=\{0\}\).
  \item \(\mathcal E_n\) --- \texttt{sample event}; \texttt{event that every required empirical covariate-\allowbreak{}instrument cell is nonempty}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\mathcal E_n=\{\min_{x\in\mathcal X,\ z\in\{0,1\}}N_{n,x,z}\ge1\}\).
  \item \(m^{\mathrm{wit}}_{n,x}\) --- \texttt{nonnegative real number}; \texttt{cellwise always-\allowbreak{}selected-\allowbreak{}complier mass in the boundary diagnostic}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} For the diagnostic support \(\mathcal X=\{1,2\}\), set \(m^{\mathrm{wit}}_{n,1}=1/4\) and \(m^{\mathrm{wit}}_{n,2}=1/\sqrt n\), with \(n\ge16\).
  \item \(\mathcal A_n^{\mathrm{wit}}\) --- \texttt{finite atom table}; \texttt{complete forty-\allowbreak{}eight-\allowbreak{}atom boundary-\allowbreak{}diagnostic table}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} For every \(x\in\{1,2\}\), \(z\in\{0,1\}\), and \(r\in\{0,1,2\}\), the table has exactly four rows \[(x,z,1,1,1,1,r,r;1/48),\quad(x,z,0,0,1,1,r,r;1/48),\] \[(x,z,0,1,1,1,r,r;m^{\mathrm{wit}}_{n,x}/12),\quad(x,z,0,1,0,0,r,r;(1/2-m^{\mathrm{wit}}_{n,x})/12).\] Each tuple lists \((X_{ni},Z_{ni},D_{ni}(0),D_{ni}(1),S_{ni}(0),S_{ni}(1),Y_{ni}(0),Y_{ni}(1);\text{probability})\), and every tuple not displayed has probability zero.
  \item \(P_n^{\mathrm{wit}}\) --- \texttt{probability law}; \texttt{two-\allowbreak{}covariate three-\allowbreak{}outcome changing-\allowbreak{}support diagnostic law}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} The law supported exactly on \(\mathcal A_n^{\mathrm{wit}}\), with observed variables generated by \(D_{ni}=D_{ni}(Z_{ni})\), \(S_{ni}=S_{ni}(D_{ni})\), and \(Y_{ni}=Y_{ni}(D_{ni})\) when \(S_{ni}=1\).
  \item \(\Sigma^{\mathrm{wit}}(m)\) --- \texttt{symmetric matrix-\allowbreak{}valued map}; \(\mathbb R^{6\times6}\); \texttt{conditional raw-\allowbreak{}capacity contrast covariance block implied by the atom table}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Let \(A_m=(1/4+m)/3\), \(B=1/12\), \(v_0(m)=(A_m,A_m,A_m,-B,-B,-B)^{\prime}\), and \(v_1(m)=(-B,-B,-B,A_m,A_m,A_m)^{\prime}\). Define \(\Sigma^{\mathrm{wit}}(m)=4\{(A_m+B)I_6-v_0(m)v_0(m)^{\prime}-v_1(m)v_1(m)^{\prime}\}\).
  \item \(\mathcal H_{\mathrm{adapt}}\) --- \texttt{construction handle}; \texttt{method handle for tuning-\allowbreak{}free face retention}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \texttt{Adapt automatic generalized-\allowbreak{}moment selection to the finite projection,\allowbreak{} gap,\allowbreak{} survivor-\allowbreak{}minimum,\allowbreak{} cut,\allowbreak{} and mass-\allowbreak{}cap slacks by studentizing each slack with its empirical covariance and calibrating a data-\allowbreak{}dependent near-\allowbreak{}face threshold through the common multiplier process.}
\end{itemize}

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:iid-row]\label{ass:iid-row}
For every \(n\), \(O_{n1},\ldots,O_{nn}\) are independent and identically distributed under \(P_n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (row-wise i.i.d. triangular-array sampling, \cite{ChernozhukovChetverikovKato2017}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:instrument-exogeneity]\label{ass:instrument-exogeneity}
\(Z_{ni}\perp(D_{ni}(0),D_{ni}(1),S_{ni}(0),S_{ni}(1),Y_{ni}(0),Y_{ni}(1))\mid X_{ni}\) under \(P_n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (conditional instrumental-variable independence, \cite{ChenFlores2015}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:treatment-consistency]\label{ass:treatment-consistency}
\(D_{ni}=D_{ni}(Z_{ni})\) almost surely under \(P_n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (treatment consistency, \cite{ChenFlores2015}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:selection-consistency]\label{ass:selection-consistency}
\(S_{ni}=S_{ni}(D_{ni})\) almost surely under \(P_n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (selection consistency, \cite{ChenFlores2015}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:outcome-consistency]\label{ass:outcome-consistency}
\(Y_{ni}=Y_{ni}(D_{ni})\) whenever \(S_{ni}=1\), almost surely under \(P_n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (observed-outcome consistency under selection, \cite{ChenFlores2015}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:exclusion]\label{ass:exclusion}
The potential selection and outcome variables depend on instrument assignment only through \(D_{ni}(z)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (instrument exclusion restriction, \cite{ChenFlores2015}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:no-defiers]\label{ass:no-defiers}
\(D_{ni}(1)\ge D_{ni}(0)\) almost surely under \(P_n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (no-defiers monotonicity, \cite{ChenFlores2015}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:weak-selection-monotonicity]\label{ass:weak-selection-monotonicity}
For each \(x\in\mathcal X\), \(\sigma_{n,x}\{S_{ni}(1)-S_{ni}(0)\}\ge0\) almost surely conditional on \(C_{ni}=1\) and \(X_{ni}=x\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (covariate-specific weak sample-selection monotonicity, \cite{DongHeiler2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:cell-mass]\label{ass:cell-mass}
\(\min_{x\in\mathcal X}p_{n,x}\ge p_0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (finite-cell mass overlap, \cite{ChernozhukovLeeRosen2013}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:instrument-overlap]\label{ass:instrument-overlap}
\(\varepsilon\le e_{n,x}\le1-\varepsilon\) for every \(x\in\mathcal X\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (instrument overlap, \cite{DongHeiler2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:aggregate-mass]\label{ass:aggregate-mass}
\(M_n\ge m_0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (aggregate always-selected-complier overlap, \cite{DongHeiler2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:raw-covariance-band]\label{ass:raw-covariance-band}
Every eigenvalue of \(\Sigma_{c,n}\) on the nonredundant conditional-contrast space lies in \([\underline\lambda,\overline\lambda]\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (finite-dimensional nondegenerate Gaussian covariance band, \cite{ChernozhukovChetverikovKato2017}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:tolerance-vanishes]\label{ass:tolerance-vanishes}
\(a_n\to0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (vanishing moment-selection tolerance, \cite{AndrewsSoares2010}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:tolerance-dominates-noise]\label{ass:tolerance-dominates-noise}
\(\sqrt n\,a_n\to\infty\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (diverging sub-root moment-selection threshold, \cite{AndrewsSoares2010}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:oracle-separation]\label{ass:oracle-separation}
Every population-inactive coordinate, gap, survivor-minimum, lower-cut, upper-cut, and mass-cap branch has slack at least \(\delta_0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (uniform slack separation for moment selection, \cite{AndrewsBarwick2012}).
\end{assumption}

\section{Main results}

\begin{proposition}[prop:finite-sample-totalization]\label{prop:finite-sample-totalization}
Let \(\mathcal E_n\) be the event that every empirical \((X,Z)\) cell is nonempty. Uniformly over \((P_n)_{n\ge1}\in\mathcal P\), \[\sup_{(P_n)\in\mathcal P}P_n(\mathcal E_n^c)\le 2J(1-p_0\varepsilon)^n\longrightarrow0.\] On \(\mathcal E_n\), every denominator fallback in \(\widehat e_{n,x}\), \(\widehat q^d_{n,z,x,k}\), and \(\widehat\psi_{w,n}\) is inactive, and the totalized \(\widehat w_n\), \(\widehat v_n\), endpoint estimators, retained face family, multiplier process, critical value, and \(\mathrm{CI}_n\) equal their ordinary empirical-cell counterparts. Consequently totalization changes any coverage probability by at most \(2J(1-p_0\varepsilon)^n\) and does not alter the limit in \(\texttt{thm:uniform-face-envelope}\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix a row \(n\) and a law in \(\mathcal P\). For \(x\in\mathcal X\) and \(z\in\{0,1\}\), \(\texttt{ass:cell-mass}\) and \(\texttt{ass:instrument-overlap}\) give
\[
 \pi_{n,x,z}:=P_n(X_{n1}=x,Z_{n1}=z)
 =p_{n,x}e_{n,x}^{z}(1-e_{n,x})^{1-z}
 \ge p_0\varepsilon .
\]
By row-wise independence in \(\texttt{ass:iid-row}\),
\[
 P_n(N_{n,x,z}=0)=(1-\pi_{n,x,z})^n
 \le (1-p_0\varepsilon)^n .
\]
The union bound over the \(2J\) pairs \((x,z)\) therefore yields the uniform estimate
\[
 \sup_{(P_n)\in\mathcal P}P_n(\mathcal E_n^c)
 \le \sum_{x=1}^{J}\sum_{z=0}^{1}P_n(N_{n,x,z}=0)
 \le 2J(1-p_0\varepsilon)^n\longrightarrow0.
\]
On \(\mathcal E_n\), \(N_{n,x,z}\ge1\) for both values of \(z\), and hence \(N_{n,x}\ge2\). Consequently
\[
 \max\{N_{n,x},1\}=N_{n,x},\qquad
 \max\{N_{n,x,z},1\}=N_{n,x,z},\qquad
 \max\{N_{n,x,z}/n,1/n\}=N_{n,x,z}/n.
\]
Thus every totalized conditional proportion and every component of \(\widehat\psi_{w,n}\) equals its ordinary nonempty-cell expression. By \(\texttt{def:finite-sample-totalization}\), \(\texttt{def:unscreened-plugin}\), \(\texttt{def:face-envelope}\), and \(\texttt{def:confidence-interval}\), all the remaining objects listed in the proposition are deterministic measurable compositions of those proportions. They therefore agree with their ordinary empirical-cell counterparts on \(\mathcal E_n\).

Give the ordinary procedure any measurable extension on \(\mathcal E_n^c\) and couple that extension with the totalized procedure on the same sample. For every event \(A_n\) defined from either procedure,
\[
 \left|P_n(A_n^{\mathrm{tot}})-P_n(A_n^{\mathrm{ord}})\right|
 \le P_n(\mathcal E_n^c)
 \le2J(1-p_0\varepsilon)^n.
\]
Taking \(A_n\) to be the whole-set coverage event proves the final assertion, including that totalization does not change the limit in \(\texttt{thm:uniform-face-envelope}\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This proposition closes the finite-sample domain of every empirical and multiplier object without changing the uniform first-order experiment. \textit{Gap.} Asymptotic cell-probability arguments often leave conditional empirical ratios undefined on empty cells; the explicit exponential bound makes the proposed whole-set procedure a genuine finite-sample map. \textit{Consumer.} It permits software for sparse Job Corps covariate cells to return a deterministic interval on every dataset while preserving the theorem's uniform coverage guarantee.

\begin{lemma}[lem:finite-secant-envelope]\label{lem:finite-secant-envelope}
Uniformly over \((P_n)_{n\ge1}\in\mathcal P\), with probability tending to one, \[\max\{\sqrt n(\widehat\theta_{L,n}-\theta_{L,n}),\sqrt n(\theta_{U,n}-\widehat\theta_{U,n})\}\le\max_{r\in\mathcal R_n^0(P_n)}\{g^L_{n,r}{}^{\prime}n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^n\psi_{w,n}(O_{ni}),g^U_{n,r}{}^{\prime}n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^n\psi_{w,n}(O_{ni})\}+o_{P_n}(1),\] where \(\mathcal R_n^0(P_n)\) is contained in \(\mathcal R_n^{\mathrm{ret}}\) with probability tending to one.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write \(d=2JK\), \(w=w_n\), \(\widehat w=\widehat w_n\), and \(\Delta=\widehat w-w\). We first establish a uniform first-stage expansion. Let
\[
 \mathcal F_n=\left\{\min_{x\in\mathcal X,\,z\in\{0,1\}}
                   N_{n,x,z}/n\ge p_0\varepsilon/2\right\}.
\]
Every joint-cell frequency has mean at least \(p_0\varepsilon\) and variance at most \(1/(4n)\). Chebyshev's inequality followed by the union bound gives
\[
 \sup_{(P_n)\in\mathcal P}P_n(\mathcal F_n^c)
 \le \frac{2J}{n p_0^2\varepsilon^2}.
\]
On \(\mathcal F_n\), each coordinate of \(\widehat w\) is a twice continuously differentiable function of the finite vector of empirical multinomial frequencies, on a neighborhood on which every denominator is at least \(p_0\varepsilon/2\). Direct differentiation of the two conditional ratios and their covariate weight gives exactly the displayed coordinate of \(\psi_{w,n}\). The second derivatives are bounded by a constant depending only on \(p_0,\varepsilon,J,K\). Since the empirical multinomial vector has Euclidean error \(O_{P_n}(n^{-1/2})\) uniformly, Taylor's formula yields
\[
 \sqrt n\,\Delta
 =n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^n\psi_{w,n}(O_{ni})+R_n,
 \qquad
 \sup_{(P_n)\in\mathcal P}P_n(\lVert R_n\rVert>\eta)\longrightarrow0
\]
for every \(\eta>0\). In particular, \(\lVert\Delta\rVert=O_{P_n}(n^{-1/2})\) uniformly. The empty-cell complement is already negligible by \(\texttt{prop:finite-sample-totalization}\).

We next verify the finite piecewise linear-fractional representation. For \(u\in\mathbb R^d\), apply \(\Pi_+\) blockwise and write \(M(u)\), \(N_L(u)\), and \(N_U(u)\) for the three displayed capacity functionals. Fixing an orthant mask, a survivor-minimum choice, a lower gap choice, a lower zero-or-cut choice, and an upper cut-or-mass-cap choice produces a region \(r\in\mathcal R\) on which
\[
 M(u)=B_r'u,\qquad N_L(u)=A_r^{L\prime}u,\qquad
 N_U(u)=A_r^{U\prime}u.
\]
This follows by substituting the definitions: the lower cut coefficient on the treated block is
\(-\mathbf1\{k\le t\}+\gamma\mathbf1_K\), its control coefficient is
\(\mathbf1\{k\le t\}-\gamma\mathbf1_K\), and the upper coefficients are precisely the strict-tail and strict-prefix vectors displayed in \(A_r^U\). Hence this step uses only the explicit finite min--cut branches and no latent linear program.

For completeness, the quotient is well ordered for every nonnegative block. Put \(L=\sum_kv_k^\ell\), \(H=\sum_kv_k^h\), and
\[
 d_t=\sum_{k\le t}(v_k^h-v_k^\ell)+\min\{L-H,0\},
 \qquad u_s=\sum_{k<s}v_k^h+\sum_{k>s}v_k^\ell.
\]
If \(L\le H\), then \(d_t=L_{>t}-H_{>t}\); if \(s\le t\), this is at most \(L_{>s}\le u_s\), while if \(s>t\), the equivalent expression
\(d_t=H_{\le t}-L_{\le t}-(H-L)\) is at most \(H_{<s}\le u_s\). If \(L>H\), then \(d_t=H_{\le t}-L_{\le t}\); the same two conclusions follow respectively from the equivalent expression
\(d_t=L_{>t}-H_{>t}-(L-H)\) and from \(d_t\le H_{\le t}\). Moreover \(d_t\le\min\{L,H\}\) in both cases. Thus
\[
 0\le b_L(v)\le b_U(v)\le m(v).
\]
After summing over cells, whenever \(M(u)>0\), both endpoint maps lie in \([0,1]\) and are ordered.

On region \(r\), define \(F_L(u)=N_L(u)/M(u)\) and \(F_U(u)=N_U(u)/M(u)\). The ordinary quotient rule gives
\[
 \nabla F_L(u)=\frac{A_r^L-F_L(u)B_r}{M(u)},
 \qquad
 \nabla\{-F_U(u)\}=-\frac{A_r^U-F_U(u)B_r}{M(u)}.
\]
The function \(M\) is globally Lipschitz because it is a finite sum of minima of positive-part linear forms. Let \(L_M\) be a Lipschitz constant. By \(\texttt{ass:aggregate-mass}\), on
\(\lVert\Delta\rVert\le m_0/(2L_M)\), the segment \(w_s=w+s\Delta\), \(0\le s\le1\), obeys
\[
 M(w_s)\ge M(w)-L_M\lVert\Delta\rVert\ge m_0/2.
\]
This event has probability tending to one uniformly.

The segment meets only finitely many regions. Subdivide it at the crossing times; on a boundary interval choose either incident formula, since continuity makes their directional derivatives along the interval equal. The fundamental theorem of calculus then gives
\[
 F_L(\widehat w)-F_L(w)
 =\int_0^1\nabla F_{L,r(s)}(w_s)'\Delta\,ds,
\]
\[
 F_U(w)-F_U(\widehat w)
 =\int_0^1\nabla\{-F_{U,r(s)}\}(w_s)'\Delta\,ds.
\]
If \(r\) is encountered, continuity of the piecewise-linear functions implies
\(B_r'w=M(w)+O(\lVert\Delta\rVert)\) and
\(A_r^{e\prime}w=N_e(w)+O(\lVert\Delta\rVert)\). On \(M\ge m_0/2\), the quotient rule therefore implies, uniformly over the fixed finite coefficient family,
\[
 \nabla F_{L,r}(w_s)=g^L_{n,r}+O(\lVert\Delta\rVert),
 \qquad
 \nabla\{-F_{U,r}\}(w_s)=g^U_{n,r}+O(\lVert\Delta\rVert).
\]
Let \(\mathcal S_n\) be the set of regions encountered by the segment. Integration produces a convex combination of the corresponding linear forms, and hence
\[
 F_L(\widehat w)-F_L(w)
 \le\max_{r\in\mathcal S_n}g^L_{n,r}{}'\Delta+C\lVert\Delta\rVert^2,
\]
\[
 F_U(w)-F_U(\widehat w)
 \le\max_{r\in\mathcal S_n}g^U_{n,r}{}'\Delta+C\lVert\Delta\rVert^2.
\]

It remains to localize \(\mathcal S_n\). Each region is described by a member of a fixed finite list of signed affine slacks: coordinate-release slacks, gap slacks, survivor-minimum slacks, pairwise lower zero-or-cut slacks, and pairwise upper cut-or-mass-cap slacks. Rescale their nonzero coefficient vectors to have norm one. If the segment enters a region, every inequality defining that region which is violated at \(w\) is violated by at most \(\lVert\Delta\rVert\), by comparison with the entry point. The deterministic localization constants in the definition of \(\mathcal R_n^0(P_n)\) therefore give
\[
 P_n\{\mathcal S_n\subseteq\mathcal R_n^0(P_n)\}\longrightarrow1
\]
uniformly, because \(\lVert\Delta\rVert=o_{P_n}(a_n)\) follows from \(\texttt{ass:tolerance-dominates-noise}\). Each empirical-population slack difference is bounded by a fixed multiple of \(\lVert\Delta\rVert\). The population tolerance \(a_n/4\), the empirical tolerance \(a_n\), and the same rate consequently give
\[
 P_n\{\mathcal R_n^0(P_n)\subseteq\mathcal R_n^{\mathrm{ret}}\}
 \longrightarrow1
\]
uniformly. The condition \(a_n\to0\) ensures that all these neighborhoods eventually lie in the aggregate-mass neighborhood just used.

The displayed capacity inequality shows that endpoint clipping is inactive whenever \(\widehat M_n>0\); if \(\widehat M_n=0\), both totalized endpoint estimates are zero. Since \(\widehat M_n=M_n+O_{P_n}(n^{-1/2})\) and \(M_n\ge m_0\), the former case has probability tending to one uniformly. Finally,
\(\sqrt n\lVert\Delta\rVert^2=o_{P_n}(1)\). Multiplying the two secant bounds by \(\sqrt n\), inserting the first-stage expansion, and using the two set inclusions proves exactly the inequality and retention assertion in the lemma.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the deterministic bridge from a drifting nonsmooth quotient to a finite Gaussian maximum. \textit{Gap.} Directional-delta results linearize at one law; they do not dominate both signed endpoint secants along paths that cross projection and survivor-support faces. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies a reusable verification device for exact finite capacity bounds without solving a bootstrap linear program.

\begin{theorem}[thm:uniform-face-envelope]\label{thm:uniform-face-envelope}
Conditional on the established exact-capacity and closed sharp-endpoint mapping \(\mathfrak I_{\mathrm{PT}}\), for fixed \(J\), fixed \(K\), and \(\alpha\in(0,1/2)\), the procedure defined on every finite sample by \(\texttt{def:finite-sample-totalization}\) satisfies \[\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{(P_n)\in\mathcal P}P_n\{\Theta_{I,n}\subseteq\mathrm{CI}_n\}\ge1-\alpha.\] The infimum includes arrays with simultaneous endpoint ties, zero raw capacities, arbitrary rates of cellwise survivor-mass disappearance, and zero survivor-mass gaps; \(\mathrm{CI}_n\) contains no deterministic padding beyond its conditional multiplier critical value.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Set
\[
 Z_n=n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^n\psi_{w,n}(O_{ni}),
 \qquad
 T_n=\max\{\sqrt n(\widehat\theta_{L,n}-\theta_{L,n}),
              \sqrt n(\theta_{U,n}-\widehat\theta_{U,n})\}.
\]
By \(\texttt{lem:finite-secant-envelope}\), uniformly over \(\mathcal P\), on events with probability tending to one,
\[
 T_n\le S_n+o_{P_n}(1),
 \quad
 S_n=\max_{r\in\mathcal R_n^0(P_n)}
 \{g^L_{n,r}{}'Z_n,g^U_{n,r}{}'Z_n\},
 \quad
 \mathcal R_n^0(P_n)\subseteq\mathcal R_n^{\mathrm{ret}}.
\]

We verify the Gaussian experiment directly. Under cell mass and instrument overlap, every coordinate of \(\psi_{w,n}\) is bounded by a constant depending only on \(p_0,\varepsilon,J,K\), and it has mean zero. For fixed \(t\in\mathbb R^{2JK}\), a third-order expansion, uniform in the law, gives
\[
 E_{P_n}\exp(i t'Z_n)
 =\left(1-\frac{t'\Sigma_{w,n}t}{2n}
          +O(n^{-3/2}\lVert t\rVert^3)\right)^n.
\]
Thus, along every subsequence on which \(\Sigma_{w,n}\to\Sigma\),
\[
 E_{P_n}\exp(i t'Z_n)\longrightarrow
 \exp(-t'\Sigma t/2).
\]
Uniform second moments give tightness, and the last display identifies every subsequential limit as \(N(0,\Sigma)\). This proves the required fixed-dimensional triangular-array Gaussian approximation without a growing-dimensional argument.

The covariance is nondegenerate on the nonredundant observable contrast space. Let \(\gamma_n\) stack the conditional contrasts, let \(\psi_{\gamma,n}\) be their influence vector under total-sample scaling, and let \(D_{p,n}\) be block diagonal with blocks \(p_{n,x}I_{2K}\). Directly from the product rule used in the first-stage calculation,
\[
 \psi_{w,n}=D_{p,n}\psi_{\gamma,n}
 +\bigl((\mathbf1\{X_{ni}=x\}-p_{n,x})\gamma_{n,x}\bigr)_{x\in\mathcal X}.
\]
The first summand has conditional mean zero given \(X_{ni}\), while the second is a function of \(X_{ni}\); their cross covariance is zero. Therefore
\[
 \Sigma_{w,n}\succeq D_{p,n}\Sigma_{c,n}D_{p,n}
 \succeq p_0^2\underline\lambda I
\]
on that space, by \(\texttt{ass:raw-covariance-band}\) and \(\texttt{ass:cell-mass}\). Bounded influence coordinates give a finite uniform upper eigenvalue bound.

Define
\[
 \widehat\Sigma_{w,n}=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n
 \widehat\psi_{w,n}(O_{ni})\widehat\psi_{w,n}(O_{ni})'.
\]
On the overlap event used in the secant proof, the score formula is Lipschitz in the finite empirical-frequency vector. Hence
\[
 n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n
 \lVert\widehat\psi_{w,n}(O_{ni})-\psi_{w,n}(O_{ni})\rVert^2
 =o_{P_n}(1)
\]
uniformly. Each entry of the oracle sample covariance has variance at most \(C/n\). Expanding
\(\widehat\psi\widehat\psi'-\psi\psi'\) and applying Cauchy--Schwarz yields
\[
 \lVert\widehat\Sigma_{w,n}-\Sigma_{w,n}\rVert=o_{P_n}(1).
\]
The endpoint and aggregate-mass consistency already proved, \(M_n\ge m_0\), and finiteness of \(\mathcal R\) similarly give
\[
 \max_{r\in\mathcal R_n^0(P_n),\,e\in\{L,U\}}
 \lVert\widehat g^e_{n,r}-g^e_{n,r}\rVert=o_{P_n}(1).
\]
Conditionally on the data, \(G_n^*\) is exactly centered Gaussian with covariance \(\widehat\Sigma_{w,n}\).

We now justify quantile transfer, including zero gradients. Restrict coefficient vectors to the nonredundant observable space. The pairs \((A_r^e,B_r)\) range over a fixed finite collection. If \(A_r^e\) is not proportional to \(B_r\), compactness of \([0,1]\) and finiteness give
\[
 c_A:=\min_{(r,e):\,A_r^e\notin\operatorname{span}(B_r)}
       \inf_{\vartheta\in[0,1]}\lVert A_r^e-\vartheta B_r\rVert>0.
\]
The exact-capacity mapping identifies \(M_n\) with the unconditional survivor-complier probability, so \(M_n\le1\). Each such face gradient therefore has norm at least \(c_A\). If \(A_r^e=cB_r\), the quotient on that region is the constant \(c\), so its directional derivative along any segment lying in that region is zero. Thus, if \(\mathcal R_n^0(P_n)\) contains a nonconstant region, it supplies a Gaussian form whose variance is at least
\[
 v_0=p_0^2\underline\lambda c_A^2>0.
\]
If \(\mathcal R_n^0(P_n)\) contains no nonconstant region, then on the localization event every encountered quotient piece is constant. Continuity across the encountered boundaries makes those constants equal, so both corresponding endpoint secants are exactly zero. This is the constant-or-nondegenerate dichotomy specific to the finite linear-fractional capacity map.

Let \(q_n^0\) be the conditional \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile of the multiplier maximum restricted to \(\mathcal R_n^0(P_n)\), using estimated gradients. Set inclusion gives, pathwise,
\[
 c_{n,1-\alpha}\ge q_n^0.
\]
In the nonconstant case the limiting Gaussian maximum dominates a \(N(0,v_0)\) coordinate, and hence its \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile is at least
\(\sqrt{v_0}\,\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha)>0\), because \(\alpha<1/2\). At every positive \(t\), a finite Gaussian maximum has no atom: the event that the maximum equals \(t\) is contained in the union, over nondegenerate coordinates, of their zero-probability equality events; degenerate coordinates equal zero. This continuity is uniform at the relevant quantiles. Indeed, if \(t\ge q_0>0\) and \(0<\eta<q_0/2\), then the density of \(N(0,\sigma^2)\) on \([t-\eta,t+\eta]\), maximized over \(\sigma>0\), is bounded by a constant times \(q_0^{-1}\); a finite union bound therefore makes the probability that any coordinate lies in this interval \(O(\eta/q_0)\), uniformly in all coordinate variances.

To finish rigorously, suppose the desired coverage bound failed along a sequence of laws. From bounded covariances, endpoint values, masses, and the finite collection of possible inner index sets, extract a subsequence on which all of these converge and the inner index set is constant. The characteristic-function calculation gives convergence of \(Z_n\); covariance and gradient consistency give conditional convergence of the multiplier maximum, for example by coupling both Gaussian vectors to the same fixed-dimensional standard normal vector. In the nonconstant case the preceding continuity transfers generalized-inverse quantiles and absorbs the \(o_{P_n}(1)\) secant remainder. In the constant case the relevant endpoint errors are exactly zero on the localization event. Both cases give limiting probability at least \(1-\alpha\), contradicting the assumed failure. Therefore
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{(P_n)\in\mathcal P}
 P_n\{T_n\le c_{n,1-\alpha}\}\ge1-\alpha.
\]

The critical value is nonnegative. Indeed, the retained family is nonempty, and its maximum dominates any one centered Gaussian form. A nondegenerate centered Gaussian form has median zero, while a degenerate one equals zero; since \(1-\alpha>1/2\), its generalized-inverse \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile cannot be negative. Consequently \(T_n\le c_{n,1-\alpha}\) implies
\[
 \widehat\theta_{L,n}-c_{n,1-\alpha}/\sqrt n\le\theta_{L,n},
 \qquad
 \theta_{U,n}\le\widehat\theta_{U,n}+c_{n,1-\alpha}/\sqrt n.
\]
Clipping preserves these inequalities because \(0\le\theta_{L,n}\le\theta_{U,n}\le1\). The algebra in the secant proof also gives
\(0\le\widehat\theta_{L,n}\le\widehat\theta_{U,n}\le1\) whenever \(\widehat M_n>0\), and both estimates are zero under the denominator fallback, so the off-support singleton convention is asymptotically irrelevant. Finally, \(\texttt{def:imported-partialtransport-mapping}\) invokes the established result \(\mathfrak I_{\mathrm{PT}}\), which proves rather than defines the identification equality
\[
 \Theta_{I,n}=[\theta_{L,n},\theta_{U,n}].
\]
Thus the last two endpoint inequalities imply \(\Theta_{I,n}\subseteq\mathrm{CI}_n\). This proves the stated uniform whole-set coverage, including simultaneous ties and changing survivor support, with no deterministic padding.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the headline uniform inference result at the joint boundary geometry actually generated by survivor-benefit bounds. \textit{Gap.} Gabriel, Sachs, and Jensen document undercoverage of the ordinary \(n\)-out-of-\(n\) bootstrap at boundary laws and use a conservative \(m\)-out-of-\(n\) remedy; they do not prove uniform, no-padding, whole-identified-set coverage for the projected survivor-complier quotient with changing support. Fang and Santos and Firpo, Galvao, and Parker do not supply that field-level result either. \textit{Consumer.} A Job Corps analysis can report an honest confidence interval for the full sharp range of the probability that an always-employed complier moves to a higher wage category.

\begin{theorem}[thm:oracle-face-equivalence]\label{thm:oracle-face-equivalence}
Under \(\texttt{ass:oracle-separation}\), the retained empirical family equals the population active projection-and-branch family with probability tending to one, and the conditional critical value \(c_{n,1-\alpha}\) differs by \(o_{P_n}(1)\) from the oracle face-envelope critical value that knows this family.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(\mathcal R_n^{\mathrm{act}}(P_n)\) be the population family of all compatible regions whose defining projection, gap, survivor-minimum, lower-branch, upper-cut, and mass-cap inequalities hold at \(w_n\), including every branch tied at equality. Under \(\texttt{ass:oracle-separation}\), every invalid alternative violates at least one such inequality by at least \(\delta_0\).

The first-stage expansion established in \(\texttt{thm:uniform-face-envelope}\) gives
\[
 \lVert\widehat w_n-w_n\rVert=O_{P_n}(n^{-1/2}).
\]
Every defining slack belongs to a fixed finite affine family. Thus, writing \(s\) for any normalized slack and \(\widehat s\) for its empirical value,
\[
 \max_s|\widehat s-s|=O_{P_n}(n^{-1/2})=o_{P_n}(a_n).
\]
By \(\texttt{ass:tolerance-vanishes}\), \(a_n<\delta_0/2\) eventually. On an event with probability tending to one, every zero-slack active choice is within \(a_n\) of its empirical boundary, every strictly active sign is selected, and every inactive choice remains more than \(a_n\) from the corresponding boundary, maximum, or minimum. Both constructions impose the same Cartesian compatibility restrictions. Hence
\[
 P_n\{\mathcal R_n^{\mathrm{ret}}=\mathcal R_n^{\mathrm{act}}(P_n)\}
 \longrightarrow1.
\]

Define the oracle critical value as the generalized-inverse \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile of
\[
 \max_{r\in\mathcal R_n^{\mathrm{act}}(P_n)}
 \{g^L_{n,r}{}'G_n,g^U_{n,r}{}'G_n\},
 \qquad G_n\sim N(0,\Sigma_{w,n}).
\]
The covariance and quotient calculations in \(\texttt{thm:uniform-face-envelope}\) give
\[
 \lVert\widehat\Sigma_{w,n}-\Sigma_{w,n}\rVert=o_{P_n}(1),
 \qquad
 \max_{r\in\mathcal R_n^{\mathrm{act}}(P_n),\,e\in\{L,U\}}
 \lVert\widehat g^e_{n,r}-g^e_{n,r}\rVert=o_{P_n}(1).
\]
On the family-equality event, empirical and oracle maxima have the same fixed finite index set. Coupling their Gaussian vectors through a common standard normal vector shows that the maxima differ by \(o_{P_n}(1)\) conditionally.

If at least one oracle face is nonconstant, the constant-or-nondegenerate argument in \(\texttt{thm:uniform-face-envelope}\) bounds its variance away from zero. The oracle \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile is then positive and its distribution function is continuous there, so generalized-inverse quantiles converge. If every active face is constant, activity implies \(A_r^e=\theta_{e,n}B_r\). On the recovered local family the empirical quotient has that same constant value, whence \(\widehat g^e_{n,r}=0\) exactly and both maxima and both critical values equal zero. In either case,
\[
 c_{n,1-\alpha}-c^{\mathrm{oracle}}_{n,1-\alpha}=o_{P_n}(1).
\]
Together with recovery of the family, this proves both assertions.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem shows that uniform protection has no first-order critical-value cost on regular separated faces. \textit{Gap.} Generalized moment selection handles slack inequalities, but not simultaneous support release and nested ratio branches in this explicit capacity map. \textit{Consumer.} Applied users obtain ordinary smooth-face precision when Job Corps cells are well separated while retaining protection when they are not.

\begin{proposition}[prop:smooth-face-reduction]\label{prop:smooth-face-reduction}
If each cell survivor mass is positive and every endpoint has a unique separated projection, minimum, and cut branch, then \(\mathrm{CI}_n\) is first-order equivalent to the ordinary joint Gaussian delta-method interval based on the two active quotient gradients.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Under the stated premise and \(\texttt{ass:oracle-separation}\), there are unique compatible active regions \(r_L\) and \(r_U\) for the two endpoint numerators, with the common projection and survivor-minimum choices fixed. The separation margin and
\(\lVert\widehat w_n-w_n\rVert=O_{P_n}(n^{-1/2})\) imply that the segment from \(w_n\) to \(\widehat w_n\) stays in those regions with probability tending to one. Positivity of each cell survivor mass ensures that the displayed quotient formulas are defined there; the aggregate denominator is already bounded below in the model class.

Let \(g_n^L=g^L_{n,r_L}\) and \(g_n^U=g^U_{n,r_U}\). On the stable regions, the ordinary quotient rule and the first-stage Taylor expansion give
\[
 \sqrt n(\widehat\theta_{L,n}-\theta_{L,n})
 =g_n^{L\prime}n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^n\psi_{w,n}(O_{ni})+o_{P_n}(1),
\]
\[
 \sqrt n(\theta_{U,n}-\widehat\theta_{U,n})
 =g_n^{U\prime}n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^n\psi_{w,n}(O_{ni})+o_{P_n}(1).
\]
By \(\texttt{thm:oracle-face-equivalence}\), the retained family equals these active choices with probability tending to one, and its critical value differs by \(o_{P_n}(1)\) from
\[
 q_{1-\alpha}\!\left(
 \max\{g_n^{L\prime}G_n^*,g_n^{U\prime}G_n^*\}
 \mid O_{n1},\ldots,O_{nn}\right).
\]
This is exactly the ordinary joint one-sided Gaussian delta-method critical value for the two signed endpoint errors. The two expansions and critical-value equivalence show that the corresponding lower and upper confidence limits differ from those of the ordinary joint Gaussian interval by \(o_{P_n}(n^{-1/2})\). Clipping at the known support boundaries is common to both constructions and preserves this first-order equivalence.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the required regular-case reduction and guards against paying for nonexistent ties. \textit{Gap.} It recovers the familiar smooth endpoint limit as a corollary rather than imposing smoothness on the main class. \textit{Consumer.} It lets standard software and influence-function calculations validate the new implementation on well-separated empirical designs.

\begin{theorem}[thm:ordinary-ordinal-reduction]\label{thm:ordinary-ordinal-reduction}
Under perfect compliance, \(D_{ni}(0)=0\) and \(D_{ni}(1)=1\), and universal selection, \(S_{ni}(0)=S_{ni}(1)=1\), one has \(\ell_{n,x,k}=f_{1,n,x,k}\), \(h_{n,x,k}=f_{0,n,x,k}\), \(w_{n,x}=p_{n,x}(f_{1,n,x,0},\ldots,f_{1,n,x,K-1},f_{0,n,x,0},\ldots,f_{0,n,x,K-1})^{\prime}\), and \(M_n=1\). Consequently \(\Theta_{I,n}=[\sum_x p_{n,x}b_L((f_{1,n,x,0},\ldots,f_{1,n,x,K-1},f_{0,n,x,0},\ldots,f_{0,n,x,K-1})^{\prime}),\sum_x p_{n,x}b_U((f_{1,n,x,0},\ldots,f_{1,n,x,K-1},f_{0,n,x,0},\ldots,f_{0,n,x,K-1})^{\prime})]\), the covariate-stratified ordinary sharp ordinal-benefit bounds; without covariate stratification these are the Lu--Ding--Dasgupta bounds.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(n\), \(x\in\mathcal X\), and \(k\in\mathcal Y\).  All equalities between potential variables in the hypotheses are understood \(P_n\)-almost surely.  By \(\texttt{ass:cell-mass}\) and the declared condition \(p_0>0\),
\[
P_n(X_{n1}=x)=p_{n,x}\ge p_0>0.
\]
By \(\texttt{ass:instrument-overlap}\), the definition of \(e_{n,x}\), and \(0<\varepsilon<1/2\),
\[
P_n(Z_{n1}=1\mid X_{n1}=x)=e_{n,x}\ge\varepsilon>0,
\qquad
P_n(Z_{n1}=0\mid X_{n1}=x)=1-e_{n,x}\ge\varepsilon>0.
\]
Thus every conditional probability below is defined on an event of positive probability.

Perfect compliance and treatment consistency give
\[
D_{n1}=D_{n1}(Z_{n1})=Z_{n1}.
\]
Universal selection and selection consistency then give
\[
S_{n1}=S_{n1}(D_{n1})=1.
\]
On this event, outcome consistency gives \(Y_{n1}=Y_{n1}(D_{n1})\).  Consequently, using first the definition of the observed selected-outcome cells and then conditional instrument exogeneity,
\[
\begin{aligned}
q^1_{n,1,x,k}
&=P_n(D_{n1}=1,S_{n1}=1,Y_{n1}=k\mid Z_{n1}=1,X_{n1}=x)\\
&=P_n(Y_{n1}(1)=k\mid Z_{n1}=1,X_{n1}=x)\\
&=P_n(Y_{n1}(1)=k\mid X_{n1}=x)
=f_{1,n,x,k},\\
q^1_{n,0,x,k}&=0,\\
q^0_{n,0,x,k}
&=P_n(D_{n1}=0,S_{n1}=1,Y_{n1}=k\mid Z_{n1}=0,X_{n1}=x)\\
&=P_n(Y_{n1}(0)=k\mid Z_{n1}=0,X_{n1}=x)\\
&=P_n(Y_{n1}(0)=k\mid X_{n1}=x)
=f_{0,n,x,k},\\
q^0_{n,1,x,k}&=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
The two zero equalities follow because the conditioning event fixes \(D_{n1}=Z_{n1}\).  The definitions of the raw capacities therefore yield
\[
\ell_{n,x,k}=q^1_{n,1,x,k}-q^1_{n,0,x,k}=f_{1,n,x,k},
\qquad
h_{n,x,k}=q^0_{n,0,x,k}-q^0_{n,1,x,k}=f_{0,n,x,k}.
\]

Put
\[
\varphi_{n,x}
=\bigl(f_{1,n,x,0},\ldots,f_{1,n,x,K-1},
f_{0,n,x,0},\ldots,f_{0,n,x,K-1}\bigr)^{\prime}.
\]
The definition of the unconditional raw-capacity block now gives
\[
w_{n,x}=p_{n,x}\varphi_{n,x}.
\]
Every coordinate of \(\varphi_{n,x}\) is nonnegative, so the coordinatewise definition of \(\Pi_+\) implies
\[
(\Pi_+w_n)_x=w_{n,x}=p_{n,x}\varphi_{n,x}.
\]
Because each conditional potential-outcome marginal is a probability mass function,
\[
\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}f_{1,n,x,k}
=\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}f_{0,n,x,k}=1.
\]
Hence, by the definition of \(m\),
\[
m((\Pi_+w_n)_x)
=\min\left\{p_{n,x}\sum_k f_{1,n,x,k},
p_{n,x}\sum_k f_{0,n,x,k}\right\}=p_{n,x}.
\]
Summing over the finite support and using \(\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}p_{n,x}=1\) proves
\[
M_n=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}m((\Pi_+w_n)_x)=1.
\]

We next use elementary positive homogeneity of the corrected finite cut formulas.  For every \(c\ge0\) and \(v\in\mathbb R_+^{2K}\), multiplication of every affine branch by \(c\), together with
\(\min\{ca,0\}=c\min\{a,0\}\), gives
\[
b_L(cv)=c,b_L(v),
\qquad
b_U(cv)=c,b_U(v).
\]
This is a direct max--min calculation from the corrected definitions of \(b_L\) and \(b_U\); no optimization problem is latent in the step.  Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
N_{L,n}
&=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}b_L((\Pi_+w_n)_x)
=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}p_{n,x}b_L(\varphi_{n,x}),\\
N_{U,n}
&=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}b_U((\Pi_+w_n)_x)
=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}p_{n,x}b_U(\varphi_{n,x}).
\end{aligned}
\]
Substitution of these identities and \(M_n=1\) into \(\texttt{def:sharp-benefit-set}\), whose sharpness and endpoint attainability are supplied by \(\texttt{def:imported-partialtransport-mapping}\), yields exactly
\[
\Theta_{I,n}
=\left[
\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}p_{n,x}b_L(\varphi_{n,x}),
\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}p_{n,x}b_U(\varphi_{n,x})
\right].
\]
Moreover, perfect compliance implies \(C_{n1}=1\), and universal selection implies \(A_{n1}=1\).  Thus the causal target reduces to
\[
\theta_n=P_n\{Y_{n1}(1)>Y_{n1}(0)\},
\]
whereas the displayed interval is an observed-marginal sharp identifying functional; their relationship is the sharpness conclusion imported above, not a definition of \(\theta_n\).

It remains to verify the named ordinary-bound reduction.  Suppress \(x\), write
\(r_k=f_{1,n,k}\), \(s_k=f_{0,n,k}\), and define
\[
\Delta_j=\sum_{k=j}^{K-1}(r_k-s_k),
\qquad 0\le j\le K,
\]
so \(\Delta_0=\Delta_K=0\).  Equal total masses make the gap term in the corrected lower formula zero, and hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
b_L((r,s))
&=\max\left\{0,\max_{0\le t<K}\sum_{k\le t}(s_k-r_k)\right\}\\
&=\max\left\{0,\max_{0\le t<K}\Delta_{t+1}\right\}
=\max_{0\le j<K}\Delta_j.
\end{aligned}
\]
For the upper formula, for every \(0\le j<K\),
\[
\sum_{k<j}s_k+\sum_{k>j}r_k
=1+\Delta_j-r_j.
\]
The cut with \(j=0\) is at most one, so the outer mass cap is redundant, and
\[
b_U((r,s))
=1+\min_{0\le j<K}(\Delta_j-r_j).
\]
These are precisely the sharp strict-benefit formulas in \(\texttt{lem:lu-ding-dasgupta-strict-benefit-bounds}\), with their category count renamed from \(J\) to \(K\).  This proves the unstratified Lu--Ding--Dasgupta reduction.  Applying the same finite formulas conditionally in each positive-mass covariate cell and averaging with \(p_{n,x}\), as already derived above, proves the stated covariate-stratified reduction.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This reduction checks that the selected-complier capacity formulas collapse to the established ordinal-benefit geometry when compliance and observability cease to be latent. \textit{Gap.} Lu, Ding, and Dasgupta establish the ordinary ordinal-benefit bounds; this proposition is a required reduction, not a novelty claim. \textit{Consumer.} It gives implementers an exact benchmark for validating the capacity and min-cut code against standard two-arm ordinal data.

\begin{proposition}[prop:witness-legality]\label{prop:witness-legality}
Set \(J=2\), \(K=3\), and \(\sigma_{n,1}=\sigma_{n,2}=1\). For \(n\ge16\), the atom table \(\mathcal A_n^{\mathrm{wit}}\) sums to one and induces \(P_n^{\mathrm{wit}}\) with \(p_{n,1}=p_{n,2}=e_{n,1}=e_{n,2}=1/2\). Its only nonzero selected observed cells, for every \(x\in\{1,2\}\) and \(k\in\{0,1,2\}\), are \[q^1_{n,0,x,k}=1/12,\quad q^1_{n,1,x,k}=1/12+m^{\mathrm{wit}}_{n,x}/3,\quad q^0_{n,1,x,k}=1/12,\quad q^0_{n,0,x,k}=1/12+m^{\mathrm{wit}}_{n,x}/3.\] Hence \(\ell_{n,x,k}=h_{n,x,k}=m^{\mathrm{wit}}_{n,x}/3\), \(m((\Pi_+w_n)_x)=m^{\mathrm{wit}}_{n,x}/2\), \(b_L((\Pi_+w_n)_x)=0\), and \(b_U((\Pi_+w_n)_x)=m^{\mathrm{wit}}_{n,x}/3\). Thus \(M_n=1/8+1/(2\sqrt n)\), \(N_{L,n}=0\), \(N_{U,n}=1/12+1/(3\sqrt n)\), and \(\Theta_{I,n}=[0,2/3]\). The two conditional-contrast covariance blocks are \(\Sigma^{\mathrm{wit}}(m^{\mathrm{wit}}_{n,1})\) and \(\Sigma^{\mathrm{wit}}(m^{\mathrm{wit}}_{n,2})\); the eigenvalues of \(\Sigma^{\mathrm{wit}}(m)\) are \((2+4m)/3\) with multiplicity four, \((2+4m-4m^2)/3\), and \((1-4m^2)/3\). Consequently every eigenvalue of \(\Sigma_{c,n}\) lies in \([1/4,1]\), and \(P_n^{\mathrm{wit}}\in\mathcal P\) for constants satisfying \(p_0\le1/2\), \(\varepsilon\le1/2\), \(m_0\le1/8\), \(\underline\lambda\le1/4\), and \(\overline\lambda\ge1\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Technique.} We use direct finite-atom enumeration for the probability and identification calculations, the quotient-rule influence function for conditional multinomial probabilities for the covariance calculation, and an explicit rank-two spectral decomposition for the eigenvalue calculation.

Write \(m_x=m^{\mathrm{wit}}_{n,x}\). Because \(n\geq16\),
\[
0<m_2=n^{-1/2}\leq\frac14,
\qquad
m_1=\frac14.
\]
Consequently all four displayed atom weights in \(\mathcal A_n^{\mathrm{wit}}\) are nonnegative. For each fixed \((x,z,r)\), their sum is
\[
\frac1{48}+\frac1{48}+\frac{m_x}{12}
+\frac{1/2-m_x}{12}
=\frac1{24}+\frac1{24}=\frac1{12}.
\]
There are three values of \(r\), so
\[
P_n^{\mathrm{wit}}(X_{n1}=x,Z_{n1}=z)=3\left(\frac1{12}\right)=\frac14
\]
for each of the four pairs \((x,z)\). Summing over those pairs proves that the table has total mass one. It also gives
\[
p_{n,x}=\sum_{z=0}^1\frac14=\frac12,
\qquad
e_{n,x}=\frac{P_n^{\mathrm{wit}}(X_{n1}=x,Z_{n1}=1)}{p_{n,x}}
=\frac{1/4}{1/2}=\frac12,
\]
where the division is valid because \(p_{n,x}=1/2>0\).

Conditional on \((X_{n1},Z_{n1})=(x,z)\), the four rows for a fixed \(r\) have probabilities
\[
\frac1{12},\quad \frac1{12},\quad
\frac{m_x}{3},\quad \frac{1/2-m_x}{3},
\]
respectively. This conditional distribution of
\[
(D_{n1}(0),D_{n1}(1),S_{n1}(0),S_{n1}(1),Y_{n1}(0),Y_{n1}(1))
\]
does not depend on \(z\), which verifies \(\texttt{ass:instrument-exogeneity}\). The construction in \(\texttt{def:witness-atom-law}\) generates the observed variables as \(D_{ni}=D_{ni}(Z_{ni})\), \(S_{ni}=S_{ni}(D_{ni})\), and \(Y_{ni}=Y_{ni}(D_{ni})\) whenever \(S_{ni}=1\); hence treatment, selection, and outcome consistency hold. The potential selection and potential outcomes in every atom are indexed only by treatment, so \(\texttt{ass:exclusion}\) holds. The only treatment-type pairs are
\[
(D_{ni}(0),D_{ni}(1))\in\{(1,1),(0,0),(0,1)\},
\]
which proves \(D_{ni}(1)\geq D_{ni}(0)\) and therefore \(\texttt{ass:no-defiers}\). On both complier rows, \(S_{ni}(1)-S_{ni}(0)=0\). With \(\sigma_{n,x}=1\), this proves the inequality in \(\texttt{ass:weak-selection-monotonicity}\). Taking the observations in row \(n\) to be independent copies of this one-unit law gives \(\texttt{ass:iid-row}\).

We next enumerate the observed selected cells. At \(z=0\), only the always-taker row contributes to \((D,S,Y)=(1,1,k)\), whereas the never-taker and always-selected-complier rows contribute to \((D,S,Y)=(0,1,k)\). At \(z=1\), the always-taker and always-selected-complier rows contribute to \((D,S,Y)=(1,1,k)\), whereas only the never-taker row contributes to \((D,S,Y)=(0,1,k)\). Dividing the relevant atom weights by \(P_n^{\mathrm{wit}}(X_{n1}=x,Z_{n1}=z)=1/4\) yields, for every \(x\in\{1,2\}\) and \(k\in\{0,1,2\}\),
\[
q^1_{n,0,x,k}=\frac1{12},
\qquad
q^1_{n,1,x,k}=\frac1{12}+\frac{m_x}{3},
\]
\[
q^0_{n,1,x,k}=\frac1{12},
\qquad
q^0_{n,0,x,k}=\frac1{12}+\frac{m_x}{3}.
\]
The four atom types exhaust the support, so these are all the nonzero selected observed cells. By the definitions of the raw capacities,
\[
\ell_{n,x,k}=q^1_{n,1,x,k}-q^1_{n,0,x,k}=\frac{m_x}{3},
\qquad
h_{n,x,k}=q^0_{n,0,x,k}-q^0_{n,1,x,k}=\frac{m_x}{3}.
\]

Let \(v_x=(\Pi_+w_n)_x\). Since \(p_{n,x}=1/2\) and all preceding contrasts are positive, projection leaves the block unchanged and
\[
v_x^\ell=v_x^h=\frac{m_x}{6}(1,1,1)^{\prime}.
\]
Thus \(\sum_kv_{x,k}^\ell=\sum_kv_{x,k}^h=m_x/2\), and the definition of \(m(\cdot)\) gives
\[
m(v_x)=\frac{m_x}{2}.
\]
For every \(t\in\{0,1,2\}\),
\[
\sum_{k\leq t}(v_{x,k}^h-v_{x,k}^\ell)=0,
\qquad
\min\!\left\{\sum_kv_{x,k}^\ell-\sum_kv_{x,k}^h,0\right\}=0.
\]
The stated lower-capacity formula therefore gives \(b_L(v_x)=0\). For the upper capacity, exactly two coordinates occur in the two disjoint collections \(\{k:k<t\}\) and \(\{k:k>t\}\), for each \(t\in\{0,1,2\}\). Hence
\[
\sum_{k<t}v_{x,k}^h+\sum_{k>t}v_{x,k}^\ell
=2\frac{m_x}{6}=\frac{m_x}{3}.
\]
Since \(m_x/3\leq m_x/2=m(v_x)\), the stated upper-capacity formula gives \(b_U(v_x)=m_x/3\).

Summing the two cell contributions and substituting \(m_1=1/4\) and \(m_2=n^{-1/2}\) gives
\[
M_n=\frac{m_1+m_2}{2}=\frac18+\frac1{2\sqrt n},
\qquad
N_{L,n}=0,
\qquad
N_{U,n}=\frac{m_1+m_2}{3}=\frac1{12}+\frac1{3\sqrt n}.
\]
In particular \(M_n>0\), so both endpoint divisions are valid, and
\[
\frac{N_{L,n}}{M_n}=0,
\qquad
\frac{N_{U,n}}{M_n}
=\frac{(m_1+m_2)/3}{(m_1+m_2)/2}=\frac23.
\]
The established exact partial-transport mapping in \(\texttt{def:imported-partialtransport-mapping}\) proves that these observed-law functionals are the attained sharp endpoints; it does not define the causal parameter to equal either endpoint. Consequently
\[
\Theta_{I,n}=\left[\frac{N_{L,n}}{M_n},\frac{N_{U,n}}{M_n}\right]
=\left[0,\frac23\right].
\]
Under the particular displayed latent coupling, \(Y_{n1}(1)=Y_{n1}(0)\) on every atom. Moreover,
\[
P_n^{\mathrm{wit}}(C_{n1}=1,A_{n1}=1)
=\sum_{x=1}^2p_{n,x}m_x=M_n>0.
\]
Thus the causal target is well defined for this coupling and equals \(\theta_n=0\), which belongs to the sharp observed-law identified set above. Attainability of every other point in the interval by a compatible latent coupling is supplied by the imported identification result, not by this particular diagonal witness coupling.

It remains to calculate the covariance. Fix a covariate cell and abbreviate
\[
m=m_x,
\qquad
A=A_m=\frac{1/4+m}{3},
\qquad
B=\frac1{12},
\qquad
c=A+B.
\]
At \(z=1\), the probabilities of the three selected treated categories are each \(A\), and those of the three selected control categories are each \(B\). Give the treated indicators sign \(+1\) and the control indicators sign \(-1\). The resulting signed one-hot vector has mean
\[
v_0=(A,A,A,-B,-B,-B)^{\prime}
\]
and second-moment matrix \(\operatorname{diag}(A,A,A,B,B,B)\). At \(z=0\), give the treated indicators sign \(-1\) and the control indicators sign \(+1\). Its mean and second-moment matrix are
\[
v_1=(-B,-B,-B,A,A,A)^{\prime},
\qquad
\operatorname{diag}(B,B,B,A,A,A),
\]
respectively.

For clarity, the influence formula used here follows directly by differentiating a ratio. If \(q=P(H\mid C)=P(H\cap C)/P(C)\) and \(P(C)>0\), then perturbing its numerator and denominator by their centered indicators gives
\[
\frac{\mathbf 1\{H\cap C\}-P(H\cap C)}{P(C)}
-\frac{P(H\cap C)}{P(C)^2}\{\mathbf 1\{C\}-P(C)\}
=\frac{\mathbf 1\{C\}}{P(C)}\{\mathbf 1\{H\}-q\}.
\]
Here \(P_n^{\mathrm{wit}}(X_{n1}=x,Z_{n1}=z)=1/4>0\). The two instrument-cell contributions have disjoint supports and mean zero. Therefore the covariance of the six conditional contrasts under total-sample \(\sqrt n\) scaling is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Sigma_x
&=4\Bigl\{\operatorname{diag}(A,A,A,B,B,B)-v_0v_0^{\prime}\\
&\hspace{25mm}+\operatorname{diag}(B,B,B,A,A,A)-v_1v_1^{\prime}\Bigr\}\\
&=4\{cI_6-v_0v_0^{\prime}-v_1v_1^{\prime}\}
=\Sigma^{\mathrm{wit}}(m).
\end{aligned}
\]
For distinct covariate cells, the corresponding influence functions have disjoint supports and mean zero, so their cross-covariance is zero. Hence \(\Sigma_{c,n}\) is block diagonal with blocks \(\Sigma^{\mathrm{wit}}(m^{\mathrm{wit}}_{n,1})\) and \(\Sigma^{\mathrm{wit}}(m^{\mathrm{wit}}_{n,2})\).

We diagonalize each block explicitly. Let
\[
u_\ell=3^{-1/2}(1,1,1,0,0,0)^{\prime},
\qquad
u_h=3^{-1/2}(0,0,0,1,1,1)^{\prime}.
\]
On the four-dimensional orthogonal complement of \(\operatorname{span}\{u_\ell,u_h\}\), both \(v_0v_0^{\prime}\) and \(v_1v_1^{\prime}\) vanish, so the eigenvalue is
\[
4c=\frac{2+4m}{3}
\]
with multiplicity four. In the orthonormal basis \((u_\ell,u_h)\), the matrix \(v_0v_0^{\prime}+v_1v_1^{\prime}\) is
\[
3\begin{pmatrix}
A^2+B^2&-2AB\\
-2AB&A^2+B^2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its eigenvalues are \(3(A-B)^2\) and \(3(A+B)^2\). Since \(A-B=m/3\) and \(A+B=c=(1+2m)/6\), the remaining two eigenvalues of \(\Sigma^{\mathrm{wit}}(m)\) are
\[
4\{c-3(A-B)^2\}=\frac{2+4m-4m^2}{3}
\]
and
\[
4\{c-3(A+B)^2\}=\frac{1-4m^2}{3}.
\]
This proves the asserted spectrum and its multiplicities.

Both witness masses lie in \((0,1/4]\). On \([0,1/4]\), the first displayed eigenvalue lies in \([2/3,1]\); the second has derivative \((4-8m)/3>0\) and lies in \([2/3,11/12]\); and the third is decreasing and lies in \([1/4,1/3]\). Thus every eigenvalue of \(\Sigma_{c,n}\) lies in \([1/4,1]\), and the entire twelve-dimensional conditional-contrast space is nonredundant.

Finally, \(p_{n,x}=e_{n,x}=1/2\) implies \(p_{n,x}\geq p_0\) when \(p_0\leq1/2\), and \(\varepsilon\leq e_{n,x}\leq1-\varepsilon\) when \(\varepsilon\leq1/2\). Also
\[
M_n=\frac18+\frac1{2\sqrt n}\geq\frac18\geq m_0
\]
when \(m_0\leq1/8\). The spectral calculation verifies \(\texttt{ass:raw-covariance-band}\) whenever \(\underline\lambda\leq1/4\) and \(\overline\lambda\geq1\). Together with the sampling, conditional-IV, consistency, exclusion, treatment-monotonicity, and selection-monotonicity checks above, these are exactly the member properties in \(\texttt{def:model-class}\). Hence, in the rowwise membership notation of the proposition, \(P_n^{\mathrm{wit}}\in\mathcal P\) for every \(n\geq16\) under the stated restrictions on the constants.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The complete atom calculation shows that changing survivor support and simultaneous cut ties coexist with ordinary instrument overlap and a uniformly nondegenerate raw first stage. \textit{Gap.} This is an internal legality and stress-test result for the main uniform theorem, not a claim that a named published implementation fails. \textit{Consumer.} It gives method developers a reproducible boundary benchmark for testing Job Corps-style implementations without relying on an underdetermined latent law.

\begin{lemma}[lem:lu-ding-dasgupta-strict-benefit-bounds]\label{lem:lu-ding-dasgupta-strict-benefit-bounds}
Let \(r=(r_0,\ldots,r_{K-1})\) and \(s=(s_0,\ldots,s_{K-1})\) be probability vectors on \(\{0,\ldots,K-1\}\), and put \(\Delta_j=\sum_{k=j}^{K-1}(r_k-s_k)\). Among all couplings \((U,V)\) with respective marginals \(r\) and \(s\), the sharp lower and upper bounds on \(P(U>V)\) are \(\max_{0\le j<K}\Delta_j\) and \(1+\min_{0\le j<K}(\Delta_j-r_j)\), respectively.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[thm:automatic-studentized-face-retention]\label{thm:automatic-studentized-face-retention}
There is an explicit implementation of \(\mathcal H_{\mathrm{adapt}}\) requiring no analyst-specified sequence \(a_n\). For every full region \(r\in\mathcal R\), write its closure as the intersection of the affine half-spaces generated by its coordinate signs, gap sign, survivor-minimum comparisons, lower zero-or-cut comparisons, and upper cut-or-mass-cap comparisons. Obtain their inward-oriented normals by pairwise subtraction of the displayed finite branch coefficient vectors, so the closure inequalities have the form \(d'w\ge0\); restrict them to the nonredundant raw-capacity space, discard identically zero normals, and normalize the remainder. Call the resulting finite set for region \(r\) \(\mathcal D_r\), and put \(\mathcal D=\bigcup_r\mathcal D_r\). Define
\[
 \widehat\Sigma_{w,n}=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n
 \widehat\psi_{w,n}(O_{ni})\widehat\psi_{w,n}(O_{ni})',
 \qquad
 \widehat\sigma_{d,n}^2=d'\widehat\Sigma_{w,n}d,
\]
\[
 \beta_n=\{\log(n+\mathrm e^2)\}^{-1},\qquad
 H_n^*=\max_{d\in\mathcal D:\,\widehat\sigma_{d,n}>0}
 \frac{|d'G_n^*|}{\widehat\sigma_{d,n}},
\]
where the maximum of the empty set is zero, and let \(\widehat\kappa_n\) be the conditional generalized-inverse \((1-\beta_n)\)-quantile of \(H_n^*\). Retain
\[
 \widehat{\mathcal R}_n^{\mathrm{ad}}
 =\left\{r\in\mathcal R:
 d'\widehat w_n\ge-\widehat\kappa_n\widehat\sigma_{d,n}/\sqrt n
 \text{ for every }d\in\mathcal D_r\right\},
\]
with identically zero half-spaces retained automatically. Let \(\widetilde c_{n,1-\alpha}\) be the conditional \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile of the maximum of
\(\widehat g^L_{n,r}{}'G_n^*\) and \(\widehat g^U_{n,r}{}'G_n^*\) over \(r\in\widehat{\mathcal R}_n^{\mathrm{ad}}\), and form the clipped confidence interval by replacing \(c_{n,1-\alpha}\) with \(\widetilde c_{n,1-\alpha}\) in \(\mathrm{CI}_n\). Then, for fixed \(J,K\) and \(\alpha\in(0,1/2)\),
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{(P_n)\in\mathcal P}
 P_n\{\Theta_{I,n}\text{ is contained in the adaptive confidence interval}\}
 \ge1-\alpha.
\]
Under \(\texttt{ass:oracle-separation}\), \(\widehat{\mathcal R}_n^{\mathrm{ad}}\) equals the population active family with probability tending to one and
\(\widetilde c_{n,1-\alpha}-c^{\mathrm{oracle}}_{n,1-\alpha}=o_{P_n}(1)\). All normals and gradients are obtained from the displayed finite branches; the rule solves no latent linear program.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The construction is finite and well defined on every sample. In particular, the region containing \(\widehat w_n\) satisfies all of its defining inequalities with nonnegative slack, so \(\widehat{\mathcal R}_n^{\mathrm{ad}}\) is nonempty. We prove that its random studentized radii have exactly the two rate properties used by deterministic face localization.

The covariance decomposition in \(\texttt{thm:uniform-face-envelope}\), together with \(\texttt{ass:cell-mass}\) and \(\texttt{ass:raw-covariance-band}\), gives constants \(0<\underline\sigma\le\overline\sigma<\infty\) such that
\[
 \underline\sigma^2\le d'\Sigma_{w,n}d\le\overline\sigma^2
 \qquad(d\in\mathcal D)
\]
uniformly. Here the normals were normalized after restriction to the nonredundant space; identically zero restrictions were discarded. The score-covariance calculation in that theorem gives
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}
 |\widehat\sigma_{d,n}^2-d'\Sigma_{w,n}d|=o_{P_n}(1)
\]
uniformly. Thus, with probability tending to one uniformly, every empirical standard deviation lies in
\([\underline\sigma/2,2\overline\sigma]\).

Conditionally on the data, each standardized coordinate in \(H_n^*\) is exactly \(N(0,1)\). Since \(\mathcal D\) is fixed and finite, domination of one absolute standard normal and the Gaussian union bound imply on the preceding event
\[
 \Phi^{-1}(1-\beta_n/2)
 \le\widehat\kappa_n
 \le\sqrt{2\log(2|\mathcal D|/\beta_n)}.
\]
Because \(\beta_n=1/\log(n+\mathrm e^2)\), it follows that
\[
 \widehat\kappa_n\longrightarrow\infty,
 \qquad
 \widehat\kappa_n/\sqrt n\longrightarrow0
\]
in probability, uniformly. Therefore the branch-specific radii
\(t_{d,n}=\widehat\kappa_n\widehat\sigma_{d,n}/\sqrt n\) satisfy
\[
 \min_{d\in\mathcal D}\sqrt n\,t_{d,n}\longrightarrow\infty,
 \qquad
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D}t_{d,n}\longrightarrow0
\]
in probability, uniformly.

Now consider any region met by the segment from \(w_n\) to \(\widehat w_n\). If \(d\) is one of its normalized defining normals, comparison with a point of entry gives
\[
 d'\widehat w_n\ge-\lVert\widehat w_n-w_n\rVert.
\]
The uniform first-stage expansion gives
\(\lVert\widehat w_n-w_n\rVert=O_{P_n}(n^{-1/2})\). Since every \(\sqrt n\,t_{d,n}\) diverges, every segment region belongs to \(\widehat{\mathcal R}_n^{\mathrm{ad}}\) with probability tending to one uniformly. The pathwise quotient calculation in \(\texttt{lem:finite-secant-envelope}\) consequently gives the same signed-endpoint majorization, with the adaptive family replacing the deterministic retained family. The covariance consistency, finite Gaussian comparison, and constant-or-nondegenerate quantile argument in \(\texttt{thm:uniform-face-envelope}\) then give
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{(P_n)\in\mathcal P}
 P_n\{\Theta_{I,n}\text{ is contained in the adaptive interval}\}
 \ge1-\alpha.
\]
No deterministic padding is introduced: the only confidence-radius term is the conditional multiplier quantile divided by \(\sqrt n\).

Finally impose \(\texttt{ass:oracle-separation}\). Every inactive region violates a defining inequality by at least \(\delta_0\), while every tied active inequality has zero population slack. The maximum adaptive radius tends to zero, so inactive regions are rejected with probability tending to one. For a zero-slack active inequality,
\[
 \frac{\sqrt n\,|d'(\widehat w_n-w_n)|}{\widehat\sigma_{d,n}}=O_{P_n}(1)
 =o_{P_n}(\widehat\kappa_n),
\]
so every tied active choice is retained. Compatibility then gives
\[
 P_n\{\widehat{\mathcal R}_n^{\mathrm{ad}}
       =\mathcal R_n^{\mathrm{act}}(P_n)\}\longrightarrow1.
\]
Applying the finite Gaussian quantile comparison from \(\texttt{thm:oracle-face-equivalence}\) on this equality event proves
\(\widetilde c_{n,1-\alpha}-c^{\mathrm{oracle}}_{n,1-\alpha}=o_{P_n}(1)\). The fixed formula for \(\beta_n\) is part of the rule, so the analyst specifies neither \(a_n\) nor any other rate sequence.
\end{proof}

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The complete law \(P_n^{\mathrm{wit}}\) is a boundary stress test, not a theorem that any named published implementation undercovers. Numerical experiments with empirical optimizer-only and survivor-positive screening indicate sensitivity to discarded projection, cut, survivor-minimum, mass-cap, and denominator directions, but no published procedure has been verified to use exactly that composite shortcut. Accordingly no strict single-face undercoverage probability is claimed. Finite multiplier simulation error is also outside the exact-conditional-quantile theorem.

\section{Honest scope}

The proposal fixes \(J\) and \(K\), assumes aggregate survivor-complier mass and the nonredundant raw-capacity covariance remain bounded away from zero, and uses the nonnegative-orthant projection. It imposes no lower bound on an individual cell survivor mass, no survivor-gap sign margin, and no cut separation in the main theorem. The exact capacity and closed sharp-endpoint theorem \(\texttt{pid\_slate\_benefit\_partialtransport}\) is an established input and is not in the to-prove contribution graph. Every empirical object is defined on all finite samples by the stated denominator fallbacks, and the probability that any such fallback is activated vanishes uniformly. The open contribution is uniform no-padding whole-set inference for the imported mapping; the smooth unique-face and perfect-compliance reductions are proposed corollaries, and the finite atom array is a methodological stress test rather than an undercoverage claim. Automatic data-driven face retention is open. Continuous covariates, weak aggregate denominators, and simulated-quantile error are outside scope.
