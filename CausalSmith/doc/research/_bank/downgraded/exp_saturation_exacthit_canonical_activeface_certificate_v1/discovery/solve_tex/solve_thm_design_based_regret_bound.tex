%%% FIELD sharp-proof
Put \(R_{C,c}(z)=W_{C,c}(z)-\mu_z\) and
\[
b_{C,c,k}=M_k^{-1}\sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}R_{C,c}(z).
\]
Under either design in the statement, assignments are independent across clusters, a slice-\(k\) hit has probability \(r_{C,k}>0\), and conditional on that hit the assignment is uniform on \(\mathcal S_k\). For nominal-label Bernoulli assignment, the last assertion follows because, conditional on its treated count, an exchangeable Bernoulli assignment is uniform over assignments with that count. Hence
\[
\mathbb E\left[\frac{\mathbf 1\{Z_c\in\mathcal S_k\}R_{C,c}(Z_c)}{r_{C,k}}\right]=b_{C,c,k}.
\]
The definition of \(\widehat U_C\) therefore gives
\[
\mathbb E(\widehat U_{C,k})=M_k^{-1}\sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}\mu_z+C^{-1}\sum_{c=1}^Cb_{C,c,k}=U_{C,k}.
\]
Only assignment is random here, by Assumption~\(\texttt{ass:assignment-only-randomness}\).

Define the centered cluster vector
\[
X_{C,c,k}=\frac{\mathbf 1\{Z_c\in\mathcal S_k\}R_{C,c}(Z_c)}{r_{C,k}}-b_{C,c,k}.
\]
Distinct target slices are disjoint. Direct multiplication and the conditional uniformity just proved yield
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(X_{C,c,i},X_{C,c,j})=
\begin{cases}
r_{C,k}^{-1}M_k^{-1}\sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}R_{C,c}(z)^2-b_{C,c,k}^2,&i=j=k,\\
-b_{C,c,i}b_{C,c,j},&i\ne j.
\end{cases}
\tag{1}
\]
Moreover, \(\sqrt C(\widehat U_C-U_C)=C^{-1/2}\sum_{c=1}^CX_{C,c}\).

Let \(t_{C,c}(z)=W_{C,c}(z)\) and \(\lambda_C=C^{-1}\sum_c\delta_{t_{C,c}}\). The type space \(T\) is finite, so every subsequence has a further subsequence on which \(\lambda_C\to\lambda\). Assumption~\(\texttt{ass:cell-margin-convergence}\) gives
\[
\sum_{t:t(z)=w}\lambda_t=f_{z,w}.
\]
By the definition of \(\mu_z\) and Assumption~\(\texttt{ass:common-welfare-tie}\),
\[
\sum_t\lambda_tu_k(t)=M_k^{-1}\sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}\mu_z=\mu_0.
\]
Thus \(\lambda\in\Lambda(f,\mu_0)\). Averaging (1), using \(r_C\to r\) with every \(r_k>0\), and applying the definitions of \(A\), \(g\), and \(\Xi\), gives
\[
C^{-1}\sum_c\operatorname{Cov}(X_{C,c})\longrightarrow
\operatorname{diag}\{A_k(\lambda)/r_k\}_{k=1}^K-\Xi(\lambda)=\Sigma(r,\lambda).
\tag{2}
\]
Thus no subsequential asymptotic covariance lies outside \(\mathfrak S(r;f,\mu_0)\).

Conversely, fix \(\lambda\in\Lambda(f,\mu_0)\). Because \(T\) is finite, choose integers \(N_{C,t}\ge0\), summing to \(C\), such that \(N_{C,t}/C\to\lambda_t\), by rounding all but one of the finitely many \(C\lambda_t\)'s and assigning the residual to the last type. For every occurrence of type \(t\), realize each \(t(z)\in Wgrid\) by setting exactly \(n_0t(z)\) of the binary unit potential outcomes at assignment \(z\) equal to one. Unrestricted binary interference permits these choices independently across \(z\). The empirical type law then tends to \(\lambda\); consequently its cell margins tend to \(f\), its limiting slice means equal \(\mu_0\), and (2) tends to \(\Sigma(r,\lambda)\). This proves attainability.

For fixed \(r\) and \(Dtest\), expansion of \(A\) and \(\Xi\) makes \(\operatorname{tr}\{Dtest\,\Sigma(r,\lambda)\}\) affine in the finitely many coordinates \(\lambda_t\). The definition of \(\Lambda\) imposes finitely many linear equalities and nonnegativity constraints, and compactness gives both extrema. When \(f\), \(r\), and \(Dtest\) are rational, all coefficients are rational. If the maximization is written \(\max\{c^{\mathsf T}\lambda:A\lambda=b,\lambda\ge0\}\), primal and dual feasible vectors with equal objective certify optimality by weak duality,
\[
c^{\mathsf T}\lambda\le y^{\mathsf T}b\quad\text{whenever}\quad A\lambda=b,\ \lambda\ge0,\ A^{\mathsf T}y\ge c.
\]
Finite rational Gaussian elimination and simplex pivoting produce rational optimal basic primal and dual solutions. Replacing \(c\) by \(-c\) gives the lower support value. This proves the certificate claim.

%%% FIELD quadratic-proof
In the balanced instance every target cell has the Bernoulli distribution on \(\{0,1\}\) with mean \(1/2\). Let \(X\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\). In the first coupling set every coordinate on \(\mathcal S_1\cup\mathcal S_2\) equal to \(X\); in the second set every coordinate on \(\mathcal S_1\) equal to \(X\) and every coordinate on \(\mathcal S_2\) equal to \(1-X\). Complete the third slice in the same balanced fashion in both couplings and use the fixed off-target margins from \(\texttt{def:balanced-six-unit-instance}\). Each assignment-cell marginal is then \(f\), and every slice mean is \(1/2\), so both couplings belong to \(\Lambda(f,1/2)\). They give, respectively,
\[
(g_1,g_2)=(X-1/2,X-1/2),\qquad
(g_1,g_2)=(X-1/2,1/2-X).
\]
Thus \(A_1=A_2=1/4\), both relevant diagonal entries of \(\Xi\) are \(1/4\), and \(\Xi_{12}\) is respectively \(1/4\) and \(-1/4\). The definition of \(\Sigma\) gives common first two diagonal entries and respective off-diagonal entries \(-1/4\) and \(1/4\). Their difference has a \(2\)-by-\(2\) principal block with zero diagonal and nonzero off-diagonal, whose eigenvalues have opposite signs. Therefore neither matrix dominates the other in Loewner order.

Incomparability alone does not prove the final assertion, so we establish it separately. Write \(d_k=1/(4r_k)\). Since \(M_2=M_3=15\), for each type \(t\), each of \(g_2(t)\) and \(g_3(t)\) is an odd multiple of \(1/30\). Hence \(\Xi_{kk}\ge1/900\) for \(k=2,3\). Equality is attained by mixing with probability \(1/2\) a type having seven ones and eight zeros on the slice and its cellwise complement; every cell then has the required Bernoulli\((1/2)\) margin. Consequently
\[
\sup_{\Omega\in\mathfrak S(r;f,1/2)}e_k^{\mathsf T}\Omega e_k=d_k-1/900,\qquad k=2,3.
\tag{3}
\]
Constant-within-slice couplings attain \(g_2=-g_3\) and \(g_2=g_3\), respectively. Since a square is nonnegative,
\[
\sup_{\Omega\in\mathfrak S(r;f,1/2)}(e_2+e_3)^{\mathsf T}\Omega(e_2+e_3)=d_2+d_3,
\]
and
\[
\sup_{\Omega\in\mathfrak S(r;f,1/2)}(e_2-e_3)^{\mathsf T}\Omega(e_2-e_3)=d_2+d_3.
\tag{4}
\]
If one symmetric matrix \(Q\) represented every directional support, (3) would force \(Q_{22}=d_2-1/900\) and \(Q_{33}=d_3-1/900\). The plus equation in (4) would then force \(Q_{23}=1/900\), whereas the minus equation would force \(Q_{23}=-1/900\), a contradiction. Hence no such representing matrix exists; in particular, no matrix can both dominate the image and equal its support in every direction.

%%% FIELD kkt-proof
By Theorem~\(\texttt{thm:sharp-covariance-set}\), \(\Lambda(f,\mu_0)\) is a nonempty compact polytope and has finitely many vertices. For \(i<j\) and a vertex \(\lambda\), define
\[
\phi_{ij,\lambda}(r)=\frac{A_i(\lambda)}{r_i}+\frac{A_j(\lambda)}{r_j}-(e_i-e_j)^{\mathsf T}\Xi(\lambda)(e_i-e_j).
\]
The maximized expression is affine in \(\lambda\), so its maximum over the polytope occurs at a vertex and
\[
H(r;f)=\max_{i<j}\max_{\lambda\in\operatorname{vert}(\Lambda(f,\mu_0))}\phi_{ij,\lambda}(r).
\tag{5}
\]
Every \(A_k(\lambda)\ge0\). Thus each \(\phi_{ij,\lambda}\) is convex on the positive orthant, and their finite maximum is convex. Because \(0<\rho<1/K\), \(\Delta_K(\rho)\) is nonempty and compact, proving exact-count attainment by continuity. Every entry of \(B\) is strictly positive because \(1\le m_k,m_\ell\le n_0-1\); hence \(Bp\) is bounded away from zero as \(p\) ranges over the compact simplex, proving nominal-label attainment.

For an active scenario,
\[
[\nabla\phi_{ij,\lambda}(r)]_k=-\frac{A_k(\lambda)}{r_k^2}\mathbf1\{k\in\{i,j\}\}.
\tag{6}
\]
The subdifferential of the finite maximum (5) is the convex hull of its active gradients. Therefore the necessary-and-sufficient convex KKT condition for the exact problem is
\[
0\in\operatorname{conv}\{\nabla\phi_{ij,\lambda}(r):(i,j,\lambda)\text{ is active}\}+N_{\Delta_K(\rho)}(r).
\tag{7}
\]
Sufficiency follows from the subgradient inequality; necessity follows by separating zero from the sum when (7) fails, which gives a feasible descent direction. Carath\'eodory's theorem in \(\mathbb R^K\) expresses the convex-hull element using at most \(K+1\) active gradients. For the nominal problem the chain rule replaces every active gradient by \(B^{\mathsf T}\nabla\phi_{ij,\lambda}(Bp)\), and the same normal-cone argument applies on \(\Delta_K\).

Each active vertex scenario is certified by primal feasibility in the finite linear program and a dual feasible vector of equal objective, as in Theorem~\(\texttt{thm:sharp-covariance-set}\). Scenario activity, the primal and dual support certificates, nonnegative convex weights summing to one, and the normal multipliers thus form a finite certificate. Conversely, these data verify (7), and convexity proves global optimality.

%%% FIELD regret-proof
Let
\[
E_C=\sqrt C(\widehat U_C-U_C),\qquad h_C=\sqrt C(U_C-\mu_0\mathbf1_K).
\]
Theorem~\(\texttt{thm:sharp-covariance-set}\) gives, on a further subsequence, a \(\lambda\in\Lambda(f,\mu_0)\) for which the covariance of \(E_C\) tends to \(\Sigma(r,\lambda)\). For fixed \(a\in\mathbb R^K\), \(a^{\mathsf T}E_C\) is a sum of independent centered cluster contributions. Since \(r_{C,k}\to r_k>0\), inverse hit probabilities are eventually bounded; since \(0\le W\le1\), every unnormalized contribution is bounded. Thus each summand after the \(C^{-1/2}\) normalization is \(O(C^{-1/2})\), uniformly in the cluster. This verifies the stated finite-population Lindeberg condition (indeed its remainder is eventually zero at every fixed threshold). The Lindeberg--Feller characteristic-function proof, applied to this bounded triangular array and covariance limit (2), yields
\[
a^{\mathsf T}E_C\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,a^{\mathsf T}\Sigma(r,\lambda)a).
\]
The assertion remains true when the limiting variance is zero, by Chebyshev's inequality. Cram\'er--Wold therefore gives
\[
E_C\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,\Sigma(r,\lambda)).
\tag{8}
\]
Thus the Gaussian limit is derived from independent assignment, positivity, bounded fixed outcomes, and the declared Lindeberg condition rather than assumed.

Assumption~\(\texttt{ass:local-welfare-array}\) gives \(h_C\to h\), and the empirical-best selector acts on \(h_C+E_C\). Pass to a further subsequence on which the least maximizer \(i_C\) of \(h_C\) is constant, equal to \(i\). Then \(i\) maximizes \(h\). Put \(d_{ij}=h_i-h_j\ge0\). If \(j\ne i\) is selected, then \(h_{C,j}+E_{C,j}\ge h_{C,i}+E_{C,i}\). The scaled loss is at most the range of \(h_C\), which is uniformly bounded because \(h_C\to h\). Hence (8), one-dimensional contrast convergence, and bounded convergence for these loss indicators give
\[
\limsup_C\sqrt C\,\mathbb E\left[\max_kU_{C,k}-U_{C,\delta_{\mathrm{EB}}(\widehat U_C)}\right]
\le\sum_{j\ne i}d_{ij}\Phi\left(-\frac{d_{ij}}{\sqrt{V_{ij}(r,\lambda)}}\right),
\tag{9}
\]
where a term is zero if \(d_{ij}=0\), and also if \(V_{ij}=0\) and \(d_{ij}>0\), because the limiting contrast is then the negative constant \(-d_{ij}\).

By the definition of \(\kappa\), each term in (9) is at most \(\kappa\sqrt{V_{ij}(r,\lambda)}\), and every such variance is at most \(H(r;f)\). Therefore
\[
\limsup_C\sqrt C\,\mathbb E\left[\max_kU_{C,k}-U_{C,\delta_{\mathrm{EB}}(\widehat U_C)}\right]
\le\kappa(K-1)\sqrt{H(r;f)}.
\]
Uniformity over \(h\) in a compact set follows by contradiction: a violating sequence has a convergent \(h\)-subsequence; finiteness of \(T\) then supplies a compatible-coupling subsequence, and the preceding argument applies. Finite-\(C\) regret is bounded by one because every \(U_{C,k}\in[0,1]\), while \(\widehat U_C\) is defined on every assignment outcome. Finally, \(rStar\) and \(pStar\) minimize \(H\) on their respective domains by definition and Theorem~\(\texttt{thm:robust-design-kkt}\), and the displayed bound is increasing in \(H\).

For every target cell in the balanced instance, \((t(z)-1/2)^2=1/4\), whence \(A_k(\lambda)=1/4\). For any compatible coupling,
\[
V_{ij}(r,\lambda)=\frac1{4r_i}+\frac1{4r_j}-\mathbb E_\lambda\{g_i(t)-g_j(t)\}^2
\le\frac14\left(\frac1{r_i}+\frac1{r_j}\right).
\tag{10}
\]
The compatible coupling that sets every target-slice coordinate equal to one common \(X\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\) has \(g_1=g_2=g_3=X-1/2\), so equality in (10) holds simultaneously for all pairs. This is the sharp primal support witness, while nonnegativity of the displayed square is the matching support upper certificate. Taking the maximum proves the formula for \(H\).

For \(r\in\Delta_3(\rho)\), the maximum of the three pair sums is at least their average:
\[
\max_{i<j}(r_i^{-1}+r_j^{-1})\ge\frac23\sum_{k=1}^3r_k^{-1}\ge6,
\]
where the last inequality is Cauchy--Schwarz, \((\sum r_k)(\sum r_k^{-1})\ge9\). Equality holds at \(r=(1/3,1/3,1/3)\), which is feasible because \(\rho<1/3\). Thus \(H=3/2\). At that interior point all pair functions are active, and equal weights \(1/3\) make the weighted gradient proportional to \(-\mathbf1_3\), the normal to the simplex; this is exact KKT stationarity.

Substitution of \(n_0=6\) and \(m=(1,2,4)\) into the definition of \(B\) gives the displayed rational matrix. Direct common-denominator arithmetic gives

1024/4149+775/2766+3925/8298=1

and

\]
Thus all three pair functions are active and each equals \(2(1383/250)\); multiplication by \(1/4\) gives \(H(BpStar;f)=1383/500\).
