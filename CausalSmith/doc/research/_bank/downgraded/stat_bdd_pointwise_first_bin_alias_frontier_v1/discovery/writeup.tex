\section*{stat\_\allowbreak{}bdd\_\allowbreak{}pointwise\_\allowbreak{}first\_\allowbreak{}bin\_\allowbreak{}alias\_\allowbreak{}frontier}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} An analyst observes only \(Z_\Delta=(Y,T,Q_\Delta(R))\): bounded outcome, sharp-treatment side, and floor-rounded distance from one fixed boundary point; raw location, polar angle, and raw radius are unavailable. For each attainable released law \(Q\), its first-bin side means \(\eta_t(Q)=\mathbb E_Q[Y\mid T=t,Q_\Delta(R)=0]\) give clipped anchor intervals \([\underline{\theta}_t(Q),\overline{\theta}_t(Q)]\), where \(\underline{\theta}_t(Q)=\max\{0,\eta_t(Q)-L\Delta\}\) and \(\overline{\theta}_t(Q)=\min\{1,\eta_t(Q)+L\Delta\}\). Thus \(I_\Delta(Q)\) is contained in the directly computable certified outer interval \([\underline{\theta}_1(Q)-\overline{\theta}_0(Q),\overline{\theta}_1(Q)-\underline{\theta}_0(Q)]\); equality and exact sharp endpoints are not claimed. The maximal release-fiber diameter satisfies \(2c_L\Delta\leq\omega_\Delta\leq4L\Delta\). Supporting statistical frontiers give absolute-risk and honest-expected-length order \(\max\{n^{-1/4},\Delta\}\), with squared risk of the corresponding squared order.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Exact-coordinate boundary methods do not describe \(I_\Delta(Q)\) after angle and within-bin radius are removed.  Scalar measurement-error methods require auxiliary discrepancy information to restore identification, while manipulation-based partial-identification methods concern latent assignment types rather than a known deterministic annular release; neither evaluates the release fiber of a fixed bivariate point-boundary effect.

\paragraph{Niche.} The released population law fixes annular outcome distributions but not the latent boundary anchors.  In a legal uniform-half-disc Bernoulli subclass, the radial Jacobian admits an exact first-bin null direction, creating order-\(\Delta\) causal ambiguity even with an infinite sample; no design-wide orthogonality is asserted.

\paragraph{Fill.} The paper fixes the analyst's observation \(\sigma\)-field and leads with the release-fiber identified set \(I_\Delta(Q)\). From the observable first-bin means \(\eta_t(Q)\), it gives the directly computable certified outer interval \([\underline{\theta}_1(Q)-\overline{\theta}_0(Q),\overline{\theta}_1(Q)-\underline{\theta}_0(Q)]\), where \(\underline{\theta}_t(Q)=\max\{0,\eta_t(Q)-L\Delta\}\) and \(\overline{\theta}_t(Q)=\min\{1,\eta_t(Q)+L\Delta\}\), and proves \(2c_L\Delta\leq\omega_\Delta\leq4L\Delta\). Matching minimax-risk and honest-length frontiers then separate this population ambiguity from the additional \(n^{-1/4}\) sampling cost.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:iid-sampling]\label{ass:iid-sampling}
\((X_i,Y_i(0),Y_i(1))_{i=1}^n\) are independent and identically distributed under \(P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (independent random sampling, \cite{CattaneoTitiunikYu2026Distance}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:uniform-disc-design]\label{ass:uniform-disc-design}
For the fixed class constant \(c_0\in(0,1/2]\), \(\mathbb P_P(T=t,\lVert X-b\rVert_2<H)\geq c_0H^2\) for every \(t\in\{0,1\}\) and every \(H\in(0,1/2]\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (two-sided quadratic local design mass, \cite{CattaneoTitiunikYu2026Distance}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:sharp-boundary-assignment]\label{ass:sharp-boundary-assignment}
There is a known Borel side partition \((\mathcal X_{0,P},\mathcal X_{1,P})\) of \(\mathcal D\) such that \(T=\mathbf 1\{X\in\mathcal X_{1,P}\}\) almost surely under \(P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (sharp assignment across a known bivariate boundary, \cite{CattaneoTitiunikYu2026Distance}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:consistency]\label{ass:consistency}
\(Y=T Y(1)+(1-T)Y(0)\) almost surely under \(P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (potential-outcome consistency, \cite{HahnToddVanderKlaauw2001RD}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:radial-lipschitz]\label{ass:radial-lipschitz}
For every \(t\in\{0,1\}\), for \(P_X\)-almost every \(x\in\mathcal X_{t,P}\) satisfying \(\lVert x-b\rVert_2\leq1/2\),
\[
 |\mu_t(x)-\theta_t|\leq L\lVert x-b\rVert_2.
\]
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (side-specific point-anchored boundary envelope, \cite{ArmstrongKolesar2020Honest}).
\end{assumption}

\section{Definitions}

\begin{definition}[def:half-disc-class]\label{def:half-disc-class}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Unknown-\allowbreak{}design bivariate bounded-\allowbreak{}outcome point-\allowbreak{}anchored boundary class}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\mathcal P_L\) is the set of one-unit laws \(P\) such that \(P_X(\mathcal D)=1\); for every \(t\in\{0,1\}\) and \(H\in(0,1/2]\), \(\mathbb P_P(T=t,\lVert X-b\rVert_2<H)\geq c_0H^2\); \((\mathcal X_{0,P},\mathcal X_{1,P})\) is a known Borel partition of \(\mathcal D\) and \(T=\mathbf1\{X\in\mathcal X_{1,P}\}\) almost surely; \(Y=TY(1)+(1-T)Y(0)\) almost surely; for each \(t\) and for \(P_X\)-almost every \(x\in\mathcal X_{t,P}\), the conditional law of \(Y(t)\) given \(X=x\) is supported on \([0,1]\) and has mean \(\mu_t(x)\); and the point-anchored envelope
\[
 |\mu_t(x)-\theta_t|\leq L\lVert x-b\rVert_2
\]
holds for every \(t\) and for \(P_X\)-almost every side-\(t\) point \(x\) of radius at most \(1/2\), where \(\theta_t\in[0,1]\) and \(\mu_t(b)=\theta_t\).  The sampling condition \(\mathrm{ass:iid\text{-}sampling}\) is imposed on the sample and is not a member property of a one-unit law.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Member properties.} \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}uniform-\allowbreak{}disc-\allowbreak{}design}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}sharp-\allowbreak{}boundary-\allowbreak{}assignment}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}consistency}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}radial-\allowbreak{}lipschitz}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:rounded-experiment]\label{def:rounded-experiment}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Rounded-\allowbreak{}radius observation experiment}

The observation \(\sigma\)-field is exactly \(\sigma(Z_{\Delta,1},\ldots,Z_{\Delta,n})\); it excludes \(X_i\), angle, and raw \(R_i\).

\begin{definition}[def:bin-aware-estimator]\label{def:bin-aware-estimator}

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For \(t\in\{0,1\}\), let \(N_{t,n,\Delta}=\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf 1\{T_i=t,Q_\Delta(R_i)<H_{n,\Delta}\}\).  Define
\[
 \widehat m_{t,n,\Delta}=
 \begin{cases}
 \Pi_{[0,1]}\!\left(S_{t,n,\Delta}/N_{t,n,\Delta}\right),&N_{t,n,\Delta}>0,\\
 1/2,&N_{t,n,\Delta}=0,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 H_{n,\Delta}=\Delta\left\lceil n^{-1/4}/\Delta\right\rceil,
\]
where \(\Pi_{[0,1]}\) is Euclidean clipping, and set \(\widehat\tau_{n,\Delta}=\widehat m_{1,n,\Delta}-\widehat m_{0,n,\Delta}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{n}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}Delta}; \texttt{Z\_\allowbreak{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}Delta}.
\end{definition}

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{n}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}Delta}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathcal T\_\allowbreak{}\{n,\allowbreak{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}Delta\}\allowbreak{}}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathcal P\_\allowbreak{}L}.

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{n}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}Delta}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}alpha}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathcal C\_\allowbreak{}\{n,\allowbreak{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}Delta\}\allowbreak{}(\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}alpha)}.

\begin{definition}[def:total-honest-interval]\label{def:total-honest-interval}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Bias-\allowbreak{}enlarged conditional-\allowbreak{}Hoeffding interval}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For each \(t\in\{0,1\}\), set \([\ell_t,u_t]=[0,1]\) when \(N_{t,n,\Delta}=0\).  When \(N_{t,n,\Delta}=k>0\), put

 \overline Y_t=\frac{S_{t,n,\Delta}}k,
 \qquad
 r_{k,\alpha}=\sqrt{\frac{\log(4/\alpha)}{2k}},

With \(B_{n,\Delta}=LH_{n,\Delta}\), define the closed bounded interval

 C_{n,\Delta,\alpha}
 =\bigl[\ell_1-u_0-2B_{n,\Delta},\ u_1-\ell_0+2B_{n,\Delta}\bigr].
\]
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{n}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}Delta}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}alpha}; \texttt{L}; \texttt{Z\_\allowbreak{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}Delta}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:first-bin-alias]\label{def:first-bin-alias}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Embedded uniform-\allowbreak{}half-\allowbreak{}disc exact first-\allowbreak{}bin alias pair}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For each sign, construct \(P^{\mathrm{alias}}_{\pm,\Delta}\) by drawing \(X\) uniformly on \(\mathcal D\), taking \(\mathcal X_{1,P}=\{x\in\mathcal D:e_1^{\top}(x-b)\geq0\}\), assigning \(T=\mathbf1\{X\in\mathcal X_{1,P}\}\), and drawing conditionally independent Bernoulli potential outcomes given \(X\) with
\[
 \mu_{0,\pm}(x)=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \mu_{1,\pm}(x)=\frac12\pm c_L\Delta\phi\!\left(\frac{\lVert x-b\rVert_2}{\Delta}\right)
 \mathbf1\{\lVert x-b\rVert_2<\Delta\}.
\]
Finally set \(Y=TY(1)+(1-T)Y(0)\), where \(\phi(u)=(1-u)(1-2u)\) and \(c_L=\min\{L/8,1/16\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{L}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}Delta}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}phi}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:joint-lower-handle]\label{def:joint-lower-handle}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Embedded uniform-\allowbreak{}half-\allowbreak{}disc joint lower handle}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Let \(\mathfrak H_{n,\Delta}=(P_{0,n,\Delta},P_{1,n,\Delta})\) denote the following pair.  Under both laws, \(X\) is uniform on \(\mathcal D\), \(\mathcal X_{1,P}=\{x\in\mathcal D:e_1^{\top}(x-b)\geq0\}\), assignment is sharp, and the control mean is \(1/2\).  Under \(P_{0,n,\Delta}\) the treated mean is \(1/2\); under \(P_{1,n,\Delta}\) it is
\[
 \frac12+\frac{c_L}{4}\left\{(h_n-\lVert x-b\rVert_2)_+
 +\Delta\phi\!\left(\frac{\lVert x-b\rVert_2}{\Delta}\right)
 \mathbf1\{\lVert x-b\rVert_2<\Delta\}\right\}.
\]
Given \(X\), the potential outcomes are conditionally independent Bernoulli variables with these means, and \(Y=TY(1)+(1-T)Y(0)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{n}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}Delta}; \texttt{L}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}phi}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:released-identified-set]\label{def:released-identified-set}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Released-\allowbreak{}law identified set and maximal fiber diameter}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For \(0<\Delta\leq\Delta_0\), let
\[
 \mathcal Q_\Delta=\{\mathcal L_P(Z_\Delta):P\in\mathcal P_L\}.
\]
For every \(Q\in\mathcal Q_\Delta\), define the nonempty release-fiber identified set and its diameter by
\[
 I_\Delta(Q)=\{\tau_P(b):P\in\mathcal P_L\ \text{and}\ \mathcal L_P(Z_\Delta)=Q\},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{diam} I_\Delta(Q)=\sup_{a,a'\in I_\Delta(Q)}|a-a'|.
\]
The maximal population ambiguity of the rounded release is
\[
 \omega_\Delta=\sup_{Q\in\mathcal Q_\Delta}\operatorname{diam} I_\Delta(Q).
\]
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}Delta}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathcal P\_\allowbreak{}L}; \texttt{Z\_\allowbreak{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}Delta}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:sharp-release-fiber-handle]\label{def:sharp-release-fiber-handle}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Sharp release-\allowbreak{}fiber endpoint-\allowbreak{}program handle}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Reduce each release fiber to two side-specific extremal programs over latent first-bin conditional-mean functions whose released bin averages are fixed by \(Q\), whose values lie in \([0,1]\), and whose deviations from their boundary anchors satisfy the point-anchored \(L\)-Lipschitz envelope; then couple the two endpoint programs through \(\tau_P(b)=\theta_1-\theta_0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}Delta}; \texttt{I\_\allowbreak{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}Delta}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathcal P\_\allowbreak{}L}.
\end{definition}

\section{Main results}

\begin{proposition}[prop:release-fiber-diameter]\label{prop:release-fiber-diameter}
For every \(0<\Delta\leq\Delta_0\), every release law \(Q\in\mathcal Q_\Delta\) has positive probability on each first-bin side event \(\{T=t,Q_\Delta(R)=0\}\), and
\[
 2c_L\Delta\leq\omega_\Delta\leq4L\Delta.
\]
Consequently, the full-data causal functional \(\tau_P(b)\) is generally not point identified from a fixed-\(\Delta\) released law, and its worst release-fiber diameter is of order \(\Delta\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(0<\Delta\leq\Delta_0\).  By \(\mathrm{lem:boundary\text{-}anchor\text{-}uniqueness}\), the anchors and hence \(\tau_P(b)\) are well-defined for every \(P\in\mathcal P_L\).

First fix an arbitrary \(Q\in\mathcal Q_\Delta\).  By \(\mathrm{def:released\text{-}identified\text{-}set}\), there is a law \(P\in\mathcal P_L\) satisfying \(\mathcal L_P(Z_\Delta)=Q\).  For \(t\in\{0,1\}\), define
\[
 A_{t,\Delta}=\{T=t,Q_\Delta(R)=0\}.
\]
The definition \(Q_\Delta(r)=\Delta\lfloor r/\Delta\rfloor\), together with \(R\in[0,1]\) and \(\Delta>0\), gives the exact event equality
\[
 A_{t,\Delta}=\{T=t,R<\Delta\}.
\]
Because \(0<\Delta\leq\Delta_0\leq1/4<1/2\), the two-sided local-mass condition \(\mathrm{ass:uniform\text{-}disc\text{-}design}\), as incorporated in \(\mathrm{def:half\text{-}disc\text{-}class}\), yields
\[
 Q(A_{t,\Delta})
 =\mathbb P_P(T=t,R<\Delta)
 \geq c_0\Delta^2>0.
\]
Thus every attainable released law assigns positive probability to each first-bin side event, and the released-law conditional mean
\[
 \nu_t(Q)=\mathbb E_Q[Y\mid A_{t,\Delta}]
\]
is well defined.

Now take arbitrary \(P,P'\in\mathcal P_L\) in the same release fiber, so that
\(\mathcal L_P(Z_\Delta)=\mathcal L_{P'}(Z_\Delta)=Q\).  Write \((\mu_t,\theta_t)\) and \((\mu'_t,\theta'_t)\) for their respective side regressions and anchors.  Since \(Y\) and \(A_{t,\Delta}\) are measurable functions of \(Z_\Delta\), equality of released laws makes \(\nu_t(Q)\) their common conditional outcome mean.  Apply the general-event assertion of \(\mathrm{lem:conditional\text{-}local\text{-}binomial\text{-}law}\) under each law, with auxiliary sample size \(256\), which is admissible because \(256\geq256\).  This gives
\[
 \nu_t(Q)
 =\mathbb E_P[\mu_t(X)\mid A_{t,\Delta}]
 =\mathbb E_{P'}[\mu'_t(X)\mid A_{t,\Delta}].
\]
On \(A_{t,\Delta}\), one has \(R<\Delta\leq1/2\).  Positivity of the conditioning event, conditional Jensen's inequality, and the point-anchored envelope \(\mathrm{ass:radial\text{-}lipschitz}\) therefore imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |\theta_t-\nu_t(Q)|
 &\leq \mathbb E_P\!\left[|\theta_t-\mu_t(X)|\mid A_{t,\Delta}\right] \\
 &\leq L\mathbb E_P[R\mid A_{t,\Delta}]
 \leq L\Delta,
 \\[3pt]
 |\theta'_t-\nu_t(Q)|
 &\leq \mathbb E_{P'}\!\left[|\theta'_t-\mu'_t(X)|\mid A_{t,\Delta}\right] \\
 &\leq L\mathbb E_{P'}[R\mid A_{t,\Delta}]
 \leq L\Delta.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The triangle inequality consequently gives \(|\theta_t-\theta'_t|\leq2L\Delta\) for each \(t\).  Using \(\tau_P(b)=\theta_1-\theta_0\) and the corresponding equality under \(P'\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |\tau_P(b)-\tau_{P'}(b)|
 &=| (\theta_1-\theta_0)-(\theta'_1-\theta'_0)| \\
 &\leq |\theta_1-\theta'_1|+|\theta_0-\theta'_0| \\
 &\leq4L\Delta.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Because \(P\) and \(P'\) were arbitrary in the fiber, \(\operatorname{diam}I_\Delta(Q)\leq4L\Delta\).  Taking the supremum over \(Q\in\mathcal Q_\Delta\), as prescribed by \(\mathrm{def:released\text{-}identified\text{-}set}\), proves
\[
 \omega_\Delta\leq4L\Delta.
\]

For the converse inequality, \(\mathrm{lem:first\text{-}bin\text{-}alias}\) supplies laws \(P^{\mathrm{alias}}_{+,\Delta},P^{\mathrm{alias}}_{-,\Delta}\in\mathcal P_L\) having one common released law, denoted by \(Q^{\mathrm{alias}}_\Delta\), and satisfying
\[
 \tau_{P^{\mathrm{alias}}_{+,\Delta}}(b)
 -\tau_{P^{\mathrm{alias}}_{-,\Delta}}(b)
 =2c_L\Delta.
\]
Both target values therefore belong to \(I_\Delta(Q^{\mathrm{alias}}_\Delta)\), whence
\[
 \omega_\Delta
 \geq\operatorname{diam}I_\Delta(Q^{\mathrm{alias}}_\Delta)
 \geq2c_L\Delta.
\]
Since \(c_L>0\) and \(\Delta>0\), this fiber contains two distinct structural target values.  Hence \(\tau_P(b)\) is generally not point identified in the fixed-width released experiment, while
\[
 2c_L\Delta\leq\omega_\Delta\leq4L\Delta
\]
shows that its maximal release-fiber diameter is of order \(\Delta\), with constants determined by the fixed class constants \(c_L\) and \(L\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the headline partial-identification result. For each released law \(Q\), define \(\eta_t(Q)=\mathbb E_Q[Y\mid T=t,Q_\Delta(R)=0]\), \(\underline{\theta}_t(Q)=\max\{0,\eta_t(Q)-L\Delta\}\), and \(\overline{\theta}_t(Q)=\min\{1,\eta_t(Q)+L\Delta\}\). The proposition's proof certifies the directly computable outer inclusion \(I_\Delta(Q)\subseteq[\underline{\theta}_1(Q)-\overline{\theta}_0(Q),\overline{\theta}_1(Q)-\underline{\theta}_0(Q)]\) and quantifies worst population ambiguity by \(2c_L\Delta\leq\omega_\Delta\leq4L\Delta\); it does not assert sharp release-specific endpoints. \textit{Gap.} Exact-coordinate, scalar measurement-error, and manipulation-type results do not calculate this bivariate radial release-fiber diameter over the unknown-design point-anchored class. \textit{Consumer.} An analyst or data steward can compute \(\eta_t(Q)\), form \(\underline{\theta}_t(Q)=\max\{0,\eta_t(Q)-L\Delta\}\) and \(\overline{\theta}_t(Q)=\min\{1,\eta_t(Q)+L\Delta\}\), and report \([\underline{\theta}_1(Q)-\overline{\theta}_0(Q),\overline{\theta}_1(Q)-\underline{\theta}_0(Q)]\) as a certified outer interval for \(I_\Delta(Q)\), while the diameter bound reports the worst fixed-precision ambiguity before sampling error.

\begin{openendedquestion}[oeq:sharp-release-fiber]\label{oeq:sharp-release-fiber}
Characterize the exact endpoint map \(Q\mapsto(\inf I_\Delta(Q),\sup I_\Delta(Q))\) for every attainable released law \(Q\in\mathcal Q_\Delta\), and determine the exact value of the maximal diameter \(\omega_\Delta\) over \(\mathcal P_L\).
\end{openendedquestion}
For every attainable release law \(Q\), the first-bin side probabilities are positive and the observable conditional means \(\eta_t(Q)=\mathbb E_Q[Y\mid T=t,Q_\Delta(R)=0]\) are defined.  The proved, computable outer endpoint map is
\[
 I_\Delta(Q)\subseteq
 [\underline\theta_1(Q)-\overline\theta_0(Q),
   \overline\theta_1(Q)-\underline\theta_0(Q)],
\]
where
\[
 \underline\theta_t(Q)=\max\{0,\eta_t(Q)-L\Delta\},
 \qquad
 \overline\theta_t(Q)=\min\{1,\eta_t(Q)+L\Delta\}.
\]
Moreover, \(2c_L\Delta\leq\omega_\Delta\leq4L\Delta\).  This is an outer bound, not a sharp endpoint characterization.
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Exact endpoints would upgrade the computable first-bin outer interval to a sharp release-law-specific characterization of \(I_\Delta(Q)\). \textit{Gap.} The full latent-disintegration program, including the released outcome-distribution and all-radius local-mass constraints, remains unsolved. \textit{Consumer.} A future solution would let data stewards report the narrowest compatible population interval rather than the present certified outer interval.

\begin{lemma}[lem:first-bin-alias]\label{lem:first-bin-alias}
For every \(0<\Delta\leq\Delta_0\), \(\int_0^1u\phi(u)\,du=0\), both \(P^{\mathrm{alias}}_{+,\Delta}\) and \(P^{\mathrm{alias}}_{-,\Delta}\) belong to \(\mathcal P_L\), their released one-unit laws of \(Z_\Delta\) are identical, and \(\tau_{P^{\mathrm{alias}}_{+,\Delta}}(b)-\tau_{P^{\mathrm{alias}}_{-,\Delta}}(b)=2c_L\Delta\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The polynomial in \(\mathrm{def:first\text{-}bin\text{-}alias}\) is
\[
 \phi(u)=1-3u+2u^2,
 \qquad
 \int_0^1u\phi(u)\,du
 =\int_0^1(u-3u^2+2u^3)\,du
 =\frac12-1+\frac12=0.
\]
Furthermore, \(\phi(1)=0\), \(|\phi(u)|\leq1\) for \(u\in[0,1]\), and \(\phi'(u)=-3+4u\), whence \(\sup_{u\in[0,1]}|\phi'(u)|=3\).  Define
\[
 a_\Delta(r)=\Delta\phi(r/\Delta)\mathbf1\{0\leq r<\Delta\}.
\]
The two pieces agree at \(r=\Delta\), and the derivative bound shows that \(a_\Delta\) is continuous and \(3\)-Lipschitz on \([0,1]\).  The radius map is \(1\)-Lipschitz because
\[
 \bigl|\lVert x-b\rVert_2-\lVert x'-b\rVert_2\bigr|
 \leq\lVert x-x'\rVert_2.
\]

Consider either law in \(\mathrm{def:first\text{-}bin\text{-}alias}\).  Choose the control anchor \(\theta_0=1/2\) and the treated anchor
\[
 \theta_{1,\pm}=\frac12\pm c_L\Delta
 =\frac12\pm c_La_\Delta(0).
\]
For every treated point \(x\) with radius \(r\leq1/2\),
\[
 |\mu_{1,\pm}(x)-\theta_{1,\pm}|
 =c_L|a_\Delta(r)-a_\Delta(0)|
 \leq3c_Lr\leq Lr,
\]
where the last inequality uses \(c_L\leq L/8\).  The control deviation is zero.  Also
\[
 |c_La_\Delta(r)|\leq c_L\Delta
 \leq\frac1{16}\frac14=\frac1{64},
\]
using \(c_L\leq1/16\) and \(\Delta\leq\Delta_0\leq1/4\); therefore the constructed Bernoulli means lie in \([0,1]\).

The construction supplies the known half-plane partition, sharp assignment, consistency, and conditionally Bernoulli potential outcomes, which in particular satisfy the bounded-outcome conditional-mean requirement.  Uniformity of \(X\) on the unit disc and polar integration give, for each \(t\in\{0,1\}\) and \(0<H\leq1/2\),
\[
 \mathbb P(T=t,R<H)
 =\frac1\pi\int_{\Theta_t}\int_0^H r\,dr\,d\vartheta
 =\frac{H^2}{2}
 \geq c_0H^2,
 \qquad |\Theta_t|=\pi,
\]
by \(c_0\leq1/2\).  Thus both laws belong to \(\mathcal P_L\).  This is membership through an embedded uniform-half-disc subclass, not an assertion of design-wide orthogonality.

The distribution of \((T,Q_\Delta(R))\) is common to the two signs.  Within the treated first bin, the difference of their joint success masses is
\[
 \frac1\pi\int_{\Theta_1}\int_0^\Delta
 2c_L\Delta\phi(r/\Delta)r\,dr\,d\vartheta
 =2c_L\Delta^3\int_0^1u\phi(u)\,du=0.
\]
Outside this bin the perturbation vanishes, and every control cell is unchanged.  Because the released outcome is binary under these two constructed laws, equality of a released cell's total mass and success mass also gives equality of its failure mass.  Hence the entire one-unit laws of \(Z_\Delta=(Y,T,Q_\Delta(R))\) coincide.  Finally, \(\phi(0)=1\) and the declared boundary anchors give
\[
 \tau_{P^{\mathrm{alias}}_{+,\Delta}}(b)=c_L\Delta,
 \qquad
 \tau_{P^{\mathrm{alias}}_{-,\Delta}}(b)=-c_L\Delta.
\]
Their difference is \(2c_L\Delta\), as claimed.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This embedded uniform-half-disc Bernoulli construction supplies the attainable side of the \(\Theta(\Delta)\) release-fiber claim without imposing radiality or uniformity on the main class. \textit{Gap.} Scalar rounding results do not furnish the radial-Jacobian first-bin null direction for a release hiding bivariate coordinates within annuli. \textit{Consumer.} Data stewards can see why increasing sample size alone cannot remove the fixed-bin resolution floor.

\begin{theorem}[thm:minimax-frontier]\label{thm:minimax-frontier}
There exist constants \(0<c<C<\infty\), depending only on \(L\), \(c_0\), and \(\Delta_0\), such that for every \(n\geq256\) and \(0<\Delta\leq\Delta_0\), \(c\rho_{n,\Delta}\leq\mathfrak R^{(1)}_{n,\Delta}\leq C\rho_{n,\Delta}\) and \(c\rho_{n,\Delta}^2\leq\mathfrak R^{(2)}_{n,\Delta}\leq C\rho_{n,\Delta}^2\). The upper bounds are attained, up to \(C\), by \(\widehat\tau_{n,\Delta}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(h=h_n\), \(H=H_{n,\Delta}\), and \(\rho=\rho_{n,\Delta}\).  By \(\mathrm{def:bin\text{-}aware\text{-}estimator}\),
\[
 H=\Delta\left\lceil\frac{h}{\Delta}\right\rceil,
 \qquad h=n^{-1/4}.
\]
The inequalities \(x\leq\lceil x\rceil<x+1\), together with \(n\geq256\), \(0<\Delta\leq\Delta_0\), and \(\Delta_0\leq1/4\), give
\[
 h\leq H<h+\Delta\leq2\rho,
 \qquad 0<H<\frac12.
\]

We first establish the upper bounds using the single law-independent estimator in \(\mathrm{def:bin\text{-}aware\text{-}estimator}\).  Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_L\) and \(t\in\{0,1\}\).  Apply \(\mathrm{lem:conditional\text{-}local\text{-}binomial\text{-}law}\), and abbreviate its quantities by \(q_t\), \(\nu_t\), \(N_t\), and \(S_t\).  The class membership \(P\in\mathcal P_L\) and the displayed range of \(H\) imply
\[
 q_t\geq c_0H^2>0,
 \qquad |\nu_t-\theta_t|\leq LH,
 \qquad N_t\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,q_t).
\]
For every integer \(k\geq1\) having positive count probability, the same lemma gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[\frac{S_t}{k}\,\middle|\,N_t=k\right]=\nu_t,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}_P\!\left(\frac{S_t}{k}\,\middle|\,N_t=k\right)\leq\frac1{4k}.
\]
The selected outcomes are supported on \([0,1]\), so \(0\leq S_t/k\leq1\); consequently, the clipping in the estimator leaves \(S_t/k\) unchanged when \(N_t=k>0\).

We next derive the needed random-denominator inequalities.  If \(N\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,q)\) with \(q>0\), finite expansion and \((k+1)^{-1}=\int_0^1x^k\,dx\) yield
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\frac1{N+1}\right]
 =\int_0^1(1-q+qx)^n\,dx
 =\frac{1-(1-q)^{n+1}}{(n+1)q}
 \leq\frac1{(n+1)q}.
\]
Since \(k^{-1}\leq2(k+1)^{-1}\) for \(k\geq1\), and since \((\mathbb E U)^2\leq\mathbb E[U^2]\) for the nonnegative finite-valued variable \(U=\mathbf1\{N>0\}/\sqrt N\),
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\frac{\mathbf1\{N>0\}}N\right]
 \leq\frac2{(n+1)q}
 \leq\frac2{nq},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E\!\left[\frac{\mathbf1\{N>0\}}{\sqrt N}\right]
 \leq\sqrt{\frac2{nq}}.
\]
Furthermore, \(\mathbb P(N=0)=(1-q)^n\leq e^{-nq}\).  The elementary inequalities \(x^{1/2}e^{-x}\leq1\) and \(xe^{-x}\leq1\), valid for \(x>0\), therefore show
\[
 \mathbb P(N=0)\leq(nq)^{-1/2},
 \qquad
 \mathbb P(N=0)\leq(nq)^{-1}.
\]

The conditional variance inequality and \((\mathbb E|V|)^2\leq\mathbb E[V^2]\) imply, on \(N_t=k>0\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[\left|\frac{S_t}{k}-\nu_t\right|\,\middle|\,N_t=k\right]
 \leq\frac1{2\sqrt k},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[\left(\frac{S_t}{k}-\nu_t\right)^2\,\middle|\,N_t=k\right]
 \leq\frac1{4k}.
\]
On \(N_t=0\), the fixed fallback is \(1/2\), and \(0\leq\nu_t\leq1\) gives
\[
 |1/2-\nu_t|\leq\frac12,
 \qquad (1/2-\nu_t)^2\leq\frac14.
\]
Averaging over \(N_t\) and applying the random-denominator bounds with \(q=q_t\) proves
\[
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat m_{t,n,\Delta}-\nu_t|
 \leq\left(\frac1{\sqrt2}+\frac12\right)\frac1{\sqrt{nq_t}},
\]
and
\[
 \mathbb E_P[(\widehat m_{t,n,\Delta}-\nu_t)^2]
 \leq\frac3{4nq_t}.
\]
Let \(A=1/\sqrt2+1/2\).  The inequalities \(q_t\geq c_0H^2\), \(|\nu_t-\theta_t|\leq LH\), and \((a+b)^2\leq2a^2+2b^2\) now give
\[
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat m_{t,n,\Delta}-\theta_t|
 \leq LH+\frac{A}{\sqrt{c_0n}\,H},
\]
\[
 \mathbb E_P[(\widehat m_{t,n,\Delta}-\theta_t)^2]
 \leq2L^2H^2+\frac3{2c_0nH^2}.
\]
Because \(H\geq h=n^{-1/4}\),
\[
 \frac1{\sqrt n\,H}\leq h\leq\rho,
 \qquad
 \frac1{nH^2}\leq h^2\leq\rho^2.
\]
Together with \(H<2\rho\), the identity \(\tau_P(b)=\theta_1-\theta_0\), the triangle inequality, and \((x-y)^2\leq2x^2+2y^2\), these estimates imply
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_L}
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat\tau_{n,\Delta}-\tau_P(b)|
 \leq C_1\rho,
 \qquad
 C_1=4L+\frac{2A}{\sqrt{c_0}},
\]
and
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_L}
 \mathbb E_P[(\widehat\tau_{n,\Delta}-\tau_P(b))^2]
 \leq C_2\rho^2,
 \qquad
 C_2=32L^2+\frac6{c_0}.
\]
The counts and sums are measurable functions of the released sample \(Z_\Delta^n\), the bandwidth is known, and the zero-count fallback is fixed.  Hence \(\widehat\tau_{n,\Delta}\in\mathcal T_{n,\Delta}\).  Taking the two infima in \(\mathrm{def:minimax\text{-}risks}\) proves the asserted upper bounds and their attainment, up to constants, by the displayed estimator.

We now separate the population and sampling meanings of the converse.  By \(\mathrm{prop:release\text{-}fiber\text{-}diameter}\),
\[
 2c_L\Delta\leq\omega_\Delta\leq4L\Delta.
\]
Thus, for every fixed \(\Delta>0\), the structural full-data functional \(\tau_P(b)\) is generally not point identified by the released one-unit law; the \(\Delta\) term is a population release-fiber ambiguity cost.  In contrast, \(h=n^{-1/4}\) is the local sampling-error scale.  A single same-class pair carrying both scales is supplied by \(\mathrm{thm:single\text{-}joint\text{-}pair}\).

Let \(P_0=P_{0,n,\Delta}\) and \(P_1=P_{1,n,\Delta}\) be that pair, let \(Q_j=\mathcal L_{P_j}(Z_\Delta^n)\), and put \(\vartheta_j=\tau_{P_j}(b)\).  With \(\kappa=c_L/4>0\) and \(\delta=|\vartheta_1-\vartheta_0|\), the theorem gives
\[
 P_0,P_1\in\mathcal P_L,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\leq\frac14,
 \qquad
 \delta=\kappa(h+\Delta)\geq\kappa\rho.
\]
Fix any \(a\in\mathcal T_{n,\Delta}\).  Set \(\lambda=Q_0+Q_1\) and \(f_j=dQ_j/d\lambda\).  For every released sample \(z\),
\[
 |a(z)-\vartheta_0|+|a(z)-\vartheta_1|\geq\delta,
\]
and
\[
 (a(z)-\vartheta_0)^2+(a(z)-\vartheta_1)^2
 =2\left(a(z)-\frac{\vartheta_0+\vartheta_1}{2}\right)^2+\frac{\delta^2}{2}
 \geq\frac{\delta^2}{2}.
\]
Moreover, directly from the density formula for total variation,
\[
 \int\min(f_0,f_1)\,d\lambda
 =\frac12\int(f_0+f_1-|f_0-f_1|)\,d\lambda
 =1-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1).
\]
Since \(f_j\geq\min(f_0,f_1)\), multiplication of the two pointwise loss inequalities by \(\min(f_0,f_1)\), followed by integration and the fact that a maximum is at least half the corresponding sum, yields
\[
 \max_{j\in\{0,1\}}\mathbb E_{Q_j}|a-\vartheta_j|
 \geq\frac\delta2\{1-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\}
 \geq\frac{3\kappa}{8}\rho,
\]
and
\[
 \max_{j\in\{0,1\}}\mathbb E_{Q_j}(a-\vartheta_j)^2
 \geq\frac{\delta^2}{4}\{1-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\}
 \geq\frac{3\kappa^2}{16}\rho^2.
\]
The supremum over \(\mathcal P_L\) dominates the maximum over \(P_0,P_1\).  Taking the infimum over \(a\in\mathcal T_{n,\Delta}\) proves both minimax lower bounds.  Finally, choose
\[
 c=\min\left\{\frac{3\kappa}{8},\frac{3\kappa^2}{16}\right\}>0,
 \qquad
 C=1+\max\{C_1,C_2,c\}.
\]
These constants satisfy \(c<C<\infty\) and depend only on the permitted class constants.  All inequalities are uniform over \(n\geq256\) and \(0<\Delta\leq\Delta_0\), which completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:total-honest-interval]\label{thm:total-honest-interval}
For every \(n\geq256\) and \(0<\Delta\leq\Delta_0\), the interval \(C_{n,\Delta,\alpha}\) is defined for every released sample, satisfies \(\inf_{P\in\mathcal P_L}\mathbb P_P(\tau_P(b)\in C_{n,\Delta,\alpha})\geq1-\alpha\), and obeys \(\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_L}\mathbb E_P[\operatorname{length}(C_{n,\Delta,\alpha})]\leq C_{L,c_0,\alpha,\Delta_0}\rho_{n,\Delta}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(n\geq256\), \(0<\Delta\leq\Delta_0\), and \(P\in\mathcal P_L\), and abbreviate
\[
 h=h_n=n^{-1/4},\qquad H=H_{n,\Delta},\qquad B=LH.
\]
By the definitions of \(K_{n,\Delta}\) and \(H_{n,\Delta}\),
\[
 h\leq H<h+\Delta\leq\frac12,
\]
where the last inequality uses \(n\geq256\), hence \(h\leq1/4\), and \(\Delta\leq\Delta_0\leq1/4\).  For \(t\in\{0,1\}\), \(\mathrm{lem:conditional\text{-}local\text{-}binomial\text{-}law}\) gives
\[
 q_t:=\mathbb P_P(T=t,R<H)\geq c_0H^2>0,
 \qquad
 \nu_t:=\mathbb E_P[\mu_t(X)\mid T=t,R<H],
\]
\[
 |\nu_t-\theta_t|\leq LH=B,
 \qquad
 N_{t,n,\Delta}\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,q_t).
\]
The same lemma states that, conditional on \(N_{t,n,\Delta}=k\geq1\), the selected outcomes \(Y_{t,1}^{\star},\ldots,Y_{t,k}^{\star}\) are independent, supported on \([0,1]\), have common mean \(\nu_t\), and sum to \(S_{t,n,\Delta}\).  The released and latent localization events agree because \(H=K_{n,\Delta}\Delta\) and
\[
 \{Q_\Delta(R)<H\}
 =\{\Delta\lfloor R/\Delta\rfloor<K_{n,\Delta}\Delta\}
 =\{R<K_{n,\Delta}\Delta\}
 =\{R<H\}.
\]

We prove conditional coverage while respecting the cited concentration lemma's strict domain.  Fix \(k\geq1\) and set
\[
 \overline Y_t=\frac{S_{t,n,\Delta}}k,
 \qquad
 r=r_{k,\alpha}=\sqrt{\frac{\log(4/\alpha)}{2k}}>0.
\]
The event that the clipped lower endpoint exceeds \(\nu_t\) is contained in
\[
 \{\overline Y_t-\nu_t>r\}.
\]
If \(r<1-\nu_t\), then \(\mathrm{lem:hoeffding\text{-}bounded\text{-}average}\), applied conditionally with \(p=\nu_t\), implies
\[
 \mathbb P_P(\overline Y_t-\nu_t>r\mid N_{t,n,\Delta}=k)
 \leq\mathbb P_P(\overline Y_t-\nu_t\geq r\mid N_{t,n,\Delta}=k)
 \leq e^{-2kr^2}=\frac\alpha4.
\]
If \(r\geq1-\nu_t\), bounded support gives \(\overline Y_t-\nu_t\leq1-\nu_t\leq r\), so the strict event is empty and the same bound holds.

For the opposite deviation, define \(V_{t,j}^{\star}=1-Y_{t,j}^{\star}\).  Conditional on the count, these variables are independent, supported on \([0,1]\), and have common mean \(1-\nu_t\).  The event that the clipped upper endpoint is below \(\nu_t\) is contained in
\[
 \{\nu_t-\overline Y_t>r\}
 =\left\{\frac1k\sum_{j=1}^kV_{t,j}^{\star}-(1-\nu_t)>r\right\}.
\]
If \(r<\nu_t=1-(1-\nu_t)\), the cited lemma applied to the complements gives probability at most \(e^{-2kr^2}=\alpha/4\).  If \(r\geq\nu_t\), then \(\overline Y_t\geq0\) implies \(\nu_t-\overline Y_t\leq\nu_t\leq r\), so the strict event is empty.  Consequently, in all endpoint and interior cases,
\[
 \mathbb P_P\!\left(\nu_t\notin[\ell_t,u_t],\middle|,N_{t,n,\Delta}=k\right)
 \leq\frac\alpha2.
\]
When \(N_{t,n,\Delta}=0\), the side interval is \([0,1]\) and contains \(\nu_t\) surely.  Averaging over the count therefore gives
\[
 \mathbb P_P\{\nu_t\in[\ell_t,u_t]\}\geq1-\frac\alpha2.
\]
A union bound yields an event \(E\) with \(\mathbb P_P(E)\geq1-\alpha\) on which both side inclusions hold.  On \(E\),
\[
 \nu_1-\nu_0\in[\ell_1-u_0,u_1-\ell_0].
\]
The point-anchor bounds imply
\[
 |\tau_P(b)-(\nu_1-\nu_0)|
 =| (\theta_1-\nu_1)-(\theta_0-\nu_0)|
 \leq2B.
\]
Thus \(\mathrm{def:total\text{-}honest\text{-}interval}\) gives \(\tau_P(b)\in C_{n,\Delta,\alpha}\) on \(E\).  Since \(P\) was arbitrary,
\[
 \inf_{P\in\mathcal P_L}\mathbb P_P\{\tau_P(b)\in C_{n,\Delta,\alpha}\}\geq1-\alpha.
\]

The counts and sums in the endpoints are measurable functions of \(Z_\Delta^n\), and all subsequent operations are Borel measurable.  For every released sample, \(0\leq\ell_t\leq u_t\leq1\), and
\[
 (u_1-\ell_0+2B)-(\ell_1-u_0-2B)
 =(u_1-\ell_1)+(u_0-\ell_0)+4B\geq0.
\]
Therefore the construction is an ordered, closed, bounded, \(\sigma(Z_\Delta^n)\)-measurable interval, including when either local count is zero.  This proves totality.

It remains to bound expected length.  Put \(a_\alpha=\sqrt{\log(4/\alpha)/2}\).  At a positive count \(k\), clipping can only shorten the side interval, so
\[
 u_t-\ell_t\leq\frac{2a_\alpha}{\sqrt{k}},
\]
whereas the length is one at count zero.  If \(N\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,q)\) with \(q>0\), integration of its probability generating function gives
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\frac1{N+1}\right]
 =\int_0^1(1-q+qx)^n\,dx
 =\frac{1-(1-q)^{n+1}}{(n+1)q}
 \leq\frac1{(n+1)q}.
\]
Since \(k^{-1}\leq2(k+1)^{-1}\) for \(k\geq1\), and \((\mathbb E U)^2\leq\mathbb E[U^2]\) for \(U=\mathbf1\{N>0\}/\sqrt N\),
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\frac{\mathbf1\{N>0\}}{\sqrt N}\right]
 \leq\sqrt{\mathbb E\!\left[\frac{\mathbf1\{N>0\}}N\right]}
 \leq\sqrt{\frac2{nq}}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \mathbb P(N=0)=(1-q)^n\leq e^{-nq}\leq(nq)^{-1/2},
\]
where the last step uses \(x^{1/2}e^{-x}\leq1\) for \(x>0\).  Applying these estimates with \(q=q_t\) yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_P[u_t-\ell_t]
 &\leq \mathbb P_P(N_{t,n,\Delta}=0)
   +2a_\alpha\mathbb E_P\!\left[
      \frac{\mathbf1\{N_{t,n,\Delta}>0\}}{\sqrt{N_{t,n,\Delta}}}
    \right]\\
 &\leq\frac{1+2\sqrt{\log(4/\alpha)}}{\sqrt{nq_t}}
 \leq\frac{1+2\sqrt{\log(4/\alpha)}}{\sqrt{c_0nH^2}}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The interval length is
\[
 \operatorname{length}(C_{n,\Delta,\alpha})
 =(u_1-\ell_1)+(u_0-\ell_0)+4LH.
\]
Finally,
\[
 H<h+\Delta\leq2\rho_{n,\Delta},
 \qquad
 \frac1{\sqrt nH}\leq\frac1{\sqrt nh}=h\leq\rho_{n,\Delta}.
\]
Summing the two side bounds gives the uniform estimate
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_L}\mathbb E_P[\operatorname{length}(C_{n,\Delta,\alpha})]
 \leq
 \left\{
 \frac{2+4\sqrt{\log(4/\alpha)}}{\sqrt{c_0}}+8L
 \right\}\rho_{n,\Delta}.
\]
The displayed finite constant depends only on \(L\), \(c_0\), and \(\alpha\), and hence is admissible as \(C_{L,c_0,\alpha,\Delta_0}\).  This completes the proof.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This supporting inference result combines conditional fixed-count Hoeffding concentration with sidewise point-anchor bias enlargement and remains total when a local count is zero. \textit{Gap.} Ordinary-running-variable intervals do not directly handle latent annuli, unknown bivariate design, quadratic local mass, bounded non-Bernoulli outcomes, and no within-bin coordinate. \textit{Consumer.} The interval is implementable from bounded outcome, treatment side, rounded radius, observed local counts, and the declared smoothness envelope.

\begin{theorem}[thm:honest-length-lower]\label{thm:honest-length-lower}
There is a constant \(c_{L,\alpha,\Delta_0}>0\) such that, for every \(n\geq256\) and \(0<\Delta\leq\Delta_0\), every \(C\in\mathcal C_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\) satisfies \(\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_L}\mathbb E_P[\operatorname{length}(C)]\geq c_{L,\alpha,\Delta_0}\rho_{n,\Delta}\); hence \(\mathfrak W_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\asymp\rho_{n,\Delta}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(n\geq256\), \(0<\Delta\leq\Delta_0\), and an arbitrary \(C\in\mathcal C_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\).  Let \((P_0,P_1)\) be the same-class pair furnished by \(\mathrm{thm:single\text{-}joint\text{-}pair}\), and define
\[
 Q_j=\mathcal L_{P_j}(Z_\Delta^n),
 \qquad
 \vartheta_j=\tau_{P_j}(b),
 \qquad
 \delta=|\vartheta_1-\vartheta_0|,
 \quad j\in\{0,1\}.
\]
That theorem gives \(P_0,P_1\in\mathcal P_L\) and
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\leq\frac14,
 \qquad
 \delta=\kappa(h_n+\Delta),
 \qquad
 \kappa=\frac{c_L}{4}>0.
\]
By \(\mathrm{def:honest\text{-}class}\), the interval \(C\) is \(\sigma(Z_\Delta^n)\)-measurable and is honest under both laws.  Hence, for \(A_j=\{\vartheta_j\in C\}\),
\[
 Q_j(A_j)\geq1-\alpha,
 \qquad j\in\{0,1\}.
\]
For any measurable event \(A\), the density formula for total variation gives \(|Q_1(A)-Q_0(A)|\leq\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\).  Applied to \(A_1\), this yields
\[
 Q_0(A_1)\geq Q_1(A_1)-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)
 \geq1-\alpha-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1).
\]
The union bound under \(Q_0\) therefore implies
\[
 Q_0(A_0\cap A_1)
 \geq1-Q_0(A_0^c)-Q_0(A_1^c)
 \geq1-2\alpha-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)
 \geq\frac34-2\alpha>0,
\]
where the strict positivity follows from \(0<\alpha<1/4\).  On \(A_0\cap A_1\), the interval \(C\) contains both targets, and therefore \(\operatorname{length}(C)\geq\delta\).  Since interval length is nonnegative,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_{P_0}[\operatorname{length}(C)]
 &\geq\delta Q_0(A_0\cap A_1)\\
 &\geq\frac{c_L}{4}\left(\frac34-2\alpha\right)(h_n+\Delta)\\
 &\geq\frac{c_L}{4}\left(\frac34-2\alpha\right)\rho_{n,\Delta}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Because \(P_0\in\mathcal P_L\), the supremum over \(P\in\mathcal P_L\) is at least this expectation.  The inequality holds for every \(C\in\mathcal C_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\), so \(\mathrm{def:honest\text{-}width}\) gives
\[
 \mathfrak W_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)
 \geq c_{L,\alpha,\Delta_0}\rho_{n,\Delta},
 \qquad
 c_{L,\alpha,\Delta_0}:=\frac{c_L}{4}\left(\frac34-2\alpha\right)>0.
\]
This constant is independent of \(n\) and \(\Delta\); allowing its notation to include \(\Delta_0\) is harmless.

For the matching upper bound, \(\mathrm{thm:total\text{-}honest\text{-}interval}\) proves that \(C_{n,\Delta,\alpha}\) is total, uniformly honest over \(\mathcal P_L\), and has worst-case expected length at most \(C_{L,c_0,\alpha,\Delta_0}\rho_{n,\Delta}\).  Hence \(C_{n,\Delta,\alpha}\in\mathcal C_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\), and using it as a candidate in the infimum defining \(\mathfrak W_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\) yields
\[
 \mathfrak W_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)
 \leq C_{L,c_0,\alpha,\Delta_0}\rho_{n,\Delta}.
\]
The lower and upper inequalities prove \(\mathfrak W_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\asymp\rho_{n,\Delta}\) uniformly over all allowed \(n\) and \(\Delta\), as claimed.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This supporting lower bound uses the proved single sampling-plus-aliasing pair to show that every uniformly honest interval pays the same \(\max\{n^{-1/4},\Delta\}\) order. \textit{Gap.} General modulus and discrete-score results do not evaluate this radial \(n^{-1/4}\)-to-\(\Delta\) transition under the stated released field. \textit{Consumer.} The result certifies the conditional-Hoeffding interval's rate without using unavailable within-bin information.

\begin{theorem}[thm:single-joint-pair]\label{thm:single-joint-pair}
For every \(n\ge256\) and \(0<\Delta\le\Delta_0\), the handle \(\mathfrak H_{n,\Delta}\) admits one same-class baseline--alternative realization with both difficulties present.  Specifically, put \(\kappa=c_L/4\), \(b_h(r)=(h_n-r)_+\),
\[
 a_\Delta(r)=\Delta\phi(r/\Delta)\mathbf 1\{0\le r<\Delta\},
 \qquad
 g_{n,\Delta}(r)=\kappa\{b_h(r)+a_\Delta(r)\}.
\]
There are \(P_{0,n,\Delta},P_{1,n,\Delta}\in\mathcal P_L\) with \(m_{0,0}=m_{0,1}=1/2\), \(m_{1,0}=1/2\), and \(m_{1,1}=1/2+g_{n,\Delta}\), for which
\[
 \operatorname{TV}\!\left(\mathcal L_{P_{0,n,\Delta}}(Z_\Delta^n),
 \mathcal L_{P_{1,n,\Delta}}(Z_\Delta^n)\right)
 \le\frac14<1-2\alpha
\]
and
\[
 \tau_{P_{1,n,\Delta}}(b)-\tau_{P_{0,n,\Delta}}(b)
 =\kappa(h_n+\Delta).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(n\geq256\) and \(0<\Delta\leq\Delta_0\), and abbreviate \(h=h_n\) and \(\kappa=c_L/4\).  Then \(h\leq1/4\) and \(\Delta\leq1/4\).  From \(\phi(u)=1-3u+2u^2\), one has \(\phi(1)=0\) and \(\sup_{u\in[0,1]}|\phi'(u)|=3\).  Consequently
\[
 a_\Delta(r)=\Delta\phi(r/\Delta)\mathbf1\{0\leq r<\Delta\}
\]
is \(3\)-Lipschitz, satisfies \(a_\Delta(0)=\Delta\), and, by \(\mathrm{lem:first\text{-}bin\text{-}alias}\), has the exact null moment
\[
 \int_0^\Delta ra_\Delta(r)\,dr
 =\Delta^3\int_0^1u\phi(u)\,du=0.
\]
The function \(b_h(r)=(h-r)_+\) is \(1\)-Lipschitz and satisfies \(b_h(0)=h\).  Therefore
\[
 g_{n,\Delta}(r)=\kappa\{b_h(r)+a_\Delta(r)\}
\]
is \(4\kappa=c_L\leq L\)-Lipschitz, and
\[
 |g_{n,\Delta}(r)|
 \leq\kappa(h+\Delta)
 \leq\frac{c_L}{8}
 \leq\frac1{128}<\frac12.
\]

Take the laws named in \(\mathrm{def:joint\text{-}lower\text{-}handle}\), and abbreviate them by \(P_0=P_{0,n,\Delta}\) and \(P_1=P_{1,n,\Delta}\).  Under \(P_0\), choose both anchors equal to \(1/2\).  Under \(P_1\), choose \(\theta_0=1/2\) and
\[
 \theta_1=\frac12+g_{n,\Delta}(0)
 =\frac12+\kappa(h+\Delta).
\]
The range bound puts every conditional Bernoulli mean in \([0,1]\).  For every treated point of radius \(r\leq1/2\),
\[
 |\mu_1(x)-\theta_1|
 =|g_{n,\Delta}(r)-g_{n,\Delta}(0)|
 \leq c_Lr\leq Lr,
\]
and all other point-anchor deviations vanish.  The construction supplies the known sharp half-plane partition, consistency, and conditional Bernoulli laws.  Uniform polar integration gives, for each side and every \(0<H\leq1/2\),
\[
 \mathbb P(T=t,R<H)=\frac{H^2}{2}\geq c_0H^2,
\]
because \(c_0\leq1/2\).  Thus both laws belong to the bounded-outcome superclass \(\mathcal P_L\).  Their target separation is
\[
 \tau_{P_{1,n,\Delta}}(b)-\tau_{P_{0,n,\Delta}}(b)
 =g_{n,\Delta}(0)=\kappa(h+\Delta).
\]

It remains to bound the distance between the released laws.  Partition \([0,1]\) into the positive-mass quantizer cells
\[
 A_j=[j\Delta,\min\{(j+1)\Delta,1\}),
\]
with the immaterial radius-one endpoint assigned arbitrarily, and define
\[
 w_j=\int_{A_j}r\,dr,
 \qquad
 \overline b_j=w_j^{-1}\int_{A_j}rb_h(r)\,dr.
\]
For the uniform disc cut into half-discs,
\[
 w_j=\mathbb P(T=1,Q_\Delta(R)=j\Delta).
\]
The alias term is supported in the complete first cell and has zero \(r\)-weighted integral there; it vanishes in all other cells.  Hence, cell by cell, the treated conditional success probability is \(1/2\) under \(P_0\) and \(1/2+\kappa\overline b_j\) under \(P_1\), while all control cells agree.

Let \(Q_k=\mathcal L_{P_k}(Z_\Delta)\).  Direct summation over the two Bernoulli outcomes gives
\[
 \chi^2\!\left(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2+x),
                    \operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\right)=4x^2.
\]
Consequently, Jensen's inequality within each quantizer cell yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 d:=\chi^2(Q_1,Q_0)
 &=4\kappa^2\sum_jw_j\overline b_j^2\\
 &\leq4\kappa^2\int_0^1rb_h(r)^2\,dr\\
 &=4\kappa^2\int_0^h r(h-r)^2\,dr
 =4\kappa^2\left(\frac{h^4}{12}\right)
 =\frac{\kappa^2h^4}{3}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:iid\text{-}sampling}\) makes the likelihood ratio a product.  Expanding its second moment and using \(nh^4=1\) gives
\[
 1+\chi^2(Q_1^{\otimes n},Q_0^{\otimes n})
 =(1+d)^n
 \leq\exp(nd)
 \leq\exp(\kappa^2/3).
\]
Since \(\kappa=c_L/4\leq1/64\), the elementary inequality \(e^x-1\leq2x\) for \(0\leq x\leq1\) implies
\[
 \chi^2(Q_1^{\otimes n},Q_0^{\otimes n})
 \leq\frac{2\kappa^2}{3}.
\]
Writing \(M=dQ_1^{\otimes n}/dQ_0^{\otimes n}\), Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(Q_1^{\otimes n},Q_0^{\otimes n})
 =\frac12\mathbb E_{Q_0^{\otimes n}}|M-1|
 \leq\frac12\{\mathbb E_{Q_0^{\otimes n}}(M-1)^2\}^{1/2}
 \leq\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt6}<\frac14.
\]
Finally, \(0<\alpha<1/4\) implies \(1/4<1-2\alpha\).  This proves the same-class realization, separation, and asserted total-variation bound.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The proved uniform-half-disc Bernoulli pair combines a local sampling bump and exact alias direction while charging released divergence only for the sampling component. \textit{Gap.} No cited comparator supplies one same-class pair with both order-\(n^{-1/4}\) and order-\(\Delta\) displacement in this latent-annular experiment. \textit{Consumer.} The settled pair is the common lower certificate for absolute risk, squared risk, and honest expected length.

\begin{lemma}[lem:conditional-local-binomial-law]\label{lem:conditional-local-binomial-law}
Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_L\), \(n\geq256\), \(0<\Delta\leq\Delta_0\), and \(t\in\{0,1\}\).  For every event \(B\in\sigma(T,Q_\Delta(R))\) with \(B\subseteq\{T=t\}\) and \(\mathbb P_P(B)>0\), the conditional outcome is supported on \([0,1]\) and
\[
 \mathbb E_P[Y\mid B]=\mathbb E_P[\mu_t(X)\mid B].
\]
In particular, put \(A_t=\{T=t,Q_\Delta(R)<H_{n,\Delta}\}\).  Then \(A_t=\{T=t,R<H_{n,\Delta}\}\), its probability \(q_t\) is at least \(c_0H_{n,\Delta}^2>0\), and, with
\[
 \nu_t=\mathbb E_P[\mu_t(X)\mid A_t],
\]
one has \(|\nu_t-\mu_t(b)|\leq LH_{n,\Delta}\) and \(N_{t,n,\Delta}\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,q_t)\).  Conditional on \(N_{t,n,\Delta}=k\geq1\), list the selected outcomes in increasing sample-index order as \(Y_{t,1}^{\star},\ldots,Y_{t,k}^{\star}\).  They are independent and identically distributed, supported on \([0,1]\), and have common mean \(\nu_t\); moreover,
\[
 S_{t,n,\Delta}=\sum_{j=1}^kY_{t,j}^{\star},\qquad
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[\frac{S_{t,n,\Delta}}k\,\middle|\,N_{t,n,\Delta}=k\right]=\nu_t,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}_P\!\left(\frac{S_{t,n,\Delta}}k\,\middle|\,N_{t,n,\Delta}=k\right)\leq\frac1{4k}.
\]
Outside the embedded Bernoulli subclass, the conditional sum need not be binomial.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(B\in\sigma(T,Q_\Delta(R))\) satisfy \(B\subseteq\{T=t\}\) and \(\mathbb P_P(B)>0\).  By \(\mathrm{ass:sharp\text{-}boundary\text{-}assignment}\), \(T\) is a measurable function of \(X\), and the definitions of \(R\) and \(Q_\Delta\) make \(Q_\Delta(R)\) a measurable function of \(X\).  Thus \(B\in\sigma(X)\), up to a \(P\)-null set.  By \(\mathrm{ass:consistency}\), \(Y=Y(t)\) on \(B\).  The bounded-outcome conditional-law member property in \(\mathrm{def:half\text{-}disc\text{-}class}\) then gives \(0\leq Y\leq1\) on \(B\), and iterated conditioning gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P[Y\mid B]
 =\mathbb E_P[Y(t)\mid B]
 =\mathbb E_P\!\left[\mathbb E_P[Y(t)\mid X]\,\middle|\,B\right]
 =\mathbb E_P[\mu_t(X)\mid B].
\]
This proves the first assertion without imposing a Bernoulli law on a general member of \(\mathcal P_L\).

Put \(h=h_n\), \(H=H_{n,\Delta}\), and \(K=K_{n,\Delta}\).  By definition, \(H=K\Delta\) with integer \(K\geq1\).  Hence, pointwise for \(R\in[0,1]\),
\[
 Q_\Delta(R)<H
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \Delta\lfloor R/\Delta\rfloor<K\Delta
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 R<K\Delta=H.
\]
Moreover, \(n\geq256\), \(0<\Delta\leq\Delta_0\leq1/4\), and the ceiling inequality imply
\[
 0<h=n^{-1/4}\leq\frac14,
 \qquad
 h\leq H=\Delta\left\lceil\frac h\Delta\right\rceil<h+\Delta\leq\frac12.
\]
Thus the local-mass condition applies to
\[
 A_t=\{T=t,Q_\Delta(R)<H\}=\{T=t,R<H\}
\]
and yields
\[
 q_t:=\mathbb P_P(A_t)\geq c_0H^2>0.
\]
The first part, with \(B=A_t\), defines
\[
 \nu_t=\mathbb E_P[Y\mid A_t]
      =\mathbb E_P[\mu_t(X)\mid A_t].
\]
On \(A_t\), one has \(R<H\leq1/2\).  The point-anchor envelope \(\mathrm{ass:radial\text{-}lipschitz}\), the identity \(\mu_t(b)=\theta_t\), and the triangle inequality for conditional expectation give
\[
 \left|\nu_t-\mu_t(b)\right|
 =\left|\mathbb E_P[\mu_t(X)-\theta_t\mid A_t]\right|
 \leq\mathbb E_P[|\mu_t(X)-\theta_t|\mid A_t]
 \leq L\mathbb E_P[R\mid A_t]
 \leq LH.
\]
Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:boundary\text{-}anchor\text{-}uniqueness}\) ensures that the declared anchor is a well-defined functional of \(P\).

For sample unit \(i\), set \(I_i=\mathbf1\{A_{t,i}\}\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:iid\text{-}sampling}\) makes the pairs \((I_i,Y_i)\) independent and identically distributed, with \(\mathbb P_P(I_i=1)=q_t\).  Therefore
\[
 N_{t,n,\Delta}=\sum_{i=1}^n I_i\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,q_t).
\]
Fix \(k\geq1\) with \(\mathbb P_P(N_{t,n,\Delta}=k)>0\).  Conditional on any indicator configuration \((I_1,\ldots,I_n)\) having exactly \(k\) ones, independence across units implies that the outcomes at the selected indices are independent, while identical sampling makes every selected outcome have law \(\mathcal L_P(Y\mid A_t)\).  This product law is the same for every such indicator configuration.  Mixing over the configurations conditional on their sum therefore leaves that product law unchanged.  Hence \(Y_{t,1}^{\star},\ldots,Y_{t,k}^{\star}\) are conditionally independent and identically distributed, are supported on \([0,1]\), and have common mean \(\nu_t\).  By the definition of the localized outcome sum,
\[
 S_{t,n,\Delta}=\sum_{j=1}^kY_{t,j}^{\star}
 \quad\text{on }\{N_{t,n,\Delta}=k\},
\]
and consequently
\[
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[\frac{S_{t,n,\Delta}}k\,\middle|\,N_{t,n,\Delta}=k\right]=\nu_t.
\]
Finally, if \(0\leq W\leq1\) and \(\mathbb E[W]=\nu\), then \(W^2\leq W\), so
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(W)=\mathbb E[W^2]-\nu^2
 \leq\nu-\nu^2\leq\frac14.
\]
Conditional independence now gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_P\!\left(\frac{S_{t,n,\Delta}}k\,\middle|\,N_{t,n,\Delta}=k\right)
 =\frac1{k^2}\sum_{j=1}^k
 \operatorname{Var}_P(Y_{t,j}^{\star}\mid N_{t,n,\Delta}=k)
 \leq\frac1{4k}.
\]
This proves all assertions.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This selected-sample lemma supplies the bounded-outcome conditional product law, random-count distribution, variance bound, and point-anchor bias used by the supporting procedures. \textit{Gap.} Outside the Bernoulli lower subclass, the selected sum is not binomial; only the selected count is binomial and the selected outcomes are conditionally iid and bounded. \textit{Consumer.} The observed-count estimator uses the inverse-count consequences, and the total interval uses the conditional bounded-outcome product law.

\begin{lemma}[lem:boundary-anchor-uniqueness]\label{lem:boundary-anchor-uniqueness}
For every \(P\in\mathcal P_L\) and \(t\in\{0,1\}\), there is at most one \(a\in[0,1]\) such that \(|\mu_t(x)-a|\leq L\lVert x-b\rVert_2\) for \(P_X\)-almost every \(x\in\mathcal X_{t,P}\) with \(\lVert x-b\rVert_2\leq1/2\). Consequently, the declared \(\theta_t\) and \(\tau_P(b)=\theta_1-\theta_0\) are well-defined functionals of \(P\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_L\) and \(t\in\{0,1\}\).  Suppose that \(a,a'\in[0,1]\) both satisfy the point-anchor envelope in the statement.  For every \(H\in(0,1/2]\), the local-mass condition in \(\mathrm{ass:uniform\text{-}disc\text{-}design}\) gives
\[
 \mathbb P_P(T=t,R<H)\geq c_0H^2>0.
\]
Each candidate envelope holds outside a \(P_X\)-null subset of the side-\(t\), radius-at-most-\(1/2\) region.  Removing the union of these two null sets from \(\{T=t,R<H\}\) leaves a set of positive probability.  At every \(x\) in that remaining set,
\[
 |a-a'|
 \leq |a-\mu_t(x)|+|\mu_t(x)-a'|
 \leq 2L\lVert x-b\rVert_2
 <2LH.
\]
The left side is deterministic, and this inequality holds for every \(H\in(0,1/2]\).  Letting \(H\downarrow0\) yields \(|a-a'|=0\), hence \(a=a'\).  Existence of the declared anchor \(\theta_t\) is a member condition in \(\mathrm{def:half\text{-}disc\text{-}class}\), so the anchor is unique.  Consequently \(\theta_t\), and therefore \(\tau_P(b)=\theta_1-\theta_0\), is a well-defined functional of \(P\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Positive side mass at every shrinking radius makes each point anchor unique, so \(\tau_P(b)\) is a full-data functional before \(I_\Delta(Q)\) is formed. \textit{Gap.} This almost-everywhere uniqueness step is specific to the combination of the point envelope and shrinking-radius local mass. \textit{Consumer.} The identified set, minimax risks, and coverage statements all use the uniquely determined anchor contrast.

\begin{lemma}[lem:hoeffding-bounded-average]\label{lem:hoeffding-bounded-average}
Let \(k\geq1\), and let \(W_1,\ldots,W_k\) be independent random variables satisfying \(0\leq W_j\leq1\) almost surely and \(\mathbb E[W_j]=p\) for every \(j\).  Then, for every \(r\) satisfying \(0<r<1-p\),
\[
 \mathbb P\!\left(\frac1k\sum_{j=1}^kW_j-p\geq r\right)\leq\exp(-2kr^2).
\]
\end{lemma}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This classical one-sided inequality supplies finite-sample concentration for a selected average of independent bounded outcomes. \textit{Gap.} Hoeffding1963 supplies only the fixed-count concentration leaf; conditioning on a random spatial count and adding point-anchor bias are specific to this note. \textit{Consumer.} The total conditional-Hoeffding interval invokes the bound on each side and on complemented outcomes.

\begin{proposition}[prop:fine-quantization-regimes]\label{prop:fine-quantization-regimes}
Along every sequence with \(0<\Delta\leq n^{-1/4}\), the unknown-design frontier has minimax absolute-error and honest expected-length order \(n^{-1/4}\) and squared-risk order \(n^{-1/2}\).  Along every sequence with \(n^{-1/4}=o(\Delta)\), the corresponding orders are \(\Delta\) and \(\Delta^2\), respectively.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By definition,
\[
 h_n=n^{-1/4},
 \qquad
 \rho_{n,\Delta}=\max\{h_n,\Delta\}.
\]
If \(0<\Delta\leq n^{-1/4}=h_n\), then \(\rho_{n,\Delta}=h_n=n^{-1/4}\).  Substitution into \(\mathrm{thm:minimax\text{-}frontier}\) gives minimax absolute-error order \(n^{-1/4}\) and squared-risk order
\[
 (n^{-1/4})^2=n^{-1/2}.
\]
Substitution into \(\mathrm{thm:honest\text{-}length\text{-}lower}\), including its matching upper conclusion, gives honest expected-length order \(n^{-1/4}\).

Now consider any sequence satisfying \(n^{-1/4}=o(\Delta)\).  By the definition of little-oh, \(n^{-1/4}/\Delta\to0\), so \(n^{-1/4}<\Delta\) for all sufficiently large indices.  Thus \(\rho_{n,\Delta}=\Delta\) eventually.  The same two theorems then give minimax absolute-error and honest expected-length order \(\Delta\), and minimax squared-risk order \(\Delta^2\).  These are substitutions in the bounded-outcome point-anchored rounded-release experiment; no equivalence with an exact-coordinate experiment is asserted.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This supporting corollary separates the \(n^{-1/4}\) sampling regime from the \(\Delta\)-dominated population-ambiguity regime. \textit{Gap.} Exact-coordinate rate benchmarks do not imply the fixed-width latent-annular result. \textit{Consumer.} Applied users can diagnose whether sample size or released radial precision is the active limitation.

\section{Interpretation}

The causal estimand remains the full-data boundary contrast \(\tau_P(b)=\theta_1-\theta_0\), not the observable first-bin contrast. For each released law \(Q\), the observable means \(\eta_t(Q)\) and the point-anchored envelope produce a directly computable interval that contains every compatible value in \(I_\Delta(Q)\); the inclusion is certified but need not be sharp. Across released laws, \(\omega_\Delta\asymp\Delta\) measures worst population ambiguity. Finite sampling adds the \(n^{-1/4}\) boundary-regression scale, so \(\Delta\leq n^{-1/4}\) is sample-limited, while \(n^{-1/4}=o(\Delta)\) is release-precision-limited.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The computable release-law endpoint map is proved only as an outer bound, and the exact endpoints and sharp maximal-diameter constant remain open because a general released outcome law does not reveal a feasible latent within-bin disintegration.  The fiber upper bound and statistical upper bounds cover the full unknown-design, \([0,1]\)-bounded-outcome point-anchored class but require the declared quadratic local-mass constant, known side partition, fixed boundary point, and envelope constant.  Matching lower bounds use a legal uniform-half-disc Bernoulli subclass.  The results do not apply when raw coordinates, polar angle, or within-bin radius remain available, do not establish the published Cattaneo--Titiunik--Yu class, and do not assert workflow-wide validity for ordinary full-coordinate \(\texttt{rd2d}\) analyses.

\section{Honest scope}

The standing partial-identification result is the computable outer inclusion \(I_\Delta(Q)\subseteq[\underline{\theta}_1(Q)-\overline{\theta}_0(Q),\overline{\theta}_1(Q)-\underline{\theta}_0(Q)]\), with endpoints obtained by clipping \(\eta_t(Q)\pm L\Delta\) to \([0,1]\), together with \(2c_L\Delta\leq\omega_\Delta\leq4L\Delta\). The interval is not asserted to equal the identified set; exact release-law-specific endpoints and the sharp maximal-diameter constant remain open through the sharp release-fiber endpoint question. Supporting statistical results give matching minimax-risk and honest-expected-length orders uniformly over \(n\geq256\) and \(0<\Delta\leq\Delta_0\), not sharp leading constants. Applicability requires the observation field to be exactly \(\sigma(Z_{\Delta,1},\ldots,Z_{\Delta,n})\); raw coordinates, angle, and within-bin radius must be unavailable.
