By definition,
\[
 \rho_{n,\Delta}=\max\{n^{-1/4},\Delta\}.
\]
If \(0<\Delta\leq n^{-1/4}\), then \(\rho_{n,\Delta}=n^{-1/4}\).  Substitution into \(\mathrm{thm:minimax\text{-}frontier}\) gives
\[
 \mathfrak R^{(1)}_{n,\Delta}\asymp n^{-1/4},
 \qquad
 \mathfrak R^{(2)}_{n,\Delta}\asymp n^{-1/2},
\]
and substitution into \(\mathrm{thm:honest\text{-}length\text{-}lower}\), including its matching upper bound, gives
\[
 \mathfrak W_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\asymp n^{-1/4}.
\]
The theorem constants are uniform in all permitted \(n\) and \(\Delta\), so these orders are uniform in the stated fine-quantization regime.

Along any sequence satisfying \(n^{-1/4}=o(\Delta)\), one eventually has \(n^{-1/4}<\Delta\), and hence \(\rho_{n,\Delta}=\Delta\).  The same substitutions yield
\[
 \mathfrak R^{(1)}_{n,\Delta}\asymp\Delta,
 \qquad
 \mathfrak R^{(2)}_{n,\Delta}\asymp\Delta^2,
 \qquad
 \mathfrak W_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\asymp\Delta.
\]
Thus the first regime is sampling dominated and the second is coarsening dominated, without invoking an exact-coordinate experiment.
