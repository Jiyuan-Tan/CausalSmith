Let \(C\in\mathcal C_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\), and use the pair \((P_0,P_1)\) from \(\mathrm{thm:single\text{-}joint\text{-}pair}\).  Write \(\vartheta_j=\tau_{P_j}(b)\),
\[
 d=|\vartheta_1-\vartheta_0|=\kappa(h_n+\Delta),
 \qquad
 v=\operatorname{TV}(\mathcal L_{P_0}(Z_\Delta^n),\mathcal L_{P_1}(Z_\Delta^n))\le\frac14.
\]
Honesty as defined in \(\mathrm{def:honest\text{-}class}\) implies
\[
 \mathbb P_{P_0}(\vartheta_0\in C)\ge1-\alpha,
 \qquad
 \mathbb P_{P_1}(\vartheta_1\in C)\ge1-\alpha.
\]
The total-variation inequality transfers the second event to \(P_0\):
\[
 \mathbb P_{P_0}(\vartheta_1\in C)
 \ge\mathbb P_{P_1}(\vartheta_1\in C)-v
 \ge1-\alpha-v.
\]
Therefore, under \(P_0\),
\[
 \mathbb P_{P_0}(\{\vartheta_0,\vartheta_1\}\subseteq C)
 \ge1-2\alpha-v
 \ge\frac34-2\alpha>0.
\]
Because \(C\) is an interval, its length is at least \(d\) on this event.  It follows that
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_L}\mathbb E_P[\operatorname{length}(C)]
 \ge\mathbb E_{P_0}[\operatorname{length}(C)]
 \ge d\left(\frac34-2\alpha\right)
 \ge\kappa\left(\frac34-2\alpha\right)\rho_{n,\Delta}.
\]
Taking the infimum over honest intervals proves the claimed lower bound.  Conversely, \(\mathrm{thm:total\text{-}honest\text{-}interval}\) puts \(C_{n,\Delta,\alpha}\) in the honest class and bounds its worst expected length by a constant multiple of \(\rho_{n,\Delta}\).  Using \(\mathrm{def:honest\text{-}width}\) gives
\[
 c_{L,\alpha,\Delta_0}\rho_{n,\Delta}
 \le\mathfrak W_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)
 \le C_{L,c_0,\alpha,\Delta_0}\rho_{n,\Delta},
\]
which is the asserted order statement.
