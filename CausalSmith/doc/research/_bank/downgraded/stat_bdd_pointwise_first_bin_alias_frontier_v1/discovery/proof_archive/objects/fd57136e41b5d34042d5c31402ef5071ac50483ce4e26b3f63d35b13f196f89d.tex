Fix \(n\geq256\), \(0<\Delta\leq\Delta_0\), and an arbitrary \(C\in\mathcal C_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\).  Let \((P_0,P_1)\) be the Bernoulli pair furnished by \(\mathrm{thm:single\text{-}joint\text{-}pair}\), and define
\[
 Q_j=\mathcal L_{P_j}(Z_\Delta^n),
 \qquad
 \vartheta_j=\tau_{P_j}(b),
 \qquad
 \delta=|\vartheta_1-\vartheta_0|,
 \quad j\in\{0,1\}.
\]
The theorem gives \(P_0,P_1\in\mathcal P_L\); its pair is the explicit Bernoulli uniform-half-disc subclass embedded in the bounded-outcome superclass.  With \(\kappa=c_L/4\), it also gives
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\leq\frac14,
 \qquad
 \delta=\kappa(h_n+\Delta).
\]
By the definition of \(\mathcal C_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\), the interval \(C\) is \(\sigma(Z_\Delta^n)\)-measurable and is honest at both laws.  Thus, for \(A_j=\{\vartheta_j\in C\}\),
\[
 Q_j(A_j)\geq1-\alpha,
 \qquad j\in\{0,1\}.
\]
The variational characterization of total variation, applied to the measurable event \(A_1\), yields
\[
 Q_0(A_1)\geq Q_1(A_1)-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)
 \geq1-\alpha-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1).
\]
Therefore the union bound under \(Q_0\) gives the sharper retained factor
\[
 Q_0(A_0\cap A_1)
 \geq1-Q_0(A_0^c)-Q_0(A_1^c)
 \geq1-2\alpha-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)
 \geq\frac34-2\alpha>0,
\]
where positivity uses \(0<\alpha<1/4\).  On \(A_0\cap A_1\), the closed interval \(C\) contains both targets, so \(\operatorname{length}(C)\geq\delta\).  Nonnegativity of interval length now implies
\[
 \mathbb E_{P_0}[\operatorname{length}(C)]
 \geq\delta Q_0(A_0\cap A_1)
 \geq\frac{c_L}{4}\left(\frac34-2\alpha\right)(h_n+\Delta)
 \geq\frac{c_L}{4}\left(\frac34-2\alpha\right)\rho_{n,\Delta}.
\]
Since \(P_0\in\mathcal P_L\), the supremum over \(P\in\mathcal P_L\) is at least this expectation.  The inequality holds for every \(C\in\mathcal C_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\), so \(\mathrm{def:honest\text{-}width}\) gives
\[
 \mathfrak W_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)
 \geq c_{L,\alpha,\Delta_0}\rho_{n,\Delta},
 \qquad
 c_{L,\alpha,\Delta_0}:=\frac{c_L}{4}\left(\frac34-2\alpha\right)>0.
\]
The constant is independent of \(n\) and \(\Delta\); allowing it to be indexed by \(\Delta_0\) is harmless.

For the matching upper bound, \(\mathrm{thm:total\text{-}honest\text{-}interval}\) proves that the released-sample interval \(C_{n,\Delta,\alpha}\) is total, uniformly honest over \(\mathcal P_L\), and has worst-case expected length at most \(C_{L,c_0,\alpha,\Delta_0}\rho_{n,\Delta}\).  Hence \(C_{n,\Delta,\alpha}\in\mathcal C_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\), and using it as a candidate in the infimum defining \(\mathfrak W_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\) yields
\[
 \mathfrak W_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)
 \leq C_{L,c_0,\alpha,\Delta_0}\rho_{n,\Delta}.
\]
The lower and upper inequalities prove \(\mathfrak W_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\asymp\rho_{n,\Delta}\) uniformly over all allowed \(n\) and \(\Delta\).
