Write \(h=h_n\), \(H=H_{n,\Delta}\), and \(\rho=\rho_{n,\Delta}\).  The ceiling definition gives
\[
 h\le H<h+\Delta\le2\rho,
 \qquad
 \frac1{\sqrt n\,H}\le\frac1{\sqrt n\,h}=h\le\rho,
 \qquad
 \frac1{nH^2}\le h^2\le\rho^2.
\]

We first prove the estimator upper bounds from the conditional selected-sample law.  Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_L\) and a side \(t\), and abbreviate \(N=N_{t,n,\Delta}\), \(q=q_t\), \(\nu=\nu_t\), and \(\theta=\theta_t\).  By \(\mathrm{lem:conditional\text{-}local\text{-}binomial\text{-}law}\),
\[
 q\ge c_0H^2,
 \qquad |\nu-\theta|\le LH,
 \qquad N\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,q).
\]
For a binomial variable with \(q>0\), direct summation gives
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\frac1{N+1}\right]
 =\frac{1-(1-q)^{n+1}}{(n+1)q}
 \le\frac1{(n+1)q}.
\]
Since \(1/k\le2/(k+1)\) for \(k\ge1\), and since \((1-q)^n\le e^{-nq}\le\min\{1,(nq)^{-1}\}\),
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\frac{\mathbf1\{N>0\}}N\right]\le\frac2{nq},
 \quad
 \mathbb E\!\left[\frac{\mathbf1\{N>0\}}{\sqrt N}\right]\le\sqrt{\frac2{nq}},
 \quad
 \mathbb P(N=0)\le\min\{1,(nq)^{-1}\}.
\]
Here the middle inequality is Cauchy--Schwarz.

When \(N=k>0\), the sample mean already lies in \([0,1]\), and its conditional variance is at most \(1/(4k)\).  The fixed zero-count fallback differs from \(\theta\in[0,1]\) by at most \(1/2\).  Conditional Jensen and the preceding display yield, for a numerical constant \(A\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat m_{t,n,\Delta}-\theta_t|
 \le LH+\frac12\mathbb E\!\left[\frac{\mathbf1\{N>0\}}{\sqrt N}\right]
       +\frac12\mathbb P(N=0)
 \le LH+\frac{A}{\sqrt{nq}}
 \le LH+\frac{A}{\sqrt{c_0}\sqrt n\,H},
\]
and, using the conditional mean \(\nu\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P[(\widehat m_{t,n,\Delta}-\theta_t)^2]
 \le L^2H^2+\frac14\mathbb E\!\left[\frac{\mathbf1\{N>0\}}N\right]
       +\frac14\mathbb P(N=0)
 \le L^2H^2+\frac{3}{4nq}
 \le L^2H^2+\frac{3}{4c_0nH^2}.
\]
The triangle inequality and \((x-y)^2\le2x^2+2y^2\), applied to the two side errors, therefore give constants \(C_1,C_2<\infty\), depending only on \(L\) and \(c_0\), such that
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_L}\mathbb E_P|\widehat\tau_{n,\Delta}-\tau_P(b)|
 \le C_1\rho,
 \qquad
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_L}\mathbb E_P[(\widehat\tau_{n,\Delta}-\tau_P(b))^2]
 \le C_2\rho^2.
\]
The estimator is measurable with respect to \(Z_\Delta^n\) by \(\mathrm{def:bin\text{-}aware\text{-}estimator}\), so it belongs to \(\mathcal T_{n,\Delta}\).  Taking the infima in \(\mathrm{def:minimax\text{-}risks}\) proves both upper bounds.

For the lower bounds, use the two-point testing argument and the single pair from \(\mathrm{thm:single\text{-}joint\text{-}pair}\).  Let its target gap be
\[
 d=\kappa(h+\Delta)\ge\kappa\rho
\]
and its released-sample total variation be \(v\le1/4\).  For any released-data estimator \(\widetilde\tau\), classify it according to which of the two target values is closer.  On every classification error its absolute error is at least \(d/2\), and its squared error is at least \(d^2/4\).  The sum of the two classification-error probabilities is at least \(1-v\), by the defining variational inequality for total variation.  Hence
\[
 \max_{j\in\{0,1\}}\mathbb E_{P_j}|\widetilde\tau-\tau_{P_j}(b)|
 \ge\frac d4(1-v)\ge\frac{3d}{16},
\]
and
\[
 \max_{j\in\{0,1\}}\mathbb E_{P_j}[(\widetilde\tau-\tau_{P_j}(b))^2]
 \ge\frac{d^2}{8}(1-v)\ge\frac{3d^2}{32}.
\]
Both laws lie in \(\mathcal P_L\); taking the supremum over that class and then the infimum over \(\mathcal T_{n,\Delta}\) gives lower bounds by fixed positive multiples of \(\rho\) and \(\rho^2\).  Choosing the smaller lower constant and the larger upper constant proves the theorem.  The target throughout is the full-data functional \(\tau_P(b)\); measurability only restricts the decision rule to the released experiment.
