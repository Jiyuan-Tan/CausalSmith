Let \(B\in\sigma(T,Q_\Delta(R))\) satisfy \(B\subseteq\{T=t\}\) and \(\mathbb P_P(B)>0\).  By \(\mathrm{ass:sharp\text{-}boundary\text{-}assignment}\), \(T\) is a measurable function of \(X\), and the definitions of \(R\) and \(Q_\Delta\) make \(Q_\Delta(R)\) a measurable function of \(X\).  Thus \(B\in\sigma(X)\), up to a \(P\)-null set.  By \(\mathrm{ass:consistency}\), \(Y=Y(t)\) on \(B\).  The bounded-outcome conditional-mean condition \(\mathrm{ass:radial\text{-}bernoulli}\) then gives \(0\leq Y\leq1\) on \(B\), and iterated conditioning gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P[Y\mid B]
 =\mathbb E_P[Y(t)\mid B]
 =\mathbb E_P\!\left[\mathbb E_P[Y(t)\mid X]\,\middle|\,B\right]
 =\mathbb E_P[\mu_t(X)\mid B].
\]
This proves the first assertion without imposing a Bernoulli law on a general member of \(\mathcal P_L\).

Put \(h=h_n\), \(H=H_{n,\Delta}\), and \(K=K_{n,\Delta}\).  By definition, \(H=K\Delta\) with integer \(K\geq1\).  Hence, pointwise for \(R\in[0,1]\),
\[
 Q_\Delta(R)<H
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \Delta\lfloor R/\Delta\rfloor<K\Delta
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 R<K\Delta=H.
\]
Moreover, \(n\geq256\), \(0<\Delta\leq\Delta_0\leq1/4\), and the ceiling inequality imply
\[
 0<h=n^{-1/4}\leq\frac14,
 \qquad
 h\leq H=\Delta\left\lceil\frac h\Delta\right\rceil<h+\Delta\leq\frac12.
\]
Thus the local-mass condition applies to
\[
 A_t=\{T=t,Q_\Delta(R)<H\}=\{T=t,R<H\}
\]
and yields
\[
 q_t:=\mathbb P_P(A_t)\geq c_0H^2>0.
\]
The first part, with \(B=A_t\), defines
\[
 \nu_t=\mathbb E_P[Y\mid A_t]
      =\mathbb E_P[\mu_t(X)\mid A_t].
\]
On \(A_t\), one has \(R<H\leq1/2\).  The point-anchor envelope \(\mathrm{ass:radial\text{-}lipschitz}\), the identity \(\mu_t(b)=\theta_t\), and the triangle inequality for conditional expectation give
\[
 \left|\nu_t-\mu_t(b)\right|
 =\left|\mathbb E_P[\mu_t(X)-\theta_t\mid A_t]\right|
 \leq\mathbb E_P[|\mu_t(X)-\theta_t|\mid A_t]
 \leq L\mathbb E_P[R\mid A_t]
 \leq LH.
\]
Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:boundary\text{-}anchor\text{-}uniqueness}\) ensures that the declared anchor is a well-defined functional of \(P\).

For sample unit \(i\), set \(I_i=\mathbf1\{A_{t,i}\}\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:iid\text{-}sampling}\) makes the pairs \((I_i,Y_i)\) independent and identically distributed, with \(\mathbb P_P(I_i=1)=q_t\).  Therefore
\[
 N_{t,n,\Delta}=\sum_{i=1}^n I_i\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,q_t).
\]
Fix \(k\geq1\) with \(\mathbb P_P(N_{t,n,\Delta}=k)>0\).  Conditional on any indicator configuration \((I_1,\ldots,I_n)\) having exactly \(k\) ones, independence across units implies that the outcomes at the selected indices are independent, while identical sampling makes every selected outcome have law \(\mathcal L_P(Y\mid A_t)\).  This product law is the same for every such indicator configuration.  Mixing over the configurations conditional on their sum therefore leaves that product law unchanged.  Hence \(Y_{t,1}^{\star},\ldots,Y_{t,k}^{\star}\) are conditionally independent and identically distributed, are supported on \([0,1]\), and have common mean \(\nu_t\).  By the definition of the localized outcome sum,
\[
 S_{t,n,\Delta}=\sum_{j=1}^kY_{t,j}^{\star}
 \quad\text{on }\{N_{t,n,\Delta}=k\},
\]
and consequently
\[
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[\frac{S_{t,n,\Delta}}k\,\middle|\,N_{t,n,\Delta}=k\right]=\nu_t.
\]
Finally, if \(0\leq W\leq1\) and \(\mathbb E[W]=\nu\), then \(W^2\leq W\), so
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(W)=\mathbb E[W^2]-\nu^2
 \leq\nu-\nu^2\leq\frac14.
\]
Conditional independence now gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_P\!\left(\frac{S_{t,n,\Delta}}k\,\middle|\,N_{t,n,\Delta}=k\right)
 =\frac1{k^2}\sum_{j=1}^k
 \operatorname{Var}_P(Y_{t,j}^{\star}\mid N_{t,n,\Delta}=k)
 \leq\frac1{4k}.
\]
This proves all assertions.
