Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_L\) and \(t\in\{0,1\}\).  Suppose that \(a,a'\in[0,1]\) both satisfy the point-anchor envelope in the statement.  For every \(H\in(0,1/2]\), the local-mass condition in \(\mathrm{ass:uniform\text{-}disc\text{-}design}\) gives
\[
 \mathbb P_P(T=t,R<H)\geq c_0H^2>0.
\]
Each candidate envelope holds outside a \(P_X\)-null subset of the side-\(t\), radius-at-most-\(1/2\) region.  Removing the union of these two null sets from \(\{T=t,R<H\}\) leaves a set of positive probability.  At every \(x\) in that remaining set,
\[
 |a-a'|
 \leq |a-\mu_t(x)|+|\mu_t(x)-a'|
 \leq 2L\lVert x-b\rVert_2
 <2LH.
\]
The left side is deterministic, and this inequality holds for every \(H\in(0,1/2]\).  Letting \(H\downarrow0\) yields \(|a-a'|=0\), hence \(a=a'\).  Existence of the declared anchor \(\theta_t\) is a member condition in \(\mathrm{def:half\text{-}disc\text{-}class}\), so the anchor is unique.  Consequently \(\theta_t\), and therefore \(\tau_P(b)=\theta_1-\theta_0\), is a well-defined functional of \(P\).
