Fix \(C\in\mathcal C_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\), and take the embedded uniform-half-disc pair in \(\mathrm{thm:single\text{-}joint\text{-}pair}\).  Write \(Q_0,Q_1\) for its released product laws, \(\theta_0,\theta_1\) for its targets, and \(\delta=|\theta_1-\theta_0|\).  That theorem gives
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\leq\frac14,
 \qquad
 \delta=\kappa(h_n+\Delta)\geq\kappa\rho_{n,\Delta},
 \qquad \kappa=\frac{c_L}{4}>0.
\]
Both laws are in the unknown-design superclass, so honesty gives
\[
 Q_0\{\theta_0\in C\}\geq1-\alpha,
 \qquad
 Q_1\{\theta_1\in C\}\geq1-\alpha.
\]
By the variational definition of total variation,
\[
 Q_0\{\theta_1\in C\}
 \geq Q_1\{\theta_1\in C\}-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)
 \geq1-\alpha-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1).
\]
The union bound under \(Q_0\) yields
\[
 Q_0\{\theta_0\in C,\theta_1\in C\}
 \geq1-2\alpha-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)
 \geq\frac34-2\alpha>0,
\]
where positivity uses \(\alpha<1/4\).  A real interval containing both targets has length at least \(\delta\).  Hence
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_L}\mathbb E_P[\operatorname{length}(C)]
 \geq\mathbb E_{Q_0}[\operatorname{length}(C)]
 \geq\delta\left(\frac34-2\alpha\right)
 \geq\kappa\left(\frac34-2\alpha\right)\rho_{n,\Delta}.
\]
The first inequality is precisely the subclass-to-superclass transfer: the supremum over \(\mathcal P_L\) dominates its value at the uniform-half-disc member \(P_{0,n,\Delta}\).  Taking the infimum over all honest closed bounded interval-valued procedures proves the lower bound for \(\mathfrak W_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\).  The interval from \(\mathrm{thm:total\text{-}honest\text{-}interval}\) belongs to this class and supplies the matching upper bound, so \(\mathfrak W_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\asymp\rho_{n,\Delta}\).
