Fix \(0<\Delta\leq\Delta_0\).  Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:boundary\text{-}anchor\text{-}uniqueness}\) ensures that \(\tau_P(b)\) is a well-defined functional of every \(P\in\mathcal P_L\).  First consider any \(Q\in\mathcal Q_\Delta\).  By \(\mathrm{def:released\text{-}identified\text{-}set}\), there exists at least one \(P\in\mathcal P_L\) with \(\mathcal L_P(Z_\Delta)=Q\).  For \(t\in\{0,1\}\), define the released event
\[
 A_{t,\Delta}=\{T=t,Q_\Delta(R)=0\}.
\]
Because \(R\in[0,1]\) and \(Q_\Delta(r)=\Delta\lfloor r/\Delta\rfloor\),
\[
 A_{t,\Delta}=\{T=t,R<\Delta\}.
\]
The class membership of \(P\), the two-sided local-mass condition \(\mathrm{ass:uniform\text{-}disc\text{-}design}\), and \(0<\Delta\leq\Delta_0\leq1/4<1/2\) imply
\[
 Q(A_{t,\Delta})=\mathbb P_P(A_{t,\Delta})\geq c_0\Delta^2>0.
\]
Thus the following released-law functional is well defined:
\[
 \nu_t(Q)=\mathbb E_Q[Y\mid A_{t,\Delta}].
\]

Let \(P'\in\mathcal P_L\) be any other member of the same release fiber, so \(\mathcal L_{P'}(Z_\Delta)=Q\).  Equality of the released laws makes both the probability of \(A_{t,\Delta}\) and the conditional mean \(\nu_t(Q)\) common to \(P\) and \(P'\).  Instantiate the general-event part of \(\mathrm{lem:conditional\text{-}local\text{-}binomial\text{-}law}\) at the admissible auxiliary sample size \(n=256\); that part is a one-unit conditional-mean identity and does not otherwise involve \(n\).  Applied under each law, it gives
\[
 \nu_t(Q)
 =\mathbb E_P[\mu_t(X)\mid A_{t,\Delta}]
 =\mathbb E_{P'}[\mu'_t(X)\mid A_{t,\Delta}],
\]
where \(\mu'_t\) and \(\theta'_t\) denote the regression and anchor under \(P'\).  On \(A_{t,\Delta}\), one has \(R<\Delta\leq1/2\).  Hence the point-anchor envelope \(\mathrm{ass:radial\text{-}lipschitz}\), conditional Jensen's inequality, and positivity of the conditioning event imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |\theta_t-\nu_t(Q)|
 &\leq\mathbb E_P[|\theta_t-\mu_t(X)|\mid A_{t,\Delta}]
 \leq L\mathbb E_P[R\mid A_{t,\Delta}]
 \leq L\Delta,\\
 |\theta'_t-\nu_t(Q)|&\leq L\Delta.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The triangle inequality therefore gives \(|\theta_t-\theta'_t|\leq2L\Delta\) for each side.  Consequently,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |\tau_P(b)-\tau_{P'}(b)|
 &=| (\theta_1-\theta_0)-(\theta'_1-\theta'_0)|\\
 &\leq|\theta_1-\theta'_1|+|\theta_0-\theta'_0|
 \leq4L\Delta.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Taking the supremum first over \(P,P'\) in a fixed fiber and then over \(Q\in\mathcal Q_\Delta\) proves \(\omega_\Delta\leq4L\Delta\).

For the reverse inequality, invoke \(\mathrm{lem:first\text{-}bin\text{-}alias}\).  It provides two laws \(P^{\mathrm{alias}}_{+,\Delta},P^{\mathrm{alias}}_{-,\Delta}\in\mathcal P_L\) with the same released one-unit law, say \(Q_\Delta^{\mathrm{alias}}\), and target separation
\[
 \left|\tau_{P^{\mathrm{alias}}_{+,\Delta}}(b)
       -\tau_{P^{\mathrm{alias}}_{-,\Delta}}(b)\right|
 =2c_L\Delta.
\]
Both target values belong to \(I_\Delta(Q_\Delta^{\mathrm{alias}})\) by definition.  Therefore
\[
 \omega_\Delta
 \geq\operatorname{diam}I_\Delta(Q_\Delta^{\mathrm{alias}})
 \geq2c_L\Delta.
\]
Since \(c_L>0\) and \(\Delta>0\), this common released law has two distinct compatible values of \(\tau_P(b)\).  Thus the structural full-data causal functional is generally not point identified at fixed \(\Delta\), while the two displayed bounds quantify its worst-fiber ambiguity.
