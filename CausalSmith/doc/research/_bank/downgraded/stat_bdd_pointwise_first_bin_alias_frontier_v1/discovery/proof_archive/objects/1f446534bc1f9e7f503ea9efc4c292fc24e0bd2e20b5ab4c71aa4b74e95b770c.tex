Fix \(n\geq256\), \(0<\Delta\leq\Delta_0\), and \(P\in\mathcal P_L\), and abbreviate
\[
 h=h_n=n^{-1/4},\qquad H=H_{n,\Delta},\qquad B=LH.
\]
By the definitions of \(K_{n,\Delta}\) and \(H_{n,\Delta}\),
\[
 h\leq H<h+\Delta\leq\frac12,
\]
where the last inequality uses \(n\geq256\), hence \(h\leq1/4\), and \(\Delta\leq\Delta_0\leq1/4\).  For \(t\in\{0,1\}\), \(\mathrm{lem:conditional\text{-}local\text{-}binomial\text{-}law}\) gives
\[
 q_t:=\mathbb P_P(T=t,R<H)\geq c_0H^2>0,
 \qquad
 \nu_t:=\mathbb E_P[\mu_t(X)\mid T=t,R<H],
\]
\[
 |\nu_t-\theta_t|\leq LH=B,
 \qquad
 N_{t,n,\Delta}\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,q_t).
\]
It also states that, conditional on \(N_{t,n,\Delta}=k\geq1\), the selected outcomes \(Y_{t,1}^{\star},\ldots,Y_{t,k}^{\star}\) are independent, supported on \([0,1]\), have common mean \(\nu_t\), and sum to \(S_{t,n,\Delta}\).  The released and latent localization events agree because \(H=K_{n,\Delta}\Delta\) and
\[
 \{Q_\Delta(R)<H\}
 =\{\Delta\lfloor R/\Delta\rfloor<K_{n,\Delta}\Delta\}
 =\{R<K_{n,\Delta}\Delta\}
 =\{R<H\}.
\]

We first prove conditional coverage without extending the cited concentration theorem beyond its attested domain.  Fix \(k\geq1\) and put
\[
 \overline Y_t=\frac{S_{t,n,\Delta}}k,
 \qquad
 r=r_{k,\alpha}=\sqrt{\frac{\log(4/\alpha)}{2k}}>0.
\]
The strict upper-deviation event that can put the lower endpoint above \(\nu_t\) is contained in
\[
 \{\overline Y_t-\nu_t>r\}.
\]
If \(r<1-\nu_t\), \(\mathrm{lem:hoeffding\text{-}bounded\text{-}average}\), applied with \(p=\nu_t\), gives
\[
 \mathbb P_P(\overline Y_t-\nu_t>r\mid N_{t,n,\Delta}=k)
 \leq \exp(-2kr^2)=\frac\alpha4.
\]
If \(r\geq1-\nu_t\), then \(\overline Y_t\leq1\) implies
\[
 \overline Y_t-\nu_t\leq1-\nu_t\leq r,
\]
so this strict event is empty and the same probability bound holds trivially.

For the opposite strict deviation, write \(V_{t,j}^{\star}=1-Y_{t,j}^{\star}\).  Conditional on the count, these variables are independent, supported on \([0,1]\), and have common mean \(1-\nu_t\).  The event that can put the upper endpoint below \(\nu_t\) is contained in
\[
 \{\nu_t-\overline Y_t>r\}
 =\left\{\frac1k\sum_{j=1}^kV_{t,j}^{\star}-(1-\nu_t)>r\right\}.
\]
If \(r<\nu_t=1-(1-\nu_t)\), the same cited lemma applied to the complements gives probability at most \(e^{-2kr^2}=\alpha/4\).  If \(r\geq\nu_t\), then \(\overline Y_t\geq0\) gives \(\nu_t-\overline Y_t\leq\nu_t\leq r\), so the strict event is empty.  Thus, in every case,
\[
 \mathbb P_P\!\left(|\overline Y_t-\nu_t|>r_{k,\alpha}\,\middle|\,N_{t,n,\Delta}=k\right)
 \leq\frac\alpha2.
\]
Because \(\nu_t\in[0,1]\), intersecting \([\overline Y_t-r,\overline Y_t+r]\) with \([0,1]\) cannot remove \(\nu_t\) on the complementary event.  When \(N_{t,n,\Delta}=0\), the side interval is \([0,1]\) and contains \(\nu_t\) surely.  Averaging the conditional bounds over the count therefore yields
\[
 \mathbb P_P\{\nu_t\in[\ell_t,u_t]\}\geq1-\frac\alpha2.
\]
A union bound produces an event \(E\) with \(\mathbb P_P(E)\geq1-\alpha\) on which both side inclusions hold.  On \(E\),
\[
 \nu_1-\nu_0\in[\ell_1-u_0,u_1-\ell_0].
\]
The two point-anchor bounds then imply
\[
 |\tau_P(b)-(\nu_1-\nu_0)|
 =| (\theta_1-\nu_1)-(\theta_0-\nu_0)|
 \leq2B.
\]
The construction in \(\mathrm{def:total\text{-}honest\text{-}interval}\) consequently gives \(\tau_P(b)\in C_{n,\Delta,\alpha}\) on \(E\).  Since \(P\) was arbitrary,
\[
 \inf_{P\in\mathcal P_L}\mathbb P_P\{\tau_P(b)\in C_{n,\Delta,\alpha}\}\geq1-\alpha.
\]

The counts and sums in the endpoints are measurable functions of \(Z_\Delta^n\), and every subsequent operation is Borel measurable.  For every released sample, \(0\leq\ell_t\leq u_t\leq1\), and
\[
 (u_1-\ell_0+2B)-(\ell_1-u_0-2B)
 =(u_1-\ell_1)+(u_0-\ell_0)+4B\geq0.
\]
Thus the construction is an ordered, closed, bounded, \(\sigma(Z_\Delta^n)\)-measurable interval even when either local count is zero.  This proves totality.

It remains to bound expected length.  Put \(a_\alpha=\sqrt{\log(4/\alpha)/2}\).  At a positive count \(k\), clipping can only shorten the side interval, so
\[
 u_t-\ell_t\leq\frac{2a_\alpha}{\sqrt{k}},
\]
whereas its length is one at count zero.  If \(N\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,q)\) with \(q>0\), then integration of its probability generating function gives
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\frac1{N+1}\right]
 =\int_0^1(1-q+qx)^n\,dx
 =\frac{1-(1-q)^{n+1}}{(n+1)q}
 \leq\frac1{(n+1)q}.
\]
Since \(k^{-1}\leq2(k+1)^{-1}\) for integers \(k\geq1\), Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\frac{\mathbf1\{N>0\}}{\sqrt N}\right]
 \leq\sqrt{\mathbb E\!\left[\frac{\mathbf1\{N>0\}}N\right]}
 \leq\sqrt{\frac2{nq}}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \mathbb P(N=0)=(1-q)^n\leq e^{-nq}\leq(nq)^{-1/2},
\]
where the last inequality follows from \(x^{1/2}e^{-x}\leq1\) for \(x>0\).  Applying these bounds with \(q=q_t\) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_P[u_t-\ell_t]
 &\leq \mathbb P_P(N_{t,n,\Delta}=0)
   +2a_\alpha\mathbb E_P\!\left[
      \frac{\mathbf1\{N_{t,n,\Delta}>0\}}{\sqrt{N_{t,n,\Delta}}}
    \right]\\
 &\leq\frac{1+2\sqrt{\log(4/\alpha)}}{\sqrt{nq_t}}
 \leq\frac{1+2\sqrt{\log(4/\alpha)}}{\sqrt{c_0nH^2}}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The interval length is exactly
\[
 \operatorname{length}(C_{n,\Delta,\alpha})
 =(u_1-\ell_1)+(u_0-\ell_0)+4LH.
\]
Finally, the aligned bandwidth satisfies
\[
 H<h+\Delta\leq2\rho_{n,\Delta},
 \qquad
 \frac1{\sqrt nH}\leq\frac1{\sqrt nh}=h\leq\rho_{n,\Delta}.
\]
Summing the two side bounds therefore yields the explicit uniform estimate
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_L}\mathbb E_P[\operatorname{length}(C_{n,\Delta,\alpha})]
 \leq
 \left\{
 \frac{2+4\sqrt{\log(4/\alpha)}}{\sqrt{c_0}}+8L
 \right\}\rho_{n,\Delta}.
\]
This is the asserted expected-length bound, with a finite constant depending only on \(L,c_0,\alpha\), and hence allowed to be denoted \(C_{L,c_0,\alpha,\Delta_0}\).
