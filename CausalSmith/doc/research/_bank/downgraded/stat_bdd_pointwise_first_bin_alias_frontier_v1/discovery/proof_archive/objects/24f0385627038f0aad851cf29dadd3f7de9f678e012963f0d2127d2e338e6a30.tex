By definition,
\[
 \rho_{n,\Delta}=\max\{n^{-1/4},\Delta\}.
\]
If \(0<\Delta\leq n^{-1/4}\), then \(\rho_{n,\Delta}=n^{-1/4}\).  Substitution into \(\mathrm{thm:minimax\text{-}frontier}\) gives
\[
 \mathfrak R^{(1)}_{n,\Delta}\asymp n^{-1/4},
 \qquad
 \mathfrak R^{(2)}_{n,\Delta}\asymp(n^{-1/4})^2=n^{-1/2}.
\]
Substitution into \(\mathrm{thm:honest\text{-}length\text{-}lower}\), including the matching upper bound proved there, gives
\[
 \mathfrak W_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\asymp n^{-1/4}.
\]
The constants in both cited theorems are uniform over all permitted \(n\) and \(\Delta\), so these orders are uniform throughout the stated fine-quantization regime.

Now consider a sequence satisfying \(n^{-1/4}=o(\Delta)\).  By the definition of little-oh, there is an index after which \(n^{-1/4}<\Delta\), and hence after that index
\[
 \rho_{n,\Delta}=\Delta.
\]
The same two theorem substitutions then yield
\[
 \mathfrak R^{(1)}_{n,\Delta}\asymp\Delta,
 \qquad
 \mathfrak R^{(2)}_{n,\Delta}\asymp\Delta^2,
 \qquad
 \mathfrak W_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\asymp\Delta.
\]
This proves the two asserted regimes without replacing the released annular experiment by an exact-coordinate experiment.
