By the declared definition of the frontier,
\[
 \rho_{n,\Delta}=\max\{n^{-1/4},\Delta\}.
\]
If \(0<\Delta\leq n^{-1/4}\), this identity gives \(\rho_{n,\Delta}=n^{-1/4}\) pointwise.  Substitution into Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:minimax\text{-}frontier}\) gives
\[
 \mathfrak R^{(1)}_{n,\Delta}\asymp n^{-1/4},
 \qquad
 \mathfrak R^{(2)}_{n,\Delta}\asymp(n^{-1/4})^2=n^{-1/2},
\]
and substitution into Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:honest\text{-}length\text{-}lower}\), including its matching upper bound, gives
\[
 \mathfrak W_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\asymp n^{-1/4}.
\]
The constants are uniform because the constants in the two invoked theorems are uniform over all permitted \(n\) and \(\Delta\).

Along a sequence for which \(n^{-1/4}=o(\Delta)\), the ratio \(n^{-1/4}/\Delta\) tends to zero, so eventually \(n^{-1/4}<\Delta\) and therefore \(\rho_{n,\Delta}=\Delta\).  The same two theorem substitutions yield
\[
 \mathfrak R^{(1)}_{n,\Delta}\asymp\Delta,
 \qquad
 \mathfrak R^{(2)}_{n,\Delta}\asymp\Delta^2,
 \qquad
 \mathfrak W_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\asymp\Delta.
\]
Thus the first regime is sampling dominated and the second is coarsening dominated; no exact-coordinate reduction is used.
