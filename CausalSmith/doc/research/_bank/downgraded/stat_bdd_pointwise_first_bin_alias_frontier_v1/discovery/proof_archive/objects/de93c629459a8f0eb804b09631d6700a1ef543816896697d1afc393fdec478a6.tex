Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_L\), and abbreviate \(H=H_{n,\Delta}\).  For each side, \(\mathrm{lem:conditional\text{-}local\text{-}binomial\text{-}law}\) gives a positive local probability \(q_t\geq c_0H^2\), a local success mean \(\nu_t\), and
\[
 S_{t,n,\Delta}\mid N_{t,n,\Delta}=k\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(k,\nu_t),
 \qquad |\nu_t-\mu_t(b)|\leq LH.
\]
If \(k>0\), \(\mathrm{lem:clopper\text{-}pearson\text{-}conditional\text{-}coverage}\) gives conditional coverage at least \(1-\alpha/2\) for \(\nu_t\); if \(k=0\), the stipulated interval \([0,1]\) covers \(\nu_t\) surely.  Averaging over \(N_{t,n,\Delta}\) and applying the union bound shows that both \(\nu_0\) and \(\nu_1\) lie in their respective intervals with probability at least \(1-\alpha\).  On that event,
\[
 \nu_1-\nu_0\in[\ell_1-u_0,u_1-\ell_0]
\]
and
\[
 |\tau_P(b)-(\nu_1-\nu_0)|
 \leq|\mu_1(b)-\nu_1|+|\mu_0(b)-\nu_0|
 \leq2LH.
\]
Thus \(\tau_P(b)\in C_{n,\Delta,\alpha}\) with probability at least \(1-\alpha\).  Every endpoint is a measurable function of the released counts, and the zero-count branches avoid division and inversion altogether.  The interval is therefore a closed bounded interval-valued map for every sample.

It remains to bound expected length without an asymptotic approximation.  Put \(\beta=\alpha/4\).  For a realized \(k\geq1\), \(s\in\{0,\ldots,k\}\), and \(\widehat p=s/k\), write \(I^{\mathrm{CP}}_{k,\alpha/2}(s)=[\ell,u]\).  At a nontrivial upper endpoint, put \(d=u-\widehat p>0\) and let \(W\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(k,u)\).  On \(\{W\leq s\}\), one has \(|W-ku|\geq kd\), so the pointwise inequality
\[
 \mathbf1\{W\leq s\}\leq\frac{(W-ku)^2}{k^2d^2}
\]
and \(\mathbb E_u[(W-ku)^2]=ku(1-u)\), together with the endpoint equation in \(\mathrm{lem:clopper\text{-}pearson\text{-}conditional\text{-}coverage}\), give
\[
 \beta=\mathbb P_u(W\leq s)
 \leq\frac{ku(1-u)}{k^2d^2}
 \leq\frac{\widehat p+d}{kd^2}.
\]
Hence \(d^2\leq\widehat p/(k\beta)+d/(k\beta)\), which implies
\[
 u-\widehat p\leq\sqrt{\frac{\widehat p}{k\beta}}+\frac1{k\beta}.
\]
At a nontrivial lower endpoint, let \(d_-=\widehat p-\ell>0\) and \(W\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(k,\ell)\).  The same indicator argument on \(\{W\geq s\}\) gives
\[
 \beta\leq\frac{k\ell(1-\ell)}{k^2d_-^2}
 \leq\frac{\widehat p}{kd_-^2},
\]
and hence
\[
 \widehat p-\ell\leq\sqrt{\frac{\widehat p}{k\beta}}.
\]
The boundary conventions satisfy the same combined inequality, so for every \(s\),
\[
 u-\ell\leq2\sqrt{\frac{\widehat p}{k\beta}}+\frac1{k\beta}.
\]
Conditional on \(N_t=k\), Jensen's inequality and \(\mathbb E(S_t/k\mid N_t=k)=\nu_t\leq1\) yield
\[
 \mathbb E[u_t-\ell_t\mid N_t=k]
 \leq\frac2{\sqrt{k\beta}}+\frac1{k\beta}.
\]
For \(N_t\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,q_t)\), direct integration of the binomial probability generating function gives
\[
 \mathbb E\frac1{N_t+1}
 =\int_0^1(1-q_t+q_tx)^n\,dx
 =\frac{1-(1-q_t)^{n+1}}{(n+1)q_t}
 \leq\frac1{(n+1)q_t}.
\]
Since \(1/k\leq2/(k+1)\) for \(k\geq1\), Cauchy--Schwarz gives the second of the following bounds from the first, and \((1-q_t)^n\leq e^{-nq_t}\leq(nq_t)^{-1}\) gives the third:
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\frac{\mathbf1\{N_t>0\}}{N_t}\right]\leq\frac2{nq_t},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E\!\left[\frac{\mathbf1\{N_t>0\}}{\sqrt{N_t}}\right]\leq\sqrt{\frac2{nq_t}},
 \qquad
 \mathbb P(N_t=0)\leq\frac1{nq_t}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \mathbb E_P(u_t-\ell_t)
 \leq\frac{2\sqrt2}{\sqrt{\beta nq_t}}
 +\left(1+\frac2\beta\right)\frac1{nq_t}
 \leq\frac{2\sqrt2}{\sqrt{\beta c_0nH^2}}
 +\left(1+\frac2\beta\right)\frac1{c_0nH^2}.
\]
The constructed interval has length
\[
 (u_1-\ell_1)+(u_0-\ell_0)+4LH.
\]
Finally, \(h_n\leq H<h_n+\Delta\leq2\rho_{n,\Delta}\), \((\sqrt nH)^{-1}\leq h_n\), and \((nH^2)^{-1}\leq h_n^2\leq\rho_{n,\Delta}\).  Summing the two side bounds therefore gives
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_L}\mathbb E_P[\operatorname{length}(C_{n,\Delta,\alpha})]
 \leq C_{L,c_0,\alpha,\Delta_0}\rho_{n,\Delta}.
\]
This proves total honesty and the uniform expected-length bound in every \(\Delta\)-regime.
