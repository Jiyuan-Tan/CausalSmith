Let \(B\in\sigma(T,Q_\Delta(R))\) satisfy \(B\subseteq\{T=t\}\) and \(\mathbb P_P(B)>0\).  Consistency gives \(Y=Y(t)\) on \(B\).  The conditional Bernoulli member condition and iterated conditioning therefore imply
\[
 \mathbb P_P(Y=1\mid B)
 =\mathbb E_P\!\left[\mathbb P_P(Y(t)=1\mid X)\mid B\right]
 =\mathbb E_P[\mu_t(X)\mid B].
\]
As \(Y\in\{0,1\}\), its conditional law is determined by this success probability, proving the first assertion.

Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:boundary\text{-}anchor\text{-}uniqueness}\) ensures that the declared \(\theta_t=\mu_t(b)\) is a well-defined functional of the law rather than a choice of regression version.

Put \(h=h_n\), \(H=H_{n,\Delta}\), and \(K=K_{n,\Delta}\).  Since \(H=K\Delta\), the floor quantizer satisfies, pointwise on \([0,1]\),
\[
 Q_\Delta(R)<H
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \lfloor R/\Delta\rfloor<K
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad R<K\Delta=H.
\]
Moreover, \(n\geq256\) and \(\Delta\leq\Delta_0\leq1/4\) give
\[
 0<h=n^{-1/4}\leq\frac14,
 \qquad h\leq H<h+\Delta\leq\frac12.
\]
Thus the two-sided local-mass member condition applies and yields
\[
 q_t:=\mathbb P_P(A_t)
 =\mathbb P_P(T=t,R<H)
 \geq c_0H^2>0.
\]
The first part of the proof with \(B=A_t\) shows that \(Y\mid A_t\) is Bernoulli with parameter
\[
 \nu_t=\mathbb E_P[\mu_t(X)\mid A_t].
\]
The point-anchored envelope applies \(P_X\)-almost surely throughout \(A_t\).  Using \(\mu_t(b)=\theta_t\), conditional Jensen's inequality for absolute value, and \(R<H\) on \(A_t\), one obtains
\[
 |\nu_t-\mu_t(b)|
 =|\mathbb E_P[\mu_t(X)-\theta_t\mid A_t]|
 \leq\mathbb E_P[|\mu_t(X)-\theta_t|\mid A_t]
 \leq L\mathbb E_P[R\mid A_t]
 \leq LH.
\]

By independent identical sampling, the pairs
\[
 \left(\mathbf1\{A_{t,i}\},\mathbf1\{A_{t,i}\}Y_i\right),
 \qquad i=1,\ldots,n,
\]
are iid.  Hence \(N_{t,n,\Delta}\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,q_t)\).  Their one-unit joint probability generating function, with \(w\) marking local membership and \(z\) marking a success, is
\[
 1-q_t+q_t(1-\nu_t)w+q_t\nu_twz.
\]
After taking the \(n\)-th power, the coefficient of \(w^k\) is proportional to \((1-\nu_t+\nu_tz)^k\).  Conditional on every count value \(k\) of positive probability, this is the probability generating function of \(\operatorname{Binomial}(k,\nu_t)\), proving the final assertion, including the degenerate case \(k=0\).
