By definition,
\[
 \rho_{n,\Delta}=\max\{n^{-1/4},\Delta\}.
\]
If \(0<\Delta\leq n^{-1/4}\), the maximum equals \(n^{-1/4}\).  Substitution into \(\mathrm{thm:minimax\text{-}frontier}\) and \(\mathrm{thm:honest\text{-}length\text{-}lower}\) gives absolute-error risk and honest expected length of order \(n^{-1/4}\), and squared-error risk of order \(n^{-1/2}\).  If \(n^{-1/4}=o(\Delta)\), then eventually \(\Delta>n^{-1/4}\), so the maximum equals \(\Delta\); the same substitution gives orders \(\Delta\) and \(\Delta^2\).  No exact-coordinate theorem is invoked in either deduction.
