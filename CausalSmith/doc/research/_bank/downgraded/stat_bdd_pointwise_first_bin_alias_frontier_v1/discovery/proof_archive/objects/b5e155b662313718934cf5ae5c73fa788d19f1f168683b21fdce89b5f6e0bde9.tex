Fix \(n\geq256\), \(0<\Delta\leq\Delta_0\), and \(P\in\mathcal P_L\), and abbreviate
\[
 h=h_n=n^{-1/4},\qquad H=H_{n,\Delta},\qquad B=LH.
\]
Because \(H=\Delta\lceil h/\Delta\rceil\),
\[
 h\leq H<h+\Delta\leq\frac12.
\]
Here the last inequality uses \(n\geq256\), hence \(h\leq1/4\), and \(\Delta\leq\Delta_0\leq1/4\).  Thus the radius \(H\) lies in the range on which the local-mass and Lipschitz assumptions apply.

For \(t\in\{0,1\}\), Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:conditional\text{-}local\text{-}binomial\text{-}law}\), using independent sampling, the two-sided local-mass condition, consistency, the Bernoulli regression condition, and sidewise Lipschitz continuity, gives
\[
 q_t:=\mathbb P_P\{T=t,R<H\}\geq c_0H^2>0,
 \qquad
 \nu_t:=\mathbb E_P[\mu_t(X)\mid T=t,R<H],
\]
\[
 |\nu_t-\mu_t(b)|\leq LH=B,
 \qquad
 N_{t,n,\Delta}\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,q_t),
\]
and, at every \(k\) having positive count probability,
\[
 S_{t,n,\Delta}\mid\{N_{t,n,\Delta}=k\}
 \sim\operatorname{Binomial}(k,\nu_t).
\]
The equality of the rounded and unrounded localization events used here follows from bin alignment:
\[
 \{Q_\Delta(R)<H\}
 =\{\Delta\lfloor R/\Delta\rfloor<K_{n,\Delta}\Delta\}
 =\{R<K_{n,\Delta}\Delta\}
 =\{R<H\}.
\]

If \(N_{t,n,\Delta}=0\), the side interval in \(\mathrm{def:total\text{-}honest\text{-}interval}\) is \([0,1]\), which contains \(\nu_t\).  If \(N_{t,n,\Delta}=k>0\), Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:clopper\text{-}pearson\text{-}conditional\text{-}coverage}\) and the preceding conditional binomial law give conditional coverage at least \(1-\alpha/2\).  Averaging over the count therefore yields
\[
 \mathbb P_P\{\nu_t\in[\ell_t,u_t]\}\geq1-\alpha/2.
\]
No independence between the two side intervals is required: the union bound gives an event \(E\) with \(\mathbb P_P(E)\geq1-\alpha\) on which both inclusions hold.  On \(E\),
\[
 \nu_1-\nu_0\in[\ell_1-u_0,u_1-\ell_0]
\]
and, by the two displayed Lipschitz bounds,
\[
 \left|\tau_P(b)-(\nu_1-\nu_0)\right|
 \leq|\mu_1(b)-\nu_1|+|\mu_0(b)-\nu_0|
 \leq2B.
\]
Consequently \(\tau_P(b)\in C_{n,\Delta,\alpha}\) on \(E\), proving
\[
 \inf_{P\in\mathcal P_L}
 \mathbb P_P\{\tau_P(b)\in C_{n,\Delta,\alpha}\}\geq1-\alpha.
\]
The counts and successes are measurable functions of \(Z_\Delta^n\).  The Clopper--Pearson endpoints are functions on the finite count-success state space, and the zero-count branch is explicit.  Moreover \(0\leq\ell_t\leq u_t\leq1\) and \(B<\infty\).  Hence the two displayed endpoints define an ordered, closed, bounded, \(\sigma(Z_\Delta^n)\)-measurable interval on every sample.

It remains to prove its expected-length order.  Put \(\beta=\alpha/4>0\).  Fix \(k\geq1\), \(s\in\{0,\ldots,k\}\), \(\widehat p=s/k\), and write
\[
 I^{\mathrm{CP}}_{k,\alpha/2}(s)=[\ell,u].
\]
When the upper endpoint is nontrivial, let \(d=u-\widehat p>0\) and let \(W\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(k,u)\).  On \(\{W\leq s\}\), \(|W-ku|\geq kd\); therefore the pointwise inequality
\[
 \mathbf1\{W\leq s\}\leq\frac{(W-ku)^2}{k^2d^2}
\]
and the binomial variance identity imply, using the upper endpoint equation from the cited Clopper--Pearson lemma,
\[
 \beta=\mathbb P_u(W\leq s)
 \leq\frac{u(1-u)}{kd^2}
 \leq\frac{\widehat p+d}{kd^2}.
\]
Thus \(d^2\leq \widehat p/(k\beta)+d/(k\beta)\).  For nonnegative \(A,D,d\), the implication \(d^2\leq A+Dd\Rightarrow d\leq\sqrt A+D\) follows because, when \(d>D\), \((d-D)^2\leq d(d-D)\leq A\).  Applying it with \(A=\widehat p/(k\beta)\) and \(D=1/(k\beta)\) gives
\[
 u-\widehat p\leq\sqrt{\frac{\widehat p}{k\beta}}+\frac1{k\beta}.
\]
For a nontrivial lower endpoint, put \(d_-=\widehat p-\ell>0\) and take \(W\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(k,\ell)\).  On \(\{W\geq s\}\), \(|W-k\ell|\geq kd_-\).  The lower endpoint equation, the same pointwise second-moment argument, and \(\ell(1-\ell)\leq\ell\leq\widehat p\) give
\[
 \beta\leq\frac{\ell(1-\ell)}{kd_-^2}
 \leq\frac{\widehat p}{kd_-^2},
 \qquad
 \widehat p-\ell\leq\sqrt{\frac{\widehat p}{k\beta}}.
\]
At \(s=0\), the convention \(\ell=0\) and the upper bound with \(\widehat p=0\) apply; at \(s=k\), the convention \(u=1=\widehat p\) and the lower bound apply.  Hence, for every \(s\),
\[
 u-\ell\leq2\sqrt{\frac{\widehat p}{k\beta}}+\frac1{k\beta}.
\]
Conditional on \(N_{t,n,\Delta}=k>0\), concavity of the square root and the conditional binomial mean identity yield
\[
 \mathbb E_P[u_t-\ell_t\mid N_{t,n,\Delta}=k]
 \leq\frac{2}{\sqrt{k\beta}}
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[\sqrt{S_{t,n,\Delta}/k}\mid N_{t,n,\Delta}=k\right]
 +\frac1{k\beta}
 \leq\frac2{\sqrt{k\beta}}+\frac1{k\beta}.
\]

For \(N\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,q)\) with \(q>0\), direct integration of its probability generating function gives
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\frac1{N+1}\right]
 =\int_0^1(1-q+qx)^n\,dx
 =\frac{1-(1-q)^{n+1}}{(n+1)q}
 \leq\frac1{(n+1)q}.
\]
Since \(k^{-1}\leq2(k+1)^{-1}\) for \(k\geq1\), and since the elementary Cauchy--Schwarz inequality applies to the finite binomial sum,
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\frac{\mathbf1\{N>0\}}N\right]\leq\frac2{nq},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E\!\left[\frac{\mathbf1\{N>0\}}{\sqrt N}\right]
 \leq\sqrt{\mathbb E\!\left[\frac{\mathbf1\{N>0\}}N\right]}
 \leq\sqrt{\frac2{nq}}.
\]
Also
\[
 \mathbb P(N=0)=(1-q)^n\leq e^{-nq}\leq\frac1{nq},
\]
where the final inequality follows from \(e^x\geq x\) for \(x>0\).  Recalling that the zero-count side interval has length one, the last three displays imply
\[
 \mathbb E_P[u_t-\ell_t]
 \leq\frac{2\sqrt2}{\sqrt{\beta nq_t}}
 +\left(1+\frac2\beta\right)\frac1{nq_t}
 \leq\frac{2\sqrt2}{\sqrt{\beta c_0nH^2}}
 +\left(1+\frac2\beta\right)\frac1{c_0nH^2}.
\]

The constructed interval has the exact length
\[
 \operatorname{length}(C_{n,\Delta,\alpha})
 =(u_1-\ell_1)+(u_0-\ell_0)+4LH.
\]
By the definition \(\rho_{n,\Delta}=\max\{h,\Delta\}\),
\[
 H<h+\Delta\leq2\rho_{n,\Delta},
 \qquad
 \frac1{\sqrt n H}\leq\frac1{\sqrt n h}=h\leq\rho_{n,\Delta},
 \qquad
 \frac1{nH^2}\leq\frac1{nh^2}=h^2\leq\rho_{n,\Delta}.
\]
Summing the two side bounds therefore proves, for example with \(\beta=\alpha/4\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_L}\mathbb E_P[\operatorname{length}(C_{n,\Delta,\alpha})]
 \leq
 \left\{
 \frac{4\sqrt2}{\sqrt{\beta c_0}}
 +\frac{2(1+2/\beta)}{c_0}+8L
 \right\}\rho_{n,\Delta}.
\]
This is the asserted bound with a finite constant \(C_{L,c_0,\alpha,\Delta_0}\); the displayed admissible constant happens not to increase with \(\Delta_0\).  All bounds are finite-sample and uniform over the full class and every permitted quantization width.
