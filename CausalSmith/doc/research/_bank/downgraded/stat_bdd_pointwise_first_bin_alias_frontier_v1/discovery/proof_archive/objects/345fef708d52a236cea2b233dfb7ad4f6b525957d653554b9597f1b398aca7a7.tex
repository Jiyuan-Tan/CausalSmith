Fix \(0<\Delta\leq\Delta_0\).  By \(\mathrm{lem:boundary\text{-}anchor\text{-}uniqueness}\), the anchors and hence \(\tau_P(b)\) are well-defined for every \(P\in\mathcal P_L\).

First fix an arbitrary \(Q\in\mathcal Q_\Delta\).  By \(\mathrm{def:released\text{-}identified\text{-}set}\), there is a law \(P\in\mathcal P_L\) satisfying \(\mathcal L_P(Z_\Delta)=Q\).  For \(t\in\{0,1\}\), define
\[
 A_{t,\Delta}=\{T=t,Q_\Delta(R)=0\}.
\]
The definition \(Q_\Delta(r)=\Delta\lfloor r/\Delta\rfloor\), together with \(R\in[0,1]\) and \(\Delta>0\), gives the exact event equality
\[
 A_{t,\Delta}=\{T=t,R<\Delta\}.
\]
Because \(0<\Delta\leq\Delta_0\leq1/4<1/2\), the two-sided local-mass condition \(\mathrm{ass:uniform\text{-}disc\text{-}design}\), as incorporated in \(\mathrm{def:half\text{-}disc\text{-}class}\), yields
\[
 Q(A_{t,\Delta})
 =\mathbb P_P(T=t,R<\Delta)
 \geq c_0\Delta^2>0.
\]
Thus every attainable released law assigns positive probability to each first-bin side event, and the released-law conditional mean
\[
 \nu_t(Q)=\mathbb E_Q[Y\mid A_{t,\Delta}]
\]
is well defined.

Now take arbitrary \(P,P'\in\mathcal P_L\) in the same release fiber, so that
\(\mathcal L_P(Z_\Delta)=\mathcal L_{P'}(Z_\Delta)=Q\).  Write \((\mu_t,\theta_t)\) and \((\mu'_t,\theta'_t)\) for their respective side regressions and anchors.  Since \(Y\) and \(A_{t,\Delta}\) are measurable functions of \(Z_\Delta\), equality of released laws makes \(\nu_t(Q)\) their common conditional outcome mean.  Apply the general-event assertion of \(\mathrm{lem:conditional\text{-}local\text{-}binomial\text{-}law}\) under each law, with auxiliary sample size \(256\), which is admissible because \(256\geq256\).  This gives
\[
 \nu_t(Q)
 =\mathbb E_P[\mu_t(X)\mid A_{t,\Delta}]
 =\mathbb E_{P'}[\mu'_t(X)\mid A_{t,\Delta}].
\]
On \(A_{t,\Delta}\), one has \(R<\Delta\leq1/2\).  Positivity of the conditioning event, conditional Jensen's inequality, and the point-anchored envelope \(\mathrm{ass:radial\text{-}lipschitz}\) therefore imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |\theta_t-\nu_t(Q)|
 &\leq \mathbb E_P\!\left[|\theta_t-\mu_t(X)|\mid A_{t,\Delta}\right] \\
 &\leq L\mathbb E_P[R\mid A_{t,\Delta}]
 \leq L\Delta,
 \\[3pt]
 |\theta'_t-\nu_t(Q)|
 &\leq \mathbb E_{P'}\!\left[|\theta'_t-\mu'_t(X)|\mid A_{t,\Delta}\right] \\
 &\leq L\mathbb E_{P'}[R\mid A_{t,\Delta}]
 \leq L\Delta.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The triangle inequality consequently gives \(|\theta_t-\theta'_t|\leq2L\Delta\) for each \(t\).  Using \(\tau_P(b)=\theta_1-\theta_0\) and the corresponding equality under \(P'\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |\tau_P(b)-\tau_{P'}(b)|
 &=| (\theta_1-\theta_0)-(\theta'_1-\theta'_0)| \\
 &\leq |\theta_1-\theta'_1|+|\theta_0-\theta'_0| \\
 &\leq4L\Delta.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Because \(P\) and \(P'\) were arbitrary in the fiber, \(\operatorname{diam}I_\Delta(Q)\leq4L\Delta\).  Taking the supremum over \(Q\in\mathcal Q_\Delta\), as prescribed by \(\mathrm{def:released\text{-}identified\text{-}set}\), proves
\[
 \omega_\Delta\leq4L\Delta.
\]

For the converse inequality, \(\mathrm{lem:first\text{-}bin\text{-}alias}\) supplies laws \(P^{\mathrm{alias}}_{+,\Delta},P^{\mathrm{alias}}_{-,\Delta}\in\mathcal P_L\) having one common released law, denoted by \(Q^{\mathrm{alias}}_\Delta\), and satisfying
\[
 \tau_{P^{\mathrm{alias}}_{+,\Delta}}(b)
 -\tau_{P^{\mathrm{alias}}_{-,\Delta}}(b)
 =2c_L\Delta.
\]
Both target values therefore belong to \(I_\Delta(Q^{\mathrm{alias}}_\Delta)\), whence
\[
 \omega_\Delta
 \geq\operatorname{diam}I_\Delta(Q^{\mathrm{alias}}_\Delta)
 \geq2c_L\Delta.
\]
Since \(c_L>0\) and \(\Delta>0\), this fiber contains two distinct structural target values.  Hence \(\tau_P(b)\) is generally not point identified in the fixed-width released experiment, while
\[
 2c_L\Delta\leq\omega_\Delta\leq4L\Delta
\]
shows that its maximal release-fiber diameter is of order \(\Delta\), with constants determined by the fixed class constants \(c_L\) and \(L\).
