Put \(h=h_n\), \(H=H_{n,\Delta}\), and \(\rho=\rho_{n,\Delta}\).  Quantizer alignment and the restrictions on \(n\) and \(\Delta\) give
\[
 h\leq H<h+\Delta\leq2\rho,
 \qquad 0<H\leq\frac12.
\]
Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_L\) and \(t\in\{0,1\}\).  Use the notation \(A_t,q_t,\nu_t,N_t,S_t\) from \(\mathrm{lem:conditional\text{-}local\text{-}binomial\text{-}law}\).  That lemma gives
\[
 q_t\geq c_0H^2,
 \qquad |\nu_t-\mu_t(b)|\leq LH,
 \qquad N_t\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,q_t),
 \qquad S_t\mid N_t=k\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(k,\nu_t).
\]
For a binomial \(N\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,q)\), direct integration of its probability generating function gives
\[
 \mathbb E\frac1{N+1}
 =\int_0^1(1-q+qx)^n\,dx
 =\frac{1-(1-q)^{n+1}}{(n+1)q}
 \leq\frac1{(n+1)q}.
\]
Since \(1/k\leq2/(k+1)\) for \(k\geq1\),
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\frac{\mathbf1\{N>0\}}N\right]\leq\frac2{nq},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E\!\left[\frac{\mathbf1\{N>0\}}{\sqrt N}\right]\leq\sqrt{\frac2{nq}}.
\]
Also \(\mathbb P(N=0)=(1-q)^n\leq e^{-nq}\), and the elementary maxima of \(x^{1/2}e^{-x}\) and \(xe^{-x}\) imply
\[
 \mathbb P(N=0)\leq(nq)^{-1/2},
 \qquad
 \mathbb P(N=0)\leq(nq)^{-1}.
\]

Conditional on \(N_t=k>0\), the clipped ratio in \(\mathrm{def:bin\text{-}aware\text{-}estimator}\) equals \(S_t/k\), has conditional mean \(\nu_t\), and has conditional variance at most \(1/(4k)\).  On \(N_t=0\), its fixed value \(1/2\) differs from \(\nu_t\in[1/4,3/4]\) by at most \(1/4\).  Conditional Cauchy--Schwarz and the preceding inverse-count bounds therefore give the explicit inequalities
\[
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat m_{t,n,\Delta}-\nu_t|
 \leq\frac12\sqrt{\frac2{nq_t}}+\frac1{4\sqrt{nq_t}}
 =\left(\frac1{\sqrt2}+\frac14\right)\frac1{\sqrt{nq_t}},
\]
and
\[
 \mathbb E_P[(\widehat m_{t,n,\Delta}-\nu_t)^2]
 \leq\frac14\frac2{nq_t}+\frac1{16}\frac1{nq_t}
 =\frac9{16nq_t}.
\]
Combining these inequalities with the explicit bias bound gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat m_{t,n,\Delta}-\mu_t(b)|
 \leq LH+\left(\frac1{\sqrt2}+\frac14\right)\frac1{\sqrt{c_0nH^2}},
\]
and, by \((a+b)^2\leq2a^2+2b^2\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P[(\widehat m_{t,n,\Delta}-\mu_t(b))^2]
 \leq2L^2H^2+\frac9{8c_0nH^2}.
\]
Since \(H\geq h=n^{-1/4}\),
\[
 \frac1{\sqrt n H}\leq h\leq\rho,
 \qquad
 \frac1{nH^2}\leq h^2\leq\rho^2.
\]
The triangle inequality for absolute loss and \((x-y)^2\leq2x^2+2y^2\) for squared loss now give, uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal P_L\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat\tau_{n,\Delta}-\tau_P(b)|\leq C_1\rho,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_P[(\widehat\tau_{n,\Delta}-\tau_P(b))^2]\leq C_2\rho^2,
\]
where \(C_1,C_2\) depend only on \(L,c_0,\Delta_0\).  The estimator uses only \(N_t,S_t\), which are measurable functions of \(Z_\Delta^n\), and its zero-count fallback is fixed; hence it is one law-independent member of \(\mathcal T_{n,\Delta}\).  Taking the infimum in \(\mathrm{def:minimax\text{-}risks}\) proves both upper bounds.

For the converse, take the uniform-half-disc pair from \(\mathrm{thm:single\text{-}joint\text{-}pair}\).  Its proof verifies that both laws are members of the unknown-design superclass.  Let their released product laws be \(Q_0,Q_1\), targets be \(\theta_0,\theta_1\), and \(\delta=|\theta_1-\theta_0|\).  Then
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\leq\frac14,
 \qquad
 \delta=\kappa(h+\Delta)\geq\kappa\rho,
 \qquad \kappa=\frac{c_L}{4}>0.
\]
For any released-data estimator \(a\), take densities \(q_0,q_1\) relative to \(Q_0+Q_1\).  Pointwise, \(|a-\theta_0|+|a-\theta_1|\geq\delta\), while \(\int\min(q_0,q_1)=1-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\).  Therefore
\[
 \max_{k\in\{0,1\}}\mathbb E_{Q_k}|a-\theta_k|
 \geq\frac\delta2\{1-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\}
 \geq\frac{3\kappa}{8}\rho.
\]
Likewise, \((a-\theta_0)^2+(a-\theta_1)^2\geq\delta^2/2\) pointwise, so
\[
 \max_{k\in\{0,1\}}\mathbb E_{Q_k}(a-\theta_k)^2
 \geq\frac{\delta^2}{4}\{1-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\}
 \geq\frac{3\kappa^2}{16}\rho^2.
\]
The supremum over the superclass is at least the maximum over this embedded pair.  Taking the infimum over all released-data estimators proves the lower bounds and completes the theorem.
