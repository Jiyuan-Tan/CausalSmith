By definition,
\[
 \rho_{n,\Delta}=\max\{n^{-1/4},\Delta\}.
\]
If \(0<\Delta\leq n^{-1/4}\), then exactly
\[
 \rho_{n,\Delta}=n^{-1/4},
 \qquad
 \rho_{n,\Delta}^2=n^{-1/2}.
\]
Substitution into the two-sided bounds of \(\mathrm{thm:minimax\text{-}frontier}\) gives absolute-error order \(n^{-1/4}\) and squared-risk order \(n^{-1/2}\), while substitution into \(\mathrm{thm:honest\text{-}length\text{-}lower}\), including its proved upper comparison, gives honest expected-length order \(n^{-1/4}\).

Now consider any sequence for which \(n^{-1/4}=o(\Delta)\).  Dividing the frontier by \(\Delta>0\) gives
\[
 \frac{\rho_{n,\Delta}}\Delta
 =\max\left\{\frac{n^{-1/4}}\Delta,1\right\}\longrightarrow1,
 \qquad
 \frac{\rho_{n,\Delta}^2}{\Delta^2}\longrightarrow1.
\]
The same two theorems therefore yield absolute minimax risk and honest expected-length order \(\Delta\), and squared minimax risk order \(\Delta^2\), as claimed.
