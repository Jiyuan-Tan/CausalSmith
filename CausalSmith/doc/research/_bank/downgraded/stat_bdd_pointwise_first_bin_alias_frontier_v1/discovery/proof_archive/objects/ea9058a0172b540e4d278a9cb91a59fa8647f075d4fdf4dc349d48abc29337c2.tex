Put \(h=h_n\), \(H=H_{n,\Delta}\), and \(\rho=\rho_{n,\Delta}\).  By \(\mathrm{def:bin\text{-}aware\text{-}estimator}\),
\[
 H=\Delta\left\lceil\frac h\Delta\right\rceil,
 \qquad h=n^{-1/4}.
\]
The inequalities \(x\leq\lceil x\rceil<x+1\), together with \(n\geq256\) and \(0<\Delta\leq\Delta_0\leq1/4\), imply
\[
 h\leq H<h+\Delta\leq2\rho,
 \qquad 0<H<\frac12.
\]

We first prove the upper bounds with the single law-independent estimator in \(\mathrm{def:bin\text{-}aware\text{-}estimator}\).  Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_L\) and \(t\in\{0,1\}\).  Applying \(\mathrm{lem:conditional\text{-}local\text{-}binomial\text{-}law}\), abbreviate its quantities by \(q_t,\nu_t,N_t,S_t\).  The class conditions and \(0<H<1/2\) give
\[
 q_t\geq c_0H^2>0,
 \qquad |\nu_t-\theta_t|\leq LH,
 \qquad N_t\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,q_t).
\]
For every \(k\geq1\) having positive count probability, the same lemma gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[\frac{S_t}{k}\,\middle|\,N_t=k\right]=\nu_t,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}_P\!\left(\frac{S_t}{k}\,\middle|\,N_t=k\right)\leq\frac1{4k}.
\]
The selected outcomes lie in \([0,1]\), so \(S_t/k\in[0,1]\); the clipping operation therefore leaves the positive-count average unchanged.

We next derive the required random-denominator bounds.  If \(N\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,q)\) with \(q>0\), finite expansion and \((k+1)^{-1}=\int_0^1x^k\,dx\) yield
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\frac1{N+1}\right]
 =\int_0^1(1-q+qx)^n\,dx
 =\frac{1-(1-q)^{n+1}}{(n+1)q}
 \leq\frac1{(n+1)q}.
\]
Since \(k^{-1}\leq2(k+1)^{-1}\) for \(k\geq1\), and since Cauchy--Schwarz applies to the bounded random variable \(\mathbf1\{N>0\}/\sqrt N\),
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\frac{\mathbf1\{N>0\}}N\right]
 \leq\frac2{nq},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E\!\left[\frac{\mathbf1\{N>0\}}{\sqrt N}\right]
 \leq\sqrt{\frac2{nq}}.
\]
Also, independence gives
\[
 \mathbb P(N=0)=(1-q)^n\leq e^{-nq}.
\]
For \(x>0\), \(x^{1/2}e^{-x}\leq1\) and \(xe^{-x}\leq1\), whence
\[
 \mathbb P(N=0)\leq(nq)^{-1/2},
 \qquad
 \mathbb P(N=0)\leq(nq)^{-1}.
\]

Conditional Cauchy--Schwarz and the conditional variance bound imply, on \(N_t=k>0\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[\left|\frac{S_t}{k}-\nu_t\right|\,\middle|\,N_t=k\right]
 \leq\frac1{2\sqrt k},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[\left(\frac{S_t}{k}-\nu_t\right)^2\,\middle|\,N_t=k\right]
 \leq\frac1{4k}.
\]
On \(N_t=0\), the fallback is \(1/2\), while \(\nu_t\in[0,1]\), so its absolute and squared deviations from \(\nu_t\) are at most \(1/2\) and \(1/4\), respectively.  Averaging over \(N_t\) and using the preceding bounds gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat m_{t,n,\Delta}-\nu_t|
 \leq\left(\frac1{\sqrt2}+\frac12\right)\frac1{\sqrt{nq_t}},
\]
\[
 \mathbb E_P[(\widehat m_{t,n,\Delta}-\nu_t)^2]
 \leq\frac3{4nq_t}.
\]
Writing \(A=1/\sqrt2+1/2\), using \(q_t\geq c_0H^2\), \(|\nu_t-\theta_t|\leq LH\), and \((a+b)^2\leq2a^2+2b^2\), we obtain
\[
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat m_{t,n,\Delta}-\theta_t|
 \leq LH+\frac{A}{\sqrt{c_0n}\,H},
\]
\[
 \mathbb E_P[(\widehat m_{t,n,\Delta}-\theta_t)^2]
 \leq2L^2H^2+\frac3{2c_0nH^2}.
\]
Since \(H\geq h=n^{-1/4}\),
\[
 \frac1{\sqrt nH}\leq h\leq\rho,
 \qquad
 \frac1{nH^2}\leq h^2\leq\rho^2.
\]
Combining these inequalities with \(H<2\rho\), \(\tau_P(b)=\theta_1-\theta_0\), the triangle inequality, and \((x-y)^2\leq2x^2+2y^2\), gives
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_L}
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat\tau_{n,\Delta}-\tau_P(b)|
 \leq C_1\rho,
 \qquad C_1=4L+\frac{2A}{\sqrt{c_0}},
\]
and
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_L}
 \mathbb E_P[(\widehat\tau_{n,\Delta}-\tau_P(b))^2]
 \leq C_2\rho^2,
 \qquad C_2=32L^2+\frac6{c_0}.
\]
The counts and sums are measurable functions of \(Z_\Delta^n\), the bandwidth is known, and the zero-count fallback is fixed.  Thus \(\widehat\tau_{n,\Delta}\in\mathcal T_{n,\Delta}\).  Taking the two infima in \(\mathrm{def:minimax\text{-}risks}\) proves both upper bounds and their attainment by the displayed estimator up to constants.

The two components of the converse have distinct statistical meanings.  By \(\mathrm{prop:release\text{-}fiber\text{-}diameter}\),
\[
 2c_L\Delta\leq\omega_\Delta\leq4L\Delta.
\]
Thus the structural target is generally not point identified by a fixed-\(\Delta\) released law, and \(\Delta\) is a population release-fiber ambiguity cost.  This does not redefine \(\tau_P(b)\).  In contrast, \(h=n^{-1/4}\) is the local sampling-error scale.  We combine both scales with the single same-class certificate of \(\mathrm{thm:single\text{-}joint\text{-}pair}\).

Let \(P_j=P_{j,n,\Delta}\), \(Q_j=\mathcal L_{P_j}(Z_\Delta^n)\), and \(\vartheta_j=\tau_{P_j}(b)\) for \(j\in\{0,1\}\).  With \(\kappa=c_L/4>0\) and \(\delta=|\vartheta_1-\vartheta_0|\), that theorem gives
\[
 P_0,P_1\in\mathcal P_L,
 \qquad \operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\leq\frac14,
 \qquad \delta=\kappa(h+\Delta)\geq\kappa\rho.
\]
Fix any estimator \(a\in\mathcal T_{n,\Delta}\), let \(\lambda=Q_0+Q_1\), and write \(f_j=dQ_j/d\lambda\).  For every released sample \(z\),
\[
 |a(z)-\vartheta_0|+|a(z)-\vartheta_1|\geq\delta,
\]
and completion of the square gives
\[
 (a(z)-\vartheta_0)^2+(a(z)-\vartheta_1)^2
 =2\left(a(z)-\frac{\vartheta_0+\vartheta_1}{2}\right)^2+\frac{\delta^2}{2}
 \geq\frac{\delta^2}{2}.
\]
Moreover, by the density formula for total variation,
\[
 \int\min(f_0,f_1)\,d\lambda
 =1-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1).
\]
Because \(f_j\geq\min(f_0,f_1)\), multiplying the pointwise loss inequalities by \(\min(f_0,f_1)\), integrating, and using that a maximum is at least half a sum yields
\[
 \max_{j\in\{0,1\}}\mathbb E_{Q_j}|a-\vartheta_j|
 \geq\frac\delta2\{1-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\}
 \geq\frac{3\kappa}{8}\rho,
\]
and
\[
 \max_{j\in\{0,1\}}\mathbb E_{Q_j}(a-\vartheta_j)^2
 \geq\frac{\delta^2}{4}\{1-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\}
 \geq\frac{3\kappa^2}{16}\rho^2.
\]
The supremum over \(\mathcal P_L\) dominates the maximum over \(P_0,P_1\).  Taking the infimum over \(a\in\mathcal T_{n,\Delta}\) proves both lower bounds.  Finally, choose
\[
 c=\min\left\{\frac{3\kappa}{8},\frac{3\kappa^2}{16}\right\}>0,
 \qquad
 C=1+\max\{C_1,C_2,c\}.
\]
These constants are finite, satisfy \(c<C\), and depend only on the permitted class constants.  This proves every assertion uniformly over the stated ranges of \(n\) and \(\Delta\).
