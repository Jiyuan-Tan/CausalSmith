Write \(h=h_n\), \(H=H_{n,\Delta}\), \(p=p_{n,\Delta}\), and \(\rho=\rho_{n,\Delta}\).  Since \(H=\Delta\lceil h/\Delta\rceil\),
\[
 h\le H\le h+\Delta\le2\rho,
 \qquad
 0<H\le\frac12,
 \qquad
 p=\frac{H^2}{2}>0.
\]
The last upper bound uses \(h\le1/4\) and \(\Delta\le\Delta_0\le1/4\).

We first prove achievability.  Because \(H\) is a quantizer boundary,
\[
 \{Q_\Delta(R)<H\}=\{R<H\}.
\]
For \(t\in\{0,1\}\), \(\mathrm{lem:radial\text{-}grouped\text{-}law}\), consistency, and iid sampling imply
\[
 S_{t,n,\Delta}\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,q_t),
 \qquad
 q_t=\int_0^H r m_t(r)\,dr=p\mu_t,
 \qquad
 \mu_t=\frac1p\int_0^H r m_t(r)\,dr.
\]
The Lipschitz assumption gives the explicit boundary bias
\[
 |\mu_t-m_t(0)|
 \le\frac1p\int_0^H rLr\,dr
 =\frac{2LH}{3}=:B.
\]
Let \(V_t=S_{t,n,\Delta}/(np)\).  Then \(\mathbb E_PV_t=\mu_t\), and, since \(q_t\le p\),
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_P(V_t)
 =\frac{q_t(1-q_t)}{np^2}
 \le\frac1{np}.
\]
Euclidean projection onto \([0,1]\) cannot increase distance from the point \(m_t(0)\in[0,1]\).  Hence
\[
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat m_{t,n,\Delta}-m_t(0)|
 \le B+(np)^{-1/2}
\]
and
\[
 \mathbb E_P[(\widehat m_{t,n,\Delta}-m_t(0))^2]
 \le B^2+(np)^{-1}.
\]
Now \((np)^{-1}=2/(nH^2)\le2h^2\), because \(H\ge h=n^{-1/4}\).  Applying the triangle inequality to absolute loss and \((x-y)^2\le2x^2+2y^2\) to squared loss gives, uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal P_L\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat\tau_{n,\Delta}-\tau_P(b)|
 \le2B+2(np)^{-1/2}
 \le\left(\frac{8L}{3}+2\sqrt2\right)\rho
\]
and
\[
 \mathbb E_P[(\widehat\tau_{n,\Delta}-\tau_P(b))^2]
 \le4\{B^2+(np)^{-1}\}
 \le\left(\frac{64L^2}{9}+8\right)\rho^2.
\]
The counts are functions only of \(Z_\Delta^n\), so \(\widehat\tau_{n,\Delta}\in\mathcal T_{n,\Delta}\) by \(\mathrm{def:rounded\text{-}experiment}\).  Taking the infimum in \(\mathrm{def:minimax\text{-}risks}\) proves both upper bounds with this explicit witness.

For the converse, use the two laws from \(\mathrm{thm:single\text{-}joint\text{-}pair}\).  Let their released product laws be \(Q_0,Q_1\), their targets be \(\theta_0,\theta_1\), and \(\delta=|\theta_1-\theta_0|\).  Then
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\le\frac14,
 \qquad
 \delta=\kappa(h+\Delta)\ge\kappa\rho,
 \qquad \kappa=\frac{c_L}{4}>0.
\]
For any released-data estimator \(a\), take densities \(q_0,q_1\) relative to \(Q_0+Q_1\).  Pointwise, \(|a-\theta_0|+|a-\theta_1|\ge\delta\), and \(\int\min(q_0,q_1)=1-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\).  Therefore
\[
 \mathbb E_{Q_0}|a-\theta_0|+\mathbb E_{Q_1}|a-\theta_1|
 \ge\delta\{1-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\},
\]
so the larger of the two risks is at least \(3\delta/8\ge3\kappa\rho/8\).  Likewise,
\[
 (a-\theta_0)^2+(a-\theta_1)^2\ge\frac{\delta^2}{2}
\]
pointwise, which yields a maximum squared risk of at least
\[
 \frac{\delta^2}{4}\{1-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\}
 \ge\frac{3\kappa^2}{16}\rho^2.
\]
Both laws are in the same class \(\mathcal P_L\), so taking the supremum over that class and then the infimum over estimators proves the two lower bounds.  Choosing the smaller lower constant and the larger upper constant proves the theorem with constants depending only on the fixed class constants.
