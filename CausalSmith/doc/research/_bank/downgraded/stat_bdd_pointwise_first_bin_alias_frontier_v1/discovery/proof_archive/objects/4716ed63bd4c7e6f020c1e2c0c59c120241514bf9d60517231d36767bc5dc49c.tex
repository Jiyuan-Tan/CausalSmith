This is an algebraic specialization of the proved frontiers.  If \(0<\Delta\le n^{-1/4}=h_n\), then
\[
 \rho_{n,\Delta}=\max\{h_n,\Delta\}=h_n=n^{-1/4}.
\]
Substitution into \(\mathrm{thm:minimax\text{-}frontier}\) gives absolute-risk order \(n^{-1/4}\) and squared-risk order \(n^{-1/2}\); substitution into \(\mathrm{thm:honest\text{-}length\text{-}lower}\), including its upper bound from \(\mathrm{thm:total\text{-}honest\text{-}interval}\), gives honest expected-length order \(n^{-1/4}\).

If instead \(n^{-1/4}=o(\Delta)\) along a sequence, then \(h_n/\Delta\to0\), so eventually \(h_n<\Delta\) and
\[
 \rho_{n,\Delta}=\Delta.
\]
The same two frontier results then give absolute-risk and honest-length order \(\Delta\), and squared-risk order \(\Delta^2\).  The constants are uniform over the stated ranges because the parent theorems' constants are.
