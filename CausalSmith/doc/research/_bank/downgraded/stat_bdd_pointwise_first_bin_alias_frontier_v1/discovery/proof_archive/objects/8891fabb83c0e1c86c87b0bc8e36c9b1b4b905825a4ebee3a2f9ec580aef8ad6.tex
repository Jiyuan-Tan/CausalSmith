By definition,
\[
 \rho_{n,\Delta}=\max\{n^{-1/4},\Delta\}.
\]
If \(0<\Delta\le n^{-1/4}\), this maximum equals \(n^{-1/4}\) exactly.  Substitution into \(\mathrm{thm:minimax\text{-}frontier}\) and \(\mathrm{thm:honest\text{-}length\text{-}lower}\) gives absolute-error risk and honest expected length of order \(n^{-1/4}\), while the squared-error risk has order \((n^{-1/4})^2=n^{-1/2}\).  If \(n^{-1/4}=o(\Delta)\), then eventually \(\Delta>n^{-1/4}\), so the same maximum equals \(\Delta\); substitution gives absolute-error risk and honest expected length of order \(\Delta\), and squared-error risk of order \(\Delta^2\).  These are precisely the two asserted reductions.
