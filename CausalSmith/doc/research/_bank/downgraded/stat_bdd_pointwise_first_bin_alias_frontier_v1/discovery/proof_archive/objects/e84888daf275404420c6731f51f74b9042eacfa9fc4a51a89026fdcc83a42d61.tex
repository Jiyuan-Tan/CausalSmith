Put \(h=h_n\), \(H=H_{n,\Delta}\), and \(\rho=\rho_{n,\Delta}\).  The bin-aligned bandwidth definition, \(n\geq256\), and \(\Delta\leq\Delta_0\leq1/4\) give
\[
 h\leq H<h+\Delta\leq2\rho,
 \qquad 0<H<\frac12.
\]
Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_L\) and \(t\in\{0,1\}\).  Apply \(\mathrm{lem:conditional\text{-}local\text{-}binomial\text{-}law}\), and abbreviate its quantities by \(q_t,\nu_t,N_t,S_t\).  It gives
\[
 q_t\geq c_0H^2,
 \qquad |\nu_t-\theta_t|\leq LH,
 \qquad N_t\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,q_t),
\]
and, for every \(k\geq1\) of positive count probability,
\[
 \mathbb E_P[S_t/k\mid N_t=k]=\nu_t,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}_P(S_t/k\mid N_t=k)\leq\frac1{4k}.
\]
The conditional support also gives \(0\leq S_t/k\leq1\), so the clipping in \(\mathrm{def:bin\text{-}aware\text{-}estimator}\) does not alter the ratio.

We first establish the needed random-count bounds.  If \(N\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,q)\) with \(q>0\), integration of its probability generating function yields
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\frac1{N+1}\right]
 =\int_0^1(1-q+qx)^n\,dx
 =\frac{1-(1-q)^{n+1}}{(n+1)q}
 \leq\frac1{(n+1)q}.
\]
Since \(k^{-1}\leq2(k+1)^{-1}\) for every integer \(k\geq1\), and by Cauchy--Schwarz,
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\frac{\mathbf1\{N>0\}}N\right]\leq\frac2{nq},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E\!\left[\frac{\mathbf1\{N>0\}}{\sqrt N}\right]
 \leq\sqrt{\frac2{nq}}.
\]
Also \(\mathbb P(N=0)=(1-q)^n\leq e^{-nq}\).  Since \(x^{1/2}e^{-x}\leq1\) and \(xe^{-x}\leq1\) for \(x>0\),
\[
 \mathbb P(N=0)\leq(nq)^{-1/2},
 \qquad
 \mathbb P(N=0)\leq(nq)^{-1}.
\]

On \(N_t=k>0\), conditional Cauchy--Schwarz and the variance bound yield
\[
 \mathbb E_P[|S_t/k-\nu_t|\mid N_t=k]\leq\frac1{2\sqrt k}.
\]
On \(N_t=0\), the fixed fallback obeys \(|1/2-\nu_t|\leq1/2\) and \((1/2-\nu_t)^2\leq1/4\), since \(0\leq\nu_t\leq1\).  Averaging over the count and using the preceding bounds gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat m_{t,n,\Delta}-\nu_t|
 \leq\frac12\sqrt{\frac2{nq_t}}+\frac1{2\sqrt{nq_t}}
 =\left(\frac1{\sqrt2}+\frac12\right)\frac1{\sqrt{nq_t}},
\]
and
\[
 \mathbb E_P[(\widehat m_{t,n,\Delta}-\nu_t)^2]
 \leq\frac14\frac2{nq_t}+\frac14\frac1{nq_t}
 =\frac3{4nq_t}.
\]
Adding the point-anchor bias and using \((a+b)^2\leq2a^2+2b^2\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat m_{t,n,\Delta}-\theta_t|
 \leq LH+\left(\frac1{\sqrt2}+\frac12\right)\frac1{\sqrt{c_0nH^2}},
\]
\[
 \mathbb E_P[(\widehat m_{t,n,\Delta}-\theta_t)^2]
 \leq2L^2H^2+\frac3{2c_0nH^2}.
\]
Because \(H\geq h=n^{-1/4}\),
\[
 \frac1{\sqrt nH}\leq h\leq\rho,
 \qquad
 \frac1{nH^2}\leq h^2\leq\rho^2.
\]
Using \(\tau_P(b)=\theta_1-\theta_0\), the triangle inequality, and \((x-y)^2\leq2x^2+2y^2\), there are finite constants \(C_1,C_2\), depending only on \(L\) and \(c_0\), such that
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_L}\mathbb E_P|\widehat\tau_{n,\Delta}-\tau_P(b)|\leq C_1\rho,
 \qquad
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_L}\mathbb E_P[(\widehat\tau_{n,\Delta}-\tau_P(b))^2]\leq C_2\rho^2.
\]
The counts and localized outcome sums are measurable functions of \(Z_\Delta^n\), and the zero-count branch is fixed.  Thus \(\widehat\tau_{n,\Delta}\) is one law-independent member of \(\mathcal T_{n,\Delta}\).  Taking the infima in \(\mathrm{def:minimax\text{-}risks}\) proves both upper bounds over the bounded-outcome superclass.

For the converse, use the pair from \(\mathrm{thm:single\text{-}joint\text{-}pair}\).  Let \(Q_0,Q_1\) be its released product laws, let \(\vartheta_0,\vartheta_1\) be the corresponding targets, and set \(\delta=|\vartheta_1-\vartheta_0|\).  The joint-pair theorem gives, with \(\kappa=c_L/4>0\),
\[
 P_{0,n,\Delta},P_{1,n,\Delta}\in\mathcal P_L,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\leq\frac14,
 \qquad
 \delta=\kappa(h+\Delta)\geq\kappa\rho.
\]
For any released-data estimator \(a\), take densities \(q_0,q_1\) with respect to \(Q_0+Q_1\).  Pointwise,
\[
 |a-\vartheta_0|+|a-\vartheta_1|\geq\delta,
 \qquad
 (a-\vartheta_0)^2+(a-\vartheta_1)^2\geq\frac{\delta^2}{2},
\]
where the second inequality follows by minimizing the left side over \(a\).  Since
\[
 \int\min(q_0,q_1)=1-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1),
\]
multiplying the pointwise inequalities by \(\min(q_0,q_1)\), integrating, and using that a maximum is at least half a sum yield
\[
 \max_{j\in\{0,1\}}\mathbb E_{Q_j}|a-\vartheta_j|
 \geq\frac\delta2\{1-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\}
 \geq\frac{3\kappa}{8}\rho,
\]
\[
 \max_{j\in\{0,1\}}\mathbb E_{Q_j}(a-\vartheta_j)^2
 \geq\frac{\delta^2}{4}\{1-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\}
 \geq\frac{3\kappa^2}{16}\rho^2.
\]
The supremum over \(\mathcal P_L\) dominates the maximum over this same-class pair.  Taking the infimum over all released-data estimators proves the two lower bounds.  Choosing \(c\) no larger than both displayed lower constants and \(C\) no smaller than \(C_1,C_2\) proves the theorem, with the allowed dependence on \(L\), \(c_0\), and \(\Delta_0\).
