Fix \(n\ge256\) and \(0<\Delta\le\Delta_0\), and let \(P_{0,n,\Delta}\) and \(P_{1,n,\Delta}\) be the baseline and alternative laws named by \(\mathrm{def:joint\text{-}lower\text{-}handle}\).  Write \(h=h_n=n^{-1/4}\) and \(\kappa=c_L/4\).  Since \(n\ge256\) and \(\Delta\le\Delta_0\le1/4\),
\[
 0<h\le\frac14,
 \qquad 0<\Delta\le\frac14.
\]
Under either law, \(X\) is uniform on the unit disc \(\mathcal D\), and the two assignment regions are half-discs.  Therefore polar integration gives, for \(t\in\{0,1\}\) and \(0<H\le1/2\),
\[
 \mathbb P(T=t,R<H)
 =\frac{(\pi H^2)/2}{\pi}
 =\frac{H^2}{2}
 \ge c_0H^2,
\]
where the last inequality uses \(c_0\le1/2\).  Thus the support and two-sided local-mass requirements in \(\mathrm{def:half\text{-}disc\text{-}class}\) hold.  The sharp-assignment, consistency, and conditional Bernoulli requirements hold by the construction and \(\mathrm{ass:sharp\text{-}boundary\text{-}assignment}\), \(\mathrm{ass:consistency}\), and \(\mathrm{ass:radial\text{-}bernoulli}\).

It remains to verify the range and point-anchor requirements.  On \([0,1]\),
\[
 |\phi(u)|=|(1-u)(1-2u)|\le1,
 \qquad
 \phi'(u)=-3+4u,
 \qquad
 |\phi'(u)|\le3.
\]
Because \(\phi(1)=0\), the function \(a_\Delta(r)=\Delta\phi(r/\Delta)\mathbf1\{0\le r<\Delta\}\), extended by zero for \(r\ge\Delta\), is continuous and \(3\)-Lipschitz on \([0,\infty)\).  The function \(b_h(r)=(h-r)_+\) is \(1\)-Lipschitz.  Hence \(g_{n,\Delta}=\kappa(b_h+a_\Delta)\) is \(4\kappa=c_L\)-Lipschitz.  Since \(c_L\le L/8\le L\),
\[
 |g_{n,\Delta}(r)-g_{n,\Delta}(0)|\le Lr.
\]
Moreover, \(c_L\le1/16\), so \(\kappa\le1/64\), and
\[
 |g_{n,\Delta}(r)|
 \le\kappa(h+\Delta)
 \le\frac{1}{128}.
\]
Consequently the alternative treated mean lies in \([0,1]\), and its anchor is
\[
 \theta_{1,1}=\frac12+g_{n,\Delta}(0)
 =\frac12+\kappa(h+\Delta).
\]
The other three side-by-law means are constantly \(1/2\), so their anchors equal \(1/2\) and satisfy the envelope.  This proves
\[
 P_{0,n,\Delta},P_{1,n,\Delta}\in\mathcal P_L.
\]
By the definition of \(\tau_P(b)\), the common control anchor cancels and therefore
\[
 \tau_{P_{1,n,\Delta}}(b)-\tau_{P_{0,n,\Delta}}(b)
 =g_{n,\Delta}(0)
 =\kappa(h_n+\Delta).
\]

We next bound the distance between the released laws.  Put \(W=(T,Q_\Delta(R))\).  Its law is the same under the two constructions.  Conditional on a positive-probability value of \(W\), \(Y\) is Bernoulli with its conditional average success probability by \(\mathrm{lem:conditional\text{-}local\text{-}binomial\text{-}law}\).  On a control value of \(W\), that probability is \(1/2\) under both laws.  On a treated value, write the alternative-minus-baseline conditional success probability as
\[
 \delta(W)=\kappa\,\mathbb E[b_h(R)+a_\Delta(R)\mid W].
\]
The alias component is zero outside the first bin.  In the first treated bin, uniform-disc polar density is proportional to \(r\), and \(\mathrm{lem:first\text{-}bin\text{-}alias}\) gives \(\int_0^1u\phi(u)\,du=0\).  Thus
\[
 \int_0^\Delta r a_\Delta(r)\,dr
 =\Delta^3\int_0^1u\phi(u)\,du
 =0.
\]
It follows that, on treated values of \(W\),
\[
 \delta(W)=\kappa\,\mathbb E[b_h(R)\mid W].
\]

Let \(Q_0\) and \(Q_1\) be the one-unit released laws under \(P_{0,n,\Delta}\) and \(P_{1,n,\Delta}\), respectively.  They have the same marginal law of \(W\), and under \(Q_0\), \(Y\mid W\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\).  Hence the likelihood ratio \(L_1=dQ_1/dQ_0\) equals \(1+2\delta(W)\) when \(Y=1\), equals \(1-2\delta(W)\) when \(Y=0\), and equals one on the control side.  Therefore, using conditional Jensen's inequality,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \chi^2(Q_1,Q_0)
 &=\mathbb E_{Q_0}[(L_1-1)^2]\\
 &=4\mathbb E[\mathbf1\{T=1\}\delta(W)^2]\\
 &\le4\kappa^2\mathbb E[\mathbf1\{T=1\}b_h(R)^2].
 \end{aligned}
\]
Uniform-disc polar integration on the treated half-disc now yields
\[
 \mathbb E[\mathbf1\{T=1\}b_h(R)^2]
 =\int_0^h r(h-r)^2\,dr
 =\frac{h^4}{12},
\]
and hence
\[
 \chi^2(Q_1,Q_0)\le\frac{\kappa^2h^4}{3}.
\]
By \(\mathrm{ass:iid\text{-}sampling}\), the product likelihood ratio is \(L_n=\prod_{i=1}^nL_{1,i}\).  Independence gives the exact identity
\[
 1+\chi^2(Q_1^{\otimes n},Q_0^{\otimes n})
 =\mathbb E_{Q_0^{\otimes n}}[L_n^2]
 =\{\mathbb E_{Q_0}[L_1^2]\}^n
 =\{1+\chi^2(Q_1,Q_0)\}^n.
\]
Using \(1+x\le e^x\) and \(nh^4=1\),
\[
 \chi^2(Q_1^{\otimes n},Q_0^{\otimes n})
 \le e^{\kappa^2/3}-1.
\]
The defining likelihood-ratio formula for total variation and Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(Q_1^{\otimes n},Q_0^{\otimes n})
 =\frac12\mathbb E_{Q_0^{\otimes n}}|L_n-1|
 \le\frac12\sqrt{e^{\kappa^2/3}-1}.
\]
Since \(0\le\kappa^2/3\le1\), convexity of the exponential on \([0,1]\) implies \(e^x-1\le(e-1)x<2x\) there.  Consequently,
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(Q_1^{\otimes n},Q_0^{\otimes n})
 \le\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt6}
 \le\frac{1}{64\sqrt6}
 <\frac14.
\]
Finally \(\alpha<1/4\) implies \(1-2\alpha>1/2>1/4\), proving the asserted uniform testing bound and completing the construction.
