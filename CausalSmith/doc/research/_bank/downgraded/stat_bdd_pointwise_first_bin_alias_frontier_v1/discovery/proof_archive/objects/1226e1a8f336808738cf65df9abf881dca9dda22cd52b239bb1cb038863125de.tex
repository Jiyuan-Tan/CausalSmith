Fix \(C\in\mathcal C_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\), and take the same-class pair in \(\mathrm{thm:single\text{-}joint\text{-}pair}\).  Write \(Q_0,Q_1\) for its released product laws, \(\theta_0,\theta_1\) for its targets, and \(\delta=|\theta_1-\theta_0|\).  The pair theorem gives
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\le\frac14,
 \qquad
 \delta=\kappa(h_n+\Delta)\ge\kappa\rho_{n,\Delta},
 \qquad \kappa=\frac{c_L}{4}>0.
\]
Honesty under the two member laws gives
\[
 Q_0\{\theta_0\in C\}\ge1-\alpha,
 \qquad
 Q_1\{\theta_1\in C\}\ge1-\alpha.
\]
By the defining variational property of total variation,
\[
 Q_0\{\theta_1\in C\}
 \ge Q_1\{\theta_1\in C\}-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)
 \ge1-\alpha-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1).
\]
The union bound under \(Q_0\) consequently yields
\[
 Q_0\{\theta_0\in C,\ \theta_1\in C\}
 \ge1-2\alpha-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)
 \ge\frac34-2\alpha>0.
\]
Whenever a real interval contains both targets, its length is at least \(\delta\).  Therefore
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_L}\mathbb E_P\operatorname{length}(C)
 \ge\mathbb E_{Q_0}\operatorname{length}(C)
 \ge\delta\left(\frac34-2\alpha\right)
 \ge\kappa\left(\frac34-2\alpha\right)\rho_{n,\Delta}.
\]
The coefficient is strictly positive because \(\alpha<1/4\), and it is independent of \(n\) and \(\Delta\).  Taking the infimum over the honest class proves the lower bound for \(\mathfrak W_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\).  The interval in \(\mathrm{thm:total\text{-}honest\text{-}interval}\) belongs to that class and has worst-case expected length at most a constant times \(\rho_{n,\Delta}\), proving the reverse inequality and hence \(\mathfrak W_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\asymp\rho_{n,\Delta}\).
