Fix \(n\geq256\), \(0<\Delta\leq\Delta_0\), and \(P\in\mathcal P_L\), and write
\[
 h=h_n=n^{-1/4},\qquad H=H_{n,\Delta},\qquad B=LH.
\]
The aligned-bandwidth definition gives
\[
 h\leq H<h+\Delta\leq\frac12,
\]
because \(h\leq1/4\) and \(\Delta\leq\Delta_0\leq1/4\).

For \(t\in\{0,1\}\), \(\mathrm{lem:conditional\text{-}local\text{-}binomial\text{-}law}\) gives
\[
 q_t:=\mathbb P_P(T=t,R<H)\geq c_0H^2>0,
 \qquad
 \nu_t:=\mathbb E_P[\mu_t(X)\mid T=t,R<H],
\]
\[
 |\nu_t-\theta_t|=|\nu_t-\mu_t(b)|\leq LH=B,
 \qquad
 N_{t,n,\Delta}\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,q_t),
\]
and, for every count value \(k\) having positive probability,
\[
 S_{t,n,\Delta}\mid\{N_{t,n,\Delta}=k\}
 \sim\operatorname{Binomial}(k,\nu_t).
\]
The equality between rounded and unrounded localization used here is exact because
\[
 \{Q_\Delta(R)<H\}
 =\{\Delta\lfloor R/\Delta\rfloor<K_{n,\Delta}\Delta\}
 =\{R<K_{n,\Delta}\Delta\}
 =\{R<H\}.
\]

If \(N_{t,n,\Delta}=0\), the side interval is \([0,1]\) and contains \(\nu_t\).  If the count is \(k>0\), the revalidated cited leaf \(\mathrm{lem:clopper\text{-}pearson\text{-}conditional\text{-}coverage}\), applied to the conditional binomial law, gives conditional coverage at least \(1-\alpha/2\).  Averaging over the count gives
\[
 \mathbb P_P\{\nu_t\in[\ell_t,u_t]\}\geq1-\alpha/2.
\]
By a union bound there is an event \(E\) of probability at least \(1-\alpha\) on which both side inclusions hold.  On \(E\),
\[
 \nu_1-\nu_0\in[\ell_1-u_0,u_1-\ell_0]
\]
and the two point-anchor bias inequalities imply
\[
 |\tau_P(b)-(\nu_1-\nu_0)|
 =|(\theta_1-\nu_1)-(\theta_0-\nu_0)|
 \leq2B.
\]
Thus \(\tau_P(b)\in C_{n,\Delta,\alpha}\) on \(E\), uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal P_L\).

The counts and successes are measurable functions of \(Z_\Delta^n\); the Clopper--Pearson endpoints are functions on a finite count-success state space; and the zero-count branch is explicit.  Since \(0\leq\ell_t\leq u_t\leq1\) and \(B<\infty\), the endpoints in \(\mathrm{def:total\text{-}honest\text{-}interval}\) form an ordered, closed, bounded, \(\sigma(Z_\Delta^n)\)-measurable interval on every sample.  This proves totality and honesty.

For expected length, put \(\beta=\alpha/4>0\).  Fix \(k\geq1\), \(s\in\{0,\ldots,k\}\), set \(\widehat p=s/k\), and write
\[
 I^{\mathrm{CP}}_{k,\alpha/2}(s)=[\ell,u].
\]
For a nontrivial upper endpoint, let \(d=u-\widehat p>0\) and \(W\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(k,u)\).  On \(\{W\leq s\}\), \(|W-ku|\geq kd\), so the upper endpoint equation in the cited leaf and the binomial variance identity yield
\[
 \beta=\mathbb P_u(W\leq s)
 \leq\frac{u(1-u)}{kd^2}
 \leq\frac{\widehat p+d}{kd^2}.
\]
Thus \(d^2\leq \widehat p/(k\beta)+d/(k\beta)\).  If nonnegative numbers satisfy \(d^2\leq A+Dd\), then either \(d\leq D\), or \((d-D)^2\leq d(d-D)\leq A\); in both cases \(d\leq\sqrt A+D\).  Therefore
\[
 u-\widehat p\leq\sqrt{\frac{\widehat p}{k\beta}}+\frac1{k\beta}.
\]
For a nontrivial lower endpoint, set \(d_-=\widehat p-\ell>0\) and take \(W\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(k,\ell)\).  On \(\{W\geq s\}\), \(|W-k\ell|\geq kd_-\).  The lower endpoint equation and \(\ell(1-\ell)\leq\ell\leq\widehat p\) give
\[
 \beta\leq\frac{\ell(1-\ell)}{kd_-^2}
 \leq\frac{\widehat p}{kd_-^2},
 \qquad
 \widehat p-\ell\leq\sqrt{\frac{\widehat p}{k\beta}}.
\]
The cited boundary conventions cover \(s=0\) and \(s=k\), so for every \(s\),
\[
 u-\ell\leq2\sqrt{\frac{\widehat p}{k\beta}}+\frac1{k\beta}.
\]
Conditionally on \(N_{t,n,\Delta}=k>0\), Jensen's inequality and \(0\leq S_{t,n,\Delta}/k\leq1\) imply
\[
 \mathbb E_P[u_t-\ell_t\mid N_{t,n,\Delta}=k]
 \leq\frac2{\sqrt{k\beta}}+\frac1{k\beta}.
\]

For \(N\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,q)\), \(q>0\),
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\frac1{N+1}\right]
 =\int_0^1(1-q+qx)^n\,dx
 \leq\frac1{(n+1)q}.
\]
Using \(k^{-1}\leq2(k+1)^{-1}\), Cauchy--Schwarz, and \((1-q)^n\leq e^{-nq}\leq(nq)^{-1}\), one obtains
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\frac{\mathbf1\{N>0\}}N\right]\leq\frac2{nq},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E\!\left[\frac{\mathbf1\{N>0\}}{\sqrt N}\right]\leq\sqrt{\frac2{nq}},
 \qquad
 \mathbb P(N=0)\leq\frac1{nq}.
\]
The zero-count side interval has length one.  Consequently
\[
 \mathbb E_P[u_t-\ell_t]
 \leq\frac{2\sqrt2}{\sqrt{\beta nq_t}}
 +\left(1+\frac2\beta\right)\frac1{nq_t}
 \leq\frac{2\sqrt2}{\sqrt{\beta c_0nH^2}}
 +\left(1+\frac2\beta\right)\frac1{c_0nH^2}.
\]

The constructed interval has exact length
\[
 \operatorname{length}(C_{n,\Delta,\alpha})
 =(u_1-\ell_1)+(u_0-\ell_0)+4LH.
\]
Finally,
\[
 H<h+\Delta\leq2\rho_{n,\Delta},
 \qquad
 \frac1{\sqrt nH}\leq h\leq\rho_{n,\Delta},
 \qquad
 \frac1{nH^2}\leq h^2\leq\rho_{n,\Delta}.
\]
Summing the two side bounds gives, with \(\beta=\alpha/4\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_L}\mathbb E_P[\operatorname{length}(C_{n,\Delta,\alpha})]
 \leq
 \left\{
 \frac{4\sqrt2}{\sqrt{\beta c_0}}
 +\frac{2(1+2/\beta)}{c_0}+8L
 \right\}\rho_{n,\Delta}.
\]
This finite constant has the permitted dependence on \(L,c_0,\alpha,\Delta_0\) and is uniform in every allowed \(n\) and \(\Delta\).
