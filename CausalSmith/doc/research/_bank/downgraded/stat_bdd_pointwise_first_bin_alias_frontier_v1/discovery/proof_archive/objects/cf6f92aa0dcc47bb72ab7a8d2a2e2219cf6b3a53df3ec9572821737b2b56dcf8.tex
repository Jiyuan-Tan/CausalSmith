By definition, \(h_n=n^{-1/4}\) and \(\rho_{n,\Delta}=\max\{h_n,\Delta\}\).  If \(0<\Delta\leq n^{-1/4}=h_n\), then \(\rho_{n,\Delta}=h_n=n^{-1/4}\).  Substitution into \(\mathrm{thm:minimax\text{-}frontier}\) gives minimax absolute-error order \(n^{-1/4}\) and squared-risk order \((n^{-1/4})^2=n^{-1/2}\), while substitution into \(\mathrm{thm:honest\text{-}length\text{-}lower}\), including its matching upper conclusion, gives honest expected-length order \(n^{-1/4}\).

Now consider any sequence for which \(h_n=n^{-1/4}=o(\Delta)\).  By the definition of little-oh, \(h_n/\Delta\to0\), so \(h_n<\Delta\) for all sufficiently large indices on the sequence.  Consequently \(\rho_{n,\Delta}=\Delta\) eventually.  The same two theorems then give, respectively, minimax absolute-error and honest expected-length order \(\Delta\), and minimax squared-risk order \(\Delta^2\).  These are exactly the two asserted regime statements.
