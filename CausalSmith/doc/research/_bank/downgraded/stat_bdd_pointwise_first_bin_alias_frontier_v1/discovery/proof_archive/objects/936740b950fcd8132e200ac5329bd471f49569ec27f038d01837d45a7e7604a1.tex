Fix \(n\geq256\), \(0<\Delta\leq\Delta_0\), and \(C\in\mathcal C_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\).  By \(\mathrm{thm:single\text{-}joint\text{-}pair}\), there are \(P_{0,n,\Delta},P_{1,n,\Delta}\in\mathcal P_L\).  Define
\[
 Q_j=\mathcal L_{P_{j,n,\Delta}}(Z_\Delta^n),
 \qquad
 \vartheta_j=\tau_{P_{j,n,\Delta}}(b),
 \qquad j\in\{0,1\}.
\]
The same theorem gives
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\leq\frac14,
 \qquad
 \delta:=|\vartheta_1-\vartheta_0|
 =\kappa(h_n+\Delta),
 \qquad
 \kappa=\frac{c_L}{4}>0.
\]
Since \(\rho_{n,\Delta}=\max\{h_n,\Delta\}\),
\[
 h_n+\Delta\geq\rho_{n,\Delta}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \delta\geq\kappa\rho_{n,\Delta}.
\]

The definition of \(\mathcal C_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\) and the membership of both laws in \(\mathcal P_L\) imply
\[
 Q_0\{\vartheta_0\in C\}\geq1-\alpha,
 \qquad
 Q_1\{\vartheta_1\in C\}\geq1-\alpha.
\]
For the measurable event \(A_1=\{\vartheta_1\in C\}\), the definition of total variation gives
\[
 Q_0(A_1)
 \geq Q_1(A_1)-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)
 \geq1-\alpha-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1).
\]
Consequently, the elementary inequality \(Q_0(A_0\cap A_1)\geq Q_0(A_0)+Q_0(A_1)-1\), with \(A_0=\{\vartheta_0\in C\}\), yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Q_0\{\vartheta_0\in C,\ \vartheta_1\in C\}
 &\geq1-2\alpha-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\\
 &\geq\frac34-2\alpha>0,
 \end{aligned}
\]
where the strict inequality uses \(\alpha\in(0,1/4)\).  On this event the closed real interval \(C\) contains both \(\vartheta_0\) and \(\vartheta_1\), and therefore has length at least \(\delta\).  Hence
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_L}\mathbb E_P[\operatorname{length}(C)]
 &\geq\mathbb E_{P_{0,n,\Delta}}[\operatorname{length}(C)]\\
 &\geq\delta\,Q_0\{\vartheta_0\in C,\ \vartheta_1\in C\}\\
 &\geq\frac{c_L}{4}\left(\frac34-2\alpha\right)\rho_{n,\Delta}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The first inequality is valid because \(P_{0,n,\Delta}\in\mathcal P_L\).  Thus the claimed lower bound holds with
\[
 c_{L,\alpha,\Delta_0}
 =\frac{c_L}{4}\left(\frac34-2\alpha\right)>0.
\]
This constant is independent of \(n\), \(\Delta\), \(c_0\), and in fact \(\Delta_0\), which is stronger than the stated dependence allowance.

For the matching upper bound, \(\mathrm{thm:total\text{-}honest\text{-}interval}\) states that the explicit interval \(C_{n,\Delta,\alpha}\) is a closed bounded \(\sigma(Z_\Delta^n)\)-measurable interval, has coverage at least \(1-\alpha\) uniformly over \(\mathcal P_L\), and satisfies
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_L}
 \mathbb E_P[\operatorname{length}(C_{n,\Delta,\alpha})]
 \leq C_{L,c_0,\alpha,\Delta_0}\rho_{n,\Delta}.
\]
It follows from \(\mathrm{def:honest\text{-}class}\) that \(C_{n,\Delta,\alpha}\in\mathcal C_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\), and then \(\mathrm{def:honest\text{-}width}\) gives
\[
 \mathfrak W_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)
 \leq C_{L,c_0,\alpha,\Delta_0}\rho_{n,\Delta}.
\]
Taking the infimum of the preceding lower bound over \(C\in\mathcal C_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\) and combining the two inequalities proves \(\mathfrak W_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\asymp\rho_{n,\Delta}\), uniformly over all allowed \(n\) and \(\Delta\).
