Fix the objects in the statement.  First use conditioning and consistency.  If \(B\in\sigma(T,Q_\Delta(R))\), \(B\subseteq\{T=t\}\), and \(\mathbb P_P(B)>0\), then \(B\in\sigma(X)\), because both \(T\) and \(Q_\Delta(R)\) are functions of \(X\) by \(\mathrm{ass:sharp\text{-}boundary\text{-}assignment}\) and \(\mathrm{def:rounded\text{-}experiment}\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:consistency}\) gives \(Y=Y(t)\) on \(B\).  The conditional-mean member property in \(\mathrm{def:half\text{-}disc\text{-}class}\) therefore implies
\[
 \mathbb E_P[Y\mathbf1_B]
 =\mathbb E_P[Y(t)\mathbf1_B]
 =\mathbb E_P[\mu_t(X)\mathbf1_B].
\]
Division by the positive number \(\mathbb P_P(B)\) proves
\[
 \mathbb E_P[Y\mid B]=\mathbb E_P[\mu_t(X)\mid B].
\]
The same member property and consistency show that \(Y\mid B\) is supported on \([0,1]\).

Next use bin alignment.  Write \(H_{n,\Delta}=K_{n,\Delta}\Delta\), where \(K_{n,\Delta}=\lceil h_n/\Delta\rceil\) is a positive integer.  For every \(r\in[0,1]\),
\[
 Q_\Delta(r)<H_{n,\Delta}
 \iff \Delta\lfloor r/\Delta\rfloor<K_{n,\Delta}\Delta
 \iff r<K_{n,\Delta}\Delta.
\]
Thus \(A_t=\{T=t,R<H_{n,\Delta}\}\).  Moreover,
\[
 h_n\le H_{n,\Delta}<h_n+\Delta\le\frac12,
\]
because \(n\ge256\) gives \(h_n=n^{-1/4}\le1/4\) and \(\Delta\le\Delta_0\le1/4\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:uniform\text{-}disc\text{-}design}\) consequently gives
\[
 q_t:=\mathbb P_P(A_t)\ge c_0H_{n,\Delta}^2>0.
\]
By the first part and \(\mathrm{ass:radial\text{-}lipschitz}\),
\[
 \nu_t=\mathbb E_P[Y\mid A_t]
 =\mathbb E_P[\mu_t(X)\mid A_t],
 \qquad
 |\nu_t-\mu_t(b)|
 \le \mathbb E_P[|\mu_t(X)-\theta_t|\mid A_t]
 \le LH_{n,\Delta},
\]
where \(\mu_t(b)=\theta_t\) and its uniqueness is supplied by \(\mathrm{lem:boundary\text{-}anchor\text{-}uniqueness}\).

Finally use the conditional thinning law, derived directly from \(\mathrm{ass:iid\text{-}sampling}\).  The indicators \(\mathbf1_{A_t}(X_i)\) are independent Bernoulli variables of common success probability \(q_t\), so
\[
 N_{t,n,\Delta}\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,q_t).
\]
Conditional on any specified set of \(k\) successful indices, independence makes the corresponding outcomes independent with common law \(\mathcal L_P(Y\mid A_t)\); this conditional law does not depend on the specified index set.  Mixing over the index sets conditional on \(N_{t,n,\Delta}=k\) therefore leaves the ordered selected outcomes independent and identically distributed with that same law.  Hence, for \(k\ge1\),
\[
 S_{t,n,\Delta}=\sum_{j=1}^kY_{t,j}^{\star},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[\frac{S_{t,n,\Delta}}k\,\middle|\,N_{t,n,\Delta}=k\right]=\nu_t.
\]
If \(W\in[0,1]\) has mean \(p\), then \(W^2\le W\), and therefore
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(W)=\mathbb E[W^2]-p^2\le p(1-p)\le\frac14.
\]
Independence of the selected outcomes now gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_P\!\left(\frac{S_{t,n,\Delta}}k\,\middle|\,N_{t,n,\Delta}=k\right)
 =\frac1{k^2}\sum_{j=1}^k\operatorname{Var}_P(Y_{t,j}^{\star}\mid N_{t,n,\Delta}=k)
 \le\frac1{4k}.
\]
Nothing in this argument makes a general bounded outcome Bernoulli, which proves the final qualification.
