Fix \(n\ge256\), \(0<\Delta\le\Delta_0\), and \(C\in\mathcal C_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\).  Apply \(\mathrm{thm:single\text{-}joint\text{-}pair}\), the resolution of \(\mathrm{oeq:single\text{-}joint\text{-}pair}\), and abbreviate its two released product laws by \(Q_0,Q_1\), its two targets by \(\vartheta_j=\tau_{P_{j,n,\Delta}}(b)\), and their separation by
\[
 d=\vartheta_1-\vartheta_0
 =\kappa(h_n+\Delta),
 \qquad \kappa=\frac{c_L}{4}>0.
\]
Both laws belong to \(\mathcal P_L\), and \(C\) is honest over that class by \(\mathrm{def:honest\text{-}class}\).  Hence
\[
 Q_0\{\vartheta_0\in C\}\ge1-\alpha,
 \qquad
 Q_1\{\vartheta_1\in C\}\ge1-\alpha.
\]
The defining supremum characterization of total variation and the joint-pair bound imply
\[
 Q_0\{\vartheta_1\in C\}
 \ge Q_1\{\vartheta_1\in C\}-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)
 \ge1-\alpha-\frac14.
\]
The union bound, applied under \(Q_0\), therefore gives
\[
 Q_0\{\vartheta_0\in C,\ \vartheta_1\in C\}
 \ge1-2\alpha-\frac14
 =\frac34-2\alpha>0.
\]
On this event the interval \(C\) contains both endpoints, so its length is at least \(d\).  It follows equation by equation that
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_L}\mathbb E_P[\operatorname{length}(C)]
 &\ge \mathbb E_{P_{0,n,\Delta}}[\operatorname{length}(C)]\\
 &\ge d\left(\frac34-2\alpha\right)\\
 &=\kappa\left(\frac34-2\alpha\right)(h_n+\Delta)\\
 &\ge\kappa\left(\frac34-2\alpha\right)\rho_{n,\Delta},
 \end{aligned}
\]
where the last line uses \(h_n+\Delta\ge\max\{h_n,\Delta\}=\rho_{n,\Delta}\).  Thus the claimed lower bound holds with
\[
 c_{L,\alpha,\Delta_0}
 =\frac{c_L}{4}\left(\frac34-2\alpha\right)>0;
\]
the notation permits dependence on \(\Delta_0\), although this displayed valid choice does not require it.  Taking the infimum over \(C\in\mathcal C_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\) proves
\[
 \mathfrak W_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)
 \ge c_{L,\alpha,\Delta_0}\rho_{n,\Delta}.
\]
For the reverse inequality, \(\mathrm{thm:total\text{-}honest\text{-}interval}\) states that the released-sample interval \(C_{n,\Delta,\alpha}\) is everywhere defined, is honest over \(\mathcal P_L\), and has worst-case expected length at most \(C_{L,c_0,\alpha,\Delta_0}\rho_{n,\Delta}\).  By \(\mathrm{def:total\text{-}honest\text{-}interval}\) it is a closed bounded, released-sample measurable interval, hence it belongs to \(\mathcal C_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\).  The infimum in \(\mathrm{def:honest\text{-}width}\) is therefore no larger than its worst-case expected length.  Combining the two inequalities proves \(\mathfrak W_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\asymp\rho_{n,\Delta}\).
