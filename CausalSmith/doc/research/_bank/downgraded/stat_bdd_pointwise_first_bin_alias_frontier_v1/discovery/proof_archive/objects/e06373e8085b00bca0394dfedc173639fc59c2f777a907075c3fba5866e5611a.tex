The polynomial in \(\mathrm{def:first\text{-}bin\text{-}alias}\) is
\[
 \phi(u)=1-3u+2u^2,
 \qquad
 \int_0^1u\phi(u)\,du=\frac12-1+\frac12=0.
\]
Also \(\phi(1)=0\), \(|\phi(u)|\leq1\) on \([0,1]\), and \(\phi'(u)=-3+4u\), so \(\sup_{u\in[0,1]}|\phi'(u)|=3\).  Consequently
\[
 x\longmapsto \Delta\phi(\lVert x-b\rVert_2/\Delta)
 \mathbf1\{\lVert x-b\rVert_2<\Delta\}
\]
is continuous at radius \(\Delta\) and is \(3\)-Lipschitz, because the radius map is \(1\)-Lipschitz.  Since \(c_L\leq L/8\), the perturbed treated means are at most \(3c_L\leq L\)-Lipschitz.  Since \(c_L\leq1/16\) and \(\Delta\leq\Delta_0\leq1/4\), their displacement from \(1/2\) is at most \(1/64<1/4\); hence all means lie in \([1/4,3/4]\).

The construction gives sharp assignment and consistency.  For the uniform disc and half-plane sides, polar integration gives, for every \(H\leq1/2\) and each \(t\),
\[
 \mathbb P(T=t,R<H)=\frac1\pi\int_0^H\int_{\Theta_t}r\,d\vartheta\,dr
 =\frac{H^2}{2}\geq c_0H^2,
 \qquad |\Theta_t|=\pi,
\]
where the last inequality uses \(c_0\leq1/2\).  Thus every member property of the unknown-design class is satisfied, so both constructed laws belong to \(\mathcal P_L\).  This calculation exhibits the uniform half-disc laws only as an embedded least-favorable subclass; it makes no orthogonality assertion for another design.

The two laws have the same distribution of \((T,Q_\Delta(R))\).  In the treated first bin their difference in joint success mass is
\[
 \frac1\pi\int_{\Theta_1}\int_0^\Delta
 2c_L\Delta\phi(r/\Delta)r\,dr\,d\vartheta
 =2c_L\Delta^3\int_0^1u\phi(u)\,du=0.
\]
Outside that bin the perturbation vanishes, and every control cell is unchanged.  The binary-mixture assertion in \(\mathrm{lem:conditional\text{-}local\text{-}binomial\text{-}law}\) then shows that equality of the covariate-cell masses and success masses gives equality of the entire one-unit laws of \(Z_\Delta\).  Finally, \(\phi(0)=1\) and the target is \(\mu_1(b)-\mu_0(b)\), so the two targets are \(c_L\Delta\) and \(-c_L\Delta\), respectively.  Their difference is \(2c_L\Delta\).
