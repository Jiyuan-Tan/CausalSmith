First let \(B\in\sigma(T,Q_\Delta(R))\) satisfy the conditions in the statement.  On \(B\), consistency gives \(Y=Y(t)\).  By \(\mathrm{ass:radial\text{-}bernoulli}\), iterated conditioning, and the fact that a binary law is determined by its success probability,
\[
 \mathbb P_P(Y=1\mid B)
 =\mathbb E_P[\mathbb P_P(Y(t)=1\mid X)\mid B]
 =\mathbb E_P[\mu_t(X)\mid B].
\]
This proves the first assertion.  Now write \(h=h_n\) and \(H=H_{n,\Delta}\).  Since \(H=K_{n,\Delta}\Delta\) is an integer multiple of \(\Delta\), the definition of the floor quantizer gives
\[
 \{Q_\Delta(R)<H\}=\{R<H\}.
\]
Moreover, \(h\leq H<h+\Delta\leq1/2\), because \(n\geq256\) gives \(h\leq1/4\) and \(\Delta\leq\Delta_0\leq1/4\).  The local-mass member property \(\mathrm{ass:uniform\text{-}disc\text{-}design}\) therefore gives
\[
 q_t:=\mathbb P_P(A_t)\geq c_0H^2>0.
\]
The general assertion with \(B=A_t\) shows that \(Y\mid A_t\) is Bernoulli with parameter \(\nu_t\).  The spatial Lipschitz condition applies because every \(X\) in \(A_t\) lies on side \(t\) and has radius below \(H\leq1/2\).  Hence
\[
 |\nu_t-\mu_t(b)|
 \leq\mathbb E_P[|\mu_t(X)-\mu_t(b)|\mid A_t]
 \leq L\mathbb E_P[R\mid A_t]
 \leq LH.
\]
Finally, iid sampling implies that the pairs \((\mathbf1_{A_{t,i}},\mathbf1_{A_{t,i}}Y_i)\) are iid.  In particular, \(N_{t,n,\Delta}\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,q_t)\).  Their joint one-unit probability generating function is
\[
 1-q_t+q_t(1-\nu_t)w+q_t\nu_t wz,
\]
where \(w\) marks membership and \(z\) marks success.  Raising this expression to the \(n\)-th power and comparing the coefficient of \(w^k\) shows that, conditional on \(N_{t,n,\Delta}=k\), the generating function of \(S_{t,n,\Delta}\) is \((1-\nu_t+\nu_t z)^k\).  This is the asserted binomial law, including the degenerate case \(k=0\).
