Fix \(n\geq256\) and \(0<\Delta\leq\Delta_0\), and put \(h=h_n\), \(\kappa=c_L/4\).  Then \(h\leq1/4\) and \(\Delta\leq1/4\).  The calculation in \(\mathrm{lem:first\text{-}bin\text{-}alias}\) shows that
\[
 a_\Delta(r)=\Delta\phi(r/\Delta)\mathbf1\{r<\Delta\}
\]
is \(3\)-Lipschitz, satisfies \(a_\Delta(0)=\Delta\), and obeys
\[
 \int_0^\Delta r a_\Delta(r)\,dr
 =\Delta^3\int_0^1u\phi(u)\,du=0.
\]
The triangular bump \(b_h(r)=(h-r)_+\) is \(1\)-Lipschitz and satisfies \(b_h(0)=h\).  Thus \(g_{n,\Delta}=\kappa(b_h+a_\Delta)\) is \(4\kappa=c_L\leq L\)-Lipschitz.  Moreover,
\[
 |g_{n,\Delta}(r)|\leq\kappa(h+\Delta)\leq\frac{c_L}{8}\leq\frac1{128}<\frac14.
\]

Use the two laws named in \(\mathrm{def:joint\text{-}lower\text{-}handle}\).  Composition with the radius preserves the Lipschitz bound because \(|\lVert x-b\rVert_2-\lVert x'-b\rVert_2|\leq\lVert x-x'\rVert_2\).  The preceding range bound, the conditional Bernoulli construction, sharp assignment, and consistency verify the corresponding member properties.  Uniform polar integration gives, for each side and every \(H\leq1/2\),
\[
 \mathbb P(T=t,R<H)=\frac{H^2}{2}\geq c_0H^2,
\]
using \(c_0\leq1/2\).  Hence both laws lie in the unknown-design class \(\mathcal P_L\).  They form an embedded uniform-half-disc subclass, not a restriction imposed on the upper theorem.  Their boundary targets differ by
\[
 g_{n,\Delta}(0)=\kappa(h+\Delta).
\]

It remains to bound the released likelihood distance.  Partition \([0,1]\) into the quantizer cells \(A_j=[j\Delta,\min\{(j+1)\Delta,1\})\) of positive mass and put
\[
 w_j=\int_{A_j}r\,dr,
 \qquad
 \bar b_j=w_j^{-1}\int_{A_j}rb_h(r)\,dr.
\]
For the uniform half-disc, \(w_j=\mathbb P(T=1,Q_\Delta(R)=j\Delta)\).  The alias term is supported on the complete first cell and has zero \(r\)-weighted integral there; it vanishes on every other cell.  The binary-mixture calculation in \(\mathrm{lem:conditional\text{-}local\text{-}binomial\text{-}law}\), applied cell by cell, therefore shows that the treated success probability in cell \(j\) is \(1/2\) under \(P_0\) and \(1/2+\kappa\bar b_j\) under \(P_1\).  Every control cell is unchanged.

Let \(Q_k=\mathcal L_{P_k}(Z_\Delta)\).  Direct calculation gives
\[
 \chi^2\!\left(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2+x),\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\right)=4x^2.
\]
Jensen's inequality within each cell consequently yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 d:=\chi^2(Q_1,Q_0)
 &=4\kappa^2\sum_jw_j\bar b_j^2\\
 &\leq4\kappa^2\int_0^1rb_h(r)^2\,dr
 =4\kappa^2\int_0^h r(h-r)^2\,dr
 =\frac{\kappa^2h^4}{3}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Iid sampling makes the likelihood ratio a product, and expanding its second moment gives
\[
 1+\chi^2(Q_1^{\otimes n},Q_0^{\otimes n})=(1+d)^n
 \leq\exp(nd)\leq\exp(\kappa^2/3),
\]
because \(nh^4=1\).  Since \(\kappa\leq1/64\), the elementary bound \(e^x-1\leq2x\) for \(0\leq x\leq1\) gives
\[
 \chi^2(Q_1^{\otimes n},Q_0^{\otimes n})\leq\frac{2\kappa^2}{3}.
\]
If \(M=dQ_1^{\otimes n}/dQ_0^{\otimes n}\), Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(Q_1^{\otimes n},Q_0^{\otimes n})
 =\frac12\mathbb E_{Q_0^{\otimes n}}|M-1|
 \leq\frac12\{\mathbb E_{Q_0^{\otimes n}}(M-1)^2\}^{1/2}
 \leq\frac\kappa{\sqrt6}<\frac14.
\]
Finally, \(\alpha<1/4\) implies \(1/4<1-2\alpha\).  This proves the same-class pair, the claimed separation, and the uniform total-variation bound.
