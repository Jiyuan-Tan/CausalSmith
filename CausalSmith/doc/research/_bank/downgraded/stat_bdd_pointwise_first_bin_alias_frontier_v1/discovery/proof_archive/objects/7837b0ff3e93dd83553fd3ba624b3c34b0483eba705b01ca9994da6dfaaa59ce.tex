Recall from the definition of the sampling--aliasing frontier that
\[
 \rho_{n,\Delta}=\max\{n^{-1/4},\Delta\}.
\]
Along any sequence satisfying \(0<\Delta\le n^{-1/4}\), this identity gives \(\rho_{n,\Delta}=n^{-1/4}\) and \(\rho_{n,\Delta}^2=n^{-1/2}\).  Substitution into the two uniform equivalences of \(\mathrm{thm:minimax\text{-}frontier}\) yields minimax absolute-error order \(n^{-1/4}\) and squared-risk order \(n^{-1/2}\); substitution into \(\mathrm{thm:honest\text{-}length\text{-}lower}\), whose proof also supplies the matching upper bound, yields honest expected-length order \(n^{-1/4}\).

Along any sequence satisfying \(n^{-1/4}=o(\Delta)\), the ratio \(n^{-1/4}/\Delta\) tends to zero.  Hence \(n^{-1/4}<\Delta\) eventually, so eventually
\[
 \rho_{n,\Delta}=\Delta,
 \qquad
 \rho_{n,\Delta}^2=\Delta^2.
\]
The same substitutions into \(\mathrm{thm:minimax\text{-}frontier}\) and \(\mathrm{thm:honest\text{-}length\text{-}lower}\) give, respectively, minimax absolute-error and honest expected-length order \(\Delta\), and minimax squared-risk order \(\Delta^2\), as asserted.
