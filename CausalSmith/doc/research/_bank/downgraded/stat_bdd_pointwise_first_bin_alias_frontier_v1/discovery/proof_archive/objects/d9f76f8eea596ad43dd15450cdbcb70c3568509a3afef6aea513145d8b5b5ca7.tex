Fix \(n\geq256\), \(0<\Delta\leq\Delta_0\), and an arbitrary \(C\in\mathcal C_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\).  Use the same-class pair supplied by Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:single\text{-}joint\text{-}pair}\), and write
\[
 Q_j=\mathcal L_{P_{j,n,\Delta}}(Z_\Delta^n),
 \qquad
 \theta_j=\tau_{P_{j,n,\Delta}}(b),
 \qquad j\in\{0,1\}.
\]
That theorem establishes both class membership \(P_{j,n,\Delta}\in\mathcal P_L\) and
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\leq\frac14,
 \qquad
 \delta:=|\theta_1-\theta_0|
 =\kappa(h_n+\Delta),
 \qquad
 \kappa=\frac{c_L}{4}>0.
\]
Since \(h_n+\Delta\geq\max\{h_n,\Delta\}=\rho_{n,\Delta}\), one has \(\delta\geq\kappa\rho_{n,\Delta}\).

Honesty and class membership give
\[
 Q_0\{\theta_0\in C\}\geq1-\alpha,
 \qquad
 Q_1\{\theta_1\in C\}\geq1-\alpha.
\]
For every measurable event \(A\), the defining variational inequality for total variation is \(|Q_1(A)-Q_0(A)|\leq\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\).  Applying it to \(A=\{\theta_1\in C\}\) yields
\[
 Q_0\{\theta_1\in C\}
 \geq1-\alpha-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1).
\]
The elementary intersection inequality \(Q_0(A\cap B)\geq Q_0(A)+Q_0(B)-1\) then gives
\[
 Q_0\{\theta_0\in C,\ \theta_1\in C\}
 \geq1-2\alpha-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)
 \geq\frac34-2\alpha.
\]
This lower probability is positive because \(0<\alpha<1/4\).  On the displayed event, the real interval \(C\) contains both fixed numbers \(\theta_0\) and \(\theta_1\), so its length is at least \(\delta\).  Therefore
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_L}\mathbb E_P[\operatorname{length}(C)]
 \geq\mathbb E_{P_{0,n,\Delta}}[\operatorname{length}(C)]
 \geq\delta\left(\frac34-2\alpha\right)
 \geq\frac{c_L}{4}\left(\frac34-2\alpha\right)\rho_{n,\Delta}.
\]
The first inequality is the valid subclass-to-superclass transfer: the supremum over \(\mathcal P_L\) dominates its value at the explicit member \(P_{0,n,\Delta}\).  Since \(C\) was arbitrary, taking the infimum over \(\mathcal C_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\) and invoking \(\mathrm{def:honest\text{-}width}\) proves the lower bound with
\[
 c_{L,\alpha,\Delta_0}
 :=\frac{c_L}{4}\left(\frac34-2\alpha\right)>0.
\]
The admissible constant is independent of \(\Delta_0\), which is stronger than the allowed dependence.  By Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:total\text{-}honest\text{-}interval}\), the explicitly constructed interval is a closed bounded released-sample-measurable map with uniform coverage at least \(1-\alpha\); hence it belongs to \(\mathcal C_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\), and its expected length is at most \(C_{L,c_0,\alpha,\Delta_0}\rho_{n,\Delta}\).  Taking the infimum also on this upper side yields
\[
 \mathfrak W_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\asymp\rho_{n,\Delta},
\]
with constants uniform in \(n\) and \(\Delta\).
