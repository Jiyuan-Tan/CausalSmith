Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_L\).  The construction in \(\mathrm{def:total\text{-}honest\text{-}interval}\) specifies an interval for every possible count, including \(N_{t,n,\Delta}=0\), and its endpoints are measurable functions of the released counts and sums.  Its lower endpoint does not exceed its upper endpoint because their difference is the sum of two nonnegative sidewise widths and \(4LH_{n,\Delta}\).  Thus it is always a closed bounded released-data interval.

For coverage, condition on \(N_{t,n,\Delta}=k>0\).  By \(\mathrm{lem:conditional\text{-}local\text{-}binomial\text{-}law}\), the selected outcomes are independent, supported on \([0,1]\), and have common mean \(\nu_t\).  Put
\[
 r=r_{k,\alpha}=\sqrt{\frac{\log(4/\alpha)}{2k}}.
\]
If \(\nu_t<\ell_t\), then necessarily \(\overline Y_t-\nu_t>r\); if \(\nu_t>u_t\), then \(\nu_t-\overline Y_t>r\).  Apply \(\mathrm{lem:hoeffding\text{-}bounded\text{-}average}\) to the selected outcomes for the first event and to their complements for the second.  Whenever its displayed range condition on \(r\) is not met, the corresponding strict event is empty; otherwise the cited inequality gives
\[
 \mathbb P_P(\overline Y_t-\nu_t>r\mid N_t=k)\le e^{-2kr^2}=\frac\alpha4,
\]
and the same bound for \(\nu_t-\overline Y_t>r\).  Therefore
\[
 \mathbb P_P(\nu_t\notin[\ell_t,u_t]\mid N_t=k)\le\frac\alpha2.
\]
For \(k=0\), the interval is \([0,1]\) and contains \(\nu_t\) surely.  Averaging over the count proves sidewise failure probability at most \(\alpha/2\).

Let \(B=LH_{n,\Delta}\).  The bias conclusion of \(\mathrm{lem:conditional\text{-}local\text{-}binomial\text{-}law}\) gives \(|\nu_t-\theta_t|\le B\).  Hence, on the event that both side intervals contain their respective \(\nu_t\),
\[
 \theta_t\in[\ell_t-B,u_t+B],\qquad t=0,1,
\]
and subtraction gives
\[
 \tau_P(b)=\theta_1-\theta_0
 \in[\ell_1-u_0-2B,u_1-\ell_0+2B]
 =C_{n,\Delta,\alpha}.
\]
The union bound over the two sides proves coverage at least \(1-\alpha\), uniformly over \(P\).

For expected length, let \(w_t=u_t-\ell_t\).  Clipping can only reduce width, so
\[
 w_t\le\mathbf1\{N_t=0\}
 +2\sqrt{\frac{\log(4/\alpha)}2}\,
   \frac{\mathbf1\{N_t>0\}}{\sqrt{N_t}}.
\]
For \(N_t\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,q_t)\), direct summation and \(1/k\le2/(k+1)\) for \(k\ge1\) give
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\frac1{N_t+1}\right]
 =\frac{1-(1-q_t)^{n+1}}{(n+1)q_t}
 \le\frac1{nq_t},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E\!\left[\frac{\mathbf1\{N_t>0\}}{\sqrt{N_t}}\right]
 \le\sqrt{\frac2{nq_t}}.
\]
Also \(\mathbb P(N_t=0)=(1-q_t)^n\le\min\{1,(nq_t)^{-1}\}\le(nq_t)^{-1/2}\).  Combining these inequalities with \(q_t\ge c_0H_{n,\Delta}^2\) gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P[w_t]
 \le \frac{C_\alpha}{\sqrt{nq_t}}
 \le\frac{C_\alpha}{\sqrt{c_0}\sqrt n\,H_{n,\Delta}}
 \le\frac{C_\alpha}{\sqrt{c_0}}h_n.
\]
Finally,
\[
 \operatorname{length}(C_{n,\Delta,\alpha})=w_1+w_0+4LH_{n,\Delta},
\]
and \(H_{n,\Delta}<h_n+\Delta\le2\rho_{n,\Delta}\).  Taking the supremum over \(P\) proves the asserted expected-length bound with a constant depending only on \(L,c_0,\alpha\), and hence admissibly on \(\Delta_0\) as well.
