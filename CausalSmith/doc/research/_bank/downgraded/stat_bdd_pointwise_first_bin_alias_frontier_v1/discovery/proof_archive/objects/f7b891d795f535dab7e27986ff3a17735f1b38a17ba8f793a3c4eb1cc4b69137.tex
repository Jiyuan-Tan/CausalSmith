Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_L\) and \(t\in\{0,1\}\).  Suppose \(a,a'\in[0,1]\) each satisfies the displayed point-anchor envelope.  For any \(H\in(0,1/2]\), the local-mass member condition gives
\[
 \mathbb P_P(T=t,R<H)\geq c_0H^2>0.
\]
Each candidate envelope holds outside a \(P_X\)-null subset of the side-\(t\), radius-at-most-\(1/2\) region.  Removing the union of those two null sets from \(\{T=t,R<H\}\) leaves a nonempty set.  At any \(x\) in that set,
\[
 |a-a'|
 \leq|a-\mu_t(x)|+|\mu_t(x)-a'|
 \leq2L\lVert x-b\rVert_2
 <2LH.
\]
This holds for every \(H\in(0,1/2]\).  Taking \(H\downarrow0\) gives \(|a-a'|=0\), hence \(a=a'\).  Existence of the declared anchor is part of the class construction, so it is unique for each side.  Therefore \(\theta_t\), and hence \(\tau_P(b)=\theta_1-\theta_0\), is a well-defined functional of \(P\).
