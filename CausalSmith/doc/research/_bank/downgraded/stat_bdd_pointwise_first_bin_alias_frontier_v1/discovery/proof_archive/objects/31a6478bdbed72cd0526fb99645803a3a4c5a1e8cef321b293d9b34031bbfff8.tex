The definition of the frontier gives
\[
 \rho_{n,\Delta}=\max\{h_n,\Delta\}
 =\max\{n^{-1/4},\Delta\}.
\]
First suppose \(0<\Delta\leq n^{-1/4}\).  Then
\[
 \rho_{n,\Delta}=n^{-1/4}.
\]
Substitution into \(\mathrm{thm:minimax\text{-}frontier}\) gives, with constants uniform over the stated range,
\[
 \mathfrak R^{(1)}_{n,\Delta}\asymp n^{-1/4},
 \qquad
 \mathfrak R^{(2)}_{n,\Delta}
 \asymp (n^{-1/4})^2=n^{-1/2}.
\]
Substitution into \(\mathrm{thm:honest\text{-}length\text{-}lower}\), including the matching upper bound proved in that theorem, gives
\[
 \mathfrak W_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\asymp n^{-1/4}.
\]
This establishes the first regime.

Now let \((n,\Delta)\) vary along any sequence satisfying \(n^{-1/4}=o(\Delta)\).  By the definition of little-oh, \(n^{-1/4}/\Delta\to0\); hence there is an index after which \(n^{-1/4}<\Delta\).  At every such index,
\[
 \rho_{n,\Delta}=\Delta.
\]
Applying the same two established theorems therefore yields
\[
 \mathfrak R^{(1)}_{n,\Delta}\asymp\Delta,
 \qquad
 \mathfrak R^{(2)}_{n,\Delta}\asymp\Delta^2,
 \qquad
 \mathfrak W_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\asymp\Delta.
\]
Thus the sampling scale is active under fine quantization, whereas the released radial resolution is active when \(n^{-1/4}=o(\Delta)\).  The argument uses only the rounded-radius frontier theorems and does not replace the released annular experiment by an exact-coordinate experiment.
