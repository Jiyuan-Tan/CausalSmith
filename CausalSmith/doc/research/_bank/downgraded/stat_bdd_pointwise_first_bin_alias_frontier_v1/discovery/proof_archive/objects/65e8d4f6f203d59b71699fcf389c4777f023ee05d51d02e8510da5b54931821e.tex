Fix \(n\geq256\), \(0<\Delta\leq\Delta_0\), and \(P\in\mathcal P_L\), and write
\[
 h=h_n=n^{-1/4},\qquad H=H_{n,\Delta},\qquad B=LH.
\]
The aligned-bandwidth definition gives
\[
 h\leq H<h+\Delta\leq\frac12,
\]
using \(h\leq1/4\) and \(\Delta\leq\Delta_0\leq1/4\).  For \(t\in\{0,1\}\), lemma \(\mathrm{lem:conditional\text{-}local\text{-}binomial\text{-}law}\) gives
\[
 q_t:=\mathbb P_P(T=t,R<H)\geq c_0H^2>0,
 \qquad
 \nu_t:=\mathbb E_P[\mu_t(X)\mid T=t,R<H],
\]
\[
 |\nu_t-\theta_t|\leq LH=B,
 \qquad
 N_{t,n,\Delta}\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,q_t).
\]
It also states that, conditional on \(N_{t,n,\Delta}=k\geq1\), the selected outcomes are independent, supported on \([0,1]\), and have common mean \(\nu_t\), with sum \(S_{t,n,\Delta}\).  Rounded and latent localization agree exactly because
\[
 \{Q_\Delta(R)<H\}
 =\{\Delta\lfloor R/\Delta\rfloor<K_{n,\Delta}\Delta\}
 =\{R<K_{n,\Delta}\Delta\}
 =\{R<H\}.
\]

If \(N_{t,n,\Delta}=0\), the side interval in \(\mathrm{def:total\text{-}honest\text{-}interval}\) is \([0,1]\), which contains \(\nu_t\).  If the count is \(k>0\), apply \(\mathrm{lem:hoeffding\text{-}bounded\text{-}average}\), conditionally on that count, first to the selected outcomes and then to their complements \(1-Y_{t,j}^{\star}\), with
\[
 r_{k,\alpha}=\sqrt{\frac{\log(4/\alpha)}{2k}}.
\]
Because \(0<\alpha<1/4\), this radius is real and positive.  The two one-sided bounds and the union bound give
\[
 \mathbb P_P\!\left(\left|\frac{S_{t,n,\Delta}}k-\nu_t\right|>r_{k,\alpha}\,\middle|\,N_{t,n,\Delta}=k\right)
 \leq2e^{-2kr_{k,\alpha}^2}=\frac\alpha2.
\]
Since \(\nu_t\in[0,1]\), intersecting the untruncated interval with \([0,1]\) cannot remove \(\nu_t\).  Averaging over all count values, including the zero-count branch, yields
\[
 \mathbb P_P\{\nu_t\in[\ell_t,u_t]\}\geq1-\frac\alpha2.
\]
A union bound gives an event \(E\) of probability at least \(1-\alpha\) on which both side inclusions hold.  On \(E\),
\[
 \nu_1-\nu_0\in[\ell_1-u_0,u_1-\ell_0].
\]
The two point-anchor bias bounds imply
\[
 |\tau_P(b)-(\nu_1-\nu_0)|
 =| (\theta_1-\nu_1)-(\theta_0-\nu_0)|
 \leq2B.
\]
Therefore \(\tau_P(b)\in C_{n,\Delta,\alpha}\) on \(E\).  Since \(P\) was arbitrary, the coverage is uniform over \(\mathcal P_L\).

The counts and localized outcome sums are measurable functions of \(Z_\Delta^n\), and every operation in the endpoint formulas is Borel measurable.  For every sample, \(0\leq\ell_t\leq u_t\leq1\), and
\[
 (u_1-\ell_0+2B)-(\ell_1-u_0-2B)
 =(u_1-\ell_1)+(u_0-\ell_0)+4B\geq0.
\]
Thus the construction is an ordered, closed, bounded, \(\sigma(Z_\Delta^n)\)-measurable interval even if either local count is zero.  This proves totality.

For expected length, put \(a_\alpha=\sqrt{\log(4/\alpha)/2}\).  At a positive count \(k\), clipping can only shorten the side interval, so
\[
 u_t-\ell_t\leq\frac{2a_\alpha}{\sqrt k};
\]
at a zero count its length is one.  If \(N\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,q)\), then
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\frac1{N+1}\right]
 =\int_0^1(1-q+qx)^n\,dx
 =\frac{1-(1-q)^{n+1}}{(n+1)q}
 \leq\frac1{(n+1)q}.
\]
The inequality \(k^{-1}\leq2(k+1)^{-1}\) and Cauchy--Schwarz imply
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\frac{\mathbf1\{N>0\}}{\sqrt N}\right]
 \leq\sqrt{\mathbb E\!\left[\frac{\mathbf1\{N>0\}}N\right]}
 \leq\sqrt{\frac2{nq}}.
\]
Also \(\mathbb P(N=0)=(1-q)^n\leq e^{-nq}\leq(nq)^{-1/2}\), because \(x^{1/2}e^{-x}\leq1\) for \(x>0\).  Hence
\[
 \mathbb E_P[u_t-\ell_t]
 \leq\frac{1+2\sqrt2a_\alpha}{\sqrt{nq_t}}
 =\frac{1+2\sqrt{\log(4/\alpha)}}{\sqrt{nq_t}}
 \leq\frac{1+2\sqrt{\log(4/\alpha)}}{\sqrt{c_0nH^2}}.
\]
The interval length is exactly
\[
 \operatorname{length}(C_{n,\Delta,\alpha})
 =(u_1-\ell_1)+(u_0-\ell_0)+4LH.
\]
Finally,
\[
 H<h+\Delta\leq2\rho_{n,\Delta},
 \qquad
 \frac1{\sqrt nH}\leq h\leq\rho_{n,\Delta}.
\]
Summing the two side bounds gives the explicit uniform inequality
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_L}\mathbb E_P[\operatorname{length}(C_{n,\Delta,\alpha})]
 \leq
 \left\{
 \frac{2+4\sqrt{\log(4/\alpha)}}{\sqrt{c_0}}+8L
 \right\}\rho_{n,\Delta}.
\]
This proves the asserted expected-length bound.
