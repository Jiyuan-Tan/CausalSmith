Fix \(n\geq256\), \(0<\Delta\leq\Delta_0\), and \(C\in\mathcal C_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\).  Let \(P_0=P_{0,n,\Delta}\) and \(P_1=P_{1,n,\Delta}\) be the same-class pair in \(\mathrm{thm:single\text{-}joint\text{-}pair}\), and define
\[
 Q_j=\mathcal L_{P_j}(Z_\Delta^n),
 \qquad \vartheta_j=\tau_{P_j}(b),
 \qquad j\in\{0,1\}.
\]
That theorem, with \(\kappa=c_L/4>0\), gives
\[
 P_0,P_1\in\mathcal P_L,
 \qquad \operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\leq\frac14,
 \qquad |\vartheta_1-\vartheta_0|=\kappa(h_n+\Delta).
\]
Because \(C\) is measurable with respect to the released sample by \(\mathrm{def:honest\text{-}class}\), the events
\[
 A_j=\{\vartheta_j\in C\}
\]
are measurable under both \(Q_0\) and \(Q_1\).  Honesty and \(P_j\in\mathcal P_L\) imply
\[
 Q_j(A_j)=\mathbb P_{P_j}(\vartheta_j\in C)\geq1-\alpha,
 \qquad j\in\{0,1\}.
\]
The defining event inequality for total variation gives
\[
 Q_0(A_1)\geq Q_1(A_1)-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)
 \geq1-\alpha-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1).
\]
Consequently, the union bound under \(Q_0\) yields
\[
 Q_0(A_0\cap A_1)
 \geq1-Q_0(A_0^c)-Q_0(A_1^c)
 \geq1-2\alpha-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)
 \geq\frac34-2\alpha.
\]
The standing condition \(\alpha<1/4\) makes the last quantity strictly larger than \(1/4\).  On \(A_0\cap A_1\), the closed interval \(C\) contains both \(\vartheta_0\) and \(\vartheta_1\), and hence
\[
 \operatorname{length}(C)\geq|\vartheta_1-\vartheta_0|
 =\kappa(h_n+\Delta).
\]
Since interval length is a nonnegative released-sample measurable random variable,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_L}\mathbb E_P[\operatorname{length}(C)]
 &\geq\mathbb E_{P_0}[\operatorname{length}(C)]\\
 &=\mathbb E_{Q_0}[\operatorname{length}(C)]\\
 &\geq\kappa(h_n+\Delta)Q_0(A_0\cap A_1)\\
 &\geq\frac\kappa4(h_n+\Delta)
 \geq\frac{c_L}{16}\rho_{n,\Delta}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus the asserted lower bound holds, for example with \(c_{L,\alpha,\Delta_0}=c_L/16>0\).  Taking the infimum over \(C\in\mathcal C_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\) and using \(\mathrm{def:honest\text{-}width}\) gives
\[
 \mathfrak W_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\geq\frac{c_L}{16}\rho_{n,\Delta}.
\]

For the reverse inequality, \(\mathrm{thm:total\text{-}honest\text{-}interval}\) proves that the explicit, total interval \(C_{n,\Delta,\alpha}\) is released-sample measurable, has uniform coverage at least \(1-\alpha\), and has worst-case expected length at most \(C_{L,c_0,\alpha,\Delta_0}\rho_{n,\Delta}\).  Therefore \(C_{n,\Delta,\alpha}\in\mathcal C_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\), and the infimum in \(\mathrm{def:honest\text{-}width}\) satisfies
\[
 \mathfrak W_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)
 \leq C_{L,c_0,\alpha,\Delta_0}\rho_{n,\Delta}.
\]
The two displays prove \(\mathfrak W_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\asymp\rho_{n,\Delta}\) uniformly in the stated ranges.
