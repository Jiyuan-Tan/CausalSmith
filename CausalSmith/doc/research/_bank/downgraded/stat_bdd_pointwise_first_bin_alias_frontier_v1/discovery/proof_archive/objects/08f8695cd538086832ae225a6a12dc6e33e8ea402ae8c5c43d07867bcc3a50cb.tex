By definition,
\[
 h_n=n^{-1/4},
 \qquad
 \rho_{n,\Delta}=\max\{h_n,\Delta\}.
\]
If \(0<\Delta\leq n^{-1/4}=h_n\), then \(\rho_{n,\Delta}=h_n=n^{-1/4}\).  Substitution into \(\mathrm{thm:minimax\text{-}frontier}\) gives minimax absolute-error order \(n^{-1/4}\) and squared-risk order
\[
 (n^{-1/4})^2=n^{-1/2}.
\]
Substitution into \(\mathrm{thm:honest\text{-}length\text{-}lower}\), including its matching upper conclusion, gives honest expected-length order \(n^{-1/4}\).

Along a sequence satisfying \(n^{-1/4}=o(\Delta)\), the definition of little-oh gives \(n^{-1/4}/\Delta\to0\), so \(n^{-1/4}<\Delta\) eventually.  Consequently \(\rho_{n,\Delta}=\Delta\) eventually.  The same two theorems then give minimax absolute-error and honest expected-length order \(\Delta\), and minimax squared-risk order \(\Delta^2\).  These are conclusions for the bounded-outcome point-anchored rounded-radius experiment; no equivalence to an exact-coordinate experiment is used or asserted.
