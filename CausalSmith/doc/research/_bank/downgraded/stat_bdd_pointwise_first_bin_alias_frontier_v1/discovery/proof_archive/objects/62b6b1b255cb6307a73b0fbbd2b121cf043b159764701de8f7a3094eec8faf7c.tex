Fix \(n\geq256\), \(0<\Delta\leq\Delta_0\), and \(C\in\mathcal C_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\).  By \(\mathrm{thm:single\text{-}joint\text{-}pair}\), there are laws \(P_{0,n,\Delta},P_{1,n,\Delta}\in\mathcal P_L\).  Define their released-sample laws and target values by
\[
 Q_j=\mathcal L_{P_{j,n,\Delta}}(Z_\Delta^n),
 \qquad
 \vartheta_j=\tau_{P_{j,n,\Delta}}(b),
 \qquad j\in\{0,1\}.
\]
The same theorem, whose product-law conclusion uses \(\mathrm{ass:iid\text{-}sampling}\) and the released experiment \(\mathrm{def:rounded\text{-}experiment}\), gives
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\leq\frac14,
 \qquad
 \delta:=|\vartheta_1-\vartheta_0|
 =\kappa(h_n+\Delta),
 \qquad
 \kappa=\frac{c_L}{4}>0.
\]
By the definition \(\rho_{n,\Delta}=\max\{h_n,\Delta\}\),
\[
 h_n+\Delta\geq\rho_{n,\Delta}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \delta\geq\kappa\rho_{n,\Delta}.
\]

Let \(A_j=\{\vartheta_j\in C\}\).  Membership of \(C\) in the honest class defined by \(\mathrm{def:honest\text{-}class}\), together with \(P_{j,n,\Delta}\in\mathcal P_L\), yields
\[
 Q_j(A_j)=\mathbb P_{P_{j,n,\Delta}}(\vartheta_j\in C)\geq1-\alpha,
 \qquad j\in\{0,1\}.
\]
Because \(C\) is measurable with respect to \(\sigma(Z_\Delta^n)\), \(A_1\) is a measurable event in the released experiment.  The defining variational inequality for total variation therefore implies
\[
 Q_0(A_1)
 \geq Q_1(A_1)-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)
 \geq1-\alpha-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1).
\]
Applying \(Q_0(A_0\cap A_1)\geq Q_0(A_0)+Q_0(A_1)-1\) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Q_0(A_0\cap A_1)
 &\geq1-2\alpha-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\\
 &\geq\frac34-2\alpha>0,
 \end{aligned}
\]
where the last strict inequality uses the declared restriction \(\alpha\in(0,1/4)\).  On \(A_0\cap A_1\), the closed real interval \(C\) contains both \(\vartheta_0\) and \(\vartheta_1\), so \(\operatorname{length}(C)\geq\delta\).  Consequently,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_L}\mathbb E_P[\operatorname{length}(C)]
 &\geq \mathbb E_{P_{0,n,\Delta}}[\operatorname{length}(C)]\\
 &\geq \delta Q_0(A_0\cap A_1)\\
 &\geq \frac{c_L}{4}\left(\frac34-2\alpha\right)\rho_{n,\Delta}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The first inequality uses \(P_{0,n,\Delta}\in\mathcal P_L\); all remaining inequalities were established above.  Hence the asserted lower bound holds with
\[
 c_{L,\alpha,\Delta_0}
 =\frac{c_L}{4}\left(\frac34-2\alpha\right)>0.
\]
This constant is uniform in \(n\) and \(\Delta\); its permitted dependence on \(\Delta_0\) is in fact unnecessary.

It remains to justify the concluding two-sided order for \(\mathfrak W_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\).  Under \(\mathrm{def:total\text{-}honest\text{-}interval}\), every endpoint of \(C_{n,\Delta,\alpha}\) is a deterministic function of the released counts \((N_{t,n,\Delta},S_{t,n,\Delta})_{t=0,1}\); these counts are measurable functions of \(Z_\Delta^n\), and the zero-count branch is explicitly defined.  Thus \(C_{n,\Delta,\alpha}\) is a closed bounded \(\sigma(Z_\Delta^n)\)-measurable interval for every released sample.  By \(\mathrm{thm:total\text{-}honest\text{-}interval}\), it also satisfies
\[
 \inf_{P\in\mathcal P_L}
 \mathbb P_P\{\tau_P(b)\in C_{n,\Delta,\alpha}\}
 \geq1-\alpha
\]
and
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_L}
 \mathbb E_P[\operatorname{length}(C_{n,\Delta,\alpha})]
 \leq C_{L,c_0,\alpha,\Delta_0}\rho_{n,\Delta}.
\]
Therefore \(C_{n,\Delta,\alpha}\in\mathcal C_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\) by \(\mathrm{def:honest\text{-}class}\), and \(\mathrm{def:honest\text{-}width}\) yields
\[
 \mathfrak W_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)
 \leq C_{L,c_0,\alpha,\Delta_0}\rho_{n,\Delta}.
\]
Taking the infimum over \(C\in\mathcal C_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\) in the already proved lower inequality and combining it with this explicit upper witness proves
\[
 \mathfrak W_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\asymp\rho_{n,\Delta}
\]
uniformly over every \(n\geq256\) and \(0<\Delta\leq\Delta_0\).
