By the definitions of \(h_n\) and \(\rho_{n,\Delta}\),
\[
 h_n=n^{-1/4},
 \qquad
 \rho_{n,\Delta}=\max\{n^{-1/4},\Delta\}.
\]
First suppose \(0<\Delta\leq n^{-1/4}\).  Then \(\rho_{n,\Delta}=n^{-1/4}\).  Substitution into \(\mathrm{thm:minimax\text{-}frontier}\) gives, with constants uniform over the permitted \(n\) and \(\Delta\),
\[
 \mathfrak R^{(1)}_{n,\Delta}\asymp n^{-1/4},
 \qquad
 \mathfrak R^{(2)}_{n,\Delta}\asymp
 \bigl(n^{-1/4}\bigr)^2=n^{-1/2}.
\]
Substitution into \(\mathrm{thm:honest\text{-}length\text{-}lower}\), including the matching upper bound proved there, gives
\[
 \mathfrak W_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\asymp n^{-1/4}.
\]

Next consider any sequence in the stated parameter space for which \(n^{-1/4}=o(\Delta)\).  By the definition of little-oh, \(n^{-1/4}/\Delta\to0\); hence, eventually, \(n^{-1/4}<\Delta\), and therefore \(\rho_{n,\Delta}=\Delta\).  The same two theorem substitutions yield, eventually along that sequence,
\[
 \mathfrak R^{(1)}_{n,\Delta}\asymp\Delta,
 \qquad
 \mathfrak R^{(2)}_{n,\Delta}\asymp\Delta^2,
 \qquad
 \mathfrak W_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\asymp\Delta.
\]
Thus the first regime is governed by the quadratic-local-mass sampling scale and the second by radial coarsening; no exact-coordinate experiment is invoked.
