Fix \(n\geq256\), \(0<\Delta\leq\Delta_0\), and an arbitrary \(C\in\mathcal C_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\).  Let \((P_0,P_1)\) be the pair furnished by \(\mathrm{thm:single\text{-}joint\text{-}pair\text{-}resolution}\), let
\[
 Q_j=\mathcal L_{P_j}(Z_\Delta^n),
 \qquad
 \vartheta_j=\tau_{P_j}(b),
 \qquad
 \delta=|\vartheta_1-\vartheta_0|,
 \quad j\in\{0,1\}.
\]
That theorem gives \(P_0,P_1\in\mathcal P_L\),
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\leq\frac14,
 \qquad
 \delta=\frac{c_L}{4}(h_n+\Delta).
\]
By the definition of \(\mathcal C_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\), the interval \(C\) is \(\sigma(Z_\Delta^n)\)-measurable and is honest at both members of the pair.  Hence, for \(A_j=\{\vartheta_j\in C\}\),
\[
 Q_j(A_j)\geq1-\alpha,
 \qquad j\in\{0,1\}.
\]
The defining variational inequality for total variation, applied to the measurable event \(A_1\), yields
\[
 Q_0(A_1)\geq Q_1(A_1)-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)
 \geq1-\alpha-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1).
\]
The union bound under \(Q_0\) therefore gives
\[
 Q_0(A_0\cap A_1)
 \geq1-Q_0(A_0^c)-Q_0(A_1^c)
 \geq1-2\alpha-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)
 \geq\frac14,
\]
where the last inequality uses \(\alpha<1/4\) and \(\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\leq1/4\).  On \(A_0\cap A_1\), the closed interval \(C\) contains both \(\vartheta_0\) and \(\vartheta_1\), so \(\operatorname{length}(C)\geq\delta\).  Positivity of interval length now implies
\[
 \mathbb E_{P_0}[\operatorname{length}(C)]
 \geq\delta Q_0(A_0\cap A_1)
 \geq\frac{c_L}{16}(h_n+\Delta)
 \geq\frac{c_L}{16}\rho_{n,\Delta},
\]
because \(h_n+\Delta\geq\max\{h_n,\Delta\}=\rho_{n,\Delta}\).  Since \(P_0\in\mathcal P_L\), the supremum over \(P\in\mathcal P_L\) is no smaller than this expectation.  The argument holds for every \(C\in\mathcal C_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\), so taking the infimum in \(\mathrm{def:honest\text{-}width}\) proves
\[
 \mathfrak W_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)
 \geq \frac{c_L}{16}\rho_{n,\Delta}.
\]
Thus one may take \(c_{L,\alpha,\Delta_0}=c_L/16>0\).

For the matching upper bound, \(\mathrm{thm:total\text{-}honest\text{-}interval}\) states that the released-sample interval \(C_{n,\Delta,\alpha}\) is defined on every sample, is honest uniformly over \(\mathcal P_L\), and has worst-case expected length at most \(C_{L,c_0,\alpha,\Delta_0}\rho_{n,\Delta}\).  Its construction is a deterministic function of the observed counts and successes and produces a closed bounded interval, so, by \(\mathrm{def:honest\text{-}class}\), it belongs to \(\mathcal C_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\).  Using it as a candidate in the infimum defining \(\mathfrak W_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\) yields
\[
 \mathfrak W_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)
 \leq C_{L,c_0,\alpha,\Delta_0}\rho_{n,\Delta}.
\]
Together with the lower bound, this proves \(\mathfrak W_{n,\Delta}(\alpha)\asymp\rho_{n,\Delta}\), uniformly over the stated \(n\) and \(\Delta\) ranges.
