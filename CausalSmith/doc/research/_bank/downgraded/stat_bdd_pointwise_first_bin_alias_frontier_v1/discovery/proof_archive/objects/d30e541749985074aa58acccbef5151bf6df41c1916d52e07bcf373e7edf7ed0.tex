Put \(h=h_n\), \(H=H_{n,\Delta}\), and \(\rho=\rho_{n,\Delta}\).  From \(H=\Delta\lceil h/\Delta\rceil\), \(n\geq256\), and \(\Delta\leq\Delta_0\leq1/4\),
\[
 h\leq H<h+\Delta\leq2\rho,
 \qquad 0<H\leq\frac12.
\]
Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_L\) and \(t\in\{0,1\}\).  Use \(A_t,q_t,\nu_t,N_t,S_t\) from \(\mathrm{lem:conditional\text{-}local\text{-}binomial\text{-}law}\).  That lemma and the definition \(\mu_t(b)=\theta_t\) give
\[
 q_t\geq c_0H^2,
 \qquad |\nu_t-\theta_t|\leq LH,
 \qquad N_t\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,q_t),
 \qquad S_t\mid N_t=k\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(k,\nu_t).
\]
In particular, \(0\leq\nu_t\leq1\).

For \(N\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,q)\) with \(q>0\), integrating its probability generating function gives
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\frac1{N+1}\right]
 =\int_0^1(1-q+qx)^n\,dx
 =\frac{1-(1-q)^{n+1}}{(n+1)q}
 \leq\frac1{(n+1)q}.
\]
Since \(k^{-1}\leq2(k+1)^{-1}\) for \(k\geq1\), and then by Cauchy--Schwarz,
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\frac{\mathbf1\{N>0\}}N\right]\leq\frac2{nq},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E\!\left[\frac{\mathbf1\{N>0\}}{\sqrt N}\right]
 \leq\sqrt{\frac2{nq}}.
\]
Also \((1-q)^n\leq e^{-nq}\).  The elementary inequalities \(x^{1/2}e^{-x}\leq1\) and \(xe^{-x}\leq1\) for \(x>0\) imply
\[
 \mathbb P(N=0)\leq(nq)^{-1/2},
 \qquad
 \mathbb P(N=0)\leq(nq)^{-1}.
\]

On \(N_t=k>0\), clipping does nothing because \(0\leq S_t/k\leq1\).  The ratio has conditional mean \(\nu_t\) and variance \(\nu_t(1-\nu_t)/k\leq1/(4k)\).  On \(N_t=0\), the fixed fallback obeys
\[
 |1/2-\nu_t|\leq\frac12,
 \qquad (1/2-\nu_t)^2\leq\frac14,
\]
which uses only the full Bernoulli range \([0,1]\).  Conditional Cauchy--Schwarz and the inverse-count bounds therefore give
\[
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat m_{t,n,\Delta}-\nu_t|
 \leq\frac12\sqrt{\frac2{nq_t}}
      +\frac1{2\sqrt{nq_t}}
 =\left(\frac1{\sqrt2}+\frac12\right)\frac1{\sqrt{nq_t}},
\]
and
\[
 \mathbb E_P[(\widehat m_{t,n,\Delta}-\nu_t)^2]
 \leq\frac14\frac2{nq_t}+\frac14\frac1{nq_t}
 =\frac3{4nq_t}.
\]
Adding the point-anchor bias and using \((a+b)^2\leq2a^2+2b^2\) yield
\[
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat m_{t,n,\Delta}-\theta_t|
 \leq LH+\left(\frac1{\sqrt2}+\frac12\right)
              \frac1{\sqrt{c_0nH^2}},
\]
and
\[
 \mathbb E_P[(\widehat m_{t,n,\Delta}-\theta_t)^2]
 \leq2L^2H^2+\frac3{2c_0nH^2}.
\]
Since \(H\geq h=n^{-1/4}\),
\[
 \frac1{\sqrt nH}\leq h\leq\rho,
 \qquad
 \frac1{nH^2}\leq h^2\leq\rho^2.
\]
The identity \(\tau_P(b)=\theta_1-\theta_0\), the triangle inequality, and \((x-y)^2\leq2x^2+2y^2\) now imply constants \(C_1,C_2<\infty\), depending only on \(L\) and \(c_0\), such that
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_L}
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat\tau_{n,\Delta}-\tau_P(b)|\leq C_1\rho,
 \qquad
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_L}
 \mathbb E_P[(\widehat\tau_{n,\Delta}-\tau_P(b))^2]\leq C_2\rho^2.
\]
The counts and successes are measurable functions of \(Z_\Delta^n\), and the zero-count branch is fixed, so \(\widehat\tau_{n,\Delta}\) is one law-independent member of \(\mathcal T_{n,\Delta}\).  Taking the infima in \(\mathrm{def:minimax\text{-}risks}\) proves both upper bounds.

For the converse, use the pair in \(\mathrm{thm:single\text{-}joint\text{-}pair}\).  Its two laws belong to the embedded uniform-half-disc subclass and hence to the unknown-design point-anchored superclass.  Let \(Q_0,Q_1\) be their released product laws, let \(\vartheta_0,\vartheta_1\) be their targets, and set \(\delta=|\vartheta_1-\vartheta_0|\).  The theorem gives
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\leq\frac14,
 \qquad
 \delta=\kappa(h+\Delta)\geq\kappa\rho,
 \qquad \kappa=\frac{c_L}{4}>0.
\]
For any released-data estimator \(a\), take densities \(q_0,q_1\) with respect to \(Q_0+Q_1\).  Pointwise,
\[
 |a-\vartheta_0|+|a-\vartheta_1|\geq\delta,
 \qquad
 (a-\vartheta_0)^2+(a-\vartheta_1)^2\geq\frac{\delta^2}{2},
\]
and \(\int\min(q_0,q_1)=1-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\).  Multiplying by the minimum density, integrating, and bounding a maximum below by half the sum give
\[
 \max_{j\in\{0,1\}}\mathbb E_{Q_j}|a-\vartheta_j|
 \geq\frac\delta2\{1-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\}
 \geq\frac{3\kappa}{8}\rho,
\]
and
\[
 \max_{j\in\{0,1\}}\mathbb E_{Q_j}(a-\vartheta_j)^2
 \geq\frac{\delta^2}{4}\{1-\operatorname{TV}(Q_0,Q_1)\}
 \geq\frac{3\kappa^2}{16}\rho^2.
\]
The supremum over \(\mathcal P_L\) dominates the maximum over this pair.  Taking the infimum over estimators proves the lower bounds.  Choose \(c\) as the smaller of the two displayed lower constants and choose \(C\) larger than \(c\), \(C_1\), and \(C_2\).  This proves the simultaneous assertion with the allowed dependence on \(L,c_0,\Delta_0\).
