\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} Announced staggered policies can make one future-treated cohort serve as an anticipatory comparison in several Callaway--Sant'Anna risk sets. Each implementation cohort is attached to one known canonical announcement-and-implementation path, and the reference path has neither event. Over the proposal-specific canonical-path class, PT--All makes the reference-path conditional means additive, preregistered edgewise level boxes bound announcement-to-implementation responses, and bounded support constrains every reference-path cohort--time mean. The admissible anticipation vectors are exactly the primitive box intersected with the fundamental-cycle equations. The resulting theory gives the minimal-face dimension, the bridge frontier for variation independence, negative-cycle endpoint certificates, and min-cost-circulation endpoints for every signed ATT aggregate. A conditional endpoint-mixture construction now proves that every bounded cycle-compatible mean vector extends to one common law of all path potential outcomes, so the affine ATT region and its circulation endpoints are causally sharp rather than conditionally sharp. Simultaneous bounded-mean bands yield finite-sample honest outer confidence sets by one joint optimization. The only transfer to the Callaway--Sant'Anna published setup is the explicit zero-anticipation rectangular-anchor reduction; no inclusion relation with Fenaroli's published class is asserted.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Callaway--Sant'Anna not-yet-treated comparisons can reuse future-treated cohorts, but their identification theorem avoids contaminated comparison cells under its limited-anticipation design. Fenaroli's Section 4.2 uses never-treated controls and notes the additional comparison-group anticipation problem. Neither published class supplies joint cycle restrictions for reused contaminated cells or a common-law completion proving sharpness of all overlapping targets at once.

\paragraph{Niche.} A deterministic canonical policy path for each cohort makes anticipation a well-typed path contrast. In the proposal-specific class, PT--All turns the no-announcement mean surface into an incidence potential, edgewise level intervals encode responses between the known announcement and implementation dates, and bounded outcome support becomes a complete bipartite interval-tension restriction. Cycles govern compatibility, bridges govern primitive-coordinate independence, and ATT weights become node demands.

\paragraph{Fill.} The project proves the cycle fiber and its face-correct dimension, necessary and sufficient bridge and endpoint frontiers, a bounded-outcome causal completion theorem, exact affine identified regions, min-cost-circulation endpoint formulas, and finite-sample outer coverage. The completion is constructive: one endpoint-mixture law preserves the entire observed panel and realizes every target simultaneously. The result is exact for the proposal-specific canonical-path, PT--All, edgewise-level-box class. It does not claim to settle the larger or differently restricted classes of Callaway--Sant'Anna or Fenaroli.

  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} For \(N_h>0\), \(\rho_h=(\overline y-\underline y)\sqrt{\log(2(|\mathcal E|+|\mathcal J|)/\eta)/(2N_h)}\); for \(N_h=0\), \(\rho_h=\overline y-\underline y\).
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathcal R\_\allowbreak{}\{1-\allowbreak{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}eta\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{random rectangle}; \texttt{Simultaneous bounded-\allowbreak{}mean confidence region.}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\mathcal R_{1-\eta}\) is the Cartesian product of \([\widehat\mu_e-\rho_{h(e)},\widehat\mu_e+\rho_{h(e)}]\cap[\underline y,\overline y]\) and \([\widehat\nu_j-\rho_{g_j},\widehat\nu_j+\rho_{g_j}]\cap[\underline y,\overline y]\), using \([\underline y,\overline y]\) when the relevant cohort count is zero.
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}Theta\textasciicircum{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathrm\{out\}\allowbreak{}\}\allowbreak{}\_\allowbreak{}\{1-\allowbreak{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}eta\}\allowbreak{}(w)} --- \texttt{random closed subset of the real line}; \texttt{Jointly optimized finite-\allowbreak{}sample outer confidence set.}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat\Theta^{\mathrm{out}}_{1-\eta}(w)=\bigcup_{(\mu',\nu')\in\mathcal R_{1-\eta}}\{\sum_jw_j\nu'_j-s(w)^{\mathsf T}\beta:\beta\in\mathcal Q_Y(\mu')\}\).
\end{itemize}

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:cohort-overlap]\label{ass:cohort-overlap}
\(P_{\mathrm{obs}}(G=h)>0\) for every cohort vertex incident to an edge in \(\mathcal E\) or used by a target in \(\mathcal J\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Staggered-adoption cohort overlap, \cite{CallawaySantAnna2021}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:announced-consistency]\label{ass:announced-consistency}
\(Y_t=Y_t(p^G)\) almost surely for every \(t\in\mathcal T\), where units with \(G=r\) realize the complete canonical path \(p^r\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Intervention-indexed consistency for an announced policy, \cite{PiccininniTchetgenStensrud2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:pt-all]\label{ass:pt-all}
\(E_P[Y_t(p^\infty)\mid G=h]=\alpha_h+\lambda_t\) for every \((h,t)\in\mathcal H\times\mathcal T\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This is a proposal-specific all-cohort additive mean restriction, not a maintained condition attributed to Callaway--Sant'Anna. Under the canonical path with neither announcement nor implementation, scheduled cohorts may differ in mean outcome levels but share calendar-time mean movements; this interpretation can be assessed on cells preceding the known dates \(\mathsf a(h)\). A single collection of cohort and time effects is substantively needed when the same future-treated comparison cell is reused in several risk sets, because otherwise those uses need not correspond to one coherent no-announcement mean surface.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:bounded-anticipation]\label{ass:bounded-anticipation}
\(a_e\in[\ell_e,u_e]\) for every \(e\in\mathcal E\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This is a proposal-specific edgewise sensitivity restriction, distinct from Fenaroli's cohort-level bounds on anticipation-related increments. The application protocol records the public decision date \(\mathsf a(h)\) and implementation date \(h\) for every canonical cohort path, declares \(t<\mathsf a(h)\) clean, and preregisters a separate interval \([\ell_e,u_e]\) for each announcement-to-implementation cell \(\mathsf a(h)\le t<h\). Each interval is calibrated on the outcome scale from subject-matter limits on the largest plausible response to that announced path, supplemented where available by placebo shifts from comparable preannouncement cohorts; asymmetric responses are represented by asymmetric endpoints.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:bounded-potential-outcomes]\label{ass:bounded-potential-outcomes}
\(Y_t(p^r)\in[\underline y,\overline y]\) almost surely for every \((t,r)\in\mathcal T\times\mathcal R\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Known bounded outcome support, \cite{Manski2003}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:iid-sampling]\label{ass:iid-sampling}
\(O_1,\ldots,O_n\) are independent draws from \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Independent superpopulation sampling, \cite{CallawaySantAnna2021}).
\end{assumption}

\section{Main results}

\begin{theorem}[thm:cycle-fiber]\label{thm:cycle-fiber}
For every \(a\in\prod_{e\in\mathcal E}[\ell_e,u_e]\), there exists a vertex potential \(\beta\in\mathbb R^{\mathcal V}\) satisfying \(\mu=B^{\mathsf T}\beta+a\) if and only if \(Ca=C\mu\). Consequently, the set of all anticipation vectors admitting the proposal-specific all-cohort additive decomposition is exactly \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{cyc}}(\mu)\). If this fiber is nonempty, its affine dimension is \[\dim\operatorname{aff}\mathcal A_{\mathrm{cyc}}(\mu)=|\mathcal I(\mu)|-\operatorname{rank}(C_{\mathcal I(\mu)}).\] In the relative-interior case \(\mathcal I(\mu)=\mathcal W\), this reduces to \(|\mathcal W|-\operatorname{rank}(C_{\mathcal W})\). The face-correct formula remains valid for disconnected graphs, zero-width edges, and fibers lying on proper box faces.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(\mathcal P=\mathcal A_{\mathrm{cyc}}(\mu)\).  The proof uses incidence--cycle orthogonality and then a relative-interior tangent-space argument.  Fix \(a\in\prod_{e\in\mathcal E}[\ell_e,u_e]\).  By the defining property of the fundamental-cycle matrix,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \exists\beta\in\mathbb R^{\mathcal V}:\ \mu=B^{\mathsf T}\beta+a
 &\Longleftrightarrow \mu-a\in\operatorname{im}(B^{\mathsf T})\\
 &\Longleftrightarrow \mu-a\in\ker C\\
 &\Longleftrightarrow Ca=C\mu .
 \end{aligned}
\]
The first equivalence is algebra, the second is the identity \(\ker C=\operatorname{im}(B^{\mathsf T})\) in the definition of \(C\), and the last is linearity.  Assumption~\ref{ass:bounded-anticipation} supplies exactly the displayed box restriction.  This proves both the equivalence and, by Definition~\ref{def:cycle-fiber}, the asserted description of the anticipation fiber.  Notice that no connectedness or rank condition has been imposed; the \(c\) component gauges are already contained in \(\ker(B^{\mathsf T})\).

Suppose next that \(\mathcal P\ne\varnothing\), set \(I=\mathcal I(\mu)\), and let
\[
 D=\operatorname{span}\{a-a':a,a'\in\mathcal P\}
\]
be the direction space of \(\operatorname{aff}\mathcal P\).  If \(e\notin I\), the definition of \(I\) says that the \(e\)-th coordinate is constant on \(\mathcal P\).  Also, every difference of two points of \(\mathcal P\) is annihilated by \(C\).  Consequently
\[
 D\subseteq K_I:=\{d\in\mathbb R^{\mathcal E}:d_e=0\text{ for }e\notin I,\ Cd=0\}.
\]
For the reverse inclusion, choose \(a^\circ\in\operatorname{relint}\mathcal P\).  Such a point exists without an extra assumption: choose finitely many points of \(\mathcal P\) whose affine hull is \(\operatorname{aff}\mathcal P\); the average of a maximal affinely independent subcollection lies in the relative interior of their full-dimensional simplex, which is contained in \(\mathcal P\).  For every \(e\in I\),
\[
 \ell_e<a^\circ_e<u_e. \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, if, for example, \(a^\circ_e=\ell_e\), choose \(a'\in\mathcal P\) with \(a'_e>a^\circ_e\), which exists because coordinate \(e\) varies.  Relative interiority permits \(a^\circ+\varepsilon(a^\circ-a')\in\mathcal P\) for all sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\), but its \(e\)-th coordinate is below \(\ell_e\), a contradiction.  The upper inequality is identical.  If \(d\in K_I\), finiteness of \(I\) and (1) give a sufficiently small \(\delta>0\) for which \(a^\circ+\delta d\) remains in the box.  Since \(Cd=0\), it also satisfies \(C(a^\circ+\delta d)=C\mu\), and hence belongs to \(\mathcal P\).  Thus \(\delta d\in D\), proving \(K_I\subseteq D\).  Therefore
\[
 \dim\operatorname{aff}\mathcal P
 =\dim K_I
 =\dim\ker(C_I)
 =|I|-\operatorname{rank}(C_I),
\]
where the last equality is rank--nullity.  Taking \(I=\mathcal W\) yields the relative-interior formula.  Coordinates of zero width are outside \(I\), a proper active box face merely makes \(I\) smaller, and the argument is componentwise, so the same formula covers all boundary, zero-width, and disconnected cases stated in the theorem.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem turns reused not-yet-treated cells into an explicit, basis-invariant joint restriction rather than an assumed Cartesian box. \textit{Gap.} Fenaroli2026, Section 4.2 fixes never-treated controls and observes that using not-yet-treated comparisons requires accounting for anticipation in the comparison group; it does not characterize the compatibility restrictions created when such cells are reused across risk sets. JochmansWeidner2019, Section 2 supplies incidence and component-gauge algebra but no anticipation identified set. \textit{Consumer.} A Callaway--Sant'Anna risk-set audit can report the exact number and location of anticipation degrees of freedom before any ATT aggregation is computed.

\begin{theorem}[thm:variation-independence]\label{thm:variation-independence}
The primitive anticipation coordinates are variation independent, \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{cyc}}(\mu)=\prod_{e\in\mathcal E}[\ell_e,u_e]\), if and only if \(C_{\mathcal W}=0\) and \(C_{\mathcal F}\overline a_{\mathcal F}=C\mu\). Equivalently, fixed coordinates satisfy every fixed-cycle equation and every positive-width edge in \(\mathcal W\) is a bridge of \((\mathcal V,\mathcal E)\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write \(R=\prod_{e\in\mathcal E}[\ell_e,u_e]\).  The technique is a coordinatewise box-perturbation argument followed by the graphic bridge criterion.  Every \(a\in R\) has the same fixed subvector \(a_{\mathcal F}=\overline a_{\mathcal F}\).  If \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{cyc}}(\mu)=R\), then \(Ca=C\mu\) for every \(a\in R\).  Fix an interior value for every coordinate in \(\mathcal W\).  For any \(e\in\mathcal W\), perturb only coordinate \(e\) by a nonzero sufficiently small amount \(\delta\).  Subtraction of the two cycle equations gives
\[
 \delta C_{\{e\}}=0,
\]
so the \(e\)-th column of \(C\) is zero.  This holds for every \(e\in\mathcal W\), hence \(C_{\mathcal W}=0\).  Substitution of any point of \(R\) then gives
\[
 C_{\mathcal F}\overline a_{\mathcal F}=C\mu. \tag{2}
\]
Conversely, if \(C_{\mathcal W}=0\) and (2) holds, every \(a\in R\) obeys
\[
 Ca=C_{\mathcal W}a_{\mathcal W}+C_{\mathcal F}\overline a_{\mathcal F}=C\mu,
\]
and Definition~\ref{def:cycle-fiber} gives \(R\subseteq\mathcal A_{\mathrm{cyc}}(\mu)\).  The reverse inclusion is part of that definition, proving equality.  This establishes the variation-independence criterion in the product-range sense of \cite{Dawid2001}; the argument itself proves the specialized claim rather than importing it.

It remains to translate \(C_{\mathcal W}=0\) into graph language.  The rows of \(C\) are a basis of the signed cycle space.  A column \(C_{\{e\}}\) is zero exactly when the coordinate of edge \(e\) vanishes in every cycle-space vector, equivalently when no graph cycle contains \(e\).  In a finite undirected graph, deleting an edge disconnects its two endpoints if and only if that edge belongs to no cycle: if another endpoint-to-endpoint path remains, adjoining \(e\) makes a cycle, while a cycle containing \(e\) supplies such a path after deletion.  Thus \(C_{\{e\}}=0\) exactly when \(e\) is a bridge.  Applying this to all \(e\in\mathcal W\), together with (2), proves the final equivalence, including disconnected graphs and zero-width coordinates.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The bridge frontier gives a design-level if-and-only-if answer to when an uncoupled independent-box analysis is exact. \textit{Gap.} Dawid2001 defines variation independence as a product-range property. No located DiD result links that property to bridges in a cohort--time anticipation graph. \textit{Consumer.} Researchers choosing not-yet-treated controls can determine from the risk-set graph alone whether cellwise sensitivity analysis is jointly coherent.

\begin{theorem}[thm:endpoint-exactness]\label{thm:endpoint-exactness}
For any \(e_0\in\mathcal W\), the nominal lower endpoint \(a_{e_0}=\ell_{e_0}\) is attained in \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{cyc}}(\mu)\) if and only if the interval-tension system obtained by replacing \([\ell_{e_0},u_{e_0}]\) with \([\ell_{e_0},\ell_{e_0}]\) contains a vertex potential. This holds if and only if its two-arc difference-constraint digraph has no negative directed cycle. The analogous equivalence with \([u_{e_0},u_{e_0}]\) characterizes upper-endpoint attainment; applying the same test to the augmented edge multiset \(\mathcal E\sqcup\mathcal K\) characterizes endpoint exactness in \(\mathcal A_Y(\mu)\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The proof uses an interval-tension reduction and proves the finite difference-constraint certificate directly.  First replace the interval at \(e_0\) by \([\ell_{e_0},\ell_{e_0}]\), leaving all other intervals unchanged; denote the resulting endpoints by \(\ell^*\) and \(u^*\).  By Theorem~\ref{thm:cycle-fiber}, a vector in the original fiber attains \(a_{e_0}=\ell_{e_0}\) if and only if there is a \(\beta\in\mathbb R^{\mathcal V}\) such that
\[
 a=\mu-B^{\mathsf T}\beta,
 \qquad
 \ell^*_e\le \mu_e-(B^{\mathsf T}\beta)_e\le u^*_e
 \quad(e\in\mathcal E).
\]
Rearranging each displayed pair gives the interval-tension system
\[
 \mu_e-u^*_e\le \beta_{h(e)}-\beta_{t(e)}
 \le \mu_e-\ell^*_e. \tag{3}
\]
Thus endpoint attainment and existence of the stated vertex potential are equivalent.  Replacing the interval by \([u_{e_0},u_{e_0}]\) proves the analogous algebraic equivalence for the upper endpoint.

For completeness, consider any finite oriented multigraph with lower and upper tensions \(L_e\le U_e\).  Encode
\[
 L_e\le\beta_{h(e)}-\beta_{t(e)}\le U_e
\]
by an arc \(t(e)\to h(e)\) of weight \(U_e\) and an arc \(h(e)\to t(e)\) of weight \(-L_e\).  If \(\beta\) is feasible, each arc \(v\to v'\) of weight \(q\) satisfies \(\beta_{v'}-\beta_v\le q\); summing around a directed cycle proves that its total weight is nonnegative.  Conversely, suppose there is no negative directed cycle.  Add a new source with a zero-weight arc to every vertex, and let \(\delta_v\) be the minimum weight of a source-to-\(v\) path.  A minimum exists because any repeated-vertex portion is a nonnegative cycle and can be deleted, leaving one of finitely many simple paths.  For every arc \(v\to v'\) of weight \(q\), appending that arc to a shortest path gives
\[
 \delta_{v'}\le\delta_v+q.
\]
Taking \(\beta_v=\delta_v\) satisfies all encoded inequalities.  This proves, in both directions, the negative-cycle certificate used in (3).

Finally, on the augmented multigraph \(\mathcal E\sqcup\mathcal K\), the observed-edge tensions are \([\mu_e-u_e,\mu_e-\ell_e]\), while the baseline edges have tensions \([\underline y,\overline y]\).  These are exactly the inequalities defining \(\mathcal Q_Y(\mu)\) in Definition~\ref{def:bounded-completion-polyhedron}; their two-arc graph is \(\mathcal D(d^-,d^+)\).  Pinning either endpoint on \(e_0\) and applying the certificate just proved therefore characterizes endpoint exactness in \(\mathcal A_Y(\mu)\).  This last step uses Assumption~\ref{ass:bounded-potential-outcomes} precisely to supply the baseline interval and no stronger support restriction.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Coordinatewise sharpness can fail even when the joint fiber is nonempty, so the endpoint certificate prevents reporting unattainable box extremes. \textit{Gap.} Fenaroli2026 derives closed-form cohortwise endpoints under never-treated comparison groups. No adjacent anticipation paper provides a necessary-and-sufficient negative-cycle certificate after a comparison cohort is reused across risk sets. \textit{Consumer.} Sensitivity tables for the Indonesian anti-cheating rollout can flag which nominal cohort-year anticipation bounds are actually reachable under all shared comparisons.

\begin{theorem}[thm:sharp-att-region]\label{thm:sharp-att-region}
For every signed weight vector \(w\),
\[
 \Theta_I(w;P_{\mathrm{obs}})
 =\left\{\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}w_j\nu_j-s(w)^{\mathsf T}\beta:
 \beta\in\mathcal Q_Y(\mu)\right\}.
\]
Hence the joint identified region for \((\theta_j)_{j\in\mathcal J}\) is exactly the affine image of \(\mathcal Q_Y(\mu)\), and every exposed endpoint is attained by one full law rather than by target-specific laws.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The proof distinguishes the causal identified set from the observed-law optimization and proves both inclusions.  Let \(P\in\mathfrak M(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\).  Assumption~\ref{ass:pt-all} supplies \((\alpha_h,\lambda_t)\), and therefore the vertex vector \(\beta\) defined by \(\beta_h=\alpha_h\) and \(\beta_t=-\lambda_t\).  For an edge \(e=(h,t)\in\mathcal E\), Assumption~\ref{ass:cohort-overlap}, Assumption~\ref{ass:announced-consistency}, and the definition of \(a_e\) imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mu_e
 &=E_P[Y_t(p^h)\mid G=h]\\
 &=E_P[Y_t(p^\infty)\mid G=h]+a_e\\
 &=(B^{\mathsf T}\beta)_e+a_e.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
Assumption~\ref{ass:bounded-anticipation} and (12) give
\[

\begin{theorem}[thm:circulation-endpoints]\label{thm:circulation-endpoints}
If \(\mathcal Q_Y(\mu)\ne\varnothing\), then for every signed \(w\), the sharp aggregate endpoints in Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp-att-region} equal
\[
 L_w=\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}w_j\nu_j-\operatorname{MC}(s(w);d^-,d^+),
 \qquad
 U_w=\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}w_j\nu_j+\operatorname{MC}(-s(w);d^-,d^+).
\]
Primal and dual optimizers recover an attaining fixed-effect potential and anticipation vector. Feasibility of \(\mathcal Q_Y(\mu)\) is equivalent to absence of a negative directed cycle in the augmented interval-tension digraph.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write \(A:=B^+\).  The proof uses a residual-network derivation of the support function.  For every augmented edge oriented from cohort \(h\) to time \(t\), form two directed arcs.  The positive arc \(t\to h\) has incidence column \(q=A_e\) and cost \(c=d^+_e\); the negative arc \(h\to t\) has incidence column \(q=-A_e\) and cost \(c=-d^-_e\).  A nonnegative arc flow is a pair \((x^+,x^-)\), with node balance and cost
\[
 A(x^+-x^-),
 \qquad
 (d^+)^{\mathsf T}x^+-(d^-)^{\mathsf T}x^-.
\tag{19}
\]
For a vertex potential \(\beta\), every reduced cost \(c-q^{\mathsf T}\beta\) is nonnegative if and only if
\[
 d^-\le A^{\mathsf T}\beta\le d^+,
\tag{20}
\]
which is exactly \(\beta\in\mathcal Q_Y(\mu)\).  Hence a flow \(x\) with balance \(b\) and any \(\beta\in\mathcal Q_Y(\mu)\) obey the weak-duality identity
\[
 \sum_r c_rx_r-b^{\mathsf T}\beta
 =\sum_r x_r(c_r-q_r^{\mathsf T}\beta)\ge0.
\tag{21}
\]

We next derive equality.  Component balance of \(b\) permits a feasible signed flow: within each connected component, route demands along a spanning tree and split each signed edge flow into its two nonnegative directions.  Choose \(\beta^0\in\mathcal Q_Y(\mu)\), which exists by hypothesis.  Summing the nonnegative reduced costs relative to \(\beta^0\) around any directed cycle shows that every directed cycle has nonnegative cost.  Any feasible finite flow decomposes into source-to-sink path flows and directed cycle flows.  Deleting the cycle flows preserves balance and cannot increase cost.  The total path-flow mass is \(\frac12\sum_v|b_v|\); after deleting repetitions, only finitely many simple paths remain.  Thus a minimizing sequence can be taken in a bounded closed finite-dimensional set, and an optimal flow \(x^*\) exists.

Construct the residual digraph of \(x^*\): retain every original arc with its cost, and, whenever \(x_r^*>0\), add the reverse arc with cost \(-c_r\).  A negative residual cycle would admit a positive augmentation, no larger than the least flow on its reverse arcs, preserving balance and strictly lowering cost.  Therefore the residual digraph has no negative directed cycle.  Add a source with a zero-cost arc to each vertex and let \(\delta_v\) be the shortest-path distance to \(v\).  The difference-constraint argument in Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint-exactness} gives, for every original arc \(r:u\to v\),
\[
 \delta_v-\delta_u\le c_r.
\tag{22}
\]
If \(x_r^*>0\), the reverse residual arc also gives \(\delta_u-\delta_v\le-c_r\), so equality holds in (22).  Taking \(\beta_v^*:=\delta_v\), equation (22) yields \(\beta^*\in\mathcal Q_Y(\mu)\), and equality on every positive-flow arc gives
\[
 \sum_r c_rx_r^*
 =\sum_r x_r^*q_r^{\mathsf T}\beta^*
 =b^{\mathsf T}\beta^*.
\tag{23}
\]
Together, (21) and (23) prove the computable support identity
\[
 \max_{\beta\in\mathcal Q_Y(\mu)}b^{\mathsf T}\beta
 =\operatorname{MC}(b;d^-,d^+).
\tag{24}
\]

For \(b=s(w)\), flow feasibility is immediate because every \(s_j\) is the incidence vector of the \(\mathcal K\)-edge \((g_j,t_j)\); assigning signed edge coefficient \(w_j\) and splitting by sign gives balance \(s(w)\).  By Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp-att-region}, minimizing the aggregate subtracts the maximum of \(s(w)^{\mathsf T}\beta\), whereas maximizing it adds the maximum of \(-s(w)^{\mathsf T}\beta\).  Applying (24) to \(s(w)\) and \(-s(w)\) proves
\[
 L_w=\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}w_j\nu_j-
 \operatorname{MC}(s(w);d^-,d^+),
 \qquad
 U_w=\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}w_j\nu_j+
 \operatorname{MC}(-s(w);d^-,d^+).
\tag{25}
\]
The dual optimizer \(\beta^*\) is an attaining fixed-effect potential, and \(a^*:=\mu-B^{\mathsf T}\beta^*\) is its anticipation vector.  The sharpness and attainment conclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp-att-region} supplies one attaining causal law, while \(x^*\) and the zero reduced costs certify the endpoint.  Finally, the augmented interval-tension part of Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint-exactness} gives
\[
 \mathcal Q_Y(\mu)\ne\varnothing
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \mathcal D(d^-,d^+)\text{ has no negative directed cycle},
\]
which proves the feasibility assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:finite-sample-outer-coverage]\label{thm:finite-sample-outer-coverage}
Under independent sampling and bounded potential outcomes, for every fixed signed \(w\),
\[
 \inf_{P\in\mathfrak M(P_{\mathrm{obs}})}P^n\left\{
 \Theta_I(w;P_{\mathrm{obs}})
 \subseteq\widehat\Theta^{\mathrm{out}}_{1-\eta}(w)
 \right\}\ge 1-\eta.
\]
The two endpoints of \(\widehat\Theta^{\mathrm{out}}_{1-\eta}(w)\) are obtained by one joint interval-tension optimization: replace each observed-edge tension by
\[
 [\widehat\mu_e-\rho_{h(e)}-u_e,\widehat\mu_e+\rho_{h(e)}-\ell_e]
 \cap[\underline y-u_e,\overline y-\ell_e],
\]
retain the bounded-baseline tensions, and optimize the simultaneous target-mean bands with the signed weights. The claim remains valid when the population fiber is a singleton, when optimizer faces change, and when some sample cohort counts are zero.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(M:=|\mathcal E|+|\mathcal J|\) and \(R:=\overline y-\underline y>0\).  If \(M=0\), no sample-mean coordinate is present and the asserted inclusion is deterministic, so suppose \(M\ge1\).  We first derive the bounded-mean tail inequality used by the simultaneous region.

For a random variable \(X\in[\underline y,\overline y]\), put
\[
 g(\lambda):=\log E\exp\{\lambda(X-EX)\}.
\]
Boundedness permits differentiation under the expectation, so \(g(0)=g'(0)=0\), and \(g''(\lambda)\) is the variance under the exponentially tilted law.  Every random variable \(V\in[\underline y,\overline y]\) satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(V)
 \le(EV-\underline y)(\overline y-EV)
 \le {R^2\over4}.
\tag{26}
\]
The first inequality follows by taking expectations in \((V-\underline y)(\overline y-V)\ge0\), and the second follows by completing the square.  Integrating the bound on \(g''\) twice gives
\[
 E\exp\{\lambda(X-EX)\}\le\exp\{\lambda^2R^2/8\}.
\tag{27}
\]
For \(N\) independent copies, Markov's inequality, (27), and minimization over \(\lambda>0\) yield
\[
 \Pr\{\overline X-EX\ge r\}
 \le\inf_{\lambda>0}
 \exp\{-\lambda Nr+N\lambda^2R^2/8\}
 =\exp\{-2Nr^2/R^2\}.
\]
Applying the same argument to \(-X\) proves
\[
 \Pr\{|\overline X-EX|>r\}
 \le2\exp\{-2Nr^2/R^2\}.
\tag{28}
\]

Condition on the complete vector of cohort assignments.  Under Assumption~\ref{ass:iid-sampling}, whenever \(N_h>0\), each coordinate mean in cohort \(h\) averages \(N_h\) conditionally independent observations with its corresponding conditional mean and range \(R\).  Substitution of \(r=\rho_h\) into (28) gives failure probability at most \(\eta/M\) for each of the \(M\) coordinates.  A coordinate whose cohort count is zero is covered surely because its factor is \([\underline y,\overline y]\).  A conditional union bound and then averaging over the assignments give
\[
 P_{\mathrm{obs}}^n\{(\mu,\nu)\in\mathcal R_{1-\eta}\}
 \ge1-\eta.
\tag{29}
\]

On the event in (29), the population pair \((\mu,\nu)\) is one of the pairs in the union defining \(\widehat\Theta^{\mathrm{out}}_{1-\eta}(w)\).  Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp-att-region} therefore gives the deterministic inclusion
\[
 \Theta_I(w;P_{\mathrm{obs}})
 \subseteq\widehat\Theta^{\mathrm{out}}_{1-\eta}(w).
\tag{30}
\]
Every \(P\in\mathfrak M(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\) has the same observed projection \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\), so the probability in (29) is uniform over this class.  Equations (29)--(30) prove the displayed finite-sample coverage bound.

It remains to establish the advertised joint computation.  For each observed edge define
\[
 \underline m_e:=\max\{\widehat\mu_e-\rho_{h(e)},\underline y\},
 \qquad
 \overline m_e:=\min\{\widehat\mu_e+\rho_{h(e)},\overline y\},
\tag{31}
\]
and use \([\underline m_e,\overline m_e]=[\underline y,\overline y]\) when the cohort count is zero.  For a fixed \(\beta\), there exists a \(\mu'_e\in[\underline m_e,\overline m_e]\) satisfying
\[
 \mu'_e-u_e\le(B^{\mathsf T}\beta)_e\le\mu'_e-\ell_e
\]
if and only if the intervals \([\underline m_e,\overline m_e]\) and \([(B^{\mathsf T}\beta)_e+\ell_e,(B^{\mathsf T}\beta)_e+u_e]\) intersect.  For closed real intervals this is equivalent to
\[
 \underline m_e-u_e\le(B^{\mathsf T}\beta)_e
 \le\overline m_e-\ell_e.
\tag{32}
\]
By (31), the interval in (32) is exactly
\[
 [\widehat\mu_e-\rho_{h(e)}-u_e,
   \widehat\mu_e+\rho_{h(e)}-\ell_e]
 \cap[\underline y-u_e,\overline y-\ell_e].
\tag{33}
\]
Retain \([\underline y,\overline y]\) on every \(\mathcal K\)-edge.  The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:circulation-endpoints}, with (33) in place of the population tension, optimizes the \(\beta\)-part.

If \([\underline n_j,\overline n_j]\) is the simultaneous band for \(\nu_j\), separability of the target-mean box gives
\[
 \min_{\nu'}\sum_jw_j\nu'_j
 =\sum_{w_j\ge0}w_j\underline n_j
  +\sum_{w_j<0}w_j\overline n_j,
\tag{34}
\]
and the maximum interchanges the two endpoints.  Equations (33)--(34), together with the retained baseline tensions, are one finite interval-tension optimization whose projection is exactly the union in Definition~\ref{def:outer-confidence-set}.  No step used uniqueness or constancy of an active face, and a zero count merely replaces one factor by the full support interval.  Thus singleton fibers, active-face changes, and zero counts require no additional regularity condition.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:causal-completion]\label{thm:causal-completion}
The exhaustive extension property is valid under the maintained restrictions.  Specifically, for every \(a\in\mathcal A_Y(\mu)\), every observed law \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\) with outcomes in \([\underline y,\overline y]\) satisfying Assumption~\ref{ass:cohort-overlap}, and every \(\beta\in\mathcal Q_Y(\mu)\) such that \(a=\mu-B^{\mathsf T}\beta\), there exists one law \(P\in\mathfrak M(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\) that preserves the full joint law of \(O\), realizes \(a\) and \(\beta\), obeys consistency on \(p^G\), and simultaneously satisfies
\[
 \theta_j=\nu_j-s_j^{\mathsf T}\beta
 \qquad(j\in\mathcal J).
\]
The same law defines all \(\{Y_t(p^r):t\in\mathcal T,r\in\mathcal R\}\) jointly.  No additional cross-path compatibility inequality is required.  In particular, in \(\mathfrak W_3\), \(\mathsf{Comp}_3(z)\ne\varnothing\) for every \(z\in[-1,1/2]\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(a\in\mathcal A_Y(\mu)\), an observed law \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\) satisfying the hypotheses, and \(\beta\in\mathcal Q_Y(\mu)\) such that \(a=\mu-B^{\mathsf T}\beta\).  The proof uses a conditional endpoint-mixture construction.  Put
\[
 m_{ht}:=\beta_h-\beta_t=\alpha_h+\lambda_t,
 \qquad (h,t)\in\mathcal K,
\tag{1}
\]
where \(\alpha_h:=\beta_h\) and \(\lambda_t:=-\beta_t\), as in the definition of \(\beta\).  The \(\mathcal K\)-inequalities in Definition~\ref{def:bounded-completion-polyhedron} give
\[
 \underline y\le m_{ht}\le\overline y
 \qquad((h,t)\in\mathcal K).
\tag{2}
\]
For an observed pre-implementation edge \(e=(h,t)\), the assumed relation between \(a\) and \(\beta\) gives
\[
 \mu_e=m_{ht}+a_e.
\tag{3}
\]
Moreover, the observed-edge inequalities defining \(\mathcal Q_Y(\mu)\) give
\[
 \mu_e-u_e\le m_{ht}\le\mu_e-\ell_e,
\]
and subtracting this display from \(\mu_e\) yields \(\ell_e\le a_e\le u_e\).  Thus all divisions and mixture probabilities used below are supported by (2), and the prescribed anticipation vector obeys Assumption~\ref{ass:bounded-anticipation}.

Let \(O\sim P_{\mathrm{obs}}\), and enlarge its probability space by a random variable \(U\sim\operatorname{Unif}[0,1]\) independent of \(O\).  Since \(\overline y>\underline y\), for \(m\in[\underline y,\overline y]\) define
\[
 \Phi(m,U):=\underline y+(\overline y-\underline y)
 \mathbf 1\!\left\{U\le
 \frac{m-\underline y}{\overline y-\underline y}\right\}.
\tag{4}
\]
The ratio in (4) belongs to \([0,1]\).  Direct integration therefore gives
\[
 \Phi(m,U)\in\{\underline y,\overline y\}
 \quad\text{almost surely},
 \qquad E[\Phi(m,U)]=m.
\tag{5}
\]
On this one product probability space, simultaneously for every \((t,r)\in\mathcal T\times\mathcal R\), set
\[
 Y_t(p^r):=
 \begin{cases}
 Y_t,&r=G,\\
 \Phi(m_{Gt},U),&r\ne G.
 \end{cases}
\tag{6}
\]
Because \(G\) takes values in the finite cohorts, \(r=\infty\) always uses the second line.  Formula (6) is a single joint construction of all regime-path potential outcomes; the use of a common \(U\) merely selects one admissible cross-path coupling.  It leaves the full observed vector \(O\), including all of its within-unit dependence, unchanged.  It also gives \(Y_t(p^G)=Y_t\) identically, which verifies Assumption~\ref{ass:announced-consistency}.  The observed outcomes are supported on \([\underline y,\overline y]\) by hypothesis, and (5) gives the same support for every unobserved path, so Assumption~\ref{ass:bounded-potential-outcomes} holds.

For every positive-probability cohort \(h\), equations (5)--(6) imply
\[
 E_P[Y_t(p^\infty)\mid G=h]=m_{ht}=\alpha_h+\lambda_t.
\tag{7}
\]
If a cohort has zero probability, finiteness of \(\mathcal H\) permits the regular conditional kernel at that null stratum to be chosen as the endpoint mixture (4) with mean \(m_{ht}\); this fixes a version satisfying (7) without changing the joint law.  Thus (7) verifies Assumption~\ref{ass:pt-all} at every declared cohort--time cell.

Now take \(e=(h,t)\in\mathcal E\).  Assumption~\ref{ass:cohort-overlap} makes the following conditional means well defined.  Consistency, preservation of \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\), (3), and (7) yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_P[Y_t(p^h)-Y_t(p^\infty)\mid G=h]
 &=E_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}}[Y_t\mid G=h]-m_{ht}\\
 &=\mu_e-m_{ht}=a_e.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
The already proved box membership of \(a\) makes (8) exactly Assumption~\ref{ass:bounded-anticipation}.  Consequently the law constructed in (6) projects to \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\) and satisfies every member property in Definition~\ref{def:announced-policy-law-class}; hence \(P\in\mathfrak M(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\).

For a target \(j\in\mathcal J\), overlap, consistency, (1), and (7) give, in this same law,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \theta_j
 &=E_P[Y_{t_j}(p^{g_j})-Y_{t_j}(p^\infty)\mid G=g_j]\\
 &=E_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}}[Y_{t_j}\mid G=g_j]-m_{g_jt_j}\\
 &=\nu_j-s_j^{\mathsf T}\beta.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
This proves simultaneous completion for all targets, rather than separate target-wise attainability.

Finally consider \(\mathfrak W_3\).  For \(-1\le z\le1/2\), choose
\[
 m_{h2}=2,
 \qquad m_{h3}=2-z,
 \qquad h\in\{4,5,6\}.
\tag{10}
\]
These means lie in \([0,4]\).  The observed means in Definition~\ref{def:three-cohort-witness} satisfy
\[
 \mu_{h2}-m_{h2}=0,
 \qquad
 (\mu_{4,3}-m_{4,3},\mu_{5,3}-m_{5,3},\mu_{6,3}-m_{6,3})
 =(z+1/2,z,z).
\tag{11}
\]
The range of \(z\) makes every coordinate in (11) belong to its stipulated interval.  Taking, for example, \(\alpha_h=0\), \(\lambda_2=2\), and \(\lambda_3=2-z\) supplies the required additive potential.  Applying (6) with (10) therefore produces an element of \(\mathsf{Comp}_3(z)\) for every \(z\in[-1,1/2]\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This construction is the causal sharpness bridge: every feasible bounded mean surface is realized by one full law preserving the entire observed panel and all overlapping targets. \textit{Gap.} Torgovitsky2019 highlights full-distribution extension as a sharpness requirement, but no cited result supplies this announced-policy canonical-path completion under PT--All and edgewise anticipation boxes. \textit{Consumer.} The sharp ATT-region and circulation theorems use the construction to convert each optimizer into one common attaining causal law.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The exhaustive early kill test remains computational evidence rather than part of the proof. This solve enumerated all 511 nonempty subgraphs of the three-cohort by three-period bipartite graph and tested two deterministic rational-input instances per graph, including zero-width coordinates and disconnected graphs. Across 1,022 feasibility comparisons, the direct linear program, interval-tension program, and negative-cycle certificate agreed; across all 922 feasible cases, direct support, node-potential support, and split-flow transshipment values agreed to numerical tolerance. The causal completion theorem does not rest on that enumeration; it follows from the explicit endpoint-mixture construction. That construction proves existence and mean sharpness only. Its common latent uniform is one admissible coupling and carries no claim that cross-path dependence, marginal distributions beyond their means and support, or sensitivity magnitudes are identified from the observed law.

\section{Honest scope}

The completed result is sharp over \(\mathfrak M(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\): the proposal-specific class of known canonical announcement-and-implementation paths satisfying consistency, PT--All for the no-announcement reference path, preregistered edgewise level bounds on pre-implementation path effects, cohort overlap where conditional targets are used, and bounded potential outcomes. Every feasible \(\beta\in\mathcal Q_Y(\mu)\) is embedded in one common full law, so no target-specific completion or additional cross-path compatibility inequality is needed. This is not the published Callaway--Sant'Anna class and not Fenaroli's published class. The only Callaway--Sant'Anna transfer claimed is the zero-anticipation two-by-two formula under the explicit rectangular anchor \(\mathsf{RA}\); no class inclusion or Fenaroli-class transfer is claimed. The minimal-face formula uses \(\mathcal I(\mu)\) when feasibility forces a proper box face, and finite circulation endpoints require \(\mathcal Q_Y(\mu)\ne\varnothing\). The sensitivity intervals, policy paths, and announcement dates are fixed before outcome analysis. Covariate-conditional extensions, learned sensitivity magnitudes, endogenous or heterogeneous announcements, continuous timing, distributional treatment effects, and uniform optimality of the outer confidence set remain outside scope.
