For every signed weight vector \(w\),

 \Theta_I(w;P_{\mathrm{obs}})
 =\left\{\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}w_j\nu_j-s(w)^{\mathsf T}\beta:
 \beta\in\mathcal Q_Y(\mu)\right\}.
\]
Hence the joint identified region for \((\theta_j)_{j\in\mathcal J}\) is exactly the affine image of \(\mathcal Q_Y(\mu)\), and every exposed endpoint is attained by one full law rather than by target-specific laws.

If \(\mathcal Q_Y(\mu)\ne\varnothing\), then for every signed \(w\), the sharp aggregate endpoints in Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp-att-region} equal
\[
 L_w=\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}w_j\nu_j-\operatorname{MC}(s(w);d^-,d^+),
 \qquad
 U_w=\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}w_j\nu_j+\operatorname{MC}(-s(w);d^-,d^+).
\]
Primal and dual optimizers recover an attaining fixed-effect potential and anticipation vector. Feasibility of \(\mathcal Q_Y(\mu)\) is equivalent to absence of a negative directed cycle in the augmented interval-tension digraph.

Under independent sampling and bounded potential outcomes, for every fixed signed \(w\),
\[
 \inf_{P\in\mathfrak M(P_{\mathrm{obs}})}P^n\left\{
 \Theta_I(w;P_{\mathrm{obs}})
 \subseteq\widehat\Theta^{\mathrm{out}}_{1-\eta}(w)
 \right\}\ge 1-\eta.

The two endpoints of \(\widehat\Theta^{\mathrm{out}}_{1-\eta}(w)\) are obtained by one joint interval-tension optimization: replace each observed-edge tension by
\[
 [\widehat\mu_e-\rho_{h(e)}-u_e,\widehat\mu_e+\rho_{h(e)}-\ell_e]
 \cap[\underline y-u_e,\overline y-\ell_e],

retain the bounded-baseline tensions, and optimize the simultaneous target-mean bands with the signed weights. The claim remains valid when the population fiber is a singleton, when optimizer faces change, and when some sample cohort counts are zero.

Fix \(a\in\mathcal A_Y(\mu)\), an observed law \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\) satisfying the hypotheses, and \(\beta\in\mathcal Q_Y(\mu)\) such that \(a=\mu-B^{\mathsf T}\beta\).  The proof uses a conditional endpoint-mixture construction.  Put
\[
 m_{ht}:=\beta_h-\beta_t=\alpha_h+\lambda_t,
 \qquad (h,t)\in\mathcal K,
\tag{1}
\]
where \(\alpha_h:=\beta_h\) and \(\lambda_t:=-\beta_t\), as in the definition of \(\beta\).  The \(\mathcal K\)-inequalities in Definition~\ref{def:bounded-completion-polyhedron} give
\[
 \underline y\le m_{ht}\le\overline y
 \qquad((h,t)\in\mathcal K).
\tag{2}
\]
For an observed pre-implementation edge \(e=(h,t)\), the assumed relation between \(a\) and \(\beta\) gives
\[
 \mu_e=m_{ht}+a_e.
\tag{3}
\]
Moreover, the observed-edge inequalities defining \(\mathcal Q_Y(\mu)\) give
\[
 \mu_e-u_e\le m_{ht}\le\mu_e-\ell_e,
\]
and subtracting this display from \(\mu_e\) yields \(\ell_e\le a_e\le u_e\).  Thus all divisions and mixture probabilities used below are supported by (2), and the prescribed anticipation vector obeys Assumption~\ref{ass:bounded-anticipation}.

Let \(O\sim P_{\mathrm{obs}}\), and enlarge its probability space by a random variable \(U\sim\operatorname{Unif}[0,1]\) independent of \(O\).  Since \(\overline y>\underline y\), for \(m\in[\underline y,\overline y]\) define
\[
 \Phi(m,U):=\underline y+(\overline y-\underline y)
 \mathbf 1\!\left\{U\le
 \frac{m-\underline y}{\overline y-\underline y}\right\}.
\tag{4}
\]
The ratio in (4) belongs to \([0,1]\).  Direct integration therefore gives
\[
 \Phi(m,U)\in\{\underline y,\overline y\}
 \quad\text{almost surely},
 \qquad E[\Phi(m,U)]=m.
\tag{5}
\]
On this one product probability space, simultaneously for every \((t,r)\in\mathcal T\times\mathcal R\), set
\[
 Y_t(p^r):=
 \begin{cases}
 Y_t,&r=G,\\
 \Phi(m_{Gt},U),&r\ne G.
 \end{cases}
\tag{6}
\]
Because \(G\) takes values in the finite cohorts, \(r=\infty\) always uses the second line.  Formula (6) is a single joint construction of all regime-path potential outcomes; the use of a common \(U\) merely selects one admissible cross-path coupling.  It leaves the full observed vector \(O\), including all of its within-unit dependence, unchanged.  It also gives \(Y_t(p^G)=Y_t\) identically, which verifies Assumption~\ref{ass:announced-consistency}.  The observed outcomes are supported on \([\underline y,\overline y]\) by hypothesis, and (5) gives the same support for every unobserved path, so Assumption~\ref{ass:bounded-potential-outcomes} holds.

For every positive-probability cohort \(h\), equations (5)--(6) imply
\[
 E_P[Y_t(p^\infty)\mid G=h]=m_{ht}=\alpha_h+\lambda_t.
\tag{7}
\]
If a cohort has zero probability, finiteness of \(\mathcal H\) permits the regular conditional kernel at that null stratum to be chosen as the endpoint mixture (4) with mean \(m_{ht}\); this fixes a version satisfying (7) without changing the joint law.  Thus (7) verifies Assumption~\ref{ass:pt-all} at every declared cohort--time cell.

Now take \(e=(h,t)\in\mathcal E\).  Assumption~\ref{ass:cohort-overlap} makes the following conditional means well defined.  Consistency, preservation of \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\), (3), and (7) yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_P[Y_t(p^h)-Y_t(p^\infty)\mid G=h]
 &=E_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}}[Y_t\mid G=h]-m_{ht}\\
 &=\mu_e-m_{ht}=a_e.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
The already proved box membership of \(a\) makes (8) exactly Assumption~\ref{ass:bounded-anticipation}.  Consequently the law constructed in (6) projects to \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\) and satisfies every member property in Definition~\ref{def:announced-policy-law-class}; hence \(P\in\mathfrak M(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\).

For a target \(j\in\mathcal J\), overlap, consistency, (1), and (7) give, in this same law,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \theta_j
 &=E_P[Y_{t_j}(p^{g_j})-Y_{t_j}(p^\infty)\mid G=g_j]\\
 &=E_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}}[Y_{t_j}\mid G=g_j]-m_{g_jt_j}\\
 &=\nu_j-s_j^{\mathsf T}\beta.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
This proves simultaneous completion for all targets, rather than separate target-wise attainability.

Finally consider \(\mathfrak W_3\).  For \(-1\le z\le1/2\), choose
\[
 m_{h2}=2,
 \qquad m_{h3}=2-z,
 \qquad h\in\{4,5,6\}.
\tag{10}
\]
These means lie in \([0,4]\).  The observed means in Definition~\ref{def:three-cohort-witness} satisfy
\[
 \mu_{h2}-m_{h2}=0,
 \qquad
 (\mu_{4,3}-m_{4,3},\mu_{5,3}-m_{5,3},\mu_{6,3}-m_{6,3})
 =(z+1/2,z,z).
\tag{11}
\]
The range of \(z\) makes every coordinate in (11) belong to its stipulated interval.  Taking, for example, \(\alpha_h=0\), \(\lambda_2=2\), and \(\lambda_3=2-z\) supplies the required additive potential.  Applying (6) with (10) therefore produces an element of \(\mathsf{Comp}_3(z)\) for every \(z\in[-1,1/2]\).

The proof distinguishes the causal identified set from the observed-law optimization and proves both inclusions.  Let \(P\in\mathfrak M(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\).  Assumption~\ref{ass:pt-all} supplies \((\alpha_h,\lambda_t)\), and therefore the vertex vector \(\beta\) defined by \(\beta_h=\alpha_h\) and \(\beta_t=-\lambda_t\).  For an edge \(e=(h,t)\in\mathcal E\), Assumption~\ref{ass:cohort-overlap}, Assumption~\ref{ass:announced-consistency}, and the definition of \(a_e\) imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mu_e
 &=E_P[Y_t(p^h)\mid G=h]\\
 &=E_P[Y_t(p^\infty)\mid G=h]+a_e\\
 &=(B^{\mathsf T}\beta)_e+a_e.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
Assumption~\ref{ass:bounded-anticipation} and (12) give
\[
 \mu_e-u_e\le(B^{\mathsf T}\beta)_e\le\mu_e-\ell_e.
\tag{13}
\]
For every \((h,t)\in\mathcal K\), Assumptions~\ref{ass:pt-all} and~\ref{ass:bounded-potential-outcomes} give
\[
 \underline y\le\alpha_h+\lambda_t\le\overline y.
\tag{14}
\]
Thus (13)--(14) are exactly the inequalities defining \(\beta\in\mathcal Q_Y(\mu)\).  At target \(j\), the same consistency calculation yields
\[
 \theta_j=\nu_j-(\alpha_{g_j}+\lambda_{t_j})
 =\nu_j-s_j^{\mathsf T}\beta.
\tag{15}
\]
Multiplying (15) by \(w_j\) and summing gives
\[
 \theta_w=\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}w_j\nu_j-s(w)^{\mathsf T}\beta.
\tag{16}
\]
This proves the outer inclusion of the causal identified set in the displayed observed-law affine image.

Conversely, fix \(\beta\in\mathcal Q_Y(\mu)\) and define
\[
 a:=\mu-B^{\mathsf T}\beta.
\tag{17}
\]
Definition~\ref{def:bounded-completion-polyhedron} says precisely that \(a\in\mathcal A_Y(\mu)\).  Theorem~\ref{thm:causal-completion} then supplies one law \(P\in\mathfrak M(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\) realizing (17) and all equations (15) simultaneously.  Therefore every point of the affine image in the statement is causally attainable.  Together with (16), this proves equality and sharpness.

Applying the same two inclusions before multiplying by \(w_j\) proves that the joint identified region is
\[
 \left\{(\nu_j-s_j^{\mathsf T}\beta)_{j\in\mathcal J}:
 \beta\in\mathcal Q_Y(\mu)\right\}.
\tag{18}
\]
For completeness, exposed endpoints in (18) are attained.  The augmented graph is finite, and every tension \(((B^+)^{\mathsf T}\beta)_e\) lies in a bounded closed interval.  Fix one additive gauge in each augmented connected component.  Differences along a spanning forest are then bounded, so the gauge-fixed feasible set is closed and bounded, hence compact.  Each \(s_j\) is the incidence vector of the augmented edge \((g_j,t_j)\in\mathcal K\); consequently \(s(w)\) sums to zero on each relevant component, and its objective is gauge invariant.  Every nonempty exposed face therefore contains an optimizer.  Theorem~\ref{thm:causal-completion} turns that optimizer into one common attaining full law.
%%% FIELD proof-circulation-endpoints
Write \(A:=B^+\).  The proof uses a residual-network derivation of the support function.  For every augmented edge oriented from cohort \(h\) to time \(t\), form two directed arcs.  The positive arc \(t\to h\) has incidence column \(q=A_e\) and cost \(c=d^+_e\); the negative arc \(h\to t\) has incidence column \(q=-A_e\) and cost \(c=-d^-_e\).  A nonnegative arc flow is a pair \((x^+,x^-)\), with node balance and cost
\[
 A(x^+-x^-),
 \qquad
 (d^+)^{\mathsf T}x^+-(d^-)^{\mathsf T}x^-.
\tag{19}
\]
For a vertex potential \(\beta\), every reduced cost \(c-q^{\mathsf T}\beta\) is nonnegative if and only if
\[
 d^-\le A^{\mathsf T}\beta\le d^+,
\tag{20}
\]
which is exactly \(\beta\in\mathcal Q_Y(\mu)\).  Hence a flow \(x\) with balance \(b\) and any \(\beta\in\mathcal Q_Y(\mu)\) obey the weak-duality identity
\[
 \sum_r c_rx_r-b^{\mathsf T}\beta
 =\sum_r x_r(c_r-q_r^{\mathsf T}\beta)\ge0.
\tag{21}
\]

We next derive equality.  Component balance of \(b\) permits a feasible signed flow: within each connected component, route demands along a spanning tree and split each signed edge flow into its two nonnegative directions.  Choose \(\beta^0\in\mathcal Q_Y(\mu)\), which exists by hypothesis.  Summing the nonnegative reduced costs relative to \(\beta^0\) around any directed cycle shows that every directed cycle has nonnegative cost.  Any feasible finite flow decomposes into source-to-sink path flows and directed cycle flows.  Deleting the cycle flows preserves balance and cannot increase cost.  The total path-flow mass is \(\frac12\sum_v|b_v|\); after deleting repetitions, only finitely many simple paths remain.  Thus a minimizing sequence can be taken in a bounded closed finite-dimensional set, and an optimal flow \(x^*\) exists.

Construct the residual digraph of \(x^*\): retain every original arc with its cost, and, whenever \(x_r^*>0\), add the reverse arc with cost \(-c_r\).  A negative residual cycle would admit a positive augmentation, no larger than the least flow on its reverse arcs, preserving balance and strictly lowering cost.  Therefore the residual digraph has no negative directed cycle.  Add a source with a zero-cost arc to each vertex and let \(\delta_v\) be the shortest-path distance to \(v\).  The difference-constraint argument in Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint-exactness} gives, for every original arc \(r:u\to v\),
\[
 \delta_v-\delta_u\le c_r.
\tag{22}
\]
If \(x_r^*>0\), the reverse residual arc also gives \(\delta_u-\delta_v\le-c_r\), so equality holds in (22).  Taking \(\beta_v^*:=\delta_v\), equation (22) yields \(\beta^*\in\mathcal Q_Y(\mu)\), and equality on every positive-flow arc gives
\[
 \sum_r c_rx_r^*
 =\sum_r x_r^*q_r^{\mathsf T}\beta^*
 =b^{\mathsf T}\beta^*.
\tag{23}
\]
Together, (21) and (23) prove the computable support identity
\[
 \max_{\beta\in\mathcal Q_Y(\mu)}b^{\mathsf T}\beta
 =\operatorname{MC}(b;d^-,d^+).
\tag{24}
\]

For \(b=s(w)\), flow feasibility is immediate because every \(s_j\) is the incidence vector of the \(\mathcal K\)-edge \((g_j,t_j)\); assigning signed edge coefficient \(w_j\) and splitting by sign gives balance \(s(w)\).  By Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp-att-region}, minimizing the aggregate subtracts the maximum of \(s(w)^{\mathsf T}\beta\), whereas maximizing it adds the maximum of \(-s(w)^{\mathsf T}\beta\).  Applying (24) to \(s(w)\) and \(-s(w)\) proves
\[
 L_w=\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}w_j\nu_j-
 \operatorname{MC}(s(w);d^-,d^+),
 \qquad
 U_w=\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}w_j\nu_j+
 \operatorname{MC}(-s(w);d^-,d^+).
\tag{25}
\]
The dual optimizer \(\beta^*\) is an attaining fixed-effect potential, and \(a^*:=\mu-B^{\mathsf T}\beta^*\) is its anticipation vector.  The sharpness and attainment conclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp-att-region} supplies one attaining causal law, while \(x^*\) and the zero reduced costs certify the endpoint.  Finally, the augmented interval-tension part of Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint-exactness} gives
\[
 \mathcal Q_Y(\mu)\ne\varnothing
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \mathcal D(d^-,d^+)\text{ has no negative directed cycle},
\]
which proves the feasibility assertion.

Let \(M:=|\mathcal E|+|\mathcal J|\) and \(R:=\overline y-\underline y>0\).  If \(M=0\), no sample-mean coordinate is present and the asserted inclusion is deterministic, so suppose \(M\ge1\).  We first derive the bounded-mean tail inequality used by the simultaneous region.

For a random variable \(X\in[\underline y,\overline y]\), put
\[
 g(\lambda):=\log E\exp\{\lambda(X-EX)\}.
\]
Boundedness permits differentiation under the expectation, so \(g(0)=g'(0)=0\), and \(g''(\lambda)\) is the variance under the exponentially tilted law.  Every random variable \(V\in[\underline y,\overline y]\) satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(V)
 \le(EV-\underline y)(\overline y-EV)
 \le {R^2\over4}.
\tag{26}
\]
The first inequality follows by taking expectations in \((V-\underline y)(\overline y-V)\ge0\), and the second follows by completing the square.  Integrating the bound on \(g''\) twice gives
\[
 E\exp\{\lambda(X-EX)\}\le\exp\{\lambda^2R^2/8\}.
\tag{27}
\]
For \(N\) independent copies, Markov's inequality, (27), and minimization over \(\lambda>0\) yield
\[
 \Pr\{\overline X-EX\ge r\}
 \le\inf_{\lambda>0}
 \exp\{-\lambda Nr+N\lambda^2R^2/8\}
 =\exp\{-2Nr^2/R^2\}.
\]
Applying the same argument to \(-X\) proves
\[
 \Pr\{|\overline X-EX|>r\}
 \le2\exp\{-2Nr^2/R^2\}.
\tag{28}
\]

Condition on the complete vector of cohort assignments.  Under Assumption~\ref{ass:iid-sampling}, whenever \(N_h>0\), each coordinate mean in cohort \(h\) averages \(N_h\) conditionally independent observations with its corresponding conditional mean and range \(R\).  Substitution of \(r=\rho_h\) into (28) gives failure probability at most \(\eta/M\) for each of the \(M\) coordinates.  A coordinate whose cohort count is zero is covered surely because its factor is \([\underline y,\overline y]\).  A conditional union bound and then averaging over the assignments give
\[
 P_{\mathrm{obs}}^n\{(\mu,\nu)\in\mathcal R_{1-\eta}\}
 \ge1-\eta.
\tag{29}
\]

On the event in (29), the population pair \((\mu,\nu)\) is one of the pairs in the union defining \(\widehat\Theta^{\mathrm{out}}_{1-\eta}(w)\).  Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp-att-region} therefore gives the deterministic inclusion
\[
 \Theta_I(w;P_{\mathrm{obs}})
 \subseteq\widehat\Theta^{\mathrm{out}}_{1-\eta}(w).
\tag{30}
\]
Every \(P\in\mathfrak M(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\) has the same observed projection \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\), so the probability in (29) is uniform over this class.  Equations (29)--(30) prove the displayed finite-sample coverage bound.

It remains to establish the advertised joint computation.  For each observed edge define
\[
 \underline m_e:=\max\{\widehat\mu_e-\rho_{h(e)},\underline y\},
 \qquad
 \overline m_e:=\min\{\widehat\mu_e+\rho_{h(e)},\overline y\},
\tag{31}
\]
and use \([\underline m_e,\overline m_e]=[\underline y,\overline y]\) when the cohort count is zero.  For a fixed \(\beta\), there exists a \(\mu'_e\in[\underline m_e,\overline m_e]\) satisfying
\[
 \mu'_e-u_e\le(B^{\mathsf T}\beta)_e\le\mu'_e-\ell_e
\]
if and only if the intervals \([\underline m_e,\overline m_e]\) and \([(B^{\mathsf T}\beta)_e+\ell_e,(B^{\mathsf T}\beta)_e+u_e]\) intersect.  For closed real intervals this is equivalent to
\[
 \underline m_e-u_e\le(B^{\mathsf T}\beta)_e
 \le\overline m_e-\ell_e.
\tag{32}
\]
By (31), the interval in (32) is exactly
\[
 [\widehat\mu_e-\rho_{h(e)}-u_e,
   \widehat\mu_e+\rho_{h(e)}-\ell_e]
 \cap[\underline y-u_e,\overline y-\ell_e].
\tag{33}
\]
Retain \([\underline y,\overline y]\) on every \(\mathcal K\)-edge.  The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:circulation-endpoints}, with (33) in place of the population tension, optimizes the \(\beta\)-part.

If \([\underline n_j,\overline n_j]\) is the simultaneous band for \(\nu_j\), separability of the target-mean box gives
\[
 \min_{\nu'}\sum_jw_j\nu'_j
 =\sum_{w_j\ge0}w_j\underline n_j
  +\sum_{w_j<0}w_j\overline n_j,
\tag{34}
\]
and the maximum interchanges the two endpoints.  Equations (33)--(34), together with the retained baseline tensions, are one finite interval-tension optimization whose projection is exactly the union in Definition~\ref{def:outer-confidence-set}.  No step used uniqueness or constancy of an active face, and a zero count merely replaces one factor by the full support interval.  Thus singleton fibers, active-face changes, and zero counts require no additional regularity condition.

Each claim is an exact algebraic reduction.

First suppose \(\ell=u=0\), \(C\mu=0\), and \(\mathcal Q_Y(\mu)\ne\varnothing\).  Definition~\ref{def:cycle-fiber} gives
\[
 \mathcal A_{\mathrm{cyc}}(\mu)
 =\{a:a=0,\ C\mu=0\}=\{0\}.
\tag{35}
\]
Under \(\mathsf{RA}\), all three anchoring edges for target \(j\) are clean.  Additivity of the reference-path mean surface gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \alpha_{g_j}+\lambda_{t_j}
 &=(\alpha_{g_j}+\lambda_{b_j})
   +(\alpha_{q_j}+\lambda_{t_j})
   -(\alpha_{q_j}+\lambda_{b_j})\\
 &=\mu_{(g_j,b_j)}+\mu_{(q_j,t_j)}-\mu_{(q_j,b_j)}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{36}
\]
The overlap clause in \(\mathsf{RA}\) makes the comparison-cohort conditional means well defined, and its three-edge rectangle places all four vertices in one connected component.  Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp-att-region} now gives the singleton value
\[
 \theta_j=(\nu_j-\mu_{(g_j,b_j)})
 -(\mu_{(q_j,t_j)}-\mu_{(q_j,b_j)}).
\tag{37}
\]

Second, suppose every positive-width edge is a bridge and the fixed-cycle equations hold.  Theorem~\ref{thm:variation-independence} then gives the full primitive box.  Under the stated slackness premise, the bounded-baseline inequalities delete neither a box point nor a support optimizer.  To make the independent-box reduction explicit, let \(b\) be component-balanced and route it on a spanning forest to obtain \(q\in\mathbb R^{\mathcal E}\) with \(Bq=b\).  For \(a=\mu-B^{\mathsf T}\beta\),
\[
 b^{\mathsf T}\beta
 =q^{\mathsf T}B^{\mathsf T}\beta
 =q^{\mathsf T}(\mu-a).
\tag{38}
\]
Consequently the support values are obtained coordinatewise:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \max_a q^{\mathsf T}a
 &=\sum_{q_e\ge0}q_eu_e+\sum_{q_e<0}q_e\ell_e,\\
 \min_a q^{\mathsf T}a
 &=\sum_{q_e\ge0}q_e\ell_e+\sum_{q_e<0}q_eu_e.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{39}
\]
Equations (38)--(39), and the support identity in Theorem~\ref{thm:circulation-endpoints}, show that the corresponding transshipment support values equal the independent-box calculation.

Third, order the four-cycle coordinates as \((a_1,a_2,a_3,a_4)\).  The box restrictions give \(a_1,a_3\in[0,1]\) and \(a_2=a_4=0\), while \(C\mu=0\) gives
\[
 a_1-a_2+a_3-a_4=0.
\]
Thus \(a_1+a_3=0\), which forces \(a_1=a_3=0\).  Hence the fiber is \(\{0\}\) and \(\mathcal I(\mu)=\varnothing\).  Theorem~\ref{thm:cycle-fiber} yields
\[
 \dim\operatorname{aff}\mathcal A_{\mathrm{cyc}}(\mu)
 =0-\operatorname{rank}(C_{\varnothing})=0.
\tag{40}
\]
By contrast, \(\mathcal W=\{1,3\}\) and \(\operatorname{rank}(C_{\mathcal W})=1\), so the inapplicable unqualified expression is \(2-1=1\).

Finally consider \(\mathfrak W_3\).  The clean coordinates give \(a_{h,2}=0\).  For any two cohorts \(h,h'\), the four-cycle equation is equality of their baseline time differences:
\[
 (\mu_{h,2}-a_{h,2})-(\mu_{h,3}-a_{h,3})
 = (\mu_{h',2}-a_{h',2})-(\mu_{h',3}-a_{h',3}).
\tag{41}
\]
Substitution of the rational means from Definition~\ref{def:three-cohort-witness} into (41) gives
\[
 a_{4,3}-\tfrac12=a_{5,3}=a_{6,3}=:z.
\tag{42}
\]
The three intervals \([-1,1]\) require
\[
 -1\le z\le1,
 \qquad
 -1\le z+\tfrac12\le1,
\]
whose intersection is \([-1,1/2]\).  Conversely, every \(z\) in this intersection satisfies every cycle equation and box restriction, so Theorem~\ref{thm:cycle-fiber} proves
\[
 \mathcal A_{\mathrm{cyc}}(\mu)
 =\{a:a_{h,2}=0,\ (a_{4,3},a_{5,3},a_{6,3})=(z+1/2,z,z),\ -1\le z\le1/2\}.
\tag{43}
\]
Equation (42) gives \(a_{4,3}-a_{5,3}=1/2\), and
\[
 \psi_3=\frac{a_{4,3}+a_{5,3}}{2}=z+\tfrac14
 \in[-\tfrac34,\tfrac34].
\tag{44}
\]
The endpoints are attained at \(z=-1\) and \(z=1/2\).  Over the uncoupled square \([-1,1]^2\), the same average ranges over \([-1,1]\), proving the strict contraction.
