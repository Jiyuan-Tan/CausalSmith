%%% FIELD proof-cycle-fiber
Put \(\mathcal P=\mathcal A_{\mathrm{cyc}}(\mu)\).  The proof uses incidence--cycle orthogonality and then a relative-interior tangent-space argument.  Fix \(a\in\prod_{e\in\mathcal E}[\ell_e,u_e]\).  By the defining property of the fundamental-cycle matrix,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \exists\beta\in\mathbb R^{\mathcal V}:\ \mu=B^{\mathsf T}\beta+a
 &\Longleftrightarrow \mu-a\in\operatorname{im}(B^{\mathsf T})\\
 &\Longleftrightarrow \mu-a\in\ker C\\
 &\Longleftrightarrow Ca=C\mu .
 \end{aligned}
\]
The first equivalence is algebra, the second is the identity \(\ker C=\operatorname{im}(B^{\mathsf T})\) in the definition of \(C\), and the last is linearity.  Assumption~\ref{ass:bounded-anticipation} supplies exactly the displayed box restriction.  This proves both the equivalence and, by Definition~\ref{def:cycle-fiber}, the asserted description of the anticipation fiber.  Notice that no connectedness or rank condition has been imposed; the \(c\) component gauges are already contained in \(\ker(B^{\mathsf T})\).

Suppose next that \(\mathcal P\ne\varnothing\), set \(I=\mathcal I(\mu)\), and let
\[
 D=\operatorname{span}\{a-a':a,a'\in\mathcal P\}
\]
be the direction space of \(\operatorname{aff}\mathcal P\).  If \(e\notin I\), the definition of \(I\) says that the \(e\)-th coordinate is constant on \(\mathcal P\).  Also, every difference of two points of \(\mathcal P\) is annihilated by \(C\).  Consequently
\[
 D\subseteq K_I:=\{d\in\mathbb R^{\mathcal E}:d_e=0\text{ for }e\notin I,\ Cd=0\}.
\]
For the reverse inclusion, choose \(a^\circ\in\operatorname{relint}\mathcal P\).  Such a point exists without an extra assumption: choose finitely many points of \(\mathcal P\) whose affine hull is \(\operatorname{aff}\mathcal P\); the average of a maximal affinely independent subcollection lies in the relative interior of their full-dimensional simplex, which is contained in \(\mathcal P\).  For every \(e\in I\),
\[
 \ell_e<a^\circ_e<u_e. \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, if, for example, \(a^\circ_e=\ell_e\), choose \(a'\in\mathcal P\) with \(a'_e>a^\circ_e\), which exists because coordinate \(e\) varies.  Relative interiority permits \(a^\circ+\varepsilon(a^\circ-a')\in\mathcal P\) for all sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\), but its \(e\)-th coordinate is below \(\ell_e\), a contradiction.  The upper inequality is identical.  If \(d\in K_I\), finiteness of \(I\) and (1) give a sufficiently small \(\delta>0\) for which \(a^\circ+\delta d\) remains in the box.  Since \(Cd=0\), it also satisfies \(C(a^\circ+\delta d)=C\mu\), and hence belongs to \(\mathcal P\).  Thus \(\delta d\in D\), proving \(K_I\subseteq D\).  Therefore
\[
 \dim\operatorname{aff}\mathcal P
 =\dim K_I
 =\dim\ker(C_I)
 =|I|-\operatorname{rank}(C_I),
\]
where the last equality is rank--nullity.  Taking \(I=\mathcal W\) yields the relative-interior formula.  Coordinates of zero width are outside \(I\), a proper active box face merely makes \(I\) smaller, and the argument is componentwise, so the same formula covers all boundary, zero-width, and disconnected cases stated in the theorem.

%%% FIELD proof-variation-independence
Write \(R=\prod_{e\in\mathcal E}[\ell_e,u_e]\).  The technique is a coordinatewise box-perturbation argument followed by the graphic bridge criterion.  Every \(a\in R\) has the same fixed subvector \(a_{\mathcal F}=\overline a_{\mathcal F}\).  If \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{cyc}}(\mu)=R\), then \(Ca=C\mu\) for every \(a\in R\).  Fix an interior value for every coordinate in \(\mathcal W\).  For any \(e\in\mathcal W\), perturb only coordinate \(e\) by a nonzero sufficiently small amount \(\delta\).  Subtraction of the two cycle equations gives
\[
 \delta C_{\{e\}}=0,
\]
so the \(e\)-th column of \(C\) is zero.  This holds for every \(e\in\mathcal W\), hence \(C_{\mathcal W}=0\).  Substitution of any point of \(R\) then gives
\[
 C_{\mathcal F}\overline a_{\mathcal F}=C\mu. \tag{2}
\]
Conversely, if \(C_{\mathcal W}=0\) and (2) holds, every \(a\in R\) obeys
\[
 Ca=C_{\mathcal W}a_{\mathcal W}+C_{\mathcal F}\overline a_{\mathcal F}=C\mu,
\]
and Definition~\ref{def:cycle-fiber} gives \(R\subseteq\mathcal A_{\mathrm{cyc}}(\mu)\).  The reverse inclusion is part of that definition, proving equality.  This establishes the variation-independence criterion in the product-range sense of \cite{Dawid2001}; the argument itself proves the specialized claim rather than importing it.

It remains to translate \(C_{\mathcal W}=0\) into graph language.  The rows of \(C\) are a basis of the signed cycle space.  A column \(C_{\{e\}}\) is zero exactly when the coordinate of edge \(e\) vanishes in every cycle-space vector, equivalently when no graph cycle contains \(e\).  In a finite undirected graph, deleting an edge disconnects its two endpoints if and only if that edge belongs to no cycle: if another endpoint-to-endpoint path remains, adjoining \(e\) makes a cycle, while a cycle containing \(e\) supplies such a path after deletion.  Thus \(C_{\{e\}}=0\) exactly when \(e\) is a bridge.  Applying this to all \(e\in\mathcal W\), together with (2), proves the final equivalence, including disconnected graphs and zero-width coordinates.

%%% FIELD proof-endpoint-exactness
The proof uses an interval-tension reduction and proves the finite difference-constraint certificate directly.  First replace the interval at \(e_0\) by \([\ell_{e_0},\ell_{e_0}]\), leaving all other intervals unchanged; denote the resulting endpoints by \(\ell^*\) and \(u^*\).  By Theorem~\ref{thm:cycle-fiber}, a vector in the original fiber attains \(a_{e_0}=\ell_{e_0}\) if and only if there is a \(\beta\in\mathbb R^{\mathcal V}\) such that
\[
 a=\mu-B^{\mathsf T}\beta,
 \qquad
 \ell^*_e\le \mu_e-(B^{\mathsf T}\beta)_e\le u^*_e
 \quad(e\in\mathcal E).
\]
Rearranging each displayed pair gives the interval-tension system
\[
 \mu_e-u^*_e\le \beta_{h(e)}-\beta_{t(e)}
 \le \mu_e-\ell^*_e. \tag{3}
\]
Thus endpoint attainment and existence of the stated vertex potential are equivalent.  Replacing the interval by \([u_{e_0},u_{e_0}]\) proves the analogous algebraic equivalence for the upper endpoint.

For completeness, consider any finite oriented multigraph with lower and upper tensions \(L_e\le U_e\).  Encode
\[
 L_e\le\beta_{h(e)}-\beta_{t(e)}\le U_e
\]
by an arc \(t(e)\to h(e)\) of weight \(U_e\) and an arc \(h(e)\to t(e)\) of weight \(-L_e\).  If \(\beta\) is feasible, each arc \(v\to v'\) of weight \(q\) satisfies \(\beta_{v'}-\beta_v\le q\); summing around a directed cycle proves that its total weight is nonnegative.  Conversely, suppose there is no negative directed cycle.  Add a new source with a zero-weight arc to every vertex, and let \(\delta_v\) be the minimum weight of a source-to-\(v\) path.  A minimum exists because any repeated-vertex portion is a nonnegative cycle and can be deleted, leaving one of finitely many simple paths.  For every arc \(v\to v'\) of weight \(q\), appending that arc to a shortest path gives
\[
 \delta_{v'}\le\delta_v+q.
\]
Taking \(\beta_v=\delta_v\) satisfies all encoded inequalities.  This proves, in both directions, the negative-cycle certificate used in (3).

Finally, on the augmented multigraph \(\mathcal E\sqcup\mathcal K\), the observed-edge tensions are \([\mu_e-u_e,\mu_e-\ell_e]\), while the baseline edges have tensions \([\underline y,\overline y]\).  These are exactly the inequalities defining \(\mathcal Q_Y(\mu)\) in Definition~\ref{def:bounded-completion-polyhedron}; their two-arc graph is \(\mathcal D(d^-,d^+)\).  Pinning either endpoint on \(e_0\) and applying the certificate just proved therefore characterizes endpoint exactness in \(\mathcal A_Y(\mu)\).  This last step uses Assumption~\ref{ass:bounded-potential-outcomes} precisely to supply the baseline interval and no stronger support restriction.

The exhaustive extension property is valid under the maintained restrictions.  Specifically, for every \(a\in\mathcal A_Y(\mu)\), every observed law \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\) with outcomes in \([\underline y,\overline y]\) satisfying Assumption~\ref{ass:cohort-overlap}, and every \(\beta\in\mathcal Q_Y(\mu)\) such that \(a=\mu-B^{\mathsf T}\beta\), there exists one law \(P\in\mathfrak M(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\) that preserves the full joint law of \(O\), realizes \(a\) and \(\beta\), obeys consistency on \(p^G\), and simultaneously satisfies

 \qquad(j\in\mathcal J).

The same law defines all \(\{Y_t(p^r):t\in\mathcal T,r\in\mathcal R\}\) jointly.  No additional cross-path compatibility inequality is required.  In particular, in \(\mathfrak W_3\), \(\mathsf{Comp}_3(z)\ne\varnothing\) for every \(z\in[-1,1/2]\).

Fix \(a\), \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\), and \(\beta\) as in the statement.  The proof is a conditional endpoint-mixture construction.  For \((h,t)\in\mathcal K\), define the baseline mean
\[
 m_{ht}:=\beta_h-\beta_t=\alpha_h+\lambda_t.
\]
The \(\mathcal K\)-inequalities in Definition~\ref{def:bounded-completion-polyhedron} imply
\[
 \underline y\le m_{ht}\le\overline y. \tag{4}
\]
For every observed pre-implementation edge \(e=(h,t)\), the relation \(a=\mu-B^{\mathsf T}\beta\) gives
\[
 \mu_e=m_{ht}+a_e, \tag{5}
\]
and the observed-edge inequalities in \(\mathcal Q_Y(\mu)\) give \(\ell_e\le a_e\le u_e\).  Thus (4) and (5) establish all mean and support facts required before constructing random variables.

Let \(U\) be uniform on \([0,1]\) and independent of an observed draw \(O\sim P_{\mathrm{obs}}\).  For \(m\in[\underline y,\overline y]\), put
\[
 \Phi(m,U)=\underline y+(\overline y-\underline y)
 \mathbf 1\left\{U\le {m-\underline y\over \overline y-\underline y}\right\}.
\]
Because \(\overline y>\underline y\), the displayed ratio lies in \([0,1]\), and direct integration gives
\[
 \Phi(m,U)\in\{\underline y,\overline y\},
 \qquad E[\Phi(m,U)]=m. \tag{6}
\]
On the product probability space carrying \((O,U)\), define all potential outcomes, simultaneously for \(t\in\mathcal T\) and \(r\in\mathcal R\), by
\[
 Y_t(p^r)=
 \begin{cases}
 Y_t,& r=G,\\
 \Phi(m_{Gt},U),& r\ne G.
 \end{cases} \tag{7}
\]
Here \(G\) is finite-valued, so \(r=\infty\) always falls in the second line.  Formula (7) is one joint construction, not a collection of target-specific laws.  It preserves the complete dependence structure of the observed vector because it does not alter \(O\).  It also gives \(Y_t=Y_t(p^G)\) identically, proving Assumption~\ref{ass:announced-consistency}, and (6), together with observed boundedness, proves Assumption~\ref{ass:bounded-potential-outcomes} for every path.

Conditional on \(G=h\), (6)--(7) imply
\[
 E_P[Y_t(p^\infty)\mid G=h]=m_{ht}=\alpha_h+\lambda_t, \tag{8}
\]
which is Assumption~\ref{ass:pt-all}.  On every edge \(e=(h,t)\in\mathcal E\), cohort overlap makes the conditional means in the following calculation well defined, and consistency, (5), and (8) yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_P[Y_t(p^h)-Y_t(p^\infty)\mid G=h]
 &=E_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}}[Y_t\mid G=h]-m_{ht}\\
 &=\mu_e-m_{ht}=a_e.
 \end{aligned} \tag{9}
\]
Thus the constructed law realizes the prescribed \(a\), and its box membership proves Assumption~\ref{ass:bounded-anticipation}.  Values of conditional kernels at a zero-probability cohort not incident to \(\mathcal E\) and not used by \(\mathcal J\) may be set by the same formula; as usual, those values select a version and do not alter the joint law.  This does not affect any conditional quantity protected by Assumption~\ref{ass:cohort-overlap}.

For each target \(j\), overlap and (7)--(8) give, in the same law,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \theta_j
 &=E_P[Y_{t_j}(p^{g_j})-Y_{t_j}(p^\infty)\mid G=g_j]\\
 &=E_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}}[Y_{t_j}\mid G=g_j]-m_{g_jt_j}\\
 &=\nu_j-s_j^{\mathsf T}\beta.
 \end{aligned} \tag{10}
\]
All defining properties of \(\mathfrak M(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\) have now been checked, so (7) proves the exhaustive extension property.

For the diagnostic \(\mathfrak W_3\), take \(m_{h2}=2\) and \(m_{h3}=2-z\) for \(h\in\{4,5,6\}\).  When \(-1\le z\le1/2\), these means lie in \([0,4]\), and
\[
 \begin{array}{c|ccc}
 &h=4&h=5&h=6\\ \hline
 \mu_{h3}-m_{h3}&z+1/2&z&z
 \end{array}
\]
while \(\mu_{h2}-m_{h2}=0\).  These values lie in the stipulated anticipation intervals.  Applying (7) therefore produces an element of \(\mathsf{Comp}_3(z)\) for every \(z\in[-1,1/2]\), proving the final claim.

The proof separates the causal target from the observed-law optimization and proves both inclusions.  Let \(P\in\mathfrak M(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\).  Assumption~\ref{ass:pt-all} supplies \((\alpha_h,\lambda_t)\) and hence \(\beta\).  For every \(e=(h,t)\in\mathcal E\), cohort overlap, consistency, and the definition of \(a_e\) give
\[
 \mu_e=E_P[Y_t(p^h)\mid G=h]
 =E_P[Y_t(p^\infty)\mid G=h]+a_e
 =(B^{\mathsf T}\beta)_e+a_e. \tag{11}
\]
Assumption~\ref{ass:bounded-anticipation} rearranges (11) to
\[
 \mu_e-u_e\le(B^{\mathsf T}\beta)_e\le\mu_e-\ell_e. \tag{12}
\]
Assumption~\ref{ass:bounded-potential-outcomes} and parallel trends give, for every \((h,t)\in\mathcal K\),
\[
 \underline y\le\alpha_h+\lambda_t\le\overline y. \tag{13}
\]
Equations (12)--(13) say exactly that \(\beta\in\mathcal Q_Y(\mu)\).  At each target, the same consistency calculation gives
\[
 \theta_j=\nu_j-(\alpha_{g_j}+\lambda_{t_j})
 =\nu_j-s_j^{\mathsf T}\beta.
\]
After multiplying by \(w_j\) and summing,
\[
 \theta_w=\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}w_j\nu_j-s(w)^{\mathsf T}\beta. \tag{14}
\]
This proves that the causal identified set is contained in the displayed affine image; in particular, the image has not been declared to be the causal target.

Conversely, fix \(\beta\in\mathcal Q_Y(\mu)\) and define \(a=\mu-B^{\mathsf T}\beta\).  Definition~\ref{def:bounded-completion-polyhedron} gives \(a\in\mathcal A_Y(\mu)\).  Theorem~\ref{thm:causal-completion} then supplies one \(P\in\mathfrak M(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\) realizing this \(\beta\) and all equations \(\theta_j=\nu_j-s_j^{\mathsf T}\beta\) simultaneously.  Hence every point in the affine image is causally attainable, proving the reverse inclusion and sharpness.

Applying the same two-inclusion argument before aggregation shows that the joint identified region is
\[
 \left\{(\nu_j-s_j^{\mathsf T}\beta)_{j\in\mathcal J}:
 \beta\in\mathcal Q_Y(\mu)\right\}.
\]
Finally, all augmented-edge tensions \((B^+)^{\mathsf T}\beta\) lie in bounded intervals.  After fixing one gauge in each connected component, vertex differences along a spanning forest are bounded, so the gauge-fixed feasible set is compact.  Each \(s_j\), and therefore \(s(w)\), is component-balanced because it is an incidence vector of the augmented edge \((g_j,t_j)\in\mathcal K\).  Thus its objective is gauge invariant and attains its extrema on that compact representative set.  The completion theorem turns every such optimizer into one attaining full law, which proves the assertion about exposed endpoints.

%%% FIELD proof-circulation-endpoints
Let \(A=B^+\).  The proof is a residual-network derivation of the support function, specialized to the interval-tension polyhedron.  For each augmented edge oriented from \(h\) to \(t\), make two directed arcs: the \(+\) arc \(t\to h\) has node-incidence vector \(q=A_e\) and cost \(c=d^+_e\), while the \(-\) arc \(h\to t\) has incidence \(q=-A_e\) and cost \(c=-d^-_e\).  A nonnegative arc flow is the pair \((x^+,x^-)\), its node balance is \(A(x^+-x^-)\), and its cost is
\[
 (d^+)^{\mathsf T}x^+-(d^-)^{\mathsf T}x^-.
\]
For a vertex potential \(\beta\), all reduced costs are nonnegative exactly when
\[
 c-q^{\mathsf T}\beta\ge0\quad\text{for every arc},
\]
which expands to \(d^-\le A^{\mathsf T}\beta\le d^+\), namely \(\beta\in\mathcal Q_Y(\mu)\).  Therefore any feasible flow of balance \(b\) and any \(\beta\in\mathcal Q_Y(\mu)\) satisfy the weak-duality identity
\[
 \sum_{	ext{arcs}}c_rx_r-b^{\mathsf T}\beta
 =\sum_{	ext{arcs}}x_r(c_r-q_r^{\mathsf T}\beta)\ge0. \tag{15}
\]

We next prove equality in (15), rather than assuming linear-programming duality.  Because \(b\) is component-balanced, an augmented-graph flow with balance \(b\) exists: route each component's demands along a spanning tree and split the resulting signed edge flow into nonnegative directions.  If \(\mathcal Q_Y(\mu)\ne\varnothing\), choose \(\beta^0\) in it.  Summing reduced costs relative to \(\beta^0\) around a directed cycle shows that every directed cycle has nonnegative cost.  Any feasible flow decomposes into finitely many source-to-sink path flows plus directed cycles.  Deleting its cycle flows preserves \(b\) and cannot raise cost.  The total path mass is bounded by \(\frac12\sum_v|b_v|\), and only finitely many simple paths exist.  Hence a minimizing sequence may be chosen in a bounded closed finite-dimensional set, so an optimal flow \(x^*\) exists.

Form the residual digraph at \(x^*\): retain every original arc with its cost and, whenever \(x^*_r>0\), add the reverse arc with cost \(-c_r\).  A negative residual cycle would permit a positive augmentation, no larger than the smallest flow on its reverse arcs, that preserves balance and strictly lowers cost.  Thus no such cycle exists.  Add a zero-cost source and let \(\delta_v\) be shortest-path distance to \(v\).  The shortest-path argument proved in Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint-exactness} gives, for every original arc \(r:u\to v\),
\[
 \delta_v-\delta_u\le c_r. \tag{16}
\]
If \(x^*_r>0\), its reverse residual arc also gives \(\delta_u-\delta_v\le-c_r\), so equality holds in (16).  Taking \(\beta^*_v=\delta_v\), (16) shows \(\beta^*\in\mathcal Q_Y(\mu)\), and complementary equality on every positive-flow arc yields
\[
 \sum_r c_rx^*_r
 =\sum_r x^*_r q_r^{\mathsf T}\beta^*
 =b^{\mathsf T}\beta^*.
\]
Together with (15), this proves the computable support-function identity
\[
 \max_{\beta\in\mathcal Q_Y(\mu)}b^{\mathsf T}\beta
 =\operatorname{MC}(b;d^-,d^+). \tag{17}
\]
This is the min-cost transshipment technique used computationally in modern flow algorithms such as \cite{ChenKyngLiuPengProbstGutenbergSachdeva2022}; all logical steps needed here were derived above.

For \(b=s(w)\), feasibility can also be seen directly because \(s_j\) is the incidence vector of the \(\mathcal K\)-edge \((g_j,t_j)\), so a signed flow with edge coefficient \(w_j\), split into its positive and negative parts, has balance \(s(w)\).  By Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp-att-region}, minimizing the aggregate subtracts the maximum of \(s(w)^{\mathsf T}\beta\), while maximizing it adds the maximum of \(-s(w)^{\mathsf T}\beta\).  Applying (17) twice gives
\[
 L_w=\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}w_j\nu_j-
 \operatorname{MC}(s(w);d^-,d^+),
 \qquad
 U_w=\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}w_j\nu_j+
 \operatorname{MC}(-s(w);d^-,d^+).
\]
The dual optimizer \(\beta^*\) is an attaining fixed-effect potential, \(a^*=\mu-B^{\mathsf T}\beta^*\) is its anticipation vector, and Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp-att-region} supplies an attaining full causal law.  The primal optimizer \(x^*\) and the zero reduced-cost equalities certify the endpoint.  Finally, the augmented part of Theorem~\ref{thm:endpoint-exactness} says exactly that \(\mathcal Q_Y(\mu)\ne\varnothing\) if and only if \(\mathcal D(d^-,d^+)\) has no negative directed cycle, completing every assertion.

Let \(M=|\mathcal E|+|\mathcal J|\) and \(R=\overline y-\underline y\).  If \(M=0\), there are no sample mean coordinates and the conclusion is deterministic, so consider \(M\ge1\).  The proof uses one conditional exponential-moment bound and a union bound; it does not require differentiability or stability of the endpoint map.

We first derive the needed bounded-mean inequality.  If \(X\in[\underline y,\overline y]\) and
\[
 g(\lambda)=\log E\exp\{\lambda(X-EX)\},
\]
then \(g(0)=g'(0)=0\), while \(g''(\lambda)\) is the variance under the exponentially tilted law.  Any random variable \(V\in[\underline y,\overline y]\) satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(V)
 \le(EV-\underline y)(\overline y-EV)
 \le R^2/4;
\]
the first inequality follows by taking expectations in \((V-\underline y)(\overline y-V)\ge0\), and the second by completing the square.  Twice integrating the bound on \(g''\) gives
\[
 E\exp\{\lambda(X-EX)\}\le\exp(\lambda^2R^2/8). \tag{18}
\]
For \(N\) independent copies, Markov's inequality, (18), and minimization over \(\lambda>0\) give
\[
 \Pr\{\overline X-EX\ge r\}
 \le\inf_{\lambda>0}\exp\{-\lambda Nr+N\lambda^2R^2/8\}
 =\exp\{-2Nr^2/R^2\}.
\]
Applying the same calculation to \(-X\) proves
\[
 \Pr\{|\overline X-EX|>r\}\le2\exp\{-2Nr^2/R^2\}. \tag{19}
\]
This is the self-contained bounded-variable calculation underlying \cite{Hoeffding1963}.

Condition on the complete vector of cohort assignments.  Under Assumption~\ref{ass:iid-sampling}, whenever \(N_h>0\), each cell mean in cohort \(h\) averages \(N_h\) conditionally independent variables having the appropriate conditional mean and range \(R\).  Substituting \(r=\rho_h\) in (19) gives failure probability at most \(\eta/M\) for each of the \(M\) coordinates.  A coordinate with \(N_h=0\) is covered surely because its factor is defined to be \([\underline y,\overline y]\).  The conditional union bound, followed by averaging over assignments, therefore proves
\[
 P_{\mathrm{obs}}^n\{(\mu,\nu)\in\mathcal R_{1-\eta}\}\ge1-\eta. \tag{20}
\]

On the event in (20), the particular population pair \((\mu,\nu)\) is one of the pairs in the union defining \(\widehat\Theta^{\mathrm{out}}_{1-\eta}(w)\).  Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp-att-region} then gives the deterministic inclusion
\[
 \Theta_I(w;P_{\mathrm{obs}})
 \subseteq\widehat\Theta^{\mathrm{out}}_{1-\eta}(w). \tag{21}
\]
Every \(P\in\mathfrak M(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\) induces the same observed law \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\), so (20)--(21) hold uniformly over that class and imply the displayed infimum bound.

It remains to verify the advertised computation.  For an observed edge let
\[
 \underline m_e=\max\{\widehat\mu_e-\rho_{h(e)},\underline y\},
 \qquad
 \overline m_e=\min\{\widehat\mu_e+\rho_{h(e)},\overline y\},
\]
with \([\underline m_e,\overline m_e]=[\underline y,\overline y]\) at zero count.  Eliminating \(\mu'_e\in[\underline m_e,\overline m_e]\) from
\[
 \mu'_e-u_e\le(B^{\mathsf T}\beta)_e\le\mu'_e-\ell_e
\]
is exact: the two intervals \([\underline m_e,\overline m_e]\) and \([(B^{\mathsf T}\beta)_e+\ell_e,(B^{\mathsf T}\beta)_e+u_e]\) intersect if and only if
\[
 \underline m_e-u_e\le(B^{\mathsf T}\beta)_e\le\overline m_e-\ell_e. \tag{22}
\]
The interval in (22) is precisely
\[
 [\widehat\mu_e-\rho_{h(e)}-u_e,
   \widehat\mu_e+\rho_{h(e)}-\ell_e]
 \cap[\underline y-u_e,\overline y-\ell_e].
\]
Retain \([\underline y,\overline y]\) on every \(\mathcal K\)-edge.  The endpoint proof for Theorem~\ref{thm:circulation-endpoints}, applied with these random tensions, optimizes the \(\beta\)-part.  If \([\underline n_j,\overline n_j]\) denotes the simultaneous band for \(\nu_j\), the target-mean part is optimized jointly but explicitly as
\[
 \min_{\nu'}\sum_jw_j\nu'_j
 =\sum_{w_j\ge0}w_j\underline n_j+
   \sum_{w_j<0}w_j\overline n_j,
\]
with lower and upper endpoints interchanged for the maximum.  Equations (22) and this signed box optimization constitute one finite interval-tension program whose projection is exactly the union in Definition~\ref{def:outer-confidence-set}.  The proof uses neither uniqueness nor a fixed active face; zero counts merely select full-support factors.  Hence singleton fibers, changing optimizer faces, and zero counts require no separate regularity argument.

%%% FIELD proof-known-corners
Each claim follows by direct substitution, which supplies exact algebraic unit tests.

First suppose \(\ell=u=0\), \(C\mu=0\), and \(\mathcal Q_Y(\mu)\ne\varnothing\).  Definition~\ref{def:cycle-fiber} gives
\[
 \mathcal A_{\mathrm{cyc}}(\mu)=\{a:a=0,\ C\mu=0\}=\{0\}.
\]
Under \(\mathsf{RA}\), the three clean edges for target \(j\) have zero anticipation.  Additivity of the baseline surface gives

 \alpha_{g_j}+\lambda_{t_j}
 = (\alpha_{g_j}+\lambda_{b_j})
 +(\alpha_{q_j}+\lambda_{t_j})
 -(\alpha_{q_j}+\lambda_{b_j})
 =\mu_{(g_j,b_j)}+\mu_{(q_j,t_j)}-\mu_{(q_j,b_j)}.

Consistency at the treated target and Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp-att-region} therefore yield the singleton value
\[
 \theta_j=(\nu_j-\mu_{(g_j,b_j)})
 -(\mu_{(q_j,t_j)}-\mu_{(q_j,b_j)}).
\]
The overlap clause in \(\mathsf{RA}\) makes every comparison conditional mean well defined, and the three-edge rectangle places all four vertices in one component, eliminating a disconnected target gauge.

Second, if every edge of positive width is a bridge, Theorem~\ref{thm:variation-independence} removes every cycle coupling after the fixed-cycle compatibility equations are met.  Under the stated premise that the bounded-baseline inequalities remain slack throughout that primitive box, they remove no point or support optimizer, so \(\mathcal A_Y(\mu)\) projects onto the full product box.  Consequently, for any linear coefficient vector \(q\), its anticipation support values are the independent-box expressions
\[
 \max_a q^{\mathsf T}a
 =\sum_{q_e\ge0}q_eu_e+sum_{q_e<0}q_e\ell_e,
 \qquad
 \min_a q^{\mathsf T}a
 =\sum_{q_e\ge0}q_e\ell_e+sum_{q_e<0}q_eu_e.
\]
The transshipment values equal these same support values by Theorem~\ref{thm:circulation-endpoints}.  This proves the independent-box reduction asserted in the second corner.

Third, for the ordered four-cycle in the statement, write its coordinates as \((a_1,a_2,a_3,a_4)\).  The box and fixed coordinates give \(a_1,a_3\in[0,1]\) and \(a_2=a_4=0\), while the only cycle equation is
\[
 a_1-a_2+a_3-a_4=0.
\]
Thus \(a_1+a_3=0\), which forces \(a_1=a_3=0\).  The fiber is \(\{0\}\), so \(\mathcal I(\mu)=\varnothing\), and Theorem~\ref{thm:cycle-fiber} gives
\[
 \dim\operatorname{aff}\mathcal A_{\mathrm{cyc}}(\mu)
 =0-\operatorname{rank}(C_{\varnothing})=0.
\]
In contrast, \(\mathcal W=\{1,3\}\) and \(\operatorname{rank}(C_{\mathcal W})=1\), so the inapplicable unqualified expression is \(2-1=1\).

Finally consider \(\mathfrak W_3\).  The fixed clean coordinates give \(a_{h,2}=0\).  For any two cohorts \(h,h'\), the four-cycle equation is equivalently equality of their baseline time differences:
\[
 (\mu_{h,2}-a_{h,2})-(\mu_{h,3}-a_{h,3})
 =(\mu_{h',2}-a_{h',2})-(\mu_{h',3}-a_{h',3}). \tag{23}
\]
Substituting the specified rational means into (23) gives
\[
 a_{4,3}-\tfrac12=a_{5,3}=a_{6,3}=:z.
\]
The three intervals \([-1,1]\) imply simultaneously
\[
 -1\le z\le1,
 \qquad -1\le z+\tfrac12\le1,
\]
whose intersection is \([-1,1/2]\).  Conversely, every \(z\) in this intersection satisfies all equations and bounds, so Theorem~\ref{thm:cycle-fiber} proves
\[
 \mathcal A_{\mathrm{cyc}}(\mu)
 =\{a:a_{h,2}=0,\ (a_{4,3},a_{5,3},a_{6,3})=(z+1/2,z,z),\ -1\le z\le1/2\}.
\]
It follows that \(a_{4,3}-a_{5,3}=1/2\), and
\[
 \psi_3={a_{4,3}+a_{5,3}\over2}=z+\tfrac14
 \in[-\tfrac34,\tfrac34].
\]
Both endpoints are attained at \(z=-1\) and \(z=1/2\), respectively.  Over the uncoupled square \([-1,1]^2\), the same average has support \([-1,1]\), proving the strict contraction.
