%%% FIELD def-local-branch-construction
Fix a population raw branch \(W\) and a radius \(r>0\) such that the closed Frobenius ball \(\mathcal B_r(W)=\{U:\lVert U-W\rVert_F\leq r\}\) contains no other zero of \(F_{\mathrm{res}}(\,cdot\,;z)\).  For a moment vector \(\zeta\), whenever
\[
 \underset{U\in\mathcal B_r(W)}{\operatorname{argmin}}\,
 \lVert F_{\mathrm{res}}(U;\zeta)\rVert_2^2
\]
is a singleton, define \(\widehat W_{\mathrm{loc}}(\zeta)\) to be its unique element.  Thus the construction is a fixed-ball global least-squares selector and is left undefined at moment vectors for which the displayed argmin is not a singleton.

%%% FIELD proof-block-certificate
By \(\text{ass:strict-decrease}\), every \(H_j\) is positive definite, so all square roots and inverses below exist.  Define
\[
 U=H_1^{-1/2}WH_0^{1/2}.
\]
The first congruence in \(\text{def:raw-fiber}\) is equivalent to
\[
 UU^{\mathsf T}=H_1^{-1/2}WH_0W^{\mathsf T}H_1^{-1/2}=I_p.
\]
Because \(U\) is square, this is equivalent to \(U\in O(p)\), and the inverse change of variables is \(W=H_1^{1/2}UH_0^{-1/2}\).  Substitution in the second congruence gives
\[
 URU^{\mathsf T}=S,
 \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad SU=UR.
\]
The real symmetric matrices \(R\) and \(S\) admit an orthogonal intertwiner only if their spectra, including multiplicities, agree.  Conversely, if they agree, the relation \(SU=UR\) says that \(U\) maps each \(\lambda\)-eigenspace of \(R\) isometrically onto the corresponding eigenspace of \(S\).  Relative to the declared orthonormal frames this gives, uniquely,
\[
 U=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}Q_\lambda O_\lambda P_\lambda^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad O_\lambda=Q_\lambda^{\mathsf T}UP_\lambda\in O(m_\lambda).
\]

The two mean equations in \(\text{def:raw-fiber}\) combine as
\[
 U H_0^{-1/2}[d_0,d_1]=H_1^{-1/2}[d_1,d_2].
\]
Projection onto the \(\lambda\)-eigenspace of \(S\) is therefore exactly
\[
 O_\lambda A_\lambda=B_\lambda.
\]
Orthogonality makes the block Gram identity necessary:
\[
 B_\lambda^{\mathsf T}B_\lambda
 =A_\lambda^{\mathsf T}O_\lambda^{\mathsf T}O_\lambda A_\lambda
 =A_\lambda^{\mathsf T}A_\lambda.
\]

For sufficiency, suppose that the spectra match and the Gram identity holds in every block.  On \(\operatorname{ran}(A_\lambda)\) define
\[
 L_\lambda(A_\lambda c)=B_\lambda c,
 \qquad c\in\mathbb R^2.
\]
This is well defined, since \(A_\lambda c=0\) implies
\[
 \lVert B_\lambda c\rVert_2^2
 =c^{\mathsf T}B_\lambda^{\mathsf T}B_\lambda c
 =c^{\mathsf T}A_\lambda^{\mathsf T}A_\lambda c=0.
\]
Polarization of the same identity shows that \(L_\lambda\) is an isometry onto \(\operatorname{ran}(B_\lambda)\).  Both ranges consequently have dimension \(r_\lambda\).  Choose orthonormal frames \(F_A,F_B\) for these ranges so that \(L_\lambda F_A=F_B\), and orthonormal frames \(N_A,N_B\) for their orthogonal complements.  Every orthogonal extension, and no other one, is
\[
 O_\lambda=F_BF_A^{\mathsf T}+N_BZ_\lambda N_A^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad Z_\lambda\in O(k_\lambda).
\]
It satisfies \(O_\lambda A_\lambda=B_\lambda\).  Thus the block completion set is a torsor for \(O(k_\lambda)\); the conventions are that \(O(0)\) is trivial and \(O(1)=\{-1,1\}\).  Since the tangent space of \(O(k)\) consists of skew-symmetric \(k\)-by-\(k\) matrices, it has dimension \(k(k-1)/2\).  Substitution into the inverse whitening map yields, exactly once for each collection of completions,
\[
 W=H_1^{1/2}\left(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
 Q_\lambda O_\lambda P_\lambda^{\mathsf T}\right)H_0^{-1/2}.
\]
This proves the nonemptiness certificate, the parameterization, and every asserted block dimension.

%%% FIELD proof-admissible-fiber
The structural assumptions first imply observable restrictions.  By \(\text{ass:linear-flow}\), \(\text{ass:initial-gaussian}\), \(\text{ass:common-measurement-noise}\), and \(\text{ass:measurement-independence}\),
\[
 m_k=V^k(\mu_0-x^\star)+x^\star,
 \qquad C_k=V^k\Sigma(V^{\mathsf T})^k+E.
\]
Consequently common noise cancels and successive means difference to
\[
 H_j=V^j(\Sigma-V\Sigma V^{\mathsf T})(V^{\mathsf T})^j,
 \qquad d_j=V^j(V-I_p)(\mu_0-x^\star).
\]
Hence
\[
 VH_0V^{\mathsf T}=H_1,quad VH_1V^{\mathsf T}=H_2,quad
 Vd_0=d_1,quad Vd_1=d_2,
\]
so the transition of every structural explanation belongs to \(\mathcal F_{\mathrm{raw}}\).

If \(g\) is an eigenvalue of \(G\), spectral mapping gives an eigenvalue \(e^{hg}\) of \(V\).  Assumptions \(\text{ass:spacing}\) and \(\text{ass:hurwitz}\) give \(|e^{hg}|<1\), hence \(\rho(V)<1\).  A negative real value would require \(\operatorname{Im}(hg)=\pi\) modulo \(2\pi\), which \(\text{ass:alias-strip}\) excludes; zero is impossible for an exponential.  Thus \(\operatorname{spec}(V)\cap(-\infty,0]=\varnothing\).  Moreover
\[
 \Sigma-V\Sigma V^{\mathsf T}=H_0.
\]
The stable Stein equation has a unique solution, as shown below, so \(T_V=\Sigma\) and \(E_V=C_0-T_V=E\succ0\).  Every explanation therefore yields a member of \(\mathcal F_{\mathrm{adm}}\).

Conversely, fix \(W\in\mathcal F_{\mathrm{adm}}\).  Because \(\rho(W)<1\), the series
\[
 T_W=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}W^jH_0(W^{\mathsf T})^j
\]
converges and satisfies \(T_W-WT_WW^{\mathsf T}=H_0\).  Its zeroth summand and \(H_0\succ0\) imply \(T_W\succ0\).  It is unique because vectorization of the homogeneous equation gives
\[
 (I_{p^2}-W\otimes W)\operatorname{vec}(T)=0,
\]
and every eigenvalue of \(W\otimes W\) is a product of two eigenvalues of \(W\), of modulus less than one.  Set \(E_W=C_0-T_W\), which is positive definite by \(\text{def:admissible-fiber}\).  Then
\[
 WT_WW^{\mathsf T}+E_W=T_W-H_0+C_0=C_1.
\]
For \(\widetilde C_k=W^kT_W(W^{\mathsf T})^k+E_W\),
\[
 \widetilde C_k-\widetilde C_{k+1}=W^kH_0(W^{\mathsf T})^k.
\]
The two raw congruences make the right side equal to \(H_k\) for \(k=0,1,2\).  Since \(\widetilde C_0=C_0\), telescoping yields \(\widetilde C_k=C_k\) for all \(k\in K\).

Stability gives \(1\notin\operatorname{spec}(W)\), so define
\[
 x_W^\star=(I_p-W)^{-1}(m_1-Wm_0),
 \qquad \widetilde m_0=m_0,
 \qquad \widetilde m_{k+1}=W\widetilde m_k+(I_p-W)x_W^\star.
\]
Then \(\widetilde m_1=m_1\), and its increments satisfy
\[
 \widetilde m_{k+1}-\widetilde m_k
 =W(\widetilde m_k-\widetilde m_{k-1}).
\]
The raw equations \(Wd_0=d_1\) and \(Wd_1=d_2\) therefore imply \(\widetilde m_2=m_2\) and \(\widetilde m_3=m_3\).  The reconstructed initial mean is \(\mu_0=m_0\).

The spectral-cut condition places \(W\) in the domain of the principal matrix logarithm.  On a complex Jordan block with eigenvalue \(w\), its eigenvalue is \(\log|w|+i\operatorname{Arg}(w)\), with \(-\pi<\operatorname{Arg}(w)<\pi\).  Conjugate blocks give conjugate logarithms, so \(\operatorname{Log}(W)\) is real.  Thus
\[
 G_W=h^{-1}\operatorname{Log}(W)
\]
is real, has imaginary parts in the required alias strip, and is Hurwitz because \(|w|<1\).  Also \(\exp(hG_W)=W\).  If a competing real logarithm \(L\) has spectrum in the same open strip, the holomorphic identity \(\operatorname{Log}(e^z)=z\) on that strip and matrix functional calculus give \(L=\operatorname{Log}(e^L)=\operatorname{Log}(W)\).  Hence the generator is unique.

Take \(X_0\sim\mathcal N(m_0,T_W)\), generate the deterministic affine trajectory using \((W,x_W^\star)\), and add mutually independent \(\mathcal N(0,E_W)\) errors independent of \(X_0\).  The preceding equations give exactly the means \(m_k\) and covariances \(C_k\); Gaussian laws are determined by these moments, so all four observed marginal laws are reproduced.  Necessity recovers each coordinate of any explanation from its transition, while \(\theta(W,z)\) contains \(W\) itself.  The map is therefore both surjective and injective.  Its image is precisely the structural identified set, and it is a singleton exactly when \(\mathcal F_{\mathrm{adm}}\) is a singleton.

%%% FIELD proof-three-snapshot-classification
By \(\text{ass:strict-decrease}\), \(H_0,H_1\succ0\).  Put
\[
 a=H_0^{-1/2}d_0,\qquad c=H_1^{-1/2}d_1,\qquad
 U=H_1^{-1/2}WH_0^{1/2}.
\]
The inverse map is \(W=H_1^{1/2}UH_0^{-1/2}\), and direct multiplication gives
\[
 WH_0W^{\mathsf T}=H_1\Longleftrightarrow U\in O(p),
 \qquad Wd_0=d_1\Longleftrightarrow Ua=c.
\]
Thus \(\mathcal F_3\) is in bijection with \(\{U\in O(p):Ua=c\}\).  Necessity of the norm condition follows from \(U^{\mathsf T}U=I_p\).  Conversely, if \(\lVert a\rVert_2=\lVert c\rVert_2>0\), extend the two normalized vectors to orthonormal bases and map one basis to the other; if the common norm is zero, every orthogonal matrix works.  This proves the exact nonemptiness certificate.

Fix a solution \(U_0\).  Every solution is uniquely \(U_0R_0\), where
\[
 R_0\in\operatorname{Stab}(a)=\{R\in O(p):Ra=a\}.
\]
If \(a=0\), the stabilizer is \(O(p)\).  If \(a\ne0\), it fixes \(\operatorname{span}(a)\) pointwise and is arbitrary orthogonal on \(a^\perp\), hence isomorphic to \(O(p-1)\).  Since \(r=\operatorname{rank}(a)\in\{0,1\}\), the fiber is a torsor for \(O(p-r)\).  The tangent space of \(O(s)\) consists of the skew-symmetric \(s\)-by-\(s\) matrices, so
\[
 \dim\mathcal F_3=\frac{(p-r)(p-r-1)}2.
\]

We next identify, rather than define, the filtered set.  Let \(W\in\mathcal F_3\) pass the three strict filters.  Stability makes
\[
 T_W=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}W^jH_0(W^{\mathsf T})^j
\]
convergent, positive definite, and the unique solution of \(T_W-WT_WW^{\mathsf T}=H_0\), by the same Kronecker-product argument used in \(\text{thm:admissible-fiber}\).  Put
\[
 E'=C_0-T_W\succ0,\qquad \mu'_0=m_0,\qquad
 x'=(I_p-W)^{-1}(m_1-Wm_0).
\]
The inverse exists because \(\rho(W)<1\).  The spectral cut and \(h>0\) give the unique real generator \(G'=h^{-1}\operatorname{Log}(W)\), which is Hurwitz and lies in the open alias strip, exactly as in \(\text{thm:admissible-fiber}\).

Generate \(X'_0\sim\mathcal N(m_0,T_W)\), the affine deterministic trajectory with \((W,x')\), and independent \(\mathcal N(0,E')\) measurement errors.  The equilibrium equation gives the means \(m_0,m_1\), while
\[
 Wm_1+(I_p-W)x'=m_1+W(m_1-m_0)=m_1+d_1=m_2
\]
uses \(Wd_0=d_1\).  For covariances,
\[
 T_W+E'=C_0,\qquad WT_WW^{\mathsf T}+E'=C_1,
\]
and the congruence \(WH_0W^{\mathsf T}=H_1\) gives
\[
 W^2T_W(W^{\mathsf T})^2+E'
 =C_0-H_0-H_1=C_2.
\]
Hence this construction reproduces the three Gaussian marginal laws.

Conversely, any structural explanation has
\[
 C_k=V^k\Sigma(V^{\mathsf T})^k+E,\qquad
 m_k=V^k(\mu_0-x^\star)+x^\star.
\]
Cancellation and differencing give \(VH_0V^{\mathsf T}=H_1\) and \(Vd_0=d_1\).  The Hurwitz, spacing, and alias-strip assumptions give stability and cut avoidance, while the unique Stein solution gives \(T_V=\Sigma\) and \(C_0-T_V=E\succ0\).  Thus the filtered intersection is exactly the transition identified set.  The filters are relatively open: stability and cut avoidance are open spectral conditions, and
\[
 \operatorname{vec}(T_W)=(I_{p^2}-W\otimes W)^{-1}\operatorname{vec}(H_0)
\]
is continuous on the stable set, after which strict positive definiteness is open.  Therefore every admissible point in a positive-dimensional torsor has a positive-dimensional local ambiguity.

If \(p=2\) and \(d_0\ne0\), then \(r=1\) and the stabilizer is \(O(1)=\{-1,1\}\).  In an orthonormal basis adapted to \(a\), its two elements are \(\operatorname{diag}(1,1)\) and \(\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\), so the two raw completions have opposite determinants; the whitening factors have positive determinant and do not change this conclusion.  A real matrix of negative determinant has a negative real eigenvalue: nonreal eigenvalues occur in conjugate pairs whose products are positive, so a negative product of all eigenvalues requires an unpaired negative real eigenvalue.  The negative-determinant completion therefore fails the principal-logarithm cut.  At most one completion is admissible, and model compatibility supplies the true admissible completion, so exactly one remains and its full reconstruction is unique.

At a true explanation,
\[
 d_0=(V-I_p)(\mu_0-x^\star).
\]
Because \(\rho(V)<1\), \(V-I_p\) is invertible; hence \(d_0=0\) if and only if \(\mu_0=x^\star\), a proper affine locus in the mean parameter.  Thus \(d_0\ne0\) is open and dense.  If \(d_0=0\), the norm certificate is equivalent to \(d_1=0\), and the torsor is \(O(p)\), of dimension \(p(p-1)/2\), positive already for \(p=2\).  Finally, any positive-dimensional family constructed in this deterministic Gaussian class remains observationally equivalent in a larger unknown-common-noise model containing it with the same transition target.  This proves precisely the stated transfer of nonidentification.

%%% FIELD proof-snapshot-frontier
The three-snapshot certificate and exact local dimensions follow from \(\text{thm:three-snapshot-exact-classification}\).  For \(p=2\) and \(d_0\ne0\), it gives two opposite-determinant raw completions and proves that the negative-determinant member fails the principal-logarithm cut.  Model compatibility supplies one admissible member, hence the transition and, by the Stein, logarithm, equilibrium, and initial-mean reconstructions, the full explanation are unique.  If \(p=2\) and \(d_0=0\), a relatively open part of an \(O(2)\) torsor remains and has dimension one.  If \(p\ge3\), the local dimension is
\[
 \frac{(p-1)(p-2)}2>0\quad\text{when }d_0\ne0,
 \qquad
 \frac{p(p-1)}2>0\quad\text{when }d_0=0.
\]
Moreover, \(d_0=(V-I_p)(\mu_0-x^\star)\), and \(V-I_p\) is invertible because \(\rho(V)<1\).  Thus \(d_0=0\) is exactly the proper affine locus \(\mu_0=x^\star\), so \(d_0\ne0\) is generic in the mean parameter.  This proves the three-snapshot side of the frontier.

For four snapshots, \(H_0,H_1,H_2\succ0\) by \(\text{ass:strict-decrease}\).  If \(W\in\mathcal F_{\mathrm{raw}}\), then \(WH_0W^{\mathsf T}=H_1\succ0\) makes \(W\) invertible.  Using both raw congruences,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 M_1W
 &=H_2H_1^{-1}W\\
 &=(WH_1W^{\mathsf T})(WH_0W^{\mathsf T})^{-1}W\\
 &=WH_1W^{\mathsf T}W^{-\mathsf T}H_0^{-1}W^{-1}W
 =WH_1H_0^{-1}=WM_0.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Since \(d_1=Wd_0\), induction gives
\[
 M_1^jd_1=WM_0^jd_0,\qquad 0\leq j<p.
\]
Columnwise this is \(K_1=WK_0\).  Hence, whenever \(\det K_0\ne0\), every raw candidate equals the observable rational map
\[
 W=K_1K_0^{-1}=\mathcal K((m_k),(C_k)).
\]
In particular the causal transition, which was defined structurally before this calculation, satisfies \(V=K_1K_0^{-1}\); therefore there is at most one raw candidate on the cyclic chart.

To show genericity, choose pairwise distinct \(v_i\in(0,1)\) and set
\[
 V=\operatorname{diag}(v_1,\ldots,v_p),\qquad
 G=h^{-1}\operatorname{diag}(\log v_1,\ldots,\log v_p),
 \qquad \Sigma=E=I_p.
\]
Choose \(u=\mu_0-x^\star\) with every coordinate nonzero.  Then all assumptions and strict filters hold, and
\[
 H_j=\operatorname{diag}\bigl(v_i^{2j}(1-v_i^2)\bigr)\succ0,\qquad
 M_0=\operatorname{diag}(v_1^2,\ldots,v_p^2),\qquad
 d_0=(V-I_p)u.
\]
The \(i\)-th row of \(K_0\) is
\[
 d_{0i}(1,v_i^2,\ldots,v_i^{2(p-1)}),
\]
so the Vandermonde formula gives
\[
 \det K_0=\left(\prod_{i=1}^pd_{0i}\right)
 \prod_{1\leq i<j\leq p}(v_j^2-v_i^2)\ne0.
\]
On \(\det H_0\ne0\), each entry of \(M_0=H_1\operatorname{adj}(H_0)/\det H_0\), and hence \(\det K_0\), is rational in the structural parameters.  Clearing a power of \(\det H_0\) yields a polynomial numerator.  The example makes that numerator nonzero, so it is not the zero polynomial.  A nonzero real polynomial has a zero set with empty interior, by induction on the number of variables using the finite-root property in one variable.  Thus failure of cyclicity lies in a proper algebraic subset of the admissible parameter domain.

On the complement, the rational map gives the only raw candidate.  For a model-compatible law it is the true \(V\), so it passes all filters; for an arbitrary proposed law the filters in \(\text{def:admissible-fiber}\) are the existence check.  The bijection in \(\text{thm:admissible-fiber}\) then reconstructs the unique generator, latent covariance, measurement covariance, equilibrium, and initial mean.  Thus a fourth snapshot is generically necessary for \(p\ge3\) and generically sufficient on the cyclic chart, with the stated dimension-two elbow.

%%% FIELD proof-information-nullity
Let \(A\) perturb a compatible transition \(W\).  Differentiating \(\text{def:residual-map}\) gives
\[
 D_WF[A]=\bigl(
 \operatorname{vech}(AH_0W^{\mathsf T}+WH_0A^{\mathsf T}),
 \operatorname{vech}(AH_1W^{\mathsf T}+WH_1A^{\mathsf T}),
 Ad_0,Ad_1\bigr).
\]
Set
\[
 U=H_1^{-1/2}WH_0^{1/2}\in O(p),\qquad
 Z=H_1^{-1/2}AH_0^{1/2}.
\]
The first linearized congruence is \(ZU^{\mathsf T}+UZ^{\mathsf T}=0\), so \(Z=UJ\) for a unique skew-symmetric \(J\).  Substitution in the second linearized congruence yields
\[
 JR+RJ^{\mathsf T}=0,\qquad\text{hence}\qquad JR=RJ.
\]
The mean equations become
\[
 JH_0^{-1/2}d_0=0,\qquad JH_0^{-1/2}d_1=0.
\]
Since \(R\) is symmetric, commutation makes \(J\) block diagonal in the eigenspaces of \(R\).  In block \(\lambda\), the last equations say that \(J_\lambda\) annihilates \(\operatorname{ran}(A_\lambda)\).  Relative to this \(r_\lambda\)-dimensional range and its orthogonal complement,
\[
 J_\lambda=0_{r_\lambda}\oplus\widetilde J_\lambda,\qquad
 \widetilde J_\lambda^{\mathsf T}=-\widetilde J_\lambda,\qquad
 \widetilde J_\lambda\in\mathbb R^{k_\lambda\times k_\lambda}.
\]
All changes of variables are invertible, and the skew-symmetric \(k\)-by-\(k\) matrices have dimension \(k(k-1)/2\).  Therefore
\[
 \dim\ker(D_WF)=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
 \frac{k_\lambda(k_\lambda-1)}2.
\]

Consider next the full structural coordinate vector \((W,T,E,x^\star,\mu_0)\).  A zero moment derivative has zero derivatives of every \(H_j,d_j\), so its \(W\)-component belongs to the kernel just computed.  Conversely, for \(A\in\ker(D_WF)\), the perturbation \(\dot T\) that keeps \(H_0=T-WTW^{\mathsf T}\) fixed is uniquely determined by
\[
 \dot T-W\dot TW^{\mathsf T}=ATW^{\mathsf T}+WTA^{\mathsf T},
\]
because \(\rho(W)<1\) makes the Stein operator invertible.  Keeping \(C_0=T+E\) fixed forces \(\dot E=-\dot T\); keeping \(m_0=\mu_0\) fixed forces \(\dot\mu_0=0\); and keeping
\[
 m_1=W\mu_0+(I_p-W)x^\star
\]
fixed uniquely forces
\[
 \dot x^\star=-(I_p-W)^{-1}A(\mu_0-x^\star).
\]
The equations \(Ad_0=Ad_1=0\) then keep \(m_2,m_3\) fixed, and the two linearized congruences keep \(C_2,C_3\) fixed.  Projection onto \(A\) is consequently a linear bijection between the full moment-map kernel and \(\ker(D_WF)\).  The principal logarithm is locally a smooth inverse of the exponential on the alias-strip chart, so replacing \(W\) by \(G_W\) does not change nullity.

For one nonsingular Gaussian marginal, a mean perturbation \(u\) and symmetric covariance perturbation \(A_C\) have Fisher quadratic form
\[
 u^{\mathsf T}C^{-1}u+
 \frac12\operatorname{tr}(C^{-1}A_CC^{-1}A_C).
\]
This follows by differentiating the Gaussian log density and taking its second moment.  Writing \(A_C=C^{1/2}BC^{1/2}\), the second term is \(\operatorname{tr}(B^2)/2\), so the form vanishes exactly when \(u=0\) and \(A_C=0\).  Under \(\text{ass:unpaired-sampling}\), the destructive experiment consists of independent samples from the four marginal laws; its Fisher form is the positive sum of the four marginal forms and has exactly the full moment-map kernel.  Its nullity is therefore the displayed stabilizer dimension.  It is zero exactly when every \(k_\lambda\leq1\), which by \(\text{thm:block-certificate}\) is exactly the finite raw-stratum condition.

%%% FIELD proof-cyclic-perturbation
Use operator norm for matrices, Euclidean norm for vectors, and Frobenius-induced operator norm on maps over \(\mathbb S^p\).  Write \(\Delta A=\widehat A-A\).  The coordinatewise perturbation condition gives
\[
 \lVert\Delta H_j\rVert\leq2\epsilon,\qquad
 \lVert\Delta d_j\rVert_2\leq2\epsilon.
\]
If \(\epsilon\leq a_0/4\), then \(\widehat H_i\succeq(a_0/2)I_p\) for \(i=0,1\), so
\[
 \lVert\widehat H_i^{-1}\rVert\leq2/a_0,\qquad
 \lVert H_i^{-1}\rVert\leq1/a_0.
\]
The inverse identity \(\widehat H_i^{-1}-H_i^{-1}=-\widehat H_i^{-1}(\Delta H_i)H_i^{-1}\) implies
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \lVert\widehat M_i-M_i\rVert
 &\leq \lVert\Delta H_{i+1}\rVert\lVert\widehat H_i^{-1}\rVert
 +\lVert H_{i+1}\rVert\lVert\widehat H_i^{-1}\rVert
   \lVert\Delta H_i\rVert\lVert H_i^{-1}\rVert\\
 &\leq\left(\frac4{a_0}+\frac{4B_{\mathrm{norm}}}{a_0^2}\right)\epsilon
 =c_M\epsilon.
 \end{aligned}
\]
If also \(\epsilon\leq1/c_M\), then the definition of \(L_M\) gives \(\max(\lVert M_i\rVert,\lVert\widehat M_i\rVert)\leq L_M\).  The telescoping identity
\[
 \widehat M_i^j-M_i^j
 =\sum_{\ell=0}^{j-1}\widehat M_i^\ell
 (\widehat M_i-M_i)M_i^{j-1-\ell}
\]
therefore yields
\[
 \lVert\widehat M_i^j\widehat d_i-M_i^jd_i\rVert_2
 \leq\bigl(2L_M^j+jL_M^{j-1}c_MD_{\mathrm{norm}}\bigr)\epsilon,
\]
with the second summand zero for \(j=0\).  Summing squared column bounds and using operator norm no larger than Frobenius norm proves
\[
 \lVert\widehat K_i-K_i\rVert\leq c_K\epsilon,\qquad i=0,1.
\]

On the cyclic chart, \(\kappa=\sigma_{\min}(K_0)>0\) and \(K_1=VK_0\) by \(\text{thm:snapshot-frontier}\).  If \(\epsilon\leq\kappa/(2c_K)\), then
\[
 \sigma_{\min}(\widehat K_0)\geq\kappa-c_K\epsilon\geq\kappa/2,
 \qquad \lVert\widehat K_0^{-1}\rVert\leq2/\kappa.
\]
Thus \(\widehat W_{\mathrm{cyc}}\) is defined and
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \widehat W_{\mathrm{cyc}}-V
 &=\bigl((\widehat K_1-K_1)-V(\widehat K_0-K_0)\bigr)\widehat K_0^{-1},\\
 \lVert\widehat W_{\mathrm{cyc}}-V\rVert
 &\leq\frac{2c_K(1+\lVert V\rVert)}\kappa\epsilon=c_V\epsilon.
 \end{aligned}
\]

Now suppose \(\epsilon<\epsilon_{\mathrm{rec}}\) and abbreviate \(\widehat W=\widehat W_{\mathrm{cyc}}\).  The definitions of \(\delta_{\mathrm{stab}}\) and \(\delta_{\log}\), together with
\[
 \lVert\widehat W-V\rVert<c_V\epsilon
 <\min\{\delta_{\mathrm{stab}}/2,\delta_{\log}/2,1\},
\]
show that \(\rho(\widehat W)<1\) and \(\operatorname{spec}(\widehat W)\cap(-\infty,0]=\varnothing\).  For the Stein operators,
\[
 (\mathcal L_{\widehat W}-\mathcal L_V)(A)
 =-(\widehat W-V)A\widehat W^{\mathsf T}-VA(\widehat W-V)^{\mathsf T},
\]
and \(\lVert\widehat W\rVert\leq\lVert V\rVert+1\), hence
\[
 \lVert\mathcal L_{\widehat W}-\mathcal L_V\rVert_{F\to F}
 \leq c_V(2\lVert V\rVert+1)\epsilon<\sigma_{\mathrm{Stein}}/2.
\]
Factoring \(\mathcal L_{\widehat W}\) through \(\mathcal L_V\) and applying the geometric inverse series gives
\[
 \lVert\mathcal L_{\widehat W}^{-1}\rVert_{F\to F}
 \leq2\sigma_{\mathrm{Stein}}^{-1}.
\]
Subtracting the population and perturbed Stein equations gives
\[
 \mathcal L_{\widehat W}(\widehat T_{\mathrm{cyc}}-T_V)
 =\Delta H_0-(\mathcal L_{\widehat W}-\mathcal L_V)(T_V).
\]
Since \(\lVert\Delta H_0\rVert_F\leq2\sqrt p\epsilon\),
\[
 \lVert\widehat T_{\mathrm{cyc}}-T_V\rVert
 \leq2\sigma_{\mathrm{Stein}}^{-1}
 [2\sqrt p+c_V(2\lVert V\rVert+1)\lVert T_V\rVert_F]\epsilon
 =c_T\epsilon.
\]
It follows that
\[
 \lVert\widehat E_{\mathrm{cyc}}-E_V\rVert
 \leq\lVert\widehat C_0-C_0\rVert+
 \lVert\widehat T_{\mathrm{cyc}}-T_V\rVert
 \leq c_E\epsilon.
\]
If \(\epsilon<\epsilon_{\mathrm{adm}}\), Weyl's variational inequality gives
\[
 \lambda_{\min}(\widehat T_{\mathrm{cyc}})>t_{\min}/2>0,
 \qquad \lambda_{\min}(\widehat E_{\mathrm{cyc}})>e_{\min}/2>0.
\]

For the remaining coordinates, let
\[
 \mathcal B=\{U:\lVert U-V\rVert\leq\delta_{\log}/2\},\qquad
 Q_t(U)=I_p+t(U-I_p).
\]
Every \(Q_t(U)\) is invertible for \(U\in\mathcal B\) and \(t\in[0,1]\): for \(t>0\), singularity would put \(-(1-t)/t\) in \(\operatorname{spec}(U)\cap(-\infty,0]\).  Compactness therefore makes
\[
 q_0=\sup_{U\in\mathcal B,,0\leq t\leq1}\lVert Q_t(U)^{-1}\rVert<\infty,
 \qquad b_0=\sup_{U\in\mathcal B}\lVert U-I_p\rVert<\infty.
\]
Jordan-block integration of the scalar identity
\(
 \frac{d}{dt}\log(1+t(z-1))=(z-1)/(1+t(z-1))
\)
gives
\[
 \operatorname{Log}(U)=\int_0^1(U-I_p)Q_t(U)^{-1}\,dt.
\]
The resolvent identity then gives, for \(A,B\in\mathcal B\),
\[
 \lVert\operatorname{Log}(A)-\operatorname{Log}(B)\rVert
 \leq\left(q_0+\frac{b_0q_0^2}{2}\right)\lVert A-B\rVert.
\]
Thus the generator error is at most
\[
 h^{-1}\left(q_0+\frac{b_0q_0^2}{2}\right)c_V\epsilon.
\]

Let \(\eta=\lVert(I_p-V)^{-1}\rVert\).  The distance from \(I_p-V\) to singular matrices is \(\eta^{-1}\), and a singular \(I_p-U\) implies \(\rho(U)\geq1\); hence \(\delta_{\mathrm{stab}}\leq\eta^{-1}\).  Therefore \(\eta\lVert\widehat W-V\rVert<1/2\), and a geometric inverse series yields
\[
 \lVert(I_p-\widehat W)^{-1}\rVert\leq2\eta.
\]
Subtracting the two equilibrium equations gives the exact identity
\[
 (I_p-\widehat W)(\widehat x^\star-x^\star)
 =\Delta m_1-\widehat W\Delta m_0+(\widehat W-V)(x^\star-m_0),
\]
and hence
\[
 \lVert\widehat x^\star-x^\star\rVert
 \leq2\eta[2+\lVert V\rVert+c_V(\lVert m_0\rVert+\lVert x^\star\rVert)]\epsilon.
\]
Finally, \(\widehat\mu_0=\widehat m_0\), so \(\lVert\widehat\mu_0-\mu_0\rVert\leq\epsilon\).  Every operation in the full plug-in is therefore defined for \(\epsilon<\epsilon_{\mathrm{rec}}\), and all strict covariance filters hold for \(\epsilon<\epsilon_{\mathrm{adm}}\).  Only the population identity \(K_1=VK_0\) was used; no empirical spectral match, probe-Gram equality, or exact residual equality was imposed.

%%% FIELD proof-branchwise-inference
Identify \(U\) with \(u=\operatorname{vec}(U)\), write \(f(u;\zeta)=F_{\mathrm{res}}(U;\zeta)\), and define
\[
 Q(u;\zeta)=\frac12\lVert f(u;\zeta)\rVert_2^2,
 \qquad \Psi(u;\zeta)=D_uf(u;\zeta)^{\mathsf T}f(u;\zeta).
\]
At the exact branch \((w,z)\), \(f(w;z)=0\), and therefore
\[
 D_u\Psi(w;z)=D_uf(w;z)^{\mathsf T}D_uf(w;z).
\]
The condition \(\gamma_W>0\) makes this matrix positive definite, with least eigenvalue \(\gamma_W^2\).  Put \(A_0=[D_u\Psi(w;z)]^{-1}\) and \(\Phi_\zeta(u)=u-A_0\Psi(u;\zeta)\).  Since \(D_u\Phi_z(w)=0\), continuity supplies a closed ball \(B_0\) about \(w\), contained in the interior of the fixed isolating ball, and a neighborhood \(\mathcal Z_0\) of \(z\) such that every \(\Phi_\zeta\) maps \(B_0\) into itself and has a common Lipschitz constant smaller than one.  Iteration is then Cauchy at a geometric rate, and completeness of \(B_0\) gives a unique fixed point \(u(\zeta)\).  It is the unique stationary point of \(Q(\,\cdot\,;\zeta)\) in \(B_0\).  Continuity of the Hessian from its positive-definite value at \((w,z)\) makes this point a strict local minimizer.

We now prove that the definition's global selector equals this contraction-selected point; nonzero-residual local minima elsewhere in the isolating ball are irrelevant.  Choose a smaller open neighborhood \(B_1\) of \(w\) whose closure lies in \(B_0\).  At \(z\), the only zero of \(f(\,\cdot\,;z)\) in the fixed closed ball is \(w\).  The compact complement of \(B_1\) in that ball therefore has a positive minimum
\[
 c=\min_{u\in\mathcal B_r(w)\setminus B_1}Q(u;z)>0.
\]
Uniform continuity on this compact set gives, after shrinking \(\mathcal Z_0\),
\[
 \inf_{u\in\mathcal B_r(w)\setminus B_1}Q(u;\zeta)>c/2,
 \qquad Q(w;\zeta)<c/2.
\]
Continuity and compactness ensure that a global minimizer over the fixed ball exists, and these inequalities force every such minimizer into \(B_1\).  It is then an interior stationary point and hence equals the unique contraction fixed point \(u(\zeta)\).  Thus the argmin in the corrected \(\text{def:local-branch-estimator}\) is a singleton throughout \(\mathcal Z_0\), despite any extraneous nonzero-residual local minima outside \(B_1\).

The map \(f\) is polynomial in \(u\) and affine in the moment coordinates, hence smooth.  Differentiating \(\Psi(u(\zeta);\zeta)=0\) at \(z\) is legitimate because \(D_u\Psi(w;z)\) is invertible; equivalently, subtracting the fixed-point equations and using the inverse linearization gives
\[
 Du(z)[a]=-\bigl(D_WF^{\mathsf T}D_WF\bigr)^{-1}
 D_WF^{\mathsf T}D_zF[a].
\]
Repeated differentiation gives smoothness.  Strict stability, logarithm-cut, Stein, covariance-positivity, and equilibrium margins make every operation in \(\theta(U,\zeta)\) smooth near \((W,z)\): the Stein coordinate follows by inversion of its nonsingular linear operator, the principal logarithm by its local resolvent integral, and the equilibrium by inversion of \(I_p-U\).  Hence
\[
 g(\zeta)=\theta(U(\zeta),\zeta)
\]
is smooth and \(Dg(z)=J_\theta\).

It remains to derive the sampling limit.  For snapshot \(k\), write \(Y_k=m_k+Z_k\) with \(Z_k\sim\mathcal N(0,C_k)\), and define the centered single-observation moment vector
\[
 \xi_k=\bigl(Z_k,\operatorname{vech}(Z_kZ_k^{\mathsf T}-C_k)\bigr).
\]
It has finite moments of every order.  Differentiating the Gaussian moment-generating function gives
\[
 \mathbb E[Z_{ki}Z_{kj}Z_{k\ell}]=0,\qquad
 \operatorname{Cov}(Z_{ki}Z_{kj},Z_{kr}Z_{ks})
 =(C_k)_{ir}(C_k)_{js}+(C_k)_{is}(C_k)_{jr}.
\]
Thus the mean and covariance influence blocks are uncorrelated.  If \(L_p\) is the elimination matrix and \(\mathsf K_p\) the commutation matrix, the snapshot contribution in interlaced coordinates is
\[
 \Omega_k=\frac1{\pi_k}\operatorname{diag}\left(
 C_k,\ L_p(I_{p^2}+\mathsf K_p)(C_k\otimes C_k)L_p^{\mathsf T}\right).
\]
The usual sample covariance has the same first-order influence function: centering by the sample mean changes the average of \(Z_kZ_k^{\mathsf T}\) by an outer product of order \(O_{\mathbb P}(n_k^{-1})\), and using divisor \(n_k-1\) changes it by another \(O_{\mathbb P}(n_k^{-1})\) term.

For any fixed vector \(t\), finite second moments give
\[
 \mathbb E\exp\{it^{\mathsf T}\xi_k/\sqrt n\}
 =1-\frac{t^{\mathsf T}\operatorname{Cov}(\xi_k)t}{2n}+o(n^{-1}),
\]
so its \(n\)-th power converges to the Gaussian characteristic function.  By \(\text{ass:unpaired-sampling}\), samples from different snapshot labels are independent; by \(\text{ass:sample-shares}\), \(n_k/N\to\pi_k\in(0,1)\).  Combining the four characteristic functions yields
\[
 \sqrt N(\widehat z-z)\Longrightarrow\mathcal N(0,\Omega),
\]
where, if \(\mathsf P\) permutes the interlaced ordering into the declared ordering of \(z\),
\[
 \Omega=\mathsf P\operatorname{diag}(\Omega_0,\Omega_1,\Omega_2,\Omega_3)\mathsf P^{\mathsf T}.
\]
All correlations induced later by forming adjacent differences are retained because those differences are fixed linear maps of this full moment vector.

Taylor expansion of the smooth branch map now gives
\[
 \sqrt N\{g(\widehat z)-g(z)\}
 =J_\theta\sqrt N(\widehat z-z)+o_{\mathbb P}(1),
\]
and therefore
\[
 \sqrt N\bigl(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{loc}}-\theta(W,z)\bigr)
 \Longrightarrow\mathcal N(0,J_\theta\Omega J_\theta^{\mathsf T}).
\]
The last coordinate is \(\widehat\mu_0=\widehat m_0\).  Sample moments converge to their population values, the sample shares converge to \(\pi_k\), and the unique local solution converges to \(W\).  The displayed Gaussian fourth-moment formula and continuity of the branch derivatives then show that replacing \(C_k,\pi_k,W,J_\theta\), and \(\Omega\) by their empirical plug-in versions consistently estimates \(J_\theta\Omega J_\theta^{\mathsf T}\).  This is branchwise inference conditional on the specified regular population branch and requires no exact empirical spectral or Gram equality.

%%% FIELD proof-sharp-exceptional-fibers
By \(\text{thm:block-certificate}\), a nonempty raw fiber is a product of torsors for \(O(k_\lambda)\).  Since \(O(0)\) is a singleton, \(O(1)=\{-1,1\}\), and \(O(k)\) has positive dimension \(k(k-1)/2\) for \(k\ge2\), the raw fiber is finite exactly when every \(k_\lambda\leq1\), and then
\[
 \#\mathcal F_{\mathrm{raw}}=2^{\#\{\lambda:k_\lambda=1\}}=2^q.
\]
If some \(k_\lambda\ge2\), the strict admissibility filters are relatively open on the product torsor: stability and cut avoidance are open spectral conditions, while
\[
 \operatorname{vec}(T_W)=(I_{p^2}-W\otimes W)^{-1}\operatorname{vec}(H_0)
\]
is continuous on the stable set and strict positivity of \(C_0-T_W\) is open.  Every admissible candidate then lies in a positive-dimensional relatively open fiber.

On a finite stratum with \(q\ge1\), flip the residual sign in one block with \(k_\lambda=1\).  This is a fixed-point-free involution of the raw fiber and reverses \(\det(W)\), because the whitening factors have positive determinants.  Hence exactly \(2^{q-1}\) candidates have each determinant sign.  Every raw candidate is invertible.  If a real invertible matrix avoids \(( -\infty,0]\), its real eigenvalues are positive and each nonreal conjugate pair has positive product, so its determinant is positive.  Therefore every negative-determinant candidate fails the logarithm cut and
\[
 \#\mathcal F_{\mathrm{adm}}\leq2^{q-1}.
\]
If \(q=0\), the raw fiber already has one member.  Since \(q\leq\sum_\lambda k_\lambda\leq\sum_\lambda m_\lambda=p\), every finite admissible fiber has at most \(2^{p-1}\) members.

We construct equality.  Let \(Q_\circ e_i=e_{i+1}\) for \(i<p\) and \(Q_\circ e_p=e_1\).  For \(D=\operatorname{diag}(s_1,\ldots,s_p)\), \(s_i\in\{-1,1\}\),
\[
 (Q_\circ D)^p=\left(\prod_{i=1}^ps_i\right)I_p,\qquad
 \det(Q_\circ)=(-1)^{p-1}.
\]
If \(\det(Q_\circ D)>0\), then \(\prod_i s_i=(-1)^{p-1}\).  The eigenvalue \(-1\) is impossible, since the power identity would imply \((-1)^p=(-1)^{p-1}\).  An orthogonal matrix has no other negative real eigenvalue, so every orientation-preserving \(Q_\circ D\) avoids the logarithm cut.

Fix \(\alpha\in(0,1)\).  Cut avoidance of the finitely many matrices \(\alpha Q_\circ D\) with positive determinant persists for all positive diagonal \(A\) sufficiently close to \(\alpha I_p\).  Indeed, otherwise sequences \(A_n\to\alpha I_p\) and \(t_n\ge0\) with \(\det(t_nI_p+A_nQ_\circ D)=0\) would have a bounded convergent subsequence \(t_n\to t\), contradicting cut avoidance of \(\alpha Q_\circ D\).  Choose in the common neighborhood
\[
 A=\operatorname{diag}(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_p),\qquad
 0<\alpha_i<1,\qquad \alpha_i^2\ne\alpha_j^2\ (i\ne j).
\]
Then every \(AQ_\circ D\) is stable because its norm is \(\max_i\alpha_i<1\), and every positive-determinant one avoids the cut.

Set
\[
 d_0=d_1=d_2=0,\qquad H_0=I_p,\qquad H_1=A^2,\qquad
 H_2=AQ_\circ A^2Q_\circ^{\mathsf T}A.
\]
All differences are positive definite.  Here \(R=A^2\) and \(S=Q_\circ A^2Q_\circ^{\mathsf T}\), with distinct one-dimensional spectral blocks and zero probes.  Thus \(m_\lambda=1\), \(r_\lambda=0\), \(k_\lambda=1\), and \(q=p\).  The block certificate yields exactly the \(2^p\) matrices
\[
 W_D=AQ_\circ D.
\]
Directly,
\[
 W_DH_0W_D^{\mathsf T}=A^2=H_1,\qquad
 W_DH_1W_D^{\mathsf T}=AQ_\circ A^2Q_\circ^{\mathsf T}A=H_2,
\]
because \(DA^2D=A^2\), and the mean equations are automatic.

For each \(D\), let
\[
 T_D=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}W_D^j(W_D^{\mathsf T})^j,
\]
so \(T_D\succ0\) and \(T_D-W_DT_DW_D^{\mathsf T}=I_p\).  Choose
\[
 c>\max\left\{1+\lambda_{\max}(H_0+H_1+H_2),
 \max_D\lambda_{\max}(T_D)\right\}
\]
and define
\[
 C_0=cI_p,quad C_1=C_0-H_0,quad C_2=C_1-H_1,quad
 C_3=C_2-H_2,quad m_0=m_1=m_2=m_3=0.
\]
All \(C_k\) are positive definite.  For each branch, \(E_D=C_0-T_D\succ0\), and the Stein equation plus the two congruences gives
\[
 C_k=W_D^kT_D(W_D^{\mathsf T})^k+E_D,\qquad 0\leq k\leq3.
\]
Thus these are genuine strict-decrease Gaussian snapshot laws under every branch.

Exactly half of the sign matrices make \(\det(W_D)>0\).  All of those \(2^{p-1}\) branches are stable, avoid the cut, and have \(E_D\succ0\), hence are admissible.  Every negative-determinant branch has a negative real eigenvalue by the conjugate-pair product argument and fails the cut.  Consequently
\[
 \#\mathcal F_{\mathrm{adm}}=2^{p-1}.
\]
The bijection in \(\text{thm:admissible-fiber}\) turns these into exactly \(2^{p-1}\) distinct structural explanations, proving sharpness.
