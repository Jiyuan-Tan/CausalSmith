Use the operator norm for ordinary matrices, the Euclidean norm for vectors, and the operator norm induced by the Frobenius norm for linear maps on \(\mathbb S^p\).  Put \(\Delta A=\widehat A-A\).  By the perturbation hypothesis and the definitions of \(H_j\) and \(d_j\),
\[
 \lVert\Delta H_j\rVert
 =\lVert(\widehat C_j-C_j)-(\widehat C_{j+1}-C_{j+1})\rVert
 \le 2\epsilon,
 \qquad
 \lVert\Delta d_j\rVert
 \le 2\epsilon.
\]
Assumption \(\text{ass:strict-decrease}\) gives \(a_0>0\).  If \(\epsilon\le a_0/4\), then for every unit vector \(u\) and \(i\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
 u^{\mathsf T}\widehat H_i u
 \ge u^{\mathsf T}H_i u-\lVert\Delta H_i\rVert
 \ge a_0-2\epsilon
 \ge \frac{a_0}{2}.
\]
Thus \(\widehat H_i\succ0\), and the variational characterization of the least eigenvalue gives
\[
 \lVert\widehat H_i^{-1}\rVert\le \frac{2}{a_0},
 \qquad
 \lVert H_i^{-1}\rVert\le \frac{1}{a_0}.
\]
For \(i\in\{0,1\}\), the definitions in \(\text{def:krylov-map}\) and \(\text{def:empirical-cyclic-estimator}\) give \(M_i=H_{i+1}H_i^{-1}\) and \(\widehat M_i=\widehat H_{i+1}\widehat H_i^{-1}\).  The inverse identity
\[
 \widehat H_i^{-1}-H_i^{-1}
 =-\widehat H_i^{-1}(\Delta H_i)H_i^{-1}
\]
therefore yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \lVert\widehat M_i-M_i\rVert
 &\le \lVert\Delta H_{i+1}\rVert\lVert\widehat H_i^{-1}\rVert
 +\lVert H_{i+1}\rVert\lVert\widehat H_i^{-1}\rVert
   \lVert\Delta H_i\rVert\lVert H_i^{-1}\rVert\\
 &\le \left(\frac{4}{a_0}+\frac{4B_{\mathrm{norm}}}{a_0^2}\right)\epsilon
 =c_M\epsilon.
 \end{aligned}
\]
If also \(\epsilon\le1/c_M\), then \(\lVert\widehat M_i-M_i\rVert\le1\); hence the definition of \(L_M\) implies
\[
 \max\{\lVert M_i\rVert,\lVert\widehat M_i\rVert\}\le L_M.
\]
For every integer \(j\ge1\), the telescoping identity
\[
 \widehat M_i^j-M_i^j
 =\sum_{\ell=0}^{j-1}\widehat M_i^\ell
   (\widehat M_i-M_i)M_i^{j-1-\ell}
\]
and submultiplicativity give
\[
 \lVert\widehat M_i^j-M_i^j\rVert
 \le jL_M^{j-1}c_M\epsilon.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \lVert\widehat M_i^j\widehat d_i-M_i^jd_i\rVert
 &\le \lVert\widehat M_i^j\rVert\lVert\Delta d_i\rVert
 +\lVert\widehat M_i^j-M_i^j\rVert\lVert d_i\rVert\\
 &\le \left(2L_M^j+jL_M^{j-1}c_MD_{\mathrm{norm}}\right)\epsilon.
 \end{aligned}
\]
For \(j=0\), the same inequality holds with the second summand equal to zero.  The squared Frobenius norm of a matrix is the sum of the squared Euclidean norms of its columns, and its operator norm is at most its Frobenius norm.  The definition of \(c_K\) therefore gives, simultaneously for \(i=0,1\),
\[
 \lVert\widehat K_i-K_i\rVert\le c_K\epsilon.
\]

On the population cyclic chart, \(\det K_0\ne0\), so \(\kappa=\sigma_{\min}(K_0)>0\).  The rational recovery identity in \(\text{thm:snapshot-frontier}\) is \(K_1=VK_0\).  If \(\epsilon\le\kappa/(2c_K)\), then
\[
 \sigma_{\min}(\widehat K_0)
 \ge \sigma_{\min}(K_0)-\lVert\widehat K_0-K_0\rVert
 \ge \frac{\kappa}{2}.
\]
Thus \(\widehat K_0\) is invertible and \(\lVert\widehat K_0^{-1}\rVert\le2/\kappa\).  Using \(K_1=VK_0\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \widehat W_{\mathrm{cyc}}-V
 &=\widehat K_1\widehat K_0^{-1}-V\widehat K_0\widehat K_0^{-1}\\
 &=\bigl((\widehat K_1-K_1)-V(\widehat K_0-K_0)\bigr)\widehat K_0^{-1}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
It follows that
\[
 \lVert\widehat W_{\mathrm{cyc}}-V\rVert
 \le \frac{2c_K(1+\lVert V\rVert)}{\kappa}\epsilon
 =c_V\epsilon.
\]
This proves the first assertion under the three displayed restrictions on \(\epsilon\).

Suppose next that \(\epsilon<\epsilon_{\mathrm{rec}}\), and abbreviate \(\widehat W=\widehat W_{\mathrm{cyc}}\).  Every one of the first three restrictions just used is then satisfied.  Moreover, the terms \(1/c_V\), \(\delta_{\mathrm{stab}}/(2c_V)\), and \(\delta_{\log}/(2c_V)\) in the definition of \(\epsilon_{\mathrm{rec}}\) imply
\[
 \lVert\widehat W-V\rVert
 \le c_V\epsilon
 <\min\left\{1,\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{stab}}}{2},
                    \frac{\delta_{\log}}{2}\right\}.
\]
By the definitions of \(\delta_{\mathrm{stab}}\) and \(\delta_{\log}\), a matrix at this distance from \(V\) belongs neither to \(\{U:\rho(U)\ge1\}\) nor to \(\{U:\operatorname{spec}(U)\cap(-\infty,0]\ne\varnothing\}\).  Hence
\[
 \rho(\widehat W)<1,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{spec}(\widehat W)\cap(-\infty,0]=\varnothing.
\]
Thus the stability and principal-logarithm filters are preserved.

We next verify the Stein reconstruction.  For \(A\in\mathbb S^p\), direct expansion of the definition of \(\mathcal L_U\) gives
\[
 (\mathcal L_{\widehat W}-\mathcal L_V)(A)
 =-(\widehat W-V)A\widehat W^{\mathsf T}
  -VA(\widehat W-V)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Because \(\lVert\widehat W-V\rVert<1\), one has \(\lVert\widehat W\rVert\le\lVert V\rVert+1\), and therefore
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \lVert\mathcal L_{\widehat W}-\mathcal L_V\rVert_{F\to F}
 &\le \lVert\widehat W-V\rVert
       (\lVert\widehat W\rVert+\lVert V\rVert)\\
 &\le c_V(2\lVert V\rVert+1)\epsilon
 <\frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{Stein}}}{2}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The last strict inequality is the final restriction in \(\epsilon_{\mathrm{rec}}\).  By \(\text{thm:admissible-fiber}\), the population explanation has \(\rho(V)<1\).  Equivalently, the eigenvalues of the vectorized Stein operator are \(1-\lambda_a(V)\lambda_b(V)\), none of which is zero; hence \(\mathcal L_V\) is invertible.  By the definition of \(\sigma_{\mathrm{Stein}}\),
\[
 \lVert\mathcal L_V^{-1}\rVert_{F\to F}=\sigma_{\mathrm{Stein}}^{-1}.
\]
Factoring
\[
 \mathcal L_{\widehat W}
 =\mathcal L_V\left[I+\mathcal L_V^{-1}
   (\mathcal L_{\widehat W}-\mathcal L_V)\right]
\]
shows that the operator inside brackets differs from the identity by norm less than \(1/2\).  Its geometric inverse series converges, so \(\mathcal L_{\widehat W}\) is invertible and
\[
 \lVert\mathcal L_{\widehat W}^{-1}\rVert_{F\to F}
 \le2\sigma_{\mathrm{Stein}}^{-1}.
\]

The population and perturbed Stein equations are
\[
 \mathcal L_V(T_V)=H_0,
 \qquad
 \mathcal L_{\widehat W}(\widehat T_{\mathrm{cyc}})=\widehat H_0.
\]
Subtracting them and adding and subtracting \(\mathcal L_{\widehat W}(T_V)\) gives
\[
 \mathcal L_{\widehat W}(\widehat T_{\mathrm{cyc}}-T_V)
 =\Delta H_0-(\mathcal L_{\widehat W}-\mathcal L_V)(T_V).
\]
Since \(\lVert\Delta H_0\rVert_F\le\sqrt p\lVert\Delta H_0\rVert\le2\sqrt p\,\epsilon\), the preceding bounds and the definition of \(c_T\) yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \lVert\widehat T_{\mathrm{cyc}}-T_V\rVert
 &\le\lVert\widehat T_{\mathrm{cyc}}-T_V\rVert_F\\
 &\le2\sigma_{\mathrm{Stein}}^{-1}
 \left[2\sqrt p+c_V(2\lVert V\rVert+1)\lVert T_V\rVert_F\right]\epsilon\\
 &=c_T\epsilon.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Using \(E_V=C_0-T_V\) and \(\widehat E_{\mathrm{cyc}}=\widehat C_0-\widehat T_{\mathrm{cyc}}\), we further obtain the operator-norm bound
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \lVert\widehat E_{\mathrm{cyc}}-E_V\rVert
 &\le\lVert\widehat C_0-C_0\rVert
     +\lVert\widehat T_{\mathrm{cyc}}-T_V\rVert\\
 &\le(1+c_T)\epsilon
 =c_E\epsilon.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The bijection in \(\text{thm:admissible-fiber}\), together with the positive latent and measurement covariances in the frozen model, identifies \(T_V=\Sigma\succ0\) and \(E_V=E\succ0\).  If \(\epsilon<\epsilon_{\mathrm{adm}}\), the two additional restrictions in the definition of \(\epsilon_{\mathrm{adm}}\) and the variational formula for the least eigenvalue give
\[
 \lambda_{\min}(\widehat T_{\mathrm{cyc}})
 \ge t_{\min}-c_T\epsilon
 >\frac{t_{\min}}2>0
\]
and
\[
 \lambda_{\min}(\widehat E_{\mathrm{cyc}})
 \ge e_{\min}-c_E\epsilon
 >\frac{e_{\min}}2>0.
\]
Thus both covariance-positivity filters are preserved.

For completeness, we now derive a resolvent bound for the principal-logarithm coordinate.  Let
\[
 \mathcal B=\left\{U\in\mathbb R^{p\times p}:
 \lVert U-V\rVert\le\frac{\delta_{\log}}2\right\},
 \qquad
 Q_t(U)=I_p+t(U-I_p),\quad 0\le t\le1.
\]
The definition of \(\delta_{\log}\) makes \(\mathcal B\) a compact subset of the principal-logarithm domain.  For \(U\in\mathcal B\), the matrix \(Q_t(U)\) is invertible: this is immediate at \(t=0\), while for \(t>0\), singularity would imply
\[
 -\frac{1-t}{t}\in\operatorname{spec}(U)\cap(-\infty,0],
\]
a contradiction.  Continuity of matrix inversion on invertible matrices and compactness of \(\mathcal B\times[0,1]\) imply that the shifted-resolvent constants
\[
 q=\sup_{U\in\mathcal B,\,0\le t\le1}\lVert Q_t(U)^{-1}\rVert,
 \qquad
 b=\sup_{U\in\mathcal B}\lVert U-I_p\rVert
\]
are finite.

We also verify the integral representation used below.  If \(J=\lambda I+N\) is a complex Jordan block of \(U\), with \(N\) nilpotent, then the rational functional calculus gives the finite expansion
\[
 (J-I)Q_t(J)^{-1}
 =\sum_{r=0}^{\dim J-1}\frac{1}{r!}
 \left.\frac{\partial^r}{\partial z^r}
 \frac{z-1}{1+t(z-1)}\right|_{z=\lambda}N^r.
\]
For a scalar \(z\notin(-\infty,0]\), the segment \(1+t(z-1)\) avoids zero and the principal scalar logarithm obeys
\[
 \frac{d}{dt}\log(1+t(z-1))
 =\frac{z-1}{1+t(z-1)}.
\]
Integration from zero to one, followed by the finitely many derivatives with respect to \(z\), shows block by block that
\[
 \operatorname{Log}(U)
 =\int_0^1(U-I_p)Q_t(U)^{-1}\,dt.
\]
This also shows directly that the principal logarithm is real when \(U\) is real.

Put \(F_t(U)=(U-I_p)Q_t(U)^{-1}\).  For \(A,B\in\mathcal B\), the inverse identity gives
\[
 Q_t(A)^{-1}-Q_t(B)^{-1}
 =-tQ_t(A)^{-1}(A-B)Q_t(B)^{-1}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \lVert F_t(A)-F_t(B)\rVert
 &\le\lVert A-B\rVert\lVert Q_t(A)^{-1}\rVert\\
 &\quad+\lVert B-I_p\rVert
       \lVert Q_t(A)^{-1}-Q_t(B)^{-1}\rVert\\
 &\le(q+tbq^2)\lVert A-B\rVert.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Integrating this inequality yields the explicit local constant
\[
 L_{\log}=q+\frac{bq^2}{2}<\infty,
 \qquad
 \lVert\operatorname{Log}(A)-\operatorname{Log}(B)\rVert
 \le L_{\log}\lVert A-B\rVert.
\]
Both \(V\) and \(\widehat W\) lie in \(\mathcal B\), so
\[
 \left\lVert h^{-1}\operatorname{Log}(\widehat W)
       -h^{-1}\operatorname{Log}(V)\right\rVert
 \le h^{-1}L_{\log}c_V\epsilon.
\]
This is the asserted linear principal-logarithm bound, with a constant determined by shifted resolvents throughout the stated logarithm-margin ball.

It remains to control the equilibrium and initial mean.  Set
\[
 \eta=\lVert(I_p-V)^{-1}\rVert.
\]
This number is finite because \(\rho(V)<1\).  We first relate it to the frozen stability margin.  For every invertible real matrix \(A\), the operator-norm distance from \(A\) to the singular real matrices equals \(\sigma_{\min}(A)\): if \(A+R\) is singular and \(x\) is a unit null vector, then
\[
 \sigma_{\min}(A)\le\lVert Ax\rVert=\lVert Rx\rVert\le\lVert R\rVert,
\]
whereas a rank-one perturbation formed from left and right singular vectors corresponding to \(\sigma_{\min}(A)\) attains equality.  Apply this with \(A=I_p-V\).  Every \(U\) for which \(I_p-U\) is singular has \(1\in\operatorname{spec}(U)\), hence \(\rho(U)\ge1\).  Therefore
\[
 \delta_{\mathrm{stab}}
 \le\sigma_{\min}(I_p-V)
 =\eta^{-1}.
\]
Because \(\lVert\widehat W-V\rVert<\delta_{\mathrm{stab}}/2\), it follows that
\[
 \eta\lVert\widehat W-V\rVert<\frac12.
\]
The factorization
\[
 I_p-\widehat W
 =(I_p-V)\left[I_p-(I_p-V)^{-1}(\widehat W-V)\right]
\]
and the geometric inverse series now imply
\[
 \lVert(I_p-\widehat W)^{-1}\rVert\le2\eta.
\]
Thus the equilibrium coordinate in \(\text{def:parameter-map}\) is defined.

Write
\[
 x^\star=(I_p-V)^{-1}(m_1-Vm_0),
 \qquad
 \widehat x^\star=(I_p-\widehat W)^{-1}
 (\widehat m_1-\widehat W\widehat m_0).
\]
Subtracting the defining equilibrium equations gives the exact identity
\[
 (I_p-\widehat W)(\widehat x^\star-x^\star)
 =\Delta m_1-\widehat W\Delta m_0
  +(\widehat W-V)(x^\star-m_0).
\]
Since \(\lVert\widehat W\rVert\le\lVert V\rVert+1\), the preceding inverse bound and the perturbation assumptions imply
\[
 \lVert\widehat x^\star-x^\star\rVert
 \le2\eta\left[2+\lVert V\rVert
 +c_V(\lVert m_0\rVert+\lVert x^\star\rVert)\right]\epsilon.
\]
Finally, \(\text{def:parameter-map}\) defines the initial-mean coordinate as \(\mu_0=m_0\), and hence
\[
 \lVert\widehat\mu_0-\mu_0\rVert
 =\lVert\widehat m_0-m_0\rVert
 \le\epsilon.
\]

We have proved that, for \(\epsilon<\epsilon_{\mathrm{rec}}\), every inverse, logarithm, and Stein solve occurring in \(\theta(\widehat W,\widehat z)\) is defined, and we have supplied linear bounds for each coordinate.  For \(\epsilon<\epsilon_{\mathrm{adm}}\), the reconstructed latent and measurement covariances are additionally positive definite.  The only exact raw identity used was the population relation \(K_1=VK_0\).  At no point did the argument impose equality of empirical spectra, empirical probe Gram matrices, or empirical raw residuals.  Thus it establishes a well-defined off-model reconstruction from perturbed moments and does not assert that \(\widehat W_{\mathrm{cyc}}\) satisfies the exact population raw equations.
