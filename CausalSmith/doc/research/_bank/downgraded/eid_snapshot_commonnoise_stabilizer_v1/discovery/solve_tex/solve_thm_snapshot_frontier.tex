Assume \(p\ge 2\) and \(H_0,H_1\succ0\).  The three-snapshot raw fiber \(\mathcal F_3\) is nonempty if and only if
\[
 \lVert H_0^{-1/2}d_0\rVert_2=\lVert H_1^{-1/2}d_1\rVert_2.
\]
When this equality holds, put \(r=\operatorname{rank}(H_0^{-1/2}d_0)\in\{0,1\}\).  Under the bijection
\[
 U=H_1^{-1/2}WH_0^{1/2},
\]
the raw fiber is noncanonically a torsor for \(O(p-r)\), and its dimension is
\[
 \frac{(p-r)(p-r-1)}{2}.
\]
Its intersection with the strict filter set
\[
 \left\{W:\rho(W)<1,\ \operatorname{spec}(W)\cap(-\infty,0]=\varnothing,\ C_0-T_W\succ0\right\}
\]
is exactly the transition identified set for the three observed Gaussian snapshot laws and is relatively open in \(\mathcal F_3\).  Consequently every nonempty local admissible branch has the displayed dimension.  If \(d_0\ne0\) and \(p\ge3\), this dimension is positive.  If \(p=2\) and \(d_0\ne0\), the raw fiber consists of exactly two candidates with opposite determinants.  A real matrix with negative determinant has a negative real eigenvalue, so at most one of these candidates passes the principal-logarithm cut; for model-compatible observed laws exactly one passes all three filters, and the full structural explanation is identified.  This is the generic mean regime in dimension two.  If \(d_0=0\), nonemptiness is equivalent to \(d_1=0\), the raw fiber is a torsor for \(O(p)\), and continuous ambiguity occurs already at \(p=2\).  The continuous nonidentification conclusions for \(p\ge3\), and for \(d_0=0\) when \(p=2\), transfer to every unknown-common-measurement-covariance model whose model class contains the present stable, embeddable, deterministic Gaussian class as a subclass and has the same transition target.

By \(\text{ass:strict-decrease}\), the matrices \(H_0\) and \(H_1\) are positive definite, so their symmetric positive-definite square roots and inverse square roots exist.  Define
\[
 a=H_0^{-1/2}d_0,\qquad c=H_1^{-1/2}d_1,
 \qquad U=H_1^{-1/2}WH_0^{1/2}.
\]
The inverse change of variables is
\[
 W=H_1^{1/2}UH_0^{-1/2},
\]
so this is a bijection on \(\mathbb R^{p\times p}\).  Direct multiplication gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 WH_0W^{\mathsf T}=H_1
 &\Longleftrightarrow
 H_1^{-1/2}WH_0W^{\mathsf T}H_1^{-1/2}=I_p\\
 &\Longleftrightarrow UU^{\mathsf T}=I_p
 \Longleftrightarrow U\in O(p).
 \end{aligned}
\]
For the last equivalence, \(U\) is square and \(UU^{\mathsf T}=I_p\) makes it invertible, whence also \(U^{\mathsf T}U=I_p\).  Likewise,
\[
 Wd_0=d_1
 \Longleftrightarrow
 H_1^{-1/2}WH_0^{1/2}H_0^{-1/2}d_0=H_1^{-1/2}d_1
 \Longleftrightarrow Ua=c.
\]
Thus \(\text{def:three-fiber}\) gives the exact observable representation
\[
 \mathcal F_3\longleftrightarrow\{U\in O(p):Ua=c\}.
\]

If this set is nonempty, orthogonality gives
\[
 \lVert c\rVert_2^2=c^{\mathsf T}c
 =a^{\mathsf T}U^{\mathsf T}Ua
 =a^{\mathsf T}a=\lVert a\rVert_2^2.
\]
Conversely, suppose \(\lVert a\rVert_2=\lVert c\rVert_2\).  If the common norm is zero, then \(a=c=0\), and every \(U\in O(p)\) is a solution.  If it is positive, extend \(a/\lVert a\rVert_2\) and \(c/\lVert c\rVert_2\) to orthonormal bases of \(\mathbb R^p\).  The linear map carrying the first basis to the second is orthogonal and sends \(a\) to \(c\).  This proves both directions of the norm certificate.

Fix one solution \(U_0\).  Every other solution has the unique form
\[
 U=U_0R_0,
 \qquad
 R_0=U_0^{\mathsf T}U\in O(p),
 \qquad R_0a=a,
\]
and conversely every such \(R_0\) gives a solution.  Hence the solution set is a torsor for
\[
 \operatorname{Stab}(a)=\{R_0\in O(p):R_0a=a\}.
\]
If \(a=0\), this is \(O(p)\).  If \(a\ne0\), the orthogonal decomposition
\[
 \mathbb R^p=\operatorname{span}(a)\oplus a^\perp
\]
shows that a stabilizer element is the identity on \(\operatorname{span}(a)\) and is arbitrary orthogonal on \(a^\perp\); thus the stabilizer is isomorphic to \(O(p-1)\).  Since the rank of the one-column matrix \(a\) is zero or one according as \(a=0\) or \(a\ne0\), these cases are precisely \(O(p-r)\).

For completeness, the dimension is obtained from a local chart.  Differentiating \(Q^{\mathsf T}Q=I_s\) at \(I_s\) gives \(Z^{\mathsf T}+Z=0\), and a skew-symmetric \(s\)-by-\(s\) matrix has \(s(s-1)/2\) free upper-triangular entries.  Near zero and \(I_s\), respectively, the Cayley transforms
\[
 Z\longmapsto(I_s-Z)(I_s+Z)^{-1},
 \qquad
 Q\longmapsto(I_s-Q)(I_s+Q)^{-1}
\]
are inverse; transposition directly verifies that the first image is orthogonal and the second image is skew-symmetric.  Left multiplication gives the same chart at every point of \(O(s)\).  Taking \(s=p-r\) proves
\[
 \dim\mathcal F_3=\frac{(p-r)(p-r-1)}2.
\]

We next prove that the strict-filter intersection is the structural transition identified set, rather than defining compatibility by those filters.  Let \(W\in\mathcal F_3\) satisfy the three strict filters.  Since \(\rho(W)<1\), the Jordan normal form gives constants \(A<\infty\), \(b\in(0,1)\), and \(j_0\) such that \(\lVert W^j\rVert\le Ab^j\) for \(j\ge j_0\).  Therefore
\[
 T_W=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}W^jH_0(W^{\mathsf T})^j
\]
converges, is positive definite because its zeroth summand is \(H_0\succ0\), and satisfies
\[
 T_W-WT_WW^{\mathsf T}=H_0.
\]
It is unique: if \(D-WDW^{\mathsf T}=0\), vectorization yields
\[
 \bigl(I_{p^2}-W\otimes W\bigr)\operatorname{vec}(D)=0.
\]
The eigenvalues of \(W\otimes W\) are products of eigenvalues of \(W\), so its spectral radius is \(\rho(W)^2<1\); hence the displayed coefficient matrix is invertible and \(D=0\).

Set
\[
 E'=C_0-T_W\succ0,
 \qquad \mu'_0=m_0,
 \qquad x'=(I_p-W)^{-1}(m_1-Wm_0).
\]
The last inverse exists because \(\rho(W)<1\) implies \(1\notin\operatorname{spec}(W)\).  The spectral-cut condition implies that \(W\) is invertible and permits the real principal logarithm.  To verify the required properties, on a complex Jordan block \(J=\lambda I+N\), where \(N^s=0\), define
\[
 \operatorname{Log}(J)
 =\bigl(\log|\lambda|+i\operatorname{Arg}(\lambda)\bigr)I
 +\sum_{q=1}^{s-1}\frac{(-1)^{q+1}}q\left(\frac N\lambda\right)^q,
 \qquad -\pi<\operatorname{Arg}(\lambda)<\pi.
\]
The finite nilpotent logarithm and exponential series are inverse, so exponentiating returns \(J\).  Conjugate blocks receive conjugate logarithms, and therefore the resulting logarithm of the real matrix \(W\) is real.  With
\[
 G'=h^{-1}\operatorname{Log}(W),
\]
the positivity \(h>0\) from \(\text{ass:spacing}\) and \(|\lambda|<1\) give, for every eigenvalue \(g'\) corresponding to \(\lambda\),
\[
 \operatorname{Re}(g')=h^{-1}\log|\lambda|<0,
 \qquad
 -\frac\pi h<\operatorname{Im}(g')<\frac\pi h.
\]
Thus \(G'\) satisfies \(\text{ass:hurwitz}\) and \(\text{ass:alias-strip}\), and \(\exp(hG')=W\).  This generator is unique in the open alias strip: on that strip the scalar identity \(\operatorname{Log}(e^z)=z\) holds, and substitution into the finite power series on every Jordan block gives \(\operatorname{Log}(e^{hL})=hL\) for any competing logarithm \(L\) with spectrum in the strip.  Hence \(L=G'\).

Construct \(X'_0\sim\mathcal N(m_0,T_W)\), put
\[
 X'_k=W^k(X'_0-x')+x'\qquad(k=0,1,2),
\]
and take mutually independent \(\varepsilon'_k\sim\mathcal N(0,E')\), independently of \(X'_0\).  This construction satisfies \(\text{ass:linear-flow}\), \(\text{ass:initial-gaussian}\), \(\text{ass:common-measurement-noise}\), and \(\text{ass:measurement-independence}\).  Since
\[
 (I_p-W)x'=m_1-Wm_0,
\]
the first reconstructed mean is \(m_1\), and the equation \(Wd_0=d_1\) gives the second:
\[
 Wm_1+(I_p-W)x'
 =m_1+W(m_1-m_0)
 =m_1+Wd_0=m_1+d_1=m_2.
\]
For the covariances, the Stein identity and \(WH_0W^{\mathsf T}=H_1\) yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 T_W+E'&=C_0,\\
 WT_WW^{\mathsf T}+E'&=C_0-H_0=C_1,\\
 W^2T_W(W^{\mathsf T})^2+E'
 &=W(T_W-H_0)W^{\mathsf T}+E'\\
 &=T_W-H_0-WH_0W^{\mathsf T}+E'\\
 &=C_0-H_0-H_1=C_2.
 \end{aligned}
\]
All variables are Gaussian, so these mean and covariance equalities prove equality of the three marginal laws.

Conversely, every structural explanation obeying the frozen assumptions has
\[
 C_k=V^k\Sigma(V^{\mathsf T})^k+E,
 \qquad
 m_k=V^k(\mu_0-x^\star)+x^\star.
\]
Cancellation of the common \(E\) and subtraction of successive means give
\[
 VH_0V^{\mathsf T}=H_1,
 \qquad Vd_0=d_1,
\]
so \(V\in\mathcal F_3\).  The eigenvalues of \(V=\exp(hG)\) are \(e^{hg}\) for eigenvalues \(g\) of \(G\).  By \(\text{ass:hurwitz}\), \(\text{ass:spacing}\), and \(\text{ass:alias-strip}\),
\[
 \rho(V)<1,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{spec}(V)\cap(-\infty,0]=\varnothing.
\]
Moreover \(\Sigma-V\Sigma V^{\mathsf T}=H_0\); uniqueness of the stable Stein solution gives \(T_V=\Sigma\), and hence \(C_0-T_V=E\succ0\).  Thus every structural transition belongs to the strict-filter intersection.  Together with the preceding construction, this proves equality of that observable intersection and the three-law transition identified set.  It also proves that a surviving \(W\) uniquely determines the generator, latent covariance, common measurement covariance, equilibrium, and initial mean.

The filter set is open.  For stability, if \(W_n\to W\) and each \(W_n\) had an eigenvalue \(\lambda_n\) with \(|\lambda_n|\ge1\), boundedness of \(\lVert W_n\rVert\) would give a convergent subsequence; continuity of \(\det(\lambda I_p-W)\) would then give an eigenvalue of \(W\) with modulus at least one.  This contradicts \(\rho(W)<1\).  The same subsequence argument with \(\lambda_n\in(-\infty,0]\) proves openness of the spectral-cut condition.  On the stable set,
\[
 \operatorname{vec}(T_W)
 =\bigl(I_{p^2}-W\otimes W\bigr)^{-1}\operatorname{vec}(H_0)
\]
is continuous in \(W\), and continuity of the least eigenvalue makes \(C_0-T_W\succ0\) open.  The strict-filter intersection is therefore relatively open in \(\mathcal F_3\).  At every model-compatible point it contains the true transition, so a positive-dimensional torsor supplies a continuous local family of structural explanations.

It remains to analyze the dimension-two elbow.  Because \(H_0^{-1/2}\) is invertible, \(d_0\ne0\) is equivalent to \(a\ne0\), hence to \(r=1\).  When \(p=2\), the stabilizer is \(O(1)=\{-1,1\}\), so the raw fiber has exactly two members.  In an orthonormal basis whose first vector is \(a/\lVert a\rVert_2\), the two stabilizer matrices are
\[
 R_+=\operatorname{diag}(1,1),
 \qquad R_-=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1).
\]
They have opposite determinants.  Since the determinants of \(H_1^{1/2}\) and \(H_0^{-1/2}\) are positive, the corresponding two matrices
\[
 W_\pm=H_1^{1/2}U_0R_\pm H_0^{-1/2}
\]
also have opposite determinants.

We now prove the determinant obstruction.  Let \(A\) be a real matrix with \(\det(A)<0\).  Its nonreal eigenvalues, counted with algebraic multiplicity, occur in conjugate pairs, and each pair contributes \(z\overline z=|z|^2>0\) to the product of all eigenvalues.  Since the total product is the negative real number \(\det(A)\), at least one unpaired real eigenvalue is negative.  Thus \(\operatorname{spec}(A)\cap(-\infty,0)\ne\varnothing\).  Applied to \(W_+\) and \(W_-\), this shows that their negative-determinant member fails the principal-logarithm cut.  Therefore the strict-filter intersection has cardinality at most one.  For model-compatible laws it contains the true transition by the converse argument above, so its cardinality is exactly one.  The reconstruction already proved then identifies the full structural explanation.

At a true explanation,
\[
 d_0=m_1-m_0=(V-I_p)(\mu_0-x^\star).
\]
Because \(\rho(V)<1\), the matrix \(V-I_p\) is invertible.  Hence \(d_0=0\) if and only if \(\mu_0=x^\star\), a proper affine locus in the mean parameter; its complement is open and dense.  This proves the generic dimension-two claim.  If \(d_0=0\), then \(a=0\), and the norm certificate is equivalent to \(c=0\), hence to \(d_1=0\).  The torsor is then \(O(p)\), with dimension \(p(p-1)/2\), which is positive already for \(p=2\).

Finally, in the positive-dimensional cases, every pair or continuum of deterministic Gaussian explanations constructed above remains observationally equivalent in any larger unknown-common-measurement-covariance model that contains this class and has the same transition target.  Identification in a class implies identification in each subclass; contraposition proves the stated transfer of nonidentification.  No such transfer claim is made for the identified \(p=2\), \(d_0\ne0\) regime.

For model-compatible three-snapshot laws, the exact dimension-dependent frontier is as follows.  If \(p=2\) and \(d_0\ne0\), the two raw \(O(1)\) completions have opposite determinants, the negative-determinant completion fails the principal-logarithm cut, and the admissible transition and full structural explanation are unique.  If \(p=2\) and \(d_0=0\), a continuous local \(O(2)\) ambiguity remains.  If \(p\ge3\), every model-compatible law has continuous local ambiguity: its dimension is \((p-1)(p-2)/2\) when \(d_0\ne0\) and \(p(p-1)/2\) when \(d_0=0\).  The condition \(d_0\ne0\) is generic in the mean parameter.  With four snapshots, \(\det K_0\ne0\) implies the rational recovery identity \(V=K_1K_0^{-1}\), and \(\det K_0\ne0\) holds outside a proper algebraic subset of the admissible parameter domain.  Thus three snapshots generically identify the full explanation when \(p=2\); a fourth snapshot is generically necessary when \(p\ge3\) and is generically sufficient on the cyclic chart whenever the recovered candidate passes the admissibility filters.
%%% FIELD frontier-proof
The exact three-snapshot compatibility certificate, torsor classification, strict-filter characterization, and local dimensions are proved in \(\text{thm:three-snapshot-exact-classification}\).  In particular, for model-compatible laws the filtered intersection is nonempty.  When \(p=2\) and \(d_0\ne0\), that theorem gives two raw candidates of opposite determinant and proves that the negative-determinant candidate has a negative real eigenvalue.  It therefore fails the principal-logarithm cut, while the true transition passes all filters.  Exactly one admissible transition remains, and the Stein, logarithm, equilibrium, and initial-mean reconstructions give exactly one full structural explanation.  When \(p=2\) and \(d_0=0\), the same theorem gives a relatively open local piece of an \(O(2)\) torsor, of dimension one.  For \(p\ge3\), it gives dimension
\[
 \frac{(p-1)(p-2)}2>0
\]
when \(d_0\ne0\), and dimension
\[
 \frac{p(p-1)}2>0
\]
when \(d_0=0\).  These are continuous local ambiguity fibers.

At a true structural explanation, \(\text{ass:linear-flow}\) gives
\[
 d_0=(V-I_p)(\mu_0-x^\star).
\]
Every eigenvalue of \(V=\exp(hG)\) is \(e^{hg}\) for an eigenvalue \(g\) of \(G\).  Thus \(\text{ass:hurwitz}\) and \(h>0\) from \(\text{ass:spacing}\) imply \(\rho(V)<1\), so \(V-I_p\) is invertible.  It follows that
\[
 d_0=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mu_0=x^\star.
\]
For fixed admissible \(V\) and \(x^\star\), this is a proper affine subset of the mean parameter \(\mu_0\), and its complement is open and dense.  Hence \(d_0\ne0\) is generic.  Combining this fact with the preceding cases proves that three snapshots generically identify when \(p=2\), whereas they generically fail to identify when \(p\ge3\).  Therefore an additional snapshot is generically necessary in dimensions at least three.

We now prove the four-snapshot recovery assertion from the observable equations.  By \(\text{ass:strict-decrease}\), \(H_0,H_1,H_2\) are positive definite and hence invertible.  If \(W\in\mathcal F_{\mathrm{raw}}\), then \(WH_0W^{\mathsf T}=H_1\succ0\) implies \(\operatorname{rank}(W)=p\), so \(W\) is invertible.  The two covariance congruences in \(\text{def:raw-fiber}\) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 M_1W
 &=H_2H_1^{-1}W\\
 &=(WH_1W^{\mathsf T})(WH_0W^{\mathsf T})^{-1}W\\
 &=WH_1W^{\mathsf T}W^{-\mathsf T}H_0^{-1}W^{-1}W\\
 &=WH_1H_0^{-1}=WM_0.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The first mean equation is \(d_1=Wd_0\).  By induction,
\[
 M_1^jd_1=M_1^jWd_0=WM_0^jd_0
 \qquad(0\le j<p).
\]
Taking these vectors as columns yields
\[
 K_1=WK_0.
\]
Consequently, on the cyclic chart \(\det K_0\ne0\), every raw candidate equals the observable rational functional
\[
 W=K_1K_0^{-1}=\mathcal K((m_k),(C_k)).
\]
In particular, the structural transition satisfies \(V=K_1K_0^{-1}\).  This proves equality of the causal transition and the observable recovery map; it does not define the causal target by that map.  There is at most one raw and hence at most one admissible transition on this chart.

It remains to show that the chart is generic.  Choose pairwise distinct numbers \(v_i\in(0,1)\), and set
\[
 V=\operatorname{diag}(v_1,\ldots,v_p),
 \qquad
 G=h^{-1}\operatorname{diag}(\log v_1,\ldots,\log v_p),
 \qquad
 \Sigma=I_p,
 \qquad E=I_p.
\]
Choose \(u=\mu_0-x^\star\) with every coordinate nonzero.  Then \(G\) is real, Hurwitz, and in the principal alias strip, and \(V=\exp(hG)\).  The observed covariances satisfy
\[
 H_j=\operatorname{diag}\bigl(v_i^{2j}(1-v_i^2)\bigr)\succ0
 \qquad(j=0,1,2),
\]
because the common \(E\) cancels from adjacent differences.  Moreover,
\[
 M_0=H_1H_0^{-1}=\operatorname{diag}(v_1^2,\ldots,v_p^2),
 \qquad d_0=(V-I_p)u.
\]
Every coordinate \(d_{0i}=(v_i-1)u_i\) is nonzero.  The \(i\)-th row of \(K_0\) is
\[
 d_{0i}(1,v_i^2,\ldots,v_i^{2(p-1)}),
\]
so the Vandermonde determinant is
\[
 \det K_0
 =\left(\prod_{i=1}^p d_{0i}\right)
 \prod_{1\le i<j\le p}(v_j^2-v_i^2)\ne0.
\]
This example satisfies every model assumption and every strict admissibility condition.

Parameterize the admissible domain by \((V,\Sigma,E,x^\star,\mu_0)\).  The entries of \(H_j\) and \(d_j\) are polynomial in these coordinates.  Since
\[
 M_0=H_1\operatorname{adj}(H_0)/\det(H_0),
\]
the entries of \(K_0\), and therefore \(\det K_0\), are rational on \(\det H_0\ne0\).  Multiplication by a sufficiently high power of \(\det H_0\) gives a polynomial numerator.  The preceding admissible example makes this numerator nonzero, so it is not the zero polynomial.  Its zero set is therefore a proper algebraic subset.  Indeed, a nonzero univariate polynomial has finitely many roots, and induction on the number of coordinates, applied to its coefficient polynomials, shows that a nonzero multivariate real polynomial cannot vanish on an open set.  Hence \(\det K_0\ne0\) on an open dense complement of a proper algebraic subset of the admissible parameter domain.

On this complement, the rational map recovers the sole raw candidate.  For model-compatible laws that candidate is the true \(V\), so it necessarily passes stability, the principal-logarithm cut, Stein reconstruction, and recovered-noise positivity.  More generally, for arbitrary proposed moment laws, passage of those filters is the observable existence certificate.  By the bijection in \(\text{thm:admissible-fiber}\), a surviving singleton identifies \(G\), the latent initial covariance, the common measurement covariance, the equilibrium, and \(\mu_0=m_0\).  Thus the fourth snapshot is generically sufficient on the cyclic chart, completing the exact dimension-dependent frontier.
