%%% FIELD block-proof
By \(\text{ass:strict-decrease}\), the three matrices \(H_j\) are positive definite, so every square root and inverse used below exists.  Put
\[
 U=H_1^{-1/2}WH_0^{1/2}.
\]
The first covariance equation in \(\text{def:raw-fiber}\) is equivalent to
\[
 UU^{\mathsf T}
 =H_1^{-1/2}WH_0W^{\mathsf T}H_1^{-1/2}=I_p.
\]
Since \(U\) is square, this says exactly that \(U\in O(p)\), and the inverse change of variables is \(W=H_1^{1/2}UH_0^{-1/2}\).  Under this change of variables, the second covariance equation is
\[
 H_1^{1/2}URU^{\mathsf T}H_1^{1/2}=H_2,
 \qquad\text{or equivalently}\qquad URU^{\mathsf T}=S.
\]
Thus \(SU=UR\).  Because \(R\) and \(S\) are real symmetric positive-definite matrices, an orthogonal intertwiner exists only if their eigenvalues, including multiplicities, agree.  If they agree, their spectral resolutions give
\[
 U=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}Q_\lambda O_\lambda P_\lambda^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad O_\lambda\in O(m_\lambda),
\]
and this representation is unique relative to the fixed orthonormal frames \(P_\lambda,Q_\lambda\): indeed, \(SU=UR\) maps the \(\lambda\)-eigenspace of \(R\) into the \(\lambda\)-eigenspace of \(S\), and \(O_\lambda=Q_\lambda^{\mathsf T}UP_\lambda\).

The two mean equations become, after multiplication by \(H_1^{-1/2}\),
\[
 U H_0^{-1/2}[d_0,d_1]=H_1^{-1/2}[d_1,d_2].
\]
Projecting this equality onto the \(\lambda\)-eigenspace of \(S\) gives precisely
\[
 O_\lambda A_\lambda=B_\lambda.
\]
Necessity of the block Gram condition follows from orthogonality:
\[
 B_\lambda^{\mathsf T}B_\lambda
 =A_\lambda^{\mathsf T}O_\lambda^{\mathsf T}O_\lambda A_\lambda
 =A_\lambda^{\mathsf T}A_\lambda.
\]

Conversely, suppose the spectra match and the displayed Gram equality holds in every block.  For a fixed \(\lambda\), define on \(\operatorname{ran}(A_\lambda)\)
\[
 L_\lambda(A_\lambda c)=B_\lambda c,
 \qquad c\in\mathbb R^2.
\]
This is well defined: if \(A_\lambda c=0\), then
\[
 \lVert B_\lambda c\rVert^2
 =c^{\mathsf T}B_\lambda^{\mathsf T}B_\lambda c
 =c^{\mathsf T}A_\lambda^{\mathsf T}A_\lambda c=0.
\]
The same Gram identity shows that \(L_\lambda\) preserves all inner products and maps onto \(\operatorname{ran}(B_\lambda)\).  In particular the two ranges have the common dimension \(r_\lambda\).  Choose orthonormal frames \(F_A,F_B\) for these ranges and frames \(N_A,N_B\) for their orthogonal complements, arranging \(F_B^{\mathsf T}L_\lambda F_A=I_{r_\lambda}\).  Every orthogonal extension, and no other one, is
\[
 O_\lambda=F_BF_A^{\mathsf T}+N_B Z_\lambda N_A^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad Z_\lambda\in O(k_\lambda).
\]
It satisfies \(O_\lambda A_\lambda=B_\lambda\).  Hence the choices form a torsor for \(O(k_\lambda)\), including the conventions that \(O(0)\) is trivial and \(O(1)=\{-1,1\}\).  Since \(\dim O(k)=k(k-1)/2\), substitution into the inverse whitening map yields exactly, without repetition,
\[
 W=H_1^{1/2}\left(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}Q_\lambda O_\lambda P_\lambda^{\mathsf T}\right)H_0^{-1/2}.
\]
This proves both directions of the certificate and the asserted fiber dimensions.
%%% FIELD branch-proof
Identify a matrix \(U\) with \(u=\operatorname{vec}(U)\), write \(f(u;\zeta)=F_{\mathrm{res}}(U;\zeta)\), and let
\[
 Q(u;\zeta)=\frac12\lVert f(u;\zeta)\rVert_2^2,
 \qquad
 \Psi(u;\zeta)=D_uf(u;\zeta)^{\mathsf T}f(u;\zeta).
\]
At the fixed exact branch \((w,z)\), \(f(w;z)=0\).  Therefore
\[
 D_u\Psi(w;z)=D_uf(w;z)^{\mathsf T}D_uf(w;z).
\]
The hypothesis \(\gamma_W>0\) makes this matrix positive definite, with smallest eigenvalue \(\gamma_W^2\).  Set \(A_0=[D_u\Psi(w;z)]^{-1}\) and \(\Phi_\zeta(u)=u-A_0\Psi(u;\zeta)\).  Its derivative in \(u\) is zero at \((w,z)\).  Continuity therefore supplies neighborhoods on which \(\Phi_\zeta\) is a contraction and maps a closed ball about \(w\) into itself.  Iteration is Cauchy at a geometric rate, so it has a unique fixed point \(u(\zeta)\) there; this fixed point is the unique local stationary point.  Continuity of the Hessian keeps it positive definite, so the point is the unique local minimizer.  At the population moment \(z\), compactness of the fixed isolating ball and the absence of any other raw root imply that the residual norm is bounded away from zero outside every sufficiently small neighborhood of \(w\).  Uniform continuity in \(\zeta\) then forces the minimizer over that ball to lie in the contraction neighborhood for all \(\zeta\) sufficiently close to \(z\).  Thus it is exactly the branch-local estimator \(\widehat W_{\mathrm{loc}}\) from \(\text{def:local-branch-estimator}\).  Subtracting the two fixed-point equations and applying a first-order Taylor expansion proves differentiability, and repeated differentiation proves smoothness.  Its first derivative is
\[
 Du(z)[a]=-\bigl(D_WF^{\mathsf T}D_WF\bigr)^{-1}
 D_WF^{\mathsf T}D_zF[a].
\]
The isolating-ball hypothesis therefore selects this branch and excludes every other population root from the local construction.

Strict stability, logarithm-cut, Stein, covariance-positivity, and equilibrium margins make every operation in \(\theta(U,\zeta)\) smooth near \((W,z)\).  In particular, the Stein derivative is obtained by solving an invertible linear equation, the logarithm derivative by its resolvent integral, and the equilibrium derivative by differentiating an inverse.  The composite branch map
\[
 g(\zeta)=\theta(U(\zeta),\zeta)
\]
is therefore smooth, and its derivative at \(z\) is exactly \(J_\theta\).

It remains to establish the moment limit used by the theorem.  For one snapshot, write \(Y=m+Z\) with \(Z\sim\mathcal N(0,C)\), and define the centered single-observation moment vector
\[
 \xi=\bigl(Z,\operatorname{vech}(ZZ^{\mathsf T}-C)\bigr).
\]
It has finite moments of every order.  Differentiating the Gaussian moment-generating function \(\exp(t^{\mathsf T}Ct/2)\) shows
\[
 \mathbb E[Z_iZ_jZ_\ell]=0,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Cov}(Z_iZ_j,Z_rZ_s)=C_{ir}C_{js}+C_{is}C_{jr}.
\]
Thus the mean and covariance blocks of \(\operatorname{Cov}(\xi)\) have zero cross-covariance.  More explicitly, if \(L_p\) is the elimination matrix satisfying \(\operatorname{vech}(A)=L_p\operatorname{vec}(A)\) for symmetric \(A\), and \(\mathsf K_p\operatorname{vec}(A)=\operatorname{vec}(A^{\mathsf T})\), then the contribution of snapshot \(k\) in snapshot-interlaced coordinates is
\[
 \Omega_k=\frac1{\pi_k}\operatorname{diag}\!\left(
 C_k,\ L_p(I_{p^2}+\mathsf K_p)(C_k\otimes C_k)L_p^{\mathsf T}\right).
\]
Let \(\mathsf P\) be the fixed permutation taking the snapshot-interlaced stack to the declared order \(z=(m_0,\ldots,m_3,\operatorname{vech}C_0,\ldots,\operatorname{vech}C_3)\).  Then
\[
 \Omega=\mathsf P\operatorname{diag}(\Omega_0,\Omega_1,\Omega_2,\Omega_3)\mathsf P^{\mathsf T}.
\]
The usual sample covariance differs from the average of \(ZZ^{\mathsf T}\) by the outer product of the sample-mean error and, if the divisor is \(n-1\), an asymptotically negligible factor; this difference is \(O_{\mathbb P}(n^{-1})\).

For completeness, the finite-dimensional Gaussian limit follows from the characteristic function calculation
\[
 \mathbb E\exp\!\left(i t^{\mathsf T}\xi/\sqrt n\right)
 =1-\frac{t^{\mathsf T}\operatorname{Cov}(\xi)t}{2n}+o(n^{-1}),
\]
whose \(n\)-th power tends to \(\exp[-t^{\mathsf T}\operatorname{Cov}(\xi)t/2]\).  By \(\text{ass:unpaired-sampling}\), the samples at distinct snapshot labels are independent.  Applying the calculation at each snapshot and using \(n_k/N\to\pi_k\in(0,1)\) from \(\text{ass:sample-shares}\) gives
\[
 \sqrt N(\widehat z-z)\Longrightarrow\mathcal N(0,\Omega).
\]
The blocks from different snapshot labels are independent; all correlations created later by forming \(H_j,d_j\) are retained because these differences are fixed linear transformations of the full vector \(z\).

Taylor expansion of the smooth map \(g\) gives
\[
 \sqrt N\{g(\widehat z)-g(z)\}
 =J_\theta\sqrt N(\widehat z-z)+o_{\mathbb P}(1),
\]
and substitution of the preceding characteristic-function limit proves
\[
 \sqrt N\bigl(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{loc}}-\theta(W,z)\bigr)
 \Longrightarrow\mathcal N(0,J_\theta\Omega J_\theta^{\mathsf T}).
\]
In particular the last coordinate is \(\widehat\mu_0=\widehat m_0\).  The empirical covariances converge to \(C_k\), the deterministic shares converge to \(\pi_k\), and the smooth local solution converges to \(W\).  The explicit Gaussian fourth-moment formula above and continuity of all derivatives therefore imply that replacing \((z,W,\Omega,J_\theta)\) by their empirical plug-in counterparts converges in probability to \(J_\theta\Omega J_\theta^{\mathsf T}\).  This establishes consistent covariance estimation separately on every specified regular branch, without requiring empirical spectral or Gram equalities.
%%% FIELD sharp-proof
By \(\text{thm:block-certificate}\), a nonempty raw fiber is a product, over \(\lambda\in\Lambda\), of torsors for \(O(k_\lambda)\).  The group \(O(0)\) is a singleton, \(O(1)=\{-1,1\}\), and \(O(k)\) has local dimension \(k(k-1)/2>0\) when \(k\geq2\).  Hence the raw fiber is finite exactly when every \(k_\lambda\leq1\), and then
\[
 \#\mathcal F_{\mathrm{raw}}
 =\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\#O(k_\lambda)
 =2^{\#\{\lambda:k_\lambda=1\}}=2^q.
\]
If some \(k_\lambda\geq2\), the product torsor has positive local dimension at every point.  The three admissibility requirements in \(\text{def:admissible-fiber}\) are strict and relatively open on this torsor.  Indeed, spectral radius is continuous; avoidance of \(( -\infty,0]\) persists when the roots of the characteristic polynomial are perturbed sufficiently little; and, on \(\rho(W)<1\),
\[
 \operatorname{vec}(T_W)
 =\bigl(I_{p^2}-W\otimes W\bigr)^{-1}\operatorname{vec}(H_0)
\]
is continuous, so \(C_0-T_W\succ0\) is open.  Thus every admissible candidate on such a stratum lies in a positive-dimensional relatively open fiber.

We next prove the universal finite-fiber upper bound.  Suppose first that \(q\geq1\), and fix a block \(\lambda_0\) with \(k_{\lambda_0}=1\).  In the parameterization of \(\text{thm:block-certificate}\), changing only the sign of the one-dimensional residual isometry in that block defines a fixed-point-free involution of \(\mathcal F_{\mathrm{raw}}\).  In orthonormal bases adapted to the probe span and its residual complement, this operation multiplies \(\det(O_{\lambda_0})\), and therefore \(\det(W)\), by \(-1\).  The \(2^q\) raw candidates consequently split into \(2^{q-1}\) matrices of each determinant sign.

Every raw candidate is invertible, because \(WH_0W^{\mathsf T}=H_1\) and \(H_0,H_1\succ0\).  If a real invertible matrix \(W\) satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{spec}(W)\cap(-\infty,0]=\varnothing,
\]
then all of its real eigenvalues are positive, while each nonreal conjugate pair \(z,\overline z\) contributes \(z\overline z=|z|^2>0\) to the determinant.  Hence \(\det(W)>0\).  The logarithm-cut filter therefore rejects every negative-determinant raw candidate, so
\[
 \#\mathcal F_{\mathrm{adm}}\leq 2^{q-1}\qquad(q\geq1).
\]
If \(q=0\), the raw fiber itself has one member.  Finally,
\[
 q\leq\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}k_\lambda
 \leq\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}m_\lambda=p.
\]
Together with \(p\geq2\) from \(\text{ass:dimension}\), this gives \(\#\mathcal F_{\mathrm{adm}}\leq2^{p-1}\) on every finite stratum, including the case \(q=0\).  By the bijection in \(\text{thm:admissible-fiber}\), the same bound holds for compatible structural explanations.

It remains to attain the bound while satisfying all strict filters.  Let \(Q_\circ\) be the cyclic permutation matrix defined by
\[
 Q_\circ e_i=e_{i+1}\quad(1\leq i<p),
 \qquad Q_\circ e_p=e_1.
\]
Thus \(Q_\circ\in O(p)\) and \(\det(Q_\circ)=(-1)^{p-1}\).  For a sign matrix
\[
 D=\operatorname{diag}(s_1,\ldots,s_p),
 \qquad s_i\in\{-1,1\},
\]
one has
\[
 (Q_\circ D)^p=\left(\prod_{i=1}^p s_i\right)I_p.
\]
If \(\det(Q_\circ D)>0\), then \(\prod_i s_i=(-1)^{p-1}\).  Were \(-1\) an eigenvalue of \(Q_\circ D\), applying the preceding power identity to a corresponding nonzero eigenvector would give
\[
 (-1)^p=\prod_{i=1}^p s_i=(-1)^{p-1},
\]
a contradiction.  Because a real eigenvalue of an orthogonal matrix is either \(-1\) or \(1\), every orientation-preserving \(Q_\circ D\) avoids the negative real axis.

Fix \(\alpha\in(0,1)\).  At \(A_0=\alpha I_p\), each matrix \(A_0Q_\circ D\) with \(\det(Q_\circ D)>0\) is stable and avoids \(( -\infty,0]\).  This cut avoidance persists simultaneously for all such \(D\) when a positive diagonal matrix \(A\) is sufficiently close to \(A_0\).  To verify this without a spectral-gap assumption, suppose the contrary for one fixed \(D\).  There would be positive diagonal matrices \(A_n\to A_0\), numbers \(t_n\geq0\), and
\[
 \det(t_nI_p+A_nQ_\circ D)=0.
\]
For all large \(n\), \(\lVert A_n\rVert_2\leq c\) for some \(c<1\).  Since \(-t_n\) is then an eigenvalue of \(A_nQ_\circ D\), \(0\leq t_n\leq c\).  A subsequence has \(t_n\to t\in[0,c]\), and continuity of the determinant would imply
\[
 \det(tI_p+\alpha Q_\circ D)=0,
\]
contradicting the established cut avoidance at \(A_0\).  There are only \(2^{p-1}\) positive-orientation sign matrices, so one neighborhood works for all of them.  Choose within that neighborhood
\[
 A=\operatorname{diag}(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_p),
 \qquad 0<\alpha_i<1,
 \qquad \alpha_i^2\neq\alpha_j^2\quad(i\neq j).
\]
For every sign matrix \(D\),
\[
 \lVert AQ_\circ D\rVert_2=\lVert A\rVert_2
 =\max_i\alpha_i<1,
\]
so every raw candidate below is stable; by the choice of \(A\), every positive-determinant one also passes the logarithm-cut filter.

Define zero mean differences and the three covariance differences by
\[
 d_0=d_1=d_2=0,
 \qquad H_0=I_p,
 \qquad H_1=A^2,
 \qquad H_2=AQ_\circ A^2Q_\circ^{\mathsf T}A.
\]
All three \(H_j\) are positive definite, so \(\text{ass:strict-decrease}\) holds.  Moreover
\[
 R=A^2,
 \qquad S=Q_\circ A^2Q_\circ^{\mathsf T}.
\]
The squared diagonal entries of \(A\) are distinct, so the common spectral blocks are one dimensional, with source eigenvectors \(e_i\) and target eigenvectors \(Q_\circ e_i\).  Both probe matrices in every block vanish because all \(d_j\) vanish.  Hence
\[
 m_\lambda=1,\qquad r_\lambda=0,\qquad k_\lambda=1,\qquad q=p.
\]
The block parameterization in \(\text{thm:block-certificate}\) now gives, exactly once each, the \(2^p\) raw matrices
\[
 W_D=AQ_\circ D,
 \qquad D=\operatorname{diag}(s_1,\ldots,s_p),
 \qquad s_i\in\{-1,1\}.
\]
Directly, their defining equations are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 W_DH_0W_D^{\mathsf T}&=A^2=H_1,\\
 W_DH_1W_D^{\mathsf T}
 &=AQ_\circ DA^2DQ_\circ^{\mathsf T}A
 =AQ_\circ A^2Q_\circ^{\mathsf T}A=H_2,
 \end{aligned}
\]
where \(DA^2D=A^2\); the mean equations hold because the probes are zero.

It remains to realize these differences as positive-definite Gaussian level covariances and to make the reconstructed measurement covariance positive on every branch.  For each of the finitely many sign matrices define
\[
 T_D=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}W_D^j(W_D^{\mathsf T})^j.
\]
The bound \(\lVert W_D\rVert_2<1\) makes this series converge, its zeroth term gives \(T_D\succ0\), and shifting the series proves
\[
 T_D-W_DT_DW_D^{\mathsf T}=I_p=H_0.
\]
Let
\[
 b=1+\lambda_{\max}(H_0+H_1+H_2)
\]
and choose
\[
 c>\max\left\{b,\max_D\lambda_{\max}(T_D)\right\}.
\]
Set
\[
 C_0=cI_p,
 \qquad C_1=C_0-H_0,
 \qquad C_2=C_1-H_1,
 \qquad C_3=C_2-H_2,
 \qquad m_0=m_1=m_2=m_3=0.
\]
The choice \(c>b\) makes every \(C_k\) positive definite.  For each \(D\), put \(E_D=C_0-T_D\).  The choice of \(c\) gives \(E_D\succ0\).  Using the Stein equation and the two raw congruences successively yields, for \(k=0,1,2,3\),
\[
 C_k=W_D^kT_D(W_D^{\mathsf T})^k+E_D\succ0.
\]
Thus the displayed \(C_k\) are well-defined positive-definite observed covariance matrices, their adjacent differences are exactly the prescribed strict positive-definite \(H_j\), and \(E_D\) is a common positive-definite measurement covariance across the four snapshots within each explanation.

Because \(\det(A)>0\), exactly half of the sign matrices satisfy
\[
 \det(W_D)=\det(A)\det(Q_\circ D)>0.
\]
All of these \(2^{p-1}\) candidates pass stability, the logarithm cut, and \(E_D\succ0\), so they belong to \(\mathcal F_{\mathrm{adm}}\).  Every remaining candidate has negative determinant and therefore has a negative real eigenvalue by the conjugate-pair determinant argument above; it fails the logarithm-cut filter.  Hence
\[
 \#\mathcal F_{\mathrm{adm}}=2^{p-1}.
\]
Finally, \(\text{thm:admissible-fiber}\) reconstructs from every surviving \(W_D\) a unique real Hurwitz generator in the principal-log alias strip, the covariance \(T_D\), the positive common measurement covariance \(E_D\), the equilibrium \(x_D^\star=0\), and the initial mean \(\mu_0=0\).  Its bijection proves that these are exactly \(2^{p-1}\) distinct compatible structural explanations.  This proves attainability and completes the sharp classification.
%%% FIELD information-proof
Let \(A\) be a perturbation of a compatible transition \(W\).  Differentiating the four components of \(F_{\mathrm{res}}\) at the exact moment vector gives
\[
 D_WF[A]=\bigl(\operatorname{vech}(AH_0W^{\mathsf T}+WH_0A^{\mathsf T}),
 \operatorname{vech}(AH_1W^{\mathsf T}+WH_1A^{\mathsf T}),Ad_0,Ad_1\bigr).
\]
Put
\[
 U=H_1^{-1/2}WH_0^{1/2}\in O(p),
 \qquad
 Z=H_1^{-1/2}AH_0^{1/2}.
\]
The first linearized covariance equation is \(ZU^{\mathsf T}+UZ^{\mathsf T}=0\).  Thus \(Z=UJ\) for a unique skew-symmetric matrix \(J\).  Substitution in the second equation, using \(H_1=H_0^{1/2}RH_0^{1/2}\), gives
\[
 JR+RJ^{\mathsf T}=0,
 \qquad\text{hence}\qquad JR=RJ.
\]
The two mean equations are equivalent to
\[
 JH_0^{-1/2}d_0=0,
 \qquad
 JH_0^{-1/2}d_1=0.
\]
Because \(R\) is symmetric, \(JR=RJ\) makes \(J\) block diagonal in its eigenspaces.  In the \(\lambda\)-block, the last two equations say precisely that the skew-symmetric block \(J_\lambda\) annihilates \(\operatorname{ran}(A_\lambda)\), an \(r_\lambda\)-dimensional subspace.  Relative to that subspace and its orthogonal complement,
\[
 J_\lambda=0_{r_\lambda}\oplus \widetilde J_\lambda,
 \qquad
 \widetilde J_\lambda^{\mathsf T}=-\widetilde J_\lambda,
 \qquad
 \widetilde J_\lambda\in\mathbb R^{k_\lambda\times k_\lambda}.
\]
All changes of variables above are invertible.  Since the space of skew-symmetric \(k\)-by-\(k\) matrices has dimension \(k(k-1)/2\),
\[
 \dim\ker(D_WF)=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\frac{k_\lambda(k_\lambda-1)}2.
\]

Now consider the full structural coordinates \((W,T,E,x^\star,\mu_0)\) and the map from them to the four Gaussian means and covariances.  If their moment derivative vanishes, then the derivatives of all \(H_j,d_j\) vanish, so its \(W\)-component belongs to the kernel just computed.  Conversely, fix \(A\in\ker(D_WF)\).  The derivative \(\dot T\) required to keep \(H_0=T-WTW^{\mathsf T}\) fixed is uniquely determined by
\[
 \dot T-W\dot TW^{\mathsf T}=ATW^{\mathsf T}+WTA^{\mathsf T},
\]
because the stable Stein operator is invertible.  Keeping \(C_0=T+E\) fixed then uniquely forces \(\dot E=-\dot T\).  Keeping \(m_0=\mu_0\) fixed forces \(\dot\mu_0=0\), and keeping
\[
 m_1=W\mu_0+(I_p-W)x^\star
\]
fixed uniquely forces
\[
 \dot x^\star=-(I_p-W)^{-1}A(\mu_0-x^\star).
\]
The relations \(Ad_0=Ad_1=0\) then keep \(m_2,m_3\) fixed, and the two linearized congruences keep \(C_2,C_3\) fixed.  Hence projection onto the \(W\)-coordinate is a linear bijection between the kernel of the full moment derivative and \(\ker(D_WF)\).  Replacing \(W\) by its principal-logarithm coordinate \(G_W\) is a local smooth bijection and does not alter this nullity.

Finally, for one Gaussian marginal with positive-definite covariance \(C\), a mean perturbation \(u\) and symmetric covariance perturbation \(A_C\) have Fisher quadratic form
\[
 u^{\mathsf T}C^{-1}u+
 \frac12\operatorname{tr}(C^{-1}A_CC^{-1}A_C).
\]
This follows by differentiating the Gaussian log density and evaluating its second moments; after writing \(A_C=C^{1/2}BC^{1/2}\), the second term is \(\frac12\operatorname{tr}(B^2)\geq0\), with equality only for \(A_C=0\), while the first term vanishes only for \(u=0\).  By \(\text{ass:unpaired-sampling}\), the destructive-snapshot experiment draws independent samples separately from the four marginal laws.  Its Fisher quadratic form is therefore the positive weighted sum of the four marginal forms, and its kernel is exactly the kernel of the full parameter-to-moment derivative.  Thus the Fisher-information nullity equals
\[
 \sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\frac{k_\lambda(k_\lambda-1)}2.
\]
It is zero exactly when every \(k_\lambda\leq1\), which is exactly the finite-raw-stratum condition given by the product of orthogonal completion fibers in \(\text{thm:block-certificate}\).
