Let \(\mathcal C_4(E)\) denote, only within this proof, the set of transition matrices that occur in structural explanations of the four observed marginal laws with the supplied measurement covariance \(E\), subject to \(\text{ass:spacing}\), \(\text{ass:linear-flow}\), \(\text{ass:hurwitz}\), \(\text{ass:alias-strip}\), \(\text{ass:initial-gaussian}\), \(\text{ass:common-measurement-noise}\), and \(\text{ass:measurement-independence}\).  Thus \(\mathcal C_4(E)\) is a causal-model compatibility set introduced for the argument; the observable set \(\mathcal F_{\mathrm{known}}(E)\) remains the functional defined in \(\text{def:known-noise-fiber}\).  We prove the non-definitional equality
\[
 \mathcal C_4(E)=\mathcal F_{\mathrm{known}}(E).
\]

For necessity, take \(W\in\mathcal C_4(E)\).  Write \(L_k=\operatorname{Cov}(X_k)\).  By \(\text{ass:linear-flow}\) and \(\text{ass:initial-gaussian}\),
\[
 \mathbb E[X_k]=W^k(\mu_0-x^\star)+x^\star,
 \qquad
 L_k=W^k\Sigma(W^{\mathsf T})^k .
\]
Because \(W=\exp(hG)\) is invertible and \(\Sigma\succ0\), congruence by \(W^k\) yields \(L_k\succ0\) for every \(k\in K\).  The errors have zero mean and covariance \(E\) by \(\text{ass:common-measurement-noise}\).  By \(\text{ass:measurement-independence}\), each \(\varepsilon_k\) is independent of \(X_0\), and therefore is independent of the deterministic affine function \(X_k\) of \(X_0\).  Hence
\[
 m_k=\mathbb E[X_k],
 \qquad
 C_k=L_k+E,
 \qquad
 \widetilde C_k=C_k-E=L_k\succ0.
\]
The covariance recursion and the affine mean formula give
\[
 W\widetilde C_kW^{\mathsf T}=\widetilde C_{k+1}\quad(k=0,1,2)
\]
and
\[
 d_k=m_{k+1}-m_k
     =W^k(W-I_p)(\mu_0-x^\star)\quad(k=0,1,2).
\]
Consequently \(Wd_0=d_1\) and \(Wd_1=d_2\).

We next check the structural filters.  Over \(\mathbb C\), triangularize \(G\).  The power-series definition of the exponential then shows that the diagonal entries of \(\exp(hG)\) are \(e^{hg}\), where \(g\) ranges over the eigenvalues of \(G\).  Thus \(h>0\) from \(\text{ass:spacing}\) and \(\operatorname{Re}(g)<0\) from \(\text{ass:hurwitz}\) imply
\[
 \rho(W)=\max_{g\in\operatorname{spec}(G)}e^{h\operatorname{Re}(g)}<1.
\]
If \(e^{hg}\) were a negative real number, then \(h\operatorname{Im}(g)=(2\ell+1)\pi\) for some \(\ell\in\mathbb Z\).  This contradicts \(\text{ass:alias-strip}\), which gives \(-\pi<h\operatorname{Im}(g)<\pi\); moreover an exponential eigenvalue is never zero.  Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{spec}(W)\cap(-\infty,0]=\varnothing.
\]

Cancellation of the common covariance gives
\[
 H_0=C_0-C_1=L_0-L_1=L_0-WL_0W^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Thus \(L_0\) solves the Stein equation in the definition of \(T_W\).  If \(D-WDW^{\mathsf T}=0\), vectorization gives
\[
 \bigl(I_{p^2}-W\otimes W\bigr)\operatorname{vec}(D)=0.
\]
Triangularizing the two factors shows directly that the diagonal entries, hence the eigenvalues, of \(W\otimes W\) are the products \(\lambda_i\lambda_j\) of eigenvalues of \(W\).  Their moduli are strictly below one because \(\rho(W)<1\).  Hence \(I_{p^2}-W\otimes W\) is invertible, so \(D=0\).  The Stein solution is therefore unique, and
\[
 T_W=L_0=\widetilde C_0,
 \qquad
 E_W=C_0-T_W=C_0-\widetilde C_0=E.
\]
All conditions in \(\text{def:known-noise-fiber}\) hold.  This proves \(\mathcal C_4(E)\subseteq\mathcal F_{\mathrm{known}}(E)\).

For sufficiency, take \(W\in\mathcal F_{\mathrm{known}}(E)\), and define
\[
 \Sigma=\widetilde C_0,
 \qquad
 \mu_0=m_0,
 \qquad
 b=m_1-Wm_0.
\]
Membership in the fiber gives \(\Sigma\succ0\) and \(\rho(W)<1\).  In particular, \(1\notin\operatorname{spec}(W)\), so \(I_p-W\) is invertible.  Define
\[
 x^\star=(I_p-W)^{-1}b.
\]
Then \(b=(I_p-W)x^\star\), and the affine recursion \(u\mapsto Wu+b\), begun at \(m_0\), gives \(m_1\) at its first step.  The two probe restrictions in \(\text{def:known-noise-fiber}\) give the remaining observed means:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Wm_1+b
 &=m_1+W(m_1-m_0)
 =m_1+Wd_0
 =m_1+d_1=m_2,\\
 Wm_2+b
 &=m_2+W(m_2-m_1)
 =m_2+Wd_1
 =m_2+d_2=m_3.
 \end{aligned}
\]

The spectral-cut condition in the fiber implies that \(W\) is invertible and that every eigenvalue lies in the domain \(\mathbb C\setminus(-\infty,0]\) of the scalar principal logarithm.  To make the logarithm step explicit, put a complex Jordan block of \(W\) in the form \(J=\lambda I+N\), where \(N^s=0\) and \(-\pi<\operatorname{Arg}(\lambda)<\pi\), and set
\[
 \operatorname{Log}(J)
 =\bigl(\log|\lambda|+i\operatorname{Arg}(\lambda)\bigr)I
  +\sum_{r=1}^{s-1}\frac{(-1)^{r+1}}{r}\left(\frac{N}{\lambda}\right)^r.
\]
The nilpotent sum is finite, and formal composition of the finite nilpotent parts of the logarithm and exponential series gives \(\exp(\operatorname{Log}(J))=J\).  Since \(W\) is real, its nonreal Jordan data occur in conjugate pairs; the displayed construction respects conjugation and therefore represents a real matrix \(\operatorname{Log}(W)\).  Its eigenvalues are the scalar principal logarithms of the eigenvalues of \(W\).  It follows that
\[
 G_W=h^{-1}\operatorname{Log}(W)
\]
is real and satisfies
\[
 \exp(hG_W)=W,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Re}\operatorname{spec}(G_W)
 =h^{-1}\log|\operatorname{spec}(W)|<0,
 \qquad
 -\frac{\pi}{h}<\operatorname{Im}\operatorname{spec}(G_W)<\frac{\pi}{h}.
\]
Here division by \(h\) is valid by \(\text{ass:spacing}\).  Thus the constructed generator satisfies \(\text{ass:hurwitz}\) and \(\text{ass:alias-strip}\).  Moreover, if \(L\) is any logarithm of \(W\) whose spectrum lies in the open strip \(-\pi<\operatorname{Im}(z)<\pi\), the scalar identity \(\operatorname{Log}(e^z)=z\) holds on a neighborhood of its spectrum.  Applying the corresponding power series on each generalized eigenspace gives
\[
 L=\operatorname{Log}(e^L)=\operatorname{Log}(W).
\]
Thus the open alias strip introduces no further generator branch.

Now construct \(X_0\sim\mathcal N(m_0,\Sigma)\), set
\[
 X_k=W^k(X_0-x^\star)+x^\star,
\]
and take mutually independent \(\varepsilon_k\sim\mathcal N(0,E)\), independent of \(X_0\).  The supplied \(E\) is positive definite by \(\text{ass:common-measurement-noise}\), so these errors exist and satisfy the required support and independence conditions.  The preceding affine recursion gives \(\mathbb E[X_k]=m_k\).  The three level congruences in \(\text{def:known-noise-fiber}\) give inductively
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(X_k)
 =W^k\Sigma(W^{\mathsf T})^k
 =\widetilde C_k
 \qquad(k\in K).
\]
Therefore
\[
 \mathbb E[X_k+\varepsilon_k]=m_k,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Cov}(X_k+\varepsilon_k)=\widetilde C_k+E=C_k.
\]
Both laws are nonsingular Gaussian laws, and a Gaussian law is determined by its mean and covariance.  Hence the constructed marginal law at each snapshot equals the observed marginal law.  This proves \(W\in\mathcal C_4(E)\), and therefore proves the reverse inclusion.  Notice also that \(E_W=E\succ0\); fixing this residual equals the supplied covariance and is strictly more restrictive than the unknown-noise inequality \(C_0-T_W\succ0\).

If only snapshots \(0,1,2\) are retained, the same necessary calculation yields exactly
\[
 W\widetilde C_0W^{\mathsf T}=\widetilde C_1,
 \qquad
 W\widetilde C_1W^{\mathsf T}=\widetilde C_2,
 \qquad
 Wd_0=d_1.
\]
Conversely, these equations and \(\widetilde C_k\succ0\) reproduce the three covariance and affine-mean recursions after setting \(b=m_1-Wm_0\).  They do not silently supply the distinct stability, logarithm, Stein, or known-noise equality filters; those filters remain the restrictions required for membership in the present structural class.

By \(\text{lem:aalto-model-scope}\), the published full-state recursion has moment equations
\[
 P_{j+1}=AP_jA^{\mathsf T}+Q,
 \qquad
 \bar m_{j+1}=A\bar m_j+b.
\]
The substitutions
\[
 A=W,
 \qquad
 P_j=\widetilde C_j,
 \qquad
 Q=0,
 \qquad
 b=(I_p-W)x^\star
\]
map every present noise-subtracted moment equation into that published recursion.  This is only an equation-level inclusion: the published model exactly observes its state, while the present model observes a Gaussian state through an additional supplied common measurement-error covariance.  The inclusion is strict at model scope.  Indeed, the published recursion permits \(A=2I_p\), \(Q=0\), and any positive-definite initial covariance, whereas this transition is excluded here because \(\rho(2I_p)=2\) and every transition allowed by \(\text{ass:hurwitz}\) has spectral radius below one.  The cited scope also permits known nonzero \(Q\), does not impose continuous-time embeddability or the principal-log cut, and, by \(\text{lem:aalto-model-scope}\), needs only finite second moments for its moment argument.  This proves the asserted strict subclass comparison without equating the two observation-model classes.

It remains to prove the finite sign reduction.  For three supplied-noise snapshots define the positive-definite whitened invariants and whitened transition
\[
 R_0=\widetilde C_0^{-1/2}\widetilde C_1\widetilde C_0^{-1/2},
 \qquad
 R_1=\widetilde C_1^{-1/2}\widetilde C_2\widetilde C_1^{-1/2},
 \qquad
 U=\widetilde C_1^{-1/2}W\widetilde C_0^{1/2}.
\]
The square roots and inverses exist because the three \(\widetilde C_k\) are positive definite.  The first congruence is equivalent to \(UU^{\mathsf T}=I_p\), so \(U\) is orthogonal.  Substitution into the second congruence gives
\[
 UR_0U^{\mathsf T}=R_1.
\]
Suppose the two invariants have the same simple ordered spectrum.  Choose orthogonal eigendecompositions
\[
 R_0=P\Lambda P^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad
 R_1=Q_1\Lambda Q_1^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad
 \Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_p),
\]
where \(\lambda_i\ne\lambda_j\) for \(i\ne j\).  The matrix \(D=Q_1^{\mathsf T}UP\) is orthogonal and obeys \(D\Lambda D^{\mathsf T}=\Lambda\), hence \(D\Lambda=\Lambda D\).  Entrywise,
\[
 (\lambda_j-\lambda_i)D_{ij}=0.
\]
Simplicity forces \(D_{ij}=0\) when \(i\ne j\), and orthogonality then forces \(D_{ii}\in\{-1,1\}\).  Therefore every covariance-compatible transition is one of the explicit candidates
\[
 W_D=\widetilde C_1^{1/2}Q_1DP^{\mathsf T}\widetilde C_0^{-1/2},
 \qquad
 D=\operatorname{diag}(s_1,\ldots,s_p),
 \qquad
 s_i\in\{-1,1\}.
\]
This is a computable list of cardinality at most \(2^p\).  Define the computable probe coordinates
\[
 a=P^{\mathsf T}\widetilde C_0^{-1/2}d_0,
 \qquad
 c=Q_1^{\mathsf T}\widetilde C_1^{-1/2}d_1.
\]
The equation \(Wd_0=d_1\) is equivalent to \(Da=c\).  If every source coordinate \(a_i\) is nonzero, then every sign is forced by
\[
 s_i=\frac{c_i}{a_i},
\]
where division is valid precisely because \(a_i\ne0\); compatibility requires each ratio to lie in \(\{-1,1\}\).  For four-snapshot compatibility, test every surviving candidate against
\[
 W_D\widetilde C_2W_D^{\mathsf T}=\widetilde C_3,
 \qquad
 W_Dd_1=d_2,
\]
and against \(\rho(W_D)<1\), \(\operatorname{spec}(W_D)\cap(-\infty,0]=\varnothing\), existence of the Stein reconstruction, and \(E_{W_D}=E\).  The sign list exhausts the first two covariance congruences, and these final tests are exactly the remaining restrictions in \(\text{def:known-noise-fiber}\).  Hence their survivors are exactly \(\mathcal F_{\mathrm{known}}(E)\), completing the reduction and classification.

Section 2 of AaltoLamolineGoncalves2022 studies, in a locally denoted dimension \(n\), the exactly observed full-state recursion \(Z_j=A Z_{j-1}+b+\eta_{j-1}\), where the process innovations have known covariance \(Q\) and the temporal orthogonality specified there.  Its moment equations are \(\bar m_j=A\bar m_{j-1}+b\) and \(P_j=AP_{j-1}A^{\mathsf T}+Q\).  Theorem 2.1 assumes that \(A\) is invertible but imposes neither stability nor continuous-time embeddability, and the paragraph immediately following its proof states that finite second moments, rather than Gaussianity, suffice for these moment equations and the stated identification argument.
