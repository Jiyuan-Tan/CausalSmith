\section*{eid\_\allowbreak{}snapshot\_\allowbreak{}commonnoise\_\allowbreak{}stabilizer}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} Three independent Gaussian snapshots have an exact dimension-dependent identification frontier under one unknown common positive-definite measurement covariance.  When \(p=2\) and \(d_0\ne0\), the two raw \(O(1)\) completions have opposite determinants and the negative-determinant completion fails the principal-logarithm cut, so model-compatible laws identify the full explanation.  The exceptional \(d_0=0\) regime retains a continuous \(O(2)\) ambiguity.  For \(p\ge3\), three snapshots generically retain a continuous \(O(p-1)\) ambiguity, whereas a fourth snapshot generically identifies through \(K_1K_0^{-1}\).  The four-snapshot block Gram certificate enumerates the raw stabilizer fiber; stability, the logarithm cut, Stein reconstruction, and strict noise positivity then determine exact structural compatibility.  Stabilizer dimension equals information nullity, and the sharp finite-fiber maximum is \(2^{p-1}\).  On each specified regular finite branch, a fixed-ball global residual projection is uniquely selected near the population moment and supports Gaussian moment inference, although a data-based global initializer and stable labeling rule across all noncyclic branches remain open.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Published snapshot-identification results either use supplied state covariances or work in broader known-noise transition models without classifying the fiber created by one unknown common additive covariance.  They do not expose the dimension-two elbow at which the two raw three-snapshot completions have opposite determinants and the principal-logarithm cut removes exactly one.  A supplied \(E\) places the present stable, embeddable, Gaussian, zero-process-noise system inside the broader Aalto--Lamoline--Goncalves class, which also permits known process covariance, arbitrary invertible discrete-time dynamics, and finite-second-moment non-Gaussian laws; it does not make the two model classes equal.

\paragraph{Niche.} Common measurement noise cancels from adjacent covariance differences but re-enters through level-covariance positivity.  With three snapshots this gives an exactly computable one-vector orthogonal matching problem: it generically collapses to one admissible explanation in dimension two through a determinant obstruction, leaves an \(O(2)\) continuum at zero displacement, and leaves positive-dimensional ambiguity generically in dimensions at least three.  The fourth snapshot adds a second congruence that generically collapses the higher-dimensional stabilizer through a rational Krylov identity.

\paragraph{Fill.} The paper supplies the exact three-snapshot norm certificate and \(O(p-r)\) classification, including the sharp dimension-two determinant elbow; an if-and-only-if four-snapshot block Gram certificate; a bijective admissibility map; the complete exceptional-stratum classification; and the sharp attainable finite-fiber maximum \(2^{p-1}\), together with rational perturbation theory and branchwise inference.  It also identifies the precise supplied-noise subclass relation and retains certified global initialization and branch labeling as the remaining open construction problem.

\section{Related work}

AaltoLamolineGoncalves2022 provides the closest congruence architecture.  Its published class allows known process covariance \(Q\), arbitrary invertible discrete-time dynamics, and finite-second-moment non-Gaussian laws.  After a supplied measurement covariance \(E\) is subtracted, the model studied here is only its stable, principal-log-embeddable, Gaussian, \(Q=0\) subclass; the inherited consequence used here is the consecutive latent-covariance congruence, not equality of model classes or an exact reduction to every part of its theorem.  AaltoGoncalves2019 also studies identification from ensemble snapshots, but its setup does not furnish the exact fiber classification for an unknown common additive measurement covariance used here.  DunikEtAl2017 surveys state-space noise-covariance estimation methods; those methods address noise specification and estimation rather than the destructive-snapshot identified set induced by a shared unknown measurement covariance.  It does not provide the present unknown-common-noise norm certificate, the \(O(p-r)\) torsor, the dimension-two determinant selection, the two-probe residual-rank classification, or the sharp \(2^{p-1}\) admissible finite-fiber maximum.  WangEtAl2024 proves a three-time-point result for deterministic affine dynamics from supplied state moments and motivates the \(\texttt{covar\_lin\_dyn}\) consumer, but supplied covariances remove the common-noise torsor and therefore do not reveal the \(p=2\) principal-logarithm elbow.  QWENDY2025 uses four supplied covariance matrices and simple spectra to resolve a homogeneous congruence problem, but it contains neither an unknown common additive covariance nor the exact three-snapshot classification proved here.  MorimotoKashima2025 and InoueEtAl2026 estimate stochastic linear dynamics from direct snapshot marginals by Schrodinger-bridge optimization without a global common-noise identification certificate.  Blevins2017 and DingQiuChen2009 concern continuous-time reconstruction and logarithm/alias questions after a discrete transition is available; neither treats its recovery from unpaired common-noise covariance differences or the resulting stabilizer fiber.  KoltchinskiiLounici2017 supplies spectral perturbation tools, while HauensteinSottile2012 supplies root certificates; neither solves basis-invariant finite-branch enumeration with matrix-inequality filters.  ChernozhukovHongTamer2007, AndrewsCheng2012, and Drton2009 respectively frame set-valued, weak-identification, and singular-boundary inference issues around the regular-stratum results, but do not provide a globally labeled finite-branch procedure.  KongEtAl2023 studies process and measurement covariance identification from a paired stationary time series with known system matrices, a different information regime from destructive marginal snapshots.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:dimension]\label{ass:dimension}
The state dimension satisfies \(p\ge 2\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Finite-dimensional linear system, \cite{AaltoLamolineGoncalves2022}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:spacing]\label{ass:spacing}
The snapshot spacing satisfies \(h>0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Positive sampling interval, \cite{Blevins2017}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:linear-flow]\label{ass:linear-flow}
For every \(k\in K\), the latent state obeys \(X_k=V^k(X_0-x^\star)+x^\star\) with \(V=\exp(hG)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Time-homogeneous linear flow, \cite{WangEtAl2024}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:hurwitz]\label{ass:hurwitz}
Every eigenvalue of \(G\) has strictly negative real part.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Hurwitz stability, \cite{Blevins2017}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:alias-strip]\label{ass:alias-strip}
Every eigenvalue \(g\) of \(G\) satisfies \( -\pi/h<\operatorname{Im}(g)<\pi/h\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Principal-logarithm alias strip, \cite{Blevins2017}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:initial-gaussian]\label{ass:initial-gaussian}
The initial state satisfies \(X_0\sim\mathcal N(\mu_0,\Sigma)\) with \(\Sigma\succ0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Nonsingular Gaussian initial law, \cite{AaltoLamolineGoncalves2022}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:common-measurement-noise]\label{ass:common-measurement-noise}
For every \(k\in K\), \(\varepsilon_k\sim\mathcal N(0,E)\) with the same \(E\succ0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Time-homogeneous Gaussian measurement covariance, \cite{DunikEtAl2017}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:measurement-independence]\label{ass:measurement-independence}
The family \(\{\varepsilon_k:k\in K\}\) is mutually independent and independent of \(X_0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Classical independent measurement error, \cite{DunikEtAl2017}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:strict-decrease]\label{ass:strict-decrease}
For each \(j\in\{0,1,2\}\), the adjacent covariance difference satisfies \(H_j\succ0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Strict transient covariance decrease holds for open neighborhoods of diagonal stable contractions with positive latent covariance, including heterogeneous coordinatewise decay observed before equilibrium.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:unpaired-sampling]\label{ass:unpaired-sampling}
At each \(k\in K\), the observed sample consists of \(n_k\) independent draws from the marginal law of \(Y_k\), independently across snapshot labels.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Independent population-snapshot sampling, \cite{AaltoGoncalves2019}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:sample-shares]\label{ass:sample-shares}
For every \(k\in K\), \(n_k/N\to\pi_k\in(0,1)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Positive limiting sample shares, \cite{KoltchinskiiLounici2017}).
\end{assumption}

\section{Main results}

\begin{theorem}[thm:block-certificate]\label{thm:block-certificate}
The raw fiber \(\mathcal F_{\mathrm{raw}}\) is nonempty if and only if \(R\) and \(S\) have identical eigenvalues with identical multiplicities and \(A_\lambda^{\mathsf T}A_\lambda=B_\lambda^{\mathsf T}B_\lambda\) for every \(\lambda\in\Lambda\). When these conditions hold, every member appears exactly once as \[W=H_1^{1/2}\left(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}Q_\lambda O_\lambda P_\lambda^{\mathsf T}\right)H_0^{-1/2},\qquad O_\lambda A_\lambda=B_\lambda,\quad O_\lambda\in O(m_\lambda),\] where the completion fiber of block \(\lambda\) is a torsor for \(O(k_\lambda)\) and has dimension \(k_\lambda(k_\lambda-1)/2\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By \(\text{ass:strict-decrease}\), every \(H_j\) is positive definite, so all square roots and inverses below exist.  Define
\[
 U=H_1^{-1/2}WH_0^{1/2}.
\]
The first congruence in \(\text{def:raw-fiber}\) is equivalent to
\[
 UU^{\mathsf T}=H_1^{-1/2}WH_0W^{\mathsf T}H_1^{-1/2}=I_p.
\]
Because \(U\) is square, this is equivalent to \(U\in O(p)\), and the inverse change of variables is \(W=H_1^{1/2}UH_0^{-1/2}\).  Substitution in the second congruence gives
\[
 URU^{\mathsf T}=S,
 \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad SU=UR.
\]
The real symmetric matrices \(R\) and \(S\) admit an orthogonal intertwiner only if their spectra, including multiplicities, agree.  Conversely, if they agree, the relation \(SU=UR\) says that \(U\) maps each \(\lambda\)-eigenspace of \(R\) isometrically onto the corresponding eigenspace of \(S\).  Relative to the declared orthonormal frames this gives, uniquely,
\[
 U=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}Q_\lambda O_\lambda P_\lambda^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad O_\lambda=Q_\lambda^{\mathsf T}UP_\lambda\in O(m_\lambda).
\]

The two mean equations in \(\text{def:raw-fiber}\) combine as
\[
 U H_0^{-1/2}[d_0,d_1]=H_1^{-1/2}[d_1,d_2].
\]
Projection onto the \(\lambda\)-eigenspace of \(S\) is therefore exactly
\[
 O_\lambda A_\lambda=B_\lambda.
\]
Orthogonality makes the block Gram identity necessary:
\[
 B_\lambda^{\mathsf T}B_\lambda
 =A_\lambda^{\mathsf T}O_\lambda^{\mathsf T}O_\lambda A_\lambda
 =A_\lambda^{\mathsf T}A_\lambda.
\]

For sufficiency, suppose that the spectra match and the Gram identity holds in every block.  On \(\operatorname{ran}(A_\lambda)\) define
\[
 L_\lambda(A_\lambda c)=B_\lambda c,
 \qquad c\in\mathbb R^2.
\]
This is well defined, since \(A_\lambda c=0\) implies
\[
 \lVert B_\lambda c\rVert_2^2
 =c^{\mathsf T}B_\lambda^{\mathsf T}B_\lambda c
 =c^{\mathsf T}A_\lambda^{\mathsf T}A_\lambda c=0.
\]
Polarization of the same identity shows that \(L_\lambda\) is an isometry onto \(\operatorname{ran}(B_\lambda)\).  Both ranges consequently have dimension \(r_\lambda\).  Choose orthonormal frames \(F_A,F_B\) for these ranges so that \(L_\lambda F_A=F_B\), and orthonormal frames \(N_A,N_B\) for their orthogonal complements.  Every orthogonal extension, and no other one, is
\[
 O_\lambda=F_BF_A^{\mathsf T}+N_BZ_\lambda N_A^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad Z_\lambda\in O(k_\lambda).
\]
It satisfies \(O_\lambda A_\lambda=B_\lambda\).  Thus the block completion set is a torsor for \(O(k_\lambda)\); the conventions are that \(O(0)\) is trivial and \(O(1)=\{-1,1\}\).  Since the tangent space of \(O(k)\) consists of skew-symmetric \(k\)-by-\(k\) matrices, it has dimension \(k(k-1)/2\).  Substitution into the inverse whitening map yields, exactly once for each collection of completions,
\[
 W=H_1^{1/2}\left(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
 Q_\lambda O_\lambda P_\lambda^{\mathsf T}\right)H_0^{-1/2}.
\]
This proves the nonemptiness certificate, the parameterization, and every asserted block dimension.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the complete observable certificate and stabilizer parameterization on which every identification claim rests. \textit{Gap.} AaltoLamolineGoncalves2022, Theorem 2.1, whitens two covariance equations with known propagated noise; it does not difference out an unknown common measurement covariance or give the blockwise two-probe Gram criterion and residual stabilizers. \textit{Consumer.} A gene-network analyst can enumerate every transition consistent with four unpaired noisy snapshot laws instead of reporting an arbitrary optimizer.

\begin{theorem}[thm:admissible-fiber]\label{thm:admissible-fiber}
The map \(W\mapsto\theta(W,z)\), whose last coordinate is \(\mu_0=m_0\), is a bijection from \(\mathcal F_{\mathrm{adm}}\) to all structural explanations of the four observed Gaussian snapshot laws. Equivalently, a raw candidate is compatible if and only if \(\rho(W)<1\), \(\operatorname{spec}(W)\cap(-\infty,0]=\varnothing\), and the Stein reconstruction satisfies \(E_W=C_0-T_W\succ0\). Global identification holds if and only if \(\mathcal F_{\mathrm{adm}}\) is a singleton.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The structural assumptions first imply observable restrictions.  By \(\text{ass:linear-flow}\), \(\text{ass:initial-gaussian}\), \(\text{ass:common-measurement-noise}\), and \(\text{ass:measurement-independence}\),
\[
 m_k=V^k(\mu_0-x^\star)+x^\star,
 \qquad C_k=V^k\Sigma(V^{\mathsf T})^k+E.
\]
Consequently common noise cancels and successive means difference to
\[
 H_j=V^j(\Sigma-V\Sigma V^{\mathsf T})(V^{\mathsf T})^j,
 \qquad d_j=V^j(V-I_p)(\mu_0-x^\star).
\]
Hence
\[
 VH_0V^{\mathsf T}=H_1,quad VH_1V^{\mathsf T}=H_2,quad
 Vd_0=d_1,quad Vd_1=d_2,
\]
so the transition of every structural explanation belongs to \(\mathcal F_{\mathrm{raw}}\).

If \(g\) is an eigenvalue of \(G\), spectral mapping gives an eigenvalue \(e^{hg}\) of \(V\).  Assumptions \(\text{ass:spacing}\) and \(\text{ass:hurwitz}\) give \(|e^{hg}|<1\), hence \(\rho(V)<1\).  A negative real value would require \(\operatorname{Im}(hg)=\pi\) modulo \(2\pi\), which \(\text{ass:alias-strip}\) excludes; zero is impossible for an exponential.  Thus \(\operatorname{spec}(V)\cap(-\infty,0]=\varnothing\).  Moreover
\[
 \Sigma-V\Sigma V^{\mathsf T}=H_0.
\]
The stable Stein equation has a unique solution, as shown below, so \(T_V=\Sigma\) and \(E_V=C_0-T_V=E\succ0\).  Every explanation therefore yields a member of \(\mathcal F_{\mathrm{adm}}\).

Conversely, fix \(W\in\mathcal F_{\mathrm{adm}}\).  Because \(\rho(W)<1\), the series
\[
 T_W=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}W^jH_0(W^{\mathsf T})^j
\]
converges and satisfies \(T_W-WT_WW^{\mathsf T}=H_0\).  Its zeroth summand and \(H_0\succ0\) imply \(T_W\succ0\).  It is unique because vectorization of the homogeneous equation gives
\[
 (I_{p^2}-W\otimes W)\operatorname{vec}(T)=0,
\]
and every eigenvalue of \(W\otimes W\) is a product of two eigenvalues of \(W\), of modulus less than one.  Set \(E_W=C_0-T_W\), which is positive definite by \(\text{def:admissible-fiber}\).  Then
\[
 WT_WW^{\mathsf T}+E_W=T_W-H_0+C_0=C_1.
\]
For \(\widetilde C_k=W^kT_W(W^{\mathsf T})^k+E_W\),
\[
 \widetilde C_k-\widetilde C_{k+1}=W^kH_0(W^{\mathsf T})^k.
\]
The two raw congruences make the right side equal to \(H_k\) for \(k=0,1,2\).  Since \(\widetilde C_0=C_0\), telescoping yields \(\widetilde C_k=C_k\) for all \(k\in K\).

Stability gives \(1\notin\operatorname{spec}(W)\), so define
\[
 x_W^\star=(I_p-W)^{-1}(m_1-Wm_0),
 \qquad \widetilde m_0=m_0,
 \qquad \widetilde m_{k+1}=W\widetilde m_k+(I_p-W)x_W^\star.
\]
Then \(\widetilde m_1=m_1\), and its increments satisfy
\[
 \widetilde m_{k+1}-\widetilde m_k
 =W(\widetilde m_k-\widetilde m_{k-1}).
\]
The raw equations \(Wd_0=d_1\) and \(Wd_1=d_2\) therefore imply \(\widetilde m_2=m_2\) and \(\widetilde m_3=m_3\).  The reconstructed initial mean is \(\mu_0=m_0\).

The spectral-cut condition places \(W\) in the domain of the principal matrix logarithm.  On a complex Jordan block with eigenvalue \(w\), its eigenvalue is \(\log|w|+i\operatorname{Arg}(w)\), with \(-\pi<\operatorname{Arg}(w)<\pi\).  Conjugate blocks give conjugate logarithms, so \(\operatorname{Log}(W)\) is real.  Thus
\[
 G_W=h^{-1}\operatorname{Log}(W)
\]
is real, has imaginary parts in the required alias strip, and is Hurwitz because \(|w|<1\).  Also \(\exp(hG_W)=W\).  If a competing real logarithm \(L\) has spectrum in the same open strip, the holomorphic identity \(\operatorname{Log}(e^z)=z\) on that strip and matrix functional calculus give \(L=\operatorname{Log}(e^L)=\operatorname{Log}(W)\).  Hence the generator is unique.

Take \(X_0\sim\mathcal N(m_0,T_W)\), generate the deterministic affine trajectory using \((W,x_W^\star)\), and add mutually independent \(\mathcal N(0,E_W)\) errors independent of \(X_0\).  The preceding equations give exactly the means \(m_k\) and covariances \(C_k\); Gaussian laws are determined by these moments, so all four observed marginal laws are reproduced.  Necessity recovers each coordinate of any explanation from its transition, while \(\theta(W,z)\) contains \(W\) itself.  The map is therefore both surjective and injective.  Its image is precisely the structural identified set, and it is a singleton exactly when \(\mathcal F_{\mathrm{adm}}\) is a singleton.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The algebraic orthogonal fiber becomes scientifically meaningful only after stability, logarithm, Stein, and noise-positivity filters are jointly shown necessary and sufficient. \textit{Gap.} Neither AaltoLamolineGoncalves2022 nor WangEtAl2024 characterizes the compatible level-covariance and continuous-time generator fiber when the same unknown positive-definite measurement covariance contaminates every snapshot. \textit{Consumer.} The public \(\texttt{covar\_lin\_dyn}\) workflow can add joint calibration of \(E\) and an exact ambiguity certificate before interpreting a recovered interaction matrix.

\begin{theorem}[thm:snapshot-frontier]\label{thm:snapshot-frontier}
For model-compatible three-snapshot laws, the exact dimension-dependent frontier is as follows.  If \(p=2\) and \(d_0\ne0\), the two raw \(O(1)\) completions have opposite determinants, the negative-determinant completion fails the principal-logarithm cut, and the admissible transition and full structural explanation are unique.  If \(p=2\) and \(d_0=0\), a continuous local \(O(2)\) ambiguity remains.  If \(p\ge3\), every model-compatible law has continuous local ambiguity: its dimension is \((p-1)(p-2)/2\) when \(d_0\ne0\) and \(p(p-1)/2\) when \(d_0=0\).  The condition \(d_0\ne0\) is generic in the mean parameter.  With four snapshots, \(\det K_0\ne0\) implies the rational recovery identity \(V=K_1K_0^{-1}\), and \(\det K_0\ne0\) holds outside a proper algebraic subset of the admissible parameter domain.  Thus three snapshots generically identify the full explanation when \(p=2\); a fourth snapshot is generically necessary when \(p\ge3\) and is generically sufficient on the cyclic chart whenever the recovered candidate passes the admissibility filters.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The three-snapshot certificate and exact local dimensions follow from \(\text{thm:three-snapshot-exact-classification}\).  For \(p=2\) and \(d_0\ne0\), it gives two opposite-determinant raw completions and proves that the negative-determinant member fails the principal-logarithm cut.  Model compatibility supplies one admissible member, hence the transition and, by the Stein, logarithm, equilibrium, and initial-mean reconstructions, the full explanation are unique.  If \(p=2\) and \(d_0=0\), a relatively open part of an \(O(2)\) torsor remains and has dimension one.  If \(p\ge3\), the local dimension is
\[
 \frac{(p-1)(p-2)}2>0\quad\text{when }d_0\ne0,
 \qquad
 \frac{p(p-1)}2>0\quad\text{when }d_0=0.
\]
Moreover, \(d_0=(V-I_p)(\mu_0-x^\star)\), and \(V-I_p\) is invertible because \(\rho(V)<1\).  Thus \(d_0=0\) is exactly the proper affine locus \(\mu_0=x^\star\), so \(d_0\ne0\) is generic in the mean parameter.  This proves the three-snapshot side of the frontier.

For four snapshots, \(H_0,H_1,H_2\succ0\) by \(\text{ass:strict-decrease}\).  If \(W\in\mathcal F_{\mathrm{raw}}\), then \(WH_0W^{\mathsf T}=H_1\succ0\) makes \(W\) invertible.  Using both raw congruences,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 M_1W
 &=H_2H_1^{-1}W\\
 &=(WH_1W^{\mathsf T})(WH_0W^{\mathsf T})^{-1}W\\
 &=WH_1W^{\mathsf T}W^{-\mathsf T}H_0^{-1}W^{-1}W
 =WH_1H_0^{-1}=WM_0.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Since \(d_1=Wd_0\), induction gives
\[
 M_1^jd_1=WM_0^jd_0,\qquad 0\leq j<p.
\]
Columnwise this is \(K_1=WK_0\).  Hence, whenever \(\det K_0\ne0\), every raw candidate equals the observable rational map
\[
 W=K_1K_0^{-1}=\mathcal K((m_k),(C_k)).
\]
In particular the causal transition, which was defined structurally before this calculation, satisfies \(V=K_1K_0^{-1}\); therefore there is at most one raw candidate on the cyclic chart.

To show genericity, choose pairwise distinct \(v_i\in(0,1)\) and set
\[
 V=\operatorname{diag}(v_1,\ldots,v_p),\qquad
 G=h^{-1}\operatorname{diag}(\log v_1,\ldots,\log v_p),
 \qquad \Sigma=E=I_p.
\]
Choose \(u=\mu_0-x^\star\) with every coordinate nonzero.  Then all assumptions and strict filters hold, and
\[
 H_j=\operatorname{diag}\bigl(v_i^{2j}(1-v_i^2)\bigr)\succ0,\qquad
 M_0=\operatorname{diag}(v_1^2,\ldots,v_p^2),\qquad
 d_0=(V-I_p)u.
\]
The \(i\)-th row of \(K_0\) is
\[
 d_{0i}(1,v_i^2,\ldots,v_i^{2(p-1)}),
\]
so the Vandermonde formula gives
\[
 \det K_0=\left(\prod_{i=1}^pd_{0i}\right)
 \prod_{1\leq i<j\leq p}(v_j^2-v_i^2)\ne0.
\]
On \(\det H_0\ne0\), each entry of \(M_0=H_1\operatorname{adj}(H_0)/\det H_0\), and hence \(\det K_0\), is rational in the structural parameters.  Clearing a power of \(\det H_0\) yields a polynomial numerator.  The example makes that numerator nonzero, so it is not the zero polynomial.  A nonzero real polynomial has a zero set with empty interior, by induction on the number of variables using the finite-root property in one variable.  Thus failure of cyclicity lies in a proper algebraic subset of the admissible parameter domain.

On the complement, the rational map gives the only raw candidate.  For a model-compatible law it is the true \(V\), so it passes all filters; for an arbitrary proposed law the filters in \(\text{def:admissible-fiber}\) are the existence check.  The bijection in \(\text{thm:admissible-fiber}\) then reconstructs the unique generator, latent covariance, measurement covariance, equilibrium, and initial mean.  Thus a fourth snapshot is generically necessary for \(p\ge3\) and generically sufficient on the cyclic chart, with the stated dimension-two elbow.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem locates the exact snapshot threshold by dimension: three snapshots generically suffice in dimension two through the determinant obstruction, while the fourth destructive snapshot is the generic identification resource in dimensions at least three. \textit{Gap.} Known-state-covariance results do not cover the shared unknown additive covariance and therefore miss both sides of the elbow: the negative-determinant rejection that resolves the generic \(p=2\) raw pair and the positive-dimensional \(O(p-1)\) fiber that persists generically for \(p\ge3\). \textit{Consumer.} Experimental designers receive an observable population certificate for three-snapshot compatibility and a sharp collection rule: a fourth destructive snapshot is generically required for \(p\ge3\), while generic model-compatible \(p=2\) systems with nonzero mean displacement are already identified from three.

\begin{theorem}[thm:information-nullity]\label{thm:information-nullity}
At every compatible explanation, \[\dim\ker(D_WF)=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\frac{k_\lambda(k_\lambda-1)}{2}.\] The derivative of the full Gaussian parameter-to-moment map has the same kernel after the Stein, measurement-covariance, equilibrium, and initial-mean coordinates are included. Consequently the branchwise Fisher information is nonsingular exactly on the finite raw strata \(k_\lambda\le1\) for all \(\lambda\). The Fisher-information claim is for the destructive-snapshot experiment with independent samples drawn separately from the four marginal laws.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(A\) perturb a compatible transition \(W\).  Differentiating \(\text{def:residual-map}\) gives
\[
 D_WF[A]=\bigl(
 \operatorname{vech}(AH_0W^{\mathsf T}+WH_0A^{\mathsf T}),
 \operatorname{vech}(AH_1W^{\mathsf T}+WH_1A^{\mathsf T}),
 Ad_0,Ad_1\bigr).
\]
Set
\[
 U=H_1^{-1/2}WH_0^{1/2}\in O(p),\qquad
 Z=H_1^{-1/2}AH_0^{1/2}.
\]
The first linearized congruence is \(ZU^{\mathsf T}+UZ^{\mathsf T}=0\), so \(Z=UJ\) for a unique skew-symmetric \(J\).  Substitution in the second linearized congruence yields
\[
 JR+RJ^{\mathsf T}=0,\qquad\text{hence}\qquad JR=RJ.
\]
The mean equations become
\[
 JH_0^{-1/2}d_0=0,\qquad JH_0^{-1/2}d_1=0.
\]
Since \(R\) is symmetric, commutation makes \(J\) block diagonal in the eigenspaces of \(R\).  In block \(\lambda\), the last equations say that \(J_\lambda\) annihilates \(\operatorname{ran}(A_\lambda)\).  Relative to this \(r_\lambda\)-dimensional range and its orthogonal complement,
\[
 J_\lambda=0_{r_\lambda}\oplus\widetilde J_\lambda,\qquad
 \widetilde J_\lambda^{\mathsf T}=-\widetilde J_\lambda,\qquad
 \widetilde J_\lambda\in\mathbb R^{k_\lambda\times k_\lambda}.
\]
All changes of variables are invertible, and the skew-symmetric \(k\)-by-\(k\) matrices have dimension \(k(k-1)/2\).  Therefore
\[
 \dim\ker(D_WF)=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
 \frac{k_\lambda(k_\lambda-1)}2.
\]

Consider next the full structural coordinate vector \((W,T,E,x^\star,\mu_0)\).  A zero moment derivative has zero derivatives of every \(H_j,d_j\), so its \(W\)-component belongs to the kernel just computed.  Conversely, for \(A\in\ker(D_WF)\), the perturbation \(\dot T\) that keeps \(H_0=T-WTW^{\mathsf T}\) fixed is uniquely determined by
\[
 \dot T-W\dot TW^{\mathsf T}=ATW^{\mathsf T}+WTA^{\mathsf T},
\]
because \(\rho(W)<1\) makes the Stein operator invertible.  Keeping \(C_0=T+E\) fixed forces \(\dot E=-\dot T\); keeping \(m_0=\mu_0\) fixed forces \(\dot\mu_0=0\); and keeping
\[
 m_1=W\mu_0+(I_p-W)x^\star
\]
fixed uniquely forces
\[
 \dot x^\star=-(I_p-W)^{-1}A(\mu_0-x^\star).
\]
The equations \(Ad_0=Ad_1=0\) then keep \(m_2,m_3\) fixed, and the two linearized congruences keep \(C_2,C_3\) fixed.  Projection onto \(A\) is consequently a linear bijection between the full moment-map kernel and \(\ker(D_WF)\).  The principal logarithm is locally a smooth inverse of the exponential on the alias-strip chart, so replacing \(W\) by \(G_W\) does not change nullity.

For one nonsingular Gaussian marginal, a mean perturbation \(u\) and symmetric covariance perturbation \(A_C\) have Fisher quadratic form
\[
 u^{\mathsf T}C^{-1}u+
 \frac12\operatorname{tr}(C^{-1}A_CC^{-1}A_C).
\]
This follows by differentiating the Gaussian log density and taking its second moment.  Writing \(A_C=C^{1/2}BC^{1/2}\), the second term is \(\operatorname{tr}(B^2)/2\), so the form vanishes exactly when \(u=0\) and \(A_C=0\).  Under \(\text{ass:unpaired-sampling}\), the destructive experiment consists of independent samples from the four marginal laws; its Fisher form is the positive sum of the four marginal forms and has exactly the full moment-map kernel.  Its nullity is therefore the displayed stabilizer dimension.  It is zero exactly when every \(k_\lambda\leq1\), which by \(\text{thm:block-certificate}\) is exactly the finite raw-stratum condition.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This equality turns geometric nonidentification into an exact statistical-information diagnosis. \textit{Gap.} Drton2009 derives tangent-cone limits for general semialgebraic singularities, but no inspected work identifies the nullity of snapshot information with an orthogonal stabilizer dimension. \textit{Consumer.} Branch diagnostics can use \(\gamma_W\) to distinguish a regular repeated-eigenvalue branch from a genuinely positive-dimensional ambiguity.

\begin{theorem}[thm:cyclic-perturbation]\label{thm:cyclic-perturbation}
On the cyclic chart \(\det K_0\ne0\), suppose every mean and covariance coordinate of \(\widehat z\) differs from its population counterpart by at most \(\epsilon\) in Euclidean or operator norm, respectively. If \(\epsilon\le\min\{a_0/4,1/c_M,\kappa/(2c_K)\}\), then \(\widehat W_{\mathrm{cyc}}=\widehat K_1\widehat K_0^{-1}\) is defined and \(\lVert\widehat W_{\mathrm{cyc}}-V\rVert\le c_V\epsilon\). If in addition \(\epsilon<\epsilon_{\mathrm{rec}}\), then \(\widehat W_{\mathrm{cyc}}\) remains stable and off the principal-logarithm cut, its Stein operator is invertible, and every coordinate of the full plug-in \(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{cyc}}=\theta(\widehat W_{\mathrm{cyc}},\widehat z)\) is defined. If \(\epsilon<\epsilon_{\mathrm{adm}}\), then \(\widehat T_{\mathrm{cyc}}\succ0\) and \(\widehat E_{\mathrm{cyc}}\succ0\), so every dynamical, logarithm, Stein, and covariance-positivity admissibility filter is preserved. Quantitatively, \(\lVert\widehat T_{\mathrm{cyc}}-T_V\rVert\le c_T\epsilon\) and \(\lVert\widehat E_{\mathrm{cyc}}-E_V\rVert\le c_E\epsilon\), and the principal logarithm, equilibrium, and initial-mean coordinates obey linear bounds determined by their resolvent, \(\lVert(I_p-V)^{-1}\rVert\), and the moment error. These domain conclusions concern the reconstruction from perturbed moments; they do not assert the exact population raw equalities for \(\widehat W_{\mathrm{cyc}}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use operator norm for matrices, Euclidean norm for vectors, and Frobenius-induced operator norm on maps over \(\mathbb S^p\).  Write \(\Delta A=\widehat A-A\).  The coordinatewise perturbation condition gives
\[
 \lVert\Delta H_j\rVert\leq2\epsilon,\qquad
 \lVert\Delta d_j\rVert_2\leq2\epsilon.
\]
If \(\epsilon\leq a_0/4\), then \(\widehat H_i\succeq(a_0/2)I_p\) for \(i=0,1\), so
\[
 \lVert\widehat H_i^{-1}\rVert\leq2/a_0,\qquad
 \lVert H_i^{-1}\rVert\leq1/a_0.
\]
The inverse identity \(\widehat H_i^{-1}-H_i^{-1}=-\widehat H_i^{-1}(\Delta H_i)H_i^{-1}\) implies
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \lVert\widehat M_i-M_i\rVert
 &\leq \lVert\Delta H_{i+1}\rVert\lVert\widehat H_i^{-1}\rVert
 +\lVert H_{i+1}\rVert\lVert\widehat H_i^{-1}\rVert
   \lVert\Delta H_i\rVert\lVert H_i^{-1}\rVert\\
 &\leq\left(\frac4{a_0}+\frac{4B_{\mathrm{norm}}}{a_0^2}\right)\epsilon
 =c_M\epsilon.
 \end{aligned}
\]
If also \(\epsilon\leq1/c_M\), then the definition of \(L_M\) gives \(\max(\lVert M_i\rVert,\lVert\widehat M_i\rVert)\leq L_M\).  The telescoping identity
\[
 \widehat M_i^j-M_i^j
 =\sum_{\ell=0}^{j-1}\widehat M_i^\ell
 (\widehat M_i-M_i)M_i^{j-1-\ell}
\]
therefore yields
\[
 \lVert\widehat M_i^j\widehat d_i-M_i^jd_i\rVert_2
 \leq\bigl(2L_M^j+jL_M^{j-1}c_MD_{\mathrm{norm}}\bigr)\epsilon,
\]
with the second summand zero for \(j=0\).  Summing squared column bounds and using operator norm no larger than Frobenius norm proves
\[
 \lVert\widehat K_i-K_i\rVert\leq c_K\epsilon,\qquad i=0,1.
\]

On the cyclic chart, \(\kappa=\sigma_{\min}(K_0)>0\) and \(K_1=VK_0\) by \(\text{thm:snapshot-frontier}\).  If \(\epsilon\leq\kappa/(2c_K)\), then
\[
 \sigma_{\min}(\widehat K_0)\geq\kappa-c_K\epsilon\geq\kappa/2,
 \qquad \lVert\widehat K_0^{-1}\rVert\leq2/\kappa.
\]
Thus \(\widehat W_{\mathrm{cyc}}\) is defined and
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \widehat W_{\mathrm{cyc}}-V
 &=\bigl((\widehat K_1-K_1)-V(\widehat K_0-K_0)\bigr)\widehat K_0^{-1},\\
 \lVert\widehat W_{\mathrm{cyc}}-V\rVert
 &\leq\frac{2c_K(1+\lVert V\rVert)}\kappa\epsilon=c_V\epsilon.
 \end{aligned}
\]

Now suppose \(\epsilon<\epsilon_{\mathrm{rec}}\) and abbreviate \(\widehat W=\widehat W_{\mathrm{cyc}}\).  The definitions of \(\delta_{\mathrm{stab}}\) and \(\delta_{\log}\), together with
\[
 \lVert\widehat W-V\rVert<c_V\epsilon
 <\min\{\delta_{\mathrm{stab}}/2,\delta_{\log}/2,1\},
\]
show that \(\rho(\widehat W)<1\) and \(\operatorname{spec}(\widehat W)\cap(-\infty,0]=\varnothing\).  For the Stein operators,
\[
 (\mathcal L_{\widehat W}-\mathcal L_V)(A)
 =-(\widehat W-V)A\widehat W^{\mathsf T}-VA(\widehat W-V)^{\mathsf T},
\]
and \(\lVert\widehat W\rVert\leq\lVert V\rVert+1\), hence
\[
 \lVert\mathcal L_{\widehat W}-\mathcal L_V\rVert_{F\to F}
 \leq c_V(2\lVert V\rVert+1)\epsilon<\sigma_{\mathrm{Stein}}/2.
\]
Factoring \(\mathcal L_{\widehat W}\) through \(\mathcal L_V\) and applying the geometric inverse series gives
\[
 \lVert\mathcal L_{\widehat W}^{-1}\rVert_{F\to F}
 \leq2\sigma_{\mathrm{Stein}}^{-1}.
\]
Subtracting the population and perturbed Stein equations gives
\[
 \mathcal L_{\widehat W}(\widehat T_{\mathrm{cyc}}-T_V)
 =\Delta H_0-(\mathcal L_{\widehat W}-\mathcal L_V)(T_V).
\]
Since \(\lVert\Delta H_0\rVert_F\leq2\sqrt p\epsilon\),
\[
 \lVert\widehat T_{\mathrm{cyc}}-T_V\rVert
 \leq2\sigma_{\mathrm{Stein}}^{-1}
 [2\sqrt p+c_V(2\lVert V\rVert+1)\lVert T_V\rVert_F]\epsilon
 =c_T\epsilon.
\]
It follows that
\[
 \lVert\widehat E_{\mathrm{cyc}}-E_V\rVert
 \leq\lVert\widehat C_0-C_0\rVert+
 \lVert\widehat T_{\mathrm{cyc}}-T_V\rVert
 \leq c_E\epsilon.
\]
If \(\epsilon<\epsilon_{\mathrm{adm}}\), Weyl's variational inequality gives
\[
 \lambda_{\min}(\widehat T_{\mathrm{cyc}})>t_{\min}/2>0,
 \qquad \lambda_{\min}(\widehat E_{\mathrm{cyc}})>e_{\min}/2>0.
\]

For the remaining coordinates, let
\[
 \mathcal B=\{U:\lVert U-V\rVert\leq\delta_{\log}/2\},\qquad
 Q_t(U)=I_p+t(U-I_p).
\]
Every \(Q_t(U)\) is invertible for \(U\in\mathcal B\) and \(t\in[0,1]\): for \(t>0\), singularity would put \(-(1-t)/t\) in \(\operatorname{spec}(U)\cap(-\infty,0]\).  Compactness therefore makes
\[
 q_0=\sup_{U\in\mathcal B,,0\leq t\leq1}\lVert Q_t(U)^{-1}\rVert<\infty,
 \qquad b_0=\sup_{U\in\mathcal B}\lVert U-I_p\rVert<\infty.
\]
Jordan-block integration of the scalar identity
\(
 \frac{d}{dt}\log(1+t(z-1))=(z-1)/(1+t(z-1))
\)
gives
\[
 \operatorname{Log}(U)=\int_0^1(U-I_p)Q_t(U)^{-1}\,dt.
\]
The resolvent identity then gives, for \(A,B\in\mathcal B\),
\[
 \lVert\operatorname{Log}(A)-\operatorname{Log}(B)\rVert
 \leq\left(q_0+\frac{b_0q_0^2}{2}\right)\lVert A-B\rVert.
\]
Thus the generator error is at most
\[
 h^{-1}\left(q_0+\frac{b_0q_0^2}{2}\right)c_V\epsilon.
\]

Let \(\eta=\lVert(I_p-V)^{-1}\rVert\).  The distance from \(I_p-V\) to singular matrices is \(\eta^{-1}\), and a singular \(I_p-U\) implies \(\rho(U)\geq1\); hence \(\delta_{\mathrm{stab}}\leq\eta^{-1}\).  Therefore \(\eta\lVert\widehat W-V\rVert<1/2\), and a geometric inverse series yields
\[
 \lVert(I_p-\widehat W)^{-1}\rVert\leq2\eta.
\]
Subtracting the two equilibrium equations gives the exact identity
\[
 (I_p-\widehat W)(\widehat x^\star-x^\star)
 =\Delta m_1-\widehat W\Delta m_0+(\widehat W-V)(x^\star-m_0),
\]
and hence
\[
 \lVert\widehat x^\star-x^\star\rVert
 \leq2\eta[2+\lVert V\rVert+c_V(\lVert m_0\rVert+\lVert x^\star\rVert)]\epsilon.
\]
Finally, \(\widehat\mu_0=\widehat m_0\), so \(\lVert\widehat\mu_0-\mu_0\rVert\leq\epsilon\).  Every operation in the full plug-in is therefore defined for \(\epsilon<\epsilon_{\mathrm{rec}}\), and all strict covariance filters hold for \(\epsilon<\epsilon_{\mathrm{adm}}\).  Only the population identity \(K_1=VK_0\) was used; no empirical spectral match, probe-Gram equality, or exact residual equality was imposed.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The rational chart gives a usable estimator on noisy empirical moments without requiring exact sample spectra or exact probe-Gram identities. \textit{Gap.} AaltoLamolineGoncalves2022 gives local sensitivity of a loss minimizer, while KoltchinskiiLounici2017 controls spectral projectors; neither supplies a gap-free rational Krylov bound propagated through Stein and matrix-logarithm recovery for common-noise snapshots. \textit{Consumer.} The \(\texttt{covar\_lin\_dyn}\) consumer receives observable conditioning diagnostics and abstention margins for stable generator recovery.

\begin{theorem}[thm:branchwise-inference]\label{thm:branchwise-inference}
Fix a finite raw branch \(W\) with \(\gamma_W>0\), strict admissibility margins, and a ball containing no other raw branch. Choose any measurable total completion from \(\text{def:local-branch-estimator}\). On a sufficiently small neighborhood of the population moment \(z\), the branch-local residual estimator \(\widehat W_{\mathrm{loc}}\), defined as the unique global minimizer over that fixed ball, is a smooth off-model extension. Under independent Gaussian snapshot sampling its full moment plug-in \(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{loc}}=\theta(\widehat W_{\mathrm{loc}},\widehat z)\), including the initial-mean coordinate \(\widehat\mu_0=\widehat m_0\), satisfies \[\sqrt N\bigl(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{loc}}-\theta(W,z)\bigr)\;\Longrightarrow\;\mathcal N(0,J_\theta\Omega J_\theta^{\mathsf T}).\] Replacing population moments and the recovered local branch in \(J_\theta\Omega J_\theta^{\mathsf T}\) yields a consistent branchwise covariance estimator.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Identify \(U\) with \(u=\operatorname{vec}(U)\), write \(f(u;\zeta)=F_{\mathrm{res}}(U;\zeta)\), and define
\[
 Q(u;\zeta)=\frac12\lVert f(u;\zeta)\rVert_2^2,
 \qquad \Psi(u;\zeta)=D_uf(u;\zeta)^{\mathsf T}f(u;\zeta).
\]
At the exact branch \((w,z)\), \(f(w;z)=0\), and therefore
\[
 D_u\Psi(w;z)=D_uf(w;z)^{\mathsf T}D_uf(w;z).
\]
The condition \(\gamma_W>0\) makes this matrix positive definite, with least eigenvalue \(\gamma_W^2\).  Put \(A_0=[D_u\Psi(w;z)]^{-1}\) and \(\Phi_\zeta(u)=u-A_0\Psi(u;\zeta)\).  Since \(D_u\Phi_z(w)=0\), continuity supplies a closed ball \(B_0\) about \(w\), contained in the interior of the fixed isolating ball, and a neighborhood \(\mathcal Z_0\) of \(z\) such that every \(\Phi_\zeta\) maps \(B_0\) into itself and has a common Lipschitz constant smaller than one.  Iteration is then Cauchy at a geometric rate, and completeness of \(B_0\) gives a unique fixed point \(u(\zeta)\).  It is the unique stationary point of \(Q(\,\cdot\,;\zeta)\) in \(B_0\).  Continuity of the Hessian from its positive-definite value at \((w,z)\) makes this point a strict local minimizer.

We now prove that the definition's global selector equals this contraction-selected point; nonzero-residual local minima elsewhere in the isolating ball are irrelevant.  Choose a smaller open neighborhood \(B_1\) of \(w\) whose closure lies in \(B_0\).  At \(z\), the only zero of \(f(\,\cdot\,;z)\) in the fixed closed ball is \(w\).  The compact complement of \(B_1\) in that ball therefore has a positive minimum
\[
 c=\min_{u\in\mathcal B_r(w)\setminus B_1}Q(u;z)>0.
\]
Uniform continuity on this compact set gives, after shrinking \(\mathcal Z_0\),
\[
 \inf_{u\in\mathcal B_r(w)\setminus B_1}Q(u;\zeta)>c/2,
 \qquad Q(w;\zeta)<c/2.
\]
Continuity and compactness ensure that a global minimizer over the fixed ball exists, and these inequalities force every such minimizer into \(B_1\).  It is then an interior stationary point and hence equals the unique contraction fixed point \(u(\zeta)\).  Thus the argmin in the corrected \(\text{def:local-branch-estimator}\) is a singleton throughout \(\mathcal Z_0\), despite any extraneous nonzero-residual local minima outside \(B_1\). Every allowed total completion agrees with this unique smooth selector on \(\mathcal Z_0\). Since \(\widehat z\to z\) in probability, \(\mathbb P(\widehat z\notin\mathcal Z_0)\to0\); the fixed measurable fallback therefore changes the estimator only on an event of vanishing probability.

The map \(f\) is polynomial in \(u\) and affine in the moment coordinates, hence smooth.  Differentiating \(\Psi(u(\zeta);\zeta)=0\) at \(z\) is legitimate because \(D_u\Psi(w;z)\) is invertible; equivalently, subtracting the fixed-point equations and using the inverse linearization gives
\[
 Du(z)[a]=-\bigl(D_WF^{\mathsf T}D_WF\bigr)^{-1}
 D_WF^{\mathsf T}D_zF[a].
\]
Repeated differentiation gives smoothness.  Strict stability, logarithm-cut, Stein, covariance-positivity, and equilibrium margins make every operation in \(\theta(U,\zeta)\) smooth near \((W,z)\): the Stein coordinate follows by inversion of its nonsingular linear operator, the principal logarithm by its local resolvent integral, and the equilibrium by inversion of \(I_p-U\).  Hence
\[
 g(\zeta)=\theta(U(\zeta),\zeta)
\]
is smooth and \(Dg(z)=J_\theta\).

It remains to derive the sampling limit.  For snapshot \(k\), write \(Y_k=m_k+Z_k\) with \(Z_k\sim\mathcal N(0,C_k)\), and define the centered single-observation moment vector
\[
 \xi_k=\bigl(Z_k,\operatorname{vech}(Z_kZ_k^{\mathsf T}-C_k)\bigr).
\]
It has finite moments of every order.  Differentiating the Gaussian moment-generating function gives
\[
 \mathbb E[Z_{ki}Z_{kj}Z_{k\ell}]=0,\qquad
 \operatorname{Cov}(Z_{ki}Z_{kj},Z_{kr}Z_{ks})
 =(C_k)_{ir}(C_k)_{js}+(C_k)_{is}(C_k)_{jr}.
\]
Thus the mean and covariance influence blocks are uncorrelated.  If \(L_p\) is the elimination matrix and \(\mathsf K_p\) the commutation matrix, the snapshot contribution in interlaced coordinates is
\[
 \Omega_k=\frac1{\pi_k}\operatorname{diag}\left(
 C_k,\ L_p(I_{p^2}+\mathsf K_p)(C_k\otimes C_k)L_p^{\mathsf T}\right).
\]
The usual sample covariance has the same first-order influence function: centering by the sample mean changes the average of \(Z_kZ_k^{\mathsf T}\) by an outer product of order \(O_{\mathbb P}(n_k^{-1})\), and using divisor \(n_k-1\) changes it by another \(O_{\mathbb P}(n_k^{-1})\) term.

For any fixed vector \(t\), finite second moments give
\[
 \mathbb E\exp\{it^{\mathsf T}\xi_k/\sqrt n\}
 =1-\frac{t^{\mathsf T}\operatorname{Cov}(\xi_k)t}{2n}+o(n^{-1}),
\]
so its \(n\)-th power converges to the Gaussian characteristic function.  By \(\text{ass:unpaired-sampling}\), samples from different snapshot labels are independent; by \(\text{ass:sample-shares}\), \(n_k/N\to\pi_k\in(0,1)\).  Combining the four characteristic functions yields
\[
 \sqrt N(\widehat z-z)\Longrightarrow\mathcal N(0,\Omega),
\]
where, if \(\mathsf P\) permutes the interlaced ordering into the declared ordering of \(z\),
\[
 \Omega=\mathsf P\operatorname{diag}(\Omega_0,\Omega_1,\Omega_2,\Omega_3)\mathsf P^{\mathsf T}.
\]
All correlations induced later by forming adjacent differences are retained because those differences are fixed linear maps of this full moment vector.

Taylor expansion of the smooth branch map now gives
\[
 \sqrt N\{g(\widehat z)-g(z)\}
 =J_\theta\sqrt N(\widehat z-z)+o_{\mathbb P}(1),
\]
and therefore
\[
 \sqrt N\bigl(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{loc}}-\theta(W,z)\bigr)
 \Longrightarrow\mathcal N(0,J_\theta\Omega J_\theta^{\mathsf T}).
\]
The last coordinate is \(\widehat\mu_0=\widehat m_0\).  Sample moments converge to their population values, the sample shares converge to \(\pi_k\), and the unique local solution converges to \(W\).  The displayed Gaussian fourth-moment formula and continuity of the branch derivatives then show that replacing \(C_k,\pi_k,W,J_\theta\), and \(\Omega\) by their empirical plug-in versions consistently estimates \(J_\theta\Omega J_\theta^{\mathsf T}\).  This is branchwise inference conditional on the specified regular population branch and requires no exact empirical spectral or Gram equality.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Regular finite fibers require inference for every specified explanation rather than a standard error attached to an arbitrary local optimum.  The fixed-ball global criterion and compact residual gap make the population-indexed branch selector unique near the law. \textit{Gap.} MorimotoKashima2025 provides an iterative fit and AaltoLamolineGoncalves2022 provides local sensitivity, but neither proves that a fixed isolating-ball global residual projection is the unique smooth selector near each regular common-noise branch or derives its Gaussian plug-in law. \textit{Consumer.} Applied users who have a certified population branch neighborhood can report a confidence region for that explanation without silently selecting a spurious nonzero-residual local minimum; global discovery and labeling of all such neighborhoods remains separate.

\begin{proposition}[prop:known-noise-reduction]\label{prop:known-noise-reduction}
Suppose that the common measurement covariance \(E\) is supplied, and set \(\widetilde C_k=C_k-E\).  Within the present stable, principal-log-embeddable, Gaussian, zero-process-noise structural model, the transition fiber compatible with the four observed marginal laws is exactly \(\mathcal F_{\mathrm{known}}(E)\).  Its covariance restrictions are \(W\widetilde C_kW^{\mathsf T}=\widetilde C_{k+1}\) for \(k=0,1,2\), and its residual-noise restriction is the equality \(E_W=E\), equivalently \(C_0-E-T_W=0\), rather than only the unknown-noise inequality \(C_0-T_W\succ0\).  With only snapshots \(k=0,1,2\), the covariance and affine-mean consequences are exactly \(W\widetilde C_0W^{\mathsf T}=\widetilde C_1\), \(W\widetilde C_1W^{\mathsf T}=\widetilde C_2\), and \(Wd_0=d_1\).  Under the identification of the published discrete transition with \(W\), its state covariances with \(\widetilde C_k\), and its known process covariance with \(Q=0\), these are an equation-level subclass of AaltoLamolineGoncalves2022, Theorem 2.1 and Corollary 2.1.  The inclusion is strict at model scope: their full-state system permits a known nonzero process covariance, an invertible discrete-time transition without stability or continuous-time embeddability restrictions, and finite-second-moment non-Gaussian laws, whereas it has no measurement-error layer.  Thus only the displayed moment-equation consequence, not equality of observation-model classes, is inherited.  If the two three-snapshot whitened congruence invariants have the same simple spectrum and every source probe coordinate is nonzero, the three-snapshot equations are computably reduced to at most \(2^p\) sign candidates.  The candidates for \(\mathcal F_{\mathrm{known}}(E)\) are then tested against the remaining fourth-snapshot congruence and probe equation and against the stability, principal-logarithm, Stein, and equality \(E_W=E\) filters.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let \(\mathcal C_4(E)\) denote, only within this proof, the set of transition matrices that occur in structural explanations of the four observed marginal laws with the supplied measurement covariance \(E\), subject to \(\text{ass:spacing}\), \(\text{ass:linear-flow}\), \(\text{ass:hurwitz}\), \(\text{ass:alias-strip}\), \(\text{ass:initial-gaussian}\), \(\text{ass:common-measurement-noise}\), and \(\text{ass:measurement-independence}\).  Thus \(\mathcal C_4(E)\) is a causal-model compatibility set introduced for the argument; the observable set \(\mathcal F_{\mathrm{known}}(E)\) remains the functional defined in \(\text{def:known-noise-fiber}\).  We prove the non-definitional equality
\[
 \mathcal C_4(E)=\mathcal F_{\mathrm{known}}(E).
\]

For necessity, take \(W\in\mathcal C_4(E)\).  Write \(L_k=\operatorname{Cov}(X_k)\).  By \(\text{ass:linear-flow}\) and \(\text{ass:initial-gaussian}\),
\[
 \mathbb E[X_k]=W^k(\mu_0-x^\star)+x^\star,
 \qquad
 L_k=W^k\Sigma(W^{\mathsf T})^k .
\]
Because \(W=\exp(hG)\) is invertible and \(\Sigma\succ0\), congruence by \(W^k\) yields \(L_k\succ0\) for every \(k\in K\).  The errors have zero mean and covariance \(E\) by \(\text{ass:common-measurement-noise}\).  By \(\text{ass:measurement-independence}\), each \(\varepsilon_k\) is independent of \(X_0\), and therefore is independent of the deterministic affine function \(X_k\) of \(X_0\).  Hence
\[
 m_k=\mathbb E[X_k],
 \qquad
 C_k=L_k+E,
 \qquad
 \widetilde C_k=C_k-E=L_k\succ0.
\]
The covariance recursion and the affine mean formula give
\[
 W\widetilde C_kW^{\mathsf T}=\widetilde C_{k+1}\quad(k=0,1,2)
\]
and
\[
 d_k=m_{k+1}-m_k
     =W^k(W-I_p)(\mu_0-x^\star)\quad(k=0,1,2).
\]
Consequently \(Wd_0=d_1\) and \(Wd_1=d_2\).

We next check the structural filters.  Over \(\mathbb C\), triangularize \(G\).  The power-series definition of the exponential then shows that the diagonal entries of \(\exp(hG)\) are \(e^{hg}\), where \(g\) ranges over the eigenvalues of \(G\).  Thus \(h>0\) from \(\text{ass:spacing}\) and \(\operatorname{Re}(g)<0\) from \(\text{ass:hurwitz}\) imply
\[
 \rho(W)=\max_{g\in\operatorname{spec}(G)}e^{h\operatorname{Re}(g)}<1.
\]
If \(e^{hg}\) were a negative real number, then \(h\operatorname{Im}(g)=(2\ell+1)\pi\) for some \(\ell\in\mathbb Z\).  This contradicts \(\text{ass:alias-strip}\), which gives \(-\pi<h\operatorname{Im}(g)<\pi\); moreover an exponential eigenvalue is never zero.  Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{spec}(W)\cap(-\infty,0]=\varnothing.
\]

Cancellation of the common covariance gives
\[
 H_0=C_0-C_1=L_0-L_1=L_0-WL_0W^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Thus \(L_0\) solves the Stein equation in the definition of \(T_W\).  If \(D-WDW^{\mathsf T}=0\), vectorization gives
\[
 \bigl(I_{p^2}-W\otimes W\bigr)\operatorname{vec}(D)=0.
\]
Triangularizing the two factors shows directly that the diagonal entries, hence the eigenvalues, of \(W\otimes W\) are the products \(\lambda_i\lambda_j\) of eigenvalues of \(W\).  Their moduli are strictly below one because \(\rho(W)<1\).  Hence \(I_{p^2}-W\otimes W\) is invertible, so \(D=0\).  The Stein solution is therefore unique, and
\[
 T_W=L_0=\widetilde C_0,
 \qquad
 E_W=C_0-T_W=C_0-\widetilde C_0=E.
\]
All conditions in \(\text{def:known-noise-fiber}\) hold.  This proves \(\mathcal C_4(E)\subseteq\mathcal F_{\mathrm{known}}(E)\).

For sufficiency, take \(W\in\mathcal F_{\mathrm{known}}(E)\), and define
\[
 \Sigma=\widetilde C_0,
 \qquad
 \mu_0=m_0,
 \qquad
 b=m_1-Wm_0.
\]
Membership in the fiber gives \(\Sigma\succ0\) and \(\rho(W)<1\).  In particular, \(1\notin\operatorname{spec}(W)\), so \(I_p-W\) is invertible.  Define
\[
 x^\star=(I_p-W)^{-1}b.
\]
Then \(b=(I_p-W)x^\star\), and the affine recursion \(u\mapsto Wu+b\), begun at \(m_0\), gives \(m_1\) at its first step.  The two probe restrictions in \(\text{def:known-noise-fiber}\) give the remaining observed means:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Wm_1+b
 &=m_1+W(m_1-m_0)
 =m_1+Wd_0
 =m_1+d_1=m_2,\\
 Wm_2+b
 &=m_2+W(m_2-m_1)
 =m_2+Wd_1
 =m_2+d_2=m_3.
 \end{aligned}
\]

The spectral-cut condition in the fiber implies that \(W\) is invertible and that every eigenvalue lies in the domain \(\mathbb C\setminus(-\infty,0]\) of the scalar principal logarithm.  To make the logarithm step explicit, put a complex Jordan block of \(W\) in the form \(J=\lambda I+N\), where \(N^s=0\) and \(-\pi<\operatorname{Arg}(\lambda)<\pi\), and set
\[
 \operatorname{Log}(J)
 =\bigl(\log|\lambda|+i\operatorname{Arg}(\lambda)\bigr)I
  +\sum_{r=1}^{s-1}\frac{(-1)^{r+1}}{r}\left(\frac{N}{\lambda}\right)^r.
\]
The nilpotent sum is finite, and formal composition of the finite nilpotent parts of the logarithm and exponential series gives \(\exp(\operatorname{Log}(J))=J\).  Since \(W\) is real, its nonreal Jordan data occur in conjugate pairs; the displayed construction respects conjugation and therefore represents a real matrix \(\operatorname{Log}(W)\).  Its eigenvalues are the scalar principal logarithms of the eigenvalues of \(W\).  It follows that
\[
 G_W=h^{-1}\operatorname{Log}(W)
\]
is real and satisfies
\[
 \exp(hG_W)=W,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Re}\operatorname{spec}(G_W)
 =h^{-1}\log|\operatorname{spec}(W)|<0,
 \qquad
 -\frac{\pi}{h}<\operatorname{Im}\operatorname{spec}(G_W)<\frac{\pi}{h}.
\]
Here division by \(h\) is valid by \(\text{ass:spacing}\).  Thus the constructed generator satisfies \(\text{ass:hurwitz}\) and \(\text{ass:alias-strip}\).  Moreover, if \(L\) is any logarithm of \(W\) whose spectrum lies in the open strip \(-\pi<\operatorname{Im}(z)<\pi\), the scalar identity \(\operatorname{Log}(e^z)=z\) holds on a neighborhood of its spectrum.  Applying the corresponding power series on each generalized eigenspace gives
\[
 L=\operatorname{Log}(e^L)=\operatorname{Log}(W).
\]
Thus the open alias strip introduces no further generator branch.

Now construct \(X_0\sim\mathcal N(m_0,\Sigma)\), set
\[
 X_k=W^k(X_0-x^\star)+x^\star,
\]
and take mutually independent \(\varepsilon_k\sim\mathcal N(0,E)\), independent of \(X_0\).  The supplied \(E\) is positive definite by \(\text{ass:common-measurement-noise}\), so these errors exist and satisfy the required support and independence conditions.  The preceding affine recursion gives \(\mathbb E[X_k]=m_k\).  The three level congruences in \(\text{def:known-noise-fiber}\) give inductively
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(X_k)
 =W^k\Sigma(W^{\mathsf T})^k
 =\widetilde C_k
 \qquad(k\in K).
\]
Therefore
\[
 \mathbb E[X_k+\varepsilon_k]=m_k,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Cov}(X_k+\varepsilon_k)=\widetilde C_k+E=C_k.
\]
Both laws are nonsingular Gaussian laws, and a Gaussian law is determined by its mean and covariance.  Hence the constructed marginal law at each snapshot equals the observed marginal law.  This proves \(W\in\mathcal C_4(E)\), and therefore proves the reverse inclusion.  Notice also that \(E_W=E\succ0\); fixing this residual equals the supplied covariance and is strictly more restrictive than the unknown-noise inequality \(C_0-T_W\succ0\).

If only snapshots \(0,1,2\) are retained, the same necessary calculation yields exactly
\[
 W\widetilde C_0W^{\mathsf T}=\widetilde C_1,
 \qquad
 W\widetilde C_1W^{\mathsf T}=\widetilde C_2,
 \qquad
 Wd_0=d_1.
\]
Conversely, these equations and \(\widetilde C_k\succ0\) reproduce the three covariance and affine-mean recursions after setting \(b=m_1-Wm_0\).  They do not silently supply the distinct stability, logarithm, Stein, or known-noise equality filters; those filters remain the restrictions required for membership in the present structural class.

By \(\text{lem:aalto-model-scope}\), the published full-state recursion has moment equations
\[
 P_{j+1}=AP_jA^{\mathsf T}+Q,
 \qquad
 \bar m_{j+1}=A\bar m_j+b.
\]
The substitutions
\[
 A=W,
 \qquad
 P_j=\widetilde C_j,
 \qquad
 Q=0,
 \qquad
 b=(I_p-W)x^\star
\]
map every present noise-subtracted moment equation into that published recursion.  This is only an equation-level inclusion: the published model exactly observes its state, while the present model observes a Gaussian state through an additional supplied common measurement-error covariance.  The inclusion is strict at model scope.  Indeed, the published recursion permits \(A=2I_p\), \(Q=0\), and any positive-definite initial covariance, whereas this transition is excluded here because \(\rho(2I_p)=2\) and every transition allowed by \(\text{ass:hurwitz}\) has spectral radius below one.  The cited scope also permits known nonzero \(Q\), does not impose continuous-time embeddability or the principal-log cut, and, by \(\text{lem:aalto-model-scope}\), needs only finite second moments for its moment argument.  This proves the asserted strict subclass comparison without equating the two observation-model classes.

It remains to prove the finite sign reduction.  For three supplied-noise snapshots define the positive-definite whitened invariants and whitened transition
\[
 R_0=\widetilde C_0^{-1/2}\widetilde C_1\widetilde C_0^{-1/2},
 \qquad
 R_1=\widetilde C_1^{-1/2}\widetilde C_2\widetilde C_1^{-1/2},
 \qquad
 U=\widetilde C_1^{-1/2}W\widetilde C_0^{1/2}.
\]
The square roots and inverses exist because the three \(\widetilde C_k\) are positive definite.  The first congruence is equivalent to \(UU^{\mathsf T}=I_p\), so \(U\) is orthogonal.  Substitution into the second congruence gives
\[
 UR_0U^{\mathsf T}=R_1.
\]
Suppose the two invariants have the same simple ordered spectrum.  Choose orthogonal eigendecompositions
\[
 R_0=P\Lambda P^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad
 R_1=Q_1\Lambda Q_1^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad
 \Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_p),
\]
where \(\lambda_i\ne\lambda_j\) for \(i\ne j\).  The matrix \(D=Q_1^{\mathsf T}UP\) is orthogonal and obeys \(D\Lambda D^{\mathsf T}=\Lambda\), hence \(D\Lambda=\Lambda D\).  Entrywise,
\[
 (\lambda_j-\lambda_i)D_{ij}=0.
\]
Simplicity forces \(D_{ij}=0\) when \(i\ne j\), and orthogonality then forces \(D_{ii}\in\{-1,1\}\).  Therefore every covariance-compatible transition is one of the explicit candidates
\[
 W_D=\widetilde C_1^{1/2}Q_1DP^{\mathsf T}\widetilde C_0^{-1/2},
 \qquad
 D=\operatorname{diag}(s_1,\ldots,s_p),
 \qquad
 s_i\in\{-1,1\}.
\]
This is a computable list of cardinality at most \(2^p\).  Define the computable probe coordinates
\[
 a=P^{\mathsf T}\widetilde C_0^{-1/2}d_0,
 \qquad
 c=Q_1^{\mathsf T}\widetilde C_1^{-1/2}d_1.
\]
The equation \(Wd_0=d_1\) is equivalent to \(Da=c\).  If every source coordinate \(a_i\) is nonzero, then every sign is forced by
\[
 s_i=\frac{c_i}{a_i},
\]
where division is valid precisely because \(a_i\ne0\); compatibility requires each ratio to lie in \(\{-1,1\}\).  For four-snapshot compatibility, test every surviving candidate against
\[
 W_D\widetilde C_2W_D^{\mathsf T}=\widetilde C_3,
 \qquad
 W_Dd_1=d_2,
\]
and against \(\rho(W_D)<1\), \(\operatorname{spec}(W_D)\cap(-\infty,0]=\varnothing\), existence of the Stein reconstruction, and \(E_{W_D}=E\).  The sign list exhausts the first two covariance congruences, and these final tests are exactly the remaining restrictions in \(\text{def:known-noise-fiber}\).  Hence their survivors are exactly \(\mathcal F_{\mathrm{known}}(E)\), completing the reduction and classification.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:three-snapshot-exact-classification]\label{thm:three-snapshot-exact-classification}
Assume \(p\ge 2\) and \(H_0,H_1\succ0\).  The three-snapshot raw fiber \(\mathcal F_3\) is nonempty if and only if
\[
 \lVert H_0^{-1/2}d_0\rVert_2=\lVert H_1^{-1/2}d_1\rVert_2.
\]
When this equality holds, put \(r=\operatorname{rank}(H_0^{-1/2}d_0)\in\{0,1\}\).  Under the bijection
\[
 U=H_1^{-1/2}WH_0^{1/2},
\]
the raw fiber is noncanonically a torsor for \(O(p-r)\), and its dimension is
\[
 \frac{(p-r)(p-r-1)}{2}.
\]
Its intersection with the strict filter set
\[
 \left\{W:\rho(W)<1,\ \operatorname{spec}(W)\cap(-\infty,0]=\varnothing,\ C_0-T_W\succ0\right\}
\]
is exactly the transition identified set for the three observed Gaussian snapshot laws and is relatively open in \(\mathcal F_3\).  Consequently every nonempty local admissible branch has the displayed dimension.  If \(d_0\ne0\) and \(p\ge3\), this dimension is positive.  If \(p=2\) and \(d_0\ne0\), the raw fiber consists of exactly two candidates with opposite determinants.  A real matrix with negative determinant has a negative real eigenvalue, so at most one of these candidates passes the principal-logarithm cut; for model-compatible observed laws exactly one passes all three filters, and the full structural explanation is identified.  This is the generic mean regime in dimension two.  If \(d_0=0\), nonemptiness is equivalent to \(d_1=0\), the raw fiber is a torsor for \(O(p)\), and continuous ambiguity occurs already at \(p=2\).  The continuous nonidentification conclusions for \(p\ge3\), and for \(d_0=0\) when \(p=2\), transfer to every unknown-common-measurement-covariance model whose model class contains the present stable, embeddable, deterministic Gaussian class as a subclass and has the same transition target.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By \(\text{ass:strict-decrease}\), \(H_0,H_1\succ0\).  Put
\[
 a=H_0^{-1/2}d_0,\qquad c=H_1^{-1/2}d_1,\qquad
 U=H_1^{-1/2}WH_0^{1/2}.
\]
The inverse map is \(W=H_1^{1/2}UH_0^{-1/2}\), and direct multiplication gives
\[
 WH_0W^{\mathsf T}=H_1\Longleftrightarrow U\in O(p),
 \qquad Wd_0=d_1\Longleftrightarrow Ua=c.
\]
Thus \(\mathcal F_3\) is in bijection with \(\{U\in O(p):Ua=c\}\).  Necessity of the norm condition follows from \(U^{\mathsf T}U=I_p\).  Conversely, if \(\lVert a\rVert_2=\lVert c\rVert_2>0\), extend the two normalized vectors to orthonormal bases and map one basis to the other; if the common norm is zero, every orthogonal matrix works.  This proves the exact nonemptiness certificate.

Fix a solution \(U_0\).  Every solution is uniquely \(U_0R_0\), where
\[
 R_0\in\operatorname{Stab}(a)=\{R\in O(p):Ra=a\}.
\]
If \(a=0\), the stabilizer is \(O(p)\).  If \(a\ne0\), it fixes \(\operatorname{span}(a)\) pointwise and is arbitrary orthogonal on \(a^\perp\), hence isomorphic to \(O(p-1)\).  Since \(r=\operatorname{rank}(a)\in\{0,1\}\), the fiber is a torsor for \(O(p-r)\).  The tangent space of \(O(s)\) consists of the skew-symmetric \(s\)-by-\(s\) matrices, so
\[
 \dim\mathcal F_3=\frac{(p-r)(p-r-1)}2.
\]

We next identify, rather than define, the filtered set.  Let \(W\in\mathcal F_3\) pass the three strict filters.  Stability makes
\[
 T_W=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}W^jH_0(W^{\mathsf T})^j
\]
convergent, positive definite, and the unique solution of \(T_W-WT_WW^{\mathsf T}=H_0\), by the same Kronecker-product argument used in \(\text{thm:admissible-fiber}\).  Put
\[
 E'=C_0-T_W\succ0,\qquad \mu'_0=m_0,\qquad
 x'=(I_p-W)^{-1}(m_1-Wm_0).
\]
The inverse exists because \(\rho(W)<1\).  The spectral cut and \(h>0\) give the unique real generator \(G'=h^{-1}\operatorname{Log}(W)\), which is Hurwitz and lies in the open alias strip, exactly as in \(\text{thm:admissible-fiber}\).

Generate \(X'_0\sim\mathcal N(m_0,T_W)\), the affine deterministic trajectory with \((W,x')\), and independent \(\mathcal N(0,E')\) measurement errors.  The equilibrium equation gives the means \(m_0,m_1\), while
\[
 Wm_1+(I_p-W)x'=m_1+W(m_1-m_0)=m_1+d_1=m_2
\]
uses \(Wd_0=d_1\).  For covariances,
\[
 T_W+E'=C_0,\qquad WT_WW^{\mathsf T}+E'=C_1,
\]
and the congruence \(WH_0W^{\mathsf T}=H_1\) gives
\[
 W^2T_W(W^{\mathsf T})^2+E'
 =C_0-H_0-H_1=C_2.
\]
Hence this construction reproduces the three Gaussian marginal laws.

Conversely, any structural explanation has
\[
 C_k=V^k\Sigma(V^{\mathsf T})^k+E,\qquad
 m_k=V^k(\mu_0-x^\star)+x^\star.
\]
Cancellation and differencing give \(VH_0V^{\mathsf T}=H_1\) and \(Vd_0=d_1\).  The Hurwitz, spacing, and alias-strip assumptions give stability and cut avoidance, while the unique Stein solution gives \(T_V=\Sigma\) and \(C_0-T_V=E\succ0\).  Thus the filtered intersection is exactly the transition identified set.  The filters are relatively open: stability and cut avoidance are open spectral conditions, and
\[
 \operatorname{vec}(T_W)=(I_{p^2}-W\otimes W)^{-1}\operatorname{vec}(H_0)
\]
is continuous on the stable set, after which strict positive definiteness is open.  Therefore every admissible point in a positive-dimensional torsor has a positive-dimensional local ambiguity.

If \(p=2\) and \(d_0\ne0\), then \(r=1\) and the stabilizer is \(O(1)=\{-1,1\}\).  In an orthonormal basis adapted to \(a\), its two elements are \(\operatorname{diag}(1,1)\) and \(\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\), so the two raw completions have opposite determinants; the whitening factors have positive determinant and do not change this conclusion.  A real matrix of negative determinant has a negative real eigenvalue: nonreal eigenvalues occur in conjugate pairs whose products are positive, so a negative product of all eigenvalues requires an unpaired negative real eigenvalue.  The negative-determinant completion therefore fails the principal-logarithm cut.  At most one completion is admissible, and model compatibility supplies the true admissible completion, so exactly one remains and its full reconstruction is unique.

At a true explanation,
\[
 d_0=(V-I_p)(\mu_0-x^\star).
\]
Because \(\rho(V)<1\), \(V-I_p\) is invertible; hence \(d_0=0\) if and only if \(\mu_0=x^\star\), a proper affine locus in the mean parameter.  Thus \(d_0\ne0\) is open and dense.  If \(d_0=0\), the norm certificate is equivalent to \(d_1=0\), and the torsor is \(O(p)\), of dimension \(p(p-1)/2\), positive already for \(p=2\).  Finally, any positive-dimensional family constructed in this deterministic Gaussian class remains observationally equivalent in a larger unknown-common-noise model containing it with the same transition target.  This proves precisely the stated transfer of nonidentification.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This theorem gives the exact observable three-snapshot compatibility certificate and the full O(p-r) ambiguity geometry, including the zero-displacement p=2 elbow that generic-only statements miss. \textit{Gap.} Supplied-covariance and known-noise three-time-point results do not characterize the unknown-common-measurement-noise norm certificate, its O(p-r) torsor, or the d\_0=0 continuous-ambiguity elbow. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies the sharp three-snapshot nonidentification half of the three-versus-four frontier and transfers that failure to any larger unknown-common-noise model containing this deterministic Gaussian subclass.

\begin{proposition}[prop:sharp-exceptional-fibers]\label{prop:sharp-exceptional-fibers}
Whenever the raw four-snapshot fiber is nonempty, it is finite if and only if \(k_\lambda\leq 1\) for every \(\lambda\in\Lambda\); on a finite stratum it contains exactly \(2^q\) candidates before admissibility filtering.  If some \(k_\lambda\geq 2\), every admissible candidate lies in a positive-dimensional relatively open fiber.  On a finite stratum with \(q\geq1\), at most \(2^{q-1}\) raw candidates are admissible, while for \(q=0\) at most one is admissible.  Consequently, in dimension \(p\geq2\), every finite admissible fiber has at most \(2^{p-1}\) members.  This bound is sharp: for every \(p\geq2\), there are strict-decrease Gaussian snapshot laws for which \(\mathcal F_{\mathrm{raw}}\) has \(2^p\) members and \(\mathcal F_{\mathrm{adm}}\), and hence the compatible structural-explanation fiber, has exactly \(2^{p-1}\) members.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By \(\text{thm:block-certificate}\), a nonempty raw fiber is a product of torsors for \(O(k_\lambda)\).  Since \(O(0)\) is a singleton, \(O(1)=\{-1,1\}\), and \(O(k)\) has positive dimension \(k(k-1)/2\) for \(k\ge2\), the raw fiber is finite exactly when every \(k_\lambda\leq1\), and then
\[
 \#\mathcal F_{\mathrm{raw}}=2^{\#\{\lambda:k_\lambda=1\}}=2^q.
\]
If some \(k_\lambda\ge2\), the strict admissibility filters are relatively open on the product torsor: stability and cut avoidance are open spectral conditions, while
\[
 \operatorname{vec}(T_W)=(I_{p^2}-W\otimes W)^{-1}\operatorname{vec}(H_0)
\]
is continuous on the stable set and strict positivity of \(C_0-T_W\) is open.  Every admissible candidate then lies in a positive-dimensional relatively open fiber.

On a finite stratum with \(q\ge1\), flip the residual sign in one block with \(k_\lambda=1\).  This is a fixed-point-free involution of the raw fiber and reverses \(\det(W)\), because the whitening factors have positive determinants.  Hence exactly \(2^{q-1}\) candidates have each determinant sign.  Every raw candidate is invertible.  If a real invertible matrix avoids \(( -\infty,0]\), its real eigenvalues are positive and each nonreal conjugate pair has positive product, so its determinant is positive.  Therefore every negative-determinant candidate fails the logarithm cut and
\[
 \#\mathcal F_{\mathrm{adm}}\leq2^{q-1}.
\]
If \(q=0\), the raw fiber already has one member.  Since \(q\leq\sum_\lambda k_\lambda\leq\sum_\lambda m_\lambda=p\), every finite admissible fiber has at most \(2^{p-1}\) members.

We construct equality.  Let \(Q_\circ e_i=e_{i+1}\) for \(i<p\) and \(Q_\circ e_p=e_1\).  For \(D=\operatorname{diag}(s_1,\ldots,s_p)\), \(s_i\in\{-1,1\}\),
\[
 (Q_\circ D)^p=\left(\prod_{i=1}^ps_i\right)I_p,\qquad
 \det(Q_\circ)=(-1)^{p-1}.
\]
If \(\det(Q_\circ D)>0\), then \(\prod_i s_i=(-1)^{p-1}\).  The eigenvalue \(-1\) is impossible, since the power identity would imply \((-1)^p=(-1)^{p-1}\).  An orthogonal matrix has no other negative real eigenvalue, so every orientation-preserving \(Q_\circ D\) avoids the logarithm cut.

Fix \(\alpha\in(0,1)\).  Cut avoidance of the finitely many matrices \(\alpha Q_\circ D\) with positive determinant persists for all positive diagonal \(A\) sufficiently close to \(\alpha I_p\).  Indeed, otherwise sequences \(A_n\to\alpha I_p\) and \(t_n\ge0\) with \(\det(t_nI_p+A_nQ_\circ D)=0\) would have a bounded convergent subsequence \(t_n\to t\), contradicting cut avoidance of \(\alpha Q_\circ D\).  Choose in the common neighborhood
\[
 A=\operatorname{diag}(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_p),\qquad
 0<\alpha_i<1,\qquad \alpha_i^2\ne\alpha_j^2\ (i\ne j).
\]
Then every \(AQ_\circ D\) is stable because its norm is \(\max_i\alpha_i<1\), and every positive-determinant one avoids the cut.

Set
\[
 d_0=d_1=d_2=0,\qquad H_0=I_p,\qquad H_1=A^2,\qquad
 H_2=AQ_\circ A^2Q_\circ^{\mathsf T}A.
\]
All differences are positive definite.  Here \(R=A^2\) and \(S=Q_\circ A^2Q_\circ^{\mathsf T}\), with distinct one-dimensional spectral blocks and zero probes.  Thus \(m_\lambda=1\), \(r_\lambda=0\), \(k_\lambda=1\), and \(q=p\).  The block certificate yields exactly the \(2^p\) matrices
\[
 W_D=AQ_\circ D.
\]
Directly,
\[
 W_DH_0W_D^{\mathsf T}=A^2=H_1,\qquad
 W_DH_1W_D^{\mathsf T}=AQ_\circ A^2Q_\circ^{\mathsf T}A=H_2,
\]
because \(DA^2D=A^2\), and the mean equations are automatic.

For each \(D\), let
\[
 T_D=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}W_D^j(W_D^{\mathsf T})^j,
\]
so \(T_D\succ0\) and \(T_D-W_DT_DW_D^{\mathsf T}=I_p\).  Choose
\[
 c>\max\left\{1+\lambda_{\max}(H_0+H_1+H_2),
 \max_D\lambda_{\max}(T_D)\right\}
\]
and define
\[
 C_0=cI_p,quad C_1=C_0-H_0,quad C_2=C_1-H_1,quad
 C_3=C_2-H_2,quad m_0=m_1=m_2=m_3=0.
\]
All \(C_k\) are positive definite.  For each branch, \(E_D=C_0-T_D\succ0\), and the Stein equation plus the two congruences gives
\[
 C_k=W_D^kT_D(W_D^{\mathsf T})^k+E_D,\qquad 0\leq k\leq3.
\]
Thus these are genuine strict-decrease Gaussian snapshot laws under every branch.

Exactly half of the sign matrices make \(\det(W_D)>0\).  All of those \(2^{p-1}\) branches are stable, avoid the cut, and have \(E_D\succ0\), hence are admissible.  Every negative-determinant branch has a negative real eigenvalue by the conjugate-pair product argument and fails the cut.  Consequently
\[
 \#\mathcal F_{\mathrm{adm}}=2^{p-1}.
\]
The bijection in \(\text{thm:admissible-fiber}\) turns these into exactly \(2^{p-1}\) distinct structural explanations, proving sharpness.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The proposition classifies every exceptional stratum and proves that \(2^{p-1}\) is the exact worst-case number of admissible explanations on a finite stratum. \textit{Gap.} AaltoLamolineGoncalves2022 mentions repeated-eigenvalue orthogonal blocks, but does not give the exact residual-rank criterion after two mean probes or the sharp branch count surviving every dynamical and positivity filter. \textit{Consumer.} Any complete solver for snapshot dynamics must budget for up to \(2^{p-1}\) scientifically admissible outputs and report continuous fibers rather than silently selecting one model.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The exact population conditions--spectral matching, block Gram equality, and the three-snapshot whitened norm equality--must not be imposed as empirical accept-or-reject equalities, because unconstrained noisy sample moments satisfy them with probability zero.  On the cyclic chart, \(\widehat W_{\mathrm{cyc}}=\widehat K_1\widehat K_0^{-1}\) is an off-model rational estimator; \(\epsilon_{\mathrm{rec}}\) guarantees that its full reconstruction is defined and \(\epsilon_{\mathrm{adm}}\) additionally preserves reconstructed-covariance positivity.  On a specified regular noncyclic finite branch, the local estimator is the global residual minimizer over a fixed population-centered isolating ball.  Full column rank of the residual derivative yields a contraction-selected stationary point, while compact separation of the sole population root from the complement forces every nearby global minimizer into that contraction neighborhood and makes it unique.  This removes dependence on arbitrary local optima but does not make the estimator a globally implementable branch finder: the isolating ball and branch label remain population-indexed until the certification question is solved.  No empirical exact spectrum, exact probe Gram matrix, or exact raw residual equality is required.

\section{Honest scope}

The theorem-level deliverables are the exact three-snapshot norm certificate and \(O(p-r)\) fibers, the dimension-two determinant elbow, the dimension-dependent three-versus-four frontier, the four-snapshot block certificate and admissibility bijection, the sharp attainable finite-stratum maximum \(2^{p-1}\), the information-nullity identity, cyclic perturbation bounds, and a population-branch-indexed local Gaussian limit law under \(\gamma_W>0\).  For the local limit, the estimator is a fixed-ball global residual projection whose singleton existence and smoothness are proved only on a sufficiently small neighborhood of the chosen regular branch; the result does not assume that an arbitrary local optimizer is the desired branch.  Three snapshots generically identify model-compatible systems when \(p=2\), except on the \(d_0=0\) continuous-ambiguity locus, while a fourth snapshot is generically necessary for \(p\ge3\) and generically sufficient on the cyclic chart.  A larger unknown-common-noise model inherits the deterministic Gaussian subclass's continuous nonidentification witnesses, not its dimension-two identification conclusion.  The global certified initializer, exhaustive branch certificate, stable empirical labeling, and simultaneous coverage across all noncyclic finite branches remain open; no uniform inference is claimed at branch collisions, positivity boundaries, logarithm cuts, or stabilizer-dimension jumps.
