Fix \(G\in\mathcal F\), and write \(e(G)=|E(G)|\).  Define a coefficient matrix \(B^{G}\in\mathbb R^{N\times N}\) by
\[
 B^{G}_{ji}=\begin{cases}
 \beta,& i\to j\text{ is an edge of }G,\\
 0,& i\to j\text{ is not an edge of }G.
 \end{cases}
\]
Because \(G\) is loopless, \(B^{G}_{ii}=0\) for every \(i\in V\).  By the definition of \(B^{G}\),
\[
 \operatorname{supp}(B^{G})=E(G)
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 |B^{G}_{ji}|=\beta\quad\text{for every }i\to j\text{ in }G.
\]
Thus the exact-support and edge-margin clauses in \(\mathfrak K_G(L_B,\kappa,\beta,s_-,s_+)\) hold.

There are at most \(N(N-1)\) directed edges in a loopless digraph on \(N\) labeled vertices.  The operator norm is bounded by the Frobenius norm, so
\[
 \lVert B^{G}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le \lVert B^{G}\rVert_{F}
 =\beta\sqrt{e(G)}
 \le\beta\sqrt{N(N-1)}
 \le N\beta
 \le\frac18.
\]
The last inequality uses \(0<\beta\le 1/(8N)\).  Since \(L_B\ge1\), this also gives \(\lVert B^{G}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le L_B\), as required by the coefficient-bound clause.

Fix \(S\in\mathcal I\) and put \(R=V\setminus S\).  The principal submatrix \(B^{G}_{R,R}\) is a coordinate compression of \(B^{G}\), whence
\[
 \lVert B^{G}_{R,R}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le \lVert B^{G}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le\frac18.
\]
For every \(y\in\mathbb R^{R}\), the reverse triangle inequality therefore yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \lVert Q_{R,R}y\rVert_2
 &=\lVert (I_R-B^{G}_{R,R})y\rVert_2\\
 &\ge \lVert y\rVert_2-\lVert B^{G}_{R,R}y\rVert_2\\
 &\ge \bigl(1-\lVert B^{G}_{R,R}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\bigr)\lVert y\rVert_2\\
 &\ge\frac78\lVert y\rVert_2.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Taking the infimum over \(\lVert y\rVert_2=1\) gives
\[
 \sigma_{\min}(Q_{V\setminus S,V\setminus S})\ge\frac78\ge\kappa,
\]
where the final inequality uses \(0<\kappa\le1/2\).  If \(R=\varnothing\), the displayed lower-bound formulation is vacuous and the unique linear map on the zero-dimensional space is invertible; equivalently, the minimum-singular-value margin is satisfied under the standard zero-dimensional convention.  Hence the inverse-margin clause holds for every \(S\in\mathcal I\), and in particular every required structural subsystem is invertible.

Choose any \(s^{\circ}\in(s_-,s_+)\), for example \(s^{\circ}=(s_-+s_+)/2\), and set \(s_i=s^{\circ}\) for every \(i\in V\).  On a finite product probability space, let \((Z_i)_{i\in V}\) be independent variables having density \(z\mapsto e^{-|z|}/2\), and define \(\varepsilon_i=s_iZ_i\).  Then each \(s_i\in[s_-,s_+]\), so the Laplace-scale clause holds.  Moreover, direct integration gives
\[
 \mathbb E|Z_i|^4
 =\frac12\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|z|^4e^{-|z|}\,dz
 =\int_0^{\infty}z^4e^{-z}\,dz
 =4!
 =24.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \mathbb E|\varepsilon_i|^4
 =s_i^4\mathbb E|Z_i|^4
 =24s_i^4
 \le24s_+^4
 \qquad(i\in V).
\]
All clauses in the definition of \(\mathfrak K_G(L_B,\kappa,\beta,s_-,s_+)\) are therefore satisfied by \((B^{G},(s_i)_{i\in V})\).  Since \(G\in\mathcal F\) was arbitrary, the compact Laplace class is nonempty for every \(G\in\mathcal F\), with the claimed uniform fourth-moment bound.
