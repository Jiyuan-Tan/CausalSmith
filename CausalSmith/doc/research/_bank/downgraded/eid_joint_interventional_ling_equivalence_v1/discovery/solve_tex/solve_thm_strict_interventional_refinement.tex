%%% FIELD strict-interventional-refinement-proof
Write an arbitrary exact-support mechanism on \(G_\star\), as in Definition \(\texttt{def:witness-mechanisms}\), as
\[
 L=\varepsilon_L,\qquad
 X_1=cL+aX_2+\varepsilon_1,\qquad
 X_2=bX_1+\varepsilon_2,\qquad
 X_3=dL+\varepsilon_3,
\]
where \(a,b,c,d\ne0\) and \(1-ab\ne0\). Define
\[
 \alpha=b^{-1},\qquad \beta=a^{-1},\qquad
 \gamma=-ca^{-1},\qquad \delta=d
\]
and
\[
 \eta_L=\varepsilon_L,\qquad
 \eta_1=-b^{-1}\varepsilon_2,\qquad
 \eta_2=-a^{-1}\varepsilon_1,\qquad
 \eta_3=\varepsilon_3.
\]
All divisions are valid because \(ab\ne0\). Assumption \(\texttt{ass:source-independence}\) implies that \((\eta_L,\eta_1,\eta_2,\eta_3)\) has mutually independent coordinates: this tuple is obtained from \((\varepsilon_L,\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2,\varepsilon_3)\) by a coordinate permutation followed by nonzero coordinatewise scalings. The same transformation preserves nonconstancy, because \(rY\) constant with \(r\ne0\) would imply that \(Y=r^{-1}(rY)\) is constant. It preserves non-Gaussianity for the identical reason: if \(rY\) were Gaussian, its nonzero rescaling \(Y=r^{-1}(rY)\) would be Gaussian. Thus Assumptions \(\texttt{ass:source-nondegeneracy}\) and \(\texttt{ass:source-nongaussianity}\) are preserved. No expectation or moment has been used.

The same endogenous vector satisfies the \(H_\star\) equations sample by sample. Indeed, the \(G_\star\) equation for \(X_2\) gives
\[
 \alpha X_2+\eta_1
 =b^{-1}(X_2-\varepsilon_2)
 =X_1,
\]
and the equation for \(X_1\) gives
\[
 \gamma L+\beta X_1+\eta_2
 =-ca^{-1}L+a^{-1}(X_1-\varepsilon_1)
 =X_2.
\]
Also \(L=\eta_L\) and \(X_3=\delta L+\eta_3\). Therefore the two observed vectors are equal almost surely under this coupling, hence have the same observational law.

This map stays in the exact-support strata. By Assumption \(\texttt{ass:exact-support}\), all four displayed structural coefficients for \(G_\star\) are nonzero. Consequently \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta\ne0\). Moreover
\[
 1-\alpha\beta
 =1-\frac{1}{ab}
 =\frac{ab-1}{ab}\ne0.
\]
For completeness, in either witness graph the only directed cycle is the two-cycle on \(\{X_1,X_2\}\). For a target \(S\subseteq\{X_1,X_2,X_3\}\), direct expansion of the relevant principal structural matrix gives
\[
 \det Q^{G_\star}_{V\setminus S,V\setminus S}
 =\begin{cases}
 1-ab,&S\cap\{X_1,X_2\}=\varnothing,\\[2pt]
 1,&S\cap\{X_1,X_2\}\ne\varnothing,
 \end{cases}
\]
and
\[
 \det Q^{H_\star}_{V\setminus S,V\setminus S}
 =\begin{cases}
 1-\alpha\beta,&S\cap\{X_1,X_2\}=\varnothing,\\[2pt]
 1,&S\cap\{X_1,X_2\}\ne\varnothing.
 \end{cases}
\]
The off-cycle arrows \(L\to X_1\) or \(L\to X_2\) and \(L\to X_3\) occur strictly below the diagonal after ordering the strongly connected components and hence do not change these determinants. Thus Assumption \(\texttt{ass:regime-solvability}\) holds in every intervention regime on one side if and only if it holds on the other; no stability condition is being imposed.

Conversely, let an arbitrary \(H_\star\) mechanism have nonzero coefficients \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta\), with \(1-\alpha\beta\ne0\), and admissible independent sources \(\eta\). Put
\[
 a=\beta^{-1},\qquad b=\alpha^{-1},\qquad
 c=-\gamma\beta^{-1},\qquad d=\delta
\]
and
\[
 \varepsilon_L=\eta_L,\qquad
 \varepsilon_1=-\beta^{-1}\eta_2,\qquad
 \varepsilon_2=-\alpha^{-1}\eta_1,\qquad
 \varepsilon_3=\eta_3.
\]
Again these sources obey independence, nonconstancy, and non-Gaussianity. Also
\[
 1-ab=1-\frac{1}{\alpha\beta}
 =\frac{\alpha\beta-1}{\alpha\beta}\ne0.
\]
Substitution shows that this construction is inverse to the preceding map on the coefficient tuple and on every coordinate source law. We have therefore obtained a bijection between the entire exact-support observational mechanism spaces, including source laws without a center or a finite moment. Definition \(\texttt{def:graph-law-set}\) now gives
\[
 \mathcal P_{\{\varnothing\}}(G_\star)
 =\mathcal P_{\{\varnothing\}}(H_\star).
\]

We next analyze the intervention \(\operatorname{do}(X_1=z)\). By Assumption \(\texttt{ass:perfect-intervention}\), the equation for \(X_1\) is replaced by the constant \(z\), while all other coefficients and all unaffected source laws remain fixed. For every \(G_\star\) mechanism this gives
\[
 L=\varepsilon_L,\qquad
 X_2=bz+\varepsilon_2,\qquad
 X_3=d\varepsilon_L+\varepsilon_3.
\]
The random variable \(X_2\) is measurable with respect to \(\sigma(\varepsilon_2)\), while \(X_3\) is measurable with respect to \(\sigma(\varepsilon_L,\varepsilon_3)\). Mutual source independence therefore implies
\[
 X_2\mathbin{\perp\!\!\!\perp}X_3
 \quad\text{under every such kernel}.
\]
This conclusion is distributional and uses no moments.

For an arbitrary \(H_\star\) mechanism the intervened equations instead read
\[
 X_2=\gamma\eta_L+\beta z+\eta_2,
 \qquad
 X_3=\delta\eta_L+\eta_3.
\]
If this mechanism additionally obeys Assumption \(\texttt{ass:source-second-moment}\), every covariance in the following expansion exists. Mutual independence gives zero covariance between distinct source coordinates, and hence
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Cov}(X_2,X_3)
 &=\gamma\delta\operatorname{Var}(\eta_L)
   +\gamma\operatorname{Cov}(\eta_L,\eta_3)
   +\delta\operatorname{Cov}(\eta_2,\eta_L)
   +\operatorname{Cov}(\eta_2,\eta_3)\\
 &=\gamma\delta\operatorname{Var}(\eta_L).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Finite second moment and Assumption \(\texttt{ass:source-nondegeneracy}\) imply \(\operatorname{Var}(\eta_L)>0\): variance zero would imply \(\eta_L=\mathbb E[\eta_L]\) almost surely. Exact support gives \(\gamma\delta\ne0\), so the displayed covariance is nonzero. Under the inverse parametrization, \(a=\beta^{-1}\), \(c=-\gamma\beta^{-1}\), \(d=\delta\), and \(\eta_L=\varepsilon_L\). Therefore
\[
 \gamma\delta\operatorname{Var}(\eta_L)
 =-\frac ca d\sigma_L^2\ne0.
\]
For a finite-second-moment \(G_\star\) mechanism, the already proved independence gives \(\operatorname{Cov}(X_2,X_3)=0\). This proves the covariance clauses on precisely the stated finite-second-moment submodel; centering is unnecessary because covariance is invariant under translations.

It remains to prove separation of the full moment-free graph law sets, rather than only of their finite-moment submodels. Consider the admissible \(H_\star\) mechanism
\[
 (\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta)=(3,2,-2,1)
\]
with mutually independent standard Laplace sources having density \(u\mapsto e^{-|u|}/2\). Each source is nonconstant and non-Gaussian, and \(1-\alpha\beta=-5\ne0\); the determinant calculation above proves regime solvability for every observed intervention target. Symmetry gives \(\mathbb E[\eta_L]=0\), and two integrations by parts give
\[
 \mathbb E[\eta_L^2]
 =\int_{\mathbb R}u^2\frac{e^{-|u|}}2\,du
 =\int_0^\infty u^2e^{-u}\,du
 =2.
\]
Thus its \(\{X_1\}\)-intervention kernel satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(X_2,X_3)
 =(-2)(1)(2)=-4.
\]
In particular, \(X_2\) and \(X_3\) are not independent under this kernel. No \(G_\star\) mechanism, including one whose sources lack moments, can produce that same kernel, because every \(G_\star\) kernel makes these two coordinates independent. Hence this \(H_\star\) joint response-kernel tuple is absent from \(\mathcal P_{\mathcal I}(G_\star)\) whenever \(\{X_1\}\in\mathcal I\), whereas it belongs to \(\mathcal P_{\mathcal I}(H_\star)\). Thus
\[
 \mathcal P_{\mathcal I}(G_\star)
 \ne
 \mathcal P_{\mathcal I}(H_\star).
\]
Together with observational equality, this is strict refinement. The argument concerns population perfect-intervention kernels at fixed \(z\); it uses no overlap or positivity condition and no boundary or limiting argument.
