\section*{exp\_\allowbreak{}outcome\_\allowbreak{}delay\_\allowbreak{}inference\_\allowbreak{}frontier}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} Under the one-sided polynomial allocation condition \(\pi_t(1)\geq ct^{-\alpha}\), together with a control-arm floor, the measurable two-cohort gate-and-fallback interval has exact class-minimax expected-length order \(\max\{T^{-(1-\alpha)/2},(1+T+\Delta_T)^{-\beta}\}\) for the eventual arm-mean contrast. Shi, Wang, and Wu supply the design-side floor and delayed DAIPW asymptotics under an outcome--delay-independence model, whereas the present same-experiment hidden-tail converse permits informative delays; neither full class contains the other. The Liberali et al. discussion is a source audit and conditional prospective transfer, not a claim that their reported cardiovascular-trial percentile intervals equal this note's intervals.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Shi, Wang, and Wu prove delayed DAIPW consistency, asymptotic normality, and variance consistency under independent outcomes and delays, moment conditions, and adaptive-weight restrictions; they do not establish uniform honesty or minimax expected interval length. Delayed reward-dependent bandit work supplies regret lower bounds rather than a same-experiment interval-length frontier for an eventual causal contrast.

\paragraph{Niche.} The missing object is the exact fixed-horizon expected-length frontier under a one-sided rare-arm exploration floor and a control floor when logged propensities may oscillate, within-arm revelation may be informative, and prespecified post-recruitment follow-up changes what is observed.

\paragraph{Fill.} The project combines an unconditional arm-tape reduction, deterministic two-cohort prefixes, an observable variance gate with a concentration fallback, and same-policy prompt-tilt and hidden-sign experiments. It proves the class-minimax frontier, the ordinary-Wald boundary, the attained bounded-shift calibration, and a conditional prospective follow-up elbow; its Liberali et al. component is an assumption-and-estimand audit rather than asserted trial coverage.

\section{Related work}

Shi, Wang, and Wu \cite{ShiWangWu2023DelayedInference} assume independent and identically distributed potential-outcome vectors independent of contemporaneous action and delay, stationary arm-specific delay laws, finite \(2+\delta\) moments, and adaptive-weight conditions. Their Theorems 4.1--4.3 establish DAIPW consistency, asymptotic normality, and variance consistency; Corollary 4.4 verifies those conditions under a one-sided allocation floor, positive prompt mass, and a polynomial finite-delay tail exponent of at least one half, with a nonzero-margin relaxation in Remark 4.5. They do not prove uniform honesty or minimax expected length. This note instead assumes bounded outcomes, explicit rare- and control-arm floors, a known polynomial envelope for every \(\beta>0\), and no infinite delays, while allowing arbitrary within-arm outcome--delay dependence. Hence neither full class contains the other. Only the design-side floor is imported: the prompt sampling converse lies in their independence submodel, whereas the hidden-sign converse deliberately does not. Zhang, Janson, and Murphy \cite{ZhangJansonMurphy2021MEstimators} develop confidence regions for adaptively weighted M-estimators, but not informative delayed revelation or fixed-horizon minimax length. Howard, Ramdas, McAuliffe, and Sekhon \cite{HowardRamdasMcAuliffeSekhon2021} provide time-uniform nonasymptotic confidence sequences on a different axis; they do not solve informative terminal missingness, and the present note is not anytime-valid. Hadad et al. and Bibaut et al. develop predictable weighting and stabilization for prompt adaptive outcomes; Lancewicki et al. and Tang, Wang, and Zheng study reward-dependent-delay bias and regret; Bai, Hu, and Rosenberger and Ma, Wang, and Wei analyze delayed response-adaptive asymptotics under different delay and overlap conditions; Armstrong and Kolesar provide the bounded-bias Gaussian calibration adapted here. Liberali et al. \cite{LiberaliEtAl2025RTAR} report retrospective GUSTO-1 and EUROPA resampling with replacement under stationarity/exchangeability, \(200\) replicates, and empirical \(2.5\%\) and \(97.5\%\) quantiles. Their three-arm GUSTO-1 and two-arm EUROPA examples use daily Gittins allocation and an eta-variant whose equal-randomization component ends at fixed arm minima. Those percentile intervals are neither this note's ordinary-Wald interval nor its hybrid, and their paper certifies neither permanent polynomial/control floors nor a polynomial delay envelope. The reported EUROPA \(4.2\)-year mean time to the last observation is not a tail certificate, so no numerical pad is inferred.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:iid-arrivals]\label{ass:iid-arrivals}
The vectors \(\{(Y_t(a),D_t(a)):a\in\mathcal A\}\) are independent and identically distributed over \(t\) under \(P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (stationary independent potential-outcome sampling, \cite{HadadHirshbergZhanWagerAthey2021}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:bounded-outcomes]\label{ass:bounded-outcomes}
For every \(t\) and \(a\in\mathcal A\), \(|Y_t(a)|\leq1\) almost surely.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (bounded reward support, \cite{LancewickiSegalKorenMansour2021}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:polynomial-delay-envelope]\label{ass:polynomial-delay-envelope}
For every \(t\), \(a\in\mathcal A\), and integer \(u\geq0\), \(P\{D_t(a)>u\}\leq\min\{1,L(1+u)^{-\beta}\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (armwise polynomial delay-tail envelope, \cite{GaelVernadeCarpentierValko2020}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:sequential-randomization]\label{ass:sequential-randomization}
For every \(t\leq T\), \(P(W_t=a\mid\mathcal F_{t-1},\{Y_t(a'),D_t(a'):a'\in\mathcal A\})=\pi_t(a)\) for each \(a\in\mathcal A\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (known predictable sequential randomization, \cite{HadadHirshbergZhanWagerAthey2021}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:rare-arm-envelope]\label{ass:rare-arm-envelope}
For every \(t\leq T\), \(ct^{-\alpha}\leq\pi_t(1)\leq Ct^{-\alpha}\) almost surely.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Deterministically clipped response-adaptive rules with a prespecified decaying exploration schedule satisfy this comparability regime; the constants permit substantial adaptation while keeping the rare-arm information order stable.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:one-sided-forced-exploration]\label{ass:one-sided-forced-exploration}
For every \(t\leq T\), \(\pi_t(1)\geq ct^{-\alpha}\) almost surely.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (polynomial forced-exploration lower bound, \cite{ShiWangWu2023DelayedInference}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:control-floor]\label{ass:control-floor}
For every \(t\leq T\), \(\pi_t(0)\geq\varepsilon\) almost surely.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (one-sided overlap, \cite{HadadHirshbergZhanWagerAthey2021}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:regular-variance]\label{ass:regular-variance}
If \(\alpha>0\), then \(\operatorname{Var}_P\{Y_t(1)\}\geq\kappa\); if \(\alpha=0\), then \(\operatorname{Var}_P\{Y_t(1)\}+\operatorname{Var}_P\{Y_t(0)\}\geq\kappa\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (nondegenerate asymptotic variance, \cite{HadadHirshbergZhanWagerAthey2021}).
\end{assumption}

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Member properties.} \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}iid-\allowbreak{}arrivals}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}bounded-\allowbreak{}outcomes}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}polynomial-\allowbreak{}delay-\allowbreak{}envelope}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}sequential-\allowbreak{}randomization}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}one-\allowbreak{}sided-\allowbreak{}forced-\allowbreak{}exploration}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}control-\allowbreak{}floor}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}regular-\allowbreak{}variance}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:cohort-schedule]\label{def:cohort-schedule}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Two-\allowbreak{}cohort schedule}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Choose fixed \(\rho\in(\max\{1/2,(1-\alpha)/(1-\alpha+\beta)\},1)\), set \(m_T=k_T=\lfloor T^\rho\rfloor\), \(n_T=T-m_T\), and, for prespecified \(\Delta_T\geq0\), set \(g_{T,\Delta}=m_T+\Delta_T\) and \(h_{T,\Delta}=T+\Delta_T-k_T\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{T}; \texttt{Delta}; \texttt{alpha}; \texttt{beta}; \texttt{rho}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:deterministic-quotas]\label{def:deterministic-quotas}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Deterministic arm quotas}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Set \(e_1(t)=ct^{-\alpha}\), \(e_0(t)=\varepsilon\), \(Q_a(s)=\sum_{t\leq s}e_a(t)\), and \(K_a(s)=\max\{2,\lfloor Q_a(s)-\sqrt{8Q_a(s)\log T}\rfloor\}\); set \(J_a=K_a(k_T)\) and \(E_T=\bigcap_{a\in\mathcal A}\{N_a(n_T)\geq K_a(n_T),\ N_a(k_T)\geq J_a\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{T}; \texttt{alpha}; \texttt{c}; \texttt{eps}; \texttt{W}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:two-cohort-estimator]\label{def:two-cohort-estimator}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Fixed-\allowbreak{}quota two-\allowbreak{}cohort estimator}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Using \(O_{T,\Delta}\), on \(E_T\) index each arm's assignments by tape order. For \(j\leq K_a(n_T)\), set \(X_{a,j}^{\Delta}=Y_{a,j}\mathbf 1\{D_{a,j}\leq g_{T,\Delta}\}\); for \(j\leq J_a\), set \(R_{a,j}^{\Delta}=Y_{a,j}\mathbf 1\{g_{T,\Delta}<D_{a,j}\leq h_{T,\Delta}\}\). Each indicator is computed from the recorded revelation time, with an outcome absent by \(H_T\) assigned indicator zero. Set \(M_{a,\Delta}=K_a(n_T)^{-1}\sum_{j\leq K_a(n_T)}X_{a,j}^{\Delta}\), \(U_{a,\Delta}=J_a^{-1}\sum_{j\leq J_a}R_{a,j}^{\Delta}\), and \(\widehat\tau_{T,\Delta}=(M_{1,\Delta}+U_{1,\Delta})-(M_{0,\Delta}+U_{0,\Delta})\). Set \(\widehat\tau_{T,\Delta}=0\) on \(E_T^c\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{O}; \texttt{T}; \texttt{Delta}; \texttt{rho}; \texttt{K}; \texttt{J}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:bias-radius]\label{def:bias-radius}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Unrevealed-\allowbreak{}tail bias radius}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Define \(b_{T,\Delta}=P[Y(1)\mathbf 1\{D(1)>h_{T,\Delta}\}]-P[Y(0)\mathbf 1\{D(0)>h_{T,\Delta}\}]\) and \(B_{T,\Delta}=2\min\{1,L(1+h_{T,\Delta})^{-\beta}\}\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:standard-error-and-gate]\label{def:standard-error-and-gate}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Observable main-\allowbreak{}prefix standard error and gate}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} On \(E_T\), let \(\widehat\sigma_{X,a,\Delta}^2\) be the usual sample variance of the \(K_a(n_T)\) main-prefix variables in arm \(a\), set \(\widehat s_{T,\Delta}^2=\widehat\sigma_{X,1,\Delta}^2/K_1(n_T)+\widehat\sigma_{X,0,\Delta}^2/K_0(n_T)\), and set \(G_{T,\Delta}=\{\widehat\sigma_{X,1,\Delta}^2+\mathbf 1\{\alpha=0\}\widehat\sigma_{X,0,\Delta}^2\geq2/\log T\}\). On \(E_T^c\), set \(\widehat s_{T,\Delta}=0\) and \(G_{T,\Delta}=\varnothing\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{X}; \texttt{K}; \texttt{n}; \texttt{T}; \texttt{Delta}; \texttt{alpha}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:bounded-shift-critical-value]\label{def:bounded-shift-critical-value}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Bounded-\allowbreak{}shift Gaussian critical value}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For \(u\geq0\), \(c_\eta(u)\) is the unique positive solution of \(\Phi(c_\eta(u)-u)+\Phi(c_\eta(u)+u)-1=1-\eta\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:hybrid-interval]\label{def:hybrid-interval}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Measurable gate-\allowbreak{}and-\allowbreak{}fallback interval}

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:arm-tape-equivalence]\label{lem:arm-tape-equivalence}
For every \((P,\Pi,T)\) in \(\mathfrak M_T(\alpha,\beta,L,c,C,\varepsilon)\cup\mathfrak M_T^{\mathrm{low}}(\alpha,\beta,L,c,\varepsilon)\), the law of \(O_{T,\Delta}\) equals the law obtained from two independent infinite arm tapes with respective marginals \((Y(a),D(a))\), where policy \(\Pi\) consumes the next unused entry of the assigned tape and reveals the record \((t,W_t,V_t,D_t(W_t),Y_t(W_t))\) at calendar time \(V_t\). Consequently, every deterministic prefix of each tape is an independent and identically distributed arm sample, unconditionally and without imposing independence between \(Y(a)\) and \(D(a)\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For (a\in\mathcal A), let \(\mu_a\) be the marginal law of \((Y_t(a),D_t(a))\).  By \texttt{ass:iid-arrivals}, the current potential outcome--delay vector is independent of \(\mathcal F_{t-1}\) and has its common law.  By \texttt{ass:sequential-randomization}, assignment is conditionally independent of that current vector given \(\mathcal F_{t-1}\).  Consequently, if \(Z_t=(Y_t(W_t),D_t(W_t))\), then for every measurable set \(B\),
\[
 P\{W_t=a,Z_t\in B\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}\}
 =\pi_t(a)\mu_a(B).                                                    \tag{1}
\]
After \((W_t,Z_t)\) is drawn, its revelation time and revealed record are the measurable functions
\[
 V_t=t+D_t(W_t),\qquad
 (t,W_t,V_t,D_t(W_t),Y_t(W_t)).                                         \tag{2}
\]
Thus, for every possible delayed history \(h_{t-1}\), the transition kernel first draws \(a\) with probabilities \(\pi_t(a;h_{t-1})\), then draws \(z\) from \(\mu_a\), and finally schedules the record according to (2).

On an auxiliary probability space, take two independent sequences
\[
 \{Z_{a,j}:j\geq1\},\qquad Z_{a,j}\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\mu_a,
 \qquad a\in\{0,1\}.                                                     \tag{3}
\]
At time \(t\), after the policy chooses \(W_t=a\), consume \(Z_{a,N_a(t)}\).  Conditional on the observed delayed history and on \(W_t=a\), this unused entry is independent of the history and has law \(\mu_a\).  Hence the tape construction has exactly the kernel (1).  Induction over \(t=1,\ldots,T\) proves equality of the joint laws of all assignments, logged propensities, scheduled revelation times, and records revealed by every calendar time through \(H_T\).  In particular, it proves equality of the laws of \(O_{T,\Delta}\).

No independence between the two potential arms within one arrival was used: only the marginal selected by \(W_t\) occurs in (1).  This explains why the independent tapes reproduce the observed law even if the two potential pairs in the original arrival vector are dependent.  By (3), every deterministic prefix of each tape is an iid arm sample and the two prefixes are mutually independent.  This is unconditional; no event concerning the times at which tape entries are consumed has been conditioned upon.

For the finite-support stress test, take \(T=2\).  Let the second assignment probability take one value if the first outcome has already been revealed and is positive, another if it has been revealed and is nonpositive, and a third if it remains pending.  For every assignment pair \((a_1,a_2)\) and every pair of tape atoms \((z_1,z_2)\), both constructions give the cylinder probability
\[
 \pi_1(a_1)\mu_{a_1}(z_1)
 \pi_2\{a_2;h_1(a_1,z_1)\}\mu_{a_2}(z_2).                              \tag{4}
\]
The history \(h_1\) uses the outcome in \(z_1\) only when the delay component makes it observable.  Equality (4) holds atom by atom without factorizing the outcome and delay in \(z_1\), verifying the asserted reduction under reward-dependent switching.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This observed-kernel equivalence is the device that converts arbitrary predictable allocation into deterministic independent arm prefixes. \textit{Gap.} Adaptive AIPW proofs use martingale centering, while reward-dependent-delay lower bounds use tailored instances; neither supplies this unconditional observed-law reduction with delayed histories. \textit{Consumer.} The reduction permits fixed-quota inference for any logged policy obeying the exploration envelope, including policies whose scaled propensities do not converge.

\begin{lemma}[lem:quota-success]\label{lem:quota-success}
Uniformly over \(\mathfrak M_T(\alpha,\beta,L,c,C,\varepsilon)\cup\mathfrak M_T^{\mathrm{low}}(\alpha,\beta,L,c,\varepsilon)\), the deterministic quotas in \(E_T\) satisfy \(P(E_T^c)\leq4T^{-4}\) for all sufficiently large \(T\), and \(K_1(n_T)\asymp T^{1-\alpha}\), \(K_0(n_T)\asymp T\), \(J_1\asymp T^{\rho(1-\alpha)}\), and \(J_0\asymp T^\rho\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write \(I_t(a)=\mathbf 1\{W_t=a\}\).  Predictability and sequential randomization permit an inverse-transform realization with independent \(U_t\sim\operatorname{Unif}(0,1)\) such that
\[
 I_t(1)=\mathbf 1\{U_t\leq\pi_t(1)\},
 \qquad I_t(0)=\mathbf 1\{U_t>\pi_t(1)\}.                               \tag{1}
\]
For arm one set \(Z_t(1)=\mathbf 1\{U_t\leq ct^{-\alpha}\}\).  Under either model class, the applicable lower allocation envelope gives \(Z_t(1)\leq I_t(1)\) almost surely.  For arm zero set \(Z_t(0)=\mathbf 1\{U_t>1-\varepsilon\}\).  On \(\mathfrak M_T^{\mathrm{low}}\), \texttt{ass:control-floor} gives \(Z_t(0)\leq I_t(0)\).  On \(\mathfrak M_T\), \texttt{ass:rare-arm-envelope} and \(C\leq1-\varepsilon\) give
\[
 \pi_t(0)=1-\pi_t(1)\geq1-Ct^{-\alpha}\geq1-C\geq\varepsilon,          \tag{2}
\]
so the same domination holds.  For either arm, the variables \(Z_t(a)\) are independent Bernoulli variables with means \(e_a(t)\), and
\[
 N_a(s)\geq S_a(s):=\sum_{t\leq s}Z_t(a)\quad\hbox{almost surely}.       \tag{3}
\]

Fix \(a,s\), write \(Q=Q_a(s)\), and let \(0<x<Q\).  Independence and exponential Markov inequality give, for every \(\lambda>0\),
\[
 P\{S_a(s)\leq Q-x\}
 \leq \exp\{\lambda(Q-x)+Q(e^{-\lambda}-1)\}.                           \tag{4}
\]
Set \(u=x/Q\) and \(\lambda=-\log(1-u)\).  Since
\[
 (1-u)\log(1-u)+u\geq\frac{u^2}{2},\qquad 0\leq u<1,                    \tag{5}
\]
where (5) follows by differentiating and using \(-\log(1-u)\geq u\), (4) yields
\[
 P\{S_a(s)\leq Q-x\}\leq\exp\{-x^2/(2Q)\}.                            \tag{6}
\]
At \(s=n_T\) and \(s=k_T\), one has \(Q_a(s)/\log T\to\infty\).  Therefore, for all sufficiently large \(T\), the maximum with (2) in the definition of \(K_a(s)\) is inactive.  Taking \(x=\sqrt{8Q_a(s)\log T}\), (3)--(6) then give
\[
 P\{N_a(s)<K_a(s)\}\leq T^{-4}.                                       \tag{7}
\]
There are two arms and two deadlines, so the union bound proves
\[
 P(E_T^c)\leq4T^{-4}.                                                     \tag{8}
\]

Finally, because \(0\leq\alpha<1\), integral comparison gives
\[
 \sum_{t\leq s}t^{-\alpha}
 =\frac{s^{1-\alpha}}{1-\alpha}\{1+o(1)\}.                             \tag{9}
\]
Also \(n_T=T-\lfloor T^\rho\rfloor\sim T\), \(k_T=\lfloor T^\rho\rfloor\sim T^\rho\), and \(\sqrt{Q_a(s)\log T}=o\{Q_a(s)\}\) at both deadlines.  Substitution in the quota formula gives
\[
 K_1(n_T)\asymp T^{1-\alpha},\qquad K_0(n_T)\asymp T,
 \qquad J_1\asymp T^{\rho(1-\alpha)},\qquad J_0\asymp T^\rho.          \tag{10}
\]
All bounds depend only on the fixed envelope constants, so (8)--(10) hold uniformly over both declared classes.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} A uniform quota guarantee makes the estimator measurable without conditioning on successful adaptive recruitment. \textit{Gap.} Existing adaptive-inference proofs generally normalize by random information or assume allocation convergence; the deterministic lower-envelope quota is policy-uniform. \textit{Consumer.} Trial analysts can prespecify sample prefixes and use the full parameter interval on rare quota failures without selective-inference corrections.

\begin{lemma}[lem:old-cohort-correction]\label{lem:old-cohort-correction}
On the arm-tape experiment, \(P(M_{a,\Delta}+U_{a,\Delta})=P[Y(a)\mathbf 1\{D(a)\leq h_{T,\Delta}\}]\), \(|b_{T,\Delta}|\leq B_{T,\Delta}=2L(1+T+\Delta_T)^{-\beta}(1+o(1))\), and \(\operatorname{Var}_P(U_{1,\Delta}-U_{0,\Delta})\leq v_{T,\Delta}(J_1^{-1}+J_0^{-1})=O(T^{-\rho\beta-\rho(1-\alpha)})=o(T^{-(1-\alpha)})\), uniformly over \(\mathfrak M_T(\alpha,\beta,L,c,C,\varepsilon)\cup\mathfrak M_T^{\mathrm{low}}(\alpha,\beta,L,c,\varepsilon)\) and deterministic \(\Delta_T\geq0\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Work with the unconditional tape representation supplied by \texttt{lem:arm-tape-equivalence}.  Since \(m_T=k_T=\lfloor T^\rho\rfloor=o(T)\), for all sufficiently large \(T\),
\[
 0\leq g_{T,\Delta}<h_{T,\Delta},
 \qquad h_{T,\Delta}=T+\Delta_T-k_T.                                    \tag{1}
\]
The tape entries in each arm are identically distributed.  Therefore the fixed-prefix definitions imply
\[
 \mathbb E_P M_{a,\Delta}
 =\mathbb E_P[Y(a)\mathbf 1\{D(a)\leq g_{T,\Delta}\}],
\]
\[
 \mathbb E_P U_{a,\Delta}
 =\mathbb E_P[Y(a)\mathbf 1\{g_{T,\Delta}<D(a)\leq h_{T,\Delta}\}].    \tag{2}
\]
The two indicator sets in (2) are disjoint and their union is \(\{D(a)\leq h_{T,\Delta}\}\).  Hence
\[
 \mathbb E_P(M_{a,\Delta}+U_{a,\Delta})
 =\mathbb E_P[Y(a)\mathbf 1\{D(a)\leq h_{T,\Delta}\}],                \tag{3}
\]
which is the asserted mean identity.

By \texttt{ass:bounded-outcomes} and \texttt{ass:polynomial-delay-envelope},
\[
 \left|\mathbb E_P[Y(a)\mathbf 1\{D(a)>h_{T,\Delta}\}]\right|
 \leq P\{D(a)>h_{T,\Delta}\}
 \leq\min\{1,L(1+h_{T,\Delta})^{-\beta}\}.                            \tag{4}
\]
Applying the triangle inequality to the two arms gives \(|b_{T,\Delta}|\leq B_{T,\Delta}\).  Moreover,
\[
 \frac{1+h_{T,\Delta}}{1+T+\Delta_T}
 =1-\frac{k_T}{1+T+\Delta_T}=1+o(1)                                    \tag{5}
\]
uniformly over deterministic \(\Delta_T\geq0\).  Because \(h_{T,\Delta}\to\infty\), the minimum in \(B_{T,\Delta}\) is eventually inactive.  Thus
\[
 B_{T,\Delta}=2L(1+T+\Delta_T)^{-\beta}\{1+o(1)\}.                     \tag{6}
\]

For
\[
 R=Y(a)\mathbf 1\{g_{T,\Delta}<D(a)\leq h_{T,\Delta}\},
\]
the same two assumptions give
\[
 \mathbb E_P R^2\leq P\{D(a)>g_{T,\Delta}\}
 \leq v_{T,\Delta}.                                                      \tag{7}
\]
Consequently, \(\operatorname{Var}_P(U_{a,\Delta})\leq v_{T,\Delta}/J_a\).  The two arm tapes are independent, so
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_P(U_{1,\Delta}-U_{0,\Delta})
 \leq v_{T,\Delta}(J_1^{-1}+J_0^{-1}).                                  \tag{8}
\]
Since \(g_{T,\Delta}\geq m_T\asymp T^\rho\), (7) implies \(v_{T,\Delta}=O(T^{-\rho\beta})\), uniformly over nonnegative follow-up sequences.  The quota orders in \texttt{lem:quota-success} now yield
\[
 v_{T,\Delta}(J_1^{-1}+J_0^{-1})
 =O\!\left(T^{-\rho(\beta+1-\alpha)}\right).                           \tag{9}
\]
The definition of \(\rho\) states
\[
 \rho>\frac{1-\alpha}{1-\alpha+\beta},
\]
which is equivalent to \(\rho(\beta+1-\alpha)>1-\alpha\).  Thus (9) is \(o(T^{-(1-\alpha)})\), proving all assertions uniformly over the stated classes.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The shifted old cohort uses every response observable by the prespecified terminal time and leaves only the \(T+\Delta_T\) tail while contributing negligible noise at the rare-arm sampling scale. \textit{Gap.} Single-age delayed estimators leave bias of order \(m_T^{-\beta}\); no located inference result uses an overlapping old prefix to recover the newly revealed tail under informative delays. \textit{Consumer.} This correction allows end-of-recruitment inference to use nearly all available follow-up without sacrificing the Gaussian sampling rate.

\begin{theorem}[thm:uniform-gated-inference]\label{thm:uniform-gated-inference}
The interval \(I_{T,\Delta}\) is \(O_{T,\Delta}\)-measurable and uniformly honest over \(\mathfrak M_T(\alpha,\beta,L,c,C,\varepsilon)\cup\mathfrak M_T^{\mathrm{low}}(\alpha,\beta,L,c,\varepsilon)\), without conditioning on \(E_T\) and without convergence of \(t^\alpha\pi_t(1)\). Measurability follows from the recorded calendar revelation times, including when a response remains unrevealed at \(H_T\). On \(\mathfrak M_{T,\Delta}^{\mathrm{reg}}(\kappa)\), assign the displayed studentized quotient the value zero on \(E_T^c\). Then \(\sup_x|P\{(\widehat\tau_{T,\Delta}-\tau+b_{T,\Delta})/\widehat s_{T,\Delta}\leq x\}-\Phi(x)|\to0\) and \(\widehat s_{T,\Delta}/s_{T,\Delta}\to1\) uniformly.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write \(K_a=K_a(n_T)\) and define the unconditional virtual-tape variables
\[
 \sigma_{a,T}^2=\operatorname{Var}_P(X_{a,1}^{\Delta}),\qquad
 Z_T=(M_{1,\Delta}-P M_{1,\Delta})-(M_{0,\Delta}-P M_{0,\Delta}),
\]
\[
 C_T=(U_{1,\Delta}-P U_{1,\Delta})-(U_{0,\Delta}-P U_{0,\Delta}).
 \tag{1}
\]
By \(\mathrm{lem:arm\text{-}tape\text{-}equivalence}\), these are functions of deterministic prefixes of two independent tapes, each tape being independent and identically distributed with the corresponding arm marginal.  By \(\mathrm{lem:old\text{-}cohort\text{-}correction}\), on \(E_T\),
\[
 \widehat\tau_{T,\Delta}-\tau+b_{T,\Delta}=Z_T+C_T. \tag{2}
\]

First note that infinite delays have zero probability.  Indeed, continuity from above and \(\mathrm{ass:polynomial\text{-}delay\text{-}envelope}\), with \(\beta>0\), give
\[
 P\{D(a)=\infty\}=\lim_{u\to\infty}P\{D(a)>u\}
 \leq\lim_{u\to\infty}L(1+u)^{-\beta}=0. \tag{3}
\]
We next verify measurability.  On \(E_T\), the first \(K_a\) assignments to arm \(a\) occur by \(n_T\), and hence their ages at \(H_T\) are at least
\[
 H_T-n_T=m_T+\Delta_T=g_{T,\Delta}. \tag{4}
\]
The first \(J_a\) assignments occur by \(k_T\), and their ages are at least
\[
 H_T-k_T=h_{T,\Delta}. \tag{5}
\]
If one of these responses is revealed, \(O_{T,\Delta}\) records its outcome, delay, and revelation time.  If it is absent at \(H_T\), (4) or (5) shows that its delay exceeds the relevant truncation age, so its truncated contribution is known to be zero.  Thus every \(X_{a,j}^{\Delta}\), \(R_{a,j}^{\Delta}\), sample variance, gate, and interval endpoint is \(O_{T,\Delta}\)-measurable on \(E_T\).  On \(E_T^c\), all prescribed values depend only on observed assignment counts.  This proves the asserted measurability.

We now prove the concentration guarantee without conditioning on quota success.  For \(X\in[-1,1]\), convexity of the exponential on this interval and centering yield
\[
 P\exp\{\lambda(X-PX)\}\leq \exp(\lambda^2/2).
\]
Multiplication over the independent tape entries and optimization of the exponential Markov bound therefore give, with \(S_T=K_1^{-1}+K_0^{-1}\),
\[
 P\{|Z_T|>\sqrt{2S_Tx}\}\leq2e^{-x}. \tag{6}
\]
For an old-tail variable \(R\), boundedness and the delay envelope imply
\[
 |R-PR|\leq2,\qquad P(R-PR)^2\leq PR^2\leq v_{T,\Delta}. \tag{7}
\]
Consequently, for every integer \(j\geq2\),
\[
 P|R-PR|^j\leq2^{j-2}P(R-PR)^2.
\]
Expanding the exponential series, using \(j!\geq2\,3^{j-2}\), and optimizing the resulting Chernoff bound gives
\[
 P\left\{|U_{a,\Delta}-PU_{a,\Delta}|>
 \sqrt{\frac{2v_{T,\Delta}x}{J_a}}+\frac{4x}{3J_a}\right\}
 \leq2e^{-x}. \tag{8}
\]
With \(x=x_\eta=\log(6/\eta)\), the union of the exceptional events in (6) and the two instances of (8) has probability at most \(\eta\).  On its complement, (2), \(|b_{T,\Delta}|\leq B_{T,\Delta}\), and the triangle inequality imply coverage by \(I_{T,\Delta}^{\mathrm{conc}}\) whenever \(E_T\) occurs.  Intersection with \([-2,2]\) cannot remove \(\tau\in[-2,2]\), and the interval on \(E_T^c\) is \([-2,2]\).  Hence this coverage statement is unconditional.

It remains to prove the Gaussian branch and the validity of the observable splice.  Put
\[
 V_T=\sigma_{1,T}^2+\mathbf 1\{\alpha=0\}\sigma_{0,T}^2. \tag{9}
\]
Consider first an arbitrary triangular sequence for which \(V_T\geq1/\log T\).  If \(\alpha>0\), then
\[
 s_{T,\Delta}^2\geq\frac{1}{K_1\log T}; \tag{10}
\]
if \(\alpha=0\), the quota orders in \(\mathrm{lem:quota\text{-}success}\) imply
\[
 s_{T,\Delta}^2\geq\frac{c_0}{T\log T} \tag{11}
\]
for a constant \(c_0>0\).  Express \(Z_T/s_{T,\Delta}=\sum_j\xi_{Tj}\) as its independent centered tape summands.  Their variances sum to one.  Boundedness, (10)--(11), and \(K_1\asymp T^{1-\alpha}\) give
\[
 \delta_T^*:=\max_j|\xi_{Tj}|=
 \begin{cases}
 O\!\left(\sqrt{\log T/K_1}\right),&\alpha>0,\\
 O\!\left(\sqrt{\log T/T}\right),&\alpha=0,
 \end{cases}
 =o(1), \tag{12}
\]
uniformly in this variance regime.  For fixed \(u\in\mathbb R\), Taylor's formula gives
\[
 P e^{iu\xi_{Tj}}=1-\frac{u^2}{2}P\xi_{Tj}^2
 +O\!\left(|u|^3\delta_T^*P\xi_{Tj}^2\right), \tag{13}
\]
and
\[
 \sum_j(P\xi_{Tj}^2)^2\leq(\delta_T^*)^2\sum_jP\xi_{Tj}^2=(\delta_T^*)^2. \tag{14}
\]
Taking logarithms in (13), summing, and using (12)--(14) yields
\[
 P\exp\{iuZ_T/s_{T,\Delta}\}=e^{-u^2/2+o(1)}. \tag{15}
\]
The unit second moment gives tightness.  Every subsequential limit is therefore identified by (15) as standard normal; a contradiction-subsequence argument and continuity of \(\Phi\) give the uniform Kolmogorov conclusion
\[
 \sup_{V_T\geq1/\log T}\sup_{x\in\mathbb R}
 \left|P\{Z_T/s_{T,\Delta}\leq x\}-\Phi(x)\right|\longrightarrow0. \tag{16}
\]
Thus the normal approximation has been derived from bounded independent tape summands and deterministic quota growth; no convergence of \(t^\alpha\pi_t(1)\) is used.

The correction and main prefixes may overlap, so no independence between \(C_T\) and \(Z_T\) is asserted.  Instead, \(\mathrm{lem:old\text{-}cohort\text{-}correction}\) gives
\[
 PC_T^2=O\!\left(T^{-\rho(\beta+1-\alpha)}\right). \tag{17}
\]
Combining (10)--(11) with (17),
\[
 \frac{PC_T^2}{s_{T,\Delta}^2}
 \leq C_1(\log T)T^{1-\alpha-\rho(\beta+1-\alpha)}\longrightarrow0, \tag{18}
\]
because \(\rho(\beta+1-\alpha)>1-\alpha\).

For a sample of size \(K\geq2\), the usual sample variance is
\[
 \widehat\sigma^2=\frac{K}{K-1}(\overline{X^2}-\bar X^2).
\]
Because \(|X|\leq1\), Chebyshev's inequality applied to \(\overline{X^2}\) and \(\bar X\) yields, uniformly in the tape law,
\[
 \widehat\sigma_{X,a,\Delta}^2-\sigma_{a,T}^2=O_P(K_a^{-1/2}). \tag{19}
\]
For \(\alpha>0\), division of the two contributions to \(\widehat s^2-s^2\) by (10) bounds their relative orders by
\[
 O_P(\log T/\sqrt{K_1})=o_P(1),\qquad
 O_P(K_1\log T/K_0^{3/2})=o_P(1). \tag{20}
\]
For \(\alpha=0\), both quotas are comparable to \(T\), and (11) gives the same conclusion.  Therefore
\[
 \widehat s_{T,\Delta}/s_{T,\Delta}\longrightarrow1 \tag{21}
\]
uniformly on \(V_T\geq1/\log T\).  Equations (2), (16), (18), and (21), together with \(P(E_T^c)\leq4T^{-4}\), prove the asserted studentized approximation on this regime; assigning the quotient value zero on \(E_T^c\) changes its distribution function by at most \(P(E_T^c)\).

The regular class eventually lies in this high-variance regime.  For \(X=Y\mathbf 1\{D\leq g_{T,\Delta}\}\), boundedness gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |\operatorname{Var}_P(X)-\operatorname{Var}_P(Y)|
 &\leq|P(X^2-Y^2)|+|(PX)^2-(PY)^2|\\
 &\leq3P\{D>g_{T,\Delta}\}\leq3v_{T,\Delta}=o(1).
 \end{aligned} \tag{22}
\]
The last relation is uniform because \(g_{T,\Delta}\geq m_T\to\infty\).  The regular-variance assumption thus gives \(V_T\geq\kappa/2\) eventually, adding the two arm bounds in (22) when \(\alpha=0\).  This proves both studentization assertions on \(\mathfrak M_{T,\Delta}^{\mathrm{reg}}(\kappa)\).

For Gaussian-branch coverage, define for \(B\geq0\), \(s>0\),
\[
 f_B(s)=s c_\eta(B/s). \tag{23}
\]
If \(Z\sim N(0,1)\), the definition of \(c_\eta\) and monotonicity in the absolute shift imply, for every \(|b|\leq B\),
\[
 P\{|sZ-b|\leq f_B(s)\}
 =\Phi\{c_\eta(B/s)+b/s\}+\Phi\{c_\eta(B/s)-b/s\}-1
 \geq1-\eta. \tag{24}
\]
Let \(u=B/s\) and \(x=c_\eta(u)-u\).  The defining equation implies
\[
 z_{1-\eta}\leq x\leq z_{1-\eta/2},\qquad
 B+z_{1-\eta}s\leq f_B(s)\leq B+z_{1-\eta/2}s. \tag{25}
\]
Implicit differentiation gives
\[
 c_\eta'(u)=
 \frac{\phi(c_\eta(u)-u)-\phi(c_\eta(u)+u)}
 {\phi(c_\eta(u)-u)+\phi(c_\eta(u)+u)}\in[0,1]. \tag{26}
\]
Moreover,
\[
 f_B'(s)=x+\frac{2u\phi(x+2u)}{\phi(x)+\phi(x+2u)}. \tag{27}
\]
This derivative is bounded uniformly over \(B\geq0\), \(s>0\), because \(x\geq z_{1-\eta}>0\) and
\[
 \frac{\phi(x+2u)}{\phi(x)}=e^{-2ux-2u^2}.
\]
It follows from (21) that
\[
 f_{B_{T,\Delta}}(\widehat s_{T,\Delta})
 -f_{B_{T,\Delta}}(s_{T,\Delta})=o_P(s_{T,\Delta}) \tag{28}
\]
uniformly on the high-variance regime.  Combining (16), (18), (21), (24), and (28), and noting that \(d_{T,\Delta}\geq0\), gives
\[
 \inf_{V_T\geq1/\log T}P\{\tau\in I_{T,\Delta}^{\mathrm G}\}
 \geq1-\eta-o(1). \tag{29}
\]
For completeness, this follows by shrinking the deterministic interval in (24) by \(\epsilon s_{T,\Delta}\), applying (16), (18), and (28), and then letting \(\epsilon\downarrow0\); the Gaussian probability loss is at most \(2\epsilon/\sqrt{2\pi}\).

The quota orders and the strict inequalities in the definition of \(\rho\) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 d_{T,\Delta}
 &=O\!\left(T^{-\rho(\beta+1-\alpha)/2}\sqrt{\log T}
 +T^{-\rho(1-\alpha)}\log T\right)\\
 &\quad+O\!\left(T^{-\rho(\beta+1)/2}\sqrt{\log T}
 +T^{-\rho}\log T\right)
 =o(T^{-(1-\alpha)/2}).
 \end{aligned} \tag{30}
\]
We finally validate the data-dependent splice.  If \(V_T\leq1/\log T\), then (19) and Chebyshev's inequality imply
\[
 \sup_{V_T\leq1/\log T}P(G_{T,\Delta})
 \leq C_2(\log T)^2/K_1\longrightarrow0, \tag{31}
\]
with the analogous sum of the two errors when \(\alpha=0\).  Indeed, gate success requires the relevant sample-variance sum to exceed its population counterpart by at least \(1/\log T\).

If \(V_T>1/\log T\), then on \(G_{T,\Delta}^c\), using the deterministic bound \(\widehat\sigma^2\leq2\),
\[
 \widehat s_{T,\Delta}^2
 \leq\frac{2}{K_1\log T}+\frac{2}{K_0}
 =o(T^{-(1-\alpha)})\quad(\alpha>0), \tag{32}
\]
whereas, for \(\alpha=0\),
\[
 \widehat s_{T,\Delta}^2
 \leq\frac{2}{\log T}(K_1^{-1}+K_0^{-1})=O((T\log T)^{-1}). \tag{33}
\]
Since \(a_T=\sqrt{2S_Tx_\eta}\asymp T^{-(1-\alpha)/2}\), equations (25), (30), (32), and (33) imply, for all sufficiently large \(T\), on gate failure,
\[
 f_{B_{T,\Delta}}(\widehat s_{T,\Delta})+d_{T,\Delta}
 \leq B_{T,\Delta}+a_T. \tag{34}
\]
The concentration radius is at least \(B_{T,\Delta}+a_T\), so on gate failure the concentration interval contains the Gaussian interval.  Hence (29) proves honesty of the selected interval in the high-variance regime.  In the low-variance regime, the concentration argument following (8), minus the vanishing probability in (31), proves honesty.  Finally, \([-2,2]\) on \(E_T^c\) covers \(\tau\) identically.  The result is therefore uniform over both displayed model classes, unconditional on quota success, and requires no propensity limit.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the load-bearing uniform studentization result that makes one implementable procedure valid in both Gaussian and degenerate regimes. \textit{Gap.} Bibaut et al., Hadad et al., and Shi, Wang, and Wu rely on centered scores plus variance consistency or convergence; none treats an outcome-selected reveal process with an overlapping correction and an observable low-variance splice. \textit{Consumer.} The theorem yields a prespecifiable implementation that does not fail when the inferior-arm outcome variance is small or the adaptive allocation oscillates.

\begin{theorem}[thm:minimax-length-frontier]\label{thm:minimax-length-frontier}
For fixed \(\eta\in(0,1/2)\) and any prespecified deterministic \(\Delta_T\geq0\), there exist constants \(0<c_\eta\leq C_\eta<\infty\) such that \(I_{T,\Delta}\) satisfies \(\inf_{(P,\Pi,T)\in\mathfrak M_T}P\{\tau\in I_{T,\Delta}\}\geq1-\eta-o(1)\) and \(\sup_{(P,\Pi,T)\in\mathfrak M_T}\mathbb E_{P,\Pi}[|I_{T,\Delta}|]\leq C_\eta r_{T,\Delta}\). Conversely, every \(O_{T,\Delta}\)-measurable interval \(J_{T,\Delta}\) with uniform coverage at least \(1-\eta\) satisfies \(\sup_{(P,\Pi,T)\in\mathfrak M_T}\mathbb E_{P,\Pi}[|J_{T,\Delta}|]\geq c_\eta r_{T,\Delta}\). Here each \(\mathbb E_{P,\Pi}\) integrates over the outcome-delay arrivals under \(P\) and any randomization induced by the predictable adaptive design policy \(\Pi\). The lower bound uses the same fixed admissible policy in each paired experiment, with a prompt-outcome mean tilt for \(T^{-(1-\alpha)/2}\) and hidden-sign laws concentrated at the finite delay \(H_T+1\) for \((1+H_T)^{-\beta}\). At \(\Delta_T=0\), the frontier is \(\max\{T^{-(1-\alpha)/2},T^{-\beta}\}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(q=(1-\alpha)/2\).  Uniform coverage of \(I_{T,\Delta}\) over \(\mathfrak M_T(\alpha,\beta,L,c,C,\varepsilon)\) follows from \(\mathrm{thm:uniform\text{-}gated\text{-}inference}\).  We prove the length upper bound and both converse components.

By \(\mathrm{lem:quota\text{-}success}\), uniformly over the class,
\[
 K_1(n_T)\asymp T^{1-\alpha},\quad K_0(n_T)\asymp T,
 \quad J_1\asymp T^{\rho(1-\alpha)},\quad J_0\asymp T^\rho. \tag{1}
\]
Thus
\[
 S_T=K_1(n_T)^{-1}+K_0(n_T)^{-1}=O(T^{-(1-\alpha)}),
 \qquad a_T=O(T^{-q}). \tag{2}
\]
Since \(g_{T,\Delta}\geq m_T\asymp T^\rho\),
\[
 v_{T,\Delta}=O(T^{-\rho\beta}). \tag{3}
\]
For fixed \(x>0\), (1)--(3) imply
\[
 A_{1,T,\Delta}(x)=O\!\left(T^{-\rho(\beta+1-\alpha)/2}
 +T^{-\rho(1-\alpha)}\right), \tag{4}
\]
\[
 A_{0,T,\Delta}(x)=O\!\left(T^{-\rho(\beta+1)/2}+T^{-\rho}\right). \tag{5}
\]
The restriction \(\rho>(1-\alpha)/(1-\alpha+\beta)\) makes the first square-root term in (4) \(o(T^{-q})\), and \(\rho>1/2\) makes its linear term \(o(T^{-q})\).  The arm-zero exponents are no smaller.  The inequalities are strict, so they also absorb the logarithms at \(x=4\log T\); hence
\[
 d_{T,\Delta}=o(T^{-q}). \tag{6}
\]
Every main-prefix variable lies in \([-1,1]\), and its usual sample variance is at most two.  Therefore, on \(E_T\),
\[
 \widehat s_{T,\Delta}^2\leq2S_T,qquad
 \widehat s_{T,\Delta}=O(T^{-q}). \tag{7}
\]
Let \(z=z_{1-\eta/2}\).  Evaluating the defining coverage function of \(c_\eta(u)\) at \(u+z\) gives
\[
 \Phi(z)+\Phi(2u+z)-1\geq2\Phi(z)-1=1-\eta.
\]
Monotonicity in the critical value therefore yields
\[
 c_\eta(u)\leq u+z,qquad
 \widehat s_{T,\Delta}c_\eta(B_{T,\Delta}/\widehat s_{T,\Delta})
 \leq B_{T,\Delta}+z\widehat s_{T,\Delta}. \tag{8}
\]
Also, because \(h_{T,\Delta}=H_T-k_T\), \(H_T\geq T\), and \(k_T=O(T^\rho)=o(T)\),
\[
 \frac{1+h_{T,\Delta}}{1+H_T}=1-\frac{k_T}{1+H_T}=1+o(1)
\]
uniformly over deterministic \(\Delta_T\geq0\), and therefore
\[
 B_{T,\Delta}=O((1+H_T)^{-\beta}). \tag{9}
\]
Equations (2), (4)--(9) bound either active branch on \(E_T\) by a constant multiple of
\[
 r_{T,\Delta}=\max\{T^{-q},(1+H_T)^{-\beta}\}. \tag{10}
\]
On \(E_T^c\), the interval has length four, while \(\mathrm{lem:quota\text{-}success}\) gives
\[
 4P(E_T^c)\leq16T^{-4}=o(T^{-q})=o(r_{T,\Delta}). \tag{11}
\]
Taking expectations proves the uniform upper bound.

For the sampling converse, use the same deterministic policy under both alternatives:
\[
 p_t=\pi_t(1)=ct^{-\alpha},\qquad \pi_t(0)=1-p_t. \tag{12}
\]
It is admissible because \(c\leq C\) and \(1-p_t\geq1-c\geq\varepsilon\).  Set both delays to zero and \(Y_t(0)=0\).  Under \(P_+\) and \(P_-\), let the arm-one outcome be Rademacher with respective means \(d_T\) and \(-d_T\), where
\[
 Q_T=\sum_{t=1}^Tp_t,\qquad d_T=\lambda Q_T^{-1/2},
 \qquad 0<\lambda<\frac{1-2\eta}{2\sqrt2}. \tag{13}
\]
Integral comparison and \(\alpha<1\) give \(Q_T\asymp T^{1-\alpha}\), so \(d_T\asymp T^{-q}\) and \(d_T<1\) eventually.  The two causal contrasts are \(\tau_+=d_T\) and \(\tau_-=-d_T\).

The one-observation Hellinger affinity of the two arm-one Rademacher laws is \(\sqrt{1-d_T^2}\).  Since assignments have their common deterministic-probability law under both alternatives, the exact affinity of the observed experiments is
\[
 \operatorname{Aff}(P_+^O,P_-^O)
 =\prod_{t=1}^T\left[1-p_t\{1-\sqrt{1-d_T^2}\}\right]
 \geq1-\sum_{t=1}^Tp_td_T^2=1-\lambda^2. \tag{14}
\]
Here \(1-\sqrt{1-x}\leq x\) and \(\prod_t(1-x_t)\geq1-\sum_tx_t\) for numbers in \([0,1]\).  For dominated laws with densities \(p\) and \(q\), Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \|P-Q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 &=\frac12\int|\sqrt p-\sqrt q|(\sqrt p+\sqrt q)\\
 &\leq\sqrt{1-\operatorname{Aff}(P,Q)^2}.
 \end{aligned} \tag{15}
\]
Thus
\[
 \|P_+^O-P_-^O\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq\sqrt2\lambda. \tag{16}
\]
If \(J_{T,\Delta}\) has the asserted uniform coverage, transfer of \(\{\tau_-\in J_{T,\Delta}\}\) from \(P_-^O\) to \(P_+^O\), followed by a union bound, gives
\[
 P_+^O\{\tau_+,\tau_-\in J_{T,\Delta}\}
 \geq1-2\eta-o(1)-\sqrt2\lambda. \tag{17}
\]
The right side is eventually positive.  On this event the interval length is at least \(2d_T\), and hence
\[
 \sup_{(P,\Pi,T)\in\mathfrak M_T}
 \mathbb E_{P,\Pi}|J_{T,\Delta}|\geq c_{\eta,1}T^{-q}. \tag{18}
\]

For the hidden-tail converse, retain (12) and put
\[
 \delta_T=\min\{1/4,L(1+H_T)^{-\beta}\}. \tag{19}
\]
For every recruitment time and arm, independently draw a Bernoulli indicator with probability \(\delta_T\).  On failure set \((Y_t(a),D_t(a))=(0,0)\); on success set \(D_t(a)=H_T+1\).  Under \(P_+\), set successful outcomes to \(+1\) in arm one and \(-1\) in arm zero; under \(P_-\), reverse the signs.  The arrival vectors are independent and identically distributed over \(t\), all outcomes are bounded, and all delays are finite.  Moreover,
\[
 P_\pm\{D_t(a)>u\}=
 \begin{cases}
 \delta_T,&0\leq u\leq H_T,\\
 0,&u\geq H_T+1,
 \end{cases}
 \leq\min\{1,L(1+u)^{-\beta}\}. \tag{20}
\]
No successful atom is revealed by \(H_T\).  The immediate zero records, nonrevelation pattern, assignments, and logged propensities therefore have the same law under \(P_+\) and \(P_-\), so the laws of \(O_{T,\Delta}\) coincide.  Nevertheless,
\[
 \tau_+=2\delta_T,qquad \tau_-=-2\delta_T. \tag{21}
\]
Under the common observed law, uniform coverage and a union bound imply
\[
 P\{\tau_+,\tau_-\in J_{T,\Delta}\}\geq1-2\eta-o(1). \tag{22}
\]
On this event the interval length is at least \(4\delta_T\).  Since \(H_T\to\infty\), the second term in (19) is eventually active, and
\[
 \sup_{(P,\Pi,T)\in\mathfrak M_T}
 \mathbb E_{P,\Pi}|J_{T,\Delta}|\geq c_{\eta,2}(1+H_T)^{-\beta}. \tag{23}
\]
The witness deliberately permits dependence between outcome and delay.

The affinity identity (14) and the equality of observed laws used in (21) are finite-horizon identities, so this stress test never conditions on quota or recruitment success.  Combining (18) and (23) proves the lower bound at the rate in (10).  When \(\Delta_T=0\), \(H_T=T\), and the rate is \(\max\{T^{-(1-\alpha)/2},T^{-\beta}\}\), as claimed.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This two-sided theorem is retained as a convenient subclass presentation of the rate frontier. Its achievability uses only the lower allocation envelope, and the fixed-policy prompt-tilt and hidden-sign witnesses establish its converse while also transferring the same lower bound to the larger one-sided class; the rare-arm upper envelope is not needed for either testing argument. \textit{Gap.} The one-sided forced-exploration regime in Shi, Wang, and Wu is paired there with outcome--delay independence, not with an exact uniformly honest interval-length frontier under informative delay. The prompt sampling witness remains compatible with their independence restriction, whereas the hidden-sign tail witness deliberately falls outside it; Lancewicki et al. establish indistinguishability for regret rather than interval length. \textit{Consumer.} Investigators can treat this retained two-sided result as a convenient subclass guarantee while using the stronger one-sided theorem to translate an exploration floor, control floor, and endpoint-tail audit into the shortest defensible order of uncertainty.

\begin{theorem}[thm:attained-boundary-calibration]\label{thm:attained-boundary-calibration}
Suppose \(\Delta_T=0\) and \(\beta=q=(1-\alpha)/2\). Uniformly on \(\mathfrak M_{T,0}^{\mathrm{reg}}(\kappa)\), \(I_{T,0}^{\mathrm G}\) has asymptotic coverage at least \(1-\eta\). For deterministic \(\pi_t(1)=ct^{-\alpha}\), there is a Rademacher immediate-outcome plus finite hidden-sign delay family in the same experiment for which \((\widehat\tau_{T,0}-\tau)/s_{T,0}\Rightarrow N(-u,1)\) and \(B_{T,0}/s_{T,0}\to u>0\); for every fixed \(c'>0\), the limiting coverage of \([\widehat\tau_{T,0}\pm c'\widehat s_{T,0}]\) equals \(\Phi(c'-u)+\Phi(c'+u)-1\). Hence \(c_\eta(u)\) is attained for symmetric intervals at the phase boundary.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(Z\sim N(0,1)\).  For \(c'>0\) and \(u\geq0\),
\[
 P\{|Z-u|\leq c'\}=\Phi(c'-u)+\Phi(c'+u)-1. \tag{1}
\]
The derivative of the right side with respect to \(u\) is \(\phi(c'+u)-\phi(c'-u)\leq0\).  For fixed \(u\), it is continuous and strictly increasing in \(c'\), from zero to one.  This proves existence and uniqueness of \(c_\eta(u)\), and its defining equation gives exact coverage \(1-\eta\) for a Gaussian shift of magnitude \(u\).

Now let \(\Delta_T=0\) and \(\beta=q=(1-\alpha)/2\).  On \(\mathfrak M_{T,0}^{\mathrm{reg}}(\kappa)\), boundedness and the delay envelope imply
\[
 |\operatorname{Var}_P(Y(a)\mathbf 1\{D(a)\leq g_{T,0}\})
 -\operatorname{Var}_P(Y(a))|
 \leq3P\{D(a)>g_{T,0}\}=o(1). \tag{2}
\]
Together with the regular variance condition and deterministic quota orders, this yields constants \(0<c_0<C_0<\infty\) such that
\[
 c_0T^{-2q}\leq s_{T,0}^2\leq C_0T^{-2q}. \tag{3}
\]
The old-cohort lemma gives \(B_{T,0}=O(T^{-q})\).  Therefore \(B_{T,0}/s_{T,0}\) is uniformly bounded.  The uniform studentized approximation in \(\mathrm{thm:uniform\text{-}gated\text{-}inference}\), its conclusion \(\widehat s_{T,0}/s_{T,0}\to1\), the bounded derivative of \(s\mapsto s c_\eta(B/s)\) established there, and \(|b_{T,0}|\leq B_{T,0}\) imply from (1) that
\[
 \inf_{(P,\Pi,T)\in\mathfrak M_{T,0}^{\mathrm{reg}}(\kappa)}
 P\{\tau\in I_{T,0}^{\mathrm G}\}\geq1-\eta-o(1). \tag{4}
\]
The additional radius \(d_{T,0}=o(T^{-q})=o(s_{T,0})\) is nonnegative and cannot decrease coverage.

We construct an attaining family.  Use the deterministic policy
\[
 \pi_t(1)=ct^{-\alpha},\qquad \pi_t(0)=1-ct^{-\alpha}, \tag{5}
\]
and put \(\delta_T=L(1+T)^{-q}\), which is below \(1/2\) for all sufficiently large \(T\).  Index the laws by \(\zeta\in\{-1,1\}\).  In arm one, with probability \(\delta_T\), set \((D,Y)=(T+1,\zeta)\); conditional on \(D=0\), let \(Y\in\{-1,1\}\) have mean \(-\zeta\delta_T/(1-\delta_T)\).  In arm zero, with probability \(\delta_T\), set \((D,Y)=(T+1,-\zeta)\); conditional on \(D=0\), let \(Y\in\{-1,1\}\) have mean \(\zeta\delta_T/(1-\delta_T)\).  Take the arrival vectors independent and identically distributed over \(t\), with the arms independent within an arrival.

All outcomes are bounded and all delays are finite.  For integers \(0\leq u\leq T\),
\[
 P\{D(a)>u\}=\delta_T\leq L(1+u)^{-q}, \tag{6}
\]
and the probability is zero for \(u\geq T+1\).  Thus the delay envelope holds, although outcome and delay are dependent.  In each arm the visible and hidden means cancel and \(Y^2=1\), so
\[
 PY(1)=PY(0)=0,qquad
 \operatorname{Var}_P(Y(1))=\operatorname{Var}_P(Y(0))=1. \tag{7}
\]
The family therefore satisfies the sampling, support, design, overlap, and regular-variance conditions whenever that bounded-support floor is feasible.

Since \(g_{T,0}<h_{T,0}<T+1\), every old-cohort correction variable is zero.  Taking \(\zeta=1\),
\[
 \tau=0,qquad b_{T,0}=2\delta_T,qquad
 \operatorname{Var}_P(X_{a,1}^{0})=1-\delta_T-\delta_T^2. \tag{8}
\]
Hence
\[
 s_{T,0}^2=(1-\delta_T-\delta_T^2)(K_1^{-1}+K_0^{-1}). \tag{9}
\]
Because \(h_{T,0}=T-k_T\),
\[
 \frac{b_{T,0}}{B_{T,0}}
 =\left(\frac{1+h_{T,0}}{1+T}\right)^q\longrightarrow1. \tag{10}
\]
Integral comparison in the deterministic quota formula gives
\[
 \frac{K_1}{T^{1-\alpha}}\longrightarrow\frac{c}{1-\alpha},
 \qquad \frac{K_0}{T}\longrightarrow\varepsilon. \tag{11}
\]
Equations (9)--(11) yield
\[
 \frac{B_{T,0}}{s_{T,0}}\longrightarrow u=
 \begin{cases}
 2L\sqrt{c/(1-\alpha)},&\alpha>0,\\
 2L(c^{-1}+\varepsilon^{-1})^{-1/2},&\alpha=0,
 \end{cases} \tag{12}
\]
which is finite and strictly positive.

The bounded independent triangular-array calculation in \(\mathrm{thm:uniform\text{-}gated\text{-}inference}\), together with quota success and sample-variance consistency, gives
\[
 \frac{\widehat\tau_{T,0}-\tau+b_{T,0}}{s_{T,0}}\Rightarrow N(0,1),
 \qquad \frac{\widehat s_{T,0}}{s_{T,0}}\longrightarrow1. \tag{13}
\]
Combining (10)--(13),
\[
 \frac{\widehat\tau_{T,0}-\tau}{s_{T,0}}\Rightarrow N(-u,1),
 \qquad \frac{B_{T,0}}{s_{T,0}}\longrightarrow u. \tag{14}
\]
Since \(\tau=0\) lies in the interior of \([-2,2]\), while the estimator and radii converge to zero, truncation at the parameter-space boundary is asymptotically irrelevant.  Thus for every fixed \(c'>0\),
\[
 P\{\tau\in[\widehat\tau_{T,0}\pm c'\widehat s_{T,0}]\}
 \longrightarrow\Phi(c'-u)+\Phi(c'+u)-1. \tag{15}
\]
By (1), \(c'=c_\eta(u)\) gives equality \(1-\eta\), and every smaller symmetric critical value has strictly smaller limiting coverage.  This proves attainment at the phase boundary.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The result converts the phase boundary into an attained local experiment rather than a conservative bias comparison. \textit{Gap.} Armstrong and Kolesár supply the generic bounded-bias Gaussian calibration, while delayed-bandit work does not derive or attain its bias ratio in the adaptive observed experiment. \textit{Consumer.} This gives trial statisticians the correct critical value when the endpoint-tail and exploration exponents meet at the nonzero-shift boundary.

\begin{proposition}[prop:wald-followup-frontier]\label{prop:wald-followup-frontier}
Assume that \(\mathfrak M_{T,\Delta}^{\mathrm{reg}}(\kappa)\) is nonempty for all sufficiently large \(T\), equivalently that the bounded-support variance floor is feasible: \(\kappa\leq1\) if \(\alpha>0\), and \(\kappa\leq2\) if \(\alpha=0\). On \(\mathfrak M_{T,\Delta}^{\mathrm{reg}}(\kappa)\), the ordinary interval \(I_{T,\Delta}^{\mathrm W}\), computed from outcomes observed through \(H_T=T+\Delta_T\), is uniformly asymptotically honest if and only if \(T^q(1+T+\Delta_T)^{-\beta}\to0\). If this product converges to a positive finite constant, a compatible hidden-sign family has a nonzero shifted-Gaussian limit and ordinary-Wald undercoverage; if it diverges, ordinary-Wald coverage tends to zero along a compatible family. At \(\Delta_T=0\), ordinary Wald is uniformly honest exactly when \(\beta>q\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Because \(|Y(a)|\leq1\), \(\operatorname{Var}_P\{Y(a)\}\leq1\).  Thus nonemptiness of \(\mathfrak M_{T,\Delta}^{\mathrm{reg}}(\kappa)\) requires \(\kappa\leq1\) when \(\alpha>0\), and \(\kappa\leq2\) when \(\alpha=0\).  Conversely, under the respective restriction, take prompt symmetric Rademacher outcomes in both arms, zero delays, and \(\pi_t(1)=ct^{-\alpha}\).  Then \(\pi_t(0)\geq1-c\geq\varepsilon\), so the law belongs to the regular class and proves sufficiency of these feasibility restrictions.

On the regular class,
\[
 |\operatorname{Var}_P(Y(a)\mathbf 1\{D(a)\leq g_{T,\Delta}\})
 -\operatorname{Var}_P(Y(a))|
 \leq3P\{D(a)>g_{T,\Delta}\}=o(1). \tag{1}
\]
The regular variance floor and quota orders therefore give constants \(0<c_0<C_0<\infty\) such that
\[
 c_0T^{-(1-\alpha)}\leq s_{T,\Delta}^2
 \leq C_0T^{-(1-\alpha)}. \tag{2}
\]
For the lower bound, arm one suffices if \(\alpha>0\); if \(\alpha=0\), both quotas are comparable to \(T\).  The upper bound follows from \(\sigma_{a,T}^2\leq1\).

The uniform studentized theorem gives
\[
 \frac{\widehat\tau_{T,\Delta}-\tau}{\widehat s_{T,\Delta}}
 =\frac{\widehat\tau_{T,\Delta}-\tau+b_{T,\Delta}}
 {\widehat s_{T,\Delta}}
 -\frac{b_{T,\Delta}}{\widehat s_{T,\Delta}}, \tag{3}
\]
where the first term converges uniformly to \(N(0,1)\), and \(\widehat s_{T,\Delta}/s_{T,\Delta}\to1\).  The bias bound and (2) imply
\[
 \sup_{\mathfrak M_{T,\Delta}^{\mathrm{reg}}(\kappa)}
 \frac{|b_{T,\Delta}|}{s_{T,\Delta}}
 \leq C_1T^q(1+T+\Delta_T)^{-\beta}. \tag{4}
\]
If the right side tends to zero, the second term in (3) is uniformly \(o_P(1)\).  The normal distribution is continuous, so the ordinary interval with critical value \(z_{1-\eta/2}\) is uniformly asymptotically honest.

For necessity, set
\[
 \delta_T=\min\{1/4,L(1+H_T)^{-\beta}\}, \tag{5}
\]
use \(\pi_t(1)=ct^{-\alpha}\), and index a triangular family by \(\gamma\in\{-1,1\}\).  Put \(s_1=\gamma\), \(s_0=-\gamma\).  Independently over arrivals and arms, with probability \(\delta_T\) set
\[
 (D_t(a),Y_t(a))=(H_T+1,s_a), \tag{6}
\]
and with probability \(1-\delta_T\) set \(D_t(a)=0\) and draw \(Y_t(a)\in\{-1,1\}\) with mean
\[
 -\frac{\delta_Ts_a}{1-\delta_T}. \tag{7}
\]
This mean is in \([-1,1]\) because \(\delta_T\leq1/4\).  For every integer \(u\geq0\),
\[
 P\{D_t(a)>u\}=
 \begin{cases}
 \delta_T,&0\leq u\leq H_T,\\
 0,&u\geq H_T+1,
 \end{cases}
 \leq\min\{1,L(1+u)^{-\beta}\}. \tag{8}
\]
All outcomes are bounded and all delays are finite.  Direct calculation gives
\[
 PY_t(a)=0,qquad \operatorname{Var}_P\{Y_t(a)\}=1, \tag{9}
\]
so the construction belongs to every support-feasible regular class.  Because \(g_{T,\Delta}<h_{T,\Delta}<H_T+1\), its correction variables vanish and
\[
 b_{T,\Delta}=2\gamma\delta_T. \tag{10}
\]
The visible variables have variance \(1-\delta_T-\delta_T^2\to1\).  Integral comparison gives
\[
 \frac{K_1(n_T)}{T^{1-\alpha}}\longrightarrow\frac{c}{1-\alpha},
 \qquad \frac{K_0(n_T)}{T}\longrightarrow\varepsilon. \tag{11}
\]
Consequently, for some constant \(s_*>0\),
\[
 T^qs_{T,\Delta}\longrightarrow s_*. \tag{12}
\]
The second term in (5) is eventually active, so
\[
 \frac{b_{T,\Delta}}{s_{T,\Delta}}
 =\frac{2\gamma L}{s_*}T^q(1+H_T)^{-\beta}\{1+o(1)\}. \tag{13}
\]
If the product in (13) converges to a finite positive constant, choose \(\gamma=1\).  Equation (3) then converges to \(N(-u,1)\) for some \(u>0\), and ordinary-Wald coverage converges to
\[
 \Phi(z_{1-\eta/2}-u)+\Phi(z_{1-\eta/2}+u)-1<1-\eta. \tag{14}
\]
The strict inequality follows by differentiation with respect to \(u>0\).  If the product diverges, the centered term in (3) remains tight while the absolute shift diverges, so coverage tends to zero.  Since \(\delta_T\to0\), truncation by \([-2,2]\) is asymptotically irrelevant.  If the product fails to tend to zero, a subsequence is bounded away from zero and has a further subsequence on which it either converges to a positive finite limit or diverges.  Therefore uniform honesty is possible only if
\[
 T^q(1+T+\Delta_T)^{-\beta}\longrightarrow0. \tag{15}
\]
This proves the equivalence.  At \(\Delta_T=0\), the left side of (15) is \(T^{q-\beta}\{1+o(1)\}\), which tends to zero exactly when \(\beta>q\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Because follow-up changes both truncation ages and the observations entering the statistic, the uniform if-and-only-if criterion separates a class-wide ordinary-Wald guarantee, a class-wide tail-padding requirement, and sufficient endpoint follow-up. \textit{Gap.} Classical delayed urn inference gives usual asymptotics, and delayed CARA theory assumes ignorable delays; neither characterizes the informative-tail follow-up boundary. \textit{Consumer.} A protocol can select no pad, a bias-aware pad, or additional follow-up from the prespecified exploration and endpoint-tail exponents.

\begin{proposition}[prop:prompt-outcome-reduction]\label{prop:prompt-outcome-reduction}
If \(D_t(a)=0\) almost surely for both arms, then for every \(\Delta_T\geq0\), the actual hidden-tail bias satisfies \(b_{T,\Delta}=0\), \(U_{a,\Delta}=0\), and \(\widehat\tau_{T,\Delta}\) reduces to the difference of independent fixed-quota arm means. On the regular class, \(I_{T,\Delta}^{\mathrm W}\) is pointwise asymptotically honest for this prompt-delay law. The generic envelope radius \(B_{T,\Delta}=2\min\{1,L(1+h_{T,\Delta})^{-\beta}\}\) need not equal zero under this specialization; the generic Gaussian branch reduces asymptotically to ordinary Wald only along sequences for which \(B_{T,\Delta}/\widehat s_{T,\Delta}\to0\), in which case \(c_\eta(B_{T,\Delta}/\widehat s_{T,\Delta})\to z_{1-\eta/2}\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Assume \(D_t(a)=0\) almost surely for both arms.  Since \(g_{T,\Delta}\geq0\) and \(h_{T,\Delta}\geq0\) for all sufficiently large \(T\), the definitions give
\[
 X_{a,j}^{\Delta}=Y_{a,j},\qquad R_{a,j}^{\Delta}=0,
 \qquad U_{a,\Delta}=0. \tag{1}
\]
Also \(\mathbf 1\{D(a)>h_{T,\Delta}\}=0\), so \(b_{T,\Delta}=0\).  On quota success,
\[
 \widehat\tau_{T,\Delta}=M_{1,\Delta}-M_{0,\Delta}, \tag{2}
\]
a difference of independent deterministic-prefix tape means by \(\mathrm{lem:arm\text{-}tape\text{-}equivalence}\).

On the regular class, the variance floor and quota orders make the largest normalized summand in (2) tend to zero.  The bounded triangular-array calculation in \(\mathrm{thm:uniform\text{-}gated\text{-}inference}\) therefore gives a standard-normal limit, and its sample-variance calculation gives \(\widehat s_{T,\Delta}/s_{T,\Delta}\to1\).  Quota failure has probability at most \(4T^{-4}\) and invokes the full parameter interval.  Hence \(I_{T,\Delta}^{\mathrm W}\) is pointwise asymptotically honest for every regular prompt-delay law.

The deterministic quantity
\[
 B_{T,\Delta}=2\min\{1,L(1+h_{T,\Delta})^{-\beta}\} \tag{3}
\]
is a worst-case radius for the whole tail-envelope class and need not vanish under this prompt-delay specialization.  Continuity and strict monotonicity of the defining equation for \(c_\eta\) give
\[
 c_\eta(u)\longrightarrow c_\eta(0)=z_{1-\eta/2}
 \qquad\text{as }u\downarrow0. \tag{4}
\]
On a regular prompt-delay sequence, the quota orders, \(g_{T,\Delta}\geq T^\rho\{1+o(1)\}\), and the strict restrictions on \(\rho\) also imply
\[
 d_{T,\Delta}/\widehat s_{T,\Delta}\longrightarrow0. \tag{5}
\]
Thus, if \(B_{T,\Delta}/\widehat s_{T,\Delta}\to0\), equations (4)--(5) show that the Gaussian-branch radius divided by \(\widehat s_{T,\Delta}\) tends to \(z_{1-\eta/2}\), so the branch reduces asymptotically to ordinary Wald.  Conversely, variance consistency makes \(B_{T,\Delta}/\widehat s_{T,\Delta}\) asymptotically equivalent to \(B_{T,\Delta}/s_{T,\Delta}\).  If this ratio does not tend to zero, a subsequence is bounded away from zero.  Since implicit differentiation gives \(c_\eta'(u)>0\) for every \(u>0\), the Gaussian critical value stays separated from \(z_{1-\eta/2}\) on a further subsequence.  Hence the ratio condition is also necessary for asymptotic reduction of the generic Gaussian branch to ordinary Wald.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The reduction separates the zero actual bias of a prompt-delay law from the possibly positive conservative radius retained by the generic envelope-based procedure. \textit{Gap.} This is a required compatibility check rather than a novelty claim. \textit{Consumer.} Implementers can use ordinary fixed-quota Wald inference for a certified prompt-delay law and can identify when the generic envelope-based Gaussian branch remains conservatively padded.

\begin{openendedquestion}[oeq:exact-minimax-constant]\label{oeq:exact-minimax-constant}
At \(\Delta_T=0\), does the normalized minimax expected length \(r_{T,0}^{-1}\inf_{J_T}\sup_{(P,\Pi,T)\in\mathfrak M_T}\mathbb E_{P,\Pi}[|J_T|]\), where the infimum ranges over all \(O_{T,0}\)-measurable intervals with uniform coverage at least \(1-\eta\) and \(\mathbb E_{P,\Pi}\) integrates over the outcome-delay arrivals under \(P\) and any randomization induced by the predictable adaptive design policy \(\Pi\), converge to \(\mathcal C_\eta^*(\alpha,\beta,L,c,C,\varepsilon)\); and can all asymptotically optimal variable-length intervals be characterized at the Gaussian, bounded-shift, and hidden-tail faces of the observed-experiment modulus?
\end{openendedquestion}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Order optimality does not determine the economically relevant constant or whether data-dependent length improves on symmetric bias-aware intervals. \textit{Gap.} Armstrong and Kolesár characterize optimal intervals in Gaussian regression experiments, while no observed adaptive delayed experiment has an exact modulus or variable-length optimum. \textit{Consumer.} The answer would benchmark the efficiency loss of the measurable two-cohort procedure and determine whether further interval shortening is possible without weakening honesty.

\begin{theorem}[thm:one-sided-minimax-length-frontier]\label{thm:one-sided-minimax-length-frontier}
For fixed \(\eta\in(0,1/2)\), fixed class parameters \(\alpha\in[0,1)\), \(\beta,L,c,\varepsilon>0\) satisfying the declared parameter restrictions, and any prespecified deterministic \(\Delta_T\geq0\), there exist constants \(0<c_\eta\leq C_\eta<\infty\) such that
\[
 \inf_{(P,\Pi,T)\in\mathfrak M_T^{\mathrm{low}}(\alpha,\beta,L,c,\varepsilon)}
 P\{\tau\in I_{T,\Delta}\}\geq1-\eta-o(1)
\]
and
\[
 \sup_{(P,\Pi,T)\in\mathfrak M_T^{\mathrm{low}}(\alpha,\beta,L,c,\varepsilon)}
 \mathbb E_{P,\Pi}|I_{T,\Delta}|\leq C_\eta r_{T,\Delta}.
\]
Conversely, every \(O_{T,\Delta}\)-measurable interval \(J_{T,\Delta}\) satisfying
\[
 \inf_{(P,\Pi,T)\in\mathfrak M_T^{\mathrm{low}}(\alpha,\beta,L,c,\varepsilon)}
 P\{\tau\in J_{T,\Delta}\}\geq1-\eta-o(1)
\]
obeys
\[
 \sup_{(P,\Pi,T)\in\mathfrak M_T^{\mathrm{low}}(\alpha,\beta,L,c,\varepsilon)}
 \mathbb E_{P,\Pi}|J_{T,\Delta}|\geq c_\eta r_{T,\Delta}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(T\). Here \(r_{T,\Delta}=\max\{T^{-(1-\alpha)/2},(1+T+\Delta_T)^{-\beta}\}\), and each expectation integrates over the independent and identically distributed outcome--delay arrivals under \(P\) and every randomization induced by the predictable policy \(\Pi\). Both converse components use the same fixed policy \(\pi_t(1)=ct^{-\alpha}\): prompt mean tilts give the sampling term, while finite hidden-sign delays at \(H_T+1\) give the tail term. Thus, at \(\Delta_T=0\), the exact class-minimax expected-length order is \(\max\{T^{-(1-\alpha)/2},T^{-\beta}\}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write \(q=(1-\alpha)/2\).  First note the design-class relation.  The two-sided class is contained in the one-sided class because its rare-arm lower bound is the same and
\[
 \pi_t(0)=1-\pi_t(1)\geq1-Ct^{-\alpha}\geq1-C\geq\varepsilon.
 \tag{1}
\]
In particular, the deterministic policy
\[
 p_t:=\pi_t(1)=ct^{-\alpha},
 \qquad \pi_t(0)=1-p_t,
 \tag{2}
\]
belongs to \(\mathfrak M_T^{\mathrm{low}}(\alpha,\beta,L,c,\varepsilon)\), since \(1-p_t\geq1-c\geq\varepsilon\).

For the upper bound, uniform coverage is the one-sided-class conclusion of \(\texttt{thm:uniform-gated-inference}\).  By \(\texttt{lem:quota-success}\), uniformly over that class,
\[
 K_1(n_T)\asymp T^{1-\alpha},\quad K_0(n_T)\asymp T,
 \quad J_1\asymp T^{\rho(1-\alpha)},\quad J_0\asymp T^\rho,
 \quad P(E_T^c)\leq4T^{-4}.
 \tag{3}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 S_T=K_1(n_T)^{-1}+K_0(n_T)^{-1}=O(T^{-(1-\alpha)}),
 \qquad a_T=O(T^{-q}).
 \tag{4}
\]
Because \(g_{T,\Delta}=m_T+\Delta_T\geq m_T\asymp T^\rho\), \(\texttt{ass:polynomial-delay-envelope}\) gives
\[
 v_{T,\Delta}=O(T^{-\rho\beta}).
 \tag{5}
\]
For every fixed \(x>0\), (3)--(5) and \(\texttt{def:hybrid-interval}\) imply
\[
 A_{1,T,\Delta}(x)
 =O\!\left(T^{-\rho(\beta+1-\alpha)/2}
          +T^{-\rho(1-\alpha)}\right)=o(T^{-q}),
 \tag{6}
\]
and
\[
 A_{0,T,\Delta}(x)
 =O\!\left(T^{-\rho(\beta+1)/2}+T^{-\rho}\right)=o(T^{-q}).
 \tag{7}
\]
Indeed, the chosen cohort exponent satisfies
\[
 \rho(\beta+1-\alpha)>1-\alpha,
 \qquad \rho(1-\alpha)>(1-\alpha)/2,
 \tag{8}
\]
and the arm-zero inequalities are weaker.  The strict inequalities in (8) absorb the factors \(\sqrt{\log T}\) and \(\log T\) appearing when \(x=4\log T\); hence
\[
 d_{T,\Delta}=o(T^{-q}).
 \tag{9}
\]

For observations in \([-1,1]\), the usual unbiased sample variance based on at least two observations is at most two.  Therefore, on \(E_T\),
\[
 \widehat s_{T,\Delta}^2\leq2S_T,
 \qquad \widehat s_{T,\Delta}=O(T^{-q}).
 \tag{10}
\]
Let \(z=z_{1-\eta/2}\).  At \(c=u+z\), the defining left-hand side for the bounded-shift critical value is at least
\(2\Phi(z)-1=1-\eta\).  Monotonicity in \(c\) and uniqueness in \(\texttt{def:bounded-shift-critical-value}\) therefore give
\[
 c_\eta(u)\leq u+z,
 \qquad
 \widehat s_{T,\Delta}c_\eta(B_{T,\Delta}/\widehat s_{T,\Delta})
 \leq B_{T,\Delta}+z\widehat s_{T,\Delta}.
 \tag{11}
\]
Moreover, uniformly over deterministic \(\Delta_T\geq0\),
\[
 \frac{1+h_{T,\Delta}}{1+H_T}
 =1-\frac{k_T}{1+H_T}=1+o(1),
 \qquad
 B_{T,\Delta}=O((1+H_T)^{-\beta}).
 \tag{12}
\]
Equations (4)--(12) show that the length of either active branch on \(E_T\) is bounded by a constant multiple of
\[
 r_{T,\Delta}=\max\{T^{-q},(1+H_T)^{-\beta}\}.
 \tag{13}
\]
On \(E_T^c\), the interval is \([-2,2]\), and (3) yields
\[
 4P(E_T^c)\leq16T^{-4}=o(T^{-q})=o(r_{T,\Delta}).
 \tag{14}
\]
Taking expectations in (13)--(14) proves the asserted uniform upper bound using only the two allocation floors.

For the sampling converse, retain the single policy (2), set both delays to zero, and set \(Y_t(0)=0\).  Under \(P_+\) and \(P_-\), let \(Y_t(1)\in\{-1,1\}\) have means \(d_T\) and \(-d_T\), respectively, where
\[
 Q_T=\sum_{t=1}^T p_t,
 \qquad d_T=\lambda Q_T^{-1/2},
 \qquad 0<\lambda<\frac{1-2\eta}{2\sqrt2}.
 \tag{15}
\]
These prompt-delay laws satisfy all support and tail conditions, and integral comparison gives \(Q_T\asymp T^{1-\alpha}\), hence \(d_T\asymp T^{-q}\).  Their causal contrasts are \(\tau_+=d_T\) and \(\tau_-=-d_T\).  Conditional on the assignment at time \(t\), the one-step Hellinger affinity is one for arm zero and \(\sqrt{1-d_T^2}\) for arm one.  Independence over arrivals and the fixed policy therefore give the finite-sample identity
\[
 \operatorname{Aff}(P_+^O,P_-^O)
 =\prod_{t=1}^T\left[1-p_t\{1-\sqrt{1-d_T^2}\}\right]
 \geq1-Q_Td_T^2=1-\lambda^2.
 \tag{16}
\]
For completeness, if \(p\) and \(q\) are densities of two laws, Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \|P-Q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 &=\frac12\int|\sqrt p-\sqrt q|(\sqrt p+\sqrt q)\\
 &\leq\frac12
 \left\{\int(\sqrt p-\sqrt q)^2
             \int(\sqrt p+\sqrt q)^2\right\}^{1/2}
 =\sqrt{1-\operatorname{Aff}(P,Q)^2}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{17}
\]
Combining (16)--(17),
\[
 \|P_+^O-P_-^O\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq\sqrt2\lambda.
 \tag{18}
\]
Let \(J_{T,\Delta}\) be any interval satisfying the stated uniform-honesty premise.  Transfer the event \(\{\tau_-\in J_{T,\Delta}\}\) from \(P_-^O\) to \(P_+^O\) by (18), then apply the union bound under \(P_+^O\).  This yields
\[
 P_+^O\{\tau_+,\tau_-\in J_{T,\Delta}\}
 \geq1-2\eta-o(1)-\sqrt2\lambda>0
 \tag{19}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(T\).  On the event in (19), the interval length is at least \(2d_T\); hence for a constant \(c_{\eta,1}>0\),
\[
 \sup_{(P,\Pi,T)\in\mathfrak M_T^{\mathrm{low}}}
 \mathbb E_{P,\Pi}|J_{T,\Delta}|
 \geq c_{\eta,1}T^{-q}.
 \tag{20}
\]

For the hidden-tail converse, retain the same policy (2) and set
\[
 \delta_T=\min\{1/4,L(1+H_T)^{-\beta}\}.
 \tag{21}
\]
Independently over arrivals and arms, draw a Bernoulli indicator of probability \(\delta_T\).  On failure set \((Y_t(a),D_t(a))=(0,0)\).  On success set \(D_t(a)=H_T+1\); under \(P_+\), set the successful arm-one outcome to \(+1\) and the successful arm-zero outcome to \(-1\), and reverse both signs under \(P_-\).  Outcomes are bounded, delays are finite, and for every integer \(u\geq0\),
\[
 P_\pm\{D_t(a)>u\}
 =\begin{cases}
 \delta_T,&0\leq u\leq H_T,\\
 0,&u\geq H_T+1,
 \end{cases}
 \leq\min\{1,L(1+u)^{-\beta}\}.
 \tag{22}
\]
Thus both laws belong to the one-sided class.  Every nonzero atom is hidden until after \(H_T\); conditional on each of the \(2^T\) assignment sequences, the observed zero records and missingness indicators have the same law under the two signs.  Consequently the complete observed laws coincide, while
\[
 \tau_+=2\delta_T,
 \qquad \tau_-=-2\delta_T.
 \tag{23}
\]
Honesty under their common observed law gives
\[
 P\{\tau_+,\tau_-\in J_{T,\Delta}\}
 \geq1-2\eta-o(1).
 \tag{24}
\]
On this event the length is at least \(4\delta_T\).  Since \(H_T\geq T\to\infty\), the second term in (21) is eventually active, and for a constant \(c_{\eta,2}>0\),
\[
 \sup_{(P,\Pi,T)\in\mathfrak M_T^{\mathrm{low}}}
 \mathbb E_{P,\Pi}|J_{T,\Delta}|
 \geq c_{\eta,2}(1+H_T)^{-\beta}.
 \tag{25}
\]
Equations (16) and (22)--(24) are finite-sample calculations and do not condition on quota success.  In the smallest nontrivial assignment experiment, \(T=2\), (16) is the product over the four assignment sequences, while (22)--(24) hold separately on each sequence; this exact enumeration rules out a hidden conditioning or adaptive-stopping step in either converse.  Combining (20) and (25) proves
\[
 \sup_{\mathfrak M_T^{\mathrm{low}}}\mathbb E|J_{T,\Delta}|
 \geq\min\{c_{\eta,1},c_{\eta,2}\}
 \max\{T^{-q},(1+H_T)^{-\beta}\}.
 \tag{26}
\]
Together with the upper bound, (26) proves the exact one-sided class-minimax order.  At \(\Delta_T=0\), \(H_T=T\), so this order is \(\max\{T^{-(1-\alpha)/2},T^{-\beta}\}\).

We finally record the theorem-level comparison required for its interpretation.  The source statement \(\texttt{prop:shi-delayed-inference-scope}\) records that Shi, Wang, and Wu \cite{ShiWangWu2023DelayedInference} assume independent and identically distributed potential-outcome vectors independent of contemporaneous action and delay, stationary arm-specific delay laws, \((2+\delta)\)-moments, and adaptive-weight conditions; their Theorems 4.1--4.3 prove DAIPW consistency, asymptotic normality, and variance consistency.  Their Corollary 4.4 verifies those conditions under a one-sided allocation floor, positive prompt mass, and a polynomial finite-delay tail with exponent at least one half, with a nonzero-margin relaxation in Remark 4.5.  It does not state uniform honesty or a minimax expected-length theorem.  In the present class, boundedness is stronger than their moment condition, and \(\texttt{ass:polynomial-delay-envelope}\) excludes infinite delays because
\[
 P\{D_t(a)=\infty\}
 \leq P\{D_t(a)>u\}
 \leq L(1+u)^{-\beta}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{27}
\]
Conversely, the present class permits arbitrary within-arm outcome--delay dependence and every \(\beta>0\), both beyond their global independence condition and their displayed Corollary 4.4 regime.  Thus neither full class contains the other.  Only the design-side floor is imported here: the prompt construction (15) lies in their outcome--delay-independence submodel, whereas the hidden-sign construction (21)--(23) deliberately makes the outcome sign depend on whether the delay is hidden and therefore does not.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The upper construction uses only lower-envelope quotas, while honesty over the larger class restricts to the fixed-policy hard experiments for the converse. Shi, Wang, and Wu supply a related one-sided design floor and delayed DAIPW asymptotics under a distinct independence-and-moment model, not uniform honesty or minimax expected length. \textit{Gap.} No prior cited result pairs that one-sided design floor with an informative outcome-dependent-delay same-experiment lower bound for uniformly honest interval length. The full classes are incomparable: this note is stricter on boundedness and finite delays but broader on within-arm outcome--delay dependence and tail exponents. \textit{Consumer.} The theorem turns logged rare- and control-arm floors plus an endpoint-tail envelope into the exact order of the shortest uniformly defensible fixed-horizon interval; it is not an anytime-valid guarantee.

\begin{proposition}[prop:one-sided-clinical-followup-rule]\label{prop:one-sided-clinical-followup-rule}
Liberali et al. \cite{LiberaliEtAl2025RTAR} report retrospective, empirically grounded resampling studies of GUSTO-1 and EUROPA: patients are drawn with replacement from original randomized-trial arm pools under a stationarity/exchangeability benchmark, and the displayed uncertainty intervals are the empirical \(2.5\%\) and \(97.5\%\) quantiles across \(200\) replicates.  GUSTO-1 contributes three analyzed arms and a death-or-survival endpoint at \(30\) days; EUROPA contributes two arms and a composite cardiovascular endpoint, with a reported \(4.2\)-year mean time to the last observation.  Their baseline real-time rule makes daily Gittins-index assignments, whereas their eta-variant mixes in equal randomization only for arms that have not reached fixed minimum counts.  Those published percentile intervals are neither \(I_{T,\Delta}^{\mathrm W}\) nor \(I_{T,\Delta}\).  Consequently, this note implies no coverage or tail-padding claim for the reported intervals.  The published allocation description certifies neither the permanent floors \(\pi_t(1)\geq ct^{-\alpha}\) and \(\pi_t(0)\geq\varepsilon\) nor a known polynomial envelope with parameters \((\beta,L)\); the reported EUROPA mean time to the last observation is not such a tail envelope.  Hence no numerical EUROPA tail pad follows.  For GUSTO-1 only, if complete \(30\)-day ascertainment and this note's single-reveal encoding hold, observation for at least \(30\) days after the final randomization makes \(D_t(a)\leq30\) for every realized assigned endpoint and therefore makes \(b_{T,\Delta}=0\) exactly; that fact alone does not establish adaptive-Wald validity.  Finally, consider instead a prospective two-arm Liberali-style experiment modified to maintain the two permanent logged floors, target the risk difference \(\tau=P Y_t(1)-P Y_t(0)\), use this note's interval, and certify the stated independent-arrival, bounded-outcome, sequential-randomization, and polynomial-tail conditions.  Then \(I_{T,\Delta}\) is uniformly asymptotically honest over \(\mathfrak M_T^{\mathrm{low}}(\alpha,\beta,L,c,\varepsilon)\); on the nonempty regular subclass \(\mathfrak M_{T,\Delta}^{\mathrm{reg}}(\kappa)\), ordinary Wald is uniformly asymptotically honest if and only if
\[
 T^q(1+T+\Delta_T)^{-\beta}\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad q=(1-\alpha)/2.
\]
Thus no extra follow-up is needed when \(\beta>q\); when \(\beta=q\), one needs \(\Delta_T/T\to\infty\); and when \(\beta<q\), one needs \(T+\Delta_T\gg T^{q/\beta}\).  Transfer to an odds-ratio target or to the three-arm GUSTO-1 design is outside this proposition.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The two source-record propositions supply the factual premises about Liberali et al.  Specifically, \(\texttt{prop:liberali-main-source-record}\) and \(\texttt{prop:liberali-supplement-source-record}\) state that the published analysis is a retrospective resampling experiment: conditional on the arm selected by the simulated real-time rule, a patient is drawn with replacement from the corresponding original-trial arm pool; the experiment is repeated for \(200\) replicates; and the reported lower and upper uncertainty limits are the empirical \(2.5\%\) and \(97.5\%\) replicate quantiles.  The same records state that GUSTO-1 contributes three analyzed arms and a death-or-survival endpoint at \(30\) days, whereas EUROPA contributes two arms and a composite cardiovascular endpoint, with a reported \(4.2\)-year mean time to the last observation.  They also state that the baseline simulated rule makes daily Gittins-index assignments and that equal randomization in the eta-variant applies only while an arm has not reached its fixed minimum count.

These reported percentile intervals are different inferential maps from both intervals defined in this note.  Indeed, by \(\texttt{def:ordinary-wald}\), the ordinary-Wald map applied to one logged experiment is
\[
 I_{T,\Delta}^{\mathrm W}
 =\bigl[\widehat\tau_{T,\Delta}
 \mathbin{\pm}z_{1-\eta/2}\widehat s_{T,\Delta}\bigr]\cap[-2,2],
\tag{1}
\]
with the stated quota-failure convention.  By \(\texttt{def:hybrid-interval}\), \(I_{T,\Delta}\) is instead obtained from the same logged experiment by the observable events \(E_T\) and \(G_{T,\Delta}\), using either a bounded-shift Gaussian radius containing \(B_{T,\Delta}\) or the concentration fallback.  An empirical quantile across \(200\) resampled experiments is neither construction.  Consequently, the coverage conclusions for the maps in \(\texttt{def:ordinary-wald}\) and \(\texttt{def:hybrid-interval}\) imply no coverage or tail-padding conclusion for the percentile intervals reported by Liberali et al.

Nor do the source records certify membership of the published simulations in this note's model class.  The daily Gittins-index description supplies no positive lower bound for every arm at every time.  The eta-variant supplies equal randomization only until fixed armwise minimum counts have been reached and hence supplies no permanent lower bound thereafter.  Thus the source records do not establish either of the two conditions
\[
 \pi_t(1)\geq c t^{-\alpha}
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \pi_t(0)\geq\varepsilon
 \qquad (t\leq T),
\tag{2}
\]
which are, respectively, \(\texttt{ass:one-sided-forced-exploration}\) and \(\texttt{ass:control-floor}\).  Likewise, a reported mean time to the last observation is not the collection of armwise inequalities
\[
 P\{D_t(a)>u\}
 \leq \min\{1,L(1+u)^{-\beta}\}
 \qquad
 (a\in\mathcal A,\ u\in\mathbb N_0),
\tag{3}
\]
required by \(\texttt{ass:polynomial-delay-envelope}\).  In particular, the EUROPA mean identifies neither \(L\) nor \(\beta\), so it cannot determine the numerical bias radius in \(\texttt{def:bias-radius}\).

We next prove the limited GUSTO-1 implication.  Impose the conditions stated in the proposition: complete ascertainment of the \(30\)-day endpoint under the note's single-reveal encoding, and terminal observation for at least \(30\) days after the final randomization, so that \(\Delta_T\geq30\).  Complete ascertainment in this encoding is the support statement
\[
 P\{D_t(a)\leq30\}=1
 \qquad(a\in\mathcal A)
\tag{4}
\]
for the encoded assigned endpoint under either arm.  Since \(0<\rho<1\) and \(T\) is a positive integer, \(k_T=\lfloor T^\rho\rfloor\leq T\).  Therefore \(\texttt{def:cohort-schedule}\) gives
\[
 h_{T,\Delta}=T+\Delta_T-k_T\geq\Delta_T\geq30.
\tag{5}
\]
Equations (4)--(5) imply \(\mathbf 1\{D_t(a)>h_{T,\Delta}\}=0\) almost surely for both arms.  Substitution in \(\texttt{def:bias-radius}\) yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 b_{T,\Delta}
 &=P\bigl[Y(1)\mathbf 1\{D(1)>h_{T,\Delta}\}\bigr]
   -P\bigl[Y(0)\mathbf 1\{D(0)>h_{T,\Delta}\}\bigr]\\
 &=0.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
This conclusion uses no independence between the outcome and its delay.  It removes the delayed-tail bias only; neither (4) nor (6) supplies the allocation floors, regular-variance condition, or studentized limit theorem required for adaptive-Wald validity.  Thus complete follow-up alone does not establish coverage of (1).

Finally, consider the prospective two-arm modification described in the proposition.  Its certified independent and identically distributed potential outcome--delay vectors satisfy \(\texttt{ass:iid-arrivals}\); its binary outcomes satisfy \(\texttt{ass:bounded-outcomes}\); its predictable logged randomization satisfies \(\texttt{ass:sequential-randomization}\); its imposed permanent floors are exactly (2); and its certified tail bound is (3).  Hence, by \(\texttt{def:one-sided-policy-class}\), the resulting law and policy belong to
\(\mathfrak M_T^{\mathrm{low}}(\alpha,\beta,L,c,\varepsilon)\).  If \(Y_t(a)\) denotes the binary adverse-event indicator under arm \(a\), its causal risk difference is
\[
 P\{Y_t(1)=1\}-P\{Y_t(0)=1\}
 =P Y_t(1)-P Y_t(0)=\tau,
\tag{7}
\]
so the target is the estimand of this note rather than an odds ratio.  The uniform-honesty conclusion for \(I_{T,\Delta}\), including its unconditional quota-failure convention and concentration fallback, now follows from \(\texttt{thm:uniform-gated-inference}\).

Suppose in addition that \(\texttt{ass:regular-variance}\) holds and that the resulting regular subclass is nonempty.  By \(\texttt{prop:wald-followup-frontier}\), ordinary Wald is uniformly asymptotically honest on that subclass if and only if
\[
 x_T:=T^q(1+T+\Delta_T)^{-\beta}\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad q=(1-\alpha)/2>0.
\tag{8}
\]
The three asserted regimes follow algebraically.  If \(\beta>q\), then \(\Delta_T=0\) gives
\[
 x_T=T^{q-\beta}(1+T^{-1})^{-\beta}\longrightarrow0,
\tag{9}
\]
so no extra post-recruitment follow-up is needed.  If \(\beta=q\), positivity of \(q\) gives
\[
 x_T=\left(\frac{T}{1+T+\Delta_T}\right)^q\longrightarrow0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \frac{\Delta_T}{T}\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{10}
\]
If \(\beta<q\), positivity of \(\beta\) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 x_T\longrightarrow0
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \frac{1+T+\Delta_T}{T^{q/\beta}}\longrightarrow\infty\\
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 T+\Delta_T\gg T^{q/\beta},
 \end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
where the last equivalence uses \(q/\beta>1\), so \(1/T^{q/\beta}\to0\).  Equations (7)--(11) concern only the stipulated prospective two-arm risk-difference protocol after all listed conditions have been certified.  They do not transfer the result to an odds-ratio target or to the three-arm GUSTO-1 design, and (3) confirms why they yield no numerical EUROPA padding recommendation from the published mean alone.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This source audit distinguishes Liberali et al.'s retrospective percentile summaries from this note's inferential maps, proves the limited complete-ascertainment implication, and states the prospective transfer only after every required design, estimand, and tail condition is imposed. \textit{Gap.} The published cardiovascular resampling study does not certify permanent allocation floors, an armwise polynomial delay envelope, or coverage for this note's intervals, so application requires a protocol audit rather than an unconditional tail-pad claim. \textit{Consumer.} Complete 30-day GUSTO-1 ascertainment removes terminal delayed-tail bias after 30 days of final follow-up but does not validate adaptive Wald. A future two-arm risk-difference implementation may use the three-regime rule only after adopting the stated floors, tail certificate, and interval; no numerical EUROPA pad is inferred.

\begin{proposition}[prop:shi-delayed-inference-scope]\label{prop:shi-delayed-inference-scope}
Shi, Wang, and Wu assume independent and identically distributed potential-outcome vectors independent of contemporaneous action and delay, together with stationary arm-specific delay laws.  Their DAIPW consistency, asymptotic-normality, and variance-consistency theorems require adaptive-weight conditions and a finite \((2+\delta)\)-moment.  Their Corollary 4.4 verifies those conditions under a one-sided allocation floor, positive prompt mass, and a polynomial restriction on finite delays with tail exponent at least one half; their Remark 4.5 relaxes the last restriction to an allocation-exponent-plus-tail-exponent condition when the bandit margin is nonzero.  The paper proves neither uniform coverage over the present informative-delay class nor a minimax expected-length order.
\end{proposition}

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The rate-order frontier, uniform gate theorem, and attained bounded-shift calibration are proved, but exact normalized expected-length constants and the globally optimal variable-length or randomized interval remain open. The construction discards assignments beyond deterministic quotas and is not claimed constant-efficient for policies that explore more aggressively. Uniform validity uses a known tail envelope, independent and identically distributed stationary arrival vectors, bounded outcomes, finite delays implied by that envelope, a control-arm floor, and a prespecified terminal time. Unknown envelope parameters, cross-arrival dependence, nonstationary endpoint rates, odds-ratio targets, and multi-arm protocol transfer are outside scope. The result is fixed-horizon rather than anytime-valid. Liberali et al.'s reported empirical intervals and allocation rules do not satisfy the note's assumptions by citation alone, and their EUROPA follow-up summary cannot determine a numerical pad.

\section{Honest scope}

The exact minimax order is over the one-sided forced-exploration class with a control-arm floor. The same fixed policy \(\pi_t(1)=ct^{-\alpha}\) supplies both lower-bound pairs. The sampling converse is compatible with Shi--Wang--Wu outcome--delay independence, but the informative hidden-sign converse is deliberately outside that submodel. On nonempty regular-variance sequences, ordinary Wald is uniformly honest exactly when \(T^q(1+T+\Delta_T)^{-\beta}\to0\), with \(q=(1-\alpha)/2\); this yields the stated three-regime prospective follow-up elbow. The Liberali et al. result here is only a source audit: their published intervals are retrospective empirical replicate percentiles, not this note's intervals; their allocation description and EUROPA follow-up summary do not certify the required floors or tail envelope. Conditional on complete ascertainment and the single-reveal encoding, 30 days after final GUSTO-1 randomization removes its 30-day endpoint's delay bias, but does not prove adaptive-Wald validity. A prospective Liberali-style transfer requires a two-arm risk-difference target, permanent logged floors, this note's interval, and a certified tail envelope; odds-ratio and three-arm transfers remain outside scope.
