%%% FIELD proof-prop-one-sided-exploration-achievability
Fix \(\eta\in(0,1/2)\), the declared class parameters, and the prespecified deterministic sequence \(\Delta_T\geq0\).  Write \(\mathfrak M_T^{\mathrm{low}}=\mathfrak M_T^{\mathrm{low}}(\alpha,\beta,L,c,\varepsilon)\).  The already established \texttt{thm:one-sided-minimax-length-frontier}, applied to the interval \(I_{T,\Delta}\) constructed in \texttt{def:hybrid-interval}, provides a constant \(C_\eta<\infty\) and a deterministic sequence \(\delta_T\to0\) such that
\[
 \inf_{(P,\Pi,T)\in\mathfrak M_T^{\mathrm{low}}}
 P\{\tau\in I_{T,\Delta}\}
 \geq 1-\eta-\delta_T                                                        \tag{1}
\]
and
\[
 \sup_{(P,\Pi,T)\in\mathfrak M_T^{\mathrm{low}}}
 \mathbb E_{P,\Pi}|I_{T,\Delta}|
 \leq C_\eta r_{T,\Delta}.                                                   \tag{2}
\]
Equation \((1)\) is uniform honesty because the infimum ranges over the entire one-sided class and \(\delta_T\) does not depend on \((P,\Pi,T)\).  Equation \((2)\) is the asserted uniform expected-length bound.  By \texttt{def:one-sided-policy-class}, membership in this class requires the rare-arm floor \(\pi_t(1)\geq ct^{-\alpha}\) and the control-arm floor \(\pi_t(0)\geq\varepsilon\), but no rare-arm upper envelope.  Hence the deterministic quotas used by \(I_{T,\Delta}\) depend only on the two lower envelopes and are exactly the quotas named in the proposition.

For completeness, the converse half of \texttt{thm:one-sided-minimax-length-frontier} also supplies a constant \(c_\eta>0\) such that every uniformly honest \(O_{T,\Delta}\)-measurable interval \(J_{T,\Delta}\) satisfies
\[
 \sup_{(P,\Pi,T)\in\mathfrak M_T^{\mathrm{low}}}
 \mathbb E_{P,\Pi}|J_{T,\Delta}|
 \geq c_\eta r_{T,\Delta}                                                    \tag{3}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(T\).  Combining \((2)\) and \((3)\) shows that the constructive upper bound extracted by this proposition attains the exact class-minimax order \(r_{T,\Delta}\).  A particular policy that allocates the rare arm more frequently may have a smaller policy-specific risk or a better constant, but this does not change the minimax order over \(\mathfrak M_T^{\mathrm{low}}\).
