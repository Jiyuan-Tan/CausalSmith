Consider a stationary independent binary-endpoint experiment with predictable sequential randomization, logged propensities satisfying \(\pi_t(1)\geq ct^{-\alpha}\) and \(\pi_t(0)\geq\varepsilon\), and potential delays satisfying \(P\{D_t(a)>u\}\leq\min\{1,L(1+u)^{-\beta}\}\).  The armwise risk difference is the causal contrast \(\tau=P Y_t(1)-P Y_t(0)\).  From records observed through \(H_T=T+\Delta_T\), compute \(\widehat\tau_{T,\Delta}\) and its intervals as defined above.  Uniform asymptotic honesty of the unpadded ordinary-Wald interval \(I_{T,\Delta}^{\mathrm W}\) over the one-sided policy class \(\mathfrak M_T^{\mathrm{low}}(\alpha,\beta,L,c,\varepsilon)\) requires
\[
 T^q(1+T+\Delta_T)^{-\beta}\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad q=(1-\alpha)/2.
\]
On the regular subclass \(\mathfrak M_{T,\Delta}^{\mathrm{reg}}(\kappa)\), this condition is also sufficient for ordinary Wald.  Without the regular-variance condition, the uniformly honest choice is the measurable hybrid \(I_{T,\Delta}\), which uses the bias-aware Gaussian interval only on the observable gate \(G_{T,\Delta}\) and otherwise uses the concentration interval; with the same quota-failure convention, the concentration interval may instead be used alone.  With no extra follow-up, the displayed condition holds when \(\beta>q\).  When \(\beta=q\), it holds if and only if \(\Delta_T/T\to\infty\); when \(\beta<q\), it holds if and only if \(T+\Delta_T\gg T^{q/\beta}\).  Necessity is class-wide rather than pointwise: prompt-delay laws with \(b_{T,\Delta}=0\) may admit ordinary-Wald inference.  The hidden-tail witness used for the tail necessity deliberately permits outcome--delay dependence, whereas the separate sampling-rate converse is compatible with outcome--delay independence.

%%% FIELD clinical-proof
Because a binary endpoint satisfies \(Y_t(a)\in\{0,1\}\subset[-1,1]\), the support condition holds.  The stationary independent sampling and predictable sequential-randomization conditions in the statement are exactly the corresponding sampling and design assumptions.  Together with
\[
 \pi_t(1)\geq ct^{-\alpha},\qquad \pi_t(0)\geq\varepsilon,
 \qquad
 P\{D_t(a)>u\}\leq\min\{1,L(1+u)^{-\beta}\},
 \tag{1}
\]
the definition of \(\mathfrak M_T^{\mathrm{low}}(\alpha,\beta,L,c,\varepsilon)\) therefore puts the experiment directly in the one-sided policy class.  No upper bound on \(\pi_t(1)\) is used in this inclusion.  Moreover, for a binary endpoint,
\[
 P Y_t(1)-P Y_t(0)
 =P\{Y_t(1)=1\}-P\{Y_t(0)=1\}=\tau,
 \tag{2}
\]
so the reported risk difference is the stated causal contrast, rather than merely an observed-law regression functional.

The estimator and all three interval branches are measurable functions of \(O_{T,\Delta}\).  Indeed, assignments determine tape order and the quota event \(E_T\); for each recruited unit, the recorded revelation time determines whether its response contributes to \(X_{a,j}^{\Delta}\) or \(R_{a,j}^{\Delta}\); and the definitions prescribe zero for an absent response and \([-2,2]\) on quota failure.  Thus this construction neither conditions on quota success nor requires a limit for \(t^\alpha\pi_t(1)\).

For completeness, the concentration-only recommendation follows directly from the fixed-tape representation.  Write \(K_a=K_a(n_T)\), \(J_a=K_a(k_T)\), and let \(\widetilde\tau_{T,\Delta}\) denote the unconditional virtual estimator formed from these fixed tape prefixes, before applying the quota-failure convention.  If \(Z\) is centered and lies in an interval of length \(r\), convexity of the exponential gives
\[
 E e^{\lambda Z}\leq \exp(\lambda^2r^2/8).
 \tag{3}
\]
To verify (3), bound \(e^{\lambda z}\) by the chord joining its endpoint values, take expectations using \(EZ=0\), and differentiate the logarithm of the resulting two-point expression twice; its second derivative is at most \(r^2/4\), while its value and first derivative at zero are zero.  Applied to the independent tape variables with coefficients \(K_1^{-1}\) and \(-K_0^{-1}\), (3) yields
\[
 P\!\left(\left|(M_{1,\Delta}-EM_{1,\Delta})-(M_{0,\Delta}-EM_{0,\Delta})\right|>
 \sqrt{2S_Tx}\right)\leq2e^{-x},
 \quad S_T=K_1^{-1}+K_0^{-1}.
 \tag{4}
\]
For \(R=Y\mathbf 1\{g_{T,\Delta}<D\leq h_{T,\Delta}\}\), boundedness and the delay envelope give
\[
 |R|\leq1,\qquad E R^2\leq P\{D>g_{T,\Delta}\}\leq v_{T,\Delta}.
 \tag{5}
\]
If \(Z=R-ER\), then \(|Z|\leq2\), and expansion of the exponential, the inequalities \(|EZ^j|\leq2^{j-2}EZ^2\) for \(j\geq2\) and \(j!\geq2\,3^{j-2}\), and \(1+y\leq e^y\) show, for \(0\leq\lambda<3/2\),
\[
 E e^{\lambda Z}
 \leq \exp\!\left\{\frac{\lambda^2EZ^2}{2(1-2\lambda/3)}\right\}.
 \tag{6}
\]
Chernoff's inequality applied to (6), first with \(\lambda\) and then with \(-\lambda\), gives for each arm
\[
 P\!\left(\left|U_{a,\Delta}-EU_{a,\Delta}\right|>
 \sqrt{\frac{2v_{T,\Delta}x}{J_a}}+\frac{4x}{3J_a}\right)\leq2e^{-x}.
 \tag{7}
\]
The linear term in (7) is weakly larger than the optimized Bernstein term, so the displayed inequality follows from (6).  By the arm-tape equivalence, the samples used above are independent fixed prefixes even when outcome and delay are dependent.  The old-cohort correction identity gives
\[
 E\widetilde\tau_{T,\Delta}=\tau-b_{T,\Delta},
 \qquad |b_{T,\Delta}|\leq B_{T,\Delta}.
 \tag{8}
\]
On \(E_T\), the observed estimator equals \(\widetilde\tau_{T,\Delta}\).  Combining (4), the two instances of (7), and (8), and taking \(x=x_\eta=\log(6/\eta)\), a union bound therefore gives
\[
 P\{\tau\notin I_{T,\Delta}^{\mathrm{conc}}\text{ on }E_T\}
 \leq 2e^{-x_\eta}+2e^{-x_\eta}+2e^{-x_\eta}=\eta.
 \tag{9}
\]
On \(E_T^c\) the prescribed interval is \([-2,2]\), which contains \(\tau\) by (2).  Hence the concentration fallback is honest without conditioning on \(E_T\).  The uniform gated-inference theorem additionally proves uniform honesty of the hybrid interval and, on \(\mathfrak M_{T,\Delta}^{\mathrm{reg}}(\kappa)\), the uniform studentized approximation and consistency of \(\widehat s_{T,\Delta}\).

We next verify that the necessity invoked by a binary-endpoint protocol is not an artifact of outcomes taking values in \([-1,1]\).  Let
\[
 x_T=T^q(1+H_T)^{-\beta},\qquad
 \delta_T=\min\left\{\frac14,\frac L2(1+H_T)^{-\beta}\right\},
 \tag{10}
\]
and use the deterministic policy
\[
 \pi_t(1)=ct^{-\alpha},\qquad \pi_t(0)=1-ct^{-\alpha}\geq1-c\geq\varepsilon.
 \tag{11}
\]
For a sign \(\sigma\in\{-1,1\}\), put \((r_1^\sigma,r_0^\sigma)=(1,0)\) when \(\sigma=1\) and \((r_1^\sigma,r_0^\sigma)=(0,1)\) when \(\sigma=-1\).  Independently over arrivals, define the arm-\(a\) law by
\[
 (D(a),Y(a))=
 \begin{cases}
 (H_T+1,r_a^\sigma),&\text{with probability }\delta_T,\\
 (0,Z_a^\sigma),&\text{with probability }1-\delta_T,
 \end{cases}
 \quad
 Z_a^\sigma\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}\!\left(
 \frac{1/2-\delta_T r_a^\sigma}{1-\delta_T}\right).
 \tag{12}
\]
Since \(0<\delta_T\leq1/4\), both Bernoulli probabilities in (12) belong to \([0,1]\).  For \(0\leq u\leq H_T\),
\[
 P\{D(a)>u\}=\delta_T
 \leq \frac L2(1+H_T)^{-\beta}
 \leq L(1+u)^{-\beta},
 \tag{13}
\]
and for \(u\geq H_T+1\) this probability is zero.  Thus (12) obeys the polynomial delay envelope.  Direct calculation gives
\[
 E Y(a)=\delta_T r_a^\sigma+(1-\delta_T)
 \frac{1/2-\delta_T r_a^\sigma}{1-\delta_T}=\frac12,
 \qquad \operatorname{Var}\{Y(a)\}=\frac14.
 \tag{14}
\]
Consequently \(\tau=0\), whereas the response visible by \(H_T\) has mean \(1/2-\delta_T r_a^\sigma\).  Because the delayed component in (12) has delay \(H_T+1>h_{T,\Delta}\), it enters neither cohort correction, and therefore
\[
 b_{T,\Delta}=\delta_T(r_1^\sigma-r_0^\sigma)=\sigma\delta_T.
 \tag{15}
\]
This is a finite-delay, binary-outcome member of the same one-sided experiment.  It deliberately makes delay informative through (12).

The deterministic quota formula and \(n_T/T\to1\) imply
\[
 K_1(n_T)\sim\frac{c}{1-\alpha}T^{1-\alpha},
 \qquad K_0(n_T)\sim\varepsilon T.
 \tag{16}
\]
Indeed, \(\sum_{t\leq n_T}t^{-\alpha}\sim n_T^{1-\alpha}/(1-\alpha)\), and the square-root subtraction in the quota is \(o(Q_a(n_T))\).  Under (12), \(P\{X_{a,1}^{\Delta}=1\}=1/2-\delta_T r_a^\sigma\to1/2\), so (16) gives, for a constant \(\gamma\in(0,\infty)\),
\[
 T^q s_{T,\Delta}\longrightarrow\gamma,
 \quad
 \gamma^2=
 \begin{cases}
 (1-\alpha)/(4c),&\alpha>0,\\
 (c^{-1}+\varepsilon^{-1})/4,&\alpha=0.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{17}
\]
Since \(H_T\geq T\to\infty\), the second term in the minimum in (10) is eventually active.  Hence
\[
 \frac{|b_{T,\Delta}|}{s_{T,\Delta}}
 =\frac{L}{2\gamma}x_T\{1+o(1)\}.
 \tag{18}
\]
The family in (12) belongs to the regular subclass with variance-floor parameter \(\kappa_0=1/4\) when \(\alpha>0\), and with \(\kappa_0=1/2\) when \(\alpha=0\), by (14).  The uniform studentized theorem applied with this fixed feasible \(\kappa_0\), together with (15), yields
\[
 \frac{\widehat\tau_{T,\Delta}-\tau}{\widehat s_{T,\Delta}}
 \ \Rightarrow\ N(-\sigma u,1)
 \tag{19}
\]
along every subsequence on which the right-hand side of (18) tends to \(u\in(0,\infty)\); if it diverges, the absolute shift diverges in probability.  For \(z=z_{1-\eta/2}\), the limiting coverage in the finite-shift case is
\[
 \Phi(z-u)+\Phi(z+u)-1<2\Phi(z)-1=1-\eta,
 \tag{20}
\]
where strictness follows because the derivative in \(u>0\) is \(\phi(z+u)-\phi(z-u)<0\).  In the divergent-shift case the coverage tends to zero.  If \(x_T\not\to0\), it has either a subsequence bounded away from zero with a further finite positive limit, or an unbounded subsequence; (19)--(20) then disprove uniform honesty of ordinary Wald.  This establishes the asserted necessary condition on the full one-sided policy class.  The ordinary-Wald follow-up proposition supplies the converse implication on \(\mathfrak M_{T,\Delta}^{\mathrm{reg}}(\kappa)\): when \(x_T\to0\), ordinary Wald is uniformly asymptotically honest.

It remains only to solve the deterministic follow-up inequality.  Since \(q=(1-\alpha)/2>0\), when \(\Delta_T=0\) and \(\beta>q\),
\[
 T^q(1+T)^{-\beta}\sim T^{q-\beta}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{21}
\]
When \(\beta=q\),
\[
 T^q(1+T+\Delta_T)^{-q}\longrightarrow0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \frac{1+T+\Delta_T}{T}\longrightarrow\infty
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \frac{\Delta_T}{T}\longrightarrow\infty.
 \tag{22}
\]
When \(\beta<q\), positivity permits taking the \(\beta\)-th root, and \(q/\beta>1\), so
\[
 T^q(1+T+\Delta_T)^{-\beta}\longrightarrow0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \frac{1+T+\Delta_T}{T^{q/\beta}}\longrightarrow\infty
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 T+\Delta_T\gg T^{q/\beta}.
 \tag{23}
\]
Finally, a prompt-delay law has \(D(a)=0\) and hence \(b_{T,\Delta}=0\); the hidden-tail shift in (18) is then absent.  Thus the necessity above is a uniform class statement and does not assert failure at every law.  The sampling component of the one-sided minimax-length theorem uses prompt delays and is therefore compatible with outcome--delay independence; only the hidden-tail construction (12), which proves the tail obstruction relevant here, uses outcome--delay dependence.
