Fix \(E,E'\in\mathcal R(C)\).  We first construct an exact rational representation of \(g_E\); the same construction applies to \(E'\).

For a vertex \(j\) having nonempty parent set \(P_{Ej}\), write
\[
 \Delta_{Ej}(\Sigma)=\det\Gamma_{Ej}(\Sigma),
 \qquad
 \Gamma_{Ej}(\Sigma)=A_{Ej}^{\mathsf T}
 \Sigma_{P_{Ej},P_{Ej}}A_{Ej}.
\]
The columns of \(A_{Ej}\) are linearly independent.  Indeed, if \(A_{Ej}u=0\), then, for a row indexed by any \(i\in B\in\Pi^E_j\), that row equals the coordinate \(u_B\); since every block is nonempty, every coordinate of \(u\) is zero.  Because \(\Sigma\in\mathbb S^p_{++}\), every principal submatrix \(\Sigma_{P_{Ej},P_{Ej}}\) is positive definite.  Consequently, for every nonzero \(u\),
\[
 u^{\mathsf T}\Gamma_{Ej}(\Sigma)u
 =(A_{Ej}u)^{\mathsf T}
   \Sigma_{P_{Ej},P_{Ej}}(A_{Ej}u)>0.
\]
Thus \(\Gamma_{Ej}(\Sigma)\) is positive definite and
\(\Delta_{Ej}(\Sigma)>0\) throughout \(\mathbb S^p_{++}\).  This proves, rather than assumes, the condition needed for division below.

By \(\texttt{def:off-model-block-ls}\), the constrained coefficient vector is
\[
 \beta_{Ej}(\Sigma)
 =\Gamma_{Ej}(\Sigma)^{-1}h_{Ej}(\Sigma)
 =\frac{\operatorname{adj}(\Gamma_{Ej}(\Sigma))
              h_{Ej}(\Sigma)}{\Delta_{Ej}(\Sigma)}.
\]
Every entry of \(\Gamma_{Ej}\) and \(h_{Ej}\) is a polynomial, in fact a linear polynomial, in the \(p(p+1)/2\) free symmetric entries of \(\Sigma\).  The adjugate formula therefore shows that every entry of row \(j\) of \(L_E(\Sigma)\) is a rational function with polynomial numerator and, when the parent set is nonempty, denominator \(\Delta_{Ej}\).  An empty-parent row is identically zero and introduces no denominator.

Since the nonzero pattern of \(L_E(\Sigma)\) is contained in the edge set of the acyclic graph \(G_E\), a topological permutation makes \(L_E(\Sigma)\) strictly triangular.  Hence \(L_E(\Sigma)^p=0\), and direct multiplication gives
\[
 (I_p-L_E(\Sigma))
 \left(I_p+L_E(\Sigma)+\cdots+L_E(\Sigma)^{p-1}\right)
 =I_p-L_E(\Sigma)^p=I_p.
\]
Thus
\[
 (I_p-L_E(\Sigma))^{-1}
 =I_p+L_E(\Sigma)+\cdots+L_E(\Sigma)^{p-1}.
\]
It follows from \(\texttt{def:component-effect-set}\) that each reported coordinate \(g_{E,k}\) has an exactly computable representation
\[
 g_{E,k}(\Sigma)=\frac{P_{E,k}(\Sigma)}{Q_{E,k}(\Sigma)},
\]
where \(P_{E,k}\) and \(Q_{E,k}\) are polynomials and \(Q_{E,k}\) is a finite product, allowing repeated factors, of the determinants \(\Delta_{Ej}\).  Therefore \(Q_{E,k}(\Sigma)>0\) on \(\mathbb S^p_{++}\).  All additions and multiplications involved are finite because \(p\) and \(\mathcal Q\) are finite.  Applying the same argument to \(E'\), and cross-multiplying, yields
\[
 g_{E,k}(\Sigma)-g_{E',k}(\Sigma)
 =\frac{N_k(\Sigma)}{Q_{E,k}(\Sigma)Q_{E',k}(\Sigma)},
\]
where
\[
 N_k=P_{E,k}Q_{E',k}-P_{E',k}Q_{E,k}
\]
is a polynomial and the displayed denominator is strictly positive on the positive-definite cone.  Hence
\[
 g_{E,k}(\Sigma)=g_{E',k}(\Sigma)
 \text{ for every }\Sigma\in\mathbb S^p_{++}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 N_k(\Sigma)=0
 \text{ for every }\Sigma\in\mathbb S^p_{++}.
\]

We now justify the polynomial-identity step.  Regard a symmetric matrix as a vector in \(\mathbb R^d\), where \(d=p(p+1)/2\).  The cone \(\mathbb S^p_{++}\) is nonempty, since it contains \(I_p\), and open: if \(\Sigma\) is positive definite, continuity of the quadratic form on the unit sphere gives \(c=\min_{\lVert x\rVert=1}x^{\mathsf T}\Sigma x>0\), and every symmetric perturbation \(H\) with operator norm less than \(c\) satisfies \(x^{\mathsf T}(\Sigma+H)x>0\) for every nonzero \(x\).  A polynomial on \(\mathbb R^d\) that vanishes on a nonempty open set is the zero polynomial.  For completeness, choose an open box inside that set and induct on \(d\).  The case \(d=1\) is the elementary fact that a nonzero univariate polynomial has only finitely many roots.  For the induction step, expand in the last variable.  Fixing the first \(d-1\) variables in the corresponding open box gives a univariate polynomial vanishing on an interval, so each coefficient polynomial vanishes throughout an open box in \(\mathbb R^{d-1}\); the induction hypothesis makes every coefficient identically zero.  Applying this fact to \(N_k\) proves
\[
 N_k(\Sigma)=0
 \text{ for every }\Sigma\in\mathbb S^p_{++}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \text{every monomial coefficient of }N_k\text{ is zero}.
\]

This gives the promised deterministic exact finite procedure.  For every reported coordinate, form the block Gram matrices and score vectors symbolically, use adjugates to form the rational entries of \(L_E\) and \(L_{E'}\), evaluate the finite nilpotent sums, cross-multiply their positive determinant products, expand each \(N_k\) in the standard monomial basis, and test its finitely many exact coefficients.  The maps \(g_E\) and \(g_{E'}\) are identical exactly when all of these coefficient tables are zero.  The expanded tables, together with the displayed symbolic construction, are checkable exact identity certificates; a nonzero listed coefficient certifies nonidentity.  Mere agreement at a finite list of numerical covariance matrices does not imply the required polynomial identity: without an additional proved interpolation argument, a nonzero polynomial can vanish at every point of any prescribed finite list.  No bound polynomial in a compact arithmetic-circuit encoding is asserted.
