By \(\texttt{lem:blocked-model-geometry}\), the identity covariance belongs to both models and
\[
 T_C^{\circ}=\operatorname{span}\{v_{jB}:j\in V,\ B\in\Pi_j\},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{supp}(v_{jB})=A_{jB}:=\{\{i,j\}:i\in B\}.
\]
Every unordered edge of \(G\) lies in exactly one set \(A_{jB}\): its orientation supplies its unique head \(j\), and the partition \(\Pi_j\) supplies the unique block \(B\) containing its tail. Therefore the nonempty supports \(A_{jB}\) are pairwise disjoint. If
\[
 w=\sum_{j\in V}\sum_{B\in\Pi_j}a_{jB}v_{jB}\neq0,
\]
then each coordinate belongs to at most one summand, so no coordinate can cancel and
\[
 \operatorname{supp}(w)=\bigcup_{a_{jB}\neq0}A_{jB}.
\]
It follows in both directions that the inclusion-minimal nonempty supports of vectors in \(T_C^{\circ}\) are precisely the sets \(A_{jB}\): a generator support is minimal because every nonzero vector supported inside it is a nonzero scalar multiple of that generator, and a support involving at least two generators properly contains each participating generator support. Their union is \(\operatorname{skel}(G)\).

Suppose \(\mathcal M(C)=\mathcal M(D)\). These are the same embedded subset of \(\mathbb S^p_{++}\), so their tangent spaces at the common point \(I_p\) agree; projection off the diagonal gives \(T_C^{\circ}=T_D^{\circ}\). Hence the two collections of minimal supports just characterized agree. A member of that collection having at least two edges is a labelled star with a unique common endpoint: if \(\{\{i,j\}:i\in B\}\) has at least two distinct edges, their only common vertex is \(j\). Thus its center recovers the target \(j\), and its other endpoints recover the labelled block \(B\). Such supports recover exactly
\[
 \mathcal N_j(C)=\{B\in\Pi_j:|B|\geq2\}
\]
at every target. A one-edge support has two possible centers and recovers only its underlying unordered edge. Applying the reconstruction to \(D\) yields
\[
 \operatorname{skel}(G)=\operatorname{skel}(H),
 \qquad
 \mathcal N_j(C)=\mathcal N_j(D)\quad(j\in V).
\]
Finally suppose \(i\to j\) in \(G\) and \(j\to i\) in \(H\). If the block of \(i\) in \(\Pi_j\) had size at least two, it would be a recovered member \(B\in\mathcal N_j(D)\) containing \(i\), which would force \(i\in\operatorname{pa}_H(j)\), contrary to \(j\to i\). Hence that block is \(\{i\}\). The same argument with \(C,D\) and \(i,j\) interchanged shows that the reversed edge occupies the singleton block \(\{j\}\) at target \(i\) in \(D\).

%%% FIELD proof-reversed-edge-separator
By \(\texttt{lem:tangent-block-recovery}\), the reversed edge is a singleton block in both states and
\[
 \mathcal N_j(C)=\mathcal N_j(D)\quad(j\in V).
\]
By \(\texttt{lem:edge-coloured-model-equivalence-markov}\), \(G\) and \(H\) have the same unshielded colliders.

Fix \(S\in\mathcal N_x(C)\) and \(s\in S\). Tangent recovery gives \(S\in\mathcal N_x(D)\), so \(s\to x\) occurs in both graphs. If \(s\) and \(y\) were nonadjacent, then \(s\to x\to y\) in \(G\) would become the unshielded collider \(s\to x\leftarrow y\) in \(H\), contradicting equality of unshielded colliders. Hence \(s\) and \(y\) are adjacent. In \(G\), the orientation \(y\to s\) would create the directed cycle \(y\to s\to x\to y\); acyclicity from \(\texttt{def:blocked-state-class}\) therefore forces \(s\to y\). Since \(s\) was arbitrary,
\[
 S\subseteq\operatorname{pa}_G(y).
\]

Suppose, for a contradiction, that \(S\) is not contained in a single block of \(\Pi_y\). Since \(\Pi_y\) partitions \(\operatorname{pa}_G(y)\), there is a block \(T\in\Pi_y\) with \(T\cap S\neq\varnothing\) and \(S\nsubseteq T\). The block of the arrow \(x\to y\) is \(\{x\}\), so \(x\notin T\). In the model for \(C\), choose every innovation variance equal to one, choose coefficient one on the singleton block \(\{x\}\) at target \(y\), coefficient one on the block \(T\) at target \(y\), and coefficient zero on every other block. These parameters belong to \(\Theta_C\), and \(\texttt{def:covariance-model}\) gives a positive-definite covariance \(\Sigma\in\mathcal M(C)\). All variables other than \(X_y\) are then mutually independent standard Gaussians, and
\[
 X_y=X_x+\sum_{r\in T}X_r+\varepsilon_y,
 \qquad \varepsilon_y\sim N(0,1),
\]
with \(\varepsilon_y\) independent of the other displayed variables.

Put \(P=\operatorname{pa}_H(x)\) and \(Q=P\setminus\{y\}\). Since \(S\in\mathcal N_x(D)\), \(S\subseteq Q\). Projecting \(X_y\) linearly on \(X_Q\) removes exactly the independent summands indexed by \(T\cap Q\); its residual is
\[
 Z=X_x+\sum_{r\in T\setminus Q}X_r+\varepsilon_y,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(Z)=2+|T\setminus Q|.
\]
The residual of \(X_x\) after projection on \(X_Q\) is \(X_x\). Consequently the normal equations, equivalently the Frisch--Waugh residual calculation, give the coefficient on \(X_y\) in the regression of \(X_x\) on \((X_Q,X_y)\) as
\[
 q=\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(X_x,Z)}{\operatorname{Var}(Z)}
   =\frac{1}{2+|T\setminus Q|}>0.
\]
Before adding \(X_y\), the coefficient of every member of \(X_Q\) is zero. The coefficient of \(X_s\) in the projection of \(X_y\) on \(X_Q\) is \(\mathbf 1\{s\in T\}\), because these regressors are mutually independent with unit variance. Substitution into the normal equations therefore yields
\[
 \operatorname{coef}_{X_s}\{X_x\mid X_P\}
 =-q\,\mathbf 1\{s\in T\},\qquad s\in S.
\]
The conditions \(T\cap S\neq\varnothing\) and \(S\nsubseteq T\) make these coefficients take both values \(-q\) and \(0\) on the one block \(S\). But \(S\in\mathcal N_x(D)\), and the parent-regression recovery formula in \(\texttt{lem:blocked-model-geometry}\) says that every covariance in \(\mathcal M(D)\) has equal regression coefficients on every block of \(\Pi^D_x\). Thus \(\Sigma\notin\mathcal M(D)\), contradicting \(\mathcal M(C)=\mathcal M(D)\). We have proved that every \(S\in\mathcal N_x(C)\) is contained in a member of \(\mathcal N_y(C)\); the containing block is nonsingleton because \(|S|\geq2\).

Apply the same proved argument to the reversed arrow \(y\to x\) in \(D\), with \((D,y)\) in place of \((C,x)\). Using targetwise tangent recovery to identify the two states' families, every member of \(\mathcal N_y(C)\) is contained in a member of \(\mathcal N_x(C)\). If \(S\in\mathcal N_x(C)\), choose \(T\in\mathcal N_y(C)\) and \(U\in\mathcal N_x(C)\) with \(S\subseteq T\subseteq U\). The blocks \(S,U\) belong to the same partition and intersect, so \(S=U\), whence \(S=T=U\). Reversing the roles gives the converse membership, and therefore
\[
 \mathcal N_x(C)=\mathcal N_y(C).
\]

%%% FIELD proof-complete-blocked-equivalence
Fix \(C=(G,\Pi)\) and \(D=(H,\Lambda)\) in \(\mathfrak S_p\). By \(\texttt{def:blocked-state-class}\), their graphs are finite labelled DAGs and each target family partitions its parent set. By \(\texttt{def:covariance-model}\), the coefficient on every block ranges over all of \(\mathbb R\), each innovation variance lies in \((0,\infty)\), and \(\mathcal M(E)\) is the full positive-definite covariance image of the corresponding linear Gaussian structural model. Thus the structural target is the state modulo \(\mathcal R(C)\); equality of observable covariance-model images is a separate relation whose equivalence to that target is proved below.

First suppose \(\mathcal M(C)=\mathcal M(D)\). The cited result \(\texttt{lem:edge-coloured-model-equivalence-markov}\) gives
\[
 \operatorname{skel}(G)=\operatorname{skel}(H),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{uc}(G)=\operatorname{uc}(H).
\]
The proved tangent-support lemma gives
\[
 \mathcal N_j(C)=\mathcal N_j(D),\qquad j\in V.
\]
If an adjacency \(\{x,y\}\) is oriented oppositely in \(G\) and \(H\), \(\texttt{lem:reversed-edge-separator}\) gives \(\mathcal N_x(C)=\mathcal N_y(C)\). Contrapositively, every adjacency whose endpoint families differ has the same orientation in the two graphs. With the common skeleton and common vertex-indexed families, \(\texttt{def:family-mismatch-edges}\) then gives \(F(C)=F(D)\). All four coordinates in \(\texttt{def:static-signature}\) agree, hence \(\mathsf S(C)=\mathsf S(D)\).

Conversely suppose \(\mathsf S(C)=\mathsf S(D)\). Equality of the first two signature coordinates and \(\texttt{lem:verma-pearl-markov-equivalence}\) show that \(G\) and \(H\) are Markov equivalent. Abbreviate
\[
 \mathcal N_j:=\mathcal N_j(C)=\mathcal N_j(D),\qquad j\in V.
\]
By \(\texttt{lem:chickering-monotone-covered-reversals}\), there is a sequence
\[
 G=G_0,G_1,\ldots,G_r=H,
 \qquad r=\delta(C,D),
\]
of distinct covered-edge reversals, with every intermediate graph an acyclic member of the same Markov-equivalence class. Because the sequence has exactly as many distinct reversals as the number of initially disagreeing edges and ends at \(H\), it reverses each initially disagreeing edge exactly once and no initially agreeing edge.

We lift this sequence by induction. Let \(E_t=(G_t,\Pi^t)\), starting with \(E_0=C\), and maintain
\[
 \mathcal N_k(E_t)=\mathcal N_k\quad(k\in V).
\]
Suppose the next covered arrow is \(i\to j\). Its underlying edge disagrees between \(G\) and \(H\). If \(\mathcal N_i\neq\mathcal N_j\), the orientation of this edge belongs to \(F(C)\), while equality \(F(C)=F(D)\) forces that orientation to agree in \(G\) and \(H\), a contradiction. Hence
\[
 \mathcal N_i=\mathcal N_j=:\mathcal N.
\]
Let \(B\in\Pi^t_j\) be the unique block containing \(i\). If \(|B|\geq2\), then \(B\in\mathcal N_j(E_t)=\mathcal N\), so equality of endpoint families gives \(B\in\mathcal N_i(E_t)\). A member of the latter family is contained in \(\operatorname{pa}_{G_t}(i)\), but \(i\in B\), which would make \(i\) its own parent. A DAG has no self-loop, so \(B=\{i\}\).

Coveredness gives, for \(P=\operatorname{pa}_{G_t}(i)\),
\[
 \operatorname{pa}_{G_t}(j)=P\cup\{i\}.
\]
Put \(U=\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal N}A\). The nonsingleton blocks at both endpoints are exactly \(\mathcal N\). Any \(k\in P\setminus U\) must therefore occupy the singleton block \(\{k\}\) in both endpoint partitions. It follows that
\[
 \Pi^t_i=\mathcal N\cup\{\{k\}:k\in P\setminus U\},
 \qquad
 \Pi^t_j=\Pi^t_i\cup\{\{i\}\}.
\]
These are precisely the three definedness conditions in \(\texttt{def:legal-reversal}\). Applying \(\operatorname{rev}_{i\to j}\) produces \(E_{t+1}\) with graph \(G_{t+1}\), and \(\texttt{lem:legal-reversal-model-invariance}\) preserves every nonsingleton family, completing the induction.

After \(r\) steps the graph is \(H\). Given a parent set \(P_j\) and a compatible nonsingleton family \(\mathcal N_j\), the only possible partition is
\[
 \mathcal N_j\cup
 \left\{\{k\}:k\in P_j\setminus\bigcup_{B\in\mathcal N_j}B\right\}.
\]
The final lifted state and \(D\) have the same graph and the same nonsingleton families, so this formula makes their partitions equal. The final state is \(D\), proving \(D\in\mathcal R(C)\).

Finally suppose \(D\in\mathcal R(C)\). By \(\texttt{def:reversal-component}\), there is a finite legal-reversal path from \(C\) to \(D\). Repeated use of \(\texttt{lem:legal-reversal-model-invariance}\) gives \(\mathcal M(D)=\mathcal M(C)\). This completes the cycle of implications among model equality, static-signature equality, and legal-reversal connectivity.

The lifted Chickering path has \(\delta(C,D)\) steps and reverses every disagreeing edge exactly once. Conversely, a legal move changes the orientation of only one skeleton edge. Each endpoint-disagreeing edge must be reversed an odd, hence positive, number of times along any path, so every path has at least \(\delta(C,D)\) moves. Therefore
\[
 d_{\mathrm{rev}}(C,D)=\delta(C,D).
\]

For later use, if \(B\in\mathcal N_j(C)\) and \(i\in B\), then \(i\to j\) in \(G\). Equality \(\mathcal N_i(C)=\mathcal N_j(C)\) would put the same set \(B\), which contains \(i\), inside \(\operatorname{pa}_G(i)\), again creating a self-parent. Hence
\[
 B\in\mathcal N_j(C),\ i\in B
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \mathcal N_i(C)\neq\mathcal N_j(C)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 i\to j\in F(C).
\]
If no parent block of \(C\) is a singleton, every arrow is covered by the first premise, hence every arrow lies in \(F(C)\) and is fixed by the static criterion. The graph and every nonsingleton family then determine the whole partition, so \(\mathcal R(C)=\{C\}\). This agrees with the cited BPEC conclusion \(\texttt{lem:bpec-proper-identifiability}\) on its proper no-singleton subclass. No coefficient is declared identified merely because its arrow is fixed: the result identifies the graph--partition state modulo the displayed equivalence, whereas numerical coefficients remain parameters of the observational law.

Together with \(\texttt{thm:complete-blocked-equivalence}\), this gives

 \operatorname{Enum}(C)=\mathcal R(C)
 =\{D\in\mathfrak S_p:\mathsf S(D)=\mathsf S(C)\}.

For the static decision bound, compare adjacency matrices in \(O(p^2)\) operations. The lift supplies at most one blocked state per DAG. Sharpness holds for the complete-skeleton all-singleton state. %%% FIELD proof-blocked-model-geometry
Fix \(E\in\mathfrak S_p\). A topological ordering of the acyclic graph \(G_E\) makes \(B_E\) strictly triangular, so \(B_E^p=0\) and
\[
 (I_p-B_E)^{-1}=I_p+B_E+\cdots+B_E^{p-1}.
\]
Thus \(\phi_E\) is polynomial in \((\beta_E,\omega_E)\). Its value is positive definite because it is the congruence of the positive-definite diagonal matrix \(\operatorname{diag}(\omega_E)\) by the invertible matrix \((I_p-B_E)^{-1}\).

Write \(P_j=\operatorname{pa}_{G_E}(j)\). Under the structural equations associated with \(\texttt{def:covariance-model}\),
\[
 X_j=(B_E)_{jP_j}X_{P_j}+\varepsilon_j.
\]
Every member of \(P_j\) precedes \(j\) topologically and is a function of innovations other than \(\varepsilon_j\). The diagonal innovation covariance therefore gives \(\operatorname{Cov}(X_{P_j},\varepsilon_j)=0\). Since every principal submatrix of a positive-definite covariance is positive definite,
\[
 (B_E)_{jP_j}=\Sigma_{jP_j}\Sigma_{P_jP_j}^{-1},
 \qquad
 \omega_{Ej}=\Sigma_{jj}-\Sigma_{jP_j}\Sigma_{P_jP_j}^{-1}\Sigma_{P_jj},
\]
with the usual empty-parent convention. These formulas recover every block coefficient and innovation variance. They are rational and smooth on \(\mathbb S^p_{++}\), because their denominators are positive principal minors. They define a smooth left inverse to \(\phi_E\) on its image. Hence \(\phi_E\) is injective, its differential is injective, and it is a homeomorphism onto its image; it is therefore a smooth embedding. Its dimension is
\[
 d_E=p+\sum_{j\in V}|\Pi^E_j|,
\]
and its image is connected because \(\Theta_E=\mathbb R^{b_E}\times(0,\infty)^p\) is connected.

For arbitrary \(\Sigma\in\mathbb S^p_{++}\), define
\[
 \widetilde B_{jP_j}(\Sigma)=\Sigma_{jP_j}\Sigma_{P_jP_j}^{-1},
 \qquad
 \widetilde B_{ji}(\Sigma)=0\quad(i\notin P_j),
\]
and
\[
 \widetilde\Omega(\Sigma)
 =(I_p-\widetilde B(\Sigma))\Sigma(I_p-\widetilde B(\Sigma))^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Then \(\Sigma\in\mathcal M(E)\) if and only if
\[
 \widetilde B_{ji}(\Sigma)=\widetilde B_{jk}(\Sigma)
 \quad\text{for every }i,k\in B\in\Pi^E_j,
\]
and every off-diagonal entry of \(\widetilde\Omega(\Sigma)\) vanishes. Necessity is the recovery formula. Conversely, the first family assembles \(\widetilde B\) from one coefficient per prescribed block. Its zero pattern is acyclic, so \(I_p-\widetilde B\) is invertible. The second family makes \(\widetilde\Omega\) diagonal, and its diagonal entries are strictly positive because it is an invertible congruence of \(\Sigma\). Therefore
\[
 \Sigma=(I_p-\widetilde B)^{-1}\widetilde\Omega(I_p-\widetilde B)^{-\mathsf T}\in\mathcal M(E).
\]
The displayed conditions are finitely many continuous rational equalities on \(\mathbb S^p_{++}\), proving relative closedness. Their denominators are products of positive principal minors, so clearing them gives equivalent polynomial equalities on this cone.

Finally, \(I_p\) is obtained at \(\beta_E=0\) and \(\omega_E=(1,\ldots,1)\). Differentiation there gives
\[
 d\Sigma=dB_E+dB_E^{\mathsf T}+\operatorname{diag}(d\omega_E).
\]
The derivative in coordinate \(\beta_{jB}\) is one exactly on the off-diagonal coordinates \(\{\{i,j\}:i\in B\}\), whereas innovation-variance derivatives are diagonal. Projection off the diagonal yields
\[
 \operatorname{proj}_{\mathrm{off}}T_{I_p}\mathcal M(E)
 =\operatorname{span}\{v_{jB}:j\in V,\ B\in\Pi^E_j\},
 \qquad
 (v_{jB})_{\{i,j\}}=\mathbf 1\{i\in B\}.
\]

%%% FIELD proof-legal-reversal-model-invariance
Let \(S=\operatorname{pa}_{G_E}(i)\). Definedness in \(\texttt{def:legal-reversal}\) says
\[
 \operatorname{pa}_{G_E}(j)=S\cup\{i\},
 \qquad \{i\}\in\Pi^E_j,
 \qquad \Pi^E_j\setminus\{\{i\}\}=\Pi^E_i.
\]
The reversal preserves acyclicity. Indeed, if replacing \(i\to j\) by \(j\to i\) created a cycle, the old graph would contain a directed path from \(i\) to \(j\) not using \(i\to j\). If \(k\to j\) were the last edge on that path, coveredness would give \(k\in S=\operatorname{pa}_{G_E}(i)\), so the path segment \(i\leadsto k\) followed by \(k\to i\) would already be a directed cycle. The transported families partition the new parent sets. Thus the reversed state \(E'\) belongs to \(\mathfrak S_p\). Its new parent sets and partitions satisfy
\[
 \operatorname{pa}_{G_{E'}}(j)=S,
 \quad \operatorname{pa}_{G_{E'}}(i)=S\cup\{j\},
 \quad \Pi^{E'}_j=\Pi^E_i,
 \quad \Pi^{E'}_i=\Pi^E_i\cup\{\{j\}\},
\]
so the inverse move \(\operatorname{rev}_{j\to i}(E')\) is defined and equals \(E\).

It remains to prove equality of the full covariance images. Conditional on \(X_S\), write the two affected structural equations in \(E\) as
\[
 X_i=a^{\mathsf T}X_S+\varepsilon_i,
 \qquad
 X_j=bX_i+d^{\mathsf T}X_S+\varepsilon_j,
\]
where \(u=\operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_i)>0\) and \(v=\operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_j)>0\). The vectors \(a,d\) are each constant on every block of the common partition \(\Pi^E_i\). Define
\[
 t=v+b^2u>0,
 \qquad q=\frac{bu}{t},
 \qquad \eta_j=b\varepsilon_i+\varepsilon_j,
 \qquad \eta_i=\varepsilon_i-q\eta_j.
\]
Direct substitution gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 X_j&=(d+ba)^{\mathsf T}X_S+\eta_j,\\
 X_i&=qX_j+\{a-q(d+ba)\}^{\mathsf T}X_S+\eta_i.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(\eta_j)=t,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(\eta_i)=\frac{uv}{t}>0,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Cov}(\eta_i,\eta_j)=bu-qt=0.
\]
The pair \((\eta_i,\eta_j)\) is jointly Gaussian, hence its zero covariance implies independence. It is a linear transformation of \((\varepsilon_i,\varepsilon_j)\), so it is also independent of all remaining innovations. Both \(d+ba\) and \(a-q(d+ba)\) are constant on each block of \(\Pi^E_i\), and \(q\) occupies the transported singleton block \(\{j\}\). Thus the transformed equations are admissible for \(E'\), proving \(\mathcal M(E)\subseteq\mathcal M(E')\). Applying the same construction to the inverse legal move gives the reverse inclusion.

Removing singleton blocks from \(\Pi^{E'}_j=\Pi^E_i\) and \(\Pi^{E'}_i=\Pi^E_i\cup\{\{j\}\}\), while leaving every other partition unchanged, preserves the nonsingleton family at every vertex.

%%% FIELD proof-fixed-p-gaussian-covariance-calculus
Let \(Z\) be the \(p\)-by-\(n\) matrix whose columns are \(\Sigma_0^{-1/2}X^{(r)}\). Its entries have a joint Lebesgue density. For \(n\geq p\), failure of row rank \(p\) is the common zero set of all \(p\)-by-\(p\) minors; it has Lebesgue measure zero because at least one such determinant polynomial is nonzero. Hence the data matrix has row rank \(p\) almost surely and
\[
 S_n=n^{-1}XX^{\mathsf T}\in\mathbb S^p_{++}
\]
almost surely.

Differentiating the centered Gaussian moment-generating function \(\exp(t^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_0t/2)\) four times at zero gives
\[
 \mathbb E[X_iX_jX_kX_l]
 =\Sigma_{0,ij}\Sigma_{0,kl}
  +\Sigma_{0,ik}\Sigma_{0,jl}
  +\Sigma_{0,il}\Sigma_{0,jk}.
\]
Subtracting \(\mathbb E[X_iX_j]\mathbb E[X_kX_l]\) proves
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(X_iX_j,X_kX_l)
 =\Sigma_{0,ik}\Sigma_{0,jl}+\Sigma_{0,il}\Sigma_{0,jk}.
\]
Summing the coordinate variances gives \(\mathbb E\|S_n-\Sigma_0\|_F^2=O(n^{-1})\), so \(S_n\to\Sigma_0\) in \(L^2\).

Put \(W_r=\operatorname{vech}(X^{(r)}X^{(r)\mathsf T}-\Sigma_0)\). These vectors are independent and centered, have covariance \(V_0\), and possess finite third moments. For each fixed vector \(t\), Taylor expansion of the characteristic function, with the third-order remainder bounded using \(\mathbb E|t^{\mathsf T}W_1|^3<\infty\), gives
\[
 \mathbb E\exp\{it^{\mathsf T}W_1/\sqrt n\}
 =1-\frac{t^{\mathsf T}V_0t}{2n}+O(n^{-3/2}).
\]
Taking the \(n\)-th power and applying the Cram\'er--Wold criterion yields
\[
 \sqrt n\,\operatorname{vech}(S_n-\Sigma_0)\Longrightarrow N(0,V_0).
\]

For any nonzero symmetric matrix \(A\), the corresponding quadratic-form direction has variance
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(X^{\mathsf T}AX)
 =2\operatorname{tr}\{(\Sigma_0^{1/2}A\Sigma_0^{1/2})^2\}>0.
\]
Thus \(V_0\) is positive definite on nonzero symmetric directions. If \(f\) is continuously differentiable, Taylor's formula gives
\[
 \sqrt n\{f(S_n)-f(\Sigma_0)\}
 =\mathrm Df(\Sigma_0)[\sqrt n(S_n-\Sigma_0)]+o_{\mathbb P}(1).
\]
The preceding multivariate limit gives the delta-method normal law. The formula for \(V_0\) is polynomial in \(\Sigma_0\), so replacing \(\Sigma_0\) by \(S_n\) is consistent. When \(\mathrm Df(\Sigma_0)\neq0\), positive definiteness makes the limiting variance strictly positive, and division by its plug-in standard error gives the studentized standard normal limit.

%%% FIELD proof-canonical-pdag-component-lift
For \(j\in V\), abbreviate
\[
 \mathcal N_j=\mathcal N_j(C),
 \qquad U_j=\bigcup_{B\in\mathcal N_j}B.
\]
If \(B\in\mathcal N_j\) and \(i\in B\), then \(i\to j\) in \(G\). If \(\mathcal N_i=\mathcal N_j\), then \(B\in\mathcal N_i\); but every member of \(\mathcal N_i\) is contained in \(\operatorname{pa}_G(i)\), whereas \(i\in B\), contradicting acyclicity and the absence of self-loops. Therefore
\[
 \mathcal N_i\neq\mathcal N_j,
 \qquad i\to j\in F(C).
\]

The ordinary CPDAG of \(G\) represents exactly the DAGs Markov equivalent to \(G\). The PDAG \(\mathcal P_C\) additionally fixes each orientation in \(F(C)\) as in \(G\); this background knowledge is compatible because \(G\) itself is an extension. Let \(H\) be a consistent DAG extension of \(\mathcal P_C\). The preceding display forces \(i\to j\) in \(H\) for every \(i\in B\in\mathcal N_j\), so
\[
 U_j\subseteq\operatorname{pa}_H(j).
\]
The members of \(\mathcal N_j\) are pairwise disjoint. Hence
\[
 \Lambda^H_j=\mathcal N_j\cup
 \{\{i\}:i\in\operatorname{pa}_H(j)\setminus U_j\}
\]
is a partition of \(\operatorname{pa}_H(j)\). Since \(H\) is acyclic, \(\mathsf L_C(H)=(H,\Lambda^H)\) lies in \(\mathfrak S_p\) and has nonsingleton family \(\mathcal N_j\) at every vertex. It has the skeleton and unshielded colliders of \(G\) and respects all orientations in \(F(C)\); therefore
\[
 \mathsf S(\mathsf L_C(H))=\mathsf S(C).
\]
The equivalence theorem gives \(\mathsf L_C(H)\in\mathcal R(C)\).

Conversely, if \(D=(H,\Lambda)\in\mathcal R(C)\), the equivalence theorem gives \(\mathsf S(D)=\mathsf S(C)\). Thus \(H\) is Markov equivalent to \(G\), respects all background arrows in \(F(C)\), and is a consistent extension of \(\mathcal P_C\). Its nonsingleton family at \(j\) is \(\mathcal N_j\), while every parent outside \(U_j\) must form a singleton block. Consequently \(\Lambda_j=\Lambda^H_j\), so \(D=\mathsf L_C(H)\). The graph coordinate makes the lift injective, and the two directions make it surjective. The displayed formula depends only on \(H\) and the fixed families, proving path independence.

Apply \(\texttt{lem:pdag-consistent-extension-enumeration}\) to \(\mathcal P_C\). Its \(O(p^3)\) initialization includes passage to the equivalent Meek-closed MPDAG, after which it emits every consistent extension exactly once with \(O(p+m)\) delay. The lift scans the \(p\) vertices and \(m\) parent incidences once, reuses the fixed nonsingleton blocks, and appends the singleton remainder blocks. Its time and representation size are \(O(p+m)\), so composition preserves exact-once output and \(O(p+m)\) delay without state memoization.

For a shortest certificate to an emitted \(D=(H,\Lambda)\), let \(K\) be the current DAG. If \(K\neq H\), apply \(\texttt{lem:chickering-monotone-covered-reversals}\) to \(K,H\). Its first reversal shows that some currently disagreeing arrow is covered; scanning at most \(m\) arrows and comparing parent-incidence rows finds one in \(O(mp)\) time. Reverse it and repeat. Each step reduces the disagreement count by one, so there are exactly \(\delta(C,D)\leq m\) steps and total time \(O(m^2p)=O(p^5)\). Every arrow in \(F(C)\) has the same orientation in the current graph and \(H\), so none is reversed. The legality argument in \(\texttt{thm:complete-blocked-equivalence}\), applied to the canonical lift at each step, makes every reversal a defined legal move. The resulting state path has length \(\delta(C,D)\), which that theorem proves minimal.

For the cardinality bound, assign each component DAG its lexicographically first topological order. A fixed skeleton together with a topological order determines all arrow orientations, so this assignment is injective. The lift gives one state per graph, and there are \(p!\) total orders; hence \(|\mathcal R(C)|\leq p!\). For sharpness take a complete skeleton with all blocks singleton. Each total order produces a distinct transitive tournament. A complete skeleton has no unshielded collider, while \(\mathcal N_j=\varnothing\) and \(F(C)=\varnothing\) at every vertex, so all \(p!\) states have the same static signature and lie in one component.

Finally, the breadth-first fallback applies exactly the reversible legal moves in \(\texttt{def:legal-reversal}\). Its exact visited set is the connected component \(\mathcal R(C)\), so finiteness gives termination and no duplication. Breadth-first predecessors minimize move count, and the equivalence theorem identifies that count with \(\delta(C,D)\).

%%% FIELD proof-generic-bic-component-recovery
Write \(d_E=p+\sum_{j\in V}|\Pi^E_j|\). By \(\texttt{lem:blocked-model-geometry}\), each \(\mathcal M(E)\) is a connected, relatively closed, embedded submanifold of \(\mathbb S^p_{++}\) of dimension \(d_E\), cut out there by finitely many polynomial equalities after clearing positive denominators.

We first verify that \(\mathcal E_C\) is proper. Fix \(D\) with \(d_D\leq d_C\) and \(\mathcal M(D)\neq\mathcal M(C)\). If \(d_D<d_C\), an inclusion \(\mathcal M(C)\subseteq\mathcal M(D)\) would inject a \(d_C\)-dimensional tangent space into a \(d_D\)-dimensional one. If \(d_D=d_C\), such an inclusion would make \(\mathcal M(C)\) open in \(\mathcal M(D)\) by equal-dimensional embedded-manifold charts and closed there by relative closedness. Connectedness of \(\mathcal M(D)\) would force equality. Both cases contradict the choice of \(D\); hence \(\mathcal M(C)\nsubseteq\mathcal M(D)\). At least one polynomial equation defining \(\mathcal M(D)\) therefore has a nonzero polynomial pullback through the polynomial map \(\phi_C\), and its zero set contains \(\phi_C^{-1}(\mathcal M(C)\cap\mathcal M(D))\).

For any reported effect coordinate nonconstant on \(\mathcal M(C)\), composition with \(\phi_C\) is a nonconstant rational function on the open parameter domain \(\Theta_C\). In characteristic zero its gradient cannot vanish identically; clearing its positive denominators makes at least one gradient coordinate a nonzero polynomial. There are finitely many states and reported coordinates. The product of all nonzero polynomials obtained above is nonzero and cannot vanish throughout the open set \(\Theta_C\). Hence the finite union \(\mathcal E_C\) is a proper algebraic subset. The assumed exclusion \(\theta_C\notin\mathcal E_C\) in particular gives
\[
 \Sigma_0\notin\mathcal M(D)
 \quad\text{for every }D\text{ with }d_D\leq d_C
 \text{ and }\mathcal M(D)\neq\mathcal M(C).
\]

By \(\texttt{lem:fixed-p-gaussian-covariance-calculus}\), \(S_n\in\mathbb S^p_{++}\) almost surely whenever \(n\geq p\). For every nonempty parent set, the disjoint nonempty columns of \(A_{Ej}\) are linearly independent, and therefore, for \(u\neq0\),
\[
 u^{\mathsf T}\Gamma_{Ej}(S_n)u
 =(A_{Ej}u)^{\mathsf T}S_{n,P_{Ej}P_{Ej}}(A_{Ej}u)>0.
\]
The joint sample covariance of the block-sum regressors and \(X_j\) is the restriction of \(S_n\) under a full-column-rank linear map, hence is positive definite. Its Schur complement is the constrained residual variance, which is strictly positive.

For completeness, acyclicity gives \(\det(I_p-B_E)=1\), and the centered Gaussian negative log likelihood, up to a state-independent constant, separates as
\[
 -2\ell_n(B_E,\omega_E)
 =n\sum_{j\in V}\left\{\log\omega_{Ej}
 +\omega_{Ej}^{-1}n^{-1}\sum_{r=1}^n
 \bigl(X_j^{(r)}-(B_E)_{jP_{Ej}}X_{P_{Ej}}^{(r)}\bigr)^2\right\}.
\]
The positive-definite Gram systems have the unique constrained least-squares solutions in \(\texttt{def:global-bic}\), and minimizing in each \(\omega_{Ej}>0\) gives its strictly positive residual variance. Thus every statewise likelihood supremum on the probability-one positive-definite branch is finite and attained.

Up to an additive constant, the population likelihood contrast from \(\Sigma_0\) to \(\Sigma\) is
\[
 K(\Sigma_0,\Sigma)=\frac12\left\{
 \operatorname{tr}(\Sigma^{-1}\Sigma_0)
 -\log\det(\Sigma^{-1}\Sigma_0)-p\right\}.
\]
Diagonalizing \(\Sigma^{-1/2}\Sigma_0\Sigma^{-1/2}\) and using \(u-\log u-1\geq0\), with equality only at \(u=1\), shows \(K\geq0\), with equality only at \(\Sigma=\Sigma_0\). The same scalar expression diverges at zero and infinity, so its sublevel sets are compact subsets of \(\mathbb S^p_{++}\). Relative closedness and exclusion of \(\Sigma_0\) imply
\[
 \kappa_D:=\inf_{\Sigma\in\mathcal M(D)}K(\Sigma_0,\Sigma)>0
\]
for every nonequivalent \(D\) with \(d_D\leq d_C\).

Since \(S_n\to\Sigma_0\) in probability, it lies with probability tending to one in a fixed compact Loewner neighborhood of \(\Sigma_0\). Coercivity of the eigenvalue contrast uniformly localizes every sample optimizer to a fixed compact sublevel set. On that set the normalized likelihood is continuous, and its only sample-dependent term is affine in \(S_n\), so it converges uniformly to its population counterpart. Consequently
\[
 \sup_{\Sigma\in\mathcal M(D)}\ell_n(\Sigma)
 -\sup_{\Sigma\in\mathcal M(C)}\ell_n(\Sigma)
 \leq -n\kappa_D+o_{\mathbb P}(n)
\]
for each such \(D\). No \(O(\log n)\) penalty difference can offset this order-\(n\) loss.

If \(d_D>d_C\), let \(\ell_n(S_n)\) be the saturated Gaussian maximum. Because \(\Sigma_0\in\mathcal M(C)\),
\[
 \sup_{\mathcal M(D)}\ell_n-\sup_{\mathcal M(C)}\ell_n
 \leq \ell_n(S_n)-\ell_n(\Sigma_0).
\]
Writing \(\Delta_n=S_n-\Sigma_0\), the first-order terms in the likelihood expansion cancel at the saturated optimum, and \(\sqrt n\Delta_n=O_{\mathbb P}(1)\). The second-order Taylor remainder, multiplied by \(n\), is therefore \(O_{\mathbb P}(1)\). Hence
\[
 \operatorname{BIC}_n(D)-\operatorname{BIC}_n(C)
 \geq(d_D-d_C)\log n-O_{\mathbb P}(1)
 \longrightarrow+\infty
\]
in probability.

The class \(\mathfrak S_p\) is finite for fixed \(p\). A union bound shows that, with probability tending to one, every nonequivalent state has criterion strictly larger than that of \(C\). Any remaining minimizer has covariance image \(\mathcal M(C)\); equal embedded images have equal dimension, hence equal BIC penalty as well. By \(\texttt{thm:complete-blocked-equivalence}\), every such minimizer satisfies
\[
 \mathcal R(\widehat C_n)=\mathcal R(C).
\]
This proves consistency of the stated global component selector and makes no claim about greedy GECS.

We first connect the observable functional to the causal target. If \(E\in\mathcal R(C)\), then \(\texttt{thm:complete-blocked-equivalence}\) gives \(\mathcal M(E)=\mathcal M(C)\), so \(\Sigma_0\in\mathcal M(E)\). The injectivity in \(\texttt{lem:blocked-model-geometry}\) gives a unique structural parameter \((B_E,\omega_E)\) producing \(\Sigma_0\). In the associated linear Gaussian NPSEM,
\[
 X=B_EX+\varepsilon,
 \qquad \varepsilon\sim N_p(0,\operatorname{diag}(\omega_E)),
\]
with mutually independent innovations of positive variance. For \(P_{Ej}=\operatorname{pa}_{G_E}(j)\), innovation independence gives
\[
 \Sigma_{0,P_{Ej},j}
 =\Sigma_{0,P_{Ej},P_{Ej}}(B_E)_{jP_{Ej}}^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Writing \((B_E)_{jP_{Ej}}^{\mathsf T}=A_{Ej}\beta_{Ej}\), the constrained normal equations are
\[
 h_{Ej}(\Sigma_0)
 =A_{Ej}^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_{0,P_{Ej},P_{Ej}}A_{Ej}\beta_{Ej}
 =\Gamma_{Ej}(\Sigma_0)\beta_{Ej}.
\]
The Gram matrix is positive definite, so uniqueness of the solution in \(\texttt{def:off-model-block-ls}\) gives \(L_E(\Sigma_0)=B_E\).

Under the intervention \(\operatorname{do}(X_a=t)\), let \(r_k\) be the derivative of \(\mathbb E[X_k\mid\operatorname{do}(X_a=t)]\) with respect to \(t\). Acyclicity gives \(r_a=1\), while for \(k\neq a\),
\[
 r_k=\sum_i(B_E)_{ki}r_i.
\]
Every parent of \(a\) is a nondescendant of \(a\), so its derivative is zero and the row \(a\) obeys the same rearranged system. Hence
\[
 (I_p-B_E)r=e_a,
 \qquad
 r_y=((I_p-B_E)^{-1})_{ya}.
\]
For \(a\neq y\), this derivative is the total causal effect. Therefore \(g_E(\Sigma_0)\) equals the member-labelled causal-effect vector, and \(\mathcal T_C(\Sigma_0)\) is the finite set of causal effects compatible with the identified component. This equality has been derived from the NPSEM and is not a definition of the causal target as an observed regression functional.

By \(\texttt{lem:fixed-p-gaussian-covariance-calculus}\), \(S_n\in\mathbb S^p_{++}\) almost surely for \(n\geq p\), and \(S_n\to\Sigma_0\) in probability. Positive definiteness and full column rank of each block-incidence matrix make every \(\Gamma_{Ej}(S_n)\) positive definite. Each \(L_E\), and therefore each \(g_E\), is rational and smooth on this cone; acyclicity makes \(I_p-L_E(\Sigma)\) invertible there.

Let
\[
 A_n=\{\mathcal R(\widehat C_n)=\mathcal R(C)\}.
\]
The BIC theorem gives \(\Pr(A_n)\to1\). On \(A_n\), the selected and true components have the same finite labelled state set. Continuity and finiteness imply
\[
 d_H\{\mathcal T_{\widehat C_n}(S_n),\mathcal T_C(\Sigma_0)\}
 \leq\max_{E\in\mathcal R(C)}
 \|g_E(S_n)-g_E(\Sigma_0)\|
 \xrightarrow{\mathbb P}0.
\]
The probability of \(A_n^c\) vanishes, proving unconditional Hausdorff convergence in probability.

For simultaneous coverage retain state and query labels even when numerical effects coincide. A coordinate of \(g_E\) that is structurally constant is zero: at zero structural coefficients it is zero, and if a directed path exists one can make only the coefficients along one such path nonzero, so the coordinate is not constant. Such zero coordinates have exact singleton intervals. For every remaining coordinate \(f=g_{E,k}\), the assumption \(\theta_C\notin\mathcal E_C\) gives \(\mathrm Df(\Sigma_0)\neq0\). The covariance of the Gaussian sample-covariance coordinates is
\[
 (V_0)_{ij,kl}
 =\Sigma_{0,ik}\Sigma_{0,jl}+\Sigma_{0,il}\Sigma_{0,jk}.
\]
By \(\texttt{lem:fixed-p-gaussian-covariance-calculus}\), \(V_0\) is positive definite on nonzero symmetric directions. Thus every nonconstant coordinate has a strictly positive asymptotic variance, its plug-in variance is consistent, and its studentized error converges marginally to \(N(0,1)\).

Define the oracle labelled intervals by applying the same formula to every \(E\in\mathcal R(C)\), regardless of the selected state. On \(A_n\) they coincide exactly with the labelled intervals in \(\mathfrak I_{n,\alpha}(\widehat C_n)\). Let \(K\) be the fixed number of labelled nonconstant coordinates. If \(K\geq1\), each two-sided interval with critical value \(z_{1-\alpha/(2K)}\) has limiting noncoverage at most \(\alpha/K\). The union bound, which does not require a nonsingular joint effect covariance, gives
\[
 \limsup_{\substack{n\to\infty\\ n\geq p}}
 \Pr\{\text{some oracle labelled coordinate is not covered}\}
 \leq\alpha.
\]
If \(K=0\), every interval is exact and the same assertion holds with probability zero on the left. Finally,
\[
 \Pr\{A_n\text{ and all selected labelled coordinates are covered}\}
 \geq1-\Pr(A_n^c)
 -\Pr\{\text{some oracle labelled coordinate is not covered}\}.
\]
Taking lower limits proves the displayed simultaneous bound. Coincident values remain separately labelled, and singularity of their stacked covariance is harmless because the construction uses only positive marginal variances.
