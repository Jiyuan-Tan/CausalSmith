%%% FIELD proof-blocked-model-geometry
Fix \(E\in\mathfrak S_p\), and choose a topological ordering of \(G_E\), which exists by \(\texttt{def:blocked-state-class}\). In that ordering \(B_E\) is strictly lower triangular and hence \(B_E^p=0\). Therefore
\[
 (I_p-B_E)^{-1}=I_p+B_E+\cdots+B_E^{p-1}.
\]
The covariance map in \(\texttt{def:covariance-model}\) is consequently polynomial in \((\beta_E,\omega_E)\), and its value is positive definite because it is the congruence of the positive-definite matrix \(\operatorname{diag}(\omega_E)\) by the invertible matrix \((I_p-B_E)^{-1}\).

Write \(P_j=\operatorname{pa}_{G_E}(j)\). For \(\Sigma=\phi_E(\beta_E,\omega_E)\), the structural equation at \(j\) is
\[
 X_j=(B_E)_{jP_j}X_{P_j}+\varepsilon_j.
\]
Every parent of \(j\) precedes \(j\) in the chosen topological order, so \(X_{P_j}\) is a function of innovations other than \(\varepsilon_j\). The diagonal innovation covariance in \(\texttt{def:covariance-model}\) and Gaussianity give \(\operatorname{Cov}(X_{P_j},\varepsilon_j)=0\). Since the principal matrix \(\Sigma_{P_jP_j}\) is positive definite, including the empty case under the empty-matrix convention,
\[
 (B_E)_{jP_j}=\Sigma_{jP_j}\Sigma_{P_jP_j}^{-1},
 \qquad
 \omega_{Ej}=\Sigma_{jj}-\Sigma_{jP_j}\Sigma_{P_jP_j}^{-1}\Sigma_{P_jj}.
\]
These formulas recover every repeated block coefficient and every innovation variance. They are rational and smooth on \(\mathbb S^p_{++}\), because their denominators are positive principal minors. Thus they give a smooth left inverse of \(\phi_E\) on its image. It follows that \(\phi_E\) is injective, has injective differential, is a homeomorphism onto its image, and hence is a smooth embedding. Its image has dimension
\[
 d_E=\dim(\Theta_E)=p+\sum_{j\in V}|\Pi^E_j|,
\]
and is connected because \(\Theta_E=\mathbb R^{b_E}\times(0,\infty)^p\) is connected.

We next establish the asserted global equations and relative closedness. For arbitrary \(\Sigma\in\mathbb S^p_{++}\), define
\[
 \widetilde B_{jP_j}(\Sigma)=\Sigma_{jP_j}\Sigma_{P_jP_j}^{-1},
 \qquad
 \widetilde B_{ji}(\Sigma)=0\quad(i\notin P_j).
\]
Then \(\Sigma\in\mathcal M(E)\) if and only if both of the following finite families of equalities hold:
\[
 \widetilde B_{ji}(\Sigma)=\widetilde B_{jk}(\Sigma)
 \quad\text{whenever }i,k\in B\in\Pi^E_j,
\]
and every off-diagonal entry of
\[
 \widetilde\Omega(\Sigma)
 =(I_p-\widetilde B(\Sigma))\Sigma(I_p-\widetilde B(\Sigma))^{\mathsf T}
\]
vanishes. Necessity follows from the recovery formulas above. Conversely, the first family assembles \(\widetilde B\) from an admissible \(\beta_E\). Its zero pattern is that of the acyclic graph \(G_E\), so \(I_p-\widetilde B\) is invertible. The second family makes \(\widetilde\Omega\) diagonal, and its diagonal entries are strictly positive because \(\widetilde\Omega\) is an invertible congruence of \(\Sigma\). Hence \(\Sigma=(I_p-\widetilde B)^{-1}\widetilde\Omega(I_p-\widetilde B)^{-\mathsf T}\), which is an element of \(\mathcal M(E)\). These are continuous rational equalities on \(\mathbb S^p_{++}\), so their common zero set is relatively closed. Their denominators are products of positive principal minors; clearing those denominators gives finitely many equivalent polynomial equalities.

Finally, \(I_p\) is obtained at \(\beta_E=0\) and \(\omega_E=(1,\ldots,1)\). Differentiating the covariance formula there gives
\[
 d\Sigma=dB_E+dB_E^{\mathsf T}+\operatorname{diag}(d\omega_E).
\]
The derivative in coordinate \(\beta_{jB}\) equals one precisely on the unordered off-diagonal coordinates \(\{\{i,j\}:i\in B\}\), whereas the innovation-variance derivatives are diagonal. Projecting off the diagonal proves
\[
 \operatorname{proj}_{\mathrm{off}}T_{I_p}\mathcal M(E)
 =\operatorname{span}\{v_{jB}:j\in V,\ B\in\Pi^E_j\},
 \qquad (v_{jB})_{\{i,j\}}=\mathbf 1\{i\in B\},
\]
as required.

%%% FIELD proof-tangent-block-recovery
By \(\texttt{lem:blocked-model-geometry}\),
\[
 T_C^{\circ}=\operatorname{span}\{v_{jB}:j\in V,\ B\in\Pi_j\},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{supp}(v_{jB})=\{\{i,j\}:i\in B\}.
\]
Every unordered edge of \(G\) occurs in exactly one generator support: it belongs to the unique partition block at the head of that edge. Distinct generator supports are therefore disjoint. Consequently, if
\[
 w=\sum_{j,B}a_{jB}v_{jB}\neq0,
\]
then no coordinate cancellation is possible and
\[
 \operatorname{supp}(w)=
 \bigcup_{a_{jB}\neq0}\operatorname{supp}(v_{jB}).
\]
It follows that each generator support is inclusion-minimal among the nonempty supports of vectors in \(T_C^{\circ}\), and conversely every inclusion-minimal nonempty support is one generator support. Their union is \(\operatorname{skel}(G)\).

If \(\mathcal M(C)=\mathcal M(D)\), the two embedded model images have the same tangent space at their common point \(I_p\), so \(T_C^{\circ}=T_D^{\circ}\). Hence their collections of inclusion-minimal nonempty supports agree. A support of cardinality at least two is a labelled star with a unique common endpoint. That endpoint is the target \(j\), and the other endpoints recover the labelled block \(B\); exactly these supports recover \(\mathcal N_j(C)\). A one-edge support has two possible centers and therefore recovers only the underlying unordered edge. Applying the same support reconstruction to \(D\) gives
\[
 \operatorname{skel}(G)=\operatorname{skel}(H),
 \qquad
 \mathcal N_j(C)=\mathcal N_j(D)\quad(j\in V).
\]
If an edge has opposite orientations in \(G\) and \(H\), it cannot belong to a recovered nonsingleton support at either endpoint. Its block is therefore a singleton in both states.

%%% FIELD proof-reversed-edge-separator
By \(\texttt{lem:tangent-block-recovery}\), the reversed edge is a singleton block in both states and
\[
 \mathcal N_j(C)=\mathcal N_j(D)\quad(j\in V).
\]
By \(\texttt{lem:edge-coloured-model-equivalence-markov}\), \(G\) and \(H\) have the same unshielded colliders.

Fix \(S\in\mathcal N_x(C)\) and \(s\in S\). Tangent recovery says that \(s\to x\) occurs in both graphs. If \(s\) and \(y\) were nonadjacent, the configuration \(s\to x\to y\) in \(G\) would become the unshielded collider \(s\to x\leftarrow y\) in \(H\), contradicting equality of unshielded colliders. Thus \(s\) and \(y\) are adjacent. In \(G\), the orientation \(y\to s\) would create the directed cycle \(y\to s\to x\to y\), so acyclicity forces \(s\to y\). Since \(s\) was arbitrary,
\[
 S\subseteq\operatorname{pa}_G(y).
\]

Suppose that \(S\) is not contained in one block of \(\Pi_y\). Because \(\Pi_y\) partitions \(\operatorname{pa}_G(y)\), there is a block \(T\in\Pi_y\) such that \(T\cap S\neq\varnothing\) and \(S\nsubseteq T\). The block containing \(x\to y\) is \(\{x\}\), by tangent recovery, so \(x\notin T\). Choose in the model for \(C\) all innovation variances equal to one, the coefficients on \(x\to y\) and on the block \(T\) equal to one, and every other block coefficient equal to zero. These values lie in the parameter space of \(\texttt{def:covariance-model}\), and the resulting covariance is positive definite. The structural variables other than \(X_y\) are mutually independent standard Gaussians, while
\[
 X_y=X_x+\sum_{r\in T}X_r+\varepsilon_y.
\]

Put \(P=\operatorname{pa}_H(x)\) and \(Q=P\setminus\{y\}\). Since \(S\in\mathcal N_x(D)\), one has \(S\subseteq Q\). Residualizing \(X_y\) with respect to \(X_Q\) leaves
\[
 Z=X_x+\sum_{r\in T\setminus Q}X_r+\varepsilon_y,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(Z)=2+|T\setminus Q|.
\]
All summands displayed in \(Z\) are mutually independent. Hence the coefficient on \(X_y\) in the population regression of \(X_x\) on \((X_Q,X_y)\) is
\[
 q=\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(X_x,Z)}{\operatorname{Var}(Z)}
 =\frac{1}{2+|T\setminus Q|}>0.
\]
Before \(X_y\) is added, every coefficient in the regression of \(X_x\) on \(X_Q\) is zero. The partitioned-regression identity, obtained here by substituting the residual \(Z\) into the normal equations, therefore gives for every \(s\in S\)
\[
 \operatorname{coef}(X_s)=-q\,\mathbf 1\{s\in T\}.
\]
Because \(T\cap S\neq\varnothing\) and \(S\nsubseteq T\), these coefficients take both values \(-q\) and \(0\) on the block \(S\). On the other hand, \(S\in\mathcal N_x(D)\), and the parent-regression recovery formula proved in \(\texttt{lem:blocked-model-geometry}\) requires every covariance in \(\mathcal M(D)\) to have equal coefficients on \(S\). The constructed covariance thus belongs to \(\mathcal M(C)\setminus\mathcal M(D)\), contradicting model equality. Every \(S\in\mathcal N_x(C)\) is consequently contained in a member of \(\mathcal N_y(C)\).

Apply the same argument after exchanging \((C,x)\) with \((D,y)\). Targetwise tangent recovery identifies the families in the exchanged states, so every member of \(\mathcal N_y(C)\) is contained in a member of \(\mathcal N_x(C)\). If \(S\subseteq T\subseteq U\), with \(S,U\in\mathcal N_x(C)\) and \(T\in\mathcal N_y(C)\), then the intersecting blocks \(S\) and \(U\) of the same partition must coincide. Thus \(S=T=U\). Both inclusions follow, proving
\[
 \mathcal N_x(C)=\mathcal N_y(C).
\]

%%% FIELD proof-legal-reversal-model-invariance
Let \(S=\operatorname{pa}_{G_E}(i)\). Definedness of the move in \(\texttt{def:legal-reversal}\) says
\[
 \operatorname{pa}_{G_E}(j)=S\cup\{i\},
 \qquad
 \{i\}\in\Pi^E_j,
 \qquad
 \Pi^E_j\setminus\{\{i\}\}=\Pi^E_i.
\]
Reversing this covered edge preserves acyclicity. Indeed, if the reversal created a directed cycle, then before reversal there was a directed path from \(i\) to \(j\) other than \(i\to j\). If \(k\to j\) is its last edge, coveredness gives \(k\in S=\operatorname{pa}_{G_E}(i)\), so the initial segment \(i\leadsto k\) followed by \(k\to i\) was already a directed cycle, a contradiction. The transported families in \(\texttt{def:legal-reversal}\) partition the new parent sets. Hence the reversed state \(E'\) belongs to \(\mathfrak S_p\), and inspection of the transported partitions shows that \(\operatorname{rev}_{j\to i}(E')\) is defined and equals \(E\).

It remains to compare the full covariance images. Conditional on \(X_S\), write the two affected structural equations in \(E\) as
\[
 X_i=a^{\mathsf T}X_S+\varepsilon_i,
 \qquad
 X_j=bX_i+d^{\mathsf T}X_S+\varepsilon_j,
\]
where \(u=\operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_i)>0\) and \(v=\operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_j)>0\). The vectors \(a\) and \(d\) are constant on each block of the common partition \(\Pi^E_i\). Define
\[
 t=v+b^2u>0,
 \qquad q=\frac{bu}{t},
 \qquad \eta_j=b\varepsilon_i+\varepsilon_j,
 \qquad \eta_i=\varepsilon_i-q\eta_j.
\]
Direct substitution yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 X_j&=(d+ba)^{\mathsf T}X_S+\eta_j,\\
 X_i&=qX_j+\{a-q(d+ba)\}^{\mathsf T}X_S+\eta_i.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(\eta_j)=t,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(\eta_i)=\frac{uv}{t}>0,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Cov}(\eta_i,\eta_j)=bu-qt=0.
\]
The pair \((\eta_i,\eta_j)\) is jointly Gaussian, so zero covariance makes its components independent. It is a linear transformation of \((\varepsilon_i,\varepsilon_j)\), and is therefore independent of all other innovations. Both \(d+ba\) and \(a-q(d+ba)\) remain constant on each block of \(\Pi^E_i\), while \(q\) occupies the transported singleton \(\{j\}\). Thus every covariance in \(\mathcal M(E)\) belongs to \(\mathcal M(E')\). Applying the same construction to the already established inverse legal move gives the reverse inclusion.

Finally, \(\Pi^{E'}_j=\Pi^E_i\), \(\Pi^{E'}_i=\Pi^E_i\cup\{\{j\}\}\), and all other partitions are unchanged. Removing singleton blocks therefore preserves every nonsingleton family.

Suppose first that every block of \(C\) is a singleton. Then \(\mathcal N_j(C)=\varnothing\) for every \(j\), and hence \(F(C)=\varnothing\). The static criterion in \(\texttt{thm:complete-blocked-equivalence}\) reduces to equality of skeletons and unshielded colliders. By \(\texttt{lem:verma-pearl-markov-equivalence}\), the states in \(\mathcal R(C)\) have exactly the DAGs in the ordinary Markov-equivalence class of \(G\); their parent partitions are necessarily all-singleton. The distance conclusion of \(\texttt{thm:complete-blocked-equivalence}\) gives \(d_{\mathrm{rev}}(C,D)=\delta(C,D)\).

Suppose next that no parent block of \(C\) is a singleton. For any edge \(i\to j\), its block \(B\in\mathcal N_j(C)\) contains \(i\), whereas no member of \(\mathcal N_i(C)\) can contain \(i\), because a vertex is not its own parent. Thus \(\mathcal N_i(C)\neq\mathcal N_j(C)\), so every edge lies in \(F(C)\). The static criterion fixes the orientation of every skeleton edge. It also fixes every nonsingleton family, which here is the entire partition. Therefore \(\mathcal R(C)=\{C\}\), agreeing with the proper BPEC boundary stated in \(\texttt{lem:bpec-proper-identifiability}\).

Finally, assume \(G=H\) and \(\mathcal M(C)=\mathcal M(D)\). By \(\texttt{lem:tangent-block-recovery}\), \(\mathcal N_j(C)=\mathcal N_j(D)\) for every \(j\). The common graph gives identical parent sets. Each partition is uniquely obtained by taking its nonsingleton blocks and adding singleton blocks for every parent outside their union. Hence \(\Pi_j=\Lambda_j\) for all \(j\), and therefore \(\Pi=\Lambda\).

%%% FIELD proof-fixed-p-gaussian-covariance-calculus
Let \(Z\) be the \(p\)-by-\(n\) matrix with columns \(\Sigma_0^{-1/2}X^{(r)}\). Its entries possess a joint Lebesgue density. When \(n\geq p\), failure of row rank \(p\) is the common zero set of all \(p\)-by-\(p\) minors. This set has Lebesgue measure zero because at least one of those determinant polynomials is nonzero. Hence the data matrix has row rank \(p\) almost surely, and
\[
 S_n=n^{-1}XX^{\mathsf T}\in\mathbb S^p_{++}
\]
almost surely.

Differentiating the Gaussian moment-generating function \(\exp(t^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_0t/2)\) four times at zero gives
\[
 \mathbb E[X_iX_jX_kX_l]
 =\Sigma_{0,ij}\Sigma_{0,kl}
  +\Sigma_{0,ik}\Sigma_{0,jl}
  +\Sigma_{0,il}\Sigma_{0,jk}.
\]
After subtracting \(\mathbb E[X_iX_j]\mathbb E[X_kX_l]\), this proves
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(X_iX_j,X_kX_l)
 =\Sigma_{0,ik}\Sigma_{0,jl}+\Sigma_{0,il}\Sigma_{0,jk}.
\]
Summing the coordinate variances gives \(\mathbb E\|S_n-\Sigma_0\|_F^2=O(n^{-1})\), hence \(S_n\to\Sigma_0\) in \(L^2\).

For the limit law, put \(W_r=\operatorname{vech}(X^{(r)}X^{(r)\mathsf T}-\Sigma_0)\). These vectors are independent and centered, have covariance \(V_0\), and possess finite third moments. For each fixed vector \(t\), Taylor expansion with its third-order remainder bounded by \(\mathbb E|t^{\mathsf T}W_1|^3\) gives
\[
 \mathbb E\exp\{it^{\mathsf T}W_1/\sqrt n\}
 =1-\frac{t^{\mathsf T}V_0t}{2n}+O(n^{-3/2}).
\]
The \(n\)-th power converges to \(\exp(-t^{\mathsf T}V_0t/2)\). The characteristic-function criterion therefore yields
\[
 \sqrt n\,\operatorname{vech}(S_n-\Sigma_0)\Longrightarrow N(0,V_0).
\]

If \(A\neq0\) is symmetric, the corresponding quadratic-form direction has variance
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(X^{\mathsf T}AX)
 =2\operatorname{tr}\{(\Sigma_0^{1/2}A\Sigma_0^{1/2})^2\}>0.
\]
Thus \(V_0\) is positive definite on nonzero symmetric directions. For a continuously differentiable scalar functional \(f\), Taylor's formula gives
\[
 \sqrt n\{f(S_n)-f(\Sigma_0)\}
 =\mathrm Df(\Sigma_0)[\sqrt n(S_n-\Sigma_0)]+o_{\mathbb P}(1).
\]
The preceding limit gives the corresponding centered normal law. Replacing \(\Sigma_0\) by \(S_n\) in the displayed polynomial formula for \(V_0\) is consistent by \(S_n\to\Sigma_0\); when \(\mathrm Df(\Sigma_0)\neq0\), positive definiteness makes the limiting variance strictly positive, and division by the plug-in standard error gives the stated studentized standard normal limit.

%%% FIELD proof-generic-bic-component-recovery
Write \(d_E=p+\sum_{j\in V}|\Pi^E_j|\). By \(\texttt{lem:blocked-model-geometry}\), every \(\mathcal M(E)\) is a connected, relatively closed, embedded submanifold of \(\mathbb S^p_{++}\) of dimension \(d_E\), and it is cut out there by finitely many polynomial equalities after positive denominators are cleared.

We first prove that the exceptional set in \(\texttt{def:generic-exception}\) is proper. Fix \(D\) with \(d_D\leq d_C\) and \(\mathcal M(D)\neq\mathcal M(C)\). If \(d_D<d_C\), the embedded \(d_C\)-manifold \(\mathcal M(C)\) cannot be contained in the embedded \(d_D\)-manifold \(\mathcal M(D)\), because the differential of an inclusion would inject a \(d_C\)-dimensional tangent space into a \(d_D\)-dimensional one. If \(d_D=d_C\) and \(\mathcal M(C)\subseteq\mathcal M(D)\), equal-dimensional submanifold charts make \(\mathcal M(C)\) open in \(\mathcal M(D)\), while relative closedness makes it closed there. Connectedness of \(\mathcal M(D)\) would imply equality, again a contradiction. Therefore \(\mathcal M(C)\nsubseteq\mathcal M(D)\). At least one polynomial equation defining \(\mathcal M(D)\) pulls back through the polynomial map \(\phi_C\) to a nonzero polynomial on \(\Theta_C\); its zero set contains the pullback of \(\mathcal M(C)\cap\mathcal M(D)\).

For any reported effect coordinate that is nonconstant on \(\mathcal M(C)\), composition with \(\phi_C\) is a nonconstant rational function on the open parameter domain \(\Theta_C\). Its gradient is not identically zero. After multiplication by powers of its positive block-Gram denominators, at least one gradient coordinate is a nonzero polynomial, so the joint zero-gradient locus is contained in a proper polynomial zero set. There are finitely many states and reported coordinates. The product of all the nonzero polynomials just obtained remains nonzero, and a nonzero real polynomial cannot vanish on the open set \(\Theta_C\). Thus \(\mathcal E_C\subsetneq\Theta_C\) is proper algebraic. Since \(\theta_C\notin\mathcal E_C\),
\[
 \Sigma_0\notin\mathcal M(D)
 \quad\text{for every }D\text{ with }d_D\leq d_C
 \text{ and }\mathcal M(D)\neq\mathcal M(C).
\]

By \(\texttt{lem:fixed-p-gaussian-covariance-calculus}\), \(S_n\in\mathbb S^p_{++}\) almost surely for every \(n\geq p\). If \(P_{Ej}\neq\varnothing\), the nonempty disjoint block columns of \(A_{Ej}\) are linearly independent. Hence for \(u\neq0\),
\[
 u^{\mathsf T}\Gamma_{Ej}(S_n)u
 =(A_{Ej}u)^{\mathsf T}S_{n,P_{Ej}P_{Ej}}(A_{Ej}u)>0.
\]
The joint sample covariance of the block-sum regressors and \(X_j\) is also positive definite, because it is the restriction of \(S_n\) along a full-column-rank linear map. Its Schur complement is exactly the constrained residual variance, so that variance is strictly positive. Thus, on the probability-one event in question, every inverse in \(\texttt{def:global-bic}\) exists and every statewise Gaussian likelihood supremum is finite and attained.

We now compare the finitely many criteria. Up to a term independent of \(\Sigma\),
\[
 \ell_n(\Sigma)=-\frac n2\{\log\det\Sigma+\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma^{-1}S_n)\}.
\]
The population likelihood contrast is
\[
 K(\Sigma_0,\Sigma)=\frac12\left\{
 \operatorname{tr}(\Sigma^{-1}\Sigma_0)
 -\log\det(\Sigma^{-1}\Sigma_0)-p\right\}.
\]
Diagonalizing \(\Sigma^{-1/2}\Sigma_0\Sigma^{-1/2}\) and using \(u-\log u-1\geq0\), with equality only at \(u=1\), shows that \(K\geq0\), with equality only when \(\Sigma=\Sigma_0\). The same scalar expression diverges when an eigenvalue tends to zero or infinity, so its sublevel sets are compact subsets of \(\mathbb S^p_{++}\). Relative closedness of \(\mathcal M(D)\) and exclusion of \(\Sigma_0\) therefore imply
\[
 \kappa_D:=\inf_{\Sigma\in\mathcal M(D)}K(\Sigma_0,\Sigma)>0
\]
for every nonequivalent \(D\) with \(d_D\leq d_C\).

Because \(S_n\to\Sigma_0\) in probability, with probability tending to one it lies in a fixed compact neighborhood of \(\Sigma_0\) bounded above and below in Loewner order. Coercivity of the preceding eigenvalue expression is uniform over that neighborhood, so every relevant sample optimizer lies in one fixed compact sublevel set. On that set the normalized log likelihood depends continuously, and in fact affinely through its trace term, on \(S_n\); hence it converges uniformly to its population counterpart. It follows that
\[
 \sup_{\Sigma\in\mathcal M(D)}\ell_n(\Sigma)
 -\sup_{\Sigma\in\mathcal M(C)}\ell_n(\Sigma)
 \leq -n\kappa_D+o_{\mathbb P}(n)
\]
for each such \(D\). An \(O(\log n)\) penalty difference cannot offset this order-\(n\) loss.

If instead \(d_D>d_C\), let \(\ell_n(S_n)\) denote the saturated Gaussian maximum. Since \(\Sigma_0\in\mathcal M(C)\),
\[
 \sup_{\mathcal M(D)}\ell_n-\sup_{\mathcal M(C)}\ell_n
 \leq \ell_n(S_n)-\ell_n(\Sigma_0).
\]
Put \(\Delta_n=S_n-\Sigma_0\). The first-order terms cancel in the Taylor expansion of the right-hand side at \(\Sigma_0\), while \(\sqrt n\,\Delta_n=O_{\mathbb P}(1)\) by \(\texttt{lem:fixed-p-gaussian-covariance-calculus}\). Multiplication by \(n\) therefore gives
\[
 \ell_n(S_n)-\ell_n(\Sigma_0)=O_{\mathbb P}(1).
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \operatorname{BIC}_n(D)-\operatorname{BIC}_n(C)
 \geq(d_D-d_C)\log n-O_{\mathbb P}(1)
 \longrightarrow+\infty
\]
in probability.

The class \(\mathfrak S_p\) is finite. A union bound over its states shows that, with probability tending to one, every nonequivalent state has larger criterion than \(C\). Any remaining minimizer has model image \(\mathcal M(C)\); equality of embedded images forces equality of their dimensions, so tied equivalent states also have the same BIC penalty. By \(\texttt{thm:complete-blocked-equivalence}\), every such minimizer satisfies
\[
 \mathcal R(\widehat C_n)=\mathcal R(C).
\]
This proves the asserted component recovery and concerns only the global minimizer defined in \(\texttt{def:global-bic}\), not a greedy search procedure.

%%% FIELD proof-simultaneous-component-effect-inference
We first prove that the displayed observable maps equal the causal effects at the true covariance. If \(E\in\mathcal R(C)\), then \(\texttt{thm:complete-blocked-equivalence}\) gives \(\mathcal M(E)=\mathcal M(C)\), so \(\Sigma_0\in\mathcal M(E)\). Let \((B_E,\omega_E)\) be its unique structural parameter, whose uniqueness follows from \(\texttt{lem:blocked-model-geometry}\). For target \(j\), independence of its innovation and its parents gives
\[
 \Sigma_{0,P_{Ej},j}=\Sigma_{0,P_{Ej},P_{Ej}}(B_E)_{jP_{Ej}}^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Writing \((B_E)_{jP_{Ej}}^{\mathsf T}=A_{Ej}\beta_{Ej}\), the block normal equations become
\[
 h_{Ej}(\Sigma_0)
 =A_{Ej}^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_{0,P_{Ej},P_{Ej}}A_{Ej}\beta_{Ej}
 =\Gamma_{Ej}(\Sigma_0)\beta_{Ej}.
\]
The Gram matrix is positive definite, so the solution in \(\texttt{def:off-model-block-ls}\) is unique and \(L_E(\Sigma_0)=B_E\).

Under an intervention setting coordinate \(a\), let \(r_k\) be the derivative of \(X_k\) with respect to the intervention value. Acyclicity gives \(r_a=1\), and for \(k\neq a\),
\[
 r_k=\sum_i(B_E)_{ki}r_i.
\]
No parent of \(a\) is a descendant of \(a\), so its derivative is zero; consequently the row \(a\) of the same system also reads \(r_a-\sum_i(B_E)_{ai}r_i=1\). Thus
\[
 (I_p-B_E)r=e_a,
 \qquad
 r_y=((I_p-B_E)^{-1})_{ya}.
\]
For \(a\neq y\), this is the total causal effect of \(a\) on \(y\). Hence \(g_E(\Sigma_0)\) is the member-labelled causal-effect vector and \(\mathcal T_C(\Sigma_0)\) is the finite causal target; it is not being defined to equal an observed regression functional.

By \(\texttt{lem:fixed-p-gaussian-covariance-calculus}\), \(S_n\in\mathbb S^p_{++}\) almost surely for \(n\geq p\), and \(S_n\to\Sigma_0\) in probability. Positive definiteness and the full column rank of every block-incidence matrix imply that all \(\Gamma_{Ej}(S_n)\) are positive definite. Thus all maps and derivatives in the theorem are evaluated on their declared open domain.

Let

 A_n=\{\mathcal R(\widehat C_n)=\mathcal R(C)\}.

By \(\texttt{thm:generic-bic-component-recovery}\), \(\Pr(A_n)\to1\). On \(A_n\), the two components have the same finite state index set. Continuity of each rational map \(g_E\) at \(\Sigma_0\) gives
\[
 d_H\{\mathcal T_{\widehat C_n}(S_n),\mathcal T_C(\Sigma_0)\}
 \leq\max_{E\in\mathcal R(C)}
 \|g_E(S_n)-g_E(\Sigma_0)\|
 \xrightarrow{\mathbb P}0.
\]
The complement of \(A_n\) has probability tending to zero, which proves the unconditional Hausdorff convergence in probability.

For coverage, retain member labels even when distinct states have the same numerical effect. Flatten the finitely many member and query coordinates. A coordinate that is structurally constant is zero, since all structural coefficients may be set to zero and \(a\neq y\), and its singleton interval covers it with probability one. For every nonconstant coordinate \(f=g_{E,k}\), exclusion of \(\theta_C\) from \(\mathcal E_C\) gives \(\mathrm Df(\Sigma_0)\neq0\). The covariance of the Gaussian sample-covariance coordinates is
\[
 (V_0)_{ij,kl}
 =\Sigma_{0,ik}\Sigma_{0,jl}+\Sigma_{0,il}\Sigma_{0,jk}.
\]
By \(\texttt{lem:fixed-p-gaussian-covariance-calculus}\), it is positive definite on nonzero symmetric directions. Therefore every plug-in studentized error converges marginally to \(N(0,1)\).

On \(A_n\), let \(K\) be the fixed number of labelled nonconstant coordinates. If \(K\geq1\), each two-sided interval using \(z_{1-\alpha/(2K)}\) has limiting noncoverage at most \(\alpha/K\). The union bound, which requires no nonsingularity of the joint covariance of the coordinates, gives
\[
 \limsup_{\substack{n\to\infty\\ n\geq p}}
 \Pr\{\text{some labelled coordinate is not covered}\}
 \leq\alpha.
\]
If \(K=0\), all intervals are exact and the same conclusion is immediate. Finally,
\[
 \Pr(A_n\cap\{\text{all labelled coordinates are covered}\})
 \geq1-\Pr(A_n^c)
 -\Pr\{\text{some labelled coordinate is not covered}\}.
\]
Taking lower limits proves the asserted simultaneous bound. Coincident effect values do not alter the labelled union bound, and singularity of the stacked covariance is harmless because only the strictly positive marginal variances are studentized.
