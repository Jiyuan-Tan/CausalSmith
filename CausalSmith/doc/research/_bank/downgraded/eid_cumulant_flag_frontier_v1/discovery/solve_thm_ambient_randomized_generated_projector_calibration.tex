%%% FIELD proof-ambient-projector-expansion
Fix an arbitrary triangular array \(\theta_n\in\Theta\), and suppress the triangular-array index on its population quantities.  The observations are independent across environments and, in environment \(e\), \(W_{e,1},\ldots,W_{e,n_e}\) are identically distributed with law \(P_e\).  By balanced shares,
\[
 n_e=n\pi_e\geq n\underline\pi. \tag{1}
\]
Consequently every environment eventually has at least \(R_{\max}\) observations, so the small-sample fallback in the definition of the cumulant estimator is eventually inactive.

We first obtain all moment bounds needed below from the declared source envelope.  The coefficient restrictions give
\[
 |\alpha|\leq a_1,
 \qquad |\beta|\leq B,
 \qquad |\gamma|=|\alpha|\,|\gamma/\alpha|\leq a_1q_1. \tag{2}
\]
The structural equations therefore read
\[
 T_e=\alpha\epsilon_{u,e}+\epsilon_{t,e},
 \qquad
 Y_e=(\beta\alpha+\gamma)\epsilon_{u,e}
       +\beta\epsilon_{t,e}+\epsilon_y. \tag{3}
\]
Put \(s_0=2R_{\max}+\delta\).  Since \(s_0>1\), convexity gives
\[
 |x_1+\cdots+x_j|^{s_0}
 \leq j^{s_0-1}\sum_{a=1}^j|x_a|^{s_0}. \tag{4}
\]
Applying (4) to (3), then using (2) and the source moment envelope, gives a constant \(K_W<\infty\), depending only on the fixed envelopes in \(\Theta\), such that
\[
 \sup_n\max_{e\in\mathcal E}
 \mathbb E_{\theta_n}\|W_{e,1}\|^{2R_{\max}+\delta}
 \leq K_W. \tag{5}
\]
In particular, H\"older's inequality bounds uniformly every observed moment through order \(2R_{\max}\).  The finite moment--cumulant polynomial then bounds uniformly \(C,c_k,m_k\), and \(h_k:=m_k-\beta c_k\), for \(2\leq k\leq R_{\max}\).

We next derive the linear representations rather than assume them.  For integrable products through order \(k\), the joint cumulant is
\[
 \kappa_k(X_1,\ldots,X_k)
 =\sum_{\mathcal P\in\Pi_k}
 (|\mathcal P|-1)!(-1)^{|\mathcal P|-1}
 \prod_{D\in\mathcal P}
 \mathbb E\!\left[\prod_{j\in D}X_j\right], \tag{6}
\]
where \(\Pi_k\) is the finite set of partitions of \(\{1,\ldots,k\}\).  For a partition with \(s\) blocks, replace the product of its \(s\) expectations by the symmetrized average of the corresponding block monomials over \(s\) distinct observations.  Thus each unbiased sample cumulant appearing in the construction is a fixed finite linear combination of symmetric \(U\)-statistics of orders at most \(k\), and every constituent kernel has total polynomial degree at most \(k\).

Consider one order-\(s\) constituent \(U_{n_e,s}(g)\).  Successive subtraction of conditional expectations produces centered canonical kernels \(g_j\), \(1\leq j\leq s\), and the exact algebraic decomposition
\[
 U_{n_e,s}(g)-\mathbb Eg
 =\frac1{n_e}\sum_{i=1}^{n_e}\psi_{g,e}(W_{e,i})
  +\sum_{j=2}^s {s\choose j}U_{n_e,j}(g_j), \tag{7}
\]
where
\[
 \psi_{g,e}(w)
 =s\{\mathbb E[g(w,W_{e,2},\ldots,W_{e,s})]-\mathbb Eg\},
 \qquad \mathbb E\psi_{g,e}(W_{e,1})=0. \tag{8}
\]
If two distinct unordered \(j\)-tuples index summands in \(U_{n_e,j}(g_j)\), one tuple contains an index absent from the other.  Conditioning on all remaining observations and using degeneracy in that argument shows that their covariance is zero.  Hence
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[U_{n_e,j}(g_j)^2\right]
 ={n_e\choose j}^{-1}\mathbb E[g_j^2]
 \leq K n_e^{-j}. \tag{9}
\]
The last bound is uniform: a squared constituent kernel uses observed moments of order at most \(2R_{\max}\), which are bounded by (5), and there are only finitely many orders and partitions.  Therefore all projections of order at least two in (7) are \(O_{L^2}(n_e^{-1})\), uniformly.

Apply the environment-versus-baseline signs in the definitions of \(C,c_k,m_k\) to the first projections (8), and stack them.  This defines centered arrays \(\psi^C_{e,n}\), \(\psi^c_{e,k,n}\), and \(\psi^m_{e,k,n}\).  Since \(n_e=n\pi_e\), equations (1), (7), and (9) give, simultaneously over the finitely many relevant orders,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sqrt n(\widehat C-C)
 &=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}\sum_{i=1}^{n_e}
      \pi_e^{-1}\psi^C_{e,n}(W_{e,i})+o_{L^2}(1),\\
 \sqrt n(\widehat c_k-c_k)
 &=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}\sum_{i=1}^{n_e}
      \pi_e^{-1}\psi^c_{e,k,n}(W_{e,i})+o_{L^2}(1),\\
 \sqrt n(\widehat m_k-m_k)
 &=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}\sum_{i=1}^{n_e}
      \pi_e^{-1}\psi^m_{e,k,n}(W_{e,i})+o_{L^2}(1).
 \end{aligned} \tag{10}
\]
Indeed, multiplication of the \(O_{L^2}(n_e^{-1})\) remainder by \(\sqrt n\) gives \(O(n^{-1/2})\) by (1).

Let
\[
 \nu=\min\{1,\delta/R_{\max}\}>0. \tag{11}
\]
Each first projection in (10) is a finite sum of polynomials of degree at most \(R_{\max}\) in one observation, with coefficients made from uniformly bounded lower-order moments.  Conditional Jensen, (5), and
\((2+\nu)R_{\max}\leq2R_{\max}+\delta\) therefore imply
\[
 \sup_{n,e,\theta_n\in\Theta}\mathbb E_{\theta_n}\!\left[
 \|\psi^C_{e,n}\|^{2+\nu}
 +\sum_{k=2}^{R_{\max}}
 \{\|\psi^c_{e,k,n}\|^{2+\nu}
   +\|\psi^m_{e,k,n}\|^{2+\nu}\}\right]<\infty. \tag{12}
\]

We now linearize the generated projector.  Put \(C^+=(C^\top C)^{-1}C^\top\) and \(P_C=CC^+\), so \(Q_A=I_{E-1}-P_C\).  The calibration gap licenses the inverse and gives
\[
 \|C^+\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\sigma_d(C)^{-1}\leq\kappa_0^{-1}. \tag{13}
\]
For \(\|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\kappa_0/2\), the variational definition of singular values yields
\[
 \sigma_d(C+H)\geq\sigma_d(C)-\|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \geq\kappa_0/2. \tag{14}
\]
Writing \(G=C^\top C\) and \(D=C^\top H+H^\top C+H^\top H\), the exact inverse identity
\[
 (G+D)^{-1}=G^{-1}-G^{-1}DG^{-1}
 +G^{-1}DG^{-1}D(G+D)^{-1} \tag{15}
\]
and direct substitution into
\(P_{C+H}=(C+H)\{(C+H)^\top(C+H)\}^{-1}(C+H)^\top\)
give
\[
 P_{C+H}-P_C
 =Q_AHC^+ +(Q_AHC^+)^\top+R_C(H),
 \qquad \|R_C(H)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \leq K_P\|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2. \tag{16}
\]
The constant is uniform because (5)--(6) bound \(\|C\|\), (13) bounds \(G^{-1}\), and (14) bounds the last inverse in (15).

Taking \(H=\widehat C-C\), equations (10) and (12) show that \(\|H\|=O_{\mathbb P}(n^{-1/2})\) uniformly.  Thus (16) applies with probability tending to one uniformly, with an \(O_{\mathbb P}(n^{-1})\) remainder.  On this event the rank-\(d\) SVD projector is the column-space projector of \(\widehat C\), so
\[
 \widehat Q-Q_A
 =-Q_A(\widehat C-C)C^+
  -\{Q_A(\widehat C-C)C^+\}^\top
  +o_{\mathbb P}(n^{-1/2}) \tag{17}
\]
uniformly.

The already established cumulant-cancellation lemma applies to every \(\theta_n\in\Theta\) and gives
\[
 h_k=m_k-\beta c_k=\alpha^{k-1}\gamma u_k\in A=\operatorname{col}(C),
 \qquad Q_Ah_k=0. \tag{18}
\]
Consequently the transposed derivative term vanishes on \(h_k\):
\[
 \{Q_A(\widehat C-C)C^+\}^\top h_k
 =C^{+\top}(\widehat C-C)^\top Q_Ah_k=0. \tag{19}
\]
Put \(\widehat h_k=\widehat m_k-\beta\widehat c_k\).  Expanding the product, using (10) and (17)--(19), and using the uniform bounds on \(\beta,h_k,C^+\), gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sqrt n\,\widehat Q\widehat h_k
 &=\sqrt n\,Q_A(\widehat h_k-h_k)
   +\sqrt n(\widehat Q-Q_A)h_k
   +\sqrt n(\widehat Q-Q_A)(\widehat h_k-h_k)\\
 &=\sqrt n\,Q_A\{(\widehat m_k-m_k)
       -\beta(\widehat c_k-c_k)\}
   -\sqrt n\,Q_A(\widehat C-C)C^+h_k
   +o_{\mathbb P}(1).
 \end{aligned} \tag{20}
\]
The product in the first line after multiplication by \(\sqrt n\) is \(O_{\mathbb P}(n^{-1/2})\), uniformly, because each estimation error is \(O_{\mathbb P}(n^{-1/2})\).  Substitution of (10) into (20) proves, simultaneously for \(d+2\leq k\leq R_{\max}\),
\[
 \sqrt n\,\widehat Q(\widehat m_k-\beta\widehat c_k)
 =\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}\sum_{i=1}^{n_e}
   \pi_e^{-1}\zeta_{e,k,n}(W_{e,i})+o_{\mathbb P}(1), \tag{21}
\]
where
\[
 \zeta_{e,k,n}
 =Q_A\{\psi^m_{e,k,n}-\beta\psi^c_{e,k,n}
          -\psi^C_{e,n}C^+h_k\}. \tag{22}
\]
This establishes both the sign and the form of the same-sample estimated-plane correction.  No step has used a breakout or a lower bound on a projected cumulant.

It remains to prove the asserted Gaussian approximation without imposing a covariance-rank condition.  Stack (22) over \(d+2\leq k\leq R_{\max}\).  Equations (12)--(13) and the uniform bound on \(h_k\) give
\[
 \sup_{n,e,\theta_n\in\Theta}
 \mathbb E_{\theta_n}\|\zeta_{e,n}\|^{2+\nu}<\infty. \tag{23}
\]
Index the independent summands by \(a=(e,i)\), and set
\[
 X_a=n^{-1/2}\pi_e^{-1}\zeta_{e,n}(W_{e,i}),
 \qquad L_n=\sum_a\mathbb E\|X_a\|^{2+\nu}.
\]
Balanced shares and (23) imply
\[
 L_n
 \leq K n^{-1-\nu/2}
       \sum_{e\in\mathcal E}n_e\pi_e^{-(2+\nu)}
 \leq K'n^{-\nu/2}. \tag{24}
\]
For each \(a\), let \(G_a\) be an independent centered Gaussian vector having the covariance of \(X_a\).  In the fixed stacked dimension, the Gaussian moment formula and Lyapunov's inequality give
\[
 \mathbb E\|G_a\|^{2+\nu}
 \leq K\{\mathbb E\|X_a\|^2\}^{1+\nu/2}
 \leq K\mathbb E\|X_a\|^{2+\nu}. \tag{25}
\]

For a function \(f\) bounded by one and one-Lipschitz, let
\(f_\varepsilon(x)=\mathbb E[f(x+\varepsilon Z_0)]\), where \(Z_0\) is standard Gaussian in the fixed stacked dimension.  Coupling gives \(\|f_\varepsilon-f\|_\infty\leq K\varepsilon\).  Differentiating the Gaussian convolution gives
\[
 \sup_x\|D^2f_\varepsilon(x)\|\leq K\varepsilon^{-1},
 \qquad
 \sup_x\|D^3f_\varepsilon(x)\|\leq K\varepsilon^{-2}.
\]
The minimum of the bounds furnished by these two displays, together with
\(\min\{u,v\}\leq u^\nu v^{1-\nu}\), yields
\[
 \|D^2f_\varepsilon(x)-D^2f_\varepsilon(y)\|
 \leq K\varepsilon^{-(1+\nu)}\|x-y\|^\nu. \tag{26}
\]
The integral second-order Taylor remainder is therefore at most
\(K\varepsilon^{-(1+\nu)}\|v\|^{2+\nu}\).  Replace the \(X_a\)'s successively by the \(G_a\)'s.  At each replacement, the constant, linear, and quadratic Taylor terms have equal expectations because the paired variables have the same mean and covariance.  Equations (25)--(26) give
\[
 \left|\mathbb Ef_\varepsilon\!\left(\sum_aX_a\right)
       -\mathbb Ef_\varepsilon\!\left(\sum_aG_a\right)\right|
 \leq K\varepsilon^{-(1+\nu)}L_n. \tag{27}
\]
After removing the smoothing,
\[
 d_{\mathrm{BL}}\!\left(\mathcal L\!\left(\sum_aX_a\right),
                         \mathcal L\!\left(\sum_aG_a\right)\right)
 \leq K\{\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{-(1+\nu)}L_n\}. \tag{28}
\]
If \(L_n>0\), choose \(\varepsilon=L_n^{1/(2+\nu)}\); if \(L_n=0\), all summands on both sides vanish almost surely.  Equations (24) and (28) prove uniform convergence to zero.  Finally,
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}\!\left(\sum_aG_a\right)
 =\frac1n\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}n_e\pi_e^{-2}
   \mathbb E[\zeta_{e,n}\zeta_{e,n}^\top]
 =\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}\pi_e^{-1}
   \mathbb E[\zeta_{e,n}\zeta_{e,n}^\top]
 =\Sigma_{n,\theta_n}. \tag{29}
\]
Thus the comparison law is \(N(0,\Sigma_{n,\theta_n})\).  Its construction used only positive semidefiniteness: when the covariance is singular it is supported on its image, and when the covariance is zero it is a point mass.  Since the triangular array was arbitrary and every displayed bound is uniform over the fixed envelopes defining \(\Theta\), (21)--(29) prove every asserted uniformity and singular-covariance clause.

Write \(\mathcal K=\{d+2,\ldots,R_{\max}\}\) and use the block maximum norm from the randomized-jitter construction.  Its dimension \(D_{\mathrm{AR}}=(E-1)(R_{\max}-d-1)\) is fixed.  All constants below depend only on the fixed envelopes defining \(\Theta\), never on \(\theta\in\Theta\).  Balanced shares give \(n_e\geq n\underline\pi\), so every stratum eventually contains at least \(R_{\max}\) observations.  The finite-sample fallback makes the confidence set defined before that point, while the formulas in the definition apply eventually.

At the true candidate \(b=\beta\), the ambient generated-projector expansion gives
\[
 H_n(\beta)=Z_n+R_n,
 \qquad
 Z_n=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}\sum_{i=1}^{n_e}
 \pi_e^{-1}\zeta_{e,n}(W_{e,i}), \tag{1}
\]
where \(R_n=o_{\mathbb P}(1)\) uniformly and
\[
 \Sigma_{n,\theta}=\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}\pi_e^{-1}
 \mathbb E_\theta[\zeta_{e,n}\zeta_{e,n}^\top]. \tag{2}
\]
That lemma also proves, for
\[
 \nu=\min\{1,\delta/R_{\max}\}>0,
\]
the uniform moment bound
\[
 \sup_{n,e,\theta\in\Theta}
 \mathbb E_\theta\|\zeta_{e,n}\|^{2+\nu}\leq K. \tag{3}
\]
The exact relation used at \(b=\beta\) is
\(Q_A(m_k-\beta c_k)=0\), so (1)--(3) do not require a breakout.

We first make the approximation rates explicit.  With
\(X_{e,i,n}=n^{-1/2}\pi_e^{-1}\zeta_{e,n}(W_{e,i})\), balanced shares and (3) imply
\[
 \sum_{e\in\mathcal E}\sum_{i=1}^{n_e}
 \mathbb E\|X_{e,i,n}\|^{2+\nu}
 \leq K n^{-\nu/2}. \tag{4}
\]
The Gaussian smoothing and covariance-matched Lindeberg replacement derived in the expansion proof bound the bounded-Lipschitz distance by
\[
 K\{s+n^{-\nu/2}s^{-1-\nu}\}.
\]
Taking \(s=n^{-\nu/(4+2\nu)}\) yields
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}
 d_{\mathrm{BL}}\bigl(\mathcal L_\theta(Z_n),
 N(0,\Sigma_{n,\theta})\bigr)
 \leq K n^{-\nu/(4+2\nu)}. \tag{5}
\]
No eigenvalue has been divided by in this comparison.

We also need a rate for the expansion remainder because the later jitter vanishes.  The degenerate projections of order at least two have root-\(n\) mean square \(O(n^{-1})\), by the distinct-index covariance cancellation used in the preceding proof.  Moreover,
\[
 \mathbb E\|\widehat C-C\|^2\leq K/n,
 \qquad
 \Pr\{\|\widehat C-C\|>\kappa_0/2\}\leq K/n. \tag{6}
\]
On the complementary good event, the spectral-projector remainder is bounded by
\(K\sqrt n\|\widehat C-C\|^2\); the remaining nonlinear terms are bounded by \(K\sqrt n\) times a product of two root-mean-square \(O(n^{-1/2})\) estimation errors.  Cauchy--Schwarz, (6), and truncation by one therefore give
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}
 \mathbb E_\theta[1\wedge\|R_n\|]
 \leq K n^{-1/2}. \tag{7}
\]
Since \(\nu\leq1\), combining (5)--(7) gives
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}
 d_{\mathrm{BL}}\bigl(\mathcal L_\theta(H_n(\beta)),
 N(0,\Sigma_{n,\theta})\bigr)
 \leq K n^{-\nu/(4+2\nu)}. \tag{8}
\]

We next control the covariance of the estimated leave-one-out influences at a rate faster than the squared jitter scale.  For an order-\(s\) constituent kernel \(g\), denote its population first projection by
\[
 \psi_{g,e}(w)=s\{\mathbb E[g(w,W_{e,2},\ldots,W_{e,s})]-\mathbb Eg\}.
\]
For \(s\geq2\), conditional on \(W_{e,i}\), the first average in \(\widehat\psi_{g,e,i}\) is an order-\((s-1)\) \(U\)-statistic based on the remaining observations.  In its conditional variance, disjoint indexing tuples are conditionally independent, whereas only \(O(n_e^{2s-3})\) ordered pairs overlap, out of \(O(n_e^{2s-2})\) pairs.  The kernel second moments are uniformly bounded by the source moment and coefficient envelopes.  The same overlap count applies to \(U_{e,s}(g)\); for \(s=1\) the conditional-estimation term is absent.  After taking the fixed linear combinations and contrast signs, this proves
\[
 \mathbb E R_n^\psi\leq K/n,
 \qquad
 R_n^\psi=\frac1n\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}\sum_{i=1}^{n_e}\pi_e^{-2}
 \|\widehat\psi_{e,i}-\psi_{e,n}(W_{e,i})\|^2. \tag{9}
\]
Conditional Jensen applied to the leave-one-out averages and ordinary Jensen applied to the \(U\)-statistics also give uniform \((2+\nu)\)-moment bounds for both estimated and population projections, because their polynomial degree is at most \(R_{\max}\) and
\((2+\nu)R_{\max}\leq2R_{\max}+\delta\).

Put \(h_k=m_k-\beta c_k\), \(\widehat h_k=\widehat m_k-\beta\widehat c_k\), and
\[
 L_n^2=\|\widehat Q-Q_A\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2
 +\|\widehat C^\dagger-C^+\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2
 +\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}\|\widehat h_k-h_k\|^2. \tag{10}
\]
On \(\{\|\widehat C-C\|\leq\kappa_0/2\}\), singular-value perturbation and the inverse identity give
\(\|\widehat C^\dagger-C^+\|\leq K\|\widehat C-C\|\) and
\(\|\widehat Q-Q_A\|\leq K\|\widehat C-C\|\).  Off that event, the threshold in the definition gives \(\|\widehat C^\dagger\|\leq2/\kappa_0\), while both projectors have operator norm at most one; (6) controls the event.  Together with the sample-cumulant mean-square bounds, this yields
\[
 \mathbb E L_n^2\leq K/n,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E L_n\leq K n^{-1/2}. \tag{11}
\]

Let \(a=\nu/2\) and set
\[
 Y_n=1+\frac1n\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}\sum_{i=1}^{n_e}\pi_e^{-2}
 \{\|\psi_{e,n}(W_{e,i})\|^2+\|\widehat\psi_{e,i}\|^2\}.
\]
Convexity and the \((2+\nu)\)-moment bounds imply
\[
 \mathbb E Y_n^{1+a}\leq K. \tag{12}
\]
Expand \(\widehat\zeta_{e,i}(\beta)-\zeta_{e,n}(W_{e,i})\) and add and subtract one estimated factor at a time.  The bounds on \(C^+,\widehat C^\dagger,h_k\), together with empirical Cauchy--Schwarz, give the deterministic inequality
\[
 B_n:=\frac1n\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}\sum_{i=1}^{n_e}\pi_e^{-2}
 \|\widehat\zeta_{e,i}(\beta)-\zeta_{e,n}(W_{e,i})\|^2
 \leq K\{R_n^\psi+L_n^2Y_n\}. \tag{13}
\]
This inequality uses no independence between the global errors in \(L_n\) and the empirical envelope \(Y_n\).

Define
\[
 \widetilde\Sigma_n=\frac1n\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}\sum_{i=1}^{n_e}\pi_e^{-2}
 \zeta_{e,n}(W_{e,i})\zeta_{e,n}(W_{e,i})^\top
\]
and let \(\widehat\Sigma_n(\beta)\) be the analogous empirical covariance formed from \(\widehat\zeta_{e,i}(\beta)\).  Expanding the difference of the two outer products and applying empirical Cauchy--Schwarz with (13) gives
\[
 \|\widehat\Sigma_n(\beta)-\widetilde\Sigma_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \leq K\{(R_n^\psi Y_n)^{1/2}+L_nY_n
              +R_n^\psi+L_n^2Y_n\}. \tag{14}
\]
We now integrate (14) without an independence assumption.  For every \(t>0\), (9) and (12) imply
\[
 \mathbb E[1\wedge R_n^\psi Y_n]
 \leq t\mathbb ER_n^\psi+\Pr(Y_n>t)
 \leq K\{t/n+t^{-(1+a)}\}.
\]
Taking \(t=n^{1/(2+a)}\) and applying Jensen to the square root gives
\[
 \mathbb E[1\wedge(R_n^\psi Y_n)^{1/2}]
 \leq K n^{-\kappa},
 \qquad
 \kappa=\frac{1+a}{2(2+a)}. \tag{15}
\]
Similarly, (11)--(12), split over \(\{Y_n\leq t\}\), give
\[
 \mathbb E[1\wedge L_nY_n]
 \leq K\{tn^{-1/2}+t^{-(1+a)}\}
 \leq K n^{-\kappa}
\]
upon choosing \(t=n^{1/\{2(2+a)\}}\).  The remaining terms in (14) are faster: \(\mathbb ER_n^\psi\leq K/n\), and the choice \(t=n^{1/(2+a)}\) gives
\[
 \mathbb E[1\wedge L_n^2Y_n]
 \leq K n^{-(1+a)/(2+a)}.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathbb E_\theta\!\left[
 1\wedge\|\widehat\Sigma_n(\beta)-\widetilde\Sigma_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \right]
 \leq K n^{-\kappa}. \tag{16}
\]

For the fluctuation of \(\widetilde\Sigma_n\), each matrix
\(\zeta_{e,n}\zeta_{e,n}^\top\) has a uniformly bounded \((1+a)\)-moment by (3).  Truncate its norm at
\(\ell_n=n^{1/\{2(1+a)\}}\).  The truncation bias is at most \(K\ell_n^{-a}\).  Independence across observations, fixed dimension, and balanced shares bound the expected norm of the centered truncated average by
\[
 K n^{-1/2}\ell_n^{(1-a)/2},
\]
because \(x^2\mathbf 1\{x\leq\ell_n\}\leq\ell_n^{1-a}x^{1+a}\).  This term is no larger than the truncation bias.  Also,
\[
 \mathbb E\widetilde\Sigma_n
 =\frac1n\sum_e n_e\pi_e^{-2}
   \mathbb E[\zeta_{e,n}\zeta_{e,n}^\top]
 =\Sigma_{n,\theta}.
\]
It follows that
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathbb E_\theta
 \|\widetilde\Sigma_n-\Sigma_{n,\theta}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \leq K n^{-a/\{2(1+a)\}}
 =K n^{-\nu/(4+2\nu)}. \tag{17}
\]
By the definition of \(\varrho_{\mathrm{AR}}\),
\[
 2\varrho_{\mathrm{AR}}=\frac{a}{2(1+a)},
 \qquad
 \kappa-2\varrho_{\mathrm{AR}}
 =\frac{1}{2(2+a)(1+a)}>0. \tag{18}
\]
Combining (16)--(18), and recalling \(\tau_n=n^{-\chi}\) with
\(0<\chi<\varrho_{\mathrm{AR}}\), gives
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathbb E_\theta\!\left[
 1\wedge\|\widehat\Sigma_n(\beta)-\Sigma_{n,\theta}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \right]
 \leq K n^{-2\varrho_{\mathrm{AR}}}=o(\tau_n^2). \tag{19}
\]

Conditionally on the observations, \(G_n^{*(j)}(\beta)\) is exactly centered Gaussian with covariance \(\widehat\Sigma_n(\beta)\).  For positive semidefinite matrices \(S,T\), the integral representation
\[
 S^{1/2}=\frac1\pi\int_0^\infty t^{-1/2}S(S+tI)^{-1}\,dt
\]
and the resolvent identity, with the integral split at
\(t=\|S-T\|_{\mathrm{op}}\), give
\[
 \|S^{1/2}-T^{1/2}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \leq K\|S-T\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{1/2}. \tag{20}
\]
This calculation is valid for singular matrices because \(S+tI\) and \(T+tI\) are invertible for every \(t>0\).  Couple the conditional multiplier Gaussian and \(N(0,\Sigma_{n,\theta})\) as
\(\widehat\Sigma_n(\beta)^{1/2}Z\) and
\(\Sigma_{n,\theta}^{1/2}Z\), where \(Z\sim N(0,I_{D_{\mathrm{AR}}})\).  Equations (19)--(20), truncation by one, and Jensen give
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathbb E_\theta
 d_{\mathrm{BL}}\bigl(\mathcal L^*(G_n^{*(j)}(\beta)),
 N(0,\Sigma_{n,\theta})\bigr)
 \leq K n^{-\varrho_{\mathrm{AR}}}. \tag{21}
\]

It remains to remove all atomic discontinuities.  Let
\(G_{n,\theta}\sim N(0,\Sigma_{n,\theta})\), let
\(\xi\sim N(0,1)\) be independent, and define
\[
 F_{n,\theta}^{\circ}(t)
 =\Pr\{\|G_{n,\theta}\|_{\max}+\tau_n\xi\leq t\}.
\]
For each \(t\), the map
\[
 x\longmapsto
 \Phi\!\left(\frac{t-\|x\|_{\max}}{\tau_n}\right)
\]
is bounded by one and has Lipschitz constant at most
\((\sqrt{2\pi}\tau_n)^{-1}\).  Thus (8), (21), and
\(\chi<\varrho_{\mathrm{AR}}\) imply
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in\Theta,t\in\mathbb R}
 \left|\Pr_\theta\{V_n(\beta)\leq t\}
 -F_{n,\theta}^{\circ}(t)\right|
 \leq K n^{\chi-2\varrho_{\mathrm{AR}}}\longrightarrow0, \tag{22}
\]
and
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathbb E_\theta\sup_{t\in\mathbb R}
 \left|\Pr^*\{V_n^{*(j)}(\beta)\leq t\}
 -F_{n,\theta}^{\circ}(t)\right|
 \leq K n^{\chi-\varrho_{\mathrm{AR}}}\longrightarrow0. \tag{23}
\]
The ideal distribution is continuous and strictly increasing even when
\(\Sigma_{n,\theta}\) is singular or zero, because it has the everywhere positive density
\[
 t\longmapsto
 \mathbb E\!\left[\tau_n^{-1}\phi\!\left(
 \frac{t-\|G_{n,\theta}\|_{\max}}{\tau_n}\right)\right]. \tag{24}
\]

Condition on the observations and the observed-statistic jitter \(\xi_0\), and put
\[
 p_n^*(\beta)=\Pr^*\{V_n^{*(j)}(\beta)\geq V_n(\beta)\}.
\]
The multiplier jitter makes the conditional law continuous, so the weak inequality is immaterial.  Equation (23) gives, uniformly in probability,
\[
 p_n^*(\beta)
 =1-F_{n,\theta}^{\circ}(V_n(\beta))+o_{\mathbb P}(1). \tag{25}
\]
For any \(u\in(0,1)\), strict continuity of \(F_{n,\theta}^{\circ}\) and its inverse show from (22) that
\[
 \left|\Pr_\theta\{F_{n,\theta}^{\circ}(V_n(\beta))\leq u\}-u\right|
 \leq
 \sup_t\left|\Pr_\theta\{V_n(\beta)\leq t\}
                  -F_{n,\theta}^{\circ}(t)\right|.
\]
Hence (22) and (25), with an arbitrary vanishing deterministic tolerance, imply
\[
 p_n^*(\beta)\Longrightarrow\operatorname{Unif}(0,1)
 \quad\text{uniformly over }\Theta. \tag{26}
\]

Conditionally on the observations and \(\xi_0\), the \(m_n\) indicators defining
\(\widehat p_{n,m_n}(\beta)\) are independent Bernoulli variables with success probability \(p_n^*(\beta)\).  Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{Var}\!\left(
 \widehat p_{n,m_n}(\beta)\mid\text{observations},\xi_0
 \right)\leq\frac1{4m_n}. \tag{27}
\]
Chebyshev's inequality and \(m_n\to\infty\) make the Monte Carlo error uniformly \(o_{\mathbb P}(1)\).  Since \(\alpha_0\in(0,1/2)\) is a continuity point of the limiting uniform law, (26)--(27) give
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}
 \left|\Pr_\theta\{\widehat p_{n,m_n}(\beta)>\alpha_0\}
 -(1-\alpha_0)\right|\longrightarrow0. \tag{28}
\]
By definition, the event in (28) is exactly
\(\{\beta\in\mathcal C_n^{\mathrm{RJ}}\}\).  The probability integrates over the stratified observations, the observed jitter, all Gaussian multipliers, and all Monte Carlo jitters.  This proves the coverage assertion for the entire ambient class, including no-breakout laws and singular, rank-changing, or zero influence covariances.  Every ingredient of the construction is a finite function of the observed strata and the specified auxiliary standard normals, so the set is implementable.  Moreover \(\tau_n\to0\), so the added jitter vanishes on the root-\(n\) projected-moment scale.  The proof contains no diameter or expected-length bound, and therefore establishes no such optimality.

Finally, let a reference statistic have distribution function \(F\) with an atom at \(x_0\).  The deterministic rule accepting strictly below \(x_0\) has probability \(F(x_0-)\), while the deterministic rule accepting at or below \(x_0\) has probability \(F(x_0)\).  Under
\[
 F(x_0-)<1-\alpha_0<F(x_0),
\]
neither equals \(1-\alpha_0\).  This proves exactly the stated limitation on the two deterministic single-threshold conventions.  It does not imply a broader impossibility: for example, conditional randomization on the equality event with probability
\[
 \frac{1-\alpha_0-F(x_0-)}{F(x_0)-F(x_0-)}
\]
attains acceptance probability \(1-\alpha_0\).
