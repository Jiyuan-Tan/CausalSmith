%%% FIELD proof-environment-sharpness
The columns of \(C\) lie in \(\mathbb R^{E-1}\).  Hence the assumption \(\operatorname{rank}(C)=d\) first gives \(d\leq E-1\).  By the definitions of \(A\) and \(Q_A\), the latter is the orthogonal projector onto \(A^\perp\), and
\[
 \dim(A^\perp)=(E-1)-d.
\]
If a finite breakout occurs, then \(Q_Ac_k\neq0\) for some \(k\geq d+2\).  Thus \(A^\perp\neq\{0\}\), so \((E-1)-d\geq1\), or equivalently \(E\geq d+2\).  At the boundary \(E=d+1\), full rank gives
\[
 A=\operatorname{col}(C)=\mathbb R^d,
 \qquad
 Q_A=0,
\]
which rules out every finite breakout.

It remains to prove that this dimension requirement is attainable uniformly over all prescribed breakout orders.  Fix \(d\geq1\), \(E\geq d+2\), and \(R_{\max}\geq d+2\).  For every \(s\in\{d+2,\ldots,R_{\max}\}\) and \(1\leq j\leq s\), define
\[
 \nu_{s j}=(-1)^{s-j}{s\choose j}.
\]
The binomial identity
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^{s}(-1)^{s-j}{s\choose j}e^{jt}=(e^t-1)^s
\]
and differentiation at \(t=0\) imply, for integer \(k\geq1\),
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^{s}\nu_{s j}j^k=0\quad(1\leq k<s),
 \qquad
 \sum_{j=1}^{s}\nu_{s j}j^s=s!.
 \tag{2}
\]
Indeed, the omitted \(j=0\) term has zero \(k\)-th derivative for \(k\geq1\), while \((e^t-1)^s\) has a zero of order \(s\) at zero and its \(s\)-th derivative there is \(s!\).  This is exactly the alternating-intensity component of the delayed-order compound-Poisson handle.

Let
\[
 K=\max_{d+2\leq s\leq R_{\max}}\max_{1\leq j\leq s}|\nu_{s j}|,
\]
choose fixed \(L>0\) and \(\eta>0\), and choose one fixed \(\varepsilon\) with
\[
 0<\varepsilon<\frac{L}{2K}.
 \tag{3}
\]
For nonnegative intensities \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_J\), write
\[
 X(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_J)
 =\sum_{j=1}^J j(N_j-\lambda_j),
 \qquad N_j\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda_j)
\]
with the \(N_j\)'s independent.  This variable is centered, and its complex moment generating function satisfies
\[
 \mathbb E e^{zX}
 =\exp\!\left\{\sum_{j=1}^J\lambda_j(e^{jz}-1-jz)\right\}.
 \tag{4}
\]
Thus it has an entire moment generating function, and differentiation of the logarithm in (4) gives
\[
 \kappa_k(X)=\sum_{j=1}^J\lambda_jj^k,
 \qquad k\geq2.
 \tag{5}
\]

Fix a desired \(r\in\{d+2,\ldots,R_{\max}\}\).  In environment \(e\), construct the latent noise as a centered compound-Poisson variable with jumps \(1,\ldots,d\) and intensities
\[
 \lambda^{u}_{e,j}=L+\eta\mathbf 1\{e=j\},
 \qquad 1\leq j\leq d.
 \tag{6}
\]
Construct the treatment noise as a centered compound-Poisson variable with jumps \(1,\ldots,R_{\max}\) and intensities
\[
 \lambda^{t,r}_{e,j}
 =L+\varepsilon\nu_{rj}\mathbf 1\{e=d+1\}\mathbf 1\{j\leq r\},
 \qquad 1\leq j\leq R_{\max}.
 \tag{7}
\]
The coordinate \(e=d+1\) exists because \(E\geq d+2\).  Equations (3) and (7) give \(\lambda^{t,r}_{e,j}>L/2>0\), so every displayed compound-Poisson law is legal.  Take all source variables mutually independent within each environment, take the invariant \(\epsilon_y\) to be standard Gaussian and independent of the other sources, and set
\[
 \alpha=1,
 \qquad \beta=0,
 \qquad \gamma=1.
 \tag{8}
\]
The sources are centered by construction, and (4), together with the Gaussian moment generating function, shows that all of them have entire moment generating functions.

Let \(\mathbf e_j\) denote the \(j\)-th coordinate vector of \(\mathbb R^{E-1}\).  From (5)--(7), the latent cumulant contrast and the treatment-noise cumulant contrast are respectively
\[
 u_k=\eta(1^k,2^k,\ldots,d^k,0,\ldots,0)^\top
\]
and
\[
 v_k
 =\varepsilon\left(\sum_{j=1}^{r}\nu_{rj}j^k\right)\mathbf e_{d+1}.
 \tag{9}
\]
Independence of \(U_e\) and \(\epsilon_{t,e}\), the equation \(T_e=U_e+\epsilon_{t,e}\) from (8), and additivity of log moment generating functions yield additivity of their cumulants.  Consequently
\[
 c_k=u_k+v_k.
 \tag{10}
\]
For \(2\leq k\leq d+1\), we have \(k<r\), so (2) makes \(v_k=0\).  Therefore, with
\[
 V_d=(i^{\ell+1})_{1\leq i,\ell\leq d},
\]
the calibration matrix is
\[
 C=
 \begin{bmatrix}
  \eta V_d\\
  0_{(E-1-d)\times d}
 \end{bmatrix}.
 \tag{11}
\]
To verify that \(V_d\) is nonsingular, suppose \(V_dx=0\) and put \(q(z)=\sum_{\ell=1}^d x_\ell z^{\ell-1}\).  For every \(i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\), equation \(V_dx=0\) gives \(i^2q(i)=0\), hence \(q(i)=0\).  Repeated application of the factor theorem shows that a polynomial of degree at most \(d-1\) with these \(d\) distinct roots is zero.  Thus \(x=0\), proving that \(V_d\) is nonsingular.

It follows from (11) that
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(C)=d,
 \qquad
 A=\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf e_1,\ldots,\mathbf e_d\},
 \qquad
 \sigma_d(C)=\eta\sigma_d(V_d)>0.
 \tag{12}
\]
In particular, (9) shows directly that \(u_k\in A\) for every integer \(k\geq2\), as required by the latent-profile-plane assumption.  For \(d+2\leq k<r\), equations (2), (9), and (10) give \(Q_Ac_k=0\).  At order \(r\), the same equations give
\[
 Q_Ac_r
 =\varepsilon r!\,\mathbf e_{d+1}\neq0.
 \tag{13}
\]
The definition of the first breakout now yields \(r^\star=r\).

Finally, all constants can be chosen once, independently of \(r\).  The values \(L\), \(\eta\), and \(\varepsilon\) were fixed using only \(d\) and \(R_{\max}\), and the calibration matrix (11) does not depend on \(r\).  Thus one may take
\[
 \kappa_0=\frac12\eta\sigma_d(V_d)>0.
\]
There are only finitely many candidate orders, environments, and source laws in (6)--(8).  Each has finite absolute moments of every positive order, so
\[
 M
 =1+\max_{\substack{d+2\leq r\leq R_{\max}\,,\ e\in\mathcal E\\
 X\in\{\epsilon_{u,e},\epsilon^{(r)}_{t,e},\epsilon_y\}}}
 \mathbb E|X|^{2R_{\max}+\delta}
 <\infty
 \tag{14}
\]
is one common moment envelope.  For example, the one-time choices
\[
 B=1,
 \qquad a_0=\frac12,
 \qquad a_1=2,
 \qquad q_0=\frac12,
 \qquad q_1=2
\]
contain the coefficients in (8) and satisfy all coefficient inequalities.  Equations (3), (12), and (14) verify positivity, the fixed singular gap, and the common moment bound.  Hence the same collection of bounds admits, for every prescribed \(r\), centered independent entire-moment-generating-function sources with \(r^\star=r\).  Together with the dimension obstruction, this proves the claimed sharp threshold.

%%% FIELD proof-two-environment-reduction
Assume \(d=1\), \(E=2\), and \(\operatorname{rank}(C)=1\).  Then \(E=d+1\), so the boundary conclusion of the environment-sharpness theorem gives
\[
 A=\mathbb R,
 \qquad
 Q_A=0.
\]
Equivalently, \(C=[c_2]\) is a one-by-one matrix with \(c_2\neq0\), and the projector formula gives
\[
 Q_A
 =1-c_2(c_2^2)^{-1}c_2
 =0.
\]
It follows that \(Q_Ac_k=0\) for every \(k\geq3=d+2\).  By the convention in the definition of \(r^\star\), every calibrated parameter at this boundary has \(r^\star=\infty\).  The definition
\[
 \mathcal B=\{\theta\in\Theta:d+2\leq r^\star\leq R_{\max}\}
\]
therefore implies \(\mathcal B=\varnothing\).

Now fix \(\theta\in\mathcal N_\rho\).  Write primes for the structural quantities under \(S\theta\).  The source-swap definition gives, in every environment,
\[
 \alpha'=1,
 \quad
 \beta'=\beta+\frac{\gamma}{\alpha},
 \quad
 \gamma'=-\frac{\gamma}{\alpha},
 \quad
 \epsilon'_{u,e}=\epsilon_{t,e},
 \quad
 \epsilon'_{t,e}=\alpha\epsilon_{u,e}.
\]
Couple the two representations using these displayed source variables.  Their treatments then agree pointwise:
\[
 T'_e
 =\alpha'\epsilon'_{u,e}+\epsilon'_{t,e}
 =\epsilon_{t,e}+\alpha\epsilon_{u,e}
 =T_e.
 \tag{15}
\]
Using \(\epsilon_{t,e}=T_e-\alpha U_e\), their outcomes also agree pointwise:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Y'_e
 &=\left(\beta+\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}\right)T'_e
   -\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}\epsilon_{t,e}+\epsilon_y\\
 &=\left(\beta+\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}\right)T_e
   -\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}(T_e-\alpha U_e)+\epsilon_y\\
 &=\beta T_e+\gamma U_e+\epsilon_y
 =Y_e.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{16}
\]
Equations (15)--(16) imply
\[
 P_e^{\theta}=P_e^{S\theta}
 \qquad(e\in\mathcal E).
\]
The two direct effects are \(\beta\) and \(\beta+\gamma/\alpha\).  The no-breakout clause of the already-established population-identification-frontier theorem applies to \(\theta\in\mathcal N_\rho\): it places both \(\theta\) and \(S\theta\) in \(\mathcal D_\rho\) and states that these effects are distinct.  Concretely, the distinction follows from the ambient lower confounding bound \(|\gamma/\alpha|\geq q_0>0\), which is among that theorem's declared hypotheses.  Thus every element of a nonempty \(\mathcal N_\rho\) has a legal observationally equivalent partner with a different direct effect.  For \(d=1\) and \(E=2\), this is precisely the simultaneous-noise nonidentification configuration referenced in Kivva et al., Theorem 3.6; the reference is a scope comparison, while (15)--(16) establish the claim directly.
