Fix \(\theta\in\mathcal N_\rho\), and write \(\theta'=S\theta\), \(b_0=\beta(\theta)\), and \(b_1=\beta(\theta')\).  By \(\text{thm:population-identification-frontier}\) and the definition of \(\mathcal N_\rho\), both \(\theta\) and \(\theta'\) belong to \(\mathcal D_\rho\), their observed environment laws agree, and
\[
 P_e^{\theta}=P_e^{\theta'}\quad(e\in\mathcal E),
 \qquad
 b_1=b_0+\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}.
 \tag{1}
\]
The quotient in (1) is well defined: the treatment-loading condition used by \(\text{thm:population-identification-frontier}\) gives \(a_0\leq |\alpha|\), while \(a_0>0\), hence \(\alpha\neq0\).  Moreover, the confounding bound gives
\[
 \left|b_1-b_0\right|=\left|\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}\right|\geq q_0>0,
 \tag{2}
\]
so the two effects are distinct.

Let \(\mathbb P\) denote the common law of the observed sample, together with any parameter-independent auxiliary randomization used to construct \(\mathcal C_n\).  Such a common law exists because (1) gives equality of every environment-specific observed law, and the sampling design is the same at \(\theta\) and \(\theta'\).  Define the measurable events
\[
 H_0=\{b_0\in\mathcal C_n\},
 \qquad
 H_1=\{b_1\in\mathcal C_n\}.
\]
Coverage at least \(1-\alpha_0\) at each of the two parameters, evaluated under their common observed-data law, says
\[
 \mathbb P(H_0)\geq1-\alpha_0,
 \qquad
 \mathbb P(H_1)\geq1-\alpha_0.
 \tag{3}
\]
Therefore the union bound applied to the complements in (3) yields
\[
 \mathbb P(H_0\cap H_1)
 =1-\mathbb P(H_0^c\cup H_1^c)
 \geq1-\mathbb P(H_0^c)-\mathbb P(H_1^c)
 \geq1-2\alpha_0.
 \tag{4}
\]
On \(H_0\cap H_1\), the interval \(\mathcal C_n\) contains both \(b_0\) and \(b_1\).  Hence, by the defining order property of an interval and (1),
\[
 \operatorname{length}(\mathcal C_n)
 \geq |b_1-b_0|
 =\left|\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}\right|.
 \tag{5}
\]
Since interval length is nonnegative, multiplying (5) by \(\mathbf 1_{H_0\cap H_1}\), taking expectation under \(\theta\), and then using (4) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_\theta\!\left[\operatorname{length}(\mathcal C_n)\right]
 &\geq
 \mathbb E_\theta\!\left[
   \operatorname{length}(\mathcal C_n)\mathbf 1_{H_0\cap H_1}
 \right]\\
 &\geq
 \left|\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}\right|
 \mathbb P(H_0\cap H_1)\\
 &\geq
 (1-2\alpha_0)\left|\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}\right|.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Finally, \(\alpha_0\in(0,1/2)\) ensures that the displayed lower bound is nonnegative and nonvacuous; by (2), it is strictly positive.
