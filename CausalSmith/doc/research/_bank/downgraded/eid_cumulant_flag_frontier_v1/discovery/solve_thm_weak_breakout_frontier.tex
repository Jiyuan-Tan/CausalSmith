%%% FIELD proof-weak-breakout-frontier
The structural causal target is the coefficient \(\beta\): under the intervention that sets treatment to \(t\), the structural equation gives
\[
Y_e(t)=\beta t+\gamma U_e+\epsilon_y,
\]
so the unit change in the interventional outcome holding the exogenous variables fixed is \(\beta\).  This is distinct from the observable cumulant quotient.  The already established cumulant-cancellation lemma and identification-frontier theorem prove that, on a finite-breakout law, the quotient equals this structural target.  We now establish the statistical assertions.

Put \(s_0=2R_{\max}+\delta\).  From the treatment-loading, confounding, effect, and source-moment bounds,
\[
 |\gamma|=|\alpha|\,|\gamma/\alpha|\leq a_1q_1,
\]
and Minkowski's inequality in \(L^{s_0}\), applied to the two structural equations, gives a finite constant \(K_0\), depending only on the fixed model bounds, such that
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\max_{e\in\mathcal E}
 \mathbb E_\theta\!left[|T_e|^{s_0}+|Y_e|^{s_0}\right]\leq K_0. \tag{1}
\]
Here the division in \(\gamma/\alpha\) is licensed by \(|\alpha|\geq a_0>0\).  In particular, all moments used below exist uniformly.

We first verify the stochastic accuracy of the stipulated cumulant statistics.  If \(X_1,\ldots,X_k\) are coordinates, each equal to \(T_e\) or \(Y_e\), coefficient comparison in the finite-degree part of the formal identity \(\log(1+x)=\sum_{j\geq1}(-1)^{j-1}x^j/j\), applied to the joint moment generating function, yields
\[
 \operatorname{cum}(X_1,\ldots,X_k)
 =\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak P_k}(|\pi|-1)!(-1)^{|\pi|-1}
   \prod_{D\in\pi}\mathbb E\!\left[\prod_{j\in D}X_j\right], \tag{2}
\]
where \(\mathfrak P_k\) is the finite set of partitions of \(\{1,\ldots,k\}\).  For \(\pi=\{D_1,\ldots,D_s\}\), an unbiased estimator of the product in (2) is
\[
 \frac{1}{(n_e)_s}
 \sum_{i_1,\ldots,i_s\ {m distinct}}
 \prod_{a=1}^s\prod_{j\in D_a}X_{j,e,i_a}. \tag{3}
\]
Indeed, independence of distinct observations makes the expectation of every summand in (3) equal to the corresponding product of moments.  Symmetrizing the finite sum of (3) over the partitions in (2) gives exactly an admissible symmetrized unbiased cumulant U-statistic from the estimator definition.

The square of a summand in (3) has total degree at most \(2k\leq2R_{\max}<s_0\).  Thus (1), Lyapunov's inequality, and H\"older's inequality bound its second moment uniformly.  In the variance double sum, two summands based on disjoint observation-index sets are independent and have zero covariance.  For a fixed \(s\), only \(O(n_e^{2s-1})\) of the \(O(n_e^{2s})\) ordered pairs overlap.  Since the number of partitions, environments, and admitted orders is fixed, the balanced-share condition \(n_e\geq\underline\pi n\) gives a constant \(K_1<\infty\) for which, whenever all \(n_e\geq R_{\max}\),
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}
 \mathbb E_\theta\!\left[
 \|\widehat c_k-c_k\|_2^2+\|\widehat m_k-m_k\|_2^2
 \right]\leq \frac{K_1}{n},
 \qquad 2\leq k\leq R_{\max}. \tag{4}
\]
The same finite polynomial formula (2), together with (1), also supplies uniform deterministic bounds on \(\|c_k\|_2\) and \(\|m_k\|_2\).

Let \(H=\widehat C-C\), and let \(\widehat A\) be the rank-\(d\) left singular subspace used to define \(\widehat Q\).  On the event \(\|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\kappa_0/2\), if \(x=Cy\) is a unit vector in \(A\), then \(\|y\|_2\leq\kappa_0^{-1}\) and
\[
 \operatorname{dist}(x,\widehat A)
 \leq\|Hy\|_2\leq\kappa_0^{-1}\|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}. \tag{5}
\]
Moreover, directly from the variational definition of a singular value,
\[
 \sigma_d(\widehat C)
 \geq\sigma_d(C)-\|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \geq\kappa_0/2. \tag{6}
\]
Consequently, if \(z=\widehat Cy\) is a unit vector in \(\widehat A\), then \(\|y\|_2\leq2/\kappa_0\) and
\[
 \operatorname{dist}(z,A)\leq2\kappa_0^{-1}\|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}. \tag{7}
\]
For completeness, decomposing a vector into its projections on two equal-dimensional subspaces bounds the operator norm of the difference of their orthogonal projectors by the sum of the two directed distances in (5) and (7).  Since \(Q_A\) and \(\widehat Q\) are the complementary projectors, (5)--(7) imply the convenient bound
\[
 \|\widehat Q-Q_A\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \leq\frac{4}{\kappa_0}\|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}. \tag{8}
\]
On the complementary event, the left side is at most two.  Equation (4), applied to the fixed \(d\) columns of \(H\), and Markov's inequality therefore give
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}
 \mathbb E_\theta\|\widehat Q-Q_A\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2
 \leq\frac{K_2}{n} \tag{9}
\]
for another uniform constant \(K_2\).  This argument does not invert an influence covariance and remains valid when that covariance is singular.

For \(|b|\leq B\), define the proof intermediates
\[
 g_k(b)=Q_A(m_k-bc_k),\qquad
 \widehat g_k(b)=\widehat Q(\widehat m_k-b\widehat c_k),
\]
and
\[
 \Delta_n=\max_{d+2\leq k\leq R_{\max}}
 \sup_{|b|\leq B}\|\widehat g_k(b)-g_k(b)\|_2.
\]
The exact decomposition
\[
 \widehat g_k(b)-g_k(b)
 =\widehat Q\{(\widehat m_k-m_k)-b(\widehat c_k-c_k)\}
  +(\widehat Q-Q_A)(m_k-bc_k) \tag{10}
\]
combined with (4), (9), the population bounds following (4), and the finiteness of the admitted order set yields
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathbb E_\theta\Delta_n^2\leq\frac{K_3}{n}. \tag{11}
\]
Thus (11) includes the generated-projector error rather than treating the plane as known.

By the cumulant-cancellation lemma, for every \(\theta\in\mathcal D_\rho\),
\[
 g_k(b)=(\beta-b)Q_Ac_k,
 \qquad g_k(\beta)=0. \tag{12}
\]
This is the step relating the observable moments to the causal target.  If \(\theta\in\mathcal M_r(\lambda_n)\), the defining optimization and (10)--(12) imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |\widehat\beta-\beta|\,\|Q_Ac_r\|_2
 &=\|g_r(\widehat\beta)\|_2\\
 &\leq \|\widehat g_r(\widehat\beta)\|_2+\Delta_n\\
 &\leq \max_k\|\widehat g_k(\widehat\beta)\|_2+\Delta_n\\
 &\leq \max_k\|\widehat g_k(\beta)\|_2+\Delta_n
 \leq2\Delta_n.
 \end{aligned} \tag{13}
\]
The minimizer exists because the objective is continuous on the compact interval \([-B,B]\).  Since the shell has \(\|Q_Ac_r\|_2\geq\lambda_n\), while both \(\widehat\beta\) and \(\beta\) lie in \([-B,B]\), (11)--(13) give
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in\mathcal M_r(\lambda_n)}
 \mathbb E_\theta(\widehat\beta-\beta)^2
 \leq K_4\min\!\left\{B^2,\frac{1}{n\lambda_n^2}\right\}. \tag{14}
\]

Choose the deterministic radius in the Anderson--Rubin definition so that
\[
 t_{\alpha_0}^2\geq K_3/\alpha_0. \tag{15}
\]
Let \(\mathcal R_n\) denote the raw norm-sublevel set appearing in that definition.  On \(\{\Delta_n\leq t_{\alpha_0}/\sqrt n\}\), (12) puts \(\beta\) in \(\mathcal R_n\).  Equations (11), (15), and Markov's inequality therefore imply
\[
 \inf_{\theta\in\mathcal D_\rho}
 \Pr_\theta\{\beta\in\mathcal C_n\}\geq1-\alpha_0. \tag{16}
\]
Indeed, on this event the raw set cannot be empty, so the singleton fallback does not affect coverage.  The small-sample fallback \([-B,B]\) covers identically.

Every constraint defining \(\mathcal R_n\) is a convex sublevel set of the norm of an affine function of the scalar \(b\); hence their intersection with \([-B,B]\) is an interval.  If \(b,b'\in\mathcal R_n\), then (10)--(12) and the shell lower bound give
\[
 |b-b'|\lambda_n
 \leq\|g_r(b)-g_r(b')\|_2
 \leq2\left(\frac{t_{\alpha_0}}{\sqrt n}+\Delta_n\right). \tag{17}
\]
If \(\mathcal R_n\) is empty, the prescribed fallback is a singleton and has length zero.  Thus the box bound, (11), (17), and Cauchy--Schwarz yield
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in\mathcal M_r(\lambda_n)}
 \mathbb E_\theta\operatorname{length}(\mathcal C_n)
 \leq K_5\min\!\left\{B,\frac{1}{\sqrt n\lambda_n}\right\}. \tag{18}
\]
Both (13) and (17) use the true \(r\) only in the analysis; the estimator and interval maximize over all admitted orders and therefore do not require knowledge of \(r\).

It remains only to cover the finitely many sample sizes at which some \(n_e<R_{\max}\).  Balanced shares imply that this can occur only when \(n<R_{\max}/\underline\pi\).  Formula (2) and (1) give a uniform constant \(\Lambda<\infty\) with \(\|Q_Ac_k\|_2\leq\Lambda\).  The deterministic error and length bounds \(B^2\) and \(2B\), together with \(\lambda_n\leq\Lambda\) for a nonempty shell, show that increasing \(K_4,K_5\) makes (14) and (18) valid also for these finitely many \(n\).

We next construct one fixed compatible experiment giving both lower bounds, uniformly over the finite set of possible \(r\).  Fix arbitrary \(d\geq1\), \(E\geq d+2\), and \(R_{\max}\geq d+2\).  Set
\[
 J=[19/10,23/10],\qquad
 \Sigma=\begin{pmatrix}2&5\\5&14\end{pmatrix},
\]
and, for \(b\in J\), define
\[
 f_b=\frac{5-2b}{3-b},\qquad
 g_b=\frac{1}{3-b},\qquad z_b=b-1. \tag{19}
\]
All denominators and all three quantities in (19) are bounded away from zero on \(J\).  Let \(V_b,W_b,Z_b\) be independent centered Gaussian variables with variances \(f_b,g_b,z_b\), respectively.  Direct algebra gives
\[
 f_b+g_b=2,\qquad 3f_b+bg_b=5,\qquad
 9f_b+b^2g_b+z_b=14, \tag{20}
\]
and hence
\[
 G_b=(V_b+W_b,\,3V_b+bW_b+Z_b)\sim\mathcal N(0,\Sigma) \tag{21}
\]
for every \(b\in J\).

Independently, construct
\[
 L_e=\sum_{j=1}^d j(N_{e,j}-\lambda_{e,j}),
\]
where the independent Poisson rates have a common positive baseline and the jump-\(j\) rate is increased by a fixed \(\eta>0\) only in environment \(e=j\); environments \(e>d\) retain the baseline rates.  Define the structural sources and coefficients by
\[
 \epsilon_{u,e}=L_e+V_b,\qquad
 \epsilon_{t,e}=W_b,\qquad
 \epsilon_y=Z_b,qquad
 \alpha=1,\qquad \beta=b,\qquad \gamma=3-b. \tag{22}
\]
Before the perturbation introduced below, the observed variables satisfy
\[
 (T_e,Y_e)=(L_e,3L_e)+G_b, \tag{23}
\]
so their entire joint law is independent of \(b\), not merely their covariance.  The latent cumulant-shift vector at order \(k\) is
\[
 u_k=\eta(1^k,\ldots,d^k,0,\ldots,0)^\top,
\]
because the added Gaussian component is invariant across environments.  Consequently the calibration matrix has leading block \(\eta V_d\), its column space is
\(A=\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf e_1,\ldots,\mathbf e_d\}\), and \(u_k\in A\) at every order.

For an admitted \(r\), let \(\operatorname{He}_r\) be the probabilists' Hermite polynomial and set
\[
 h_r(x)=e^{-x^2}\operatorname{He}_r(\sqrt3x),
 \qquad \mu_r=\frac{r!}{3^{(r+1)/2}}. \tag{24}
\]
Both \(h_r\) and its derivative are bounded.  If \(N\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\), the substitution \(u=\sqrt3x\) and the Rodrigues identity, followed by \(r\) integrations by parts, give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E[N^kh_r(N)]
 &=3^{-(k+1)/2}\mathbb E[N^k\operatorname{He}_r(N)]\\
 &=\begin{cases}
 0,&0\leq k<r,\\
 \mu_r,&k=r.
 \end{cases}
 \end{aligned} \tag{25}
\]
Every boundary term is zero because a polynomial times a Gaussian density tends to zero at both endpoints.

Only in the spare environment \(e=d+1\), replace the density of \(W_b\) by
\[
 p_{b,\lambda}(w)=\phi_{g_b}(w)
 \left\{1+\frac{\lambda}{\mu_rg_b^{r/2}}
 h_r\!\left(\frac{w}{\sqrt{g_b}}\right)\right\}. \tag{26}
\]
Because \(J\) is compact and the admitted set of \(r\)'s is finite, there is one \(\lambda_0>0\), common to all \(r\), such that the bracket in (26) lies in \([1/2,3/2]\) whenever \(0<\lambda\leq\lambda_0\).  Equation (25), first with \(k=0\) and then with \(k=1\), proves respectively that (26) integrates to one and remains centered.  More generally, (25) shows that all raw moments below order \(r\) are unchanged and that the order-\(r\) raw moment increases by exactly \(\lambda\).  Since the order-\(r\) cumulant is the order-\(r\) moment plus a polynomial in lower moments, (2) proves that the order-\(r\) cumulant also increases by exactly \(\lambda\).  Gaussian domination of (26) gives an entire moment generating function and uniform moments of every fixed order.

Because \(r\geq d+2\), this perturbation leaves every column of \(C\) unchanged.  The lower-order projected contrasts vanish, whereas at order \(r\),
\[
 Q_Ac_r=\lambda\mathbf e_{d+1},\qquad
 Q_Am_r=b\lambda\mathbf e_{d+1}. \tag{27}
\]
The second equality follows either directly from multilinearity and independence of the structural sources, or from cumulant cancellation.  Choose once and for all \(B>23/10\), coefficient bounds containing \(\alpha=1\) and \(\gamma=3-b\) for \(b\in J\), a gap \(\kappa_0<\eta\sigma_d(V_d)\), and a moment envelope larger than the compact supremum over \(b\in J\), \(0\leq\lambda\leq\lambda_0\), and the finite set of admitted \(r\)'s.  The Poisson and Gaussian sources are centered, mutually independent, and have entire moment generating functions.  Thus all ambient-class assumptions, including the latent-plane and calibration conditions, hold with fixed constants, and (27) places every resulting law in \(\mathcal M_r(\lambda)\).  This constructs one fixed compatible \(\Theta\), common to all \(r\).

We bound the divergence of the full observed-data laws, so the lower bound is not an artifact of moment compression.  Under the unperturbed Gaussian law (21), Gaussian completion of squares gives
\[
 \mathbb E_b[W_b\mid G_b=x]=\ell_b^\top x,
 \qquad \ell_b^\top=g_b(1,b)\Sigma^{-1}, \tag{28}
\]
and
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \tau_b^2
 &=\operatorname{Var}_b(W_b\mid G_b)\\
 &=g_b-g_b^2(1,b)\Sigma^{-1}(1,b)^\top
 =-\frac{(b-1)(2b-5)}{3(b-3)^2}>0.
 \end{aligned} \tag{29}
\]
The strict positivity in (29) is uniform on \(J\).  Conditioning the likelihood ratio in (26) on \(G_b=x\) shows that the perturbed density of \(G_b\) is
\[
 \phi_\Sigma(x)\{1+\lambda R_b(x)\},
\]
where
\[
 R_b(x)=\frac{1}{\mu_rg_b^{r/2}}
 \mathbb E\!\left[
 h_r\!\left(\frac{\ell_b^\top x+\tau_bN}{\sqrt{g_b}}\right)
 \right]. \tag{30}
\]
The coefficient functions in (28)--(30) are continuously differentiable on \(J\), and \(g_b,\tau_b\) are bounded away from zero.  Boundedness of \(h_r,h_r'\) permits differentiation under the standard-normal expectation, with dominating variable \(K(1+\|x\|_2+|N|)\).  Uniformly over the finite set of \(r\)'s,
\[
 |R_b(x)|\leq K_6,
 \qquad |\partial_bR_b(x)|\leq K_7(1+\|x\|_2). \tag{31}
\]
The mean-value theorem and the finite second moment of \(\mathcal N(0,\Sigma)\) now imply
\[
 \int\{R_b(x)-R_{b'}(x)\}^2\phi_\Sigma(x)\,dx
 \leq K_8|b-b'|^2. \tag{32}
\]
After decreasing \(\lambda_0\) so that \(\lambda_0K_6\leq1/2\), direct substitution of the two densities into the chi-square divergence gives
\[
 \chi^2(P^G_{b,\lambda},P^G_{b',\lambda})
 \leq2K_8\lambda^2|b-b'|^2. \tag{33}
\]
Adding the independent \(L_e\) and applying the common measurable map in (23) cannot increase chi-square divergence: the image likelihood ratio is the conditional expectation of the original likelihood ratio, so conditional Jensen's inequality applies to its squared centered value.  All environments other than \(d+1\) have a law independent of \(b\).  Independence across observations and \(1+x\leq e^x\) therefore yield
\[
 1+\chi^2(P_{b,\lambda}^{,n},P_{b',\lambda}^{,n})
 \leq\exp\{K_9n\lambda^2|b-b'|^2\}. \tag{34}
\]
For any two dominated laws, Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 \|P-Q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 =\frac12\int\left|\frac{dP}{dQ}-1\right|dQ
 \leq\frac12\sqrt{\chi^2(P,Q)}. \tag{35}
\]

Fix \(b_0=2\), and put
\[
 h_n=c\min\!\left\{B,\frac{1}{\sqrt n\lambda}\right\},
 \qquad b_1=b_0+h_n. \tag{36}
\]
Choose the fixed \(c>0\) small enough that \(b_1\in J\) and that (34)--(35) imply
\[
 \|P_{b_0,\lambda}^{,n}-P_{b_1,\lambda}^{,n}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \leq v<\min\{1/2,1-2\alpha_0\} \tag{37}
\]
for all \(0<\lambda\leq\lambda_0\).  For an arbitrary estimator, let \(D\) be the event on which it is closer to \(b_0\) than to \(b_1\).  On \(D^c\) its squared error under the first law is at least \(h_n^2/4\), and on \(D\) its squared error under the second law is at least \(h_n^2/4\).  The definition of total variation then gives
\[
 \max_{j=0,1}\mathbb E_{b_j,\lambda}
 (\widetilde\beta-b_j)^2
 \geq\frac{h_n^2}{8}(1-v). \tag{38}
\]
If a random interval \(\mathcal I_n\) covers each parameter with probability at least \(1-\alpha_0\), then under the first law the events \(\{b_0\in\mathcal I_n\}\) and \(\{b_1\in\mathcal I_n\}\) have intersection probability at least \(1-2\alpha_0-v\).  On their intersection, interval connectedness forces length at least \(h_n\).  Hence
\[
 \mathbb E_{b_0,\lambda}\operatorname{length}(\mathcal I_n)
 \geq h_n(1-2\alpha_0-v). \tag{39}
\]
Both laws in (38)--(39) belong to the same shell, so these bounds apply even when honesty is required only on that shell.

For signals bounded away from zero, the same fixed \(\Theta\) contains a standard parametric path.  Indeed, (1)--(2) give the fixed upper bound \(\Lambda\) already used above, so a nonempty shell must have \(\lambda\leq\Lambda\).  If \(\lambda_0\leq\lambda\leq\Lambda\), take a member of the nonempty shell, retain its \(\alpha\), latent sources, and treatment sources, choose a fixed \(q\in(q_0,q_1)\), put \(\gamma=\alpha q\), take an independent centered Gaussian \(\epsilon_y\) with a fixed sufficiently small positive variance \(\sigma_y^2\), and vary \(\beta\) over a fixed interior subinterval of \([-B,B]\).  These changes leave \(C,A,r^\star\), and the shell strength unchanged and preserve every ambient-class assumption.  In the more informative experiment observing \((U,T,Y)\), two effects separated by \(h\) have
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_\beta^n,P_{\beta+h}^n)
 =\frac{h^2}{2\sigma_y^2}\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}n_e\mathbb E[T_e^2]
 \leq K_{10}nh^2. \tag{40}
\]
Marginalizing \(U\) can only decrease relative entropy, again by conditional Jensen's inequality applied to the image likelihood ratio.  To relate (40) to total variation without an additional premise, apply data processing to the binary indicator of an event attaining total variation.  The resulting binary relative entropy, as a function of its first success probability, has second derivative at least four; Taylor's theorem gives
\(\operatorname{KL}(P,Q)\geq2\|P-Q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}^2\).  Taking \(h=c\min\{B,n^{-1/2}\}\) with \(c\) small and repeating (38)--(39) proves lower bounds of orders
\[
 \min\{B^2,n^{-1}\}
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \min\{B,n^{-1/2}\}. \tag{41}
\]
Because \(\lambda\in[\lambda_0,\Lambda]\), the two expressions in (41) are, up to constants depending only on the fixed experiment, respectively equivalent to
\[
 \min\!\left\{B^2,\frac{1}{n\lambda^2}\right\}
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \min\!\left\{B,\frac{1}{\sqrt n\lambda}\right\}. \tag{42}
\]

Taking the infimum in (38) over all estimators and the infimum in (39) over all intervals honest on the shell, and combining the resulting lower bounds with (14) and (18), proves the asserted two minimax orders on the single fixed compatible experiment.  Uniform coverage on \(\mathcal D_\rho\) is stronger than shell-only coverage, so the same interval lower bound applies to intervals honest on \(\mathcal D_\rho\).

Finally, if \(\sqrt n\lambda_n\to\infty\), (14) proves uniform mean-square, hence uniform probabilistic, consistency of \(\widehat\beta\) along every nonempty shell sequence.  Conversely, if this divergence fails, some subsequence has \(\sqrt n\lambda_n\) bounded.  Along that subsequence \(\lambda_n\to0\), so eventually \(\lambda_n\leq\lambda_0\), and (36)--(38) bound the minimax squared risk away from zero.  No uniformly consistent estimator exists on that subsequence.  This proves the stated if-and-only-if frontier.
