%%% FIELD proof-uniform-generated-projector-expansion
Fix an arbitrary triangular array in \(\mathcal M_r(\lambda_n)\), and suppress the array index \(n\) on population quantities when no confusion can result.  All bounds below are uniform over this array.  The frozen sampling design says that the observations are independent within and across environments and that, within environment \(e\), the variables \(W_{e,i}=(T_{e,i},Y_{e,i})\), \(1\leq i\leq n_e\), are identically distributed with law \(P_e\).  By balanced shares,
\[
 n_e=n\pi_e\geq n\underline\pi . \tag{1}
\]
Consequently, for all sufficiently large \(n\), every \(n_e\) is at least \(R_{\max}\), so the small-sample fallback in the definition of the cumulant-SVD estimator is inactive.

We first obtain the moment bounds needed for all subsequent uniform statements.  The treatment-loading, effect, and confounding bounds imply
\[
 |\alpha|\leq a_1,\qquad |\beta|\leq B,
 \qquad |\gamma|=|\alpha|\,|\gamma/\alpha|\leq a_1q_1. \tag{2}
\]
The structural equations can be written as
\[
 T_e=\alpha\epsilon_{u,e}+\epsilon_{t,e},
 \qquad
 Y_e=(\beta\alpha+\gamma)\epsilon_{u,e}
       +\beta\epsilon_{t,e}+\epsilon_y. \tag{3}
\]
For \(s=2R_{\max}+\delta\), the inequality
\[
 |x_1+\cdots+x_j|^s\leq j^{s-1}\sum_{a=1}^j|x_a|^s \tag{4}
\]
and the source moment envelope, applied to (2)--(3), give a finite constant \(K_W\), depending only on the fixed envelopes, such that
\[
 \sup_n\max_{e\in\mathcal E}
 \mathbb E\!\left[\|W_{e,1}\|^{2R_{\max}+\delta}\right]
 \leq K_W. \tag{5}
\]
In particular, every population moment and cumulant of \((T_e,Y_e)\) through order \(R_{\max}\) is uniformly bounded.  It follows that \(\|C\|\), \(\|c_k\|\), \(\|m_k\|\), and
\(\|h_k\|=\|m_k-\beta c_k\|\) are uniformly bounded for \(2\leq k\leq R_{\max}\).

We next construct the asserted first projections and control the degenerate remainders.  For random variables \(X_1,\ldots,X_k\), the joint cumulant polynomial is
\[
 \kappa_k(X_1,\ldots,X_k)
 =\sum_{\mathcal P\in\Pi_k}
 (|\mathcal P|-1)!(-1)^{|\mathcal P|-1}
 \prod_{D\in\mathcal P}
 \mathbb E\!\left[\prod_{j\in D}X_j\right], \tag{6}
\]
where \(\Pi_k\) is the finite set of partitions of \(\{1,\ldots,k\}\).  For a partition with \(s\) blocks, estimating its product of expectations by averaging the block monomials over \(s\) distinct observations and symmetrizing produces a symmetric \(U\)-statistic of order \(s\).  Hence each stipulated unbiased sample cumulant of order \(k\) is a fixed finite linear combination of symmetric \(U\)-statistics of orders at most \(k\), whose kernels have total polynomial degree at most \(k\).

Consider one such order-\(s\) statistic \(U_{n_e,s}(g)\).  Let \(g_j\) be its canonical centered projection of order \(j\), and define its first projection by
\[
 \psi_g(w)=s\{\mathbb E[g(w,W_2,\ldots,W_s)]-\mathbb E g\}.
\]
Successive subtraction of conditional expectations gives the exact Hoeffding identity
\[
 U_{n_e,s}(g)-\mathbb E g
 =\frac1{n_e}\sum_{i=1}^{n_e}\psi_g(W_{e,i})
  +\sum_{j=2}^s {s\choose j}U_{n_e,j}(g_j). \tag{7}
\]
Each \(g_j\) is degenerate in every argument.  Thus two summands in \(U_{n_e,j}(g_j)\), indexed by distinct unordered \(j\)-tuples, have covariance zero: conditioning on an index that occurs in only one tuple annihilates the product.  Therefore
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[U_{n_e,j}(g_j)^2\right]
 ={n_e\choose j}^{-1}\mathbb E[g_j^2]
 \leq K n_e^{-j}. \tag{8}
\]
The last inequality is uniform because the squared kernel has total degree at most \(2R_{\max}\), so its expectation is bounded by (5) and H\"older's inequality.  Since there are only finitely many orders, partitions, environments, and coordinates, (7)--(8) show that the sum of all terms of projection order at least two is \(O_{L^2}(n_e^{-1})\), uniformly.

Apply the deterministic signs defining each environment-versus-baseline contrast, and stack the resulting centered first projections.  This defines the arrays \(\psi^C_{e,n}\), \(\psi^c_{e,k,n}\), and \(\psi^m_{e,k,n}\); in particular,
\[
 \mathbb E[\psi^C_{e,n}(W_{e,1})]=0,
 \quad
 \mathbb E[\psi^c_{e,k,n}(W_{e,1})]=0,
 \quad
 \mathbb E[\psi^m_{e,k,n}(W_{e,1})]=0. \tag{9}
\]
Because \(n_e=n\pi_e\), equations (1), (7), and (8) yield, simultaneously over all relevant \(k\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sqrt n(\widehat C-C)
 &=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}\sum_{i=1}^{n_e}
     \pi_e^{-1}\psi^C_{e,n}(W_{e,i})+o_{L^2}(1),\\
 \sqrt n(\widehat c_k-c_k)
 &=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}\sum_{i=1}^{n_e}
     \pi_e^{-1}\psi^c_{e,k,n}(W_{e,i})+o_{L^2}(1),\\
 \sqrt n(\widehat m_k-m_k)
 &=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}\sum_{i=1}^{n_e}
     \pi_e^{-1}\psi^m_{e,k,n}(W_{e,i})+o_{L^2}(1).
 \end{aligned} \tag{10}
\]
Here and below, uniform \(o_{L^2}(1)\) means that the supremum of the second moment of the remainder over the stated arrays tends to zero.  Indeed, after multiplication by \(\sqrt n\), the bound \(O_{L^2}(n_e^{-1})\) in each environment is \(O(n^{-1/2})\) by (1).

Put
\[
 \nu=\min\{1,\delta/R_{\max}\}>0.
\]
Each first projection above is a finite sum of polynomials of degree at most \(R_{\max}\) in one observation, with uniformly bounded coefficients supplied by lower-order moments.  Conditional Jensen and (5) therefore imply
\[
 \sup_{n,e}\mathbb E\!\left[
 \|\psi^C_{e,n}\|^{2+\nu}
 +\sum_{k=2}^{R_{\max}}
 \{\|\psi^c_{e,k,n}\|^{2+\nu}
   +\|\psi^m_{e,k,n}\|^{2+\nu}\}
 \right]<\infty, \tag{11}
\]
because \((2+\nu)R_{\max}\leq2R_{\max}+\delta\).

We now linearize the generated projector.  Let \(P_C=CC^+\), where
\(C^+=(C^\top C)^{-1}C^\top\), so \(Q_A=I_{E-1}-P_C\).  The calibration gap gives
\[
 \|C^+\|=\sigma_d(C)^{-1}\leq\kappa_0^{-1}. \tag{12}
\]
For a perturbation \(H\) with \(\|H\|\leq\kappa_0/2\), every unit vector \(v\in\mathbb R^d\) satisfies
\[
 \|(C+H)v\|\geq\|Cv\|-\|Hv\|
 \geq\sigma_d(C)-\|H\|\geq\kappa_0/2,
\]
so \(\sigma_d(C+H)\geq\kappa_0/2\).  Expanding the inverse in
\[
 P_{C+H}=(C+H)\{(C+H)^\top(C+H)\}^{-1}(C+H)^\top
\]
by the exact identity
\[
 (G+D)^{-1}=G^{-1}-G^{-1}DG^{-1}
 +G^{-1}DG^{-1}D(G+D)^{-1}, \tag{13}
\]
with \(G=C^\top C\) and \(D=C^\top H+H^\top C+H^\top H\), and then collecting the terms linear in \(H\), gives
\[
 P_{C+H}-P_C
 =Q_AHC^+ +(Q_AHC^+)^\top+R_C(H),
 \qquad \|R_C(H)\|\leq K_P\|H\|^2. \tag{14}
\]
The constant \(K_P\) is uniform: (12) bounds the inverse of \(G\), the preceding direct singular-value bound controls the last inverse in (13), and (5)--(6) bound \(\|C\|\).

Taking \(H=\widehat C-C\), equation (10) and (11) give
\(\|H\|=O_{\mathbb P}(n^{-1/2})\) uniformly.  Hence the event on which (14) applies has probability tending to one uniformly, and its remainder is \(O_{\mathbb P}(n^{-1})\).  On that event the rank-\(d\) SVD projector equals \(P_{\widehat C}\).  It follows that
\[
 \widehat Q-Q_A
 =-Q_A(\widehat C-C)C^+
  -\{Q_A(\widehat C-C)C^+\}^\top
  +o_{\mathbb P}(n^{-1/2}) \tag{15}
\]
uniformly.

By the already-established cumulant-cancellation lemma,
\[
 h_k=m_k-\beta c_k=\alpha^{k-1}\gamma u_k\in A=\operatorname{col}(C),
 \qquad Q_Ah_k=0. \tag{16}
\]
Consequently, the transpose term in (15) annihilates \(h_k\):
\[
 \{Q_A(\widehat C-C)C^+\}^\top h_k
 =C^{+\top}(\widehat C-C)^\top Q_Ah_k=0. \tag{17}
\]
Write \(\widehat h_k=\widehat m_k-\beta\widehat c_k\).  Equations (10)--(17), together with the uniform bounds on \(\beta\), \(h_k\), and \(C^+\), show that
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sqrt n\,\widehat Q\widehat h_k
 &=\sqrt n\,Q_A(\widehat h_k-h_k)
   +\sqrt n(\widehat Q-Q_A)h_k
   +\sqrt n(\widehat Q-Q_A)(\widehat h_k-h_k)\\
 &=\sqrt n\,Q_A\{(\widehat m_k-m_k)
       -\beta(\widehat c_k-c_k)\}
   -\sqrt n\,Q_A(\widehat C-C)C^+h_k+o_{\mathbb P}(1).
 \end{aligned} \tag{18}
\]
The last product in the first line is \(O_{\mathbb P}(n^{-1/2})\), uniformly, because each factor before multiplication by \(\sqrt n\) is \(O_{\mathbb P}(n^{-1/2})\).  Substitution of (10) into (18) gives
\[
 \sqrt n\,\widehat Q(\widehat m_k-\beta\widehat c_k)
 =\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}\sum_{i=1}^{n_e}
   \pi_e^{-1}\zeta_{e,k,n}(W_{e,i})+o_{\mathbb P}(1), \tag{19}
\]
where
\[
 \zeta_{e,k,n}
 =Q_A\{\psi^m_{e,k,n}-\beta\psi^c_{e,k,n}
          -\psi^C_{e,n}C^+h_k\}. \tag{20}
\]
There are only finitely many \(k\), so the remainders in (19) are simultaneous.  Equation (20) also displays the same-sample estimated-plane correction, including its sign.

It remains to prove the uniform Gaussian approximation without imposing nonsingularity.  Stack (20) over \(d+2\leq k\leq R_{\max}\), and call the resulting fixed-dimensional vector \(\zeta_{e,n}\).  From (11), (12), and the uniform bound on \(h_k\),
\[
 \sup_{n,e}\mathbb E\|\zeta_{e,n}\|^{2+\nu}<\infty. \tag{21}
\]
Index the independent summands of the stacked \(Z_n\) by \(a=(e,i)\), and set
\[
 X_a=n^{-1/2}\pi_e^{-1}\zeta_{e,n}(W_{e,i}),
 \qquad L_n=\sum_a\mathbb E\|X_a\|^{2+\nu}.
\]
Equations (1) and (21) yield
\[
 L_n\leq K n^{-1-\nu/2}\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}n_e\pi_e^{-(2+\nu)}
 =K n^{-\nu/2}\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}\pi_e^{-(1+\nu)}
 \leq K' n^{-\nu/2}. \tag{22}
\]

For each \(a\), let \(G_a\) be an independent centered Gaussian vector with
\(\operatorname{Cov}(G_a)=\operatorname{Cov}(X_a)\), all the \(G_a\)'s being independent of all the \(X_a\)'s.  A Gaussian moment calculation gives
\[
 \mathbb E\|G_a\|^{2+\nu}
 \leq K\{\operatorname{tr}\operatorname{Cov}(X_a)\}^{1+\nu/2}
 =K\{\mathbb E\|X_a\|^2\}^{1+\nu/2}
 \leq K\mathbb E\|X_a\|^{2+\nu}, \tag{23}
\]
where the last step follows by applying Jensen's inequality to the convex map \(x\mapsto x^{1+\nu/2}\) and the nonnegative variable \(\|X_a\|^2\).  We now give a direct Lindeberg replacement bound.  If \(f\) satisfies \(\|f\|_\infty\leq1\) and \(\operatorname{Lip}(f)\leq1\), let
\(f_\varepsilon(x)=\mathbb E[f(x+\varepsilon Z_0)]\), with \(Z_0\) a standard Gaussian vector in the fixed ambient dimension.  Directly differentiating the Gaussian convolution gives
\[
 \sup_x\|D^2f_\varepsilon(x)\|\leq K\varepsilon^{-1},
 \qquad
 \sup_x\|D^3f_\varepsilon(x)\|\leq K\varepsilon^{-2}.
\]
Taking the minimum of the bound obtained from each display and using
\(\min\{A,Bt\}\leq A^{1-\nu}(Bt)^\nu\) shows
\[
 \|D^2f_\varepsilon(x)-D^2f_\varepsilon(y)\|
 \leq K\varepsilon^{-(1+\nu)}\|x-y\|^\nu. \tag{24}
\]
The integral form of the second-order Taylor remainder and (24) imply
\[
 |f_\varepsilon(x+v)-f_\varepsilon(x)-Df_\varepsilon(x)v
   -\tfrac12v^\top D^2f_\varepsilon(x)v|
 \leq K\varepsilon^{-(1+\nu)}\|v\|^{2+\nu}. \tag{25}
\]
Replace the \(X_a\)'s successively by the \(G_a\)'s.  Conditional on the sum of all terms other than the one currently replaced, the constant, linear, and quadratic terms in (25) have the same expectations because
\[
 \mathbb E X_a=\mathbb E G_a=0,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E[X_aX_a^\top]=\mathbb E[G_aG_a^\top].
\]
Thus telescoping, (23), and (25) give
\[
 \left|\mathbb Ef_\varepsilon\!\left(\sum_aX_a\right)
       -\mathbb Ef_\varepsilon\!\left(\sum_aG_a\right)\right|
 \leq K\varepsilon^{-(1+\nu)}L_n. \tag{26}
\]
Moreover, Lipschitz continuity gives
\[
 \sup_x|f_\varepsilon(x)-f(x)|
 \leq\varepsilon\mathbb E\|Z_0\|. \tag{27}
\]
Combining (26)--(27), then taking the supremum over the bounded-Lipschitz class, yields
\[
 d_{\mathrm{BL}}\!\left(\mathcal L\!\left(\sum_aX_a\right),
                         \mathcal L\!\left(\sum_aG_a\right)\right)
 \leq K\{\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{-(1+\nu)}L_n\}. \tag{28}
\]
If \(L_n>0\), choose \(\varepsilon=L_n^{1/(2+\nu)}\); if \(L_n=0\), every summand is almost surely zero and the two laws coincide.  Equations (22) and (28) then prove uniform convergence to zero.

Finally, independence, centering, and \(n_e=n\pi_e\) show that the covariance of the Gaussian sum is exactly
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Cov}\!\left(\sum_aG_a\right)
 &=\operatorname{Cov}\!\left(\sum_aX_a\right)\\
 &=\frac1n\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}n_e\pi_e^{-2}
   \mathbb E[\zeta_{e,n}\zeta_{e,n}^\top]
 =\sum_{e\in\mathcal E}\pi_e^{-1}
   \mathbb E[\zeta_{e,n}\zeta_{e,n}^\top]
 =\Sigma_n. \tag{29}
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore \(\sum_aG_a\sim N(0,\Sigma_n)\), and (28)--(29) establish the claimed uniform bounded-Lipschitz approximation.  No inverse eigenvalue of \(\Sigma_n\) entered the argument.  If \(\Sigma_n\) is singular, the Gaussian sum is supported on \(\operatorname{im}(\Sigma_n)\); if \(\Sigma_n=0\), it is the point mass at zero.  This completes every asserted part of the lemma.
