%%% FIELD population-cancellation-statement
For every \(\theta\in\Theta_{\mathrm{pop}}\) and every integer \(k\geq2\),
\[
 m_k-\beta c_k=\alpha^{k-1}\gamma u_k\in A,
 \qquad Q_Am_k=\beta Q_Ac_k.
\]
These identities require only finite moments through order \(k\), source independence, and the population-plane restriction; they do not require a moment generating function.
%%% FIELD population-cancellation-proof
Fix \(\theta\in\Theta_{\mathrm{pop}}\) and an integer \(k\geq2\).  All moments appearing below are finite by the all-absolute-moments member property of \(\Theta_{\mathrm{pop}}\).  For random variables \(X_1,\ldots,X_k\) with finite absolute moments through order \(k\), their joint cumulant is the finite moment--cumulant polynomial
\[
 \kappa_k(X_1,\ldots,X_k)
 =\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak P_k}(|\pi|-1)!(-1)^{|\pi|-1}
   \prod_{D\in\pi}\mathbb E\!\left[\prod_{j\in D}X_j\right],             \tag{1}
\]
where \(\mathfrak P_k\) is the set of partitions of \(\{1,\ldots,k\}\).  Formula (1) is multilinear because every argument occurs exactly once in every expanded monomial.  It also makes a cumulant containing variables from two independent source families vanish.  Indeed, retain from the finite formal moment series in indeterminates \(t_1,\ldots,t_k\) only monomials in which every exponent is at most one and expand the formal logarithm \(\log(1+z)=\sum_{j=1}^k(-1)^{j-1}z^j/j\).  The coefficient of \(t_1\cdots t_k\) is (1).  Independence factors the formal moment series, so its formal logarithm is a sum of terms involving only one source family and has no mixed coefficient.  This is a finite polynomial argument and requires no analytic moment generating function.

Define the proof-local treatment-source contrast
\[
 v_k=\bigl(\kappa_k(\epsilon_{t,e})-
                 \kappa_k(\epsilon_{t,0})\bigr)_{e=1}^{E-1}.
\]
The structural equations in the population-class definition give
\[
 T_e=\alpha\epsilon_{u,e}+\epsilon_{t,e},
 \qquad
 Y_e=(\alpha\beta+\gamma)\epsilon_{u,e}
       +\beta\epsilon_{t,e}+\epsilon_y.                                  \tag{2}
\]
By multilinearity of (1), mutual independence of the three sources, and the preceding mixed-source cancellation,
\[
 \kappa_k(T_e)=\alpha^k\kappa_k(\epsilon_{u,e})
                  +\kappa_k(\epsilon_{t,e}),                              \tag{3}
\]
and, because \(k\geq2\),
\[
 \kappa_k(Y_e,T_e,\ldots,T_e)
 =(\alpha\beta+\gamma)\alpha^{k-1}\kappa_k(\epsilon_{u,e})
   +\beta\kappa_k(\epsilon_{t,e}).                                       \tag{4}
\]
The invariant outcome source contributes nothing to (4), since selecting \(\epsilon_y\) in the first argument produces a mixed cumulant with the independent treatment arguments.

Subtracting the baseline-environment versions of (3)--(4) and applying the definitions of \(u_k,c_k,m_k\) gives
\[
 c_k=\alpha^ku_k+v_k,                                                       \tag{5}
\]
and
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 m_k
 &=(\alpha\beta+\gamma)\alpha^{k-1}u_k+\beta v_k\\
 &=\beta(\alpha^ku_k+v_k)+\alpha^{k-1}\gamma u_k
  =\beta c_k+\alpha^{k-1}\gamma u_k.
 \end{aligned}                                                            \tag{6}
\]
The population-plane restriction gives \(u_k\in A\), so (6) proves \(m_k-\beta c_k\in A\).  The calibration-rank condition gives \(\operatorname{rank}(C)=d\), hence \(C^\top C\) is invertible and the declared \(Q_A\) is the orthogonal projector onto \(A^\perp\).  Applying \(Q_A\) to (6) proves \(Q_Am_k=\beta Q_Ac_k\).
%%% FIELD ambient-subset-statement
The bounded calibrated class is a subclass of the population flag class:
\[
 \Theta\subseteq\Theta_{\mathrm{pop}}.
\]
%%% FIELD ambient-subset-proof
Let \(\theta\in\Theta\).  The ambient-class definition supplies the same three structural equations as the population-class definition.  Its source-independence member property supplies mutual independence within every environment, its all-absolute-moments member property supplies finite absolute moments of every positive integer order, its calibration-rank property gives \(\operatorname{rank}(C)=d\), and its latent-profile-plane property gives \(u_k\in A\) for every integer \(k\geq2\).  These are precisely the substantive membership requirements of \(\Theta_{\mathrm{pop}}\), with the positive-dimension and environment-indexing conditions supplying the shared typing conditions.  The remaining centering, coefficient, moment-envelope, order-cap, and singular-gap restrictions defining \(\Theta\) only make the class smaller.  Thus \(\theta\in\Theta_{\mathrm{pop}}\), proving the inclusion.
%%% FIELD population-frontier-statement
In the population flag class \(\Theta_{\mathrm{pop}}\), the structural direct effect \(\beta\) is identified at every parameter with \(r^\star<\infty\), including when \(r^\star>R_{\max}\).  More precisely, for every integer \(k\geq2\) such that \(Q_Ac_k\neq0\),
\[
 \beta=\frac{\langle Q_Ac_k,Q_Am_k\rangle}
              {\lVert Q_Ac_k\rVert_2^2}.                                  \tag{1}
\]
On the bounded legal-swap domain \(\mathcal D_\rho\), \(\beta\) is identified if and only if \(r^\star<\infty\).  At every \(\theta\in\mathcal N_\rho\), the parameters \(\theta\) and \(S\theta\) both lie in \(\mathcal D_\rho\), induce the same collection \(\{P_e:e\in\mathcal E\}\), and have the distinct effects \(\beta\) and \(\beta+\gamma/\alpha\).
%%% FIELD population-frontier-proof
The causal target is the structural coefficient \(\beta\), not the right-hand side of (1).  Under the intervention setting treatment to \(t\), while leaving the exogenous variables fixed, the outcome equation gives
\[
 Y_e(t)=\beta t+\gamma U_e+\epsilon_y,
 \qquad Y_e(t+1)-Y_e(t)=\beta.                                             \tag{2}
\]
We now prove that the observable quotient equals this intervention contrast under the population restrictions.

Let \(\theta\in\Theta_{\mathrm{pop}}\), and suppose \(Q_Ac_k\neq0\) for some integer \(k\geq2\).  The population cumulant-cancellation lemma gives
\[
 Q_Am_k=\beta Q_Ac_k.                                                       \tag{3}
\]
Taking the Euclidean inner product of (3) with \(Q_Ac_k\) yields
\[
 \langle Q_Ac_k,Q_Am_k\rangle
 =\beta\lVert Q_Ac_k\rVert_2^2.                                          \tag{4}
\]
The assumed nonzero flag makes the denominator in (4) strictly positive, so division proves (1).

Every object on the right of (1) is determined by the observed laws.  All required moments exist in \(\Theta_{\mathrm{pop}}\), so the laws \(P_e\) determine \(c_k\) and \(m_k\).  They also determine \(C=[c_2\mid\cdots\mid c_{d+1}]\).  The rank condition \(\operatorname{rank}(C)=d\) makes \(C^\top C\) invertible, so the observed laws determine
\[
 Q_A=I_{E-1}-C(C^\top C)^{-1}C^\top.                                    \tag{5}
\]
Let \(\widetilde\theta\in\Theta_{\mathrm{pop}}\) induce the same collection of observed laws as \(\theta\).  It has the same \(c_j,m_j,C,Q_A\) for every \(j\), and hence the same \(r^\star\).  If \(r^\star<\infty\), applying (1) at \(k=r^\star\) to both representations gives
\[
 \widetilde\beta
 =\frac{\langle Q_Ac_{r^\star},Q_Am_{r^\star}\rangle}
        {\lVert Q_Ac_{r^\star}\rVert_2^2}
 =\beta.                                                                   \tag{6}
\]
No order cap entered the argument, so (6) includes \(r^\star>R_{\max}\).  This proves point identification relative to the broad population class.

We next specialize to \(\mathcal D_\rho\).  The ambient-subclass lemma gives \(\Theta\subseteq\Theta_{\mathrm{pop}}\), hence every member of \(\mathcal B\), whose breakout is finite, is identified by (6).  Equivalently, any second member of \(\mathcal D_\rho\) with the same observed laws has the same finite breakout, cannot lie in the no-breakout branch, and has the same effect by (6).

Let instead \(\theta\in\mathcal N_\rho\).  The treatment-loading bound in \(\Theta_\rho^\circ\) gives \(|\alpha|\geq a_0+\rho>0\), so the proof-local ratio \(q=\gamma/\alpha\) is well defined.  Put
\[
 V_e=\alpha\epsilon_{u,e},
 \qquad W_e=\epsilon_{t,e}.
\]
The observed variables under \(\theta\) are
\[
 T_e=V_e+W_e,
 \qquad
 Y_e=(\beta+q)V_e+\beta W_e+\epsilon_y.                                   \tag{7}
\]
Under \(S\theta\), the latent and treatment sources are respectively \(W_e\) and \(V_e\), while the coefficients are \(1,\beta+q,-q\).  Therefore
\[
 T'_e=W_e+V_e=T_e,
\]
and
\[
 Y'_e=(\beta+q)(W_e+V_e)-qW_e+\epsilon_y
     =(\beta+q)V_e+\beta W_e+\epsilon_y=Y_e.                              \tag{8}
\]
Thus the swap preserves the complete observed law in every environment, not only the selected cumulants.

By definition of \(\mathcal N_\rho\), both \(\theta\) and \(S\theta\) lie in \(\Theta_\rho^\circ\), and both have infinite breakout.  To check that the second representation itself lies in \(\mathcal N_\rho\), compute
\[
 S^2\theta
 =\left(1,\beta,q,
   \{\alpha\epsilon_{u,e},\epsilon_{t,e}\}_{e\in\mathcal E},\epsilon_y\right).       \tag{9}
\]
The loading value one is interior because it is already the loading of \(S\theta\); the effect \(\beta\) and confounding ratio \(q\) obey the interior bounds because \(\theta\) does.  The two source families in (9) are precisely the two source families of \(S\theta\) with their structural roles reversed, so their centering, independence, all-order moments, and \((2R_{\max}+\delta)\)-moment bounds follow from \(S\theta\in\Theta_\rho^\circ\).  The latent contrast at order \(k\) in (9) is \(\alpha^ku_k\in A\), and (7)--(8) show that its observed \(C\), calibration gap, and infinite breakout are unchanged.  Hence \(S^2\theta\in\Theta_\rho^\circ\) and \(r^\star(S^2\theta)=\infty\), which proves
\[
 S\theta\in\mathcal N_\rho\subseteq\mathcal D_\rho.
\]

Finally, the two effects differ by \(q=\gamma/\alpha\), and the confounding bound gives \(|q|\geq q_0>0\).  Two parameters in \(\mathcal D_\rho\) therefore have identical observed laws and distinct causal targets, so no observed-law functional identifies \(\beta\) at any member of \(\mathcal N_\rho\).  Combining this explicit obstruction with finite-breakout identification proves the if-and-only-if frontier on \(\mathcal D_\rho\).
%%% FIELD pairwise-rescue-statement
Let \(d=1\), \(E=3\), and \(r\in\{3,\ldots,R_{\max}\}\).  Put \(x_0=0,x_1=1,x_2=2\) and \(y_0=0,y_1=2,y_2=-1\), and define
\[
 a=(x_1-x_0,x_2-x_0)^\top=(1,2)^\top,
 \qquad b=(y_1-y_0,y_2-y_0)^\top=(2,-1)^\top.
\]
There exist positive \(\eta,\zeta,\varepsilon\) and centered compound-Poisson latent and treatment sources with cumulant-shift profiles
\[
 u_k=\eta a,
 \qquad
 w_k:=\bigl(\kappa_k(\epsilon_{t,e})-
                  \kappa_k(\epsilon_{t,0})\bigr)_{e=1}^2
      =\zeta a+\varepsilon\Delta_{r,k}b,
 \qquad
 \Delta_{r,k}=\sum_{j=0}^r(-1)^{r-j}\binom rj j^k,                       \tag{1}
\]
where \(\Delta_{r,k}=0\) for \(1\leq k<r\) and \(\Delta_{r,r}=r!\).  Choose positive Poisson baselines so that every intensity is positive, and choose \(q>0\), an invariant centered Gaussian outcome source, and compatible interior model bounds satisfying
\[
 a_0+\rho<1<a_1-\rho,\qquad
 q_0+\rho\leq q\leq q_1-\rho,\qquad
 q/2\leq B-\rho,\qquad
 \eta+\zeta\geq\kappa_0+\rho,                                           \tag{2}
\]
with all source \((2R_{\max}+\delta)\)-moments at most \(M-\rho\).  With \(\alpha=1\), \(\beta=-q/2\), and \(\gamma=q\), the resulting parameter has \(A=\operatorname{span}(a)\), \(r^\star=r\), and is jointly identified.  Both source laws change between every pair of environments.  Nevertheless, on each of the three two-environment restrictions the scalar calibration contrast \(c_2^{ef}\) is nonzero and the legal source swap gives an observationally equivalent no-breakout representation with effect \(q/2\).  Jointly, the swapped latent profile at order \(r\) is not in \(A\), so that swapped representation is excluded by the maintained population-plane restriction.
%%% FIELD pairwise-rescue-proof
For \(e\in\{0,1,2\}\), let
\[
 N^u_e\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(L_u+\eta x_e),
 \qquad
 \epsilon_{u,e}=N^u_e-(L_u+\eta x_e),                                    \tag{3}
\]
where \(L_u>0\).  Independently, for \(j=1,\ldots,r\), let
\[
 N^t_{e,j}\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda_{e,j}^t),
 \qquad
 \lambda_{e,j}^t
 =L_j+\zeta x_e\mathbf 1\{j=1\}
   +\varepsilon y_e(-1)^{r-j}\binom rj,                                  \tag{4}
\]
and set
\[
 \epsilon_{t,e}=\sum_{j=1}^r j\bigl(N^t_{e,j}-\lambda_{e,j}^t\bigr).      \tag{5}
\]
For every \(j\), choose
\[
 L_j>\max_{e\in\{0,1,2\}}
 \left|\zeta x_e\mathbf 1\{j=1\}
   +\varepsilon y_e(-1)^{r-j}\binom rj\right|.                           \tag{6}
\]
Then every intensity in (4) is strictly positive.  Take all counts mutually independent and take \(\epsilon_y\) to be an independent centered Gaussian variable.  Compensating each count proves centering.  Finite compound-Poisson sums and the Gaussian source have moment generating functions finite on the real line, and therefore have finite absolute moments of every order.  Choosing \(M\) above the maximum of the finitely many required source moments, including the same sources after pairwise swapping, gives the stipulated \(\rho\)-slack.

For a compensated Poisson variable with jump size \(j\) and intensity \(\lambda\), differentiating \(\lambda(e^{jt}-1-jt)\) shows that every cumulant of order \(k\geq2\) is \(\lambda j^k\).  Thus (3) gives
\[
 u_k=\eta(x_1-x_0,x_2-x_0)^\top=\eta a.                                  \tag{7}
\]
Equations (4)--(5) give
\[
 w_k=\zeta a+\varepsilon b
       \sum_{j=0}^r(-1)^{r-j}\binom rj j^k.                              \tag{8}
\]
For \(1\leq k\), the sum in (8) is the \(r\)-th forward difference at zero of the polynomial \(z\mapsto z^k\).  The binomial identity
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^r(-1)^{r-j}\binom rj e^{jt}=(e^t-1)^r                        \tag{9}
\]
and differentiation at \(t=0\) give
\[
 \Delta_{r,k}=0\quad(1\leq k<r),
 \qquad \Delta_{r,r}=r!.                                                  \tag{10}
\]
This formulation never invokes \(0^0\).

Set \(\alpha=1\), \(\beta=-q/2\), and \(\gamma=q\).  By source independence and cumulant additivity, the observed treatment contrasts are
\[
 c_k=u_k+w_k=(\eta+\zeta)a+\varepsilon\Delta_{r,k}b.                      \tag{11}
\]
Since \(r\geq3\), (10) gives \(\Delta_{r,2}=0\).  Hence
\[
 C=[c_2]=[(\eta+\zeta)a],
 \qquad A=\operatorname{span}(a),
 \qquad \sigma_1(C)=(\eta+\zeta)\|a\|_2\geq\kappa_0+\rho.             \tag{12}
\]
The latent profiles (7) lie in \(A\) at every order.  Equations (10)--(11) show \(Q_Ac_k=0\) for \(3\leq k<r\).  Because
\[
 \langle a,b\rangle=1\cdot2+2\cdot(-1)=0,
 \qquad b\neq0,                                                           \tag{13}
\]
we have
\[
 Q_Ac_r=\varepsilon r!b\neq0.                                             \tag{14}
\]
Thus \(r^\star=r\), and the population identification theorem identifies the original effect jointly by the quotient at order \(r\).

For distinct \(e,f\in\{0,1,2\}\), the latent variance contrast is \(\eta(x_e-x_f)\neq0\), because \(x_0,x_1,x_2\) are pairwise distinct.  By (8) and \(\Delta_{r,2}=0\), the treatment-source variance contrast is \(\zeta(x_e-x_f)\neq0\).  Variances differ, so both source laws change in every environment pair.  The observed scalar calibration contrast for that pair is
\[
 c_2^{ef}=(\eta+\zeta)(x_e-x_f)\neq0.                                    \tag{15}
\]
In a two-environment restriction with \(d=1\), the nonzero scalar (15) spans the entire one-dimensional contrast space.  Its orthogonal projector is zero, so every cumulant flag vanishes in either source ordering.  The inequalities (2), the moment choice, and positivity (6) make both orderings legal in the corresponding pairwise bounded interior.  Directly, the original representation is
\[
 T_e=\epsilon_{u,e}+\epsilon_{t,e},
 \qquad
 Y_e=\frac q2\epsilon_{u,e}-\frac q2\epsilon_{t,e}+\epsilon_y,           \tag{16}
\]
whereas its source swap uses latent source \(\epsilon_{t,e}\), treatment source \(\epsilon_{u,e}\), and coefficients \((1,q/2,-q)\).  Substitution gives exactly the same \((T_e,Y_e)\) in (16), while the effect changes from \(-q/2\) to \(q/2\).  This proves a legal unequal-effect no-breakout ambiguity separately for every pair.

Finally, the jointly swapped latent source is the original treatment source, whose order-\(r\) profile is
\[
 w_r=\zeta a+\varepsilon r!b.                                             \tag{17}
\]
By (13) and \(\varepsilon>0\), (17) is not in \(A=\operatorname{span}(a)\).  Thus the pairwise swaps cannot be assembled into an admissible joint representation: the joint swap violates the population-plane restriction.  Equations (14)--(17) establish the simultaneous pairwise ambiguity and joint rescue.
%%% FIELD ambient-weak-frontier-statement
For every fixed admissible bounded class \(\Theta\), each \(r\in\{d+2,\ldots,R_{\max}\}\), and every nonempty shell \(\mathcal M_r(\lambda_n)\), the cumulant-SVD estimator satisfies
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in\mathcal M_r(\lambda_n)}
 \mathbb E_\theta(\widehat\beta-\beta)^2
 \leq K\min\left\{B^2,\frac1{n\lambda_n^2}\right\}.                    \tag{1}
\]
For a deterministic \(t_{\alpha_0}\) depending only on the fixed model envelopes, the conservative interval \(\mathcal C_n\) satisfies
\[
 \inf_{\theta\in\Theta}\Pr_\theta\{\beta\in\mathcal C_n\}
 \geq1-\alpha_0                                                         \tag{2}
\]
and
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in\mathcal M_r(\lambda_n)}
 \mathbb E_\theta\operatorname{length}(\mathcal C_n)
 \leq K\min\left\{B,\frac1{\sqrt n\lambda_n}\right\},                  \tag{3}
\]
without knowing \(r\).  Moreover, for every \(d\geq1\), \(E\geq d+2\), \(R_{\max}\geq d+2\), and \(\delta>0\), there is one fixed compatible choice of the bounded experiment, common to all admitted \(r\), on which the matching minimax lower bounds hold for squared error and for intervals honest on the shell alone, hence also for intervals honest on \(\Theta\).  On that experiment the two minimax quantities have the orders in (1) and (3), and uniform consistency along a shell sequence is possible if and only if \(\sqrt n\lambda_n\to\infty\).
%%% FIELD ambient-weak-frontier-proof
Put \(s_0=2R_{\max}+\delta\).  The treatment-loading, confounding, effect, and source-moment bounds imply
\[
 |\gamma|=|\alpha|\,|\gamma/\alpha|\leq a_1q_1.
\]
Minkowski's inequality in \(L^{s_0}\), applied to the two structural equations, therefore gives a constant \(K_0<\infty\), depending only on the fixed envelopes, such that
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\max_{e\in\mathcal E}
 \mathbb E_\theta\left[|T_e|^{s_0}+|Y_e|^{s_0}\right]\leq K_0.           \tag{4}
\]
The ratio used here is well defined because \(|\alpha|\geq a_0>0\).

For \(k\leq R_{\max}\), the moment--cumulant polynomial proved in the population cumulant-cancellation lemma expresses every cumulant used here as a finite polynomial in moments of total degree at most \(k\).  If \(\pi=\{D_1,\ldots,D_s\}\) is a partition occurring in that polynomial, an unbiased estimator of its product of moments in environment \(e\) is
\[
 \frac1{(n_e)_s}
 \sum_{\substack{i_1,\ldots,i_s\\\text{distinct}}}
 \prod_{a=1}^s\prod_{j\in D_a}X_{j,e,i_a}.                               \tag{5}
\]
Independence across observations proves unbiasedness term by term.  The square of a summand in (5) has total degree at most \(2k\leq2R_{\max}<s_0\), so (4), Lyapunov's inequality, and H\"older's inequality bound its second moment uniformly.  In the variance double sum, disjoint index tuples are independent, and only \(O(n_e^{2s-1})\) of the \(O(n_e^{2s})\) pairs overlap.  The admitted orders, partitions, and environments are finite, while balanced shares give \(n_e\geq\underline\pi n\).  Consequently, whenever all \(n_e\geq R_{\max}\),
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}
 \mathbb E_\theta\left[
  \|\widehat c_k-c_k\|_2^2+\|\widehat m_k-m_k\|_2^2
 \right]\leq\frac{K_1}{n},
 \qquad 2\leq k\leq R_{\max}.                                           \tag{6}
\]
The same polynomial and (4) uniformly bound \(\|c_k\|_2\) and \(\|m_k\|_2\).

Let \(H=\widehat C-C\), and let \(\widehat A\) be the rank-\(d\) left singular subspace used to construct \(\widehat Q\).  If \(\|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\kappa_0/2\) and \(x=Cy\) is a unit vector in \(A\), then \(\|y\|_2\leq\kappa_0^{-1}\) and
\[
 \operatorname{dist}(x,\widehat A)
 \leq\|Hy\|_2\leq\kappa_0^{-1}\|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}.                    \tag{7}
\]
The variational definition of singular values gives
\[
 \sigma_d(\widehat C)\geq\sigma_d(C)-\|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \geq\kappa_0/2.                                                          \tag{8}
\]
Thus, if \(z=\widehat Cy\) is a unit vector in \(\widehat A\), then \(\|y\|_2\leq2/\kappa_0\) and
\[
 \operatorname{dist}(z,A)\leq2\kappa_0^{-1}\|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}.         \tag{9}
\]
The two directed distances (7) and (9), applied to the orthogonal decompositions for two equal-dimensional subspaces, imply
\[
 \|\widehat Q-Q_A\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \leq4\kappa_0^{-1}\|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}.                                \tag{10}
\]
On the complementary event the left side is at most two.  Equation (6) for the fixed \(d\) columns of \(H\), followed by Markov's inequality, therefore yields
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}
 \mathbb E_\theta\|\widehat Q-Q_A\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2\leq\frac{K_2}{n}. \tag{11}
\]
This calculation never inverts an influence covariance and remains valid when that covariance is singular.

For \(|b|\leq B\), define
\[
 g_k(b)=Q_A(m_k-bc_k),
 \qquad \widehat g_k(b)=\widehat Q(\widehat m_k-b\widehat c_k),
\]
and
\[
 \Delta_n=\max_{d+2\leq k\leq R_{\max}}
 \sup_{|b|\leq B}\|\widehat g_k(b)-g_k(b)\|_2.
\]
The exact decomposition
\[
 \widehat g_k(b)-g_k(b)
 =\widehat Q\{(\widehat m_k-m_k)-b(\widehat c_k-c_k)\}
  +(\widehat Q-Q_A)(m_k-bc_k)                                             \tag{12}
\]
and (6), (11), and the population cumulant bounds imply
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathbb E_\theta\Delta_n^2\leq\frac{K_3}{n}.    \tag{13}
\]
This uniform same-sample generated-plane control uses neither \(r\), \(\lambda_n\), nor a nonzero flag.

By the ambient-subclass lemma and population cumulant cancellation, every \(\theta\in\Theta\) obeys
\[
 g_k(b)=(\beta-b)Q_Ac_k,
 \qquad g_k(\beta)=0.                                                      \tag{14}
\]
If \(\theta\in\mathcal M_r(\lambda_n)\), optimality of the estimator and (12)--(14) yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |\widehat\beta-\beta|\,\|Q_Ac_r\|_2
 &=\|g_r(\widehat\beta)\|_2\\
 &\leq\|\widehat g_r(\widehat\beta)\|_2+\Delta_n\\
 &\leq\max_k\|\widehat g_k(\widehat\beta)\|_2+\Delta_n\\
 &\leq\max_k\|\widehat g_k(\beta)\|_2+\Delta_n
 \leq2\Delta_n.
 \end{aligned}                                                            \tag{15}
\]
The minimizer exists because the objective is continuous on the compact interval \([-B,B]\).  The shell gives \(\|Q_Ac_r\|_2\geq\lambda_n\), while both effects lie in \([-B,B]\).  Equations (13)--(15) therefore prove (1), after changing the constant.

Choose \(t_{\alpha_0}\) so that
\[
 t_{\alpha_0}^2\geq K_3/\alpha_0.                                        \tag{16}
\]
Let \(\mathcal R_n\) be the raw norm-sublevel set in the definition of \(\mathcal C_n\).  By (14), on \(\{\Delta_n\leq t_{\alpha_0}/\sqrt n\}\) the true \(\beta\) belongs to \(\mathcal R_n\).  On this event the raw set is nonempty, so its empty-set fallback is irrelevant.  Markov's inequality, (13), and (16) give
\[
 \inf_{\theta\in\Theta}
 \Pr_\theta\{\beta\in\mathcal C_n\}\geq1-\alpha_0.                    \tag{17}
\]
Thus conservative coverage is valid on the entire bounded ambient class, including no-breakout parameters.  The prescribed small-sample set \([-B,B]\) covers identically.

Each constraint defining \(\mathcal R_n\) is a convex sublevel set of the norm of an affine function of the scalar \(b\), so their intersection with \([-B,B]\) is an interval.  If \(b,b'\in\mathcal R_n\), then on the shell, (12)--(14) give
\[
 |b-b'|\lambda_n
 \leq\|g_r(b)-g_r(b')\|_2
 \leq2\left(\frac{t_{\alpha_0}}{\sqrt n}+\Delta_n\right).               \tag{18}
\]
If \(\mathcal R_n\) is empty, the fallback is a singleton and has length zero.  The box bound \(2B\), (13), (18), and Cauchy--Schwarz prove (3).  The true \(r\) appears only in the analysis; both procedures maximize over all admitted orders.

Balanced shares imply that some \(n_e<R_{\max}\) only when \(n<R_{\max}/\underline\pi\).  The deterministic error and length bounds \(4B^2\) and \(2B\), together with the uniform cumulant bound \(\|Q_Ac_k\|_2\leq\Lambda\), allow enlargement of the constants in (1) and (3) to cover these finitely many sample sizes whenever the shell is nonempty.

We now construct one compatible bounded experiment giving both lower bounds, common to all admitted \(r\).  Put
\[
 J=[19/10,23/10],
 \qquad \Sigma=\begin{pmatrix}2&5\\5&14\end{pmatrix},
\]
and, for \(b\in J\), define
\[
 f_b=\frac{5-2b}{3-b},\qquad
 g_b=\frac1{3-b},\qquad z_b=b-1.                                         \tag{19}
\]
All denominators and all three variances are bounded away from zero on \(J\).  Let \(V_b,W_b,Z_b\) be independent centered Gaussian variables with variances \(f_b,g_b,z_b\).  Direct substitution gives
\[
 f_b+g_b=2,
 \qquad3f_b+bg_b=5,
 \qquad9f_b+b^2g_b+z_b=14.                                                \tag{20}
\]
Consequently
\[
 G_b=(V_b+W_b,3V_b+bW_b+Z_b)\sim\mathcal N(0,\Sigma)                     \tag{21}
\]
for every \(b\in J\).

Independently construct
\[
 L_e=\sum_{j=1}^d j(N_{e,j}-\lambda_{e,j}),
\]
where the independent Poisson rates have a common positive baseline and the jump-\(j\) rate is increased by a fixed \(\eta>0\) only in environment \(e=j\); environments \(e>d\) retain the baseline rates.  Define
\[
 \epsilon_{u,e}=L_e+V_b,\qquad
 \epsilon_{t,e}=W_b,\qquad
 \epsilon_y=Z_b,\qquad
 \alpha=1,\quad\beta=b,\quad\gamma=3-b.                                 \tag{22}
\]
Before the perturbation below,
\[
 (T_e,Y_e)=(L_e,3L_e)+G_b,                                                 \tag{23}
\]
so the complete observed law is independent of \(b\).  The latent profiles are \(u_k=\eta(1^k,\ldots,d^k,0,\ldots,0)^\top\), whence the leading calibration block is \(\eta V_d\), \(A=\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf e_1,\ldots,\mathbf e_d\}\), and \(u_k\in A\) for all \(k\).

For an admitted \(r\), let \(\operatorname{He}_r\) be the probabilists' Hermite polynomial and set
\[
 h_r(x)=e^{-x^2}\operatorname{He}_r(\sqrt3x),
 \qquad \mu_r=\frac{r!}{3^{(r+1)/2}}.                                    \tag{24}
\]
Both \(h_r\) and its derivative are bounded.  If \(N\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\), the substitution \(u=\sqrt3x\), the Rodrigues identity, and \(r\) integrations by parts give
\[
 \mathbb E[N^kh_r(N)]
 =3^{-(k+1)/2}\mathbb E[N^k\operatorname{He}_r(N)]
 =\begin{cases}0,&0\leq k<r,\\ \mu_r,&k=r.\end{cases}                   \tag{25}
\]
Every boundary term vanishes because a polynomial times a Gaussian density tends to zero at both endpoints.

Only in environment \(e=d+1\), replace the density of \(W_b\) by
\[
 p_{b,\lambda}(w)=\phi_{g_b}(w)
 \left\{1+\frac{\lambda}{\mu_rg_b^{r/2}}
 h_r\!\left(\frac w{\sqrt{g_b}}\right)\right\}.                         \tag{26}
\]
Compactness of \(J\) and finiteness of the admitted order set give one \(\lambda_0>0\), common to all \(r\), for which the bracket lies in \([1/2,3/2]\) whenever \(0<\lambda\leq\lambda_0\).  Formula (25) at \(k=0,1\) proves normalization and centering.  It shows that raw moments below \(r\) are unchanged and the order-\(r\) raw moment increases by exactly \(\lambda\).  Since the order-\(r\) cumulant is the order-\(r\) moment plus a polynomial in lower moments, its increase is also \(\lambda\).  Gaussian domination gives every absolute moment and the required fixed moment envelope.

Because \(r\geq d+2\), the perturbation leaves \(C\) unchanged.  Lower projected contrasts vanish and
\[
 Q_Ac_r=\lambda\mathbf e_{d+1},
 \qquad Q_Am_r=b\lambda\mathbf e_{d+1}.                                  \tag{27}
\]
Choose fixed coefficient bounds containing \(\alpha=1\), \(b\in J\), and \(\gamma=3-b\); choose \(B>23/10\), \(\kappa_0<\eta\sigma_d(V_d)\), and the moment envelope above the compact supremum over \(b\in J\), \(0\leq\lambda\leq\lambda_0\), and the finite order set.  All ambient assumptions then hold with constants common to \(r\), and (27) places each law in \(\mathcal M_r(\lambda)\).

We bound divergence for the complete observations.  Under (21), Gaussian completion of squares gives
\[
 \mathbb E_b[W_b\mid G_b=x]=\ell_b^\top x,
 \qquad \ell_b^\top=g_b(1,b)\Sigma^{-1},                                 \tag{28}
\]
and
\[
 \tau_b^2=\operatorname{Var}_b(W_b\mid G_b)
 =g_b-g_b^2(1,b)\Sigma^{-1}(1,b)^\top
 =-\frac{(b-1)(2b-5)}{3(b-3)^2}>0.                                       \tag{29}
\]
The last quantity is uniformly positive on \(J\).  Conditioning the likelihood ratio in (26) on \(G_b=x\) gives observed density
\[
 \phi_\Sigma(x)\{1+\lambda R_b(x)\},
 \qquad
 R_b(x)=\frac1{\mu_rg_b^{r/2}}
 \mathbb E\left[h_r\!\left(
 \frac{\ell_b^\top x+\tau_bN}{\sqrt{g_b}}\right)\right].               \tag{30}
\]
The coefficients in (28)--(30) are continuously differentiable on \(J\), and \(g_b,\tau_b\) are bounded away from zero.  Boundedness of \(h_r,h_r'\) permits differentiation under the Gaussian expectation with envelope \(K(1+\|x\|_2+|N|)\).  Uniformly over the finite order set,
\[
 |R_b(x)|\leq K_4,
 \qquad |\partial_bR_b(x)|\leq K_5(1+\|x\|_2).                           \tag{31}
\]
The mean-value theorem and the finite second moment of \(\mathcal N(0,\Sigma)\) imply
\[
 \int\{R_b(x)-R_{b'}(x)\}^2\phi_\Sigma(x)\,dx
 \leq K_6|b-b'|^2.                                                        \tag{32}
\]
After reducing \(\lambda_0\) so that \(\lambda_0K_4\leq1/2\), substitution of the two densities into chi-square divergence yields
\[
 \chi^2(P^G_{b,\lambda},P^G_{b',\lambda})
 \leq2K_6\lambda^2|b-b'|^2.                                              \tag{33}
\]
Adding the independent \(L_e\) and applying the common measurable map (23) cannot increase chi-square divergence: the image likelihood ratio is the conditional expectation of the original likelihood ratio, and conditional Jensen's inequality applies to its squared centered value.  The other environments have laws independent of \(b\).  Independence across observations and \(1+x\leq e^x\) therefore give
\[
 1+\chi^2(P_{b,\lambda}^{n},P_{b',\lambda}^{n})
 \leq\exp\{K_7n\lambda^2|b-b'|^2\}.                                    \tag{34}
\]
For dominated laws, Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 \|P-Q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq\frac12\sqrt{\chi^2(P,Q)}.                  \tag{35}
\]

Fix \(b_0=2\), let
\[
 h_n=c\min\left\{B,\frac1{\sqrt n\lambda}\right\},
 \qquad b_1=b_0+h_n,                                                       \tag{36}
\]
and choose fixed \(c>0\) small enough that \(b_1\in J\) and (34)--(35) imply
\[
 \|P_{b_0,\lambda}^{n}-P_{b_1,\lambda}^{n}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \leq v<\min\{1/2,1-2\alpha_0\}.                                       \tag{37}
\]
For any estimator, let \(D\) be the event that it is closer to \(b_0\) than to \(b_1\).  Its squared error is at least \(h_n^2/4\) under the first law on \(D^c\) and under the second law on \(D\).  The definition of total variation gives
\[
 \max_{j=0,1}\mathbb E_{b_j,\lambda}
 (\widetilde\beta-b_j)^2\geq\frac{h_n^2}{8}(1-v).                        \tag{38}
\]
If a random interval covers both parameters with probability at least \(1-\alpha_0\), then under the first law the two coverage events intersect with probability at least \(1-2\alpha_0-v\).  Connectedness forces length at least \(h_n\) there, so
\[
 \mathbb E_{b_0,\lambda}\operatorname{length}(\mathcal I_n)
 \geq h_n(1-2\alpha_0-v).                                                \tag{39}
\]
Both laws lie in the same shell; hence (38)--(39) apply even under shell-only honesty.

For signals \(\lambda\in[\lambda_0,\Lambda]\), the same fixed bounded experiment contains a regular parametric subpath.  Retain a shell member's \(\alpha\), latent sources, and treatment sources; choose fixed \(q\in(q_0,q_1)\), set \(\gamma=\alpha q\), use an independent centered Gaussian outcome noise with fixed positive variance \(\sigma_y^2\), and vary \(\beta\) over a fixed interior interval.  These changes leave \(C,A,r^\star\), and shell strength unchanged and fit the fixed envelopes chosen with slack.  In the more informative experiment observing \((U,T,Y)\), two effects separated by \(h\) have
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_\beta^n,P_{\beta+h}^n)
 =\frac{h^2}{2\sigma_y^2}
   \sum_{e\in\mathcal E}n_e\mathbb E[T_e^2]
 \leq K_8nh^2.                                                           \tag{40}
\]
Marginalization cannot increase relative entropy, by conditional Jensen's inequality for the image likelihood ratio.  Applying data processing to an indicator event attaining total variation and Taylor's theorem for binary relative entropy gives \(\operatorname{KL}(P,Q)\geq2\|P-Q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}^2\).  Taking \(h=c\min\{B,n^{-1/2}\}\) and repeating (38)--(39) gives lower orders \(\min\{B^2,n^{-1}\}\) and \(\min\{B,n^{-1/2}\}\).  Uniformly for \(\lambda\in[\lambda_0,\Lambda]\), these are equivalent, up to constants fixed by the experiment, to the expressions in (1) and (3).

Taking infima over estimators and over intervals honest on the shell, and combining (38)--(40) with the upper bounds, proves the two minimax orders on this one fixed compatible experiment.  Shell-only honesty is weaker than honesty on \(\Theta\), so the interval lower bound also applies to the latter class.

If \(\sqrt n\lambda_n\to\infty\), (1) proves uniform mean-square and hence uniform probabilistic consistency on every nonempty shell sequence.  Conversely, if this divergence fails, some subsequence has \(\sqrt n\lambda_n\) bounded.  Along a further subsequence \(\lambda_n\leq\lambda_0\), and (36)--(38) bound minimax risk away from zero.  If no such further subsequence existed, \(\lambda_n\) would be bounded below and \(\sqrt n\lambda_n\) would diverge, a contradiction.  Thus no uniformly consistent estimator exists on the bounded subsequence.  This proves the if-and-only-if consistency frontier on the fixed lower-bound experiment.
