%%% FIELD thm-moment-order-sharpness-statement
For every \(d\geq1\), \(E\geq d+2\), and \(R_{\max}\geq d+2\), there is one fixed compatible choice of the bounded model \(\Theta\), common to all \(r\in\{d+2,\ldots,R_{\max}\}\), such that for every such \(r\) the resulting \(\mathcal B\) contains a nondegenerate finite-dimensional Poisson--Gaussian subclass and two members \(\theta_0,\theta_1\) satisfying \(\beta(\theta_0)\neq\beta(\theta_1)\), \(r^\star(\theta_0)=r^\star(\theta_1)=r\), and \(\mathbb E_{\theta_0}[T_e^pY_e^{\ell}]=\mathbb E_{\theta_1}[T_e^pY_e^{\ell}]\) for every \(e\in\mathcal E\) and every \(p,\ell\geq0\) with \(p+\ell<r\).
%%% FIELD thm-moment-order-sharpness-proof
Fix \(d\geq1\), \(E\geq d+2\), and \(R_{\max}\geq d+2\).  The already established environment-sharpness theorem shows that \(E\geq d+2\) is exactly the environmental regime in which a finite breakout can occur.  We now construct, with one collection of bounds for all admissible \(r\), unequal-effect pairs that cannot be distinguished by lower-order mixed moments.

For \(r\in\{d+2,\ldots,R_{\max}\}\), define
\[
 \nu_{rj}=(-1)^{r-j}{r\choose j},\qquad 1\leq j\leq r.
\]
The identity
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^{r}(-1)^{r-j}{r\choose j}e^{jx}=(e^x-1)^r
 \tag{1}
\]
is the binomial theorem applied to \(e^x-1\).  Differentiating (1) \(k\) times at zero gives
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^{r}\nu_{rj}j^k=0\quad(1\leq k<r),
 \qquad
 \sum_{j=1}^{r}\nu_{rj}j^r=r!.
 \tag{2}
\]
Thus the signed measure with masses \(\nu_{rj}\) at \(j\) annihilates every positive-order monomial below degree \(r\), but not the degree-\(r\) monomial.

Put
\[
 K=\max_{d+2\leq s\leq R_{\max}}\max_{1\leq j\leq s}|\nu_{sj}|<\infty
\]
and fix \(\eta>0\) and \(0<\varepsilon<(2K)^{-1}\).  Let \(L\) vary in \([1,2]\).  For \(1\leq j\leq R_{\max}\) and \(e\in\mathcal E\), define baseline intensities
\[
 \Lambda^{V}_{e,j}=L+\eta\mathbf 1\{1\leq e=j\leq d\},
 \qquad
 \Lambda^{W}_{e,j}=L+2\eta\mathbf 1\{1\leq e=j\leq d\}.
 \tag{3}
\]
For an intensity vector \(\Lambda=(\Lambda_1,\ldots,\Lambda_{R_{\max}})\) and \(\sigma^2>0\), let
\[
 X(\Lambda,\sigma^2)
 =G_{\sigma}+
   \sum_{j=1}^{R_{\max}}j\{N_j-\Lambda_j\},
 \tag{4}
\]
where \(G_{\sigma}\sim N(0,\sigma^2)\), the variables \(N_j\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\Lambda_j)\) are independent, and the Gaussian variable is independent of all the counts.  Its log moment generating function is
\[
 \log\mathbb E e^{xX(\Lambda,\sigma^2)}
 =\frac{\sigma^2x^2}{2}
  +\sum_{j=1}^{R_{\max}}\Lambda_j(e^{jx}-1-jx),
 \tag{5}
\]
which is finite for every real \(x\).  Formula (4) is centered, and differentiating (5) shows that for every \(k\geq2\),
\[
 \kappa_k\{X(\Lambda,\sigma^2)\}
 =\sigma^2\mathbf 1\{k=2\}+
   \sum_{j=1}^{R_{\max}}\Lambda_jj^k.
 \tag{6}
\]

Choose \((\sigma_V^2,\sigma_W^2,\sigma_y^2)\in[1,2]^3\).  Let \(V_e^\circ\) and \(W_e^\circ\) have the laws in (4) with intensities \(\Lambda^V_e\) and \(\Lambda^W_e\), and variances \(\sigma_V^2\) and \(\sigma_W^2\), respectively.  Let \(Z\sim N(0,\sigma_y^2)\).  All sources are chosen mutually independent within each environment.  Consider first the two unperturbed structural representations
\[
 \begin{array}{c|ccc|ccc}
 &\alpha&\beta&\gamma&\epsilon_{u,e}&\epsilon_{t,e}&\epsilon_y\\ \hline
 \theta_0^\circ&1&2&1&V_e^\circ&W_e^\circ&Z\\
 \theta_1^\circ&1&3&-1&W_e^\circ&V_e^\circ&Z.
 \end{array}
 \tag{7}
\]
The second row is the exact source swap \(S\theta_0^\circ\).  More directly, the structural equations give in both rows
\[
 T_e=V_e^\circ+W_e^\circ,
 \qquad
 Y_e=3V_e^\circ+2W_e^\circ+Z.
 \tag{8}
\]
Hence the complete baseline observed laws, not merely selected moments, coincide.

Fix an admissible \(r\).  Because \(E\geq d+2\), the environment \(e=d+1\) exists.  Form \(W_e^{(r)}\) from \(W_e^\circ\) by replacing, only when \(e=d+1\), its jump-\(j\) intensity by
\[
 \Lambda^W_{d+1,j}+\varepsilon\nu_{rj}\mathbf 1\{j\leq r\}.
 \tag{9}
\]
Define \(V_e^{(r)}\) analogously from \(V_e^\circ\).  Every intensity in (9) is at least
\[
 1-\varepsilon K>\frac12,
 \tag{10}
\]
so both perturbations are probability laws of the form (4), rather than signed laws.  Define
\[
 \begin{array}{c|ccc|ccc}
 &\alpha&\beta&\gamma&\epsilon_{u,e}&\epsilon_{t,e}&\epsilon_y\\ \hline
 \theta_{0,r}&1&2&1&V_e^\circ&W_e^{(r)}&Z\\
 \theta_{1,r}&1&3&-1&W_e^\circ&V_e^{(r)}&Z.
 \end{array}
 \tag{11}
\]
Thus the order-specific perturbation is always placed in the treatment noise.  In particular, it does not alter either latent cumulant profile.

We next prove lower-order observational equality.  Define the entire function
\[
 H_r(x)=\varepsilon\sum_{j=1}^{r}\nu_{rj}(e^{jx}-1-jx).
 \tag{12}
\]
By (2), every derivative of \(H_r\) at zero of order smaller than \(r\) vanishes, whereas
\[
 H_r^{(r)}(0)=\varepsilon r!.
 \tag{13}
\]
Let \(M_e^\circ(s,t)\) denote the common joint moment generating function of the baseline pair in (8).  Outside the spare environment, the observed laws in (11) equal their respective baseline laws and hence agree.  In the spare environment, independence, (5), and the coefficients with which the perturbed treatment source enters \((T,Y)\) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 M_{0,d+1}(s,t)&=M_{d+1}^\circ(s,t)\exp\{H_r(s+2t)\},\\
 M_{1,d+1}(s,t)&=M_{d+1}^\circ(s,t)\exp\{H_r(s+3t)\}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{14}
\]
Equations (12)--(13) imply that \(H_r(x)=x^rg_r(x)\) for an entire \(g_r\).  Consequently, each exponential factor in (14) equals one plus an analytic function all of whose monomials have total degree at least \(r\).  Taking \(p\) derivatives in \(s\) and \(\ell\) derivatives in \(t\) at \((0,0)\), and using that derivatives of a joint moment generating function are mixed moments, yields
\[
 \mathbb E_{\theta_{0,r}}[T_e^pY_e^\ell]
 =\mathbb E_{\theta_{1,r}}[T_e^pY_e^\ell]
 \quad
 \text{for every }e\in\mathcal E\text{ and }p+\ell<r.
 \tag{15}
\]
This also covers total degrees zero and one because the laws are normalized and the sources are centered.

It remains to verify the calibration plane, the first breakout, and uniform membership in one bounded model.  By (3), (6), and (2), the treatment cumulant contrasts of either member of (11) are, for every \(k\geq2\),
\[
 c_k
 =3\eta\sum_{j=1}^{d}j^k\mathbf e_j
  +h_{r,k}\mathbf e_{d+1},
 \qquad
 h_{r,k}=\varepsilon\sum_{j=1}^{r}\nu_{rj}j^k.
 \tag{16}
\]
In particular, \(h_{r,k}=0\) for \(2\leq k<r\), and \(h_{r,r}=\varepsilon r!\).  Since \(r\geq d+2\), the calibration matrix is
\[
 C=3\eta
 \begin{bmatrix}
 V_d\\
 0_{(E-1-d)\times d}
 \end{bmatrix},
 \qquad
 V_d=[j^k]_{j=1,\ldots,d;\ k=2,\ldots,d+1}.
 \tag{17}
\]
Factoring \(j^2\) from row \(j\) reduces \(V_d\) to the ordinary Vandermonde matrix, so
\[
 \det(V_d)=
 \left(\prod_{j=1}^{d}j^2\right)
 \left(\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq d}(j-i)\right)\neq0.
 \tag{18}
\]
Therefore \(C\) has rank \(d\),
\[
 A=\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf e_1,\ldots,\mathbf e_d\},
 \qquad
 \sigma_d(C)=3\eta\sigma_d(V_d)>0,
 \tag{19}
\]
and \(Q_A\) deletes precisely the first \(d\) coordinates.  Moreover, the latent profiles in (11) are
\[
 u_k(\theta_{0,r})=\eta\sum_{j=1}^{d}j^k\mathbf e_j,
 \qquad
 u_k(\theta_{1,r})=2\eta\sum_{j=1}^{d}j^k\mathbf e_j,
 \tag{20}
\]
for every \(k\geq2\).  Thus both profiles belong to \(A\) at every order.  Finally, (2) and (16) give
\[
 Q_Ac_k=0\quad(d+2\leq k<r),
 \qquad
 Q_Ac_r=\varepsilon r!\mathbf e_{d+1}\neq0.
 \tag{21}
\]
By the definition of the first breakout, (21) proves
\[
 r^\star(\theta_{0,r})=r^\star(\theta_{1,r})=r.
 \tag{22}
\]

Choose the fixed coefficient bounds
\[
 B=4,qquad a_0=\frac12,qquad a_1=\frac32,qquad
 q_0=\frac12,qquad q_1=\frac32,
 \tag{23}
\]
and choose
\[
 \kappa_0=\frac32\eta\sigma_d(V_d)>0.
 \tag{24}
\]
Both rows of (11) satisfy the effect, treatment-loading, and two-sided confounding bounds because their coefficient triples are \((1,2,1)\) and \((1,3,-1)\).  Equations (18)--(20) verify the calibration-rank, calibration-gap, and latent-profile-plane conditions.  Centering and within-environment source independence hold by construction, and (5) verifies the entire-moment-generating-function condition.

For completeness, one moment envelope works for every \(r\) and every parameter value just used.  Across the finite collection of orders, all intensities are bounded above by \(2+2\eta+\varepsilon K\), all jumps are bounded by \(R_{\max}\), and all Gaussian variances are at most two.  Hence (5) is bounded uniformly over this collection at \(x=1\) and \(x=-1\).  For \(s=2R_{\max}+\delta\), the elementary bound
\[
 |x|^s\leq C_s(e^x+e^{-x}),
 \qquad
 C_s=\sup_{z\geq0}z^se^{-z}<\infty,
 \tag{25}
\]
therefore gives a finite uniform upper bound on the required absolute source moments.  Choose \(M\) strictly larger than this bound.  The same \(M\), the same \(\kappa_0\), and the coefficient bounds in (23) apply to every \(r\).  Thus each parameter in (11) belongs to this single \(\Theta\), and (22), together with \(d+2\leq r\leq R_{\max}\), puts it in \(\mathcal B\).

For each \(r\), let \(L\) and the three Gaussian variances range over \([1,2]^4\), and include both rows of (11).  This is a finite-dimensional subclass with nonempty continuous parameter rectangles; its compound-Poisson intensities are strictly positive by (10), and its Gaussian components have strictly positive variances.  It is therefore a nondegenerate Poisson--Gaussian subclass of \(\mathcal B\).  Choosing the same interior rectangle point in its two rows gives members \(\theta_0=\theta_{0,r}\) and \(\theta_1=\theta_{1,r}\) for which (15) and (22) hold, while
\[
 \beta(\theta_0)=2\neq3=\beta(\theta_1).
\]
Because all bounds and the subclass parameter rectangle were chosen independently of \(r\), this proves the claim with one fixed compatible bounded model.
