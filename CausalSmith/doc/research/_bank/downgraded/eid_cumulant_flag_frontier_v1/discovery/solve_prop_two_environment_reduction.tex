Assume \(d=1\) and \(E=2\).  Then \(E=d+1\).  By the rank assumption \(\operatorname{rank}(C)=d=1\), the hypotheses of \(\text{thm:environment-sharpness}\) apply.  Its boundary conclusion gives
\[
A=\mathbb R^d=\mathbb R
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
Q_A=0.
\]
Equivalently, writing the one-by-one calibration matrix as \(C=[c_2]\), rank one implies \(c_2\ne0\), so \(C^\top C=c_2^2>0\) and
\[
Q_A
=I_1-C(C^\top C)^{-1}C^\top
=1-c_2(c_2^2)^{-1}c_2
=0.
\]
Thus \(Q_Ac_k=0\) for every integer \(k\ge3=d+2\).  Hence the set \(\{k\ge d+2:Q_Ac_k\ne0\}\) is empty and the first-breakout convention yields \(r^\star=\infty\).  In particular no parameter satisfying the calibration condition can have a finite breakout, and therefore the finite-breakout branch obeys
\[
\mathcal B=\varnothing.
\]

Now let \(\theta\in\mathcal N_\rho\).  The already established \(\text{thm:identification-frontier}\), applied to this no-breakout parameter, states that \(\theta\) and \(S\theta\) induce the same observed law in every environment:
\[
P_e^{\theta}=P_e^{S\theta}
\qquad(e\in\mathcal E).
\]
By the source-swap definition, their direct treatment effects are respectively
\[
\beta(\theta)=\beta,
\qquad
\beta(S\theta)=\beta+\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}.
\]
The same frontier theorem states that these two effects are distinct; its conclusion uses the maintained nonzero-confounding bound in the ambient model.  Moreover, membership in \(\mathcal N_\rho\) makes this a legal swap pair, and the theorem places both representations in \(\mathcal D_\rho\).  Consequently every nonempty surviving no-breakout branch exhibits observational equivalence with unequal causal effects.  This is exactly the simultaneous-source ambiguity asserted by the proposition at the two-environment boundary; the reference to Kivva et al., Theorem 3.6, is a comparison of scope and is not used as a proof premise.
