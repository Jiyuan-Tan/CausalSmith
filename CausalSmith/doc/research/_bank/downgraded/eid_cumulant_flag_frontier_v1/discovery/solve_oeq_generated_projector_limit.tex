%%% FIELD generated-projector-dichotomy-statement
The answer is a randomized-versus-deterministic calibration dichotomy.  Put
\[
 \nu=\min\{1,\delta/R_{\max}\},
 \qquad
 \varrho_{\mathrm{AR}}=\frac{\nu}{8+4\nu}.
\]
For every deterministic choice \(0<\chi<\varrho_{\mathrm{AR}}\) and every deterministic integer sequence \(m_n\to\infty\), the randomized-jitter Gaussian-multiplier Anderson--Rubin set \(\mathcal C_n^{\mathrm{RJ}}\) is implementable and satisfies
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}
 \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}
 \left|
 \Pr_\theta\{\beta\in\mathcal C_n^{\mathrm{RJ}}\}
 -(1-\alpha_0)
 \right|=0.                                                   \tag{1}
\]
Consequently, for every fixed \(r\in\{d+2,\ldots,R_{\max}\}\) and every positive sequence \(\lambda_n\) for which \(\mathcal M_r(\lambda_n)\) is nonempty for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}
 \sup_{\theta\in\mathcal M_r(\lambda_n)}
 \left|
 \Pr_\theta\{\beta\in\mathcal C_n^{\mathrm{RJ}}\}
 -(1-\alpha_0)
 \right|=0.                                                   \tag{2}
\]
Both probabilities are unconditional over the observations, Gaussian multipliers, observed-statistic jitter, and Monte Carlo jitters.  The conclusions remain valid when the same-sample estimated-\(A\) influence covariance is singular, changes rank along a triangular array, or is identically zero; no covariance inverse or lower positive-eigenvalue bound is required.  The jitter magnitude is \(\tau_n=n^{-\chi}\to0\), so (1)--(2) give asymptotically exact, rather than conservative, null calibration while the added randomization vanishes on the root-\(n\) projected-moment scale.

This randomization cannot in general be replaced by either deterministic convention at an atom.  If a reference statistic has distribution function \(F\), has an atom at \(x_0\), and
\[
 F(x_0-)<1-\alpha_0<F(x_0),                                  \tag{3}
\]
then the deterministic rules accepting below \(x_0\) and accepting at or below \(x_0\) have acceptance probabilities \(F(x_0-)\) and \(F(x_0)\), respectively, and neither equals \(1-\alpha_0\).  Randomized tie-breaking does attain the target.  Thus implementable vanishing-jitter Gaussian-multiplier calibration answers the question affirmatively, whereas deterministic strict or weak single-threshold calibration is not uniformly exact at all atomic laws.

%%% FIELD generated-projector-dichotomy-proof
By the first-breakout-shell definition,
\[
 \mathcal M_r(\lambda_n)\subseteq\Theta                         \tag{4}
\]
for each admissible \(r\) and \(\lambda_n\).  The balanced-share premise used by the ambient randomized calibration theorem gives \(n_e\geq\underline\pi n\) in every environment.  Since \(R_{\max}\) is fixed and \(\underline\pi>0\), every environment therefore has at least \(R_{\max}\) observations for all sufficiently large \(n\); before that boundary, the randomized Gaussian Anderson--Rubin definition supplies the explicit fallback \(\mathcal C_n^{\mathrm{RJ}}=[-B,B]\).  Hence its construction is implementable at every sample size and its cumulant statistics are well defined whenever they are used.

Fix \(0<\chi<\varrho_{\mathrm{AR}}\) and a deterministic sequence \(m_n\to\infty\).  The ambient randomized generated-projector calibration theorem applies under the balanced-share, moment-envelope, and calibration-gap conditions incorporated into its hypotheses.  It proves
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}
 \left|
 \Pr_\theta\{\beta\in\mathcal C_n^{\mathrm{RJ}}\}
 -(1-\alpha_0)
 \right|\longrightarrow0.                                    \tag{5}
\]
That theorem uses the generated-projector influence expansion at the true candidate and a multiplier covariance comparison that never divides by an eigenvalue.  Its conclusion therefore applies to every positive semidefinite influence covariance, including a singular covariance, a covariance whose rank changes with \(n\), and the zero matrix.  Equation (5) is exactly (1).

For a nonempty shell, (4) and the elementary monotonicity of a supremum give, for every sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\sup_{\theta\in\mathcal M_r(\lambda_n)}
 \left|
 \Pr_\theta\{\beta\in\mathcal C_n^{\mathrm{RJ}}\}
 -(1-\alpha_0)
 \right| \\
 &\qquad\leq
 \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}
 \left|
 \Pr_\theta\{\beta\in\mathcal C_n^{\mathrm{RJ}}\}
 -(1-\alpha_0)
 \right|.
                                                               \tag{6}
 \end{aligned}
\]
Taking limits in (6) and using (5) proves (2).  Because the probability in (5) is unconditional over every auxiliary draw in the randomized Gaussian Anderson--Rubin definition, the same is true in (6).

We make explicit why the construction also covers atomic reference laws.  Let \(G_n\) be a centered Gaussian vector with any positive semidefinite covariance, possibly zero, and let \(\xi\sim N(0,1)\) be independent.  For \(\tau_n=n^{-\chi}>0\), the distribution function of the jittered comparison statistic is
\[
 F_n^{\circ}(t)
 =\Pr\{\|G_n\|_{\max}+\tau_n\xi\leq t\}
 =\mathbb E\!\left[
   \Phi\!\left(\frac{t-\|G_n\|_{\max}}{\tau_n}\right)
  \right].                                                    \tag{7}
\]
Dominated differentiation is licensed by the bounded normal density, and its density is
\[
 f_n^{\circ}(t)
 =\mathbb E\!\left[
   \tau_n^{-1}\phi\!\left(
    \frac{t-\|G_n\|_{\max}}{\tau_n}
   \right)
  \right]>0                                                   \tag{8}
\]
for every real \(t\), because \(\tau_n>0\) and the standard normal density is everywhere positive.  Thus the comparison distribution is continuous and strictly increasing even if the unjittered statistic is a point mass.  The ambient calibration theorem couples the observed and conditional multiplier laws to (7), and its quantitative covariance-replacement rate is negligible relative to \(\tau_n^2\).  Its continuous-distribution inversion and Monte Carlo approximation with \(m_n\to\infty\) therefore yield the exact limit (5).  Finally, \(\chi>0\) gives \(\tau_n\to0\), which proves that the device removing the atoms vanishes asymptotically.

It remains to verify the deterministic part of the dichotomy.  Let \(X\) have distribution function \(F\) and an atom at \(x_0\).  Then
\[
 \Pr\{X<x_0\}=F(x_0-),
 \qquad
 \Pr\{X\leq x_0\}=F(x_0).                                  \tag{9}
\]
Under (3), neither quantity in (9) equals \(1-\alpha_0\).  The denominator in
\[
 q_0=
 \frac{1-\alpha_0-F(x_0-)}{F(x_0)-F(x_0-)}                    \tag{10}
\]
is strictly positive because \(x_0\) is an atom, and (3) gives \(0<q_0<1\).  If \(U\) is uniform on \((0,1)\), independent of \(X\), the rule accepting when \(X<x_0\), or when \(X=x_0\) and \(U\leq q_0\), has probability
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Pr\{X<x_0\}+q_0\Pr\{X=x_0\}
 &=F(x_0-)+q_0\{F(x_0)-F(x_0-)\}\\
 &=1-\alpha_0.                                                \tag{11}
 \end{aligned}
\]
Equations (9)--(11) prove precisely that the two deterministic single-threshold conventions fail at a straddling atom while randomized tie-breaking succeeds.  Together with (5)--(8), this proves every assertion.
