%%% FIELD proof-individual-aggregation-bridge
Write the normalized time effects as \(\lambda _1=0\) and \(\lambda_t\in\mathbb R\) for \(2\le t\le T\).  For every \(g\in C\) and \(1\le i\le n_g\), define
\[
Q_{gi}:=\sum_{s<g}Y_{gis},
\qquad
L_g(\lambda):=\sum_{s<g}\exp(\lambda_s).
\tag{1}
\]
Every cohort in \(C\subseteq\{2,\ldots,T,\infty\}\) is untreated in period \(1\), so \(L_g(\lambda)>0\).  Conditional on \(\lambda\), the terms of the individual PPML criterion that depend on \(\alpha_{gi}\) are
\[
\ell_{gi}(\alpha_{gi};\lambda)
=Q_{gi}\alpha_{gi}
+\sum_{s<g}Y_{gis}\lambda_s
-\exp(\alpha_{gi})L_g(\lambda).
\tag{2}
\]
If \(Q_{gi}=0\), nonnegativity of the outcomes gives
\(\partial\ell_{gi}/\partial\alpha_{gi}=-\exp(\alpha_{gi})L_g(\lambda)<0\), so the supremum is approached only as \(\alpha_{gi}\to-\infty\).  The assumed finite existence of the individual fit therefore implies \(Q_{gi}>0\).  Moreover,
\[
\frac{\partial\ell_{gi}}{\partial\alpha_{gi}}
=Q_{gi}-\exp(\alpha_{gi})L_g(\lambda),
\qquad
\frac{\partial^2\ell_{gi}}{\partial\alpha_{gi}^2}
=-\exp(\alpha_{gi})L_g(\lambda)<0.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus the unique profiled intercept and the resulting fitted value are
\[
\widehat\alpha_{gi}(\lambda)
=\log Q_{gi}-\log L_g(\lambda),
\qquad
\exp\{\widehat\alpha_{gi}(\lambda)+\lambda_t\}
=\frac{Q_{gi}\exp(\lambda_t)}{L_g(\lambda)}.
\tag{4}
\]

Set \(\overline Q_g:=\sum_{s<g}\overline Y_{gs}\).  By the definition of the cohort mean,
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{n_g}Q_{gi}=n_g\overline Q_g.
\tag{5}
\]
Summing (4) over the individuals in cohort \(g\), for every \(1\le t\le T\), including treated periods, gives
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{n_g}
\exp\{\widehat\alpha_{gi}(\lambda)+\lambda_t\}
=n_g\frac{\overline Q_g\exp(\lambda_t)}{L_g(\lambda)}.
\tag{6}
\]

We next identify the common time effects.  In the cohort-mean parameterization write \(x_{gt}^{\prime}\beta=\gamma_g+\lambda_t\), with \(\lambda_1=0\).  The cohort-intercept coordinate of
\(X^{\prime}\widehat P_U[\overline Y_U-\exp(X\beta)]=0\) is
\[
0=\widehat p_g\sum_{s<g}
\{\overline Y_{gs}-\exp(\gamma_g+\lambda_s)\}.
\tag{7}
\]
The sample-share condition and the positive-share assumption give
\(\widehat p_g=n_g/N=p_g>0\).  Hence the unique finite profiled cohort intercept satisfies
\[
\exp\{\widehat\gamma_g(\lambda)\}
=\frac{\overline Q_g}{L_g(\lambda)},
\qquad
\widetilde\mu_{gt}(\lambda)
:=\exp\{\widehat\gamma_g(\lambda)+\lambda_t\}
=\frac{\overline Q_g\exp(\lambda_t)}{L_g(\lambda)}.
\tag{8}
\]

At the assumed finite individual maximizer, each unrestricted time-effect score vanishes.  For \(2\le t\le T\), substituting (6) and using \(n_g/N=p_g\) yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
0
&=\frac1N\sum_{g:\,t<g}\sum_{i=1}^{n_g}
\left[Y_{git}-
\exp\{\widehat\alpha_{gi}(\lambda)+\lambda_t\}\right]\\
&=\sum_{g:\,t<g}p_g
\left[\overline Y_{gt}-
\frac{\overline Q_g\exp(\lambda_t)}{L_g(\lambda)}\right]\\
&=\sum_{g:\,t<g}p_g
\{\overline Y_{gt}-\widetilde\mu_{gt}(\lambda)\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
Equation (9) is exactly the time coordinate of the cohort-mean PPML score after its cohort intercepts have been profiled by (8).  Conversely, (8) and (9) give all coordinates of that score.  Therefore the normalized time component of the individual fit is a normalized solution of the cohort-mean score equations.  Uniqueness of both normalized fits makes their time components equal.  Combining (6) and (8) at this common solution proves
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{n_g}\widehat\mu^{\mathrm{MK}}_{git}
=n_g\widehat\mu_{gt}
\qquad(g\in C,\ 1\le t\le T).
\tag{10}
\]

It remains to compare the reported ratios.  The treated-support assumption gives \(D\ne\varnothing\).  Since \(n_g\ge1\) and every fitted exponential is strictly positive, both treated imputation denominators are strictly positive.  Thus the positive-denominator branches of both definitions apply.  The definitions of \(\widehat A\) and \(\widehat B\), the equality \(n_g=Np_g\), and (10) give
\[
\sum_{(g,t)\in D}\sum_{i=1}^{n_g}Y_{git}
=\sum_{(g,t)\in D}n_g\overline Y_{gt}
=N\sum_{(g,t)\in D}p_g\overline Y_{gt}
=N\widehat A,
\tag{11}
\]
and
\[
\sum_{(g,t)\in D}\sum_{i=1}^{n_g}
\widehat\mu^{\mathrm{MK}}_{git}
=\sum_{(g,t)\in D}n_g\widehat\mu_{gt}
=N\sum_{(g,t)\in D}p_g\widehat\mu_{gt}
=N\widehat B.
\tag{12}
\]
Cancelling the common positive factor \(N\) in (11)--(12) proves
\[
\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{MK}}^{\mathrm{ind}}
=\frac{N\widehat A}{N\widehat B}-1
=\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{CM}}.
\tag{13}
\]
This is a deterministic sample identity.  The within-cohort sampling assumption is part of the stated statistical setting but is not needed for (1)--(13).

%%% FIELD statement-mk-positive-cycle-nonuniversality
For every fixed adoption calendar \(C\) admitted by \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{bd}}\) with \(q>0\), there exist strictly positive rational shares \(p_g\) summing to one, deterministic balanced-panel sequences with \(n_g/N=p_g\), and a law \(P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{bd}}\) supported on \((0,\infty)^T\) such that
\[
\mathbb E_P[Y_{gt}(1)]=\mathbb E_P[Y_{gt}(0)]\quad((g,t)\in U),
\qquad
\mathbb E_P[Y_{gt}(0)]=a_gb_t\quad(g\in C,\ 1\le t\le T).
\]
On the balanced, equal-observation-weight, no-covariate, no-offset intersection with the Moreau--Kastler individual-imputation specification, both normalized PPML fits exist uniquely, \(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{MK}}^{\mathrm{ind}}=\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{CM}}\) samplewise, and
\[
\operatorname{AVar}_P\!\left(\sqrt N\{\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{MK}}^{\mathrm{ind}}-\theta_{\mathrm{PTT}}\}\right)-V^{\star}
=\Gamma>0.
\]
The restriction of \(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{eff}}\), which is regular on the larger cohort-mean experiment, is therefore a strictly lower-variance regular competitor on this intersection.  Consequently the published Moreau--Kastler individual-imputation estimator is not universally semiparametrically efficient when \(q>0\).  This one-way conclusion neither identifies \(V^{\star}\) as the efficiency bound in the unrestricted richer individual-level experiment nor transfers the \(q=0\) cohort-mean efficiency conclusion to that experiment.

%%% FIELD proof-mk-positive-cycle-nonuniversality
Fix an adoption calendar \(C\) admitted by \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{bd}}\) and suppose \(q>0\).  Put
\[
p_g=|C|^{-1}\quad(g\in C),
\qquad
n_{g,k}=k,
\qquad
N_k=k|C|,
\tag{1}
\]
for integers \(k\ge1\).  These shares are strictly positive and rational, sum to one, and satisfy \(n_{g,k}/N_k=p_g\) exactly.  Choose any positive normalized factor vectors \(a,b\) and any strictly positive treated-cell means.  They determine a strictly positive interior observed mean vector \(m\), with \(m_{gt}=a_gb_t\) on \(U\).

Because \(q>0\), choose a nonzero sufficiently small covariance coordinate \(x\) in the fixed-support family.  The realizable-quadratic-loss theorem gives a corresponding \(P_\rho\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{bd}}\) with mean \(m\), unchanged cycle Gram matrix \(K_0\succ0\), and
\[
\Gamma
=B^{-2}x^{\prime}D_\mu^{-1}K_0^{-1}D_\mu^{-1}x
>0.
\tag{2}
\]
The strict inequality follows because \(B>0\), every diagonal entry of \(D_\mu\) is positive, \(K_0^{-1}\succ0\), and \(x\ne0\).  This law is in fact supported on strictly positive outcomes.  Indeed, the bounded-family definition gives
\[
Y_{gt}=m_{gt}+s^0_{gt}\varepsilon_{gt},
\qquad
s^0_{gt}=\sqrt{\tau_0m_{gt}},
\qquad
0<\tau_0<\min_{g,t}m_{gt}.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus \(0<s^0_{gt}<m_{gt}\), and since \(\varepsilon_{gt}\in\{-1,1\}\),
\[
Y_{gt}\ge m_{gt}-s^0_{gt}>0
\tag{4}
\]
at every support point.  The support is finite, so it is bounded and has finite fourth moments.

The causal completion in the same bounded-family definition sets \(Y_{gt}(1)=Y_{gt}(0)=Y_{gt}\) on \(U\), and sets \(Y_{gt}(0)=\mu_{gt}=a_gb_t\) on \(D\).  Consequently
\[
\mathbb E_{P_\rho}[Y_{gt}(1)]
=\mathbb E_{P_\rho}[Y_{gt}(0)]
\quad((g,t)\in U),
\qquad
\mathbb E_{P_\rho}[Y_{gt}(0)]=a_gb_t
\quad(g\in C,\ 1\le t\le T).
\tag{5}
\]
This proves average no anticipation and multiplicative parallel trends; consistency is part of the same completion.  In the Moreau--Kastler individual-effects notation the subclass is embedded by the cohort-constant choices \(\alpha_{gi}=\log a_g\) and time effects \(\beta_t^{\mathrm{MK}}=\log b_t\), with \(\beta_1^{\mathrm{MK}}=0\), equal observation weights, and no covariates or offsets.

For completeness, both sample PPML fits are finite and unique under this law.  Every realized untreated outcome is positive by (4).  Under the normalization of the first-period time effect, the individual untreated design has full column rank: if a perturbation obeys \(\delta\alpha_{gi}+\delta\lambda_t=0\) on every untreated observation, the period-one observations give \(\delta\alpha_{gi}=0\) for all \(g,i\), and the never-treated cohort's observations then give \(\delta\lambda_t=0\) for every \(t\).  The same argument, with one intercept per cohort, gives full column rank of \(X\).  For either design matrix \(W\), positive weights \(w_j\), and positive responses \(y_j\), the criterion
\[
\ell(\xi)=\sum_jw_j\{y_jW_j^{\prime}\xi-
\exp(W_j^{\prime}\xi)\}
\tag{6}
\]
has Hessian
\[
\nabla^2\ell(\xi)
=-W^{\prime}\operatorname{diag}
\{w_j\exp(W_j^{\prime}\xi)\}W\prec0.
\tag{7}
\]
It is also coercive.  Along any unbounded parameter sequence, pass to a subsequence whose normalized direction converges.  Full column rank supplies a nonzero limiting fitted-index direction.  If any fitted index diverges positively, its negative exponential term sends (6) to \(-\infty\); otherwise a fitted index diverges negatively, and its strictly positive response makes the corresponding linear term tend to \(-\infty\).  Hence every upper level set is bounded, so continuity and (7) give a finite unique normalized maximizer.  The aggregation-bridge proposition now applies to (1) and yields the exact sample identity
\[
\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{MK}}^{\mathrm{ind}}
=\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{CM}}.
\tag{8}
\]

It remains to compare variances without importing an efficiency bound into a different experiment.  The cohort-mean PPML linearization gives asymptotic variance \(h_{\mathrm{CM}}^{\prime}\Omega h_{\mathrm{CM}}\).  The weighted-cycle and canonical-gradient results give
\[
J^{\prime}h_{\mathrm{CM}}=J^{\prime}h^{\star}=c,
\qquad
\ker(J^{\prime})=\operatorname{col}(R),
\qquad
R^{\prime}\Omega h^{\star}=0.
\tag{9}
\]
Therefore \(h_{\mathrm{CM}}-h^{\star}=R\gamma\) for some \(\gamma\), and multiplication by \(R^{\prime}\Omega\) gives
\[
b_{\mathrm{cyc}}=K\gamma,
\qquad
\gamma=K^{-1}b_{\mathrm{cyc}}.
\tag{10}
\]
Using (9)--(10), the definition of \(\Gamma\), and
\(V^{\star}=h^{\star\prime}\Omega h^{\star}\), we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
h_{\mathrm{CM}}^{\prime}\Omega h_{\mathrm{CM}}-V^{\star}
&=2h^{\star\prime}\Omega R\gamma
+\gamma^{\prime}R^{\prime}\Omega R\gamma\\
&=\gamma^{\prime}K\gamma
=b_{\mathrm{cyc}}^{\prime}K^{-1}b_{\mathrm{cyc}}
=\Gamma.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
Equations (2), (8), and (11) imply
\[
\operatorname{AVar}_{P_\rho}\!\left(
\sqrt{N_k}\{\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{MK}}^{\mathrm{ind}}
-\theta_{\mathrm{PTT}}\}\right)-V^{\star}
=\Gamma>0.
\tag{12}
\]

Finally, the efficient-attainment theorem says that \(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{eff}}\) is regular on the larger cohort-mean experiment and has variance \(V^{\star}\).  Restricting a regular estimator to the displayed Moreau--Kastler intersection preserves regularity, so (12) exhibits a regular competitor with strictly smaller variance.  This refutes universal efficiency of the individual-imputation estimator for every calendar with \(q>0\).  The argument uses \(V^{\star}\) as the attained variance of that competitor and as the cohort-mean bound only; it neither identifies the bound for the unrestricted richer individual-level experiment nor makes any assertion about individual-level efficiency when \(q=0\), where (2) supplies no strict-loss direction.
