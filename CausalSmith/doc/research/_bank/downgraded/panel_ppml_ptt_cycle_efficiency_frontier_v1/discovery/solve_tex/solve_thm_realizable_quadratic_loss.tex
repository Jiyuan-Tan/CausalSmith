%%% FIELD proof-realizable-quadratic-loss
Fix the interior mean vector \(m\) and suppose \(q>0\).  Let
\[
\mathcal E=U\setminus\mathcal T_0
=\{(g,t):g<\infty,\ 2\le t<g\}
\]
index the \(q\) fundamental cycles.  The definition of \(\tau_0\) gives, for every observed coordinate,
\[
0<s^0_{gt}=\sqrt{\tau_0m_{gt}}<m_{gt}.                 \tag{1}
\]
For a finite cohort \(g\), the mass function in the definition of \(P_\rho\) obeys
\[
2^{-T}\left[1+\sum_{t=2}^{g-1}\rho_{gt}e_te_g\right]
\ge
2^{-T}\left[1-\sum_{t=2}^{g-1}|\rho_{gt}|\right]>0.   \tag{2}
\]
Its masses sum to one because each nonconstant Rademacher character has zero sum on \(\{-1,1\}^T\).  Orthogonality of those characters also yields
\[
\mathbb E_\rho[\varepsilon_{gj}]=0,
\qquad
\mathbb E_\rho[\varepsilon_{gj}^2]=1,
\qquad
\mathbb E_\rho[\varepsilon_{gt}\varepsilon_{gg}]=\rho_{gt}
\quad(2\le t<g),                                      \tag{3}
\]
and every other distinct-coordinate covariance is zero.  Consequently the observed vector
\(Y_g=m_g+s_g^0\odot\varepsilon_g\) has mean \(m_g\), marginal variances \((s^0_{gj})^2\), and its only off-diagonal covariances are
\[
\operatorname{Cov}_{P_\rho}(Y_{gt},Y_{gg})
=s^0_{gt}s^0_{gg}\rho_{gt}=x_{gt},
\qquad (g,t)\in\mathcal E.                             \tag{4}
\]
Every vertex of the hypercube has positive probability by (2).  If \(v\ne0\), the function \(e\mapsto v^{\prime}(s_g^0\odot e)\) is not constant on that hypercube, because changing a coordinate with \(v_js^0_{gj}\ne0\) changes its value.  Hence
\(v^{\prime}\Sigma_g(\rho)v>0\), so each covariance block is positive definite.  Equation (1) gives
\[
0<m_{gj}-s^0_{gj}\le Y_{gj}\le m_{gj}+s^0_{gj}<\infty. \tag{5}
\]
There are finitely many cohorts and coordinates, so (5) supplies a common finite bound and all fourth moments are finite.

Take independent draws from this vector law within each cohort and independent samples across distinct cohorts, retaining the fixed positive shares and sample-size sequences from \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{bd}}\).  Use the potential-outcome completion specified in the definition of \(P_\rho\): observed and untreated potential outcomes agree on \(U\), the observed and treated potential outcomes agree on \(D\), and the missing untreated outcome on \(D\) is the constant \(\mu_{gt}\).  Consistency and no anticipation then hold, and
\(\mathbb E_PY_{gt}(0)=\mu_{gt}=a_gb_t\) at every cohort--time cell.  Positivity follows from the positive-factor assumption.  Together with the stipulated stratified sampling and shares in \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{bd}}\), (2)--(5) prove
\[
P_\rho\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{bd}}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\mathbb E_{P_\rho}Y=m.                                \tag{6}
\]

We next calculate the loss without an approximation.  At \(\rho=0\),
\[
\Sigma_{g0}=\operatorname{diag}((s^0_{g1})^2,\ldots,(s^0_{gT})^2)
=\tau_0\operatorname{diag}(m_g).
\]
Because \(m_{gt}=\mu_{gt}\) on \(U\), the untreated coordinates of the product of the base covariance and the PPML coefficient are
\[
(\Omega(0)h_{\mathrm{CM}})_U
=\tau_0M P_U^{-1}h_{\mathrm{CM},U}
=\tau_0MX S^{-1}c_\beta.                              \tag{7}
\]
The weighted-cycle theorem gives \(R^{\prime}J=0\); since \(R_D=0\) and \(J_U=MX\), it follows from (7) that
\[
b_{\mathrm{cyc}}(0)=R^{\prime}\Omega(0)h_{\mathrm{CM}}=0. \tag{8}
\]
Every perturbation in (4) connects the untreated coordinate \((g,t)\) to the treated coordinate \((g,g)\).  It therefore leaves the untreated--untreated covariance block unchanged.  Since every column of \(R\) is supported on \(U\),
\[
K(x)=R^{\prime}\Omega(x)R
=R^{\prime}\Omega(0)R=K_0.                            \tag{9}
\]

For completeness, calculate the response of every cycle coordinate directly.  From the definitions of \(\Omega\), \(\alpha^{\mathrm{CM}}\), and \(F\), for every \((g,j)\in U\),
\[
(\Omega h_{\mathrm{CM}})_{gj}
=\frac1{p_g}\sum_{s=1}^T
\operatorname{Cov}_P(Y_{gj},Y_{gs})h_{\mathrm{CM},gs}
=\mu_{gj}F_{gj}.                                      \tag{10}
\]
Thus the row associated with the fundamental cycle \(e=(g,t)\) satisfies
\[
r_e^{\prime}\Omega h_{\mathrm{CM}}
=F_{g1}-F_{gt}-F_{\infty1}+F_{\infty t}.              \tag{11}
\]
On a treated cell, the PPML coefficient definition gives
\[
\alpha^{\mathrm{CM}}_{gg}
=h_{\mathrm{CM},gg}/p_g=1/B.                          \tag{12}
\]
The covariance change in (4) consequently changes \(F_{gt}\) by
\(x_{gt}/(B\mu_{gt})\).  It changes none of the other four terms in (11), and a perturbation indexed by a different element of \(\mathcal E\) changes none of the terms in this cycle response.  Combining (8), (11), and (12), in the ordering of the columns of \(R\), gives the exact diagonal map
\[
b_{\mathrm{cyc}}(x)
=-B^{-1}D_\mu^{-1}x.                                  \tag{13}
\]
The base covariance \(\Omega(0)\) is positive definite by positive shares and positive diagonal variances, while \(R\) has rank \(q\) by the weighted-cycle theorem.  Hence \(K_0\succ0\).  Substitution of (9) and (13) into the cycle-loss definition gives
\[
\Gamma(x)
=B^{-2}x^{\prime}D_\mu^{-1}K_0^{-1}D_\mu^{-1}x.       \tag{14}
\]
The matrix in (14) is positive definite because \(B>0\), \(D_\mu\) has a positive diagonal, and \(K_0\succ0\).  Therefore \(\Gamma(x)>0\) for every nonzero admissible \(x\).

It remains to prove ambient, rather than merely pathwise, realizability.  Suppose
\(\|\Sigma-\Sigma_0\|_{\mathcal A}<\delta_0\).  For each cohort, the Rayleigh quotient and the definition of \(\delta_0\) imply
\[
\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma_g)
\ge \lambda_{\min}(\Sigma_{g0})
-\|\Sigma_g-\Sigma_{g0}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
>\lambda_0/2>0.                                        \tag{15}
\]
Let \(A_g=\Sigma_g^{1/2}\), \(A_{g0}=\Sigma_{g0}^{1/2}=\operatorname{diag}(s_g^0)\), and \(Q_g=A_g-A_{g0}\).  Direct multiplication shows that
\[
A_gQ_g+Q_gA_{g0}=\Sigma_g-\Sigma_{g0}.
\]
The integral
\[
Q_g=\int_0^\infty
e^{-uA_g}(\Sigma_g-\Sigma_{g0})e^{-uA_{g0}}\,du
\]
solves this Sylvester equation.  Taking operator norms and integrating the exponential bounds gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|Q_g\|_{\mathrm{op}}
&\le
\frac{\|\Sigma_g-\Sigma_{g0}\|_{\mathrm{op}}}
{\sqrt{\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma_g)}+
 \sqrt{\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma_{g0})}}\\
&<\frac{\delta_0}{\sqrt{\lambda_0}}.                 \tag{16}
\end{aligned}
\]
Let \(\varepsilon_g\) now have independent Rademacher coordinates and set
\(Y_g=m_g+A_g\varepsilon_g\).  Then \(\mathbb E Y_g=m_g\) and
\(\operatorname{Var}(Y_g)=\Sigma_g\).  Since
\(\|\varepsilon_g\|_2=\sqrt T\), equations (1), (16), and the defining upper bound on \(\delta_0\) imply, coordinatewise,
\[
\begin{aligned}
Y_{gt}
&=m_{gt}+s^0_{gt}\varepsilon_{gt}+(Q_g\varepsilon_g)_t\\
&\ge m_{gt}-s^0_{gt}-\sqrt T\|Q_g\|_{\mathrm{op}}\\
&>d_0-\frac{\sqrt T\delta_0}{\sqrt{\lambda_0}}
>d_0/2>0.                                               \tag{17}
\end{aligned}
\]
Moreover, (16) gives the uniform coordinatewise upper bound
\[
Y_{gt}
\le m_{gt}+s^0_{gt}+\frac{\sqrt T\delta_0}{\sqrt{\lambda_0}},
\]
so these laws share a finite outcome envelope over the whole covariance ball.  Take independent draws within cohorts and independent samples across cohorts.  Applying the same potential-outcome completion used in (6), and using (15), proves
\[
\mathbb B_{\mathcal A}(\Sigma_0,\delta_0)
\subset\mathcal L(m).                                  \tag{18}
\]

Equation (13) also proves that the linear map \(\mathcal L_m\) has rank \(q\): for each coordinate \(e=(g,t)\in\mathcal E\), a sufficiently small admissible choice \(x=ue_e\), with \(u\ne0\), maps to the nonzero multiple
\(-u(B\mu_{gt})^{-1}e_e\), and these \(q\) images span \(\mathbb R^q\).  In particular \(\|\mathcal L_m\|_{\mathrm{op}}>0\).  Because \(q>0\), choose one such coordinate and then choose \(|u|>0\) still smaller if necessary.  The defining strict inequality for \(\rho\) remains valid, and continuity of the finite-dimensional covariance path at \(u=0\) ensures
\(\|\Sigma(ue_e)-\Sigma_0\|_{\mathcal A}<\delta_0\).  Set \(x_0=ue_e\).  Then
\[
\|\Sigma(x_0)-\Sigma_0\|_{\mathcal A}<\delta_0,
\qquad
\mathcal L_m(\Sigma(x_0))
=-B^{-1}D_\mu^{-1}x_0\ne0.                            \tag{19}
\]
Both arguments in the minimum defining \(\eta_0\) are therefore positive.  If
\(\|\Sigma-\Sigma(x_0)\|_{\mathcal A}<\eta_0\), the triangle inequality and the first radius restriction give
\[
\|\Sigma-\Sigma_0\|_{\mathcal A}<\delta_0,            \tag{20}
\]
so (18) implies \(\Sigma\in\mathcal L(m)\).  Linearity and continuity of \(\mathcal L_m\), followed by the second radius restriction, give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\mathcal L_m(\Sigma)\|_2
&\ge \|\mathcal L_m(\Sigma(x_0))\|_2
-\|\mathcal L_m\|_{\mathrm{op}}
  \|\Sigma-\Sigma(x_0)\|_{\mathcal A}\\
&>\tfrac12\|\mathcal L_m(\Sigma(x_0))\|_2>0.         \tag{21}
\end{aligned}
\]
For every covariance in this ball, positive shares and covariance interior imply \(\Omega\succ0\); full column rank of \(R\) then implies \(K=R^{\prime}\Omega R\succ0\).  Equations (20)--(21) and the cycle-loss definition yield
\[
\mathbb B_{\mathcal A}(\Sigma(x_0),\eta_0)
\subset\mathcal L(m)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\Gamma=b_{\mathrm{cyc}}^{\prime}K^{-1}b_{\mathrm{cyc}}>0
\]
throughout that ambient open ball, completing the proof.

Fix the interior mean \(m\) and the positive share vector \(p\), and write
\(\mathcal E=\{(g,t):g<\infty,\ 2\le t<g\}\) for the non-tree untreated edges.  First observe that the PPML coefficient is feasible for the target derivative.  The weighted-cycle theorem gives \(\dim\ker(J^{\prime})=q\).  Since \(J\) has \(|U|+|D|\) rows and \(|C|+T-1+|D|\) columns, the identity \(q=|U|-|C|-T+1\) implies that \(J\) has full column rank.  Its block structure then implies that \(X\) has full column rank.  Positive weights consequently make
\(S=X^{\prime}P_UMX\) positive definite.  Since \(M\) and \(P_U\) are commuting positive diagonal matrices, the definitions of \(J\), \(h_{\mathrm{CM}}\), and \(c\) give
\[
J^{\prime}h_{\mathrm{CM}}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
X^{\prime}MP_UXS^{-1}c_\beta\\
(p_g/B)_{(g,t)\in D}
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}c_\beta\\c_D\end{pmatrix}
=c.                                                     \tag{1}
\]
The covariance-interior and positive-share assumptions give \(\Omega\succ0\).  Direct substitution in the definition of \(h^\star\) gives
\(J^{\prime}h^\star=c\), and Lagrange minimization of
\(h^{\prime}\Omega h\) subject to \(J^{\prime}h=c\) gives this unique minimizer.

By the weighted-cycle theorem, every feasible difference is in
\(\ker(J^{\prime})=\operatorname{col}(R)\).  Therefore, when \(q>0\), write
\(h^\star-h_{\mathrm{CM}}=R\gamma\).  Moreover
\[
\Omega h^\star
=J(J^{\prime}\Omega^{-1}J)^{-1}c,
\]
so \(R^{\prime}\Omega h^\star=0\) because \(R^{\prime}J=0\).  It follows that
\[
0=R^{\prime}\Omega(h_{\mathrm{CM}}+R\gamma)
=b_{\mathrm{cyc}}+K\gamma.
\]
Since \(R\) has full column rank and \(\Omega\succ0\), \(K\succ0\), and hence
\[
h^\star=h_{\mathrm{CM}}-RK^{-1}b_{\mathrm{cyc}}.       \tag{2}
\]
The orthogonality just established and (2) give the Pythagorean identity
\[
h_{\mathrm{CM}}^{\prime}\Omega h_{\mathrm{CM}}
-h^{\star\prime}\Omega h^\star
=b_{\mathrm{cyc}}^{\prime}K^{-1}b_{\mathrm{cyc}}
=\Gamma.                                               \tag{3}
\]
For \(q=0\), the same identities hold with empty cycle matrices: the weighted-cycle theorem gives \(\ker(J^{\prime})=\{0\}\), so the two feasible coefficients in (1) coincide and \(\Gamma=0\).

The cohort-mean PPML linearization identifies
\(h_{\mathrm{CM}}^{\prime}Z\) as the estimator's influence function and its root-\(N\) asymptotic variance as
\(h_{\mathrm{CM}}^{\prime}\Omega h_{\mathrm{CM}}\).  The canonical-gradient theorem identifies \(h^{\star\prime}Z\) and \(V^\star=h^{\star\prime}\Omega h^\star\) as the canonical gradient and information bound.  Thus (3), or equivalently the primitive-balance characterization, proves at every fixed law that
\[
\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{CM}}\text{ is efficient}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
b_{\mathrm{cyc}}=0.                                   \tag{4}
\]

If \(q=0\), the right side of (4) is vacuous, and hence the estimator is efficient at every bounded law with the fixed \(m\) and \(p\).  Conversely, suppose \(q>0\).  The realizable-quadratic-loss theorem supplies, for this same \(m\), a nonzero admissible \(x\) and a law \(P_\rho\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{bd}}\) for which
\[
\Gamma(x)
=B^{-2}x^{\prime}D_\mu^{-1}K_0^{-1}D_\mu^{-1}x>0.     \tag{5}
\]
At that law, (3) and (5) show that the PPML asymptotic variance strictly exceeds the canonical bound.  It is therefore false that PPML is efficient at every fixed-mean bounded law.  This proves the asserted topological equivalence.

For an arbitrary realizable covariance at the fixed mean, the definition of \(\mathcal L_m\) and (4) give
\[
\{\Sigma\in\mathcal L(m):\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{CM}}
\text{ is efficient at }\Sigma\}
=\mathcal L(m)\cap\ker(\mathcal L_m).                 \tag{6}
\]
The exact diagonal response in the realizable-quadratic-loss theorem maps the \(q\) symmetric covariance directions
\(\operatorname{Cov}(Y_{gt},Y_{gg})\), \((g,t)\in\mathcal E\), to
\(-e_{gt}/(B\mu_{gt})\).  These images are linearly independent because \(B\mu_{gt}>0\).  Since the codomain of \(\mathcal L_m\) is \(\mathbb R^q\),
\[
\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal L_m)=q.                  \tag{7}
\]
Thus its kernel is a proper codimension-\(q\) linear subspace when \(q>0\); the ambient realizability and strict-loss balls from the realizable-quadratic-loss theorem show that both the classification and its strict complement occur for legal bounded outcome laws, rather than only for formal covariance perturbations.

Finally, let \(0<\lambda_{\min}(K)\le\lambda_{\max}(K)\) be the extreme eigenvalues of \(K\).  Applying the Rayleigh quotient to \(K^{-1}\) gives
\[
\frac{\|b_{\mathrm{cyc}}\|_2^2}{\lambda_{\max}(K)}
\le
b_{\mathrm{cyc}}^{\prime}K^{-1}b_{\mathrm{cyc}}
\le
\frac{\|b_{\mathrm{cyc}}\|_2^2}{\lambda_{\min}(K)}. \tag{8}
\]
The middle term is \(\Gamma\) by definition.  Equations (4)--(8) provide necessity, sufficiency, exact codimension, and a sharp spectral characterization for the cohort-mean estimator.  The final comparison in the theorem is therefore a positioning statement: none of these mathematical conclusions is taken as a premise from Ciani and Fisher's observation or simulations.
