\begin{proof}
Write
\[
\widetilde{\overline Y}
=\operatorname{col}_{g\in C}(\overline Y_g),
\qquad
\widetilde m=\operatorname{col}_{g\in C}(m_g),
\]
in cohort-major order, and define
\[
W_{gN}
=\frac1{\sqrt{n_g}}\sum_{i=1}^{n_g}(Y_{gi}-m_g),
\qquad
W_N=\operatorname{col}_{g\in C}(W_{gN}).
\tag{1}
\]
Since \(n_g/N\to p_g>0\), every \(n_g\to\infty\). For an arbitrary
block vector \(a=\operatorname{col}_{g\in C}(a_g)\), the scalar
i.i.d. central limit theorem, finite second moments, and mutual
independence of the cohort samples give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E\exp\{it\,a^\prime W_N\}
&=\prod_{g\in C}
\mathbb E\exp\{it\,a_g^\prime W_{gN}\}\\
&\longrightarrow
\prod_{g\in C}
\exp\!\left\{-\frac{t^2}{2}a_g^\prime\Sigma_ga_g\right\}\\
&=\exp\!\left\{-\frac{t^2}{2}
a^\prime\operatorname{blockdiag}_{g\in C}(\Sigma_g)a\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
Hence, by the Cram\'er--Wold device,
\[
W_N\Rightarrow
\mathcal N\!\left(
0,\operatorname{blockdiag}_{g\in C}(\Sigma_g)
\right).
\tag{3}
\]
This conclusion remains valid when some \(\Sigma_g\) are singular;
the corresponding Gaussian law is then degenerate.

Let
\[
D_N=\operatorname{blockdiag}_{g\in C}
\left(\sqrt{\frac N{n_g}}\,I_T\right),
\qquad
D=\operatorname{blockdiag}_{g\in C}
\left(p_g^{-1/2}I_T\right).
\tag{4}
\]
Share convergence gives \(D_N\to D\), while
\[
\sqrt N(\widetilde{\overline Y}-\widetilde m)=D_NW_N.
\tag{5}
\]
Slutsky's theorem therefore yields
\[
\sqrt N(\widetilde{\overline Y}-\widetilde m)
\Rightarrow
\mathcal N\!\left(
0,\operatorname{blockdiag}_{g\in C}(\Sigma_g/p_g)
\right).
\tag{6}
\]
Finally, the untreated--treated ordering is obtained from the
cohort-major ordering by the fixed stacking matrix \(\Pi\):
\[
\sqrt N(\overline Y-m)
=\Pi\sqrt N(\widetilde{\overline Y}-\widetilde m).
\tag{7}
\]
Applying the continuous mapping theorem to (6) proves
\[
\sqrt N(\overline Y-m)
\Rightarrow
\mathcal N(0,\Omega),
\qquad
\Omega
=\Pi\operatorname{blockdiag}_{g\in C}
(\Sigma_g/p_g)\Pi^\prime.
\]
\end{proof}
