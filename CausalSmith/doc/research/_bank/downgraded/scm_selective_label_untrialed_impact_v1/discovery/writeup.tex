\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} Repeated trials cannot accompany every updated human--AI policy, and in pretrial applications ground truth is revealed only by an endogenous action. The paper identifies the exact observable--target image of legal finite response types and makes the SLCC-specific primitive bracket-stratum construction its structural headline: an arbitrary-finite extension of the exact thirty-two-row quotient, a bracket-supported law with two exact \(199/800\) full-versus-label-blind gaps, and a small full-support mixture yielding a nonempty relative-interior region of two-sided improvement. The optimal-dual-face equivalence is used as a generic LP diagnostic for that construction. Generic simultaneous multinomial confidence-region propagation supplies a finite-sample implementation corollary. At boundary laws, Euclidean projection has the paired LP directional limit, and an explicit centered \(m\)-out-of-\(n\) subsampling schedule gives pointwise fixed-law conservative simultaneous endpoint coverage even at the certified nondegenerate zero atom; no uniform-validity, first-atom-aware, or dominance claim is made.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} The structural comparators do not provide the present SLCC-specific primitive bracket-stratum construction: a legal arbitrary-finite extension of the exact thirty-two-row quotient, an exact bracket-supported two-sided full-versus-label-blind gap, and a full-support perturbation producing a nonempty relative-interior refinement region. The dual-face equivalence itself is only the generic equality criterion for an LP and its coarsened-dual restriction. For inference, existing moment-inequality and identified-set subsampling results use appropriate inverted statistics or impose quantile-continuity or stochastic-ordering conditions, while stochastic-spanning work already treats a possible zero atom under a significance-level versus jump-bound restriction; those results do not directly establish conservative simultaneous coverage for this coupled projection--LP endpoint statistic.

\paragraph{Niche.} A three-performance SLCC bracket makes trialed low and high configurations constrain an untrialed middle configuration in opposite directions according to latent correctness, so selectively revealed truth has a stable two-sided identifying value. The same finite geometry exposes a coupled boundary statistic with projection and two endpoint ties, for which a fixed-law global-sign argument can retain the atom rather than exclude it.

\paragraph{Fill.} The paper proves the exact finite-response converse, constructs the legal arbitrary-finite bracket extension, verifies exact \(199/800\) gaps before a small full-support perturbation, and obtains a nonempty relative-interior two-sided refinement region robust to duplicate endpoint arms, arbitrary positive randomization, extra policies, performance values, and strata. It separately proves pointwise conservative simultaneous endpoint coverage at projection and LP ties using the explicit schedules \(m_n=\max\{1,\lfloor n^{1/3}\rfloor\}\) and \(\eta_n=\min\{\alpha/2,n^{-1/12}\}\), effective-range Gaussian approximation, and a global-sign argument at zero atoms.

\section{Related work}

Zhang, Skalnes, Chen, and Oberst (2026, Assumptions 3.1--3.3, Theorem 4.1, the Tightness of Bounds discussion, Theorem 5.1, and Appendix G) supply the correctness, performance-order, and neutral restrictions, but do not claim their main bounds are tight and do not treat the present endogenous reveal action. Chen and Oberst (2025, Theorems 3.1--3.2) prove attained bounds in the preceding performance-mediated model without the selective-label correctness quotient. Lakkaraju et al. (2017, p. 4 and Proposition 4.1) formalize decision-induced labels through contraction across decision makers rather than a sharp compatible-SCM fiber. Imai et al. (2023) supply the randomized PSA consumer, while Ben-Michael et al. (2025, Theorem 3 and Theorem S3) analyze classification-risk contrasts under a different baseline-potential-outcome design. Duarte et al. (2024, Proposition 1 and Theorem 1) provide generic finite response-function optimization, and Section 6 with Appendix E provides generic propagation of simultaneous observable-law confidence regions. Accordingly, the optimal-dual-face equivalence here is a generic LP restriction diagnostic, not the field-level novelty. The structural contribution is the SLCC-specific primitive bracket-stratum construction: a legal arbitrary-finite extension of the exact thirty-two-row quotient, exact bracket-supported \(199/800\) gaps, a small full-support mixture, and a nonempty relative-interior region of two-sided full-versus-label-blind improvement robust to duplicate arms, positive randomization probabilities, extra policies, performance values, and strata. Kaido, Molinari, and Stoye (2019) provide the calibrated-projection benchmark. Fang and Santos (2019, Theorem 2.1 and Section 4) provide the directional delta method and convex-projection derivative, while the paired LP optimal-face composition is specialized here; Hong and Li (2018) provide a numerical-derivative alternative. Andrews and Guggenberger (2009, introduction at pp. 3--5) establish uniform validity for appropriate inverted moment-inequality test statistics, warn that direct subsampling of estimated-set endpoints is not uniformly valid, and record first-order equivalence of subsampling and the \(m\)-out-of-\(n\) bootstrap when the squared subsample size divided by sample size vanishes. Romano and Shaikh (2010, Theorems 2.2--2.3) obtain pointwise identified-set subsampling coverage under divergence of the subsample size, a vanishing subsample fraction, and continuity at the smallest target quantile, and uniform coverage under their high-level stochastic-ordering condition (21). Arvanitis, Scaillet, and Topaloglou (2020, Lemma 2 and Theorem 2) already allow a stochastic-spanning saddle limit to have a bounded jump at zero and obtain exactness under a significance-level versus jump-bound restriction. The distinct inference result here is narrower: fixed-law conservative simultaneous coverage for the coupled projection--LP endpoint statistic, using explicit schedules and a vanishing-slack/global-sign argument at a certified nondegenerate zero atom. It is not uniform validity, not the first atom-aware subsampling result, and does not dominate those procedures.

  \item \texttt{S\_\allowbreak{}strict} --- \texttt{two-\allowbreak{}sided strict-\allowbreak{}refinement region}; \(2^{\mathfrak O}\); \texttt{observable laws for which revealed labels strictly improve both endpoints}
  \item \texttt{Bracket\_\allowbreak{}SLCC} --- \texttt{primitive finite-\allowbreak{}design predicate}; \texttt{one observed stratum brackets an untrialed middle-\allowbreak{}performance policy by trialed endpoint configurations}
\end{itemize}

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:randomized-policy-assignment]\label{ass:randomized-policy-assignment}
\(D\perp\!\!\!\perp (S,Z,(A_c)_{c\in\mathcal C},(Y_c)_{c\in\mathcal C})\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Randomized multi-arm assignment, \cite{ImaiJiangGreinerHalenShin2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:known-arm-law]\label{ass:known-arm-law}
\(\Pr(D=j)=\rho_j\) for every \(j\in J_0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Known randomized arm probabilities, \cite{ImaiJiangGreinerHalenShin2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:common-action-response]\label{ass:common-action-response}
Under policy \(j\in J\) and stratum \(S=s\), the structural action is \(A=A_{c_j(s)}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Performance-mediated common action response, \cite{ChenOberst2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:common-outcome-response]\label{ass:common-outcome-response}
Under policy \(j\in J\) and stratum \(S=s\), the structural outcome is \(Y=Y_{c_j(s)}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Performance-mediated common outcome response, \cite{ChenOberst2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:reveal-action]\label{ass:reveal-action}
\(R=A\) almost surely.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Action-dependent selective labels, \cite{LakkarajuKleinbergLeskovecLudwigMullainathan2017}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:masked-truth]\label{ass:masked-truth}
\(Z_{\mathrm{obs}}=Z\) when \(R=1\), and \(Z_{\mathrm{obs}}=\star\) when \(R=0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Deterministic selective-label masking, \cite{LakkarajuKleinbergLeskovecLudwigMullainathan2017}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:counterfactual-correctness]\label{ass:counterfactual-correctness}
For every unit and configurations \(c=(r,q)\), \(c'=(r',q')\) in \(\mathcal C\), \(r=Z\) and \(r'\neq Z\) imply \(Y_c\ge Y_{c'}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Unit-level counterfactual correctness, \cite{ZhangSkalnesChenOberst2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:correct-performance-order]\label{ass:correct-performance-order}
For every unit, \(r=Z\) and \(q\le q'\) imply \(Y_{(r,q)}\le Y_{(r,q')}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Correct-prediction performance order, \cite{ZhangSkalnesChenOberst2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:incorrect-performance-order]\label{ass:incorrect-performance-order}
For every unit, \(r\neq Z\) and \(q\le q'\) imply \(Y_{(r,q)}\ge Y_{(r,q')}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Incorrect-prediction performance order, \cite{ZhangSkalnesChenOberst2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:neutral-performance-invariance]\label{ass:neutral-performance-invariance}
For every unit and \(q,q'\in\mathcal Q\), \(Y_{(r_{\mathrm{neu}},q)}=Y_{(r_{\mathrm{neu}},q')}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Performance-invariant neutral recommendation, \cite{ZhangSkalnesChenOberst2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:iid-finite-sampling]\label{ass:iid-finite-sampling}
\(O_1,\ldots,O_n\) are independent and identically distributed with law \(p\in\mathfrak O\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Independent multinomial sampling, \cite{DuarteFinkelsteinKnoxMummoloShpitser2024}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[thm:finite-response-converse]\label{thm:finite-response-converse}
The observable--target image of \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\) is exactly \(\{(Bw,h_e^{\top}w):w\in\Delta_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\}\). In the forward direction, every \(M\in\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\) induces its distribution over legal deterministic response types. In the converse direction, every \(w\in\Delta_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\) canonically defines an attaining SCM \(M_w\in\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\) by drawing \(t\sim w\), independently drawing \(D\sim\rho\), and reading the structural responses from \(t\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(M\in\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\), and define the finite random response type
\[
T=\bigl(S,Z,(A_c)_{c\in\mathcal C},(Y_c)_{c\in\mathcal C}\bigr),
\qquad
w_t=\Pr_M(T=t).
\]
The counterfactual-correctness, correct-performance-order, incorrect-performance-order, and neutral-performance-invariance assumptions are exactly the four restrictions in \(\text{def:legal-response-types}\).  Hence \(T\in\mathcal T_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\) almost surely.  Therefore \(w_t\geq0\) for every legal type and \(\sum_t w_t=1\), so \(w\in\Delta_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\) by \(\text{def:response-simplex}\).

Fix an observable cell \(o=(j,s,a,y,\ell)\in\mathcal O\).  Randomized policy assignment and the known arm law give
\[
\Pr_M(D=j,T=t)=\Pr_M(D=j)\Pr_M(T=t)=\rho_jw_t.
\tag{1}
\]
Conditional on \(D=j\) and \(T=t\), the common-action and common-outcome assumptions give, on \(S=s\),
\[
A=a_{c_j(s)}(t),
\qquad
Y=y_{c_j(s)}(t).
\tag{2}
\]
The reveal-action and masked-truth assumptions further give \(\ell=z_t\) when \(a=1\) and \(\ell=\star\) when \(a=0\).  Partitioning by the finite values of \(T\), and then using (1), (2), and \(\text{def:selective-label-incidence-map}\), yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr_M(O_i=o)
&=\sum_{t\in\mathcal T_{\mathrm{SLCC}}}
 \rho_jw_t\mathbf 1\!\left\{s_t=s,\ a_{c_j(s)}(t)=a,\ y_{c_j(s)}(t)=y,
 \ \ell=z_t\text{ if }a=1,\ \ell=\star\text{ if }a=0\right\}\\
&=\sum_{t\in\mathcal T_{\mathrm{SLCC}}}B_{o,t}w_t.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
Thus the observable law is \(p=Bw\).  Under intervention to policy \(e\), the common-outcome response gives the causal target
\[
\tau_e(M)=\mathbb E_M[Y_{c_e(S)}]
=\sum_{t\in\mathcal T_{\mathrm{SLCC}}}w_t y_{c_e(s_t)}(t)
=h_e^{\top}w.
\tag{4}
\]
This proves the forward inclusion.

Conversely, fix \(w\in\Delta_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\).  On a finite probability space draw \(T=t\) with probability \(w_t\), independently draw \(D=j\) with probability \(\rho_j\), and define \(S,Z,(A_c),(Y_c)\) by the coordinates of \(T\).  Under policy \(j\), set
\[
A=A_{c_j(S)},
\qquad
Y=Y_{c_j(S)},
\qquad
R=A,
\]
and set \(Z_{\mathrm{obs}}=Z\) on \(R=1\) and \(Z_{\mathrm{obs}}=\star\) on \(R=0\).  Independence of the two draws proves randomized assignment, and the marginal law of \(D\) proves the known arm law.  The displayed structural equations prove both common-response assumptions, reveal-action, and masking.  Every type receiving positive mass belongs to \(\mathcal T_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\), so the four unit-level outcome restrictions hold pointwise.  Therefore the constructed model \(M_w\) belongs to \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\).  Repeating (3)--(4) gives observable law \(Bw\) and causal target \(h_e^{\top}w\).  This proves the converse inclusion and hence the claimed equality of observable--target images.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The converse is the substantive bridge from every feasible latent optimizer to a legally realized human--AI SCM. \textit{Gap.} DuarteFinkelsteinKnoxMummoloShpitser2024, Proposition 1 and Theorem 1, give the general discrete response-function construction; this theorem identifies the exact quotient required by action-dependent truth revelation and the ZhangSkalnesChenOberst2026 common \(q\)-indexed order restrictions. \textit{Consumer.} It licenses sharp evaluation of an untrialed PSA or assisted-decision policy without treating an algebraic completion as a causal model.

\begin{proposition}[prop:sharp-selective-label-interval]\label{prop:sharp-selective-label-interval}
As a corollary of \(\text{thm:finite-response-converse}\), for every \(p\in\mathfrak O\) the identified set of \(\tau_e\) is \([L(p),U(p)]\); compactness attains both endpoints and mixtures of endpoint SCMs attain every intermediate value.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(p\in\mathfrak O\) and define its latent fiber
\[
F(p)=\{v\in\Delta_{\mathrm{SLCC}}:Bv=p\}.
\]
It is nonempty because \(p\in B\Delta_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\), convex because its constraints are linear, and compact because it is a closed subset of the finite-dimensional compact simplex.  By \(\text{thm:finite-response-converse}\), the compatible causal values are exactly
\[
\{\tau_e(M):M\in\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{SLCC}},\ P_M(O_i)=p\}
=\{h_e^{\top}v:v\in F(p)\}.
\tag{1}
\]
The continuous linear image on the right of (1) is a nonempty compact convex subset of \(\mathbb R\), hence a compact interval.  Its attained minimum and maximum are \(L(p)\) and \(U(p)\) by \(\text{def:sharp-endpoint-programs}\).

To verify that no value between the endpoints is omitted, choose minimizer \(v_L\) and maximizer \(v_U\).  For \(0\leq\gamma\leq1\),
\[
v_\gamma=(1-\gamma)v_L+\gamma v_U\in F(p),
\qquad
h_e^{\top}v_\gamma=(1-\gamma)L(p)+\gamma U(p).
\]
The converse construction in \(\text{thm:finite-response-converse}\) produces a legal SCM for every \(v_\gamma\).  Thus the causal identified set is exactly \([L(p),U(p)]\), both endpoints are attained, and every intermediate value is attained.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This corollary records the causal interpretation of the two programs without treating LP compactness as the novelty kernel. \textit{Gap.} The generic optimization implication is informed by DuarteFinkelsteinKnoxMummoloShpitser2024; the application-specific advance is carried by the complete strict-refinement certificate. \textit{Consumer.} It converts each reported LP interval into the exact set of deployment means compatible with the model.

\begin{lemma}[lem:z-blind-coarsening]\label{lem:z-blind-coarsening}
For every \(p\in\mathfrak O\), feasibility under \(Bv=p\) implies feasibility under \(K_{\mathrm{blind}}Bv=K_{\mathrm{blind}}p\), hence \([L(p),U(p)]\subseteq[L_{\mathrm{blind}}(K_{\mathrm{blind}}p),U_{\mathrm{blind}}(K_{\mathrm{blind}}p)]\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For \(p\in\mathfrak O\), define
\[
F(p)=\{v\in\Delta_{\mathrm{SLCC}}:Bv=p\},
\qquad
F_b(K_{\mathrm{blind}}p)
=\{v\in\Delta_{\mathrm{SLCC}}:K_{\mathrm{blind}}Bv=K_{\mathrm{blind}}p\}.
\]
If \(v\in F(p)\), applying the linear map \(K_{\mathrm{blind}}\) to \(Bv=p\) proves \(v\in F_b(K_{\mathrm{blind}}p)\).  Thus
\[
F(p)\subseteq F_b(K_{\mathrm{blind}}p).
\tag{1}
\]
Minimization of \(h_e^{\top}v\) over the larger set in (1) cannot increase its value, and maximization over that set cannot decrease its value.  By \(\text{def:z-blind-projection}\),
\[
L_{\mathrm{blind}}(K_{\mathrm{blind}}p)\leq L(p)
\leq U(p)\leq U_{\mathrm{blind}}(K_{\mathrm{blind}}p),
\]
which is equivalent to the asserted interval inclusion.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The one-line coarsening fact fixes the direction of comparison used by the strict certificate. \textit{Gap.} This is a data-processing lemma, not a claim to extend LakkarajuKleinbergLeskovecLudwigMullainathan2017. \textit{Consumer.} It makes any strict endpoint gap interpretable as information supplied by selectively revealed labels.

\begin{proof}
Fix \(p\in\mathfrak O\) and \(n\geq1\).  For each cell \(o\in\mathcal O\), the indicator variables \(\mathbf 1\{O_i=o\}\) are independent Bernoulli variables with success probability \(p_o\) under \(\text{ass:iid-finite-sampling}\).  Hence
\[
N_o=\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf 1\{O_i=o\}\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,p_o).
\tag{1}
\]
By the defining inversion of the two-sided Clopper--Pearson interval at error \(\alpha/|\mathcal O|\), each marginal interval obeys
\[
\Pr_p\{p_o\notin[\ell_o(N_o),u_o(N_o)]\}
\leq\frac{\alpha}{|\mathcal O|}.
\tag{2}
\]
Indeed, its lower endpoint is obtained by inverting the upper-tail binomial test and its upper endpoint by inverting the lower-tail test, each at half the displayed marginal error; the two rejection probabilities sum to at most \(\alpha/|\mathcal O|\).  No independence among the counts is used.  The union bound applied to (2) gives
\[
\Pr_p\{p\in\mathcal C_{n,\alpha}\}
\geq1-\sum_{o\in\mathcal O}\frac{\alpha}{|\mathcal O|}
=1-\alpha.
\tag{3}
\]

On the event \(p\in\mathcal C_{n,\alpha}\), we also have \(p\in\mathfrak O\), so \(\mathcal C_{n,\alpha}\cap\mathfrak O\neq\varnothing\).  Every \(v\in\Delta_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\) satisfying \(Bv=p\) is then feasible for the confidence optimization.  By \(\text{prop:sharp-selective-label-interval}\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
[L(p),U(p)]
&=\{h_e^{\top}v:v\in\Delta_{\mathrm{SLCC}},\ Bv=p\}\\
&\subseteq
\{h_e^{\top}v:v\in\Delta_{\mathrm{SLCC}},\ Bv\in\mathcal C_{n,\alpha}\}
=\mathcal I_{n,\alpha}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
If the intersection is empty, \(\text{def:whole-set-confidence-construction}\) returns \([0,1]\), which contains the identified set because \(h_e\) is binary and the latent weights form a probability vector.  Combining (3)--(4) yields
\[
\Pr_p\{[L(p),U(p)]\subseteq\mathcal I_{n,\alpha}\}\geq1-\alpha.
\]
Finally, whenever \(\mathcal C_{n,\alpha}\cap\mathfrak O\neq\varnothing\), the feasible weight set is nonempty because \(\mathfrak O=B\Delta_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\).  It is a closed subset of the compact simplex, since \(B\) is linear and \(\mathcal C_{n,\alpha}\) is closed.  Its linear image under \(v\mapsto h_e^{\top}v\) is therefore a compact interval, so both implementation optima are attained.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This implementation corollary supplies an exact conservative reporting procedure while acknowledging the generic propagation theorem as prior art. \textit{Gap.} DuarteFinkelsteinKnoxMummoloShpitser2024, Section 6 and Appendix E, already establish generic confidence-region propagation; the contribution here is its explicit implementation for the SLCC incidence map, not a new inference principle. \textit{Consumer.} The aihuman analysis can report a finite-sample envelope for the entire sharp PSA deployment interval.

\begin{theorem}[thm:projection-composed-directional-limit]\label{thm:projection-composed-directional-limit}
For every fixed \(p\in\mathfrak O\), the map \(x\mapsto\theta(\Pi_{\mathfrak O}x)\) is Hadamard directionally differentiable at \(p\) with derivative \(\Gamma'_p\), and \(\sqrt n\{\widehat\theta-\theta(p)\}\rightsquigarrow\Gamma'_p(G_p)\). This formula remains valid when \(p\) is on a projection face and either endpoint has multiple optimal bases.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(d=|\mathcal O|\) and \(\Phi=\theta\circ\Pi_{\mathfrak O}\).  We first differentiate the Euclidean projection without an interior-point assumption.  Let \(t_k\downarrow0\), let \(g_k\to g\) in \(\mathbb R^d\), and set
\[
x_k=p+t_kg_k,
\qquad
q_k=\Pi_{\mathfrak O}(x_k),
\qquad
d_k=\frac{q_k-p}{t_k}.
\tag{1}
\]
Because \(p\in\mathfrak O\), the minimizing property of \(q_k\) gives
\[
\|q_k-x_k\|_2\leq\|p-x_k\|_2=t_k\|g_k\|_2,
\]
and hence \(\|d_k\|_2\leq2\|g_k\|_2\).  Thus \((d_k)\) is bounded.  Projection onto the nonempty closed convex polytope \(\mathfrak O\) is characterized by the first-order inequality
\[
\langle x_k-q_k,v-q_k\rangle\leq0
\qquad(v\in\mathfrak O).
\tag{2}
\]
Every subsequential limit \(d_*\) of \((d_k)\) belongs to \(T_{\mathfrak O}(p)\) by the definition of that closed tangent cone.  If \(z\) is radial, meaning \(p+\varepsilon z\in\mathfrak O\) for some \(\varepsilon>0\), convexity implies \(p+t_kz\in\mathfrak O\) for all sufficiently large \(k\).  Substitute this point for \(v\) in (2), divide by \(t_k^2\), and pass along a subsequence for which \(d_k\to d_*\).  This gives
\[
\langle g-d_*,z-d_*\rangle\leq0.
\tag{3}
\]
Radial directions are dense in \(T_{\mathfrak O}(p)\) by its defining closure, so (3) holds for every \(z\) in that cone.  Expanding squared distances shows that (3) is equivalent to
\[
\|g-d_*\|_2\leq\|g-z\|_2
\qquad(z\in T_{\mathfrak O}(p)).
\]
Strict convexity gives the unique minimizer, so
\[
d_*=\Pi_{T_{\mathfrak O}(p)}g.
\tag{4}
\]
Every convergent subsequence has the same limit and the original sequence is bounded; hence the whole sequence converges to (4).  This proves Hadamard directional differentiability of \(\Pi_{\mathfrak O}\) at \(p\), with derivative \(g\mapsto\Pi_{T_{\mathfrak O}(p)}g\).

We next derive the endpoint derivatives from finiteness.  The observable--target set
\[
\mathcal C=\operatorname{conv}\{(B_{\cdot,t},h_e(t)):t\in\mathcal T_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\}
\tag{5}
\]
is a finite polytope.  Eliminating the finitely many simplex coordinates from its defining finite system of linear equalities and inequalities gives a finite half-space representation of \(\mathcal C\).  Solving those inequalities for the last coordinate shows that there are finite index sets \(R_L,R_U\) and dual-feasible affine functions
\[
\ell_r(x)=\alpha_r+\lambda_r^{\top}x\quad(r\in R_L),
\qquad
u_s(x)=\bar\alpha_s+\bar\lambda_s^{\top}x\quad(s\in R_U)
\]
such that, throughout \(\mathfrak O\),
\[
L(x)=\max_{r\in R_L}\ell_r(x),
\qquad
U(x)=\min_{s\in R_U}u_s(x).
\tag{6}
\]
For completeness, dual feasibility follows by evaluating each supporting inequality on every generator in (5): it gives \(\alpha_r\mathbf 1+B^{\top}\lambda_r\leq h_e\) for lower supports and \(\bar\alpha_s\mathbf 1+B^{\top}\bar\lambda_s\geq h_e\) for upper supports.  Conversely, multiplying either columnwise inequality by feasible simplex weights proves weak duality, so the active supports in (6) attain the corresponding dual value.

Let
\[
R_L(p)=\{r:\ell_r(p)=L(p)\},
\qquad
R_U(p)=\{s:u_s(p)=U(p)\}.
\]
Because the families are finite, every inactive affine function has a strictly positive gap at \(p\).  Hence, if \(z_k\to z\) and \(p+t_kz_k\in\mathfrak O\), inactive indices cannot optimize for all sufficiently large \(k\), and
\[
\frac{L(p+t_kz_k)-L(p)}{t_k}
\longrightarrow\max_{r\in R_L(p)}\lambda_r^{\top}z,
\tag{7}
\]
\[
\frac{U(p+t_kz_k)-U(p)}{t_k}
\longrightarrow\min_{s\in R_U(p)}\bar\lambda_s^{\top}z.
\tag{8}
\]
These finite active-index formulas agree with optimization over the entire unnormalized optimal faces.  Indeed, if \((\alpha,\lambda)\in\Lambda_L(p)\), dual feasibility gives
\[
\alpha+\lambda^{\top}(p+t_kz_k)\leq L(p+t_kz_k),
\qquad
\alpha+\lambda^{\top}p=L(p).
\]
Subtract, divide by \(t_k\), and use (7) to obtain
\[
\lambda^{\top}z\leq\max_{r\in R_L(p)}\lambda_r^{\top}z.
\]
Every active pair in (7) belongs to \(\Lambda_L(p)\), so equality holds after maximizing over the full face.  The analogous upper-dual argument gives
\[
L'_p(z)=\max_{(\alpha,\lambda)\in\Lambda_L(p)}\lambda^{\top}z,
\qquad
U'_p(z)=\min_{(\alpha,\lambda)\in\Lambda_U(p)}\lambda^{\top}z.
\tag{9}
\]
These expressions are gauge invariant on the tangent cone: every \(z\in T_{\mathfrak O}(p)\) satisfies \(\mathbf 1^{\top}z=0\), and replacing \((\alpha,\lambda)\) by \((\alpha-c,\lambda+c\mathbf 1)\) leaves \(\lambda^{\top}z\) unchanged.

For the sequence in (1), equation (4) gives \(d_k\to z=\Pi_{T_{\mathfrak O}(p)}g\).  Apply (7)--(9) to \(q_k=p+t_kd_k\).  We obtain
\[
\frac{\Phi(p+t_kg_k)-\Phi(p)}{t_k}
\longrightarrow
\left(
\max_{(\alpha,\lambda)\in\Lambda_L(p)}\lambda^{\top}z,
\min_{(\alpha,\lambda)\in\Lambda_U(p)}\lambda^{\top}z
\right)
=\Gamma'_p(g).
\tag{10}
\]
Thus \(\Phi\) is Hadamard directionally differentiable at \(p\).  The derivative is continuous because projection onto a closed convex cone is nonexpansive and the two endpoint coordinates in (9) are finite piecewise-linear functions on the tangent cone.  Multiple active projection faces and multiple optimal LP bases remain inside the projection, maximum, and minimum in (10).

It remains to derive the stochastic limit.  Under \(\text{ass:iid-finite-sampling}\), write \(X_i=\mathbf e_{O_i}-p\).  These vectors are independent, centered, bounded, and have covariance \(\Sigma_p\).  For every \(a\in\mathbb R^d\) and \(u\in\mathbb R\), Taylor expansion with a remainder bounded uniformly by a constant times \(n^{-3/2}|a^{\top}X_i|^3\) gives
\[
\mathbb E\exp\!\left(\frac{iu a^{\top}X_i}{\sqrt n}\right)
=1-\frac{u^2a^{\top}\Sigma_pa}{2n}+O(n^{-3/2}).
\]
Raising this characteristic function to the \(n\)-th power yields
\[
\mathbb E\exp\!\left(iu a^{\top}\sqrt n(\widehat p-p)\right)
\longrightarrow\exp\!\left(-\frac{u^2a^{\top}\Sigma_pa}{2}\right).
\tag{11}
\]
Thus every linear contrast converges to the corresponding contrast of \(G_p\), including zero-variance contrasts, and the joint characteristic functions converge to that of \(G_p\).  Hence
\[
X_n:=\sqrt n(\widehat p-p)\rightsquigarrow G_p.
\tag{12}
\]

Define \(f_n(g)=\sqrt n\{\Phi(p+g/\sqrt n)-\Phi(p)\}\).  Equation (10) says that whenever \(g_n\to g\), one has \(f_n(g_n)\to\Gamma'_p(g)\).  This sequential property is uniform on every compact set: otherwise a violating sequence in a compact set would have a convergent subsequence contradicting (10) and continuity of \(\Gamma'_p\).  Since (12) makes \((X_n)\) tight, for every bounded continuous test function \(\psi\), uniform convergence on a compact set carrying arbitrarily high probability gives
\[
\mathbb E\psi(f_n(X_n))-\mathbb E\psi(\Gamma'_p(X_n))\longrightarrow0.
\]
Continuity of \(\Gamma'_p\) and (12) give \(\Gamma'_p(X_n)\rightsquigarrow\Gamma'_p(G_p)\).  Since \(p\in\mathfrak O\), \(\Phi(p)=\theta(p)\), and \(\text{def:projection-endpoint-estimator}\) gives \(\Phi(\widehat p)=\widehat\theta\).  Therefore
\[
\sqrt n\{\widehat\theta-\theta(p)\}
\rightsquigarrow\Gamma'_p(G_p),
\]
with no projection-face or optimal-basis uniqueness assumption.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The limit law exposes both sources of nonregularity instead of imposing margin conditions that remove simultaneous ties. \textit{Gap.} FangSantos2019, Theorem 2.1 and Section 4, supplies the directional delta method and projection derivative; ZhangSkalnesChenOberst2026, Theorem 5.1, assumes margin separation. The new formula composes the observable-polytope projection with both LP optimal-face maps for this causal identified set. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies pointwise standard-error simulation and diagnoses exactly when ordinary Gaussian endpoint intervals fail in finite human--AI trials.

\begin{proposition}[prop:trialed-policy-reduction]\label{prop:trialed-policy-reduction}
If there is \(j^{\star}\in J_0\) with \(c_e(s)=c_{j^{\star}}(s)\) for every \(s\in\mathcal S\), then \(L(p)=U(p)=\mathbb E[Y\mid D=j^{\star}]\). Separately, if \(A_c=1\) for every \(c\in\mathcal C\), then \(Z\) is fully observed, but full revelation alone does not point-identify every distinct untrialed configuration: there exist two legal SCMs with the same fully labeled trial law and different untrialed means. Identification may nevertheless occur at special observed laws, through configuration overlap, through the neutral-invariance restriction, or under further identifying restrictions.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Suppose first that \(c_e(s)=c_{j^\star}(s)\) for every \(s\in\mathcal S\), where \(j^\star\in J_0\).  Then for every legal response type,
\[
h_e(t)=y_{c_e(s_t)}(t)=y_{c_{j^\star}(s_t)}(t).
\tag{1}
\]
The known arm law has \(\rho_{j^\star}>0\) because \(\rho\) lies in the relative interior of the trial-arm simplex.  For every compatible latent mixture \(v\), randomized assignment and the common-outcome response imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_v[Y\mid D=j^\star]
&=\rho_{j^\star}^{-1}
\sum_t v_t\rho_{j^\star}y_{c_{j^\star}(s_t)}(t)\\
&=h_e^{\top}v,
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
where (1) was used in the last equality.  The conditional mean on the left of (2) is determined by the observed law \(p\).  Consequently every \(v\) in the fiber \(\{v:Bv=p\}\) has the same target, and \(\text{prop:sharp-selective-label-interval}\) gives
\[
L(p)=U(p)=\mathbb E[Y\mid D=j^\star].
\]

For the separate revelation claim, if \(A_c=1\) for every \(c\in\mathcal C\), then the reveal-action assumption gives \(R=1\), and masked truth gives \(Z_{\mathrm{obs}}=Z\) almost surely.  Full revelation does not by itself identify a distinct untrialed configuration.  Consider one stratum, one trialed configuration \((1,q_\ell)\), one untrialed configuration \((1,q_h)\), and \(q_\ell<q_h\).  In two deterministic SCMs set \(Z=1\), set every action response to one, and set every neutral outcome to zero.  For the \(r=1\) outcome path, set
\[
(Y_{(1,q_\ell)},Y_{(1,q_h)})=(0,0)
\quad\text{in the first model},
\qquad
(Y_{(1,q_\ell)},Y_{(1,q_h)})=(0,1)
\quad\text{in the second}.
\tag{3}
\]
Both paths are nondecreasing, as correct-performance order requires for \(Z=1\).  The incorrect neutral path is constant, so neutral invariance and incorrect-performance order hold, and the correct outcomes dominate the neutral outcomes, so counterfactual correctness holds.  Both models can use the same randomized arm law and therefore belong to \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\).  Their trialed laws, including the always-revealed label \(Z=1\), coincide because both have trialed outcome zero; their untrialed means are respectively zero and one by (3).  This proves nonidentification from full revelation alone, while the first part proves identification under configuration overlap.  Special observed fibers, neutral-invariance coincidences, or additional restrictions may still collapse the sharp interval, exactly as the statement allows.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The sanity check recovers ordinary randomized identification exactly under configuration equality and keeps full revelation logically separate from policy overlap. \textit{Gap.} ImaiJiangGreinerHalenShin2023 identifies observed randomized recommendation arms; the reduction embeds precisely that case and makes no universal identification claim for a distinct untrialed configuration. \textit{Consumer.} Implementations can test the standard RCT case without falsely declaring identification merely because all labels are revealed.

\begin{lemma}[lem:finite-incidence-duality-continuity]\label{lem:finite-incidence-duality-continuity}
Let \(B\) have finitely many columns \(b_t\), let \(h_e(t)\in\{0,1\}\), and let \(\mathfrak O=B\Delta_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\).  The lower and upper programs attain their optima.  Moreover,
\[
L(p)=\max_{(\alpha,\lambda)\in\mathcal D_L}\{\alpha+\lambda^{\top}p\},
\qquad
U(p)=\min_{(\alpha,\lambda)\in\mathcal D_U}\{\alpha+\lambda^{\top}p\},
\]
and both optimal faces are nonempty for every \(p\in\mathfrak O\).  The four value functions obtained from \(B\) and \(K_{\mathrm{blind}}B\) are finite piecewise-affine functions, continuous on their entire relative domains.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write \(b_t=B_{\cdot,t}\), \(h_t=h_e(t)\), and form the finite observable--target hull
\[
\mathcal C=\{(Bv,h_e^{\top}v):v\in\Delta_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\}
=\operatorname{conv}\{(b_t,h_t):t\in\mathcal T_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\}.
\tag{1}
\]
The simplex is compact and the map in (1) is linear.  Therefore \(\mathcal C\), every fiber \(\{v\in\Delta_{\mathrm{SLCC}}:Bv=p\}\), and its image under \(v\mapsto h_e^{\top}v\) are compact.  For \(p\in\mathfrak O\), the last image is also nonempty and convex, hence a compact interval.  Its endpoints are attained and equal \(L(p)\) and \(U(p)\) by \(\text{def:sharp-endpoint-programs}\).

We derive duality without silently normalizing the mass multiplier.  The membership condition \((x,z)\in\mathcal C\) is the finite linear system
\[
v_t\geq0\quad(t\in\mathcal T_{\mathrm{SLCC}}),
\qquad
\mathbf 1^{\top}v=1,
\qquad
Bv=x,
\qquad
h_e^{\top}v=z.
\tag{2}
\]
Replace equalities by pairs of inequalities and eliminate the coordinates of \(v\) successively.  At each elimination step, inequalities with positive coefficient give upper bounds, those with negative coefficient give lower bounds, and those with zero coefficient remain; existence of the eliminated coordinate is equivalent to every retained lower bound being at most every retained upper bound.  Thus each step preserves equivalence and finiteness.  After finitely many steps, there exist finite \(R\), vectors \(a_r\), and scalars \(d_r,c_r\) such that
\[
\mathcal C=\{(x,z):a_r^{\top}x+d_rz\leq c_r\text{ for every }r\in R\}.
\tag{3}
\]
The valid inequalities \(-z\leq0\) and \(z\leq1\), which follow from \(h_t\in\{0,1\}\), may be added to (3).  Hence \(R_-:=\{r:d_r<0\}\) and \(R_+:=\{r:d_r>0\}\) are nonempty.  For fixed \(p\in\mathfrak O\), the constraints with \(d_r=0\) are already satisfied, and solving (3) for \(z\) gives
\[
L(p)=\max_{r\in R_-}\frac{c_r-a_r^{\top}p}{d_r},
\qquad
U(p)=\min_{r\in R_+}\frac{c_r-a_r^{\top}p}{d_r}.
\tag{4}
\]

For \(r\in R_-\), write its affine function in (4) as \(\alpha_r+\lambda_r^{\top}p\).  Substituting every generator \((b_t,h_t)\) into the defining inequality in (3), and dividing by \(d_r<0\), gives
\[
\alpha_r+\lambda_r^{\top}b_t\leq h_t
\qquad\text{for every }t.
\tag{5}
\]
Thus \((\alpha_r,\lambda_r)\in\mathcal D_L\).  Conversely, if \((\alpha,\lambda)\in\mathcal D_L\) and \(Bv=p\), then
\[
\alpha+\lambda^{\top}p
=\sum_t v_t(\alpha+\lambda^{\top}b_t)
\leq\sum_tv_th_t=h_e^{\top}v.
\tag{6}
\]
Taking the minimum in (6) and then choosing an index attaining the finite maximum in (4) proves
\[
L(p)=\max_{(\alpha,\lambda)\in\mathcal D_L}
\{\alpha+\lambda^{\top}p\},
\tag{7}
\]
and exhibits at least one optimizer.  Repeating (5)--(7) for \(r\in R_+\), with inequalities reversed after division by \(d_r>0\), gives
\[
U(p)=\min_{(\alpha,\lambda)\in\mathcal D_U}
\{\alpha+\lambda^{\top}p\},
\tag{8}
\]
again with attainment.

Equation (4) is a maximum or minimum of finitely many affine functions.  Therefore \(L\) and \(U\) are finite piecewise-affine and continuous on all of their relative domain \(\mathfrak O\), including its boundary.  Applying exactly the same argument to the finite matrix \(K_{\mathrm{blind}}B\) proves attainment, finite-polyhedral duality, and continuity for \(L_{\mathrm{blind}}\) and \(U_{\mathrm{blind}}\) on \(K_{\mathrm{blind}}\mathfrak O\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:general-dual-face-strict-refinement-characterization]\label{thm:general-dual-face-strict-refinement-characterization}
For every finite selective-label incidence model and every \(p\in\mathfrak O\),
\[
L(p)>L_{\mathrm{blind}}(K_{\mathrm{blind}}p)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\Lambda_L(p)\cap\mathcal E_K=\varnothing,
\]
and
\[
U(p)<U_{\mathrm{blind}}(K_{\mathrm{blind}}p)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\Lambda_U(p)\cap\mathcal E_K=\varnothing.
\]
Equivalently, the lower inequality is strict exactly when the linear system
\[
\alpha\mathbf 1+B^{\top}\lambda\leq h_e,\qquad
\lambda=K_{\mathrm{blind}}^{\top}\mu,\qquad
\alpha+\lambda^{\top}p=L(p)
\]
is infeasible, and the upper inequality is strict exactly when the system obtained by replacing \(\leq,L\) with \(\geq,U\) is infeasible.  Consequently \(\mathcal S_{\mathrm{strict}}\) is relatively open in \(\mathfrak O\).  If \(\mathsf{Bracket}_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\) holds, then \(\mathcal S_{\mathrm{strict}}\cap\operatorname{relint}(\mathfrak O)\) is nonempty and contains a nonempty relatively open subset of \(\mathfrak O\).  A law supported on the bracket stratum has both full-versus-blind gaps equal to \(199/800\); mixing it by a sufficiently small positive amount with any law assigning positive mass to every legal response type gives the asserted relative-interior region.  Duplicate low- or high-performance arms, arbitrary positive randomization probabilities, additional performance values, other strata, and configurations away from the bracket stratum do not invalidate this construction.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(p\in\mathfrak O\).  By \(\text{lem:finite-incidence-duality-continuity}\), the full lower program has the attained dual representation
\[
 L(p)=\max_{(\alpha,\lambda)\in\mathcal D_L}
       \{\alpha+\lambda^{\top}p\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Apply the same lemma to the finite incidence matrix \(B_{\mathrm{blind}}=K_{\mathrm{blind}}B\), as defined in \(\text{def:z-blind-projection}\).  Its attained lower dual is
\[
 L_{\mathrm{blind}}(K_{\mathrm{blind}}p)
 =\max_{\alpha,\mu}
 \left\{\alpha+\mu^{\top}K_{\mathrm{blind}}p:
 \alpha\mathbf 1+B^{\top}K_{\mathrm{blind}}^{\top}\mu\leq h_e\right\}.
\tag{2}
\]
Under the substitution \(\lambda=K_{\mathrm{blind}}^{\top}\mu\), the feasible objective pairs in (2) are exactly the elements of \(\mathcal D_L\cap\mathcal E_K\), by \(\text{def:dual-refinement-faces}\), and
\[
 \alpha+\mu^{\top}K_{\mathrm{blind}}p
 =\alpha+\lambda^{\top}p.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus (2) is the restriction of (1) to \(\mathcal D_L\cap\mathcal E_K\).  Both optima are attained by \(\text{lem:finite-incidence-duality-continuity}\).  Therefore their values are equal if and only if a maximizer of (1) lies in the restricted set:
\[
 L_{\mathrm{blind}}(K_{\mathrm{blind}}p)=L(p)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \Lambda_L(p)\cap\mathcal E_K\neq\varnothing.
\tag{4}
\]
Indeed, a point in the intersection attains both values, while equality of the values makes any attained optimizer of (2) an element of \(\Lambda_L(p)\cap\mathcal E_K\).  Since \(\text{lem:z-blind-coarsening}\) gives
\(L_{\mathrm{blind}}(K_{\mathrm{blind}}p)\leq L(p)\), (4) implies
\[
 L(p)>L_{\mathrm{blind}}(K_{\mathrm{blind}}p)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \Lambda_L(p)\cap\mathcal E_K=\varnothing.
\tag{5}
\]

The upper argument is identical with the order reversed.  Specifically, \(\text{lem:finite-incidence-duality-continuity}\) and \(\text{def:z-blind-projection}\) give
\[
 U(p)=\min_{(\alpha,\lambda)\in\mathcal D_U}
       \{\alpha+\lambda^{\top}p\},
\tag{6}
\]
and
\[
 U_{\mathrm{blind}}(K_{\mathrm{blind}}p)
 =\min_{\alpha,\mu}
 \left\{\alpha+\mu^{\top}K_{\mathrm{blind}}p:
 \alpha\mathbf 1+B^{\top}K_{\mathrm{blind}}^{\top}\mu\geq h_e\right\}.
\tag{7}
\]
Equation (7) is the restriction of (6) to \(\mathcal D_U\cap\mathcal E_K\).  Attainment and the inequality
\(U(p)\leq U_{\mathrm{blind}}(K_{\mathrm{blind}}p)\) from \(\text{lem:z-blind-coarsening}\) yield
\[
 U(p)<U_{\mathrm{blind}}(K_{\mathrm{blind}}p)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \Lambda_U(p)\cap\mathcal E_K=\varnothing.
\tag{8}
\]
Unfolding membership in \(\mathcal D_L\), \(\mathcal D_U\), and \(\mathcal E_K\) in (5) and (8) gives exactly the two advertised finite linear feasibility systems.  Hence the dual-face equivalences are also computable diagnostics once the two full endpoint values have been computed.

Define on \(\mathfrak O\)
\[
 g_L(p)=L(p)-L_{\mathrm{blind}}(K_{\mathrm{blind}}p),
 \qquad
 g_U(p)=U_{\mathrm{blind}}(K_{\mathrm{blind}}p)-U(p).
\tag{9}
\]
The four endpoint functions are continuous on their relative domains by \(\text{lem:finite-incidence-duality-continuity}\), and \(K_{\mathrm{blind}}\) is linear.  Thus both functions in (9) are continuous on \(\mathfrak O\).  By \(\text{def:strict-refinement-region}\),
\[
 \mathcal S_{\mathrm{strict}}
 =g_L^{-1}((0,\infty))\cap g_U^{-1}((0,\infty)),
\tag{10}
\]
so \(\mathcal S_{\mathrm{strict}}\) is relatively open in \(\mathfrak O\).

It remains to establish the selective-label-specific nonemptiness assertion.  Assume \(\mathsf{Bracket}_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\), and choose \(s_*\in\mathcal S\) and \(q_\ell<q_m<q_h\) supplied by \(\text{def:bracket-stratum-design}\).  For each row \(t_i^{32}\) of \(\text{def:complete-32-type-table}\), construct a response type \(\bar t_i\) on the given finite design as follows.  Set its stratum to \(s_*\), retain the displayed outcome coordinates at \(q_\ell,q_m,q_h\), and, for every additional \(q\in\mathcal Q\), put
\[
 Y_{(1,q)}(\bar t_i)=
 \begin{cases}
 Y_{(1,q_\ell)}(t_i^{32}),&q<q_m,\\
 Y_{(1,q_m)}(t_i^{32}),&q=q_m,\\
 Y_{(1,q_h)}(t_i^{32}),&q>q_m.
 \end{cases}
\tag{11}
\]
For a row with \(z=0\), set \(Y_{(0,q)}(\bar t_i)=1\) for all \(q\); for a row with \(z=1\), set \(Y_{(0,q)}(\bar t_i)=0\) for all \(q\).  Retain the two displayed action bits \(A_{(1,q_\ell)}\) and \(A_{(1,q_h)}\), and set every other action coordinate, including \(A_{(1,q_m)}\), to zero.

These extensions belong to \(\mathcal T_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\).  To verify this directly from \(\text{def:legal-response-types}\), consider first \(z=0\).  The correct-recommendation path \(Y_{(0,q)}\) is constantly one, while (11) extends the displayed nonincreasing incorrect-recommendation path; hence correctness, correct-performance order, incorrect-performance order, and neutral invariance all hold.  For \(z=1\), the incorrect neutral path is constantly zero, while (11) extends the displayed nondecreasing correct-recommendation path; the same four restrictions again hold.  No condition in \(\text{def:legal-response-types}\) restricts action coordinates, so the action assignment is legal.

Let \(w_0\in\Delta_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\) assign to \(\bar t_i\) the weight \(w_i^{32}(\pi^{32,\circ})\) from \(\text{def:32-coordinate-mixture}\), and assign zero to every other legal type.  The defining conditions \(\pi^{32,\circ}\in\mathcal P_{32}\) give \(\varepsilon_{32}>0\), \(a_{32}>0\), and \(b_{32}=\pi_1+a_{32}>0\).  Moreover, because thirty rows receive \(\varepsilon_{32}\) and \(\pi_2=\pi_3\),
\[
 30\varepsilon_{32}+a_{32}+b_{32}
 =\frac{30\pi_4}{24}+\left(\pi_2-\frac{\pi_4}{8}\right)
  +\left(\pi_1+\pi_2-\frac{\pi_4}{8}\right)
 =\pi_1+2\pi_2+\pi_4=1.
\tag{12}
\]
Thus the assigned vector is indeed in \(\Delta_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\).  Put
\[
 p_0=Bw_0.
\tag{13}
\]

We next show that all four endpoint programs at \(p_0\) reduce exactly to the certified thirty-two-row programs.  If a trial arm \(j\) uses \((1,q_d)\) at \(s_*\), where \(d\in\{\ell,h\}\), then \(\text{def:selective-label-incidence-map}\) makes its observable block equal to \(\rho_j\) times the corresponding deterministic cell indicator.  The matching block of \(B^{32}\) equals one half times that indicator by \(\text{def:complete-32-type-table}\).  Consequently, for every selected extension,
\[
 B_{j,\cdot}=2\rho_j B^{32}_{d,\cdot}
 \quad\text{on the arm block }j.
\tag{14}
\]
Every multiplier in (14) is positive by \(\text{def:bracket-stratum-design}\).  Selecting one low arm and one high arm and dividing their blocks by \(2\rho_j\) therefore recovers both canonical endpoint blocks; duplicate endpoint arms add only copies rescaled by known positive constants.

Now let \(v\in\Delta_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\) satisfy \(Bv=p_0\).  The law in (13) assigns zero probability to every cell with stratum different from \(s_*\).  For any \(s\neq s_*\), summing the incidence entries over all arm, action, outcome, and admissible-label cells at stratum \(s\) gives
\[
 \sum_{j\in J_0}\rho_j\sum_{t:S(t)=s}v_t
 =\sum_{t:S(t)=s}v_t,
\tag{15}
\]
where \(\sum_{j\in J_0}\rho_j=1\) follows from the probability-vector definition of \(\rho\).  Nonnegativity of \(v\) and (15) force \(v_t=0\) whenever \(S(t)\neq s_*\).

Restrict a remaining legal type to the three configurations \((1,q_\ell),(1,q_m),(1,q_h)\), its two endpoint action bits, and its endpoint observable cells.  By the quotient-cover conclusion of \(\text{thm:complete-32-type-refinement-certificate}\), there is a displayed row having the same two full observable blocks, the same target value \(Y_{(1,q_m)}\), and the same two label-blind blocks.  Aggregating \(v\) over chosen displayed representatives produces a vector \(\gamma\in\Delta_{31}\).  Equations (14)--(15) then give
\[
 Bv=p_0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 B^{32}\gamma=p^{32,\circ},
 \qquad
 h_e^{\top}v=(h^{32})^{\top}\gamma.
\tag{16}
\]
The reverse implication in (16) is realized by lifting each displayed row to the fixed legal extension \(\bar t_i\).  For a vector feasible only for the blind equation, repeat the aggregation using the label-blind quotient part of the same certificate; this may produce a different vector \(\gamma\in\Delta_{31}\), and it gives
\[
 K_{\mathrm{blind}}Bv=K_{\mathrm{blind}}p_0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 K^{32}B^{32}\gamma=K^{32}p^{32,\circ},
 \qquad
 h_e^{\top}v=(h^{32})^{\top}\gamma,
\tag{17}
\]
again with both directions realized.  Therefore the feasible objective-value sets in the two full programs and the two blind programs are exactly their certified thirty-two-row counterparts.

The exact values in \(\text{thm:complete-32-type-refinement-certificate}\), together with (16)--(17), yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 L(p_0)&=\frac{397}{800},&
 U(p_0)&=\frac{601}{800},\\
 L_{\mathrm{blind}}(K_{\mathrm{blind}}p_0)&=\frac{99}{400},&
 U_{\mathrm{blind}}(K_{\mathrm{blind}}p_0)&=1.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]
Hence
\[
 g_L(p_0)=g_U(p_0)=\frac{199}{800}>0.
\tag{19}
\]

To move the witness into the relative interior, use finiteness of \(\mathcal T_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\) and choose \(u\in\Delta_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\) with \(u_t>0\) for every legal type.  For \(0<\delta<1\), define
\[
 w_\delta=(1-\delta)w_0+\delta u,
 \qquad p_\delta=Bw_\delta.
\tag{20}
\]
Every coordinate of \(w_\delta\) is positive.  For completeness, let \(b_1,\ldots,b_N\) be the distinct columns of \(B\), and aggregate the coordinates of \(w_\delta\) on equal columns to obtain weights \(\beta_k>0\) with \(\sum_k\beta_k=1\) and \(p_\delta=\sum_k\beta_kb_k\).  Put
\[
 H=\{x\in\mathbb R^N:\mathbf 1^{\top}x=0\},
 \qquad
 V=\operatorname{span}\{b_k-b_1:2\leq k\leq N\}.
\tag{21}
\]
The linear map \(T:H\to V\), \(T(x)=\sum_kx_kb_k\), is surjective by (21).  Choose a linear right inverse \(R:V\to H\).  Because the spaces are finite dimensional, there is \(0<C<\infty\) such that \(\|Rd\|_\infty\leq C\|d\|_2\) for all \(d\in V\).  If \(V\neq\{0\}\) and
\[
 \|d\|_2<\frac{\min_k\beta_k}{C},
\tag{22}
\]
then \(\beta+Rd\) is coordinatewise positive, has mass one, and generates \(p_\delta+d\).  If \(V=\{0\}\), the same relative-interior conclusion is immediate.  Since \(p_\delta+V\) is the affine hull direction of \(\mathfrak O\), (22) proves
\[
 p_\delta\in\operatorname{relint}(\mathfrak O).
\tag{23}
\]

By (20), \(p_\delta\to p_0\) as \(\delta\downarrow0\).  Continuity of the gap functions in (9) and the positive values in (19) imply that, for all sufficiently small \(\delta>0\),
\[
 g_L(p_\delta)>0,
 \qquad
 g_U(p_\delta)>0.
\tag{24}
\]
Equations (10), (23), and (24) show that
\(\mathcal S_{\mathrm{strict}}\cap\operatorname{relint}(\mathfrak O)\neq\varnothing\).  The relative-interior property in (23) supplies a relative neighborhood of \(p_\delta\) contained in \(\mathfrak O\), and continuity in (9) supplies a relative neighborhood on which both inequalities in (24) persist.  Their intersection is a nonempty relatively open subset of \(\mathfrak O\) contained in \(\mathcal S_{\mathrm{strict}}\cap\operatorname{relint}(\mathfrak O)\).

The construction used only one low and one high arm to recover the canonical blocks in (14).  Therefore duplicate endpoint arms and arbitrary positive randomization probabilities merely add positively rescaled copies.  Formula (11) handles additional performance values; (15) isolates the bracket stratum at the boundary witness; and the full-support perturbation (20) accommodates all other strata and configurations while retaining strictness by continuity.  This proves every asserted robustness clause and completes the theorem.
\end{proof}

Their role is to certify and compute the value of revealed labels while supporting the theorem's distinct SLCC-specific primitive bracket-stratum construction and arbitrary-finite extension. \textit{Gap.} The contribution is the legal extension of the exact thirty-two-row quotient to arbitrary finite bracket designs, the bracket-supported exact \(199/800\) gaps, and the full-support perturbation producing a nonempty relative-interior two-sided refinement region robust to duplicate arms, positive randomization, extra policies, performance values, and strata; no field-level novelty is attributed to the restriction identity itself. \textit{Consumer.} An analyst can use the two displayed linear feasibility systems as computable diagnostics, while the bracket construction certifies that simultaneous strict refinement is attained on a robust nonempty region in the stated SLCC designs.

\begin{lemma}[lem:raic-multivariate-berry-esseen-convex-sets]\label{lem:raic-multivariate-berry-esseen-convex-sets}
Let \(X_i\), indexed by a finite or countable set, be independent centered random vectors in \(\mathbb R^d\), let \(W=\sum_iX_i\), and suppose \(\sum_i\operatorname{Var}(X_i)=I_d\).  Then, for every measurable convex set \(A\subseteq\mathbb R^d\), \[\left|\Pr\{W\in A\}-\mathcal N(0,I_d)\{A\}\right|\leq(42d^{1/4}+16)\sum_i\mathbb E\|X_i\|_2^3.\] No conclusion for a singular total covariance is asserted.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[thm:atomic-tie-subsampling-validity-global-sign]\label{thm:atomic-tie-subsampling-validity-global-sign}
Fix \(p\in\mathfrak O\), put \(\beta=1-\alpha\), write \((D_L,D_U)=\Gamma'_p\), and define \(H(g)=\max\{D_L(g),-D_U(g)\}\).  Let
\[
V_p=\{g:\mathbf 1^{\top}g=0,\ g_o=0\text{ whenever }p_o=0\}.
\]
Suppose the active projection cones and extreme slopes of \(\Lambda_L(p)\) and \(\Lambda_U(p)\), refined by their zero and comparison hyperplanes, yield a finite polyhedral fan on \(V_p\) such that: (i) locally,
\[
\theta(\Pi_{\mathfrak O}(p+x))-\theta(p)=(D_L(x),D_U(x)),
\]
with both coordinates linear on each fan cone; and (ii) every retained facet normal and endpoint slope is nonzero on \(V_p\).  Let \(\mathcal A_p\) be the finite closure under row selection and sign changes of matrices whose rows are the restrictions to \(V_p\) of those retained normals and slopes.  With \(\mathbf e_o\) denoting the standard basis vector of observable cell \(o\), define, from an independent size-\(m\) categorical sample,
\[
X_m=m^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^m(\mathbf e_{O_i}-p),
\qquad
\delta_m(p)=\max_{A\in\mathcal A_p}\sup_x
\left|\Pr_p\{AX_m\leq x\}-\Pr\{AG_p\leq x\}\right|.
\]
Assume Gaussian nondegeneracy on \(V_p\):
\[
v^{\top}\Sigma_pv>0\quad\text{for every nonzero }v\in V_p.
\]
Use the fixed schedules
\[
m_n=\max\{1,\lfloor n^{1/3}\rfloor\},
\qquad
\eta_n=\min\{\alpha/2,n^{-1/12}\},
\]
and let \(c^{\mathrm{sub}}_{n,1-\alpha+\eta_n}\) be the lower conditional \((1-\alpha+\eta_n)\)-quantile specified in \(\text{def:atomic-tie-subsampling-handle}\).  Then \(C(p)<\infty\), \(\delta_m(p)\leq C(p)m^{-1/2}\), every atom of \(H(G_p)\) is at zero, and
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\Pr_p\left\{
L(p)\geq\widehat L-\frac{c^{\mathrm{sub}}_{n,1-\alpha+\eta_n}}{\sqrt n},
\quad
U(p)\leq\widehat U+\frac{c^{\mathrm{sub}}_{n,1-\alpha+\eta_n}}{\sqrt n}
\right\}\geq1-\alpha.
\]
No projection-cone or LP-basis uniqueness, and no restriction on full-dimensional fan cones where \(H\) vanishes, is required.  The constant and the finite Boolean-event family are fixed-law objects and need not be uniform as \(p\) approaches covariance-rank changes.

The atomic case is nonvacuous in the thirty-two-type table.  At
\[
p_\dagger=\tfrac12(B^{32}_{\cdot,3}+B^{32}_{\cdot,5})
=\tfrac14(\mathbf e_2+\mathbf e_3+2\mathbf e_7),
\]
both endpoint programs have distinct optimal slopes and the observable law lies on a nontrivial projection face.  The exact certificate in \(\text{prop:32-nondegenerate-atomic-tie-certificate}\) gives relatively open regions on which \(H=0\) and \(H>0\), respectively, and hence
\[
0<\Pr\{H(G_{p_\dagger})=0\}<1.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(d=|\mathcal O|\), put \(\Phi=\theta\circ\Pi_{\mathfrak O}\), and define
\[
X_n=\sqrt n(\widehat p-p),
\qquad
T_n=\max\{\sqrt n(\widehat L-L(p)),\sqrt n(U(p)-\widehat U)\}.
\tag{1}
\]
By \(\text{lem:finite-multinomial-clt}\), under \(\text{ass:iid-finite-sampling}\),
\[
X_n\rightsquigarrow G_p,
\qquad
X_n=O_{\Pr}(1).
\tag{2}
\]
Moreover, \(X_n\in V_p\) almost surely: its coordinates sum to zero, and if \(p_o=0\), then the nonnegative count \(N_o\) equals zero almost surely.

By the local finite-fan hypothesis in the theorem, there is \(r>0\) such that
\[
\Phi(p+x)-\Phi(p)=(D_L(x),D_U(x))
\quad\text{for }x\in V_p\text{ with }\|x\|_2<r.
\tag{3}
\]
Both coordinates are continuous, positively homogeneous, and linear on finitely many fan cones. Hence there is \(C_D<\infty\) such that
\[
|D_L(x)|\vee|D_U(x)|\leq C_D\|x\|_2
\qquad(x\in V_p).
\tag{4}
\]
Equations (2)--(4), consistently with \(\text{thm:projection-composed-directional-limit}\), imply
\[
T_n=H(X_n)
\tag{5}
\]
with probability tending to one, and \(H(X_n)\rightsquigarrow H(G_p)\).

Put \(m=m_n\). Conditional on the full sample, let \(\widehat p_b\) be the empirical law of a uniformly selected size-\(m\) subsample without replacement, and define
\[
a_n=\sqrt m(\widehat p-p)=\sqrt{m/n}\,X_n.
\tag{6}
\]
When both empirical laws lie in the neighborhood in (3), the centered subsampling statistic whose conditional lower quantile defines \(c^{\mathrm{sub}}_{n,1-\alpha+\eta_n}\) equals
\[
S_{n,b}=\max\left\{
D_L\bigl(\sqrt m(\widehat p_b-p)\bigr)-D_L(a_n),
D_U(a_n)-D_U\bigl(\sqrt m(\widehat p_b-p)\bigr)
\right\}.
\tag{7}
\]
This follows by applying (3) separately at \(\widehat p_b\) and \(\widehat p\), subtracting the two endpoint coordinates, multiplying by \(\sqrt m\), and using positive homogeneity.

We compare (7) to an independent size-\(m\) experiment. Couple the ordered indices sampled without replacement to \(m\) indices sampled with replacement from \(\{1,\ldots,n\}\). Coupling fails only if two with-replacement indices coincide, so the union bound gives conditional failure probability at most
\[
\frac{m(m-1)}{2n}.
\tag{8}
\]
Conditional on the data, a with-replacement observation has law \(\widehat p\). For one such observation, assign common mass \(\min\{\widehat p_o,p_o\}\) to the matched pair \((o,o)\) and couple the two residual categorical laws arbitrarily; this gives a draw from \(\widehat p\), a draw from \(p\), and mismatch probability
\[
1-\sum_{o\in\mathcal O}\min\{\widehat p_o,p_o\}
=\frac12\|\widehat p-p\|_1.
\]
Taking these couplings independently over the \(m\) positions and applying the union bound gives total mismatch probability at most
\[
\frac m2\|\widehat p-p\|_1.
\tag{9}
\]
On \(\{\|X_n\|_2\leq M\}\), (9) is at most \(m\sqrt d M/(2\sqrt n)\).

On the successful coupling event, \(\sqrt m(\widehat p_b-p)\) equals
\[
X_m=m^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^m(\mathbf e_{O_i}-p)
\]
for an independent size-\(m\) sample, and (7) becomes
\[
K_{a_n}(X_m),
\qquad
K_a(g)=\max\{D_L(g)-D_L(a),D_U(a)-D_U(g)\}.
\tag{10}
\]
The localization failure is exponentially small. Each coordinate of \(\mathbf e_{O_i}-p\) is centered and bounded by one in absolute value. Applying the exponential Markov inequality coordinatewise and taking a finite union gives constants \(C_0,c_0>0\), depending only on the fixed law and the neighborhood, such that
\[
\Pr_p\{\|X_m\|_2>r\sqrt m/2\}\leq C_0e^{-c_0m}.
\tag{11}
\]
For fixed \(M\), equations (6), (8)--(11) bound the conditional coupling and localization error on \(\{\|X_n\|_2\leq M\}\) by
\[
q_{n,M}=\frac{m(m-1)}{2n}
+\frac{m\sqrt d M}{2\sqrt n}+C_0e^{-c_0m}.
\tag{12}
\]

On each pair of refined fan cones containing \(a\) and \(g\), the event \(K_a(g)\leq x\) is an intersection of finitely many linear inequalities. The finite union over cone pairs is a finite Boolean combination of lower orthants whose row matrices belong to \(\mathcal A_p\). Finite inclusion--exclusion and \(\text{lem:finite-alphabet-fan-berry-esseen-explicit-schedule}\) therefore bound the independent-multinomial to Gaussian comparison by a fixed finite multiple of
\[
\delta_m(p)\leq C(p)m^{-1/2}.
\tag{13}
\]
Rank-zero row families contribute no error because their linear images are deterministically zero. This is the effective-range step needed when \(\Sigma_p\) is singular.

Equation (4) also gives, for every \(a,g\in V_p\),
\[
|K_a(g)-H(g)|
\leq |D_L(a)|\vee|D_U(a)|
\leq C_D\|a\|_2.
\tag{14}
\]
On \(\{\|X_n\|_2\leq M\}\), the right side at \(a=a_n\) is at most \(C_DM\sqrt{m/n}\). Every nonzero normal and endpoint slope retained in \(\mathcal A_p\) has positive Gaussian variance by the assumed nondegeneracy on \(V_p\); its Gaussian image therefore has a bounded density. The Gaussian probability of each slab introduced by the displacement in (14) is bounded by a fixed multiple of \(\sqrt{m/n}\), and hence by a fixed multiple of \(m/\sqrt n\).

For the fixed schedules, \(\text{lem:finite-alphabet-fan-berry-esseen-explicit-schedule}\) gives
\[
\delta_m(p)=O(n^{-1/6}),
\qquad
\frac m{\sqrt n}=O(n^{-1/6}),
\qquad
\frac{m^2}{n}=O(n^{-1/3}),
\qquad
e^{-c_0m}=O(e^{-c'n^{1/3}})
\tag{15}
\]
for some \(c'>0\). Since \(\eta_n=n^{-1/12}\) eventually, every term in (15) is \(o(\eta_n)\). Combining (2) and (12)--(15), and then letting \(M\to\infty\), proves at every continuity point \(x\) of the law of \(H(G_p)\) that
\[
\Pr^*\{S_{n,b}\leq x\}\longrightarrow
F(x):=\Pr\{H(G_p)\leq x\}
\quad\text{in probability},
\tag{16}
\]
where \(\Pr^*\) is conditional probability given the full sample.

On the relative interior of each full-dimensional cone of the fan refined by endpoint-comparison and zero hyperplanes, \(H\) is one homogeneous linear functional. Cone boundaries lie in finitely many proper hyperplanes whose normals are nonzero on \(V_p\); Gaussian nondegeneracy makes their probabilities zero. A nonzero linear functional of \(G_p\) has a continuous Gaussian law, while a zero linear functional is identically zero. It follows that
\[
F\text{ can have an atom only at }0.
\tag{17}
\]

Write \(\beta=1-\alpha\), let \(c_n=c^{\mathrm{sub}}_{n,\beta+\eta_n}\), using the lower-quantile convention, and put \(q_\beta=\inf\{x:F(x)\geq\beta\}\). Suppose first that \(q_\beta\neq0\). For every sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\), choose the continuity point \(x_\varepsilon=q_\beta-\varepsilon\); by the definition of the lower quantile, \(F(x_\varepsilon)<\beta\). Equation (16) and \(\eta_n\downarrow0\) imply \(\Pr\{c_n>x_\varepsilon\}\to1\). Combining this with (5), weak convergence, and continuity of \(F\) at the nonzero point \(q_\beta\), then letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), gives
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\Pr_p\{T_n\leq c_n\}
\geq F(q_\beta)\geq\beta.
\tag{18}
\]

It remains to treat \(q_\beta=0\), where
\[
F(0-)\leq\beta\leq F(0).
\tag{19}
\]
If \(H(X_n)=0\), positive homogeneity and (6) imply
\[
D_L(a_n)\leq0,
\qquad
D_U(a_n)\geq0.
\tag{20}
\]
For every \(g\in V_p\), the global sign implication
\[
\begin{aligned}
K_{a_n}(g)<0
&\Longrightarrow D_L(g)<D_L(a_n)\leq0
\ \text{and}\ D_U(g)>D_U(a_n)\geq0\\
&\Longrightarrow H(g)<0
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]
uses neither a selected projection cone nor a unique endpoint slope. The event \(\{H(g)<0\}\) is a finite union of intersections of strict half-spaces from the refined fan. Approximation from below, finite inclusion--exclusion, and the same coupling comparison as in (12)--(13) therefore give, uniformly on \(\{\|X_n\|_2\leq M,H(X_n)=0\}\),
\[
\Pr^*\{S_{n,b}<0\}
\leq F(0-)+O(\delta_m(p))+O_M(m/\sqrt n)
+O(m^2/n)+O(e^{-c_0m}).
\tag{22}
\]
By (15), the error in (22) is \(o(\eta_n)\). If \(F(0-)<\beta\), it is eventually smaller than the fixed gap to \(\beta\); if \(F(0-)=\beta\), it is smaller than the quantile slack \(\eta_n\) with probability tending to one. A lower \((\beta+\eta_n)\)-quantile is nonnegative whenever the conditional mass strictly below zero is less than \(\beta+\eta_n\). Letting \(M\to\infty\) using (2), we obtain
\[
\Pr_p\{H(X_n)=0,\ c_n<0\}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{23}
\]

We also have \(c_n=o_{\Pr}(1)\). For every \(\varepsilon>0\), \(F(-\varepsilon)<\beta\), so (16) gives \(\Pr\{c_n> -\varepsilon\}\to1\). Moreover, \(F(\varepsilon)>\beta\). This is immediate if \(F(0)>\beta\). If \(F(0)=\beta\) and \(H\) takes a positive value, continuity, positive homogeneity, and Gaussian full support on \(V_p\) give positive probability to \(0<H(G_p)\leq\varepsilon\). If \(H\) takes no positive value, then \(F(\varepsilon)=1\). Another use of (16) gives \(\Pr\{c_n\leq\varepsilon\}\to1\). Therefore
\[
c_n=o_{\Pr}(1).
\tag{24}
\]

The zero set of \(H\) is a finite union of interiors of full-dimensional zero cones and subsets of finitely many proper fan hyperplanes. Its boundary is Gaussian-null by nondegeneracy, so it is a continuity set for (2). Consequently
\[
\Pr_p\{H(X_n)=0\}\longrightarrow F(0)-F(0-).
\tag{25}
\]
For each \(\varepsilon>0\), equations (5), (23), and (24) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\Pr_p\{T_n\leq c_n\}
&\geq \lim_{n\to\infty}\Pr_p\{H(X_n)\leq-\varepsilon\}\\
&\quad+\lim_{n\to\infty}\Pr_p\{H(X_n)=0\}\\
&=F(-\varepsilon)+F(0)-F(0-).
\end{aligned}
\]
Letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) makes the right side \(F(0)\geq\beta\). Together with (18),
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\Pr_p\{T_n\leq c_n\}\geq1-\alpha.
\tag{26}
\]
By (1), the event in (26) is exactly
\[
L(p)\geq\widehat L-\frac{c_n}{\sqrt n},
\qquad
U(p)\leq\widehat U+\frac{c_n}{\sqrt n}.
\]
The quantile level is well typed because \(0<\eta_n\leq\alpha/2\), so \(0<1-\alpha+\eta_n<1\).

Finally, \(\text{prop:32-nondegenerate-atomic-tie-certificate}\) supplies the exact early-kill verification at \(p_\dagger\). Its tangent representations, all-column polar inequalities, matching feasible type-weight directions, and all-column dual certificates establish
\[
(D_L,D_U,H)(g_0)=(0,6/5,0),
\qquad
(D_L,D_U,H)(g_1)=(12/5,0,12/5).
\]
It also proves that both endpoint faces contain distinct slopes, that the projection face is nontrivial, and that there are relatively open zero and positive regions. The same certificate gives full Gaussian support on the two-dimensional space \(V_{p_\dagger}\), so both regions have positive Gaussian probability. Hence
\[
0<\Pr\{H(G_{p_\dagger})=0\}<1,
\]
which proves nonvacuity without projection-cone or LP-basis uniqueness.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} For the specified coupled projection--LP endpoint statistic, effective-range Gaussian approximation, finite active-cone geometry, explicit schedules, vanishing quantile slack, and a global-sign implication yield pointwise fixed-law conservative simultaneous coverage even when the joint directional limit has a nondegenerate atom at zero. \textit{Gap.} Moment-inequality and identified-set subsampling results use appropriate inverted statistics or quantile-continuity and stochastic-ordering conditions, and atom-aware stochastic-spanning subsampling already exists under a significance-level versus jump-bound restriction. This theorem's narrower distinction is the fixed-law conservative simultaneous projection--LP endpoint result at a certified zero atom; it does not claim uniformity, priority, or dominance. \textit{Consumer.} At a fixed law satisfying the finite-fan, nonzero-restriction, and Gaussian-nondegeneracy conditions, boundary implementation can use \(m_n=\max\{1,\lfloor n^{1/3}\rfloor\}\) and \(\eta_n=\min\{\alpha/2,n^{-1/12}\}\) for simultaneous endpoint reporting without projection-cone or LP-basis uniqueness.

\begin{lemma}[lem:finite-alphabet-fan-berry-esseen-explicit-schedule]\label{lem:finite-alphabet-fan-berry-esseen-explicit-schedule}
Fix \(p\in\mathfrak O\) and a finite family \(\mathcal A_p\) of real matrices.  Let
\[
X_m=m^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^m(\mathbf e_{O_i}-p),
\qquad G_p\sim\mathcal N(0,\Sigma_p),
\]
where the \(O_i\) in this display are an independent size-\(m\) sample from \(p\).  For \(M\in\mathcal A_p\), set
\[
\Omega_M=M\Sigma_pM^{\top},\qquad r_M=\operatorname{rank}(\Omega_M),
\]
and define
\[
\delta_m(p)=\max_{M\in\mathcal A_p}\sup_{x\in\mathbb R^{\operatorname{rows}(M)}}
\left|\Pr_p\{MX_m\leq x\}-\Pr\{MG_p\leq x\}\right|.
\]
With the maximum over an empty set interpreted as zero, put
\[
C(p)=\max\left\{0,\max_{M\in\mathcal A_p:r_M>0}
\left(42r_M^{1/4}+16\right)
\sum_{o\in\mathcal O}p_o
\left\|\Omega_M^{\dagger/2}M(\mathbf e_o-p)\right\|_2^3\right\}.
\]
Then \(C(p)<\infty\) for fixed \(p\) and
\[
\delta_m(p)\leq C(p)m^{-1/2}.
\]
This assertion restricts and whitens on \(\operatorname{range}(\Omega_M)\); it does not apply an identity-covariance theorem directly to a singular \(\Omega_M\).  The constant is pointwise and need not be uniform as covariance ranks degenerate.  Moreover, for
\[
m_n=\max\{1,\lfloor n^{1/3}\rfloor\},
\qquad
\eta_n=\min\{\alpha/2,n^{-1/12}\},
\]
one has
\[
\delta_{m_n}(p)=O(n^{-1/6}),\qquad
\frac{m_n}{\sqrt n}=O(n^{-1/6}),\qquad
\frac{m_n^2}{n}=O(n^{-1/3}),
\]
and, for every fixed \(c>0\), \(e^{-cm_n}=O(e^{-c'n^{1/3}})\) for some \(c'>0\); every displayed term is \(o(\eta_n)\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(M\in\mathcal A_p\), abbreviate \(\Omega=\Omega_M\), and put \(r=r_M\).  The categorical summand
\[
W=M(\mathbf e_{O_1}-p)
\]
is centered and has covariance
\[
\mathbb E_p[WW^{\top}]
=M\{\operatorname{diag}(p)-pp^{\top}\}M^{\top}=\Omega.
\tag{1}
\]
If \(r=0\), positive semidefiniteness gives \(\Omega=0\), and
\[
\mathbb E_p\|W\|_2^2=\operatorname{tr}(\Omega)=0.
\]
Thus \(W=0\) almost surely.  The Gaussian vector \(MG_p\) also has covariance zero and is zero almost surely, so the lower-orthant approximation error for this \(M\) is zero.

Suppose \(r>0\).  Choose \(Q\in\mathbb R^{\operatorname{rows}(M)\times r}\) whose columns form an orthonormal basis of \(\operatorname{range}(\Omega)\), and define
\[
\overline\Omega=Q^{\top}\Omega Q.
\]
This matrix is positive definite.  Indeed, if \(u\neq0\), then \(Qu\) is a nonzero vector in \(\operatorname{range}(\Omega)=\ker(\Omega)^{\perp}\), so
\[
u^{\top}\overline\Omega u=(Qu)^{\top}\Omega(Qu)>0.
\tag{2}
\]
For an independent size-\(m\) categorical sample, set
\[
\xi_i=m^{-1/2}\overline\Omega^{-1/2}Q^{\top}M(\mathbf e_{O_i}-p),
\qquad i=1,\ldots,m.
\]
Equations (1)--(2) give
\[
\mathbb E_p\xi_i=0,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^m\mathbb E_p[\xi_i\xi_i^{\top}]
=\overline\Omega^{-1/2}Q^{\top}\Omega Q\overline\Omega^{-1/2}=I_r.
\tag{3}
\]

If \(a\in\ker(\Omega)\), then
\[
0=a^{\top}\Omega a=\sum_{o\in\mathcal O}p_o
\{a^{\top}M(\mathbf e_o-p)\}^2.
\]
Therefore \(a^{\top}M(\mathbf e_o-p)=0\) for every \(o\) with \(p_o>0\).  Hence the summands, \(MX_m\), and \(MG_p\) lie in \(\operatorname{range}(\Omega)\) almost surely.  For every lower orthant \(( -\infty,x]\) in the original row space, its inverse image under the injective map \(u\mapsto Q\overline\Omega^{1/2}u\) is the convex set
\[
K_x=\{u\in\mathbb R^r:Q\overline\Omega^{1/2}u\leq x\}.
\tag{4}
\]
Apply \(\text{lem:raic-multivariate-berry-esseen-convex-sets}\) to (3)--(4).  It gives
\[
\sup_x\left|\Pr_p\{MX_m\leq x\}-\Pr\{MG_p\leq x\}\right|
\leq(42r^{1/4}+16)\sum_{i=1}^m\mathbb E_p\|\xi_i\|_2^3.
\tag{5}
\]
Since the \(m\) summands are identically distributed and contain the factor \(m^{-1/2}\),
\[
\sum_{i=1}^m\mathbb E_p\|\xi_i\|_2^3
=m^{-1/2}\sum_{o\in\mathcal O}p_o
\left\|\overline\Omega^{-1/2}Q^{\top}M(\mathbf e_o-p)\right\|_2^3.
\tag{6}
\]
On \(\operatorname{range}(\Omega)\),
\[
\Omega^{\dagger/2}=Q\overline\Omega^{-1/2}Q^{\top}.
\]
Because every positive-probability vector \(M(\mathbf e_o-p)\) lies in this range and \(Q\) is an isometry, the norm in (6) equals
\[
\left\|\Omega^{\dagger/2}M(\mathbf e_o-p)\right\|_2.
\tag{7}
\]
Equations (5)--(7), followed by the maximum over the finite family \(\mathcal A_p\), prove
\[
\delta_m(p)\leq C(p)m^{-1/2}.
\tag{8}
\]
Every term defining \(C(p)\) is finite for fixed \(p\) and fixed \(M\), and the family is finite, so \(C(p)<\infty\).  The use of \(\Omega^{\dagger/2}\) also shows why this constant need not be uniform near covariance-rank changes.

For all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
\frac12n^{1/3}\leq m_n\leq n^{1/3},
\qquad
\eta_n=n^{-1/12}.
\]
Consequently (8) and exponent arithmetic give
\[
\delta_{m_n}(p)=O(n^{-1/6}),
\qquad
\frac{m_n}{\sqrt n}=O(n^{-1/6}),
\qquad
\frac{m_n^2}{n}=O(n^{-1/3}).
\tag{9}
\]
After division by \(\eta_n=n^{-1/12}\), these orders are \(O(n^{-1/12})\), \(O(n^{-1/12})\), and \(O(n^{-1/4})\), respectively, and hence vanish.  Finally, \(m_n\geq n^{1/3}/2\) eventually, so for fixed \(c>0\),
\[
e^{-cm_n}\leq e^{-(c/2)n^{1/3}}=o(n^{-1/12})=o(\eta_n).
\]
This proves the effective-range bound and every explicit schedule comparison.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Effective-range whitening turns every possibly singular fan-matrix comparison into the identity-covariance problem covered by Raič's explicit convex-set bound. \textit{Gap.} The prior theorem only assumed a law-dependent approximation error; this lemma supplies a computable fixed-law constant and an explicit feasible rate. \textit{Consumer.} The atomic-tie theorem uses the resulting \(m_n^{-1/2}\) error to validate the fixed subsample and slack schedules.

\section{Interpretation}

Revealed ground truth is valuable here because the legal SLCC response paths make the trialed low- and high-performance policies bracket the untrialed middle-performance outcome. The exact quotient shows that this value is not a boundary artifact: after a full-support perturbation, both endpoint improvements persist on a relatively open subset of the observable polytope. At boundary laws used for inference, projection and endpoint ties produce a directionally nonlinear Gaussian law that may put mass at zero; the explicit subsampling procedure is conservative at that atom by a fixed-law global-sign calculation, not by assuming the atom away.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The atomic-tie theorem is pointwise at a fixed observable law. It requires a finite refined fan, every retained normal and endpoint slope to be nonzero on the sampling support space, and Gaussian nondegeneracy on that space. Its Berry--Esseen constant and Boolean-event family are fixed-law objects and need not remain controlled near covariance-rank changes. The result proves conservative simultaneous coverage for the stated coupled projection--LP endpoint statistic and schedules; it does not establish uniform validity, a general endpoint-subsampling principle, exact coverage at every nominal level, or dominance over inverted-test and atom-aware spanning procedures.

\section{Honest scope}

The identification results concern finite strata and policies and binary actions, labels, and outcomes. The explicit strictness certificate uses three distinct externally certified performance levels; the arbitrary-finite bracket construction permits duplicate endpoint arms, any strictly positive trial randomization probabilities, extra policies, performance values, and strata while preserving a nonempty relative-interior two-sided gain after a sufficiently small full-support perturbation. The optimal-dual-face equivalence is a generic LP restriction diagnostic supporting that construction, not a standalone field-level novelty. Generic confidence-region propagation is an implementation corollary. The projection-composed limit and atomic-tie subsampling theorem are pointwise; the latter retains all finite-fan, nonzero-restriction, Gaussian-nondegeneracy, and nonuniform-near-rank-change limitations and claims neither priority over atom-aware subsampling nor superiority to existing inverted-test procedures. Equality of the untrialed and a trialed configuration recovers the randomized observed mean. Full revelation of \(Z\) removes masking but does not identify a distinct untrialed configuration. Multiclass actions, dynamic policies, violation budgets, joint deployment targets, policy learning, and uniform boundary inference remain outside this kernel.
