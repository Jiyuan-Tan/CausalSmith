Put \(d=|\mathcal O|\) and \(\Phi=\theta\circ\Pi_{\mathfrak O}\).  We first differentiate the projection.  Let \(t_k\downarrow0\), let \(g_k\to g\) in \(\mathbb R^d\), and set
\[
x_k=p+t_kg_k,\qquad q_k=\Pi_{\mathfrak O}(x_k),\qquad d_k=\frac{q_k-p}{t_k}.
\tag{1}
\]
Because \(p\in\mathfrak O\), the defining minimum property of the projection gives
\[
\|q_k-x_k\|_2\leq \|p-x_k\|_2=t_k\|g_k\|_2,
\]
and hence \(\|d_k\|_2\leq2\|g_k\|_2\).  Thus \((d_k)\) is bounded.  Projection onto the nonempty closed convex polytope \(\mathfrak O\) is characterized by
\[
\langle x_k-q_k,v-q_k\rangle\leq0
\qquad(v\in\mathfrak O).
\tag{2}
\]
Every subsequential limit \(d\) of \((d_k)\) lies in \(T_{\mathfrak O}(p)\) by the definition of that closed tangent cone.  If \(z\) is a radial tangent direction, so that \(p+t_kz\in\mathfrak O\) for all sufficiently large \(k\) after an inessential common rescaling of \(z\), substitute \(v=p+t_kz\) in (2), divide by \(t_k^2\), and pass to the relevant subsequence to obtain
\[
\langle g-d,z-d\rangle\leq0.
\tag{3}
\]
Radial directions are dense in \(T_{\mathfrak O}(p)\), and both sides of (3) are continuous in \(z\), so (3) holds for every \(z\in T_{\mathfrak O}(p)\).  The variational characterization of Euclidean projection onto a closed convex cone now uniquely gives
\[
d=\Pi_{T_{\mathfrak O}(p)}g.
\tag{4}
\]
Every convergent subsequence has the same limit (4), while \((d_k)\) is bounded; consequently the whole sequence converges.  This proves that \(\Pi_{\mathfrak O}\) is Hadamard directionally differentiable at \(p\), tangentially to \(\mathbb R^d\), with derivative \(g\mapsto\Pi_{T_{\mathfrak O}(p)}g\).  No relative-interior condition on \(p\) was used.

We next differentiate the two endpoint programs on their relative domain.  By \(\text{lem:finite-incidence-duality-continuity}\), there are finite families of affine functions
\[
\ell_r(x)=\alpha_r+\lambda_r^{\top}x\quad(r\in R_L),
\qquad
u_s(x)=\bar\alpha_s+\bar\lambda_s^{\top}x\quad(s\in R_U)
\]
such that, on \(\mathfrak O\),
\[
L(x)=\max_{r\in R_L}\ell_r(x),
\qquad
U(x)=\min_{s\in R_U}u_s(x),
\tag{5}
\]
and every pair indexing these representations is feasible for the corresponding full dual.  Let
\[
R_L(p)=\{r:\ell_r(p)=L(p)\},
\qquad
R_U(p)=\{s:u_s(p)=U(p)\}.
\]
For \(r\notin R_L(p)\) the gap \(L(p)-\ell_r(p)\) is strictly positive, and there are only finitely many indices.  Therefore, whenever \(z_k\to z\) and \(p+t_kz_k\in\mathfrak O\), all inactive indices disappear from the maximum in (5) for sufficiently large \(k\).  It follows that
\[
\frac{L(p+t_kz_k)-L(p)}{t_k}longrightarrow
\max_{r\in R_L(p)}\lambda_r^{\top}z.
\tag{6}
\]
The same finite-gap argument for the minimum gives
\[
\frac{U(p+t_kz_k)-U(p)}{t_k}longrightarrow
\min_{s\in R_U(p)}\bar\lambda_s^{\top}z.
\tag{7}
\]

Equations (6)--(7) equal the formulas using the entire, unnormalized optimal dual faces.  Indeed, if \((\alpha,\lambda)\in\Lambda_L(p)\), dual feasibility implies
\[
\alpha+\lambda^{\top}(p+t_kz_k)\leq L(p+t_kz_k),
\qquad
\alpha+\lambda^{\top}p=L(p).
\]
After subtraction, division by \(t_k\), and passage to the limit, (6) yields
\[
\lambda^{\top}z\leq\max_{r\in R_L(p)}\lambda_r^{\top}z.
\tag{8}
\]
Conversely, each active pair from (5) belongs to \(\Lambda_L(p)\), so equality holds after maximizing (8) over the full face.  Thus
\[
L'_p(z)=\max_{(\alpha,\lambda)\in\Lambda_L(p)}\lambda^{\top}z.
\tag{9}
\]
The analogous inequalities, with their directions reversed, give
\[
U'_p(z)=\min_{(\alpha,\lambda)\in\Lambda_U(p)}\lambda^{\top}z.
\tag{10}
\]
Although the dual pairs are unnormalized, (9)--(10) are gauge safe: every \(z\in T_{\mathfrak O}(p)\) satisfies \(\mathbf 1^{\top}z=0\), since \(\mathfrak O\) lies in the probability simplex, and the transformation \((\alpha,\lambda)\mapsto(\alpha-c,\lambda+c\mathbf1)\) does not change \(\lambda^{\top}z\).

For the sequence in (1), equation (4) says \((q_k-p)/t_k\to z:=\Pi_{T_{\mathfrak O}(p)}g\).  Apply (6)--(10) with \(q_k=p+t_kd_k\).  We obtain
\[
\frac{\Phi(p+t_kg_k)-\Phi(p)}{t_k}longrightarrow
\left(
\max_{(\alpha,\lambda)\in\Lambda_L(p)}\lambda^{\top}z,
\min_{(\alpha,\lambda)\in\Lambda_U(p)}\lambda^{\top}z
\right)
=\Gamma'_p(g).
\tag{11}
\]
This is Hadamard directional differentiability.  The derivative is continuous because it is the composition of the continuous projection onto a closed convex cone with a finite piecewise-linear endpoint map.  Multiple active facets, and hence multiple optimal LP bases, are retained in the maximum and minimum in (11).

Under \(\text{ass:iid-finite-sampling}\), \(\text{lem:finite-multinomial-clt}\) gives
\[
\sqrt n(\widehat p-p)\rightsquigarrow G_p.
\tag{12}
\]
The estimator in \(\text{def:projection-endpoint-estimator}\) satisfies \(\widehat\theta=\Phi(\widehat p)\) and \(\Phi(p)=\theta(p)\), because \(p\in\mathfrak O\).  Apply \(\text{lem:hadamard-directional-delta-method}\) to (11)--(12).  This yields
\[
\sqrt n\{\widehat\theta-\theta(p)\}\rightsquigarrow\Gamma'_p(G_p),
\]
including when \(p\) is on a projection face and either optimal dual face is non-singleton.
