Let \(d=|\mathcal O|\), let \(\Phi=\theta\circ\Pi_{\mathfrak O}\), and define
\[
X_n=\sqrt n(\widehat p-p),
\qquad
T_n=\max\{\sqrt n(\widehat L-L(p)),\sqrt n(U(p)-\widehat U)\}.
\tag{1}
\]
By \(\text{lem:finite-multinomial-clt}\), under \(\text{ass:iid-finite-sampling}\),
\[
X_n\rightsquigarrow G_p,
\qquad X_n=O_{\Pr}(1).
\tag{2}
\]
Moreover, \(X_n\in V_p\) almost surely: its coordinates sum to zero, and \(p_o=0\) implies \(N_o=0\) almost surely.  The local fan hypothesis supplies \(r>0\) such that
\[
\Phi(p+x)-\Phi(p)=(D_L(x),D_U(x))
\quad\text{for }x\in V_p,\ \|x\|_2<r.
\tag{3}
\]
The two coordinates are continuous, positively homogeneous, and linear on finitely many cones.  Hence some \(C_D<\infty\) satisfies
\[
|D_L(x)|\vee|D_U(x)|\leq C_D\|x\|_2,
\qquad x\in V_p.
\tag{4}
\]
Equations (2)--(4), equivalently the directional limit in \(\text{thm:projection-composed-directional-limit}\), give
\[
T_n=H(X_n)
\tag{5}
\]
with probability tending to one and \(H(X_n)\rightsquigarrow H(G_p)\).

Write \(m=m_n\), let \(\Pr^*\) denote probability conditional on the full sample, let \(\widehat p_b\) be the empirical law in a uniformly chosen size-\(m\) subsample without replacement, and put
\[
a_n=\sqrt m(\widehat p-p)=\sqrt{m/n}\,X_n.
\tag{6}
\]
When both empirical laws lie in the neighborhood in (3), the statistic whose conditional lower quantile defines \(c^{\mathrm{sub}}_{n,1-\alpha+\eta_n}\) is exactly
\[
S_{n,b}=\max\left\{
D_L\bigl(\sqrt m(\widehat p_b-p)\bigr)-D_L(a_n),
D_U(a_n)-D_U\bigl(\sqrt m(\widehat p_b-p)\bigr)
\right\}.
\tag{7}
\]
Indeed, apply (3) at \(\widehat p_b\) and \(\widehat p\), subtract, multiply by \(\sqrt m\), and use positive homogeneity.

We compare (7) to an independent size-\(m\) experiment.  Couple the ordered indices sampled without replacement to \(m\) indices sampled with replacement from \(\{1,\ldots,n\}\).  A failure requires a repeated with-replacement index, so its conditional probability is at most
\[
\frac{m(m-1)}{2n}.
\tag{8}
\]
Next couple each draw from the empirical law \(\widehat p\) to a draw from \(p\) by assigning matched category \(o\) probability \(\min\{\widehat p_o,p_o\}\) and distributing the two residual masses over unmatched categories.  The mismatch probability is therefore
\[
1-\sum_o\min\{\widehat p_o,p_o\}
=\operatorname{TV}(\widehat p,p)=\frac12\|\widehat p-p\|_1.
\]
Using independent copies of this construction and the union bound gives total conditional mismatch probability at most
\[
\frac m2\|\widehat p-p\|_1.
\tag{9}
\]
On \(\{\|X_n\|_2\leq M\}\), (9) is at most \(m\sqrt d M/(2\sqrt n)\).

On the successful coupling event, \(\sqrt m(\widehat p_b-p)=X_m\) for the independent vector in \(\text{lem:finite-alphabet-fan-berry-esseen-explicit-schedule}\), and (7) becomes
\[
K_{a_n}(X_m),
\qquad
K_a(g)=\max\{D_L(g)-D_L(a),D_U(a)-D_U(g)\}.
\tag{10}
\]
The local-neighborhood failure is exponentially small.  Each coordinate of \(\mathbf e_{O_i}-p\) is centered and lies in \([-1,1]\).  The elementary bound \(e^u\leq1+u+C u^2\) on a fixed bounded interval, followed by independence, exponential Markov inequality, and a union bound over the \(d\) coordinates, gives constants \(C_0,c_0>0\) such that
\[
\Pr_p\{\|X_m\|_2>r\sqrt m/2\}\leq C_0e^{-c_0m}.
\tag{11}
\]
For fixed \(M\), equations (6), (8)--(11) therefore bound the conditional coupling and localization error on \(\{\|X_n\|_2\leq M\}\) by
\[
q_{n,M}=\frac{m(m-1)}{2n}+\frac{m\sqrt d M}{2\sqrt n}+C_0e^{-c_0m}.
\tag{12}
\]

On every pair of fan cones containing \(a\) and \(g\), the event \(K_a(g)\leq x\) is an intersection of linear inequalities.  Taking the finite union over cone pairs makes it a finite Boolean combination of lower orthants whose row matrices belong to \(\mathcal A_p\).  Finite inclusion--exclusion and \(\text{lem:finite-alphabet-fan-berry-esseen-explicit-schedule}\) therefore bound the multinomial-to-Gaussian error by a fixed finite multiple of
\[
\delta_m(p)\leq C(p)m^{-1/2}.
\tag{13}
\]
Moreover, (4) gives
\[
|K_{a_n}(g)-H(g)|
\leq |D_L(a_n)|\vee|D_U(a_n)|
\leq C_D\|a_n\|_2.
\tag{14}
\]
On \(\{\|X_n\|_2\leq M\}\), this is at most \(C_D M\sqrt{m/n}\).  For every nonzero normal in the finite pool, Gaussian nondegeneracy on \(V_p\) gives a centered normal scalar with positive variance and hence a bounded density.  Gaussian slab probabilities created by (14) are consequently bounded by a fixed multiple of \(\sqrt{m/n}\), which is no larger than a fixed multiple of \(m/\sqrt n\).

For the explicit schedules, the helper lemma gives
\[
\delta_m(p)=O(n^{-1/6}),\quad
m/\sqrt n=O(n^{-1/6}),\quad
m^2/n=O(n^{-1/3}),\quad
e^{-c_0m}=O(e^{-c'n^{1/3}}),
\tag{15}
\]
and every term is \(o(\eta_n)\), where \(\eta_n=n^{-1/12}\) eventually.  Since \(X_n=O_{\Pr}(1)\), equations (12)--(15) imply, at every continuity point \(x\) of the distribution of \(H(G_p)\),
\[
\Pr^*\{S_{n,b}\leq x\}\longrightarrow
F(x):=\Pr\{H(G_p)\leq x\}
\quad\text{in probability}.
\tag{16}
\]

On each full-dimensional cone of the fan refined by endpoint-comparison and zero hyperplanes, \(H\) is one homogeneous linear functional.  Proper cone boundaries lie in finitely many hyperplanes with normals nonzero on \(V_p\), so Gaussian nondegeneracy makes them null.  A nonzero linear functional of \(G_p\) has a continuous Gaussian distribution, whereas a zero linear functional is identically zero.  Therefore
\[
F\text{ can have an atom only at }0.
\tag{17}
\]

Let \(c_n=c^{\mathrm{sub}}_{n,\beta+\eta_n}\), using the lower-quantile convention, and let \(q_\beta=\inf\{x:F(x)\geq\beta\}\).  If \(q_\beta\neq0\), then for every sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\), the point \(x_\varepsilon=q_\beta-\varepsilon\) is a continuity point and \(F(x_\varepsilon)<\beta\).  Equation (16) and \(\eta_n\downarrow0\) imply \(\Pr\{c_n>x_\varepsilon\}\to1\).  Combining this with (5), weak convergence, and continuity at the nonzero point \(q_\beta\), then letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), yields
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\Pr\{T_n\leq c_n\}\geq F(q_\beta)\geq\beta.
\tag{18}
\]

It remains to treat \(q_\beta=0\), where
\[
F(0-)\leq\beta\leq F(0).
\tag{19}
\]
If \(H(X_n)=0\), then positive homogeneity and (6) give
\[
D_L(a_n)\leq0,
\qquad D_U(a_n)\geq0.
\tag{20}
\]
For every \(g\in V_p\), the global sign implication
\[
\begin{aligned}
K_{a_n}(g)<0
&\Longrightarrow D_L(g)<D_L(a_n)\leq0
\ \text{and}\ D_U(g)>D_U(a_n)\geq0\\
&\Longrightarrow H(g)<0
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]
uses neither a selected projection cone nor a unique LP slope.  The event \(\{H(g)<0\}\) is a finite union of intersections of strict half-spaces from the refined fan.  Approximation from below and the same finite inclusion--exclusion comparison used for (13) give, uniformly on \(\{\|X_n\|_2\leq M,H(X_n)=0\}\),
\[
\Pr^*\{S_{n,b}<0\}
\leq F(0-)+O(\delta_m(p))+O_M(m/\sqrt n)+O(m^2/n)+O(e^{-c_0m}).
\tag{22}
\]
By (15), the error in (22) is \(o(\eta_n)\).  If \(F(0-)<\beta\), it is eventually smaller than the fixed gap to \(\beta\); if \(F(0-)=\beta\), it is smaller than the added slack \(\eta_n\) with probability tending to one.  A lower \((\beta+\eta_n)\)-quantile is nonnegative whenever the conditional mass strictly below zero is less than \(\beta+\eta_n\).  Letting \(M\to\infty\) using (2), we obtain
\[
\Pr\{H(X_n)=0,\ c_n<0\}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{23}
\]

Also \(c_n=o_{\Pr}(1)\).  Indeed, for every \(\varepsilon>0\), \(F(-\varepsilon)<\beta\), so (16) gives \(c_n>-\varepsilon\) with probability tending to one.  Moreover \(F(\varepsilon)>\beta\): this is immediate if \(F(0)>\beta\); if \(F(0)=\beta\) and \(H\) takes a positive value, continuity, positive homogeneity, and Gaussian full support give positive probability to \(0<H(G_p)\leq\varepsilon\); if \(H\) takes no positive value, then \(F(\varepsilon)=1\).  A second use of (16) gives \(c_n\leq\varepsilon\) with probability tending to one.  Hence
\[
c_n=o_{\Pr}(1).
\tag{24}
\]

The zero set of \(H\) is a finite union of interiors of full-dimensional zero cones and subsets of finitely many proper fan hyperplanes.  Its boundary is Gaussian-null by nondegeneracy.  Thus it is a continuity set for (2), and
\[
\Pr\{H(X_n)=0\}\longrightarrow F(0)-F(0-).
\tag{25}
\]
For each \(\varepsilon>0\), equations (5), (23), and (24) cover, apart from a probability tending to zero, both disjoint events \(\{H(X_n)\leq-\varepsilon\}\) and \(\{H(X_n)=0\}\).  Equations (17) and (25) therefore give
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\Pr\{T_n\leq c_n\}
\geq F(-\varepsilon)+F(0)-F(0-).
\]
Letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) makes the right-hand side \(F(0)\geq\beta\).  Together with (18),
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\Pr\{T_n\leq c_n\}\geq1-\alpha.
\tag{26}
\]
By (1), the event in (26) is precisely the two endpoint inequalities in the statement.  The level is always well typed because \(0<\eta_n\leq\alpha/2\), so \(0<1-\alpha+\eta_n<1\).

Finally, \(\text{prop:32-nondegenerate-atomic-tie-certificate}\) supplies the required exact early-kill verification at \(p_\dagger\): the corrected projections satisfy all thirty-two polar inequalities and orthogonality, matching rational primal and dual certificates give the four displayed endpoint derivatives, distinct optimal slopes occur for both LPs, and open Gaussian-positive regions have respectively \(H=0\) and \(H>0\).  Hence its atom is strictly between zero and one, completing the nonvacuity claim.
