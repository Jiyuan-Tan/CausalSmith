Fix \(M\in\mathcal A_p\) and abbreviate \(\Omega=\Omega_M\) and \(r=r_M\).  For one categorical observation define
\[
W=M(\mathbf e_{O_1}-p).
\]
By \(\text{ass:iid-finite-sampling}\), \(W\) is centered and
\[
\mathbb E_p[WW^{\top}]
=M\{\operatorname{diag}(p)-pp^{\top}\}M^{\top}
=M\Sigma_pM^{\top}
=\Omega.
\tag{1}
\]
If \(r=0\), then \(\Omega=0\), because \(\Omega\) is positive semidefinite, and
\[
\mathbb E_p\|W\|_2^2=\operatorname{tr}(\Omega)=0.
\]
Thus \(W=0\) almost surely.  The centered Gaussian vector \(MG_p\) also has covariance zero and is therefore zero almost surely.  Hence the lower-orthant approximation error for this \(M\) is zero.

Suppose now that \(r>0\).  Choose \(Q\in\mathbb R^{\operatorname{rows}(M)\times r}\) whose columns form an orthonormal basis of \(\operatorname{range}(\Omega)\), and put
\[
\overline\Omega=Q^{\top}\Omega Q.
\]
The matrix \(\overline\Omega\) is positive definite.  Indeed, if \(u\ne0\), then \(Qu\) is a nonzero element of \(\operatorname{range}(\Omega)=\ker(\Omega)^{\perp}\); since \(\Omega\) is positive semidefinite,
\[
u^{\top}\overline\Omega u=(Qu)^{\top}\Omega(Qu)>0.
\tag{2}
\]
For an independent size-\(m\) sample from \(p\), define
\[
\xi_i=m^{-1/2}\overline\Omega^{-1/2}Q^{\top}M(\mathbf e_{O_i}-p),
\qquad i=1,\ldots,m.
\]
Equations (1)--(2) and \(\text{ass:iid-finite-sampling}\) give
\[
\mathbb E_p\xi_i=0,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^m\mathbb E_p[\xi_i\xi_i^{\top}]
=\overline\Omega^{-1/2}Q^{\top}\Omega Q\overline\Omega^{-1/2}
=I_r.
\tag{3}
\]

If \(a\in\ker(\Omega)\), then
\[
\mathbb E_p(a^{\top}W)^2=a^{\top}\Omega a=0,
\]
so \(a^{\top}W=0\) almost surely.  Consequently both \(MX_m\) and \(MG_p\) lie in \(\operatorname{range}(\Omega)\) almost surely.  Moreover,
\[
MX_m=Q\overline\Omega^{1/2}\sum_{i=1}^m\xi_i.
\]
For each lower orthant \(( -\infty,x]\) in the row space of \(M\), its inverse image under the injective linear map \(u\mapsto Q\overline\Omega^{1/2}u\) is
\[
K_x=\{u\in\mathbb R^r:Q\overline\Omega^{1/2}u\leq x\},
\tag{4}
\]
an intersection of finitely many closed half-spaces and hence a measurable convex set.  The whitened version of \(MG_p\) is a centered Gaussian vector with covariance \(I_r\).  Applying \(\text{lem:raic-multivariate-berry-esseen-convex-sets}\) to (3) and (4) therefore yields
\[
\sup_x\left|\Pr_p\{MX_m\leq x\}-\Pr\{MG_p\leq x\}\right|
\leq(42r^{1/4}+16)\sum_{i=1}^m\mathbb E_p\|\xi_i\|_2^3.
\tag{5}
\]
Because the \(m\) summands are identically distributed and each contains the factor \(m^{-1/2}\),
\[
\sum_{i=1}^m\mathbb E_p\|\xi_i\|_2^3
=m^{-1/2}\sum_{o\in\mathcal O}p_o
\left\|\overline\Omega^{-1/2}Q^{\top}M(\mathbf e_o-p)\right\|_2^3.
\tag{6}
\]
To identify the norm in (6), observe from (1) that
\[
\Omega=\sum_{o\in\mathcal O}p_o
M(\mathbf e_o-p)M(\mathbf e_o-p)^{\top}.
\]
Thus, if \(p_o>0\), every vector in \(\ker(\Omega)\) is orthogonal to \(M(\mathbf e_o-p)\), and hence \(M(\mathbf e_o-p)\in\operatorname{range}(\Omega)\).  On that range the Moore--Penrose inverse square root is
\[
\Omega^{\dagger/2}=Q\overline\Omega^{-1/2}Q^{\top}.
\]
Since \(Q\) is an isometry, for every \(o\) with \(p_o>0\),
\[
\left\|\overline\Omega^{-1/2}Q^{\top}M(\mathbf e_o-p)\right\|_2
=\left\|\Omega^{\dagger/2}M(\mathbf e_o-p)\right\|_2.
\tag{7}
\]
Cells with \(p_o=0\) contribute zero to (6).  Substituting (6)--(7) into (5), then maximizing over the finite family \(\mathcal A_p\), proves
\[
\delta_m(p)\leq C(p)m^{-1/2}.
\tag{8}
\]
For fixed \(p\), each summand in the definition of \(C(p)\) is finite; the cell set \(\mathcal O\) and matrix family \(\mathcal A_p\) are finite.  Therefore \(C(p)<\infty\).  The argument is pointwise in \(p\): as \(p\) varies toward a covariance-rank change, a positive eigenvalue inverted by \(\Omega_M^{\dagger/2}\) may tend to zero, so no uniform bound is asserted.

It remains to check the explicit schedules.  Because \(\alpha\in(0,1)\), for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
\frac12n^{1/3}\leq m_n=\lfloor n^{1/3}\rfloor\leq n^{1/3},
\qquad
\eta_n=n^{-1/12}.
\]
Equation (8) and elementary exponent arithmetic now give
\[
\delta_{m_n}(p)=O(n^{-1/6}),
\qquad
\frac{m_n}{\sqrt n}=O(n^{-1/6}),
\qquad
\frac{m_n^2}{n}=O(n^{-1/3}).
\tag{9}
\]
After division by \(\eta_n=n^{-1/12}\), the three orders in (9) become, respectively, \(O(n^{-1/12})\), \(O(n^{-1/12})\), and \(O(n^{-1/4})\), all of which tend to zero.  Finally, for every fixed \(c>0\), the lower bound on \(m_n\) gives
\[
e^{-cm_n}\leq e^{-(c/2)n^{1/3}}=o(n^{-1/12})=o(\eta_n).
\]
Taking \(c'=c/2>0\) establishes the claimed exponential order and every displayed \(o(\eta_n)\) comparison.
