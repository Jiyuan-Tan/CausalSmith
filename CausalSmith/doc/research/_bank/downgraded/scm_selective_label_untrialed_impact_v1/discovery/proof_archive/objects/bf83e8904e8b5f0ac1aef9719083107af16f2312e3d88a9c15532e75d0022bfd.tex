Fix \(p\in\mathfrak O\).  By \(\text{lem:finite-incidence-duality-continuity}\), the full lower program has the attained dual representation
\[
 L(p)=\max_{(\alpha,\lambda)\in\mathcal D_L}
       \{\alpha+\lambda^{\top}p\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Apply the same lemma to the finite incidence matrix \(B_{\mathrm{blind}}=K_{\mathrm{blind}}B\), as defined in \(\text{def:z-blind-projection}\).  Its attained lower dual is
\[
 L_{\mathrm{blind}}(K_{\mathrm{blind}}p)
 =\max_{\alpha,\mu}
 \left\{\alpha+\mu^{\top}K_{\mathrm{blind}}p:
 \alpha\mathbf 1+B^{\top}K_{\mathrm{blind}}^{\top}\mu\leq h_e\right\}.
\tag{2}
\]
Under the substitution \(\lambda=K_{\mathrm{blind}}^{\top}\mu\), the feasible objective pairs in (2) are exactly the elements of \(\mathcal D_L\cap\mathcal E_K\), by \(\text{def:dual-refinement-faces}\), and
\[
 \alpha+\mu^{\top}K_{\mathrm{blind}}p
 =\alpha+\lambda^{\top}p.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus (2) is the restriction of (1) to \(\mathcal D_L\cap\mathcal E_K\).  Both optima are attained by \(\text{lem:finite-incidence-duality-continuity}\).  Therefore their values are equal if and only if a maximizer of (1) lies in the restricted set:
\[
 L_{\mathrm{blind}}(K_{\mathrm{blind}}p)=L(p)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \Lambda_L(p)\cap\mathcal E_K\neq\varnothing.
\tag{4}
\]
Indeed, a point in the intersection attains both values, while equality of the values makes any attained optimizer of (2) an element of \(\Lambda_L(p)\cap\mathcal E_K\).  Since \(\text{lem:z-blind-coarsening}\) gives
\(L_{\mathrm{blind}}(K_{\mathrm{blind}}p)\leq L(p)\), (4) implies
\[
 L(p)>L_{\mathrm{blind}}(K_{\mathrm{blind}}p)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \Lambda_L(p)\cap\mathcal E_K=\varnothing.
\tag{5}
\]

The upper argument is identical with the order reversed.  Specifically, \(\text{lem:finite-incidence-duality-continuity}\) and \(\text{def:z-blind-projection}\) give
\[
 U(p)=\min_{(\alpha,\lambda)\in\mathcal D_U}
       \{\alpha+\lambda^{\top}p\},
\tag{6}
\]
and
\[
 U_{\mathrm{blind}}(K_{\mathrm{blind}}p)
 =\min_{\alpha,\mu}
 \left\{\alpha+\mu^{\top}K_{\mathrm{blind}}p:
 \alpha\mathbf 1+B^{\top}K_{\mathrm{blind}}^{\top}\mu\geq h_e\right\}.
\tag{7}
\]
Equation (7) is the restriction of (6) to \(\mathcal D_U\cap\mathcal E_K\).  Attainment and the inequality
\(U(p)\leq U_{\mathrm{blind}}(K_{\mathrm{blind}}p)\) from \(\text{lem:z-blind-coarsening}\) yield
\[
 U(p)<U_{\mathrm{blind}}(K_{\mathrm{blind}}p)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \Lambda_U(p)\cap\mathcal E_K=\varnothing.
\tag{8}
\]
Unfolding membership in \(\mathcal D_L\), \(\mathcal D_U\), and \(\mathcal E_K\) in (5) and (8) gives exactly the two advertised finite linear feasibility systems.  Hence the dual-face equivalences are also computable diagnostics once the two full endpoint values have been computed.

Define on \(\mathfrak O\)
\[
 g_L(p)=L(p)-L_{\mathrm{blind}}(K_{\mathrm{blind}}p),
 \qquad
 g_U(p)=U_{\mathrm{blind}}(K_{\mathrm{blind}}p)-U(p).
\tag{9}
\]
The four endpoint functions are continuous on their relative domains by \(\text{lem:finite-incidence-duality-continuity}\), and \(K_{\mathrm{blind}}\) is linear.  Thus both functions in (9) are continuous on \(\mathfrak O\).  By \(\text{def:strict-refinement-region}\),
\[
 \mathcal S_{\mathrm{strict}}
 =g_L^{-1}((0,\infty))\cap g_U^{-1}((0,\infty)),
\tag{10}
\]
so \(\mathcal S_{\mathrm{strict}}\) is relatively open in \(\mathfrak O\).

It remains to establish the selective-label-specific nonemptiness assertion.  Assume \(\mathsf{Bracket}_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\), and choose \(s_*\in\mathcal S\) and \(q_\ell<q_m<q_h\) supplied by \(\text{def:bracket-stratum-design}\).  For each row \(t_i^{32}\) of \(\text{def:complete-32-type-table}\), construct a response type \(\bar t_i\) on the given finite design as follows.  Set its stratum to \(s_*\), retain the displayed outcome coordinates at \(q_\ell,q_m,q_h\), and, for every additional \(q\in\mathcal Q\), put
\[
 Y_{(1,q)}(\bar t_i)=
 \begin{cases}
 Y_{(1,q_\ell)}(t_i^{32}),&q<q_m,\\
 Y_{(1,q_m)}(t_i^{32}),&q=q_m,\\
 Y_{(1,q_h)}(t_i^{32}),&q>q_m.
 \end{cases}
\tag{11}
\]
For a row with \(z=0\), set \(Y_{(0,q)}(\bar t_i)=1\) for all \(q\); for a row with \(z=1\), set \(Y_{(0,q)}(\bar t_i)=0\) for all \(q\).  Retain the two displayed action bits \(A_{(1,q_\ell)}\) and \(A_{(1,q_h)}\), and set every other action coordinate, including \(A_{(1,q_m)}\), to zero.

These extensions belong to \(\mathcal T_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\).  To verify this directly from \(\text{def:legal-response-types}\), consider first \(z=0\).  The correct-recommendation path \(Y_{(0,q)}\) is constantly one, while (11) extends the displayed nonincreasing incorrect-recommendation path; hence correctness, correct-performance order, incorrect-performance order, and neutral invariance all hold.  For \(z=1\), the incorrect neutral path is constantly zero, while (11) extends the displayed nondecreasing correct-recommendation path; the same four restrictions again hold.  No condition in \(\text{def:legal-response-types}\) restricts action coordinates, so the action assignment is legal.

Let \(w_0\in\Delta_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\) assign to \(\bar t_i\) the weight \(w_i^{32}(\pi^{32,\circ})\) from \(\text{def:32-coordinate-mixture}\), and assign zero to every other legal type.  The defining conditions \(\pi^{32,\circ}\in\mathcal P_{32}\) give \(\varepsilon_{32}>0\), \(a_{32}>0\), and \(b_{32}=\pi_1+a_{32}>0\).  Moreover, because thirty rows receive \(\varepsilon_{32}\) and \(\pi_2=\pi_3\),
\[
 30\varepsilon_{32}+a_{32}+b_{32}
 =\frac{30\pi_4}{24}+\left(\pi_2-\frac{\pi_4}{8}\right)
  +\left(\pi_1+\pi_2-\frac{\pi_4}{8}\right)
 =\pi_1+2\pi_2+\pi_4=1.
\tag{12}
\]
Thus the assigned vector is indeed in \(\Delta_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\).  Put
\[
 p_0=Bw_0.
\tag{13}
\]

We next show that all four endpoint programs at \(p_0\) reduce exactly to the certified thirty-two-row programs.  If a trial arm \(j\) uses \((1,q_d)\) at \(s_*\), where \(d\in\{\ell,h\}\), then \(\text{def:selective-label-incidence-map}\) makes its observable block equal to \(\rho_j\) times the corresponding deterministic cell indicator.  The matching block of \(B^{32}\) equals one half times that indicator by \(\text{def:complete-32-type-table}\).  Consequently, for every selected extension,
\[
 B_{j,\cdot}=2\rho_j B^{32}_{d,\cdot}
 \quad\text{on the arm block }j.
\tag{14}
\]
Every multiplier in (14) is positive by \(\text{def:bracket-stratum-design}\).  Selecting one low arm and one high arm and dividing their blocks by \(2\rho_j\) therefore recovers both canonical endpoint blocks; duplicate endpoint arms add only copies rescaled by known positive constants.

Now let \(v\in\Delta_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\) satisfy \(Bv=p_0\).  The law in (13) assigns zero probability to every cell with stratum different from \(s_*\).  For any \(s\neq s_*\), summing the incidence entries over all arm, action, outcome, and admissible-label cells at stratum \(s\) gives
\[
 \sum_{j\in J_0}\rho_j\sum_{t:S(t)=s}v_t
 =\sum_{t:S(t)=s}v_t,
\tag{15}
\]
where \(\sum_{j\in J_0}\rho_j=1\) follows from the probability-vector definition of \(\rho\).  Nonnegativity of \(v\) and (15) force \(v_t=0\) whenever \(S(t)\neq s_*\).

Restrict a remaining legal type to the three configurations \((1,q_\ell),(1,q_m),(1,q_h)\), its two endpoint action bits, and its endpoint observable cells.  By the quotient-cover conclusion of \(\text{thm:complete-32-type-refinement-certificate}\), there is a displayed row having the same two full observable blocks, the same target value \(Y_{(1,q_m)}\), and the same two label-blind blocks.  Aggregating \(v\) over chosen displayed representatives produces a vector \(\gamma\in\Delta_{31}\).  Equations (14)--(15) then give
\[
 Bv=p_0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 B^{32}\gamma=p^{32,\circ},
 \qquad
 h_e^{\top}v=(h^{32})^{\top}\gamma.
\tag{16}
\]
The reverse implication in (16) is realized by lifting each displayed row to the fixed legal extension \(\bar t_i\).  For a vector feasible only for the blind equation, repeat the aggregation using the label-blind quotient part of the same certificate; this may produce a different vector \(\gamma\in\Delta_{31}\), and it gives
\[
 K_{\mathrm{blind}}Bv=K_{\mathrm{blind}}p_0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 K^{32}B^{32}\gamma=K^{32}p^{32,\circ},
 \qquad
 h_e^{\top}v=(h^{32})^{\top}\gamma,
\tag{17}
\]
again with both directions realized.  Therefore the feasible objective-value sets in the two full programs and the two blind programs are exactly their certified thirty-two-row counterparts.

The exact values in \(\text{thm:complete-32-type-refinement-certificate}\), together with (16)--(17), yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 L(p_0)&=\frac{397}{800},&
 U(p_0)&=\frac{601}{800},\\
 L_{\mathrm{blind}}(K_{\mathrm{blind}}p_0)&=\frac{99}{400},&
 U_{\mathrm{blind}}(K_{\mathrm{blind}}p_0)&=1.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]
Hence
\[
 g_L(p_0)=g_U(p_0)=\frac{199}{800}>0.
\tag{19}
\]

To move the witness into the relative interior, use finiteness of \(\mathcal T_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\) and choose \(u\in\Delta_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\) with \(u_t>0\) for every legal type.  For \(0<\delta<1\), define
\[
 w_\delta=(1-\delta)w_0+\delta u,
 \qquad p_\delta=Bw_\delta.
\tag{20}
\]
Every coordinate of \(w_\delta\) is positive.  For completeness, let \(b_1,\ldots,b_N\) be the distinct columns of \(B\), and aggregate the coordinates of \(w_\delta\) on equal columns to obtain weights \(\beta_k>0\) with \(\sum_k\beta_k=1\) and \(p_\delta=\sum_k\beta_kb_k\).  Put
\[
 H=\{x\in\mathbb R^N:\mathbf 1^{\top}x=0\},
 \qquad
 V=\operatorname{span}\{b_k-b_1:2\leq k\leq N\}.
\tag{21}
\]
The linear map \(T:H\to V\), \(T(x)=\sum_kx_kb_k\), is surjective by (21).  Choose a linear right inverse \(R:V\to H\).  Because the spaces are finite dimensional, there is \(0<C<\infty\) such that \(\|Rd\|_\infty\leq C\|d\|_2\) for all \(d\in V\).  If \(V\neq\{0\}\) and
\[
 \|d\|_2<\frac{\min_k\beta_k}{C},
\tag{22}
\]
then \(\beta+Rd\) is coordinatewise positive, has mass one, and generates \(p_\delta+d\).  If \(V=\{0\}\), the same relative-interior conclusion is immediate.  Since \(p_\delta+V\) is the affine hull direction of \(\mathfrak O\), (22) proves
\[
 p_\delta\in\operatorname{relint}(\mathfrak O).
\tag{23}
\]

By (20), \(p_\delta\to p_0\) as \(\delta\downarrow0\).  Continuity of the gap functions in (9) and the positive values in (19) imply that, for all sufficiently small \(\delta>0\),
\[
 g_L(p_\delta)>0,
 \qquad
 g_U(p_\delta)>0.
\tag{24}
\]
Equations (10), (23), and (24) show that
\(\mathcal S_{\mathrm{strict}}\cap\operatorname{relint}(\mathfrak O)\neq\varnothing\).  The relative-interior property in (23) supplies a relative neighborhood of \(p_\delta\) contained in \(\mathfrak O\), and continuity in (9) supplies a relative neighborhood on which both inequalities in (24) persist.  Their intersection is a nonempty relatively open subset of \(\mathfrak O\) contained in \(\mathcal S_{\mathrm{strict}}\cap\operatorname{relint}(\mathfrak O)\).

The construction used only one low and one high arm to recover the canonical blocks in (14).  Therefore duplicate endpoint arms and arbitrary positive randomization probabilities merely add positively rescaled copies.  Formula (11) handles additional performance values; (15) isolates the bracket stratum at the boundary witness; and the full-support perturbation (20) accommodates all other strata and configurations while retaining strictness by continuity.  This proves every asserted robustness clause and completes the theorem.
