Use the row and column orders fixed by \(\text{def:complete-32-type-table}\).  We first verify legality and the observable--target cover.  For a row with \(z=0\), the displayed neutral-outcome triple is \(111\), and the displayed \(r=1\) triple is one of
\[
000,\qquad 100,\qquad 110,\qquad 111.
\tag{19}
\]
Thus the neutral outcome is constant, the \(r=1\) outcome is nonincreasing in \(q\), and the neutral outcome is at least every \(r=1\) outcome.  These are respectively neutral invariance, incorrect-performance order, and counterfactual correctness from \(\text{def:legal-response-types}\); correct-performance order is automatic because the correct neutral path is constant.  For a row with \(z=1\), the displayed neutral triple is \(000\), and the displayed \(r=1\) triple is one of
\[
000,\qquad 001,\qquad 011,\qquad 111.
\tag{20}
\]
Now the neutral outcome is constant, the correct \(r=1\) outcome is nondecreasing, and every \(r=1\) outcome is at least the neutral outcome.  Hence all four restrictions in \(\text{def:legal-response-types}\) again hold.  The action array is unrestricted by that definition.  The displayed arrays and labels differ wherever their row indices differ, so the thirty-two rows are distinct legal full response types.

For completeness, let \(t\) be any legal response type in the specialized design.  Since \(\mathcal S^{32}=\{s_0\}\), its stratum is \(s_0\).  By \(\text{def:32-design-specialization}\) and the incidence-column construction in \(\text{def:complete-32-type-table}\), \(B^{32}_{\cdot,t}\) reads only
\[
\bigl(A_{(1,q_\ell)},Y_{(1,q_\ell)}\bigr),
\qquad
\bigl(A_{(1,q_h)},Y_{(1,q_h)}\bigr),
\tag{21}
\]
together with \(z\) in an endpoint cell for which the corresponding action equals one.  The target coordinate \(h^{32}(t)\) reads only \(Y_{(1,q_m)}\).  In particular, it does not read \(A_{(1,q_m)}\).  Although the untrialed policy uses \((1,q_m)\) to define its deployment action, \(e_{32}\notin J^{32}_0\), so that action is absent from the trial incidence map; the target is outcome-only, so it is absent from \(h^{32}\) as well.  The same is true of every action coordinate at a configuration not used by a trial arm.  Since \(\text{def:legal-response-types}\) imposes restrictions only on outcomes and no cross-coordinate restriction on actions, all such action coordinates may be fixed to their displayed values without changing \(B^{32}_{\cdot,t}\), \(h^{32}(t)\), or \(K^{32}B^{32}_{\cdot,t}\).

It remains to cover the outcomes read in (21) and by the target.  If \(z=0\), neutral invariance makes the correct neutral triple constant.  When that constant is zero, correctness forces the incorrect \(r=1\) triple to be \(000\); when it is one, incorrect-performance order permits exactly the four paths in (19).  The omitted neutral-zero case has the same read coordinates as the displayed representative with \(r=1\) path \(000\).  If \(z=1\), the incorrect neutral triple is constant.  When that constant is one, correctness forces the correct \(r=1\) triple to be \(111\); when it is zero, correct-performance order permits exactly the four paths in (20).  The omitted neutral-one case has the same read coordinates as the displayed representative with \(r=1\) path \(111\).  Finally, the four displayed endpoint-action patterns exhaust
\[
\bigl(A_{(1,q_\ell)},A_{(1,q_h)}\bigr)\in\{0,1\}^2.
\tag{22}
\]
Thus every legal response type has the same \((B^{32}_{\cdot,t},h^{32}(t))\), and hence the same \((K^{32}B^{32}_{\cdot,t},h^{32}(t))\), as at least one displayed row.  This is a complete quotient-generating cover; no injectivity or minimality is asserted.  Indeed, rows \(1\) and \(17\) have the same observable column and target because both endpoint actions are zero and both read endpoint outcomes and the middle outcome are zero.  Inspection of the table also shows that each of the six possible cells in each trial arm occurs.  Therefore the twelve displayed cells are exactly the feasible observable rows, and the listed columns generate the full observable polytope without omitting a column that can alter any of the four endpoint programs.

We next calculate those programs.  For \(\pi_3=\pi_2\), direct multiplication of the weights in \(\text{def:32-coordinate-mixture}\) by the two half-incidence entries in every table column gives
\[
\begin{split}
p(\pi)=\bigl(&\pi_4/6,\pi_4/6,\pi_4/24,\pi_2/2+\pi_4/24,\pi_4/8,
\pi_1/2+\pi_2/2-\pi_4/24,\\
&\pi_4/6,\pi_4/6,\pi_4/8,\pi_2/2-\pi_4/24,\pi_4/24,
\pi_1/2+\pi_2/2+\pi_4/24\bigr)^{\top},
\end{split}
\tag{23}
\]
and coarsening the two revealed-label cells with equal action and outcome gives
\[
K^{32}p(\pi)=\bigl(\pi_4/6,\pi_4/6,\pi_2/2+\pi_4/12,
\pi_1/2+\pi_2/2+\pi_4/12,\pi_4/6,\pi_4/6,
\pi_2/2+\pi_4/12,\pi_1/2+\pi_2/2+\pi_4/12\bigr)^{\top}.
\tag{24}
\]
In the full and blind row orders, respectively, define
\[
\lambda_L=(0,0,0,0,0,2,0,0,0,0,2,0)^{\top},
\]
\[
\lambda_U=(0,0,-2,0,0,0,2,2,2,0,2,2)^{\top},
\]
\[
\mu_L=(0,2,0,2,-2,0,-2,0)^{\top},
\qquad
\mu_U=(2,2,2,2,0,0,0,0)^{\top}.
\tag{25}
\]
For each displayed column \(i\), the monotone paths (19)--(20) imply
\[
\lambda_L^{\top}B^{32}_{\cdot,i}\leq h^{32}_i
\leq\lambda_U^{\top}B^{32}_{\cdot,i}.
\tag{26}
\]
For the lower inequality, the left side is the indicator of the low-arm cell \((1,1,1)\) plus the indicator of the high-arm cell \((1,1,0)\); only the summand matching the unit's label can be nonzero, and endpoint monotonicity then forces the middle outcome to one.  For the upper inequality, when \(z=0\) the right side is \(1-\mathbf 1\{A_{(1,q_\ell)}=1,Y_{(1,q_\ell)}=0\}\), and when \(z=1\) it is \(1-\mathbf 1\{A_{(1,q_h)}=1,Y_{(1,q_h)}=0\}\).  In either case a middle outcome of one forces the relevant endpoint outcome to one by the appropriate performance order.  Similarly, column by column,
\[
\mu_L^{\top}K^{32}B^{32}_{\cdot,i}
=Y_{(1,q_\ell)}(t_i^{32})+Y_{(1,q_h)}(t_i^{32})-1
\leq h_i^{32}
\leq 1
=\mu_U^{\top}K^{32}B^{32}_{\cdot,i}.
\tag{27}
\]
The left inequality holds for every binary monotone three-point path: if both endpoint values are one, the middle value is one, and otherwise the left side is nonpositive.  The quotient-generating cover already proved shows that every omitted legal column has the same incidence and target entries as a displayed column, so (26)--(27) hold for every legal column.  With simplex multiplier \(\alpha=0\), they are therefore feasible dual certificates.  Indeed, for any \(v\geq0\) with \(\mathbf 1^{\top}v=1\) and \(B^{32}v=p(\pi)\), multiplying (26) by \(v_i\) and summing gives
\[
\lambda_L^{\top}p(\pi)\leq (h^{32})^{\top}v
\leq\lambda_U^{\top}p(\pi).
\]
The identical calculation using (27) applies to every blind-feasible \(v\).  Substitution of (23)--(24) therefore yields
\[
L\geq\pi_1+\pi_2,qquad
U\leq\pi_1+\pi_2+\pi_4,qquad
L_{\mathrm{blind}}\geq\pi_1,qquad
U_{\mathrm{blind}}\leq1.
\tag{28}
\]

For matching primal certificates, notation \(\{i:x,\ldots\}\) lists the nonzero coordinates of a nonnegative vector.  Put \(r_1=(1,0,0)\), \(r_2=(0,1,0)\), and \(r_3=(0,1,8)\) in \((\pi_1,\pi_2,\pi_4)\)-coordinates, and let \(P(r_k)\) denote the linear expression on the right side of (23) evaluated at \(r_k\).  Define
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|l}

FL&\{32:1\}&\{20:1,32:1\}&
\{3:2/3,6:2,7:2,14:2/3,18:1/3,21:2/3,22:5/3,23:5/3,31:1/3\}\\

\tag{29}

Adding the two half-incidence entries of every listed column verifies, for \(k=1,2,3\),
\[
B^{32}v_k^{FL}=B^{32}v_k^{FU}=P(r_k),
\qquad
K^{32}B^{32}v_k^{BL}=K^{32}B^{32}v_k^{BU}=K^{32}P(r_k).
\tag{30}

The masses of the vectors for \(k=1,2,3\) are \(1,2,10\), and their target sums are
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
X&(h^{32})^{\top}v_1^X&(h^{32})^{\top}v_2^X&(h^{32})^{\top}v_3^X\\\hline
FL&1&1&1\\

\end{array}
\tag{31}
\]
For \(X\in\{FL,FU,BL,BU\}\), set
\[
v^X(\pi)=\pi_1v_1^X+left(\pi_2-\frac{\pi_4}{8}\right)v_2^X
+\frac{\pi_4}{8}v_3^X.
\tag{32}
\]
The coefficients are nonnegative because \(\pi\in\mathcal P_{32}\) entails \(\pi_1>0\), \(\pi_4>0\), and \(\pi_2>\pi_4/8\).  Using \(\pi_3=\pi_2\) and \(\sum_{k=1}^4\pi_k=1\), its mass is
\[
\pi_1+2\left(\pi_2-\frac{\pi_4}{8}\right)
+10\frac{\pi_4}{8}
=\pi_1+2\pi_2+\pi_4=1.
\tag{33}
\]
Moreover,
\[
(\pi_1,\pi_2,\pi_4)
=\pi_1r_1+\left(\pi_2-\frac{\pi_4}{8}\right)r_2
+\frac{\pi_4}{8}r_3.
\tag{34}

Equations (30), (33), and (34) prove primal feasibility of the four vectors in (32), and (31) gives objectives \(\pi_1+\pi_2\), \(\pi_1+\pi_2+\pi_4\), \(\pi_1\), and \(1\), respectively.  These reverse the four inequalities in (28), so
\[
L=\pi_1+\pi_2,qquad
U=\pi_1+\pi_2+\pi_4,qquad
L_{\mathrm{blind}}=\pi_1,qquad
U_{\mathrm{blind}}=1.
\tag{35}
\]
At \(\pi^{32}=(1,1,1,1)^{\top}/4\), (35) gives \([1/2,3/4]\) versus \([1/4,1]\), and (23) gives the stated vector \(p^{32}\).  At \(\pi^{32,\circ}=(198,199,199,204)^{\top}/800\), it gives \([397/800,601/800]\) versus \([99/400,1]\), and (23) gives the stated vector \(p^{32,\circ}\).

\varepsilon_{32}=\frac{\pi_4}{24}>0,
\qquad
a_{32}=\pi_2-\frac{\pi_4}{8}>0,
\qquad

Also \(30\varepsilon_{32}+a_{32}+b_{32}=\pi_1+2\pi_2+\pi_4=1\), so \(w^{32}(\pi)\) is a probability vector assigning positive weight to every displayed generator.  If an affine supporting functional is nonnegative on every generator of \(\mathfrak O^{32}\) and vanishes at their strictly positive convex combination, it must vanish at every generator.  Therefore that combination belongs to \(\operatorname{relint}(\mathfrak O^{32})\).  This applies to both displayed choices of \(\pi\).  By \(\text{thm:finite-response-converse}\), each feasible primal vector in (32) canonically constructs a legal SCM attaining its objective.  Thus (35) gives sharp causal endpoints, not merely extrema of an unrelated regression program.
