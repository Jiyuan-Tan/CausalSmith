Fix \(p\in\mathfrak O\).  By \(\text{lem:finite-incidence-duality-continuity}\), the full lower dual optimum is attained on \(\Lambda_L(p)\).  The blind lower dual is
\[
\max_{\alpha,\mu}\left\{\alpha+\mu^{\top}K_{\mathrm{blind}}p:
\alpha\mathbf 1+B^{\top}K_{\mathrm{blind}}^{\top}\mu\leq h_e\right\}.
\tag{9}
\]
Under the substitution \(\lambda=K_{\mathrm{blind}}^{\top}\mu\), its feasible set is exactly \(\mathcal D_L\cap\mathcal E_K\), and its objective is \(\alpha+\lambda^{\top}p\).  Thus (9) is the maximum of the full lower-dual objective over a subset of \(\mathcal D_L\).  Attainment of both programs gives
\[
L_{\mathrm{blind}}(K_{\mathrm{blind}}p)=L(p)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\Lambda_L(p)\cap\mathcal E_K\neq\varnothing.
\tag{10}
\]
The inclusion in \(\text{lem:z-blind-coarsening}\) also gives the weak inequality in the same direction.  Negating equality in that ordered pair proves the first claimed strictness equivalence.

Likewise, the blind upper dual is
\[
\min_{\alpha,\mu}\left\{\alpha+\mu^{\top}K_{\mathrm{blind}}p:
\alpha\mathbf 1+B^{\top}K_{\mathrm{blind}}^{\top}\mu\geq h_e\right\}.
\tag{11}
\]
It is the restriction of the full upper dual to \(\mathcal D_U\cap\mathcal E_K\).  Therefore
\[
U_{\mathrm{blind}}(K_{\mathrm{blind}}p)=U(p)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\Lambda_U(p)\cap\mathcal E_K\neq\varnothing,
\tag{12}
\]
and the second strictness equivalence follows.  Equations (10)--(12) also prove the displayed finite feasibility tests: after \(L(p)\) and \(U(p)\) are computed by the finite primal programs, every constraint in either test is a linear equality or inequality in \((\alpha,\lambda,\mu)\).  Thus both endpoints strictly improve exactly when both finite systems are infeasible.

By \(\text{lem:finite-incidence-duality-continuity}\), all four value functions are continuous on their entire relative domains.  Since \(K_{\mathrm{blind}}\) is linear, the two gap functions
\[
g_L(p)=L(p)-L_{\mathrm{blind}}(K_{\mathrm{blind}}p),
\qquad
g_U(p)=U_{\mathrm{blind}}(K_{\mathrm{blind}}p)-U(p)
\tag{13}
\]
are continuous on \(\mathfrak O\).  Hence
\[
\mathcal S_{\mathrm{strict}}=g_L^{-1}((0,\infty))\cap g_U^{-1}((0,\infty))
\tag{14}
\]
is relatively open in \(\mathfrak O\).  This proves openness without restricting to relative-interior points.

It remains to prove nonemptiness from the primitive bracket condition.  Fix \(s_*\), \(q_\ell<q_m<q_h\) supplied by \(\mathsf{Bracket}_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\).  Start with a row of \(\text{def:complete-32-type-table}\).  At the three distinguished performance values retain its outcomes.  If \(z=0\), set the neutral outcome equal to one at every performance value and extend the nonincreasing \(r=1\) triple by
\[
Y_{(1,q)}=
\begin{cases}
Y_{(1,q_\ell)},&q<q_m,\\
Y_{(1,q_m)},&q=q_m,\\
Y_{(1,q_h)},&q>q_m.
\end{cases}
\tag{15}
\]
If \(z=1\), set the neutral outcome equal to zero and use the same formula, now for the nondecreasing triple.  Formula (15) is monotone in the required direction, agrees at \(q_\ell,q_m,q_h\), and its values lie below the neutral value when \(z=0\) and above the neutral value when \(z=1\).  Thus correctness, both performance orders, and neutral invariance hold at every additional \(q\).  Retain the two endpoint action bits from the row and set actions at unused configurations arbitrarily, for example to zero.  This extends every one of the thirty-two quotient representatives to a legal response type for the general design.

Conversely, restriction of any legal response type with \(S=s_*\) to \(q_\ell,q_m,q_h\) has one of the four binary monotone outcome paths appearing in the table.  Its endpoint action pair is one of the four displayed pairs.  By \(\text{thm:complete-32-type-refinement-certificate}\), it therefore has the same trial-observable column and the same middle-performance target as one of the thirty-two representatives.  Additional performance values and configurations away from \(s_*\) create only duplicate observable--target columns.

Now put the weights \(w^{32}(\pi^{32,\circ})\) on any fixed legal extensions of the thirty-two rows and put zero mass outside \(s_*\).  For a trial arm \(j\) using endpoint \(d\in\{\ell,h\}\), each expanded incidence entry is \(\rho_j\) times the corresponding conditional-cell indicator, whereas the canonical table entry is one half of that indicator.  Hence the expanded block is obtained from the canonical endpoint block by multiplication by \(2\rho_j>0\), with one copy for every duplicate arm.  Because at least one positive-probability arm uses each endpoint, selecting one such block of each kind and dividing by \(2\rho_j\) is a left inverse.  Equality of two expanded full laws is consequently equivalent to equality of their canonical full laws.  The same argument after summing the two revealed-label cells proves the corresponding equivalence for the blind laws.  Moreover, because every \(\rho_j>0\), an expanded law with zero probability outside \(s_*\) can be generated only by response types with \(S=s_*\).  Thus neither other strata nor duplicated/rescaled arms enlarge either relevant fiber.

The four general endpoint programs at the constructed law are therefore identical, after quotienting duplicate columns, to the four canonical programs at \(p^{32,\circ}\).  The complete certificate gives
\[
L-L_{\mathrm{blind}}=\frac{397-198}{800}=\frac{199}{800},
\qquad
U_{\mathrm{blind}}-U=1-\frac{601}{800}=\frac{199}{800}.
\tag{16}
\]
Call this expanded law \(p_0\).  This proves \(p_0\in\mathcal S_{\mathrm{strict}}\), without restricting any other stratum or any unused configuration.

Finally, let \(u\in\Delta_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\) assign strictly positive mass to every legal response type; it exists because the type set is finite and nonempty.  For \(0<\delta<1\), put
\[
w_\delta=(1-\delta)w_0+\delta u,
\qquad p_\delta=Bw_\delta.
\tag{17}
\]
Every coordinate of \(w_\delta\) is positive.  Therefore \(p_\delta\in\operatorname{relint}(\mathfrak O)\): if an affine supporting functional of \(\mathfrak O=\operatorname{conv}\{b_t\}\) is nonnegative on every generator and vanishes at \(p_\delta=\sum_t(w_\delta)_tb_t\), strict positivity forces it to vanish on every generator, so it cannot define a proper supporting face containing \(p_\delta\).  By (13), (16), and continuity, \(g_L(p_\delta)>0\) and \(g_U(p_\delta)>0\) for every sufficiently small \(\delta>0\).  Hence
\[
p_\delta\in\mathcal S_{\mathrm{strict}}\cap\operatorname{relint}(\mathfrak O).
\tag{18}
\]
The intersection of the relatively open set (14) with \(\operatorname{relint}(\mathfrak O)\) is a nonempty relatively open region of \(\mathfrak O\), completing the proof.
