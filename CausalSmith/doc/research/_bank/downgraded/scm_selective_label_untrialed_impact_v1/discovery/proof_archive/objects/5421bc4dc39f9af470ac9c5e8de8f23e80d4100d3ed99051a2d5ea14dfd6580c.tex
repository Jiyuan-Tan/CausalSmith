Let \(M\in\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\), and define the finite random response type
\[
T=\bigl(S,Z,(A_c)_{c\in\mathcal C},(Y_c)_{c\in\mathcal C}\bigr),
\qquad
w_t=\Pr_M(T=t).
\]
The counterfactual-correctness, correct-performance-order, incorrect-performance-order, and neutral-performance-invariance assumptions are exactly the four restrictions in \(\text{def:legal-response-types}\).  Hence \(T\in\mathcal T_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\) almost surely.  Therefore \(w_t\geq0\) for every legal type and \(\sum_t w_t=1\), so \(w\in\Delta_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\) by \(\text{def:response-simplex}\).

Fix an observable cell \(o=(j,s,a,y,\ell)\in\mathcal O\).  Randomized policy assignment and the known arm law give
\[
\Pr_M(D=j,T=t)=\Pr_M(D=j)\Pr_M(T=t)=\rho_jw_t.
\tag{1}
\]
Conditional on \(D=j\) and \(T=t\), the common-action and common-outcome assumptions give, on \(S=s\),
\[
A=a_{c_j(s)}(t),
\qquad
Y=y_{c_j(s)}(t).
\tag{2}
\]
The reveal-action and masked-truth assumptions further give \(\ell=z_t\) when \(a=1\) and \(\ell=\star\) when \(a=0\).  Partitioning by the finite values of \(T\), and then using (1), (2), and \(\text{def:selective-label-incidence-map}\), yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr_M(O_i=o)
&=\sum_{t\in\mathcal T_{\mathrm{SLCC}}}
 \rho_jw_t\mathbf 1\!\left\{s_t=s,\ a_{c_j(s)}(t)=a,\ y_{c_j(s)}(t)=y,
 \ \ell=z_t\text{ if }a=1,\ \ell=\star\text{ if }a=0\right\}\\
&=\sum_{t\in\mathcal T_{\mathrm{SLCC}}}B_{o,t}w_t.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
Thus the observable law is \(p=Bw\).  Under intervention to policy \(e\), the common-outcome response gives the causal target
\[
\tau_e(M)=\mathbb E_M[Y_{c_e(S)}]
=\sum_{t\in\mathcal T_{\mathrm{SLCC}}}w_t y_{c_e(s_t)}(t)
=h_e^{\top}w.
\tag{4}
\]
This proves the forward inclusion.

Conversely, fix \(w\in\Delta_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\).  On a finite probability space draw \(T=t\) with probability \(w_t\), independently draw \(D=j\) with probability \(\rho_j\), and define \(S,Z,(A_c),(Y_c)\) by the coordinates of \(T\).  Under policy \(j\), set
\[
A=A_{c_j(S)},
\qquad
Y=Y_{c_j(S)},
\qquad
R=A,
\]
and set \(Z_{\mathrm{obs}}=Z\) on \(R=1\) and \(Z_{\mathrm{obs}}=\star\) on \(R=0\).  Independence of the two draws proves randomized assignment, and the marginal law of \(D\) proves the known arm law.  The displayed structural equations prove both common-response assumptions, reveal-action, and masking.  Every type receiving positive mass belongs to \(\mathcal T_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\), so the four unit-level outcome restrictions hold pointwise.  Therefore the constructed model \(M_w\) belongs to \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\).  Repeating (3)--(4) gives observable law \(Bw\) and causal target \(h_e^{\top}w\).  This proves the converse inclusion and hence the claimed equality of observable--target images.
