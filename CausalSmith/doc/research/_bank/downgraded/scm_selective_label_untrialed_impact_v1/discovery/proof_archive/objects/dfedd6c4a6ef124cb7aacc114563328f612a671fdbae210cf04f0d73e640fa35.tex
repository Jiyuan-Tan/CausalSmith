Fix \(p\in\mathfrak O\).  By \(\text{lem:finite-incidence-duality-continuity}\), the full lower dual optimum is attained on \(\Lambda_L(p)\), and the blind lower dual is
\[
\max_{\alpha,\mu}\left\{\alpha+\mu^{\top}K_{\mathrm{blind}}p:
\alpha\mathbf 1+B^{\top}K_{\mathrm{blind}}^{\top}\mu\leq h_e\right\}.
\tag{37}
\]
Under the substitution \(\lambda=K_{\mathrm{blind}}^{\top}\mu\), its feasible set is exactly \(\mathcal D_L\cap\mathcal E_K\), and its objective is \(\alpha+\lambda^{\top}p\).  Thus (37) is the maximum of the full lower-dual objective over the embedded blind subset.  Attainment of both finite programs gives
\[
L_{\mathrm{blind}}(K_{\mathrm{blind}}p)=L(p)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\Lambda_L(p)\cap\mathcal E_K\neq\varnothing.
\tag{38}
\]
By \(\text{lem:z-blind-coarsening}\), the blind lower endpoint is no larger than the full lower endpoint.  Negating equality in this ordered pair proves
\[
L(p)>L_{\mathrm{blind}}(K_{\mathrm{blind}}p)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\Lambda_L(p)\cap\mathcal E_K=\varnothing.
\tag{39}
\]

Likewise, the blind upper dual is
\[
\min_{\alpha,\mu}\left\{\alpha+\mu^{\top}K_{\mathrm{blind}}p:
\alpha\mathbf 1+B^{\top}K_{\mathrm{blind}}^{\top}\mu\geq h_e\right\},
\tag{40}
\]
the restriction of the full upper dual to \(\mathcal D_U\cap\mathcal E_K\).  Hence
\[
U_{\mathrm{blind}}(K_{\mathrm{blind}}p)=U(p)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\Lambda_U(p)\cap\mathcal E_K\neq\varnothing.
\tag{41}
\]
The upper inclusion from \(\text{lem:z-blind-coarsening}\) then gives
\[
U(p)<U_{\mathrm{blind}}(K_{\mathrm{blind}}p)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\Lambda_U(p)\cap\mathcal E_K=\varnothing.
\tag{42}
\]
Equations (38)--(42) also prove the advertised computable tests: after the finite primal programs produce \(L(p)\) and \(U(p)\), every displayed condition is a finite linear equality or inequality in \((\alpha,\lambda,\mu)\).

By \(\text{lem:finite-incidence-duality-continuity}\), the four endpoint functions are continuous on their relative domains.  Since \(K_{\mathrm{blind}}\) is linear, the gap functions
\[
g_L(p)=L(p)-L_{\mathrm{blind}}(K_{\mathrm{blind}}p),
\qquad
g_U(p)=U_{\mathrm{blind}}(K_{\mathrm{blind}}p)-U(p)
\tag{43}
\]
are continuous on \(\mathfrak O\).  Therefore
\[
\mathcal S_{\mathrm{strict}}
=g_L^{-1}((0,\infty))\cap g_U^{-1}((0,\infty))
\tag{44}
\]
is relatively open in \(\mathfrak O\), including at relative-boundary laws.

It remains to establish nonemptiness under \(\mathsf{Bracket}_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\).  Fix \(s_*\in\mathcal S\) and \(q_\ell<q_m<q_h\) supplied by \(\text{def:bracket-stratum-design}\).  Extend each row of \(\text{def:complete-32-type-table}\) to every additional performance value as follows.  If \(z=0\), set the neutral outcome to one for every \(q\) and extend its nonincreasing \(r=1\) path by
\[
Y_{(1,q)}=
\begin{cases}
Y_{(1,q_\ell)},&q<q_m,\\
Y_{(1,q_m)},&q=q_m,\\
Y_{(1,q_h)},&q>q_m.
\end{cases}
\tag{45}
\]
If \(z=1\), set the neutral outcome to zero for every \(q\) and use (45) for the nondecreasing \(r=1\) path.  Formula (45) agrees at the three distinguished values, is monotone in the required direction, and stays below the neutral value when \(z=0\) and above it when \(z=1\).  Thus correctness, both performance orders, and neutral invariance hold at every additional \(q\).

Retain the two endpoint action bits.  Fix arbitrarily, say at zero, every action coordinate not read by the trial incidence map at \(s_*\).  This explicitly includes \(A_{(1,q_m)}\): deployment uses it under \(e\), but \(e\notin J_0\), so no randomized trial row reads it, and the target \(h_e\) reads only \(Y_{(1,q_m)}\).  It also includes action coordinates at all other nontrialed configurations.  The legal-type definition places no cross-coordinate restriction on these actions, so the extension remains legal.  This constructs legal general-design extensions of all thirty-two displayed rows.

Conversely, restriction of any legal response type with \(S=s_*\) to \(q_\ell,q_m,q_h\) has one of the four binary monotone three-point paths covered by \(\text{thm:complete-32-type-refinement-certificate}\), after replacing the unread neutral constant by its canonical displayed representative when necessary.  Its endpoint action pair is one of the four binary pairs; all action coordinates not read by the trial incidence map, including \(A_{(1,q_m)}\), may be fixed without changing the trial law or the outcome-only target.  By that theorem's complete quotient-generating cover, it therefore shares its trial-observable column and middle target with a displayed representative.  Additional performance values and off-stratum configurations do not create new observable--target columns in a law supported on \(s_*\).

Put weights \(w^{32}(\pi^{32,\circ})\) on fixed legal extensions of the thirty-two rows and zero mass outside \(s_*\).  A trial arm \(j\) using endpoint \(d\in\{\ell,h\}\) contributes \(\rho_j\) times its conditional-cell indicator, while the canonical table contributes one half of that indicator.  Thus its expanded block is the canonical endpoint block multiplied by \(2\rho_j>0\).  Because the bracket condition supplies at least one arm of each endpoint kind and every \(\rho_j>0\), selecting one block of each kind and dividing by \(2\rho_j\) is a left inverse.  Consequently, equality of expanded full laws is equivalent to equality of their canonical full laws; the same statement holds after the revealed-label cells are summed by the blind map.  Moreover, an expanded law having zero probability outside \(s_*\) cannot be generated by any positive weight on a response type with a different stratum, because every trial arm has positive probability.  Hence other strata and duplicated or rescaled endpoint arms enlarge neither relevant fiber.

The four endpoint programs at the constructed law are therefore the canonical programs at \(p^{32,\circ}\), after quotienting duplicate columns.  The complete certificate gives
\[
L-L_{\mathrm{blind}}=\frac{397-198}{800}=\frac{199}{800},
\qquad
U_{\mathrm{blind}}-U=1-\frac{601}{800}=\frac{199}{800}.
\tag{46}
\]
Call the expanded law \(p_0\).  Equation (46) proves \(p_0\in\mathcal S_{\mathrm{strict}}\) without a restriction on other strata or on configurations away from \(s_*\).

Because \(\mathcal T_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\) is finite and nonempty, choose \(u\in\Delta_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\) with \(u_t>0\) for every legal response type.  For \(0<\delta<1\), let
\[
w_\delta=(1-\delta)w_0+\delta u,
\qquad
p_\delta=Bw_\delta.
\tag{47}
\]
Every coordinate of \(w_\delta\) is positive.  If a supporting affine functional of \(\mathfrak O=\operatorname{conv}\{B_{\cdot,t}:t\in\mathcal T_{\mathrm{SLCC}}\}\) is nonnegative on every generator and vanishes at \(p_\delta\), strict positivity in (47) forces it to vanish on every generator.  Hence \(p_\delta\in\operatorname{relint}(\mathfrak O)\).  By continuity in (43), the two gaps remain positive for every sufficiently small \(\delta>0\), since both equal \(199/800\) at \(\delta=0\).  Thus
\[
p_\delta\in\mathcal S_{\mathrm{strict}}\cap\operatorname{relint}(\mathfrak O).
\tag{48}
\]
By the definition of relative interior as interior in the affine hull, \(\operatorname{relint}(\mathfrak O)\) is relatively open in \(\mathfrak O\).  Hence (44) shows that \(\mathcal S_{\mathrm{strict}}\cap\operatorname{relint}(\mathfrak O)\) is relatively open in \(\mathfrak O\), and (48) shows that it is nonempty.  The block-rescaling and extension arguments above also establish the claimed robustness to duplicate endpoint arms, arbitrary positive randomization probabilities, extra performance values, other strata, and off-stratum configurations.
