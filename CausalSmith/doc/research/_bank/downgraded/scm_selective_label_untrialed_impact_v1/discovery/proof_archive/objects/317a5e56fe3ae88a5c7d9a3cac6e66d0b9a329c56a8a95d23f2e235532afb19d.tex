Fix \(M\in\mathcal A_p\) and abbreviate \(\Omega=\Omega_M\) and \(r=r_M\).  The categorical summand \(W=M(\mathbf e_{O_1}-p)\) is centered and satisfies
\[
\mathbb E_p[WW^{\top}]=M\{\operatorname{diag}(p)-pp^{\top}\}M^{\top}=\Omega.
\tag{1}
\]
If \(r=0\), then \(\Omega=0\), because it is positive semidefinite, and
\[
\mathbb E_p\|W\|_2^2=\operatorname{tr}(\Omega)=0.
\]
Thus \(W=0\) almost surely.  The centered Gaussian vector \(MG_p\) also has covariance zero and is zero almost surely, so the lower-orthant approximation error for this \(M\) equals zero.

Suppose \(r>0\).  Choose a matrix \(Q\in\mathbb R^{\operatorname{rows}(M)\times r}\) whose columns form an orthonormal basis of \(\operatorname{range}(\Omega)\), and set
\[
\overline\Omega=Q^{\top}\Omega Q.
\]
The matrix \(\overline\Omega\) is positive definite: if \(u\neq0\), then \(Qu\) is a nonzero vector in \(\operatorname{range}(\Omega)\), which is orthogonal to \(\ker(\Omega)\), and hence
\[
u^{\top}\overline\Omega u=(Qu)^{\top}\Omega(Qu)>0.
\tag{2}
\]
For the independent categorical observations define
\[
\xi_i=m^{-1/2}\overline\Omega^{-1/2}Q^{\top}M(\mathbf e_{O_i}-p),
\qquad i=1,\ldots,m.
\]
Equations (1)--(2) give
\[
\mathbb E_p\xi_i=0,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^m\mathbb E_p[\xi_i\xi_i^{\top}]
=\overline\Omega^{-1/2}Q^{\top}\Omega Q\overline\Omega^{-1/2}=I_r.
\tag{3}
\]

If \(a\in\ker(\Omega)\), then \(\mathbb E_p(a^{\top}W)^2=a^{\top}\Omega a=0\), so \(a^{\top}W=0\) almost surely.  Therefore both \(MX_m\) and \(MG_p\) lie in \(\operatorname{range}(\Omega)\) almost surely.  For every lower orthant \(( -\infty,x]\) in the original row space, its inverse image under the injective linear map
\[
u\longmapsto Q\overline\Omega^{1/2}u
\]
is the convex set
\[
K_x=\{u\in\mathbb R^r:Q\overline\Omega^{1/2}u\leq x\}.
\tag{4}
\]
Applying \(\text{lem:raic-multivariate-berry-esseen-convex-sets}\) to (3) and (4) gives
\[
\sup_x\left|\Pr_p\{MX_m\leq x\}-\Pr\{MG_p\leq x\}\right|
\leq(42r^{1/4}+16)\sum_{i=1}^m\mathbb E_p\|\xi_i\|_2^3.
\tag{5}
\]
Because the \(m\) summands are identically distributed and carry the factor \(m^{-1/2}\),
\[
\sum_{i=1}^m\mathbb E_p\|\xi_i\|_2^3
=m^{-1/2}\sum_{o\in\mathcal O}p_o
\left\|\overline\Omega^{-1/2}Q^{\top}M(\mathbf e_o-p)\right\|_2^3.
\tag{6}
\]
On \(\operatorname{range}(\Omega)\), the Moore--Penrose inverse square root is
\[
\Omega^{\dagger/2}=Q\overline\Omega^{-1/2}Q^{\top}.
\]
Since \(M(\mathbf e_o-p)\in\operatorname{range}(\Omega)\) for every \(o\) with \(p_o>0\), and since \(Q\) is an isometry, the norm in (6) equals
\[
\left\|\Omega^{\dagger/2}M(\mathbf e_o-p)\right\|_2.
\tag{7}
\]
Equations (5)--(7), followed by the maximum over the finite family \(\mathcal A_p\), prove
\[
\delta_m(p)\leq C(p)m^{-1/2}.
\tag{8}
\]
Every term defining \(C(p)\) is finite for a fixed finite matrix and a fixed \(p\), and \(\mathcal A_p\) is finite, so \(C(p)<\infty\).  This argument also displays why the conclusion is not uniform near a covariance-rank change: the nonzero eigenvalues inverted in \(\Omega^{\dagger/2}\) may approach zero.

It remains to verify the announced schedule.  For all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
\tfrac12n^{1/3}\leq m_n\leq n^{1/3},
\qquad
\eta_n=n^{-1/12}.
\]
Consequently (8) and elementary exponent arithmetic yield
\[
\delta_{m_n}(p)=O(n^{-1/6}),\qquad
m_n/\sqrt n=O(n^{-1/6}),\qquad
m_n^2/n=O(n^{-1/3}).
\tag{9}
\]
After division by \(\eta_n=n^{-1/12}\), the three orders in (9) become respectively \(O(n^{-1/12})\), \(O(n^{-1/12})\), and \(O(n^{-1/4})\), all tending to zero.  Finally, \(m_n\geq n^{1/3}/2\) eventually, so
\[
e^{-cm_n}\leq e^{-(c/2)n^{1/3}}=o(n^{-1/12})=o(\eta_n).
\]
This proves every claimed comparison.
