Fix \(M\in\mathcal A_p\), abbreviate \(\Omega=\Omega_M\), and put \(r=r_M\).  The categorical summand
\[
W=M(\mathbf e_{O_1}-p)
\]
is centered and has covariance
\[
\mathbb E_p[WW^{\top}]
=M\{\operatorname{diag}(p)-pp^{\top}\}M^{\top}=\Omega.
\tag{1}
\]
If \(r=0\), positive semidefiniteness gives \(\Omega=0\), and
\[
\mathbb E_p\|W\|_2^2=\operatorname{tr}(\Omega)=0.
\]
Thus \(W=0\) almost surely.  The Gaussian vector \(MG_p\) also has covariance zero and is zero almost surely, so the lower-orthant approximation error for this \(M\) is zero.

Suppose \(r>0\).  Choose \(Q\in\mathbb R^{\operatorname{rows}(M)\times r}\) whose columns form an orthonormal basis of \(\operatorname{range}(\Omega)\), and define
\[
\overline\Omega=Q^{\top}\Omega Q.
\]
This matrix is positive definite.  Indeed, if \(u\neq0\), then \(Qu\) is a nonzero vector in \(\operatorname{range}(\Omega)=\ker(\Omega)^{\perp}\), so
\[
u^{\top}\overline\Omega u=(Qu)^{\top}\Omega(Qu)>0.
\tag{2}
\]
For an independent size-\(m\) categorical sample, set
\[
\xi_i=m^{-1/2}\overline\Omega^{-1/2}Q^{\top}M(\mathbf e_{O_i}-p),
\qquad i=1,\ldots,m.
\]
Equations (1)--(2) give
\[
\mathbb E_p\xi_i=0,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^m\mathbb E_p[\xi_i\xi_i^{\top}]
=\overline\Omega^{-1/2}Q^{\top}\Omega Q\overline\Omega^{-1/2}=I_r.
\tag{3}
\]

If \(a\in\ker(\Omega)\), then
\[
0=a^{\top}\Omega a=\sum_{o\in\mathcal O}p_o
\{a^{\top}M(\mathbf e_o-p)\}^2.
\]
Therefore \(a^{\top}M(\mathbf e_o-p)=0\) for every \(o\) with \(p_o>0\).  Hence the summands, \(MX_m\), and \(MG_p\) lie in \(\operatorname{range}(\Omega)\) almost surely.  For every lower orthant \(( -\infty,x]\) in the original row space, its inverse image under the injective map \(u\mapsto Q\overline\Omega^{1/2}u\) is the convex set
\[
K_x=\{u\in\mathbb R^r:Q\overline\Omega^{1/2}u\leq x\}.
\tag{4}
\]
Apply \(\text{lem:raic-multivariate-berry-esseen-convex-sets}\) to (3)--(4).  It gives
\[
\sup_x\left|\Pr_p\{MX_m\leq x\}-\Pr\{MG_p\leq x\}\right|
\leq(42r^{1/4}+16)\sum_{i=1}^m\mathbb E_p\|\xi_i\|_2^3.
\tag{5}
\]
Since the \(m\) summands are identically distributed and contain the factor \(m^{-1/2}\),
\[
\sum_{i=1}^m\mathbb E_p\|\xi_i\|_2^3
=m^{-1/2}\sum_{o\in\mathcal O}p_o
\left\|\overline\Omega^{-1/2}Q^{\top}M(\mathbf e_o-p)\right\|_2^3.
\tag{6}
\]
On \(\operatorname{range}(\Omega)\),
\[
\Omega^{\dagger/2}=Q\overline\Omega^{-1/2}Q^{\top}.
\]
Because every positive-probability vector \(M(\mathbf e_o-p)\) lies in this range and \(Q\) is an isometry, the norm in (6) equals
\[
\left\|\Omega^{\dagger/2}M(\mathbf e_o-p)\right\|_2.
\tag{7}
\]
Equations (5)--(7), followed by the maximum over the finite family \(\mathcal A_p\), prove
\[
\delta_m(p)\leq C(p)m^{-1/2}.
\tag{8}
\]
Every term defining \(C(p)\) is finite for fixed \(p\) and fixed \(M\), and the family is finite, so \(C(p)<\infty\).  The use of \(\Omega^{\dagger/2}\) also shows why this constant need not be uniform near covariance-rank changes.

For all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
\frac12n^{1/3}\leq m_n\leq n^{1/3},
\qquad
\eta_n=n^{-1/12}.
\]
Consequently (8) and exponent arithmetic give
\[
\delta_{m_n}(p)=O(n^{-1/6}),
\qquad
\frac{m_n}{\sqrt n}=O(n^{-1/6}),
\qquad
\frac{m_n^2}{n}=O(n^{-1/3}).
\tag{9}
\]
After division by \(\eta_n=n^{-1/12}\), these orders are \(O(n^{-1/12})\), \(O(n^{-1/12})\), and \(O(n^{-1/4})\), respectively, and hence vanish.  Finally, \(m_n\geq n^{1/3}/2\) eventually, so for fixed \(c>0\),
\[
e^{-cm_n}\leq e^{-(c/2)n^{1/3}}=o(n^{-1/12})=o(\eta_n).
\]
This proves the effective-range bound and every explicit schedule comparison.
