Let \(d=|\mathcal O|\), let \(\Phi=\theta\circ\Pi_{\mathfrak O}\), and put
\[
X_n=\sqrt n(\widehat p-p),\qquad
T_n=\max\{\sqrt n(\widehat L-L(p)),\sqrt n(U(p)-\widehat U)\}.
\]
By \(\text{lem:finite-multinomial-clt}\), whose sampling premise is \(\text{ass:iid-finite-sampling}\),
\[
X_n\rightsquigarrow G_p,
\qquad X_n=O_p(1).
\tag{1}
\]
Moreover \(X_n\in V_p\) almost surely: its coordinates sum to zero, and \(p_o=0\) implies \(N_o=0\) almost surely.  By local exactness in condition (i), there is \(r>0\) such that
\[
\Phi(p+x)-\Phi(p)=(D_L(x),D_U(x))
\quad\text{for every }x\in V_p\text{ with }\|x\|_2<r.
\tag{2}
\]
Each directional coordinate is continuous, positively homogeneous, and linear on every member of a finite fan.  Hence, for some finite \(C_D\),
\[
|D_L(x)|\vee|D_U(x)|\leq C_D\|x\|_2,
\qquad x\in V_p.
\tag{3}
\]
Equations (1)--(3) and positive homogeneity give
\[
T_n=H(X_n)
\tag{4}
\]
with probability tending to one.  Since \(H\) is continuous, (1) also gives \(H(X_n)\rightsquigarrow H(G_p)\).

We next compare the conditional subsampling distribution with an independent size-\(m\) experiment.  Write \(m=m_n\), let \(\Pr^*\) denote probability conditional on the full size-\(n\) sample, let \(\widehat p_b\) denote the empirical law in a uniformly chosen size-\(m\) subsample without replacement, and define
\[
a_n=\sqrt m(\widehat p-p)=\sqrt{m/n}\,X_n.
\tag{5}
\]
When both empirical laws lie in the neighborhood in (2), the centered statistic whose conditional quantile defines \(c^{\mathrm{sub}}_{n,1-\alpha+\eta_n}\) is exactly
\[
S_{n,b}=\max\left\{
D_L\bigl(\sqrt m(\widehat p_b-p)\bigr)-D_L(a_n),
D_U(a_n)-D_U\bigl(\sqrt m(\widehat p_b-p)\bigr)
\right\}.
\tag{6}
\]
Indeed, (6) follows by applying (2) separately at \(\widehat p_b\) and \(\widehat p\), subtracting, multiplying by \(\sqrt m\), and using positive homogeneity.

Couple the ordered indices sampled without replacement to \(m\) independent indices sampled with replacement from \(\{1,\ldots,n\}\).  A coupling failure requires a repeated with-replacement index, so its conditional probability is at most
\[
\frac{m(m-1)}{2n}.
\tag{7}
\]
For each with-replacement draw, couple the empirical categorical law \(\widehat p\) and the population law \(p\) by assigning matching category \(o\) probability \(\min\{\widehat p_o,p_o\}\) and distributing the two residual masses over unmatched categories.  Its mismatch probability is
\[
\operatorname{TV}(\widehat p,p)
=\frac12\|\widehat p-p\|_1.
\]
Thus, by the union bound, all \(m\) categories agree except on an event of conditional probability at most
\[
m\operatorname{TV}(\widehat p,p)
=\frac m2\|\widehat p-p\|_1.
\tag{8}
\]
On \(\{\|X_n\|_2\leq M\}\), the last display is at most \(m\sqrt d M/(2\sqrt n)\).  On the successful coupling event,
\[
\sqrt m(\widehat p_b-p)=X_m
\]
for the independent-
\(p\) vector in the statement, and the comparison variable is therefore
\[
K_{a_n}(X_m)
=\max\{D_L(X_m)-D_L(a_n),D_U(a_n)-D_U(X_m)\}.
\tag{9}
\]

It remains to bound failure of the local representation (2).  Each coordinate of \(\mathbf e_{O_i}-p\) is centered and bounded.  Expanding its moment generating function and using \(e^v\leq1+v+C v^2\) on a fixed bounded interval yields, for all sufficiently small \(|u|\),
\[
\mathbb E_p\exp\{u(\mathbf 1\{O_i=o\}-p_o)\}\leq\exp(C_1u^2).
\]
Independence, exponential Markov inequality, and a union bound over the \(d\) coordinates therefore give constants \(C_2,c_2>0\) such that
\[
\Pr_p\{\|X_m\|_2>r\sqrt m/2\}\leq C_2e^{-c_2m}.
\tag{10}
\]
For fixed \(M\) and all sufficiently large \(n\), \(\|X_n\|_2\leq M\) puts \(\widehat p\) in the radius-
\(r/2\) neighborhood.  Equations (7)--(10) then bound the conditional total-variation error between \(S_{n,b}\) and \(K_{a_n}(X_m)\), on \(\{\|X_n\|_2\leq M\}\), by
\[
q_{n,M}:=\frac{m(m-1)}{2n}+\frac{m\sqrt d M}{2\sqrt n}+C_2e^{-c_2m}.
\tag{11}
\]

We now prove that the multinomial approximation number in the statement vanishes.  For each \(A\in\mathcal A_p\), (1), with \(n\) replaced by \(m\), gives
\[
AX_m\rightsquigarrow AG_p,
\tag{12}
\]
because \(x\mapsto f(Ax)\) is bounded and continuous whenever \(f\) is bounded and continuous, so this is an immediate application of the definition of weak convergence.
Delete identically zero rows from \(A\).  By condition (ii) and the definition of \(\mathcal A_p\), every remaining row is nonzero on \(V_p\).  Nondegeneracy of \(G_p\) on \(V_p\) then gives every remaining marginal of \(AG_p\) strictly positive variance.  Consequently the boundary of each lower orthant has \(AG_p\)-probability zero.  Pointwise convergence of the corresponding distribution functions follows from (12).  A finite grid on a sufficiently large compact box, monotonicity between grid points, and tightness outside that box upgrade pointwise convergence to uniform convergence over lower orthants.  Since \(\mathcal A_p\) is finite,
\[
\delta_m(p)\longrightarrow0.
\tag{13}
\]

On each pair of fan cones containing \(a_n\) and the argument \(g\), the event \(K_{a_n}(g)\leq x\) is an intersection of inequalities whose normals are endpoint slopes and fan-facet normals.  Taking the finite union over cone pairs and using the closure of \(\mathcal A_p\) under row selection and sign changes expresses this event as a finite Boolean combination of lower orthants governed by matrices in \(\mathcal A_p\).  Inclusion--exclusion and (13) therefore compare \(K_{a_n}(X_m)\) with \(K_{a_n}(G_p)\), uniformly over those finitely many pieces.  Furthermore, (3) gives the deterministic bound
\[
|K_{a_n}(g)-H(g)|
\leq |D_L(a_n)|\vee|D_U(a_n)|
\leq C_D\|a_n\|_2.
\tag{14}
\]
On \(\{\|X_n\|_2\leq M\}\), equations (5), (11), (13), and (14), together with \(m^2/n\to0\), imply at every continuity point \(x\) of the distribution of \(H(G_p)\) that
\[
\Pr^*\{S_{n,b}\leq x\}\longrightarrow
\Pr\{H(G_p)\leq x\}
\quad\text{in probability}.
\tag{15}
\]
Since \(X_n=O_p(1)\), the restriction to bounded \(X_n\) may be removed after taking \(M\to\infty\).

Let
\[
F(x)=\Pr\{H(G_p)\leq x\}.
\]
On the interior of each full-dimensional cone of the fan refined by the endpoint-comparison and zero hyperplanes, \(H\) is one linear functional.  Proper fan boundaries are contained in finitely many hyperplanes with normals nonzero on \(V_p\), so condition (ii) and Gaussian nondegeneracy give them probability zero.  A nonzero linear functional of \(G_p\) has a continuous law; a zero linear functional is identically zero.  Because every linear piece is homogeneous and has zero intercept, it follows that
\[
F\text{ can have an atom only at }0.
\tag{16}
\]

Put \(c_n=c^{\mathrm{sub}}_{n,\beta+\eta_n}\) and \(q_\beta=\inf\{x:F(x)\geq\beta\}\).  First suppose \(q_\beta\neq0\).  For every sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\), the nonzero number \(x_\varepsilon=q_\beta-\varepsilon\) satisfies \(F(x_\varepsilon)<\beta\).  By (15) and \(\eta_n\to0\),
\[
\Pr\{c_n>x_\varepsilon\}\longrightarrow1.
\]
Equations (4), (16), and weak convergence give
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\Pr\{T_n\leq c_n\}
\geq F(x_\varepsilon).
\]
Letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), continuity at the nonzero quantile yields
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\Pr\{T_n\leq c_n\}
\geq F(q_\beta)\geq\beta.
\tag{17}
\]

It remains to treat \(q_\beta=0\), for which
\[
F(0-)\leq\beta\leq F(0).
\tag{18}
\]
The only delicate case is an observation for which \(H(X_n)=0\).  Fix such a realization \(z=X_n\).  Since \(H(z)=\max\{D_L(z),-D_U(z)\}=0\), both entries in the maximum are nonpositive.  Positive homogeneity and (5) therefore give
\[
D_L(a_n)=\sqrt{m/n}\,D_L(z)\leq0,
\qquad
D_U(a_n)=\sqrt{m/n}\,D_U(z)\geq0.
\tag{19}
\]
For every \(g\in V_p\), (19) implies the global sign preservation
\[
\begin{aligned}
K_{a_n}(g)<0
&\ \Longrightarrow\ D_L(g)<D_L(a_n)\leq0
\text{ and }D_U(g)>D_U(a_n)\geq0\\
&\ \Longrightarrow\ H(g)=\max\{D_L(g),-D_U(g)\}<0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]
This implication uses neither a choice of active cone nor uniqueness of an endpoint slope.

The event \(\{H(g)<0\}\) is a finite union of intersections of strict linear inequalities from the refined fan.  Approximating strict thresholds from below, using inclusion--exclusion, and applying the definition of \(\delta_m(p)\) gives a finite constant \(C_F\), depending only on the fixed fan, such that
\[
\Pr_p\{H(X_m)<0\}\leq F(0-)+C_F\delta_m(p).
\tag{21}
\]
Combining (11), (20), and (21), uniformly on \(\{\|X_n\|_2\leq M,H(X_n)=0\}\), gives
\[
\Pr^*\{S_{n,b}<0\}
\leq F(0-)+C_F\delta_m(p)
+\frac{m(m-1)}{2n}+\frac{m\sqrt d M}{2\sqrt n}+C_2e^{-c_2m}.
\tag{22}
\]

When \(F(0-)=\beta\), we need the exponential remainder in (22) to be negligible relative to \(\eta_n\).  Here \(\beta>0\), so \(H(G_p)<0\) with positive probability.  Hence at least one endpoint slope \(\ell\), nonzero on \(V_p\), occurs as a row in \(\mathcal A_p\).  The scalar \(\ell^{\top}X_m\) has at most \((m+1)^d\) support points and therefore has a jump of size at least \((m+1)^{-d}\), whereas \(\ell^{\top}G_p\) is continuous by nondegeneracy.  Comparing the two distribution functions immediately before and at that jump proves
\[
\delta_m(p)\geq\frac{1}{2(m+1)^d}.
\tag{23}
\]
Thus \(e^{-c_2m}=o(\delta_m(p))\).  Define
\[
R_n=\delta_{m_n}(p)+\frac{m_n}{\sqrt n}+\frac{m_n^2}{n}.
\]
The slack hypothesis gives \(R_n=o(\eta_n)\).  If \(F(0-)=\beta\), equations (22)--(23) make the right-hand side of (22) \(\beta+o_p(\eta_n)\); if \(F(0-)<\beta\), (13), \(m_n^2/n\to0\), and (22) instead leave a fixed positive gap below \(\beta\).  Since a lower \((\beta+\eta_n)\)-quantile of a finite distribution is nonnegative whenever its mass strictly below zero is less than \(\beta+\eta_n\), and since \(X_n=O_p(1)\), we obtain
\[
\Pr\{H(X_n)=0,\ c_n<0\}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{23a}
\]

We also have \(c_n=o_p(1)\).  For every \(\varepsilon>0\), \(q_\beta=0\) gives \(F(-\varepsilon)<\beta\), so (15) implies \(c_n> -\varepsilon\) with probability tending to one.  Also \(F(\varepsilon)>\beta\): this is immediate if \(F(0)>\beta\); if \(F(0)=\beta\) and \(H\) takes a positive value, continuity, positive homogeneity, and full Gaussian support on \(V_p\) give a positive-probability open set where \(0<H(G_p)\leq\varepsilon\); and if \(H\) never takes a positive value, then \(F(\varepsilon)=1\).  A second application of (15) gives \(c_n\leq\varepsilon\) with probability tending to one.  Therefore
\[
c_n=o_p(1).
\tag{23b}
\]

The zero set \(Z_H=\{g\in V_p:H(g)=0\}\) is a finite union of interiors of full-dimensional cones on which the selected linear functional is zero, together with subsets of finitely many zero and fan-facet hyperplanes.  Its boundary is therefore contained in a finite union of hyperplanes whose normals are nonzero on \(V_p\).  Gaussian nondegeneracy gives \(\Pr\{G_p\in\partial Z_H\}=0\).  Continuous functions that equal one on closed subsets of \(Z_H\), vanish outside successively smaller neighborhoods of \(Z_H\), and differ only within successively smaller neighborhoods of \(\partial Z_H\) squeeze its indicator.  Applying (1) to these functions and then shrinking the neighborhoods gives
\[
\Pr\{H(X_n)=0\}\longrightarrow
\Pr\{H(G_p)=0\}=F(0)-F(0-).
\tag{23c}
\]
For every \(\varepsilon>0\), equations (4), (23a), and (23b) cover, with probability tending to one, both the observations satisfying \(H(X_n)\leq-\varepsilon\) and those satisfying \(H(X_n)=0\).  Using (16), (23c), and disjointness of these events yields
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\Pr\{T_n\leq c_n\}
\geq F(-\varepsilon)+F(0)-F(0-).
\]
Letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), the right-hand side tends to \(F(0)\), which is at least \(\beta\) by (18).  Together with (17), this proves
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\Pr\{T_n\leq c_n\}\geq1-\alpha.
\tag{23d}
\]
By the definition of \(T_n\), the event in (23d) is exactly
\[
\left\{L(p)\geq\widehat L-c_n/\sqrt n,\quad
U(p)\leq\widehat U+c_n/\sqrt n\right\},
\]
which proves the simultaneous endpoint coverage claim.  Since \(\eta_n\downarrow0\) and \(\alpha>0\), the conditional quantile level \(1-\alpha+\eta_n\) lies in \((0,1)\) for every sufficiently large \(n\), which is all that the limiting assertion requires.

We finally verify the boundary certificate.  By \(\text{thm:complete-32-type-refinement-certificate}\), the thirty-two-row table is the complete observable--target quotient.  Every column of \(B^{32}\) is nonnegative and has one entry \(1/2\) in each trial-arm block.  If a convex combination of columns equals
\[
p_\star=\tfrac12(\mathbf e_1+\mathbf e_7),
\]
every positively weighted column must be supported on rows \(1\) and \(7\).  Inspection of \(\text{def:complete-32-type-table}\) shows that precisely columns \(1\) and \(17\) have this support, that they coincide, and that \(h^{32}_1=h^{32}_{17}=0\).  Hence \(p_\star\) is a vertex, its fiber is the segment joining types \(1\) and \(17\), and every point of that segment is optimal for both endpoint programs with value zero.  The multinomial covariance on the two positive cells gives
\[
G_{p_\star}=\zeta(\mathbf e_1-\mathbf e_7),
\qquad \zeta\sim\mathcal N(0,1/4).
\tag{24}
\]

Suppose \(\zeta>0\).  The table has four high-arm outgoing cells, rows \(8,9,10,12\), reachable while retaining low-arm row \(1\).  To see that high-arm row \(11\) cannot improve the projection, divide candidate tangent coordinates by \(\zeta\), let \(t\geq0\) be the coordinate of row \(11\), and let \(r\geq0\) be the sum of the other four high-arm outgoing coordinates.  Every table column entering row \(11\) also enters low-arm row \(2\) or row \(5\).  Therefore the low-arm outgoing mass is at least \(t\), and the low-arm squared-distance contribution is at least
\[
(1+t)^2+t^2/2.
\]
For fixed \((r,t)\), equal allocation among the other four high-arm cells minimizes their sum of squares, so the high-arm contribution is at least
\[
(1-r-t)^2+r^2/4+t^2.
\]
The sum of these two displays is convex on \(r,t\geq0\); its derivative in \(t\) is \(2r+7t\), so its minimum has \(t=0\), after which minimizing in \(r\) gives \(r=4/5\).  If the four accessible outgoing coordinates are \(x_1,\ldots,x_4\), with \(s=\sum_{k=1}^4x_k\), the remaining normalized objective is
\[
1+(1-s)^2+\sum_{k=1}^4x_k^2.
\]
For fixed \(s\), the identity \(\sum_{k=1}^4(x_k-s/4)^2=\sum_{k=1}^4x_k^2-s^2/4\geq0\) gives \(\sum_kx_k^2\geq s^2/4\), with equality at \(x_k=s/4\).  Minimizing \((1-s)^2+s^2/4\) gives \(s=4/5\), hence \(x_k=1/5\).  Therefore
\[
\Pi_{T_{\mathfrak O^{32}}(p_\star)}
\{\zeta(\mathbf e_1-\mathbf e_7)\}
=\frac{\zeta}{5}(-4\mathbf e_7+\mathbf e_8+\mathbf e_9+\mathbf e_{10}+\mathbf e_{12}).
\tag{25}
\]
For \(\zeta<0\), the arm-swapped enumeration uses low-arm outgoing rows \(2,3,4,5\) and gives
\[
\Pi_{T_{\mathfrak O^{32}}(p_\star)}
\{\zeta(\mathbf e_1-\mathbf e_7)\}
=\frac{|\zeta|}{5}(-4\mathbf e_1+\mathbf e_2+\mathbf e_3+\mathbf e_4+\mathbf e_5).
\tag{26}
\]
The restored \(\mathbf e_k\) notation in (25)--(26) denotes the standard basis in the twelve observable coordinates.

All four outgoing cells on either ray in (25)--(26) have target-zero representatives, and all target coordinates are nonnegative.  Thus
\[
D_L(G_{p_\star})=0.
\tag{27}
\]
Exactly two of the four outgoing cells on each ray also have target-one representatives.  Since an incidence entry is \(1/2\), producing observable derivative \(|\zeta|/5\) in either such cell requires type-weight derivative \(2|\zeta|/5\).  Assigning the two cells to their target-one representatives attains target derivative \(4|\zeta|/5\).  Completeness of the table shows that the other two outgoing cells have no target-one representative, so the same number is an upper bound.  Consequently
\[
D_U(G_{p_\star})=\frac45|\zeta|,
\qquad H(G_{p_\star})=0.
\tag{28}
\]

Because \(\mathfrak O^{32}\) is a polytope, a sufficiently small neighborhood of \(p_\star\) agrees with the corresponding neighborhood of \(p_\star+T_{\mathfrak O^{32}}(p_\star)\): this follows directly by separating the finitely many active inequalities at \(p_\star\) from the finitely many inactive inequalities, which retain positive slack.  If \(y=\Pi_{\mathfrak O^{32}}(p_\star+x)\), projection optimality against the feasible point \(p_\star\) gives
\[
\|y-p_\star\|_2^2\leq x^{\top}(y-p_\star)
\leq\|x\|_2\|y-p_\star\|_2,
\]
and hence \(\|y-p_\star\|_2\leq\|x\|_2\).  Small inputs therefore project within the common neighborhood, so (25)--(26) are exact local projection formulas.  The target-zero and target-one allocations used in (27)--(28) remain feasible with weights linear in \(|\zeta|\) for all sufficiently small \(|\zeta|\), and completeness of the table supplies the matching linear upper bounds.  Thus the endpoint formulas are locally exact on both rays of
\[
V_{p_\star}=\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf e_1-\mathbf e_7\}.
\]
Those rays form a finite fan, and its only proper facet has a normal nonzero on this one-dimensional support.  The covariance in (28) is strictly positive on that support, so Gaussian nondegeneracy also holds.  This verifies conditions (i)--(ii) while the endpoint fibers retain multiple optima.

Finally, (13) and \(m_n^2/n\to0\) imply
\[
r_n:=\delta_{m_n}(p_\star)+m_n/\sqrt n+m_n^2/n\longrightarrow0.
\]
The positive nonincreasing sequence
\[
\eta_n^\star=\sup_{k\geq n}\sqrt{r_k}+n^{-1}
\]
satisfies \(\eta_n^\star\downarrow0\) and
\[
0\leq\frac{r_n}{\eta_n^\star}\leq\sqrt{r_n}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Hence admissible slack schedules exist, and every decreasing schedule satisfying the theorem's displayed dominance condition yields the asserted pointwise conditional coverage.  Equations (24)--(28) give the exact unit atom at zero and complete the thirty-two-type specialization.
