Let \(d=|\mathcal O|\), let \(\Phi=\theta\circ\Pi_{\mathfrak O}\), and put
\[
X_n=\sqrt n(\widehat p-p),\qquad
T_n=\max\{\sqrt n(\widehat L-L(p)),\sqrt n(U(p)-\widehat U)\}.
\]
By \(\text{lem:finite-multinomial-clt}\), using \(\text{ass:iid-finite-sampling}\),
\[
X_n\rightsquigarrow G_p
\quad\text{and hence}\quad X_n=O_p(1).
\tag{1}
\]
The support restrictions in the definition of \(V_p\) hold almost surely for \(X_n\): its coordinates sum to zero, and if \(p_o=0\), then no observation occupies cell \(o\).  By condition (i), there is \(r>0\) such that
\[
\Phi(p+x)-\Phi(p)=(D_L(x),D_U(x))
\quad\text{for every }x\in V_p\text{ with }\|x\|_2<r.
\tag{2}
\]
Both directional coordinates are continuous, positively homogeneous, piecewise-linear functions on the finite fan.  Therefore there is a finite constant \(C_D\) such that
\[
|D_L(x)|\vee |D_U(x)|\leq C_D\|x\|_2,
\qquad x\in V_p.
\tag{3}
\]
Equations (1)--(2) give
\[
T_n=H(X_n)
\tag{4}
\]
with probability tending to one.  Since \(H\) is continuous, every bounded continuous test function \(f\) gives another bounded continuous function \(f\circ H\); the definition of weak convergence in (1) therefore gives \(H(X_n)\rightsquigarrow H(G_p)\).

We next establish the approximation that will be used for the conditional subsampling law.  Write \(m=m_n\), let \(\widehat p_b\) be the empirical law in a uniformly sampled size-\(m\) subsample without replacement, and define
\[
a_n=\sqrt m(\widehat p-p)=\sqrt{m/n}\,X_n.
\tag{5}
\]
Whenever \(\widehat p\) and \(\widehat p_b\) are in the neighborhood in (2), the centered subsample statistic is
\[
S_{n,b}
=\max\{D_L(\sqrt m(\widehat p_b-p))-D_L(a_n),
D_U(a_n)-D_U(\sqrt m(\widehat p_b-p))\}.
\tag{6}
\]
To compare (6) with an independent experiment, first couple the ordered sample of indices without replacement to \(m\) indices sampled with replacement from \(\{1,\ldots,n\}\).  The two samples agree unless a repeated index occurs, so, conditionally on the data, the failure probability is at most
\[
\frac{m(m-1)}{2n}.
\tag{7}
\]
Next couple one draw from the empirical categorical law \(\widehat p\) to one draw from \(p\) by assigning matching category \(o\) probability \(\min\{\widehat p_o,p_o\}\) and distributing the two residual masses over the unmatched categories.  Its mismatch probability is \(1-\sum_o\min\{\widehat p_o,p_o\}=\operatorname{TV}(\widehat p,p)\).  Applying this construction independently to the \(m\) draws and using the union bound, all categories agree except on an event of conditional probability at most
\[
m\,\operatorname{TV}(\widehat p,p)
=\frac m2\|\widehat p-p\|_1
=O_p(m/\sqrt n),
\tag{8}
\]
where the final equality follows from (1) and finiteness of \(d\).  On the successful coupling event,
\[
\sqrt m(\widehat p_b-p)=X_m
\]
for the independent-
\(p\) vector in the statement.  Thus the comparison variable is
\[
K_{a_n}(X_m)
=\max\{D_L(X_m)-D_L(a_n),D_U(a_n)-D_U(X_m)\}.
\tag{9}
\]
Notice that the population centering in (9), rather than a translation inside \(D_L\) or \(D_U\), is forced by the identity \(\sqrt m(\widehat p_b-p)=\sqrt m(\widehat p_b-\widehat p)+a_n\).

The exceptional event on which (2) cannot be used has probability tending to zero.  More precisely, boundedness and centering of each categorical summand give \(\mathbb E\exp\{u(e_{O_i,o}-p_o)\}\leq\exp(Cu^2)\) for all sufficiently small \(|u|\), by the power-series bound \(e^v\leq1+v+C'v^2\) on a bounded interval.  Independence, exponential Markov inequality, and a union bound over the finite coordinates then give an exponential bound \(C_0e^{-c_0m}\) for the independent-
\(p\) event \(\|X_m\|_2>r\sqrt m/2\), while (1) and (5) put \(\widehat p\) in the radius-
\(r/2\) neighborhood with probability tending to one.  This exponential term is harmless in the sole case below where an \(o(\eta_n)\) comparison is needed.  Indeed, if \(F(0-)=\beta>0\), then \(H(G_p)<0\) with positive probability, so at least one nonzero endpoint slope belongs to \(\mathcal A_p\).  The law of its value on \(X_m\) is discrete with at most \((m+1)^d\) support points and hence has an atom of size at least \((m+1)^{-d}\).  Its Gaussian counterpart is continuous by nondegeneracy.  Comparing the distribution functions immediately before and at that atom yields
\[
\delta_m(p)\geq\frac{1}{2(m+1)^d}.
\tag{10}
\]
Consequently \(e^{-c_0m}=o(\delta_m(p))\).  If \(F(0-)<\beta\), only convergence of the exceptional probability to zero is needed because there is a fixed probability gap.

We now verify that \(\delta_m(p)\to0\), rather than treating it as an additional assumption.  Equation (1), with \(n\) replaced by \(m\), implies
\[
AX_m\rightsquigarrow AG_p
\tag{11}
\]
for every \(A\in\mathcal A_p\): for each bounded continuous \(f\), the function \(x\mapsto f(Ax)\) is bounded and continuous in the defining convergence (1).  Delete any identically zero rows of \(A\).  Every remaining row is nonzero on \(V_p\), by condition (ii) and the construction of \(\mathcal A_p\), and therefore has strictly positive Gaussian variance because \(G_p\) is nondegenerate on \(V_p\).  Hence every marginal of \(AG_p\) is continuous.  Its joint distribution function is continuous as well, because the probability of the boundary of any lower orthant is bounded by the sum of the point probabilities of its marginals.  A finite grid on a sufficiently large compact box, monotonicity of distribution functions between grid points, and tightness outside the box upgrade the pointwise convergence from (11) to uniform convergence over lower orthants.  Since \(\mathcal A_p\) is finite,
\[
\delta_m(p)\longrightarrow0.
\tag{12}
\]

The events obtained by selecting a fan cone and comparing the two affine expressions in (9) are finite Boolean combinations of half-spaces whose normals are rows of matrices in \(\mathcal A_p\).  Closure of \(\mathcal A_p\) under row selection and sign changes, inclusion--exclusion, (3), and (12) therefore show that, conditionally on the data,
\[
S_{n,b}\rightsquigarrow H(G_p)
\quad\text{in probability}.
\tag{13}
\]
For clarity, the quantitative error in this comparison on \(\{\|X_n\|_2\leq M\}\), at every continuity point of the limiting distribution, is bounded by a constant depending only on the fixed fan and \(M\) times
\[
R_n:=\delta_m(p)+\frac m{\sqrt n}+\frac{m^2}{n}.
\tag{14}
\]
Indeed, (7)--(8) give the last two terms; (5) and (3) move each nonzero Gaussian linear threshold by at most \(C M\sqrt{m/n}\leq C M m/\sqrt n\); and a nonzero Gaussian linear functional has a bounded density, so every resulting Gaussian slab has probability at most another constant times its width.  The same half-space comparison with threshold zero will be used below.

Let \(F(x)=\Pr\{H(G_p)\leq x\}\).  On each full-dimensional cone of the refined fan, the comparison hyperplanes select one of the two endpoint slopes, so \(H\) is a single linear functional there.  Proper fan boundaries have Gaussian probability zero by condition (ii) and nondegeneracy.  A nonzero linear functional of \(G_p\) has a continuous distribution, while a zero linear functional equals zero.  Positive homogeneity also rules out a nonzero constant linear piece.  It follows that
\[
F\text{ can have an atom only at }0.
\tag{15}
\]

Let \(c_n=c^{\mathrm{sub}}_{n,\beta+\eta_n}\) and \(q_\beta=\inf\{x:F(x)\geq\beta\}\).  Suppose first that \(q_\beta\neq0\).  For every sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\), the point \(x_\varepsilon=q_\beta-\varepsilon\) is nonzero and satisfies \(F(x_\varepsilon)<\beta\).  By (13), the conditional probability \(\Pr^*\{S_{n,b}\leq x_\varepsilon\}\) is below \(\beta+\eta_n\) with probability tending to one, and hence \(c_n>x_\varepsilon\) with probability tending to one.  The indicator of \(( -\infty,x_\varepsilon]\) can be squeezed from above and below by bounded continuous functions whose limiting expectations differ by arbitrarily little, because (15) gives \(\Pr\{H(G_p)=x_\varepsilon\}=0\).  Applying this squeeze to the weak convergence following (4) gives
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\Pr\{T_n\leq c_n\}
\geq F(x_\varepsilon).
\]
Letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) and using continuity of \(F\) at the nonzero point \(q_\beta\) yields
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\Pr\{T_n\leq c_n\}\geq F(q_\beta)\geq\beta.
\tag{16}
\]

It remains to handle \(q_\beta=0\), where
\[
F(0-)\leq\beta\leq F(0).
\tag{17}
\]
Fix a realization \(z=X_n\) satisfying \(H(z)=0\).  Since a maximum is zero only if both its arguments are nonpositive, positive homogeneity and (5) give
\[
D_L(a_n)=\sqrt{m/n}\,D_L(z)\leq0,
\qquad
D_U(a_n)=\sqrt{m/n}\,D_U(z)\geq0.
\tag{18}
\]
If \(g\) lies in the interior of a full-dimensional cone on which \(H\equiv0\), condition (iii) makes (9) nonnegative.  Indeed, when the lower slope vanishes,
\[
D_L(g)-D_L(a_n)=-D_L(a_n)\geq0,
\]
and when the upper slope vanishes,
\[
D_U(a_n)-D_U(g)=D_U(a_n)\geq0.
\]
In fact, (18) gives the global sign implication
\[
K_{a_n}(g)<0
\ \Longrightarrow\ D_L(g)<D_L(a_n)\leq0
\text{ and }D_U(g)>D_U(a_n)\geq0
\ \Longrightarrow\ H(g)<0.
\tag{19a}
\]
Thus the zero-flatness condition supplies an enumerable cone-wise certificate for the sign preservation, while (19a) shows that proper fan boundaries cannot create an unaccounted negative centered value.  The half-space comparison underlying (14), together with (18)--(19a), therefore gives, uniformly on \(\{\|X_n\|_2\leq M,H(X_n)=0\}\),
\[
\Pr^*\{S_{n,b}<0\}
\leq F(0-)+C_M R_n+r_{n,M},
\tag{19}
\]
where \(r_{n,M}=o(\eta_n)\) if \(F(0-)=\beta\), by (10), and \(r_{n,M}=o(1)\) if \(F(0-)<\beta\).  Thus (10) absorbs the local-neighborhood exception when equality holds in the left inequality of (17), while the fixed probability gap absorbs it otherwise.  By the assumed slack domination, \(R_n/\eta_n\to0\).  The conditional subsampling distribution has finite support, and its lower \((\beta+\eta_n)\)-quantile is nonnegative whenever its mass strictly below zero is smaller than \(\beta+\eta_n\).  Since \(X_n=O_p(1)\), (17)--(19) therefore imply
\[
\Pr\{H(X_n)=0,\ c_n<0\}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{20}
\]

We also have \(c_n=o_p(1)\).  For each \(\varepsilon>0\), the definition \(q_\beta=0\) gives \(F(-\varepsilon)<\beta\), so (13) implies \(c_n> -\varepsilon\) with probability tending to one.  Moreover \(F(\varepsilon)>\beta\).  This is immediate if \(F(0)>\beta\).  If \(F(0)=\beta\) and \(H\) takes a positive value, continuity and positive homogeneity give an open Gaussian-positive set on which \(0<H\leq\varepsilon\); if \(H\) never takes a positive value, then \(F(\varepsilon)=1>\beta\).  Applying (13) again yields \(c_n\leq\varepsilon\) with probability tending to one.  Hence
\[
c_n=o_p(1).
\tag{21}
\]

The zero set of \(H\) is a union of full-dimensional zero-cone interiors and subsets of finitely many proper fan hyperplanes.  Its boundary is therefore Gaussian-null.  Continuous functions that equal one away from an arbitrarily small neighborhood of this boundary and zero on the opposite side squeeze the indicator of the zero set; applying (1) to those functions gives
\[
\Pr\{H(X_n)=0\}\longrightarrow F(0)-F(0-).
\tag{22}
\]
For any \(\varepsilon>0\), (20)--(21) cover, with probability tending to one, both observations for which \(H(X_n)\leq-\varepsilon\) and observations for which \(H(X_n)=0\).  Using (4), (15), and (22),
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\Pr\{T_n\leq c_n\}
\geq F(-\varepsilon)+F(0)-F(0-).
\]
Letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) makes the right-hand side \(F(0)\), which is at least \(\beta\) by (17).  Together with (16), this proves
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\Pr\{T_n\leq c_n\}\geq1-\alpha.
\tag{23}
\]
By the definition of \(T_n\), the event in (23) is exactly
\[
\left\{L(p)\geq\widehat L-c_n/\sqrt n,\quad
U(p)\leq\widehat U+c_n/\sqrt n\right\},
\]
which proves the asserted simultaneous endpoint coverage.

It remains to verify the promised boundary certificate.  By \(\text{thm:complete-32-type-refinement-certificate}\), the table contains the full observable--target quotient.  Every column of \(B^{32}\) is nonnegative and has one entry \(1/2\) in each trial-arm block.  If a convex combination of columns equals
\[
p_\star=\tfrac12(\mathbf e_1+\mathbf e_7),
\]
then every positively weighted column must be supported on rows \(1\) and \(7\).  Inspection of \(\text{def:complete-32-type-table}\) shows that precisely columns \(1\) and \(17\) have this support, that they coincide, and that \(h^{32}_1=h^{32}_{17}=0\).  Thus \(p_\star\) is a vertex, its fiber is the segment joining types \(1\) and \(17\), and every point of that segment is optimal for both endpoint programs with value zero.  The multinomial covariance on the two positive cells gives
\[
G_{p_\star}=\zeta(\mathbf e_1-\mathbf e_7),
\qquad \zeta\sim\mathcal N(0,1/4).
\tag{24}
\]

For \(\zeta>0\), the table supplies four high-arm outgoing cells, rows \(8,9,10,12\), while retaining row \(1\) in the low arm.  We first verify that using the remaining high-arm cell, row \(11\), cannot improve the projection.  Divide all candidate tangent coordinates by \(\zeta\), let \(t\geq0\) be the coordinate of row \(11\), and let \(r\geq0\) be the sum of the other four high-arm outgoing coordinates.  Every table column entering row \(11\) also enters low-arm row \(2\) or row \(5\).  Thus the low-arm outgoing mass is at least \(t\), and its squared-distance contribution is at least \((1+t)^2+t^2/2\).  For fixed \((r,t)\), equal allocation among the other four high-arm cells minimizes their sum of squares, so the high-arm contribution is at least \((1-r-t)^2+r^2/4+t^2\).  The sum
\[
(1+t)^2+\frac{t^2}{2}+(1-r-t)^2+\frac{r^2}{4}+t^2
\]
is convex on \(r,t\geq0\); its derivative in \(t\) is \(2r+7t\), and its minimum is therefore at \(t=0\), followed by \(r=4/5\).  Hence the low-arm block is fixed at the projection, and if the four accessible outgoing masses are \(x_1,\ldots,x_4\), with \(s=\sum_{k=1}^4x_k\), the squared Euclidean distance, after division by \(\zeta^2\), is
\[
1+(1-s)^2+\sum_{k=1}^4x_k^2.
\]
For a fixed \(s\), the sum of squares is minimized at \(x_k=s/4\); minimizing \((1-s)^2+s^2/4\) gives \(s=4/5\) and \(x_k=1/5\).  Hence
\[
\Pi_{T_{\mathfrak O^{32}}(p_\star)}
\{\zeta(\mathbf e_1-\mathbf e_7)\}
=\frac{\zeta}{5}(-4\mathbf e_7+mathbf e_8+mathbf e_9+mathbf e_{10}+mathbf e_{12}).
\tag{25}
\]
For \(\zeta<0\), the arm-swapped calculation uses low-arm outgoing rows \(2,3,4,5\) and yields
\[
\Pi_{T_{\mathfrak O^{32}}(p_\star)}
\{\zeta(\mathbf e_1-\mathbf e_7)\}
=\frac{|\zeta|}{5}(-4\mathbf e_1+mathbf e_2+mathbf e_3+mathbf e_4+mathbf e_5).
\tag{26}
\]
All four outgoing cells in either (25) or (26) have target-zero representatives in the complete table, and all targets are nonnegative.  Therefore the lower directional value is
\[
D_L(G_{p_\star})=0.
\tag{27}
\]
Exactly two of the four outgoing cells on either ray also have target-one representatives.  Because an incidence entry equals \(1/2\), producing probability \(|\zeta|/5\) in either such cell requires type-weight derivative \(2|\zeta|/5\).  Assigning both cells to their target-one representatives attains target derivative \(4|\zeta|/5\).  The exhaustive table shows that the other two outgoing cells have no target-one representative, so this value is also an upper bound.  Consequently
\[
D_U(G_{p_\star})=\frac45|\zeta|,
\qquad H(G_{p_\star})=0.
\tag{28}
\]
Because \(\mathfrak O^{32}\) is a polytope, a sufficiently small neighborhood of \(p_\star\) agrees with the corresponding neighborhood of \(p_\star+T_{\mathfrak O^{32}}(p_\star)\).  If \(y=\Pi_{\mathfrak O^{32}}(p_\star+x)\), projection optimality with the feasible point \(p_\star\) gives \(\|y-p_\star\|_2^2\leq x^{\top}(y-p_\star)\), and hence \(\|y-p_\star\|_2\leq\|x\|_2\).  Therefore sufficiently small inputs project inside that common neighborhood, and (25)--(26) are exact local projection formulas, not merely first-order approximations.  The exhaustive target-zero and target-one allocations used in (27)--(28) remain feasible with weights linear in \(|\zeta|\) for all sufficiently small \(|\zeta|\), and their matching upper bounds are linear as well.  Thus the endpoint formulas are locally exact.  The two rays of the one-dimensional support \(V_{p_\star}\) form the required finite fan; their only proper facet has a nonzero normal on that support.  Equation (27) supplies the zero lower slope and (28) gives \(-D_U\leq0\), so condition (iii) holds even though both LP fibers have multiple optima.  Finally, (12) and \(m_n^2/n\to0\) imply
\[
r_n:=\delta_{m_n}(p_\star)+m_n/\sqrt n+m_n^2/n\longrightarrow0.
\]
The decreasing majorant
\[
\eta_n=\sup_{k\geq n}\sqrt{r_k}
\]
(with an arbitrarily small strictly decreasing perturbation if needed) satisfies \(\eta_n\downarrow0\) and \(r_n/\eta_n\to0\).  Thus (24)--(28) give the exact unit atom at zero and verify every condition of the conservative atomic-tie conclusion on the stated thirty-two-type boundary law.
