Put \(I_b=\{v\in\Omega_V:B(v)=b\}\), and let \(M\in\mathfrak M_\delta(\mathcal P,q)\).  By \(\text{ass:intervention-selection-margin}\) and \(\delta>0\),
\[
d_M\geq\delta>0,
\]
so every division below is legitimate.  Recursiveness gives a unique solution under each hard intervention.  Hence the definition of the hard-intervention submodel gives consistency: on the factual event \(\{V=v,S=1\}\) with \(v\in I_b\), replacing the already realized value \(B=b\) by \(do(B=b)\) leaves the recursive solution unchanged.  In particular, on that event,
\[
A^b=A(v),\qquad S^b=1.
\]
Since \(h(A^b)-h_-\geq0\), the disjoint factual events indexed by \(I_b\), \(\text{ass:selected-law-fit}\), and \(\text{ass:population-selection-margin}\) therefore give
\[
\begin{aligned}
n_M-h_-d_M
&=\mathbb E_M\!\left[(h(A^b)-h_-)\mathbf 1\{S^b=1\}\right]\\
&\geq\sum_{v\in I_b}(h(A(v))-h_-)\Pr_M(V=v,S=1)\\
&=\rho\sum_{v\in I_b}q(v)(h(A(v))-h_-)\\
&\geq\delta\sum_{v\in I_b}q(v)(h(A(v))-h_-).
\end{aligned}
\]
Also \(d_M\leq1\).  The sum in the last display is nonnegative, so division by \(d_M\) yields
\[
\theta(M)-h_-=\frac{n_M-h_-d_M}{d_M}
\geq \frac{\delta}{d_M}\sum_{v\in I_b}q(v)(h(A(v))-h_-)
\geq\delta\sum_{v\in I_b}q(v)(h(A(v))-h_-).
\]
Thus \(\theta(M)\geq L_\delta\), with \(L_\delta\) as in \(\text{def:saturated-envelope}\).  Applying exactly the same argument to the nonnegative outcome \(h_+-h\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
h_+d_M-n_M
&=\mathbb E_M\!\left[(h_+-h(A^b))\mathbf 1\{S^b=1\}\right]\\
&\geq\rho\sum_{v\in I_b}q(v)(h_+-h(A(v)))\\
&\geq\delta\sum_{v\in I_b}q(v)(h_+-h(A(v))),
\end{aligned}
\]
and hence
\[
\theta(M)\leq h_+-\delta\sum_{v\in I_b}q(v)(h_+-h(A(v)))=U_\delta.
\]
This proves \(\Theta_\delta(\mathcal P,q)\subseteq[L_\delta,U_\delta]\).

We next construct legal endpoint models.  Finiteness of \(\Omega_A\) supplies states \(a_-\) and \(a_+\) satisfying \(h(a_-)=h_-\) and \(h(a_+)=h_+\).  Since \(|\Omega_B|\geq2\), fix \(b^\dagger\neq b\).  Introduce one exogenous source \(U\).  For every \(v\in\Omega_V\), give \(U\) a state \(u_v\) of mass
\[
\Pr(U=u_v)=\delta q(v).
\]
If \(\delta<1\), add a state \(u_0\) of mass \(1-\delta\); when \(\delta=1\), omit this zero-mass state.  All displayed masses are positive and there are at most \(m+1\) of them.

Use the recursive order consisting of \(B\) first and then \(V\setminus B\) and \(S\).  Set \(B(u_v)=B(v)\) and \(B(u_0)=b^\dagger\).  For each \(X\in V\setminus B\), complete a table \(X=f_X(B,U)\) so that
\[
f_X(B(v),u_v)=X(v).
\]
Set \(S(B(v),u_v)=1\), set \(S(b^\dagger,u_0)=0\) when \(u_0\) is present, and set
\[
S(b,u)=1\quad\text{for every positive-mass state }u.
\]
For the lower model, complete the coordinates of the \(A\)-table at intervention input \(b\) by
\[
A(b,u_v)=
\begin{cases}
A(v),&B(v)=b,\\
a_-,&B(v)\neq b,
\end{cases}
\qquad A(b,u_0)=a_-;
\]
for the upper model replace every displayed \(a_-\) by \(a_+\).  These prescriptions do not conflict with the factual table entries because \(b^\dagger\neq b\); all entries not yet specified may be filled arbitrarily.  This is a recursive SCM with a single exogenous source.

In either endpoint model, exactly the \(u_v\)-types are factually selected.  Consequently
\[
\Pr(V=v,S=1)=\delta q(v),\qquad \rho=\Pr(S=1)=\delta.
\]
Under \(do(B=b)\), every positive-mass type is selected, so \(d_M=1\geq\delta\).  Moreover,
\[
\begin{aligned}
n_M^{(-)}
&=\delta\sum_{v\in I_b}q(v)h(A(v))
+\left(1-\delta\sum_{v\in I_b}q(v)\right)h_-\\
&=h_-+\delta\sum_{v\in I_b}q(v)(h(A(v))-h_-)=L_\delta,
\end{aligned}
\]
and similarly
\[
\begin{aligned}
n_M^{(+)}
&=\delta\sum_{v\in I_b}q(v)h(A(v))
+\left(1-\delta\sum_{v\in I_b}q(v)\right)h_+\\
&=h_+-\delta\sum_{v\in I_b}q(v)(h_+-h(A(v)))=U_\delta.
\end{aligned}
\]
Because \(d_M=1\), these are also the two values of \(\theta(M)\).

It remains to verify the exact graph restriction rather than merely a permitted graph.  By \(\text{ass:positive-selected-law}\), every \(u_v\) has positive mass.  For each coordinate \(X\in V\), the hypothesis that its state space has at least two elements and positivity of every joint cell provide positive-mass states of \(U\) at which the table for \(X\) differs (with the endogenous input \(B\) held fixed when \(X\notin B\)).  Thus \(U\) is an actual essential parent of every observed vertex under the definition of \(G_S(M)\), not merely a formal parent slot.  For every distinct \(X,Y\in V\), the latent fork \(X\leftarrow U\rightarrow Y\) therefore induces an adjacency after retaining \(S\), latent projection, and selected-MAG projection.  The resulting selected MAG has complete observed skeleton.  A complete skeleton has no nonadjacent vertex pair and hence no nontrivial separation; it also has no discriminating path with nonadjacent endpoints.  Consequently its selected MAG is in the equivalence class represented by the complete all-circle COPAG.  Hence \(G_S(M)\in\mathcal G(\mathcal P)\), verifying \(\text{ass:exact-copag-stratum}\).  Both endpoint SCMs consequently belong to \(\mathfrak M_\delta(\mathcal P,q)\).

For any \(t\in[0,1]\), take disjointly labelled source states from the lower and upper constructions, with their masses multiplied by \(1-t\) and \(t\), respectively.  This is still a one-source recursive SCM; its factual selected cells remain \(\delta q(v)\), its intervention denominator remains one, and the same common-source argument gives exact complete-COPAG legality.  Linearity of expectation gives
\[
\theta(M_t)=(1-t)L_\delta+tU_\delta.
\]
Thus every point of \([L_\delta,U_\delta]\) is attained, and together with the universal inequalities this proves
\[
\Theta_\delta(\mathcal P,q)=[L_\delta,U_\delta].
\]

If \(h\) is constant, then \(h_-=h_+\) and directly \(n_M=h_-d_M\), so every compatible model has \(\theta(M)=h_-\); the construction above proves nonemptiness, hence the fiber is \(\{h_-\}\).  Finally, if the intervention is vacuous, the intervention submodel equals the factual model.  Using \(\sum_vq(v)=1\) and \(\text{ass:selected-law-fit}\),
\[
d_M=\Pr(S=1)=\sum_v c_v(M)=\rho,
\qquad
n_M=\sum_v h(A(v))c_v(M)=\rho\sum_vq(v)h(A(v)).
\]
Since \(\rho\geq\delta>0\), division gives \(\theta(M)=\sum_vq(v)h(A(v))\).  For nonemptiness, take one source with states \(u_v\) of masses \(\delta q(v)\), together when \(\delta<1\) with a state \(u_0\) of mass \(1-\delta\).  Put \(V(u_v)=v\), \(S(u_v)=1\), and \(S(u_0)=0\), with \(V(u_0)\) arbitrary.  Then \(c_v=\delta q(v)\) and, because the intervention is vacuous, \(d_M=\rho=\delta\).  Positivity of \(q\) makes the common source an essential parent of every observed vertex, so the preceding complete-skeleton argument verifies graph legality.  Thus the vacuous-intervention fiber is exactly the asserted singleton.

For the binary outcome \(h^*(a)=a\), intervention \(b=0\), and \(\delta^*=1/4\), \(\text{def:saturated-envelope}\) gives
\[
L_{\delta^*}=\delta^*q^*(0,1)=\frac14\frac2{10}=\frac1{20},
\qquad
U_{\delta^*}=1-\delta^*q^*(0,0)=1-\frac14\frac1{10}=\frac{39}{40}.
\]
The hypotheses of \(\text{thm:saturated-closed-form}\) hold for \(\mathcal P^*\), so this calculation already proves sharpness of the interval.  We now exhibit the asserted five-type endpoint certificates.

Let a single exogenous source \(U\) have states \(u_1,\ldots,u_5\) with respective masses
\[
(w_1,w_2,w_3,w_4,w_5)=\frac1{40}(1,2,3,4,30).
\]
In both models define
\[
(B(u_i))_{i=1}^5=(0,0,1,1,1),
\]
and define the two rows of the selection table by
\[
(S(0,u_i))_{i=1}^5=(1,1,1,1,1),
\qquad
(S(1,u_i))_{i=1}^5=(1,1,1,1,0).
\]
For the lower endpoint use
\[
(A(0,u_i))_{i=1}^5=(0,1,0,0,0),
\qquad
(A(1,u_i))_{i=1}^5=(0,1,0,1,0),
\]
whereas for the upper endpoint use
\[
(A(0,u_i))_{i=1}^5=(0,1,1,1,1),
\qquad
(A(1,u_i))_{i=1}^5=(0,1,0,1,0).
\]
The natural selected cells of \(u_1,u_2,u_3,u_4\) are, respectively, \((0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1)\), while \(u_5\) is naturally unselected.  Therefore
\[
\rho=\sum_{i=1}^4w_i=\frac14,
\qquad
\bigl(c_{00},c_{01},c_{10},c_{11}\bigr)=\frac1{40}(1,2,3,4)=\rho q^*.
\]
Under \(do(B=0)\), the first selection row shows that \(d_M=\sum_iw_i=1\) in both models.  The lower and upper numerators are
\[
n_M^{(-)}=w_2=\frac2{40}=\frac1{20},
\qquad
n_M^{(+)}=w_2+w_3+w_4+w_5=\frac{39}{40}.
\]
Because the denominators equal one, these are the corresponding intervention means.

Finally, \(U\) is an actual essential parent of \(B\), \(A\), and \(S\): the displayed positive-mass table rows vary with \(U\) for each vertex.  Hence the latent projection contains an adjacency between \(B\) and \(A\), and its two-vertex selected MAG is represented by the complete all-circle COPAG \(\mathcal P^*\).  Thus both five-type SCMs satisfy the exact graph restriction, completing the certificate.

%%% FIELD proof-confidence-fiber
Define the coverage events
\[
E_q=\{q\in C_n\},\qquad E_g=\{\mathcal P\in\Pi_n\}.
\]
The two assumed marginal guarantees and the elementary union bound imply
\[
\Pr(E_q\cap E_g)
=1-\Pr(E_q^c\cup E_g^c)
\geq1-\Pr(E_q^c)-\Pr(E_g^c)
\geq1-\alpha_q-\alpha_g.
\]
On \(E_q\cap E_g\), the particular pair \((\mathcal P,q)\) occurs among the pairs indexing the union in \(\text{def:confidence-fiber}\).  Therefore, pointwise on this event,
\[
\Theta_\delta(\mathcal P,q)
\subseteq
\bigcup_{\mathcal P'\in\Pi_n,\,q'\in C_n}
\Theta_\delta(\mathcal P',q')
\subseteq
\operatorname{cl}\operatorname{conv}
\bigcup_{\mathcal P'\in\Pi_n,\,q'\in C_n}
\Theta_\delta(\mathcal P',q')
=\widehat{\mathfrak C}_n.
\]
Taking probabilities proves
\[
\Pr\!\left(\Theta_\delta(\mathcal P,q)\subseteq\widehat{\mathfrak C}_n\right)
\geq1-\alpha_q-\alpha_g.
\]
The argument uses only event inclusion; in particular, no differentiability, compactness, attainment, or uniqueness of an optimizer has entered.

For the saturated binary configuration, write \(q_{ij}=q(B=i,A=j)\).  By \(\text{thm:saturated-closed-form}\), with \(b=0\) and \(h(a)=a\),
\[
\Theta_\delta(\mathcal P,q)=[\delta q_{01},\,1-\delta q_{00}].
\]
Under the declared i.i.d. sampling law, each cell count has its corresponding binomial marginal distribution.  Because \(l_{01}\) and \(l_{00}\) are one-sided exact lower confidence limits with error allocation \(\alpha_q/2\), their defining coverage properties are
\[
\Pr(l_{01}\leq q_{01})\geq1-\frac{\alpha_q}{2},
\qquad
\Pr(l_{00}\leq q_{00})\geq1-\frac{\alpha_q}{2}.
\]
No independence between these two limits is required.  A second application of the union bound gives
\[
\Pr(l_{01}\leq q_{01},\ l_{00}\leq q_{00})\geq1-\alpha_q.
\]
On this simultaneous event, \(\delta>0\) gives
\[
\delta l_{01}\leq\delta q_{01},
\qquad
1-\delta q_{00}\leq1-\delta l_{00},
\]
and hence
\[
\Theta_\delta(\mathcal P,q)
=[\delta q_{01},1-\delta q_{00}]
\subseteq[\delta l_{01},1-\delta l_{00}].
\]
Thus the reported finite-sample interval covers the entire sharp fiber with probability at least \(1-\alpha_q\), as claimed.
