\section*{scm\_\allowbreak{}selpag\_\allowbreak{}delta\_\allowbreak{}closed\_\allowbreak{}fiber}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} The exact actual-parent COPAG fiber for a positive, potentially unfaithful selected law is not proved closed.  What is established is a bounded finite graph-legal response fiber indexed by at most \(N!\) saturated orders, with essential-feature signs that exactly determine its selected MAG and singly exponential one-block real-algebraic certificates for its endpoint membership.  Independently of graph restrictions, consistency and the two calibrated selection margins give a universal closed-form outer envelope; one-source complete-COPAG witnesses attain it, and any permitted family containing those witnesses inherits the interval by sandwiching.  The binary interval \(\left[1/20,39/40\right]\) is an audit instance, not the headline.  Confidence-fiber inference provides finite-sample outer coverage for the survivor-selected cardiac-surgery use case of Lee et al., while universal legal lifting remains open.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Chen and Mooij give a sound-and-complete COPAG identification algorithm on specified model classes; Yang et al. optimize over faithful no-selection PAG-compatible SCMs; Duarte et al. optimize a fixed canonical DAG; and Carey et al. characterize normalized selected-marginalized models. None settles whether the exact actual-parent COPAG fiber for a positive, potentially unfaithful selected law is closed or has attained endpoints. This note also leaves that general question open, while formalizing it and proving bounded-finite and saturated special-case results.

\paragraph{Niche.} The tractable object constructed here is the bounded finite graph-legal response fiber, a finite union of semialgebraic strata. Its essential-feature signs determine the exact selected MAG, and real-algebraic procedures decide whether a boundary value of this bounded image has a legal representative or is excluded from all bounded branches. Equality with, and closure or attainment of, the full arbitrary-SCM fiber remain exactly \(\text{oeq:universal-legal-lifting}\).

\paragraph{Fill.} The project supplies an invariant essential-feature index and exact polynomial legality test, embeds finite response architectures into at most \(N!\) saturated ordered superarchitectures, and gives singly exponential one-block endpoint certificates for the bounded finite image.  It proves a graph-free universal selection-margin envelope with one-source complete-COPAG attainment, designates the compelled-collider case as the first unresolved kill test, and retains finite-sample confidence-fiber coverage without asserting endpoint convergence for the arbitrary-SCM fiber.

\section{Related work}

Richardson and Spirtes define maximal ancestral graphs under latent marginalization and selection conditioning.  Evans canonicalizes margins of latent DAGs and relates latent projection to intervention, but does not prove closure of an exact functional-essentiality stratum.  Rosset, Gisin, and Wolfe give network hidden-variable cardinality bounds; their sourcewise reduction motivates the finite support step but does not preserve strict actual-edge witnesses.  Duarte et al. fix a canonical DAG and optimize over graph-permitted finite NPSEMs, without requiring every displayed edge to remain functionally essential.  Yang et al. treat faithful no-selection PAG-compatible SCMs; their edge semantics and the actual-parent, potentially unfaithful selected-MAG semantics here are generally incomparable.  Carey et al. characterize normalized selected-marginalized models, whereas the present margins constrain absolute natural and interventional selection probabilities.  Chen and Mooij give a sound-and-complete COPAG identification algorithm and calculus on specified model classes and distinguish \(\mathcal M(G)\) from the positive discrete class \(\mathcal M_d^+(\mathcal P)\); positivity of the selected observed law \(q\) here is not equated with all positivity conditions defining \(\mathcal M_d^+(\mathcal P)\).  Boeken, Forr\'e, and Mooij establish openness and density of faithful laws, which does not supply an equal-moment lift at an unfaithful boundary.  Basu supplies the real-algebraic sign-sampling and one-block elimination algorithms used for bounded-compiler certification.  Chernozhukov, Hong, and Tamer, Imbens and Manski, and Chang, Guo, and Malinsky motivate confidence-set inversion, while the formal inference result here is only finite-sample outer coverage.  Lee et al. are the clinical consumer: their survivor-conditioned cardiac-surgery analysis uses FCI and LV-IDA under acknowledged selection bias and graph ambiguity.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:positive-selected-law]\label{ass:positive-selected-law}
\(q(v)>0\) for every \(v\in\Omega_V\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Strict positivity of the selected observational kernel, \cite{ChenMooij2026sIDP}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:exact-copag-stratum]\label{ass:exact-copag-stratum}
\(G_S(M)\in\mathcal G(\mathcal P)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Exact selected-MAG membership in a COPAG equivalence class, \cite{ChenMooij2026sIDP}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:selected-law-fit]\label{ass:selected-law-fit}
\(c_v(M)=\rho q(v)\) for every \(v\in\Omega_V\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Compatibility with a selected multinomial law up to its common normalization, \cite{DuarteEtAl2024AutoBounds}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:population-selection-margin]\label{ass:population-selection-margin}
\(\delta\le\rho\le1\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} The lower bound is a calibrated sensitivity restriction on the population fraction entering the selected cohort. In survivor-selected studies it can be varied over values compatible with registry counts of eligible operations and observed discharge-alive records; it is not labeled as ordinary overlap or used merely to make a denominator positive.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:intervention-selection-margin]\label{ass:intervention-selection-margin}
\(d_M\ge\delta\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} The lower bound limits how much the intervention may reduce survival or ascertainment. It can be calibrated from a clinically defensible minimum discharge-alive rate under \(do(B=b)\), informed by external outcome surveillance or trial safety data; the mathematical domain requirement for \(n_M/d_M\) is only \(d_M>0\).
\end{assumption}

\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:moment-map]\label{def:moment-map}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Selected-\allowbreak{}law and intervention moment map}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\zeta(M)=((\Pr_M(V=v,S=1))_{v\in\Omega_V},\mathbb E_M[h(A^b)\mathbf 1\{S^b=1\}],\Pr_M(S^b=1))\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{M}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:architecture-list]\label{def:architecture-list}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Saturated ordered response-\allowbreak{}superarchitecture list}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For each topological order on \(W\), form one saturated superarchitecture containing every forward directed slot and one formal independent source \(U_C\) for every nonempty \(C\subseteq W\).  Thus at most \(A_{\max}=N!\) labeled superarchitectures are generated.  Every source has at most \(K_{\max}=m+3\) slots, including zero-mass slots, and every dense deterministic response table is enumerated, with at most \(R_{\max}\) table systems per order.  To embed a sparse architecture without loss, make each added directed input inessential.  Represent an absent source by a simplex vertex, with one slot of mass one and all remaining slots of mass zero, and make every incident table ignore it; an all-zero probability vector is never used.  This simplex-vertex convention is only a canonical embedding of a missing source and is not imposed on every parameter point having no active source incidence.  If a formally named \(U_C\) is active only on \(D\subsetneq C\), its realized child set is \(D\).  Before graph projection, discard empty realized child sets and merge formal sources with the same nonempty realized child set into one tuple source.  Retain precisely the exact projected selected MAGs \(G\in\mathcal G(\mathcal P)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{Pcal}; \texttt{W}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:graph-legality-stratum]\label{def:graph-legality-stratum}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Normalized exact graph-\allowbreak{}legality stratum}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For a saturated order \(T\), fixed deterministic tables, and masses \(p_T\), retain every directed sign \(Z_{\operatorname{dir}(i,j)}(T,p_T)\) and every formal source-incidence sign \(Z_{\operatorname{src}(C,j)}(T,p_T)\).  A directed slot is actual exactly when its sign is positive.  For each formal \(U_C\), define solely from its active incidence signs
\[
D_C(p_T)=\{j\in C:Z_{\operatorname{src}(C,j)}(T,p_T)>0\}.
\]
Discard a source with \(D_C(p_T)=\varnothing\) when forming the actual graph, without constraining its mass vector to a simplex vertex and without requiring its literal table entries to be constant: the zero incidence signs already say that any variation is functionally inessential outside exogenous null sets.  Replace every remaining formal label \(C\) by its realized nonempty child set \(D_C(p_T)\), and merge sources with equal realized child sets into one tuple source.  Apply the finite latent-and-selection projection rules to the resulting actual directed edges and normalized nonempty sources.  The formula \(\Psi_{T,G}(p_T)\) is the quantifier-free Boolean combination of the complete positive-or-zero incidence sign pattern and the finite projection test; it is true exactly when \(G_S(M_{T,p_T})=G\).  Every endogenous table comparison in \(Z_e\) ranges over all table inputs, including off-support endogenous assignments.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{T}; \texttt{pT}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:finite-legal-fiber]\label{def:finite-legal-fiber}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Finite graph-\allowbreak{}legal response fiber}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\mathfrak F_\delta(\mathcal P,q)=\bigcup_T\{(p_T,\rho):p_T\text{ satisfies the exact graph-legality stratum},\ \Phi_T(p_T)_{c_v}=\rho q(v)\text{ for every }v\in\Omega_V,\ \delta\le\rho\le1,\ \Phi_T(p_T)_d\ge\delta\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}architecture-\allowbreak{}list}; \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}graph-\allowbreak{}legality-\allowbreak{}stratum}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:certified-lift-procedure]\label{def:certified-lift-procedure}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Certified bounded finite legality procedure}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Generate the at most \(A_{\max}=N!\) saturated ordered superarchitectures, enumerate their dense deterministic response-table systems up to \(R_{\max}\), and expand every coordinate of \(\Phi_T\) and every \(Z_e\) coefficient by coefficient as a rational polynomial.  Introduce \(t\) through \(\Phi_T(p_T)_n-t\Phi_T(p_T)_d=0\), and form \(\mathsf{Lift}(t)\) as the finite disjunction over table systems and \(G\in\mathcal G(\mathcal P)\).  Use the one-block existential quantifier-elimination algorithm in \(\text{lem:basu-one-block-elimination}\) to report INFEASIBLE or to produce a univariate sign formula and isolate the algebraic infimum and supremum of the bounded finite image.  At an isolated closure endpoint, decide \(\mathsf{Lift}(t)\) using \(\text{lem:basu-sign-sampling}\).  The certificate \(\mathsf{Cert}(t)\) is either a real-univariate representation with Thom encoding of a satisfying legal response point, or, for every bounded table branch, a finite set of real-univariate sample points with complete sign lists meeting every semialgebraically connected component of every realizable sign condition and proving the branch empty.  A sign-invariant cylindrical decomposition may instead be retained as an optional certificate format, but it is not used for the intrinsic complexity bound.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}architecture-\allowbreak{}list}; \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}graph-\allowbreak{}legality-\allowbreak{}stratum}; \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}finite-\allowbreak{}legal-\allowbreak{}fiber}; \texttt{lem:\allowbreak{}basu-\allowbreak{}sign-\allowbreak{}sampling}; \texttt{lem:\allowbreak{}basu-\allowbreak{}one-\allowbreak{}block-\allowbreak{}elimination}.
\end{definition}

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\Theta_\delta(\mathcal P,q)=\{\theta(M):M\in\mathfrak M_\delta(\mathcal P,q)\}\), with the output INFEASIBLE when \(\mathfrak M_\delta(\mathcal P,q)=\varnothing\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}admissible-\allowbreak{}class}.
\end{definition}

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:essential-feature-iff]\label{lem:essential-feature-iff}
Fix an enumerated finite architecture \(T\), its deterministic tables, and source masses \(p_T\). For every \(i,j\in W\), the minimal structural graph has the directed edge \(i\to j\) if and only if \(Z_{\operatorname{dir}(i,j)}(T,p_T)>0\). For every nonempty child-set index \(C\subseteq W\) and \(j\in C\), its source \(U_C\) is an actual parent of \(j\) if and only if \(Z_{\operatorname{src}(C,j)}(T,p_T)>0\). The endogenous table comparisons include assignments off the observational support, while the exogenous contexts are integrated under \(p_T\). Therefore \(G_S(M_{T,p_T})=G\) if and only if \(\Psi_{T,G}(p_T)\), and this equivalence is unchanged by permutations of source values, response labels, or sources having the same child-set index.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix an enumerated saturated finite architecture \(T\), a deterministic table system in that architecture, and a point \(p_T\) in the product of source probability simplexes.  In particular, every source mass is nonnegative and the masses of each source sum to one.

First consider \(e=\operatorname{dir}(i,j)\).  By the definition of \(Z_e\), its finite sum can be indexed so that
\[
 Z_e(T,p_T)=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X_e} I_e(x)w_{p_T}(x),
 \qquad I_e(x)\in\{0,1\},
 \qquad w_{p_T}(x)\ge 0.
\]
Here \(x\) records an exogenous response context, \(w_{p_T}(x)\) is the corresponding monomial in the source masses and hence is the probability of that context, and \(I_e(x)=1\) exactly when there are two endogenous input assignments to the table of \(j\), differing only in the coordinate \(i\), at which the two outputs differ.  The comparison of endogenous inputs ranges over all table inputs, including assignments outside the observational support, by the definition of the graph-legality stratum; only the exogenous context is weighted by \(p_T\).

All summands in this finite sum are nonnegative.  Consequently,
\[
 Z_e(T,p_T)>0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \text{there is an }x\in\mathcal X_e\text{ such that }
 I_e(x)=1\text{ and }w_{p_T}(x)>0.
\]
Indeed, a positive summand implies positivity of the sum, while positivity of a finite sum of nonnegative terms implies that at least one summand is positive.  Since \(w_{p_T}(x)>0\) means that \(x\) is a positive-probability exogenous context, the condition on the right is precisely actual functional dependence of the structural equation for \(j\) on the endogenous input \(i\), with dependence tested over all endogenous assignments.  By the definition of the minimal structural graph used in \(G_S\),
\[
 i\longrightarrow j\text{ is an edge of the minimal structural graph}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 Z_{\operatorname{dir}(i,j)}(T,p_T)>0.
\]

Now fix a nonempty \(C\subseteq W\), a vertex \(j\in C\), and \(e=\operatorname{src}(C,j)\).  Expanding its defining polynomial gives
\[
 Z_e(T,p_T)
 =\sum_{(u,u',x)\in\mathcal Y_e}
 J_e(u,u',x)\,p_C(u)p_C(u')w_{-C,p_T}(x),
\]
where \(u\ne u'\) are two slots of \(U_C\), \(x\) is a context of all other sources, \(w_{-C,p_T}(x)\ge0\) is its probability, and \(J_e(u,u',x)=1\) exactly when, at one common endogenous input assignment and the common other-source context \(x\), the table for \(j\) gives different outputs at \(U_C=u\) and \(U_C=u'\).  Again all terms are nonnegative, so
\[
 Z_e(T,p_T)>0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \begin{gathered}
 \text{there exist }u\ne u'\text{ and }x\text{ with }
 p_C(u)>0,\ p_C(u')>0,\ w_{-C,p_T}(x)>0,\\
 \text{and }J_e(u,u',x)=1.
 \end{gathered}
\]
The right-hand side is exactly actual functional dependence of the equation for \(j\) on the exogenous source \(U_C\), outside exogenous null sets.  Hence
\[
 U_C\text{ is an actual parent of }j
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 Z_{\operatorname{src}(C,j)}(T,p_T)>0.
\]
In particular, source values of zero mass cannot create an actual edge.

It follows that the complete positive-or-zero sign pattern indexed by \(\mathcal I_T\) recovers every directed edge and every source-to-child incidence of the minimal structural graph.  For each formal source \(U_C\), the definition
\[
 D_C(p_T)=\{j\in C:Z_{\operatorname{src}(C,j)}(T,p_T)>0\}
\]
therefore gives exactly its actual child set.  If \(D_C(p_T)=\varnothing\), that source has no actual child and may be discarded from the graph even when its mass vector is nondegenerate or its literal table entries vary only on null contexts.  No simplex-vertex condition is needed for this implication.  If distinct formal sources have the same nonempty realized child set \(D\), replacing them by their tuple source preserves their joint product law, lets each structural table unpack the same source coordinates, and has source-to-child incidences exactly \(D\).  Thus this merging preserves the induced SCM law, the normalized incidence pattern, and the latent projection, although it replaces several redundant latent vertices by one tuple vertex.  Discarding the empty realized sources and merging equal nonempty realized child sets therefore do not alter the subsequent selected-MAG projection.

The latent-and-selection projection is a deterministic finite operation on this normalized actual graph.  By the definition of \(\Psi_{T,G}\), it applies exactly that normalization and projection to the incidence pattern recovered above.  Therefore
\[
 G_S(M_{T,p_T})=G
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \Psi_{T,G}(p_T).
\]

Finally, a simultaneous permutation of source-value labels and their masses bijects the indices \(u,u'\) in the source-feature sums; a permutation of response labels bijects the corresponding response contexts; and a permutation of sources with the same realized child-set index permutes coordinates of the tuple source.  Each operation merely reindexes the displayed finite sums and leaves every equality \(Z_e=0\) and inequality \(Z_e>0\) unchanged after the corresponding label transport.  The normalized realized child sets and their projected selected MAG are consequently unchanged.  This proves all assertions.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This lemma makes actual-edge legality a complete finite polynomial test rather than an informal list of permitted slots. \textit{Gap.} Duarte et al. encode a supplied graph, and Yang et al. encode PAG masks; neither gives an invariant if-and-only-if polynomial test for the exact selected MAG with off-support functional dependence. \textit{Consumer.} The certified bounding procedure uses the equivalence to reject response programs whose zero-mass limits silently delete a parent or confounding source.

\begin{theorem}[thm:finite-stratum-compiler]\label{thm:finite-stratum-compiler}
For each of the at most \(N!\) topological orders on \(W\), one saturated superarchitecture containing every forward directed slot and one potential source \(U_C\) for every nonempty \(C\subseteq W\) contains every finite retained-variable response architecture having that order: unused directed inputs are made inessential, and an absent source has a canonical embedding as a simplex-vertex source ignored by every incident table.  Sources with the same realized nonempty child set can be merged without changing any structural solution, so at most \(H_{\max}=2^N-1\) active independent sources are needed.  On any fixed permitted architecture, sourcewise barycentric reduction preserves \(\zeta(M)\) with at most \(K_{\max}=m+3\) values per source, after which the model embeds in its saturated ordered superarchitecture and its dense deterministic tables are covered by \(R_{\max}\).  Every point of \(\mathfrak F_\delta(\mathcal P,q)\) induces an SCM in \(\mathfrak M_\delta(\mathcal P,q)\), and the target-value image of \(\mathfrak F_\delta(\mathcal P,q)\) is exactly the intervention-value set of the enumerated bounded finite SCMs satisfying actual selected-MAG legality.  Sourcewise barycentric reduction is asserted to preserve moments, not strict legality; no assertion identifies the bounded image with the image of all arbitrary admissible SCMs.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We prove the assertions in four steps.

First fix a topological order of \(W\).  By \(\text{def:architecture-list}\), its saturated superarchitecture contains every forward directed argument and every formal source \(U_C\), \(\varnothing\ne C\subseteq W\).  Consider any finite retained-variable response architecture having this order.  Replace each missing directed argument by a table argument on which the corresponding structural function is constant.  If a source is absent, put its mass vector at a simplex vertex, with one slot of mass one and all other slots of mass zero, and make every incident table constant in that source argument.  Thus no all-zero mass vector is used.  If a source has actual child set \(D\), install its law and table arguments in the formal slot \(U_D\), after applying the merging operation proved next if necessary.  These extensions change no effective structural equation and hence change no factual or interventional solution.  Every formerly sparse finite architecture therefore embeds in the saturated architecture for its order.  There are at most \(N!\) orders.

We next justify source merging and the realized-child-set convention.  Group mutually independent exogenous sources according to their nonempty sets of actual retained children.  For each \(\varnothing\ne C\subseteq W\), let \(U_C\) be the tuple of all original sources with actual retained child set \(C\), equipped with the product law inherited from their mutual independence.  Tuples for distinct \(C\)'s use disjoint original coordinates and hence remain mutually independent.  Substitute the tuple coordinates into the old structural equations.  Evaluation in topological order proves, node by node, that every structural solution under every intervention is unchanged.  A retained equation depends essentially on the tuple precisely when it depends essentially on at least one tuple coordinate.  One direction is immediate.  For the converse, join two tuple values by changing one coordinate at a time; if the endpoint outputs differ, the output changes at some step.  Thus tuple merging preserves the minimal structural graph.  Sources with no actual retained child may be discarded.  There are exactly \(2^N-1=H_{\max}\) nonempty subsets of \(W\), establishing the active-source bound.

For an arbitrary parameter point of a saturated superarchitecture, define
\[
D_C(p_T)=\{j\in C:Z_{\operatorname{src}(C,j)}(T,p_T)>0\}.
\]
By \(\text{lem:essential-feature-iff}\), \(D_C(p_T)\) is exactly the actual retained child set of the formal source \(U_C\).  Consequently a formal source with empty \(D_C(p_T)\) is absent from the minimal structural graph and is discarded for projection, even if its mass vector is nondegenerate or its literal table varies only on null exogenous contexts.  No normalization constraint is imposed on that parameter point.  When \(D_C(p_T)\ne\varnothing\), relabel the source by \(D_C(p_T)\); tuple-merging all formal sources with the same realized nonempty child set preserves solutions and the minimal graph by the preceding paragraph.  Therefore the corrected formula \(\Psi_{T,G}(p_T)\), which projects only after empty-set deletion and equal-set merging, is equivalent to \(G_S(M_{T,p_T})=G\).  This is an interpretation of the active incidence signs, not an extra restriction on source masses or tables.

Second, fix any permitted response architecture with independent sources \(U_1,\ldots,U_H\), where \(H\leq H_{\max}\).  Define
\[
F(U_1,\ldots,U_H)=
\left(
  (\mathbf 1\{V=v,S=1\})_{v\in\Omega_V},
  h(A^b)\mathbf 1\{S^b=1\},
  \mathbf 1\{S^b=1\}
\right)\in\mathbb R^{m+2}.
\]
The boundedness of \(h\) and finiteness of \(\Omega_V\) make \(F\) integrable, and \(\mathbb E_M F=\zeta(M)\) by \(\text{def:moment-map}\).  Hold all source laws except that of \(U_k\) fixed and define
\[
g_k(u)=
\int F(u_1,\ldots,u_{k-1},u,u_{k+1},\ldots,u_H)
      \prod_{r\ne k}dP_r(u_r).
\]
Mutual independence and iterated integration give
\[
\zeta(M)=\int g_k(u)\,dP_k(u).
\]

We record the elementary barycenter argument used here.  If an integrable random vector \(Y\) takes values in \(\mathbb R^d\), then \(\mathbb EY\) belongs to the convex hull of its essential range.  Approximation by integrable simple random vectors first places \(\mathbb EY\) in the closure of that convex hull.  If it were not in the convex hull itself, then it would lie on the relative boundary of the closed convex hull: a point in the relative interior of a convex set's closure is in the relative interior of the convex set.  A supporting affine functional at that boundary is nonnegative relative to its value at \(\mathbb EY\).  Its nonnegative difference has expectation zero and hence vanishes almost surely.  Restricting the essential range to the resulting proper affine hyperplane and inducting on affine dimension proves convex-hull membership.  By the definition of convex hull there is then a finite convex representation.  If it uses more than \(d+1\) points, an affine dependence among them permits changing the weights, while preserving their sum and barycenter, until one weight vanishes.  Iteration leaves at most \(d+1\) actual essential-range values.

Apply this argument with \(d=m+2\).  There are \(L_k\leq m+3=K_{\max}\), values \(u_{k,1},\ldots,u_{k,L_k}\), and weights \(w_{k,r}\geq0\) such that
\[
\sum_{r=1}^{L_k}w_{k,r}=1,
\qquad
\sum_{r=1}^{L_k}w_{k,r}g_k(u_{k,r})
=\int g_k(u)\,dP_k(u).
\]
Replacing only \(P_k\) by this discrete law preserves \(\zeta(M)\) and product factorization.  Repeat for \(k=1,\ldots,H\), always using the currently installed laws of the other sources.  Every replacement preserves the whole moment vector, so the terminal model still has moment vector \(\zeta(M)\) and every source has at most \(K_{\max}\) values.  In particular,
\[
\rho=\sum_{v\in\Omega_V}c_v(M)
\]
is preserved as one common normalization.  Since retained state spaces and reduced source supports are finite, every structural function is a deterministic response table.  Embedding the reduced architecture in its saturated order only adds inessential directed inputs and canonical simplex-vertex ignored sources, so its dense table system is among those counted by \(R_{\max}\).  This reduction does not assert that any strict inequality \(Z_e>0\) survives.

Third, take \((p_T,\rho)\in\mathfrak F_\delta(\mathcal P,q)\).  Its saturated topological order, deterministic tables, and mutually independent source simplexes define a recursive finite SCM \(M_{T,p_T}\).  The graph-legality stratum determines active incidence signs, discards empty realized source child sets, and merges duplicates only for interpreting the actual graph.  The essential-feature lemma gives a \(G\in\mathcal G(\mathcal P)\) such that
\[
G_S(M_{T,p_T})=G.
\]
The remaining constraints in \(\text{def:finite-legal-fiber}\) give, for each \(v\in\Omega_V\),
\[
c_v(M_{T,p_T})=\Phi_T(p_T)_{c_v}=\rho q(v),
\qquad
\delta\leq\rho\leq1,
\qquad
d_{M_{T,p_T}}=\Phi_T(p_T)_d\geq\delta.
\]
Together with \(\text{ass:positive-selected-law}\), these are exactly the member conditions in \(\text{def:admissible-class}\).  Hence \(M_{T,p_T}\in\mathfrak M_\delta(\mathcal P,q)\).  Since \(\delta>0\), its denominator is positive and
\[
\theta(M_{T,p_T})=
\frac{\Phi_T(p_T)_n}{\Phi_T(p_T)_d}
\]
is well defined.

Finally, every enumerated bounded finite SCM with actual selected MAG in \(\mathcal G(\mathcal P)\) has a topological order and embeds in the corresponding saturated superarchitecture by the first step.  Its deterministic table system is counted by \(R_{\max}\), its product source masses define \(p_T\), and \(\rho=\sum_vc_v\).  Selected-law fit and the two margins put \((p_T,\rho)\) in \(\mathfrak F_\delta(\mathcal P,q)\), while the essential-feature lemma selects its exact graph branch.  The two inclusions prove equality of the two bounded finite target-value images.  None of these steps proves the legal lifting asked for in \(\text{oeq:universal-legal-lifting}\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The compiler gives a maximal finite response language with one saturated superarchitecture per order while keeping moment reduction logically separate from graph-legal lifting. \textit{Gap.} Source merging and sourcewise cardinality reduction preserve structural solutions or selected moments, but do not by themselves preserve strict functional-essentiality witnesses under support deletion. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies an auditable bounded candidate language for the Lee et al. FCI and LV-IDA setting without treating that language as the full arbitrary-SCM class.

\begin{theorem}[thm:certified-attainment-procedure]\label{thm:certified-attainment-procedure}
For every finite rational instance, the certified procedure uses at most \(A_{\max}=N!\) saturated ordered superarchitectures and, for each order, at most \(R_{\max}\) dense deterministic response-table systems.  It expands the moment and essential-feature polynomials coefficient by coefficient and reports INFEASIBLE or isolates the algebraic infimum and supremum of the bounded finite graph-legal image.  At either isolated closure endpoint \(t\), it decides \(\mathsf{Lift}(t)\).  If true, \(\mathsf{Cert}(t)\) contains a branch \((T,G)\) and a real-univariate representation with Thom encoding of algebraic \(p_T,\rho\) whose exact graph is verified by \(\Psi_{T,G}\).  If false, \(\mathsf{Cert}(t)\) contains, for every enumerated bounded branch, real-univariate sample points and their complete sign lists meeting every semialgebraically connected component of every realizable sign condition and proving that the branch is empty; a sign-invariant cylindrical decomposition is an optional alternative certificate.  For one existential block of \(p_{\mathrm{dim}}\) response parameters and one free endpoint variable, with \(s_{\mathrm{count}}\) input polynomials, maximum degree \(D_{\mathrm{deg}}\), and expanded integer coefficient bit length \(\tau_{\mathrm{eff}}\), block elimination uses at most
\[
A_{\max}R_{\max}s_{\mathrm{count}}^{\,2(p_{\mathrm{dim}}+1)}D_{\mathrm{deg}}^{O(p_{\mathrm{dim}})}
\]
ring operations, produces univariate output degree \(D_{\mathrm{deg}}^{O(p_{\mathrm{dim}})}\), and has intermediate and output integer bit lengths \(\tau_{\mathrm{eff}}D_{\mathrm{deg}}^{O(p_{\mathrm{dim}})}\).  In coarse bit-operation notation the bound is
\[
A_{\max}R_{\max}(s_{\mathrm{count}}D_{\mathrm{deg}})^{O(p_{\mathrm{dim}})}\operatorname{poly}(\tau_{\mathrm{eff}}).
\]
A negative endpoint certificate excludes only the enumerated bounded finite compiler branches and does not resolve universal legal lifting for arbitrary admissible SCMs.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix one saturated order and one deterministic response-table system.  Let its \(H\leq H_{\max}\) formal source-mass vectors be \(p_1,\ldots,p_H\).  For every coordinate \(y\) of the moment map there are rational table coefficients \(a_y(u_1,\ldots,u_H)\) for which
\[
\Phi_T(p_T)_y
=\sum_{u_1,\ldots,u_H}
  a_y(u_1,\ldots,u_H)\prod_{k=1}^H p_k(u_k).
\]
For a selected cell or \(d\), each coefficient is zero or one; for \(n\), it is either zero or a value of the rational map \(h\).  Thus every moment coordinate is a rational polynomial of degree at most \(H\).  By \(\text{lem:essential-feature-iff}\), a directed-feature polynomial integrates a zero-one table indicator over at most one copy of every source and has degree at most \(H\).  A source-feature polynomial uses two values of its own source and at most one value of every other source, so its degree is at most \(H+1\).  Simplex normalization and nonnegativity, the equations
\[
\Phi_T(p_T)_{c_v}=\rho q(v)\qquad(v\in\Omega_V),
\]
the inequalities \(\delta\leq\rho\leq1\) and \(\Phi_T(p_T)_d\geq\delta\), the complete active-incidence sign conditions used by \(\Psi_{T,G}\), and
\[
\Phi_T(p_T)_n-t\Phi_T(p_T)_d=0
\]
are consequently a finite Boolean combination of rational polynomial sign conditions.  The realized child sets \(D_C\), deletion of empty sets, merging of equal nonempty sets, and selected projection depend only on that finite complete sign pattern.  They impose no extra equation requiring an inessential formal source to be degenerate or its table to be literally constant.  The declared rationality of \(q\), \(\delta\), and \(h\) permits clearing positive common denominators without changing any sign.  By definition, \(s_{\mathrm{count}}\), \(D_{\mathrm{deg}}\), and \(\tau_{\mathrm{eff}}\) bound, respectively, the number, degree, and expanded integer coefficient length of the resulting polynomials.

The saturated architecture definition gives at most \(A_{\max}=N!\) orders.  For a fixed order, every retained equation has all forward directed arguments and every potentially incident source argument, so the dense deterministic table enumeration is bounded by \(R_{\max}\).  A sparse architecture has a canonical embedding using inessential added directed inputs and simplex-vertex ignored absent sources, but the table branch itself ranges over every enumerated mass vector and table.  Absence and activity for an arbitrary parameter point are read only from the essential-feature signs.  Thus missing arrows and sources need not be enumerated as separate architectures, and there are at most \(A_{\max}R_{\max}\) table branches.

For each branch, all response parameters, including \(\rho\), form one existential block of size at most \(p_{\mathrm{dim}}\), while \(t\) is one free variable.  Apply \(\text{lem:basu-one-block-elimination}\), namely Basu's Theorem 2.27 and its preceding block-elimination discussion, with \(\omega=1\), \(k_1=p_{\mathrm{dim}}\), and \(\ell=1\).  It returns an equivalent quantifier-free formula in \(t\).  Its ring-operation cost is
\[
s_{\mathrm{count}}^{\,2(p_{\mathrm{dim}}+1)}
D_{\mathrm{deg}}^{O(p_{\mathrm{dim}})},
\]
the output degree is \(D_{\mathrm{deg}}^{O(p_{\mathrm{dim}})}\), and integer intermediate and output bit lengths are bounded by
\[
\tau_{\mathrm{eff}}D_{\mathrm{deg}}^{O(p_{\mathrm{dim}})}.
\]
Multiplication by the number of table branches gives the displayed ring-operation bound.  Exact integer arithmetic on operands of the displayed bit lengths, together with exact univariate root isolation, is polynomial in those bit lengths and in the univariate output size; it is therefore absorbed in the coarser bit-operation expression
\[
A_{\max}R_{\max}
(s_{\mathrm{count}}D_{\mathrm{deg}})^{O(p_{\mathrm{dim}})}
\operatorname{poly}(\tau_{\mathrm{eff}}).
\]
Theorem 2.18 of the cited survey is a bound on connected components, not a quantifier-elimination theorem, and is not used.

The strict feature inequalities remain strict in the formula, so its existential projection is the exact bounded branch of \(\mathsf{Lift}(t)\), not the closure of that branch.  If no projected branch is nonempty, the procedure reports INFEASIBLE.  Otherwise every feasible endpoint variable is bounded.  Indeed, pointwise on \(\{S^b=1\}\),
\[
h_-\leq h(A^b)\leq h_+,
\]
and multiplication by \(\mathbf 1\{S^b=1\}\) followed by expectation gives
\[
h_-d\leq n\leq h_+d.
\]
The intervention margin gives \(d\geq\delta>0\); therefore the endpoint equation implies
\[
h_-\leq t=\frac nd\leq h_+.
\]
The projected set is a bounded univariate semialgebraic set.  Between consecutive real roots of its finitely many output polynomials every sign is constant.  Its infimum and supremum must therefore be either \(h_-\), \(h_+\), or a real root of an output polynomial.  Hence they are real algebraic, have degree \(D_{\mathrm{deg}}^{O(p_{\mathrm{dim}})}\), and can be isolated by a Thom encoding.

Fix either isolated endpoint \(t_0\), and regard its Thom encoding as specifying the ordered real algebraic coefficient extension obtained after substitution.  Apply \(\text{lem:basu-sign-sampling}\), Basu's Theorem 2.16 with Definitions 2.12--2.13, to the branch polynomials.  Specializing its algebraic-set polynomial to the zero polynomial makes the ambient zero set the full \(p_{\mathrm{dim}}\)-dimensional response space.  It produces a finite set of real-univariate sample points meeting every semialgebraically connected component of every realizable sign condition.  Asking also for all signs costs
\[
s_{\mathrm{count}}^{\,p_{\mathrm{dim}}+1}
D_{\mathrm{deg}}^{O(p_{\mathrm{dim}})}
\]
ring operations, which is dominated by the preceding one-block elimination bound after enlarging the absolute constants.

If some sample sign vector satisfies all simplex, moment, margin, endpoint, and graph-sign clauses, its real-univariate representation and Thom encoding give algebraic \(p_T,\rho\).  By \(\text{lem:essential-feature-iff}\), the signs in \(\Psi_{T,G}\) verify
\[
G_S(M_{T,p_T})=G\in\mathcal G(\mathcal P),
\]
so this is the asserted positive certificate.

Conversely, suppose no sample sign vector satisfies a given branch.  If a response point satisfied it, that point would lie in a semialgebraically connected component of a realizable sign condition of the branch polynomials.  The sample set meets that component, and the defining Boolean formula depends only on the complete sign vector; its sample would therefore satisfy the same branch, a contradiction.  Hence the branch is empty.  Collecting these sample representations and sign lists for all finitely many branches proves \(\neg\mathsf{Lift}(t_0)\).  A full sign-invariant cylindrical decomposition may be retained instead, but its potentially doubly exponential construction is not the intrinsic complexity asserted here.  Finally, every branch quantified in this argument belongs to the bounded finite compiler.  Thus a negative certificate excludes all and only those enumerated bounded branches; without an affirmative resolution of \(\text{oeq:universal-legal-lifting}\), it does not exclude an equal-moment model in the arbitrary-SCM superclass.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The procedure provides exact endpoint membership or bounded-branch exclusion data at singly exponential one-block complexity. \textit{Gap.} Fixed-graph polynomial optimization does not enumerate exact selected-MAG sign strata or distinguish an attained bounded endpoint from a graph-deleting closure value. \textit{Consumer.} The retained certificate distinguishes an attained bounded-model mechanism from a bounded-compiler closure artifact, while its scope warning prevents extrapolation to arbitrary SCMs.

\begin{openendedquestion}[oeq:universal-legal-lifting]\label{oeq:universal-legal-lifting}
Does \(q(v)>0\) for every \(v\in\Omega_V\), together with the calibrated restrictions \(\rho\ge\delta\) and \(d_M\ge\delta\), imply both that every moment vector of an arbitrary admissible SCM has an equal-moment representative in the bounded finite legal fiber and that every limiting vector of bounded graph-legal moments has an equal-moment finite SCM whose actual selected MAG remains in \(\mathcal G(\mathcal P)\)? Resolve first the binary three-observed-variable COPAG with a compelled collider by stratifying the invariant essential-feature polynomials \(Z_e\). An affirmative resolution must give a legal lift for every boundary stratum; a negative resolution must give a positive rational \(q\), a rational \(\delta\), and an algebraic endpoint for which the negative certificate \(\mathsf{Cert}(t)\) excludes every legal architecture.
\end{openendedquestion}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This remains the sole nonroutine topological bottleneck between exact actual-parent graph semantics and attained extrema for the full arbitrary-SCM fiber. \textit{Gap.} The isolated-selection compelled-collider subcase has a legal lift, but neither faithfulness-density results nor saturated finite architecture enumeration settles selected graphs with latent or selection ancestry. \textit{Consumer.} Its resolution determines whether arbitrary-SCM selection-COPAG bounds may be reported as attained; until then, only bounded-image certificates and subclass attainment have that interpretation.

\begin{theorem}[thm:compact-algebraic-fiber]\label{thm:compact-algebraic-fiber}
If the universal legal-lifting property in Open-ended Question 1 holds, then every nonempty \(\Theta_\delta(\mathcal P,q)\) is a compact semialgebraic subset of \([h_-,h_+]\), hence a finite union of closed intervals and points; \(\ell\) and \(u\) are attained algebraic numbers and admit finite response-SCM witnesses. With \(H_{\max}=2^N-1\), source support bounded by \(K_{\max}=m+3\), parameter count \(p_{\mathrm{dim}}=O(H_{\max}K_{\max})\), constraint count \(s_{\mathrm{count}}=O(m+H_{\max}K_{\max}+NH_{\max}+N^3)\), degree \(D_{\mathrm{deg}}=O(H_{\max}+N)\), and expanded coefficient bit length \(\tau_{\mathrm{eff}}\), cylindrical algebraic decomposition gives the per-architecture factor \((s_{\mathrm{count}}D_{\mathrm{deg}})^{2^{O(p_{\mathrm{dim}})}}\operatorname{poly}(\tau_{\mathrm{eff}})\) in the certified procedure.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Assume the affirmative universal legal-lifting property posed in \(\text{oeq:universal-legal-lifting}\).  Thus (i) every moment vector of an arbitrary member of \(\mathfrak M_\delta(\mathcal P,q)\) has an equal-moment representative in the bounded finite legal fiber, and (ii) every limit of moment vectors from that bounded fiber has an equal-moment finite SCM whose actual selected MAG belongs to \(\mathcal G(\mathcal P)\).  Define the bounded-fiber moment image
\[
 \mathcal Z_{\mathrm b}
 =\left\{\Phi_T(p_T):
   \text{for some enumerated branch and some }\rho,
   (p_T,\rho)\in\mathfrak F_\delta(\mathcal P,q)
  \right\}\subseteq\mathbb R^{m+2}.
\]
Write a member as \(z=((c_v)_{v\in\Omega_V},n,d)\).  By \(\text{def:finite-legal-fiber}\), every such member satisfies
\[
 0\le c_v\le1,\qquad 0\le d\le1,
 \qquad c_v=\rho q(v),\qquad \delta\le\rho\le1,
 \qquad d\ge\delta.
\]
Since \(h_-\le h(A^b)\le h_+\), multiplication by the nonnegative indicator \(\mathbf 1\{S^b=1\}\) and expectation give
\[
 h_-d
 \le \mathbb E[h(A^b)\mathbf 1\{S^b=1\}]
 =n
 \le h_+d.
\]
In particular, \(|n|\le\max\{|h_-|,|h_+|\}\), so \(\mathcal Z_{\mathrm b}\) is bounded.

We next show that \(\mathcal Z_{\mathrm b}\) is closed; this is the step for which the second lifting clause is essential, because a fixed exact-graph stratum can contain strict inequalities.  Let \(z^{(r)}\in\mathcal Z_{\mathrm b}\) and suppose \(z^{(r)}\to z=((c_v)_v,n,d)\).  For each \(r\), let \(\rho_r\) be the common normalization in \(\text{def:finite-legal-fiber}\).  Since \(q\) is a probability mass function,
\[
 \rho_r
 =\rho_r\sum_{v\in\Omega_V}q(v)
 =\sum_{v\in\Omega_V}c_v^{(r)}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \rho_r\longrightarrow
 \rho:=\sum_{v\in\Omega_V}c_v,
 \qquad \delta\le\rho\le1,
 \qquad c_v=\rho q(v)\quad(v\in\Omega_V),
 \qquad d\ge\delta.
\]
The second lifting clause supplies a finite SCM \(M_0\) such that \(\zeta(M_0)=z\) and \(G_S(M_0)\in\mathcal G(\mathcal P)\).  The preceding displayed equalities and inequalities show, respectively, selected-law fit and both margin conditions for \(M_0\).  Hence \(M_0\in\mathfrak M_\delta(\mathcal P,q)\) by \(\text{def:admissible-class}\).  Applying the first lifting clause to \(M_0\) yields an equal-moment representative in \(\mathfrak F_\delta(\mathcal P,q)\).  Therefore \(z\in\mathcal Z_{\mathrm b}\), proving closedness.  Closedness and boundedness in the finite-dimensional space \(\mathbb R^{m+2}\) imply that \(\mathcal Z_{\mathrm b}\) is compact.

The first lifting clause and \(\text{thm:finite-stratum-compiler}\) give both inclusions in
\[
 \{\zeta(M):M\in\mathfrak M_\delta(\mathcal P,q)\}
 =\mathcal Z_{\mathrm b}:
\]
the first clause maps every arbitrary admissible SCM into the right-hand side without changing its moments, while the compiler maps every bounded legal response point to an admissible SCM.  Because \(d\ge\delta>0\), the map
\[
 ((c_v)_v,n,d)\longmapsto \frac nd
\]
is continuous on \(\mathcal Z_{\mathrm b}\).  The definition of \(\theta(M)\) and \(\text{def:sharp-compatibility-fiber}\) therefore yield
\[
 \Theta_\delta(\mathcal P,q)
 =\left\{\frac nd:((c_v)_v,n,d)\in\mathcal Z_{\mathrm b}\right\}.
\]
Thus a nonempty \(\Theta_\delta(\mathcal P,q)\) is compact.  The earlier inequality \(h_-d\le n\le h_+d\), divided by the strictly positive \(d\), also proves
\[
 \Theta_\delta(\mathcal P,q)\subseteq[h_-,h_+].
\]

For semialgebraicity, fix an enumerated order, deterministic table system, and selected MAG.  By \(\text{def:graph-legality-stratum}\) and \(\text{def:finite-legal-fiber}\), that branch is specified by a finite Boolean combination of rational polynomial equalities and strict or weak sign conditions.  For a real variable \(t\), the additional polynomial equation
\[
 \Phi_T(p_T)_n-t\Phi_T(p_T)_d=0
\]
is equivalent to \(t=n/d\), because the same branch imposes \(d\ge\delta>0\).  There are finitely many branches, and the block-elimination conclusion in \(\text{thm:certified-attainment-procedure}\) therefore gives a quantifier-free univariate polynomial sign formula defining exactly the bounded-fiber target image.  By the equality of images proved above, this formula defines \(\Theta_\delta(\mathcal P,q)\).  The finitely many real roots of its nonzero polynomials partition the line into finitely many root points and open intervals on each of which every polynomial sign, and hence membership, is constant.  Compactness includes every finite boundary point of an included component.  It follows that \(\Theta_\delta(\mathcal P,q)\) is a finite union of closed intervals and points.

All coefficients entering the branch formulas are rational.  The endpoint-isolation conclusion of \(\text{thm:certified-attainment-procedure}\) consequently shows that the infimum and supremum of the bounded finite image are real algebraic numbers.  Since the image is now compact and agrees with the full fiber, its outer endpoints \(\ell\) and \(u\) belong to the image.  Hence \(\mathsf{Lift}(\ell)\) and \(\mathsf{Lift}(u)\) are true.  At either value, the positive-certificate clause of \(\text{thm:certified-attainment-procedure}\) returns a legal branch and a real-univariate representation with Thom encoding of algebraic \(p_T\) and \(\rho\).  The encoded response point induces, by \(\text{thm:finite-stratum-compiler}\), a finite response SCM in \(\mathfrak M_\delta(\mathcal P,q)\) attaining that endpoint.  This proves algebraicity, attainment, and the asserted witness property.

It remains to verify the displayed coarse size bounds.  Consider one saturated order and write \(H\le H_{\max}\) for its number of formal source slots and \(K\le K_{\max}\) for their maximum support.  Eliminating one mass per normalized source simplex leaves at most \(H(K-1)\) source-mass variables; adjoining the common normalization \(\rho\) changes this by one.  Therefore
\[
 p_{\mathrm{dim}}=O(H_{max}K_{max}).
\]
There are \(O(HK)\) simplex equations and sign conditions, \(m\) selected-cell equations, and only constantly many margin and endpoint conditions.  The invariant index in \(\text{def:graph-legality-stratum}\) contains \(O(N^2)\) directed-incidence tests and
\[
 \sum_{\varnothing\ne C\subseteq W}|C|
 =N2^{N-1}
 \le NH_{\max}
\]
source-incidence tests.  The finite latent-and-selection projection is precomputed for each sign pattern; allowing the stated conservative \(O(N^3)\) bookkeeping term gives
\[
 s_{\mathrm{count}}
 =O\!\left(m+H_{\max}K_{\max}+NH_{\max}+N^3\right).
\]
A moment monomial selects at most one mass from each independent source and hence has degree at most \(H\).  A source-essentiality witness may select two masses from the distinguished source and one from each other source, and the equation involving the free endpoint variable adds at most one to total degree.  Thus every polynomial used by the branch has degree at most \(H+1\), and in particular the advertised conservative bound is
\[
 D_{\mathrm{deg}}=O(H_{\max}+N).
\]
Substitution in the one-block bound of \(\text{thm:certified-attainment-procedure}\) gives the stronger intrinsic per-architecture estimate
\[
 s_{\mathrm{count}}^{\,2(p_{\mathrm{dim}}+1)}
 D_{\mathrm{deg}}^{O(p_{\mathrm{dim}})}
 \operatorname{poly}(\tau_{\mathrm{eff}}).
\]
After replacing \(s_{\mathrm{count}}D_{\mathrm{deg}}\) by its maximum with \(2\), this is bounded above by the deliberately coarser optional sign-invariant cylindrical-decomposition envelope
\[
 (s_{\mathrm{count}}D_{\mathrm{deg}})^{2^{O(p_{\mathrm{dim}})}}
 \operatorname{poly}(\tau_{\mathrm{eff}}).
\]
Thus the displayed cylindrical-decomposition factor in the statement is valid, while the certified procedure in fact enjoys the sharper one-block existential bound already recorded in \(\text{thm:certified-attainment-procedure}\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Compactness converts the algebraic compiler into sharp, attained causal conclusions and explicit certificates. \textit{Gap.} Rosset, Gisin, and Wolfe derive closed semialgebraic correlation sets after cardinality reduction, but exact selected-MAG inequalities and shared selection normalization are absent. \textit{Consumer.} Attained algebraic witnesses allow auditors to display concrete best- and worst-case mechanisms rather than numerical optimizer traces.

\begin{theorem}[thm:saturated-closed-form]\label{thm:saturated-closed-form}
If \(\mathcal P\) is the complete all-circle COPAG, every observed variable has at least two states, and \(|\Omega_B|\ge2\), then \(\Theta_\delta(\mathcal P,q)=[L_\delta,U_\delta]\), where \(L_\delta\) and \(U_\delta\) are given by the closed-form saturated envelope. Both endpoints are attained by a one-source SCM with at most \(m+1\) positive-mass response types. If \(h\) is constant, the interval is the singleton \(\{h_-\}\); if the intervention is vacuous, the fiber is the singleton \(\{\sum_{v\in\Omega_V}q(v)h(A(v))\}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply \(\text{thm:saturated-envelope-transfer}\) to the complete all-circle COPAG in the theorem.  Its graph-free implication applies to every \(M\in\mathfrak M_\delta(\mathcal P,q)\), because \(\text{def:admissible-class}\) supplies a common \(\rho\in[\delta,1]\) with \(c_v(M)=\rho q(v)\) for all \(v\), as well as \(d_M\geq\delta\).  Therefore
\[
\Theta_\delta(\mathcal P,q)\subseteq[L_\delta,U_\delta]. \tag{1}
\]
The same transfer theorem constructs, using one exogenous source, models \(M_t\in\mathfrak M_\delta(\mathcal P,q)\) satisfying
\[
\theta(M_t)=(1-t)L_\delta+tU_\delta,
\qquad 0\leq t\leq1. \tag{2}
\]
Equation (2) proves the reverse inclusion in (1).  It also states that the endpoint models \(M_0\) and \(M_1\) have at most \(m+1\) positive-mass response types.  Hence
\[
\Theta_\delta(\mathcal P,q)=[L_\delta,U_\delta]
\]
with both endpoints attained as claimed.

If \(h\) is constant, then \(h_-=h_+\) and, for every compatible model,
\[
n_M=\mathbb E_M\!\left[h_-\mathbf 1\{S^b=1\}\right]=h_-d_M.
\]
The margin \(d_M\geq\delta>0\) permits division, giving \(\theta(M)=h_-\).  The witness construction in \(\text{thm:saturated-envelope-transfer}\) proves nonemptiness under the nonvacuous hypotheses, so the fiber is \(\{h_-\}\).

Finally, if the intervention is vacuous, its submodel is the factual model.  Selected-law fit and normalization of \(q\) give
\[
d_M=\Pr_M(S=1)=\sum_{v\in\Omega_V}c_v(M)=\rho
\]
and
\[
n_M=\sum_{v\in\Omega_V}h(A(v))c_v(M)
=\rho\sum_{v\in\Omega_V}q(v)h(A(v)).
\]
The population margin \(\rho\geq\delta>0\) makes division legitimate, so every compatible SCM has
\[
\theta(M)=\sum_{v\in\Omega_V}q(v)h(A(v)). \tag{3}
\]
For nonemptiness in the complete-COPAG class, let one source have states \(u_v\) of masses \(\delta q(v)\), together, when \(\delta<1\), with one state \(u_0\) of mass \(1-\delta\).  Put \(V(u_v)=v\), \(S(u_v)=1\), and \(S(u_0)=0\), choosing \(V(u_0)\) arbitrarily.  Then \(c_v=\delta q(v)\), \(\rho=d_M=\delta\), and strict positivity of \(q\), together with at least two states per observed variable, makes the common source an actual functional-essential parent of every observed vertex.  The latent-fork argument in \(\text{thm:saturated-envelope-transfer}\) therefore gives a complete selected MAG and exact membership in the complete all-circle COPAG class.  Thus a compatible model exists, and (3) proves the asserted singleton fiber.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The closed form is an exact benchmark that exposes how the selection margin, factual treated cells, and outcome range determine the bound. \textit{Gap.} Duarte et al. provide a general polynomial program, but not this analytic selection-margin formula over a saturated COPAG. \textit{Consumer.} It gives a transparent sensitivity curve in \(\delta\) for screening intervention claims before running the general compiler.

\begin{proposition}[prop:five-type-witness]\label{prop:five-type-witness}
For \(\mathcal P^*\), \(q^*=(1,2,3,4)/10\), \(\delta^*=1/4\), intervention \(do(B=0)\), and \(h^*(a)=a\), the sharp fiber is \(\Theta_{\delta^*}(\mathcal P^*,q^*)=[1/20,39/40]\). The lower and upper endpoints are attained by five response types with masses \((1,2,3,4,30)/40\), common-source selected-MAG legality, and intervention-specific selection probability one.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For the binary outcome \(h^*(a)=a\), intervention \(b=0\), and \(\delta^*=1/4\), \(\text{def:saturated-envelope}\) gives
\[
L_{\delta^*}=\delta^*q^*(0,1)=\frac14\frac2{10}=\frac1{20},
\qquad
U_{\delta^*}=1-\delta^*q^*(0,0)=1-\frac14\frac1{10}=\frac{39}{40}.
\]
The hypotheses of \(\text{thm:saturated-closed-form}\) hold for \(\mathcal P^*\), so this calculation already proves sharpness of the interval.  We now exhibit the asserted five-type endpoint certificates.

Let a single exogenous source \(U\) have states \(u_1,\ldots,u_5\) with respective masses
\[
(w_1,w_2,w_3,w_4,w_5)=\frac1{40}(1,2,3,4,30).
\]
In both models define
\[
(B(u_i))_{i=1}^5=(0,0,1,1,1),
\]
and define the two rows of the selection table by
\[
(S(0,u_i))_{i=1}^5=(1,1,1,1,1),
\qquad
(S(1,u_i))_{i=1}^5=(1,1,1,1,0).
\]
For the lower endpoint use
\[
(A(0,u_i))_{i=1}^5=(0,1,0,0,0),
\qquad
(A(1,u_i))_{i=1}^5=(0,1,0,1,0),
\]
whereas for the upper endpoint use
\[
(A(0,u_i))_{i=1}^5=(0,1,1,1,1),
\qquad
(A(1,u_i))_{i=1}^5=(0,1,0,1,0).
\]
The natural selected cells of \(u_1,u_2,u_3,u_4\) are, respectively, \((0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1)\), while \(u_5\) is naturally unselected.  Therefore
\[
\rho=\sum_{i=1}^4w_i=\frac14,
\qquad
\bigl(c_{00},c_{01},c_{10},c_{11}\bigr)=\frac1{40}(1,2,3,4)=\rho q^*.
\]
Under \(do(B=0)\), the first selection row shows that \(d_M=\sum_iw_i=1\) in both models.  The lower and upper numerators are
\[
n_M^{(-)}=w_2=\frac2{40}=\frac1{20},
\qquad
n_M^{(+)}=w_2+w_3+w_4+w_5=\frac{39}{40}.
\]
Because the denominators equal one, these are the corresponding intervention means.

Finally, \(U\) is an actual essential parent of \(B\), \(A\), and \(S\): the displayed positive-mass table rows vary with \(U\) for each vertex.  Hence the latent projection contains an adjacency between \(B\) and \(A\), and its two-vertex selected MAG is represented by the complete all-circle COPAG \(\mathcal P^*\).  Thus both five-type SCMs satisfy the exact graph restriction, completing the certificate.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The witness is an exact arithmetic regression test for both the formula and the graph-legality implementation. \textit{Gap.} No named comparator reports this selected-law and selection-margin instance or its two attained response constructions. \textit{Consumer.} Implementers can reject a compiler that returns anything other than \([1/20,39/40]\) or fails to recover endpoint SCMs.

\begin{proposition}[prop:moment-compression-warning]\label{prop:moment-compression-warning}
There exists a positive selected law on two binary observed variables and a one-parameter family of finite SCMs with constant moment vector \(\zeta(M)\) such that the SCM has a common-source bidirected edge for every interior parameter value and loses that edge at both boundary values. Therefore, preservation of \(\zeta(M)\) alone does not preserve exact graph legality, although the boundary moment vector has a distinct legal lift.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let \(V=\{X,Y\}\), with \(\Omega_X=\Omega_Y=\{0,1\}\).  To instantiate all coordinates of the moment map, take \(A=\{Y\}\), \(B=\{X\}\), \(b=0\), and \(h(y)=y\).  Let \(\mathcal P\) be the saturated two-vertex COPAG whose represented class contains the selected MAG \(G^{\leftrightarrow}\) consisting of the single edge \(X\leftrightarrow Y\).  The selection vertex in the construction below is isolated, so its selection projection neither creates nor removes this edge.

For \(t\in[0,1]\), define a finite recursive SCM \(M_t\) with mutually independent exogenous sources \(U,E_X,E_Y,E_S\) having laws
\[
 U\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(t),\qquad
 E_X,E_Y\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2),\qquad
 E_S\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/4).
\]
The source \(U\) is shared by \(X\) and \(Y\), while each of \(E_X,E_Y,E_S\) has only its correspondingly subscripted child.  Set
\[
 X=E_X\oplus U,\qquad Y=E_Y\oplus U,\qquad S=E_S,
\]
where \(\oplus\) is addition modulo two.  These equations contain no endogenous parent relations and hence form a recursive SCM with finite state spaces.

Fix \((x,y)\in\{0,1\}^2\), and write \(p_t(0)=1-t\) and \(p_t(1)=t\) for the masses of \(U\).  Mutual independence and the fair laws of \(E_X,E_Y\) give the unconditional finite-sum identity
\[
 \Pr_{M_t}(X=x,Y=y)
 =\sum_{u=0}^1 p_t(u)\Pr(E_X=x\oplus u)\Pr(E_Y=y\oplus u)
 =\sum_{u=0}^1p_t(u)\frac14=\frac14.
\]
This identity holds at \(t=0\) and \(t=1\) as well, without conditioning on a null event.  Because \(S=E_S\) is independent of \((U,E_X,E_Y)\),
\[
 c_{(x,y)}(M_t)
 =\Pr_{M_t}(X=x,Y=y,S=1)
 =\frac14\frac14=\frac1{16}.
\]
In particular \(\rho=\Pr_{M_t}(S=1)=1/4\), and the selected law is
\[
 q(x,y)=\Pr_{M_t}(X=x,Y=y\mid S=1)
 =\frac{c_{(x,y)}(M_t)}{\rho}=\frac14.
\]
Thus \(q\) is rational and strictly positive on its four-point support.

Under \(do(X=0)\), recursive intervention replaces only the equation for \(X\), so
\[
 Y^0=E_Y\oplus U,\qquad S^0=E_S.
\]
The same finite-sum calculation gives
\[
 \Pr_{M_t}(Y^0=1)=\sum_{u=0}^1p_t(u)\Pr(E_Y=1\oplus u)=\frac12.
\]
Moreover, \(Y^0\), being a function of \((E_Y,U)\), is independent of \(S^0=E_S\) by mutual independence of the exogenous sources.  Hence
\[
 d_{M_t}=\Pr_{M_t}(S^0=1)=\frac14
\]
and
\[
 n_{M_t}
 =\mathbb E_{M_t}\!\left[h(Y^0)\mathbf 1\{S^0=1\}\right]
 =\Pr_{M_t}(Y^0=1,S^0=1)
 =\frac12\frac14=\frac18.
\]
By the selected-law and intervention moment-map definition, all coordinates are consequently constant:
\[
 \zeta(M_t)
 =\left(\left(\frac1{16}\right)_{(x,y)\in\{0,1\}^2},\frac18,\frac14\right)
 \quad\text{for every }t\in[0,1].
\]

It remains to determine the exact graph.  If \(0<t<1\), both source values of \(U\) have positive mass.  For each \(e_X\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
 e_X\oplus0\ne e_X\oplus1,
\]
and the identical comparison holds in the response table of \(Y\) for each \(e_Y\).  Therefore the nonnegative essential-feature polynomials for the two incidences \(U\to X\) and \(U\to Y\) are strictly positive: each has a witness using two positive-mass values of \(U\), and the other-source context in that witness has positive mass.  Thus the realized child set of this formal common source is \(\{X,Y\}\).  There is no directed incidence among \(X,Y,S\), since no structural equation uses another endogenous variable, and no private source has two children.  The graph-legality-stratum definition consequently retains the common source, and latent projection produces exactly \(X\leftrightarrow Y\), with \(S\) isolated.  Hence
\[
 G_S(M_t)=G^{\leftrightarrow}\in\mathcal G(\mathcal P),
 \qquad 0<t<1.
\]

At \(t=0\), only \(U=0\) has positive mass, and at \(t=1\), only \(U=1\) has positive mass.  At either endpoint there are not two positive-mass source values with which to witness a response-table change.  Both common-source incidence polynomials are therefore zero.  Under the normalized exact graph-legality rule, the realized child set is then empty and the formal source is discarded from graph projection; this conclusion does not require its literal response tables to be constant.  Only mutually independent private sources remain, so neither endpoint graph contains an \(X\)--\(Y\) edge.  Thus both \(G_S(M_0)\) and \(G_S(M_1)\) lose the bidirected edge present for every interior value, although their moment vectors are unchanged.

Finally, the same boundary moment vector has a distinct graph-legal lift.  Namely, \(M_{1/2}\) differs from each endpoint in the law of \(U\), satisfies \(G_S(M_{1/2})=G^{\leftrightarrow}\in\mathcal G(\mathcal P)\), and the calculation above gives
\[
 \zeta(M_{1/2})=\zeta(M_0)=\zeta(M_1).
\]
This proves all assertions.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The proposition rules out the tempting but invalid inference from Carathéodory moment preservation to exact-edge preservation. \textit{Gap.} Rosset, Gisin, and Wolfe preserve observed behavior, not functional essentiality; this example identifies the first proof step that must change. \textit{Consumer.} It prevents a bounding package from certifying graph legality solely from preserved observational and query moments.

\begin{theorem}[thm:confidence-fiber]\label{thm:confidence-fiber}
Suppose \(\Pr(q\in C_n)\ge1-\alpha_q\) and \(\Pr(\mathcal P\in\Pi_n)\ge1-\alpha_g\). Then the confidence fiber satisfies \(\Pr(\Theta_\delta(\mathcal P,q)\subseteq\widehat{\mathfrak C}_n)\ge1-\alpha_q-\alpha_g\) without differentiability, compactness, or a unique active optimizer. In the saturated binary witness, simultaneous one-sided exact binomial lower limits \(l_{01}\) and \(l_{00}\), each with error allocation \(\alpha_q/2\), yield the finite-sample interval \([\delta l_{01},1-\delta l_{00}]\) covering the entire sharp fiber with probability at least \(1-\alpha_q\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Define the coverage events
\[
E_q=\{q\in C_n\},\qquad E_g=\{\mathcal P\in\Pi_n\}.
\]
The two assumed marginal guarantees and the elementary union bound imply
\[
\Pr(E_q\cap E_g)
=1-\Pr(E_q^c\cup E_g^c)
\geq1-\Pr(E_q^c)-\Pr(E_g^c)
\geq1-\alpha_q-\alpha_g.
\]
On \(E_q\cap E_g\), the particular pair \((\mathcal P,q)\) occurs among the pairs indexing the union in \(\text{def:confidence-fiber}\).  Therefore, pointwise on this event,
\[
\Theta_\delta(\mathcal P,q)
\subseteq
\bigcup_{\mathcal P'\in\Pi_n,\,q'\in C_n}
\Theta_\delta(\mathcal P',q')
\subseteq
\operatorname{cl}\operatorname{conv}
\bigcup_{\mathcal P'\in\Pi_n,\,q'\in C_n}
\Theta_\delta(\mathcal P',q')
=\widehat{\mathfrak C}_n.
\]
Taking probabilities proves
\[
\Pr\!\left(\Theta_\delta(\mathcal P,q)\subseteq\widehat{\mathfrak C}_n\right)
\geq1-\alpha_q-\alpha_g.
\]
The argument uses only event inclusion; in particular, no differentiability, compactness, attainment, or uniqueness of an optimizer has entered.

For the saturated binary configuration, write \(q_{ij}=q(B=i,A=j)\).  By \(\text{thm:saturated-closed-form}\), with \(b=0\) and \(h(a)=a\),
\[
\Theta_\delta(\mathcal P,q)=[\delta q_{01},\,1-\delta q_{00}].
\]
Under the declared i.i.d. sampling law, each cell count has its corresponding binomial marginal distribution.  Because \(l_{01}\) and \(l_{00}\) are one-sided exact lower confidence limits with error allocation \(\alpha_q/2\), their defining coverage properties are
\[
\Pr(l_{01}\leq q_{01})\geq1-\frac{\alpha_q}{2},
\qquad
\Pr(l_{00}\leq q_{00})\geq1-\frac{\alpha_q}{2}.
\]
No independence between these two limits is required.  A second application of the union bound gives
\[
\Pr(l_{01}\leq q_{01},\ l_{00}\leq q_{00})\geq1-\alpha_q.
\]
On this simultaneous event, \(\delta>0\) gives
\[
\delta l_{01}\leq\delta q_{01},
\qquad
1-\delta q_{00}\leq1-\delta l_{00},
\]
and hence
\[
\Theta_\delta(\mathcal P,q)
=[\delta q_{01},1-\delta q_{00}]
\subseteq[\delta l_{01},1-\delta l_{00}].
\]
Thus the reported finite-sample interval covers the entire sharp fiber with probability at least \(1-\alpha_q\), as claimed.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Finite-sample event inclusion remains valid at singular active faces, unattained endpoints, and collapsed identification intervals. \textit{Gap.} Chernozhukov, Hong, and Tamer treat a fixed criterion, Imbens and Manski treat interval endpoints, and Chang, Guo, and Malinsky treat post-discovery point effects; none covers a learned selection-COPAG compatibility fiber. \textit{Consumer.} The method attaches calibrated uncertainty to the graph and effect ambiguity in the survivor-selected cardiac-surgery FCI and LV-IDA analysis.

\begin{lemma}[lem:basu-sign-sampling]\label{lem:basu-sign-sampling}
Let \(Q\) be a polynomial in \(p_{\mathrm{dim}}\) variables, let \(\mathscr P\) contain at most \(s_{\mathrm{count}}\) polynomials in the same variables, suppose that all these polynomials have degree at most \(D_{\mathrm{deg}}\), and let \(p_0\) denote the real dimension of the zero set of \(Q\).  Over a real closed coefficient field, there is an algorithm computing a finite set meeting every semialgebraically connected component of every realizable sign condition of \(\mathscr P\) over that zero set in \(s_{\mathrm{count}}^{p_0}D_{\mathrm{deg}}^{O(p_{\mathrm{dim}})}\) ring operations.  Returning the signs of every polynomial at every sample costs \(s_{\mathrm{count}}^{p_0+1}D_{\mathrm{deg}}^{O(p_{\mathrm{dim}})}\) ring operations.  The algebraic samples may be recorded by real-univariate representations whose selected roots are specified by Thom encodings.  Taking \(Q=0\) and \(p_0=p_{\mathrm{dim}}\) gives the ambient-space bounds used here.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[lem:basu-one-block-elimination]\label{lem:basu-one-block-elimination}
Let a first-order formula over a real closed field have at most \(s_{\mathrm{count}}\) input polynomials of degree at most \(D_{\mathrm{deg}}\), one block of \(p_{\mathrm{dim}}\) quantified variables, and one free variable.  It has an equivalent quantifier-free formula computable in
\[
s_{\mathrm{count}}^{\,2(p_{\mathrm{dim}}+1)}D_{\mathrm{deg}}^{O(p_{\mathrm{dim}})}
\]
ring operations.  Its output polynomials have degree \(D_{\mathrm{deg}}^{O(p_{\mathrm{dim}})}\).  For integer input coefficients of bit length at most \(\tau_{\mathrm{eff}}\), all intermediate and output integer bit lengths are at most \(\tau_{\mathrm{eff}}D_{\mathrm{deg}}^{O(p_{\mathrm{dim}})}\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[thm:saturated-envelope-transfer]\label{thm:saturated-envelope-transfer}
Suppose \(q(v)>0\) for every \(v\in\Omega_V\).  Every recursive SCM \(M\) for which there is a \(\rho\in[\delta,1]\) satisfying \(c_v(M)=\rho q(v)\) for all \(v\in\Omega_V\) and \(d_M\geq\delta\) obeys the graph-free bounds
\[
L_\delta\leq\theta(M)\leq U_\delta.
\]
If \(\mathcal P\) is the complete all-circle COPAG, every observed variable has at least two states, and \(|\Omega_B|\geq2\), there is a one-source family \(\{M_t:0\leq t\leq1\}\subseteq\mathfrak M_\delta(\mathcal P,q)\) such that
\[
\theta(M_t)=(1-t)L_\delta+tU_\delta.
\]
Each endpoint witness has at most \(m+1\) positive-mass response types.  Consequently, for any family \(\mathscr C\) of recursive SCMs all satisfying the graph-free premises above and containing every \(M_t\),
\[
\{\theta(M):M\in\mathscr C\}=[L_\delta,U_\delta].
\]
In particular, the same equality holds for every permitted-graph family satisfying those premises and containing the complete-COPAG witnesses.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put
\[
I_b=\{v\in\Omega_V:B(v)=b\},\qquad
R_- =\sum_{v\in I_b}q(v)\{h(A(v))-h_-\},\qquad
R_+ =\sum_{v\in I_b}q(v)\{h_+-h(A(v))\}.
\]
Finiteness of \(\Omega_A\) implies that the extrema defining \(h_-\) and \(h_+\) are attained.  Their definitions give \(R_-\geq0\) and \(R_+\geq0\).  Also
\[
U_\delta-L_\delta
=(h_+-h_-)\left\{1-\delta\sum_{v\in I_b}q(v)\right\}\geq0,
\]
because \(0<\delta\leq1\) and \(\sum_{v\in I_b}q(v)\leq1\).  Thus the claimed interval is nonempty.

First fix an arbitrary recursive SCM \(M\) satisfying the three graph-free premises in the statement.  The margin \(d_M\geq\delta>0\) makes the division in \(\theta(M)=n_M/d_M\) legitimate.  For every \(v\in I_b\), recursive consistency gives
\[
\{V=v,S=1\}\subseteq\{A^b=A(v),S^b=1\}. \tag{1}
\]
To verify (1), order the endogenous variables topologically.  On \(\{V=v,S=1\}\), replacing the structural equations for \(B\) by the already realized value \(b\) does not change \(B\).  Induction along the remaining structural equations leaves every later endogenous variable, including \(A\) and \(S\), unchanged.  The factual events on the left side of (1), indexed by \(v\in I_b\), are disjoint.  Since \(h(A^b)-h_-\geq0\), consistency (1), selected-law fit, and \(\rho\geq\delta\) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
n_M-h_-d_M
&=\mathbb E_M\!\left[\{h(A^b)-h_-\}\mathbf 1\{S^b=1\}\right]\\
&\geq\sum_{v\in I_b}\{h(A(v))-h_-\}\Pr_M(V=v,S=1)\\
&=\rho R_-\\
&\geq\delta R_-.
\end{aligned} \tag{2}
\]
Because \(d_M\) is a probability and is positive, \(0<d_M\leq1\).  Dividing (2) by \(d_M\) therefore yields
\[
\theta(M)-h_-
=\frac{n_M-h_-d_M}{d_M}
\geq\frac{\delta R_-}{d_M}
\geq\delta R_-,
\]
which is \(\theta(M)\geq L_\delta\) by \(\text{def:saturated-envelope}\).  Applying the same argument to the nonnegative outcome map \(h_+-h\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
h_+d_M-n_M
&=\mathbb E_M\!\left[\{h_+-h(A^b)\}\mathbf 1\{S^b=1\}\right]\\
&\geq\sum_{v\in I_b}\{h_+-h(A(v))\}\Pr_M(V=v,S=1)\\
&=\rho R_+\\
&\geq\delta R_+.
\end{aligned} \tag{3}
\]
Division by \(d_M\leq1\) gives
\[
h_+-\theta(M)
=\frac{h_+d_M-n_M}{d_M}
\geq\frac{\delta R_+}{d_M}
\geq\delta R_+,
\]
or \(\theta(M)\leq U_\delta\).  This proves the outer bound without using any condition on \(G_S(M)\).

We now construct witnesses in the exact complete-COPAG subclass.  Choose \(a_-,a_+\in\Omega_A\) such that \(h(a_-)=h_-\) and \(h(a_+)=h_+\).  The hypothesis \(|\Omega_B|\geq2\) supplies a value \(b^\dagger\in\Omega_B\setminus\{b\}\).  Let one exogenous source \(U\) have a state \(u_v\) for every \(v\in\Omega_V\), with
\[
\Pr(U=u_v)=\delta q(v).
\]
When \(\delta<1\), add a state \(u_0\) of mass \(1-\delta\); when \(\delta=1\), omit that state.  Since \(q(v)>0\), all displayed \(u_v\)-states have positive mass, and the masses sum to one.  Hence each endpoint source has at most \(m+1\) positive-mass states.

Choose a recursive order in which the vertices of \(B\) precede those of \(V\setminus B\), which in turn precede \(S\).  Give the vector \(B\) its natural value \(B(v)\) at \(u_v\), and give it the natural value \(b^\dagger\) at \(u_0\), if that state exists.  For each \(X\in V\setminus B\), take a response table \(f_X(B,U)\) whose factual entries satisfy
\[
f_X(B(v),u_v)=X(v). \tag{4}
\]
For the selection table impose
\[
f_S(B(v),u_v)=1,qquad
f_S(b,u)=1\ \text{for every positive-mass state }u, \tag{5}
\]
and, if \(u_0\) exists, impose \(f_S(b^\dagger,u_0)=0\).  In the lower endpoint model, complete the intervention entries of the coordinates of \(A\) so that
\[
A(b,u_v)=
\begin{cases}
A(v),&B(v)=b,\\
a_-,&B(v)\neq b,
\end{cases}
\qquad
A(b,u_0)=a_-\quad(\delta<1). \tag{6-}
\]
For the upper endpoint model use
\[
A(b,u_v)=
\begin{cases}
A(v),&B(v)=b,\\
a_+,&B(v)\neq b,
\end{cases}
\qquad
A(b,u_0)=a_+\quad(\delta<1). \tag{6+}
\]
There is no conflict among (4)--(6): if \(B(v)=b\), the intervention entry is exactly the factual entry; every changed \(u_v\)-entry has \(B(v)\neq b\); and \(b^\dagger\neq b\).  Fill all remaining response-table entries arbitrarily.  The resulting finite tables define recursive one-source SCMs.

Under the natural regime, precisely the \(u_v\)-types are selected.  Therefore
\[
c_v(M)=\Pr_M(V=v,S=1)=\delta q(v)\quad(v\in\Omega_V),
\qquad \rho=\delta. \tag{7}
\]
By (5), every positive-mass type is selected after \(\operatorname{do}(B=b)\), and hence
\[
d_M=1\geq\delta. \tag{8}
\]
Equations (6-) and (8) give, for the lower model,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\theta(M)
=n_M
&=\delta\sum_{v\in I_b}q(v)h(A(v))
+\left\{1-\delta\sum_{v\in I_b}q(v)\right\}h_-\\
&=h_-+\delta\sum_{v\in I_b}q(v)\{h(A(v))-h_-\}\\
&=L_\delta.
\end{aligned} \tag{9-}
\]
Likewise, (6+) and (8) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\theta(M)
=n_M
&=\delta\sum_{v\in I_b}q(v)h(A(v))
+\left\{1-\delta\sum_{v\in I_b}q(v)\right\}h_+\\
&=h_+-\delta\sum_{v\in I_b}q(v)\{h_+-h(A(v))\}\\
&=U_\delta.
\end{aligned} \tag{9+}
\]

It remains to prove exact graphical legality.  Fix \(X\in V\).  If \(X\in B\), the assumption that \(X\) has at least two states and positivity of every joint cell provide positive-mass source states at which the natural response of \(X\) differs.  If \(X\notin B\), choose \(v,v'\in\Omega_V\) with \(B(v)=B(v')\), \(X(v)\neq X(v')\), and all other coordinates fixed.  Such cells exist because the joint state space is a Cartesian product and \(X\) has at least two states; positivity of \(q\) makes both corresponding source states positive-mass.  Equation (4) yields
\[
f_X(B(v),u_v)=X(v)\neq X(v')=f_X(B(v),u_{v'}).
\]
Thus \(U\) is an actual functional-essential parent of every observed vertex, under the actual-parent convention defining \(G_S(M)\), with all other endogenous inputs held fixed.  Consequently, for every distinct \(X,Y\in V\), the latent-fork path \(X\leftarrow U\to Y\) is present and remains open after conditioning on \(S\) and on any subset of \(V\setminus\{X,Y\}\): its only nonendpoint, \(U\), is an unconditioned noncollider.  The selected maximal projection therefore makes every pair of observed vertices adjacent.  Its selected MAG has complete skeleton.  A complete-skeleton selected MAG has no unshielded collider and no discriminating path with nonadjacent endpoints, so it belongs to the Markov-equivalence class represented by the complete all-circle COPAG.  Hence
\[
G_S(M)\in\mathcal G(\mathcal P). \tag{10}
\]
Equations (7), (8), and (10), together with \(\rho=\delta\in[\delta,1]\), prove that both endpoint SCMs belong to \(\mathfrak M_\delta(\mathcal P,q)\).

For \(0<t<1\), take disjointly labelled copies of the lower and upper source states, multiply their masses by \(1-t\) and \(t\), and use the corresponding response tables on the two copies.  At \(t=0\) and \(t=1\), use the respective endpoint SCM.  This still gives a recursive SCM with one exogenous source.  Equations (7) and (8) are preserved by mixing, and the positive-mass essential-source argument proving (10) remains valid.  Thus \(M_t\in\mathfrak M_\delta(\mathcal P,q)\) for every \(t\in[0,1]\).  Since its intervention denominator is one and expectation is linear in the source law,
\[
\theta(M_t)=(1-t)L_\delta+tU_\delta. \tag{11}
\]

Finally let \(\mathscr C\) satisfy the hypotheses in the statement.  The graph-free bound already proved gives
\[
\{\theta(M):M\in\mathscr C\}\subseteq[L_\delta,U_\delta].
\]
Because \(\mathscr C\) contains every \(M_t\), equation (11) gives the reverse inclusion:
\[
[L_\delta,U_\delta]
=\{\theta(M_t):0\leq t\leq1\}
\subseteq\{\theta(M):M\in\mathscr C\}.
\]
This proves the family-sandwich assertion, including its permitted-graph specialization.
\end{proof}

\section{Interpretation}

The closed-form envelope is a universal partial-identification statement derived from recursive-SCM consistency, selected-law fit, bounded outcomes, and the two calibrated selection margins; graph restrictions are used only to place the attaining witnesses in the complete all-circle COPAG subclass.  Therefore any broader permitted-graph family satisfying the graph-free premises and containing those witnesses has the same interval by sandwiching.  By contrast, the algebraic endpoint procedure characterizes only the bounded finite graph-legal image.  A positive certificate is an attained bounded-model mechanism, while a negative certificate says only that every bounded branch fails and cannot be reported as nonattainment over arbitrary SCMs.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

Saturated order-indexed superarchitectures remove redundant subset enumeration but do not close the legality topology.  In the positive binary exclusive-or family, moment-preserving support deletion can send a shared-source essentiality polynomial to zero while holding the selected law and intervention moment fixed.  An equal-moment legal lift exists in that example, so it is a warning against naive compression rather than a negative answer.  More generally, a bounded-compiler exclusion certificate does not exclude a repair outside the finite support bound.

\section{Honest scope}

Established results cover child-set source merging within the retained-variable response language, sourcewise moment-preserving finite support on a fixed permitted architecture, embedding into at most \(N!\) saturated ordered superarchitectures, exact polynomial legality for bounded finite response models, singly exponential existential-semialgebraic endpoint certification for that bounded image, a universal selection-margin outer envelope with exact complete-COPAG subclass attainment and sandwich transfer, and finite-sample confidence-fiber event inclusion.  Equality between arbitrary selected SCMs and the bounded compiler, preservation of exact actual-parent semantics through arbitrary canonicalization or compression, and closure or attainment of the full positive-unfaithful COPAG fiber remain open.  No general endpoint-convergence claim is made.  The interval \(\left[1/20,39/40\right]\) is a binary audit instance, not evidence for universal lifting.
