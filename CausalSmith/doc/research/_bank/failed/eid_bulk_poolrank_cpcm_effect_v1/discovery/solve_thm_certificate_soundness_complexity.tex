%%% FIELD proof-polynomial-class-inclusion
Fix a member of \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{poly}}(\mathfrak G,\Theta,q)\), a type \(r\), and a topological ordering of its DAG \(d_r\).  At a root \(s\), the mass \(e^{-\alpha_{rs}}\alpha_{rs}^{v_s}/v_s!\) is positive for every \(v_s\in\mathbb N_0\), because \(\alpha_{rs}>0\) by \(\mathrm{ass{:}poisson\text{-}root\text{-}positive\text{-}polynomial\text{-}dag}\).  At a non-root, the same assumption gives
\[
\lambda_{rs,\theta}(v)=\alpha_{rs}+
\sum_{k\in\operatorname{pa}_{d_r}(s)}b_{rsk}v_k^{q_{sk}}>0,
\]
because the intercept and every declared-edge coefficient are strictly positive and the parent counts are nonnegative.  Hence every conditional Poisson mass is positive.  The DAG factorization therefore yields
\[
 p_{r,\theta}(v)=\prod_{s=1}^{p}
 p_{rs,\theta}\bigl(v_s\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_{d_r}(s)}\bigr)>0
 \qquad(v\in\mathbb N_0^p). \tag{1}
\]
Thus the observational law has full support.

For causal sufficiency, let \((U_{rs})_{s=1}^p\) be mutually independent \(\operatorname{Unif}(0,1)\) variables.  In topological order, define each \(V_s\) as the generalized inverse of its stipulated root or conditional Poisson distribution evaluated at \(U_{rs}\).  The probability integral transform for a discrete distribution shows that the resulting conditional kernel is exactly \(p_{rs,\theta}\).  The exogenous variables are jointly independent, so this construction is a causally sufficient acyclic structural representation of (1).

We next prove causal minimality rather than assuming it.  Fix a declared edge \(k\to s\), and fix all other parents of \(s\) at zero.  Full support in (1) makes each of the conditioning events
\[
 \{V_k=x,\ V_{\operatorname{pa}(s)\setminus\{k\}}=0\},
 \qquad x\in\mathbb N_0,
\]
have positive probability.  On those events the conditional rate of \(V_s\) is
\[
 \lambda_s(x)=\alpha_{rs}+b_{rsk}x^{q_{sk}}. \tag{2}
\]
By \(b_{rsk}>0\) and \(q_{sk}\in\mathbb N\),
\(\lambda_s(1)-\lambda_s(0)=b_{rsk}>0\).  In particular,
\[
 \Pr(V_s=0\mid V_k=1,V_{\operatorname{pa}(s)\setminus\{k\}}=0)
 =e^{-\alpha_{rs}-b_{rsk}}
 \ne e^{-\alpha_{rs}}
 =\Pr(V_s=0\mid V_k=0,V_{\operatorname{pa}(s)\setminus\{k\}}=0). \tag{3}
\]
Thus \(V_s\) is not conditionally independent of \(V_k\) given its other parents.  This holds for every declared edge, so deletion of any edge destroys the Markov factorization; equivalently, the generating DAG is causally minimal.

It remains to verify pairwise identifiability.  Fix the same edge and an admissible set \(S\) satisfying
\[
 \operatorname{pa}_{d_r}(s)\setminus\{k\}
 \subseteq S\subseteq
 \operatorname{nd}_{d_r}(s)\setminus\{k,s\}.
\]
By (1), \(\Pr(V_S=0)>0\) and
\(\Pr(V_k=x,V_S=0)>0\) for every \(x\in\mathbb N_0\).  The local Markov property of the displayed DAG factorization implies that, after all other parents have been fixed, additionally conditioning on nondescendants that are not parents does not change the child kernel.  Hence under \(V_S=0\) the conditional rate remains (2), which is strictly increasing and unbounded.  By the frozen \(\mathrm{lem{:}poisson\text{-}cpcm\text{-}reversibility}\), failure of bivariate Poisson-CPCM direction identification can occur only when the forward rate is \(\exp(ax+b)\) with \(a<0\), a strictly decreasing function.  Equation (2) cannot have that form.  The conditioned pair is therefore identifiable.  Since the edge and admissible set were arbitrary, the maintained pairwise-identifiability requirement holds; if there are no edges it is vacuous.

We have proved full support, causal sufficiency, causal minimality, and the pairwise-identifiability condition for every type.  These are precisely the relevant membership requirements of \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{CPCM}}\) in \(\mathrm{def{:}cpcm\text{-}class}\), so the asserted inclusion follows.

%%% FIELD proof-polynomial-pgf-regularity
Fix a graph tuple \(\boldsymbol d\).  It suffices to work type by type because the parameter blocks of distinct types are disjoint.  Choose a topological ordering.  At a root, every moment of \(V_s\sim\operatorname{Pois}(\alpha_s)\) is a polynomial in \(\alpha_s\), hence is uniformly bounded on the compact coefficient box.  If the claim is known for all predecessors of a non-root \(s\), then for every integer \(m\geq0\),
\[
 \mathbb E_\theta[V_s^m\mid V_{\operatorname{pa}(s)}]
 =\sum_{k=0}^{m}
 \left\{\begin{matrix}m\\ k\end{matrix}\right\}
 \lambda_s(V_{\operatorname{pa}(s)})^k. \tag{4}
\]
The rate is a fixed polynomial in predecessor counts and its coefficients are uniformly bounded on \(\Theta_{\boldsymbol d}\).  Expansion of its powers and H\"older's inequality reduce the expectation of every term in (4) to finitely many predecessor moments.  Topological induction therefore proves
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in\Theta_{\boldsymbol d}}
 \mathbb E_\theta\!\left[\left(1+\sum_{s=1}^pV_s\right)^m\right]<\infty
 \qquad(m\in\mathbb N_0). \tag{5}
\]
The same induction applies after fixing the intervention coordinate at \(1\) or \(2\).

Every rate has a uniform positive lower bound on the box.  If \(t\) indexes the intercept or an edge coefficient in the rate of node \(s\), write
\[
 a_{st}(v_{\operatorname{pa}(s)})
 \in\{1,v_k^{q_{sk}}:k\in\operatorname{pa}(s)\}.
\]
For the positive joint mass \(p_\theta(v)\), direct differentiation gives
\[
 \partial_t\log p_\theta(v)=
 a_{st}(v_{\operatorname{pa}(s)})
 \left\{\frac{v_s}{\lambda_s(v_{\operatorname{pa}(s)})}-1\right\}. \tag{6}
\]
First and second parameter derivatives of \(p_\theta(v)\) are consequently bounded in absolute value by \(p_\theta(v)\) times a fixed polynomial in \(v\), uniformly over the box.  This gives the needed uniform summability without silently invoking a pointwise dominating law: if that polynomial has degree \(d\), then for any \(a\geq1\), (5) and Markov's inequality give
\[
 \sup_\theta\sum_{\sum_s v_s>R}
 |\partial^\nu p_\theta(v)|
 \leq \frac{C}{R^a}
 \sup_\theta\mathbb E_\theta
 \left(1+\sum_sV_s\right)^{d+a}
 \longrightarrow0,
 \qquad |\nu|\leq2. \tag{7}
\]
Thus the derivative series are uniformly Cauchy.  Termwise differentiation on each finite truncation followed by (7) proves twice continuous differentiability of the joint mass, every PGF on \((0,1)^p\), and every fixed intervened first-moment contrast.  Equation (5) also proves finiteness of all moments uniformly on the box.

The positive observational law determines every conditional mean at every parent vector.  Hence it determines
\[
 \lambda_s(x)=\mathbb E_\theta[V_s\mid V_{\operatorname{pa}(s)}=x],
 \qquad
 \alpha_s=\lambda_s(0),
 \qquad
 b_{sk}=\lambda_s(e_k)-\lambda_s(0), \tag{8}
\]
where \(e_k\) has its \(k\)-coordinate equal to one and its other coordinates zero.  This proves injectivity of the coefficient-to-law map within a fixed graph.

For differential injectivity, group the scores from (6) by node:
\[
 S_s=f_s(V_{\operatorname{pa}(s)})
 \frac{V_s-\lambda_s(V_{\operatorname{pa}(s)})}
 {\lambda_s(V_{\operatorname{pa}(s)})},
 \qquad
 f_s=\bigl(1,(V_k^{q_{sk}})_{k\in\operatorname{pa}(s)}\bigr)^{\mathsf T}. \tag{9}
\]
In the topological filtration, each \(S_s\) has conditional mean zero given all preceding variables.  Hence different node blocks are orthogonal, while the information block for node \(s\) is
\[
 I_s=\mathbb E_\theta\!\left[\frac{f_sf_s^{\mathsf T}}{\lambda_s}\right]. \tag{10}
\]
If \(c^{\mathsf T}I_sc=0\), positivity of \(\lambda_s\) and full support from \(\mathrm{prop{:}polynomial\text{-}class\text{-}inclusion}\) imply \(c^{\mathsf T}f_s(x)=0\) for every \(x\in\mathbb N_0^{|\operatorname{pa}(s)|}\).  Evaluation at zero and then at each coordinate unit vector forces all entries of \(c\) to vanish.  Thus every block in (10) is positive definite.  For every nonzero full parameter direction \(a\),
\[
 \mathbb E_\theta[(a^{\mathsf T}S)^2]>0. \tag{11}
\]

Suppose the directional derivative of the PGF vanished on a nonempty open subset of \((0,1)^p\).  By (7), it is the absolutely convergent signed power series
\[
 \sum_{v\in\mathbb N_0^p}p_\theta(v)a^{\mathsf T}S(v)z^v. \tag{12}
\]
Successively fix all but one coordinate.  Here is the needed one-variable argument.  If a power series vanishes on an open subinterval of its convergence interval, all derivatives vanish at an interior point, so its Taylor expansion vanishes on a neighborhood of that point.  Overlapping convergence neighborhoods continue the zero identity through \((0,1)\).  If a coefficient were nonzero, choose its least index \(m\); division by \(z^m\) and passage to \(z\downarrow0\) would give that coefficient equal to zero, a contradiction.  Thus every coefficient vanishes.  Iterating this argument over the \(p\) coordinates makes every coefficient in (12) zero.  Since \(p_\theta(v)>0\), this would make \(a^{\mathsf T}S(v)=0\) for all \(v\), contradicting (11).  The coefficient-to-law differential is therefore injective.

Finally, \(\mathrm{prop{:}polynomial\text{-}class\text{-}inclusion}\) puts every displayed law in the pairwise-identifiable CPCM class, and the frozen \(\mathrm{lem{:}cpcm\text{-}dag\text{-}identification}\) gives a unique labeled generating DAG.  Thus two distinct labeled graph tuples have disjoint law images.  Coordinatewise continuity of the mass functions and compactness of the coefficient boxes make each image compact in the product topology on probability masses.  This proves all assertions, including their finite stacking over types.

The unconditional termination and one-sided soundness proved in \(\mathrm{thm{:}certificate\text{-}soundness\text{-}complexity}\), together with the literal return rule in \(\mathrm{def{:}certificate\text{-}procedure}\), now forces \(\mathfrak C(\Xi_t)=\mathtt{CERTIFIED}\) for every sufficiently large \(t\).  None of these hypotheses has been verified for \(\Xi_0\) or \(\Xi_1\), so no benchmark conclusion follows.

%%% FIELD proof-finite-certified-design-frontier
The caps make \(\mathfrak X_{\mathrm{adm}}\) finite.  Indeed there are finitely many choices of \(J\), finitely many matrices in \(\{0,\ldots,m_{\max}\}^{J\times K}\), finitely many nonempty grid subsets of the finite set \(\mathcal Z_{\mathrm{cand}}\) with size at most \(L_{\max}\), finitely many declared allocations and well caps, and finitely many integer truncation, moment, Taylor, subdivision, and precision choices under \(\mathfrak b\).  The constraint \(\mathfrak B(\Xi)\leq B_{\max}\) only removes candidates.  Write the resulting set as \(\{\Xi_1,\ldots,\Xi_N\}\).

For a certified candidate define the maximization-oriented vector
\[
 y(\Xi)=\bigl(\underline\Gamma_{\mathcal T},
 \underline\kappa_{\mathcal T},
 \underline\Lambda_{\mathcal T},
 \underline\mu_{\mathcal T},
 -N_{\mathrm{req}},-w_{\mathrm{cert}},-\mathfrak B(\Xi)\bigr). \tag{33}
\]
Order candidates by their finite encodings.  First apply the exact syntax, rank, simplex, box, and arithmetic-cap tests.  Maintain the set \(C\) of completed certified candidates.  If interval calculations for an unfinished candidate \(x\) prove an upper envelope \(U(x)\) satisfying \(y(x)\preceq U(x)\) for every possible certified completion, and some \(c\in C\) satisfies \(y(c)\succeq U(x)\) with one strict coordinate, discard \(x\).  This is sound because transitivity gives \(y(c)\succeq y(x)\) with a strict coordinate.  Otherwise run the remaining finite certificate tasks.  When all unpruned candidates have terminated, delete from \(C\) each candidate dominated by another member of \(C\).

The one-sided conclusions of \(\mathrm{thm{:}certificate\text{-}soundness\text{-}complexity}\) validate every infeasibility decision and every bound used in this comparison.  A pruned candidate cannot be Pareto maximal.  Every retained candidate is certified and is not dominated by any certified candidate, because the final comparison is exhaustive over all certified, unpruned completions.  Conversely, a certified nondominated candidate can be neither soundly infeasible nor soundly dominated by an envelope, so it survives.  The returned set is exactly \(\mathfrak P_{\mathrm{adm}}\).

Nothing in the frozen assumptions forces a candidate to be infeasible or dominated before its certificate tasks are evaluated.  For an explicit generic-class certificate, take an admissible instance whose finite candidate sets and caps leave a singleton \(\mathfrak X_{\mathrm{adm}}=\{\Xi\}\) and for which \(\mathfrak C(\Xi)=\mathtt{CERTIFIED}\).  Its frontier is \(\{\Xi\}\), and its only candidate must undergo the tests establishing certification.  More generally, if all certified vectors (33) are pairwise incomparable, dominance pruning removes none.  Therefore the algorithm is exact and its pruning rules can be useful, but no strict worst-case reduction from exhaustive evaluation follows without an additional structural dominance theorem.

%%% FIELD statement-frontier-answer
The finite Pareto set \(\mathfrak P_{\mathrm{adm}}\) is exactly computable by deterministic finite enumeration together with sound certified-infeasibility and upper-envelope dominance pruning.  An unfinished candidate is safely discarded when an already certified candidate weakly dominates, with one strict coordinate, a proved upper envelope for every objective vector the unfinished candidate can still attain.  Final pairwise comparison of all unpruned certified outputs returns exactly the frontier.  The frozen assumptions do not guarantee that this avoids evaluating every admissible candidate: in the worst case all candidates can remain feasible and pairwise nondominated.

%%% FIELD proof-frontier-answer
This is exactly the algorithm and obstruction proved in \(\mathrm{thm{:}finite\text{-}certified\text{-}design\text{-}frontier}\).  Its finiteness argument supplies termination, its envelope inequality proves safe pruning, and its final exhaustive comparison proves equality with \(\mathfrak P_{\mathrm{adm}}\).  Its singleton and pairwise-incomparable certificates show that no strict worst-case reduction from evaluating every surviving admissible input follows from the frozen assumptions.  Hence the open-ended question has the stated qualified affirmative answer.
