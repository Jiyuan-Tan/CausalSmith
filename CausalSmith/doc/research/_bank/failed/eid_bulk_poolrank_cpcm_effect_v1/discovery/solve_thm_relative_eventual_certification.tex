%%% FIELD proof-relative-eventual-certification
Fix the substantive entries of \(\Xi\) and the sequence \((\Xi_t)\) from the statement.  All graph-specific parameter boxes and their pairwise products are compact, and there are only finitely many graph tuples.  By \(\mathrm{lem{:}polynomial\text{-}pgf\text{-}regularity}\), the exact moment maps and all derivatives used by the procedure are continuous on these boxes.  The stipulated positive denominator and positive-definiteness margins make the exact covariance, sandwich, and contrast-variance maps continuous on the inference boxes as well.  The assumed uniform convergence of the tail errors, Taylor-enclosure widths, terminal mesh diameters, and outward-rounding errors therefore implies the following common enclosure property: there is a deterministic sequence \(e_t\downarrow0\) such that every lower endpoint used at stage \(t\) is at least the infimum of its exact scalar target over the relevant leaf minus \(e_t\), and every upper endpoint is at most the corresponding supremum plus \(e_t\).  The analogous operator-norm excess for every required matrix enclosure is at most \(e_t\).  Enlarging \(e_t\) by a fixed finite factor if necessary covers all finitely many kinds of tests.

Let \(\gamma_*>0\) be a common lower bound for the exact wrong-graph objectives on all nonempty wrong-graph comparison products.  Such a number exists by the strict uniform graph-separation hypothesis and finiteness of \(\mathfrak G\).  If
\[
 f_{\boldsymbol d,\boldsymbol d'}(\vartheta,\vartheta')
 =\left\lVert m_{\boldsymbol d,\mathcal T}(\vartheta)
        -m_{\boldsymbol d',\mathcal T}(\vartheta')\right\rVert_W^2,
\]
then \(f_{\boldsymbol d,\boldsymbol d'}\geq\gamma_*\) on every such product.  Consequently, once \(e_t<\gamma_*/2\), every wrong-graph leaf has a strictly positive lower enclosure and passes.  When there is no wrong-graph comparison, both the exact and certified graph bounds are \(+\infty\) by \(\mathrm{def{:}grid\text{-}criterion}\) and \(\mathrm{def{:}certificate\text{-}procedure}\), so no numerical test is required.

We next prove eventual success of the remote-pair branch without asserting a uniform negative distance margin merely from membership in the open set \(\{D<0\}\).  Fix a graph \(\boldsymbol d\), put
\[
 K_{\boldsymbol d}=\Theta_{\boldsymbol d}\times\Theta_{\boldsymbol d},
 \qquad
 D(\vartheta,\vartheta')=lVert\vartheta-\vartheta'\rVert_2^2-r_{\mathrm{id}}^2,
\]
and define
\[
 f(\vartheta,\vartheta')=
 \left\lVert m_{\boldsymbol d,\mathcal T}(\vartheta)
              -m_{\boldsymbol d,\mathcal T}(\vartheta')\right\rVert_W^2,
 \qquad
 S=\{x\in K_{\boldsymbol d}:D(x)\geq0\}.
\]
First suppose \(S\neq\varnothing\).  The strict uniform remote slack supplies \(\lambda_*>0\), common over the finitely many nonempty remote tasks, such that
\[
 f(x)\geq\lambda_*\qquad(x\in S).
\]
Let
\[
 U=\{x\in K_{\boldsymbol d}:f(x)>\lambda_*/2\}.
\]
Continuity of \(f\) makes \(U\) relatively open in \(K_{\boldsymbol d}\), and the preceding inequality gives \(S\subset U\).  Hence the complement \(C=K_{\boldsymbol d}\setminus U\) is compact and disjoint from \(S\).  Thus \(D<0\) on \(C\).  If \(C\neq\varnothing\), continuity and compactness give the genuine uniform separation
\[
 \delta_{\boldsymbol d}:=-\max_{x\in C}D(x)>0,
 \qquad D(x)\leq-\delta_{\boldsymbol d}\quad(x\in C).
\]
This margin is obtained on the compact complement \(C\), not on the whole open set \(\{D<0\}\).  Uniform continuity of \(D\) on \(K_{\boldsymbol d}\) yields \(a_{\boldsymbol d}>0\) such that
\[
 \lVert x-y\rVert_2<a_{\boldsymbol d}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 |D(x)-D(y)|<\delta_{\boldsymbol d}/2.
\]
Consider any closed terminal product leaf \(B\subset K_{\boldsymbol d}\) with diameter below \(a_{\boldsymbol d}\).  If \(B\cap C=\varnothing\), then \(B\subset U\), whence \(f>lambda_*/2\) throughout \(B\); for \(e_t<\lambda_*/4\), its moment-distance lower enclosure is positive and the corrected branch rule passes \(B\), whether or not \(B\) straddles \(D=0\).  If \(B\cap C\neq\varnothing\), choose \(x\in B\cap C\).  For every \(y\in B\),
\[
 D(y)\leq D(x)+|D(y)-D(x)|
 <-\delta_{\boldsymbol d}/2.
\]
For \(e_t<\delta_{\boldsymbol d}/4\), the upper enclosure of \(D\) on \(B\) is therefore negative, so the branch discards \(B\).  If \(C=\varnothing\), every leaf is contained in \(U\) and the first case alone applies.  This gives the required finite-cover/mesh argument: once the terminal mesh is smaller than the minimum of the finitely many positive \(a_{\boldsymbol d}\), every leaf lies in the passing case or meets a compact set on which the distance constraint has the displayed negative margin and hence lies in the discard case.  If instead \(S=\varnothing\), compactness directly gives \(\max_{K_{\boldsymbol d}}D=-\delta_{\boldsymbol d}<0\), so all sufficiently accurate leaves are discarded and the remote bound is \(+\infty\).  It follows that every remote tree eventually terminates successfully under precisely the corrected remote-subset rule in \(\mathrm{def{:}certificate\text{-}procedure}\).

For the local tests, let \(\mu_*>0\) and \(\kappa_*>0\) be common lower bounds for the stipulated exact chart strong-monotonicity margins and Jacobian singular values.  The deterministic chart trees are assumed eventually to produce a valid cover.  On every resulting chart, the exact symmetric projected-Jacobian matrix has least eigenvalue at least \(\mu_*\), while
\[
 J_{\boldsymbol d,\mathcal T}(\vartheta)^{\mathsf T}
 J_{\boldsymbol d,\mathcal T}(\vartheta)
 \succeq \kappa_*^2 I.
\]
Uniform derivative-enclosure and rounding convergence makes the corresponding interval \(LDL^{\mathsf T}\) tests succeed for all sufficiently large \(t\).  Indeed, one may fix positive dyadic numbers strictly below \(\mu_*\) and \(\kappa_*\); the arithmetic-precision mesh eventually contains such test values, and the uniform matrix-enclosure error is eventually smaller than their slack.  The eventual valid-cover hypothesis then supplies the required coverage and co-containment checks.  The same argument, applied to the strict uniform interiority, denominator, pivot, and positive-definiteness margins in the statement, makes every remaining algebraic and covariance test succeed.  The exact rank computation succeeds because \(\operatorname{rank}(M)=K\), and the allocation checks succeed because \(\rho\in\Delta_J^{\mathbb Q}\) and every \(\rho_j>0\).

It remains to verify the precision check.  Compactness of the inference boxes and continuity of \(V_\tau\) show that the maximum defining \(v_\tau^\circ\) is finite and attained.  Put
\[
 s_*=N_{\max}-N_{\mathrm{req}}^\circ>0.
\]
Uniform convergence of the covariance, derivative, matrix-inverse, and contrast enclosures implies that the recorded variance upper bound \(\max_a(\overline v_\tau)_a\) converges from above to \(v_\tau^\circ\).  Since \(\alpha\in(0,1)\) and \(w_{\max}>0\) are fixed, the map
\[
 v\longmapsto z_{1-\alpha/2}^2v/w_{\max}^2
\]
is continuous.  Therefore, for all sufficiently large \(t\),
\[
 N_{\mathrm{req}}(\Xi_t)
 <N_{\mathrm{req}}^\circ+s_*/2
 <N_{\max}.
\]

There are finitely many graph tuples and test categories, so the preceding thresholds have a common finite index \(t_0\).  For every \(t\geq t_0\), the syntax and box checks, full-rank and positive-allocation checks, all graph, remote, chart, covariance, interiority, and linear-algebra checks, and the required-well check pass.  The literal return rule in \(\mathrm{def{:}certificate\text{-}procedure}\) therefore gives
\[
 \mathfrak C(\Xi_t)=\mathtt{CERTIFIED}\qquad(t\geq t_0).
\]
This conclusion is consistent with the unconditional finite termination and one-sided soundness of \(\mathrm{thm{:}certificate\text{-}soundness\text{-}complexity}\).  The proof uses the theorem's stated strict-slack and uniform-convergence hypotheses only; it does not establish them for either prescribed synthetic benchmark.
