Because \(C\) has row rank \(p\), its \(p\) rows can be extended by \(n-p\) standard coordinate rows to a basis of \(\mathbb R^n\). Choose such an extension \(T_0\in\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb R)\), with \((T_0)_{\mathsf O,:}=C\), and write \(W_0=T_0^{-1}\). Fix \(j\in J_x(C)\).

We first justify the shear in the certificate. Since
\[
 e_j^{\mathsf T}=e_j^{\mathsf T}W_0T_0
 =\sum_{i=1}^n(W_0)_{ji}(T_0)_{i,:},
\]
the coordinates of \(e_j^{\mathsf T}\) in the row basis of \(T_0\) are the entries of row \(j\) of \(W_0\). Moreover, because the \(p-1\) rows of \(C_{-x,:}\) are independent, restriction of those rows to the coordinates other than \(j\) loses rank precisely when a nonzero linear combination is supported on coordinate \(j\). Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(C_{-x,-j})<p-1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 e_j^{\mathsf T}\in\operatorname{rowspan}(C_{-x,:})
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 (W_0)_{jx}=0\ \text{ and }\ (W_0)_{j\ell}=0\ \text{for all }\ell\in\mathsf L.
\]
The left side is false because \(j\in J_x(C)\). If \((W_0)_{jx}\ne0\), take \(T=T_0\) and \(W=W_0\). Otherwise there is \(\ell\in\mathsf L\) with \((W_0)_{j\ell}\ne0\); in particular \(n>p\). Set
\[
 T=(I_n+e_\ell e_x^{\mathsf T})T_0,
 \qquad
 W=W_0(I_n-e_\ell e_x^{\mathsf T}).
\]
Since \(x\ne\ell\), \((e_\ell e_x^{\mathsf T})^2=0\), so the displayed shear matrices are mutual inverses and \(W=T^{-1}\). Only latent row \(\ell\) of \(T_0\) changes; hence \(T_{\mathsf O,:}=C\). Furthermore
\[
 W_{jx}=(W_0)_{jx}-(W_0)_{j\ell}=-(W_0)_{j\ell}\ne0.
\]

Because \(W^{-1}=T\) and \(T_{xj}=C_{xj}\ne0\), the cofactor identity gives
\[
 (-1)^{j+x}\det(W_{-j,-x})
 =\operatorname{cof}_{jx}(W)
 =\det(W)T_{xj}\ne0.
\]
Expanding this nonzero determinant exhibits a permutation of the rows other than \(j\) onto the columns other than \(x\) for which every selected entry of \(W_{-j,-x}\) is nonzero. Augment it by assigning source row \(j\) to equation column \(x\). Let \(\Pi\) be the resulting row-permutation matrix, so row \(x\) of \(\Pi\) is \(e_j^{\mathsf T}\) and every diagonal entry of \(\Pi W\) is nonzero. Thus
\[
 D=\operatorname{diag}(\Pi W),\qquad
 Q_*=D^{-1}\Pi W,\qquad
 H_*=D^{-1}\Pi
\]
are well defined. The matrix \(H_*\) is monomial, \(Q_*\) is invertible, and
\[
 (Q_*)_{ii}=D_{ii}^{-1}(\Pi W)_{ii}=1
 \quad(i\in\mathsf S).
\]
Also
\[
 Q_*^{-1}H_*
 =W^{-1}\Pi^{-1}D D^{-1}\Pi=T,
\]
so \([Q_*^{-1}H_*]_{\mathsf O,:}=C\), which proves exact equality of the entire observed law, not merely equality of a covariance or cumulant. The augmentation has \((H_*)_{xj}=D_{xx}^{-1}\ne0\), hence \(s_{\mathcal M_j}(x)=j\). Finally
\[
 (Q_*^{-1})_{xx}
 =e_x^{\mathsf T}T\Pi^{-1}De_x
 =T_{xj}D_{xx}=C_{xj}D_{xx}\ne0.
\]
Since \(Q_*\) is invertible, the principal-cofactor identity yields
\[
 \det((Q_*)_{-x,-x})=\det(Q_*)(Q_*^{-1})_{xx}\ne0.
\]
All conditions in the completion-fiber definition are verified, so \(\mathcal M_j=(Q_*,H_*)\in\mathfrak F_x(C,\varepsilon)\).

If \(C\) is rational, the standard-coordinate basis extension may be selected using exact Gaussian elimination, so \(T_0\) and \(W_0\) are rational. The shear and permutation preserve rationality, all entries of \(D\) are nonzero rational numbers, and inversion of this diagonal matrix preserves rationality. Hence the returned \(Q_*\) and \(H_*\) are rational.
