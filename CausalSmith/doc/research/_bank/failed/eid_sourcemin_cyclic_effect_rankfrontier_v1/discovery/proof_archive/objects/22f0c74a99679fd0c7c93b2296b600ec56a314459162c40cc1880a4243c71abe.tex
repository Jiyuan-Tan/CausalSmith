For a fixed normalized representation \(X=C\varepsilon\) with mutually independent, nondegenerate, moment-determinate non-Gaussian sources and pairwise nonproportional mixing columns, write \(A_\nu=\{j:\operatorname{cum}_\nu(\varepsilon_j)\ne0\}\) and \(R_\nu=|A_\nu|\). Whenever \(R_\nu\ge2\) and \(\nu\ge2R_\nu-1\), a three-block reshape with degrees \((R_\nu-1,R_\nu-1,\nu-2R_\nu+2)\) has a unique minimal rank-\(R_\nu\) decomposition and recovers exactly the active projective directions; the case \(R_\nu=1\) is rank-one and immediate. In particular, starting at \(\nu=2n-1\), the fixed degrees \((n-1,n-1,\nu-2n+2)\) give an exact population inverse which unions the recovered directions and stops after a finite representation-dependent order with \(\{[c_1],\ldots,[c_n]\}\). It therefore recovers every full-law response-vector frontier \(\mathcal R_x^{\mathrm{vec}}(C)\) and every scalar projection \(\mathcal R_{xy}(C)\), without cross-order label alignment. No finite stopping order is uniform over the stated moment-determinate class. Already for \(p=n=2\), that class admits two fixed separated direction sets and sequences of observational experiments whose \(N\)-sample total-variation distance tends to zero while their response frontiers remain separated. Consequently no direction or frontier inverse is uniformly consistent there, and no confidence procedure with coverage strictly greater than one half can have uniformly vanishing Hausdorff uncertainty. A uniform root-\(N\) heterogeneous-order frontier law therefore requires quantitative non-Gaussianity, moment, Veronese-conditioning, angular, probe/eigen-gap, deletion-rank, and denominator margins; the common-order separated confidence theorem retains its simultaneous scalar and vector coverage and root-\(N\) conclusions.
