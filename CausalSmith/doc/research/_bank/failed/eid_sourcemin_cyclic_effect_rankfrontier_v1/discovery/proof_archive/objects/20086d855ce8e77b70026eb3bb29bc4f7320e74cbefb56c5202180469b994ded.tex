The anchor assumptions make \((n,C,\varepsilon)\) an irreducible ICA representation of \(P_X\). We first prove that \(J_x(C)\) is nonempty, which also supplies at least one completion through the witness lemma. Extend the full-row-rank matrix \(C\) to \(T_0\in\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb R)\), with \((T_0)_{\mathsf O,:}=C\), and write \(W_0=T_0^{-1}\). The \((x,x)\) entry of \(T_0W_0=I_n\) is
\[
 1=\sum_{j=1}^n C_{xj}(W_0)_{jx}. \tag{32}
\]
Thus some \(j\) satisfies \(C_{xj}(W_0)_{jx}\ne0\). The inverse-cofactor identity gives
\[
 (W_0)_{jx}=\frac{(-1)^{x+j}\det((T_0)_{-x,-j})}{\det(T_0)}, \tag{33}
\]
so \((T_0)_{-x,-j}\) is nonsingular. Its \(p-1\) observed rows are \(C_{-x,-j}\); they are linearly independent, whence \(\operatorname{rank}(C_{-x,-j})=p-1\). Together with \(C_{xj}\ne0\), this proves \(j\in J_x(C)\).

Take an arbitrary \(\mathcal M\in\mathfrak F_x(P_X)\). The law-fiber response-and-rank lemma supplies a matched anchor source \(j\in J_x(C)\) and proves
\[
 \boldsymbol\theta_{-x\leftarrow x}(\mathcal M)
 =\frac{C_{-x,j}}{C_{xj}}
 \in\mathcal R_x^{\mathrm{vec}}(C). \tag{34}
\]
It also proves, by the cited irreducible-ICA theorem, that the completion source count is \(n\) and its observed mixing is \(C\Pi D\). This proves the first inclusion and the claimed invariance across the entire observational-law fiber, not merely across a fixed source representation.

Conversely, let \(r=C_{-x,j}/C_{xj}\in\mathcal R_x^{\mathrm{vec}}(C)\). The canonical witness lemma returns \(\mathcal M_j\in\mathfrak F_x(P_X)\) and gives \(\boldsymbol\theta_{-x\leftarrow x}(\mathcal M_j)=r\). Hence every frontier point is attained by a full same-law structural completion. The two inclusions prove the vector equality. Taking coordinate \(y\) in (34) and in every witness proves the scalar equality; it is a projection of the same completion-wise vector set, not a Cartesian recombination across outcomes.

For complexity, Gaussian elimination extends the rows of \(C\) and inverts \(T_0\) in \(O(n^3)\) arithmetic operations. Equivalence (16) scans the rows of \(W_0\) and classifies all admissible columns in \(O(n^2)\) additional operations. For each of at most \(n\) admissible sources, the shear is \(O(n^2)\), an augmenting-path algorithm finds the complementary perfect matching in \(O(n^3)\), and row normalization is \(O(n^2)\). Thus all full witnesses cost \(O(n^4)\). Exact zero tests are part of the real-arithmetic model; no floating-point or bit-complexity claim is made. Each vector point has its indexed witness, and selecting one index for every distinct scalar projection yields one exact witness per scalar value.
