Because \(C\) has row rank \(p\), its rows extend to a basis of \(\mathbb R^n\); choose such an extension \(T_0\in\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb R)\) with \((T_0)_{\mathsf O,:}=C\), and put \(W_0=T_0^{-1}\). We first record the elementary deletion criterion
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(C_{-x,-j})<p-1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 e_j^{\top}\in\operatorname{rowspan}(C_{-x,:})
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 (W_0)_{jx}=0\ \text{ and }\ (W_0)_{j\ell}=0\ \text{ for every }\ell\in\mathsf L. \tag{5}
\]
For the first equivalence, the \(p-1\) rows of \(C_{-x,:}\) are independent because they are a subset of the independent rows of \(C\). Their restrictions to the coordinates other than \(j\) are dependent exactly when a nonzero linear combination is supported only at coordinate \(j\); independence before restriction makes that combination a nonzero multiple of \(e_j^{\top}\). For the second equivalence, the identity \(W_0T_0=I_n\) gives the unique expansion
\[
 e_j^{\top}=\sum_{i=1}^n (W_0)_{ji}(T_0)_{i,:}.
\]
It belongs to the span of the observed rows other than \(x\) exactly when its coefficients on row \(x\) and on all latent rows vanish. This proves (5).

Now fix \(j\in J_x(C)\). If \((W_0)_{jx}\ne0\), set \(T=T_0\) and \(W=W_0\). If \((W_0)_{jx}=0\), the full deletion rank in the definition of \(J_x(C)\), together with (5), supplies \(\ell\in\mathsf L\) with \((W_0)_{j\ell}\ne0\). Define
\[
 T=(I_n+e_\ell e_x^{\top})T_0,
 \qquad
 W=W_0(I_n-e_\ell e_x^{\top}). \tag{6}
\]
Since \(x\ne\ell\), \((e_\ell e_x^{\top})^2=0\), so the two shear matrices in (6) are inverse and \(W=T^{-1}\). The shear changes only latent row \(\ell\), hence \(T_{\mathsf O,:}=C\), and
\[
 W_{jx}=(W_0)_{jx}-(W_0)_{j\ell}=-(W_0)_{j\ell}\ne0. \tag{7}
\]
When \(n=p\), the set \(\mathsf L\) is empty and (5) shows directly that the second case cannot occur.

Since \(W^{-1}=T\) and \(T_{xj}=C_{xj}\ne0\), the cofactor identity gives
\[
 (-1)^{j+x}\det(W_{-j,-x})
 =\operatorname{cof}(W)_{jx}
 =\det(W)(W^{-1})_{xj}
 =\det(W)C_{xj}\ne0. \tag{8}
\]
Thus \(W_{-j,-x}\) is nonsingular. In the determinant expansion of this matrix, at least one permutation term is nonzero. Its nonzero entries give a perfect matching between the rows other than \(j\) and the columns other than \(x\). Augment that matching by assigning row \(j\) to column \(x\), and let \(\Pi\) be the row-permutation matrix placing every matched row in its matched column. All diagonal entries of \(\Pi W\) are nonzero. Therefore
\[
 D=\operatorname{diag}(\Pi W),\qquad
 Q=D^{-1}\Pi W,\qquad
 H=D^{-1}\Pi \tag{9}
\]
are well defined. The matrix \(H\) is monomial, \(Q\) is invertible, and (9) gives \(Q_{ii}=1\) for all \(i\). Moreover,
\[
 Q^{-1}H
 =W^{-1}\Pi^{-1}D D^{-1}\Pi
 =T,
\]
so \([Q^{-1}H]_{\mathsf O,:}=T_{\mathsf O,:}=C\). Row \(x\) of \(H\) has its unique nonzero entry in column \(j\), whence \(s_{\mathcal M_j}(x)=j\). Finally, deleting row and column \(x\) in (9) gives, up to the complementary row permutation,
\[
 Q_{-x,-x}=D_{-x,-x}^{-1}(\Pi W)_{-x,-x},
\]
and \((\Pi W)_{-x,-x}\) is a row permutation of the nonsingular matrix \(W_{-j,-x}\) from (8). Hence \(\det(Q_{-x,-x})\ne0\). All defining conditions of \(\mathfrak F_x(C,\varepsilon)\) have now been verified.

If \(C\) is rational, Gaussian elimination can extend its rows using rational coordinate rows, so \(T_0\), \(W_0\), and the shear in (6) are rational. The permutation is integral, every entry of \(D\) is a nonzero rational number, and (9) uses only rational arithmetic and division by those entries. Thus the returned \(Q_j\) and \(H_j\) are rational.
