Fix \(\mathcal M=(m_{\mathcal M},Q,H,\xi^{\mathcal M})\in\mathfrak F_x(P_X)\), and abbreviate
\[
 T=Q^{-1}H,
 \qquad W=T^{-1}=H^{-1}Q,
 \qquad A=A^{\mathcal M}=T_{\mathsf O,:}. \tag{5}
\]
All inverses in (5) exist: \(Q\) is nonsingular by observational solvability, and a monomial matrix \(H\) is nonsingular because each of its unique row and column entries is nonzero. Let \(k=s_{\mathcal M}(x)\). By the definition of a monomial assignment there is an \(h\ne0\) such that
\[
 H_{:,k}=h e_x,
 \qquad e_k^\top H^{-1}=h^{-1}e_x^\top. \tag{6}
\]
The unit structural diagonal and (5)--(6) imply
\[
 W_{kx}=h^{-1}Q_{xx}=h^{-1}\ne0. \tag{7}
\]
The inverse-cofactor identity applied to \(W=T^{-1}\) gives
\[
 W_{kx}=\frac{(-1)^{k+x}\det(T_{-x,-k})}{\det(T)}. \tag{8}
\]
Thus \(T_{-x,-k}\) is nonsingular. Its observed rows other than \(x\) are exactly \(A_{-x,-k}\); a subset of the rows of a nonsingular matrix is linearly independent, and therefore
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(A_{-x,-k})=p-1. \tag{9}
\]

Next, (6) gives \(T_{:,k}=hQ^{-1}e_x\), hence
\[
 A_{xk}=h(Q^{-1})_{xx}. \tag{10}
\]
The principal cofactor identity is
\[
 \det(Q_{-x,-x})=\det(Q)(Q^{-1})_{xx}. \tag{11}
\]
Both determinants on the left and the first determinant on the right are nonzero by the two solvability conditions in the definition of \(\mathfrak F_x(P_X)\). Consequently \((Q^{-1})_{xx}\ne0\), and (10) gives \(A_{xk}\ne0\). Equations (9)--(10) show \(k\in J_x(A)\).

To compute the intervention response, replace the \(x\)-th structural equation by \(V_x=t\). For every row other than \(x\), differentiation of \(QV=H\xi^{\mathcal M}\) with respect to \(t\) gives
\[
 Q_{-x,-x}\frac{\partial V_{-x}}{\partial t}=-Q_{-x,x}. \tag{12}
\]
Postintervention solvability permits inversion. On the other hand, the rows other than \(x\) of \(Q(Q^{-1})_{:,x}=e_x\) give
\[
 Q_{-x,-x}(Q^{-1})_{-x,x}=-Q_{-x,x}(Q^{-1})_{xx}. \tag{13}
\]
Division by the nonzero \((Q^{-1})_{xx}\) and comparison of (12)--(13) yield
\[
 \boldsymbol\theta_{-x\leftarrow x}(\mathcal M)
 =\frac{(Q^{-1})_{\mathsf O\setminus\{x\},x}}{(Q^{-1})_{xx}}
 =\frac{A_{-x,k}}{A_{xk}}, \tag{14}
\]
where the last equality uses \(A_{:,k}=h(Q^{-1})_{\mathsf O,x}\). Coordinate \(y\) gives the scalar response.

The anchor assumptions make \((n,C,\varepsilon)\) an irreducible ICA representation of \(P_X\): its sources are mutually independent and nondegenerate, its columns are nonzero and pairwise nonproportional, and every anchor source is non-Gaussian. Membership of \(\mathcal M\) makes \((m_{\mathcal M},A,\xi^{\mathcal M})\) another irreducible representation of the same law. The cited irreducible-ICA uniqueness lemma therefore gives \(m_{\mathcal M}=n\) and \(A=C\Pi D\). Apply the monomial-invariance lemma to the admissible index \(k\). If \(j=\pi(k)\), then \(j\in J_x(C)\), and substituting (4) into (14) proves both displayed response formulas.
