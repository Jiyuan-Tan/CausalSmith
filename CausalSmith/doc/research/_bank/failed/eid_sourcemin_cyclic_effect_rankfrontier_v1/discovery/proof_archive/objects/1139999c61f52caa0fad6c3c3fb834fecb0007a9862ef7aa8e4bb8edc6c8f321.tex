Define the causal response-set target by the left side of (17); it ranges over structural mechanisms and is not defined to equal an observed-law functional. We prove its equality with the computable deletion-rank map on the right.

The canonical law-fiber witness lemma first gives \(J_x(C)\ne\varnothing\). For the necessity inclusion, fix an arbitrary \(\mathcal M\in\mathfrak F_x(P_X)\). By the cited irreducible-ICA law-uniqueness lemma, \(m_{\mathcal M}=n\) and
\[
 A^{\mathcal M}=C\Pi D \tag{19}
\]
for a permutation matrix \(\Pi\) and an invertible diagonal matrix \(D\). Write \(\Pi e_k=e_{\pi(k)}\), and let \(k=s_{\mathcal M}(x)\). The law-fiber response-rank lemma gives
\[
 A^{\mathcal M}_{xk}\ne0,
 \qquad \operatorname{rank}(A^{\mathcal M}_{-x,-k})=p-1,
 \qquad
 \boldsymbol\theta_{\leftarrow x}(\mathcal M)
 =\frac{A^{\mathcal M}_{-x,k}}{A^{\mathcal M}_{xk}}. \tag{20}
\]
The monomial-frontier invariance lemma applied to (19) turns (20) into
\[
 \pi(k)\in J_x(C),
 \qquad
 \boldsymbol\theta_{\leftarrow x}(\mathcal M)
 =\frac{C_{-x,\pi(k)}}{C_{x,\pi(k)}}
 \in\mathcal R_x^{\mathrm{vec}}(C). \tag{21}
\]
This proves the inclusion of the causal target in the observable functional for every completion of the observational law, not merely for a fixed source tuple.

For sufficiency, take any \(r\in\mathcal R_x^{\mathrm{vec}}(C)\). By definition, \(r=C_{-x,j}/C_{xj}\) for some \(j\in J_x(C)\). The canonical witness lemma constructs
\[
 \mathcal M_j=(n,Q_j,H_j,\varepsilon)\in\mathfrak F_x(P_X),
 \qquad A^{\mathcal M_j}=C,
 \qquad
 \boldsymbol\theta_{\leftarrow x}(\mathcal M_j)=r. \tag{22}
\]
Thus every value of the observable deletion-rank map is attained by a full structural completion having exactly the canonical representation. Equations (21)--(22) prove (17) and its sharpness over the whole law fiber.

For any completion, the definition of the scalar hard-intervention effect and the response-rank lemma give
\[
 \theta_{y\leftarrow x}(\mathcal M)
 =\operatorname{pr}_y\boldsymbol\theta_{\leftarrow x}(\mathcal M).
\]
Projecting both sides of (17) coordinatewise therefore proves (18); in particular, no Cartesian combination of effects from different source indices is introduced.

If distinct \(j,k\in J_x(C)\) had equal vector ratios, adjoining their common treatment coordinate \(1\) would give
\[
 \frac{C_{:,j}}{C_{xj}}=\frac{C_{:,k}}{C_{xk}}.
\]
Because both denominators are nonzero, this would make columns \(c_j\) and \(c_k\) proportional, contrary to the distinct-directions assumption. Hence the index-to-vector map is injective. Since \(J_x(C)\) is nonempty, the vector target is a singleton exactly when \(|J_x(C)|=1\).

For the scalar claim, the row of \(C_{\{x,y\},J_x(C)}\) indexed by \(x\) is nonzero entrywise. This two-row matrix has rank one if and only if
\[
 C_{xj}C_{yk}-C_{xk}C_{yj}=0
 \quad\text{for all }j,k\in J_x(C). \tag{23}
\]
Division by \(C_{xj}C_{xk}\ne0\) shows that (23) is equivalent to equality of all ratios \(C_{yj}/C_{xj}\), hence to \(|\mathcal R_{xy}(C)|=1\). By (18), this is exactly scalar point identification over the law fiber.

Finally, the canonical witness lemma computes every admissible index and its exact completion in \(O(n^4)\) operations and preserves rationality. The vector and scalar ratios require only \(O(pn)\) additional arithmetic, and selecting one index for each repeated scalar ratio cannot increase the bound. This proves the stated certificate and complexity conclusions.
