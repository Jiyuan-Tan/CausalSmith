Write \(\lambda_{j,\nu}=\operatorname{cum}_\nu(\varepsilon_j)\). Mutual independence implies additivity of the log characteristic function:
\[
 \log\mathbb E\exp(i t^{\top}X)
 =\sum_{j=1}^n\log\mathbb E\exp\{i(t^{\top}c_j)\varepsilon_j\}.
\]
Equating the formal derivatives of total order \(\nu\), which exist because the moment sequences exist, gives the observable cumulant tensor
\[
 \mathcal T_\nu=\operatorname{cum}_\nu(X)
 =\sum_{j=1}^n\lambda_{j,\nu}c_j^{\otimes \nu}. \tag{23}
\]
Set \(m_{\mathrm V}=n-1\). For \(\nu\ge R_0=3m_{\mathrm V}\), take \(\ell_1=\ell_2=m_{\mathrm V}\) and \(\ell_3=\nu-2m_{\mathrm V}\), so \(\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3\ge m_{\mathrm V}\). Let \(A_\nu=\{j:\lambda_{j,\nu}\ne0\}\) and \(R_\nu=|A_\nu|\). Reshaping (23) into three blocks yields
\[
 \widetilde{\mathcal T}_\nu
 =\sum_{j\in A_\nu}\lambda_{j,\nu}
 \operatorname{vec}(c_j^{\otimes \ell_1})\otimes
 \operatorname{vec}(c_j^{\otimes \ell_2})\otimes
 \operatorname{vec}(c_j^{\otimes \ell_3}). \tag{24}
\]
If \(R_\nu=0\), the tensor is zero and contributes no direction. If \(R_\nu\ge1\), the Veronese--Lagrange lemma applies because each block degree is at least \(n-1\ge R_\nu-1\). Thus each of the three factor matrices in (24) has Kruskal rank \(R_\nu\). For \(R_\nu\ge2\),
\[
 k_{\ell_1}+k_{\ell_2}+k_{\ell_3}=3R_\nu\ge2R_\nu+2,
\]
so Kruskal's cited Theorem 4a makes (24) essentially unique, up to one common permutation and reciprocal component scalings. The case \(R_\nu=1\) is immediate. The decomposition has minimal rank \(R_\nu\): its mode-one matricization has rank \(R_\nu\), since the first factor matrix has full column rank and the Khatri--Rao product of the other two factor matrices has full column rank. For the latter assertion, applying a left-inverse row for one column of the second factor matrix to a putative linear dependence isolates a nonzero third-factor column and forces that coefficient to vanish.

This gives an explicit population recovery operation at order \(\nu\): minimize \(R\) over integers \(0\le R\le n\) and variables \(\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_R\ne0\) and nonzero block factors \(z_{s\ell}\), subject to
\[
 \widetilde{\mathcal T}_\nu=\sum_{\ell=1}^R\gamma_\ell z_{1\ell}\otimes z_{2\ell}\otimes z_{3\ell},
 \qquad z_{1\ell}\in\{\operatorname{vec}(z^{\otimes \ell_1}):z\ne0\},
\]
with the analogous Veronese constraints of degrees \(\ell_2\) and \(\ell_3\) on \(z_{2\ell}\) and \(z_{3\ell}\), and with the three factors in each summand required to have a common projective preimage. The preceding rank and uniqueness argument proves that its solution set is exactly the active directions in (24), modulo permutation and component scalings. The Veronese map is injective on real projective space: if \(z^{\otimes \ell_1}\) and \(w^{\otimes \ell_1}\) are proportional while \(z,w\) are not, a linear functional vanishing at \(w\) but not at \(z\), contracted in all \(\ell_1\) modes, gives a contradiction. Hence every active \([c_j]\) is computable from the exact tensor.

By the cited Marcinkiewicz cumulant theorem, every nondegenerate non-Gaussian source with moments of all orders has infinitely many nonzero cumulants. Therefore, for each \(j\), some \(\nu_j\ge R_0\) has \(\lambda_{j,\nu_j}\ne0\). Since there are only \(n\) sources,
\[
 K_{C,\varepsilon}=\max_{1\le j\le n}\nu_j<\infty. \tag{25}
\]
Incrementing \(\nu\) from \(R_0\), decomposing (24), and taking the set-theoretic union of the recovered projective directions stops as soon as the union has its known size \(n\), no later than (25). Pairwise nonproportionality prevents accidental merging. No labels need be transported between orders. Column ratios and deletion ranks are invariant under nonzero rescaling and permutation, so applying the deletion-rank map to these projective directions recovers every \(\mathcal R_{xy}(C)\).

We next prove that the stopping order and inference conclusion cannot be uniform under the frozen qualitative assumptions. Fix any finite \(K\ge3\). The near-Gaussian moment-matching lemma supplies a non-Gaussian moment-determinate law \(F\) whose first \(K\) moments equal those of \(\mathcal N(0,1)\). Let two independent sources have law \(F\), and consider
\[
 C_0=I_2,
 \qquad
 C_1=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\gamma&-\sin\gamma\\[2pt]\sin\gamma&\cos\gamma\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad 0<\gamma<\pi/4. \tag{26}
\]
Both matrices have normalized, nonzero, nonproportional columns. Independence and equality of the univariate moments through \(K\) imply that all joint source moments through total degree \(K\) are Gaussian. Orthogonal invariance of the Gaussian moments then shows that the two observed models in (26) have identical moments, and hence identical cumulant tensors, through order \(K\), although their projective direction sets are separated by angle \(\gamma\). No inverse using a uniformly fixed finite order can distinguish them.

For the statistical obstruction, use the same bounded perturbation construction with laws \(F_t\) satisfying \(d_{\mathrm{TV}}(F_t,\mathcal N(0,1))\to0\). Let \(P_{0,t}\) and \(P_{1,t}\) be the observed laws under the two matrices in (26). Total variation contracts under measurable maps and obeys the product bound, while both orthogonal images of two independent standard Gaussians equal \(\mathcal N(0,I_2)\). Hence
\[
 d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P_{0,t},P_{1,t})
 \le4d_{\mathrm{TV}}(F_t,\mathcal N(0,1)). \tag{27}
\]
Choose \(t=t_N\) so that the right side is at most \(N^{-2}\). The product inequality gives
\[
 d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P_{0,t_N}^{\otimes N},P_{1,t_N}^{\otimes N})\le N^{-1}\longrightarrow0. \tag{28}
\]
The two direction sets in (26) remain a fixed positive projective distance apart. If a direction estimator were uniformly consistent, choosing the nearer of those two fixed sets would define a test whose sum of error probabilities tends to zero. For every test region \(A\), however,
\[
 P_{0,t_N}^{\otimes N}(A^c)+P_{1,t_N}^{\otimes N}(A)
 \ge1-d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P_{0,t_N}^{\otimes N},P_{1,t_N}^{\otimes N}),
\]
which tends to one by (28), a contradiction.

The causal frontiers are separated as well. For \(x=1,y=2\), direct evaluation of the deletion minors gives
\[
 \mathcal R_{12}(C_0)=\{0\},
 \qquad
 \mathcal R_{12}(C_1)=\{\tan\gamma,-\cot\gamma\}. \tag{29}
\]
Thus the same testing argument rules out uniform Hausdorff consistency for the frontier. More generally, suppose a random confidence region for the frontier has uniform coverage \(1-\alpha>1/2\) and uniformly vanishing Hausdorff diameter. By (28), under the first experiment it contains the first frontier with probability at least \(1-\alpha\) and the second with probability at least \(1-\alpha-o(1)\). Their intersection has probability at least \(1-2\alpha-o(1)>0\), but on that intersection its diameter is at least the fixed Hausdorff separation in (29), a contradiction.

Two deterministic boundary sequences show why the causal map additionally needs denominator and deletion-rank margins. After harmless column normalization, for
\[
 D_t=\begin{pmatrix}t&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad t\to0,
\]
the frontier \(\mathcal R_{12}(D_t)=\{1/t,1\}\) for \(t\ne0,1\), whereas \(\mathcal R_{12}(D_0)=\{1\}\). For
\[
 E_0=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0&1\\1&0&0&0\\0&0&1&1\end{pmatrix}
\]
and \(E_t\) obtained by replacing its \((2,2)\) entry by \(t\ne0\), one checks \(\mathcal R_{12}(E_0)=\{0\}\) but \(\mathcal R_{12}(E_t)=\{0,t,1\}\). All columns are nonzero and pairwise nonproportional near zero, and the matrices have full row rank. Thus qualitative nonvanishing and rank conditions alone do not yield a uniform modulus. Quantitative lower cumulant, moment, Veronese-conditioning, angular, probe/eigen-gap, deletion-rank, and denominator controls are precisely the type of hypotheses needed for a robust finite-order analysis; the frozen common-order separated class supplies them, and the already proved common-order inverse and confidence theorem retain their simultaneous coverage and root-\(N\) Hausdorff conclusion. The heterogeneous population inverse is therefore positive, while heterogeneous uniform inference over the unrestricted class is negative rather than solved.
