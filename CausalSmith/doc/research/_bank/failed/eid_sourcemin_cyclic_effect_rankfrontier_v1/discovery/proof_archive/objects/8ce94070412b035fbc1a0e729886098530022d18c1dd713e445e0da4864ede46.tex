For every source \(j\in\mathsf S\) and order \(\nu\ge1\), define
\[
 \lambda_{j,\nu}=\operatorname{cum}_\nu(\varepsilon_j).
\]
The assumption \(\mathrm{ass:all\text{-}source\text{-}moments}\) makes this cumulant finite at every order. For integrable products, the joint moment--cumulant identity is the finite polynomial
\[
 \operatorname{cum}(Z_1,\ldots,Z_\nu)
 =\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak P_\nu}
 (|\pi|-1)!(-1)^{|\pi|-1}
 \prod_{B\in\pi}\mathbb E\!\left[\prod_{s\in B}Z_s\right]. \tag{20}
\]
Multilinearity follows directly by expanding each expectation in (20). If the arguments split into two nonempty independent subfamilies, moment factorization in every summand of (20), followed by grouping the partitions according to their induced partitions on the two subfamilies, makes the alternating sum zero. Substituting \(X_i=\sum_{j=1}^n C_{ij}\varepsilon_j\), using this multilinearity, and applying \(\mathrm{ass:independent\text{-}sources}\) therefore yields
\[
 \mathcal T_\nu:=\operatorname{cum}_\nu(X)
 =\sum_{j=1}^n\lambda_{j,\nu}c_j^{\otimes\nu}
 =\sum_{j\in A_\nu}\lambda_{j,\nu}c_j^{\otimes\nu}. \tag{21}
\]

Fix \(\nu\ge2n-1\), set \(s=n-1\) and \(t=\nu-2n+2\), and reshape (21) into three mode blocks. Since \(n\ge p\ge2\), \(s\ge1\), and the order restriction gives \(t\ge1\). Hence
\[
 \widetilde{\mathcal T}_\nu
 =\sum_{j\in A_\nu}\lambda_{j,\nu}a_j\otimes b_j\otimes d_j,
 \qquad
 a_j=b_j=\operatorname{vec}(c_j^{\otimes s}),
 \qquad
 d_j=\operatorname{vec}(c_j^{\otimes t}). \tag{22}
\]
Let \(R=R_\nu\). Suppose first that \(R\ge2\). Pairwise nonproportionality of the columns of \(C\), together with \(s=n-1\ge R-1\), permits application of \(\mathrm{lem:veronese\text{-}lagrange\text{-}rank}\), which gives Kruskal rank \(R\) to each of the first two factor matrices in (22). Every pair of third-mode columns is linearly independent. Indeed, if \(z^{\otimes t}\) and \(w^{\otimes t}\) were proportional, contraction against \(f^{\otimes t}\), for any linear functional \(f\) vanishing on \(w\), would give \(f(z)^t=0\). Every functional vanishing on \(w\) would then vanish on \(z\), so \(z\in\operatorname{span}(w)\), contrary to pairwise nonproportionality. Thus the third factor matrix has Kruskal rank at least two, and
\[
 k_1+k_2+k_3\ge R+R+2=2R+2. \tag{23}
\]
The hypotheses of \(\mathrm{lem:kruskal\text{-}three\text{-}factor\text{-}uniqueness}\) now hold, so (22) is unique up to a common permutation and reciprocal nonzero term scalings.

The length \(R\) is also minimal. Let \(A=[a_j]_{j\in A_\nu}\), \(B=[b_j]_{j\in A_\nu}\), and \(K=[b_j\otimes d_j]_{j\in A_\nu}\). The first two matrices have full column rank. If \(\sum_j q_jb_j\otimes d_j=0\), apply to the \(b_j\)-component the row of a left inverse of \(B\) that selects column \(j\). This gives \(q_jd_j=0\); since \(d_j\ne0\), \(q_j=0\). Hence \(K\) has full column rank. The first matricization of (22) equals
\[
 A\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_{j,\nu})K^\top. \tag{24}
\]
It has matrix rank \(R\), because both outer factors have column rank \(R\) and each \(\lambda_{j,\nu}\), \(j\in A_\nu\), is nonzero by definition. A sum of fewer than \(R\) rank-one tensors has a first matricization of rank below \(R\), contradicting (24). Therefore the tensor rank is exactly \(R\). Kruskal uniqueness, followed by injectivity of the projective Veronese map proved by the contraction argument above, shows that every least feasible decomposition returns exactly \(\{[c_j]:j\in A_\nu\}\).

The minimization in \(\mathrm{def:multi\text{-}order\text{-}handle}\) is an explicit finite population-oracle operation. At fixed \(\nu\) and \(R\), its variables are \(\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_R\in\mathbb R\) and \(z_1,\ldots,z_R\in\mathbb R^p\). Its constraints are the finitely many coordinate equations
\[
 \widetilde{\mathcal T}_\nu
 =\sum_{h=1}^{R}\gamma_h
 \operatorname{vec}(z_h^{\otimes(n-1)})\otimes
 \operatorname{vec}(z_h^{\otimes(n-1)})\otimes
 \operatorname{vec}(z_h^{\otimes(\nu-2n+2)}), \tag{25}
\]
together with \(\gamma_h^2>0\) and \(\lVert z_h\rVert_2^2>0\). The handle queries exact feasibility of (25) for the finite list \(R=0,\ldots,n\) and selects the least feasible value. Exact projective equality of two nonzero returned vectors is decided by vanishing of all two-by-two minors of the matrix having those vectors as columns. Thus every operation at a processed order is specified. The population law supplies exact cumulants; no finite-sample numerical claim is being made.

If \(R=0\), (21) is the zero tensor and the empty decomposition is least feasible. If \(R=1\), (21) is a nonzero rank-one tensor, and proportional nonzero tensor powers have proportional base vectors by the contraction argument following (22). Thus the unique active direction is recovered. This handles every possible active rank.

The same reasoning gives the ex-post adaptive certificate. If \(R_\nu\ge2\) and \(\nu\ge2R_\nu-1\), use block degrees
\[
 (R_\nu-1,R_\nu-1,\nu-2R_\nu+2). \tag{26}
\]
The first two degrees are at least \(R_\nu-1\), while the third is positive. The first two Kruskal ranks are therefore \(R_\nu\), the third is at least two, and the arguments (23)--(24) prove uniqueness and minimality. Because \(R_\nu\) is known only after solving the order-\(\nu\) systems, this is an ex-post certificate. The executable schedule in \(\mathrm{def:multi\text{-}order\text{-}handle}\) instead uses the known source count \(n\) and begins at \(2n-1\); it never presupposes \(R_\nu\).

We next prove finite pointwise stopping. The hypotheses of \(\mathrm{lem:marcinkiewicz\text{-}cumulant\text{-}tail}\) hold for each source: all moments exist by \(\mathrm{ass:all\text{-}source\text{-}moments}\), nondegeneracy is \(\mathrm{ass:nondegenerate\text{-}sources}\), and non-Gaussianity is \(\mathrm{ass:nongaussian\text{-}sources}\). Hence every source has infinitely many nonzero cumulants of order at least three. Their orders are unbounded, so for each \(j\) there is a finite \(\nu_j\ge2n-1\) with \(\lambda_{j,\nu_j}\ne0\). Since the source count is the fixed finite integer \(n\),
\[
 K_{C,\varepsilon}=\max_{1\le j\le n}\nu_j<\infty. \tag{27}
\]
The orderwise recovery already proved implies that processing every order through \(K_{C,\varepsilon}\) includes every \([c_j]\). Pairwise nonproportionality prevents erroneous projective deduplication, and no direction outside this set is returned. The handle stops when the union first has cardinality \(n\), and it returns
\[
 \{[c_1],\ldots,[c_n]\}=\mathcal D(P_X), \tag{28}
\]
where the equality is the full-law conclusion of \(\mathrm{thm:sharp\text{-}effect\text{-}frontier}\). No cross-order label alignment is required. Choosing arbitrary nonzero representatives yields \(C'=C\Pi D\); \(\mathrm{lem:frontier\text{-}monomial\text{-}invariance}\) gives \(\mathcal V_x(C')=\mathcal V_x(C)\) and \(\mathcal R_{xy}(C')=\mathcal R_{xy}(C)\). The sharp-effect theorem then proves that these evaluated sets, rather than their definitions alone, equal the causal law-fiber targets. Notice that moment determinacy has not been used anywhere in this positive argument.

We now prove that no cumulant cutoff is uniform, even on the stricter moment-determinate subclass of the all-finite-moments class. Fix a finite \(K\ge3\) and \(\rho>0\). By \(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}moment\text{-}near\text{-}gaussian\text{-}witness}\), there is a centered, variance-one, non-Gaussian, moment-determinate law \(F\) with
\[
 d_{\mathrm{TV}}(F,G)<\rho,
 \qquad G=\mathcal N(0,1), \tag{29}
\]
whose raw moments through order \(K\), and therefore its cumulants through order \(K\), equal those of \(G\). Moment determinacy here concerns the full finite moment sequence, so this witness belongs to the all-finite-moments class. Moreover, \(\mathrm{lem:marcinkiewicz\text{-}cumulant\text{-}tail}\) guarantees that some cumulant of \(F\) beyond order \(K\) is nonzero; matching an arbitrarily prescribed finite initial segment therefore delays, but cannot eliminate, informative orders.

Give two sources the independent law \(F\), choose \(0<\gamma<\pi/4\), and set
\[
 C_0=I_2,
 \qquad
 C_1=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \cos\gamma&-\sin\gamma\\
 \sin\gamma&\cos\gamma
 \end{pmatrix}. \tag{30}
\]
Both matrices are normalized and full rank and have nonzero pairwise nonproportional columns. Independence makes all joint source moments of total degree at most \(K\) equal those under \(G^{\otimes2}\). Since \(C_1\) is orthogonal and \(G^{\otimes2}\) is orthogonally invariant, the two observed laws generated by (30) have identical moments, hence identical cumulants, through order \(K\), while their projective direction sets differ. Since \(K\) was arbitrary, no finite cumulant cutoff recovers directions throughout even this stricter subclass.

For the statistical boundary, for each \(N\) take the preceding witness with
\[
 d_{\mathrm{TV}}(F_N,G)\le\frac{1}{4N^2}, \tag{31}
\]
and let \(P_{k,N}\) be the observed law generated by \(C_k\) and two independent \(F_N\) sources. Total variation contracts under measurable pushforward, because every output event is pulled back to an input event. A telescoping expansion of product signed measures and the triangle inequality also give, for every integer \(m\ge1\),
\[
 d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\mu^{\otimes m},\nu^{\otimes m})
 \le m\,d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\mu,\nu). \tag{32}
\]
Each \(P_{k,N}\) is within \(2d_{\mathrm{TV}}(F_N,G)\) of the common Gaussian law \(G^{\otimes2}\), which is unchanged by either orthogonal matrix in (30). Thus the triangle inequality, (31), and (32) imply
\[
 d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P_{0,N},P_{1,N})\le N^{-2},
 \qquad
 d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P_{0,N}^{\otimes N},P_{1,N}^{\otimes N})\le N^{-1}\longrightarrow0. \tag{33}
\]

The two projective direction sets in (30) have fixed positive Hausdorff separation. A uniformly consistent direction-set estimator would induce a test that selects the nearer of these two fixed targets, with both error probabilities tending to zero. For every test event \(E\), however, the definition of total variation yields
\[
 P_{0,N}^{\otimes N}(E^c)+P_{1,N}^{\otimes N}(E)
 \ge1-d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P_{0,N}^{\otimes N},P_{1,N}^{\otimes N}), \tag{34}
\]
whose right side tends to one by (33), a contradiction.

The corresponding causal frontiers are also fixed and separated. Applying \(\mathrm{def:admissible\text{-}source\text{-}set}\) with \(x=1\) and \(y=2\) gives
\[
 \mathcal R_{12}(C_0)=\{0\},
 \qquad
 \mathcal R_{12}(C_1)=\{\tan\gamma,-\cot\gamma\}. \tag{35}
\]
Thus the same nearest-target argument excludes a uniformly consistent frontier estimator. More strongly, let \(\mathcal U_N\) be a random compact set having uniform frontier coverage at least \(1-\beta\), where \(\beta<1/2\). Under \(P_{0,N}^{\otimes N}\), coverage of the first target has probability at least \(1-\beta\). Coverage of the second target has probability at least \(1-\beta\) under \(P_{1,N}^{\otimes N}\), and (33) transfers that event to \(P_{0,N}^{\otimes N}\) with probability at least \(1-\beta-o(1)\). Their intersection therefore has probability at least \(1-2\beta-o(1)>0\). On that intersection, \(0\in\mathcal R_{12}(C_0)\) and \(-\cot\gamma\in\mathcal U_N\), whence
\[
 d_H(\mathcal U_N,\mathcal R_{12}(C_0))\ge\cot\gamma. \tag{36}
\]
No uniformly honest frontier-containing confidence union with coverage strictly above one half can therefore have uniformly vanishing Hausdorff error. Because every \(F_N\) is moment-determinate, these impossibility statements hold on the stricter moment-determinate subclass and hence also on the larger all-finite-moments class.

Finally, two explicit families prove discontinuity at the asserted deterministic boundaries. Column normalization may be applied because \(\mathrm{lem:frontier\text{-}monomial\text{-}invariance}\) preserves the frontiers. For
\[
 D_t=\begin{pmatrix}t&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix},
\]
the matrices are full rank and have nonzero nonproportional columns for every sufficiently small \(|t|\) with \(t\ne1\). Direct deletion-rank calculation gives
\[
 \mathcal R_{12}(D_t)=\{1/t,1\}\quad(t\ne0),
 \qquad
 \mathcal R_{12}(D_0)=\{1\}. \tag{37}
\]
This is discontinuity at a vanishing treatment loading. For
\[
 E_t=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 1&1&0&1\\
 1&t&0&0\\
 0&0&1&1
 \end{pmatrix},
\]
the matrices are full row rank with nonzero pairwise nonproportional columns for all sufficiently small \(|t|\). Deleting row one and each candidate column gives
\[
 \mathcal R_{12}(E_0)=\{0\},
 \qquad
 \mathcal R_{12}(E_t)=\{0,t,1\}\quad(t\ne0). \tag{38}
\]
At \(t=0\), only columns two and four are admissible and both ratios are zero. At \(t\ne0\), columns one, two, and four are admissible and yield ratios \(1,t,0\), respectively. This proves discontinuity at a deletion-rank boundary.

Equations (33), (37), and (38) show that the qualitative all-finite-moments assumptions give neither a uniform stopping order nor a uniform statistical modulus. Quantitative non-Gaussianity, moment, Veronese-conditioning, angular, probe/eigen-gap, denominator, and deletion-rank margins are therefore indispensable for a uniform root-\(N\) conclusion. On the fixed-order separated iid class, \(\mathrm{thm:uniform\text{-}confidence\text{-}frontier}\) supplies simultaneous vector and scalar coverage with \(O(N^{-1/2})\) Hausdorff error. This completes both the pointwise population-oracle construction on the all-finite-moments class and the nonuniform inference boundary, without using moment determinacy in the positive result.
