Because \(\Pi\) is a permutation matrix and \(D\) is nonsingular diagonal, there is a bijection \(\pi:\{1,\ldots,n\}\to\{1,\ldots,n\}\) and nonzero numbers \(d_k=D_{kk}\) such that
\[
 A_{:,k}=d_k C_{:,\pi(k)}. \tag{1}
\]
Thus \(A_{xk}\ne0\) if and only if \(C_{x,\pi(k)}\ne0\). After deleting row \(x\) and column \(k\), equation (1) gives
\[
 A_{-x,-k}=C_{-x,-\pi(k)}\Pi_{-k,-\pi(k)}D_{-k,-k}, \tag{2}
\]
where \(\Pi_{-k,-\pi(k)}\) is a square permutation matrix and \(D_{-k,-k}\) is nonsingular diagonal. Right multiplication by the two invertible matrices in (2) preserves row rank, so
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(A_{-x,-k})
 =\operatorname{rank}(C_{-x,-\pi(k)}). \tag{3}
\]
The definition of \(J_x\), together with (1)--(3), proves the asserted equivalence of admissible indices. For an admissible \(k\), cancellation of the nonzero scalar \(d_k\) in (1) yields
\[
 \frac{A_{-x,k}}{A_{xk}}
 =\frac{d_kC_{-x,\pi(k)}}{d_kC_{x,\pi(k)}}
 =\frac{C_{-x,\pi(k)}}{C_{x,\pi(k)}}. \tag{4}
\]
Taking coordinate \(y\) gives the scalar equality. Since \(\pi\) is bijective, (4) and the definitions of the vector and scalar frontiers prove both set equalities.
