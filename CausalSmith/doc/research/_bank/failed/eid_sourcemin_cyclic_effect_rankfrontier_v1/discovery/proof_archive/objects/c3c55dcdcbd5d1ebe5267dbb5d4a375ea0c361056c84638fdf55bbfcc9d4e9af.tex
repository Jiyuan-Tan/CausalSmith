Put \(e=\lVert\mathcal T'_r-\mathcal T_r\rVert_F\). Contract the last \(q\) modes against \(u^{\otimes(q-1)}\otimes w\), matricize the remaining modes in two blocks of degree \(d\), and denote the resulting matrices by \(M_w,M'_w\). Contraction by unit vectors, matricization, and orthogonal compression do not increase Frobenius norm, so every compressed difference below has operator norm at most \(e\).

Let \(U\) be an orthonormal basis for \(\operatorname{range}(V_d)\), and set
\[
 S=U^\top V_d,
 \qquad A_w=U^\top M_wU
 =S\operatorname{diag}\!\left(\lambda_j(u^\top c_j)^{q-1}(w^\top c_j)\right)S^\top. \tag{23}
\]
The lifted-rank margin gives \(s_{\min}(S)=s_{\min}(V_d)\ge\sigma\), while \(\lVert S\rVert\le\lVert V_d\rVert_F=\sqrt n\). For \(w=u\), every diagonal magnitude in (23) is at least \(\kappa\sigma^q\). Hence
\[
 s_{\min}(A_u)\ge\kappa\sigma^q s_{\min}(S)^2
 \ge\kappa\sigma^{q+2}=\eta,
 \qquad \lVert A_u^{-1}\rVert\le\eta^{-1}. \tag{24}
\]
The same factorization also gives \(\lVert A_w\rVert\le n\Lambda\) for every unit \(w\).

Compress the primed contractions using this same \(U\), so \(A'_w=U^\top M'_wU\) and \(\lVert A'_w-A_w\rVert\le e\). Since \(e<\eta/2\), (24) and the Neumann series imply
\[
 \lVert(A'_u)^{-1}\rVert\le2\eta^{-1}. \tag{25}
\]
Writing \(S'=U^\top V'_d\), the factorization \(A'_u=S'D'_u(S')^\top\), with nonsingular diagonal \(D'_u\), and invertibility of \(A'_u\) show that \(S'\) is invertible. Therefore, for \(G_w=A_wA_u^{-1}\) and \(G'_w=A'_w(A'_u)^{-1}\),
\[
 G_w=S\operatorname{diag}\!\left(\frac{w^\top c_j}{u^\top c_j}\right)S^{-1},
 \quad
 G'_w=S'\operatorname{diag}\!\left(\frac{w^\top c'_j}{u^\top c'_j}\right)(S')^{-1}. \tag{26}
\]
The inverse identity and (24)--(25) give
\[
 \lVert G'_w-G_w\rVert
 \le\left(\frac{2}{\eta}+\frac{2n\Lambda}{\eta^2}\right)e
 =he. \tag{27}
\]

For \(w=v\), the eigenvalues of \(G_v\) are real and pairwise separated by at least \(\sigma\). Around each eigenvalue draw a complex circle of radius \(g=\sigma/3\). From (26), the resolvent on a circle is bounded by \(\chi/g\), where \(\chi=\lVert S\rVert\lVert S^{-1}\rVert\le\sqrt n/\sigma\). The condition \(e<\sigma/(6\chi h)\) makes \((\chi/g)he<1/2\). The resolvent identity and Neumann series therefore show that every circle contains exactly one eigenvalue of \(G'_v\), counted algebraically, and that the corresponding rank-one spectral projectors satisfy
\[
 \lVert P'_j-P_j\rVert
 \le \frac{2\chi^2he}{g}
 =\frac{6\chi^2he}{\sigma},
 \qquad
 \lVert P_j\rVert\le\chi,
 \qquad
 \lVert P'_j\rVert\le2\chi. \tag{28}
\]
Indeed, integrate \((zI-G'_v)^{-1}(G'_v-G_v)(zI-G_v)^{-1}\) around the circle; its length divided by \(2\pi\) is \(g\), which gives (28). Continuity along \(G_v+t(G'_v-G_v)\) and the integer-valued rank of a projector justify the one-eigenvalue pairing. This supplies one common permutation \(\pi\).

For each coordinate vector \(e_i\), define \(z_{ij}=\operatorname{tr}(G_{e_i}P_j)\) and define \(z'_{i,\pi(j)}\) analogously. Simultaneous diagonalization in (26) gives
\[
 z_j=\frac{c_j}{u^\top c_j},
 \qquad
 z'_{\pi(j)}=\frac{c'_{\pi(j)}}{u^\top c'_{\pi(j)}}. \tag{29}
\]
Using \(|\operatorname{tr}(R)|\le n\lVert R\rVert\), \(\lVert G_{e_i}\rVert\le n\Lambda/\eta\), and (27)--(28), we obtain coordinatewise
\[
 |z'_{i,\pi(j)}-z_{ij}|
 \le nh\left(2\chi+\frac{6n\Lambda\chi^2}{\eta\sigma}\right)e
 =L_ze. \tag{30}
\]
The probe orientation fixes the sign in (29), and unit normalization gives \(\lVert z_j\rVert=1/(u^\top c_j)\ge1\), with the same statement for \(z'_{\pi(j)}\). Normalization on vectors of norm at least one is two-Lipschitz, so (30) yields
\[
 \lVert c'_{\pi(j)}-c_j\rVert_2
 \le2\sqrt p\,L_ze. \tag{31}
\]
Squaring (31), summing over \(j\), and taking square roots proves
\[
 \lVert C'_{\pi}-C\rVert_F
 \le2\sqrt{np}\,L_ze=L_Ce.
\]
Only the displayed numerical restrictions were used, which proves the extension to the estimator's feasible factor set.
