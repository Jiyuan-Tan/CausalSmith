Fix a tuple \(\zeta\in\mathfrak P_{p,n,r}(a,\delta,\sigma,\kappa,\Lambda,M)\). Unit normalization gives
\[
 \lVert X\rVert_2\le\sum_{j=1}^n|\varepsilon_j|.
\]
The convexity inequality \((\sum_j t_j)^{2r}\le n^{2r-1}\sum_jt_j^{2r}\), followed by the source-moment envelope, yields
\[
 \mathbb E\lVert X\rVert_2^{2r}
 \le n^{2r-1}\sum_{j=1}^n\mathbb E|\varepsilon_j|^{2r}
 \le n^{2r}M\le H. \tag{13}
\]
Let \(\mu\in\mathbb R^m\) collect the nonconstant raw moments through total degree \(r\), and let \(\widetilde\mu\) be their untrimmed empirical averages. If a coordinate has degree \(k\le r\), its squared monomial is bounded by \(1+\lVert X\rVert_2^{2r}\). Thus (13) gives variance at most \(1+H\) for every coordinate and
\[
 \mathbb E\lVert\widetilde\mu-\mu\rVert_2^2
 \le\frac{m(1+H)}{N}. \tag{14}
\]
Moreover every true coordinate has absolute value at most \((1+H)^{1/2}\le B_m\). Coordinatewise clipping to \([-B_m,B_m]\) is projection onto an interval containing the true coordinate, so it cannot increase the Euclidean error. Markov's inequality applied to (14) therefore proves, for
\[
 E_N=\{\lVert\widehat\mu-\mu\rVert_2\le e_N\},
\qquad
 \inf_{\zeta\in\mathfrak P_{p,n,r}}P_X^{\otimes N}(E_N)\ge1-\alpha. \tag{15}
\]

For an ordered tensor coordinate, the moment--cumulant formula is
\[
 \operatorname{cum}(X_{i_1},\ldots,X_{i_r})
 =\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak P_r}(-1)^{|\pi|-1}(|\pi|-1)!
   \prod_{A\in\pi}\mathbb E\!\left[\prod_{k\in A}X_{i_k}\right]. \tag{16}
\]
On the moment box \([-B_m,B_m]^m\), telescoping a product with \(|\pi|\) factors bounds its change by \(|\pi|B_m^{|\pi|-1}\lVert\widehat\mu-\mu\rVert_2\). There are \(p^r\) ordered tensor coordinates, so (16) and the Frobenius norm give, on \(E_N\),
\[
 \lVert\widehat{\mathcal T}_r-\mathcal T_r\rVert_F
 \le p^{r/2}\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak P_r}(|\pi|-1)!|\pi|B_m^{|\pi|-1}e_N
 =\tau_N. \tag{17}
\]

The true normalized factor pair is feasible for the minimum-distance problem. The feasible numerical restrictions are closed and bounded: the columns lie on unit spheres, the cumulants lie in the compact set \(\{t:\kappa\le|t|\le\Lambda\}\), and all singular-value, loading, and pencil-gap restrictions are closed. Nonemptiness is assumed. Hence a minimizer exists, and the fixed lexicographic selection makes \((\widehat C,\widehat\lambda)\) a statistic. Its optimality and (17) imply
\[
 \left\lVert\sum_j\widehat\lambda_j\widehat c_j^{\otimes r}
       -\mathcal T_r\right\rVert_F
 \le 2\tau_N. \tag{18}
\]
The deterministic tensor-pencil argument in the common-order local-inverse lemma uses only the numerical restrictions imposed on these two factor pairs. Therefore, if \(2\tau_N<\epsilon_0\), (18) supplies one common permutation under which
\[
 \lVert\widehat C-C\rVert_F\le 2L_C\tau_N=b_N. \tag{19}
\]

Suppose also \(b_N<\min\{a,\delta\}/2\). Both \(C\) and the feasible fit obey the alternative that each loading is zero or has magnitude at least \(a\). Hence (19) prevents a zero loading in either matrix from being paired with a nonzero loading in the other. The zero patterns agree. For every deletion matrix, Weyl's singular-value inequality and (19) give
\[
 \left|s_{\min}(\widehat C_{-i,-j})-s_{\min}(C_{-i,-j})\right|
 \le\lVert\widehat C-C\rVert_F<\delta/2.
\]
Because each of the two feasible singular values is either zero or at least \(\delta\), their zero status agrees. Consequently \(\widehat J_x=J_x(C)\) simultaneously for every observed \(x\).

For \(j\in J_x(C)=\widehat J_x\), both denominators have magnitude at least \(a\), while unit normalization bounds \(|C_{yj}|\le1\). Using (19) coordinatewise gives
\[
 \left|\frac{\widehat C_{yj}}{\widehat C_{xj}}-\frac{C_{yj}}{C_{xj}}\right|
 \le \frac{|\widehat C_{yj}-C_{yj}|}{|\widehat C_{xj}|}
 +\frac{|C_{yj}|\,|\widehat C_{xj}-C_{xj}|}{|\widehat C_{xj}|\,|C_{xj}|}
 \le(a^{-1}+a^{-2})b_N=w_N. \tag{20}
\]
Thus every true ratio belongs to its interval in \(\mathcal U_{xy}\). Conversely, every point of that interval is within \(w_N\) of its estimated center and, by (20), within another \(w_N\) of the corresponding true ratio. Hence
\[
 \mathcal R_{xy}(C)\subseteq\mathcal U_{xy},
 \qquad
 d_H(\mathcal U_{xy},\mathcal R_{xy}(C))\le2w_N \tag{21}
\]
simultaneously for all \(x\ne y\). If either deterministic informative-regime inequality fails, the definition gives \(\mathcal U_{xy}=[-a^{-1},a^{-1}]\); every admissible ratio lies in this interval because its numerator is at most one and its denominator is at least \(a\). Coverage therefore holds in both regimes on the single event \(E_N\), and (15) proves the displayed uniform probability bound. Finally, with dimensions, margins, \(M\), and \(\alpha\) fixed, \(L_m\), \(L_C\), and \(H\) are constants, so \(\tau_N=O(N^{-1/2})\) and \(2w_N=O(N^{-1/2})\).
