Fix \(\mathcal M=(Q,H)\in\mathfrak F_x(C,\varepsilon)\), put \(T=Q^{-1}H\), and put \(W=T^{-1}=H^{-1}Q\). Both matrices are invertible by observational solvability and the monomial-source condition, and fixed-law matching gives \(T_{\mathsf O,:}=C\). Write \(j=s_{\mathcal M}(x)\) and \(h=H_{xj}\ne0\). Because column \(j\) of the monomial matrix \(H\) has its sole nonzero entry in row \(x\), row \(j\) of \(H^{-1}\) has its sole nonzero entry \(h^{-1}\) in column \(x\). Hence, using \(Q_{xx}=1\),
\[
 W_{jx}=h^{-1}Q_{xx}=h^{-1}\ne0.
\]
The cofactor identity for \(T^{-1}=W\) gives
\[
 \det(T_{-x,-j})=(-1)^{x+j}\det(T)W_{jx}\ne0.
\]
Thus the \(n-1\) rows of \(T_{-x,-j}\) are independent. Their subfamily indexed by \(\mathsf O\setminus\{x\}\) is exactly the row family of \(C_{-x,-j}\), whence
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(C_{-x,-j})=p-1.
\]
It remains to verify the loading condition. With \(A=Q^{-1}\), the same monomial calculation gives \(C_{xj}=A_{xx}h\). The principal-cofactor identity and postintervention solvability give
\[
 A_{xx}=\frac{\det(Q_{-x,-x})}{\det Q}\ne0,
\]
so \(C_{xj}\ne0\). Both defining conditions of \(J_x(C)\) hold, proving \(s_{\mathcal M}(x)\in J_x(C)\).
