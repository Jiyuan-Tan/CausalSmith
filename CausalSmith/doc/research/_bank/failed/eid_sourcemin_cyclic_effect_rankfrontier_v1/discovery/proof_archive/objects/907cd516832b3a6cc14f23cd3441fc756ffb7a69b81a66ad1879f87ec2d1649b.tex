We first prove nonemptiness without assuming a completion. Extend the full-row-rank matrix \(C\) to \(T_0\in\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb R)\), and put \(W_0=T_0^{-1}\). The \((x,x)\)-entry of \(T_0W_0=I_n\) is
\[
1=\sum_{j=1}^n C_{xj}(W_0)_{jx}. \tag{24}
\]
Therefore some \(j\) has \(C_{xj}(W_0)_{jx}\ne0\). For that \(j\), the inverse-cofactor formula gives
\[
0\ne (W_0)_{jx}
=\frac{(-1)^{j+x}\det((T_0)_{-x,-j})}{\det T_0}. \tag{25}
\]
Thus \((T_0)_{-x,-j}\) is nonsingular. Its observed rows other than \(x\), restricted away from column \(j\), are the rows of \(C_{-x,-j}\); as a subset of the rows of a nonsingular matrix they are linearly independent. Hence
\[
\operatorname{rank}(C_{-x,-j})=p-1.
\]
Together with \(C_{xj}\ne0\), this proves \(j\in J_x(C)\), so \(J_x(C)\ne\varnothing\). The canonical law-fiber witness lemma then gives \(\mathcal M_j\in\mathfrak F_x(P_X)\), proving that the law fiber is nonempty.

For necessity, take an arbitrary \(\mathcal M\in\mathfrak F_x(P_X)\). The law-fiber response-rank lemma gives
\[
\boldsymbol\theta_{\leftarrow x}(\mathcal M)\in\mathcal R_x^{\mathrm{vec}}(C), \tag{26}
\]
so the attainable set is contained in the displayed frontier. Conversely, let \(r\in\mathcal R_x^{\mathrm{vec}}(C)\). By the frontier definition there is \(j\in J_x(C)\) such that
\[
r=\frac{C_{-x,j}}{C_{xj}}. \tag{27}
\]
The canonical witness lemma returns \(\mathcal M_j\in\mathfrak F_x(P_X)\) whose response vector equals (27). Hence every frontier point is attained, establishing the reverse inclusion and sharpness. Taking the \(y\)-coordinate on both sides is a surjective coordinate projection of the same set, and the canonical witness formula gives
\[
\theta_{y\leftarrow x}(\mathcal M_j)=\frac{C_{yj}}{C_{xj}}.
\]
This proves the scalar equality without forming any cross-outcome Cartesian product.

We next prove the identification criteria. Because the attainable sets are nonempty, a response is identified over the law fiber precisely when its sharp set has one element. Suppose two indices \(j,k\in J_x(C)\) have equal vector ratios. Then
\[
\frac{C_{-x,j}}{C_{xj}}=\frac{C_{-x,k}}{C_{xk}}. \tag{28}
\]
Both denominators are nonzero. Restoring the \(x\)-coordinate, which equals one after division by the treatment loading, shows from (28) that
\[
\frac{c_j}{C_{xj}}=\frac{c_k}{C_{xk}}.
\]
Thus \(c_j\) and \(c_k\) are proportional. The distinct-direction assumption forces \(j=k\). Therefore the map \(j\mapsto C_{-x,j}/C_{xj}\) is injective on \(J_x(C)\), and
\[
|\mathcal R_x^{\mathrm{vec}}(C)|=1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad |J_x(C)|=1. \tag{29}
\]
For the scalar criterion, every \(C_{xj}\), \(j\in J_x(C)\), is nonzero, so the treatment row of \(C_{\{x,y\},J_x(C)}\) is nonzero. This two-row matrix has rank one exactly when
\[
C_{xj}C_{yk}-C_{xk}C_{yj}=0
\quad\text{for all }j,k\in J_x(C). \tag{30}
\]
Dividing (30) by the nonzero product \(C_{xj}C_{xk}\) shows that (30) is equivalent to equality of all ratios \(C_{yj}/C_{xj}\), hence to \(|\mathcal R_{xy}(C)|=1\). If either singleton condition fails, choose two indices giving distinct corresponding values. The canonical witness lemma supplies two completions in the same observational-law fiber realizing those distinct values, so they are exact countermodels.

Finally, one Gaussian elimination extends the rows of \(C\) and computes \(W_0=T_0^{-1}\) in \(O(n^3)\) arithmetic operations. Equivalence (15) classifies every \(j\) after scanning row \(j\) of \(W_0\), for \(O(n^2)\) total tests after the inverse. For each of at most \(n\) admissible indices, the shear costs \(O(n^2)\), an augmenting-path perfect-matching algorithm on the \((n-1)\)-by-\((n-1)\) nonzero pattern costs \(O(n^3)\), and permutation plus diagonal normalization costs \(O(n^2)\). Thus all witnesses cost \(O(n^4)\) operations. Computing vector ratios and deduplicating them costs at most \(O(n^3)\), so it does not alter the bound. Exact zero tests are part of this real-arithmetic model; no floating-point or bit-complexity assertion is made. The rationality statement is exactly the last conclusion of the canonical witness lemma.
