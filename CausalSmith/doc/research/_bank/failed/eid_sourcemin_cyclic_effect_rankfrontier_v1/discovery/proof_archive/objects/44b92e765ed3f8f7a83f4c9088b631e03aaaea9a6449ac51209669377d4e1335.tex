The causal target is the set of equilibrium responses over \(\mathfrak F_x^{\mathrm{law}}(P_X)\); the deletion-rank sets are observable-law functionals only after their equality to that target and their invariance to the choice of mixing representative have been proved.

We first establish the full-law identification assertion. The displayed representation \(P_X=\mathcal L(C\varepsilon)\) is one of the representations unioned in the definition of \(\mathcal D(P_X)\): the nonzero-column and distinct-direction assumptions make \(C\) irreducible, and the independent-source, nondegeneracy, and non-Gaussianity assumptions give the required source properties. Let \(P_X=\mathcal L(A\xi)\) be any other representation appearing in that union, with \(A\in\mathbb R^{p\times m}\). Applying \(\mathrm{lem:dai\text{-}irreducible\text{-}oica\text{-}uniqueness}\) to \(C\varepsilon\) and \(A\xi\) gives
\[
 m=n,\qquad A=C\Pi D, \tag{10}
\]
for a permutation matrix \(\Pi\) and a nonsingular diagonal matrix \(D\). Hence
\[
 \{[a_k]:1\le k\le m\}=\{[c_j]:j\in\mathsf S\}. \tag{11}
\]
Because (11) holds for every member of the defining union and the reference representation supplies one member, the definition gives
\[
 \mathcal D(P_X)=\{[c_j]:j\in\mathsf S\}. \tag{12}
\]
It is therefore the unique unordered projective direction set common to all such representations. This deduction uses only published full-law uniqueness through the declared lemma; it supplies identification as a functional of \(P_X\), not an algorithm for evaluating (12).

Next, \(J_x(C)\) is nonempty. By \(\mathrm{ass:full\text{-}row\text{-}rank}\), the \(p\) observed rows of \(C\) are linearly independent and can be extended, as in \(\mathrm{def:witness\text{-}algorithm}\), to \(T_0\in\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb R)\); put \(W_0=T_0^{-1}\). The \((x,x)\) entry of \(T_0W_0=I_n\) is
\[
 1=\sum_{j=1}^n C_{xj}(W_0)_{jx}. \tag{13}
\]
Thus there is an index \(j\) with \(C_{xj}(W_0)_{jx}\ne0\). Expanding the inverse entry by its cofactor gives
\[
 (W_0)_{jx}=(-1)^{x+j}\frac{\det((T_0)_{-x,-j})}{\det(T_0)}. \tag{14}
\]
The denominator is nonzero and the left side is nonzero, so \((T_0)_{-x,-j}\) is nonsingular. Its rows indexed by \(\mathsf O\setminus\{x\}\), restricted to the columns other than \(j\), are exactly \(C_{-x,-j}\). A row subfamily of a nonsingular matrix is linearly independent; hence \(\operatorname{rank}(C_{-x,-j})=p-1\). Together with \(C_{xj}\ne0\), \(\mathrm{def:admissible\text{-}source\text{-}set}\) yields \(j\in J_x(C)\).

For necessity, fix \(\widetilde{\mathcal M}\in\mathfrak F_x^{\mathrm{law}}(P_X)\). The hypotheses required by \(\mathrm{lem:law\text{-}fiber\text{-}response\text{-}rank}\) are exactly the declared nonzero-column, distinct-direction, independent-source, source-nondegeneracy, and source-non-Gaussianity assumptions. That lemma, using monomial canonicalization and postintervention solvability, gives
\[
 m_{\widetilde{\mathcal M}}=n,
 \qquad j=\bar s_{\widetilde{\mathcal M}}(x)\in J_x(C),
 \qquad
 \rho_x(\widetilde{\mathcal M})
 =\left(\frac{C_{ij}}{C_{xj}}\right)_{i\in\mathsf O\setminus\{x\}}. \tag{15}
\]
In particular the denominator is nonzero, the deletion rank is \(p-1\), and every causal response in the law fiber lies in \(\mathcal V_x(C)\).

For sufficiency, let \(v\in\mathcal V_x(C)\). By \(\mathrm{def:response\text{-}vector\text{-}frontier}\), some \(j\in J_x(C)\) satisfies
\[
 v=\left(\frac{C_{ij}}{C_{xj}}\right)_{i\in\mathsf O\setminus\{x\}}. \tag{16}
\]
The hypotheses of \(\mathrm{lem:canonical\text{-}exact\text{-}c\text{-}law\text{-}witness}\) are the same declared source and direction assumptions. That lemma applies the shear-and-matching certificate and returns
\[
 \widetilde{\mathcal M}_j=(n,Q_j,H_j,\varepsilon)
 \in\mathfrak F_x^{\mathrm{law}}(P_X),
 \qquad
 \rho_x(\widetilde{\mathcal M}_j)=v. \tag{17}
\]
Thus every vector in \(\mathcal V_x(C)\) is attained. Combining (15) and (17) proves the sharp response-vector equality.

Coordinate projection at \(y\) sends the vector indexed by \(j\) to \(C_{yj}/C_{xj}\). Applying this projection to the proved vector equality and using \(\mathrm{def:effect\text{-}frontier}\) proves the scalar equality with \(\mathcal R_{xy}(C)\). Moreover, any exact matrix \(C'\) obtained by choosing one nonzero representative of each member of \(\mathcal D(P_X)\) satisfies \(C'=C\Pi D\) after ordering its columns. By \(\mathrm{lem:frontier\text{-}monomial\text{-}invariance}\),
\[
 \mathcal V_x(C')=\mathcal V_x(C),
 \qquad
 \mathcal R_{xy}(C')=\mathcal R_{xy}(C). \tag{18}
\]
Equations (12) and (18) prove that the rank-and-ratio functionals are well defined from the identified projective set, even though the qualitative theorem gives no evaluation algorithm for that set.

A causal response is identified over a completion fiber precisely when it is constant on that fiber. The two sharp set equalities therefore show that the response vector is identified exactly when \(\mathcal V_x(C)\) is a singleton and that the \(y\)-coordinate is identified exactly when \(\mathcal R_{xy}(C)\) is a singleton. If the latter is not a singleton, choose \(j,k\in J_x(C)\) such that
\[
 \frac{C_{yj}}{C_{xj}}\ne\frac{C_{yk}}{C_{xk}}. \tag{19}
\]
The two witnesses \(\widetilde{\mathcal M}_j\) and \(\widetilde{\mathcal M}_k\) in (17) both have observed law \(P_X\), satisfy observational and postintervention solvability by membership in the law fiber, and have different \(y\)-coordinate responses by (19). They are the required exact same-law SCM countermodels.

It remains to verify the conditional complexity claim. Take an exact irreducible representative \(C\) as input. Gaussian elimination extends its rows to \(T_0\) and computes \(W_0=T_0^{-1}\) in \(O(n^3)\) exact real-arithmetic operations. There are at most \(n\) deletion matrices \(C_{-x,-j}\); each rank test costs \(O(n^3)\), so all membership tests for \(J_x(C)\) cost \(O(n^4)\). For each of at most \(n\) admissible indices, \(\mathrm{def:witness\text{-}algorithm}\) performs an \(O(n^2)\) shear, finds a perfect matching in an \((n-1)\)-by-\((n-1)\) nonzero pattern by at most \(n-1\) augmentations each scanning \(O(n^2)\) entries, and performs \(O(n^2)\) row normalizations. Hence all witnesses cost \(O(n^4)\). Forming ratios and selecting one witness for each distinct value is lower order. This bound begins with exact \(C\); no step purports to compute \(C\) from \(P_X\). If \(C\) is rational, \(\mathrm{lem:canonical\text{-}exact\text{-}c\text{-}law\text{-}witness}\) gives rational \(Q_j,H_j\). No argument imposes a spectral-radius or dynamical-stability condition, and moment determinacy is not used in this theorem.
