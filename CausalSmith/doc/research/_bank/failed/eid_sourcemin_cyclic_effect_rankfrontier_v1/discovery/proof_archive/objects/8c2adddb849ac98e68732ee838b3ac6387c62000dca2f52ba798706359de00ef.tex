The causal target is the set of equilibrium response vectors over \(\mathfrak F_x^{\mathrm{law}}(P_X)\). The deletion-rank frontiers become observed-law identifying functionals only after equality with that target and invariance to the exact mixing representative are proved.

First identify the projective directions from the full observational law. The displayed representation \(P_X=\mathcal L(C\varepsilon)\) is one of the representations in the union defining \(\mathcal D(P_X)\): \(\mathrm{ass:nonzero\text{-}columns}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:distinct\text{-}directions}\) make \(C\) irreducible, while \(\mathrm{ass:independent\text{-}sources}\), \(\mathrm{ass:nondegenerate\text{-}sources}\), and \(\mathrm{ass:nongaussian\text{-}sources}\) give the required source properties. Let \(P_X=\mathcal L(A\xi)\) be any other representation appearing in that union, with \(A\in\mathbb R^{p\times m}\). The hypotheses of \(\mathrm{lem:dai\text{-}irreducible\text{-}oica\text{-}uniqueness}\) are satisfied, so there are a permutation matrix \(\Pi\) and a nonsingular diagonal matrix \(D\) such that
\[
 m=n,
 \qquad
 A=C\Pi D. \tag{11}
\]
Consequently
\[
 \{[a_k]:1\le k\le m\}=\{[c_j]:j\in\mathsf S\}. \tag{12}
\]
Equation (12) holds for every member of the defining union, and the reference representation supplies a member. The definition of \(\mathcal D(P_X)\) therefore gives
\[
 \mathcal D(P_X)=\{[c_j]:j\in\mathsf S\}. \tag{13}
\]
This is uniqueness as a functional of the full law; it is not an algorithm for evaluating that functional.

Next, \(J_x(C)\) is nonempty. Put \(A_x=C_{-x,:}\). Full row rank of \(C\) implies \(\operatorname{rank}(A_x)=p-1\), because deleting one row from a linearly independent set of \(p\) rows leaves \(p-1\) independent rows. Rank--nullity gives
\[
 \dim\ker(A_x)=n-p+1,
 \qquad
 \dim\ker(C)=n-p. \tag{14}
\]
Hence some \(z\in\ker(A_x)\setminus\ker(C)\) exists. Since \(A_xz=0\), the nonzero vector \(Cz\) can be nonzero only in coordinate \(x\), and therefore
\[
 0\ne(Cz)_x=\sum_{j=1}^n C_{xj}z_j. \tag{15}
\]
At least one summand has both \(C_{xj}\ne0\) and \(z_j\ne0\). The kernel-row characterization in \(\mathrm{lem:shared\text{-}kernel\text{-}matching\text{-}certificate}\) then implies \(j\in J_x(C)\), so \(J_x(C)\ne\varnothing\).

For necessity, fix \(\widetilde{\mathcal M}\in\mathfrak F_x^{\mathrm{law}}(P_X)\). The declared irreducibility and source assumptions are precisely the hypotheses of \(\mathrm{lem:law\text{-}fiber\text{-}response\text{-}rank}\). It gives
\[
 m_{\widetilde{\mathcal M}}=n,
 \qquad
 j=\bar s_{\widetilde{\mathcal M}}(x)\in J_x(C),
 \qquad
 \rho_x(\widetilde{\mathcal M})
 =\left(\frac{C_{ij}}{C_{xj}}\right)_{i\in\mathsf O\setminus\{x\}}. \tag{16}
\]
Thus \(C_{xj}\ne0\), \(\operatorname{rank}(C_{-x,-j})=p-1\), and every response in the law fiber belongs to \(\mathcal V_x(C)\).

For sufficiency, take \(j\in J_x(C)\). The conclusion of \(\mathrm{lem:shared\text{-}kernel\text{-}matching\text{-}certificate}\) supplies \(\mathcal M_j=(Q_j,H_j)\in\mathfrak F_x(C,\varepsilon)\) with \(s_{\mathcal M_j}(x)=j\). Define
\[
 \widetilde{\mathcal M}_j=(n,Q_j,H_j,\varepsilon). \tag{17}
\]
Fixed-fiber membership supplies the unit diagonal of \(Q_j\), monomiality of \(H_j\), \(\det(Q_j)\ne0\), \(\det((Q_j)_{-x,-x})\ne0\), and
\[
 [Q_j^{-1}H_j]_{\mathsf O,:}=C. \tag{18}
\]
The source coordinates are independent, nondegenerate, and non-Gaussian by the declared assumptions; (18), together with the nonzero-column and distinct-direction assumptions on \(C\), gives irreducibility of the observed mixing. Finally,
\[
 \mathcal L([Q_j^{-1}H_j]_{\mathsf O,:}\varepsilon)
 =\mathcal L(C\varepsilon)=P_X. \tag{19}
\]
Every defining condition of \(\mathfrak F_x^{\mathrm{law}}(P_X)\), including observational and postintervention solvability, therefore holds for (17).

Because the exact observed mixing in (18) is \(C\), pairwise distinct directions make the aligned source at row \(x\) equal to \(j\). By \(\mathrm{lem:law\text{-}hard\text{-}intervention\text{-}response}\), and because column \(j\) is the unique source assigned to row \(x\) of the monomial matrix \(H_j\), for every \(i\in\mathsf O\setminus\{x\}\),
\[
 [\rho_x(\widetilde{\mathcal M}_j)]_i
 =\frac{(Q_j^{-1})_{ix}}{(Q_j^{-1})_{xx}}
 =\frac{(Q_j^{-1}H_j)_{ij}}{(Q_j^{-1}H_j)_{xj}}
 =\frac{C_{ij}}{C_{xj}}. \tag{20}
\]
The division is valid because \(j\in J_x(C)\) entails \(C_{xj}\ne0\). Thus every element of \(\mathcal V_x(C)\) is attained. Combining this with (16) proves
\[
 \{\rho_x(\widetilde{\mathcal M}):\widetilde{\mathcal M}\in\mathfrak F_x^{\mathrm{law}}(P_X)\}
 =\mathcal V_x(C). \tag{21}
\]

Projection onto coordinate \(y\) sends the vector indexed by \(j\) in (21) to \(C_{yj}/C_{xj}\). The definition of \(\mathcal R_{xy}(C)\) therefore gives
\[
 \{[\rho_x(\widetilde{\mathcal M})]_y:\widetilde{\mathcal M}\in\mathfrak F_x^{\mathrm{law}}(P_X)\}
 =\mathcal R_{xy}(C). \tag{22}
\]
If \(C'\) is another exact representative obtained by selecting and ordering one nonzero vector from every member of the set in (13), then \(C'=C\Pi D\). Applying \(\mathrm{lem:frontier\text{-}monomial\text{-}invariance}\) gives
\[
 \mathcal V_x(C')=\mathcal V_x(C),
 \qquad
 \mathcal R_{xy}(C')=\mathcal R_{xy}(C). \tag{23}
\]
Equations (13) and (23) establish representative independence.

A response is point identified over the completion fiber exactly when it is constant on that fiber. The sharp equalities (21)--(22) show that the whole response vector is identified if and only if \(\mathcal V_x(C)\) is a singleton, and its \(y\)-coordinate is identified if and only if \(\mathcal R_{xy}(C)\) is a singleton. If the latter is not a singleton, choose \(j,k\in J_x(C)\) satisfying
\[
 \frac{C_{yj}}{C_{xj}}\ne\frac{C_{yk}}{C_{xk}}. \tag{24}
\]
The two completions (17) associated with \(j\) and \(k\) both have observational law \(P_X\), both are observationally and postintervention solvable by their law-fiber membership, and have different \(y\)-coordinate responses by (20) and (24). They are exact same-law SCM countermodels.

Finally, conditional on exact \(C\), \(\mathrm{lem:shared\text{-}kernel\text{-}matching\text{-}certificate}\) computes one basis extension and inverse, all admissibility decisions from one kernel basis, and all leave-one-row matchings from one saturating matching and an alternating forest. It materializes every dense witness pair \((Q_j,H_j)\) in \(O(n^3)\) exact real-arithmetic operations. Equations (20)--(22) compute all vector and scalar frontier values from at most \(n\) columns, and exact deduplication remains within \(O(n^3)\). The same lemma proves worst-case output optimality for dense all-witness output. This bound is conditional on an exact irreducible representative \(C\); (13) is qualitative full-law identification and supplies neither an evaluator nor an end-to-end complexity bound for recovering \(C\) from \(P_X\). No assumption or construction imposes a spectral-radius or dynamical-stability restriction. This completes the proof.
