For a fixed normalized representation \(X=C\varepsilon\) with mutually independent, nondegenerate, moment-determinate non-Gaussian sources and pairwise nonproportional mixing columns, set \(m_{\mathrm V}=n-1\) and \(R_0=3m_{\mathrm V}\). There is an exact population inverse which, for each cumulant order \(\nu\ge R_0\), reshapes \(\operatorname{cum}_\nu(X)\) with block degrees at least \(m_{\mathrm V}\), takes its unique minimal three-factor decomposition, and unions the recovered projective directions. It stops after a finite representation-dependent order and recovers \(\{[c_1],\ldots,[c_n]\}\), hence every \(\mathcal R_{xy}(C)\), without cross-order label alignment. No finite stopping order is uniform over the stated moment-determinate class. Already for \(p=n=2\), that class admits two fixed, separated direction sets and associated sequences of observational experiments whose \(N\)-sample total-variation distance tends to zero. Consequently no inverse is uniformly consistent there, and no confidence procedure with coverage strictly greater than one half can have uniformly vanishing Hausdorff uncertainty, so a uniform root-\(N\) heterogeneous-order frontier law does not follow without quantitative non-Gaussianity, moment, Veronese-conditioning, angular, probe/eigen-gap, deletion-rank, and denominator margins. The existing common-order separated theorem retains its simultaneous coverage and root-\(N\) conclusion.
