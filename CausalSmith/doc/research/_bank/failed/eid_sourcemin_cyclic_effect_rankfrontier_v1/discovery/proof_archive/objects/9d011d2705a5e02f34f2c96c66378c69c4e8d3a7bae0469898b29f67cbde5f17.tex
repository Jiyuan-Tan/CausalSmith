Assume \(n=p\). Then \(\mathsf L=\varnothing\) and the full-row-rank matrix \(C\) is invertible, so the basis extension in the certificate is \(T_0=C\) and \(W_0=C^{-1}\). For \(j\in J_x(C)\), the equivalence proved in the shear-matching lemma reads
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(C_{-x,-j})<p-1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad (W_0)_{jx}=0,
\]
because there are no latent coordinates. Thus admissibility forces \((W_0)_{jx}\ne0\), and no shear is taken.

More generally, every square-fiber completion is an admissible row-permutation candidate. Indeed, fixed-law matching now gives \(Q^{-1}H=C\), hence \(Q=HC^{-1}\). Since \(H\) is monomial, there are a permutation matrix \(\Pi\) and an invertible diagonal matrix \(D\) such that \(H=D^{-1}\Pi\). Therefore
\[
 Q=D^{-1}\Pi C^{-1}.
\]
The unit diagonal of \(Q\) says exactly that the selected diagonal entries of \(\Pi C^{-1}\) are nonzero and that \(D=\operatorname{diag}(\Pi C^{-1})\). Conversely, every such admissible row permutation and normalization has monomial \(H=D^{-1}\Pi\), satisfies \(Q^{-1}H=C\), and belongs to the fiber precisely when its intervention minor is nonzero. This is the square specialization of the witness construction.

The cited LiNG-D result records that Section 4 of Lacerda et al. constructs candidates from these admissible row permutations and that its Theorem 1 makes the output a distribution-entailment equivalence class. The sharp-frontier theorem applies the additional postintervention-solvability filter and gives the exact query projection
\[
 \left\{\theta_{y\leftarrow x}(\mathcal M):
 \mathcal M\text{ is an admissible square candidate with }
 \det(Q_{-x,-x})\ne0\right\}
 =\mathcal R_{xy}(C).
\]
Thus the new statement is strictly more query-specific than the published class description while leaving its square candidate construction unchanged.
