Write \(d_k=D_{kk}\ne0\). By the definition of \(\pi\),
\[
A^{\mathcal M}_{xk}=d_kC_{x,\pi(k)}. \tag{1}
\]
After deleting column \(k\), the remaining columns of \(A^{\mathcal M}_{-x,-k}\) are a permutation of the remaining columns of \(C_{-x,-\pi(k)}\), each multiplied by a nonzero diagonal entry of \(D\). Thus there are a permutation matrix \(P_k\) and a nonsingular diagonal matrix \(D_{-k}\) such that
\[
A^{\mathcal M}_{-x,-k}=C_{-x,-\pi(k)}P_kD_{-k}. \tag{2}
\]
Right multiplication by the two invertible matrices in (2) preserves rank. Combining (1), (2), and the definition of \(J_x\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
k\in J_x(A^{\mathcal M})
&\Longleftrightarrow
A^{\mathcal M}_{xk}\ne0\ \text{ and }
\operatorname{rank}(A^{\mathcal M}_{-x,-k})=p-1\\
&\Longleftrightarrow
C_{x,\pi(k)}\ne0\ \text{ and }
\operatorname{rank}(C_{-x,-\pi(k)})=p-1\\
&\Longleftrightarrow \pi(k)\in J_x(C). \tag{3}
\end{aligned}
\]
Whenever these conditions hold, division is legal by (1), and column scaling cancels:
\[
\frac{A^{\mathcal M}_{-x,k}}{A^{\mathcal M}_{xk}}
=\frac{d_kC_{-x,\pi(k)}}{d_kC_{x,\pi(k)}}
=\frac{C_{-x,\pi(k)}}{C_{x,\pi(k)}}. \tag{4}
\]
Because \(\pi\) is a bijection, (3)--(4) identify the two vector sets. Taking their \(y\)-coordinate gives the scalar equality.
