Membership in \(\mathfrak F_x(P_X)\) gives
\[
\mathcal L(A^{\mathcal M}\xi^{\mathcal M})=P_X=\mathcal L(C\varepsilon). \tag{5}
\]
The supplied representation has nonzero pairwise nonproportional columns by the corresponding population assumptions, and its sources are mutually independent, nondegenerate, and non-Gaussian. The definition of the law fiber imposes the same properties on \((A^{\mathcal M},\xi^{\mathcal M})\). The cited all-non-Gaussian irreducible ICA uniqueness result applied to (5) therefore yields
\[
m_{\mathcal M}=n,\qquad A^{\mathcal M}=C\Pi D, \tag{6}
\]
with \(\Pi\) a permutation matrix and \(D\) nonsingular diagonal.

Let \(k=s_{\mathcal M}(x)\). Since \(H\) is monomial, there is a unique \(h=H_{xk}\ne0\) and
\[
H_{:,k}=h e_x. \tag{7}
\]
Writing \(T=Q^{-1}H\), equations (7) and the definition of \(A^{\mathcal M}\) give, for every observed \(i\),
\[
A^{\mathcal M}_{ik}=T_{ik}=h(Q^{-1})_{ix}. \tag{8}
\]
Observational solvability and postintervention solvability make both determinants in the cofactor identity nonzero, so
\[
(Q^{-1})_{xx}=\frac{\det(Q_{-x,-x})}{\det Q}\ne0. \tag{9}
\]
Consequently \(A^{\mathcal M}_{xk}=h(Q^{-1})_{xx}\ne0\), and division in the claimed ratios is valid. Equations (8)--(9), together with the scalar and vector response definitions, yield
\[
Q_{-x,-x}V_{-x}=H_{-x,:}\xi^{\mathcal M}-Q_{-x,x}t
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{\partial V_{-x}}{\partial t}=-Q_{-x,-x}^{-1}Q_{-x,x}.
\]
On the other hand, the rows other than \(x\) of \(QQ^{-1}e_x=e_x\) give
\[
Q_{-x,-x}(Q^{-1})_{-x,x}+Q_{-x,x}(Q^{-1})_{xx}=0.
\]
Postintervention solvability permits multiplication by \(Q_{-x,-x}^{-1}\), and (9) permits division by \((Q^{-1})_{xx}\). Hence the intervention derivative equals \((Q^{-1})_{-x,x}/(Q^{-1})_{xx}\). Restricting this vector to observed rows and then using (8)--(9) gives
\[
\boldsymbol\theta_{\leftarrow x}(\mathcal M)
=\frac{(Q^{-1})_{\mathsf O\setminus\{x\},x}}{(Q^{-1})_{xx}}
=\frac{A^{\mathcal M}_{-x,k}}{A^{\mathcal M}_{xk}},
\qquad
\theta_{y\leftarrow x}(\mathcal M)
=\frac{A^{\mathcal M}_{yk}}{A^{\mathcal M}_{xk}}, \tag{10}
\]
where every occurrence of \(-x\) in (10) retains only the observed rows other than \(x\).

It remains to prove the deletion-rank condition. The matrix \(T=Q^{-1}H\) is invertible. From (7) and monomiality,
\[
e_k^{\mathsf T}H^{-1}=h^{-1}e_x^{\mathsf T};
\]
hence the unit structural diagonal gives
\[
(T^{-1})_{kx}=(H^{-1}Q)_{kx}=h^{-1}Q_{xx}=h^{-1}\ne0. \tag{11}
\]
The inverse-cofactor formula applied to (11) implies
\[
0\ne (T^{-1})_{kx}
=\frac{(-1)^{x+k}\det(T_{-x,-k})}{\det T}. \tag{12}
\]
Thus \(T_{-x,-k}\) is nonsingular. Its observed rows other than \(x\) are exactly \(A^{\mathcal M}_{-x,-k}\); a subset of the rows of a nonsingular matrix is linearly independent. Therefore
\[
\operatorname{rank}(A^{\mathcal M}_{-x,-k})=p-1. \tag{13}
\]
Together with the already proved nonzero loading, (13) says \(k\in J_x(A^{\mathcal M})\). Finally, the monomial-frontier invariance lemma applied to (6) places the vector in \(\mathcal R_x^{\mathrm{vec}}(C)\), and its coordinate statement places each scalar in \(\mathcal R_{xy}(C)\).
