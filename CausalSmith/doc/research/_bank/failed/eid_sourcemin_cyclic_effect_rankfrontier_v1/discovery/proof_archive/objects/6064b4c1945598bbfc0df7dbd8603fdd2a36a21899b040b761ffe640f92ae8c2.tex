For each source \(j\in\mathsf S\) and integer order \(\nu\ge1\), define
\[
 \lambda_{j,\nu}=\operatorname{cum}_\nu(\varepsilon_j). \tag{17}
\]
The new assumption \(\mathrm{ass:all\text{-}source\text{-}moments}\) states exactly that \(\mathbb E|\varepsilon_j|^k<\infty\) for every \(j\) and every integer \(k\ge1\). Hence (17) is well defined at every order. It also gives every moment of each observed coordinate: for an integer \(\nu\ge1\), convexity yields
\[
 |X_i|^\nu
 =\left|\sum_{j=1}^n C_{ij}\varepsilon_j\right|^\nu
 \le n^{\nu-1}\sum_{j=1}^n |C_{ij}|^\nu|\varepsilon_j|^\nu,
\]
whose expectation is finite by \(\mathrm{ass:all\text{-}source\text{-}moments}\).

For integrable products through order \(\nu\), the joint cumulant is given by the moment--cumulant formula
\[
 \operatorname{cum}(Z_1,\ldots,Z_\nu)
 =\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak P_\nu}
 (|\pi|-1)!(-1)^{|\pi|-1}
 \prod_{B\in\pi}\mathbb E\!\left[\prod_{s\in B}Z_s\right]. \tag{18}
\]
This finite polynomial is multilinear. If the arguments split into two nonempty independent subfamilies, factorization of the moments in (18), grouped by the induced partitions of the two subfamilies, makes the alternating sum zero. Substitute \(X_i=\sum_j C_{ij}\varepsilon_j\), expand by multilinearity, and apply \(\mathrm{ass:independent\text{-}sources}\). Every mixed-source term vanishes, leaving
\[
 \mathcal T_\nu:=\operatorname{cum}_\nu(X)
 =\sum_{j=1}^n\lambda_{j,\nu}c_j^{\otimes\nu}
 =\sum_{j\in A_\nu}\lambda_{j,\nu}c_j^{\otimes\nu}. \tag{19}
\]
Thus (19) follows only from all finite moments, linearity of the structural representation, and source independence; moment determinacy is not used.

Fix \(\nu\ge2n-1\), set \(s=n-1\) and \(t=\nu-2n+2\), and reshape (19) into three blocks. Since \(n\ge p\ge2\), \(s\ge1\); the order restriction gives \(t\ge1\). Therefore
\[
 \widetilde{\mathcal T}_\nu
 =\sum_{j\in A_\nu}\lambda_{j,\nu}a_j\otimes b_j\otimes d_j,
 \quad
 a_j=b_j=\operatorname{vec}(c_j^{\otimes s}),
 \quad
 d_j=\operatorname{vec}(c_j^{\otimes t}). \tag{20}
\]
Write \(R=R_\nu\). Suppose first that \(R\ge2\). By \(\mathrm{ass:distinct\text{-}directions}\), the active vectors are pairwise nonproportional. Since \(s=n-1\ge R-1\), \(\mathrm{lem:veronese\text{-}lagrange\text{-}rank}\) gives Kruskal rank \(R\) to each of the first two factor matrices in (20).

Every pair of third-mode columns is linearly independent. Indeed, suppose \(z^{\otimes t}=\beta w^{\otimes t}\) for nonzero \(z,w\) and \(\beta\ne0\). If a linear functional \(f\) vanishes on \(w\), contraction with \(f^{\otimes t}\) gives \(f(z)^t=0\), hence \(f(z)=0\). Thus every functional annihilating \(w\) also annihilates \(z\), so \(z\in\operatorname{span}(w)\). Its contrapositive and distinct directions show that the third factor matrix has Kruskal rank at least two. Consequently
\[
 k_1+k_2+k_3\ge R+R+2=2R+2. \tag{21}
\]
By \(\mathrm{lem:kruskal\text{-}three\text{-}factor\text{-}uniqueness}\), the decomposition (20) is unique up to a common permutation and reciprocal nonzero scalings.

Its length is minimal. Let \(A=[a_j]_{j\in A_\nu}\), \(B=[b_j]_{j\in A_\nu}\), and \(K=[b_j\otimes d_j]_{j\in A_\nu}\). The first two have full column rank. If \(\sum_j q_jb_j\otimes d_j=0\), apply the row of a left inverse of \(B\) selecting column \(j\) to the first tensor component; this yields \(q_jd_j=0\). Since \(d_j\ne0\), every \(q_j=0\), so \(K\) also has full column rank. The first matricization of (20) is
\[
 A\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_{j,\nu})K^\top. \tag{22}
\]
Both outer factors have column rank \(R\), and each diagonal entry is nonzero by the definition of \(A_\nu\); hence (22) has matrix rank \(R\). A sum of fewer than \(R\) rank-one tensors has every matricization of rank below \(R\), a contradiction. Thus the tensor rank and the least feasible length are exactly \(R\). Kruskal uniqueness then implies that every least feasible decomposition returns precisely \(\{[c_j]:j\in A_\nu\}\).

The minimization in \(\mathrm{def:multi\text{-}order\text{-}handle}\) is an explicit finite population-oracle operation. At fixed \(\nu\) and candidate \(R\), the variables are \(\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_R\in\mathbb R\) and \(z_1,\ldots,z_R\in\mathbb R^p\). The constraints are the finitely many coordinate equations
\[
 \widetilde{\mathcal T}_\nu
 =\sum_{h=1}^{R}\gamma_h
 \operatorname{vec}(z_h^{\otimes(n-1)})\otimes
 \operatorname{vec}(z_h^{\otimes(n-1)})\otimes
 \operatorname{vec}(z_h^{\otimes(\nu-2n+2)}), \tag{23}
\]
together with \(\gamma_h^2>0\) and \(\lVert z_h\rVert_2^2>0\). The handle queries exact feasibility for \(R=0,\ldots,n\) and selects the least feasible value. Exact projective equality of two nonzero returned vectors is decided by the vanishing of every two-by-two minor of the matrix having those vectors as columns. Thus all operations at a processed order are specified; no finite-sample numerical claim is being made.

If \(R=0\), (19) is the zero tensor and the empty sum is the least feasible output. If \(R=1\), (19) is a nonzero rank-one tensor. The contraction argument following (20) proves injectivity of the projective Veronese map, so the unique active direction is recovered. This handles all active ranks.

The same reasoning gives the adaptive certificate. If \(R_\nu\ge2\) and \(\nu\ge2R_\nu-1\), use the block degrees
\[
 (R_\nu-1,R_\nu-1,\nu-2R_\nu+2). \tag{24}
\]
The first two degrees are at least \(R_\nu-1\), the third is positive, and the arguments (20)--(22) give two Kruskal ranks equal to \(R_\nu\), a third Kruskal rank at least two, uniqueness, and minimality. Because \(R_\nu\) is known only after the order-\(\nu\) systems are solved, (24) is an ex-post certificate. The executable schedule uses the known \(n\) and starts at \(2n-1\), without presupposing \(R_\nu\).

Termination now uses exactly the all-moments premise. By \(\mathrm{ass:all\text{-}source\text{-}moments}\), every source has moments of every order; by \(\mathrm{ass:nondegenerate\text{-}sources}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:nongaussian\text{-}sources}\), it is nondegenerate and non-Gaussian. Hence \(\mathrm{lem:marcinkiewicz\text{-}cumulant\text{-}tail}\) says that each source has infinitely many nonzero cumulants of order at least three. Their orders are unbounded, so for every \(j\) there is a finite \(\nu_j\ge2n-1\) with \(\lambda_{j,\nu_j}\ne0\). Therefore
\[
 K_{C,\varepsilon}:=\max_{1\le j\le n}\nu_j<\infty. \tag{25}
\]
Orderwise recovery through (25) includes every \([c_j]\). Pairwise nonproportionality prevents false projective deduplication, and the orderwise result returns no extraneous direction. The union therefore reaches cardinality \(n\) and the handle stops, returning
\[
 \{[c_1],\ldots,[c_n]\}=\mathcal D(P_X), \tag{26}
\]
where the equality is the full-law conclusion of \(\mathrm{thm:sharp\text{-}effect\text{-}frontier}\). No cross-order labels are needed. Choosing arbitrary nonzero representatives produces \(C'=C\Pi D\); \(\mathrm{lem:frontier\text{-}monomial\text{-}invariance}\) then gives \(\mathcal V_x(C')=\mathcal V_x(C)\) and \(\mathcal R_{xy}(C')=\mathcal R_{xy}(C)\). The sharp-frontier theorem proves, rather than defines, that these evaluated sets equal the causal law-fiber response targets.

We next prove that no cumulant cutoff is uniform even on the stricter moment-determinate subclass. Fix an integer \(K\ge3\) and \(\rho>0\). By \(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}moment\text{-}near\text{-}gaussian\text{-}witness}\), there is a centered, variance-one, non-Gaussian, moment-determinate law \(F\) with
\[
 d_{\mathrm{TV}}(F,G)<\rho,
 \qquad G=\mathcal N(0,1), \tag{27}
\]
whose raw moments, and hence cumulants, through order \(K\) equal those of \(G\). A moment-determinate law here has its full moment sequence, so it belongs to the all-finite-moments class. Moreover, \(\mathrm{lem:marcinkiewicz\text{-}cumulant\text{-}tail}\) ensures that this nondegenerate non-Gaussian \(F\) has some nonzero cumulant above \(K\): arbitrary finite matching delays, rather than eliminates, an informative order.

Give two sources independent law \(F\), fix \(0<\gamma<\pi/4\), and define
\[
 C_0=I_2,
 \qquad
 C_1=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \cos\gamma&-\sin\gamma\\
 \sin\gamma&\cos\gamma
 \end{pmatrix}. \tag{28}
\]
Both matrices have unit columns, full row rank, and nonzero pairwise nonproportional columns. Source independence makes all joint source moments of total degree at most \(K\) equal to those under \(G^{\otimes2}\). The density of \(G^{\otimes2}\) is a function only of the squared Euclidean norm, so the orthogonal matrices in (28) leave it invariant. Consequently the two observed laws have identical moments and cumulants through order \(K\), although their projective direction sets differ. Since \(K\) was arbitrary, no fixed finite cumulant cutoff identifies the directions throughout either the stricter moment-determinate subclass or the containing all-finite-moments class.

For the statistical boundary, for each \(N\) choose the witness in (27) with
\[
 d_{\mathrm{TV}}(F_N,G)\le\frac{1}{4N^2}, \tag{29}
\]
and let \(P_{k,N}\) be the observed law generated by \(C_k\) and two independent \(F_N\) sources. Total variation contracts under measurable pushforward, because the preimage of each measurable event is measurable. A telescoping expansion of product signed measures and the triangle inequality give
\[
 d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\mu^{\otimes m},\nu^{\otimes m})
 \le m\,d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\mu,\nu). \tag{30}
\]
Each \(P_{k,N}\) is within \(2d_{\mathrm{TV}}(F_N,G)\) of the common Gaussian law, since that law is invariant under both matrices in (28). The triangle inequality, (29), and (30) imply
\[
 d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P_{0,N},P_{1,N})\le N^{-2},
 \qquad
 d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P_{0,N}^{\otimes N},P_{1,N}^{\otimes N})\le N^{-1}\longrightarrow0. \tag{31}
\]

Equip real projective space with any metric inducing its usual compact topology and finite direction sets with the associated Hausdorff metric. The two direction sets in (28) are distinct fixed finite sets and hence have positive Hausdorff separation. A uniformly consistent direction-set estimator would produce a test by selecting the nearer of these two targets, with both errors tending to zero. For every test event \(E\), however, the definition of total variation gives
\[
 P_{0,N}^{\otimes N}(E^c)+P_{1,N}^{\otimes N}(E)
 \ge1-d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P_{0,N}^{\otimes N},P_{1,N}^{\otimes N})\longrightarrow1, \tag{32}
\]
contradicting such a test.

The causal frontiers are also fixed and separated. Applying \(\mathrm{def:admissible\text{-}source\text{-}set}\) at \(x=1\), \(y=2\) gives
\[
 \mathcal R_{12}(C_0)=\{0\},
 \qquad
 \mathcal R_{12}(C_1)=\{\tan\gamma,-\cot\gamma\}. \tag{33}
\]
Indeed, only the first column of \(C_0\) has nonzero treatment loading and full complementary deletion rank; both columns of \(C_1\) do, with the displayed ratios. The nearest-target argument using (31)--(32) therefore rules out a uniformly consistent frontier estimator.

More strongly, let \(\mathcal U_N\) be a random compact set with uniform coverage at least \(1-\beta\) for every frontier in the class, where \(\beta<1/2\). Under \(P_{0,N}^{\otimes N}\), coverage of \(\mathcal R_{12}(C_0)\) has probability at least \(1-\beta\). Coverage of \(\mathcal R_{12}(C_1)\) has probability at least \(1-\beta\) under \(P_{1,N}^{\otimes N}\), and (31) transfers this event to \(P_{0,N}^{\otimes N}\) with probability at least \(1-\beta-o(1)\). The intersection therefore has probability at least \(1-2\beta-o(1)>0\). On it, \(0\in\mathcal R_{12}(C_0)\) and \(-\cot\gamma\in\mathcal U_N\), so
\[
 d_H(\mathcal U_N,\mathcal R_{12}(C_0))\ge\cot\gamma. \tag{34}
\]
Thus no uniformly honest frontier-containing confidence union with coverage strictly above one half has uniformly vanishing Hausdorff error. The witnesses used here are moment-determinate; the conclusion therefore holds a fortiori on the all-finite-moments class.

Finally, two explicit families prove discontinuity of the deterministic frontier map. Columnwise normalization may be imposed without changing a frontier by \(\mathrm{lem:frontier\text{-}monomial\text{-}invariance}\). Let
\[
 D_t=\begin{pmatrix}t&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
For sufficiently small \(|t|\) with \(t\ne1\), these matrices have full row rank and nonzero nonproportional columns. Direct deletion-rank calculation gives
\[
 \mathcal R_{12}(D_t)=\{1/t,1\}\quad(t\ne0),
 \qquad
 \mathcal R_{12}(D_0)=\{1\}. \tag{35}
\]
This is discontinuity at a vanishing treatment loading. Next let
\[
 E_t=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 1&1&0&1\\
 1&t&0&0\\
 0&0&1&1
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
For sufficiently small \(|t|\), the matrices have full row rank and nonzero nonproportional columns. Deleting row one and each candidate column gives
\[
 \mathcal R_{12}(E_0)=\{0\},
 \qquad
 \mathcal R_{12}(E_t)=\{0,t,1\}\quad(t\ne0). \tag{36}
\]
At \(t=0\), exactly columns two and four are admissible and both ratios are zero; at \(t\ne0\), exactly columns one, two, and four are admissible and their ratios are \(1,t,0\). This is discontinuity at a deletion-rank boundary.

Equations (31), (35), and (36) show that qualitative all-finite-moments assumptions alone provide neither a uniform stopping order nor a uniform statistical modulus. Quantitative non-Gaussianity, moment, Veronese-conditioning, angular, probe/eigen-gap, denominator, and deletion-rank margins are therefore indispensable for a uniform root-\(N\) claim of the stated kind. On the separated common-order iid class, which keeps its fixed-order source moment envelope, \(\mathrm{thm:uniform\text{-}confidence\text{-}frontier}\) supplies simultaneous vector and scalar coverage with \(O(N^{-1/2})\) Hausdorff error. This completes the pointwise population-oracle construction and the nonuniformity boundary under the corrected, weaker positive premise.
