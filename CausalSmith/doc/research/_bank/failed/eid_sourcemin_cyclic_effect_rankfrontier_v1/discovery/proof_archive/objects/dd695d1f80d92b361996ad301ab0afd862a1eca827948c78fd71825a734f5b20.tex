Full row rank permits extending the \(p\) observed rows of \(C\) to a basis \(T_0\in\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb R)\); write \(W_0=T_0^{-1}\). Fix \(j\in J_x(C)\). We first justify the possible shear in the certificate. Since the rows of \(T_0\) form a basis and \(W_0T_0=I_n\),
\[
e_j^{\mathsf T}=\sum_{r=1}^n(W_0)_{jr}(T_0)_{r,:}. \tag{14}
\]
The \(p-1\) rows \(C_{-x,:}\) are linearly independent. Their restrictions away from coordinate \(j\) are dependent exactly when a nonzero linear combination is supported only at \(j\), which, by (14), is equivalent to \(e_j^{\mathsf T}\) belonging to their row span. The uniqueness of coordinates in the \(T_0\)-basis therefore gives
\[
\operatorname{rank}(C_{-x,-j})<p-1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
(W_0)_{jx}=0\ \text{ and }\ (W_0)_{j\ell}=0\text{ for every }\ell\in\mathsf L. \tag{15}
\]
Because \(j\in J_x(C)\), the left side of (15) is false. If \((W_0)_{jx}\ne0\), set \(T_j=T_0\) and \(W_j=W_0\). Otherwise (15) provides \(\ell\in\mathsf L\) with \((W_0)_{j\ell}\ne0\). Since \(x\ne\ell\),
\[
(I_n+e_\ell e_x^{\mathsf T})^{-1}=I_n-e_\ell e_x^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Set
\[
T_j=(I_n+e_\ell e_x^{\mathsf T})T_0,
\qquad
W_j=W_0(I_n-e_\ell e_x^{\mathsf T}). \tag{16}
\]
Only latent row \(\ell\) of \(T_0\) changes, so \((T_j)_{\mathsf O,:}=C\), and
\[
(W_j)_{jx}=-(W_0)_{j\ell}\ne0. \tag{17}
\]
Thus in both cases \(T_j=W_j^{-1}\), the observed rows of \(T_j\) equal \(C\), and \((W_j)_{jx}\ne0\).

Since \(j\in J_x(C)\), also \(C_{xj}\ne0\). The inverse-cofactor identity, now applied to \(W_j\), gives
\[
0\ne \det(W_j)(T_j)_{xj}
=(-1)^{j+x}\det((W_j)_{-j,-x}), \tag{18}
\]
because \((T_j)_{xj}=C_{xj}\). Hence the determinant expansion of \((W_j)_{-j,-x}\) has a nonzero monomial. Its selected nonzero entries give a perfect matching from rows other than \(j\) to columns other than \(x\). Adjoin \(j\mapsto x\), and choose \(\Pi_j\) so that the row assigned to equation \(i\) becomes row \(i\) of \(\Pi_jW_j\). Every diagonal entry of \(\Pi_jW_j\) is then nonzero. Therefore
\[
D_j=\operatorname{diag}(\Pi_jW_j)
\]
is invertible. Define \(Q_j=D_j^{-1}\Pi_jW_j\) and \(H_j=D_j^{-1}\Pi_j\). The matrix \(Q_j\) is invertible and has unit diagonal, while \(H_j\) is monomial. Direct multiplication yields the exact completion identity
\[
Q_j^{-1}H_j
=W_j^{-1}\Pi_j^{-1}D_jD_j^{-1}\Pi_j
=T_j,
\qquad
[Q_j^{-1}H_j]_{\mathsf O,:}=C. \tag{19}
\]
The augmented matching puts original row \(j\) at equation row \(x\), so
\[
(H_j)_{xj}=D_{j,xx}^{-1}\ne0,
\qquad s_{\mathcal M_j}(x)=j. \tag{20}
\]
Moreover \(\Pi_j^{-1}e_x=e_j\), whence
\[
(Q_j^{-1})_{xx}
=e_x^{\mathsf T}T_j\Pi_j^{-1}D_je_x
=C_{xj}D_{j,xx}\ne0. \tag{21}
\]
Since \(Q_j\) is invertible, its cofactor identity and (21) imply
\[
\det((Q_j)_{-x,-x})=\det(Q_j)(Q_j^{-1})_{xx}\ne0. \tag{22}
\]
Thus the observational and postintervention systems are both solvable.

The source vector is exactly \(\varepsilon\), so the frozen independence, nondegeneracy, non-Gaussianity, and moment-determinacy assumptions apply. Equation (19), together with the nonzero and nonproportional column assumptions on \(C\), proves that \((A^{\mathcal M_j},\varepsilon)=(C,\varepsilon)\) is source-minimal and that
\[
\mathcal L(A^{\mathcal M_j}\varepsilon)=\mathcal L(C\varepsilon)=P_X. \tag{23}
\]
Equations (19), (20), (22), and (23) verify every defining condition of \(\mathfrak F_x(P_X)\). Finally, from \(Q_j^{-1}=T_j\Pi_j^{-1}D_j\), for each observed \(i\),
\[
(Q_j^{-1})_{ix}=C_{ij}D_{j,xx}.
\]
Division by the nonzero \(x\)-entry in (21) proves both displayed response formulas.

If \(C\) is rational, Gaussian elimination can extend its rows using standard coordinate rows, so \(T_0\) is rational. Matrix inversion, the shear (16), permutations, extraction of the nonzero rational diagonal, and its inversion preserve rationality. Hence \(Q_j\) and \(H_j\) are rational.
