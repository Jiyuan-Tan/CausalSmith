By the convention \(\Pi e_k=e_{\pi(k)}\), the \(k\)-th column of \(\widetilde C\) is
\[
 \widetilde C_{:,k}=d_k C_{:,\pi(k)}. \tag{1}
\]
Since \(d_k\ne0\), equation (1) gives
\[
 \widetilde C_{xk}\ne0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 C_{x,\pi(k)}\ne0. \tag{2}
\]
After deleting column \(k\), the remaining columns of \(\widetilde C_{-x,-k}\) are obtained from those of \(C_{-x,-\pi(k)}\) by a column permutation and multiplication by a nonsingular diagonal matrix. Both operations preserve row rank, and hence
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(\widetilde C_{-x,-k})
 =\operatorname{rank}(C_{-x,-\pi(k)}). \tag{3}
\]
Equations (2)--(3) and the definition of \(J_x\) prove the membership equivalence. Dividing the \((-x)\)-coordinates of (1) by its \(x\)-coordinate cancels \(d_k\) and proves the displayed response-ratio identity. Because \(\pi\) is bijective, taking the images of the two admissible-index sets gives equality of the vector frontiers. Taking coordinate \(y\) gives equality of the scalar frontiers by their definitions.
