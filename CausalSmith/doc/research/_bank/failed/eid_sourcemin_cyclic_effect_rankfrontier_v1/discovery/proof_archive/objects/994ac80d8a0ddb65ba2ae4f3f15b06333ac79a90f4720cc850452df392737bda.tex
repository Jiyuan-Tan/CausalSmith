Assume \(n=p\).  By \(\operatorname{rank}(C)=p\) from
\textnormal{ass:full-row-rank}, the square matrix \(C\) is nonsingular.  Since
\(\mathsf O=\{1,\ldots,p\}=\{1,\ldots,n\}\), the basis extension in
\textnormal{def:witness-algorithm} is forced to be
\[
 T_0=C,\qquad W_0=T_0^{-1}=C^{-1},
\]
and \(\mathsf L=\varnothing\).  Thus there is no latent row on which a shear
could be performed.

We first relate the square candidates to the observational-law fiber.  By
\textnormal{lem:lacerda-square-row-permutation}, a square candidate obtained
from the unmixing matrix \(W_0\) has the form
\[
 Q=D^{-1}\Pi W_0,
 \qquad
 D=\operatorname{diag}(\Pi W_0),
\]
where \(\Pi\) is a row-permutation matrix and every diagonal entry of
\(\Pi W_0\) is nonzero.  Equip this candidate with
\[
 H=D^{-1}\Pi.
\]
Then \(H\) is monomial, \(Q_{ii}=1\) for every \(i\), and both \(Q\) and
\(H\) are nonsingular.  Direct multiplication gives
\[
 Q^{-1}H
 =\bigl(D^{-1}\Pi W_0\bigr)^{-1}D^{-1}\Pi
 =W_0^{-1}\Pi^\top D D^{-1}\Pi
 =C.
\]
Consequently, if the candidate also satisfies
\(\det(Q_{-x,-x})\ne0\), then \(\mathcal M=(Q,H)\) satisfies every defining
condition of \(\mathfrak F_x(C,\varepsilon)\) in
\textnormal{def:completion-fiber}.  The assumptions
\textnormal{ass:nonzero-columns}, \textnormal{ass:distinct-directions},
\textnormal{ass:independent-sources},
\textnormal{ass:nondegenerate-sources}, and
\textnormal{ass:nongaussian-sources} then also show that the canonical
embedding
\[
 \widetilde{\mathcal M}=(p,Q,H,\varepsilon)
\]
belongs to \(\mathfrak F_x^{\mathrm{law}}(P_X)\).  Indeed, its observed
mixing matrix is exactly \(C\), so its observed law is \(P_X\), and all the
remaining membership conditions are the conditions just verified or the five
displayed source and mixing assumptions.

The postintervention minor also guarantees that the response denominator is
nonzero.  The cofactor identity applied to \(Q^{-1}\) gives
\[
 (Q^{-1})_{xx}=\frac{\det(Q_{-x,-x})}{\det(Q)}\ne0.
\]
Hence, by \textnormal{def:equilibrium-effect} and
\textnormal{lem:law-hard-intervention-response},
\[
 \theta_{y\leftarrow x}(\mathcal M)
 =\frac{(Q^{-1})_{yx}}{(Q^{-1})_{xx}}
 =[\rho_x(\widetilde{\mathcal M})]_y.
\]
The forward inclusion in \textnormal{thm:sharp-effect-frontier} therefore
implies
\[
 \theta_{y\leftarrow x}(\mathcal M)\in\mathcal R_{xy}(C).
\]
This proves that the effect projection of the stated postintervention-solvable
square candidate set is contained in \(\mathcal R_{xy}(C)\).

For the reverse inclusion, let \(r\in\mathcal R_{xy}(C)\).  By
\textnormal{def:effect-frontier} and
\textnormal{def:admissible-source-set}, there is an index \(j\) such that
\[
 r=\frac{C_{yj}}{C_{xj}},\qquad
 C_{xj}\ne0,\qquad
 \operatorname{rank}(C_{-x,-j})=p-1.
\]
Because \(C_{-x,-j}\) is square, its rank condition is equivalent to
\(\det(C_{-x,-j})\ne0\).  The cofactor formula for \(W_0=C^{-1}\) yields
\[
 (W_0)_{jx}
 =\frac{(-1)^{x+j}\det(C_{-x,-j})}{\det(C)}\ne0.
\]
Thus the first branch of \textnormal{def:witness-algorithm} retains
\(T_j=T_0=C\); no shear is used.  A second application of the cofactor formula,
now to \(W_0^{-1}=C\), gives
\[
 C_{xj}
 =\frac{(-1)^{x+j}\det((W_0)_{-j,-x})}{\det(W_0)},
\]
and hence
\[
 \det((W_0)_{-j,-x})
 =(-1)^{x+j}\frac{C_{xj}}{\det(C)}\ne0.
\]
In the determinant expansion of \((W_0)_{-j,-x}\), at least one permutation
term is therefore nonzero.  The entries in that term form a perfect matching
between the rows other than \(j\) and the columns other than \(x\).  Augmenting
it by matching row \(j\) to column \(x\) produces a row permutation
\(\Pi_j\) for which every diagonal entry of \(\Pi_jW_0\) is nonzero.  With
\[
 D_j=\operatorname{diag}(\Pi_jW_0),\qquad
 Q_j=D_j^{-1}\Pi_jW_0,\qquad
 H_j=D_j^{-1}\Pi_j,
\]
this is precisely an admissible square row-permutation candidate of
\textnormal{lem:lacerda-square-row-permutation}.  Moreover, deleting structural
row \(x\) and variable column \(x\) leaves, up to a row permutation and
nonsingular diagonal row scaling, the matrix \((W_0)_{-j,-x}\).  Therefore
\[
 \det((Q_j)_{-x,-x})\ne0.
\]
Equivalently, this membership and postintervention-solvability conclusion is
also the square specialization of
\textnormal{lem:shear-matching-sufficiency}.

The exact-\(C\) law witness in
\textnormal{lem:canonical-exact-c-law-witness} is
\[
 \widetilde{\mathcal M}_j=(p,Q_j,H_j,\varepsilon)
 \in\mathfrak F_x^{\mathrm{law}}(P_X),
\]
and its entire observed response vector is
\[
 \rho_x(\widetilde{\mathcal M}_j)
 =\left(\frac{C_{ij}}{C_{xj}}\right)_{i\in\mathsf O\setminus\{x\}}.
\]
By \textnormal{def:equilibrium-effect} and
\textnormal{lem:law-hard-intervention-response}, the effect of its underlying
square candidate is its \(y\)-coordinate, namely
\[
 \theta_{y\leftarrow x}(\mathcal M_j)
 =[\rho_x(\widetilde{\mathcal M}_j)]_y
 =\frac{C_{yj}}{C_{xj}}=r.
\]
Thus every element of \(\mathcal R_{xy}(C)\) is attained by a square
row-permutation candidate with nonsingular intervention minor, proving the
reverse inclusion and hence the asserted equality.

Finally, \textnormal{thm:sharp-effect-frontier} states the whole-vector law-fiber
identity
\[
 \{\rho_x(\widetilde{\mathcal M}):
   \widetilde{\mathcal M}\in\mathfrak F_x^{\mathrm{law}}(P_X)\}
 =\mathcal V_x(C)
\]
and its coordinate projection
\[
 \operatorname{pr}_y\mathcal V_x(C)=\mathcal R_{xy}(C).
\]
The preceding exact-\(C\) witnesses show that the postintervention-solvable
square candidates attain
that same coordinate projection.  The distribution-entailment statement in
\textnormal{lem:lacerda-distribution-entailment-equivalence} justifies the
observational equivalence-class description, but it is not used to infer
intervention solvability: candidates with \(\det(Q_{-x,-x})=0\) are explicitly
discarded.  No spectral-radius or dynamical-stability condition entered any
step.
