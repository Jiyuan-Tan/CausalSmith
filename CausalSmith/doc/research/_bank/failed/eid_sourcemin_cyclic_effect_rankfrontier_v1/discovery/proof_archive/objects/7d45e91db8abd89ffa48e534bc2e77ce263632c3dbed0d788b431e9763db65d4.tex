Fix \(\zeta=(P_X,C,\varepsilon,\lambda)\in\mathfrak P_{p,n,r}(a,\delta,\sigma,\kappa,\Lambda,M)\). Unit normalization gives
\[
 \lVert X\rVert_2\le\sum_{j=1}^n|\varepsilon_j|.
\]
For nonnegative \(t_j\), convexity gives \((\sum_jt_j)^{2r}\le n^{2r-1}\sum_jt_j^{2r}\). The source-moment envelope therefore implies
\[
 \mathbb E\lVert X\rVert_2^{2r}
 \le n^{2r-1}\sum_{j=1}^n\mathbb E|\varepsilon_j|^{2r}
 \le n^{2r}M=H. \tag{39}
\]
Let \(\mu\in\mathbb R^m\) collect all nonconstant raw moments through degree \(r\), and let \(\widetilde\mu\) collect their untrimmed empirical averages. If a coordinate has degree at most \(r\), its squared monomial is at most \(1+\lVert X\rVert_2^{2r}\). Independence of the \(N\) observations and (39) give
\[
 \mathbb E\lVert\widetilde\mu-\mu\rVert_2^2
 \le\frac{m(1+H)}{N}. \tag{40}
\]
Every true moment coordinate has absolute value at most \((1+H)^{1/2}\le1+H=B_m\). Coordinatewise clipping to \([-B_m,B_m]\) is projection onto an interval containing that true coordinate and cannot increase Euclidean error. Hence, by \(P(Z>t)\le\mathbb E Z/t\) for the nonnegative variable \(Z=\lVert\widehat\mu-\mu\rVert_2^2\),
\[
 E_N=\{\lVert\widehat\mu-\mu\rVert_2\le e_N\},
 \qquad
 \inf_{\zeta\in\mathfrak P_{p,n,r}}P_X^{\otimes N}(E_N)\ge1-\alpha. \tag{41}
\]

For an ordered tensor coordinate, the moment--cumulant identity is
\[
 \operatorname{cum}(X_{i_1},\ldots,X_{i_r})
 =\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak P_r}(-1)^{|\pi|-1}(|\pi|-1)!
 \prod_{A\in\pi}\mathbb E\!\left[\prod_{k\in A}X_{i_k}\right]. \tag{42}
\]
On \([-B_m,B_m]^m\), telescoping a product of \(|\pi|\) factors bounds its change by \(|\pi|B_m^{|\pi|-1}\lVert\widehat\mu-\mu\rVert_2\). Summing (42) and then the squares of its \(p^r\) ordered coordinates gives, on \(E_N\),
\[
 \lVert\widehat{\mathcal T}_r-\mathcal T_r\rVert_F
 \le p^{r/2}\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak P_r}(|\pi|-1)!|\pi|B_m^{|\pi|-1}e_N
 =\tau_N. \tag{43}
\]

The true factor pair is feasible for the minimum-distance problem. The numerical feasible set is compact: normalized columns range over a product of unit spheres, cumulants range over the compact set \(\{t:\kappa\le|t|\le\Lambda\}\), and the loading, singular-value, and pencil-gap alternatives are closed. Its nonemptiness is assumed. A continuous objective therefore has a minimizer; successive minimization of its finitely many coordinates over the compact argmin set defines the stated lexicographic selection. Optimality and (43) imply
\[
 \left\lVert\sum_{j=1}^n\widehat\lambda_j\widehat c_j^{\otimes r}-\mathcal T_r\right\rVert_F
 \le2\tau_N. \tag{44}
\]
The numerical pencil-inverse lemma applies directly to the fitted and true factor pairs. If \(2\tau_N<\epsilon_0\), it supplies one common permutation for which
\[
 \lVert\widehat C-C\rVert_F\le2L_C\tau_N=b_N. \tag{45}
\]

Assume also \(b_N<\min\{a,\delta\}/2\). Both feasible matrices obey the alternative that each loading is either zero or has magnitude at least \(a\). A zero entry in one matrix paired with a nonzero entry in the other would make the Frobenius distance at least \(a\), contradicting (45); hence their zero patterns agree. For a deletion matrix \(G\) with \(p-1\) rows, its least singular value satisfies
\[
 s_{\min}(G)=\inf_{\lVert z\rVert_2=1}\lVert G^\top z\rVert_2.
\]
The triangle inequality therefore gives \(|s_{\min}(G')-s_{\min}(G)|\le\lVert G'-G\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\). Applied to every deletion pair and then bounded by (45), this difference is less than \(\delta/2\). Since each feasible deletion singular value is either zero or at least \(\delta\), their zero statuses agree. Consequently
\[
 \widehat J_x=J_x(C) \tag{46}
\]
simultaneously for every observed \(x\), after the same common source permutation.

For \(j\in J_x(C)=\widehat J_x\), both denominators have magnitude at least \(a\), unit normalization gives \(|C_{ij}|\le1\), and (45) bounds every coordinate error by \(b_N\). Thus, for every \(i\ne x\),
\[
 \left|\frac{\widehat C_{ij}}{\widehat C_{xj}}-
 \frac{C_{ij}}{C_{xj}}\right|
 \le\frac{|\widehat C_{ij}-C_{ij}|}{|\widehat C_{xj}|}
 +\frac{|C_{ij}|\,|\widehat C_{xj}-C_{xj}|}
 {|\widehat C_{xj}|\,|C_{xj}|}
 \le(a^{-1}+a^{-2})b_N=w_N. \tag{47}
\]
Taking the Euclidean norm of the \(p-1\) inequalities in (47) gives
\[
 \left\lVert\frac{\widehat C_{-x,j}}{\widehat C_{xj}}-
 \frac{C_{-x,j}}{C_{xj}}\right\rVert_2
 \le\sqrt{p-1}\,w_N=w_N^{\mathrm{vec}}. \tag{48}
\]
Therefore every true response vector lies in its corresponding confidence ball. Conversely, every point of that ball is at distance at most \(w_N^{\mathrm{vec}}\) from its estimated center and, by (48), at most another \(w_N^{\mathrm{vec}}\) from the corresponding true vector. Hence
\[
 \mathcal R_x^{\mathrm{vec}}(C)\subseteq\mathcal U_x^{\mathrm{vec}},
 \qquad
 d_H(\mathcal U_x^{\mathrm{vec}},\mathcal R_x^{\mathrm{vec}}(C))
 \le2w_N^{\mathrm{vec}}. \tag{49}
\]
The same argument in coordinate \(y\) gives the scalar inclusion and the bound \(2w_N\). Because every ball in (49) is indexed by one source and contains its entire estimated response vector, no false cross-outcome Cartesian combination is introduced.

If either informative-regime inequality fails, the fallback vector ball has radius \(\sqrt{p-1}/a\). Every admissible true vector belongs to it because all \(p-1\) numerators have magnitude at most one and the denominator has magnitude at least \(a\). The scalar fallback interval is covered by the same coordinate bound. Thus coverage holds in both regimes on the single event \(E_N\), simultaneously for all ordered queries, and (41) proves the displayed uniform probability inequality. The sharp-effect-frontier theorem equates these observable frontiers with the full observational-law-fiber causal targets. Finally, with dimensions, margins, \(M\), and \(\alpha\) fixed, \(L_m,L_C,H\) are constants, so \(\tau_N=O(N^{-1/2})\), \(2w_N=O(N^{-1/2})\), and \(2w_N^{\mathrm{vec}}=O(N^{-1/2})\).
