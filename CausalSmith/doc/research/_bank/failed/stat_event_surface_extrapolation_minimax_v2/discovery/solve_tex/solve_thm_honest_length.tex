%%% FIELD pareto-def
For a coefficient array \(a=(a_{g,t}:(g,t)\in O)\), define
\[
 A_t(a)=\sum_{g\in G_t}a_{g,t},\qquad
 v_t(a)_j=a_{j,t}\mathbf 1\{j\in G_t\},
\]
\[
 H(a)=\sum_{t=1}^{T-m}|A_t(a)|
       \left(\frac{T-t}{T}\right)^\beta,
 \qquad
 D(a)=\sum_{t=1}^{T-m}\inf_{c\in\mathbb R}
       \|v_t(a)-cq\|_1,
\]
and
\[
 w_j(a)=\sum_{(g,t)\in O}a_{g,t}b_{(g,t),j},
\]
\[
 E(a)=\sum_{t=1}^{T-m}\left{
       \sum_{g\in G_t}\frac{a_{g,t}^2}{q_g}
       +\frac{A_t(a)^2}{q_{>t}}
       \right}
       +\sum_{j=1}^T\frac{w_j(a)^2}{q_j}.
\]
The baseline-cancellation Pareto frontier is the finite-dimensional convex program
\[
 \Gamma_{T,m}(h,d)=\inf\left\{E(a):a\in\mathbb R^{|O|},\ 
 \sum_{t=1}^{T-m}A_t(a)=1,\ H(a)\le h,\ D(a)\le d\right\},
\]
where the infimum of the empty set is \(+\infty\).
%%% FIELD risk-def
\[
 R^{\star}=\inf_{\widetilde\theta}\sup_{x\in\mathcal M_{T,m}}
 \mathbb E_x[(\widetilde\theta(Z)-\ell(x))^2],
\]
where the infimum is over Borel-measurable real-valued statistics.  Also,
\[
 \operatorname{EL}^{\star}_{\alpha}
 =\inf_C\sup_{x\in\mathcal M_{T,m}}
 \mathbb E_x[\operatorname{length}C(Z)],
\]
where the infimum is over Borel-measurable maps into the set of finite closed intervals such that \(C(Z)\) is interval-valued almost surely under every \(x\in\mathcal M_{T,m}\) and
\[
 \inf_{x\in\mathcal M_{T,m}}
 \Pr_x\{\ell(x)\in C(Z)\}\ge1-\alpha.
\]

The candidate diagnostic calendar functional and one of its minimizing depths are
\[
 \rho_{N,T,m}=\min_{m\le k\le\lfloor\delta T\rfloor}
 \left\{L(k/T)^{\beta}+Bk/(T-k)+\sigma\sqrt{T/N}/k\right\},
\]
\[
 k^{\star}\in\operatorname*{argmin}_{m\le k\le\lfloor\delta T\rfloor}
 \left\{L(k/T)^{\beta}+Bk/(T-k)+\sigma\sqrt{T/N}/k\right\}.
\]
No equivalence between this diagnostic and the exact nested-covariance modulus is part of the definition.
%%% FIELD pareto-statement
Write reverse date \(r=T-t\), \(R_r=q_{>T-r}\), and \(Q_r=1-R_r\).  For every coefficient array \(a\),
\[
 a^{\prime}\Sigma_{\mathrm{nest}}a=\frac{\sigma^2}{N}E(a),
\]
and, if \(\sum_tA_t(a)=1\), the affine statistic \(a^{\prime}Z\) has worst-case absolute bias for \(f_T\) at most
\[
 L H(a)+B D(a).
\]
For \(m\le s\le T-1\), define the terminal-tail contrast by
\[
 \phi^{(s)}_j=
 \begin{cases}
 1,&j>T-s,\\
 -R_s/Q_s,&j\le T-s.
 \end{cases}
\]
Then, for all \(h,d\ge0\),
\[
 \Gamma_{T,m}(h,d)\ge
 \sup_{m\le s\le T-1}
 \frac{Q_s}{R_s}
 \left[
 1-(1+\|\phi^{(s)}\|_\infty)h(T/s)^\beta
   -\|\phi^{(s)}\|_\infty d
 \right]_+^2,
 \tag{P1}
\]
and
\[
 \Gamma_{T,m}(h,d)\ge
 \sup_{m\le s\le T-1}
 \frac{[1-h(T/s)^\beta]_+^2}{\sum_{r=m}^sR_r}.
 \tag{P2}
\]
There are constants \(0<c_0<C_0<\infty\), \(C_H<\infty\), and \(C_D<\infty\), depending only on \(\beta,\delta,c_q,C_q\), such that, uniformly for \(m\le k\le\lfloor\delta T\rfloor\), with
\[
 d_k=\min\{1,R_k/Q_k\},
\]
the pooled and baseline-cancelled endpoints obey
\[
 \Gamma_{T,m}((k/T)^\beta,d_k)
 \le2(Q_k^{-1}+R_k^{-1})\le C_0T/k,
 \tag{P3}
\]
and
\[
 c_0T/k^2\le
 \Gamma_{T,m}(C_H(k/T)^\beta,C_D)
 \le C_0T/k^2.
 \tag{P4}
\]
For every \(\lambda\in[0,1]\), explicit interpolation of the two coefficient arrays gives
\[
 \Gamma_{T,m}\!\left(
 [(1-\lambda)+\lambda C_H](k/T)^\beta,
 (1-\lambda)d_k+\lambda C_D
 \right)
 \le C_0\left\{\frac{T}{k^2}+(1-\lambda)^2\frac{T}{k}\right\}.
 \tag{P5}
\]
The proposed unit-normalized interpolation is nevertheless false at the support boundary.  Precisely, put \(d_m^{\min}=\min\{1,R_m/Q_m\}\).  Then
\[
 \Gamma_{T,m}((m/T)^\beta,d)=+\infty\quad(d<d_m^{\min}),
 \tag{P6}
\]
whereas
\[
 \frac1{R_m}\le
 \Gamma_{T,m}((m/T)^\beta,d)
 \le2(Q_m^{-1}+R_m^{-1})
 \quad(d\ge d_m^{\min}).
 \tag{P7}
\]
Thus \(\Gamma_{T,m}((m/T)^\beta,1)\asymp T/m\).  In particular, for equal shares and \(m=k=o(T)\), \(d_m^{\min}=m/(T-m)>m/T\): the claimed orders \(\Gamma((k/T)^\beta,k/T)\asymp T/k\) and \(\Gamma((k/T)^\beta,1)\asymp T/k^2\) are respectively replaced by \(+\infty\) and \(T/k\).  Hence no interpolation depending only on the unit-normalized pair \(((k/T)^\beta,d)\) can hold uniformly over \(m\le k\); the exact convex program and the bounds \((\mathrm{P1})\)--\((\mathrm{P7})\) are the corrected uniform characterization.

%%% FIELD pareto-proof
We prove the claims in seven steps.

First, expand the covariance in \(\text{def:nested-covariance}\).  The date-block formula in \(\text{def:contemporaneous-covariance}\) and the definition of \(w(a)\) give, term by term,
\[
 a^{\prime}\Sigma_{\mathrm{nest}}a
 =\frac{\sigma^2}{N}\left[
 \sum_t\left\{\sum_{g\in G_t}\frac{a_{g,t}^2}{q_g}
 +\frac{A_t(a)^2}{q_{>t}}\right\}
 +\sum_j\frac{w_j(a)^2}{q_j}\right]
 =\frac{\sigma^2}{N}E(a).                                      \tag{1}
\]
By \(\text{lem:score-covariance}\), \(\mathbb E_x Z=Kx\).  If \(\sum_tA_t(a)=1\), then \(\text{def:observation-operator}\) yields
\[
 \mathbb E_x[a^{\prime}Z]-f_T
 =\sum_tA_t(a)(f_t-f_T)+\sum_t v_t(a)^{\prime}u_{\cdot,t}.       \tag{2}
\]
The first sum is bounded in absolute value by \(LH(a)\) using \(\text{ass:holder-calendar}\).  For every \(c\in\mathbb R\), \(\text{ass:calendar-centering}\) and \(\text{ass:bounded-cohort-deviation}\) give
\[
 |v_t(a)^{\prime}u_{\cdot,t}|
 =|(v_t(a)-cq)^{\prime}u_{\cdot,t}|
 \le B\|v_t(a)-cq\|_1.                                         \tag{3}
\]
Taking the infimum over \(c\), summing (3), and using (2) proves the bias bound.  Every division in (1)--(3) is valid because \(q_g>0\) and \(q_{>t}>0\) on \(O\), by \(\text{ass:balanced-cohorts}\) and \(t\le T-m<T\).

Second, fix \(s\in\{m,\ldots,T-1\}\).  Positivity of the cohort shares gives \(0<R_s<1\), hence \(Q_s>0\), and the displayed tail contrast is well defined.  Direct calculation gives
\[
 q^{\prime}\phi^{(s)}=R_s-Q_sR_s/Q_s=0,
 \qquad
 \sum_{j=1}^Tq_j(\phi^{(s)}_j)^2=R_s/Q_s.                        \tag{4}
\]
For each date choose a minimizer \(c_t\) of \(c\mapsto\|v_t(a)-cq\|_1\), which exists because this continuous function is coercive, and write \(v_t(a)=c_tq+\varepsilon_t\).  If \(\bar\phi_t=R_{T-t}^{-1}\sum_{j\in C_t}q_j\phi^{(s)}_j\), the baseline-loading definition gives
\[
 (\phi^{(s)})^{\prime}w(a)
 =\sum_t\{\bar\phi_tA_t(a)-(\phi^{(s)})^{\prime}v_t(a)\}
 =\sum_t\bar\phi_tA_t(a)-\sum_t(\phi^{(s)})^{\prime}\varepsilon_t. \tag{5}
\]
For reverse date \(r=T-t\le s\), the control pool is contained in the terminal \(s\) cohorts, so \(\bar\phi_t=1\).  For \(r>s\), \(|1-\bar\phi_t|\le1+\|\phi^{(s)}\|_\infty\).  Therefore \(H(a)\le h\), \(D(a)\le d\), and \(\sum_tA_t(a)=1\) imply
\[
 |(\phi^{(s)})^{\prime}w(a)|
 \ge\left[
 1-(1+\|\phi^{(s)}\|_\infty)h(T/s)^\beta
   -\|\phi^{(s)}\|_\infty d
 \right]_+.                                                       \tag{6}
\]
Indeed, the dates with \(r>s\) have Holder weight at least \((s/T)^\beta\), and \(\sum_t\|\varepsilon_t\|_1=D(a)\).  Weighted Cauchy--Schwarz, (4), and the fact that the baseline sum is a nonnegative summand of \(E(a)\) yield
\[
 E(a)\ge\sum_j\frac{w_j(a)^2}{q_j}
 \ge\frac{Q_s}{R_s}|(\phi^{(s)})^{\prime}w(a)|^2.                \tag{7}
\]
Taking the infimum over feasible \(a\) and then the supremum over \(s\) proves \((\mathrm{P1})\).

Third, the same localization gives
\[
 \left|\sum_{r=m}^sA_{T-r}(a)\right|
 \ge[1-h(T/s)^\beta]_+ .                                        \tag{8}
\]
Applying weighted Cauchy--Schwarz to the control-pool summand of \(E(a)\),
\[
 \sum_{r=m}^s\frac{A_{T-r}(a)^2}{R_r}
 \ge\frac{[1-h(T/s)^\beta]_+^2}{\sum_{r=m}^sR_r},               \tag{9}
\]
proves \((\mathrm{P2})\).

Fourth, for the slow endpoint take only date \(t=T-k\) and set
\[
 a_{g,T-k}=q_g/Q_k\quad(g\le T-k),
 \qquad a_{g,t}=0\quad(t\ne T-k).                               \tag{10}
\]
Then \(A_{T-k}=1\), \(H(a)=(k/T)^\beta\), and choosing \(c=Q_k^{-1}\) or \(c=0\) shows
\[
 D(a)\le d_k=\min\{R_k/Q_k,1\}.                                \tag{11}
\]
For (10), \(w_j=-q_j/Q_k\) on \(G_{T-k}\) and \(w_j=q_j/R_k\) on \(C_{T-k}\).  Substitution into (1) gives exactly
\[
 E(a)=2(Q_k^{-1}+R_k^{-1}).                                     \tag{12}
\]
By balanced shares and \(k\le\delta T\),
\[
 c_qk/T\le R_k\le C_qk/T,
 \qquad Q_k\ge c_q(1-\delta),                                  \tag{13}
\]
so (12) is at most \(C_0T/k\).  This proves \((\mathrm{P3})\).

Fifth, we construct the fast endpoint without an implicit transportation step.  Put
\[
 \gamma_\delta=\min\{1/32,(1-\delta)/(32\delta)\}.
\]
For \(k\ge2/\gamma_\delta\), let \(p=\lfloor\gamma_\delta k\rfloor\) and define four ordered reverse-time blocks
\[
 I_i=\{k+ip,\ldots,k+(i+1)p-1\},\qquad i=0,1,2,3.                \tag{14}
\]
Since \(m\le k\) and \(k\le\delta T\), (14) lies in \(\{m,\ldots,T-1\}\); moreover \(p\asymp_\delta k\).  Define
\[
 U_i=\sum_{r\in I_i}R_r^{-1}\quad(i=1,2),
\]
and put
\[
 \eta_1=\frac{U_2}{p(U_2-U_1)},
 \qquad
 \eta_2=\frac{U_1}{p(U_1-U_2)},
 \qquad
 A_r=\eta_1\mathbf1\{r\in I_1\}+\eta_2\mathbf1\{r\in I_2\}. \tag{15}
\]
Because \(R_r\) is strictly increasing, \(U_1>U_2\), so the denominators in (15) are nonzero.  Pairing \(r\in I_1\) with \(r+p\in I_2\), balanced shares give
\[
 U_1-U_2
 =\sum_{r\in I_1}\frac{R_{r+p}-R_r}{R_rR_{r+p}}
 \ge c_{\delta,q}\,p^2T/k^2,
 \qquad U_i\le C_{\delta,q}pT/k.                                \tag{16}
\]
Thus \(|\eta_i|\le C_{\delta,q}/k\), \(\sum_r|A_r|\le C_{\delta,q}\), and (15) satisfies
\[
 \sum_rA_r=1,
 \qquad \sum_rA_r/R_r=0.                                       \tag{17}
\]

Index cohorts by reverse cohort \(s=T-j\), for which cell eligibility is \(s\ge r\).  Set
\[
 P_s=q_{T-s}\sum_{r>s}\frac{A_r}{R_r}.                          \tag{18}
\]
Equations (15)--(17) imply that \(P_s=0\) outside the transition range from the start of \(I_1\) to the end of \(I_2\),
\[
 |P_s|\le C_{\delta,q}/k,
 \qquad \sum_s|P_s|\le C_{\delta,q},
 \qquad \sum_sP_s=\sum_r\frac{A_r}{R_r}\sum_{s<r}q_{T-s}
 =\sum_rA_r=1.                                                   \tag{19}
\]
Define the first array by
\[
 a^{(1)}_{s,r}=\frac{P_s}{p}\mathbf1\{r\in I_0\},              \tag{20}
\]
for transition rows \(s\), and zero otherwise.  Every cell in (20) is eligible because \(I_0<I_1\le s\).  Its column totals are
\[
 C_r:=\sum_sa^{(1)}_{s,r}=p^{-1}\mathbf1\{r\in I_0\}.          \tag{21}
\]
Put \(d_r=A_r-C_r\); then \(\sum_rd_r=0\).  Define the second array by
\[
 a^{(2)}_{s,r}=\frac{d_r}{p}\mathbf1\{s\in I_3\},              \tag{22}
\]
for \(r\in I_0\cup I_1\cup I_2\), and zero otherwise.  Every cell in (22) is eligible because \(r<I_3\le s\).  Equations (20)--(22) show explicitly that the column totals of \(a=a^{(1)}+a^{(2)}\) are \(C_r+d_r=A_r\).  Its transition-row totals are \(P_s\), its \(I_3\)-row totals are \(p^{-1}\sum_rd_r=0=P_s\), and all other row totals vanish.  Consequently, for every reverse cohort \(s\),
\[
 \sum_{r\le s}a_{s,r}=P_s
 =q_{T-s}\sum_{r>s}A_r/R_r,                                    \tag{23}
\]
which is exactly \(w_{T-s}(a)=0\).

Equations (15), (19)--(22), and \(p\asymp_\delta k\) give the cellwise and total-variation bounds
\[
 |a_{s,r}|\le C_{\delta,q}/k^2,
 \qquad \sum_{s,r}|a_{s,r}|\le C_{\delta,q}.                   \tag{24}
\]
There are at most \(C_\delta k^2\) nonzero cells and \(q_{T-s}\ge c_q/T\).  Hence
\[
 \sum_{s,r}\frac{a_{s,r}^2}{q_{T-s}}\le C_{\delta,q}T/k^2,
 \qquad
 \sum_r\frac{A_r^2}{R_r}\le C_{\delta,q}T/k^2.                \tag{25}
\]
Also, \(D(a)\le\sum_{s,r}|a_{s,r}|\le C_D\) by choosing \(c=0\) in each date, and
\[
 H(a)\le\sum_r|A_r|\{(k+3p)/T\}^\beta
 \le C_H(k/T)^\beta.                                           \tag{26}
\]
Combining (23)--(26) with (1) proves the fast upper bound.  If \(k<2/\gamma_\delta\), the pooled array (10) has \(D(a)\le1\) and \(E(a)\le C_\delta T/k\le C_\delta(2/\gamma_\delta)T/k^2\); enlarging \(C_H,C_D,C_0\) therefore covers these finitely many depths as well.

For the fast converse, suppose \(H(a)\le C_H(k/T)^\beta\).  Choose \(\Lambda=(2C_H)^{1/\beta}+1\).  If \(\Lambda k<T\), then
\[
 \sum_{r>\Lambda k}|A_{T-r}(a)|\le C_H\Lambda^{-\beta}<1/2,
\]
so \(|\sum_{r=m}^{\lfloor\Lambda k\rfloor}A_{T-r}(a)|\ge1/2\).  Weighted Cauchy--Schwarz and \(R_r\le C_qr/T\) imply
\[
 E(a)\ge
 \sum_{r=m}^{\lfloor\Lambda k\rfloor}
 \frac{A_{T-r}(a)^2}{R_r}
 \ge\frac{1/4}{\sum_{r=m}^{\lfloor\Lambda k\rfloor}R_r}
 \ge c_{\beta,\delta,q}T/k^2.                                  \tag{27}
\]
If \(\Lambda k\ge T\), Cauchy--Schwarz over all observed cells instead gives
\[
 \sum_{t,g\in G_t}\frac{a_{g,t}^2}{q_g}
 \ge\frac{(\sum_tA_t(a))^2}{\sum_t\sum_{g\in G_t}q_g}
 \ge\frac1T\ge\frac{T}{\Lambda^2k^2}.                         \tag{28}
\]
Equations (27)--(28) prove the lower half of \((\mathrm{P4})\).

Sixth, let \(a^{\mathrm{slow}}\) and \(a^{\mathrm{fast}}\) be the preceding arrays and set \(a_\lambda=(1-\lambda)a^{\mathrm{slow}}+\lambda a^{\mathrm{fast}}\).  The functionals \(H\) and \(D\) are convex, while \(\sqrt{E(a)}\) is a norm because the cell-square term in \(E\) is positive definite.  Thus
\[
 H(a_\lambda)\le[(1-\lambda)+\lambda C_H](k/T)^\beta,
 \qquad
 D(a_\lambda)\le(1-\lambda)d_k+\lambda C_D,                   \tag{29}
\]
and
\[
 E(a_\lambda)
 \le2(1-\lambda)^2E(a^{\mathrm{slow}})
    +2\lambda^2E(a^{\mathrm{fast}})
 \le C_0\left\{(1-\lambda)^2T/k+T/k^2\right\}.               \tag{30}
\]
This proves \((\mathrm{P5})\).  Together, \((\mathrm{P1})\), \((\mathrm{P2})\), and \((\mathrm{P5})\) are computable lower and upper interpolation bounds with every optimization variable and constraint exposed.

Seventh, take the near-boundary case \(k=m\) and the exact Holder budget \(h=(m/T)^\beta\).  Since every observed reverse date satisfies \(r\ge m\), any feasible array must satisfy
\[
 (m/T)^\beta
 =h\ge H(a)ge(m/T)^\beta\sum_r|A_{T-r}(a)|
 \ge(m/T)^\beta\left|\sum_rA_{T-r}(a)\right|
 =(m/T)^\beta.                                                   \tag{31}
\]
Equality throughout (31), and strict increase of \(r^\beta\), force
\[
 A_{T-m}(a)=1,
 \qquad A_{T-r}(a)=0\quad(r>m).                                 \tag{32}
\]
For a vector \(v\) supported on \(G_{T-m}\) with \(\sum_jv_j=1\), the triangle inequality gives, for every \(c\in\mathbb R\),
\[
 \|v-cq\|_1
 \ge |1-cQ_m|+|c|R_m.
\]
Minimizing the right side over \(c\) gives \(\min\{1,R_m/Q_m\}=d_m^{\min}\), and the pooled vector in (10) attains it.  Hence no feasible coefficient array exists when \(d<d_m^{\min}\), proving \((\mathrm{P6})\).  When \(d\ge d_m^{\min}\), the control-pool term and (32) give \(E(a)\ge1/R_m\), while the pooled array and (12) give the upper bound in \((\mathrm{P7})\).  By (13), both finite bounds have order \(T/m\).  For equal shares, \(R_m=m/T\), \(Q_m=1-m/T\), so if \(m=o(T)\), then \(d_m^{\min}=m/(T-m)>m/T\).  This proves the two advertised refutations and completes the corrected characterization under the unchanged assumptions.
%%% FIELD minimax-proof
By \(\text{lem:score-covariance}\), the statistical experiment is exactly \(Z\sim\mathcal N(Kx,\Sigma_{\mathrm{nest}})\).  Let \(e=(\mathbf 1_T,0)\).  Adding any multiple of \(e\) preserves the Holder increments, the cohort bound, and calendar centering, so \(e\) is a lineality direction of \(2\mathcal M_{T,m}\).  Moreover, \(Ke=\mathbf 1_{|O|}\ne0\) and \(\ell(e)=1\).  Positive definiteness of \(\Sigma_{\mathrm{nest}}\) therefore implies
\[
 d_e:=\|Ke\|_{\Sigma_{\mathrm{nest}}^{-1}}>0.                   \tag{32}
\]
If a modulus maximizer at radius \(r>0\) had Mahalanobis norm strictly less than \(r\), adding a sufficiently small positive multiple of \(e\) would remain feasible and strictly increase \(\ell\), a contradiction.  Also, adding \((s-r)e/d_e\) to an optimizer at \(r<s\) proves
\[
 \omega_{\Sigma_{\mathrm{nest}}}(s)
 \ge\omega_{\Sigma_{\mathrm{nest}}}(r)+(s-r)/d_e.
\]
Hence the constraint is active and the right derivative is positive at every radius; in particular, \(1\in\mathcal R_+\).

Write \(\omega=\omega_{\Sigma_{\mathrm{nest}}}\), \(s_1=\omega'(1)\), and \(b_1=\{\omega(1)-s_1\}/2\).  Concavity, monotonicity, and \(\omega(0)\ge0\) give
\[
 0<s_1\le\omega(1)-\omega(0)\le\omega(1),
 \qquad 0\le b_1\le\omega(1)/2.                                \tag{33}
\]
The exact-bias assertion in \(\text{lem:total-modulus-rule}\) and the Gaussian variance calculation yield
\[
 \sup_x\mathbb E_x[(\widehat\theta_1-\ell(x))^2]
 =b_1^2+s_1^2
 \le\frac14\omega(1)^2+\omega(1)^2
 =\frac54\omega(1)^2.                                          \tag{34}
\]
Because the defining sequence \(r_{\mathrm{MSE},j}\) approaches the infimum over \(\mathcal R_+\), and radius one is admissible, (34) proves the upper end of \((\mathrm{M1})\).  The middle inequality follows because \(R^\star\) is the infimum over all measurable estimators.

For the lower bound, let \(x^-_1,x^+_1\) be the canonical modulus endpoints at radius one.  Their target separation is \(\omega(1)\), and their Gaussian mean separation has squared Mahalanobis norm one.  If \(P_-\) and \(P_+\) denote their laws, direct integration of the Gaussian likelihood ratio gives
\[
 \chi^2(P_+,P_-)=e-1.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz applied to that likelihood ratio gives
\[
 \|P_+-P_-\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le\frac12\sqrt{e-1}.                \tag{35}
\]
For any estimator \(\widetilde\theta\), classify the endpoint according to which of \(\ell(x^-_1)\) and \(\ell(x^+_1)\) is closer to \(\widetilde\theta\).  On a classification error the squared estimation error is at least \(\omega(1)^2/4\).  The sum of the two testing errors is at least \(1-\|P_+-P_-\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\).  Consequently
\[
 \max_{x\in\{x^-_1,x^+_1\}}
 \mathbb E_x[(\widetilde\theta-\ell(x))^2]
 \ge\frac{\omega(1)^2}{8}
 \left(1-\frac12\sqrt{e-1}\right).                            \tag{36}
\]
Both endpoints are in \(\mathcal M_{T,m}\).  Taking the supremum over that class and then the infimum over estimators proves the lower end of \((\mathrm{M1})\).

It remains to prove the computable assertion \((\mathrm{M2})\).  For feasible \((h,d)\) with finite \(\Gamma_{T,m}(h,d)\), the constraint set is closed in the finite-dimensional space \(\mathbb R^{|O|}\).  The cell-square summand of \(E(a)\), together with \(q_g>0\), makes \(E\) coercive.  Thus a minimizer \(a_{h,d}\) exists.  The bias and variance identities proved in \(\text{lem:uniform-bounded-lift}\) give
\[
 \sup_{x\in\mathcal M_{T,m}}
 \mathbb E_x[(a_{h,d}^{\prime}Z-\ell(x))^2]
 \le(Lh+Bd)^2+\frac{\sigma^2}{N}\Gamma_{T,m}(h,d).              \tag{37}
\]
The statistic \(a_{h,d}^{\prime}Z\) is one fixed data-driven estimator for the entire class, not a law-dependent oracle.  Taking the infimum in (37) proves \((\mathrm{M2})\).  Finally, \((\mathrm{P6})\)--\((\mathrm{P7})\) from the same lemma establish the boundary refutation, so neither \((\mathrm{M1})\) nor \((\mathrm{M2})\) is replaced by the unproved diagnostic calendar formula.

Fix \(j\), put \(r=r_{\mathrm{CI},j}\), \(b=b_r\), and \(s=s_r>0\).  The score-covariance lemma and the exact-bias and variance conclusions of the total-modulus rule give, for every \(x\in\mathcal M_{T,m}\),

 \frac{\widehat\theta_r-\ell(x)}s\sim\mathcal N(t_x,1),
 \qquad |t_x|\le b/s.                                           \tag{38}
\]
For fixed \(c\ge0\), the function \(t\mapsto\Pr\{|\mathcal N(t,1)|\le c\}\) is even and nonincreasing on \([0,\infty)\): differentiating it for \(t>0\) gives \(\varphi(c+t)-\varphi(c-t)\le0\), where \(\varphi\) is the standard normal density.  The definition of \(\operatorname{cv}_\alpha\) and (38) therefore imply the displayed uniform coverage.  Measurability follows from \(\text{lem:total-modulus-rule}\).  The factor \(1+j^{-1}\) affine half-length assertion is exactly the defining inequality for \(r_{\mathrm{CI},j}\).

Let \(z_\alpha=\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha/2)\).  The triangle inequality shows that
\[
 \Pr\{|\mathcal N(t,1)|\le t+z_\alpha\}\ge1-\alpha
 \qquad(t\ge0),
\]
so \(\operatorname{cv}_\alpha(t)\le t+z_\alpha\).  The lineality argument in the minimax proof shows \(1\in\mathcal R_+\).  At radius one, (33) gives \(b_1\le\omega(1)/2\) and \(s_1\le\omega(1)\).  Hence

 2s_1\operatorname{cv}_\alpha(b_1/s_1)
 \le2b_1+2z_\alpha s_1

The infimum-approaching property of \(r_{\mathrm{CI},j}\) and (39) prove the upper expected-length bound; interval length is deterministic for each member of this affine family.  The middle inequality follows because these intervals are measurable and honest and therefore feasible in the corrected definition of \(\operatorname{EL}^{\star}_\alpha\).

For the converse, \(0<r_\alpha<1\) because \(0<1-2\alpha<1\).  Let \(x^-_{r_\alpha},x^+_{r_\alpha}\) be the canonical modulus endpoints and let \(P_-,P_+\) be their laws.  Their target separation is \(\omega(r_\alpha)\), and the Gaussian likelihood-ratio calculation used in (35) gives

 \|P_+-P_-\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \le\frac12\sqrt{e^{r_\alpha^2}-1}
 =\frac{1-2\alpha}{2}.                                         \tag{40}

Let \(C(Z)\) be any measurable interval with uniform coverage at least \(1-\alpha\).  Under \(P_-\), coverage of the lower endpoint has probability at least \(1-\alpha\).  1-2\alpha-\|P_+-P_-\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \ge\frac{1-2\alpha}{2}.                                       \tag{41}

 \sup_x\mathbb E_x[\operatorname{length}C(Z)]
 \ge\frac{1-2\alpha}{2}\omega(r_\alpha).                       \tag{42}
