\section*{panel\_\allowbreak{}interference\_\allowbreak{}decoding\_\allowbreak{}adaptation}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} Sparse direct and spillover effects are decoded from a post-period mean through a loading subspace learned from the untreated pre-panel. Conditional on a nonempty primitive class with ordered loading and noise bounds and \(K\ge\sqrt r\), the paper derives a finite-sample globally honest confidence set and its sharp margin-indexed expected-diameter frontier, proves equality of global and margin-oracle rates throughout the weak-margin region, and establishes a maximal-margin adaptation gain in the \(N=3,r=s=1\) experiment once an explicit four-line classifier has error at most \(\alpha/2\). The central open theorem is the same-sample oracle characterization over the remaining nonempty margin strata, including disconnected loading components and finite classification transitions, without beta-min, post-factor excitation, distinct loading strengths, or an assumed subspace rate.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} HeLiShiMiao2026 turns panel interference into sparse robust regression but relies on a majority-valid-controls count, asymptotic selection, and coordinatewise inference. Sparse-confidence theory assumes an observed design, while projector-confidence theory does not propagate geometry into a causal sparse-vector diameter frontier. No opened paper characterizes global-versus-margin-oracle honest diameter when the loading operator is estimated from the same fixed-factor panel.

\paragraph{Niche.} The exact Gaussian subclass makes the two information channels visible: post-period noise perturbs the sparse decomposition, and pre-period noise perturbs the loading geometry. Constant post factors force lower bounds to pay for geometry only through the original pre observations, while maximal-margin strata may split into finitely classifiable components.

\paragraph{Fill.} The project combines an exact pre-panel second-moment region based only on the signal-versus-noise gap, finite support enumeration, every-sample diameter control, compensated-rotation lower bounds, and component-aware Gaussian decision inversion. On every nonempty primitive class it delivers the adaptive frontier, the weak-margin oracle equivalence, and a rank-one maximal-margin result from an explicit four-hypothesis experiment; the finite same-sample oracle profile on intermediate nonempty margins remains the open kernel.

\section{Related work}

HeLiShiMiao2026, Introduction, Assumptions 1--4, Theorem 1, its proof, and Discussion, moves from contaminated donors to a factor-adjusted sparse outlier vector and applied direct-and-spillover targets. The present core is narrower in errors but sharper in inferential quantifiers; its global-honesty and adaptive-frontier theorems provide finite-sample joint coverage and a margin-indexed diameter frontier on this Gaussian subclass, not a replacement for their full mixing model. Wei2026, Sections 3--4, Lemma 1, Theorem 1, Theorem 2, and Conclusion, gives donor-classification-free sharp scalar partial identification by full-simplex reduction and Farkas certificates in a different envelope model; it informs the finite inversion architecture but is not a comparator the present point-identified model claims to tighten. NicklVanDeGeer2013, Theorems 3--5 and their testing reduction, and CaiGuo2017 supply composite-mixture lower-bound logic for sparse confidence procedures with observed random designs. CaiLow2004 supplies the ordered-modulus view of subclass-versus-union expected length, while EvansHansenStark2005 supplies confidence-set decision duality; neither identifies the four nuisance-profiled Gaussian loading hypotheses nor the pathwise post-inversion diameter used at the maximal rank-one margin. NaumovSpokoinyUlyanov2017, Theorem 1 and its Gaussian comparison proof, supplies the projector-confidence exemplar, but its i.i.d. covariance experiment differs from the deterministic normalized factor panel; here the relevant eigengap is only the \(d_{\mathrm{lo}}^2\) separation between the signal subspace and noise, so repeated nonzero loading strengths are allowed. ChandrasekaranEtAl2011 and FickusMixon2013 own the generic sparse-subspace and erasure-robust geometry, so the uniqueness lemma is supporting rather than headline novelty. GoffMbakop2026 shows how estimated linear-system solvability can be tested uniformly, but its coefficient regularity does not characterize the disconnected same-sample oracle frontier. The centered, normalized, and bounded deterministic factor paths, the post-factor envelopes, the latent-mean bound, and the bounded effect envelope are transparent restrictions of the present Gaussian subclass rather than conditions attributed to HeLiShiMiao2026.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:rank-range]\label{ass:rank-range}
\(1\le r<N\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (fixed-factor rank restriction, \cite{HeLiShiMiao2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:sparsity-positive]\label{ass:sparsity-positive}
\(1\le s\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (positive sparsity budget, \cite{HeLiShiMiao2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:dimension-feasible]\label{ass:dimension-feasible}
\(2s+r\le N\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (sparse-plus-subspace dimension feasibility, \cite{ChandrasekaranEtAl2011}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:pre-length]\label{ass:pre-length}
\(T_0\ge r+1\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (fixed-factor normalization feasibility, \cite{HeLiShiMiao2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:loading-bounds-feasible]\label{ass:loading-bounds-feasible}
\(d_{\mathrm{lo}}\le d_{\mathrm{hi}}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (ordered compact loading-strength bounds, \cite{HeLiShiMiao2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:noise-bounds-feasible]\label{ass:noise-bounds-feasible}
\(\sigma_{\mathrm{lo}}\le\sigma_{\mathrm{hi}}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (ordered compact noise-scale bounds, \cite{HeLiShiMiao2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:factor-bound-feasible]\label{ass:factor-bound-feasible}
\(K\ge\sqrt r\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (bounded normalized factor-path feasibility, \cite{HeLiShiMiao2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:loading-strength]\label{ass:loading-strength}
\(d_{\mathrm{lo}}\le D_{jj}\le d_{\mathrm{hi}}\) for every \(1\le j\le r\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (bounded positive factor strength, \cite{HeLiShiMiao2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:pre-centered]\label{ass:pre-centered}
\(F\mathbf 1_{T_0}=0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Demeaned untreated panels and balanced seasonal or policy-free factor cycles have an exactly centered deterministic pre-factor path.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:pre-normalized]\label{ass:pre-normalized}
\(FF^{\top}=T_0I_r\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Balanced orthogonal latent-score cycles satisfy this exact normalization, which fixes the scale of the deterministic pre-factor path relative to the loading strengths.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:pre-bounded]\label{ass:pre-bounded}
\(\max_{1\le t\le T_0}\|F_{\cdot t}\|_2\le K\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Bounded seasonal, macroeconomic, or environmental latent scores satisfy this restriction; repeated signed coordinate cycles attain it whenever \(K\ge\sqrt r\).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:post-covariance-upper]\label{ass:post-covariance-upper}
\(T_1^{-1}(G-a\mathbf 1_{T_1}^{\top})(G-a\mathbf 1_{T_1}^{\top})^{\top}\preceq I_r\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} The restriction covers constant and bounded-energy post-factor paths and excludes only post-intervention latent dispersion above the prespecified outcome scale.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:post-bounded]\label{ass:post-bounded}
\(\max_{1\le u\le T_1}\|G_{\cdot u}-a\|_2\le K\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Panels with bounded latent business-cycle, conflict-intensity, or pollution trajectories satisfy this centered-path envelope, and constant post factors are included.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:latent-mean-bound]\label{ass:latent-mean-bound}
\(\|m\|_2\le A\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} After outcomes are put on a substantive common scale, applications can bound the average untreated common shock over the post block; fixed latent mean shifts remain admissible.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:effect-sparsity]\label{ass:effect-sparsity}
\(\|\beta\|_0\le s\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (sparse attributable effects, \cite{HeLiShiMiao2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:effect-bound]\label{ass:effect-bound}
\(\|\beta\|_\infty\le B\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Normalized outcomes with a known physical or policy scale provide a coordinatewise cap on attributable changes; the envelope permits arbitrarily small nonzero effects and imposes no beta-min separation.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:gaussian-errors]\label{ass:gaussian-errors}
\(\operatorname{vec}(E_0,E_1)\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2I_{N(T_0+T_1)})\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (independent homoskedastic Gaussian errors, \cite{NaumovSpokoinyUlyanov2017}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:pre-potential-model]\label{ass:pre-potential-model}
\(Y^{\mathrm{pre}}(0)=\Lambda F+E_0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (untreated interactive fixed-effects model, \cite{HeLiShiMiao2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:post-untreated-model]\label{ass:post-untreated-model}
\(Y^{\mathrm{post}}(0)=\Lambda G+E_1\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (stable-loading post-period factor model, \cite{HeLiShiMiao2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:post-effect-model]\label{ass:post-effect-model}
\(Y^{\mathrm{post}}(1)=Y^{\mathrm{post}}(0)+\beta\mathbf 1_{T_1}^{\top}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (additive attributable-effect model, \cite{HeLiShiMiao2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:observed-pre]\label{ass:observed-pre}
\(Y_0=Y^{\mathrm{pre}}(0)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (pre-period consistency, \cite{HeLiShiMiao2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:observed-post]\label{ass:observed-post}
\(Y_1=Y^{\mathrm{post}}(1)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (post-period consistency, \cite{HeLiShiMiao2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:margin]\label{ass:margin}
\(\kappa_s(L)\ge\gamma\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (restricted sparse singular-value margin, \cite{NicklVanDeGeer2013}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{lemma}[lem:post-mean-law]\label{lem:post-mean-law}
For every \(\vartheta\in\mathcal M_{T_0,T_1}(0)\), \(z=m+\beta+\eta\), where \(\eta\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2I_N/T_1)\) and \(\eta\) is independent of \(Y_0\). This law remains exact when the post factors are constant.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By \texttt{ass:observed-post}, \texttt{ass:post-effect-model}, and \texttt{ass:post-untreated-model},
\[
 Y_1=\Lambda G+E_1+\beta\mathbf 1_{T_1}^{\top}.
\]
Right-multiplication by \(T_1^{-1}\mathbf 1_{T_1}\), followed by the definitions of \(a\), \(m\), and \(z\), gives
\[
 z=\Lambda\left(T_1^{-1}G\mathbf 1_{T_1}\right)+\beta
      +T_1^{-1}E_1\mathbf 1_{T_1}
   =m+\beta+\eta,
 \qquad
 \eta=T_1^{-1}E_1\mathbf 1_{T_1}.
\]
Under \texttt{ass:gaussian-errors}, the \(T_1\) columns of \(E_1\) are independent \(\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2I_N)\) vectors.  A linear combination of this jointly Gaussian vector is Gaussian, and direct covariance calculation yields
\[
 \mathbb E_\vartheta\eta=0,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Cov}_\vartheta(\eta)
 =T_1^{-2}\sum_{u=1}^{T_1}\sigma^2I_N
 =\frac{\sigma^2}{T_1}I_N.
\]
The same error assumption makes \(E_1\) independent of \(E_0\).  By \texttt{ass:observed-pre} and \texttt{ass:pre-potential-model}, \(Y_0=\Lambda F+E_0\), where \(\Lambda F\) is deterministic at the fixed parameter point.  Hence \(\eta\) is independent of \(Y_0\).  None of these equalities uses centered post-factor variation.  In particular, when \(G=a\mathbf 1_{T_1}^{\top}\), its centered covariance is zero and the displayed law is unchanged.

Finally, \texttt{ass:post-effect-model} gives \(Y_{iu}^{\mathrm{post}}(1)-Y_{iu}^{\mathrm{post}}(0)=\beta_i\) for every \(u\).  Therefore \(\beta_i=T_1^{-1}\sum_{u=1}^{T_1}[Y_{iu}(1)-Y_{iu}(0)]\): the causal target is the labeled potential-outcome contrast, while \(z=m+\beta+\eta\) is the observed-law reduction used to identify or bound it.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The exact post-mean reduction isolates sparse decoding from post-factor variation without imposing a covariance lower bound. \textit{Gap.} HeLiShiMiao2026 uses long-post asymptotics in its fixed-\(N\) result; this exact Gaussian reduction covers arbitrary \(T_1\), including constant post factors. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies the post-period likelihood for simultaneous per-unit effect regions in the Jerusalem-conflict and Beijing-pollution analyses.

\begin{lemma}[lem:pre-projector-region]\label{lem:pre-projector-region}
Uniformly over \(\mathcal M_{T_0,T_1}(0)\), \(\mathbb E_\vartheta\|S_0-(UD^2U^{\top}+\sigma^2I_N)\|_F^2=[2(N+1)\sigma^2\operatorname{tr}(D^2)+N(N+1)\sigma^4]/T_0\). The gap between the \(r\)-dimensional signal eigenspace and the noise eigenspace is at least \(d_{\mathrm{lo}}^2\), even when loading strengths repeat. Consequently, \(\Pr_\vartheta\{P\in\mathcal K_0(Y_0)\}\ge1-\alpha/2\), \(\mathbb E_\vartheta\|\widehat P-P\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\min\{1,2\sqrt{V_0/T_0}/d_{\mathrm{lo}}^2\}\), and \(\mathbb E_\vartheta\|\widehat P-P\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2\le\min\{1,4V_0/(T_0d_{\mathrm{lo}}^4)\}\). No eigengap among the nonzero eigenvalues is required.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(\vartheta\in\mathcal M_{T_0,T_1}(0)\).  By \texttt{ass:observed-pre} and \texttt{ass:pre-potential-model}, put
\[
 X=UDF,
 \qquad
 Y_0=X+E_0.
\]
By \texttt{ass:pre-normalized} and \(U^{\top}U=I_r\),
\[
 T_0^{-1}XX^{\top}=UD^2U^{\top},
 \qquad
 \lVert X\rVert_F^2=T_0\operatorname{tr}(D^2).
\]
Let
\[
 \Sigma_0=UD^2U^{\top}+\sigma^2I_N,
 \quad
 L_0=T_0^{-1}(XE_0^{\top}+E_0X^{\top}),
 \quad
 W_0=T_0^{-1}(E_0E_0^{\top}-T_0\sigma^2I_N).
\]
Then \(S_0-\Sigma_0=L_0+W_0\).  Under \texttt{ass:gaussian-errors}, direct expansion of the entries gives
\[
 \mathbb E\lVert XE_0^{\top}\rVert_F^2
   =N\sigma^2\lVert X\rVert_F^2,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E\operatorname{tr}\{(XE_0^{\top})^2\}
   =\sigma^2\lVert X\rVert_F^2.
\]
Since \(\lVert A+A^{\top}\rVert_F^2=2\lVert A\rVert_F^2+2\operatorname{tr}(A^2)\), it follows that
\[
 \mathbb E\lVert L_0\rVert_F^2
 =\frac{2(N+1)\sigma^2\lVert X\rVert_F^2}{T_0^2}
 =\frac{2(N+1)\sigma^2\operatorname{tr}(D^2)}{T_0}.
\]
For \(i=j\), the variance of \(T_0^{-1}\sum_t(E_{0,it}^2-\sigma^2)\) is \(2\sigma^4/T_0\); for \(i\ne j\), the variance of \(T_0^{-1}\sum_tE_{0,it}E_{0,jt}\) is \(\sigma^4/T_0\).  Summing all \(N^2\) entries yields
\[
 \mathbb E\lVert W_0\rVert_F^2
 =\frac{\{2N+N(N-1)\}\sigma^4}{T_0}
 =\frac{N(N+1)\sigma^4}{T_0}.
\]
Every term in \(\mathbb E\langle L_0,W_0\rangle_F\) is a centered Gaussian third moment and hence is zero; this also follows directly by changing the sign of the relevant centered Gaussian vector.  Therefore
\[
 \mathbb E_\vartheta\lVert S_0-\Sigma_0\rVert_F^2
 =\frac{2(N+1)\sigma^2\operatorname{tr}(D^2)+N(N+1)\sigma^4}{T_0}.
\]

The eigenvalues of \(\Sigma_0\) on \(L\) are \(\sigma^2+D_{jj}^2\), while every eigenvalue on \(L^{\perp}\) is \(\sigma^2\).  By \texttt{ass:loading-strength}, their separating gap is at least \(g=d_{\mathrm{lo}}^2\); no separation among the \(D_{jj}^2\)'s is used.

For completeness, set \(\Delta=S_0-\Sigma_0\) and \(e=\lVert\widehat P-P\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\).  If \(\lVert\Delta\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\ge g/2\), then \(e\le1\le2\lVert\Delta\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}/g\), because two equal-rank orthogonal projectors are at operator distance at most one.  Otherwise, let \(\widehat U\) be an orthonormal frame selected by the fixed tie rule for the top \(r\) eigenvalues of \(S_0\), and write \(S_0\widehat U=\widehat U\widehat A\).  The variational characterization of ordered eigenvalues gives
\[
 \lambda_{\min}(\widehat A)-\sigma^2
 \ge g-\lVert\Delta\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}>g/2.
\]
Multiplication of the eigen-equation by \(I_N-P\) gives
\[
 (I_N-P)\widehat U(\widehat A-\sigma^2I_r)
 =(I_N-P)\Delta\widehat U.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \lVert(I_N-P)\widehat U\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le\frac{\lVert\Delta\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}}
          {g-\lVert\Delta\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}}
 \le\frac{2\lVert\Delta\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}}{g}.
\]
For equal-rank orthogonal projectors the left side is \(\lVert\widehat P-P\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\): both equal the largest sine of the principal angles, as follows by writing orthonormal bases in principal-angle coordinates.  Thus in all cases
\[
 e\le\min\left\{1,\frac{2\lVert\Delta\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}}{d_{\mathrm{lo}}^2}\right\}.
\]
Since \(\lVert\Delta\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le\lVert\Delta\rVert_F\), the exact moment identity, \texttt{ass:loading-strength}, and the bounds on \(\sigma\) imply
\[
 \mathbb E e\le\min\left\{1,\frac{2\sqrt{V_0/T_0}}{d_{\mathrm{lo}}^2}\right\},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E e^2\le\min\left\{1,\frac{4V_0}{T_0d_{\mathrm{lo}}^4}\right\}.
\]
Indeed, \(\operatorname{tr}(D^2)\le rd_{\mathrm{hi}}^2\), so the numerator of the second moment is bounded by \(V_0\) as defined in \texttt{def:loading-region}.

If \(\delta_0=1\), every pair of rank-\(r\) orthogonal projectors is at distance at most one, and hence \(P\in\mathcal K_0(Y_0)\) surely.  If \(\delta_0<1\), then the definition of \(\delta_0\) and the second-moment Markov inequality give
\[
 \Pr_\vartheta\{P\notin\mathcal K_0(Y_0)\}
 =\Pr_\vartheta\{e>\delta_0\}
 \le\frac{\mathbb Ee^2}{\delta_0^2}
 \le\frac\alpha2.
\]
This proves every assertion in the lemma, uniformly over \texttt{def:model-class}.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The nuisance subspace rate is derived from the deterministic-factor Gaussian primitives using only the signal-versus-noise gap \(d_{\mathrm{lo}}^2\). \textit{Gap.} NaumovSpokoinyUlyanov2017 treats centered i.i.d. covariance observations and bootstrap projector loss; the displayed identity is for a deterministic normalized factor array with additive Gaussian panel noise. \textit{Consumer.} It gives the finite-sample geometry input needed by any full-panel interference interval that propagates loading uncertainty.

\begin{lemma}[lem:four-line-classification]\label{lem:four-line-classification}
In the specialization \(N=3,r=s=1\) and \(\gamma=\kappa_\star=1/\sqrt3\), the maximal-margin loading class is exactly \(\{\operatorname{range}(P_h):h\in\mathcal H_4\}\). Distinct projectors satisfy \(\lVert P_h-P_{h'}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}=2\sqrt2/3\). For the four-hypothesis Gaussian experiment \(\mathcal E_4(T_0)\), with nuisance parameters \(d,f,\sigma\) profiled over their full stated sets, the nearest-projector rule obeys \(\max_{h\in\mathcal H_4}\sup_{d,f,\sigma}\mathbb P_{h,d,f,\sigma}(\widehat h\ne h)\le p_{\mathrm{cls}}(T_0)=\min\{1,18V_0/(T_0d_{\mathrm{lo}}^4)\}\). Hence \(T_0\ge\overline T\) implies uniform misclassification probability at most \(\alpha/2\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Specialize to \(N=3,r=s=1\), and write \(L=\operatorname{span}(u)\) with \(\lVert u\rVert_2=1\).  The complementary-row expression in the definition of \(\kappa_s\) has complements of size one, and therefore
\[
 \kappa_1(L)=\min_{1\le i\le3}|u_i|.
\]
Since \(\sum_i u_i^2=1\), the minimum is at most \(1/\sqrt3\).  Equality holds if and only if every \(u_i^2=1/3\).  After quotienting by the immaterial global sign and choosing the first coordinate positive, the four resulting lines are exactly \(\operatorname{range}(P_h)\), \(h\in\mathcal H_4\).  This proves \(\kappa_\star=1/\sqrt3\) and the asserted maximal-margin classification.

If \(h\ne h'\), then \(|u_h^{\top}u_{h'}|=1/3\).  A direct two-dimensional calculation on \(\operatorname{span}(u_h,u_{h'})\) gives
\[
 \lVert P_h-P_{h'}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
 =\sqrt{1-(u_h^{\top}u_{h'})^2}
 =\frac{2\sqrt2}{3}.
\]
Let the true label be \(h\), and put \(e=\lVert\widehat P-P_h\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\).  If \(\widehat h\ne h\), the nearest-projector definition in \texttt{def:four-line-experiment} and the triangle inequality imply
\[
 \frac{2\sqrt2}{3}
 \le\lVert P_{\widehat h}-P_h\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le\lVert P_{\widehat h}-\widehat P\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
     +\lVert\widehat P-P_h\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le2e.
\]
Thus misclassification implies \(e^2\ge2/9\).  The second-moment bound in the proved \texttt{lem:pre-projector-region} is uniform in every nuisance \(d,f,\sigma\) appearing in \(\mathcal E_4(T_0)\), so
\[
 \sup_{h,d,f,\sigma}\Pr_{h,d,f,\sigma}(\widehat h\ne h)
 \le\min\left\{1,\frac{9}{2}\frac{4V_0}{T_0d_{\mathrm{lo}}^4}\right\}
 =p_{\mathrm{cls}}(T_0).
\]
Finally, \(T_0\ge\overline T\) and the definition of \(\overline T\) imply
\[
 \frac{18V_0}{T_0d_{\mathrm{lo}}^4}\le\frac\alpha2.
\]
The profiling in \texttt{def:four-line-experiment} is therefore fully covered by the same uniform inequality.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The explicit profiled experiment turns the endpoint geometry into a uniform finite-sample decision bound with a declared error budget. \textit{Gap.} EvansHansenStark2005 supplies abstract confidence-set decision duality but does not identify these four Gaussian components or profile the panel nuisances \(d,f,\sigma\). \textit{Consumer.} It certifies when a loading-margin oracle may spend part of its noncoverage budget on component classification without widening the reported effect set.

\begin{theorem}[thm:global-honesty]\label{thm:global-honesty}
Whenever \(\mathcal M_{T_0,T_1}(0)\ne\varnothing\), the support-enumerated set \(\widehat{\mathcal C}_{\mathrm{ad}}(Y_0,Y_1)\) is a measurable compact subset of \([-B,B]^N\), is computable by finitely many support-specific compact semialgebraic feasibility problems, is independent of \(\gamma\), and satisfies \(\inf_{\vartheta\in\mathcal M_{T_0,T_1}(0)}\Pr_\vartheta\{\beta\in\widehat{\mathcal C}_{\mathrm{ad}}(Y_0,Y_1)\}\ge0.95\). Empty and singleton outputs have Euclidean diameter zero. This guarantee includes positive-margin Gaussian instances with more affected units than the majority-valid-controls count in HeLiShiMiao2026 permits.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For a fixed support \(S\subseteq[N]\), \(|S|\le s\), let \(\mathcal F_S(Y_0,Y_1)\) be the set of triples \((b,P',m')\) satisfying the constraints in \texttt{def:adaptive-confidence-set} and \(\operatorname{supp}(b)\subseteq S\).  The set of rank-\(r\) orthogonal projectors is compact, as are \([-B,B]^N\) and the closed Euclidean \(A\)-ball.  All defining equalities and inequalities are closed in \((b,P',m')\).  Hence every \(\mathcal F_S(Y_0,Y_1)\) is compact, and its projection onto \(b\) is compact.  The union is over finitely many supports, so \(\widehat{\mathcal C}_{\mathrm{ad}}\) is a compact subset of \([-B,B]^N\), including when it is empty.

The fixed eigenspace tie rule in \texttt{def:loading-region} makes \(\widehat P\) a Borel function of \(S_0\), and \(S_0\) and \(z\) are continuous functions of the observations.  Here is a direct measurability check.  For every open \(O\subseteq[-B,B]^N\), write
\[
 O_j=\{b\in[-B,B]^N:\operatorname{dist}(b,O^c)\ge j^{-1}\},
 \qquad O=\bigcup_{j\ge1}O_j.
\]
For fixed \(S,j\), encode all equalities and inequalities defining \(\mathcal F_S\), together with \(b\in O_j\), by one nonnegative maximum-violation function.  It is Borel in the data and continuous in the compact decision variables.  Its infimum is Borel because it equals the infimum over a fixed countable dense subset of the compact decision space.  Compactness makes the infimum zero exactly when a feasible triple exists.  Therefore
\[
 \{(Y_0,Y_1):\widehat{\mathcal C}_{\mathrm{ad}}(Y_0,Y_1)\cap O\ne\varnothing\}
\]
is a countable union over \(j\) and a finite union over \(S\) of Borel sets.  This is precisely measurability of the compact-valued procedure.

The same description is an explicit finite computation: enumerate the \(\sum_{k=0}^s{N\choose k}\) supports, compute \(S_0,\widehat P,z\), and for each support solve the displayed compact feasibility problem in the entries of \(b,P',m'\).  Projector identities, support restrictions, range membership \(P'm'=m'\), quadratic norm constraints, and the operator-norm constraint are semialgebraic; the latter can be checked by the principal minors of the two symmetric matrices \(\delta_0I_N\pm(P'-\widehat P)\).  Thus no support recovery or beta-min condition is hidden in the computation.

Let
\[
 E_0^\star=\{P\in\mathcal K_0(Y_0)\},
 \qquad
 E_1^\star=\{\lVert\eta\rVert_2\le\varepsilon_1\}.
\]
The proved \texttt{lem:pre-projector-region} gives \(\Pr(E_0^\star)\ge1-\alpha/2\).  The proved \texttt{lem:post-mean-law}, \(\sigma\le\sigma_{\mathrm{hi}}\), and the definition of the chi-square quantile give \(\Pr(E_1^\star)\ge1-\alpha/2\).  On their intersection, the true triple \((\beta,P,m)\) satisfies every constraint in \texttt{def:adaptive-confidence-set}; in particular, \texttt{ass:effect-sparsity}, \texttt{ass:effect-bound}, and \texttt{ass:latent-mean-bound} are part of \texttt{def:model-class}.  Hence
\[
 \inf_{\vartheta\in\mathcal M_{T_0,T_1}(0)}
 \Pr_\vartheta\{\beta\in\widehat{\mathcal C}_{\mathrm{ad}}\}
 \ge1-\Pr((E_0^\star)^c)-\Pr((E_1^\star)^c)
 \ge1-\alpha=0.95.
\]
By definition, an empty or singleton subset has diameter zero.

For the comparison clause, take \(N=8,r=2,s=3\), let \(V\) have \(i\)-th row \((1,i)\), and set \(U=V(V^{\top}V)^{-1/2}\).  Every two-row submatrix \(V_C\) has determinant \(j-i\ne0\); multiplication by the invertible matrix \((V^{\top}V)^{-1/2}\) shows that every \(U_C\), \(|C|=N-2s=2\), has positive least singular value.  Finiteness of the set of \(C\)'s gives \(\kappa_3(L)>0\).  Thus three affected coordinates are permitted and decoded, whereas the majority-valid-controls count used for the \(N=8,r=2\) comparison in \cite{HeLiShiMiao2026} permits only two.  This example changes no part of the coverage proof.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} A single set delivers finite-sample simultaneous coverage without a beta-min condition, support recovery, or known loading geometry. \textit{Gap.} HeLiShiMiao2026 gives selection-based asymptotic coordinate inference under a majority-valid-controls count; this theorem gives finite-sample joint coverage on the stated Gaussian subclass throughout the unidentified union. \textit{Consumer.} The construction yields joint and coordinate-projected uncertainty for the full RRSC interference map rather than isolated post-selection intervals.

\begin{theorem}[thm:adaptive-frontier]\label{thm:adaptive-frontier}
Whenever \(\mathcal M_{T_0,T_1}(0)\ne\varnothing\), there are constants \(0<c<C<\infty\), depending only on the fixed dimensions, \(\alpha\), and the stated bounded class constants, such that for every \(T_0,T_1\) and every nonempty margin stratum, \(c\rho_{\mathrm{ad}}(\gamma)\le R_{\mathrm{ad}}(\gamma)\le\sup_{\vartheta\in\mathcal M_{T_0,T_1}(\gamma)}\mathbb E_\vartheta[\operatorname{diam}_2\{\widehat{\mathcal C}_{\mathrm{ad}}\}]\le C\rho_{\mathrm{ad}}(\gamma)\). The lower bound is obtained from legal pairs in the original full-panel Gaussian experiment, including constant-post-factor compensated loading rotations, direct post-noise pairs, and bounded-signal saturation pairs.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We first prove the upper bound.  Fix a true loading projector \(P\) with \(\kappa_s(L)\ge\gamma\), put \(e=\lVert\widehat P-P\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\), and take any \(b_0,b_1\in\widehat{\mathcal C}_{\mathrm{ad}}\).  Choose their feasible witnesses \(P_j,m_j\), and define \(r_j=z-m_j-b_j\), so \(\lVert r_j\rVert_2\le\varepsilon_1\).  With \(h=b_0-b_1\), subtraction gives
\[
 h=(m_1-m_0)+(r_1-r_0).
\]
Each \(P_j\in\mathcal K_0(Y_0)\), hence
\[
 \lVert P_j-P\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le\lVert P_j-\widehat P\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
     +\lVert\widehat P-P\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le\delta_0+e.
\]
Because \(P_jm_j=m_j\) and \(\lVert m_j\rVert_2\le A\),
\[
 \lVert(I_N-P)m_j\rVert_2
 =\lVert(P_j-P)m_j\rVert_2
 \le A(\delta_0+e).
\]
The vector \(h\) has support at most \(2s\), so \texttt{ass:margin} and the definition of \(\kappa_s\) give, when \(\gamma>0\),
\[
 \gamma\lVert h\rVert_2
 \le\lVert(I_N-P)h\rVert_2
 \le2A(\delta_0+e)+2\varepsilon_1.
\]
Two further bounds hold without division: the triangle inequality gives \(\lVert h\rVert_2\le2A+2\varepsilon_1\), and the two \(s\)-sparse cube vectors satisfy \(\lVert h\rVert_2\le2B\sqrt s\).  Consequently, on every data realization,
\[
 \operatorname{diam}_2(\widehat{\mathcal C}_{\mathrm{ad}})
 \le\min\left\{2B\sqrt s, 2A+2\varepsilon_1,
 \frac{2\varepsilon_1+2A(\delta_0+e)}{\gamma}\right\},
\]
where the last term is omitted at \(\gamma=0\).  This inequality does not condition on coverage of the true parameter.

The chi-square quantile is fixed because \(N\) and \(\alpha\) are fixed, so \(\varepsilon_1\le C_1T_1^{-1/2}\).  From \texttt{def:loading-region} and the proved \texttt{lem:pre-projector-region},
\[
 \delta_0+\mathbb Ee\le C_0T_0^{-1/2},
\]
with constants depending only on the declared fixed class constants.  Taking expectations in the pathwise bound and using
\[
 \min\{c,x+y+z\}\le\min\{c,x\}+\min\{c,y\}+\min\{c,z\}
\]
shows
\[
 \sup_{\vartheta\in\mathcal M_{T_0,T_1}(\gamma)}
 \mathbb E_\vartheta\operatorname{diam}_2(\widehat{\mathcal C}_{\mathrm{ad}})
 \le C\min\left\{B\sqrt s, T_1^{-1/2}+
 \min\left[A,\frac{T_1^{-1/2}+AT_0^{-1/2}}{\gamma}\right]\right\}
 =C\rho_{\mathrm{ad}}(\gamma).
\]
At \(\gamma=0\), the second pathwise bound gives the stipulated interpretation of the inner quotient.  The proved \texttt{thm:global-honesty} places this procedure in \(\mathfrak H_0\), so \texttt{def:adaptive-risk} yields the first upper inequality in the theorem.

We next prove the lower bound using only legal laws for the original observations.  We use the following elementary two-point calculation.  If a confidence set is honest with noncoverage \(\alpha\) at laws \(Q_0,Q_1\), then, under \(Q_0\),
\[
 Q_0\{\beta_0,\beta_1\in\mathcal C\}
 \ge1-2\alpha-\lVert Q_0-Q_1\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}},
\]
and hence
\[
 \mathbb E_{Q_0}\operatorname{diam}_2(\mathcal C)
 \ge\lVert\beta_1-\beta_0\rVert_2
 \{1-2\alpha-\lVert Q_0-Q_1\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\}.
\]
This follows by transferring the \(Q_1\)-coverage event to \(Q_0\) and applying the union bound.  For two Gaussian vectors with common covariance \(\sigma^2I\) and mean difference \(\Delta\), rotating coordinates toward \(\Delta\) shows directly from the likelihood-ratio halfspace that
\[
 \lVert Q_0-Q_1\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
 =2\Phi\left(\frac{\lVert\Delta\rVert_2}{2\sigma}\right)-1
 \le\frac{\lVert\Delta\rVert_2}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}.
\]
Thus it is enough below to keep the Euclidean difference of the complete Gaussian mean arrays below a sufficiently small fixed multiple of \(\sigma\).

We also record why every required margin value can be used.  The function \(P\mapsto\kappa_s(\operatorname{range}P)\) is continuous because it is the minimum of finitely many continuous least-singular-value functions.  The Grassmannian is path connected: principal-angle rotations give a continuous path between any two \(r\)-planes.  A plane containing a coordinate vector has margin zero.  On the other hand, take an \(N\times r\) Vandermonde matrix with distinct row nodes and orthonormalize its columns; every submatrix with at least \(r\) rows has rank \(r\), and \(N-2s\ge r\) by \texttt{ass:dimension-feasible}.  Its margin is positive.  Compactness gives an attained positive maximum \(\kappa_\star\), and the continuous image of the connected Grassmannian contains every value in \([0,\kappa_\star]\).  Since nonemptiness of \texttt{def:model-class} supplies one legal \(D,F\), those same arrays can be combined with any such orthonormal \(U\); constant post paths then supply all means used below.

For the direct post-noise pair, use any \(L\) with margin at least \(\gamma\), keep \(U,D,F,\sigma\) fixed, take constant post factors with \(m=0\), and set \(\beta_0=0\), \(\beta_1=t e_j\).  Both points belong to \(\mathcal M_{T_0,T_1}(\gamma)\) when \(0<t\le B\).  Their pre means agree and their complete post mean arrays differ in norm by \(\sqrt{T_1}t\).  Choosing \(t=c_1\min\{B,T_1^{-1/2}\}\) gives bounded total variation and hence the lower term
\[
 \min\{B,T_1^{-1/2}\}.
\]

For fixed-geometry weak decoding, let \(\gamma>0\), choose \(L\) with margin exactly \(\gamma\), and choose a unit \(2s\)-sparse \(h\) attaining the margin.  Partition its support into disjoint sets \(J_0,J_1\) of size at most \(s\), and write \(h=h_0+h_1\) for the corresponding restrictions.  Set \(\beta_0=-th_1\) and \(\beta_1=th_0\), so \(\beta_1-\beta_0=th\) and both effect vectors are \(s\)-sparse.  Put
\[
 m_0=\frac t2Ph,
 \qquad m_1=-\frac t2Ph.
\]
Then the post mean difference is
\[
 (m_1+\beta_1)-(m_0+\beta_0)=t(I_N-P)h,
\]
whose norm is \(t\gamma\).  Both loading spaces and all pre means agree.  For
\[
 t=c_2\min\left\{B,A,\frac{T_1^{-1/2}}{\gamma}\right\},
\]
with \(c_2\) fixed and small, all mean and cube bounds hold and the full-data total variation is bounded away from \(1-2\alpha\).  This gives the lower term
\[
 \min\left\{B,A,\frac{T_1^{-1/2}}{\gamma}\right\}.
\]

For the loading-uncertainty channel, use the same \(L,h\).  Write
\[
 h=\rho v+\gamma w,
 \qquad \rho=\sqrt{1-\gamma^2},
 \qquad v\in L,quad w\in L^\perp,quad \lVert v\rVert_2=\lVert w\rVert_2=1.
\]
Here \(\rho>0\): averaging the row energies over complements of size \(N-2s\) gives \(\kappa_\star^2\le(N-2s)/N<1\).  Set \(M=A/2\), \(m_0=Mv\), \(m_1=m_0-th\), and split \(th=\beta_1-\beta_0\) into two \(s\)-sparse effects.  If \(t\le A/8\), then \(\lVert m_j\rVert_2\le A\) and \(m_1\ne0\).  Let \(R\) be the rotation in \(\operatorname{span}(v,w)\) carrying \(v\) to \(m_1/\lVert m_1\rVert_2\), fixing its orthogonal complement, and set \(U_1=RU_0\).  Then \(m_1\in\operatorname{col}(U_1)\), and
\[
 m_1+\beta_1=m_0+\beta_0,
\]
so constant post factors make all post-observation means exactly equal.  The rotation angle \(\theta\) obeys
\[
 \sin\theta=\frac{t\gamma}{\lVert m_1\rVert_2}
 \le\frac{t\gamma}{M-t}
 \le\frac{8t\gamma}{3A}.
\]
Keeping \(D,F,\sigma\) fixed, \texttt{ass:pre-normalized} gives
\[
 \lVert(U_1-U_0)DF\rVert_F^2
 =T_0\lVert(U_1-U_0)D\rVert_F^2
 \le C T_0d_{\mathrm{hi}}^2\frac{t^2\gamma^2}{A^2}.
\]
Indeed, \(R\) fixes \(L\cap v^\perp\), so the Hilbert--Schmidt norm of \((R-I_N)|_L\) is \(\lVert Rv-v\rVert_2\le\sqrt2\sin\theta\).  The first point is in \(\mathcal M_{T_0,T_1}(\gamma)\), while the rotated point is in \(\mathcal M_{T_0,T_1}(0)\), which is exactly sufficient because global honesty covers both.  Taking
\[
 t=c_3\min\left\{B,A,\frac{AT_0^{-1/2}}{\gamma}\right\}
\]
keeps the original full-data Gaussian total variation bounded and yields the third lower term.

When \(\gamma=0\), the attained sparse vector \(h\in L\) and the two decompositions constructed in the proved uniqueness lemma give two identical complete laws separated by \(c\min\{A,B\}\); the direct pair still gives \(c\min\{B,T_1^{-1/2}\}\).

Finally, with \(x=T_1^{-1/2}\) and \(y=AT_0^{-1/2}\),
\[
 \min\left\{B,x+\min\left(A,\frac{x+y}{\gamma}\right)\right\}
 \le \min(B,x)+\min(B,A,x/\gamma)+\min(B,A,y/\gamma).
\]
The right side is at most three times the largest of the three proved lower terms.  Replacing \(B\) by \(B\sqrt s\) changes only a fixed-dimensional constant.  The two-point factor \(1-2\alpha-\mathrm{TV}\) is positive by the fixed small choices of \(c_1,c_2,c_3\).  Taking the infimum over every globally honest procedure proves \(R_{\mathrm{ad}}(\gamma)\ge c\rho_{\mathrm{ad}}(\gamma)\).  All pairs use the original \(Y_0,Y_1\) observations, constant post factors, legal exact-normalized \(F\), and the declared bounded-signal constraints.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the first sharp expected-diameter frontier for a full sparse causal-effect vector when the decoding operator is learned from the same panel. \textit{Gap.} NicklVanDeGeer2013 characterizes sparse-vector honesty with an observed regression design; it does not contain the pre-panel loading-rotation channel or the \(\kappa_s(L)\)-indexed bounded-signal frontier. \textit{Consumer.} It quantifies the shortest globally valid full-vector uncertainty achievable in the fixed-factor analyses of HeLiShiMiao2026.

\begin{theorem}[thm:weak-margin-equivalence]\label{thm:weak-margin-equivalence}
Whenever \(\mathcal M_{T_0,T_1}(0)\ne\varnothing\), for every nonempty stratum with \(0\le\gamma\le\kappa_\star/2\), \(R_{\mathrm{or}}(\gamma)\asymp R_{\mathrm{ad}}(\gamma)\asymp\rho_{\mathrm{ad}}(\gamma)\), uniformly in \(T_0,T_1\). Thus global honesty imposes no rate adaptation penalty throughout the weak-decoding half of the attainable margin range.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Because \(\mathcal M_{T_0,T_1}(\gamma)\subseteq\mathcal M_{T_0,T_1}(0)\), every globally honest procedure is \(\gamma\)-honest.  Definitions \texttt{def:global-honesty}, \texttt{def:oracle-honesty}, \texttt{def:adaptive-risk}, and \texttt{def:oracle-risk} therefore give
\[
 R_{\mathrm{or}}(\gamma)\le R_{\mathrm{ad}}(\gamma)
 \le C\rho_{\mathrm{ad}}(\gamma)
\]
by the proved \texttt{thm:adaptive-frontier}.  It remains to obtain the same-stratum lower bound.

The direct post-noise pair and the fixed-geometry weak-decoding pair in the proof of \texttt{thm:adaptive-frontier} already put both parameter points in \(\mathcal M_{T_0,T_1}(\gamma)\).  They supply, respectively,
\[
 \min\{B,T_1^{-1/2}\},
 \qquad
 \min\{B,A,T_1^{-1/2}/\gamma\}.
\]
For the pre-panel term, suppose \(0<\gamma\le\kappa_\star/2\).  The attained-range argument in that proof supplies a loading space \(L_0\) with \(\kappa_s(L_0)=2\gamma\) and a unit \(2s\)-sparse minimizer \(h\).  Repeat the compensated-rotation construction with \(2\gamma\) in place of \(\gamma\), choosing
\[
 t=c\min\{B,A,AT_0^{-1/2}/\gamma\}
\]
for a sufficiently small fixed \(c\).  Its full-observation Gaussian mean distance is bounded, so the same elementary total-variation calculation applies.

It remains only to verify membership of the alternative in the oracle stratum.  For arbitrary rank-\(r\) projectors \(Q,Q'\) and every unit \(2s\)-sparse \(x\),
\[
 \left|\lVert(I_N-Q)x\rVert_2-\lVert(I_N-Q')x\rVert_2\right|
 \le\lVert(Q-Q')x\rVert_2
 \le\lVert Q-Q'\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}.
\]
Taking minima proves
\[
 |\kappa_s(\operatorname{range}Q)-\kappa_s(\operatorname{range}Q')|
 \le\lVert Q-Q'\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}.
\]
In the rotation construction, projector distance equals the sine of the rotation angle and is at most a fixed multiple of \(t(2\gamma)/A\).  The constant \(c\) can be chosen, independently of \(T_0,T_1,\gamma\), so that this distance is at most \(\gamma\).  Therefore the rotated alternative has margin at least \(2\gamma-\gamma=\gamma\), and both laws used in the lower bound belong to \(\mathcal M_{T_0,T_1}(\gamma)\).  This supplies
\[
 \min\{B,A,AT_0^{-1/2}/\gamma\}.
\]
The same three-term comparison used in \texttt{thm:adaptive-frontier} yields \(R_{\mathrm{or}}(\gamma)\ge c\rho_{\mathrm{ad}}(\gamma)\).  At \(\gamma=0\), the two model classes and the two honesty classes coincide by definition, so \(R_{\mathrm{or}}(0)=R_{\mathrm{ad}}(0)\).  Combining the bounds proves the asserted uniform equivalence throughout \(0\le\gamma\le\kappa_\star/2\), including unequal sample sizes and all saturation regimes.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem rules out the tempting but false claim that the unidentified boundary automatically creates an adaptation tax on every positive stratum. \textit{Gap.} CaiLow2004 and NicklVanDeGeer2013 show that honesty may obstruct adaptation on other unions; the loading-margin union instead has an exact no-penalty region that requires same-stratum compensated rotations. \textit{Consumer.} It tells users with weak estimated decoding geometry that supplying an external lower margin bound cannot improve the achievable order of interval width.

\begin{proposition}[prop:rank-one-endpoint]\label{prop:rank-one-endpoint}
Assume \(\mathcal M_{T_0,T_1}(0)\ne\varnothing\). In the admissible specialization \(N=3,r=s=1\), \(\kappa_\star=1/\sqrt3\). For every nonempty endpoint stratum \(\mathcal M_{T_0,T_1}(1/\sqrt3)\), if \(T_0\ge\overline T\), the decision program \(\widehat{\mathcal C}_{\mathrm{end}}\) satisfies \(\inf_{\vartheta\in\mathcal M_{T_0,T_1}(1/\sqrt3)}\Pr_\vartheta\{\beta\in\widehat{\mathcal C}_{\mathrm{end}}\}\ge1-\alpha\) and, on every data realization, \(\operatorname{diam}_2(\widehat{\mathcal C}_{\mathrm{end}})\le\min\{2B,2\sqrt3\varepsilon_1\}\). Consequently, uniformly in \(T_1\ge1\), \(R_{\mathrm{or}}(1/\sqrt3)\asymp\min\{B,T_1^{-1/2}\}\), whereas \(R_{\mathrm{ad}}(1/\sqrt3)\asymp\rho_{\mathrm{ad}}(1/\sqrt3)\). The event \(\widehat h\ne h\) uses the reserved \(\alpha/2\) noncoverage budget and contributes no additive expected-diameter term because the displayed diameter bound is pathwise for every selected line.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The proved \texttt{lem:four-line-classification} gives \(\kappa_\star=1/\sqrt3\), identifies the endpoint stratum with the four lines \(P_h\), and gives
\[
 \sup_{\vartheta\in\mathcal M_{T_0,T_1}(1/\sqrt3)}
 \Pr_\vartheta(\widehat h\ne h)\le\alpha/2
\]
whenever \(T_0\ge\overline T\).  Conditional on the true label \(h\), the proved \texttt{lem:post-mean-law} gives \(z=m+\beta+\eta\), with \(m\in\operatorname{range}(P_h)\), and the definition of \(\varepsilon_1\) gives \(\Pr(\lVert\eta\rVert_2>\varepsilon_1)\le\alpha/2\).  On the intersection of correct classification and the post-noise event, the true \(m,\beta\) witness feasibility in \texttt{def:endpoint-confidence-set}.  The union bound therefore gives
\[
 \inf_{\vartheta\in\mathcal M_{T_0,T_1}(1/\sqrt3)}
 \Pr_\vartheta\{\beta\in\widehat{\mathcal C}_{\mathrm{end}}\}
 \ge1-\alpha.
\]
The compactness and measurability argument from the proved \texttt{thm:global-honesty} applies verbatim after replacing the compact loading region by the measurable selected projector \(P_{\widehat h}\).

For a pathwise diameter bound, take any two accepted vectors \(b_0,b_1\), with witnesses \(m_0,m_1\in\operatorname{range}(P_{\widehat h})\) and residuals \(r_j=z-m_j-b_j\).  Then
\[
 (I_3-P_{\widehat h})(b_0-b_1)
 =(I_3-P_{\widehat h})(r_1-r_0).
\]
The difference \(b_0-b_1\) is at most two-sparse, and every selected line has margin \(1/\sqrt3\).  Hence
\[
 \lVert b_0-b_1\rVert_2\le2\sqrt3\,\varepsilon_1.
\]
If the supports agree, the cube gives distance at most \(2B\); if they differ, it gives at most \(\sqrt2B\le2B\).  Therefore, for every realization and every selected label, including a wrong one,
\[
 \operatorname{diam}_2(\widehat{\mathcal C}_{\mathrm{end}})
 \le\min\{2B,2\sqrt3\varepsilon_1\}.
\]
This explains why classification error consumes coverage probability but creates no expected-diameter remainder.

The last display and \(\varepsilon_1\asymp T_1^{-1/2}\) give
\[
 R_{\mathrm{or}}(1/\sqrt3)\le C\min\{B,T_1^{-1/2}\}.
\]
For the reverse inequality, fix any endpoint line and legal pre parameters, use constant post factors with \(m=0\), and compare \(\beta_0=0\) with \(\beta_1=t e_j\), where \(t=c\min\{B,T_1^{-1/2}\}\).  Both laws remain in the endpoint stratum, their pre laws coincide, and their full post Gaussian mean arrays differ by \(\sqrt{T_1}t\).  The elementary two-point and Gaussian total-variation calculation in the proof of \texttt{thm:adaptive-frontier} gives
\[
 R_{\mathrm{or}}(1/\sqrt3)\ge c'\min\{B,T_1^{-1/2}\}.
\]
Finally, the proved \texttt{thm:adaptive-frontier} evaluated at \(\gamma=1/\sqrt3\) gives \(R_{\mathrm{ad}}(1/\sqrt3)\asymp\rho_{\mathrm{ad}}(1/\sqrt3)\).  This proves all conclusions uniformly in \(T_1\ge1\) under the stated threshold on \(T_0\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The endpoint result proves exactly when finite component classification can be charged to noncoverage rather than to expected diameter. \textit{Gap.} EvansHansenStark2005 gives general confidence-set decision duality, but not this nuisance-profiled four-line panel experiment, its explicit finite-\(T_0\) error threshold, or its post-noise-only endpoint width. \textit{Consumer.} It gives a transparent diagnostic for when a certified maximal loading margin makes margin-aware intervals much shorter than globally honest intervals.

\begin{openendedquestion}[oeq:all-margin-oracle]\label{oeq:all-margin-oracle}
Assuming \(\mathcal M_{T_0,T_1}(0)\ne\varnothing\), use \(\mathfrak H_{\mathrm{or}}(\gamma;T_0,T_1)\) to construct an evaluable profile \(\Phi_{\mathrm{or}}(\gamma;T_0,T_1)\) and a \(\gamma\)-aware confidence set, and prove \(R_{\mathrm{or}}(\gamma)\asymp\Phi_{\mathrm{or}}(\gamma;T_0,T_1)\) at the same sample sizes for every nonempty stratum. The characterization must cover disconnected near-maximal loading geometries, all finite component-classification transitions, unequal \(T_0,T_1\), zero margin, small effects, and \(A\)- and \(B\)-saturation, with lower bounds evaluated under the original full observations rather than a noiseless-label reduction.
\end{openendedquestion}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the load-bearing open content: constant-rescaled pairwise moduli do not determine risk across disconnected component thresholds. \textit{Gap.} CaiLow2004 treats ordered moduli for linear functionals and EvansHansenStark2005 treats restricted-location confidence decisions; neither evaluates the sparse-compatible, estimated-subspace component experiment generated here. \textit{Consumer.} Its solution determines when a supplied geometry certificate materially shortens full-vector intervals and makes the oracle benchmark operational.

\begin{theorem}[thm:adaptation-ratio]\label{thm:adaptation-ratio}
Whenever \(\mathcal M_{T_0,T_1}(0)\ne\varnothing\), on every nonempty stratum with \(0\le\gamma\le\kappa_\star/2\), \(\mathfrak A(\gamma)\asymp1\), uniformly in \(T_0,T_1\). In the specialization \(N=3,r=s=1\), at \(\gamma=\kappa_\star=1/\sqrt3\), for every nonempty endpoint stratum and \(T_0\ge\overline T\), \(\mathfrak A(1/\sqrt3)\asymp\rho_{\mathrm{ad}}(1/\sqrt3)/\min\{B,T_1^{-1/2}\}\). Along unsaturated sequences with \(T_0,T_1\to\infty\) and \(T_1/T_0\to\infty\), this endpoint ratio has order \(1+A\sqrt{T_1/T_0}\) under the fixed-class constants. No ratio formula is asserted on the unresolved intermediate-margin region.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The direct post-noise two-point bound in the proofs of \texttt{thm:adaptive-frontier} and \texttt{prop:rank-one-endpoint} implies \(R_{\mathrm{or}}(\gamma)>0\) on every nonempty stratum, so the ratio in \texttt{def:oracle-risk} is well defined.  For \(0\le\gamma\le\kappa_\star/2\), the proved \texttt{thm:weak-margin-equivalence} gives constants \(0<c<C<\infty\) such that
\[
 c\rho_{\mathrm{ad}}(\gamma)\le R_{\mathrm{or}}(\gamma)
 \le R_{\mathrm{ad}}(\gamma)\le C\rho_{\mathrm{ad}}(\gamma).
\]
Division by the positive oracle risk yields \(1\le\mathfrak A(\gamma)\le C/c\), hence \(\mathfrak A(\gamma)\asymp1\), uniformly in \(T_0,T_1\).

In the specialization \(N=3,r=s=1\), \(\gamma=\kappa_\star=1/\sqrt3\), and \(T_0\ge\overline T\), the proved \texttt{prop:rank-one-endpoint} and \texttt{thm:adaptive-frontier} give
\[
 R_{\mathrm{or}}(1/\sqrt3)\asymp\min\{B,T_1^{-1/2}\},
 \qquad
 R_{\mathrm{ad}}(1/\sqrt3)\asymp\rho_{\mathrm{ad}}(1/\sqrt3).
\]
Dividing proves the displayed endpoint formula in the theorem.

Along a sequence with \(T_0,T_1\to\infty\), all fixed positive \(A,B\) caps are eventually inactive.  With \(a_j=T_j^{-1/2}\), the definition of \(\rho_{\mathrm{ad}}\) then gives, at the fixed endpoint,
\[
 \rho_{\mathrm{ad}}(1/\sqrt3)
 \asymp a_1+A a_0,
 \qquad
 \min\{B,T_1^{-1/2}\}=a_1.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \mathfrak A(1/\sqrt3)
 \asymp\frac{a_1+Aa_0}{a_1}
 =1+A\sqrt{T_1/T_0}.
\]
If \(T_1/T_0\to\infty\), this is the asserted divergent order.  No step of this proof supplies a formula on the intermediate-margin region, so the theorem correctly makes none.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The stated ratio records only the weak-margin and certified endpoint regimes supported by matching oracle results. \textit{Gap.} No cited panel or sparse-confidence paper derives both the no-penalty weak-margin region and the finite-classification maximal-margin gap in this original panel experiment. \textit{Consumer.} The ratio is a decision rule for whether collecting more pre periods or supplying a credible loading-margin restriction can improve uncertainty in an interference analysis.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The general oracle step is not established by the available modulus sandwich: upper and lower bounds evaluate sparse-compatible subspace moduli at different resolution constants, and that mismatch is material when a new loading component becomes statistically reachable. The explicit four-line result settles only \(\gamma=1/\sqrt3\) when \(T_0\ge\overline T\); short-pre-panel classification and all intermediate high-margin profiles remain part of the open question. In the bounded \(N=8,r=2,s=3\) kill test, direct enumeration reproduced \(\kappa_3=0.06586041095768684\) for the polynomial frame, and a seeded search over 200000 Gaussian-QR frames found margin \(0.09652308016949927\). The latter is only a numerical lower bound on \(\kappa_\star\), not a certificate of maximality or of its component geometry, so it cannot determine the same-sample oracle transition.

\section{Honest scope}

The class is a homoskedastic Gaussian subclass of HeLiShiMiao2026 with fixed \(N,r,s\), unknown deterministic factors, \(T_0\ge r+1\), ordered primitive bounds \(d_{\mathrm{lo}}\le d_{\mathrm{hi}}\) and \(\sigma_{\mathrm{lo}}\le\sigma_{\mathrm{hi}}\), and \(K\ge\sqrt r\). The centered, normalized, and bounded deterministic pre-factor path, the post covariance and centered-norm bounds, the latent-mean bound, and the effect envelope are proposal-specific restrictions with the application rationales stated in their assumption nodes. Every global-honesty and minimax-risk claim is conditional on \(\mathcal M_{T_0,T_1}(0)\ne\varnothing\), and every margin-specific claim is additionally restricted to nonempty \(\mathcal M_{T_0,T_1}(\gamma)\). The deterministic sparse-decomposition equivalence holds for every \(L\in\operatorname{Gr}(r,N)\), whether or not that subspace is realized by the panel model. Only its complete-observed-law converse is conditioned on realization: if a zero-margin \(L\) is the loading space of an admissible member, two legal effect vectors with constant post factors and common pre parameters generate the same full law. Repeated loading strengths are allowed because projector control uses only the \(d_{\mathrm{lo}}^2\) signal-versus-noise gap. There is no post-factor covariance lower bound, beta-min condition, correct-support event, or oracle subspace. The global adaptive frontier and weak-margin equivalence are proved here; the all-margin oracle profile remains the first-class open question. At \(\gamma=0\) diameter saturates at the bounded-signal scale, with known \(L\) the post component reduces to Gaussian sparse-mean inversion, and for \(N=3,r=s=1\) maximal margin reduces to four lines and removes the oracle pre-panel term only when the explicit classifier fits inside the reserved noncoverage budget.
