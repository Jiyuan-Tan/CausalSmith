\section*{eid\_\allowbreak{}lvsemme\_\allowbreak{}dog\_\allowbreak{}effect\_\allowbreak{}certificate}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} For Yang et al.'s canonical separable, minimal, rank-faithful linear model with latent confounding, measurement error, and known labels, the note proves the exact jointly attained intervention-column image over every minimum-edge DOG reconstruction. A fixed mleaf contributes \(e_u\); otherwise the image consists of normalized retained columns from globally feasible pairs, together with \(e_u\) when the intervention label can be an mleaf. Every vector has one attaining minimum model, the cardinality bound \(q+1\) is sharp for every \(q\ge1\) by an explicit Bernoulli-zonotope separability construction, and scalar singletonness is exactly a zero/minor comparison. Exact rational completion lies in promise-\(\mathrm{NP}\), with optimization and search in \(\mathrm{FP}^{\mathrm{NP}}\); fixed per-block interaction width is deterministically tractable even with arbitrarily many ambiguous groups overall. The unrestricted deterministic complexity remains open, and projected group marginals do not factor the full total-effect-matrix image.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Yang et al.'s Theorem 3 gives a generic minimum-edge DOG class and Algorithm 2 enumerates a Cartesian family, but it does not state the complete labeled joint total-effect-column image or classify exact constrained minimum completion. Prior latent-variable LiNGAM effect criteria omit simultaneous measurement-label restrictions and center-to-mleaf reassignment. Exact downstream cancellations make local edge scores invalid.

\paragraph{Niche.} The intervention column depends only on the selected center/noise pair in the intervention label's unique recovered group, although membership of that pair in a global minimum remains coupled across groups. This separates an exact algebraic identification theorem from a sharply localized discrete optimization problem while preserving coherent joint witnesses.

\paragraph{Fill.} The note proves the normalized reconstruction, complete jointly attained intervention-column image, scalar and joint singleton minor criteria, a universal sharp \(q+1\) cap with an explicit separable in-class witness, a faithful twelve-edge plateau obstruction, promise-\(\mathrm{NP}\) and \(\mathrm{FP}^{\mathrm{NP}}\) upper bounds, deterministic fixed-width block tractability, the oracle-to-all-images reduction, and the no-measurement equivalence. It leaves only the unrestricted deterministic completion classification open.

\section{Related work}

Yang, Nafea, Kiyavash, Zhang, and Ghassami (2026), Definition 2, Theorems 2--3, Algorithms 1--2, Proposition 7, and Appendix A.4 supply the canonical model, corrected rank-faithfulness and minimality setting, recovered ancestral ordered groups, and probability-one simultaneous DOG faithfulness used here. Their Cartesian enumeration does not imply a polynomial minimum-completion algorithm. Salehkaleybar, Ghassami, Kiyavash, and Zhang (2020), Section 4, provide the closest normalized-column effect-image precedent without simultaneous measurement error. Tramontano, Kivva, Salehkaleybar, Drton, and Kiyavash (2024), Theorem 3.3, give the complete unknown-graph criterion in the no-measurement subclass; the reduction proposition proves agreement there rather than claiming that criterion as new. Tramontano, Drton, and Etesami (2026) use determinant ranks and nonintersecting paths for a different identification problem, and Wienoebst, Luttermann, Bannach, and Liskiewicz (2023) give linear-delay Markov-equivalence traversal; neither controls the coupled support cancellations in exact mixed-DOG completion. Maathuis, Kalisch, and Buehlmann (2009) motivate reporting possible effects without full DAG enumeration, but their local CPDAG computation does not supply globally minimum mixed-DOG pairs. The present computational contribution is therefore an exact verification/oracle envelope and a fixed-block-width algorithm, not a resolution of unrestricted complexity.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:canonical-lvsemme]\label{ass:canonical-lvsemme}
\(M\) is a canonical linear LV-SEM-ME in the sense of Yang et al., Definition 2.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Canonical linear LV-SEM-ME, Definition 2, \cite{YangNafeaKiyavashZhangGhassami2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:separability]\label{ass:separability}
The mixing matrix of \(M\) is identifiable from \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\) up to permutation and nonzero scaling of its columns.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (LV-SEM-ME separability, Assumption 1, \cite{YangNafeaKiyavashZhangGhassami2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:minimality]\label{ass:minimality}
\(M\) is minimal in the following sense: there is no other linear LV-SEM-ME \(M'\) having strictly fewer unobserved variables than \(M\), the same observability indicators \(\ell\) for the labeled substantive variables, and the same retained mixing matrix \(W\) as \(M\) up to a permutation and nonzero rescaling of its columns.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (LV-SEM-ME minimality, Assumption 4, \cite{YangNafeaKiyavashZhangGhassami2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:rank-faithfulness]\label{ass:rank-faithfulness}
For every substantive variable \(V_i\) and every pair \((J,K)\) in either family specified in Yang et al.'s Assumption 3—either \(J\subseteq\operatorname{An}_M(V_i)\) and \(K\subseteq\operatorname{PP}_M(V_i)\), or \(V_i\) is an mleaf with parent \(V_j\), \(J\subseteq\operatorname{An}_M(V_i)\setminus\{V_j\}\), and \(K\subseteq\operatorname{PP}_M(V_j)\)—the rank of \(W^J_{K\cup\{V_i\}}\) equals the minimum cardinality of a vertex bottleneck from \(J\) to \(K\cup\{V_i\}\), allowing endpoints in the bottleneck.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (LV-SEM-ME faithfulness, Assumption 3, \cite{YangNafeaKiyavashZhangGhassami2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:known-observation-labels]\label{ass:known-observation-labels}
The observation-label map \(\ell\) is known.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Known observability indicators, Section 2 problem description, \cite{YangNafeaKiyavashZhangGhassami2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:yang-generic-domain]\label{ass:yang-generic-domain}
\(\theta\in\Theta_{p,q,\ell}^{\mathrm{gen}}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Stratumwise generic LV-SEM-ME recovery and DOG equivalence, Theorems 2--3 and Appendix A.4, \cite{YangNafeaKiyavashZhangGhassami2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:first-stage-coverage]\label{ass:first-stage-coverage}
The externally supplied aligned simultaneous region satisfies \(\Pr_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}}\!\left(W\in\mathcal C_n\right)\geq 1-\alpha+o(1)\) for the fixed data-generating law.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Honest confidence-region coverage supplied as an external premise, \cite{RomanoShaikh2010}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{lemma}[lem:observation-group-partition]\label{lem:observation-group-partition}
For every \((\theta,M,P_{\mathrm{obs}},W,\mathcal G(W,\ell))\in\mathfrak M_{p,q,\ell}^{\mathrm{Yang}}(\mathbb R)\), the label-restricted recovered candidate sets are pairwise disjoint and \(V=(\bigsqcup_{g=1}^{k}R_g)\sqcup V_{\mathrm{fix}}(\mathcal G,\ell)\). Hence \(g_{\mathcal G,\ell}(u)\) is defined uniquely for every \(u\in V\). If \(g_{\mathcal G,\ell}(u)=g\in[k]\), then \(u\) is the cogent center in a legal completion exactly when \(r_g=u\), and otherwise it is an mleaf in that group. If \(g_{\mathcal G,\ell}(u)=\bot\), then \(u\) is a fixed singleton mleaf in every legal completion.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By \(\text{lem:yang-algorithm-equivalence}\), under \(\text{ass:canonical-lvsemme}\), \(\text{ass:minimality}\), \(\text{ass:rank-faithfulness}\), and \(\text{ass:known-observation-labels}\), Algorithm 1 returns an ancestral ordered grouping that is a partition in causal group order. Every substantive label is assigned to exactly one recovered observation group. Restricting admissible center roles by the known map \(\ell\) can delete a center candidacy, but cannot duplicate a label or assign it to a second group. Hence the nonempty candidate-center sets \(R_1,\ldots,R_k\) are pairwise disjoint.

By the definition of \(V_{\mathrm{fix}}\),
\[
V_{\mathrm{fix}}(\mathcal G,\ell)=V\setminus\bigcup_{g=1}^{k}R_g.
\]
Pairwise disjointness therefore gives
\[
V=\left(\bigsqcup_{g=1}^{k}R_g\right)\sqcup V_{\mathrm{fix}}(\mathcal G,\ell).
\]
Thus, for each \(u\in V\), exactly one clause in the definition of \(g_{\mathcal G,\ell}\) applies: either \(u\in R_g\) for a unique \(g\in[k]\), or \(u\in V_{\mathrm{fix}}\) and \(g_{\mathcal G,\ell}(u)=\bot\).

A legal completion \(\omega=((r_h,c_h))_{h=1}^{k}\) selects exactly one row \(r_g\in R_g\). Consequently, if \(u\in R_g\), then \(u\) is the cogent center exactly when \(r_g=u\); every other row of that recovered observation group has the mleaf role. By the label and singleton constraints in the definition of \(\Omega\), every label in \(V_{\mathrm{fix}}\) remains a fixed singleton mleaf. These conclusions use the corrected Yang minimality and rank-faithfulness assumptions only through the cited algorithm-equivalence result.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} A total label-to-group map is necessary to bind the group index in every intervention-specific image and to separate switchable group members from fixed singleton mleafs. \textit{Gap.} Yang et al. recover label-restricted AOG groups, but the effect layer requires the induced per-label partition and fixed-mleaf branch to be stated explicitly. \textit{Consumer.} The partition makes every subsequent intervention-specific effect set a well-defined function of its displayed arguments.

\begin{lemma}[lem:normalized-reconstruction]\label{lem:normalized-reconstruction}
For every \(\omega\in\Omega(W,\mathcal G,\ell)\), all pivots in \(\Delta_{\omega}\) are nonzero, \(Q_{\omega}\) is unit lower triangular in group order, and \(M_{\omega}\) obtained from \(A_{\omega},B^{C}_{\omega},D_{\omega},B^{L}_{\omega}\) is a legal canonical reconstruction whose retained mixing matrix is exactly \(W\). The construction is invariant to independent nonzero rescaling of the columns of \(W\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(\omega\in\Omega(W,\mathcal G,\ell)\), and abbreviate \(R=R(\omega)\), \(C=C(\omega)\), \(L=L(\omega)\), and \(H=H(\omega)\). The pivot and causal-order clauses in legality give
\[
W_{r_gc_g}\ne0\quad(g\in[k]),\qquad W_{r_gc_h}=0\quad(g<h).
\]
It follows that \(\Delta_\omega\) is invertible and
\[
(Q_\omega)_{gg}=1,\qquad (Q_\omega)_{gh}=0\quad(g<h).
\]
Thus \(Q_\omega\) is unit lower triangular and invertible.

Let \(N_C\) denote the selected independent structural sources and put \(\overline N_C=\Delta_\omega N_C\). In substantive row order \((R,L)\), the reconstructed equations are
\[
X_R=A_\omega X_R+B^C_\omega N_H+\overline N_C,
\qquad X_L=D_\omega X_R+B^L_\omega N_H.
\]
Because \(I-A_\omega=Q_\omega^{-1}\), their retained mixing matrix in source order \((\overline N_C,N_H)\) is
\[
\overline W_\omega=
\begin{pmatrix}
Q_\omega&Q_\omega B^C_\omega\\
D_\omega Q_\omega&D_\omega Q_\omega B^C_\omega+B^L_\omega
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The defining identities give
\[
Q_\omega=W[R,C]\Delta_\omega^{-1},\qquad Q_\omega B^C_\omega=W[R,H],
\]
\[
D_\omega Q_\omega=W[L,C]\Delta_\omega^{-1},\qquad
D_\omega Q_\omega B^C_\omega+B^L_\omega=W[L,H].
\]
Therefore
\[
\overline W_\omega=
\begin{pmatrix}
W[R,C]\Delta_\omega^{-1}&W[R,H]\\
W[L,C]\Delta_\omega^{-1}&W[L,H]
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Returning from \(\overline N_C\) to \(N_C\) multiplies the selected columns by \(\Delta_\omega\), so the retained mixing matrix is exactly \(W\).

The inverse of a unit lower-triangular matrix is unit lower triangular. Hence \(A_\omega=I-Q_\omega^{-1}\) is strictly lower triangular, proving acyclicity of the center submodel. The block \(D_\omega\) points from centers to mleaf rows, \(B^C_\omega,B^L_\omega\) load root latent sources, and the right substantive blocks are zero because mleafs have no substantive children. All remaining observation-label, latent-child, pivot, and canonical support conditions are clauses of \(\omega\in\Omega\). Thus \(M_\omega\) is legal and canonical.

Let \(S\) be an invertible diagonal column-scaling matrix and put \(W'=WS\). For the selected and complementary diagonal blocks,
\[
\Delta'_\omega=\Delta_\omega S_C,
\qquad W'[R,C](\Delta'_\omega)^{-1}=W[R,C]\Delta_\omega^{-1}=Q_\omega.
\]
Thus \(A'_\omega=A_\omega\) and \(D'_\omega=D_\omega\), whereas
\[
(B^C_\omega)'=B^C_\omega S_H,
\qquad (B^L_\omega)'=B^L_\omega S_H.
\]
The latter changes only the units of independent latent sources. Column permutation merely relabels selected and complementary sources. This is precisely invariance under the recovered permutation-and-scaling indeterminacy.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Choice-specific pivot normalization is required before any mixing-column ratio can represent a labeled intervention effect. \textit{Gap.} Yang et al.'s Algorithm 2 reconstructs from a chosen row and column assignment, but its displayed formulas do not isolate the column-rescaling invariant normalization used by the proposed effect image. \textit{Consumer.} Every subsequent image value and countermodel is computed from this legal normalized reconstruction.

\begin{theorem}[thm:local-effect-column]\label{thm:local-effect-column}
For every legal choice \(\omega\in\Omega(W,\mathcal G,\ell)\) and \(u,v\in V\), \[\tau_{\omega}(v\leftarrow u)=\begin{cases}1,&v=u,\\0,&v\ne u\text{ and }g_{\mathcal G,\ell}(u)=\bot,\\0,&v\ne u,\ g_{\mathcal G,\ell}(u)=g\in[k],\text{ and }r_g\ne u,\\W_{v c_g}/W_{u c_g},&g_{\mathcal G,\ell}(u)=g\in[k]\text{ and }\omega_g=(u,c_g).\end{cases}\] Thus, on the cogent-group branch, changing choices outside the uniquely bound group \(g_{\mathcal G,\ell}(u)\) leaves the entire \(u\)-intervention column unchanged; on the fixed singleton-mleaf branch every off-diagonal effect is zero.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a legal \(\omega\). In substantive row order \((R(\omega),L(\omega))\), the coefficient matrix among substantive variables is
\[
G_\omega=\begin{pmatrix}A_\omega&0\\D_\omega&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The zero right-hand blocks are the canonical mleaf condition. Since \(I-A_\omega=Q_\omega^{-1}\), block multiplication gives
\[
(I-G_\omega)^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}Q_\omega&0\\D_\omega Q_\omega&I\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Acyclicity makes \(G_\omega\) nilpotent, so
\[
(I-G_\omega)^{-1}=I+G_\omega+G_\omega^2+\cdots.
\]
Its \((v,u)\) entry is the sum of products of structural coefficients along all directed paths from \(u\) to \(v\), with the length-zero path contributing one when \(v=u\). Under the surgical intervention fixing \(X_u=x\), differentiating the recursively ordered structural equations with respect to \(x\) gives this same path recursion. Thus the labeled linear total-effect column is the \(u\)-th column of \((I-G_\omega)^{-1}\); this proves its relation to the structural intervention rather than defining it from \(W\).

If \(u\in L(\omega)\), the displayed inverse has \(u\)-th column \(e_u\), so the diagonal effect is one and every off-diagonal effect is zero. By \(\text{lem:observation-group-partition}\), this covers both \(g_{\mathcal G,\ell}(u)=\bot\) and the case \(g_{\mathcal G,\ell}(u)=g\in[k]\) with \(r_g\ne u\).

If \(u=r_g\), the same column is column \(g\) of
\[
\begin{pmatrix}Q_\omega\\D_\omega Q_\omega\end{pmatrix}
=W[V,C(\omega)]\Delta_\omega^{-1},
\]
hence equals
\[
\frac{W_{\bullet c_g}}{W_{uc_g}}.
\]
The denominator is nonzero by the legal-pivot condition. Taking coordinate \(v\) proves every scalar branch. The vector depends only on \((r_g,c_g)\), so choices outside the unique group containing \(u\) cannot alter the entire intervention column.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This locality identity converts an exponentially large model class into a projection of feasible local choices. \textit{Gap.} Tramontano et al. analyze column swaps without simultaneous measurement-label and center reassignment; Yang et al. do not state the all-pairs total-effect projection. \textit{Consumer.} The identity lets an analyst report all possible effects of one labeled exposure without reconstructing unrelated DOG coordinates.

\begin{theorem}[thm:complete-effect-image]\label{thm:complete-effect-image}
For every \(\theta\in\Theta_{p,q,\ell}^{\mathrm{gen}}\), every \((\theta,M,P_{\mathrm{obs}},W,\mathcal G(W,\ell))\in\mathfrak M_{p,q,\ell}^{\mathrm{Yang}}(\mathbb R)\), and every \(u,v\in V\), the complete image of \(\tau_{\omega}(v\leftarrow u)\) over all minimum-edge DOG representatives is exactly \(S_{vu}(W,\mathcal G(W,\ell),\ell)\) in \(\text{def:effect-image}\), and \(|S_{vu}(W,\mathcal G(W,\ell),\ell)|\leq q+1\). For every \(s\in S_{vu}(W,\mathcal G(W,\ell),\ell)\) there is an attaining \(\omega_s\in\Omega^{\star}(W,\mathcal G(W,\ell),\ell)\); if the image is not a singleton, two such minimum completions furnish observationally equivalent countermodels with unequal labeled total effects. The qualifier is relative Zariski generic over each real canonical coefficient variety, equivalently Lebesgue-almost-everywhere in each stratum's free coefficient coordinates, while source-law separability remains a separate assumption; no probability claim is made for rational inputs.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a tuple covered by the theorem. By \(\text{thm:observational-dog-column-completeness}\), the observationally compatible minimum-edge DOG class is indexed by \(\Omega^\star\). By \(\text{thm:deterministic-joint-effect-image}\), its exact joint \(u\)-intervention-column image is \(S_{\bullet u}\). The definition \(\text{def:effect-image}\) then takes the coordinate projection
\[
S_{vu}=\{x_v:x\in S_{\bullet u}\}.
\]
Consequently
\[
S_{vu}=\{\tau_\omega(v\leftarrow u):\omega\in\Omega^\star\}.
\]
This identifies the causal image with a functional of the recovered mixing class; it does not define the causal effect.

If \(g_{\mathcal G,\ell}(u)=\bot\), the joint set is \(\{e_u\}\), so \(S_{uu}=\{1\}\) and \(S_{vu}=\{0\}\) for \(v\ne u\). On the cogent-group branch, taking coordinate \(v\) gives
\[
S_{vu}=\left\{\frac{W_{vc}}{W_{uc}}:c\in L_u\right\}
\cup\bigl(\{0\}\text{ if }z_u=1\bigr)\quad(v\ne u),
\]
and gives \(S_{uu}=\{1\}\). Every denominator is a nonzero legal pivot. Projection cannot increase cardinality, so
\[
|S_{vu}|\le |S_{\bullet u}|\le q+1.
\]

Every scalar value is a coordinate of a vector in the finite joint image. The joint-image theorem supplies one minimum completion attaining that entire vector. If the scalar image is not a singleton, two such completions attain unequal scalars; observational completeness makes them faithful observationally equivalent countermodels on \(\Theta_{p,q,\ell}^{\mathrm{gen}}\). That theorem also supplies the stratumwise relative-Zariski and Lebesgue-almost-everywhere qualifications. Source-law separability remains separate, and no probability assertion is made for rational input points.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The pairwise image is retained as a coordinate projection of the exact joint intervention-column target. \textit{Gap.} Scalar lists alone can hide that different coordinates were attained by different minimum models. \textit{Consumer.} Pairwise reporting remains available without sacrificing coherent joint witnesses.

\begin{proposition}[prop:singleton-minor-corollary]\label{prop:singleton-minor-corollary}
For every population tuple covered by \(\text{thm:complete-effect-image}\) and every \(u,v\in V\), singletonness follows by direct equality comparison in the displayed image. Precisely, \( |S_{vu}(W,\mathcal G(W,\ell),\ell)|=1\) if and only if one of the following mutually compatible branches holds: \(v=u\); \(v\ne u\) and \(g_{\mathcal G,\ell}(u)=\bot\); or \(v\ne u\), \(g_{\mathcal G,\ell}(u)\in[k]\), and either \(L_u(W,\mathcal G,\ell)=\varnothing\), or \(z_u(W,\mathcal G,\ell)=1\) with \(W_{vc}=0\) for every \(c\in L_u(W,\mathcal G,\ell)\), or \(z_u(W,\mathcal G,\ell)=0\), \(L_u(W,\mathcal G,\ell)\ne\varnothing\), and \(W_{vc}W_{ud}-W_{vd}W_{uc}=0\) for every \(c,d\in L_u(W,\mathcal G,\ell)\). The common value is one when \(v=u\), zero on the fixed singleton-mleaf branch and on either cogent-group branch admitting zero, and \(W_{vc}/W_{uc}\) for any admissible \(c\in L_u(W,\mathcal G,\ell)\) in the remaining branch.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By \(\text{thm:complete-effect-image}\), \(S_{vu}\) is the \(v\)-coordinate projection of the exact joint image. If \(v=u\), every normalized column and \(e_u\) has coordinate one, so \(S_{uu}=\{1\}\). If \(v\ne u\) and \(g_{\mathcal G,\ell}(u)=\bot\), then \(S_{vu}=\{0\}\).

Suppose \(v\ne u\) and \(g_{\mathcal G,\ell}(u)=g\in[k]\). For every \(c\in L_u\), the pair \((u,c)\) occurs in a legal completion, so \(W_{uc}\ne0\). If \(L_u=\varnothing\), nonemptiness of \(\Omega^\star\) gives an attaining pair in group \(g\), necessarily with center different from \(u\). Thus \(z_u=1\), and the image is \(\{0\}\).

If \(z_u=1\), zero belongs to the scalar projection. It is the only value exactly when every displayed ratio is zero. For each \(c\in L_u\),
\[
\frac{W_{vc}}{W_{uc}}=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad W_{vc}=0,
\]
because the pivot is nonzero. This proves the zero branch, including empty \(L_u\) vacuously.

If \(z_u=0\) and \(L_u\ne\varnothing\), no zero is adjoined, and singletonness is equality of all ratios. For \(c,d\in L_u\), both denominators are nonzero, so
\[
\frac{W_{vc}}{W_{uc}}=\frac{W_{vd}}{W_{ud}}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
W_{vc}W_{ud}-W_{vd}W_{uc}=0.
\]
Thus the pairwise minors are necessary and sufficient, and any admissible ratio is the common value. The cases are exhaustive.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This transparent algebraic corollary tells a consumer how to read singletonness and its common value directly from the exact image. \textit{Gap.} The contribution is the complete mixed-DOG image; the zero and cross-multiplication conditions are its direct finite-set equality test and are not advertised as an independently defined certification theorem. \textit{Consumer.} An analyst can test whether the reported finite image collapses without introducing a second diagnostic object whose definition merely restates singletonness.

Indeed every endpoint combination has positive probability, and convex hull commutes with a finite Minkowski sum.

\qquad
(B^L_\omega)_h

so \(\operatorname{An}(l)\nsubseteq\operatorname{An}(r)\), excluding the witness in \(\text{lem:yang-minimality-criterion}\). For \(q=1\) the criterion is vacuous. One-hot measurement errors supply the known measured labels without changing the retained matrix.

\begin{theorem}[thm:comp-edge-promise-np]\label{thm:comp-edge-promise-np}

\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
An assignment \(\omega=((r_g,c_g))_{g=1}^{k}\) has at most \(k\le\min(p,q)\) pairs, so its binary encoding is polynomial in the input bit length. Given \(\omega\), check \(r_g\in R_g\), \(c_g\in K_g\), the optional equality \(\omega_g=(r,c)\), distinctness of selected rows and columns, known-label restrictions, and all explicitly encoded Algorithm-2 pivot and canonicality constraints. Reject any zero selected pivot.

Clear rational denominators and compute \(Q_\omega^{-1}\), \(A_\omega\), \(B^C_\omega\), \(D_\omega\), and \(B^L_\omega\) by fraction-free elimination and exact multiplication. Bit sizes remain polynomial. If an \(m\times m\) integer matrix has entries of at most \(h\) bits, the Leibniz bound gives determinant bit length
\[
O\bigl(m(h+\log m)\bigr).
\]
Clearing denominators raises \(h\) by at most the total input bit length. Cramer's rule bounds inverse numerators and denominators by such minors, and polynomially many subsequent rational operations preserve polynomial bit length.

Verify lower triangularity or acyclicity, latent-child, mleaf, label, and canonical support conditions by exact zero tests. The four blocks contain
\[
k^2+k(q-k)+(p-k)k+(p-k)(q-k)=pq
\]
positions, so support counting and comparison with \(t\) are polynomial. This verifier accepts exactly the promised yes instances, proving both versions are in promise-\(\mathrm{NP}\).

The optimum is an integer in \([0,pq]\), so binary search with an \(\mathrm{NP}\) oracle determines it in \(O(\log(pq+1))\) queries. For search, ask the \(\mathrm{NP}\) language whether an accepted optimal certificate extends each successive candidate bit prefix. One branch remains feasible until a full certificate is recovered. Thus optimization and witness recovery belong to \(\mathrm{FP}^{\mathrm{NP}}\). This gives neither a deterministic polynomial optimizer nor an unrestricted hardness result.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem gives an exact polynomial-bit verifier and standard optimization/search upper bounds without prejudging unrestricted deterministic complexity. \textit{Gap.} Exact rational cancellations require bit-size control and cannot be certified by floating-point support tests. \textit{Consumer.} Implementers obtain a sound verification routine and an \(\mathrm{FP}^{\mathrm{NP}}\) envelope.

\begin{lemma}[lem:block-completion-decomposition]\label{lem:block-completion-decomposition}
Suppose \(V=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^sV_j\) and \(C_{\mathrm{src}}=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^sC_j\), with \(\widetilde W[V_i,C_j]=0\) whenever \(i\ne j\), and suppose every candidate group \(g\) is contained in one block in the sense that \(R_g\subseteq V_j\) and \(K_g\subseteq C_j\) for a unique \(j\).  Then, after simultaneous block permutations, every legal normalized reconstruction is a direct sum of its block reconstructions, legality factors across blocks, and
\[
\Omega=\prod_{j=1}^s\Omega_j,
\qquad
E(\omega)=\sum_{j=1}^sE_j(\omega_j),
\qquad
e^\star=\sum_{j=1}^se_j^\star,
\qquad
\Omega^\star=\prod_{j=1}^s\Omega_j^\star.
\]
A prescribed pair affects only its containing block.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a legal choice \(\omega\). Since every selected pair lies in one block, reordering selected rows and columns by block makes \(\Delta_\omega\) block diagonal. The cross-block zero hypothesis also makes \(\widetilde W[R(\omega),C(\omega)]\) block diagonal. Therefore
\[
Q_\omega
=\widetilde W[R(\omega),C(\omega)]\Delta_\omega^{-1}
=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{s}Q_{\omega_j},
\qquad
Q_\omega^{-1}=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{s}Q_{\omega_j}^{-1}.
\]
The matrices \(\widetilde W[R,H]\), \(\widetilde W[L,C]\), and \(\widetilde W[L,H]\) are block diagonal under the same order. Substitution into \(\text{def:normalized-reconstruction}\) gives
\[
A_\omega=\bigoplus_jA_{\omega_j},\qquad
B^C_\omega=\bigoplus_jB^C_{\omega_j},\qquad
D_\omega=\bigoplus_jD_{\omega_j},\qquad
B^L_\omega=\bigoplus_jB^L_{\omega_j}.
\]

Candidate membership, nonzero pivots, observation labels, mleaf restrictions, and latent-child conditions are local to a group and therefore to its block. A block direct sum is acyclic exactly when each block is acyclic, and no cross-block coefficient can violate canonicality. Hence a global assignment is legal exactly when all block restrictions are legal:
\[
\Omega=\prod_{j=1}^{s}\Omega_j.
\]
The support size of a direct sum is additive, so
\[
E(\omega)=\sum_{j=1}^{s}E_j(\omega_j).
\]
Minimizing over the Cartesian product gives
\[
e^\star
=\min_{(\omega_1,\ldots,\omega_s)\in\prod_j\Omega_j}\sum_jE_j(\omega_j)
=\sum_{j=1}^{s}\min_{\omega_j\in\Omega_j}E_j(\omega_j)
=\sum_{j=1}^{s}e_j^\star.
\]
Combining local minimizers attains equality. Conversely, replacing any nonminimal component of a purported global minimizer would strictly reduce the sum. Thus
\[
\Omega^\star=\prod_{j=1}^{s}\Omega_j^\star.
\]
A prescribed pair lies in a unique block and restricts only that factor.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:yang-dog-faithfulness-preservation]\label{lem:yang-dog-faithfulness-preservation}
For a canonical LV-SEM-ME \(M\), Yang et al.'s center/noise parameter rewriting from \(M\) to each member of its finite DOG equivalence class is invertible; under their continuous-coefficient genericity regime, all members of that class satisfy LV-SEM-ME faithfulness simultaneously with probability one.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[thm:block-interaction-tractability]\label{thm:block-interaction-tractability}
Fix a nonnegative integer \(b\). Consider a promised rational instance admitting the block partitions in \(\text{lem:block-completion-decomposition}\). Call a group \(g\) nontrivial when \(|R_g||K_g|>1\), and suppose every block contains at most \(b\) nontrivial groups. Then both \(\operatorname{CompOpt}(\widetilde W,\mathcal G,\ell)\) and every prescribed-pair problem \(\operatorname{CompOpt}(\widetilde W,\mathcal G,\ell;g,r,c)\) are solvable deterministically in
\[
(pq)^b\operatorname{poly}(p,q,\text{input bit length})
\]
time. The algorithm returns an attaining legal completion whenever one exists and an exact infeasibility certificate otherwise. Arbitrarily many nontrivial groups are allowed overall. Every complete block assignment, including the unique choices of groups that are not nontrivial, is subjected to the exact \(\mathrm{COMP\text{-}EDGE}\) verification procedure; singleton candidate sets are not presumed legal.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(b\) independently of the input. In block \(j\), let \(I_j\) be its set of nontrivial groups and put \(m_g=|R_g||K_g|\). By hypothesis \(|I_j|\le b\), and \(m_g\le pq\). A group outside \(I_j\) contributes exactly one syntactic candidate pair. Hence the number of complete syntactic assignments in block \(j\) is
\[
\prod_{g\in I_j}m_g\le(pq)^{|I_j|}\le(pq)^b.
\]
This is only a candidate count, not a legality conclusion.

Enumerate these complete block assignments. For each, run the deterministic exact verification procedure constructed in \(\text{thm:comp-edge-promise-np}\), restricted to the block matrices. In particular, it checks the unique pair of every group with \(m_g=1\), rejects a zero pivot or any failed label, acyclicity, latent-child, mleaf, or canonical support condition, computes the four normalized blocks by fraction-free elimination, and counts exact support. The verifier proof bounds each run by a polynomial in \(p\), \(q\), and the input bit length. Retain a legal block assignment of minimum support, or declare the block infeasible if none is accepted.

By \(\text{lem:block-completion-decomposition}\), global legality is blockwise legality and
\[
E(\omega)=\sum_jE_j(\omega_j),\qquad e^\star=\sum_je_j^\star.
\]
If every block is feasible, combining retained local minimizers is a legal global minimizer. If one block is infeasible, the product formula for \(\Omega\) certifies global infeasibility. Blocks with no candidate group contribute the unique empty assignment and a fixed zero support contribution. There are at most \(k\le\min(p,q)\) blocks containing candidate groups, while the total number of nonempty row--source blocks is at most \(p+q\); both counts are absorbed into the polynomial factor. The total running time is
\[
(pq)^b\operatorname{poly}(p,q,\text{input bit length}).
\]

For a prescribed pair \((g,r,c)\), first reject if \((r,c)\notin R_g\times K_g\). Otherwise restrict enumeration only in the unique block containing \(g\) to assignments with \(\omega_g=(r,c)\); all other blocks are enumerated and verified as above. The count cannot increase, and the decomposition proves that the combined result is optimal under the prescription or that it is infeasible. Because the hypothesis is per block, arbitrarily many nontrivial groups may occur over arbitrarily many blocks. The faithful plateau is consistent with this theorem: all three of its nontrivial groups lie in one interacting block, so it is handled by joint block enumeration, not by a false additive per-group score.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} A fixed bound on nontrivial groups per independent interaction block permits exhaustive local verification and combination of exact block minima. \textit{Gap.} The tractable condition must be syntactic and must not silently treat one-candidate groups as legal. \textit{Consumer.} Arbitrarily many ambiguous groups are supported overall when each block has fixed interaction width.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The effect image needs only the projected global-minimum pairs \(P_g\), but unrestricted computation of those projections remains unclassified. The faithful benchmark has a thirteen-edge strict coordinate trap and a coupled repair attaining the twelve-edge optimum, so additive per-group costs and strict coordinate descent are invalid. The bounded-block theorem enumerates complete assignments only within blocks having at most fixed \(b\) nontrivial groups and verifies every assignment exactly; it does not assume that a singleton candidate is legal. Population observational completeness is relative-Zariski generic over real canonical coefficient strata with source-law separability separate. Rational inputs are used only for deterministic bit complexity, with no probability claim on rational points.

\section{Honest scope}

The structural target is the complete joint column \(\{(\tau_\omega(v\leftarrow u))_{v\in V}:\omega\in\Omega^\star\}\), and each scalar image is only its coordinate projection. Deterministic reconstruction and image equalities hold for fixed \(W\), recovered groups, known labels, and a nonempty finite global argmin. Equality with the full observationally compatible minimum-edge Yang DOG class additionally uses separability, corrected minimality and Assumption 3, and \(\theta\in\Theta_{p,q,\ell}^{\mathrm{gen}}\); Appendix A.4's finite-class probability-one argument is used only there. The \(q+1\) joint-column cap is attained for every \(q\ge1\) by a two-row rational construction whose independent Bernoulli sources make the full mixing directions identifiable from the observed support zonotope. Exact \(\mathrm{COMP\text{-}EDGE}\) verification is polynomial, optimization/search have \(\mathrm{FP}^{\mathrm{NP}}\) upper bounds, and fixed block interaction width is polynomial for fixed \(b\). No unrestricted polynomial-time or hardness classification is claimed, and no factorization of the full total-effect-matrix image across \(P_g\) marginals is claimed. Inference remains external: a supplied simultaneous region containing \(W\) transfers coverage to the union of these exact images by event inclusion, but this core constructs neither that region nor a uniform first-stage limit theorem.
