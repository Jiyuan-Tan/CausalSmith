%%% FIELD upper-proof
Put \(J=J_M\), \(h=h_M\), \(I_j=[jh,(j+1)h)\), \(K_\ell=[\ell h,(\ell+1)h)\), and \(d_j=1-jh\), with the right endpoint included in the last covariate cell. Let \(p(z_t,z_x)\) be the vector of the ten monomials of total degree at most three, and put \(L:=\sup_{[0,1]^2}\lVert p\rVert<\infty\). Define
\[
B_*:=\int_{[0,1]^2}(1-z_t)^2p(z_t,z_x)p(z_t,z_x)^{\mathsf T}\,dz_t\,dz_x,
\qquad \beta_*:=\lambda_{\min}(B_*).
\]
For every nonzero vector \(u\), the polynomial \(u^{\mathsf T}p\) is nonzero on an open subset. Hence \(u^{\mathsf T}B_*u>0\), so \(\beta_*>0\). This number and all thresholds below depend only on the fixed polynomial basis and the displayed class constants.

We first derive the concentration inequality used below. If independent centered random variables \(Z_i\) satisfy \(|Z_i|\le b\) and \(\sum_i E_P Z_i^2\le v\), expansion of the exponential series and \(k!\ge 2\,3^{k-2}\) for \(k\ge2\) give, for \(0<s<3/b\),
\[
\log E_P\exp\!\left(s\sum_i Z_i\right)
\le \frac{s^2v}{2(1-sb/3)}.
\]
Exponential Markov inequality, optimized at \(s=u/(v+bu/3)\), and the same calculation for \(-Z_i\), imply
\[
P\!\left(\left|\sum_i Z_i\right|>u\right)
\le 2\exp\!\left\{-\frac{u^2}{2(v+bu/3)}\right\}. \tag{1}
\]
Thus this concentration property is derived from bounded summands rather than imposed on \(\mathcal P_4\).

For arm \(a\) and cell \((j,\ell)\), complete the hazard part of the learning handle by
\[
R_{aj\ell}:=\sum_{i=1}^M\mathbf 1\{A_i=a,X_i\in K_\ell\}
\int_{I_j}Y_{i,\mathrm{risk}}(t)p_{j\ell}(t,X_i)p_{j\ell}(t,X_i)^{\mathsf T}\,dt,
\]
\[
b_{aj\ell}:=\sum_{i=1}^M\mathbf 1\{A_i=a,X_i\in K_\ell,U_i\in I_j,\Delta_i=1\}
p_{j\ell}(U_i,X_i),
\]
where \(p_{j\ell}(t,x):=p((t-jh)/h,(x-\ell h)/h)\). Conditional independent censoring and the continuous event and censoring laws before one give
\[
E_P\{Y_{\mathrm{risk}}(t)\mid A=a,X=x\}=S_a(t\mid x)G_a(t\mid x),
\quad
P\{U\in dt,\Delta=1\mid A=a,X=x\}=S_a(t\mid x)G_a(t\mid x)\lambda_a(t,x)\,dt. \tag{2}
\]
The density, overlap, event-hazard, and censoring-residual bands in \(\mathcal P_4\) yield
\[
f(x)\pi_a(x)S_a(t\mid x)g_a(t,x)\ge e^{-2}/16.
\]
For \(t=jh+hz_t\), one has \(1-t=d_j-hz_t\ge d_j(1-z_t)\). A change of variables in the population Gram matrix therefore gives
\[
\lambda_{\min}\{E_P R_{aj\ell}\}
\ge (e^{-2}\beta_*/16)Mh^2d_j^2. \tag{3}
\]
An entry of one summand of \(R_{aj\ell}\) is bounded by \(L^2h\). By Cauchy--Schwarz, (2), and the upper primitive bands, its variance is at most \(Ch^3d_j^2\). Applying (1) entrywise, using \(\lVert B\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le10\max_{r,s}|B_{rs}|\), and applying Weyl's inequality to (3) show that an event \(\mathcal E^R_{aj\ell}\) on which both the required eigenvalue lower bound and the corresponding trace upper bound hold satisfies
\[
P\{(\mathcal E^R_{aj\ell})^c\}
\le C\exp(-cMhd_j^2)
\le C\exp(-cMh^3), \tag{4}
\]
and, on \(\mathcal E^R_{aj\ell}\),
\[
\lambda_{\min}(R_{aj\ell})\ge e^{-2}\beta_*Mh^2d_j^2/32,
\qquad \operatorname{tr}(R_{aj\ell})\le CMh^2d_j^2.
\]

Let \(\lambda^0_{aj\ell}=p_{j\ell}^{\mathsf T}\gamma^0_{aj\ell}\) be the third-order Taylor polynomial of \(\lambda_a\) at the lower-left cell corner, interpreted one-sidedly on boundary cells. The radius-\(20\) \(C^{3,1}\) condition and the multivariate Taylor formula give
\[
\sup_{I_j\times K_\ell}|\lambda_a-\lambda^0_{aj\ell}|\le Ch^4. \tag{5}
\]
The normal equations have the exact decomposition
\[
\begin{split}
b_{aj\ell}-R_{aj\ell}\gamma^0_{aj\ell}
={}&\sum_{i=1}^M\mathbf 1\{A_i=a,X_i\in K_\ell\}
\int_{I_j}p_{j\ell}(t,X_i)\,dM_{a,i}(t)\\
&+\sum_{i=1}^M\mathbf 1\{A_i=a,X_i\in K_\ell\}
\int_{I_j}Y_{i,\mathrm{risk}}(t)p_{j\ell}(t,X_i)
\{\lambda_a(t,X_i)-\lambda^0_{aj\ell}(t,X_i)\}\,dt.
\end{split} \tag{6}
\]
For every coordinate, one subject's contribution to the first sum in (6) is centered by (2), is bounded by a class constant, and has variance at most \(Ch^2d_j^2\). The variance identity follows by expanding the one-jump expression, using both identities in (2), and applying Fubini. Taking
\[
u=C\{(Mh^2d_j^2\log(M+2))^{1/2}+\log(M+2)\}
\]
in (1), with a sufficiently large class-uniform constant, makes the failure probability at most \(CM^{-5}\) for each coordinate. On \(\mathcal E^R_{aj\ell}\), equations (5)--(6), the trace bound, and inversion of the Gram matrix give
\[
\sup_{I_j\times K_\ell}|\widehat\lambda_a-\lambda_a|
\le C\left[h^4+\sqrt{\frac{\log(M+2)}{Mh^2d_j^2}}
+\frac{\log(M+2)}{Mh^2d_j^2}\right]. \tag{7}
\]
Pointwise clipping cannot increase this error because the true hazard belongs to the clipping interval. Since \(d_j\ge h\), the right side of (7) is at most \(Cr_M\).

For the rescaled-risk fit, set
\[
Q_{aj\ell}:=\sum_{i=1}^M\mathbf 1\{A_i=a,X_i\in K_\ell\}
\int_{I_j}(1-t)^2p_{j\ell}(t,X_i)p_{j\ell}(t,X_i)^{\mathsf T}\,dt,
\]
\[
c_{aj\ell}:=\sum_{i=1}^M\mathbf 1\{A_i=a,X_i\in K_\ell\}
\int_{I_j}Y_{i,\mathrm{risk}}(t)p_{j\ell}(t,X_i)\,dt.
\]
Now \(f\pi_a\ge1/8\), so
\[
\lambda_{\min}(E_PQ_{aj\ell})\ge(\beta_*/8)Mh^2d_j^2. \tag{8}
\]
An entry of one summand is bounded by \(Chd_j^2\) and has variance at most \(Ch^3d_j^4\). The argument for (4) gives a Gram failure probability at most \(C\exp(-cMh)\). The function \(q_a=S_ag_a\) has a class-uniform \(C^{3,1}\) norm: differentiation under the integral defining \(S_a\), followed by the product and chain rules, uses only the event-hazard band and the two radius-\(20\) Hölder conditions. Its cellwise cubic Taylor error is consequently at most \(Ch^4\).

For each coordinate, one subject's centered response contribution is
\[
\mathbf 1\{A_i=a,X_i\in K_\ell\}\int_{I_j}p_{j\ell}(t,X_i)
\{Y_{i,\mathrm{risk}}(t)-(1-t)^2q_a(t,X_i)\}\,dt.
\]
It is bounded by \(Ch\), and (2) plus Cauchy--Schwarz bounds its variance by \(Ch^3d_j^2\). Applying (1), (8), and the Taylor bound yields, simultaneously on the cell,
\[
\lVert\widehat q_a^{\mathrm{raw}}-q_a\rVert_\infty
\le C\left[h^4+\sqrt{\frac{\log(M+2)}{Mhd_j^2}}
+\frac{\log(M+2)}{Mhd_j^2}\right]
\le Cr_M. \tag{9}
\]
The event-hazard band gives \(e^{-2}\le S_a,\widehat S_a\le1\), and the exponential map gives
\[
\lVert\widehat S_a-S_a\rVert_\infty
\le\lVert\widehat\lambda_a-\lambda_a\rVert_\infty.
\]
Because \(q_a=S_ag_a\), division by \(\widehat S_a\), followed by clipping to \([1/2,2]\), transforms (7) and (9) into
\[
\lVert\widehat g_a-g_a\rVert_\infty\le Cr_M. \tag{10}
\]

Finally, on every \(K_\ell\), regress \(A\) on the four normalized monomials of degrees zero through three. The population Gram matrix has minimum eigenvalue at least \(Mh\beta_\pi/2\), where \(\beta_\pi>0\) is the smallest eigenvalue of \(\int_0^1v(z)v(z)^{\mathsf T}\,dz\). Entrywise use of (1) gives Gram failure probability \(C\exp(-cMh)\). Taylor expansion of \(\pi_1\), followed by (1) applied to
\[
\mathbf 1\{X_i\in K_\ell\}v(X_i)\{A_i-\pi_1(X_i)\},
\]
gives
\[
\lVert\widehat\pi_1-\pi_1\rVert_\infty
\le C\left[h^4+\sqrt{\frac{\log(M+2)}{Mh}}
+\frac{\log(M+2)}{Mh}\right]
\le Cr_M. \tag{11}
\]
Clipping is again nonexpansive relative to the true in-band function.

There are two arms, \(J^2\) time--covariate cells, and \(J\) propensity cells, while all matrix dimensions are fixed. A union bound over (4) and its \(Q\)- and propensity analogues contributes at most \(CJ^2\exp(-cMh^3)\). Choosing the constants in the numerator thresholds so that their union contributes at most \(CM^{-2}\), equations (7), (10), and (11) prove the displayed probability inequality.

On every failed threshold event, use the deterministic in-band fallback stipulated in the learning handle. Matrix inversion on the open set above threshold, clipping, finite sums, and parameter-dependent Lebesgue integrals are Borel measurable; the fallbacks therefore make the learner a total measurable sample map. For all sufficiently large \(M\), the floor in \(J_M\) gives
\[
h_M\asymp\{\log(M+2)/M\}^{1/12}.
\]
Consequently the first two terms in \(r_M\) are of order \((\log M/M)^{1/3}\), while the third is smaller. All constants used above are uniform in the arbitrary measurable density \(f\) because only its displayed lower and upper bands entered. This proves the theorem.
%%% FIELD lower-statement
There are constants \(c_0,c_1>0\), depending only on the displayed primitive-class constants, such that, for every sufficiently large \(M\),
\[
\inf_{\widetilde\lambda}\ \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_4}
P^{\otimes M}\!\left\{
\max_{a\in\{0,1\}}\lVert\widetilde\lambda_a-\lambda_a\rVert_\infty
\ge c_0\left(\frac{\log M}{M}\right)^{1/3}
\right\}\ge c_1.
\]
The infimum ranges over all measurable estimators of the pair of event hazards based on the \(M\) observed data units. Thus the uniform sup-norm exponent in \(\textnormal{thm:primitive-endpoint-concentration}\) is minimax sharp over the same class \(\mathcal P_4\).
%%% FIELD lower-proof
We construct a finite subclass of \(\mathcal P_4\) and derive the testing inequality used for it. Fix a nonnegative function \(\varphi\in C_c^\infty((1/4,3/4)^2)\) with \(\lVert\varphi\rVert_\infty=\varphi(1/2,1/2)=1\). Such a function is obtained, for example, by taking the product of two normalized copies of the function that equals
\[
\exp\!\left\{-\frac{1}{(s-1/4)(3/4-s)}\right\}
\]
on \((1/4,3/4)\) and equals zero outside that interval. Let \(C_\varphi\) dominate the suprema of its derivatives through order four. Choose a fixed \(\rho>0\) so small that
\[
\rho\le\frac12,
\qquad
1+\rho\le2,
\qquad
1+\rho C_\varphi\le20. \tag{12}
\]
The choice depends on no unknown feature of a law.

Let \(\gamma>0\) be a fixed constant to be selected below and, for sufficiently large \(M\), define
\[
b:=\left(\frac{\gamma\log M}{M}\right)^{1/12},
\qquad
N:=\left\lfloor\frac{1}{2b}\right\rfloor.
\tag{13}
\]
Then \(b<1/8\), \(N\to\infty\), and
\[
\log N\ge\log\frac{1}{4b}
=\frac1{12}\{\log M-\log\log M-\log\gamma\}-\log4
\ge\frac1{13}\log M \tag{14}
\]
for every sufficiently large \(M\).

Define a baseline law \(P_0\) as follows. Let \(X\) be uniform on \([0,1]\), let \(A\) be independent of \(X\) with \(P_0(A=1)=1/2\), set \(g_0=g_1=1\), and set both event hazards equal to one. Conditional on \(X\), take \(T(0)\), \(T(1)\), and \(C\) mutually independent and independent of \(A\), with the stated event hazards and with censoring survival \(G_a(t\mid x)=(1-t)^2\) for \(0\le t<1\). Extend each event hazard as one after time one and impose consistency by \(T=T(A)\). This construction satisfies conditional exchangeability and conditional independent censoring. Its density, propensity, event hazard, and censoring residual lie strictly inside their displayed bands; the constant functions satisfy all three radius-\(20\) Hölder restrictions; \(G_a\) is continuous, nonincreasing, and tends to zero at one; and the event and censoring distributions have no atom before one. Hence \(P_0\in\mathcal P_4\).

For \(j=1,\ldots,N\), put \(x_j:=2(j-1)b\) and define
\[
B_j(t,x):=\rho b^4
\varphi\!\left(\frac{1-t}{b},\frac{x-x_j}{b}\right),
\qquad
\lambda_{1,j}(t,x):=1+B_j(t,x),
\qquad
\lambda_{0,j}(t,x):=1. \tag{15}
\]
Let \(P_j\) be generated as \(P_0\), except that the arm-one event hazard is \(\lambda_{1,j}\). The support of \(B_j\) is contained in
\[
(1-3b/4,1-b/4)\times(x_j+b/4,x_j+3b/4).
\tag{16}
\]
The choice of \(N\) in (13) keeps every set in (16) inside \([0,1)^2\), and their covariate projections are pairwise disjoint. For a multi-index \(\nu\) with \(|\nu|\le3\),
\[
\lVert\partial^\nu B_j\rVert_\infty
\le \rho C_\varphi b^{4-|\nu|}\le\rho C_\varphi,
\]
and the Lipschitz seminorm of every third derivative is at most \(\rho C_\varphi\), by the mean-value theorem and the fourth-derivative bound. Equations (12) and (15) therefore put \(\lambda_{1,j}\) in \(\mathcal H^4_{20}([0,1]^2)\) and in the band \([1/2,2]\). All other primitives are unchanged from \(P_0\). The perturbation vanishes in a neighborhood of every boundary, so the extension beyond one remains continuous and atom-free. It follows directly from every member condition of \(\mathcal P_4\) that
\[
P_j\in\mathcal P_4,\qquad j=1,\ldots,N. \tag{17}
\]
Moreover, at \(t=1-b/2\) and \(x=x_j+b/2\), the \(j\)-th bump equals \(\rho b^4\) and every other bump vanishes. Consequently
\[
\max_{a\in\{0,1\}}\lVert\lambda_{a,j}-\lambda_{a,k}\rVert_\infty
\ge\rho b^4,
\qquad j\ne k. \tag{18}
\]

We next calculate information in the observed law. Conditional on \((A=1,X=x)\), the likelihood ratio of the observed pair \((U,\Delta)\) under \(P_j\) relative to \(P_0\) has logarithm
\[
\Delta\log\{1+B_j(U,x)\}-\int_0^U B_j(t,x)\,dt. \tag{19}
\]
Indeed, for an observed event at \(u\), the density is \(S_{1,j}(u\mid x)G_1(u\mid x)\lambda_{1,j}(u,x)\), whereas for an observed censoring at \(u\), it is \(S_{1,j}(u\mid x)\{-dG_1(u\mid x)\}\); dividing either density by its baseline counterpart gives (19). Taking expectation under \(P_j\), using Fubini and
\[
P_j(U\ge t\mid A=1,X=x)=S_{1,j}(t\mid x)G_1(t\mid x),
\]
gives the exact identity
\[
\begin{split}
\operatorname{KL}(P_j,P_0)
={}&\frac12\int_0^1\int_0^1
S_{1,j}(t\mid x)G_1(t\mid x)\\
&\quad\times\bigl[\{1+B_j(t,x)\}\log\{1+B_j(t,x)\}-B_j(t,x)\bigr],dt,dx.
\end{split} \tag{20}
\]
For \(0\le y\le1/2\), the function \((1+y)\log(1+y)-y\) has value and first derivative zero at zero and second derivative \((1+y)^{-1}\le1\); hence it is at most \(y^2/2\). On the support (16), \(G_1(t\mid x)=(1-t)^2\le b^2\), while \(S_{1,j}\le1\), \(B_j^2\le\rho^2b^8\), and the support has Lebesgue area at most \(b^2\). Therefore (20) yields a constant \(C_0<\infty\), independent of \(j\), \(M\), and the unknown law, such that
\[
\operatorname{KL}(P_j,P_0)\le C_0b^{12},
\qquad
\operatorname{KL}(P_j^{\otimes M},P_0^{\otimes M})
=M\operatorname{KL}(P_j,P_0)
\le C_0\gamma\log M. \tag{21}
\]
The product identity in (21) follows by writing the product likelihood ratio as the product of the \(M\) one-observation likelihood ratios and taking expectations.

For completeness, we derive the finite testing bound rather than adding a high-level information hypothesis. Let \(J\) be uniform on \(\{1,\ldots,N\}\), and conditional on \(J=j\), let the sample \(Z\) have law \(Q_j:=P_j^{\otimes M}\). Write \(\overline Q:=N^{-1}\sum_jQ_j\). Direct integration of the logarithms gives
\[
\frac1N\sum_{j=1}^N\operatorname{KL}(Q_j,P_0^{\otimes M})
=\frac1N\sum_{j=1}^N\operatorname{KL}(Q_j,\overline Q)
+\operatorname{KL}(\overline Q,P_0^{\otimes M}). \tag{22}
\]
Thus the mutual information \(I(J;Z)=N^{-1}\sum_j\operatorname{KL}(Q_j,\overline Q)\) is bounded above by the left side of (22). If \(\widehat J\) is any decoder and \(p_e=P(\widehat J\ne J)\), introduce the error indicator \(E=\mathbf 1\{\widehat J\ne J\}\). Since \(E\) is determined by \((J,Z)\), the chain rule for the finite conditional entropy gives
\[
H(J\mid Z)=H(E\mid Z)+H(J\mid E,Z)
\le\log2+p_e\log N. \tag{23}
\]
Because \(H(J)=\log N=I(J;Z)+H(J\mid Z)\), equations (22)--(23) imply
\[
p_e\ge1-\frac{I(J;Z)+\log2}{\log N}. \tag{24}
\]
Choose \(\gamma>0\) in (13) so that \(13C_0\gamma\le1/8\). By (14), (21), and (22), \(I(J;Z)\le(1/8)\log N\) for all sufficiently large \(M\). Also \(\log2\le(1/8)\log N\) eventually, so (24) yields
\[
\inf_{\widehat J}\frac1N\sum_{j=1}^NQ_j(\widehat J\ne j)\ge\frac34. \tag{25}
\]

Now fix any measurable hazard estimator \(\widetilde\lambda\). Decode by choosing the smallest \(j\) minimizing
\[
\max_{a\in\{0,1\}}\lVert\widetilde\lambda_a-\lambda_{a,j}\rVert_\infty.
\]
If the loss under \(P_j\) is strictly below \(\rho b^4/2\), the separation (18) and the triangle inequality force this decoder to equal \(j\). Hence (25) implies
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_4}P^{\otimes M}\!\left\{
\max_a\lVert\widetilde\lambda_a-\lambda_a\rVert_\infty
\ge\frac{\rho b^4}{2}\right\}
\ge\max_{1\le j\le N}Q_j\!\left\{
\max_a\lVert\widetilde\lambda_a-\lambda_{a,j}\rVert_\infty
\ge\frac{\rho b^4}{2}\right\}
\ge\frac34. \tag{26}
\]
Since \(b^4=\gamma^{1/3}(\log M/M)^{1/3}\), taking \(c_0=\rho\gamma^{1/3}/2\), \(c_1=3/4\), and then the infimum over all measurable \(\widetilde\lambda\) proves the claim. The subclass fixed \(f=1\), \(\pi_1=1/2\), and \(g_a=1\), so (17) also shows that the converse is genuinely over the original class allowing arbitrary measurable band-bounded \(f\), rather than over a substituted model.
