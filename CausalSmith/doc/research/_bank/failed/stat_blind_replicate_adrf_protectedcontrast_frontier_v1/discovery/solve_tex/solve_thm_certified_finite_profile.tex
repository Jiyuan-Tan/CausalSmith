%%% FIELD def-certified-finite-profile
Set \(\tau_n=\epsilon_n^2\).  Use Taylor--Legendre enclosures of order at most a fixed multiple of \(K_n\) for the compactly supported latent density and marked density transforms, with the multiple chosen so that their exponential Taylor remainders on \(\mathcal C_*\) are at most \(\tau_n\).  Truncate each error law at radius proportional to \(\tau_n^{-1/2}\) using its second-moment tail envelope and enclose the truncated transform by positive atomic quadrature and Taylor order proportional to \(\tau_n^{-1/2}\).  Enumerate rational finite-law witnesses consisting of rational stratum masses, certified spline density and marked-density pieces, positive rational error quadratures and tail bounds, and a bounded Bernoulli potential-outcome schedule.  Outward-rounded Bernstein branch-and-bound produces rational boxes \(b\).  A box is retained in \(\mathscr B_{n,\alpha}\) only if it carries a finite certificate for one such witness law in its prospective decoder, its recorded interval encloses \(\{\mathscr D_n(Q):Q\in\operatorname{Dec}_n(b)\}\), that interval has radius at most \(\tau_n\) and lower endpoint at most \(\epsilon_n^2\), and all endpoint coordinate and remainder enclosures used below have radius at most \(\zeta_n\).  Boxes failing the witness, model-membership, or constraint tests are discarded.  All enumeration, subdivision, comparisons, and tie breaking use a fixed deterministic rational ordering.
%%% FIELD def-decoded-certified-profile
For a retained witness-certified box \(b\in\mathscr B_{n,\alpha}\), let \(\operatorname{Dec}_n(b)\) be the set of all \(Q\in\mathcal M\) whose stratum masses, Taylor--Legendre coordinates and certified remainders for \(f_{x,Q}\) and \(q_{x,Q}\), positive error-quadrature coordinates, and second-moment tail components lie in the corresponding intervals of \(b\).  The finite witness carried by \(b\) is required to satisfy these coordinate restrictions, so \(\operatorname{Dec}_n(b)\neq\varnothing\).  The recorded constraint interval is required to contain \(\mathscr D_n(Q)\) for every \(Q\in\operatorname{Dec}_n(b)\).  Define
\[
 \Theta^{\mathrm{cert}}_{n,\alpha}
 =\bigcup_{b\in\mathscr B_{n,\alpha}}\operatorname{Dec}_n(b)\subseteq\mathcal M.
\]
There is no oracle test of abstract decoder nonemptiness: nonemptiness is supplied only by the finite witness certificate.
%%% FIELD def-certified-endpoint-evaluation
For a retained witness-certified box \(b\in\mathscr B_{n,\alpha}\) and \(a\in I\), let \(J_{b,n}(a)\) be an outward-rounded interval satisfying
\[
 \{\mu_Q(a):Q\in\operatorname{Dec}_n(b)\}
 \subseteq J_{b,n}(a),
 \qquad \operatorname{rad}J_{b,n}(a)\le\zeta_n.
\]
It is computed from \(\mu_Q(a)=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}p_{x,Q}q_{x,Q}(a)/f_{x,Q}(a)\), with every denominator enclosure intersected with \([\kappa,\infty)\).  Boxes without an accepted finite witness have already been discarded and are not used in the optimization.  If \(\mathscr B_{n,\alpha}\neq\varnothing\), define
\[
 \underline m^{\mathrm{cert}}_n(a)
 =\min_{b\in\mathscr B_{n,\alpha}}\inf J_{b,n}(a),
 \qquad
 \overline m^{\mathrm{cert}}_n(a)
 =\max_{b\in\mathscr B_{n,\alpha}}\sup J_{b,n}(a).
\]
If \(\mathscr B_{n,\alpha}=\varnothing\), set both functions equal to zero on \(I\).

Freeze \(p_x\), \(f_x=f^{\circ}\), and two fixed nonidentical asymmetric error densities formed as distinct mixtures of shifted and scaled \(\operatorname{sinc}^4\) densities.  Put a compactly supported weighted Jacobi reproducing-kernel, moment-annihilating packet into a Bernoulli conditional mark, while leaving the latent density and both error laws fixed.  Choose its amplitude to preserve strict \(H^\beta\) slack and the Bernoulli range, use its factorial attenuation in the observed signed marked channel, and finish with the honest-band testing reduction.

For every sufficiently large \(n\), the certified finite-profile algorithm terminates and returns a finite, measurably selected set \(\mathscr B_{n,\alpha}\).  Every retained box carries a finite checkable certificate of a law in its decoder, and
\[
 \Theta_{n,\alpha}
 \subseteq\Theta^{\mathrm{cert}}_{n,\alpha}
 \subseteq
 \{Q\in\mathcal M:\mathscr D_n(Q)\le\epsilon_n^2+2\tau_n\}.
\]
For every \(a\in I\) and \(b\in\mathscr B_{n,
\alpha}\),
\[
 \{\mu_Q(a):Q\in\operatorname{Dec}_n(b)\}
 \subseteq J_{b,n}(a),
 \qquad \operatorname{rad}J_{b,n}(a)\le\zeta_n.
\]
There is a constant \(C<\infty\), depending only on the fixed constants of \(\mathcal M\), such that
\[
 |\mathscr B_{n,\alpha}|
 \le
 \exp\!\left\{C\left[
 K_n^2\log\!\left(\frac{C K_n}{\tau_n}\right)
 +\tau_n^{-1/2}\log\!\left(\frac C{\tau_n}\right)
 +\zeta_n^{-1/(\beta-1/2)}
   \log\!\left(\frac C{\zeta_n}\right)
 \right]\right\}.
\]
When \(\beta>3/2\), the endpoint-entropy term is absorbed into the simpler \(K_n^2\) coordinate regime, and the last display is at most
\[
 \exp\!\left\{C\left[
 K_n^2\log\!\left(\frac{C K_n}{\tau_n\wedge\zeta_n}\right)
 +\tau_n^{-1/2}\log\!\left(\frac C{\tau_n}\right)
 \right]\right\}.
\]
No comparison between the certified endpoints and the exact-profile endpoints is asserted.
%%% FIELD proof-certified-finite-profile
Write \(s=\beta-1/2>0\).  We first establish the compactness and finite-witness facts used by the algorithm.  If \(h\) is supported on \(\mathcal A\) and \(\|h\|_{H^\beta}\le R\), Fourier inversion and Cauchy--Schwarz give, with constants depending only on the Fourier convention,
\[
 \|h\|_\infty
 \le C\left\|(1+|t|^2)^{\beta/2}\widehat h(t)\right\|_2
       \left\|(1+|t|^2)^{-\beta/2}\right\|_2
 \le C R.                                                    \tag{1}
\]
The last norm is finite exactly because \(\beta>1/2\).  Moreover, for every \(T\ge1\),
\[
 \sup_a\left|\int_{|t|>T}e^{-ita}\widehat h(t)\,dt\right|
 \le R\left\{\int_{|t|>T}(1+|t|^2)^{-\beta}\,dt\right\}^{1/2}
 \le C R T^{-s}.                                             \tag{2}
\]
On \([-T,T]\), translation of the inverse Fourier integral is uniformly continuous, uniformly over the Sobolev ball.  Equation (2), followed by a finite mesh on the bounded frequency interval, therefore proves total boundedness in the uniform norm.  A dyadic discretization makes the count explicit: keep the Fourier blocks up to \(T\asymp u^{-1/s}\), quantize each retained block outward at its Sobolev-weighted accuracy, and sum the numbers of scalar coordinates over the blocks.  This gives a uniform \(u\)-net of each supported Sobolev ball of cardinality at most
\[
 N_\infty(u)\le
 \exp\left\{C u^{-1/s}\log(C/u)\right\}.                    \tag{3}
\]
This construction is an elementary ellipsoid covering: on the block \(2^j\le |t|<2^{j+1}\), the admissible \(L^2\)-radius is at most \(R2^{-j\beta}\); quantization at block contribution \(u2^{-j s}/\sum_{\ell\le\log_2T}2^{-\ell s}\) uses \(O(2^j)\) real coordinates, and summing the resulting logarithms through \(2^j\le T\) yields (3).

The model constraints admit a dense family of finitely certified laws.  Indeed, bounded outcomes and consistency imply
\[
 |q_x(a)|=|E[Y\mid A=a,X=x]|f_x(a)\le B_Y f_x(a)              \tag{4}
\]
for almost every \(a\).  Given a model law, mix each \(f_x\) by an arbitrarily small amount with \(f^{\circ}\), scale each \(q_x\) by the complementary factor, and mix the stratum masses by an arbitrarily small amount with the uniform distribution.  The strict inequalities \(\|f^{\circ}\|_{H^\beta}<R_f\), \(\|f^{\circ}\|_{H^\beta}/2<R_q\), \(\inf_I f^{\circ}>\kappa\), and \(p_{\min}<|\mathcal X|^{-1}\) show, by convexity, that the resulting density, marked-density, overlap, and stratum constraints have positive slack.  Scaling each error toward zero preserves independence and the centering of the second error and gives strict second-moment slack.

Next push the dose functions slightly into the interior of \(\mathcal A\), convolve the positive measure \(f_x(a)\,da\) and the dominated signed measure \(q_x(a)\,da\) with the same nonnegative compactly supported spline kernel, and approximate the kernel coefficients rationally.  Common convolution preserves (4), while the preceding slack preserves all strict model inequalities under a sufficiently fine approximation.  Approximate the two error laws weakly on a large compact interval by positive rational atomic laws; for the second law, choose the final two rational atom weights and locations to impose exactly one linear equation, zero weighted mean.  The preceding scaling provides enough moment slack for this vanishing correction and does not impose symmetry.  The second-moment slack and
\[
 Q(|U_j|>T)\le M_e/T^2                                      \tag{5}
\]
ensure that this approximation is uniform for the characteristic functions on \([ -\nu_*,\nu_*]\).  Finally set \(m_x=q_x/f_x\) on \(I\) and generate a potential-outcome schedule taking values \(\{-B_Y,B_Y\}\), with conditional success probability \((m_x(a)+B_Y)/(2B_Y)\), from a uniform random variable independent of \(A\) given \(X\).  This explicitly enforces boundedness, weak unconfoundedness, consistency, and continuity of the conditional causal mean on \(I\).  Positivity of splines, the inequalities (4), Sobolev norms, exact centering, moment bounds, and all finite coefficient memberships are checkable by rational Bernstein enclosures.  We have therefore constructed a countable, finitely certified family dense in the model coordinates used by \(\mathscr D_n\) and by the endpoint maps.

We next justify the error-coordinate reduction.  The second-moment assumption implies, for \(s,t\in[-\nu_*,\nu_*]\),
\[
 |\varphi_j'(t)|\le E|U_j|\le\sqrt{M_e},
 \qquad
 |\varphi_j'(t)-\varphi_j'(s)|
 \le E[U_j^2]|t-s|\le M_e|t-s|.                              \tag{6}
\]
Piecewise-linear interpolation on a mesh of spacing proportional to \(u^{1/2}\), followed by outward quantization of the complex knot values at precision \(u\), therefore gives an \(O(u)\) uniform enclosure using \(O(u^{-1/2})\) complex coordinates.  Hence the protected-frequency error-transform class has a witness-centered cover of size at most
\[
 \exp\{C u^{-1/2}\log(C/u)\}.                                \tag{7}
\]
Finite atomic witnesses can realize a dense subset of the admissible transform class by the approximation already constructed above.

For the promised positive quadrature implementation, put \(T\asymp\tau_n^{-1/2}\).  Equation (5) makes the tail contribution at most a fixed multiple of \(\tau_n\).  On \([-T,T]\), the vector of moments through order \(2m\) belongs to the convex hull of
\[
 u\longmapsto(1,u,\ldots,u^{2m}).
\]
If a convex representation uses more than \(2m+2\) atoms, affine dependence permits one weight to be decreased to zero without changing the moment vector; iteration leaves at most \(2m+2\) positive atoms.  Taylor's formula encloses the exponential remainder and (5) encloses the tails.  Choose \(m\) according to
\[
 m\asymp T+\log(1/\tau_n)\asymp\tau_n^{-1/2}                 \tag{8}
\]
is enough to make the exponential Taylor remainder uniformly \(O(\tau_n)\) for \(|t|\le\nu_*\) and \(|u|\le T\).  Separately, compact support of \(f_x\) and \(q_x\), together with
\[
 \log(1/\tau_n)\asymp\log n\asymp K_n\log K_n,              \tag{9}
\]
allows their Taylor--Legendre remainders to be controlled with order \(O(K_n)\).  Thus the paired channels have finite outward enclosures involving \(O(K_n^2+\tau_n^{-1/2})\) scalar coefficient, quadrature, tail, and product coordinates.

Compactness from (1)--(3), tightness from (5), and density of the certified witnesses imply that a finite collection of witness-centered boxes covers the model-coordinate image.  For each such box, outward interval arithmetic supplies \([d_b^-,d_b^+]\) containing \(\mathscr D_n(Q)\) for every decoded \(Q\).  Subdivision terminates once
\[
 \operatorname{rad}[d_b^-,d_b^+]\le\tau_n,
 \qquad
 \sup_{a\in I}\operatorname{rad}J_{b,n}(a)\le\zeta_n.       \tag{10}
\]
The witness is part of the finite data of the cell, so its membership is verified rather than inferred by deciding whether an abstract infinite-dimensional decoder is nonempty.

Let \(Q\in\Theta_{n,\alpha}\).  A witness-certified covering cell \(b\) contains its model coordinates.  Since its constraint interval is outward,
\[
 d_b^-\le\mathscr D_n(Q)\le\epsilon_n^2,                    \tag{11}
\]
so this cell passes the retention rule.  Hence \(Q\in\operatorname{Dec}_n(b)\), proving
\[
 \Theta_{n,\alpha}\subseteq\Theta^{\mathrm{cert}}_{n,\alpha}. \tag{12}
\]
Conversely, if \(b\) is retained and \(Q\in\operatorname{Dec}_n(b)\), (10) and the convention that the radius is half the interval length give
\[
 \mathscr D_n(Q)
 \le d_b^+
 \le d_b^-+2\tau_n
 \le\epsilon_n^2+2\tau_n.                                  \tag{13}
\]
The decoder admits only members of \(\mathcal M\), so (12)--(13) prove the two profile inclusions.  Every retained decoder is nonempty because it contains its certified witness.

For endpoint evaluation, model overlap gives \(f_{x,Q}(a)\ge\kappa>0\) for \(a\in I\).  Thus intersection of a denominator enclosure with \([\kappa,\infty)\) retains every true denominator and makes interval division valid.  Inclusion monotonicity of outward interval addition, multiplication, and division yields
\[
 \{\mu_Q(a):Q\in\operatorname{Dec}_n(b)\}
 \subseteq J_{b,n}(a),                                      \tag{14}
\]
and the stopping rule (10) gives \(\operatorname{rad}J_{b,n}(a)\le\zeta_n\).  Notice that (14) concerns the enlarged decoded profile only; no proximity to an exact-profile optimizer is used or claimed.

It remains to count boxes.  Quantizing the \(O(K_n^2)\) latent-transform and product coordinates finely enough for constraint radius \(\tau_n\) costs at most
\[
 \exp\left\{C K_n^2\log(CK_n/\tau_n)\right\}.               \tag{15}
\]
By (7), the two error transforms contribute at most
\[
 \exp\left\{C\tau_n^{-1/2}\log(C/\tau_n)\right\}.           \tag{16}
\]
Applying (3) with \(u=\zeta_n\) to the endpoint remainders costs at most
\[
 \exp\left\{C\zeta_n^{-1/s}\log(C/\zeta_n)\right\}.         \tag{17}
\]
Multiplication of (15)--(17) proves the general cardinality bound.  If \(\beta>3/2\), then \(s>1\), and the definitions of \(K_n\) and \(\zeta_n\) imply
\[
 \zeta_n^{-1/s}\log(C/\zeta_n)
 \le C K_n^2\log(CK_n/\zeta_n),                             \tag{18}
\]
so (15)--(18) give the stated simpler endpoint-coordinate regime, with the separate error-law entropy term retained.  Finally, empirical channels enter the finite interval calculations through finite sums of continuous functions of the observations.  A countable rational enumeration, fixed subdivision order, closed weak inequalities, and deterministic tie breaking make every retained-box indicator measurable; finite minima and maxima of the resulting interval-valued continuous functions are jointly measurable in the sample and \(a\).  This proves termination, finite witness generation, coverage, enlarged-profile maximality, endpoint enclosure, complexity, and measurability.
%%% FIELD proof-honest-upper-band
Let
\[
 E_P=\{\mathscr D_n(P)\le\epsilon_n^2\}.
\]
By the established continuous-channel concentration proposition and the i.i.d. sampling assumption,
\[
 \inf_{P\in\mathcal M}P_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\otimes n}(E_P)
 \ge1-\alpha                                                   \tag{15}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\).  On \(E_P\), the true law is in \(\Theta_{n,\alpha}\), hence in \(\Theta^{\mathrm{cert}}_{n,\alpha}\) by the repaired finite-profile theorem.  It therefore belongs to a retained decoder, and the certified endpoint enclosure places \(\mu_P(a)\) in that box's interval for every \(a\in I\).  Consequently
\[
 \underline m^{\mathrm{cert}}_n(a)
 \le\mu_P(a)\le
 \overline m^{\mathrm{cert}}_n(a),\qquad a\in I.              \tag{16}
\]
Bounded potential outcomes and consistency give \(|\mu_P(a)|\le B_Y\).  Thus clipping and the outward addition of \(\zeta_n\) cannot remove the true curve, and (16) implies
\[
 L_n(a)\le\mu_P(a)\le U_n(a)\quad\text{for every }a\in I. \tag{17}
\]
The joint measurability established by the finite-profile theorem passes through finite minima, maxima, and clipping.  Each retained decoder contains its witness, so the lower certified endpoint does not exceed the upper one; in the empty case both band endpoints are zero.  Hence \(L_n\le U_n\), and (15)--(17) prove \((L_n,U_n)\in\mathfrak B_{n,\alpha}\).

We bound width directly on the enlarged decoded profile.  Regard the square root of \(\mathscr D_n(Q)\) as the norm of the difference between the empirical channel vector and the channel vector of \(Q\) in the Hilbert direct sum used in the definition of \(\mathscr D_n\).  Since \(\tau_n=\epsilon_n^2\), the repaired profile inclusion gives, for \(Q,R\in\Theta^{\mathrm{cert}}_{n,\alpha}\),
\[
 \mathscr D_n(Q)\vee\mathscr D_n(R)
 \le\epsilon_n^2+2\tau_n=3\epsilon_n^2.                     \tag{18}
\]
The Hilbert-space triangle inequality therefore gives
\[
 \left[\sum_x\left\{\int_{\mathcal C_*}
 \left(|F_{x,Q}-F_{x,R}|^2+B_Y^{-2}|G_{x,Q}-G_{x,R}|^2\right)
 +|p_{x,Q}-p_{x,R}|^2\right\}\right]^{1/2}
 \le2\sqrt3\,\epsilon_n.                                    \tag{19}
\]
Apply the established paired protected inverse theorem with \(\delta=2\sqrt3\epsilon_n\).  Because \(\epsilon_n\asymp\sqrt{\log(en/\alpha)/n}\), it yields
\[
 \sup_{Q,R\in\Theta^{\mathrm{cert}}_{n,\alpha}}
 \|\mu_Q-\mu_R\|_{L^\infty(I)}
 \le C\left\{\frac{\log\log(1/\epsilon_n)}
                      {\log(1/\epsilon_n)}\right\}^{\beta-1/2}
 \le C K_n^{1/2-\beta}=C r_n.                               \tag{20}
\]
This is valid for every \(\beta>1/2\); no certificate-complexity restriction is used.

Suppose the certified profile is nonempty.  Let \(b_-\) attain the minimum defining \(\underline m_n^{\mathrm{cert}}(a)\), let \(b_+\) attain the maximum defining \(\overline m_n^{\mathrm{cert}}(a)\), and choose represented laws \(Q_-\in\operatorname{Dec}_n(b_-)\) and \(Q_+\in\operatorname{Dec}_n(b_+)\), which exist by their finite witness certificates.  From inclusion in an interval of radius at most \(\zeta_n\),
\[
 \inf J_{b_-,n}(a)\ge\mu_{Q_-}(a)-2\zeta_n,
 \qquad
 \sup J_{b_+,n}(a)\le\mu_{Q_+}(a)+2\zeta_n.                 \tag{21}
\]
Combining (20)--(21), uniformly in \(a\), gives
\[
 \overline m_n^{\mathrm{cert}}(a)-
 \underline m_n^{\mathrm{cert}}(a)
 \le C r_n+4\zeta_n.                                        \tag{22}
\]
Clipping cannot increase width, and the definition of the band adds at most \(2\zeta_n\).  Since
\[
 \frac{\zeta_n}{r_n}=\frac1{\log(en)}\longrightarrow0,      \tag{23}
\]
(22)--(23) imply \(W_n\le C r_n\) on every sample with nonempty certified profile.  When that profile is empty, \(W_n=0\).  Thus the deterministic width bound implies
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M}
 E_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\otimes n}}[W_n]\le C r_n,
\]
which proves the theorem without an unsupported exact-profile endpoint-proximity assertion.
%%% FIELD proof-minimax-width-frontier
The repaired upper-band theorem supplies one data-driven member of \(\mathfrak B_{n,\alpha}\), so the definition of minimax expected maximal width gives
\[
 \mathfrak R_n
 \le\sup_{P\in\mathcal M}E_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\otimes n}}[W_n]
 \le C r_n.                                                   \tag{24}
\]
For the converse, take an arbitrary \((L_n,U_n)\in\mathfrak B_{n,\alpha}\) and the two laws supplied by the established fixed-nuisance marked-pair theorem.  Define
\[
 A_j=\{L_n(a)\le\mu_{j,n}(a)\le U_n(a)
          \text{ for every }a\in I\},\qquad j\in\{0,1\}.
\]
Uniform honesty gives
\[
 P_{j,n,\mathrm{obs}}^{\otimes n}(A_j)\ge1-\alpha.           \tag{25}
\]
The total-variation inequality \(P(B)\ge Q(B)-\|P-Q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\), applied to \(A_1\), and the marked-pair bound give
\[
 P_{0,n,\mathrm{obs}}^{\otimes n}(A_1)
 \ge1-\alpha-\eta.                                          \tag{26}
\]
By the union bound, (25)--(26) imply
\[
 P_{0,n,\mathrm{obs}}^{\otimes n}(A_0\cap A_1)
 \ge1-2\alpha-\eta>0,                                       \tag{27}
\]
where positivity is exactly the declared restriction \(\eta<1-2\alpha\).  On \(A_0\cap A_1\), both causal curves are contained pointwise in the same band, so
\[
 W_n\ge\|\mu_{1,n}-\mu_{0,n}\|_{L^\infty(I)}\ge c r_n.     \tag{28}
\]
Taking expectation under \(P_{0,n,\mathrm{obs}}^{\otimes n}\), using (27), then taking the supremum over \(\mathcal M\) and infimum over honest bands yields
\[
 \mathfrak R_n\ge c(1-2\alpha-\eta)r_n.                     \tag{29}
\]
The constants \(c_K>0\) and \(\beta>1/2\) are fixed.  Hence
\[
 K_n\asymp\frac{\log n}{\log\log n},
 \qquad
 r_n=K_n^{1/2-\beta}
 \asymp\left\{\frac{\log\log n}{\log n}\right\}^{\beta-1/2}. \tag{30}
\]
Combining (24), (29), and (30) proves both asserted inequalities and the equivalent frontier for the broadened smoothness range.
