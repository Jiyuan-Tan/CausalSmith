\section*{stat\_\allowbreak{}counterfactual\_\allowbreak{}wasserstein\_\allowbreak{}rough\_\allowbreak{}nuisance\_\allowbreak{}frontier}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} The known-uniform-design benchmark isolates a way marginal counterfactual generation can be harder than direct smooth-density estimation: the same rough propensity field is reused across all outcome locations even though the covariate distribution itself is known. The target is marginal-mixture \(W_1\), which is strictly weaker than GANICE's design-averaged statewise extended Wasserstein loss; an explicit smooth separation prevents importing that paper's lower bound or continuous-conditioning upper/lower theory, while convexity preserves a one-way, generally nonsharp upper-bound implication. The project determines the complete minimax expected \(W_1\) frontier over this positive sectional-Hölder class, including critical logarithms and an if-and-only-if oracle boundary. Its negative side uses a shared-sign causal mixture whose marginal transport separation survives covariate averaging; its positive side uses simultaneous finite-order correction of a growing outcome-wavelet dictionary and finite Kantorovich--Rubinstein projection to a discrete probability law. The representative legal tuple \(d=1,m=6,s=4,\alpha=\beta=27/200\) yields the proposed lower exponent \(243/697\), strictly slower than both scalar and oracle comparators. The full asymmetric known-design match, not that isolated bound or a claim about general factual generators, is the headline open content.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Even with the covariate law fixed and known to be uniform, DoubleGen's oracle transfer and first-order nuisance bound do not determine whether rough propensity and treated conditional-density sections enlarge marginal \(W_1\) risk. GANICE develops continuous-conditioning minimax upper/lower theory for design-averaged statewise Wasserstein loss, matched only up to logarithmic and implementation-transfer factors, but a smooth positive pair can have positive statewise-average distance and identical marginal mixtures, so its lower bound and continuous-conditioning upper/lower theory do not settle this target. Direct \(W_1\) density theory removes confounding, fixed counterfactual-density projections do not reconstruct a refining law, and the public ACIC 2023 announcement does not expose a theorem that settles this exact known-design multivariate loss.

\paragraph{Niche.} The missing benchmark is a same-class all-estimator frontier that holds the covariate design fixed and known, thereby isolating how outcome dimension, outcome smoothness, and separate propensity and covariate-sectional smoothness interact through marginal-mixture transport rather than through covariate-density estimation or averaged conditional transport.

\paragraph{Fill.} A shared-propensity multiscale lower construction is matched by a high-rank higher-order coefficient estimator that exploits the known uniform basis geometry and by a finite probability projection. The resulting known-design marginal-mixture theorem provides every non-oracle rate, every critical logarithm, and the necessary-and-sufficient oracle boundary, without claiming a frontier for unknown-design factual generators or importing an extended-loss converse.

\section{Related work}

LuedtkeFukumizu2025DoubleGen is a procedure-side motivation anchor: its Introduction studies direct marginal counterfactual generation, Theorem 2 transfers oracle lower bounds, Theorem 3 gives a conditional total-variation upper bound with a first-order nuisance-product term, and Section 7 asks for a sharper lower bound when nuisances are nonsmooth. For any fixed DoubleGen loss, generator class, and nuisance-map classes for which its boundary and mixed-Lipschitz hypotheses are separately verified, its oracle-transfer and nuisance-product results apply to that specified procedure; this observation neither defines a subfamily of the present model nor asserts that the lower-bound alternatives satisfy those procedure-dependent hypotheses. TamanoImaizumi2026GANICE studies a different causal loss. In arXiv:2605.10206v1, Definition 1 and equations (1)--(2) define extended Wasserstein distance as the target-design average of statewise outcome-space \(W_1\). Theorem 2 gives a logarithmically inflated continuous-conditioning upper bound under an implementation-transfer condition, and Theorem 3 gives the lower bound, so the upper and lower theory is matched only up to logarithmic and transfer factors. The smooth construction in prop:ganice-loss-separation has identical marginal mixtures but positive statewise-average distance, so GANICE's lower bound and continuous-conditioning upper/lower theory do not transfer to this proposal's marginal-mixture \(W_1\); the convexity inequality in that proposition shows that compatible extended-loss upper bounds can still yield generally nonsharp marginal upper bounds. In the primary author version arXiv:1902.01778v4 of NilesWeedBerthet2022, Section 2.2.1, Theorem 1 and its displayed bound on PDF page 6 give the positive-density upper rate over the stated Besov class, while Theorem 3 and its displayed bound on the same page give the corresponding all-estimator lower rate. At transport order \(1\) and density dimension \(m\ge3\), their oracle exponent is \((s+1)/(2s+m)\); prop:oracle-randomized-reduction requires that exponent on the present classical Hölder submodel rather than treating the published Besov formulation as a conditional-density assumption. Section 3, Theorem 4 gives the negative-Besov comparison, and Section 4.1 constructs the wavelet estimate and projects it to a density. In the primary arXiv:2102.12034v1 source corresponding to KennedyBalakrishnanWasserman2023, equation (3) on PDF page 4 defines the same marginal counterfactual density, whereas equation (6) on page 6 fixes a finite-dimensional projection; Theorem 1 with equation (17) on page 13 gives its efficient influence function, and Theorem 3 on page 17 gives the one-step projection rate. These exact locators separate the refining law-valued \(W_1\) target here from their fixed-projection theory. RobinsEtAl2017HOIF supplies the projection-kernel U-statistic architecture and, under its additional projection and nuisance regularity conditions, Corollary 9.1's scalar missing-response rate. LiuEtAl2017EmpiricalHOIF motivates empirical kernels for an unknown covariate density but does not justify the present known-uniform-design assumption; here it informs only the correction architecture. KennedyEtAl2024MinimaxCATE supplies the localized fuzzy-hypothesis and localized higher-order elbow template, but its Hölder conditions concern scalar propensity, regression, and CATE functions rather than conditional-density sections, and its target is pointwise CATE rather than a transported marginal law. KimKimKennedy2018 gives first-order doubly robust density estimation under \(L_q\) loss, and LuedtkeChung2024 gives regularized Hilbert-valued one-step inference; neither addresses this known-design global \(W_1\) frontier. DanceEtAl2026DecFM derives an efficient influence correction for a flow-matching moment and motivates a benchmark consumer, but its broader unknown-design setting is not covered by this theorem. KaddourEtAl2025Survey catalogs causal generative modeling but does not state a nuisance-sensitive known-design marginal Wasserstein frontier. Kennedy2023ACIC is disclosed as priority-relevant but externally unverifiable beyond its public abstract; exact loss, model, rate, and possible subsumption remain a source-verification kill test, and no first counterfactual-density minimax claim is made.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:sample-size]\label{ass:sample-size}
\(n\ge 1\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Positive sample size, \cite{Tsybakov2009}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:covariate-dimension]\label{ass:covariate-dimension}
\(d\ge 1\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Positive Euclidean dimension, \cite{NilesWeedBerthet2022}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:outcome-dimension]\label{ass:outcome-dimension}
\(m\ge 3\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (High-dimensional Wasserstein regime, \cite{NilesWeedBerthet2022}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:propensity-smoothness-index]\label{ass:propensity-smoothness-index}
\(\alpha>0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Positive Hölder smoothness, \cite{KennedyEtAl2024MinimaxCATE}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:covariate-section-smoothness-index]\label{ass:covariate-section-smoothness-index}
\(\beta>0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Positive Hölder smoothness, \cite{KennedyEtAl2024MinimaxCATE}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:outcome-smoothness-index]\label{ass:outcome-smoothness-index}
\(s>0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Positive density smoothness, \cite{NilesWeedBerthet2022}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:radius]\label{ass:radius}
\(L\ge 10\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} L>=10 is a deliberate fixed benchmark normalization, not a condition attributed to NilesWeedBerthet2022. It provides slack for the uniform reference density and the stated smooth perturbation and lower-bound witness families.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:iid-sampling]\label{ass:iid-sampling}
The observations \(O_i=(X_i,A_i,Y_i)\), \(i=1,\ldots,n\), are independent and identically distributed according to \(P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Independent and identically distributed sampling, \cite{KennedyBalakrishnanWasserman2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:known-uniform-design]\label{ass:known-uniform-design}
\(X\sim\operatorname{Unif}(\mathcal X)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Known uniform random-design benchmark, \cite{Tsybakov2009}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:consistency]\label{ass:consistency}
\(Y=Y(A)\) almost surely.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Consistency, \cite{KennedyBalakrishnanWasserman2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:ignorability]\label{ass:ignorability}
\(Y(1)\perp A\mid X\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Conditional exchangeability, \cite{KennedyBalakrishnanWasserman2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:overlap]\label{ass:overlap}
\(1/4\le e(x)\le 3/4\) for every \(x\in\mathcal X\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform overlap, \cite{KennedyBalakrishnanWasserman2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:propensity-holder]\label{ass:propensity-holder}
\(e\in\mathcal H_d^{\alpha}(L)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Hölder-smooth propensity, \cite{KennedyEtAl2024MinimaxCATE}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:treated-normalization]\label{ass:treated-normalization}
\(\int_{\mathcal Y}\eta(y\mid x)\,dy=1\) for every \(x\in\mathcal X\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Conditional-density normalization, \cite{KennedyBalakrishnanWasserman2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:treated-density-bounds]\label{ass:treated-density-bounds}
\(1/2\le\eta(y\mid x)\le3/2\) for every \((y,x)\in\mathcal Y\times\mathcal X\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Uniform positivity and boundedness of the treated conditional-density family is a deliberate, nonstandard primitive condition of this benchmark. It is satisfied by sufficiently small bounded exponential or additive tilts of the uniform density, including the separately smooth families used to model stable outcome support across covariate strata. The condition excludes support degeneration while allowing rough covariate variation and imposes no restriction on the control-arm law.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:x-sectional-holder]\label{ass:x-sectional-holder}
\(x\mapsto\eta(y\mid x)\in\mathcal H_d^{\beta}(L)\) uniformly for \(y\in\mathcal Y\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Uniform-in-\(y\) covariate-sectional Hölder regularity is a deliberate, nonstandard primitive condition of this known-design benchmark. It is satisfied by separately smooth normalized conditional-density families, including bounded finite-dimensional exponential tilts with smooth outcome features and Hölder-smooth coefficient maps in the covariates. Such families model covariates changing the weights of stable outcome features uniformly over a compact outcome support; the benchmark does not require mixed derivative bounds.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:y-sectional-holder]\label{ass:y-sectional-holder}
\(y\mapsto\eta(y\mid x)\in\mathcal H_m^s(L)\) uniformly for \(x\in\mathcal X\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Uniform-in-\(x\) outcome-sectional Hölder regularity is a deliberate, nonstandard primitive condition of this known-design benchmark. It is satisfied by separately smooth normalized conditional-density families, including bounded finite-dimensional exponential tilts whose outcome features are uniformly \(s\)-Hölder and whose covariate-dependent coefficients remain in a compact set. These families describe conditional distributions with uniformly regular outcome shapes across compact covariate strata, without requiring any mixed \((x,y)\)-derivative.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:uniform-control-witness]\label{ass:uniform-control-witness}
The conditional law of \(Y\mid A=0,X=x\) has Lebesgue density \(\eta_0^{\mathrm{w}}(y\mid x)=1\) for every \((y,x)\in\mathcal Y\times\mathcal X\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This condition defines only a nonempty lower-bound witness subfamily, not ambient model regularity. It is realized whenever the control outcome is independent of the covariates and uniform on the compact outcome cube, while the treated conditional density and propensity vary freely subject to the ambient conditions. It neither links arm \(0\) to arm \(1\) nor narrows the upper-bound model.
\end{assumption}

\section{Main results}

\begin{theorem}[thm:interaction-lower-envelope]\label{thm:interaction-lower-envelope}
There are constants \(0<c<C<\infty\), depending only on \(d,m,\alpha,\beta,s,L\), such that for all sufficiently large \(n\), \(R_n(d,m,\alpha,\beta,s,L)\ge c\,\underline r_n(d,m,\alpha,\beta,s,L)-C e^{-cn}\). The alternatives belong to the explicitly named subfamily \(\mathcal M_{0,\mathrm{unif}}(d,m,\alpha,\beta,s,L)\subseteq\mathcal M(d,m,\alpha,\beta,s,L)\), use one propensity sign per covariate cell shared across every outcome cell, and retain both \(a^2b^2\) and \(b^4\) in the full-sample mixture calculation. Uniformity of arm \(0\) is only a testing-witness choice and imposes no restriction linking arm \(0\) to arm \(1\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a feasible quadruple \((h,\delta,a,b)\) in \(\texttt{def:interaction-envelope}\).  We first suppose that \(h=H^{-1}\) and \(\delta=D^{-1}\) for integers \(H,D\); the rounding argument is given at the end.

Choose nonzero functions \(u\in C_c^\infty((0,1)^d)\) and \(v\in C_c^\infty((0,1)^m)\), each bounded in absolute value by one, such that reflection of the first coordinate changes its sign.  In particular,
\[
 \int u=\int v=0,\qquad
 \kappa_u:=\int u^2>0,\qquad \kappa_v:=\int v^2>0.
\]
Partition \(\mathcal X\) into \(K=H^d=h^{-d}\) cubes \(C_i\), and partition \(\mathcal Y\) into \(M=D^m=\delta^{-m}\) cubes \(D_j\).  Let \(u_i\) and \(v_j\) be the translates and rescalings of \(u\) and \(v\) to those cubes, extended by zero.  Their supports are separated from the cell boundaries.  For \(\lambda\in\{-1,1\}^K\) and \(\theta\in\{-1,1\}^M\), define
\[
 U_\lambda(x):=\sum_{i=1}^K\lambda_i u_i(x),\qquad
 V_\theta(y):=\sum_{j=1}^M\theta_jv_j(y),
\]
\[
 e_\lambda(x):={1+aU_\lambda(x)\over2},\qquad
 \eta_{\lambda,\theta}(y\mid x)
 :=1+{bU_\lambda(x)V_\theta(y)\over1+aU_\lambda(x)}.
 \tag{1}
\]
The control density is \(\eta_0^{\mathrm w}=1\), as required by \(\texttt{ass:uniform-control-witness}\).

We verify class membership rather than treating it as part of the construction.  Since the supports are disjoint, \(|U_\lambda|\leq1\) and \(|V_\theta|\leq1\).  Taking the class constant \(c_L\) in \(\texttt{def:interaction-envelope}\) sufficiently small gives \(a+b\leq1/4\).  Hence (1) gives
\[
 {3\over8}\leq e_\lambda\leq {5\over8},\qquad
 1-{b\over1-a}\leq\eta_{\lambda,\theta}
 \leq1+{b\over1-a},
\]
which imply \(\texttt{ass:overlap}\) and \(\texttt{ass:treated-density-bounds}\).  Oddness of \(v\) gives \(\int_{\mathcal Y}V_\theta=0\), so (1) also gives \(\int\eta_{\lambda,\theta}(y\mid x)\,dy=1\), which is \(\texttt{ass:treated-normalization}\).

Let \(t>0\), put \(r=\lceil t\rceil-1\), and let \(w_C\) be a translate of \(w(\cdot/h)\) supported away from the boundary of a cube of width \(h\).  Direct differentiation through order \(r\), followed by the mean-value bound for the order-\(r\) derivatives, gives
\[
 \lVert w_C\rVert_{\mathcal H_d^t}\leq C_t h^{-t}.
 \tag{2}
\]
This also covers integer \(t\), because then the order-\((t-1)\) derivative is Lipschitz with constant at most \(C_t h^{-t}\).  The derivatives of \(z\mapsto z/(1+az)\) are uniformly bounded for \(|z|\leq1\) and \(a\leq1/4\).  Applying (2), the chain rule, and disjointness of supports yields
\[
 \lVert e_\lambda\rVert_{\mathcal H_d^\alpha}
 \leq C(1+ah^{-\alpha}),\qquad
 \sup_y\lVert\eta_{\lambda,\theta}(y\mid\cdot)\rVert_{\mathcal H_d^\beta}
 \leq C(1+bh^{-\beta}),
\]
\[
 \sup_x\lVert\eta_{\lambda,\theta}(\cdot\mid x)\rVert_{\mathcal H_m^s}
 \leq C(1+b\delta^{-s}).
 \tag{3}
\]
The three amplitude restrictions in \(\texttt{def:interaction-envelope}\) make the right sides of (3) at most \(L\), after decreasing the fixed \(c_L\) once.  Thus \(\texttt{ass:propensity-holder}\), \(\texttt{ass:x-sectional-holder}\), and \(\texttt{ass:y-sectional-holder}\) hold.  Notice that the second and third inequalities in (3) are separate; no mixed derivative has been used.

To produce a causal law, draw \(X\) uniformly, draw \(Y(1)\) and \(Y(0)\) conditionally independently given \(X=x\) with respective densities \(\eta_{\lambda,\theta}(\cdot\mid x)\) and one, draw \(A\) conditionally independently of the potential outcomes with probability \(e_\lambda(x)\), and set \(Y=Y(A)\).  This construction verifies \(\texttt{ass:known-uniform-design}\), \(\texttt{ass:ignorability}\), and \(\texttt{ass:consistency}\).  Consequently every resulting law \(P_{\lambda,\theta}\) belongs to \(\mathcal M_{0,\mathrm{unif}}\subseteq\mathcal M\).

We next compute the target.  Pairing each \(z\) with its reflection and using the oddness of \(u\) gives, exactly,
\[
 \int_{C_i}{\lambda_i u_i(x)\over1+a\lambda_i u_i(x)}\,dx
 =-ah^d\kappa(a),\qquad
 \kappa(a):=\int_{(0,1)^d}{u(z)^2\over1-a^2u(z)^2}\,dz.
 \tag{4}
\]
Because \(a\leq1/4\), there are constants \(0<c_u<C_u<\infty\) with \(c_u\leq\kappa(a)\leq C_u\).  Summing (4) over \(K=h^{-d}\) cells and using \(\texttt{def:target-density}\) gives
\[
 q_\theta(y)=1-ab\kappa(a)V_\theta(y),
 \tag{5}
\]
independently of \(\lambda\).

If \(\theta,\theta'\) differ on the index set \(J\), set
\[
 f_{\theta,\theta'}(y)
 :=-c_v\delta\sum_{j\in J}
       \operatorname{sgn}(\theta_j-\theta'_j)v_j(y),
\]
where \(c_v>0\) is small enough that \(f_{\theta,\theta'}\) is one-Lipschitz.  Such a single \(c_v\) works because the supports are disjoint and \(\lVert\nabla(\delta v_j)\rVert_\infty=\lVert\nabla v\rVert_\infty\).  For every coupling \(\gamma\) of \(Q_\theta,Q_{\theta'}\),
\[
 \int f_{\theta,\theta'}\,d(Q_\theta-Q_{\theta'})
 =\int\{f_{\theta,\theta'}(y)-f_{\theta,\theta'}(y')\}\,d\gamma
 \leq\int\lVert y-y'\rVert_2\,d\gamma.
\]
Taking the infimum over \(\gamma\), and then using (5), yields
\[
 W_1(Q_\theta,Q_{\theta'})
 \geq c\delta ab\,{d_H(\theta,\theta')\over M}.
 \tag{6}
\]
Thus the separation is for the marginal law itself.

It remains to bound the testing distance.  Let \(P_0\) have uniform \(X,Y\) and independent \(A\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\).  Conditional on \(X\in C_i\), the exact one-observation likelihood ratio is
\[
 {dP_{\lambda,\theta}\over dP_0}(O)
 =1+\lambda_iS_\theta(O),\qquad
 S_\theta(O):=u_i(X)\{a(2A-1)+bA V_\theta(Y)\}.
 \tag{7}
\]
The formula includes the control-arm term \(-a\lambda_i u_i(X)\).  Also \(E_{0,i}S_\theta=0\).

Mix \(\lambda_i\) uniformly.  Given \(N\) observations in cell \(i\), the mixture likelihood is
\[
 L_{\theta,N}=\frac12\left\{\prod_{\ell=1}^N(1+S_\theta(O_\ell))
                      +\prod_{\ell=1}^N(1-S_\theta(O_\ell))\right\}.
 \tag{8}
\]
For neighboring \(\theta,\theta'\), define \(z=E_{0,i}S_\theta^2\) and \(z'=E_{0,i}S_\theta S_{\theta'}\).  Direct use of \(E A=1/2\), \(\int V_\theta=0\), and disjoint outcome supports gives
\[
 z=\kappa_u\left(a^2+{b^2\kappa_v\over2}\right),\qquad
 z-z'=\kappa_u\kappa_v b^2\delta^m,\qquad |z'|\leq z.
 \tag{9}
\]
Expanding (8) over even subsets, products indexed by distinct subsets are orthogonal because \(E_{0,i}S_\theta=0\).  Therefore
\[
 E_{0,i}(L_{\theta,N}-L_{\theta',N})^2
 =2\sum_{\substack{2\leq k\leq N\\ k\ \mathrm{even}}}{N\choose k}(z^k-(z')^k).
 \tag{10}
\]
Since \(|z'|\leq z\),
\[
 0\leq z^k-(z')^k\leq k(z-z')z^{k-1},
\]
and summing the binomial derivative from \(k=2\) upward gives
\[
 E_{0,i}(L_{\theta,N}-L_{\theta',N})^2
 \leq C N(N-1)b^2\delta^m(a^2+b^2)
          \{1+C(a^2+b^2)\}^{N}.
 \tag{11}
\]
The factor \(b^2(a^2+b^2)=a^2b^2+b^4\) retains both interaction terms.

Because \(|S_\theta|\leq a+b<1\), arithmetic--geometric mean in (8) gives
\[
 L_{\theta,N}\geq
 \prod_{\ell=1}^N\sqrt{1-S_\theta(O_\ell)^2}
 \geq\{1-(a+b)^2\}^{N/2}.
 \tag{12}
\]
Using \((\sqrt x-\sqrt y)^2=(x-y)^2/(\sqrt x+\sqrt y)^2\), (11), and (12), the squared Hellinger distance between the two conditional cell mixtures is at most
\[
 C N(N-1)b^2\delta^m(a^2+b^2)
       \exp\{CN(a^2+b^2)\}.
 \tag{13}
\]

Now Poissonize with total mean \(2n\).  Counts in the \(K\) covariate cells are independent Poisson variables with mean \(t=2nh^d\).  For every \(R>0\),
\[
 E\{N(N-1)R^N\}=t^2R^2e^{t(R-1)}.
\]
The constraint \(nh^d\leq c_L\) and the smallness of \(a+b\) thus turn (13) into the per-cell bound
\[
 C( nh^d)^2b^2\delta^m(a^2+b^2).
\]
Squared Hellinger distance tensorizes subadditively over product experiments.  Mixing the \(K\) propensity signs independently and denoting the resulting Poissonized law by \(\overline{\mathbb P}_\theta\), we obtain
\[
 H^2(\overline{\mathbb P}_\theta,
     \overline{\mathbb P}_{\theta'})
 \leq Cn^2h^d\delta^m b^2(a^2+b^2)
 \leq Cc_L
 \tag{14}
\]
for every neighboring pair.  Choose \(c_L\) so that the last quantity is at most \(1/16\).  Since total variation is at most Hellinger distance, every neighboring total variation distance is at most \(1/4\).

For completeness, the testing reduction is elementary.  Given any probability-valued estimator \(\widehat Q\), let \(\widehat\theta\) be a deterministically tie-broken minimizer of \(W_1(\widehat Q,Q_\vartheta)\) over the finite hypercube.  The triangle inequality, the minimizing property, and (6) imply
\[
 W_1(\widehat Q,Q_\theta)
 \geq {1\over2}W_1(Q_{\widehat\theta},Q_\theta)
 \geq c\delta ab\,{d_H(\widehat\theta,\theta)\over M}.
 \tag{15}
\]
Pairing \(\theta\) with the vertex obtained by flipping coordinate \(j\), the elementary two-testing inequality gives
\[
 2^{-M}\sum_\theta
 \overline{\mathbb P}_\theta(\widehat\theta_j\neq\theta_j)
 \geq 2^{-M}\sum_{\theta:\,\theta_j=1}
       \{1-\operatorname{TV}(\overline{\mathbb P}_\theta,
                  \overline{\mathbb P}_{\theta^{(j)}})\}
 \geq {3\over8}.
\]
Summing over \(j\) in (15) proves Poissonized Bayes risk at least \(c\delta ab\).  Since each mixture component belongs to the named witness subfamily and has the same target for every mixed \(\lambda\), its Bayes risk is bounded above by the supremum risk over \(\mathcal M_{0,\mathrm{unif}}\).

To de-Poissonize, take any estimator based on exactly \(n\) observations and apply it to the first \(n\) points of a Poisson experiment of mean \(2n\) when at least \(n\) points occur, using an arbitrary fixed probability measure otherwise.  Since \(W_1\leq\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal Y)=\sqrt m\), its Poissonized Bayes risk is at most its fixed-\(n\) Bayes risk plus \(\sqrt m\,P\{\operatorname{Pois}(2n)<n\}\).  Markov's inequality applied to \(e^{-tN}\), with \(t=\log2\), gives
\[
 P\{\operatorname{Pois}(2n)<n\}
 \leq\exp\{n\log2-n\} \leq e^{-cn}.
\]
It follows that
\[
 R_n(d,m,\alpha,\beta,s,L)\geq c\delta ab-Ce^{-cn}.
 \tag{16}
\]

Finally, for arbitrary feasible \(h,\delta\), put \(\bar h=1/\lceil h^{-1}\rceil\) and \(\bar\delta=1/\lceil\delta^{-1}\rceil\), so \(h/2\leq\bar h\leq h\) and \(\delta/2\leq\bar\delta\leq\delta\).  Put \(\bar a=2^{-\alpha}a\) and \(\bar b=2^{-\max(\beta,s)}b\).  The barred quadruple remains feasible, after the single fixed choice of sufficiently small \(c_L\), and
\[
 \bar\delta\bar a\bar b
 \geq2^{-1-\alpha-\max(\beta,s)}\delta ab.
\]
Apply (16) to the barred quadruple and take the supremum over the defining feasible set in \(\texttt{def:interaction-envelope}\).  This proves the asserted bound, with constants depending only on \(d,m,\alpha,\beta,s,L\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the first same-class obstruction that can make marginal transport strictly harder than both direct density estimation and a scalar missing-response functional. \textit{Gap.} LuedtkeFukumizu2025DoubleGen imports an oracle lower bound and explicitly says it may be loose for nonsmooth nuisances; even after fixing the covariate law to be known uniform, that paper gives no difficulty-sensitive transport converse, while KennedyEtAl2024MinimaxCATE has a scalar or pointwise fuzzy hypothesis rather than separation for an entire marginal law. \textit{Consumer.} The result gives DoubleGen and DecFM a known-uniform-design stress-test benchmark showing when generator capacity cannot remove observational indistinguishability caused by rough shared nuisances even before unknown-design difficulty is introduced.

\begin{proposition}[prop:representative-nonoracle]\label{prop:representative-nonoracle}
At \(d=1\), \(m=6\), \(s=4\), and \(\alpha=\beta=27/200\), the interaction envelope gives \(R_n\gtrsim n^{-243/697}\); moreover \(243/697<27/77<5/14\), so this legal subfamily is slower than both the scalar smooth-functional benchmark \(n^{-27/77}\) and the oracle \(W_1\) benchmark \(n^{-5/14}\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use the feasible choices, up to fixed sufficiently small amplitude constants,
\[
 h=n^{-800/697},\qquad \delta=n^{-27/697},\qquad
 a=h^{27/200}=n^{-108/697},\qquad
 b=h^{27/200}=\delta^4=n^{-108/697}.
\]
They obey \(nh=n^{-103/697}\leq c_L\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Moreover,
\[
 n^2h\delta^6b^2(a^2+b^2)
 \asymp n^{,2-(800+162+432)/697}=1,
\]
and the fixed amplitude constants make this quantity at most \(c_L\).  Hence \(\texttt{thm:interaction-lower-envelope}\) and the definition of the envelope give
\[
 R_n\gtrsim \delta ab=n^{-(27+108+108)/697}=n^{-243/697};
\]
the exponentially small subtraction is negligible.  Finally,
\[
 {27\over77}-{243\over697}={108\over53669}>0,
 \qquad
 {5\over14}-{243\over697}={83\over9758}>0.
\]
Since \(r_{\mathrm{or},n}(6,4)=n^{-5/14}\) by \(\texttt{def:oracle-rate}\), these identities prove every asserted strict exponent comparison.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} A concrete strict exponent separation prevents the proposal from collapsing to a generic maximum of two published rates. \textit{Gap.} RobinsEtAl2017HOIF supplies the scalar exponent and NilesWeedBerthet2022 supplies the oracle exponent, but neither likelihood construction contains the shared propensity across a multivariate outcome packing. \textit{Consumer.} This tuple is an early known-design benchmark for testing finite-order correction code before solving the full asymmetric phase diagram.

\begin{lemma}[lem:kr-probability-projection]\label{lem:kr-probability-projection}
Let \(\widetilde Q\) be any finite signed unit-mass measure on \(\mathcal Y\), let \(G_{\varepsilon}\subset\mathcal Y\) be a finite grid of mesh at most \(\varepsilon\), and let \(\Pi_{\varepsilon}(\widetilde Q)\) minimize the Kantorovich--Rubinstein distance from \(\widetilde Q\) over probability measures supported on \(G_{\varepsilon}\).  Then a minimizer exists and, for every probability law \(\mu\) on \(\mathcal Y\),
\[
 W_1\{\Pi_{\varepsilon}(\widetilde Q),\mu\}
 \leq2\lVert\widetilde Q-\mu\rVert_{\mathrm{KR}}+C_m\varepsilon.
\]
Its probability masses define an executable categorical sampler.  If \(\widetilde Q\) is finitely supported with explicitly given atoms and weights, in particular after a finite-grid quantization, a finite linear program with deterministic tie-breaking computes the projection.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The probability simplex on the finite set \(G_\varepsilon\) is compact.  The map from its mass vector \(p\) to \(\lVert\sum_{g\in G_\varepsilon}p_g\delta_g-\widetilde Q\rVert_{\mathrm{KR}}\) is continuous, because changing \(p\) by \(p'\) changes this norm by at most \(\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal Y)\lVert p-p'\rVert_1\).  Thus a minimizer exists.

Choose a Borel nearest-grid map \(T:\mathcal Y\to G_\varepsilon\), with deterministic tie-breaking, and set \(\mu_\varepsilon=T_\#\mu\).  The coupling \((\operatorname{id},T)_\#\mu\) and the mesh condition give
\[
 W_1(\mu,\mu_\varepsilon)\leq C_m\varepsilon.
 \tag{17}
\]
By \(\texttt{lem:kantorovich-rubinstein-duality}\), the Kantorovich--Rubinstein norm agrees with \(W_1\) on differences of probability measures.  The minimizing property, the triangle inequality for that norm, and (17) therefore give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 W_1\{\Pi_\varepsilon(\widetilde Q),\mu\}
 &=\lVert\Pi_\varepsilon(\widetilde Q)-\mu\rVert_{\mathrm{KR}}\\
 &\leq\lVert\Pi_\varepsilon(\widetilde Q)-\widetilde Q\rVert_{\mathrm{KR}}
       +\lVert\widetilde Q-\mu\rVert_{\mathrm{KR}}\\
 &\leq\lVert\mu_\varepsilon-\widetilde Q\rVert_{\mathrm{KR}}
       +\lVert\widetilde Q-\mu\rVert_{\mathrm{KR}}\\
 &\leq2\lVert\widetilde Q-\mu\rVert_{\mathrm{KR}}+C_m\varepsilon.
\end{aligned}
\]

If \(\widetilde Q\) has finite support, the union of that support and \(G_\varepsilon\) is finite.  On this union the dual norm is the maximum of a linear functional over the finitely many constraints \(|f(z)-f(z')|\leq\lVert z-z'\rVert_2\), after fixing one potential value to zero.  Finite-dimensional linear-programming duality converts minimization of this polyhedral convex objective over the probability simplex into a finite linear program.  Successive lexicographic minimization of the coordinates gives deterministic tie-breaking.  The resulting nonnegative masses sum to one, so drawing a grid point with those masses is the stated categorical sampler.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Higher-order coefficient correction can be signed, so an order-preserving projection theorem is needed to deliver an actual generative distribution. \textit{Gap.} NilesWeedBerthet2022 projects direct-sample wavelet estimates to densities or discrete measures; the present statement isolates a finite-grid signed-input oracle inequality suitable after causal higher-order correction. \textit{Consumer.} The known-design reconstruction benchmark can return probability masses and sample categorically without clipping away multiscale cancellation.

\begin{proposition}[prop:oracle-randomized-reduction]\label{prop:oracle-randomized-reduction}
On the submodel \(e(x)=1/2\) and \(\eta(y\mid x)=q_1(y)\), the experiment contains an independent Bernoulli half-sample from \(Q_1\), and the minimax expected \(W_1\) risk over the corresponding positive Hölder-density benchmark is of order \(r_{\mathrm{or},n}(m,s)\) for \(m\ge3\). Thus every claimed frontier must reproduce the Niles-Weed--Berthet direct-sample rate exponent when confounding and covariate variation vanish.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let \(\mathcal F_{m,s,L}\) be the set of densities \(q\) on \(\mathcal Y\) satisfying \(1/2\leq q\leq3/2\), \(\int q=1\), and \(q\in\mathcal H_m^s(L)\).  For \(q\in\mathcal F_{m,s,L}\), set \(e\equiv1/2\), \(\eta(y\mid x)=q(y)\), and use the uniform control density.  Constants belong to every declared Hölder ball because \(L\geq10\), and all sectional, normalization, density-bound, overlap, consistency, and ignorability assumptions in \(\texttt{def:model-class}\) hold.  This is therefore a submodel of \(\mathcal M\), and \(\texttt{def:target-density}\) gives \(q_1=q\).

In this submodel, \(A_i\) are independent \(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\) variables, independent of \(X_i\), and, conditional on the treatment labels, the outcomes with \(A_i=1\) are independent draws from \(Q_1\).  The remaining observed variables have a fixed law not depending on \(q\).  This proves the asserted Bernoulli-half-sample description.

We first prove the upper bound.  Choose compactly supported boundary wavelets of regularity strictly greater than \(s\), as in \(\texttt{lem:niles-weed-berthet-bounded-upper}\).  If \(\psi_{jk}\) is an \(L_2\)-normalized wavelet at level \(j\), Taylor expansion through order \(r=\lceil s\rceil-1\), its vanishing moments, and the order-\(r\) Hölder condition give
\[
 |\langle q,\psi_{jk}\rangle|
 \leq C L,2^{-j(s+m/2)}.
\]
There are at most \(C2^{jm}\) wavelets at that level, and hence
\[
 2^{js}\left(\sum_k|\langle q,\psi_{jk}\rangle|^2\right)^{1/2}
 \leq CL.
 \tag{18}
\]
Thus the classical Hölder ball is contained in the radius-\(CL\) Besov class with integrability index two and summability index infinity used by the cited theorem.  This calculation also covers integer \(s\), because the convention in \(\mathcal H_m^s(L)\) makes the derivative of order \(s-1\) Lipschitz.  Since \(q\geq1/2\), \(\texttt{lem:niles-weed-berthet-bounded-upper}\), with \(p'=2\), yields a direct-sample probability estimator with risk at most
\[
 Ck^{-(s+1)/(2s+m)}
 \tag{19}
\]
from \(k\) treated observations.

Let \(N_1=\sum_iA_i\) and \(k=\lfloor n/4\rfloor\).  On \(N_1\geq k\), apply (19) to the first \(k\) treated outcomes, and otherwise output a fixed probability law.  A direct exponential-moment bound for the binomial variable gives \(P(N_1<k)\leq e^{-cn}\).  Since \(W_1\leq\sqrt m\),
\[
 \sup_{q\in\mathcal F_{m,s,L}}E_qW_1(\widehat Q,Q_1)
 \leq Cn^{-(s+1)/(2s+m)}+\sqrt m e^{-cn}
 \lesssim r_{\mathrm{or},n}(m,s).
 \tag{20}
\]

For the converse, we give a same-Hölder-class construction.  Let \(w\in C_c^\infty((0,1)^m)\) be nonzero, have integral zero, and be bounded by one.  At a reciprocal-integer scale \(\delta\), place disjoint rescaled copies \(w_j\) in the \(M=\delta^{-m}\) outcome cubes and put
\[
 q_\theta(y)=1+c_0\delta^s\sum_{j=1}^M\theta_jw_j(y),
 \qquad \theta\in\{-1,1\}^M.
 \tag{21}
\]
For a sufficiently small fixed \(c_0=c_0(m,s,L)\), scaling exactly as in (2) shows that every density in (21) belongs to \(\mathcal F_{m,s,L}\).  Neighboring one-observation laws satisfy
\[
 H^2(Q_\theta,Q_{\theta^{(j)}})
 \leq C\int(q_\theta-q_{\theta^{(j)}})^2
 \leq Cc_0^2\delta^{2s+m},
\]
where the first inequality uses \(q_\theta,q_{\theta^{(j)}}\geq1/2\).  Consequently their \(n\)-fold squared Hellinger distance is at most \(Cnc_0^2\delta^{2s+m}\).  Taking \(\delta\asymp n^{-1/(2s+m)}\) and decreasing \(c_0\) makes every neighboring total variation distance at most \(1/4\).  The one-Lipschitz critics \(c\delta\sum_j\sigma_jw_j\), with signs chosen according to the changed coordinates, give
\[
 W_1(Q_\theta,Q_{\theta'})
 \geq c\delta^{s+1}{d_H(\theta,\theta')\over M}.
\]
The pairing argument in (15) therefore proves direct-sample minimax risk at least
\[
 c\delta^{s+1}=c n^{-(s+1)/(2s+m)}.
 \tag{22}
\]

Finally, the direct experiment with \(n\) draws from \(q\) can simulate the causal experiment: generate independent treatment labels and covariates; use the corresponding direct draws for treated outcomes and independent uniform draws for control outcomes.  Hence the direct experiment is at least as informative, so (22) is also a lower bound for the causal submodel.  Combining (20), (22), and \(\texttt{def:oracle-rate}\) proves the proposition.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This reduction is the mandatory sanity check for the phase diagram and pins the baseline that rough nuisances may or may not exceed. \textit{Gap.} NilesWeedBerthet2022 proves the direct-sample rate over positive Besov density classes; the proposition requires the same exponent on the present classical Hölder benchmark and embeds that direct experiment as a randomized no-covariate-variation submodel. \textit{Consumer.} It certifies that the known-design correction does not lose rate when the benchmark experiment becomes an oracle generation problem.

\begin{proposition}[prop:ganice-loss-separation]\label{prop:ganice-loss-separation}
There exists \(\varepsilon_0=\varepsilon_0(d,m,\alpha,\beta,s,L)>0\) such that, for every \(0<\varepsilon_{\mathrm{sep}}\le\varepsilon_0\), the functions \(g_{\mathrm{sep}}(x)=\cos(2\pi x_1)\), \(h_{\mathrm{sep}}(y)=\cos(2\pi y_1)\), and \(\eta_{\pm}(y\mid x)=1\pm\varepsilon_{\mathrm{sep}}g_{\mathrm{sep}}(x)h_{\mathrm{sep}}(y)\) are normalized positive smooth conditional densities. With \(e\equiv1/2\) and the uniform arm-\(0\) witness, they induce two laws in \(\mathcal M_{0,\mathrm{unif}}(d,m,\alpha,\beta,s,L)\). Because \(\int_{\mathcal X}g_{\mathrm{sep}}(x)\,dx=0\), \(Q_+=Q_-=\operatorname{Unif}(\mathcal Y)\) and \(W_1(Q_+,Q_-)=0\), whereas \(W_1\{\eta_+(y\mid x)\,dy,\eta_-(y\mid x)\,dy\}=2\varepsilon_{\mathrm{sep}}|g_{\mathrm{sep}}(x)|/\pi^2\) and \(\int_{\mathcal X}W_1\{\eta_+(y\mid x)\,dy,\eta_-(y\mid x)\,dy\}\,dx=4\varepsilon_{\mathrm{sep}}/\pi^3>0\). More generally, for any measurable probability kernels \(\mu_x\) and \(\nu_x\), \[W_1\!\left(\int_{\mathcal X}\mu_x\,dx,\int_{\mathcal X}\nu_x\,dx\right)\le\int_{\mathcal X}W_1(\mu_x,\nu_x)\,dx.\] Consequently, GANICE's extended-loss lower bound and its continuous-conditioning minimax upper/lower theory, which is matched only up to logarithmic and implementation-transfer factors, do not transfer as a marginal-mixture \(W_1\) frontier. Compatible extended-loss upper bounds can nevertheless imply generally nonsharp marginal upper bounds through this convexity inequality.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write \(r_t=\lceil t\rceil-1\) and \(\gamma_t=t-r_t\in(0,1]\), as in the three frozen H\"older-ball definitions.  For a smooth function \(f\) on a cube, let
\[
N_{k,t}(f):=1\vee\max_{|\kappa|\le r_t}\|\partial^\kappa f\|_\infty
 \vee\max_{|\kappa|=r_t}[\partial^\kappa f]_{\gamma_t}.
\]
The two finite constants \(C_\beta:=N_{d,\beta}(g_{\mathrm{sep}})\) and \(C_s:=N_{m,s}(h_{\mathrm{sep}})\) are finite because cosine is smooth on a compact cube.  Set
\[
\varepsilon_0:=\min\left\{\frac12,\frac{L-1}{C_\beta},\frac{L-1}{C_s}\right\}>0.
\]
Positivity follows from \(L\ge10\), which is \(\texttt{ass:radius}\), and finiteness of \(C_\beta,C_s\).  This choice is permitted to be viewed as a function of all six fixed class inputs even though it does not vary effectively with some of them.

Fix \(0<\varepsilon_{\mathrm{sep}}\le\varepsilon_0\).  Since \(|g_{\mathrm{sep}}|\le1\) and \(|h_{\mathrm{sep}}|\le1\),
\[
\frac12\le 1-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{sep}}
 \le \eta_\pm(y\mid x)
 \le 1+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{sep}}\le\frac32.
\]
Thus \(\texttt{ass:treated-density-bounds}\) holds.  Moreover,
\[
\int_{\mathcal Y}h_{\mathrm{sep}}(y)\,dy
=\left\{\int_0^1\cos(2\pi u)\,du\right\}
 \prod_{j=2}^m\int_0^1dy_j=0,
\]
so \(\int_{\mathcal Y}\eta_\pm(y\mid x)\,dy=1\) for every \(x\), proving \(\texttt{ass:treated-normalization}\).  For fixed \(y\), each derivative and terminal H\"older seminorm in \(x\) is bounded by the corresponding quantity for the constant function plus \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{sep}}|h_{\mathrm{sep}}(y)|C_\beta\).  Hence its maximum is at most \(1+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{sep}}C_\beta\le L\), proving \(\texttt{ass:x-sectional-holder}\).  The identical argument with \(x,y,\beta,C_\beta\) replaced by \(y,x,s,C_s\) proves \(\texttt{ass:y-sectional-holder}\).  Finally, \(e(x)=1/2\) lies in \([1/4,3/4]\), proving \(\texttt{ass:overlap}\), and every positive-order derivative and H\"older seminorm of \(e\) vanishes while \(\|e\|_\infty=1/2\le L\); hence \(\texttt{ass:propensity-holder}\) holds for every \(\alpha>0\).

It remains to check that these analytic objects induce causal laws in the stated class, rather than merely observed conditional densities.  For \(\sigma\in\{+1,-1\}\), define a joint law of \((X,A,Y(0),Y(1))\), relative to Lebesgue measure in \(x,y_0,y_1\) and counting measure in \(a\), by
\[
dP_\sigma(x,a,y_0,y_1)
=dx\,\frac12\,dy_0\,\eta_\sigma(y_1\mid x)\,dy_1,
\qquad a\in\{0,1\},
\]
and set \(Y=Y(A)\).  The preceding normalization and the unit volumes of \(\mathcal X\) and \(\mathcal Y\) show that this is a probability law.  Its covariate marginal is uniform, as required by \(\texttt{ass:known-uniform-design}\); conditionally on \(X\), \(A\) is Bernoulli \(1/2\) and independent of \((Y(0),Y(1))\), so \(Y(1)\perp A\mid X\), as required by \(\texttt{ass:ignorability}\); and the definition of \(Y\) gives \(\texttt{ass:consistency}\).  The observed arm-\(1\) density is \(\eta_\sigma\), whereas the observed arm-\(0\) density is identically one, which is \(\texttt{ass:uniform-control-witness}\).  All remaining index and support conditions are the frozen assumptions.  Therefore \(\texttt{def:model-class}\) and \(\texttt{def:uniform-control-witness-subfamily}\) imply
\[
P_+,P_-\in\mathcal M_{0,\mathrm{unif}}(d,m,\alpha,\beta,s,L).
\]

We next compute the marginal target laws.  Fubini's theorem applies because the displayed densities are bounded.  Since
\[
\int_{\mathcal X}g_{\mathrm{sep}}(x)\,dx
=\left\{\int_0^1\cos(2\pi u)\,du\right\}
 \prod_{j=2}^d\int_0^1dx_j=0,
\]
the density of either marginal is
\[
q_\sigma(y)=\int_{\mathcal X}\eta_\sigma(y\mid x)\,dx
=1+\sigma\varepsilon_{\mathrm{sep}}h_{\mathrm{sep}}(y)
 \int_{\mathcal X}g_{\mathrm{sep}}(x)\,dx=1.
\]
Thus \(Q_+=Q_-=\operatorname{Unif}(\mathcal Y)\).  The diagonal coupling of this law with itself has zero cost, and nonnegativity of the cost in \(\texttt{def:wasserstein-loss}\) therefore gives \(W_1(Q_+,Q_-)=0\).

For the statewise distance, fix \(x\) and put \(c=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{sep}}g_{\mathrm{sep}}(x)\), so \(|c|\le1/2\).  Let \(\lambda_{m-1}\) denote the uniform probability law on \([0,1]^{m-1}\), and let \(\rho_c\) be the probability law on \([0,1]\) with density
\[
p_c(t)=1+c\cos(2\pi t).
\]
The two conditional laws factor as \(\rho_c\otimes\lambda_{m-1}\) and \(\rho_{-c}\otimes\lambda_{m-1}\).  We first calculate their one-dimensional transport distance directly from the primal definition.  Their distribution functions satisfy
\[
D_c(t):=F_c(t)-F_{-c}(t)=\frac{c}{\pi}\sin(2\pi t),
\qquad D_c(0)=D_c(1)=0.
\]
Because \(p_{\pm c}\ge1/2\), the two distribution functions are continuous and strictly increasing.  If \(U\sim\operatorname{Unif}[0,1]\), the pair \((F_c^{-1}(U),F_{-c}^{-1}(U))\) is a coupling.  For every \(t\), the two events that its coordinates do not exceed \(t\) are respectively \(\{U\le F_c(t)\}\) and \(\{U\le F_{-c}(t)\}\).  Applying Tonelli's theorem to the elementary layer-cake identity for the distance between two points of \([0,1]\) gives
\[
\mathbb E\bigl|F_c^{-1}(U)-F_{-c}^{-1}(U)\bigr|
=\int_0^1|F_c(t)-F_{-c}(t)|\,dt
=\int_0^1|D_c(t)|\,dt.
\]
This is an upper bound for the one-dimensional primal transport infimum.  For the reverse inequality define the absolutely continuous function
\[
\phi_c(t):=-\int_0^t\operatorname{sgn}\{D_c(u)\}\,du.
\]
It is one-Lipschitz.  For every coupling \(\gamma\) of \(\rho_c\) and \(\rho_{-c}\), the Lipschitz inequality and integration by parts, with the displayed zero boundary values, give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int|u-v|\,d\gamma(u,v)
&\ge \int\{\phi_c(u)-\phi_c(v)\}\,d\gamma(u,v)\\
&=\int_0^1\phi_c(t)D_c'(t)\,dt
=-\int_0^1\phi_c'(t)D_c(t)\,dt
=\int_0^1|D_c(t)|\,dt.
\end{aligned}
\]
Taking the infimum over \(\gamma\) matches the quantile-coupling upper bound.  Consequently,
\[
W_1(\rho_c,\rho_{-c})
=\frac{|c|}{\pi}\int_0^1|\sin(2\pi t)|\,dt
=\frac{2|c|}{\pi^2}.
\]
For every coupling of the two \(m\)-dimensional product laws, projection onto the first coordinate produces a coupling of \(\rho_c,\rho_{-c}\), and \(|y_1-y_1'|\le\|y-y'\|_2\); hence the product-law distance is at least the last display.  Conversely, adjoining one common independent draw from \(\lambda_{m-1}\) to the quantile coupling is an \(m\)-dimensional coupling with exactly the same cost.  Thus
\[
W_1\{\eta_+(y\mid x)\,dy,\eta_-(y\mid x)\,dy\}
=\frac{2\varepsilon_{\mathrm{sep}}|g_{\mathrm{sep}}(x)|}{\pi^2}.
\]
Since the other \(d-1\) coordinates have unit volume and \(\int_0^1|\cos(2\pi u)|\,du=2/\pi\), integration over \(x\) yields
\[
\int_{\mathcal X}W_1\{\eta_+(y\mid x)\,dy,\eta_-(y\mid x)\,dy\}\,dx
=\frac{2\varepsilon_{\mathrm{sep}}}{\pi^2}\frac{2}{\pi}
=\frac{4\varepsilon_{\mathrm{sep}}}{\pi^3}>0.
\]

Finally let \(x\mapsto\mu_x\) and \(x\mapsto\nu_x\) be measurable probability kernels, and define their mixtures by \(\bar\mu(B)=\int_{\mathcal X}\mu_x(B)\,dx\) and \(\bar\nu(B)=\int_{\mathcal X}\nu_x(B)\,dx\).  These are probability laws because \(\mathcal X\) has unit volume.  The Kantorovich--Rubinstein formula in \(\texttt{lem:kantorovich-rubinstein-duality-cube}\), followed by Fubini's theorem for bounded Lipschitz functions, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
W_1(\bar\mu,\bar\nu)
&=\sup_{\operatorname{Lip}(f)\le1}
  \int_{\mathcal X}\left\{\int_{\mathcal Y}f\,d\mu_x-
  \int_{\mathcal Y}f\,d\nu_x\right\}dx\\
&\le\int_{\mathcal X}\sup_{\operatorname{Lip}(f)\le1}
 \left\{\int_{\mathcal Y}f\,d\mu_x-
 \int_{\mathcal Y}f\,d\nu_x\right\}dx\\
&=\int_{\mathcal X}W_1(\mu_x,\nu_x)\,dx.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last integrand is measurable and bounded by \(\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal Y)=\sqrt m\): after anchoring each critic at the origin, a countable uniformly dense family of one-Lipschitz functions is obtained from rational grids and rational function values, and the cited dual formula writes the integrand as the supremum of the corresponding countable family of measurable kernel integrals.  Thus every integral and interchange in the display is well defined.

By \(\texttt{lem:ganice-extended-loss-is-statewise-average}\), GANICE's extended loss for the constructed conditional pair equals the strictly positive statewise average \(4\varepsilon_{\mathrm{sep}}/\pi^3\), although the ordinary Wasserstein distance between the marginal mixtures is zero.  Hence no positive lower comparison from extended loss to marginal-mixture loss holds uniformly, so an extended-loss lower bound cannot by itself yield a marginal-mixture lower bound.  The published scope recorded in \(\texttt{lem:ganice-continuous-frontier-scope}\) therefore does not transfer as a marginal-mixture frontier.  In the other direction, the proved convexity inequality shows that every compatible extended-loss upper bound is also a marginal-mixture upper bound.  The equality-versus-strict-positivity witness shows why that implication can be nonsharp.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The smooth equal-marginal separation prevents design-averaged conditional transport theory from being mistaken for the weaker marginal-mixture transport problem while recording the valid one-way upper-bound implication. \textit{Gap.} TamanoImaizumi2026GANICE Definition 1 and equations (1)--(2) define extended Wasserstein loss as design-averaged statewise \(W_1\); Theorem 2 gives a logarithmically inflated upper bound under an implementation-transfer condition, and Theorem 3 gives the continuous-conditioning lower bound. The proposition shows that its loss can stay positive when the present marginal \(W_1\) is exactly zero. \textit{Consumer.} A referee or practitioner comparing GANICE with the known-design marginal reconstruction benchmark can distinguish its nontransferable extended-loss lower bound and continuous-conditioning upper/lower theory from the valid, generally nonsharp convexity-based marginal upper implication.

\section{Honest scope}

The target is the marginal law of \(Y(1)\), not design-averaged statewise Wasserstein loss, an individual-effect law, or inference for a nonsmooth scalar Wasserstein functional. The covariate law is fixed and known to be uniform, so the theorem is a known-design benchmark and does not claim the frontier for general factual generators with an unknown covariate law. DoubleGen comparisons are procedure-side and require separate verification of its loss-, generator-, and nuisance-class hypotheses; no DoubleGen law subfamily is defined here. GANICE's extended-loss lower bound and continuous-conditioning minimax upper/lower theory do not transfer to marginal-mixture \(W_1\), although compatible upper bounds may transfer nonsharply by convexity.
