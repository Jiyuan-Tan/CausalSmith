On the canonical boundary chart, \(X\sim\operatorname{Unif}[0,1]\), \(d_1(x)=x\), and \(d_0(x)=1-x\).
%%% FIELD ass-boundary-link-condition
With \(\alpha_a=(\gamma_a-1)^{-1}\) when \(\gamma_a<\infty\) and \(\alpha_a=0\) when \(\gamma_a=\infty\), the binary propensity obeys, \(P_X\)-almost surely,
\[
 \eta(x)=\log\frac{\pi_1(x)}{\pi_0(x)}
 =\alpha_1\log d_1(x)-\alpha_0\log d_0(x)+g_1(x)-g_0(x),
 \qquad
 (\pi_1(x),\pi_0(x))=(\Lambda\{\eta(x)\},\Lambda\{-\eta(x)\}),
\]
with arbitrary coherent values at the two \(P_X\)-null endpoints.
%%% FIELD def-boundary-learner-construction
For a generic fitting sample size \(N\in\{n,m\}\), put
\[
 L_{1,N}(x)=\log\{x\vee N^{-b_0}\},
 \qquad
 L_{0,N}(x)=\log\{(1-x)\vee N^{-b_0}\},
\]
and
\[
 \eta_{J_N}(x;\alpha_0,\alpha_1,\beta)
 =\alpha_1L_{1,N}(x)-\alpha_0L_{0,N}(x)
   +\sum_{j=1}^{J_N}\beta_jB_{j,J_N}(x),
 \qquad
 p_{J_N}(x;\alpha_0,\alpha_1,\beta)
 =\Lambda\{\eta_{J_N}(x;\alpha_0,\alpha_1,\beta)\}.
\]
Let \(\Theta_{J_N}=[0,J_N]^2\times[-J_N,J_N]^{J_N}\).  On a training sample excluding fold \(I_k\), define \((\widehat\alpha_{0,-k},\widehat\alpha_{1,-k},\widehat\beta_{-k})\) as the unique maximizer over \(\Theta_{J_N}\) of
\[
 \sum_{i\notin I_k}
 \left[A_i\log p_{J_N}(X_i;\alpha_0,\alpha_1,\beta)
 +(1-A_i)\log\{1-p_{J_N}(X_i;\alpha_0,\alpha_1,\beta)\}\right]
 -e^{-J_N}\left(\alpha_0^2+\alpha_1^2+sum_{j=1}^{J_N}\beta_j^2\right).
\]
The strictly concave ridge makes the maximizer unique; no global probability-range constraint is imposed.  Set
\[
 \widehat\eta_{-k}(x)=
 \eta_{J_N}(x;\widehat\alpha_{0,-k},\widehat\alpha_{1,-k},\widehat\beta_{-k}),
 \qquad
 \widehat\pi_{1,-k}(x)=\Lambda\{\widehat\eta_{-k}(x)\},
 \qquad
 \widehat\pi_{0,-k}(x)=1-\widehat\pi_{1,-k}(x).
\]
Thus the fitted probabilities are endpoint-safe and binary coherent.  At the full sample use \(N=n\), and in a refit use its actual size \(N=m\).

Fix a target heavy arm \(a\in\mathcal A_{\mathrm H}\) and put \(\epsilon_{a,n}=M_n^{-(\gamma_a-1)}\).  On
\[

 \min\left(\frac{M_n}{\widehat h_{a,n}},\frac1{2(1+\epsilon_{a,n})}\right)\right\},
\]
set
\[
 \widehat\pi^{\mathrm{esc}}_{a,-k}(x)
 =(1+\epsilon_{a,n})\widehat\pi_{a,-k}(x),
 \qquad
 \widehat\pi^{\mathrm{esc}}_{1-a,-k}(x)
 =1-\widehat\pi^{\mathrm{esc}}_{a,-k}(x).
\]
Outside this set leave the fitted pair unchanged.  The displayed cutoff keeps both probabilities strictly between zero and one, so no projection or \(e^{-J_n}\) probability-range constraint is used.  Compute \(E^{\mathrm{esc}}_{a,n}\) and \(D^{\mathrm{esc}}_{a,n}\) from the target-arm perturbation.

Suppose that \(\pi_0(x)+\pi_1(x)=1\), both propensities are positive, and, with \(\alpha_a=0\) on a bounded-support arm,
\[
 \pi_a(x)=d_a(x)^{\alpha_a}e^{g_a(x)},\qquad a\in\{0,1\},
\]
whenever \(d_0(x)d_1(x)>0\).  Then
\[
 \log\frac{\pi_1(x)}{\pi_0(x)}
 =\alpha_1\log d_1(x)-\alpha_0\log d_0(x)+g_1(x)-g_0(x),
\]
and \(\pi_1(x)=\Lambda\{\eta(x)\}\), \(\pi_0(x)=\Lambda\{-\eta(x)\}\).  Hence the joint log-odds representation contains every law satisfying the former paired links.
%%% FIELD lem-paired-proof
By positivity, both logarithms are finite off the two endpoints.  Taking logarithms of the paired links and subtracting gives
\[
 \log\pi_1(x)-\log\pi_0(x)
 =\alpha_1\log d_1(x)-\alpha_0\log d_0(x)+g_1(x)-g_0(x).
\]
The left side is \(\eta(x)\).  Since \(\pi_0=1-\pi_1\), exponentiation gives \(\pi_1/(1-\pi_1)=e^\eta\), and therefore
\[
 \pi_1=\frac{e^\eta}{1+e^\eta}=\Lambda(\eta),
 \qquad
 \pi_0=\frac1{1+e^\eta}=\Lambda(-\eta).
\]
Under the canonical uniform chart, the endpoints have \(P_X\)-measure zero, so arbitrary coherent endpoint values do not change the law.  This proves the claimed inclusion without adding a law restriction beyond the separately disclosed canonical-chart restriction.

For a Gaussian arm it is a deterministic multiple of \(\sum_{i=1}^N\mathrm{IF}_{a,P}(O_i;\tau)\).  These finite sums are measurable and unchanged by reindexing.  Concatenation of the two arm vectors preserves both properties.

On the canonical chart \(d_1(x)=x\), \(d_0(x)=1-x\), a mixed neighborhood is generated by \(X\sim\operatorname{Unif}[0,1]\), \(\pi_1(x)=x^{3/2}/2\), \(\pi_0(x)=1-x^{3/2}/2\), and \(Y(a)=a+X+\zeta\) with centered logistic \(\zeta\). Write \(w(x)=\pi_0(x)\pi_1(x)\), \(\ell_1(x)=\log x\), and \(\ell_0(x)=\log(1-x)\).  The canonical chart, the joint boundary link, boundedness of \(g_1-g_0\), and positivity imply uniform constants \(0<c<C<\infty\) such that
\[
 c x^{\alpha_1}(1-x)^{\alpha_0}le w(x)le
 C x^{\alpha_1}(1-x)^{\alpha_0},\qquad 0<x<1.
\tag{B1}
\]
Indeed, writing \(R=e^{g_1-g_0}x^{\alpha_1}(1-x)^{-\alpha_0}\), one has \(w=R/(1+R)^2\); comparison with \(x^{\alpha_1}(1-x)^{\alpha_0}\) follows separately on \([0,1/2]\) and \([1/2,1]\).  This is where the canonical chart and the common Hölder radius are used.

For one observation the joint Bernoulli log likelihood is \(A\eta-\log(1+e^\eta)\).  Direct differentiation in directions \(v,z\) gives
\[
 \dot\ell_\eta(v)=(A-\pi_1)v,
 \qquad
 -E_P\ddot\ell_\eta(v,z)=E_P\{w(X)v(X)z(X)\}.
\tag{B2}
\]
Thus the score is centered and the information inner product is \(\langle v,z\rangle_w=\int_0^1wvz\), with no inverse propensity factor.

Let \(I_j=((j-1)/J,j/J]\).  From (B1),
\[
 \mu_j:=\int_{I_j}w(x)\,dx
 \asymp J^{-1}(j/J)^{\alpha_1}\{(J-j+1)/J\}^{\alpha_0}.
\tag{B3}
\]
On each cell, rescaling \(u=Jx-j+1\) and finite-dimensional norm equivalence for degree-\(r\) polynomials yield, for \(s_J=\sum_jv_jB_{j,J}\),
\[
 c\sum_j\mu_jv_j^2\le\|s_J\|_w^2\le C\sum_j\mu_jv_j^2.
\tag{B4}
\]
The constants are uniform because the spline degree is fixed and adjacent \(\mu_j\)'s have bounded ratios.

We now profile both logarithms.  Let \(\Pi_J\) be weighted projection on the spline space and \(r_{a,J}=(I-\Pi_J)\ell_a\).  On the first cell, \(\ell_1=-\log J+\log u\), the constant is in the spline space, and \(\log u\) has strictly positive distance from the polynomials of degree \(r\) in \(L^2(u^{\alpha_1}du)\).  Hence
\[
 \|r_{1,J}\|_w^2\ge cJ^{-(\alpha_1+1)}.
\tag{B5}
\]
Piecewise Taylor approximation on all later cells and the same first-cell rescaling give the reverse inequality.  Thus \(\|r_{1,J}\|_w^2\asymp J^{-(\alpha_1+1)}\).  Reflection gives
\[
 \|r_{0,J}\|_w^2\asymp J^{-(\alpha_0+1)}.
\tag{B6}
\]
For a linear combination, the nonsingular \(\log u\) residual occurs on the first cell only through the coefficient of \(\ell_1\), and on the last cell only through the coefficient of \(\ell_0\).  The other logarithm is analytic there; its degree-\(r\) Taylor remainder is \(O(J^{-(r+1)})\).  Since \(2r+1>\alpha_\star\), these cross remainders are of smaller squared order than (B5)--(B6).  Therefore
\[
 \|(I-\Pi_J)(c_1\ell_1-c_0\ell_0)\|_w^2
 \ge c\{c_1^2J^{-(\alpha_1+1)}+c_0^2J^{-(\alpha_0+1)}\}.
\tag{B7}
\]
Equations (B4) and (B7), by the Schur-complement identity for profiling, imply for the full coefficient vector \(u\)
\[
 u^TE_P\{w(X)Z_J(X)Z_J(X)^T\}u
 \ge cJ^{-(\alpha_\star+1)}\|u\|_2^2,
\tag{B8}
\]
where \(Z_J=(\ell_1,-\ell_0,B_{1,J},\ldots,B_{J,J})^T\).

The clipped logarithms differ from the true logarithms only on sets of length \(N^{-b_0}\).  Since \(b_0>1\), an iid training or evaluation sample hits either set with probability at most \(2N^{1-b_0}=o(1)\).  For the empirical Hessian, only \(O(J)\) pairs of spline coordinates overlap.  For each such pair, scalar Bernstein's exponential-moment argument applied to the bounded summands gives relative error
\[
 O_P\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{n\mu_j}}+\frac{\log n}{n\mu_j}\right).
\tag{B9}
\]
The same calculation for the two profiled log residuals costs at most two additional powers of \(\log n\).  Since \(\min_j\mu_j\asymp J^{-(\alpha_\star+1)}\), the stated global condition makes (B9) uniformly \(o_P(1)\).  Equations (B4)--(B7), applied after a union bound over the fixed-width spline bands and the four log blocks, show that the empirical Hessian lies between one half and twice its population counterpart with probability tending uniformly to one.

Choose the spline quasi-interpolant \(g_J\) of \(g_1-g_0\).  Hölder smoothness and \(r+1\ge s\) give \(\|g_J-(g_1-g_0)\|_\infty\le CJ^{-s}\).  On the boundary cells its Taylor remainder is \(O(J^{-s}u^s)\); dividing its inner product with the profiled log residual by (B5) or (B6) therefore gives an \(O(J^{-s})\) population profiling bias, not the cruder inverse-eigenvalue bound.  The compact parameter box contains this candidate for all large \(J\).

The score variance in any direction equals its information norm by (B2).  Solving the fixed-band empirical normal equations using (B3)--(B9), and then the two profiled equations using (B5)--(B7), yields on cell \(I_j\)
\[
 \sup_{x\in I_j}|\widehat\eta(x)-\eta(x)|
 =O_P\!\left((\log n)^C\left[J^{-s}+(n\mu_j)^{-1/2}\right]\right).
\tag{B10}
\]
This follows directly by multiplying the centered score on cell \(j\), whose variance is \(\mu_j/n\), by the inverse local information \(O(\mu_j^{-1})\); finite overlap in (B4) bounds the neighboring terms, while (B7) bounds the two profile coefficients.  The ridge derivative is \(O(e^{-J}J\sqrt J)\), smaller than every displayed order.  Concavity and empirical Hessian coercivity keep the maximizer in this local neighborhood, so the compact constraints are inactive asymptotically.

For arm \(a\), differentiation of \(\log\pi_a\) with respect to \(\eta\) multiplies by the opposite propensity and has absolute value at most one.  Near arm \(a\)'s zero endpoint this multiplier is bounded below; near the other endpoint it cancels the small information weight.  Applying (B10), (B3), and reflection gives
\[
 \sup_{d_a(x)\ge n^{-b_0}}|\log\widehat\pi_a(x)-\log\pi_a(x)|
 =O_P\!\left((\log n)^C\delta_{a,n}\right).

 O_P\!\left(\frac m{H_{a,P,m}}\frac{H_{a,P,n}}n\right)
 =O_P\{(m/n)^{1-1/\gamma_a}\}
\]
on a heavy arm and \(O_P\{(m/n)^{1/2}\}\) on a Gaussian arm; gamma separation makes both uniform \(o_P(1)\).  Scale consistency gives \(\widehat\ell_{a,n}-\ell_{a,P,n}=o_P(1)\).  Recombination therefore proves the conditional bounded-Lipschitz approximation to \(Z_{P,n}\).

Half-line indicators are approximated above and below by Lipschitz ramps of width \(\eta\).  Apply this smoothing to the conditional approximation and the full-sample joint-QTE approximation, then let \(n\to\infty\) and \(\eta\downarrow0\).  The coordinate anti-concentration modulus gives the asymmetric pointwise coverage.  The map \(x\mapsto\|x\|_\infty\) is one-Lipschitz, so the maximum anti-concentration modulus gives simultaneous coverage for the finite set \(\mathcal T\).

For the internal diagnostic, choose a two-heavy row with \(\gamma_{0,P_n}=\gamma+c/(2\log n)\) and \(\gamma_{1,P_n}=\gamma-c/(2\log n)\).  Hall inversion gives
\[
 \log\{h_{0,P_n,n}/h_{1,P_n,n}\}\to-c/\gamma^2,
 \qquad
 \log\{h_{0,P_n,m}/h_{1,P_n,m}\}\to-\beta c/\gamma^2
\]
when \(m=n^\beta\).  Thus \(T^{\mathrm{raw}}_{B,n}\) uses a different arm mixture, whereas armwise standardization followed by full-sample recombination reproduces the first ratio.  This proves exactly the internal diagnostic stated, without attributing it to any published estimator.
