%%% FIELD proof-width-two-enumeration
Assume \(b\leq 2\).  We first identify failure with reachability in the auxiliary network.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:width-two-bow-characterization}, for every \(S\subseteq E^{?}\),
\[
 \operatorname{Fail}(S)=1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \begin{gathered}
 \text{there is }\beta=(x,r,e^{\to},e^{\leftrightarrow})\in\mathcal B
 \text{ with }e^{\to},e^{\leftrightarrow}\in E^{\mathrm{det}}\cup S,\\
 \text{and }r\text{ reaches some vertex of }Y
 \text{ in the directed part of }G_S[V\setminus X].
 \end{gathered}
\tag{1}
\]
For a fixed \(\beta\), Definition~\ref{def:width-two-auxiliary-network} represents its two bow edges by
\(\sigma_H\to a_\beta\to r_\beta\), represents the directed graph on \(V\setminus X\) by the body of \(H^{\mathrm{aux}}\), and joins every \(y\in Y\) deterministically to \(t_H\).  A labelled arc is retained in \(H^{\mathrm{aux}}_S\) exactly when its uncertain edge belongs to \(S\).  Consequently, the right-hand side of (1) holds if and only if \(H^{\mathrm{aux}}_S\) contains a directed \(\sigma_H\)-to-\(t_H\) path.  The converse direction here uses the fact that every arc leaving \(\sigma_H\) starts one of the private bow paths, so every such auxiliary path selects a member of \(\mathcal B\) and then follows only body arrows to \(Y\).

We next verify the injectivity needed by the projection rather than taking it as an asserted property of the construction.  A bidirected uncertain edge can label only the first arc of the private path for the unique bow having its typed endpoint pair, and a directed uncertain edge out of \(X\) can label only the second arc of that same private path.  A directed body edge has both endpoints in \(V\setminus X\), so it cannot also be a directed bow edge.  No bidirected edge labels a body arc.  Since \(E^{\mathrm{det}}\cap E^{?}=\varnothing\) by Assumption~\ref{ass:edge-partition}, deterministic and uncertain copies cannot collide.  Hence every \(e\in E^{?}\) labels at most one auxiliary arc.

Let \(A_H^{?}\) denote the labelled auxiliary arcs, write \(a_e=(q_e^-,q_e^+)\) for the arc labelled by \(e\), and define
\[
 U(S)=\{u_e:a_e\in A_H^{?},\ e\in S\}.
\]
Definitions~\ref{def:transition-vertices}--\ref{def:transition-arcs} give the exact equivalence
\[
 \sigma_H\leadsto t_H\text{ in }H^{\mathrm{aux}}_S
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \sigma\leadsto t\text{ in }
 T^{\mathrm{proj}}[\{\sigma,t\}\cup U(S)].
\tag{2}
\]
Indeed, given a path on the left, list its labelled arcs in their path order.  The deterministic segment before the first labelled arc, between every two consecutive labelled arcs, and after the last labelled arc gives, respectively, the transition arcs from \(\sigma\), between the corresponding vertices \(u_e\), and to \(t\).  If the path has no labelled arc, it gives \(\sigma\to t\).  Conversely, replace each transition arc by a deterministic path witnessing the reachability condition in Definition~\ref{def:transition-arcs}, and insert the labelled auxiliary arc at every internal transition vertex.  Concatenation gives the path on the left.  It cannot repeat a vertex, because \(H^{\mathrm{aux}}\) is acyclic.  The same argument shows that \(T^{\mathrm{proj}}\) is acyclic: a directed cycle among transition vertices, after expansion and insertion of their labelled arcs, would give a directed cycle in \(H^{\mathrm{aux}}\), while \(\sigma\) has no incoming transition arc and \(t\) has no outgoing transition arc.

By Lemma~\ref{lem:failure-upward} and Definition~\ref{def:obstruction-hypergraph},
\[
 C\in\mathcal O_{XY}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{Fail}(C)=1
 \text{ and }\operatorname{Fail}(D)=0
 \text{ for every }D\subsetneq C.
\tag{3}
\]
For the nontrivial implication, if every one-edge deletion of \(C\) succeeds but some \(D\subsetneq C\) fails, choose \(e\in C\setminus D\); upward closure would make \(C\setminus\{e\}\) fail, a contradiction.

Let \(C\in\mathcal O_{XY}\).  Equations (1)--(2) give a transition path whose internal labels form a set \(D\subseteq C\).  That path also proves \(\operatorname{Fail}(D)=1\), so (3) forces \(D=C\).  This transition path is induced.  Otherwise it has an arc from a path vertex \(v_i\) to a nonconsecutive later vertex \(v_j\), and replacing the subpath \(v_i,\ldots,v_j\) by that arc produces a transition path whose internal labels are a proper subset of \(C\), contradicting (3).

Conversely, let \(P=(\sigma=v_0,v_1,\ldots,v_k=t)\) be an induced directed path in \(T^{\mathrm{proj}}\), and put \(C=L(P)\).  Equation (2) gives \(\operatorname{Fail}(C)=1\).  Suppose that a proper subset \(D\subsetneq C\) failed.  Another application of (2) would give a transition path all of whose internal vertices belong to \(\{u_e:e\in D\}\).  The relative order of any two vertices common to two directed paths in a DAG is the same: reversing their order would create directed paths in both directions and hence a directed cycle.  Thus this second path is obtained from the ordered internal vertex set of \(P\) by omitting at least one vertex.  At some omission, two vertices consecutive on the second path are nonconsecutive on \(P\); the arc between them is a chord of \(P\), a contradiction.  Therefore no proper subset of \(C\) fails, and (3) yields \(C\in\mathcal O_{XY}\).

This correspondence is one-to-one.  If two induced transition paths had the same label union, injectivity of the auxiliary labels would give them the same internal vertex set.  Acyclicity fixes the relative order of that set by the preceding argument, so the two paths would be identical.  We have therefore proved
\[
 \mathcal O_{XY}
 =\{L(P):P\text{ is an induced directed }\sigma\text{-to-}t
       \text{ path in }T^{\mathrm{proj}}\}.
\tag{4}
\]
Notice that (3)--(4) impose minimality against every auxiliary path and hence against every competing bow start; minimality is not merely local to one selected bow.

It remains to prove the enumeration guarantees.  At a search state \((P,H)\), with last prefix vertex \(v\), maintain the following invariant: \(P\) is an induced prefix; \(H\) contains \(v\) and \(t\) but none of the earlier prefix vertices; and every vertex that could create a chord from an earlier prefix vertex to a later suffix vertex has already been deleted.  The invariant holds initially.  If an induced completion has next vertex \(w\), it cannot later use any member of \(N_H^+(v)\setminus\{w\}\), because the arc from \(v\) to that member would be a chord.  It also cannot revisit \(v\).  Hence it survives in
\[
 H_w=H-\bigl(\{v\}\cup(N_H^+(v)\setminus\{w\})\bigr).
\]
Conversely, if \(t\) is reachable from \(w\) in \(H_w\), choose a shortest directed \(w\)-to-\(t\) path in \(H_w\).  A shortest directed path has no forward chord, since such a chord would shorten it.  Appending it to \(P\cdot w\) creates no chord from \(v\), by the definition of \(H_w\), and no chord from an earlier prefix vertex, by the induction invariant.  Thus the child has an induced completion.  The reachability test is therefore exact: every created child contains an output, and every rejected child contains none.  Children with distinct \(w\) partition the completions by their next vertex.  Induction on the remaining path length proves coverage and exhaustion, while the partition and the bijection (4) prove duplicate suppression.  Every emitted support is globally minimal by (4).

For the complexity bound, put \(N=|W^{\mathrm{proj}}|\) and \(M=|A^{\mathrm{proj}}|\).  The auxiliary network has polynomial size: an ADMG has only polynomially many typed edges and \(|\mathcal B|\leq |X|\,|V\setminus X|\).  Constructing \(T^{\mathrm{proj}}\) requires only polynomially many reachability searches in the deterministic auxiliary subgraph, so preprocessing is polynomial.  At a state there are at most \(N\) candidate out-neighbors, and construction of \(H_w\) together with a reachability search costs \(O(N+M)\), giving \(O(N(N+M))\) work for a complete scan of one state.  The recursion depth is at most \(N\).  Between consecutive outputs, depth-first search finishes scans on at most \(N\) old stack frames and descends through at most \(N\) new frames; the same bound applies before the first output and from the last output to certified exhaustion.  Thus the conservative worst-case delay is
\[
 O\bigl(N^2(N+M)\bigr),
\]
which is polynomial in the input size.  Storing the fixed transition graph and one surviving-vertex bit set per stack level uses polynomial working space.

Finally, if \(\sigma\to t\) is present, its induced path emits \(\varnothing\).  Every longer \(\sigma\)-to-\(t\) path has \(\sigma\to t\) as a chord; algorithmically, choosing any other first neighbor deletes \(t\), so that branch fails its reachability test.  Hence the empty obstruction is emitted once and suppresses every nonminimal nonempty support.  This also completes the boundary case and all claimed preprocessing, delay, space, coverage, minimality, duplicate-suppression, and exhaustion guarantees.
%%% FIELD proof-deterministic-diamond-projection
In \(\mathcal D\), the only bidirected edge of the full envelope is \(e_2=x\leftrightarrow r\).  Hence \(\{x,r\}\) is a bidirected district of size two, every other district is a singleton, and Definition~\ref{def:deterministic-diamond-instance} gives \(b=2\).

There is exactly one available bow,
\[
 \beta=(x,r,e_1,e_2),
 \qquad e_1=x\to r,
 \qquad e_2=x\leftrightarrow r.
\]
In \(H^{\mathrm{aux}}\), its private path is
\[
 \sigma_H\xrightarrow{e_2}a_\beta
 \xrightarrow{e_1}r.
\]
All remaining arcs on a route from \(r\) to \(t_H\) are deterministic.  In particular, the two distinct paths
\[
 r\to a\to c\to y\to t_H
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 r\to b\to c\to y\to t_H
\]
both witness the single Boolean reachability relation from the head \(r\) of the arc labelled \(e_1\) to \(t_H\).  By Definition~\ref{def:transition-arcs}, they therefore create one transition arc \(u_{e_1}\to t\), rather than two copies.

The two uncertain auxiliary arcs give precisely the internal transition vertices \(u_{e_2}\) and \(u_{e_1}\).  Length-zero deterministic reachability gives \(\sigma\to u_{e_2}\) and \(u_{e_2}\to u_{e_1}\).  There is no deterministic route from \(\sigma_H\) to the tail \(a_\beta\) of the \(e_1\)-arc, nor from the head \(a_\beta\) of the \(e_2\)-arc to \(t_H\), because the omitted arc in each case is the other uncertain bow arc.  There is also no deterministic \(\sigma_H\)-to-\(t_H\) route.  Consequently the unique directed, and hence unique induced, transition path is
\[
 \sigma\to u_{e_2}\to u_{e_1}\to t.
\]
Definition~\ref{def:transition-labels} gives its label union as
\[
 L(\sigma,u_{e_2},u_{e_1},t)=\{e_1,e_2\}.
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:width-two-enumeration} now yields
\(\mathcal O_{XY}=\{\{e_1,e_2\}\}\), and its exact-once enumeration result shows that this support is emitted once despite the two deterministic diamond arms.
