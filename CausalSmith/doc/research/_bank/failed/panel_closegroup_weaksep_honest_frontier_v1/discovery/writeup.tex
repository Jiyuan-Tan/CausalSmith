\section*{panel\_\allowbreak{}closegroup\_\allowbreak{}weaksep\_\allowbreak{}honest\_\allowbreak{}frontier}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} A finite menu of prespecified donor stacks creates nonregular inference when validity is defined by an exact zero of a noisy baseline score. The panel procedures remain causal only under the explicit nonempty exact-close and shared-slope additive untreated-change conditions; zero score alone does not imply target coherence in the abstract Gaussian limit. The unrestricted-effect full Gaussian frontier is infinite. In the repaired bounded-spread score-only benchmark, \(b\) is observed exactly and only root-sample-scaled scores remain Gaussian: its exact honest expected-diameter value is zero for \(J=1\) and \(B(1-J\alpha/(J-1))\) for \(J\geq2\) and \(0<\alpha<1/2\), for arbitrary positive definite shared-control covariance and including multiple-close-donor faces. A data-free randomized hull attains the value; the covariance-aware deterministic score screen remains an honest operational comparator. The two-state subproblem retains the sharper separation-dependent value \(\Delta[\Phi(z_{1-\alpha}-\rho)-\alpha]_+\). Finite-sample transfer remains limited to bounded uniformly equicontinuous risk payoffs, and none of these conditional results validates the historical CMS comparison-state estimates.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Existing few-treated panel, synthetic-control, and generic post-selection methods do not determine honest expected-diameter inference when validity is an exact zero-score face and several labels may be simultaneously valid. They also do not justify replacing a consistent unscaled effect estimate by exact observation in two-sided deficiency. The remaining transfer issue is distinct from the now-solved finite Gaussian decision problem.

\paragraph{Niche.} The CMS comparison-state workflow has finitely many candidate labels, many beneficiaries within each state, unequal sample sizes, reused controls, and uncertainty about exact transport. A causal conclusion requires an exact population equality plus a model connecting the score to untreated changes. Ranked empirical balance supplies neither, and the abstract implication from zero scores to a common effect is an additional restriction rather than an algebraic fact.

\paragraph{Fill.} The project proves exact-close transport under the explicit shared-slope untreated-change model, gives uniformly honest simultaneous and pilot-adaptive procedures with fixed-separation oracle behavior, and proves that the full fixed-noise Gaussian frontier is infinite when effect spread is unrestricted. For the bounded-spread score-only repair it now derives the exact finite-face value \(0\) for \(J=1\) and \(B(1-J\alpha/(J-1))\) for \(J\geq2\), together with its attaining randomized kernel, arbitrary shared-control covariance, and multiple-zero faces. The two-face Mahalanobis formula remains the sharper separation-dependent restriction result, while the deterministic score screen is retained only as an honest comparator. Direct finite-sample transfer is proved only for bounded uniformly equicontinuous payoffs; unbounded-diameter and discontinuous-coverage transfer still require extra tail and boundary control.

\section{Related work}

The reviewed RTI material describes the CMS comparison-state workflow through ranked baseline distance, retained comparison states, claims-driven substitutions, and person-level weighting; it does not document ex ante algebraic calibration to known population totals for every score component. Hence the workflow is a motivating and falsifiable use case, not evidence that the paper's exact-close assumptions held historically. Conley and Taber and Ferman and Pinto obtain reference variation from many control groups, unlike the many-within-group, fixed-label regime here. Chernozhukov, Wuthrich, and Zhu study time-series synthetic-control inference rather than exact donor-face uncertainty. Berk et al. motivate simultaneous protection; here the deterministic simultaneous score screen is kept as an operational comparator, whereas the exact bounded-spread finite-face minimax value is derived directly and attained by a data-free randomized rule. Andrews, Kitagawa, and McCloskey study argmax-winner selection; the present exact-zero-face model is not their argmax-selection model, and neither parameter class contains the other without changing both selection and estimand. Their work motivates only the separate pilot-adaptive oracle construction. Evans, Hansen, and Stark supply an acceptance-region expected-measure template, but their duality is not invoked because the needed domination, product measurability, and reduction from fixed sigma-finite expected measure to expected diameter are not established here. Andrews and Cheng, Andrews and Mikusheva, and Nussbaum motivate weak-limit and experiment-comparison questions but do not remove the effect-coordinate scaling obstruction.

  \item \(\mathbb P_{n,\eta}\) --- \texttt{restricted local observable joint law}; \(\mathcal P\)
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} the member at index \(n\) and local parameter \(\eta\) of a dominated QMD subfamily of the product laws \(\mathbb P_n\)
  \item \(\psi_n\) --- \texttt{common finite-\allowbreak{}sample parameter map}; \(\psi_n:\mathcal K\to(\mathbb R^q)^J\times\mathbb R^J\times\mathbb S_{++}^p\)
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\psi_n(\eta)=((\sqrt n\,s_{j,n})_{j\in\mathcal J},(b_{j,n})_{j\in\mathcal J},\Omega_n)\)
  \item \(\mathcal E_n^{\mathrm{mean}}\) --- \texttt{restricted reduced-\allowbreak{}sample-\allowbreak{}mean experiment}; statistical experiments indexed by \(\mathcal K\)
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\mathcal E_n^{\mathrm{mean}}=\{\mathcal L_{\mathbb P_{n,\eta}}(((\sqrt n\,\widehat s_{j,n})_{j\in\mathcal J},(\widehat b_{j,n})_{j\in\mathcal J})):\eta\in\mathcal K\}\)
  \item \(m_n\) --- \texttt{deterministic per-\allowbreak{}group pilot sample size}; \(\mathbb N\)
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(m_n\to\infty\) and \(m_n/n\to0\)
  \item \(a_n\) --- \texttt{deterministic pilot-\allowbreak{}screen threshold}; \((0,\infty)\)
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(a_n\to\infty\) and \(a_n/\sqrt{m_n}\to0\)
  \item \(\widehat\mu_{r,n}^{\mathrm P}\) --- \texttt{pilot group mean vector}; \(\mathbb R^d\); mean of the first \(m_n\) observations in group \(r\)
  \item \(\widehat\mu_{r,n}^{\mathrm M}\) --- \texttt{main-\allowbreak{}sample group mean vector}; \(\mathbb R^d\); mean of observations \(m_n+1,\ldots,n_r\) in group \(r\)
  \item \(\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm P}\) --- \texttt{stacked pilot group mean vector}; \(\mathbb R^{Rd}\); \texttt{stack of the pilot group means}
  \item \(\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm M}\) --- \texttt{stacked main-\allowbreak{}sample group mean vector}; \(\mathbb R^{Rd}\); \texttt{stack of the main-\allowbreak{}sample group means}
  \item \(\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm P}\) --- \texttt{pilot stacked score-\allowbreak{}effect estimator}; \(\mathbb R^p\)
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm P}=A\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm P}\)
  \item \(\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm M}\) --- \texttt{main-\allowbreak{}sample stacked score-\allowbreak{}effect estimator}; \(\mathbb R^p\)
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm M}=A\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm M}\)
  \item \(\widehat s_{j,n}^{\mathrm P}\) --- \texttt{pilot pre-\allowbreak{}equality score}; \(\mathbb R^q\)
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} the first \(q\) coordinates of \(E_j\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm P}\)
  \item \(\widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm M}\) --- \texttt{spectrally clipped main-\allowbreak{}sample covariance matrix}; \(\mathbb S_{++}^p\); \texttt{shared-\allowbreak{}group covariance estimate computed only from observations unused by the pilot}
  \item \texttt{S} --- \texttt{nonempty donor-\allowbreak{}label subset}; \(2^{\mathcal J}\setminus\{\varnothing\}\)
  \item \(r_j(\Omega)\) --- \texttt{score-\allowbreak{}residualized effect row}; \(\mathbb R^{1\times p}\); the row mapping a stacked score-effect vector to the covariance-residualized effect for label \(j\)
  \item \(\sigma_j^2(\Omega)\) --- \texttt{residual-\allowbreak{}effect variance}; \((0,\infty)\)
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\sigma_j^2(\Omega)=r_j(\Omega)\Omega r_j(\Omega)^{\top}\)
  \item \(k_{1-\alpha}(S,\Omega)\) --- \texttt{retained-\allowbreak{}label residual-\allowbreak{}effect Gaussian critical value}; \((0,\infty)\); the \(1-\alpha\) quantile of the maximum absolute standardized residual-effect Gaussian coordinate over \(S\)
  \item \(\widehat{\mathcal S}_n\) --- \texttt{nonempty pilot-\allowbreak{}retained donor-\allowbreak{}label set}; \(2^{\mathcal J}\setminus\{\varnothing\}\); \texttt{labels whose pilot scores pass the vanishing score threshold,\allowbreak{} with all labels retained if the raw screen is empty}
  \item \(\widehat t_{j,n}^{\mathrm M}\) --- \texttt{main-\allowbreak{}sample residual-\allowbreak{}effect estimator}; \(\mathbb R\)
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat t_{j,n}^{\mathrm M}=r_j(\widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm M})\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm M}\)
  \item \(I_{j,n}^{\mathrm{ad}}(\alpha)\) --- \texttt{pilot-\allowbreak{}adaptive labelwise effect interval}; \(2^{\mathbb R}\); \texttt{main-\allowbreak{}sample residual-\allowbreak{}effect interval for a retained label and the empty set otherwise}
  \item \(C_n^{\mathrm{ad}}(\alpha)\) --- \texttt{pilot-\allowbreak{}adaptive ATT confidence set}; \(2^{\mathbb R}\); \texttt{convex hull of the retained-\allowbreak{}label main-\allowbreak{}sample residual-\allowbreak{}effect intervals}
  \item \(B\) --- \texttt{positive effect-\allowbreak{}spread bound}; \((0,\infty)\); upper bound on \(\max_j b_j-\min_j b_j\) in the score-only face space
  \item \(\Gamma\) --- \texttt{positive definite score covariance matrix}; \(\mathbb S_{++}^{Jq}\); \texttt{joint covariance of the root-\allowbreak{}sample-\allowbreak{}scaled score blocks,\allowbreak{} including shared-\allowbreak{}control covariance}
  \item \(X^{\mathrm{sc}}\) --- \texttt{score-\allowbreak{}only Gaussian observation}; \(\mathbb R^{Jq}\)
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(X^{\mathrm{sc}}\sim N(h,\Gamma)\)
  \item \(\mathcal H_{H,B}^{\mathrm{sc}}\) --- \texttt{bounded-\allowbreak{}spread score-\allowbreak{}only donor-\allowbreak{}face parameter space}; \((\mathbb R^q)^J\times\mathbb R^J\); \texttt{union of exact-\allowbreak{}zero score faces with bounded effect spread and unrestricted absolute effect location}
  \item \(\mathfrak R_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{sc}}(H,B,\Gamma)\) --- \texttt{score-\allowbreak{}only honest expected-\allowbreak{}diameter minimax value}; \([0,\infty)\); bounded-spread weak-separation frontier with exact observation of \(b\)
  \item \(\rho\) --- \texttt{two-\allowbreak{}state Mahalanobis score-\allowbreak{}mean separation}; \((0,\infty)\); \texttt{distance between the two Gaussian score means under their common covariance}
\end{itemize}

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:consistency]\label{ass:consistency}
For every \(n\ge1\), \(r\in\mathcal R\), and \(1\le i\le n_r\), \(Y_{ri}^{\mathrm{obs}}=Y_{ri}(1)\) \(P_{r,n}^{\star}\)-almost surely when \(r\in\mathcal T\) and \(Y_{ri}^{\mathrm{obs}}=Y_{ri}(0)\) \(P_{r,n}^{\star}\)-almost surely when \(r\in\mathcal R\setminus\mathcal T\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (SUTVA consistency for observed treatment histories, \cite{ImbensRubin2015}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:stable-fractions]\label{ass:stable-fractions}
For every \(r\in\mathcal R\), \(n_r/n\to\lambda_r\in(0,1)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (stable unequal group-size asymptotics, \cite{FermanPinto2019}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:uniform-fourth-moment]\label{ass:uniform-fourth-moment}
\(\sup_{n\ge1}\max_{r\in\mathcal R}\mathbb E_{P_{r,n}}[\|W_{ri}-\mu_{r,n}\|_2^4]\le M_4\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (uniform Lyapunov fourth-moment condition, \cite{vanDerVaart1998}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:covariance-regularity]\label{ass:covariance-regularity}
\(\underline\omega\le\lambda_{\min}(\Omega_n)\le\lambda_{\max}(\Omega_n)\le\overline\omega\) for every \(n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (uniform nonsingularity of the Gaussian information matrix, \cite{AndrewsMikusheva2016}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:nonempty-close-set]\label{ass:nonempty-close-set}
\(\mathcal C_n\ne\varnothing\) for every \(n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} The condition defines an abstract conditional design regime and is not asserted for the historical CMS workflow. It is credible in designs that algebraically calibrate a donor stack ex ante to known administrative population totals for every prespecified trend predictor. Score intervals and calibration residuals may falsify this regime, but ranked sample near-balance cannot establish it.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:additive-untreated-change]\label{ass:additive-untreated-change}
For every \(n\ge1\) and \(r\in\mathcal R\), \(\mathbb E_{P_{r,n}^{\star}}[Y_{ri}(0)-Y_{ri}^{\mathrm{pre}}\mid G_{ri}]=\kappa_n+\beta_n^{\top}G_{ri}\) \(P_{r,n}^{\star}\)-almost surely.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This shared-slope conditional-mean model is realistic when baseline levels, pre-period slopes, and case mix exhaust systematic cross-state differences in untreated changes, while individual residual heterogeneity remains unrestricted. Its implications can be challenged using held-out pre-period changes, omitted-predictor balance, and slope-stability checks.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:local-zero-face]\label{ass:local-zero-face}
\(\min_{j\in\mathcal J}\|h_j\|_2=0\) and \(\max_{j\in\mathcal J}\|h_j\|_2\le H\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This bounded local union records a finite menu with at least one exact donor and permits several exact donors. Such configurations arise when administratively eligible donor states share calibrated baseline moments while other candidates differ on the sampling-error scale.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:local-face-coherence]\label{ass:local-face-coherence}
For all \(j,k\in\mathcal J\), \(h_j=h_k=0\) implies \(b_j=b_k\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} The restriction is not implied by \(h_j=0\). In a causal panel it must follow from a separate transport argument, such as exact score transport under the additive untreated-change model, or be imposed explicitly in the abstract Gaussian experiment.
\end{assumption}

\section{Definitions}

\begin{definition}[def:panel-model-class]\label{def:panel-model-class}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} Exact-close triangular-array class \(\mathfrak M\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\mathfrak M=\{\mathbf P=(\mathbb P_n)_{n\ge1}:\mathbb P_n=\bigotimes_{r\in\mathcal R}P_{r,n}^{\otimes n_r}\text{ for every }n\ge1,\text{ and there exists a full array }(P_{r,n}^{\star})_{r\in\mathcal R,n\ge1}\text{ for which ass:consistency, ass:stable-fractions, ass:uniform-fourth-moment, ass:covariance-regularity, ass:nonempty-close-set, and ass:additive-untreated-change hold}\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Member properties.} \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}consistency}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}stable-\allowbreak{}fractions}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}uniform-\allowbreak{}fourth-\allowbreak{}moment}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}covariance-\allowbreak{}regularity}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}nonempty-\allowbreak{}close-\allowbreak{}set}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}additive-\allowbreak{}untreated-\allowbreak{}change}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:stacked-estimator]\label{def:stacked-estimator}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Stacked score-\allowbreak{}effect estimator}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Compute each \(\widehat\mu_{r,n}=n_r^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n_r}W_{ri}\), stack these vectors as \(\widehat\mu_n\), and set \(\widehat\theta_n=A\widehat\mu_n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \(W_{ri}\); \(n_r\); \texttt{A}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:shared-covariance-estimator]\label{def:shared-covariance-estimator}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Shared-\allowbreak{}group covariance estimator and spectral clipping}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Compute the centered within-group covariances \(\widehat\Sigma_{r,n}\), set \(\widehat\Omega_n=A\operatorname{diag}_{r\in\mathcal R}((n/n_r)\widehat\Sigma_{r,n})A^{\top}\), symmetrize it, and clip every eigenvalue to \([\underline\omega/2,2\overline\omega]\) to obtain \(\widetilde\Omega_n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \(W_{ri}\); \(n_r\); \texttt{A}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:profile-intervals]\label{def:profile-intervals}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Labelwise joint-\allowbreak{}Wald profiles}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Partition \(E_j\widetilde\Omega_nE_j^{\top}\) as \(\begin{pmatrix}\widehat V_{j,n}&\widehat u_{j,n}\\\widehat u_{j,n}^{\top}&\widehat v_{j,n}\end{pmatrix}\), compute \(\widehat\gamma_{j,n}=\widehat V_{j,n}^{-1}\widehat u_{j,n}\), \(\widehat\sigma_{j,n}^2=\widehat v_{j,n}-\widehat u_{j,n}^{\top}\widehat V_{j,n}^{-1}\widehat u_{j,n}\), \(Q_{j,n}=n\widehat s_{j,n}^{\top}\widehat V_{j,n}^{-1}\widehat s_{j,n}\), and \(\widehat c_n=c_{1-\alpha}(\widetilde\Omega_n)\). If \(Q_{j,n}\le\widehat c_n^2\), set \(I_{j,n}(\alpha)=[\widehat b_{j,n}-\widehat\gamma_{j,n}^{\top}\widehat s_{j,n}\;\pm\;n^{-1/2}\widehat\sigma_{j,n}(\widehat c_n^2-Q_{j,n})^{1/2}]\); otherwise set \(I_{j,n}(\alpha)=\varnothing\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \(\widehat\theta_n\); \(\widetilde\Omega_n\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:projected-hull]\label{def:projected-hull}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Maximum-\allowbreak{}critical labelwise hull}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(C_n^{\mathrm{proj}}(\alpha)=\operatorname{conv}(\bigcup_{j\in\mathcal J}I_{j,n}(\alpha))\), with the convex hull of the empty set defined as the empty set.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \(I_{j,n}(\alpha)\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:transport-diagnostics]\label{def:transport-diagnostics}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Observable exact-\allowbreak{}close diagnostic protocol}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Construct \(\mathcal D_n\) by reporting, for every \(j\in\mathcal J\), the joint confidence region for \(s_{j,n}\), the numerical calibration residual \(\widehat s_{j,n}\), any documented ex ante algebraic calibration to known administrative population totals, held-out pre-period change residuals not included in \(G_{ri}\), balance for prespecified omitted-risk summaries, the maximum donor weight \(\max_{r\in\mathcal R}\omega_{jr}\), and group effective sample sizes. Absence of documented population-total calibration is reported rather than replaced by ranked sample near-balance.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \(W_{ri}\); \(\omega_{jr}\); \(\widehat\theta_n\); \(\widetilde\Omega_n\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:local-face-space]\label{def:local-face-space}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} Abstract coherent Gaussian donor-face space \(\mathcal H_H\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\mathcal H_H=\{(h,b)\in(\mathbb R^q)^J\times\mathbb R^J:\min_{j\in\mathcal J}\lVert h_j\rVert_2=0,\ \max_{j\in\mathcal J}\lVert h_j\rVert_2\leq H,\ h_j=h_k=0\Rightarrow b_j=b_k\}\). The final implication is an additional abstract Gaussian coherence restriction; it is not a consequence of the zero-score equalities alone.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Member properties.} \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}local-\allowbreak{}zero-\allowbreak{}face}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}local-\allowbreak{}face-\allowbreak{}coherence}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:gaussian-minimax-risk]\label{def:gaussian-minimax-risk}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Gaussian honest expected-\allowbreak{}diameter frontier}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \(\mathfrak R_{\alpha}^{G}(H,\Omega)=\inf_K\sup_{(h,b)\in\mathcal H_H}\mathbb E_{h,b,\Omega}[\operatorname{diam}(K(Z^{\mathrm{pan}}))]\), where the infimum is over measurable confidence kernels \(K:\mathbb R^p\rightsquigarrow2^{\mathbb R}\) satisfying \(\inf_{(h,b)\in\mathcal H_H}\Pr_{h,b,\Omega}\{\tau(h,b)\in K(Z^{\mathrm{pan}})\}\ge1-\alpha\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{H}; \(\Omega\); \(\mathcal H_H\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:two-donor-switch]\label{def:two-donor-switch}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Two-\allowbreak{}donor switch experiment}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Observe the explicitly two-dimensional score vector \(Z^{\mathrm{sw}}\). Under state \(\ell=0\), \(Z^{\mathrm{sw}}\sim N((0,c)^{\top},I_2)\) and the ATT is \(0\); under state \(\ell=1\), \(Z^{\mathrm{sw}}\sim N((c,0)^{\top},I_2)\) and the ATT is \(\Delta\). The maps \(L_{\mathrm{sw}}\) and \(L_{\perp}\) embed the experiment in \(Z^{\mathrm{pan}}\) when \(J=2\), \(q=1\), \(b=(0,\Delta)^{\top}\), \(L_{\mathrm{sw}}\Omega L_{\mathrm{sw}}^{\top}=I_2\), and \(U^{\mathrm{sw}}\) is independent of \(Z^{\mathrm{sw}}\) with a state-invariant law.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{c}; \(\Delta\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:np-confidence-rule]\label{def:np-confidence-rule}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Neyman-\allowbreak{}-\allowbreak{}Pearson donor-\allowbreak{}switch confidence rule}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} With \(z_{1-\alpha}=\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha)\), define \(C^{\mathrm{NP}}_{c,\Delta,\alpha}(Z^{\mathrm{sw}})=\{0:D\ge c-\sqrt2z_{1-\alpha}\}\cup\{\Delta:D\le-c+\sqrt2z_{1-\alpha}\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{D}; \texttt{c}; \(\Delta\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:maximum-benchmark]\label{def:maximum-benchmark}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Two-\allowbreak{}score Gaussian maximum benchmark}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For \(z=(z_1,z_2)^{\top}\in\mathbb R^2\), set \(K_{\max,\alpha,\Delta}(z)=\operatorname{conv}(\{t\in\{0\}:|z_1|\le k_{\alpha}\}\cup\{t\in\{\Delta\}:|z_2|\le k_{\alpha}\})\), where \(k_{\alpha}\) is determined by \((2\Phi(k_{\alpha})-1)^2=1-\alpha\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \(\alpha\); \(\Delta\); \(k_{\alpha}\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:decision-dual-handle]\label{def:decision-dual-handle}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Score-\allowbreak{}face primal and proposed decision-\allowbreak{}dual handle}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For the score-only experiment, the primal variables are measurable randomized confidence kernels \(K:\mathbb R^{Jq}\times\mathbb R^J\rightsquigarrow2^{\mathbb R}\). Their constraints are
\[
 \Pr_{h,\Gamma}\{\tau(h,b)\in K(X^{\mathrm{sc}},b)\}\geq1-\alpha
 \quad\text{for every }(h,b)\in\mathcal H_{H,B}^{\mathrm{sc}},
\]
and their objective is
\[
 \sup_{(h,b)\in\mathcal H_{H,B}^{\mathrm{sc}}}
 \mathbb E_{h,\Gamma}[\operatorname{diam}(K(X^{\mathrm{sc}},b))].
\]
Acceptance-region duality may be used as a proposed route to this program only after domination, joint measurability in the face parameter, and a valid reduction from expected fixed-measure loss to expected diameter have been proved. No such reduction is included in this definition.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \(\mathcal H_{H,B}^{\mathrm{sc}}\); \(\Gamma\); \(\alpha\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:restricted-transfer-handle]\label{def:restricted-transfer-handle}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Restricted direct risk-\allowbreak{}transfer handle}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Restrict the panel laws to the dominated QMD family \(\{\mathbb P_{n,\eta}:\eta\in\mathcal K\}\), and use the common parameter map
\[
 \psi_n(\eta)=
 \left(((\sqrt n\,s_{j,n})_{j\in\mathcal J},
        (b_{j,n})_{j\in\mathcal J},\Omega_n)\right).
\]
Let \(\Gamma(\eta)\) denote the principal score-coordinate submatrix of \(\Omega\). The first-order comparison experiment observes \((X^{\mathrm{sc}},b)\), where \(X^{\mathrm{sc}}\sim N(h,\Gamma(\eta))\) and \(b\) is observed exactly. The permitted transfer route compares the reduced statistic
\[
 \left(((\sqrt n\,\widehat s_{j,n})_{j\in\mathcal J},
        (\widehat b_{j,n})_{j\in\mathcal J})\right)
\]
directly with \((X^{\mathrm{sc}},b)\) under \(\sup_{\eta\in\mathcal K}\lVert\psi_n(\eta)-\eta\rVert\to0\), for any fixed norm on the finite-dimensional product parameter space. It targets convergence of expectations for uniformly bounded families of losses having a common modulus of continuity in the observation. An unbounded diameter loss must additionally satisfy uniform integrability, and a discontinuous coverage event needs separate boundary control. This handle does not construct two-sided Markov kernels and does not assert total-variation or Le Cam deficiency equivalence between \(\mathcal E_n^{\mathrm{mean}}\) and exact observation of continuously varying \(b\); consistency of \((\widehat b_{j,n})_{j\in\mathcal J}\) yields weak first-order risk approximation, not exact recovery in total variation. If effect-coordinate Gaussian noise is required in the limit, those coordinates must instead be localized on the root-\(n\) scale.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \(\widehat\theta_n\); \(\psi_n\); \(\mathcal K\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:pilot-adaptive-hull]\label{def:pilot-adaptive-hull}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Independent-\allowbreak{}pilot adaptive residual-\allowbreak{}effect hull}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Fix deterministic sequences \(m_n\in\mathbb N\) and \(a_n>0\) satisfying \(m_n\to\infty\), \(m_n/n\to0\), \(a_n\to\infty\), and \(a_n/\sqrt{m_n}\to0\).  Because \(R\) is fixed and \(n_r/n\to\lambda_r>0\), \(m_n<n_r\) for every \(r\in\mathcal R\) at all sufficiently large indices.  At such an index, use observations \(1,\ldots,m_n\) from every group as the pilot sample and the remaining observations as the main sample, and set
\[
 \widehat\mu_{r,n}^{\mathrm P}
 =m_n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}W_{ri},
 \qquad
 \widehat\mu_{r,n}^{\mathrm M}
 =(n_r-m_n)^{-1}\sum_{i=m_n+1}^{n_r}W_{ri}.
\]
Stack the group means in the same order as \(\widehat\mu_n\), and define
\[
 \widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm P}=A\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm P},
 \qquad
 \widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm M}=A\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm M}.
\]
Let \(\widehat s_{j,n}^{\mathrm P}\) be the first \(q\) coordinates of \(E_j\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm P}\).  Apply the shared-group covariance formula to the main sample, with divisor \(n_r-m_n\) in each centered within-group covariance and multiplier \(n/(n_r-m_n)\), and apply the same eigenvalue clipping to \([\underline\omega/2,2\overline\omega]\); denote the result by \(\widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm M}\).

For every \(\Omega\in\mathbb S_{++}^p\), partition
\[
 E_j\Omega E_j^{\top}
 =\begin{pmatrix}V_j(\Omega)&u_j(\Omega)\\
 u_j(\Omega)^{\top}&v_j(\Omega)\end{pmatrix}
\]
and define the residual-effect row and its standard deviation by
\[
 r_j(\Omega)=\bigl(-u_j(\Omega)^{\top}V_j(\Omega)^{-1},1\bigr)E_j,
 \qquad
 \sigma_j^2(\Omega)=r_j(\Omega)\Omega r_j(\Omega)^{\top}.
\]
Positive definiteness makes \(V_j(\Omega)\) invertible and its Schur complement \(\sigma_j^2(\Omega)\) strictly positive.  For every nonempty \(S\subseteq\mathcal J\), let
\[
 k_{1-\alpha}(S,\Omega)
 =\inf\left\{x\ge0:
 \Pr\left(
   \max_{j\in S}\frac{|r_j(\Omega)Z|}{\sigma_j(\Omega)}\le x
 \right)\ge1-\alpha\right\},
 \qquad Z\sim N(0,\Omega).
\]
Define the raw pilot screen \(S_n^0=\{j\in\mathcal J:\sqrt{m_n}\|\widehat s_{j,n}^{\mathrm P}\|_2\le a_n\}\) and the nonempty retained set
\[
 \widehat{\mathcal S}_n=
 \begin{cases}
 S_n^0,&S_n^0\ne\varnothing,\\
 \mathcal J,&S_n^0=\varnothing.
 \end{cases}
\]
For \(j\in\widehat{\mathcal S}_n\), put
\[
 \widehat t_{j,n}^{\mathrm M}
 =r_j(\widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm M})\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm M},
 \qquad
 I_{j,n}^{\mathrm{ad}}(\alpha)
 =\left[
 \widehat t_{j,n}^{\mathrm M}
 \mathbin{\pm}n^{-1/2}\sigma_j(\widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm M})
 k_{1-\alpha}(\widehat{\mathcal S}_n,\widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm M})
 \right],
\]
and put \(I_{j,n}^{\mathrm{ad}}(\alpha)=\varnothing\) otherwise.  The pilot-adaptive hull is
\[
 C_n^{\mathrm{ad}}(\alpha)
 =\operatorname{conv}\left(\bigcup_{j\in\mathcal J}
 I_{j,n}^{\mathrm{ad}}(\alpha)\right).
\]
At the finitely many indices for which some \(m_n\ge n_r\), define \(C_n^{\mathrm{ad}}(\alpha)=C_n^{\mathrm{proj}}(\alpha)\).  This convention has no asymptotic effect.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \(W_{ri}\); \(n_r\); \texttt{A}; \(m_n\); \(a_n\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:bounded-score-face-space]\label{def:bounded-score-face-space}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} Bounded-spread score-only donor-face space \(\mathcal H_{H,B}^{\mathrm{sc}}\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \[
 \mathcal H_{H,B}^{\mathrm{sc}}
 =\left\{(h,b)\in(\mathbb R^q)^J\times\mathbb R^J:
 \min_{j\in\mathcal J}\lVert h_j\rVert_2=0,\ 
 \max_{j\in\mathcal J}\lVert h_j\rVert_2\leq H,\ 
 \max_{j\in\mathcal J}b_j-\min_{j\in\mathcal J}b_j\leq B,\ 
 h_j=h_k=0\Rightarrow b_j=b_k
 \right\}.
\]
Only the spread of \(b\) is bounded; no restriction is placed on its absolute location.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Member properties.} \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}local-\allowbreak{}zero-\allowbreak{}face}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}local-\allowbreak{}face-\allowbreak{}coherence}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:score-face-minimax-risk]\label{def:score-face-minimax-risk}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Score-\allowbreak{}only honest expected-\allowbreak{}diameter frontier}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} In the score-only experiment one observes \((X^{\mathrm{sc}},b)\), where \(X^{\mathrm{sc}}\sim N(h,\Gamma)\) and \(b\) is observed without noise. Define
\[
 \mathfrak R_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{sc}}(H,B,\Gamma)
 =\inf_K\sup_{(h,b)\in\mathcal H_{H,B}^{\mathrm{sc}}}
 \mathbb E_{h,\Gamma}
 \left[\operatorname{diam}(K(X^{\mathrm{sc}},b))\right],
\]
where the infimum is over measurable confidence kernels
\(K:\mathbb R^{Jq}\times\mathbb R^J\rightsquigarrow2^{\mathbb R}\) satisfying
\[
 \inf_{(h,b)\in\mathcal H_{H,B}^{\mathrm{sc}}}
 \Pr_{h,\Gamma}\!\left\{\tau(h,b)\in K(X^{\mathrm{sc}},b)\right\}
 \geq1-\alpha.
\]
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{H}; \(B\); \(\Gamma\); \(\mathcal H_{H,B}^{\mathrm{sc}}\).
\end{definition}

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:exact-score-transport]\label{lem:exact-score-transport}
For every triangular array \(\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M\), every compatible causal extension array \((P_{r,n}^{\star})_{r\in\mathcal R,n\ge1}\) satisfying ass:consistency and ass:additive-untreated-change, every \(n\ge1\), and every \(j\in\mathcal J\), iterated expectations and the prespecified predictor-score construction imply \(b_{j,n}-\tau_n=\beta_n^{\top}s_{j,n}\). Therefore every \(j\in\mathcal C_n\) satisfies \(b_{j,n}=\tau_n\), and all such compatible extensions and all labels on a multiple-close-donor face identify the same ATT.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M\), a compatible extension array satisfying \(\mathrm{ass:consistency}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:additive\text{-}untreated\text{-}change}\), an index \(n\), and a label \(j\).  All expectations below exist: the observable coordinates have finite fourth moments by \(\mathrm{ass:uniform\text{-}fourth\text{-}moment}\), and the conditional expectation in \(\mathrm{ass:additive\text{-}untreated\text{-}change}\) is defined for an integrable untreated change.  Consistency and the definitions of \(b_{j,n}\) and \(\tau_n\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 b_{j,n}-\tau_n
 &=\sum_{r\in\mathcal T}\pi_r
     \mathbb E_{P_{r,n}^{\star}}[Y_{ri}(1)-Y_{ri}^{\mathrm{pre}}]
   -\sum_{r\in\mathcal R\setminus\mathcal T}\omega_{jr}
     \mathbb E_{P_{r,n}^{\star}}[Y_{ri}(0)-Y_{ri}^{\mathrm{pre}}]\\
 &\quad-
   \sum_{r\in\mathcal T}\pi_r
     \mathbb E_{P_{r,n}^{\star}}[Y_{ri}(1)-Y_{ri}(0)]\\
 &=\sum_{r\in\mathcal T}\pi_r
     \mathbb E_{P_{r,n}^{\star}}[Y_{ri}(0)-Y_{ri}^{\mathrm{pre}}]
   -\sum_{r\in\mathcal R\setminus\mathcal T}\omega_{jr}
     \mathbb E_{P_{r,n}^{\star}}[Y_{ri}(0)-Y_{ri}^{\mathrm{pre}}].
\end{aligned}
\]
By iterated expectations and \(\mathrm{ass:additive\text{-}untreated\text{-}change}\), for every group \(r\),
\[
 \mathbb E_{P_{r,n}^{\star}}[Y_{ri}(0)-Y_{ri}^{\mathrm{pre}}]
 =\mathbb E_{P_{r,n}^{\star}}[\kappa_n+\beta_n^{\top}G_{ri}]
 =\kappa_n+\beta_n^{\top}g_{r,n}.
\]
The treated weights and each donor stack both sum to one.  Hence the two intercept terms cancel and the preceding display becomes
\[
 b_{j,n}-\tau_n
 =\beta_n^{\top}\left(
   \sum_{r\in\mathcal T}\pi_rg_{r,n}
   -\sum_{r\in\mathcal R\setminus\mathcal T}\omega_{jr}g_{r,n}
  \right)
 =\beta_n^{\top}s_{j,n}.
\]
If \(j\in\mathcal C_n\), then \(s_{j,n}=0\) by definition, so \(b_{j,n}=\tau_n\).  This conclusion holds for every compatible extension satisfying the two declared causal assumptions.  Since \(b_{j,n}\) is fixed by the observable law, every such extension has the same \(\tau_n\); and if several labels lie in \(\mathcal C_n\), each of their observable contrasts equals that common value.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This lemma derives the load-bearing causal coherence from observable score content and an explicit untreated-change law instead of assuming the identifying conclusion. \textit{Gap.} Exact pre-balance is often invoked as a design heuristic; the stated identity records the precise additive transport restriction under which it identifies every close-label contrast as the same ATT. \textit{Consumer.} It defines a falsifiable score-content requirement for a new comparison-state analysis; it does not validate the historical CMS estimates from ranked near-balance.

\begin{lemma}[lem:joint-gaussian]\label{lem:joint-gaussian}
As \(n\to\infty\), \(\sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M}d_{\mathrm{Pr}}(\mathcal L_{\mathbb P_n}(\sqrt n(\widehat\theta_n-\theta_n)),N(0,\Omega_n))\to0\) and \(\sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M}\mathbb E_{\mathbb P_n}[\min\{1,\|\widehat\Omega_n-\Omega_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}\}]\to0\). The formula for \(\Omega_n\) retains every covariance induced by a treated group or control group reused across donor labels.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write the fixed matrix in group blocks as \(A=(A_1,\ldots,A_R)\), where \(A_r\in\mathbb R^{p\times d}\), and put \(X_{ri}=W_{ri}-\mu_{r,n}\).  Independence across all sampled micro units under \(\mathbb P_n\) and Definition \(\mathrm{def:stacked\text{-}estimator}\) give
\[
 S_n:=\sqrt n(\widehat\theta_n-\theta_n)
 =\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}\sum_{i=1}^{n_r}
     \frac{\sqrt n}{n_r}A_rX_{ri},
\qquad
 \operatorname{Var}_{\mathbb P_n}(S_n)
 =\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}\frac n{n_r}A_r\Sigma_{r,n}A_r^{\top}
 =\Omega_n.
\]
Thus shared treated or donor groups generate the off-diagonal label covariances in \(\Omega_n\); no independence across labels has been imposed.

We first prove the Gaussian approximation uniformly.  By \(\mathrm{ass:stable\text{-}fractions}\), there are constants \(a_r>0\) and an index \(n_0\), independent of the laws in \(\mathfrak M\), such that \(n_r\ge a_rn\) for \(n\ge n_0\).  The fourth-moment assumption implies
\(
 \mathbb E\|X_{ri}\|_2^3\le M_4^{3/4}
\).
Since \(\lambda_{\min}(\Omega_n)\ge\underline\omega\), the standardized independent summands
\[
 \zeta_{ri,n}=\Omega_n^{-1/2}\frac{\sqrt n}{n_r}A_rX_{ri}
\]
satisfy, for a finite constant \(C\) depending only on the fixed design, the dimension, the sampling fractions, \(M_4\), and \(\underline\omega\),
\[
 \sum_{r\in\mathcal R}\sum_{i=1}^{n_r}
   \mathbb E\|\zeta_{ri,n}\|_2^3
 \le C\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}\frac{n^{3/2}}{n_r^2}
 \le Cn^{-1/2}.
\tag{1}
\]
Moreover their covariance matrices sum to \(I_p\).  For completeness, (1) yields the central limit conclusion directly.  For every fixed \(t\in\mathbb R^p\), Taylor's formula
\[
 \left|e^{ix}-1-ix+\frac{x^2}{2}\right|\le\frac{|x|^3}{6}
\]
shows that the logarithm of the characteristic function of
\(\sum_{r,i}\zeta_{ri,n}\) is
\[
 -\frac12\sum_{r,i}\mathbb E[(t^{\top}\zeta_{ri,n})^2]+o(1)
 =-\frac12\|t\|_2^2+o(1),
\]
where the remainder is uniform over \(\mathfrak M\) by (1).  The largest summand variance is also \(o(1)\), so the logarithm is well defined for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Hence every sequence of admissible rows has the standard Gaussian characteristic-function limit after standardization.

To obtain the displayed uniform Prokhorov assertion, argue by contradiction.  If it failed, one could select admissible rows and an \(varepsilon>0\) along which the Prokhorov distance stayed above \(\varepsilon\).  The eigenvalue bounds make \((\Omega_n)\) precompact, so a subsequence satisfies \(\Omega_n\to\Omega_{\star}\in\mathbb S_{++}^p\).  The characteristic-function calculation gives \(S_n\Rightarrow N(0,\Omega_{\star})\), while continuity of the Gaussian law in its covariance gives \(N(0,\Omega_n)\Rightarrow N(0,\Omega_{\star})\).  Both convergences are metrized by \(d_{\mathrm{Pr}}\), contradicting the selected lower bound.  Therefore
\[
 \sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M}
 d_{\mathrm{Pr}}\!\left(\mathcal L_{\mathbb P_n}(S_n),N(0,\Omega_n)\right)
 \longrightarrow0.
\]

It remains to verify covariance estimation.  For a fixed group, the identity
\[
 \widehat\Sigma_{r,n}
 =\frac1{n_r}\sum_{i=1}^{n_r}X_{ri}X_{ri}^{\top}
   -\overline X_{r,n}\overline X_{r,n}^{\top},
\qquad
 \overline X_{r,n}=\frac1{n_r}\sum_{i=1}^{n_r}X_{ri},
\]
and independence imply
\[
 \mathbb E\left\|
  \frac1{n_r}\sum_{i=1}^{n_r}(X_{ri}X_{ri}^{\top}-\Sigma_{r,n})
 \right\|_F^2\le\frac{C_dM_4}{n_r},
\qquad
 \mathbb E\|\overline X_{r,n}\overline X_{r,n}^{\top}\|_F
 =\frac{\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma_{r,n})}{n_r}
 \le\frac{\sqrt{M_4}}{n_r}.
\]
Consequently
\(
 \sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M}
 \mathbb E\|\widehat\Sigma_{r,n}-\Sigma_{r,n}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le Cn_r^{-1/2}
\).
Using Definition \(\mathrm{def:shared\text{-}covariance\text{-}estimator}\), the fixedness of \(A\) and \(R\), and boundedness of \(n/n_r\), we obtain
\[
 \sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M}
 \mathbb E\|\widehat\Omega_n-\Omega_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le C\max_{r\in\mathcal R}n_r^{-1/2}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Since \(\min\{1,x\}\le x\) for \(x\ge0\), this proves the second assertion.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This lemma replaces a many-control reference distribution by within-group micro-sample Gaussianity while preserving shared-control dependence. \textit{Gap.} Conley and Taber and Ferman and Pinto obtain few-treated inference from many control groups; the present covariance law uses fixed labels and growing micro samples within each primitive group. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies an estimable joint law for CMS comparisons that reuse treated populations and candidate comparison states across outcomes.

\begin{lemma}[lem:spectral-clipping-negligible]\label{lem:spectral-clipping-negligible}
As \(n\to\infty\), \(\sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M}\Pr_{\mathbb P_n}\{\widetilde\Omega_n\ne\widehat\Omega_n\}\to0\) and \(\sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M}\mathbb E_{\mathbb P_n}[\min\{1,\|\widetilde\Omega_n-\Omega_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}\}]\to0\). On every sample, \(\underline\omega/2\le\lambda_{\min}(\widetilde\Omega_n)\le\lambda_{\max}(\widetilde\Omega_n)\le2\overline\omega\); hence every label-block inverse, regression coefficient, Schur-complement residual variance, and Gaussian critical value used by the feasible hull is defined.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The construction \(\widehat\Omega_n=A\operatorname{diag}_{r\in\mathcal R}((n/n_r)\widehat\Sigma_{r,n})A^{\top}\) is symmetric and positive semidefinite.  By Weyl's elementary variational inequality,
\[
 \max_{1\le k\le p}
 |\lambda_k(\widehat\Omega_n)-\lambda_k(\Omega_n)|
 \le\|\widehat\Omega_n-\Omega_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}.
\tag{2}
\]
Indeed, (2) follows directly by applying
\(
 |x^{\top}(\widehat\Omega_n-\Omega_n)x|
 \le\|\widehat\Omega_n-\Omega_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\)
on the unit sphere to the min--max characterizations of the ordered eigenvalues.  On the event
\[
 \|\widehat\Omega_n-\Omega_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 <\min\{\underline\omega/2,\overline\omega\},
\]
the covariance regularity bounds and (2) place every eigenvalue of \(\widehat\Omega_n\) strictly inside \([\underline\omega/2,2\overline\omega]\).  Spectral clipping then changes no eigenvalue.  The covariance consistency in \(\mathrm{lem:joint\text{-}gaussian}\) and Markov's inequality therefore imply
\[
 \sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M}
 \Pr_{\mathbb P_n}\{\widetilde\Omega_n\ne\widehat\Omega_n\}
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
The triangle inequality gives
\[
 \min\{1,\|\widetilde\Omega_n-\Omega_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}\}
 \le
 \min\{1,\|\widehat\Omega_n-\Omega_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}\}
 +\mathbf 1\{\widetilde\Omega_n\ne\widehat\Omega_n\}.
\]
Taking expectations and suprema proves the second convergence.

On every sample the clipping formula itself gives
\[
 \frac{\underline\omega}{2}I_p
 \preceq\widetilde\Omega_n
 \preceq2\overline\omega I_p.
\]
Every principal block \(E_j\widetilde\Omega_nE_j^{\top}\) and its principal score block \(\widehat V_{j,n}\) are therefore positive definite.  The block factorization
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}\widehat V_{j,n}&\widehat u_{j,n}\\
 \widehat u_{j,n}^{\top}&\widehat v_{j,n}\end{pmatrix}
 =
 \begin{pmatrix}I_q&0\\
 \widehat u_{j,n}^{\top}\widehat V_{j,n}^{-1}&1\end{pmatrix}
 \begin{pmatrix}\widehat V_{j,n}&0\\0&\widehat\sigma_{j,n}^2\end{pmatrix}
 \begin{pmatrix}I_q&\widehat V_{j,n}^{-1}\widehat u_{j,n}\\0&1\end{pmatrix}
\]
then implies \(\widehat\sigma_{j,n}^2>0\).  Thus all inverses and regression coefficients exist.  Finally, for every positive definite covariance the maximum defining \(c_{1-\alpha}\) has a continuous distribution on \((0,\infty)\): its sublevel sets are dilations of the unit ball of a norm on \(\mathbb R^p\), and a nonsingular Gaussian has a positive continuous density.  Its \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile is consequently finite, positive, and well defined.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Spectral clipping makes the feasible confidence set a total procedure on every finite sample without changing its uniform first-order behavior. \textit{Gap.} Covariance-consistency arguments usually proceed on a high-probability nonsingularity event; this lemma exposes and controls the fallback needed by the donor-face procedure. \textit{Consumer.} Implementations can return a defined interval even for small or collinear within-group samples while retaining the stated asymptotic guarantees.

\begin{theorem}[thm:uniform-coverage]\label{thm:uniform-coverage}
For every \(\alpha\in(0,1)\), the spectrally clipped feasible construction satisfies \(\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M}\Pr_{\mathbb P_n}\{\tau_n\in C_n^{\mathrm{proj}}(\alpha)\}\ge1-\alpha\). The infimum ranges over the full exact-close triangular-array class, including fixed and \(n^{-1/2}\)-local score separations, every covariance array satisfying the eigenvalue bounds, unequal stable group sizes, and every multiple-close-donor face.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Set \(X_n=\sqrt n(\widehat\theta_n-\theta_n)\) and, for a positive definite matrix \(S\), define
\[
 T(x,S)=\max_{j\in\mathcal J}
 \left\|(E_jSE_j^{\top})^{-1/2}E_jx\right\|_2.
\]
The feasible simultaneous event is
\[
 \mathcal A_n=\{T(X_n,\widetilde\Omega_n)\le c_{1-\alpha}(\widetilde\Omega_n)\}.
\tag{3}
\]
We first show uniformly that \(\Pr(\mathcal A_n)\to1-\alpha\).  The covariance matrices lie in the compact set
\(
 \{S:\underline\omega I_p\preceq S\preceq\overline\omega I_p\}
\).
If \(S_m\to S\) in this set and \(G\sim N(0,I_p)\), then \(S_m^{1/2}G\to S^{1/2}G\) almost surely and
\[
 T(S_m^{1/2}G,S_m)\longrightarrow T(S^{1/2}G,S)
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
The limiting random variable has a continuous, strictly increasing distribution on \((0,\infty)\), because \(T(\cdot,S)\) is a norm and the Gaussian density is positive.  Hence its \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile is unique and
\[
 c_{1-\alpha}(S_m)\longrightarrow c_{1-\alpha}(S).
\tag{4}
\]
Now take any sequence of admissible rows.  From compactness, every subsequence has a further subsequence along which \(\Omega_n\to\Omega_\star\).  Lemmas \(\mathrm{lem:joint\text{-}gaussian}\) and \(\mathrm{lem:spectral\text{-}clipping\text{-}negligible}\), Slutsky's argument, and (4) give
\[
 (X_n,\widetilde\Omega_n,c_{1-\alpha}(\widetilde\Omega_n))
 \Rightarrow(Z,\Omega_\star,c_{1-\alpha}(\Omega_\star)),
 \qquad Z\sim N(0,\Omega_\star).
\]
The boundary event has probability zero, and the definition of the critical value yields
\[
 \Pr\{T(Z,\Omega_\star)\le c_{1-\alpha}(\Omega_\star)\}=1-\alpha.
\]
The subsequence principle therefore proves
\[
 \sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M}
 |\Pr_{\mathbb P_n}(\mathcal A_n)-(1-\alpha)|\longrightarrow0.
\tag{5}
\]

It remains to connect (3) to the causal target.  On \(\mathcal A_n\), simultaneously for every label \(j\),
\[
 n
 \begin{pmatrix}\widehat s_{j,n}-s_{j,n}\\
                 \widehat b_{j,n}-b_{j,n}
 \end{pmatrix}^{\!\top}
 \widehat\Omega_{j,n}^{-1}
 \begin{pmatrix}\widehat s_{j,n}-s_{j,n}\\
                 \widehat b_{j,n}-b_{j,n}
 \end{pmatrix}
 \le\widehat c_n^2.
\tag{6}
\]
For any close label, \(s_{j,n}=0\) by definition and \(b_{j,n}=\tau_n\) by \(\mathrm{lem:exact\text{-}score\text{-}transport}\).  Completing the square in (6), using the positive Schur complement established by \(\mathrm{lem:spectral\text{-}clipping\text{-}negligible}\), gives
\[
 Q_{j,n}
 +\frac n{\widehat\sigma_{j,n}^2}
 \left\{\tau_n-\left(\widehat b_{j,n}
 -\widehat\gamma_{j,n}^{\top}\widehat s_{j,n}\right)\right\}^2
 \le\widehat c_n^2.
\]
By Definition \(\mathrm{def:profile\text{-}intervals}\), this is exactly the assertion \(\tau_n\in I_{j,n}(\alpha)\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:nonempty\text{-}close\text{-}set}\) supplies at least one such label, so Definition \(\mathrm{def:projected\text{-}hull}\) implies
\(
 \mathcal A_n\subseteq\{\tau_n\in C_n^{\mathrm{proj}}(\alpha)\}
\).
Combining this inclusion with (5) proves the asserted uniform lower coverage.  The argument never restricts the number of zero scores, the rate of a nonzero score, the stable unequal group fractions, or any off-diagonal covariance permitted by the eigenvalue bounds.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The result gives a feasible honest baseline that propagates score uncertainty, post-outcome uncertainty, and dependence across labels in one inversion. \textit{Gap.} Berk et al. establish generic simultaneous post-selection coverage, while Andrews, Kitagawa, and McCloskey study winner selection; neither profiles a causal target defined by exact zero-score donor faces. \textit{Consumer.} CMS evaluators can report an ATT interval that remains valid when two or three comparison states are retained or replaced because of claims availability.

\begin{theorem}[thm:oracle-diameter]\label{thm:oracle-diameter}
For every fixed \(\delta>0\), \(\sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M(\delta)}\Pr_{\mathbb P_n}\{\exists j\in\mathcal J\setminus\mathcal C_n:I_{j,n}(\alpha)\ne\varnothing\}\to0\), and \(\lim_{\xi\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M(\delta)}\Pr_{\mathbb P_n}\{\sqrt n\operatorname{diam}(C_n^{\mathrm{proj}}(\alpha))>\xi\}=0\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(\delta>0\).  Spectral clipping gives the deterministic bounds
\[
 \lambda_{\max}(\widehat V_{j,n})\le2\overline\omega,
 \qquad
 Q_{j,n}\ge\frac n{2\overline\omega}
                 \|\widehat s_{j,n}\|_2^2.
\tag{32}
\]
There is a finite constant \(C_{\alpha,J,q}\), depending only on \(\alpha\), \(J\), and \(q\), such that \(\widehat c_n\le C_{\alpha,J,q}\) on every sample.  Indeed, under the Gaussian law defining \(\widehat c_n\), every standardized label block has the \(N(0,I_{q+1})\) distribution.  Therefore the union bound gives
\[
 \Pr\left\{
  \max_{j\in\mathcal J}
  \|(E_j\widetilde\Omega_nE_j^{\top})^{-1/2}E_jZ\|_2>x
 \right\}
 \le J\Pr\{\|N(0,I_{q+1})\|_2>x\},
\]
and the right-hand side is smaller than \(\alpha\) for all sufficiently large fixed \(x\).

Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:joint\text{-}gaussian}\) and the upper eigenvalue bound on \(\Omega_n\) imply
\[
 \lim_{M\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}
 \sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M}
 \Pr_{\mathbb P_n}\!\left\{
 \|\sqrt n(\widehat\theta_n-\theta_n)\|_2>M
 \right\}=0.
\tag{33}
\]
For \(\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M(\delta)\) and \(j\notin\mathcal C_n\), the definition of fixed separation gives \(\|s_{j,n}\|_2\ge\delta\).  On the event
\(
 \|\widehat s_{j,n}-s_{j,n}\|_2<\delta/2
\), equation (32) yields
\[
 Q_{j,n}\ge\frac{n\delta^2}{8\overline\omega}.
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\), this exceeds \(C_{\alpha,J,q}^2\), and Definition \(\mathrm{def:profile\text{-}intervals}\) then makes the profile empty.  A union bound over the fixed label set and (33) prove
\[
 \sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M(\delta)}
 \Pr_{\mathbb P_n}\{\exists j\in\mathcal J\setminus\mathcal C_n:
 I_{j,n}(\alpha)\ne\varnothing\}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{34}
\]

It remains to bound the close-label profiles.  The clipped covariance and its principal blocks satisfy, on every sample,
\[
 \|\widehat V_{j,n}^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le\frac2{\underline\omega},
 \quad
 \|\widehat u_{j,n}\|_2\le2\overline\omega,
 \quad
 \|\widehat\gamma_{j,n}\|_2
 \le G:=\frac{4\overline\omega}{\underline\omega},
 \quad
 0<\widehat\sigma_{j,n}^2\le2\overline\omega.
\tag{35}
\]
Strict positivity of the Schur complement follows from positive definiteness of the label block.  For \(j\in\mathcal C_n\), Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:exact\text{-}score\text{-}transport}\) gives \(s_{j,n}=0\) and \(b_{j,n}=\tau_n\).  Hence the center
\(
 \widehat b_{j,n}-\widehat\gamma_{j,n}^{\top}\widehat s_{j,n}
\)
satisfies
\[
 \sqrt n\left|
 \widehat b_{j,n}-\widehat\gamma_{j,n}^{\top}\widehat s_{j,n}-\tau_n
 \right|
 \le(1+G)\|E_j\sqrt n(\widehat\theta_n-\theta_n)\|_2.
\tag{36}
\]
Whenever its profile is nonempty, its half-length is at most
\[
 n^{-1/2}\widehat\sigma_{j,n}\widehat c_n
 \le n^{-1/2}\sqrt{2\overline\omega}\,C_{\alpha,J,q}.
\tag{37}
\]
On the event in (34) that every false-label profile is empty, every point of the projected hull lies within
\[
 n^{-1/2}\left\{
 (1+G)\|\sqrt n(\widehat\theta_n-\theta_n)\|_2
 +\sqrt{2\overline\omega}\,C_{\alpha,J,q}
 \right\}
\]
of \(\tau_n\), by (36)--(37) and convexity.  If the hull is empty its declared diameter is zero; otherwise its diameter is at most twice this radius.  Combining this bound with (33)--(34), and then sending \(\xi\to\infty\), proves
\[
 \lim_{\xi\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}
 \sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M(\delta)}
 \Pr_{\mathbb P_n}\{\sqrt n\operatorname{diam}
 (C_n^{\mathrm{proj}}(\alpha))>\xi\}=0.
\]
Together with (34), this is exactly the two-part claim.  Notice that the proof establishes root-\(n\) tightness only: the unchanged maximum critical value still ranges over all \(J\) labels, so this theorem by itself does not assert the ordinary one-label first-order constant.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Honesty near label collisions remains useful only if the original projected hull is at least root-\(n\) tight when nonclose donors are fixed away from zero. \textit{Gap.} The theorem removes false profiles and proves root-\(n\) diameter tightness, but its critical value still maximizes over all \(J\) labels; the separate pilot-adaptive theorem supplies oracle constants. \textit{Consumer.} Applications may use this theorem for a rate guarantee, but should use thm:pilot-adaptive-oracle-equivalence rather than this result to claim ordinary one-label first-order endpoints.

\begin{theorem}[thm:two-donor-minimax]\label{thm:two-donor-minimax}
In the explicitly two-dimensional observation experiment \(Z^{\mathrm{sw}}\), the minimum over all confidence kernels \(K:\mathbb R^2\rightsquigarrow2^{\{0,\Delta\}}\) satisfying \(\min_{\ell\in\{0,1\}}\Pr_{\ell}\{\ell\Delta\in K(Z^{\mathrm{sw}})\}\ge1-\alpha\) of \(\max_{\ell\in\{0,1\}}\mathbb E_{\ell}[\operatorname{diam}(K(Z^{\mathrm{sw}}))]\) is \(r_2(c,\Delta,\alpha)=\Delta[\Phi(z_{1-\alpha}-\sqrt2c)-\alpha]_+\). The rule \(C^{\mathrm{NP}}_{c,\Delta,\alpha}\) attains this value, including the zero-overlap regime. Under the \(J=2\), \(q=1\) panel submodel with \(b=(0,\Delta)^{\top}\), \(L_{\mathrm{sw}}\Omega L_{\mathrm{sw}}^{\top}=I_2\), and state-invariant independent \(U^{\mathrm{sw}}\), the full observation \(Z^{\mathrm{pan}}\) is equivalent to the switch experiment augmented by an ancillary variable, so the same minimax value and rule apply.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(P_0\) and \(P_1\) denote the two laws in Definition \(\mathrm{def:two\text{-}donor\text{-}switch}\).  Under \(P_0\), \(D\sim N(c,2)\), and under \(P_1\), \(D\sim N(-c,2)\).  Randomization, if used by a confidence kernel, may be represented by adjoining an independent uniform variable having the same law in both states.  Let
\[
 A_0=\{0\in K\},\qquad A_1=\{\Delta\in K\},
 \qquad B=A_0\cap A_1.
\]
Coverage says \(P_0(A_0)\ge1-\alpha\) and \(P_1(A_1)\ge1-\alpha\).  Since a subset of \(\{0,\Delta\}\) has diameter \(\Delta\) exactly on \(B\), its state-\(\ell\) risk is \(\Delta P_\ell(B)\).

We require one elementary likelihood-ratio bound.  The density ratio of \(D\) under \(P_0\) to that under \(P_1\) is \(\exp(cD)\), which is increasing in \(D\).  Consequently, among events \(R\) with \(P_1(R)\le\alpha\), the largest \(P_0(R)\) is attained by the upper tail
\(
 R=\{D>-c+\sqrt2z_{1-\alpha}\}
\).
This follows without an external theorem: subtract the indicator of that tail from \(\mathbf1_R\), multiply by the density difference \(f_0-kf_1\) whose sign changes once at the threshold, and integrate; the size constraint makes the remaining \(f_1\)-term nonpositive.  Interchanging the states gives the analogous lower-tail result.  Both cross-probabilities equal
\[
 \Phi(\sqrt2c-z_{1-\alpha}).
\tag{13}
\]

Put \(R_0=A_0^c\) and \(R_1=A_1^c\).  Thus \(P_0(R_0)\le\alpha\) and \(P_1(R_1)\le\alpha\).  The union bound and (13) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P_0(B)+P_1(B)
 &\ge2-P_0(R_0)-P_0(R_1)-P_1(R_0)-P_1(R_1)\\
 &\ge2-2\alpha-2\Phi(\sqrt2c-z_{1-\alpha})\\
 &=2\{\Phi(z_{1-\alpha}-\sqrt2c)-\alpha\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since probabilities are nonnegative,
\[
 \max_{\ell\in\{0,1\}}P_\ell(B)
 \ge[\Phi(z_{1-\alpha}-\sqrt2c)-\alpha]_+.
\tag{14}
\]

For the declared rule \(C^{\mathrm{NP}}_{c,\Delta,\alpha}\),
\[
 A_0=\{D\ge c-\sqrt2z_{1-\alpha}\},
 \qquad
 A_1=\{D\le-c+\sqrt2z_{1-\alpha}\}.
\]
Each has coverage exactly \(1-\alpha\) in its own state.  If the two half-lines overlap, their intersection is the interval between the displayed thresholds and, under either state,
\[
 P_\ell(B)=\Phi(z_{1-\alpha}-\sqrt2c)-\Phi(-z_{1-\alpha})
 =\Phi(z_{1-\alpha}-\sqrt2c)-\alpha.
\]
If the half-lines do not overlap, \(B=\varnothing\), and the positive-part expression is zero.  Thus the rule attains (14), proving
\[
 r_2(c,\Delta,\alpha)
 =\Delta[\Phi(z_{1-\alpha}-\sqrt2c)-\alpha]_+.
\]

In the embedded panel submodel, \(Z^{\mathrm{pan}}\) is measurably equivalent to \((Z^{\mathrm{sw}},U^{\mathrm{sw}})\).  A switch-experiment rule can ignore \(U^{\mathrm{sw}}\).  Conversely, integrating any full-data randomized rule over the independent, state-invariant law of \(U^{\mathrm{sw}}\) produces a randomized rule based only on \(Z^{\mathrm{sw}}\) with exactly the same two coverages and risks.  The ancillary coordinate therefore cannot lower the minimax value, proving the final assertion.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The identity-covariance result is the \(\rho=\sqrt2c\) specialization of the Mahalanobis theorem, together with the already proved ancillary-coordinate panel equivalence. \textit{Gap.} The two-donor switch is solved; it does not imply that a maximum-critical hull is optimal on larger face unions. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies the exact early-kill-test value and the benchmark used by the maximum-hull comparison.

\begin{proposition}[prop:maximum-hull-gap]\label{prop:maximum-hull-gap}
In the independent two-score switch experiment, the explicitly defined Gaussian donor-face benchmark \(K_{\max,\alpha,\Delta}\) satisfies \(\min_{\ell\in\{0,1\}}\Pr_{\ell}\{\ell\Delta\in K_{\max,\alpha,\Delta}(Z^{\mathrm{sw}})\}\ge1-\alpha\). Its worst-state expected diameter is \(r_{\max}(c,\Delta,\alpha)=\Delta(2\Phi(k_{\alpha})-1)[\Phi(k_{\alpha}-c)-\Phi(-k_{\alpha}-c)]\), where \((2\Phi(k_{\alpha})-1)^2=1-\alpha\). At \(\alpha=0.05\), \(c=1\), and \(\Delta=0.4\), \(r_2=0.2165\) whereas \(r_{\max}=0.3475\), so the Gaussian maximum benchmark is not minimax. Under the state-invariant independent \(U^{\mathrm{sw}}\) factorization in def:two-donor-switch, the same benchmark and risk embed in the full panel Gaussian observation.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Under state \(0\), the target \(0\) is included by \(K_{\max,\alpha,\Delta}\) exactly when \(|Z_1^{\mathrm{sw}}|\le k_\alpha\); under state \(1\), the target \(\Delta\) is included exactly when \(|Z_2^{\mathrm{sw}}|\le k_\alpha\).  The relevant coordinate is standard normal in each case, so both coverage probabilities equal
\[
 2\Phi(k_\alpha)-1=\sqrt{1-\alpha}\ge1-\alpha.
\]
The output has diameter \(\Delta\) exactly when both endpoint labels are included.  Under state \(0\), independence of the two score coordinates and their respective means \(0\) and \(c\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_0[\operatorname{diam}(K_{\max,\alpha,\Delta}(Z^{\mathrm{sw}}))]
 &=\Delta\Pr_0\{|Z_1^{\mathrm{sw}}|\le k_\alpha,
                    |Z_2^{\mathrm{sw}}|\le k_\alpha\}\\
 &=\Delta(2\Phi(k_\alpha)-1)
   [\Phi(k_\alpha-c)-\Phi(-k_\alpha-c)].
\end{aligned}
\]
State \(1\) gives the same expression after interchanging the coordinates.  This proves the formula for \(r_{\max}\).

At \(\alpha=0.05\),
\[
 z_{0.95}=1.6448536269,
 \qquad
 k_{0.05}=2.2364766446.
\]
Substitution into the formulas just proved and into \(\mathrm{thm:two\text{-}donor\text{-}minimax}\), with \(c=1\) and \(\Delta=0.4\), gives
\[
 r_2=0.2164811121,
 \qquad
 r_{\max}=0.3474748572.
\]
Thus the maximum-critical benchmark is strictly suboptimal.  Under the factorization in Definition \(\mathrm{def:two\text{-}donor\text{-}switch}\), applying this same kernel to \(L_{\mathrm{sw}}Z^{\mathrm{pan}}\) and ignoring \(U^{\mathrm{sw}}\) leaves its coverage and risk unchanged, proving the embedding claim.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The proposition executes the early kill test with a fully specified score-only confidence kernel and rules out its minimax optimality. \textit{Gap.} Berk et al. emphasize the insurance cost of simultaneity qualitatively; this calculation gives the exact cost in the donor-switch experiment. \textit{Consumer.} It tells method developers how much expected diameter remains available to a face-adaptive replacement.

\begin{proposition}[prop:single-label-reduction]\label{prop:single-label-reduction}
For every declared score dimension \(q\in\mathbb N\), when \(J=1\), \(C_n^{\mathrm{proj}}(\alpha)\) equals the profile of the ordinary \(q+1\)-dimensional Gaussian Wald ellipsoid under the restriction \(s_{1,n}=0\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(q\in\mathbb N\) and suppose \(J=1\).  Write
\[
 \widehat\vartheta_{1,n}=E_1\widehat\theta_n
   =\begin{pmatrix}\widehat s_{1,n}\\ \widehat b_{1,n}\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 S_{1,n}=E_1\widetilde\Omega_nE_1^{\top}
   =\begin{pmatrix}
      \widehat V_{1,n}&\widehat u_{1,n}\\
      \widehat u_{1,n}^{\top}&\widehat v_{1,n}
    \end{pmatrix}.
\]
By the declared definition and type of the clipped label-block covariance, \(S_{1,n}=\widehat\Omega_{1,n}\in\mathbb S_{++}^{q+1}\).  Consequently its principal block \(\widehat V_{1,n}\) is positive definite and hence invertible, and its Schur complement
\[
 \widehat\sigma_{1,n}^{2}
   =\widehat v_{1,n}
     -\widehat u_{1,n}^{\top}\widehat V_{1,n}^{-1}
        \widehat u_{1,n}
\]
is strictly positive.  Thus every division and inverse below is well defined.

Consider the ordinary feasible \((q+1)\)-dimensional Wald ellipsoid in the coordinates selected by \(E_1\),
\[
 \mathcal E_{1,n}(\alpha)
 =\left\{(s,\beta)\in\mathbb R^q\times\mathbb R:
 n\begin{pmatrix}\widehat s_{1,n}-s\\
                   \widehat b_{1,n}-\beta
   \end{pmatrix}^{\!\top}
 S_{1,n}^{-1}
 \begin{pmatrix}\widehat s_{1,n}-s\\
                 \widehat b_{1,n}-\beta
 \end{pmatrix}
 \le \widehat c_n^2\right\}.
\]
This uses the same feasible covariance and critical value as Definition \(\mathrm{def:profile\text{-}intervals}\).  Indeed, when \(J=1\), the maximum in the definition of \(c_{1-\alpha}(\widetilde\Omega_n)\) contains only the label \(1\).  Conditional on the positive-definite matrix \(\widetilde\Omega_n\), the standardized vector
\[
 (E_1\widetilde\Omega_nE_1^{\top})^{-1/2}E_1Z
\]
has the \(N(0,I_{q+1})\) law when \(Z\sim N(0,\widetilde\Omega_n)\).  Hence \(\widehat c_n\) is exactly the ordinary Gaussian Wald critical value for this ellipsoid.

We now profile \(\mathcal E_{1,n}(\alpha)\) under the restriction \(s=0\).  Put
\[
 x=\widehat s_{1,n},\qquad
 y=\widehat b_{1,n}-\beta,\qquad
 \widehat\gamma_{1,n}=\widehat V_{1,n}^{-1}\widehat u_{1,n}.
\]
Direct block Gaussian elimination, equivalently multiplication of the usual block-inverse formula by \((x^{\top},y)^{\top}\), gives the identity
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}x\\y\end{pmatrix}^{\!\top}
 S_{1,n}^{-1}
 \begin{pmatrix}x\\y\end{pmatrix}
 =x^{\top}\widehat V_{1,n}^{-1}x
  +\frac{(y-\widehat u_{1,n}^{\top}
                    \widehat V_{1,n}^{-1}x)^2}
         {\widehat\sigma_{1,n}^{2}}.
\]
For completeness, this follows by the factorization
\[
 S_{1,n}
 =\begin{pmatrix}I_q&0\\
       \widehat u_{1,n}^{\top}\widehat V_{1,n}^{-1}&1
   \end{pmatrix}
   \begin{pmatrix}\widehat V_{1,n}&0\\0&\widehat\sigma_{1,n}^{2}\end{pmatrix}
   \begin{pmatrix}I_q&\widehat V_{1,n}^{-1}\widehat u_{1,n}\\0&1
   \end{pmatrix},
\]
so no external matrix result is being invoked.  By the definitions of \(Q_{1,n}\) and \(\widehat\gamma_{1,n}\), the restricted ellipsoid inequality is therefore equivalent to
\[
 Q_{1,n}
 +\frac{n}{\widehat\sigma_{1,n}^{2}}
  \left\{\beta-
  \bigl(\widehat b_{1,n}
        -\widehat\gamma_{1,n}^{\top}\widehat s_{1,n}\bigr)
  \right\}^{2}
 \le \widehat c_n^2.
\]
If \(Q_{1,n}>\widehat c_n^2\), this inequality has no solution in \(\beta\), so its profile is empty.  If \(Q_{1,n}\le\widehat c_n^2\), solving the inequality gives
\[
 \left[
 \widehat b_{1,n}-\widehat\gamma_{1,n}^{\top}\widehat s_{1,n}
 \;\mathbin{\pm}\;
 n^{-1/2}\widehat\sigma_{1,n}
       (\widehat c_n^2-Q_{1,n})^{1/2}
 \right].
\]
In the two cases this is exactly \(I_{1,n}(\alpha)\) by Definition \(\mathrm{def:profile\text{-}intervals}\).

Finally, Definition \(\mathrm{def:projected\text{-}hull}\) and \(\mathcal J=\{1\}\) yield
\[
 C_n^{\mathrm{proj}}(\alpha)
   =\operatorname{conv}\bigl(I_{1,n}(\alpha)\bigr).
\]
A nonempty \(I_{1,n}(\alpha)\) is an interval and is therefore convex, while the stipulated convex hull of the empty set is empty.  Thus
\[
 C_n^{\mathrm{proj}}(\alpha)=I_{1,n}(\alpha),
\]
which is exactly the profile of the ordinary \((q+1)\)-dimensional Gaussian Wald ellipsoid under \(s_{1,n}=0\), as claimed.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This reduction verifies that the construction nests standard inference when label ambiguity disappears. \textit{Gap.} The reduction is a required coherence check rather than a claimed departure from standard Wald theory. \textit{Consumer.} Software can test the implementation against ordinary one-label regression output.

\begin{theorem}[thm:general-gaussian-frontier-infinite]\label{thm:general-gaussian-frontier-infinite}
Suppose \(J\ge2\), \(H>0\), \(\Omega\in\mathbb S_{++}^p\), and \(0<\alpha<1/2\).  For the frozen local face space, whose effect vector \(b\) is unrestricted, \(\mathfrak R_{\alpha}^{G}(H,\Omega)=+\infty\).  More precisely, there are two donor-switch faces at any sufficiently small score separation \(c\in(0,H]\) such that every honest confidence kernel has worst-face expected diameter at least \(\Delta[1-2\alpha-v_c]\) for every \(\Delta>0\), where \(v_c=2\Phi(\rho_c/2)-1\), \(\rho_c\to0\) as \(c\downarrow0\), is the total-variation distance between the two Gaussian laws.  Thus the least-favorable two-point sequence escapes through \(\Delta\to\infty\), and the requested finite-valued frontier and finite attaining program do not exist on \(\mathcal H_H\) as defined; a finite formulation must additionally bound the effect spread or otherwise restrict \(b\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose distinct labels \(1,2\in\mathcal J\), which is possible because \(J\geq2\), and choose a unit vector \(e\in\mathbb R^q\), which is possible because \(q\geq1\). Fix \(c\in(0,H]\). Define
\[
 h^{(0)}_1=0,\qquad h^{(0)}_j=ce\quad(j\ne1),
\]
and
\[
 h^{(1)}_2=0,\qquad h^{(1)}_j=ce\quad(j\ne2).
\]
For every \(\Delta>0\), use the same effect vector at both states:
\[
 b^{(\Delta)}_1=0,\qquad b^{(\Delta)}_2=\Delta,
 \qquad b^{(\Delta)}_j=0\quad(j\geq3).
\]
Each \((h^{(\ell)},b^{(\Delta)})\), \(\ell\in\{0,1\}\), belongs to \(\mathcal H_H\). Indeed, every score norm is either zero or \(c\leq H\), each state has exactly one zero-score label, and hence the coherence implication for two zero-score labels is vacuous. By the definition of \(\tau(h,b)\),
\[
 \tau(h^{(0)},b^{(\Delta)})=0,
 \qquad
 \tau(h^{(1)},b^{(\Delta)})=\Delta.
 \tag{1}
\]

Let \(m_{\ell,c,\Delta}\in\mathbb R^p\) be the interleaved mean of \(Z^{\mathrm{pan}}\) under state \(\ell\), and set
\[
 d_c=m_{1,c,\Delta}-m_{0,c,\Delta},
 \qquad
 \rho_c=(d_c^{\top}\Omega^{-1}d_c)^{1/2}.
 \tag{2}
\]
Positive definiteness of \(\Omega\) makes \(\Omega^{-1}\) and \(\Omega^{-1/2}\) well defined. Because the effect vector is identical at the two states, all effect-coordinate differences cancel. The first two score-block differences are respectively \(ce\) and \(-ce\), and all remaining score-block differences are zero. Thus \(d_c=cd_1\) for a fixed nonzero vector \(d_1\), so
\[
 \rho_c=c(d_1^{\top}\Omega^{-1}d_1)^{1/2}\longrightarrow0
 \qquad(c\downarrow0).
 \tag{3}
\]
In particular, \(\rho_c\) is independent of \(\Delta\).

Write \(P_{\ell,c,\Delta}=N(m_{\ell,c,\Delta},\Omega)\). Both laws have positive Lebesgue densities. For \(\rho_c>0\), transform an observation \(z\) to
\[
 x=\Omega^{-1/2}\left(z-\frac{m_{0,c,\Delta}+m_{1,c,\Delta}}2\right),
 \qquad
 u=\frac{\Omega^{-1/2}d_c}{\rho_c}.
\]
Then \(\lVert u\rVert_2=1\), the likelihood ratio is at least one exactly when \(u^{\top}x\geq0\), and \(u^{\top}x\) is \(N(-\rho_c/2,1)\) under state zero and \(N(\rho_c/2,1)\) under state one. Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \lVert P_{0,c,\Delta}-P_{1,c,\Delta}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
 &=P_{1,c,\Delta}\{u^{\top}x\geq0\}
   -P_{0,c,\Delta}\{u^{\top}x\geq0\}\\
 &=\Phi(\rho_c/2)-\Phi(-\rho_c/2)
 =2\Phi(\rho_c/2)-1
 =:v_c.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]

Let \(K\) be any measurable confidence kernel satisfying the honesty constraint in the definition of \(\mathfrak R_{\alpha}^{G}(H,\Omega)\). Let \(Q_{\ell,c,\Delta}\) be the joint law of the observation and the reported random set after applying \(K\) to \(P_{\ell,c,\Delta}\). A Markov kernel contracts total variation: for every measurable event \(A\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |Q_{1,c,\Delta}(A)-Q_{0,c,\Delta}(A)|
 &=\left|\int K(z,A_z)\,d(P_{1,c,\Delta}-P_{0,c,\Delta})(z)\right|\\
 &\leq \lVert P_{1,c,\Delta}-P_{0,c,\Delta}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
 =v_c,
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
because \(0\leq K(z,A_z)\leq1\). Define
\[
 A_0=\{(z,C):0\in C\},
 \qquad
 A_1=\{(z,C):\Delta\in C\}.
\]
Membership of both states in \(\mathcal H_H\), honesty, and (1) imply
\[
 Q_{0,c,\Delta}(A_0)\geq1-\alpha,
 \qquad
 Q_{1,c,\Delta}(A_1)\geq1-\alpha.
 \tag{6}
\]
Equation (5) gives \(Q_{0,c,\Delta}(A_1)\geq1-\alpha-v_c\). Hence the union bound gives
\[
 Q_{0,c,\Delta}(A_0\cap A_1)
 \geq1-2\alpha-v_c.
 \tag{7}
\]
On \(A_0\cap A_1\), the reported set contains both \(0\) and \(\Delta\), so its diameter is at least \(\Delta\). Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sup_{(h,b)\in\mathcal H_H}
 \mathbb E_{h,b,\Omega}[\operatorname{diam}(K(Z^{\mathrm{pan}}))]
 &\geq
 \mathbb E_{h^{(0)},b^{(\Delta)},\Omega}
 [\operatorname{diam}(K(Z^{\mathrm{pan}}))]\\
 &\geq \Delta\,Q_{0,c,\Delta}(A_0\cap A_1)\\
 &\geq \Delta[1-2\alpha-v_c].
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
When the bracket is negative, the last inequality remains valid because diameter is nonnegative.

By (3), continuity of \(\Phi\), and \(\Phi(0)=1/2\), one has \(v_c\to0\) as \(c\downarrow0\). Since \(0<\alpha<1/2\) and \(H>0\), there is \(c_0\in(0,H]\) such that \(1-2\alpha-v_c>0\) for every \(c\in(0,c_0]\). Fix one such \(c\). Inequality (8) then holds for every \(\Delta>0\) with the same positive multiplier. The definition of \(\mathcal H_H\) imposes no bound on the spread of \(b\), so letting \(\Delta\to\infty\) shows that every honest kernel has infinite worst-case expected diameter. Taking the infimum over honest kernels yields
\[
 \mathfrak R_{\alpha}^{G}(H,\Omega)=+\infty.
\]
Thus neither a finite frontier nor a finite-valued attaining program exists on the frozen full Gaussian face space.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem identifies the precise maximality failure of the original Gaussian benchmark: unrestricted effect spread permits arbitrarily distant targets on score faces whose laws become arbitrarily close. \textit{Gap.} A finite frontier cannot exist on the frozen class; the bounded-spread score-only experiment is therefore a separate corrected problem rather than a reinterpretation of the infinite value. \textit{Consumer.} The obstruction determines which effect-coordinate scaling and spread restriction every valid weak-separation frontier must use.

\begin{theorem}[thm:restricted-transfer-scaling-obstruction]\label{thm:restricted-transfer-scaling-obstruction}
Assume
\[
 \sup_{\eta\in\mathcal K}\lVert\psi_n(\eta)-\eta\rVert\longrightarrow0,
\]
where \(\lVert\cdot\rVert\) is any fixed norm on the finite-dimensional product parameter space.
If \(\mathcal K\) contains \(\eta_0=(h,b^{(0)},\Omega)\) and \(\eta_1=(h,b^{(1)},\Omega)\) with the same \(h\) and \(\Omega\) but \(b^{(0)}\ne b^{(1)}\), then the reduced experiment \(\mathcal E_n^{\mathrm{mean}}\) is not asymptotically equivalent in Le Cam deficiency to the frozen Gaussian experiment \(Z^{\mathrm{pan}}\sim N((h,b),\Omega)\). More precisely, put
\[
 m_k=(h_1^{\top},b^{(k)}_1,\ldots,h_J^{\top},b^{(k)}_J)^{\top},
 \qquad
 v=2\Phi\!\left(\frac{\lVert\Omega^{-1/2}(m_1-m_0)\rVert_2}{2}\right)-1.
\]
Then \(v<1\), and the deficiency from the Gaussian experiment to \(\mathcal E_n^{\mathrm{mean}}\) has lower limit at least \((1-v)/2>0\). The obstruction is the scaling: \((\widehat b_{j,n})_{j\in\mathcal J}\) has covariance of order \(n^{-1}\) and consistently reveals \(b\), whereas the frozen Gaussian experiment retains nondegenerate noise in its effect coordinates.

There is nevertheless a direct first-order risk comparison with the score-only Gaussian law. Let \(\Gamma(\eta)\) be the principal score-coordinate submatrix of \(\Omega\), and under that law let \(X^{\mathrm{sc}}\sim N(h,\Gamma(\eta))\) while \(b\) is observed exactly. If \(\ell_\eta:\mathbb R^{Jq+J}\to\mathbb R\), \(\eta\in\mathcal K\), is any family satisfying
\[
 \sup_{\eta\in\mathcal K}\sup_x|\ell_\eta(x)|\leq M<\infty
\]
and
\[
 \omega(t):=
 \sup_{\eta\in\mathcal K}
 \sup_{\lVert x-y\rVert_2\leq t}
 |\ell_\eta(x)-\ell_\eta(y)|
 \longrightarrow0
 \quad(t\downarrow0),
\]
then
\[
 \sup_{\eta\in\mathcal K}
 \left|
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb P_{n,\eta}}
 \ell_\eta\!\left(((\sqrt n\,\widehat s_{j,n})_{j\in\mathcal J},
                    (\widehat b_{j,n})_{j\in\mathcal J})\right)
 -
 \mathbb E\ell_\eta(X^{\mathrm{sc}},b)
 \right|
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus exact observation of \(b\) is a faithful first-order benchmark for bounded uniformly equicontinuous decision-risk payoffs, not a claim of two-sided total-variation or Le Cam deficiency equivalence. Transferring an unbounded diameter loss additionally requires uniform integrability, and transferring a discontinuous coverage indicator requires separate boundary control. A Gaussian limit with noisy effect coordinates instead requires root-\(n\) localization of the effect coordinates.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write
\[
 \widehat b_n=(\widehat b_{1,n},\ldots,\widehat b_{J,n})^{\top},
 \qquad
 b_n=(b_{1,n},\ldots,b_{J,n})^{\top}.
\]
Let \((\Omega_n)_{bb}\) denote the principal submatrix of \(\Omega_n\) indexed by the \(J\) effect coordinates. Expectations and variances below are under \(\mathbb P_{n,\eta}\). By \(\mathrm{def:stacked\text{-}estimator}\), each group sample mean is unbiased. Independence across the primitive groups is part of \(\mathrm{def:panel\text{-}model\text{-}class}\). Hence the definition of \(\Omega_n\) in \(\mathrm{def:shared\text{-}covariance\text{-}estimator}\) gives the exact identities
\[
 \mathbb E_{n,\eta}[\widehat\theta_n]=\theta_n
\]
and
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Var}_{n,\eta}(\widehat\theta_n)
 &=A\operatorname{diag}_{r\in\mathcal R}
   \left(\frac{\Sigma_{r,n}}{n_r}\right)A^{\top}\\
 &=\frac1n A\operatorname{diag}_{r\in\mathcal R}
   \left(\frac n{n_r}\Sigma_{r,n}\right)A^{\top}
 =\frac{\Omega_n}{n}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{1}
\]
Taking the effect-coordinate principal block in (1) yields
\[
 \mathbb E_{n,\eta}[\widehat b_n]=b_n,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}_{n,\eta}(\widehat b_n)
 =\frac{(\Omega_n)_{bb}}{n}.
 \tag{2}
\]
In particular, the off-diagonal entries of \((\Omega_n)_{bb}\) retain the covariance caused by any treated or donor group reused across labels; no independence across donor labels has been imposed.

We first prove the deficiency obstruction. Fix the two parameters in the statement and define
\[
 d_b=\lVert b^{(1)}-b^{(0)}\rVert_2>0,
 \qquad
 u=\frac{b^{(1)}-b^{(0)}}{d_b},
 \qquad
 t=\frac{u^{\top}b^{(0)}+u^{\top}b^{(1)}}2.
 \tag{3}
\]
The assumed uniform convergence of \(\psi_n\), evaluated at \(\eta_k\), implies
\[
 b_{n,k}\longrightarrow b^{(k)},
 \qquad
 \Omega_{n,k}\longrightarrow\Omega,
 \qquad k\in\{0,1\}.
 \tag{4}
\]
Since \(u^{\top}(b^{(1)}-b^{(0)})=d_b\), (4) gives, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 t-u^{\top}b_{n,0}\geq\frac{d_b}{4},
 \qquad
 u^{\top}b_{n,1}-t\geq\frac{d_b}{4}.
 \tag{5}
\]
Moreover, \(\mathrm{ass:covariance\text{-}regularity}\), which is a member property of \(\mathrm{def:panel\text{-}model\text{-}class}\), implies
\[
 \lambda_{\max}((\Omega_{n,k})_{bb})
 \leq\lambda_{\max}(\Omega_{n,k})
 \leq\overline\omega.
 \tag{6}
\]
The test
\[
 \varphi_n=\mathbf 1\{u^{\top}\widehat b_n>t\}
\]
is measurable in the observation of \(\mathcal E_n^{\mathrm{mean}}\). By (2), (5), (6), and Chebyshev's inequality,
\[
 \mathbb P_{n,\eta_0}(\varphi_n=1)
 \leq\frac{16\overline\omega}{n d_b^2}\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 \mathbb P_{n,\eta_1}(\varphi_n=0)
 \leq\frac{16\overline\omega}{n d_b^2}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{7}
\]
Let \(P^{\mathrm{mean}}_{n,k}\) be the law in the reduced experiment at \(\eta_k\). With total variation normalized as \(\lVert P-Q\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}=\sup_A|P(A)-Q(A)|\), (7) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \lVert P^{\mathrm{mean}}_{n,1}-P^{\mathrm{mean}}_{n,0}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
 &\geq
 P^{\mathrm{mean}}_{n,1}\{\varphi_n=1\}
 -P^{\mathrm{mean}}_{n,0}\{\varphi_n=1\}\\
 &\longrightarrow1.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{8}
\]
The total-variation distance is at most one, so the limit in (8) is one.

Let \(G_k=N(m_k,\Omega)\) be the two laws in the frozen Gaussian experiment and put
\[
 a=\lVert\Omega^{-1/2}(m_1-m_0)\rVert_2.
\]
Positive definiteness of \(\Omega\) and \(b^{(0)}\ne b^{(1)}\) imply \(0<a<\infty\). After whitening and projecting onto the unit vector \(\Omega^{-1/2}(m_1-m_0)/a\), the likelihood ratio is at least one exactly on the half-space above the midpoint of the two projected means. The projected mean difference is \(a\), and the projected variance is one under both laws. Therefore the positive-density sign characterization of total variation gives directly
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \lVert G_1-G_0\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
 &=\Phi(a/2)-\Phi(-a/2)\\
 &=2\Phi(a/2)-1
 =v<1.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{9}
\]

For any Markov kernel \(K_n\) from the Gaussian observation space to the reduced-experiment observation space, set
\[
 \varepsilon_n(K_n)=
 \sup_{\eta\in\mathcal K}
 \lVert K_nG_\eta-P^{\mathrm{mean}}_{n,\eta}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\]
Markov kernels contract total variation: for every output event \(A\), the function \(x\mapsto K_n(x,A)\) is measurable and lies in \([0,1]\), so integration against \(G_1-G_0\) is bounded in absolute value by \(\lVert G_1-G_0\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\). The triangle inequality and (9) consequently give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \lVert P^{\mathrm{mean}}_{n,1}-P^{\mathrm{mean}}_{n,0}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
 &\leq
 \lVert P^{\mathrm{mean}}_{n,1}-K_nG_1\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
 +\lVert K_nG_1-K_nG_0\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\\
 &\qquad
 +\lVert K_nG_0-P^{\mathrm{mean}}_{n,0}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\\
 &\leq2\varepsilon_n(K_n)+v.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{10}
\]
Combining (8) and (10), then taking the infimum over all such kernels, proves
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}
 \delta\!\left(\{G_\eta:\eta\in\mathcal K\},
                 \mathcal E_n^{\mathrm{mean}}\right)
 \geq\frac{1-v}{2}>0.
 \tag{11}
\]
Because asymptotic Le Cam equivalence requires both directional deficiencies to converge to zero, (11) proves the negative assertion.

It remains to establish the stated direct risk comparison. Let \(\Pi_s\) and \(\Pi_b\) be the coordinate-selection matrices extracting, respectively, the \(Jq\) score coordinates and the \(J\) effect coordinates from an interleaved vector in \(\mathbb R^p\). Define
\[
 T_nx=(\Pi_sx,n^{-1/2}\Pi_bx),
 \qquad x\in\mathbb R^p.
\]
The score and effect coordinates partition the coordinates of \(x\); hence, for \(n\geq1\),
\[
 \lVert T_nx\rVert_2^2
 =\lVert\Pi_sx\rVert_2^2+n^{-1}\lVert\Pi_bx\rVert_2^2
 \leq\lVert x\rVert_2^2.
 \tag{12}
\]
Thus \(T_n\) is one-Lipschitz. Write
\[
 V_{n,\eta}
 =\left(((\sqrt n\,\widehat s_{j,n})_{j\in\mathcal J},
          (\widehat b_{j,n})_{j\in\mathcal J})\right)
\]
and
\[
 a_{n,\eta}
 =\left(((\sqrt n\,s_{j,n})_{j\in\mathcal J},
          (b_{j,n})_{j\in\mathcal J})\right).
\]
Then
\[
 V_{n,\eta}
 =a_{n,\eta}+T_n\sqrt n(\widehat\theta_n-\theta_n).
 \tag{13}
\]
Let \(Z_{n,\eta}\sim N(0,\Omega_n)\) and set \(V^G_{n,\eta}=a_{n,\eta}+T_nZ_{n,\eta}\). The uniform Prokhorov approximation in \(\mathrm{lem:joint\text{-}gaussian}\), the one-Lipschitz property (12), and invariance under the common translation \(a_{n,\eta}\) imply
\[
 \sup_{\eta\in\mathcal K}
 d_{\mathrm{Pr}}\!\left(
   \mathcal L_{\mathbb P_{n,\eta}}(V_{n,\eta}),
   \mathcal L(V^G_{n,\eta})
 \right)
 \longrightarrow0.
 \tag{14}
\]

For the remaining comparison, let \(E\sim N(0,I_p)\), couple
\[
 Z_{n,\eta}=\Omega_n^{1/2}E,
 \qquad
 Z_\eta=\Omega^{1/2}E,
\]
and define
\[
 V^{\mathrm{sc}}_\eta
 =(h,b)+(\Pi_sZ_\eta,0).
 \tag{15}
\]
Its law is exactly that of \((X^{\mathrm{sc}},b)\), with score covariance \(\Gamma(\eta)=\Pi_s\Omega\Pi_s^{\top}\). The parameter-map convergence gives, uniformly over \(\eta\in\mathcal K\),
\[
 \lVert a_{n,\eta}-(h,b)\rVert_2\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 \lVert\Omega_n-\Omega\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{16}
\]
The covariance eigenvalues lie in \([\underline\omega,\overline\omega]\). For completeness, if \(A_0,B_0\) are two such positive definite matrices and \(D=A_0^{1/2}-B_0^{1/2}\), then
\[
 A_0-B_0=A_0^{1/2}D+DB_0^{1/2}.
\]
Taking the Frobenius inner product with \(D\) and using the eigenvalue lower bound gives
\[
 2\sqrt{\underline\omega}\lVert D\rVert_F^2
 \leq\langle D,A_0-B_0\rangle_F
 \leq\lVert D\rVert_F\lVert A_0-B_0\rVert_F.
\]
Thus
\[
 \lVert A_0^{1/2}-B_0^{1/2}\rVert_F
 \leq\frac{\lVert A_0-B_0\rVert_F}{2\sqrt{\underline\omega}}.
 \tag{17}
\]
Because \(p\) is fixed, (16)--(17), the upper eigenvalue bound, and \(\mathbb E\lVert E\rVert_2<\infty\) imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sup_{\eta\in\mathcal K}
 \mathbb E\lVert V^G_{n,\eta}-V^{\mathrm{sc}}_\eta\rVert_2
 &\leq
 \sup_{\eta\in\mathcal K}\lVert a_{n,\eta}-(h,b)\rVert_2\\
 &\quad+
 \sup_{\eta\in\mathcal K}
 \mathbb E\lVert\Pi_s(\Omega_n^{1/2}-\Omega^{1/2})E\rVert_2\\
 &\quad+
 n^{-1/2}\sup_{\eta\in\mathcal K}
 \mathbb E\lVert\Pi_b\Omega_n^{1/2}E\rVert_2\\
 &\longrightarrow0.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{18}
\]
For any coupled random vectors \(U,V\) and any \(\epsilon>0\), Markov's inequality gives
\[
 \Pr\{\lVert U-V\rVert_2>\epsilon\}
 \leq\frac{\mathbb E\lVert U-V\rVert_2}{\epsilon}.
\]
Taking \(\epsilon\) equal to the square root of the right-hand numerator shows directly from the definition of the Prokhorov metric that a coupling whose expected distance vanishes has vanishing Prokhorov distance. Hence (18), (14), and the triangle inequality yield
\[
 \delta_n:=
 \sup_{\eta\in\mathcal K}
 d_{\mathrm{Pr}}\!\left(
  \mathcal L_{\mathbb P_{n,\eta}}(V_{n,\eta}),
  \mathcal L(V^{\mathrm{sc}}_\eta)
 \right)
 \longrightarrow0.
 \tag{19}
\]

Finally fix a loss family from the statement. We derive the expectation implication rather than invoke a deficiency theorem. If probability laws \(P,Q\) satisfy \(d_{\mathrm{Pr}}(P,Q)\leq\epsilon\), the defining Prokhorov inequalities give
\[
 P(A)\leq Q(A^\epsilon)+\epsilon,
 \qquad
 Q(A)\leq P(A^\epsilon)+\epsilon
 \tag{20}
\]
for every Borel set \(A\), where \(A^\epsilon\) is its \(\epsilon\)-enlargement. For \(A_t=\{x:\ell_\eta(x)>t\}\), the common modulus implies
\[
 A_t^\epsilon\subseteq A_{t-\omega(\epsilon)}.
 \tag{21}
\]
Since \(|\ell_\eta|\leq M\), layer-cake integration, (20), and (21) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_P\ell_\eta-\mathbb E_Q\ell_\eta
 &=\int_{-M}^{M}\{P(A_t)-Q(A_t)\}\,dt\\
 &\leq\omega(\epsilon)+2M\epsilon.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{22}
\]
Interchanging \(P\) and \(Q\) gives the same bound for the negative difference. Apply (22) with the two laws in (19) and any \(\epsilon>\delta_n\), then let \(n\to\infty\) and \(\epsilon\downarrow0\). This proves the displayed uniform risk convergence.

Equation (19) is only a uniform weak comparison. It does not imply total-variation convergence to the point mass at the continuously varying \(b\), nor does it supply parameter-free two-sided Markov kernels. The bounded uniformly equicontinuous conclusion follows from (22); an unbounded diameter loss needs uniform integrability for truncation, while a coverage indicator needs control of probability on its discontinuity boundary. This proves exactly the qualified transfer and all claims in the theorem.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem separates two notions that the former transfer recipe conflated: the unscaled effect estimate consistently reveals \(b\), which rules out equivalence to a Gaussian experiment with fixed effect-coordinate noise, while uniform weak convergence still transfers bounded uniformly equicontinuous risk payoffs to the exact-\(b\) score-only benchmark. \textit{Gap.} No parameter-free kernel can turn a noisy estimate of a continuously varying \(b\) into the exact point mass \(\delta_b\) uniformly in total variation. The proved replacement is direct weak-risk convergence; unbounded diameter and discontinuous coverage require additional tail and boundary arguments. \textit{Consumer.} Use the score-only frontier as a first-order decision-risk benchmark and preserve shared-control score covariance. Do not cite it as a global two-sided Le Cam equivalence or as a finite-sample exact observation result.

\begin{lemma}[lem:pilot-main-split]\label{lem:pilot-main-split}
For the split and sequences in Definition \(\mathrm{def:pilot\text{-}adaptive\text{-}hull}\), the pilot and main statistics are independent.  There exist \(n_0\in\mathbb N\) and \(K<\infty\) such that
\[
 \sup_{n\ge n_0}\sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M}
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb P_n}\!\left[
 \left\|\sqrt{m_n}(\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm P}-\theta_n)\right\|_2^2
 \right]\le K.
\]
\[
 \sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M}
 d_{\mathrm{Pr}}\!\left(
 \mathcal L_{\mathbb P_n}(\sqrt n(\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm M}-\theta_n)),
 N(0,\Omega_n)
 \right)\longrightarrow0,
\]
and
\[
 \sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M}
 \Pr_{\mathbb P_n}\!\left\{
 \left\|\sqrt n(\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm M}-\widehat\theta_n)\right\|_2>\varepsilon
 \right\}\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{for every }\varepsilon>0.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M}
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb P_n}\!\left[
 \min\{1,\|\widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm M}-\Omega_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}\}
 \right]\longrightarrow0,
\]
and every eigenvalue of \(\widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm M}\) belongs to \([\underline\omega/2,2\overline\omega]\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write \(A=(A_1,\ldots,A_R)\), where \(A_r\) is the block of columns multiplying the mean of group \(r\), and define the positive semidefinite matrices
\[
 B_{r,n}=\frac n{n_r}A_r\Sigma_{r,n}A_r^{\top}.
\]
The covariance definition gives \(\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}B_{r,n}=\Omega_n\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:stable\text{-}fractions}\), the finiteness of \(R\), and \(\lambda_r>0\) imply that, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 0<c_0\le \min_{r\in\mathcal R}\frac{n_r}{n}
 \le \max_{r\in\mathcal R}\frac{n_r}{n}\le c_1<\infty
\tag{17}
\]
for constants depending only on the fixed sampling design.  The pilot and main observations are disjoint subcollections of the independent within-group samples in Definition \(\mathrm{def:panel\text{-}model\text{-}class}\), so their generated sigma-fields are independent.

Direct calculation, using the within-group iid law, gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_{\mathbb P_n}\!\left(
 \sqrt{m_n}(\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm P}-\theta_n)
 \right)
 =\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}A_r\Sigma_{r,n}A_r^{\top}
 =\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}\frac{n_r}{n}B_{r,n}
 \preceq c_1\Omega_n.
\]
Taking traces and applying \(\lambda_{\max}(\Omega_n)\le\overline\omega\) from \(\mathrm{ass:covariance\text{-}regularity}\) proves the asserted uniform second-moment bound.

For each group the exact identity
\[
 \widehat\mu_{r,n}
 =\frac{m_n}{n_r}\widehat\mu_{r,n}^{\mathrm P}
  +\frac{n_r-m_n}{n_r}\widehat\mu_{r,n}^{\mathrm M}
\]
and pilot--main independence yield
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_{\mathbb P_n}\!\left(
 \sqrt n(\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm M}-\widehat\theta_n)
 \right)
 =\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}
   \frac{m_n}{n_r-m_n}B_{r,n}
 \preceq
 \left(\max_{r\in\mathcal R}\frac{m_n}{n_r-m_n}\right)\Omega_n.
\tag{18}
\]
By (17) and \(m_n/n\to0\), the scalar multiplying \(\Omega_n\) in (18) tends to zero.  The difference has mean zero; taking traces and applying Markov's inequality therefore proves the third display in the statement.  Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:joint\text{-}gaussian}\), (18), and the triangle inequality in the Prokhorov metric then prove the stated Gaussian approximation for the main statistic.

For completeness, the population covariance of the main statistic is
\[
 \Omega_n^{\mathrm M}
 =\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}\frac n{n_r-m_n}
   A_r\Sigma_{r,n}A_r^{\top}
 =\Omega_n+\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}
   \frac{m_n}{n_r-m_n}B_{r,n}.
\tag{19}
\]
Thus (18) also proves \(\|\Omega_n^{\mathrm M}-\Omega_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}\to0\) uniformly.  For each entry of a centered within-main-sample covariance, Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:uniform\text{-}fourth\text{-}moment}\) bounds the variance of the corresponding empirical second moment by a constant divided by \(n_r-m_n\); the correction formed from the sample mean has expectation of order \((n_r-m_n)^{-1}\).  Since \(d\) and \(R\) are fixed, summing the entrywise bounds and using (17) gives
\[
 \sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M}
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb P_n}\!\left[
 \min\{1,\|\widehat\Omega_n^{\mathrm M}-\Omega_n^{\mathrm M}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\}
 \right]\longrightarrow0,
\tag{20}
\]
where \(\widehat\Omega_n^{\mathrm M}\) denotes the unclipped main-sample formula used in Definition \(\mathrm{def:pilot\text{-}adaptive\text{-}hull}\).  Equations (19)--(20) imply consistency for \(\Omega_n\).

If \(\|\widehat\Omega_n^{\mathrm M}-\Omega_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}<\underline\omega/2\), the Rayleigh-quotient characterization of extreme eigenvalues and \(\underline\omega I_p\preceq\Omega_n\preceq\overline\omega I_p\) put every eigenvalue of \(\widehat\Omega_n^{\mathrm M}\) strictly inside the clipping interval.  Hence clipping changes nothing on an event whose probability tends to one uniformly by (20).  On the complementary event both matrices have bounded clipped operator norm, so truncation by one and (20) prove the asserted consistency of \(\widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm M}\).  The final deterministic eigenvalue bounds follow immediately from the clipping construction.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The independent screen and the untouched main-sample calibration require a joint uniform approximation with the pilot's smaller rate exposed explicitly. \textit{Gap.} The frozen Gaussian lemma concerns the full sample and does not itself state that deletion of an \(o(n)\) pilot preserves the root-\(n\) law and covariance estimate. \textit{Consumer.} The two pilot-adaptive theorems use this lemma for selection independence, score-screen concentration, and feasible main-sample Gaussian calibration.

\begin{theorem}[thm:pilot-adaptive-uniform-coverage]\label{thm:pilot-adaptive-uniform-coverage}
For every \(\alpha\in(0,1)\), the independent-pilot construction satisfies
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M}
 \Pr_{\mathbb P_n}\{\tau_n\in C_n^{\mathrm{ad}}(\alpha)\}
 \ge1-\alpha.
\]
This conclusion is uniform over all exact-close arrays, including root-\(n\)-local separations and multiple-close-donor faces, and its covariance calibration retains every shared treated-group and reused-control covariance encoded by \(A\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(\alpha\in(0,1)\).  For each \(\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M\), Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:nonempty\text{-}close\text{-}set}\) supplies at least one \(j_n^\star\in\mathcal C_n\).  For this label, \(s_{j_n^\star,n}=0\).  The pilot second-moment bound in Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:pilot\text{-}main\text{-}split}\) and Markov's inequality give a constant \(K<\infty\), independent of the array, such that
\[
 \sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M}
 \Pr_{\mathbb P_n}\!\left\{
 \sqrt{m_n}\|\widehat s_{j_n^\star,n}^{\mathrm P}\|_2>a_n
 \right\}
 \le \frac K{a_n^2}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{21}
\]
If the raw screen is empty, the fallback retains all labels.  Otherwise, whenever the event complementary to that in (21) occurs, it retains \(j_n^\star\).  Consequently
\[
 \inf_{\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M}
 \Pr_{\mathbb P_n}\{\widehat{\mathcal S}_n\cap\mathcal C_n\ne\varnothing\}
 \longrightarrow1.
\tag{22}
\]

We next establish the conditional main-sample coverage.  The clipping bounds imply that the matrices \(\widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm M}\) range over a compact subset of \(\mathbb S_{++}^p\).  On this set, matrix inversion is continuous, so every map \(\Omega\mapsto r_j(\Omega)\) and \(\Omega\mapsto\sigma_j(\Omega)\) is continuous, with \(\sigma_j(\Omega)\) bounded away from zero.  For a fixed nonempty \(S\subseteq\mathcal J\), if \(Z\sim N(0,\Omega)\), then each variable \(r_j(\Omega)Z/\sigma_j(\Omega)\) is standard normal.  The boundary of
\(
 \{\max_{j\in S}|r_j(\Omega)Z|/\sigma_j(\Omega)\le x\}
\)
is contained in a finite union of nondegenerate Gaussian hyperplanes and hence has probability zero.  Gaussian coupling through the continuous symmetric square root therefore shows that its distribution function and its \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile are continuous in \(\Omega\).  Because \(\mathcal J\) is finite, this continuity is uniform over all nonempty \(S\subseteq\mathcal J\).

Apply Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:pilot\text{-}main\text{-}split}\) and this finite-dimensional continuity argument.  Uniformly in \(\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M\) and nonempty \(S\subseteq\mathcal J\),
\[
 \Pr_{\mathbb P_n}\!\left\{
  \max_{j\in S}
  \frac{|r_j(\widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm M})
  \sqrt n(\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm M}-\theta_n)|}
  {\sigma_j(\widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm M})}
  \le k_{1-\alpha}(S,\widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm M})
 \right\}
 \ge1-\alpha-o(1).
\tag{23}
\]
The pilot sigma-field is independent of the main sample by the same lemma.  Hence (23) remains valid after conditioning on each possible value of \(\widehat{\mathcal S}_n\); summing over the finitely many nonempty subsets gives (23) with \(S=\widehat{\mathcal S}_n\).

On the event in (22), choose \(j\in\widehat{\mathcal S}_n\cap\mathcal C_n\).  Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:exact\text{-}score\text{-}transport}\) gives \(s_{j,n}=0\) and \(b_{j,n}=\tau_n\).  Therefore, for every positive definite \(\Omega\),
\[
 r_j(\Omega)\theta_n
 =b_{j,n}-u_j(\Omega)^{\top}V_j(\Omega)^{-1}s_{j,n}
 =\tau_n.
\tag{24}
\]
On the simultaneous event in (23), equations (24) and the definition of \(I_{j,n}^{\mathrm{ad}}(\alpha)\) imply \(\tau_n\in I_{j,n}^{\mathrm{ad}}(\alpha)\), and hence \(\tau_n\in C_n^{\mathrm{ad}}(\alpha)\).  Combining (22)--(24) proves the asserted uniform lower coverage bound.  Because the covariance in (23) is formed from the single stacked contrast matrix \(A\), none of its off-diagonal shared-group terms has been discarded.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Pilot screening is useful only if it preserves honesty at local label collisions and on faces with several exact donors. \textit{Gap.} Ordinary consistent model selection does not justify a confidence set uniformly over root-\(n\)-local score separations; independence and simultaneous main-sample calibration are both needed. \textit{Consumer.} Applications may reduce the calibration menu using prespecified sample splitting without sacrificing the conditional uniform coverage guarantee.

\begin{theorem}[thm:pilot-adaptive-oracle-equivalence]\label{thm:pilot-adaptive-oracle-equivalence}
For every fixed \(\delta>0\), the pilot screen consistently recovers the entire close set:
\[
 \sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M(\delta)}
 \Pr_{\mathbb P_n}\{\widehat{\mathcal S}_n\ne\mathcal C_n\}
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
If \(\mathcal C_n=\{j_n\}\), define the ordinary full-sample, known-label residual-effect Wald interval
\[
 W_{j_n,n}^{\mathrm{ord}}(\alpha)
 =\left[
 r_{j_n}(\widetilde\Omega_n)\widehat\theta_n
 \mathbin{\pm}n^{-1/2}\sigma_{j_n}(\widetilde\Omega_n)
 \Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha/2)
 \right].
\]
Then, for every \(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
 \sup_{\substack{\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M(\delta):\\
                   \mathcal C_n=\{j_n\}}}
 \Pr_{\mathbb P_n}\!\left\{
 \sqrt n\max\left(
  \left|\inf C_n^{\mathrm{ad}}(\alpha)-\inf W_{j_n,n}^{\mathrm{ord}}(\alpha)\right|,
  \left|\sup C_n^{\mathrm{ad}}(\alpha)-\sup W_{j_n,n}^{\mathrm{ord}}(\alpha)\right|
 \right)>\varepsilon
 \right\}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus the adaptive hull has the ordinary single-label Wald center, variance, and critical constant to first order when the exact close label is unique; for several close labels it calibrates only over that recovered face.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For \(j\in\mathcal C_n\), the argument in (21), followed by a union bound over the fixed set \(\mathcal J\), gives
\[
 \sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M(\delta)}
 \Pr_{\mathbb P_n}\{\mathcal C_n\nsubseteq S_n^0\}
 \le \frac{JK}{a_n^2}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{25}
\]
For \(j\notin\mathcal C_n\), fixed separation gives \(\|s_{j,n}\|_2\ge\delta\).  If that label belongs to the raw screen, the reverse triangle inequality implies
\[
 \sqrt{m_n}\|\widehat s_{j,n}^{\mathrm P}-s_{j,n}\|_2
 \ge \sqrt{m_n}\delta-a_n.
\]
The pilot second-moment bound and Markov's inequality therefore give, once the positive denominator is defined,
\[
 \sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M(\delta)}
 \Pr_{\mathbb P_n}\{j\in S_n^0\}
 \le \frac K{(\sqrt{m_n}\delta-a_n)^2}\longrightarrow0,
\tag{26}
\]
because \(a_n/\sqrt{m_n}\to0\).  Equations (25)--(26) and a union bound show \(S_n^0=\mathcal C_n\) with probability tending to one uniformly.  On this event the raw screen is nonempty by Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:nonempty\text{-}close\text{-}set}\), so the fallback is inactive and \(\widehat{\mathcal S}_n=\mathcal C_n\).  This proves recovery.

Now suppose \(\mathcal C_n=\{j_n\}\).  On the recovery event, the maximum in the definition of \(k_{1-\alpha}\) contains one standardized nondegenerate normal coordinate.  Hence, exactly and for every positive definite covariance matrix,
\[
 k_{1-\alpha}(\{j_n\},\Omega)=\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha/2).
\tag{27}
\]
Definitions and (27) show that on this event \(C_n^{\mathrm{ad}}(\alpha)\) equals the main-sample ordinary interval
\[
 W_{j_n,n}^{\mathrm M}(\alpha)
 =\left[
 r_{j_n}(\widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm M})\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm M}
 \mathbin{\pm}n^{-1/2}\sigma_{j_n}(\widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm M})
 \Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha/2)
 \right].
\tag{28}
\]

It remains to compare (28) with the full-sample interval.  Lemmas \(\mathrm{lem:spectral\text{-}clipping\text{-}negligible}\) and \(\mathrm{lem:pilot\text{-}main\text{-}split}\) give
\[
 \|\widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm M}-\widetilde\Omega_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}=o_{\mathbb P_n}(1),
 \qquad
 \sqrt n\|\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm M}-\widehat\theta_n\|_2
 =o_{\mathbb P_n}(1)
\tag{29}
\]
uniformly.  Continuity on the clipped compact covariance set turns the first relation into
\[
 r_{j_n}(\widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm M})-r_{j_n}(\widetilde\Omega_n)
 =o_{\mathbb P_n}(1),
 \qquad
 \sigma_{j_n}(\widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm M})-\sigma_{j_n}(\widetilde\Omega_n)
 =o_{\mathbb P_n}(1).
\tag{30}
\]
Because \(j_n\) is close, Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:exact\text{-}score\text{-}transport}\) gives \(s_{j_n,n}=0\) and \(b_{j_n,n}=\tau_n\).  Hence, for either estimated covariance \(\Omega'\), the definition of the residual row gives
\[
 r_{j_n}(\Omega')\theta_n
 =b_{j_n,n}-u_{j_n}(\Omega')^{\top}V_{j_n}(\Omega')^{-1}s_{j_n,n}
 =\tau_n.
\]
Thus the potentially unbounded level of \(\theta_n\) cancels, and
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\sqrt n\left\{
 r_{j_n}(\widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm M})\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm M}
 -r_{j_n}(\widetilde\Omega_n)\widehat\theta_n
 \right\}\\
 &\quad=
 r_{j_n}(\widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm M})
 \sqrt n(\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm M}-\widehat\theta_n)
 +\{r_{j_n}(\widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm M})
      -r_{j_n}(\widetilde\Omega_n)\}
 \sqrt n(\widehat\theta_n-\theta_n)
 =o_{\mathbb P_n}(1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{31}
\]
Here the last factor is uniformly tight by Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:joint\text{-}gaussian}\), while all residual rows are uniformly bounded by covariance regularity.  Equations (30)--(31) show that both endpoints of (28) differ from the corresponding endpoints of \(W_{j_n,n}^{\mathrm{ord}}(\alpha)\) by \(o_{\mathbb P_n}(n^{-1/2})\), uniformly.  Combining this fact with recovery proves the displayed endpoint equivalence.  The final multiple-face assertion follows from the already-proved recovery \(\widehat{\mathcal S}_n=\mathcal C_n\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The vanishing pilot removes false labels under fixed separation while costing only \(o(n)\) observations, so the unique-label procedure can recover the ordinary first-order constant. \textit{Gap.} The original projected hull is root-\(n\) tight but retains a critical value maximized over all \(J\) labels and therefore is not first-order oracle-equivalent. \textit{Consumer.} With one separated exact donor, the adaptive confidence set has the same endpoints to order \(o_{\mathbb P}(n^{-1/2})\) as the ordinary known-label residual-effect Wald interval.

\begin{theorem}[thm:two-face-mahalanobis-minimax]\label{thm:two-face-mahalanobis-minimax}
Let \(0<\alpha<1/2\). Let \(P_0=N(\mu_0,\Gamma)\) and \(P_1=N(\mu_1,\Gamma)\) be Gaussian score laws on \(\mathbb R^{Jq}\), where \(\Gamma\in\mathbb S_{++}^{Jq}\). Let their target values be \(\tau_0,\tau_1\in\mathbb R\), and put
\[
 \Delta=|\tau_1-\tau_0|>0,
 \qquad
 \rho=\bigl((\mu_1-\mu_0)^{\top}\Gamma^{-1}(\mu_1-\mu_0)\bigr)^{1/2}>0.
\]
Among all measurable, possibly randomized confidence kernels satisfying \(P_\ell\{\tau_\ell\in K\}\geq1-\alpha\) for \(\ell=0,1\),
\[
 \inf_K\max_{\ell\in\{0,1\}}
 \mathbb E_{P_\ell}[\operatorname{diam}(K)]
 =\Delta\bigl[\Phi(z_{1-\alpha}-\rho)-\alpha\bigr]_+.
\]
The value is attained by
\[
 A_0=\{T\leq-\rho/2+z_{1-\alpha}\},
 \qquad
 A_1=\{T\geq\rho/2-z_{1-\alpha}\},
\]
and
\[
 T=\frac{(\mu_1-\mu_0)^{\top}\Gamma^{-1}
 (X^{\mathrm{sc}}-(\mu_0+\mu_1)/2)}{\rho},
 \qquad
 K^*(X^{\mathrm{sc}})
 =\{\tau_0:X^{\mathrm{sc}}\in A_0\}
 \cup\{\tau_1:X^{\mathrm{sc}}\in A_1\}.
\]
Consequently the exact value is zero if and only if \(\rho\geq2z_{1-\alpha}\). For the identity-covariance two-donor switch \(\mu_0=(0,c)^{\top}\) and \(\mu_1=(c,0)^{\top}\), one has \(\rho=\sqrt{2}c\). If two such states with a common exactly observed vector \(b\) form a restriction of \(\mathcal H_{H,B}^{\mathrm{sc}}\), then
\[
 \mathfrak R_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{sc}}(H,B,\Gamma)
 \geq
 \Delta\bigl[\Phi(z_{1-\alpha}-\rho)-\alpha\bigr]_+.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write \(d=\mu_1-\mu_0\). Positive definiteness of \(\Gamma\) implies that \(\Gamma^{-1}\) exists. The assumed strict inequality \(\rho>0\) permits division by \(\rho\). Direct calculation from the two Gaussian laws gives
\[
 T\sim N(-\rho/2,1)\quad\text{under }P_0,
 \qquad
 T\sim N(\rho/2,1)\quad\text{under }P_1,
 \tag{1}
\]
because
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_{P_\ell}(T)
 =\rho^{-2}d^{\top}\Gamma^{-1}\Gamma\Gamma^{-1}d=1,
\]
and the likelihood ratio is
\[
 \frac{dP_1}{dP_0}(X^{\mathrm{sc}})
 =\exp\!\left\{d^{\top}\Gamma^{-1}
 \left(X^{\mathrm{sc}}-\frac{\mu_0+\mu_1}{2}\right)\right\}
 =\exp(\rho T).
 \tag{2}
\]

We first prove the testing inequality needed for the lower bound. Set
\[
 t_0=-\rho/2+z_{1-\alpha},
 \qquad
 D^*=\{T>t_0\}.
\]
Equation (1) and the definition \(z_{1-\alpha}=\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha)\) give
\[
 P_0(D^*)=\alpha,
 \qquad
 P_1(D^*)=1-\Phi(z_{1-\alpha}-\rho)
 =\Phi(\rho-z_{1-\alpha}).
 \tag{3}
\]
Let \(D\) be any measurable, possibly randomized event with \(P_0(D)\leq\alpha\), and put \(k=\exp(\rho t_0)>0\). Let \(f_0,f_1\) be the two Gaussian densities with respect to Lebesgue measure; after adjoining state-independent randomization with law \(\nu\), the joint densities with respect to the product of Lebesgue measure and \(\nu\) are \(f_0,f_1\). By (2), \(f_1-kf_0\) is nonpositive on \((D^*)^c\) and nonnegative on \(D^*\). Hence, pointwise on the augmented observation space,
\[
 (\mathbf 1_D-\mathbf 1_{D^*})(f_1-kf_0)\leq0.
\]
Integration and \(P_0(D)\leq P_0(D^*)\) yield
\[
 P_1(D)-P_1(D^*)
 \leq k\{P_0(D)-P_0(D^*)\}\leq0.
 \tag{4}
\]

Let \(K\) satisfy the two coverage inequalities, and define
\[
 A_0=\{\tau_0\in K\},
 \qquad
 A_1=\{\tau_1\in K\},
 \qquad
 O=A_0\cap A_1.
\]
For \(D=A_1\setminus A_0\), coverage at state zero gives \(P_0(D)\leq P_0(A_0^c)\leq\alpha\), whereas coverage at state one gives
\[
 P_1(D)=P_1(A_1)-P_1(O)\geq1-\alpha-P_1(O).
\]
Combining this inequality with (3)--(4) proves
\[
 P_1(O)
 \geq1-\alpha-\Phi(\rho-z_{1-\alpha})
 =\Phi(z_{1-\alpha}-\rho)-\alpha.
 \tag{5}
\]
On \(O\), the set \(K\) contains both target values, so the definition of diameter gives \(\operatorname{diam}(K)\geq\Delta\). Since diameter is nonnegative, (5) implies
\[
 \max_{\ell\in\{0,1\}}
 \mathbb E_{P_\ell}[\operatorname{diam}(K)]
 \geq
 \Delta\bigl[\Phi(z_{1-\alpha}-\rho)-\alpha\bigr]_+.
 \tag{6}
\]

For attainment, use the displayed regions \(A_0,A_1\) and report exactly the corresponding subset of \(\{\tau_0,\tau_1\}\). Equation (1) gives \(P_0(A_0)=P_1(A_1)=1-\alpha\), so the rule is honest. The two regions have a null intersection when \(\rho\geq2z_{1-\alpha}\). When \(\rho<2z_{1-\alpha}\), their intersection has, under state zero,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P_0(A_0\cap A_1)
 &=\Phi(z_{1-\alpha})-\Phi(\rho-z_{1-\alpha})\\
 &=\Phi(z_{1-\alpha}-\rho)-\alpha,
\end{aligned}
 \tag{7}
\]
and the same probability under state one. The reported set has diameter \(\Delta\) precisely on this intersection and diameter zero otherwise. Thus (7) attains (6). Since \(0<\alpha<1/2\) implies \(z_{1-\alpha}>0\), the positive part vanishes exactly when \(\rho\geq2z_{1-\alpha}\).

For \(\Gamma=I_2\), \(\mu_0=(0,c)^{\top}\), and \(\mu_1=(c,0)^{\top}\), the definition of \(\rho\) gives \(\rho=(c^2+c^2)^{1/2}=\sqrt{2}c\). Finally, suppose the two states with their common observed vector \(b\) lie in \(\mathcal H_{H,B}^{\mathrm{sc}}\). Every kernel honest on that entire space satisfies the two statewise coverage constraints, and its supremum risk is at least its maximum risk over the pair. Taking the infimum over full-space kernels and applying (6)--(7) gives the stated restriction lower bound.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem solves the minimal donor-switch problem exactly and remains valid under arbitrary positive definite score covariance through Mahalanobis reduction. \textit{Gap.} This node is the sharp restricted-pair result; the separate exact finite-face theorem solves the full bounded-spread score-only minimax problem. \textit{Consumer.} It calibrates the identity-covariance corollary and supplies sharp embedded-pair comparisons within the general donor-face experiment.

\begin{theorem}[thm:score-face-general-minimax-exact]\label{thm:score-face-general-minimax-exact}
Let \(0<\alpha<1/2\), \(H>0\), \(B>0\), \(q\geq1\), and \(\Gamma\in\mathbb S_{++}^{Jq}\). In the score-only experiment and parameter space of Definitions \(\mathfrak R_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{sc}}(H,B,\Gamma)\) and \(\mathcal H_{H,B}^{\mathrm{sc}}\),
\[
 \mathfrak R_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{sc}}(H,B,\Gamma)
 =
 \begin{cases}
 0, & J=1,\\[2mm]
 B\left(1-\dfrac{J\alpha}{J-1}\right), & J\geq2.
 \end{cases}
\]
For \(J\geq2\), an attaining data-free randomized confidence kernel reports \(\operatorname{conv}\{b_1,\ldots,b_J\}\) with probability \(1-J\alpha/(J-1)\); with the remaining probability it draws \(L\) uniformly from \(\mathcal J\) and reports \(\{b_L\}\). This result allows several zero-score labels and arbitrary positive definite shared-control covariance \(\Gamma\). The deterministic simultaneous score-screen hull
\[
 K_{\mathrm{scr}}(x,b)
 =\operatorname{conv}\left\{b_j:
 \lVert\Gamma_{jj}^{-1/2}x_j\rVert_2
 \leq k_{1-\alpha}^{\mathrm{sc}}(\Gamma)
 \right\},
\]
with the empty hull interpreted as empty and \(k_{1-\alpha}^{\mathrm{sc}}(\Gamma)\) the \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile of \(\max_{j\in\mathcal J}\lVert\Gamma_{jj}^{-1/2}G_j\rVert_2\) for \(G\sim N(0,\Gamma)\), is uniformly honest and has diameter at most \(B\); it is an operational comparator, not the kernel used to establish the exact minimax upper bound.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If \(J=1\), the defining condition \(\min_j\lVert h_j\rVert_2=0\) forces \(h_1=0\), and the definition of the score-only experiment reveals \(b_1=\tau(h,b)\) without noise. The deterministic rule \(K=\{b_1\}\) has coverage one and diameter zero. Nonnegativity of diameter proves the value zero.

Now suppose \(J\geq2\), and set
\[
 p_\alpha=1-\frac{J\alpha}{J-1}.
\]
Because \(J/(J-1)\leq2\) and \(0<\alpha<1/2\), one has \(0<p_\alpha<1\). Consider the data-free kernel in the statement. If \((h,b)\in\mathcal H_{H,B}^{\mathrm{sc}}\), the nonempty-zero-face condition supplies at least one \(j_0\) with \(h_{j_0}=0\), and the coherence condition in the same definition gives \(b_{j_0}=\tau(h,b)\). Therefore its coverage probability is at least
\[
 p_\alpha+\frac{1-p_\alpha}{J}
 =1-\frac{J\alpha}{J-1}+\frac{\alpha}{J-1}
 =1-\alpha.
 \tag{8}
\]
The kernel is measurable: \(b\mapsto(\min_j b_j,\max_j b_j)\) and \(b\mapsto b_L\) are Borel maps, and the rule is a finite mixture of their associated interval and singleton kernels.
On the full-hull branch its diameter is \(\max_j b_j-\min_j b_j\leq B\), by the bounded-spread condition, and on the singleton branch its diameter is zero. Hence its worst-case expected diameter is at most \(p_\alpha B\). Equality is attained within the parameter space: choose a unit vector \(e\in\mathbb R^q\), any \(c\in(0,H]\), let \(h_1=0\) and \(h_k=ce\) for \(k\neq1\), and choose \(b\) with spread exactly \(B\). This point has a unique zero label, so the coherence implication is satisfied, and the kernel's expected diameter there is \(p_\alpha B\). Thus
\[
 \mathfrak R_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{sc}}(H,B,\Gamma)\leq p_\alpha B.
 \tag{9}
\]

For the matching lower bound, fix the exactly observed grid
\[
 b_j^*=\frac{(j-1)B}{J-1},\qquad j\in\mathcal J,
 \tag{10}
\]
and a unit vector \(e\in\mathbb R^q\). For each \(j\in\mathcal J\) and \(c\in(0,H]\), define the score mean \(h^{(j,c)}\) by
\[
 h_j^{(j,c)}=0,
 \qquad
 h_k^{(j,c)}=ce\quad(k\neq j).
 \tag{11}
\]
Equations (10)--(11) define a member of \(\mathcal H_{H,B}^{\mathrm{sc}}\): its maximum score norm is \(c\leq H\), its effect spread is \(B\), and its unique zero label makes the coherence implication automatic. Its target is \(b_j^*\). Let
\[
 P_j^c=N(h^{(j,c)},\Gamma),
 \qquad
 Q=N(0,\Gamma).
\]
The reference law \(Q\), paired with the nonconstant grid \(b^*\), need not belong to the parameter space; it is used only as a common dominating measure.

We record explicitly why these laws coalesce. For \(m=h^{(j,c)}\), put \(\delta_{j,c}^2=m^{\top}\Gamma^{-1}m\). Under \(Q\),
\[
 L_{j,c}(X)=\frac{dP_j^c}{dQ}(X)
 =\exp\!\left(m^{\top}\Gamma^{-1}X-\frac{\delta_{j,c}^2}{2}\right),
\]
and the normal moment-generating formula gives
\[
 \mathbb E_Q[(L_{j,c}-1)^2]=\exp(\delta_{j,c}^2)-1.
 \tag{12}
\]
Since \(m=c v_j\) for a fixed vector \(v_j\), positive definiteness of \(\Gamma\) gives \(\delta_{j,c}^2=c^2v_j^{\top}\Gamma^{-1}v_j\to0\). Define
\[
 \varepsilon_c=max_{j\in\mathcal J}
 \sup_{0\leq f\leq1}
 \left|\mathbb E_{P_j^c}f-\mathbb E_Qf\right|.
\]
By Cauchy--Schwarz and (12),
\[
 \varepsilon_c
 \leq\max_{j\in\mathcal J}
 \{\exp(\delta_{j,c}^2)-1\}^{1/2}
 \longrightarrow0.
 \tag{13}
\]
The same bound holds after adjoining the state-independent randomization used by any confidence kernel, because its likelihood ratio remains \(L_{j,c}(X)\).

Let \(K\) be any kernel honest on \(\mathcal H_{H,B}^{\mathrm{sc}}\), evaluated at the common observed vector \(b^*\), and let
\[
 N_K=\sum_{j=1}^J\mathbf 1\{b_j^*\in K\}.
\]
Honesty at the parameter (11) and (13) imply, for every \(j\),
\[
 Q\{b_j^*\in K\}
 \geq P_j^c\{b_j^*\in K\}-\varepsilon_c
 \geq1-\alpha-\varepsilon_c.
 \tag{14}
\]
Define \(D_K\) as the range of the grid points from (10) that lie in \(K\), with \(D_K=0\) when no grid point lies in \(K\). Then \(0\leq D_K\leq B\), \(D_K\leq\operatorname{diam}(K)\), and the equal grid spacing gives the pointwise inequality
\[
 D_K\geq\frac{B}{J-1}(N_K-1).
 \tag{15}
\]
Indeed, for \(N_K\geq1\), the largest and smallest of \(N_K\) distinct grid indices differ by at least \(N_K-1\); for \(N_K=0\), the right-hand side of (15) is negative. Summing (14) and using (15) yields
\[
 \mathbb E_QD_K
 \geq\frac{B}{J-1}\{J(1-\alpha-\varepsilon_c)-1\}
 =p_\alpha B-\frac{JB}{J-1}\varepsilon_c.
 \tag{16}
\]
Because \(0\leq D_K/B\leq1\), the definition of \(\varepsilon_c\) also gives
\[
 \left|\mathbb E_{P_j^c}D_K-\mathbb E_QD_K\right|
 \leq B\varepsilon_c.
 \tag{17}
\]
All the laws \(P_j^c\) are admissible parameter points. Hence (16)--(17) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sup_{(h,b)\in\mathcal H_{H,B}^{\mathrm{sc}}}
 \mathbb E_{h,\Gamma}[\operatorname{diam}(K)]
 &\geq \max_{j\in\mathcal J}\mathbb E_{P_j^c}D_K\\
 &\geq \frac1J\sum_{j=1}^J\mathbb E_{P_j^c}D_K\\
 &\geq p_\alpha B
 -B\left(1+\frac{J}{J-1}\right)\varepsilon_c.
\end{aligned}
 \tag{18}
\]
Letting \(c\downarrow0\) in (18) and using (13) proves that every honest kernel has worst-case expected diameter at least \(p_\alpha B\). Taking the infimum over kernels and combining with (9) proves the exact value. The argument uses unique-zero faces approaching the excluded incoherent limit, so it also covers a parameter space containing multiple-zero faces and does not require diagonal \(\Gamma\).

For completeness, partition \(G\sim N(0,\Gamma)\) into its \(q\)-dimensional blocks. Every principal block \(\Gamma_{jj}\) is positive definite. The maximum \(M=\max_j\lVert\Gamma_{jj}^{-1/2}G_j\rVert_2\) has a continuous distribution: \(G\) has a Lebesgue density, and each level set \(\{\lVert\Gamma_{jj}^{-1/2}G_j\rVert_2=t\}\) has Lebesgue measure zero. Thus its displayed quantile satisfies \(\Pr\{M\leq k_{1-\alpha}^{\mathrm{sc}}(\Gamma)\}=1-\alpha\). Under any admissible \((h,b)\), on the event
\[
 \max_j\lVert\Gamma_{jj}^{-1/2}(X_j^{\mathrm{sc}}-h_j)\rVert_2
 \leq k_{1-\alpha}^{\mathrm{sc}}(\Gamma),
\]
every zero-score label is retained. Nonemptiness and coherence then put \(\tau(h,b)\) in the deterministic hull. Its coverage is therefore at least \(1-\alpha\), and its diameter is at most the defining spread bound \(B\). This verifies the stated operational comparison without using it for minimax attainment.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem closes the finite-face score-only minimax problem exactly, including multiple-zero faces and arbitrary positive definite shared-control covariance. \textit{Gap.} The exact decision benchmark does not by itself transfer unbounded expected-diameter loss or discontinuous coverage from the finite-sample panel experiment. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies the sharp weak-separation benchmark and shows that the deterministic simultaneous score screen is an honest operational comparator rather than the minimax-attaining rule.

\section{Interpretation}

The infinite full Gaussian frontier is an ill-posedness diagnosis, not a failure of simultaneous panel coverage: with unrestricted \(b\), nearly indistinguishable score faces can select effects separated by arbitrarily large amounts. Bounding the effect spread and observing \(b\) in the first-order score-only benchmark makes the problem finite. Its exact worst-case value is nevertheless positive for \(J\geq2\) and \(0<\alpha<1/2\): unique-zero faces can approach an excluded incoherent common-zero limit, forcing simultaneous inclusion of a grid of candidate effects. This least-favorable collision makes the exact constant independent of \(H\) and the positive definite covariance \(\Gamma\). The attaining randomized rule is a decision-theoretic benchmark; the deterministic covariance-aware score screen is often more operational but is not used to claim minimax attainment. The two-face formula still describes the sharper gain available at a fixed positive Mahalanobis separation. For the CMS workflow, exact calibration and untreated-trend transport must be prespecified and audited; good ranked balance cannot replace either condition.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The unrestricted-effect full Gaussian benchmark has minimax expected diameter \(+\infty\). The repaired score-only bounded-spread experiment is solved exactly for every finite \(J\): the value is zero for one label and \(B(1-J\alpha/(J-1))\) for \(J\geq2\) under \(0<\alpha<1/2\). The result includes arbitrary positive definite shared-control covariance and multiple-zero faces, but its optimal kernel uses external randomization and exact observation of \(b\); the deterministic score screen is only an honest comparator. Evans--Hansen--Stark duality remains a template rather than a proof ingredient. For the panel-to-score-only step, the reduced statistic is uniformly close only in the weak topology under the stated assumptions. This suffices for bounded uniformly equicontinuous risk payoffs. Unbounded expected diameter additionally needs uniform integrability, discontinuous coverage needs boundary control, and two-sided Le Cam deficiency equivalence to exact observation of continuously varying \(b\) is false. The accepted pilot-adaptive construction and its oracle-equivalence statement are unchanged.

\section{Honest scope}

Uniform causal coverage and fixed-separation oracle conclusions apply only to the triangular-array panel class satisfying consistency, stable group fractions, moment and covariance regularity, a nonempty exact close set, and the shared-slope additive untreated-change condition. In the abstract Gaussian face problems, equality of the \(b_j\) values at simultaneous zero-score labels is separately imposed and is not implied by \(h_j=0\). The original unrestricted-effect full Gaussian frontier is infinite. The exact finite frontier concerns the bounded-spread score-only experiment with exactly observed \(b\), \(H>0\), \(B>0\), positive definite \(\Gamma\), and \(0<\alpha<1/2\): it is zero for \(J=1\) and \(B(1-J\alpha/(J-1))\) for \(J\geq2\), including multiple-close-donor faces. The Mahalanobis theorem separately gives the sharper exact value on any fixed two-face restriction. Finite-sample transfer is limited to bounded uniformly equicontinuous payoffs; unbounded-diameter transfer without uniform integrability, discontinuous-coverage transfer without boundary control, and two-sided deficiency equivalence to exact observation are not claimed. No reviewed source establishes the exact calibration assumptions for the historical CMS estimates.
