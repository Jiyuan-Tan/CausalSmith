%%% FIELD def-pilot-adaptive-hull-construction
Fix deterministic sequences \(m_n\in\mathbb N\) and \(a_n>0\) satisfying \(m_n\to\infty\), \(m_n/n\to0\), \(a_n\to\infty\), and \(a_n/\sqrt{m_n}\to0\).  Because \(R\) is fixed and \(n_r/n\to\lambda_r>0\), \(m_n<n_r\) for every \(r\in\mathcal R\) at all sufficiently large indices.  At such an index, use observations \(1,\ldots,m_n\) from every group as the pilot sample and the remaining observations as the main sample, and set
\[
 \widehat\mu_{r,n}^{\mathrm P}
 =m_n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{m_n}W_{ri},
 \qquad
 \widehat\mu_{r,n}^{\mathrm M}
 =(n_r-m_n)^{-1}\sum_{i=m_n+1}^{n_r}W_{ri}.
\]
Stack the group means in the same order as \(\widehat\mu_n\), and define
\[
 \widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm P}=A\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm P},
 \qquad
 \widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm M}=A\widehat\mu_n^{\mathrm M}.
\]
Let \(\widehat s_{j,n}^{\mathrm P}\) be the first \(q\) coordinates of \(E_j\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm P}\).  Apply the shared-group covariance formula to the main sample, with divisor \(n_r-m_n\) in each centered within-group covariance and multiplier \(n/(n_r-m_n)\), and apply the same eigenvalue clipping to \([\underline\omega/2,2\overline\omega]\); denote the result by \(\widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm M}\).

For every \(\Omega\in\mathbb S_{++}^p\), partition
\[
 E_j\Omega E_j^{\top}
 =\begin{pmatrix}V_j(\Omega)&u_j(\Omega)\\
 u_j(\Omega)^{\top}&v_j(\Omega)\end{pmatrix}
\]
and define the residual-effect row and its standard deviation by
\[
 r_j(\Omega)=\bigl(-u_j(\Omega)^{\top}V_j(\Omega)^{-1},1\bigr)E_j,
 \qquad
 \sigma_j^2(\Omega)=r_j(\Omega)\Omega r_j(\Omega)^{\top}.
\]
Positive definiteness makes \(V_j(\Omega)\) invertible and its Schur complement \(\sigma_j^2(\Omega)\) strictly positive.  For every nonempty \(S\subseteq\mathcal J\), let
\[
 k_{1-\alpha}(S,\Omega)
 =\inf\left\{x\ge0:
 \Pr\left(
   \max_{j\in S}\frac{|r_j(\Omega)Z|}{\sigma_j(\Omega)}\le x
 \right)\ge1-\alpha\right\},
 \qquad Z\sim N(0,\Omega).
\]
Define the raw pilot screen \(S_n^0=\{j\in\mathcal J:\sqrt{m_n}\|\widehat s_{j,n}^{\mathrm P}\|_2\le a_n\}\) and the nonempty retained set
\[
 \widehat{\mathcal S}_n=
 \begin{cases}
 S_n^0,&S_n^0\ne\varnothing,\\
 \mathcal J,&S_n^0=\varnothing.
 \end{cases}
\]
For \(j\in\widehat{\mathcal S}_n\), put
\[
 \widehat t_{j,n}^{\mathrm M}
 =r_j(\widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm M})\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm M},
 \qquad
 I_{j,n}^{\mathrm{ad}}(\alpha)
 =\left[
 \widehat t_{j,n}^{\mathrm M}
 \mathbin{\pm}n^{-1/2}\sigma_j(\widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm M})
 k_{1-\alpha}(\widehat{\mathcal S}_n,\widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm M})
 \right],
\]
and put \(I_{j,n}^{\mathrm{ad}}(\alpha)=\varnothing\) otherwise.  The pilot-adaptive hull is
\[
 C_n^{\mathrm{ad}}(\alpha)
 =\operatorname{conv}\left(\bigcup_{j\in\mathcal J}
 I_{j,n}^{\mathrm{ad}}(\alpha)\right).
\]
At the finitely many indices for which some \(m_n\ge n_r\), define \(C_n^{\mathrm{ad}}(\alpha)=C_n^{\mathrm{proj}}(\alpha)\).  This convention has no asymptotic effect.

%%% FIELD lem-pilot-main-split-statement
For the split and sequences in Definition \(\mathrm{def:pilot\text{-}adaptive\text{-}hull}\), the pilot and main statistics are independent.  There exist \(n_0\in\mathbb N\) and \(K<\infty\) such that
\[
 \sup_{n\ge n_0}\sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M}
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb P_n}\!\left[
 \left\|\sqrt{m_n}(\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm P}-\theta_n)\right\|_2^2
 \right]\le K.
\]
\[
 \sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M}
 d_{\mathrm{Pr}}\!\left(
 \mathcal L_{\mathbb P_n}(\sqrt n(\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm M}-\theta_n)),
 N(0,\Omega_n)
 \right)\longrightarrow0,
\]
and
\[
 \sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M}
 \Pr_{\mathbb P_n}\!\left\{
 \left\|\sqrt n(\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm M}-\widehat\theta_n)\right\|_2>\varepsilon
 \right\}\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{for every }\varepsilon>0.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M}
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb P_n}\!\left[
 \min\{1,\|\widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm M}-\Omega_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}\}
 \right]\longrightarrow0,
\]
and every eigenvalue of \(\widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm M}\) belongs to \([\underline\omega/2,2\overline\omega]\).

%%% FIELD lem-pilot-main-split-proof
Write \(A=(A_1,\ldots,A_R)\), where \(A_r\) is the block of columns multiplying the mean of group \(r\), and define the positive semidefinite matrices
\[
 B_{r,n}=\frac n{n_r}A_r\Sigma_{r,n}A_r^{\top}.
\]
The covariance definition gives \(\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}B_{r,n}=\Omega_n\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:stable\text{-}fractions}\), the finiteness of \(R\), and \(\lambda_r>0\) imply that, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 0<c_0\le \min_{r\in\mathcal R}\frac{n_r}{n}
 \le \max_{r\in\mathcal R}\frac{n_r}{n}\le c_1<\infty
\tag{17}
\]
for constants depending only on the fixed sampling design.  The pilot and main observations are disjoint subcollections of the independent within-group samples in Definition \(\mathrm{def:panel\text{-}model\text{-}class}\), so their generated sigma-fields are independent.

Direct calculation, using the within-group iid law, gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_{\mathbb P_n}\!\left(
 \sqrt{m_n}(\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm P}-\theta_n)
 \right)
 =\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}A_r\Sigma_{r,n}A_r^{\top}
 =\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}\frac{n_r}{n}B_{r,n}
 \preceq c_1\Omega_n.
\]
Taking traces and applying \(\lambda_{\max}(\Omega_n)\le\overline\omega\) from \(\mathrm{ass:covariance\text{-}regularity}\) proves the asserted uniform second-moment bound.

For each group the exact identity
\[
 \widehat\mu_{r,n}
 =\frac{m_n}{n_r}\widehat\mu_{r,n}^{\mathrm P}
  +\frac{n_r-m_n}{n_r}\widehat\mu_{r,n}^{\mathrm M}
\]
and pilot--main independence yield
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_{\mathbb P_n}\!\left(
 \sqrt n(\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm M}-\widehat\theta_n)
 \right)
 =\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}
   \frac{m_n}{n_r-m_n}B_{r,n}
 \preceq
 \left(\max_{r\in\mathcal R}\frac{m_n}{n_r-m_n}\right)\Omega_n.
\tag{18}
\]
By (17) and \(m_n/n\to0\), the scalar multiplying \(\Omega_n\) in (18) tends to zero.  The difference has mean zero; taking traces and applying Markov's inequality therefore proves the third display in the statement.  Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:joint\text{-}gaussian}\), (18), and the triangle inequality in the Prokhorov metric then prove the stated Gaussian approximation for the main statistic.

For completeness, the population covariance of the main statistic is
\[
 \Omega_n^{\mathrm M}
 =\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}\frac n{n_r-m_n}
   A_r\Sigma_{r,n}A_r^{\top}
 =\Omega_n+sum_{r\in\mathcal R}
   \frac{m_n}{n_r-m_n}B_{r,n}.
\tag{19}
\]
Thus (18) also proves \(\|\Omega_n^{\mathrm M}-\Omega_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}\to0\) uniformly.  For each entry of a centered within-main-sample covariance, Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:uniform\text{-}fourth\text{-}moment}\) bounds the variance of the corresponding empirical second moment by a constant divided by \(n_r-m_n\); the correction formed from the sample mean has expectation of order \((n_r-m_n)^{-1}\).  Since \(d\) and \(R\) are fixed, summing the entrywise bounds and using (17) gives
\[
 \sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M}
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb P_n}\!\left[
 \min\{1,\|\widehat\Omega_n^{\mathrm M}-\Omega_n^{\mathrm M}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\}
 \right]\longrightarrow0,
\tag{20}
\]
where \(\widehat\Omega_n^{\mathrm M}\) denotes the unclipped main-sample formula used in Definition \(\mathrm{def:pilot\text{-}adaptive\text{-}hull}\).  Equations (19)--(20) imply consistency for \(\Omega_n\).

If \(\|\widehat\Omega_n^{\mathrm M}-\Omega_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}<\underline\omega/2\), the Rayleigh-quotient characterization of extreme eigenvalues and \(\underline\omega I_p\preceq\Omega_n\preceq\overline\omega I_p\) put every eigenvalue of \(\widehat\Omega_n^{\mathrm M}\) strictly inside the clipping interval.  Hence clipping changes nothing on an event whose probability tends to one uniformly by (20).  On the complementary event both matrices have bounded clipped operator norm, so truncation by one and (20) prove the asserted consistency of \(\widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm M}\).  The final deterministic eigenvalue bounds follow immediately from the clipping construction.

%%% FIELD thm-pilot-adaptive-uniform-coverage-statement
For every \(\alpha\in(0,1)\), the independent-pilot construction satisfies
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M}
 \Pr_{\mathbb P_n}\{\tau_n\in C_n^{\mathrm{ad}}(\alpha)\}
 \ge1-\alpha.
\]
This conclusion is uniform over all exact-close arrays, including root-\(n\)-local separations and multiple-close-donor faces, and its covariance calibration retains every shared treated-group and reused-control covariance encoded by \(A\).

%%% FIELD thm-pilot-adaptive-uniform-coverage-proof
Fix \(\alpha\in(0,1)\).  For each \(\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M\), Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:nonempty\text{-}close\text{-}set}\) supplies at least one \(j_n^\star\in\mathcal C_n\).  For this label, \(s_{j_n^\star,n}=0\).  The pilot second-moment bound in Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:pilot\text{-}main\text{-}split}\) and Markov's inequality give a constant \(K<\infty\), independent of the array, such that
\[
 \sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M}
 \Pr_{\mathbb P_n}\!\left\{
 \sqrt{m_n}\|\widehat s_{j_n^\star,n}^{\mathrm P}\|_2>a_n
 \right\}
 \le \frac K{a_n^2}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{21}
\]
If the raw screen is empty, the fallback retains all labels.  Otherwise, whenever the event complementary to that in (21) occurs, it retains \(j_n^\star\).  Consequently
\[
 \inf_{\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M}
 \Pr_{\mathbb P_n}\{\widehat{\mathcal S}_n\cap\mathcal C_n\ne\varnothing\}
 \longrightarrow1.
\tag{22}
\]

We next establish the conditional main-sample coverage.  The clipping bounds imply that the matrices \(\widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm M}\) range over a compact subset of \(\mathbb S_{++}^p\).  On this set, matrix inversion is continuous, so every map \(\Omega\mapsto r_j(\Omega)\) and \(\Omega\mapsto\sigma_j(\Omega)\) is continuous, with \(\sigma_j(\Omega)\) bounded away from zero.  For a fixed nonempty \(S\subseteq\mathcal J\), if \(Z\sim N(0,\Omega)\), then each variable \(r_j(\Omega)Z/\sigma_j(\Omega)\) is standard normal.  The boundary of
\(
 \{\max_{j\in S}|r_j(\Omega)Z|/\sigma_j(\Omega)\le x\}
\)
is contained in a finite union of nondegenerate Gaussian hyperplanes and hence has probability zero.  Gaussian coupling through the continuous symmetric square root therefore shows that its distribution function and its \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile are continuous in \(\Omega\).  Because \(\mathcal J\) is finite, this continuity is uniform over all nonempty \(S\subseteq\mathcal J\).

Apply Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:pilot\text{-}main\text{-}split}\) and this finite-dimensional continuity argument.  Uniformly in \(\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M\) and nonempty \(S\subseteq\mathcal J\),
\[
 \Pr_{\mathbb P_n}\!\left\{
  \max_{j\in S}
  \frac{|r_j(\widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm M})
  \sqrt n(\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm M}-\theta_n)|}
  {\sigma_j(\widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm M})}
  \le k_{1-\alpha}(S,\widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm M})
 \right\}
 \ge1-\alpha-o(1).
\tag{23}
\]
The pilot sigma-field is independent of the main sample by the same lemma.  Hence (23) remains valid after conditioning on each possible value of \(\widehat{\mathcal S}_n\); summing over the finitely many nonempty subsets gives (23) with \(S=\widehat{\mathcal S}_n\).

On the event in (22), choose \(j\in\widehat{\mathcal S}_n\cap\mathcal C_n\).  Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:exact\text{-}score\text{-}transport}\) gives \(s_{j,n}=0\) and \(b_{j,n}=\tau_n\).  Therefore, for every positive definite \(\Omega\),
\[
 r_j(\Omega)\theta_n
 =b_{j,n}-u_j(\Omega)^{\top}V_j(\Omega)^{-1}s_{j,n}
 =\tau_n.
\tag{24}
\]
On the simultaneous event in (23), equations (24) and the definition of \(I_{j,n}^{\mathrm{ad}}(\alpha)\) imply \(\tau_n\in I_{j,n}^{\mathrm{ad}}(\alpha)\), and hence \(\tau_n\in C_n^{\mathrm{ad}}(\alpha)\).  Combining (22)--(24) proves the asserted uniform lower coverage bound.  Because the covariance in (23) is formed from the single stacked contrast matrix \(A\), none of its off-diagonal shared-group terms has been discarded.

%%% FIELD thm-pilot-adaptive-oracle-equivalence-statement
For every fixed \(\delta>0\), the pilot screen consistently recovers the entire close set:
\[
 \sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M(\delta)}
 \Pr_{\mathbb P_n}\{\widehat{\mathcal S}_n\ne\mathcal C_n\}
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
If \(\mathcal C_n=\{j_n\}\), define the ordinary full-sample, known-label residual-effect Wald interval
\[
 W_{j_n,n}^{\mathrm{ord}}(\alpha)
 =\left[
 r_{j_n}(\widetilde\Omega_n)\widehat\theta_n
 \mathbin{\pm}n^{-1/2}\sigma_{j_n}(\widetilde\Omega_n)
 \Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha/2)
 \right].
\]
Then, for every \(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
 \sup_{\substack{\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M(\delta):\\
                   \mathcal C_n=\{j_n\}}}
 \Pr_{\mathbb P_n}\!\left\{
 \sqrt n\max\left(
  \left|\inf C_n^{\mathrm{ad}}(\alpha)-\inf W_{j_n,n}^{\mathrm{ord}}(\alpha)\right|,
  \left|\sup C_n^{\mathrm{ad}}(\alpha)-\sup W_{j_n,n}^{\mathrm{ord}}(\alpha)\right|
 \right)>\varepsilon
 \right\}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus the adaptive hull has the ordinary single-label Wald center, variance, and critical constant to first order when the exact close label is unique; for several close labels it calibrates only over that recovered face.

%%% FIELD thm-pilot-adaptive-oracle-equivalence-proof
For \(j\in\mathcal C_n\), the argument in (21), followed by a union bound over the fixed set \(\mathcal J\), gives
\[
 \sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M(\delta)}
 \Pr_{\mathbb P_n}\{\mathcal C_n\nsubseteq S_n^0\}
 \le \frac{JK}{a_n^2}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{25}
\]
For \(j\notin\mathcal C_n\), fixed separation gives \(\|s_{j,n}\|_2\ge\delta\).  If that label belongs to the raw screen, the reverse triangle inequality implies
\[
 \sqrt{m_n}\|\widehat s_{j,n}^{\mathrm P}-s_{j,n}\|_2
 \ge \sqrt{m_n}\delta-a_n.
\]
The pilot second-moment bound and Markov's inequality therefore give, once the positive denominator is defined,
\[
 \sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M(\delta)}
 \Pr_{\mathbb P_n}\{j\in S_n^0\}
 \le \frac K{(\sqrt{m_n}\delta-a_n)^2}\longrightarrow0,
\tag{26}
\]
because \(a_n/\sqrt{m_n}\to0\).  Equations (25)--(26) and a union bound show \(S_n^0=\mathcal C_n\) with probability tending to one uniformly.  On this event the raw screen is nonempty by Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:nonempty\text{-}close\text{-}set}\), so the fallback is inactive and \(\widehat{\mathcal S}_n=\mathcal C_n\).  This proves recovery.

Now suppose \(\mathcal C_n=\{j_n\}\).  On the recovery event, the maximum in the definition of \(k_{1-\alpha}\) contains one standardized nondegenerate normal coordinate.  Hence, exactly and for every positive definite covariance matrix,
\[
 k_{1-\alpha}(\{j_n\},\Omega)=\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha/2).
\tag{27}
\]
Definitions and (27) show that on this event \(C_n^{\mathrm{ad}}(\alpha)\) equals the main-sample ordinary interval
\[
 W_{j_n,n}^{\mathrm M}(\alpha)
 =\left[
 r_{j_n}(\widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm M})\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm M}
 \mathbin{\pm}n^{-1/2}\sigma_{j_n}(\widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm M})
 \Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha/2)
 \right].
\tag{28}
\]

It remains to compare (28) with the full-sample interval.  Lemmas \(\mathrm{lem:spectral\text{-}clipping\text{-}negligible}\) and \(\mathrm{lem:pilot\text{-}main\text{-}split}\) give
\[
 \|\widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm M}-\widetilde\Omega_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}=o_{\mathbb P_n}(1),
 \qquad
 \sqrt n\|\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm M}-\widehat\theta_n\|_2
 =o_{\mathbb P_n}(1)
\tag{29}
\]
uniformly.  Continuity on the clipped compact covariance set turns the first relation into
\[
 r_{j_n}(\widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm M})-r_{j_n}(\widetilde\Omega_n)
 =o_{\mathbb P_n}(1),
 \qquad
 \sigma_{j_n}(\widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm M})-sigma_{j_n}(\widetilde\Omega_n)
 =o_{\mathbb P_n}(1).
\tag{30}
\]
Because \(j_n\) is close, Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:exact\text{-}score\text{-}transport}\) gives \(s_{j_n,n}=0\) and \(b_{j_n,n}=\tau_n\).  Hence, for either estimated covariance \(\Omega'\), the definition of the residual row gives
\[
 r_{j_n}(\Omega')\theta_n
 =b_{j_n,n}-u_{j_n}(\Omega')^{\top}V_{j_n}(\Omega')^{-1}s_{j_n,n}
 =\tau_n.
\]
Thus the potentially unbounded level of \(\theta_n\) cancels, and
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\sqrt n\left\{
 r_{j_n}(\widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm M})\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm M}
 -r_{j_n}(\widetilde\Omega_n)\widehat\theta_n
 \right\}\\
 &\quad=
 r_{j_n}(\widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm M})
 \sqrt n(\widehat\theta_n^{\mathrm M}-\widehat\theta_n)
 +\{r_{j_n}(\widetilde\Omega_n^{\mathrm M})
      -r_{j_n}(\widetilde\Omega_n)\}
 \sqrt n(\widehat\theta_n-\theta_n)
 =o_{\mathbb P_n}(1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{31}
\]
Here the last factor is uniformly tight by Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:joint\text{-}gaussian}\), while all residual rows are uniformly bounded by covariance regularity.  Equations (30)--(31) show that both endpoints of (28) differ from the corresponding endpoints of \(W_{j_n,n}^{\mathrm{ord}}(\alpha)\) by \(o_{\mathbb P_n}(n^{-1/2})\), uniformly.  Combining this fact with recovery proves the displayed endpoint equivalence.  The final multiple-face assertion follows from the already-proved recovery \(\widehat{\mathcal S}_n=\mathcal C_n\).

%%% FIELD thm-oracle-diameter-proof
Fix \(\delta>0\).  Spectral clipping gives the deterministic bounds
\[
 \lambda_{\max}(\widehat V_{j,n})\le2\overline\omega,
 \qquad
 Q_{j,n}\ge\frac n{2\overline\omega}
                 \|\widehat s_{j,n}\|_2^2.
\tag{32}
\]
There is a finite constant \(C_{\alpha,J,q}\), depending only on \(\alpha\), \(J\), and \(q\), such that \(\widehat c_n\le C_{\alpha,J,q}\) on every sample.  Indeed, under the Gaussian law defining \(\widehat c_n\), every standardized label block has the \(N(0,I_{q+1})\) distribution.  Therefore the union bound gives
\[
 \Pr\left\{
  \max_{j\in\mathcal J}
  \|(E_j\widetilde\Omega_nE_j^{\top})^{-1/2}E_jZ\|_2>x
 \right\}
 \le J\Pr\{\|N(0,I_{q+1})\|_2>x\},
\]
and the right-hand side is smaller than \(\alpha\) for all sufficiently large fixed \(x\).

Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:joint\text{-}gaussian}\) and the upper eigenvalue bound on \(\Omega_n\) imply
\[
 \lim_{M\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}
 \sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M}
 \Pr_{\mathbb P_n}\!\left\{
 \|\sqrt n(\widehat\theta_n-\theta_n)\|_2>M
 \right\}=0.
\tag{33}
\]
For \(\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M(\delta)\) and \(j\notin\mathcal C_n\), the definition of fixed separation gives \(\|s_{j,n}\|_2\ge\delta\).  On the event
\(
 \|\widehat s_{j,n}-s_{j,n}\|_2<\delta/2
\), equation (32) yields
\[
 Q_{j,n}\ge\frac{n\delta^2}{8\overline\omega}.
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\), this exceeds \(C_{\alpha,J,q}^2\), and Definition \(\mathrm{def:profile\text{-}intervals}\) then makes the profile empty.  A union bound over the fixed label set and (33) prove
\[
 \sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M(\delta)}
 \Pr_{\mathbb P_n}\{\exists j\in\mathcal J\setminus\mathcal C_n:
 I_{j,n}(\alpha)\ne\varnothing\}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{34}
\]

It remains to bound the close-label profiles.  The clipped covariance and its principal blocks satisfy, on every sample,
\[
 \|\widehat V_{j,n}^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le\frac2{\underline\omega},
 \quad
 \|\widehat u_{j,n}\|_2\le2\overline\omega,
 \quad
 \|\widehat\gamma_{j,n}\|_2
 \le G:=\frac{4\overline\omega}{\underline\omega},
 \quad
 0<\widehat\sigma_{j,n}^2\le2\overline\omega.
\tag{35}
\]
Strict positivity of the Schur complement follows from positive definiteness of the label block.  For \(j\in\mathcal C_n\), Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:exact\text{-}score\text{-}transport}\) gives \(s_{j,n}=0\) and \(b_{j,n}=\tau_n\).  Hence the center
\(
 \widehat b_{j,n}-\widehat\gamma_{j,n}^{\top}\widehat s_{j,n}
\)
satisfies
\[
 \sqrt n\left|
 \widehat b_{j,n}-\widehat\gamma_{j,n}^{\top}\widehat s_{j,n}-\tau_n
 \right|
 \le(1+G)\|E_j\sqrt n(\widehat\theta_n-\theta_n)\|_2.
\tag{36}
\]
Whenever its profile is nonempty, its half-length is at most
\[
 n^{-1/2}\widehat\sigma_{j,n}\widehat c_n
 \le n^{-1/2}\sqrt{2\overline\omega}\,C_{\alpha,J,q}.
\tag{37}
\]
On the event in (34) that every false-label profile is empty, every point of the projected hull lies within
\[
 n^{-1/2}\left\{
 (1+G)\|\sqrt n(\widehat\theta_n-\theta_n)\|_2
 +\sqrt{2\overline\omega}\,C_{\alpha,J,q}
 \right\}
\]
of \(\tau_n\), by (36)--(37) and convexity.  If the hull is empty its declared diameter is zero; otherwise its diameter is at most twice this radius.  Combining this bound with (33)--(34), and then sending \(\xi\to\infty\), proves
\[
 \lim_{\xi\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}
 \sup_{\mathbf P\in\mathfrak M(\delta)}
 \Pr_{\mathbb P_n}\{\sqrt n\operatorname{diam}
 (C_n^{\mathrm{proj}}(\alpha))>\xi\}=0.
\]
Together with (34), this is exactly the two-part claim.  Notice that the proof establishes root-\(n\) tightness only: the unchanged maximum critical value still ranges over all \(J\) labels, so this theorem by itself does not assert the ordinary one-label first-order constant.
