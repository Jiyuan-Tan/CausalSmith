Assume
\[
 \sup_{\eta\in\mathcal K}\lVert\psi_n(\eta)-\eta\rVert\longrightarrow0,
\]
where \(\lVert\cdot\rVert\) is any fixed norm on the finite-dimensional product parameter space.
If \(\mathcal K\) contains \(\eta_0=(h,b^{(0)},\Omega)\) and \(\eta_1=(h,b^{(1)},\Omega)\) with the same \(h\) and \(\Omega\) but \(b^{(0)}\ne b^{(1)}\), then the reduced experiment \(\mathcal E_n^{\mathrm{mean}}\) is not asymptotically equivalent in Le Cam deficiency to the frozen Gaussian experiment \(Z^{\mathrm{pan}}\sim N((h,b),\Omega)\). More precisely, put
\[
 m_k=(h_1^{\top},b^{(k)}_1,\ldots,h_J^{\top},b^{(k)}_J)^{\top},
 \qquad
 v=2\Phi\!\left(\frac{\lVert\Omega^{-1/2}(m_1-m_0)\rVert_2}{2}\right)-1.
\]
Then \(v<1\), and the deficiency from the Gaussian experiment to \(\mathcal E_n^{\mathrm{mean}}\) has lower limit at least \((1-v)/2>0\). The obstruction is the scaling: \((\widehat b_{j,n})_{j\in\mathcal J}\) has covariance of order \(n^{-1}\) and consistently reveals \(b\), whereas the frozen Gaussian experiment retains nondegenerate noise in its effect coordinates.

There is nevertheless a direct first-order risk comparison with the score-only Gaussian law. Let \(\Gamma(\eta)\) be the principal score-coordinate submatrix of \(\Omega\), and under that law let \(X^{\mathrm{sc}}\sim N(h,\Gamma(\eta))\) while \(b\) is observed exactly. If \(\ell_\eta:\mathbb R^{Jq+J}\to\mathbb R\), \(\eta\in\mathcal K\), is any family satisfying
\[
 \sup_{\eta\in\mathcal K}\sup_x|\ell_\eta(x)|\leq M<\infty
\]
and
\[
 \omega(t):=
 \sup_{\eta\in\mathcal K}
 \sup_{\lVert x-y\rVert_2\leq t}
 |\ell_\eta(x)-\ell_\eta(y)|
 \longrightarrow0
 \quad(t\downarrow0),
\]
then
\[
 \sup_{\eta\in\mathcal K}
 \left|
 \mathbb E_{\mathbb P_{n,\eta}}
 \ell_\eta\!\left(((\sqrt n\,\widehat s_{j,n})_{j\in\mathcal J},
                    (\widehat b_{j,n})_{j\in\mathcal J})\right)
 -
 \mathbb E\ell_\eta(X^{\mathrm{sc}},b)
 \right|
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus exact observation of \(b\) is a faithful first-order benchmark for bounded uniformly equicontinuous decision-risk payoffs, not a claim of two-sided total-variation or Le Cam deficiency equivalence. Transferring an unbounded diameter loss additionally requires uniform integrability, and transferring a discontinuous coverage indicator requires separate boundary control. A Gaussian limit with noisy effect coordinates instead requires root-\(n\) localization of the effect coordinates.

Restrict the panel laws to the dominated QMD family \(\{\mathbb P_{n,\eta}:\eta\in\mathcal K\}\), and use the common parameter map
\[
 \psi_n(\eta)=
 \left(((\sqrt n\,s_{j,n})_{j\in\mathcal J},
        (b_{j,n})_{j\in\mathcal J},\Omega_n)\right).
\]
Let \(\Gamma(\eta)\) denote the principal score-coordinate submatrix of \(\Omega\). The first-order comparison experiment observes \((X^{\mathrm{sc}},b)\), where \(X^{\mathrm{sc}}\sim N(h,\Gamma(\eta))\) and \(b\) is observed exactly. The permitted transfer route compares the reduced statistic
\[
 \left(((\sqrt n\,\widehat s_{j,n})_{j\in\mathcal J},
        (\widehat b_{j,n})_{j\in\mathcal J})\right)
\]
directly with \((X^{\mathrm{sc}},b)\) under \(\sup_{\eta\in\mathcal K}\lVert\psi_n(\eta)-\eta\rVert\to0\), for any fixed norm on the finite-dimensional product parameter space. It targets convergence of expectations for uniformly bounded families of losses having a common modulus of continuity in the observation. An unbounded diameter loss must additionally satisfy uniform integrability, and a discontinuous coverage event needs separate boundary control. This handle does not construct two-sided Markov kernels and does not assert total-variation or Le Cam deficiency equivalence between \(\mathcal E_n^{\mathrm{mean}}\) and exact observation of continuously varying \(b\); consistency of \((\widehat b_{j,n})_{j\in\mathcal J}\) yields weak first-order risk approximation, not exact recovery in total variation. If effect-coordinate Gaussian noise is required in the limit, those coordinates must instead be localized on the root-\(n\) scale.

%%% FIELD target-proof
Write
\[
 \widehat b_n=(\widehat b_{1,n},\ldots,\widehat b_{J,n})^{\top},
 \qquad
 b_n=(b_{1,n},\ldots,b_{J,n})^{\top}.
\]
Let \((\Omega_n)_{bb}\) denote the principal submatrix of \(\Omega_n\) indexed by the \(J\) effect coordinates. Expectations and variances below are under \(\mathbb P_{n,\eta}\). By \(\mathrm{def:stacked\text{-}estimator}\), each group sample mean is unbiased. Independence across the primitive groups is part of \(\mathrm{def:panel\text{-}model\text{-}class}\). Hence the definition of \(\Omega_n\) in \(\mathrm{def:shared\text{-}covariance\text{-}estimator}\) gives the exact identities
\[
 \mathbb E_{n,\eta}[\widehat\theta_n]=\theta_n
\]
and
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Var}_{n,\eta}(\widehat\theta_n)
 &=A\operatorname{diag}_{r\in\mathcal R}
   \left(\frac{\Sigma_{r,n}}{n_r}\right)A^{\top}\\
 &=\frac1n A\operatorname{diag}_{r\in\mathcal R}
   \left(\frac n{n_r}\Sigma_{r,n}\right)A^{\top}
 =\frac{\Omega_n}{n}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{1}
\]
Taking the effect-coordinate principal block in (1) yields
\[
 \mathbb E_{n,\eta}[\widehat b_n]=b_n,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}_{n,\eta}(\widehat b_n)
 =\frac{(\Omega_n)_{bb}}{n}.
 \tag{2}
\]
In particular, the off-diagonal entries of \((\Omega_n)_{bb}\) retain the covariance caused by any treated or donor group reused across labels; no independence across donor labels has been imposed.

We first prove the deficiency obstruction. Fix the two parameters in the statement and define
\[
 d_b=\lVert b^{(1)}-b^{(0)}\rVert_2>0,
 \qquad
 u=\frac{b^{(1)}-b^{(0)}}{d_b},
 \qquad
 t=\frac{u^{\top}b^{(0)}+u^{\top}b^{(1)}}2.
 \tag{3}
\]
The assumed uniform convergence of \(\psi_n\), evaluated at \(\eta_k\), implies
\[
 b_{n,k}\longrightarrow b^{(k)},
 \qquad
 \Omega_{n,k}\longrightarrow\Omega,
 \qquad k\in\{0,1\}.
 \tag{4}
\]
Since \(u^{\top}(b^{(1)}-b^{(0)})=d_b\), (4) gives, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 t-u^{\top}b_{n,0}\geq\frac{d_b}{4},
 \qquad
 u^{\top}b_{n,1}-t\geq\frac{d_b}{4}.
 \tag{5}
\]
Moreover, \(\mathrm{ass:covariance\text{-}regularity}\), which is a member property of \(\mathrm{def:panel\text{-}model\text{-}class}\), implies
\[
 \lambda_{\max}((\Omega_{n,k})_{bb})
 \leq\lambda_{\max}(\Omega_{n,k})
 \leq\overline\omega.
 \tag{6}
\]
The test
\[
 \varphi_n=\mathbf 1\{u^{\top}\widehat b_n>t\}
\]
is measurable in the observation of \(\mathcal E_n^{\mathrm{mean}}\). By (2), (5), (6), and Chebyshev's inequality,
\[
 \mathbb P_{n,\eta_0}(\varphi_n=1)
 \leq\frac{16\overline\omega}{n d_b^2}\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 \mathbb P_{n,\eta_1}(\varphi_n=0)
 \leq\frac{16\overline\omega}{n d_b^2}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{7}
\]
Let \(P^{\mathrm{mean}}_{n,k}\) be the law in the reduced experiment at \(\eta_k\). With total variation normalized as \(\lVert P-Q\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}=\sup_A|P(A)-Q(A)|\), (7) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \lVert P^{\mathrm{mean}}_{n,1}-P^{\mathrm{mean}}_{n,0}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
 &\geq
 P^{\mathrm{mean}}_{n,1}\{\varphi_n=1\}
 -P^{\mathrm{mean}}_{n,0}\{\varphi_n=1\}\\
 &\longrightarrow1.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{8}
\]
The total-variation distance is at most one, so the limit in (8) is one.

Let \(G_k=N(m_k,\Omega)\) be the two laws in the frozen Gaussian experiment and put
\[
 a=\lVert\Omega^{-1/2}(m_1-m_0)\rVert_2.
\]
Positive definiteness of \(\Omega\) and \(b^{(0)}\ne b^{(1)}\) imply \(0<a<\infty\). After whitening and projecting onto the unit vector \(\Omega^{-1/2}(m_1-m_0)/a\), the likelihood ratio is at least one exactly on the half-space above the midpoint of the two projected means. The projected mean difference is \(a\), and the projected variance is one under both laws. Therefore the positive-density sign characterization of total variation gives directly
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \lVert G_1-G_0\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
 &=\Phi(a/2)-\Phi(-a/2)\\
 &=2\Phi(a/2)-1
 =v<1.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{9}
\]

For any Markov kernel \(K_n\) from the Gaussian observation space to the reduced-experiment observation space, set
\[
 \varepsilon_n(K_n)=
 \sup_{\eta\in\mathcal K}
 \lVert K_nG_\eta-P^{\mathrm{mean}}_{n,\eta}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\]
Markov kernels contract total variation: for every output event \(A\), the function \(x\mapsto K_n(x,A)\) is measurable and lies in \([0,1]\), so integration against \(G_1-G_0\) is bounded in absolute value by \(\lVert G_1-G_0\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\). The triangle inequality and (9) consequently give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \lVert P^{\mathrm{mean}}_{n,1}-P^{\mathrm{mean}}_{n,0}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
 &\leq
 \lVert P^{\mathrm{mean}}_{n,1}-K_nG_1\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
 +\lVert K_nG_1-K_nG_0\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\\
 &\qquad
 +\lVert K_nG_0-P^{\mathrm{mean}}_{n,0}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\\
 &\leq2\varepsilon_n(K_n)+v.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{10}
\]
Combining (8) and (10), then taking the infimum over all such kernels, proves
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}
 \delta\!\left(\{G_\eta:\eta\in\mathcal K\},
                 \mathcal E_n^{\mathrm{mean}}\right)
 \geq\frac{1-v}{2}>0.
 \tag{11}
\]
Because asymptotic Le Cam equivalence requires both directional deficiencies to converge to zero, (11) proves the negative assertion.

It remains to establish the stated direct risk comparison. Let \(\Pi_s\) and \(\Pi_b\) be the coordinate-selection matrices extracting, respectively, the \(Jq\) score coordinates and the \(J\) effect coordinates from an interleaved vector in \(\mathbb R^p\). Define
\[
 T_nx=(\Pi_sx,n^{-1/2}\Pi_bx),
 \qquad x\in\mathbb R^p.
\]
The score and effect coordinates partition the coordinates of \(x\); hence, for \(n\geq1\),
\[
 \lVert T_nx\rVert_2^2
 =\lVert\Pi_sx\rVert_2^2+n^{-1}\lVert\Pi_bx\rVert_2^2
 \leq\lVert x\rVert_2^2.
 \tag{12}
\]
Thus \(T_n\) is one-Lipschitz. Write
\[
 V_{n,\eta}
 =\left(((\sqrt n\,\widehat s_{j,n})_{j\in\mathcal J},
          (\widehat b_{j,n})_{j\in\mathcal J})\right)
\]
and
\[
 a_{n,\eta}
 =\left(((\sqrt n\,s_{j,n})_{j\in\mathcal J},
          (b_{j,n})_{j\in\mathcal J})\right).
\]
Then
\[
 V_{n,\eta}
 =a_{n,\eta}+T_n\sqrt n(\widehat\theta_n-\theta_n).
 \tag{13}
\]
Let \(Z_{n,\eta}\sim N(0,\Omega_n)\) and set \(V^G_{n,\eta}=a_{n,\eta}+T_nZ_{n,\eta}\). The uniform Prokhorov approximation in \(\mathrm{lem:joint\text{-}gaussian}\), the one-Lipschitz property (12), and invariance under the common translation \(a_{n,\eta}\) imply
\[
 \sup_{\eta\in\mathcal K}
 d_{\mathrm{Pr}}\!\left(
   \mathcal L_{\mathbb P_{n,\eta}}(V_{n,\eta}),
   \mathcal L(V^G_{n,\eta})
 \right)
 \longrightarrow0.
 \tag{14}
\]

For the remaining comparison, let \(E\sim N(0,I_p)\), couple
\[
 Z_{n,\eta}=\Omega_n^{1/2}E,
 \qquad
 Z_\eta=\Omega^{1/2}E,
\]
and define
\[
 V^{\mathrm{sc}}_\eta
 =(h,b)+(\Pi_sZ_\eta,0).
 \tag{15}
\]
Its law is exactly that of \((X^{\mathrm{sc}},b)\), with score covariance \(\Gamma(\eta)=\Pi_s\Omega\Pi_s^{\top}\). The parameter-map convergence gives, uniformly over \(\eta\in\mathcal K\),
\[
 \lVert a_{n,\eta}-(h,b)\rVert_2\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 \lVert\Omega_n-\Omega\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{16}
\]
The covariance eigenvalues lie in \([\underline\omega,\overline\omega]\). For completeness, if \(A_0,B_0\) are two such positive definite matrices and \(D=A_0^{1/2}-B_0^{1/2}\), then
\[
 A_0-B_0=A_0^{1/2}D+DB_0^{1/2}.
\]
Taking the Frobenius inner product with \(D\) and using the eigenvalue lower bound gives
\[
 2\sqrt{\underline\omega}\lVert D\rVert_F^2
 \leq\langle D,A_0-B_0\rangle_F
 \leq\lVert D\rVert_F\lVert A_0-B_0\rVert_F.
\]
Thus
\[
 \lVert A_0^{1/2}-B_0^{1/2}\rVert_F
 \leq\frac{\lVert A_0-B_0\rVert_F}{2\sqrt{\underline\omega}}.
 \tag{17}
\]
Because \(p\) is fixed, (16)--(17), the upper eigenvalue bound, and \(\mathbb E\lVert E\rVert_2<\infty\) imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sup_{\eta\in\mathcal K}
 \mathbb E\lVert V^G_{n,\eta}-V^{\mathrm{sc}}_\eta\rVert_2
 &\leq
 \sup_{\eta\in\mathcal K}\lVert a_{n,\eta}-(h,b)\rVert_2\\
 &\quad+
 \sup_{\eta\in\mathcal K}
 \mathbb E\lVert\Pi_s(\Omega_n^{1/2}-\Omega^{1/2})E\rVert_2\\
 &\quad+
 n^{-1/2}\sup_{\eta\in\mathcal K}
 \mathbb E\lVert\Pi_b\Omega_n^{1/2}E\rVert_2\\
 &\longrightarrow0.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{18}
\]
For any coupled random vectors \(U,V\) and any \(\epsilon>0\), Markov's inequality gives
\[
 \Pr\{\lVert U-V\rVert_2>\epsilon\}
 \leq\frac{\mathbb E\lVert U-V\rVert_2}{\epsilon}.
\]
Taking \(\epsilon\) equal to the square root of the right-hand numerator shows directly from the definition of the Prokhorov metric that a coupling whose expected distance vanishes has vanishing Prokhorov distance. Hence (18), (14), and the triangle inequality yield
\[
 \delta_n:=
 \sup_{\eta\in\mathcal K}
 d_{\mathrm{Pr}}\!\left(
  \mathcal L_{\mathbb P_{n,\eta}}(V_{n,\eta}),
  \mathcal L(V^{\mathrm{sc}}_\eta)
 \right)
 \longrightarrow0.
 \tag{19}
\]

Finally fix a loss family from the statement. We derive the expectation implication rather than invoke a deficiency theorem. If probability laws \(P,Q\) satisfy \(d_{\mathrm{Pr}}(P,Q)\leq\epsilon\), the defining Prokhorov inequalities give
\[
 P(A)\leq Q(A^\epsilon)+\epsilon,
 \qquad
 Q(A)\leq P(A^\epsilon)+\epsilon
 \tag{20}
\]
for every Borel set \(A\), where \(A^\epsilon\) is its \(\epsilon\)-enlargement. For \(A_t=\{x:\ell_\eta(x)>t\}\), the common modulus implies
\[
 A_t^\epsilon\subseteq A_{t-\omega(\epsilon)}.
 \tag{21}
\]
Since \(|\ell_\eta|\leq M\), layer-cake integration, (20), and (21) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_P\ell_\eta-\mathbb E_Q\ell_\eta
 &=\int_{-M}^{M}\{P(A_t)-Q(A_t)\}\,dt\\
 &\leq\omega(\epsilon)+2M\epsilon.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{22}
\]
Interchanging \(P\) and \(Q\) gives the same bound for the negative difference. Apply (22) with the two laws in (19) and any \(\epsilon>\delta_n\), then let \(n\to\infty\) and \(\epsilon\downarrow0\). This proves the displayed uniform risk convergence.

Equation (19) is only a uniform weak comparison. It does not imply total-variation convergence to the point mass at the continuously varying \(b\), nor does it supply parameter-free two-sided Markov kernels. The bounded uniformly equicontinuous conclusion follows from (22); an unbounded diameter loss needs uniform integrability for truncation, while a coverage indicator needs control of probability on its discontinuity boundary. This proves exactly the qualified transfer and all claims in the theorem.
