%%% FIELD proof-general-infinite
Choose distinct labels \(1,2\in\mathcal J\), which is possible because \(J\geq2\), and choose a unit vector \(e\in\mathbb R^q\), which is possible because \(q\geq1\). Fix \(c\in(0,H]\). Define
\[
 h^{(0)}_1=0,\qquad h^{(0)}_j=ce\quad(j\ne1),
\]
and
\[
 h^{(1)}_2=0,\qquad h^{(1)}_j=ce\quad(j\ne2).
\]
For every \(\Delta>0\), use the same effect vector at both states:
\[
 b^{(\Delta)}_1=0,\qquad b^{(\Delta)}_2=\Delta,
 \qquad b^{(\Delta)}_j=0\quad(j\geq3).
\]
Each \((h^{(\ell)},b^{(\Delta)})\), \(\ell\in\{0,1\}\), belongs to \(\mathcal H_H\). Indeed, every score norm is either zero or \(c\leq H\), each state has exactly one zero-score label, and hence the coherence implication for two zero-score labels is vacuous. By the definition of \(\tau(h,b)\),
\[
 \tau(h^{(0)},b^{(\Delta)})=0,
 \qquad
 \tau(h^{(1)},b^{(\Delta)})=\Delta.
 \tag{1}
\]

Let \(m_{\ell,c,\Delta}\in\mathbb R^p\) be the interleaved mean of \(Z^{\mathrm{pan}}\) under state \(\ell\), and set
\[
 d_c=m_{1,c,\Delta}-m_{0,c,\Delta},
 \qquad
 \rho_c=(d_c^{\top}\Omega^{-1}d_c)^{1/2}.
 \tag{2}
\]
Positive definiteness of \(\Omega\) makes \(\Omega^{-1}\) and \(\Omega^{-1/2}\) well defined. Because the effect vector is identical at the two states, all effect-coordinate differences cancel. The first two score-block differences are respectively \(ce\) and \(-ce\), and all remaining score-block differences are zero. Thus \(d_c=cd_1\) for a fixed nonzero vector \(d_1\), so
\[
 \rho_c=c(d_1^{\top}\Omega^{-1}d_1)^{1/2}\longrightarrow0
 \qquad(c\downarrow0).
 \tag{3}
\]
In particular, \(\rho_c\) is independent of \(\Delta\).

Write \(P_{\ell,c,\Delta}=N(m_{\ell,c,\Delta},\Omega)\). Both laws have positive Lebesgue densities. For \(\rho_c>0\), transform an observation \(z\) to
\[
 x=\Omega^{-1/2}\left(z-\frac{m_{0,c,\Delta}+m_{1,c,\Delta}}2\right),
 \qquad
 u=\frac{\Omega^{-1/2}d_c}{\rho_c}.
\]
Then \(\lVert u\rVert_2=1\), the likelihood ratio is at least one exactly when \(u^{\top}x\geq0\), and \(u^{\top}x\) is \(N(-\rho_c/2,1)\) under state zero and \(N(\rho_c/2,1)\) under state one. Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \lVert P_{0,c,\Delta}-P_{1,c,\Delta}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
 &=P_{1,c,\Delta}\{u^{\top}x\geq0\}
   -P_{0,c,\Delta}\{u^{\top}x\geq0\}\\
 &=\Phi(\rho_c/2)-\Phi(-\rho_c/2)
 =2\Phi(\rho_c/2)-1
 =:v_c.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]

Let \(K\) be any measurable confidence kernel satisfying the honesty constraint in the definition of \(\mathfrak R_{\alpha}^{G}(H,\Omega)\). Let \(Q_{\ell,c,\Delta}\) be the joint law of the observation and the reported random set after applying \(K\) to \(P_{\ell,c,\Delta}\). A Markov kernel contracts total variation: for every measurable event \(A\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |Q_{1,c,\Delta}(A)-Q_{0,c,\Delta}(A)|
 &=\left|\int K(z,A_z)\,d(P_{1,c,\Delta}-P_{0,c,\Delta})(z)\right|\\
 &\leq \lVert P_{1,c,\Delta}-P_{0,c,\Delta}\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}
 =v_c,
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
because \(0\leq K(z,A_z)\leq1\). Define
\[
 A_0=\{(z,C):0\in C\},
 \qquad
 A_1=\{(z,C):\Delta\in C\}.
\]
Membership of both states in \(\mathcal H_H\), honesty, and (1) imply
\[
 Q_{0,c,\Delta}(A_0)\geq1-\alpha,
 \qquad
 Q_{1,c,\Delta}(A_1)\geq1-\alpha.
 \tag{6}
\]
Equation (5) gives \(Q_{0,c,\Delta}(A_1)\geq1-\alpha-v_c\). Hence the union bound gives
\[
 Q_{0,c,\Delta}(A_0\cap A_1)
 \geq1-2\alpha-v_c.
 \tag{7}
\]
On \(A_0\cap A_1\), the reported set contains both \(0\) and \(\Delta\), so its diameter is at least \(\Delta\). Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sup_{(h,b)\in\mathcal H_H}
 \mathbb E_{h,b,\Omega}[\operatorname{diam}(K(Z^{\mathrm{pan}}))]
 &\geq
 \mathbb E_{h^{(0)},b^{(\Delta)},\Omega}
 [\operatorname{diam}(K(Z^{\mathrm{pan}}))]\\
 &\geq \Delta\,Q_{0,c,\Delta}(A_0\cap A_1)\\
 &\geq \Delta[1-2\alpha-v_c].
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
When the bracket is negative, the last inequality remains valid because diameter is nonnegative.

By (3), continuity of \(\Phi\), and \(\Phi(0)=1/2\), one has \(v_c\to0\) as \(c\downarrow0\). Since \(0<\alpha<1/2\) and \(H>0\), there is \(c_0\in(0,H]\) such that \(1-2\alpha-v_c>0\) for every \(c\in(0,c_0]\). Fix one such \(c\). Inequality (8) then holds for every \(\Delta>0\) with the same positive multiplier. The definition of \(\mathcal H_H\) imposes no bound on the spread of \(b\), so letting \(\Delta\to\infty\) shows that every honest kernel has infinite worst-case expected diameter. Taking the infimum over honest kernels yields
\[
 \mathfrak R_{\alpha}^{G}(H,\Omega)=+\infty.
\]
Thus neither a finite frontier nor a finite-valued attaining program exists on the frozen full Gaussian face space.

%%% FIELD def-local-face
\(\mathcal H_H=\{(h,b)\in(\mathbb R^q)^J\times\mathbb R^J:\min_{j\in\mathcal J}\lVert h_j\rVert_2=0,\ \max_{j\in\mathcal J}\lVert h_j\rVert_2\leq H,\ h_j=h_k=0\Rightarrow b_j=b_k\}\). The final implication is an additional abstract Gaussian coherence restriction; it is not a consequence of the zero-score equalities alone.

%%% FIELD def-bounded-score-face
\[
 \mathcal H_{H,B}^{\mathrm{sc}}
 =\left\{(h,b)\in(\mathbb R^q)^J\times\mathbb R^J:
 \min_{j\in\mathcal J}\lVert h_j\rVert_2=0,\ 
 \max_{j\in\mathcal J}\lVert h_j\rVert_2\leq H,\ 
 \max_{j\in\mathcal J}b_j-\min_{j\in\mathcal J}b_j\leq B,\ 
 h_j=h_k=0\Rightarrow b_j=b_k
 \right\}.
\]
Only the spread of \(b\) is bounded; no restriction is placed on its absolute location.

%%% FIELD def-score-risk
In the score-only experiment one observes \((X^{\mathrm{sc}},b)\), where \(X^{\mathrm{sc}}\sim N(h,\Gamma)\) and \(b\) is observed without noise. Define
\[
 \mathfrak R_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{sc}}(H,B,\Gamma)
 =\inf_K\sup_{(h,b)\in\mathcal H_{H,B}^{\mathrm{sc}}}
 \mathbb E_{h,\Gamma}
 \left[\operatorname{diam}(K(X^{\mathrm{sc}},b))\right],
\]
where the infimum is over measurable confidence kernels
\(K:\mathbb R^{Jq}\times\mathbb R^J\rightsquigarrow2^{\mathbb R}\) satisfying
\[
 \inf_{(h,b)\in\mathcal H_{H,B}^{\mathrm{sc}}}
 \Pr_{h,\Gamma}\!\left\{\tau(h,b)\in K(X^{\mathrm{sc}},b)\right\}
 \geq1-\alpha.
\]

%%% FIELD def-decision-dual
For the score-only experiment, the primal variables are measurable randomized confidence kernels \(K:\mathbb R^{Jq}\times\mathbb R^J\rightsquigarrow2^{\mathbb R}\). Their constraints are
\[
 \Pr_{h,\Gamma}\{\tau(h,b)\in K(X^{\mathrm{sc}},b)\}\geq1-\alpha
 \quad\text{for every }(h,b)\in\mathcal H_{H,B}^{\mathrm{sc}},
\]
and their objective is
\[
 \sup_{(h,b)\in\mathcal H_{H,B}^{\mathrm{sc}}}
 \mathbb E_{h,\Gamma}[\operatorname{diam}(K(X^{\mathrm{sc}},b))].
\]
Acceptance-region duality may be used as a proposed route to this program only after domination, joint measurability in the face parameter, and a valid reduction from expected fixed-measure loss to expected diameter have been proved. No such reduction is included in this definition.

%%% FIELD def-restricted-transfer
A correctly scaled transfer attempt compares the reduced mean experiment \(\mathcal E_n^{\mathrm{mean}}\) only with the score-only limit that observes \(b\) exactly and observes \(X^{\mathrm{sc}}\sim N(h,\Gamma)\). It must construct Markov kernels in both directions whose deficiencies tend to zero uniformly on a specified dominated compact QMD submodel, and it must separately prove uniform integrability for the diameter losses before transferring expected risk. The previously proposed target with fixed Gaussian noise in the unscaled effect coordinates is excluded by the scaling obstruction. This handle asserts no equivalence: domination, product measurability, uniform likelihood-process approximation, and the expected-risk transfer remain proof obligations.

%%% FIELD stmt-two-face
Let \(0<\alpha<1/2\). Let \(P_0=N(\mu_0,\Gamma)\) and \(P_1=N(\mu_1,\Gamma)\) be Gaussian score laws on \(\mathbb R^{Jq}\) with \(\Gamma\in\mathbb S_{++}^{Jq}\), let their target values be \(\tau_0,\tau_1\in\mathbb R\), and put \(\Delta=|\tau_1-\tau_0|>0\) and \(\rho=((\mu_1-\mu_0)^{\top}\Gamma^{-1}(\mu_1-\mu_0))^{1/2}>0\). Among all measurable confidence kernels satisfying \(P_\ell\{\tau_\ell\in K\}\geq1-\alpha\) for \(\ell=0,1\),
\[
 \inf_K\max_{\ell\in\{0,1\}}
 \mathbb E_{P_\ell}[\operatorname{diam}(K)]
 =\Delta[\Phi(z_{1-\alpha}-\rho)-\alpha]_+.
\]
The value is attained by the likelihood-ratio acceptance regions
\[
 A_0=\{T\leq-\rho/2+z_{1-\alpha}\},
 \qquad
 A_1=\{T\geq\rho/2-z_{1-\alpha}\},
\]
where
\[
 T=\frac{(\mu_1-\mu_0)^{\top}\Gamma^{-1}
 (X^{\mathrm{sc}}-(\mu_0+\mu_1)/2)}{\rho},
 \qquad
 K^*(X^{\mathrm{sc}})=
 \{\tau_0:X^{\mathrm{sc}}\in A_0\}\cup
 \{\tau_1:X^{\mathrm{sc}}\in A_1\}.
\]
In particular, the exact value is zero if and only if \(\rho\geq2z_{1-\alpha}\). For the identity-covariance two-donor switch \(\mu_0=(0,c)^{\top}\), \(\mu_1=(c,0)^{\top}\), one has \(\rho=\sqrt2c\). If two such states with a common exactly observed \(b\) form a restriction of \(\mathcal H_{H,B}^{\mathrm{sc}}\), then
\[
 \mathfrak R_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{sc}}(H,B,\Gamma)
 \geq\Delta[\Phi(z_{1-\alpha}-\rho)-\alpha]_+.
\]
For every \(J\geq1\), including faces with several zero-score labels and arbitrary shared-control covariance \(\Gamma\),
\[
 0\leq\mathfrak R_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{sc}}(H,B,\Gamma)\leq B.
\]
The upper bound is delivered by the simultaneous score-screen hull
\[
 K_{\mathrm{scr}}(x,b)
 =\operatorname{conv}\left\{b_j:
 \lVert\Gamma_{jj}^{-1/2}x_j\rVert_2\leq k_{1-\alpha}^{\mathrm{sc}}(\Gamma)
 \right\},
\]
with the empty hull interpreted as empty, where \(\Gamma_{jj}\) is the \(j\)-th principal score block and \(k_{1-\alpha}^{\mathrm{sc}}(\Gamma)\) is the \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile of \(\max_{j\in\mathcal J}\lVert\Gamma_{jj}^{-1/2}G_j\rVert_2\) for \(G\sim N(0,\Gamma)\).

%%% FIELD proof-two-face
Write \(d=\mu_1-\mu_0\). Positive definiteness of \(\Gamma\) implies that \(\Gamma^{-1}\) exists and that \(\rho>0\) is well defined. For
\[
 T=\frac{d^{\top}\Gamma^{-1}(X^{\mathrm{sc}}-(\mu_0+\mu_1)/2)}{\rho},
\]
direct calculation gives
\[
 T\sim N(-\rho/2,1)\quad\text{under }P_0,
 \qquad
 T\sim N(\rho/2,1)\quad\text{under }P_1,
 \tag{9}
\]
and the likelihood ratio is \(dP_1/dP_0=\exp(\rho T)\).

We first derive the necessary testing inequality. Put
\[
 t_0=-\rho/2+z_{1-\alpha},
 \qquad D^*=\{T>t_0\}.
\]
By (9), \(P_0(D^*)=\alpha\) and
\[
 P_1(D^*)=1-\Phi(z_{1-\alpha}-\rho)=\Phi(\rho-z_{1-\alpha}).
 \tag{10}
\]
If \(D\) is any measurable, possibly randomized event with \(P_0(D)\leq\alpha\), let \(k=\exp(\rho t_0)\). Because \(f_1-kf_0\) is nonpositive off \(D^*\) and nonnegative on \(D^*\),
\[
 (\mathbf 1_D-\mathbf 1_{D^*})(f_1-kf_0)\leq0
\]
pointwise on the observation space augmented by any state-independent randomization variable. Integration and \(P_0(D)\leq P_0(D^*)\) give
\[
 P_1(D)-P_1(D^*)
 \leq k\{P_0(D)-P_0(D^*)\}\leq0.
 \tag{11}
\]
Thus (10) is the largest state-one probability of an event having state-zero probability at most \(\alpha\); this conclusion was derived directly from likelihood-ratio ordering.

Now let \(K\) be any confidence kernel satisfying the two coverage constraints, and define the inclusion events
\[
 A_0=\{\tau_0\in K\},
 \qquad A_1=\{\tau_1\in K\},
 \qquad O=A_0\cap A_1.
\]
The event \(D=A_1\setminus A_0\) obeys
\[
 P_0(D)\leq P_0(A_0^c)\leq\alpha
\]
and
\[
 P_1(D)=P_1(A_1)-P_1(O)geq1-\alpha-P_1(O).
\]
Applying (11) yields
\[
 P_1(O)\geq1-\alpha-\Phi(\rho-z_{1-\alpha})
 =\Phi(z_{1-\alpha}-\rho)-\alpha.
 \tag{12}
\]
On \(O\), the set \(K\) contains both target values and therefore has diameter at least \(\Delta\). Nonnegativity of diameter and (12) imply
\[
 \max_{\ell\in\{0,1\}}
 \mathbb E_{P_\ell}[\operatorname{diam}(K)]
 \geq\Delta[\Phi(z_{1-\alpha}-\rho)-\alpha]_+.
 \tag{13}
\]

For attainability, take the displayed regions \(A_0\) and \(A_1\) and report exactly the corresponding subset of \(\{\tau_0,\tau_1\}\). Equation (9) gives \(P_0(A_0)=P_1(A_1)=1-\alpha\). Their intersection is empty up to a null boundary when
\(
 \rho/2-z_{1-\alpha}\geq-\rho/2+z_{1-\alpha}
\), equivalently \(\rho\geq2z_{1-\alpha}\). In that case the risk is zero. If \(\rho<2z_{1-\alpha}\), then under state zero
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P_0(A_0\cap A_1)
 &=\Phi(z_{1-\alpha})-\Phi(\rho-z_{1-\alpha})\\
 &=\Phi(z_{1-\alpha}-\rho)-\alpha,
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
and the same calculation under state one gives the same probability. The rule's diameter is exactly \(\Delta\) on the intersection and zero otherwise, so (14) attains (13). Since \(0<\alpha<1/2\) implies \(z_{1-\alpha}>0\), the positive-part expression vanishes exactly at and above \(2z_{1-\alpha}\). For \(\Gamma=I_2\), \(\mu_0=(0,c)^{\top}\), and \(\mu_1=(c,0)^{\top}\), direct substitution gives \(\rho=(c^2+c^2)^{1/2}=\sqrt2c\).

Suppose next that the two states, with the same exactly observed \(b\), are contained in \(\mathcal H_{H,B}^{\mathrm{sc}}\). Any kernel honest on the full score-face space is honest at those two states, and its supremum risk over the full space is at least its maximum risk over the pair. Taking the infimum over full-space kernels and using (13)--(14) proves the displayed general-\(J\) lower bound by restriction.

It remains to prove the finite upper bound. Partition \(G\sim N(0,\Gamma)\) and \(X^{\mathrm{sc}}\) into their \(q\)-dimensional label blocks. Every principal block \(\Gamma_{jj}\) is positive definite because \(\Gamma\) is positive definite, so \(\Gamma_{jj}^{-1/2}\) exists. Define
\[
 M=\max_{j\in\mathcal J}
 \lVert\Gamma_{jj}^{-1/2}G_j\rVert_2.
\]
The random variable \(M\) is finite almost surely. Its distribution is continuous because \(G\) has a Lebesgue density and every level set of any nonconstant quadratic norm has Lebesgue measure zero. Hence the finite quantile \(k_{1-\alpha}^{\mathrm{sc}}(\Gamma)\) in the statement satisfies
\[
 \Pr\{M\leq k_{1-\alpha}^{\mathrm{sc}}(\Gamma)\}=1-\alpha.
 \tag{15}
\]
Use the displayed score-screen hull. Under parameter \((h,b)\), the centered vector \(X^{\mathrm{sc}}-h\) has the same joint law as \(G\), including all covariance induced by shared controls. On the event in (15), every label with \(h_j=0\) is screened in. The definition of \(\mathcal H_{H,B}^{\mathrm{sc}}\) guarantees at least one such label, and its coherence restriction guarantees that every such label has value \(\tau(h,b)\). Therefore
\[
 \Pr_{h,\Gamma}\{\tau(h,b)\in K_{\mathrm{scr}}(X^{\mathrm{sc}},b)\}
 \geq1-\alpha
\]
uniformly over \((h,b)\in\mathcal H_{H,B}^{\mathrm{sc}}\), including faces with several zero-score labels. Because the accepted values form a subset of \(\{b_1,\ldots,b_J\}\), the spread restriction gives, for every observation,
\[
 \operatorname{diam}\{K_{\mathrm{scr}}(X^{\mathrm{sc}},b)\}
 \leq \max_j b_j-\min_j b_j\leq B.
\]
The screen is measurable because it is determined by finitely many continuous inequalities, and the finite convex hull has measurable minimum and maximum endpoints on each acceptance pattern. Thus it is an admissible confidence kernel, proving \(\mathfrak R_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{sc}}(H,B,\Gamma)\leq B\). The lower bound by zero follows from nonnegativity of diameter.

%%% FIELD stmt-two-donor-current
In the explicitly two-dimensional observation experiment \(Z^{\mathrm{sw}}\), the minimum over all confidence kernels \(K:\mathbb R^2\rightsquigarrow2^{\{0,\Delta\}}\) satisfying \(\min_{\ell\in\{0,1\}}\Pr_{\ell}\{\ell\Delta\in K(Z^{\mathrm{sw}})\}\geq1-\alpha\) of \(\max_{\ell\in\{0,1\}}\mathbb E_{\ell}[\operatorname{diam}(K(Z^{\mathrm{sw}}))]\) is \(r_2(c,\Delta,\alpha)=\Delta[\Phi(z_{1-\alpha}-\sqrt2c)-\alpha]_+\). The rule \(C^{\mathrm{NP}}_{c,\Delta,\alpha}\) attains this value, including the zero-overlap regime. Under the \(J=2\), \(q=1\) panel submodel with \(b=(0,\Delta)^{\top}\), \(L_{\mathrm{sw}}\Omega L_{\mathrm{sw}}^{\top}=I_2\), and state-invariant independent \(U^{\mathrm{sw}}\), the full observation \(Z^{\mathrm{pan}}\) is equivalent to the switch experiment augmented by an ancillary variable, so the same minimax value and rule apply.
