Let \(0<\alpha<1/2\). Let \(P_0=N(\mu_0,\Gamma)\) and \(P_1=N(\mu_1,\Gamma)\) be Gaussian score laws on \(\mathbb R^{Jq}\), where \(\Gamma\in\mathbb S_{++}^{Jq}\). Let their target values be \(\tau_0,\tau_1\in\mathbb R\), and put
\[
 \Delta=|\tau_1-\tau_0|>0,
 \qquad
 \rho=\bigl((\mu_1-\mu_0)^{\top}\Gamma^{-1}(\mu_1-\mu_0)\bigr)^{1/2}>0.
\]
Among all measurable, possibly randomized confidence kernels satisfying \(P_\ell\{\tau_\ell\in K\}\geq1-\alpha\) for \(\ell=0,1\),
\[
 \inf_K\max_{\ell\in\{0,1\}}
 \mathbb E_{P_\ell}[\operatorname{diam}(K)]
 =\Delta\bigl[\Phi(z_{1-\alpha}-\rho)-\alpha\bigr]_+.
\]
The value is attained by
\[
 A_0=\{T\leq-\rho/2+z_{1-\alpha}\},
 \qquad
 A_1=\{T\geq\rho/2-z_{1-\alpha}\},
\]
and
\[
 T=\frac{(\mu_1-\mu_0)^{\top}\Gamma^{-1}
 (X^{\mathrm{sc}}-(\mu_0+\mu_1)/2)}{\rho},
 \qquad
 K^*(X^{\mathrm{sc}})
 =\{\tau_0:X^{\mathrm{sc}}\in A_0\}
 \cup\{\tau_1:X^{\mathrm{sc}}\in A_1\}.
\]
Consequently the exact value is zero if and only if \(\rho\geq2z_{1-\alpha}\). For the identity-covariance two-donor switch \(\mu_0=(0,c)^{\top}\) and \(\mu_1=(c,0)^{\top}\), one has \(\rho=\sqrt{2}c\). If two such states with a common exactly observed vector \(b\) form a restriction of \(\mathcal H_{H,B}^{\mathrm{sc}}\), then
\[
 \mathfrak R_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{sc}}(H,B,\Gamma)
 \geq
 \Delta\bigl[\Phi(z_{1-\alpha}-\rho)-\alpha\bigr]_+.

Let \(0<\alpha<1/2\). Let \(P_0=N(\mu_0,\Gamma)\) and \(P_1=N(\mu_1,\Gamma)\) be Gaussian score laws on \(\mathbb R^{Jq}\), where \(\Gamma\in\mathbb S_{++}^{Jq}\). Let their target values be \(\tau_0,\tau_1\in\mathbb R\), and put
\[
 \Delta=|\tau_1-\tau_0|>0,
 \qquad
 \rho=\bigl((\mu_1-\mu_0)^{\top}\Gamma^{-1}(\mu_1-\mu_0)\bigr)^{1/2}>0.

Among all measurable, possibly randomized confidence kernels satisfying \(P_\ell\{\tau_\ell\in K\}\geq1-\alpha\) for \(\ell=0,1\),

 \inf_K\max_{\ell\in\{0,1\}}

\]
The value is attained by

 A_0=\{T\leq-\rho/2+z_{1-\alpha}\},
 \qquad

and

%%% FIELD pair-proof
Write \(d=\mu_1-\mu_0\). Positive definiteness of \(\Gamma\) implies that \(\Gamma^{-1}\) exists. The assumed strict inequality \(\rho>0\) permits division by \(\rho\). Direct calculation from the two Gaussian laws gives
\[
 T\sim N(-\rho/2,1)\quad\text{under }P_0,
 \qquad
 T\sim N(\rho/2,1)\quad\text{under }P_1,
 \tag{1}
\]
because
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_{P_\ell}(T)
 =\rho^{-2}d^{\top}\Gamma^{-1}\Gamma\Gamma^{-1}d=1,
\]
and the likelihood ratio is
\[
 \frac{dP_1}{dP_0}(X^{\mathrm{sc}})
 =\exp\!\left\{d^{\top}\Gamma^{-1}
 \left(X^{\mathrm{sc}}-\frac{\mu_0+\mu_1}{2}\right)\right\}
 =\exp(\rho T).
 \tag{2}
\]

We first prove the testing inequality needed for the lower bound. Set
\[
 t_0=-\rho/2+z_{1-\alpha},
 \qquad
 D^*=\{T>t_0\}.
\]
Equation (1) and the definition \(z_{1-\alpha}=\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha)\) give
\[
 P_0(D^*)=\alpha,
 \qquad
 P_1(D^*)=1-\Phi(z_{1-\alpha}-\rho)
 =\Phi(\rho-z_{1-\alpha}).
 \tag{3}
\]
Let \(D\) be any measurable, possibly randomized event with \(P_0(D)\leq\alpha\), and put \(k=\exp(\rho t_0)>0\). Let \(f_0,f_1\) be the two Gaussian densities with respect to Lebesgue measure; after adjoining state-independent randomization with law \(\nu\), the joint densities with respect to the product of Lebesgue measure and \(\nu\) are \(f_0,f_1\). By (2), \(f_1-kf_0\) is nonpositive on \((D^*)^c\) and nonnegative on \(D^*\). Hence, pointwise on the augmented observation space,
\[
 (\mathbf 1_D-\mathbf 1_{D^*})(f_1-kf_0)\leq0.
\]
Integration and \(P_0(D)\leq P_0(D^*)\) yield
\[
 P_1(D)-P_1(D^*)
 \leq k\{P_0(D)-P_0(D^*)\}\leq0.
 \tag{4}
\]

Let \(K\) satisfy the two coverage inequalities, and define
\[
 A_0=\{\tau_0\in K\},
 \qquad
 A_1=\{\tau_1\in K\},
 \qquad
 O=A_0\cap A_1.
\]
For \(D=A_1\setminus A_0\), coverage at state zero gives \(P_0(D)\leq P_0(A_0^c)\leq\alpha\), whereas coverage at state one gives
\[
 P_1(D)=P_1(A_1)-P_1(O)\geq1-\alpha-P_1(O).
\]
Combining this inequality with (3)--(4) proves
\[
 P_1(O)
 \geq1-\alpha-\Phi(\rho-z_{1-\alpha})
 =\Phi(z_{1-\alpha}-\rho)-\alpha.
 \tag{5}
\]
On \(O\), the set \(K\) contains both target values, so the definition of diameter gives \(\operatorname{diam}(K)\geq\Delta\). Since diameter is nonnegative, (5) implies
\[
 \max_{\ell\in\{0,1\}}
 \mathbb E_{P_\ell}[\operatorname{diam}(K)]
 \geq
 \Delta\bigl[\Phi(z_{1-\alpha}-\rho)-\alpha\bigr]_+.
 \tag{6}
\]

For attainment, use the displayed regions \(A_0,A_1\) and report exactly the corresponding subset of \(\{\tau_0,\tau_1\}\). Equation (1) gives \(P_0(A_0)=P_1(A_1)=1-\alpha\), so the rule is honest. The two regions have a null intersection when \(\rho\geq2z_{1-\alpha}\). When \(\rho<2z_{1-\alpha}\), their intersection has, under state zero,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P_0(A_0\cap A_1)
 &=\Phi(z_{1-\alpha})-\Phi(\rho-z_{1-\alpha})\\
 &=\Phi(z_{1-\alpha}-\rho)-\alpha,
\end{aligned}
 \tag{7}
\]
and the same probability under state one. The reported set has diameter \(\Delta\) precisely on this intersection and diameter zero otherwise. Thus (7) attains (6). Since \(0<\alpha<1/2\) implies \(z_{1-\alpha}>0\), the positive part vanishes exactly when \(\rho\geq2z_{1-\alpha}\).

For \(\Gamma=I_2\), \(\mu_0=(0,c)^{\top}\), and \(\mu_1=(c,0)^{\top}\), the definition of \(\rho\) gives \(\rho=(c^2+c^2)^{1/2}=\sqrt{2}c\). Finally, suppose the two states with their common observed vector \(b\) lie in \(\mathcal H_{H,B}^{\mathrm{sc}}\). Every kernel honest on that entire space satisfies the two statewise coverage constraints, and its supremum risk is at least its maximum risk over the pair. Taking the infimum over full-space kernels and applying (6)--(7) gives the stated restriction lower bound.
%%% FIELD general-statement
Let \(0<\alpha<1/2\), \(H>0\), \(B>0\), \(q\geq1\), and \(\Gamma\in\mathbb S_{++}^{Jq}\). In the score-only experiment and parameter space of Definitions \(\mathfrak R_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{sc}}(H,B,\Gamma)\) and \(\mathcal H_{H,B}^{\mathrm{sc}}\),
\[
 \mathfrak R_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{sc}}(H,B,\Gamma)
 =
 \begin{cases}
 0, & J=1,\\[2mm]
 B\left(1-\dfrac{J\alpha}{J-1}\right), & J\geq2.
 \end{cases}
\]
For \(J\geq2\), an attaining data-free randomized confidence kernel reports \(\operatorname{conv}\{b_1,\ldots,b_J\}\) with probability \(1-J\alpha/(J-1)\); with the remaining probability it draws \(L\) uniformly from \(\mathcal J\) and reports \(\{b_L\}\). This result allows several zero-score labels and arbitrary positive definite shared-control covariance \(\Gamma\). The deterministic simultaneous score-screen hull
\[
 K_{\mathrm{scr}}(x,b)
 =\operatorname{conv}\left\{b_j:
 \lVert\Gamma_{jj}^{-1/2}x_j\rVert_2
 \leq k_{1-\alpha}^{\mathrm{sc}}(\Gamma)
 \right\},
\]
with the empty hull interpreted as empty and \(k_{1-\alpha}^{\mathrm{sc}}(\Gamma)\) the \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile of \(\max_{j\in\mathcal J}\lVert\Gamma_{jj}^{-1/2}G_j\rVert_2\) for \(G\sim N(0,\Gamma)\), is uniformly honest and has diameter at most \(B\); it is an operational comparator, not the kernel used to establish the exact minimax upper bound.
%%% FIELD general-proof
If \(J=1\), the defining condition \(\min_j\lVert h_j\rVert_2=0\) forces \(h_1=0\), and the definition of the score-only experiment reveals \(b_1=\tau(h,b)\) without noise. The deterministic rule \(K=\{b_1\}\) has coverage one and diameter zero. Nonnegativity of diameter proves the value zero.

Now suppose \(J\geq2\), and set
\[
 p_\alpha=1-\frac{J\alpha}{J-1}.
\]
Because \(J/(J-1)\leq2\) and \(0<\alpha<1/2\), one has \(0<p_\alpha<1\). Consider the data-free kernel in the statement. If \((h,b)\in\mathcal H_{H,B}^{\mathrm{sc}}\), the nonempty-zero-face condition supplies at least one \(j_0\) with \(h_{j_0}=0\), and the coherence condition in the same definition gives \(b_{j_0}=\tau(h,b)\). Therefore its coverage probability is at least
\[
 p_\alpha+\frac{1-p_\alpha}{J}
 =1-\frac{J\alpha}{J-1}+\frac{\alpha}{J-1}
 =1-\alpha.
 \tag{8}
\]
The kernel is measurable: \(b\mapsto(\min_j b_j,\max_j b_j)\) and \(b\mapsto b_L\) are Borel maps, and the rule is a finite mixture of their associated interval and singleton kernels.
On the full-hull branch its diameter is \(\max_j b_j-\min_j b_j\leq B\), by the bounded-spread condition, and on the singleton branch its diameter is zero. Hence its worst-case expected diameter is at most \(p_\alpha B\). Equality is attained within the parameter space: choose a unit vector \(e\in\mathbb R^q\), any \(c\in(0,H]\), let \(h_1=0\) and \(h_k=ce\) for \(k\neq1\), and choose \(b\) with spread exactly \(B\). This point has a unique zero label, so the coherence implication is satisfied, and the kernel's expected diameter there is \(p_\alpha B\). Thus
\[
 \mathfrak R_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{sc}}(H,B,\Gamma)\leq p_\alpha B.
 \tag{9}
\]

For the matching lower bound, fix the exactly observed grid
\[
 b_j^*=\frac{(j-1)B}{J-1},\qquad j\in\mathcal J,
 \tag{10}
\]
and a unit vector \(e\in\mathbb R^q\). For each \(j\in\mathcal J\) and \(c\in(0,H]\), define the score mean \(h^{(j,c)}\) by
\[
 h_j^{(j,c)}=0,
 \qquad
 h_k^{(j,c)}=ce\quad(k\neq j).
 \tag{11}
\]
Equations (10)--(11) define a member of \(\mathcal H_{H,B}^{\mathrm{sc}}\): its maximum score norm is \(c\leq H\), its effect spread is \(B\), and its unique zero label makes the coherence implication automatic. Its target is \(b_j^*\). Let
\[
 P_j^c=N(h^{(j,c)},\Gamma),
 \qquad
 Q=N(0,\Gamma).
\]
The reference law \(Q\), paired with the nonconstant grid \(b^*\), need not belong to the parameter space; it is used only as a common dominating measure.

We record explicitly why these laws coalesce. For \(m=h^{(j,c)}\), put \(\delta_{j,c}^2=m^{\top}\Gamma^{-1}m\). Under \(Q\),
\[
 L_{j,c}(X)=\frac{dP_j^c}{dQ}(X)
 =\exp\!\left(m^{\top}\Gamma^{-1}X-\frac{\delta_{j,c}^2}{2}\right),
\]
and the normal moment-generating formula gives
\[
 \mathbb E_Q[(L_{j,c}-1)^2]=\exp(\delta_{j,c}^2)-1.
 \tag{12}
\]
Since \(m=c v_j\) for a fixed vector \(v_j\), positive definiteness of \(\Gamma\) gives \(\delta_{j,c}^2=c^2v_j^{\top}\Gamma^{-1}v_j\to0\). Define
\[
 \varepsilon_c=max_{j\in\mathcal J}
 \sup_{0\leq f\leq1}
 \left|\mathbb E_{P_j^c}f-\mathbb E_Qf\right|.
\]
By Cauchy--Schwarz and (12),
\[
 \varepsilon_c
 \leq\max_{j\in\mathcal J}
 \{\exp(\delta_{j,c}^2)-1\}^{1/2}
 \longrightarrow0.
 \tag{13}
\]
The same bound holds after adjoining the state-independent randomization used by any confidence kernel, because its likelihood ratio remains \(L_{j,c}(X)\).

Let \(K\) be any kernel honest on \(\mathcal H_{H,B}^{\mathrm{sc}}\), evaluated at the common observed vector \(b^*\), and let
\[
 N_K=\sum_{j=1}^J\mathbf 1\{b_j^*\in K\}.
\]
Honesty at the parameter (11) and (13) imply, for every \(j\),
\[
 Q\{b_j^*\in K\}
 \geq P_j^c\{b_j^*\in K\}-\varepsilon_c
 \geq1-\alpha-\varepsilon_c.
 \tag{14}
\]
Define \(D_K\) as the range of the grid points from (10) that lie in \(K\), with \(D_K=0\) when no grid point lies in \(K\). Then \(0\leq D_K\leq B\), \(D_K\leq\operatorname{diam}(K)\), and the equal grid spacing gives the pointwise inequality
\[
 D_K\geq\frac{B}{J-1}(N_K-1).
 \tag{15}
\]
Indeed, for \(N_K\geq1\), the largest and smallest of \(N_K\) distinct grid indices differ by at least \(N_K-1\); for \(N_K=0\), the right-hand side of (15) is negative. Summing (14) and using (15) yields
\[
 \mathbb E_QD_K
 \geq\frac{B}{J-1}\{J(1-\alpha-\varepsilon_c)-1\}
 =p_\alpha B-\frac{JB}{J-1}\varepsilon_c.
 \tag{16}
\]
Because \(0\leq D_K/B\leq1\), the definition of \(\varepsilon_c\) also gives
\[
 \left|\mathbb E_{P_j^c}D_K-\mathbb E_QD_K\right|
 \leq B\varepsilon_c.
 \tag{17}
\]
All the laws \(P_j^c\) are admissible parameter points. Hence (16)--(17) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sup_{(h,b)\in\mathcal H_{H,B}^{\mathrm{sc}}}
 \mathbb E_{h,\Gamma}[\operatorname{diam}(K)]
 &\geq \max_{j\in\mathcal J}\mathbb E_{P_j^c}D_K\\
 &\geq \frac1J\sum_{j=1}^J\mathbb E_{P_j^c}D_K\\
 &\geq p_\alpha B
 -B\left(1+\frac{J}{J-1}\right)\varepsilon_c.
\end{aligned}
 \tag{18}
\]
Letting \(c\downarrow0\) in (18) and using (13) proves that every honest kernel has worst-case expected diameter at least \(p_\alpha B\). Taking the infimum over kernels and combining with (9) proves the exact value. The argument uses unique-zero faces approaching the excluded incoherent limit, so it also covers a parameter space containing multiple-zero faces and does not require diagonal \(\Gamma\).

For completeness, partition \(G\sim N(0,\Gamma)\) into its \(q\)-dimensional blocks. Every principal block \(\Gamma_{jj}\) is positive definite. The maximum \(M=\max_j\lVert\Gamma_{jj}^{-1/2}G_j\rVert_2\) has a continuous distribution: \(G\) has a Lebesgue density, and each level set \(\{\lVert\Gamma_{jj}^{-1/2}G_j\rVert_2=t\}\) has Lebesgue measure zero. Thus its displayed quantile satisfies \(\Pr\{M\leq k_{1-\alpha}^{\mathrm{sc}}(\Gamma)\}=1-\alpha\). Under any admissible \((h,b)\), on the event
\[
 \max_j\lVert\Gamma_{jj}^{-1/2}(X_j^{\mathrm{sc}}-h_j)\rVert_2
 \leq k_{1-\alpha}^{\mathrm{sc}}(\Gamma),
\]
every zero-score label is retained. Nonemptiness and coherence then put \(\tau(h,b)\) in the deterministic hull. Its coverage is therefore at least \(1-\alpha\), and its diameter is at most the defining spread bound \(B\). This verifies the stated operational comparison without using it for minimax attainment.
