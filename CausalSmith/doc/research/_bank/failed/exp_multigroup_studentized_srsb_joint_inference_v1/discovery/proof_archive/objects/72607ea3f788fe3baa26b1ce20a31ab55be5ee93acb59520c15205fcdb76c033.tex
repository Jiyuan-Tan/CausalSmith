Fix a block \(b\) and condition throughout on \(\mathcal F_{b-1}\).  All expectations and covariances in the first part of the proof are conditional on this history.  Under \(P_0\), exchangeability and the balance constraint give
\[
\mathbb E_0x_{gb}=0,
\qquad
\mathbb E_0(x_{gb}x_{hb})=-\frac1{G-1}\quad(g\ne h).
\tag{1}
\]
Indeed, the first equality follows by complement symmetry, and expanding \((\sum_gx_{gb})^2=0\), then using exchangeability and \(x_{gb}^2=1\), gives the second.  Hence, for arbitrary deterministic vectors \(r_1,\ldots,r_G\),
\[
\operatorname{Var}_0\!\left(G^{-1/2}\sum_gx_{gb}r_g\right)
=\frac1{G-1}\sum_g(r_g-\bar r)(r_g-\bar r)^\top,
\qquad
\bar r=G^{-1}\sum_gr_g.
\tag{2}
\]
The definition of \(V_{gb}\) and the centering of \(H_g\) imply \(\sum_gV_{gb}=0\): for \(b\ge2\), the lagged-outcome term sums to \(G\bar Y^{\mathrm{obs}}_{b-1}-G\bar Y^{\mathrm{obs}}_{b-1}=0\), and the claim for \(b=1\) is immediate.  Applying (1)--(2) to \(a_{gb}\) and to \(V_{gb}/\sigma_b\), and using the definition of \(W_b\), yields
\[
\operatorname{Var}_0(A_b)
=\frac1{G-1}\sum_g(a_{gb}-\bar a_b)(a_{gb}-\bar a_b)^\top,
\quad
\operatorname{Cov}_0(A_b,W_b)
=\frac1{(G-1)\sigma_b}\sum_g(a_{gb}-\bar a_b)V_{gb},
\quad
\operatorname{Var}_0(W_b)=1.
\tag{3}
\]
The division by \(\sigma_b\) is legitimate because \(\texttt{ass:imbalance-variance-window}\) gives \(\sigma_b^2\ge c_\sigma>0\).

By \(\texttt{lem:score-decomposition}\), \(R_b=A_b+U_b\), \(\mathbb E(U_b\mid x_b,\mathcal F_{b-1})=0\), the cross covariance of \(A_b\) and \(U_b\) is zero, and the conditional covariance contributed by \(U_b\) is \(K_b\).  Because \(W_b\) is a function of \(x_b\), the same conditional-centering identity also gives \(\operatorname{Cov}_0(U_b,W_b)=0\).  Thus (3), the definitions of \(\Sigma_{b,0}\) and \(\beta_b\), and iterated expectation imply
\[
\mathbb E_0R_b=0,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_0(R_b)=\Sigma_{b,0},
\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}_0(R_b,W_b)=\beta_b,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_0(W_b)=1.
\tag{4}
\]

Let \((R_b^\star,W_b^\star)\) be the centered covariance-matched Gaussian pair.  Equation (4) implies \(W_b^\star\sim N(0,1)\).  Put
\[
E_b^\star=R_b^\star-\beta_bW_b^\star.
\tag{5}
\]
Direct covariance algebra using (4) gives
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(E_b^\star,W_b^\star)=0,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(E_b^\star)=\Sigma_{b,0}-\beta_b\beta_b^\top.
\tag{6}
\]
The joint characteristic function of the Gaussian pair in (6) therefore factors, so \(E_b^\star\) and \(W_b^\star\) are independent.  This argument inverts only the scalar variance \(\operatorname{Var}(W_b^\star)=1\), and hence remains valid when the score covariance is singular.

For \(Z\sim N(0,1)\), completing the square in its density gives
\[
\mathbb E e^{-\kappa Z^2}=(1+2\kappa)^{-1/2}.
\tag{7}
\]
Differentiation under the integral is justified by the integrable envelope \(z^2e^{-z^2/2}\), and differentiation of (7) gives
\[
\mathbb E\{Z^2e^{-\kappa Z^2}\}=(1+2\kappa)^{-3/2}.
\tag{8}
\]
Consequently,
\[
c_\star:=\mathbb Ef_{\eta,\kappa}(Z)
=\eta+(1-\eta)(1+2\kappa)^{-1/2}\ge\eta>0,
\tag{9}
\]
and (8)--(9), together with \(\texttt{def:projection-gain}\), imply
\[
\frac{\mathbb E\{Z^2f_{\eta,\kappa}(Z)\}}{c_\star}=v_{\eta,\kappa}.
\tag{10}
\]
Using \(R_b^\star=\beta_bW_b^\star+E_b^\star\), the independence and centering in (6), and (10), we obtain
\[
\frac{\mathbb E\{R_b^\star R_b^{\star\top}f_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b^\star)\}}
     {\mathbb Ef_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b^\star)}
=\Sigma_{b,0}-(1-v_{\eta,\kappa})\beta_b\beta_b^\top.
\tag{11}
\]

It remains to transfer (11) to the actual soft design.  Under complementation \(x\mapsto-x\), the pair \((A_b,W_b)\) becomes \((-A_b,-W_b)\), while \(f_{\eta,\kappa}\) is even.  The complement-symmetric law in \(\texttt{def:soft-two-stage-design}\) therefore gives \(\mathbb E_{\mathrm{RR}}(A_b)=0\).  Conditional centering of \(U_b\) from \(\texttt{lem:score-decomposition}\) then gives \(\mathbb E_{\mathrm{RR}}(R_b)=0\).  The Radon--Nikodym ratio defining the soft design consequently yields
\[
\Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}
=\frac{\mathbb E_0\{R_bR_b^\top f_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b)\}}
       {\mathbb E_0\{f_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b)\}}.
\tag{12}
\]
Both the denominator in (12) and its Gaussian counterpart in (11) are at least \(\eta\).  Moreover, covariance matching, \(0<f_{\eta,\kappa}\le1\), Cauchy--Schwarz, and \(\texttt{lem:block-moments}\) give
\[
\left\|\mathbb E\{R_b^\star R_b^{\star\top}f_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b^\star)\}\right\|
\le \mathbb E\|R_b^\star\|^2
=\mathbb E_0\|R_b\|^2
\le\{\mathbb E_0\|R_b\|^4\}^{1/2}
\le C_R^{1/2}.
\tag{13}
\]
Write \(N,D\) for the numerator and denominator in (12), and \(N^\star,D^\star\) for their Gaussian counterparts.  Since \(D,D^\star\ge\eta\),
\[
\left\|\frac ND-\frac{N^\star}{D^\star}\right\|
\le\frac{\|N-N^\star\|}{\eta}
+\frac{\|N^\star\|\,|D-D^\star|}{\eta^2}.
\tag{14}
\]
By \(\texttt{thm:weighted-gaussian-transport}\), the two discrepancies on the right of (14) are bounded by a deterministic sequence tending to zero uniformly over blocks, histories, and members of \(\mathcal M_{G,B}\).  Equations (11)--(14) therefore prove
\[
\Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}
=\Sigma_{b,0}-(1-v_{\eta,\kappa})\beta_b\beta_b^\top+r_{Gb},
\qquad
\sup_{b,\mathcal F_{b-1}}\|r_{Gb}\|\longrightarrow0
\tag{15}
\]
as \(G\to\infty\).  The blockwise bound (15) contains no \(B\), so no restriction on \(G/B\) is imposed.

Now take any sequence with \(G\to\infty\) on which \(\texttt{ass:cr-covariance-stabilization}\), \(\texttt{ass:rr-covariance-stabilization}\), and \(\texttt{ass:projection-stabilization}\) hold.  Averaging (15) over the \(B\) blocks gives
\[
\left\|
\frac1B\sum_b\Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}
-\frac1B\sum_b\Sigma_{b,0}
+(1-v_{\eta,\kappa})\frac1B\sum_b\beta_b\beta_b^\top
\right\|
\le\sup_{b,\mathcal F_{b-1}}\|r_{Gb}\|\longrightarrow0.
\tag{16}
\]
The three stated stabilizations and uniqueness of limits in probability applied to (16) imply
\[
\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}=\Omega_{\mathrm{CR}}-(1-v_{\eta,\kappa})P.
\tag{17}
\]
No positive-definiteness condition enters this argument.

Finally, \(P\succeq0\).  Indeed, for every fixed \(c\in\mathbb R^2\), the random quantity
\[
\frac1B\sum_b(c^\top\beta_b)^2
\]
is nonnegative and converges in probability to the deterministic number \(c^\top Pc\), which therefore cannot be negative.  Since \(\kappa>0\) and \(0<\eta<1\),
\[
(1+2\kappa)^{-3/2}<(1+2\kappa)^{-1/2},
\qquad
0<v_{\eta,\kappa}<1.
\tag{18}
\]
For every fixed \(c\ne0\), (17) consequently gives
\[
c^\top\Omega_{\mathrm{CR}}c-c^\top\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}c
=(1-v_{\eta,\kappa})c^\top Pc.
\tag{19}
\]
The first factor on the right of (19) is strictly positive and the second is nonnegative.  Thus soft rerandomization has strictly smaller limiting variance for exactly those contrasts satisfying \(c^\top Pc>0\), proving every assertion.
