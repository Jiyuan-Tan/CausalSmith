We verify every member condition and every displayed calculation.  Give each of the four pairs \((H_g,J_g)\in\{-1,1\}^2\) exactly \(G/4\) groups.  The endpoints in \(\texttt{def:adaptive-witness}\) are constant over units.  With \(\theta_g=4a_{Dg}\), \(\delta_g=L-\theta_g\), and \(h_g=a_{Sg}/2+a_{Dg}-L/2\), direct substitution at \(q_L=1/4\) and \(q_H=3/4\) gives
\[
 m_{gbq_H}-m_{gbq_L}=(a_{Dg},a_{Sg})^\top.                    \tag{16}
\]
Indeed, the first coordinate is one half of the high-minus-low own-treatment effect, namely \(\theta_g(q_H-q_L)/2=a_{Dg}\), and the second coordinate is the corresponding high-minus-low average exposure score, which simplifies to \(a_{Sg}\).  Because each own-treatment potential endpoint is constant within group and the design fixes the treated count, \(\widehat m_{gbq}=m_{gbq}\); hence \(e_{gbq}=0\), \(T_{gbq}=0\), and \(K_b=0\).

The observed group mean under saturation \(q\) is
\[
 h_g+q(\delta_g+\theta_gq).
\]
Writing \(q=1/2+x_{gb}/4\) and using \(q^2-q=-3/16\) at both menu values gives
\[
 \bar Y^{\mathrm{obs}}_{gb}
 =\frac{a_{Sg}}2+\frac{a_{Dg}}4+\frac L4x_{gb}.               \tag{17}
\]
All four type averages and every balanced-sign average are zero, so the grand mean in (17) is zero.  Apart from the negligible initial block, an A block follows a B block and a B block follows an A block.  Thus (17), \(\lambda=1\), and the definition of \(V_{gb}\) give
\[
 V_{gb}^{A}=\frac32H_g+\frac14J_g+x_{g,b-1},
 \qquad
 V_{gb}^{B}=\frac54H_g+\frac12J_g.                            \tag{18}
\]
The A-block kernel therefore depends nontrivially on the preceding realized assignment through the preceding observed outcome; the design is genuinely adaptive and remains predictable.

The endpoints are bounded in absolute value by \(9/2\), so the family uses any fixed envelope \(M\geq9/2\).  Defining the full schedule endpoint at time \(2b\) as the average of the displayed response function evaluated at the two most recent own-treatment and saturation exposures gives the displayed value when exposures are held fixed; this extension is nonanticipating, has lag-one memory, and obeys partial and stratified interference.  Hence all causal-structural and design-based finiteness assumptions hold.

The imbalance-variance window holds uniformly over histories.  For A blocks, the finite-population norm and the reverse triangle inequality give
\[
 \sigma_{A,b}
 \geq\sqrt{\frac G{G-1}}\left(\frac{\sqrt{37}}4-1\right)>0,
\]
and boundedness gives a uniform upper bound.  B blocks have deterministic variance
\[
 \sigma_{B,b}^2=\frac G{G-1}\frac{29}{16},
\]
and the first block has variance \(G/(G-1)\).  Thus \(\texttt{ass:imbalance-variance-window}\) holds.

For any fixed centered bounded array \(z_g\), complement symmetry gives
\(\mathbb E_{\mathrm{RR}}(G^{-1}\sum_gx_{gb}z_g\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})=0\).  Under \(P_0\), the exact balanced-sampling covariance formula makes its conditional second moment \(O(G^{-1})\); the likelihood ratio \(f_{\eta,\kappa}/C_b\leq1/\eta\) transfers the same order to the soft law.  The martingale variance identity therefore implies that block averages of the cross-products \(G^{-1}\sum_gx_{g,b-1}H_g\) and \(G^{-1}\sum_gx_{g,b-1}J_g\) converge to zero in probability.  Applying this to (18) yields
\[
 \sigma_A^2\longrightarrow_P\frac{37}{16}+1=\frac{53}{16},
 \qquad \sigma_B^2\longrightarrow\frac{29}{16}.              \tag{19}
\]
Using (16)--(19) in the definition of \(\beta_b\) gives
\[
 \beta_A\longrightarrow_P\frac{(6,1)^\top}{\sqrt{53}},
 \qquad
 \beta_B\longrightarrow\frac{(2,5)^\top}{\sqrt{29}}.        \tag{20}
\]
The alternating proportions converge to one half.  Hence
\[
 P=\frac12\left\{
 \frac1{53}\begin{pmatrix}36&6\\6&1\end{pmatrix}
 +\frac1{29}\begin{pmatrix}4&10\\10&25\end{pmatrix}
 \right\}.                                                    \tag{21}
\]
Both summands in (21) are PSD.  Their generating vectors \((6,1)^\top\) and \((2,5)^\top\) are linearly independent, and explicitly
\[
 \det(P)=\frac{(6\cdot5-1\cdot2)^2}{4\cdot53\cdot29}
 =\frac{196}{1537}>0.
\]
Thus \(P\succ0\).

Under complete randomization, (16), equal representation of the four types, and \(K_b=0\) give \(B^{-1}\sum_b\Sigma_{b,0}\to I_2\).  Equations (19)--(20) verify the projection stabilization.  Before assuming RR stabilization, apply the weighted-transport theorem directly to this bounded family and use the Gaussian decomposition \(R_b^\star=\beta_bW_b^\star+E_b^\star\).  The two normal integrals displayed in the proof of \(\texttt{thm:projection-covariance}\) give the uniform block expansion
\[
 \Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}
 =\Sigma_{b,0}-(1-v_{\eta,\kappa})\beta_b\beta_b^\top+o(1).
\]
Averaging this expansion with the just-proved CR and projection limits gives
\[
 B^{-1}\sum_b\Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}
 \longrightarrow_P I_2-(1-v_{\eta,\kappa})P=:\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}.
\]
Since each \(\|\beta_A\|^2=37/53<1\), \(\|\beta_B\|^2=1\), and \(v_{\eta,\kappa}>0\), the last matrix is positive definite.  This verifies both RR covariance assumptions as well as the CR and projection assumptions; imposing the stated comparable \(G,B\) growth verifies \(\texttt{ass:joint-growth}\).  Therefore \(\mathcal W^{AB}\subset\mathcal M_{G,B}\).  Now all premises of \(\texttt{thm:projection-covariance}\) have been verified for the witness.  Finally, (21) gives \(c^\top Pc>0\) for every \(c\neq0\), so that theorem's strict-improvement criterion proves that every nonzero joint contrast is strictly improved.
