Fix an even \(G\) and condition on an arbitrary realized \(\mathcal F_{b-1}\).  All quantities in the first-stage finite population are then deterministic.  We use Euclidean norm for vectors and Frobenius norm for matrices.  The constants below depend only on the fixed envelopes and on \(\eta,\kappa\), and not on \(b\), the history, \(B\), or the particular member of \(\mathcal M_{G,B}\).

First reduce the balanced-sign problem to independent oriented pairs.  Bounded potential outcomes and the fixed contrast coefficients give a constant \(L_a<\infty\) such that \(\|a_{gb}\|\leq L_a\).  From the definition of \(V_{gb}\),
\[
 \sum_{g=1}^G V_{gb}=0,
 \qquad
 |V_{gb}|\leq K_H+2\lambda M=:L_V.
\]
The first equality uses \(\sum_gH_g=0\) and the grand-mean centering in the definition of \(V_{gb}\); the second uses \(|H_g|\leq K_H\) and \(|\bar Y^{\mathrm{obs}}_{gb}|,|\bar Y^{\mathrm{obs}}_b|\leq M\).  By \(\texttt{ass:imbalance-variance-window}\), \(\sigma_b\geq\sqrt{c_\sigma}>0\).  Put
\[
 \bar a_b=G^{-1}\sum_{g=1}^Ga_{gb},
 \qquad
 y_g=\begin{pmatrix}a_{gb}-\bar a_b\\ V_{gb}/\sigma_b\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb R^3.
\]
Then \(\sum_gy_g=0\), \(\max_g\|y_g\|\leq L_y\) for a fixed \(L_y\), and, because every \(x\in\mathcal X_G\) satisfies \(\sum_gx_g=0\),
\[
 S_G:=G^{-1/2}\sum_{g=1}^Gx_gy_g=(A_b^\top,W_b)^\top. \tag{1}
\]

Let \(\mathfrak m=\{\{r_j,s_j\}:1\leq j\leq G/2\}\) be a uniform perfect matching of \(\{1,\ldots,G\}\), and, independently, let \(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_{G/2}\) be independent Rademacher variables.  Give the two vertices of pair \(j\) the signs \((\xi_j,-\xi_j)\), after fixing an arbitrary ordering \((r_j,s_j)\) within that pair.  For each balanced sign vector, exactly \((G/2)!\) perfect matchings pair its positive vertices bijectively with its negative vertices, and the orientations are then unique.  Hence the resulting sign vector is exactly uniform on \(\mathcal X_G\).  Therefore, conditionally on \(\mathfrak m\),
\[
 S_G=G^{-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^{G/2}\xi_jd_j,
 \qquad d_j=y_{r_j}-y_{s_j},
 \qquad C_{\mathfrak m}:=\operatorname{Var}(S_G\mid\mathfrak m)
       =G^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{G/2}d_jd_j^\top.                 \tag{2}
\]
This proves, rather than assumes, the perfect-matching representation named in \(\texttt{def:perfect-matching-handle}\).

We next prove that the random covariance in (2) concentrates, including when it is singular.  A fixed unordered pair is an edge of a uniform perfect matching with probability \(1/(G-1)\).  The identity
\[
 \sum_{1\leq r<s\leq G}(y_r-y_s)(y_r-y_s)^\top
 =G\sum_{g=1}^Gy_gy_g^\top-Big(\sum_{g=1}^Gy_g\Big)
       \Big(\sum_{g=1}^Gy_g\Big)^\top
 =G\sum_{g=1}^Gy_gy_g^\top
\]
thus gives
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathfrak m}C_{\mathfrak m}
 =\frac1{G-1}\sum_{g=1}^Gy_gy_g^\top.                     \tag{3}
\]
Under the uniform balanced-sign law, exchangeability and \(\sum_gx_g=0\) give \(\mathbb E_0x_g=0\), \(\mathbb E_0x_g^2=1\), and \(\mathbb E_0x_gx_h=-1/(G-1)\) for \(g\ne h\).  Expanding (1), using \(\sum_gy_g=0\), shows that the right side of (3) is exactly
\[
 C_G:=\operatorname{Var}_0(S_G).                            \tag{4}
\]

For completeness, the required variance bound for \(C_{\mathfrak m}\) follows from an elementary exposure martingale.  Fix one entry and write its edge kernel as \(h_{rs}\); boundedness of the \(y_g\)'s gives \(|h_{rs}|\leq H_0\).  Reveal the matching one edge at a time.  If \(R\) is the set of \(r\geq4\) vertices remaining before the next edge is revealed, the conditional expectation of the unrevealed, unnormalized edge sum is
\[
 \frac1{r-1}\sum_{\{i,j\}\subset R}h_{ij}.                \tag{5}
\]
After revealing \(\{u,v\}\), it is
\[
 h_{uv}+\frac1{r-3}
       \sum_{\{i,j\}\subset R\setminus\{u,v\}}h_{ij}.    \tag{6}
\]
Subtracting (5) from (6), splitting the first sum into pairs disjoint from \(\{u,v\}\) and pairs incident to it, and using \(|h_{ij}|\leq H_0\), bounds the absolute difference by \(5H_0\).  The last edge has zero martingale increment.  Since an entry of \(C_{\mathfrak m}\) is the edge sum divided by \(G\), every increment of its Doob martingale is bounded by \(5H_0/G\).  The increments are orthogonal because each has conditional mean zero; consequently
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_{\mathfrak m}\{(C_{\mathfrak m})_{k\ell}\}
 =\sum_j\mathbb E(D_{j,k\ell}^2)
 \leq \frac G2\left(\frac{5H_0}{G}\right)^2
 \leq \frac{C}{G}.                                       \tag{7}
\]
Summing over the nine entries and applying Cauchy--Schwarz yields
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathfrak m}\|C_{\mathfrak m}-C_G\|
 \leq C G^{-1/2}.                                         \tag{8}
\]
Neither (3) nor (7) uses an eigenvalue lower bound.

Let \(h:\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb R\) be bounded with bounded derivatives through order three.  For fixed \(\mathfrak m\), replace the independent summands \(\xi_jd_j/\sqrt G\), one at a time, by \(\zeta_jd_j/\sqrt G\), where the \(\zeta_j\)'s are independent \(N(0,1)\) variables.  In the third-order Taylor expansion about the sum of all other terms, the constant terms agree, the linear terms cancel because \(\mathbb E\xi_j=\mathbb E\zeta_j=0\), and the quadratic terms cancel because \(\mathbb E\xi_j^2=\mathbb E\zeta_j^2=1\).  The integral remainder and \(\|d_j\|\leq2L_y\) therefore give
\[
 \left|\mathbb E\{h(S_G)\mid\mathfrak m\}
       -\mathbb Eh\{N(0,C_{\mathfrak m})\}\right|
 \leq C\|D^3h\|_\infty
       \sum_{j=1}^{G/2}\frac{\|d_j\|^3}{G^{3/2}}
 \leq C_hG^{-1/2}.                                        \tag{9}
\]
For positive-semidefinite \(C_1,C_2\), Gaussian integration by parts along \(C(t)=(1-t)C_1+tC_2\), first with \(C(t)+\delta I_3\), gives
\[
 \frac{d}{dt}\mathbb Eh\{N(0,C(t)+\delta I_3)\}
 =\frac12\sum_{k,\ell}(C_2-C_1)_{k\ell}
      \mathbb E\,\partial_{k\ell}h\{N(0,C(t)+\delta I_3)\}.
\]
Integrating in \(t\), bounding the Hessian, and then sending \(\delta\downarrow0\) by dominated convergence proves
\[
 |\mathbb Eh\{N(0,C_1)\}-\mathbb Eh\{N(0,C_2)\}|
 \leq C\|D^2h\|_\infty\|C_1-C_2\|.                     \tag{10}
\]
The regularization establishes (10) on the exact covariance ranges, so singularity causes no exception.  Averaging (9) and using (8)--(10) proves
\[
 \sup_{b,\,\mathcal F_{b-1},\,(Y,H)\in\mathcal M_{G,B}}
 \left|\mathbb E_0h(A_b,W_b)-\mathbb Eh(A_b^\star,W_b^\star)\right|
 \longrightarrow0                                         \tag{11}
\]
for every fixed bound on the derivatives of \(h\).  No step in (1)--(11) contains \(B\) or a restriction on \(G/B\).

Apply (11) first to \(h(a,w)=f_{\eta,\kappa}(w)\); all derivatives of this function that are used above are bounded.  This proves the scalar comparison.  To pass to the quadratic comparison, choose a smooth \(\chi_L:\mathbb R^2\to[0,1]\) equal to one on \(\{\|a\|\leq L\}\) and zero on \(\{\|a\|\geq2L\}\), and apply (11), entry by entry, to
\[
 h_{ij,L}(a,w)=a_ia_jf_{\eta,\kappa}(w)\chi_L(a).          \tag{12}
\]
By \(\texttt{lem:score-decomposition}\), \(A_b=\mathbb E_0(R_b\mid x_b,\mathcal F_{b-1})\).  Conditional Jensen and \(\texttt{lem:block-moments}\) hence give
\[
 \mathbb E_0\|A_b\|^4\leq\mathbb E_0\|R_b\|^4\leq C_R. \tag{13}
\]
The covariance of \(A_b^\star\) is bounded by (13), and the fourth moment of a centered Gaussian two-vector is a quadratic polynomial in its covariance entries; it is therefore uniformly bounded as well.  Since \(0<f_{\eta,\kappa}\leq1\), on either side of the comparison,
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\|A\|^2\mathbf1\{\|A\|>L\}
                  f_{\eta,\kappa}(W)\right]
 \leq L^{-2}\mathbb E\|A\|^4\leq C L^{-2}.              \tag{14}
\]
For fixed \(L\), (11)--(12) make the truncated discrepancy vanish as \(G\to\infty\); then (14) allows \(L\to\infty\).  This proves the asserted weighted \(A_bA_b^\top\) comparison uniformly.

It remains to add the second-stage noise.  The fixed probabilities \(p_{dq}\geq1/4\), bounded outcomes, and fixed coefficients imply a constant \(L_e\) with \(\|e_{gbq}\|\leq L_e\).  Conditional on \((x_b,\mathcal F_{b-1})\), \(\texttt{lem:score-decomposition}\) makes the vectors \(e_{gb,Q_{gb}}\) independent and centered.  Repeating the replacement calculation (9) in the two score coordinates replaces
\[
 U_b=2G^{-1/2}\sum_ge_{gb,Q_{gb}}
\]
by a conditionally centered Gaussian \(E_b(x_b)\), at error \(O(G^{-1/2})\) for every bounded smooth test of \((A_b+U_b,W_b)\), with conditional covariance
\[
 K_b(x_b)=\frac4G\sum_gT_{gb,Q_{gb}}
 =K_b+\frac2G\sum_gx_{gb}(T_{gbq_H}-T_{gbq_L}).            \tag{15}
\]
The covariance matrices \(T_{gbq}\) are uniformly bounded because \(e_{gbq}\) is.  With \(D_g=T_{gbq_H}-T_{gbq_L}\), the exact balanced-sign covariance calculation used in (4) gives
\[
 \mathbb E_0\|K_b(x_b)-K_b\|^2
 =\frac4{G(G-1)}\sum_{g=1}^G\|D_g-\bar D\|^2
 \leq \frac CG.                                           \tag{16}
\]
Apply (10) conditionally on \(x_b\), and then Cauchy--Schwarz with (16), to replace \(E_b(x_b)\) by \(E_b^\star\sim N_2(0,K_b)\), independent of \((A_b,W_b)\), at an \(O(G^{-1/2})\) cost.  Finally, for a smooth test \(h(r,w)\), the convolution
\[
 \widetilde h(a,w)=\mathbb E\{h(a+E_b^\star,w)\}
\]
has derivative bounds no larger than those of \(h\).  Thus (11) applied to \(\widetilde h\) proves smooth-test convergence of \((R_b,W_b)\) to \((A_b^\star+E_b^\star,W_b^\star)\).  By \(\texttt{lem:score-decomposition}\),
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(A_b^\star+E_b^\star)=\operatorname{Var}_0(A_b)+K_b
 =\operatorname{Var}_0(R_b),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Cov}(A_b^\star+E_b^\star,W_b^\star)
 =\operatorname{Cov}_0(R_b,W_b).                            \tag{17}
\]
Hence this centered Gaussian vector has exactly the law specified for \((R_b^\star,W_b^\star)\), including on a proper covariance range.

Use a cutoff of \(r_ir_jf_{\eta,\kappa}(w)\) exactly as in (12).  The actual fourth moment is bounded by \(\texttt{lem:block-moments}\); the covariance identity (17) and the Gaussian fourth-moment formula bound the comparison fourth moment.  The tail estimate (14), with \(R_b\) in place of \(A_b\), therefore proves the asserted weighted \(R_bR_b^\top\) comparison.

Let \(\delta_G\) be the supremum, over all admissible \(B,b\), histories, and arrays, of the sum of the three discrepancies just proved.  The uniform arguments show \(\delta_G\to0\) along even \(G\to\infty\), and (13) plus the corresponding Gaussian bounds show \(\sup_G\delta_G<\infty\).  Define
\[
 \varepsilon_G=\sup\{\delta_m:m\geq G,\ m\in2\mathbb N\}.
\]
Then \(\varepsilon_G\) is deterministic, nonincreasing, and tends to zero, and it bounds both displays in the theorem.  This completes the history-uniform transport proof with arbitrary \(B\) and without a condition on \(G/B\).
