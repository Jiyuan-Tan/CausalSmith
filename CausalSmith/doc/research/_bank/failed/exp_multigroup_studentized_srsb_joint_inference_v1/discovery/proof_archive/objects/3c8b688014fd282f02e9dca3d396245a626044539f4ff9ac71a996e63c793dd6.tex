Fix a block and condition throughout on \(\mathcal F_{b-1}\).  The fixed potential outcomes make each \(u_{k,b}\) nonrandom under this conditioning, and the logged first- and second-order probabilities are denoted by \(\pi_k:=\pi_{k,b}\) and \(\pi_{k\ell}:=\pi_{k\ell,b}\).  The definitions of the causal block target and the Horvitz--Thompson block estimator give the exact representation
\[
 R_b=\frac1{\sqrt G\,n}\sum_{k\in\mathcal K_b}
       \left(\frac{I_k}{\pi_k}-1\right)u_{k,b}.             \tag{7}
\]
All divisions in (7) are valid.  Indeed, complementation preserves \(P_0\), sends \(W_b(x)\) to \(-W_b(x)\), and leaves the even function \(f_{\eta,\kappa}\{W_b(x)\}\) unchanged.  Pairing every balanced assignment with its complement therefore gives conditional first-stage saturation probability \(1/2\).  Since \(p_{dq}\ge1/4\), the two-stage definition gives \(\pi_k=p_{dq}/2\ge1/8\).  Taking the conditional covariance of (7) and using
\(\mathbb E(I_kI_\ell\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})=\pi_{k\ell}\) yields
\[
 \Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}
 =\frac1{Gn^2}\sum_{k,\ell\in\mathcal K_b}
   \frac{\pi_{k\ell}-\pi_k\pi_\ell}{\pi_k\pi_\ell}
   u_{k,b}u_{\ell,b}^\top.                                 \tag{8}
\]

If \(\pi_{k\ell}>0\), conditional expectation of the corresponding summand in \(\widehat\Gamma_b\) replaces \(I_kI_\ell\) by \(\pi_{k\ell}\), exactly recovering its ordered-pair term in (8).  If \(k\ne\ell\) and \(\pi_{k\ell}=0\), the two ordered terms for \((k,\ell)\) and \((\ell,k)\) in (8) equal
\[
 -u_{k,b}u_{\ell,b}^\top-u_{\ell,b}u_{k,b}^\top.
\]
For the corresponding unordered pair, the completion in \(\widehat\Gamma_b\) has conditional expectation
\(u_{k,b}u_{k,b}^\top+u_{\ell,b}u_{\ell,b}^\top\).  Its excess over the two missing covariance terms is therefore
\[
 (u_{k,b}+u_{\ell,b})(u_{k,b}+u_{\ell,b})^\top\succeq0.   \tag{9}
\]
Summing (9) over \(\mathcal Z_b\), with the normalization in \(\texttt{def:completed-studentizer}\), proves
\[
 \mathbb E(\widehat\Gamma_b\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})
 =\Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}+D_b,
 \qquad D_b\succeq0.                                      \tag{10}
\]
The completion is observable because it uses only realized cell outcomes and logged design probabilities.  It introduces no unobserved cross-potential-outcome product.

We record why the matrices in (10) are uniformly bounded even when \(G\) does not diverge.  The outcome envelope and the fixed contrast coefficients give \(\sup_{k,b}\|u_{k,b}\|<\infty\).  By \(\texttt{lem:pair-probability-control}\), for all sufficiently large \(G\), feasible cross-group pair probabilities are bounded away from zero and their covariance coefficients are \(O(G^{-1})\), whereas feasible within-group pair probabilities are bounded away from zero.  There are \(O(G^2n^2)\) ordered cross-group pairs, each carrying after the outer normalization a coefficient of order \(O(G^{-2}n^{-2})\), and \(O(Gn^2)\) within-group pairs, each carrying a coefficient of order \(O(G^{-1}n^{-2})\).  The impossible-pair completion has the same bounded count-normalization product.  The finitely many smaller admissible values of \(G\) are also uniformly controlled: \(f_{\eta,\kappa}\ge\eta>0\) gives the soft law the same finite first-stage support as \(P_0\), and the fixed-count second stage has a positive minimum over every feasible cell pair.  Hence there is a constant \(C_\Gamma<\infty\), independent of \(B\), \(G\), the block, and the realized history, such that
\[
 \|\widehat\Gamma_b\|_F+\|D_b\|_F\le C_\Gamma.           \tag{11}
\]
Because the soft weight is strictly positive on all of \(\mathcal X_G\), the zero-pair set is the zero-pair set of the underlying balanced first stage and fixed-count second stage; it does not depend on the numerical, history-dependent tilt.  Thus the fixed potential outcomes make \(D_b\) deterministic.

Put
\[
 M_b=\widehat\Gamma_b-
     \mathbb E(\widehat\Gamma_b\mid\mathcal F_{b-1}).
\]
Then \(\{M_b,\mathcal F_b\}\) is an entrywise matrix martingale-difference sequence.  Conditional orthogonality across distinct blocks and (11) give
\[
 \mathbb E\left\|B^{-1}\sum_{b=1}^BM_b\right\|_F^2
 =B^{-2}\sum_{b=1}^B\mathbb E\|M_b\|_F^2
 \le\frac{4C_\Gamma^2}{B}\longrightarrow0.                \tag{12}
\]
Therefore \(B^{-1}\sum_bM_b=o_P(1)\).  Averaging (10), applying (12), and then using \(\texttt{ass:rr-covariance-stabilization}\) proves
\[
 \widehat\Gamma
 =\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}+B^{-1}\sum_{b=1}^BD_b+o_P(1).       \tag{13}
\]
Again, only \(B\to\infty\) was used.

The deterministic positive-semidefinite averages
\(\bar D_B=B^{-1}\sum_bD_b\) are bounded by (11).  Every subsequence therefore has a further subsequence on which \(\bar D_B\to D_\infty\succeq0\); along that subsequence, (13) yields
\[
 \widehat\Gamma\longrightarrow_P
 \Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}+D_\infty\succeq\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}. \tag{14}
\]
This proves the asserted Loewner domination of every probability limit point.  More directly, the eigenvalue perturbation inequality, (13), and \(\bar D_B\succeq0\) imply
\[
 \lambda_{\min}(\widehat\Gamma)
 \ge\lambda_{\min}(\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}})+o_P(1).          \tag{15}
\]
Assumption \(\texttt{ass:rr-limit-positive}\) makes the fixed term on the right strictly positive.  Since \(r_B\downarrow0\), (15) shows that clipping is inactive with probability tending to one and
\[
 \|\widehat\Gamma_+^{-1}\|=O_P(1).
\]
This completes both the finite-block identity and the arbitrary-\(G\), many-block concentration claim.
