Give each of the four pairs \((H_g,J_g)\in\{-1,1\}^2\) exactly \(G/4\) groups.  We verify the model-class conditions without first assuming class membership.  Write
\[
 y_{gb}(d,q)=h_g+d(\delta_g+\theta_gq)
\]
for the displayed block-specific response in \(\texttt{def:adaptive-witness}\).  It is constant over units.  A full potential-outcome schedule realizing these sustained endpoints is obtained, for \(t\geq2\), by averaging \(y_{g,\lceil t/2\rceil}\) at the unit's two exposures at times \(t-1\) and \(t\), and by using its current-exposure value at \(t=1\).  This schedule uses no assignment outside group \(g\), uses no future assignment, and depends on the past only through the immediately preceding exposure.  It therefore satisfies partial interference, contemporaneous stratified interference, nonanticipation, and the stated lag-one memory restriction.  When the two block assignments are held equal to \((d,q)\), the average equals \(y_{gb}(d,q)=Y_{gbi}(d,q)\), as required.

For type A blocks the largest absolute endpoint is at most \(5/2\).  For type B blocks, direct inspection over \(d\in\{0,1\}\), \(q\in\{1/4,3/4\}\), and \((a_{Dg},a_{Sg})\in\{-1,1\}^2\) gives the sharp envelope \(9/2\).  Thus one may take \(M=9/2\); the average extension has the same bound.  Taking \(K_H=1\) bounds the centered baseline score.  The outcomes are fixed, and the only randomness is the two-stage assignment, establishing design-based finiteness.

The score algebra is explicit.  The direct coordinate of \(m_{gbq_H}-m_{gbq_L}\) is
\[
 \frac{\theta_g(q_H-q_L)}2=a_{Dg},
\]
and its spillover coordinate is
\[
 2h_g+\delta_g+\frac{\theta_g}{2}=a_{Sg}.
\]
Hence
\[
 a_{gb}=(a_{Dg},a_{Sg})^\top.                               \tag{39}
\]
Since exactly \(np_{dq}\) units occur in each own-treatment cell and the endpoints are constant over units,
\[
 \frac1n\sum_i\frac{\mathbf1\{A_{gbi}=d\}Y_{gbi}(d,q)}{p_{dq}}
 =Y_{gbi}(d,q).
\]
Thus \(e_{gbq}=0\), \(T_{gbq}=0\), and \(K_b=0\).

The realized group mean under saturation \(q\) is
\[
 h_g+q(\delta_g+\theta_gq).
\]
Writing \(q=1/2+x_{gb}/4\) and using \(q^2-q=-3/16\) at both menu values gives
\[
 \bar Y^{\mathrm{obs}}_{gb}
 =\frac{a_{Sg}}2+\frac{a_{Dg}}4+\frac L4x_{gb}.             \tag{40}
\]
The four equally represented types have zero averages of \(H_g\), \(J_g\), and \(H_gJ_g\), and every realized balanced sign vector has zero average.  Thus the grand mean corresponding to (40) is zero.  Apart from the first block, an A block follows a B block and a B block follows an A block.  Since \(\lambda=1\), equation (40) and the definition of \(V_{gb}\) give
\[
 V_{gb}^{A}=\frac32H_g+\frac14J_g+x_{g,b-1},
 \qquad
 V_{gb}^{B}=\frac54H_g+\frac12J_g.                          \tag{41}
\]

This dependence is genuinely adaptive, not merely formal.  The previous assignments \(x_{b-1}=H\) and \(x_{b-1}=-H\) both have positive conditional probability because \(f_{\eta,\kappa}\geq\eta\).  Put \(K_g=H_gJ_g\), which is another balanced sign vector.  In the two histories, the A-block balance vectors are \(5H/2+J/4\) and \(H/2+J/4\), respectively.  For the candidate current assignments \(x=J\) and \(x=K\), the latter always has standardized imbalance zero, whereas the former has squared standardized imbalance \((G-1)/101\) in the first history and \((G-1)/5\) in the second.  Since \(\eta<1\), \(\kappa>0\), and \(f_{\eta,\kappa}\) is strictly decreasing in \(w^2\), the conditional probability ratio \(P_{\mathrm{RR},b}(J)/P_{\mathrm{RR},b}(K)\) differs between the histories.  Thus prior observed outcomes alter the next assignment law.

We next verify the uniform imbalance-variance window before invoking any model-class result.  For an A block, let \(d_g=3H_g/2+J_g/4\).  Equal representation gives \(G^{-1}\sum_gd_g^2=37/16\), while \(G^{-1}\sum_gx_{g,b-1}^2=1\).  The reverse triangle inequality therefore yields, for every history,
\[
 \sigma_{A,b}
 =\frac{\|d+x_{b-1}\|_2}{\sqrt{G-1}}
 \geq\sqrt{\frac G{G-1}}\left(\frac{\sqrt{37}}4-1\right)>0. \tag{42}
\]
The corresponding upper bound replaces the minus sign by a plus sign.  A B block and the first block satisfy, respectively,
\[
 \sigma_{B,b}^2=\frac G{G-1}\frac{29}{16},
 \qquad \sigma_1^2=\frac G{G-1}.                            \tag{43}
\]
Equations (42)--(43) supply constants \(0<c_\sigma<C_\sigma<\infty\) uniformly over histories.

To obtain the claimed limits, define
\[
 z_{H,b}=G^{-1}\sum_gx_{g,b-1}H_g,
 \qquad z_{J,b}=G^{-1}\sum_gx_{g,b-1}J_g.
\]
Complement symmetry makes their conditional means zero.  Under \(P_0\), the balanced-sign covariance identity and the centering and unit norms of \(H\) and \(J\) give
\[
 \mathbb E_0z_{H,b}^2=\mathbb E_0z_{J,b}^2=\frac1{G-1}.
\]
Because the conditional likelihood ratio is \(f_{\eta,\kappa}(W)/C_b\leq1/\eta\),
\[
 \sup_b\mathbb E_{\mathrm{RR}}(z_{H,b}^2+z_{J,b}^2)
 \leq\frac2{\eta(G-1)}.                                    \tag{44}
\]
Expansion of (41) now gives
\[
 \sigma_{A,b}^2
 =\frac G{G-1}\left(\frac{53}{16}+3z_{H,b}+\frac12z_{J,b}\right)
 \longrightarrow_P\frac{53}{16},                           \tag{45}
\]
uniformly in the block index in the sense that the supremum of the marginal error probabilities tends to zero.  Equation (43) gives \(\sigma_{B,b}^2\to29/16\) deterministically.

Using (39), equal representation, and (41) in the definition of \(\beta_b\) yields
\[
 \beta_{A,b}
 =\frac{G}{(G-1)\sigma_{A,b}}
   \begin{pmatrix}3/2+z_{H,b}\\1/4+z_{J,b}\end{pmatrix}
 \longrightarrow_P\frac{(6,1)^\top}{\sqrt{53}},
 \qquad
 \beta_{B,b}\longrightarrow\frac{(2,5)^\top}{\sqrt{29}}. \tag{46}
\]
The lower bound (42) and (44) also give convergence of the alternating block averages of \(\beta_b\beta_b^\top\): the mean norm of the A-block error is \(O(G^{-1/2})\), so Markov's inequality applies to its block average without requiring independence.  Since the two block proportions tend to one half,
\[
 P=\frac12\left\{
 \frac1{53}\begin{pmatrix}36&6\\6&1\end{pmatrix}
 +\frac1{29}\begin{pmatrix}4&10\\10&25\end{pmatrix}
 \right\}.                                                   \tag{47}
\]
Both summands are positive semidefinite.  Their generating vectors \((6,1)^\top\) and \((2,5)^\top\) are linearly independent, and
\[
 \det(P)=\frac{(6\cdot5-1\cdot2)^2}{4\cdot53\cdot29}
 =\frac{196}{1537}>0.
\]
Thus \(P\succ0\).

Under complete randomization, (39), equal representation, and \(K_b=0\) give
\[
 \Sigma_{b,0}=\frac G{G-1}I_2,
 \qquad B^{-1}\sum_b\Sigma_{b,0}\longrightarrow I_2.       \tag{48}
\]
This proves complete-randomization stabilization, while (46)--(47) prove projection stabilization.  Crucially, we now use the pre-membership result \(\texttt{lem:adaptive-witness-pre-membership-transport}\), whose premises were verified by (39) and (42), rather than the class-level transport theorem.  It gives uniformly over the witness histories
\[
 \Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}
 =\Sigma_{b,0}-(1-v_{\eta,\kappa})\beta_b\beta_b^\top+o(1).
\]
Average this equality and apply (47)--(48) to obtain
\[
 B^{-1}\sum_b\Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}
 \longrightarrow_P I_2-(1-v_{\eta,\kappa})P
 =:\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}.                                   \tag{49}
\]
Furthermore,
\[
 P\preceq\frac12\left(\frac{37}{53}+1\right)I_2
 =\frac{45}{53}I_2\prec I_2.
\]
The formula in \(\texttt{def:projection-gain}\), together with \(0<\eta<1\) and \(\kappa>0\), gives \(0<v_{\eta,\kappa}<1\).  Therefore equation (49) implies
\[
 \Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}\succeq
 \left\{1-(1-v_{\eta,\kappa})\frac{45}{53}\right\}I_2\succ0. \tag{50}
\]
Thus RR covariance stabilization and RR positive definiteness have both been derived, not assumed.

Equations (42)--(50), the structural verification above, and any triangular index sequence satisfying the stipulated \(c_\rho\leq G/B\leq C_\rho\) verify every member condition in \(\texttt{def:model-class}\).  Hence \(\mathcal W^{AB}\subset\mathcal M_{G,B}\).  Only now do we invoke \(\texttt{thm:projection-covariance}\).  By (47), \(c^\top Pc>0\) for every \(c\neq0\), so its strict-improvement characterization shows that every nonzero direct--spillover contrast has smaller asymptotic variance under the adaptive soft design.  This proves all parts of the proposition without circular use of model-class membership.
