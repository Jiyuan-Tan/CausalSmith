Let
\[
 X_B=\sqrt{GB}(\widehat\tau-\tau),
 \qquad
 \bar D_B=B^{-1}\sum_{b=1}^BD_b.
\]
Fix an arbitrary sequence allowed by the proposed statement and then an arbitrary subsequence.  The bound (11) proved in \(\texttt{thm:studentizer}\) makes the deterministic sequence \(\{\bar D_B\}\) bounded in the finite-dimensional space of symmetric \(2\)-by-\(2\) matrices.  It has a further subsequence such that
\[
 \bar D_B\longrightarrow D_\infty
 \quad\text{for some }D_\infty\succeq0.                  \tag{16}
\]
On this further subsequence, \(\texttt{thm:joint-design-clt}\), \(\texttt{thm:studentizer}\), and (16) give
\[
 X_B\Rightarrow X,
 \qquad
 \widehat\Gamma_+\longrightarrow_P\Gamma_\infty,
 \qquad
 X\sim N_2(0,\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}),
 \quad
 \Gamma_\infty:=\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}+D_\infty.           \tag{17}
\]
For completeness, the joint convergence implicit in (17) follows without an independence condition: tightness of \(X_B\) permits restriction to a compact set with arbitrarily high probability, and uniform continuity of any bounded continuous test function on that compact set, together with \(\widehat\Gamma_+\to_P\Gamma_\infty\), replaces its second argument by \(\Gamma_\infty\).  The first convergence in (17) then supplies the limit.

Assumption \(\texttt{ass:rr-limit-positive}\) and \(D_\infty\succeq0\) imply that both \(\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}\) and \(\Gamma_\infty\) are positive definite.  Hence matrix inversion and the quadratic-form map are continuous at every point in the support of the limit in (17), and the same compact-set argument yields
\[
 X_B^\top\widehat\Gamma_+^{-1}X_B
 \Rightarrow X^\top\Gamma_\infty^{-1}X.                  \tag{18}
\]
Write \(X\stackrel d=\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}^{1/2}Z\), where \(Z\sim N_2(0,I_2)\).  Since \(\Gamma_\infty\succeq\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}\), inversion reverses Loewner order, and therefore
\[
 0\prec
 H:=\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}^{1/2}
       \Gamma_\infty^{-1}
       \Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}^{1/2}
 \preceq I_2.                                               \tag{19}
\]
Diagonalize \(H=O\operatorname{diag}(h_1,h_2)O^\top\), where \(0<h_j\le1\).  Rotational invariance of the standard normal and a coupling with the same two normal coordinates give
\[
 X^\top\Gamma_\infty^{-1}X
 \stackrel d=h_1Z_1^2+h_2Z_2^2
 \le Z_1^2+Z_2^2
 \quad\text{almost surely}.                                \tag{20}
\]
The variable on the right has the \(\chi^2_2\) distribution.  The variable on the left of (20) is a sum of two positive multiples of independent squared standard normals and thus has no atom at the positive quantile \(\chi^2_{2,1-\alpha}\).  From (18)--(20),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \liminf_B\Pr_{\mathrm{RR}}\!\left\{
  X_B^\top\widehat\Gamma_+^{-1}X_B
  \le\chi^2_{2,1-\alpha}\right\}
 &\ge
 \Pr\{Z_1^2+Z_2^2\le\chi^2_{2,1-\alpha}\}\\
 &=1-\alpha.                                                \tag{21}
 \end{aligned}
\]
By \(\texttt{def:simultaneous-wald-region}\), the event in (21) is exactly \(\{\tau\in\mathcal C_\alpha\}\).  If the claimed liminf failed on the original sequence, a violating subsequence would have the further subsequence just analyzed, contradicting (21).  Thus the result holds along every \(B\to\infty\) sequence, without a condition on \(G/B\).
