Fix a block \(b\) and condition throughout on \(\mathcal F_{b-1}\).  By \(\texttt{lem:score-decomposition}\), under the complete-randomization law \(P_0\),
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_0(R_b\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})=\Sigma_{b,0},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Cov}_0(R_b,W_b\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})=\beta_b,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}_0(W_b\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})=1.                 \tag{1}
\]
The first equality uses both the balanced-sign covariance and the unchanged within-group component \(K_b\) supplied by that lemma; the second is the definition of \(\beta_b\); and the third follows from the exact standardization in the definition of \(W_b\).  Complement symmetry of \(P_0\) gives \(\mathbb E_0W_b=0\), and the same symmetry together with the conditional centering of \(U_b\) gives \(\mathbb E_0R_b=0\).

Let \((R_b^\star,W_b^\star)\) be the covariance-matched Gaussian pair.  Equation (1) implies that \(W_b^\star\sim N(0,1)\).  Define
\[
 E_b^\star=R_b^\star-\beta_bW_b^\star.
\]
Then
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(E_b^\star,W_b^\star)=0,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(E_b^\star)=\Sigma_{b,0}-\beta_b\beta_b^\top. \tag{2}
\]
Because the pair is jointly Gaussian, zero covariance implies independence.  This argument also covers singular \(\Sigma_{b,0}\): all variables are defined on the exact covariance range, and (2) is merely an orthogonal decomposition on that range, with no matrix inversion.

For \(Z\sim N(0,1)\), direct Gaussian integration gives
\[
 \mathbb E\exp(-\kappa Z^2)=(1+2\kappa)^{-1/2}.
\]
Differentiation with respect to \(\kappa\) is justified by domination on every compact subinterval of \((0,\infty)\), and yields
\[
 \mathbb E\{Z^2\exp(-\kappa Z^2)\}=(1+2\kappa)^{-3/2}.
\]
Consequently, if
\[
 c_\star=\mathbb Ef_{\eta,\kappa}(Z)
 =\eta+(1-\eta)(1+2\kappa)^{-1/2},
\]
then \(c_\star\geq\eta>0\) and, by \(\texttt{def:projection-gain}\),
\[
 \frac{\mathbb E\{Z^2f_{\eta,\kappa}(Z)\}}{c_\star}
 =v_{\eta,\kappa}.                                             \tag{3}
\]
Using (2), the independence of \(E_b^\star\) and \(W_b^\star\), and their zero means, we therefore obtain
\[
 \frac{\mathbb E\{R_b^\star R_b^{\star\top}
 f_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b^\star)\}}
      {\mathbb Ef_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b^\star)}
 =\Sigma_{b,0}-(1-v_{\eta,\kappa})\beta_b\beta_b^\top.       \tag{4}
\]

We next identify the corresponding ratio under the actual design.  The map \(x\mapsto-x\) preserves \(\mathcal X_G\) and \(P_0\), sends \((A_b,W_b)\) to \((-A_b,-W_b)\), and leaves the even function \(f_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b)\) unchanged.  Hence the soft law has \(\mathbb E_{\mathrm{RR}}(A_b\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})=0\).  By \(\texttt{lem:score-decomposition}\), \(\mathbb E(U_b\mid x_b,\mathcal F_{b-1})=0\), so
\(\mathbb E_{\mathrm{RR}}(R_b\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})=0\).  The definition of \(P_{\mathrm{RR},b}\) now gives
\[
 \Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}
 =\frac{\mathbb E_0\{R_bR_b^\top f_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b)
          \mid\mathcal F_{b-1}\}}
        {\mathbb E_0\{f_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b)\mid\mathcal F_{b-1}\}}. \tag{5}
\]
Both denominators in (4) and (5) are at least \(\eta\).  Moreover,
\[
 \left\|\mathbb E\{R_b^\star R_b^{\star\top}
 f_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b^\star)\}\right\|
 \leq \mathbb E\|R_b^\star\|^2
 =\mathbb E_0\|R_b\|^2
 \leq \{\mathbb E_0\|R_b\|^4\}^{1/2},                       \tag{6}
\]
and the last quantity is uniformly bounded by \(\texttt{lem:block-moments}\).  If \(N,D\) and \(N^\star,D^\star\) denote the numerators and denominators in (5) and (4), respectively, then
\[
 \left\|\frac ND-\frac{N^\star}{D^\star}\right\|
 \leq \frac{\|N-N^\star\|}{\eta}
      +\frac{\|N^\star\|\,|D-D^\star|}{\eta^2}.             \tag{7}
\]
The two comparisons in \(\texttt{thm:weighted-gaussian-transport}\), together with (6)--(7), show that the right-hand side of (7) is \(o(1)\), uniformly over blocks, realized histories, and members of \(\mathcal M_{G,B}\).  Combining (4) and (5),
\[
 \Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}
 =\Sigma_{b,0}-(1-v_{\eta,\kappa})\beta_b\beta_b^\top+o(1), \tag{8}
\]
with a uniform remainder.  This proves the blockwise assertion.

Averaging (8) over \(b\) preserves the \(o(1)\) remainder.  The covariance-stabilization and projection-stabilization conditions in \(\texttt{def:model-class}\), including \(\texttt{ass:cr-covariance-stabilization}\) and \(\texttt{ass:projection-stabilization}\), therefore give
\[
 \Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}
 =\Omega_{\mathrm{CR}}-(1-v_{\eta,\kappa})P.                 \tag{9}
\]
Here \(P\succeq0\): for every fixed \(c\in\mathbb R^2\), each random quantity
\[
 \frac1B\sum_{b\in\mathcal B_B}(c^\top\beta_b)^2
\]
is nonnegative and converges in probability to the deterministic number \(c^\top Pc\), which consequently is nonnegative.

Finally, since \(\kappa>0\),
\((1+2\kappa)^{-3/2}<(1+2\kappa)^{-1/2}\).  Because \(0<\eta<1\), the formula in (3) implies \(0<v_{\eta,\kappa}<1\).  Thus for every fixed \(c\neq0\), equation (9) gives
\[
 c^\top\Omega_{\mathrm{CR}}c-c^\top\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}c
 =(1-v_{\eta,\kappa})c^\top Pc.
\]
The first factor on the right is strictly positive and the second is nonnegative.  Hence the variance difference is strictly positive if and only if \(c^\top Pc>0\), which is the asserted characterization of every strictly improved contrast.
