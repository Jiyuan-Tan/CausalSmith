Fix a member of \(\mathcal M_{G,B}\), a block \(b\), and condition throughout on \(\mathcal F_{b-1}\).  By design-based finiteness and the lag-one structural conditions contained in the class, each \(u_{k,b}\) is fixed under this conditioning.  Abbreviate the logged probabilities by \(\pi_k:=\pi_{k,b}\) and \(\pi_{k\ell}:=\pi_{k\ell,b}\).  The definitions of the causal block target and the Horvitz--Thompson block estimator give
\[
 R_b=\frac1{\sqrt G\,n}\sum_{k\in\mathcal K_b}
       \left(\frac{I_k}{\pi_k}-1\right)u_{k,b}.             \tag{7}
\]
All divisions in (7) are valid.  Indeed, complementation maps \(\mathcal X_G\) to itself, preserves \(P_0\), sends \(W_b(x)\) to \(-W_b(x)\), and leaves the even function \(f_{\eta,\kappa}\{W_b(x)\}\) unchanged.  Pairing every balanced assignment with its complement therefore gives
\[
 \Pr_{\mathrm{RR}}(Q_{gb}=q_H\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})
 =\Pr_{\mathrm{RR}}(Q_{gb}=q_L\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})=\frac12.
\]
Since \(p_{dq}\geq1/4\), the fixed-count second stage gives \(\pi_k=p_{dq}/2\geq1/8\).  Taking the conditional covariance of (7) and using \(\mathbb E(I_kI_\ell\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})=\pi_{k\ell}\) yields
\[
 \Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}
 =\frac1{Gn^2}\sum_{k,\ell\in\mathcal K_b}
   \frac{\pi_{k\ell}-\pi_k\pi_\ell}{\pi_k\pi_\ell}
   u_{k,b}u_{\ell,b}^\top.                                 \tag{8}
\]

If \(\pi_{k\ell}>0\), conditional expectation of the corresponding summand in \(\widehat\Gamma_b\) replaces \(I_kI_\ell\) by \(\pi_{k\ell}\), exactly recovering its ordered-pair term in (8).  If \(k\ne\ell\) and \(\pi_{k\ell}=0\), the two ordered terms indexed by \((k,\ell)\) and \((\ell,k)\) in (8) are
\[
 -u_{k,b}u_{\ell,b}^\top-u_{\ell,b}u_{k,b}^\top.
\]
For the corresponding unordered pair, the completion in \(\widehat\Gamma_b\) has conditional expectation \(u_{k,b}u_{k,b}^\top+u_{\ell,b}u_{\ell,b}^\top\).  Its excess over the two missing covariance terms is
\[
 (u_{k,b}+u_{\ell,b})(u_{k,b}+u_{\ell,b})^\top\succeq0.   \tag{9}
\]
Summing (9) over \(\mathcal Z_b\), with the normalization in \(\texttt{def:completed-studentizer}\), proves
\[
 \mathbb E(\widehat\Gamma_b\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})
 =\Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}+D_b,
 \qquad D_b\succeq0.                                      \tag{10}
\]
Every term of the completion is observable because an occurrence of \(u_{k,b}\) is multiplied by \(I_k\), and every probability is logged; no unobserved cross-potential-outcome product is used.

We next establish uniform boundedness.  The outcome envelope and fixed contrast coefficients give \(\sup_{k,b}\|u_{k,b}\|<\infty\).  By \(\texttt{lem:pair-probability-control}\), for all sufficiently large \(G\), feasible cross-group pair probabilities are bounded away from zero and their covariance coefficients are \(O(G^{-1})\), while feasible within-group pair probabilities are bounded away from zero.  The first claim also follows directly from the soft likelihood-ratio lower bound \(f_{\eta,\kappa}/C_b\geq\eta\) and the complete-randomization pair probabilities.  For the fixed-count second stage, every feasible same-group pair probability is uniformly positive over \(n\in4\mathbb N\): the relevant sampling factors are first-order probabilities or one of
\[
 \frac{nq(nq-1)}{n(n-1)},\qquad
 \frac{n(1-q)\{n(1-q)-1\}}{n(n-1)},\qquad
 \frac{nq\,n(1-q)}{n(n-1)},
\]
and a factor that vanishes corresponds precisely to an impossible pair handled by the completion.  There are \(O(G^2n^2)\) ordered cross-group pairs, each carrying, after the outer normalization, a coefficient of order \(O(G^{-2}n^{-2})\), and \(O(Gn^2)\) within-group pairs, each carrying a coefficient of order \(O(G^{-1}n^{-2})\).  For \(G\geq4\), impossible pairs are within-group and have the same bounded count-normalization product.  The remaining admissible value \(G=2\) has only \(O(n^2)\) additional impossible cross-group pairs, still offset by the factor \((Gn^2)^{-1}\).  Consequently a constant \(C_\Gamma<\infty\), independent of \(B\), \(G\), \(n\), the block, and the history, satisfies
\[
 \|\widehat\Gamma_b\|_F+\|D_b\|_F\leq C_\Gamma.           \tag{11}
\]
Because the soft weight is strictly positive on all of \(\mathcal X_G\), the zero-pair set is determined by the support of balanced first-stage assignment and fixed-count second-stage assignment, not by the numerical history-dependent tilt.  Together with the fixed potential outcomes, this makes \(D_b\) deterministic for each \(b\).

Put
\[
 M_b=\widehat\Gamma_b-
     \mathbb E(\widehat\Gamma_b\mid\mathcal F_{b-1}).
\]
Then \(\{M_b,\mathcal F_b\}\) is an entrywise matrix martingale-difference sequence.  Conditional orthogonality across distinct blocks and (11) give
\[
 \mathbb E\left\|B^{-1}\sum_{b=1}^BM_b\right\|_F^2
 =B^{-2}\sum_{b=1}^B\mathbb E\|M_b\|_F^2
 \leq\frac{4C_\Gamma^2}{B}\longrightarrow0.                \tag{12}
\]
Thus \(B^{-1}\sum_bM_b=o_P(1)\).  Averaging (10), applying (12), and using the separately imposed rerandomization covariance stabilization proves
\[
 \widehat\Gamma
 =\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}+B^{-1}\sum_{b=1}^BD_b+o_P(1).       \tag{13}
\]
Only \(B\to\infty\) has been used.

The deterministic positive-semidefinite averages \(\bar D_B=B^{-1}\sum_bD_b\) are bounded by (11).  Every subsequence therefore has a further subsequence on which \(\bar D_B\to D_\infty\succeq0\).  Along that further subsequence, (13) gives
\[
 \widehat\Gamma\longrightarrow_P
 \Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}+D_\infty\succeq\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}. \tag{14}
\]
This proves the asserted Loewner domination of every probability limit point.  If the additional condition \(\lambda_{\min}(\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}})>0\) holds, Weyl's eigenvalue inequality, (13), and \(\bar D_B\succeq0\) imply
\[
 \lambda_{\min}(\widehat\Gamma)
 \geq\lambda_{\min}(\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}})+o_P(1).          \tag{15}
\]
Since \(r_B\downarrow0\), (15) shows that clipping is inactive with probability tending to one and \(\|\widehat\Gamma_+^{-1}\|=O_P(1)\).  This proves the finite-block identity, the expansion under covariance stabilization, and the additional clipping conclusion under positive definiteness, with their scopes separated as stated.
