We prove a uniform comparison, conditionally on an arbitrary \(\mathcal F_{b-1}\); all bounds below are deterministic and independent of that history.  Bounded endpoints give \(\|a_{gb}\|\leq L_a\).  The definition of \(V_{gb}\) gives \(|V_{gb}|\leq K_H+2\lambda M=:L_V\), while \(\texttt{ass:imbalance-variance-window}\) gives \(\sigma_b\geq\sqrt{c_\sigma}\).  Thus the centered three-vectors
\[
 y_g=\left((a_{gb}-\bar a_b)^\top,V_{gb}/\sigma_b\right)^\top\in\mathbb R^3,
 \qquad G^{-1}\sum_gy_g=0,
\]
are uniformly bounded.  Balanced signs annihilate constants, so
\[
 (A_b,W_b)=G^{-1/2}\sum_gx_{gb}y_g.
\]

Use the perfect-matching representation from \(\texttt{def:perfect-matching-handle}\).  Conditional on a uniform perfect matching \(\mathfrak m=\{\{r_j,s_j\}:1\leq j\leq G/2\}\),
\[
 S_G:=G^{-1/2}\sum_gx_{gb}y_g
 =G^{-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^{G/2}\xi_jd_j,
 \qquad d_j=y_{r_j}-y_{s_j},
\]
where the \(\xi_j\)'s are independent Rademacher variables.  Its conditional covariance is
\(C_{\mathfrak m}=G^{-1}\sum_jd_jd_j^\top\).  Averaging over matchings gives
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathfrak m}C_{\mathfrak m}
 =\operatorname{Var}_0(S_G)=:C_G.
\]
Indeed, a fixed unordered pair is matched with probability \(1/(G-1)\), and expanding \(\sum_{r<s}(y_r-y_s)(y_r-y_s)^\top\), using \(\sum_gy_g=0\), gives the equality.  A switching of two edges of a matching changes at most four bounded rank-one summands; hence it changes every entry of \(C_{\mathfrak m}\) by at most \(C/G\).  Revealing the matching successively and applying the elementary martingale variance identity to these bounded differences gives
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathfrak m}\|C_{\mathfrak m}-C_G\|^2\leq C/G. \tag{1}
\]
This switching proof uses boundedness only and therefore is uniform over singular as well as nonsingular \(C_G\).

Let \(h:\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb R\) have bounded derivatives through order three.  Replace the independent summands \(\xi_jd_j/\sqrt G\), one at a time, by independent Gaussian summands \(N(0,d_jd_j^\top/G)\).  Third-order Taylor expansion around the sum of the unreplaced terms cancels the first two orders because each pair of summands has the same mean and covariance.  The remainder is bounded by a constant times the third derivative norm times the third absolute moment.  Since \(\|d_j\|\leq C\),
\[
 \left|\mathbb E\{h(S_G)\mid\mathfrak m\}
       -\mathbb Eh(N(0,C_{\mathfrak m}))\right|
 \leq C_h\sum_{j=1}^{G/2}\frac{\|d_j\|^3}{G^{3/2}}
 \leq C_hG^{-1/2}. \tag{2}
\]
No inverse covariance occurs in this Lindeberg replacement.  For positive-semidefinite matrices \(C_1,C_2\), differentiating the Gaussian interpolation \(N(0,(1-t)C_1+tC_2)\) after adjoining independent Gaussian increments gives
\[
 |\mathbb Eh(N(0,C_1))-\mathbb Eh(N(0,C_2))|
 \leq C\|D^2h\|_\infty\|C_1-C_2\|. \tag{3}
\]
This identity also holds on a proper covariance range, by adding \(\delta I_3\) and sending \(\delta\downarrow0\).  Combining (1)--(3) proves, uniformly over the core class,
\[
 \sup_{\|h\|_{C^3}\leq L}
 \left|\mathbb E_0h(A_b,W_b)-\mathbb Eh(A_b^\star,W_b^\star)\right|
 \longrightarrow0                                                   \tag{4}
\]
for every fixed \(L<\infty\).

Taking \(h(a,w)=f_{\eta,\kappa}(w)\), whose derivatives are bounded, proves the first scalar comparison.  For the quadratic comparison, choose a smooth cutoff \(\chi_L(a)\), equal to one on \(\|a\|\leq L\) and zero on \(\|a\|\geq2L\), and apply (4) to
\(h_{ij,L}(a,w)=a_ia_jf_{\eta,\kappa}(w)\chi_L(a)\).  The matching fourth-moment calculation in \(\texttt{lem:block-moments}\) gives a uniform bound on \(\mathbb E_0\|A_b\|^4\).  A centered Gaussian with the same bounded covariance also has a uniformly bounded fourth moment.  Therefore, on both sides,
\[
 \mathbb E\{\|A_b\|^2\mathbf1(\|A_b\|>L)\}
 \leq L^{-2}\mathbb E\|A_b\|^4\leq C/L^2.             \tag{5}
\]
First let \(G\to\infty\) in the cutoff comparison and then \(L\to\infty\).  Equations (4)--(5) prove the asserted \(A_bA_b^\top\) comparison without a score-variance lower bound.

It remains to add the fixed-count noise.  Conditional on \(x_b\),
\[
 U_b=\frac2{\sqrt G}\sum_ge_{gb,Q_{gb}}
\]
is a sum of independent centered uniformly bounded vectors.  The same smooth Lindeberg replacement as in (2), applied conditionally on \(x_b\), replaces it with a centered Gaussian having covariance
\[
 K_b(x_b)=\frac4G\sum_gT_{gb,Q_{gb}}
 =K_b+\frac2G\sum_gx_{gb}(T_{gbq_H}-T_{gbq_L}),                 \tag{6}
\]
with uniform error \(O(G^{-1/2})\).  Every entry of the last balanced-sign average in (6) has second moment \(O(G^{-1})\), by the exact covariance formula for sampling half the signs and boundedness of \(T_{gbq}\).  Hence
\[
 \mathbb E_0\|K_b(x_b)-K_b\|^2=O(G^{-1}).                     \tag{7}
\]
Gaussian covariance interpolation (3), conditional on \((A_b,W_b)\), replaces \(K_b(x_b)\) by \(K_b\) at an \(o(1)\) cost.  Applying (4) after convolving the test function in its score coordinates with \(N_2(0,K_b)\) shows that \((R_b,W_b)\) is, for bounded smooth tests, uniformly approximated by
\[
 (A_b^\star+E_b^\star,W_b^\star),
 \qquad E_b^\star\sim N_2(0,K_b),
 \quad E_b^\star\perp(A_b^\star,W_b^\star).
\]
By \(\texttt{lem:score-decomposition}\), this Gaussian vector has exactly the conditional \(P_0\)-covariance of \((R_b,W_b)\), so it has the law specified for \((R_b^\star,W_b^\star)\).  The uniform fourth-moment bound for \(R_b\), and the corresponding Gaussian fourth-moment bound, permit the same cutoff argument (5).  This proves the weighted \(R_bR_b^\top\) comparison.

Let \(\delta_G\) be the supremum, over the stated class, of the sum of the three discrepancies just proved.  The argument shows \(\delta_G\to0\).  Defining
\(\varepsilon_G=\sup\{\delta_m:m\geq G,\ m\in2\mathbb N\}\) gives a deterministic decreasing sequence with \(\varepsilon_G\downarrow0\) and establishes both displayed inequalities.
