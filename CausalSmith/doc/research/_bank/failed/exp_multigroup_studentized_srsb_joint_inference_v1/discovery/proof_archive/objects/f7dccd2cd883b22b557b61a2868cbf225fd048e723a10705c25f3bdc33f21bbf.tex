Fix a block \(b\) and condition on \(\mathcal F_{b-1}\).  Under \(P_0\), complement symmetry gives \(\mathbb E_0A_b=0\).  By \(\texttt{lem:score-decomposition}\), \(\mathbb E(U_b\mid x_b,\mathcal F_{b-1})=0\), so iterated expectation gives \(\mathbb E_0R_b=0\) and \(\operatorname{Cov}_0(U_b,W_b)=0\).  The balanced-sign covariance formula and the unchanged second-stage covariance in that lemma yield
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_0(R_b\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})=\Sigma_{b,0},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Cov}_0(R_b,W_b\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})=\beta_b,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}_0(W_b\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})=1.          \tag{1}
\]
The middle equality is also the defining formula for \(\beta_b\), and the last equality is the exact standardization in the definition of \(W_b\); its division is legitimate by \(\texttt{ass:imbalance-variance-window}\).

Let \((R_b^\star,W_b^\star)\) be the covariance-matched Gaussian pair.  Equation (1) implies \(W_b^\star\sim N(0,1)\).  Define
\[
 E_b^\star=R_b^\star-\beta_bW_b^\star.
\]
Then direct covariance algebra gives
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(E_b^\star,W_b^\star)=0,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(E_b^\star)=\Sigma_{b,0}-\beta_b\beta_b^\top. \tag{2}
\]
The joint Gaussian characteristic function in (2) factors into its \(E_b^\star\) and \(W_b^\star\) factors, so they are independent.  This reasoning uses \(\operatorname{Var}(W_b^\star)=1\), not an inverse of \(\Sigma_{b,0}\), and remains valid when the score covariance is singular.

For \(Z\sim N(0,1)\), completing the square in the Gaussian density gives
\[
 \mathbb E e^{-\kappa Z^2}=(1+2\kappa)^{-1/2}.
\]
Differentiating this integral with respect to \(\kappa\) is justified by the integrable Gaussian envelope \(z^2e^{-z^2/2}\), and gives
\[
 \mathbb E\{Z^2e^{-\kappa Z^2}\}=(1+2\kappa)^{-3/2}.       \tag{3}
\]
Consequently, with
\[
 c_\star:=\mathbb Ef_{\eta,\kappa}(Z)
 =\eta+(1-\eta)(1+2\kappa)^{-1/2}\geq\eta>0,
\]
the definition \(\texttt{def:projection-gain}\) gives
\[
 \frac{\mathbb E\{Z^2f_{\eta,\kappa}(Z)\}}{c_\star}
 =v_{\eta,\kappa}.                                        \tag{4}
\]
Using \(R_b^\star=\beta_bW_b^\star+E_b^\star\), the independence and centering in (2), and (4), we obtain
\[
 \frac{\mathbb E\{R_b^\star R_b^{\star\top}
                     f_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b^\star)\}}
      {\mathbb Ef_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b^\star)}
 =\Sigma_{b,0}-(1-v_{\eta,\kappa})\beta_b\beta_b^\top.   \tag{5}
\]

We now identify the actual soft-design covariance.  The map \(x\mapsto-x\) preserves \(\mathcal X_G\) and \(P_0\), changes \((A_b,W_b)\) to \((-A_b,-W_b)\), and leaves the even weight \(f_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b)\) unchanged.  Hence \(\mathbb E_{\mathrm{RR}}(A_b\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})=0\).  Conditional centering of \(U_b\) from \(\texttt{lem:score-decomposition}\) then implies \(\mathbb E_{\mathrm{RR}}(R_b\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})=0\).  By the Radon--Nikodym ratio in \(\texttt{def:soft-two-stage-design}\),
\[
 \Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}
 =\frac{\mathbb E_0\{R_bR_b^\top f_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b)
                         \mid\mathcal F_{b-1}\}}
        {\mathbb E_0\{f_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b)
                         \mid\mathcal F_{b-1}\}}.          \tag{6}
\]
Both the denominator in (6) and its Gaussian counterpart in (5) are at least \(\eta\).  Moreover, covariance matching and \(\texttt{lem:block-moments}\) give
\[
 \left\|\mathbb E\{R_b^\star R_b^{\star\top}
                         f_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b^\star)\}\right\|
 \leq\mathbb E\|R_b^\star\|^2
 =\mathbb E_0\|R_b\|^2
 \leq\{\mathbb E_0\|R_b\|^4\}^{1/2}\leq C_R^{1/2}.     \tag{7}
\]
If \(N,D\) are the numerator and denominator in (6), and \(N^\star,D^\star\) those in (5), then the positivity just noted gives the deterministic ratio bound
\[
 \left\|\frac ND-\frac{N^\star}{D^\star}\right\|
 \leq\frac{\|N-N^\star\|}{\eta}
     +\frac{\|N^\star\|\,|D-D^\star|}{\eta^2}.            \tag{8}
\]
By \(\texttt{thm:weighted-gaussian-transport}\), both discrepancies on the right of (8) are bounded by a deterministic quantity tending to zero as \(G\to\infty\), uniformly over blocks and histories.  Equations (5)--(8) therefore prove
\[
 \Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}
 =\Sigma_{b,0}-(1-v_{\eta,\kappa})\beta_b\beta_b^\top+r_{Gb},
 \qquad
 \sup_{b,\mathcal F_{b-1}}\|r_{Gb}\|\longrightarrow0.     \tag{9}
\]
This is the claimed blockwise conclusion.  Its proof uses \(G\to\infty\) but nowhere uses \(B\) or \(G/B\).

Now take any sequence with \(G\to\infty\) along which \(\texttt{ass:cr-covariance-stabilization}\), \(\texttt{ass:rr-covariance-stabilization}\), and \(\texttt{ass:projection-stabilization}\) hold.  Averaging (9) gives
\[
 \left\|\frac1B\sum_b\Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}
 -\frac1B\sum_b\Sigma_{b,0}
 +(1-v_{\eta,\kappa})\frac1B\sum_b\beta_b\beta_b^\top\right\|
 \leq\sup_{b,\mathcal F_{b-1}}\|r_{Gb}\|\longrightarrow0. \tag{10}
\]
The three stated stabilizations and uniqueness of limits in probability applied to (10) imply
\[
 \Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}
 =\Omega_{\mathrm{CR}}-(1-v_{\eta,\kappa})P.               \tag{11}
\]
No relative-growth condition is needed.

Finally, \(P\succeq0\): for each fixed \(c\), every \(B^{-1}\sum_b(c^\top\beta_b)^2\) is nonnegative, and its convergence in probability to the deterministic \(c^\top Pc\) precludes a negative limit.  Since \(\kappa>0\),
\[
 (1+2\kappa)^{-3/2}<(1+2\kappa)^{-1/2}.
\]
Together with \(0<\eta<1\), the displayed formula in \(\texttt{def:projection-gain}\) therefore gives \(0<v_{\eta,\kappa}<1\).  For every fixed \(c\ne0\), (11) now yields
\[
 c^\top\Omega_{\mathrm{CR}}c-c^\top\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}c
 =(1-v_{\eta,\kappa})c^\top Pc.                            \tag{12}
\]
The first factor on the right is strictly positive and the second is nonnegative.  Thus (12) is strictly positive if and only if \(c^\top Pc>0\), proving the exact characterization of all strictly improved contrasts.
