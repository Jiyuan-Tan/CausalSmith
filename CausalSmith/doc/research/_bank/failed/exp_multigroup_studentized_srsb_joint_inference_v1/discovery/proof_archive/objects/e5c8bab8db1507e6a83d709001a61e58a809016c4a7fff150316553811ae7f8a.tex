Fix a block and condition on \(\mathcal F_{b-1}\).  By \(\texttt{lem:score-decomposition}\), under \(P_0\),
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_0(R_b\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})=\Sigma_{b,0},
 \qquad \operatorname{Cov}_0(R_b,W_b\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})=\beta_b,
 \qquad \operatorname{Var}_0(W_b\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})=1.    \tag{32}
\]
The covariance-matched Gaussian pair consequently has the orthogonal decomposition
\[
 R_b^\star=\beta_bW_b^\star+E_b^\star,
 \qquad E_b^\star\perp W_b^\star,
 \qquad \operatorname{Var}(E_b^\star)=\Sigma_{b,0}-\beta_b\beta_b^\top. \tag{33}
\]
This remains valid when the score covariance is singular: define \(E_b^\star=R_b^\star-\beta_bW_b^\star\); (32) makes it uncorrelated with \(W_b^\star\), and joint Gaussianity makes it independent on the exact covariance range.

For \(Z\sim N(0,1)\), elementary Gaussian integration gives
\[
 \mathbb Ee^{-\kappa Z^2}=(1+2\kappa)^{-1/2}.
\]
Differentiating both sides with respect to \(\kappa\), justified by domination by \(Z^2\), yields
\[
 \mathbb E\{Z^2e^{-\kappa Z^2}\}=(1+2\kappa)^{-3/2}.
\]
Thus, with
\[
 c_\star=\mathbb Ef_{\eta,\kappa}(Z)
 =\eta+(1-\eta)(1+2\kappa)^{-1/2}\geq\eta,
\]
the definition in \(\texttt{def:projection-gain}\) gives
\[
 \frac{\mathbb E\{Z^2f_{\eta,\kappa}(Z)\}}{c_\star}
 =v_{\eta,\kappa}.                                          \tag{34}
\]
Using (33), independence, and \(\mathbb E(E_b^\star)=0\), equations (33)--(34) imply
\[
 \frac{\mathbb E\{R_b^\star R_b^{\star\top}f_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b^\star)\}}
      {\mathbb Ef_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b^\star)}
 =\Sigma_{b,0}-(1-v_{\eta,\kappa})\beta_b\beta_b^\top.     \tag{35}
\]

Under the actual soft law, \(\mathbb E(U_b\mid x_b,\mathcal F_{b-1})=0\).  Complementation \(x_b\mapsto-x_b\) preserves \(P_0\), leaves the even tilt unchanged, and reverses \(A_b\), so the conditional mean of \(R_b=A_b+U_b\) is zero.  It follows from the definition of \(P_{\mathrm{RR},b}\) that
\[
 \Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}
 =\frac{\mathbb E_0\{R_bR_b^\top f_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b)\mid\mathcal F_{b-1}\}}
        {\mathbb E_0\{f_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b)\mid\mathcal F_{b-1}\}}.   \tag{36}
\]
The two denominators in (35)--(36) are at least \(\eta>0\).  The numerator and denominator comparisons in \(\texttt{thm:weighted-gaussian-transport}\) are uniform over blocks and histories.  Moreover \(\mathbb E\|R_b^\star\|^2=\mathbb E_0\|R_b\|^2\) is uniformly bounded by \(\texttt{lem:block-moments}\) and Cauchy--Schwarz.  The elementary ratio inequality applied to (35)--(36) therefore gives
\[
 \left\|\Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}-\Sigma_{b,0}
 +(1-v_{\eta,\kappa})\beta_b\beta_b^\top\right\|=o(1)      \tag{37}
\]
uniformly over blocks and realized histories.

Average (37) over \(b\).  The uniform remainder remains \(o(1)\).  Assumptions \(\texttt{ass:cr-covariance-stabilization}\), \(\texttt{ass:projection-stabilization}\), and \(\texttt{ass:rr-covariance-stabilization}\) identify the three deterministic limits, hence
\[
 \Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}
 =\Omega_{\mathrm{CR}}-(1-v_{\eta,\kappa})P.                \tag{38}
\]
The matrix \(P\) is positive semidefinite.  Indeed, for every fixed \(c\), each average \(B^{-1}\sum_b(c^\top\beta_b)^2\) is nonnegative and converges in probability to the deterministic number \(c^\top Pc\), which therefore cannot be negative.

Finally, \(\kappa>0\) implies
\((1+2\kappa)^{-3/2}<(1+2\kappa)^{-1/2}\).  Since \(0<\eta<1\), (34) gives \(0<v_{\eta,\kappa}<1\).  For any fixed \(c\neq0\), (38) yields
\[
 c^\top\Omega_{\mathrm{CR}}c-c^\top\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}c
 =(1-v_{\eta,\kappa})c^\top Pc.
\]
The factor \(1-v_{\eta,\kappa}\) is strictly positive and \(c^\top Pc\geq0\); therefore the difference is strictly positive if and only if \(c^\top Pc>0\), proving the claimed characterization.
