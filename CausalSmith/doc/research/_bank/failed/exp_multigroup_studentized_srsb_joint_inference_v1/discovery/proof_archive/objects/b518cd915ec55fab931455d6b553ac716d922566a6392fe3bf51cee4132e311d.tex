Fix any sequence in the proposed statement and any \(t\in\mathbb R^2\).  No step below requires \(G\to\infty\), so \(G\) may be fixed, bounded and varying, or divergent.  If \(t=0\), the asserted scalar projection is identically zero.  Suppose henceforth that \(t\ne0\), and define
\[
 \xi_{Bb}=B^{-1/2}t^\top R_b,
 \qquad
 \mathcal V_B=\sum_{b=1}^B
   \mathbb E(\xi_{Bb}^2\mid\mathcal F_{b-1}),
 \qquad
 V_B=\mathbb E\mathcal V_B.
\]
By \(\texttt{lem:martingale-centering}\), \(\{\xi_{Bb},\mathcal F_b:1\le b\le B\}\) is a martingale-difference array.  By the definition of \(\Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}\),
\[
 \mathcal V_B
 =t^\top\left(B^{-1}\sum_{b=1}^B\Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}\right)t.
\]
Therefore \(\texttt{ass:rr-covariance-stabilization}\) gives
\[
 \mathcal V_B\longrightarrow_P v_t,
 \qquad
 v_t:=t^\top\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}t.                         \tag{1}
\]
Assumption \(\texttt{ass:rr-limit-positive}\) implies \(v_t>0\).

The fourth-moment bound in \(\texttt{lem:block-moments}\), conditional on each realized history, gives a constant \(C_t<\infty\), independent of \(B\), \(G\), the block, and the history, for which
\[
 \sum_{b=1}^B\mathbb E(\xi_{Bb}^4\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})
 =B^{-2}\sum_{b=1}^B
   \mathbb E\{(t^\top R_b)^4\mid\mathcal F_{b-1}\}
 \le \frac{C_t}{B}.                                       \tag{2}
\]
The conditional Cauchy--Schwarz inequality and the same bound also imply
\[
 t^\top\Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}t
 =\mathbb E\{(t^\top R_b)^2\mid\mathcal F_{b-1}\}
 \le C_t^{1/2}.                                            \tag{3}
\]
Thus \(0\le\mathcal V_B\le C_t^{1/2}\) almost surely.  The boundedness in (3) makes \(\{\mathcal V_B\}\) uniformly integrable, so (1) yields
\[
 V_B\longrightarrow v_t,
 \qquad
 \frac{\mathcal V_B}{V_B}\longrightarrow_P1.             \tag{4}
\]
In particular, \(V_B>0\) for all sufficiently large \(B\).

We next derive, rather than assume, the Lindeberg condition in \(\texttt{lem:li-zhao-martingale-clt}\).  For every \(\epsilon>0\), the elementary inequality \(z^2\mathbf1\{|z|\ge a\}\le z^4/a^2\), used with \(a=\epsilon V_B^{-1/2}\), gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &V_B^{-1}\sum_{b=1}^B
  \mathbb E\left[\xi_{Bb}^2
   \mathbf1\{|\xi_{Bb}|\ge\epsilon V_B^{-1/2}\}\right]\\
 &\qquad\le
 \epsilon^{-2}\sum_{b=1}^B\mathbb E(\xi_{Bb}^4)
 \le \frac{C_t}{\epsilon^2B}\longrightarrow0,             \tag{5}
 \end{aligned}
\]
where the last inequality follows by taking expectations in (2).  Equations (4)--(5) verify every normalization and Lindeberg premise of \(\texttt{lem:li-zhao-martingale-clt}\).  Consequently
\[
 V_B^{-1/2}\sum_{b=1}^B\xi_{Bb}\Rightarrow N(0,1).
\]
Combining this conclusion with \(V_B\to v_t\) in (4) gives
\[
 t^\top B^{-1/2}\sum_{b=1}^BR_b
 \Rightarrow N(0,t^\top\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}t).             \tag{6}
\]
Since (6) holds for every \(t\in\mathbb R^2\), all one-dimensional characteristic functions of the vector on the left converge to
\(\exp\{-t^\top\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}t/2\}\), the characteristic function of \(N_2(0,\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}})\).  This proves the asserted bivariate convergence.  Finally,
\[
 \sqrt{GB}(\widehat\tau-\tau)
 =B^{-1/2}\sum_{b=1}^BR_b
\]
is the exact identity in \(\texttt{lem:martingale-centering}\).  The constants in (2)--(3) are uniform in \(G\), and the only diverging denominator used in (2) and (5) is \(B\); hence no relative-growth condition has entered the proof.
