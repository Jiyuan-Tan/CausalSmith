Condition throughout on \(\mathcal F_{b-1}\), and abbreviate \(\pi_{k,b}\) by \(\pi_k\).  Consistency and the Horvitz--Thompson representation give
\[
 R_b=\frac1{\sqrt G\,n}\sum_{k\in\mathcal K_b}
       \left(\frac{I_k}{\pi_k}-1\right)u_{k,b}.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}
 =\frac1{Gn^2}\sum_{k,\ell\in\mathcal K_b}
   \frac{\pi_{k\ell,b}-\pi_k\pi_\ell}{\pi_k\pi_\ell}
   u_{k,b}u_{\ell,b}^\top.                                  \tag{12}
\]
For \(\pi_{k\ell,b}>0\), taking the conditional expectation of the corresponding summand of \(\widehat\Gamma_b\) replaces \(I_kI_\ell\) by \(\pi_{k\ell,b}\), and hence recovers exactly its term in (12).  If \(\pi_{k\ell,b}=0\), the two ordered terms in (12) equal
\(-u_ku_\ell^\top-u_\ell u_k^\top\).  The observable completion for the unordered pair \(\{k,\ell\}\) has conditional expectation
\(u_ku_k^\top+u_\ell u_\ell^\top\).  Its excess over the two missing covariance terms is
\[
 u_ku_k^\top+u_\ell u_\ell^\top+u_ku_\ell^\top+u_\ell u_k^\top
 =(u_k+u_\ell)(u_k+u_\ell)^\top.
\]
Summing proves the exact identity
\[
 \mathbb E(\widehat\Gamma_b\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})
 =\Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}+D_b,
 \qquad D_b\succeq0.                                      \tag{13}
\]
The zero-pair set is deterministic given the finite design support: because \(f_{\eta,\kappa}\geq\eta>0\), the soft law has exactly the same support as \(P_0\), and all second-stage feasible assignments have positive probability.  Thus \(D_b\) is a fixed finite-population matrix, not a random function of the realized assignment.

We next prove concentration.  By \(\texttt{lem:pair-probability-control}\), feasible cross-group pair probabilities are bounded away from zero and
\(|\pi_{k\ell,b}-\pi_k\pi_\ell|=O(G^{-1})\), uniformly.  Feasible within-group pair probabilities and all first-order probabilities are bounded away from zero.  There are \(O(G^2n^2)\) cross-group ordered pairs, each coefficient in the normalized observed sum is \(O(G^{-2}n^{-2})\), and \(O(Gn^2)\) within-group pairs, each normalized coefficient is \(O(G^{-1}n^{-2})\).  Since \(\|u_{k,b}\|\) is uniformly bounded, these counts show
\[
 \|\widehat\Gamma_b\|+\|D_b\|\leq C                              \tag{14}
\]
uniformly for all sufficiently large \(G\).  The finitely many smaller \(G\)'s may be absorbed into \(C\).

The matrices
\(M_b=\widehat\Gamma_b-\mathbb E(\widehat\Gamma_b\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})\)
are martingale differences.  Applying the scalar variance identity to each of their four entries and using (14) gives
\[
 \mathbb E\left\|B^{-1}\sum_{b=1}^BM_b\right\|_F^2\leq C/B,
\]
so their average is \(o_P(1)\).  Combining this with (13) and \(\texttt{ass:rr-covariance-stabilization}\) proves
\[
 \widehat\Gamma
 =\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}+B^{-1}\sum_{b=1}^BD_b+o_P(1).           \tag{15}
\]
The deterministic PSD averages in (15) are bounded by (14).  Hence every subsequence has a further subsequence on which they converge to some \(D_\infty\succeq0\), and along it \(\widehat\Gamma\to_P\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}+D_\infty\succeq\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}\).  This proves the stated subsequential Loewner domination.

Finally, Weyl's eigenvalue inequality and (15) imply
\[
 \lambda_{\min}(\widehat\Gamma)
 \geq\lambda_{\min}(\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}})+o_P(1),
\]
because the middle matrix in (15) is PSD.  Assumption \(\texttt{ass:rr-limit-positive}\) and \(r_B\downarrow0\) show that clipping is asymptotically inactive with probability tending to one and that \(\widehat\Gamma_+^{-1}=O_P(1)\).  Thus the clipped studentizer is asymptotically invertible.  Notice that the raw finite-sample matrix is only symmetric in general; positivity needed for inversion is supplied asymptotically and by the explicit clipping operation.
