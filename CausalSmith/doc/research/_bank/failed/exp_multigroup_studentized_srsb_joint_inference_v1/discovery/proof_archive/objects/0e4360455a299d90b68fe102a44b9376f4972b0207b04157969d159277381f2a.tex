Write \(X_{G,B}=\sqrt{GB}(\widehat\tau-\tau)\).  Consider an arbitrary subsequence.  By boundedness of \(B^{-1}\sum_bD_b\), choose a further subsequence along which this deterministic matrix converges to \(D_\infty\succeq0\).  The proved central limit theorem and studentizer result give jointly, by Slutsky's theorem (proved here by convergence in probability of the second coordinate),
\[
 (X_{G,B},\widehat\Gamma_+)
 \Rightarrow (X,\Gamma_\infty),
 \qquad X\sim N_2(0,\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}),
 \quad \Gamma_\infty=\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}+D_\infty.
\]
Both matrices are positive definite.  If \(Z\sim N_2(0,I_2)\), then
\[
 X^\top\Gamma_\infty^{-1}X
 \stackrel d=Z^\top
 \Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}^{1/2}\Gamma_\infty^{-1}
 \Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}^{1/2}Z.
\]
Since \(\Gamma_\infty\succeq\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}\), inversion reverses Loewner order and every eigenvalue of the middle matrix is in \((0,1]\).  Diagonalizing it and using the same two independent standard-normal coordinates gives the pathwise coupling
\[
 Z^\top\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}^{1/2}\Gamma_\infty^{-1}
 \Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}^{1/2}Z
 \leq Z_1^2+Z_2^2.
\]
The right side has the \(\chi^2_2\) law.  The normal distribution assigns zero mass to the boundary at \(\chi^2_{2,1-\alpha}\), so the continuous-mapping theorem yields, along the selected further subsequence,
\[
 \liminf\Pr\!\left\{
 GB(\widehat\tau-\tau)^\top\widehat\Gamma_+^{-1}(\widehat\tau-\tau)
 \leq\chi^2_{2,1-\alpha}\right\}\geq1-\alpha.
\]
If the asserted global liminf failed, a subsequence violating it would possess the further subsequence just analyzed, a contradiction.  By the definition of \(\mathcal C_\alpha\), the event in braces is exactly \(\{\tau\in\mathcal C_\alpha\}\), which proves the result.
