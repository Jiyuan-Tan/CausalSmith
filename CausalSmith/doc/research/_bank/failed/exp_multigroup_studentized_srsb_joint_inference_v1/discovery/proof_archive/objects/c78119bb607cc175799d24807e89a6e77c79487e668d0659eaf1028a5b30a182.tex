Fix a block \(b\) and condition throughout on an arbitrary realized history in \(\mathcal F_{b-1}\).  No model-class membership or covariance-stabilization premise is used.  Write \(a_{gb}=(a_{Dg},a_{Sg})^\top\) for the two coordinates specified by \(\texttt{def:adaptive-witness}\), and write \(Y_{gb}(d,q)=Y_{gbi}(d,q)\), which is independent of \(i\) in that construction.  Substitution into the four coefficients \(c_{dq}\) gives
\[
m_{gbq_H}-m_{gbq_L}=(a_{Dg},a_{Sg})^\top.                 \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, the direct coordinate of the left side is
\[
\frac12\{Y_{gb}(1,q_H)-Y_{gb}(0,q_H)
             -Y_{gb}(1,q_L)+Y_{gb}(0,q_L)\}
=\frac{\theta_g(q_H-q_L)}2=a_{Dg},
\]
where \(q_H-q_L=1/2\) and \(\theta_g=4a_{Dg}\).  Its spillover coordinate is
\[
\frac12\sum_{q\in\{q_L,q_H\}}\{Y_{gb}(0,q)+Y_{gb}(1,q)\}
=2h_g+\delta_g+\frac{\theta_g}{2}=a_{Sg},
\]
by \(\delta_g=L-\theta_g\) and \(h_g=a_{Sg}/2+a_{Dg}-L/2\).

At saturation \(q\), the second stage in \(\texttt{def:soft-two-stage-design}\) assigns exactly \(np_{dq}\) units to own-treatment cell \(d\).  Since the witness outcome is constant over units,
\[
\frac1n\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_n}
 \frac{\mathbf 1\{A_{gbi}=d\}Y_{gbi}(d,q)}{p_{dq}}
=Y_{gbi}(d,q).
\]
Thus \(\widehat m_{gbq}=m_{gbq}\), \(e_{gbq}=0\), \(U_b=0\), and, by \(\texttt{def:score-splitting}\), \(R_b=A_b\).  In particular, \(K_b=0\).

At either saturation, \(q=1/2+x_{gb}/4\) and \(q^2-q=-3/16\).  The realized group mean therefore obeys
\[
\begin{aligned}
\bar Y^{\mathrm{obs}}_{gb}
&=h_g+q(\delta_g+\theta_gq)\\
&=\frac{a_{Sg}}2+\frac{a_{Dg}}4+\frac L4x_{gb}.
\end{aligned}                                               \tag{2}
\]
The four pairs \((H_g,J_g)\in\{-1,1\}^2\) occur equally often, and every realized \(x_b\) is balanced.  Hence all three terms on the right side of (2) have grand mean zero.  Since the block types alternate and \(\lambda=1\), the predictable balance vectors, apart from \(V_{g1}=H_g\), are
\[
V_{gb}^{A}=\frac32H_g+\frac14J_g+x_{g,b-1},
\qquad
V_{gb}^{B}=\frac54H_g+\frac12J_g.                        \tag{3}
\]

We now establish the history-uniform Gaussian comparison directly.  Define
\[
y_g=\left(a_{Dg},a_{Sg},\frac{V_{gb}}{\sigma_b}\right)^\top,
\qquad
S_G=G^{-1/2}\sum_{g=1}^Gx_{gb}y_g=(A_b^\top,W_b)^\top.
\]
For every block type and every history, \(\sum_gy_g=0\).  The first two coordinates of \(y_g\) have absolute value one.  Equation (3) gives \(|V_{gb}|\leq11/4\).  On an A block, put \(d_g=3H_g/2+J_g/4\).  Equal representation of the four types gives \(G^{-1}\sum_gd_g^2=37/16\), while \(\|x_{b-1}\|_2=\sqrt G\).  The reverse triangle inequality yields
\[
\sigma_b=\frac{\|d+x_{b-1}\|_2}{\sqrt{G-1}}
\geq\sqrt{\frac G{G-1}}\left(\frac{\sqrt{37}}4-1\right)>0. \tag{4}
\]
On a B block, direct calculation gives \(\sigma_b^2=29G/\{16(G-1)\}\), and initially \(\sigma_1^2=G/(G-1)\).  Consequently there is a deterministic \(K<\infty\), independent of \(G\), \(b\), and the history, such that
\[
\max_{1\leq g\leq G}\|y_g\|\leq K.                     \tag{5}
\]

Draw a uniform perfect matching \(\mathfrak m\) of \(\{1,\ldots,G\}\), write its pairs as \(\{r_j,s_j\}\), and independently draw Rademacher orientations \(\epsilon_j\), setting \(x_{r_j}=\epsilon_j\) and \(x_{s_j}=-\epsilon_j\).  A fixed balanced sign vector is generated by exactly \((G/2)!\) matching-orientation pairs.  Since the total number of such pairs is \((G-1)!!2^{G/2}\), its probability is
\[
\frac{(G/2)!}{(G-1)!!2^{G/2}}=\binom{G}{G/2}^{-1}.
\]
Thus this construction has exactly law \(P_0\), and, conditionally on \(\mathfrak m\),
\[
S_G=\sum_{j=1}^{G/2}\epsilon_jd_j,
\qquad
d_j=G^{-1/2}(y_{r_j}-y_{s_j}),
\qquad
C_{\mathfrak m}=\sum_jd_jd_j^\top.                       \tag{6}
\]
Let \(C_G=\operatorname{Cov}_0(S_G)\).  Each unordered edge belongs to a uniform perfect matching with probability \(1/(G-1)\).  The identity
\[
\sum_{g<h}(y_g-y_h)(y_g-y_h)^\top
=G\sum_g(y_g-\bar y)(y_g-\bar y)^\top
\]
therefore implies
\[
\mathbb E_{\mathfrak m}C_{\mathfrak m}
=\frac1{G-1}\sum_g(y_g-\bar y)(y_g-\bar y)^\top=C_G.    \tag{7}
\]
Moreover, for any fixed matrix entry, \(C_{\mathfrak m}\) has the form \(G^{-1}\sum_{g<h}I_{gh}c_{gh}\), with \(|c_{gh}|\leq4K^2\).  Here
\[
\Pr(I_{gh}=1)=\frac1{G-1},
\]
the joint probability is zero for two distinct edges sharing a vertex, and it is \(1/\{(G-1)(G-3)\}\) for two disjoint edges.  The diagonal, overlapping-edge, and disjoint-edge contributions to the variance are respectively bounded by
\[
G^{-2}O(G^2)O(G^{-1}),\qquad
G^{-2}O(G^3)O(G^{-2}),\qquad
G^{-2}O(G^4)O(G^{-3}).
\]
All are \(O(G^{-1})\), uniformly under (5).  Summing over the nine entries gives a constant \(C<\infty\) such that
\[
\mathbb E_{\mathfrak m}\|C_{\mathfrak m}-C_G\|_F^2
\leq \frac{CK^4}{G}.                                     \tag{8}
\]

Let \(Z_{\mathfrak m}=\sum_jg_jd_j\), where the \(g_j\) are independent standard normal variables.  For a function \(h:\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb R\) with bounded derivatives through order three, replace the summands in (6) one at a time.  The constant, linear, and quadratic Taylor terms agree because \(\epsilon_j\) and \(g_j\) have the same first two moments.  Taylor's integral remainder, (5), and \(\|d_j\|\leq2K/\sqrt G\) give
\[
\left|\mathbb E\{h(S_G)-h(Z_{\mathfrak m})\mid\mathfrak m\}\right|
\leq C\|D^3h\|_\infty\sum_j\|d_j\|^3
\leq \frac{CK^3\|D^3h\|_\infty}{\sqrt G}.              \tag{9}
\]
Let \(Z_G\sim N_3(0,C_G)\).  If \(C_0,C_1\) are positive semidefinite and \(C_t=(1-t)C_0+tC_1\), Gaussian integration by parts gives
\[
\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb Eh\{N(0,C_t)\}
=\frac12\operatorname{tr}\left[(C_1-C_0)
                 \mathbb ED^2h\{N(0,C_t)\}\right].      \tag{10}
\]
For singular covariances, apply the identity to \(C_t+\delta I_3\) and let \(\delta\downarrow0\); boundedness of \(D^2h\) permits dominated convergence.  Integrating (10), then using (8) and Cauchy--Schwarz together with (9), proves the uniform bound
\[
\left|\mathbb E_0h(S_G)-\mathbb Eh(Z_G)\right|
\leq\frac{C_K}{\sqrt G}
 \{\|D^2h\|_\infty+\|D^3h\|_\infty\}.                 \tag{11}
\]
This argument uses the exact covariance matrices themselves and never divides by a score-coordinate variance, so it remains valid on a singular covariance range.

Taking \(h(s)=f_{\eta,\kappa}(s_3)\) in (11) proves the scalar comparison because this fixed function has bounded derivatives through order three.  For the quadratic comparison, let \(\chi_L\) be a smooth cutoff equal to one on \(\|s_{1:2}\|\leq L\) and zero on \(\|s_{1:2}\|\geq2L\), and apply (11), entry by entry, to
\[
h_{rs,L}(s)=s_rs_s f_{\eta,\kappa}(s_3)\chi_L(s_{1:2}),
\qquad r,s\in\{1,2\}.
\]
The relevant second- and third-derivative norms are bounded by \(C(1+L^2)\).  Conditional on a matching, the independent-sign fourth-moment identity gives
\[
\mathbb E\left(\sum_j\epsilon_jd_{jr}\right)^4
=3\left(\sum_jd_{jr}^2\right)^2-2\sum_jd_{jr}^4
\leq12K^4.
\]
The covariance bound following from (6) gives a uniform fourth-moment bound for \(Z_G\) as well.  Therefore, for a constant depending only on \(K\),
\[
\mathbb E\{\|S_{G,1:2}\|^2\mathbf1(\|S_{G,1:2}\|>L)\}
+\mathbb E\{\|Z_{G,1:2}\|^2\mathbf1(\|Z_{G,1:2}\|>L)\}
\leq C_KL^{-2}.                                           \tag{12}
\]
Choose \(L=G^{1/16}\).  The smoothed comparison error in (11) is then \(O(G^{-3/8})\), and the truncation error in (12) is \(O(G^{-1/8})\).  Hence a single deterministic modulus tending to zero works uniformly over blocks and histories.  The first two coordinates of \(Z_G\) and its third coordinate have exactly the joint law of \((A_b^\star,W_b^\star)\), by their definition as the covariance-matched Gaussian pair.  This proves both weighted comparisons in the lemma.

It remains to derive the covariance expansion.  Complementation \(x\mapsto-x\) preserves \(P_0\), leaves the even weight \(f_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b)\) unchanged, and sends \(A_b\) to \(-A_b\).  Since \(R_b=A_b\), the conditional soft-law score is exactly centered and
\[
\Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}
=\frac{\mathbb E_0\{A_bA_b^\top f_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b)\}}
       {\mathbb E_0f_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b)}.                \tag{13}
\]
Also \(K_b=0\), so \(\operatorname{Var}_0(A_b)=\Sigma_{b,0}\), and \(\beta_b=\operatorname{Cov}_0(A_b,W_b)\).  Since \(\operatorname{Var}_0(W_b)=1\), the covariance-matched Gaussian pair admits the possibly singular orthogonal decomposition
\[
A_b^\star=\beta_bW_b^\star+E_b^\star,
\qquad
E_b^\star\perp W_b^\star,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(E_b^\star)=\Sigma_{b,0}-\beta_b\beta_b^\top. \tag{14}
\]
Here \(W_b^\star\sim N(0,1)\).  For \(Z\sim N(0,1)\), direct Gaussian integration gives
\[
\mathbb Ee^{-\kappa Z^2}=(1+2\kappa)^{-1/2},
\qquad
\mathbb E\{Z^2e^{-\kappa Z^2}\}=(1+2\kappa)^{-3/2}.
\]
Using (14) and the definition in \(\texttt{def:projection-gain}\), the Gaussian analogue of the ratio in (13) is therefore
\[
\Sigma_{b,0}-(1-v_{\eta,\kappa})\beta_b\beta_b^\top.     \tag{15}
\]
Both exact and Gaussian denominators are at least \(\eta>0\).  Their numerators are uniformly bounded by (5), and the weighted comparisons already proved make both numerator and denominator errors tend uniformly to zero.  Comparing the two ratios in (13) and (15) proves
\[
\sup_{b,\mathcal F_{b-1}}
\left\|\Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}-\Sigma_{b,0}
 +(1-v_{\eta,\kappa})\beta_b\beta_b^\top\right\|\longrightarrow0,
\]
which is the remaining assertion.
