\section*{exp\_\allowbreak{}multigroup\_\allowbreak{}studentized\_\allowbreak{}srsb\_\allowbreak{}joint\_\allowbreak{}inference}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} Predictable soft rerandomization balances lagged group outcomes while preserving the fixed sustained direct-and-spillover target.  The bounded lag-one partial-interference class contains only structural and design-regularity conditions.  On this class, complement symmetry gives exact first-order propensities, score splitting isolates the balanced-sign component, and singular-support Gaussian transport is uniform without covariance-limit assumptions.  Many-block joint inference separately requires rerandomization covariance stabilization and a positive-definite limit, whereas the projection identity \(\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}=\Omega_{\mathrm{CR}}-(1-v_{\eta,\kappa})P\) is a distinct \(G\to\infty\) result requiring the complete-randomization, rerandomization, and projection stabilizations but not positive definiteness.  Logged pair probabilities and a positive-semidefinite completion yield an observable conservative \(2\)-by-\(2\) studentizer and simultaneous Wald coverage.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Existing papers separately cover static spillover-and-carryover designs, sequential balance, adaptive martingale inference, and static rerandomization projection geometry.  They do not provide the history-uniform soft-tilt quadratic-moment transport that joins fixed-half group saturations to a joint direct-and-spillover score, nor the resulting separation between arbitrary-\(G\), many-block inference conditions and the \(G\to\infty\) projection conditions.

\paragraph{Niche.} A predictable complement-symmetric kernel preserves exact joint Horvitz--Thompson unbiasedness while allowing prior outcomes to guide balance.  Structural membership is kept separate from covariance stabilization, so the same bounded design class supports both fixed-\(G\) many-block inference and growing-\(G\) efficiency analysis under their own explicit hypotheses.

\paragraph{Fill.} The project derives exact unbiasedness, a two-stage martingale central limit theorem under separately stated rerandomization covariance conditions, an observable positive-semidefinite-conservative matrix studentizer, simultaneous coverage, a structural-class uniform singular Gaussian transport theorem, and a growing-\(G\) projection covariance identity under exactly the three required stabilization conditions.

\section{Related work}

Hudgens--Halloran (2008) and Jiang--Imai--Malani (2023) define and estimate direct and spillover contrasts under static two-stage partial interference.  Bojinov--Rambachan--Shephard (2021) supplies the ordinary no-future-assignment nonanticipation convention for dynamic potential outcomes; the stronger lag-one finite-memory equality used here is an imposed restriction tailored to endpoints after two-period held assignments and is not attributed to that paper.  Bojinov--Simchi-Levi--Zhao (2023) is relevant as repeated-block design-based switchback methodology, but it does not supply the partial-interference fixed-half saturation and fixed-count two-stage score used here.  Yu--Ma--Liu (2025) adds carryover and a minimax timing design, but its first-stage law is fixed rather than predictably reweighted by prior outcomes.  Zhou et al. (2018) optimize sequential covariate balance without spillover or carryover inference.  Li--Zhao (2026) provides general adaptive finite-population IPW and martingale theory, including sequential rerandomization; the present contribution is the design-specific conditional covariance and Lindeberg verification for the fixed-half, fixed-count joint sustained-endpoint score.  Li--Ding--Rubin (2018) and Lu et al. (2023) give static hard-rerandomization projection geometry.  Li--Ding (2017), Shi--Ding (2026), and Tian--Yang--Ding (2025) supply finite-population Gaussian approximation tools, but not radial soft weighting of unbounded score quadratics on singular covariance support.  Cohen--Fogarty (2022) is relevant as an alternative Gaussian-prepivoted route to finite-population calibration, whereas this note delivers a direct conservative completed-Wald region.  Masoero et al. (2024) is relevant for the broader multiple-randomization interference setting, but does not give the repeated outcome-adaptive soft-rerandomization joint-inference result.  Jizhou Liu (2026) covers static two-stage covariate-adaptive inference, while the present design is predictably reweighted by prior outcomes across blocks.  He--Tchetgen Tchetgen (2026) covers broad exposure-mapping-agnostic two-stage estimators, central limit theory, and conservative variance procedures, but not the logged-pair PSD completion and simultaneous ellipse for this sustained lag-one target.  Liu--Shaikh--Zhong (2026) covers static saturation randomization tests; these results sharpen the boundary around the outcome-adaptive repeated-block theorem proved here.

\section{Setup}

Target (estimand / structural parameter): \(\tau=(\tau_D,\tau_S)^\top=\sum_{(d,q)\in\mathcal D\times\mathcal Q}c_{dq}\mu_{dq}\), the fixed finite-population sustained-exposure contrast for each admissible finite \(G\) and \(B\).
Primary functional / estimator / diagnostic: \(\widehat\tau=\sum_{d,q}c_{dq}(GBn)^{-1}\sum_{b,g,i}I_{gbi,dq}Y^{\mathrm{obs}}_{g,2b,i}/\pi_{gbi,dq}\), with \(\sqrt{GB}(\widehat\tau-\tau)\Rightarrow N_2(0,\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}})\) along every sequence in the structural class \(\mathcal M_{G,B}\) for which \(B\to\infty\), \(B^{-1}\sum_{b\in\mathcal B_B}\Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}\to_P\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}\), and \(\lambda_{\min}(\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}})>0\), while the admissible even \(G\) may remain fixed or vary arbitrarily with \(B\).

  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat\tau_b=(Gn)^{-1}\sum_{g,i,d,q}c_{dq}I_{gbi,dq}Y^{\mathrm{obs}}_{g,2b,i}/\pi_{gbi,dq}\).
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}tau} --- \texttt{joint Horvitz-\allowbreak{}-\allowbreak{}Thompson effect estimator}; \(\mathbb R^2\); \texttt{headline estimator}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat\tau=B^{-1}\sum_{b\in\mathcal B_B}\widehat\tau_b=\sum_{d,q}c_{dq}\widehat\mu_{dq}\).
  \item \texttt{m\_\allowbreak{}\{gbq\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{group saturation-\allowbreak{}specific causal score}; \(\mathbb R^2\); \texttt{first-\allowbreak{}stage score}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(m_{gbq}=\sum_{d\in\mathcal D}c_{dq}n^{-1}\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_n}Y_{gbi}(d,q)\).

  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat m_{gbq}=\sum_{d\in\mathcal D}c_{dq}n^{-1}\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_n}\mathbf 1\{A_{gbi}=d\}Y_{gbi}(d,q)/p_{dq}\).

  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(e_{gbq}=\widehat m_{gbq}-m_{gbq}\).
  \item \texttt{a\_\allowbreak{}\{gb\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{high-\allowbreak{}versus-\allowbreak{}low group score difference}; \(\mathbb R^2\); \texttt{balanced-\allowbreak{}sign score}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(a_{gb}=m_{gbq_H}-m_{gbq_L}\).
  \item \texttt{T\_\allowbreak{}\{gbq\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{within-\allowbreak{}group score covariance}; \(\mathbb S_+^2\); \texttt{second-\allowbreak{}stage covariance}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(T_{gbq}=\operatorname{Var}(e_{gbq}\mid Q_{gb}=q,\mathcal F_{b-1})\).
  \item \texttt{R\_\allowbreak{}b} --- \texttt{normalized block estimation error}; \(\mathbb R^2\); \texttt{martingale increment}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(R_b=\sqrt G(\widehat\tau_b-\tau_b)\).
  \item \texttt{A\_\allowbreak{}b} --- \texttt{balanced-\allowbreak{}sign component of the block score}; \(\mathbb R^2\); \texttt{rerandomized score component}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(A_b=G^{-1/2}\sum_{g\in\mathcal G_G}x_{gb}a_{gb}\).
  \item \texttt{U\_\allowbreak{}b} --- \texttt{within-\allowbreak{}group component of the block score}; \(\mathbb R^2\); \texttt{second-\allowbreak{}stage score component}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(U_b=2G^{-1/2}\sum_{g\in\mathcal G_G}e_{gb,Q_{gb}}\).
  \item \texttt{K\_\allowbreak{}b} --- \texttt{unchanged within-\allowbreak{}group covariance component}; \(\mathbb S_+^2\); \texttt{covariance component}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(K_b=(2/G)\sum_{g\in\mathcal G_G}(T_{gbq_L}+T_{gbq_H})\).

  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}=\operatorname{Var}_{\mathrm{RR}}(R_b\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})\).
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}beta\_\allowbreak{}b} --- \texttt{score-\allowbreak{}on-\allowbreak{}imbalance projection loading}; \(\mathbb R^2\); \texttt{efficiency loading}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\beta_b=\operatorname{Cov}_0(R_b,W_b\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})=((G-1)\sigma_b)^{-1}\sum_g(a_{gb}-\bar a_b)V_{gb}\).
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}Omega\_\allowbreak{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathrm\{RR\}\allowbreak{}\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{limiting soft-\allowbreak{}rerandomization covariance}; \(\mathbb S_{++}^2\); \texttt{limit covariance}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} The deterministic limit of \(B^{-1}\sum_b\Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}\).
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}Omega\_\allowbreak{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathrm\{CR\}\allowbreak{}\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{limiting complete-\allowbreak{}randomization covariance}; \(\mathbb S_+^2\); \texttt{benchmark limit covariance}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} The deterministic limit of \(B^{-1}\sum_b\Sigma_{b,0}\).
  \item \texttt{P} --- \texttt{limiting projection covariance}; \(\mathbb S_+^2\); \texttt{precision-\allowbreak{}gain matrix}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} The deterministic limit of \(B^{-1}\sum_b\beta_b\beta_b^\top\).
  \item \texttt{(A\_\allowbreak{}b\textasciicircum{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}star,\allowbreak{}W\_\allowbreak{}b\textasciicircum{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}star)} --- \texttt{covariance-\allowbreak{}matched Gaussian pair}; \(\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R\); \texttt{Gaussian comparison object}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} A centered Gaussian vector with the exact conditional \(P_0\)-covariance of \((A_b,W_b)\), supported on that covariance range when singular.
  \item \texttt{(R\_\allowbreak{}b\textasciicircum{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}star,\allowbreak{}W\_\allowbreak{}b\textasciicircum{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}star)} --- \texttt{covariance-\allowbreak{}matched Gaussian score-\allowbreak{}and-\allowbreak{}imbalance pair}; \(\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R\); \texttt{Gaussian comparison object}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} A centered Gaussian vector with the exact conditional \(P_0\)-covariance of \((R_b,W_b)\), supported on that covariance range when singular.
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}varepsilon\_\allowbreak{}G} --- \texttt{history-\allowbreak{}uniform Gaussian-\allowbreak{}transport modulus}; \([0,\infty)\); \texttt{open probability object}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} A deterministic sequence derived by \(\texttt{oeq:weighted-gaussian-transport}\).
  \item \texttt{v\_\allowbreak{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}eta,\allowbreak{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}kappa\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{standard-\allowbreak{}normal soft-\allowbreak{}tilt variance}; \((0,1)\); \texttt{universal shrinkage constant}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(v_{\eta,\kappa}=\{\eta+(1-\eta)(1+2\kappa)^{-3/2}\}/\{\eta+(1-\eta)(1+2\kappa)^{-1/2}\}\).
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathcal K\_\allowbreak{}b} --- \texttt{block exposure-\allowbreak{}cell index set}; \(\mathcal G_G\times\mathcal I_n\times\mathcal D\times\mathcal Q\); \texttt{studentizer index set}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Each \(k=(g,i,d,q)\in\mathcal K_b\) indexes one potential exposure cell in block \(b\).
  \item \texttt{u\_\allowbreak{}\{k,\allowbreak{}b\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{vector-\allowbreak{}valued exposure-\allowbreak{}cell outcome}; \(\mathbb R^2\); \texttt{covariance summand}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} For \(k=(g,i,d,q)\), \(u_{k,b}=c_{dq}Y_{gbi}(d,q)\).
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathcal Z\_\allowbreak{}b} --- \texttt{unordered impossible exposure-\allowbreak{}pair set}; \(2^{\{\{k,\ell\}:k,\ell\in\mathcal K_b\}}\); \texttt{PSD-\allowbreak{}completion index}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\mathcal Z_b=\{\{k,\ell\}:k\neq\ell,\ \pi_{k\ell,b}=0\}\).
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}Gamma\_\allowbreak{}b} --- \texttt{observable completed block covariance estimator}; \(\mathbb S^2\); \texttt{block studentizer}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} The pairwise Horvitz--Thompson covariance sum augmented by a diagonal completion for \(\mathcal Z_b\).
  \item \texttt{D\_\allowbreak{}b} --- \texttt{deterministic positive-\allowbreak{}semidefinite completion bias}; \(\mathbb S_+^2\); \texttt{conservativeness certificate}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(D_b=(Gn^2)^{-1}\sum_{\{k,\ell\}\in\mathcal Z_b}(u_{k,b}+u_{\ell,b})(u_{k,b}+u_{\ell,b})^\top\).
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}Gamma} --- \texttt{average observable covariance estimator}; \(\mathbb S^2\); \texttt{joint studentizer}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\widehat\Gamma=B^{-1}\sum_{b\in\mathcal B_B}\widehat\Gamma_b\).
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}Gamma\_\allowbreak{}+\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{spectrally clipped covariance estimator}; \(\mathbb S_{++}^2\); \texttt{invertible studentizer}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} If \(\widehat\Gamma=U\operatorname{diag}(\gamma_1,\gamma_2)U^\top\), then \(\widehat\Gamma_+=U\operatorname{diag}(\max\{\gamma_1,r_B\},\max\{\gamma_2,r_B\})U^\top\).
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathcal C\_\allowbreak{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}alpha} --- \texttt{simultaneous Wald confidence ellipse}; \(2^{\mathbb R^2}\); \texttt{confidence region}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \(\mathcal C_\alpha=\{t\in\mathbb R^2:GB(\widehat\tau-t)^\top\widehat\Gamma_+^{-1}(\widehat\tau-t)\leq\chi^2_{2,1-\alpha}\}\).
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathcal M\_\allowbreak{}\{G,\allowbreak{}B\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{triangular finite-\allowbreak{}population design class}; \(2^{\mathfrak P}\); \texttt{theorem domain}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} The class defined by \(\texttt{def:model-class}\).
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathcal W\textasciicircum{}\{AB\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{alternating-\allowbreak{}block adaptive witness family}; \(\mathcal M_{G,B}\); \texttt{nonvacuity witness}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} The explicitly constructed family in \(\texttt{def:adaptive-witness}\).
\end{itemize}

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:design-based-finiteness]\label{ass:design-based-finiteness}
Conditional on the finite population \(\{Y_{gti}:g\in\mathcal G_G,\ i\in\mathcal I_n\}\), the only randomness is generated by \(x_b\) and \(A_{gbi}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Finite-population randomization inference, \cite{LiDing2017FPCLT}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:partial-interference]\label{ass:partial-interference}
For every unit, \(Y_{gti}\) is invariant to treatment and saturation assignments outside group \(g\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Partial interference across groups, \cite{HudgensHalloran2008}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:stratified-interference]\label{ass:stratified-interference}
Within group \(g\), the endpoint response depends on the contemporaneous assignment vector only through the unit's own treatment \(d\) and the realized saturation \(q\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Stratified interference, \cite{HudgensHalloran2008}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:nonanticipation]\label{ass:nonanticipation}
For every \(t\leq2B\), if \(h,h'\in\mathcal H_{2B}\) agree through period \(t\), then \(Y_{gti}(h)=Y_{gti}(h')\); assignments after period \(t\) do not affect the period-\(t\) endpoint.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Nonanticipating potential outcomes, \cite{BojinovRambachanShephard2021}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:finite-memory-lag-one]\label{ass:finite-memory-lag-one}
For every \(2\leq t\leq2B\), if \(h,h'\in\mathcal H_{2B}\) agree at periods \(t-1\) and \(t\), then \(Y_{gti}(h)=Y_{gti}(h')\) regardless of the remaining history entries.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This is an imposed finite-memory restriction, not a consequence of ordinary nonanticipation. It is interpretable when saturation and own treatment are held fixed within each two-period block, so the endpoint depends on the block's sustained exposure while earlier blocks may affect the predictable randomization rule but not the endpoint response.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:imbalance-variance-window]\label{ass:imbalance-variance-window}
Uniformly over \(b\in\mathcal B_B\) and realized \(\mathcal F_{b-1}\), \(c_\sigma\leq\sigma_b^2\leq C_\sigma\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Finite-population nondegenerate variance condition, \cite{LiDing2017FPCLT}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:rr-covariance-stabilization]\label{ass:rr-covariance-stabilization}
\(B^{-1}\sum_{b\in\mathcal B_B}\Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}\to_P\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Predictable quadratic-variation stabilization, \cite{LiZhao2026Adaptive}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:rr-limit-positive]\label{ass:rr-limit-positive}
\(\lambda_{\min}(\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}})>0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Positive-definite target covariance, \cite{LiZhao2026Adaptive}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:cr-covariance-stabilization]\label{ass:cr-covariance-stabilization}
\(B^{-1}\sum_{b\in\mathcal B_B}\Sigma_{b,0}\to_P\Omega_{\mathrm{CR}}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Finite-population covariance stabilization, \cite{LiDingRubin2018}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:projection-stabilization]\label{ass:projection-stabilization}
\(B^{-1}\sum_{b\in\mathcal B_B}\beta_b\beta_b^\top\to_P P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This is an imposed predictable-array stabilization condition for the history-adaptive sequence. Li--Ding--Rubin (2018) motivates the static projection analogy but does not state this adaptive block-average hypothesis.
\end{assumption}

\section{Main results}

\begin{proposition}[prop:exact-unbiasedness]\label{prop:exact-unbiasedness}
For every finite \(G\), \(B\), and history, complement symmetry gives \(\Pr_{\mathrm{RR}}(Q_{gb}=q_H\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})=\Pr_{\mathrm{RR}}(Q_{gb}=q_L\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})=1/2\). Hence \(\pi_{gbi,dq}=p_{dq}/2\geq1/8\) and \(\mathbb E_{\mathrm{RR}}(\widehat\tau)=\tau\) exactly.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix a block \(b\) and condition on \(\mathcal F_{b-1}\).  By the definition of \(\mathcal X_G\), the map \(x\mapsto-x\) is a bijection of \(\mathcal X_G\), and \(P_0(-x)=P_0(x)\).  Since \(Z_b(-x)=-Z_b(x)\), also \(W_b(-x)=-W_b(x)\).  The weight is even, because
\[
 f_{\eta,\kappa}(-w)=\eta+(1-\eta)e^{-\kappa(-w)^2}=f_{\eta,\kappa}(w).
\]
Consequently \(P_{\mathrm{RR},b}(-x\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})=P_{\mathrm{RR},b}(x\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})\).  Pairing every \(x\) with \(-x\) gives
\[
 \Pr(x_{gb}=1\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})
 =\Pr(x_{gb}=-1\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})=\frac12.
\]
The definition of \(Q_{gb}\) proves the two asserted saturation probabilities.

Conditional on \(Q_{gb}=q\), the fixed-count simple random sample includes unit \(i\) with probability \(q\) when \(d=1\) and with probability \(1-q\) when \(d=0\).  Thus, by the preceding first-stage calculation and the definition of \(p_{dq}\),
\[
 \pi_{gbi,dq}
 =\Pr(Q_{gb}=q\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})
   \Pr(A_{gbi}=d\mid Q_{gb}=q,\mathcal F_{b-1})
 =\frac{p_{dq}}2.
\]
Since \(q_L=1/4\) and \(q_H=3/4\), \(p_{dq}\geq1/4\), whence \(\pi_{gbi,dq}\geq1/8\), so every division in the Horvitz--Thompson estimator is valid.

Assumptions \(\texttt{ass:partial-interference}\), \(\texttt{ass:stratified-interference}\), \(\texttt{ass:nonanticipation}\), and \(\texttt{ass:finite-memory-lag-one}\) imply consistency at the endpoint of the held two-period block:
\[
 I_{gbi,dq}Y^{\mathrm{obs}}_{g,2b,i}
 =I_{gbi,dq}Y_{gbi}(d,q).
\]
The potential endpoint on the right is fixed under \(\texttt{ass:design-based-finiteness}\).  Therefore
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[
   \frac{I_{gbi,dq}Y^{\mathrm{obs}}_{g,2b,i}}{\pi_{gbi,dq}}
   \mathrel{\bigg|}\mathcal F_{b-1}\right]
 =Y_{gbi}(d,q).
\]
Summing this equality with coefficient \(c_{dq}/(Gn)\) proves
\(\mathbb E(\widehat\tau_b\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})=\tau_b\).  Iterated expectation and block averaging then give
\[
 \mathbb E(\widehat\tau)
 =\frac1B\sum_{b=1}^B\mathbb E(\widehat\tau_b)
 =\frac1B\sum_{b=1}^B\tau_b=\tau.
\]
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Exact first-order symmetry prevents adaptive normalizers from creating bias or hidden first-order weights. \textit{Gap.} YuMaLiu2025 Theorem 3 treats a fixed minimax two-stage design. LiZhao2026Adaptive Theorem 1 allows adaptive logged assignment probabilities, including sequential rerandomization, but does not supply the conditional two-stage fixed-half/fixed-count exposure calculation that yields this complement-symmetric one-half saturation propensity and the resulting cell probability p\_dq/2. \textit{Consumer.} A prospective multigroup market experiment can log the adaptive kernel and retain an exactly interpretable joint sustained-exposure target.

\begin{lemma}[lem:score-decomposition]\label{lem:score-decomposition}
For every block, \(R_b=A_b+U_b\), \(\mathbb E(U_b\mid x_b,\mathcal F_{b-1})=0\), and the group summands of \(U_b\) are conditionally independent. Consequently \(\Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}=\operatorname{Var}_{\mathrm{RR}}(A_b\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})+K_b\), the cross covariance is zero, and exactly the same \(K_b\) appears under \(P_0\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(b\) and condition on \(\mathcal F_{b-1}\).  The complement-symmetry calculation from \(\texttt{def:soft-two-stage-design}\) gives \(\pi_{gbi,dq}=p_{dq}/2\).  Only the realized saturation contributes and hence
\[
 \sqrt G\,\widehat\tau_b
 =\frac{2}{\sqrt G}\sum_{g=1}^G\widehat m_{gb,Q_{gb}}.
\]
On the other hand, the definition of \(m_{gbq}\) gives
\[
 \sqrt G\,\tau_b
 =\frac1{\sqrt G}\sum_{g=1}^G(m_{gbq_L}+m_{gbq_H}).
\]
For \(x_{gb}=1\), the difference between twice the selected mean and the sum of the two means is \(a_{gb}\); for \(x_{gb}=-1\), it is \(-a_{gb}\).  Substituting \(\widehat m_{gbq}=m_{gbq}+e_{gbq}\) therefore yields, exactly,
\[
 R_b=\frac1{\sqrt G}\sum_gx_{gb}a_{gb}
       +\frac2{\sqrt G}\sum_ge_{gb,Q_{gb}}=A_b+U_b.
\]

Within a group, fixed-count simple random sampling and the definition of \(p_{dq}\) imply \(\mathbb E(\widehat m_{gbq}\mid Q_{gb}=q,\mathcal F_{b-1})=m_{gbq}\).  Thus
\[
 \mathbb E(e_{gb,Q_{gb}}\mid x_b,\mathcal F_{b-1})=0,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E(U_b\mid x_b,\mathcal F_{b-1})=0.
\]
The second-stage samples are independent across groups conditional on \(x_b\), so the summands of \(U_b\) are conditionally independent.  It follows by conditional covariance additivity that
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(U_b\mid x_b,\mathcal F_{b-1})
 =\frac4G\sum_gT_{gb,Q_{gb}}.
\]
The conditional mean-zero identity also gives
\(\operatorname{Cov}(A_b,U_b\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})=0\).  Complement symmetry makes each \(Q_{gb}\) marginally high or low with probability \(1/2\), both under the soft law and under \(P_0\).  Hence under either first-stage law
\[
 \mathbb E\{\operatorname{Var}(U_b\mid x_b,\mathcal F_{b-1})
          \mid\mathcal F_{b-1}\}
 =\frac2G\sum_g(T_{gbq_L}+T_{gbq_H})=K_b.
\]
The law of total covariance now proves
\[
 \Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}
 =\operatorname{Var}_{\mathrm{RR}}(A_b\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})+K_b,
\]
with zero cross covariance, and proves that precisely the same \(K_b\) is present under \(P_0\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This exact split isolates the only covariance component that the first-stage soft tilt can change. \textit{Gap.} JiangImaiMalani2023 derives static two-stage covariance formulas, but not this predictable balanced-sign decomposition with unchanged fixed-count within-group covariance. \textit{Consumer.} The decomposition is the algebraic bridge from the adaptive design to the projection-efficiency theorem and the observable studentizer.

\begin{lemma}[lem:martingale-centering]\label{lem:martingale-centering}
The block scores satisfy \(\mathbb E(R_b\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})=0\), and \(\sqrt{GB}(\widehat\tau-\tau)=B^{-1/2}\sum_{b\in\mathcal B_B}R_b\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By \(\texttt{prop:exact-unbiasedness}\), conditional on \(\mathcal F_{b-1}\), \(\mathbb E(\widehat\tau_b\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})=\tau_b\).  Assumptions \(\texttt{ass:nonanticipation}\) and \(\texttt{ass:finite-memory-lag-one}\) ensure that \(\tau_b\), which uses the two-period sustained-exposure endpoints of block \(b\), is a fixed finite-population contrast rather than a function of the current random draw.  Therefore
\[
 \mathbb E(R_b\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})
 =\sqrt G\{\mathbb E(\widehat\tau_b\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})-\tau_b\}=0.
\]
Moreover, the definitions of \(\widehat\tau\), \(\tau\), and \(R_b\) give the pathwise identity
\[
 \sqrt{GB}(\widehat\tau-\tau)
 =\frac{\sqrt{GB}}B\sum_{b=1}^B(\widehat\tau_b-\tau_b)
 =B^{-1/2}\sum_{b=1}^BR_b.
\]
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Predictable centering makes the across-block accumulation a design-based martingale rather than a pseudo-target drift. \textit{Gap.} LiZhao2026Adaptive supplies the general adaptive martingale template, including sequential rerandomization, but leaves to the present design the conditional derivation that the fixed-half first stage plus fixed-count second stage produces a centered joint score for the sustained lag-one endpoint target. \textit{Consumer.} This identity supplies the filtration structure needed for the root-\(GB\) limit law.

\begin{lemma}[lem:block-moments]\label{lem:block-moments}
There exists a finite constant \(C_R\), independent of \(G\), \(B\), \(b\), and the history, such that \(\mathbb E_0\|R_b\|^4\leq C_R\) and \(\mathbb E_{\mathrm{RR}}(\|R_b\|^4\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})\leq C_R/\eta\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
All constants below may depend on the fixed constants \(M\), \(n\), \(q_L\), and \(q_H\), but not on \(G\), \(B\), \(b\), or the realized history.  The fixed contrast coefficients and \(|Y_{gbi}(d,q)|\leq M\) give constants \(L_a,L_e<\infty\) such that
\[
 \|a_{gb}\|\leq L_a,
 \qquad \|e_{gbq}\|\leq L_e
\]
for all indices.  The second inequality also follows uniformly if \(n\) varies through \(4\mathbb N\), because \(p_{dq}\geq1/4\) and every term is an average of bounded endpoints.

Generate a uniform balanced sign vector by first drawing a uniform perfect matching of \(\{1,\ldots,G\}\), and then, independently for each matched pair \(\{r_j,s_j\}\), assigning opposite signs with a Rademacher orientation \(\xi_j\).  Every balanced sign vector is compatible with exactly \((G/2)!\) such oriented matchings, so this construction has law \(P_0\).  Conditional on the matching,
\[
 A_b=G^{-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^{G/2}\xi_j(a_{r_jb}-a_{s_jb}).
\]
For a scalar Rademacher sum, expansion of the fourth power and cancellation of monomials containing an odd power of some \(\xi_j\) give
\[
 \mathbb E\left(\sum_j\xi_jd_j\right)^4
 =\sum_jd_j^4+6\sum_{j<k}d_j^2d_k^2
 \leq3\left(\sum_jd_j^2\right)^2.
\]
Applying this to each coordinate and using norm equivalence in \(\mathbb R^2\) proves \(\mathbb E_0\|A_b\|^4\leq C_A\).

Conditional on \(x_b\), the group terms in \(U_b\) are independent, centered, and bounded by \(2L_e/\sqrt G\).  The same fourth-power expansion, now with expectations in place of Rademacher averages, gives
\[
 \mathbb E(\|U_b\|^4\mid x_b,\mathcal F_{b-1})\leq C_U.
\]
Since \(\|u+v\|^4\leq8(\|u\|^4+\|v\|^4)\), \(\texttt{lem:score-decomposition}\) yields
\[
 \mathbb E_0\|R_b\|^4\leq C_R
\]
for a finite uniform \(C_R\).  Finally,
\[
 \frac{dP_{\mathrm{RR},b}}{dP_0}(x\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})
 =\frac{f_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b(x))}{C_b}leq\frac1\eta,
\]
because \(f_{\eta,\kappa}\leq1\) and \(C_b\geq\eta\).  Conditional second-stage laws are identical under the two designs, so likelihood-ratio domination gives
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathrm{RR}}(\|R_b\|^4\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})
 \leq C_R/\eta.
\]
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The uniform fourth moment supplies the actual conditional Lindeberg control rather than treating covariance stabilization as a substitute. \textit{Gap.} LiDing2017FPCLT gives finite-population leverage conditions, but not likelihood-ratio transfer of a joint two-stage score under a predictable soft tilt. \textit{Consumer.} The martingale central limit theorem uses this bound to control every Cram\'er--Wold contrast.

\begin{lemma}[lem:pair-probability-control]\label{lem:pair-probability-control}
There is a constant \(C_\pi<\infty\), independent of block and history, such that for distinct groups \(g\neq h\), \(\left|\mathbb E_{\mathrm{RR}}(x_{gb}x_{hb}\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})\right|\leq C_\pi/G\). Therefore every feasible cross-group exposure-pair probability equals its product-marginal benchmark plus \(O(G^{-1})\) uniformly, while every feasible within-group pair probability is bounded away from zero.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix distinct groups \(g\neq h\), condition on \(\mathcal F_{b-1}\), and use the uniform perfect-matching representation.  The event that \(g\) is paired with \(h\) has probability \(1/(G-1)\), and its contribution to \(|\mathbb E_0[x_gx_hf_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b)]|\) is therefore at most \(1/(G-1)\).

On the complementary event, \(g\) and \(h\) lie in two different matching pairs.  Conditional on all other orientations, write
\[
 W_b=S+\xi r+\zeta s,
\]
where \(\xi,\zeta\) are the two independent orientations.  From \(|V_{gb}|\leq K_H+2\lambda M\) and \(\sigma_b^2\geq c_\sigma\),
\[
 |r|\vee|s|\leq\frac{2(K_H+2\lambda M)}{\sqrt{Gc_\sigma}}.
\]
The conditional expectation of \(x_gx_hf_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b)\) is, up to a sign, one quarter of the mixed difference
\[
 f(S+r+s)-f(S+r-s)-f(S-r+s)+f(S-r-s).
\]
Twice applying the fundamental theorem of calculus bounds its absolute value by
\(4\|f_{\eta,\kappa}''\|_\infty|rs|\leq C/G\).  Averaging the two matching cases yields
\[
 |\mathbb E_0[x_gx_hf_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b)]|\leq C/G.
\]
Since \(C_b\geq\eta\), division by the soft-kernel normalizer gives
\[
 |\mathbb E_{\mathrm{RR}}(x_{gb}x_{hb}\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})|
 \leq C_\pi/G.
\]

Complement symmetry gives \(\mathbb E_{\mathrm{RR}}x_{gb}=0\).  Thus, for \(s,t\in\{-1,1\}\),
\[
 \Pr(x_{gb}=s,x_{hb}=t\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})
 =\frac14\{1+st\,\mathbb E_{mathrm{RR}}(x_{gb}x_{hb}\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})\}
 =\frac14+O(G^{-1}).
\]
Conditional on the two saturations, the within-group samples are independent across groups.  Multiplication by the appropriate factors \(p_{dq}\) and \(p_{er}\), each at least \(1/4\), proves the uniform product-marginal approximation for every cross-group exposure pair.

For a feasible pair within one group, the first-stage factor is \(1/2\).  The second-stage factor is one of the simple-random-sampling probabilities
\[
 q,\quad 1-q,\quad
 \frac{nq(nq-1)}{n(n-1)},\quad
 \frac{n(1-q)\{n(1-q)-1\}}{n(n-1)},\quad
 \frac{nq\,n(1-q)}{n(n-1)}.
\]
Terms equal to zero are precisely infeasible pairs.  Over \(n\in4\mathbb N\) and \(q\in\{1/4,3/4\}\), every positive displayed term is bounded below by a universal positive constant (the smallest two-treated boundary occurs at \(n=8\)).  This proves the final assertion.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Uniform pair control turns the exact PSD completion identity into a concentrating observable matrix estimator. \textit{Gap.} Static two-stage variance papers compute pair probabilities directly; predictable soft weighting requires a new mixed finite-difference bound under the fixed-half support. \textit{Consumer.} The bound controls the \(O(G^2)\) cross-group terms in the conservative covariance estimator.

\begin{theorem}[thm:joint-design-clt]\label{thm:joint-design-clt}
For every sequence of finite-population designs belonging to \(\mathcal M_{G,B}\) along which \(B\to\infty\), with the admissible even value of \(G\) allowed to depend arbitrarily on \(B\) and in particular allowed to remain fixed, suppose separately that
\[
B^{-1}\sum_{b\in\mathcal B_B}\Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}\to_P\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\lambda_{\min}(\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}})>0.
\]
Then
\[
\sqrt{GB}(\widehat\tau-\tau)=B^{-1/2}\sum_{b\in\mathcal B_B}R_b\ \Rightarrow\ N_2(0,\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}).
\]
Thus the root-\(GB\) limit law requires no restriction on \(G/B\); it is derived from martingale centering, the uniform block fourth-moment bound, and the two displayed theorem-specific covariance conditions rather than from membership in the structural class.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a sequence covered by the proposed statement and condition on the fixed finite population.  Membership in \(\mathcal M_{G,B}\) supplies the structural and bounded-design conditions, while the two displayed covariance conditions are imposed separately.  No step below requires \(G\to\infty\), so \(G\) may be fixed, bounded and varying, or divergent.

Fix \(t\in\mathbb R^2\).  The assertion for \(t=0\) is immediate.  For \(t\ne0\), define
\[
 \xi_{Bb}=B^{-1/2}t^\top R_b,
 \qquad
 \mathcal V_B=\sum_{b=1}^B
   \mathbb E(\xi_{Bb}^2\mid\mathcal F_{b-1}),
 \qquad
 V_B=\mathbb E\mathcal V_B.
\]
By \(\texttt{lem:martingale-centering}\), \(\{\xi_{Bb},\mathcal F_b:1\leq b\leq B\}\) is a martingale-difference array.  By the definition of \(\Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}\),
\[
 \mathcal V_B
 =t^\top\left(B^{-1}\sum_{b=1}^B\Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}\right)t.
\]
The separately imposed rerandomization covariance stabilization therefore gives
\[
 \mathcal V_B\longrightarrow_P v_t,
 \qquad
 v_t:=t^\top\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}t.                         \tag{1}
\]
The separately imposed condition \(\lambda_{\min}(\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}})>0\) implies \(v_t>0\).

The conditional fourth-moment bound in \(\texttt{lem:block-moments}\) gives a constant \(C_t<\infty\), independent of \(B\), \(G\), the block, and the realized history, such that
\[
 \sum_{b=1}^B\mathbb E(\xi_{Bb}^4\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})
 =B^{-2}\sum_{b=1}^B
   \mathbb E\{(t^\top R_b)^4\mid\mathcal F_{b-1}\}
 \leq \frac{C_t}{B}.                                      \tag{2}
\]
Conditional Cauchy--Schwarz and the same bound imply
\[
 t^\top\Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}t
 =\mathbb E\{(t^\top R_b)^2\mid\mathcal F_{b-1}\}
 \leq C_t^{1/2}.                                           \tag{3}
\]
Thus \(0\leq\mathcal V_B\leq C_t^{1/2}\) almost surely.  This deterministic bound makes \(\{\mathcal V_B\}\) uniformly integrable, so (1) yields
\[
 V_B\longrightarrow v_t,
 \qquad
 \frac{\mathcal V_B}{V_B}\longrightarrow_P1.             \tag{4}
\]
In particular, \(V_B>0\) for all sufficiently large \(B\).

We derive the Lindeberg condition rather than assume it.  For every \(\epsilon>0\), the elementary inequality \(z^2\mathbf1\{|z|\geq a\}\leq z^4/a^2\), with \(a=\epsilon V_B^{-1/2}\), gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &V_B^{-1}\sum_{b=1}^B
  \mathbb E\left[\xi_{Bb}^2
   \mathbf1\{|\xi_{Bb}|\geq\epsilon V_B^{-1/2}\}\right]\\
 &\qquad\leq
 \epsilon^{-2}\sum_{b=1}^B\mathbb E(\xi_{Bb}^4)
 \leq \frac{C_t}{\epsilon^2B}\longrightarrow0,            \tag{5}
 \end{aligned}
\]
where the last inequality follows by taking expectations in (2).  Equations (4)--(5) verify the normalization and Lindeberg premises of \(\texttt{lem:li-zhao-martingale-clt}\).  Hence
\[
 V_B^{-1/2}t^\top B^{-1/2}\sum_{b=1}^BR_b\Rightarrow N(0,1).
\]
Combining this conclusion with \(V_B\to v_t\) gives
\[
 t^\top B^{-1/2}\sum_{b=1}^BR_b
 \Rightarrow N(0,t^\top\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}t).             \tag{6}
\]
Since (6) holds for every \(t\in\mathbb R^2\), the Cram\'er--Wold characterization gives
\[
 B^{-1/2}\sum_{b=1}^BR_b\Rightarrow N_2(0,\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}).
\]
Finally, \(\texttt{lem:martingale-centering}\) gives the exact identity
\[
 \sqrt{GB}(\widehat\tau-\tau)=B^{-1/2}\sum_{b=1}^BR_b.
\]
The constants in (2)--(3) are uniform in \(G\), and the only diverging denominator used in (2) and (5) is \(B\).  Therefore no relative-growth condition on \(G/B\) has entered the argument.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The result separates primitive structural membership from the rerandomization covariance stabilization and positive-definiteness conditions needed for arbitrary-group many-block inference. \textit{Gap.} Yu--Ma--Liu studies a fixed minimax schedule. Li--Zhao's adaptive-design theorems encompass sequential rerandomization through logged adaptive probabilities, but do not provide the design-specific premise needed here: a conditional covariance and uniform Lindeberg verification for the joint score generated by a fixed-half saturation draw, fixed-count within-group assignments, and sustained lag-one endpoints. This theorem supplies that two-stage score construction before applying the general martingale template. \textit{Consumer.} Prospective multigroup experiments obtain joint direct-and-spillover inference from many blocks without requiring group growth.

\begin{theorem}[thm:projection-covariance]\label{thm:projection-covariance}
As \(G\to\infty\), uniformly over blocks and histories and without any restriction on \(G/B\), \(\Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}=\Sigma_{b,0}-(1-v_{\eta,\kappa})\beta_b\beta_b^\top+o(1)\). Along every sequence with \(G\to\infty\) on which the complete-randomization, soft-rerandomization, and projection covariance stabilizations hold,
\[
\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}=\Omega_{\mathrm{CR}}-(1-v_{\eta,\kappa})P.
\]
For every fixed contrast \(c\in\mathbb R^2\setminus\{0\}\), soft rerandomization has strictly smaller asymptotic variance than complete randomization if and only if \(c^\top Pc>0\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a block \(b\) and condition throughout on \(\mathcal F_{b-1}\). Under \(P_0\), the map \(x\mapsto-x\) preserves both \(\mathcal X_G\) and its uniform law. Since the definition of \(A_b\) gives \(A_b(-x)=-A_b(x)\), it follows that
\[
\mathbb E_0(A_b\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})=0.                                      \tag{1}
\]
By \(\texttt{lem:score-decomposition}\),
\(\mathbb E(U_b\mid x_b,\mathcal F_{b-1})=0\). Iterated conditional expectation and the fact that \(W_b\) is measurable with respect to \((x_b,\mathcal F_{b-1})\) therefore give
\[
\mathbb E_0(R_b\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})=0,
\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}_0(U_b,W_b\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})=0.                         \tag{2}
\]
The balanced-sign covariance identity, the unchanged second-stage covariance asserted by \(\texttt{lem:score-decomposition}\), the definition of \(\beta_b\), and the exact standardization in the definition of \(W_b\) now yield
\[
\operatorname{Var}_0(R_b\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})=\Sigma_{b,0},
\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}_0(R_b,W_b\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})=\beta_b,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_0(W_b\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})=1.                            \tag{3}
\]
The division defining \(W_b\) is legitimate because \(\texttt{ass:imbalance-variance-window}\) gives \(\sigma_b^2\geq c_\sigma>0\).

Let \((R_b^\star,W_b^\star)\) be the centered covariance-matched Gaussian pair. Equation (3) implies \(W_b^\star\sim N(0,1)\). Define
\[
E_b^\star=R_b^\star-\beta_bW_b^\star.                                      \tag{4}
\]
Direct covariance algebra using (3) gives
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(E_b^\star,W_b^\star)=0,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(E_b^\star)=\Sigma_{b,0}-\beta_b\beta_b^\top.            \tag{5}
\]
The vector in (5) is jointly Gaussian, including when its covariance is singular. Its joint characteristic function factors because the cross-covariance is zero; hence \(E_b^\star\) and \(W_b^\star\) are independent. This argument only uses the scalar identity \(\operatorname{Var}(W_b^\star)=1\), so no inverse of the possibly singular score covariance is taken.

For \(Z\sim N(0,1)\), completing the square in the Gaussian density gives
\[
\mathbb E e^{-\kappa Z^2}=(1+2\kappa)^{-1/2}.                               \tag{6}
\]
Differentiation under the integral is justified by the integrable envelope \(z^2e^{-z^2/2}\), and differentiating (6) with respect to \(\kappa\) gives
\[
\mathbb E\{Z^2e^{-\kappa Z^2}\}=(1+2\kappa)^{-3/2}.                        \tag{7}
\]
Consequently,
\[
c_\star:=\mathbb E f_{\eta,\kappa}(Z)
=\eta+(1-\eta)(1+2\kappa)^{-1/2}\geq\eta>0,                               \tag{8}
\]
and \(\texttt{def:projection-gain}\), (7), and (8) imply
\[
\frac{\mathbb E\{Z^2f_{\eta,\kappa}(Z)\}}{c_\star}
=v_{\eta,\kappa}.                                                          \tag{9}
\]
Substituting \(R_b^\star=\beta_bW_b^\star+E_b^\star\), using the independence and centering established after (5), and then applying (9), we obtain
\[
\frac{\mathbb E\{R_b^\star R_b^{\star\top}f_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b^\star)\}}
     {\mathbb E f_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b^\star)}
=\Sigma_{b,0}-(1-v_{\eta,\kappa})\beta_b\beta_b^\top.                    \tag{10}
\]

We next identify the covariance under the actual soft design. Complementation changes \((A_b,W_b)\) to \((-A_b,-W_b)\), whereas \(f_{\eta,\kappa}\) is even. Thus the Radon--Nikodym formula in \(\texttt{def:soft-two-stage-design}\) and pairing of \(x\) with \(-x\) give
\[
\mathbb E_{\mathrm{RR}}(A_b\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})=0.                       \tag{11}
\]
Conditional centering of \(U_b\) in \(\texttt{lem:score-decomposition}\), followed by iterated expectation, gives \(\mathbb E_{\mathrm{RR}}(R_b\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})=0\). Therefore
\[
\Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}
=\frac{\mathbb E_0\{R_bR_b^\top f_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b)
                         \mid\mathcal F_{b-1}\}}
        {\mathbb E_0\{f_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b)
                         \mid\mathcal F_{b-1}\}}.                           \tag{12}
\]
Both the denominator in (12) and its Gaussian counterpart in (10) are at least \(\eta\). Moreover, covariance matching, Cauchy--Schwarz, and \(\texttt{lem:block-moments}\) give
\[
\left\|\mathbb E\{R_b^\star R_b^{\star\top}
                         f_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b^\star)\}\right\|
\leq\mathbb E\|R_b^\star\|^2
=\mathbb E_0\|R_b\|^2
\leq\{\mathbb E_0\|R_b\|^4\}^{1/2}
\leq C_R^{1/2}.                                                            \tag{13}
\]
Let \(N,D\) denote the numerator and denominator in (12), and let \(N^\star,D^\star\) denote their Gaussian counterparts in (10). Since \(D,D^\star\geq\eta\), elementary ratio algebra and (13) imply
\[
\left\|\frac ND-\frac{N^\star}{D^\star}\right\|
\leq\frac{\|N-N^\star\|}{\eta}
    +\frac{C_R^{1/2}|D-D^\star|}{\eta^2}.                                \tag{14}
\]
By \(\texttt{thm:weighted-gaussian-transport}\), the two discrepancies on the right-hand side of (14) are bounded uniformly over blocks, histories, and members of \(\mathcal M_{G,B}\) by a deterministic sequence tending to zero. Combining (10), (12), and (14) proves
\[
\Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}
=\Sigma_{b,0}-(1-v_{\eta,\kappa})\beta_b\beta_b^\top+r_{Gb},
\qquad
\sup_{b,\mathcal F_{b-1}}\|r_{Gb}\|\longrightarrow0                       \tag{15}
\]
as \(G\to\infty\). The blockwise argument does not involve \(B\), so (15) imposes no condition on \(G/B\).

Now consider any sequence with \(G\to\infty\) on which \(\texttt{ass:cr-covariance-stabilization}\), \(\texttt{ass:rr-covariance-stabilization}\), and \(\texttt{ass:projection-stabilization}\) hold. Averaging (15) over \(b\) yields
\[
\left\|\frac1B\sum_b\Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}
-\frac1B\sum_b\Sigma_{b,0}
+(1-v_{\eta,\kappa})\frac1B\sum_b\beta_b\beta_b^\top\right\|
\leq\sup_{b,\mathcal F_{b-1}}\|r_{Gb}\|\longrightarrow0.                 \tag{16}
\]
The three stated stabilizations and uniqueness of limits in probability applied to (16) imply
\[
\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}
=\Omega_{\mathrm{CR}}-(1-v_{\eta,\kappa})P.                               \tag{17}
\]
No positive-definiteness condition is used.

Finally, \(P\succeq0\). Indeed, for every fixed \(c\in\mathbb R^2\), each random variable
\[
\frac1B\sum_b(c^\top\beta_b)^2
\]
is nonnegative and, by \(\texttt{ass:projection-stabilization}\), converges in probability to the deterministic number \(c^\top Pc\); hence \(c^\top Pc\geq0\). Because \(\kappa>0\) and \(0<\eta<1\),
\[
(1+2\kappa)^{-3/2}<(1+2\kappa)^{-1/2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
0<v_{\eta,\kappa}<1.                                                       \tag{18}
\]
For each fixed \(c\neq0\), equation (17) therefore gives
\[
c^\top\Omega_{\mathrm{CR}}c-c^\top\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}c
=(1-v_{\eta,\kappa})c^\top Pc.                                           \tag{19}
\]
The first factor on the right-hand side of (19) is strictly positive and the second is nonnegative. Thus the limiting variance is strictly smaller under soft rerandomization exactly for the contrasts satisfying \(c^\top Pc>0\), which proves all three assertions.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The growing-group identity uses exactly complete-randomization, rerandomization, and projection stabilization, with no positive-definiteness requirement. \textit{Gap.} Static hard-rerandomization geometry does not cover predictable soft tilting of a joint direct-and-spillover score. \textit{Consumer.} The criterion classifies every scientifically chosen contrast that gains asymptotic precision.

\begin{theorem}[thm:studentizer]\label{thm:studentizer}
For every member of \(\mathcal M_{G,B}\) operated under the predictable soft two-stage design and every block,
\[
\mathbb E(\widehat\Gamma_b\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})=\Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}+D_b,
\qquad D_b\succeq0.
\]
Along every sequence in \(\mathcal M_{G,B}\) with \(B\to\infty\) and arbitrary admissible even \(G\), including fixed \(G\), suppose separately that
\[
B^{-1}\sum_{b\in\mathcal B_B}\Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}\to_P\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}.
\]
Then
\[
\widehat\Gamma=\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}+B^{-1}\sum_{b\in\mathcal B_B}D_b+o_P(1).
\]
Both \(\widehat\Gamma_b\) and \(D_b\) are uniformly bounded in Frobenius norm, and every probability limit point of \(\widehat\Gamma\) dominates \(\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}\) in Loewner order.  If, in addition, \(\lambda_{\min}(\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}})>0\), then clipping is inactive with probability tending to one and \(\|\widehat\Gamma_+^{-1}\|=O_P(1)\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a member of \(\mathcal M_{G,B}\), a block \(b\), and condition throughout on \(\mathcal F_{b-1}\).  By design-based finiteness and the lag-one structural conditions contained in the class, each \(u_{k,b}\) is fixed under this conditioning.  Abbreviate the logged probabilities by \(\pi_k:=\pi_{k,b}\) and \(\pi_{k\ell}:=\pi_{k\ell,b}\).  The definitions of the causal block target and the Horvitz--Thompson block estimator give
\[
 R_b=\frac1{\sqrt G\,n}\sum_{k\in\mathcal K_b}
       \left(\frac{I_k}{\pi_k}-1\right)u_{k,b}.             \tag{7}
\]
All divisions in (7) are valid.  Indeed, complementation maps \(\mathcal X_G\) to itself, preserves \(P_0\), sends \(W_b(x)\) to \(-W_b(x)\), and leaves the even function \(f_{\eta,\kappa}\{W_b(x)\}\) unchanged.  Pairing every balanced assignment with its complement therefore gives
\[
 \Pr_{\mathrm{RR}}(Q_{gb}=q_H\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})
 =\Pr_{\mathrm{RR}}(Q_{gb}=q_L\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})=\frac12.
\]
Since \(p_{dq}\geq1/4\), the fixed-count second stage gives \(\pi_k=p_{dq}/2\geq1/8\).  Taking the conditional covariance of (7) and using \(\mathbb E(I_kI_\ell\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})=\pi_{k\ell}\) yields
\[
 \Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}
 =\frac1{Gn^2}\sum_{k,\ell\in\mathcal K_b}
   \frac{\pi_{k\ell}-\pi_k\pi_\ell}{\pi_k\pi_\ell}
   u_{k,b}u_{\ell,b}^\top.                                 \tag{8}
\]

If \(\pi_{k\ell}>0\), conditional expectation of the corresponding summand in \(\widehat\Gamma_b\) replaces \(I_kI_\ell\) by \(\pi_{k\ell}\), exactly recovering its ordered-pair term in (8).  If \(k\ne\ell\) and \(\pi_{k\ell}=0\), the two ordered terms indexed by \((k,\ell)\) and \((\ell,k)\) in (8) are
\[
 -u_{k,b}u_{\ell,b}^\top-u_{\ell,b}u_{k,b}^\top.
\]
For the corresponding unordered pair, the completion in \(\widehat\Gamma_b\) has conditional expectation \(u_{k,b}u_{k,b}^\top+u_{\ell,b}u_{\ell,b}^\top\).  Its excess over the two missing covariance terms is
\[
 (u_{k,b}+u_{\ell,b})(u_{k,b}+u_{\ell,b})^\top\succeq0.   \tag{9}
\]
Summing (9) over \(\mathcal Z_b\), with the normalization in \(\texttt{def:completed-studentizer}\), proves
\[
 \mathbb E(\widehat\Gamma_b\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})
 =\Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}+D_b,
 \qquad D_b\succeq0.                                      \tag{10}
\]
Every term of the completion is observable because an occurrence of \(u_{k,b}\) is multiplied by \(I_k\), and every probability is logged; no unobserved cross-potential-outcome product is used.

We next establish uniform boundedness.  The outcome envelope and fixed contrast coefficients give \(\sup_{k,b}\|u_{k,b}\|<\infty\).  By \(\texttt{lem:pair-probability-control}\), for all sufficiently large \(G\), feasible cross-group pair probabilities are bounded away from zero and their covariance coefficients are \(O(G^{-1})\), while feasible within-group pair probabilities are bounded away from zero.  The first claim also follows directly from the soft likelihood-ratio lower bound \(f_{\eta,\kappa}/C_b\geq\eta\) and the complete-randomization pair probabilities.  For the fixed-count second stage, every feasible same-group pair probability is uniformly positive over \(n\in4\mathbb N\): the relevant sampling factors are first-order probabilities or one of
\[
 \frac{nq(nq-1)}{n(n-1)},\qquad
 \frac{n(1-q)\{n(1-q)-1\}}{n(n-1)},\qquad
 \frac{nq\,n(1-q)}{n(n-1)},
\]
and a factor that vanishes corresponds precisely to an impossible pair handled by the completion.  There are \(O(G^2n^2)\) ordered cross-group pairs, each carrying, after the outer normalization, a coefficient of order \(O(G^{-2}n^{-2})\), and \(O(Gn^2)\) within-group pairs, each carrying a coefficient of order \(O(G^{-1}n^{-2})\).  For \(G\geq4\), impossible pairs are within-group and have the same bounded count-normalization product.  The remaining admissible value \(G=2\) has only \(O(n^2)\) additional impossible cross-group pairs, still offset by the factor \((Gn^2)^{-1}\).  Consequently a constant \(C_\Gamma<\infty\), independent of \(B\), \(G\), \(n\), the block, and the history, satisfies
\[
 \|\widehat\Gamma_b\|_F+\|D_b\|_F\leq C_\Gamma.           \tag{11}
\]
Because the soft weight is strictly positive on all of \(\mathcal X_G\), the zero-pair set is determined by the support of balanced first-stage assignment and fixed-count second-stage assignment, not by the numerical history-dependent tilt.  Together with the fixed potential outcomes, this makes \(D_b\) deterministic for each \(b\).

Put
\[
 M_b=\widehat\Gamma_b-
     \mathbb E(\widehat\Gamma_b\mid\mathcal F_{b-1}).
\]
Then \(\{M_b,\mathcal F_b\}\) is an entrywise matrix martingale-difference sequence.  Conditional orthogonality across distinct blocks and (11) give
\[
 \mathbb E\left\|B^{-1}\sum_{b=1}^BM_b\right\|_F^2
 =B^{-2}\sum_{b=1}^B\mathbb E\|M_b\|_F^2
 \leq\frac{4C_\Gamma^2}{B}\longrightarrow0.                \tag{12}
\]
Thus \(B^{-1}\sum_bM_b=o_P(1)\).  Averaging (10), applying (12), and using the separately imposed rerandomization covariance stabilization proves
\[
 \widehat\Gamma
 =\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}+B^{-1}\sum_{b=1}^BD_b+o_P(1).       \tag{13}
\]
Only \(B\to\infty\) has been used.

The deterministic positive-semidefinite averages \(\bar D_B=B^{-1}\sum_bD_b\) are bounded by (11).  Every subsequence therefore has a further subsequence on which \(\bar D_B\to D_\infty\succeq0\).  Along that further subsequence, (13) gives
\[
 \widehat\Gamma\longrightarrow_P
 \Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}+D_\infty\succeq\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}. \tag{14}
\]
This proves the asserted Loewner domination of every probability limit point.  If the additional condition \(\lambda_{\min}(\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}})>0\) holds, Weyl's eigenvalue inequality, (13), and \(\bar D_B\succeq0\) imply
\[
 \lambda_{\min}(\widehat\Gamma)
 \geq\lambda_{\min}(\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}})+o_P(1).          \tag{15}
\]
Since \(r_B\downarrow0\), (15) shows that clipping is inactive with probability tending to one and \(\|\widehat\Gamma_+^{-1}\|=O_P(1)\).  This proves the finite-block identity, the expansion under covariance stabilization, and the additional clipping conclusion under positive definiteness, with their scopes separated as stated.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The finite-block completion identity needs no covariance limit, its many-block expansion needs rerandomization covariance stabilization, and clipping additionally needs positive definiteness. \textit{Gap.} Jiang--Imai--Malani treats static two-stage designs; this matrix result handles logged history-dependent pair probabilities and many-block concentration. \textit{Consumer.} The completed matrix supports simultaneous inference even when some exposure pairs are structurally impossible.

\begin{theorem}[thm:wald-coverage]\label{thm:wald-coverage}
Along every sequence in \(\mathcal M_{G,B}\) with \(B\to\infty\) and arbitrary admissible even \(G\), including fixed \(G\), suppose separately that
\[
B^{-1}\sum_{b\in\mathcal B_B}\Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}\to_P\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\lambda_{\min}(\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}})>0.
\]
Then the observable ellipse satisfies
\[
\liminf_{B\to\infty}\Pr_{\mathrm{RR}}\{\tau\in\mathcal C_\alpha\}\geq1-\alpha.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a sequence covered by the proposed statement and put
\[
 X_B=\sqrt{GB}(\widehat\tau-\tau),
 \qquad
 \bar D_B=B^{-1}\sum_{b=1}^BD_b.
\]
The separately imposed rerandomization covariance stabilization and positive-definiteness conditions are exactly those required by \(\texttt{thm:joint-design-clt}\) and by the clipping part of \(\texttt{thm:studentizer}\).  Therefore
\[
 X_B\Rightarrow N_2(0,\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}),
 \qquad
 \widehat\Gamma_+
 =\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}+\bar D_B+o_P(1).                    \tag{16}
\]

Fix an arbitrary subsequence.  The uniform bound on \(D_b\) in \(\texttt{thm:studentizer}\) makes the deterministic sequence \(\{\bar D_B\}\) bounded in the finite-dimensional space of symmetric \(2\)-by-\(2\) matrices.  It has a further subsequence such that
\[
 \bar D_B\longrightarrow D_\infty
 \quad\text{for some }D_\infty\succeq0.                  \tag{17}
\]
Along this further subsequence, (16)--(17) imply the joint convergence
\[
 (X_B,\widehat\Gamma_+)\Rightarrow(X,\Gamma_\infty),
 \qquad
 X\sim N_2(0,\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}),
 \quad
 \Gamma_\infty:=\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}+D_\infty.           \tag{18}
\]
No independence assertion is needed in (18), because the second coordinate converges in probability to a deterministic matrix.  The positive-definiteness condition and \(D_\infty\succeq0\) imply that both \(\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}\) and \(\Gamma_\infty\) are positive definite.  Continuity of matrix inversion and of the quadratic-form map at (18) therefore gives
\[
 X_B^\top\widehat\Gamma_+^{-1}X_B
 \Rightarrow X^\top\Gamma_\infty^{-1}X.                  \tag{19}
\]

Write \(X\stackrel d=\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}^{1/2}Z\), where \(Z\sim N_2(0,I_2)\).  Since \(\Gamma_\infty\succeq\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}\succ0\), inversion reverses Loewner order, so
\[
 0\prec
 H:=\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}^{1/2}
       \Gamma_\infty^{-1}
       \Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}^{1/2}
 \preceq I_2.                                               \tag{20}
\]
Diagonalize \(H=O\operatorname{diag}(h_1,h_2)O^\top\), with \(0<h_j\leq1\).  Rotational invariance of \(Z\), coupled using the same two standard-normal coordinates, gives
\[
 X^\top\Gamma_\infty^{-1}X
 \stackrel d=h_1Z_1^2+h_2Z_2^2
 \leq Z_1^2+Z_2^2
 \quad\text{almost surely}.                                \tag{21}
\]
The variable on the right has the \(\chi^2_2\) law, and the variable on the left is a positive weighted sum of two independent squared standard normals, hence has no atom at the positive quantile \(\chi^2_{2,1-\alpha}\).  Applying (19)--(21) at this continuity point yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \liminf_B\Pr_{\mathrm{RR}}\!\left\{
  X_B^\top\widehat\Gamma_+^{-1}X_B
  \leq\chi^2_{2,1-\alpha}\right\}
 &\geq
 \Pr\{Z_1^2+Z_2^2\leq\chi^2_{2,1-\alpha}\}\\
 &=1-\alpha.                                                \tag{22}
 \end{aligned}
\]
By \(\texttt{def:simultaneous-wald-region}\), the event in (22) is exactly \(\{\tau\in\mathcal C_\alpha\}\).  If the asserted liminf failed on the original sequence, a violating subsequence would have the further subsequence just analyzed, contradicting (22).  This proves the result for every \(B\to\infty\) sequence in the structural class satisfying the two separately stated rerandomization covariance conditions, with no restriction on \(G/B\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The observable joint region inherits validity from the arbitrary-group martingale limit and the conservative completed studentizer under their two explicit covariance conditions. \textit{Gap.} Yu--Ma--Liu's prospective market design does not provide this history-dependent completed joint region. Liu2026CovariateAdaptive gives asymptotic inference for static two-stage covariate-adaptive randomization under partial interference, whereas this theorem uses a predictably outcome-history-dependent fixed-half soft kernel over repeated blocks. HeTchetgen2026 supplies broad exposure-mapping-agnostic two-stage estimators, central limit theory, and conservative variance procedures, but not this logged-pair-probability PSD completion coupled to a simultaneous Wald ellipse for the sustained lag-one joint target. \textit{Consumer.} Market experimenters obtain one simultaneous region for sustained direct and saturation-spillover effects.

\begin{proposition}[prop:complete-randomization-reduction]\label{prop:complete-randomization-reduction}
On the boundary extension \(\eta=1\), or equivalently when \(\kappa=0\), \(f_{\eta,\kappa}\equiv1\), \(P_{\mathrm{RR},b}=P_0\), \(v_{\eta,\kappa}=1\), and \(\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}=\Omega_{\mathrm{CR}}\); the estimator and studentizer reduce to the ordinary balanced two-stage Horvitz--Thompson construction.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix a block \(b\) and condition throughout on \(\mathcal F_{b-1}\).  The potential outcomes are fixed, and the only probabilities below are induced by the two stages of the design in \(\mathrm{def:soft\mbox{-}two\mbox{-}stage\mbox{-}design}\).

First consider either of the two boundary cases.  If \(\eta=1\), then for every \(w\in\mathbb R\),
\[
 f_{\eta,\kappa}(w)=1+(1-1)e^{-\kappa w^2}=1.
\]
If \(\kappa=0\), then, using \(0<\eta\leq1\),
\[
 f_{\eta,0}(w)=\eta+(1-\eta)e^0=1.
\]
Thus the two parameter restrictions are equivalent as restrictions on the resulting randomization kernel, although they are not assertions that the parameter values themselves are equal.  In either case, the definition of \(C_b\) gives
\[
 C_b=\mathbb E_0[1\mid\mathcal F_{b-1}]=1,
\]
and consequently, for every \(x\in\mathcal X_G\),
\[
 P_{\mathrm{RR},b}(x\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})
 =P_0(x)\frac{1}{1}=P_0(x).
\]
The division is valid because \(C_b=1>0\).  Since the conditional second-stage law given \(x_b\) in \(\mathrm{def:soft\mbox{-}two\mbox{-}stage\mbox{-}design}\) does not involve \(\eta\) or \(\kappa\), equality of the first-stage laws implies equality of the complete conditional laws of all saturation and treatment indicators under soft rerandomization and balanced complete randomization.

For completeness, this equality also yields the ordinary first-order Horvitz--Thompson weights explicitly.  Because \(G\) is even, the map \(x\mapsto-x\) is a bijection of \(\mathcal X_G\), preserves \(P_0\), and interchanges \(x_g=1\) with \(x_g=-1\).  Hence
\[
 \Pr_0(Q_{gb}=q_H)=\Pr_0(Q_{gb}=q_L)=\frac12.
\]
Conditional on \(Q_{gb}=q\), uniform sampling of exactly \(nq\) units gives
\[
 \Pr(A_{gbi}=d\mid Q_{gb}=q,\mathcal F_{b-1})=p_{dq}.
\]
It follows that
\[
 \pi_{gbi,dq}=\frac12p_{dq}>0,
\]
where positivity follows from \(q_L=1/4\) and \(q_H=3/4\).  Thus the estimator in \(\mathrm{def:joint\mbox{-}ht\mbox{-}estimator}\) is pointwise the balanced two-stage Horvitz--Thompson estimator, with cell weight \(2/p_{dq}\).  Equality of the complete assignment laws also makes every logged pair probability \(\pi_{k\ell,b}\) equal to its complete-randomization value.

We next verify directly that the covariance benchmark also coincides.  Under the just-derived inclusion probabilities, the definitions of \(\widehat\tau_b\) and \(\widehat m_{gbq}\) give
\[
 \widehat\tau_b=\frac2G\sum_{g\in\mathcal G_G}\widehat m_{gb,Q_{gb}}.
\]
The definitions of \(\tau_b\), \(m_{gbq}\), \(e_{gbq}\), and \(a_{gb}\) therefore imply the exact decomposition
\[
 R_b
 =\frac1{\sqrt G}\sum_{g\in\mathcal G_G}x_{gb}a_{gb}
  +\frac2{\sqrt G}\sum_{g\in\mathcal G_G}e_{gb,Q_{gb}}.
\tag{1}
\]
Indeed, the deterministic part of the difference is
\[
 \frac2G\sum_g\left\{\frac{1+x_{gb}}2m_{gbq_H}
 +\frac{1-x_{gb}}2m_{gbq_L}\right\}
 -\frac1G\sum_g(m_{gbq_H}+m_{gbq_L})
 =\frac1G\sum_gx_{gb}a_{gb}.
\]

For each \(q\) and \(d\), the marginal inclusion probability in the uniform fixed-size within-group sample is \(p_{dq}\).  Hence finite-population Horvitz--Thompson algebra gives
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[
  \frac1n\sum_i\frac{\mathbf1\{A_{gbi}=d\}Y_{gbi}(d,q)}{p_{dq}}
  \mathrel{\Big|}Q_{gb}=q,\mathcal F_{b-1}\right]
 =\frac1n\sum_iY_{gbi}(d,q),
\]
so \(\mathbb E(e_{gbq}\mid Q_{gb}=q,\mathcal F_{b-1})=0\).  The second-stage samples are conditionally independent across groups.  Therefore the two terms in (1) have zero cross-covariance by iterated expectation, and the conditional covariance of the second term, first given \(x_b\) and then averaged over \(P_0\), is
\[
 \mathbb E_0\!\left[
  \frac4G\sum_gT_{gb,Q_{gb}}\mathrel{\Big|}\mathcal F_{b-1}\right]
 =\frac2G\sum_g(T_{gbq_L}+T_{gbq_H})=K_b.
\tag{2}
\]

Complement symmetry gives \(\mathbb E_0x_{gb}=0\).  Exchangeability under the uniform balanced-sign law and the identity \(\sum_gx_{gb}=0\) give, for \(g\neq h\),
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}_0(x_{gb},x_{hb})=-\frac1{G-1},
\]
because
\[
 0=\operatorname{Var}_0\!\left(\sum_gx_{gb}\right)
 =G+G(G-1)\operatorname{Cov}_0(x_{1b},x_{2b}).
\]
Writing \(\bar a_b=G^{-1}\sum_ga_{gb}\), the covariance of the first term in (1) is consequently
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Var}_0\!\left(G^{-1/2}\sum_gx_{gb}a_{gb}
 \mathrel{\Big|}\mathcal F_{b-1}\right)
 &=\frac1G\left\{\sum_ga_{gb}a_{gb}^{\top}
 -\frac1{G-1}\sum_{g\neq h}a_{gb}a_{hb}^{\top}\right\}\\
 &=\frac1{G-1}\sum_g(a_{gb}-\bar a_b)(a_{gb}-\bar a_b)^{\top}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
Combining (1)--(3) yields
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_0(R_b\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})
 =\frac1{G-1}\sum_g(a_{gb}-\bar a_b)(a_{gb}-\bar a_b)^{\top}+K_b
 =\Sigma_{b,0}.
\]
Since the conditional assignment law under \(P_{\mathrm{RR},b}\) is exactly the same law,
\[
 \Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}
 =\operatorname{Var}_{\mathrm{RR}}(R_b\mid\mathcal F_{b-1})
 =\Sigma_{b,0}
\]
for every block and every finite \(G,B,n\).  Hence the two block-average covariance sequences are termwise identical, and their deterministic limits satisfy
\[
 \Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}=\Omega_{\mathrm{CR}}.
\]

The displayed formula in \(\mathrm{def:projection\mbox{-}gain}\) has a strictly positive denominator in both boundary cases.  Direct substitution gives
\[
 v_{1,\kappa}=\frac{1}{1}=1,
 \qquad
 v_{\eta,0}
 =\frac{\eta+(1-\eta)}{\eta+(1-\eta)}=1,
\]
so the projection gain \((1-v_{\eta,\kappa})P\) is the zero matrix, consistently with the covariance equality.

Finally, \(\widehat\Gamma_b\) in \(\mathrm{def:completed\mbox{-}studentizer}\) is an explicit function only of the realized indicators, observed cell outcomes, and logged first- and second-order inclusion probabilities.  These indicators and inclusion probabilities have exactly their balanced complete-randomization values at either boundary.  Substitution into that displayed formula therefore gives pointwise the ordinary balanced two-stage completed Horvitz--Thompson covariance estimator; averaging over blocks and clipping at the unchanged threshold \(r_B\) preserve this equality.  Thus both \(\widehat\tau\) and \(\widehat\Gamma\) reduce exactly to their ordinary balanced two-stage complete-randomization constructions, which proves the proposition.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The reduction checks that the new design nests its complete-randomization benchmark without altering the causal target. \textit{Gap.} This is a required sanity reduction rather than a novelty claim. \textit{Consumer.} Implementers can recover the familiar design by switching off the soft balance penalty.

\begin{proposition}[prop:adaptive-full-rank-witness]\label{prop:adaptive-full-rank-witness}
The family \(\mathcal W^{AB}\) belongs to \(\mathcal M_{G,B}\), its type A and type B imbalance variances converge to \(53/16\) and \(29/16\), and \[P=\frac12\left\{\frac1{53}\begin{pmatrix}36&6\\6&1\end{pmatrix}+\frac1{29}\begin{pmatrix}4&10\\10&25\end{pmatrix}\right\}\succ0.\] Thus the design is genuinely adaptive through prior observed outcomes and every nonzero joint contrast is strictly improved.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Give each pair \((H_g,J_g)\in\{-1,1\}^2\) exactly \(G/4\) groups, as prescribed by \(\texttt{def:adaptive-witness}\).  We first check all primitive structural conditions directly.  Put
\[
y_{gb}(d,q)=h_g+d(\delta_g+\theta_gq).
\]
A full fixed potential-outcome schedule realizing these endpoints is
\[
Y_{gti}(h)=y_{g,\lceil t/2\rceil}(d_t,q_t),
\]
where \((d_t,q_t)\) is unit \(i\)'s own-treatment and group-saturation exposure at period \(t\) in history \(h\).  This schedule does not depend on assignments outside group \(g\), depends on the within-group contemporaneous vector only through \((d_t,q_t)\), uses no future assignment, and ignores all periods other than \(t\).  It therefore satisfies partial interference, stratified interference, nonanticipation, and the lag-one finite-memory restriction.  When both periods of block \(b\) have exposure \((d,q)\), the endpoint is exactly \(Y_{gbi}(d,q)=y_{gb}(d,q)\).

For type A blocks, direct enumeration of \(d\in\{0,1\}\), \(q\in\{1/4,3/4\}\), and \((a_{Dg},a_{Sg})\in\{-1,1\}^2\) gives \(|y_{gb}(d,q)|\leq5/2\).  For type B blocks the same finite enumeration gives \(|y_{gb}(d,q)|\leq9/2\).  Thus the outcome envelope can be instantiated as \(M=9/2\).  Also \(|H_g|=1\) and \(\sum_gH_g=0\), so \(K_H=1\) is valid.  The schedule is fixed and the only randomness is generated by the two assignment stages, which verifies design-based finiteness.

The calculation (1) in \(\texttt{lem:adaptive-witness-pre-membership-transport}\) gives
\[
a_{gb}=(a_{Dg},a_{Sg})^\top.                              \tag{16}
\]
That lemma also establishes \(e_{gbq}=0\), \(T_{gbq}=0\), \(K_b=0\), the observed-mean identity
\[
\bar Y^{\mathrm{obs}}_{gb}
=\frac{a_{Sg}}2+\frac{a_{Dg}}4+\frac L4x_{gb},            \tag{17}
\]
and, after the initialization block, the predictable vectors
\[
V_{gb}^{A}=\frac32H_g+\frac14J_g+x_{g,b-1},
\qquad
V_{gb}^{B}=\frac54H_g+\frac12J_g.                        \tag{18}
\]

The dependence on prior outcomes is genuine.  The two previous assignments \(x_{b-1}=H\) and \(x_{b-1}=-H\) are balanced and have positive conditional probability because \(f_{\eta,\kappa}\geq\eta>0\).  Put \(K_g=H_gJ_g\); equal representation makes both \(J\) and \(K\) balanced sign vectors.  In the two histories, the A-block vectors in (18) are \(5H/2+J/4\) and \(H/2+J/4\), respectively.  Orthogonality of the four equally represented types gives \(W_b(K)=0\) in both histories, whereas
\[
W_b(J)^2=\frac{G-1}{101}
\quad\hbox{and}\quad
W_b(J)^2=\frac{G-1}{5},                                   \tag{19}
\]
respectively.  Within either history, the ratio \(P_{\mathrm{RR},b}(J)/P_{\mathrm{RR},b}(K)\) is the corresponding ratio of soft weights, because the common normalizer and equal \(P_0\)-masses cancel.  Since \(0<\eta<1\), \(\kappa>0\), and \(f_{\eta,\kappa}\) is strictly decreasing as a function of its squared argument, (19) shows that this conditional assignment ratio changes with the realized prior outcome history.  Thus the design is genuinely adaptive.

We next establish the variance window and its limits.  On an A block write \(d_g=3H_g/2+J_g/4\).  Equal representation gives \(G^{-1}\sum_gd_g^2=37/16\), and \(G^{-1}\sum_gx_{g,b-1}^2=1\).  The reverse and ordinary triangle inequalities give
\[
\sqrt{\frac G{G-1}}\left(\frac{\sqrt{37}}4-1\right)
\leq\sigma_{A,b}
\leq\sqrt{\frac G{G-1}}\left(\frac{\sqrt{37}}4+1\right). \tag{20}
\]
For a B block and the initialization block, respectively,
\[
\sigma_{B,b}^2=\frac G{G-1}\frac{29}{16},
\qquad
\sigma_1^2=\frac G{G-1}.                                 \tag{21}
\]
The lower endpoint in (20) is positive.  Since \(G\geq4\), equations (20)--(21) supply constants \(0<c_\sigma<C_\sigma<\infty\) independent of every history and block.  Together with the preceding structural checks, this verifies membership of the witness arrays in \(\mathcal M_{G,B}\).

For a noninitial A block define
\[
z_{H,b}=G^{-1}\sum_gx_{g,b-1}H_g,
\qquad
z_{J,b}=G^{-1}\sum_gx_{g,b-1}J_g.
\]
The balanced-sign covariance identity and the centering and unit norms of \(H\) and \(J\) give
\[
\mathbb E_0z_{H,b}^2=\mathbb E_0z_{J,b}^2=\frac1{G-1}.    \tag{22}
\]
Conditionally on \(\mathcal F_{b-2}\), the likelihood ratio of the soft law for \(x_{b-1}\) relative to \(P_0\) is \(f_{\eta,\kappa}(W_{b-1})/C_{b-1}\leq1/\eta\), because \(f_{\eta,\kappa}\leq1\) and \(C_{b-1}\geq\eta\).  Hence
\[
\sup_b\mathbb E_{\mathrm{RR}}(z_{H,b}^2+z_{J,b}^2)
\leq\frac2{\eta(G-1)}.                                   \tag{23}
\]
Expanding (18) now gives
\[
\sigma_{A,b}^2
=\frac G{G-1}\left(\frac{53}{16}+3z_{H,b}+\frac12z_{J,b}\right)
\longrightarrow_P\frac{53}{16},                          \tag{24}
\]
uniformly over the A-block positions, by (23) and Chebyshev's inequality.  Equation (21) gives \(\sigma_{B,b}^2\to29/16\) deterministically.  The one initialization block has asymptotic frequency zero as \(B\to\infty\).

Using (16), equal representation, and (18) in the definition of \(\beta_b\) yields
\[
\beta_{A,b}
=\frac{G}{(G-1)\sigma_{A,b}}
  \begin{pmatrix}3/2+z_{H,b}\\[2pt]1/4+z_{J,b}\end{pmatrix}
\longrightarrow_P\frac{(6,1)^\top}{\sqrt{53}},
\qquad
\beta_{B,b}\longrightarrow\frac{(2,5)^\top}{\sqrt{29}}. \tag{25}
\]
The uniform positive lower bound in (20), the smoothness of the displayed map on that bounded-away-from-zero domain, and (23) imply
\[
\sup_{b\ {
m of\ type\ A}}
\mathbb E\left\|\beta_{A,b}-\frac{(6,1)^\top}{\sqrt{53}}\right\|
=O(G^{-1/2}).                                               \tag{26}
\]
Markov's inequality applied to the average of these norms proves convergence of the block average without requiring independence across blocks.  Since A and B blocks each have limiting frequency one half, (25)--(26) give
\[
P=\frac12\left\{
\frac1{53}\begin{pmatrix}36&6\\6&1\end{pmatrix}
+\frac1{29}\begin{pmatrix}4&10\\10&25\end{pmatrix}
\right\}.                                                  \tag{27}
\]
Both summands are positive semidefinite.  Their generating vectors \((6,1)^\top\) and \((2,5)^\top\) are linearly independent, and
\[
\det(P)=\frac{(6\cdot5-1\cdot2)^2}{4\cdot53\cdot29}
=\frac{196}{1537}>0.
\]
Thus \(P\succ0\).

It remains to certify that the stabilization premises needed for \(\texttt{thm:projection-covariance}\) hold for this family, rather than assuming them.  Under complete randomization, (16), equal representation, and \(K_b=0\) give
\[
\Sigma_{b,0}=\frac G{G-1}I_2,
\qquad
\frac1B\sum_{b=1}^B\Sigma_{b,0}\longrightarrow I_2.     \tag{28}
\]
Equation (27) and the preceding averaging argument prove projection stabilization.  The history-uniform expansion in \(\texttt{lem:adaptive-witness-pre-membership-transport}\), which was proved without a model-class or stabilization premise, then yields
\[
\frac1B\sum_{b=1}^B\Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}
\longrightarrow_P I_2-(1-v_{\eta,\kappa})P
=:\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}.                                  \tag{29}
\]
Indeed, the average of the lemma's uniform \(o(1)\) remainder is still \(o(1)\), while (27)--(28) control the two leading terms.  For every vector \(u\), \(uu^\top\preceq\|u\|^2I_2\); hence (27) gives
\[
P\preceq\frac12\left(\frac{37}{53}+1\right)I_2
=\frac{45}{53}I_2\prec I_2.                              \tag{30}
\]
Writing \(t=(1+2\kappa)^{-1/2}\in(0,1)\), the definition of the tilt constant becomes
\[
v_{\eta,\kappa}=\frac{\eta+(1-\eta)t^3}{\eta+(1-\eta)t},
\]
so \(0<v_{\eta,\kappa}<1\).  Equations (29)--(30) imply
\[
\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}}
\succeq\left\{1-(1-v_{\eta,\kappa})\frac{45}{53}\right\}I_2
\succ0.                                                    \tag{31}
\]
Thus complete-randomization covariance stabilization, projection stabilization, rerandomization covariance stabilization, and positive definiteness have all been derived for the witness.

All premises of \(\texttt{thm:projection-covariance}\) are now verified along the alternating sequence with \(G\to\infty\) and \(B\to\infty\).  That theorem and (27) imply that every \(c\in\mathbb R^2\setminus\{0\}\) satisfies \(c^\top Pc>0\), and hence has strictly smaller asymptotic variance under soft rerandomization than under complete randomization.  This proves every assertion of the proposition.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The witness first establishes structural-class membership and then derives the separate covariance stabilizations needed to invoke the growing-group projection result. \textit{Gap.} Static examples use a fixed projection direction; this construction alternates two outcome-adaptive directions while retaining one standardized tilt constant. \textit{Consumer.} Simulation designers obtain a falsifiable adaptive benchmark with positive gain for every nonzero joint contrast.

\begin{lemma}[lem:li-zhao-martingale-clt]\label{lem:li-zhao-martingale-clt}
Let \(\{\xi_{Tt},\mathcal F_{Tt}:1\leq t\leq T\}\) be a square-integrable martingale-difference array, let \(\mathcal V_T=\sum_{t=1}^T\mathbb E(\xi_{Tt}^2\mid\mathcal F_{T,t-1})\), and let \(V_T=\mathbb E(\mathcal V_T)\). If \(\mathcal V_T/V_T=1+o_P(1)\) and, for every \(\epsilon>0\), \(V_T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^T\mathbb E[\xi_{Tt}^2\mathbf1\{|\xi_{Tt}|\geq\epsilon V_T^{-1/2}\}]=o_P(1)\), then \(V_T^{-1/2}\sum_{t=1}^T\xi_{Tt}\Rightarrow N(0,1)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[thm:weighted-gaussian-transport]\label{thm:weighted-gaussian-transport}
There exists a deterministic sequence \(\varepsilon_G\downarrow0\) such that, uniformly over blocks, histories, and members of \(\mathcal M_{G,B}\),
\[
\left|\mathbb E_0f_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b)-\mathbb Ef_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b^\star)\right|
+\left\|\mathbb E_0[A_bA_b^\top f_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b)]-\mathbb E[A_b^\star A_b^{\star\top}f_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b^\star)]\right\|
\leq\varepsilon_G,
\]
and
\[
\left\|\mathbb E_0[R_bR_b^\top f_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b)]-\mathbb E[R_b^\star R_b^{\star\top}f_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b^\star)]\right\|
\leq\varepsilon_G.
\]
The comparison is on the exact covariance range and remains valid when the score covariance is singular; no positive lower bound on either score-coordinate variance is required.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix an even \(G\) and condition on an arbitrary realization of \(\mathcal F_{b-1}\).  Under \(\texttt{ass:design-based-finiteness}\), the first-stage finite population is then deterministic; throughout this proof, \(\mathbb E_0\) includes the benchmark first-stage draw and the prescribed conditional second-stage draws.  All norms below are Euclidean for vectors and Frobenius for matrices.  Constants may depend on the fixed quantities \(M,K_H,c_\sigma,\lambda,\eta,\kappa\), but not on \(G,B,b\), the history, or the member of \(\mathcal M_{G,B}\).

We first obtain uniform deterministic envelopes.  The definition of \(m_{gbq}\), the four fixed vectors \(c_{dq}\), and \(|Y_{gbi}(d,q)|\leq M\) imply \(\|a_{gb}\|\leq L_a\) for a finite constant \(L_a\).  Also, the definition of \(V_{gb}\), the centering of \(H_g\), and the definition of \(\bar Y_b^{\mathrm{obs}}\) give
\[
 \sum_{g=1}^G V_{gb}=0,
 \qquad
 |V_{gb}|\leq K_H+2\lambda M=:L_V.                                      \tag{1}
\]
By \(\texttt{ass:imbalance-variance-window}\), \(\sigma_b\geq\sqrt{c_\sigma}>0\).  Define
\[
 \bar a_b=G^{-1}\sum_{g=1}^G a_{gb},
 \qquad
 y_g=\begin{pmatrix}a_{gb}-\bar a_b\\ V_{gb}/\sigma_b\end{pmatrix}
 \in\mathbb R^3.                                                         \tag{2}
\]
Equations (1)--(2) imply \(\sum_g y_g=0\) and \(\max_g\|y_g\|\leq L_y\) for a history-uniform constant \(L_y\).  Since every \(x\in\mathcal X_G\) satisfies \(\sum_gx_g=0\), the definitions of \(A_b\) and \(W_b\) yield
\[
 S_G:=G^{-1/2}\sum_{g=1}^Gx_gy_g=(A_b^\top,W_b)^\top.                    \tag{3}
\]

We now prove the perfect-matching representation named in \(\texttt{def:perfect-matching-handle}\).  Draw a uniform perfect matching
\(
 \mathfrak m=\{\{r_j,s_j\}:1\leq j\leq G/2\}
\)
of \(\{1,\ldots,G\}\).  After fixing an ordering of the two vertices in every pair, draw independent Rademacher variables \(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_{G/2}\) and assign signs \((\xi_j,-\xi_j)\) to \((r_j,s_j)\).  A fixed balanced sign vector is generated exactly by the \((G/2)!\) matchings that pair its positive vertices bijectively with its negative vertices, with a unique orientation for each such matching.  Thus the generated sign vector has exactly law \(P_0\).  Conditional on \(\mathfrak m\),
\[
 S_G=G^{-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^{G/2}\xi_jd_j,
 \qquad
 d_j=y_{r_j}-y_{s_j},
 \qquad
 C_{\mathfrak m}:=\operatorname{Var}(S_G\mid\mathfrak m)
     =G^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{G/2}d_jd_j^\top.                                 \tag{4}
\]

The conditional covariance in (4) concentrates even when its limit is singular.  A fixed unordered pair occurs in a uniform perfect matching with probability \(1/(G-1)\).  Hence
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathfrak m}C_{\mathfrak m}
 =\frac1{G(G-1)}\sum_{1\leq r<s\leq G}(y_r-y_s)(y_r-y_s)^\top
 =\frac1{G-1}\sum_{g=1}^Gy_gy_g^\top=:C_G,                              \tag{5}
\]
where the second equality uses \(\sum_gy_g=0\).  Under \(P_0\), exchangeability and \(\sum_gx_g=0\) give
\(
 \mathbb E_0x_g=0
\),
\(
 \mathbb E_0x_g^2=1
\), and
\(
 \mathbb E_0x_gx_h=-1/(G-1)
\)
for \(g\ne h\).  Expanding (3) therefore shows that
\[
 C_G=\operatorname{Var}_0(S_G).                                          \tag{6}
\]

For an entry of the unnormalized edge sum in (4), put
\(
 h_{rs}=(y_r-y_s)_k(y_r-y_s)_\ell
\); then \(|h_{rs}|\leq H_0\) uniformly.  Reveal the matching one edge at a time.  If \(R\) is the set of \(r\geq4\) unrevealed vertices, the conditional expectation of the remaining edge sum is
\[
 \frac1{r-1}\sum_{\{i,j\}\subset R}h_{ij}.                              \tag{7}
\]
After revealing \(\{u,v\}\), its contribution together with the conditional expectation of the new remainder is
\[
 h_{uv}+\frac1{r-3}
       \sum_{\{i,j\}\subset R\setminus\{u,v\}}h_{ij}.                  \tag{8}
\]
Splitting the sum in (7) into pairs incident to \(u\) or \(v\) and pairs disjoint from them shows directly from \(|h_{ij}|\leq H_0\) that the difference between (7) and (8) is at most \(C H_0\); the final edge has zero increment.  After division by \(G\), each increment of the Doob martingale for \((C_{\mathfrak m})_{k\ell}\) is therefore at most \(C H_0/G\).  Orthogonality of martingale differences gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_{\mathfrak m}\{(C_{\mathfrak m})_{k\ell}\}
 \leq \frac G2\left(\frac{C H_0}{G}\right)^2
 \leq \frac{C_1}{G}.                                                     \tag{9}
\]
Summing (9) over the nine entries and applying Cauchy--Schwarz yields
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mathfrak m}\|C_{\mathfrak m}-C_G\|\leq C_2G^{-1/2}.       \tag{10}
\]
No eigenvalue lower bound was used in (5)--(10).

Let \(h:\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb R\) be bounded with bounded derivatives through order three.  For fixed \(\mathfrak m\), replace the independent summands \(\xi_jd_j/\sqrt G\) in (4), one at a time, by \(\zeta_jd_j/\sqrt G\), where the \(\zeta_j\) are independent \(N(0,1)\) variables.  In the third-order Taylor expansion around the sum of all other terms, the constant, linear, and quadratic expectations agree because
\[
 \mathbb E\xi_j=\mathbb E\zeta_j=0,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E\xi_j^2=\mathbb E\zeta_j^2=1.                                \tag{11}
\]
The integral remainder, \(\|d_j\|\leq2L_y\), and the finite third absolute moment of \(\zeta_j\) give
\[
 \left|\mathbb E\{h(S_G)\mid\mathfrak m\}
       -\mathbb Eh\{N(0,C_{\mathfrak m})\}\right|
 \leq C\|D^3h\|_\infty
       \sum_{j=1}^{G/2}\frac{\|d_j\|^3}{G^{3/2}}
 \leq C_3\|D^3h\|_\infty G^{-1/2}.                                    \tag{12}
\]

For positive-semidefinite \(C_1,C_2\), put
\(
 C(t)=(1-t)C_1+tC_2
\)
and first replace it by the positive-definite matrix \(C_\delta(t)=C(t)+\delta I_3\).  If \(\phi_{C_\delta(t)}\) is its Gaussian density, direct differentiation of the determinant and inverse in that density gives
\[
 \partial_t\phi_{C_\delta(t)}(z)
 =\frac12\sum_{k,\ell}(C_2-C_1)_{k\ell}
       \partial_{k\ell}\phi_{C_\delta(t)}(z).                            \tag{13}
\]
Multiplying (13) by \(h(z)\), integrating over \(z\), and integrating twice by parts is legitimate because \(h\) and its first two derivatives are bounded while the density and its derivatives have Gaussian tails.  The boundary terms vanish, giving
\[
 \frac d{dt}\mathbb Eh\{N(0,C(t)+\delta I_3)\}
 =\frac12\sum_{k,\ell}(C_2-C_1)_{k\ell}
   \mathbb E\,\partial_{k\ell}h\{N(0,C(t)+\delta I_3)\}.               \tag{14}
\]
Integrating (14), bounding the Hessian, and sending \(\delta\downarrow0\) by dominated convergence yields
\[
 |\mathbb Eh\{N(0,C_1)\}-\mathbb Eh\{N(0,C_2)\}|
 \leq C_4\|D^2h\|_\infty\|C_1-C_2\|.                                 \tag{15}
\]
The regularization proves (15) on the exact covariance ranges, including singular ones.  Averaging (12) over \(\mathfrak m\) and using (10) and (15) proves the uniform smooth-test estimate
\[
 \left|\mathbb E_0h(A_b,W_b)-\mathbb Eh(A_b^\star,W_b^\star)\right|
 \leq C_5G^{-1/2}
       \bigl(\|D^2h\|_\infty+\|D^3h\|_\infty\bigr).                   \tag{16}
\]
Here the Gaussian vector in (16) has covariance \(C_G\), so it is precisely the covariance-matched pair in the theorem.  Every constant in (16) is uniform in the block, history, \(B\), and structural-class member.

Apply (16) to \(h(a,w)=f_{\eta,\kappa}(w)\).  Its derivatives through order three are bounded, so
\[
 \left|\mathbb E_0f_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b)
       -\mathbb Ef_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b^\star)\right|
 \leq C_6G^{-1/2}.                                                        \tag{17}
\]
For the quadratic comparison, let \(\chi_L:\mathbb R^2\to[0,1]\) be smooth, equal to one on \(\{\|a\|\leq L\}\), and zero on \(\{\|a\|\geq2L\}\).  For \(L\geq1\), every second or third derivative of
\[
 h_{ij,L}(a,w)=a_ia_jf_{\eta,\kappa}(w)\chi_L(a)                        \tag{18}
\]
is bounded by \(C_7(1+L^2)\).  By \(\texttt{lem:score-decomposition}\),
\(
 A_b=\mathbb E_0(R_b\mid x_b,\mathcal F_{b-1})
\).  Conditional Jensen and \(\texttt{lem:block-moments}\) imply
\[
 \mathbb E_0\|A_b\|^4\leq\mathbb E_0\|R_b\|^4\leq C_R.               \tag{19}
\]
Write \(A_b^\star=LZ\), where \(Z\sim N_2(0,I_2)\) and \(LL^\top=\operatorname{Var}(A_b^\star)\); such a positive-semidefinite square root exists even when the covariance is singular.  Expanding in an eigenbasis and using \(\mathbb EZ_j^2=1\), \(\mathbb EZ_j^4=3\), and independence gives
\[
 \mathbb E\|A_b^\star\|^4
 =\{\operatorname{tr}\operatorname{Var}(A_b^\star)\}^2
   +2\operatorname{tr}\{\operatorname{Var}(A_b^\star)^2\}
 \leq3\{\mathbb E\|A_b^\star\|^2\}^2\leq C_8,                       \tag{20}
\]
where the last inequality follows from covariance matching and (19).  Since \(0<f_{\eta,\kappa}\leq1\), for either \(A=A_b\) or \(A=A_b^\star\),
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[\|A\|^2\mathbf1\{\|A\|>L\}
                   f_{\eta,\kappa}(W)\right]
 \leq L^{-2}\mathbb E\|A\|^4\leq C_9L^{-2}.                           \tag{21}
\]
Using (16) on (18), then (21), and choosing \(L=G^{1/8}\), gives, entry by entry and hence in matrix norm,
\[
 \left\|\mathbb E_0[A_bA_b^\top f_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b)]
 -\mathbb E[A_b^\star A_b^{\star\top}f_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b^\star)]
 \right\|
 \leq C_{10}\left\{\frac{1+L^2}{\sqrt G}+L^{-2}\right\}
 \leq C_{11}G^{-1/4}.                                                     \tag{22}
\]

It remains to add the second-stage noise.  Since \(p_{dq}\geq1/4\), the bounded endpoints and the fixed contrast vectors imply \(\|e_{gbq}\|\leq L_e\) uniformly.  Conditional on \((x_b,\mathcal F_{b-1})\), \(\texttt{lem:score-decomposition}\) makes the vectors \(e_{gb,Q_{gb}}\) independent and centered.  The same one-at-a-time Taylor replacement used in (11)--(12), now in the two score coordinates, replaces
\(
 U_b=2G^{-1/2}\sum_ge_{gb,Q_{gb}}
\)
by a conditionally centered Gaussian \(E_b(x_b)\) with covariance
\[
 K_b(x_b)=\frac4G\sum_gT_{gb,Q_{gb}}
 =K_b+\frac2G\sum_gx_{gb}(T_{gbq_H}-T_{gbq_L}),                         \tag{23}
\]
at error at most \(C_{12}\|D^3h\|_\infty G^{-1/2}\) for every bounded smooth test of \((A_b+U_b,W_b)\).  This is a direct Lindeberg replacement; it uses only conditional independence, centering, covariance matching, and the uniform third-moment bound supplied by \(L_e\).

Put \(D_g=T_{gbq_H}-T_{gbq_L}\) and \(\bar D=G^{-1}\sum_gD_g\).  The matrices \(D_g\) are uniformly bounded because \(e_{gbq}\) is bounded.  The balanced-sign covariances used in (6) give the exact identity
\[
 \mathbb E_0\|K_b(x_b)-K_b\|^2
 =\frac4{G(G-1)}\sum_{g=1}^G\|D_g-\bar D\|^2
 \leq \frac{C_{13}}G.                                                     \tag{24}
\]
On a common extension, let \(E_b^\star\sim N_2(0,K_b)\) be independent of both \((A_b,W_b)\) and \((A_b^\star,W_b^\star)\).  Conditional Gaussian interpolation as in (13)--(15), followed by Cauchy--Schwarz and (24), replaces \(E_b(x_b)\) by \(E_b^\star\) at error at most \(C_{14}\|D^2h\|_\infty G^{-1/2}\).  Finally define
\(
 \widetilde h(a,w)=\mathbb E h(a+E_b^\star,w)
\).  Its derivative bounds do not exceed those of \(h\), so (16) applied to \(\widetilde h\) proves
\[
 \left|\mathbb E_0h(R_b,W_b)
       -\mathbb Eh(A_b^\star+E_b^\star,W_b^\star)\right|
 \leq C_{15}G^{-1/2}
       \bigl(\|D^2h\|_\infty+\|D^3h\|_\infty\bigr).                   \tag{25}
\]
By \(\texttt{lem:score-decomposition}\),
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(A_b^\star+E_b^\star)
 =\operatorname{Var}_0(A_b)+K_b=\operatorname{Var}_0(R_b),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Cov}(A_b^\star+E_b^\star,W_b^\star)
 =\operatorname{Cov}_0(R_b,W_b).                                         \tag{26}
\]
Both vectors are centered.  Thus the Gaussian vector on the right of (25) is exactly \((R_b^\star,W_b^\star)\), including when its covariance is singular.

Apply (25) to the cutoff version of \(r_ir_jf_{\eta,\kappa}(w)\).  The actual fourth moment is uniformly bounded by \(\texttt{lem:block-moments}\).  For the comparison vector, the square-root expansion used in (20), covariance matching in (26), and \(\mathbb E_0\|R_b\|^2\leq C_R^{1/2}\) give the same uniform fourth-moment bound.  Repeating (18)--(22) with \(R_b\) in place of \(A_b\) yields
\[
 \left\|\mathbb E_0[R_bR_b^\top f_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b)]
 -\mathbb E[R_b^\star R_b^{\star\top}f_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b^\star)]
 \right\|\leq C_{16}G^{-1/4}.                                           \tag{27}
\]
After enlarging one deterministic constant to cover the finitely many smallest even \(G\), equations (17), (22), and (27) are jointly bounded by
\(
 \varepsilon_G=C G^{-1/4}
\).  This sequence is deterministic, nonincreasing along even \(G\), and tends to zero.  The bounds are uniform in \(b\), the realized history, \(B\), and every member of the structural class \(\mathcal M_{G,B}\).  The proof used no covariance-limit assumption and no lower bound on either coordinate variance, completing the claim.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The singular-support weighted moment approximation is uniform over the structural bounded-design class and invokes none of the four covariance-limit assumptions. \textit{Gap.} Available finite-population Gaussian approximations do not directly transfer the soft-weighted unbounded quadratic score on a possibly singular covariance range. \textit{Consumer.} The growing-group projection theorem uses the transported weighted second moment before applying any covariance stabilization.

\begin{lemma}[lem:adaptive-witness-pre-membership-transport]\label{lem:adaptive-witness-pre-membership-transport}
For the arrays generated by \(\texttt{def:adaptive-witness}\), using only their displayed bounded construction and the conditional design in \(\texttt{def:soft-two-stage-design}\), uniformly over \(b\in\mathcal B_B\) and every realized history through block \(b-1\),
\[
 \left|\mathbb E_0f_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b)-\mathbb Ef_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b^\star)\right|
 +\left\|\mathbb E_0\{A_bA_b^\top f_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b)\}
 -\mathbb E\{A_b^\star A_b^{\star\top}f_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b^\star)\}\right\|
 \longrightarrow0
\]
as \(G\to\infty\).  The comparison uses the exact covariance range and requires no lower bound on a score-coordinate variance.  Moreover, without using a covariance-stabilization or model-class premise,
\[
 \left\|\Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}-\Sigma_{b,0}
 +(1-v_{\eta,\kappa})\beta_b\beta_b^\top\right\|\longrightarrow0.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a block \(b\) and condition throughout on an arbitrary realized history in \(\mathcal F_{b-1}\).  No model-class membership or covariance-stabilization premise is used.  Write \(a_{gb}=(a_{Dg},a_{Sg})^\top\) for the two coordinates specified by \(\texttt{def:adaptive-witness}\), and write \(Y_{gb}(d,q)=Y_{gbi}(d,q)\), which is independent of \(i\) in that construction.  Substitution into the four coefficients \(c_{dq}\) gives
\[
m_{gbq_H}-m_{gbq_L}=(a_{Dg},a_{Sg})^\top.                 \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, the direct coordinate of the left side is
\[
\frac12\{Y_{gb}(1,q_H)-Y_{gb}(0,q_H)
             -Y_{gb}(1,q_L)+Y_{gb}(0,q_L)\}
=\frac{\theta_g(q_H-q_L)}2=a_{Dg},
\]
where \(q_H-q_L=1/2\) and \(\theta_g=4a_{Dg}\).  Its spillover coordinate is
\[
\frac12\sum_{q\in\{q_L,q_H\}}\{Y_{gb}(0,q)+Y_{gb}(1,q)\}
=2h_g+\delta_g+\frac{\theta_g}{2}=a_{Sg},
\]
by \(\delta_g=L-\theta_g\) and \(h_g=a_{Sg}/2+a_{Dg}-L/2\).

At saturation \(q\), the second stage in \(\texttt{def:soft-two-stage-design}\) assigns exactly \(np_{dq}\) units to own-treatment cell \(d\).  Since the witness outcome is constant over units,
\[
\frac1n\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_n}
 \frac{\mathbf 1\{A_{gbi}=d\}Y_{gbi}(d,q)}{p_{dq}}
=Y_{gbi}(d,q).
\]
Thus \(\widehat m_{gbq}=m_{gbq}\), \(e_{gbq}=0\), \(U_b=0\), and, by \(\texttt{def:score-splitting}\), \(R_b=A_b\).  In particular, \(K_b=0\).

At either saturation, \(q=1/2+x_{gb}/4\) and \(q^2-q=-3/16\).  The realized group mean therefore obeys
\[
\begin{aligned}
\bar Y^{\mathrm{obs}}_{gb}
&=h_g+q(\delta_g+\theta_gq)\\
&=\frac{a_{Sg}}2+\frac{a_{Dg}}4+\frac L4x_{gb}.
\end{aligned}                                               \tag{2}
\]
The four pairs \((H_g,J_g)\in\{-1,1\}^2\) occur equally often, and every realized \(x_b\) is balanced.  Hence all three terms on the right side of (2) have grand mean zero.  Since the block types alternate and \(\lambda=1\), the predictable balance vectors, apart from \(V_{g1}=H_g\), are
\[
V_{gb}^{A}=\frac32H_g+\frac14J_g+x_{g,b-1},
\qquad
V_{gb}^{B}=\frac54H_g+\frac12J_g.                        \tag{3}
\]

We now establish the history-uniform Gaussian comparison directly.  Define
\[
y_g=\left(a_{Dg},a_{Sg},\frac{V_{gb}}{\sigma_b}\right)^\top,
\qquad
S_G=G^{-1/2}\sum_{g=1}^Gx_{gb}y_g=(A_b^\top,W_b)^\top.
\]
For every block type and every history, \(\sum_gy_g=0\).  The first two coordinates of \(y_g\) have absolute value one.  Equation (3) gives \(|V_{gb}|\leq11/4\).  On an A block, put \(d_g=3H_g/2+J_g/4\).  Equal representation of the four types gives \(G^{-1}\sum_gd_g^2=37/16\), while \(\|x_{b-1}\|_2=\sqrt G\).  The reverse triangle inequality yields
\[
\sigma_b=\frac{\|d+x_{b-1}\|_2}{\sqrt{G-1}}
\geq\sqrt{\frac G{G-1}}\left(\frac{\sqrt{37}}4-1\right)>0. \tag{4}
\]
On a B block, direct calculation gives \(\sigma_b^2=29G/\{16(G-1)\}\), and initially \(\sigma_1^2=G/(G-1)\).  Consequently there is a deterministic \(K<\infty\), independent of \(G\), \(b\), and the history, such that
\[
\max_{1\leq g\leq G}\|y_g\|\leq K.                     \tag{5}
\]

Draw a uniform perfect matching \(\mathfrak m\) of \(\{1,\ldots,G\}\), write its pairs as \(\{r_j,s_j\}\), and independently draw Rademacher orientations \(\epsilon_j\), setting \(x_{r_j}=\epsilon_j\) and \(x_{s_j}=-\epsilon_j\).  A fixed balanced sign vector is generated by exactly \((G/2)!\) matching-orientation pairs.  Since the total number of such pairs is \((G-1)!!2^{G/2}\), its probability is
\[
\frac{(G/2)!}{(G-1)!!2^{G/2}}=\binom{G}{G/2}^{-1}.
\]
Thus this construction has exactly law \(P_0\), and, conditionally on \(\mathfrak m\),
\[
S_G=\sum_{j=1}^{G/2}\epsilon_jd_j,
\qquad
d_j=G^{-1/2}(y_{r_j}-y_{s_j}),
\qquad
C_{\mathfrak m}=\sum_jd_jd_j^\top.                       \tag{6}
\]
Let \(C_G=\operatorname{Cov}_0(S_G)\).  Each unordered edge belongs to a uniform perfect matching with probability \(1/(G-1)\).  The identity
\[
\sum_{g<h}(y_g-y_h)(y_g-y_h)^\top
=G\sum_g(y_g-\bar y)(y_g-\bar y)^\top
\]
therefore implies
\[
\mathbb E_{\mathfrak m}C_{\mathfrak m}
=\frac1{G-1}\sum_g(y_g-\bar y)(y_g-\bar y)^\top=C_G.    \tag{7}
\]
Moreover, for any fixed matrix entry, \(C_{\mathfrak m}\) has the form \(G^{-1}\sum_{g<h}I_{gh}c_{gh}\), with \(|c_{gh}|\leq4K^2\).  Here
\[
\Pr(I_{gh}=1)=\frac1{G-1},
\]
the joint probability is zero for two distinct edges sharing a vertex, and it is \(1/\{(G-1)(G-3)\}\) for two disjoint edges.  The diagonal, overlapping-edge, and disjoint-edge contributions to the variance are respectively bounded by
\[
G^{-2}O(G^2)O(G^{-1}),\qquad
G^{-2}O(G^3)O(G^{-2}),\qquad
G^{-2}O(G^4)O(G^{-3}).
\]
All are \(O(G^{-1})\), uniformly under (5).  Summing over the nine entries gives a constant \(C<\infty\) such that
\[
\mathbb E_{\mathfrak m}\|C_{\mathfrak m}-C_G\|_F^2
\leq \frac{CK^4}{G}.                                     \tag{8}
\]

Let \(Z_{\mathfrak m}=\sum_jg_jd_j\), where the \(g_j\) are independent standard normal variables.  For a function \(h:\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb R\) with bounded derivatives through order three, replace the summands in (6) one at a time.  The constant, linear, and quadratic Taylor terms agree because \(\epsilon_j\) and \(g_j\) have the same first two moments.  Taylor's integral remainder, (5), and \(\|d_j\|\leq2K/\sqrt G\) give
\[
\left|\mathbb E\{h(S_G)-h(Z_{\mathfrak m})\mid\mathfrak m\}\right|
\leq C\|D^3h\|_\infty\sum_j\|d_j\|^3
\leq \frac{CK^3\|D^3h\|_\infty}{\sqrt G}.              \tag{9}
\]
Let \(Z_G\sim N_3(0,C_G)\).  If \(C_0,C_1\) are positive semidefinite and \(C_t=(1-t)C_0+tC_1\), Gaussian integration by parts gives
\[
\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb Eh\{N(0,C_t)\}
=\frac12\operatorname{tr}\left[(C_1-C_0)
                 \mathbb ED^2h\{N(0,C_t)\}\right].      \tag{10}
\]
For singular covariances, apply the identity to \(C_t+\delta I_3\) and let \(\delta\downarrow0\); boundedness of \(D^2h\) permits dominated convergence.  Integrating (10), then using (8) and Cauchy--Schwarz together with (9), proves the uniform bound
\[
\left|\mathbb E_0h(S_G)-\mathbb Eh(Z_G)\right|
\leq\frac{C_K}{\sqrt G}
 \{\|D^2h\|_\infty+\|D^3h\|_\infty\}.                 \tag{11}
\]
This argument uses the exact covariance matrices themselves and never divides by a score-coordinate variance, so it remains valid on a singular covariance range.

Taking \(h(s)=f_{\eta,\kappa}(s_3)\) in (11) proves the scalar comparison because this fixed function has bounded derivatives through order three.  For the quadratic comparison, let \(\chi_L\) be a smooth cutoff equal to one on \(\|s_{1:2}\|\leq L\) and zero on \(\|s_{1:2}\|\geq2L\), and apply (11), entry by entry, to
\[
h_{rs,L}(s)=s_rs_s f_{\eta,\kappa}(s_3)\chi_L(s_{1:2}),
\qquad r,s\in\{1,2\}.
\]
The relevant second- and third-derivative norms are bounded by \(C(1+L^2)\).  Conditional on a matching, the independent-sign fourth-moment identity gives
\[
\mathbb E\left(\sum_j\epsilon_jd_{jr}\right)^4
=3\left(\sum_jd_{jr}^2\right)^2-2\sum_jd_{jr}^4
\leq12K^4.
\]
The covariance bound following from (6) gives a uniform fourth-moment bound for \(Z_G\) as well.  Therefore, for a constant depending only on \(K\),
\[
\mathbb E\{\|S_{G,1:2}\|^2\mathbf1(\|S_{G,1:2}\|>L)\}
+\mathbb E\{\|Z_{G,1:2}\|^2\mathbf1(\|Z_{G,1:2}\|>L)\}
\leq C_KL^{-2}.                                           \tag{12}
\]
Choose \(L=G^{1/16}\).  The smoothed comparison error in (11) is then \(O(G^{-3/8})\), and the truncation error in (12) is \(O(G^{-1/8})\).  Hence a single deterministic modulus tending to zero works uniformly over blocks and histories.  The first two coordinates of \(Z_G\) and its third coordinate have exactly the joint law of \((A_b^\star,W_b^\star)\), by their definition as the covariance-matched Gaussian pair.  This proves both weighted comparisons in the lemma.

It remains to derive the covariance expansion.  Complementation \(x\mapsto-x\) preserves \(P_0\), leaves the even weight \(f_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b)\) unchanged, and sends \(A_b\) to \(-A_b\).  Since \(R_b=A_b\), the conditional soft-law score is exactly centered and
\[
\Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}
=\frac{\mathbb E_0\{A_bA_b^\top f_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b)\}}
       {\mathbb E_0f_{\eta,\kappa}(W_b)}.                \tag{13}
\]
Also \(K_b=0\), so \(\operatorname{Var}_0(A_b)=\Sigma_{b,0}\), and \(\beta_b=\operatorname{Cov}_0(A_b,W_b)\).  Since \(\operatorname{Var}_0(W_b)=1\), the covariance-matched Gaussian pair admits the possibly singular orthogonal decomposition
\[
A_b^\star=\beta_bW_b^\star+E_b^\star,
\qquad
E_b^\star\perp W_b^\star,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(E_b^\star)=\Sigma_{b,0}-\beta_b\beta_b^\top. \tag{14}
\]
Here \(W_b^\star\sim N(0,1)\).  For \(Z\sim N(0,1)\), direct Gaussian integration gives
\[
\mathbb Ee^{-\kappa Z^2}=(1+2\kappa)^{-1/2},
\qquad
\mathbb E\{Z^2e^{-\kappa Z^2}\}=(1+2\kappa)^{-3/2}.
\]
Using (14) and the definition in \(\texttt{def:projection-gain}\), the Gaussian analogue of the ratio in (13) is therefore
\[
\Sigma_{b,0}-(1-v_{\eta,\kappa})\beta_b\beta_b^\top.     \tag{15}
\]
Both exact and Gaussian denominators are at least \(\eta>0\).  Their numerators are uniformly bounded by (5), and the weighted comparisons already proved make both numerator and denominator errors tend uniformly to zero.  Comparing the two ratios in (13) and (15) proves
\[
\sup_{b,\mathcal F_{b-1}}
\left\|\Sigma_{b,\mathrm{RR}}-\Sigma_{b,0}
 +(1-v_{\eta,\kappa})\beta_b\beta_b^\top\right\|\longrightarrow0,
\]
which is the remaining assertion.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This pre-membership comparison breaks the logical cycle between certifying the explicit adaptive witness and invoking the class-level weighted-transport theorem. \textit{Gap.} The class-level transport theorem applies only after all model-class conditions hold; the witness needs a bounded-array perfect-matching proof before its RR covariance stabilization can be certified. \textit{Consumer.} The proposition on the alternating adaptive witness uses this lemma to derive RR covariance stabilization and positive definiteness before concluding model-class membership.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The structural class does not itself guarantee covariance stabilization or a positive-definite limiting covariance.  The arbitrary-\(G\), \(B\to\infty\) central limit theorem and Wald guarantee therefore apply only on sequences satisfying rerandomization covariance stabilization and positive definiteness.  The projection formula is instead a \(G\to\infty\) approximation and requires complete-randomization, rerandomization, and projection stabilization, though not positive definiteness.  The completed studentizer can be conservative by the nonvanishing average completion, and the population gain matrix \(P\) is not generally observable.  Exact enumeration over \(1{,}536\) assignments at \(G=n=4\) remains a finite diagnostic rather than evidence for either asymptotic regime.

\section{Honest scope}

The estimand is a sustained two-period endpoint contrast under ordinary nonanticipation and an imposed lag-one finite-memory restriction; contemporaneous and carryover components are not separately identified.  Structural membership requires bounded fixed potential outcomes, partial and stratified interference, nonanticipation, lag-one memory, and a uniform imbalance-variance window.  Many-block inference additionally assumes rerandomization covariance stabilization and \(\lambda_{\min}(\Omega_{\mathrm{RR}})>0\), but permits fixed or arbitrarily varying even \(G\).  The projection identity instead requires \(G\to\infty\) and exactly the complete-randomization, rerandomization, and projection stabilization conditions; it does not require a positive-definite rerandomization limit.  The matrix \(P\) depends on unobserved potential-outcome scores, so \(c^\top Pc>0\) is a theoretical characterization rather than an observable deployment screen.  The Yu--Ma--Liu market calibration and the Bojinov--Shephard trading experiment are prospective design consumers only; ten historical markets do not validate either asymptotic regime.
