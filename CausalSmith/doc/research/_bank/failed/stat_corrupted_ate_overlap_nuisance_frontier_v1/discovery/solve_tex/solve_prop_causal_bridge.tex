Let \(\lambda_d\) denote Lebesgue measure on \([0,1]^d\).  For
\(a\in\{0,1\}\), put
\[
p_a(x)=P(A=a\mid X=x),
\qquad
m_a(x)=\mathbb E_{P^{\mathrm F}}[Y(a)\mid X=x],
\]
where \(m_a\) is any measurable version.  Such conditional expectations are
finite because \(0\leq Y(a)\leq1\).  Since \(P\) is the
\((X,A,Y)\)-margin of \(P^{\mathrm F}\), the conditional law of the observed
variables used below is the same under these two laws.

First we verify the positivity needed for conditioning.  The polynomial-overlap
and calibration-envelope assumptions, together with \(\kappa>0\) and
\(0\leq x_1\leq1\), give
\[
p_1(x)=e(x)=x_1^\kappa u(x),
\qquad
0\leq e(x)\leq\frac12,
\qquad
e(x)>0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ x_1>0.
\]
Consequently,
\[
p_0(x)=1-e(x)\geq\frac12>0
\quad\text{for every }x,
\]
whereas \(p_1(x)>0\) away from
\(F=\{x\in[0,1]^d:x_1=0\}\).  The uniform-design assumption gives
\[
P^{\mathrm F}(X\in F)=P(X\in F)=\lambda_d(F)=0.
\]
It follows that \(p_a(X)>0\) almost surely for both treatment levels.

We next connect the observed and potential conditional means.  Conditional
exchangeability implies, for each \(a\),
\[
\mathbb E_{P^{\mathrm F}}[Y(a)\mid X,A]
=\mathbb E_{P^{\mathrm F}}[Y(a)\mid X]
=m_a(X)
\quad P^{\mathrm F}\text{-almost surely}.
\]
Let \(g_a(x)=\mathbb E_P[Y\mid X=x,A=a]\) on the set where
\(p_a(x)>0\).  By the defining property of conditional expectation,
consistency, iterated conditioning, and conditional exchangeability, in that
order,
\[
\begin{aligned}
p_a(X)g_a(X)
&=\mathbb E_{P^{\mathrm F}}[\mathbf 1\{A=a\}Y\mid X]\\
&=\mathbb E_{P^{\mathrm F}}[\mathbf 1\{A=a\}Y(a)\mid X]\\
&=\mathbb E_{P^{\mathrm F}}\!\left[
  \mathbf 1\{A=a\}
  \mathbb E_{P^{\mathrm F}}[Y(a)\mid X,A]
  \mathrel{\big|}X\right]\\
&=\mathbb E_{P^{\mathrm F}}[\mathbf 1\{A=a\}m_a(X)\mid X]\\
&=p_a(X)m_a(X)
\quad P^{\mathrm F}\text{-almost surely}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Because \(p_a(X)>0\) almost surely, division is valid off a null set and yields
\(g_a(X)=m_a(X)\) almost surely.  The outcome-regression-law assumption says
that \(g_a(x)=\mu_a(x)\) whenever \(p_a(x)>0\).  Hence
\[
m_a(X)=\mu_a(X)
\quad P^{\mathrm F}\text{-almost surely},
\qquad a\in\{0,1\}.
\]
For \(a=1\), no equality at the nonidentified face \(F\) was used.  Moreover,
the outcome-smoothness assumption with \(\beta>0\) makes \(\mu_1\) continuous,
and the set \(\{x:x_1>0\}\) is dense in \([0,1]^d\); thus its boundary value,
if mentioned, is uniquely fixed by continuous extension.  In any event,
\(\lambda_d(F)=0\), so that value cannot affect the integral below.  The same
smoothness assumption also makes each \(\mu_a\) bounded and Borel measurable on
the compact cube, so all displayed integrals are finite.

Finally, boundedness of the potential outcomes permits linearity and the tower
property.  Using successively the definition of the causal ATE, the tower
property, the almost-sure equalities just proved, and the uniform-design
assumption, we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
\theta^{\mathrm F}(P^{\mathrm F})
&=\mathbb E_{P^{\mathrm F}}[Y(1)-Y(0)]\\
&=\mathbb E_{P^{\mathrm F}}[m_1(X)-m_0(X)]\\
&=\mathbb E_P[\mu_1(X)-\mu_0(X)]\\
&=\int_{[0,1]^d}\{\mu_1(x)-\mu_0(x)\}\,\mathrm dx\\
&=\theta(P),
\end{aligned}
\]
where the final equality is the definition of the clean observed-law
functional.  This proves the claimed bridge without asserting that the clean
law, or \(\theta(P)\), is point identified from the contaminated law \(R\).
