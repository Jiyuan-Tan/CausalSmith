%%% FIELD proof-lipschitz-lower-pair
Apply \(\texttt{lem:lipschitz-lower-pair-all-kappa}\) with \(d=1\), \(\beta=1\), \(\alpha=1\), and \(\kappa=2\).  Its hypotheses hold because the present statement assumes \(L\geq4\), and its class-membership conclusion gives
\[
 P_{0,h},P_{1,h}\in\mathcal P_{1,1,1,2,L}
 \qquad(0<h\leq1/4).
\]
Substitution of \(\kappa=2\) into the three formulas of that lemma gives, equation by equation,
\[
 \left|\theta(P_{1,h})-\theta(P_{0,h})\right|=\frac{h^2}{8},
\]
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(P_{0,h},P_{1,h})
 =\frac{h^{2+2}}{8(2+1)(2+2)}
 =\frac{h^4}{96},
\]
and
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_{0,h},P_{1,h})
 \leq \frac{h^{2+3}}{4(2+1)(2+2)(2+3)}
 =\frac{h^5}{240}.
\]

For \(n\geq4^5\), put \(h=n^{-1/5}\).  Then \(0<h\leq1/4\), so the preceding bounds apply.  Independence in the product experiment and the definition of Kullback--Leibler divergence give
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_{0,h}^{,n},P_{1,h}^{,n})
 =n\operatorname{KL}(P_{0,h},P_{1,h})
 \leq \frac{nh^5}{240}=\frac1{240},
\]
where the equality follows by integrating the sum of the \(n\) coordinate log-likelihood ratios under \(P_{0,h}^{,n}\).  The target separation is
\[
 \left|\theta(P_{1,h})-\theta(P_{0,h})\right|
 =\frac{n^{-2/5}}8.
\]
The final realization clause of \(\texttt{lem:lipschitz-lower-pair-all-kappa}\) states that, when \(\kappa>1\), this displayed boundary pair and its two-point argument realize the clean component of the minimax lower bound.  Hence the present choice gives a constant multiple of \(n^{-2/5}\).

If \(2\leq n<4^5\), choose instead \(h=1/4\).  This scale is admissible and
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_{0,1/4}^{,n},P_{1,1/4}^{,n})
 \leq \frac{n4^{-5}}{240}<\frac1{240},
 \qquad
 \left|\theta(P_{1,1/4})-\theta(P_{0,1/4})\right|=\frac1{128}.
\]
Since \(n^{-2/5}\leq1\), the last separation is at least \(n^{-2/5}/128\).  Thus the same two-point conclusion applies after reducing its numerical constant, uniformly over this finite range.

It remains to verify the explicit contamination collision.  Let \(0<\epsilon\leq1/10\), set
\[
 q=\frac{\epsilon}{1-\epsilon},
 \qquad
 h=\frac{q^{1/4}}4,
\]
and note that \(0<q\leq1/9\), hence \(0<h<1/4\).  By the exact total-variation formula already proved,
\[
 t=\operatorname{TV}(P_{0,h},P_{1,h})
 =\frac{h^4}{96}
 =\frac{q}{4^4\,96}
 \leq q=\frac{\epsilon}{1-\epsilon}.
\]
Consequently the construction in \(\texttt{def:lipschitz-lower-pair}\) applies, with
\[
 c=\frac{(1-\epsilon)t}{\epsilon}=\frac tq\in[0,1]
 \quad\text{and}\quad H=P_{0,h}.
\]
Because \(S_+\) and \(S_-\) are the probability normalizations of the positive and negative parts of \(P_{1,h}-P_{0,h}\), their defining signed-measure identity is
\[
 P_{1,h}-P_{0,h}=t(S_+-S_-).
\]
Using this identity, \(\epsilon c=(1-\epsilon)t\), and the definitions of \(Q_{0,h}\) and \(Q_{1,h}\), we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
 (1-\epsilon)P_{0,h}+\epsilon Q_{0,h}
 &=\{1-(1-\epsilon)t\}P_{0,h}+(1-\epsilon)tS_+,\\
 (1-\epsilon)P_{1,h}+\epsilon Q_{1,h}
 &= (1-\epsilon)\{P_{0,h}+t(S_+-S_-)\}
    +(1-\epsilon)tS_-+\{\epsilon-(1-\epsilon)t\}P_{0,h}\\
 &=\{1-(1-\epsilon)t\}P_{0,h}+(1-\epsilon)tS_+.
\end{aligned}
\]
Both mixtures therefore equal the law denoted by \(R_h\) in \(\texttt{def:lipschitz-lower-pair}\).  Finally,
\[
 \left|\theta(P_{1,h})-\theta(P_{0,h})\right|
 =\frac{h^2}{8}
 =\frac1{128}\left\{\frac{\epsilon}{1-\epsilon}\right\}^{1/2}.
\]
This proves every assertion of the corrected specialization.  At \(\epsilon=0\) the contamination lower-bound term is zero, but the explicit contaminants in \(\texttt{def:lipschitz-lower-pair}\) are not defined; this is exactly why the collision sentence is restricted to \(\epsilon>0\).
