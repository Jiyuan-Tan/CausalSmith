Because \(a_L>1\), the series in (1) is finite.  Fix an \(\varepsilon\) satisfying (1), and define
\[
c_q:=1-2\varepsilon\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}j^{-a_L}>0.
\tag{9}
\]
Fix \(K\ge2\).  Before choosing \(a\), set
\[
\kappa_K:=\min\!\left\{
\frac{c_q}{K^2},\frac{1-\eta}{K},
\frac{1-\eta}{\eta K},
\varepsilon-c_{\rm link},C_{\rm link}-\varepsilon
\right\}>0.
\tag{10}
\]
This number depends on the fixed \(K\) and the declared constants but not on \(a\).  Now fix an arbitrary \(a\in[a_L,a_U]\) and use the mass function \(q_a\) in (2).

The declared orthonormality of the DCT bases gives
\[
K^{-1}\sum_{x=1}^Ku_j(x)u_\ell(x)=\mathbf 1\{j=\ell\},
\qquad
K^{-1}\sum_{w=1}^Kv_j(w)v_\ell(w)=\mathbf 1\{j=\ell\},
\tag{11}
\]
with \(u_1=v_1=1\).  The displayed cosine formulas also give
\[
|u_j(x)|\le\sqrt2,
\qquad
|v_j(w)|\le\sqrt2
\quad (j\ge2).
\tag{12}
\]
Consequently every nonconstant basis vector has uniform-coordinate mean zero.  Summing (2) in either coordinate and applying (11) gives
\[
\sum_wq_a(x,w)=K^{-1},
\qquad
\sum_xq_a(x,w)=K^{-1},
\qquad
\sum_{x,w}q_a(x,w)=1.
\tag{13}
\]
By (12), \(a\ge a_L\), and (9),
\[
q_a(x,w)
\ge K^{-2}\left\{1-2\varepsilon\sum_{j=2}^Kj^{-a}\right\}
\ge c_qK^{-2}>0.
\tag{14}
\]
Thus \(q_a\) is a probability mass function with uniform marginals.  Since \(0<\eta<1/2\),
\[
\frac1K-\frac{\eta}{K}=\frac{1-\eta}{K}>0,
\qquad
\frac{1}{\eta K}-\frac1K=\frac{1-\eta}{\eta K}>0.
\tag{15}
\]
Equations (10), (13)--(15) therefore imply both the four marginal-balance requirements in \(\mathrm{def:model\mbox{-}class}\) and the sharper common-design inequalities
\[
q_a(x,w)\ge\kappa_K,
\quad
q_{a,X}(x)-\frac\eta K\ge\kappa_K,
\quad
\frac1{\eta K}-q_{a,X}(x)\ge\kappa_K,
\tag{16}
\]
with the analogous two marginal inequalities for \(q_{a,W}(w)\).  The same \(\kappa_K\), chosen before \(a\), works for every \(a\in[a_L,a_U]\).

Dividing (2) by \(q_{a,X}(x)=K^{-1}\), using (11), and applying the definition of the conditional-expectation operator yields
\[
Av_1=u_1,
\qquad
Av_j=\varepsilon j^{-a}u_j
\quad (2\le j\le K).
\tag{17}
\]
Write \(g=\sum_{j=1}^Kg_jv_j\).  Under the uniform \(W\)-marginal, the restriction \(E_P\{g(W)\}=0\) is equivalent to \(g_1=0\).  Hence (11), (17), and \(Lv_j=jv_j\) give
\[
\|Ag\|_{L^2_X}^2
=\varepsilon^2\sum_{j=2}^Kj^{-2a}g_j^2
=\varepsilon^2\|L^{-a}g\|_{\ell^2_{K,W}}^2.
\tag{18}
\]
Thus the link inequalities in \(\mathrm{def:model\mbox{-}class}\) hold.  More precisely, (10) and (18) give the common-design slack inequalities
\[
(c_{\rm link}+\kappa_K)\|L^{-a}g\|_{\ell^2_{K,W}}
\le \|Ag\|_{L^2_X}
\le (C_{\rm link}-\kappa_K)\|L^{-a}g\|_{\ell^2_{K,W}}.
\tag{19}
\]
Independently of fixed \(K\), the retained link slack is bounded below by
\[
s_{\rm link}:=\min\{\varepsilon-c_{\rm link},C_{\rm link}-\varepsilon\}>0.
\tag{20}
\]
Equations (16) and (19) explicitly discharge the conditional common-design premise used by the fixed-support argument on the compatibility subregime (1); no choice in them depends on \(a\).

The only nonzero DCT coefficient of \(h_0\equiv\eta\) is \(\theta_1=\eta\).  The declared ranges \(0<\eta<1/2\) and \(R>1/2\) imply
\[
\sum_{j=1}^Kj^{2\beta}\theta_j^2=\eta^2<R^2,
\qquad
\eta=h_0(w)<1-\eta
\quad\text{for every }w.
\tag{21}
\]
Thus \(h_0\in\mathcal H_\beta(R,\eta)\), its source and upper-box constraints are slack, and all lower-box constraints are active.  Let \(V\) be the \(K\times K\) matrix with entries \(V_{wj}=v_j(w)\).  Equation (11) says \(K^{-1}V^\top V=I_K\), so the rows of \(V\) are linearly independent.  Up to sign, these rows are exactly the gradients in \(\theta\) of the active inequalities \(\eta-\sum_j\theta_jv_j(w)\le0\).  The active gradients therefore satisfy the linear-independence constraint qualification, proving that \(h_0\) is a regular lower-box vertex.  Conditional expectation preserves constants, so (17) gives
\[
Ah_0=\eta=b_0,
\qquad
d(P_0)=0
\tag{22}
\]
by \(\mathrm{def:bridge\mbox{-}class}\) and the definition of the observable defect.

For a sign vector \(s\), equations (3) and (12) imply
\[
\|f_s\|_\infty
\le \frac{\delta}{\sqrt{M_K}}\sum_{j\in B_K}|u_j(x)|
\le\delta\sqrt{2M_K}.
\tag{23}
\]
Under the stated amplitude restriction, (4) and (23) imply
\[
\frac\eta2\le P_s(Y=1\mid X=x,W=w)\le\frac{3\eta}{2},
\qquad
P_s(Y=0\mid X=x,W=w)\ge1-\frac{3\eta}{2}>\frac14.
\tag{24}
\]
Because \(\eta/2<1/4\), both conditional outcome probabilities under \(P_s\), and also those under \(P_0\), are at least \(\eta/2\).  Combining (14) and (24), every three-way cell under every displayed law is bounded below by
\[
cK^{-2},
\qquad
c:=c_q\eta/2>0.
\tag{25}
\]
Changing the conditional outcome kernel in (4) does not alter \(q_a\), its marginals, or \(A\).  Equations (13)--(20) consequently verify directly every member property of \(\mathrm{def:model\mbox{-}class}\), including link slack uniform in \(a,K,n\), and hence
\[
P_0,P_s\in\mathcal P_K(a).
\tag{26}
\]

Let \(\Pi_{B_K}\) be the \(L^2(P_X)\)-orthogonal projection onto \(\operatorname{span}\{u_j:j\in B_K\}\).  From the definitions of \(J_K\), \(B_K\), and \(M_K\),
\[
K/2\le J_K\le K,
\qquad
K/2\le M_K\le K,
\tag{27}
\]
so both \(J_K\) and \(M_K\) are comparable to \(K\).  For any \(h_\theta\in\mathcal H_\beta(R,\eta)\), (17), the source constraint in \(\mathrm{def:bridge\mbox{-}class}\), and \(j\ge J_K\) on the block give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\Pi_{B_K}Ah_\theta\|_{L^2_X}^2
&=\varepsilon^2\sum_{j\in B_K}j^{-2a}\theta_j^2 \\
&=\varepsilon^2\sum_{j\in B_K}j^{-2(a+\beta)}j^{2\beta}\theta_j^2 \\
&\le\varepsilon^2J_K^{-2(a+\beta)}
       \sum_{j\in B_K}j^{2\beta}\theta_j^2 \\
&\le\varepsilon^2R^2J_K^{-2(a+\beta)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{28}
\]
Under \(P_s\), equation (4) gives \(b_s=\eta+f_s\), so \(\Pi_{B_K}b_s=f_s\).  DCT orthonormality in (11) gives \(\|f_s\|_{L^2_X}=\delta\).  Orthogonal projection is a contraction; hence the reverse triangle inequality and (28) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
d(P_s)
&=\inf_{h_\theta\in\mathcal H_\beta(R,\eta)}
  \|b_s-Ah_\theta\|_{L^2_X} \\
&\ge\inf_{h_\theta\in\mathcal H_\beta(R,\eta)}
  \|f_s-\Pi_{B_K}Ah_\theta\|_{L^2_X} \\
&\ge\delta-\varepsilon R J_K^{-(a+\beta)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{29}
\]
This proves (5) while profiling over the complete source-and-box image; no inactive box constraint has been removed from the infimum.

Write \(E_0\) for expectation under \(P_0\), and define \(\chi^2(Q,P)=E_P\{(dQ/dP-1)^2\}\) whenever \(Q\ll P\).  For one observation, the likelihood ratio of \(P_s\) with respect to \(P_0\) is
\[
\ell_s(O)
=1+\frac{f_s(X)(Y-\eta)}{\eta(1-\eta)}.
\tag{30}
\]
Conditioning on \(X\), using the Bernoulli variance \(\eta(1-\eta)\) under \(P_0\), and then using uniformity of \(P_X\) and (11), we obtain for any sign vectors \(s,t\)
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_0(\ell_s\ell_t)
&=1+\frac{\langle f_s,f_t\rangle_{L^2_X}}{\eta(1-\eta)} \\
&=1+\frac{\delta^2}{M_K\eta(1-\eta)}
      \sum_{j\in B_K}s_jt_j.
\end{aligned}
\tag{31}
\]
The amplitude restriction entails \(\delta^2\le\eta^2/(8M_K)<\eta(1-\eta)\), so the last expression in (31) is positive.  By \(\mathrm{ass:iid\mbox{-}sampling}\) and the exact second-moment identity for this finite likelihood-ratio mixture,
\[
\begin{aligned}
1+\chi^2(\overline P_n,P_0^{\otimes n})
&=2^{-2M_K}\sum_{s,t}\{E_0(\ell_s\ell_t)\}^n \\
&\le2^{-2M_K}\sum_{s,t}
\exp\!\left\{
 \frac{n\delta^2}{M_K\eta(1-\eta)}
 \sum_{j\in B_K}s_jt_j
\right\} \\
&=\cosh\!\left(
 \frac{n\delta^2}{M_K\eta(1-\eta)}
\right)^{M_K}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{32}
\]
The inequality is \((1+x)^n\le e^{nx}\) for \(x>-1\); the final equality follows by averaging the independent Rademacher products \(s_jt_j\).  Since \((\log\cosh x)'=\tanh x\le x\) for \(x\ge0\), integration from zero and evenness give \(\log\cosh x\le x^2/2\) for every real \(x\).  Applying this inequality to (32) proves (6).

It remains to select the amplitude uniformly.  Choose \(c_*>0\), depending only on \(\eta\) and \(\epsilon_{\rm tot}\), so small that
\[
\frac12\left[
\exp\!\left\{\frac{c_*^4}{2\eta^2(1-\eta)^2}\right\}-1
\right]^{1/2}<1-\epsilon_{\rm tot},
\tag{33}
\]
and set
\[
\delta=c_*\frac{M_K^{1/4}}{\sqrt n}.
\tag{34}
\]
By (27), if \(K=o(n^{2/3})\), then
\[
\delta\sqrt{2M_K}
\le\sqrt2c_*\frac{K^{3/4}}{\sqrt n}=o(1),
\tag{35}
\]
so the amplitude condition holds for all sufficiently large \(n\).  In particular, this positivity argument covers every polynomial law \(K_n\asymp n^\kappa\) with \(\kappa<2/3\).

For \(a\in[a_L,a_U]\), (27) and \(K\ge2\) imply
\[
J_K^{-(a+\beta)}
\le2^{a_U+\beta}K^{-(a_L+\beta)}.
\tag{36}
\]
Choose \(c_0>0\), independently of \(a,K,n\), sufficiently small that
\[
\varepsilon R2^{a_U+\beta}c_0
\le c_*2^{-5/4}.
\tag{37}
\]
Under (7), equations (27), (34), (36), and (37) yield
\[
\varepsilon RJ_K^{-(a+\beta)}\le\frac\delta2,
\qquad
d(P_s)\ge\frac\delta2
\ge c_1\frac{K^{1/4}}{\sqrt n},
\qquad
c_1:=c_*2^{-5/4}.
\tag{38}
\]
Thus the tail restriction at the worst endpoint \(a_L\) controls the image tail simultaneously for every \(a\ge a_L\).

Substitution of (34) into (6) makes its exponent equal to \(c_*^4/\{2\eta^2(1-\eta)^2\}\).  For any \(Q\ll P\), Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
\|Q-P\|_{\rm TV}
=\frac12E_P\left|\frac{dQ}{dP}-1\right|
\le\frac12\sqrt{\chi^2(Q,P)}.
\tag{39}
\]
Thus (6), (33), and (39) show
\[
\|\overline P_n-P_0^{\otimes n}\|_{\rm TV}<1-\epsilon_{\rm tot}.
\tag{40}
\]
For every measurable test \(\phi=\phi(O_1,\ldots,O_n)\in[0,1]\), the variational definition of total variation, followed by the fact that a supremum dominates a finite average, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_K(a):d(P)=0}E_{P^{\otimes n}}\phi
+\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_K(a):d(P)\ge c_1K^{1/4}/\sqrt n}
 E_{P^{\otimes n}}(1-\phi) \\
&\quad\ge
E_{P_0^{\otimes n}}\phi
+2^{-M_K}\sum_sE_{P_s^{\otimes n}}(1-\phi) \\
&\quad\ge1-\|\overline P_n-P_0^{\otimes n}\|_{\rm TV}
>\epsilon_{\rm tot}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{41}
\]
The null and alternatives in (41) are legal by (22), (26), and (38).  Hence no test satisfies the total-error requirement at this separation.  By \(\mathrm{def:minimax\mbox{-}radius}\), together with monotonicity of its alternative set in the separation, (8) follows.  The constants \(c,c_0,c_1\) depend only on the fixed quantities in (1), \(a_L,a_U,\beta,R,\eta\), and \(\epsilon_{\rm tot}\), and not on \(a,K,n\).  This completes every asserted legality, separation, affinity, and uniformity claim.
