\section*{stat\_\allowbreak{}plm\_\allowbreak{}honest\_\allowbreak{}adaptation}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} For a primitive JMS partially linear law and fixed training-realized nuisance fits supplied within stated \(L^8(P_X)\) radii, one interval is honest over exact-Gaussian and shrinking fourth-cumulant-separated branches of the projected evaluation experiment. Four explicit Bonferroni-calibrated median-of-means events use nonempty finite-sample block partitions and cover safe DML, four residual moments, centered Appell inversion, and its empirical slope. The non-Gaussian finite-sample length is \(\min\{d_n,B_n/\delta_n+d_np_{\mathrm{sel},n}\}\), with an explicit bound on \(p_{\mathrm{sel},n}\), and reduces to \(\min\{d_n,B_n/\delta_n\}\) when \(\delta_n/s_n\to\infty\). On supplied-fit sequences satisfying the stated conditional-transfer compatibility, the certified Gaussian lower coordinate is \(d_n^-\): the nuisance-product/log term comes from the transferred support-localized JMS fuzzy construction, while \(n^{-1/2}\) comes from a separate compatible two-point family proved inside the present class. The literal full-class statement of JMS Theorem 3.2 is not used for this sum because its bounded-\(Y\), full-support Gaussian-treatment conditions force slope degeneracy under conditional outcome orthogonality, and its displayed root-\(N\) witness has unbounded Gaussian outcome. Conditional outcome-noise nondegeneracy is confined to the lower subexperiment. The within-non-Gaussian or constrained-risk branchwise converse and removal of the Gaussian logarithm remain open; no matching Pareto frontier or global union-supremum improvement is claimed.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Published structure-agnostic PLM theory and the sibling repository paper study separate Gaussian or fixed-non-Gaussian point-estimation classes and fixed-separation spectral constructions, not one conditionally specified projected experiment with union honesty, shrinking cumulant separation, and branchwise expected length. The literal bounded-outcome formulation of JMS Theorem 3.2 also cannot be transferred wholesale to the present Gaussian branch: with conditionally full-support Gaussian treatment and \(E[\xi\mid X,T]=0\), bounded \(Y\) forces the partially linear slope to vanish, while its Appendix D root-\(N\) witness uses an unbounded Gaussian outcome.

\paragraph{Niche.} The informative object is the two-coordinate expected-length region; the global union supremum remains governed by the Gaussian coordinate.

\paragraph{Fill.} The proved upper component combines explicit nonempty-block four-event calibration, a finite-sample branch-selection error envelope, a safe unnormalized DML interval, and a fully centered split Appell inversion. The Gaussian converse is certified at \(d_n^-\) only under explicit conditional transfer of the source-localized nuisance-product supports and a compatible nonempty two-point path. The localized fuzzy construction supplies the product/log term, and the paper's own two-point argument supplies the parametric term; their maximum is converted to the displayed sum. The unsafe aggregate JMS lemma is removed rather than used as a full-class inclusion theorem. Conditional outcome-noise nondegeneracy is auxiliary to that lower subexperiment. The selector impossibility theorem closes the bounded-chi-square cross-branch fuzzy route at strong separation, while the actual strong-regime lower frontier remains open.

\section{Related work}

Jin, Mackey, and Syrgkanis inform two distinct pieces of the Gaussian-coordinate argument, but their literal full-class Theorem 3.2 is not used as a logical premise here. Its statement combines bounded \(Y\), conditionally full-support Gaussian treatment noise, and \(E[\xi\mid X,T]=0\); those conditions force a nonzero partially linear slope to be impossible because \(E[Y\mid X,T]=\theta T+f(X)\) would otherwise be unbounded in \(T\). Consistently with this mismatch, Appendix D equation (56) derives only the nuisance-product/log term at the retained \(\Lambda_n\) factor from moment-matched fuzzy supports, whereas equations (57)--(58) derive the parametric term using an unbounded Gaussian-outcome model. The present theorem conditionally transfers only the support-localized equation-(56) construction and independently proves the root-\(n\) term on a compatible embedded two-point path; it combines the two lower bounds through \(\max\{x,y\}\ge (x+y)/2\). Their Theorem 5.4 informs the fixed-separation ACE component but does not give a union-honest shrinking-cumulant interval. The auxiliary condition \(E_P[\xi^2\mid X,T]\ge c_{\xi}\) is not attributed to their envelope and is used only for the Gaussian expected-length lower subexperiment. Mackey, Syrgkanis, and Zadik, Theorems 5 and 8--10, inform the Gaussian obstruction and higher-order score. Cai and Low, Theorem 1 and Proposition 2, inform branchwise expected length, while their Gaussian model has no learned PLM residual. Cai and Low's 2011 Hermite mixture method informs the fuzzy-prior handle. Kennedy, Balakrishnan, Robins, and Wasserman inform localized product-mixture calculations. Chernozhukov et al. supply the split DML benchmark. The sibling repository paper \(\texttt{stat\_sa\_plm\_cumulant\_converse}\) proves fixed-separation spectral and estimation constructions and explicitly leaves shrinking separation, adaptive switching, uniform inference, and a local matching lower bound unresolved. The present proved contribution is the conditional supplied-fit shrinking-separation upper frontier together with the conditional Gaussian coordinate just described; it does not claim a matching Pareto frontier. Quessy and Mailhot analyze centered and studentized normality statistics without nuisance-contaminated residuals. \section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:outcome-equation]\label{ass:outcome-equation}
\(P\{Y=\theta_0T+f_0(X)+\xi\}=1\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (partially linear outcome equation, \cite{Robinson1988PLM}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:treatment-equation]\label{ass:treatment-equation}
\(P\{T=g_0(X)+\eta\}=1\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (partially linear treatment equation, \cite{Robinson1988PLM}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:outcome-orthogonality]\label{ass:outcome-orthogonality}
\(E_P[\xi\mid X,T]=0\) almost surely.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (partially linear conditional mean restriction, \cite{Robinson1988PLM}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:independent-treatment-noise]\label{ass:independent-treatment-noise}
\(\eta\perp X\) under \(P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (independent treatment noise in ACE, \cite{JinMackeySyrgkanis2025HardNormal}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:unit-treatment-variance]\label{ass:unit-treatment-variance}
\(E_P[\eta^2]=1\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (known treatment-noise variance normalization, \cite{JinMackeySyrgkanis2025HardNormal}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:uniform-envelope]\label{ass:uniform-envelope}
\(\max\{|\theta_0|,\|g_0(X)\|_\infty,\|q_0(X)\|_\infty,\|\eta\|_{\psi_2},\|\xi\|_{\psi_2}\}\le C_{\mathrm{env}}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (bounded-regression sub-Gaussian JMS envelope, \cite{JinMackeySyrgkanis2025HardNormal}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:outcome-noise-variance]\label{ass:outcome-noise-variance}
\(E_P[\xi^2\mid X,T]\ge c_{\xi}\) almost surely.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This auxiliary nondegeneracy restriction is satisfied by homoskedastic outcome innovations with positive variance and by heteroskedastic models whose conditional scale is uniformly bounded away from zero.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:gaussian-branch]\label{ass:gaussian-branch}
\(\eta\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\) under \(P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Gaussian treatment-noise branch, \cite{JinMackeySyrgkanis2025HardNormal}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:non-gaussian-branch]\label{ass:non-gaussian-branch}
\(|\kappa_4|\ge\delta_n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Shrinking fourth-cumulant separation includes smooth Gaussian--Rademacher convolutions and models treatment shocks approaching Gaussianity.
\end{assumption}

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:dml-bias]\label{lem:dml-bias}
For every \(P\in\mathcal P_n\) and fixed \((\widehat g,\widehat q)\in\mathcal H_n(P)\), \(E_P[(Y-\widehat q(X))(T-\widehat g(X))]-\theta_0=E_P[(\widehat g(X)-g_0(X))(\widehat q(X)-q_0(X))]\), whose absolute value is at most \(\varepsilon_{g,n}\varepsilon_{q,n}\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(a(X)=\widehat g(X)-g_0(X)\) and \(b(X)=\widehat q(X)-q_0(X)\).  Since \(g_0(X)=E_P[T\mid X]\) and \(\eta=T-g_0(X)\),
\[
E_P[\eta\mid X]=0.
\]
The outcome equation and outcome orthogonality give
\[
q_0(X)=E_P[Y\mid X]=\theta_0g_0(X)+f_0(X)
\quad\text{and hence}\quad
Y-q_0(X)=\theta_0\eta+\xi.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_P[(Y-\widehat q(X))(T-\widehat g(X))]
&=E_P[(\theta_0\eta+\xi-b(X))(\eta-a(X))]\\
&=\theta_0E_P[\eta^2]-\theta_0E_P[\eta a(X)]
  +E_P[\xi\eta]-E_P[\xi a(X)]\\
&\hspace{2em}-E_P[b(X)\eta]+E_P[a(X)b(X)].
\end{aligned}
\]
The three terms containing one factor \(\eta\) and a function of \(X\) vanish by \(E_P[\eta\mid X]=0\).  The two terms containing \(\xi\) vanish because \(E_P[\xi\mid X,T]=0\), while \(\eta\) and \(a(X)\) are measurable with respect to \(\sigma(X,T)\).  Finally, \(E_P[\eta^2]=1\) by the unit-variance assumption.  Thus
\[
E_P[(Y-\widehat q(X))(T-\widehat g(X))]-\theta_0=E_P[a(X)b(X)].
\]
The supplied-fit definition and monotonicity of \(L^p\)-norms on a probability space imply \(\lVert a\rVert_2\le\lVert a\rVert_8\le\varepsilon_{g,n}\) and \(\lVert b\rVert_2\le\lVert b\rVert_8\le\varepsilon_{q,n}\).  Cauchy--Schwarz therefore yields
\[
\left|E_P[a(X)b(X)]\right|
\le \lVert a\rVert_2\lVert b\rVert_2
\le\varepsilon_{g,n}\varepsilon_{q,n},
\]
which proves both assertions.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The identity makes known unit residual variance a safe unnormalized DML branch. \textit{Gap.} Normalized DML analyses estimate a denominator; this exact identity supplies the coverage-preserving fallback here. \textit{Consumer.} It gives a valid interval for analysts using slow black-box nuisance fits.

\begin{lemma}[lem:appell-identities]\label{lem:appell-identities}
For every \(P\in\mathcal P_n\) and fixed \((\widehat g,\widehat q)\in\mathcal H_n(P)\), \(E_P[A_{3,r}(r)]=0\), \(E_P[rA_{3,r}(r)]=k_{4,r}\), and \(|k_{4,r}-\kappa_4|\le8V_{\mathrm{env}}\varepsilon_{g,n}^4\). Moreover, \(|E_P[(Y-\widehat q(X)-\theta_0r)A_{3,r}(r)]|\le8V_{\mathrm{env}}(\varepsilon_{g,n}^3\varepsilon_{q,n}+\varepsilon_{g,n}^4)\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(a(X)=\widehat g(X)-g_0(X)\), \(\bar a=E_P[a(X)]\), and \(u=a(X)-\bar a\).  From \(E_P[\eta\mid X]=0\) and independence of \(\eta\) and \(X\),
\[
\mu_r=-\bar a,
\qquad r-\mu_r=\eta-u,
\qquad v_r=1+E_P[u^2].
\]
Write \(W=r-\mu_r\).  Directly from the definition of \(A_{3,r}\),
\[
E_P[A_{3,r}(r)]
=E_P[W^3]-3v_rE_P[W]-c_{3,r}=0.
\]
Moreover, since \(r=W+\mu_r\), the preceding equality gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_P[rA_{3,r}(r)]
&=E_P[W\{W^3-3v_rW-c_{3,r}\}]\\
&=E_P[W^4]-3v_r^2=k_{4,r}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Both \(\eta\) and \(-u\) are centered and independent.  Expanding the second and fourth moments of their sum cancels the mixed \(6E_P[\eta^2]E_P[u^2]\) term, so
\[
k_{4,r}=\kappa_4+E_P[u^4]-3\{E_P[u^2]\}^2.
\]
If \(a'\) is an independent copy of \(a(X)\), conditional Jensen and \(|x-y|^4\le8(|x|^4+|y|^4)\) give
\[
E_P|u|^4\le E|a-a'|^4\le16E_P|a|^4\le16\varepsilon_{g,n}^4.
\]
Hence
\[
|k_{4,r}-\kappa_4|
\le E_P[u^4]+3\{E_P[u^2]\}^2
\le4E_P[u^4]
\le64\varepsilon_{g,n}^4
\le8V_{\mathrm{env}}\varepsilon_{g,n}^4,
\]
where the last inequality uses \(V_{\mathrm{env}}=2^{16}(1+C_{\mathrm{env}})^8\ge8\).

It remains to prove the outcome-score bound.  Put \(b(X)=\widehat q(X)-q_0(X)\).  The calculation in \(\mathrm{lem:dml\mbox{-}bias}\) gives
\[
Y-\widehat q(X)-\theta_0r=\xi+\theta_0a(X)-b(X).
\]
Outcome orthogonality removes the \(\xi\)-term after multiplication by the \(\sigma(X,T)\)-measurable polynomial \(A_{3,r}(r)\).  If \(\sigma_u^2=E_P[u^2]\), independence and \(E_P\eta=0\), \(E_P\eta^2=1\) show, by expanding the cubic, that
\[
E_P[A_{3,r}(r)\mid X]
=3\sigma_u^2u-u^3+E_P[u^3]=:D(u).
\]
Minkowski gives \(\lVert u\rVert_8\le2\lVert a\rVert_8\le2\varepsilon_{g,n}\).  Therefore
\[
\lVert D(u)\rVert_{8/3}
\le3\lVert u\rVert_2^2\lVert u\rVert_{8/3}
 +\lVert u\rVert_8^3+|E_Pu^3|
\le40\varepsilon_{g,n}^3.
\]
Since \(L^{8/3}(P)\subset L^{8/7}(P)\), H\"older's inequality, \(|\theta_0|\le C_{\mathrm{env}}\), and the supplied \(L^8\) bounds yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|E_P[(Y-\widehat q(X)-\theta_0r)A_{3,r}(r)]\right|
&=\left|E_P[(\theta_0a(X)-b(X))D(u)]\right|\\
&\le40\{C_{\mathrm{env}}\varepsilon_{g,n}^4
 +\varepsilon_{g,n}^3\varepsilon_{q,n}\}\\
&\le8V_{\mathrm{env}}\{\varepsilon_{g,n}^3\varepsilon_{q,n}
 +\varepsilon_{g,n}^4\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The final inequality follows from \(C_{\mathrm{env}}\ge1\) and the displayed definition of \(V_{\mathrm{env}}\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} These identities derive the denominator and the \(B_n\) remainder for the centered, variance-corrected polynomial. \textit{Gap.} Fixed-cumulant ACE theory does not give shrinking-denominator control after all lower residual cumulants are estimated independently. \textit{Consumer.} They permit Appell inversion without treating residual mean, variance, or skewness as known.

\begin{lemma}[lem:calibration-event]\label{lem:calibration-event}
If \(n\ge\lceil8\log(32/\alpha)\rceil\), then \(b_n(\alpha/16)=\lceil8\log(32/\alpha)\rceil\) and every calibrated partition has nonempty blocks. Uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal U_n\) and fixed \((\widehat g,\widehat q)\in\mathcal H_n(P)\), \(Q_P(E_D^c)\le\alpha/4\), \(Q_P(E_K^c)\le\alpha/4\), \(Q_P(E_A^c)\le\alpha/4\), and \(Q_P(E_S^c)\le\alpha/4\); hence \(Q_P(E_D\cap E_K\cap E_A\cap E_S)\ge1-\alpha\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We first record an elementary median-of-means calculation.  Let \(W_1,\ldots,W_n\) be independent with mean \(m_W\) and standard deviation at most \(V_{\mathrm{env}}\), and use \(b=b_n(\beta)\) nonempty blocks.  Every block has size at least \(n/(2b)\).  At radius \(t=4V_{\mathrm{env}}\sqrt{b/n}\), Chebyshev's inequality makes the probability that a particular block average differs from \(m_W\) by more than \(t\) at most \(1/8\).  Independence of the blocks and the union bound over subsets of at least \(b/2\) bad blocks give
\[
\Pr\{|\operatorname{MOM}_{\beta}-m_W|>t\}
\le 2^b(1/8)^{b/2}=2^{-b/2}.
\]
If \(b\ge8\log(2/\beta)\) and \(0<\beta\le1/2\), then
\[
2^{-b/2}\le(\beta/2)^{4\log2}\le\beta^2/8\le\beta.
\tag{1}
\]
This proves the needed deviation result without an external concentration theorem.

For completeness, all variance premises in (1) follow from the stated envelope.  Under the defining convention for the Orlicz norm, Markov's inequality applied to \(\exp(\eta^2/C_{\mathrm{env}}^2)\), followed by tail integration, bounds every moment of \(\eta\) through order eight by the corresponding numerical multiple of \(C_{\mathrm{env}}^j\); the same holds for \(\xi\).  Minkowski and the supplied-fit bounds then give finite, uniform \(L^8\) bounds for
\[
r=\eta-a(X),qquad
Y-\widehat q(X)=\theta_0\eta+\xi-b(X),
\]
where \(a=\widehat g-g_0\) and \(b=\widehat q-q_0\).  H\"older's inequality consequently bounds the standard deviations of the safe score and of \(r^j\), \(1\le j\le4\), by \(V_{\mathrm{env}}\).  The deliberately conservative value \(2^{16}(1+C_{\mathrm{env}})^8\) dominates each constant obtained by these expansions.

The estimated-polynomial scores require one additional conditioning step.  Set \(R=8(1+C_{\mathrm{env}})^2\) and let
\[
F_K=\bigcap_{j=1}^4\{|\widehat m_j|\le R^j\}.
\]
The preceding moment bounds give \(\lVert r\rVert_8\le R/8\).  Markov's inequality on each block and the same bad-block count as in (1) therefore imply
\[
Q_P(F_K^c)\le4\,2^{-b_n(\alpha/16)/2}.
\tag{2}
\]
Clipping cannot enlarge this probability because \(R^j\le R^4=2^{12}(1+C_{\mathrm{env}})^8<V_{\mathrm{env}}\).  On \(F_K\),
\[
|\widehat\mu|\le R,qquad
|\widehat v|\le2R^2,qquad
|\widehat c_3|\le6R^3.
\]
Expanding \(\widehat A_3\), applying H\"older with the just-established \(L^8\) bounds, and using \(R^4=V_{\mathrm{env}}/16\), shows conditionally on \(D_K\) that the standard deviations of
\[
(Y-\widehat q(X))\widehat A_3(r)
\quad\text{and}\quad
r\widehat A_3(r)
\]
are at most \(V_{\mathrm{env}}\).  Thus (1), conditionally on \(D_K\), applies on \(F_K\).

For the safe score, \(\mathrm{lem:dml\mbox{-}bias}\) puts its mean within \(\varepsilon_{g,n}\varepsilon_{q,n}\) of \(\theta_0\); hence (1) at \(\beta=\alpha/4\) gives \(Q_P(E_D^c)\le\alpha/4\).  Applying (1) to the four raw moments and taking a union bound gives \(Q_P(E_K^c)\le\alpha/4\).  Applying conditional (1) on \(F_K\), then adding (2), proves separately \(Q_P(E_A^c)\le\alpha/4\) and \(Q_P(E_S^c)\le\alpha/4\).  Indeed, the sharper bounds in (1)--(2) give
\[
Q_P(E_D^c)+Q_P(E_K^c)+Q_P(E_A^c)+Q_P(E_S^c)
\le3\,2^{-b_n(\alpha/4)/2}+12\,2^{-b_n(\alpha/16)/2}
\le\alpha.
\]
The assumed lower bound on \(n\) makes \(b_n(\alpha/16)=\lceil8\log(32/\alpha)\rceil\); since \(b_n(\alpha/4)\le b_n(\alpha/16)\le n\), all displayed partitions are nonempty.  The union bound now proves
\[
Q_P(E_D\cap E_K\cap E_A\cap E_S)\ge1-\alpha.
\]
Every moment bound above is uniform in the indicated law and supplied-fit pair, so all conclusions are uniform over the stated union.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} It makes the simultaneous error allocation for coverage, selection, and length operational. \textit{Gap.} The closest fixed-cumulant analysis does not calibrate a union-honest pretest and inversion on independent folds. \textit{Consumer.} It yields auditable finite-sample critical radii depending only on \(C_{\mathrm{env}}\), \(n\), and \(\alpha\).

\begin{lemma}[lem:branch-selection-error]\label{lem:branch-selection-error}
There is a universal numerical choice of \(C_{\mathrm{sel}}>0\) such that, for every \(n\ge\lceil8\log(32/\alpha)\rceil\), \[\max\left\{\sup_{P\in\mathcal G_n:(\widehat g,\widehat q)\in\mathcal H_n(P)}Q_P(\phi_n=1),\ \sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n(\delta_n):(\widehat g,\widehat q)\in\mathcal H_n(P)}Q_P(\phi_n=0)\right\}\le p_{\mathrm{sel},n}=\min\left\{1,C_{\mathrm{sel}}\left[(s_n/\delta_n)^2+\{\rho_n(\alpha/16)/\delta_n\}^2\right]\right\}.\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(a=\widehat g-g_0\) and \(u=a-E_Pa\).  The cumulant expansion in \(\mathrm{lem:appell\mbox{-}identities}\) actually gives the sharper envelope
\[
|k_{4,r}-\kappa_4|
\le64\varepsilon_{g,n}^4.
\tag{3}
\]
We next control estimation rather than merely a fixed confidence event.  For a block mean with variance \(\sigma^2/m\), Chebyshev followed by the same bad-block count used in \(\mathrm{lem:calibration\mbox{-}event}\) gives, for \(t^2\ge8\sigma^2/m\),
\[
\Pr\{|\operatorname{MOM}-EW|>t\}
\le2^b\left(\frac{\sigma^2}{mt^2}\right)^{b/2}.
\tag{4}
\]
Integrating \(2t\) times (4) above its threshold and using the trivial bound below it proves, uniformly for every \(b\ge4\),
\[
E|\operatorname{MOM}-EW|^2\le C\sigma^2 b/n
\tag{5}
\]
for a numerical \(C\).  Applying (5) to the four raw residual moments, using their standard-deviation bound \(V_{\mathrm{env}}\), and expanding the displayed polynomial defining \(\widehat k_4\) yields
\[
E_{Q_P}|\widehat k_4-k_{4,r}|^2
\le C\rho_n(\alpha/16)^2.
\tag{6}
\]
To justify the polynomial step without a bounded-score assumption, split on the event \(F_K\) constructed in the calibration proof.  On \(F_K\) the polynomial map has bounded derivatives; on its complement, its clipped value is bounded and the tail estimate (4), integrated once more, has order at most the right side of (6).  The choices \(R^4=V_{\mathrm{env}}/16\) and \(b_n(\alpha/16)\ge4\) make the resulting constant numerical because all envelope dependence is already carried by \(\rho_n\).

Under \(P\in\mathcal G_n\), \(\kappa_4=0\).  Under \(P\in\mathcal N_n(\delta_n)\), \(|\kappa_4|\ge\delta_n\).  In either branch, a wrong decision implies
\[
|\widehat k_4-k_{4,r}|
\ge\delta_n/4
\]
unless \(64\varepsilon_{g,n}^4\ge\delta_n/4\).  Markov's inequality applied to (6), with the latter exceptional case absorbed by the trivial probability bound one, therefore gives
\[
\max\left\{
\sup_{P\in\mathcal G_n}Q_P(\phi_n=1),
\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n(\delta_n)}Q_P(\phi_n=0)
\right\}
\le\min\left\{1,C_{\mathrm{sel}}\left[
\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{g,n}^4}{\delta_n}\right)^2
+\left(\frac{\rho_n(\alpha/16)}{\delta_n}\right)^2
\right]\right\}.
\]
Here and above the suprema are restricted to supplied pairs in \(\mathcal H_n(P)\).  Since \(\varepsilon_{g,n}^4\le s_n\), increasing the universal numerical constant gives exactly the displayed bound by \(p_{\mathrm{sel},n}\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The explicit two-sided pretest error controls the fallback contribution to non-Gaussian expected length. \textit{Gap.} Fixed-cumulant ACE bounds do not quantify branch-selection errors for nuisance-contaminated residual cumulants at shrinking separation. \textit{Consumer.} It lets an interval implementation report a finite-sample upper bound on selecting the wrong score branch.

\begin{theorem}[thm:adaptive-upper-frontier]\label{thm:adaptive-upper-frontier}
For every \(n\ge\lceil8\log(32/\alpha)\rceil\), the explicitly calibrated \(C_n^*\) obeys \(\inf_{P\in\mathcal U_n:(\widehat g,\widehat q)\in\mathcal H_n(P)}Q_P\{\theta_0\in C_n^*\}\ge1-\alpha\), \(L_G(C_n^*)\lesssim d_n\), and the finite-sample bound \(L_N(C_n^*)\lesssim\min\{d_n,B_n/\delta_n+d_np_{\mathrm{sel},n}\}\), where \(p_{\mathrm{sel},n}\le\min\{1,C_{\mathrm{sel}}[(s_n/\delta_n)^2+\{\rho_n(\alpha/16)/\delta_n\}^2]\}\). Consequently, for fixed \(\alpha\) and \(C_{\mathrm{env}}\), if \(\delta_n/s_n\to\infty\), then \(L_N(C_n^*)\lesssim\min\{d_n,B_n/\delta_n\}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write \(a=\widehat g-g_0\), \(b=\widehat q-q_0\), and retain the natural-moment event \(F_K\) from the calibration proof.  On \(E_K\cap F_K\), direct subtraction of the raw-moment formulas, followed by the mean-value identity for polynomials, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
|\widehat\mu-\mu_r|+|\widehat v-v_r|+|\widehat c_3-c_{3,r}|
&\le C V_{\mathrm{env}}\rho_n(\alpha/16),\\
\lVert\widehat A_3-A_{3,r}\rVert_{8/3}
&\le C V_{\mathrm{env}}\rho_n(\alpha/16).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
When \(\rho_n(\alpha/16)\le1\), (7) is the ordinary Lipschitz bound on the bounded coefficient set supplied by \(F_K\).  When the radius exceeds one, the same display follows from the separate coefficient bounds on \(F_K\), after enlarging the numerical constant.  Thus there is no division by a vanishing quantity in (7).

H\"older's inequality, the uniform \(L^8\) envelope, (7), and \(\mathrm{lem:appell\mbox{-}identities}\) imply
\[
\left|E_P[(Y-\widehat q(X)-\theta_0r)\widehat A_3(r)\mid D_K]\right|
\le8V_{\mathrm{env}}\left\{
\varepsilon_{g,n}^3\varepsilon_{q,n}+\varepsilon_{g,n}^4
+\rho_n(\alpha/16)\right\}.
\tag{8}
\]
On \(E_A\), the definition of \(a_A\) and (8) give
\[
|\widehat H_A-\theta_0\widehat S_A|\le a_A,
\]
so \(\theta_0\in I_A\), using also \(|\theta_0|\le C_{\mathrm{env}}\).  On \(E_D\), \(\theta_0\in I_D\).  The sharper probability calculation in the proof of \(\mathrm{lem:calibration\mbox{-}event}\) gives
\[
Q_P(E_D\cap E_K\cap E_A\cap F_K)\ge1-\alpha.
\tag{9}
\]
On the event in (9), if \(\phi_n=0\) the reported interval is \(I_D\); if \(\phi_n=1\), both component intervals contain \(\theta_0\), their intersection is nonempty, and the reported interval contains \(\theta_0\).  This proves
\[
\inf_{P\in\mathcal U_n:(\widehat g,\widehat q)\in\mathcal H_n(P)}
Q_P\{\theta_0\in C_n^*\}\ge1-\alpha.
\]

Irrespective of the selected branch, \(C_n^*\subseteq I_D\) or \(C_n^*=I_D\).  Hence
\[
|C_n^*|\le|I_D|=2a_D
=2\{\varepsilon_{g,n}\varepsilon_{q,n}+\rho_n(\alpha/4)\}
\lesssim d_n,
\tag{10}
\]
where, for fixed \(\alpha\) and \(C_{\mathrm{env}}\), \(\rho_n(\alpha/4)\lesssim n^{-1/2}\).  Taking the Gaussian supremum in (10) proves \(L_G(C_n^*)\lesssim d_n\).

For the non-Gaussian coordinate, the same median-of-means second-moment calculation as in (4)--(6), now applied also to the slope score, combines with (3) and (7) to give
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n(\delta_n):(\widehat g,\widehat q)\in\mathcal H_n(P)}
E_{Q_P}|\widehat S_A-\kappa_4|^2\lesssim B_n^2.
\tag{11}
\]
All three terms in \(B_n\) have a declared origin: the evaluation fluctuation is \(n^{-1/2}\), the estimated-Appell outcome remainder is \(\varepsilon_{g,n}^3\varepsilon_{q,n}\), and residual-cumulant contamination is \(\varepsilon_{g,n}^4\).  Also \(a_A\lesssim B_n\).

If \(\delta_n\le cB_n\) for a sufficiently large fixed reciprocal \(c^{-1}\), (10), boundedness of \(d_n\), and \(B_n/\delta_n\ge c^{-1}\) give both the safe bound and the asserted Appell-form bound.  Suppose instead that \(\delta_n>cB_n\).  On \(\{|\widehat S_A-\kappa_4|\le\delta_n/2\}\), one has \(|\widehat S_A|\ge\delta_n/2\); the inversion definition then yields
\[
|I_A|\le\frac{2a_A}{|\widehat S_A|}\lesssim\frac{B_n}{\delta_n}.
\tag{12}
\]
By (11) and Markov's inequality, the complement of this slope event has probability \(O(B_n^2/\delta_n^2)\).  If \(\phi_n=0\), the interval has length at most the right side of (10), and \(\mathrm{lem:branch\mbox{-}selection\mbox{-}error}\) bounds this event by \(p_{\mathrm{sel},n}\).  If \(\phi_n=1\), the construction is either an intersection of length at most \(|I_A|\) or the safe interval.  Combining these cases, (10)--(12), and \(d_nB_n/\delta_n=O(1)\), gives
\[
L_N(C_n^*)
\lesssim \frac{B_n}{\delta_n}+d_np_{\mathrm{sel},n}
+d_n\frac{B_n^2}{\delta_n^2}
\lesssim \frac{B_n}{\delta_n}+d_np_{\mathrm{sel},n}.
\]
Together with the unconditional safe bound (10), this is
\[
L_N(C_n^*)\lesssim
\min\left\{d_n,\frac{B_n}{\delta_n}+d_np_{\mathrm{sel},n}\right\}.
\]

Finally fix \(\alpha\) and \(C_{\mathrm{env}}\).  Then \(\rho_n(\alpha/16)\lesssim n^{-1/2}\le s_n\), so \(\delta_n/s_n\to\infty\) implies \(p_{\mathrm{sel},n}=O((s_n/\delta_n)^2)=o(1)\).  With \(x=n^{-1/2}\), \(g=\varepsilon_{g,n}\), and \(q=\varepsilon_{q,n}\), expansion and \(0\le g,q\le C_{\mathrm{env}}\) give
\[
d_ns_n=(x+gq)(x+g^4)
\le C\{x+g^3q+g^4\}=CB_n.
\tag{13}
\]
Consequently,
\[
d_np_{\mathrm{sel},n}
\lesssim d_n(s_n/\delta_n)^2
\le C(s_n/\delta_n)(B_n/\delta_n)
\lesssim B_n/\delta_n
\]
eventually.  Substitution in the finite-sample bound proves
\[
L_N(C_n^*)\lesssim\min\{d_n,B_n/\delta_n\}.
\]
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the constructive field-tier branchwise upper frontier in a single coherent evaluation experiment. \textit{Gap.} Jin, Mackey, and Syrgkanis give separate point-estimation results with a cumulant bounded away from zero, and the sibling repository paper gives fixed-separation spectral estimation; neither gives a selected union-honest interval under shrinking separation. \textit{Consumer.} A DoubleML implementation can retain a safe interval and shorten it only after an independent residual-cumulant diagnostic.

\begin{theorem}[thm:gaussian-coordinate-lower]\label{thm:gaussian-coordinate-lower}
Fix a deterministic sequence of supplied pairs \((\widehat g,\widehat q)\), and set \(N=3n\).  Suppose that
\[
 \varepsilon_{g,n}\varepsilon_{q,n}
 =o\!\left((\log(3n))^{-1/2}\right)
\]
along the indices for which \(\varepsilon_{g,n}\varepsilon_{q,n}>0\).  Assume the following conditional-transfer compatibility condition.  There are constants \(A=A(C_{\mathrm{env}},c_\xi)>0\), \(K=K(C_{\mathrm{env}},c_\xi)\ge e(1\vee C_{\mathrm{env}})\), and \(h_0=h_0(C_{\mathrm{env}},c_\xi)>0\), independent of \(n\), with \(A^2\ge c_\xi\), such that:

\(\mathrm{CT1}\) for every sufficiently large \(n\), there are a covariate law \(P_{X,n}\) and measurable functions \(g_{*,n},q_{*,n}\) for which, for every \(|v|\le h_0\), the law \(P_{n,v}\) defined by
\[
 X\sim P_{X,n},\qquad
 T=g_{*,n}(X)+\eta,\qquad
 Y=q_{*,n}(X)+v\eta+A\zeta,
 \qquad \eta,\zeta\stackrel{\mathrm{ind}}{\sim}\mathcal N(0,1),
\]
with \((\eta,\zeta)\perp X\), belongs to \(\mathcal G_{\xi,n}\) and satisfies \((\widehat g,\widehat q)\in\mathcal H_n(P_{n,v})\);

\(\mathrm{CT2}\) whenever \(\varepsilon_{g,n}\varepsilon_{q,n}>0\), the fixed source center \((\widehat g,\widehat q/A)\) and radii
\[
 a_N=\frac{\varepsilon_{g,n}}{K},
 \qquad
 b_N=\frac{\varepsilon_{q,n}}{KA}
\]
satisfy the hypotheses of \(\textup{lem:jms-support-localized-product-benchmark}\), and the measurable outcome-rescaling map \(S_A(x,t,y)=(x,t,Ay)\) sends every law \(R\) in the two cited fuzzy supports to \(P=R\circ S_A^{-1}\in\mathcal G_{\xi,n}\) with \((\widehat g,\widehat q)\in\mathcal H_n(P)\) and \(\theta_0(P)=A\vartheta(R)\).

Then there exists \(c>0\), depending only on \(\alpha\), \(C_{\mathrm{env}}\), and \(c_\xi\), such that, for all sufficiently large \(n\), every interval \(C_n\) satisfying
\[
 \inf_{P\in\mathcal U_n:(\widehat g,\widehat q)\in\mathcal H_n(P)}
 Q_P\{\theta_0\in C_n\}\ge1-\alpha
\]
also satisfies
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal G_{\xi,n}:(\widehat g,\widehat q)\in\mathcal H_n(P)}
 E_{Q_P}|C_n|\ge c d_n^- ,
\]
and therefore \(L_G(C_n)\ge c d_n^-\).  The conditional variance restriction defining \(\mathcal G_{\xi,n}\), as well as \((\mathrm{CT1})\)--\((\mathrm{CT2})\), is confined to this Gaussian lower-bound subexperiment and is not a hypothesis of the adaptive upper-frontier theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(N=3n\).  By \(\textup{def:projected-experiment}\), concatenating the mutually independent folds \(D_K,D_D,D_A\) identifies \(Q_P\) with \(P^{\otimes N}\).  Thus every interval measurable with respect to the three folds is an interval in the \(N\)-observation experiment.

We first establish the nuisance-product lower bound on an index for which \(\varepsilon_{g,n}\varepsilon_{q,n}>0\).  Define
\[
 a_N=\frac{\varepsilon_{g,n}}{K},
 \qquad
 b_N=\frac{\varepsilon_{q,n}}{KA}.
\]
The constants \(A\) and \(K\) in \(\mathrm{CT2}\) do not depend on \(n\), so the product-smallness hypothesis in the theorem gives
\[
 a_Nb_N
 =\frac{\varepsilon_{g,n}\varepsilon_{q,n}}{K^2A}
 =o\!\left((\log N)^{-1/2}\right).
\]
Condition \(\mathrm{CT2}\) therefore permits application of \(\textup{lem:jms-support-localized-product-benchmark}\), with \(s=8\), source supplied fit \((\widehat g,\widehat q/A)\), and source radii \((a_N,b_N)\).  This invocation supplies only the nuisance-product/logarithmic mechanism from the two moment-matched fuzzy supports; no parametric term is attributed to that construction.

Let \(\widehat\theta\) be any real-valued estimator in the transformed \(N\)-observation experiment.  For source observations \(z_1,\ldots,z_N\), define
\[
 \widehat\vartheta_R(z_1,\ldots,z_N)
 =A^{-1}\widehat\theta(S_Az_1,\ldots,S_Az_N),
 \qquad S_A(x,t,y)=(x,t,Ay).
\]
Take \(\gamma=3/4\); since \(\alpha<1/2\), one has \(\gamma>\alpha\).  The cited support-localized benchmark yields \(i\in\{0,1\}\) and \(R\in\operatorname{supp}(\nu_{i,N})\) such that
\[
 R^{\otimes N}\!\left\{
 \left|\widehat\vartheta_R-\vartheta(R)\right|
 \ge c_\gamma a_Nb_N
 \left(\frac{\log(1/a_N)}{\log N}\right)^3
 \right\}\ge\gamma .
\]
Set \(P=R\circ S_A^{-1}\).  By \(\mathrm{CT2}\), this law satisfies
\[
 P\in\mathcal G_{\xi,n},
 \qquad
 (\widehat g,\widehat q)\in\mathcal H_n(P),
 \qquad
 \theta_0(P)=A\vartheta(R).
\]
Because \(S_A\) is a measurable bijection and \(Q_P=P^{\otimes N}\), the preceding probability is exactly
\[
 Q_P\!\left\{
 \left|\widehat\theta-\theta_0(P)\right|
 \ge c_\gamma A a_Nb_N
 \left(\frac{\log(1/a_N)}{\log N}\right)^3
 \right\}\ge\gamma .
\]
The use of the source radius \(b_N=\varepsilon_{q,n}/(KA)\) before the pushforward is essential, and it gives
\[
 A a_Nb_N
 =\frac{\varepsilon_{g,n}\varepsilon_{q,n}}{K^2}.
\]

Let \(\ell_n=1\vee\log(1/\varepsilon_{g,n})\).  On the present positive-product index, \(0<\varepsilon_{g,n}\le C_{\mathrm{env}}\).  Since \(K\ge e(1\vee C_{\mathrm{env}})\),
\[
 \log(1/a_N)=\log(K/\varepsilon_{g,n})\ge\ell_n.
\]
There is a universal constant \(c_0\ge1\) such that \(\log(3n)\le c_0\log(en)\) for every \(n\ge1\).  Consequently, using \(\textup{def:rate-scales}\),
\[
 A a_Nb_N
 \left(\frac{\log(1/a_N)}{\log N}\right)^3
 \ge c_1\varepsilon_{g,n}\varepsilon_{q,n}
 \min\!\left\{1,
 \left(\frac{\ell_n}{\log(en)}\right)^3\right\}
 =c_1\varepsilon_{g,n}\varepsilon_{q,n}\Lambda_n,
\]
where \(c_1>0\) is independent of \(n\).  Hence every estimator has error at least \(c_2\varepsilon_{g,n}\varepsilon_{q,n}\Lambda_n\), with probability at least \(\gamma\), at some law in the transferred fuzzy support.

By \(\mathrm{CT2}\), every law in that support belongs to \(\mathcal G_{\xi,n}\), is compatible with the supplied pair, and hence belongs to \(\mathcal U_n\) by \(\textup{def:gaussian-nondegenerate-subclass}\) and \(\textup{def:honesty-union}\).  The assumed union honesty of \(C_n\) therefore implies honesty over this transferred support.  Apply \(\textup{lem:honest-interval-length-reduction}\) with \(r=c_2\varepsilon_{g,n}\varepsilon_{q,n}\Lambda_n\), \(\beta=\gamma\), and this support as \(\mathscr R\).  Since \(\gamma-\alpha>0\), there is \(c_3>0\), independent of \(n\), such that
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal G_{\xi,n}:(\widehat g,\widehat q)\in\mathcal H_n(P)}
 E_{Q_P}|C_n|
 \ge c_3\varepsilon_{g,n}\varepsilon_{q,n}\Lambda_n.
\]
If \(\varepsilon_{g,n}\varepsilon_{q,n}=0\), the same inequality is automatic because its right-hand side is zero; no fuzzy-support invocation is then needed.

We now obtain the parametric term by a separate compatible two-point experiment.  Set
\[
 \beta=\frac{\alpha+1/2}{2},
 \qquad \alpha<\beta<\frac12.
\]
Use the covariate law \(P_{X,n}\) and functions \(g_{*,n},q_{*,n}\) furnished by \(\mathrm{CT1}\).  For \(j\in\{0,1\}\), put
\[
 v_0=0,
 \qquad
 v_1=\Delta=\frac{h}{\sqrt N},
 \qquad
 P_j=P_{n,v_j},
\]
where \(h>0\) will be fixed independently of \(n\).  For all sufficiently large \(n\), \(\Delta\le h_0\), so \(\mathrm{CT1}\) gives
\[
 P_j\in\mathcal G_{\xi,n},
 \qquad
 (\widehat g,\widehat q)\in\mathcal H_n(P_j).
\]
Under \(P_j\), the defining equations in \(\mathrm{CT1}\) can be rewritten as
\[
 Y=v_jT+\{q_{*,n}(X)-v_jg_{*,n}(X)\}+A\zeta.
\]
Thus the partially linear coefficient is \(\theta_0(P_j)=v_j\), the structural nuisance is \(f_{0,j}=q_{*,n}-v_jg_{*,n}\), and the outcome disturbance is \(\xi_j=A\zeta\).  In particular, independence of \(\zeta\) from \((X,\eta)\), together with \(T=g_{*,n}(X)+\eta\), gives
\[
 E_{P_j}[\xi_j\mid X,T]=0,
 \qquad
 E_{P_j}[\xi_j^2\mid X,T]=A^2\ge c_\xi.
\]
The remaining membership conditions are already part of \(\mathrm{CT1}\).

Under \(P_0\), conditional division of the two Gaussian outcome densities gives the one-observation likelihood ratio
\[
 L=\frac{dP_1}{dP_0}
 =\exp\!\left\{
 \frac{\Delta\eta\{Y-q_{*,n}(X)\}}{A^2}
 -\frac{\Delta^2\eta^2}{2A^2}
 \right\}.
\]
Under \(P_0\), \(\{Y-q_{*,n}(X)\}/A=\zeta\) is standard Gaussian and independent of \(\eta\).  The standard-normal moment-generating identity therefore yields
\[
 E_{P_0}[L^2\mid X,\eta]
 =\exp(\Delta^2\eta^2/A^2).
\]
Since \(\eta\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\), direct Gaussian integration gives
\[
 E_{P_0}[L^2]
 =\left(1-\frac{2\Delta^2}{A^2}\right)^{-1/2}.
\]
Independence of the \(N\) observations now implies
\[
 \chi^2\!\left(P_1^{\otimes N},P_0^{\otimes N}\right)
 =\left(1-\frac{2h^2}{A^2N}\right)^{-N/2}-1.
\]
Choose \(h\le A/2\).  Then \(x=2h^2/(A^2N)\le1/2\), and the elementary inequality \(-\log(1-x)\le2x\), valid for \(0\le x\le1/2\), gives
\[
 \chi^2\!\left(P_1^{\otimes N},P_0^{\otimes N}\right)
 \le \exp(2h^2/A^2)-1.
\]
Reduce \(h>0\), if necessary, so that it also satisfies
\[
 \exp(2h^2/A^2)-1\le4(1-2\beta)^2.
\]
This choice depends only on \(\alpha\), \(A\), and the fixed feasibility radius \(h_0\).  By the definition of total variation and the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality,
\[
 \left\|P_1^{\otimes N}-P_0^{\otimes N}\right\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 =\frac12E_{P_0^{\otimes N}}|L_N-1|
 \le\frac12\sqrt{\chi^2(P_1^{\otimes N},P_0^{\otimes N})}
 \le1-2\beta,
\]
where \(L_N=dP_1^{\otimes N}/dP_0^{\otimes N}\).

For an arbitrary estimator \(\widehat\theta\), define the disjoint events
\[
 A_j=\{|\widehat\theta-v_j|<\Delta/2\},
 \qquad j\in\{0,1\}.
\]
Their disjointness and the defining variational inequality imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P_0^{\otimes N}(A_0)+P_1^{\otimes N}(A_1)
 &\le P_0^{\otimes N}(A_0)+P_0^{\otimes N}(A_1)
   +\left\|P_1^{\otimes N}-P_0^{\otimes N}\right\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\\
 &\le1+\left\|P_1^{\otimes N}-P_0^{\otimes N}\right\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
It follows that
\[
 \max_{j\in\{0,1\}}
 P_j^{\otimes N}\!\left\{
 |\widehat\theta-v_j|\ge\frac{h}{2\sqrt N}
 \right\}
 \ge\frac{1-\|P_1^{\otimes N}-P_0^{\otimes N}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}}{2}
 \ge\beta.
\]
The two laws lie in the honesty union and are compatible with the supplied fits by \(\mathrm{CT1}\).  Applying \(\textup{lem:honest-interval-length-reduction}\) with \(\mathscr R=\{P_0,P_1\}\) and \(r=h/(2\sqrt N)\) yields a constant \(c_4>0\) such that
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal G_{\xi,n}:(\widehat g,\widehat q)\in\mathcal H_n(P)}
 E_{Q_P}|C_n|
 \ge c_4N^{-1/2}
 \ge c_5n^{-1/2}.
\]

The same supremum is bounded below by the parametric term and by the nuisance-product/logarithmic term.  Using \(\max\{x,y\}\ge(x+y)/2\) and \(\textup{def:rate-scales}\), we conclude that
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal G_{\xi,n}:(\widehat g,\widehat q)\in\mathcal H_n(P)}
 E_{Q_P}|C_n|
 \ge c\left\{n^{-1/2}
 +\varepsilon_{g,n}\varepsilon_{q,n}\Lambda_n\right\}
 =c d_n^-.
\]
Here \(c>0\) depends only on \(\alpha\), \(C_{\mathrm{env}}\), and \(c_\xi\), because \(A,K,h_0\) have precisely that dependence in \(\mathrm{CT1}\)--\(\mathrm{CT2}\).  Finally, \(\mathcal G_{\xi,n}\subseteq\mathcal G_n\) and \(\textup{def:length-vector}\) imply \(L_G(C_n)\ge c d_n^-\).  The argument uses the conditional outcome-variance restriction and \(\mathrm{CT1}\)--\(\mathrm{CT2}\) only to form these Gaussian lower-bound subexperiments; neither condition is asserted for the adaptive upper-frontier theorem.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem records the source-supported Gaussian coordinate on transfer-compatible supplied-fit sequences, with the fuzzy nuisance-product/log and parametric two-point mechanisms proved separately. \textit{Gap.} The result remains conditional on exact support compatibility and retains \(\Lambda_n\); the frozen core does not prove either unconditional transfer for every supplied fit or the full \(d_n\) coordinate. \textit{Consumer.} On its stated subexperiment it rules out Gaussian-coordinate expected length below \(d_n^-\), without asserting a global union-supremum improvement or a matching vector frontier.

\begin{openendedquestion}[oeq:remove-gaussian-log]\label{oeq:remove-gaussian-log}
Can the multiscale Gaussian-prior handle prove \(L_G(C_n)\gtrsim d_n\) for every interval honest over \(\mathcal G_n\)? Success requires Gaussian-branch projected mixtures with bounded chi-square distance and \(\theta_0\)-separation \(c\varepsilon_{g,n}\varepsilon_{q,n}\) whenever \(\varepsilon_{g,n}\varepsilon_{q,n}\gg n^{-1/2}\), without a logarithmic restriction on the radii.
\end{openendedquestion}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} It isolates the precise criterion for upgrading \(d_n^-\) to \(d_n\). \textit{Gap.} The published Gaussian lower experiment retains \(\Lambda_n\), and no cited result removes it on this class. \textit{Consumer.} A positive answer closes the only logarithmic gap in the Gaussian coordinate.

\begin{proposition}[prop:sufficient-shortening]\label{prop:sufficient-shortening}
If \(B_n/\delta_n=o(d_n)\), then \(\delta_n/s_n\to\infty\), \(L_N(C_n^*)=o(d_n)\), and \(L_G(C_n^*)\lesssim d_n\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Assume \(B_n/\delta_n=o(d_n)\), equivalently \(\delta_nd_n/B_n\to\infty\).  Inequality (13) from the proof of the adaptive upper theorem is purely algebraic and says \(d_ns_n\le CB_n\).  Since all three scales are positive,
\[
\frac{\delta_n}{s_n}
\ge\frac{\delta_nd_n}{CB_n}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
The asymptotic clause of \(\mathrm{thm:adaptive\mbox{-}upper\mbox{-}frontier}\) is therefore applicable and gives
\[
L_N(C_n^*)\lesssim\min\{d_n,B_n/\delta_n\}=o(d_n).
\]
The same theorem gives \(L_G(C_n^*)\lesssim d_n\).  This establishes all three conclusions without strengthening the law class or the nuisance-radius conditions.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} It isolates the constructive criterion for strict non-Gaussian shortening. \textit{Gap.} Fixed-separation ACE does not state a shrinking-signal condition for honest expected-length improvement. \textit{Consumer.} It gives analysts a conservative threshold for deploying the Appell inversion.

\begin{proposition}[prop:representative-regimes]\label{prop:representative-regimes}
Fix \(\alpha\in(0,1/2)\). If \(\varepsilon_{g,n}=\varepsilon_{q,n}=\delta_n=n^{-1/8}\), then eventually \(n\ge\lceil8\log(32/\alpha)\rceil\), \(b_n(\alpha/16)=\lceil8\log(32/\alpha)\rceil\), \(\rho_n(\alpha/16)=O(n^{-1/2})\), \(p_{\mathrm{sel},n}=O(n^{-3/4})\), \(d_n\asymp d_n^-\asymp n^{-1/4}\), \(s_n\asymp B_n\asymp n^{-1/2}\), and \((L_G(C_n^*),L_N(C_n^*))=(O(n^{-1/4}),O(n^{-3/8}))\). If \(\varepsilon_{g,n}=0\) and \(\delta_n\to0\), then \(\Lambda_n=1\), \(d_n^-=d_n=B_n=n^{-1/2}\), and no rate shortening occurs.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
First take \(\varepsilon_{g,n}=\varepsilon_{q,n}=\delta_n=n^{-1/8}\).  For fixed \(\alpha\), the deterministic threshold \(\lceil8\log(32/\alpha)\rceil\) is eventually below \(n\), so
\[
b_n(\alpha/16)=\lceil8\log(32/\alpha)\rceil,
\qquad \rho_n(\alpha/16)=O(n^{-1/2}).
\]
Substitution into the rate definitions gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
d_n&=n^{-1/2}+n^{-1/4}\asymp n^{-1/4},\\
s_n&=n^{-1/2}+n^{-1/2}\asymp n^{-1/2},\\
B_n&=n^{-1/2}+n^{-1/2}+n^{-1/2}\asymp n^{-1/2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Furthermore,
\[
\Lambda_n
=\min\left\{1,
\left[\frac{1\vee(\log n)/8}{\log(en)}\right]^3\right\}
\longrightarrow8^{-3},
\]
and hence \(d_n^-=n^{-1/2}+n^{-1/4}\Lambda_n\asymp n^{-1/4}\).  Both squared ratios in the selection envelope have order
\[
\left(\frac{n^{-1/2}}{n^{-1/8}}\right)^2=n^{-3/4},
\]
so \(p_{\mathrm{sel},n}=O(n^{-3/4})\).  Finally \(B_n/\delta_n\asymp n^{-3/8}=o(n^{-1/4})\).  The adaptive upper theorem and the sufficient-shortening proposition therefore yield
\[
\bigl(L_G(C_n^*),L_N(C_n^*)\bigr)
=\bigl(O(n^{-1/4}),O(n^{-3/8})\bigr).
\]

If \(\varepsilon_{g,n}=0\), the definitions instead give
\[
\Lambda_n=1,qquad
d_n^-=d_n=B_n=n^{-1/2},
\qquad s_n=n^{-1/2}.
\]
Because \(0<\delta_n\le1\), \(B_n/\delta_n\ge d_n\).  Thus the minimum in the adaptive upper bound remains the safe \(n^{-1/2}\) scale; in this perfect-treatment-fit benchmark the advertised branchwise rate map has no shortening, including when \(\delta_n\to0\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The reductions verify a genuine gain and the perfect-treatment-fit benchmark. \textit{Gap.} No comparator reports these shrinking-cumulant calculations for one union-honest interval. \textit{Consumer.} They give calibrated simulation and software checks.

\begin{lemma}[lem:honest-interval-length-reduction]\label{lem:honest-interval-length-reduction}
Let \(\mathscr R\) be a collection of laws for an observation \(W\), let \(\vartheta:\mathscr R\to\mathbb R\), and let \(r>0\) and \(0\le\alpha<\beta\le1\).  Suppose that for every real-valued estimator \(\widehat\vartheta(W)\) there is an \(R\in\mathscr R\) such that
\[
 R\{|\widehat\vartheta-\vartheta(R)|\ge r\}\ge\beta.
\]
Then every random interval \(C(W)\) satisfying
\(\inf_{R\in\mathscr R}R\{\vartheta(R)\in C\}\ge1-\alpha\) obeys
\[
 \sup_{R\in\mathscr R}E_R|C|\ge2r(\beta-\alpha).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If \(C\) is unbounded with positive probability under some \(R\), the asserted lower bound is immediate.  Otherwise write its endpoints as \(\ell(W)\le u(W)\), put \(|C|=u-\ell\), and define its midpoint estimator by \(m(W)=(\ell+u)/2\), with an arbitrary value on the event that \(C\) is empty.  By the assumed testing property applied to \(m\), there is \(R_*\in\mathscr R\) for which
\[
 R_*\{|m-\vartheta(R_*)|\ge r\}\ge\beta.
\]
On the event \(\{\vartheta(R_*)\in C\}\), the interval geometry gives
\[
 |m-\vartheta(R_*)|\le \frac{|C|}{2}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \{|m-\vartheta(R_*)|\ge r\}\cap
 \{\vartheta(R_*)\in C\}
 \subseteq \{|C|\ge2r\}.
\]
Honesty and the union bound therefore imply
\[
 R_*\{|C|\ge2r\}
 \ge \beta-R_*\{\vartheta(R_*)\notin C\}
 \ge\beta-\alpha.
\]
Integrating the nonnegative random variable \(|C|\) yields
\[
 E_{R_*}|C|\ge2rR_*\{|C|\ge2r\}
 \ge2r(\beta-\alpha),
\]
which proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:jms-support-localized-product-benchmark]\label{lem:jms-support-localized-product-benchmark}
Fix \(\gamma\in(1/2,1)\), \(1\le s\le\infty\), and constants \(C_\theta,C_g,C_q>0\).  Consider the Gaussian-treatment partially linear construction (44) of Jin, Mackey, and Syrgkanis with treatment-noise variance one, a fixed supplied center \(\bar h_N=(\bar g_N,\bar q_N)\) satisfying \(\|\bar g_N\|_\infty\le C_g\) and \(\|\bar q_N\|_\infty\le C_q\), and nuisance radii \(a_N,b_N>0\).  Let \(\mathfrak S_N(a_N,b_N;\bar h_N)\) be the particular support of laws \(R_{\lambda,\rho}\) generated in (44): each such law obeys
\[
 T=g_R(X)+\eta,\qquad
 Y=\vartheta(R)T+f_R(X)+\xi,\qquad
 \eta\mid X\sim\mathcal N(0,1),\qquad
 E_R[\xi\mid X,T]=0,
\]
has \(|\vartheta(R)|\le C_\theta\), \(\|g_R\|_\infty\le C_g\), and \(\|q_R\|_\infty\le C_q\), where \(q_R(x)=E_R[Y\mid X=x]\), and satisfies
\[
 \|g_R-\bar g_N\|_{L^s(R_X)}\le a_N,
 \qquad
 \|q_R-\bar q_N\|_{L^s(R_X)}\le b_N.
\]
Assume that the bounded-center recentering described immediately before construction (44) makes this support well defined.  If \(a_Nb_N=o((\log N)^{-1/2})\), the two moment-matched priors \(\nu_{0,N},\nu_{1,N}\) used in Appendix D are supported on \(\mathfrak S_N(a_N,b_N;\bar h_N)\) and have the following support-localized consequence: for every estimator \(\widehat\vartheta\) based on \(N\) observations, for all sufficiently large \(N\), there are \(i\in\{0,1\}\) and \(R\in\operatorname{supp}(\nu_{i,N})\) such that

 R^{\otimes N}\!\left\{

 \ge c_\gamma a_Nb_N
 \left(\frac{\log(1/a_N)}{\log N}\right)^3

\]
This is only the nuisance-product conclusion obtained from their fuzzy supports in equation (56); it does not include the separate \(N^{-1/2}\) mechanism of their equations (57)--(58).
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[thm:no-cross-branch-bounded-chi-square]\label{thm:no-cross-branch-bounded-chi-square}
Fix \(\alpha\in(0,1/2)\) and \(C_{\mathrm{env}}\), and suppose \(\delta_n/s_n\to\infty\).  For each \(n\), let \(\Pi_{G,n}\) and \(\Pi_{N,n}\) be arbitrary priors supported respectively on \(\mathcal G_n\) and \(\mathcal N_n(\delta_n)\).  Suppose one deterministic supplied pair \((\widehat g,\widehat q)\) belongs to \(\mathcal H_n(P)\) for every law in the union of the two supports, and define the projected-experiment mixtures
\[
 \overline Q_{G,n}=\int Q_P\,d\Pi_{G,n}(P),
 \qquad
 \overline Q_{N,n}=\int Q_P\,d\Pi_{N,n}(P).
\]
Then \(p_{\mathrm{sel},n}=o(1)\), and, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 \|\overline Q_{N,n}-\overline Q_{G,n}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \ge1-2p_{\mathrm{sel},n},
\]
\[
 \min\left\{
 \chi^2(\overline Q_{N,n},\overline Q_{G,n}),
 \chi^2(\overline Q_{G,n},\overline Q_{N,n})
 \right\}
 \ge \frac{(1-p_{\mathrm{sel},n})^2}{p_{\mathrm{sel},n}}-1
 \longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Consequently, in the regime \(\delta_n/s_n\to\infty\), no Rademacher-partition exponential-Hermite construction, nor any other construction with the stated branch support and common supplied fits, can have uniformly bounded chi-square distance between its Gaussian and non-Gaussian projected mixtures.  Thus the conjunctive construction question has a negative answer.  This result makes no claim about existence of such a pair when \(\delta_n\lesssim s_n\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(\mathcal E_n=\{\phi_n=1\}\), where \(\phi_n\) is the split residual-cumulant test in \(\textup{def:cumulant-test}\).  Lemma \(\textup{lem:branch-selection-error}\) and the common supplied-fit condition give, for every law in the Gaussian prior support,
\[
 Q_P(\mathcal E_n)\le p_{\mathrm{sel},n},
\]
and, for every law in the non-Gaussian prior support,
\[
 Q_P(\mathcal E_n^c)\le p_{\mathrm{sel},n}.
\]
Integrating the first inequality with respect to \(\Pi_{G,n}\) and the second with respect to \(\Pi_{N,n}\) yields
\[
 \overline Q_{G,n}(\mathcal E_n)\le p_{\mathrm{sel},n},
 \qquad
 \overline Q_{N,n}(\mathcal E_n^c)\le p_{\mathrm{sel},n}.
\tag{1}
\]

For fixed \(\alpha\) and \(C_{\mathrm{env}}\), the explicit median-of-means calibration in \(\textup{def:mom-calibration}\) gives
\[
 \rho_n(\alpha/16)=O(n^{-1/2}).
\]
Definition \(\textup{def:rate-scales}\) gives \(s_n\ge n^{-1/2}\).  Therefore \(\textup{def:selection-error-envelope}\) implies, for a constant depending only on the fixed envelope and confidence level,
\[
 p_{\mathrm{sel},n}
 \le C\left\{
 \left(\frac{s_n}{\delta_n}\right)^2
 + \left(\frac{n^{-1/2}}{\delta_n}\right)^2
 \right\}
 \le 2C\left(\frac{s_n}{\delta_n}\right)^2
 =o(1).
\tag{2}
\]
In particular, \(0<p_{\mathrm{sel},n}<1\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Apply \(\textup{lem:mixture-test-chi-square-certificate}\) to (1), with \(\mathsf M_0=\overline Q_{G,n}\), \(\mathsf M_1=\overline Q_{N,n}\), \(\psi=\phi_n\), and \(e=p_{\mathrm{sel},n}\).  It gives both asserted divergence inequalities.  Equation (2) makes their common lower bound diverge.  The proof did not use the partition size, the form of the nuisance priors, a Hermite expansion, or the target displacement.  It therefore supplies an analytic infeasibility certificate for every finite Rademacher-partition program satisfying the requested support, common-fit, and bounded-chi-square constraints in the strongly separated regime.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:cross-fuzzy-does-not-close-frontier]\label{thm:cross-fuzzy-does-not-close-frontier}
Fix \(\alpha\in(0,1/2)\) and \(C_{\mathrm{env}}\).  A cross-branch fuzzy-hypothesis argument has the following exact branchwise content.  Let \(r_n>0\) be any deterministic sequence.  Suppose \(\Pi_{G,n}\) and \(\Pi_{N,n}\) have the support and common-fit properties in \(\textup{thm:no-cross-branch-bounded-chi-square}\), write \(\overline Q_{G,n}=\int Q_P\,d\Pi_{G,n}(P)\) and \(\overline Q_{N,n}=\int Q_P\,d\Pi_{N,n}(P)\), and suppose their target values lie in deterministic intervals \(J_{G,n}\) and \(J_{N,n}\) whose distance is at least \(c_0r_n\), where \(c_0>0\).  Every interval \(C_n\) honest over \(\mathcal U_n\) then satisfies
\[
 \min\{L_G(C_n),L_N(C_n)\}
 \ge c_0r_n
 \left[1-2\alpha-|\overline Q_{N,n}-\overline Q_{G,n}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\right]_+.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus, in a regime where a separate construction gives total variation at most a fixed \(\tau<1-2\alpha\) and \(r_n\asymp d_n^-\), equation (3) gives the desired non-Gaussian lower bound of order \(d_n^-\), without needing the side condition \(L_G(C_n)\lesssim d_n\).  In contrast, when \(\delta_n/s_n\to\infty\), every cross-branch pair with the required support and common fits has total variation at least \(1-2p_{\mathrm{sel},n}\to1\) and chi-square divergence tending to infinity in both directions.  Hence the bounded-chi-square Gaussian-versus-non-Gaussian fuzzy-Hermite handle cannot imply
\[
 L_N(C_n)\gtrsim\min\{d_n^-,B_n/\delta_n\}
\]
in that regime, even after restricting attention to intervals with \(L_G(C_n)\lesssim d_n\).  The asserted strong-separation lower face of \(\mathcal A_n\), and therefore the claimed necessity of \(B_n/\delta_n=o(d_n)\) for strict shortening, requires a different lower-bound experiment, such as two target-separated priors both supported inside \(\mathcal N_n(\delta_n)\), or a quantitatively explicit constrained-risk argument allowing diverging cross-branch divergence.  It is not a consequence of the stipulated cross-branch bounded-chi-square construction.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Because every law in the two prior supports belongs to \(\mathcal U_n\) and is compatible with the common supplied pair, union honesty gives
\[
 Q_P\{\theta_0(P)\in C_n\}\ge1-\alpha
\]
on both supports.  Apply \(\textup{lem:two-mixture-interval-transfer}\) with \(\mathcal P_0=\mathcal G_n\), \(\mathcal P_1=\mathcal N_n(\delta_n)\), \(\vartheta(P)=\theta_0(P)\), and \(r=c_0r_n\).  The lemma bounds the prior-average expected length on each branch by the right side of (3).  Definition \(\textup{def:length-vector}\) makes each branch supremum at least its corresponding prior average, proving (3).

If the mixture total variation is at most \(\tau<1-2\alpha\), the bracket in (3) is at least \(1-2\alpha-\tau>0\).  Taking \(r_n\asymp d_n^-\) therefore proves the stated conditional weak-scale consequence.  Notice that this calculation uses only honesty, target-band separation, and mixture closeness; no upper restriction on \(L_G(C_n)\) is used.

Now suppose \(\delta_n/s_n\to\infty\).  Theorem \(\textup{thm:no-cross-branch-bounded-chi-square}\) gives
\[
 \|\overline Q_{N,n}-\overline Q_{G,n}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \ge1-2p_{\mathrm{sel},n}\longrightarrow1
\]
and divergence of both chi-square orientations.  Hence the bounded-chi-square premise requested by \(\textup{def:fuzzy-hermite-handle}\) is impossible in precisely the regime where the proposed second branch of the frontier is meant to apply.  The additional restriction \(L_G(C_n)\lesssim d_n\) concerns a property of the interval and cannot alter this incompatibility between the two mixture laws.  Consequently the stipulated construction proves neither the strong-separation lower bound nor its claimed shortening necessity.  A within-\(\mathcal N_n(\delta_n)\) experiment is not ruled out by the branch selector, because both of its priors induce the same selector label; establishing such an experiment is a separate mathematical task.  This proves the negative answer and the exact conditional statement in (3).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:mixture-test-chi-square-certificate]\label{lem:mixture-test-chi-square-certificate}
Let \(\mathsf M_0\) and \(\mathsf M_1\) be probability measures on one measurable space, and let \(\psi\) be a measurable \(\{0,1\}\)-valued test.  If, for some \(e\in[0,1)\),
\[
 \mathsf M_0\{\psi=1\}\le e,
 \qquad
 \mathsf M_1\{\psi=0\}\le e,
\]
then
\[
 \|\mathsf M_1-\mathsf M_0\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\ge 1-2e.
\]
If \(0<e<1\), then, with chi-square divergence defined to be \(+\infty\) in the absence of the indicated absolute continuity,
\[
 \chi^2(\mathsf M_1,\mathsf M_0)
 \ge \frac{(1-e)^2}{e}-1,
 \qquad
 \chi^2(\mathsf M_0,\mathsf M_1)
 \ge \frac{(1-e)^2}{e}-1.
\]
For \(e=0\), both displayed chi-square divergences are \(+\infty\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(A=\{\psi=1\}\).  The two error inequalities give
\[
 \mathsf M_1(A)-\mathsf M_0(A)
 \ge (1-e)-e=1-2e.
\]
The definition of total variation as the supremum of event-probability differences therefore proves
\[
 \|\mathsf M_1-\mathsf M_0\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\ge1-2e.
\]

Suppose first that \(0<e<1\) and \(\mathsf M_1\ll\mathsf M_0\), and write \(L=d\mathsf M_1/d\mathsf M_0\).  Cauchy--Schwarz, the bound \(\mathsf M_0(A)\le e\), and \(\int L^2\,d\mathsf M_0=1+\chi^2(\mathsf M_1,\mathsf M_0)\) yield
\[
 1-e
 \le \mathsf M_1(A)
 =\int_A L\,d\mathsf M_0
 \le \left\{e\bigl(1+\chi^2(\mathsf M_1,\mathsf M_0)\bigr)\right\}^{1/2}.
\]
Squaring and rearranging gives the first chi-square bound.  If absolute continuity fails, that bound holds by the stated \(+\infty\) convention.  Apply the same argument with the measures interchanged and with \(A^c=\{\psi=0\}\): now \(\mathsf M_1(A^c)\le e\) and \(\mathsf M_0(A^c)\ge1-e\), which proves the reverse chi-square bound.  Finally, when \(e=0\), \(\mathsf M_0(A)=0\) and \(\mathsf M_1(A)=1\), while \(\mathsf M_1(A^c)=0\) and \(\mathsf M_0(A^c)=1\).  Absolute continuity fails in both directions, so both divergences equal \(+\infty\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:two-mixture-interval-transfer]\label{lem:two-mixture-interval-transfer}
Let \(\mathcal P_0\) and \(\mathcal P_1\) be two classes of laws for a common observation, let \(\vartheta\) be a real parameter, fix \(\alpha\in[0,1/2)\), and let \(\Pi_0\) and \(\Pi_1\) be priors supported on the respective classes.  Write \(\mathsf M_j=\int R\,d\Pi_j(R)\) for the induced observation mixture.  Suppose that deterministic intervals \(J_0,J_1\subset\mathbb R\) satisfy \(\vartheta(R)\in J_j\) for every \(R\in\operatorname{supp}(\Pi_j)\), and put
\[
 r=\inf\{|u-v|:u\in J_0,\ v\in J_1\}>0.
\]
If a random interval \(C\) obeys \(R\{\vartheta(R)\in C\}\ge1-\alpha\) on both prior supports, then, for \(j=0,1\),
\[
 \sup_{R\in\mathcal P_j}E_R|C|
 \ge
 r\left[1-2\alpha-\|\mathsf M_1-\mathsf M_0\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\right]_+.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For \(j=0,1\), let \(E_j=\{C\cap J_j\ne\varnothing\}\).  If \(R\in\operatorname{supp}(\Pi_j)\), then \(\vartheta(R)\in J_j\), so
\[
 \{\vartheta(R)\in C\}\subseteq E_j.
\]
The assumed coverage and integration with respect to \(\Pi_j\) therefore give \(\mathsf M_j(E_j)\ge1-\alpha\).  Total variation transfers the other event to mixture \(\mathsf M_1\):
\[
 \mathsf M_1(E_0)
 \ge \mathsf M_0(E_0)-\|\mathsf M_1-\mathsf M_0\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \ge1-\alpha-\|\mathsf M_1-\mathsf M_0\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\]
The union bound now implies
\[
 \mathsf M_1(E_0\cap E_1)
 \ge1-2\alpha-\|\mathsf M_1-\mathsf M_0\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\]
On \(E_0\cap E_1\), the interval \(C\) meets both \(J_0\) and \(J_1\).  By the definition of \(r\), this forces \(|C|\ge r\).  Hence
\[
 \int E_R|C|\,d\Pi_1(R)
 =E_{\mathsf M_1}|C|
 \ge r\left[1-2\alpha-\|\mathsf M_1-\mathsf M_0\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\right]_+.
\]
The supremum over \(\mathcal P_1\) is at least this prior average.  Interchanging indices \(0\) and \(1\) proves the same conclusion for \(\mathcal P_0\).  If \(C\) is unbounded with positive probability, the corresponding expectation is infinite and the conclusion remains valid.
\end{proof}

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The certified Gaussian lower coordinate is \(d_n^-\), not \(d_n\), and is conditional on two explicit compatibility requirements: transfer of the support-localized JMS fuzzy priors after outcome rescaling, and existence of a nonempty Gaussian two-point path around a compatible anchor. The cited localized fuzzy support supplies only the nuisance-product/log term; the parametric term is proved separately inside the paper and the two bounds are combined only afterward. The literal bounded-\(Y\) statement of JMS Theorem 3.2 cannot provide a direct full-class inclusion, because full-support Gaussian treatment plus conditional outcome orthogonality makes that bounded-outcome class slope-degenerate, and its Appendix D parametric witness instead has unbounded Gaussian outcome. The auxiliary subclass \(\mathcal G_{\xi,n}\) is used only for this converse. The upper theorem needs none of these lower-experiment restrictions and does not identify a matching Pareto boundary. Its finite-sample guarantee starts at \(n=\lceil8\log(32/\alpha)\rceil\), when every requested calibration block count is available. The early bounded-chi-square cross-branch Rademacher/Hermite route is resolved negatively at strong separation because the common-fit selector makes the mixtures asymptotically singular; a within-non-Gaussian or constrained-risk experiment is still required for the actual lower frontier.

\section{Honest scope}

The law class is primitive and contains no learner-performance condition. All upper-frontier inference is conditional on fixed supplied fits and uniform over their radius set. The Appell polynomial estimates the first four raw residual moments on a fold independent of inversion. Every median-of-means operator partitions the sample into \(b_n(\beta)=\min\{n,\lceil8\log(2/\beta)\rceil\}\) nonempty blocks, and the upper theorem assumes \(n\ge\lceil8\log(32/\alpha)\rceil\). Four named events receive error \(\alpha/4\) each, while the separate bound \(p_{\mathrm{sel},n}\) quantifies wrong-branch selection. The Gaussian lower theorem is explicitly conditional: for positive nuisance-product indices, only the source-localized fuzzy supports are invoked, with treatment radius \(\varepsilon_{g,n}/K\), outcome fit \(\widehat q/A\), and outcome radius \(\varepsilon_{q,n}/(KA)\), followed by the pushforward \(S_A(x,t,y)=(x,t,Ay)\). A separate compatible two-point path supplies \(n^{-1/2}\). No direct inclusion of the literal JMS Theorem 3.2 class is asserted, because its bounded-outcome condition excludes the unbounded Gaussian-outcome root-\(N\) witness and is slope-degenerate under the present orthogonality condition. The novel restriction \(E_P[\xi^2\mid X,T]\ge c_{\xi}\) defines only \(\mathcal G_{\xi,n}\) and is used only in that converse. The sibling repository paper settles fixed-separation spectral estimation, not this local-to-Gaussian union-honest length problem. The branchwise converse and removal of \(\Lambda_n\) are open. No matching Pareto boundary or improvement of global union-supremum expected length is claimed.
