%%% FIELD proof-dml-bias
Put \(a(X)=\widehat g(X)-g_0(X)\) and \(b(X)=\widehat q(X)-q_0(X)\).  Since \(g_0(X)=E_P[T\mid X]\) and \(\eta=T-g_0(X)\),
\[
E_P[\eta\mid X]=0.
\]
The outcome equation and outcome orthogonality give
\[
q_0(X)=E_P[Y\mid X]=\theta_0g_0(X)+f_0(X)
\quad\text{and hence}\quad
Y-q_0(X)=\theta_0\eta+\xi.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_P[(Y-\widehat q(X))(T-\widehat g(X))]
&=E_P[(\theta_0\eta+\xi-b(X))(\eta-a(X))]\\
&=\theta_0E_P[\eta^2]-\theta_0E_P[\eta a(X)]
  +E_P[\xi\eta]-E_P[\xi a(X)]\\
&\hspace{2em}-E_P[b(X)\eta]+E_P[a(X)b(X)].
\end{aligned}
\]
The three terms containing one factor \(\eta\) and a function of \(X\) vanish by \(E_P[\eta\mid X]=0\).  The two terms containing \(\xi\) vanish because \(E_P[\xi\mid X,T]=0\), while \(\eta\) and \(a(X)\) are measurable with respect to \(\sigma(X,T)\).  Finally, \(E_P[\eta^2]=1\) by the unit-variance assumption.  Thus
\[
E_P[(Y-\widehat q(X))(T-\widehat g(X))]-\theta_0=E_P[a(X)b(X)].
\]
The supplied-fit definition and monotonicity of \(L^p\)-norms on a probability space imply \(\lVert a\rVert_2\le\lVert a\rVert_8\le\varepsilon_{g,n}\) and \(\lVert b\rVert_2\le\lVert b\rVert_8\le\varepsilon_{q,n}\).  Cauchy--Schwarz therefore yields
\[
\left|E_P[a(X)b(X)]\right|
\le \lVert a\rVert_2\lVert b\rVert_2
\le\varepsilon_{g,n}\varepsilon_{q,n},
\]
which proves both assertions.

%%% FIELD proof-appell-identities
Let \(a(X)=\widehat g(X)-g_0(X)\), \(\bar a=E_P[a(X)]\), and \(u=a(X)-\bar a\).  From \(E_P[\eta\mid X]=0\) and independence of \(\eta\) and \(X\),
\[
\mu_r=-\bar a,
\qquad r-\mu_r=\eta-u,
\qquad v_r=1+E_P[u^2].
\]
Write \(W=r-\mu_r\).  Directly from the definition of \(A_{3,r}\),
\[
E_P[A_{3,r}(r)]
=E_P[W^3]-3v_rE_P[W]-c_{3,r}=0.
\]
Moreover, since \(r=W+\mu_r\), the preceding equality gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_P[rA_{3,r}(r)]
&=E_P[W\{W^3-3v_rW-c_{3,r}\}]\\
&=E_P[W^4]-3v_r^2=k_{4,r}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Both \(\eta\) and \(-u\) are centered and independent.  Expanding the second and fourth moments of their sum cancels the mixed \(6E_P[\eta^2]E_P[u^2]\) term, so
\[
k_{4,r}=\kappa_4+E_P[u^4]-3\{E_P[u^2]\}^2.
\]
If \(a'\) is an independent copy of \(a(X)\), conditional Jensen and \(|x-y|^4\le8(|x|^4+|y|^4)\) give
\[
E_P|u|^4\le E|a-a'|^4\le16E_P|a|^4\le16\varepsilon_{g,n}^4.
\]
Hence
\[
|k_{4,r}-\kappa_4|
\le E_P[u^4]+3\{E_P[u^2]\}^2
\le4E_P[u^4]
\le64\varepsilon_{g,n}^4
\le8V_{\mathrm{env}}\varepsilon_{g,n}^4,
\]
where the last inequality uses \(V_{\mathrm{env}}=2^{16}(1+C_{\mathrm{env}})^8\ge8\).

It remains to prove the outcome-score bound.  Put \(b(X)=\widehat q(X)-q_0(X)\).  The calculation in \(\mathrm{lem:dml\mbox{-}bias}\) gives
\[
Y-\widehat q(X)-\theta_0r=\xi+\theta_0a(X)-b(X).
\]
Outcome orthogonality removes the \(\xi\)-term after multiplication by the \(\sigma(X,T)\)-measurable polynomial \(A_{3,r}(r)\).  If \(\sigma_u^2=E_P[u^2]\), independence and \(E_P\eta=0\), \(E_P\eta^2=1\) show, by expanding the cubic, that
\[
E_P[A_{3,r}(r)\mid X]
=3\sigma_u^2u-u^3+E_P[u^3]=:D(u).
\]
Minkowski gives \(\lVert u\rVert_8\le2\lVert a\rVert_8\le2\varepsilon_{g,n}\).  Therefore
\[
\lVert D(u)\rVert_{8/3}
\le3\lVert u\rVert_2^2\lVert u\rVert_{8/3}
 +\lVert u\rVert_8^3+|E_Pu^3|
\le40\varepsilon_{g,n}^3.
\]
Since \(L^{8/3}(P)\subset L^{8/7}(P)\), H\"older's inequality, \(|\theta_0|\le C_{\mathrm{env}}\), and the supplied \(L^8\) bounds yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|E_P[(Y-\widehat q(X)-\theta_0r)A_{3,r}(r)]\right|
&=\left|E_P[(\theta_0a(X)-b(X))D(u)]\right|\\
&\le40\{C_{\mathrm{env}}\varepsilon_{g,n}^4
 +\varepsilon_{g,n}^3\varepsilon_{q,n}\}\\
&\le8V_{\mathrm{env}}\{\varepsilon_{g,n}^3\varepsilon_{q,n}
 +\varepsilon_{g,n}^4\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The final inequality follows from \(C_{\mathrm{env}}\ge1\) and the displayed definition of \(V_{\mathrm{env}}\).

%%% FIELD proof-calibration-event
We first record an elementary median-of-means calculation.  Let \(W_1,\ldots,W_n\) be independent with mean \(m_W\) and standard deviation at most \(V_{\mathrm{env}}\), and use \(b=b_n(\beta)\) nonempty blocks.  Every block has size at least \(n/(2b)\).  At radius \(t=4V_{\mathrm{env}}\sqrt{b/n}\), Chebyshev's inequality makes the probability that a particular block average differs from \(m_W\) by more than \(t\) at most \(1/8\).  Independence of the blocks and the union bound over subsets of at least \(b/2\) bad blocks give
\[
\Pr\{|\operatorname{MOM}_{\beta}-m_W|>t\}
\le 2^b(1/8)^{b/2}=2^{-b/2}.
\]
If \(b\ge8\log(2/\beta)\) and \(0<\beta\le1/2\), then
\[
2^{-b/2}\le(\beta/2)^{4\log2}\le\beta^2/8\le\beta.
\tag{1}
\]
This proves the needed deviation result without an external concentration theorem.

For completeness, all variance premises in (1) follow from the stated envelope.  Under the defining convention for the Orlicz norm, Markov's inequality applied to \(\exp(\eta^2/C_{\mathrm{env}}^2)\), followed by tail integration, bounds every moment of \(\eta\) through order eight by the corresponding numerical multiple of \(C_{\mathrm{env}}^j\); the same holds for \(\xi\).  Minkowski and the supplied-fit bounds then give finite, uniform \(L^8\) bounds for
\[
r=\eta-a(X),qquad
Y-\widehat q(X)=\theta_0\eta+\xi-b(X),
\]
where \(a=\widehat g-g_0\) and \(b=\widehat q-q_0\).  H\"older's inequality consequently bounds the standard deviations of the safe score and of \(r^j\), \(1\le j\le4\), by \(V_{\mathrm{env}}\).  The deliberately conservative value \(2^{16}(1+C_{\mathrm{env}})^8\) dominates each constant obtained by these expansions.

The estimated-polynomial scores require one additional conditioning step.  Set \(R=8(1+C_{\mathrm{env}})^2\) and let
\[
F_K=\bigcap_{j=1}^4\{|\widehat m_j|\le R^j\}.
\]
The preceding moment bounds give \(\lVert r\rVert_8\le R/8\).  Markov's inequality on each block and the same bad-block count as in (1) therefore imply
\[
Q_P(F_K^c)\le4\,2^{-b_n(\alpha/16)/2}.
\tag{2}
\]
Clipping cannot enlarge this probability because \(R^j\le R^4=2^{12}(1+C_{\mathrm{env}})^8<V_{\mathrm{env}}\).  On \(F_K\),
\[
|\widehat\mu|\le R,qquad
|\widehat v|\le2R^2,qquad
|\widehat c_3|\le6R^3.
\]
Expanding \(\widehat A_3\), applying H\"older with the just-established \(L^8\) bounds, and using \(R^4=V_{\mathrm{env}}/16\), shows conditionally on \(D_K\) that the standard deviations of
\[
(Y-\widehat q(X))\widehat A_3(r)
\quad\text{and}\quad
r\widehat A_3(r)
\]
are at most \(V_{\mathrm{env}}\).  Thus (1), conditionally on \(D_K\), applies on \(F_K\).

For the safe score, \(\mathrm{lem:dml\mbox{-}bias}\) puts its mean within \(\varepsilon_{g,n}\varepsilon_{q,n}\) of \(\theta_0\); hence (1) at \(\beta=\alpha/4\) gives \(Q_P(E_D^c)\le\alpha/4\).  Applying (1) to the four raw moments and taking a union bound gives \(Q_P(E_K^c)\le\alpha/4\).  Applying conditional (1) on \(F_K\), then adding (2), proves separately \(Q_P(E_A^c)\le\alpha/4\) and \(Q_P(E_S^c)\le\alpha/4\).  Indeed, the sharper bounds in (1)--(2) give
\[
Q_P(E_D^c)+Q_P(E_K^c)+Q_P(E_A^c)+Q_P(E_S^c)
\le3\,2^{-b_n(\alpha/4)/2}+12\,2^{-b_n(\alpha/16)/2}
\le\alpha.
\]
The assumed lower bound on \(n\) makes \(b_n(\alpha/16)=\lceil8\log(32/\alpha)\rceil\); since \(b_n(\alpha/4)\le b_n(\alpha/16)\le n\), all displayed partitions are nonempty.  The union bound now proves
\[
Q_P(E_D\cap E_K\cap E_A\cap E_S)\ge1-\alpha.
\]
Every moment bound above is uniform in the indicated law and supplied-fit pair, so all conclusions are uniform over the stated union.

%%% FIELD proof-branch-selection-error
Let \(a=\widehat g-g_0\) and \(u=a-E_Pa\).  The cumulant expansion in \(\mathrm{lem:appell\mbox{-}identities}\) actually gives the sharper envelope
\[
|k_{4,r}-\kappa_4|
\le64\varepsilon_{g,n}^4.
\tag{3}
\]
We next control estimation rather than merely a fixed confidence event.  For a block mean with variance \(\sigma^2/m\), Chebyshev followed by the same bad-block count used in \(\mathrm{lem:calibration\mbox{-}event}\) gives, for \(t^2\ge8\sigma^2/m\),
\[
\Pr\{|\operatorname{MOM}-EW|>t\}
\le2^b\left(\frac{\sigma^2}{mt^2}\right)^{b/2}.
\tag{4}
\]
Integrating \(2t\) times (4) above its threshold and using the trivial bound below it proves, uniformly for every \(b\ge4\),
\[
E|\operatorname{MOM}-EW|^2\le C\sigma^2 b/n
\tag{5}
\]
for a numerical \(C\).  Applying (5) to the four raw residual moments, using their standard-deviation bound \(V_{\mathrm{env}}\), and expanding the displayed polynomial defining \(\widehat k_4\) yields
\[
E_{Q_P}|\widehat k_4-k_{4,r}|^2
\le C\rho_n(\alpha/16)^2.
\tag{6}
\]
To justify the polynomial step without a bounded-score assumption, split on the event \(F_K\) constructed in the calibration proof.  On \(F_K\) the polynomial map has bounded derivatives; on its complement, its clipped value is bounded and the tail estimate (4), integrated once more, has order at most the right side of (6).  The choices \(R^4=V_{\mathrm{env}}/16\) and \(b_n(\alpha/16)\ge4\) make the resulting constant numerical because all envelope dependence is already carried by \(\rho_n\).

Under \(P\in\mathcal G_n\), \(\kappa_4=0\).  Under \(P\in\mathcal N_n(\delta_n)\), \(|\kappa_4|\ge\delta_n\).  In either branch, a wrong decision implies
\[
|\widehat k_4-k_{4,r}|
\ge\delta_n/4
\]
unless \(64\varepsilon_{g,n}^4\ge\delta_n/4\).  Markov's inequality applied to (6), with the latter exceptional case absorbed by the trivial probability bound one, therefore gives
\[
\max\left\{
\sup_{P\in\mathcal G_n}Q_P(\phi_n=1),
\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n(\delta_n)}Q_P(\phi_n=0)
\right\}
\le\min\left\{1,C_{\mathrm{sel}}\left[
\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{g,n}^4}{\delta_n}\right)^2
+\left(\frac{\rho_n(\alpha/16)}{\delta_n}\right)^2
\right]\right\}.
\]
Here and above the suprema are restricted to supplied pairs in \(\mathcal H_n(P)\).  Since \(\varepsilon_{g,n}^4\le s_n\), increasing the universal numerical constant gives exactly the displayed bound by \(p_{\mathrm{sel},n}\).

%%% FIELD proof-adaptive-upper-frontier
Write \(a=\widehat g-g_0\), \(b=\widehat q-q_0\), and retain the natural-moment event \(F_K\) from the calibration proof.  On \(E_K\cap F_K\), direct subtraction of the raw-moment formulas, followed by the mean-value identity for polynomials, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
|\widehat\mu-\mu_r|+|\widehat v-v_r|+|\widehat c_3-c_{3,r}|
&\le C V_{\mathrm{env}}\rho_n(\alpha/16),\\
\lVert\widehat A_3-A_{3,r}\rVert_{8/3}
&\le C V_{\mathrm{env}}\rho_n(\alpha/16).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
When \(\rho_n(\alpha/16)\le1\), (7) is the ordinary Lipschitz bound on the bounded coefficient set supplied by \(F_K\).  When the radius exceeds one, the same display follows from the separate coefficient bounds on \(F_K\), after enlarging the numerical constant.  Thus there is no division by a vanishing quantity in (7).

H\"older's inequality, the uniform \(L^8\) envelope, (7), and \(\mathrm{lem:appell\mbox{-}identities}\) imply
\[
\left|E_P[(Y-\widehat q(X)-\theta_0r)\widehat A_3(r)\mid D_K]\right|
\le8V_{\mathrm{env}}\left\{
\varepsilon_{g,n}^3\varepsilon_{q,n}+\varepsilon_{g,n}^4
+\rho_n(\alpha/16)\right\}.
\tag{8}
\]
On \(E_A\), the definition of \(a_A\) and (8) give
\[
|\widehat H_A-\theta_0\widehat S_A|\le a_A,
\]
so \(\theta_0\in I_A\), using also \(|\theta_0|\le C_{\mathrm{env}}\).  On \(E_D\), \(\theta_0\in I_D\).  The sharper probability calculation in the proof of \(\mathrm{lem:calibration\mbox{-}event}\) gives
\[
Q_P(E_D\cap E_K\cap E_A\cap F_K)\ge1-\alpha.
\tag{9}
\]
On the event in (9), if \(\phi_n=0\) the reported interval is \(I_D\); if \(\phi_n=1\), both component intervals contain \(\theta_0\), their intersection is nonempty, and the reported interval contains \(\theta_0\).  This proves
\[
\inf_{P\in\mathcal U_n:(\widehat g,\widehat q)\in\mathcal H_n(P)}
Q_P\{\theta_0\in C_n^*\}\ge1-\alpha.
\]

Irrespective of the selected branch, \(C_n^*\subseteq I_D\) or \(C_n^*=I_D\).  Hence
\[
|C_n^*|\le|I_D|=2a_D
=2\{\varepsilon_{g,n}\varepsilon_{q,n}+\rho_n(\alpha/4)\}
\lesssim d_n,
\tag{10}
\]
where, for fixed \(\alpha\) and \(C_{\mathrm{env}}\), \(\rho_n(\alpha/4)\lesssim n^{-1/2}\).  Taking the Gaussian supremum in (10) proves \(L_G(C_n^*)\lesssim d_n\).

For the non-Gaussian coordinate, the same median-of-means second-moment calculation as in (4)--(6), now applied also to the slope score, combines with (3) and (7) to give
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal N_n(\delta_n):(\widehat g,\widehat q)\in\mathcal H_n(P)}
E_{Q_P}|\widehat S_A-\kappa_4|^2\lesssim B_n^2.
\tag{11}
\]
All three terms in \(B_n\) have a declared origin: the evaluation fluctuation is \(n^{-1/2}\), the estimated-Appell outcome remainder is \(\varepsilon_{g,n}^3\varepsilon_{q,n}\), and residual-cumulant contamination is \(\varepsilon_{g,n}^4\).  Also \(a_A\lesssim B_n\).

If \(\delta_n\le cB_n\) for a sufficiently large fixed reciprocal \(c^{-1}\), (10), boundedness of \(d_n\), and \(B_n/\delta_n\ge c^{-1}\) give both the safe bound and the asserted Appell-form bound.  Suppose instead that \(\delta_n>cB_n\).  On \(\{|\widehat S_A-\kappa_4|\le\delta_n/2\}\), one has \(|\widehat S_A|\ge\delta_n/2\); the inversion definition then yields
\[
|I_A|\le\frac{2a_A}{|\widehat S_A|}\lesssim\frac{B_n}{\delta_n}.
\tag{12}
\]
By (11) and Markov's inequality, the complement of this slope event has probability \(O(B_n^2/\delta_n^2)\).  If \(\phi_n=0\), the interval has length at most the right side of (10), and \(\mathrm{lem:branch\mbox{-}selection\mbox{-}error}\) bounds this event by \(p_{\mathrm{sel},n}\).  If \(\phi_n=1\), the construction is either an intersection of length at most \(|I_A|\) or the safe interval.  Combining these cases, (10)--(12), and \(d_nB_n/\delta_n=O(1)\), gives
\[
L_N(C_n^*)
\lesssim \frac{B_n}{\delta_n}+d_np_{\mathrm{sel},n}
+d_n\frac{B_n^2}{\delta_n^2}
\lesssim \frac{B_n}{\delta_n}+d_np_{\mathrm{sel},n}.
\]
Together with the unconditional safe bound (10), this is
\[
L_N(C_n^*)\lesssim
\min\left\{d_n,\frac{B_n}{\delta_n}+d_np_{\mathrm{sel},n}\right\}.
\]

Finally fix \(\alpha\) and \(C_{\mathrm{env}}\).  Then \(\rho_n(\alpha/16)\lesssim n^{-1/2}\le s_n\), so \(\delta_n/s_n\to\infty\) implies \(p_{\mathrm{sel},n}=O((s_n/\delta_n)^2)=o(1)\).  With \(x=n^{-1/2}\), \(g=\varepsilon_{g,n}\), and \(q=\varepsilon_{q,n}\), expansion and \(0\le g,q\le C_{\mathrm{env}}\) give
\[
d_ns_n=(x+gq)(x+g^4)
\le C\{x+g^3q+g^4\}=CB_n.
\tag{13}
\]
Consequently,
\[
d_np_{\mathrm{sel},n}
\lesssim d_n(s_n/\delta_n)^2
\le C(s_n/\delta_n)(B_n/\delta_n)
\lesssim B_n/\delta_n
\]
eventually.  Substitution in the finite-sample bound proves
\[
L_N(C_n^*)\lesssim\min\{d_n,B_n/\delta_n\}.
\]

%%% FIELD proof-sufficient-shortening
Assume \(B_n/\delta_n=o(d_n)\), equivalently \(\delta_nd_n/B_n\to\infty\).  Inequality (13) from the proof of the adaptive upper theorem is purely algebraic and says \(d_ns_n\le CB_n\).  Since all three scales are positive,
\[
\frac{\delta_n}{s_n}
\ge\frac{\delta_nd_n}{CB_n}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
The asymptotic clause of \(\mathrm{thm:adaptive\mbox{-}upper\mbox{-}frontier}\) is therefore applicable and gives
\[
L_N(C_n^*)\lesssim\min\{d_n,B_n/\delta_n\}=o(d_n).
\]
The same theorem gives \(L_G(C_n^*)\lesssim d_n\).  This establishes all three conclusions without strengthening the law class or the nuisance-radius conditions.

%%% FIELD proof-representative-regimes
First take \(\varepsilon_{g,n}=\varepsilon_{q,n}=\delta_n=n^{-1/8}\).  For fixed \(\alpha\), the deterministic threshold \(\lceil8\log(32/\alpha)\rceil\) is eventually below \(n\), so
\[
b_n(\alpha/16)=\lceil8\log(32/\alpha)\rceil,
\qquad \rho_n(\alpha/16)=O(n^{-1/2}).
\]
Substitution into the rate definitions gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
d_n&=n^{-1/2}+n^{-1/4}\asymp n^{-1/4},\\
s_n&=n^{-1/2}+n^{-1/2}\asymp n^{-1/2},\\
B_n&=n^{-1/2}+n^{-1/2}+n^{-1/2}\asymp n^{-1/2}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Furthermore,
\[
\Lambda_n
=\min\left\{1,
\left[\frac{1\vee(\log n)/8}{\log(en)}\right]^3\right\}
\longrightarrow8^{-3},
\]
and hence \(d_n^-=n^{-1/2}+n^{-1/4}\Lambda_n\asymp n^{-1/4}\).  Both squared ratios in the selection envelope have order
\[
\left(\frac{n^{-1/2}}{n^{-1/8}}\right)^2=n^{-3/4},
\]
so \(p_{\mathrm{sel},n}=O(n^{-3/4})\).  Finally \(B_n/\delta_n\asymp n^{-3/8}=o(n^{-1/4})\).  The adaptive upper theorem and the sufficient-shortening proposition therefore yield
\[
\bigl(L_G(C_n^*),L_N(C_n^*)\bigr)
=\bigl(O(n^{-1/4}),O(n^{-3/8})\bigr).
\]

If \(\varepsilon_{g,n}=0\), the definitions instead give
\[
\Lambda_n=1,qquad
d_n^-=d_n=B_n=n^{-1/2},
\qquad s_n=n^{-1/2}.
\]
Because \(0<\delta_n\le1\), \(B_n/\delta_n\ge d_n\).  Thus the minimum in the adaptive upper bound remains the safe \(n^{-1/2}\) scale; in this perfect-treatment-fit benchmark the advertised branchwise rate map has no shortening, including when \(\delta_n\to0\).
