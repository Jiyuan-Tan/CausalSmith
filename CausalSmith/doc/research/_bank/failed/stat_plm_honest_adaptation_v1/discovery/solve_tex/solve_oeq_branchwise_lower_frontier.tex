%%% FIELD lem-mixture-test-statement
Let \(\mathsf M_0\) and \(\mathsf M_1\) be probability measures on one measurable space, and let \(\psi\) be a measurable \(\{0,1\}\)-valued test.  If, for some \(e\in[0,1)\),
\[
 \mathsf M_0\{\psi=1\}\le e,
 \qquad
 \mathsf M_1\{\psi=0\}\le e,
\]
then
\[
 \|\mathsf M_1-\mathsf M_0\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\ge 1-2e.
\]
If \(0<e<1\), then, with chi-square divergence defined to be \(+\infty\) in the absence of the indicated absolute continuity,
\[
 \chi^2(\mathsf M_1,\mathsf M_0)
 \ge \frac{(1-e)^2}{e}-1,
 \qquad
 \chi^2(\mathsf M_0,\mathsf M_1)
 \ge \frac{(1-e)^2}{e}-1.
\]
For \(e=0\), both displayed chi-square divergences are \(+\infty\).
%%% FIELD lem-mixture-test-proof
Put \(A=\{\psi=1\}\).  The two error inequalities give
\[
 \mathsf M_1(A)-\mathsf M_0(A)
 \ge (1-e)-e=1-2e.
\]
The definition of total variation as the supremum of event-probability differences therefore proves
\[
 \|\mathsf M_1-\mathsf M_0\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\ge1-2e.
\]

Suppose first that \(0<e<1\) and \(\mathsf M_1\ll\mathsf M_0\), and write \(L=d\mathsf M_1/d\mathsf M_0\).  Cauchy--Schwarz, the bound \(\mathsf M_0(A)\le e\), and \(\int L^2\,d\mathsf M_0=1+\chi^2(\mathsf M_1,\mathsf M_0)\) yield
\[
 1-e
 \le \mathsf M_1(A)
 =\int_A L\,d\mathsf M_0
 \le \left\{e\bigl(1+\chi^2(\mathsf M_1,\mathsf M_0)\bigr)\right\}^{1/2}.
\]
Squaring and rearranging gives the first chi-square bound.  If absolute continuity fails, that bound holds by the stated \(+\infty\) convention.  Apply the same argument with the measures interchanged and with \(A^c=\{\psi=0\}\): now \(\mathsf M_1(A^c)\le e\) and \(\mathsf M_0(A^c)\ge1-e\), which proves the reverse chi-square bound.  Finally, when \(e=0\), \(\mathsf M_0(A)=0\) and \(\mathsf M_1(A)=1\), while \(\mathsf M_1(A^c)=0\) and \(\mathsf M_0(A^c)=1\).  Absolute continuity fails in both directions, so both divergences equal \(+\infty\).
%%% FIELD lem-two-mixture-interval-statement
Let \(\mathcal P_0\) and \(\mathcal P_1\) be two classes of laws for a common observation, let \(\vartheta\) be a real parameter, fix \(\alpha\in[0,1/2)\), and let \(\Pi_0\) and \(\Pi_1\) be priors supported on the respective classes.  Write \(\mathsf M_j=\int R\,d\Pi_j(R)\) for the induced observation mixture.  Suppose that deterministic intervals \(J_0,J_1\subset\mathbb R\) satisfy \(\vartheta(R)\in J_j\) for every \(R\in\operatorname{supp}(\Pi_j)\), and put
\[
 r=\inf\{|u-v|:u\in J_0,\ v\in J_1\}>0.
\]
If a random interval \(C\) obeys \(R\{\vartheta(R)\in C\}\ge1-\alpha\) on both prior supports, then, for \(j=0,1\),
\[
 \sup_{R\in\mathcal P_j}E_R|C|
 \ge
 r\left[1-2\alpha-\|\mathsf M_1-\mathsf M_0\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\right]_+.
\]
%%% FIELD lem-two-mixture-interval-proof
For \(j=0,1\), let \(E_j=\{C\cap J_j\ne\varnothing\}\).  If \(R\in\operatorname{supp}(\Pi_j)\), then \(\vartheta(R)\in J_j\), so
\[
 \{\vartheta(R)\in C\}\subseteq E_j.
\]
The assumed coverage and integration with respect to \(\Pi_j\) therefore give \(\mathsf M_j(E_j)\ge1-\alpha\).  Total variation transfers the other event to mixture \(\mathsf M_1\):
\[
 \mathsf M_1(E_0)
 \ge \mathsf M_0(E_0)-\|\mathsf M_1-\mathsf M_0\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \ge1-\alpha-\|\mathsf M_1-\mathsf M_0\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\]
The union bound now implies
\[
 \mathsf M_1(E_0\cap E_1)
 \ge1-2\alpha-\|\mathsf M_1-\mathsf M_0\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\]
On \(E_0\cap E_1\), the interval \(C\) meets both \(J_0\) and \(J_1\).  By the definition of \(r\), this forces \(|C|\ge r\).  Hence
\[
 \int E_R|C|\,d\Pi_1(R)
 =E_{\mathsf M_1}|C|
 \ge r\left[1-2\alpha-\|\mathsf M_1-\mathsf M_0\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\right]_+.
\]
The supremum over \(\mathcal P_1\) is at least this prior average.  Interchanging indices \(0\) and \(1\) proves the same conclusion for \(\mathcal P_0\).  If \(C\) is unbounded with positive probability, the corresponding expectation is infinite and the conclusion remains valid.
%%% FIELD thm-no-cross-branch-statement
Fix \(\alpha\in(0,1/2)\) and \(C_{\mathrm{env}}\), and suppose \(\delta_n/s_n\to\infty\).  For each \(n\), let \(\Pi_{G,n}\) and \(\Pi_{N,n}\) be arbitrary priors supported respectively on \(\mathcal G_n\) and \(\mathcal N_n(\delta_n)\).  Suppose one deterministic supplied pair \((\widehat g,\widehat q)\) belongs to \(\mathcal H_n(P)\) for every law in the union of the two supports, and define the projected-experiment mixtures
\[
 \overline Q_{G,n}=\int Q_P\,d\Pi_{G,n}(P),
 \qquad
 \overline Q_{N,n}=\int Q_P\,d\Pi_{N,n}(P).
\]
Then \(p_{\mathrm{sel},n}=o(1)\), and, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 \|\overline Q_{N,n}-\overline Q_{G,n}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \ge1-2p_{\mathrm{sel},n},
\]
\[
 \min\left\{
 \chi^2(\overline Q_{N,n},\overline Q_{G,n}),
 \chi^2(\overline Q_{G,n},\overline Q_{N,n})
 \right\}
 \ge \frac{(1-p_{\mathrm{sel},n})^2}{p_{\mathrm{sel},n}}-1
 \longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Consequently, in the regime \(\delta_n/s_n\to\infty\), no Rademacher-partition exponential-Hermite construction, nor any other construction with the stated branch support and common supplied fits, can have uniformly bounded chi-square distance between its Gaussian and non-Gaussian projected mixtures.  Thus the conjunctive construction question has a negative answer.  This result makes no claim about existence of such a pair when \(\delta_n\lesssim s_n\).
%%% FIELD thm-no-cross-branch-proof
Let \(\mathcal E_n=\{\phi_n=1\}\), where \(\phi_n\) is the split residual-cumulant test in \(\textup{def:cumulant-test}\).  Lemma \(\textup{lem:branch-selection-error}\) and the common supplied-fit condition give, for every law in the Gaussian prior support,
\[
 Q_P(\mathcal E_n)\le p_{\mathrm{sel},n},
\]
and, for every law in the non-Gaussian prior support,
\[
 Q_P(\mathcal E_n^c)\le p_{\mathrm{sel},n}.
\]
Integrating the first inequality with respect to \(\Pi_{G,n}\) and the second with respect to \(\Pi_{N,n}\) yields
\[
 \overline Q_{G,n}(\mathcal E_n)\le p_{\mathrm{sel},n},
 \qquad
 \overline Q_{N,n}(\mathcal E_n^c)\le p_{\mathrm{sel},n}.
\tag{1}
\]

For fixed \(\alpha\) and \(C_{\mathrm{env}}\), the explicit median-of-means calibration in \(\textup{def:mom-calibration}\) gives
\[
 \rho_n(\alpha/16)=O(n^{-1/2}).
\]
Definition \(\textup{def:rate-scales}\) gives \(s_n\ge n^{-1/2}\).  Therefore \(\textup{def:selection-error-envelope}\) implies, for a constant depending only on the fixed envelope and confidence level,
\[
 p_{\mathrm{sel},n}
 \le C\left\{
 \left(\frac{s_n}{\delta_n}\right)^2
 \left(\frac{n^{-1/2}}{\delta_n}\right)^2
 \right\}
 \le 2C\left(\frac{s_n}{\delta_n}\right)^2
 =o(1).
\tag{2}
\]
In particular, \(0<p_{\mathrm{sel},n}<1\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Apply \(\textup{lem:mixture-test-chi-square-certificate}\) to (1), with \(\mathsf M_0=\overline Q_{G,n}\), \(\mathsf M_1=\overline Q_{N,n}\), \(\psi=\phi_n\), and \(e=p_{\mathrm{sel},n}\).  It gives both asserted divergence inequalities.  Equation (2) makes their common lower bound diverge.  The proof did not use the partition size, the form of the nuisance priors, a Hermite expansion, or the target displacement.  It therefore supplies an analytic infeasibility certificate for every finite Rademacher-partition program satisfying the requested support, common-fit, and bounded-chi-square constraints in the strongly separated regime.
%%% FIELD thm-cross-fuzzy-frontier-statement
Fix \(\alpha\in(0,1/2)\) and \(C_{\mathrm{env}}\).  A cross-branch fuzzy-hypothesis argument has the following exact branchwise content.  Let \(r_n>0\) be any deterministic sequence.  Suppose \(\Pi_{G,n}\) and \(\Pi_{N,n}\) have the support and common-fit properties in \(\textup{thm:no-cross-branch-bounded-chi-square}\), write \(\overline Q_{G,n}=\int Q_P\,d\Pi_{G,n}(P)\) and \(\overline Q_{N,n}=\int Q_P\,d\Pi_{N,n}(P)\), and suppose their target values lie in deterministic intervals \(J_{G,n}\) and \(J_{N,n}\) whose distance is at least \(c_0r_n\), where \(c_0>0\).  Every interval \(C_n\) honest over \(\mathcal U_n\) then satisfies
\[
 \min\{L_G(C_n),L_N(C_n)\}
 \ge c_0r_n
 \left[1-2\alpha-|\overline Q_{N,n}-\overline Q_{G,n}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\right]_+.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus, in a regime where a separate construction gives total variation at most a fixed \(\tau<1-2\alpha\) and \(r_n\asymp d_n^-\), equation (3) gives the desired non-Gaussian lower bound of order \(d_n^-\), without needing the side condition \(L_G(C_n)\lesssim d_n\).  In contrast, when \(\delta_n/s_n\to\infty\), every cross-branch pair with the required support and common fits has total variation at least \(1-2p_{\mathrm{sel},n}\to1\) and chi-square divergence tending to infinity in both directions.  Hence the bounded-chi-square Gaussian-versus-non-Gaussian fuzzy-Hermite handle cannot imply
\[
 L_N(C_n)\gtrsim\min\{d_n^-,B_n/\delta_n\}
\]
in that regime, even after restricting attention to intervals with \(L_G(C_n)\lesssim d_n\).  The asserted strong-separation lower face of \(\mathcal A_n\), and therefore the claimed necessity of \(B_n/\delta_n=o(d_n)\) for strict shortening, requires a different lower-bound experiment, such as two target-separated priors both supported inside \(\mathcal N_n(\delta_n)\), or a quantitatively explicit constrained-risk argument allowing diverging cross-branch divergence.  It is not a consequence of the stipulated cross-branch bounded-chi-square construction.
%%% FIELD thm-cross-fuzzy-frontier-proof
Because every law in the two prior supports belongs to \(\mathcal U_n\) and is compatible with the common supplied pair, union honesty gives
\[
 Q_P\{\theta_0(P)\in C_n\}\ge1-\alpha
\]
on both supports.  Apply \(\textup{lem:two-mixture-interval-transfer}\) with \(\mathcal P_0=\mathcal G_n\), \(\mathcal P_1=\mathcal N_n(\delta_n)\), \(\vartheta(P)=\theta_0(P)\), and \(r=c_0r_n\).  The lemma bounds the prior-average expected length on each branch by the right side of (3).  Definition \(\textup{def:length-vector}\) makes each branch supremum at least its corresponding prior average, proving (3).

If the mixture total variation is at most \(\tau<1-2\alpha\), the bracket in (3) is at least \(1-2\alpha-\tau>0\).  Taking \(r_n\asymp d_n^-\) therefore proves the stated conditional weak-scale consequence.  Notice that this calculation uses only honesty, target-band separation, and mixture closeness; no upper restriction on \(L_G(C_n)\) is used.

Now suppose \(\delta_n/s_n\to\infty\).  Theorem \(\textup{thm:no-cross-branch-bounded-chi-square}\) gives
\[
 \|\overline Q_{N,n}-\overline Q_{G,n}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \ge1-2p_{\mathrm{sel},n}\longrightarrow1
\]
and divergence of both chi-square orientations.  Hence the bounded-chi-square premise requested by \(\textup{def:fuzzy-hermite-handle}\) is impossible in precisely the regime where the proposed second branch of the frontier is meant to apply.  The additional restriction \(L_G(C_n)\lesssim d_n\) concerns a property of the interval and cannot alter this incompatibility between the two mixture laws.  Consequently the stipulated construction proves neither the strong-separation lower bound nor its claimed shortening necessity.  A within-\(\mathcal N_n(\delta_n)\) experiment is not ruled out by the branch selector, because both of its priors induce the same selector label; establishing such an experiment is a separate mathematical task.  This proves the negative answer and the exact conditional statement in (3).
