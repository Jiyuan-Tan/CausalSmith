%%% FIELD proof-fourth-trace
Write \(X=X_{G_n}\), and note from its definition that \(X\) is symmetric,
\(X_{ii}=0\), and, for \(i\ne j\),
\[
X_{ij}=(B_{G_n})_{ij}-p_{n,d_n}.
\tag{1}
\]
Expand the trace as
\[
\operatorname{tr}(X^4)=
\sum_{a,b,c,e\in V_n}X_{ab}X_{bc}X_{ce}X_{ea}.
\tag{2}
\]
Every summand having two adjacent equal indices is zero by \(X_{ii}=0\).
Among the remaining summands, exactly three equality patterns are possible.
If there are two distinct indices, they alternate, giving
\(n(n-1)\) summands with expectation
\(\mathbb E X_{12}^4=\mu_4\).  If there are three distinct indices, either
\(a=c\) or \(b=e\), but not both.  Each case has
\(n(n-1)(n-2)\) ordered choices and, by label exchangeability, expectation
\(\mathbb E[X_{12}^2X_{13}^2]=\mu_{22}\).  Finally, four distinct indices
give \(n(n-1)(n-2)(n-3)\) ordered choices and expectation
\(\mu_{\mathrm{cyc}}(n,d_n)\).  Substitution in (2) proves the displayed
identity in the lemma.

It remains to prove the two bounds.  A simple \(d_n\)-regular graph has at
most
\[
1+d_n+d_n(d_n-1)=d_n^2+1
\]
vertices in a closed radius-two ball.  Hence, for a fixed realization,
each row of \(B_{G_n}\) has at most \(d_n^2\) off-diagonal ones.  Label
exchangeability therefore gives
\[
p_{n,d_n}
=\frac{\mathbb E\{m_1(G_n)-1\}}{n-1}
\le \frac{d_n^2}{n-1}.
\tag{3}
\]
Because an off-diagonal entry of \(B_{G_n}\) is Bernoulli with mean
\(p=p_{n,d_n}\),
\[
\mu_4=p(1-p)^4+(1-p)p^4\le 2p
\le\frac{2d_n^2}{n-1}.
\tag{4}
\]

For three distinct labels put
\(a=\mathbb E[(B_{G_n})_{12}(B_{G_n})_{13}]\).  Conditional on the first
row, the ordered pair of labels \((2,3)\) is uniform among distinct labels
different from \(1\).  Thus
\[
a=\frac{\mathbb E[(m_1(G_n)-1)(m_1(G_n)-2)]}
{(n-1)(n-2)}
\le\frac{d_n^4}{(n-1)(n-2)}.
\tag{5}
\]
For \(b\in\{0,1\}\), \((b-p)^2\le b+p^2\).  Applying this inequality to
the two factors and then using (5) yields
\[
\mu_{22}
\le a+2p^3+p^4.
\tag{6}
\]
Along \(d_n=o(\sqrt n)\), eventually \(d_n^2/n\le1/2\).  Equations
(3)--(6), with the harmless factors \(n/(n-2)\) absorbed, then give
\[
\mu_{22}\le C\frac{d_n^4}{n^2}
\]
for one numerical \(C\), uniformly over every such sequence.  No
independence among entries of \(B_{G_n}\) has been used.

%%% FIELD statement-adjacency-gap
There is a universal \(\alpha<\infty\) such that, whenever
\(1\le d_n\le n^{2/3}\) and \(n\) is sufficiently large,
\[
\Pr_{G_n}\!\left\{\lambda_{\star}(A_{G_n})
\le\alpha\sqrt{d_n}\right\}\ge1-n^{-2}.
\]

%%% FIELD statement-sparse-replacement
There is a universal \(L<\infty\) such that, uniformly over every
admissible sequence satisfying \(d_n\le n^{2/5}\),
\[
\Pr_{G_n}\!\left\{\|B_{G_n}P_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le Ld_n^{7/4}\right\}\longrightarrow1.
\]

%%% FIELD proof-sparse-operator
Put \(d=d_n\), let \(A=A_{G_n}\), and define
\[
W=A^2+A-(d-1)I_n,
\quad E=W-B_{G_n}.
\tag{7}
\]
The diagonal entries of both \(W\) and \(B_{G_n}\) equal one.  For
\(i\ne j\), \(W_{ij}=A_{ij}+(A^2)_{ij}\) is the number of direct edges
and length-two walks connecting \(i\) and \(j\), whereas
\((B_{G_n})_{ij}=\mathbf 1\{W_{ij}>0\}\).  Hence \(E\) is symmetric and
entrywise nonnegative.  Since every row sum of \(W\) is \(d^2+1\), if
\(e_i=\sum_jE_{ij}\), then
\[
e_i=d^2+1-m_i(G_n).
\tag{8}
\]
Regularity gives \(A\iota_n=d\iota_n\), so \(A\) commutes with \(P_n\).
The triangle inequality, \(\|E\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le
\sqrt{\|E\|_1\|E\|_\infty}=\max_i e_i\), and \(\|P_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}=1\)
therefore give
\[
\|B_{G_n}P_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le \|A P_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2+
\|A P_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}+(d-1)+\max_i e_i.
\tag{9}
\]

We next prove the collision bound directly under the uniform simple
regular law.  Let \(H\) be any revealed simple partial graph.  Conditional
on \(H\), let \(F\) be uniform over the edge-disjoint simple completions
for which \(H\cup F\) is \(d\)-regular.  Set
\(r_v=d-\deg_H(v)\) and \(R=\sum_vr_v\).  At a vertex \(u\) with
\(r_u>0\), mark a uniformly chosen residual edge.  If \(R\ge8d^2\), then
for every \(S\subseteq V_n\setminus\{u\}\),
\[
\Pr\{\hbox{the marked endpoint lies in }S\mid H\}
\le \frac{2\sum_{v\in S}r_v}{R}
\le\frac{2d|S|}{R}.
\tag{10}
\]
To verify (10), fix an endpoint \(v\).  From a marked completion containing
\(uv\), choose an oriented residual edge \(xy\), delete \(uv,xy\), insert
\(ux,vy\), and mark \(ux\).  Choices with coincident vertices remove at
most \(4d\) orientations.  The requirements that \(ux\) and \(vy\) not
already be edges of \(H\cup F\) remove at most \(d^2\) orientations each.
Thus at least \(R-2d^2-4d\ge R/2\) forward switchings are valid.  From any
resulting marked completion, an inverse with old endpoint \(v\) is
specified by one of at most \(r_v\) residual neighbors of \(v\).  Double
counting marked completions gives probability at most \(2r_v/R\); summing
over \(v\in S\) proves (10).  Uniformity is exact because every compatible
completion has the same conditional probability and exactly \(r_u\)
possible marks.

Fix a root \(i\).  First expose its \(d\) incident edges, and then expose
all still-unrevealed edges incident to its \(d\) neighbors, ordering
residual incident edges uniformly when a mark is needed.  There are at
most \(d(d-1)\le d^2\) latter reveals.  Let \(I\) be the number of edges
internal to \(N_{G_n}(i)\), let \(O\) be the number from
\(N_{G_n}(i)\) to vertices outside \(N_{G_n}[i]\), and let \(L_i\) be
the number of distinct outside vertices reached.  Then
\[
2I+O=d(d-1),\quad m_i(G_n)=1+d+L_i.
\tag{11}
\]
Call a latter reveal bad if its other endpoint has already been discovered.
Every internal edge is bad once, and exactly \(O-L_i\) of the outside
reveals repeat an outside endpoint.  Thus the number of bad reveals is
\(F_i=I+O-L_i\), while (8) and (11) imply
\[
e_i=2I+O-L_i\le2F_i.
\tag{12}
\]

At every such reveal the discovered set contains at most \(d^2+1\le2d^2\)
vertices.  At most \(d^2\) edges have been exposed altogether.  Under
\(d\le n^{2/5}\), for all sufficiently large \(n\) the remaining residual
mass is at least \(nd/2\) and at least \(8d^2\).  Equation (10) consequently
bounds every conditional bad-reveal probability by
\[
q_n:=\frac{2d(2d^2)}{nd/2}=\frac{8d^2}{n}.
\tag{13}
\]
For any specified \(t\) reveal positions, iterated conditioning bounds the
probability that all are bad by \(q_n^t\).  A union bound over their
locations therefore gives
\[
\Pr(F_i\ge t)\le {d^2\choose t}q_n^t
\le\left(\frac{8e d^4}{nt}\right)^t,
\quad 1\le t\le d^2.
\tag{14}
\]
Zero-padding the exploration after it terminates justifies the fixed
budget in (14).

Take \(t=\lceil d^{7/4}\rceil\), which is at most \(d^2\) for \(d\ge2\).
By (12), (14), a union bound over roots, and \(d\le n^{2/5}\),
\[
\Pr\{\max_i e_i\ge2t\}
\le n\left(\frac{8e d^4}{nt}\right)^t
\le n\left(8e n^{-1/10}\right)^t
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{15}
\]
The last convergence holds even for arbitrarily slow divergence because
\(d\to\infty\) implies \(t\to\infty\).

The cited adjacency-gap lemma, applied with its fixed constants, gives
\(\lambda_\star(A)\le\alpha\sqrt d\) except on an event of probability at
most \(n^{-2}\).  For a symmetric regular adjacency matrix, spectral
decomposition on \(\operatorname{range}(P_n)=\iota_n^\perp\) gives
\(\|AP_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\lambda_\star(A)\).  On the intersection of this
event with (15), equation (9) yields
\[
\|B_{G_n}P_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le \alpha^2d+\alpha\sqrt d+d-1+2\lceil d^{7/4}\rceil
\le Ld^{7/4}
\]
for one numerical \(L\) and all sufficiently large \(n\).  The two failure
probabilities tend to zero uniformly along every sequence in the stated
range, proving the theorem.

%%% FIELD proof-balanced-converse
Fix a revealed graph \(G\), a graph-measurable assignment law
\(\mathcal D_G\), and any measurable estimator, including its independent
randomization \(U\).  For \(e\in\{0,1\}\), independently draw
\(p_e\) uniformly on \([0,1]\), and conditional on \(p_e\) draw
\(X_{ie}\), \(i\in V_n\), independently as Bernoulli variables with
success probability \(p_e\).  Define a random schedule by
\[
y_i(z)=
\begin{cases}
X_{i1},&z|_{N_G[i]}=1,\\
X_{i0},&z|_{N_G[i]}=0,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\tag{16}
\]
Every realization of (16) is fixed before assignment randomization, is a
function only of \(z|_{N_G[i]}\), and has endpoint values in \([0,1]\).
It therefore satisfies the fixed-schedule, ANI, and both empirical
second-moment assumptions, so this prior is supported on exactly
\(\mathcal M(G,\tau_{\mathrm{GATE}},2)\).

Consider one arm after an assignment has exposed exactly \(k\) of its
endpoint values, and let \(S\) be the number of observed successes.
Bayes' formula gives the posterior density proportional to
\(p^S(1-p)^{k-S}\), hence the beta density with parameters
\(S+1,k-S+1\).  If \(N=n-k\), conditional variance for the unobserved
Bernoulli values gives
\[
\operatorname{Var}\!\left(\frac1n\sum_iX_{ie}\,\middle|\,\text{observed
endpoint values}\right)
=\frac{N}{n^2}\mathbb E[p_e(1-p_e)\mid S]
+\frac{N^2}{n^2}\operatorname{Var}(p_e\mid S).
\tag{17}
\]
Under the uniform prior the beta-binomial predictive law satisfies
\[
\Pr(S=s)={k\choose s}\int_0^1p^s(1-p)^{k-s}\,dp=\frac1{k+1}.
\tag{18}
\]
Moreover \(\mathbb E[p_e(1-p_e)]=1/6\),
\[
\operatorname{Var}(p_e\mid S=s)
=\frac{(s+1)(k-s+1)}{(k+2)^2(k+3)},
\]
and direct summation gives
\[
\frac1{k+1}\sum_{s=0}^k(s+1)(k-s+1)
=\frac{(k+2)(k+3)}6.
\]
Averaging (17) over the observed endpoint values therefore yields
\[
v_n(k)=\frac{n-k}{6n^2}+
\frac{(n-k)^2}{6n^2(k+2)}
=\frac{(n-k)(n+2)}{6n^2(k+2)}.
\tag{19}
\]

Let \(T_G(z)\) and \(C_G(z)\) be the units whose closed neighborhoods are
fully treated and fully controlled.  By the definition of
\(\beta_{\mathrm{bal}}(G)\), for every \(z\),
\[
\min\{|T_G(z)|,|C_G(z)|\}\le\beta_{\mathrm{bal}}(G).
\tag{20}
\]
The two sets are disjoint, so \(\beta_{\mathrm{bal}}(G)\le n/2\).
Conditional on \(Z\), the two arm posteriors remain independent: their
priors are independent, the assignment law is outcome blind, and all
nonendpoint observations under (16) equal zero.  The auxiliary variable
\(U\) is independent and supplies no information about the schedule.
Since \(v_n(k)\) decreases in \(k\), equations (19)--(20) show, after
averaging over the endpoint observations, that the posterior variance of
\(\tau_{\mathrm{GATE}}(y)\) is at least
\[
v_n(\beta_{\mathrm{bal}}(G))
\ge\frac1{12\{\beta_{\mathrm{bal}}(G)+2\}}.
\tag{21}
\]
For any estimator, expansion around the posterior mean equals the posterior
variance plus a nonnegative squared term.  Bayes risk is therefore at
least (21).  Supremum risk dominates Bayes risk for a prior supported on
the outcome class, and the design and estimator were arbitrary.  Hence
\[
R_2(G)\ge\frac1{12\{\beta_{\mathrm{bal}}(G)+2\}}.
\tag{22}
\]

We now bound the graph quantity in (22).  A pair of sets \(T,C\) of equal
size \(s\) can be simultaneously fully exposed to opposite arms if and
only if every \(T\)-to-\(C\) distance exceeds two.  Necessity follows
because two closed neighborhoods at distance at most two intersect.
Conversely, when all such distances exceed two, treat
\(\bigcup_{t\in T}N_G[t]\) and control its complement; every member of
\(T\) is fully treated and every member of \(C\) fully controlled.  Thus
\[
\{\beta_{\mathrm{bal}}(G)\ge s\}
=\{\exists T,C:\ |T|=|C|=s, \operatorname{dist}_G(T,C)>2\}.
\tag{23}
\]

Choose a numerical \(K>192\), write \(d=d_n\), and set
\[
s=\left\lceil K\frac{n\log d}{d^2}\right\rceil.
\tag{24}
\]
Degree divergence and sparsity imply, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
that \(s\to\infty\), \(s\le n/4\), and \(ds/n\to0\).

We use two exact simple-completion switching estimates.  With the notation
of the marked-completion argument in (10), if \(R\ge8d^2\), then
\[
\Pr\{\hbox{marked endpoint lies in }S\mid H\}
\le\frac{2R_S}{R},\quad R_S=\sum_{v\in S}r_v.
\tag{25}
\]
If instead \(R_S\ge8d^2\), the reverse switching gives
\[
\Pr\{\hbox{marked endpoint lies in }S\mid H\}
\ge\frac{R_S}{3R}.
\tag{26}
\]
For completeness, to prove (26), start with a marked residual edge
\(ux\), \(x\notin S\), select an oriented residual edge \(wy\) with
\(w\in S\), and replace \(ux,wy\) by \(uw,xy\), marking \(uw\).
Among the \(R_S\) choices, coincidences and the two forbidden-edge tests
discard at most \(4d+2d^2\le R_S/2\).  Each resulting marked-in-\(S\)
state has at most \(R\) inverses.  If \(A_0,A_1\) count marked-outside and
marked-inside states, double counting gives
\(A_0R_S/2\le A_1R\), hence
\(A_1/(A_0+A_1)\ge R_S/(2R+R_S)\ge R_S/(3R)\).

Fix disjoint \(T,C\) of size \(s\), and expose every edge incident to
\(T\).  Conditional after every reveal, the compatible simple regular
completions are uniform.  At most \(ds\) edges have been exposed, so the
residual mass is at least \(nd-2ds\ge nd/2\ge8d^2\).  Call a reveal bad
when its other endpoint is in \(T\) or is an already discovered outside
neighbor of \(T\).  This set has at most \(2ds\) vertices, and (25) bounds
each conditional bad probability by
\[
q=\frac{8ds}{n}.
\tag{27}
\]
Let \(I\) count internal \(T\)-edges, let \(O\) count edges leaving
\(T\), and let \(L\) count distinct outside neighbors.  Then
\(2I+O=ds\), and the number of bad reveals is \(I+O-L\).  If
\(L<ds/2\), this number exceeds \(ds/4\): when \(O\le ds/2\), already
\(I\ge ds/4\); when \(O>ds/2\),
\(I+O-L=ds/2+O/2-L>O/2>ds/4\).  The adaptive union bound used in (14)
therefore gives
\[
\Pr(L<ds/2)\le2^{ds}q^{ds/4}.
\tag{28}
\]

On \(L\ge ds/2\), let \(W=N_{G_n}(T)\setminus T\).  If \(C\cap W\ne
\varnothing\), the event in (23) fails.  Otherwise the residual mass in
\(W\), after exposing \(T\), obeys
\[
R_W\ge d|W|-ds\ge d^2s/2-ds\ge d^2s/4\ge8d^2.
\tag{29}
\]
Expose the edges incident to \(C\).  Conditional on avoidance of \(W\),
the quantity \(R_W\) is unchanged.  Equation (26) and \(R\le nd\) show
that every reveal hits \(W\) with conditional probability at least
\(ds/(12n)\).  At least \(ds/2\) reveal steps are needed because one
internal \(C\)-edge consumes at most two \(C\)-degrees.  Hence
\[
\Pr\{\operatorname{dist}_{G_n}(T,C)>2,\ L\ge ds/2\}
\le\exp\!\left(-\frac{d^2s^2}{24n}\right).
\tag{30}
\]

There are at most \((en/s)^{2s}\) ordered disjoint pairs.  From (24), for
all sufficiently large \(d\),
\[
\log(en/s)\le3\log d,\quad
q\le16K\frac{\log d}{d}\le d^{-1/2}.
\tag{31}
\]
The logarithm of the union-bound contribution from (28) is therefore at
most
\[
6s\log d+ds\log2-\frac18ds\log d
\le-\frac1{32}ds\log d,
\tag{32}
\]
after increasing the fixed lower threshold for \(d\).  The corresponding
logarithm for (30) is at most
\[
6s\log d-\frac{d^2s^2}{24n}
\le-\left(\frac K{24}-6\right)s\log d.
\tag{33}
\]
Both bounds tend to minus infinity.  Equations (23)--(33) prove that, for
a numerical \(C_\beta\),
\[
\Pr\!\left\{\beta_{\mathrm{bal}}(G_n)
\le C_\beta\frac{n\log(d_n+1)}{d_n^2}\right\}\longrightarrow1.
\tag{34}
\]
The rounding in (24) is absorbed because
\(n\log d_n/d_n^2\to\infty\).  Combining (22) and (34), and absorbing the
additive two in (22) by the same divergence, gives a universal \(c>0\)
such that
\[
\Pr\!\left\{R_2(G_n)\ge
c\frac{d_n^2}{n\log(d_n+1)}\right\}\longrightarrow1.
\]
The initial design and estimator were arbitrary, so the lower bound has
the unrestricted quantifiers asserted in the theorem.

%%% FIELD proof-seed-upper
Fix a sufficiently large revealed graph \(G\) satisfying the stated
operator bound, and abbreviate
\[
d=d_n,\quad M=d^2+1,\quad k=k_{n,d_n},\quad B=B_G,\quad P=P_n,
\quad m_i=m_i(G),\quad q_i=1-m_i/n,\quad \pi_i=q_i^k.
\]
Degree sparsity implies \(M<n\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Since
\(m_i\le1+d+d(d-1)=M\),
\[
q_i\ge1-M/n>0,\quad \pi_i>0.
\tag{35}
\]
Thus every division below is valid.

On the augmented seed space define
\[
U_1=\frac1k\sum_{\ell=1}^k y_{J_\ell,1},\quad
U_0=\frac1n\sum_{i\in V_n}
\frac{\mathbf1\{Z^{\mathrm{seed}}|_{N_G[i]}=0\}}{\pi_i}y_{i0}.
\tag{36}
\]
Every seed is fully treated because its closed neighborhood is included in
the treated union.  Unit \(i\) is fully controlled exactly when every seed
avoids \(S_i(G)\): the neighborhoods \(N_G[J_\ell]\) and \(N_G[i]\)
intersect exactly when \(J_\ell\in S_i(G)\).  Independence of the uniform
seeds gives
\[
\Pr\{i\text{ is fully controlled}\mid G\}=q_i^k=\pi_i.
\tag{37}
\]
The fixed-schedule and ANI assumptions now imply
\[
\mathbb E(U_1\mid G)=\frac1n\sum_i y_{i1},\quad
\mathbb E(U_0\mid G)=\frac1n\sum_i y_{i0},
\tag{38}
\]
and the treated endpoint second-moment bound gives
\[
\operatorname{Var}(U_1\mid G)
\le\frac1{kn}\sum_i y_{i1}^2\le\frac1k.
\tag{39}
\]

Let \(h_{ij}=|S_i(G)\cap S_j(G)|=(B^2)_{ij}\), let
\(D_q=\operatorname{diag}(q_i)\), and define
\[
K=\frac1nD_q^{-1}BPB D_q^{-1}.
\tag{40}
\]
A single seed avoids \(S_i\cup S_j\) with probability
\(1-(m_i+m_j-h_{ij})/n\).  Therefore
\[
\frac{\Pr\{i,j\text{ are fully controlled}\mid G\}}{\pi_i\pi_j}
=\left(1+K_{ij}\right)^k.
\tag{41}
\]
Indeed, \((BPB)_{ij}=h_{ij}-m_im_j/n\), which gives (41) after division by
\(q_iq_j\).  Hence the covariance matrix of the normalized control
indicators is
\[
Q=\sum_{r=1}^k{k\choose r}K^{\circ r}.
\tag{42}
\]
The matrix \(K\) is positive semidefinite because
\[
K=\frac1n(D_q^{-1}BP)(D_q^{-1}BP)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
If two matrices are Gram matrices, their entrywise product is the Gram
matrix of the tensor products of their representing vectors.  Thus every
Schur power in (42) is positive semidefinite.

For a positive semidefinite \(K\), a symmetric matrix \(H\), and
\(a\ge\max_iK_{ii}\), Schur multiplying
\(-\|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}I\preceq H\preceq\|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}I\) by \(K\)
gives
\[
\|K\circ H\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le a\|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}.
\tag{43}
\]
Here (35) gives
\[
K_{ii}=\frac{m_i}{n-m_i}\le a_0:=\frac{M}{n-M},
\tag{44}
\]
and
\[
\|K\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le\frac{\|BP\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2}{n(1-M/n)^2},\quad
\frac{\|K\|_{\mathrm{op}}}{a_0}
\le\frac{\|BP\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2}{M(1-M/n)}.
\tag{45}
\]
Applying (43) inductively in (42), then using (44)--(45), yields
\[
\|Q\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le\frac{\|BP\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2}{M(1-M/n)}
\{(1-M/n)^{-k}-1\}.
\tag{46}
\]
By the control endpoint second-moment bound,
\[
\operatorname{Var}(U_0\mid G)
=\frac1{n^2}y_0^{\mathsf T}Qy_0
\le\frac{\|Q\|_{\mathrm{op}}}{n}.
\tag{47}
\]
Equations (38)--(47) and
\(\operatorname{Var}(U_1-U_0)\le2\operatorname{Var}(U_1)+
2\operatorname{Var}(U_0)\) prove
\[
\mathbb E[(U_1-U_0-\tau_{\mathrm{GATE}}(y))^2\mid G]
\le \frac2k+
\frac{2\|BP\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2\{(1-M/n)^{-k}-1\}}
{nM(1-M/n)}.
\tag{48}
\]

Let \(\mathcal F=\sigma(G,Z^{\mathrm{seed}},Y)\).  The schedule is fixed,
so \(Y\) is determined by \((G,Z^{\mathrm{seed}})\).  Whenever
\(J_\ell=i\), ANI makes \(Y_i=y_{i1}\), and the control term in (36) is
already \(\mathcal F\)-measurable.  The definition of \(w_i\) consequently
gives
\[
\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{seed}}
=\mathbb E[U_1-U_0\mid\mathcal F].
\tag{49}
\]
The tower property proves unbiasedness, and conditional Jensen shows that
the MSE of \(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{seed}}\) is no larger than (48).  This
also proves observed-data measurability; the weights are conditional
averages over a finite seed space and depend only on \(G\) and the realized
assignment.

It remains to evaluate (48).  Put \(x=M/n\) and
\(h=-\log(1-x)\).  Degree sparsity gives \(x\to0\), so eventually
\(h\le2x\).  Also
\(\log(d+1)/(4h)\to\infty\); hence the defining floor satisfies
\[
k\ge\frac{\log(d+1)}{8h},\quad
(1-x)^{-k}=e^{hk}\le(d+1)^{1/4}.
\tag{50}
\]
Using the assumed \(\|BP\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le Ld^{7/4}\), equations
(48)--(50), \(M\asymp d^2\), and \(1-x\ge1/2\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sup_y\mathbb E[(\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{seed}}-
\tau_{\mathrm{GATE}}(y))^2\mid G]
&\le \frac{32M}{n\log(d+1)}
+\frac{4L^2d^{7/2}(d+1)^{1/4}}{nM}\\
&\le C\frac{d^2}{n\log(d+1)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{51}
\]
The final inequality uses the elementary uniform bound
\(\log(d+1)\le C_0d^{1/4}\) for \(d\ge1\).  All constants are numerical
once the universal \(L\) in the graph event is fixed.  This proves the
claimed worst-case bound over the exact outcome class.

%%% FIELD proof-finite-compiler
The first branch test compares the integers \(d_n^2+1\) and \(n\), so it
always terminates.  If it fails, the compiler forms no seed-side object.
It outputs \(Z=\boldsymbol{1}_n\) and evaluates
\(n^{-1}\sum_iY_i\) using one pass through the observed vector, which costs
\(O(n)\) time and \(O(n)\) output storage.

Suppose \(d_n^2+1<n\).  A breadth-two exploration from every root examines
at most \(d_n+d_n^2=O(d_n^2)\) adjacency-list entries per root.  Writing
the dense Boolean matrix costs an additional \(O(n^2)\), so computing
\(B_G\) and all row sums \(m_i(G)\) costs
\[
O(nd_n^2+n^2)
\tag{52}
\]
elementary operations.  The strict branch inequality implies
\(m_i(G)\le d_n^2+1<n\), hence every
\(\pi_i^{(0)}(G)=(1-m_i(G)/n)^{k_{n,d_n}}\) is positive.

The integer \(k=k_{n,d_n}\) is computable without floating-point
comparisons.  With \(M=d_n^2+1\), search for the largest integer \(j\ge1\)
satisfying
\[
\left(1-\frac Mn\right)^{-4j}\le d_n+1,
\tag{53}
\]
returning one if no such \(j\) exists.  Monotonicity of the logarithm shows
that (53) is exactly the floor rule in the definition, including equality
cases.  The search terminates because \((1-M/n)^{-4}>1\), and every test
in (53) is rational.

There are exactly \(n^k\) ordered seed tuples.  For each tuple, mark the
union of its \(k\) closed neighborhoods in \(O(k(d_n+1)+n)\) time, use the
resulting \(n\)-bit assignment as a group key, and update the \(n\) seed
multiplicity totals for that group.  After division by the group count and
by \(k\), these totals are exactly the conditional probabilities
\(w_i(z;G)\).  Thus full grouping costs
\[
O\!\left(n^k[n+k(d_n+1)]\right)
\tag{54}
\]
time.  There are at most \(\min\{2^n,n^k\}\) groups, each storing an
assignment and \(n\) multiplicity totals, giving
\[
O\!\left(n\min\{2^n,n^k\}\right)
\tag{55}
\]
memory.

The quadratic risk certificate can also be compared exactly.  For each
supported assignment \(z\), the definition of the estimator and ANI write
its centered error as \(h_z^{\mathsf T}x\), where
\(x=(y_{11},\ldots,y_{n1},y_{10},\ldots,y_{n0})\) and the coefficient
vector \(h_z\in\mathbb Q^{2n}\) is determined by the grouped weights and
the inverse exposure probabilities.  If \(p_z\in\mathbb Q\) is the group
probability, form
\[
H_G=\sum_zp_zh_zh_z^{\mathsf T}.
\tag{56}
\]
This is a rational positive semidefinite matrix and the design MSE is
\(x^{\mathsf T}H_Gx\).  The separate endpoint constraints imply
\(\|x\|_2^2\le2n\), so the exactly specified number
\[
\mathcal U_G=2n\lambda_{\max}(H_G)
\tag{57}
\]
is a valid worst-case quadratic risk certificate.  To test
\(\mathcal U_G\le1\), test positive semidefiniteness of the rational matrix
\((2n)^{-1}I_{2n}-H_G\) by exact symmetric elimination.  This terminates
after finitely many rational operations and has no floating-point tie
ambiguity.  Forming (56) costs
\(O(n^2\min\{2^n,n^k\})\) additional arithmetic operations and exact
elimination costs \(O(n^3)\); both use \(O(n^2)\) additional memory, which
is within (55).  Thus the certificate branch is total.  This explicit
choice also clarifies that ``certificate'' means a rigorously valid upper
certificate, not an oracle use of unknown outcomes.
If \(v=(v_1,v_0)\) is a unit top eigenvector of \(H_G\), then
\(\max\{\|v_1\|_2^2,\|v_0\|_2^2\}\ge1/2\).  Scaling \(v\) so that the
larger block has norm \(\sqrt n\) produces endpoint vectors satisfying both
moment constraints and shows that the actual worst-case seed risk is at
least \(n\lambda_{\max}(H_G)\).  Consequently \(\mathcal U_G\) is no more
than twice that worst-case risk.  This comparison is deterministic and can
be combined with any valid risk bound without modifying the compiler.

To sample the selected seed law, draw \(k\) independent uniform integers,
mark their closed neighborhoods, and emit the length-\(n\) assignment.
This costs \(O(k(d_n+1)+n)\) time.  For a supported realized assignment,
lookup of the precomputed group gives all \(w_i(Z;G)\); the formula in
Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}seed\mbox{-}estimator}\) then uses only
\(G,Z,Y\).  Every branch is finite, every inverse probability is formed
only where it is positive, and the claimed time and memory bounds follow.
For the boundary instance \((n,d_n)=(4,3)\), the first test fails and the
safe \(O(n)\) branch is used.

the required signed comparison, and promoting it to an assumption would
assume the crux.
