The previously established completion-switching calculation remains available.  On
the dense subrange, with \(\eta=d_n^2/n\) and
\(K=\lceil20\log n/\log(1/\eta)\rceil\), it gives, uniformly for
\(m\le 2K+2\),
\[
 \mathbb E(C_{12})_m\le \{1+o(1)\}^m\eta^m,
 \qquad
 n^2\mathbb E\{(B_{G_n})_{12}-Q_{12}\}^2
 \le n^{-18+o(1)},
\]
and hence
\[
 \|(B_{G_n}-Q)P_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}=o_{\Pr}(d_n^{7/4}).
\]

The newly prescribed graphwise conditional second-moment estimate is false.  Here
is an explicit admissible witness.  Let \(q\) range through primes and let
\(\mathcal L_q\) be the incidence graph of the projective plane obtained from
\(\mathbb F_q^3\): its points are the one-dimensional subspaces, its lines are the
two-dimensional subspaces, and containment is incidence.  Put
\[
 v=q^2+q+1,\qquad d=q+1,
 \qquad m=\lfloor q^{1/3}\rfloor,
 \qquad n=2mv,
\]
and let \(G_q\) be the disjoint union of \(m\) copies of \(\mathcal L_q\), relabeled
on \(V_n\).  Counting one-dimensional subspaces gives \(v=(q^3-1)/(q-1)\);
duality gives the same number of lines.  Each point or line has degree \(q+1=d\).
Two distinct points span a unique two-dimensional subspace, and two distinct
two-dimensional subspaces intersect in a unique one-dimensional subspace.
Consequently \(G_q\) is a simple \(d\)-regular graph.  Moreover,
\[
 \frac{n^{2/5}}d=O(q^{-1/15})\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 \frac d{\sqrt n}=O(q^{-1/6})\longrightarrow0,
\]
so these graphs lie in the prescribed dense asymptotic range, and \(nd\) is even.

Write \(A=A_{G_q}\), \(W=A^2+A-(d-1)I_n\), \(B=B_{G_q}\), and
\(E=W-B\).  For two distinct vertices in one component, vertices in the same
bipartition class have exactly one common neighbor, while vertices in opposite
classes are either incident or have neither an edge nor a length-two path.  Vertices
in different components have no route of length at most two.  Thus every
off-diagonal entry of \(W\) is zero or one, \(B=W\), and
\[
 E=0,
 \qquad Z_{V_n,V_n}=0. \tag{11}
\]

Consider the switch kernel that chooses an ordered pair of oriented edges uniformly
from the \((nd)^2\) ordered pairs, performs the corresponding simple two-switch
when valid, and otherwise stays put.  Fix in one component an incident point-line
pair \(pL\).  There are \(v-d=q^2\) points \(u\notin L\).  For each such \(u\),
there are \(d-1=q\) lines \(M\ni u\) with \(p\notin M\).  Hence the graph has
\[
 mvdq^3
\]
ordered oriented-edge pairs \((p,L),(M,u)\) for which the valid switch deletes
\(pL,uM\) and adds \(pM,uL\).

Each such switch creates at least \(8(d-2)\) ordered excess-route entries.  Indeed,
let \(r_0=L\cap M\).  For every point
\(r\in M\setminus\{u,r_0\}\), the pair \(p,r\) retains its unique old common line
and gains the distinct common line \(M\), giving \(d-2\) unordered point pairs.
The excluded point \(r_0\) is necessary because deleting \(pL\) removes its old
route.  Let \(N_0\) be the unique line through \(p,u\).  For every old line
\(N\ni p\), \(N\notin\{L,N_0\}\), the pair \(M,N\) retains its unique old
intersection point and gains the common point \(p\), giving another \(d-2\)
unordered pairs.  The excluded line \(N_0\) is necessary because deleting \(uM\)
removes its old route.  The switch edge \(uL\) gives two analogous, disjoint
families.  Symmetry of \(E\) turns the four families of \(d-2\) unordered pairs
into \(8(d-2)\) ordered entries.  Since the pre-switch
matrix in (11) is zero and the post-switch excess matrix is entrywise nonnegative,
there is no cancelling decrement.  Therefore, for \(\Delta Z=Z_{V_n,V_n}(G_q')-
Z_{V_n,V_n}(G_q)\),
\[
 \mathbb E\{(\Delta Z)^2\mid G_q\}
 \ge \frac{mvdq^3}{(nd)^2}\,64(d-2)^2
 =\frac{16q^3(d-2)^2}{mvd}
 \ge \frac{2d^2}{3m}                                      \tag{12}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(q\), where \(v\le3q^2\), \(d\le2q\), and
\(d-2\ge d/2\) were used in the last inequality.  The same lower bound holds if
one samples uniformly only among valid oriented switches, because their total
number is at most \((nd)^2\).

For \(S=T=V_n\), the proposed estimate would instead give, by (11),
\[
 \mathbb E\{(\Delta Z)^2\mid G_q\}
 \le \frac{C}{nd}\{dZ_{V_n,V_n}+d^2(|V_n|+|V_n|)\}
 =2Cd.                                                     \tag{13}
\]
But \(d/m\to\infty\), so (12) contradicts (13) for every universal \(C\).
Thus a forward/reverse switching argument cannot establish the requested
graph-uniform conditional estimate.  This witness does not refute the unconditional
tail inequality under the uniform random regular law, because \(G_q\) can be a
rare realization; proving that tail would require a good-graph conditional estimate
plus control of its exceptional set, or a different concentration argument.  The
previous exact \(n=8,d=3\) check remains
\[
 \mu_{\mathrm{cyc}}
 =\frac{360638638}{467595722405},
\]
which verifies the finite moment algebra but has no asymptotic implication.

All statistical implications after the missing dense graph certificate remain
proved.  Let
\[
 H_n=\{\|B_{G_n}P_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le Ld_n^{7/4}\}.
\]
The degree assumptions imply
\[
 r_{\log}(n,d_n)
 =\frac{d_n^2/n}{\log(d_n+1)}\longrightarrow0.
\]
On \(H_n\), the established seed upper theorem bounds the exact worst-case risk of
the seed procedure by \(C_0r_{\log}(n,d_n)\).  The bound is eventually below one,
so the established finite compiler selects that seed law on \(H_n\); its termination
and observed-data measurability follow from the established compiler proposition.
The definition of minimax risk then yields
\[
 R_2(G_n)\le C_0r_{\log}(n,d_n)\qquad\text{on }H_n.
\]
Independently, the established all-procedure converse supplies events \(L_n\) with
\(\Pr_{G_n}(L_n)\to1\) on which
\[
 R_2(G_n)\ge c_0r_{\log}(n,d_n).
\]
Hence the advertised matched frontier follows on \(H_n\cap L_n\), and would hold
with probability tending to one if \(\Pr_{G_n}(H_n)\to1\) were proved.  The sparse
branch and the dense Boolean-polynomial truncation are available, but the newly
requested graphwise switching variance estimate is disproved by (11)--(13).
Nothing in that counterexample disproves the high-probability event \(H_n\) for a
uniform random regular graph.  The unconditional upper edge, and therefore the
matched frontier, remains open.
