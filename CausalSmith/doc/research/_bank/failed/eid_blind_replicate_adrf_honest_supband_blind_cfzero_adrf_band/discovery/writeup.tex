\section*{eid\_\allowbreak{}blind\_\allowbreak{}replicate\_\allowbreak{}adrf\_\allowbreak{}honest\_\allowbreak{}supband}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} blind\_\allowbreak{}cfzero\_\allowbreak{}adrf\_\allowbreak{}band\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} This is a statistical-inference paper about a causal ADRF under two blind nonidentical asymmetric replicates. Analytic cocycle identification and an explicit structural response-surface condition establish the causal identifying functional. The finite-dimensional, semi-infinite deterministic constrained-sieve band is uniformly honest, clipped to \(0\le W_n\le2B_Y\), and has uniformly \(o(1)\) expected maximal width after Sobolev-modulus constraints are added. A parallel abstract exact-class band gives the same qualitative consistency conclusion. Neither construction proves the candidate \(r_n\) rate, and the fixed-nuisance Bernoulli lower modulus remains unresolved, so there is no matched honest-band rate.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Existing statistical results cover blind repeated-measurement density estimation, confidence bands under auxiliary-error, symmetric-error, or fixed-operator designs, and pointwise continuous-treatment inference. They do not provide a quantitative simultaneous ADRF band rate for blind nonidentical asymmetric replicate errors with open spectral zero sets. The remaining upper-side gap is an explicit modulus for the protected-cube marked analytic inverse, and the remaining lower-side gap is the fixed-nuisance Bernoulli packet needed for a sharp all-band converse.

\paragraph{Niche.} The inferential niche is a nonlinear severe inverse problem: compact treatment support supplies entire transforms, one centered replicate fixes translation, the moment radius itself supplies a protected near-zero neighborhood, and the causal target is a stratum-weighted quotient. Sobolev moduli compactify both exact and finite-sieve signal sequences, converting qualitative channel identification into uniform consistency while keeping quantitative rate inversion separate.

\paragraph{Fill.} The paper delivers two uniformly honest and uniformly consistent inference maps. The finite-dimensional, semi-infinite deterministic construction uses spline, frequency, truncation, and atomic dimensions; simultaneous probability-, marked-, and mass-channel radii; equicontinuous relaxed outer signal sieves; a joint feasible set; and measurable clipped ADRF extrema. Its expected maximal width is bounded by \(\widetilde\omega_n+2b_{\mu,n}+2B_Y|\mathcal X|e^{-np_{\min}/8}=o(1)\). No finite decision algorithm is claimed. In parallel, exact empirical-channel residuals are profiled over a compact projected nuisance class and yield a conceptual exact-class band. Both arguments are qualitative: no explicit \(O(r_n)\) upper or matching marked converse is claimed.

\section{Related work}

Capitao-Miniconi, Gassiat, and Lehéricy provide the unmarked probability-channel cocycle and compact-support density analysis that inform this paper. Their Theorem 1 assumes coordinate independence for the noise and condition H2 for the signal; compact support places the present probability signal inside H2 with \(\rho=1\). The present unmarked probability submodel is therefore contained in their normalized scalar signal-identification setting, but is stricter because it additionally fixes error moments and centers \(U_2\); their theorem also identifies error laws up to translation, whereas the present marked theorem claims only \(f_x,q_x\). Their rate and minimax theorems add second-moment and protected-frequency conditions and concern \(L^2\) density risk, so those rates do not imply an ADRF supremum-band rate. Gassiat, Le Corff, and Lehéricy supply the broader analytic-continuation principle for blind deconvolution. Huang and Zhang formulate the error-contaminated continuous-treatment ADRF and derive pointwise inference with a known everywhere-nonzero error transform. Kato and Sasaki (2018) develop deconvolution bands with an auxiliary error sample and a symmetric-error repeated-measurement route; Kato and Sasaki (2019) establish uniform bands under conventional spectral conditions. Kato, Sasaki, and Ura invert Kotlarski restrictions without completeness. Babii studies honest expected diameter for fixed linear inverse operators. Low supplies a testing-to-expected-length reduction only after a marked modulus is established. None supplies the missing quantitative marked inverse or the fixed-nuisance Bernoulli converse for the present blind quotient problem.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:iid]\label{ass:iid}
\(O_1,\ldots,O_n\) are independent draws from \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (i.i.d. superpopulation sampling, \cite{HuangZhang2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:strata]\label{ass:strata}
\(\min_{x\in\mathcal X}p_x\ge p_{\min}>0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (uniform finite-stratum positivity, \cite{HuangZhang2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:consistency]\label{ass:consistency}
\(Y=Y(A)\) almost surely.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (consistency, \cite{HuangZhang2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:unconfoundedness]\label{ass:unconfoundedness}
\(A\perp Y(a)\mid X\) for every \(a\in\mathcal A\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (continuous-treatment unconfoundedness, \cite{HuangZhang2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:error-latent-independence]\label{ass:error-latent-independence}
\((U_1,U_2)\perp(A,X,Y(\cdot),Y)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (classical repeated-measurement independence, \cite{CapitaoMiniconiGassiatLehericy2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:error-coordinate-independence]\label{ass:error-coordinate-independence}
\(U_1\perp U_2\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (independent replicate errors, \cite{CapitaoMiniconiGassiatLehericy2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:center-two]\label{ass:center-two}
\(E_P[U_2]=0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Kotlarski location normalization, \cite{KatoSasakiUra2021}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:error-moment]\label{ass:error-moment}
For a fixed \(q_e\in(1,\infty)\), \(\max_{j\in\{1,2\}}E_P[|U_j|^{q_e}]\le M_e\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This paper fixes one common \(q_e\)-moment radius for a fixed \(q_e>1\). The strict inequality supplies first-moment uniform integrability for centering, while the same radius automatically supplies the protected neighborhood used in identification and inference.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:compact-dose]\label{ass:compact-dose}
\(P(A\in\mathcal A)=1\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (compact latent-signal support, \cite{CapitaoMiniconiGassiatLehericy2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:bounded-outcome]\label{ass:bounded-outcome}
\(|Y(a)|\le B_Y\) almost surely for every \(a\in\mathcal A\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (uniformly bounded potential outcomes, \cite{HuangZhang2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:f-sobolev]\label{ass:f-sobolev}
For a fixed \(\beta\in(1/2,\infty)\), \(\sup_{x\in\mathcal X}\|f_x\|_{H^{\beta}(\mathbb R)}\le R_f\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Sobolev density ball, \cite{CapitaoMiniconiGassiatLehericy2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:q-sobolev]\label{ass:q-sobolev}
For a fixed \(\beta\in(1/2,\infty)\), \(\sup_{x\in\mathcal X}\|q_x\|_{H^{\beta}(\mathbb R)}\le R_q\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Sobolev outcome-weighted regression ball, \cite{KatoSasaki2019}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:denominator]\label{ass:denominator}
\(\inf_{x\in\mathcal X}\inf_{a\in I}f_x(a)\ge\kappa\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (continuous-treatment overlap on an interior interval, \cite{HuangZhang2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:causal-mean-continuity]\label{ass:causal-mean-continuity}
For every \(x\in\mathcal X\), the map \(a\mapsto E_P[Y(a)\mid X=x]\) is continuous on \(I\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Continuity is the explicit pointwise bridge needed to extend the almost-everywhere equality between the observed regression quotient and the stratum-specific causal mean to every dose in the target interval.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:structural-response-surface]\label{ass:structural-response-surface}
There exist a standard Borel measurable space \((\mathcal V,\mathscr V)\), a \(\mathcal V\)-valued random element \(V\), a jointly measurable map \(h:\mathcal A\times\mathcal X\times\mathcal V\to[-B_Y,B_Y]\), and one measurable event \(\Omega_0\) such that \(V\perp A\mid X\), \(P(\Omega_0)=1\), and \(Y(a,\omega)=h(a,X(\omega),V(\omega))\) for every \(\omega\in\Omega_0\) and every \(a\in\mathcal A\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} The single jointly measurable response surface and common full-probability event rule out treatment-indexed diagonal modifications on null sets and make the latent regression-to-causal-mean bridge valid for the whole dose schedule.
\end{assumption}

\begin{definition}[def:signal-sieves]\label{def:signal-sieves}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \(\mathscr F_n\) and \(\mathscr Q_n(f)\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Let \(\mathsf B_{k,K_n}\), \(1\le k\le K_n+d\), be the normalized order-\(d\) B-splines on \(\mathcal A\). Define
\[

 \end{array}
 \right\}.

\[

 |q(a)-q(b)|\le\Omega_{R_q}(|a-b|)+2a_{q,n}
 \quad\forall a,b\in\mathcal A
 \end{array}
 \right\}.
\]
These are outer approximation families: their elements need not be densities or outcome-weighted densities. Their closed continuum inequalities and integral bounds make them compact subsets of the corresponding finite-dimensional spline spaces. Because the modulus restrictions range over all \((a,b)\in\mathcal A^2\), the profiling problem is finite-dimensional but semi-infinite; no finite decision algorithm is asserted.

\end{definition}

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:factorization]\label{lem:factorization}
For every \(P\in\mathcal M\), every \(x\in\mathcal X\), and all real \(s,t\), \(F_x(s,t)=\widehat f_x(s+t)\phi_1(s)\phi_2(t)\) and \(G_x(s,t)=\widehat q_x(s+t)\phi_1(s)\phi_2(t)\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(P\in\mathcal M\), \(x\in\mathcal X\), and \(s,t\in\mathbb R\).  By
\(\mathrm{ass{:}strata}\), \(p_x>0\), so conditioning on \(X=x\) is
well defined.  From the definitions of the replicates,
\[
 e^{i(sS_1+tS_2)}=e^{i(s+t)A}e^{isU_1}e^{itU_2}.
\]
The independence in \(\mathrm{ass{:}error\text{-}latent\text{-}independence}\)
and \(\mathrm{ass{:}error\text{-}coordinate\text{-}independence}\) therefore
gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 F_x(s,t)
 &=E_P[e^{i(s+t)A}\mid X=x]E_P[e^{isU_1}]E_P[e^{itU_2}]\\
 &=\left\{\int_{\mathcal A}e^{i(s+t)a}f_x(a)\,da\right\}
      \phi_1(s)\phi_2(t)\\
 &=\widehat f_x(s+t)\phi_1(s)\phi_2(t).
\end{aligned}
\]
For the marked channel, the same two independence assumptions allow the
bounded mark to remain with the latent variables.  By the definitions of
\(m_x\) and \(q_x=m_xf_x\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 G_x(s,t)
 &=E_P[Ye^{i(s+t)A}\mid X=x]E_P[e^{isU_1}]E_P[e^{itU_2}]\\
 &=\left\{\int_{\mathcal A}e^{i(s+t)a}
      E_P[Y\mid A=a,X=x]f_x(a)\,da\right\}\phi_1(s)\phi_2(t)\\
 &=\widehat q_x(s+t)\phi_1(s)\phi_2(t).
\end{aligned}
\]
The first integrand has modulus one.  For the second,
\(\mathrm{ass{:}consistency}\) and
\(\mathrm{ass{:}bounded\text{-}outcome}\) imply \(|Y|\le B_Y\) almost
surely, so every expectation exists.  Finally,
\(\mathrm{ass{:}compact\text{-}dose}\) justifies writing both integrals over
\(\mathcal A\).  This proves the two identities.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the observable two-channel factorization on which injectivity and inference rest. \textit{Gap.} Capitao-Miniconi, Gassiat, and Lehéricy analyze only the first identity; the marked identity is absent from their model. \textit{Consumer.} The empirical characteristic-function confidence set uses these residual equations jointly.

\begin{theorem}[thm:marked-cocycle-id]\label{thm:marked-cocycle-id}
The map \(P\mapsto P_{\mathrm{obs}}\) is injective for \(\{(f_x,q_x):x\in\mathcal X\}\) over \(\mathcal M\). On a common neighborhood of zero, \(\left.\partial_t\log F_x(u,t)\right|_{t=0}=\widehat f_x'(u)/\widehat f_x(u)+\phi_2'(0)=\widehat f_x'(u)/\widehat f_x(u)\), because \(\phi_2(0)=1\) and \(\phi_2'(0)=iE_P[U_2]=0\). Together with \(\widehat f_x(0)=1\), this derivative identity uniquely fixes the latent translation without a normalization on \(U_1\). Moreover, \(\widehat q_x(u)=\widehat f_x(u)G_x(u,0)/F_x(u,0)\). Compact support makes both recovered transforms entire, so the local formulas determine \(f_x\) and \(q_x\) globally even when either error transform has an open zero set away from zero.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(P^{(0)},P^{(1)}\in\mathcal M\) induce the same observed-data law, and
fix \(x\in\mathcal X\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}strata}\) gives
\(p_x\ge p_{\min}>0\), so that common law determines \(F_x\) and \(G_x\).

We first derive a common nonvanishing neighborhood.  For either candidate
law, either \(j\in\{1,2\}\), and every real \(v\), the elementary bound
\(|e^{iz}-1|\le|z|\), H\"older's inequality, and
\(\mathrm{ass{:}error\text{-}moment}\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\phi_j(v)|
 &\ge1-|\phi_j(v)-1|\\
 &\ge1-E_P|e^{ivU_j}-1|\\
 &\ge1-|v|E_P|U_j|\\
 &\ge1-|v|\{E_P|U_j|^{q_e}\}^{1/q_e}
 \ge1-|v|M_e^{1/q_e}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus, with
\[
 \nu_*=(2M_e^{1/q_e})^{-1},\qquad c_*=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \delta=\min\left\{\frac{\nu_*}{2},\frac{1}{4B_A}\right\}>0,
\]
one has \(|\phi_j(v)|\ge c_*\) on \(|v|\le\nu_*\).  Moreover,
\(\mathrm{ass{:}compact\text{-}dose}\) and the fact that \(f_x\) is a
conditional density imply, for \(|w|\le2\delta\),
\[
 |\widehat f_x(w)-1|
 \le\int_{\mathcal A}|e^{iwa}-1|f_x(a)\,da
 \le B_A|w|\le\frac12.
\]
Hence \(|\widehat f_x(w)|\ge1/2\).  If \(|u|,|t|\le\delta\), the
factorization lemma yields
\[
 |F_x(u,t)|
 =|\widehat f_x(u+t)|\,|\phi_1(u)|\,|\phi_2(t)|
 \ge\frac18>0.                                                    \tag{1}
\]
This uses only the moment-derived neighborhood, not global nonvanishing.

The same H\"older estimate gives \(E_P|U_2|<\infty\), while compact dose support
gives \(|A|\le B_A\) almost surely.  Dominated differentiation is therefore
valid at \(t=0\).  Define the observed logarithmic derivative
\[
 D_x(u)=
 \frac{\left.\partial_tF_x(u,t)\right|_{t=0}}{F_x(u,0)},
 \qquad |u|\le\delta.
\]
Differentiating the factorization and using (1) gives
\[
 D_x(u)=\frac{\widehat f_x'(u)}{\widehat f_x(u)}
       +\frac{\phi_2'(0)}{\phi_2(0)}.
\]
Characteristic-function normalization gives \(\phi_2(0)=1\), and
\(\mathrm{ass{:}center\text{-}two}\) gives
\(\phi_2'(0)=iE_P[U_2]=0\).  Therefore every candidate satisfies
\[
 \widehat f_x'(u)=D_x(u)\widehat f_x(u),
 \qquad \widehat f_x(0)=1,
 \qquad |u|\le\delta.                                            \tag{2}
\]
For the two candidate laws, \(D_x\) is common.  Their transforms are
nonzero on this interval, so
\[
 R_x(u)=\frac{\widehat f_x^{(1)}(u)}{\widehat f_x^{(0)}(u)}
\]
satisfies \(R_x'(u)=0\) and \(R_x(0)=1\).  Consequently
\[
 \widehat f_x^{(1)}(u)=\widehat f_x^{(0)}(u),
 \qquad |u|\le\delta.                                            \tag{3}
\]
Only \(\phi_2\) was differentiated; no location normalization on \(U_1\)
has been used.

Let \(h=f_x^{(1)}-f_x^{(0)}\).  It is integrable and supported on
\(\mathcal A\).  For every \(z\in\mathbb C\), Tonelli's theorem gives
\[
 H(z):=\int_{\mathcal A}e^{iza}h(a)\,da
 =\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(iz)^k}{k!}
       \int_{\mathcal A}a^kh(a)\,da,                             \tag{4}
\]
because
\[
 \int_{\mathcal A}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}
 \frac{|za|^k}{k!}|h(a)|\,da
 \le e^{|z|B_A}\|h\|_1<\infty.
\]
Thus \(H\) is entire.  Equation (3) makes it zero on a real interval;
therefore every derivative at zero is zero, and the power series (4) gives
\(H(z)=0\) for every \(z\in\mathbb C\).

At \((u,0)\), the two channel factorizations and (1) give, for each
candidate,
\[
 \widehat q_x(u)=\widehat f_x(u)\frac{G_x(u,0)}{F_x(u,0)},
 \qquad |u|\le\delta.                                            \tag{5}
\]
The right-hand side is common, so the two marked transforms agree locally.
Furthermore, consistency and bounded outcomes imply \(|Y|\le B_Y\), hence
conditional Jensen's inequality and \(q_x=m_xf_x\) give
\[
 |q_x(a)|\le B_Yf_x(a)\quad\text{almost everywhere},
 \qquad \|q_x\|_1\le B_Y.                                       \tag{6}
\]
Each \(q_x\) is supported on \(\mathcal A\).  Applying (4) to the
difference of the two marked functions extends the local equality in (5) to
all complex arguments.

It remains only to turn equality of Fourier transforms into equality of the
underlying functions.  If \(h\) is either signal difference, then
\(h\in L^1(\mathbb R)\) and \(\widehat h(t)=0\) for every real \(t\).  For
\(\epsilon>0\), let
\[
 g_\epsilon(v)=(2\pi\epsilon)^{-1/2}e^{-v^2/(2\epsilon)}.
\]
The Gaussian integral and Fubini's theorem yield
\[
 (h*g_\epsilon)(a)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}
 e^{-ita}\widehat h(t)e^{-\epsilon t^2/2}\,dt=0.                \tag{7}
\]
Also
\[
 \|h*g_\epsilon-h\|_1
 \le\int g_\epsilon(v)\|h(\cdot-v)-h\|_1\,dv\longrightarrow0. \tag{8}
\]
Indeed, translation continuity follows first for compactly supported step
functions and then for arbitrary \(L^1\) functions by approximation; (8)
then follows by splitting at a fixed small \(|v|\) and using concentration of
\(g_\epsilon\) at zero.  Equations (7)--(8) imply \(h=0\) almost
everywhere.

Finally, \(\mathrm{ass{:}f\text{-}sobolev}\),
\(\mathrm{ass{:}q\text{-}sobolev}\), and \(\beta>1/2\) give unique
continuous representatives.  For either difference \(h\), Cauchy--Schwarz
gives
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}|\widehat h(t)|\,dt
 \le
 \left\{\int(1+t^2)^\beta|\widehat h(t)|^2\,dt\right\}^{1/2}
 \left\{\int(1+t^2)^{-\beta}\,dt\right\}^{1/2}<\infty.
\]
Fourier inversion therefore yields a continuous representative, and two
continuous representatives equal almost everywhere only if they agree
everywhere.  Since \(x\) was arbitrary, the common observed law determines
every \(f_x\) and \(q_x\).  The proof remains valid when either error
characteristic function has an open zero set away from zero.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} It makes the causal ratio a functional of the observed marked replicate law without symmetry or global nonvanishing. \textit{Gap.} Theorem 1 of Capitao-Miniconi, Gassiat, and Lehéricy gives the probability-channel cocycle; it does not state the outcome-weighted signed channel or its compatibility with the recovered probability channel. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies the law-level target set for honest-band inversion in two-recall exposure studies.

\begin{theorem}[thm:adrf-id]\label{thm:adrf-id}
For every \(P\in\mathcal M\) and \(a\in I\), the ADRF is point identified by \(\mu(a)=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}p_xq_x(a)/f_x(a)\), where every term on the right is a functional of \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(P\in\mathcal M\), \(x\in\mathcal X\), and \(a\in I\). Positivity gives \(p_x>0\). Let \((\mathcal V,\mathscr V)\), \(V\), and \(h\) be supplied by the structural-response assumption, and define
\[
 K_x(C)=P(V\in C\mid X=x),
 \qquad
 g_x(u)=\int_{\mathcal V}h(u,x,v)\,K_x(dv).
\]
Joint measurability makes \(g_x\) measurable, and boundedness makes it finite. The common probability-one structural equality, evaluated at random \(A\), and consistency give \(Y=h(A,X,V)\) almost surely. For every bounded Borel \(\psi\), conditional independence \(V\perp A\mid X\) and the density \(f_x\) yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_P[Y\psi(A)\mid X=x]
 &=\int_{\mathcal A}\int_{\mathcal V}h(u,x,v)\psi(u)K_x(dv)f_x(u)\,du\\
 &=\int_{\mathcal A}g_x(u)\psi(u)f_x(u)\,du.
 \end{aligned}
\]
By the defining property of \(m_x\), the same expectation equals \(\int m_x(u)\psi(u)f_x(u)du\). Uniqueness of Radon--Nikodym derivatives gives \(m_x=g_x\) for \(f_x(u)du\)-almost every \(u\).

The two Sobolev assumptions and \(\beta>1/2\) give continuous representatives of \(f_x,q_x\), since Cauchy--Schwarz makes their Fourier transforms integrable. Positivity gives \(f_x\ge\kappa>0\) on \(I\), so \(\widetilde m_x=q_x/f_x\) is continuous there. Since \(q_x=m_xf_x\), the preceding identity gives \(\widetilde m_x=g_x\) Lebesgue-almost everywhere on \(I\). The structural representation gives \(g_x(u)=E_P[Y(u)\mid X=x]\), and causal-mean continuity makes this function continuous on \(I\). Two continuous functions on an interval that agree almost everywhere agree everywhere. Therefore
\[
 E_P[Y(u)\mid X=x]=\frac{q_x(u)}{f_x(u)},\qquad u\in I.
\]
The finite law of total expectation yields
\[
 \mu(a)=E_P[Y(a)]
 =\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}p_xE_P[Y(a)\mid X=x]
 =\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}p_x\frac{q_x(a)}{f_x(a)}.
\]
This derives the identifying equality from causal assumptions rather than imposing it by definition. Each \(p_x\) is observed, while the marked-cocycle theorem makes every \(f_x,q_x\) a functional of \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\). Division is valid by \(f_x\ge\kappa\) on \(I\), proving point identification.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This result now proves, rather than assumes, the bridge from the analytically identified marked regression to the point-dose causal response under a genuine jointly measurable structural response surface and causal-mean continuity. \textit{Gap.} The measurement-error novelty remains blind asymmetric replication with possible spectral gaps; the causal interpretation is explicitly conditional on structural exogeneity and pointwise-version regularity. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies the causal population curve that the simultaneous band is intended to cover and is compatible with common-uniform Bernoulli lower alternatives.

\begin{lemma}[lem:mass-confidence]\label{lem:mass-confidence}
Uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal M\), \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\otimes n}((p_x)_{x\in\mathcal X}\in\mathcal P_{n,\alpha})\ge1-\alpha/3\). On this event, \(\max_x|p_x-\widehat p_x|\le[\log(6|\mathcal X|/\alpha)/(2n)]^{1/2}\); moreover \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\otimes n}(\min_xn_x<np_{\min}/2)\le|\mathcal X|e^{-np_{\min}/8}\). Put \(e_{p,n}=[\log(6|\mathcal X|/\alpha)/(2n)]^{1/2}\). If \(p=(p_x)_{x\in\mathcal X}\) and \(p'=(p'_x)_{x\in\mathcal X}\) belong to \(\mathcal P_{n,\alpha}\), while \(f_x\in\mathscr F_n\) and \(q_x\in\mathscr Q_n(f_x)\) are fixed for every \(x\), then \[\sup_{a\in I}\left|\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}(p_x-p'_x)\frac{q_x(a)}{f_x(a)}\right|\le2|\mathcal X|\left(B_Y+\frac{b_{\mu,n}}2\right)e_{p,n},\] and the right-hand side is \(o(r_n)\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(m=|\mathcal X|\) and
\[
 e_{p,n}=\left\{\frac{\log(6m/\alpha)}{2n}\right\}^{1/2}.
\]
If \(Z\in[0,1]\) and \(\psi(\lambda)=\log E\exp\{\lambda(Z-EZ)\}\), differentiation under exponential tilting gives \(\psi''(\lambda)=\operatorname{Var}_\lambda(Z)\le1/4\). Since \(\psi(0)=\psi'(0)=0\), two integrations give \(\psi(\lambda)\le\lambda^2/8\). For fixed \(x\), the i.i.d. assumption makes \(\widehat p_x\) an average of independent Bernoulli variables with mean \(p_x\). Exponential Markov inequality, optimized separately for both tails, yields
\[
 P_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\otimes n}(|\widehat p_x-p_x|>e_{p,n})
 \le2e^{-2ne_{p,n}^2}=\frac{\alpha}{3m}.
\]
A union bound gives
\[
 P_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\otimes n}\left(\max_x|\widehat p_x-p_x|\le e_{p,n}\right)\ge1-\alpha/3.
\]
The true mass vector is a probability vector, so this event is precisely its membership in \(\mathcal P_{n,\alpha}\).

For the count bound, if \(N\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,p)\), exponential Markov inequality and \(1+v\le e^v\) give, for \(t>0\),
\[
 \Pr(N\le np/2)\le\exp\{tnp/2+np(e^{-t}-1)\}.
\]
Taking \(t=\log2\) and using \((1-\log2)/2\ge1/8\) gives \(\Pr(N\le np/2)\le e^{-np/8}\). Since \(p_x\ge p_{\min}\),
\[
 P_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\otimes n}\left(\min_xn_x<np_{\min}/2\right)
 \le m e^{-np_{\min}/8}.
\]

Now let \(p,p'\in\mathcal P_{n,\alpha}\), and fix \(f_x\in\mathscr F_n\), \(q_x\in\mathscr Q_n(f_x)\). Put \(e_n=a_{q,n}+B_Ya_{f,n}\). The signal constraints give, for \(a\in I\),
\[
 f_x(a)\ge\kappa/2>0,
 \qquad |q_x(a)|\le B_Yf_x(a)+e_n.
\]
Thus division is valid and
\[
 \left|\frac{q_x(a)}{f_x(a)}\right|
 \le B_Y+\frac{2e_n}{\kappa}=B_Y+\frac{b_{\mu,n}}2.
\]
Also \(|p_x-p'_x|\le2e_{p,n}\), so
\[
 \sup_{a\in I}\left|\sum_x(p_x-p'_x)\frac{q_x(a)}{f_x(a)}\right|
 \le2m\left(B_Y+\frac{b_{\mu,n}}2\right)e_{p,n}.
\]
Finally, let \(\gamma=\beta-1/2>0\). Since \(K_n\sim\log n/\log\log n\),
\[
 b_{\mu,n}=4\kappa^{-1}C_{\beta,\mathcal A}(R_q+B_YR_f)K_n^{-\gamma}=O(r_n).
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \log(e_{p,n}/r_n)
 =-\tfrac12\log n+\gamma\log\log n-\gamma\log\log\log n+O(1)\longrightarrow-\infty.
\]
Hence \(e_{p,n}=o(r_n)\), and the displayed profiling bound is \(o(r_n)\). This proves all claims.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The empirical cell masses are random inputs to the causal mixture and must enter the same simultaneous coverage event as the two transform channels. \textit{Gap.} The blind-deconvolution comparators do not profile finite-stratum causal weights jointly with probability and marked transform restrictions. \textit{Consumer.} The bound adds cell-count uncertainty to coverage and records that mass uncertainty would be negligible at the candidate scale \(r_n\); it does not assert a shrinking total band diameter.

\begin{lemma}[lem:analytic-sieve-stability]\label{lem:analytic-sieve-stability}
For all sufficiently large \(n\), \(\inf_{P\in\mathcal M}P_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\otimes n}(\mathcal E_{n,\alpha})\ge1-\alpha\). On \(\mathcal E_{n,\alpha}\), for every \(P\in\mathcal M\) there is a tuple \(\bar\theta_n\in\Theta_{n,\alpha}\), formed from outer Jackson spline approximants and finite atomic error approximants, such that \(\|\mu_{\bar\theta_n}-\mu\|_{L^\infty(I)}\le b_{\mu,n}\). For every realized sample, the profiled clipping rule gives \(L_n(a)\le U_n(a)\) for every \(a\in I\) and \(0\le W_n\le2B_Y\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a sufficiently large \(n\). Conditional on \((X_i)_{i=1}^n\), the i.i.d. assumption makes observations in each nonempty cell independent with their law conditional on \(X=x\). Each real and imaginary part of a probability-channel summand lies in \([-1,1]\). The structural response condition and consistency give \(|Y|\le B_Y\), so the marked-channel parts lie in \([-B_Y,B_Y]\).

The bounded-variable exponential-moment calculation proved in \(\mathrm{lem{:}mass\text{-}confidence}\), applied to real and imaginary parts, gives, when \(n_x>\lambda_n\),
\[
 \Pr\left(
 |\widehat F_{x,n}(s,t)-F_x(s,t)|>2\sqrt{\lambda_n/n_x}
 \ \middle|\ (X_i)_{i=1}^n\right)\le4e^{-\lambda_n},
\]
and
\[
 \Pr\left(
 |\widehat G_{x,n}(s,t)-G_x(s,t)|>2B_Y\sqrt{\lambda_n/n_x}
 \ \middle|\ (X_i)_{i=1}^n\right)\le4e^{-\lambda_n}.
\]
If \(n_x\le\lambda_n\), channel boundedness and the empty-cell convention give the defining inequalities deterministically because \(\rho^F_{x,n}=2\) and \(\rho^G_{x,n}=2B_Y\). A conditional union bound over both channels, all cells, and all grid points gives failure probability at most
\[
 8|\mathcal X||\mathcal D_n|e^{-\lambda_n}=\alpha/6.
\]
Combining this with the mass-region failure probability \(\alpha/3\), then integrating over the labels, proves uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal M\) that
\[
 P_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\otimes n}(\mathcal E_{n,\alpha})
 \ge1-\alpha/3-\alpha/6\ge1-\alpha.
\]

On \(\mathcal E_{n,\alpha}\), apply the outer-spline embedding in every cell and the atomic-grid embedding to both error laws, and put
\[
 \bar\theta_n=
 \bigl((p_x,\widetilde f_{x,n},\widetilde q_{x,n})_{x\in\mathcal X},
       \bar\phi_{1,n},\bar\phi_{2,n}\bigr).
\]
The event places the mass vector in \(\mathcal P_{n,\alpha}\). At \((s,t)\in\mathcal D_n\), both coordinates belong to \(\Gamma_n\). Characteristic functions have modulus at most one; also \(|\widehat f_x(u)|\le1\) and \(|\widehat q_x(u)|\le B_Y\). Uniform approximation on an interval of length \(2B_A\) gives
\[
 |\widehat{\widetilde f}_{x,n}(u)-\widehat f_x(u)|\le2B_Aa_{f,n},
 \qquad
 |\widehat{\widetilde q}_{x,n}(u)-\widehat q_x(u)|\le2B_Aa_{q,n}.
\]
The atomic embedding gives
\[
 |\bar\phi_{1,n}(s)\bar\phi_{2,n}(t)-\phi_1(s)\phi_2(t)|
 \le2\tau_{e,n}.
\]
Using the factorization lemma and the bias allowances,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |\widehat{\widetilde f}_{x,n}(s+t)
   \bar\phi_{1,n}(s)\bar\phi_{2,n}(t)-F_x(s,t)|
 &\le2B_Aa_{f,n}+2\tau_{e,n}\le\delta^F_n,\\
 |\widehat{\widetilde q}_{x,n}(s+t)
   \bar\phi_{1,n}(s)\bar\phi_{2,n}(t)-G_x(s,t)|
 &\le2B_Aa_{q,n}+2B_Y\tau_{e,n}\le\delta^G_n.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The event inequalities and these bounds verify all residual restrictions, while the embedding lemmas verify all deterministic restrictions, including the new modulus conditions. Thus \(\bar\theta_n\in\Theta_{n,\alpha}\) and
\[
 \|\mu_{\bar\theta_n}-\mu\|_{L^\infty(I)}\le b_{\mu,n}.
\]

For the samplewise claims, put \(e_n=a_{q,n}+B_Ya_{f,n}\). For any \(\theta\in\Theta_{n,\alpha}\), the simplex, marked envelope, and \(f_x\ge\kappa/2\) on \(I\) imply
\[
 |\mu_\theta(a)|
 \le\sum_xp_x\left(B_Y+\frac{e_n}{f_x(a)}\right)
 \le B_Y+\frac{2e_n}{\kappa}=B_Y+\frac{b_{\mu,n}}2.
\]
If the feasible set is nonempty, write \(\ell(a)=\inf_\theta\mu_\theta(a)\) and \(u(a)=\sup_\theta\mu_\theta(a)\), with extrema over \(\Theta_{n,\alpha}\). Then
\[
 \ell(a)-b_{\mu,n}\le B_Y,
 \qquad -B_Y\le u(a)+b_{\mu,n},
 \qquad \ell(a)-b_{\mu,n}\le u(a)+b_{\mu,n}.
\]
Together with \(-B_Y\le B_Y\), these inequalities show that the maximum defining \(L_n(a)\) does not exceed the minimum defining \(U_n(a)\). Both endpoints lie in \([-B_Y,B_Y]\). If the feasible set is empty, both are zero. Hence, for every sample,
\[
 L_n(a)\le U_n(a)\quad(a\in I),
 \qquad0\le W_n\le2B_Y.
\]
This proves every assertion.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This lemma proves concentration, equicontinuous outer-sieve embedding, ADRF approximation, and universal clipping for honest coverage. \textit{Gap.} Its event argument gives no numerical inverse modulus; qualitative contraction is supplied separately by the finite-sieve diameter lemma. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies feasibility and simultaneous coverage for the explicit clipped band and its consistent strengthening.

\begin{theorem}[thm:honest-upper]\label{thm:honest-upper}
There is \(n_0<\infty\), depending only on \(\alpha\) and the fixed class indices, such that for every \(n\ge n_0\) the deterministic construction \(\mathfrak H^{\mathrm{up}}\) yields jointly sample-and-index measurable finite endpoints with \(L_n(a)\le U_n(a)\) for every \(a\in I\), \(\mathcal C_n\in\mathfrak B_{n,\alpha}\), and \(0\le W_n\le2B_Y\) almost surely under every \(P\in\mathcal M\). More explicitly, \(\inf_{P\in\mathcal M}P_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\otimes n}\{L_n(a)\le\mu(a)\le U_n(a)\text{ for every }a\in I\}\ge1-\alpha\), and coverage holds on \(\mathcal E_{n,\alpha}\). In particular, \(\sup_{P\in\mathcal M}E_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\otimes n}}[W_n]\le2B_Y\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(n\) beyond the stability threshold. At fixed \(n\), every coefficient, weight, and atom vector lives in a finite-dimensional Euclidean space. The signal-sieve constraints give
\[
 \|f\|_{L^1(\mathcal A)}\le1+6B_Aa_{f,n},
\]
and the marked envelope gives
\[
 \|q\|_{L^1(\mathcal A)}
 \le B_Y(1+2B_Aa_{f,n})+2B_A(a_{q,n}+B_Ya_{f,n}).
\]
Equivalence of norms on each fixed spline space turns these into coefficient bounds. Every continuum constraint, including each Sobolev-modulus inequality for a fixed pair \((a,b)\), is closed; their arbitrary intersection is closed. The error constraints are closed subsets of a product of the compact probability simplex and \([-H_n,H_n]^{M_n}\). Thus all deterministic constraints form a compact parameter set \(K_n^\star\). Each density coordinate satisfies \(\inf_{a\in I}f_x(a)\ge\kappa/2\), so \((a,\theta)\mapsto\mu_\theta(a)\) is continuous and finite on \(I\times K_n^\star\).

For a sample \(z\), let \(h_n(z,\theta)\) be the maximum of zero and the positive parts of all mass and channel constraint violations, with deterministic constraints absorbed into \(K_n^\star\). It is measurable in \(z\), continuous in \(\theta\), and
\[
 \Theta_{n,\alpha}(z)=\{\theta\in K_n^\star:h_n(z,\theta)=0\}.
\]
If \(K_n^\star\) is empty, the prescribed endpoints are zero. Otherwise choose a countable dense subset \(K_{n,0}^\star\). Then
\[
 d_n(z):=\inf_{\theta\in K_n^\star}h_n(z,\theta)
 =\inf_{\theta\in K_{n,0}^\star}h_n(z,\theta)
\]
is measurable, and compactness shows that the feasible set is nonempty exactly when \(d_n=0\). For positive integers \(k\), set
\[
 V_{n,k}^+(z,a)=\sup_{\theta\in K_n^\star}
 \{\mu_\theta(a)-kh_n(z,\theta)\},
 \qquad
 V_{n,k}^-(z,a)=\sup_{\theta\in K_n^\star}
 \{-\mu_\theta(a)-kh_n(z,\theta)\}.
\]
Each supremum may be taken over \(K_{n,0}^\star\), hence is jointly measurable. On \(\{d_n=0\}\), compactness gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 V_{n,k}^+(z,a)&\downarrow
 \sup_{\theta\in\Theta_{n,\alpha}(z)}\mu_\theta(a),\\
 V_{n,k}^-(z,a)&\downarrow
 -\inf_{\theta\in\Theta_{n,\alpha}(z)}\mu_\theta(a).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Indeed, comparison with a feasible point forces a maximizing sequence's violation to be \(O(k^{-1})\); every convergent subsequence has a feasible limit, and feasible points give the reverse inequalities. The empty-set convention now proves joint sample-and-index measurability of the extrema and of \(L_n,U_n\). Uniform continuity on the compact product \(I\times\Theta_{n,\alpha}(z)\) makes the extrema continuous in \(a\), so \(W_n\) is measurable by taking its supremum over a fixed countable dense subset of \(I\).

The stability lemma gives for every sample
\[
 L_n(a)\le U_n(a)\quad(a\in I),
 \qquad0\le W_n\le2B_Y.
\]
Fix \(P\in\mathcal M\). On \(\mathcal E_{n,\alpha}\), that lemma supplies \(\bar\theta_n\in\Theta_{n,\alpha}\) with
\[
 \sup_{a\in I}|\mu_{\bar\theta_n}(a)-\mu(a)|\le b_{\mu,n}.
\]
The structural response takes values in \([-B_Y,B_Y]\), so \(-B_Y\le\mu(a)\le B_Y\). Feasibility gives
\[
 \inf_{\theta\in\Theta_{n,\alpha}}\mu_\theta(a)-b_{\mu,n}\le\mu(a),
 \qquad
 \sup_{\theta\in\Theta_{n,\alpha}}\mu_\theta(a)+b_{\mu,n}\ge\mu(a).
\]
Clipping preserves these inequalities. Hence
\[
 \mathcal E_{n,\alpha}\subseteq
 \{L_n(a)\le\mu(a)\le U_n(a)\text{ for every }a\in I\}.
\]
Taking probabilities and using the uniform event bound proves coverage at least \(1-\alpha\) uniformly over \(\mathcal M\). Joint measurability, endpoint ordering, and coverage verify \(\mathcal C_n\in\mathfrak B_{n,\alpha}\), while clipping gives \(\sup_{P\in\mathcal M}E_P[W_n]\le2B_Y\). The threshold depends only on \(\alpha\) and the fixed class indices.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The deterministic profiling construction has jointly measurable finite endpoints, uniform simultaneous coverage, and universal clipping. \textit{Gap.} This base theorem records honesty; the separate finite-sieve diameter theorem supplies qualitative shrinkage but no \(O(r_n)\) rate. \textit{Consumer.} It is the coverage and measurability input for the finite-sieve honest consistent upper theorem.

\begin{proposition}[prop:all-band-converse]\label{prop:all-band-converse}
Conditional on an affirmative resolution of \(\mathrm{oeq{:}fixed\text{-}nuisance\text{-}marked\text{-}modulus}\), Low's two-point expected-length inequality gives constants \(c'>0\) and \(n_1\) such that \(\inf_{\mathcal C_n\in\mathfrak B_{n,\alpha}}\sup_{P\in\mathcal M}E_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\otimes n}}[W_n]\ge c'K_n^{1/2-\beta}\asymp c'r_n\) for every \(n\ge n_1\); positivity of \(c'\) follows from \(\eta<1-2\alpha\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Assume the affirmative resolution stated in the hypothesis.  Consequently, there are constants \(\eta\in(0,1-2\alpha)\), \(c_{\mathrm{sep}}>0\), and \(n_0<\infty\) such that, for every \(n\ge n_0\), the two full-data laws \(P_{0,n},P_{1,n}\) belong to \(\mathcal M\) and, on defining their \(n\)-sample observed laws by
\[
Q_{j,n}:=((P_{j,n})_{\mathrm{obs}})^{\otimes n},\qquad j\in\{0,1\},
\]
one has
\[
\operatorname{TV}(Q_{0,n},Q_{1,n})\le \eta,
\qquad
\Delta_n:=\|\mu_{1,n}-\mu_{0,n}\|_{L^\infty(I)}
\ge c_{\mathrm{sep}}K_n^{1/2-\beta}.
\tag{1}
\]
These are exactly the membership, contiguity, and separation conclusions in the affirmative resolution; the i.i.d. sampling assumption identifies \(Q_{j,n}\) as the relevant sample law.

Fix \(n\ge n_0\) and an arbitrary band \(\mathcal C_n=(L_n,U_n)\in\mathfrak B_{n,\alpha}\).  For \(j\in\{0,1\}\), let
\[
H_{j,n}:=\bigl\{L_n(a)\le \mu_{j,n}(a)\le U_n(a)
                  \text{ for every }a\in I\bigr\}.
\tag{2}
\]
These are the coverage events appearing in the definition of \(\mathfrak B_{n,\alpha}\).  Since \(P_{j,n}\in\mathcal M\), uniform honesty gives
\[
Q_{j,n}(H_{j,n})\ge 1-\alpha,
\qquad j\in\{0,1\}.
\tag{3}
\]
By the defining variational inequality for total variation, \(Q_{0,n}(E)\ge Q_{1,n}(E)-\operatorname{TV}(Q_{0,n},Q_{1,n})\) for every measurable event \(E\).  Applying this with \(E=H_{1,n}\), and then using (1) and (3), yields
\[
Q_{0,n}(H_{1,n})
\ge Q_{1,n}(H_{1,n})-\operatorname{TV}(Q_{0,n},Q_{1,n})
\ge 1-\alpha-\eta.
\tag{4}
\]
The union bound, (3), and (4) therefore imply
\[
Q_{0,n}(H_{0,n}\cap H_{1,n})
\ge 1-Q_{0,n}(H_{0,n}^{c})-Q_{0,n}(H_{1,n}^{c})
\ge 1-2\alpha-\eta.
\tag{5}
\]
On \(H_{0,n}\cap H_{1,n}\), the interval \([L_n(a),U_n(a)]\) contains both \(\mu_{0,n}(a)\) and \(\mu_{1,n}(a)\) for every \(a\in I\).  Hence, pointwise in \(a\),
\[
U_n(a)-L_n(a)\ge |\mu_{1,n}(a)-\mu_{0,n}(a)|,
\]
and the definition of \(W_n\) gives
\[
W_n\ge \Delta_n
\quad\text{on }H_{0,n}\cap H_{1,n}.
\tag{6}
\]
Moreover, \(W_n\ge0\) almost surely by the definition of \(\mathfrak B_{n,\alpha}\).  Combining (1), (5), and (6),
\[
E_{Q_{0,n}}[W_n]
\ge \Delta_n Q_{0,n}(H_{0,n}\cap H_{1,n})
\ge (1-2\alpha-\eta)c_{\mathrm{sep}}K_n^{1/2-\beta}.
\tag{7}
\]
This calculation is the two-point expected-length argument recorded in the stated Low lemma.  Put
\[
c':=(1-2\alpha-\eta)c_{\mathrm{sep}}.
\]
The restriction \(\eta<1-2\alpha\) and the inequality \(c_{\mathrm{sep}}>0\) imply \(c'>0\).  Because \(P_{0,n}\in\mathcal M\), (7) entails, for every \(\mathcal C_n\in\mathfrak B_{n,\alpha}\),
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M}E_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\otimes n}}[W_n]
\ge E_{Q_{0,n}}[W_n]
\ge c'K_n^{1/2-\beta}.
\]
Taking the infimum over \(\mathcal C_n\in\mathfrak B_{n,\alpha}\) proves the first asserted inequality.

It remains to verify the displayed scale equivalence under the current smoothness range.  Let
\[
x_n:=\frac{\log n}{\log\log n},
\qquad
\gamma:=\beta-\frac12.
\]
The assumption \(\beta>1/2\) gives \(\gamma>0\).  For all sufficiently large \(n\), \(x_n\ge2\) and the definition \(K_n=\lfloor x_n\rfloor\) implies
\[
\frac{x_n}{2}\le K_n\le x_n.
\]
Since \(u\mapsto u^{-\gamma}\) is decreasing on \((0,\infty)\),
\[
x_n^{-\gamma}\le K_n^{-\gamma}\le 2^\gamma x_n^{-\gamma}.
\tag{8}
\]
Finally,
\[
x_n^{-\gamma}
=\left(\frac{\log\log n}{\log n}\right)^{\beta-1/2}
=r_n.
\tag{9}
\]
Equations (8)--(9) show \(K_n^{1/2-\beta}\asymp r_n\).  Increasing \(n_0\), if necessary, to a finite \(n_1\) for which the definition of \(K_n\) and the preceding bounds all hold completes the conditional proof.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is deliberately a conditional corollary: the generic testing reduction is routine once the marked modulus has been constructed. \textit{Gap.} No cited ADRF or blind repeated-measurement paper gives a converse quantifying over every honest measurable band. \textit{Consumer.} If the marked modulus is resolved, this conditional lower bound would combine with a future \(O(r_n)\) upper theorem to establish the sharp resolution limit; neither the finite conservative band nor the qualitative exact-class band is rate-matching.

\begin{lemma}[lem:atomic-grid-embedding]\label{lem:atomic-grid-embedding}
For every \(P\in\mathcal M\), every \(j\in\{1,2\}\), and all sufficiently large \(n\), there is \(\bar\phi_{j,n}\in\Phi_{j,n}\) such that
\[
 \max_{t\in\Gamma_n}|\bar\phi_{j,n}(t)-\phi_j(t)|\le \tau_{e,n}.
\]
For \(j=2\), its representing atomic law also satisfies
\[
 \left|\sum_{\ell=1}^{M_n}w_{2\ell}u_{2\ell}\right|
 \le M_eH_n^{1-q_e}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(P\in\mathcal M\), \(j\in\{1,2\}\), and sufficiently large \(n\). Define \(U_j^H=\max\{-H_n,\min\{U_j,H_n\}\}\). The moment assumption gives
\[
 P(|U_j|>H_n)\le M_eH_n^{-q_e}.
\]
Since clipping changes a unit-modulus exponential by at most two,
\[
 |E_Pe^{itU_j^H}-\phi_j(t)|
 \le2M_eH_n^{-q_e}=\tau_{e,n}
\]
for every \(t\), and \(E_P|U_j^H|^{q_e}\le M_e\). For \(j=2\), the first moment exists by the moment-derived local-modulus lemma. Centering and \(q_e>1\) give
\[
 |E_PU_2^H|
 \le E_P[|U_2|\mathbf1\{|U_2|>H_n\}]
 \le M_eH_n^{1-q_e}.
\]

Put \(D=2|\Gamma_n|+2\) and define
\[
 v(u)=\left((\operatorname{Re}e^{itu},\operatorname{Im}e^{itu})_{t\in\Gamma_n},u,|u|^{q_e}\right)\in\mathbb R^D.
\]
The barycenter \(E_Pv(U_j^H)\) belongs to the convex hull of compact \(v([-H_n,H_n])\). Indeed, simple-law approximation places it in the closure. If a finite convex representation uses more than \(D+1\) locations, the augmented vectors \((1,v(u_r))\in\mathbb R^{D+1}\) are linearly dependent. Hence there are nonzero real \(a_r\) with \(\sum_ra_r=0\) and \(\sum_ra_rv(u_r)=0\). Moving the weights along this dependence until one reaches zero preserves nonnegativity, total mass, and barycenter. Iteration leaves at most
\[
 D+1=2|\Gamma_n|+3\le M_n
\]
locations. The convex hull is thus the continuous image of a compact simplex times compact support locations, so it is closed; the barycenter has such a representation.

Let \(\bar Q_{j,n}\) be the resulting atomic law, order its locations, and pad with zero-weight atoms at \(H_n\). Its transform \(\bar\phi_{j,n}\) agrees with that of \(U_j^H\) on \(\Gamma_n\), and it preserves the first and \(q_e\)-th absolute moments. Thus
\[
 \max_{t\in\Gamma_n}|\bar\phi_{j,n}(t)-\phi_j(t)|\le\tau_{e,n},
\]
and, for \(j=2\),
\[
 \left|\sum_{\ell=1}^{M_n}w_{2\ell}u_{2\ell}\right|
 \le M_eH_n^{1-q_e}\le2M_eH_n^{1-q_e}.
\]
Applying the moment-derived local-modulus lemma to \(\bar Q_{j,n}\) gives \(\inf_{|t|\le\nu_*}|\bar\phi_{j,n}(t)|\ge c_*\ge c_*/2\). All support, order, moment, modulus, and centering-tolerance constraints in \(\Phi_{j,n}\) follow, proving the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:outer-spline-embedding]\label{lem:outer-spline-embedding}
Under the relaxed outer-sieve construction in \(\mathrm{def{:}signal\text{-}sieves}\), for every \(P\in\mathcal M\), every \(x\in\mathcal X\), and all sufficiently large \(n\), there are splines \(\widetilde f_{x,n}\in\mathscr F_n\) and \(\widetilde q_{x,n}\in\mathscr Q_n(\widetilde f_{x,n})\) such that
\[
 \|\widetilde f_{x,n}-f_x\|_{L^\infty(\mathcal A)}\le a_{f,n},
 \qquad
 \|\widetilde q_{x,n}-q_x\|_{L^\infty(\mathcal A)}\le a_{q,n}.
\]
With the true masses \((p_x)_{x\in\mathcal X}\), their profiled ADRF satisfies
\[
 \sup_{a\in I}\left|
 \sum_{x\in\mathcal X}p_x\frac{\widetilde q_{x,n}(a)}{\widetilde f_{x,n}(a)}-\mu(a)
 \right|
 \le 2\kappa^{-1}(a_{q,n}+B_Ya_{f,n})
 \le b_{\mu,n}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(P\in\mathcal M\) and \(x\in\mathcal X\). By \(\mathrm{lem{:}sobolev\text{-}spline\text{-}jackson}\), the two Sobolev assumptions, and \(\mathrm{def{:}ecf\text{-}radii}\), there are order-\(d\) splines \(\widetilde f_{x,n}\) and \(\widetilde q_{x,n}\) such that
\[
 \|\widetilde f_{x,n}-f_x\|_{L^\infty(\mathcal A)}
 \le C_{\beta,\mathcal A}K_n^{1/2-\beta}\|f_x\|_{H^\beta}
 \le a_{f,n},
\]
and
\[
 \|\widetilde q_{x,n}-q_x\|_{L^\infty(\mathcal A)}
 \le C_{\beta,\mathcal A}K_n^{1/2-\beta}\|q_x\|_{H^\beta}
 \le a_{q,n}.
\]
Since \(\beta>1/2\) and \(K_n\to\infty\), both allowances vanish; in particular, \(a_{f,n}<\kappa/2\) eventually.

The function \(f_x\) is a nonnegative conditional density, and \(\mathrm{ass{:}compact\text{-}dose}\) gives \(\int_{\mathcal A}f_x=1\). Approximation and \(\mathrm{ass{:}denominator}\) imply
\[
 \widetilde f_{x,n}(a)\ge-a_{f,n}\quad(a\in\mathcal A),
 \qquad
 \widetilde f_{x,n}(a)\ge\kappa-a_{f,n}
 =\max\{\kappa/2,\kappa-a_{f,n}\}\quad(a\in I),
\]
and
\[
 \left|\int_{\mathcal A}\widetilde f_{x,n}(a)\,da-1\right|
 \le2B_Aa_{f,n}.
\]
The common-event response representation and consistency give \(|Y|\le B_Y\). Conditional Jensen's inequality therefore gives \(|m_x(a)|\le B_Y\) for \(f_x(a)da\)-almost every \(a\), so \(|q_x|\le B_Yf_x\) almost everywhere. By \(\mathrm{lem{:}sobolev\text{-}modulus\text{-}control}\), both functions have continuous representatives. A strict pointwise violation would persist on an interval and contradict the almost-everywhere inequality. Thus
\[
 |q_x(a)|\le B_Yf_x(a),\qquad a\in\mathcal A.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |\widetilde q_{x,n}(a)|
 &\le |q_x(a)|+a_{q,n}\\
 &\le B_Y\widetilde f_{x,n}(a)+a_{q,n}+B_Ya_{f,n},
 \qquad a\in\mathcal A.
 \end{aligned}
\]
For \(a,b\in\mathcal A\), the triangle inequality, the Jackson bounds, and the Sobolev-modulus lemma give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |\widetilde f_{x,n}(a)-\widetilde f_{x,n}(b)|
 &\le 2a_{f,n}+\Omega_{R_f}(|a-b|),\\
 |\widetilde q_{x,n}(a)-\widetilde q_{x,n}(b)|
 &\le 2a_{q,n}+\Omega_{R_q}(|a-b|).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Hence all constraints defining \(\mathscr F_n\) and \(\mathscr Q_n(\widetilde f_{x,n})\) hold.

The already established ADRF identification theorem gives
\[
 \mu(a)=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}p_x\frac{q_x(a)}{f_x(a)},\qquad a\in I.
\]
For \(a\in I\), all denominators below are positive and
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \left|\frac{\widetilde q_{x,n}(a)}{\widetilde f_{x,n}(a)}
       -\frac{q_x(a)}{f_x(a)}\right|
 &\le
 \frac{|\widetilde q_{x,n}(a)-q_x(a)|}{\widetilde f_{x,n}(a)}
 +\frac{|q_x(a)|\,|\widetilde f_{x,n}(a)-f_x(a)|}
 {f_x(a)\widetilde f_{x,n}(a)}\\
 &\le\frac{a_{q,n}+B_Ya_{f,n}}{\kappa-a_{f,n}}\\
 &\le2\kappa^{-1}(a_{q,n}+B_Ya_{f,n}).
 \end{aligned}
\]
The last division uses \(\kappa-a_{f,n}\ge\kappa/2>0\). Summing with the probability weights \(p_x\), taking the supremum over \(I\), and using the definition of \(b_{\mu,n}\) proves the claim.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The Jackson approximants satisfy the original relaxed density and marked-envelope restrictions and the new deterministic Sobolev-modulus inequalities. \textit{Gap.} This is an approximation and feasibility result; it does not quantify inversion of empirical channel discrepancies. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies a feasible near-truth tuple for uniform coverage and preserves nonemptiness after the equicontinuity refinement.

\begin{lemma}[lem:exact-class-compactness]\label{lem:exact-class-compactness}
Equip \(\mathscr K\) with the product of Euclidean distance for \((p_x)_x\),
uniform distance on \(\mathcal A\) for every \(f_x,q_x\), and any metric for
weak convergence of \(Q_1,Q_2\).  Then \(\mathscr K\) is compact.  Along
convergent sequences in this metric, \(\phi_{Q_j}\) converges uniformly on
every compact frequency interval, the fitted probability and marked channels
converge locally uniformly on \(\mathbb R^2\), and
\(\vartheta\mapsto\mu_\vartheta\) is continuous from \(\mathscr K\) to
\(C(I)\).  For every \(n\), the exact feasible set
\(\Theta^{\mathrm{ex}}_{n,\alpha}(z)\) is compact, and its finitely many
constraint-violation functions are measurable in \(z\) and continuous in
\(\vartheta\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By \(\mathrm{def{:}exact\text{-}projected\text{-}class}\), every signal
coordinate is supported on \(\mathcal A=[-B_A,B_A]\), and its
\(H^\beta\)-norm is bounded by \(R_f\) or \(R_q\).  Use the Fourier norm
convention
\[
 \|g\|_{H^\beta}^2
 =\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}(1+t^2)^\beta|\widehat g(t)|^2\,dt.\tag{1}
\]
Fourier inversion and Cauchy--Schwarz give, first for smooth functions and
then by \(H^\beta\) approximation,
\[
 |g(a)|\le\frac{\|g\|_{H^\beta}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}
 \left\{\int_{\mathbb R}(1+t^2)^{-\beta}\,dt\right\}^{1/2},     \tag{2}
\]
and
\[
 |g(a)-g(b)|\le\frac{\|g\|_{H^\beta}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}
 \left\{\int_{\mathbb R}|e^{-ita}-e^{-itb}|^2
 (1+t^2)^{-\beta}\,dt\right\}^{1/2}.                            \tag{3}
\]
The integral in (2) is finite because \(\beta>1/2\).  The integrand in (3)
converges pointwise to zero as \(|a-b|\to0\) and is bounded by
\(4(1+t^2)^{-\beta}\).  Dominated convergence therefore provides a common
modulus of continuity for each fixed-radius Sobolev ball.  Such a ball is
uniformly bounded and equicontinuous on \(\mathcal A\).

These estimates also give total boundedness directly.  Given
\(\epsilon>0\), choose a finite mesh of \(\mathcal A\) so that (3) is at
most \(\epsilon/4\) between each point and a nearest mesh point.  Partition
the bounded range in (2) into finitely many intervals of length
\(\epsilon/2\).  Two functions with the same interval pattern on the mesh
are at uniform distance at most \(\epsilon\), so finitely many selected
representatives form an \(\epsilon\)-net.

For closedness, suppose \(g_m\) is supported in \(\mathcal A\),
\(\|g_m\|_{H^\beta}\le R\), and \(g_m\to g\) uniformly on \(\mathcal A\).
The continuous representatives vanish off \(\mathcal A\), hence also at its
boundary.  The zero extension of \(g\) is continuous and
\[
 \|g_m-g\|_{L^2(\mathbb R)}
 \le(2B_A)^{1/2}\|g_m-g\|_{L^\infty(\mathcal A)}\longrightarrow0.\tag{4}
\]
Put \(h_m(t)=(1+t^2)^{\beta/2}\widehat g_m(t)\).  Equation (1) makes
\((h_m)\) bounded in \(L^2\), so a subsequence converges weakly to some
\(h\) and
\[
 \|h\|_2\le\liminf_m\|h_m\|_2.                                  \tag{5}
\]
By (4) and the Fourier isometry,
\(\widehat g_m\to\widehat g\) strongly in \(L^2\).  On the other hand,
weak convergence of \(h_m\) implies weak convergence of
\(\widehat g_m=(1+t^2)^{-\beta/2}h_m\) to
\((1+t^2)^{-\beta/2}h\).  Uniqueness of weak limits gives
\(\widehat g=(1+t^2)^{-\beta/2}h\), so \(g\in H^\beta\) and (5) preserves
the radius.  Thus the fixed-support ball is closed in \(C(\mathcal A)\), and
total boundedness proves compactness.

Apply this at radii \(R_f\) and \(R_q\).  Uniform convergence preserves
nonnegativity, the lower bound by \(\kappa\) on \(I\), and
\(|q_x|\le B_Yf_x\).  It also preserves the unit integral because
\[
 \left|\int_{\mathcal A}(f_m-f)\right|
 \le2B_A\|f_m-f\|_{L^\infty(\mathcal A)}\longrightarrow0.        \tag{6}
\]
Hence the joint signal set is compact.  The mass vectors form a closed
subset of the finite-dimensional simplex and are compact as well.

For an error coordinate, let \((Q_m)\) satisfy the common moment bound.
Markov's inequality gives
\[
 \sup_mQ_m(|u|>R)\le M_eR^{-q_e},                                 \tag{7}
\]
so the family is tight.  A diagonal subsequence of the distribution
functions on rational arguments defines a limiting distribution function;
(7) forces its limits at the two infinities to be zero and one.  Rational
sandwiching gives convergence at every continuity point.  Approximating a
bounded continuous function on \([-R,R]\) by step functions with endpoints
at continuity points, then using (7) as \(R\to\infty\), proves weak
convergence along the subsequence to a Borel probability law \(Q\):
\[
 \int h\,dQ_m\longrightarrow\int h\,dQ
 \quad\text{for every bounded continuous }h.                     \tag{8}
\]
Apply (8) to \(|u|^{q_e}\wedge L\), then let \(L\to\infty\), to get
\[
 \int|u|^{q_e}\,dQ(u)\le M_e.                                   \tag{9}
\]
Because \(q_e>1\),
\[
 \int_{|u|>R}|u|\,dQ_m(u)
 \le M_eR^{1-q_e}\longrightarrow0                               \tag{10}
\]
uniformly in \(m\), and (9) gives the same bound for \(Q\).  Applying (8)
to the identity clipped to \([-R,R]\), then using (10), proves convergence
of first moments.  Thus the centering condition on \(Q_2\) is closed.  This
proves compactness of both error-law coordinates in any metric that
metrizes weak convergence.

The power-mean inequality also gives
\(\int|u|\,dQ_m(u)\le M_e^{1/q_e}\), whence
\[
 |\phi_{Q_m}(s)-\phi_{Q_m}(t)|
 \le |s-t|M_e^{1/q_e}.                                           \tag{11}
\]
Weak convergence gives pointwise convergence of the characteristic
functions.  A finite-mesh argument using (11) for both \(Q_m\) and \(Q\)
upgrades this to uniform convergence on every compact frequency interval.
Together with the preceding coordinatewise compactness, this proves
compactness of \(\mathscr K\).

If \(\vartheta_m\to\vartheta\) in the asserted product metric, common
support gives
\[
 \sup_{r\in\mathbb R}|\widehat f_{x,m}(r)-\widehat f_x(r)|
 \le2B_A\|f_{x,m}-f_x\|_{L^\infty(\mathcal A)}\longrightarrow0, \tag{12}
\]
and similarly for \(q_x\).  Characteristic functions have modulus at most
one, \(\int f_x=1\), and \(\int|q_x|\le B_Y\).  Thus (11)--(12) prove local
uniform convergence on \(\mathbb R^2\) of both fitted channels.

For the profiled target, if \(f_x,f'_x\ge\kappa\) and
\(|q'_x|\le B_Yf'_x\) on \(I\), then
\[
 \left\|\frac{q_x}{f_x}-\frac{q'_x}{f'_x}\right\|_{L^\infty(I)}
 \le\kappa^{-1}\|q_x-q'_x\|_{L^\infty(\mathcal A)}
   +B_Y\kappa^{-1}\|f_x-f'_x\|_{L^\infty(\mathcal A)}.         \tag{13}
\]
Also \(\|q'_x/f'_x\|_\infty\le B_Y\), and therefore
\[
 \|\mu_\vartheta-\mu_{\vartheta'}\|_{L^\infty(I)}
 \le\sum_xp_x
 \left\|\frac{q_x}{f_x}-\frac{q'_x}{f'_x}\right\|_{L^\infty(I)}
 +B_Y\sum_x|p_x-p'_x|.                                          \tag{14}
\]
This proves continuity into \(C(I)\).

Finally fix \(n\) and a sample \(z\).  Feasibility is the conjunction of
finitely many mass and channel inequalities.  A typical probability-channel
violation is
\[
 \left(
 \left|\widehat F_{x,n}(z;s,t)-
 \widehat f_x(s+t)\phi_{Q_1}(s)\phi_{Q_2}(t)\right|
 -\rho^F_{x,n}(z)
 \right)_+,                                                      \tag{15}
\]
with analogous marked and mass violations.  The empirical sums, counts,
empty-cell convention, and radii are Borel measurable in \(z\); local
uniform channel convergence and continuity of the mass coordinates make all
violations continuous in \(\vartheta\).  Their common zero set is precisely
\(\Theta^{\mathrm{ex}}_{n,\alpha}(z)\), hence is closed in compact
\(\mathscr K\) and is compact.  This proves every assertion.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Compactness is the deterministic substitute for a quantitative inverse modulus in the exact projected nuisance class. \textit{Gap.} The finite relaxed sieve does not have a proved shrinking diameter, whereas exact class constraints admit a qualitative subsequence argument. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies compact feasible values, continuous target profiling, and convergent subsequences for the qualitative diameter lemma and abstract exact-class band.

\begin{lemma}[lem:qualitative-exact-grid-diameter]\label{lem:qualitative-exact-grid-diameter}
For a realized sample \(z\), write \(n_x(z)\) for its stratum counts and set
\[
 \omega_n=
 \sup_{\substack{z:\ \min_{x\in\mathcal X}n_x(z)\ge np_{\min}/2,\\
                  \Theta^{\mathrm{ex}}_{n,\alpha}(z)\ne\varnothing}}
 \operatorname{diam}_{L^\infty(I)}
 \{\mu_\vartheta:\vartheta\in
 \Theta^{\mathrm{ex}}_{n,\alpha}(z)\},                           \tag{1}
\]
with the supremum defined as zero if its indexing set is empty.  Then
\(\omega_n\to0\).  This conclusion is deterministic and qualitative; it
asserts neither an explicit modulus nor an \(O(r_n)\) bound.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Suppose \(\omega_n\not\to0\). Then there are \(\epsilon>0\), integers \(n_k\to\infty\), regular-count samples \(z_k\), and \(\vartheta_k,\vartheta'_k\in\Theta^{\mathrm{ex}}_{n_k,\alpha}(z_k)\) such that
\[
 \|\mu_{\vartheta_k}-\mu_{\vartheta'_k}\|_{L^\infty(I)}\ge\epsilon.
\]
Exact-class compactness supplies a common subsequence with \(\vartheta_k\to\vartheta\) and \(\vartheta'_k\to\vartheta'\). Both mass vectors belong to the same confidence region, so their coordinate differences tend to zero and the limiting mass vectors agree.

Because \(|\mathcal D_n|=(2K_n^2+1)^2\), \(\lambda_n=O(\log K_n)=o(n)\). Regular counts imply
\[
 \max_x\rho^F_{x,n_k}=o(1),
 \qquad \max_x\rho^G_{x,n_k}=o(1).
\]
For \(\vartheta\in\mathscr K\), let
\[
 F_{x,\vartheta}(s,t)=\widehat f_x(s+t)\phi_{Q_1}(s)\phi_{Q_2}(t),
 \quad
 G_{x,\vartheta}(s,t)=\widehat q_x(s+t)\phi_{Q_1}(s)\phi_{Q_2}(t).
\]
The compact support, \(\int|q_x|\le B_Y\), and \(\int|u|dQ_j\le M_e^{1/q_e}\) give uniform Lipschitz bounds for these channels. At grid points, feasibility of both tuples around the same empirical channels bounds their channel differences by twice the corresponding radii. Since the mesh tends to zero and the range tends to infinity, local uniform convergence from exact-class compactness permits passage to the limits and gives
\[
 F_{x,\vartheta}(s,t)=F_{x,\vartheta'}(s,t),
 \qquad
 G_{x,\vartheta}(s,t)=G_{x,\vartheta'}(s,t)
\]
for every \(x,s,t\).

The moment-derived local-modulus lemma gives \(|\phi_Q(v)|\ge1/2\) whenever \(|v|\le\nu_*\). Also \(|\widehat f_x(v)-1|\le B_A|v|\). Hence every factor in the probability channels is nonzero on a common neighborhood of zero. The exact-class centering and \(q_e>1\) imply \(\phi'_{Q_2}(0)=\phi'_{Q'_2}(0)=0\). Differentiating equality of probability channels in \(t\) at \((u,0)\) and dividing by the nonzero common value gives
\[
 \frac{\widehat f'_x(u)}{\widehat f_x(u)}
 =\frac{(\widehat{f'_x})'(u)}{\widehat{f'_x}(u)}.
\]
The transform ratio has derivative zero and equals one at zero, so the two density transforms agree locally. Compact support makes their difference entire. At an interior point of the real equality interval, all real derivatives and therefore all complex Taylor coefficients vanish, so the entire Taylor series is zero globally. If \(h=f_x-f'_x\), convolution with a centered Gaussian has Fourier transform \(\widehat h(t)e^{-\sigma^2t^2/2}=0\), and hence \(h*\gamma_\sigma=0\) by Fubini's theorem. Translation continuity in \(L^1\) gives \(h*\gamma_\sigma\to h\) in \(L^1\), proving \(f_x=f'_x\). Equality of marked and probability channels on the same neighborhood then gives
\[
 \widehat q_x(u)=\widehat f_x(u)\frac{G_{x,\vartheta}(u,0)}{F_{x,\vartheta}(u,0)}
 =\widehat{f'_x}(u)\frac{G_{x,\vartheta'}(u,0)}{F_{x,\vartheta'}(u,0)}
 =\widehat{q'_x}(u).
\]
The same Taylor-coefficient and Gaussian-convolution arguments give \(q_x=q'_x\). Thus the limiting targets agree. Continuity of \(\vartheta\mapsto\mu_\vartheta\) now contradicts the fixed separation \(\epsilon\). Therefore \(\omega_n\to0\). The proof is qualitative and gives no numerical modulus or \(O(r_n)\) rate.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the qualitative inverse result needed to turn exact empirical-channel feasibility into uniformly shrinking expected width. \textit{Gap.} It proves only vanishing diameter and deliberately supplies no numerical modulus or logarithmic rate. \textit{Consumer.} It is the deterministic width input for the abstract exact-class honest band.

\begin{theorem}[thm:honest-consistent-upper]\label{thm:honest-consistent-upper}
There exists \(n_0<\infty\), depending only on \(\alpha\) and the fixed class
indices, such that for every \(n\ge n_0\) the abstract exact-class band
\(\mathcal C_n^{\mathrm{ex}}\) has jointly sample-and-index measurable finite
endpoints, belongs to \(\mathfrak B_{n,\alpha}\), and satisfies
\[
 \inf_{P\in\mathcal M}P_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\otimes n}
 \{L_n^{\mathrm{ex}}(a)\le\mu(a)\le U_n^{\mathrm{ex}}(a)
 \text{ for every }a\in I\}\ge1-\alpha,
 \qquad 0\le W_n^{\mathrm{ex}}\le2B_Y.
\]
Moreover, with \(\omega_n\) from
\(\mathrm{lem{:}qualitative\text{-}exact\text{-}grid\text{-}diameter}\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M}
 E_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\otimes n}}[W_n^{\mathrm{ex}}]
 \le \omega_n+2B_Y|\mathcal X|e^{-np_{\min}/8}=o(1).
\]
The construction is qualitative and noncomputational; no explicit rate,
\(O(r_n)\) conclusion, or minimax claim is asserted.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By \(\mathrm{lem{:}exact\text{-}class\text{-}compactness}\), \(\mathscr K\) is compact. If it is empty, all exact feasible sets are empty and all endpoints are zero, so the assertions are immediate and coverage over \(\mathcal M\) is vacuous. Otherwise let \(h(z,\vartheta)\) be the maximum of zero and the positive parts of all mass and channel violations defining \(\Theta^{\mathrm{ex}}_{n,\alpha}(z)\). It is measurable in \(z\), continuous in \(\vartheta\), and
\[
 \Theta^{\mathrm{ex}}_{n,\alpha}(z)=\{\vartheta\in\mathscr K:h(z,\vartheta)=0\}.
\]
For a countable dense set \(K_0\subset\mathscr K\),
\[
 d(z):=\inf_{\vartheta\in\mathscr K}h(z,\vartheta)
 =\inf_{\vartheta\in K_0}h(z,\vartheta)
\]
is measurable, and compactness makes the feasible set nonempty exactly when \(d=0\). On that event define
\[
 V_m^+(z,a)=\sup_{\vartheta\in\mathscr K}\{\mu_\vartheta(a)-mh(z,\vartheta)\},
 \quad
 V_m^-(z,a)=\sup_{\vartheta\in\mathscr K}\{-\mu_\vartheta(a)-mh(z,\vartheta)\}.
\]
Each supremum may be taken over \(K_0\), so it is jointly measurable. Compactness gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 V_m^+(z,a)&\downarrow\sup_{\vartheta\in\Theta^{\mathrm{ex}}_{n,\alpha}(z)}\mu_\vartheta(a),\\
 V_m^-(z,a)&\downarrow-\inf_{\vartheta\in\Theta^{\mathrm{ex}}_{n,\alpha}(z)}\mu_\vartheta(a).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Indeed, comparison with a feasible point makes a maximizing sequence's violation \(O(m^{-1})\), and every subsequential limit is feasible. These facts, the empty-set rule, and clipping prove endpoint measurability. Target continuity from exact-class compactness makes the extrema continuous in \(a\), hence \(W_n^{\mathrm{ex}}\) is measurable by taking the supremum over a countable dense subset of \(I\).

Every \(\vartheta\in\mathscr K\) has \(|q_x/f_x|\le B_Y\) on \(I\), so \(|\mu_\vartheta(a)|\le B_Y\). Clipping preserves endpoint ordering, and
\[
 0\le W_n^{\mathrm{ex}}\le2B_Y
\]
for every sample.

Fix \(P\in\mathcal M\). Its projected tuple
\[
 \vartheta_P=((p_x,f_x,q_x)_{x\in\mathcal X},\mathcal L_P(U_1),\mathcal L_P(U_2))
\]
belongs to \(\mathscr K\): the mass, support, Sobolev, denominator, moment, and centering clauses are model conditions; consistency and the bounded structural response give \(|q_x|\le B_Yf_x\) almost everywhere and hence everywhere for continuous representatives. On \(\mathcal E_{n,\alpha}\), the true mass vector is in \(\mathcal P_{n,\alpha}\), and the exact factorization identities turn the event's empirical inequalities into the exact feasible-set restrictions. Thus
\[
 \mathcal E_{n,\alpha}\subseteq\{\vartheta_P\in\Theta^{\mathrm{ex}}_{n,\alpha}\}.
\]
The ADRF identification theorem gives \(\mu=\mu_{\vartheta_P}\) on \(I\). Profiling and clipping contain \(\mu\) simultaneously on the confidence event. The stability lemma supplies probability at least \(1-\alpha\) uniformly over \(\mathcal M\), proving honesty.

Let \(R_n=\{\min_xn_x\ge np_{\min}/2\}\). On \(R_n\), an empty feasible set has zero width and a nonempty set has width bounded by \(\omega_n\), so \(W_n^{\mathrm{ex}}\mathbf1_{R_n}\le\omega_n\). On \(R_n^c\), use clipping. The mass-confidence count bound gives
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M}E_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\otimes n}}[W_n^{\mathrm{ex}}]
 \le\omega_n+2B_Y|\mathcal X|e^{-np_{\min}/8}.
\]
The qualitative exact-grid diameter gives \(\omega_n\to0\), and the exponential term vanishes. Hence the right side is \(o(1)\), with no asserted explicit rate.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem supplies a same-assumption inferential improvement: a uniformly honest simultaneous ADRF band whose maximal expected width vanishes uniformly. \textit{Gap.} The result is existential and qualitative because compactness gives no explicit inverse modulus for the blind marked channel. \textit{Consumer.} It supports consistent whole-curve uncertainty quantification while leaving the sharp \(r_n\) upper and matching marked converse as a separate research program.

\begin{lemma}[lem:sobolev-spline-jackson]\label{lem:sobolev-spline-jackson}
For every \(\beta>1/2\), let \(d=\lceil\beta\rceil+1\).  For every
\(g\in H^\beta(\mathbb R)\), the uniform order-\(d\) B-spline spaces on
\(\mathcal A\) satisfy, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 \inf_{s\in\operatorname{span}\{\mathsf B_{k,K_n}:1\le k\le K_n+d\}}
 \|g-s\|_{L^\infty(\mathcal A)}
 \le C_{\beta,\mathcal A}K_n^{1/2-\beta}
       \|g\|_{H^\beta(\mathbb R)}.
\]
The constant \(C_{\beta,\mathcal A}<\infty\) is independent of \(g\),
\(n\), and the spline coefficients.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(h=2B_A/K_n\). Since \(K_n\to\infty\), it suffices to take \(0<h\le1\). Use the Fourier convention
\[
 \widehat g(t)=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{ita}g(a)\,da,
 \qquad g(a)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-ita}\widehat g(t)\,dt.
\]
Because \(\beta>1/2\), Cauchy--Schwarz makes \(\widehat g\) integrable. Define
\[
 g_h(a)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{|t|\le h^{-1}}e^{-ita}\widehat g(t)\,dt.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz and \(\int_{h^{-1}}^\infty t^{-2\beta}dt=(2\beta-1)^{-1}h^{2\beta-1}\) give
\[
 \|g-g_h\|_\infty\le C_1(\beta)h^{\beta-1/2}\|g\|_{H^\beta}.
\]

Use the clamped normalized B-spline basis in \(\mathrm{def{:}signal\text{-}sieves}\). It is nonnegative, forms a partition of unity, and spans all order-\(d\) splines, including polynomials of degree at most \(d-1\). Choose \(\xi_k\) in each basis support. Let \(\Lambda_{k,h}(v)\) be the \(k\)-th B-spline coefficient of the degree-\((d-1)\) Taylor polynomial of \(v\) at \(\xi_k\). After translation and dilation by \(h\), there is one interior pattern and finitely many boundary patterns. Finite-dimensional norm equivalence gives
\[
 |\Lambda_{k,h}(v)|\le C_d\max_{0\le j\le d-1}h^j|v^{(j)}(\xi_k)|.
\]
Define \(J_hv=\sum_k\Lambda_{k,h}(v)\mathsf B_{k,K_n}\). It reproduces polynomials of degree at most \(d-1\). Taylor's integral remainder and basis locality imply
\[
 \|v-J_hv\|_{L^\infty(\mathcal A)}\le C_dh^d\|v^{(d)}\|_\infty.
\]
For \(v=g_h\), differentiation under the compact-frequency integral and Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \|g_h^{(d)}\|_\infty
 &\le\frac1{2\pi}
 \left\{\int_{|t|\le h^{-1}}|t|^{2d}(1+t^2)^{-\beta}\,dt\right\}^{1/2}
 \left\{\int_{\mathbb R}(1+t^2)^\beta|\widehat g(t)|^2\,dt\right\}^{1/2}\\
 &\le C_2(\beta,d)h^{\beta-d-1/2}\|g\|_{H^\beta}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Here \(d=\lceil\beta\rceil+1>\beta\). Taking \(s=J_hg_h\) and combining the inequalities gives
\[
 \|g-s\|_{L^\infty(\mathcal A)}
 \le C_3(\beta,d)(2B_A)^{\beta-1/2}K_n^{1/2-\beta}\|g\|_{H^\beta(\mathbb R)}.
\]
Choosing \(C_{\beta,\mathcal A}\) no smaller than this fixed constant proves the stated inequality.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:moment-derived-local-modulus]\label{lem:moment-derived-local-modulus}
Let \(j\in\{1,2\}\), and let \(Q_j\) be a Borel probability law on
\(\mathbb R\) such that
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}|u|^{q_e}\,dQ_j(u)\le M_e,
 \qquad
 \phi_{Q_j}(t)=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{itu}\,dQ_j(u).
\]
For \(q_e>1\),
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}|u|\,dQ_j(u)\le M_e^{1/q_e},
 \qquad
 |\phi_{Q_j}(s)-\phi_{Q_j}(t)|
 \le M_e^{1/q_e}|s-t|,
\]
and
\[
 |\phi_{Q_j}(t)|\ge1-|t|M_e^{1/q_e}.
\]
Consequently, for \(\nu_*=(2M_e^{1/q_e})^{-1}\) and \(c_*=1/2\),
\[
 \inf_{|t|\le\nu_*}|\phi_{Q_j}(t)|\ge c_*.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Because \(q_e>1\), H\"older's inequality with respect to probability law \(Q_j\) gives
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}|u|\,dQ_j(u)
 \le\left\{\int_{\mathbb R}|u|^{q_e}\,dQ_j(u)\right\}^{1/q_e}
 \le M_e^{1/q_e}.
\]
The elementary bound \(|e^{iv}-1|\le|v|\) gives, for real \(s,t\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |\phi_{Q_j}(s)-\phi_{Q_j}(t)|
 &\le\int|e^{isu}-e^{itu}|\,dQ_j(u)\\
 &\le|s-t|\int|u|\,dQ_j(u)
 \le M_e^{1/q_e}|s-t|.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Since \(\phi_{Q_j}(0)=1\), the reverse triangle inequality yields
\[
 |\phi_{Q_j}(t)|\ge1-|t|M_e^{1/q_e}.
\]
The positivity of \(M_e\) gives \(\nu_*=(2M_e^{1/q_e})^{-1}>0\). If \(|t|\le\nu_*\), the last display gives \(|\phi_{Q_j}(t)|\ge1/2=c_*\). This proves all assertions.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:sobolev-modulus-control]\label{lem:sobolev-modulus-control}
For \(\beta>1/2\) and \(R>0\), the function \(\Omega_R\) is finite, nondecreasing, and satisfies \(\Omega_R(r)\downarrow0\) as \(r\downarrow0\). Every \(g\in H^\beta(\mathbb R)\) with \(\|g\|_{H^\beta(\mathbb R)}\le R\) has a continuous representative obeying
\[
 |g(a)-g(b)|\le\Omega_R(|a-b|),\qquad a,b\in\mathbb R.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use the Fourier norm convention
\[
 \|g\|_{H^\beta}^2=\frac1{2\pi}
 \int_{\mathbb R}(1+t^2)^\beta|\widehat g(t)|^2\,dt.
\]
Because \(\beta>1/2\), \((1+t^2)^{-\beta}\) is integrable. Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}|\widehat g(t)|\,dt
 \le
 \left\{\int_{\mathbb R}(1+t^2)^{-\beta}\,dt\right\}^{1/2}
 \left\{\int_{\mathbb R}(1+t^2)^\beta|\widehat g(t)|^2\,dt\right\}^{1/2}<\infty.
\]
Fourier inversion hence supplies a continuous representative. For \(h=a-b\), another application of Cauchy--Schwarz yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |g(a)-g(b)|
 &\le \frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}
       |e^{-ita}-e^{-itb}|\,|\widehat g(t)|\,dt\\
 &\le \frac{\|g\|_{H^\beta}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}
 \left\{\int_{\mathbb R}|e^{-ith}-1|^2(1+t^2)^{-\beta}\,dt\right\}^{1/2}\\
 &\le \Omega_R(|a-b|).
 \end{aligned}
\]
The definition makes \(\Omega_R\) nondecreasing. Its finiteness follows from \(|e^{-ith}-1|\le2\). For every sequence \(r_m\downarrow0\),
\[
 \sup_{|h|\le r_m}|e^{-ith}-1|^2\le\min\{4,t^2r_m^2\}
\]
converges pointwise to zero and is dominated by four. Dominated convergence against \((1+t^2)^{-\beta}dt\) proves \(\Omega_R(r_m)\to0\); monotonicity gives the stated limit.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:finite-sieve-diameter]\label{lem:finite-sieve-diameter}
For the equicontinuous finite signal sieves and the deterministic diameter in \(\mathrm{def{:}finite\text{-}sieve\text{-}diameter}\),
\[
 \widetilde\omega_n\longrightarrow0.
\]
This conclusion is qualitative: it supplies neither an explicit inverse modulus nor an \(O(r_n)\) bound.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Suppose, toward a contradiction, that \(\widetilde\omega_n\not\to0\). Then there are \(\epsilon>0\), integers \(n_k\to\infty\), regular-count samples \(z_k\), and pairs \(\theta_k,\theta'_k\in\Theta_{n_k,\alpha}(z_k)\) such that
\[
 \|\mu_{\theta_k}-\mu_{\theta'_k}\|_{L^\infty(I)}\ge\epsilon.
\]
We construct convergent subsequences and identify their limits.

Since \(K_n\to\infty\) and \(\beta>1/2\), \(a_{f,n},a_{q,n}\to0\). For any feasible density spline \(f\), write \(f=f_+-f_-\). The lower and integral constraints give
\[
 \int_{\mathcal A}f_-\le2B_Aa_{f,n},
 \qquad
 \int_{\mathcal A}f_+\le1+4B_Aa_{f,n},
 \qquad
 \|f\|_{L^1(\mathcal A)}\le1+6B_Aa_{f,n}.
\]
The marked envelope similarly gives
\[
 \|q\|_{L^1(\mathcal A)}
 \le B_Y(1+2B_Aa_{f,n})+2B_A(a_{q,n}+B_Ya_{f,n}).
\]
The modulus constraints and \(\mathrm{lem{:}sobolev\text{-}modulus\text{-}control}\) imply asymptotic equicontinuity: given \(\varepsilon>0\), first choose \(r>0\) so that both \(\Omega_{R_f}(r)\) and \(\Omega_{R_q}(r)\) are below \(\varepsilon/2\), and then take \(k\) large enough that both approximation additions are below \(\varepsilon/2\). Each uniform \(L^1\) bound supplies a uniformly bounded value at some point of the nondegenerate interval \(\mathcal A\); the common modulus transports that bound to every point. A finite-mesh diagonal argument therefore yields, simultaneously over finite \(\mathcal X\),
\[
 f_{x,k}\to f_x,\quad q_{x,k}\to q_x,
 \qquad
 f'_{x,k}\to f'_x,\quad q'_{x,k}\to q'_x
 \quad\text{uniformly on }\mathcal A.
\]
Passing to the constraints gives
\[
 f_x,f'_x\ge0,\quad \int_{\mathcal A}f_x=\int_{\mathcal A}f'_x=1,
 \quad f_x,f'_x\ge\kappa\text{ on }I,
 \quad |q_x|\le B_Yf_x,\quad |q'_x|\le B_Yf'_x.
\]

Represent the candidate error transforms by their atomic laws \(Q_{j,k}\) and \(Q'_{j,k}\). Their common moment bound implies
\[
 Q_{j,k}(|u|>R)\le M_eR^{-q_e},
\]
and likewise for the primed laws. Diagonal extraction of distribution functions at rational continuity points, followed by rational sandwiching and the tail bound, gives weakly convergent subsequences \(Q_{j,k}\Rightarrow Q_j\) and \(Q'_{j,k}\Rightarrow Q'_j\). Applying weak convergence to \(|u|^{q_e}\wedge L\), then letting \(L\to\infty\), preserves the moment bound. Since \(q_e>1\),
\[
 \int_{|u|>R}|u|\,dQ_{j,k}(u)\le M_eR^{1-q_e}\longrightarrow0
\]
uniformly in \(k\), and the same holds for all other sequences and limits. Hence first moments converge. Because the second-coordinate sieve imposes
\[
 \left|\int u\,dQ_{2,k}(u)\right|\le2M_eH_{n_k}^{1-q_e},
\]
and \(H_n\to\infty\), the limiting second-coordinate laws have mean zero. The power-mean bound \(\int|u|dQ_{j,k}\le M_e^{1/q_e}\) makes all characteristic functions uniformly Lipschitz. Pointwise convergence under weak convergence and a finite-mesh argument give locally uniform convergence of all four characteristic functions.

The mass vectors lie in the compact simplex, so pass to limits \(p\) and \(p'\). Both vectors lie in the same mass region, and thus
\[
 \max_x|p_{x,k}-p'_{x,k}|
 \le2\left\{\frac{\log(6|\mathcal X|/\alpha)}{2n_k}\right\}^{1/2}
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
Therefore \(p=p'\).

For a tuple \(\theta\), abbreviate its fitted channels by
\[
 F_{x,\theta}(s,t)=\widehat f_x(s+t)\phi_1(s)\phi_2(t),
 \qquad
 G_{x,\theta}(s,t)=\widehat q_x(s+t)\phi_1(s)\phi_2(t).
\]
The uniform \(L^1\) bounds, compact signal support, and uniform first moments make these channels uniformly equicontinuous on \(\mathbb R^2\). The grid mesh is \((B_AK_n)^{-1}\to0\) and its range is \(T_n=K_n/B_A\to\infty\). Moreover,
\[
 |\mathcal D_n|=(2K_n^2+1)^2,
 \qquad \lambda_n=O(\log K_n)=o(n).
\]
On regular-count samples,
\[
 \max_x\rho^F_{x,n_k}
 \le2\left(\frac{2\lambda_{n_k}}{n_kp_{\min}}\right)^{1/2}=o(1),
 \qquad
 \max_x\rho^G_{x,n_k}\le B_Y\max_x\rho^F_{x,n_k}=o(1).
\]
Also \(\delta^F_n,\delta^G_n\to0\), because \(a_{f,n},a_{q,n}\to0\) and \(\tau_{e,n}=2(\lambda_n/n)^{1/2}\to0\). Feasibility around the same empirical channels yields at every grid point
\[
 |F_{x,\theta_k}-F_{x,\theta'_k}|
 \le2(\rho^F_{x,n_k}+\delta^F_{n_k})=o(1),
 \qquad
 |G_{x,\theta_k}-G_{x,\theta'_k}|
 \le2(\rho^G_{x,n_k}+\delta^G_{n_k})=o(1).
\]
Uniform signal convergence implies convergence of their Fourier transforms, uniformly at every frequency, because the support length is \(2B_A\). Together with locally uniform convergence of the error transforms, this gives locally uniform convergence of fitted channels. Choosing nearest grid points to any fixed \((s,t)\), using uniform equicontinuity, and passing to the limit proves that the two limiting tuples have identical probability and marked channels for every \(x,s,t\).

By \(\mathrm{lem{:}moment\text{-}derived\text{-}local\text{-}modulus}\), each limiting error transform has modulus at least \(1/2\) on \([-\nu_*,\nu_*]\). Since each limiting \(f_x\) is a probability density supported on \(\mathcal A\),
\[
 |\widehat f_x(u)-1|\le B_A|u|,
 \qquad |\widehat f'_x(u)-1|\le B_A|u|.
\]
Thus every factor in both probability channels is nonzero on a common neighborhood of zero. Finite first moments and centering give \(\phi_{Q_2}'(0)=\phi_{Q'_2}'(0)=0\). Differentiate equality of the probability channels in the second frequency at \((u,0)\), divide by the nonzero common value, and use centering to obtain
\[
 \frac{\widehat f_x'(u)}{\widehat f_x(u)}
 =\frac{(\widehat{f'_x})'(u)}{\widehat{f'_x}(u)}.
\]
The ratio \(\widehat f_x/\widehat{f'_x}\) has derivative zero and equals one at zero, so the transforms agree locally. Compact support makes their difference entire. At an interior point of the real interval of equality, every real derivative and hence every complex Taylor coefficient is zero; the globally convergent Taylor series is therefore identically zero. Fourier injectivity follows directly: if \(h=f_x-f'_x\) and \(\gamma_\sigma\) is the centered Gaussian density with variance \(\sigma^2\), then Fubini's theorem gives
\[
 (h*\gamma_\sigma)(a)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}
 e^{-ita}\widehat h(t)e^{-\sigma^2t^2/2}\,dt=0,
\]
while translation continuity in \(L^1\) gives \(\|h*\gamma_\sigma-h\|_1\to0\). Thus \(f_x=f'_x\) almost everywhere, and continuity makes equality pointwise on \(I\).

On the same neighborhood, equality of both channels gives
\[
 \widehat q_x(u)
 =\widehat f_x(u)\frac{G_{x,\theta}(u,0)}{F_{x,\theta}(u,0)}
 =\widehat{f'_x}(u)\frac{G_{x,\theta'}(u,0)}{F_{x,\theta'}(u,0)}
 =\widehat{q'_x}(u).
\]
The signed functions are integrable and compactly supported. Repeating the Taylor-coefficient and Gaussian-convolution arguments gives \(q_x=q'_x\) pointwise on \(I\). Together with \(p=p'\), this identifies the limiting target functions.

Finally, uniform signal convergence, mass convergence, and the finite-sieve lower bound \(f_{x,k},f'_{x,k}\ge\kappa/2\) on \(I\) imply that both target sequences converge uniformly to \(\sum_xp_xq_x/f_x\). This contradicts separation by \(\epsilon\). Therefore \(\widetilde\omega_n\to0\). The argument supplies no numerical inverse modulus or \(O(r_n)\) rate.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Sobolev equicontinuity, moment tightness, expanding-grid agreement, centering, and marked cocycle injectivity force the regular-count worst-case target diameter to vanish. \textit{Gap.} The compactness contradiction is qualitative and yields neither an explicit modulus nor the candidate logarithmic rate. \textit{Consumer.} It is the deterministic width input for the finite-sieve uniformly consistent band theorem.

\begin{theorem}[thm:finite-sieve-honest-consistent-upper]\label{thm:finite-sieve-honest-consistent-upper}
There exists \(n_0<\infty\), depending only on \(\alpha\) and the fixed class indices, such that for every \(n\ge n_0\) the finite-dimensional, semi-infinite deterministic profiling construction \(\mathfrak H^{\mathrm{up}}\) yields a band \(\mathcal C_n\in\mathfrak B_{n,\alpha}\) with jointly sample-and-index measurable finite endpoints, simultaneous coverage at least \(1-\alpha\) uniformly over \(\mathcal M\), and \(0\le W_n\le2B_Y\) almost surely. Moreover,
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M}E_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\otimes n}}[W_n]
 \le \widetilde\omega_n+2b_{\mu,n}
   +2B_Y|\mathcal X|e^{-np_{\min}/8}=o(1).
\]
No finite decision algorithm is claimed. The conclusion is qualitative and gives neither an explicit rate, an \(O(r_n)\) bound, nor a minimax claim.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
All measurability, ordering, clipping, honesty, and coverage assertions follow from \(\mathrm{thm{:}honest\text{-}upper}\). Let
\[
 \mathcal R_n=\{\min_{x\in\mathcal X}n_x\ge np_{\min}/2\}.
\]
On \(\mathcal R_n\), an empty feasible set gives \(W_n=0\). If the feasible set is nonempty, put
\[
 \ell(a)=\inf_{\theta\in\Theta_{n,\alpha}}\mu_\theta(a),
 \qquad
 u(a)=\sup_{\theta\in\Theta_{n,\alpha}}\mu_\theta(a).
\]
Since \(L_n(a)\ge\ell(a)-b_{\mu,n}\) and \(U_n(a)\le u(a)+b_{\mu,n}\),
\[
 U_n(a)-L_n(a)\le u(a)-\ell(a)+2b_{\mu,n}.
\]
Taking the supremum over \(I\) and using the finite-sieve diameter gives
\[
 W_n\mathbf1_{\mathcal R_n}\le\widetilde\omega_n+2b_{\mu,n}.
\]
On \(\mathcal R_n^c\), clipping gives \(W_n\le2B_Y\). The count inequality in \(\mathrm{lem{:}mass\text{-}confidence}\) therefore gives, uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal M\),
\[
 E_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\otimes n}}[W_n]
 \le\widetilde\omega_n+2b_{\mu,n}
   +2B_Y|\mathcal X|e^{-np_{\min}/8}.
\]
The finite-sieve diameter tends to zero. Also \(b_{\mu,n}\to0\), because it is a fixed positive linear combination of \(K_n^{1/2-\beta}\) terms and \(\beta>1/2\); the exponential remainder also vanishes. Hence the displayed bound is \(o(1)\). No step supplies a quantitative rate for \(\widetilde\omega_n\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The finite-dimensional, semi-infinite empirical-characteristic-function profiling band is uniformly honest and has uniformly vanishing expected maximal width under the same assumptions. \textit{Gap.} The theorem proves \(o(1)\) but not \(O(r_n)\); tightness and the fixed-nuisance marked converse remain open. \textit{Consumer.} It provides qualitative whole-curve uncertainty quantification while separating consistency from the unresolved sharp-rate program.

\section{Interpretation}

The observed two-channel law identifies the continuous regression curve \(q_x/f_x\). Structural exogeneity first equates this curve with the stratum-specific causal mean almost everywhere in dose; positivity and continuity extend the equality to every target dose. The Sobolev modulus prevents increasingly oscillatory feasible splines and turns expanding-grid channel agreement into qualitative whole-curve contraction. The Melaku--Shi (2026) two-recall diet--mortality analysis motivates this design, but coverage applies to that application only insofar as its data-generating law satisfies the stated compact-support, overlap, structural-response, moment, and smoothness conditions.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The causal bridge, analytic-cocycle identification, simultaneous transform concentration, equicontinuous outer-sieve embedding, clipped uniform coverage, and qualitative finite-sieve target-diameter contraction are proved. Compactness shows \(\widetilde\omega_n\to0\), but supplies no numerical inverse modulus, so the finite-dimensional semi-infinite band has no established logarithmic rate despite uniform consistency. The sharp-rate program still requires a protected-cube quantitative inverse for both probability and signed marked channels. The fixed-nuisance Bernoulli outcome modulus remains the lower-side obstruction.

\section{Honest scope}

The model fixes compact dose support, bounded outcomes, Sobolev radii with \(\beta>1/2\), overlap, an error moment radius with \(q_e>1\), positive finite-stratum masses, and the structural causal response surface. It imposes no separate local spectral-modulus condition: \(|\phi_Q(t)|\ge1-|t|M_e^{1/q_e}\) supplies \(\nu_*=(2M_e^{1/q_e})^{-1}\) and \(c_*=1/2\). For all sufficiently large \(n\), the finite-dimensional, semi-infinite deterministic outer-sieve band is uniformly honest, clipped to width at most \(2B_Y\), and has uniformly \(o(1)\) expected maximal width. The exact-class band remains a parallel conceptual construction with the same qualitative conclusion. No \(O(r_n)\), minimax-matching, or rate-elbow claim is made; the fixed-nuisance marked-modulus open question remains unresolved.
