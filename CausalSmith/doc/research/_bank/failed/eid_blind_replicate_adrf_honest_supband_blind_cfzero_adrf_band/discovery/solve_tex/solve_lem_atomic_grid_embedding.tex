%%% FIELD derived-modulus-statement
Let \(j\in\{1,2\}\), let \(Q_j\) be a Borel probability law on
\(\mathbb R\) satisfying
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}|u|^{q_e}\,dQ_j(u)\le M_e,
 \qquad
 \phi_{Q_j}(t)=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{itu}\,dQ_j(u).
\]
Then
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}|u|\,dQ_j(u)\le M_e^{1/q_e},
 \qquad
 |\phi_{Q_j}(s)-\phi_{Q_j}(t)|
 \le M_e^{1/q_e}|s-t|,
\]
and
\[
 |\phi_{Q_j}(t)|\ge 1-|t|M_e^{1/q_e}.
\]
Consequently, for the derived constants
\(\nu_*=(2M_e^{1/q_e})^{-1}\) and \(c_*=1/2\),
\[
 \inf_{|t|\le\nu_*}|\phi_{Q_j}(t)|\ge c_*.
\]
%%% FIELD derived-modulus-proof
Because the corrected symbol range is \(q_e>1\), the conjugate exponent
\(q_e/(q_e-1)\) is finite.  H\"older's inequality with respect to the
probability measure \(Q_j\) gives
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}|u|\,dQ_j(u)
 \le
 \left\{\int_{\mathbb R}|u|^{q_e}\,dQ_j(u)\right\}^{1/q_e}
 \left\{\int_{\mathbb R}1\,dQ_j(u)\right\}^{1-1/q_e}
 \le M_e^{1/q_e}.                                                  \tag{1}
\]
For real \(v\), the fundamental theorem of calculus applied to
\(r\mapsto e^{irv}\) gives \(|e^{iv}-1|\le |v|\).  Hence, for all
\(s,t\in\mathbb R\), (1) and the triangle inequality imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |\phi_{Q_j}(s)-\phi_{Q_j}(t)|
 &\le\int_{\mathbb R}|e^{isu}-e^{itu}|\,dQ_j(u)\\
 &\le |s-t|\int_{\mathbb R}|u|\,dQ_j(u)
 \le M_e^{1/q_e}|s-t|.                                            \tag{2}
 \end{aligned}
\]
Taking \(s=t\) is not useful for the lower bound; instead use
\(\phi_{Q_j}(0)=1\), the reverse triangle inequality, and the same pointwise
bound to obtain
\[
 |\phi_{Q_j}(t)|
 \ge 1-|\phi_{Q_j}(t)-\phi_{Q_j}(0)|
 \ge 1-|t|M_e^{1/q_e}.                                            \tag{3}
\]
The positivity of \(M_e\) makes
\(\nu_*=(2M_e^{1/q_e})^{-1}>0\).  If \(|t|\le\nu_*\), then (3) yields
\[
 |\phi_{Q_j}(t)|
 \ge 1-\nu_*M_e^{1/q_e}
 =\frac12=c_*.
\]
This proves every assertion.
%%% FIELD error-sieves-construction
For \(j\in\{1,2\}\), let
\[
 \widetilde\Phi_{j,n}
 =\left\{
 t\longmapsto\sum_{\ell=1}^{M_n}w_{j\ell}e^{itu_{j\ell}}:
 \begin{array}{l}
 w_{j\ell}\ge0,\quad \sum_{\ell=1}^{M_n}w_{j\ell}=1,\\
 -H_n\le u_{j1}\le\cdots\le u_{jM_n}\le H_n,\\
 \sum_{\ell=1}^{M_n}w_{j\ell}|u_{j\ell}|^{q_e}\le M_e,\\
 \displaystyle
 \inf_{|t|\le\nu_*}
 \left|\sum_{\ell=1}^{M_n}w_{j\ell}e^{itu_{j\ell}}\right|
 \ge c_*/2
 \end{array}
 \right\}.
\]
Set \(\Phi_{1,n}=\widetilde\Phi_{1,n}\) and
\[
 \Phi_{2,n}
 =\left\{\phi\in\widetilde\Phi_{2,n}:
 \left|\sum_{\ell=1}^{M_n}w_{2\ell}u_{2\ell}\right|
 \le2M_eH_n^{1-q_e}\right\}.
\]
Here \(\nu_*=(2M_e^{1/q_e})^{-1}\) and \(c_*=1/2\) are derived from
the common moment radius rather than separately imposed class indices.
Zero-weight atoms are placed at \(H_n\), and the ordered atom vector is
selected lexicographically, which removes representation ambiguity without
centering \(U_1\).
%%% FIELD atomic-proof
Fix \(P\in\mathcal M\), \(j\in\{1,2\}\), and an \(n\) for which the
deterministic tuning objects are defined.  We use the corrected class with
\(q_e>1\), and no separately assumed local spectral modulus.  Define
\[
 U_j^H=\max\{-H_n,\min\{U_j,H_n\}\}.
\]
On \(\{|U_j|>H_n\}\), one has
\(1\le |U_j|^{q_e}H_n^{-q_e}\).  Therefore
\(\mathrm{ass{:}error\text{-}moment}\) gives
\[
 P(|U_j|>H_n)
 \le H_n^{-q_e}E_P|U_j|^{q_e}
 \le M_eH_n^{-q_e}.                                                \tag{1}
\]
Because \(U_j^H=U_j\) off this tail event and two complex numbers of unit
modulus are at distance at most two, for every \(t\in\mathbb R\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \left|E_Pe^{itU_j^H}-\phi_j(t)\right|
 &\le E_P\left|e^{itU_j^H}-e^{itU_j}\right|\\
 &\le2P(|U_j|>H_n)
 \le2M_eH_n^{-q_e}=\tau_{e,n}.                                    \tag{2}
 \end{aligned}
\]
Clipping cannot increase absolute value, so
\[
 E_P|U_j^H|^{q_e}\le E_P|U_j|^{q_e}\le M_e.                       \tag{3}
\]

For \(j=2\), the first absolute moment exists by
\(\mathrm{lem{:}moment\text{-}derived\text{-}local\text{-}modulus}\)
applied to the law of \(U_2\).  Thus the centering identity in
\(\mathrm{ass{:}center\text{-}two}\) may be subtracted from the clipped
mean.  Since \(q_e>1\), on \(\{|U_2|>H_n\}\) we have
\(|U_2|\le H_n^{1-q_e}|U_2|^{q_e}\), and hence
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |E_PU_2^H|
 &=|E_P(U_2^H-U_2)|\\
 &\le E_P\{|U_2|\mathbf 1(|U_2|>H_n)\}\\
 &\le H_n^{1-q_e}E_P|U_2|^{q_e}
 \le M_eH_n^{1-q_e}.                                              \tag{4}
 \end{aligned}
\]
This is the only step of the atomic embedding that uses the strict threshold
\(q_e>1\); it also shows why the centering constraint contracts as
\(H_n\to\infty\).

We next replace the law of \(U_j^H\), supported on \([-H_n,H_n]\), by an
atomic law preserving all grid values and the two moments needed above.  Put
\[
 D=2|\Gamma_n|+2
\]
and define the continuous map
\[
 v(u)=\left(
   (\operatorname{Re}e^{itu},\operatorname{Im}e^{itu})_{t\in\Gamma_n},
   u,|u|^{q_e}
 \right)\in\mathbb R^D.                                           \tag{5}
\]
Finite-valued approximations to \(U_j^H\) have expectations under \(v\) in
the convex hull of \(v([-H_n,H_n])\), and uniform continuity of \(v\) on
this compact interval makes those expectations converge to
\(E_Pv(U_j^H)\).  Thus the latter vector initially lies in the closure of
that convex hull.

We now prove that this convex hull is closed while simultaneously obtaining
the required support bound.  Suppose any finite convex representation uses
more than \(D+1\) locations \(u_r\), with weights \(w_r\).
Then the augmented vectors \((1,v(u_r))\in\mathbb R^{D+1}\) are linearly
dependent.  Thus there are real numbers \(a_r\), not all zero, such that
\[
 \sum_r a_r=0,
 \qquad
 \sum_r a_rv(u_r)=0.                                               \tag{6}
\]
After replacing every \(a_r\) by \(-a_r\) if necessary, at least one
\(a_r\) is positive.  With
\[
 c=\min_{r:a_r>0}\frac{w_r}{a_r}>0,
 \qquad
 w'_r=w_r-ca_r,
\]
equation (6) shows that \((w'_r)_r\) has the same total mass and barycenter,
all its entries are nonnegative, and at least one positive-weight location is
removed.  Repetition leaves at most
\[
 D+1=2|\Gamma_n|+3\le M_n                                         \tag{7}
\]
locations, where the last inequality is the definition
\(M_n=2|\Gamma_n|+4\).  It follows that the whole convex hull is the image of
the compact set consisting of the \(D\)-simplex of weights and
\((D+1)\) copies of \([-H_n,H_n]\) under the continuous barycenter map.
The convex hull is therefore compact and hence closed.  The preceding limit
argument now proves that \(E_Pv(U_j^H)\) belongs to the convex hull, and (7)
provides a representation of it using at most \(D+1\) locations.

Let \(\bar Q_{j,n}\) be the resulting atomic law.  Order its locations and
pad the representation with zero-weight atoms at \(H_n\).  By (5), its
characteristic function \(\bar\phi_{j,n}\) agrees with the characteristic
function of \(U_j^H\) at every \(t\in\Gamma_n\), its mean agrees with
\(E_PU_j^H\), its support is contained in \([-H_n,H_n]\), and
\[
 \int |u|^{q_e}\,d\bar Q_{j,n}(u)
 =E_P|U_j^H|^{q_e}\le M_e.                                        \tag{8}
\]
Combining this exact grid agreement with (2) proves
\[
 \max_{t\in\Gamma_n}
 |\bar\phi_{j,n}(t)-\phi_j(t)|
 \le\tau_{e,n}.                                                    \tag{9}
\]
For \(j=2\), exact preservation of the mean and (4) give
\[
 \left|\sum_{\ell=1}^{M_n}w_{2\ell}u_{2\ell}\right|
 \le M_eH_n^{1-q_e}
 \le2M_eH_n^{1-q_e}.                                              \tag{10}
\]

It remains only to verify the protected-frequency clause of the corrected
\(\mathrm{def{:}error\text{-}sieves}\).  Apply
\(\mathrm{lem{:}moment\text{-}derived\text{-}local\text{-}modulus}\) to
the probability law \(\bar Q_{j,n}\), using (8).  It yields directly
\[
 \inf_{|t|\le\nu_*}|\bar\phi_{j,n}(t)|
 \ge c_*\ge c_*/2.                                                 \tag{11}
\]
Thus no grid interpolation and no independent spectral assumption are
needed.  Equations (8) and (11), together with the support and ordered-atom
construction, show
\(\bar\phi_{j,n}\in\widetilde\Phi_{j,n}\).  In the second coordinate,
(10) verifies the additional centering tolerance, so
\(\bar\phi_{2,n}\in\Phi_{2,n}\); in the first coordinate,
\(\Phi_{1,n}=\widetilde\Phi_{1,n}\).  Equation (9) proves the displayed
grid approximation, and the first inequality in (10) is the stronger
representing-mean conclusion claimed by the lemma.

For every \(P\in\mathcal M\), every \(j\in\{1,2\}\), and all sufficiently
large \(n\), there is \(\bar\phi_{j,n}\in\Phi_{j,n}\) such that
\[
 \max_{t\in\Gamma_n}|\bar\phi_{j,n}(t)-\phi_j(t)|\le\tau_{e,n}.
\]
For \(j=2\), its representing atomic law also satisfies
\[
 \left|\sum_{\ell=1}^{M_n}w_{2\ell}u_{2\ell}\right|
 \le M_eH_n^{1-q_e}.
\]
