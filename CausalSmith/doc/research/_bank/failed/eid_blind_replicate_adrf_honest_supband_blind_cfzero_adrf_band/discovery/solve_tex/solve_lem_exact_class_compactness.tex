%%% FIELD exact-class-construction
Fix \(\beta\in(1/2,\infty)\) and \(q_e\in(1,\infty)\).  For a Borel
probability law \(Q_j\) on \(\mathbb R\), put
\(\phi_{Q_j}(t)=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{itu}\,dQ_j(u)\).  The exact projected
nuisance class \(\mathscr K\) is the set of tuples
\[
 \vartheta=\bigl((p_x,f_x,q_x)_{x\in\mathcal X},Q_1,Q_2\bigr)
\]
such that
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 p_x\ge p_{\min}\quad(x\in\mathcal X),\qquad
 \sum_{x\in\mathcal X}p_x=1,\\
 f_x:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R,\quad f_x\in H^\beta(\mathbb R),\quad f_x\ge0,\quad
 \int_{\mathbb R}f_x(a)\,da=1,\quad
 \operatorname{supp}(f_x)\subseteq\mathcal A,\quad
 \|f_x\|_{H^\beta}\le R_f,\quad
 \inf_{a\in I}f_x(a)\ge\kappa,\\
 q_x:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R,\quad q_x\in H^\beta(\mathbb R),\quad
 \operatorname{supp}(q_x)\subseteq\mathcal A,\quad
 \|q_x\|_{H^\beta}\le R_q,\quad
 |q_x(a)|\le B_Yf_x(a)\quad(a\in\mathbb R),\\
 \int_{\mathbb R}|u|^{q_e}\,dQ_j(u)\le M_e\quad(j=1,2),\qquad
 \int_{\mathbb R}u\,dQ_2(u)=0.
 \end{gathered}
\]
All pointwise signal constraints use the continuous representatives supplied by
\(\beta>1/2\).  No spectral-modulus condition is imposed on \(Q_1\) or
\(Q_2\).  The tuple represents the product error law \(Q_1\otimes Q_2\); it
is an outer projection of the full causal class and does not assert that every
one of its elements has a structural causal realization.  For \(a\in I\),
define
\[
 \mu_\vartheta(a)=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}p_x\frac{q_x(a)}{f_x(a)}.
\]
%%% FIELD exact-class-compactness-proof
By \(\textnormal{def:exact-projected-class}\), every signal coordinate is
supported in the common compact interval
\(\mathcal A=[-B_A,B_A]\), and its \(H^\beta\)-norm is bounded by \(R_f\)
or \(R_q\).  Use the Fourier norm convention
\[
 \|g\|_{H^\beta}^2
 =\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}(1+t^2)^\beta
       |\widehat g(t)|^2\,dt.                                      \tag{1}
\]
Fourier inversion and Cauchy--Schwarz give, initially for smooth functions and
then by approximation in \(H^\beta\),
\[
 |g(a)|
 \le \frac{\|g\|_{H^\beta}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}
       \left\{\int_{\mathbb R}(1+t^2)^{-\beta}\,dt\right\}^{1/2}, \tag{2}
\]
and
\[
 |g(a)-g(b)|
 \le \frac{\|g\|_{H^\beta}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}
 \left\{\int_{\mathbb R}|e^{-ita}-e^{-itb}|^2
                 (1+t^2)^{-\beta}\,dt\right\}^{1/2}.             \tag{3}
\]
The integral in (2) is finite precisely because the enlarged class has
\(\beta>1/2\).  In (3), the integrand tends pointwise to zero as
\(|a-b|\to0\) and is bounded by the integrable function
\(4(1+t^2)^{-\beta}\).  Dominated convergence therefore gives a common
modulus of continuity for every fixed-radius Sobolev ball.  Thus each such
ball is uniformly bounded and equicontinuous on \(\mathcal A\).

For completeness, these estimates give total boundedness directly.  Given
\(\epsilon>0\), choose a finite spatial mesh of \(\mathcal A\) such that the
common modulus in (3) is at most \(\epsilon/4\) between any point and a
nearest mesh point.  Partition the bounded range furnished by (2) into
finitely many intervals of length at most \(\epsilon/2\).  If two functions
have values in the same interval at every mesh point, then their uniform
distance is at most
\(\epsilon/4+\epsilon/2+\epsilon/4=\epsilon\).  Selecting one representative
from each nonempty pattern gives a finite uniform \(\epsilon\)-net.

We next prove closedness in the uniform metric.  Suppose that
\(g_m\) is supported in \(\mathcal A\),
\(\|g_m\|_{H^\beta}\le R\), and \(g_m\to g\) uniformly on
\(\mathcal A\).  The continuous representative of every \(g_m\) is zero off
\(\mathcal A\), hence is zero at the boundary of \(\mathcal A\).  The zero
extension of \(g\) is consequently continuous and
\[
 \|g_m-g\|_{L^2(\mathbb R)}
 \le (2B_A)^{1/2}\|g_m-g\|_{L^\infty(\mathcal A)}
 \longrightarrow0.                                               \tag{4}
\]
Set \(h_m(t)=(1+t^2)^{\beta/2}\widehat g_m(t)\).  Equation (1) makes
\((h_m)\) bounded in the Hilbert space \(L^2(\mathbb R)\).  Expanding in a
countable orthonormal basis and applying diagonal selection gives a subsequence
\(h_{m_k}\rightharpoonup h\) such that
\[
 \|h\|_2\le\liminf_{k\to\infty}\|h_{m_k}\|_2.                    \tag{5}
\]
By (4) and the Fourier isometry,
\(\widehat g_{m_k}\to\widehat g\) strongly in \(L^2\).  Multiplication by
\((1+t^2)^{-\beta/2}\) is bounded on \(L^2\), so the weak convergence also
implies
\[
 \widehat g_{m_k}\rightharpoonup(1+t^2)^{-\beta/2}h.
\]
Uniqueness of the weak limit gives
\(\widehat g=(1+t^2)^{-\beta/2}h\).  Hence \(g\in H^\beta(\mathbb R)\),
and (5) gives \(\|g\|_{H^\beta}\le R\).  The fixed-support Sobolev ball is
therefore closed in the complete space \(C(\mathcal A)\).  Together with
total boundedness, this proves its compactness in uniform distance.

Apply this conclusion at radii \(R_f\) and \(R_q\).  Uniform convergence on
the common support preserves nonnegativity, the lower bound by \(\kappa\) on
\(I\), and the domination \(|q_x|\le B_Yf_x\).  It also preserves the unit
integral because
\[
 \left|\int_{\mathcal A}(f_m-f)\right|
 \le2B_A\|f_m-f\|_{L^\infty(\mathcal A)}\longrightarrow0.         \tag{6}
\]
Thus the joint signal subset in \(\mathscr K\) is closed in a finite product
of compact spaces and is compact.  The mass vectors form a closed subset of
the finite-dimensional simplex, by \(p_x\ge p_{\min}\) and
\(\sum_xp_x=1\), and hence are compact as well.

It remains to treat an error-law coordinate.  Let \((Q_m)\) satisfy the
common \(q_e\)-moment bound.  Since
\(\mathbf 1\{|u|>R\}\le |u|^{q_e}R^{-q_e}\),
\[
 \sup_m Q_m(|u|>R)\le M_eR^{-q_e}.                                 \tag{7}
\]
This proves tightness directly.  To exhibit a weakly convergent subsequence,
diagonally select the distribution functions at the rational numbers.  The
monotone rational limits, made right-continuous by taking infima over rationals
strictly to the right, define a distribution function; (7) forces its limits
at negative and positive infinity to be zero and one.  Rational sandwiching
then gives convergence at every continuity point of the limiting distribution
function.  For a bounded continuous function, truncate to \([-R,R]\) using
(7), partition that interval at continuity points, and use uniform continuity
on each partition.  First refining the partition and then sending
\(R\to\infty\) proves weak convergence, along the selected subsequence, to a
Borel probability law \(Q\):
\[
 \int h\,dQ_m\longrightarrow\int h\,dQ
 \quad\text{for every bounded continuous }h.                      \tag{8}
\]

For every finite \(L\), the function \(|u|^{q_e}\wedge L\) is bounded and
continuous.  Equation (8) and monotone convergence in \(L\) give
\[
 \int |u|^{q_e}\,dQ(u)
 =\sup_L\int(|u|^{q_e}\wedge L)\,dQ(u)\le M_e.                   \tag{9}
\]
The enlarged error class assumes \(q_e>1\), and this strict inequality is
used in
\[
 \int_{|u|>R}|u|\,dQ_m(u)
 \le R^{1-q_e}\int|u|^{q_e}\,dQ_m(u)
 \le M_eR^{1-q_e}\longrightarrow0,                               \tag{10}
\]
uniformly in \(m\); (9) gives the same bound for \(Q\).  Apply (8) to the
identity clipped to \([-R,R]\), use (10), and then send \(R\to\infty\).
The first moments converge.  Consequently the restriction
\(\int u\,dQ_2(u)=0\) is closed.  Equations (7)--(10) prove compactness of
each error-law coordinate in any metric that metrizes weak convergence.  No
spectral-modulus restriction has been used or is part of the corrected exact
class.

The same moment calculation and the power-mean inequality give
\(\int|u|\,dQ_m(u)\le M_e^{1/q_e}\).  Therefore
\[
 |\phi_{Q_m}(s)-\phi_{Q_m}(t)|
 \le\int|e^{isu}-e^{itu}|\,dQ_m(u)
 \le |s-t|M_e^{1/q_e}.                                            \tag{11}
\]
Weak convergence gives pointwise convergence of the characteristic functions
by (8).  Covering a fixed compact frequency interval by a finite mesh and
using (11) for both \(Q_m\) and \(Q\) upgrades pointwise convergence to
uniform convergence on that interval.  Combining the mass, signal, and two
error coordinates proves compactness of \(\mathscr K\).

Now let \(\vartheta_m\to\vartheta\) in the asserted product metric.  Uniform
signal convergence and common support imply
\[
 \sup_{r\in\mathbb R}|\widehat f_{x,m}(r)-\widehat f_x(r)|
 \le2B_A\|f_{x,m}-f_x\|_{L^\infty(\mathcal A)}\longrightarrow0,  \tag{12}
\]
and the same inequality holds for \(q_x\).  Characteristic functions have
modulus at most one, while \(\int f_x=1\) and
\(\int|q_x|\le B_Y\int f_x=B_Y\).  Thus (11)--(12) imply local uniform
convergence on \(\mathbb R^2\) of both fitted channels
\[
 (s,t)\longmapsto\widehat f_x(s+t)\phi_{Q_1}(s)\phi_{Q_2}(t),
 \qquad
 (s,t)\longmapsto\widehat q_x(s+t)\phi_{Q_1}(s)\phi_{Q_2}(t).    \tag{13}
\]

For continuity of the profiled target, the bounds
\(f_x,f'_x\ge\kappa\) on \(I\) and \(|q'_x|\le B_Yf'_x\) yield
\[
 \left\|\frac{q_x}{f_x}-\frac{q'_x}{f'_x}\right\|_{L^\infty(I)}
 \le\kappa^{-1}\|q_x-q'_x\|_{L^\infty(\mathcal A)}
    +B_Y\kappa^{-1}\|f_x-f'_x\|_{L^\infty(\mathcal A)}.        \tag{14}
\]
Also \(\|q'_x/f'_x\|_{L^\infty(I)}\le B_Y\).  Hence
\[
 \|\mu_\vartheta-\mu_{\vartheta'}\|_{L^\infty(I)}
 \le\sum_xp_x
       \left\|\frac{q_x}{f_x}-\frac{q'_x}{f'_x}\right\|_{L^\infty(I)}
      +B_Y\sum_x|p_x-p'_x|,                                      \tag{15}
\]
which tends to zero in the product metric.  This proves continuity from
\(\mathscr K\) to \(C(I)\).

Finally fix \(n\) and a realized sample \(z\).  By
\(\textnormal{def:exact-joint-feasible-set}\), feasibility is the conjunction
of finitely many mass and channel inequalities.  The corresponding positive
part violations include
\[
 v^F_{x,s,t}(z,\vartheta)
 =\left(
 \left|\widehat F_{x,n}(z;s,t)
       -\widehat f_x(s+t)\phi_{Q_1}(s)\phi_{Q_2}(t)\right|
       -\rho^F_{x,n}(z)
 \right)_+,                                                     \tag{16}
\]
the analogous \(v^G_{x,s,t}\), and
\[
 v^p_x(z,\vartheta)
 =\left(
 |p_x-\widehat p_x(z)|
 -\left\{\frac{\log(6|\mathcal X|/\alpha)}{2n}\right\}^{1/2}
 \right)_+.                                                     \tag{17}
\]
The empirical sums, counts, empty-cell convention, and radii in
\(\textnormal{def:mass-confidence-region}\),
\(\textnormal{def:upper-tuning-grid}\), and
\(\textnormal{def:ecf-radii}\) are Borel measurable functions of \(z\).
Thus (16)--(17) are measurable in \(z\).  Equations (13) and continuity of
the mass coordinates show that they are continuous in \(\vartheta\).  Their
common zero set is exactly \(\Theta^{\mathrm{ex}}_{n,\alpha}(z)\), so it is
closed in compact \(\mathscr K\), and therefore compact.  This also proves
the asserted measurability and continuity of all constraint-violation
functions.
%%% FIELD qualitative-exact-grid-diameter-proof
Suppose, to the contrary, that (1) does not tend to zero.  Then there are
\(\epsilon>0\), integers \(n_k\to\infty\), realized samples \(z_k\) with
\(\min_x n_x(z_k)\ge n_kp_{\min}/2\), and tuples
\(\vartheta_k,\vartheta'_k\in
\Theta^{\mathrm{ex}}_{n_k,\alpha}(z_k)\) such that
\[
 \|\mu_{\vartheta_k}-\mu_{\vartheta'_k}\|_{L^\infty(I)}
 \ge\epsilon.                                                    \tag{2}
\]
By \(\textnormal{lem:exact-class-compactness}\), after passage to one common
subsequence,
\[
 \vartheta_k\longrightarrow\vartheta,
 \qquad
 \vartheta'_k\longrightarrow\vartheta'                           \tag{3}
\]
in the product topology of that lemma.

Both mass vectors belong to the same region from
\(\textnormal{def:mass-confidence-region}\), whence
\[
 \max_{x\in\mathcal X}|p_{x,k}-p'_{x,k}|
 \le2\left\{\frac{\log(6|\mathcal X|/\alpha)}{2n_k}\right\}^{1/2}
 \longrightarrow0.                                                \tag{4}
\]
The two limiting mass vectors are therefore equal.  From
\(\textnormal{def:upper-tuning-grid}\),
\[
 |\mathcal D_n|=(2K_n^2+1)^2,
 \qquad
 \lambda_n=O(\log K_n)=o(n).                                     \tag{5}
\]
The regular-count condition and \(\textnormal{def:ecf-radii}\) consequently
give
\[
 \max_x\rho^F_{x,n_k}
 \le2\left(\frac{2\lambda_{n_k}}{n_kp_{\min}}\right)^{1/2}=o(1),
 \qquad
 \max_x\rho^G_{x,n_k}
 \le B_Y\max_x\rho^F_{x,n_k}=o(1).                              \tag{6}
\]

For a tuple \(\vartheta\), abbreviate its fitted channels by
\[
 F_{x,\vartheta}(s,t)
 =\widehat f_x(s+t)\phi_{Q_1}(s)\phi_{Q_2}(t),
 \qquad
 G_{x,\vartheta}(s,t)
 =\widehat q_x(s+t)\phi_{Q_1}(s)\phi_{Q_2}(t).                    \tag{7}
\]
The moment constraint in \(\textnormal{def:exact-projected-class}\) and the
power-mean inequality give
\(\int|u|\,dQ_j(u)\le M_e^{1/q_e}\).  Since \(|a|\le B_A\) on
\(\mathcal A\), \(|e^{iv}-e^{iw}|\le|v-w|\), and
\(\int|q_x|\le B_Y\), direct integration of the product laws gives, uniformly
over \(\vartheta\in\mathscr K\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |F_{x,\vartheta}(s,t)-F_{x,\vartheta}(s',t')|
 &\le(B_A+M_e^{1/q_e})(|s-s'|+|t-t'|),\\
 |G_{x,\vartheta}(s,t)-G_{x,\vartheta}(s',t')|
 &\le B_Y(B_A+M_e^{1/q_e})(|s-s'|+|t-t'|).
 \end{aligned}                                                    \tag{8}
\]
At each grid point, feasibility around the same empirical transform and the
triangle inequality imply
\[
 |F_{x,\vartheta_k}-F_{x,\vartheta'_k}|\le2\rho^F_{x,n_k},
 \qquad
 |G_{x,\vartheta_k}-G_{x,\vartheta'_k}|\le2\rho^G_{x,n_k}.         \tag{9}
\]
The mesh of \(\Gamma_{n_k}\) is \((B_AK_{n_k})^{-1}\), while its range is
\(K_{n_k}/B_A\to\infty\).  For any fixed \((s,t)\), take a nearest grid
point for all sufficiently large \(k\).  Equations (6), (8), and (9) show
that the difference between the two probability channels, and separately
between the two marked channels, tends to zero at \((s,t)\).  Local uniform
channel convergence from \(\textnormal{lem:exact-class-compactness}\) permits
passage to (3), giving
\[
 F_{x,\vartheta}(s,t)=F_{x,\vartheta'}(s,t),
 \qquad
 G_{x,\vartheta}(s,t)=G_{x,\vartheta'}(s,t)                       \tag{10}
\]
for every \(x\in\mathcal X\) and every \((s,t)\in\mathbb R^2\).

We now derive the required local nonvanishing from the moment restriction;
no spectral-modulus hypothesis is used.  For any error law in the corrected
exact class,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |\phi_Q(v)|
 &\ge1-|\phi_Q(v)-1|\\
 &\ge1-\int|e^{ivu}-1|\,dQ(u)\\
 &\ge1-|v|\int|u|\,dQ(u)
 \ge1-|v|M_e^{1/q_e}.                                             \tag{11}
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus, with
\[
 \nu_*=(2M_e^{1/q_e})^{-1},\qquad c_*=1/2,                        \tag{12}
\]
every candidate error characteristic function has modulus at least \(c_*\)
on \([-\nu_*,\nu_*]\).  Also, for every candidate density,
\[
 |\widehat f_x(v)-1|
 \le\int_{\mathcal A}|e^{iva}-1|f_x(a)\,da
 \le B_A|v|.                                                      \tag{13}
\]
Put \(\delta=\min\{(4B_A)^{-1},\nu_*/2\}>0\).  Equations (11)--(13)
show that, for \(|u|,|t|\le\delta\), every factor of
\(F_{x,\vartheta}(u,t)\) and \(F_{x,\vartheta'}(u,t)\) is nonzero.

Fix \(x\), and denote the signal coordinates of \(\vartheta'\) by
\(\widetilde f_x,\widetilde q_x\).  Because \(q_e>1\), both second-coordinate
error laws have finite first moments, so their characteristic functions are
differentiable at zero.  Centering in
\(\textnormal{def:exact-projected-class}\) gives
\[
 \phi'_{Q_2}(0)=i\int u\,dQ_2(u)=0,
 \qquad
 \phi'_{Q'_2}(0)=i\int u\,dQ'_2(u)=0.                             \tag{14}
\]
Differentiate the first equality in (10) with respect to \(t\) at
\((u,0)\), divide by the common nonzero channel value, and use (14).  For
\(|u|\le\delta\),
\[
 \frac{\widehat f'_x(u)}{\widehat f_x(u)}
 =\left.\frac{\partial_tF_{x,\vartheta}(u,t)}
 {F_{x,\vartheta}(u,t)}\right|_{t=0}
 =\left.\frac{\partial_tF_{x,\vartheta'}(u,t)}
 {F_{x,\vartheta'}(u,t)}\right|_{t=0}
 =\frac{\widehat{\widetilde f}'_x(u)}
        {\widehat{\widetilde f}_x(u)}.                            \tag{15}
\]
The derivative of
\(\widehat f_x/\widehat{\widetilde f}_x\) is consequently zero on this
interval.  Since both transforms equal one at zero,
\[
 \widehat f_x(u)=\widehat{\widetilde f}_x(u),
 \qquad |u|\le\delta.                                             \tag{16}
\]

Compact support makes both transforms entire: differentiation of every order
under the integral is justified by
\(|a|\le B_A\) and
\(|e^{iza}|\le e^{B_A|\operatorname{Im}z|}\).  Applied to their difference,
(16) makes every derivative at zero vanish.  Its everywhere-convergent Taylor
series is therefore zero, so the two transforms agree on all of \(\mathbb C\).
To verify injectivity without invoking an undeclared Fourier-uniqueness result,
let \(h=f_x-\widetilde f_x\) and let \(\gamma_\sigma\) be the centered
Gaussian density of variance \(\sigma^2\).  Absolute integrability and the
Gaussian integral give
\[
 (h*\gamma_\sigma)(a)
 =\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-ita}
   \widehat h(t)e^{-\sigma^2t^2/2}\,dt=0.                         \tag{17}
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \|h*\gamma_\sigma-h\|_1
 \le\int\gamma_\sigma(y)\|h(\,\cdot-y)-h\|_1\,dy
 \longrightarrow0.                                              \tag{18}
\]
Indeed, translation continuity holds first for indicators of bounded
intervals, then for their finite linear combinations, and finally for every
\(L^1\) function by approximation; the integrand in (18) is bounded by
\(2\|h\|_1\), and the Gaussian mass concentrates at zero.  Equations
(17)--(18) give \(f_x=\widetilde f_x\) almost everywhere, hence everywhere
for their continuous \(H^\beta\) representatives.

For \(|u|\le\delta\), equations (10) and (16), together with the nonzero
denominator established above, yield
\[
 \widehat q_x(u)
 =\widehat f_x(u)\frac{G_{x,\vartheta}(u,0)}{F_{x,\vartheta}(u,0)}
 =\widehat{\widetilde f}_x(u)
   \frac{G_{x,\vartheta'}(u,0)}{F_{x,\vartheta'}(u,0)}
 =\widehat{\widetilde q}_x(u).                                   \tag{19}
\]
The domination \(|q_x|\le B_Yf_x\) makes both signed densities integrable,
and their common compact support makes their transforms entire.  The
Taylor-series and Gaussian-convolution arguments used in (16)--(18) therefore
give \(q_x=\widetilde q_x\).  This holds for every cell \(x\).  Together with
(4), the limiting tuples have the same target function.

Finally, continuity of \(\vartheta\mapsto\mu_\vartheta\) from
\(\textnormal{lem:exact-class-compactness}\) and (3) imply
\[
 \|\mu_{\vartheta_k}-\mu_{\vartheta'_k}\|_{L^\infty(I)}
 \longrightarrow
 \|\mu_\vartheta-\mu_{\vartheta'}\|_{L^\infty(I)}=0,
\]
contradicting (2).  Hence \(\omega_n\to0\).  The compactness contradiction
is qualitative and supplies no numerical modulus or \(O(r_n)\) conclusion.
