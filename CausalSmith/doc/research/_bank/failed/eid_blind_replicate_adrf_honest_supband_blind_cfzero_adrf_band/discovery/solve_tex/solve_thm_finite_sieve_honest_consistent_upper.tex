%%% FIELD def-reference-bump-construction
Put
\[
 \widetilde g_0(a)=
 \begin{cases}
  \exp\{-1/(B_A^2-a^2)\},& |a|<B_A,\\
  0,& |a|\ge B_A,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 Z_0=\int_{\mathbb R}\widetilde g_0(u)\,du,
 \qquad
 g_0(a)=Z_0^{-1}\widetilde g_0(a).
\]
Define
\[
 R_{f,0}=\|g_0\|_{H^\beta(\mathbb R)}<\infty,
 \qquad
 \kappa_0=\min_{a\in I}g_0(a)>0.
\]
Thus \(g_0\) is a normalized, nonnegative, infinitely differentiable density supported on \(\mathcal A\), and it is strictly positive on \(\operatorname{int}(\mathcal A)\).

%%% FIELD ass-primitive-compatibility-condition
The fixed indices obey the single compatibility condition
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \varnothing\ne\mathcal X\text{ is finite},\qquad
 B_A>0,\qquad
 \mathcal A=[-B_A,B_A],\qquad
 I\subset\operatorname{int}(\mathcal A)
 \text{ is a nondegenerate compact interval},\\
 \beta>\frac12,\qquad
 0<p_{\min}\le |\mathcal X|^{-1},\qquad
 0<\kappa\le\kappa_0,\qquad
 R_f\ge R_{f,0},\\
 R_q>0,\qquad M_e>0,\qquad q_e>1,\qquad B_Y\ge1.
 \end{gathered}
\]

%%% FIELD def-exact-projected-class-construction
Under \(\mathrm{ass{:}primitive\text{-}compatibility}\), with \(g_0\), \(R_{f,0}\), and \(\kappa_0\) fixed by \(\mathrm{def{:}reference\text{-}bump}\), define the exact projected nuisance class \(\mathscr K\) as follows.  For a Borel probability law \(Q_j\) on \(\mathbb R\), put
\[
 \phi_{Q_j}(t)=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{itu}\,dQ_j(u).
\]
The class \(\mathscr K\) consists of all tuples
\[
 \vartheta=\bigl((p_x,f_x,q_x)_{x\in\mathcal X},Q_1,Q_2\bigr)
\]
such that
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 p_x\ge p_{\min}\quad(x\in\mathcal X),
 \qquad \sum_{x\in\mathcal X}p_x=1,\\
 f_x\in H^\beta(\mathbb R),\qquad f_x\ge0,qquad
 \int_{\mathbb R}f_x(a)\,da=1,qquad
 \operatorname{supp}(f_x)\subseteq\mathcal A,\\
 \|f_x\|_{H^\beta(\mathbb R)}\le R_f,qquad
 \inf_{a\in I}f_x(a)\ge\kappa,\\
 q_x\in H^\beta(\mathbb R),
 \qquad \operatorname{supp}(q_x)\subseteq\mathcal A,
 \qquad \|q_x\|_{H^\beta(\mathbb R)}\le R_q,\\
 |q_x(a)|\le B_Yf_x(a)\quad(a\in\mathbb R),\\
 \int_{\mathbb R}|u|^{q_e}\,dQ_j(u)\le M_e
 \quad(j\in\{1,2\}),
 \qquad \int_{\mathbb R}u\,dQ_2(u)=0.
 \end{gathered}
\]
All pointwise signal constraints use the continuous representatives supplied by \(\beta>1/2\).  No spectral-modulus condition is imposed on \(Q_1\) or \(Q_2\).  The tuple represents the product error law \(Q_1\otimes Q_2\); it is an outer projection of the full causal class and does not assert that every element has a structural causal realization.  Primitive compatibility makes the fixed indices jointly admissible: in particular, \(g_0\) satisfies the density-radius and overlap constraints.  For \(a\in I\), define
\[
 \mu_\vartheta(a)=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}p_x\frac{q_x(a)}{f_x(a)}.
\]

%%% FIELD stmt-structural-response-implications
For every \(P\in\mathcal M\), the structural response representation has the following consequences.  First, \(A\perp Y(a)\mid X\) for every \(a\in\mathcal A\).  Second, on one event of \(P\)-probability one,
\[
 \sup_{a\in\mathcal A}|Y(a)|\le B_Y
 \quad\text{and}\quad |Y|\le B_Y.
\]
Third, for every \(x\in\mathcal X\),
\[
 |q_x(a)|\le B_Yf_x(a)
\]
holds Lebesgue-almost everywhere, and it holds for every \(a\in\mathbb R\) when \(f_x\) and \(q_x\) denote their continuous \(H^\beta(\mathbb R)\) representatives.  No pointwise assertion is made for an arbitrary version of \(m_x\).

%%% FIELD proof-structural-response-implications
Fix \(P\in\mathcal M\).  By \(\mathrm{ass{:}structural\text{-}response\text{-}surface}\), there are a standard Borel random element \(V\), a jointly measurable \(h\), and a single event \(\Omega_0\) of probability one such that
\[
 V\perp A\mid X,
 \qquad
 Y(a)=h(a,X,V)\quad\text{on }\Omega_0
 \quad\text{simultaneously for all }a\in\mathcal A,
\]
and \(h\) takes values in \([-B_Y,B_Y]\).  Fix \(a\in\mathcal A\).  For bounded Borel functions \(r\) and \(s\), conditional independence and the displayed representation give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_P[r(A)s(Y(a))\mid X]
 &=E_P[r(A)s\{h(a,X,V)\}\mid X]\\
 &=E_P[r(A)\mid X]E_P[s\{h(a,X,V)\}\mid X]\\
 &=E_P[r(A)\mid X]E_P[s(Y(a))\mid X].
 \end{aligned}
\]
This factorization characterizes \(A\perp Y(a)\mid X\).  Since \(a\) was arbitrary, the assertion holds at every dose.

The range of \(h\) and the common event \(\Omega_0\) imply directly that
\[
 \sup_{a\in\mathcal A}|Y(a)|
 =\sup_{a\in\mathcal A}|h(a,X,V)|\le B_Y
\]
on \(\Omega_0\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}compact\text{-}dose}\) gives \(A\in\mathcal A\) almost surely, and \(\mathrm{ass{:}consistency}\) gives \(Y=Y(A)\) almost surely.  Intersecting these three probability-one events yields \(|Y|\le B_Y\) on the same resulting probability-one event.

Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}strata}\) gives \(p_x>0\), so conditional expectations in cell \(x\) are well defined.  Conditional Jensen's inequality applied to \(|Y|\le B_Y\) gives
\[
 |m_x(a)|=|E_P[Y\mid A=a,X=x]|\le B_Y
\]
for \(f_x(a)\,da\)-almost every \(a\).  Because \(q_x=m_xf_x\), it follows that
\[
 |q_x(a)|\le B_Yf_x(a)
\]
for Lebesgue-almost every \(a\): on \(\{f_x>0\}\) a positive-Lebesgue-measure exceptional set would have positive \(f_x(a)\,da\)-measure, while on \(\{f_x=0\}\) the product definition gives \(q_x=0\) almost everywhere.

Finally, \(\mathrm{ass{:}f\text{-}sobolev}\), \(\mathrm{ass{:}q\text{-}sobolev}\), and \(\beta>1/2\) permit the continuous representatives supplied by \(\mathrm{lem{:}sobolev\text{-}modulus\text{-}control}\).  If their pointwise envelope failed at some \(a_0\), continuity of \(|q_x|-B_Yf_x\) would make it positive on a nonempty open interval, contradicting the almost-everywhere inequality.  Thus the envelope holds everywhere for those representatives.  The argument bounds the product \(q_x=m_xf_x\), not an arbitrary pointwise version of \(m_x\), as claimed.

%%% FIELD stmt-model-class-nonempty
Under \(\mathrm{ass{:}primitive\text{-}compatibility}\), both \(\mathcal M\) and \(\mathscr K\) are nonempty.  More explicitly, put
\[
 C_e=\max\left\{\frac{3+3^{q_e}}4,\frac{2+2^{q_e}}3\right\},
 \qquad
 \delta=\left(\frac{M_e}{2C_e}\right)^{1/q_e}.
\]
There is a full causal product law \(P_0\in\mathcal M\) with \(X\) uniform on \(\mathcal X\), conditional density \(f_x=g_0\), a singleton structural latent variable, an identically zero structural response surface, \(Y(a)=Y=0\), independent errors
\[
 Q_1^0=\frac34\delta_{-\delta}+\frac14\delta_{3\delta},
 \qquad
 Q_2^0=\frac23\delta_{-\delta}+\frac13\delta_{2\delta},
\]
and \(S_1=A+U_1\), \(S_2=A+U_2\).  These error laws are centered, asymmetric, and nonidentical.  Its projected tuple
\[
 \vartheta_0=\bigl((|\mathcal X|^{-1},g_0,0)_{x\in\mathcal X},Q_1^0,Q_2^0\bigr)
\]
belongs to \(\mathscr K\).

%%% FIELD proof-model-class-nonempty
The function \(\widetilde g_0\) in \(\mathrm{def{:}reference\text{-}bump}\) is positive on \((-B_A,B_A)\), bounded, and supported on the compact interval \(\mathcal A\).  Hence
\[
 0<Z_0=\int_{\mathbb R}\widetilde g_0(a)\,da<\infty,
\]
so \(g_0=Z_0^{-1}\widetilde g_0\) is a probability density.  The standard one-sided derivatives of \(e^{-1/u}\) vanish to every order as \(u\downarrow0\); applying the chain rule on the two sides of \(B_A^2-a^2\) shows that \(g_0\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)\).  To verify the asserted Sobolev finiteness directly, choose an integer \(m>\beta+1/2\).  Repeated integration by parts gives, for \(|t|\ge1\),
\[
 |\widehat g_0(t)|
 \le |t|^{-m}\|g_0^{(m)}\|_{L^1(\mathbb R)}.
\]
For \(|t|<1\), \(|\widehat g_0(t)|\le1\).  Therefore
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}(1+t^2)^\beta|\widehat g_0(t)|^2\,dt<\infty,
\]
which proves \(R_{f,0}<\infty\).  Because \(I\) is a compact subset of \((-B_A,B_A)\) and \(g_0\) is continuous and strictly positive there, its minimum \(\kappa_0\) on \(I\) is strictly positive.

We now construct the full law.  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}primitive\text{-}compatibility}\) makes \(\mathcal X\) finite and nonempty.  Let \(X\) be uniform on \(\mathcal X\), let \(A\) be independent of \(X\) with density \(g_0\), and take \(\mathcal V\) to be a singleton with \(V\) its constant element.  Set
\[
 h(a,x,V)=0,
 \qquad Y(a)=0\quad(a\in\mathcal A),
 \qquad Y=0.
\]
Independently of \((A,X,V,Y(\cdot),Y)\), draw \(U_1\sim Q_1^0\) and \(U_2\sim Q_2^0\), independently of each other, and set \(S_j=A+U_j\).

Since \(M_e>0\), \(C_e>0\), and \(q_e>1\), one has \(\delta>0\).  Direct calculation gives
\[
 E[U_1]=\frac34(-\delta)+\frac14(3\delta)=0,
 \qquad
 E[U_2]=\frac23(-\delta)+\frac13(2\delta)=0.
\]
The two distributions have unequal probabilities on unequal positive and negative magnitudes, so neither is symmetric about zero; their probability-mass pairs and supports differ, so they are nonidentical.  Their moments are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E|U_1|^{q_e}
 &=\delta^{q_e}\frac{3+3^{q_e}}4
 \le \delta^{q_e}C_e=\frac{M_e}{2},\\
 E|U_2|^{q_e}
 &=\delta^{q_e}\frac{2+2^{q_e}}3
 \le \delta^{q_e}C_e=\frac{M_e}{2}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(\mathrm{ass{:}center\text{-}two}\) and \(\mathrm{ass{:}error\text{-}moment}\) hold, and the product construction proves both error-independence assumptions.

For every \(x\),
\[
 p_x=|\mathcal X|^{-1}\ge p_{\min},
 \qquad f_x=g_0,
 \qquad m_x=0,
 \qquad q_x=0.
\]
The first inequality is primitive compatibility.  The bump calculation and that same assumption give
\[
 \|f_x\|_{H^\beta}=R_{f,0}\le R_f,
 \qquad
 \inf_{a\in I}f_x(a)=\kappa_0\ge\kappa,
 \qquad
 \|q_x\|_{H^\beta}=0\le R_q.
\]
The density is supported on \(\mathcal A\), so \(P(A\in\mathcal A)=1\).  The zero response surface is jointly measurable, lies in \([-B_Y,B_Y]\), and has \(V\perp A\mid X\); consistency holds because \(Y=Y(A)=0\), and \(a\mapsto E[Y(a)\mid X=x]=0\) is continuous on \(I\).  Consequently every retained member condition in \(\mathrm{def{:}model\text{-}class}\) holds and \(P_0\in\mathcal M\).

For the projected tuple \(\vartheta_0\), the mass, density, support, Sobolev, and overlap clauses were just verified.  The marked functions are zero and hence satisfy both the \(H^\beta\) radius and \(|q_x|\le B_Yf_x\).  The two error laws satisfy the stronger moment bound \(M_e/2\le M_e\), and \(Q_2^0\) is centered.  Thus every clause in \(\mathrm{def{:}exact\text{-}projected\text{-}class}\) holds, so \(\vartheta_0\in\mathscr K\).  This proves both nonemptiness assertions by explicit witnesses.

%%% FIELD stmt-factorization
For every \(P\in\mathcal M\), every \(x\in\mathcal X\), and all real \(s,t\), \(F_x(s,t)=\widehat f_x(s+t)\phi_1(s)\phi_2(t)\) and \(G_x(s,t)=\widehat q_x(s+t)\phi_1(s)\phi_2(t)\).

%%% FIELD proof-factorization
Fix \(P\in\mathcal M\), \(x\in\mathcal X\), and \(s,t\in\mathbb R\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}strata}\) gives \(p_x>0\), so conditioning on \(X=x\) is well defined.  The replicate definitions give
\[
 e^{i(sS_1+tS_2)}=e^{i(s+t)A}e^{isU_1}e^{itU_2}.
\]
By \(\mathrm{ass{:}error\text{-}latent\text{-}independence}\) and \(\mathrm{ass{:}error\text{-}coordinate\text{-}independence}\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 F_x(s,t)
 &=E_P[e^{i(s+t)A}\mid X=x]E_P[e^{isU_1}]E_P[e^{itU_2}]\\
 &=\left\{\int_{\mathcal A}e^{i(s+t)a}f_x(a)\,da\right\}
   \phi_1(s)\phi_2(t)\\
 &=\widehat f_x(s+t)\phi_1(s)\phi_2(t).
 \end{aligned}
\]
The integration domain is \(\mathcal A\) by \(\mathrm{ass{:}compact\text{-}dose}\).

For the marked channel, \(\mathrm{lem{:}structural\text{-}response\text{-}implications}\), using the retained structural response condition and consistency, gives \(|Y|\le B_Y\) almost surely.  Thus the marked expectation is integrable, and the same independence assumptions leave the mark with the latent variables:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 G_x(s,t)
 &=E_P[Ye^{i(s+t)A}\mid X=x]E_P[e^{isU_1}]E_P[e^{itU_2}]\\
 &=\left\{\int_{\mathcal A}e^{i(s+t)a}
 E_P[Y\mid A=a,X=x]f_x(a)\,da\right\}
 \phi_1(s)\phi_2(t)\\
 &=\widehat q_x(s+t)\phi_1(s)\phi_2(t),
 \end{aligned}
\]
where the last equality uses \(q_x=m_xf_x\).  This proves both identities.

%%% FIELD stmt-marked-cocycle-id
The map \(P\mapsto P_{\mathrm{obs}}\) is injective for \(\{(f_x,q_x):x\in\mathcal X\}\) over \(\mathcal M\). On a common neighborhood of zero, \(\left.\partial_t\log F_x(u,t)\right|_{t=0}=\widehat f_x'(u)/\widehat f_x(u)+\phi_2'(0)=\widehat f_x'(u)/\widehat f_x(u)\), because \(\phi_2(0)=1\) and \(\phi_2'(0)=iE_P[U_2]=0\). Together with \(\widehat f_x(0)=1\), this derivative identity uniquely fixes the latent translation without a normalization on \(U_1\). Moreover, \(\widehat q_x(u)=\widehat f_x(u)G_x(u,0)/F_x(u,0)\). Compact support makes both recovered transforms entire, so the local formulas determine \(f_x\) and \(q_x\) globally even when either error transform has an open zero set away from zero.

%%% FIELD proof-marked-cocycle-id
The explicit construction in \(\mathrm{lem{:}model\text{-}class\text{-}nonempty}\) first shows that the claim is about an inhabited model.  Let \(P^{(0)},P^{(1)}\in\mathcal M\) induce the same observed-data law, and fix \(x\in\mathcal X\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}strata}\) gives \(p_x\ge p_{\min}>0\), so the common observed law determines \(F_x\) and \(G_x\).

For either candidate, either \(j\in\{1,2\}\), and every real \(v\), the inequalities \(|e^{iz}-1|\le|z|\), H\"older's inequality, and \(\mathrm{ass{:}error\text{-}moment}\) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |\phi_j(v)|
 &\ge1-|\phi_j(v)-1|\\
 &\ge1-E_P|e^{ivU_j}-1|\\
 &\ge1-|v|E_P|U_j|\\
 &\ge1-|v|\{E_P|U_j|^{q_e}\}^{1/q_e}\\
 &\ge1-|v|M_e^{1/q_e}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus, with
\[
 \nu_*=(2M_e^{1/q_e})^{-1},
 \qquad c_*=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \delta_0=\min\left\{\frac{\nu_*}{2},\frac{1}{4B_A}\right\}>0,
\]
one has \(|\phi_j(v)|\ge c_*\) on \(|v|\le\nu_*\).  In addition, \(f_x\) is a conditional density supported on \(\mathcal A\), so, for \(|w|\le2\delta_0\),
\[
 |\widehat f_x(w)-1|
 \le\int_{\mathcal A}|e^{iwa}-1|f_x(a)\,da
 \le B_A|w|\le\frac12.
\]
Hence \(|\widehat f_x(w)|\ge1/2\).  If \(|u|,|t|\le\delta_0\), \(\mathrm{lem{:}factorization}\) yields
\[
 |F_x(u,t)|
 =|\widehat f_x(u+t)|\,|\phi_1(u)|\,|\phi_2(t)|
 \ge\frac18>0.
\]
This is a moment-derived neighborhood; no global nonvanishing condition is used.

The same H\"older bound gives \(E_P|U_2|<\infty\), and compact dose support gives \(|A|\le B_A\) almost surely.  Dominated differentiation at \(t=0\) is therefore valid.  On the nonvanishing rectangle define the observed logarithmic derivative by
\[
 D_x(u)=
 \frac{\left.\partial_tF_x(u,t)\right|_{t=0}}{F_x(u,0)},
 \qquad |u|\le\delta_0.
\]
Differentiating the factorization gives
\[
 D_x(u)=\frac{\widehat f_x'(u)}{\widehat f_x(u)}
       +\frac{\phi_2'(0)}{\phi_2(0)}.
\]
Characteristic-function normalization gives \(\phi_2(0)=1\), while \(\mathrm{ass{:}center\text{-}two}\) gives \(\phi_2'(0)=iE_P[U_2]=0\).  Thus every candidate obeys
\[
 \widehat f_x'(u)=D_x(u)\widehat f_x(u),
 \qquad \widehat f_x(0)=1,
 \qquad |u|\le\delta_0.
\]
For the two candidates, \(D_x\) is common.  Their transforms are nonzero locally, so the ratio \(R_x(u)=\widehat f_x^{(1)}(u)/\widehat f_x^{(0)}(u)\) satisfies \(R_x'(u)=0\) and \(R_x(0)=1\).  Consequently
\[
 \widehat f_x^{(1)}(u)=\widehat f_x^{(0)}(u),
 \qquad |u|\le\delta_0.
\]
Only \(\phi_2\) was differentiated; no normalization on \(U_1\) was used.

Let \(h=f_x^{(1)}-f_x^{(0)}\).  It belongs to \(L^1(\mathbb R)\) and is supported on \(\mathcal A\).  For every \(z\in\mathbb C\), Tonelli's theorem applies because
\[
 \int_{\mathcal A}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}
 \frac{|za|^k}{k!}|h(a)|\,da
 \le e^{|z|B_A}\|h\|_1<\infty,
\]
and hence
\[
 H(z)=\int_{\mathcal A}e^{iza}h(a)\,da
 =\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(iz)^k}{k!}
   \int_{\mathcal A}a^kh(a)\,da.
\]
Thus \(H\) is entire.  It vanishes on a real neighborhood of zero, so all derivatives at zero vanish; its displayed power series then gives \(H(z)=0\) for all \(z\in\mathbb C\).

At \((u,0)\), the two factorizations and local nonvanishing give, for each candidate,
\[
 \widehat q_x(u)=\widehat f_x(u)\frac{G_x(u,0)}{F_x(u,0)},
 \qquad |u|\le\delta_0.
\]
The right-hand side is common.  Moreover, \(\mathrm{lem{:}structural\text{-}response\text{-}implications}\) gives
\[
 |q_x(a)|\le B_Yf_x(a)\quad\text{almost everywhere},
 \qquad \|q_x\|_1\le B_Y.
\]
Each \(q_x\) is supported on \(\mathcal A\).  Applying the preceding entire power-series argument to \(q_x^{(1)}-q_x^{(0)}\) extends local equality to all complex arguments.

It remains to recover the functions from their real Fourier transforms.  If \(h\) denotes either signal difference, then \(h\in L^1(\mathbb R)\) and \(\widehat h(t)=0\) for every real \(t\).  For \(\epsilon>0\), let \(g_\epsilon(v)=(2\pi\epsilon)^{-1/2}e^{-v^2/(2\epsilon)}\).  The Gaussian Fourier formula and Fubini's theorem give
\[
 (h*g_\epsilon)(a)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}
 e^{-ita}\widehat h(t)e^{-\epsilon t^2/2}\,dt=0.
\]
Also,
\[
 \|h*g_\epsilon-h\|_1
 \le\int_{\mathbb R}g_\epsilon(v)
       \|h(\cdot-v)-h\|_1\,dv\longrightarrow0.
\]
Translation continuity in \(L^1\) follows by approximation with compactly supported step functions, and the limit then follows by splitting the integral at a fixed small \(|v|\) and using concentration of \(g_\epsilon\) at zero.  Hence \(h=0\) almost everywhere.  Finally, \(\mathrm{ass{:}f\text{-}sobolev}\), \(\mathrm{ass{:}q\text{-}sobolev}\), and \(\mathrm{lem{:}sobolev\text{-}modulus\text{-}control}\) supply continuous representatives because \(\beta>1/2\).  Two continuous functions equal almost everywhere only if they agree everywhere.  Since \(x\) was arbitrary, the observed law uniquely determines every \(f_x\) and \(q_x\), even if an error transform vanishes on an open set away from zero.

%%% FIELD stmt-analytic-sieve-stability
For all sufficiently large \(n\), \(\inf_{P\in\mathcal M}P_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\otimes n}(\mathcal E_{n,\alpha})\ge1-\alpha\). On \(\mathcal E_{n,\alpha}\), for every \(P\in\mathcal M\) there is a tuple \(\bar\theta_n\in\Theta_{n,\alpha}\), formed from outer Jackson spline approximants and finite atomic error approximants, such that \(\|\mu_{\bar\theta_n}-\mu\|_{L^\infty(I)}\le b_{\mu,n}\). For every realized sample, the profiled clipping rule gives \(L_n(a)\le U_n(a)\) for every \(a\in I\) and \(0\le W_n\le2B_Y\).

%%% FIELD proof-analytic-sieve-stability
Fix a sufficiently large \(n\).  Conditional on \((X_i)_{i=1}^n\), \(\mathrm{ass{:}iid}\) makes observations in every nonempty cell independent with their law conditional on \(X=x\).  Each real and imaginary part of a probability-channel summand lies in \([-1,1]\).  By \(\mathrm{lem{:}structural\text{-}response\text{-}implications}\), the retained structural response condition and consistency give \(|Y|\le B_Y\), so the marked-channel parts lie in \([-B_Y,B_Y]\).

The bounded-variable exponential-moment calculation in \(\mathrm{lem{:}mass\text{-}confidence}\), applied to real and imaginary parts, gives, when \(n_x>\lambda_n\),
\[
 \Pr\left(
 |\widehat F_{x,n}(s,t)-F_x(s,t)|>2\sqrt{\lambda_n/n_x}
 \mathrel{\Big|}(X_i)_{i=1}^n\right)\le4e^{-\lambda_n},
\]
and
\[
 \Pr\left(
 |\widehat G_{x,n}(s,t)-G_x(s,t)|>2B_Y\sqrt{\lambda_n/n_x}
 \mathrel{\Big|}(X_i)_{i=1}^n\right)\le4e^{-\lambda_n}.
\]
If \(n_x\le\lambda_n\), channel boundedness and the empty-cell convention give the defining inequalities deterministically because \(\rho^F_{x,n}=2\) and \(\rho^G_{x,n}=2B_Y\).  A conditional union bound over both channels, all cells, and all grid points has failure probability at most
\[
 8|\mathcal X||\mathcal D_n|e^{-\lambda_n}=\frac{\alpha}{6}.
\]
Combining this with the mass-region failure probability \(\alpha/3\), and then integrating over the labels, yields uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal M\)
\[
 P_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\otimes n}(\mathcal E_{n,\alpha})
 \ge1-\frac\alpha3-\frac\alpha6\ge1-\alpha.
\]

On \(\mathcal E_{n,\alpha}\), apply \(\mathrm{lem{:}outer\text{-}spline\text{-}embedding}\) in every cell and \(\mathrm{lem{:}atomic\text{-}grid\text{-}embedding}\) to both error laws, and put
\[
 \bar\theta_n=
 \bigl((p_x,\widetilde f_{x,n},\widetilde q_{x,n})_{x\in\mathcal X},
       \bar\phi_{1,n},\bar\phi_{2,n}\bigr).
\]
The confidence event places the mass vector in \(\mathcal P_{n,\alpha}\).  At \((s,t)\in\mathcal D_n\), both coordinates belong to \(\Gamma_n\).  Characteristic functions have modulus at most one; also \(|\widehat f_x(u)|\le1\), while \(\mathrm{lem{:}structural\text{-}response\text{-}implications}\) gives \(|\widehat q_x(u)|\le\|q_x\|_1\le B_Y\).  Uniform approximation on the interval of length \(2B_A\) gives
\[
 |\widehat{\widetilde f}_{x,n}(u)-\widehat f_x(u)|\le2B_Aa_{f,n},
 \qquad
 |\widehat{\widetilde q}_{x,n}(u)-\widehat q_x(u)|\le2B_Aa_{q,n}.
\]
The atomic embedding gives
\[
 |\bar\phi_{1,n}(s)\bar\phi_{2,n}(t)-\phi_1(s)\phi_2(t)|
 \le2\tau_{e,n}.
\]
Using \(\mathrm{lem{:}factorization}\) and the definitions of the bias allowances,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |\widehat{\widetilde f}_{x,n}(s+t)
   \bar\phi_{1,n}(s)\bar\phi_{2,n}(t)-F_x(s,t)|
