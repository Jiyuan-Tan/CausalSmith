%%% FIELD jackson-statement
For every \(\beta>1/2\), let \(d=\lceil\beta\rceil+1\).  For every
\(g\in H^\beta(\mathbb R)\), the uniform order-\(d\) B-spline spaces on
\(\mathcal A\) satisfy, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 \inf_{s\in\operatorname{span}\{\mathsf B_{k,K_n}:1\le k\le K_n+d\}}
 \|g-s\|_{L^\infty(\mathcal A)}
 \le C_{\beta,\mathcal A}K_n^{1/2-\beta}
       \|g\|_{H^\beta(\mathbb R)}.
\]
The constant \(C_{\beta,\mathcal A}<\infty\) is independent of \(g\),
\(n\), and the spline coefficients.
%%% FIELD jackson-proof
Put \(h=2B_A/K_n\).  Since \(K_n\to\infty\) by
\(\mathrm{def{:}upper\text{-}tuning\text{-}grid}\), it suffices to consider
\(0<h\le1\).  Use the Fourier convention
\[
 \widehat g(t)=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{ita}g(a)\,da,
 \qquad
 g(a)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-ita}\widehat g(t)\,dt.
\]
The inversion formula holds for the continuous representative of \(g\):
because \(\beta>1/2\), Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}|\widehat g(t)|\,dt
 \le
 \left\{\int_{\mathbb R}(1+t^2)^{-\beta}\,dt\right\}^{1/2}
 \left\{\int_{\mathbb R}(1+t^2)^\beta
                    |\widehat g(t)|^2\,dt\right\}^{1/2}<\infty.       \tag{1}
\]
Define
\[
 g_h(a)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{|t|\le h^{-1}}
          e^{-ita}\widehat g(t)\,dt.
\]
Another application of Cauchy--Schwarz, followed by
\(\int_{h^{-1}}^\infty t^{-2\beta}\,dt
=(2\beta-1)^{-1}h^{2\beta-1}\), yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \|g-g_h\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R)}
 &\le \frac1{2\pi}
 \left\{\int_{|t|>h^{-1}}(1+t^2)^{-\beta}\,dt\right\}^{1/2}
 \left\{\int_{\mathbb R}(1+t^2)^\beta
                    |\widehat g(t)|^2\,dt\right\}^{1/2}\\
 &\le C_1(\beta)h^{\beta-1/2}\|g\|_{H^\beta(\mathbb R)}.            \tag{2}
 \end{aligned}
\]
The division by \(2\beta-1\) is valid exactly under the declared condition
\(\beta>1/2\).

We next establish the smooth-function approximation used below.  Fix the
clamped normalized B-spline basis specified in
\(\mathrm{def{:}signal\text{-}sieves}\).  Its elements are nonnegative,
form a partition of unity on \(\mathcal A\), and span the order-\(d\) spline
space on the uniform partition.  In particular, that space contains every
polynomial of degree at most \(d-1\).  For each basis index \(k\), choose a
point \(\xi_k\) in the support of \(\mathsf B_{k,K_n}\).  If
\(v\in C^d(\mathbb R)\), let \(\Lambda_{k,h}(v)\) be the \(k\)-th B-spline
coefficient of the degree-\((d-1)\) Taylor polynomial of \(v\) at \(\xi_k\).
After translation and dilation by \(h\), there is one interior coefficient
pattern and only finitely many boundary patterns.  Finite-dimensional norm
equivalence for these finitely many patterns gives a constant \(C_d\),
independent of \(h\), \(k\), and \(v\), such that
\[
 |\Lambda_{k,h}(v)|
 \le C_d\max_{0\le j\le d-1}h^j|v^{(j)}(\xi_k)|.                    \tag{3}
\]
Define
\[
 (J_hv)(a)=\sum_{k=1}^{K_n+d}
              \Lambda_{k,h}(v)\mathsf B_{k,K_n}(a).
\]
If \(p\) is a polynomial of degree at most \(d-1\), its Taylor polynomial
of that order at every \(\xi_k\) is \(p\) itself.  Hence \(J_hp=p\).
For fixed \(a\in\mathcal A\), let \(p_a\) be the degree-\((d-1)\) Taylor
polynomial of \(v\) at \(a\).  Whenever
\(\mathsf B_{k,K_n}(a)\ne0\), locality of the order-\(d\) basis gives
\(|\xi_k-a|\le C_dh\).  Taylor's formula with integral remainder then gives,
for \(0\le j\le d-1\),
\[
 h^j|(v-p_a)^{(j)}(\xi_k)|
 \le C_dh^d\|v^{(d)}\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R)}.                     \tag{4}
\]
Since \((v-p_a)(a)=0\), polynomial reproduction, (3), (4), nonnegativity,
and the partition of unity imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |v(a)-J_hv(a)|
 &=|J_h(v-p_a)(a)|\\
 &\le \sum_k\mathsf B_{k,K_n}(a)
          |\Lambda_{k,h}(v-p_a)|\\
 &\le C_dh^d\|v^{(d)}\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R)}.                    \tag{5}
 \end{aligned}
\]

Apply (5) to \(v=g_h\).  Differentiation under the compact-frequency
integral and Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \|g_h^{(d)}\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R)}
 &\le \frac1{2\pi}
 \left\{\int_{|t|\le h^{-1}}|t|^{2d}(1+t^2)^{-\beta}\,dt\right\}^{1/2}
 \left\{\int_{\mathbb R}(1+t^2)^\beta
                    |\widehat g(t)|^2\,dt\right\}^{1/2}\\
 &\le C_2(\beta,d)h^{\beta-d-1/2}
       \|g\|_{H^\beta(\mathbb R)}.                                \tag{6}
 \end{aligned}
\]
For the last inequality, split the first integral at one and use
\(d=\lceil\beta\rceil+1>\beta\), so
\(\int_1^{h^{-1}}t^{2d-2\beta}\,dt
\le C(\beta,d)h^{-(2d-2\beta+1)}\).  Combining (2), (5), and (6) gives
the spline \(s=J_hg_h\) with
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \|g-s\|_{L^\infty(\mathcal A)}
 &\le C_3(\beta,d)h^{\beta-1/2}\|g\|_{H^\beta(\mathbb R)}\\
 &=C_3(\beta,d)(2B_A)^{\beta-1/2}K_n^{1/2-\beta}
       \|g\|_{H^\beta(\mathbb R)}.                                \tag{7}
 \end{aligned}
\]
The function \(s\) lies in the span displayed in the statement by the basis
definition.  Taking the fixed constant \(C_{\beta,\mathcal A}\) no smaller
than the constant in (7) proves the claimed uniform Jackson inequality.
%%% FIELD derived-modulus-statement
Let \(j\in\{1,2\}\), and let \(Q_j\) be a Borel probability law on
\(\mathbb R\) such that
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}|u|^{q_e}\,dQ_j(u)\le M_e,
 \qquad
 \phi_{Q_j}(t)=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{itu}\,dQ_j(u).
\]
For \(q_e>1\),
\[
 \int_{mathbb R}|u|\,dQ_j(u)\le M_e^{1/q_e},
 \qquad
 |\phi_{Q_j}(s)-\phi_{Q_j}(t)|
 \le M_e^{1/q_e}|s-t|,
\]
and
\[
 |\phi_{Q_j}(t)|\ge1-|t|M_e^{1/q_e}.
\]
Consequently, for \(\nu_*=(2M_e^{1/q_e})^{-1}\) and \(c_*=1/2\),
\[
 \inf_{|t|\le\nu_*}|\phi_{Q_j}(t)|\ge c_*.
\]
%%% FIELD derived-modulus-proof
Because \(q_e>1\), H\"older's inequality with respect to the probability
measure \(Q_j\) gives
\[
 \int_{mathbb R}|u|\,dQ_j(u)
 \le
 \left\{\int_{mathbb R}|u|^{q_e}\,dQ_j(u)\right\}^{1/q_e}
 \left\{\int_{mathbb R}1\,dQ_j(u)\right\}^{1-1/q_e}
 \le M_e^{1/q_e}.                                                   \tag{1}
\]
For real \(v\), the fundamental theorem of calculus applied to
\(r\mapsto e^{irv}\) gives \(|e^{iv}-1|\le|v|\).  Hence, for all real
\(s,t\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |\phi_{Q_j}(s)-\phi_{Q_j}(t)|
 &\le\int_{\mathbb R}|e^{isu}-e^{itu}|\,dQ_j(u)\\
 &\le|s-t|\int_{\mathbb R}|u|\,dQ_j(u)
 \le M_e^{1/q_e}|s-t|.                                             \tag{2}
 \end{aligned}
\]
Since \(\phi_{Q_j}(0)=1\), the reverse triangle inequality and (2) yield
\[
 |\phi_{Q_j}(t)|
 \ge1-|\phi_{Q_j}(t)-\phi_{Q_j}(0)|
 \ge1-|t|M_e^{1/q_e}.                                              \tag{3}
\]
The positivity of \(M_e\) makes
\(\nu_*=(2M_e^{1/q_e})^{-1}>0\).  If \(|t|\le\nu_*\), then (3) gives
\[
 |\phi_{Q_j}(t)|\ge1-\nu_*M_e^{1/q_e}=\frac12=c_*.
\]
This proves all assertions.

 t\longmapsto\sum_{\ell=1}^{M_n}w_{j\ell}e^{itu_{j\ell}}:

%%% FIELD outer-statement
Under the relaxed outer-sieve construction in
\(\mathrm{def{:}signal\text{-}sieves}\), for every \(P\in\mathcal M\),
every \(x\in\mathcal X\), and all sufficiently large \(n\), there are
splines \(\widetilde f_{x,n}\in\mathscr F_n\) and
\(\widetilde q_{x,n}\in\mathscr Q_n(\widetilde f_{x,n})\) such that
\[
 \|\widetilde f_{x,n}-f_x\|_{L^\infty(\mathcal A)}\le a_{f,n},
 \qquad
 \|\widetilde q_{x,n}-q_x\|_{L^\infty(\mathcal A)}\le a_{q,n}.
\]
With the true masses \((p_x)_{x\in\mathcal X}\), their profiled ADRF
satisfies
\[
 \sup_{a\in I}\left|
 \sum_{x\in\mathcal X}p_x
 \frac{\widetilde q_{x,n}(a)}{\widetilde f_{x,n}(a)}-\mu(a)
 \right|
 \le2\kappa^{-1}(a_{q,n}+B_Ya_{f,n})
 \le b_{\mu,n}.
\]
%%% FIELD outer-proof
Fix \(P\in\mathcal M\) and \(x\in\mathcal X\).  Apply
\(\mathrm{lem{:}sobolev\text{-}spline\text{-}jackson}\) first to \(f_x\)
and then to \(q_x\).  The bounds in \(\mathrm{ass{:}f\text{-}sobolev}\)
and \(\mathrm{ass{:}q\text{-}sobolev}\), together with the definitions of
\(a_{f,n}\) and \(a_{q,n}\) in \(\mathrm{def{:}ecf\text{-}radii}\), give
order-\(d\) splines \(\widetilde f_{x,n}\) and
\(\widetilde q_{x,n}\) such that
\[
 \|\widetilde f_{x,n}-f_x\|_{L^\infty(\mathcal A)}
 \le C_{\beta,\mathcal A}K_n^{1/2-\beta}
       \|f_x\|_{H^\beta(\mathbb R)}
 \le a_{f,n},                                                       \tag{1}
\]
and
\[
 \|\widetilde q_{x,n}-q_x\|_{L^\infty(\mathcal A)}
 \le C_{\beta,\mathcal A}K_n^{1/2-\beta}
       \|q_x\|_{H^\beta(\mathbb R)}
 \le a_{q,n}.                                                       \tag{2}
\]
The corrected range \(\beta>1/2\) gives \(1/2-\beta<0\).  Since
\(K_n\to\infty\), it follows that \(a_{f,n}\to0\), and therefore
\[
 a_{f,n}<\kappa/2                                                     \tag{3}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\).

By definition, \(f_x\) is a nonnegative conditional density.  Assumption
\(\mathrm{ass{:}compact\text{-}dose}\) gives
\(\int_{\mathcal A}f_x(a)\,da=1\).  Equations (1), (3), and
\(\mathrm{ass{:}denominator}\) therefore imply
\[
 \widetilde f_{x,n}(a)\ge-a_{f,n}\quad(a\in\mathcal A),            \tag{4}
\]
\[
 \widetilde f_{x,n}(a)\ge\kappa-a_{f,n}
 =\max\{\kappa/2,\kappa-a_{f,n}\}\quad(a\in I),                   \tag{5}
\]
and
\[
 \left|\int_{\mathcal A}\widetilde f_{x,n}(a)\,da-1\right|
 \le\int_{\mathcal A}|\widetilde f_{x,n}(a)-f_x(a)|\,da
 \le2B_Aa_{f,n}.                                                    \tag{6}
\]

We next verify the marked envelope.  The common-event representation in
\(\mathrm{ass{:}structural\text{-}response\text{-}surface}\) takes values
in \([-B_Y,B_Y]\); with \(\mathrm{ass{:}consistency}\), this gives
\(|Y|\le B_Y\) almost surely.  Conditional Jensen's inequality hence gives
\[
 |m_x(a)|=|E_P[Y\mid A=a,X=x]|\le B_Y
 \quad\text{for }f_x(a)\,da\text{-almost every }a.                  \tag{7}
\]
Because \(q_x=m_xf_x\), equation (7) implies
\(|q_x|\le B_Yf_x\) Lebesgue-almost everywhere.  This inequality has a
pointwise version under the corrected smoothness range.  Indeed, for either
\(g=f_x\) or \(g=q_x\), the corresponding Sobolev assumption and
\(\beta>1/2\) give
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}|\widehat g(t)|\,dt
 \le
 \left\{\int_{\mathbb R}(1+t^2)^{-\beta}\,dt\right\}^{1/2}
 \left\{\int_{\mathbb R}(1+t^2)^\beta
                    |\widehat g(t)|^2\,dt\right\}^{1/2}<\infty.     \tag{8}
\]
Fourier inversion and dominated convergence supply continuous
representatives of \(f_x\) and \(q_x\).  If
\(|q_x(a_0)|>B_Yf_x(a_0)\) at some \(a_0\), continuity would preserve the
strict inequality on a nonempty interval, contradicting its almost-everywhere
validity.  Hence
\[
 |q_x(a)|\le B_Yf_x(a)\quad\text{for every }a\in\mathcal A.         \tag{9}
\]
Equations (1), (2), and (9) yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |\widetilde q_{x,n}(a)|
 &\le|q_x(a)|+a_{q,n}\\
 &\le B_Y\widetilde f_{x,n}(a)+a_{q,n}+B_Ya_{f,n},
 \qquad a\in\mathcal A.                                           \tag{10}
 \end{aligned}
\]
Equations (4)--(6) are the constraints defining \(\mathscr F_n\), and (10)
is the constraint defining
\(\mathscr Q_n(\widetilde f_{x,n})\).  Thus
\[
 \widetilde f_{x,n}\in\mathscr F_n,
 \qquad
 \widetilde q_{x,n}\in\mathscr Q_n(\widetilde f_{x,n}).           \tag{11}
\]

It remains to compare the profiled quotient with the causal estimand.  The
already established \(\mathrm{thm{:}adrf\text{-}id}\) gives
\[
 \mu(a)=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}p_x\frac{q_x(a)}{f_x(a)},
 \qquad a\in I.                                                     \tag{12}
\]
For \(a\in I\), equations (1), (2), and (5), assumption
\(\mathrm{ass{:}denominator}\), and (9) show that every denominator below
is positive and
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \left|\frac{\widetilde q_{x,n}(a)}{\widetilde f_{x,n}(a)}
       -\frac{q_x(a)}{f_x(a)}\right|
 &\le
 \frac{|\widetilde q_{x,n}(a)-q_x(a)|}{\widetilde f_{x,n}(a)}
 +\frac{|q_x(a)|\,|\widetilde f_{x,n}(a)-f_x(a)|}
        {f_x(a)\widetilde f_{x,n}(a)}\\
 &\le\frac{a_{q,n}+B_Ya_{f,n}}{\kappa-a_{f,n}}\\
 &\le2\kappa^{-1}(a_{q,n}+B_Ya_{f,n}),                            \tag{13}
 \end{aligned}
\]
where the last division uses \(\kappa-a_{f,n}\ge\kappa/2>0\) from (3).
The masses are nonnegative and sum to one, so summing (13) with weights
\(p_x\), using (12), and taking the supremum over \(I\) gives
\[
 \sup_{a\in I}\left|
 \sum_{x\in\mathcal X}p_x
 \frac{\widetilde q_{x,n}(a)}{\widetilde f_{x,n}(a)}-\mu(a)
 \right|
 \le2\kappa^{-1}(a_{q,n}+B_Ya_{f,n}).                              \tag{14}
\]
Finally, \(\mathrm{def{:}ecf\text{-}radii}\) defines
\(b_{\mu,n}=4\kappa^{-1}(a_{q,n}+B_Ya_{f,n})\).  Its summands are
nonnegative, so the right-hand side of (14) is at most \(b_{\mu,n}\).

Fix \(P\in\mathcal M\), \(j\in\{1,2\}\), and a sufficiently large \(n\).
Define the clipped error
\[
 U_j^H=\max\{-H_n,\min\{U_j,H_n\}\}.
\]
On \(\{|U_j|>H_n\}\), one has
\(1\le|U_j|^{q_e}H_n^{-q_e}\).  Thus
\(\mathrm{ass{:}error\text{-}moment}\) gives
\[
 P(|U_j|>H_n)
 \le H_n^{-q_e}E_P|U_j|^{q_e}
 \le M_eH_n^{-q_e}.                                                \tag{1}
\]
Since \(U_j^H=U_j\) off this event and two unit-modulus complex numbers are
at distance at most two, for every real \(t\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |E_Pe^{itU_j^H}-\phi_j(t)|
 &\le E_P|e^{itU_j^H}-e^{itU_j}|\\
 &\le2P(|U_j|>H_n)
 \le2M_eH_n^{-q_e}=\tau_{e,n}.                                    \tag{2}
 \end{aligned}
\]
Clipping does not increase absolute value, so
\[
 E_P|U_j^H|^{q_e}\le E_P|U_j|^{q_e}\le M_e.                       \tag{3}
\]
For \(j=2\), the first moment exists by
\(\mathrm{lem{:}moment\text{-}derived\text{-}local\text{-}modulus}\).
Consequently the centering identity in \(\mathrm{ass{:}center\text{-}two}\)
may be subtracted from the clipped mean.  Since \(q_e>1\),
\(|U_2|\le H_n^{1-q_e}|U_2|^{q_e}\) on \(\{|U_2|>H_n\}\), and hence
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |E_PU_2^H|
 &=|E_P(U_2^H-U_2)|\\
 &\le E_P\{|U_2|\mathbf1(|U_2|>H_n)\}\\
 &\le H_n^{1-q_e}E_P|U_2|^{q_e}
 \le M_eH_n^{1-q_e}.                                               \tag{4}
 \end{aligned}
\]
This is the step that requires the strict threshold \(q_e>1\); it also
shows that the centering tolerance tends to zero because \(H_n\to\infty\).

We next replace the law of \(U_j^H\), supported on \([-H_n,H_n]\), by an
atomic law preserving the grid values and the two displayed moments.  Put
\[
 D=2|\Gamma_n|+2
\]
and define the continuous map
\[
 v(u)=\left(
 (\operatorname{Re}e^{itu},\operatorname{Im}e^{itu})_{t\in\Gamma_n},
 u,|u|^{q_e}\right)\in\mathbb R^D.                                \tag{5}
\]
Finite simple-function approximations to the law of \(U_j^H\) have
\(v\)-expectations in the convex hull of the compact set
\(v([-H_n,H_n])\), and uniform continuity of \(v\) makes these expectations
converge to \(E_Pv(U_j^H)\).  Thus this vector lies in the closure of the
convex hull.

same time.  If a finite convex representation uses more than \(D+1\)

\((1,v(u_r))\in\mathbb R^{D+1}\) are linearly dependent.  Hence there are
real coefficients \(a_r\), not all zero, such that

 \sum_ra_r=0,
 \qquad
 \sum_ra_rv(u_r)=0.                                                \tag{6}
\]
After changing all signs if necessary, some \(a_r>0\).  For weights \(w_r\),
put
\[
 c=\min_{r:a_r>0}\frac{w_r}{a_r}>0,
 \qquad w'_r=w_r-ca_r.
\]
Equation (6) shows that the new weights have the same sum and barycenter,

\[
 D+1=2|\Gamma_n|+3\le M_n                                         \tag{7}

locations.  It follows that the entire convex hull is the image of the
compact product of the \(D\)-simplex and \(D+1\) copies of

and closed.  The preceding limit argument proves that \(E_Pv(U_j^H)\) is in
that convex hull, and (7) provides a representation using at most \(D+1\)
locations.

Let \(m=|\mathcal X|\) and
\[
 e_{p,n}=\left\{\frac{\log(6m/\alpha)}{2n}\right\}^{1/2}.
\]
We first establish the simultaneous mass event.  If \(Z\) is supported on
\([0,1]\), define
\(\psi(\lambda)=\log E\exp\{\lambda(Z-EZ)\}\).  Boundedness permits
differentiation under exponential tilting, and
\[
 \psi''(\lambda)=\operatorname{Var}_{\lambda}(Z)
 \le E_{\lambda}[(Z-1/2)^2]\le\frac14.
\]
Since \(\psi(0)=\psi'(0)=0\), two integrations give
\[
 \log E\exp\{\lambda(Z-EZ)\}\le\frac{\lambda^2}{8},
 \qquad \lambda\in\mathbb R.                                     \tag{1}
\]
For fixed \(x\in\mathcal X\), assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}iid}\) makes
\(\widehat p_x=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf1\{X_i=x\}\) the average of
independent Bernoulli variables with mean \(p_x\).  Exponential Markov
inequality applied to (1), with the upper-tail exponent minimized at
\(\lambda=4e_{p,n}\), and the analogous lower-tail calculation give
\[
 P_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\otimes n}
 (|\widehat p_x-p_x|>e_{p,n})
 \le2e^{-2ne_{p,n}^2}=\frac{\alpha}{3m}.                           \tag{2}
\]
The union bound over the finite set \(\mathcal X\) yields, uniformly over
\(P\in\mathcal M\),
\[
 P_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\otimes n}
 \left(\max_{x\in\mathcal X}|\widehat p_x-p_x|\le e_{p,n}\right)
 \ge1-\frac\alpha3.                                                \tag{3}
\]
Every true mass vector is nonnegative and sums to one, so
\(\mathrm{def{:}mass\text{-}confidence\text{-}region}\) identifies the
event in (3) with
\(\{(p_x)_{x\in\mathcal X}\in\mathcal P_{n,\alpha}\}\).  The asserted
coordinate bound is part of the same definition.

For regular cell counts, let \(N\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,p)\).  For
\(t>0\), exponential Markov inequality and \(1+v\le e^v\) imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Pr(N\le np/2)
 &\le e^{tnp/2}E[e^{-tN}]\\
 &=e^{tnp/2}(1-p+pe^{-t})^n\\
 &\le\exp\{tnp/2+np(e^{-t}-1)\}.
 \end{aligned}                                                       \tag{4}
\]
Taking \(t=\log2\) and using \((1-\log2)/2\ge1/8\) gives
\[
 \Pr(N\le np/2)\le e^{-np(1-\log2)/2}\le e^{-np/8}.              \tag{5}
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}strata}\) gives \(p_x\ge p_{\min}\), whence
\(\{n_x<np_{\min}/2\}\subseteq\{n_x<np_x/2\}\).  A union bound in (5)
therefore yields
\[
 P_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\otimes n}
 \left(\min_{x\in\mathcal X}n_x<np_{\min}/2\right)
 \le m e^{-np_{\min}/8}.                                         \tag{6}
\]

It remains to control mass profiling.  Let \(p=(p_x)_x\) and
\(p'=(p'_x)_x\) lie in \(\mathcal P_{n,\alpha}\), and fix
\(f_x\in\mathscr F_n\) and \(q_x\in\mathscr Q_n(f_x)\) in every cell.  Put
\[
 e_n=a_{q,n}+B_Ya_{f,n}.
\]
The continuum constraints in \(\mathrm{def{:}signal\text{-}sieves}\) give,
for \(a\in I\),
\[
 f_x(a)\ge\kappa/2>0,
 \qquad |q_x(a)|\le B_Yf_x(a)+e_n.                                 \tag{7}
\]
Thus division is valid, and the definition
\(b_{\mu,n}=4e_n/\kappa\) gives
\[
 \left|\frac{q_x(a)}{f_x(a)}\right|
 \le B_Y+\frac{e_n}{f_x(a)}
 \le B_Y+\frac{2e_n}{\kappa}
 =B_Y+\frac{b_{\mu,n}}2.                                          \tag{8}
\]
The mass-region definition gives
\(|p_x-p'_x|\le|p_x-\widehat p_x|+|p'_x-\widehat p_x|\le2e_{p,n}\).
Consequently
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sup_{a\in I}\left|
 \sum_{x\in\mathcal X}(p_x-p'_x)\frac{q_x(a)}{f_x(a)}\right|
 &\le\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}|p_x-p'_x|
       \sup_{a\in I}\left|\frac{q_x(a)}{f_x(a)}\right|\\
 &\le2m\left(B_Y+\frac{b_{\mu,n}}2\right)e_{p,n}.                \tag{9}
 \end{aligned}
\]

Finally, put \(\gamma=\beta-1/2\).  The corrected declared range gives
\(\gamma>0\).  The tuning definition gives
\(K_n\sim\log n/\log\log n\), while
\(\mathrm{def{:}ecf\text{-}radii}\) gives
\[
 b_{\mu,n}
 =4\kappa^{-1}C_{\beta,\mathcal A}(R_q+B_YR_f)K_n^{-\gamma}
 =O(r_n).                                                           \tag{10}
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \log\left(\frac{e_{p,n}}{r_n}\right)
 =-\frac12\log n+\gamma\log\log n
   -\gamma\log\log\log n+O(1)\longrightarrow-\infty.             \tag{11}
\]
All class indices, \(m\), and \(\alpha\) are fixed.  Hence
\(e_{p,n}=o(r_n)\), and (10) implies
\[
 2m\left(B_Y+\frac{b_{\mu,n}}2\right)e_{p,n}=o(r_n).              \tag{12}
\]
Equations (3), (6), (9), and (12) prove every assertion.

For all sufficiently large \(n\),
\(\inf_{P\in\mathcal M}P_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\otimes n}
(\mathcal E_{n,\alpha})\ge1-\alpha\).  On \(\mathcal E_{n,\alpha}\),
for every \(P\in\mathcal M\) there is a tuple
\(\bar\theta_n\in\Theta_{n,\alpha}\), formed from outer Jackson spline
approximants and finite atomic error approximants, such that
\(\|\mu_{\bar\theta_n}-\mu\|_{L^\infty(I)}\le b_{\mu,n}\).  For every
realized sample, the profiled clipping rule gives \(L_n(a)\le U_n(a)\) for
every \(a\in I\) and \(0\le W_n\le2B_Y\).
%%% FIELD analytic-proof
Fix a sufficiently large \(n\).  We first establish the simultaneous
confidence event.  Conditional on \((X_i)_{i=1}^n\), assumption
\(\mathrm{ass{:}iid}\) makes the observations in each nonempty cell
independent with the conditional law given \(X=x\).  Each real and imaginary
part of a probability-channel summand lies in \([-1,1]\).  The common-event
response representation in \(\mathrm{def{:}model\text{-}class}\), together
with \(\mathrm{ass{:}consistency}\), gives \(|Y|\le B_Y\) almost surely, so
each real and imaginary part of a marked-channel summand lies in
\([-B_Y,B_Y]\).

Apply the moment-generating-function calculation in
\(\mathrm{lem{:}mass\text{-}confidence}\), after rescaling an interval of
length \(2c\), separately to real and imaginary parts.  Since
\(|z|>u\) implies
\(\max\{|\operatorname{Re}z|,|\operatorname{Im}z|\}>u/\sqrt2\), when
\(n_x>\lambda_n\), for each \((s,t)\in\mathcal D_n\),
\[
 \Pr\left(
 |\widehat F_{x,n}(s,t)-F_x(s,t)|
 >2\sqrt{\lambda_n/n_x}\ \middle|\ (X_i)_{i=1}^n
 \right)\le4e^{-\lambda_n},                                       \tag{1}
\]
and
\[
 \Pr\left(
 |\widehat G_{x,n}(s,t)-G_x(s,t)|
 >2B_Y\sqrt{\lambda_n/n_x}\ \middle|\ (X_i)_{i=1}^n
 \right)\le4e^{-\lambda_n}.                                       \tag{2}
\]
If \(n_x\le\lambda_n\), the empirical-channel definitions, including the
empty-cell convention, and \(|F_x|\le1\), \(|G_x|\le B_Y\) show
deterministically that the deviations are at most
\(\rho^F_{x,n}=2\) and \(\rho^G_{x,n}=2B_Y\).  A conditional union bound
over both channels, every cell, and every grid point has failure probability
at most
\[
 8|\mathcal X||\mathcal D_n|e^{-\lambda_n}=\alpha/6.               \tag{3}
\]
Combining (3) with the mass-region failure probability \(\alpha/3\) from
\(\mathrm{lem{:}mass\text{-}confidence}\), and integrating over the labels,
gives, uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal M\),
\[
 P_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\otimes n}(\mathcal E_{n,\alpha})
 \ge1-\alpha/3-\alpha/6\ge1-\alpha.                               \tag{4}
\]

Suppose now that \(\mathcal E_{n,\alpha}\) occurs.  Apply
\(\mathrm{lem{:}outer\text{-}spline\text{-}embedding}\) in every cell and
\(\mathrm{lem{:}atomic\text{-}grid\text{-}embedding}\) to both error laws.
Define
\[
 \bar\theta_n=
 \bigl((p_x,\widetilde f_{x,n},\widetilde q_{x,n})_{x\in\mathcal X},
       \bar\phi_{1,n},\bar\phi_{2,n}\bigr).
\]
The event puts its mass vector in \(\mathcal P_{n,\alpha}\).  At a point
\((s,t)\in\mathcal D_n\), both \(s\) and \(t\) belong to \(\Gamma_n\).
Characteristic functions have modulus at most one.  Furthermore,
\(|\widehat f_x(u)|\le1\), and equations (7)--(9) of the outer-embedding
proof give \(|q_x|\le B_Yf_x\), so
\(|\widehat q_x(u)|\le\int|q_x|\le B_Y\).  Uniform approximation on the
interval \(\mathcal A\), of length \(2B_A\), gives
\[
 |\widehat{\widetilde f}_{x,n}(u)-\widehat f_x(u)|
 \le2B_Aa_{f,n},
 \qquad
 |\widehat{\widetilde q}_{x,n}(u)-\widehat q_x(u)|
 \le2B_Aa_{q,n}.                                                    \tag{5}
\]
At grid points, the atomic embedding and the triangle inequality give
\[
 |\bar\phi_{1,n}(s)\bar\phi_{2,n}(t)-\phi_1(s)\phi_2(t)|
 \le|\bar\phi_{1,n}(s)-\phi_1(s)|
   +|\bar\phi_{2,n}(t)-\phi_2(t)|
 \le2\tau_{e,n}.                                                    \tag{6}
\]
The established factorization lemma, (5)--(6), and the triangle inequality
therefore imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |\widehat{\widetilde f}_{x,n}(s+t)
   \bar\phi_{1,n}(s)\bar\phi_{2,n}(t)-F_x(s,t)|
 &\le2B_Aa_{f,n}+2\tau_{e,n}\le\delta^F_n,\\
 |\widehat{\widetilde q}_{x,n}(s+t)
   \bar\phi_{1,n}(s)\bar\phi_{2,n}(t)-G_x(s,t)|
 &\le2B_Aa_{q,n}+2B_Y\tau_{e,n}\le\delta^G_n.                    \tag{7}
 \end{aligned}
\]
Combining (7) with the empirical inequalities defining
\(\mathcal E_{n,\alpha}\) verifies every residual constraint in
\(\mathrm{def{:}joint\text{-}feasible\text{-}set}\); the two embedding
lemmas verify the deterministic sieve constraints.  Hence
\[
 \bar\theta_n\in\Theta_{n,\alpha}.                                 \tag{8}
\]
The quotient conclusion of the outer-embedding lemma, with the true mass
vector, gives
\[
 \|\mu_{\bar\theta_n}-\mu\|_{L^\infty(I)}\le b_{\mu,n}.           \tag{9}
\]

Finally, consider an arbitrary realized sample; this step does not use the
confidence event.  Put \(e_n=a_{q,n}+B_Ya_{f,n}\).  For any
\(\theta\in\Theta_{n,\alpha}\), the simplex constraint, the marked outer
envelope, and \(f_x\ge\kappa/2\) on \(I\) yield
\[
 |\mu_\theta(a)|
 \le\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}p_x
 \left(B_Y+\frac{e_n}{f_x(a)}\right)
 \le B_Y+\frac{2e_n}{\kappa}
 =B_Y+\frac{b_{\mu,n}}2.                                          \tag{10}
\]
If the feasible set is nonempty, put
\(\ell(a)=\inf_{\theta\in\Theta_{n,\alpha}}\mu_\theta(a)\) and
\(u(a)=\sup_{\theta\in\Theta_{n,\alpha}}\mu_\theta(a)\).  From (10) and
\(\ell(a)\le u(a)\),
\[
 \ell(a)-b_{\mu,n}\le B_Y,
 \quad -B_Y\le u(a)+b_{\mu,n},
 \quad \ell(a)-b_{\mu,n}\le u(a)+b_{\mu,n}.                      \tag{11}
\]
Together with \(-B_Y\le B_Y\), these pairwise inequalities imply
\[
 \max\{-B_Y,\ell(a)-b_{\mu,n}\}
 \le\min\{B_Y,u(a)+b_{\mu,n}\}.
\]
Both endpoints lie in \([-B_Y,B_Y]\).  If the feasible set is empty, both
endpoints are zero by \(\mathrm{def{:}profiled\text{-}band}\).  Thus, for
every sample,
\[
 L_n(a)\le U_n(a)\quad(a\in I),
 \qquad 0\le W_n\le2B_Y.                                          \tag{12}
\]
Equations (4), (8), (9), and (12) prove the stated concentration,
outer-embedding, ADRF-approximation, and clipping conclusions.  No inverse
diameter or quantitative shrinking-width assertion is used.
