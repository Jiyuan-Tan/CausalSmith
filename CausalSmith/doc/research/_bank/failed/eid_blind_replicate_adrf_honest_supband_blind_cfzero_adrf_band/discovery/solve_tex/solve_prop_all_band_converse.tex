Assume the affirmative resolution stated in the hypothesis.  Consequently, there are constants \(\eta\in(0,1-2\alpha)\), \(c_{\mathrm{sep}}>0\), and \(n_0<\infty\) such that, for every \(n\ge n_0\), the two full-data laws \(P_{0,n},P_{1,n}\) belong to \(\mathcal M\) and, on defining their \(n\)-sample observed laws by
\[
Q_{j,n}:=((P_{j,n})_{\mathrm{obs}})^{\otimes n},\qquad j\in\{0,1\},
\]
one has
\[
\operatorname{TV}(Q_{0,n},Q_{1,n})\le \eta,
\qquad
\Delta_n:=\|\mu_{1,n}-\mu_{0,n}\|_{L^\infty(I)}
\ge c_{\mathrm{sep}}K_n^{1/2-\beta}.
\tag{1}
\]
These are exactly the membership, contiguity, and separation conclusions in the affirmative resolution; the i.i.d. sampling assumption identifies \(Q_{j,n}\) as the relevant sample law.

Fix \(n\ge n_0\) and an arbitrary band \(\mathcal C_n=(L_n,U_n)\in\mathfrak B_{n,\alpha}\).  For \(j\in\{0,1\}\), let
\[
H_{j,n}:=\bigl\{L_n(a)\le \mu_{j,n}(a)\le U_n(a)
                  \text{ for every }a\in I\bigr\}.
\tag{2}
\]
These are the coverage events appearing in the definition of \(\mathfrak B_{n,\alpha}\).  Since \(P_{j,n}\in\mathcal M\), uniform honesty gives
\[
Q_{j,n}(H_{j,n})\ge 1-\alpha,
\qquad j\in\{0,1\}.
\tag{3}
\]
By the defining variational inequality for total variation, \(Q_{0,n}(E)\ge Q_{1,n}(E)-\operatorname{TV}(Q_{0,n},Q_{1,n})\) for every measurable event \(E\).  Applying this with \(E=H_{1,n}\), and then using (1) and (3), yields
\[
Q_{0,n}(H_{1,n})
\ge Q_{1,n}(H_{1,n})-\operatorname{TV}(Q_{0,n},Q_{1,n})
\ge 1-\alpha-\eta.
\tag{4}
\]
The union bound, (3), and (4) therefore imply
\[
Q_{0,n}(H_{0,n}\cap H_{1,n})
\ge 1-Q_{0,n}(H_{0,n}^{c})-Q_{0,n}(H_{1,n}^{c})
\ge 1-2\alpha-\eta.
\tag{5}
\]
On \(H_{0,n}\cap H_{1,n}\), the interval \([L_n(a),U_n(a)]\) contains both \(\mu_{0,n}(a)\) and \(\mu_{1,n}(a)\) for every \(a\in I\).  Hence, pointwise in \(a\),
\[
U_n(a)-L_n(a)\ge |\mu_{1,n}(a)-\mu_{0,n}(a)|,
\]
and the definition of \(W_n\) gives
\[
W_n\ge \Delta_n
\quad\text{on }H_{0,n}\cap H_{1,n}.
\tag{6}
\]
Moreover, \(W_n\ge0\) almost surely by the definition of \(\mathfrak B_{n,\alpha}\).  Combining (1), (5), and (6),
\[
E_{Q_{0,n}}[W_n]
\ge \Delta_n Q_{0,n}(H_{0,n}\cap H_{1,n})
\ge (1-2\alpha-\eta)c_{\mathrm{sep}}K_n^{1/2-\beta}.
\tag{7}
\]
This calculation is the two-point expected-length argument recorded in the stated Low lemma.  Put
\[
c':=(1-2\alpha-\eta)c_{\mathrm{sep}}.
\]
The restriction \(\eta<1-2\alpha\) and the inequality \(c_{\mathrm{sep}}>0\) imply \(c'>0\).  Because \(P_{0,n}\in\mathcal M\), (7) entails, for every \(\mathcal C_n\in\mathfrak B_{n,\alpha}\),
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M}E_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\otimes n}}[W_n]
\ge E_{Q_{0,n}}[W_n]
\ge c'K_n^{1/2-\beta}.
\]
Taking the infimum over \(\mathcal C_n\in\mathfrak B_{n,\alpha}\) proves the first asserted inequality.

It remains to verify the displayed scale equivalence under the current smoothness range.  Let
\[
x_n:=\frac{\log n}{\log\log n},
\qquad
\gamma:=\beta-\frac12.
\]
The assumption \(\beta>1/2\) gives \(\gamma>0\).  For all sufficiently large \(n\), \(x_n\ge2\) and the definition \(K_n=\lfloor x_n\rfloor\) implies
\[
\frac{x_n}{2}\le K_n\le x_n.
\]
Since \(u\mapsto u^{-\gamma}\) is decreasing on \((0,\infty)\),
\[
x_n^{-\gamma}\le K_n^{-\gamma}\le 2^\gamma x_n^{-\gamma}.
\tag{8}
\]
Finally,
\[
x_n^{-\gamma}
=\left(\frac{\log\log n}{\log n}\right)^{\beta-1/2}
=r_n.
\tag{9}
\]
Equations (8)--(9) show \(K_n^{1/2-\beta}\asymp r_n\).  Increasing \(n_0\), if necessary, to a finite \(n_1\) for which the definition of \(K_n\) and the preceding bounds all hold completes the conditional proof.
