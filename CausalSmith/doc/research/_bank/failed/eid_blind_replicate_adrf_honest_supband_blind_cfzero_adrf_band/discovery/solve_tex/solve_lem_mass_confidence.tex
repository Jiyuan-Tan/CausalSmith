%%% FIELD proof-mass-confidence
Let \(m=|\mathcal X|\) and
\[
 e_{p,n}=\left\{\frac{\log(6m/\alpha)}{2n}\right\}^{1/2}.
\]
We first establish the simultaneous mass event.  If \(Z\) is any random variable supported on \([0,1]\), define
\(\psi(\lambda)=\log E\exp\{\lambda(Z-EZ)\}\).  Under exponential tilting by \(e^{\lambda Z}\), differentiation under the expectation is valid because \(Z\) is bounded, and
\[
 \psi''(\lambda)=\operatorname{Var}_{\lambda}(Z)
 \le E_{\lambda}\bigl[(Z-1/2)^2\bigr]\le\frac14.
\]
Since \(\psi(0)=\psi'(0)=0\), integrating this inequality twice gives
\[
 \log E\exp\{\lambda(Z-EZ)\}\le\frac{\lambda^2}{8},
 \qquad \lambda\in\mathbb R.                                      \tag{1}
\]
For fixed \(x\in\mathcal X\), assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}iid}\) makes
\(\widehat p_x=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf 1\{X_i=x\}\) the average of independent Bernoulli variables with common mean \(p_x\).  Applying exponential Markov inequality to (1) and minimizing the upper-tail exponent
\(-n\lambda e_{p,n}+n\lambda^2/8\) at \(\lambda=4e_{p,n}\), and applying the same argument to the lower tail, yields
\[
 P_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\otimes n}
 \bigl(|\widehat p_x-p_x|>e_{p,n}\bigr)
 \le 2e^{-2ne_{p,n}^2}=\frac{\alpha}{3m}.                           \tag{2}
\]
The union bound over the finite set \(\mathcal X\) therefore gives, uniformly in \(P\in\mathcal M\),
\[
 P_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\otimes n}
 \left(\max_{x\in\mathcal X}|\widehat p_x-p_x|\le e_{p,n}\right)
 \ge1-\frac{\alpha}{3}.                                             \tag{3}
\]
For every \(P\in\mathcal M\), the vector \((p_x)_{x\in\mathcal X}\) is nonnegative and sums to one.  Thus \(\mathrm{def{:}mass\text{-}confidence\text{-}region}\) says that the event in (3) is precisely
\(\{(p_x)_{x\in\mathcal X}\in\mathcal P_{n,\alpha}\}\), and the claimed coordinate bound holds on that event.

We next prove the regular-count inequality.  If \(N\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,p)\), then for every \(t>0\), exponential Markov inequality and \(1+v\le e^v\) imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Pr(N\le np/2)
 &\le e^{tnp/2}E[e^{-tN}]\\
 &=e^{tnp/2}(1-p+pe^{-t})^n\\
 &\le\exp\{tnp/2+np(e^{-t}-1)\}.
 \end{aligned}                                                       \tag{4}
\]
Taking \(t=\log2\) in (4), and using \((1-\log2)/2\ge1/8\), gives
\[
 \Pr(N\le np/2)\le e^{-np(1-\log2)/2}\le e^{-np/8}.                \tag{5}
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}strata}\) gives \(p_x\ge p_{\min}\).  Consequently
\(\{n_x<np_{\min}/2\}\subseteq\{n_x<np_x/2\}\), and a union bound applied to (5) proves
\[
 P_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\otimes n}
 \left(\min_{x\in\mathcal X}n_x<np_{\min}/2\right)
 \le m e^{-np_{\min}/8}.                                           \tag{6}
\]

It remains to control mass profiling.  Let \(p=(p_x)_x\) and \(p'=(p'_x)_x\) lie in \(\mathcal P_{n,\alpha}\), and fix \(f_x\in\mathscr F_n\) and \(q_x\in\mathscr Q_n(f_x)\) for every \(x\).  Put
\[
 e_n=a_{q,n}+B_Ya_{f,n}.
\]
The continuum constraints in \(\mathrm{def{:}signal\text{-}sieves}\) give, for every \(a\in I\),
\[
 f_x(a)\ge\kappa/2>0,
 \qquad |q_x(a)|\le B_Yf_x(a)+e_n.                                  \tag{7}
\]
Division in the next display is therefore justified by (7).  By
\(b_{\mu,n}=4e_n/\kappa\) from \(\mathrm{def{:}ecf\text{-}radii}\),
\[
 \left|\frac{q_x(a)}{f_x(a)}\right|
 \le B_Y+\frac{e_n}{f_x(a)}
 \le B_Y+\frac{2e_n}{\kappa}
 =B_Y+\frac{b_{\mu,n}}2.                                            \tag{8}
\]
The definition of \(\mathcal P_{n,\alpha}\) gives
\(|p_x-p'_x|\le|p_x-\widehat p_x|+|p'_x-\widehat p_x|\le2e_{p,n}\).
Hence the triangle inequality and (8) yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sup_{a\in I}\left|
 \sum_{x\in\mathcal X}(p_x-p'_x)\frac{q_x(a)}{f_x(a)}
 \right|
 &\le\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}|p_x-p'_x|
       \sup_{a\in I}\left|\frac{q_x(a)}{f_x(a)}\right|\\
 &\le2m\left(B_Y+\frac{b_{\mu,n}}2\right)e_{p,n}.                 \tag{9}
 \end{aligned}
\]

Finally, let \(\gamma=\beta-1/2\).  The enlarged declared range \(\beta>1/2\) gives exactly \(\gamma>0\).  By \(\mathrm{def{:}upper\text{-}tuning\text{-}grid}\),
\[
 K_n\sim\frac{\log n}{\log\log n},
\]
and \(\mathrm{def{:}ecf\text{-}radii}\) gives
\[
 b_{\mu,n}
 =4\kappa^{-1}C_{\beta,\mathcal A}(R_q+B_YR_f)K_n^{-\gamma}
 =O(r_n),                                                            \tag{10}
\]
because \(r_n=(\log\log n/\log n)^\gamma\).  Moreover,
\[
 \log\left(\frac{e_{p,n}}{r_n}\right)
 =-\frac12\log n+\gamma\log\log n+O(\log\log\log n)
 \longrightarrow-\infty,                                          \tag{11}
\]
where \(m\), \(\alpha\), and \(\gamma\) are fixed.  Thus \(e_{p,n}=o(r_n)\).  Combining this with (10) gives
\[
 2m\left(B_Y+\frac{b_{\mu,n}}2\right)e_{p,n}=o(r_n),               \tag{12}
\]
which, together with (3), (6), and (9), proves every assertion.
