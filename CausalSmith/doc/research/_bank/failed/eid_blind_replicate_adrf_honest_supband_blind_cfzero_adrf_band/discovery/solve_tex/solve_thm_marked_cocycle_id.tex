Let \(P^{(0)},P^{(1)}\in\mathcal M\) induce the same observed-data law, and fix \(x\in\mathcal X\). By \(\mathrm{ass{:}strata}\), \(p_x\ge p_{\min}>0\), so conditioning on \(X=x\) is well defined and the common observed law determines both \(F_x\) and \(G_x\).

We first derive, rather than assume, a common protected neighborhood. For either candidate law, either \(j\in\{1,2\}\), and every real \(v\), the elementary inequality \(|e^{iz}-1|\le |z|\) for real \(z\), followed by H\"older's inequality and \(\mathrm{ass{:}error\text{-}moment}\), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\phi_j(v)|
 &\ge 1-|\phi_j(v)-1|\\
 &\ge 1-E_P\!\left[|e^{ivU_j}-1|\right]\\
 &\ge 1-|v|E_P|U_j|\\
 &\ge 1-|v|\{E_P|U_j|^{q_e}\}^{1/q_e}\\
 &\ge 1-|v|M_e^{1/q_e}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here the fourth line is valid because the enlarged model has \(q_e>1\). Since \(M_e>0\), define
\[
 \nu_*=(2M_e^{1/q_e})^{-1}>0,
 \qquad c_*=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \delta=\min\left\{\frac{\nu_*}{2},\frac{1}{4B_A}\right\}>0.
\]
It follows that \(|\phi_j(v)|\ge c_*\) whenever \(|v|\le\nu_*\). On the other hand, \(\mathrm{ass{:}compact\text{-}dose}\) and the fact that \(f_x\) is a conditional density imply, for \(|w|\le2\delta\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\widehat f_x(w)-1|
 &\le \int_{\mathcal A}|e^{iwa}-1|f_x(a)\,da\\
 &\le |w|\int_{\mathcal A}|a|f_x(a)\,da\\
 &\le B_A|w|\le\frac12.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(|\widehat f_x(w)|\ge1/2\) on \([-2\delta,2\delta]\). If \(|u|,|t|\le\delta\), then \(|u+t|\le2\delta\) and \(|u|,|t|\le\nu_*\). The probability-channel identity from \(\mathrm{lem{:}factorization}\) consequently yields
\[
 |F_x(u,t)|
 =|\widehat f_x(u+t)|\,|\phi_1(u)|\,|\phi_2(t)|
 \ge \frac18>0.
\]
This proves the common near-origin nonvanishing needed below without any separately assumed spectral modulus.

The same moment calculation gives \(E_P|U_2|<\infty\), while \(\mathrm{ass{:}compact\text{-}dose}\) gives \(|A|\le B_A\) almost surely. Dominated differentiation is therefore valid at \(t=0\) in both the conditional expectation defining \(F_x(u,t)\) and the factorized expression. Define the observed logarithmic derivative, with \(\partial_t\log F_x\) interpreted as \((\partial_tF_x)/F_x\), by
\[
 D_x(u):=
 \frac{\left.\partial_tF_x(u,t)\right|_{t=0}}{F_x(u,0)},
 \qquad |u|\le\delta.
\]
Differentiating the factorization from \(\mathrm{lem{:}factorization}\) and dividing by its nonzero value gives
\[
 D_x(u)
 =\frac{\widehat f_x'(u)}{\widehat f_x(u)}
  +\frac{\phi_2'(0)}{\phi_2(0)}.
\]
Characteristic-function normalization gives \(\phi_2(0)=1\). The finite first moment established above permits differentiation of \(\phi_2\) at zero, and \(\mathrm{ass{:}center\text{-}two}\) gives
\[
 \phi_2'(0)=iE_P[U_2]=0.
\]
Consequently every candidate satisfies the observed ordinary differential equation
\[
 \widehat f_x'(u)=D_x(u)\widehat f_x(u),
 \qquad \widehat f_x(0)=\int_{\mathcal A}f_x(a)\,da=1,
 \qquad |u|\le\delta.
\]
For the two candidate laws, the observed function \(D_x\) is the same. Since each candidate transform is nonzero on this interval, the ratio
\[
 R_x(u)=\frac{\widehat f_x^{(1)}(u)}{\widehat f_x^{(0)}(u)}
\]
satisfies \(R_x'(u)=0\) and \(R_x(0)=1\). Hence
\[
 \widehat f_x^{(1)}(u)=\widehat f_x^{(0)}(u),
 \qquad |u|\le\delta.
\]
Only \(\phi_2\) was differentiated, so no location normalization on \(U_1\) has been used.

We next extend this equality without imposing global nonvanishing of either error transform. Let \(h=f_x^{(1)}-f_x^{(0)}\). Then \(h\in L^1(\mathbb R)\) and \(h\) is supported on \(\mathcal A=[-B_A,B_A]\). For every \(z\in\mathbb C\), absolute convergence and Tonelli's theorem give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 H(z)
 &:=\int_{\mathcal A}e^{iza}h(a)\,da\\
 &=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(iz)^k}{k!}
       \int_{\mathcal A}a^kh(a)\,da,
\end{aligned}
\]
because
\[
 \int_{\mathcal A}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}
 \frac{|za|^k}{k!}|h(a)|\,da
 \le e^{|z|B_A}\|h\|_1<\infty.
\]
Thus \(H\) is entire. Its restriction to the real interval \((-\delta,\delta)\) is zero by the preceding local equality, so every derivative at zero is zero. The displayed everywhere-convergent power series then gives \(H(z)=0\) for every \(z\in\mathbb C\). In particular, the two density transforms agree on all real frequencies.

For the marked channel, \(\mathrm{lem{:}factorization}\), evaluated at \((u,0)\), and the already proved nonvanishing give, for each candidate law,
\[
 \widehat q_x(u)
 =\widehat f_x(u)\frac{G_x(u,0)}{F_x(u,0)},
 \qquad |u|\le\delta.
\]
The right-hand side is common to the two candidates because their observed channels and their recovered \(\widehat f_x\)'s are common. Therefore
\[
 \widehat q_x^{(1)}(u)=\widehat q_x^{(0)}(u),
 \qquad |u|\le\delta.
\]
To justify analytic continuation in this signed channel, \(\mathrm{ass{:}consistency}\) and \(\mathrm{ass{:}bounded\text{-}outcome}\) imply \(|Y|=|Y(A)|\le B_Y\) almost surely. Conditional Jensen's inequality hence gives \(|m_x(a)|\le B_Y\) for \(f_x(a)\,da\)-almost every \(a\), and the definition \(q_x=m_xf_x\) yields
\[
 |q_x(a)|\le B_Yf_x(a)\quad\text{almost everywhere},
 \qquad
 \|q_x\|_1\le B_Y.
\]
Moreover \(q_x\) is supported on \(\mathcal A\) because \(f_x\) is. Applying the same absolutely convergent power-series argument to \(q_x^{(1)}-q_x^{(0)}\) shows that the local equality of marked transforms extends to all \(z\in\mathbb C\).

For completeness, global equality of the transforms implies equality of the underlying functions. If \(h\) denotes either the difference of the two \(f_x\)'s or the difference of the two \(q_x\)'s, then \(h\in L^1(\mathbb R)\) and \(\widehat h(t)=0\) for all real \(t\). For \(\epsilon>0\), put
\[
 g_\epsilon(v)=(2\pi\epsilon)^{-1/2}
 e^{-v^2/(2\epsilon)}.
\]
The Gaussian integral and Fubini's theorem, whose absolute-integrability premise follows from \(h\in L^1\) and \(e^{-\epsilon t^2/2}\in L^1\), give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 (h*g_\epsilon)(a)
 &=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}
 e^{-ita}\widehat h(t)e^{-\epsilon t^2/2}\,dt=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
Also,
\[
 \|h*g_\epsilon-h\|_1
 \le\int_{\mathbb R}g_\epsilon(v)
       \|h(\cdot-v)-h\|_1\,dv\longrightarrow0.
\]
Indeed, translation continuity is first immediate for compactly supported step functions and then follows for every \(L^1\) function by \(L^1\) approximation; splitting the last integral at any fixed small \(|v|\) and using concentration of \(g_\epsilon\) at zero proves the limit. Since every convolution in the display is zero, \(h=0\) almost everywhere.

Finally, \(\mathrm{ass{:}f\text{-}sobolev}\), \(\mathrm{ass{:}q\text{-}sobolev}\), and the enlarged range \(\beta>1/2\) provide unique continuous representatives. Indeed, for either difference \(h\), Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}|\widehat h(t)|\,dt
 \le
 \left\{\int_{\mathbb R}(1+t^2)^\beta
                 |\widehat h(t)|^2\,dt\right\}^{1/2}
 \left\{\int_{\mathbb R}(1+t^2)^{-\beta}\,dt\right\}^{1/2}
 <\infty,
\]
where the second factor is finite exactly because \(\beta>1/2\). Fourier inversion therefore supplies a continuous representative, and two continuous representatives equal almost everywhere only if they agree everywhere. Since \(x\) was arbitrary, the common observed law determines every \(f_x\) and \(q_x\). The proof used the errors only on the derived interval \([-\nu_*,\nu_*]\), so it remains valid when either error characteristic function has an open zero set away from zero.
