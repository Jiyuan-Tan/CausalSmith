%%% FIELD jackson-statement
For every \(\beta>1/2\), with \(d=\lceil\beta\rceil+1\), and every \(g\in H^\beta(\mathbb R)\), the uniform order-\(d\) B-spline spaces on \(\mathcal A\) satisfy, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 \inf_{s\in\operatorname{span}\{\mathsf B_{k,K_n}:1\le k\le K_n+d\}}
 \|g-s\|_{L^\infty(\mathcal A)}
 \le C_{\beta,\mathcal A}K_n^{1/2-\beta}
       \|g\|_{H^\beta(\mathbb R)}.
\]
The constant \(C_{\beta,\mathcal A}<\infty\) is independent of \(g\), \(n\), and the spline coefficients.
%%% FIELD jackson-proof
Write \(h=2B_A/K_n\).  Since \(K_n\to\infty\), it is enough to prove the assertion for \(0<h\le1\).  We use the Fourier convention
\[
 \widehat g(t)=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{ita}g(a)\,da,
 \qquad
 g(a)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-ita}\widehat g(t)\,dt.
\]
The second formula holds for the continuous representative of \(g\): indeed, because \(\beta>1/2\), Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}|\widehat g(t)|\,dt
 \le
 \left\{\int_{\mathbb R}(1+t^2)^{-\beta}\,dt\right\}^{1/2}
 \left\{\int_{\mathbb R}(1+t^2)^\beta|\widehat g(t)|^2\,dt\right\}^{1/2}
 <\infty.                                                        \tag{1}
\]
Define the low-frequency truncation
\[
 g_h(a)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{|t|\le h^{-1}}e^{-ita}\widehat g(t)\,dt.
\]
Another application of Cauchy--Schwarz, followed by
\(\int_{h^{-1}}^\infty t^{-2\beta}\,dt=(2\beta-1)^{-1}h^{2\beta-1}\), yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \|g-g_h\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R)}
 &\le \frac1{2\pi}
 \left\{\int_{|t|>h^{-1}}(1+t^2)^{-\beta}\,dt\right\}^{1/2}
 \left\{\int_{\mathbb R}(1+t^2)^\beta|\widehat g(t)|^2\,dt\right\}^{1/2}\\
 &\le C_1(\beta)h^{\beta-1/2}\|g\|_{H^\beta(\mathbb R)}.       \tag{2}
 \end{aligned}
\]
Here the division by \(2\beta-1\) is valid precisely because \(\beta>1/2\).

We next derive the smooth-function approximation property of the spline space used below.  Let \(v\in C^d(\mathbb R)\).  By the definition of the normalized order-\(d\) B-spline basis, its elements are nonnegative and sum to one.  By the word ``basis'' in that definition, they span the full order-\(d\) spline space on the uniform partition.  Every polynomial of degree at most \(d-1\) belongs to that space, because its restrictions to the cells are polynomials of degree at most \(d-1\) and have no derivative jumps at the knots.  It therefore has a unique B-spline coefficient vector.

For completeness, these algebraic facts give a uniformly bounded polynomial-reproducing quasi-interpolant as follows.  For each basis index \(k\), choose a point \(\xi_k\) in its support.  Given \(v\), let \(\Lambda_{k,h}(v)\) be the \(k\)-th B-spline coefficient of the degree-\((d-1)\) Taylor polynomial of \(v\) at \(\xi_k\).  There are only finitely many coefficient patterns after translation and scaling, including the two boundary patterns.  Finite-dimensional norm equivalence therefore gives a constant \(C_d\), independent of \(h\), \(k\), and \(v\), such that
\[
 |\Lambda_{k,h}(v)|
 \le C_d\max_{0\le j\le d-1}h^j|v^{(j)}(\xi_k)|.                 \tag{3}
\]
The operator
\[
 (J_hv)(a)=\sum_{k=1}^{K_n+d}\Lambda_{k,h}(v)\mathsf B_{k,K_n}(a)
\]
reproduces every polynomial of degree at most \(d-1\), because the Taylor polynomial of such a polynomial is the polynomial itself.  Fix \(a\in\mathcal A\), and let \(p_a\) be the degree-\((d-1)\) Taylor polynomial of \(v\) at \(a\).  Whenever \(\mathsf B_{k,K_n}(a)\ne0\), the support locality gives \(|\xi_k-a|\le C_dh\).  Taylor's formula with integral remainder then implies, for \(0\le j\le d-1\),
\[
 h^j\left|(v-p_a)^{(j)}(\xi_k)\right|
 \le C_dh^d\|v^{(d)}\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R)}.                  \tag{4}
\]
Since \((v-p_a)(a)=0\), polynomial reproduction, (3), (4), nonnegativity, and the partition of unity give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |v(a)-J_hv(a)|
 &=|J_h(v-p_a)(a)|\\
 &\le \sum_k\mathsf B_{k,K_n}(a)
       |\Lambda_{k,h}(v-p_a)|\\
 &\le C_dh^d\|v^{(d)}\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R)}.
                                                                    \tag{5}
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus (5) proves the required smooth-function spline estimate without a shape-preservation premise.

Apply (5) to \(v=g_h\).  Differentiation under the finite-frequency integral and Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \|g_h^{(d)}\|_{L^\infty(\mathbb R)}
 &\le \frac1{2\pi}
 \left\{\int_{|t|\le h^{-1}}|t|^{2d}(1+t^2)^{-\beta}\,dt\right\}^{1/2}
 \left\{\int_{\mathbb R}(1+t^2)^\beta|\widehat g(t)|^2\,dt\right\}^{1/2}\\
 &\le C_2(\beta,d)h^{\beta-d-1/2}
       \|g\|_{H^\beta(\mathbb R)}.                              \tag{6}
 \end{aligned}
\]
For the last inequality, split the first integral at one and use
\(d=\lceil\beta\rceil+1>\beta\), so that
\(\int_1^{h^{-1}}t^{2d-2\beta}\,dt\le C(\beta,d)h^{-(2d-2\beta+1)}\).
Combining (2), (5), and (6) produces a spline \(s=J_hg_h\) satisfying
\[
 \|g-s\|_{L^\infty(\mathcal A)}
 \le C_3(\beta)h^{\beta-1/2}\|g\|_{H^\beta(\mathbb R)}
 =C_3(\beta)(2B_A)^{\beta-1/2}K_n^{1/2-\beta}
   \|g\|_{H^\beta(\mathbb R)}.                                  \tag{7}
\]
The restriction of \(J_hg_h\) to \(\mathcal A\) lies in the span of the displayed order-\(d\) basis.  Choosing the fixed Jackson constant \(C_{\beta,\mathcal A}\) no smaller than the constant in (7) proves the claim for every \(\beta>1/2\).
%%% FIELD outer-statement
Under the relaxed outer-sieve construction in \(\mathrm{def{:}signal\text{-}sieves}\), for every \(P\in\mathcal M\), every \(x\in\mathcal X\), and all sufficiently large \(n\), there are splines \(\widetilde f_{x,n}\in\mathscr F_n\) and \(\widetilde q_{x,n}\in\mathscr Q_n(\widetilde f_{x,n})\) such that
\[
 \|\widetilde f_{x,n}-f_x\|_{L^\infty(\mathcal A)}\le a_{f,n},
 \qquad
 \|\widetilde q_{x,n}-q_x\|_{L^\infty(\mathcal A)}\le a_{q,n}.
\]
With the true masses \((p_x)_{x\in\mathcal X}\), their profiled ADRF satisfies
\[
 \sup_{a\in I}\left|
 \sum_{x\in\mathcal X}p_x\frac{\widetilde q_{x,n}(a)}{\widetilde f_{x,n}(a)}-\mu(a)
 \right|
 \le 2\kappa^{-1}(a_{q,n}+B_Ya_{f,n})
 \le b_{\mu,n}.
\]
%%% FIELD outer-proof
Fix \(P\in\mathcal M\) and \(x\in\mathcal X\).  Apply \(\mathrm{lem{:}sobolev\text{-}spline\text{-}jackson}\) first to \(f_x\) and then to \(q_x\).  Assumptions \(\mathrm{ass{:}f\text{-}sobolev}\) and \(\mathrm{ass{:}q\text{-}sobolev}\), together with the definitions of \(a_{f,n}\) and \(a_{q,n}\) in \(\mathrm{def{:}ecf\text{-}radii}\), give order-\(d\) splines \(\widetilde f_{x,n}\) and \(\widetilde q_{x,n}\) in the displayed uniform B-spline space such that
\[
 \|\widetilde f_{x,n}-f_x\|_{L^\infty(\mathcal A)}
 \le C_{\beta,\mathcal A}K_n^{1/2-\beta}\|f_x\|_{H^\beta}
 \le a_{f,n},                                                     \tag{1}
\]
and
\[
 \|\widetilde q_{x,n}-q_x\|_{L^\infty(\mathcal A)}
 \le C_{\beta,\mathcal A}K_n^{1/2-\beta}\|q_x\|_{H^\beta}
 \le a_{q,n}.                                                     \tag{2}
\]
The enlarged symbol range is \(\beta>1/2\).  Hence \(1/2-\beta<0\), and \(K_n\to\infty\) implies \(a_{f,n}\to0\).  Thus, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 a_{f,n}<\kappa/2.                                                \tag{3}
\]

By definition, \(f_x\) is a nonnegative conditional density.  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}compact\text{-}dose}\) gives \(\int_{\mathcal A}f_x(a)\,da=1\).  Therefore (1) and \(\mathrm{ass{:}denominator}\) imply
\[
 \widetilde f_{x,n}(a)\ge-a_{f,n}\quad(a\in\mathcal A),          \tag{4}
\]
\[
 \widetilde f_{x,n}(a)
 \ge\kappa-a_{f,n}
 \ge\max\{\kappa/2,\kappa-a_{f,n}\}
 \quad(a\in I),                                                  \tag{5}
\]
where the equality in the maximum follows from (3), and
\[
 \left|\int_{\mathcal A}\widetilde f_{x,n}(a)\,da-1\right|
 \le\int_{\mathcal A}|\widetilde f_{x,n}(a)-f_x(a)|\,da
 \le2B_Aa_{f,n}.                                                  \tag{6}
\]

We next verify the marked constraint.  The common-event representation in \(\mathrm{ass{:}structural\text{-}response\text{-}surface}\) takes values in \([-B_Y,B_Y]\); together with \(\mathrm{ass{:}consistency}\), it gives \(|Y|\le B_Y\) almost surely.  Conditional Jensen's inequality consequently gives
\[
 |m_x(a)|=|E_P[Y\mid A=a,X=x]|\le B_Y
 \quad\text{for }f_x(a)\,da\text{-almost every }a.               \tag{7}
\]
Because \(q_x=m_xf_x\), (7) implies \(|q_x|\le B_Yf_x\) Lebesgue-almost everywhere.  This inequality has pointwise meaning under the enlarged range.  Indeed, for either \(g=f_x\) or \(g=q_x\), \(\mathrm{ass{:}f\text{-}sobolev}\) or \(\mathrm{ass{:}q\text{-}sobolev}\) and \(\beta>1/2\) yield
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}|\widehat g(t)|\,dt
 \le
 \left\{\int_{\mathbb R}(1+t^2)^{-\beta}\,dt\right\}^{1/2}
 \left\{\int_{\mathbb R}(1+t^2)^\beta|\widehat g(t)|^2\,dt\right\}^{1/2}
 <\infty.                                                         \tag{8}
\]
Fourier inversion and dominated convergence therefore supply continuous representatives of \(f_x\) and \(q_x\).  If \(|q_x(a_0)|>B_Yf_x(a_0)\) at some \(a_0\), continuity would make the same strict inequality hold on a nonempty interval, contradicting its almost-everywhere validity.  Hence
\[
 |q_x(a)|\le B_Yf_x(a)\quad\text{for every }a\in\mathcal A.       \tag{9}
\]
Equations (1), (2), and (9) now give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |\widetilde q_{x,n}(a)|
 &\le |q_x(a)|+a_{q,n}\\
 &\le B_Y\widetilde f_{x,n}(a)+a_{q,n}+B_Ya_{f,n},
 \qquad a\in\mathcal A.                                         \tag{10}
 \end{aligned}
\]
The constraints (4)--(6) are exactly those defining \(\mathscr F_n\), and (10) is exactly the defining constraint of \(\mathscr Q_n(\widetilde f_{x,n})\) in \(\mathrm{def{:}signal\text{-}sieves}\).  Thus
\[
 \widetilde f_{x,n}\in\mathscr F_n,
 \qquad
 \widetilde q_{x,n}\in\mathscr Q_n(\widetilde f_{x,n}).          \tag{11}
\]

It remains to compare the profiled quotient with the causal estimand.  The already established \(\mathrm{thm{:}adrf\text{-}id}\) gives
\[
 \mu(a)=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}p_x\frac{q_x(a)}{f_x(a)},
 \qquad a\in I.                                                   \tag{12}
\]
For \(a\in I\), equations (1), (2), and (5), assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}denominator}\), and (9) imply that all denominators below are positive and
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \left|\frac{\widetilde q_{x,n}(a)}{\widetilde f_{x,n}(a)}
       -\frac{q_x(a)}{f_x(a)}\right|
 &\le
 \frac{|\widetilde q_{x,n}(a)-q_x(a)|}{\widetilde f_{x,n}(a)}
 +\frac{|q_x(a)|\,|\widetilde f_{x,n}(a)-f_x(a)|}
 {f_x(a)\widetilde f_{x,n}(a)}\\
 &\le\frac{a_{q,n}+B_Ya_{f,n}}{\kappa-a_{f,n}}\\
 &\le2\kappa^{-1}(a_{q,n}+B_Ya_{f,n}),                            \tag{13}
 \end{aligned}
\]
where the last division uses \(\kappa-a_{f,n}\ge\kappa/2>0\) from (3).
The numbers \(p_x=P(X=x)\) are nonnegative and sum to one.  Summing (13) with weights \(p_x\), using (12), and taking the supremum over \(I\) yields
\[
 \sup_{a\in I}\left|
 \sum_{x\in\mathcal X}p_x
 \frac{\widetilde q_{x,n}(a)}{\widetilde f_{x,n}(a)}-\mu(a)
 \right|
 \le2\kappa^{-1}(a_{q,n}+B_Ya_{f,n}).                             \tag{14}
\]
Finally, \(\mathrm{def{:}ecf\text{-}radii}\) defines
\(b_{\mu,n}=4\kappa^{-1}(a_{q,n}+B_Ya_{f,n})\), whose summands are nonnegative.  Thus the right-hand side of (14) is at most \(b_{\mu,n}\), completing the proof.
