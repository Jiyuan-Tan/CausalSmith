%%% FIELD def-sobolev-modulus-construction
For \(R>0\) and \(r\ge 0\), define
\[
 \Omega_R(r)=\frac{R}{\sqrt{2\pi}}
 \sup_{|h|\le r}
 \left\{\int_{\mathbb R}|e^{-ith}-1|^2(1+t^2)^{-\beta}\,dt\right\}^{1/2}.
\]

%%% FIELD def-signal-sieves-construction
Let \(\mathsf B_{k,K_n}\), \(1\le k\le K_n+d\), be the normalized order-\(d\) B-splines on \(\mathcal A\). Define the outer density-spline family by
\[
 \mathscr F_n=\left\{f=\sum_{k=1}^{K_n+d}c_k\mathsf B_{k,K_n}:
 \begin{array}{l}
 f(a)\ge-a_{f,n}\quad\forall a\in\mathcal A,\\
 \left|\int_{\mathcal A}f(a)\,da-1\right|\le2B_Aa_{f,n},\\
 f(a)\ge\max\{\kappa/2,\kappa-a_{f,n}\}\quad\forall a\in I,\\
 |f(a)-f(b)|\le\Omega_{R_f}(|a-b|)+2a_{f,n}
 \quad\forall a,b\in\mathcal A
 \end{array}
 \right\}.
\]
For \(f\in\mathscr F_n\), define the outer marked-spline family by
\[
 \mathscr Q_n(f)=\left\{q=\sum_{k=1}^{K_n+d}d_k\mathsf B_{k,K_n}:
 \begin{array}{l}
 |q(a)|\le B_Yf(a)+a_{q,n}+B_Ya_{f,n}\quad\forall a\in\mathcal A,\\
 |q(a)-q(b)|\le\Omega_{R_q}(|a-b|)+2a_{q,n}
 \quad\forall a,b\in\mathcal A
 \end{array}
 \right\}.
\]
These are outer approximation families: their elements need not themselves be densities or outcome-weighted densities. Their closed continuum inequalities and integral bounds make them compact subsets of the corresponding finite-dimensional spline spaces.

%%% FIELD def-finite-sieve-diameter-construction
For a realized sample \(z\), write \(n_x(z)\) for its stratum counts and \(\Theta_{n,\alpha}(z)\) for the finite-sieve feasible set. Define the deterministic regular-count diameter
\[
 \widetilde\omega_n=
 \sup_{\substack{z:\ \min_{x\in\mathcal X}n_x(z)\ge np_{\min}/2,\\
                  \Theta_{n,\alpha}(z)\ne\varnothing}}
 \operatorname{diam}_{L^\infty(I)}
 \{\mu_\theta:\theta\in\Theta_{n,\alpha}(z)\},
\]
where the supremum is zero if its indexing set is empty.

%%% FIELD lem-sobolev-modulus-control-statement
For \(\beta>1/2\) and \(R>0\), the function \(\Omega_R\) is finite, nondecreasing, and satisfies \(\Omega_R(r)\downarrow0\) as \(r\downarrow0\). Every \(g\in H^\beta(\mathbb R)\) with \(\|g\|_{H^\beta(\mathbb R)}\le R\) has a continuous representative obeying
\[
 |g(a)-g(b)|\le\Omega_R(|a-b|),\qquad a,b\in\mathbb R.
\]

%%% FIELD lem-sobolev-modulus-control-proof
Use the Fourier norm convention
\[
 \|g\|_{H^\beta}^2=\frac1{2\pi}
 \int_{\mathbb R}(1+t^2)^\beta|\widehat g(t)|^2\,dt.
\]
Because \(\beta>1/2\), the function \((1+t^2)^{-\beta}\) is integrable. Cauchy--Schwarz therefore gives
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}|\widehat g(t)|\,dt
 \le
 \left\{\int_{\mathbb R}(1+t^2)^{-\beta}\,dt\right\}^{1/2}
 \left\{\int_{\mathbb R}(1+t^2)^\beta|\widehat g(t)|^2\,dt\right\}^{1/2}<\infty.
\]
Fourier inversion hence supplies a continuous representative. For \(h=a-b\), another application of Cauchy--Schwarz yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |g(a)-g(b)|
 &\le \frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}
       |e^{-ita}-e^{-itb}|\,|\widehat g(t)|\,dt\\
 &\le \frac{\|g\|_{H^\beta}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}
 \left\{\int_{\mathbb R}|e^{-ith}-1|^2(1+t^2)^{-\beta}\,dt\right\}^{1/2}\\
 &\le \Omega_R(|a-b|).
 \end{aligned}
\]
The definition makes \(\Omega_R\) nondecreasing. Its finiteness follows from \(|e^{-ith}-1|\le2\) and integrability of \((1+t^2)^{-\beta}\). Finally, for every sequence \(r_m\downarrow0\), the bound
\[
 \sup_{|h|\le r_m}|e^{-ith}-1|^2
 \le \min\{4,t^2r_m^2\}
\]
converges pointwise to zero and is dominated by \(4\). Dominated convergence against \((1+t^2)^{-\beta}dt\) proves \(\Omega_R(r_m)\to0\), and monotonicity gives the asserted limit.

%%% FIELD outer-spline-statement
Under the relaxed outer-sieve construction in \(\mathrm{def{:}signal\text{-}sieves}\), for every \(P\in\mathcal M\), every \(x\in\mathcal X\), and all sufficiently large \(n\), there are splines \(\widetilde f_{x,n}\in\mathscr F_n\) and \(\widetilde q_{x,n}\in\mathscr Q_n(\widetilde f_{x,n})\) such that
\[
 \|\widetilde f_{x,n}-f_x\|_{L^\infty(\mathcal A)}\le a_{f,n},
 \qquad
 \|\widetilde q_{x,n}-q_x\|_{L^\infty(\mathcal A)}\le a_{q,n}.
\]
With the true masses \((p_x)_{x\in\mathcal X}\), their profiled ADRF satisfies
\[
 \sup_{a\in I}\left|
 \sum_{x\in\mathcal X}p_x\frac{\widetilde q_{x,n}(a)}{\widetilde f_{x,n}(a)}-\mu(a)
 \right|
 \le 2\kappa^{-1}(a_{q,n}+B_Ya_{f,n})
 \le b_{\mu,n}.
\]

%%% FIELD outer-spline-proof
Fix \(P\in\mathcal M\) and \(x\in\mathcal X\). By \(\mathrm{lem{:}sobolev\text{-}spline\text{-}jackson}\), \(\mathrm{ass{:}f\text{-}sobolev}\), \(\mathrm{ass{:}q\text{-}sobolev}\), and \(\mathrm{def{:}ecf\text{-}radii}\), there are order-\(d\) splines \(\widetilde f_{x,n}\) and \(\widetilde q_{x,n}\) such that
\[
 \|\widetilde f_{x,n}-f_x\|_{L^\infty(\mathcal A)}
 \le C_{\beta,\mathcal A}K_n^{1/2-\beta}\|f_x\|_{H^\beta}
 \le a_{f,n},
\]
and
\[
 \|\widetilde q_{x,n}-q_x\|_{L^\infty(\mathcal A)}
 \le C_{\beta,\mathcal A}K_n^{1/2-\beta}\|q_x\|_{H^\beta}
 \le a_{q,n}.
\]
Since \(\beta>1/2\) and \(K_n\to\infty\), both allowances vanish. In particular, \(a_{f,n}<\kappa/2\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).

The function \(f_x\) is a nonnegative conditional density, and \(\mathrm{ass{:}compact\text{-}dose}\) gives \(\int_{\mathcal A}f_x=1\). The preceding approximation and \(\mathrm{ass{:}denominator}\) imply
\[
 \widetilde f_{x,n}(a)\ge-a_{f,n}\quad(a\in\mathcal A),
 \qquad
 \widetilde f_{x,n}(a)\ge\kappa-a_{f,n}
 =\max\{\kappa/2,\kappa-a_{f,n}\}\quad(a\in I),
\]
and
\[
 \left|\int_{\mathcal A}\widetilde f_{x,n}(a)\,da-1\right|
 \le2B_Aa_{f,n}.
\]

The common-event response representation in \(\mathrm{ass{:}structural\text{-}response\text{-}surface}\), together with \(\mathrm{ass{:}consistency}\), gives \(|Y|\le B_Y\) almost surely. Conditional Jensen's inequality therefore gives \(|m_x(a)|\le B_Y\) for \(f_x(a)da\)-almost every \(a\), so \(|q_x|\le B_Yf_x\) almost everywhere. By \(\mathrm{lem{:}sobolev\text{-}modulus\text{-}control}\), both \(f_x\) and \(q_x\) have continuous representatives. A strict violation of \(|q_x(a)|\le B_Yf_x(a)\) at one point would persist on an interval and contradict the almost-everywhere inequality. Hence
\[
 |q_x(a)|\le B_Yf_x(a),\qquad a\in\mathcal A.
\]
It follows that
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |\widetilde q_{x,n}(a)|
 &\le |q_x(a)|+a_{q,n}\\
 &\le B_Y\widetilde f_{x,n}(a)+a_{q,n}+B_Ya_{f,n},
 \qquad a\in\mathcal A.
 \end{aligned}
\]

It remains to verify the new modulus constraints. For \(a,b\in\mathcal A\), the triangle inequality, the two Jackson bounds, and \(\mathrm{lem{:}sobolev\text{-}modulus\text{-}control}\) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |\widetilde f_{x,n}(a)-\widetilde f_{x,n}(b)|
 &\le 2a_{f,n}+|f_x(a)-f_x(b)|
 \le2a_{f,n}+\Omega_{R_f}(|a-b|),\\
 |\widetilde q_{x,n}(a)-\widetilde q_{x,n}(b)|
 &\le 2a_{q,n}+|q_x(a)-q_x(b)|
 \le2a_{q,n}+\Omega_{R_q}(|a-b|).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus every defining constraint of \(\mathscr F_n\) and \(\mathscr Q_n(\widetilde f_{x,n})\) holds.

By the already established \(\mathrm{thm{:}adrf\text{-}id}\),
\[
 \mu(a)=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}p_x\frac{q_x(a)}{f_x(a)},
 \qquad a\in I.
\]
For \(a\in I\), all denominators below are positive and
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \left|\frac{\widetilde q_{x,n}(a)}{\widetilde f_{x,n}(a)}
       -\frac{q_x(a)}{f_x(a)}\right|
 &\le
 \frac{|\widetilde q_{x,n}(a)-q_x(a)|}{\widetilde f_{x,n}(a)}
 +\frac{|q_x(a)|\,|\widetilde f_{x,n}(a)-f_x(a)|}
 {f_x(a)\widetilde f_{x,n}(a)}\\
 &\le\frac{a_{q,n}+B_Ya_{f,n}}{\kappa-a_{f,n}}\\
 &\le2\kappa^{-1}(a_{q,n}+B_Ya_{f,n}).
 \end{aligned}
\]
The last division uses \(\kappa-a_{f,n}\ge\kappa/2>0\). Since the masses are nonnegative and sum to one, summing this inequality with weights \(p_x\), taking the supremum over \(I\), and using \(b_{\mu,n}=4\kappa^{-1}(a_{q,n}+B_Ya_{f,n})\) proves the two displayed ADRF bounds.

%%% FIELD analytic-sieve-stability-proof
Fix a sufficiently large \(n\). Conditional on \((X_i)_{i=1}^n\), \(\mathrm{ass{:}iid}\) makes the observations in each nonempty cell independent with their law conditional on \(X=x\). Each real and imaginary part of a probability-channel summand lies in \([-1,1]\). The structural response condition and \(\mathrm{ass{:}consistency}\) give \(|Y|\le B_Y\), so the corresponding parts of a marked-channel summand lie in \([-B_Y,B_Y]\).

The bounded-variable exponential-moment calculation used in \(\mathrm{lem{:}mass\text{-}confidence}\), applied to real and imaginary parts, gives, when \(n_x>\lambda_n\),
\[
 \Pr\left(
 |\widehat F_{x,n}(s,t)-F_x(s,t)|>2\sqrt{\lambda_n/n_x}
 \ \middle|\ (X_i)_{i=1}^n\right)\le4e^{-\lambda_n},
\]
and
\[
 \Pr\left(
 |\widehat G_{x,n}(s,t)-G_x(s,t)|>2B_Y\sqrt{\lambda_n/n_x}
 \ \middle|\ (X_i)_{i=1}^n\right)\le4e^{-\lambda_n}.
\]
If \(n_x\le\lambda_n\), the channel bounds and empty-cell convention give the defining inequalities deterministically because \(\rho^F_{x,n}=2\) and \(\rho^G_{x,n}=2B_Y\). A conditional union bound over both channels, all cells, and all grid points gives failure probability at most
\[
 8|\mathcal X||\mathcal D_n|e^{-\lambda_n}=\alpha/6.
\]
Combining this with the mass-region failure probability \(\alpha/3\) from \(\mathrm{lem{:}mass\text{-}confidence}\), and integrating over the cell labels, proves uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal M\) that
\[
 P_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\otimes n}(\mathcal E_{n,\alpha})
 \ge1-\alpha/3-\alpha/6\ge1-\alpha.
\]

Suppose \(\mathcal E_{n,\alpha}\) occurs. Apply \(\mathrm{lem{:}outer\text{-}spline\text{-}embedding}\) in every cell and \(\mathrm{lem{:}atomic\text{-}grid\text{-}embedding}\) to both error laws, and set
\[
 \bar\theta_n=
 \bigl((p_x,\widetilde f_{x,n},\widetilde q_{x,n})_{x\in\mathcal X},
       \bar\phi_{1,n},\bar\phi_{2,n}\bigr).
\]
The event places its mass vector in \(\mathcal P_{n,\alpha}\). At \((s,t)\in\mathcal D_n\), both coordinates belong to \(\Gamma_n\). Characteristic functions have modulus at most one; moreover \(|\widehat f_x(u)|\le1\), while \(|q_x|\le B_Yf_x\) gives \(|\widehat q_x(u)|\le B_Y\). Uniform spline approximation on the interval of length \(2B_A\) gives
\[
 |\widehat{\widetilde f}_{x,n}(u)-\widehat f_x(u)|\le2B_Aa_{f,n},
 \qquad
 |\widehat{\widetilde q}_{x,n}(u)-\widehat q_x(u)|\le2B_Aa_{q,n}.
\]
The atomic embedding and the triangle inequality give
\[
 |\bar\phi_{1,n}(s)\bar\phi_{2,n}(t)-\phi_1(s)\phi_2(t)|
 \le2\tau_{e,n}.
\]
Using \(\mathrm{lem{:}factorization}\) and the definitions of the bias allowances,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |\widehat{\widetilde f}_{x,n}(s+t)
   \bar\phi_{1,n}(s)\bar\phi_{2,n}(t)-F_x(s,t)|
 &\le2B_Aa_{f,n}+2\tau_{e,n}\le\delta^F_n,\\
 |\widehat{\widetilde q}_{x,n}(s+t)
   \bar\phi_{1,n}(s)\bar\phi_{2,n}(t)-G_x(s,t)|
 &\le2B_Aa_{q,n}+2B_Y\tau_{e,n}\le\delta^G_n.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The empirical inequalities on \(\mathcal E_{n,\alpha}\) now verify the residual constraints in \(\mathrm{def{:}joint\text{-}feasible\text{-}set}\), and the two embedding lemmas verify all deterministic constraints, including the new Sobolev-modulus inequalities. Thus \(\bar\theta_n\in\Theta_{n,\alpha}\). The quotient conclusion of the outer-spline embedding gives
\[
 \|\mu_{\bar\theta_n}-\mu\|_{L^\infty(I)}\le b_{\mu,n}.
\]

For the samplewise assertions, put \(e_n=a_{q,n}+B_Ya_{f,n}\). For any \(\theta\in\Theta_{n,\alpha}\), the simplex constraint, marked outer envelope, and \(f_x\ge\kappa/2\) on \(I\) imply
\[
 |\mu_\theta(a)|
 \le\sum_xp_x\left(B_Y+\frac{e_n}{f_x(a)}\right)
 \le B_Y+\frac{2e_n}{\kappa}
 =B_Y+\frac{b_{\mu,n}}2.
\]
If the feasible set is nonempty, write
\(\ell(a)=\inf_{\theta\in\Theta_{n,\alpha}}\mu_\theta(a)\) and
\(u(a)=\sup_{\theta\in\Theta_{n,\alpha}}\mu_\theta(a)\). Then
\[
 \ell(a)-b_{\mu,n}\le B_Y,
 \qquad -B_Y\le u(a)+b_{\mu,n},
 \qquad \ell(a)-b_{\mu,n}\le u(a)+b_{\mu,n}.
\]
Together with \(-B_Y\le B_Y\), these inequalities show that the maximum defining \(L_n(a)\) does not exceed the minimum defining \(U_n(a)\). Both endpoints lie in \([-B_Y,B_Y]\). If the feasible set is empty, both are zero. Hence, for every sample,
\[
 L_n(a)\le U_n(a)\quad(a\in I),
 \qquad0\le W_n\le2B_Y.
\]
This proves all assertions.

%%% FIELD finite-sieve-diameter-statement
For the equicontinuous finite signal sieves and the deterministic diameter in \(\mathrm{def{:}finite\text{-}sieve\text{-}diameter}\),
\[
 \widetilde\omega_n\longrightarrow0.
\]
This is a qualitative conclusion: it supplies neither an explicit inverse modulus nor an \(O(r_n)\) bound.

%%% FIELD finite-sieve-diameter-proof
Suppose, toward a contradiction, that \(\widetilde\omega_n\not\to0\). Then there are \(\epsilon>0\), integers \(n_k\to\infty\), samples \(z_k\) with \(\min_xn_x(z_k)\ge n_kp_{\min}/2\), and pairs
\(\theta_k,\theta'_k\in\Theta_{n_k,\alpha}(z_k)\) such that
\[
 \|\mu_{\theta_k}-\mu_{\theta'_k}\|_{L^\infty(I)}\ge\epsilon.
\]
We construct convergent subsequences and identify their limits.

First, \(a_{f,n},a_{q,n}\to0\), since \(K_n\to\infty\) and \(\beta>1/2\). For any feasible density spline \(f\), write \(f=f_+-f_-\). The constraints \(f\ge-a_{f,n}\) and \(|\int_{\mathcal A}f-1|\le2B_Aa_{f,n}\) give
\[
 \int_{\mathcal A}f_-\le2B_Aa_{f,n},
 \qquad
 \int_{\mathcal A}f_+\le1+4B_Aa_{f,n},
 \qquad
 \|f\|_{L^1(\mathcal A)}\le1+6B_Aa_{f,n}.
\]
The marked envelope similarly gives
\[
 \|q\|_{L^1(\mathcal A)}
 \le B_Y(1+2B_Aa_{f,n})+2B_A(a_{q,n}+B_Ya_{f,n}).
\]
The new continuum constraints and \(\mathrm{lem{:}sobolev\text{-}modulus\text{-}control}\) imply asymptotic equicontinuity: for each \(\varepsilon>0\), one may first choose \(r>0\) so that both \(\Omega_{R_f}(r)\) and \(\Omega_{R_q}(r)\) are below \(\varepsilon/2\), and then take \(k\) large enough that \(2a_{f,n_k}\) and \(2a_{q,n_k}\) are below \(\varepsilon/2\). The uniform \(L^1\) bounds give a uniformly bounded value at some point of \(\mathcal A\), and the common modulus transports that bound to all points. Thus both signal sequences are uniformly bounded and asymptotically equicontinuous. A finite-mesh diagonal argument yields, simultaneously over the finite set \(\mathcal X\), uniform limits
\[
 f_{x,k}\to f_x,\quad q_{x,k}\to q_x,
 \qquad
 f'_{x,k}\to f'_x,\quad q'_{x,k}\to q'_x
 \quad\text{in }C(\mathcal A).
\]
Passing to the defining inequalities gives
\[
 f_x,f'_x\ge0,\quad \int_{\mathcal A}f_x=\int_{\mathcal A}f'_x=1,
 \quad f_x,f'_x\ge\kappa\text{ on }I,
 \quad |q_x|\le B_Yf_x,\quad |q'_x|\le B_Yf'_x.
\]

Next represent the four candidate error characteristic functions by their atomic laws \(Q_{j,k}\) and \(Q'_{j,k}\). Their common \(q_e\)-moment bound implies
\[
 Q_{j,k}(|u|>R)\le M_eR^{-q_e}
\]
and the same inequality for the primed laws. Diagonal extraction of the distribution functions at rational arguments, followed by rational sandwiching and the common tail bound, produces weakly convergent subsequences \(Q_{j,k}\Rightarrow Q_j\) and \(Q'_{j,k}\Rightarrow Q'_j\). Applying weak convergence first to \(|u|^{q_e}\wedge L\), and then letting \(L\to\infty\), preserves the moment bound. Moreover, since \(q_e>1\),
\[
 \int_{|u|>R}|u|\,dQ_{j,k}(u)\le M_eR^{1-q_e}\longrightarrow0
\]
uniformly in \(k\), and likewise for the other sequences and their limits. Hence first moments converge. Because the second-coordinate sieve imposes
\[
 \left|\int u\,dQ_{2,k}(u)\right|
 \le2M_eH_{n_k}^{1-q_e},
\]
and \(H_n\to\infty\), the limiting second-coordinate laws satisfy \(\int u\,dQ_2=\int u\,dQ'_2=0\). The power-mean bound \(\int|u|dQ_{j,k}\le M_e^{1/q_e}\) makes all characteristic functions uniformly Lipschitz. Pointwise convergence under weak convergence and a finite-mesh argument therefore give locally uniform convergence of the four characteristic functions.

The mass vectors lie in the compact simplex, so take further subsequences with limits \(p\) and \(p'\). Since both vectors belong to the same \(\mathcal P_{n_k,\alpha}\),
\[
 \max_x|p_{x,k}-p'_{x,k}|
 \le2\left\{\frac{\log(6|\mathcal X|/\alpha)}{2n_k}\right\}^{1/2}
 \longrightarrow0,
\]
and therefore \(p=p'\).

For a tuple \(\theta\), abbreviate its fitted channels by
\[
 F_{x,\theta}(s,t)=\widehat f_x(s+t)\phi_1(s)\phi_2(t),
 \qquad
 G_{x,\theta}(s,t)=\widehat q_x(s+t)\phi_1(s)\phi_2(t).
\]
The preceding uniform \(L^1\) bounds, compact signal support, and uniform first-error moments make these fitted channels uniformly equicontinuous on \(\mathbb R^2\). The grid has mesh \((B_AK_n)^{-1}\to0\) and range \(T_n=K_n/B_A\to\infty\). Furthermore,
\[
 |\mathcal D_n|=(2K_n^2+1)^2,
 \qquad \lambda_n=O(\log K_n)=o(n).
\]
On the regular-count samples,
\[
 \max_x\rho^F_{x,n_k}
 \le2\left(\frac{2\lambda_{n_k}}{n_kp_{\min}}\right)^{1/2}=o(1),
 \qquad
 \max_x\rho^G_{x,n_k}\le B_Y\max_x\rho^F_{x,n_k}=o(1).
\]
Also \(\delta^F_n,\delta^G_n\to0\), because \(a_{f,n},a_{q,n}\to0\) and \(\tau_{e,n}=2(\lambda_n/n)^{1/2}\to0\). Feasibility of both tuples around the same empirical channels consequently gives at every grid point
\[
 |F_{x,\theta_k}-F_{x,\theta'_k}|
 \le2(\rho^F_{x,n_k}+\delta^F_{n_k})=o(1),
 \qquad
 |G_{x,\theta_k}-G_{x,\theta'_k}|
 \le2(\rho^G_{x,n_k}+\delta^G_{n_k})=o(1).
\]
Uniform signal convergence also gives, for every real \(r\),
\[
 |\widehat f_{x,k}(r)-\widehat f_x(r)|
 \le2B_A\|f_{x,k}-f_x\|_{L^\infty(\mathcal A)},
 \qquad
 |\widehat q_{x,k}(r)-\widehat q_x(r)|
 \le2B_A\|q_{x,k}-q_x\|_{L^\infty(\mathcal A)},
\]
and the same bounds hold for the primed sequence. Together with locally uniform convergence of the error characteristic functions, these inequalities give locally uniform convergence of both fitted channels. Choose nearest grid points to any fixed \((s,t)\), use uniform equicontinuity, and pass to these locally uniform limits. The two limiting tuples have identical probability and marked channels for every \(x\in\mathcal X\) and all \(s,t\in\mathbb R\).

It remains to identify the limiting signals. By \(\mathrm{lem{:}moment\text{-}derived\text{-}local\text{-}modulus}\), each limiting error characteristic function has modulus at least \(1/2\) on \([-\nu_*,\nu_*]\). Since each limiting \(f_x\) is a probability density supported on \(\mathcal A\),
\[
 |\widehat f_x(u)-1|\le B_A|u|,
 \qquad
 |\widehat f'_x(u)-1|\le B_A|u|.
\]
Thus, on a common nonempty neighborhood of zero, every factor in both probability channels is nonzero. Finite first moments and the established centering give \(\phi_{Q_2}'(0)=\phi_{Q'_2}'(0)=0\). Differentiating the equality of the probability channels in the second frequency at \((u,0)\), dividing by the nonzero common value, and using centering yields
\[
 \frac{\widehat f_x'(u)}{\widehat f_x(u)}
 =\frac{\bigl(\widehat{f'_x}\bigr)'(u)}{\widehat{f'_x}(u)}.
\]
The ratio \(\widehat f_x/\widehat{f'_x}\) has derivative zero and equals one at zero. Hence the two transforms agree locally. Compact support makes both transforms entire; their difference vanishes on a real interval, so every Taylor coefficient at an interior point is zero and the transforms agree on all of \(\mathbb C\). To verify Fourier injectivity without an additional premise, put \(h=f_x-f'_x\) and let \(\gamma_\sigma\) be the centered Gaussian density with variance \(\sigma^2\). Fubini's theorem and the Gaussian integral give
\[
 (h*\gamma_\sigma)(a)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}
 e^{-ita}\widehat h(t)e^{-\sigma^2t^2/2}\,dt=0.
\]
Translation continuity in \(L^1\) gives
\[
 \|h*\gamma_\sigma-h\|_1
 \le\int_{\mathbb R}\gamma_\sigma(y)
       \|h(\,\cdot-y)-h\|_1\,dy\longrightarrow0
 \qquad(\sigma\downarrow0).
\]
Therefore \(f_x=f'_x\) almost everywhere and, by continuity on \(I\), pointwise there.

On the same neighborhood, equality of both channels gives
\[
 \widehat q_x(u)
 =\widehat f_x(u)\frac{G_{x,\theta}(u,0)}{F_{x,\theta}(u,0)}
 =\widehat{f'_x}(u)\frac{G_{x,\theta'}(u,0)}{F_{x,\theta'}(u,0)}
 =\widehat{q'_x}(u).
\]
Both signed functions are integrable and compactly supported. Applying the same entire-continuation argument to \(\widehat q_x-\widehat{q'_x}\), followed by the displayed Gaussian-convolution calculation with \(h=q_x-q'_x\), gives \(q_x=q'_x\) almost everywhere and hence pointwise on \(I\). Together with \(p=p'\), this identifies the two limiting target functions.

Finally, the uniform signal convergence, mass convergence, and the uniform lower bound \(f_{x,k},f'_{x,k}\ge\kappa/2\) on \(I\) imply
\[
 \mu_{\theta_k}\to\sum_xp_xq_x/f_x,
 \qquad
 \mu_{\theta'_k}\to\sum_xp_xq_x/f_x
 \quad\text{in }L^\infty(I).
\]
This contradicts their separation by \(\epsilon\). Hence \(\widetilde\omega_n\to0\). The contradiction proof is qualitative and produces no numerical inverse modulus or \(O(r_n)\) rate.

%%% FIELD honest-upper-proof
Fix \(n\) at least as large as the deterministic threshold in \(\mathrm{lem{:}analytic\text{-}sieve\text{-}stability}\). At fixed \(n\), every coefficient, weight, and atom vector lives in a finite-dimensional Euclidean space. For \(f\in\mathscr F_n\), the negative-part and integral constraints give
\[
 \|f\|_{L^1(\mathcal A)}\le1+6B_Aa_{f,n}.
\]
For \(q\in\mathscr Q_n(f)\), the marked envelope gives
\[
 \|q\|_{L^1(\mathcal A)}
 \le B_Y(1+2B_Aa_{f,n})+2B_A(a_{q,n}+B_Ya_{f,n}).
\]
Equivalence of norms on each fixed finite-dimensional spline space turns these bounds into coefficient bounds. Every continuum constraint, including each Sobolev-modulus inequality at a fixed pair \((a,b)\), is closed in the coefficients; an arbitrary intersection of these closed sets is closed. The error-law constraints are closed subsets of the product of a compact probability simplex and \([-H_n,H_n]^{M_n}\). Thus all deterministic constraints form a compact parameter set \(K_n^\star\). Every density coordinate satisfies
\[
 \inf_{a\in I}f_x(a)\ge\kappa/2,
\]
so \((a,\theta)\mapsto\mu_\theta(a)\) is continuous and finite on \(I\times K_n^\star\).

For a sample \(z\), let \(h_n(z,\theta)\) be the maximum of zero and the positive parts of all mass and channel constraint violations, with the deterministic constraints absorbed into \(K_n^\star\). Then \(h_n\) is measurable in \(z\), continuous in \(\theta\), and
\[
 \Theta_{n,\alpha}(z)=\{\theta\in K_n^\star:h_n(z,\theta)=0\}.
\]
If \(K_n^\star\) is empty, the prescribed endpoints are zero. Otherwise choose a countable dense subset \(K_{n,0}^\star\). Continuity gives
\[
 d_n(z):=\inf_{\theta\in K_n^\star}h_n(z,\theta)
 =\inf_{\theta\in K_{n,0}^\star}h_n(z,\theta),
\]
so \(d_n\) is measurable; compactness shows that the feasible set is nonempty exactly when \(d_n=0\). For positive integers \(k\), set
\[
 V_{n,k}^+(z,a)=\sup_{\theta\in K_n^\star}
 \{\mu_\theta(a)-kh_n(z,\theta)\},
 \qquad
 V_{n,k}^-(z,a)=\sup_{\theta\in K_n^\star}
 \{-\mu_\theta(a)-kh_n(z,\theta)\}.
\]
Each supremum equals the supremum over \(K_{n,0}^\star\), hence is jointly measurable. On \(\{d_n=0\}\), compactness gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 V_{n,k}^+(z,a)&\downarrow
 \sup_{\theta\in\Theta_{n,\alpha}(z)}\mu_\theta(a),\\
 V_{n,k}^-(z,a)&\downarrow
 -\inf_{\theta\in\Theta_{n,\alpha}(z)}\mu_\theta(a).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Indeed, comparison with a feasible point forces a maximizing sequence's violation to be \(O(k^{-1})\), every convergent subsequence then has a feasible limit, and feasible points give the reverse inequalities. Together with the empty-set convention, this proves joint sample-and-index measurability of the extrema and of \(L_n,U_n\). Uniform continuity on the compact product \(I\times\Theta_{n,\alpha}(z)\) makes the profiled extrema continuous in \(a\), and therefore \(W_n\) is measurable because its supremum may be taken over a fixed countable dense subset of \(I\).

The stability lemma gives for every sample
\[
 L_n(a)\le U_n(a)\quad(a\in I),
 \qquad0\le W_n\le2B_Y.
\]
Fix \(P\in\mathcal M\). On \(\mathcal E_{n,\alpha}\), that lemma supplies \(\bar\theta_n\in\Theta_{n,\alpha}\) satisfying
\[
 \sup_{a\in I}|\mu_{\bar\theta_n}(a)-\mu(a)|\le b_{\mu,n}.
\]
The structural response takes values in \([-B_Y,B_Y]\), so \(-B_Y\le\mu(a)\le B_Y\). Feasibility then implies
\[
 \inf_{\theta\in\Theta_{n,\alpha}}\mu_\theta(a)-b_{\mu,n}\le\mu(a),
 \qquad
 \sup_{\theta\in\Theta_{n,\alpha}}\mu_\theta(a)+b_{\mu,n}\ge\mu(a).
\]
Clipping preserves both inequalities. Consequently,
\[
 \mathcal E_{n,\alpha}\subseteq
 \{L_n(a)\le\mu(a)\le U_n(a)\text{ for every }a\in I\}.
\]
Taking probabilities and using the uniform event bound proves
\[
 \inf_{P\in\mathcal M}P_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\otimes n}
 \{L_n(a)\le\mu(a)\le U_n(a)\text{ for every }a\in I\}
 \ge1-\alpha.
\]
Joint measurability, endpoint ordering, and this coverage statement verify \(\mathcal C_n\in\mathfrak B_{n,\alpha}\). Finally, \(0\le W_n\le2B_Y\) gives
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M}E_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\otimes n}}[W_n]\le2B_Y.
\]
The threshold is uniform because it depends only on \(\alpha\) and the fixed class indices.

%%% FIELD finite-sieve-consistent-theorem-statement
There exists \(n_0<\infty\), depending only on \(\alpha\) and the fixed class indices, such that for every \(n\ge n_0\) the finite construction \(\mathfrak H^{\mathrm{up}}\) yields a band \(\mathcal C_n\in\mathfrak B_{n,\alpha}\) with jointly sample-and-index measurable finite endpoints, simultaneous coverage at least \(1-\alpha\) uniformly over \(\mathcal M\), and \(0\le W_n\le2B_Y\) almost surely. Moreover,
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M}E_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\otimes n}}[W_n]
 \le \widetilde\omega_n+2b_{\mu,n}
   +2B_Y|\mathcal X|e^{-np_{\min}/8}=o(1).
\]
The conclusion is qualitative and gives neither an explicit rate, an \(O(r_n)\) bound, nor a minimax claim.

%%% FIELD finite-sieve-consistent-theorem-proof
All measurability, ordering, clipping, honesty, and coverage assertions follow from \(\mathrm{thm{:}honest\text{-}upper}\). Let
\[
 \mathcal R_n=\{\min_{x\in\mathcal X}n_x\ge np_{\min}/2\}.
\]
On \(\mathcal R_n\), an empty feasible set gives \(W_n=0\). If the feasible set is nonempty, put
\[
 \ell(a)=\inf_{\theta\in\Theta_{n,\alpha}}\mu_\theta(a),
 \qquad
 u(a)=\sup_{\theta\in\Theta_{n,\alpha}}\mu_\theta(a).
\]
Since \(L_n(a)\ge\ell(a)-b_{\mu,n}\) and \(U_n(a)\le u(a)+b_{\mu,n}\),
\[
 U_n(a)-L_n(a)\le u(a)-\ell(a)+2b_{\mu,n}.
\]
Taking the supremum over \(a\in I\) and using \(\mathrm{def{:}finite\text{-}sieve\text{-}diameter}\) yields
\[
 W_n\mathbf1_{\mathcal R_n}
 \le\widetilde\omega_n+2b_{\mu,n}.
\]
On \(\mathcal R_n^c\), \(\mathrm{thm{:}honest\text{-}upper}\) gives \(W_n\le2B_Y\). The count inequality in \(\mathrm{lem{:}mass\text{-}confidence}\) therefore gives, uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal M\),
\[
 E_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\otimes n}}[W_n]
 \le\widetilde\omega_n+2b_{\mu,n}
   +2B_Y|\mathcal X|e^{-np_{\min}/8}.
\]
By \(\mathrm{lem{:}finite\text{-}sieve\text{-}diameter}\), \(\widetilde\omega_n\to0\). Also \(b_{\mu,n}\to0\), because its definition is a fixed positive linear combination of \(K_n^{1/2-\beta}\) terms and \(\beta>1/2\). The exponential remainder tends to zero. Hence the displayed deterministic bound is \(o(1)\). No step supplies a quantitative rate for \(\widetilde\omega_n\), so no \(O(r_n)\) or minimax conclusion follows.
