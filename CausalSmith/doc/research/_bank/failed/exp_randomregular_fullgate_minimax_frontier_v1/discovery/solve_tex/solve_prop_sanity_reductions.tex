Assume first the fixed-degree regime: there is an integer \(d\ge 3\) such that \(d_n=d\) at every admissible stage.  By Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}frontier\mbox{-}rate}\),
\[
r(n,d_n)=r(n,d)=\frac{d^2}{n\log(d+1)}
=\frac{d^2}{\log(d+1)}n^{-1}.
\]
Because \(d\ge3\), the multiplier \(d^2/\log(d+1)\) is finite and strictly positive.  Hence \(r(n,d_n)=\Theta(n^{-1})\).  (The same conclusion holds for any bounded sequence with \(3\le d_n\le D<\infty\), since the multiplier \(d_n^2/\log(d_n+1)\) then ranges over a finite set of positive numbers.)

The already established Theorem~\(\mathrm{thm{:}matched\mbox{-}frontier}\) supplies universal constants \(0<c<C<\infty\) such that, along the admissible stages,
\[
\Pr\!\left\{
\frac{c d^2}{\log(d+1)}n^{-1}
\le R(G_n)\le C d^2 n^{-1}
\right\}\longrightarrow1.
\]
The two coefficients multiplying \(n^{-1}\) are fixed, finite, and strictly positive.  Thus the displayed conclusion is exactly \(R(G_n)=\Theta_{\Pr}(n^{-1})\), which is the asserted parametric mean-squared-error reduction.

For the graph identity, fix a finite simple undirected \(d_n\)-regular graph \(G\) of girth at least five.  Write
\[
A=A_G,\qquad B=B_2(G),\qquad d=d_n,
\qquad P=P_n=I_n-\frac{\iota_n\iota_n^{\mathsf T}}{n}.
\]
For distinct vertices \(i,j\in V_n\), matrix multiplication gives
\[
(A^2)_{ij}=\sum_{v\in V_n}A_{iv}A_{vj},
\]
so \((A^2)_{ij}\) is the number of common neighbors of \(i\) and \(j\).  If \(A_{ij}=1\), a common neighbor would form a triangle with \(i\) and \(j\); the girth condition therefore gives
\[
i\ne j,\quad A_{ij}=1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad (A^2)_{ij}=0.
\]
If \(A_{ij}=0\), two distinct common neighbors would form a cycle of length four.  Hence there is at most one common neighbor, while there is one precisely when \(\operatorname{dist}_G(i,j)=2\).  Consequently,
\[
i\ne j,\quad A_{ij}=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
(A^2)_{ij}=\mathbf 1\{\operatorname{dist}_G(i,j)=2\}.
\]
These two implications and the definition
\(B_{ij}=\mathbf 1\{\operatorname{dist}_G(i,j)\le2\}\) show that, for \(i\ne j\),
\[
B_{ij}=(A^2+A)_{ij}.
\]
On the diagonal, simplicity gives \(A_{ii}=0\), and symmetry and \(d\)-regularity give
\[
(A^2)_{ii}=\sum_{v\in V_n}A_{iv}A_{vi}
=\sum_{v\in V_n}A_{iv}=d.
\]
Since \(B_{ii}=1\), comparison of the diagonal and off-diagonal entries proves
\[
B_2(G)=A_G^2+A_G-(d_n-1)I_n.
\tag{1}
\]

It remains to establish the claimed centered norm implication.  Regularity gives
\(A\iota_n=d\iota_n\); symmetry then also gives
\(\iota_n^{\mathsf T}A=d\iota_n^{\mathsf T}\).  Therefore
\[
AP=A-d\frac{\iota_n\iota_n^{\mathsf T}}n=PA,
\]
so \(A\) and \(P\) commute.  Direct calculation using
\(\iota_n^{\mathsf T}\iota_n=n\) gives \(P^{\mathsf T}=P\) and \(P^2=P\); thus \(P\) is an orthogonal projection and \(\|P\|_{\mathrm{op}}=1\).  Commutation and idempotence further yield
\[
(AP)^2=APAP=A^2P^2=A^2P.
\tag{2}
\]
Multiplying (1) on the right by \(P\), and then applying the triangle inequality, submultiplicativity of the Euclidean operator norm, (2), and \(\|P\|_{\mathrm{op}}=1\), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|B_2(G)P_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}
&\le \|A^2P\|_{\mathrm{op}}+\|AP\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 +(d-1)\|P\|_{\mathrm{op}}\\
&\le \|AP\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2+\|AP\|_{\mathrm{op}}+(d-1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
Suppose now, as in the proposition, that along the graph sequence there is a finite constant \(K\) for which
\(\|A_GP_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le K\sqrt{d_n}\) at every sufficiently large stage.  Since \(d_n\ge3\), (3) implies
\[
\|B_2(G)P_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le K^2d_n+K\sqrt{d_n}+d_n-1
\le (K^2+K+1)d_n.
\]
Hence \(\|B_2(G)P_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}=O(d_n)\).  Because \(d_n\ge3\) also implies \(d_n\le d_n^{7/4}\), this is stronger than the \(O(d_n^{7/4})\) sufficient scale posed in \(\mathrm{oeq{:}boolean\mbox{-}operator}\), albeit only on the stated high-girth subclass.  Thus it is a strict special-case reduction and does not assume or resolve the open Boolean-collision bound for general random regular graphs.
