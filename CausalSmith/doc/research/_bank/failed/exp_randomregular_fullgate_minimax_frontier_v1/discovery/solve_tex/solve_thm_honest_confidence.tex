Fix distinct \(i,j\).  By the definition of matrix multiplication, \(c_{ij}=(A_G^2)_{ij}\) is the number of common neighbors of \(i\) and \(j\); in particular, \(c_{ij}\) is an integer in \(\{0,\ldots,d_n\}\).  If \(A_{G,ij}=1\), then \(\operatorname{dist}_G(i,j)=1\), and both sides of the asserted off-diagonal identity equal one.  If \(A_{G,ij}=0\), then \(\operatorname{dist}_G(i,j)\le2\) holds exactly when \(c_{ij}\ge1\).  The binomial theorem gives, for every integer \(c\in\{0,\ldots,d_n\}\),
\[
\mathbf 1\{c\ge1\}=1-(1-1)^c
=\sum_{r=1}^{c}(-1)^{r+1}\binom cr
=\sum_{r=1}^{d_n}(-1)^{r+1}\binom cr,
\]
where \(\binom cr=0\) for \(r>c\).  Substitution of \(c=c_{ij}\) proves the off-diagonal formula. The summation index ranges up to \(d_n\), proving the last assertion.

Consequently, there is a finite constant \(C_{\mathrm{rob}}\), depending only on the fixed \(c_0\), such that every sequence satisfying \(d_n\ge3\) and \(d_n/\sqrt n\to0\) obeys, for all sufficiently large \(n\) and every simple \(d_n\)-regular \(G\),

\qquad

\qquad

Moreover, for every fixed \(L<\infty\), if

satisfies \(\mathcal U_G(k_n^\sharp)\le C_L r(n,d_n)\) for all sufficiently large \(n\), where \(C_L<\infty\) depends only on \(L\). Put \(S_i=\{v\in V_n:\operatorname{dist}_G(i,v)\le2\}\), \(m_i=|S_i|\), and \(q_i=1-m_i/n\).  A radius-two ball in a simple \(d_n\)-regular graph contains at most
\[
1+d_n+d_n(d_n-1)=d_n^2+1=M_n
\tag{1}
\]
vertices.  Thus \(M_n<n\) implies \(q_i\ge1-M_n/n>0\) for every \(i\), which justifies every inverse probability below.

Fix an integer \(s\ge1\), and let \(J_1,\ldots,J_s\) be independent uniform vertices.  If \(J_\ell=i\), then all vertices in \(N_G[i]\) are treated, so arbitrary neighborhood interference gives \(y_i(Z)=a_i\).  Hence the raw seed average
\[
\widetilde\mu_{1,s}=\frac1s\sum_{\ell=1}^s a_{J_\ell}
\]
is observable after retaining the seeds and satisfies
\[
\mathbb E(\widetilde\mu_{1,s}\mid G)=\mu_1,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(\widetilde\mu_{1,s}\mid G)
=\frac1s\left\{\frac1n\sum_i a_i^2-\mu_1^2\right\}\le\frac1s
\tag{2}
\]
by the treated endpoint second-moment bound.  Conditional on the union assignment, the Rao--Blackwell weights in Definition~\(\mathrm{def{:}rb\mbox{-}estimator}\) give
\[
\widehat\mu_{1,s}
=\mathbb E(\widetilde\mu_{1,s}\mid Z,G)
=\sum_i w_{i,s}(Z;G)y_i(Z).
\]
Indeed, whenever the conditional multiplicity of \(i\) is positive, \(i\) is fully treated-exposed under that union assignment, so the displayed observed outcome equals \(a_i\).  The tower property and the law of total variance applied to (2) therefore yield
\[
\mathbb E(\widehat\mu_{1,s}\mid G)=\mu_1,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(\widehat\mu_{1,s}\mid G)\le s^{-1}.
\tag{3}
\]

Unit \(i\) is fully control-exposed exactly when every seed avoids \(S_i\).  Therefore its exposure probability is \(q_i^s>0\), and arbitrary neighborhood interference makes the control Horvitz--Thompson mean
\[
\widehat\mu_{0,s}=\frac1n\sum_i
\frac{\mathbf 1\{i\in C_G(Z)\}b_i}{q_i^s}
\]
observable and unbiased for \(\mu_0\).  Write \(H=B_2(G)B_2(G)^{\mathsf T}\), so \(H_{ij}=|S_i\cap S_j|\).  One seed avoids \(S_i\cup S_j\) with probability
\[
q_{ij}=1-\frac{m_i+m_j-H_{ij}}n.
\]
Since \(q_iq_j=(1-m_i/n)(1-m_j/n)\), direct subtraction gives
\[
\frac{q_{ij}}{q_iq_j}-1
=\frac{n^{-1}H_{ij}-n^{-2}m_im_j}{q_iq_j}
=K_{G,ij},
\tag{4}
\]
where the last equality follows from \(B_2(G)\iota_n=(m_i)_i\) and the definition of \(P_n\).  Independence of the \(s\) seeds now gives the normalized control-exposure covariance matrix
\[
Q^{(s)}_{G,ij}
=\frac{q_{ij}^s}{q_i^sq_j^s}-1
=(1+K_{G,ij})^s-1
=\sum_{r=1}^s\binom sr K_{G,ij}^r.
\tag{5}
\]

The matrix \(K_G\) is positive semidefinite because it is the Gram matrix
\[
K_G=\frac1n
\{D_q(G)^{-1}B_2(G)P_n\}
\{D_q(G)^{-1}B_2(G)P_n\}^{\mathsf T}.
\tag{6}
\]
If \(K_{G,ij}=\langle u_i,u_j\rangle\), then
\(K_{G,ij}^r=\langle u_i^{\otimes r},u_j^{\otimes r}\rangle\); hence every Schur power in (5), and therefore \(Q_G^{(s)}\), is positive semidefinite.  Moreover,
\[
K_{G,ii}=\frac{(m_i/n)(1-m_i/n)}{q_i^2}
=\frac{m_i/n}{1-m_i/n}
\le\delta:=\frac{M_n}{n(1-M_n/n)}.
\tag{7}
\]
For completeness, if \(D\succeq0\), writing \(D_{ij}=\sum_t v_{ti}v_{tj}\) shows
\(D\circ H=\sum_t\operatorname{diag}(v_t)H\operatorname{diag}(v_t)\).  Thus for symmetric \(H\),
\[
\|D\circ H\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le\max_iD_{ii}\,\|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}.
\tag{8}
\]
Apply (8) with \(D=K_G^{\circ(r-1)}\) and \(H=K_G\), using (7), to obtain
\(\|K_G^{\circ r}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\delta^{r-1}\|K_G\|_{\mathrm{op}}\).  Also (6), \(q_i\ge1-M_n/n\), and \(\|P_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}=1\) imply
\[
\|K_G\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le\frac{\|B_2(G)P_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2}
{n(1-M_n/n)^2}.
\tag{9}
\]
Summing (5), then using (7)--(9) and \(1+\delta=(1-M_n/n)^{-1}\), gives
\[
\lambda_{\max}(Q_G^{(s)})
\le
\frac{\|B_2(G)P_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2}
{M_n(1-M_n/n)}
\{(1-M_n/n)^{-s}-1\}.
\tag{10}
\]
The control endpoint bound gives \(\|b\|_2^2\le n\), so
\[
\operatorname{Var}(\widehat\mu_{0,s}\mid G)
=n^{-2}b^{\mathsf T}Q_G^{(s)}b
\le
\frac{\|B_2(G)P_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2
\{(1-M_n/n)^{-s}-1\}}
{nM_n(1-M_n/n)}.
\tag{11}
\]
Both endpoint estimators are unbiased.  Combining (3) and (11) with
\(\operatorname{Var}(X-Y)\le2\operatorname{Var}(X)+2\operatorname{Var}(Y)\) proves the finite bound by \(\mathcal U_G(s)\).

We next specialize.  Put \(x=M_n/n\) and \(h=-\log(1-x)\).  The degree-growth assumption and \(d_n\ge3\) imply
\[
x=\frac{d_n^2+1}{n}\le\frac{10}{9}\frac{d_n^2}{n}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{12}
\]
Consequently, eventually \(x\le1/2\), \(h\le2x\), and \(c_0/h\ge2\).  On this eventual range the maximum in the definition of \(\widetilde k_n\) is inactive and
\[
\widetilde k_n=\left\lfloor\frac{c_0}{h}\right\rfloor
\ge\frac{c_0}{2h},
\qquad
\widetilde k_nh\le c_0.
\tag{13}
\]
The matrix \(B_2(G)\) is symmetric and nonnegative, and (1) bounds all its row and column sums by \(M_n\).  Hence
\[
\|B_2(G)P_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le\|B_2(G)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le\sqrt{\|B_2(G)\|_1\|B_2(G)\|_\infty}
\le M_n.
\tag{14}
\]
Equations (13)--(14) yield
\[
\frac{2}{\widetilde k_n}\le\frac{8M_n}{c_0n},
\qquad
(1-x)^{-\widetilde k_n}-1=e^{\widetilde k_nh}-1\le e^{c_0}-1,
\]
and therefore
\[
\mathcal U_G(\widetilde k_n)
\le\left\{\frac8{c_0}+4(e^{c_0}-1)\right\}\frac{M_n}{n}
\le C_{\mathrm{rob}}\frac{d_n^2}{n},
\tag{15}
\]
where one may take
\(C_{\mathrm{rob}}=\frac{10}{9}\{8/c_0+4(e^{c_0}-1)\}\).  The selection rule gives
\[
U(G,k_n)=\mathcal U_G(k_n)\le\mathcal U_G(\widetilde k_n).
\]
The right side of (15) tends to zero, so eventually \(U(G,k_n)\le1\).  The certified procedure then selects the seed estimator, and its asserted risk bound follows from the finite part of the proof.

Finally suppose \(\|B_2(G)P_n\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le Ld_n^{7/4}\), and put
\(s=k_n^\sharp\).  By (12), eventually the quantity inside the floor is at least two.  Hence
\[
\frac2s\le\frac{32x}{\log(d_n+1)},
\qquad
sh\le\frac14\log(d_n+1),
\]
so that
\[
\mathcal U_G(s)
\le\frac{32M_n}{n\log(d_n+1)}
+\frac{4L^2d_n^{7/2}(d_n+1)^{1/4}}{nM_n}.
\tag{16}
\]
Here we used \(x\le1/2\).  Since \(M_n\ge d_n^2\), \(M_n\le(10/9)d_n^2\), and, for \(d_n\ge3\), both \((d_n+1)^{1/4}\le(4/3)^{1/4}d_n^{1/4}\) and \(\log(d_n+1)\le2d_n^{1/4}\), the right side of (16) is at most \(C_Ld_n^2/[n\log(d_n+1)]\).  This proves the conditional log-tuned bound.
The selection rule also gives
\[
U(G,k_n)\le\mathcal U_G(k_n^\sharp)\le C_Lr(n,d_n)
\]
on the stated norm event.

For either candidate count, there are finitely many ordered seed tuples, namely \(n^s\).  Enumerating them computes their union assignments, assignment masses, conditional multiplicities, all exposure probabilities, and all Rao--Blackwell weights.  The squared operator norm is the largest eigenvalue of the rational symmetric matrix \(B_2(G)P_nB_2(G)^{\mathsf T}\), hence is a real algebraic number.  Exact root isolation and algebraic sign comparison decide which of the two displayed certificate scores is smaller, with the stipulated deterministic tie break; rational interval enclosures certify the resulting numerical value to any requested accuracy.  Thus every claimed object is a finite graph computation.

The smallest admissible regular instance is \((n,d_n)=(4,3)\).  There \(M_n=10\ge n\), so the compiler forms no seed ratio and uses the point-mass all-treated design.  Its error is \(\mu_0\), and the endpoint bound gives \(\mu_0^2\le1\), with equality for \(b_i=1\) for all \(i\); thus the fallback certificate is exact on this enumerable instance even though the fallback estimator need not be unbiased for \(\tau_n\).  At the opposite boundary, every step in (12)--(15) uses only \(d_n^2/n\to0\).  No conclusion is asserted when \(d_n\) is proportional to \(\sqrt n\), where \(x\) need not vanish.

Consequently \(|\tau_n|=|\mu_1-\mu_0|\le2\), so intersecting an interval with \([-2,2]\) cannot remove the true estimand.
