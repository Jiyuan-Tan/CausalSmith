\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} Known disturbing probes reveal contractions of both input and output ports, not a passive observed law. On the finite canonical independent-edge-source bow, global identification is exactly the conjunction of treatment-row reachability and terminal-effect reachability. Success gives an explicit synthesis- and coefficient-invariant arithmetic functional of the accessible tensor and does not require positivity. Failure of either condition is witnessed inside the primitive-positive interior, with explicit cell floors and at most \(m\) shared-edge states. An explicit \(2m-1\)-rank menu identifies every atomic bow query over the full finite class without full \(m^2\)-dimensional tomography; its binary instance has rank three. Beyond the bow, the scope stops after one legal CADMG fixing step, where the independent-edge-source lift remains open.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Friend et al. recover mechanisms from marginally complete disturbing instruments in a strictly positive Markovian DAG regime, while ordinary ADMG fixing starts from an already valid passive-law kernel. Neither gives an exact query-identification criterion for a latent bow over the full finite canonical independent-edge-source class, nor an interior obstruction that remains valid for every intermediate ambient class.

\paragraph{Niche.} The project identifies the exact query-specific bow frontier uniformly over every class between the primitive-positive interior and the full finite canonical independent-edge-source class. It separates positivity-free linear identification from strictly positive nonidentification witnesses and locates the exact \(2m-1\) non-tomographic design rank.

\paragraph{Fill.} The project proves the class-indexed bow row-span equivalence, the dual-frame-invariant identifying functional, two explicit primitive-positive obstruction families with quantitative cell and latent-support bounds, and a finite-alphabet \(2m-1\)-rank attaining menu that identifies all atomic queries over the full finite class. It retains the one-fixing independent-edge-source lift as the sole open construction.

\section{Related work}

Friend, Kissinger, Spekkens, and Wolfe (2025, Definitions 8--9, Proposition 6, and Theorem 1) formalize separable disturbing instruments and recover Markovian mechanisms under marginal informational completeness. The present result concerns a different graph and target: it gives an exact class-indexed criterion for atomic interventions on a latent-confounded bow, valid up to the full finite canonical independent-edge-source class, and needs no positivity for its identifying functional. No theorem-class containment or tightening of Friend et al. is claimed. Richardson, Evans, Robins, and Shpitser (2023, Sections 2--4) define fixability and kernel fixing when a passive-law kernel is supplied; the coordinate proposition reduces to their one-step quotient, while causal realization of a probe-reconstructed algebraic certificate remains open. Shpitser and Pearl (2006, Theorems 1--2) motivate the common-fiber witness benchmark, and Kivva et al. (2022) motivate positivity-aware failure constructions; neither supplies the present disturbing-probe twins, which lie strictly inside the primitive-positive class and therefore obstruct every larger admissible ambient class. Duarte et al. (2024) and Zhang, Tian, and Bareinboim (2022) motivate finite response-type and support-reduction methods but do not supply the unresolved shared-mechanism independent-edge-source lift. Pearl (1995) supplies the ordinary front-door endpoint checked by the explicit eight-cell reconstruction. Full branch-map tomography is used only as a finite-dimensional boundary comparator.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:independent-sources]\label{ass:independent-sources}
The joint exogenous mass satisfies \(p_{\mathcal M}^{U}(u)=\prod_{s\in\mathcal S_G}p_s(u_s)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Canonical semi-Markovian independent-source factorization, \cite{ShpitserPearl2006}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:strict-source-positivity]\label{ass:strict-source-positivity}
For every \(s\in\mathcal S_G\) and \(u_s\in\mathcal U_s\), \(p_s(u_s)>0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Primitive source positivity is stronger than observed-law positivity and is satisfied by finite latent response-type models after arbitrarily small independent full-support contamination of each source.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:strict-mechanism-positivity]\label{ass:strict-mechanism-positivity}
Every admissible cell of every structural kernel \(f_v\) is strictly positive.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Primitive mechanism positivity is stronger than positivity of the observed law and covers randomized-response, misclassification, and noisy biological transition mechanisms with a positive cell floor.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:instrument-normalization]\label{ass:instrument-normalization}
For every \(v\in V\), \(j_v\in\mathcal J_v\), and \(a_v\in\mathcal A_v\), \(\sum_{b_v\in\mathcal B_{v,j_v}}\phi_{v,(j_v,b_v)}(a_v)=1\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Per-instrument normalization of a separable finite instrument, \cite{FriendKissingerSpekkensWolfe2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:marginal-effect-completeness]\label{ass:marginal-effect-completeness}
For every \(v\in V\), \(\operatorname{span}\{\phi_{v,\beta_v}:\beta_v\in\mathcal L_v\}=\mathbb R^{\mathcal A_v}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Marginal informational completeness on the tagged effect family, \cite{FriendKissingerSpekkensWolfe2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:marginal-state-completeness]\label{ass:marginal-state-completeness}
For every \(v\in V\), \(\operatorname{span}\{\psi_{v,\beta_v}:\beta_v\in\mathcal L_v\}=\mathbb R^{\mathcal A_v}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Marginal informational completeness on the tagged state family, \cite{FriendKissingerSpekkensWolfe2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:fixable-vertex]\label{ass:fixable-vertex}
The vertex \(w\) satisfies \(\operatorname{dis}_{\mathcal C}(w)\cap\operatorname{de}_{\mathcal C}(w)=\{w\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Fixable vertex in a conditional ADMG, \cite{RichardsonEvansRobinsShpitser2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{lemma}[lem:split-port-reduction]\label{lem:split-port-reduction}
For every \(\mathcal M\in\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{pp}}^{\mathrm{edge}}(G,\mathcal A)\) and normalized separable menu \(\mathcal I\), every accessible branch law equals its coordinate in \(\mathcal E_{\mathcal I}(\mathcal M)\), and every atomic intervention query equals the displayed contraction of the same \(W_{\mathcal M}\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(\mathcal M\in\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{pp}}^{\mathrm{edge}}(G,\mathcal A)\), a regime \(\rho=(j_v:v\in V)\), and a branch vector \(\boldsymbol b=(b_v:v\in V)\).  By \texttt{ass:independent-sources}, conditional on the source configuration \(u\), the structural part of the finite generative mass is
\[
 \prod_{s\in\mathcal S_G}p_s(u_s)
 \prod_{v\in V}f_v\!\left(a_v\mid
 z_{\operatorname{pa}_G(v)},u_v,u_{\operatorname{bi}_G(v)}\right).
\]
For the selected local branch \(\beta_v=(j_v,b_v)\), the separable kernel contributes
\[
 K_{v,\beta_v}(z_v,a_v)
 =\psi_{v,\beta_v}(z_v)\phi_{v,\beta_v}(a_v).
\]
Therefore finite marginalization over \(u,a,z\) gives the branch probability
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P_{\mathcal M,\rho}(\boldsymbol b)
 &=\sum_{u,a,z}
 \prod_{s\in\mathcal S_G}p_s(u_s)
 \prod_{v\in V}
 f_v\!\left(a_v\mid z_{\operatorname{pa}_G(v)},u_v,
 u_{\operatorname{bi}_G(v)}\right)
 \phi_{v,(j_v,b_v)}(a_v)\psi_{v,(j_v,b_v)}(z_v)\\
 &=\sum_{a,z}W_{\mathcal M}(a\mid z)
 \prod_{v\in V}\phi_{v,(j_v,b_v)}(a_v)
 \psi_{v,(j_v,b_v)}(z_v)\\
 &=q_\rho(\boldsymbol b).
\end{aligned}
\]
The second equality is exactly \texttt{def:split-port-kernel}, and the third is \texttt{def:probe-evidence-map}.  These numbers form a probability law: eliminate vertices in reverse topological order.  When a current sink \(v\) is eliminated, \(z_v\) occurs in no remaining child mechanism, and
\[
 \sum_{a_v,b_v,z_v}f_v(a_v\mid\cdots)
 \phi_{v,(j_v,b_v)}(a_v)\psi_{v,(j_v,b_v)}(z_v)
 =\sum_{a_v}f_v(a_v\mid\cdots)
 \sum_{b_v}\phi_{v,(j_v,b_v)}(a_v)=1.
\]
Here \(\sum_{z_v}\psi_{v,(j_v,b_v)}(z_v)=1\) because each \(\psi_{v,\beta_v}\) is a probability state, \(\sum_{b_v}\phi_{v,(j_v,b_v)}(a_v)=1\) by \texttt{ass:instrument-normalization}, and each \(f_v\) is a stochastic kernel.  Acyclicity of \(G\) makes the elimination terminate, and the source masses then sum to one.

It remains to verify that the causal target is a contraction of the same tensor.  For \(a\in\prod_v\mathcal A_v\), define the split input \(z^{x}(a)\) by \(z^{x}_v(a)=x_v\) for \(v\in X\) and \(z^{x}_v(a)=a_v\) for \(v\notin X\).  Under \(\operatorname{do}(X=x)\), every kernel at a vertex in \(X\) is removed.  Equivalently, one may insert that normalized kernel and sum its otherwise unused structural output \(a_v\), because \(\sum_{a_v}f_v(a_v\mid\cdots)=1\), even when its source is shared with another vertex.  Thus
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P_{\mathcal M}(Y=y\mid\operatorname{do}(X=x))
 &=\sum_{u,a_{V\setminus X}}\mathbf 1\{a_Y=y\}
 \prod_s p_s(u_s)\prod_{v\notin X}
 f_v\!\left(a_v\mid z^x_{\operatorname{pa}_G(v)}(a),
 u_v,u_{\operatorname{bi}_G(v)}\right)\\
 &=\sum_a\mathbf 1\{a_Y=y\}
 W_{\mathcal M}\!\left(a\mid z^x(a)\right)\\
 &=\sum_{a,z}W_{\mathcal M}(a\mid z)\mathbf 1\{a_Y=y\}
 \prod_{v\in X}\mathbf 1\{z_v=x_v\}
 \prod_{v\notin X}\mathbf 1\{z_v=a_v\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last display is \texttt{def:atomic-query-contraction}.  Hence both every accessible branch law and the atomic intervention query are the asserted contractions of \(W_{\mathcal M}\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The reduction supplies a single finite tensor on which both the probe coordinates and the intervention target are linear contractions. \textit{Gap.} Friend et al. use local process contractions for Markovian models; this lemma fixes the independent edge-source ADMG semantics needed for the bow and one-step contracts. \textit{Consumer.} Every bow oracle and the Ananke coordinate backend use this common tensor interface.

\begin{lemma}[lem:bow-twin-coordinate-sanity]\label{lem:bow-twin-coordinate-sanity}
The operator \(T\) is an invertible \(m^2\)-by-\(m^2\) block-diagonal matrix, with one block \(I/(2m)+\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}/(2m^2)\) for each \(z_{\mathrm b}\), and its inverse block is \(2mI-\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}\). Therefore the explicit coordinate definition of \(g\) satisfies \(Tg=h\), so \(L>0\), and \(B_y^{+}-B_y^{-}=2th\). For every branch pair, \([\mathcal E_{\mathcal I}(\mathcal M_h^{+})]_{\beta_X,\beta_Y}-[\mathcal E_{\mathcal I}(\mathcal M_h^{-})]_{\beta_X,\beta_Y}=2t(A_{\mathrm b}h)_{\beta_X}[\phi_{Y_{\mathrm b},\beta_Y}(y)-\phi_{Y_{\mathrm b},\beta_Y}(y^{\dagger})]=0\), whereas \(Q_{z^{\circ},y}(\mathcal M_h^{+})-Q_{z^{\circ},y}(\mathcal M_h^{-})=2t r_{z^{\circ}}^{\mathsf T}h\ne0\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a fixed input coordinate \(z_{\mathrm b}\), the corresponding block of \(T\) is
\[
 C=\frac{1}{2m}I+\frac{1}{2m^2}J,
 \qquad J=\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Since \(J^2=mJ\), direct multiplication gives
\[
 C(2mI-J)
 =I-\frac{1}{2m}J+\frac{1}{m}J-\frac{1}{2m}J=I,
\]
and the same computation in the reverse order gives \((2mI-J)C=I\).  Thus every block is invertible with inverse \(2mI-J\).  The Kronecker factor \(\mathbf 1\{z_{\mathrm b}=z'_{\mathrm b}\}\) in \texttt{def:bow-positive-twin-construction} makes \(T\) block diagonal with exactly \(m\) such blocks, so \(T\) is an invertible \(m^2\)-by-\(m^2\) matrix.

On the block indexed by \(z_{\mathrm b}\), the displayed definition of \(g\) is precisely
\[
 g(\,cdot\,,z_{\mathrm b})=(2mI-J)h(\,\cdot\,,z_{\mathrm b}).
\]
Consequently \(Tg=h\).  The condition \(r_{z^{\circ}}^{\mathsf T}h\ne0\) implies \(h\ne0\), and invertibility of \(T\) then implies \(g\ne0\).  Since the array is finite, \(L=\lVert g\rVert_\infty>0\), so \(t=1/(2kL)\) is well defined and positive.

For the distinguished outcome \(y\), the two outcome kernels differ by
\[
 f^+_{Y_{\mathrm b}}(y\mid z_{\mathrm b},u_{\mathrm b})
 -f^-_{Y_{\mathrm b}}(y\mid z_{\mathrm b},u_{\mathrm b})
 =2t g(u_{\mathrm b},z_{\mathrm b}).
\]
Using the uniform shared source and the common treatment kernel from \texttt{def:bow-positive-twin-construction}, the response-table difference is therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
 B_y^+(a_{\mathrm b},z_{\mathrm b})-B_y^-(a_{\mathrm b},z_{\mathrm b})
 &=2t\sum_{u_{\mathrm b}}
 \left(\frac{\mathbf 1\{a_{\mathrm b}=u_{\mathrm b}\}}{2m}
 +\frac{1}{2m^2}\right)g(u_{\mathrm b},z_{\mathrm b})\\
 &=2t(Tg)(a_{\mathrm b},z_{\mathrm b})
 =2t h(a_{\mathrm b},z_{\mathrm b}).
\end{aligned}
\]
The response-table difference at \(y^{\dagger}\) is the negative of this array, and it is zero at every other outcome cell.

Expand an arbitrary branch-pair evidence coordinate using \texttt{def:probe-evidence-map}.  The bow outcome mechanism does not depend on the terminal output port \(z_{\mathrm t}\), and \(\sum_{z_{\mathrm t}}\psi_{Y_{\mathrm b},\beta_Y}(z_{\mathrm t})=1\).  Hence the preceding two nonzero outcome differences yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &[\mathcal E_{\mathcal I}(\mathcal M_h^+)]_{\beta_X,\beta_Y}
 -[\mathcal E_{\mathcal I}(\mathcal M_h^-)]_{\beta_X,\beta_Y}\\
 &\quad=2t\sum_{a_{\mathrm b},z_{\mathrm b}}
 \phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(a_{\mathrm b})
 \psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(z_{\mathrm b})h(a_{\mathrm b},z_{\mathrm b})
 \bigl[\phi_{Y_{\mathrm b},\beta_Y}(y)
 -\phi_{Y_{\mathrm b},\beta_Y}(y^{\dagger})\bigr]\\
 &\quad=2t(A_{\mathrm b}h)_{\beta_X}
 \bigl[\phi_{Y_{\mathrm b},\beta_Y}(y)
 -\phi_{Y_{\mathrm b},\beta_Y}(y^{\dagger})\bigr]=0,
\end{aligned}
\]
where \(A_{\mathrm b}h=0\) follows from \(h\in\ker(A_{\mathrm b})\).  Finally, \texttt{def:atomic-query-contraction} sums the \(y\)-response table over \(a_{\mathrm b}\) at \(z_{\mathrm b}=z^{\circ}\).  Therefore
\[
 Q_{z^{\circ},y}(\mathcal M_h^+)-Q_{z^{\circ},y}(\mathcal M_h^-)
 =2t\sum_{a_{\mathrm b}}h(a_{\mathrm b},z^{\circ})
 =2t r_{z^{\circ}}^{\mathsf T}h\ne0.
\]
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This calculation makes the repaired dimensions and the exact evidence-null and query-separation identities auditable coordinate by coordinate. \textit{Gap.} Existing hedge constructions do not provide this typed \(m^2\)-coordinate disturbing-probe cancellation identity. \textit{Consumer.} Ananke can verify a failed bow certificate using two matrix products and the displayed nonzero scalar.

\begin{lemma}[lem:bow-positive-twins]\label{lem:bow-positive-twins}
In \(G_{\mathrm b}\), if \(r_{z^{\circ}}\notin\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b})\), choose \(h\in\ker(A_{\mathrm b})\) with \(r_{z^{\circ}}^{\mathsf T}h\ne0\). The construction \((\mathcal M_h^{+},\mathcal M_h^{-})\) gives two members of \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{pp}}^{\mathrm{edge}}(G_{\mathrm b},\mathcal A)\) with identical \(\mathcal E_{\mathcal I}\), unequal \(Q_{z^{\circ},y}\), exactly \(m\) states for the shared edge source, and primitive cell floor at least \(1/(2\max\{m,k\})\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Assume \(r_{z^{\circ}}\notin\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b})\), and take the stipulated \(h\in\ker(A_{\mathrm b})\) with \(r_{z^{\circ}}^{\mathsf T}h\neq0\).  By \(\texttt{lem:bow-twin-coordinate-sanity}\), \(L>0\), so \(t=1/(2kL)\) and every object in \(\texttt{def:bow-positive-twin-construction}\) are well defined.

The shared edge source has exactly \(m\) states, each of mass \(1/m>0\); every private source has one state of mass one.  Thus the source law factors as required by \(\texttt{ass:independent-sources}\) and every primitive source mass is strictly positive as required by \(\texttt{ass:strict-source-positivity}\).  For each \(u_{\mathrm b}\), the treatment kernel is normalized because
\[
\sum_{a_{\mathrm b}}\left(\frac12\mathbf 1\{a_{\mathrm b}=u_{\mathrm b}\}+\frac1{2m}\right)=\frac12+\frac12=1,
\]
and every one of its cells is at least \(1/(2m)>0\).  For the outcome kernel, the perturbations in the \(y\) and \(y^{\dagger}\) cells cancel, so its \(k\) cells sum to one.  Moreover,
\[
\lvert t g(u_{\mathrm b},z_{\mathrm b})\rvert\leq tL=\frac1{2k}.
\]
Consequently each modified outcome cell is at least \(1/k-1/(2k)=1/(2k)>0\), and every unmodified cell equals \(1/k\).  By \(\texttt{ass:strict-mechanism-positivity}\) and \(\texttt{def:primitive-positive-edge-scm-class}\),
\[
\mathcal M_h^+,\mathcal M_h^-\in
\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{pp}}^{\mathrm{edge}}(G_{\mathrm b},\mathcal A).
\]

The source masses and mechanism bounds give the common primitive-cell floor
\[
\min\left\{1,\frac1m,\frac1{2m},\frac1{2k}\right\}
=\frac1{2\max\{m,k\}}.
\]
Finally, \(\texttt{lem:bow-twin-coordinate-sanity}\) proves coordinate by coordinate that every branch-pair evidence difference is zero, because \(A_{\mathrm b}h=0\), whereas
\[
Q_{z^{\circ},y}(\mathcal M_h^+)-Q_{z^{\circ},y}(\mathcal M_h^-)
=2t r_{z^{\circ}}^{\mathsf T}h\neq0.
\]
Thus the two legal same-observed-alphabet SCMs have identical \(\mathcal E_{\mathcal I}\), unequal causal targets, the claimed latent-cardinality bound, and the claimed strictly positive floor.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The treatment-side failure witness is an interior pair of legal edge-source SCMs, not a signed array outside the causal model, and therefore obstructs every larger admissible ambient class. \textit{Gap.} Passive-law hedge constructions and observed-law positivity results do not preserve arbitrary disturbing-probe contractions while keeping every primitive source and mechanism cell positive. \textit{Consumer.} This pair is Ananke's executable negative certificate for every failed bow treatment-row test, with exactly \(m\) shared-edge states.

\begin{theorem}[thm:bow-rowspan-iff]\label{thm:bow-rowspan-iff}
For \(G_{\mathrm b}\), arbitrary normalized separable probes at both vertices, and every SCM class \(\mathfrak C\) satisfying
\[
\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{pp}}^{\mathrm{edge}}(G_{\mathrm b},\mathcal A)
\subseteq\mathfrak C
\subseteq\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{fin}}^{\mathrm{edge}}(G_{\mathrm b},\mathcal A),
\]
\(\operatorname{GID}_{\mathcal I}^{\mathfrak C}(Q_{z^{\circ},y})\) holds if and only if \(r_{z^{\circ}}\in\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b})\) and \(e_y\in\operatorname{span}\{\phi_{Y_{\mathrm b},\beta_Y}:\beta_Y\in\mathcal L_{Y_{\mathrm b}}\}\).  On success, any \(c\) satisfying \(c^{\mathsf T}A_{\mathrm b}=r_{z^{\circ}}^{\mathsf T}\) and any admissible effect-synthesis vector \(\mu^y\) give the synthesis- and coefficient-invariant functional
\[
Q_{z^{\circ},y}=\sum_{\beta_X\in\mathcal L_{X_{\mathrm b}}}c_{\beta_X}q^{\mathrm b}_{\beta_X}(y).
\]
If the treatment condition fails, an explicit treatment-null construction gives strictly positive same-evidence models in \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{pp}}^{\mathrm{edge}}\) with unequal queries, exactly \(m\) shared-edge source states, and primitive cell floor at least \(1/(2\max\{m,k\})\).  If the terminal condition fails, an explicit effect-null construction does so with singleton sources and primitive cell floor at least \(\min\{1/m,1/(2k)\}\).  Taking \(\mathfrak C=\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{pp}}^{\mathrm{edge}}(G_{\mathrm b},\mathcal A)\) recovers \(\operatorname{GID}_{\mathcal I}(Q_{z^{\circ},y})\), while taking \(\mathfrak C=\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{fin}}^{\mathrm{edge}}(G_{\mathrm b},\mathcal A)\) gives the full finite-class equivalence and functional.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put
\[
R_Y=\operatorname{span}\{\phi_{Y_{\mathrm b},\beta_Y}:\beta_Y\in\mathcal L_{Y_{\mathrm b}}\}.
\]
Fix a class \(\mathfrak C\) satisfying
\[
\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{pp}}^{\mathrm{edge}}(G_{\mathrm b},\mathcal A)
\subseteq\mathfrak C
\subseteq\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{fin}}^{\mathrm{edge}}(G_{\mathrm b},\mathcal A).
\]

Suppose first that \(r_{z^{\circ}}\in\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b})\) and \(e_y\in R_Y\).  The first membership supplies \(c\) with
\[
c^{\mathsf T}A_{\mathrm b}=r_{z^{\circ}}^{\mathsf T},
\]
and the second supplies an admissible \(\mu^y\) by \(\texttt{def:terminal-synthesis}\).  For every \(\mathcal M\in\mathfrak C\), the upper class inclusion gives \(\mathcal M\in\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{fin}}^{\mathrm{edge}}(G_{\mathrm b},\mathcal A)\).  Applying \(\texttt{lem:finite-edge-bow-contraction}\) yields
\[
Q_{z^{\circ},y}(\mathcal M)
=\sum_{\beta_X\in\mathcal L_{X_{\mathrm b}}}
c_{\beta_X}q^{\mathrm b}_{\beta_X}(y;\mu^y).
\]
By \(\texttt{def:terminally-synthesized-bow-response}\), every term on the right is an explicit finite linear combination of coordinates of \(\mathcal E_{\mathcal I}(\mathcal M)\).  The same lemma shows that the value is independent of the admissible choices of \(c\) and \(\mu^y\).  Therefore, if \(\mathcal M,\mathcal M'\in\mathfrak C\) have equal evidence, applying this same arithmetic functional to both tensors gives
\[
Q_{z^{\circ},y}(\mathcal M)=Q_{z^{\circ},y}(\mathcal M').
\]
By \(\texttt{def:class-indexed-global-probe-identification}\), this is \(\operatorname{GID}_{\mathcal I}^{\mathfrak C}(Q_{z^{\circ},y})\).  Notice that this direction used the upper class inclusion and stochastic normalization only, not either strict-positivity assumption.

For necessity of the treatment condition, suppose \(r_{z^{\circ}}\notin\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b})\).  Let \((w_i)_{i=1}^d\) be an orthonormal basis of \(\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b})\), with the sum below interpreted as zero if \(d=0\), and define
\[
h=r_{z^{\circ}}-\sum_{i=1}^d(w_i^{\mathsf T}r_{z^{\circ}})w_i.
\]
Then \(h\) is orthogonal to every row of \(A_{\mathrm b}\), so \(A_{\mathrm b}h=0\).  The failed membership gives \(h\neq0\), and orthogonality gives
\[
r_{z^{\circ}}^{\mathsf T}h=\lVert h\rVert_2^2>0.
\]
Thus \(h\in\ker(A_{\mathrm b})\) satisfies the premise of \(\texttt{lem:bow-positive-twins}\).  That lemma constructs \(\mathcal M_h^+,\mathcal M_h^-\in\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{pp}}^{\mathrm{edge}}(G_{\mathrm b},\mathcal A)\) with equal evidence and unequal queries, exactly \(m\) shared-edge source states, and primitive cell floor at least \(1/(2\max\{m,k\})\).  The lower class inclusion puts both witnesses in \(\mathfrak C\), so the implication defining \(\operatorname{GID}_{\mathcal I}^{\mathfrak C}\) fails.

For necessity of the terminal condition, suppose \(e_y\notin R_Y\).  The lemma \(\texttt{lem:bow-terminal-effect-positive-twins}\) supplies \(\mathcal M,\mathcal M'\in\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{pp}}^{\mathrm{edge}}(G_{\mathrm b},\mathcal A)\) with singleton exogenous alphabets, a common uniform treatment mechanism, primitive cell floor at least \(\min\{1/m,1/(2k)\}\), equal evidence, and unequal queries. \begin{proposition}[prop:minimal-binary-mic-failure]\label{prop:minimal-binary-mic-failure}

\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write the two treatment-branch effects and states as columns \(\phi_0,\phi_1\in\mathbb R^2\) and \(\psi_0,\psi_1\in\mathbb R^2\).  By hypothesis, both pairs are linearly independent.  Normalization of the single two-branch treatment instrument gives
\[
 \phi_0+\phi_1=\mathbf 1.
\]

\[
 \mathbf 1e_z^{\mathsf T}
 =c_0\phi_0\psi_0^{\mathsf T}+c_1\phi_1\psi_1^{\mathsf T}
 =\Phi\begin{pmatrix}c_0&0\\0&c_1\end{pmatrix}\Psi^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad
 \Phi=(\phi_0\ \phi_1),\quad \Psi=(\psi_0\ \psi_1).
\]
Both \(\Phi\) and \(\Psi\) are invertible.  Thus the rank of the right-hand side is the number of nonzero elements of \(\{c_0,c_1\}\). \[
 r_0,r_1\notin\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b}).
\]

The independent treatment effects span \(\mathbb R^2\), as do the independent treatment states, so the treatment instrument is marginally informationally complete in both senses. Applying \texttt{thm:bow-rowspan-iff} for \(z=0\) and \(z=1\), the failed treatment row-span condition implies failure of both corresponding global-identification predicates. \end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:one-fixing-coordinate-validity]\label{prop:one-fixing-coordinate-validity}
Let \(w\) be fixable in \(\mathcal C\). If \(k_{\mathcal C}\) is a strictly positive normalized kernel on \(D\mathbin{\dot\cup}\{w\}\) given \(H\), then the coordinate formula \(\Phi_w(k_{\mathcal C})\) is strictly positive and satisfies \(N_D\Phi_w(k_{\mathcal C})=\mathbf 1\). Moreover the formula is exactly the ordinary one-step fixing quotient by the conditional mass of \(w\) given \(\operatorname{mb}_{\mathcal C}(w)\) and \(H\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put \(B=(\operatorname{mb}_{\mathcal C}(w)\cap D)\) and \(R=D\setminus B\).  Coordinates of fixed vertices that occur in the Markov blanket are already contained in \(a_H\).  Fix a context \((a_H,a_w)\).  For a value \(a_B\), the definitions of \(U_w\) and \(V_w\) give
\[
 u(a_w,a_B,a_H):=(U_wk_{\mathcal C})_{(a_w,a_B,a_H)}
   =\sum_{a_R}k_{\mathcal C}(a_R,a_B,a_w\mid a_H)
\]
and
\[
 v(a_B,a_H):=(V_wk_{\mathcal C})_{(a_B,a_H)}
   =\sum_{a'_w}\sum_{a_R}k_{\mathcal C}(a_R,a_B,a'_w\mid a_H)
   =\sum_{a'_w}u(a'_w,a_B,a_H).
\]
All alphabets are finite and nonempty.  Strict positivity of every cell of \(k_{\mathcal C}\) therefore implies \(u(a_w,a_B,a_H)>0\) and \(v(a_B,a_H)>0\), so every division in the definition of \(\Phi_w\) is valid.  That definition now yields, cell by cell,
\[
 \Phi_w(k_{\mathcal C})(a_R,a_B\mid a_H,a_w)
 =k_{\mathcal C}(a_R,a_B,a_w\mid a_H)
   \frac{v(a_B,a_H)}{u(a_w,a_B,a_H)}>0.
\]
For each fixed context \((a_H,a_w)\), finite summation and the two preceding identities give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_{a_D}\Phi_w(k_{\mathcal C})(a_D\mid a_H,a_w)
 &=\sum_{a_B}\frac{v(a_B,a_H)}{u(a_w,a_B,a_H)}
     \sum_{a_R}k_{\mathcal C}(a_R,a_B,a_w\mid a_H)\\
 &=\sum_{a_B}v(a_B,a_H)\\
 &=\sum_{a_B}\sum_{a'_w}\sum_{a_R}
     k_{\mathcal C}(a_R,a_B,a'_w\mid a_H)=1.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last equality is the assumed normalization of the kernel given \(H\).  By the definition of \(N_D\), these contextwise equalities are precisely \(N_D\Phi_w(k_{\mathcal C})=\mathbf 1\).

Finally,
\[
 \frac{u(a_w,a_B,a_H)}{v(a_B,a_H)}
 =k_{\mathcal C}(a_w\mid a_B,a_H)
 =k_{\mathcal C}\bigl(a_w\mid
   a_{\operatorname{mb}_{\mathcal C}(w)},a_H\bigr).
\]
Consequently \(\Phi_w(k_{\mathcal C})\) is exactly \(k_{\mathcal C}\) divided by the displayed conditional mass.  Assumption \(\text{ass:fixable-vertex}\) makes \(\phi_w(\mathcal C)\) a legal graphical fixing step; the positivity and normalization calculations themselves use only the displayed marginalizations and the positive normalized-kernel hypothesis.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:bow-terminal-effect-positive-twins]\label{lem:bow-terminal-effect-positive-twins}
For a normalized separable menu on \(G_{\mathrm b}\), if \(e_y\notin\operatorname{span}\{\phi_{Y_{\mathrm b},\beta_Y}:\beta_Y\in\mathcal L_{Y_{\mathrm b}}\}\), then there exist \(\mathcal M,\mathcal M'\in\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{pp}}^{\mathrm{edge}}(G_{\mathrm b},\mathcal A)\) with \(\mathcal E_{\mathcal I}(\mathcal M)=\mathcal E_{\mathcal I}(\mathcal M')\) and \(Q_{z^{\circ},y}(\mathcal M)\ne Q_{z^{\circ},y}(\mathcal M')\). They may be chosen with singleton exogenous alphabets, a common uniform treatment mechanism, and primitive cell floor at least \(\min\{1/m,1/(2k)\}\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let
\[
R_Y=\operatorname{span}\{\phi_{Y_{\mathrm b},\beta_Y}:\beta_Y\in\mathcal L_{Y_{\mathrm b}}\}
\subseteq\mathbb R^{\mathcal A_{Y_{\mathrm b}}},
\]
and suppose \(e_y\notin R_Y\).  Fix any terminal instrument label \(j_Y\), which exists because \(\mathcal J_{Y_{\mathrm b}}\) is nonempty.  By \(\texttt{ass:instrument-normalization}\),
\[
\mathbf 1=\sum_{b_Y\in\mathcal B_{Y_{\mathrm b},j_Y}}
\phi_{Y_{\mathrm b},(j_Y,b_Y)}\in R_Y.
\]
Choose a matrix \(S\) whose columns form a basis of \(R_Y\).  Its columns are linearly independent, so \(S^{\mathsf T}S\) is positive definite and invertible.  Define
\[
\gamma=e_y-S(S^{\mathsf T}S)^{-1}S^{\mathsf T}e_y.
\]
Then \(\gamma\perp R_Y\), and \(\gamma\neq0\) because \(e_y\notin R_Y\).  The orthogonal decomposition of \(e_y\) into its projection on \(R_Y\) and \(\gamma\) gives
\[
\gamma(y)=e_y^{\mathsf T}\gamma=\lVert\gamma\rVert_2^2>0.
\]
Since \(\mathbf 1\in R_Y\) and \(\gamma\perp R_Y\),
\[
\sum_{a_{\mathrm t}\in\mathcal A_{Y_{\mathrm b}}}\gamma(a_{\mathrm t})=0.
\]

Let \(L_\gamma=\lVert\gamma\rVert_\infty>0\) and \(t_\gamma=1/(2kL_\gamma)>0\).  For \(\sigma\in\{+1,-1\}\), construct \(\mathcal N^\sigma\) on \(G_{\mathrm b}\) by giving every private and bidirected-edge source a singleton alphabet with mass one and setting
\[
f_{X_{\mathrm b}}^\sigma(a_{\mathrm b}\mid u)=\frac1m,
\qquad
f_{Y_{\mathrm b}}^\sigma(a_{\mathrm t}\mid z_{\mathrm b},u)
=\frac1k+\sigma t_\gamma\gamma(a_{\mathrm t}).
\]
The terminal kernel may ignore its permitted parent and source arguments.  Its cells sum to one because \(\sum_{a_{\mathrm t}}\gamma(a_{\mathrm t})=0\), and
\[
f_{Y_{\mathrm b}}^\sigma(a_{\mathrm t}\mid z_{\mathrm b},u)
\geq\frac1k-t_\gamma L_\gamma=\frac1{2k}>0.
\]
The treatment cells equal \(1/m>0\).  The singleton source masses factor independently and equal one.  Hence \(\texttt{ass:independent-sources}\), \(\texttt{ass:strict-source-positivity}\), \(\texttt{ass:strict-mechanism-positivity}\), and \(\texttt{def:primitive-positive-edge-scm-class}\) give
\[
\mathcal N^+,\mathcal N^-\in
\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{pp}}^{\mathrm{edge}}(G_{\mathrm b},\mathcal A),
\]
with primitive cell floor at least \(\min\{1/m,1/(2k)\}\).

Expanding \(\texttt{def:split-port-kernel}\),
\[
W_{\mathcal N^\sigma}((a_{\mathrm b},a_{\mathrm t})\mid(z_{\mathrm b},z_{\mathrm t}))
=\frac1m\left(\frac1k+\sigma t_\gamma\gamma(a_{\mathrm t})\right).
\]
For any accessible branch pair, \(\texttt{def:probe-evidence-map}\), finite rearrangement of sums, and probability-state normalization give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\bar q^+_{\beta_X,\beta_Y}-\bar q^-_{\beta_X,\beta_Y}
&=\frac{2t_\gamma}{m}
\left(\sum_{a_{\mathrm b},z_{\mathrm b}}
\phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(a_{\mathrm b})
\psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(z_{\mathrm b})\right)
\left(\sum_{a_{\mathrm t},z_{\mathrm t}}
\phi_{Y_{\mathrm b},\beta_Y}(a_{\mathrm t})
\psi_{Y_{\mathrm b},\beta_Y}(z_{\mathrm t})
\gamma(a_{\mathrm t})\right)\\
&=\frac{2t_\gamma}{m}
\left(\sum_{a_{\mathrm b},z_{\mathrm b}}
\phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(a_{\mathrm b})
\psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(z_{\mathrm b})\right)
\left\langle\phi_{Y_{\mathrm b},\beta_Y},\gamma\right\rangle
=0.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last equality uses \(\phi_{Y_{\mathrm b},\beta_Y}\in R_Y\) and \(\gamma\perp R_Y\).  Thus all evidence coordinates agree.  On the other hand, \(\texttt{def:atomic-query-contraction}\) gives
\[
Q_{z^{\circ},y}(\mathcal N^\sigma)
=\sum_{a_{\mathrm b}}\frac1m
\left(\frac1k+\sigma t_\gamma\gamma(y)\right)
=\frac1k+\sigma t_\gamma\gamma(y),
\]
so
\[
Q_{z^{\circ},y}(\mathcal N^+)-Q_{z^{\circ},y}(\mathcal N^-)
=2t_\gamma\gamma(y)>0.
\]
These are the required strictly positive same-evidence twins.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Failure of terminal effect reachability is a genuine causal nonidentification obstruction witnessed by singleton-source SCMs strictly inside the primitive-positive class. \textit{Gap.} Treatment-side row reachability cannot compensate for a terminal output coordinate outside the effect span; the orthogonal perturbation is invisible to every terminal probe branch but changes the intervention target. \textit{Consumer.} Ananke can return the orthogonal terminal perturbation and the two uniform-treatment SCMs as a certified negative result with floor \(\min\{1/m,1/(2k)\}\).

\begin{theorem}[thm:binary-bow-treatment-rank-frontier]\label{thm:binary-bow-treatment-rank-frontier}
Let \(m=2\).  For every normalized binary separable probe menu \(\mathcal I\)
on \(G_{\mathrm b}\) whose treatment effects satisfy
\(\operatorname{span}\{\phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}:\beta_X\in
\mathcal L_{X_{\mathrm b}}\}=\mathbb R^2\), if
\(r_0,r_1\in\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b})\), then
\(\operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm b})\geq 3\), and hence
\(\lvert\mathcal L_{X_{\mathrm b}}\rvert\geq3\).  The explicit menu
\(\mathcal I_{\mathrm b}^{\dagger}\) has marginally complete treatment
effects, contains both \(r_0\) and \(r_1\) in
\(\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b}^{\dagger})\), and satisfies
\(\operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm b}^{\dagger})=3\).  Thus three is the exact
minimum attainable treatment branch-matrix rank among normalized marginally
effect-complete binary menus containing both bow target rows.  This is a
minimum-rank attainment statement, not a claim that every rank-three menu
contains the target rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put
\[
S=\operatorname{span}\{r_0,r_1\}\subseteq\mathbb R^4.
\]
By \(\texttt{def:bow-target-coefficient}\), with the coordinates ordered by
\((a_{\mathrm b},z_{\mathrm b})\),
\[
r_0=\mathbf 1\otimes e_0,
\qquad
r_1=\mathbf 1\otimes e_1.
\]
Because \(e_0,e_1\) form the coordinate basis of \(\mathbb R^2\),
\[
S=\{\mathbf 1\otimes t:t\in\mathbb R^2\},
\qquad
\dim(S)=2.
\]
Indeed, the map \(t\mapsto\mathbf 1\otimes t\) is linear and injective,
since \(\mathbf 1\neq0\).

Let \(\mathcal I\) be an arbitrary menu satisfying the hypotheses.  The
inclusions \(r_0,r_1\in\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b})\) imply
\[
S\subseteq\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b}),
\qquad
\operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm b})\geq2.
\]
Suppose, toward a contradiction, that
\(\operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm b})<3\).  The preceding inequality and
\(\dim(S)=2\) then give
\[
\operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm b})=2,
\qquad
\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b})=S.
\]
Fix \(\beta_X\in\mathcal L_{X_{\mathrm b}}\).  By
\(\texttt{def:bow-probe-matrix}\), reshaping the row indexed by \(\beta_X\)
as a matrix with output index \(a_{\mathrm b}\) and input index
\(z_{\mathrm b}\) yields
\[
R_{\beta_X}
=\phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}
 \psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Every matrix represented by a vector in \(S\) has two identical rows.
Writing
\(\phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}=(\phi(0),\phi(1))^{\mathsf T}\), the membership
\(R_{\beta_X}\in S\) therefore implies
\[
\bigl(\phi(0)-\phi(1)\bigr)
\psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}^{\mathsf T}=0.
\]
The vector \(\psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}\) is a probability state, so it is
nonnegative and its coordinates sum to one.  It is consequently nonzero,
and the last display forces \(\phi(0)=\phi(1)\).  Since \(\beta_X\) was
arbitrary, every treatment effect is a scalar multiple of \(\mathbf 1\),
whence
\[
\operatorname{span}\{\phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}:
\beta_X\in\mathcal L_{X_{\mathrm b}}\}
\subseteq\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf 1\}
\neq\mathbb R^2.
\]
This contradicts the marginal effect-completeness hypothesis.  Hence
\[
\operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm b})\geq3.
\]
The rank of a matrix is at most its number of rows, so also
\[
\lvert\mathcal L_{X_{\mathrm b}}\rvert\geq3.
\]

It remains to prove attainment.  Under \(\texttt{def:binary-bow-menu}\), the
three treatment rows of \(A_{\mathrm b}^{\dagger}\) are
\[
w_0=\tfrac12e_0\otimes\psi^{+},
\qquad
w_1=\tfrac12e_1\otimes\psi^{+},
\qquad
w_2=\tfrac12\mathbf 1\otimes\psi^{-}.
\]
All three treatment effects are nonnegative and, using
\(\mathbf 1=e_0+e_1\),
\[
\tfrac12e_0+\tfrac12e_1+\tfrac12\mathbf 1
=e_0+e_1
=\mathbf 1.
\]
Thus the treatment instrument satisfies
\(\texttt{ass:instrument-normalization}\).  The terminal effects also obey
\(e_0+e_1=\mathbf 1\), so the terminal instrument is normalized as well.
Moreover,
\[
e_0=2\bigl(\tfrac12e_0\bigr),
\qquad
e_1=2\bigl(\tfrac12e_1\bigr),
\]
and therefore the treatment effects span \(\mathbb R^2\).

The two treatment states are linearly independent because
\[
\det\begin{pmatrix}
3/4&1/4\\
1/4&3/4
\end{pmatrix}
=\frac{9}{16}-\frac{1}{16}
=\frac12
\neq0.
\]
If \(\alpha w_0+\beta w_1+\gamma w_2=0\), comparison of the two
output-indexed blocks, followed by multiplication by two, gives
\[
\alpha\psi^{+}+\gamma\psi^{-}=0,
\qquad
\beta\psi^{+}+\gamma\psi^{-}=0.
\]
Linear independence of \(\psi^{+},\psi^{-}\) in the first equality yields
\(\alpha=\gamma=0\), and the second equality then yields \(\beta=0\).
Consequently the three rows are linearly independent and
\[
\operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm b}^{\dagger})=3.
\]

Finally, direct coordinate calculation gives
\[
e_0=\tfrac32\psi^{+}-\tfrac12\psi^{-},
\qquad
e_1=-\tfrac12\psi^{+}+\tfrac32\psi^{-}.
\]
Since
\[
w_0+w_1=\tfrac12\mathbf 1\otimes\psi^{+},
\qquad
w_2=\tfrac12\mathbf 1\otimes\psi^{-},
\]
we obtain the explicit row-space representations
\[
r_0=\mathbf 1\otimes e_0=3(w_0+w_1)-w_2,
\qquad
r_1=\mathbf 1\otimes e_1=-(w_0+w_1)+3w_2.
\]
Thus \(r_0,r_1\in\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b}^{\dagger})\).  The
universal lower bound and this normalized, marginally effect-complete
rank-three witness prove that the minimum attainable treatment branch-matrix
rank is exactly three.  The proof uses the target-row assumption in the
lower-bound argument and therefore makes no claim that every rank-three menu
contains \(r_0\) and \(r_1\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:binary-bow-universal-rank-lower-bound]\label{thm:binary-bow-universal-rank-lower-bound}
Let \(m=2\).  For every normalized separable probe menu \(\mathcal I\) on
\(G_{\mathrm b}\), suppose that
\(\operatorname{span}\{\phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}:\beta_X\in
\mathcal L_{X_{\mathrm b}}\}=\mathbb R^2\) and that
\(r_0,r_1\in\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b})\).  Then
\(\operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm b})\ge3\), and consequently
\(\lvert\mathcal L_{X_{\mathrm b}}\rvert\ge3\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By \(\texttt{def:bow-target-coefficient}\), in the binary case
\[
r_0=\mathbf 1\otimes e_0,
\qquad
r_1=\mathbf 1\otimes e_1.
\]
Since \(e_0,e_1\) are linearly independent, \(r_0,r_1\) are linearly independent.  Their assumed membership in \(\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b})\) therefore gives \(\operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm b})\ge2\).

Assume for contradiction that \(\operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm b})=2\).  The preceding two-dimensional inclusion then forces
\[
\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b})
=\operatorname{span}\{r_0,r_1\}
=\{\mathbf 1\otimes s:s\in\mathbb R^2\}.
\]
Fix any tagged treatment branch \(\beta_X\).  By \(\texttt{def:bow-probe-matrix}\), its row is
\[
\phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}\otimes
\psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}.
\]
The state \(\psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}\) belongs to the probability simplex on the nonempty binary alphabet, so its coordinates are nonnegative and sum to one.  Hence there is a \(z_*\in\{0,1\}\) with \(\psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(z_*)>0\).  Membership of this product row in \(\{\mathbf 1\otimes s:s\in\mathbb R^2\}\) makes its two treatment-output coordinates at \(z_*\) equal:
\[
\phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(0)\psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(z_*)
=
\phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(1)\psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(z_*).
\]
Division by the strictly positive coordinate \(\psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(z_*)\) gives
\[
\phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(0)
=\phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(1).
\]
Thus every tagged treatment effect is a scalar multiple of \(\mathbf 1\), including the possible zero effect.  Their span is contained in the one-dimensional subspace \(\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf 1\}\), contradicting the assumed equality with \(\mathbb R^2\).  Rank two is impossible.  Together with the initial rank lower bound, this proves
\[
\operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm b})\ge3.
\]
Because \(A_{\mathrm b}\) has exactly one row for each \(\beta_X\in\mathcal L_{X_{\mathrm b}}\), its rank is at most its number of rows.  Therefore \(\lvert\mathcal L_{X_{\mathrm b}}\rvert\ge3\).  Instrument normalization is part of the quantified design class; the argument uses, in addition, only the probability-state normalization already built into the type of each \(\psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the universal obstruction that makes rank three necessary once treatment effects are complete and both target rows are identified. \textit{Gap.} Marginal informational completeness alone does not determine the product-row rank; the theorem proves the missing binary lower bound directly from the bow target row space. \textit{Consumer.} Ananke can use the theorem as a design-time rejection certificate for every effect-complete binary treatment menu of rank below three.

\begin{theorem}[thm:binary-bow-minimum-rank-frontier]\label{thm:binary-bow-minimum-rank-frontier}
Among normalized binary separable menus on \(G_{\mathrm b}\) whose treatment
effect and state spans are both \(\mathbb R^2\), whose terminal effects span
\(\mathbb R^2\), and for which
\(\operatorname{GID}_{\mathcal I}(Q_{z^{\circ},y})\) holds for every
\(z^{\circ},y\in\{0,1\}\) on
\(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{pp}}^{\mathrm{edge}}(G_{\mathrm b},\mathcal A)\),
the minimum possible value of \(\operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm b})\) is
three.  The minimum is attained by \(\mathcal I_{\mathrm b}^{\dagger}\),
whose treatment menu is one three-branch instrument.  This is a universal
lower bound together with an attaining menu, not a claim that every
rank-three menu identifies the queries.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(\mathcal I\) satisfy the hypotheses of the theorem.  All-query identification includes \(\operatorname{GID}_{\mathcal I}(Q_{0,0})\) and \(\operatorname{GID}_{\mathcal I}(Q_{1,0})\).  The only-if direction of \(\texttt{thm:bow-rowspan-iff}\) therefore gives
\[
r_0,r_1\in\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b}).
\]
Together with the assumed treatment-effect span \(\mathbb R^2\), these are exactly the hypotheses of the already-established \(\texttt{thm:binary-bow-universal-rank-lower-bound}\), so
\[
\operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm b})\ge3.
\]

For attainment, \(\texttt{def:binary-bow-menu}\) gives \(\mathcal I_{\mathrm b}^{\dagger}\) one treatment instrument with three branches.  By \(\texttt{thm:binary-bow-query-separation}\), this menu is normalized, its treatment effect and state spans are \(\mathbb R^2\), its terminal effect span is \(\mathbb R^2\), its treatment matrix has rank three, and every binary atomic bow query is globally identified on \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{pp}}^{\mathrm{edge}}(G_{\mathrm b},\mathcal A)\).  Thus the admissible ranks are bounded below by three and include three, proving that their minimum is exactly three.

The treatment-effect-span premise is essential rather than decorative.  If it is removed, take one normalized binary treatment instrument with branches \((\tfrac12\mathbf 1,e_0)\) and \((\tfrac12\mathbf 1,e_1)\).  Its two rows are \(r_0/2,r_1/2\), so the treatment matrix has rank two and contains both target rows, while its effect span is only \(\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf 1\}\).  Paired with the terminal basis instrument, the success direction of \(\texttt{thm:bow-rowspan-iff}\) identifies all four queries.  This exact two-state stress test shows why the theorem is a frontier for the stated effect-complete class and does not assert that rank three alone suffices.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This retained binary theorem certifies that the general \(2m-1\) frontier specializes to the exact minimum rank three under the original binary completeness conditions. \textit{Gap.} The rank-three lower bound is not a tomography statement and is false without treatment-effect completeness, as shown by the explicit rank-two stress test. \textit{Consumer.} Ananke can keep the binary minimum-rank test unchanged while adopting the finite-alphabet fixture generator.

\begin{theorem}[thm:finite-alphabet-bow-rank-frontier]\label{thm:finite-alphabet-bow-rank-frontier}
Let \(m=\lvert\mathcal A_{X_{\mathrm b}}\rvert\geq2\), \(k=\lvert\mathcal A_{Y_{\mathrm b}}\rvert\geq2\), and let \(\mathfrak C\) satisfy
\[
\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{pp}}^{\mathrm{edge}}(G_{\mathrm b},\mathcal A)
\subseteq\mathfrak C
\subseteq\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{fin}}^{\mathrm{edge}}(G_{\mathrm b},\mathcal A).
\]
For every normalized separable menu \(\mathcal I\) on \(G_{\mathrm b}\), if \(\operatorname{span}\{\phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}:\beta_X\in\mathcal L_{X_{\mathrm b}}\}=\mathbb R^m\) and \(r_{z^{\circ}}\in\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b})\) for every \(z^{\circ}\in\mathcal A_{X_{\mathrm b}}\), then \(\operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm b})\geq2m-1\) and \(\lvert\mathcal L_{X_{\mathrm b}}\rvert\geq2m-1\).  The menu \(\mathcal I_{\mathrm b}^{\mathrm{fr}}\) is normalized; its treatment effects and states span \(\mathbb R^m\), its terminal effects and states span \(\mathbb R^k\), \(\operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm b}^{\mathrm{fr}})=2m-1\), and every \(r_{z^{\circ}}\) belongs to \(\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b}^{\mathrm{fr}})\).  Consequently every \(Q_{z^{\circ},y}\) is globally identified under \(\operatorname{GID}_{\mathcal I_{\mathrm b}^{\mathrm{fr}}}^{\mathfrak C}\), including when \(\mathfrak C=\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{fin}}^{\mathrm{edge}}(G_{\mathrm b},\mathcal A)\).  For each such \(\mathfrak C\), the exact minimum treatment rank among normalized bow menus whose treatment effects and states span \(\mathbb R^m\), whose terminal effects and states span \(\mathbb R^k\), and which globally identify all atomic queries over \(\mathfrak C\), is \(2m-1\).  This attainment is non-tomographic because \(2m-1<m^2\).  When \(m=k=2\), \(\mathcal I_{\mathrm b}^{\mathrm{fr}}\) is exactly \(\mathcal I_{\mathrm b}^{\dagger}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Identify a row vector indexed by \((a_{\mathrm b},z_{\mathrm b})\) with its \(m\)-by-\(m\) matrix of coordinates.  Let
\[
R=\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b}),
\qquad
S=\operatorname{span}\{r_{z^{\circ}}:z^{\circ}\in\mathcal A_{X_{\mathrm b}}\}.
\]
By \(\texttt{def:bow-target-coefficient}\),
\[
S=\{\mathbf 1_Xt^{\mathsf T}:t\in\mathbb R^m\}.
\]
The matrices \(\mathbf 1_X\delta_i^{\mathsf T}\), \(0\leq i<m\), are linearly independent, so \(\dim S=m\).  The target-row hypothesis gives \(S\subseteq R\).

Define the linear map
\[
F:R\longrightarrow\mathbb R^m/\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf 1_X\},
\qquad
F(M)=[M\mathbf 1_X].
\]
For \(M=\mathbf 1_Xt^{\mathsf T}\in S\),
\[
M\mathbf 1_X=(t^{\mathsf T}\mathbf 1_X)\mathbf 1_X,
\]
so \(F(M)=0\).  Hence \(F\) induces \(\overline F:R/S\to\mathbb R^m/\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf 1_X\}\).  By \(\texttt{def:bow-probe-matrix}\), the row for \(\beta_X\) is
\[
M_{\beta_X}=\phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}\psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Because \(\psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}\) is a probability state,
\[
F(M_{\beta_X})=[M_{\beta_X}\mathbf 1_X]=[\phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}].
\]
The assumed treatment-effect span is \(\mathbb R^m\), so these quotient classes span \(\mathbb R^m/\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf 1_X\}\).  Thus \(\overline F\) is surjective and
\[
\dim(R/S)\geq m-1.
\]
Since \(S\subseteq R\),
\[
\operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm b})=\dim R
=\dim S+\dim(R/S)
\geq m+(m-1)=2m-1.
\]
The rank cannot exceed the number of treatment rows, proving \(\lvert\mathcal L_{X_{\mathrm b}}\rvert\geq2m-1\).

We now verify \(\mathcal I_{\mathrm b}^{\mathrm{fr}}\).  Every coordinate of \(\rho_i\) is at least \(1/(2m)>0\), and
\[
\mathbf 1_X^{\mathsf T}\rho_i=\frac12+\frac1{2m}m=1,
\]
so each \(\rho_i\) is a probability state.  At \(X_{\mathrm b}\), the branch effects sum coordinatewise to
\[
\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\frac12\delta_i
+\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\frac1{2(m-1)}\mathbf 1_X
=\frac12\mathbf 1_X+\frac12\mathbf 1_X
=\mathbf 1_X.
\]
At \(Y_{\mathrm b}\), both the effects and states are coordinate vectors and \(\sum_y e_y=\mathbf 1\).  Thus \(\texttt{ass:instrument-normalization}\) holds.  The treatment effects span \(\mathbb R^m\) because they contain nonzero multiples of every \(\delta_i\).

To verify the state span, suppose \(\sum_i\alpha_i\rho_i=0\), and put \(A=\sum_i\alpha_i\).  Its \(j\)-th coordinate says
\[
\frac12\alpha_j+\frac1{2m}A=0.
\]
Summing over \(j\) gives \(A=0\), and then every \(\alpha_j=0\).  Hence \(\rho_0,\ldots,\rho_{m-1}\) form a basis of \(\mathbb R^m\).  The terminal effects and states \((e_y)_y\) likewise form the coordinate basis of \(\mathbb R^k\).

The first \(m\) treatment rows span
\[
U=\{u\rho_0^{\mathsf T}:u\in\mathbb R^m\},
\qquad \dim U=m,
\]
and the remaining \(m-1\) rows span
\[
V=\{\mathbf 1_Xv^{\mathsf T}:v\in\operatorname{span}(\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_{m-1})\},
\qquad \dim V=m-1.
\]
Because the \(\rho_i\) form a basis, there is a dual vector \(w_0\) with \(\rho_0^{\mathsf T}w_0=1\) and \(\rho_i^{\mathsf T}w_0=0\) for \(1\leq i<m\).  If \(M\in U\cap V\), write \(M=u\rho_0^{\mathsf T}=\mathbf 1_Xv^{\mathsf T}\).  Right multiplication by \(w_0\) gives \(u=0\), because \(v\in\operatorname{span}(\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_{m-1})\).  Thus \(M=0\), so \(U\cap V=\{0\}\), and
\[
\operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm b}^{\mathrm{fr}})
=\dim(U+V)=m+(m-1)=2m-1.
\]
Moreover,
\[
\mathbf 1_X\rho_0^{\mathsf T}
=2\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\left(\frac12\delta_i\rho_0^{\mathsf T}\right)\in U,
\]
while \(\mathbf 1_X\rho_i^{\mathsf T}\in V\) for \(1\leq i<m\).  Since the \(\rho_i\) form a basis, every \(\mathbf 1_Xt^{\mathsf T}\), and hence every \(r_{z^{\circ}}=\mathbf 1_X\delta_{z^{\circ}}^{\mathsf T}\), lies in \(U+V=\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b}^{\mathrm{fr}})\).

Fix a class \(\mathfrak C\) satisfying the displayed inclusions.  Every terminal effect \(e_y\) occurs in the construction, so \(\texttt{thm:bow-rowspan-iff}\), applied to every \((z^{\circ},y)\), gives
\[
\operatorname{GID}_{\mathcal I_{\mathrm b}^{\mathrm{fr}}}^{\mathfrak C}(Q_{z^{\circ},y}).
\]
This includes the full finite class by taking \(\mathfrak C=\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{fin}}^{\mathrm{edge}}\).  Conversely, for any menu in the theorem's all-query class over this fixed \(\mathfrak C\), the only-if direction of \(\texttt{thm:bow-rowspan-iff}\) implies that every \(r_{z^{\circ}}\) belongs to \(R\).  The lower-bound argument therefore applies.  The constructed menu attains the bound while satisfying every stated effect and state completeness condition, proving that the minimum is exactly \(2m-1\) for every admissible \(\mathfrak C\).  Finally,
\[
m^2-(2m-1)=(m-1)^2>0
\]
for \(m\geq2\), proving non-tomography.

When \(m=k=2\),
\[
\rho_0=\frac12e_0+\frac14\mathbf 1=(3/4,1/4)=\psi^+,
\qquad
\rho_1=\frac12e_1+\frac14\mathbf 1=(1/4,3/4)=\psi^-.
\]
The treatment and terminal branches are therefore exactly those in \(\texttt{def:binary-bow-menu}\), proving \(\mathcal I_{\mathrm b}^{\mathrm{fr}}=\mathcal I_{\mathrm b}^{\dagger}\).

As a stress test showing that treatment-effect completeness is load-bearing, omit that premise and use one normalized treatment instrument with branches \((m^{-1}\mathbf 1_X,\delta_i)\), \(0\leq i<m\).  Its row span is exactly \(S\), so its rank is \(m\) and it contains every target row, but its effects span only \(\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf 1_X\}\).  Paired with the terminal basis instrument, \(\texttt{thm:bow-rowspan-iff}\) identifies every atomic query over every admissible \(\mathfrak C\).  Hence the \(2m-1\) lower bound cannot be extended after deleting the effect-completeness premise.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This theorem gives the exact finite-treatment-alphabet design elbow over the full finite edge-source class: query reachability plus treatment-effect completeness costs precisely \(2m-1\) treatment-row dimensions. \textit{Gap.} Prior mechanism recovery does not determine the minimum probe rank for all interventions on a latent bow; the quotient map separates the \(m\)-dimensional target subspace from the additional \(m-1\) directions forced by effect completeness. \textit{Consumer.} Ananke can generate the \(2m-1\)-branch fixture for any finite treatment alphabet, verify its spans and target rows, and recover every atomic query over the full finite canonical independent-edge-source class.

\begin{lemma}[lem:finite-edge-bow-contraction]\label{lem:finite-edge-bow-contraction}
For every \(\mathcal M\in\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{fin}}^{\mathrm{edge}}(G_{\mathrm b},\mathcal A)\) and every normalized separable menu on \(G_{\mathrm b}\), suppose \(e_y\in\operatorname{span}\{\phi_{Y_{\mathrm b},\beta_Y}:\beta_Y\in\mathcal L_{Y_{\mathrm b}}\}\).  Then every admissible effect synthesis \(\mu^y\) satisfies
\[
q^{\mathrm b}_{\beta_X}(y;\mu^y)
=\sum_{a_{\mathrm b},z_{\mathrm b}}A_{\mathrm b}[\beta_X,(a_{\mathrm b},z_{\mathrm b})]
B_{\mathcal M,y}(a_{\mathrm b},z_{\mathrm b}),
\qquad
Q_{z^{\circ},y}(\mathcal M)
=\sum_{a_{\mathrm b},z_{\mathrm b}}r_{z^{\circ}}[a_{\mathrm b},z_{\mathrm b}]
B_{\mathcal M,y}(a_{\mathrm b},z_{\mathrm b}).
\]
Consequently, whenever \(c^{\mathsf T}A_{\mathrm b}=r_{z^{\circ}}^{\mathsf T}\),
\[
Q_{z^{\circ},y}(\mathcal M)=
\sum_{\beta_X\in\mathcal L_{X_{\mathrm b}}}c_{\beta_X}q^{\mathrm b}_{\beta_X}(y;\mu^y),
\]
and the right-hand side is invariant under all admissible choices of \(c\) and \(\mu^y\).  These identities use stochastic normalization but no strict positivity.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(\mathcal M\in\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{fin}}^{\mathrm{edge}}(G_{\mathrm b},\mathcal A)\).  Membership in \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{fin}}^{\mathrm{edge}}\) supplies the independent source product and normalized stochastic kernels from \(\texttt{def:finite-edge-scm-class}\); zero cells are allowed.  In the bow graph, \(X_{\mathrm b}\) has no directed parent and \(Y_{\mathrm b}\) has only \(X_{\mathrm b}\) as a directed parent.  Expanding \(\texttt{def:split-port-kernel}\), the coordinate
\[
W_{\mathcal M}((a_{\mathrm b},a_{\mathrm t})\mid(z_{\mathrm b},z_{\mathrm t}))
\]
therefore does not depend on \(z_{\mathrm t}\): no structural factor has \(z_{\mathrm t}\) among its parent-input arguments.  Write this coordinate as \(W_{\mathcal M}(a_{\mathrm b},a_{\mathrm t}\mid z_{\mathrm b})\).

For a tagged treatment branch \(\beta_X\), expand \(\texttt{def:terminally-synthesized-bow-response}\) and then \(\texttt{def:probe-evidence-map}\).  All alphabets are finite, so the sums may be rearranged without a limiting argument:
\[
\begin{aligned}
q^{\mathrm b}_{\beta_X}(y;\mu^y)
&=\sum_{\beta_Y}\mu^y_{\beta_Y}
  \sum_{a_{\mathrm b},a_{\mathrm t},z_{\mathrm b},z_{\mathrm t}}
  W_{\mathcal M}(a_{\mathrm b},a_{\mathrm t}\mid z_{\mathrm b})
  \phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(a_{\mathrm b})
  \psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(z_{\mathrm b})
  \phi_{Y_{\mathrm b},\beta_Y}(a_{\mathrm t})
  \psi_{Y_{\mathrm b},\beta_Y}(z_{\mathrm t})\\
&=\sum_{a_{\mathrm b},a_{\mathrm t},z_{\mathrm b}}
  W_{\mathcal M}(a_{\mathrm b},a_{\mathrm t}\mid z_{\mathrm b})
  \phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(a_{\mathrm b})
  \psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(z_{\mathrm b})
  \sum_{\beta_Y}\mu^y_{\beta_Y}
  \phi_{Y_{\mathrm b},\beta_Y}(a_{\mathrm t}).
\end{aligned}
\]
The second equality uses \(\sum_{z_{\mathrm t}}\psi_{Y_{\mathrm b},\beta_Y}(z_{\mathrm t})=1\), which holds because every \(\psi_{Y_{\mathrm b},\beta_Y}\) is a probability state.  By \(\texttt{def:terminal-synthesis}\), the final sum over \(\beta_Y\) is \(\mathbf 1\{a_{\mathrm t}=y\}\).  Hence, using \(\texttt{def:bow-probe-matrix}\) and the fact that terminal-input independence permits evaluation at \(z_{\mathrm t}=y\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
q^{\mathrm b}_{\beta_X}(y;\mu^y)
&=\sum_{a_{\mathrm b},z_{\mathrm b}}
 \phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(a_{\mathrm b})
 \psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(z_{\mathrm b})
 W_{\mathcal M}((a_{\mathrm b},y)\mid(z_{\mathrm b},y))\\
&=\sum_{a_{\mathrm b},z_{\mathrm b}}
 A_{\mathrm b}[\beta_X,(a_{\mathrm b},z_{\mathrm b})]
 B_{\mathcal M,y}(a_{\mathrm b},z_{\mathrm b}).
\end{aligned}
\]

Next apply \(\texttt{def:atomic-query-contraction}\) with \(X=\{X_{\mathrm b}\}\) and \(Y=\{Y_{\mathrm b}\}\).  The indicators impose \(a_{\mathrm t}=y\), \(z_{\mathrm b}=z^{\circ}\), and \(z_{\mathrm t}=a_{\mathrm t}=y\).  Thus
\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_{z^{\circ},y}(\mathcal M)
&=\sum_{a_{\mathrm b}}W_{\mathcal M}((a_{\mathrm b},y)\mid(z^{\circ},y))\\
&=\sum_{a_{\mathrm b},z_{\mathrm b}}
 r_{z^{\circ}}[a_{\mathrm b},z_{\mathrm b}]
 B_{\mathcal M,y}(a_{\mathrm b},z_{\mathrm b}),
\end{aligned}
\]
where the last equality is \(\texttt{def:bow-target-coefficient}\).

Let \(b_y\in\mathbb R^{m^2}\) be the vectorization of \(B_{\mathcal M,y}\).  The two identities are
\[
q^{\mathrm b}(y;\mu^y)=A_{\mathrm b}b_y,
\qquad
Q_{z^{\circ},y}(\mathcal M)=r_{z^{\circ}}^{\mathsf T}b_y.
\]
If \(c^{\mathsf T}A_{\mathrm b}=r_{z^{\circ}}^{\mathsf T}\), then
\[
Q_{z^{\circ},y}(\mathcal M)
=c^{\mathsf T}A_{\mathrm b}b_y
=\sum_{\beta_X}c_{\beta_X}q^{\mathrm b}_{\beta_X}(y;\mu^y).
\]
The displayed vector \(A_{\mathrm b}b_y\) contains neither \(\mu^y\) nor \(c\).  Hence two admissible syntheses give the same response vector, and two admissible coefficients give the same scalar \(r_{z^{\circ}}^{\mathsf T}b_y\).  Every step used only finite summation, the graph's parent sets, and stochastic normalization; strict source or mechanism positivity was nowhere used.
\end{proof}

\section{Interpretation}

The ambient-class quantifier isolates the role of positivity. The identifying map is an equality of finite contractions and therefore remains valid when source masses or mechanism cells lie on the boundary of the probability simplex. The negative direction is stronger than mere boundary failure: each failed span test has two observationally indistinguishable witnesses already in the primitive-positive interior, so enlarging the model class cannot restore identification. For design, the quotient argument shows that all \(m\) intervention rows plus treatment-effect completeness require exactly \(m-1\) additional directions, giving rank \(2m-1\) rather than \(m^2\).

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The bow equivalence is complete over the full finite canonical independent-edge-source class. The later one-fixing algebraic set still uses only finite vectors and matrices: positivity, normalization, and listed overlaps do not by themselves certify that a reconstructed array has a shared-mechanism representation with mutually independent edge sources. That source-product lift is not asserted by the one-fixing certificate or coordinate quotient.

\section{Honest scope}

The finite claims are the class-indexed bow row-span equivalence for every ambient class between the primitive-positive and full finite canonical independent-edge-source classes, its positivity-free invariant functional, its two primitive-positive obstruction families, the exact \(2m-1\) finite-alphabet rank frontier, the binary rank-three menu, the minimal-menu obstruction, the rational front-door check, and positivity and normalization of one coordinate fixing quotient. The matching binary terminal branches reduce to \(e_0\otimes e_0=d_0\) and \(e_1\otimes e_1=d_1\); the front-door fixture reduces to the eight cells \((42,6;6,10;10,6;6,42)/128\) and targets \(3/8,5/8\). The shared-mechanism independent-edge-source lift from the algebraic one-step certificate, including a finite latent-support bound or a smallest extra algebraic obstruction, remains open. No all-ADMG recursive calculus, complexity theorem, or inference theorem is claimed. Under passive observation, the coordinate map reduces to the ordinary one-step fixing quotient; full branch-span tomography remains a boundary case rather than a contribution.
