Identify a row vector indexed by \((a_{\mathrm b},z_{\mathrm b})\) with its \(m\)-by-\(m\) matrix of coordinates.  Let
\[
R=\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b}),
\qquad
S=\operatorname{span}\{r_{z^{\circ}}:z^{\circ}\in\mathcal A_{X_{\mathrm b}}\}.
\]
By \(\texttt{def:bow-target-coefficient}\),
\[
S=\{\mathbf 1_Xt^{\mathsf T}:t\in\mathbb R^m\}.
\]
The matrices \(\mathbf 1_X\delta_i^{\mathsf T}\), \(0\leq i<m\), are linearly independent, so \(\dim S=m\).  The target-row hypothesis gives \(S\subseteq R\).

Define the linear map
\[
F:R\longrightarrow\mathbb R^m/\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf 1_X\},
\qquad
F(M)=[M\mathbf 1_X].
\]
For \(M=\mathbf 1_Xt^{\mathsf T}\in S\),
\[
M\mathbf 1_X=(t^{\mathsf T}\mathbf 1_X)\mathbf 1_X,
\]
so \(F(M)=0\).  Hence \(F\) induces \(\overline F:R/S\to\mathbb R^m/\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf 1_X\}\).  By \(\texttt{def:bow-probe-matrix}\), the row for \(\beta_X\) is
\[
M_{\beta_X}=\phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}\psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Because \(\psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}\) is a probability state,
\[
F(M_{\beta_X})=[M_{\beta_X}\mathbf 1_X]=[\phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}].
\]
The assumed treatment-effect span is \(\mathbb R^m\), so these quotient classes span \(\mathbb R^m/\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf 1_X\}\).  Thus \(\overline F\) is surjective and
\[
\dim(R/S)\geq m-1.
\]
Since \(S\subseteq R\),
\[
\operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm b})=\dim R
=\dim S+\dim(R/S)
\geq m+(m-1)=2m-1.
\]
The rank cannot exceed the number of treatment rows, proving \(\lvert\mathcal L_{X_{\mathrm b}}\rvert\geq2m-1\).

We now verify \(\mathcal I_{\mathrm b}^{\mathrm{fr}}\).  Every coordinate of \(\rho_i\) is at least \(1/(2m)>0\), and
\[
\mathbf 1_X^{\mathsf T}\rho_i=\frac12+\frac1{2m}m=1,
\]
so each \(\rho_i\) is a probability state.  At \(X_{\mathrm b}\), the branch effects sum coordinatewise to
\[
\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\frac12\delta_i
+\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\frac1{2(m-1)}\mathbf 1_X
=\frac12\mathbf 1_X+\frac12\mathbf 1_X
=\mathbf 1_X.
\]
At \(Y_{\mathrm b}\), both the effects and states are coordinate vectors and \(\sum_y e_y=\mathbf 1\).  Thus \(\texttt{ass:instrument-normalization}\) holds.  The treatment effects span \(\mathbb R^m\) because they contain nonzero multiples of every \(\delta_i\).

To verify the state span, suppose \(\sum_i\alpha_i\rho_i=0\), and put \(A=\sum_i\alpha_i\).  Its \(j\)-th coordinate says
\[
\frac12\alpha_j+\frac1{2m}A=0.
\]
Summing over \(j\) gives \(A=0\), and then every \(\alpha_j=0\).  Hence \(\rho_0,\ldots,\rho_{m-1}\) form a basis of \(\mathbb R^m\).  The terminal effects and states \((e_y)_y\) likewise form the coordinate basis of \(\mathbb R^k\).

The first \(m\) treatment rows span
\[
U=\{u\rho_0^{\mathsf T}:u\in\mathbb R^m\},
\qquad \dim U=m,
\]
and the remaining \(m-1\) rows span
\[
V=\{\mathbf 1_Xv^{\mathsf T}:v\in\operatorname{span}(\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_{m-1})\},
\qquad \dim V=m-1.
\]
Because the \(\rho_i\) form a basis, there is a dual vector \(w_0\) with \(\rho_0^{\mathsf T}w_0=1\) and \(\rho_i^{\mathsf T}w_0=0\) for \(1\leq i<m\).  If \(M\in U\cap V\), write \(M=u\rho_0^{\mathsf T}=\mathbf 1_Xv^{\mathsf T}\).  Right multiplication by \(w_0\) gives \(u=0\), because \(v\in\operatorname{span}(\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_{m-1})\).  Thus \(M=0\), so \(U\cap V=\{0\}\), and
\[
\operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm b}^{\mathrm{fr}})
=\dim(U+V)=m+(m-1)=2m-1.
\]
Moreover,
\[
\mathbf 1_X\rho_0^{\mathsf T}
=2\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\left(\frac12\delta_i\rho_0^{\mathsf T}\right)\in U,
\]
while \(\mathbf 1_X\rho_i^{\mathsf T}\in V\) for \(1\leq i<m\).  Since the \(\rho_i\) form a basis, every \(\mathbf 1_Xt^{\mathsf T}\), and hence every \(r_{z^{\circ}}=\mathbf 1_X\delta_{z^{\circ}}^{\mathsf T}\), lies in \(U+V=\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b}^{\mathrm{fr}})\).

Fix a class \(\mathfrak C\) satisfying the displayed inclusions.  Every terminal effect \(e_y\) occurs in the construction, so \(\texttt{thm:bow-rowspan-iff}\), applied to every \((z^{\circ},y)\), gives
\[
\operatorname{GID}_{\mathcal I_{\mathrm b}^{\mathrm{fr}}}^{\mathfrak C}(Q_{z^{\circ},y}).
\]
This includes the full finite class by taking \(\mathfrak C=\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{fin}}^{\mathrm{edge}}\).  Conversely, for any menu in the theorem's all-query class over this fixed \(\mathfrak C\), the only-if direction of \(\texttt{thm:bow-rowspan-iff}\) implies that every \(r_{z^{\circ}}\) belongs to \(R\).  The lower-bound argument therefore applies.  The constructed menu attains the bound while satisfying every stated effect and state completeness condition, proving that the minimum is exactly \(2m-1\) for every admissible \(\mathfrak C\).  Finally,
\[
m^2-(2m-1)=(m-1)^2>0
\]
for \(m\geq2\), proving non-tomography.

When \(m=k=2\),
\[
\rho_0=\frac12e_0+\frac14\mathbf 1=(3/4,1/4)=\psi^+,
\qquad
\rho_1=\frac12e_1+\frac14\mathbf 1=(1/4,3/4)=\psi^-.
\]
The treatment and terminal branches are therefore exactly those in \(\texttt{def:binary-bow-menu}\), proving \(\mathcal I_{\mathrm b}^{\mathrm{fr}}=\mathcal I_{\mathrm b}^{\dagger}\).

As a stress test showing that treatment-effect completeness is load-bearing, omit that premise and use one normalized treatment instrument with branches \((m^{-1}\mathbf 1_X,\delta_i)\), \(0\leq i<m\).  Its row span is exactly \(S\), so its rank is \(m\) and it contains every target row, but its effects span only \(\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf 1_X\}\).  Paired with the terminal basis instrument, \(\texttt{thm:bow-rowspan-iff}\) identifies every atomic query over every admissible \(\mathfrak C\).  Hence the \(2m-1\) lower bound cannot be extended after deleting the effect-completeness premise.
