Write the two treatment-branch effects and states as columns \(\phi_0,\phi_1\in\mathbb R^2\) and \(\psi_0,\psi_1\in\mathbb R^2\).  By hypothesis, both pairs are linearly independent.  Normalization of the single two-branch treatment instrument gives
\[
 \phi_0+\phi_1=\mathbf 1.
\]
Under the standard identification of a vector in \(\mathbb R^4\) with a two-by-two matrix, \texttt{def:bow-probe-matrix} identifies the two rows of \(A_{\mathrm b}\) with \(\phi_0\psi_0^{\mathsf T}\) and \(\phi_1\psi_1^{\mathsf T}\), while \texttt{def:bow-target-coefficient} identifies \(r_z\) with \(\mathbf 1e_z^{\mathsf T}\), for \(z\in\{0,1\}\).

Suppose for contradiction that \(r_z\in\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b})\).  Then there are scalars \(c_0,c_1\) such that
\[
 \mathbf 1e_z^{\mathsf T}
 =c_0\phi_0\psi_0^{\mathsf T}+c_1\phi_1\psi_1^{\mathsf T}
 =\Phi\begin{pmatrix}c_0&0\\0&c_1\end{pmatrix}\Psi^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad
 \Phi=(\phi_0\ \phi_1),\quad \Psi=(\psi_0\ \psi_1).
\]
Both \(\Phi\) and \(\Psi\) are invertible.  Thus the rank of the right-hand side is the number of nonzero elements of \(\{c_0,c_1\}\).  The left-hand side has rank one, so exactly one coefficient, say \(c_i\), is nonzero.  Equality of column spaces then forces \(\phi_i\) to be proportional to \(\mathbf 1\).  Since \(\phi_{1-i}=\mathbf 1-\phi_i\), the other effect is also proportional to \(\mathbf 1\), contradicting independence of \(\phi_0,\phi_1\).  Therefore
\[
 r_0,r_1\notin\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b}).
\]

The independent treatment effects span \(\mathbb R^2\), as do the independent treatment states, so the treatment instrument is marginally informationally complete in both senses.  By the terminal-effect-span premise, \(e_y\) belongs to the span of the terminal effects for each binary outcome \(y\).  Applying \texttt{thm:bow-rowspan-iff} for \(z=0\) and \(z=1\), the failed treatment row-span condition implies failure of both corresponding global-identification predicates.  The terminal condition cannot repair the treatment-side obstruction.
