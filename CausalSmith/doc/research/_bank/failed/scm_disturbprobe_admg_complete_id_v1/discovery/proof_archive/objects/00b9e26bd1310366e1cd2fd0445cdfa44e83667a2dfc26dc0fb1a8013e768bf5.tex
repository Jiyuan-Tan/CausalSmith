Write \(\mathbf 1=e_0+e_1\).  By the adopted single-instrument construction in
\(\texttt{def:binary-bow-menu}\), the sum of the three treatment effects is
\[
 \frac12 e_0+\frac12 e_1+\frac12\mathbf 1
 =\frac12\mathbf 1+\frac12\mathbf 1
 =\mathbf 1.
\]
At \(Y_{\mathrm b}\), the two effects sum to
\(e_0+e_1=\mathbf 1\).  Thus the treatment instrument and the terminal
instrument obey \(\texttt{ass:instrument-normalization}\).  The treatment
effects contain the nonzero scalar multiples \(e_0/2\) and \(e_1/2\), so
their span is \(\mathbb R^2\).  The two distinct treatment states satisfy
\[
 \det\!\begin{pmatrix}3/4&1/4\\[2pt]1/4&3/4\end{pmatrix}
 =\frac{9-1}{16}=\frac12\ne0.
\]
Consequently \(\psi^+\) and \(\psi^-\) are linearly independent, and the
treatment state span is also \(\mathbb R^2\).  The terminal effects are
\(e_0,e_1\), hence span \(\mathbb R^2\).  Moreover, by the definition of
\(d_i\), for \(i\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
 (e_i\otimes e_i)(a_{\mathrm t},z_{\mathrm t})
 =\mathbf 1\{a_{\mathrm t}=i\}\mathbf 1\{z_{\mathrm t}=i\}
 =d_i(a_{\mathrm t},z_{\mathrm t}).
\]

By \(\texttt{def:binary-bow-menu}\) and
\(\texttt{def:bow-probe-matrix}\), name the three treatment rows
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 w_0&=\frac12e_0\otimes\psi^+,\\
 w_1&=\frac12e_1\otimes\psi^+,\\
 w_2&=\frac12\mathbf 1\otimes\psi^-.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Suppose \(\alpha w_0+\beta w_1+\gamma w_2=0\).  Restricting this equality
to the two coordinates with \(a_{\mathrm b}=0\) gives
\[
 \frac{\alpha}{2}\psi^++\frac{\gamma}{2}\psi^-=0.
\]
The established independence of \(\psi^+\) and \(\psi^-\) yields
\(\alpha=\gamma=0\).  Restriction to the coordinates with
\(a_{\mathrm b}=1\) then gives \((\beta/2)\psi^+=0\); since
\(\psi^+\ne0\), \(\beta=0\).  Hence \(w_0,w_1,w_2\) are linearly
independent and
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm b}^{\dagger})=3<4.
\]

Direct substitution of \(\psi^+=(3/4,1/4)\) and
\(\psi^-=(1/4,3/4)\) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \frac32\psi^+-\frac12\psi^-&=e_0,\\
 -\frac12\psi^++\frac32\psi^-&=e_1.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Since \(w_0+w_1=\tfrac12\mathbf 1\otimes\psi^+\) and
\(w_2=\tfrac12\mathbf 1\otimes\psi^-\), bilinearity of the tensor product
and \(\texttt{def:bow-target-coefficient}\) imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 r_0&=3(w_0+w_1)-w_2,\\
 r_1&=-(w_0+w_1)+3w_2.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Thus \(r_0,r_1\in\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b}^{\dagger})\).
For every \(i,j\in\{0,1\}\), the treatment row-span condition for \(r_i\)
and the terminal effect-span condition for \(e_j\) satisfy the two success
conditions of \(\texttt{thm:bow-rowspan-iff}\).  That theorem therefore
gives
\[
 \operatorname{GID}_{\mathcal I_{\mathrm b}^{\dagger}}(Q_{i,j})
\]
throughout \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{pp}}^{\mathrm{edge}}(G_{\mathrm b},\mathcal A)\),
so both binary intervention-response distributions are globally identified.
Finally, the treatment split-port table has four coordinates, whereas its
probe matrix has rank three.  The associated linear map has a nonzero kernel
and is not injective, so full treatment-table tomography is impossible.  The
identification is therefore query-specific and non-tomographic.
