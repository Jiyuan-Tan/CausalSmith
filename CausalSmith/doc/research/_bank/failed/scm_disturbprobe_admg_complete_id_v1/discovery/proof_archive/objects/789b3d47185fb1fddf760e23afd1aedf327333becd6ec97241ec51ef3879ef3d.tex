Fix \(\mathcal M\in\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{pp}}^{\mathrm{edge}}(G,\mathcal A)\), a regime \(\rho=(j_v:v\in V)\), and a branch vector \(\boldsymbol b=(b_v:v\in V)\).  By \texttt{ass:independent-sources}, conditional on the source configuration \(u\), the structural part of the finite generative mass is
\[
 \prod_{s\in\mathcal S_G}p_s(u_s)
 \prod_{v\in V}f_v\!\left(a_v\mid
 z_{\operatorname{pa}_G(v)},u_v,u_{\operatorname{bi}_G(v)}\right).
\]
For the selected local branch \(\beta_v=(j_v,b_v)\), the separable kernel contributes
\[
 K_{v,\beta_v}(z_v,a_v)
 =\psi_{v,\beta_v}(z_v)\phi_{v,\beta_v}(a_v).
\]
Therefore finite marginalization over \(u,a,z\) gives the branch probability
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P_{\mathcal M,\rho}(\boldsymbol b)
 &=\sum_{u,a,z}
 \prod_{s\in\mathcal S_G}p_s(u_s)
 \prod_{v\in V}
 f_v\!\left(a_v\mid z_{\operatorname{pa}_G(v)},u_v,
 u_{\operatorname{bi}_G(v)}\right)
 \phi_{v,(j_v,b_v)}(a_v)\psi_{v,(j_v,b_v)}(z_v)\\
 &=\sum_{a,z}W_{\mathcal M}(a\mid z)
 \prod_{v\in V}\phi_{v,(j_v,b_v)}(a_v)
 \psi_{v,(j_v,b_v)}(z_v)\\
 &=q_\rho(\boldsymbol b).
\end{aligned}
\]
The second equality is exactly \texttt{def:split-port-kernel}, and the third is \texttt{def:probe-evidence-map}.  These numbers form a probability law: eliminate vertices in reverse topological order.  When a current sink \(v\) is eliminated, \(z_v\) occurs in no remaining child mechanism, and
\[
 \sum_{a_v,b_v,z_v}f_v(a_v\mid\cdots)
 \phi_{v,(j_v,b_v)}(a_v)\psi_{v,(j_v,b_v)}(z_v)
 =\sum_{a_v}f_v(a_v\mid\cdots)
 \sum_{b_v}\phi_{v,(j_v,b_v)}(a_v)=1.
\]
Here \(\sum_{z_v}\psi_{v,(j_v,b_v)}(z_v)=1\) because each \(\psi_{v,\beta_v}\) is a probability state, \(\sum_{b_v}\phi_{v,(j_v,b_v)}(a_v)=1\) by \texttt{ass:instrument-normalization}, and each \(f_v\) is a stochastic kernel.  Acyclicity of \(G\) makes the elimination terminate, and the source masses then sum to one.

It remains to verify that the causal target is a contraction of the same tensor.  For \(a\in\prod_v\mathcal A_v\), define the split input \(z^{x}(a)\) by \(z^{x}_v(a)=x_v\) for \(v\in X\) and \(z^{x}_v(a)=a_v\) for \(v\notin X\).  Under \(\operatorname{do}(X=x)\), every kernel at a vertex in \(X\) is removed.  Equivalently, one may insert that normalized kernel and sum its otherwise unused structural output \(a_v\), because \(\sum_{a_v}f_v(a_v\mid\cdots)=1\), even when its source is shared with another vertex.  Thus
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P_{\mathcal M}(Y=y\mid\operatorname{do}(X=x))
 &=\sum_{u,a_{V\setminus X}}\mathbf 1\{a_Y=y\}
 \prod_s p_s(u_s)\prod_{v\notin X}
 f_v\!\left(a_v\mid z^x_{\operatorname{pa}_G(v)}(a),
 u_v,u_{\operatorname{bi}_G(v)}\right)\\
 &=\sum_a\mathbf 1\{a_Y=y\}
 W_{\mathcal M}\!\left(a\mid z^x(a)\right)\\
 &=\sum_{a,z}W_{\mathcal M}(a\mid z)\mathbf 1\{a_Y=y\}
 \prod_{v\in X}\mathbf 1\{z_v=x_v\}
 \prod_{v\notin X}\mathbf 1\{z_v=a_v\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The last display is \texttt{def:atomic-query-contraction}.  Hence both every accessible branch law and the atomic intervention query are the asserted contractions of \(W_{\mathcal M}\).
