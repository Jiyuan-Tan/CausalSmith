By \(\texttt{def:bow-target-coefficient}\), in the binary treatment alphabet
\[
 r_0=\mathbf 1\otimes e_0,
 \qquad
 r_1=\mathbf 1\otimes e_1.
\]
Because \(e_0,e_1\) are linearly independent, so are \(r_0,r_1\).  The premise \(r_0,r_1\in\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b})\) therefore implies
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm b})\ge2.
\]

Assume for contradiction that \(\operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm b})=2\).  The inclusion of the two-dimensional subspace generated by \(r_0,r_1\) must then be equality:
\[
 \operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b})
 =\operatorname{span}\{r_0,r_1\}
 =\{\mathbf 1\otimes s:s\in\mathbb R^2\}.
\]
Fix an arbitrary tagged treatment branch \(\beta_X\).  By \(\texttt{def:bow-probe-matrix}\), its matrix row is
\[
 \phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}\otimes
 \psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}.
\]
The state \(\psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}\) belongs to \(\Delta(\{0,1\})\); hence its coordinates are nonnegative and sum to one.  Consequently there is a coordinate \(z_*\in\{0,1\}\) with \(\psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(z_*)>0\).  Membership of the displayed product row in \(\{\mathbf 1\otimes s:s\in\mathbb R^2\}\) forces its two output coordinates at this fixed \(z_*\) to agree:
\[
 \phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(0)\psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(z_*)
 =
 \phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(1)\psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(z_*).
\]
Division is valid because the state coordinate is strictly positive, and yields
\[
 \phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(0)
 =\phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(1).
\]
Thus every tagged treatment effect is a scalar multiple of \(\mathbf 1\), including the possible zero effect.  It follows that
\[
 \operatorname{span}\{\phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}:\beta_X\in\mathcal L_{X_{\mathrm b}}\}
 \subseteq\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf 1\},
\]
which is one-dimensional.  This contradicts the assumed equality of that effect span with \(\mathbb R^2\).  Hence rank two is impossible.  Together with the earlier lower bound, this proves
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm b})\ge3.
\]
The matrix \(A_{\mathrm b}\) has exactly one row for each \(\beta_X\in\mathcal L_{X_{\mathrm b}}\), so matrix rank is at most its number of rows.  Therefore
\[
 \lvert\mathcal L_{X_{\mathrm b}}\rvert\ge3.
\]
The normalization assumption is not needed for the internal rank contradiction; it fixes the theorem's admissible design class.  The only support fact used in the division is supplied by the probability-state normalization of the selected branch, as shown above.
