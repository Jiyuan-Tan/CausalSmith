Let \(\mathcal I\) be any menu in the stated class.  Simultaneous identification includes \(\operatorname{GID}_{\mathcal I}(Q_{0,0})\) and \(\operatorname{GID}_{\mathcal I}(Q_{1,0})\).  Applying the only-if direction of \(\texttt{thm:bow-rowspan-iff}\) to these two queries gives
\[
 r_0,r_1\in\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b}).
\]
The treatment-effect span hypothesis is exactly marginal effect completeness in the binary treatment space.  The universal lower bound \(\texttt{thm:binary-bow-universal-rank-lower-bound}\) therefore applies and yields
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm b})\ge3.
\]
Each tagged treatment branch contributes one row to \(A_{\mathrm b}\), so the number of tagged treatment branches is at least the matrix rank and hence is at least three.

For the reverse attainment inequality, \(\texttt{thm:binary-bow-query-separation}\) proves that the corrected single-instrument menu \(\mathcal I_{\mathrm b}^{\dagger}\) has rank exactly three, has complete treatment and terminal effect spans, and identifies \(Q_{z,y}\) for every \(z,y\in\{0,1\}\).  Thus the attainable ranks are bounded below by three and include three, proving that the minimum is exactly three.

The treatment-effect completeness premise is load-bearing.  If it is removed, take at \(X_{\mathrm b}\) two one-branch instruments, both with effect \(\mathbf 1\), and with states \(e_0\) and \(e_1\), respectively.  Each instrument is normalized, but the treatment effects span only \(\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf 1\}\).  Its treatment matrix has the two rows \(r_0,r_1\) and rank two; paired with the terminal effect basis, \(\texttt{thm:bow-rowspan-iff}\) identifies all four atomic queries.  Hence the rank-three conclusion is correctly restricted to the marginally effect-complete class and does not imply that rank alone is sufficient.
