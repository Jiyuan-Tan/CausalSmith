By \(\texttt{def:bow-target-coefficient}\),
\[
 r_0=\mathbf 1\otimes e_0
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 r_1=\mathbf 1\otimes e_1.
\]
The vectors \(e_0,e_1\) are linearly independent, so \(r_0,r_1\) are
linearly independent.  Since both rows belong to
\(\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b})\), it follows that
\(\operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm b})\ge2\).

Suppose for contradiction that \(\operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm b})=2\).
The two-dimensional inclusion then forces
\[
 \operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b})
 =\operatorname{span}\{r_0,r_1\}
 =\{\mathbf 1\otimes s:s\in\mathbb R^2\}.
\]
Fix a tagged treatment branch \(\beta_X\).  By
\(\texttt{def:bow-probe-matrix}\), its row is
\[
 \phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}\otimes
 \psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}.
\]
The vector \(\psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}\) is a probability state.  It is
therefore nonzero, and some \(z_*\in\{0,1\}\) satisfies
\(\psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(z_*)>0\).  Membership of the displayed
product row in \(\{\mathbf 1\otimes s:s\in\mathbb R^2\}\) says that its
two treatment-output coordinates at this fixed \(z_*\) are equal.  Hence
\[
 \phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(0)
 \psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(z_*)
 =
 \phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(1)
 \psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(z_*).
\]
Division by the strictly positive state coordinate gives
\[
 \phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(0)
 =\phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(1).
\]
Thus every tagged treatment effect, including a zero effect, is proportional
to \(\mathbf 1\).  Their span is contained in the one-dimensional space
\(\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf 1\}\), contradicting the assumed marginal
effect completeness.  Rank two is impossible, and therefore
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm b})\ge3.
\]
The matrix \(A_{\mathrm b}\) has one row per tagged treatment branch, and a
matrix rank is at most its number of rows.  It follows that
\(\lvert\mathcal L_{X_{\mathrm b}}\rvert\ge3\).  Normalization is not needed
for the rank implication itself; it restricts the result to the normalized
design class in the theorem.
