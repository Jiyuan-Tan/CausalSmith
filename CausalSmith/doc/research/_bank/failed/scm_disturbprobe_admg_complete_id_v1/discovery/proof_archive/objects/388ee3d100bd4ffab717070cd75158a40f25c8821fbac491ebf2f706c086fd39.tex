Fix \(\mathcal M\in\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{fin}}^{\mathrm{edge}}(G_{\mathrm b},\mathcal A)\).  Membership in \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{fin}}^{\mathrm{edge}}\) supplies the independent source product and normalized stochastic kernels from \(\texttt{def:finite-edge-scm-class}\); zero cells are allowed.  In the bow graph, \(X_{\mathrm b}\) has no directed parent and \(Y_{\mathrm b}\) has only \(X_{\mathrm b}\) as a directed parent.  Expanding \(\texttt{def:split-port-kernel}\), the coordinate
\[
W_{\mathcal M}((a_{\mathrm b},a_{\mathrm t})\mid(z_{\mathrm b},z_{\mathrm t}))
\]
therefore does not depend on \(z_{\mathrm t}\): no structural factor has \(z_{\mathrm t}\) among its parent-input arguments.  Write this coordinate as \(W_{\mathcal M}(a_{\mathrm b},a_{\mathrm t}\mid z_{\mathrm b})\).

For a tagged treatment branch \(\beta_X\), expand \(\texttt{def:terminally-synthesized-bow-response}\) and then \(\texttt{def:probe-evidence-map}\).  All alphabets are finite, so the sums may be rearranged without a limiting argument:
\[
\begin{aligned}
q^{\mathrm b}_{\beta_X}(y;\mu^y)
&=\sum_{\beta_Y}\mu^y_{\beta_Y}
  \sum_{a_{\mathrm b},a_{\mathrm t},z_{\mathrm b},z_{\mathrm t}}
  W_{\mathcal M}(a_{\mathrm b},a_{\mathrm t}\mid z_{\mathrm b})
  \phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(a_{\mathrm b})
  \psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(z_{\mathrm b})
  \phi_{Y_{\mathrm b},\beta_Y}(a_{\mathrm t})
  \psi_{Y_{\mathrm b},\beta_Y}(z_{\mathrm t})\\
&=\sum_{a_{\mathrm b},a_{\mathrm t},z_{\mathrm b}}
  W_{\mathcal M}(a_{\mathrm b},a_{\mathrm t}\mid z_{\mathrm b})
  \phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(a_{\mathrm b})
  \psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(z_{\mathrm b})
  \sum_{\beta_Y}\mu^y_{\beta_Y}
  \phi_{Y_{\mathrm b},\beta_Y}(a_{\mathrm t}).
\end{aligned}
\]
The second equality uses \(\sum_{z_{\mathrm t}}\psi_{Y_{\mathrm b},\beta_Y}(z_{\mathrm t})=1\), which holds because every \(\psi_{Y_{\mathrm b},\beta_Y}\) is a probability state.  By \(\texttt{def:terminal-synthesis}\), the final sum over \(\beta_Y\) is \(\mathbf 1\{a_{\mathrm t}=y\}\).  Hence, using \(\texttt{def:bow-probe-matrix}\) and the fact that terminal-input independence permits evaluation at \(z_{\mathrm t}=y\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
q^{\mathrm b}_{\beta_X}(y;\mu^y)
&=\sum_{a_{\mathrm b},z_{\mathrm b}}
 \phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(a_{\mathrm b})
 \psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(z_{\mathrm b})
 W_{\mathcal M}((a_{\mathrm b},y)\mid(z_{\mathrm b},y))\\
&=\sum_{a_{\mathrm b},z_{\mathrm b}}
 A_{\mathrm b}[\beta_X,(a_{\mathrm b},z_{\mathrm b})]
 B_{\mathcal M,y}(a_{\mathrm b},z_{\mathrm b}).
\end{aligned}
\]

Next apply \(\texttt{def:atomic-query-contraction}\) with \(X=\{X_{\mathrm b}\}\) and \(Y=\{Y_{\mathrm b}\}\).  The indicators impose \(a_{\mathrm t}=y\), \(z_{\mathrm b}=z^{\circ}\), and \(z_{\mathrm t}=a_{\mathrm t}=y\).  Thus
\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_{z^{\circ},y}(\mathcal M)
&=\sum_{a_{\mathrm b}}W_{\mathcal M}((a_{\mathrm b},y)\mid(z^{\circ},y))\\
&=\sum_{a_{\mathrm b},z_{\mathrm b}}
 r_{z^{\circ}}[a_{\mathrm b},z_{\mathrm b}]
 B_{\mathcal M,y}(a_{\mathrm b},z_{\mathrm b}),
\end{aligned}
\]
where the last equality is \(\texttt{def:bow-target-coefficient}\).

Let \(b_y\in\mathbb R^{m^2}\) be the vectorization of \(B_{\mathcal M,y}\).  The two identities are
\[
q^{\mathrm b}(y;\mu^y)=A_{\mathrm b}b_y,
\qquad
Q_{z^{\circ},y}(\mathcal M)=r_{z^{\circ}}^{\mathsf T}b_y.
\]
If \(c^{\mathsf T}A_{\mathrm b}=r_{z^{\circ}}^{\mathsf T}\), then
\[
Q_{z^{\circ},y}(\mathcal M)
=c^{\mathsf T}A_{\mathrm b}b_y
=\sum_{\beta_X}c_{\beta_X}q^{\mathrm b}_{\beta_X}(y;\mu^y).
\]
The displayed vector \(A_{\mathrm b}b_y\) contains neither \(\mu^y\) nor \(c\).  Hence two admissible syntheses give the same response vector, and two admissible coefficients give the same scalar \(r_{z^{\circ}}^{\mathsf T}b_y\).  Every step used only finite summation, the graph's parent sets, and stochastic normalization; strict source or mechanism positivity was nowhere used.
