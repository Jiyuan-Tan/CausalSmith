Let \(\mathcal I\) be any menu in the class quantified by the theorem.  Simultaneous identification includes \(\operatorname{GID}_{\mathcal I}(Q_{0,0})\) and \(\operatorname{GID}_{\mathcal I}(Q_{1,0})\).  Applying the only-if direction of \(\texttt{thm:bow-rowspan-iff}\) to these two queries gives, respectively,
\[
r_0\in\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b}),
\qquad
r_1\in\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b}).
\]
The class also assumes that the tagged treatment effects span \(\mathbb R^2\).  Hence \(\texttt{thm:binary-bow-universal-rank-lower-bound}\) applies and yields
\[
\operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm b})\ge3.
\]
This proves the universal lower bound.

For attainment, \(\texttt{def:binary-bow-menu}\) gives \(\mathcal I_{\mathrm b}^{\dagger}\) exactly one treatment instrument with three branches.  By \(\texttt{thm:binary-bow-query-separation}\), this menu is normalized, its treatment effect and state spans are both \(\mathbb R^2\), its terminal effects span \(\mathbb R^2\), its matrix has rank exactly three, and every \(Q_{z^{\circ},y}\), \(z^{\circ},y\in\{0,1\}\), is globally identified on \(\mathfrak M_{\mathrm{pp}}^{\mathrm{edge}}(G_{\mathrm b},\mathcal A)\).  Thus the admissible ranks are bounded below by three and include three, so their minimum is exactly three.

The treatment-effect-span premise is load-bearing for the lower bound.  Indeed, if it is removed, take two normalized one-branch treatment instruments with common effect \(\mathbf 1\) and respective states \(e_0,e_1\).  Their two rows are \(r_0,r_1\), so their treatment matrix has rank two and contains both target rows, while their effects span only \(\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf 1\}\).  Paired with terminal effects \(e_0,e_1\), the success direction of \(\texttt{thm:bow-rowspan-iff}\) identifies all four atomic bow queries.  Hence the rank-three conclusion is a frontier for the stated effect-complete class, and the proof does not claim that rank three alone suffices.
