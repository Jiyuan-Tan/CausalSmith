For the lower bound, set
\[
  S=\operatorname{span}\{r_0,r_1\}
   =\{\mathbf 1\otimes w:w\in\mathbb R^2\}.
\]
The second equality follows from \texttt{def:bow-target-coefficient}, since
\(r_0=\mathbf 1\otimes e_0\) and
\(r_1=\mathbf 1\otimes e_1\), and \(e_0,e_1\) are the coordinate basis of
\(\mathbb R^2\).  In particular, \(\dim(S)=2\).  If both target rows belong
to \(\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b})\), then
\(S\subseteq\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b})\), so
\(\operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm b})\geq2\).

Suppose, for contradiction, that
\(\operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm b})<3\).  The preceding lower bound forces
\(\operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm b})=2\), whence
\[
  \operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b})=S.
\]
For every tagged treatment branch \(\beta_X\),
\texttt{def:bow-probe-matrix} says that its row, viewed as a two-by-two matrix,
is
\[
  \phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}
  \psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Membership of this row in \(S\) says that its two matrix rows are equal.
Writing \(\phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}=(\phi(0),\phi(1))^{\mathsf T}\), this
equality is
\[
  (\phi(0)-\phi(1))
  \psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}^{\mathsf T}=0.
\]
Every \(\psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}\) is a probability vector and therefore
is nonzero.  It follows that \(\phi(0)=\phi(1)\), so every treatment effect is
proportional to \(\mathbf 1\), including the possible zero effect.  Their
span is therefore contained in the one-dimensional space
\(\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf 1\}\), contradicting the stated marginal
effect completeness.  Thus
\(\operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm b})\geq3\).  Since matrix rank cannot exceed
the number of rows, the number of tagged treatment branches is at least
three.

It remains to prove attainment from the frozen construction, rather than
assuming it.  By \texttt{def:binary-bow-menu}, the treatment rows of
\(A_{\mathrm b}^{\dagger}\) are
\[
 u^{+}=\mathbf 1\otimes\psi^{+},\qquad
 u^{-}=\mathbf 1\otimes\psi^{-},\qquad
 v_0=e_0\otimes\psi^{+},\qquad
 v_1=e_1\otimes\psi^{+},
\]
where
\(\psi^{+}=(3/4,1/4)^{\mathsf T}\) and
\(\psi^{-}=(1/4,3/4)^{\mathsf T}\).  The one-branch instruments have effect
\(\mathbf 1\), while the two-branch instrument has effects \(e_0,e_1\).
Therefore their branchwise effect sums equal \(\mathbf 1\), as required by
\texttt{ass:instrument-normalization}, and the displayed effect family spans
\(\mathbb R^2\).

Because \(u^{+}=v_0+v_1\), the four rows have rank at most three.  On the
other hand, \(v_0,v_1,u^{-}\) are linearly independent.  Indeed, if
\(\alpha v_0+\beta v_1+\gamma u^{-}=0\), its two matrix rows give
\[
  \alpha\psi^{+}+\gamma\psi^{-}=0,
  \qquad
  \beta\psi^{+}+\gamma\psi^{-}=0.
\]
The determinant of the matrix with columns \(\psi^{+},\psi^{-}\) is
\(9/16-1/16=1/2\), so those states are linearly independent.  The first
equation yields \(\alpha=\gamma=0\), and the second then yields \(\beta=0\).
Consequently
\(\operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm b}^{\dagger})=3\).

Finally, direct arithmetic gives
\[
 e_0=\frac32\psi^{+}-\frac12\psi^{-},\qquad
 e_1=-\frac12\psi^{+}+\frac32\psi^{-}.
\]
Tensoring these equalities on the left by \(\mathbf 1\) yields
\[
 r_0=\frac32u^{+}-\frac12u^{-},\qquad
 r_1=-\frac12u^{+}+\frac32u^{-}.
\]
Thus both target rows belong to
\(\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b}^{\dagger})\).  The universal lower bound
and this explicit rank-three witness together prove that the minimum
attainable rank under the stated conditions is exactly three.
