Write the two treatment-branch effects and states as column vectors \(\phi_0,\phi_1\in\mathbb R^2\) and \(\psi_0,\psi_1\in\mathbb R^2\).  By the hypothesis of the proposition, both pairs are linearly independent.  By \texttt{ass:instrument-normalization} for the single two-branch instrument,
\[
 \phi_0+\phi_1=\mathbf 1.
\]
Under the usual identification of a length-four row with a two-by-two matrix, \texttt{def:bow-probe-matrix} identifies the two rows of \(A_{\mathrm b}\) with \(\phi_0\psi_0^{\mathsf T}\) and \(\phi_1\psi_1^{\mathsf T}\), while \texttt{def:bow-target-coefficient} identifies \(r_z\) with the rank-one matrix \(\mathbf 1 e_z^{\mathsf T}\), for \(z\in\{0,1\}\).

Suppose, toward a contradiction, that \(r_z\in\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b})\).  Then for some scalars \(c_0,c_1\),
\[
 \mathbf 1e_z^{\mathsf T}
 =c_0\phi_0\psi_0^{\mathsf T}+c_1\phi_1\psi_1^{\mathsf T}
 =\Phi\begin{pmatrix}c_0&0\\0&c_1\end{pmatrix}\Psi^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad
 \Phi=(\phi_0\ \phi_1),\quad \Psi=(\psi_0\ \psi_1).
\]
Both \(\Phi\) and \(\Psi\) are invertible.  Hence the rank of the right-hand side is the number of nonzero coefficients among \(c_0,c_1\).  The left-hand side is nonzero and has rank one, so exactly one coefficient is nonzero.  If, for example, \(c_i\ne0\), equality of the column spaces forces \(\phi_i\) to be proportional to \(\mathbf 1\).  The normalization equation then makes \(\phi_{1-i}=\mathbf 1-\phi_i\) proportional to \(\mathbf 1\) as well, contradicting the assumed linear independence of the effects.  Thus \(r_z\notin\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b})\).  This argument applies to both \(z=0\) and \(z=1\).

The two independent effects span \(\mathbb R^2\), and the two independent states span \(\mathbb R^2\), so the treatment instrument is marginally informationally complete in both senses.  The terminal target-span premise and \texttt{thm:bow-rowspan-iff} now imply failure of \(\operatorname{GID}_{\mathcal I}(Q_{0,y})\) and \(\operatorname{GID}_{\mathcal I}(Q_{1,y})\), as claimed.
