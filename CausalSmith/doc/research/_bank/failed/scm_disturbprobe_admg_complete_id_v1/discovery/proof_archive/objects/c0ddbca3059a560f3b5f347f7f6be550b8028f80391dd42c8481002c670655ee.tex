Write the two treatment-branch effects and states as columns
\(\phi_0,\phi_1\in\mathbb R^2\) and
\(\psi_0,\psi_1\in\mathbb R^2\), respectively.  By the hypotheses in the
proposition, each of the two pairs is linearly independent.  The treatment
instrument has exactly these two branches, so
\texttt{ass:instrument-normalization} gives, coordinate by coordinate,
\[
  \phi_0+\phi_1=\mathbf 1.
\]
Identify a vector indexed by \((a_{\mathrm b},z_{\mathrm b})\in\{0,1\}^2\)
with the corresponding two-by-two matrix, with row index
\(a_{\mathrm b}\) and column index \(z_{\mathrm b}\).  By
\texttt{def:bow-probe-matrix}, the two rows of \(A_{\mathrm b}\) then
correspond to the rank-one matrices
\(\phi_0\psi_0^{\mathsf T}\) and
\(\phi_1\psi_1^{\mathsf T}\).  By
\texttt{def:bow-target-coefficient}, \(r_z\) corresponds to
\(\mathbf 1e_z^{\mathsf T}\) for \(z\in\{0,1\}\).

Fix \(z\in\{0,1\}\) and suppose, toward a contradiction, that
\(r_z\in\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b})\).  There are then
\(c_0,c_1\in\mathbb R\) such that
\[
 \mathbf 1e_z^{\mathsf T}
 =c_0\phi_0\psi_0^{\mathsf T}+c_1\phi_1\psi_1^{\mathsf T}
 =\Phi
   \begin{pmatrix}c_0&0\\0&c_1\end{pmatrix}
   \Psi^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad
 \Phi=(\phi_0\ \phi_1),\quad
 \Psi=(\psi_0\ \psi_1).
\]
The assumed linear independence of the effects and of the states makes both
\(\Phi\) and \(\Psi\) invertible.  Hence the rank of the last expression is
the number of nonzero entries among \(c_0,c_1\).  Its left-hand side has rank
one, so exactly one coefficient, say \(c_i\), is nonzero.  Equality of the
column spaces of the two nonzero rank-one matrices implies
\(\operatorname{span}\{\phi_i\}=\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf 1\}\).
Thus \(\phi_i=\lambda\mathbf 1\) for some nonzero \(\lambda\).  The displayed
normalization equation then gives
\(\phi_{1-i}=(1-\lambda)\mathbf 1\), contradicting the assumed linear
independence of \(\phi_0,\phi_1\).  Consequently
\[
  r_0\notin\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b})
  \quad\text{and}\quad
  r_1\notin\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b}).
\]

The independent effect pair spans \(\mathbb R^2\), and the independent state
pair spans \(\mathbb R^2\), so the treatment instrument is marginally
informationally complete in each margin.  The proposition separately assumes
that the terminal effects span \(\mathbb R^2\), so for each binary outcome
coordinate \(y\), \(e_y\) lies in their span.  Applying the ``only if''
direction of \texttt{thm:bow-rowspan-iff}, the failure of the treatment
row-span condition for \(z=0\) and for \(z=1\) implies failure of the two
corresponding global probe-identification predicates.  This proves every
claim in the proposition.
