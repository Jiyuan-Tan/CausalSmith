Put \(S=\operatorname{span}\{r_0,r_1\}\).  By
\texttt{def:bow-target-coefficient}, with the coordinates ordered by
\((a_{\mathrm b},z_{\mathrm b})\),
\[
 r_0=\mathbf 1\otimes e_0,
 \qquad
 r_1=\mathbf 1\otimes e_1.
\]
Since \(e_0,e_1\) are the coordinate basis of \(\mathbb R^2\),
\[
 S=\{\mathbf 1\otimes t:t\in\mathbb R^2\}
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \dim S=2.
\]
Indeed, the linear map \(t\mapsto\mathbf 1\otimes t\) is injective because
\(\mathbf 1\ne0\).

Now let \(\mathcal I\) be any menu satisfying the hypotheses of the theorem.
If \(r_0,r_1\in\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b})\), then
\(S\subseteq\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b})\), and hence
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm b})\geq\dim S=2.
\]
Suppose for contradiction that \(\operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm b})<3\).
The preceding inequality then gives
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm b})=2
 \quad\text{and therefore}\quad
 \operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b})=S.
\]
Fix a tagged treatment branch \(\beta_X\).  By
\texttt{def:bow-probe-matrix}, its row, reshaped as a matrix whose rows are
indexed by \(a_{\mathrm b}\) and whose columns are indexed by
\(z_{\mathrm b}\), is
\[
 R_{\beta_X}
 =\phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}
   \psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Every element of \(S\), under the same reshaping, has its two rows equal.
Writing
\(\phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}=(\phi(0),\phi(1))^{\mathsf T}\), membership
of \(R_{\beta_X}\) in \(S\) consequently gives
\[
 \bigl(\phi(0)-\phi(1)\bigr)
 \psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}^{\mathsf T}=0.
\]
The vector \(\psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}\) is a probability state, so its
coordinates sum to one and it is nonzero.  Thus at least one of its
coordinates is positive, and the displayed equality implies
\(\phi(0)=\phi(1)\).  Since \(\beta_X\) was arbitrary, every treatment
effect is a scalar multiple of \(\mathbf 1\).  Hence
\[
 \operatorname{span}\{\phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}:
       \beta_X\in\mathcal L_{X_{\mathrm b}}\}
 \subseteq\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf 1\},
\]
which contradicts the assumed equality of the left-hand side with
\(\mathbb R^2\).  Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm b})\geq3.
\]
Because the rank of a matrix is at most its number of rows, this also yields
\(\lvert\mathcal L_{X_{\mathrm b}}\rvert\geq3\).

It remains to verify attainment by the three-row menu specified in the
replacement of \texttt{def:binary-bow-menu}.  Denote its treatment rows by
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 w_0&=\tfrac12 e_0\otimes\psi^{+},\\
 w_1&=\tfrac12 e_1\otimes\psi^{+},\\
 w_2&=\tfrac12\mathbf 1\otimes\psi^{-}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
All three effects are nonnegative, and
\[
 \tfrac12e_0+\tfrac12e_1+\tfrac12\mathbf 1
 =e_0+e_1=\mathbf 1,
\]
so the single treatment instrument obeys
\texttt{ass:instrument-normalization}.  Moreover,
\(\psi^{+}=(3/4,1/4)^{\mathsf T}\) and
\(\psi^{-}=(1/4,3/4)^{\mathsf T}\) are strictly positive probability
states.  The treatment effects are marginally complete because
\[
 e_0=2\bigl(\tfrac12e_0\bigr),
 \qquad
 e_1=2\bigl(\tfrac12e_1\bigr).
\]

The two states are linearly independent, since
\[
 \det\begin{pmatrix}3/4&1/4\\[2pt]1/4&3/4\end{pmatrix}
 =\frac{9}{16}-\frac{1}{16}=\frac12\ne0.
\]
To prove independence of the three rows, suppose
\(\alpha w_0+\beta w_1+\gamma w_2=0\).  Comparing the two output-indexed
blocks and multiplying by two gives
\[
 \alpha\psi^{+}+\gamma\psi^{-}=0,
 \qquad
 \beta\psi^{+}+\gamma\psi^{-}=0.
\]
Linear independence of \(\psi^{+},\psi^{-}\) applied to the first equality
gives \(\alpha=\gamma=0\), and the second then gives \(\beta=0\).  Thus
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm b}^{\dagger})=3.
\]

Finally, direct arithmetic gives
\[
 e_0=\tfrac32\psi^{+}-\tfrac12\psi^{-},
 \qquad
 e_1=-\tfrac12\psi^{+}+\tfrac32\psi^{-}.
\]
Since
\[
 w_0+w_1=\tfrac12\mathbf 1\otimes\psi^{+}
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 w_2=\tfrac12\mathbf 1\otimes\psi^{-},
\]
the target rows satisfy
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 r_0&=\mathbf 1\otimes e_0
     =3(w_0+w_1)-w_2,\\
 r_1&=\mathbf 1\otimes e_1
     =-(w_0+w_1)+3w_2.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Consequently \(r_0,r_1\in\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b}^{\dagger})\).
The universal lower bound and this normalized, marginally effect-complete
rank-three witness prove that the minimum attainable treatment branch-matrix
rank is exactly three.  The argument establishes only this minimum and its
attainment; it does not assert that an arbitrary rank-three menu contains
the two target rows.
