Because \(m=2\) and the treatment menu consists of one two-branch instrument, enumerate its tagged branches by \(i\in\{0,1\}\), and write their effect and state vectors as \(\phi_i,\psi_i\in\mathbb R^2\).  By the linear-independence premises, the matrices
\[
 \Phi=(\phi_0\ \phi_1)
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 \Psi=(\psi_0\ \psi_1)
\]
are invertible.  Assumption \texttt{ass:instrument-normalization}, applied coordinatewise to the unique treatment instrument, gives
\[
 \phi_0+phi_1=\mathbf 1,
 \tag{1}
\]
where \(\mathbf 1=(1,1)^{\mathsf T}\).

Identify a vector indexed by \((a_{\mathrm b},z_{\mathrm b})\in\{0,1\}^2\) with its corresponding two-by-two matrix, with output index \(a_{\mathrm b}\) as the row index and input index \(z_{\mathrm b}\) as the column index.  By \texttt{def:bow-probe-matrix}, the two rows of \(A_{\mathrm b}\) then correspond to
\[
 \phi_0\psi_0^{\mathsf T}
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 \phi_1\psi_1^{\mathsf T}.
 \tag{2}
\]
By \texttt{def:bow-target-coefficient}, for \(z\in\{0,1\}\) the coefficient \(r_z\) corresponds to
\[
 \mathbf 1 e_z^{\mathsf T}.
 \tag{3}
\]

Fix \(z\in\{0,1\}\) and suppose, toward a contradiction, that \(r_z\in\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b})\).  Equations (2)--(3) give scalars \(c_0,c_1\) such that
\[
 \mathbf 1e_z^{\mathsf T}
 =c_0\phi_0\psi_0^{\mathsf T}+c_1\phi_1\psi_1^{\mathsf T}
 =\Phi
   \begin{pmatrix}c_0&0\\0&c_1\end{pmatrix}
   \Psi^{\mathsf T}.
 \tag{4}
\]
The left side of (4) is nonzero and has rank one.  Since left and right multiplication by the invertible matrices \(\Phi\) and \(\Psi^{\mathsf T}\) preserve rank, (4) implies
\[
 1=\operatorname{rank}\!\begin{pmatrix}c_0&0\\0&c_1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Hence exactly one of \(c_0,c_1\) is nonzero.  Let \(i\) be its index.  Equation (4) reduces to
\[
 \mathbf 1e_z^{\mathsf T}=c_i\phi_i\psi_i^{\mathsf T}.
 \tag{5}
\]
Both sides of (5) are nonzero rank-one matrices, so equality of their column spaces yields
\[
 \operatorname{span}\{\mathbf 1\}=\operatorname{span}\{\phi_i\}.
\]
Thus \(\phi_i=\lambda\mathbf 1\) for some \(\lambda\ne0\).  Equation (1) then gives
\[
 \phi_{1-i}=(1-\lambda)\mathbf 1,
\]
so \(\phi_0\) and \(\phi_1\) are linearly dependent, including the boundary case \(\lambda=1\) in which \(\phi_{1-i}=0\).  This contradicts the assumed linear independence of the two effects.  Therefore \(r_z\notin\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b})\).  Since \(z\) was arbitrary,
\[
 r_0,r_1\notin\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b}).
 \tag{6}
\]

The two independent treatment effects span \(\mathbb R^2\), and the two independent treatment states also span \(\mathbb R^2\); hence the treatment instrument is marginally informationally complete in both margins.  By the terminal-effect-span premise, \(e_y\) lies in the span of the terminal effects for each \(y\in\{0,1\}\).  Nevertheless, (6) violates the treatment row-span condition in \texttt{thm:bow-rowspan-iff} for each intervention value \(z\).  That theorem therefore gives, for every \(z,y\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
 \neg\operatorname{GID}_{\mathcal I}(Q_{z,y}).
\]
Thus neither binary intervention-response distribution is globally identified, despite separate marginal informational completeness and the terminal effect span.
