By \(\texttt{def:bow-target-coefficient}\), in the binary case
\[
r_0=\mathbf 1\otimes e_0,
\qquad
r_1=\mathbf 1\otimes e_1.
\]
Since \(e_0,e_1\) are linearly independent, \(r_0,r_1\) are linearly independent.  Their assumed membership in \(\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b})\) therefore gives \(\operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm b})\ge2\).

Assume for contradiction that \(\operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm b})=2\).  The preceding two-dimensional inclusion then forces
\[
\operatorname{row}(A_{\mathrm b})
=\operatorname{span}\{r_0,r_1\}
=\{\mathbf 1\otimes s:s\in\mathbb R^2\}.
\]
Fix any tagged treatment branch \(\beta_X\).  By \(\texttt{def:bow-probe-matrix}\), its row is
\[
\phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}\otimes
\psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}.
\]
The state \(\psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}\) belongs to the probability simplex on the nonempty binary alphabet, so its coordinates are nonnegative and sum to one.  Hence there is a \(z_*\in\{0,1\}\) with \(\psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(z_*)>0\).  Membership of this product row in \(\{\mathbf 1\otimes s:s\in\mathbb R^2\}\) makes its two treatment-output coordinates at \(z_*\) equal:
\[
\phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(0)\psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(z_*)
=
\phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(1)\psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(z_*).
\]
Division by the strictly positive coordinate \(\psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(z_*)\) gives
\[
\phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(0)
=\phi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}(1).
\]
Thus every tagged treatment effect is a scalar multiple of \(\mathbf 1\), including the possible zero effect.  Their span is contained in the one-dimensional subspace \(\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf 1\}\), contradicting the assumed equality with \(\mathbb R^2\).  Rank two is impossible.  Together with the initial rank lower bound, this proves
\[
\operatorname{rank}(A_{\mathrm b})\ge3.
\]
Because \(A_{\mathrm b}\) has exactly one row for each \(\beta_X\in\mathcal L_{X_{\mathrm b}}\), its rank is at most its number of rows.  Therefore \(\lvert\mathcal L_{X_{\mathrm b}}\rvert\ge3\).  Instrument normalization is part of the quantified design class; the argument uses, in addition, only the probability-state normalization already built into the type of each \(\psi_{X_{\mathrm b},\beta_X}\).
