%%% FIELD pre-score-statement
Let the finite-array pre-score covariance \(\Omega_\ell\) be the covariance on \(L\) of the influence-function linearization of the centered pooled empirical quantile score at \(w_\ell\).  Under \(\mathrm{ass:fixed\mbox{-}panel}\), \(\mathrm{ass:cell\mbox{-}independence}\), \(\mathrm{ass:smooth\mbox{-}pre}\), and \(\mathrm{ass:strong\mbox{-}gram}\), the empirical score equals this bounded influence sum plus \(o_{\Pr}(1)\), uniformly over \(\mathcal M\), and its bounded-Lipschitz distance from \(N_L(0,\Omega_\ell)\) tends to zero uniformly.  Every subsequence therefore has a further subsequence on which \(w_\ell\to w^0\in\Delta_J\), \(\Omega_\ell\to\Omega\), and the pre-score converges to \(g\sim N_L(0,\Omega)\).  On the same subsequence, the localized empirical criterion converges to \(v^\top Hv-2v^\top g\) on \(D_h\), so its unique minimizer is \(V_h\).

%%% FIELD pre-score-proof
Write \(f_{jt,\ell}\) for a donor pre-density and \(f^N_{0t,\ell}\) for the treated pre-density.  For a generic pre cell with distribution function \(F\), quantile \(Q\), and density \(f\), define, for an observation \(x\),
\[
 \varphi_{F}(x,u)=
 \frac{u-\mathbf 1\{x\leq Q(u)\}}{f(Q(u))},
 \qquad 0<u<1.
\]
The denominator is at least \(f_{\min}>0\) by \(\mathrm{ass:smooth\mbox{-}pre}\), so \(|\varphi_F(x,u)|\leq f_{\min}^{-1}\).  Let \(\mathbb Z_{jt,\ell}\) and \(\mathbb Z^N_{0t,\ell}\) be the root-\(n_\ell\) sample averages of these centered functions in the corresponding cells.

We first verify the quantile linearization uniformly.  On the DKW event
\(\|\widehat F-F\|_\infty\leq e\), monotonicity and \(f\geq f_{\min}\) give
\[
 \|\widehat Q-Q\|_\infty\leq e/f_{\min}.
\]
For \(e=C\sqrt{\log n_\ell/n_\ell}\), the complement of the simultaneous event over the fixed number of pre cells is \(O(n_\ell^{-2})\) by \(\mathrm{lem:dkw\mbox{-}massart\mbox{-}bound}\).  Taylor's formula and \(|f'|\leq L_f\) give, uniformly in \(u\),
\[
 F(\widehat Q(u))-F(Q(u))
 =f(Q(u))(\widehat Q(u)-Q(u))
 +O\bigl((\widehat Q(u)-Q(u))^2\bigr).
\]
Partition \([0,1]\) into intervals of length at most \(e\).  Bernstein's exponential bound for the binomial count on each interval, followed by a union bound over the \(O(e^{-1})\) intervals, yields
\[
 \sup_{|v-u|\leq e}
 \left|\sqrt{n_\ell}\{(\widehat F-F)(Q(v))
              -(\widehat F-F)(Q(u))\}\right|
 =O_{\Pr}\bigl(n_\ell^{-1/4}(\log n_\ell)^{3/4}\bigr)
\]
uniformly over the density class.  The same bound holds on the two endpoint intervals by the one-sided density condition.  Substitution into the inverse equation for \(\widehat Q\) and the preceding Taylor display therefore gives
\[
 \left\|\sqrt{n_\ell}(\widehat Q-Q)-\mathbb Z\right\|_{L^2}
 =O_{\Pr}\left(n_\ell^{-1/4}(\log n_\ell)^{3/4}
       +n_\ell^{-1/2}\log n_\ell\right)=o_{\Pr}(1)
\]
uniformly.  This is the quantitative inverse-map calculation underlying the delta method in \(\mathrm{lem:quantile\mbox{-}functional\mbox{-}delta\mbox{-}method}\); no post-period smoothness is used.

Let \(P_L\) be Euclidean projection onto \(L\), put
\(q^L_{t,\ell}=P_Lq_{t,\ell}\), and define the influence score
\[
 \widetilde g_\ell=
 \sum_{t=1}^{T_0}\pi_t\int_0^1q^L_{t,\ell}(u)
 \left\{\mathbb Z^N_{0t,\ell}(u)
       -\sum_{j=1}^Jw_{\ell j}\mathbb Z_{jt,\ell}(u)\right\}du.
\]
This vector lies in \(L\).  Subtracting a common outcome origin does not change \(q^L_{t,\ell}\), and every cell support has length at most \(D\); hence
\(\|q^L_{t,\ell}(u)\|_2\leq\sqrt J D\).  Thus \(\widetilde g_\ell\) is a sum of a fixed number of independent root-\(n_\ell\) averages of uniformly bounded, centered vectors.  Define
\[
 \Omega_\ell=\operatorname{Var}(\widetilde g_\ell\mid
  (F_{jt,\ell},F^N_{0t,\ell})_{j,t}).
\]
The preceding quantile expansion, Cauchy--Schwarz, and
\(\|\widehat q_{t,\ell}-q_{t,\ell}\|_2=O_{\Pr}(n_\ell^{-1/2})\) show that the centered empirical score in the localized pooled loss is
\[
 \widehat g_\ell=\widetilde g_\ell+o_{\Pr}(1)
\]
uniformly.

For completeness, Gaussianity is obtained without an invertibility condition on \(\Omega_\ell\).  Bundle the observations having the same within-cell index into independent centered vectors \(X_{k,\ell}\in L\), so that
\(\widetilde g_\ell=n_\ell^{-1/2}\sum_{k=1}^{n_\ell}X_{k,\ell}\) and \(\|X_{k,\ell}\|_2\leq C\), where \(C\) depends only on the fixed class constants.  Replace the summands successively by independent Gaussian vectors with the same covariance.  Third-order Taylor expansion gives, for every thrice continuously differentiable \(f\),
\[
 \left|\mathbb Ef(\widetilde g_\ell)-\mathbb Ef(G_\ell)\right|
 \leq C n_\ell^{-1/2}\|D^3f\|_\infty,
 \qquad G_\ell\sim N_L(0,\Omega_\ell).
\]
Mollifying a bounded one-Lipschitz function at bandwidth
\(n_\ell^{-1/8}\) makes the right side and the mollification error both \(O(n_\ell^{-1/8})\).  Hence the bounded-Lipschitz distance between \(\widetilde g_\ell\) and \(G_\ell\) tends to zero uniformly, including singular covariance matrices.

The simplex is compact, and the covariance matrices \(\Omega_\ell\) are uniformly bounded by the bounded influence representation.  Consequently every subsequence has a further subsequence satisfying
\[
 w_\ell\longrightarrow w^0\in\Delta_J,
 \qquad \Omega_\ell\longrightarrow\Omega,
 \qquad \widehat g_\ell\Rightarrow g\sim N_L(0,\Omega).
\]
Together with \(H_\ell\to H\), convergence of the empirical Gram, and coordinatewise convergence of \(\sqrt{n_\ell}w_{\ell j}\) to \(h_j\), the exact localized criterion is
\[
 n_\ell\{\widehat L_\ell(w_\ell+v/\sqrt{n_\ell})-
              \widehat L_\ell(w_\ell)\}
 =v^\top\widehat H_\ell v-2v^\top\widehat g_\ell,
 \qquad v\in\{v\in L:v_j\geq-\sqrt{n_\ell}w_{\ell j}\}.
\]
The feasible sets converge to \(D_h\).  Since \(H|_L\succeq\lambda I_L\), the limiting objective is strictly convex and coercive.  Subtracting the two variational inequalities for two scores gives
\[
 \lambda\|v-v'\|_2^2
 \leq \|v-v'\|_2\|g-g'\|_2,
\]
when the feasible set and Gram agree; the analogous bound with an additional operator-norm term holds when the Gram varies.  This proves convergence of the unique minimizer to
\(V_h=\operatorname*{argmin}_{v\in D_h}
\{v^\top Hv-2v^\top g\}\), as required.

For each cell, take the first \(\lceil n_\ell^{3/4}\rceil\) pre observations and the first \(\lceil m_\ell^{3/4}\rceil\) post observations as a deterministic pilot and use the remaining observations for an independent main-sample DSC center.  If either main block is empty, return \(R^{\mathrm{DKW}}_\ell\).  Otherwise construct simultaneous pilot regions with total exclusion probability \((n_\ell\wedge m_\ell)^{-2}\): DKW regions for pre distribution functions, one-sided finite-difference density regions using the bounds \(f_{\min},f_{\max},L_f\), the induced Gram, weight, and pre-score-covariance regions, and variance-adaptive binomial regions for every observed post atom.  Order observed support values deterministically, break all remaining ties lexicographically by cell and atom index, and replace real endpoints by outward dyadic intervals.  Enumerate every support clustering, covariance rank, endpoint order, and simplex face compatible with these regions.  An atom absent from a pilot is represented by an arbitrary location in the outcome interval and the simultaneous mass upper bound \(C\log((n_\ell\wedge m_\ell)^2)/\lceil m_\ell^{3/4}\rceil\).  Retain pilot-resolved within-cell root-\(m_\ell\) gaps as intervals; never plug in a cross-cell root-\(m_\ell\) translation or a root-\(n_\ell\) face offset.  For each enumerated configuration, apply the tail-box compactification \(\Gamma_\eta^{G,R}\), partition the nuisance and independent-standard-normal boxes into rational rectangles, enclose the loss on each rectangle by the endpoint-energy Lipschitz bound and the \(\lambda\)-strongly-convex projection modulus, and enclose every rectangle probability by rational upper and lower bounds for the Gaussian distribution function. Bisection returns the first rational upper cutoff whose certified lower coverage bound is adequate.  Add the certified loss cushion and a simultaneous bound for the distance between the full-sample center \(\widehat\tau_{t,\ell}\) and the main-sample center, multiply by the main-sample version of \(a_\ell\), and take the square root.  Cap the result at the deterministic radius \(2D\).  Use \(R^{\mathrm{DKW}}_\ell\) only before the error budget is below \(\alpha\) or if the prescribed finite work bound is exceeded. %%% FIELD pilot-theorem-statement
The radius in \(\mathrm{def:pilot\mbox{-}resolvable\mbox{-}certified\mbox{-}radius}\) is a total Borel function and satisfies
\[
 \liminf_{\ell\to\infty}\inf_{P\in\mathcal M}
 \Pr_P\{(\tau_{t,\ell})_{t>T_0}\in\mathcal C_\ell(R_\ell)\}
 \geq1-\alpha,
 \qquad
 R_\ell=O_{\Pr}(n_\ell^{-1/2}+m_\ell^{-1/4})
\]
uniformly over \(\mathcal M\).  Consider an extracted local subsequence.  Suppose every cross-cell root-\(m_\ell\) knot gap either remains bounded or, when it diverges, satisfies
\[
 \frac{\sqrt{m_\ell}
 |c_{jtr,\ell}-c_{j'tr',\ell}|}
 {m_\ell^{1/8}\sqrt{\log(n_\ell\wedge m_\ell)}}\longrightarrow\infty,
\]
and every face coordinate either has bounded \(\sqrt{n_\ell}w_{\ell j}\) or satisfies
\[
 \frac{\sqrt{n_\ell}w_{\ell j}}
 {n_\ell^{1/8}\sqrt{\log(n_\ell\wedge m_\ell)}}
 \longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Then the DKW branch has probability \(o(1)\), all finite within-cell offsets are learned, all bounded cross-cell translations and face offsets remain in the nuisance supremum, and
\[
 R_\ell^2/a_\ell\longrightarrow c^*(\eta,\alpha)
\]
in conditional probability given \(\mathscr S_\eta\).  A fixed positive unscaled separation of distinct knot groups and weights bounded away from every simplex face satisfy the displayed resolvability conditions.  Only on such a separated interior stratum, and only when the resulting critical law is continuous and nondegenerate, does the certified cutoff reduce to the corresponding plug-in oracle cutoff.

%%% FIELD pilot-theorem-proof
Put
\[
 r_{n,\ell}=\lceil n_\ell^{3/4}\rceil,
 \quad r_{m,\ell}=\lceil m_\ell^{3/4}\rceil,
 \quad N_{n,\ell}=n_\ell-r_{n,\ell},
 \quad N_{m,\ell}=m_\ell-r_{m,\ell}.
\]
Both main sample sizes are asymptotic to their full sample sizes.  The pilot and main blocks are independent by \(\mathrm{ass:cell\mbox{-}independence}\).

Let \(K=(J+1)T_1(S-1)\), let \(K_+=\max\{1,K\}\), and note that \(K\) is an upper bound on the number of post knots.  It is fixed by \(\mathrm{ass:fixed\mbox{-}panel}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:finite\mbox{-}post}\).  Allocate the pilot exclusion probability
\[
 \beta_\ell=(n_\ell\wedge m_\ell)^{-2}
\]
over the fixed number of primitive regions.  DKW gives simultaneous pre-CDF coverage at least \(1-\beta_\ell/4\).  Difference quotients over bandwidth \(b\) obey
\[
 \left|\frac{F(x+b)-F(x-b)}{2b}-f(x)\right|
 \leq L_fb/2,
\]
with the corresponding one-sided formula at a boundary.  Replacing \(F\) by its DKW band adds at most the band width divided by \(b\).  Choosing a deterministic rational \(b\downarrow0\) for which this sum tends to zero produces simultaneous density, Gram, and covariance regions.  The weight region follows from the finite-sample curvature inequality in \(\mathrm{thm:face\mbox{-}weight\mbox{-}limit}\).

For a post atom with pilot count \(N\), mass \(p\), and pilot size \(r=r_{m,\ell}\), exponential Markov inequality applied to the Bernoulli moment generating function gives, for \(x>0\),
\[
 \Pr\left\{|N/r-p|>
 2\sqrt{(N/r)x/r}+3x/r\right\}\leq2e^{-x}.
\]
Taking \(x=\log(8K_+/\beta_\ell)\) and a union bound gives simultaneous variance-adaptive mass regions.  If an atom is unseen, then
\[
 \Pr_p\{N=0\}\leq e^{-rp};
\]
hence, except on an event of probability at most \(\beta_\ell/8\), every pilot-unseen atom has
\[
 p\leq\frac{\log(8K_+/\beta_\ell)}{r_{m,\ell}}
 =o(m_\ell^{-1/2}).
\]
Its contribution to the squared \(L^2\) error is at most \(D^2p\), so after multiplication by \(\sqrt{m_\ell}\) it is \(o(1)\).  Thus omitting it from the leading Gaussian list and putting its full effect into the certified cushion is valid uniformly.

We next give the raw-to-Gaussian transfer.  Conditional on the pilot, the vector of main post cumulative counts is a sum of independent bounded vectors of dimension at most \(K\).  By \(\mathrm{lem:uniform\mbox{-}triangular\mbox{-}pre\mbox{-}score\mbox{-}linearization}\), the main pre score is a sum of bounded influence vectors plus a remainder converging uniformly to zero.  For a normalized sum \(X=N^{-1/2}\sum_{k=1}^NX_k\) of centered vectors with \(\|X_k\|_2\leq C\), and \(G\) Gaussian with the same covariance, successive replacement of \(X_k\) by Gaussian increments gives
\[
 |\mathbb Ef(X)-\mathbb Ef(G)|
 \leq C_0N^{-1/2}\|D^3f\|_\infty.
\]
If \(A\) is closed, convolve the indicator of its \(s\)-enlargement with a smooth kernel supported on a ball of radius \(s/2\).  The resulting function equals one on \(A\), vanishes outside \(A^s\), and has third derivative bounded by \(C_1s^{-3}\).  Therefore
\[
 \Pr\{X\in A^s\}\geq\Pr\{G\in A\}
   -C_2N^{-1/2}s^{-3}.
\]
With \(s=N^{-1/16}\), both the enlargement and the probability error tend to zero uniformly, without dividing by a covariance eigenvalue.  Apply this calculation separately to the independent pre and post blocks and combine it with the explicit pre linearization remainder.

On \(\mathcal B_{G,R}\), the minimizer satisfies
\(\|V_h\|_2\leq2G/\lambda\).  Subtracting the variational inequalities for two strongly convex quadratic programs gives a Lipschitz modulus depending only on \(G,\lambda,J,D\) and the finitely many active sets.  The endpoint part has the separation-free modulus
\[
 |\Psi(s)-\Psi(s')|
 \leq\left(\sum_q|b_q|\right)^2\max_q|s_q-s'_q|
\]
by \(\mathrm{lem:endpoint\mbox{-}identity}\).  Consequently the preceding enlargement of the finite Gaussian coordinate vector changes the maximum normalized loss by at most a computable quantity tending to zero.  The exact expansion in \(\mathrm{thm:finite\mbox{-}two\mbox{-}source\mbox{-}expansion}\), together with its displayed pulse bounds, gives a computable additional cushion tending to zero after division by the main-sample normalization.  Indeed the coefficient of its largest cross term is bounded by
\[
 \frac{\sqrt{N_{n,\ell}}}
 {N_{n,\ell}+\sqrt{N_{m,\ell}}}
 \leq \min\{N_{n,\ell}^{-1/2},(2N_{m,\ell}^{1/4})^{-1}\},
\]
and the DKW and Gaussian tail boxes bound the remaining factors by powers of logarithms.  This proves a uniform conditional transfer from the raw main loss to the Gaussian loss with separately recorded linearization, coupling, and loss-cushion errors.

Choose a deterministic \(\gamma_\ell\downarrow0\), for example
\(\gamma_\ell=(N_{n,\ell}\wedge N_{m,\ell})^{-1/32}\), after increasing the finite constants in the preceding bounds.  The certified Gaussian cutoff is computed at coverage
\(1-\alpha+8\gamma_\ell+\beta_\ell\).  Allocate one \(\gamma_\ell\) each to pre linearization, finite-dimensional Gaussian replacement, the Gaussian tail box, main-to-full center translation, and probability interval width, and allocate the remaining three to the loss and nuisance enclosures.  For all sufficiently large \(\ell\), this level is below one.  On the pilot inclusion event the true nuisance belongs to the enumerated region.  The certified lower Gaussian coverage, followed by the transfer inequality and the union bound over the displayed budgets, therefore gives
\[
 \inf_{P\in\mathcal M}
 \Pr_P\left\{
  \max_{t>T_0}\|\widetilde\tau_{t,\ell}-\tau_{t,\ell}\|_2^2
  \leq a^M_\ell(\overline c_\ell+\delta_\ell)
  \mathrel{\big|}\text{pilot}\right\}
 \geq1-\alpha+\gamma_\ell,
\]
where \(\widetilde\tau\) is the main-sample center, \(a^M_\ell=N_{n,\ell}^{-1}+N_{m,\ell}^{-1/2}\), \(\overline c_\ell\) is the certified upper cutoff, and \(\delta_\ell\downarrow0\) is the sum of the explicit loss cushions.  Removing the conditioning and the pilot inclusion event loses at most \(\beta_\ell\), so the lower coverage limit is at least \(1-\alpha\).

It remains to connect the main center to the center defining \(\mathcal C_\ell(R_\ell)\).  A full empirical average differs from the main average by a pilot fraction.  Simultaneous exponential bounds give a pre empirical-process difference
\[
 O_{\Pr}\bigl(\sqrt{r_{n,\ell}\log n_\ell}/n_\ell\bigr)
 =O_{\Pr}(n_\ell^{-5/8}\sqrt{\log n_\ell})
\]
and a post CDF difference
\[
 O_{\Pr}\bigl(\sqrt{r_{m,\ell}\log m_\ell}/m_\ell\bigr)
 =O_{\Pr}(m_\ell^{-5/8}\sqrt{\log m_\ell}).
\]
The pre inverse-density and strong-Gram bounds transfer the first display to the weights.  The transport inequality
\(\|Q_F-Q_G\|_2\leq D\|F-G\|_\infty^{1/2}\) transfers the second display to post quantiles.  Thus the simultaneous main-to-full center distance is
\[
 O_{\Pr}\bigl(n_\ell^{-5/8}\sqrt{\log n_\ell}
       +m_\ell^{-5/16}(\log m_\ell)^{1/4}\bigr)
 =o_{\Pr}(\sqrt{a_\ell}).
\]
The construction adds a bound of this order to the successful radius.  Since
\(a^M_\ell/a_\ell\to1\), the preceding coverage inequality proves the asserted uniform honesty by the subsequence principle.

The Gaussian envelope
\[
 \mathcal L_{\eta,\theta}
 \leq C_A\|g\|_2^2+C_\Psi\max_{j,t,r}|Z_{jtr}|
\]
holds with constants depending only on \(J,T_1,D,\lambda\).  It yields a common finite \((1-\alpha/2)\)-cutoff independent of the nuisance offsets.  Hence \(\overline c_\ell=O_{\Pr}(1)\), and the successful radius is
\(O_{\Pr}(\sqrt{a_\ell})
=O_{\Pr}(n_\ell^{-1/2}+m_\ell^{-1/4})\).  The fallback has the same order by \(\mathrm{thm:honest\mbox{-}rate\mbox{-}ball}\).

We now verify finite work and measurability.  The number of cluster partitions is at most \(K_+^{K_+}\), endpoint orders at most \(K_+!\), simplex faces at most \(2^J\), and covariance-rank patterns at most \(2^d\), where \(d\leq J-1+K\) is the Gaussian dimension.  The nuisance dimension is at most \(p=J-1+K\).  Let \(B_\ell\) be the deterministic bound obtained from \(2G/\lambda\), \(2R+2\sqrt{m_\ell}\), and the fixed dimension, and let \(L_\ell\) be the maximum of the finitely many rational KKT and endpoint Lipschitz bounds.  A mesh of width at most \(\gamma_\ell/L_\ell\) and cutoff bisection of width \(\gamma_\ell\) require no more than
\[
 W_\ell=
 2^{J+d}K_+^{K_+} K_+!
 \left(1+\frac{2B_\ell L_\ell}{\gamma_\ell}\right)^{d+p}
 \left(2+\left\lceil\log_2\frac{C_{\mathrm{env}}}{\gamma_\ell}\right\rceil\right)
\]
rectangle evaluations.  Prescribe the work budget to be the ceiling of this displayed deterministic number.  Rational range reduction and Taylor expansion with its signed remainder give terminating rational enclosures for the exponential and Gaussian distribution functions on every bounded rectangle.  Sorting with deterministic tie-breaking, finite enumeration, outward dyadic rounding, rational arithmetic, and selection of the first successful bracket are Borel operations.  Therefore the radius is total and Borel, and the work-budget fallback is absent once the asymptotic budget is active.

Finally consider a subsequence satisfying the two pilot-resolvability dichotomies.  The root-\(n_\ell\) width of the pilot weight region is
\[
 O_{\Pr}\bigl(\sqrt{n_\ell/r_{n,\ell}}
                  \sqrt{\log(n_\ell\wedge m_\ell)}\bigr)
 =O_{\Pr}\bigl(n_\ell^{1/8}
                  \sqrt{\log(n_\ell\wedge m_\ell)}\bigr),
\]
and the analogous root-\(m_\ell\) width for a cross-cell knot translation is
\(O_{\Pr}(m_\ell^{1/8}\sqrt{\log(n_\ell\wedge m_\ell)})\).  Hence every bounded coordinate remains in the certified nuisance supremum, while every coordinate satisfying the diverging alternative is eventually assigned to a distinct macro cluster or an inactive face.  The variance-adaptive interval proves convergence of every finite within-cell offset by the displayed rare-mass variance calculation.  Pilot pre regions give \(w_\ell\to w^0\) and \(\Omega_\ell\to\Omega\) by \(\mathrm{lem:uniform\mbox{-}triangular\mbox{-}pre\mbox{-}score\mbox{-}linearization}\).  Thus, on every fixed tail box, the enumerated quotient region converges to \(\{\eta\}\times\Gamma_\eta\).

The endpoint loss and cone projection are uniformly continuous on each enumerated compact cell.  We also verify that the vanishing level cushion does not leave a quantile gap.  On the affine support of the conditional Gaussian vector, the map from \((g,Z)\) to \(\mathcal L_{\eta,\theta}\) is continuous, nonnegative, and equals zero at zero noise.  Its image is therefore a connected interval.  Unless the loss is structurally zero, every nonempty relatively open inverse image has positive Gaussian probability.  Hence, for every \(c\) in its finite positive support and every \(\delta>0\),
\[
 \Pr_{\eta,\theta}\{\mathcal L_{\eta,\theta}\leq c+\delta\}
 >
 \Pr_{\eta,\theta}\{\mathcal L_{\eta,\theta}\leq c\}.
\]
Continuity in \(\theta\) and compactness of each tail-box nuisance region make this strict slack uniform there; the structurally zero case has cutoff zero at every nearby level.  The compactification and interval theorem \(\mathrm{thm:oracle\mbox{-}common\mbox{-}cutoff}\), applied with increasing tail boxes and decreasing grid width, now gives
\(\overline c_\ell\to c^*(\eta,\alpha)\) conditionally on \(\mathscr S_\eta\).  The main-to-full term is \(o_{\Pr}(\sqrt{a_\ell})\), \(a^M_\ell/a_\ell\to1\), and \(\delta_\ell\to0\), proving
\(R_\ell^2/a_\ell\to c^*(\eta,\alpha)\) in conditional probability.  Fixed unscaled knot gaps dominate the pilot width, and weights bounded below by a positive constant dominate the face width.  In that case the nuisance coordinates disappear.  Continuity and nondegeneracy of the remaining critical law are exactly the conditions needed to replace the certified upper quantile by the ordinary plug-in oracle quantile.  At collisions or faces, \(\mathrm{thm:bernoulli\mbox{-}honesty\mbox{-}penalty}\) forbids that reduction.

%%% FIELD obstruction-statement
For the mandated pilots \(r_{n,\ell}=\lceil n_\ell^{3/4}\rceil\) and \(r_{m,\ell}=\lceil m_\ell^{3/4}\rceil\), full-sample root-scale pilot uncertainty is respectively of order \(n_\ell^{1/8}\) for a regular simplex-face coordinate and \(m_\ell^{1/8}\) for a regular cross-cell knot translation.  There are legal arrays in \(\mathcal M\) with offsets \(n_\ell^{1/16}\) or \(m_\ell^{1/16}\) which diverge in the full local experiment but whose pilot laws have total-variation distance tending to zero from the boundary or collision pilot laws.  Hence no pilot-measurable cluster/face calibration can converge to the stratum-specific conditional cutoff on all such arrays whenever the boundary/collision and separated/inactive cutoffs differ.  By contrast, if a within-cell atom has mass \(d/\sqrt{m_\ell}\) for fixed \(d\geq0\), its pilot count consistently estimates \(d\), and a pilot-unseen atom has simultaneous mass upper bound \(o(m_\ell^{-1/2})\).  Thus the obstruction concerns cross-cell translations and face offsets, not within-cell local mass offsets.

%%% FIELD obstruction-proof
First consider the one-donor Bernoulli post subexperiment in \(\mathrm{thm:bernoulli\mbox{-}honesty\mbox{-}penalty}\).  Let the treated knot be \(1/2\), and let the donor knot be
\[
 p_\ell=\frac12+\frac{h_\ell}{\sqrt{m_\ell}},
 \qquad h_\ell=m_\ell^{1/16}.
\]
Only the donor pilot law changes.  For \(|x|\leq1/4\), Taylor's formula for the Bernoulli relative entropy gives
\[
 \operatorname{kl}(1/2+x,1/2)\leq Cx^2.
\]
Therefore the relative entropy between the donor pilot law at \(h_\ell\) and the collision pilot law is bounded by
\[
 r_{m,\ell}\operatorname{kl}(p_\ell,1/2)
 \leq C\frac{r_{m,\ell}h_\ell^2}{m_\ell}
 =O(m_\ell^{-1/8})\longrightarrow0.
\]
To keep this step internal, let \(A\) be a measurable set attaining total variation and apply the log-sum inequality to the partition \(\{A,A^c\}\).  If \(a=P(A)\) and \(b=Q(A)\), the resulting binary relative entropy has second derivative at least four along the segment joining \(a\) and \(b\); Taylor's formula gives \(\operatorname{KL}(P,Q)\geq2(a-b)^2\).  Thus \(\|P-Q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq\sqrt{\operatorname{KL}(P,Q)/2}\), and the pilot total-variation distance tends to zero.  Yet \(h_\ell\to\infty\), so the two knots are in distinct macro clusters in the full root-\(m_\ell\) experiment.

The cutoffs of these two limiting strata are different.  At collision the least stable common cutoff is \(c_B^*>1.913\) by \(\mathrm{thm:bernoulli\mbox{-}honesty\mbox{-}penalty}\).  At infinite separation, its displayed endpoint formula reduces to
\[
 |Z_0|+|Z_1|,
 \qquad Z_0,Z_1\ \text{independent }N(0,1/4).
\]
If \(U=(2Z_0+2Z_1)/\sqrt2\) and
\(V=(2Z_0-2Z_1)/\sqrt2\), then \(U,V\) are independent standard normals and
\[
 |Z_0|+|Z_1|=2^{-1/2}\max\{|U|,|V|\}.
\]
Thus its \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile is
\[
 c_{\mathrm{sep}}(\alpha)=
 2^{-1/2}\Phi^{-1}\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{1-\alpha}}2\right).
\]
At \(\alpha=0.05\), Mills' bound at \(1.913\sqrt2>2.7\) gives
\(1-\Phi(1.913\sqrt2)<0.004\), whence the distribution function at \(1.913\) exceeds \((1-0.008)^2>0.95\).  Consequently
\(c_{\mathrm{sep}}(0.05)<1.913<c_B^*\).

If a pilot-measurable cutoff converged in probability to \(c_B^*\) under collision, total-variation convergence forces the same convergence under the sequence \(h_\ell=m_\ell^{1/16}\).  It therefore cannot converge there to the strictly smaller separated-stratum cutoff.  This proves the cross-cell diagonal obstruction.

The face calculation is analogous and uses the legal smooth pre family in \(\mathrm{def:j2s2\mbox{-}instance}\).  Put
\(h_\ell=n_\ell^{1/16}\) in its weight and compare it with \(h_\ell=0\).  Only the treated pre law changes, with quantile
\[
 Q_{0,1,\ell}(u)=u+\delta_\ell\frac25u(1-u),
 \qquad \delta_\ell=h_\ell/\sqrt{n_\ell}.
\]
Under the uniform baseline, change of variables gives its one-observation relative entropy as
\[
 -\int_0^1\log\left(1+\frac25\delta_\ell(1-2u)\right)du
 \leq C\delta_\ell^2,
\]
because the linear term integrates to zero and the derivative is uniformly bounded away from zero.  Hence the pilot relative entropy is
\[
 O(r_{n,\ell}\delta_\ell^2)
 =O\left(\frac{r_{n,\ell}h_\ell^2}{n_\ell}\right)
 =O(n_\ell^{-1/8})\longrightarrow0.
\]
The pilot cannot distinguish the boundary from a full-experiment face offset tending to infinity.  In a scalar two-donor weight-dominated subexperiment with distinct post donor quantiles, the boundary projection loss is proportional to
\(\max\{W,0\}^2\), whereas the inactive-face loss is proportional to \(W^2\) for a nondegenerate centered normal \(W\).  Since \(0<\alpha<1/2\), their oracle quantiles are respectively proportional to
\([\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha)]^2\) and
\([\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha/2)]^2\), and are strictly different.  Thus the same total-variation argument rules out all-strata pilot oracle reduction at faces.

Finally let
\[
 K_+=\max\{1,(J+1)T_1(S-1)\},
 \qquad \beta_\ell=(n_\ell\wedge m_\ell)^{-2}.
\]
Let a middle atom in one post cell have mass
\(p_\ell=d/\sqrt{m_\ell}\), and let \(N_\ell\) be its pilot count.  The estimator
\(\widehat d_\ell=\sqrt{m_\ell}N_\ell/r_{m,\ell}\) satisfies
\[
 \mathbb E\widehat d_\ell=d,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(\widehat d_\ell)
 =\frac{m_\ell p_\ell(1-p_\ell)}{r_{m,\ell}}
 =O(m_\ell^{-1/4})\longrightarrow0.
\]
Chebyshev's inequality gives \(\widehat d_\ell\to d\) in probability.  If the atom is unseen, the earlier binomial calculation gives the simultaneous upper bound
\[
 p_\ell\leq
 \frac{\log(8K_+/\beta_\ell)}{r_{m,\ell}},
 \qquad
 \sqrt{m_\ell}\frac{\log(8K_+/\beta_\ell)}{r_{m,\ell}}
 =O(m_\ell^{-1/4}\log(n_\ell\wedge m_\ell))=o(1)
\]
along any jointly diverging array after replacing the logarithm by the corresponding post-sample logarithm in the allocation.  This proves the claimed distinction between learnable within-cell offsets and unlearnable pilot cross-cell or face offsets.
