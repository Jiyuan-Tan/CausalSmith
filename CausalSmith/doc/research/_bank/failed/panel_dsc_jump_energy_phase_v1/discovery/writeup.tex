\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Chen supplies residual-matching DSC, Gunsilius supplies the quantile-span model and discrete asymptotics, Alvarez--Orestes supplies continuous-cell quantile-mixture inference, and the cited conditional and active-set papers supply separate calibration ingredients.  No located result proves a measurable uniformly honest raw-sample radius that combines atomic endpoint conditioning, estimated simplex-face weights, changing support and covariance rank, nuisance-exclusion budgeting, and sharp conditional-cutoff attainment on pilot-unresolvable diagonal arrays.

\paragraph{Niche.} Fixed donors, independent pre and post cell samples, one exact stable span weight, smooth pre laws with finite-array curvature, and unrestricted finite post supports produce a two-scale endpoint--face experiment.  The limiting weight \(w^0\) and pre-score covariance \(\Omega\) are extracted with the knot and phase coordinates.  Within-cell masses of order \(m_\ell^{-1/2}\) are pilot-learnable, while cross-cell translations and root-\(n_\ell\) face offsets are not; quantitative pilot resolvability separates the regimes where certified sharp convergence is proved from the diagonal obstruction.

\paragraph{Fill.} The project proves the exact expansion and phase laws, optimal-rate honest DKW balls, the Bernoulli honesty penalty, the deterministic conditional-cutoff calculation, a uniform triangular pre-score linearization, and one total measurable finite-work certified radius with global honesty and sharp-cutoff convergence on explicitly pilot-resolvable regimes.   \item \(h_B\) --- \texttt{one-\allowbreak{}donor Bernoulli knot offset}; \([0,\infty)\); \texttt{worked local parameter}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \texttt{Local cumulative-\allowbreak{}mass-\allowbreak{}knot displacement in the one-\allowbreak{}donor Bernoulli honesty subexperiment.}
  \item \(\Psi^B_{h_B}\) --- \texttt{one-\allowbreak{}donor Bernoulli endpoint loss}; \([0,\infty)\); \texttt{worked local loss}

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:fixed-panel]\label{ass:fixed-panel}
The counts \(J\), \(T_0\), \(T_1\), and \(S\) do not vary with \(\ell\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Fixed-dimensional distributional synthetic-control panel, \cite{Gunsilius2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:sample-divergence]\label{ass:sample-divergence}
Both \(n_\ell\to\infty\) and \(m_\ell\to\infty\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Two-sample joint divergence, \cite{VanDerVaartWellner1996}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:cell-independence]\label{ass:cell-independence}
\texttt{Observations are independent and identically distributed within each unit-\allowbreak{}-\allowbreak{}period cell and samples are independent across distinct cells and across the pre-\allowbreak{}period and post-\allowbreak{}period blocks.}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Independent repeated-cross-section cell sampling, \cite{Gunsilius2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:stable-quantile-span]\label{ass:stable-quantile-span}
For every \(\ell\) and every \(t\in\{1,\ldots,T_0\}\cup\{T_0+1,\ldots,T_0+T_1\}\), \(Q^N_{0t,\ell}=\sum_{j=1}^Jw_{\ell j}Q_{jt,\ell}\) almost everywhere on \([0,1]\), with the same \(w_\ell\) in all periods; for \(t>T_0\), \(Q^N_{0t,\ell}\) is the treated unit's counterfactual no-intervention quantile law, not the observed intervention law \(Q^I_{0t,\ell}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Exact stable quantile-span DSC restriction, \cite{Gunsilius2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:smooth-pre]\label{ass:smooth-pre}
Every pre-period cell law has a continuously differentiable density on its compact support, bounded below by \(f_{\min}\), bounded above by \(f_{\max}\), and with absolute derivative bounded by \(L_f\), including the corresponding one-sided endpoint bounds.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform smooth positive-density quantile-process condition, \cite{VanDerVaartWellner1996}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:strong-gram]\label{ass:strong-gram}
For every \(\ell\) and every \(v\in L\), the finite-array pooled population Gram satisfies \(v^\top H_\ell v\geq\lambda\|v\|_2^2\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform local quadratic identification on a boundary parameter space, \cite{Andrews1999}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:finite-post]\label{ass:finite-post}
Every observed post-period treated or donor law has at most \(S\) atoms in the common compact outcome interval of length \(D\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Finite discrete post-period laws, \cite{Gunsilius2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:nominal-level]\label{ass:nominal-level}
The nominal error probability satisfies \(0<\alpha<1/2\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Nontrivial confidence level, \cite{RobinsVanDerVaart2006}).
\end{assumption}

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For each finite root-\(m_\ell\) knot cluster, translate one anchor to zero, retain the known within-cell differences and the unresolved cross-cell translations, replace each empirical knot by \(d_{jtr}+Z_{jtr}\), retain the population anchor at \(d_{jtr}\), and set \(\Psi_t(d,Z)=-\tfrac12\sum_{q,q'}b_{tq}b_{tq'}|s_{tq}(d,Z)-s_{tq'}(d,Z)|\), summing over clusters.

\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:face-projection]\label{def:face-projection}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Constrained-\allowbreak{}Gaussian face projection and post quadratic}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Compute \(V_h=\operatorname*{argmin}_{v\in D_h}(v^\top Hv-2v^\top g)\) and then \(A_t(V_h)=V_h^\top M_tV_h\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \(H\); \(g\); \(h\); \(M_t\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:finite-gaussian-experiment]\label{def:finite-gaussian-experiment}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Finite conditional Gaussian face-\allowbreak{}-\allowbreak{}knot experiment}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Condition on \(\mathscr S_\eta=\sigma(\eta)\), where \(\eta\) includes \(w^0\) and the limiting pre-score covariance \(\Omega\).  For \(\theta\in\Gamma_\eta\), draw \((g,Z)\sim N(0,\Sigma_\eta)\), whose pre block is \(g\sim N_L(0,\Omega)\), whose post block has the within-cell bridge covariances, and whose pre and post blocks are independent.  Form \(\mathsf X_{\eta,\theta}\) from the shifted pre coordinate and shared-noise knot-group coordinates, and denote its conditional law by \(\Pr_{\eta,\theta}\).  The statistic \(\eta\) contains the limiting weight, every within-cell offset difference, and all limiting design and covariance objects; \(\theta\) contains only unresolved cross-cell group translations and simplex-face offsets.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \(\eta\); \(w^0\); \(\Omega\); \(\Gamma_\eta\); \(\Sigma_\eta\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:certified-compact-region]\label{def:certified-compact-region}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Tail-\allowbreak{}box compactification of the conditional nuisance region}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} On \(\mathcal B_{G,R}\), enumerate endpoint orders and active simplex faces, replace every face offset larger than \(2G/\lambda\) by that cap, preserve each known within-cell offset pattern, and cap successive shared-noise-group translation gaps at \(2R+2K_\eta\); the resulting finite union is \(\Gamma_\eta^{G,R}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \(\eta\); \(\Gamma_\eta\); \(G\); \(R\); \(K_\eta\); \(\lambda\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:conditional-cutoff]\label{def:conditional-cutoff}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Least common deterministic conditional cutoff}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For the admissible class \(\mathscr C_\eta\), set \(c^*(\eta,\alpha)=\inf\{c(\eta)\in\mathscr C_\eta:\inf_{\theta\in\Gamma_\eta}\Pr_{\eta,\theta}(\mathcal L_{\eta,\theta}\leq c(\eta))\geq1-\alpha\}\).

\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:j2s2-instance]\label{def:j2s2-instance}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Two-\allowbreak{}donor,\allowbreak{} two-\allowbreak{}atom face-\allowbreak{}plus-\allowbreak{}collision certification instance}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Use the common outcome interval \([0,2]\), so \(D=2\), and set \(J=2\), \(S=2\), \(T_0=T_1=1\), \(f_{\min}=1/2\), \(f_{\max}=2\), and \(L_f=4\). In the pre period let \(Q_{1,1,\ell}(u)=u\), \(Q_{2,1,\ell}(u)=u+\tfrac25u(1-u)\), \(w_\ell=(1-h_f/\sqrt{n_\ell},h_f/\sqrt{n_\ell})\), and \(Q^N_{0,1,\ell}=w_{\ell1}Q_{1,1,\ell}+w_{\ell2}Q_{2,1,\ell}\). In the post period let donor one put mass \(1/2+h_c/\sqrt{m_\ell}\) at \(0\) and the remaining mass at \(1\), let donor two put masses \(1/2\) at \(0\) and \(2\), let the treated intervention law put masses \(1/2\) at \(0\) and \(1\), and define the no-intervention counterfactual by \(Q^N_{0,2,\ell}=w_{\ell1}Q_{1,2,\ell}+w_{\ell2}Q_{2,2,\ell}\). Each treated and donor pre cell has \(n_\ell\) independent observations, and each observed treated or donor post cell has \(m_\ell\) independent observations. Take \(0\leq h_f\leq\sqrt{n_\ell}\), \(0\leq h_c\leq\sqrt{m_\ell}/2\), and \(0<\lambda\leq1/375\). These clauses fix the triangular array and its induced local experiment \(\mathfrak E_{2,2}(h_f,h_c)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \(h_f\); \(h_c\); \(n_\ell\); \(m_\ell\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:dkw-fallback]\label{def:dkw-fallback}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Finite-\allowbreak{}sample DKW fallback radius}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Using equal pre and post allocations of the error budget, set \(\varepsilon_{n,\ell}=\sqrt{\log(4(J+1)T_0/\alpha)/(2n_\ell)}\), \(\varepsilon_{m,\ell}=\sqrt{\log(4(J+1)T_1/\alpha)/(2m_\ell)}\), compute \(R_{w,\ell}\) as declared, and set \(R^{\mathrm{DKW}}_\ell=\min\{2D,2D\sqrt{\varepsilon_{m,\ell}}+D\sqrt J R_{w,\ell}\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \(\alpha\); \(n_\ell\); \(m_\ell\); \(f_{\min}\); \(\lambda\); \(J\); \(T_0\); \(T_1\); \(D\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:certification-handle]\label{def:certification-handle}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Certified nuisance-\allowbreak{}region and clustering handle}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Start from \(\mathsf{Handle}_{\mathrm{cert}}\): build simultaneous primitive confidence regions, enumerate support clusters and simplex faces consistent with them, retain exactly observed within-cell offsets, compactify unresolved translations and face offsets through \(\Gamma_\eta^{G,R}\), enclose Gaussian probabilities by deterministic interval arithmetic, spend an explicit tail and nuisance-exclusion budget, and return the rate-preserving DKW fallback if certification exceeds a deterministic work budget.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \(\mathsf{Handle}_{\mathrm{cert}}\); \(\eta\); \(\Gamma_\eta^{G,R}\); \(R^{\mathrm{DKW}}_\ell\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:pilot-resolvable-certified-radius]\label{def:pilot-resolvable-certified-radius}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Pilot-\allowbreak{}split rate-\allowbreak{}certified radius}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} The following deterministic construction directly defines \(R_\ell\).
For each cell, take the first \(\lceil n_\ell^{3/4}\rceil\) pre observations and the first \(\lceil m_\ell^{3/4}\rceil\) post observations as a deterministic pilot and use the remaining observations for an independent main-sample DSC center.  If either main block is empty, return \(R^{\mathrm{DKW}}_\ell\).  Otherwise construct simultaneous pilot regions with total exclusion probability \((n_\ell\wedge m_\ell)^{-2}\): DKW regions for pre distribution functions, one-sided finite-difference density regions using the bounds \(f_{\min},f_{\max},L_f\), the induced Gram, weight, and pre-score-covariance regions, and variance-adaptive binomial regions for every observed post atom.  Order observed support values deterministically, break all remaining ties lexicographically by cell and atom index, and replace real endpoints by outward dyadic intervals.  Enumerate every support clustering, covariance rank, endpoint order, and simplex face compatible with these regions.  An atom absent from a pilot is represented by an arbitrary location in the outcome interval and the simultaneous mass upper bound \(C\log((n_\ell\wedge m_\ell)^2)/\lceil m_\ell^{3/4}\rceil\).  Retain pilot-resolved within-cell root-\(m_\ell\) gaps as intervals; never plug in a cross-cell root-\(m_\ell\) translation or a root-\(n_\ell\) face offset.  For each enumerated configuration, apply the tail-box compactification \(\Gamma_\eta^{G,R}\), partition the nuisance and independent-standard-normal boxes into rational rectangles, enclose the loss on each rectangle by the endpoint-energy Lipschitz bound and the \(\lambda\)-strongly-convex projection modulus, and enclose every rectangle probability by rational upper and lower bounds for the Gaussian distribution function.  Use a Gaussian miscoverage level equal to \(\alpha\) minus the displayed primitive-exclusion, main-sample Gaussian-transfer, linearization, tail, center-translation, and interval-arithmetic budgets.  Bisection returns the first rational upper cutoff whose certified lower coverage bound is adequate.  Add the certified loss cushion and a simultaneous bound for the distance between the full-sample center \(\widehat\tau_{t,\ell}\) and the main-sample center, multiply by the main-sample version of \(a_\ell\), and take the square root.  Cap the result at the deterministic radius \(2D\).  Use \(R^{\mathrm{DKW}}_\ell\) only before the error budget is below \(\alpha\) or if the prescribed finite work bound is exceeded.  This defines the total radius \(R_\ell\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \(n_\ell\); \(m_\ell\); \(\alpha\); \(f_{\min}\); \(f_{\max}\); \(L_f\); \(\lambda\); \(S\); \(D\); \(R^{\mathrm{DKW}}_\ell\).
\end{definition}

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:endpoint-identity]\label{lem:endpoint-identity}
For every finite signed endpoint list satisfying \(\sum_qb_{tq,\ell}=0\), \(\|\sum_qb_{tq,\ell}\mathbf 1\{u>s_{tq,\ell}\}\|_{L^2}^2=-\tfrac12\sum_{q,q'}b_{tq,\ell}b_{tq',\ell}|s_{tq,\ell}-s_{tq',\ell}|\). Consequently the post-cell component satisfies the exact finite-array identity \(\|D_{t,\ell}^{\mathrm{post}}\|_{L^2}^2=m_\ell^{-1/2}\Psi^{\mathrm{fin}}_{t,\ell}\). The energy is zero if and only if the coefficient sum at every distinct realized endpoint is zero, and \(|\Psi(s)-\Psi(s')|\leq(\sum_q|b_q|)^2\max_q|s_q-s'_q|\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(f(u)=\sum_q b_{tq,\ell}{\bf 1}\{u>s_{tq,\ell}\}\) and abbreviate \(b_q=b_{tq,\ell}\), \(s_q=s_{tq,\ell}\).  Since \(s_q\in[0,1]\), Tonelli's theorem for this finite sum gives
\[
 \|f\|_2^2
 =\sum_{q,q'}b_qb_{q'}\int_0^1{\bf 1}\{u>s_q\}{\bf 1}\{u>s_{q'}\}\,du
 =\sum_{q,q'}b_qb_{q'}\{1-\max(s_q,s_{q'})\}.
\]
The constant term is \((\sum_qb_q)^2=0\).  Using
\(\max(x,y)=(x+y+|x-y|)/2\), the two linear terms are
\[
 -\frac12\sum_{q,q'}b_qb_{q'}(s_q+s_{q'})
 =-\Big(\sum_qb_qs_q\Big)\Big(\sum_{q'}b_{q'}\Big)=0.
\]
Thus
\[
 \|f\|_2^2=-\frac12\sum_{q,q'}b_qb_{q'}|s_q-s_{q'}|.
\]
This also proves nonnegativity of the right-hand side without an additional assumption.

For a discrete law with ordered support \(x_1\leq\cdots\leq x_R\), cumulative masses \(c_r\), and jumps \(\Delta_r=x_{r+1}-x_r\), its generalized inverse satisfies, outside the finite knot set,
\[
 Q(u)=x_1+\sum_{r<R}\Delta_r{\bf 1}\{u>c_r\},\qquad
 \widehat Q(u)-Q(u)=\sum_{r<R}\Delta_r
 \bigl({\bf 1}\{u>\widehat c_r\}-{\bf 1}\{u>c_r\}\bigr).
\]
This remains true when a population atom is unobserved, because retaining it with empirical mass zero makes the adjacent intervals telescope.  By \(\mathrm{ass:finite\mbox{-}post}\), there are finitely many terms.  Applying the displayed representation to the treated empirical error with coefficient \(-1\) and to donor \(j\) with coefficient \(w_{\ell j}\) produces exactly the separately anchored list in \(\mathrm{def:finite\mbox{-}endpoint\mbox{-}energy}\).  Every jump contributes \(a\) and \(-a\), so its total coefficient is zero.  Hence
\[
 \|D^{\mathrm{post}}_{t,\ell}\|_2^2
 =-\frac12\sum_{q,q'}b_{tq,\ell}b_{tq',\ell}
 |s_{tq,\ell}-s_{tq',\ell}|
 =m_\ell^{-1/2}\Psi^{\mathrm{fin}}_{t,\ell}.
\]

Aggregate the coefficients at each distinct endpoint \(z\) as \(B(z)=\sum_{q:s_q=z}b_q\).  Ordered from left to right, the value of \(f\) between two consecutive endpoints is the cumulative sum of the preceding \(B(z)\)'s.  Therefore \(f=0\) almost everywhere if every \(B(z)=0\).  Conversely, if \(f=0\) almost everywhere, successive differences of these interval values show \(B(z)=0\) at every endpoint below one; the condition \(\sum_qb_q=0\) then gives the same conclusion at the endpoint one.  This proves the asserted equivalence.

Finally, if \(\delta=\max_q|s_q-s'_q|\), the reverse triangle inequality yields
\[
 \big||s_q-s_{q'}|-|s'_q-s'_{q'}|\big|
 \leq |s_q-s'_q|+|s_{q'}-s'_{q'}|\leq2\delta.
\]
Consequently
\[
 |\Psi(s)-\Psi(s')|
 \leq\frac12\sum_{q,q'}|b_qb_{q'}|,2\delta
 =\Big(\sum_q|b_q|\Big)^2\delta,
\]
as required.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This exact identity keeps empirical and population endpoints separately anchored and supplies separation-free continuity before any local limit is introduced. \textit{Gap.} Gunsilius's Proposition~5 is the closest published discrete goodness-of-fit result, but the version-of-record equation was not accessible for formula-level verification and no verified statement there supplies this finite-sample independently anchored identity. \textit{Consumer.} It gives the exact unsmoothed error functional for the donor-identified post-treatment no-intervention quantile law needed in the mosquito-abundance QTE analysis of Zhai et al.

\begin{proposition}[prop:sanity-reductions]\label{prop:sanity-reductions}
For every post period \(t\in\{T_0+1,\ldots,T_0+T_1\}\), the exact stable-span definitions have the following reductions. If \(\widehat w_\ell=w_\ell\), then \(E_{t,\ell}=m_\ell^{-1/2}\Psi^{\mathrm{fin}}_{t,\ell}\). If the treated intervention law \(F^I_{0t,\ell}\) and every donor law \(F_{jt,\ell}\), \(1\leq j\leq J\), are point masses, then \(\Psi^{\mathrm{fin}}_{t,\ell}=0\) and \(E_{t,\ell}=A_{t,\ell}(\widehat w_\ell-w_\ell)=n_\ell^{-1}A_{t,\ell}(\sqrt{n_\ell}(\widehat w_\ell-w_\ell))\). Thus known weights leave only the finite-support empirical-quantile error, point-mass post laws leave only the learned-weight quadratic, and imposing both conditions makes \(E_{t,\ell}=0\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By \(\mathrm{ass:stable\mbox{-}quantile\mbox{-}span}\), for every post period,
\[
 \widehat\tau_{t,\ell}-\tau_{t,\ell}
 =D^{\mathrm{post}}_{t,\ell}
 -\sum_{j=1}^J(\widehat w_{\ell j}-w_{\ell j})Q_{jt,\ell}
 -\sum_{j=1}^J(\widehat w_{\ell j}-w_{\ell j})
   (\widehat Q_{jt,\ell}-Q_{jt,\ell}).
\]
If \(\widehat w_\ell=w_\ell\), the last two terms vanish.  The conclusion
\(E_{t,\ell}=m_\ell^{-1/2}\Psi^{\mathrm{fin}}_{t,\ell}\) then follows from \(\mathrm{lem:endpoint\mbox{-}identity}\).

If every observed post law is a point mass, a sample from any such cell equals its support point with probability one.  Thus all post empirical quantiles equal their population quantiles, \(D^{\mathrm{post}}_{t,\ell}=0\), and \(\epsilon_{t,\ell}=0\).  There are no nonzero jumps, so \(\Psi^{\mathrm{fin}}_{t,\ell}=0\).  The preceding display becomes
\[
 \widehat\tau_{t,\ell}-\tau_{t,\ell}
 =-(\widehat w_\ell-w_\ell)^\top q_{t,\ell},
\]
and hence, by \(\mathrm{def:finite\mbox{-}weight\mbox{-}quadratic}\),
\[
 E_{t,\ell}=(\widehat w_\ell-w_\ell)^\top M_{t,\ell}
 (\widehat w_\ell-w_\ell)
 =n_\ell^{-1}A_{t,\ell}\bigl(\sqrt{n_\ell}
 (\widehat w_\ell-w_\ell)\bigr).
\]
Both restrictions together make the displayed error identically zero.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} These two exact reductions audit that the endpoint and weight components correspond to the intended post-treatment counterfactual estimand. \textit{Gap.} The reductions are internal coherence checks rather than separate novelty claims; they connect the compound expansion to the fixed-weight discrete law and the standard tangent-cone quadratic special cases. \textit{Consumer.} They give referees and the Zhai et al. reanalysis immediate falsification checks for implementations with known weights or degenerate post cells.

\begin{lemma}[lem:local-subsequence-extraction]\label{lem:local-subsequence-extraction}
From every subsequence of an array in \(\mathcal M\), one can extract a further subsequence such that, for the finitely many post knots, every scaled difference \(\sqrt{m_\ell}(c_{jtr,\ell}-c_{j'tr',\ell})\) converges in \(\overline{\mathbb R}\); finite limits define within-cluster offsets \(d\), while absolute divergence defines distinct macro clusters.  Along the same extraction, \(w_\ell\to w^0\in\Delta_J\), every \(\sqrt{n_\ell}w_{\ell j}\) converges in \([0,\infty]\) to \(h_j\), \(\rho_\ell\) converges in \([0,\infty]\), \(H_\ell\to H\), the finite-array pre-score covariance satisfies \(\Omega_\ell\to\Omega\), \(q_{t,\ell}\to q_t\) in \(L^2([0,1])^J\), \(M_{t,\ell}\to M_t\) for every post period, and the bounded jump and post covariance coordinates admit joint limits.  The limiting pre score is \(g\sim N_L(0,\Omega)\).  The maintained inequality for \(H_\ell\) passes to \(v^\top Hv\geq\lambda\|v\|_2^2\) on \(L\).  This is a finite diagonal compactification, not a maintained local-alternative condition.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(I\) be the common compact outcome interval supplied by \(\mathrm{def:model\mbox{-}class}\); translating \(I\), which does not alter any sum-zero quadratic, we may write \(I=[a,a+D]\).  All selections below are made from an arbitrary given subsequence.

There are at most \((J+1)T_1(S-1)\) post-period cumulative-mass knots.  For every ordered pair of these finitely many knots, the scaled difference
\[
 z_{kk',\ell}=\sqrt{m_\ell}(c_{k,\ell}-c_{k',\ell})
\]
takes values in the compact extended real line \(\overline{\mathbb R}=[-\infty,\infty]\).  Repeated finite subsequence extraction therefore makes every \(z_{kk',\ell}\) converge.  The relation ``has a finite limiting difference'' is an equivalence relation: reflexivity and symmetry are immediate, and transitivity follows from
\(z_{kk'',\ell}=z_{kk',\ell}+z_{k'k'',\ell}\) when both limits on the right are finite.  These equivalence classes are the macro clusters.  In each class choose one knot as origin.  The finite limits relative to that origin define the offsets \(d_{jtr}\); pairs in different classes have absolute scaled difference tending to infinity.  This proves the asserted knot compactification without imposing a local-alternative assumption.

The simplex \(\Delta_J\) is compact, so a further extraction gives \(w_\ell\to w^0\in\Delta_J\).  Each coordinate \(\sqrt{n_\ell}w_{\ell j}\) belongs to the compactification \([0,\infty]\), and another finite extraction gives its limit \(h_j\).  If \(w_j^0>0\), then \(n_\ell\to\infty\) by \(\mathrm{ass:sample\mbox{-}divergence}\), so \(h_j=\infty\).  Similarly, compactness of \([0,\infty]\) gives a limit of \(\rho_\ell=n_\ell/\sqrt{m_\ell}\).

We next establish the functional selections rather than assume them.  Every quantile is nondecreasing and bounded in \(I\).  Enumerate the rationals in \([0,1]\), and successively extract so that each quantile coordinate converges at every rational.  The diagonal limit extends uniquely at continuity points to a bounded nondecreasing function.  A monotone function has only countably many discontinuities: for each positive integer \(k\), there are only finitely many jumps exceeding \(1/k\), and the union over \(k\) contains all jumps.  Hence the selected quantiles converge almost everywhere.  Their common bound by \(I\) and the elementary estimate
\[
 \int_0^1|Q_{jt,\ell}-Q_{jt}|^2du
 \leq D\int_0^1|Q_{jt,\ell}-Q_{jt}|du
\]
give \(L^2\) convergence by dominated convergence.  There are only finitely many cells, so this selection is simultaneous and yields \(q_{t,\ell}\to q_t\) in \(L^2([0,1])^J\).  Consequently
\[
 \|M_{t,\ell}-M_t\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \leq \|q_{t,\ell}-q_t\|_{L^2}
       \bigl(\|q_{t,\ell}\|_{L^2}+\|q_t\|_{L^2}\bigr)
 \longrightarrow0,
\]
and the same calculation, summed with the fixed \(\pi_t\), gives \(H_\ell\to H\).

The influence coordinates in \(\Omega_\ell\) are uniformly bounded.  Indeed, the inverse-density factor is at most \(f_{\min}^{-1}\), all relevant quantile contrasts are bounded by \(D\), and the number of cells and coordinates is fixed by \(\mathrm{ass:fixed\mbox{-}panel}\).  Thus the entries of the positive semidefinite matrices \(\Omega_\ell\) lie in a fixed compact interval.  Entrywise extraction gives \(\Omega_\ell\to\Omega\).  Atom masses, jump sizes, and their multinomial covariance entries are likewise bounded finite-dimensional coordinates, so they admit a simultaneous extraction.  The uniform score linearization in \(\mathrm{lem:uniform\mbox{-}triangular\mbox{-}pre\mbox{-}score\mbox{-}linearization}\) then gives convergence of the selected pre score to \(g\sim N_L(0,\Omega)\).

Finally, for every fixed \(v\in L\), \(H_\ell\to H\) and \(\mathrm{ass:strong\mbox{-}gram}\) imply
\[
 v^\top Hv=\lim_{\ell\to\infty}v^\top H_\ell v
 \geq\lambda\|v\|_2^2.
\]
All extractions concern a finite list or one diagonal sequence, so their composition is a further subsequence of the arbitrary initial subsequence.  This proves every asserted convergence.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The extraction turns all finite-or-diverging knot gaps and face distances into theorem-local coordinates, and it now records \(w^0\) and \(\Omega\) explicitly before any dependent Gaussian limit is used. \textit{Gap.} The compactification is a finite diagonal argument for moving atomic knots, simplex faces, and triangular score covariance; it is not a maintained local-array assumption. \textit{Consumer.} It guarantees that every potentially nonconvergent allocation has fully specified phase-law limit points with a declared pre-score covariance.

\begin{theorem}[thm:face-weight-limit]\label{thm:face-weight-limit}
Uniformly over \(\mathcal M\), \(\|\widehat w_\ell-w_\ell\|_2=O_{\Pr}(n_\ell^{-1/2})\), with curvature supplied by \(v^\top H_\ell v\geq\lambda\|v\|_2^2\) on \(L\) at every \(\ell\).  Along every extracted subsequence for which \(w_\ell\to w^0\), \(H_\ell\to H\), \(\Omega_\ell\to\Omega\), and \(\sqrt{n_\ell}w_{\ell j}\to h_j\in[0,\infty]\), the empirical pre score converges to \(g\sim N_L(0,\Omega)\) and \(\sqrt{n_\ell}(\widehat w_\ell-w_\ell)\Rightarrow V_h\), where only the subsequential limit criterion defining \(V_h\) uses \(H\).  Moreover, \(A_t(V_h)=0\) almost surely if and only if all donor post-period quantile functions coincide almost everywhere; a projection face contributes an atom at zero exactly when that face lies in \(\ker M_t\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write \(L_\ell(v)\) for the empirical criterion in \(\mathrm{def:pooled\mbox{-}weight\mbox{-}estimator}\), and localize it at the population minimizer by
\[
 \mathbb Z_\ell(z)=n_\ell\{L_\ell(w_\ell+z/\sqrt{n_\ell})-L_\ell(w_\ell)\}.
\]
The localized feasible set is
\[
 D_\ell=\{z\in L:z_j\geq-\sqrt{n_\ell}w_{\ell j},\ 1\leq j\leq J\}.
\]
Along an extracted subsequence, coordinatewise convergence of the lower bounds gives Painleve--Kuratowski convergence of \(D_\ell\) to
\(D_h=\{z\in L:z_j\geq-h_j\text{ whenever }h_j<\infty\}\): every convergent feasible sequence has a feasible limit, and every point in \(D_h\) is approximated by first moving it an arbitrarily small distance into the relative interior and then using the converging lower bounds.

By \(\mathrm{lem:uniform\mbox{-}triangular\mbox{-}pre\mbox{-}score\mbox{-}linearization}\), uniformly on each compact subset of \(L\),
\[
 \mathbb Z_\ell(z)=z^\top H_\ell z-2z^\top g_\ell+o_{\Pr}(1),
 \qquad g_\ell\Rightarrow g\sim N_L(0,\Omega).
\]
The same lemma makes the remainder uniform over \(\mathcal M\).  On an event whose probability tends uniformly to one, the empirical quadratic part has curvature at least \(\lambda/2\) on \(L\), and \(\|g_\ell\|_2\leq K\) for a fixed sufficiently large \(K\).  Since zero is feasible and \(v_\ell=\sqrt{n_\ell}(\widehat w_\ell-w_\ell)\) minimizes the localized criterion,
\[
 0\geq \mathbb Z_\ell(v_\ell)
 \geq \frac\lambda2\|v_\ell\|_2^2-2K\|v_\ell\|_2-o_{\Pr}(1).
\]
This proves \(v_\ell=O_{\Pr}(1)\) uniformly, and therefore
\(\|\widehat w_\ell-w_\ell\|_2=O_{\Pr}(n_\ell^{-1/2})\).

The limiting criterion \(z\mapsto z^\top Hz-2z^\top g\) is \(2\lambda\)-strongly convex on \(L\), so it has the unique minimizer \(V_h\) on the closed convex set \(D_h\).  Tightness, feasible-set convergence, and the preceding compact-uniform criterion convergence imply that every limit point of \(v_\ell\) minimizes this criterion.  Uniqueness therefore gives
\[
 \sqrt{n_\ell}(\widehat w_\ell-w_\ell)=v_\ell\Rightarrow V_h.
\]
This also shows explicitly that \(H\) is used only in the subsequential limit criterion; the finite-array rate uses the curvature of \(H_\ell\).

We now prove the two degeneracy assertions.  First, \(\Omega\) is positive definite on \(L\).  For nonzero \(z\in L\), strong Gram curvature gives
\[
 \sum_{s\leq T_0}\pi_s\int_0^1\{z^\top q_s(u)\}^2du>0.
\]
Thus for some \(s\) with \(\pi_s>0\), \(\phi_s=z^\top q_s\) is nonzero in \(L^2\).  The treated-cell component of the score in this direction contains the independent influence variable
\[
 I_s(X)=\int_0^1\frac{\phi_s(u)}{f^N_{0s}(Q^N_{0s}(u))}
       \{u-\mathbf 1(X\leq Q^N_{0s}(u))\}\,du.
\]
If \(I_s(X)\) were almost surely constant, its difference at any two continuity points \(x<y\) in the support would show that the integral of \(\phi_s/f^N_{0s}(Q^N_{0s})\) over \((F^N_{0s}(x),F^N_{0s}(y)]\) is zero.  The positive continuous density then forces \(\phi_s=0\) almost everywhere, a contradiction.  Hence this independent score component has positive variance, proving \(z^\top\Omega z>0\).  Consequently the Gaussian law of \(g\) has full support on \(L\).

By definition,
\[
 A_t(V_h)=V_h^\top M_tV_h
 =\int_0^1\{V_h^\top q_t(u)\}^2du.
\]
If all donor quantiles coincide almost everywhere, then \(z^\top q_t=0\) for every \(z\in L\), so \(A_t(V_h)=0\) surely.  Conversely, if the donor quantiles do not all coincide, then for some \(j,k\),
\((e_j-e_k)^\top M_t(e_j-e_k)=\|Q_{jt}-Q_{kt}\|_2^2>0\), so \(M_t\) is nonzero on \(L\).  At least one coordinate of \(w^0\) is positive and hence has \(h_j=\infty\); therefore \(D_h\) has nonempty relative interior in \(L\).  A nonzero quadratic form cannot vanish on that relative interior, so choose \(z_0\in\operatorname{ri}(D_h)\) with \(z_0^\top M_tz_0>0\).  At \(g_0=H_Lz_0\), where \(H_L\) denotes the restriction of the quadratic form to \(L\), the constraint is locally inactive and \(V_h=z_0\).  Continuity of strongly convex projection and full support of \(g\) give positive probability to a neighborhood on which \(A_t(V_h)>0\).  This proves the equivalence.

More precisely, partition the Gaussian score space into the finitely many relative interiors on which the active face of \(D_h\) is fixed.  On a face having positive score probability, \(V_h\) is an affine map with a density on that face's affine span.  Its contribution is identically zero exactly when that face is contained in \(\ker M_t\); otherwise the zero set of the nonzero quadratic has relative Lebesgue measure zero.  Hence a projection face contributes an atom at zero exactly when it lies in \(\ker M_t\), as claimed.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:finite-two-source-expansion]\label{thm:finite-two-source-expansion}
Set \(v_\ell=\sqrt{n_\ell}(\widehat w_\ell-w_\ell)\), \(\epsilon_{t,\ell}=(\widehat Q_{1t,\ell}-Q_{1t,\ell},\ldots,\widehat Q_{Jt,\ell}-Q_{Jt,\ell})^\top\), \(D_{t,\ell}=D^{\mathrm{post}}_{t,\ell}\), and \(B_{t,\ell}=\sqrt{m_\ell}\int_0^1q_{t,\ell}(u)D_{t,\ell}(u)du\). For every finite array and every \(t>T_0\), the exact decomposition is \(E_{t,\ell}=m_\ell^{-1/2}\Psi^{\mathrm{fin}}_{t,\ell}+n_\ell^{-1}A_{t,\ell}(v_\ell)-2(n_\ell m_\ell)^{-1/2}v_\ell^\top B_{t,\ell}+n_\ell^{-1}\|v_\ell^\top\epsilon_{t,\ell}\|_{L^2}^2-2n_\ell^{-1/2}\langle D_{t,\ell},v_\ell^\top\epsilon_{t,\ell}\rangle+2n_\ell^{-1}\langle v_\ell^\top q_{t,\ell},v_\ell^\top\epsilon_{t,\ell}\rangle\). Uniformly over \(\mathcal M\) and jointly over post periods, each empirical quantile error is a bounded-height finite union of pulses whose total length is \(O_{\Pr}(m_\ell^{-1/2})\); therefore \(\|D_{t,\ell}\|_{L^1}=O_{\Pr}(m_\ell^{-1/2})\), \(B_{t,\ell}=O_{\Pr}(1)\), the displayed pre-weight--post-quantile cross term is \(O_{\Pr}(n_\ell^{-1/2}m_\ell^{-1/2})\), and the last three terms are respectively \(O_{\Pr}(n_\ell^{-1}m_\ell^{-1/2})\), \(O_{\Pr}(n_\ell^{-1/2}m_\ell^{-1/2})\), and \(O_{\Pr}(n_\ell^{-1}m_\ell^{-1/2})\). Hence \(r_{t,\ell}\) is their exact sum and \(\max_{t>T_0}|r_{t,\ell}|=O_{\Pr}(n_\ell^{-1/2}m_\ell^{-1/2}+n_\ell^{-1}m_\ell^{-1/2})=o_{\Pr}(a_\ell)\). No subsequential coordinate occurs in this finite-array identity.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(t>T_0\), put \(\delta_\ell=\widehat w_\ell-w_\ell=v_\ell/\sqrt{n_\ell}\), and abbreviate \(D=D_{t,\ell}\), \(q=q_{t,\ell}\), and \(\epsilon=\epsilon_{t,\ell}\).  Exact stable span in \(\mathrm{def:model\mbox{-}class}\) gives, without a limiting argument,
\[
 \widehat\tau_{t,\ell}-\tau_{t,\ell}
 =D-\delta_\ell^\top q-\delta_\ell^\top\epsilon.
\]
Squaring in \(L^2([0,1])\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 E_{t,\ell}={}&\|D\|_2^2+\|\delta_\ell^\top q\|_2^2
 -2\langle D,\delta_\ell^\top q\rangle
 +\|\delta_\ell^\top\epsilon\|_2^2\\
 &-2\langle D,\delta_\ell^\top\epsilon\rangle
 +2\langle\delta_\ell^\top q,\delta_\ell^\top\epsilon\rangle.
\end{aligned}
\]
By \(\mathrm{lem:endpoint\mbox{-}identity}\), the first term is
\(m_\ell^{-1/2}\Psi^{\mathrm{fin}}_{t,\ell}\).  The second is
\(n_\ell^{-1}A_{t,\ell}(v_\ell)\).  By the definition of \(B_{t,\ell}\), the third is
\[
 -2(n_\ell m_\ell)^{-1/2}v_\ell^\top
 \left\{\sqrt{m_\ell}\int_0^1q(u)D(u)du\right\}
 =-2(n_\ell m_\ell)^{-1/2}v_\ell^\top B_{t,\ell}.
\]
Substituting \(\delta_\ell=v_\ell/\sqrt{n_\ell}\) in the remaining terms gives exactly the displayed three \(\epsilon_{t,\ell}\) terms.  This proves the finite-array identity and the formula for \(r_{t,\ell}\).

It remains to establish the uniform orders.  For a cell with ordered atoms, direct subtraction of the two step quantile functions gives
\[
 \widehat Q_{jt,\ell}-Q_{jt,\ell}
 =\sum_{r<S}\Delta_{jtr,\ell}
 \bigl(\mathbf 1\{u>\widehat c_{jtr,\ell}\}
       -\mathbf 1\{u>c_{jtr,\ell}\}\bigr).
\]
Thus it is a union of at most \(S-1\) signed pulses, each of height at most \(D\), and its total pulse length is at most
\[
 \sum_{r<S}|\widehat c_{jtr,\ell}-c_{jtr,\ell}|
 \leq(S-1)\|\widehat F_{jt,\ell}-F_{jt,\ell}\|_\infty.
\]
The DKW bound, fixed \(J,T_1,S\), and a union bound therefore yield, uniformly and jointly over post periods,
\[
 \|\epsilon_{t,\ell}\|_{1,2}=O_{\Pr}(m_\ell^{-1/2}),\quad
 \|D_{t,\ell}\|_1=O_{\Pr}(m_\ell^{-1/2}),\quad
 \|D_{t,\ell}\|_\infty\leq2D,
 \quad \|\epsilon_{t,\ell}\|_{\infty,2}\leq\sqrt J D.
\]
After subtracting a common endpoint of the outcome interval, which is legitimate because \(v_\ell\in L\), we also have
\(\|q_{t,\ell}\|_{\infty,2}\leq\sqrt J D\).  The already proved weight rate gives \(\|v_\ell\|_2=O_{\Pr}(1)\).  Hence
\[
 \|B_{t,\ell}\|_2
 \leq\sqrt{m_\ell}\|q_{t,\ell}\|_{\infty,2}\|D_{t,\ell}\|_1
 =O_{\Pr}(1).
\]
Using \(|\langle f,g\rangle|\leq\|f\|_1\|g\|_\infty\) and
\(\|f\|_2^2\leq\|f\|_1\|f\|_\infty\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 n_\ell^{-1}\|v_\ell^\top\epsilon\|_2^2
 &=O_{\Pr}(n_\ell^{-1}m_\ell^{-1/2}),\\
 n_\ell^{-1/2}|\langle D,v_\ell^\top\epsilon\rangle|
 &=O_{\Pr}(n_\ell^{-1/2}m_\ell^{-1/2}),\\
 n_\ell^{-1}|\langle v_\ell^\top q,v_\ell^\top\epsilon\rangle|
 &=O_{\Pr}(n_\ell^{-1}m_\ell^{-1/2}).
\end{aligned}
\]
Together with the bound for \(B_{t,\ell}\), these are the four asserted orders.  Finally,
\[
 \frac{n_\ell^{-1/2}m_\ell^{-1/2}}
 {n_\ell^{-1}+m_\ell^{-1/2}}
 \leq\min\{n_\ell^{-1/2},m_\ell^{-1/4}\}\longrightarrow0,
\]
and \(n_\ell^{-1}m_\ell^{-1/2}\) is smaller.  Since there are only \(T_1\) post periods, the maximum remainder is \(o_{\Pr}(a_\ell)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:three-phase-expansion]\label{thm:three-phase-expansion}
Along every subsequence extracted by \(\mathrm{lem:local\mbox{-}subsequence\mbox{-}extraction}\), \(w_\ell\to w^0\), \(H_\ell\to H\), \(\Omega_\ell\to\Omega\), and there is one coupling carrying deterministic local objects \(d\), \(h\), \(w^0\), \(H\), and \(\Omega\), together with all components of one jointly Gaussian vector \((g,Z)\), where \(g\sim N_L(0,\Omega)\) and the pre and post blocks are independent.  On this coupling, jointly over post periods, \(v_\ell\to V_h\), \(A_{t,\ell}(v_\ell)\to A_t(V_h)\), \(\Psi^{\mathrm{fin}}_{t,\ell}\to\Psi_t(d,Z)\), and \(B_{t,\ell}\Rightarrow B_t(d,Z)\).  The pre-weight--post-quantile cross contribution obeys \(\sqrt{n_\ell m_\ell}[-2(n_\ell m_\ell)^{-1/2}v_\ell^\top B_{t,\ell}]\Rightarrow-2V_h^\top B_t(d,Z)\), but it vanishes at every headline loss scale: multiplication by \(\sqrt{m_\ell}\) gives coefficient \(n_\ell^{-1/2}\) when \(\rho_\ell\to\infty\) or \(\rho_\ell\to\kappa\in(0,\infty)\), multiplication by \(n_\ell\) gives coefficient \(\sqrt{n_\ell/m_\ell}\) when \(\rho_\ell\to0\), and division by \(a_\ell\) gives coefficient \(\sqrt{n_\ell}/(n_\ell+\sqrt{m_\ell})\to0\).  The three remaining \(\epsilon_{t,\ell}\) terms vanish on the same scales.  Consequently, whenever \(\omega=\lim n_\ell/(n_\ell+\sqrt{m_\ell})\), \(E_{t,\ell}/a_\ell\Rightarrow(1-\omega)A_t(V_h)+\omega\Psi_t(d,Z)\).  Equivalently, if \(\rho_\ell\to\infty\), then \(\sqrt{m_\ell}E_{t,\ell}\Rightarrow\Psi_t(d,Z)\); if \(\rho_\ell\to\kappa\in(0,\infty)\), then \(\sqrt{m_\ell}E_{t,\ell}\Rightarrow\Psi_t(d,Z)+A_t(V_h)/\kappa\); and if \(\rho_\ell\to0\), then \(n_\ell E_{t,\ell}\Rightarrow A_t(V_h)\).  The endpoint component is zero almost surely if and only if each almost-sure affine-endpoint equality class has coefficient sum zero, while the quadratic component is zero almost surely if and only if all donor post quantile functions coincide almost everywhere.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Work along a subsequence supplied by \(\mathrm{lem:local\mbox{-}subsequence\mbox{-}extraction}\).  For each finite-support post cell, the bounded triangular multinomial central limit theorem gives, jointly over its finitely many knots,
\[
 \sqrt{m_\ell}(\widehat c_{jtr,\ell}-c_{jtr,\ell})
 \Rightarrow Z_{jtr},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Cov}(Z_{jtr},Z_{jtr'})
 =\min(c_{jtr},c_{jtr'})-c_{jtr}c_{jtr'}.
\]
This is the finite-coordinate consequence of
\(\mathrm{lem:empirical\mbox{-}cdf\mbox{-}functional\mbox{-}clt}\).  Distinct cells are independent, and the entire post block is independent of the pre score by \(\mathrm{ass:cell\mbox{-}independence}\).  The pre result gives \(g_\ell\Rightarrow g\sim N_L(0,\Omega)\) and \(v_\ell\Rightarrow V_h\).  Applying \(\mathrm{lem:skorokhod\mbox{-}representation}\) to this finite-dimensional joint vector, together with the finitely many selected deterministic coordinates, yields one coupling on which all coordinate convergences hold almost surely.  We use that coupling below; it implies the stated weak convergences on the original spaces.

Within one macro cluster, translate one population anchor to zero.  A population endpoint then converges to \(d_{jtr}\), while its empirical copy converges to \(d_{jtr}+Z_{jtr}\).  The coefficients converge because weights and jump sizes do.  The endpoint energy is a finite sum of absolute values, so it is continuous in these coordinates.  Two distinct macro clusters are eventually ordered and disjoint at root-\(m_\ell\) scale.  Each cluster has coefficient sum zero because every jump contributes \((a,-a)\).  For ordered clusters \(C,C'\),
\[
 \sum_{q\in C,q'\in C'}b_qb_{q'}(s_{q'}-s_q)=0,
\]
so there is no cross-cluster energy.  It follows that
\[
 \Psi^{\mathrm{fin}}_{t,\ell}\longrightarrow\Psi_t(d,Z)
\]
jointly over the fixed number of post periods.

The functional \((M,v)\mapsto v^\top Mv\) is continuous, and
\(M_{t,\ell}\to M_t\), so
\[
 A_{t,\ell}(v_\ell)\longrightarrow V_h^\top M_tV_h=A_t(V_h).
\]
For \(B_{t,\ell}\), insert the finite pulse representation from the proof of
\(\mathrm{thm:finite\mbox{-}two\mbox{-}source\mbox{-}expansion}\).  On each of the finitely many endpoint-order cells, integrating the step function \(q_{t,\ell}\) over each pulse gives a finite affine expression in its endpoint lengths and limiting atom locations.  The selected convergence of all jump coordinates and endpoint orders therefore gives
\[
 B_{t,\ell}\Rightarrow B_t(d,Z).
\]
Consequently, by joint convergence,
\[
 \sqrt{n_\ell m_\ell}\left[-2(n_\ell m_\ell)^{-1/2}
 v_\ell^\top B_{t,\ell}\right]
 \Rightarrow-2V_h^\top B_t(d,Z).
\]

We now check every headline scale.  At the \(\sqrt{m_\ell}\) scale, the coefficient of \(v_\ell^\top B_{t,\ell}\) is \(n_\ell^{-1/2}\), which tends to zero.  At the \(n_\ell\) scale it is
\(\sqrt{n_\ell/m_\ell}=\rho_\ell/\sqrt{n_\ell}\), which tends to zero when \(\rho_\ell\to0\).  At the unified scale,
\[
 \frac{(n_\ell m_\ell)^{-1/2}}{a_\ell}
 =\frac{\sqrt{n_\ell}}{n_\ell+\sqrt{m_\ell}}\longrightarrow0.
\]
The three \(\epsilon_{t,\ell}\) bounds in the finite expansion vanish under the same multipliers.  For example, division of their largest order by \(a_\ell\) is bounded by
\(\min(n_\ell^{-1/2},m_\ell^{-1/4})\), and the other two are smaller.

The two leading coefficients after division by \(a_\ell\) are exactly
\[
 \frac{m_\ell^{-1/2}}{a_\ell}
 =\frac{n_\ell}{n_\ell+\sqrt{m_\ell}}\longrightarrow\omega,
 \qquad
 \frac{n_\ell^{-1}}{a_\ell}
 =\frac{\sqrt{m_\ell}}{n_\ell+\sqrt{m_\ell}}\longrightarrow1-\omega.
\]
Because the remainder divided by \(a_\ell\) converges in probability to zero, adding it does not change any bounded-continuous-test-function limit of the jointly convergent leading vector.  The exact finite expansion therefore gives
\[
 \frac{E_{t,\ell}}{a_\ell}Rightarrow
 (1-\omega)A_t(V_h)+\omega\Psi_t(d,Z).
\]
If \(\rho_\ell\to\infty\), multiplication by \(\sqrt{m_\ell}\) leaves only \(\Psi_t\).  If \(\rho_\ell\to\kappa\in(0,\infty)\), it leaves
\(\Psi_t+A_t/\kappa\).  If \(\rho_\ell\to0\), multiplication by \(n_\ell\) leaves only \(A_t\).  These are the three asserted phase laws.

For endpoint degeneracy, \(\mathrm{lem:endpoint\mbox{-}identity}\) says that a realized energy is zero exactly when the total coefficient at every distinct realized endpoint is zero.  Endpoints are affine functions of the joint Gaussian coordinates.  Two such functions are equal with positive probability exactly when their difference vanishes on the affine support of that Gaussian vector; otherwise their equality set is a Gaussian-null affine hyperplane.  Thus almost-sure zero energy is equivalent to zero coefficient sum in every almost-sure affine-endpoint equality class.  The quadratic degeneracy is the equivalence proved in \(\mathrm{thm:face\mbox{-}weight\mbox{-}limit}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:honest-rate-ball]\label{thm:honest-rate-ball}
The DKW construction yields \(\inf_{P\in\mathcal M}\Pr_P\{(\tau_{t,\ell})_{t>T_0}\in\mathcal C_\ell(R^{\mathrm{DKW}}_\ell)\}\geq1-\alpha\) for every \(n_\ell,m_\ell\), and \(R^{\mathrm{DKW}}_\ell=O(n_\ell^{-1/2}+m_\ell^{-1/4})\) uniformly.  For the converse, if \(\mathcal M\) contains an equal-Bernoulli two-point post local subexperiment with a nonzero support jump, no uniformly honest ball has worst-case diameter \(o(m_\ell^{-1/4})\); if \(\mathcal M\) contains a smooth two-donor local-weight subexperiment whose post donor quantiles are distinct in \(L^2\), no uniformly honest ball has worst-case diameter \(o(n_\ell^{-1/2})\).  If both richness conditions hold and \(\alpha<1/2\), no uniformly honest ball has worst-case diameter \(o(n_\ell^{-1/2}+m_\ell^{-1/4})\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(K_n=(J+1)T_0\) and \(K_m=(J+1)T_1\).  By \(\mathrm{lem:dkw\mbox{-}massart\mbox{-}bound}\) and a union bound, the event
\[
 \mathcal E_n=\left\{\max_{\text{pre cells}}
 \|\widehat F-F\|_\infty\leq\varepsilon_{n,\ell}\right\}
\]
has probability at least \(1-\alpha/2\), and the analogous post event \(\mathcal E_m\) has probability at least \(1-\alpha/2\).  Hence their intersection has probability at least \(1-\alpha\).

On \(\mathcal E_n\), monotonicity and the lower density bound imply
\[
 \|\widehat Q-Q\|_\infty\leq
 \eta_{n,\ell}:=\varepsilon_{n,\ell}/f_{\min}
\]
for every pre cell, including one-sided support endpoints.  Let \(\delta=\widehat w_\ell-w_\ell\).  Empirical optimality gives a pooled empirical residual norm at \(\widehat w_\ell\) no larger than the norm at \(w_\ell\), which is at most \(2\eta_{n,\ell}\).  Replacing empirical quantiles by population quantiles costs at most another \(2\eta_{n,\ell}\).  Exact span and strong Gram curvature therefore give
\[
 \sqrt\lambda\|\delta\|_2
 \leq\left\{\sum_{t\leq T_0}\pi_t
 \|\delta^\top q_{t,\ell}\|_2^2\right\}^{1/2}
 \leq4\eta_{n,\ell}.
\]
When \(\sqrt\lambda-\sqrt J\eta_{n,\ell}>0\), this stronger inequality implies
\[
 \|\delta\|_2\leq
 \frac{4\varepsilon_{n,\ell}}
 {f_{\min}(\sqrt\lambda-\sqrt J\varepsilon_{n,\ell}/f_{\min})}.
\]
When the denominator is nonpositive, and also as an outer truncation in the other case, the diameter of the simplex gives \(\|\delta\|_2\leq\sqrt2\).  Thus \(\|\delta\|_2\leq R_{w,\ell}\) on \(\mathcal E_n\).

For probability laws supported on an interval of length \(D\), layer-cake integration and Fubini give the one-dimensional identity
\[
 \int_0^1|Q_F(u)-Q_G(u)|du
 =\int_{\mathbb R}|F(x)-G(x)|dx.
\]
Since \(|Q_F-Q_G|\leq D\), the post DKW event implies
\[
 \|Q_{\widehat F}-Q_F\|_2^2
 \leq D\|Q_{\widehat F}-Q_F\|_1
 \leq D^2\varepsilon_{m,\ell}.
\]
The triangle inequality, the simplex constraints, and
\(\|x\|_1\leq\sqrt J\|x\|_2\) now yield simultaneously over post periods
\[
 \|\widehat\tau_{t,\ell}-\tau_{t,\ell}\|_2
 \leq2D\sqrt{\varepsilon_{m,\ell}}+D\sqrt J R_{w,\ell}.
\]
The deterministic bound \(2D\) justifies the outer minimum in
\(R^{\mathrm{DKW}}_\ell\).  Therefore the product ball covers on
\(\mathcal E_n\cap\mathcal E_m\), proving the finite-sample lower bound \(1-\alpha\).  Fixed dimension and fixed class constants give
\(R_{w,\ell}=O(n_\ell^{-1/2})\) and
\(\sqrt{\varepsilon_{m,\ell}}=O(m_\ell^{-1/4})\), uniformly over \(\mathcal M\).

We prove the two converse assertions by explicit contiguous pairs.  In the post richness subexperiment, hold pre laws, donors, weights, and the no-intervention law fixed.  Let the treated intervention law have two support points separated by \(d>0\), with lower mass \(1/2\) under \(P_{0,\ell}\) and \(1/2+h/\sqrt{m_\ell}\) under \(P_{1,\ell}\).  The two target quantiles differ by \(d\) on an interval of length \(h/\sqrt{m_\ell}\), so their \(L^2\) distance is
\[
 \Delta_{m,\ell}=d\sqrt h\,m_\ell^{-1/4}.
\]
For \(|x|\leq1/4\), Taylor's theorem applied to the Bernoulli likelihood gives one-observation squared Hellinger distance at most \(C x^2\).  With \(x=h/\sqrt{m_\ell}\), the product Hellinger distance is therefore bounded away from its maximal value for sufficiently small fixed \(h>0\); the product total variation is then bounded by a constant \(v_h<1-2\alpha\).

For the pre richness subexperiment, take its two smooth donor quantiles and change the treated pre quantile from the mixture at weight \(w\) to the mixture at weight \(w+h/\sqrt{n_\ell}\), remaining in the stipulated local simplex segment.  The inverse formula and the stated smooth density bounds make the density score in this scalar weight bounded.  Taylor's theorem, whose linear term integrates to zero, gives one-observation relative entropy \(O(h^2/n_\ell)\), so the product total variation is again below \(1-2\alpha\) for small fixed \(h\).  Keep all observed post laws fixed and take the two donor post quantiles to have \(L^2\) distance \(d_0>0\).  Stable span changes only the unobserved no-intervention counterfactual and hence changes the causal target by
\[
 \Delta_{n,\ell}=h d_0n_\ell^{-1/2}.
\]

For either pair, let \(C_\ell\) be honest at both laws and let \(\Delta_\ell\) be the displayed target distance.  Changing measure for the second coverage event gives
\[
 P_{0,\ell}\{\operatorname{diam}(C_\ell)\geq\Delta_\ell\}
 \geq1-P_{0,\ell}\{\tau_0\notin C_\ell\}
       -P_{1,\ell}\{\tau_1\notin C_\ell\}
       -\|P_{1,\ell}-P_{0,\ell}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\]
Its lower limit is positive.  This excludes the two respective little-oh diameter rates.  If both subexperiments are present, apply the appropriate pair according as \(n_\ell^{-1/2}\) or \(m_\ell^{-1/4}\) is larger.  Their maximum differs from their sum by at most a factor two, which proves the combined assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:bernoulli-honesty-penalty]\label{thm:bernoulli-honesty-penalty}
In the one-donor equal-Bernoulli post subexperiment with donor knot displacement \(h_B/\sqrt{m_\ell}\), the simplex is the singleton \(\Delta_1=\{1\}\), so \(\widehat w_\ell=w_\ell=1\), \(v_\ell=0\), and the weight quadratic, \(B_{t,\ell}\) cross contribution, and all \(\epsilon_{t,\ell}\) remainder terms are identically zero.  The anchored limit is \(\Psi^B_{h_B}=|Z_0-h_B-Z_1|+|Z_0|+|Z_1|-|Z_0-h_B|-|h_B+Z_1|+h_B\), where \(Z_0,Z_1\) are independent \(N(0,1/4)\).  Its exact distribution function reduces to a one-dimensional Gaussian integral, and the least common \(95\) percent squared-radius cutoff \(c_B^*\) satisfies \(1.913<c_B^*\leq1.914\), while the collision-oracle cutoff is \(1.38590382435\) to the displayed decimal precision.  Any plug-in-centered squared radius whose balls have asymptotic coverage at least \(0.95\) uniformly over the local offsets and whose normalized radius has a deterministic collision limit \(c\) must satisfy \(c\geq c_B^*\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
With \(J=1\), \(\Delta_1=\{1\}\).  Hence \(\widehat w_\ell=w_\ell=1\), \(v_\ell=0\), and every term in the finite expansion containing \(v_\ell\), including the weight quadratic, the \(B_{t,\ell}\) cross term, and all three \(\epsilon_{t,\ell}\) remainder terms, is identically zero.

Put the treated population knot at \(1/2\) and the donor knot at
\(1/2+h_B/\sqrt{m_\ell}\).  The empirical CDF limit gives independent
\(Z_0,Z_1\sim N(0,1/4)\).  The four separately anchored coefficient--endpoint pairs are
\[
 (-1,Z_0),\qquad(1,0),\qquad(1,h_B+Z_1),\qquad(-1,h_B).
\]
Substitution into the endpoint identity and collection of the six unordered pairs gives
\[
 \Psi^B_{h_B}=|Z_0-h_B-Z_1|+|Z_0|+|Z_1|
 -|Z_0-h_B|-|h_B+Z_1|+h_B.
\]

We reduce its distribution to one integral.  Let \(S=Z_1-Z_0\) and
\(R=Z_1+Z_0\).  Then \(S,R\) are independent \(N(0,1/2)\).  Ordering the four endpoints shows that, if \(h+S\geq0\),
\[
 \Psi^B_h=\max\{|S|,\min(|R|,2h+S)\},
\]
whereas, if \(S=-A<-h\),
\[
 \Psi^B_h=A+\min\{2\min(h,A-h),(A-|R|)_+\}.
\]
Define
\[
 P(x)=\Phi(\sqrt2x),\qquad G(x)=2P(x)-1,
 \qquad f(x)=\frac{e^{-x^2}}{\sqrt\pi}.
\]
Conditioning on \(S\) makes the reduction explicit.  For \(0\leq h<q\), the first case contributes
\[
 \{P(q-2h)-P(-h)\}+G(q)\{P(q)-P(q-2h)\}.
\]
In the second case put \(S=-A\).  Necessarily \(h<A\leq q\).  The event is automatic for \(A\leq a(h,q)\), whereas for
\(A>a(h,q)\) it is equivalent to \(|R|\geq2A-q\).  Its contribution is therefore
\[
 P(q)-P(h)-\int_{a(h,q)}^q f(z)G(2z-q)\,dz.
\]
Adding these terms, using \(P(-h)=1-P(h)\), and treating \(h\geq q\) directly yields, for \(q>0\),
\[
 F_h(q):=\Pr(\Psi^B_h\leq q)=G(q)^2,\qquad h\geq q,
\]
and, for \(0\leq h<q\),
\[
 a(h,q)=\max\{q-2h,(q+2h)/3\},
\]
\[
 F_h(q)=2P(q)^2-1+2\{1-P(q)\}P(q-2h)
 -\int_{a(h,q)}^q f(z)G(2z-q)\,dz. \tag{1}
\]
This proves the asserted one-dimensional reduction.  Differentiation under the integral is justified by the bounded smooth integrand.  For \(0\leq h\leq q/4\),
\[
 \partial_hF_h(q)=-4\{1-P(q)\}f(q-2h)
 -2f(q-2h)G(q-4h)\leq0.
\]
For \(q/4\leq h<q\),
\[
 \partial_hF_h(q)=-4\{1-P(q)\}f(q-2h)
 +\frac23f((q+2h)/3)G((4h-q)/3). \tag{2}
\]
Thus the minimum is found on the compact interval \([q/4,q]\).

We give a finite exact-arithmetic certificate for the numerical bracket.  On \(|x|\leq1.914\), use
\[
 \operatorname{erf}(x)=\frac2{\sqrt\pi}
 \sum_{k=0}^{43}\frac{(-1)^kx^{2k+1}}{k!(2k+1)}+\mathcal R_{43}(x),
 \qquad
 |\mathcal R_{43}(x)|\leq
 \frac2{\sqrt\pi}\frac{|x|^{89}}{44!\,89}.
\]
The tail terms decrease from index four onward, so the alternating-series bound proves the remainder inequality.  The rational enclosure
\[
 0.5641895835477562<\pi^{-1/2}<0.5641895835477564
\]
is certified by the identity
\(\pi=16\arctan(1/5)-4\arctan(1/239)\), which follows by repeated application of the rational tangent-addition formula with both sides in \((0,\pi)\).  Thirty terms of each alternating rational power series bracket the two arctangents, and comparison after squaring brackets the reciprocal square root.

Partition every integration interval in (1) into \(200000\) equal rational subintervals.  Since \(G(2z-q)\) is increasing,
\[
 \sum_kG(2z_k-q)\{P(z_{k+1})-P(z_k)\}
 \leq\int_a^qG(2z-q)dP(z)
 \leq\sum_kG(2z_{k+1}-q)\{P(z_{k+1})-P(z_k)\}.
\]
Partition \([q/4,q]\) into \(200000\) equal offset intervals.  Equation (2) and \(f\leq\pi^{-1/2}\) give
\[
 |\partial_hF_h(q)|
 \leq\{4(1-P(q))+2/3\}/\sqrt\pi<0.384
\]
for both rational values of \(q\) below.  Applying this nearest-grid allowance, the alternating-series remainder, and an outward rational allowance of \(10^{-8}\) yields the exact rational enclosures
\[
 F_{0.4929}(1.913)<0.949941881<0.95,
 \qquad
 \inf_{h\geq0}F_h(1.914)>0.950023317>0.95.
\]
Every operation in this certificate is a rational addition, multiplication, comparison, or a signed alternating remainder bound.  Hence, by the definition of the least common cutoff,
\(1.913<c_B^*\leq1.914\).

At collision, \(h=0\) and \(\Psi^B_0=|Z_0-Z_1|\), with
\(Z_0-Z_1\sim N(0,1/2)\).  Its \(0.95\) quantile is
\[
 q_0=\frac{\Phi^{-1}(0.975)}{\sqrt2}
 =1.38590382435
\]
to the displayed precision; substitution of the two adjacent decimal rationals into the same certified error-function series brackets \(0.975\), so this rounding does not rely on floating-point arithmetic.

Finally consider honesty.  Under collision, changing the donor mass to
\(1/2+h/\sqrt{m_\ell}\) gives likelihood ratio \(L_{h,\ell}\) satisfying
\[
 \log L_{h,\ell}
 =4h\sqrt{m_\ell}(\widehat p-1/2)-2h^2+o_{P_0}(1)
 \Rightarrow N(-2h^2,4h^2).
\]
Moreover,
\[
 E_0L_{h,\ell}^2=(1+4h^2/m_\ell)^{m_\ell}
\]
is bounded, and direct interchange of the two Bernoulli masses gives a bounded second moment for the reverse likelihood ratio.  Cauchy--Schwarz therefore proves mutual contiguity.  If
\(\sqrt{m_\ell}R_\ell^2\to c\) in collision probability, contiguity gives the same convergence for each fixed \(h\).  Joint loss convergence and continuity of (1) at every positive \(c\) imply that uniform \(0.95\) coverage requires
\[
 \inf_{h\geq0}F_h(c)\geq0.95.
\]
The definition of \(c_B^*\) forces \(c\geq c_B^*\).  Since
\(c_B^*>1.913>q_0\), collision-oracle deterministic calibration is not uniformly honest.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:oracle-common-cutoff]\label{thm:oracle-common-cutoff}
In the finite experiment \(\{\Pr_{\eta,\theta}:\theta\in\Gamma_\eta\}\) conditional on \(\mathscr S_\eta\), \(c^*(\eta,\alpha)=\sup_{\theta\in\Gamma_\eta}Q_{1-\alpha}^{\Pr_{\eta,\theta}}(\mathcal L_{\eta,\theta})\). It is the least member of \(\mathscr C_\eta\) satisfying \(\inf_{\theta\in\Gamma_\eta}\Pr_{\eta,\theta}\{\mathcal L_{\eta,\theta}\leq c(\eta)\}\geq1-\alpha\); no optimality over randomized or observation-dependent radii is claimed. For every \(\varepsilon>0\), choose \(G\) and \(R\) with certified Gaussian tail probability at most \(\varepsilon/8\). On \(\mathcal B_{G,R}\), replacing \(\Gamma_\eta\) by \(\Gamma_\eta^{G,R}\) preserves the loss, and finite active-set enumeration, interval enclosures for Gaussian rectangle probabilities, grid refinement, and bisection terminate with deterministic brackets for both the worst-case coverage probability and \(c^*(\eta,\alpha)\) of width at most \(\varepsilon\), including structural-zero cases.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(\eta\), put \(p=1-\alpha\), and define the left quantile of a nonnegative random variable by
\(Q_p(Y)=\inf\{c\geq0:\Pr(Y\leq c)\geq p\}\).  Right continuity and monotonicity of a distribution function imply
\[
 \Pr_{\eta,\theta}(\mathcal L_{\eta,\theta}\leq c)\geq p
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 c\geq Q_p^{\Pr_{\eta,\theta}}(\mathcal L_{\eta,\theta}).
\]
Intersecting over \(\theta\in\Gamma_\eta\) and using
\(\mathrm{def:conditional\mbox{-}cutoff}\) gives
\[
 c^*(\eta,\alpha)=\sup_{\theta\in\Gamma_\eta}
 Q_{1-\alpha}^{\Pr_{\eta,\theta}}(\mathcal L_{\eta,\theta}).
\]
This proves leastness in \(\mathscr C_\eta\), whose members are deterministic after conditioning on \(\eta\).  It makes no comparison with randomized or observation-dependent radii.

We next prove the compact reduction.  Since \(0\in D_h\), optimality of \(V_h\) gives
\[
 V_h^\top HV_h-2V_h^\top g\leq0,
 \qquad
 \lambda\|V_h\|_2^2\leq2\|V_h\|_2\|g\|_2.
\]
Thus \(\|V_h\|_2\leq2G/\lambda\) on \(\mathcal B_{G,R}\).  If
\(h_j>2G/\lambda\), the corresponding lower constraint is inactive; replacing it by \(2G/\lambda\) leaves the old minimizer feasible in the smaller set, and hence leaves the unique minimizer unchanged.

Within a shared-noise knot group, random endpoints move by at most \(R\) and known internal offsets have span at most \(K_\eta\).  If successive group translations differ by more than \(2R+2K_\eta\), the two groups are ordered and disjoint throughout the tail box.  Each group has coefficient sum zero.  Therefore the cross-energy sum equals
\[
 -\sum_{q\in C,q'\in C'}b_qb_{q'}(s_{q'}-s_q)=0.
\]
Reducing the gap to the cap preserves ordering, internal offsets, and loss.  Iterating and quotienting a common translation proves that the set of tail-box losses over \(\Gamma_\eta\) equals that over \(\Gamma_\eta^{G,R}\).

There are finitely many endpoint orders and simplex active sets because \(J,T_1,S\) are fixed.  On an active set, eliminate the sum constraint and solve its KKT linear system.  Strong curvature on \(L\) makes every reduced Hessian nonsingular, so \(V_h\) is affine in \((g,h)\) on that cell.  On the bounded image, \(v\mapsto v^\top M_tv\) has a computable Lipschitz constant.  The endpoint bound in \(\mathrm{lem:endpoint\mbox{-}identity}\) gives a computable Lipschitz constant for \(\Psi_t\).  Finite maxima preserve a computable modulus.  These facts remain valid at cell boundaries by continuity.  Rank uncertainty causes no decision problem: enumerate every rank and discard a pattern only when a rational minor enclosure rules it out; retaining an undecided pattern is conservative.  On a fixed rank cell, rational interval \(LDL^\top\) factorization supplies the Gaussian linear image.

Represent the possibly singular Gaussian vector as a known linear image of a standard normal vector of its rank.  Choose rational \(G,R\) so that elementary Gaussian tail bounds certify
\(\Pr(\mathcal B_{G,R}^c)\leq\varepsilon/8\).  Partition the standard-normal box and every compact nuisance cell into rational rectangles.  Interval evaluation of the affine KKT solution and the absolute-value endpoint formula gives lower and upper loss bounds on each rectangle.  A rectangle whose upper loss is at most \(c\) contributes its exact probability to a lower coverage sum; one whose lower loss is at most \(c\) contributes to an upper sum.  The probability of a standard-normal rectangle is a product of univariate Gaussian CDF differences.  Taylor series for the exponential with signed remainders, followed by interval integration, give terminating rational lower and upper bounds for every such difference.

As the mesh tends to zero, the loss enclosure width tends uniformly to zero.  Boundaries between affine/endpoint cells are finite unions of Gaussian-null hyperplanes, except for cells on which the loss is identically constant; those are detected by the KKT kernel and endpoint coefficient-sum tests and evaluated exactly.  For any nonconstant component, the continuous piecewise quadratic--linear loss has positive Gaussian probability in every nonempty relatively open inverse image in its positive range.  Hence its CDF is strictly increasing on that range.  If its \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile is zero because of an atom at zero, every positive \(\delta\) has strictly larger coverage; if the loss is structurally zero, the exact tests return cutoff zero.  Compactness of \(\Gamma_\eta^{G,R}\) converts the pointwise positive slack at \(c^*+\delta\) into uniform slack for each fixed \(\delta>0\).

It follows that successive tail enlargement, mesh refinement, probability-interval refinement, and rational bisection must eventually certify lower and upper cutoff endpoints less than \(\varepsilon\) apart.  The same rectangle sums bracket every worst-case coverage probability used during bisection to width at most \(\varepsilon\).  All enumerations are finite and every inner approximation terminates, including singular covariance ranks and structural-zero cases.  This proves the asserted certified calculation.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem gives the exact decision meaning and a terminating certified calculation for the honest local common-cutoff frontier. \textit{Gap.} Andrews--Mikusheva conditional inference and Cox--Shi active-rank testing inform the construction, but neither computes a deterministic common cutoff for a cone-projection plus signed-endpoint loss. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies a finite, auditable oracle benchmark for feasible calibration of the abundance QTE relative to the donor-identified no-intervention law.

\begin{theorem}[thm:j2s2-certification]\label{thm:j2s2-certification}
The array in \(\mathrm{def:j2s2-instance}\), with common interval \([0,2]\) and \(D=2\), belongs to \(\mathcal M\): its pre laws meet the smooth-density bounds, its same weight spans both pre and post no-intervention quantiles, its observed post laws have at most two atoms, and its pooled Gram satisfies the maintained bound for \(0<\lambda\leq1/375\). For every \(\kappa\in(0,\infty)\), \(G,R>0\), and \(\varepsilon>0\), the compactification and interval algorithm of \(\mathrm{thm:oracle-common-cutoff}\) terminates for the certified family \(\{\mathfrak E_{2,2}(h_f,h_c):0\leq h_f\leq2G/\lambda,\ 0\leq h_c\leq2R\}\). It returns an interval of width at most \(\varepsilon\) containing \(\sup_{0\leq h_f\leq2G/\lambda,\,0\leq h_c\leq2R}Q_{0.95}\{(1+\kappa)^{-1}A_t(V_{(\infty,h_f)})+\kappa(1+\kappa)^{-1}\Psi_t(h_c,Z)\}\), and every coverage probability used in the optimization is enclosed between explicit lower and upper Gaussian-cell sums differing by at most \(\varepsilon\). As \(R\to\infty\) and then \(\kappa\to\infty\), the face term vanishes and the certified family reduces to the one-donor Bernoulli problem, where \(\widehat w_\ell=w_\ell=1\) and every weight and cross term is zero, reproducing \(1.913<c_B^*\leq1.914\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For \(a\in[0,2/5]\), put \(Q_a(u)=u+au(1-u)\).  Then
\[
 Q_a'(u)=1+a(1-2u)\in[3/5,7/5],
 \qquad Q_a''(u)=-2a.
\]
The induced density obeys
\[
 f_a(Q_a(u))=\frac1{Q_a'(u)}\in[5/7,5/3]\subset[1/2,2],
 \qquad
 |f_a'(Q_a(u))|=\frac{2a}{Q_a'(u)^3}
 \leq\frac{100}{27}<4.
\]
Donor one corresponds to \(a=0\), donor two to \(a=2/5\), and the treated pre quantile is their convex combination and hence has an \(a\) in this interval.  Thus all pre cells satisfy the declared smoothness constants.  Exact span holds in the pre period by construction and in the post period by the definition of \(Q^N_{0,2,\ell}\).  The bounds on \(h_f\) make the displayed weights nonnegative and sum to one.  All observed post laws have at most two atoms in \([0,2]\), and the construction supplies the independent cell sampling and the triangular sample sizes required by \(\mathrm{def:model\mbox{-}class}\).

For \(v=(z,-z)\in L\),
\[
 v^\top H_\ell v=z^2\int_0^1
 \left\{\frac25u(1-u)\right\}^2du
 =\frac{2z^2}{375}=\frac1{375}\|v\|_2^2.
\]
Therefore the strong Gram condition holds for every
\(0<\lambda\leq1/375\), completing the proof that the array belongs to \(\mathcal M\).

For fixed \(G,R\), the nuisance rectangle
\([0,2G/\lambda]\times[0,2R]\) is compact.  The intermediate-phase law from
\(\mathrm{thm:three\mbox{-}phase\mbox{-}expansion}\) is exactly
\[
 (1+\kappa)^{-1}A_t(V_{(\infty,h_f)})
 +\kappa(1+\kappa)^{-1}\Psi_t(h_c,Z).
\]
Applying the active-set, endpoint-order, Gaussian-box, interval-grid, and bisection algorithm of \(\mathrm{thm:oracle\mbox{-}common\mbox{-}cutoff}\) proves termination and returns a width-\(\varepsilon\) interval containing the displayed supremum of \(0.95\) quantiles.  Its lower and upper Gaussian-cell sums give the asserted probability enclosures.

The limiting weight is \(w^0=(1,0)\), so every donor-two endpoint coefficient is zero.  Letting \(R\to\infty\) makes \(h_c\) range over \([0,\infty)\).  Next let \(\kappa\to\infty\).  The face coefficient tends to zero, uniformly on each Gaussian tail box by \(\|V_h\|_2\leq2G/\lambda\), while the endpoint coefficient tends to one.  Gaussian tail control passes this convergence to the \(0.95\) quantiles.  The remaining loss is precisely the one-donor Bernoulli loss, so \(\mathrm{thm:bernoulli\mbox{-}honesty\mbox{-}penalty}\) gives
\(1.913<c_B^*\leq1.914\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:dkw-massart-bound]\label{lem:dkw-massart-bound}
If \(X_1,\ldots,X_N\) are independent and identically distributed real random variables with distribution function \(F\) and empirical distribution function \(\widehat F_N\), then for every \(x>0\), \(\Pr\{\|\widehat F_N-F\|_\infty>x\}\leq 2\exp(-2Nx^2)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[lem:empirical-cdf-functional-clt]\label{lem:empirical-cdf-functional-clt}
For every fixed finite collection of independent empirical distribution functions based on independent and identically distributed cell samples, the centered root-sample-size empirical processes converge jointly to independent Brownian bridges composed with their cell distribution functions.  At finitely many atoms, evaluation gives a centered Gaussian vector with covariance \(\min(c_r,c_{r'})-c_rc_{r'}\) within a cell and zero covariance across cells.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[lem:quantile-functional-delta-method]\label{lem:quantile-functional-delta-method}
\texttt{If a map between normed function spaces is Hadamard differentiable at the population element tangentially to the support of a tight weak limit,\allowbreak{} then weak convergence of the centered input process implies weak convergence of the centered image process to the derivative applied to that limit.}
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[lem:skorokhod-representation]\label{lem:skorokhod-representation}
\texttt{If probability laws on a separable metric space converge weakly,\allowbreak{} then random elements with those laws and the limiting law can be constructed on one probability space so that the random elements converge almost surely.}
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[lem:uniform-triangular-pre-score-linearization]\label{lem:uniform-triangular-pre-score-linearization}
Let the finite-array pre-score covariance \(\Omega_\ell\) be the covariance on \(L\) of the influence-function linearization of the centered pooled empirical quantile score at \(w_\ell\).  Under \(\mathrm{ass:fixed\mbox{-}panel}\), \(\mathrm{ass:cell\mbox{-}independence}\), \(\mathrm{ass:smooth\mbox{-}pre}\), and \(\mathrm{ass:strong\mbox{-}gram}\), the empirical score equals this bounded influence sum plus \(o_{\Pr}(1)\), uniformly over \(\mathcal M\), and its bounded-Lipschitz distance from \(N_L(0,\Omega_\ell)\) tends to zero uniformly.  Every subsequence therefore has a further subsequence on which \(w_\ell\to w^0\in\Delta_J\), \(\Omega_\ell\to\Omega\), and the pre-score converges to \(g\sim N_L(0,\Omega)\).  On the same subsequence, the localized empirical criterion converges to \(v^\top Hv-2v^\top g\) on \(D_h\), so its unique minimizer is \(V_h\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write \(f_{jt,\ell}\) for a donor pre-density and \(f^N_{0t,\ell}\) for the treated pre-density.  For a generic pre cell with distribution function \(F\), quantile \(Q\), and density \(f\), define, for an observation \(x\),
\[
 \varphi_{F}(x,u)=
 \frac{u-\mathbf 1\{x\leq Q(u)\}}{f(Q(u))},
 \qquad 0<u<1.
\]
The denominator is at least \(f_{\min}>0\) by \(\mathrm{ass:smooth\mbox{-}pre}\), so \(|\varphi_F(x,u)|\leq f_{\min}^{-1}\).  Let \(\mathbb Z_{jt,\ell}\) and \(\mathbb Z^N_{0t,\ell}\) be the root-\(n_\ell\) sample averages of these centered functions in the corresponding cells.

We first verify the quantile linearization uniformly.  On the DKW event
\(\|\widehat F-F\|_\infty\leq e\), monotonicity and \(f\geq f_{\min}\) give
\[
 \|\widehat Q-Q\|_\infty\leq e/f_{\min}.
\]
For \(e=C\sqrt{\log n_\ell/n_\ell}\), the complement of the simultaneous event over the fixed number of pre cells is \(O(n_\ell^{-2})\) by \(\mathrm{lem:dkw\mbox{-}massart\mbox{-}bound}\).  Taylor's formula and \(|f'|\leq L_f\) give, uniformly in \(u\),
\[
 F(\widehat Q(u))-F(Q(u))
 =f(Q(u))(\widehat Q(u)-Q(u))
 +O\bigl((\widehat Q(u)-Q(u))^2\bigr).
\]
Partition \([0,1]\) into intervals of length at most \(e\).  Bernstein's exponential bound for the binomial count on each interval, followed by a union bound over the \(O(e^{-1})\) intervals, yields
\[
 \sup_{|v-u|\leq e}
 \left|\sqrt{n_\ell}\{(\widehat F-F)(Q(v))
              -(\widehat F-F)(Q(u))\}\right|
 =O_{\Pr}\bigl(n_\ell^{-1/4}(\log n_\ell)^{3/4}\bigr)
\]
uniformly over the density class.  The same bound holds on the two endpoint intervals by the one-sided density condition.  Substitution into the inverse equation for \(\widehat Q\) and the preceding Taylor display therefore gives
\[
 \left\|\sqrt{n_\ell}(\widehat Q-Q)-\mathbb Z\right\|_{L^2}
 =O_{\Pr}\left(n_\ell^{-1/4}(\log n_\ell)^{3/4}
       +n_\ell^{-1/2}\log n_\ell\right)=o_{\Pr}(1)
\]
uniformly.  This is the quantitative inverse-map calculation underlying the delta method in \(\mathrm{lem:quantile\mbox{-}functional\mbox{-}delta\mbox{-}method}\); no post-period smoothness is used.

Let \(P_L\) be Euclidean projection onto \(L\), put
\(q^L_{t,\ell}=P_Lq_{t,\ell}\), and define the influence score
\[
 \widetilde g_\ell=
 \sum_{t=1}^{T_0}\pi_t\int_0^1q^L_{t,\ell}(u)
 \left\{\mathbb Z^N_{0t,\ell}(u)
       -\sum_{j=1}^Jw_{\ell j}\mathbb Z_{jt,\ell}(u)\right\}du.
\]
This vector lies in \(L\).  Subtracting a common outcome origin does not change \(q^L_{t,\ell}\), and every cell support has length at most \(D\); hence
\(\|q^L_{t,\ell}(u)\|_2\leq\sqrt J D\).  Thus \(\widetilde g_\ell\) is a sum of a fixed number of independent root-\(n_\ell\) averages of uniformly bounded, centered vectors.  Define
\[
 \Omega_\ell=\operatorname{Var}(\widetilde g_\ell\mid
  (F_{jt,\ell},F^N_{0t,\ell})_{j,t}).
\]
The preceding quantile expansion, Cauchy--Schwarz, and
\(\|\widehat q_{t,\ell}-q_{t,\ell}\|_2=O_{\Pr}(n_\ell^{-1/2})\) show that the centered empirical score in the localized pooled loss is
\[
 \widehat g_\ell=\widetilde g_\ell+o_{\Pr}(1)
\]
uniformly.

For completeness, Gaussianity is obtained without an invertibility condition on \(\Omega_\ell\).  Bundle the observations having the same within-cell index into independent centered vectors \(X_{k,\ell}\in L\), so that
\(\widetilde g_\ell=n_\ell^{-1/2}\sum_{k=1}^{n_\ell}X_{k,\ell}\) and \(\|X_{k,\ell}\|_2\leq C\), where \(C\) depends only on the fixed class constants.  Replace the summands successively by independent Gaussian vectors with the same covariance.  Third-order Taylor expansion gives, for every thrice continuously differentiable \(f\),
\[
 \left|\mathbb Ef(\widetilde g_\ell)-\mathbb Ef(G_\ell)\right|
 \leq C n_\ell^{-1/2}\|D^3f\|_\infty,
 \qquad G_\ell\sim N_L(0,\Omega_\ell).
\]
Mollifying a bounded one-Lipschitz function at bandwidth
\(n_\ell^{-1/8}\) makes the right side and the mollification error both \(O(n_\ell^{-1/8})\).  Hence the bounded-Lipschitz distance between \(\widetilde g_\ell\) and \(G_\ell\) tends to zero uniformly, including singular covariance matrices.

The simplex is compact, and the covariance matrices \(\Omega_\ell\) are uniformly bounded by the bounded influence representation.  Consequently every subsequence has a further subsequence satisfying
\[
 w_\ell\longrightarrow w^0\in\Delta_J,
 \qquad \Omega_\ell\longrightarrow\Omega,
 \qquad \widehat g_\ell\Rightarrow g\sim N_L(0,\Omega).
\]
Together with \(H_\ell\to H\), convergence of the empirical Gram, and coordinatewise convergence of \(\sqrt{n_\ell}w_{\ell j}\) to \(h_j\), the exact localized criterion is
\[
 n_\ell\{\widehat L_\ell(w_\ell+v/\sqrt{n_\ell})-
              \widehat L_\ell(w_\ell)\}
 =v^\top\widehat H_\ell v-2v^\top\widehat g_\ell,
 \qquad v\in\{v\in L:v_j\geq-\sqrt{n_\ell}w_{\ell j}\}.
\]
The feasible sets converge to \(D_h\).  Since \(H|_L\succeq\lambda I_L\), the limiting objective is strictly convex and coercive.  Subtracting the two variational inequalities for two scores gives
\[
 \lambda\|v-v'\|_2^2
 \leq \|v-v'\|_2\|g-g'\|_2,
\]
when the feasible set and Gram agree; the analogous bound with an additional operator-norm term holds when the Gram varies.  This proves convergence of the unique minimizer to
\(V_h=\operatorname*{argmin}_{v\in D_h}
\{v^\top Hv-2v^\top g\}\), as required.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:pilot-resolution-obstruction]\label{lem:pilot-resolution-obstruction}
For the mandated pilots \(r_{n,\ell}=\lceil n_\ell^{3/4}\rceil\) and \(r_{m,\ell}=\lceil m_\ell^{3/4}\rceil\), full-sample root-scale pilot uncertainty is respectively of order \(n_\ell^{1/8}\) for a regular simplex-face coordinate and \(m_\ell^{1/8}\) for a regular cross-cell knot translation.  There are legal arrays in \(\mathcal M\) with offsets \(n_\ell^{1/16}\) or \(m_\ell^{1/16}\) which diverge in the full local experiment but whose pilot laws have total-variation distance tending to zero from the boundary or collision pilot laws.  Hence no pilot-measurable cluster/face calibration can converge to the stratum-specific conditional cutoff on all such arrays whenever the boundary/collision and separated/inactive cutoffs differ.  By contrast, if a within-cell atom has mass \(d/\sqrt{m_\ell}\) for fixed \(d\geq0\), its pilot count consistently estimates \(d\), and a pilot-unseen atom has simultaneous mass upper bound \(o(m_\ell^{-1/2})\).  Thus the obstruction concerns cross-cell translations and face offsets, not within-cell local mass offsets.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
First use the one-donor Bernoulli post subexperiment.  Let the donor knot be
\[
 p_\ell=\frac12+\frac{h_\ell}{\sqrt{m_\ell}},
 \qquad h_\ell=m_\ell^{1/16},
\]
and compare its pilot law with the collision pilot law.  For \(|x|\leq1/4\), Taylor's theorem gives
\(\operatorname{kl}(1/2+x,1/2)\leq Cx^2\).  With
\(r_{m,\ell}=\lceil m_\ell^{3/4}\rceil\), the pilot relative entropy is at most
\[
 C\frac{r_{m,\ell}h_\ell^2}{m_\ell}
 =O(m_\ell^{-1/8})\longrightarrow0.
\]
For completeness, applying the log-sum inequality to a total-variation maximizing partition and Taylor expanding the resulting binary relative entropy gives
\(\operatorname{KL}(P,Q)\geq2\|P-Q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}^2\).  Hence these pilot laws have total-variation distance tending to zero, although \(h_\ell\to\infty\) and the knots occupy distinct macro clusters in the full root-\(m_\ell\) experiment.  This also displays the root-full-sample pilot standard error
\(\sqrt{m_\ell/r_{m,\ell}}\asymp m_\ell^{1/8}\).

The two limiting-stratum cutoffs differ.  Collision requires the common cutoff \(c_B^*>1.913\).  At infinite separation, the endpoint formula reduces to \(|Z_0|+|Z_1|\).  If
\[
 U=(2Z_0+2Z_1)/\sqrt2,\qquad V=(2Z_0-2Z_1)/\sqrt2,
\]
then \(U,V\) are independent standard normals and
\[
 |Z_0|+|Z_1|=2^{-1/2}\max\{|U|,|V|\}.
\]
Its \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile is
\[
 c_{\mathrm{sep}}(\alpha)=2^{-1/2}
 \Phi^{-1}\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{1-\alpha}}2\right).
\]
At \(\alpha=0.05\), the elementary Mills bound
\(1-\Phi(x)<e^{-x^2/2}/(x\sqrt{2\pi})\) at
\(x=1.913\sqrt2>2.7\) shows
\(c_{\mathrm{sep}}(0.05)<1.913<c_B^*\).  A pilot-measurable cutoff converging to the collision value must, by total-variation convergence, have the same limit under \(h_\ell=m_\ell^{1/16}\), and therefore cannot converge to the separated cutoff.

The face witness is analogous and lies in \(\mathrm{def:j2s2\mbox{-}instance}\).  Put \(h_\ell=n_\ell^{1/16}\) and compare it with \(h_\ell=0\).  Only the treated pre law changes, with quantile
\[
 Q_{0,1,\ell}(u)=u+\delta_\ell\frac25u(1-u),
 \qquad \delta_\ell=h_\ell/\sqrt{n_\ell}.
\]
Change of variables gives one-observation relative entropy
\[
 -\int_0^1\log\left(1+\frac25\delta_\ell(1-2u)\right)du
 \leq C\delta_\ell^2,
\]
because the linear term integrates to zero and the derivative is uniformly positive.  Thus the pilot relative entropy is
\[
 O(r_{n,\ell}\delta_\ell^2)=O(n_\ell^{-1/8})\longrightarrow0.
\]
Nevertheless the full face offset diverges.  In a weight-dominated scalar two-donor subexperiment with distinct post donor quantiles, the boundary projection loss is a positive constant times \(\max\{W,0\}^2\), whereas the inactive-face loss is the same constant times \(W^2\), for a nondegenerate centered normal \(W\).  Their \((1-\alpha)\)-quantiles are proportional respectively to
\([\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha)]^2\) and
\([\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha/2)]^2\), which differ for \(0<\alpha<1/2\).  Total-variation indistinguishability proves the face obstruction and shows the root-full-sample pilot uncertainty \(n_\ell^{1/8}\).

Finally, let an internal atom have mass \(p_\ell=d/\sqrt{m_\ell}\), and let \(N_\ell\) be its pilot count.  Then
\[
 \widehat d_\ell=\frac{\sqrt{m_\ell}N_\ell}{r_{m,\ell}},
 \qquad
 E\widehat d_\ell=d,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(\widehat d_\ell)
 =\frac{m_\ell p_\ell(1-p_\ell)}{r_{m,\ell}}
 =O(m_\ell^{-1/4})\to0.
\]
Thus \(\widehat d_\ell\to d\) in probability.  If an atom of mass \(p\) is unseen, then
\(\Pr_p(N=0)=(1-p)^{r_{m,\ell}}\leq e^{-r_{m,\ell}p}\).  With at most
\(K_+=\max\{1,(J+1)T_1(S-1)\}\) candidates and
\(\beta_\ell=(n_\ell\wedge m_\ell)^{-2}\), a union bound shows that, outside probability \(\beta_\ell/8\), every unseen atom has
\[
 p\leq\frac{\log(8K_+/\beta_\ell)}{r_{m,\ell}},
 \qquad
 \sqrt{m_\ell}p=O(m_\ell^{-1/4}\log m_\ell)=o(1).
\]
Here \(\log(n_\ell\wedge m_\ell)\leq\log m_\ell\).  This proves that within-cell local mass offsets are learned even though face and cross-cell translations are not.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The total-variation witnesses certify why the stronger all-strata pilot claim is false rather than merely unproved. \textit{Gap.} The obstruction is specific to sublinear pilot geometry selection and leaves open full-sample or cross-fitted constructions that preserve calibration independence by another argument. \textit{Consumer.} It prevents an empirical implementation from treating slow separation as either a reliably detected oracle stratum or evidence that the common honesty penalty disappears.

\begin{theorem}[thm:pilot-resolvable-certified-calibration]\label{thm:pilot-resolvable-certified-calibration}
The radius in \(\mathrm{def:pilot\mbox{-}resolvable\mbox{-}certified\mbox{-}radius}\) is a total Borel function and satisfies
\[
 \liminf_{\ell\to\infty}\inf_{P\in\mathcal M}
 \Pr_P\{(\tau_{t,\ell})_{t>T_0}\in\mathcal C_\ell(R_\ell)\}
 \geq1-\alpha,
 \qquad
 R_\ell=O_{\Pr}(n_\ell^{-1/2}+m_\ell^{-1/4})
\]
uniformly over \(\mathcal M\).  Consider an extracted local subsequence.  Suppose every cross-cell root-\(m_\ell\) knot gap either remains bounded or, when it diverges, satisfies
\[
 \frac{\sqrt{m_\ell}
 |c_{jtr,\ell}-c_{j'tr',\ell}|}
 {m_\ell^{1/8}\sqrt{\log(n_\ell\wedge m_\ell)}}\longrightarrow\infty,
\]
and every face coordinate either has bounded \(\sqrt{n_\ell}w_{\ell j}\) or satisfies
\[
 \frac{\sqrt{n_\ell}w_{\ell j}}
 {n_\ell^{1/8}\sqrt{\log(n_\ell\wedge m_\ell)}}
 \longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Then the DKW branch has probability \(o(1)\), all finite within-cell offsets are learned, all bounded cross-cell translations and face offsets remain in the nuisance supremum, and
\[
 R_\ell^2/a_\ell\longrightarrow c^*(\eta,\alpha)
\]
in conditional probability given \(\mathscr S_\eta\).  A fixed positive unscaled separation of distinct knot groups and weights bounded away from every simplex face satisfy the displayed resolvability conditions.  Only on such a separated interior stratum, and only when the resulting critical law is continuous and nondegenerate, does the certified cutoff reduce to the corresponding plug-in oracle cutoff.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Set
\[
 r_{n,\ell}=\lceil n_\ell^{3/4}\rceil,
 \quad r_{m,\ell}=\lceil m_\ell^{3/4}\rceil,
 \quad N_{n,\ell}=n_\ell-r_{n,\ell},
 \quad N_{m,\ell}=m_\ell-r_{m,\ell}.
\]
For all sufficiently large \(\ell\), both main sizes are positive and asymptotic to the full sizes.  The deterministic split and
\(\mathrm{ass:cell\mbox{-}independence}\) make the pilot and main blocks independent.

We first construct simultaneous primitive regions.  Put
\(K=(J+1)T_1(S-1)\), \(K_+=\max(1,K)\), and
\(\beta_\ell=(n_\ell\wedge m_\ell)^{-2}\).  The number of primitive cells and knots is fixed.  Allocate \(\beta_\ell\) over them by fixed rational fractions.  The DKW inequality gives simultaneous pilot CDF bands.  If a pre density is evaluated by a centered difference quotient of rational bandwidth \(b\), with the one-sided version at a support endpoint, then
\[
 \left|\frac{F(x+b)-F(x-b)}{2b}-f(x)\right|
 \leq L_fb/2,
\]
and replacing \(F\) by its band adds at most the CDF width divided by \(b\).  Choosing, for example, the nearest smaller positive dyadic to
\(r_{n,\ell}^{-1/4}\) makes both errors vanish uniformly.  Continuous interval propagation through inversion and finite integration gives shrinking regions for quantiles, \(H_\ell\), the stable weight, and the influence covariance \(\Omega_\ell\).  The finite-sample curvature calculation in
\(\mathrm{thm:honest\mbox{-}rate\mbox{-}ball}\) supplies the weight region.

For post masses use exact binomial-tail inversion, with outward dyadic endpoints.  Its coverage is finite-sample exact.  Its useful width follows directly from the Bernoulli moment-generating function: for a pilot count \(N\), size \(r\), mass \(p\), and \(x>0\), optimizing the exponential Markov bound gives
\[
 \Pr_p\left\{|N/r-p|>
 2\sqrt{(N/r)x/r}+3x/r\right\}\leq2e^{-x}.
\]
Taking \(x=\log(8K_+/\beta_\ell)\) and a union bound gives simultaneous variance-adaptive regions.  The unseen-atom calculation in
\(\mathrm{lem:pilot\mbox{-}resolution\mbox{-}obstruction}\) shows that every pilot-unseen atom has mass \(o(m_\ell^{-1/2})\), except on the allocated event.  Its root-\(m_\ell\) contribution to squared \(L^2\) loss is at most \(D^2\sqrt{m_\ell}p=o(1)\), so the construction's explicit cushion covers it.  For a within-cell mass \(d/\sqrt{m_\ell}\), the same lemma proves
\(\sqrt{m_\ell}N/r_{m,\ell}\to d\).  Thus internal local offsets are learned, whereas no cross-cell root-scale translation or face offset has been plugged in.

We next justify the uniform raw-to-Gaussian transfer used by the certification.  Conditional on the pilot, every main post cumulative-count vector is a sum of independent centered bounded vectors in dimension at most \(K\).  The pre score equals, by
\(\mathrm{lem:uniform\mbox{-}triangular\mbox{-}pre\mbox{-}score\mbox{-}linearization}\), a bounded influence sum plus a uniform remainder.  An explicit remainder envelope follows from the same assumptions: on a DKW event of width \(\delta_N\), inverse Taylor expansion gives
\[
 \widehat Q(u)-Q(u)
 =-\frac{\widehat F(Q(u))-u}{f(Q(u))}+R_N(u).
\]
The density Taylor remainder is at most \(C\delta_N^2\), and a rational grid with mesh \(\delta_N\), followed by the exponential Markov bound for the Bernoulli moment-generating function on all grid intervals, gives
\[
 \sqrt N\|R_N\|_2
 \leq C N^{-1/4}(\log N)^{3/4}
\]
outside a computable probability tending to zero, uniformly over the stated density class.  Finite products and sums preserve this envelope for the pooled score.

For a normalized bounded sum \(X=N^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^NX_i\) and a Gaussian \(G\) with the same covariance, replace the summands one at a time by matching Gaussian increments.  Third-order Taylor expansion cancels the first two moments and gives
\[
 |Ef(X)-Ef(G)|\leq C_0N^{-1/2}\|D^3f\|_\infty.
\]
For a closed set \(A\), convolving the indicator of its \(s/2\)-enlargement with a smooth compactly supported kernel produces a function equal to one on \(A\), zero off \(A^s\), and with third derivative at most \(C_1s^{-3}\).  Therefore
\[
 \Pr(X\in A^s)\geq\Pr(G\in A)-C_2N^{-1/2}s^{-3}.
\]
Take \(s=(N_{n,\ell}\wedge N_{m,\ell})^{-1/16}\), apply the bound to the independent pre and post blocks, and add the explicit pre remainder probability.  This argument never divides by a covariance eigenvalue and hence is uniform across changing ranks.

On \(\mathcal B_{G,R}\), the projection bound gives \(\|V_h\|_2\leq2G/\lambda\).  Subtracting the two variational inequalities for strongly convex quadratic programs, or equivalently using the finite active-set KKT systems, gives a computable projection modulus depending only on \(G,\lambda,J,D\).  The endpoint modulus is
\[
 |\Psi(s)-\Psi(s')|
 \leq\left(\sum_q|b_q|\right)^2\max_q|s_q-s'_q|.
\]
Thus coordinate enlargement changes the simultaneous normalized loss by a computable \(o(1)\).  The exact expansion and its pulse bounds give another computable \(o(1)\) cushion.  In particular, the largest normalized cross coefficient satisfies
\[
 \frac{\sqrt{N_{n,\ell}}}{N_{n,\ell}+\sqrt{N_{m,\ell}}}
 \leq N_{n,\ell}^{-1/2},
\]
and DKW plus the Gaussian tail box bounds the remaining factors by fixed powers of logarithms.  The other remainder terms are smaller by the rates already proved.

Choose a deterministic dyadic \(\gamma_\ell\downarrow0\), no faster than
\((N_{n,\ell}\wedge N_{m,\ell})^{-1/32}\), and large enough to dominate the explicit linearization, replacement, and smoothing envelopes just displayed.  Spend one \(\gamma_\ell\) on each of pre linearization, Gaussian replacement, Gaussian tails, main-to-full center translation, and probability-interval width, and three further \(\gamma_\ell\)'s on loss and nuisance enclosures.  Add \(\beta_\ell\) for primitive-region exclusion.  The certified Gaussian calculation is performed at coverage
\(1-\alpha+8\gamma_\ell+\beta_\ell\), which is below one eventually.  On the primitive inclusion event, the true nuisance belongs to an enumerated region.  The certified lower Gaussian coverage and the preceding transfer therefore imply
\[
 \inf_{P\in\mathcal M}
 \Pr_P\left\{
 \max_{t>T_0}\|\widetilde\tau_{t,\ell}-\tau_{t,\ell}\|_2^2
 \leq a^M_\ell(\overline c_\ell+\delta_\ell)
 \mathrel{\big|}\text{pilot}\right\}
 \geq1-\alpha+\gamma_\ell,
\]
where \(a^M_\ell=N_{n,\ell}^{-1}+N_{m,\ell}^{-1/2}\),
\(\delta_\ell\downarrow0\) is the sum of certified loss cushions, and
\(\overline c_\ell\) is the first rational upper cutoff with adequate certified lower coverage.  Removing conditioning and the pilot inclusion event loses at most \(\beta_\ell\).

The full center is handled rather than silently substituted.  A full empirical CDF differs from its main counterpart by
\[
 O_{\Pr}\left(\frac{\sqrt{r_{n,\ell}\log n_\ell}}{n_\ell}\right)
 =O_{\Pr}(n_\ell^{-5/8}\sqrt{\log n_\ell})
\]
in pre cells and by the analogous post expression.  Inverse-density and strong-Gram bounds transfer the pre expression to weights.  The transport inequality transfers the post expression to quantiles after taking a square root.  Hence, simultaneously over post periods,
\[
 \max_t\|\widehat\tau_{t,\ell}-\widetilde\tau_{t,\ell}\|_2
 =O_{\Pr}\left(n_\ell^{-5/8}\sqrt{\log n_\ell}
 +m_\ell^{-5/16}(\log m_\ell)^{1/4}\right)
 =o_{\Pr}(\sqrt{a_\ell}).
\]
The construction adds the corresponding simultaneous certified upper bound to
\(\sqrt{a^M_\ell(\overline c_\ell+\delta_\ell)}\).  Since
\(a^M_\ell/a_\ell\to1\), the triangle inequality and the previous conditional display prove
\[
 \liminf_{\ell\to\infty}\inf_{P\in\mathcal M}
 \Pr_P\{(\tau_{t,\ell})_{t>T_0}\in\mathcal C_\ell(R_\ell)\}
 \geq1-\alpha.
\]
The claim follows for the original sequence by the subsequence principle: a failure would supply a subsequence on which the displayed uniform lower bound fails, contradicting the construction above.
Because both the estimated and target QTE curves are differences of quantiles in a common interval of length \(D\), their pointwise difference is at most \(2D\).  Capping the constructed radius at \(2D\) therefore preserves this coverage and places \(R_\ell\) in its declared range.

The Gaussian envelope
\[
 \mathcal L_{\eta,\theta}
 \leq C_A\|g\|_2^2+C_\Psi\max_{j,t,r}|Z_{jtr}|
\]
has constants depending only on the fixed dimensions, \(D\), and \(\lambda\), and is uniform in all offsets because separated zero-sum clusters decouple.  It supplies a common finite cutoff, so \(\overline c_\ell=O_{\Pr}(1)\).  The successful radius is therefore
\(O_{\Pr}(\sqrt{a_\ell})=O_{\Pr}(n_\ell^{-1/2}+m_\ell^{-1/4})\); the fallback has the same order by
\(\mathrm{thm:honest\mbox{-}rate\mbox{-}ball}\).

We verify totality, finite work, and measurability.  There are at most
\(K_+^{K_+}\) cluster partitions, \(K_+!\) endpoint orders, \(2^J\) simplex faces, and \(2^d\) covariance-rank patterns, where
\(d\leq J-1+K\).  The nuisance dimension is at most \(p=J-1+K\).  Let \(B_\ell\) be the deterministic coordinate bound obtained from the outcome range, \(2G/\lambda\), the translation caps, and the fixed dimension, and let \(L_\ell\) be the maximum of the finitely many rational KKT and endpoint moduli.  A mesh at most \(\gamma_\ell/L_\ell\) and cutoff bisection of width \(\gamma_\ell\) need no more than
\[
 W_\ell=
 2^{J+d}K_+^{K_+}K_+!
 \left(1+\frac{2B_\ell L_\ell}{\gamma_\ell}\right)^{d+p}
 \left(2+\left\lceil\log_2\frac{C_{\rm env}}{\gamma_\ell}\right\rceil\right)
\]
rectangle evaluations.  Prescribe the work limit as the ceiling of this deterministic expression.  Rational Taylor bounds for the exponential and Gaussian integral terminate on every bounded rectangle.  Sorting with lexicographic tie-breaking, finite enumeration, outward dyadic rounding, rational interval operations, and choosing the first successful bracket are Borel operations.  Before the error budget is admissible or if any finite check fails, the definition returns the already Borel DKW radius.  Hence \(R_\ell\) is total and Borel; once the asymptotic budget is active, the prescribed work bound cannot be exceeded.

It remains to prove sharp convergence on precisely the stated resolvable strata.  The root-full-sample widths of the pilot face and cross-cell regions are respectively
\[
 O_{\Pr}\bigl(n_\ell^{1/8}\sqrt{\log(n_\ell\wedge m_\ell)}\bigr),
 \qquad
 O_{\Pr}\bigl(m_\ell^{1/8}\sqrt{\log(n_\ell\wedge m_\ell)}\bigr).
\]
Thus a bounded coordinate remains in the nuisance supremum, while a coordinate satisfying the theorem's diverging ratio is eventually classified as an inactive face or a distinct macro cluster.  The variance-adaptive regions learn every finite within-cell offset.  The pre pilot regions shrink to \(w^0,H,\Omega\), including changing ranks.  On every fixed tail box, the enumerated quotient nuisance region therefore converges to \(\{\eta\}\times\Gamma_\eta\).

Uniform continuity of endpoint energy and cone projection on each compact enumerated cell, together with
\(\mathrm{thm:oracle\mbox{-}common\mbox{-}cutoff}\), makes the certified upper cutoff converge to \(c^*(\eta,\alpha)\).  To cover a possible atom at zero, note that either the loss is structurally zero, when both cutoffs are exactly zero, or every positive enlargement of a zero cutoff has strictly larger Gaussian coverage; for a positive cutoff the CDF is strictly increasing in a neighborhood of that cutoff.  Compactness makes this slack uniform over the tail-box nuisance region.  Letting the tail budget and grid width vanish proves
\(\overline c_\ell\to c^*(\eta,\alpha)\) conditionally on \(\mathscr S_\eta\).  Since the loss cushion vanishes, \(a^M_\ell/a_\ell\to1\), and the main-to-full distance is \(o_{\Pr}(\sqrt{a_\ell})\),
\[
 R_\ell^2/a_\ell\longrightarrow c^*(\eta,\alpha)
\]
in conditional probability.  The DKW branch has probability \(o(1)\) because every prescribed certification step is then inside its eventual finite budget.

A fixed positive unscaled knot separation dominates the post pilot width, and weights bounded away from every face dominate the pre pilot width.  Only in this separated interior case do the unresolved nuisance coordinates disappear.  If, in addition, the resulting critical law is continuous and nondegenerate, ordinary quantile continuity reduces the certified common cutoff to the plug-in oracle cutoff.  At collisions, faces, or slowly diverging diagonals, the nuisance supremum remains necessary; the obstruction lemma and the Bernoulli honesty penalty rule out the stronger oracle-adaptation claim.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the strongest positive feasible result supported by the fixed pilot architecture: global honesty and optimal diameter hold over \(\mathcal M\), while sharp conditional convergence is restricted by an explicit, checkable pilot-resolution condition. \textit{Gap.} Existing DSC and conditional-inference results do not combine the raw quantile score, finite-support endpoint energy, changing simplex faces, rational probability certification, and a rate-preserving fallback in this regime. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies an implementable calibration rule for separated interiors and bounded collision or face neighborhoods, including the \(J=2\), \(S=3\) stress test.

\begin{proposition}[prop:j2s3-pilot-stress-test]\label{prop:j2s3-pilot-stress-test}
Fix bounded \(h_f,h_c\geq0\) and \(d\geq0\).  Form a \(J=2\), \(S=3\) array by retaining the pre laws, weights, donor post laws, and no-intervention span of \(\mathrm{def:j2s2\mbox{-}instance}\), while replacing the treated observed post law by masses \(1/2\), \(d/\sqrt{m_\ell}\), and \(1/2-d/\sqrt{m_\ell}\) at \(0\), \(1/2\), and \(1\), respectively, for all sufficiently large \(\ell\).  This array belongs to \(\mathcal M\).  If \(N_\ell\) is the pilot count at the middle atom, then \(\sqrt{m_\ell}N_\ell/\lceil m_\ell^{3/4}\rceil\to d\) in probability.  The certified procedure retains the bounded face offset \(h_f\) and bounded cross-cell translation \(h_c\) in its nuisance supremum, its DKW fallback probability tends to zero, and its normalized squared radius converges conditionally to \(c^*(\eta,\alpha)\).  This is a bounded-offset stress test and does not imply convergence on the slowly diverging diagonal arrays of \(\mathrm{lem:pilot\mbox{-}resolution\mbox{-}obstruction}\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The pre-period laws and weights are those of \(\mathrm{def:j2s2\mbox{-}instance}\), so the smooth-density bounds, exact pre span, and the Gram lower bound \(0<\lambda\leq1/375\) remain valid.  The donor post laws are unchanged.  The modified treated law has three ordered atoms in \([0,2]\), and its masses are nonnegative for all sufficiently large \(\ell\) because \(d\) is fixed and \(m_\ell\to\infty\).  Defining the unobserved no-intervention law by the same donor quantile span preserves \(\mathrm{ass:stable\mbox{-}quantile\mbox{-}span}\).  Cell sampling remains independent.  Hence every defining condition of \(\mathcal M\) holds with \(S=3\).

The pilot middle-atom count is binomial with size
\(r_{m,\ell}=\lceil m_\ell^{3/4}\rceil\) and success probability \(d/\sqrt{m_\ell}\).  Therefore
\[
 \mathbb E\left(\frac{\sqrt{m_\ell}N_\ell}{r_{m,\ell}}\right)=d,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}\left(\frac{\sqrt{m_\ell}N_\ell}{r_{m,\ell}}\right)
 =\frac{d\sqrt{m_\ell}}{r_{m,\ell}}
   \left(1-\frac d{\sqrt{m_\ell}}\right)\\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus Chebyshev's inequality proves learning of the internal offset.  Because \(h_f\) and \(h_c\) are bounded, they satisfy the bounded alternatives in
\(\mathrm{thm:pilot\mbox{-}resolvable\mbox{-}certified\mbox{-}calibration}\): the procedure does not estimate them, but retains their full compatible sets in \(\Gamma_\eta\).  The theorem then gives vanishing fallback and conditional convergence to \(c^*(\eta,\alpha)\).  The obstruction lemma applies to offsets diverging more slowly than the pilot root-scale error, so this bounded experiment supplies no evidence for the excluded all-strata assertion.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The strengthened early-kill instance verifies learnability of a root-\(m_\ell\) within-cell mass while bounded face and cross-cell offsets remain nuisance. \textit{Gap.} It is deliberately a bounded-offset stress test and does not address the slowly diverging diagonal obstruction. \textit{Consumer.} It gives a concrete two-donor, three-atom certification test before any mosquito-abundance implementation.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The local criterion minimizes a common deterministic conditional squared-radius cutoff over \(\mathscr C_\eta\); it does not optimize expected radius, randomized rules, or arbitrary observation-dependent radii.  The finite Gaussian object is a conditional loss experiment, not unrestricted deficiency equivalence for the raw samples.  The total pilot-split radius is globally uniformly honest and rate-optimal, but convergence to \(c^*(\eta,\alpha)\) is proved only under the stated pilot-resolvability dichotomy.  Slowly diverging cross-cell translations or face offsets can be grouped conservatively for coverage yet remain pilot-indistinguishable from collisions or boundaries, so all-strata sharp attainment remains open.  Oracle calibration requires fixed unscaled knot separation, weights bounded away from every face, and a continuous nondegenerate critical law.  Applying the result to Zhai et al. additionally requires a bridge for discrete pre outcomes, beta-weighted fitting, aggregation, dependence, and interpolation.

\section{Honest scope}

The exact endpoint identity, exact six-term expansion, pulse-sharp cross-vector limit, local compactification, triangular pre-score linearization with \(w^0\) and \(\Omega\), three phase laws, degeneracy criteria, honest optimal diameter order, Bernoulli honesty penalty, deterministic common cutoff, and pilot-resolvable certified calibration theorem are proved.  The stable-span assumption identifies \(Q^N_{0t,\ell}\); it never equates the counterfactual with \(Q^I_{0t,\ell}\).  The certified radius is total, Borel, globally asymptotically honest, and rate-optimal.  It learns within-cell local masses, retains bounded cross-cell translations and face offsets as nuisance, and attains the common cutoff when every root-scale coordinate is bounded or exceeds the quantitative pilot-resolution threshold.  A \(J=2\), \(S=3\) bounded-offset stress test verifies these claims but is not evidence for all-strata convergence.  The slowly diverging diagonal obstruction leaves full feasible sharp calibration open.  Oracle reduction is restricted to fixed separated interior strata with continuous nondegenerate critical law, and no adaptation to the smaller collision-oracle cutoff is claimed.
