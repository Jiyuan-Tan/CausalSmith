Assume \(t>d/2\).  Fix
\[
 \gamma=\frac12\left(1+\frac{d}{2t}\right),
 \qquad
 q=1+\left\lfloor\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\right\rfloor,
 \qquad
 p=1+\left\lfloor\max\{\beta_e,\beta_m\}\right\rfloor .
\]
Thus \(d/(2t)<\gamma<1\) and \(q(1-\gamma)>1\).  For \(m\geq0\), partition \(\mathbb T^d\) into \(2^{md}\) congruent dyadic cells.  On each cell take the tensor Legendre polynomials of coordinate degree at most \(p\), rescaled to be orthonormal in \(L^2(dx)\) and extended by zero off that cell.  List cells lexicographically and, within a cell, list multi-indices lexicographically; this gives a deterministic local basis \(z_{M_m}(x)\in\mathbb R^{M_m}\), where \(M_m=(p+1)^d2^{md}\).  Every basis vector is cell-supported, and the span contains the global constant because the appropriately weighted sum of the cellwise constant modes equals one.  Let \(m_n\) minimize \(|\log M_m-\gamma\log n|\), choosing the smaller \(m\) in a tie, and put \(M=M_{m_n}\) and \(M_{\mathrm{proj}}=M\).  Then \(M\asymp n^\gamma=o(n)\).

Put \(I_n=\{1,\ldots,\lfloor n/2\rfloor\}\) and \(G_n=\{\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1,\ldots,n\}\).  The following defines a directed estimator for an ordered pair \((I,G)\in\{(I_n,G_n),(G_n,I_n)\}\).  For \(a\in\{0,1\}\), set
\[
 D_{a,i}=\mathbf 1\{A_i=a\},
 \qquad z_i=z_M(X_i),
 \qquad
 \widehat\Omega_{a,G}=|G|^{-1}\sum_{i\in G}D_{a,i}z_i z_i^\top,
\]
and define
\[
 \mathcal E_{a,G}=
 \left\{
  \lambda_{\min}(\widehat\Omega_{a,G})\geq\frac{\eta c_X}{2},
  \quad
  \lambda_{\max}(\widehat\Omega_{a,G})\leq2C_X
 \right\}.
\]
On \(\mathcal E_{a,G}\), put
\[
 B_{a,G}=\widehat\Omega_{a,G}^{-1},
 \qquad
 G_{a,i}=D_{a,i}z_i z_i^\top-\widehat\Omega_{a,G},
 \qquad
 R^p_{a,i}=D_{a,i}-1,
 \qquad
 R^\nu_{a,i}=D_{a,i}L_{Y_i},
\]
and, for \(\ell\geq0\), put
\[
 A_{a,\ell}(i_1,\ldots,i_\ell)
 =B_{a,G}\prod_{u=1}^{\ell}(G_{a,i_u}B_{a,G}),
 \qquad A_{a,0}=B_{a,G},
\]
where products are ordered from left to right in increasing \(u\).  If a kernel \(h(i_0,\ldots,i_{j-1})\) is displayed below, define its ordered without-replacement average by
\[
 \mathbb U_I[h]
 =\frac{1}{(|I|)_j}
   \sum_{(i_0,\ldots,i_{j-1})\in I^j_{\ne}}h(i_0,\ldots,i_{j-1}),
 \qquad (|I|)_j=|I|(|I|-1)\cdots(|I|-j+1).
\]
For \(0\leq\ell<q\), define
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 C^{(1)}_{a,\ell}
 &=\mathbb U_I\!\left[
  (K_{X_{i_0}}\otimes R^\nu_{a,i_0})
  \{z_{i_0}^\top A_{a,\ell}(i_1,\ldots,i_\ell)z_{i_{\ell+1}}\}
  R^p_{a,i_{\ell+1}}
 \right],\\
 C^{(2)}_{a,\ell}
 &=\mathbb U_I\!\left[
  (K_{X_{i_0}}\otimes R^\nu_{a,i_{\ell+1}})
  R^p_{a,i_0}
  \{z_{i_0}^\top A_{a,\ell}(i_1,\ldots,i_\ell)z_{i_{\ell+1}}\}
 \right].
 \end{aligned}
\]
For \(0\leq\ell,r<q\), let \(j=\ell+r+2\) and define the common-anchor term
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 C^{(3)}_{a,\ell,r}=\mathbb U_I\!\Big[&
 D_{a,i_0}(K_{X_{i_0}}\otimes R^\nu_{a,i_j})
 \{z_{i_0}^\top A_{a,\ell}(i_1,\ldots,i_\ell)z_{i_{\ell+1}}\}
 R^p_{a,i_{\ell+1}}\\
 &\mathrel{}\times
 \{z_{i_0}^\top A_{a,r}(i_{\ell+2},\ldots,i_{\ell+r+1})z_{i_j}\}
 \Big].
 \end{aligned}
\]
Set
\[
 \mathcal C_{a,q}^{I\mid G}
 =\sum_{\ell=0}^{q-1}(-1)^\ell
   \{C^{(1)}_{a,\ell}+C^{(2)}_{a,\ell}\}
  -\sum_{\ell,r=0}^{q-1}(-1)^{\ell+r}C^{(3)}_{a,\ell,r},
\]
\[
 \widehat\theta_a^{I\mid G}
 =|I|^{-1}\sum_{i\in I}D_{a,i}K_{X_i}\otimes L_{Y_i}
  -\mathcal C_{a,q}^{I\mid G}.
\]
If \(\mathcal E_{0,G}\cap\mathcal E_{1,G}\) holds, set
\[
 \widehat\Psi^{I\mid G}
 =\widehat\theta_1^{I\mid G}-\widehat\theta_0^{I\mid G};
\]
otherwise set \(\widehat\Psi^{I\mid G}=0\).  Let \(N_0=2(2q+3)\).  For \(n\geq N_0\), define
\[
 \widehat\Psi_n^{\mathrm{ho}}
 =\frac12\{\widehat\Psi^{I_n\mid G_n}
                 +\widehat\Psi^{G_n\mid I_n}\};
\]
for \(n<N_0\), set \(\widehat\Psi_n^{\mathrm{ho}}=0\).  Finally define the deterministic class-oracle envelope
\[
 V_\star
 =1+\sup_{m\geq1}\sup_{P\in\mathcal M}
 mE_P\|\widehat\Psi_m^{\mathrm{ho}}-\Psi(P)\|_{\mathcal H}^2
\]
and the test
\[
 \varphi_n^{\mathrm{ho}}
 =\mathbf 1\left\{
 \|\widehat\Psi_n^{\mathrm{ho}}\|_{\mathcal H}
 >\sqrt{\frac{V_\star}{\alpha n}}
 \right\},
\]
with rejection on the displayed strict inequality and nonrejection on equality.  The theorem proves \(V_\star<\infty\).  This envelope is an oracle calibration constant determined by the model class; it is not asserted to be computable from the observed sample.

%%% FIELD thm-high-smoothness-statement
If \(t>d/2\), then over every law in \(\mathcal M\) and every design density satisfying \(c_X\le f_X\le C_X\), a finite-order cross-fitted empirical higher-order SCoDiTE test has uniform type-I error at most \(\alpha+o(1)\) and uniform type-II error at most \(\beta+o(1)\) whenever \(\|\Psi(P)\|_{\mathcal H}\ge Cn^{-1/2}\). Thus \(\delta_n^\star\le Cn^{-1/2}\); this upper bound uses only the declared model assumptions, its correction has \(M=o(n)\), and it imposes no smoothness on \(f_X\). For the reverse inequality only, impose the class-richness premise that \(\mathcal M\) contains a path \((P_\theta)_{|\theta|\le\varepsilon_0}\) supported on \(\{y_-,y_+\}\), with \(P_0\in\mathcal M_0\), fixed \(P_X\) and \(e\), constant contrast \(U_{P_\theta}(x)=\theta b_Y\), nonzero derivative \(w_0=E_{P_0}[K_X]\otimes b_Y\), quadratic-mean differentiability at zero with Fisher information strictly between zero and infinity, and \(\sup_{|\theta|\le\varepsilon_0}\sum_{j\ge1}\mu_j(P_\theta)^{-2s}|\langle w_0,v_j(P_\theta)\rangle_{\mathcal H}|^2<\infty\). Under this premise, the local two-point reduction gives \(\delta_n^\star\ge cn^{-1/2}\), and hence \(\delta_n^\star\asymp n^{-1/2}\).

%%% FIELD thm-high-smoothness-proof
Write
\[
 \kappa_X^2=\sup_{x\in\mathbb T^d}k(x,x)<\infty,
 \qquad
 \kappa_Y^2=\sup_{y\in\mathcal Y}\ell(y,y)<\infty.
 \tag{1}
\]
The two inequalities are the bounded-covariate-kernel and bounded-outcome-kernel assumptions.  The reproducing property gives
\[
 \|K_x\|_{\mathcal H_X}\leq\kappa_X,
 \qquad
 \|L_y\|_{\mathcal H_Y}\leq\kappa_Y.
 \tag{2}
\]
Thus all Hilbert-valued variables used below have finite moments of every fixed order needed in the construction.

We first establish the upper bound.  For \(a\in\{0,1\}\), define
\[
 D_a=\mathbf 1\{A=a\},
 \qquad
 \pi_a(x)=P(D_a=1\mid X=x),
 \qquad
 p_a(x)=\pi_a(x)^{-1},
 \qquad
 \mu_a(dx)=\pi_a(x)P_X(dx).
 \tag{3}
\]
Here \(\pi_1=e\) and \(\pi_0=1-e\).  The overlap and density-band assumptions imply
\[
 \eta\leq\pi_a(x)\leq1-\eta,
 \qquad
 \eta c_X\leq\frac{d\mu_a}{dx}(x)\leq(1-\eta)C_X
 \quad\text{for almost every }x.
 \tag{4}
\]
Since inversion is smooth on \([\eta,1-\eta]\), the propensity Hölder assumption implies
\[
 p_a\in\mathcal H^{\beta_e}(C_p),
 \tag{5}
\]
where \(C_p<\infty\) depends only on \(\eta,\beta_e,L_e\).  Indeed, differentiating \(\pi_ap_a=1\) recursively through order \(\lfloor\beta_e\rfloor\) expresses each derivative of \(p_a\) as a finite sum of products of derivatives of \(\pi_a\) divided by a power of \(\pi_a\); (4) bounds every denominator, and the highest-order Hölder difference is bounded by adding and subtracting these products.  No property of \(f_X\) beyond (4) is used.

Let \(\mathcal S_M\) be the local polynomial span in the new high-smoothness construction.  Cellwise Taylor expansion of degree \(p\), with its Hölder remainder, supplies \(s_{p,a}\in\mathcal S_M\) and \(s_{\nu,a}\in\mathcal S_M\otimes\mathcal H_Y\) such that
\[
 \|p_a-s_{p,a}\|_{L^\infty(dx)}
 \leq C M^{-\beta_e/d},
 \qquad
 \|\nu_a-s_{\nu,a}\|_{L^\infty(dx;\mathcal H_Y)}
 \leq C M^{-\beta_m/d}.
 \tag{6}
\]
For the Hilbert-valued expansion, the usual integral Taylor identity is a Bochner integral; its norm is bounded by the integral of the Hölder remainder.  Let \(\Pi_{a,M}\) be orthogonal projection onto \(\mathcal S_M\) in \(L^2(\mu_a)\), acting componentwise on \(\mathcal H_Y\)-valued functions.  Its best-approximation property, (4), (5), (6), and the outcome Hölder assumption give
\[
 \|(I-\Pi_{a,M})(1-p_a)\|_{L^2(\mu_a)}
 \leq C M^{-\beta_e/d},
 \qquad
 \|(I-\Pi_{a,M})\nu_a\|_{L^2(\mu_a;\mathcal H_Y)}
 \leq C M^{-\beta_m/d}.
 \tag{7}
\]

Put
\[
 \Omega_a=\int z_M(x)z_M(x)^\top\,d\mu_a(x).
 \tag{8}
\]
The coordinates of \(z_M\) are orthonormal in \(L^2(dx)\), so (4) yields
\[
 \eta c_X I_M\preceq\Omega_a
 \preceq(1-\eta)C_XI_M.
 \tag{9}
\]
Only the fixed number \((p+1)^d\) of coordinates attached to the cell containing \(x\) can be nonzero.  Rescaling the fixed reference-cell Legendre basis therefore gives
\[
 \sup_x\|z_M(x)\|_2^2\leq C_BM,
 \tag{10}
\]
where \(C_B\) depends only on \(d\) and \(p\).  Notice that the constant function belongs to the span; no global basis vector was asserted to be cell-supported.

We next derive the empirical-Gram control.  Conditional on a Gram half of size \(N\asymp n\), set
\[
 \Delta_a=\Omega_a-\widehat\Omega_{a,G}.
\]
Both matrices are block diagonal, with one block of fixed dimension \((p+1)^d\) per cell.  A centered entry of one empirical block is bounded in absolute value by \(CM\), has variance at most \(CM\), and can be nonzero before centering only when \(X\) lies in that cell.  For \(|u|<3\), the power series for the exponential gives
\[
 e^u\leq1+u+\frac{u^2}{2(1-|u|/3)}.
 \tag{11}
\]
Applying (11) to each centered summand and multiplying moment-generating functions by independence proves the scalar Bernstein inequality
\[
 P\{|\widehat\Omega_{a,G}^{uv}-\Omega_a^{uv}|>x\}
 \leq2\exp\left\{-\frac{cNx^2}{M+Mx}\right\}.
 \tag{12}
\]
There are at most \(CM\) nonzero within-block entries.  A union bound, equivalence of entrywise and operator norms on each fixed-dimensional block, and integration of (12) show that, for every fixed integer \(j\geq1\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M}P_P(\mathcal E_{a,G}^c)
 \leq CM\exp(-cN/M),
 \tag{13}
\]
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M}
 E_P\!\left[
  \mathbf 1_{\mathcal E_{a,G}}
  \|\Delta_a\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{2j}
 \right]
 \leq C_j\left(\frac{M\log n}{N}\right)^j.
 \tag{14}
\]
All constants are uniform over the density band.  In particular, no derivative or estimator of \(f_X\) occurs.

We now prove the exact product-residual identity.  On \(\mathcal E_{a,G}\), define
\[
 \mathcal A_{a,q}
 =\sum_{\ell=0}^{q-1}(-1)^\ell
 \widehat\Omega_{a,G}^{-1}
 (\Delta_a\widehat\Omega_{a,G}^{-1})^\ell,
 \qquad
 Q_{a,q}f
 =z_M^\top\mathcal A_{a,q}\int z_Mf\,d\mu_a.
 \tag{15}
\]
The sampling assumption makes the estimation half independent of the Gram half.  From (3), conditioning first on \(X\) gives
\[
 E[z_MR_a^p]
 =\int z_M(1-p_a)\,d\mu_a,
 \qquad
 E[z_M\otimes R_a^\nu]
 =\int z_M\otimes\nu_a\,d\mu_a,
 \tag{16}
\]
\[
 E[D_ah(X)]=\int h\,d\mu_a,
 \qquad
 E[G_{a,i}\mid G]=\Delta_a.
 \tag{17}
\]
All indices in each ordered \(U\)-statistic are distinct.  Repeated use of independence in (16)--(17), followed by summation over \(\ell\) and \(r\), therefore gives
\[
 \sum_{\ell=0}^{q-1}(-1)^\ell
 E[C^{(1)}_{a,\ell}\mid G]
 =\int K_x\otimes\nu_a(x)\,
        Q_{a,q}(1-p_a)(x)\,d\mu_a(x),
 \tag{18}
\]
\[
 \sum_{\ell=0}^{q-1}(-1)^\ell
 E[C^{(2)}_{a,\ell}\mid G]
 =\int K_x\otimes Q_{a,q}\nu_a(x)\,
        (1-p_a(x))\,d\mu_a(x),
 \tag{19}
\]
and the common anchor in \(C^{(3)}\) yields
\[
 \sum_{\ell,r=0}^{q-1}(-1)^{\ell+r}
 E[C^{(3)}_{a,\ell,r}\mid G]
 =\int K_x\otimes Q_{a,q}\nu_a(x)\,
        Q_{a,q}(1-p_a)(x)\,d\mu_a(x).
 \tag{20}
\]
On the other hand, the definitions of \(p_a\), \(\mu_a\), and \(\nu_a\) imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &E[D_aK_X\otimes L_Y]
   -E[K_X\otimes\nu_a(X)]\\
 &\qquad
 =\int K_x\otimes\nu_a(x)\,d\mu_a(x)
  -\int K_x\otimes p_a(x)\nu_a(x)\,d\mu_a(x)\\
 &\qquad
 =\int K_x\otimes(1-p_a(x))\nu_a(x)\,d\mu_a(x).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{21}
\]
Substituting (18)--(21) into the definition of the correction proves, without approximation,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &E[\widehat\theta_a^{I\mid G}\mid G]
   -E[K_X\otimes\nu_a(X)]\\
 &\qquad
 =\int K_x\otimes
   \{(I-Q_{a,q})(1-p_a)\}(x)
   \{(I-Q_{a,q})\nu_a\}(x)\,d\mu_a(x).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{22}
\]
The two one-sided terms remove the two projected cross-products, and the common-anchor term restores their intersection.  Hence \(K_x\) is evaluated exactly at the anchor, and (22) requires no smoothness of \(x\mapsto K_x\).

Because
\(
 \Omega_a=\widehat\Omega_{a,G}+\Delta_a
\), the finite geometric-series identity and (9) imply, on \(\mathcal E_{a,G}\),
\[
 \|\mathcal A_{a,q}-\Omega_a^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \leq C_q\|\Delta_a\|_{mathrm{op}}^q.
 \tag{23}
\]
For scalar or Hilbert-valued \(f\), equations (8), (9), (15), and Cauchy--Schwarz consequently give
\[
 \|(Q_{a,q}-\Pi_{a,M})f\|_{L^2(\mu_a)}
 \leq C_q\|\Delta_a\|_{mathrm{op}}^q
       \|f\|_{L^2(\mu_a)}.
 \tag{24}
\]
The overlap bound and (2) uniformly bound the norms of \(1-p_a\) and \(\nu_a\).  Combining (7), (22), (24), \(\|K_x\|\leq\kappa_X\), and Cauchy--Schwarz in \(L^2(\mu_a)\) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\left\|E[\widehat\theta_a^{I\mid G}\mid G]
       -E[K_X\otimes\nu_a(X)]\right\|_{\mathcal H}\\
 &\qquad\leq
 C\{M^{-\beta_e/d}+\|\Delta_a\|_{mathrm{op}}^q\}
  \{M^{-\beta_m/d}+\|\Delta_a\|_{mathrm{op}}^q\}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{25}
\]

It remains to control fluctuation.  We give the overlap calculation because the statistic is Hilbert-valued.  For any deterministic matrix \(B\), (8)--(9) and cyclicity of trace give
\[
 \iint|z_M(x)^\top Bz_M(x')|^2
       \,d\mu_a(x)d\mu_a(x')
 =\operatorname{tr}(B\Omega_aB^\top\Omega_a)
 \leq CM\|B\|_{mathrm{op}}^2.
 \tag{26}
\]
Consider two copies of an order-\(j\) kernel appearing in the construction.  Conditional on \(G\), copies with disjoint estimation indices are independent and have zero covariance after centering.  If exactly \(c\geq1\) indices are shared, integrate the nonshared endpoints successively using (8); use (26) at every shared link.  The residual factors are bounded by one or by \(\kappa_Y\), the inverse matrices are bounded by \(2/(\eta c_X)\) on the stabilization event, and the fixed chain length is at most \(2q+1\).  Induction over the links therefore bounds the absolute Hilbert covariance by
\[
 C_qM^{c-1}.
 \tag{27}
\]
The proportion of pairs of ordered index tuples sharing exactly \(c\) indices is at most \(C_qN^{-c}\).  Summing (27) over overlap patterns yields
\[
 E\!\left[
  \left\|\widehat\theta_a^{I\mid G}
    -E(\widehat\theta_a^{I\mid G}\mid G)\right\|_{\mathcal H}^2
  \mathbf 1_{\mathcal E_{0,G}\cap\mathcal E_{1,G}}
 \right]
 \leq\frac{C_q}{N}
       \sum_{c=1}^{2q+1}\left(\frac{M}{N}\right)^{c-1}.
 \tag{28}
\]
For Hilbert-valued endpoints, expanding the squared norm replaces products of scalar responses by their Hilbert inner products, whose absolute values are bounded by (2); hence (27)--(28) are norm bounds, not coordinatewise claims.

The deterministic choices in the construction imply
\[
 M^{-2t/d}=o(n^{-1}),
 \qquad
 \left(\frac{M\log n}{n}\right)^q=o(n^{-1}),
 \qquad
 \frac{M}{n}=o(1).
 \tag{29}
\]
Indeed, the first relation follows from \(2\gamma t/d>1\), the second from \(q(1-\gamma)>1\), and the third from \(\gamma<1\).  Squaring (25), applying (14) with \(j=q\) and \(j=2q\), and using (29) proves that the squared conditional bias is \(o(n^{-1})\), uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal M\).  Equations (28)--(29) make the conditional variance at most \(C/n\).  If stabilization fails, the directed contrast is zero, whereas
\[
 \|\Psi(P)\|_{\mathcal H}
 \leq E\|K_X\|\,\|\nu_1(X)-\nu_0(X)\|
 \leq2\kappa_X\kappa_Y.
 \tag{30}
\]
The failure probability in (13) is \(o(n^{-1})\) by \(M\asymp n^\gamma\).  Jensen's inequality for the average of the two directed estimators, (13), and (25)--(30) thus establish
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M}
 E_P\|\widehat\Psi_n^{\mathrm{ho}}-\Psi(P)\|_{\mathcal H}^2
 \leq\frac{C}{n}
 \quad\text{for all sufficiently large }n.
 \tag{31}
\]
For each of the finitely many smaller \(n\), the estimator was set to zero.  Its risk is bounded by (30).  Thus
\[
 \sup_{m\geq1}\sup_{P\in\mathcal M}
 mE_P\|\widehat\Psi_m^{\mathrm{ho}}-\Psi(P)\|_{\mathcal H}^2<\infty,
 \tag{32}
\]
and the deterministic class-oracle envelope \(V_\star\) in the construction is finite.  This proves finiteness; it does not assert that \(V_\star\) is a data-derived or computable threshold.

If \(P\in\mathcal M_0\), then \(\Psi(P)=0\) by the null-class definition.  Markov's inequality applied to the squared norm and the definition of \(V_\star\) give
\[
 E_P\varphi_n^{\mathrm{ho}}
 \leq
 \frac{nE_P\|\widehat\Psi_n^{\mathrm{ho}}-\Psi(P)\|_{\mathcal H}^2}
      {V_\star/\alpha}
 <\alpha.
 \tag{33}
\]
If
\[
 \|\Psi(P)\|_{\mathcal H}
 \geq C_{\alpha,\beta}n^{-1/2},
 \qquad
 C_{\alpha,\beta}
 =\sqrt{V_\star}\{\alpha^{-1/2}+\beta^{-1/2}\},
 \tag{34}
\]
then nonrejection and the reverse triangle inequality imply
\[
 \|\widehat\Psi_n^{\mathrm{ho}}-\Psi(P)\|_{\mathcal H}
 \geq\sqrt{\frac{V_\star}{\beta n}}.
\]
A second application of Markov's inequality yields
\[
 E_P(1-\varphi_n^{\mathrm{ho}})<\beta.
 \tag{35}
\]
These finite-sample inequalities are stronger than the asserted asymptotic bounds and hold uniformly over the source-constrained alternatives because the calculation used only \(P\in\mathcal M\).  The minimax-radius definition therefore gives
\[
 \delta_n^\star\leq C_{\alpha,\beta}n^{-1/2}.
 \tag{36}
\]

We finally prove the conditional reverse inequality.  Impose the class-richness premise in the theorem.  The fixed \(P_X\) and constant contrast give, directly from the definition of \(\Psi\),
\[
 \Psi(P_\theta)
 =E_{P_\theta}[K_X\otimes U_{P_\theta}(X)]
 =\theta E_{P_0}[K_X]\otimes b_Y
 =\theta w_0.
 \tag{37}
\]
Let
\[
 B_s=\sup_{|\theta|\leq\varepsilon_0}
 \sum_{j\geq1}\mu_j(P_\theta)^{-2s}
 |\langle w_0,v_j(P_\theta)\rangle_{\mathcal H}|^2<\infty.
 \tag{38}
\]
For \(\theta_n=c_0n^{-1/2}\), equations (37)--(38) imply
\[
 \sum_{j\geq1}\mu_j(P_{\theta_n})^{-2s}
 |\langle\Psi(P_{\theta_n}),v_j(P_{\theta_n})\rangle_{\mathcal H}|^2
 \leq\frac{c_0^2B_s}{n}\leq R^2
 \tag{39}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Since the path lies in \(\mathcal M\), \(w_0\ne0\), and \(|\theta_n|\leq\varepsilon_0\) eventually, the alternative-class definition yields
\[
 P_{\theta_n}\in\mathcal M_1(\delta_n;s,R),
 \qquad
 \delta_n=c_0\|w_0\|_{\mathcal H}n^{-1/2}.
 \tag{40}
\]

Quadratic-mean differentiability supplies a score \(\dot\ell\in L^2_0(P_0)\), with \(I=E_{P_0}\dot\ell^2\in(0,\infty)\), such that
\[
 \int\left(
  \sqrt{dP_\theta}-\sqrt{dP_0}
  -\frac{\theta}{2}\dot\ell\sqrt{dP_0}
 \right)^2=o(\theta^2).
 \tag{41}
\]
Consequently, for \(H^2(P,Q)=\int(\sqrt{dP}-\sqrt{dQ})^2\),
\[
 H^2(P_\theta,P_0)=\frac{I\theta^2}{4}+o(\theta^2).
 \tag{42}
\]
If (41) is understood for the full-data law, its observed-data marginal satisfies the same upper bound because conditional Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\(
 (\sqrt{\int p\,dz}-\sqrt{\int q\,dz})^2
 \leq\int(\sqrt p-\sqrt q)^2\,dz
\); integrating over the observed record proves Hellinger contraction under marginalization.  Thus it suffices to continue with the induced observed laws, denoted by the same symbols.

The product-affinity identity and \(1-(1-x)^n\leq nx\) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 H^2(P_{\theta_n}^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
 &=2\left[1-\left\{1-\frac12H^2(P_{\theta_n},P_0)\right\}^{n}\right]\\
 &\leq nH^2(P_{\theta_n},P_0).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{43}
\]
Also, Cauchy--Schwarz applied to
\(
 |p-q|=|\sqrt p-\sqrt q|(\sqrt p+\sqrt q)
\) yields
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(P,Q)=\frac12\int|p-q|\leq H(P,Q).
 \tag{44}
\]
Equations (42)--(44) bound the total variation distance between the two observed product laws by \(C_Ic_0+o(1)\).  Since \(\beta<1-\alpha\), choose \(c_0>0\) so small that this bound is less than \(1-\alpha-\beta\) for all large \(n\).  For every randomized test \(\varphi_n\) satisfying \(E_{P_0}\varphi_n\leq\alpha\), the variational definition of total variation gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_{P_{\theta_n}}(1-\varphi_n)
 &\geq1-E_{P_0}\varphi_n
  -\operatorname{TV}(P_{\theta_n}^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})\\
 &>\beta.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{45}
\]
Thus no level-\(\alpha\) test controls type-II error by \(\beta\) on \(\mathcal M_1(\delta_n;s,R)\).  By monotonicity of the separated alternative classes and the definition of \(\delta_n^\star\), (40) and (45) imply
\[
 \delta_n^\star\geq c_0\|w_0\|_{\mathcal H}n^{-1/2}.
 \tag{46}
\]
Combining (36) and (46) proves \(\delta_n^\star\asymp n^{-1/2}\) under the stated class-richness premise.
