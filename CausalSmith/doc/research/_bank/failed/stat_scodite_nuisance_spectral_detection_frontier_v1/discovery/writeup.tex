\section*{stat\_\allowbreak{}scodite\_\allowbreak{}nuisance\_\allowbreak{}spectral\_\allowbreak{}detection\_\allowbreak{}frontier}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} The all-tests SCoDiTE problem has two mathematically separate branches. When \(t>d/2\), a cell-local polynomial empirical higher-order estimator with rank \(M\asymp n^\gamma=o(n)\), a finite Neumann correction, and a common \(K_X\)-anchor has uniform mean-squared error \(O(n^{-1})\) over every bounded positive design density, without smoothness of \(f_X\) or of the feature map. Its deterministic class-oracle variance envelope gives uniform size and root-rate power; a regular constant-contrast path gives the matching lower bound when that path belongs to the class. For \(0<t\le d/2\), the separate collision and occupancy handle remains unchanged: the legal fuzzy construction proves the slower uniform-design lower radius \(n^{-2t/(2t+d)}\) for \(0<s\le1/2\), while the arbitrary-density match, spectral method classification, and adaptive grid remain open. Characteristicness of the outcome kernel does not turn the covariate-smoothed null into pointwise conditional homogeneity.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Existing SCoDiTE theory is first order and pointwise in the law, direct spectral tests omit causal nuisances, and scalar empirical higher-order results do not establish a Hilbert-valued common-anchor expansion or a uniform SCoDiTE test. Below the smoothness elbow, no checked result matches the legal fuzzy lower experiment under an arbitrary bounded design density.

\paragraph{Niche.} The paper separates a proved density-robust high-smoothness root-rate theorem from the answer-open rough-design all-tests modulus, then asks when identity or resolvent spectral weighting attains that modulus.

\paragraph{Fill.} Above the elbow, a separate explicit higher-order construction uses cell-local polynomial spans, stabilized arm Gram matrices, finite Neumann chains, and two one-sided plus one common-anchor correction. Their exact inclusion--exclusion identity leaves the product of the propensity and arm-embedding projection residuals while evaluating \(K_X\) exactly, which yields uniform \(O(n^{-1})\) Hilbert risk and oracle-calibrated testing. The original collision and occupancy handle is retained only for the rough branch, where the paired exact-null tables and fuzzy lower bound feed the unresolved arbitrary-density frontier and its spectral and adaptive consumers.

\section{Related work}

Jain and Luedtke (2026) define the SCoDiTE moment, its efficient Hilbert influence function, and fixed-operator MMD and Wald tests; their first-order product-rate theory does not supply the higher-order uniform test proved here. Liu, Mukherjee, Newey, and Robins (2026, arXiv:1705.07577v5) prove semiparametric efficiency without density smoothness for scalar missing-at-random and doubly robust functionals using empirical Gram bases with rank \(o(n)\). The high-smoothness theorem adapts that architecture through an explicit common-anchor inclusion--exclusion correction and proves its risk directly; it does not import a scalar theorem. Kennedy, Balakrishnan, Robins, and Wasserman (2024) motivate the localized fuzzy-hypothesis architecture in the separate rough branch, whose nonfactorizing alternative table is specific to the SCoDiTE null. Hagrass, Sriperumbudur, and Li (2024) and Balasubramanian, Li, and Yuan (2021) provide direct-sample spectral arguments under different metrics, while Schrab et al. (2023) provide an aggregation template. None resolves the rough arbitrary-density SCoDiTE modulus or the requested spectral iff and adaptive-grid results.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:iid]\label{ass:iid}
The records \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\) are independent and identically distributed under \(P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Independent sampling, \cite{JainLuedtke2026SCoDiTE}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:consistency]\label{ass:consistency}
\(Y=AY(1)+(1-A)Y(0)\) almost surely.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Consistency, \cite{JainLuedtke2026SCoDiTE}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:exchangeability]\label{ass:exchangeability}
\((Y(0),Y(1))\perp A\mid X\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Conditional exchangeability, \cite{JainLuedtke2026SCoDiTE}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:overlap]\label{ass:overlap}
\(\eta\le e(x)\le1-\eta\) for \(P_X\)-almost every \(x\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Strong overlap, \cite{JainLuedtke2026SCoDiTE}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:density-band]\label{ass:density-band}
\(c_X\le f_X(x)\le C_X\) for Lebesgue-almost every \(x\in\mathbb T^d\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Random-design density band, \cite{KennedyBalakrishnanRobinsWasserman2024CATE}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:propensity-holder]\label{ass:propensity-holder}
\(e\in\mathcal H^{\beta_e}(L_e)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Periodic Hölder propensity, \cite{KennedyBalakrishnanRobinsWasserman2024CATE}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:outcome-holder]\label{ass:outcome-holder}
\(\nu_a\in\mathcal H^{\beta_m}(L_m;\mathcal H_Y)\) for each \(a\in\{0,1\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This maintained Hilbert-valued Hölder condition is satisfied whenever the arm-specific conditional outcome laws vary Hölder-continuously in total variation and the outcome kernel is bounded; it includes conditional location--scale models with Hölder location and scale maps and bounded Lipschitz kernel sections.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:covariate-kernel-bounded]\label{ass:covariate-kernel-bounded}
\(\sup_{x\in\mathbb T^d}k(x,x)<\infty\).

\begin{assumption}[ass:covariate-kernel-stationary]\label{ass:covariate-kernel-stationary}

\begin{assumption}[ass:outcome-kernel-characteristic]\label{ass:outcome-kernel-characteristic}

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Characteristic mean embedding, \cite{GrettonEtAl2012KernelTwoSample}).

\begin{assumption}[ass:covariance-profile]\label{ass:covariance-profile}

\section{Main results}

\begin{proposition}[prop:identified-scodite]\label{prop:identified-scodite}
Under consistency and conditional exchangeability, \(\Psi(P)=E_P[K_X\otimes\{L_{Y(1)}-L_{Y(0)}\}]\). On the two-outcome submodel supported on \(\{y_-,y_+\}\), this becomes \(\Psi(P)=E_P[K_X\otimes b_Y\{P(Y=y_+\mid A=1,X)-P(Y=y_+\mid A=0,X)\}]\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For \(a\in\{0,1\}\), consistency gives \(L_Y=L_{Y(a)}\) on \(\{A=a\}\). Conditional exchangeability and iterated expectation give, for \(P_X\)-almost every \(x\),
\[
\nu_a(x)=E_P[L_Y\mid A=a,X=x]=E_P[L_{Y(a)}\mid X=x].
\]
The sections are Bochner integrable by boundedness of the outcome kernel. Hence
\[
\Psi(P)=E_P[K_X\otimes\{\nu_1(X)-\nu_0(X)\}]
=E_P[K_X\otimes\{L_{Y(1)}-L_{Y(0)}\}].
\]
On the two-outcome submodel, with \(m_a(x)=P(Y=y_+\mid A=a,X=x)\),
\[
\nu_a(x)=m_a(x)L_{y_+}+\{1-m_a(x)\}L_{y_-}
=L_{y_-}+m_a(x)b_Y.
\]
Thus \(U(x)=\{m_1(x)-m_0(x)\}b_Y\), and substitution proves the second formula.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This fixes the law-valued causal target while supplying a legal two-outcome reduction for the lower-bound construction. \textit{Gap.} Jain and Luedtke give the general identifying moment; the embedded two-outcome reduction is needed here to audit exact-null mixtures and source membership without shrinking the ambient model to CATE. \textit{Consumer.} The reduction makes the least-favorable experiment computable while the resulting frontier continues to govern general conditional outcome laws.

\begin{theorem}[thm:fourier-null-iff]\label{thm:fourier-null-iff}
Write the translation-invariant kernel as \(k(x,x')=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^d}a_z e^{2\pi i z^\top(x-x')}\), with \(a_z\ge0\). Then \(\Psi(P)=0\) if and only if the \(\mathcal H_Y\)-valued Fourier coefficient \(\widehat{f_XU}(z)\) is zero for every \(z\) with \(a_z>0\). If \(a_z>0\) for every \(z\in\mathbb Z^d\), then, over the model class, \(\Psi(P)=0\) is equivalent to equality of the conditional laws of \(Y(1)\) and \(Y(0)\) given \(X\), \(P_X\)-almost surely. Conversely, if \(a_{z_0}=0\) for some \(z_0\), the smoothing map has a nonzero real trigonometric contrast in its kernel; whenever the corresponding sufficiently small two-outcome perturbation is admitted by the fixed model class, it is a counterexample to conditional-law homogeneity. In particular, when \(a_0=0\) and the constant two-outcome perturbation is admitted, \(U(x)=b_Y\) is an explicit counterexample.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(g(x)=f_X(x)U(x)\). Boundedness of \(\ell\) makes \(U\) essentially bounded, and the density band makes \(g\in L^1(\mathbb T^d;\mathcal H_Y)\). For a finite set \(F\subset\mathbb Z^d\), the Fourier feature representation of the partial kernel gives
\[
\left\|\int K_x^F\otimes g(x)\,dx\right\|^2
=\sum_{z\in F}a_z\left\|\int e^{2\pi i z^\top x}g(x)\,dx\right\|_{\mathcal H_Y}^2.
\]
Since \(a_z\ge0\) and \(\sum_z a_z=k(0,0)<\infty\), monotone convergence over increasing finite sets yields
\[
\|\Psi(P)\|_{\mathcal H}^2
=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^d}a_z\|\widehat g(-z)\|_{\mathcal H_Y}^2. \tag{1}
\]
All summands are nonnegative, proving the first equivalence.

Suppose every \(a_z\) is positive and \(\Psi(P)=0\). Then every Fourier coefficient of \(g\) vanishes. Let \(F_m\) be the product Fejér kernel on \(\mathbb T^d\). The convolution \(F_m*g\) is a finite linear combination of these coefficients, hence is zero. Positivity, unit integral, and concentration of \(F_m\) imply
\[
\|F_m*g-g\|_1\le\int F_m(t)\|g(\cdot-t)-g(\cdot)\|_1dt\longrightarrow0,
\]
by translation continuity in Bochner \(L^1\), after splitting the integral into a neighborhood of zero and its complement. Thus \(g=0\) almost everywhere. Since \(f_X\ge c_X>0\), \(U=0\) \(P_X\)-almost surely. The identifying proposition says that \(U(x)\) is the difference of the conditional mean embeddings of \(Y(1)\) and \(Y(0)\) given \(X=x\). Injectivity of the outcome-kernel mean map therefore makes \(U(x)=0\) equivalent to equality of those conditional laws.

If \(a_{z_0}=0\), reality of \(k\) gives \(a_{-z_0}=0\). For \(z_0\ne0\), take \(g_0(x)=\cos(2\pi z_0^\top x)b_Y\); for \(z_0=0\), take \(g_0(x)=b_Y\). This is nonzero and has Fourier support only at zero-weight frequencies, so (1) gives \(\int K_x\otimes g_0(x)dx=0\). Under uniform design, put
\[
m_1(x)=\{1+\varepsilon q(x)\}/2,\qquad
m_0(x)=\{1-\varepsilon q(x)\}/2,
\]
where \(q(x)=\cos(2\pi z_0^\top x)\) or \(q\equiv1\). For \(0<\varepsilon<1\) these are valid two-outcome probabilities, \(U(x)=\varepsilon q(x)b_Y\), and the conditional laws differ on a set of positive measure although \(\Psi=0\). Whenever this smooth path is admitted by the fixed Hölder radii and covariance-profile constants, it is the model-class witness. This qualification is necessary because the frozen assumptions do not require the class to contain any nonnull perturbation.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The minimax null must be the object actually separated by the smoothed embedding, not a stronger verbal homogeneity claim. \textit{Gap.} Park et al. prove injectivity for the unsmoothed conditional embedding, while Jain and Luedtke invoke a product-kernel argument; neither statement characterizes the centered covariate operator's quotient null. \textit{Consumer.} Kernel designers can include the constant mode when the empirical claim is conditional-law homogeneity, or report the exact quotient null otherwise.

\begin{theorem}[thm:known-propensity-root-n]\label{thm:known-propensity-root-n}
For every fixed source exponent \(s>0\) and radius \(R>0\), suppose the model class contains, for all sufficiently small \(|\vartheta|\), the uniform-design two-outcome path
\[
P_\vartheta(A=a,Y=y_\sigma\mid X=x)
=\frac{1+\vartheta(2a-1)\sigma}{4},
\qquad a\in\{0,1\},\quad \sigma\in\{-1,1\},
\]
where \(y_1=y_+\), \(y_{-1}=y_-\), the fixed kernel has \(a_0>0\), and the covariance-profile constants admit this path. Then \(\delta_{n,e}^\star\asymp n^{-1/2}\). The upper bound is attained by the bounded oracle estimator \(\widehat\Psi_e\), and the lower bound is attained on this constant Fourier coordinate. Thus covariance eigen-decay cannot improve the worst-case oracle exponent under RKHS-norm separation in every class satisfying this explicit richness premise.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Set
\[
B=\eta^{-1}\sup_x\sqrt{k(x,x)}\sup_y\sqrt{\ell(y,y)}<\infty,\qquad
Z_e(O)=K_X\otimes L_Y\left\{\frac A{e(X)}-\frac{1-A}{1-e(X)}\right\}.
\]
Overlap and bounded kernels give \(\|Z_e\|\le B\). Iterated expectation yields
\[
E_PZ_e=E_P[K_X\otimes\{\nu_1(X)-\nu_0(X)\}]=\Psi(P). \tag{2}
\]
Thus \(\widehat\Psi_e=n^{-1}\sum_iZ_e(O_i)\) is unbiased and independence gives
\[
E_P\|\widehat\Psi_e-\Psi(P)\|^2
=n^{-1}E_P\|Z_e-E_PZ_e\|^2\le B^2/n. \tag{3}
\]
Reject when \(\|\widehat\Psi_e\|>B/\sqrt{n\alpha}\). Under the null, (3) and Markov's inequality give size at most \(\alpha\). Under an alternative with
\[
\|\Psi(P)\|\ge Bn^{-1/2}\{\alpha^{-1/2}+\beta^{-1/2}\},
\]
the reverse triangle inequality and (3) give type-II error at most \(\beta\). Hence \(\delta_{n,e}^\star\le Cn^{-1/2}\).

For the converse, write \(\sigma_Y=2\mathbf1\{Y=y_+\}-1\). Direct summation on the stated path gives
\[
\frac{dP_\vartheta}{dP_0}=1+\vartheta(2A-1)\sigma_Y,\quad
\chi^2(P_\vartheta,P_0)=\vartheta^2,\quad
\chi^2(P_\vartheta^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
=(1+\vartheta^2)^n-1\le e^{n\vartheta^2}-1. \tag{4}
\]
With \(v_X=K_X\otimes b_Y\),
\[
\Psi(P_\vartheta)=\vartheta E[K_X]\otimes b_Y,\qquad
\|\Psi(P_\vartheta)\|=|\vartheta|\sqrt{a_0}\|b_Y\|. \tag{5}
\]
The influence function is \(\phi_{P_\vartheta}=v_X(2A-1)\sigma_Y-\Psi(P_\vartheta)\). If \(C=E[v_X\otimes v_X]\), then \(\Sigma_{P_\vartheta}=C-\Psi(P_\vartheta)\otimes\Psi(P_\vartheta)\). Under uniform design the constant Fourier vector is an eigenvector of \(C\) with eigenvalue \(\lambda_0=a_0\|b_Y\|^2>0\); the corresponding eigenvalue of \(\Sigma_{P_\vartheta}\) is \(\lambda_0(1-\vartheta^2)\), and the orthogonal eigenvalues are unchanged. Therefore
\[
\|\Sigma_{P_\vartheta}^{-s}\Psi(P_\vartheta)\|^2
=\vartheta^2\lambda_0^{1-2s}(1-\vartheta^2)^{-2s}. \tag{6}
\]
At \(\vartheta=c_0/\sqrt n\), (6) is below \(R^2\) for large \(n\). Choose \(c_0>0\) so that \(\frac12\sqrt{e^{c_0^2}-1}<1-\alpha-\beta\). Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
\operatorname{TV}(Q,P)\le\frac12\sqrt{\chi^2(Q,P)}.
\]
If a test had the two required error bounds, then
\[
1-\alpha-\beta\le E_{P_\vartheta}\varphi-E_{P_0}\varphi
\le\operatorname{TV}(P_\vartheta^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n}),
\]
contradicting (4). Equations (5)--(6) prove \(\delta_{n,e}^\star\ge cn^{-1/2}\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This identifies the correct oracle benchmark before nuisance and spectral effects are compared. \textit{Gap.} Direct spectral tests obtain nonparametric rates in a stronger \(L^2\)-type separation, while Jain and Luedtke do not state the all-tests RKHS-norm radius. \textit{Consumer.} It prevents the 401(k) analysis from interpreting whitening as an exponent improvement that the chosen estimand cannot support.

\begin{lemma}[lem:fuzzy-cell-algebra]\label{lem:fuzzy-cell-algebra}
For every \(x\), \(h\), and \(\omega\), write \(u=u_\omega(x)\) and \(v=v_\omega(x)\). The bump normalization and perturbation constant give \(|u|\le a_h\le c_\circ\le1/16\) and \(|v|\le b_h\le c_\circ\le1/16\). The four entries of each table in \(\mathrm{def{:}fuzzy\mbox{-}mixtures}\) sum to one and are at least \(\{1-|u|-|v|-|uv|\}/4>0\). The null table satisfies \(p_\omega=(1+r_Au)(1+s_Yv)/4\), so
\[
P_\omega(Y=y_+\mid A=1,X=x)=P_\omega(Y=y_+\mid A=0,X=x)=(1+v)/2
\]
and \(U_{P_\omega}(x)=0\). The alternative table has \(Q_\omega(A=1\mid X=x)=(1+u)/2\) and
\[
Q_\omega(Y=y_+\mid A=1,X=x)=\frac{1+u-v+uv}{2(1+u)},\qquad
Q_\omega(Y=y_+\mid A=0,X=x)=\frac{1-u-v-uv}{2(1-u)},
\]
whence
\[
Q_\omega(Y=y_+\mid A=1,X=x)-Q_\omega(Y=y_+\mid A=0,X=x)
=\frac{2uv}{1-u^2},\qquad
U_{Q_\omega}(x)=\frac{2u_\omega(x)v_\omega(x)}{1-u_\omega(x)^2}b_Y.
\]
Disjoint bump supports and the shared sign vector give \(u_\omega v_\omega=a_hb_h\sum_jg_{h,j}^2\) and \(u_\omega^2=a_h^2\sum_jg_{h,j}^2\), so \(U_{Q_\omega}\) is independent of \(\omega\) and nonnegative in its scalar coefficient; it is nonzero when \(c_\circ>0\). Moreover,
\[
2^{-K_h}\sum_\omega p_\omega(r_A,s_Y\mid x)
=2^{-K_h}\sum_\omega q_\omega(r_A,s_Y\mid x)
=\frac{1+r_As_Ya_hb_h\sum_jg_{h,j}(x)^2}{4}.
\]
Thus the uniform one-record mixtures coincide exactly. If \(a_0>0\), then
\[
\|\Psi(Q_\omega)\|_{\mathcal H}
\ge2\sqrt{a_0}c_Xc_ga_hb_h\|b_Y\|_{\mathcal H_Y},
\]
and this lower bound is strictly positive when \(c_\circ>0\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At any \(x\), at most one bump is nonzero. Since each bump is bounded by one,
\[
|u_\omega(x)|\le a_h,\qquad |v_\omega(x)|\le b_h.
\]
Because \(h\le1\) and both exponents are positive, \(a_h,b_h\le c_\circ\); the first entry in the minimum defining \(c_\circ\) gives \(c_\circ\le1/16\). In either table the nonconstant terms cancel when the four cells are summed, and the triangle inequality gives the stated positive lower bound.

For the null,
\[
(1+r_Au)(1+s_Yv)=1+r_Au+s_Yv+r_As_Yuv,
\]
so \(A\) and \(Y\) are conditionally independent given \(X\), with outcome probability \((1+v)/2\). For the alternative, summing each treatment row and dividing gives the displayed probabilities. Their difference has numerator
\[
(1+u-v+uv)(1-u)-(1-u-v-uv)(1+u)=4uv
\]
and denominator \(2(1-u^2)\). The two-outcome identification formula then proves the expression for \(U_{Q_\omega}\).

Disjointness gives \(g_{h,j}g_{h,k}=0\) for \(j\ne k\), and \(\omega_j^2=1\), whence
\[
u_\omega v_\omega=a_hb_h\sum_jg_{h,j}^2,\qquad
u_\omega^2=a_h^2\sum_jg_{h,j}^2.
\]
This proves sign-independence and nonnegativity. The fixed bump is nonzero, so strict nonzeroness follows when \(c_\circ>0\). Averaging over symmetric signs kills \(u\) and \(v\), while their shared-sign product averages to \(a_hb_h\sum_jg_{h,j}^2\), proving exact one-record cancellation.

Finally define
\[
q_h(x)=\frac{2a_hb_h\sum_jg_{h,j}(x)^2}
 {1-a_h^2\sum_jg_{h,j}(x)^2}.
\]
Then \(q_h\ge2a_hb_h\sum_jg_{h,j}^2\) and
\(\Psi(Q_\omega)=\int K_x\otimes b_Yq_h(x)f_X(x)dx\). Projection onto the constant Fourier coordinate, \(f_X\ge c_X\), and \(\int\sum_jg_{h,j}^2=c_g\) give
\[
\|\Psi(Q_\omega)\|
\ge\sqrt{a_0}\|b_Y\|\int q_hf_X
\ge2\sqrt{a_0}c_Xc_ga_hb_h\|b_Y\|.
\]
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The displayed cell algebra rules out the erroneous factorization of the alternative and certifies both the exact null and the nonzero SCoDiTE signal before any information bound is invoked. \textit{Gap.} The scalar fuzzy construction of Kennedy et al. does not record the full SCoDiTE cell table or the kernel-embedded contrast generated by its nonfactorizing alternative. \textit{Consumer.} The identities provide the auditable null and signal calculations used by every rough-regime lower bound in the proposal.

\begin{lemma}[lem:fuzzy-legality-affinity]\label{lem:fuzzy-legality-affinity}
Assume \(c_\circ>0\), and suppose that, for some fixed \(0<c_k\le C_k<\infty\), the nonzero Fourier weights of the fixed periodic kernel, ordered decreasingly, satisfy \(c_kj^{-2r}\le a_{(j)}\le C_kj^{-2r}\), with \(a_0>0\). For every sufficiently small dyadic \(h\), every full-data extension \(P_\omega,Q_\omega\) in \(\mathrm{def{:}fuzzy\mbox{-}mixtures}\) satisfies consistency and exchangeability and has \(\eta\le e_\omega\le1-\eta\). Quantitatively,
\[
\|e_\omega-1/2\|_{\mathcal H^{\beta_e}}\le C_ga_hh^{-\beta_e}/2=C_gc_\circ/2.
\]
Under \(P_\omega\), \(\nu_{0,\omega}=\nu_{1,\omega}=\nu_\star+v_\omega b_Y/2\) and
\[
\|\nu_{a,\omega}-\nu_\star\|_{\mathcal H^{\beta_m}(\mathcal H_Y)}
\le C_gb_hh^{-\beta_m}\|b_Y\|_{\mathcal H_Y}/2.
\]
Under \(Q_\omega\),
\[
\nu_{1,\omega}=\nu_\star-\frac{v_\omega(1-u_\omega)}{2(1+u_\omega)}b_Y,\qquad
\nu_{0,\omega}=\nu_\star-\frac{v_\omega(1+u_\omega)}{2(1-u_\omega)}b_Y,
\]
and
\[
\max_a\|\nu_{a,\omega}-\nu_\star\|_{\mathcal H^{\beta_m}(\mathcal H_Y)}
\le C_{\mathrm{arm}}b_hh^{-\beta_m}\|b_Y\|_{\mathcal H_Y}.
\]
Hence the declared overlap and Hölder conditions hold by the definition of \(c_\circ\). There are fixed \(0<c_{\mathrm{IF}}<C_{\mathrm{IF}}<\infty\) such that
\[
c_{\mathrm{IF}}C_{k,b}(P_X)\preceq\Sigma_{P_\omega},\Sigma_{Q_\omega}
\preceq C_{\mathrm{IF}}C_{k,b}(P_X).
\]
Consequently,
\[
c_{\mathrm{IF}}c_Xc_k\|b_Y\|^2j^{-2r}
\le\mu_j(P_\omega),\mu_j(Q_\omega)
\le C_{\mathrm{IF}}C_XC_k\|b_Y\|^2j^{-2r};
\]
whenever \(c_\Sigma,C_\Sigma\) bracket these constants, all components satisfy the covariance profile. For \(0<s\le1/2\),
\[
\|\Sigma_{Q_\omega}^{-s}\Psi(Q_\omega)\|^2
=\sum_{j\ge1}\mu_j(Q_\omega)^{-2s}
 |\langle\Psi(Q_\omega),v_j(Q_\omega)\rangle|^2
\le C_s\|U_{Q_\omega}\|_{L^2(P_X;\mathcal H_Y)}^2
\le C_s'a_h^2b_h^2,
\]
which is at most \(R^2\) for small \(h\). Write
\(\overline P_h^{(n)}=2^{-K_h}\sum_\omega P_\omega^{\otimes n}\) and
\(\overline Q_h^{(n)}=2^{-K_h}\sum_\omega Q_\omega^{\otimes n}\).
For every fixed \(C_{\mathrm{occ}}<\infty\), if
\(n\max_jp_{h,j}\le C_{\mathrm{occ}}\), then
\[
H^2(\overline Q_h^{(n)},\overline P_h^{(n)})
\le Cn(n-1)a_h^2b_h^2\sum_{j=1}^{K_h}p_{h,j}^2.
\]
Under uniform design and \(t<d/2\), choosing \(h=c_hn^{-2/(2t+d)}\) with sufficiently small fixed \(c_h>0\) makes the mixture affinity bounded away from zero while \(\|\Psi(Q_\omega)\|_{\mathcal H}\ge ch^t\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The full-data extension draws the potential outcomes conditionally on \(X\), draws \(A\) conditionally on \(X\) independently of them, and sets \(Y=AY(1)+(1-A)Y(0)\). Thus consistency and conditional exchangeability hold by construction. Both tables give \(e_\omega=(1+u_\omega)/2\), and the cell bounds together with the \(1-2\eta\) term in \(c_\circ\) imply overlap. The scaled-bump property gives
\[
\|e_\omega-1/2\|_{\mathcal H^{\beta_e}}
\le a_hC_gh^{-\beta_e}/2=C_gc_\circ/2.
\]
The conditional probabilities computed in the cell lemma give the displayed four embedding formulas. The null bound follows directly from the scaled-bump bound. For the alternative, the maps
\[
(u,v)\mapsto-\frac{v(1-u)}{2(1+u)},\qquad
(u,v)\mapsto-\frac{v(1+u)}{2(1-u)}
\]
have uniformly bounded derivatives of every required order on \([-1/8,1/8]^2\). The defining property of \(C_{\mathrm{arm}}\), applied cellwise to bumps vanishing smoothly at cell boundaries, gives the asserted alternative bound. The remaining terms in the minimum defining \(c_\circ\), and the triangle inequality around \(1/2\) and \(\nu_\star\), put these functions in the fixed Hölder balls.

For covariance, set \(V_x=K_x\otimes b_Y\), let \(m_a(x)=P(Y=y_+\mid A=a,X=x)\), and put \(\tau=m_1-m_0\). Direct substitution in the influence function and conditional variance decomposition give
\[
\Sigma_P
=E\left[V_X\otimes V_X\left\{
\frac{m_1(X)\{1-m_1(X)\}}{e(X)}
+\frac{m_0(X)\{1-m_0(X)\}}{1-e(X)}\right\}\right]
+\operatorname{Cov}\{V_X\tau(X)\}. \tag{7}
\]
All arm probabilities and treatment probabilities are bounded away from zero and one by fixed constants. The scalar in braces in (7) is therefore between two positive constants. Moreover,
\[
0\preceq\operatorname{Cov}\{V_X\tau(X)\}
\preceq E[V_X\otimes V_X\tau(X)^2]
\preceq E[V_X\otimes V_X],
\]
because \(|\tau|\le1\). This proves the covariance sandwich.

Let \(C_0\) denote the same feature second moment under normalized Lebesgue measure. The density band gives
\[
c_XC_0\preceq C_{k,b}(P_X)\preceq C_XC_0.
\]
The nonzero eigenvalues of \(C_0\) are \(a_{(j)}\|b_Y\|^2\). Applying the min--max identity
\[
\lambda_j(T)=\inf_{\dim F=j-1}\sup_{\|z\|=1,\,z\perp F}\langle z,Tz\rangle
\]
to the operator inequalities proves the eigenvalue bounds and hence covariance-profile membership under the stated compatibility of constants.

For the source calculation define \(S:L^2(P_X)\to\mathcal H\) by \(Sg=E[V_Xg(X)]\). Then \(SS^*=C_{k,b}(P_X)\), \(\Psi(Q_\omega)=S\tau_\omega\), and
\[
\|S^*z\|_2^2=\langle z,SS^*z\rangle
\le c_{\mathrm{IF}}^{-1}\langle z,\Sigma_{Q_\omega}z\rangle.
\]
By duality on the closure of the range, \(\Sigma_{Q_\omega}^{-1/2}S\) extends to a bounded operator with norm at most \(c_{\mathrm{IF}}^{-1/2}\). Since \(\Sigma_{Q_\omega}\) is bounded, spectral calculus gives, for \(0<s\le1/2\),
\[
\|\Sigma_{Q_\omega}^{-s}S\tau_\omega\|
\le\|\Sigma_{Q_\omega}\|^{1/2-s}
 \|\Sigma_{Q_\omega}^{-1/2}S\tau_\omega\|
\le C_s\|\tau_\omega\|_2. \tag{8}
\]
The cell formula and \(|u|\le1/16\) imply
\[
|\tau_\omega(x)|\le Ca_hb_h\sum_jg_{h,j}(x)^2.
\]
Disjointness, \(0\le g_{h,j}\le1\), and \(f_X\le C_X\) yield \(\|\tau_\omega\|_2^2\le C'a_h^2b_h^2\). Together with (8), this proves the source bound, including membership in the domain of the inverse power.

For the mixtures, condition on the covariates. Signs from distinct cells are independent. In a cell containing \(N\) observations, use reference probabilities
\[
R_i(r,s)=\frac{1+rs\,a_hb_hg_i^2}{4}.
\]
Relative to \(R_i\), the two component factors are \(1+\omega\xi_i\) and \(1+\omega\zeta_i\), where
\[
\xi_i=\frac{g_i(ra_h+sb_h)}{1+rs\,a_hb_hg_i^2},\qquad
\zeta_i=\frac{g_i(ra_h-sb_h)}{1+rs\,a_hb_hg_i^2}.
\]
Averaging over the sign retains only even products. Thus one-record terms cancel, and every nonzero term in the difference contains a factor \(a_hb_h\) and two distinct record indices. Squaring the absolutely convergent even-subset series, using \(|\xi_i|,|\zeta_i|\le1/7\), choosing the first two indices with opposite \(b_h\)-parity, and summing the remaining paired indices gives
\[
H_j^2\le Ca_h^2b_h^2N_j(N_j-1)
\exp\{C(a_h^2+b_h^2)N_j\}. \tag{9}
\]
Hellinger tensorization across cells and conditioning on multinomial counts give
\[
H^2(\overline Q_h^{(n)},\overline P_h^{(n)})
\le Ca_h^2b_h^2\sum_jE[(N_j)_2e^{\gamma N_j}],
\qquad 0\le\gamma\le C(a_h^2+b_h^2).
\]
For \(N_j\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,p_{h,j})\), differentiation of its probability generating function gives
\[
E[(N_j)_2e^{\gamma N_j}]
=(n)_2p_{h,j}^2e^{2\gamma}
(1-p_{h,j}+p_{h,j}e^\gamma)^{n-2}
\le C(n)_2p_{h,j}^2,
\]
where the last inequality uses \(np_{h,j}\le C_{\mathrm{occ}}\) and the uniform bound on \(\gamma\). This proves the mixture inequality.

Under uniform design, \(p_{h,j}=h^d\). At \(h=c_hn^{-2/(2t+d)}\) with \(t<d/2\), \(nh^d\) is bounded and
\[
n(n-1)a_h^2b_h^2\sum_jp_{h,j}^2
\le Cn^2h^{2t+d}=Cc_h^{2t+d}.
\]
Choosing \(c_h\) small bounds Hellinger distance away from its maximal value, while the cell lemma gives \(\|\Psi(Q_\omega)\|\ge ch^t\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This isolates every model-membership and affinity inequality needed to turn the corrected cell algebra into a legal all-tests lower bound. \textit{Gap.} Neither scalar CATE lower bounds nor direct kernel tests verify Hilbert arm smoothness, influence-covariance stability, and covariance-dependent source membership for the same exact-null causal mixture. \textit{Consumer.} The lemma makes the nuisance lower theorem reusable and gives the arbitrary-density question a concrete construction whose remaining difficulty is the occupancy match, not basic legality.

\begin{theorem}[thm:fuzzy-nuisance-lower]\label{thm:fuzzy-nuisance-lower}
Suppose \(0<s\le1/2\), \(t<d/2\), the Hölder balls contain the balanced two-outcome baseline in their interiors, and the fixed periodic kernel has \(a_0>0\) and ordered nonzero Fourier weights \(a_{(j)}\asymp j^{-2r}\), with the declared covariance-profile constants wide enough to contain the derived sandwich in \(\mathrm{lem{:}fuzzy\mbox{-}legality\mbox{-}affinity}\). Under uniform \(P_X\), \(\delta_n^\star\ge c n^{-2t/(2t+d)}\) for a class constant \(c>0\). At \(h=c_hn^{-2/(2t+d)}\), every \(P_\omega\) belongs to \(\mathcal M_0\), every \(Q_\omega\) belongs to \(\mathcal M_1(cn^{-2t/(2t+d)};s,R)\), the one-record mixtures coincide exactly, and the \(n\)-record mixtures have affinity bounded away from zero. Hence \(\delta_n^\star/\delta_{n,e}^\star\to\infty\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose dyadic \(h_n\) within a fixed factor of \(c_hn^{-2/(2t+d)}\). Since \(t<d/2\), \(K_{h_n}\gtrsim n\), so the sparse-occupancy premise holds under uniform design. The legality lemma shows that every component satisfies all fixed model conditions for large \(n\), including the covariance profile and, for \(0<s\le1/2\), the source constraint. The cell lemma gives
\[
P_\omega\in\mathcal M_0,\qquad
\|\Psi(Q_\omega)\|
\ge ca_{h_n}b_{h_n}
=cc_\circ^2h_n^t
\ge c'n^{-2t/(2t+d)}. \tag{10}
\]
The source norm is \(O(a_{h_n}^2b_{h_n}^2)=o(1)\), hence is below \(R^2\); every \(Q_\omega\) is in the separated alternative.

The mixture bound gives
\[
H^2(\overline Q_{h_n}^{(n)},\overline P_{h_n}^{(n)})
\le Cn^2h_n^{2t+d}=Cc_h^{2t+d}. \tag{11}
\]
Choose \(c_h\) so the right side makes
\(\operatorname{TV}(\overline Q_{h_n}^{(n)},\overline P_{h_n}^{(n)})<1-\alpha-\beta\), using \(\operatorname{TV}\le\sqrt{2H^2}\). If a test had the required errors for every component, averaging over \(\omega\) would give
\[
1-\alpha-\beta
\le E_{\overline Q_{h_n}^{(n)}}\varphi_n
 -E_{\overline P_{h_n}^{(n)}}\varphi_n
\le\operatorname{TV}(\overline Q_{h_n}^{(n)},\overline P_{h_n}^{(n)}),
\]
a contradiction. Hence
\[
\delta_n^\star\ge c'n^{-2t/(2t+d)}.
\]
The explicit constant-contrast path used in the oracle proof is admitted here: uniform design and \(e=1/2\) obey the design, overlap, and propensity conditions; positive interior slack admits its sufficiently small constant arm perturbations; its covariance differs from the baseline Fourier covariance only in the positive constant eigenvalue; and its source norm tends to zero for every fixed \(s\). Thus the narrowed oracle theorem gives \(\delta_{n,e}^\star\asymp n^{-1/2}\). Since
\[
\frac12-\frac{2t}{2t+d}=\frac{d-2t}{2(2t+d)}>0,
\]
the ratio diverges.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is an all-tests information bound, so it survives arbitrary higher-order correction and establishes a genuine nuisance cost. \textit{Gap.} Kennedy et al. provide a scalar CATE fuzzy bound; the exact Hilbert null, embedded signal, and covariance-source constraint are new here. \textit{Consumer.} It tells analysts when no regularization or nuisance learner can restore oracle sensitivity in observational conditional-distribution testing.

\begin{theorem}[thm:high-smoothness-density-robustness]\label{thm:high-smoothness-density-robustness}
If \(t>d/2\), then over every law in \(\mathcal M\) and every design density satisfying \(c_X\le f_X\le C_X\), a finite-order cross-fitted empirical higher-order SCoDiTE test has uniform type-I error at most \(\alpha+o(1)\) and uniform type-II error at most \(\beta+o(1)\) whenever \(\|\Psi(P)\|_{\mathcal H}\ge Cn^{-1/2}\). Thus \(\delta_n^\star\le Cn^{-1/2}\); this upper bound uses only the declared model assumptions, its correction has \(M=o(n)\), and it imposes no smoothness on \(f_X\). For the reverse inequality only, impose the class-richness premise that \(\mathcal M\) contains a path \((P_\theta)_{|\theta|\le\varepsilon_0}\) supported on \(\{y_-,y_+\}\), with \(P_0\in\mathcal M_0\), fixed \(P_X\) and \(e\), constant contrast \(U_{P_\theta}(x)=\theta b_Y\), nonzero derivative \(w_0=E_{P_0}[K_X]\otimes b_Y\), quadratic-mean differentiability at zero with Fisher information strictly between zero and infinity, and \(\sup_{|\theta|\le\varepsilon_0}\sum_{j\ge1}\mu_j(P_\theta)^{-2s}|\langle w_0,v_j(P_\theta)\rangle_{\mathcal H}|^2<\infty\). Under this premise, the local two-point reduction gives \(\delta_n^\star\ge cn^{-1/2}\), and hence \(\delta_n^\star\asymp n^{-1/2}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write
\[
 \kappa_X^2=\sup_{x\in\mathbb T^d}k(x,x)<\infty,
 \qquad
 \kappa_Y^2=\sup_{y\in\mathcal Y}\ell(y,y)<\infty.
 \tag{1}
\]
The two inequalities are the bounded-covariate-kernel and bounded-outcome-kernel assumptions.  The reproducing property gives
\[
 \|K_x\|_{\mathcal H_X}\leq\kappa_X,
 \qquad
 \|L_y\|_{\mathcal H_Y}\leq\kappa_Y.
 \tag{2}
\]
Thus all Hilbert-valued variables used below have finite moments of every fixed order needed in the construction.

We first establish the upper bound.  For \(a\in\{0,1\}\), define
\[
 D_a=\mathbf 1\{A=a\},
 \qquad
 \pi_a(x)=P(D_a=1\mid X=x),
 \qquad
 p_a(x)=\pi_a(x)^{-1},
 \qquad
 \mu_a(dx)=\pi_a(x)P_X(dx).
 \tag{3}
\]
Here \(\pi_1=e\) and \(\pi_0=1-e\).  The overlap and density-band assumptions imply
\[
 \eta\leq\pi_a(x)\leq1-\eta,
 \qquad
 \eta c_X\leq\frac{d\mu_a}{dx}(x)\leq(1-\eta)C_X
 \quad\text{for almost every }x.
 \tag{4}
\]
Since inversion is smooth on \([\eta,1-\eta]\), the propensity Hölder assumption implies
\[
 p_a\in\mathcal H^{\beta_e}(C_p),
 \tag{5}
\]
where \(C_p<\infty\) depends only on \(\eta,\beta_e,L_e\).  Indeed, differentiating \(\pi_ap_a=1\) recursively through order \(\lfloor\beta_e\rfloor\) expresses each derivative of \(p_a\) as a finite sum of products of derivatives of \(\pi_a\) divided by a power of \(\pi_a\); (4) bounds every denominator, and the highest-order Hölder difference is bounded by adding and subtracting these products.  No property of \(f_X\) beyond (4) is used.

Let \(\mathcal S_M\) be the local polynomial span in the new high-smoothness construction.  Cellwise Taylor expansion of degree \(p\), with its Hölder remainder, supplies \(s_{p,a}\in\mathcal S_M\) and \(s_{\nu,a}\in\mathcal S_M\otimes\mathcal H_Y\) such that
\[
 \|p_a-s_{p,a}\|_{L^\infty(dx)}
 \leq C M^{-\beta_e/d},
 \qquad
 \|\nu_a-s_{\nu,a}\|_{L^\infty(dx;\mathcal H_Y)}
 \leq C M^{-\beta_m/d}.
 \tag{6}
\]
For the Hilbert-valued expansion, the usual integral Taylor identity is a Bochner integral; its norm is bounded by the integral of the Hölder remainder.  Let \(\Pi_{a,M}\) be orthogonal projection onto \(\mathcal S_M\) in \(L^2(\mu_a)\), acting componentwise on \(\mathcal H_Y\)-valued functions.  Its best-approximation property, (4), (5), (6), and the outcome Hölder assumption give
\[
 \|(I-\Pi_{a,M})(1-p_a)\|_{L^2(\mu_a)}
 \leq C M^{-\beta_e/d},
 \qquad
 \|(I-\Pi_{a,M})\nu_a\|_{L^2(\mu_a;\mathcal H_Y)}
 \leq C M^{-\beta_m/d}.
 \tag{7}
\]

Put
\[
 \Omega_a=\int z_M(x)z_M(x)^\top\,d\mu_a(x).
 \tag{8}
\]
The coordinates of \(z_M\) are orthonormal in \(L^2(dx)\), so (4) yields
\[
 \eta c_X I_M\preceq\Omega_a
 \preceq(1-\eta)C_XI_M.
 \tag{9}
\]
Only the fixed number \((p+1)^d\) of coordinates attached to the cell containing \(x\) can be nonzero.  Rescaling the fixed reference-cell Legendre basis therefore gives
\[
 \sup_x\|z_M(x)\|_2^2\leq C_BM,
 \tag{10}
\]
where \(C_B\) depends only on \(d\) and \(p\).  Notice that the constant function belongs to the span; no global basis vector was asserted to be cell-supported.

We next derive the empirical-Gram control.  Conditional on a Gram half of size \(N\asymp n\), set
\[
 \Delta_a=\Omega_a-\widehat\Omega_{a,G}.
\]
Both matrices are block diagonal, with one block of fixed dimension \((p+1)^d\) per cell.  A centered entry of one empirical block is bounded in absolute value by \(CM\), has variance at most \(CM\), and can be nonzero before centering only when \(X\) lies in that cell.  For \(|u|<3\), the power series for the exponential gives
\[
 e^u\leq1+u+\frac{u^2}{2(1-|u|/3)}.
 \tag{11}
\]
Applying (11) to each centered summand and multiplying moment-generating functions by independence proves the scalar Bernstein inequality
\[
 P\{|\widehat\Omega_{a,G}^{uv}-\Omega_a^{uv}|>x\}
 \leq2\exp\left\{-\frac{cNx^2}{M+Mx}\right\}.
 \tag{12}
\]
There are at most \(CM\) nonzero within-block entries.  A union bound, equivalence of entrywise and operator norms on each fixed-dimensional block, and integration of (12) show that, for every fixed integer \(j\geq1\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M}P_P(\mathcal E_{a,G}^c)
 \leq CM\exp(-cN/M),
 \tag{13}
\]
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M}
 E_P\!\left[
  \mathbf 1_{\mathcal E_{a,G}}
  \|\Delta_a\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{2j}
 \right]
 \leq C_j\left(\frac{M\log n}{N}\right)^j.
 \tag{14}
\]
All constants are uniform over the density band.  In particular, no derivative or estimator of \(f_X\) occurs.

We now prove the exact product-residual identity.  On \(\mathcal E_{a,G}\), define
\[
 \mathcal A_{a,q}
 =\sum_{\ell=0}^{q-1}(-1)^\ell
 \widehat\Omega_{a,G}^{-1}
 (\Delta_a\widehat\Omega_{a,G}^{-1})^\ell,
 \qquad
 Q_{a,q}f
 =z_M^\top\mathcal A_{a,q}\int z_Mf\,d\mu_a.
 \tag{15}
\]
The sampling assumption makes the estimation half independent of the Gram half.  From (3), conditioning first on \(X\) gives
\[
 E[z_MR_a^p]
 =\int z_M(1-p_a)\,d\mu_a,
 \qquad
 E[z_M\otimes R_a^\nu]
 =\int z_M\otimes\nu_a\,d\mu_a,
 \tag{16}
\]
\[
 E[D_ah(X)]=\int h\,d\mu_a,
 \qquad
 E[G_{a,i}\mid G]=\Delta_a.
 \tag{17}
\]
All indices in each ordered \(U\)-statistic are distinct.  Repeated use of independence in (16)--(17), followed by summation over \(\ell\) and \(r\), therefore gives
\[
 \sum_{\ell=0}^{q-1}(-1)^\ell
 E[C^{(1)}_{a,\ell}\mid G]
 =\int K_x\otimes\nu_a(x)\,
        Q_{a,q}(1-p_a)(x)\,d\mu_a(x),
 \tag{18}
\]
\[
 \sum_{\ell=0}^{q-1}(-1)^\ell
 E[C^{(2)}_{a,\ell}\mid G]
 =\int K_x\otimes Q_{a,q}\nu_a(x)\,
        (1-p_a(x))\,d\mu_a(x),
 \tag{19}
\]
and the common anchor in \(C^{(3)}\) yields
\[
 \sum_{\ell,r=0}^{q-1}(-1)^{\ell+r}
 E[C^{(3)}_{a,\ell,r}\mid G]
 =\int K_x\otimes Q_{a,q}\nu_a(x)\,
        Q_{a,q}(1-p_a)(x)\,d\mu_a(x).
 \tag{20}
\]
On the other hand, the definitions of \(p_a\), \(\mu_a\), and \(\nu_a\) imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &E[D_aK_X\otimes L_Y]
   -E[K_X\otimes\nu_a(X)]\\
 &\qquad
 =\int K_x\otimes\nu_a(x)\,d\mu_a(x)
  -\int K_x\otimes p_a(x)\nu_a(x)\,d\mu_a(x)\\
 &\qquad
 =\int K_x\otimes(1-p_a(x))\nu_a(x)\,d\mu_a(x).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{21}
\]
Substituting (18)--(21) into the definition of the correction proves, without approximation,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &E[\widehat\theta_a^{I\mid G}\mid G]
   -E[K_X\otimes\nu_a(X)]\\
 &\qquad
 =\int K_x\otimes
   \{(I-Q_{a,q})(1-p_a)\}(x)
   \{(I-Q_{a,q})\nu_a\}(x)\,d\mu_a(x).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{22}
\]
The two one-sided terms remove the two projected cross-products, and the common-anchor term restores their intersection.  Hence \(K_x\) is evaluated exactly at the anchor, and (22) requires no smoothness of \(x\mapsto K_x\).

Because
\(
 \Omega_a=\widehat\Omega_{a,G}+\Delta_a
\), the finite geometric-series identity and (9) imply, on \(\mathcal E_{a,G}\),
\[
 \|\mathcal A_{a,q}-\Omega_a^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \leq C_q\|\Delta_a\|_{mathrm{op}}^q.
 \tag{23}
\]
For scalar or Hilbert-valued \(f\), equations (8), (9), (15), and Cauchy--Schwarz consequently give
\[
 \|(Q_{a,q}-\Pi_{a,M})f\|_{L^2(\mu_a)}
 \leq C_q\|\Delta_a\|_{mathrm{op}}^q
       \|f\|_{L^2(\mu_a)}.
 \tag{24}
\]
The overlap bound and (2) uniformly bound the norms of \(1-p_a\) and \(\nu_a\).  Combining (7), (22), (24), \(\|K_x\|\leq\kappa_X\), and Cauchy--Schwarz in \(L^2(\mu_a)\) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\left\|E[\widehat\theta_a^{I\mid G}\mid G]
       -E[K_X\otimes\nu_a(X)]\right\|_{\mathcal H}\\
 &\qquad\leq
 C\{M^{-\beta_e/d}+\|\Delta_a\|_{mathrm{op}}^q\}
  \{M^{-\beta_m/d}+\|\Delta_a\|_{mathrm{op}}^q\}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{25}
\]

It remains to control fluctuation.  We give the overlap calculation because the statistic is Hilbert-valued.  For any deterministic matrix \(B\), (8)--(9) and cyclicity of trace give
\[
 \iint|z_M(x)^\top Bz_M(x')|^2
       \,d\mu_a(x)d\mu_a(x')
 =\operatorname{tr}(B\Omega_aB^\top\Omega_a)
 \leq CM\|B\|_{mathrm{op}}^2.
 \tag{26}
\]
Consider two copies of an order-\(j\) kernel appearing in the construction.  Conditional on \(G\), copies with disjoint estimation indices are independent and have zero covariance after centering.  If exactly \(c\geq1\) indices are shared, integrate the nonshared endpoints successively using (8); use (26) at every shared link.  The residual factors are bounded by one or by \(\kappa_Y\), the inverse matrices are bounded by \(2/(\eta c_X)\) on the stabilization event, and the fixed chain length is at most \(2q+1\).  Induction over the links therefore bounds the absolute Hilbert covariance by
\[
 C_qM^{c-1}.
 \tag{27}
\]
The proportion of pairs of ordered index tuples sharing exactly \(c\) indices is at most \(C_qN^{-c}\).  Summing (27) over overlap patterns yields
\[
 E\!\left[
  \left\|\widehat\theta_a^{I\mid G}
    -E(\widehat\theta_a^{I\mid G}\mid G)\right\|_{\mathcal H}^2
  \mathbf 1_{\mathcal E_{0,G}\cap\mathcal E_{1,G}}
 \right]
 \leq\frac{C_q}{N}
       \sum_{c=1}^{2q+1}\left(\frac{M}{N}\right)^{c-1}.
 \tag{28}
\]
For Hilbert-valued endpoints, expanding the squared norm replaces products of scalar responses by their Hilbert inner products, whose absolute values are bounded by (2); hence (27)--(28) are norm bounds, not coordinatewise claims.

The deterministic choices in the construction imply
\[
 M^{-2t/d}=o(n^{-1}),
 \qquad
 \left(\frac{M\log n}{n}\right)^q=o(n^{-1}),
 \qquad
 \frac{M}{n}=o(1).
 \tag{29}
\]
Indeed, the first relation follows from \(2\gamma t/d>1\), the second from \(q(1-\gamma)>1\), and the third from \(\gamma<1\).  Squaring (25), applying (14) with \(j=q\) and \(j=2q\), and using (29) proves that the squared conditional bias is \(o(n^{-1})\), uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal M\).  Equations (28)--(29) make the conditional variance at most \(C/n\).  If stabilization fails, the directed contrast is zero, whereas
\[
 \|\Psi(P)\|_{\mathcal H}
 \leq E\|K_X\|\,\|\nu_1(X)-\nu_0(X)\|
 \leq2\kappa_X\kappa_Y.
 \tag{30}
\]
The failure probability in (13) is \(o(n^{-1})\) by \(M\asymp n^\gamma\).  Jensen's inequality for the average of the two directed estimators, (13), and (25)--(30) thus establish
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M}
 E_P\|\widehat\Psi_n^{\mathrm{ho}}-\Psi(P)\|_{\mathcal H}^2
 \leq\frac{C}{n}
 \quad\text{for all sufficiently large }n.
 \tag{31}
\]
For each of the finitely many smaller \(n\), the estimator was set to zero.  Its risk is bounded by (30).  Thus
\[
 \sup_{m\geq1}\sup_{P\in\mathcal M}
 mE_P\|\widehat\Psi_m^{\mathrm{ho}}-\Psi(P)\|_{\mathcal H}^2<\infty,
 \tag{32}
\]
and the deterministic class-oracle envelope \(V_\star\) in the construction is finite.  This proves finiteness; it does not assert that \(V_\star\) is a data-derived or computable threshold.

If \(P\in\mathcal M_0\), then \(\Psi(P)=0\) by the null-class definition.  Markov's inequality applied to the squared norm and the definition of \(V_\star\) give
\[
 E_P\varphi_n^{\mathrm{ho}}
 \leq
 \frac{nE_P\|\widehat\Psi_n^{\mathrm{ho}}-\Psi(P)\|_{\mathcal H}^2}
      {V_\star/\alpha}
 <\alpha.
 \tag{33}
\]
If
\[
 \|\Psi(P)\|_{\mathcal H}
 \geq C_{\alpha,\beta}n^{-1/2},
 \qquad
 C_{\alpha,\beta}
 =\sqrt{V_\star}\{\alpha^{-1/2}+\beta^{-1/2}\},
 \tag{34}
\]
then nonrejection and the reverse triangle inequality imply
\[
 \|\widehat\Psi_n^{\mathrm{ho}}-\Psi(P)\|_{\mathcal H}
 \geq\sqrt{\frac{V_\star}{\beta n}}.
\]
A second application of Markov's inequality yields
\[
 E_P(1-\varphi_n^{\mathrm{ho}})<\beta.
 \tag{35}
\]
These finite-sample inequalities are stronger than the asserted asymptotic bounds and hold uniformly over the source-constrained alternatives because the calculation used only \(P\in\mathcal M\).  The minimax-radius definition therefore gives
\[
 \delta_n^\star\leq C_{\alpha,\beta}n^{-1/2}.
 \tag{36}
\]

We finally prove the conditional reverse inequality.  Impose the class-richness premise in the theorem.  The fixed \(P_X\) and constant contrast give, directly from the definition of \(\Psi\),
\[
 \Psi(P_\theta)
 =E_{P_\theta}[K_X\otimes U_{P_\theta}(X)]
 =\theta E_{P_0}[K_X]\otimes b_Y
 =\theta w_0.
 \tag{37}
\]
Let
\[
 B_s=\sup_{|\theta|\leq\varepsilon_0}
 \sum_{j\geq1}\mu_j(P_\theta)^{-2s}
 |\langle w_0,v_j(P_\theta)\rangle_{\mathcal H}|^2<\infty.
 \tag{38}
\]
For \(\theta_n=c_0n^{-1/2}\), equations (37)--(38) imply
\[
 \sum_{j\geq1}\mu_j(P_{\theta_n})^{-2s}
 |\langle\Psi(P_{\theta_n}),v_j(P_{\theta_n})\rangle_{\mathcal H}|^2
 \leq\frac{c_0^2B_s}{n}\leq R^2
 \tag{39}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\).  Since the path lies in \(\mathcal M\), \(w_0\ne0\), and \(|\theta_n|\leq\varepsilon_0\) eventually, the alternative-class definition yields
\[
 P_{\theta_n}\in\mathcal M_1(\delta_n;s,R),
 \qquad
 \delta_n=c_0\|w_0\|_{\mathcal H}n^{-1/2}.
 \tag{40}
\]

Quadratic-mean differentiability supplies a score \(\dot\ell\in L^2_0(P_0)\), with \(I=E_{P_0}\dot\ell^2\in(0,\infty)\), such that
\[
 \int\left(
  \sqrt{dP_\theta}-\sqrt{dP_0}
  -\frac{\theta}{2}\dot\ell\sqrt{dP_0}
 \right)^2=o(\theta^2).
 \tag{41}
\]
Consequently, for \(H^2(P,Q)=\int(\sqrt{dP}-\sqrt{dQ})^2\),
\[
 H^2(P_\theta,P_0)=\frac{I\theta^2}{4}+o(\theta^2).
 \tag{42}
\]
If (41) is understood for the full-data law, its observed-data marginal satisfies the same upper bound because conditional Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\(
 (\sqrt{\int p\,dz}-\sqrt{\int q\,dz})^2
 \leq\int(\sqrt p-\sqrt q)^2\,dz
\); integrating over the observed record proves Hellinger contraction under marginalization.  Thus it suffices to continue with the induced observed laws, denoted by the same symbols.

The product-affinity identity and \(1-(1-x)^n\leq nx\) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 H^2(P_{\theta_n}^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
 &=2\left[1-\left\{1-\frac12H^2(P_{\theta_n},P_0)\right\}^{n}\right]\\
 &\leq nH^2(P_{\theta_n},P_0).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{43}
\]
Also, Cauchy--Schwarz applied to
\(
 |p-q|=|\sqrt p-\sqrt q|(\sqrt p+\sqrt q)
\) yields
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(P,Q)=\frac12\int|p-q|\leq H(P,Q).
 \tag{44}
\]
Equations (42)--(44) bound the total variation distance between the two observed product laws by \(C_Ic_0+o(1)\).  Since \(\beta<1-\alpha\), choose \(c_0>0\) so small that this bound is less than \(1-\alpha-\beta\) for all large \(n\).  For every randomized test \(\varphi_n\) satisfying \(E_{P_0}\varphi_n\leq\alpha\), the variational definition of total variation gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_{P_{\theta_n}}(1-\varphi_n)
 &\geq1-E_{P_0}\varphi_n
  -\operatorname{TV}(P_{\theta_n}^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})\\
 &>\beta.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{45}
\]
Thus no level-\(\alpha\) test controls type-II error by \(\beta\) on \(\mathcal M_1(\delta_n;s,R)\).  By monotonicity of the separated alternative classes and the definition of \(\delta_n^\star\), (40) and (45) imply
\[
 \delta_n^\star\geq c_0\|w_0\|_{\mathcal H}n^{-1/2}.
 \tag{46}
\]
Combining (36) and (46) proves \(\delta_n^\star\asymp n^{-1/2}\) under the stated class-richness premise.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem now has a separate explicit estimator, an exact product-residual identity, uniform \(O(n^{-1})\) Hilbert risk, finite oracle calibration, and a conditional local-path converse. \textit{Gap.} Scalar empirical higher-order efficiency theorems do not establish the common-anchor Hilbert correction or this uniform testing guarantee. \textit{Consumer.} Above the smoothness elbow, the 401(k) analysis has a mathematically size-valid oracle threshold; practical estimation of that threshold and the rough-design frontier remain separate tasks.

\begin{openendedquestion}[oeq:arbitrary-density-frontier]\label{oeq:arbitrary-density-frontier}
Flagship question: for fixed \(r>1/2\), \(s>0\), and \(0<t\le d/2\), prove or refute the candidate equivalence \(\delta_n^\star\asymp\rho_n^{\mathrm{occ}}\) uniformly over \(c_X\le f_X\le C_X\), where \(\rho_n^{\mathrm{occ}}\) is defined from the primitive cell probabilities, pair-occupancy probabilities, and Gram-inflation factor in \(\mathrm{def{:}occupancy\mbox{-}gram\mbox{-}candidate}\). The lower-bound route must use the explicit nonfactorizing \(q_\omega\) table and shared-sign family \(\{P_\omega,Q_\omega:\omega\in\{-1,1\}^{K_h}\}\) from \(\mathrm{def{:}fuzzy\mbox{-}mixtures}\) at the optimizing scale. Its named obligations are to preserve the exact \(U_{P_\omega}=0\) SCoDiTE null, the nonzero contrast \(U_{Q_\omega}=2u_\omega v_\omega b_Y/(1-u_\omega^2)\), the covariance sandwich and \(0<s\le1/2\) source bound from \(\mathrm{lem{:}fuzzy\mbox{-}legality\mbox{-}affinity}\), and bounded \(n\)-record mixture affinity at the scale selected by the nonuniform cell probabilities. The upper-bound route must either attain this candidate by collision and empirical-Gram calibration or replace it with a rigorously derived primitive occupancy--Gram functional and match that radius. For \(s>1/2\), determine whether covariance-source membership eliminates this fuzzy branch or yields an explicit \((r,s)\)-dependent frontier.
\end{openendedquestion}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This flagship claim turns the rough arbitrary-density bottleneck into a falsifiable rate formula and exposes exact-null legality and covariance-source membership as independent proof obligations. \textit{Gap.} Liu et al. prove their empirical-HOIF efficiency result for scalar missing-at-random and doubly robust functionals with \(k=o(n)\); scales with \(K_h>n\) are therefore not subsumed, but that fact alone neither proves an obstruction nor selects the proposed occupancy factor. No SCoDiTE result supplies the missing lower and upper match. \textit{Consumer.} The matched frontier determines the size-valid detection threshold for the 401(k) conditional wealth-distribution analysis and tells the analyst whether cell occupancy, rather than a heuristic whitening rule, limits sensitivity.

\begin{openendedquestion}[oeq:spectral-iff]\label{oeq:spectral-iff}
Conditional on the radius delivered by \(\mathrm{oeq{:}arbitrary\mbox{-}density\mbox{-}frontier}\), derive a necessary-and-sufficient spectral criterion for \(T_n^{\mathrm{MMD}}\) and for some \(T_{n,\lambda_n}^{\mathrm{Wald}}\) to attain \(\delta_n^\star\) at each covariance exponent \(r\) and source exponent \(s\). The criterion must be an explicit eigenwise extremal ratio incorporating source-constrained signal, null effective dimension, higher-order nuisance remainder, and empirical-operator error; failure in either named statistic must be witnessed by a legal sequence in \(\mathcal M_1\).
\end{openendedquestion}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} A method comparison is meaningful only after the all-tests modulus and nuisance amplification are in the same sequence experiment. \textit{Gap.} Hagrass et al. classify direct two-sample filters under a different loss, while Jain and Luedtke analyze fixed regularization without an iff minimax criterion. \textit{Consumer.} The criterion tells the 401(k) analyst when covariance whitening improves local power, when it only changes constants, and when it loses to identity weighting.

\begin{openendedquestion}[oeq:adaptive-grid]\label{oeq:adaptive-grid}
Construct \(T_n^{\mathrm{grid}}\) over a grid of at most \(C\log n\) regularization--rank pairs with uniform asymptotic level \(\alpha\). Prove an oracle inequality attaining every radius certified by \(\mathrm{oeq{:}spectral\mbox{-}iff}\) up to an explicit logarithmic or iterated-logarithmic factor, and prove by a multi-scale mixture that no jointly adaptive test can remove that factor over the declared compact index ranges.
\end{openendedquestion}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The practical statistic must choose both how much to whiten and how much higher-order correction to apply without invalidating size. \textit{Gap.} Direct MMDAgg and spectral aggregation use exchangeable raw samples; Jain and Luedtke leave data-driven Wald regularization open and calibrate only a fixed operator. \textit{Consumer.} This replaces the heuristic Wald regularizer in the 401(k) analysis by a size-valid, data-adaptive choice.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The high-smoothness test is calibrated by the deterministic envelope \(V_\star\), whose finiteness is proved uniformly but whose numerical value is a class-oracle constant rather than a data-derived critical value. This suffices for the minimax existence and size theorem but is not yet a practical calibration recipe. In the early-kill rough case \(d=1\) and \(\beta_e=\beta_m=0.2\), the nominal uniform-design partition has \(K_h\asymp n^{10/9}>n\), outside both the proved high-smoothness construction and the scalar empirical-HOIF regime. The remaining audit is therefore still the rough arbitrary-density upper procedure and its match to a primitive occupancy functional. The two-outcome family is only a least-favorable proof device; an attaining rough procedure must handle the multivariate wealth outcome.

\section{Honest scope}

The density-robust root-rate upper theorem is proved for \(t>d/2\) by an explicit finite-order Hilbert-valued estimator with \(M=o(n)\). Its matching root-rate lower bound, and hence equality, is conditional on the stated nondegenerate quadratic-mean differentiable constant-contrast path. The calibration envelope is deterministic and finite but oracle. The known-propensity benchmark, corrected rough uniform-design fuzzy lower branch, and Fourier quotient-null result remain separate established or posed components. For \(0<t\le d/2\), the collision and occupancy handle has not been replaced: the arbitrary-density equivalence, the spectral iff classification, and the adaptive regularization grid remain open. No claim is made that \(K_h>n\) itself proves an occupancy penalty or that characteristic constituent kernels make the smoothed null identical to conditional homogeneity.
