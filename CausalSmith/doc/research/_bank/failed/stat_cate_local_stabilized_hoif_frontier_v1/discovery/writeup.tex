\section*{stat\_\allowbreak{}cate\_\allowbreak{}local\_\allowbreak{}stabilized\_\allowbreak{}hoif\_\allowbreak{}frontier}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} On \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}=\{0<\alpha,\beta,\gamma\leq1\}\), one bounded density-free cross-pair estimator attains the optimizer-defined three-term objective without logarithmic loss.  With \(s=(\alpha+\beta)/2\) and \(s_0=d\gamma/\{2(2\gamma+d)\}\), that objective has order \(n^{-1/\{1+d/(2\gamma)+d/(4s)\}}\) below the elbow and \(n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}\) at and above it.  A uniform-design Bernoulli two-point submodel proves the positive-band lower rate \(n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}\), so the latter is the positive-band minimax rate when \(s\geq s_0\).  Below the elbow the former is an attained upper benchmark, while a matching positive-band converse remains unresolved.  The order-one region includes \(U=\{0<\alpha<\beta<\gamma<1\}\) outside the banked region.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} The algebraic three-term KBRW objective was not accompanied by a proof that the present density-free cross-pair estimator attains it on the fixed positive-band class, nor by a positive-band converse.  The published low-smoothness hard laws use density support holes, so they cannot settle the below-elbow minimax rate on \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band}}\).

\paragraph{Niche.} On the explicitly restricted positive-band class and \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\), close pairs provide the local mass \(h^{2d}/k\) needed for an order-one product remainder.  Overlap supplies a deterministic population denominator margin, while direct overlap-graph counting controls the diagonal and one-shared-index variance terms of the proved order-one pair kernel.

\paragraph{Fill.} The paper proves the explicit screened cross-pair upper bound, directly minimizes its three-term oracle objective with integer tuning, and supplies a uniform-design Bernoulli two-point lower bound inside the positive-band class.  This closes the positive-band minimax rate at and above the elbow and gives an honest upper/lower bracket below it, without density estimation or logarithmic loss.

\section{Related work}

CausalSmith attains the algebraic three-term objective appearing in KBRW on the fixed positive-band class and \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\).  KBRW Theorem 2 is instead a high-level upper theorem requiring trained nuisance and covariate-law estimators, bounded Gram eigenvalues, basis approximation, and a uniform density-ratio condition, so its full hypothesis package and the present primitive-law package are alternative regimes rather than nested theorem classes.  At the law level \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band}}\) is a restricted positive-density submodel of KBRW's broad smooth model where the parametrizations overlap, but KBRW's low-smoothness hard densities have support holes; hence that converse does not transfer.  The standard uniform-design regression submodel does transfer the oracle lower bound.  Shen, Gao, Witten, and Han (2020) and Dobriban, Mukherjee, Robins, and Wang (2026) motivate close-pair contrasts for noncausal variance problems, but neither source proves the causal numerator/denominator identity used here.  Liu, Mukherjee, Newey, and Robins (2017) and Liu, Li, Gu, and Liu (2026) concern empirical-Gram or stabilized HOIF constructions; no projection, spectral, or M\"obius theorem from those papers is imported.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:iid-sampling]\label{ass:iid-sampling}
\(O_{1:n}\sim P_O^{\otimes n}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Independent and identically distributed sampling, \cite{kennedy2024minimax}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:consistency]\label{ass:consistency}
\(Y=A Y(1)+(1-A)Y(0)\) almost surely under \(P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Potential-outcome consistency, \cite{kennedy2024minimax}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:conditional-exchangeability]\label{ass:conditional-exchangeability}
\((Y(0),Y(1))\perp A\mid X\) under \(P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Conditional exchangeability, \cite{kennedy2024minimax}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:density-band]\label{ass:density-band}
\(f_{-}\leq f_P(x)\leq f_{+}\) for Lebesgue-almost every \(x\in[0,1]^d\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Two-sided bounded random-design density, \cite{liu2017semiparametric}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:overlap]\label{ass:overlap}
\(\varepsilon\leq\pi_P(x)\leq1-\varepsilon\) for every \(x\in[0,1]^d\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform overlap, \cite{kennedy2024minimax}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:bounded-outcomes]\label{ass:bounded-outcomes}
\(\max\{|Y(0)|,|Y(1)|\}\leq L\) almost surely under \(P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniformly bounded potential outcomes, \cite{kennedy2024minimax}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:holder-propensity]\label{ass:holder-propensity}
\(\pi_P\in\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}_d(L)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (H\"older-smooth propensity score, \cite{kennedy2024minimax}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:holder-control-regression]\label{ass:holder-control-regression}
\(\mu_{0,P}\in\mathcal{H}^{\beta}_d(L)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (H\"older-smooth control regression, \cite{kennedy2024minimax}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:holder-cate]\label{ass:holder-cate}
\(\tau_P\in\mathcal{H}^{\gamma}_d(L)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (H\"older-smooth conditional average treatment effect, \cite{kennedy2024minimax}).
\end{assumption}

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathfrak\{H\}\allowbreak{}\_\allowbreak{}\{P\}\allowbreak{}}

It is the order-one signed pair contrast \((1,-1)\) at the single resolution \(\delta=h_n^*(k_n^*)^{-1/d}\).  The label \(P_n\) records only the deterministic optimizer branch on which the associated theorem is invoked.  Empty-pair and denominator-screen failures use the bounded fallback already specified in the common estimator; no empirical Gram matrix or density estimator occurs.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}optimizer}; \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}regime-\allowbreak{}partition}; \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}pair-\allowbreak{}regions}; \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}cross-\allowbreak{}pair-\allowbreak{}estimator}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:cross-pair-estimator]\label{def:cross-pair-estimator}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}tau\}\allowbreak{}\textasciicircum{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathrm\{pair\}\allowbreak{}\}\allowbreak{}\_\allowbreak{}n(x\_\allowbreak{}0)}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Set \(h=h_n^*\), \(k=k_n^*\), and
\[
 B_h=\prod_{\ell=1}^d\bigl([x_{0\ell}-h,x_{0\ell}+h]\cap[0,1]\bigr),
 \qquad \delta_{hk}=h k^{-1/d}.
\]
Define
\[
 E_{hk}=\left\{\{i,j\}:1\leq i<j\leq n,\ X_i,X_j\in B_h,
 \ \lVert X_i-X_j\rVert_\infty\leq\delta_{hk}\right\}
\]
and
\[
 \Lambda_{hk}=\binom n2\frac{h^{2d}}{k},
 \qquad a_0=\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)f_{-}^2.
\]
The denominator and numerator are
\[
 D_{hk}=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E_{hk}}(A_i-A_j)^2,
 \qquad
 T_{hk}=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E_{hk}}(A_i-A_j)(Y_i-Y_j).
\]
Set
\[
 \widetilde\tau_{hk}=
 \begin{cases}
  0, & \Lambda_{hk}<1\ \text{or}\ D_{hk}<a_0\Lambda_{hk},\\
  T_{hk}/D_{hk}, & \Lambda_{hk}\geq1\ \text{and}\ D_{hk}\geq a_0\Lambda_{hk},
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 \widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{pair}}(x_0)=
 \operatorname{clip}_{[-2L,2L]}(\widetilde\tau_{hk}).
\]
This is a measurable, everywhere-defined sample map.  It uses no empirical Gram matrix, inverse, nuisance estimate, or density estimate.

\end{definition}

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:cate-identification]\label{lem:cate-identification}
For every \(P\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{band}}\), the continuous versions selected by the H\"older restrictions satisfy \[\tau_P(x_0)=\mu_{1,P}(x_0)-\mu_{0,P}(x_0)=\mathbb{E}_P[Y(1)-Y(0)\mid X=x_0].\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band}}\).  Membership in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band}}\) makes the potential outcomes bounded and therefore integrable, so all conditional expectations below exist.  For \(a\in\{0,1\}\), consistency gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P[Y\mid A=a,X]
 =\mathbb E_P[Y(a)\mid A=a,X]
 \quad P\text{-almost surely on }\{A=a\}.
\]
Conditional exchangeability then gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P[Y(a)\mid A=a,X]
 =\mathbb E_P[Y(a)\mid X]
\]
for \(P_X\)-almost every covariate value at which the arm-\(a\) conditional law is defined.  Uniform overlap makes both arms have positive conditional probability at every covariate value, so the two displayed identities imply
\[
 \mu_{1,P}(x)-\mu_{0,P}(x)
 =\mathbb E_P[Y(1)-Y(0)\mid X=x]
 \quad\text{for }P_X\text{-almost every }x.
\]
By the definition of \(\tau_P\), the left side is \(\tau_P(x)\).  The density-band member property in \(\mathrm{def:band\mbox{-}class}\) implies that \(P_X\) and Lebesgue measure on \([0,1]^d\) have the same null sets: for every measurable \(B\),
\[
 f_-\operatorname{Leb}(B)\le P_X(B)\le f_+\operatorname{Leb}(B).
\]
Consequently the preceding equality also holds Lebesgue-almost everywhere.  The H\"older restrictions make \(\mu_{0,P}\) and \(\tau_P\) continuous.  Hence \(\mu_{0,P}+\tau_P\) is the unique continuous version of \(\mu_{1,P}\): it agrees with \(\mu_{1,P}\) almost everywhere by the definition of \(\tau_P\), and two continuous functions agreeing Lebesgue-almost everywhere on the cube agree everywhere.  Likewise, select the version of the causal conditional contrast \(\mathbb E_P[Y(1)-Y(0)\mid X=\cdot]\) equal to the continuous function \(\tau_P\); the almost-everywhere identity proves that this is a valid version.  Evaluating these selected continuous versions at the interior point \(x_0\) yields
\[
 \tau_P(x_0)=\mu_{1,P}(x_0)-\mu_{0,P}(x_0)
 =\mathbb E_P[Y(1)-Y(0)\mid X=x_0].
\]
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The point target must be a version-invariant causal law functional before its risk can be studied. \textit{Gap.} Kennedy et al. work with the same continuous H\"older CATE; this lemma makes the version choice explicit for the positive-band class. \textit{Consumer.} Every branch risk bound and the final minimax comparison use this identified point target.

\begin{lemma}[lem:optimizer-partition]\label{lem:optimizer-partition}
For every \(n\geq2\), the minimizer \((h_n^*,k_n^*)\) exists, is unique after lexicographic selection, and satisfies \(G_n+P_n=1\) and \(G_nP_n=0\). Moreover, \(r_n^*\) equals the exact infimum of the three-term KBRW objective over \(\mathcal T_n\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(n\ge2\).  For every fixed \(k\in\{1,\ldots,n^2\}\), the map \(h\mapsto\Phi_n(h,k)\) is continuous on the compact interval \([n^{-1/d},1]\).  Since there are only finitely many admissible values of \(k\), \(\Phi_n\) attains its minimum on \(\mathcal T_n\).  Its argmin is a nonempty compact subset of a finite union of compact intervals.  First minimize its \(h\)-coordinate and, among the finitely many points having that coordinate, minimize its integer \(k\)-coordinate.  This produces exactly one lexicographically smallest element, namely \((h_n^*,k_n^*)\) in \(\mathrm{def:optimizer}\).  Therefore
\[
 r_n^*=\Phi_n(h_n^*,k_n^*)
 =\min_{(h,k)\in\mathcal T_n}\Phi_n(h,k)
 =\inf_{(h,k)\in\mathcal T_n}\Phi_n(h,k).
\]
Put \(E_n=\{k_n^*(\log n)^3\le N_n^*\}\).  The two definitions in \(\mathrm{def:regime\mbox{-}partition}\) are \(G_n=\mathbf 1_{E_n}\) and \(P_n=\mathbf 1_{E_n^c}\).  Since \(E_n\) and \(E_n^c\) are disjoint and exhaustive, pointwise
\[
 G_n+P_n=1,\qquad G_nP_n=0.
\]
The weak inequality in \(E_n\) also shows explicitly that the equality boundary belongs to the \(G_n\) branch and is not omitted.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The deterministic split must cover every sample size without an unanalysed tuning boundary. \textit{Gap.} KBRW optimize asymptotic choices; the exact lexicographic optimizer and exhaustive branch selector are new bookkeeping required by a single total estimator. \textit{Consumer.} The unified estimator can switch without data-dependent model selection or uncovered equality cases.

\begin{proposition}[prop:oracle-regression-reduction]\label{prop:oracle-regression-reduction}
If \(\pi_P\) and \(\mu_{0,P}\) are supplied to the local \(R\)-normal equations and the nuisance-product correction is suppressed, then the oracle local-polynomial branch with \(k=1\) satisfies \[\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{band}}}\mathbb{E}_P|\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{or}}(x_0)-\tau_P(x_0)|\leq C\left\{h^\gamma+(nh^d)^{-1/2}\right\}.\] Optimizing \(h\) gives the classical pointwise-regression rate \(n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}\); if both outcome regressions are supplied, the direct plug-in equals \(\tau_P(x_0)\) exactly.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band}}\), and suppose that the true functions \(\pi_P\) and \(\mu_{0,P}\) are supplied.  Define the oracle residual response
\[
 Z_i=\frac{(A_i-\pi_P(X_i))(Y_i-\mu_{0,P}(X_i))}
 {\pi_P(X_i)\{1-\pi_P(X_i)\}}.
\]
Consistency and bounded potential outcomes imply \(|\mu_{a,P}(x)|\le L\) for both arms on their continuous versions.  Overlap therefore gives the uniform bound
\[
 |Z_i|\le B_Z:=\frac{2L}{\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)}.
\]
Conditioning on \(X_i=x\), and using the definitions of \(\pi_P\) and \(\mu_{a,P}\), gives the exact local \(R\)-identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_P[(A_i-\pi_P(x))(Y_i-\mu_{0,P}(x))\mid X_i=x]
 &=\pi_P(x)\{1-\pi_P(x)\}
   \{\mu_{1,P}(x)-\mu_{0,P}(x)\},\\
 \mathbb E_P[Z_i\mid X_i=x]&=\tau_P(x),
\end{aligned}
\]
where the last equality uses \(\mathrm{lem:cate\mbox{-}identification}\).

We now give an everywhere-defined oracle local-polynomial branch.  This also makes precise that \(k=1\) refers to suppression of the nuisance-product projection, not to the fixed-dimensional polynomial used to remove target smoothing bias.  Let
\[
 B_h=\{x\in[0,1]^d:\|x-x_0\|_\infty\le h\},
 \qquad m_\gamma=\lceil\gamma\rceil-1,
\]
let \(\mathcal V_\gamma=\{v\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^d:|v|\le m_\gamma\}\), and let \(b(z)=(z^v)_{v\in\mathcal V_\gamma}\), with the constant monomial first.  Write \(e_0\) for the coordinate vector selecting that monomial and set
\[
 \widehat G_h=\frac1{nh^d}\sum_{i=1}^n
 \mathbf 1\{X_i\in B_h\}b((X_i-x_0)/h)b((X_i-x_0)/h)^\top.
\]
The scaled set \(D_h=(B_h-x_0)/h\) is a product of intervals contained in \([-1,1]\), each of length at least one.  Thus finite-dimensional norm equivalence for polynomials, together with \(f_-\le f_P\le f_+\), gives constants \(0<c_0<C_0<\infty\), depending only on \(d,m_\gamma,f_-,f_+\), such that the population Gram matrix \(G_h=\mathbb E_P\widehat G_h\) obeys
\[
 c_0 I\preceq G_h\preceq C_0 I
 \quad\text{uniformly in }P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band}}
 \text{ and }h\in(0,1].
\]
Indeed, the quadratic form indexed by a coefficient vector \(a\) is an integral of the nonnegative polynomial \((a^\top b)^2\); its infimum over the compact family of such product intervals and over \(\|a\|_2=1\) is positive, because a nonzero polynomial cannot vanish on a set with nonempty interior.

Let
\[
 \mathcal E_h=\{\lambda_{\min}(\widehat G_h)\ge c_0/2,
 \ \lambda_{\max}(\widehat G_h)\le2C_0\}.
\]
On \(\mathcal E_h\), define
\[
 \widetilde\tau_h=e_0^\top\widehat G_h^{-1}
 \frac1{nh^d}\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf1\{X_i\in B_h\}
 b((X_i-x_0)/h)Z_i;
\]
off \(\mathcal E_h\), set \(\widetilde\tau_h=0\), and finally put
\[
 \widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{or}}(x_0)
 =\operatorname{clip}_{[-2L,2L]}(\widetilde\tau_h).
\]
This is a measurable, everywhere-defined local \(R\)-normal-equation estimator and uses no density estimate.

Apply the one-dimensional integral identity successively along the coordinate segments from \(x_0\) to \(X_i\).  Subtracting the resulting polynomial through degree \(m_\gamma\), and using the defining H\"older modulus on the last derivative, supplies a coefficient vector \(\vartheta_h\) whose constant coordinate is \(\tau_P(x_0)\) and such that
\[
 \tau_P(X_i)=b((X_i-x_0)/h)^\top\vartheta_h+R_i,
 \qquad |R_i|\le C_{d,\gamma}Lh^\gamma
 \quad\text{when }X_i\in B_h.
\]
This remains valid when \(\gamma\) is an integer because the definition uses \(m_\gamma=\gamma-1\) and a Lipschitz derivative of order \(m_\gamma\).  Put \(\xi_i=Z_i-\tau_P(X_i)\).  The oracle identity and i.i.d. sampling yield
\[
 \mathbb E_P[\xi_i\mid X_i]=0,
 \qquad |\xi_i|\le B_Z+2L.
\]
On \(\mathcal E_h\), exact polynomial reproduction by the empirical normal equations gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\widetilde\tau_h-\tau_P(x_0)|
 &\le \frac2{c_0}\left\|
 \frac1{nh^d}\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf1\{X_i\in B_h\}b_iR_i
 \right\|_2\\
 &\quad+\frac2{c_0}\left\|
 \frac1{nh^d}\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf1\{X_i\in B_h\}b_i\xi_i
 \right\|_2,
\end{aligned}
\]
where \(b_i=b((X_i-x_0)/h)\).  The basis is uniformly bounded on \([-1,1]^d\).  Moreover, on \(\mathcal E_h\),
\[
 \frac1{nh^d}\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf1\{X_i\in B_h\}
 =e_0^\top\widehat G_he_0\le2C_0.
\]
The remainder term is consequently at most \(C h^\gamma\).  Conditional independence and the conditional mean-zero property of \(\xi_i\) give
\[
 \mathbb E_P\left[
 \mathbf1_{\mathcal E_h}
 \left\|\frac1{nh^d}\sum_{i=1}^n
 \mathbf1\{X_i\in B_h\}b_i\xi_i\right\|_2\right]
 \le \frac{C}{\sqrt{nh^d}}.
\]
For completeness, each entry \(W\) of \(\widehat G_h-G_h\) has mean zero and second moment at most \(C/(nh^d)\).  The pointwise inequality \(\mathbf1\{|W|>t\}\le W^2/t^2\), followed by summation over the fixed number \(|\mathcal V_\gamma|^2\) of entries, therefore implies
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band}}}P(\mathcal E_h^c)
 \le\frac{C}{nh^d}.
\]
Clipping cannot increase error because \(|\tau_P(x_0)|\le2L\), and it bounds the error on \(\mathcal E_h^c\) by \(4L\).  Since \(h\ge n^{-1/d}\) entails \(nh^d\ge1\), combining the preceding bounds proves
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band}}}
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{or}}(x_0)-\tau_P(x_0)|
 \le C\{h^\gamma+(nh^d)^{-1/2}\}.
\]
Taking \(h\) proportional to \(n^{-1/(2\gamma+d)}\) balances the two terms and gives \(n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}\).  Finally, if both true outcome regressions are supplied, define the direct oracle plug-in by
\[
 \widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{plug}}(x_0)
 =\mu_{1,P}(x_0)-\mu_{0,P}(x_0).
\]
The definition of \(\tau_P\), equivalently \(\mathrm{lem:cate\mbox{-}identification}\), makes this equality exact for every sample.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the required sanity reduction to ordinary random-design pointwise regression. \textit{Gap.} The new corrections must disappear rather than create a spurious density or Gram penalty when the nuisances are oracle-known. \textit{Consumer.} Users in randomized trials or oracle nuisance benchmarks recover the familiar local-polynomial rate as a corollary.

\begin{theorem}[thm:rank-feasible-local-hoif]\label{thm:rank-feasible-local-hoif}
For every compact \(K\subset\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\), there are \(C_K<\infty\) and \(n_K\) such that, for every \((\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\in K\), every \(n\geq n_K\) with \(G_n=1\), and the estimator \(\widehat\tau_n^G(x_0)=\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{pair}}(x_0)\) instantiated by \(\mathfrak H_G\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band}}}
 \mathbb E_P\left|\widehat\tau_n^G(x_0)-\tau_P(x_0)\right|
 \leq C_K\left[(h_n^*)^\gamma+
 \left\{\frac{h_n^*}{(k_n^*)^{1/d}}\right\}^{\alpha+\beta}
 +\left(\frac1{N_n^*}\left\{1+\frac{k_n^*}{N_n^*}\right\}\right)^{1/2}\right]
 =C_Kr_n^*.
\]
This everywhere-defined estimator is the clipped explicit local cross-pair ratio.  It uses no empirical Gram matrix, inverse, M\"obius kernel, nuisance estimate, density estimate, or smoothness condition on \(f_P\).  The restriction \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\leq1\) is essential to the asserted first-difference bias bound.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For clarity, the proof starts with the exact causal identity.  With \(p=\pi_P\), \(m=\mu_{0,P}\), and \(t=\tau_P\), conditional independence from \(\mathrm{ass:iid\mbox{-}sampling}\) gives, for \((X_i,X_j)=(x,z)\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P[(A_i-A_j)(Y_i-Y_j)\mid x,z]
 =\{p(x)-p(z)\}\{m(x)-m(z)\}
  +p(x)\{1-p(z)\}t(x)+p(z)\{1-p(x)\}t(z),
\]
and
\[
 \mathbb E_P[(A_i-A_j)^2\mid x,z]
 =p(x)\{1-p(z)\}+p(z)\{1-p(x)\}.
\]
Indeed, these are obtained by substituting
\(\mathbb E(Y\mid X=x)=m(x)+p(x)t(x)\) and
\(\mathbb E(AY\mid X=x)=p(x)\{m(x)+t(x)\}\) into the four-term product expansion.  After subtracting \(\theta=\tau_P(x_0)\) times the second identity, the first-difference H\"older bounds give
\[
 \left|\mathbb E_P[(A_i-A_j)(Y_i-Y_j)-\theta(A_i-A_j)^2\mid x,z]\right|
 \leq C\left\{\delta^{\alpha+\beta}+h^\gamma\right\}
\]
on a close edge.  The full occupancy, bias, denominator-screen, and overlapping-pair variance calculation is proved in \(\mathrm{lem:cross\mbox{-}pair\mbox{-}risk\mbox{-}bound}\); it uses only the assumptions declared here and is valid for every \(n\geq2\), independently of \(G_n\).  By \(\mathrm{def:rank\mbox{-}feasible\mbox{-}handle}\), \(\widehat\tau_n^G(x_0)=\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{pair}}(x_0)\) on \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\).  Applying that lemma and using
\[
 N^{-1/2}+\frac{\sqrt{k}}{N}
 \leq2\left\{\frac1N\left(1+\frac{k}{N}\right)\right\}^{1/2}
\]
proves the displayed \(C_Kr_n^*\) bound, in particular when \(G_n=1\).  The constant in the lemma is uniform on every compact \(K\subset\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\), so one may take \(n_K=2\) and enlarge \(C_K\) if necessary.  The explicit construction contains no Gram matrix, inverse, M\"obius kernel, nuisance estimate, or density estimate.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This theorem now supplies the direct cross-pair benchmark bound on the \(G_n\) label over \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\). \textit{Gap.} The unsupported growing-rank M"obius step is removed; the causal pair identity, occupancy, screen, and collision bounds are all proved directly. \textit{Consumer.} It provides the \(G_n\)-branch inequality used by the unified theorem.

\begin{openendedquestion}[oeq:multiresolution-order-frontier]\label{oeq:multiresolution-order-frontier}
Derive, rather than posit, a finite correction-order map \(q(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,d)\), a finite family of distance resolutions, and signed weights for \(\mathfrak H_P\) whose separate propensity and control-regression vanishing moments leave bias at most \((h_n^*/(k_n^*)^{1/d})^{\alpha+\beta}\) and whose Hoeffding components are at most the variance term in \(r_n^*\), uniformly on every compact \(K\subset(0,\infty)^3\) whenever \(P_n=1\). Determine the smallest such order and exhibit a derived nonempty open set \(U\subset(0,\infty)^3\setminus\mathcal R_{\mathrm{bank}}\) with \(U\cap(\{\beta>\alpha\}\cup\{\alpha>1\})\neq\varnothing\) on which the construction works. The scheme must remain total under empty cells and singular empirical local Grams and must not estimate \(f_P\).
\end{openendedquestion}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The correction order and enlarged asymmetric smoothness frontier are the genuinely open mathematical content of the sparse-local branch. \textit{Gap.} The banked first-difference identity works only on \(0<\beta<\alpha\leq1\); Dobriban et al. remove polynomial trends for homoskedastic variance, not the two unequally smooth causal nuisances in a local \(R\)-ratio. \textit{Consumer.} A solution gives an implementable density-free estimator for sparse local windows, including a region with reversed nuisance ordering or propensity smoothness above one.

\begin{theorem}[thm:rank-stressed-close-pair]\label{thm:rank-stressed-close-pair}
For every compact \(K\subset\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\), there are \(C_K<\infty\) and \(n_K\) such that, for every \((\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\in K\), every \(n\geq n_K\) with \(P_n=1\), and the estimator \(\widehat\tau_n^P(x_0)=\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{pair}}(x_0)\) instantiated by \(\mathfrak H_P\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band}}}
 \mathbb E_P\left|\widehat\tau_n^P(x_0)-\tau_P(x_0)\right|\leq C_Kr_n^*.
\]
The correction has order one, its single resolution is \(\delta=h_n^*(k_n^*)^{-1/d}\), and its signed pair contrast is \((1,-1)\).  The validity region contains the nonempty open set
\[
 U=\{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma):0<\alpha<\beta<\gamma<1\}
 \subset\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\setminus\mathcal R_{\mathrm{bank}}.
\]
Thus the theorem supplies an explicit outside-bank open region, but it does not assert containment of all \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{bank}}\).  The total fallback has no logarithmic loss and uses neither a Gram matrix nor a density estimate or density smoothness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By \(\mathrm{def:rank\mbox{-}stressed\mbox{-}handle}\), on \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\) one has
\(\widehat\tau_n^P(x_0)=\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{pair}}(x_0)\).  The equation-by-equation calculation in \(\mathrm{lem:cross\mbox{-}pair\mbox{-}risk\mbox{-}bound}\) does not use \(G_n\) or \(P_n\); therefore its \(C r_n^*\) conclusion holds in particular on the event-independent tuning branch \(P_n=1\).  Uniformity over every compact \(K\subset\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\) follows from the same constant control.

The construction has correction order one, its sole distance resolution is \(\delta=h k^{-1/d}\), and its signed contrast is \((1,-1)\).  It is total because \(\Lambda_{hk}<1\) and denominator-screen failure both return zero before clipping.  No Gram matrix occurs, so singularity requires no additional case, and no density estimate is used.

Finally,
\[
 U=\{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma):0<\alpha<\beta<\gamma<1\}
\]
is nonempty, as witnessed by \((1/4,1/2,3/4)\), and open.  It is contained in \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\).  Every member of \(U\) violates the defining banked requirement \(\beta<\alpha\), so
\[
 U\subset\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\setminus\mathcal R_{\mathrm{bank}}.
\]
This proves an explicit open outside-bank validity set but does not assert that \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\) contains all of \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{bank}}\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This theorem applies the same total order-one pair estimator on the \(P_n\) label and proves an explicit open validity set outside the banked wedge. \textit{Gap.} It makes no unsupported claim that the corrected region contains every point of \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{bank}}\). \textit{Consumer.} It provides the \(P_n\)-branch inequality and the reversed nuisance-order witness \(U\).

\begin{theorem}[thm:unified-benchmark-attainment]\label{thm:unified-benchmark-attainment}
For every compact \(K\subset\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\), there are \(C_K<\infty\) and \(n_K\) such that, for every \((\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\in K\) and every \(n\geq n_K\), the single total estimator \(\widehat\tau_n^*(x_0)\) satisfies
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band}}}\mathbb E_P\left|\widehat\tau_n^*(x_0)-\tau_P(x_0)\right|
 \leq C_K\inf_{(h,k)\in\mathcal T_n}\left[h^\gamma+
 \left(\frac{h}{k^{1/d}}\right)^{\alpha+\beta}+
 \left\{\frac1{nh^d}\left(1+\frac{k}{nh^d}\right)\right\}^{1/2}\right]
 =C_Kr_n^*.
\]
It switches through the deterministic exhaustive partition \((G_n,P_n)\), has bounded fallbacks on every sample, and both branches instantiate the same explicit clipped cross-pair ratio.  It uses no empirical Gram matrix, M\"obius kernel, nuisance estimate, density estimate, or density smoothness.  This is attainment of the optimizer-defined KBRW objective on the explicitly restricted positive-band class and on \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\), with no claim of class inclusion or validity above first-difference smoothness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a compact \(K\subset\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\).  By \(\mathrm{ass:bounded\mbox{-}outcomes}\) and \(\mathrm{lem:cate\mbox{-}identification}\), \(\theta=\tau_P(x_0)\in[-2L,2L]\).  Projection onto this interval is nonexpansive at \(\theta\): for every real \(z\),
\[
 |\operatorname{clip}_{[-2L,2L]}(z)-\theta|\leq|z-\theta|.
\]
By \(\mathrm{lem:optimizer\mbox{-}partition}\), \(G_n,P_n\in\{0,1\}\), \(G_n+P_n=1\), and \(G_nP_n=0\).  Applying these deterministic identities to \(\mathrm{def:unified\mbox{-}estimator}\) gives, for every \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band}}\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat\tau_n^*(x_0)-\theta|
 \leq G_n\mathbb E_P|\widehat\tau_n^G(x_0)-\theta|
 +P_n\mathbb E_P|\widehat\tau_n^P(x_0)-\theta|.
\]
If \(G_n=1\), \(\mathrm{thm:rank\mbox{-}feasible\mbox{-}local\mbox{-}hoif}\) bounds the only nonzero term by \(C_Kr_n^*\).  If \(P_n=1\), \(\mathrm{thm:rank\mbox{-}stressed\mbox{-}close\mbox{-}pair}\) gives the same bound.  These two cases are exhaustive, so taking the supremum over \(P\) yields
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band}}}
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat\tau_n^*(x_0)-\tau_P(x_0)|\leq C_Kr_n^*.
\]
Finally, \(\mathrm{lem:optimizer\mbox{-}partition}\) identifies \(r_n^*\) with the exact infimum of the displayed three-term objective over \(\mathcal T_n\).  On \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\), both branch handles instantiate the same everywhere-defined clipped cross-pair map from \(\mathrm{def:cross\mbox{-}pair\mbox{-}estimator}\).  Thus the deterministic switch adds neither a transition remainder nor a logarithmic factor, and it uses no estimate or smoothness property of \(f_P\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The deterministic exhaustive switch combines two proved instances of the same total cross-pair estimator without a transition remainder; direct optimizer minimization then converts its guarantee into the explicit elbow rates. \textit{Gap.} The theorem remains restricted to \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\); below the elbow it is an attained upper benchmark rather than a positive-band minimax conclusion. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies the upper half of the positive-band minimax result above the elbow and the upper half of the honest low-smoothness bracket.

\begin{lemma}[lem:cross-pair-risk-bound]\label{lem:cross-pair-risk-bound}
Suppose \(0<\alpha\leq1\), \(0<\beta\leq1\), and \(0<\gamma\leq1\).  For every \(n\geq2\), set \(h=h_n^*\), \(k=k_n^*\), \(\delta=h k^{-1/d}\), and \(N=N_n^*=nh^d\).  The total local cross-pair estimator in \(\mathrm{def:cross\mbox{-}pair\mbox{-}estimator}\) satisfies, for a constant \(C<\infty\) depending only on \(d,L,\varepsilon,f_{-},f_{+}\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band}}}
 \mathbb E_P\left|\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{pair}}(x_0)-\tau_P(x_0)\right|
 \leq C\left\{h^\gamma+\delta^{\alpha+\beta}+N^{-1/2}+\frac{\sqrt{k}}{N}\right\}
 \leq C r_n^*.
\]
The estimator is defined on every sample, including when there is no close pair or the denominator screen fails, and it uses neither an estimate nor a smoothness condition for \(f_P\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band}}\), abbreviate \(p(x)=\pi_P(x)\), \(m(x)=\mu_{0,P}(x)\), \(t(x)=\tau_P(x)\), and put \(\theta=t(x_0)\).  By \(\mathrm{ass:bounded\mbox{-}outcomes}\), \(\mathrm{ass:consistency}\), and \(\mathrm{lem:cate\mbox{-}identification}\), \(|Y|\leq L\) almost surely and \(|\theta|\leq2L\).

We first establish the causal pair identity rather than importing a squared-contrast formula.  Conditional on \(X_i=x\), the definitions of the two outcome regressions give
\[
 \mathbb E_P(Y_i\mid X_i=x)=m(x)+p(x)t(x),
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_P(A_iY_i\mid X_i=x)=p(x)\{m(x)+t(x)\}.
\]
By \(\mathrm{ass:iid\mbox{-}sampling}\), observations \(i\) and \(j\) are conditionally independent given \((X_i,X_j)=(x,z)\).  Expanding the four products and substituting the preceding two displays therefore yields
\[
\begin{split}
 &\mathbb E_P\bigl[(A_i-A_j)(Y_i-Y_j)\mid X_i=x,X_j=z\bigr]\\
 &\quad=p(x)\{m(x)+t(x)\}+p(z)\{m(z)+t(z)\}
       -p(x)\{m(z)+p(z)t(z)\}-p(z)\{m(x)+p(x)t(x)\}\\
 &\quad=\{p(x)-p(z)\}\{m(x)-m(z)\}
       +p(x)\{1-p(z)\}t(x)+p(z)\{1-p(x)\}t(z).
\end{split}
\]
The same conditional independence and \(A^2=A\) give
\[
 \mathbb E_P\bigl[(A_i-A_j)^2\mid X_i=x,X_j=z\bigr]
 =p(x)\{1-p(z)\}+p(z)\{1-p(x)\}.
\]
Consequently the conditional expectation of the centered contrast is
\[
\begin{split}
 b(x,z)
 &:=\mathbb E_P\bigl[(A_i-A_j)(Y_i-Y_j)-\theta(A_i-A_j)^2
       \mid X_i=x,X_j=z\bigr]\\
 &=\{p(x)-p(z)\}\{m(x)-m(z)\}
   +p(x)\{1-p(z)\}\{t(x)-\theta\}
   +p(z)\{1-p(x)\}\{t(z)-\theta\}.
\end{split}
\]
Because each smoothness index is at most one, \(\mathrm{def:holder\mbox{-}ball}\) and the three H\"older assumptions imply the first-difference inequalities
\[
 |p(x)-p(z)|\leq C\lVert x-z\rVert_\infty^\alpha,
 \qquad
 |m(x)-m(z)|\leq C\lVert x-z\rVert_\infty^\beta,
 \qquad
 |t(x)-t(x_0)|\leq C\lVert x-x_0\rVert_\infty^\gamma.
\]
Here and below \(C\) may change from line to line but depends only on \(d,L,\varepsilon,f_{-},f_{+}\).  Thus, whenever \(x,z\in B_h\) and \(\lVert x-z\rVert_\infty\leq\delta\),
\[
 |b(x,z)|\leq C\{\delta^{\alpha+\beta}+h^\gamma\}.
\]
This is precisely the step that uses \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\leq1\); first differences do not justify this display for smoothness orders above one.

We next prove the required occupancy estimates from \(\mathrm{ass:density\mbox{-}band}\).  Every coordinate interval of
\(B_h=\prod_{\ell=1}^d([x_{0\ell}-h,x_{0\ell}+h]\cap[0,1])\)
has length between \(h\) and \(2h\).  Since \(k\geq1\), \(\delta\leq h\).  For every \(x\in B_h\), each coordinate interval of
\(B_h\cap\{z:\lVert z-x\rVert_\infty\leq\delta\}\)
has length at least \(\delta\).  Tonelli's theorem and the two density bounds therefore give, with \(e_{ij}=\{X_i,X_j\in B_h,\ \lVert X_i-X_j\rVert_\infty\leq\delta\}\),
\[
 f_{-}^2h^d\delta^d
 \leq \mathbb P_P(e_{12})
 \leq f_{+}^2(2h)^d(2\delta)^d.
\]
Since \(\delta^d=h^d/k\), this is
\[
 f_{-}^2\frac{h^{2d}}{k}
 \leq \mathbb P_P(e_{12})
 \leq 4^d f_{+}^2\frac{h^{2d}}{k}.
\]
Likewise, conditional integration over the shared point \(X_1\) yields
\[
 \mathbb P_P(e_{12}\cap e_{13})
 \leq f_{+}^3(2h)^d(2\delta)^{2d}
 =2^{3d}f_{+}^3\frac{h^{3d}}{k^2}.
\]

For \(p,q\in[\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]\), the bilinear function
\(p(1-q)+q(1-p)\) attains its minimum over this rectangle at a corner, where its minimum is \(2\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)\).  Hence \(\mathrm{ass:overlap}\) and the lower occupancy bound imply
\[
 \mathbb E_P D_{hk}
 =\binom n2\mathbb E_P\left[\mathbf 1_{e_{12}}(A_1-A_2)^2\right]
 \geq2\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)f_{-}^2\binom n2\frac{h^{2d}}{k}
 =2a_0\Lambda_{hk}.
\]

Put \(S_{hk}=T_{hk}-\theta D_{hk}\) and
\[
 \xi_{ij}=\mathbf 1_{e_{ij}}\bigl\{(A_i-A_j)(Y_i-Y_j)-\theta(A_i-A_j)^2\bigr\}.
\]
Then \(S_{hk}=\sum_{i<j}\xi_{ij}\), \(|\xi_{ij}|\leq4L\mathbf 1_{e_{ij}}\), and the conditional-bias and upper-occupancy bounds give
\[
 |\mathbb E_P S_{hk}|
 \leq\binom n2 C\frac{h^{2d}}{k}\{h^\gamma+\delta^{\alpha+\beta}\}
 =C\Lambda_{hk}\{h^\gamma+\delta^{\alpha+\beta}\}.
\]
Disjoint unordered pairs are independent by \(\mathrm{ass:iid\mbox{-}sampling}\), so their covariances vanish.  The diagonal terms satisfy
\[
 \sum_{i<j}\operatorname{Var}_P(\xi_{ij})
 \leq Cn^2\mathbb P_P(e_{12})
 \leq C\Lambda_{hk}.
\]
There are at most \(Cn^3\) ordered pairs of edges sharing one index.  Boundedness of \(\xi_{ij}\), the shared-edge estimate, and
\(\mathbb P_P(e_{12})^2\leq C h^{4d}/k^2\leq C h^{3d}/k^2\) imply
\[
 \left|\operatorname{Cov}_P(\xi_{12},\xi_{13})\right|
 \leq C\left\{\mathbb P_P(e_{12}\cap e_{13})+\mathbb P_P(e_{12})^2\right\}
 \leq C\frac{h^{3d}}{k^2}.
\]
Since \(N=nh^d\) and
\[
 \Lambda_{hk}=\frac{n(n-1)}{2}\frac{h^{2d}}{k}
 =\frac{n-1}{2n}\frac{N^2}{k},
\]
summing the shared-index terms proves
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_P(S_{hk})
 \leq C\left\{\Lambda_{hk}+n^3\frac{h^{3d}}{k^2}\right\}
 \leq C\left\{\Lambda_{hk}+\frac{\Lambda_{hk}^2}{N}\right\}.
\]
The identical argument with
\(\zeta_{ij}=\mathbf 1_{e_{ij}}(A_i-A_j)^2\), for which \(|\zeta_{ij}|\leq\mathbf 1_{e_{ij}}\), gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_P(D_{hk})
 \leq C\left\{\Lambda_{hk}+\frac{\Lambda_{hk}^2}{N}\right\}.
\]
Thus both the diagonal and shared-index Hoeffding components have been accounted for directly.

Suppose first that \(\Lambda_{hk}\geq1\).  Since \(\mathbb E_P D_{hk}\geq2a_0\Lambda_{hk}\), Chebyshev's inequality gives
\[
 \mathbb P_P(D_{hk}<a_0\Lambda_{hk})
 \leq\frac{\operatorname{Var}_P(D_{hk})}{a_0^2\Lambda_{hk}^2}
 \leq C\left\{N^{-1}+\Lambda_{hk}^{-1}\right\}.
\]
On the successful screen, the untruncated ratio obeys
\(T_{hk}/D_{hk}-\theta=S_{hk}/D_{hk}\), and \(D_{hk}\geq a_0\Lambda_{hk}\).  Projection onto \([-2L,2L]\) cannot increase distance from \(\theta\in[-2L,2L]\), while on screen failure the raw estimate is zero.  Cauchy--Schwarz therefore yields
\[
\begin{split}
 \mathbb E_P\left|\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{pair}}(x_0)-\theta\right|
 &\leq \frac{|\mathbb E_P S_{hk}|+\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}_P(S_{hk})}}{a_0\Lambda_{hk}}
       +2L\,\mathbb P_P(D_{hk}<a_0\Lambda_{hk})\\
 &\leq C\left\{h^\gamma+\delta^{\alpha+\beta}+N^{-1/2}+\Lambda_{hk}^{-1/2}
       +N^{-1}+\Lambda_{hk}^{-1}\right\}\\
 &\leq C\left\{h^\gamma+\delta^{\alpha+\beta}+N^{-1/2}+\frac{\sqrt{k}}{N}\right\}.
\end{split}
\]
The last line uses \(N\geq1\), \(\Lambda_{hk}\geq1\), and the displayed exact relation between \(\Lambda_{hk}\) and \(N^2/k\).

If \(\Lambda_{hk}<1\), the construction returns zero before clipping, so the crude clipped loss is at most \(4L\).  For \(n\geq2\),
\[
 \frac{\sqrt{k}}{N}
 =\left\{\frac{n-1}{2n\Lambda_{hk}}\right\}^{1/2}
 \geq\frac12,
\]
and hence the same risk bound follows after increasing \(C\).  Finally,
\[
 N^{-1/2}+\frac{\sqrt{k}}{N}
 \leq2\left\{\frac1N\left(1+\frac{k}{N}\right)\right\}^{1/2},
\]
while \(\delta^{\alpha+\beta}=(h/k^{1/d})^{\alpha+\beta}\).  The definition of \(r_n^*\) in \(\mathrm{def:optimizer}\) proves the final comparison.  All bounds are uniform over \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band}}\), completing the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:optimizer-elbow-rate]\label{prop:optimizer-elbow-rate}
Put \(s=(\alpha+\beta)/2\) and
\[
 s_0=\frac{d/4}{1+d/(2\gamma)}=\frac{d\gamma}{2(2\gamma+d)}.
\]
For every fixed \((\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\in(0,\infty)^3\), as \(n\to\infty\),
\[
 r_n^*\asymp
 \begin{cases}
 n^{-1/\{1+d/(2\gamma)+d/(4s)\}},&s<s_0,\\
 n^{-1/\{2+d/\gamma\}}=n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)},&s\geq s_0.
 \end{cases}
\]
The constants may be chosen uniformly when the smoothness triple ranges over a compact subset of either displayed regime.  Below the elbow, feasible integer-rounded tuning has
\[
 h\asymp n^{-(1/\gamma)/\{1+d/(2\gamma)+d/(4s)\}},\qquad
 k\asymp n^{\{d/(2s)-d/\gamma\}/\{1+d/(2\gamma)+d/(4s)\}}.
\]
At and above the elbow, feasible tuning has \(h\asymp n^{-1/(2\gamma+d)}\) and \(k\asymp nh^d\).  These equivalences concern the optimizer-defined oracle-rate functional and introduce no logarithmic factor.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write
\[
 D_s=1+\frac{d}{2\gamma}+\frac{d}{4s},\qquad
 \rho_s=D_s^{-1},\qquad q=\frac{1}{2+d/\gamma}.
\]
We first prove lower bounds for every feasible \((h,k)\), rather than merely evaluating candidate tunings.  Let \(N=nh^d\) and \(R=\Phi_n(h,k)\).  By \(\mathrm{def:kbrw\mbox{-}objective}\), each nonnegative summand is at most \(R\), while its last summand is at least both \(N^{-1/2}\) and \(\sqrt{k}/N\).  Thus
\[
 h^\gamma\leq R,\qquad
 \left(hk^{-1/d}\right)^{2s}\leq R,\qquad
 N^{-1/2}\leq R,\qquad
 \frac{\sqrt{k}}{N}\leq R.
\]
The second and fourth inequalities imply, respectively,
\[
 k\geq h^dR^{-d/(2s)},qquad k\leq R^2n^2h^{2d}.
\]
After cancellation of the positive factor \(h^d\), and then use of \(h^d\leq R^{d/\gamma}\), they give
\[
 1\leq n^2h^dR^{2+d/(2s)}
 \leq n^2R^{2+d/(2s)+d/\gamma}.
\]
Consequently \(R\geq n^{-\rho_s}\).  Similarly, \(N^{-1/2}\leq R\) gives \(h^d\geq n^{-1}R^{-2}\); combining this with \(h^d\leq R^{d/\gamma}\) yields
\[
 R\geq n^{-q}.
\]
The equality \(\rho_s=q\) is equivalent to
\[
 \frac{d}{4s}=1+\frac{d}{2\gamma},
\]
which is exactly \(s=s_0\).  Hence \(n^{-\rho_s}\) is the larger lower bound when \(s<s_0\), and \(n^{-q}\) is the larger lower bound when \(s\geq s_0\).  Taking the infimum over \(\mathcal T_n\) proves the required piecewise lower bound for \(r_n^*\), by \(\mathrm{def:optimizer}\).

It remains to give feasible upper bounds, including the integer and boundary restrictions in \(\mathcal T_n\).  Suppose first that \(s<s_0\), and set
\[
 a=\frac{\rho_s}{\gamma},\qquad
 b=\rho_s\left(\frac{d}{2s}-\frac{d}{\gamma}\right),qquad
 h=n^{-a},\qquad k=\left\lceil n^b\right\rceil.
\]
Because \(s_0<\gamma/2\), one has \(b>0\).  Moreover, \(s<s_0\) implies \(D_s>2+d/\gamma\), so \(0<a<1/d\), and direct subtraction gives
\[
 2D_s-\left(\frac{d}{2s}-\frac{d}{\gamma}\right)=2+\frac{2d}{\gamma}>0,
\]
so \(b<2\).  Therefore, for all sufficiently large \(n\), \(h\in[n^{-1/d},1]\) and \(1\leq k\leq n^2\).  Since \(n^b\leq k\leq2n^b\),
\[
 h^\gamma=n^{-\rho_s},qquad
 \left(\frac{h}{k^{1/d}}\right)^{2s}\leq n^{-2s(a+b/d)}=n^{-\rho_s},
\]
and
\[
 N^{-1/2}=n^{-(1-ad)/2}\leq n^{-\rho_s},qquad
 \frac{\sqrt{k}}{N}\leq\sqrt2\,n^{-(1-ad-b/2)}=\sqrt2\,n^{-\rho_s}.
\]
Here the inequality for \(N^{-1/2}\) is again equivalent to \(D_s\geq2+d/\gamma\).  The elementary inequality \(\sqrt{x+y}\leq\sqrt{x}+\sqrt{y}\) now gives \(\Phi_n(h,k)\leq Cn^{-\rho_s}\).

Suppose next that \(s\geq s_0\).  Set
\[
 h=n^{-1/(2\gamma+d)},\qquad N=nh^d=n^{2\gamma/(2\gamma+d)},\qquad k=\lceil N\rceil.
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\), this pair belongs to \(\mathcal T_n\), and \(N\leq k\leq2N\).  Hence
\[
 h^\gamma=n^{-q},qquad
 \left(\frac{h}{k^{1/d}}\right)^{2s}\leq n^{-2s/d}\leq n^{-q},
\]
where the last inequality is exactly \(s\geq s_0\), and
\[
 \left\{\frac1N\left(1+\frac{k}{N}\right)\right\}^{1/2}
 \leq\sqrt{3}\,N^{-1/2}=\sqrt3\,n^{-q}.
\]
This proves the matching upper bounds.  All exponents and the rounding margins are continuous in the smoothness parameters; compactness makes the displayed constants and the sufficiently-large-\(n\) threshold uniform within either regime.  No logarithm entered any lower or upper calculation.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:positive-band-oracle-lower-bound]\label{thm:positive-band-oracle-lower-bound}
Fix \(d,L,\varepsilon,f_{-},f_{+}\), and the interior point \(x_0\) as in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band}}\).  For every compact \(K\subset(0,\infty)^3\), there are constants \(c_K>0\) and \(n_K<\infty\) such that, uniformly over \((\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\in K\) and \(n\geq n_K\),
\[
 \inf_{\widetilde\tau_n}\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band}}}
 \mathbb E_P\left|\widetilde\tau_n(O_{1:n})-\tau_P(x_0)\right|
 \geq c_K n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)},
\]
where the infimum is over all measurable real-valued estimators.  The lower bound is witnessed by a two-point uniform-design Bernoulli submodel contained in the fixed positive-density-band class.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We give the promised submodel and verify every class condition.  Choose a nonnegative \(C^\infty\) function \(\psi\) supported on \([-1,1]^d\), with \(\psi(0)=1\) and \(\|\psi\|_\infty\leq1\).  Such a function is obtained, for example, by normalizing the product over coordinates of \(t\mapsto \exp\{-1/(1-t^2)\}\mathbf 1\{|t|<1\}\).  Let
\[
 b_h(x)=\kappa h^\gamma\psi\left(\frac{x-x_0}{h}\right),qquad
 m_\pm(x)=\frac12\pm b_h(x),
\]
where \(h\) is smaller than the \(\ell_\infty\)-distance from \(x_0\) to the boundary of \([0,1]^d\), and \(0<\kappa\leq1/4\) will be chosen below.  Under \(P_\pm\), let \(X\) be uniform on \([0,1]^d\), let \(A\mid X\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\), and, conditionally on \(X=x\), let
\[
 Y(0)\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2),qquad
 Y(1)\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(m_\pm(x)),
\]
with \(Y(0),Y(1),A\) conditionally independent.  Finally set \(Y=AY(1)+(1-A)Y(0)\).

Consistency holds by the last definition, and conditional exchangeability holds by the stipulated conditional independence.  The density is identically one, so \(f_{-}<1<f_{+}\) verifies \(\mathrm{ass:density\mbox{-}band}\).  The propensity is identically \(1/2\), which satisfies \(\varepsilon<1/2<1-\varepsilon\), and both potential outcomes take values in \(\{0,1\}\), hence are bounded in absolute value by \(L\geq1\).  The control regression is \(\mu_{0,P_\pm}\equiv1/2\), the treated regression is \(m_\pm\), and
\[
 \tau_{P_\pm}(x)=\pm b_h(x),qquad
 \tau_{P_\pm}(x_0)=\pm\kappa h^\gamma.
\]
Constant functions have all positive-order H\"older seminorms zero and supremum at most one, so \(\pi_{P_\pm}\in\mathcal H_d^\alpha(L)\) and \(\mu_{0,P_\pm}\in\mathcal H_d^\beta(L)\) for every \(\alpha,\beta>0\).  If \(m_\gamma=\lceil\gamma\rceil-1\), the chain rule gives, for \(|v|\leq m_\gamma\),
\[
 \|D^v b_h\|_\infty\leq \kappa h^{\gamma-|v|}\|D^v\psi\|_\infty,
\]
and the order-\(m_\gamma\) H\"older quotient scales as \(\kappa\) times the corresponding quotient for \(D^{m_\gamma}\psi\).  Choosing \(\kappa\) below a fixed multiple of \(L\) therefore puts both \(\pm b_h\) in \(\mathcal H_d^\gamma(L)\).  On compact smoothness sets the finitely relevant derivative and H\"older norms can be bounded uniformly, so the same positive \(\kappa\) works throughout \(K\).  Thus \(P_+,P_-\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band}}\) by \(\mathrm{def:band\mbox{-}class}\), and \(\mathrm{lem:cate\mbox{-}identification}\) shows that the two displayed values of \(\tau_{P_\pm}(x_0)\) are the corresponding causal conditional effects, not merely regression functionals.

We next bound the statistical distance without invoking any support hole.  Conditional on \((X,A)=(x,0)\), the observed outcome laws agree.  Conditional on \((X,A)=(x,1)\), they are Bernoulli with parameters \(p=1/2+b_h(x)\) and \(q=1/2-b_h(x)\).  Since \(|b_h|\leq1/4\),
\[
 \chi^2\{\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p),\operatorname{Bernoulli}(q)\}
 =\frac{(p-q)^2}{q(1-q)}\leq Cb_h(x)^2.
\]
After averaging over the common laws of \(X\) and \(A\),
\[
 \chi^2(P_{+,O},P_{-,O})\leq C\int_{[0,1]^d}b_h(x)^2\,dx
 \leq C\kappa^2h^{d+2\gamma}.
\]
If \(Z=dP_{+,O}/dP_{-,O}\), then \(\log Z\leq Z-1\) implies
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_{+,O},P_{-,O})
 =\mathbb E_{P_{-,O}}[Z\log Z]
 \leq\mathbb E_{P_{-,O}}[Z(Z-1)]
 =\chi^2(P_{+,O},P_{-,O}).
\]
Thus product additivity gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_{+,O}^{\otimes n},P_{-,O}^{\otimes n})
 \leq Cn\kappa^2h^{d+2\gamma}.
\]
For the testing step we use the equally elementary product identity
\[
 1+\chi^2(P_{+,O}^{\otimes n},P_{-,O}^{\otimes n})
 =\{1+\chi^2(P_{+,O},P_{-,O})\}^n.
\]
Indeed, the product likelihood ratio is \(\prod_{i=1}^n Z_i\), and independence gives \(\mathbb E\prod_iZ_i^2=\prod_i\mathbb EZ_i^2\).
Choose \(h=a n^{-1/(2\gamma+d)}\), with a fixed \(a>0\) small enough, and decrease \(\kappa\) if necessary.  Then both the displayed product-law Kullback--Leibler divergence and \(n\kappa^2h^{d+2\gamma}\) are bounded by an absolute small constant, and
\[
 \chi^2(P_{+,O}^{\otimes n},P_{-,O}^{\otimes n})\leq\frac14.
\]

For completeness, let \(Q=P_{-,O}^{\otimes n}\), let \(Z=dP_{+,O}^{\otimes n}/dQ\), and observe by Cauchy--Schwarz that
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(P_{+,O}^{\otimes n},P_{-,O}^{\otimes n})
 =\frac12\mathbb E_Q|Z-1|
 \leq\frac12\{\mathbb E_Q(Z-1)^2\}^{1/2}\leq\frac14.
\]
For an arbitrary estimator \(T\), use the test \(\varphi=\mathbf1\{T\geq0\}\).  With \(a_h=\kappa h^\gamma\), a testing error under either law forces absolute estimation error at least \(a_h\).  Therefore
\[
 P_+(\varphi=0)+P_-(\varphi=1)
 =1-\{P_+(\varphi=1)-P_-(\varphi=1)\}\geq1-\operatorname{TV},
\]
and hence
\[
 \mathbb E_{P_+}|T-a_h|+\mathbb E_{P_-}|T+a_h|
 \geq a_h\{P_+(\varphi=0)+P_-(\varphi=1)\}
 \geq a_h(1-\operatorname{TV})\geq\frac34a_h.
\]
At least one of the two risks is at least \(3a_h/8\), and \(a_h=\kappa a^\gamma n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}\).  Taking first the supremum over the full class and then the infimum over \(T\) proves the claim.  The compact-uniform version follows by choosing \(a\), \(\kappa\), and the boundary threshold uniformly on \(K\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:positive-band-high-s-minimax]\label{prop:positive-band-high-s-minimax}
Let \(s=(\alpha+\beta)/2\) and \(s_0=d\gamma/\{2(2\gamma+d)\}\).  For every compact
\[
 K\subset\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\cap\{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma):s\geq s_0\},
\]
there are \(0<c_K\leq C_K<\infty\) and \(n_K\) such that, uniformly over \((\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\in K\) and \(n\geq n_K\),
\[
 c_Kn^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}
 \leq
 \inf_{\widetilde\tau_n}\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band}}}
 \mathbb E_P|\widetilde\tau_n(O_{1:n})-\tau_P(x_0)|
 \leq C_Kn^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}.
\]
Thus, and only in this above-elbow regime, the displayed order is the proved positive-band minimax mean-absolute-risk rate.  If instead \(K\) is a compact subset of \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\cap\{s<s_0\}\), the proved conclusions are only the honest bracket
\[
 c_Kn^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}
 \leq
 \inf_{\widetilde\tau_n}\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band}}}
 \mathbb E_P|\widetilde\tau_n(O_{1:n})-\tau_P(x_0)|
 \leq C_Kn^{-1/\{1+d/(2\gamma)+d/(4s)\}}.
\]
The matching positive-band low-smoothness converse remains unresolved.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
On a compact \(K\subset\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\), \(\mathrm{thm:unified\mbox{-}benchmark\mbox{-}attainment}\) supplies the explicit estimator \(\widehat\tau_n^*(x_0)\) and the upper bound \(C_Kr_n^*\).  By \(\mathrm{prop:optimizer\mbox{-}elbow\mbox{-}rate}\), this is at most \(C_Kn^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}\) when \(s\geq s_0\), and at most \(C_Kn^{-1/\{1+d/(2\gamma)+d/(4s)\}}\) when \(s<s_0\).  Since the infimum over all estimators is no larger than the risk of this particular total data-driven estimator, these are the two asserted upper bounds.  The lower bound in both regimes is \(\mathrm{thm:positive\mbox{-}band\mbox{-}oracle\mbox{-}lower\mbox{-}bound}\).  The upper and lower powers coincide exactly when \(s\geq s_0\).  Below the elbow they do not coincide; no larger-class support-hole lower bound is used to close that gap, because such a lower bound need not transfer to the positive-band subclass.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:cross-pair-saturated-bound]\label{prop:cross-pair-saturated-bound}
Write \(\bar\alpha=\alpha\wedge1\), \(\bar\beta=\beta\wedge1\), and \(\bar\gamma=\gamma\wedge1\).  For arbitrary \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma>0\), set \(h=h_n^*\), \(k=k_n^*\), \(N=nh^d\), and \(\delta=hk^{-1/d}\).  The already-defined total cross-pair estimator satisfies
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band}}}
 \mathbb E_P\left|\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{pair}}(x_0)-\tau_P(x_0)\right|
 \leq C\left\{h^{\bar\gamma}+\delta^{\bar\alpha+\bar\beta}+N^{-1/2}+\frac{\sqrt{k}}{N}\right\},
\]
where \(C<\infty\) depends only on the fixed class envelopes.  This is an all-smoothness robustness bound for the order-one estimator; when any smoothness index exceeds one it is not an attainment claim for the unsaturated KBRW objective.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The only smoothness modification needed in the order-one pair calculation is the following saturated increment inequality.  If \(g\in\mathcal H_d^u(L)\) and \(0<u\leq1\), \(\mathrm{def:holder\mbox{-}ball}\) gives \(|g(x)-g(z)|\leq C L\|x-z\|_\infty^u\).  If \(u>1\), the same definition bounds every first partial derivative by \(L\), and the mean-value theorem gives \(|g(x)-g(z)|\leq C L\|x-z\|_\infty\).  Thus, for every \(u>0\),
\[
 |g(x)-g(z)|\leq CL\|x-z\|_\infty^{u\wedge1}
\]
whenever \(\|x-z\|_\infty\leq1\).

Put \(\theta=\tau_P(x_0)\), \(p_x=\pi_P(x)\), and \(m_x=\mu_{0,P}(x)\).  Conditional independence of two observations and consistency give the exact identities
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\mathbb E_P[(A_i-A_j)(Y_i-Y_j)\mid X_i=x,X_j=z]\\
 &\quad=(p_x-p_z)(m_x-m_z)+p_x(1-p_z)\tau_P(x)+p_z(1-p_x)\tau_P(z),\\
 &\mathbb E_P[(A_i-A_j)^2\mid X_i=x,X_j=z]
 =p_x(1-p_z)+p_z(1-p_x).
 \end{aligned}
\]
On an edge in \(E_{hk}\), both points lie in \(B_h\) and \(\|x-z\|_\infty\leq\delta\).  The saturated increment inequality, applied to the three H\"older assumptions, therefore implies
\[
 \left|\mathbb E_P\left[(A_i-A_j)(Y_i-Y_j)-\theta(A_i-A_j)^2\mid x,z\right]\right|
 \leq C\{\delta^{\bar\alpha+\bar\beta}+h^{\bar\gamma}\}.
\]

We record the occupancy and variance calculation to show that no new condition is hidden.  The density upper band gives
\[
 P\{\{1,2\}\in E_{hk}\}\leq C h^d\delta^d=C\frac{h^{2d}}{k}.
\]
Hence, for \(S_{hk}=T_{hk}-\theta D_{hk}\) and \(\Lambda_{hk}=\binom n2h^{2d}/k\),
\[
 |\mathbb E_PS_{hk}|\leq C\Lambda_{hk}
 \{h^{\bar\gamma}+\delta^{\bar\alpha+\bar\beta}\}.
\]
The bounded-outcome assumption makes the kernels of both \(S_{hk}\) and \(D_{hk}\) uniformly bounded.  Disjoint-pair covariances vanish by independent sampling.  Diagonal pair terms are bounded by \(C\Lambda_{hk}\).  For a one-shared-index configuration, the density band gives directly
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &P\{\{1,2\}\in E_{hk},\{1,3\}\in E_{hk}\}\\
 &\quad\leq f_+^3\int_{B_h}
 \left|B_h\cap\{z:\|z-x\|_\infty\leq\delta\}\right|^2dx
 \leq C h^d\delta^{2d}=C\frac{h^{3d}}{k^2}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
There are at most \(Cn^3\) such ordered configurations.  Since \(N=nh^d\) and \(\Lambda_{hk}\asymp N^2/k\) for \(n\geq2\), summing the covariance graph yields
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_P(S_{hk})\leq C\left(\frac{\Lambda_{hk}^2}{N}+\Lambda_{hk}\right),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}_P(D_{hk})\leq C\left(\frac{\Lambda_{hk}^2}{N}+\Lambda_{hk}\right).
\]

For the denominator margin, each coordinate interval of \(B_h\) has length at least \(h\), and \(\delta\leq h\).  Integrating first over \(x\in B_h\) and then over the at-least-\(\delta^d\) portion of the \(\delta\)-cube around \(x\) that remains in \(B_h\) gives
\[
 P\{\{1,2\}\in E_{hk}\}\geq f_-^2h^d\delta^d=f_-^2\frac{h^{2d}}{k}.
\]
The bilinear function \(p(1-q)+q(1-p)\) has minimum \(2\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)\) on \([\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]^2\).  With \(a_0=\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)f_-^2\), it follows that
\[
 \mathbb E_PD_{hk}\geq2a_0\Lambda_{hk}.
\]
When \(\Lambda_{hk}\geq1\), Chebyshev's inequality and the variance bound give
\[
 P(D_{hk}<a_0\Lambda_{hk})
 \leq C\left(N^{-1}+\Lambda_{hk}^{-1}\right).
\]
On the successful screen, clipping is nonexpansive at \(\theta\in[-2L,2L]\), so division by \(D_{hk}\geq a_0\Lambda_{hk}\), Cauchy--Schwarz, and the preceding mean and variance bounds give
\[
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{pair}}(x_0)-\theta|
 \leq C\left\{h^{\bar\gamma}+\delta^{\bar\alpha+\bar\beta}
 +N^{-1/2}+\Lambda_{hk}^{-1/2}
 +N^{-1}+\Lambda_{hk}^{-1}\right\}.
\]
Here \(N\geq1\), \(\Lambda_{hk}\geq1\), and \(\Lambda_{hk}^{-1/2}\asymp\sqrt{k}/N\), so the last two terms are absorbed by the preceding square-root terms.  If \(\Lambda_{hk}<1\), the definition uses the bounded fallback, while \(\Lambda_{hk}\asymp N^2/k<1\) implies \(\sqrt{k}/N\geq c\), which absorbs the bounded loss.  Taking the supremum over the class proves the proposition.  The exponents are saturated because the order-one contrast uses only first increments; therefore this calculation does not replace an exponent above one by its unsaturated value.
\end{proof}

\section{Interpretation}

The optimizer has an explicit elbow at \(s_0=d\gamma/\{2(2\gamma+d)\}\).  Below it, balancing localization, the product remainder, and the pair-collision term gives \(r_n^*\asymp n^{-1/\{1+d/(2\gamma)+d/(4s)\}}\); this is an attained upper benchmark, not a proved positive-band minimax rate.  At and above the elbow, \(k\asymp nh^d\) and \(h\asymp n^{-1/(2\gamma+d)}\) give \(r_n^*\asymp n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}\), matching the positive-band two-point lower bound.  Both deterministic labels invoke the same total cross-pair map.  Any KBRW benchmark comparison outside \(\gamma\geq\beta\) is only an algebraic continuation of the objective, not a transfer of KBRW's full theorem class.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The proved estimator is order one and attains the unsaturated objective only on \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\); its all-smoothness robustness bound saturates every first-difference exponent at one.  Any higher-order extension beyond \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\) would require an explicitly specified random-design contrast or localized M\"obius estimator together with the corresponding projection or normalization bounds, causal numerator/denominator expansion, exceptional-event control, and complete higher-order collision/Hoeffding accounting.  None is asserted here.

\section{Honest scope}

Identification is given by the frozen CATE identification lemma.  The note proves the oracle positive-band lower bound and hence minimax optimality above the elbow.  It does not prove a matching positive-band converse below the elbow, because the published low-smoothness hard laws use density support holes excluded by \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band}}\).  It also does not claim arbitrary-smoothness attainment, a higher-order correction, full banked-region containment, adaptive unknown-smoothness inference, or a density-induced slowdown.
