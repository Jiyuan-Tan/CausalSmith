%%% FIELD stmt-optimizer-elbow
Put \(s=(\alpha+\beta)/2\) and
\[
 s_0=\frac{d/4}{1+d/(2\gamma)}=\frac{d\gamma}{2(2\gamma+d)}.
\]
For every fixed \((\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\in(0,\infty)^3\), as \(n\to\infty\),
\[
 r_n^*\asymp
 \begin{cases}
 n^{-1/\{1+d/(2\gamma)+d/(4s)\}},&s<s_0,\\
 n^{-1/\{2+d/\gamma\}}=n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)},&s\geq s_0.
 \end{cases}
\]
The constants may be chosen uniformly when the smoothness triple ranges over a compact subset of either displayed regime.  Below the elbow, feasible integer-rounded tuning has
\[
 h\asymp n^{-(1/\gamma)/\{1+d/(2\gamma)+d/(4s)\}},\qquad
 k\asymp n^{\{d/(2s)-d/\gamma\}/\{1+d/(2\gamma)+d/(4s)\}}.
\]
At and above the elbow, feasible tuning has \(h\asymp n^{-1/(2\gamma+d)}\) and \(k\asymp nh^d\).  These equivalences concern the optimizer-defined oracle-rate functional and introduce no logarithmic factor.

%%% FIELD proof-optimizer-elbow
Write
\[
 D_s=1+\frac{d}{2\gamma}+\frac{d}{4s},\qquad
 \rho_s=D_s^{-1},\qquad q=\frac{1}{2+d/\gamma}.
\]
We first prove lower bounds for every feasible \((h,k)\), rather than merely evaluating candidate tunings.  Let \(N=nh^d\) and \(R=\Phi_n(h,k)\).  By \(\mathrm{def:kbrw\mbox{-}objective}\), each nonnegative summand is at most \(R\), while its last summand is at least both \(N^{-1/2}\) and \(\sqrt{k}/N\).  Thus
\[
 h^\gamma\leq R,qquad
 \left(hk^{-1/d}\right)^{2s}\leq R,qquad
 N^{-1/2}\leq R,qquad
 \frac{\sqrt{k}}{N}\leq R.
\]
The second and fourth inequalities imply, respectively,
\[
 k\geq h^dR^{-d/(2s)},qquad k\leq R^2n^2h^{2d}.
\]
After cancellation of the positive factor \(h^d\), and then use of \(h^d\leq R^{d/\gamma}\), they give
\[
 1\leq n^2h^dR^{2+d/(2s)}
 \leq n^2R^{2+d/(2s)+d/\gamma}.
\]
Consequently \(R\geq n^{-\rho_s}\).  Similarly, \(N^{-1/2}\leq R\) gives \(h^d\geq n^{-1}R^{-2}\); combining this with \(h^d\leq R^{d/\gamma}\) yields
\[
 R\geq n^{-q}.
\]
The equality \(\rho_s=q\) is equivalent to
\[
 \frac{d}{4s}=1+\frac{d}{2\gamma},
\]
which is exactly \(s=s_0\).  Hence \(n^{-\rho_s}\) is the larger lower bound when \(s<s_0\), and \(n^{-q}\) is the larger lower bound when \(s\geq s_0\).  Taking the infimum over \(\mathcal T_n\) proves the required piecewise lower bound for \(r_n^*\), by \(\mathrm{def:optimizer}\).

It remains to give feasible upper bounds, including the integer and boundary restrictions in \(\mathcal T_n\).  Suppose first that \(s<s_0\), and set
\[
 a=\frac{\rho_s}{\gamma},\qquad
 b=\rho_s\left(\frac{d}{2s}-\frac{d}{\gamma}\right),qquad
 h=n^{-a},\qquad k=\left\lceil n^b\right\rceil.
\]
Because \(s_0<\gamma/2\), one has \(b>0\).  Moreover, \(s<s_0\) implies \(D_s>2+d/\gamma\), so \(0<a<1/d\), and direct subtraction gives
\[
 2D_s-\left(\frac{d}{2s}-\frac{d}{\gamma}\right)=2+\frac{2d}{\gamma}>0,
\]
so \(b<2\).  Therefore, for all sufficiently large \(n\), \(h\in[n^{-1/d},1]\) and \(1\leq k\leq n^2\).  Since \(n^b\leq k\leq2n^b\),
\[
 h^\gamma=n^{-\rho_s},qquad
 \left(\frac{h}{k^{1/d}}\right)^{2s}\leq n^{-2s(a+b/d)}=n^{-\rho_s},
\]
and
\[
 N^{-1/2}=n^{-(1-ad)/2}\leq n^{-\rho_s},qquad
 \frac{\sqrt{k}}{N}\leq\sqrt2\,n^{-(1-ad-b/2)}=\sqrt2\,n^{-\rho_s}.
\]
Here the inequality for \(N^{-1/2}\) is again equivalent to \(D_s\geq2+d/\gamma\).  The elementary inequality \(\sqrt{x+y}\leq\sqrt{x}+\sqrt{y}\) now gives \(\Phi_n(h,k)\leq Cn^{-\rho_s}\).

Suppose next that \(s\geq s_0\).  Set
\[
 h=n^{-1/(2\gamma+d)},\qquad N=nh^d=n^{2\gamma/(2\gamma+d)},\qquad k=\lceil N\rceil.
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\), this pair belongs to \(\mathcal T_n\), and \(N\leq k\leq2N\).  Hence
\[
 h^\gamma=n^{-q},qquad
 \left(\frac{h}{k^{1/d}}\right)^{2s}leq n^{-2s/d}\leq n^{-q},
\]
where the last inequality is exactly \(s\geq s_0\), and
\[
 \left\{\frac1N\left(1+\frac{k}{N}\right)\right\}^{1/2}
 \leq\sqrt{3}\,N^{-1/2}=\sqrt3\,n^{-q}.
\]
This proves the matching upper bounds.  All exponents and the rounding margins are continuous in the smoothness parameters; compactness makes the displayed constants and the sufficiently-large-\(n\) threshold uniform within either regime.  No logarithm entered any lower or upper calculation.

%%% FIELD stmt-positive-band-lb
Fix \(d,L,\varepsilon,f_{-},f_{+}\), and the interior point \(x_0\) as in \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band}}\).  For every compact \(K\subset(0,\infty)^3\), there are constants \(c_K>0\) and \(n_K<\infty\) such that, uniformly over \((\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\in K\) and \(n\geq n_K\),
\[
 \inf_{\widetilde\tau_n}\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band}}}
 \mathbb E_P\left|\widetilde\tau_n(O_{1:n})-\tau_P(x_0)\right|
 \geq c_K n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)},
\]
where the infimum is over all measurable real-valued estimators.  The lower bound is witnessed by a two-point uniform-design Bernoulli submodel contained in the fixed positive-density-band class.

%%% FIELD proof-positive-band-lb
We give the promised submodel and verify every class condition.  Choose a nonnegative \(C^\infty\) function \(\psi\) supported on \([-1,1]^d\), with \(\psi(0)=1\) and \(\|\psi\|_\infty\leq1\).  Such a function is obtained, for example, by normalizing the product over coordinates of \(t\mapsto \exp\{-1/(1-t^2)\}\mathbf 1\{|t|<1\}\).  Let
\[
 b_h(x)=\kappa h^\gamma\psi\left(\frac{x-x_0}{h}\right),qquad
 m_\pm(x)=\frac12\pm b_h(x),
\]
where \(h\) is smaller than the \(\ell_\infty\)-distance from \(x_0\) to the boundary of \([0,1]^d\), and \(0<\kappa\leq1/4\) will be chosen below.  Under \(P_\pm\), let \(X\) be uniform on \([0,1]^d\), let \(A\mid X\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\), and, conditionally on \(X=x\), let
\[
 Y(0)\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2),qquad
 Y(1)\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(m_\pm(x)),
\]
with \(Y(0),Y(1),A\) conditionally independent.  Finally set \(Y=AY(1)+(1-A)Y(0)\).

Consistency holds by the last definition, and conditional exchangeability holds by the stipulated conditional independence.  The density is identically one, so \(f_{-}<1<f_{+}\) verifies \(\mathrm{ass:density\mbox{-}band}\).  The propensity is identically \(1/2\), which satisfies \(\varepsilon<1/2<1-\varepsilon\), and both potential outcomes take values in \(\{0,1\}\), hence are bounded in absolute value by \(L\geq1\).  The control regression is \(\mu_{0,P_\pm}\equiv1/2\), the treated regression is \(m_\pm\), and
\[
 \tau_{P_\pm}(x)=\pm b_h(x),qquad
 \tau_{P_\pm}(x_0)=\pm\kappa h^\gamma.
\]
Constant functions have all positive-order H\"older seminorms zero and supremum at most one, so \(\pi_{P_\pm}\in\mathcal H_d^\alpha(L)\) and \(\mu_{0,P_\pm}\in\mathcal H_d^\beta(L)\) for every \(\alpha,\beta>0\).  If \(m_\gamma=\lceil\gamma\rceil-1\), the chain rule gives, for \(|v|\leq m_\gamma\),
\[
 \|D^v b_h\|_\infty\leq \kappa h^{\gamma-|v|}\|D^v\psi\|_\infty,
\]
and the order-\(m_\gamma\) H\"older quotient scales as \(\kappa\) times the corresponding quotient for \(D^{m_\gamma}\psi\).  Choosing \(\kappa\) below a fixed multiple of \(L\) therefore puts both \(\pm b_h\) in \(\mathcal H_d^\gamma(L)\).  On compact smoothness sets the finitely relevant derivative and H\"older norms can be bounded uniformly, so the same positive \(\kappa\) works throughout \(K\).  Thus \(P_+,P_-\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band}}\) by \(\mathrm{def:band\mbox{-}class}\), and \(\mathrm{lem:cate\mbox{-}identification}\) shows that the two displayed values of \(\tau_{P_\pm}(x_0)\) are the corresponding causal conditional effects, not merely regression functionals.

We next bound the statistical distance without invoking any support hole.  Conditional on \((X,A)=(x,0)\), the observed outcome laws agree.  Conditional on \((X,A)=(x,1)\), they are Bernoulli with parameters \(p=1/2+b_h(x)\) and \(q=1/2-b_h(x)\).  Since \(|b_h|\leq1/4\),
\[
 \chi^2\{\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p),\operatorname{Bernoulli}(q)\}
 =\frac{(p-q)^2}{q(1-q)}\leq Cb_h(x)^2.
\]
After averaging over the common laws of \(X\) and \(A\),
\[
 \chi^2(P_{+,O},P_{-,O})\leq C\int_{[0,1]^d}b_h(x)^2\,dx
 \leq C\kappa^2h^{d+2\gamma}.
\]
If \(Z=dP_{+,O}/dP_{-,O}\), then \(\log Z\leq Z-1\) implies
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_{+,O},P_{-,O})
 =\mathbb E_{P_{-,O}}[Z\log Z]
 \leq\mathbb E_{P_{-,O}}[Z(Z-1)]
 =\chi^2(P_{+,O},P_{-,O}).
\]
Thus product additivity gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(P_{+,O}^{\otimes n},P_{-,O}^{\otimes n})
 \leq Cn\kappa^2h^{d+2\gamma}.
\]
For the testing step we use the equally elementary product identity
\[
 1+\chi^2(P_{+,O}^{\otimes n},P_{-,O}^{\otimes n})
 =\{1+\chi^2(P_{+,O},P_{-,O})\}^n.
\]
Indeed, the product likelihood ratio is \(\prod_{i=1}^n Z_i\), and independence gives \(\mathbb E\prod_iZ_i^2=\prod_i\mathbb EZ_i^2\).
Choose \(h=a n^{-1/(2\gamma+d)}\), with a fixed \(a>0\) small enough, and decrease \(\kappa\) if necessary.  Then both the displayed product-law Kullback--Leibler divergence and \(n\kappa^2h^{d+2\gamma}\) are bounded by an absolute small constant, and
\[
 \chi^2(P_{+,O}^{\otimes n},P_{-,O}^{\otimes n})\leq\frac14.
\]

For completeness, let \(Q=P_{-,O}^{\otimes n}\), let \(Z=dP_{+,O}^{\otimes n}/dQ\), and observe by Cauchy--Schwarz that
\[
 \operatorname{TV}(P_{+,O}^{\otimes n},P_{-,O}^{\otimes n})
 =\frac12\mathbb E_Q|Z-1|
 \leq\frac12\{\mathbb E_Q(Z-1)^2\}^{1/2}\leq\frac14.
\]
For an arbitrary estimator \(T\), use the test \(\varphi=\mathbf1\{T\geq0\}\).  With \(a_h=\kappa h^\gamma\), a testing error under either law forces absolute estimation error at least \(a_h\).  Therefore
\[
 P_+(\varphi=0)+P_-(\varphi=1)
 =1-\{P_+(\varphi=1)-P_-(\varphi=1)\}\geq1-\operatorname{TV},
\]
and hence
\[
 \mathbb E_{P_+}|T-a_h|+\mathbb E_{P_-}|T+a_h|
 \geq a_h\{P_+(\varphi=0)+P_-(\varphi=1)\}
 \geq a_h(1-\operatorname{TV})\geq\frac34a_h.
\]
At least one of the two risks is at least \(3a_h/8\), and \(a_h=\kappa a^\gamma n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}\).  Taking first the supremum over the full class and then the infimum over \(T\) proves the claim.  The compact-uniform version follows by choosing \(a\), \(\kappa\), and the boundary threshold uniformly on \(K\).

%%% FIELD stmt-positive-band-high-s
Let \(s=(\alpha+\beta)/2\) and \(s_0=d\gamma/\{2(2\gamma+d)\}\).  For every compact
\[
 K\subset\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\cap\{(\alpha,\beta,\gamma):s\geq s_0\},
\]
there are \(0<c_K\leq C_K<\infty\) and \(n_K\) such that, uniformly over \((\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\in K\) and \(n\geq n_K\),
\[
 c_Kn^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}
 \leq
 \inf_{\widetilde\tau_n}\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band}}}
 \mathbb E_P|\widetilde\tau_n(O_{1:n})-\tau_P(x_0)|
 \leq C_Kn^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}.
\]
Thus, and only in this above-elbow regime, the displayed order is the proved positive-band minimax mean-absolute-risk rate.  If instead \(K\) is a compact subset of \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\cap\{s<s_0\}\), the proved conclusions are only the honest bracket
\[
 c_Kn^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}
 \leq
 \inf_{\widetilde\tau_n}\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band}}}
 \mathbb E_P|\widetilde\tau_n(O_{1:n})-\tau_P(x_0)|
 \leq C_Kn^{-1/\{1+d/(2\gamma)+d/(4s)\}}.
\]
The matching positive-band low-smoothness converse remains unresolved.

%%% FIELD proof-positive-band-high-s
On a compact \(K\subset\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\), \(\mathrm{thm:unified\mbox{-}benchmark\mbox{-}attainment}\) supplies the explicit estimator \(\widehat\tau_n^*(x_0)\) and the upper bound \(C_Kr_n^*\).  By \(\mathrm{prop:optimizer\mbox{-}elbow\mbox{-}rate}\), this is at most \(C_Kn^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}\) when \(s\geq s_0\), and at most \(C_Kn^{-1/\{1+d/(2\gamma)+d/(4s)\}}\) when \(s<s_0\).  Since the infimum over all estimators is no larger than the risk of this particular total data-driven estimator, these are the two asserted upper bounds.  The lower bound in both regimes is \(\mathrm{thm:positive\mbox{-}band\mbox{-}oracle\mbox{-}lower\mbox{-}bound}\).  The upper and lower powers coincide exactly when \(s\geq s_0\).  Below the elbow they do not coincide; no larger-class support-hole lower bound is used to close that gap, because such a lower bound need not transfer to the positive-band subclass.

%%% FIELD stmt-cross-pair-saturated
Write \(\bar\alpha=\alpha\wedge1\), \(\bar\beta=\beta\wedge1\), and \(\bar\gamma=\gamma\wedge1\).  For arbitrary \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma>0\), set \(h=h_n^*\), \(k=k_n^*\), \(N=nh^d\), and \(\delta=hk^{-1/d}\).  The already-defined total cross-pair estimator satisfies
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band}}}
 \mathbb E_P\left|\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{pair}}(x_0)-\tau_P(x_0)\right|
 \leq C\left\{h^{\bar\gamma}+\delta^{\bar\alpha+\bar\beta}+N^{-1/2}+\frac{\sqrt{k}}{N}\right\},
\]
where \(C<\infty\) depends only on the fixed class envelopes.  This is an all-smoothness robustness bound for the order-one estimator; when any smoothness index exceeds one it is not an attainment claim for the unsaturated KBRW objective.

%%% FIELD proof-cross-pair-saturated
The only smoothness modification needed in the order-one pair calculation is the following saturated increment inequality.  If \(g\in\mathcal H_d^u(L)\) and \(0<u\leq1\), \(\mathrm{def:holder\mbox{-}ball}\) gives \(|g(x)-g(z)|\leq C L\|x-z\|_\infty^u\).  If \(u>1\), the same definition bounds every first partial derivative by \(L\), and the mean-value theorem gives \(|g(x)-g(z)|\leq C L\|x-z\|_\infty\).  Thus, for every \(u>0\),
\[
 |g(x)-g(z)|\leq CL\|x-z\|_\infty^{u\wedge1}
\]
whenever \(\|x-z\|_\infty\leq1\).

Put \(\theta=\tau_P(x_0)\), \(p_x=\pi_P(x)\), and \(m_x=\mu_{0,P}(x)\).  Conditional independence of two observations and consistency give the exact identities
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\mathbb E_P[(A_i-A_j)(Y_i-Y_j)\mid X_i=x,X_j=z]\\
 &\quad=(p_x-p_z)(m_x-m_z)+p_x(1-p_z)\tau_P(x)+p_z(1-p_x)\tau_P(z),\\
 &\mathbb E_P[(A_i-A_j)^2\mid X_i=x,X_j=z]
 =p_x(1-p_z)+p_z(1-p_x).
 \end{aligned}
\]
On an edge in \(E_{hk}\), both points lie in \(B_h\) and \(\|x-z\|_\infty\leq\delta\).  The saturated increment inequality, applied to the three H\"older assumptions, therefore implies
\[
 \left|\mathbb E_P\left[(A_i-A_j)(Y_i-Y_j)-\theta(A_i-A_j)^2\mid x,z\right]\right|
 \leq C\{\delta^{\bar\alpha+\bar\beta}+h^{\bar\gamma}\}.
\]

We record the occupancy and variance calculation to show that no new condition is hidden.  The density upper band gives
\[
 P\{\{1,2\}\in E_{hk}\}\leq C h^d\delta^d=C\frac{h^{2d}}{k}.
\]
Hence, for \(S_{hk}=T_{hk}-\theta D_{hk}\) and \(\Lambda_{hk}=\binom n2h^{2d}/k\),
\[
 |\mathbb E_PS_{hk}|\leq C\Lambda_{hk}
 \{h^{\bar\gamma}+\delta^{\bar\alpha+\bar\beta}\}.
\]
The bounded-outcome assumption makes the kernels of both \(S_{hk}\) and \(D_{hk}\) uniformly bounded.  Disjoint-pair covariances vanish by independent sampling.  Diagonal pair terms are bounded by \(C\Lambda_{hk}\).  For a one-shared-index configuration, the density band gives directly
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &P\{\{1,2\}\in E_{hk},\{1,3\}\in E_{hk}\}\\
 &\quad\leq f_+^3\int_{B_h}
 \left|B_h\cap\{z:\|z-x\|_\infty\leq\delta\}\right|^2dx
 \leq C h^d\delta^{2d}=C\frac{h^{3d}}{k^2}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
There are at most \(Cn^3\) such ordered configurations.  Since \(N=nh^d\) and \(\Lambda_{hk}\asymp N^2/k\) for \(n\geq2\), summing the covariance graph yields
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_P(S_{hk})\leq C\left(\frac{\Lambda_{hk}^2}{N}+\Lambda_{hk}\right),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}_P(D_{hk})\leq C\left(\frac{\Lambda_{hk}^2}{N}+\Lambda_{hk}\right).
\]

For the denominator margin, each coordinate interval of \(B_h\) has length at least \(h\), and \(\delta\leq h\).  Integrating first over \(x\in B_h\) and then over the at-least-\(\delta^d\) portion of the \(\delta\)-cube around \(x\) that remains in \(B_h\) gives
\[
 P\{\{1,2\}\in E_{hk}\}\geq f_-^2h^d\delta^d=f_-^2\frac{h^{2d}}{k}.
\]
The bilinear function \(p(1-q)+q(1-p)\) has minimum \(2\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)\) on \([\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]^2\).  With \(a_0=\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)f_-^2\), it follows that
\[
 \mathbb E_PD_{hk}\geq2a_0\Lambda_{hk}.
\]
When \(\Lambda_{hk}\geq1\), Chebyshev's inequality and the variance bound give
\[
 P(D_{hk}<a_0\Lambda_{hk})
 \leq C\left(N^{-1}+\Lambda_{hk}^{-1}\right).
\]
On the successful screen, clipping is nonexpansive at \(\theta\in[-2L,2L]\), so division by \(D_{hk}\geq a_0\Lambda_{hk}\), Cauchy--Schwarz, and the preceding mean and variance bounds give
\[
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{pair}}(x_0)-\theta|
 \leq C\left\{h^{\bar\gamma}+\delta^{\bar\alpha+\bar\beta}
 +N^{-1/2}+\Lambda_{hk}^{-1/2}
 +N^{-1}+\Lambda_{hk}^{-1}\right\}.
\]
Here \(N\geq1\), \(\Lambda_{hk}\geq1\), and \(\Lambda_{hk}^{-1/2}\asymp\sqrt{k}/N\), so the last two terms are absorbed by the preceding square-root terms.  If \(\Lambda_{hk}<1\), the definition uses the bounded fallback, while \(\Lambda_{hk}\asymp N^2/k<1\) implies \(\sqrt{k}/N\geq c\), which absorbs the bounded loss.  Taking the supremum over the class proves the proposition.  The exponents are saturated because the order-one contrast uses only first increments; therefore this calculation does not replace an exponent above one by its unsaturated value.

%%% FIELD proof-unified
Fix a compact \(K\subset\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\).  By \(\mathrm{ass:bounded\mbox{-}outcomes}\) and \(\mathrm{lem:cate\mbox{-}identification}\), \(\theta=\tau_P(x_0)\in[-2L,2L]\).  Projection onto this interval is nonexpansive at \(\theta\): for every real \(z\),
\[
 |\operatorname{clip}_{[-2L,2L]}(z)-\theta|\leq|z-\theta|.
\]
By \(\mathrm{lem:optimizer\mbox{-}partition}\), \(G_n,P_n\in\{0,1\}\), \(G_n+P_n=1\), and \(G_nP_n=0\).  Applying these deterministic identities to \(\mathrm{def:unified\mbox{-}estimator}\) gives, for every \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band}}\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat\tau_n^*(x_0)-\theta|
 \leq G_n\mathbb E_P|\widehat\tau_n^G(x_0)-\theta|
 +P_n\mathbb E_P|\widehat\tau_n^P(x_0)-\theta|.
\]
If \(G_n=1\), \(\mathrm{thm:rank\mbox{-}feasible\mbox{-}local\mbox{-}hoif}\) bounds the only nonzero term by \(C_Kr_n^*\).  If \(P_n=1\), \(\mathrm{thm:rank\mbox{-}stressed\mbox{-}close\mbox{-}pair}\) gives the same bound.  These two cases are exhaustive, so taking the supremum over \(P\) yields
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band}}}
 \mathbb E_P|\widehat\tau_n^*(x_0)-\tau_P(x_0)|\leq C_Kr_n^*.
\]
Finally, \(\mathrm{lem:optimizer\mbox{-}partition}\) identifies \(r_n^*\) with the exact infimum of the displayed three-term objective over \(\mathcal T_n\).  On \(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{pair}}\), both branch handles instantiate the same everywhere-defined clipped cross-pair map from \(\mathrm{def:cross\mbox{-}pair\mbox{-}estimator}\).  Thus the deterministic switch adds neither a transition remainder nor a logarithmic factor, and it uses no estimate or smoothness property of \(f_P\).
