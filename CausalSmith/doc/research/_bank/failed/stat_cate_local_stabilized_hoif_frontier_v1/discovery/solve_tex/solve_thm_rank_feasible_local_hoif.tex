Suppose \(0<\alpha\leq1\), \(0<\beta\leq1\), and \(0<\gamma\leq1\).  For every \(n\geq2\), set \(h=h_n^*\), \(k=k_n^*\), \(\delta=h k^{-1/d}\), and \(N=N_n^*=nh^d\).  The total local cross-pair estimator in \(\mathrm{def:cross\mbox{-}pair\mbox{-}estimator}\) satisfies, for a constant \(C<\infty\) depending only on \(d,L,\varepsilon,f_{-},f_{+}\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band}}}
 \mathbb E_P\left|\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{pair}}(x_0)-\tau_P(x_0)\right|
 \leq C\left\{h^\gamma+\delta^{\alpha+\beta}+N^{-1/2}+\frac{\sqrt{k}}{N}\right\}
 \leq C r_n^*.
\]
The estimator is defined on every sample, including when there is no close pair or the denominator screen fails, and it uses neither an estimate nor a smoothness condition for \(f_P\).
%%% FIELD lemma-cross-pair-proof
Fix \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band}}\), abbreviate \(p(x)=\pi_P(x)\), \(m(x)=\mu_{0,P}(x)\), \(t(x)=\tau_P(x)\), and put \(\theta=t(x_0)\).  By \(\mathrm{ass:bounded\mbox{-}outcomes}\), \(\mathrm{ass:consistency}\), and \(\mathrm{lem:cate\mbox{-}identification}\), \(|Y|\leq L\) almost surely and \(|\theta|\leq2L\).

We first establish the causal pair identity rather than importing a squared-contrast formula.  Conditional on \(X_i=x\), the definitions of the two outcome regressions give
\[
 \mathbb E_P(Y_i\mid X_i=x)=m(x)+p(x)t(x),
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_P(A_iY_i\mid X_i=x)=p(x)\{m(x)+t(x)\}.
\]
By \(\mathrm{ass:iid\mbox{-}sampling}\), observations \(i\) and \(j\) are conditionally independent given \((X_i,X_j)=(x,z)\).  Expanding the four products and substituting the preceding two displays therefore yields
\[
\begin{split}
 &\mathbb E_P\bigl[(A_i-A_j)(Y_i-Y_j)\mid X_i=x,X_j=z\bigr]\\
 &\quad=p(x)\{m(x)+t(x)\}+p(z)\{m(z)+t(z)\}
       -p(x)\{m(z)+p(z)t(z)\}-p(z)\{m(x)+p(x)t(x)\}\\
 &\quad=\{p(x)-p(z)\}\{m(x)-m(z)\}
       +p(x)\{1-p(z)\}t(x)+p(z)\{1-p(x)\}t(z).
\end{split}
\]
The same conditional independence and \(A^2=A\) give
\[
 \mathbb E_P\bigl[(A_i-A_j)^2\mid X_i=x,X_j=z\bigr]
 =p(x)\{1-p(z)\}+p(z)\{1-p(x)\}.
\]
Consequently the conditional expectation of the centered contrast is
\[
\begin{split}
 b(x,z)
 &:=\mathbb E_P\bigl[(A_i-A_j)(Y_i-Y_j)-\theta(A_i-A_j)^2
       \mid X_i=x,X_j=z\bigr]\\
 &=\{p(x)-p(z)\}\{m(x)-m(z)\}
   +p(x)\{1-p(z)\}\{t(x)-\theta\}
   +p(z)\{1-p(x)\}\{t(z)-\theta\}.
\end{split}
\]
Because each smoothness index is at most one, \(\mathrm{def:holder\mbox{-}ball}\) and the three H\"older assumptions imply the first-difference inequalities
\[
 |p(x)-p(z)|\leq C\lVert x-z\rVert_\infty^\alpha,
 \qquad
 |m(x)-m(z)|\leq C\lVert x-z\rVert_\infty^\beta,
 \qquad
 |t(x)-t(x_0)|\leq C\lVert x-x_0\rVert_\infty^\gamma.
\]
Here and below \(C\) may change from line to line but depends only on \(d,L,\varepsilon,f_{-},f_{+}\).  Thus, whenever \(x,z\in B_h\) and \(\lVert x-z\rVert_\infty\leq\delta\),
\[
 |b(x,z)|\leq C\{\delta^{\alpha+\beta}+h^\gamma\}.
\]
This is precisely the step that uses \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\leq1\); first differences do not justify this display for smoothness orders above one.

We next prove the required occupancy estimates from \(\mathrm{ass:density\mbox{-}band}\).  Every coordinate interval of
\(B_h=\prod_{\ell=1}^d([x_{0\ell}-h,x_{0\ell}+h]\cap[0,1])\)
has length between \(h\) and \(2h\).  Since \(k\geq1\), \(\delta\leq h\).  For every \(x\in B_h\), each coordinate interval of
\(B_h\cap\{z:\lVert z-x\rVert_\infty\leq\delta\}\)
has length at least \(\delta\).  Tonelli's theorem and the two density bounds therefore give, with \(e_{ij}=\{X_i,X_j\in B_h,\ \lVert X_i-X_j\rVert_\infty\leq\delta\}\),
\[
 f_{-}^2h^d\delta^d
 \leq \mathbb P_P(e_{12})
 \leq f_{+}^2(2h)^d(2\delta)^d.
\]
Since \(\delta^d=h^d/k\), this is
\[
 f_{-}^2\frac{h^{2d}}{k}
 \leq \mathbb P_P(e_{12})
 \leq 4^d f_{+}^2\frac{h^{2d}}{k}.
\]
Likewise, conditional integration over the shared point \(X_1\) yields
\[
 \mathbb P_P(e_{12}\cap e_{13})
 \leq f_{+}^3(2h)^d(2\delta)^{2d}
 =2^{3d}f_{+}^3\frac{h^{3d}}{k^2}.
\]

For \(p,q\in[\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]\), the bilinear function
\(p(1-q)+q(1-p)\) attains its minimum over this rectangle at a corner, where its minimum is \(2\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)\).  Hence \(\mathrm{ass:overlap}\) and the lower occupancy bound imply
\[
 \mathbb E_P D_{hk}
 =\binom n2\mathbb E_P\left[\mathbf 1_{e_{12}}(A_1-A_2)^2\right]
 \geq2\varepsilon(1-\varepsilon)f_{-}^2\binom n2\frac{h^{2d}}{k}
 =2a_0\Lambda_{hk}.
\]

Put \(S_{hk}=T_{hk}-\theta D_{hk}\) and
\[
 \xi_{ij}=\mathbf 1_{e_{ij}}\bigl\{(A_i-A_j)(Y_i-Y_j)-\theta(A_i-A_j)^2\bigr\}.
\]
Then \(S_{hk}=\sum_{i<j}\xi_{ij}\), \(|\xi_{ij}|\leq4L\mathbf 1_{e_{ij}}\), and the conditional-bias and upper-occupancy bounds give
\[
 |\mathbb E_P S_{hk}|
 \leq\binom n2 C\frac{h^{2d}}{k}\{h^\gamma+\delta^{\alpha+\beta}\}
 =C\Lambda_{hk}\{h^\gamma+\delta^{\alpha+\beta}\}.
\]
Disjoint unordered pairs are independent by \(\mathrm{ass:iid\mbox{-}sampling}\), so their covariances vanish.  The diagonal terms satisfy
\[
 \sum_{i<j}\operatorname{Var}_P(\xi_{ij})
 \leq Cn^2\mathbb P_P(e_{12})
 \leq C\Lambda_{hk}.
\]
There are at most \(Cn^3\) ordered pairs of edges sharing one index.  Boundedness of \(\xi_{ij}\), the shared-edge estimate, and
\(\mathbb P_P(e_{12})^2\leq C h^{4d}/k^2\leq C h^{3d}/k^2\) imply
\[
 \left|\operatorname{Cov}_P(\xi_{12},\xi_{13})\right|
 \leq C\left\{\mathbb P_P(e_{12}\cap e_{13})+\mathbb P_P(e_{12})^2\right\}
 \leq C\frac{h^{3d}}{k^2}.
\]
Since \(N=nh^d\) and
\[
 \Lambda_{hk}=\frac{n(n-1)}{2}\frac{h^{2d}}{k}
 =\frac{n-1}{2n}\frac{N^2}{k},
\]
summing the shared-index terms proves
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_P(S_{hk})
 \leq C\left\{\Lambda_{hk}+n^3\frac{h^{3d}}{k^2}\right\}
 \leq C\left\{\Lambda_{hk}+\frac{\Lambda_{hk}^2}{N}\right\}.
\]
The identical argument with
\(\zeta_{ij}=\mathbf 1_{e_{ij}}(A_i-A_j)^2\), for which \(|\zeta_{ij}|\leq\mathbf 1_{e_{ij}}\), gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_P(D_{hk})
 \leq C\left\{\Lambda_{hk}+\frac{\Lambda_{hk}^2}{N}\right\}.
\]
Thus both the diagonal and shared-index Hoeffding components have been accounted for directly.

Suppose first that \(\Lambda_{hk}\geq1\).  Since \(\mathbb E_P D_{hk}\geq2a_0\Lambda_{hk}\), Chebyshev's inequality gives
\[
 \mathbb P_P(D_{hk}<a_0\Lambda_{hk})
 \leq\frac{\operatorname{Var}_P(D_{hk})}{a_0^2\Lambda_{hk}^2}
 \leq C\left\{N^{-1}+\Lambda_{hk}^{-1}\right\}.
\]
On the successful screen, the untruncated ratio obeys
\(T_{hk}/D_{hk}-\theta=S_{hk}/D_{hk}\), and \(D_{hk}\geq a_0\Lambda_{hk}\).  Projection onto \([-2L,2L]\) cannot increase distance from \(\theta\in[-2L,2L]\), while on screen failure the raw estimate is zero.  Cauchy--Schwarz therefore yields
\[
\begin{split}
 \mathbb E_P\left|\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{pair}}(x_0)-\theta\right|
 &\leq \frac{|\mathbb E_P S_{hk}|+\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}_P(S_{hk})}}{a_0\Lambda_{hk}}
       +2L\,\mathbb P_P(D_{hk}<a_0\Lambda_{hk})\\
 &\leq C\left\{h^\gamma+\delta^{\alpha+\beta}+N^{-1/2}+\Lambda_{hk}^{-1/2}
       +N^{-1}+\Lambda_{hk}^{-1}\right\}\\
 &\leq C\left\{h^\gamma+\delta^{\alpha+\beta}+N^{-1/2}+\frac{\sqrt{k}}{N}\right\}.
\end{split}
\]
The last line uses \(N\geq1\), \(\Lambda_{hk}\geq1\), and the displayed exact relation between \(\Lambda_{hk}\) and \(N^2/k\).

If \(\Lambda_{hk}<1\), the construction returns zero before clipping, so the crude clipped loss is at most \(4L\).  For \(n\geq2\),
\[
 \frac{\sqrt{k}}{N}
 =\left\{\frac{n-1}{2n\Lambda_{hk}}\right\}^{1/2}
 \geq\frac12,
\]
and hence the same risk bound follows after increasing \(C\).  Finally,
\[
 N^{-1/2}+\frac{\sqrt{k}}{N}
 \leq2\left\{\frac1N\left(1+\frac{k}{N}\right)\right\}^{1/2},
\]
while \(\delta^{\alpha+\beta}=(h/k^{1/d})^{\alpha+\beta}\).  The definition of \(r_n^*\) in \(\mathrm{def:optimizer}\) proves the final comparison.  All bounds are uniform over \(P\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{band}}\), completing the proof.
