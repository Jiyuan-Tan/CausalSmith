Put \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}(d,j)=P(A=d,Y=j)\) for \((d,j)\in\mathcal D\times\mathcal Y_L\).  Positivity in \(\text{ass:positive-randomization}\) makes each conditional probability below well defined.  Consistency and random assignment give, successively,
\[
 P(Y=j\mid A=d)
 =P\{Y(d)=j\mid A=d\}
 =P\{Y(d)=j\}
 =p_{dj}.
\]
Thus the observable map is
\[
 P_{\mathrm{obs}}(d,j)=\pi_d p_{dj},
 \qquad d\in\mathcal D,\quad j\in\mathcal Y_L,
\]
and the observed law identifies all \(3L\) arm probabilities (subject to the three adding-up restrictions).

It remains to distinguish this observed-law conclusion from the causal identified set.  Define
\[
 q_P(u)=P\{(Y(0),Y(1),Y(2))=u\},\qquad u\in\mathcal U_L.
\]
Weak individual unimodality implies that \(q_P\in\Delta(\mathcal U_L)\), and the preceding marginal identity implies
\[
 \sum_{u:u_d=j}q_P(u)=p_{dj}.
\]
Hence \(q_P\in\Gamma_L(p)\).  By the definition of the unique-peak indicators,
\[
 P\{Y(d)>Y(d')\text{ for every }d'\ne d\}
 =\sum_{u\in\mathcal U_L}B_d(u)q_P(u)=\theta_d(q_P).
\]
This proves the inclusion of the causal identified set in \(\{\theta(q):q\in\Gamma_L(p)\}\).

For the reverse inclusion, fix any \(q\in\Gamma_L(p)\).  Draw \((Y(0),Y(1),Y(2))\) with mass function \(q\), draw \(A\) independently with probabilities \((\pi_0,\pi_1,\pi_2)\), and set \(Y=Y(A)\).  This construction satisfies random assignment, consistency, positivity, and the support restriction, and it has
\[
 P(A=d,Y=j)=\pi_d\sum_{u:u_d=j}q(u)=\pi_dp_{dj}=P_{\mathrm{obs}}(d,j).
\]
It also respects the prespecified score construction.  Indeed, choose \(t_0<c_1\) and \(t_j=c_j\) for \(1\le j<L\), and set \(H(d)=0\) and \(E(d)=t_{Y(d)}/w_E\).  Since \(w_E>0\), the resulting benefit-risk utility is \(t_{Y(d)}\), and exactly \(Y(d)\) of the thresholds \(c_1,\ldots,c_{L-1}\) are weakly below it.  Independent replication gives the sampling law in \(\text{ass:iid-sampling}\).  The constructed law therefore belongs to \(\mathcal P_L\), has the same observable distribution, and has target \(\theta(q)\).  Consequently, with \(\Theta_I(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\) denoting the causal identified set,
\[
 \Theta_I(P_{\mathrm{obs}})=\{\theta(q):q\in\Gamma_L(p)\}.
\]

Write \(n=L-1\).  We first prove full dimensionality by the affine-annihilator technique.  Let
\[
 f(x,b,z)=k+\rho_0B_0(x,b,z)+\rho_1B_1(x,b,z)+\rho_2B_2(x,b,z)
             +A_x+D_b+C_z,
\]
where \(A_0=D_0=C_0=0\), and suppose that \(f\) vanishes on every legal response triple.  The legal triples
\[
 (0,0,0),\ (0,0,j),\ (0,j,j),\ (j,j,j),\ (j,j,0),\ (0,j,0),
 \qquad 1\le j\le n,
\]
give, in that order,
\[
 k=0,quad C_j+\rho_2=0,quad D_j+C_j=0,quad
 A_j+D_j+C_j=0,quad A_j+D_j=0,quad D_j+\rho_1=0.
\]
It follows successively that \(D_j=C_j=A_j=0\) for every \(j\), and that \(\rho_1=\rho_2=0\).  The legal triple \((1,0,0)\) then gives \(\rho_0=0\).  Thus no nonzero affine functional vanishes on all vertices.  Since the ambient space has dimension \(3+3(L-1)=3L\),
\[
 \dim(Q_{3,L})=3L.
\]

Now let \(n\ge2\), let \(\omega\in\{\mathrm L,\mathrm R,\mathrm D\}^{n-1}\) end in \(\mathrm D\), and use the sequences in \(\text{def:staircase-family}\).  Extend their notation by
\[
 a_n=c_n=K,qquad d_n=2K+1.
\]
At each \(j<n\), \(d_j>a_{j-1},c_{j-1}\), both \((a_j)\) and \((c_j)\) are nondecreasing, and
\[
 \max(a_j,c_j)=d_j,qquad 0\le a_j,c_j\le K.
\]
For a legal triple \((x,b,z)\), the proposed row has slack
\[
 s(x,b,z)=K-a_x+d_b-c_z-(K+1)B_1(x,b,z).
\]
Here the coefficients of \(B_0\) and \(B_2\) are zero.  If \(x>b\), non-valley support forces \(z\le b<n\), and monotonicity gives
\[
 s(x,b,z)=K-a_x+d_b-c_z\ge d_b-c_b\ge0.
\]
The case \(z>b\) is symmetric.  If \(b>x,z\), then
\[
 s(x,b,z)=d_b-a_x-c_z-1
 \ge d_b-a_{b-1}-c_{b-1}-1=0,
\]
where the displayed equality is also valid at \(b=n\) by the definitions of \(a_n,c_n,d_n\).  In the remaining case there is no unique peak, \(x,z\le b\), and at least one of \(x,z\) equals \(b\).  For \(b<n\),
\[
 s(x,b,z)\ge K+d_b-a_b-c_b=K-\min(a_b,c_b)\ge0,
\]
whereas for \(b=n\), \(s(x,b,z)\ge K+1\).  These four cases exhaust \(\mathcal U_L\), so the row is valid.  Since \(d_1=1\) and the first letter raises at least one endpoint sequence from zero to one, one coefficient is \(-1\); hence the integer row is primitive.

We next use a tight-row rank certificate to prove facetness.  Let an arbitrary affine row
\[
 g(x,b,z)=k+uB_0+vB_1+wB_2+A_x+D_b+C_z
\]
vanish on all of the following legal tight triples:
\[
 (n,0,0),\ (n-1,0,0),\ (0,0,n),\ (0,0,n-1),\ (n-1,n-1,n-1),
\]
\[
 (j-1,j,j-1),\quad 1\le j\le n,
\]
\[
 (j,j,n)\quad\text{when }\omega_j\in\{\mathrm L,\mathrm D\},
 \qquad
 (n,j,j)\quad\text{when }\omega_j\in\{\mathrm R,\mathrm D\},
 \quad 1\le j<n,
\]
and, for \(j<n-1\), also
\[
 (j,j+1,j-1)\quad\text{when }\omega_j=\mathrm L,
 \qquad
 (j-1,j+1,j)\quad\text{when }\omega_j=\mathrm R.
\]
Their tightness follows directly from \(d_j=a_{j-1}+c_{j-1}+1\), from the update specified by the relevant letter, and from \(a_{n-1}=c_{n-1}=K\).

The first four triples imply
\[
 A_n=A_{n-1},\quad C_n=C_{n-1},\quad
 k+A_n+u=0,\quad k+C_n+w=0.
\]
Comparison with \((0,0,n)\) shows \(A_j=-D_j\) for each letter \(\mathrm L\) or \(\mathrm D\), while comparison with \((n,0,0)\) shows \(C_j=-D_j\) for each letter \(\mathrm R\) or \(\mathrm D\).  Because the final letter is \(\mathrm D\), the all-\((n-1)\) triple yields
\[
 D_{n-1}=k,qquad A_n=C_n=-k,qquad u=w=0.
\]
Comparing each additional \(\mathrm L\)-triple with \((j,j+1,j)\) gives \(C_j=C_{j-1}\); the analogous \(\mathrm R\)-comparison gives \(A_j=A_{j-1}\).  Finally, the peak triple \((j-1,j,j-1)\) gives
\[
 D_j=-A_{j-1}-C_{j-1}-k-v.
\]
Set \(\lambda=-k-v\).  Induction over the letters therefore gives
\[
 (A_j,D_j,C_j)=\lambda(-a_j,d_j,-c_j),\qquad 1\le j<n.
\]
At \(j=n-1\), this identity and \(D_{n-1}=k\) imply \(k=K\lambda\), hence \(v=-(K+1)\lambda\).  The peak equation at \(j=n\) then gives
\[
 D_n=(2K+1)\lambda,qquad A_n=C_n=-K\lambda.
\]
Thus the annihilator of the displayed tight augmented vertex rows is exactly the one-dimensional span of the proposed row.  Those rows consequently have rank \(3L\), so their affine span has dimension \(3L-1\).  Full dimensionality and validity now show that the proposed row defines a facet.  Moreover, at \((0,n,n)\),
\[
 s(0,n,n)=2K+1\ge3,
\]
so the facet is weighted and belongs to \(\mathcal W_L\).

The word is recovered from the row uniquely: at position \(j<n\), exactly \(a_j\) increases for \(\mathrm L\), exactly \(c_j\) increases for \(\mathrm R\), and both increase for \(\mathrm D\).  Hence the construction is injective.  Fixing the last letter leaves \(n-2=L-3\) free letters and therefore gives \(3^{L-3}\) distinct weighted facets.

For the all-\(\mathrm D\) word, \(a_j=c_j=d_j\) and
\[
 d_j=2d_{j-1}+1,qquad d_0=0,
\]
so \(d_j=2^j-1\), including \(d_n=2^n-1\).  For an alternating \(\mathrm L,\mathrm R\) prefix, put \(x_j=a_j+1\) and \(y_j=c_j+1\).  An \(\mathrm L\) step sends \((x,y)\) to \((x+y,y)\), and an \(\mathrm R\) step sends it to \((x,x+y)\).  Starting from \((1,1)\), after \(m=L-3\) alternating steps the two entries are consecutive Fibonacci numbers whose sum is \(\operatorname{Fib}(m+3)=\operatorname{Fib}(L)\).  The terminal \(\mathrm D\) step therefore has
\[
 K=a_{n-2}+c_{n-2}+1=x_{n-2}+y_{n-2}-1=\operatorname{Fib}(L)-1.
\]

%%% FIELD proof-sharp-three-facet-endpoints
Fix \(L\ge2\).  By \(\text{thm:dimension-and-staircase-facets}\), \(Q_{3,L}\) is full dimensional.  Its vertices are integral.  Consequently every facet hyperplane has a rational normal, which can be scaled and divided by its coordinate greatest common divisor to give exactly one valid primitive canonical row in \(\mathcal H_L\).  These facet inequalities describe the whole polytope.  To see the last assertion directly, take an interior point \(x_0\) and a point \(x\notin Q_{3,L}\).  The first boundary point on the segment from \(x_0\) to \(x\) lies on a facet; that facet's affine slack is positive at \(x_0\), zero at the boundary point, and negative at \(x\).  Thus every exterior point violates a member of \(\mathcal H_L\).

For compatible \(p\), define the nonempty compact section
\[
 K(p)=\{\vartheta\in\mathbb R^3:(\vartheta,p)\in Q_{3,L}\}
     =\{\vartheta:a_i(p)+\beta_i^\top\vartheta\ge0\text{ for every }i\}.
\]
The equality with the attainable target section follows from the convex-hull definition: convex weights on the vertices are precisely masses \(q\in\Gamma_L(p)\), and their first three coordinates are \(\theta(q)\).  In particular,
\[
 \underline\theta_d(p)=\min_{\vartheta\in K(p)}e_d^\top\vartheta,
 \qquad
 \overline\theta_d(p)=\max_{\vartheta\in K(p)}e_d^\top\vartheta.
\]

Let \(\vartheta^-\) minimize the first display's objective and let \(I^-\) be its active facet set.  We claim that
\[
 e_d\in\operatorname{cone}\{\beta_i:i\in I^-\}.
\]
This normal-cone assertion has an elementary projection proof.  If the claim failed, project \(e_d\) onto the finitely generated closed cone \(C=\operatorname{cone}\{\beta_i:i\in I^-\}\), calling the projection \(c\), and put \(z=c-e_d\).  The projection inequalities, applied also to \(0\) and \(2c\), give
\[
 \beta_i^\top z\ge0\quad(i\in I^-),
 \qquad e_d^\top z=-\lVert e_d-c\rVert^2<0.
\]
All inactive slacks are strictly positive, so \(\vartheta^-+\varepsilon z\in K(p)\) for sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\), contradicting minimality.  Hence there are nonnegative \(\lambda_i\), supported on \(I^-\), with \(\sum_i\lambda_i\beta_i=e_d\).

Any linear dependence among the positive-support \(\beta_i\)'s can be removed while preserving nonnegativity: if \(\sum_i r_i\beta_i=0\), change the sign of \(r\) so that some \(r_i>0\), and replace \(\lambda\) by \(\lambda-t r\), where
\[
 t=\min_{i:r_i>0}\frac{\lambda_i}{r_i}.
\]
At least one positive coefficient becomes zero and none becomes negative.  Iteration leaves linearly independent support, hence support size at most three.  Because every retained facet is active at \(\vartheta^-\),
\[
 -\sum_i\lambda_i a_i(p)
 =\sum_i\lambda_i\beta_i^\top\vartheta^-
 =e_d^\top\vartheta^-
 =\underline\theta_d(p).
\]
Conversely, every \(\lambda\in\mathcal D_d^-\) gives, for every \(\vartheta\in K(p)\),
\[
 0\le\sum_i\lambda_i\{a_i(p)+\beta_i^\top\vartheta\}
   =\sum_i\lambda_i a_i(p)+\vartheta_d,
\]
so it supplies a valid lower bound.  These two directions prove
\[
 \underline\theta_d(p)=\max_{\lambda\in\mathcal D_d^-}
          \left\{-\sum_i\lambda_i a_i(p)\right\}.
\]
Applying the same argument to the maximizer, or equivalently to the objective \(-e_d\), gives an independent-support vector \(\mu\ge0\) with \(\sum_i\mu_i\beta_i=-e_d\) and proves
\[
 \overline\theta_d(p)=\min_{\mu\in\mathcal D_d^+}
          \sum_i\mu_i a_i(p).
\]
There are only finitely many subsets of at most three facets, and on a linearly independent subset the coefficient solution is unique.  Thus the maxima and minima are over finite, computable lists and no division by a singular submatrix is used.  Together with \(\text{thm:randomization-identifies-marginals}\), these are sharp causal identified endpoints, not merely outer bounds.

It remains to prove that three cannot be replaced uniformly by two.  At \(L=2\), write \(x_d=p_{d1}\).  The seven legal response types are
\[
 000,001,010,011,100,110,111.
\]
Solving the affine vertex map gives the seven simplex coordinates
\[
\begin{aligned}
 q_{000}&=1-x_1-\theta_0-\theta_2,&q_{001}&=\theta_2,&q_{010}&=\theta_1,\\
 q_{011}&=x_1-x_0-\theta_1+\theta_0,&q_{100}&=\theta_0,\\
 q_{110}&=x_1-x_2-\theta_1+\theta_2,&
 q_{111}&=x_0+x_2-x_1-\theta_0-\theta_2+\theta_1.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus their seven canonical full-facet \(\beta\)-normals, in the same order, are
\[
 (-1,0,-1),(0,0,1),(0,1,0),(1,-1,0),(1,0,0),(0,-1,1),(-1,1,-1).
\]
The only normals having a negative second coordinate are \((1,-1,0)\) and \((0,-1,1)\).  With only one of them, cancellation of its positive first or third coordinate requires a normal with a negative first or third coordinate, and the displayed list shows that the remaining uncancelled coordinate cannot be zero.  Using both gives \((r,-r-s,s)\), whose first and third coordinates vanish only when \(r=s=0\).  Hence no nonnegative combination of at most two full-facet normals equals \(-e_1\).  On the other hand,
\[
 \frac12\{(-1,0,-1)+(1,-1,0)+(0,-1,1)\}=-e_1.
\]
The associated affine terms give
\[
 \theta_1\le\frac{1+x_1-x_0-x_2}{2}.
\]
At \(x_0=x_1=x_2=1/2\), this is \(\theta_1\le1/4\), and the mixture assigning mass \(1/4\) to each of \(001,010,100,111\) attains equality.  Therefore the sharp upper endpoint is \(1/4\), and every attaining full-facet certificate needs at least three normals.

Index the legal triples by \(u\in\mathcal U_L\).  Let \(M\) be the \((3L-2)\)-by-\(|\mathcal U_L|\) zero-one matrix whose first row is one and whose other rows are \(\mathbf 1\{u_d=j\}\) for \(d\in\mathcal D\) and \(1\le j<L\).  Put
\[
 b(p)=(1,(p_{dj})_{d\in\mathcal D,\,1\le j<L})^\top.
\]
Then the coupling fiber is exactly
\[
 \Gamma_L(p)=\{q\ge0:Mq=b(p)\}.
\]
Compatibility makes this a nonempty compact polytope.  A linear objective \(\theta_d(q)=\sum_uB_d(u)q(u)\) therefore attains both its minimum and its maximum, and an optimizing face contains an extreme point \(q^\star\).

Let \(S=\operatorname{supp}(q^\star)\).  The columns \((M_u)_{u\in S}\) are linearly independent.  Otherwise there is a nonzero vector \(z\), supported on \(S\), with \(Mz=0\).  For sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\), both \(q^\star+\varepsilon z\) and \(q^\star-\varepsilon z\) are nonnegative and feasible, expressing \(q^\star\) as the midpoint of two distinct feasible points and contradicting extremality.  Hence
\[
 |\operatorname{supp}(q^\star)|\le\operatorname{rank}(M)\le3L-2.
\]
This proves attainment and the asserted support bound for either endpoint.

The active facet certificate gives a direct construction.  For any facet row \(h_i\), marginalization yields
\[
 a_i(p)+\beta_i^\top\theta(q)=\sum_{u\in\mathcal U_L}s_{h_i}(u)q(u).
\]
At an endpoint, equality in the optimal nonnegative facet combination implies that the right side is zero for every facet having a positive certificate coefficient.  Since each slack and every mass are nonnegative, complementary slackness forces
\[
 q(u)=0\quad\text{unless}\quad s_{h_i}(u)=0
 \text{ for every certificate facet }i\text{ with positive weight}.
\]
Restrict \(M\) to these common tight response types and solve \(Mq=b(p)\), \(q\ge0\), selecting an extreme feasible solution by successively eliminating dependent positive-support columns as above.  This is an explicit finite exact-arithmetic construction of \(q^\star\).

If \(p\) is rational, then \(b(p)\) is rational and \(M\) is integral.  For the independent support \(S\), select \(|S|\) independent rows of \(M_S\).  The resulting nonsingular integer square system has the unique solution \(q_S^\star\); Cramer's rule makes every mass rational.  Thus the complementary-slackness construction has rational masses whenever \(p\) is rational.

 000,001,010,011,100,110,111.

The finite-sample coverage portion can be completed for the finite contrast lists.  Conditionally on pilot arm counts, form \(R_N\) by intersecting the three simplices with exact binomial confidence intervals for all \(3L\) cells, using total error \(\delta_N\); a union bound gives
\[
 P_p\{p\in R_N\}\ge1-\delta_N,
\]
including empty pilot arms by assigning their full simplices.  For a retained affine contrast \(\ell_r(p)\), main-sample arm counts \(m=(m_0,m_1,m_2)\), and error \(\eta_r\), define
\[
 c_r(t,R_N,\eta_r)=\inf\left\{c\ge0:
 \inf_{\substack{\widetilde p\in R_N\\ \ell_r(\widetilde p)=t}}
 P_{\widetilde p}\bigl(|\widehat\ell_r-t|\le c\mid m\bigr)
 \ge1-\eta_r\right\}.
\]
The conditional sample space is finite, the null slice is compact, and its probabilities are multinomial polynomials, so the displayed least-favorable quantile exists; an empty null slice is rejected.  If an arm needed by the contrast is empty, use the contrast's full simplex range.  Inverting these tests gives exact conditional contrast sets.  Allocate \(\sum_r\eta_r\le\alpha-\delta_N\), take closed interval hulls, and apply the finite maximum/minimum endpoint formulas.  On \(\{p\in R_N\}\), a union bound covers every retained contrast and hence all six endpoints with conditional probability at least \(1-\alpha+\delta_N\).  Adding pilot failure proves

 \inf_{P\in\mathcal P_L}P\{(\underline\theta_d(p),\overline\theta_d(p))_{d\in\mathcal D}
 \in\mathcal I_{N,1-\alpha}\}\ge1-\alpha
\]
for every \(N\), with zero cells, ties, and empty arms retained.  What remains unproved is the simultaneous procedure's claimed scalar Wald equivalence under data-dependent localization.

 \mathcal D_d^+=
 \left\{\mu\in\mathbb R_+^{\mathcal H_L}:
 |\operatorname{supp}(\mu)|\le3,\
