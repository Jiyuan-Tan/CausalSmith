%%% FIELD cited-lp-statement
For a finite-dimensional primal--dual linear-programming pair in standard form, if the primal has an optimal solution, then the dual has an optimal solution and the two optimal values are equal.
%%% FIELD direct-dual-statement
Fix \(L\ge2\).  Let \(G_L\) be the \(3L\)-by-\(|\mathcal U_L|\) legal-response incidence matrix with rows indexed by \((r,j)\in\mathcal D\times\mathcal Y_L\), columns indexed by \(u\in\mathcal U_L\), and entries \((G_L)_{(r,j),u}=\mathbf 1\{u_r=j\}\).  For \(d\in\mathcal D\), let \(c^{(d)}_u=B_d(u)\), and regard \(p=(p_{rj})_{r,j}\) here as the full \(3L\)-vector of arm probabilities.  For every compatible \(p\),
\[
 \underline\theta_d(p)=
 \max_{y\in\mathbb R^{3L}}
 \left\{\sum_{r\in\mathcal D}\sum_{j\in\mathcal Y_L}p_{rj}y_{rj}:
 \sum_{r\in\mathcal D}y_{r,u_r}\le B_d(u)\quad\forall u\in\mathcal U_L\right\},
\]
and
\[
 \overline\theta_d(p)=
 \min_{z\in\mathbb R^{3L}}
 \left\{\sum_{r\in\mathcal D}\sum_{j\in\mathcal Y_L}p_{rj}z_{rj}:
 \sum_{r\in\mathcal D}z_{r,u_r}\ge B_d(u)\quad\forall u\in\mathcal U_L\right\}.
\]
Both dual optima are attained.  Moreover, feasibility of either dual candidate has an \(O(L)\) exact separation oracle.  For arrays \(x=(x_{rj})\) and \(\sigma\in\{-1,1\}\), all prefix and suffix maxima can be computed in two passes, after which
\[
 \max_{u\in\mathcal U_L}\left\{\sum_{r\in\mathcal D}x_{r,u_r}-\sigma B_d(u)\right\}
\]
is the maximum, over \(k\in\mathcal Y_L\), of five explicitly evaluable region scores corresponding to \(u_0>k,u_2\le k\); \(u_2>k,u_0\le k\); \(u_0<k,u_2<k\); \(u_0=k,u_2\le k\); and \(u_0<k,u_2=k\).  Thus scalar endpoints can be computed by a finite linear program with this separator, using exact rational arithmetic whenever \(p\) is rational.  The complete all-\(L\) canonical facet stream is needed to catalogue the joint polytope, but is not needed merely to evaluate a scalar endpoint.
%%% FIELD direct-dual-proof
Fix \(L\ge2\), a compatible marginal vector \(p\), and \(d\in\mathcal D\).  By \(\text{def:coupling-fiber}\), compatibility means that the system
\[
 G_Lq=p,\qquad q\ge0
\]
has a solution supported on \(\mathcal U_L\).  Summing any one arm's \(L\) marginal equations gives \(\sum_u q(u)=1\); hence its feasible set is a closed subset of the probability simplex and is compact.  By \(\text{def:unique-peak-indicators}\), its lower-endpoint primal program is
\[
 \min_{q\ge0}\left\{(c^{(d)})^\top q:G_Lq=p\right\}.
\]
It has an optimizer and a value in \([0,1]\).  The standard-form dual is
\[
 \max_{y\in\mathbb R^{3L}}\left\{p^\top y:G_L^\top y\le c^{(d)}\right\}.
\]
The equality constraint makes \(y\) unrestricted in sign.  Applying \(\text{lem:finite-lp-strong-duality}\) gives attainment and equality of the two displayed values.  In coordinates, \((G_L^\top y)_u=\sum_r y_{r,u_r}\), which proves the stated lower formula.  Applying the same theorem to the maximization primal
\[
 \max_{q\ge0}\left\{(c^{(d)})^\top q:G_Lq=p\right\}
\]
gives the dual minimum with constraints \(G_L^\top z\ge c^{(d)}\), proving the upper formula.  These are observed-law functionals only on the compatibility domain; their equality to sharp causal endpoints follows from the separately defined coupling fiber, rather than by definition.

It remains to derive the separator.  For \(r\in\{0,2\}\) and \(k\in\mathcal Y_L\), define
\[
 P_r^{\le}(k)=\max_{0\le j\le k}x_{rj},\qquad
 P_r^{<}(k)=\max_{0\le j<k}x_{rj},\qquad
 S_r^{>}(k)=\max_{k<j<L}x_{rj},
\]
where a maximum over the empty set is \(-\infty\).  Prefix and suffix recursions compute all these quantities using a fixed number of arithmetic operations per level.  For a fixed middle value \(u_1=k\), the legal-response condition in \(\text{def:non-valley-support}\) is \(u_0\le k\) or \(u_2\le k\).  Its legal pairs \((u_0,u_2)\) are the following five disjoint regions:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{region}&\text{conditions}&B_d(u)\\ \hline
R_0&u_0>k,\ u_2\le k&\mathbf 1\{d=0\}\\
R_2&u_2>k,\ u_0\le k&\mathbf 1\{d=2\}\\
R_1&u_0<k,\ u_2<k&\mathbf 1\{d=1\}\\
R_{01}&u_0=k,\ u_2\le k&0\\
R_{12}&u_0<k,\ u_2=k&0.
\end{array}
\]
Indeed, if \(u_0>k\), legality forces \(u_2\le k\), giving \(R_0\); if \(u_2>k\), legality forces \(u_0\le k\), giving \(R_2\); and when both coordinates are at most \(k\), the three mutually exclusive possibilities are both strict, \(u_0=k\), or \(u_0<k,u_2=k\).  Maximizing the additive score separately on each region therefore gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 V_{0d}(k)&=S_0^{>}(k)+x_{1k}+P_2^{\le}(k)-\sigma\mathbf 1\{d=0\},\\
 V_{2d}(k)&=P_0^{\le}(k)+x_{1k}+S_2^{>}(k)-\sigma\mathbf 1\{d=2\},\\
 V_{1d}(k)&=P_0^{<}(k)+x_{1k}+P_2^{<}(k)-\sigma\mathbf 1\{d=1\},\\
 V_{01,d}(k)&=x_{0k}+x_{1k}+P_2^{\le}(k),\\
 V_{12,d}(k)&=P_0^{<}(k)+x_{1k}+x_{2k}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \max_{u\in\mathcal U_L}\left\{\sum_rx_{r,u_r}-\sigma B_d(u)\right\}
 =\max_{k\in\mathcal Y_L}\max\{V_{0d}(k),V_{2d}(k),V_{1d}(k),V_{01,d}(k),V_{12,d}(k)\}.
\]
For the lower dual use \(x=y\) and \(\sigma=1\); feasibility is equivalent to this maximum being at most zero.  For the upper dual use \(x=-z\) and \(\sigma=-1\), because its maximum is \(\max_u\{B_d(u)-\sum_rz_{r,u_r}\}\).  A maximizing region also returns a maximizing legal triple, hence a violated constraint when the maximum is positive.  Adding such constraints to a finite constraint-generation linear program must terminate after at most \(|\mathcal U_L|\) distinct additions; alternatively all returned constraints may be enumerated and solved at once.  All data are integral or rational when \(p\) is rational.  This proves the exact evaluation algorithm and, because it uses only \(G_L\), \(B_d\), and the five-region support partition, proves the asserted independence from a complete canonical-facet stream.
%%% FIELD sharp-target-proof
Fix \(L\ge2\).  By \(\text{thm:dimension-and-staircase-facets}\), \(Q_{3,L}\) has dimension \(3L\).  Each of its vertices is integral.  A facet contains \(3L\) affinely independent integral vertices, so solving the corresponding homogeneous affine system gives a nonzero rational normal.  Scaling to integers and dividing by the greatest common divisor, with the valid orientation and the normalization \(\alpha_{d0}=0\), gives exactly one row in \(\mathcal H_L\).  Conversely, every member of \(\mathcal H_L\) is valid and its tight vertices have affine dimension \(3L-1\), so it is a facet row.

These rows describe the whole polytope.  Indeed, take an interior point \(x_0\in Q_{3,L}\) and any \(x\notin Q_{3,L}\).  Compactness of the convex hull implies that the segment from \(x_0\) toward \(x\) has a first boundary point.  Every boundary point of a full-dimensional polytope lies on a facet; the oriented slack of that facet is positive at \(x_0\), zero at the boundary point, and negative farther along the segment at \(x\).  Thus every exterior point violates a row in \(\mathcal H_L\).

For compatible \(p\), define the section
\[
 K(p)=\{\vartheta\in\mathbb R^3:(\vartheta,p)\in Q_{3,L}\}
 =\{\vartheta:a_i(p)+\beta_i^\top\vartheta\ge0\text{ for every }i\in\mathcal H_L\}.
\]
It is nonempty and compact.  Convex weights on the vertices \(v_L(u)\) are exactly probability masses \(q\) on \(\mathcal U_L\); fixing their marginal coordinates to \(p\) imposes exactly \(q\in\Gamma_L(p)\), and their first three coordinates equal \(\theta(q)\).  Hence
\[
 K(p)=\{\theta(q):q\in\Gamma_L(p)\}.
\]
This agrees with the support-constrained primal representation in \(\text{thm:direct-support-transport-dual}\).  In particular,
\[
 \underline\theta_d(p)=\min_{\vartheta\in K(p)}e_d^\top\vartheta,
 \qquad
 \overline\theta_d(p)=\max_{\vartheta\in K(p)}e_d^\top\vartheta.
\]

Apply \(\text{lem:finite-lp-strong-duality}\) to the first finite linear program.  Its dual has variables \(\lambda_i\ge0\) and is
\[
 \max_{\lambda\ge0}\left\{-\sum_i\lambda_i a_i(p):\sum_i\lambda_i\beta_i=e_d\right\}.
\]
Both primal and dual optimizers exist and their values agree.  Let \(\vartheta^-\) and \(\lambda\) be optimal.  Weak duality is obtained equation by equation from
\[
 0\le\sum_i\lambda_i\{a_i(p)+\beta_i^\top\vartheta\}
 =\sum_i\lambda_i a_i(p)+e_d^\top\vartheta.
\]
At \(\vartheta=\vartheta^-\), equality of the primal and dual values makes the left side zero.  Every summand is nonnegative; therefore every facet with \(\lambda_i>0\) is active at \(\vartheta^-\).

If the positive-support normals are linearly dependent, choose nonzero \(r=(r_i)\) on that support with \(\sum_i r_i\beta_i=0\), reverse its sign if necessary so that some \(r_i>0\), and set
\[
 t=\min_{i:r_i>0}\frac{\lambda_i}{r_i},\qquad \lambda'_i=\lambda_i-tr_i.
\]
Then \(\lambda'\ge0\), at least one positive coefficient is removed, and \(\sum_i\lambda'_i\beta_i=e_d\).  Since each index in the support is active,
\[
 a_i(p)=-\beta_i^\top\vartheta^-\quad(\lambda_i>0),
\]
and consequently
\[
 \sum_i r_i a_i(p)=-\left(\sum_i r_i\beta_i\right)^\top\vartheta^-=0.
\]
Thus the dual objective is unchanged.  Iteration leaves linearly independent support, which has size at most three in \(\mathbb R^3\).  The resulting vector belongs to \(\mathcal D_d^-\), and strong duality gives
\[
 \underline\theta_d(p)=\max_{\lambda\in\mathcal D_d^-}
 \left\{-\sum_i\lambda_i a_i(p)\right\}.
\]
Conversely, the displayed weak-duality calculation shows directly that every member of \(\mathcal D_d^-\) supplies a valid lower bound, so no certificate outside this set is needed.

Applying the same argument to minimizing \(-e_d^\top\vartheta\) yields nonnegative \(\mu\) with \(\sum_i\mu_i\beta_i=-e_d\).  Sparsification on the active facets preserves its value and gives
\[
 \overline\theta_d(p)=\min_{\mu\in\mathcal D_d^+}\sum_i\mu_i a_i(p).
\]
There are finitely many supports of size at most three.  On a linearly independent support the coefficient vector solving the normal equation is unique.  Hence \(\mathcal D_d^-\) and \(\mathcal D_d^+\) are finite computable lists, and the formulas are sharp rather than outer bounds.  Together with \(\text{thm:randomization-identifies-marginals}\), their arguments are identified from the observed randomized-arm law, while the target remains the causal unique-best probability over compatible couplings.

It remains to prove that three cannot be replaced uniformly by two.  At \(L=2\), write \(x_d=p_{d1}\).  The seven legal response types are
\[
 000,001,010,011,100,110,111.
\]
Solving the affine vertex map gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 q_{000}&=1-x_1-\theta_0-\theta_2,&q_{001}&=\theta_2,&q_{010}&=\theta_1,\\
 q_{011}&=x_1-x_0-\theta_1+\theta_0,&q_{100}&=\theta_0,\\
 q_{110}&=x_1-x_2-\theta_1+\theta_2,&
 q_{111}&=x_0+x_2-x_1-\theta_0-\theta_2+\theta_1.
\end{aligned}
\]
The seven inequalities \(q_u\ge0\) are the canonical full facets because the seven displayed response vertices form a six-dimensional simplex.  Their \(\beta\)-normals, in the same order, are
\[
 (-1,0,-1),(0,0,1),(0,1,0),(1,-1,0),(1,0,0),(0,-1,1),(-1,1,-1).
\]
Only \((1,-1,0)\) and \((0,-1,1)\) have a negative second coordinate.  A nonnegative combination using just one of them must cancel its positive first or third coordinate with a normal having a negative coordinate there; inspection of the list shows that the other outer coordinate then cannot also vanish.  A combination using both has the form \((r,-r-s,s)\), whose first and third coordinates vanish only for \(r=s=0\).  Therefore no nonnegative combination of at most two full-facet normals equals \(-e_1\).  In contrast,
\[
 \frac12\{(-1,0,-1)+(1,-1,0)+(0,-1,1)\}=-e_1.
\]
The associated affine terms imply
\[
 \theta_1\le\frac{1+x_1-x_0-x_2}{2}.
\]
At \(x_0=x_1=x_2=1/2\), this gives \(\theta_1\le1/4\).  The compatible mixture assigning mass \(1/4\) to each of \(001,010,100,111\) has all three Bernoulli marginals equal to \(1/2\) and has \(\theta_1=1/4\).  The bound is therefore attained and sharp, while the normal calculation proves that every attaining full-facet certificate uses at least three normals.

Fix \(L\ge2\), and write \(x=(x_{rj})_{r\in\mathcal D,\,1\le j<L}\in\mathbb R^{3(L-1)}\).  For \(h_i\in\mathcal H_L\), put \(a_i(x)=\kappa_i+\sum_{r\in\mathcal D}\sum_{j=1}^{L-1}\alpha_{irj}x_{rj}\), and define the global affine extensions
\[
 F_{d,L}^-(x)=\max_{\lambda\in\mathcal D_d^-}-\sum_i\lambda_i a_i(x),
 \qquad
 F_{d,L}^+(x)=\min_{\mu\in\mathcal D_d^+}\sum_i\mu_i a_i(x).
\]
For any norm \(\|\cdot\|_r\) with \(1\le r\le\infty\) and dual exponent \(r^*\), define
\[
 C_{L,r}=\max_{d\in\mathcal D}\max\left\{
 \max_{\lambda\in\mathcal D_d^-}
 \left\|\left(\sum_i\lambda_i\alpha_{ikj}\right)_{k,j}\right\|_{r^*},
 \max_{\mu\in\mathcal D_d^+}
 \left\|\left(\sum_i\mu_i\alpha_{ikj}\right)_{k,j}\right\|_{r^*}
 \right\}.
\]
This constant is finite and computable from the fixed-\(L\) certificate lists, and for all \(x,x'\in\mathbb R^{3(L-1)}\),
\[
 \max_{d\in\mathcal D,\,s\in\{-,+\}}
 |F_{d,L}^s(x)-F_{d,L}^s(x')|\le C_{L,r}\|x-x'\|_r.
\]
On compatible marginals, \(F_{d,L}^-(p)=\underline\theta_d(p)\) and \(F_{d,L}^+(p)=\overline\theta_d(p)\).

Under independent sampling, let \(N_d=\sum_{i=1}^N\mathbf 1\{A_i=d\}\), let \(\widehat p_{dj}=N_d^{-1}\sum_i\mathbf 1\{A_i=d,Y_i=j\}\) when \(N_d>0\), and assign any simplex value when \(N_d=0\).  Put \(\pi_{\min}=\min_d\pi_d\).  For every \(M>0\),
\[
 P\left\{
 \max_{d,s}|F_{d,L}^s(\widehat p)-F_{d,L}^s(p)|>
 \frac{C_{L,2}M}{\sqrt{N\pi_{\min}}}
 \right\}
 \le \frac{6}{M^2}+\frac{12}{N\pi_{\min}}.
\]
Consequently, whenever \(N\pi_{\min}\to\infty\), every plug-in endpoint extension has error \(O_P((N\pi_{\min})^{-1/2})\); in particular, at a fixed law satisfying positive randomization the rate is \(N^{-1/2}\), with constant proportional to \(C_{L,2}/\sqrt{\pi_{\min}}\).
%%% FIELD lipschitz-proof
By \(\text{thm:sharp-three-facet-endpoints}\), each certificate set is finite and the displayed extensions agree with the sharp endpoints whenever the coupling fiber is nonempty.  For a lower certificate \(\lambda\), the gradient of its affine contrast is
\[
 -\left(\sum_i\lambda_i\alpha_{ikj}\right)_{k\in\mathcal D,\,1\le j<L},
\]
and for an upper certificate \(\mu\) the gradient is the same expression without the minus sign.  Hölder's inequality therefore gives, for every individual contrast \(f\),
\[
 |f(x)-f(x')|\le\|\nabla f\|_{r^*}\|x-x'\|_r.
\]
For any finite families of real numbers \((a_t)\) and \((b_t)\),
\[
 |\max_t a_t-\max_t b_t|\le\max_t|a_t-b_t|,
 \qquad
 |\min_t a_t-\min_t b_t|\le\max_t|a_t-b_t|.
\]
Applying these inequalities to the finite lower and upper contrast lists and then maximizing their gradient norms proves the global Lipschitz display.  Finiteness and computability of \(C_{L,r}\) follow because each certificate list is finite and its entries are obtained by solving nonsingular systems of at most three normal vectors.

For the sampling claim, let
\[
 E_N=\bigcap_{d\in\mathcal D}\{N_d\ge N\pi_d/2\}.
\]
Since \(N_d\) is binomial with mean \(N\pi_d\) and variance \(N\pi_d(1-\pi_d)\), Chebyshev's inequality and a union bound give
\[
 P(E_N^c)\le\sum_{d\in\mathcal D}\frac{4(1-\pi_d)}{N\pi_d}
 \le\frac{12}{N\pi_{\min}}.
\]
Conditional on the arm counts, the outcome counts in arm \(d\) are multinomial with probabilities \((p_{dj})_j\).  On \(E_N\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 E\left(\|\widehat p-p\|_2^2\mid(N_d)_d\right)
 &=\sum_{d\in\mathcal D}\frac1{N_d}
   \sum_{j=1}^{L-1}p_{dj}(1-p_{dj})\\
 &\le\sum_{d\in\mathcal D}\frac1{N_d}
 \le\frac6{N\pi_{\min}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The first equality uses only the coordinate variances; covariances do not enter the squared Euclidean norm.  Markov's inequality, conditional on the counts and then averaged, yields
\[
 P\left\{\|\widehat p-p\|_2>\frac{M}{\sqrt{N\pi_{\min}}},\ E_N\right\}\le\frac6{M^2}.
\]
On \(E_N\), no arm is empty, so the arbitrary empty-arm convention is irrelevant.  Combining the last two probability bounds with the deterministic Lipschitz inequality proves the finite-\(N\) display.  The asserted stochastic rates follow directly.  This derivation uses the finite affine endpoint representation and elementary multinomial moments; it does not import a rate theorem for a different, quadratic transport objective.
%%% FIELD finite-inference-statement
For every fixed \(L\ge2\), \(N\ge1\), and \(\alpha\in(0,1)\), the exact pilot-localized handle in \(\text{def:inference-handle}\) has a completion producing a random closed rectangle \(\mathcal I_{N,1-\alpha}\) such that
\[
 \inf_{P\in\mathcal P_L}P\left\{
 (\underline\theta_d(p),\overline\theta_d(p))_{d\in\mathcal D}
 \in\mathcal I_{N,1-\alpha}\right\}\ge1-\alpha.
\]
The completion uses a pilot product-simplex region with total error \(\delta_N=\min\{\alpha/2,N^{-2}\}\), exact least-favourable conditional product-multinomial inversion over compact affine null slices on the main sample, a common Bonferroni allocation summing to at most \(\alpha-\delta_N\), closed interval hulls for every retained affine certificate contrast, and the finite endpoint maximum/minimum maps.  Empty pilot arms use full simplices and any main-sample contrast involving an empty required arm uses its full logical range.  Thus the coverage assertion includes zero cells, realized empty arms, and tied active facets.  This theorem asserts finite-sample honesty only; it does not assert regular-face equivalence to a Wald interval.
%%% FIELD finite-inference-proof
Fix \(L,N,\alpha\), and split the observations as in \(\text{def:inference-handle}\).  Write \(n_0=\lfloor N^{2/3}\rfloor\) for the pilot size and \(n_1=N-n_0\) for the main size.  The two subsamples are independent by \(\text{ass:iid-sampling}\).  Conditional on the arm counts in either subsample, randomization, consistency, and \(\text{thm:randomization-identifies-marginals}\) imply that the outcome-count vectors in the three arms are independent multinomials with respective cell probabilities \((p_{dj})_{j\in\mathcal Y_L}\).

Set \(\delta_N=\min\{\alpha/2,N^{-2}\}\) and \(\gamma_N=\delta_N/(3L)\).  For \(X\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,\rho)\) and an observed count \(k\), define the closed exact cell set
\[
 C_{n,k}(\gamma_N)=
 \left\{\rho\in[0,1]:
 P_\rho(X\le k)\ge\gamma_N/2,\quad
 P_\rho(X\ge k)\ge\gamma_N/2\right\}.
\]
If \(n=0\), define this set to be \([0,1]\).  For \(n>0\), the binomial tail probabilities are continuous in \(\rho\), so the set is closed.  If the true probability is \(\rho\), the event \(P_\rho(X\le X_{\mathrm{obs}})<\gamma_N/2\) has probability at most \(\gamma_N/2\): it is either empty or of the form \(\{X_{\mathrm{obs}}\le k_0\}\), whose probability is the corresponding binomial lower tail.  The same argument for the upper tail shows
\[
 P_\rho\{\rho\in C_{n,X_{\mathrm{obs}}}(\gamma_N)\}\ge1-\gamma_N.
\]

For pilot arm \(d\), intersect its \(L\) cell sets with the simplex \(\Delta^{L-1}\).  If the arm is empty, or if the resulting intersection is empty, replace it by the full simplex.  Let \(R_N\) be the Cartesian product of the three resulting nonempty compact sets.  Each multinomial cell count is marginally binomial conditional on its arm count.  Therefore a union bound over the \(3L\) cells, followed by averaging over pilot arm counts, gives
\[
 P_p\{p\in R_N\}\ge1-3L\gamma_N=1-\delta_N.
\]
This argument allows true zero cell probabilities, because the binomial tail inversion is valid on the boundary.

For each dose and endpoint side, use the finite affine lists from \(\text{thm:sharp-three-facet-endpoints}\).  Thus a retained contrast has the form
\[
 \ell_r(p)=b_r+\sum_{d\in\mathcal D}\sum_{j=1}^{L-1}g_{rdj}p_{dj}.
\]
For a lower endpoint, a contrast \(r_*\) is pilot-uniformly optimal when \(\ell_{r_*}(\widetilde p)\ge\ell_r(\widetilde p)\) for every \(\widetilde p\in R_N\) and every contrast \(r\) in that endpoint's list.  For an upper endpoint reverse the inequality.  If such a contrast exists, retain one; otherwise retain the entire finite list.  Consequently, throughout \(R_N\), the maximum or minimum over the retained list equals the corresponding full endpoint extension.  In particular this remains true when active contrasts tie, because then the entire list is retained unless one contrast dominates throughout the pilot region.

Condition now on the pilot data and on the main arm counts \(m=(m_0,m_1,m_2)\).  If \(m_d=0\) for an arm on which \(\ell_r\) has a nonzero coefficient, assign contrast \(r\) the full compact logical range
\[
 J_r=\ell_r\left(\prod_{d\in\mathcal D}\Delta^{L-1}\right),
\]
which is a closed interval and contains \(\ell_r(p)\) surely.  Otherwise define
\[
 \widehat\ell_r=b_r+\sum_{d,j}g_{rdj}\frac{M_{dj}}{m_d},
\]
omitting arms whose coefficient vector is zero.  Let \(K_N\) be the number of nontrivial retained contrast copies and, when \(K_N>0\), use the common allocation \(\eta_N=(\alpha-\delta_N)/K_N\).  When \(K_N=0\), every interval is already a full logical range and no error allocation is needed.

For a nontrivial contrast and \(t\in\ell_r(R_N)\), its null slice
\[
 \mathcal N_r(t)=\{\widetilde p\in R_N:\ell_r(\widetilde p)=t\}
\]
is nonempty and compact.  Define
\[
 c_r(t)=\inf\left\{c\ge0:
 \inf_{\widetilde p\in\mathcal N_r(t)}
 P_{\widetilde p}\left(|\widehat\ell_r-t|\le c\mid m\right)
 \ge1-\eta_N\right\}.
\]
The conditional count sample space is finite.  For every fixed acceptance set its product-multinomial probability is a polynomial in \(\widetilde p\), hence continuous, so the inner infimum is attained on the compact null slice.  The statistic has finitely many possible values conditional on \(m\); taking \(c\) equal to the largest possible absolute deviation gives probability one.  Thus the infimum defining \(c_r(t)\) is attained at one of finitely many possible radii and is finite.  Invert the exact tests by setting
\[
 C_r=\{t\in\ell_r(R_N):|\widehat\ell_r-t|\le c_r(t)\}.
\]
If \(C_r\ne\varnothing\), set \(J_r=\operatorname{cl}\operatorname{conv}(C_r)\); if \(C_r=\varnothing\), use the full logical contrast range \(\ell_r(\prod_d\Delta^{L-1})\).  If a proposed \(t\) has an empty null slice, it is not in \(\ell_r(R_N)\) and is rejected.  In every case \(J_r\) is a nonempty compact interval.  On the event \(p\in R_N\), putting \(t=\ell_r(p)\) in the defining least-favourable probability yields
\[
 P_p\{\ell_r(p)\in J_r\mid\text{pilot},m\}\ge1-\eta_N.
\]

Write \(J_r=[L_r,U_r]\).  For a lower endpoint, whose map is \(F^-_{d,L}(p)=\max_r\ell_r(p)\), use
\[
 I_d^-=[\max_rL_r,\max_rU_r]\cap[0,1].
\]
For an upper endpoint, whose map is \(F^+_{d,L}(p)=\min_r\ell_r(p)\), use
\[
 I_d^+=[\min_rL_r,\min_rU_r]\cap[0,1].
\]
Replace either intersection by \([0,1]\) if it is empty.  If every retained true contrast lies in its interval, the elementary monotonicity of maximum and minimum puts all six true endpoints in these six intervals.  The empty-intersection fallback can only enlarge a side.  Their Cartesian product is therefore a nonempty random closed rectangle \(\mathcal I_{N,1-\alpha}\subseteq[0,1]^6\).

On \(\{p\in R_N\}\), a conditional union bound over the \(K_N\) nontrivial contrast copies gives failure probability at most
\[
 K_N\eta_N=\alpha-\delta_N.
\]
Full-range contrasts contribute zero failure probability.  Finally, another union bound with the pilot failure event gives
\[
 P_p\left\{(\underline\theta_d(p),\overline\theta_d(p))_{d\in\mathcal D}
 \notin\mathcal I_{N,1-\alpha}\right\}
 \le\delta_N+(\alpha-\delta_N)=\alpha.
\]
The argument is conditional on arbitrary realized arm counts and uniform over every compatible \(p\); hence taking the infimum over \(P\in\mathcal P_L\) proves the theorem.  No limit approximation was used.  Conversely, this proof supplies no uniform normal approximation to the least-favourable quantiles over the shrinking random pilot region, so it does not establish the separate regular-face Wald-equivalence claim.
