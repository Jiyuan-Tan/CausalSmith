%%% FIELD small-catalogue-proof
Fix \(\ell\in\{2,3,4,5\}\), put \(D_\ell=3\ell\), and order \(\mathcal U_\ell\) lexicographically.  The augmented vertex row associated with \(u\in\mathcal U_\ell\) is
\[
r_\ell(u)=(1,v_\ell(u))\in\{0,1\}^{D_\ell+1}.
\]
The only excluded triples are \((x,b,z)\) with \(x>b\) and \(z>b\).  Consequently,
\[
|\mathcal U_\ell|
=\ell^3-\sum_{b=0}^{\ell-2}(\ell-1-b)^2
=\ell^3-\frac{(\ell-1)\ell(2\ell-1)}6,
\]
which gives \(7,22,50,95\) vertices for \(\ell=2,3,4,5\), respectively.  By \(\text{thm:dimension-and-staircase-facets}\), their augmented rows span \(\mathbb R^{D_\ell+1}\).

Here is an exact, finite determinant catalogue.  For every \(D_\ell\)-element subset \(I\subset\mathcal U_\ell\), let \(R_I\) be the \(D_\ell\) by \(D_\ell+1\) matrix with rows \(r_\ell(u)\), \(u\in I\).  If \(\operatorname{rank}(R_I)=D_\ell\), define its signed-cofactor null vector by
\[
(z_I)_k=(-1)^k\det(R_I^{\widehat k}),\qquad 0\le k\le D_\ell,
\]
where \(R_I^{\widehat k}\) deletes column \(k\).  Laplace expansion gives \(R_Iz_I=0\), and the rank condition gives \(z_I\ne0\).  Divide \(z_I\) by the positive greatest common divisor of its coordinates.  Retain it if either
\[
r_\ell(u)z_I\ge0\quad\text{for every }u\in\mathcal U_\ell
\]
or the same inequalities hold after multiplication by \(-1\), choosing the valid orientation.  Let \(E_\ell\) be the set of distinct retained primitive rows.

We verify that this finite calculation is exactly a facet calculation.  If \(e\in E_\ell\), then \(e\) is primitive and valid, and the rows indexed by its generating set \(I\) are tight and have rank \(D_\ell\).  Because all augmented vertex rows have rank \(D_\ell+1\), the complete tight-row matrix of the nonzero vector \(e\) cannot have rank larger than \(D_\ell\).  Its rank is therefore exactly \(D_\ell\), so its tight vertices have affine dimension \(D_\ell-1\).  Thus \(e\in\mathcal H_\ell\).  Conversely, if \(h\in\mathcal H_\ell\), the augmented rows of its tight vertices have rank \(D_\ell\).  Choose a basis \(I\) of \(D_\ell\) such rows.  Then \(\ker(R_I)\) is one-dimensional and contains \(h\); primitive normalization and valid orientation therefore turn \(z_I\) into exactly \(h\).  Hence
\[
E_\ell=\mathcal H_\ell. \tag{1}
\]
This is the exhaustive-subdeterminant technique: (1) proves both soundness and completeness, without assuming that a numerical hull routine returned all facets.

\[
(\kappa,\beta_0,\beta_1,\beta_2,
\alpha_{01},\ldots,\alpha_{0,\ell-1},
\alpha_{11},\ldots,\alpha_{1,\ell-1},
\alpha_{21},\ldots,\alpha_{2,\ell-1}).
\]
Every determinant, greatest common divisor, slack, and rank in the preceding test is an integer.  Thus ties and zero slacks are tested exactly and no positivity, division, or limiting condition is implicit.

%%% FIELD transfer-proof
Assume that \(\text{oeq:complete-two-pass-facet-stream}\) has a positive solution.  Write \(\rho_L:\mathcal H_{L+1}\to\mathcal H_L\) for its geometric predecessor map.  The stipulated inverse type-labelled successor table has a fixed nonnegative integer multiplicity
\[
m_{st}=\#\{\text{successor labels producing type }s
                 \text{ from one predecessor of type }t\},
\qquad s,t\in\mathsf T.
\]
The table is finite and independent of \(L\), so each \(m_{st}\) is finite and independent of \(L\).  Define the explicit transfer matrix by
\[
(T_\star)_{st}=m_{st},\qquad s,t\in\mathsf T. \tag{2}
\]
This exposes every variable in the claimed recurrence; no matrix is fitted to finitely many values of \(F_L\).

Fix \(L\ge2\) and \(s\in\mathsf T\).  Unique predecessorhood partitions the type-\(s\) facets in \(\mathcal H_{L+1}\) into the disjoint fibers of \(\rho_L\).  Inverse successorhood says that the type-\(s\) part of the fiber above a predecessor \(g\) of type \(t\) has cardinality \(m_{st}\).  Therefore, using only disjoint finite-set counting and then grouping predecessors by type,
\[
\begin{aligned}
(c_{L+1})_s
&=\#\{h\in\mathcal H_{L+1}:\operatorname{type}(h)=s\}\\
&=\sum_{g\in\mathcal H_L}
   \#\{h\in\mathcal H_{L+1}:\rho_L(h)=g,
                  \operatorname{type}(h)=s\}\\
&=\sum_{t\in\mathsf T}
   \sum_{\substack{g\in\mathcal H_L\\\operatorname{type}(g)=t}}m_{st}\\
&=\sum_{t\in\mathsf T}m_{st}(c_L)_t
=(T_\star c_L)_s.
\end{aligned}\tag{3}
\]
Since (3) holds for every \(s\), it proves \(c_{L+1}=T_\star c_L\).  The finite type labels partition the finite set \(\mathcal H_L\), whence
\[
F_L=|\mathcal H_L|
=\sum_{t\in\mathsf T}\#\{h\in\mathcal H_L:\operatorname{type}(h)=t\}
=\mathbf 1^\top c_L. \tag{4}
\]
In particular, \(c_2\in\mathbb N^{|\mathsf T|}\) is a finite initial vector.  Finally, \(\text{lem:small-facet-catalogue-counts}\) gives the exact, independently certified values
\[
F_2=7,\qquad F_3=59,\qquad F_4=197,\qquad F_5=721.
\]
Equations (2)--(4) prove every assertion of the theorem under precisely its stated positive-solution premise.
