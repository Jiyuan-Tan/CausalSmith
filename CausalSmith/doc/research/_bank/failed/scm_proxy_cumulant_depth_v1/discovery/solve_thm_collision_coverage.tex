Put \(n_0=\ell+1\), \(S_0=\varepsilon_Z\), \(S_i=a_iL_i\) for \(i\in I_\ell\), \(q_\beta=(0,1,\beta)^{\mathsf T}\), and \(e_y=(0,0,1)^{\mathsf T}\).  The graph equations and the definitions of \(u_i\) and \(v_i\) give the identity
\[
 X=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}p_jS_j+q_\beta\varepsilon_T+e_y\varepsilon_Y.
\]
For independent random variables, the joint cumulant of linear combinations is the sum of the source cumulants times the corresponding loading products.  This follows directly by expanding the moment--cumulant partition formula: every mixed block involving two independent source families factors, and the alternating partition sum is zero.  Thus assumptions \(\mathrm{ass:graph}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:independence}\) imply, for every \(r\le R\),
\[
 C_r=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\operatorname{Cum}_r(S_j)p_j^{\otimes r}
      +\operatorname{Cum}_r(\varepsilon_T)q_\beta^{\otimes r}
      +\operatorname{Cum}_r(\varepsilon_Y)e_y^{\otimes r}.
\]
The coefficient of \(S_j\) in \(Z\) is one, whereas the coefficients of \(\varepsilon_T\) and \(\varepsilon_Y\) in \(Z\) are zero.  Multilinearity therefore gives, for \(f\in H_d\), \(|\eta|=m\), and \(d+m+1\le R\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 C_{d+m+1}[Z,f,X^\eta]
 &=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}
   \operatorname{Cum}_{d+m+1}(S_j)f(p_j)p_j^\eta\\
 &=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}(B_{R,d})_{(m,\eta),j}(E_df)_j.
\end{aligned}
\]
This is precisely \(A_{R,d}=B_{R,d}E_d\).

Now fix any representation in the legal fiber and suppose \(\operatorname{rank}(A_{R,d})=n_0<N_d\).  Both factors pass through \(\mathbb R^{n_0}\), so
\[
 n_0=\operatorname{rank}(A_{R,d})
 \le \min\{\operatorname{rank}(B_{R,d}),\operatorname{rank}(E_d)\}
 \le n_0.
\]
Consequently \(B_{R,d}\) has full column rank and is injective.  If \(f\in\ker A_{R,d}\), then \(B_{R,d}E_df=0\), hence \(E_df=0\), and therefore
\[
 f(p_j)=0,\qquad 0\le j\le\ell.
\]
The matrix \(A_{R,d}\) is a function only of the common observed tuple \(\zeta\).  Its rank is consequently the same in every representation in \(F_{\ell,R}(\zeta)\), so the preceding argument applies to every member of that nonempty legal fiber.

 =1-\frac{t^2\operatorname{Var}(W)}{2n}+o(n^{-1}),

where the remainder follows by truncating \(W^2\) and then using dominated convergence.  Raising this expression to the \(n\)-th power gives the centered normal characteristic function.  Applying this to every \(a\) yields
\[
 \sqrt n(\widehat\mu_R-\mu_R)\Rightarrow N(0,\Sigma_R),
 \qquad \Sigma_R=\operatorname{Var}\{H_R(X)\}.
\]
Taylor expansion of the polynomial \(\mathcal K_R\) gives
\[
 \sqrt n\{\mathcal K_R(\widehat\mu_R)-\mathcal K_R(\mu_R)\}
 =D\mathcal K_R(\mu_R)\sqrt n(\widehat\mu_R-\mu_R)+o_P(1).
\]
For fixed \(R\), each multivariate \(k\)-statistic differs from the corresponding plug-in cumulant by \(O_P(n^{-1})\): in the partition formula, the terms with repeated sample indices number \(O(n^{r-1})\) among \(O(n^r)\) tuples, and their normalized sums are tight under the available moments.  Consequently
\[
 \sqrt n(\widehat C_{\le R}-c)\Rightarrow N(0,V_P),
 \qquad
 V_P=D\mathcal K_R(\mu_R)\Sigma_RD\mathcal K_R(\mu_R)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
In particular \(\widehat C_{\le R}\to c\) in probability.

Each score is continuous.  The strict population gap and finiteness of the atlas therefore imply
\[
 \Pr_P(\chi_n=\chi_\star)\longrightarrow1.
\]
Because \(\Delta_{\chi_\star}(c)\ne0\), continuity also gives
\[
 \Pr_P\{\widehat C_{\le R}\in\mathcal U_{\chi_\star}\}\longrightarrow1.
\]
Thus the stated success event has probability tending to one, and on it \(\widehat\beta_n=g_{\chi_\star}(\widehat C_{\le R})\).

The chart construction represents \(g_{\chi_\star}\) as a ratio of polynomials whose recorded denominator is nonzero at \(c\).  It is therefore differentiable on an ambient neighborhood of \(c\).  Taylor's formula and the preceding cumulant limit yield
\[
 \sqrt n(\widehat\beta_n-\beta)
 =Dg_{\chi_\star}(c)\sqrt n(\widehat C_{\le R}-c)+o_P(1)
 \Rightarrow
 N\!\left(0,Dg_{\chi_\star}(c)V_PDg_{\chi_\star}(c)^{\mathsf T}\right).
\]
Conditioning on the success event does not change the limit: for any continuity set \(B\), the difference between the conditional and unconditional probabilities is bounded by a constant multiple of the probability of failure, which tends to zero.

An explicit sandwich estimate is obtained by setting
\[
 \widehat\psi_s=D\mathcal K_R(\widehat\mu_R)
 \{H_R(X^{(s)})-\widehat\mu_R\},
 \qquad
 \widehat V=n^{-1}\sum_{s=1}^n\widehat\psi_s\widehat\psi_s^{\mathsf T},
\]
and then using
\[
 Dg_{\chi_n}(\widehat C_{\le R})\widehat V
 Dg_{\chi_n}(\widehat C_{\le R})^{\mathsf T}
\]
on the success event.  The weak law applied to products of monomials of total degree at most \(2R\), polynomial continuity, and consistent chart selection prove convergence to the displayed asymptotic variance.  When that variance is positive, its square root studentizes the normal limit and yields the usual Wald interval.  The computable reporting domain is \(\{\chi_n\ne\bot,\widehat C_{\le R}\in\mathcal U_{\chi_n}\}\).  Outside that domain Definition \(\mathrm{def:kstat\mbox{-}estimator}\) returns \(\bot\), and no Wald interval is formed.  The strict-gap argument proves that this reporting domain contains the stated success event with probability tending to one; it does not assert that every outcome on the complement of the population-chart success event must return \(\bot\).

%%% FIELD proof-collision
Put \(B=\max\{1,M\}^R\).  For every \(\nu\in\mathcal A_R\), assumption \(\mathrm{ass:observed\mbox{-}envelope}\) implies \(|X^\nu|\le B\) almost surely.  If a centered variable \(W\) lies in an interval of length \(2B\), convexity of the exponential bounds it by the chord joining the interval endpoints; after taking expectations and optimizing the resulting two-point expression one obtains
\[
 \mathbb E e^{\lambda W}\le e^{\lambda^2B^2/2}.
\]
Markov's inequality applied to \(e^{\lambda\sum_sW_s}\), optimized at \(\lambda=t/B^2\), therefore gives
\[
 \Pr_P\{|\widehat\mu_{R,\nu}-\mu_{R,\nu}|>t\}
 \le2\exp\!\left(-\frac{nt^2}{2B^2}\right).
\]
With \(t=\rho_n=B\sqrt{2\log(2p_R/\alpha)/n}\), a union bound over the \(p_R\) coordinates yields, uniformly in \(P\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm{bd}}_{\ell,M}\),
\[
 \Pr_P\{\|\widehat\mu_R-\mu_R\|_\infty\le\rho_n\}\ge1-\alpha.
\]
Call this event \(E_n\).

On \(E_n\), the true raw moment vector is feasible in the defining box for \(Q_n\).  The moment--cumulant identity gives \(\mathcal K_R(\mu_R)=C_{\le R}\), and the true structural parameter belongs to \(\Theta_{\ell,R}\).  Hence, successively,
\[
 C_{\le R}\in Q_n,\qquad
 C_{\le R}\in\mathcal J_n,\qquad
 \mathcal J_n\ne\varnothing,\qquad
 \beta\in\mathcal B_{\ell,R}(C_{\le R})\subseteq I_n.
\]
This proves the simultaneous class-uniform coverage assertion.  On \(E_n^c\) there is no asserted coverage; the definitions totalize an empty legal intersection as \(\mathcal J_n=I_n=\varnothing\), so emptiness is a failure report rather than a point claim.

Both sets have finite quantified-real descriptions.  Indeed, introduce the raw moment coordinate \(m\), the structural coordinates, and the finitely many atomic weights and locations from \(\Theta_{\ell,R}\); impose the displayed polynomial equalities and weak or strict inequalities, and project successively to \(\zeta\) and then to \(b=\beta\).  A terminating decision procedure can be obtained recursively: isolate the real roots of the finitely many univariate coefficient, discriminant, and resultant polynomials, partition the line into sign-invariant cells, and lift these cells one variable at a time.  At each stage only finitely many polynomials and cells occur, so the recursion terminates and decides membership and projection.  Thus \(\mathcal J_n\) and \(I_n\) are sample-computable semialgebraic sets from the finite atomic presentation.

Assume now \(\rho_n\le B\).  Every empirical raw moment has absolute value at most \(B\), so each \(m\) in the raw box defining \(Q_n\) satisfies \(\|m\|_\infty\le2B\).  The line segment between any two such vectors remains in the same compact box.  The definition of \(\Lambda_{R,M}\) and the mean-value identity therefore give, for \(\zeta=\mathcal K_R(m)\) and \(\zeta'=\mathcal K_R(m')\),
\[
 \|\zeta-\zeta'\|_\infty
 \le\Lambda_{R,M}\|m-m'\|_\infty
 \le2\Lambda_{R,M}\rho_n.
\]
Thus \(\operatorname{diam}(Q_n)\le2\Lambda_{R,M}\rho_n\).  On \(E_n\), the true cumulant tuple belongs to \(Q_n\), so every \(\zeta'\in\mathcal J_n\) satisfies
\[
 \|\zeta'-C_{\le R}\|_\infty\le2\Lambda_{R,M}\rho_n.
\]
Taking the union of its legal effect fibers and invoking the definition of \(\omega_{\ell,R}\) proves
\[
 \operatorname{diam}(I_n)
 \le\omega_{\ell,R}(2\Lambda_{R,M}\rho_n;C_{\le R}).
\]

Suppose next that \(\chi_n\ne\bot\), \(\mathcal J_n\ne\varnothing\), and \(\eta_n>0\).  By definition, every \(\zeta\in\mathcal J_n\) obeys
\[
 \frac{|\Delta_{\chi_n}(\zeta)|}{1+|\Delta_{\chi_n}(\zeta)|}\ge\eta_n>0.
\]
Thus \(\mathcal J_n\subseteq\mathcal U_{\chi_n}\).  Since \(\chi_n\) belongs to the established atlas, every legal fiber over this set has the singleton effect \(g_{\chi_n}(\zeta)\in\mathbb R\), and hence
\[
 I_n=\{g_{\chi_n}(\zeta):\zeta\in\mathcal J_n\}.
\]

To quantify its diameter, clear the chart's row-reduction denominators and write \(g_{\chi}=P_\chi/Q_\chi\), where \(P_\chi,Q_\chi\) are polynomials and the factors required to keep \(Q_\chi\ne0\) occur in the recorded product \(\Delta_\chi\).  All cumulant tuples in \(Q_n\) lie in a fixed compact box depending only on \((R,M)\).  On that box, every polynomial factor of \(\Delta_\chi\), every first derivative of \(P_\chi,Q_\chi\), and \(P_\chi\) itself is bounded.  Since
\[
 |\Delta_\chi(\zeta)|\ge\frac{\eta_n}{1-\eta_n}\ge\eta_n,
\]
the product structure and the upper bounds on the other factors give \(|Q_\chi(\zeta)|\ge c_\chi\eta_n\) for all \(\zeta\in\mathcal J_n\), with \(c_\chi>0\) depending on the fixed chart and compact box.  For two points \(\zeta_1,\zeta_2\) in that set, the polynomial mean-value bound and
\[
 \frac{P_\chi(\zeta_1)}{Q_\chi(\zeta_1)}-
 \frac{P_\chi(\zeta_2)}{Q_\chi(\zeta_2)}
 =\frac{\{P_\chi(\zeta_1)-P_\chi(\zeta_2)\}Q_\chi(\zeta_2)
       +P_\chi(\zeta_2)\{Q_\chi(\zeta_2)-Q_\chi(\zeta_1)\}}
       {Q_\chi(\zeta_1)Q_\chi(\zeta_2)}
\]
give
\[
 |g_\chi(\zeta_1)-g_\chi(\zeta_2)|
 \le K_{\chi,d_\chi,R,M}\eta_n^{-2}
       \|\zeta_1-\zeta_2\|_\infty.
\]
Combining this with the diameter of \(Q_n\) proves
\[
 \operatorname{diam}(I_n)=O(\Lambda_{R,M}\rho_n\eta_n^{-2}),
\]
with the claimed chart indexing.  Therefore the displayed product tending to zero implies contraction.

No step in the coverage or modulus argument divided by a discriminant, so those bounds remain valid for triangular sequences and when \(\eta_n=0\).  The modulus is nondecreasing in \(\delta\); hence its right limit exists in \([0,\infty]\), and as the moment radius decreases to zero the preceding inequality is governed by
\[
 \lim_{\delta\downarrow0}\omega_{\ell,R}(\delta;C_{\le R}).
\]
Because \(\omega_{\ell,R}(0;C_{\le R})\ge
\operatorname{diam}\mathcal B_{\ell,R}(C_{\le R})\), this limit can be positive at a legally set-identified collision.  This completes every asserted boundary and failure case.
