Fix \(\theta\in\Theta_{2,3}\) and write its effect as \(\beta\).  Set
\[
 W=Y-\beta T,qquad
 r_0=(1,\gamma,0)^{\mathsf T},\quad
 r_i=(1,u_i,v_i-\beta u_i)^{\mathsf T}\ (i=1,2),quad

\]

These objects are defined by \texttt{def:cumulant-map}, which follows from \texttt{ass:graph} and \texttt{ass:independence}.

For a candidate \(q\), put \(\delta=\beta-q\) and
\[
 H_\delta=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&1&0\\0&\delta&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad V_q=H_\delta V,qquad e_q=H_\delta(0,1,0)^{\mathsf T}=(0,1,\delta)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
The coordinates \((Z,T,Y-qT)\) equal \((Z,T,W+\delta T)\).  Because both disturbance columns \((0,1,\beta)^{\mathsf T}\) and \((0,0,1)^{\mathsf T}\) have zero \(Z\)-coordinate, the proxy-anchored third-cumulant matrix is
\[
 M(q)=V_q\Lambda_3V_q^{\mathsf T}. \tag{1}
\]
Likewise, only the three sources entering \(Z\) contribute to the proxy-anchored covariance vector, so
\[
 \nu(q)=V_q\lambda_2. \tag{2}
\]

Assume first that \(D:=\det(M_3)\ne0\).  Since \(H(q)\) and \(H_\delta\) have determinant one, \(\det M(q)=D\).  Equation~(1) then forces both \(V_q\) and \(\Lambda_3\) to be invertible.  Define
\[
 z_q=\bigl(s_j(t_j+\delta s_j)\bigr)_{j=0}^2,
 \quad (s_0,t_0)=(\gamma,0),quad
 (s_i,t_i)=(u_i,v_i-\beta u_i).
\]
For the three sources entering \(Z\), the three entries of \(h(q)\) are respectively the sums of \(s_j(t_j+\delta s_j)\) times the coordinates \(1,s_j,t_j+\delta s_j\).  The treatment disturbance contributes \(0,\kappa^S_{t,3}\delta,\kappa^S_{t,3}\delta^2\), and the outcome disturbance contributes zero because each entry contains \(T\).  Hence
\[
 h(q)=V_q\Lambda_3z_q+\kappa^S_{t,3}\delta e_q. \tag{3}
\]
The same source-by-source calculation at order two gives
\[
 s(q)=\lambda_2^{\mathsf T}z_q+\kappa^S_{t,2}\delta. \tag{4}
\]
Combining (1)--(4) and using \(\operatorname{adj}(M(q))=D M(q)^{-1}\), one obtains
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \frac{\mathcal P_\zeta(q)}{D}
 &=s(q)-\nu(q)^{\mathsf T}M(q)^{-1}h(q)\\
 &=\delta\left(\kappa^S_{t,2}-\kappa^S_{t,3}
 \lambda_2^{\mathsf T}\Lambda_3^{-1}V_q^{-1}e_q\right).
 \end{aligned} \tag{5}
\]
But \(V_q=H_\delta V\) and \(e_q=H_\delta(0,1,0)^{\mathsf T}\), whence
\[
 V_q^{-1}e_q=V^{-1}(0,1,0)^{\mathsf T}. \tag{6}
\]
The bracket in (5) is therefore independent of \(q\).  With
\[
 K=\kappa^S_{t,2}-\kappa^S_{t,3}\lambda_2^{\mathsf T}
 \Lambda_3^{-1}V^{-1}(0,1,0)^{\mathsf T},
\]
equations (5)--(6) prove the exact factorization
\[
 \mathcal P_\zeta(q)=D(\beta-q)K. \tag{7}
\]
Although \(K\) was expressed using the chosen representation, the left side of (7) is formed from the observed tensors alone.  In particular it is affine in \(q\), \(a_1=-DK\), and, whenever \(Da_1\ne0\), its unique real or complex root is

\]
