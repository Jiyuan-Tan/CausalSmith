\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} Higher-order cumulants identify the treatment effect in the fixed single-proxy latent-confounded LiNGAM, but the published order \(k(\ell)\) is only sufficient. The proved frontier now has exact depths \(r_\beta(1)=r_\beta(2)=3\) and \(r_\beta(3)=4\), global bounds with matching square-root asymptotics, and terminating algebraic recovery maps with explicit instability certificates. The mandatory complete \((\ell,R)=(2,3)\) test has a linear target eliminant: on \(\det(M_3)a_1\ne0\), every legal representation has \(\beta=-a_0/a_1\), and the unequal-effect incidence saturation is empty. The class bridge distinguishes the paper's centered, realizable finite-atomic truncation from Tramontano et al.'s larger unrestricted diagonal-cumulant chart while showing that finite-support converse twins transfer to their actual-law class and that the upper contraction algebra transfers to their generic chart.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Tramontano et al. prove proxy-effect identification through the bivariate recovery order \(k(\ell)\), with \(k(2)=6\), but do not determine the minimum depth of the complete ternary cumulant law. Their algebraic generic chart treats diagonal cumulants as unrestricted coordinates, whereas the present legal domain also enforces positive variances and simultaneous centered moment realizability. Before this result, the generically finite \((2,3)\) map left open a disconnected legal unequal-effect branch.

\paragraph{Niche.} The graph's distinguished treatment and outcome disturbance directions allow a mixed covariance identity to be interpolated through the invertible proxy-anchored third-cumulant matrix. For two latents this produces a linear observable target eliminant even though no degree-one proxy annihilator exists.

\paragraph{Fill.} The paper proves \(r_\beta(2)=3\) by the explicit map \(-a_0/a_1\), certifies the exceptional locus by \(\det(M_3)a_1=0\), and proves that the localized unequal-effect incidence ideal is empty. Together with the established one- and three-latent cases, the exact values are now three, three, and four for \(\ell=1,2,3\). The global bounds and square-root asymptotics remain, while exact intermediate-order classification for \(\ell\ge4\) is retained as the honest frontier. For the companion implementation, the two-latent minimum input falls from the published sufficient order six to order three and gains a determinant-slope instability report.

\section{Related work}

Tramontano et al. (2025, Theorem 3.1) use a degree-\(\ell+1\) bivariate loading-root polynomial through \(k(\ell)\); Theorems 3.4--3.5 align the resulting proxy loading lists and prove effect sufficiency at that order. Their unrestricted diagonal-cumulant parameterization is the algebraic ambient chart, while the present \(\Theta_{\ell,R}\) is a centered, generic-nonzero, actual-law truncation with positive variance and simultaneous cross-order realizability. The class bridge shows exactly what transfers in each direction. For \(\ell=2\), the published workflow uses \(k(2)=6\), whereas the new complete-tensor identity gives the sharp order three and rules out a separate nonsingular legal target branch. For \(\ell=3\), the established sharp order is four rather than \(k(3)=7\). Theorem 3.6 of Tramontano et al. claims order-three nonidentification for one latent; the established one-latent theorem instead gives order-three full-fiber recovery and audits the extra algebraic root against the legal moment domain. Schkoda, Robeva, and Drton (2024) supply the bivariate root machinery, while Garcia-Puente, Spielvogel, and Sullivant (2010) supply the target-elimination viewpoint; neither gives the mixed covariance--third-cumulant linear target certificate proved here. Full-distribution overcomplete independent-component results and the banked bivariate causal-direction frontier concern different targets and do not settle this fixed-graph minimum complete-cumulant depth.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:graph]\label{ass:graph}
\[Z=\sum_{i\in I_{\ell}}a_iL_i+\varepsilon_Z,\qquad T=\gamma Z+\sum_{i\in I_{\ell}}b_iL_i+\varepsilon_T,\qquad Y=\beta T+\sum_{i\in I_{\ell}}c_iL_i+\varepsilon_Y.\]
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Fixed single-proxy lvLiNGAM graph, \cite{TramontanoKivvaSalehkaleybarKiyavashDrton2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:independence]\label{ass:independence}
\[(L_i)_{i\in I_{\ell}},\varepsilon_Z,\varepsilon_T,\varepsilon_Y\text{ are mutually independent}.\]
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Mutually independent LiNGAM sources, \cite{TramontanoKivvaSalehkaleybarKiyavashDrton2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:centered]\label{ass:centered}
\[\mathbb E[L_i]=0\ (i\in I_{\ell}),\qquad\mathbb E[\varepsilon_Z]=\mathbb E[\varepsilon_T]=\mathbb E[\varepsilon_Y]=0.\]
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Centered-source normalization, \cite{TramontanoKivvaSalehkaleybarKiyavashDrton2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:nondegenerate]\label{ass:nondegenerate}
\[\min_{S\in\{L_i:i\in I_{\ell}\}\cup\{\varepsilon_Z,\varepsilon_T,\varepsilon_Y\}}\operatorname{Var}(S)>0.\]
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Nondegenerate exogenous sources, \cite{TramontanoKivvaSalehkaleybarKiyavashDrton2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:nongaussian]\label{ass:nongaussian}
\[S\text{ is non-Gaussian for every }S\in\{L_i:i\in I_{\ell}\}\cup\{\varepsilon_Z,\varepsilon_T,\varepsilon_Y\}.\]
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Non-Gaussian LiNGAM sources, \cite{TramontanoKivvaSalehkaleybarKiyavashDrton2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:finite-moments]\label{ass:finite-moments}
\[\mathbb E|S|^m<\infty\quad\text{for every }m\ge1\text{ and every }S\in\{L_i:i\in I_{\ell}\}\cup\{\varepsilon_Z,\varepsilon_T,\varepsilon_Y\}.\]
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Finite-cumulant regularity, \cite{TramontanoKivvaSalehkaleybarKiyavashDrton2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:nonzero-graph]\label{ass:nonzero-graph}
\[\gamma\prod_{i\in I_{\ell}}a_ib_ic_i\ne0.\]
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Nonzero-edge graph stratum, \cite{TramontanoKivvaSalehkaleybarKiyavashDrton2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:iid-sampling]\label{ass:iid-sampling}
\[X^{(1)},\ldots,X^{(n)}\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}P.\]
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Independent sampling, \cite{Serfling1980}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:observed-envelope]\label{ass:observed-envelope}
\[\lVert X\rVert_{\infty}\le M\quad\text{almost surely}.\]
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Bounded observed-data envelope for simultaneous Hoeffding concentration, \cite{Hoeffding1963}).
\end{assumption}

\section{Main results}

\begin{theorem}[thm:contraction-factorization]\label{thm:contraction-factorization}
For every \(R\ge d+2\) and every \(\zeta\in\mathcal O_{\ell,R}\), the complete proxy-anchored map satisfies \(A_{R,d}=B_{R,d}E_d\) in each \(\theta\in F_{\ell,R}(\zeta)\). If \(\operatorname{rank}(A_{R,d})=\ell+1<N_d\), then every \(f\in\ker A_{R,d}\) annihilates all normalized source columns \(p_0,\ldots,p_{\ell}\) in every member of that nonempty legal fiber.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(n_0=\ell+1\), \(S_0=\varepsilon_Z\), \(S_i=a_iL_i\) for \(i\in I_\ell\), \(q_\beta=(0,1,\beta)^{\mathsf T}\), and \(e_y=(0,0,1)^{\mathsf T}\).  The graph equations and the definitions of \(u_i\) and \(v_i\) give the identity
\[
 X=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}p_jS_j+q_\beta\varepsilon_T+e_y\varepsilon_Y.
\]
For independent random variables, the joint cumulant of linear combinations is the sum of the source cumulants times the corresponding loading products.  This follows directly by expanding the moment--cumulant partition formula: every mixed block involving two independent source families factors, and the alternating partition sum is zero.  Thus assumptions \(\mathrm{ass:graph}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:independence}\) imply, for every \(r\le R\),
\[
 C_r=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\operatorname{Cum}_r(S_j)p_j^{\otimes r}
      +\operatorname{Cum}_r(\varepsilon_T)q_\beta^{\otimes r}
      +\operatorname{Cum}_r(\varepsilon_Y)e_y^{\otimes r}.
\]
The coefficient of \(S_j\) in \(Z\) is one, whereas the coefficients of \(\varepsilon_T\) and \(\varepsilon_Y\) in \(Z\) are zero.  Multilinearity therefore gives, for \(f\in H_d\), \(|\eta|=m\), and \(d+m+1\le R\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 C_{d+m+1}[Z,f,X^\eta]
 &=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}
   \operatorname{Cum}_{d+m+1}(S_j)f(p_j)p_j^\eta\\
 &=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}(B_{R,d})_{(m,\eta),j}(E_df)_j.
\end{aligned}
\]
This is precisely \(A_{R,d}=B_{R,d}E_d\).

Now fix any representation in the legal fiber and suppose \(\operatorname{rank}(A_{R,d})=n_0<N_d\).  Both factors pass through \(\mathbb R^{n_0}\), so
\[
 n_0=\operatorname{rank}(A_{R,d})
 \le \min\{\operatorname{rank}(B_{R,d}),\operatorname{rank}(E_d)\}
 \le n_0.
\]
Consequently \(B_{R,d}\) has full column rank and is injective.  If \(f\in\ker A_{R,d}\), then \(B_{R,d}E_df=0\), hence \(E_df=0\), and therefore
\[
 f(p_j)=0,\qquad 0\le j\le\ell.
\]
The matrix \(A_{R,d}\) is a function only of the common observed tuple \(\zeta\).  Its rank is consequently the same in every representation in \(F_{\ell,R}(\zeta)\), so the preceding argument applies to every member of that nonempty legal fiber.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The factorization uses all ternary compatibility equations and isolates the target without reconstructing the latent loading list. \textit{Gap.} Schkoda, Robeva, and Drton recover bivariate loading roots; their construction does not state this complete-tensor kernel identity. \textit{Consumer.} The identity supplies the lower-order recovery primitive for CEId-from-Moments.

\begin{theorem}[thm:strict-upper-and-recovery]\label{thm:strict-upper-and-recovery}
For every \(\ell\ge1\), \(\mathcal O_{\ell,U_{\ell}}\ne\varnothing\), the terminating construction returns a nonempty finite atlas \(\mathfrak A^{\mathrm{id}}_{\ell,U_{\ell}}\), and \(\bigcup_{\chi\in\mathfrak A^{\mathrm{id}}_{\ell,U_{\ell}}}(\mathcal U_{\chi}\cap\mathcal O_{\ell,U_{\ell}})\) contains a nonempty relatively Zariski-open subset of the legal image. For every \(\zeta\) in that union and every atlas chart \(\chi\) covering \(\zeta\), \(\operatorname{rank}(A_{U_{\ell},d_{\chi}})=\ell+1\), \(g_{\chi}(\zeta)\in\mathbb R\), and \(g_{\chi}(\zeta)=\beta(\theta)\) for all \(\theta\in F_{\ell,U_{\ell}}(\zeta)\). For every legal \(\zeta\) outside that union all atlas charts return \(\bot\), so the simultaneous vanishing of the listed discriminants is an explicit failure certificate, not a point estimate. Consequently \(r_{\beta}(\ell)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge2}\) and \(r_{\beta}(\ell)\le U_{\ell}<k(\ell)=(\ell+2)+\lceil(-3+\sqrt{8\ell+17})/2\rceil\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write \(n_0=\ell+1\), \(d=d_\ell\), and \(h=h_\ell\), so that \(N_d>n_0\) and \(U_\ell=d+h+1\).  For \(x\in\mathbb R^3\), let \(\operatorname{ev}_d(x)\in H_d^*\) denote evaluation at \(x\).  Evaluations at points \((1,s,t)\) span \(H_d^*\): indeed, a homogeneous form vanishing at every such point restricts to the zero polynomial in \((s,t)\), and hence is the zero form.  Moreover \(p_0\) and \(q_\beta\) are nonproportional.  Passing to the quotient of \(H_d^*\) by \(\operatorname{span}\{\operatorname{ev}_d(q_\beta)\}\), the affine evaluations still span the quotient.  Since \(n_0<N_d\), one may therefore choose \(p_1,\ldots,p_\ell\) in the affine chart so that
\[
 \operatorname{rank}E_d=n_0,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{ev}_d(q_\beta)\notin
 \operatorname{span}\{\operatorname{ev}_d(p_j):0\le j\le\ell\}.
\]
All graph exclusions \(\gamma(u_i-\gamma)(v_i-\beta u_i)\ne0\) can be imposed simultaneously, because each excluded equality and each selected evaluation minor is a proper polynomial condition and a finite intersection of nonempty polynomial nonvanishing sets in a real affine space is nonempty.

The rows of \(B_{U_\ell,d}\) are grouped by \(m=0,\ldots,h\), with \(N_m=\binom{m+2}{2}\) rows in group \(m\).  By the definition of \(h\),
\[
 \sum_{m=0}^{h}N_m=\binom{h+3}{3}\ge n_0.
\]
Partition \(\{0,\ldots,\ell\}\) into sets \(J_m\) with \(|J_m|\le N_m\).  The points can additionally be chosen so that, for every \(m\), a \(|J_m|\)-row minor of \((p_j^\eta)_{|\eta|=m,j\in J_m}\) is nonzero.  To see that each such condition is proper, choose the points successively; degree-\(m\) affine evaluations span \(H_m^*\).  Generic points satisfy all the finitely many conditions at once.  Specialize
\[
 \operatorname{Cum}_{d+m+1}(S_j)=
 \begin{cases}1,&j\in J_m,\\0,&j\notin J_m.
 \end{cases}
\]
Using the selected rows makes the resulting \(n_0\)-column minor of \(B_{U_\ell,d}\) block diagonal with nonzero diagonal blocks.  Hence that minor is a nonzero polynomial in the source cumulants and the loading coordinates.

It remains to show that its nonvanishing meets the legal real-moment domain.  Fix \(R+1\) distinct real atoms \(x_0,\ldots,x_R\) that admit a strictly positive probability vector with total mass one and mean zero.  The Vandermonde matrix \((x_k^r)_{0\le r,k\le R}\) is invertible.  After imposing mass one and mean zero, the map from the remaining \(R-1\) probability coordinates to \((m_2,\ldots,m_R)\) is therefore a local affine isomorphism.  Positivity is open, and the centered moment-to-cumulant map is triangular with diagonal entries one.  Thus each source has a nonempty Euclidean-open legal chart in \((\kappa_2,\ldots,\kappa_R)\), and the product source chart is open in all cumulant coordinates.  A nonzero polynomial cannot vanish on that open set.  It follows that the displayed \(B\)-minor, the selected \(E_d\)-minor, and all graph factors are simultaneously nonzero at some legal finite-support point.  This also proves \(\mathcal O_{\ell,U_\ell}\ne\varnothing\).

The second evaluation condition says that the functional \(f\mapsto f(q_\beta)\) does not vanish identically on \(\ker E_d\).  Choose \(f\in\ker E_d\) with \(f(q_\beta)\ne0\), and choose the treatment-source chart so that \(\operatorname{Cum}_{d+2}(\varepsilon_T)\ne0\).  The factorization theorem gives \(\ker A_{U_\ell,d}=\ker E_d\).  Contracting the cumulant decomposition twice more, the \(p_j\)-terms vanish because \(f(p_j)=0\), and the \(e_y\)-term vanishes because its treatment coordinate is zero.  Hence
\[
 C_{d+2}[f,T,T]=\operatorname{Cum}_{d+2}(\varepsilon_T)f(q_\beta)\ne0,
\]
and
\[
 C_{d+2}[f,T,Y]
 =\beta\operatorname{Cum}_{d+2}(\varepsilon_T)f(q_\beta)
 =\beta C_{d+2}[f,T,T].
\]
Selecting a nonzero rank minor, a kernel pivot, and this denominator produces one record \(\chi\) whose polynomial \(\Delta_\chi\) is nonzero at the legal point.  Gaussian elimination and the displayed ratio use only finitely many rational operations, so the ordered enumeration terminates.  The same calculation applies to every representation of any common observable tuple in \(\mathcal U_\chi\): the observable nonzero rank minor and the factorization force annihilation in every representation.  Thus this record passes the quantified identity test in Definition \(\mathrm{def:recovery\mbox{-}atlas}\), and the atlas is nonempty.

The relative set \(\mathcal O_{\ell,U_\ell}\cap\{\Delta_\chi\ne0\}\) is a nonempty relatively Zariski-open subset of the legal image.  On every covering established chart, the preceding full-fiber calculation gives \(g_\chi(\zeta)=\beta(\theta)\in\mathbb R\).  If a legal \(\zeta\) belongs to no chart domain, then \(\Delta_\chi(\zeta)=0\) for every atlas record, and each totalized map equals \(\bot\) by definition.  The definition of \(r_\beta\) now yields \(r_\beta(\ell)\le U_\ell\).

Finally, \(d_\ell+h_\ell\le\ell+1\).  Here is an elementary verification.  Put \(n_0=\ell+1\).  If \(h_\ell=1\), then \(n_0\le4\), and the inequality follows by checking \(n_0=2,3,4\) in the defining triangular numbers.  If \(h_\ell\ge2\), minimality gives
\[
 n_0>\binom{h_\ell+2}{3}\ge2h_\ell,
\]
so \(q=n_0-h_\ell\ge h_\ell+1\), and consequently
\[
 \binom{q+2}{2}-n_0
 =\frac{q^2+q+2-2h_\ell}{2}>0.
\]
The definition of \(d_\ell\) gives \(d_\ell\le q\).  Therefore \(U_\ell=d_\ell+h_\ell+1\le\ell+2\).  Since \(\ell\ge1\) implies
\[
 \left\lceil\frac{-3+\sqrt{8\ell+17}}2\right\rceil\ge1,
\]
we obtain \(U_\ell<k(\ell)\), as claimed.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the constructive strict improvement over the published sufficient depth and provides an observable instability certificate. \textit{Gap.} Tramontano et al., Theorems 3.4--3.5, guarantee identification only through the larger pairwise-recovery order \(k(\ell)\). \textit{Consumer.} CEId-from-Moments can lower its required empirical moment order and return \(\bot\) with the simultaneous-discriminant certificate whenever no recovery chart covers the observed cumulants.

\begin{theorem}[thm:one-latent-depth]\label{thm:one-latent-depth}
For \(\ell=1\), \(r_\beta(1)=3\).  On a nonempty relatively Zariski-open subset of \(\mathcal O_{1,3}\), \(M_3\) has rank two; if \(f\ne0\) spans its kernel and \(\operatorname{Cum}(f(X),T,T)\ne0\), then every nonempty legal fiber satisfies
\[
 \beta=\frac{\operatorname{Cum}(f(X),T,Y)}{\operatorname{Cum}(f(X),T,T)}.
\]
At order two there is a Zariski-dense relatively Euclidean-open subset of \(\mathcal O_{1,2}\) such that every covariance tuple in that subset has a real one-parameter legal fiber on which \(\beta\) varies; the sources along these fibers may be chosen independent, centered, non-Gaussian, and finitely supported.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For \(\ell=1\), write \(z=(1,\gamma,\beta\gamma)^{\mathsf T}\), \(p=(1,u,v)^{\mathsf T}\), \(q=(0,1,\beta)^{\mathsf T}\), and \(e=(0,0,1)^{\mathsf T}\).  The cumulant decomposition proved in Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:contraction\mbox{-}factorization}\) gives
\[
 M_3=\operatorname{Cum}_3(\varepsilon_Z)zz^{\mathsf T}
     +\operatorname{Cum}_3(a_1L_1)pp^{\mathsf T}.
\]
On the polynomial nonvanishing locus where \(z,p\) are independent and both displayed third cumulants are nonzero, \(M_3\) has rank two.  Its kernel is spanned by the linear form having coefficient vector
\[
 p\times z=(\beta\gamma u-\gamma v,\ v-\beta\gamma,\ \gamma-u).
\]
This form vanishes at both \(p\) and \(z\), while
\[
 f(q)=v-\beta u=\frac{c_1}{a_1}\ne0
\]
by the nonzero-graph condition.  After contraction, all proxy-entering sources vanish and the outcome disturbance vanishes because its treatment loading is zero.  Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{Cum}(f(X),T,T)=\operatorname{Cum}_3(\varepsilon_T)f(q),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Cum}(f(X),T,Y)=\beta\operatorname{Cum}_3(\varepsilon_T)f(q).
\]
The denominator is nonzero on the further polynomial open condition \(\operatorname{Cum}_3(\varepsilon_T)\ne0\).  Because the kernel and both contractions are functions of the common observed tensor, the argument applies to every representation in the legal fiber.  This gives order-three identification on a nonempty relatively Zariski-open legal image; equivalently, it also follows from \(U_1=3\) in Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:strict\mbox{-}upper\mbox{-}and\mbox{-}recovery}\).

We next prove that covariance alone does not identify \(\beta\).  Let \(\Sigma\) be a covariance tuple from a legal one-latent representation and keep \(\gamma\) fixed.  For a free \(u'\) near \(u\), define
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sigma_L'&=\frac{\Sigma_{ZT}-\gamma\Sigma_{ZZ}}{u'-\gamma},
 &\sigma_Z'&=\Sigma_{ZZ}-\sigma_L',\\
 \beta'&=\frac{\Sigma_{TY}-u'\Sigma_{ZY}}
                   {\Sigma_{TT}-u'\Sigma_{ZT}},
 &v'&=\frac{\Sigma_{ZY}-\beta'\gamma\sigma_Z'}{\sigma_L'},\\
 \sigma_T'&=\Sigma_{TT}-\gamma^2\sigma_Z'-(u')^2\sigma_L',
 &\sigma_Y'&=\Sigma_{YY}-(\beta'\gamma)^2\sigma_Z'-(v')^2\sigma_L'-(\beta')^2\sigma_T'.
\end{aligned}
\]
Every division is restricted to the open set where its displayed denominator is nonzero.  Direct substitution into
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \Sigma_{ZZ}&=\sigma_Z+\sigma_L,&
 \Sigma_{ZT}&=\gamma\sigma_Z+u\sigma_L,&
 \Sigma_{ZY}&=\beta\gamma\sigma_Z+v\sigma_L,\\
 \Sigma_{TT}&=\gamma^2\sigma_Z+u^2\sigma_L+\sigma_T,&
 \Sigma_{TY}&=\beta\gamma^2\sigma_Z+uv\sigma_L+\beta\sigma_T
\end{aligned}
\]
shows that the first five covariance coordinates are unchanged; the definition of \(\sigma_Y'\) fixes the sixth.  Moreover
\[
 \frac{d\beta'}{du'}
 =\frac{\Sigma_{ZT}\Sigma_{TY}-\Sigma_{ZY}\Sigma_{TT}}
        {(\Sigma_{TT}-u'\Sigma_{ZT})^2},
\]
which is nonzero on a polynomial open set.

Nonemptiness and all positivity conditions can be checked exactly.  For example, take the covariance tuple
\[
 \Sigma=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 10/9&7/3&22/3\\
 7/3&137/18&197/9\\
 22/3&197/9&206/3
 \end{pmatrix},
\]
arising from \(\beta=2\), \(\gamma=1/2\), and \((u,v)=(5/2,8)\) with positive source variances.  The derivative numerator is \(-128/27\).  At \(u'=13/5\), the same covariance is obtained with
\[
 \beta'=\frac{254}{139},\qquad
 v'=\frac{5822}{695},\qquad
 (\sigma_Z',\sigma_L',\sigma_T',\sigma_Y')
 =\left(\frac{50}{189},\frac{160}{189},\frac{82}{45},
 \frac{513800}{173889}\right),
\]
all strictly positive and with every graph coefficient nonzero.  Positivity, denominator nonvanishing, graph nonvanishing, and the nonzero derivative persist in neighborhoods.  The covariance map is locally onto there: the displayed formulas solve the source variances, \(v\), and \(\beta\) continuously from \(\Sigma\) after choosing the free \((\gamma,u)\).  Hence the common covariance tuples contain a nonempty Euclidean-open subset of the six-dimensional covariance space.  Such a set is Zariski dense, and the legal image has full Zariski closure because it contains this open set.

Finally, every positive variance \(s^2\) is realized by a centered two-point non-Gaussian law: choose \(A,B>0\) with \(AB=s^2\), put mass \(B/(A+B)\) at \(-A\), and mass \(A/(A+B)\) at \(B\).  Taking products makes the sources independent.  Thus the entire local covariance fiber has legal finite-support representatives.  Covariance-level generic identification fails, so \(r_\beta(1)\ge3\); the order-three result gives the reverse inequality.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The first exact case both validates the annihilator and prevents algebraic polynomial degree from being mistaken for legal statistical multiplicity. \textit{Gap.} Tramontano et al., Theorem 3.6, correctly computes the vanishing ideal of the complete modified algebraic map but treats diagonal cumulant coordinates algebraically; the new result audits the missing real source-moment domain and shows that polynomial degree two does not furnish a second legal preimage. \textit{Consumer.} The one-latent branch of CEId-from-Moments can use third rather than fourth cumulants.

\begin{theorem}[thm:generic-lower-fibers]\label{thm:generic-lower-fibers}
If \(\binom{R+2}{2}\le\ell+3\), then there is a Zariski-dense relatively Euclidean-open subset of \(\mathcal O_{\ell,R}\), contained in the image of legal finite-support parameter points, such that every \(\zeta\) in that subset has a curve in the nonempty fiber \(F_{\ell,R}(\zeta)\) along which \(\beta\) varies. Consequently \(L_{\ell}\le r_{\beta}(\ell)\le U_{\ell}\) and \(r_{\beta}(\ell)/\sqrt{2\ell}\to1\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(s=\ell+3\), the number of independent source columns in an order-specific cumulant decomposition, and define
\[
 q_\beta=(0,1,\beta)^{\mathsf T},\qquad e_y=(0,0,1)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Thus, in addition to \(p_0,p_1,\ldots,p_\ell\), the treatment and outcome disturbances have columns \(q_\beta\) and \(e_y\), respectively.  Fix coordinates on \(\operatorname{Sym}^r(\mathbb R^3)\), whose dimension is
\[
 N_r=\binom{r+2}{2},
\]
and let
\[
 V_r=\left[\operatorname{vec}_r(p_0^{\otimes r})\ \cdots\ \operatorname{vec}_r(p_\ell^{\otimes r})\ \operatorname{vec}_r(q_\beta^{\otimes r})\ \operatorname{vec}_r(e_y^{\otimes r})\right]\in\mathbb R^{N_r\times s}.
\]
Writing \(\kappa_{j,r}\) for the order-\(r\) cumulant of source \(j\), the complete cumulant-map definition and mutual independence give, in these coordinates,
\[
 \operatorname{vec}_r(C_r)=V_r\kappa_r,
 \qquad
 \kappa_r=(\kappa_{0,r},\ldots,\kappa_{\ell,r},\kappa_{t,r},\kappa_{y,r})^{\mathsf T}.
 \tag{1}
\]

We first construct a simultaneous full-row-rank loading locus.  The matrix with columns \(p_0,q_\beta,e_y\) is
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 1&0&0\\
 \gamma&1&0\\
 \beta\gamma&\beta&1
 \end{pmatrix},
\]
and has determinant one.  Hence \(p_0^{\otimes r},q_\beta^{\otimes r},e_y^{\otimes r}\) are linearly independent: an invertible change of basis sends them to three distinct pure-power coordinate tensors.  Moreover, the affine Veronese tensors
\[
 (1,u,v)^{\otimes r},\qquad (u,v)\in\mathbb R^2,
\]
span \(\operatorname{Sym}^r(\mathbb R^3)\).  Indeed, if a linear functional on the symmetric tensor space annihilated all of them, its associated homogeneous form \(h\) would satisfy \(h(1,u,v)=0\) for every \((u,v)\in\mathbb R^2\).  Successively viewing this as a univariate polynomial in \(v\) and then in \(u\) shows that every coefficient is zero, so \(h=0\).

Consequently, starting from the three independent tensors above, affine tensors can be adjoined one at a time outside the span already obtained.  If \(N_r\le \ell+3\), at most \(N_r-3\le\ell\) latent columns are needed.  Thus, for each \(r\in\{2,\ldots,R\}\), there is a set \(J_r\) of \(N_r\) source columns for which the determinant
\[
 D_r(\gamma,\beta,u,v)
 =\det\left(V_r[:,J_r]\right)
 \tag{2}
\]
is not the zero polynomial.  Since \(N_r\le N_R\le\ell+3\), this applies simultaneously to every retained order.  The polynomial
\[
 D_{\mathrm{load}}
 =\left(\prod_{r=2}^R D_r\right)
   \gamma\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}(u_i-\gamma)(v_i-\beta u_i)
 \tag{3}
\]
is nonzero because the real polynomial ring in the displayed structural coordinates is an integral domain and every factor is nonzero.  Its nonvanishing locus \(\mathcal G\) is therefore a nonempty real Zariski-open set.  On \(\mathcal G\), all selected matrices
\[
 W_r=V_r[:,J_r]
 \tag{4}
\]
are invertible, and all graph coefficients required to be nonzero are indeed nonzero.

We next construct a full-dimensional, genuinely real source-cumulant chart.  For each source choose \(R+1\) distinct real atoms \(x_0,\ldots,x_R\), with atoms on both sides of zero, and a probability vector \(w^0\) having every coordinate strictly positive and satisfying \(\sum_k w^0_kx_k=0\).  Such a choice is obtained, for example, by assigning sufficiently small positive masses to distinct interior atoms in \((-1,1)\) and solving for the two remaining positive masses at \(-1\) and \(1\).  The Vandermonde matrix
\[
 \mathsf V=(x_k^r)_{0\le r,k\le R}
\]
has determinant \(\prod_{k<k'}(x_{k'}-x_k)\ne0\).  Hence, after imposing \(m_0=1\) and \(m_1=0\), the remaining moments \((m_2,\ldots,m_R)\) are affine coordinates for the weights.  Strict positivity of the weights persists on an open neighborhood of the moment vector generated by \(w^0\).

For centered moments, the partition formula in the definition of the source cumulants has the triangular form
\[
 \kappa_r=m_r+P_r(m_2,\ldots,m_{r-1}),\qquad 2\le r\le R.
 \tag{5}
\]
This map has the polynomial inverse obtained recursively from (5).  It therefore sends the preceding moment neighborhood to an open neighborhood in \((\kappa_2,\ldots,\kappa_R)\).  Taking the product of these charts over the \(s\) sources gives an open set \(\mathcal K\) of independently assignable source-cumulant vectors.  Each point of \(\mathcal K\) is realized by centered, positive-variance, finite-support source laws.  Such a law is non-Gaussian because a nondegenerate Gaussian law cannot have finite support.  Taking the product of the source laws realizes their mutual independence and gives all moments finite.

Let \(\mathcal V\) be the set of legal parameter points whose structural coordinates lie in \(\mathcal G\) and whose source cumulants lie in \(\mathcal K\).  It is nonempty.  We now show both openness of its observable image and effect variation in every fiber over that image.

First fix \(\theta\in\mathcal V\).  If tensors \(C'_2,\ldots,C'_R\) are sufficiently close to \(\Phi_{\ell,R}(\theta)\), keep the structural coordinates and all unselected source cumulants fixed and, independently at each order, set
\[
 \kappa'_{J_r,r}
 =W_r^{-1}\left\{\operatorname{vec}_r(C'_r)
 -\sum_{j\notin J_r}\kappa_{j,r}\operatorname{vec}_r(p_j^{\otimes r})\right\}.
 \tag{6}
\]
Here \(p_t=q_\beta\) and \(p_y=e_y\).  Equation (4) makes every division in (6) valid.  At \(C'_r=C_r\), equation (1) shows that (6) returns the original selected cumulants.  Since there are finitely many sources and orders, continuity and openness of \(\mathcal K\) imply that all source-cumulant vectors remain in \(\mathcal K\) for every sufficiently small simultaneous tensor perturbation.  Equation (1) then shows that the perturbed tuple is attained.  Thus
\[
 \Omega_{\ell,R}:=\Phi_{\ell,R}(\mathcal V)
 \tag{7}
\]
is a nonempty Euclidean-open subset of the full cumulant-coordinate space, is contained in \(\mathcal O_{\ell,R}\), and consists of images of legal finite-support parameter points.

Now fix \(\zeta\in\Omega_{\ell,R}\) and choose \(\theta\in\mathcal V\) with \(\Phi_{\ell,R}(\theta)=\zeta\).  For real \(t\) near zero put
\[
 \beta(t)=\beta+t,qquad
 \gamma(t)=\gamma,qquad
 u_i(t)=u_i,qquad
 v_i(t)=v_i.
 \tag{8}
\]
Thus
\[
 b_i(t)=u_i-\gamma,qquad
 c_i(t)=v_i-(\beta+t)u_i.
 \tag{9}
\]
All factors in (3) remain nonzero for sufficiently small \(|t|\).  Let \(p_j(t)\) be the resulting columns and \(W_r(t)=V_r(t)[:,J_r]\).  The determinants in (2) remain nonzero near zero.  Keep the unselected cumulants fixed and define
\[
 \kappa_{J_r,r}(t)
 =W_r(t)^{-1}\left\{\operatorname{vec}_r(C_r)
 -\sum_{j\notin J_r}\kappa_{j,r}(0)
       \operatorname{vec}_r(p_j(t)^{\otimes r})\right\},
 \qquad 2\le r\le R.
 \tag{10}
\]
These functions are rational, hence continuous, in \(t\) on a neighborhood of zero.  By (1), they equal the original selected cumulants at zero and keep every complete tensor \(C_r\) exactly fixed.  Openness of \(\mathcal K\) supplies a common nonzero interval on which all cumulant vectors in (10) are realized by the centered finite-support laws constructed above.  Equations (8)--(10) therefore define a curve \(\theta(t)\in F_{\ell,R}(\zeta)\), while \(\beta(\theta(t))=\beta+t\) is nonconstant.

It remains to verify the asserted density.  A real polynomial that vanishes on the nonempty Euclidean-open set \(\Omega_{\ell,R}\) is identically zero: this follows by fixing all but one coordinate, applying the elementary one-variable fact on an interval, and inducting over the coordinates.  Hence \(\Omega_{\ell,R}\) is Zariski dense in the ambient cumulant space.  Since \(\Omega_{\ell,R}\subseteq\mathcal O_{\ell,R}\), the legal image is also Zariski dense in that space, and (7) is a Zariski-dense relatively Euclidean-open subset of \(\mathcal O_{\ell,R}\).  This proves the first assertion.

For completeness, covariance order requires a separate two-latent argument when deriving the universal lower bound of three.  The one-latent case is already supplied by the one-latent-depth theorem, and for \(\ell\ge3\) the first part applies at \(R=2\), because \(N_2=6\le\ell+3\).  Consider therefore \(\ell=2\).  For a candidate effect \(b\), put \(W_b=Y-bT\), and write \(\Sigma=(\sigma_{ab})_{a,b\in\{z,t,y\}}\) for the observed covariance.  Fix
\[
 \gamma=1,qquad u_1=2,qquad u_2=3,qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_T)=1.
\]
At the base point take all five source variances equal to one, take \(c_1=c_2=1\), and take \(b=1\).  In coordinates \((Z,T,W_1)\), this gives
\[
 K_0=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 3&6&2\\
 6&15&5\\
 2&5&3
 \end{pmatrix},
\qquad
 \Sigma_0=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 3&6&8\\
 6&15&20\\
 8&20&28
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{11}
\]
The first matrix has positive leading principal minors \(3,9,12\), so both are positive definite because the change from \((Z,T,W_1)\) to \((Z,T,Y)\) is invertible.

For \(\Sigma\) near \(\Sigma_0\), define \(A_0,A_1,A_2\) by
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 1&1&1\\
 1&2&3\\
 1&4&9
 \end{pmatrix}
 \begin{pmatrix}A_0\\A_1\\A_2\end{pmatrix}
 =
 \begin{pmatrix}\sigma_{zz}\\\sigma_{zt}\\\sigma_{tt}-1\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{12}
\]
The coefficient matrix is Vandermonde and invertible, and (12) gives \(A_0=A_1=A_2=1\) at \(\Sigma_0\).  For \((\Sigma,b)\) near \((\Sigma_0,1)\), define \(d_1,d_2\) and \(A_y\) by
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}A_1&A_2\\2A_1&3A_2\end{pmatrix}
 \begin{pmatrix}d_1\\d_2\end{pmatrix}
 =
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \sigma_{zy}-b\sigma_{zt}\\
 \sigma_{ty}-b\sigma_{tt}
 \end{pmatrix},
 \tag{13}
\]
\[
 A_y=\sigma_{yy}-2b\sigma_{ty}+b^2\sigma_{tt}
       -A_1d_1^2-A_2d_2^2.
 \tag{14}
\]
At the base point, (13)--(14) give \(d_1=d_2=A_y=1\).  Therefore there are neighborhoods on which all \(A_0,A_1,A_2,A_y\) are positive, the determinant \(A_1A_2\) of the matrix in (13) is nonzero, and both \(d_i\) are nonzero.

Equations (12)--(14) give the exact covariance decomposition
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(Z,T,W_b)=
 A_0(1,1,0)^{\otimes2}
 +A_1(1,2,d_1)^{\otimes2}
 +A_2(1,3,d_2)^{\otimes2}
 +(0,1,0)^{\otimes2}
 +A_y(0,0,1)^{\otimes2}.
 \tag{15}
\]
Indeed, its first three entries are (12), its two cross-entries with \(W_b\) are (13), and its last diagonal entry is (14).  Returning to \(Y=W_b+bT\), equation (15) is precisely the graph cumulant formula with effect \(\beta=b\), latent coefficients \(b_i=u_i-\gamma\in\{1,2\}\), and \(c_i=d_i\ne0\).  Independent centered two-point laws with the five displayed positive variances realize (15); they are finite-support and non-Gaussian.  Shrinking the neighborhoods if necessary, every covariance \(\Sigma\) in an open ball around \(\Sigma_0\) admits (15) for every \(b\) in a nontrivial interval around one.  Hence this open ball lies in \(\mathcal O_{2,2}\), and every one of its fibers contains unequal-effect legal points.  The same polynomial argument used after (10) makes this ball Zariski dense.  Thus order two cannot generically identify the effect when \(\ell=2\).

Let
\[
 R_0(\ell)=\min\left\{R\ge2:\binom{R+2}{2}>\ell+3\right\}.
\]
For every \(2\le R<R_0(\ell)\), the first part produces a Zariski-dense subset of legal observations with nonconstant effect fibers.  If such an \(R\) belonged to the identifying set in the definition of \(r_\beta(\ell)\), its nonempty relative Zariski-open singleton-fiber locus would intersect this dense nonidentification subset, a contradiction.  The preceding covariance arguments give the additional lower bound \(r_\beta(\ell)\ge3\) for every \(\ell\ge1\).  The strict upper-and-recovery theorem gives finiteness and \(r_\beta(\ell)\le U_\ell\).  Therefore
\[
 L_\ell=\max\{3,R_0(\ell)\}
 \le r_\beta(\ell)\le U_\ell.
 \tag{16}
\]

Finally, the positive roots of the defining quadratic inequalities give
\[
 d_\ell=\frac{-3+\sqrt{8\ell+9}}{2}+O(1)
          =\sqrt{2\ell}+O(1),
 \qquad
 R_0(\ell)=\frac{-3+\sqrt{8\ell+25}}{2}+O(1)
          =\sqrt{2\ell}+O(1).
 \tag{17}
\]
Also, since \(\binom{h+3}{3}\ge h^3/6\), the choice \(h=\lceil(6(\ell+1))^{1/3}\rceil\) proves \(h_\ell=O(\ell^{1/3})\).  Hence
\[
 L_\ell=\sqrt{2\ell}+O(1),
 \qquad
 U_\ell=d_\ell+h_\ell+1
       =\sqrt{2\ell}+O(\ell^{1/3}).
 \tag{18}
\]
Dividing (16) by \(\sqrt{2\ell}\) and using (18) yields
\[
 \frac{r_\beta(\ell)}{\sqrt{2\ell}}\longrightarrow1.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:three-latent-depth]\label{thm:three-latent-depth}
For \(\ell=3\), \(r_\beta(3)=4\).  There is a Zariski-dense relatively Euclidean-open subset \(\mathcal S\subseteq\mathcal O_{3,3}\) such that every \(\zeta\in\mathcal S\) has at least one preimage \(\theta\in\Theta_{3,3}\) satisfying
\[
 \operatorname{rank}D(\Phi_{3,3},\beta)(\theta)=17
 >16=\operatorname{rank}D\Phi_{3,3}(\theta),
\]
and \(F_{3,3}(\zeta)\) contains a real legal curve along which \(\beta\) varies.  At order four, \(\mathfrak A^{\mathrm{id}}_{3,4}\ne\varnothing\), and the union of its domains \(\mathcal U_\chi\cap\mathcal O_{3,4}\) contains a nonempty relatively Zariski-open subset; every covering chart returns the common legal-fiber effect, while outside all chart domains every recovery map returns \(\bot\).  Consequently the three-latent workflow may replace \(C_{\le k(3)}=C_{\le7}\) by \(C_{\le4}\) on the regular legal image, whereas \(C_{\le3}\) does not generically identify a point effect on \(\mathcal S\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Order the twenty coordinates by

 \vartheta=(\beta,\gamma,u_1,v_1,u_2,v_2,u_3,v_3,
 \kappa_{0,2},\kappa_{1,2},\kappa_{2,2},\kappa_{3,2},\kappa_{t,2},\kappa_{y,2},
 \kappa_{0,3},\kappa_{1,3},\kappa_{2,3},\kappa_{3,3},\kappa_{t,3},\kappa_{y,3}).

Order the six quadratic tensor rows by

 (2,0,0),(1,1,0),(1,0,1),(0,2,0),(0,1,1),(0,0,2)
\]
and the ten cubic rows lexicographically as
\[
\begin{gathered}
 (3,0,0),(2,1,0),(2,0,1),(1,2,0),(1,1,1),\\
 (1,0,2),(0,3,0),(0,2,1),(0,1,2),(0,0,3).
\end{gathered}

At the rational point

 \vartheta_i=\frac{(i+2)(i+3)}{i+1},\qquad 0\le i\le19,

direct differentiation of the polynomial formula in Definition \(\mathrm{def:cumulant\mbox{-}map}\) gives, for the first sixteen parameter columns,

 \det D\Phi_{3,3}[1{:}16]
 =\frac{5792045575840772656442004338637801362}{49633171875}\ne0.

Appending the row \(D\beta=(1,0,\ldots,0)\) and taking the first seventeen columns gives

 \det D(\Phi_{3,3},\beta)[1{:}17]
 =-\frac{19906139921937009119415063593996}{268019128125}\ne0.

These displayed rational determinants are exact certificates for ranks sixteen and seventeen, respectively.

The point is legal.  Its six order-two source cumulants are positive.  Given any pair \((\kappa_2,\kappa_3)\) with \(\kappa_2>0\), put \(\delta=\kappa_3/\kappa_2\) and choose the unique \(A,B>0\) satisfying \(AB=\kappa_2\) and \(B-A=\delta\). It is finite, nondegenerate, and non-Gaussian.  Taking the six sources independently realizes the specified cumulants.  At the displayed structural point \(\beta=\gamma=6\), and

 (u_1,u_2,u_3)=\left(\frac{20}{3},\frac{42}{5},\frac{72}{7}\right),
 \qquad

so every \(u_i-\gamma\) and \(v_i-\beta u_i\) is nonzero.  Thus the point lies in \(\Theta_{3,3}\).

The first determinant says that \(D_x\Phi_{3,3}\) is invertible.  For \((c,z)\) near \((c_0,z_0)=(\Phi_{3,3}(\vartheta_0),z_0)\), consider

 \mathcal T_{c,z}(x)=x-\bigl[D_x\Phi_{3,3}(\vartheta_0)\bigr]^{-1}

 D_zh=-(D_x\Phi_{3,3})^{-1}D_z\Phi_{3,3}.

 D_z\{\beta(h(c_0,z),z)\}\ne0.

\begin{proposition}[prop:no-latent-reduction]\label{prop:no-latent-reduction}
In the standard no-latent special case \(\ell=0\), \(\gamma=\operatorname{Cov}(Z,T)/\operatorname{Var}(Z)\) and \(\beta=\{\operatorname{Cov}(T,Y)-\gamma\operatorname{Cov}(Z,Y)\}/\{\operatorname{Var}(T)-\gamma\operatorname{Cov}(Z,T)\}\) whenever the denominator is nonzero.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Set \(\ell=0\).  Then \(I_0=\varnothing\), so the three structural equations in \(\mathrm{ass{:}graph}\) reduce to
\[
 Z=\varepsilon_Z,\qquad
 T=\gamma Z+\varepsilon_T,\qquad
 Y=\beta T+\varepsilon_Y.
\]
All covariances below exist because the variances occurring in \(\mathrm{ass{:}nondegenerate}\) are finite real quantities.  By \(\mathrm{ass{:}independence}\), \(\varepsilon_Z\) and \(\varepsilon_T\) are independent, and hence
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(Z,\varepsilon_T)
 =\operatorname{Cov}(\varepsilon_Z,\varepsilon_T)=0.
\]
Consequently, covariance bilinearity and the equation for \(T\) give
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(Z,T)
 =\operatorname{Cov}(Z,\gamma Z+\varepsilon_T)
 =\gamma\operatorname{Var}(Z).
\]
Moreover, \(\operatorname{Var}(Z)=\operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_Z)>0\) by \(\mathrm{ass{:}nondegenerate}\).  Division by this positive variance is therefore valid and yields
\[
 \gamma=\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(Z,T)}{\operatorname{Var}(Z)}.
\]

Again by \(\mathrm{ass{:}independence}\), \(\varepsilon_Y\) is independent of the pair \((\varepsilon_Z,\varepsilon_T)\).  Since \(Z=\varepsilon_Z\) and \(T=\gamma\varepsilon_Z+\varepsilon_T\) are measurable functions of that pair, this implies
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(Z,\varepsilon_Y)=0,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Cov}(T,\varepsilon_Y)=0.
\]
Using the equation for \(Y\), covariance bilinearity now gives
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(Z,Y)
 =\operatorname{Cov}(Z,\beta T+\varepsilon_Y)
 =\beta\operatorname{Cov}(Z,T)
\]
and
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(T,Y)
 =\operatorname{Cov}(T,\beta T+\varepsilon_Y)
 =\beta\operatorname{Var}(T).
\]
It remains to check that the displayed denominator is nonzero.  Independence of \(Z=\varepsilon_Z\) and \(\varepsilon_T\), together with the equation for \(T\), yields
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(T)
 =\gamma^2\operatorname{Var}(Z)+\operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_T).
\]
Combining this identity with \(\operatorname{Cov}(Z,T)=\gamma\operatorname{Var}(Z)\), we obtain
\[
 D:=\operatorname{Var}(T)-\gamma\operatorname{Cov}(Z,T)
 =\operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon_T)>0,
\]
where the strict inequality is \(\mathrm{ass{:}nondegenerate}\).  Finally, the two covariance identities involving \(Y\) imply
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(T,Y)-\gamma\operatorname{Cov}(Z,Y)
 =\beta\bigl\{\operatorname{Var}(T)-\gamma\operatorname{Cov}(Z,T)\bigr\}
 =\beta D.
\]
Division by \(D>0\) proves
\[
 \beta
 =\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(T,Y)-\gamma\operatorname{Cov}(Z,Y)}
 {\operatorname{Var}(T)-\gamma\operatorname{Cov}(Z,T)},
\]
as claimed.  In particular, under the stated assumptions the proposition's conditional phrase ``whenever the denominator is nonzero'' is automatically satisfied.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The frontier must reduce to ordinary residualized linear identification when latent confounding is absent. \textit{Gap.} This is a required sanity reduction, not a novelty claim. \textit{Consumer.} It checks normalization and implementation against the familiar covariance formula.

\begin{theorem}[thm:regular-chart-limit]\label{thm:regular-chart-limit}
Fix \(\ell,R\) with \(\mathfrak A^{\mathrm{id}}_{\ell,R}\ne\varnothing\).  Then \(\chi_\star\ne\bot\).  Suppose \(C_{\le R}\in\mathcal U_{\chi_\star}\) and the population score of \(\chi_\star\) has a strict gap over every other atlas chart.  Then \(g_{\chi_\star}(C_{\le R})=\beta\),
\[
 \Pr_P\{\chi_n=\chi_\star,\ \widehat C_{\le R}\in\mathcal U_{\chi_n},\ \widehat\beta_n\ne\bot\}\longrightarrow1,
\]
and, conditionally on this success event,
\[
 \sqrt n(\widehat\beta_n-\beta)\Rightarrow
 N\!\left(0,Dg_{\chi_\star}(C_{\le R})V_PDg_{\chi_\star}(C_{\le R})^{\mathsf T}\right).
\]
On the computable domain \(\{\chi_n\ne\bot,\widehat C_{\le R}\in\mathcal U_{\chi_n}\}\), the plug-in sandwich estimator indexed by \((\chi_n,d_n)\) is consistent.  If the displayed limiting variance is positive, the resulting studentized Wald interval is asymptotically valid.  Outside that computable domain the procedure returns \(\bot\) and forms no Wald interval.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(c=C_{\le R}\), and define the population score
\[
 s_\chi(c)=\frac{|\Delta_\chi(c)|}{1+|\Delta_\chi(c)|}.
\]
The atlas is finite.  Its nonemptiness makes the fixed-order maximizer \(\chi_\star\) a genuine chart index, hence \(\chi_\star\ne\bot\).  The assumption \(c\in\mathcal U_{\chi_\star}\) is exactly \(\Delta_{\chi_\star}(c)\ne0\).  Since \(\chi_\star\) is established, Definition \(\mathrm{def:recovery\mbox{-}atlas}\) gives
\[
 g_{\chi_\star}(c)=\beta.
\]

We establish the required stochastic expansion.  Put
\[
 H_R(x)=(x^\nu)_{\nu\in\mathcal A_R}.
\]
The finite \(2R\)-th moments implied by assumption \(\mathrm{ass:finite\mbox{-}moments}\) make \(H_R(X)\) square integrable.  For completeness, the multivariate i.i.d. central limit step follows from the scalar characteristic-function expansion: for every fixed vector \(a\), the centered variable \(W=a^{\mathsf T}\{H_R(X)-\mu_R\}\) satisfies
\[
 \mathbb E e^{itW/\sqrt n}
 =1-\frac{t^2\operatorname{Var}(W)}{2n}+o(n^{-1}),
\]
where the remainder follows by truncating \(W^2\) and then using dominated convergence.  Raising this expression to the \(n\)-th power gives the centered normal characteristic function.  Applying this to every \(a\) yields
\[
 \sqrt n(\widehat\mu_R-\mu_R)\Rightarrow N(0,\Sigma_R),
 \qquad \Sigma_R=\operatorname{Var}\{H_R(X)\}.
\]
Taylor expansion of the polynomial \(\mathcal K_R\) gives
\[
 \sqrt n\{\mathcal K_R(\widehat\mu_R)-\mathcal K_R(\mu_R)\}
 =D\mathcal K_R(\mu_R)\sqrt n(\widehat\mu_R-\mu_R)+o_P(1).
\]
For fixed \(R\), each multivariate \(k\)-statistic differs from the corresponding plug-in cumulant by \(O_P(n^{-1})\): in the partition formula, the terms with repeated sample indices number \(O(n^{r-1})\) among \(O(n^r)\) tuples, and their normalized sums are tight under the available moments.  Consequently
\[
 \sqrt n(\widehat C_{\le R}-c)\Rightarrow N(0,V_P),
 \qquad
 V_P=D\mathcal K_R(\mu_R)\Sigma_RD\mathcal K_R(\mu_R)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
In particular \(\widehat C_{\le R}\to c\) in probability.

Each score is continuous.  The strict population gap and finiteness of the atlas therefore imply
\[
 \Pr_P(\chi_n=\chi_\star)\longrightarrow1.
\]
Because \(\Delta_{\chi_\star}(c)\ne0\), continuity also gives
\[
 \Pr_P\{\widehat C_{\le R}\in\mathcal U_{\chi_\star}\}\longrightarrow1.
\]
Thus the stated success event has probability tending to one, and on it \(\widehat\beta_n=g_{\chi_\star}(\widehat C_{\le R})\).

The chart construction represents \(g_{\chi_\star}\) as a ratio of polynomials whose recorded denominator is nonzero at \(c\).  It is therefore differentiable on an ambient neighborhood of \(c\).  Taylor's formula and the preceding cumulant limit yield
\[
 \sqrt n(\widehat\beta_n-\beta)
 =Dg_{\chi_\star}(c)\sqrt n(\widehat C_{\le R}-c)+o_P(1)
 \Rightarrow
 N\!\left(0,Dg_{\chi_\star}(c)V_PDg_{\chi_\star}(c)^{\mathsf T}\right).
\]
Conditioning on the success event does not change the limit: for any continuity set \(B\), the difference between the conditional and unconditional probabilities is bounded by a constant multiple of the probability of failure, which tends to zero.

An explicit sandwich estimate is obtained by setting
\[
 \widehat\psi_s=D\mathcal K_R(\widehat\mu_R)
 \{H_R(X^{(s)})-\widehat\mu_R\},
 \qquad
 \widehat V=n^{-1}\sum_{s=1}^n\widehat\psi_s\widehat\psi_s^{\mathsf T},
\]
and then using
\[
 Dg_{\chi_n}(\widehat C_{\le R})\widehat V
 Dg_{\chi_n}(\widehat C_{\le R})^{\mathsf T}
\]
on the success event.  The weak law applied to products of monomials of total degree at most \(2R\), polynomial continuity, and consistent chart selection prove convergence to the displayed asymptotic variance.  When that variance is positive, its square root studentizes the normal limit and yields the usual Wald interval.  The computable reporting domain is \(\{\chi_n\ne\bot,\widehat C_{\le R}\in\mathcal U_{\chi_n}\}\).  Outside that domain Definition \(\mathrm{def:kstat\mbox{-}estimator}\) returns \(\bot\), and no Wald interval is formed.  The strict-gap argument proves that this reporting domain contains the stated success event with probability tending to one; it does not assert that every outcome on the complement of the population-chart success event must return \(\bot\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:collision-coverage]\label{thm:collision-coverage}
Fix \(R\) and suppose \(\rho_n\le\max\{1,M\}^{R}\). The sample-computable raw-moment box satisfies \(\inf_{P\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm{bd}}_{\ell,M}}\Pr_P\{\mu_R\in[\widehat\mu_R-\rho_n,\widehat\mu_R+\rho_n]\}\ge1-\alpha\). On this event \(C_{\le R}\in Q_n\), \(C_{\le R}\in\mathcal J_n\), \(\mathcal J_n\ne\varnothing\), and \(\beta\in I_n\); hence the same class-uniform \(1-\alpha\) lower bound holds for their conjunction. Off this event the total construction may return \(\mathcal J_n=I_n=\varnothing\), which is reported as failure rather than interpreted as identification. Both sets are sample-computable by real quantifier elimination from the finite atomic presentation of \(\Theta_{\ell,R}\). Moreover \(\operatorname{diam}(Q_n)\le2\Lambda_{R,M}\rho_n\), and on the coverage event \(\operatorname{diam}(I_n)\le\omega_{\ell,R}(2\Lambda_{R,M}\rho_n;C_{\le R})\). If \(\chi_n\ne\bot\), \(\mathcal J_n\ne\varnothing\), and \(\eta_n>0\), then \(\mathcal J_n\subseteq\mathcal U_{\chi_n}\), every \(g_{\chi_n}(\zeta)\) used in the projection is real-valued, and \(\operatorname{diam}(I_n)=O(\Lambda_{R,M}\rho_n\eta_n^{-2})\), with the implicit constant indexed by the defined pair \((\chi_n,d_n)\). Thus \(I_n\) contracts whenever \(\Lambda_{R,M}\rho_n\eta_n^{-2}\to0\). The coverage and modulus bounds remain valid for triangular sequences approaching the collision locus and when \(\eta_n=0\); at an exact collision the limiting diameter is governed by \(\lim_{\delta\downarrow0}\omega_{\ell,R}(\delta;C_{\le R})\), which may be positive when the legal population fiber is set identified.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(B=\max\{1,M\}^R\).  For every \(\nu\in\mathcal A_R\), assumption \(\mathrm{ass:observed\mbox{-}envelope}\) implies \(|X^\nu|\le B\) almost surely.  If a centered variable \(W\) lies in an interval of length \(2B\), convexity of the exponential bounds it by the chord joining the interval endpoints; after taking expectations and optimizing the resulting two-point expression one obtains
\[
 \mathbb E e^{\lambda W}\le e^{\lambda^2B^2/2}.
\]
Markov's inequality applied to \(e^{\lambda\sum_sW_s}\), optimized at \(\lambda=t/B^2\), therefore gives
\[
 \Pr_P\{|\widehat\mu_{R,\nu}-\mu_{R,\nu}|>t\}
 \le2\exp\!\left(-\frac{nt^2}{2B^2}\right).
\]
With \(t=\rho_n=B\sqrt{2\log(2p_R/\alpha)/n}\), a union bound over the \(p_R\) coordinates yields, uniformly in \(P\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm{bd}}_{\ell,M}\),
\[
 \Pr_P\{\|\widehat\mu_R-\mu_R\|_\infty\le\rho_n\}\ge1-\alpha.
\]
Call this event \(E_n\).

On \(E_n\), the true raw moment vector is feasible in the defining box for \(Q_n\).  The moment--cumulant identity gives \(\mathcal K_R(\mu_R)=C_{\le R}\), and the true structural parameter belongs to \(\Theta_{\ell,R}\).  Hence, successively,
\[
 C_{\le R}\in Q_n,\qquad
 C_{\le R}\in\mathcal J_n,\qquad
 \mathcal J_n\ne\varnothing,\qquad
 \beta\in\mathcal B_{\ell,R}(C_{\le R})\subseteq I_n.
\]
This proves the simultaneous class-uniform coverage assertion.  On \(E_n^c\) there is no asserted coverage; the definitions totalize an empty legal intersection as \(\mathcal J_n=I_n=\varnothing\), so emptiness is a failure report rather than a point claim.

Both sets have finite quantified-real descriptions.  Indeed, introduce the raw moment coordinate \(m\), the structural coordinates, and the finitely many atomic weights and locations from \(\Theta_{\ell,R}\); impose the displayed polynomial equalities and weak or strict inequalities, and project successively to \(\zeta\) and then to \(b=\beta\).  A terminating decision procedure can be obtained recursively: isolate the real roots of the finitely many univariate coefficient, discriminant, and resultant polynomials, partition the line into sign-invariant cells, and lift these cells one variable at a time.  At each stage only finitely many polynomials and cells occur, so the recursion terminates and decides membership and projection.  Thus \(\mathcal J_n\) and \(I_n\) are sample-computable semialgebraic sets from the finite atomic presentation.

Assume now \(\rho_n\le B\).  Every empirical raw moment has absolute value at most \(B\), so each \(m\) in the raw box defining \(Q_n\) satisfies \(\|m\|_\infty\le2B\).  The line segment between any two such vectors remains in the same compact box.  The definition of \(\Lambda_{R,M}\) and the mean-value identity therefore give, for \(\zeta=\mathcal K_R(m)\) and \(\zeta'=\mathcal K_R(m')\),
\[
 \|\zeta-\zeta'\|_\infty
 \le\Lambda_{R,M}\|m-m'\|_\infty
 \le2\Lambda_{R,M}\rho_n.
\]
Thus \(\operatorname{diam}(Q_n)\le2\Lambda_{R,M}\rho_n\).  On \(E_n\), the true cumulant tuple belongs to \(Q_n\), so every \(\zeta'\in\mathcal J_n\) satisfies
\[
 \|\zeta'-C_{\le R}\|_\infty\le2\Lambda_{R,M}\rho_n.
\]
Taking the union of its legal effect fibers and invoking the definition of \(\omega_{\ell,R}\) proves
\[
 \operatorname{diam}(I_n)
 \le\omega_{\ell,R}(2\Lambda_{R,M}\rho_n;C_{\le R}).
\]

Suppose next that \(\chi_n\ne\bot\), \(\mathcal J_n\ne\varnothing\), and \(\eta_n>0\).  By definition, every \(\zeta\in\mathcal J_n\) obeys
\[
 \frac{|\Delta_{\chi_n}(\zeta)|}{1+|\Delta_{\chi_n}(\zeta)|}\ge\eta_n>0.
\]
Thus \(\mathcal J_n\subseteq\mathcal U_{\chi_n}\).  Since \(\chi_n\) belongs to the established atlas, every legal fiber over this set has the singleton effect \(g_{\chi_n}(\zeta)\in\mathbb R\), and hence
\[
 I_n=\{g_{\chi_n}(\zeta):\zeta\in\mathcal J_n\}.
\]

To quantify its diameter, clear the chart's row-reduction denominators and write \(g_{\chi}=P_\chi/Q_\chi\), where \(P_\chi,Q_\chi\) are polynomials and the factors required to keep \(Q_\chi\ne0\) occur in the recorded product \(\Delta_\chi\).  All cumulant tuples in \(Q_n\) lie in a fixed compact box depending only on \((R,M)\).  On that box, every polynomial factor of \(\Delta_\chi\), every first derivative of \(P_\chi,Q_\chi\), and \(P_\chi\) itself is bounded.  Since
\[
 |\Delta_\chi(\zeta)|\ge\frac{\eta_n}{1-\eta_n}\ge\eta_n,
\]
the product structure and the upper bounds on the other factors give \(|Q_\chi(\zeta)|\ge c_\chi\eta_n\) for all \(\zeta\in\mathcal J_n\), with \(c_\chi>0\) depending on the fixed chart and compact box.  For two points \(\zeta_1,\zeta_2\) in that set, the polynomial mean-value bound and
\[
 \frac{P_\chi(\zeta_1)}{Q_\chi(\zeta_1)}-
 \frac{P_\chi(\zeta_2)}{Q_\chi(\zeta_2)}
 =\frac{\{P_\chi(\zeta_1)-P_\chi(\zeta_2)\}Q_\chi(\zeta_2)
       +P_\chi(\zeta_2)\{Q_\chi(\zeta_2)-Q_\chi(\zeta_1)\}}
       {Q_\chi(\zeta_1)Q_\chi(\zeta_2)}
\]
give
\[
 |g_\chi(\zeta_1)-g_\chi(\zeta_2)|
 \le K_{\chi,d_\chi,R,M}\eta_n^{-2}
       \|\zeta_1-\zeta_2\|_\infty.
\]
Combining this with the diameter of \(Q_n\) proves
\[
 \operatorname{diam}(I_n)=O(\Lambda_{R,M}\rho_n\eta_n^{-2}),
\]
with the claimed chart indexing.  Therefore the displayed product tending to zero implies contraction.

No step in the coverage or modulus argument divided by a discriminant, so those bounds remain valid for triangular sequences and when \(\eta_n=0\).  The modulus is nondecreasing in \(\delta\); hence its right limit exists in \([0,\infty]\), and as the moment radius decreases to zero the preceding inequality is governed by
\[
 \lim_{\delta\downarrow0}\omega_{\ell,R}(\delta;C_{\le R}).
\]
Because \(\omega_{\ell,R}(0;C_{\le R})\ge
\operatorname{diam}\mathcal B_{\ell,R}(C_{\le R})\), this limit can be positive at a legally set-identified collision.  This completes every asserted boundary and failure case.
\end{proof}

Together with the order-three recovery just proved, this yields \(r_\beta(2)=3\).
\end{proof}

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The contraction discriminant is initially a certificate-failure locus, not yet the true nonidentification locus. Accordingly, every chart-specific scalar construction returns \(\bot\) when its atlas is empty, its pivot or denominator vanishes, or its input lies outside the chart domain; no value on that failure locus is interpreted as an effect. The exact \(\ell=2,R=3\) real beta projection under the quantified finite-atomic source-moment constraints is the mandatory early decision. Complex fiber degree, Jacobian rank, failure of one annihilator, an unrestricted diagonal-cumulant solution, and a single finite-support witness are each insufficient for the global converse. The order-three algebraic image closure permits diagonal cumulant coordinates that need not be positive-variance centered source moments; polynomial degree alone therefore does not count legal preimages. Uniform confidence-fiber coverage uses the explicit observed-data envelope \(\lVert X\rVert_{\infty}\le M\); no observed bound is inferred from bounded latent sources or bounded disturbances with unrestricted structural coefficients. Off the simultaneous moment-coverage event the legal intersection and projected set may be empty and are reported as such. At an exact collision, honest coverage need not imply singleton contraction, and the limiting diameter is the legal-fiber modulus rather than a Wald rate.

\section{Honest scope}

The complete \((2,3)\) beta projection is settled on the explicit nonempty legal chart \(\det(M_3)a_1\ne0\): its target rational-univariate representation is linear over both the real and complex fibers, so there is no second positive-variance branch there. The result does not claim that the proxy-annihilator atlas itself works at order three for two latents; the new mixed covariance--third-cumulant map is a separate terminating chart and is included explicitly in the estimand functional. The established contraction theorem still supplies the all-\(\ell\) upper depth \(U_\ell\), and finite-support lower twins transfer to the published actual-law class, but the unrestricted diagonal algebraic chart is larger than the realizable moment domain. Exact depths for \(\ell\ge4\) and legal-real fibers at unresolved intermediate orders remain open, beginning with \((4,3)\). The bivariate causal-direction minimum-order frontier is distinct: it orients an arrow between two observed variables, whereas this paper fixes the single-proxy graph and identifies the interventional coefficient \(\beta\) from the complete trivariate cumulant map.
