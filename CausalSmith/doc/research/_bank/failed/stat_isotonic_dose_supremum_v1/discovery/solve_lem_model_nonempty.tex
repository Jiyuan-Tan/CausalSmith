%%% FIELD proof-model-nonempty
By \(\texttt{def:nonempty-witness}\), under \(P_0^{\mathrm F}\), \(W\) is uniform on \([0,1]^d\), \(A\) is uniform on \([0,1]\), and \(A\) is independent of \((W,R_0)\), where \(\mathbb P(R_0=1)=\mathbb P(R_0=-1)=1/2\). Moreover,
\[
Y(a)=c_{\theta}(a-1/2)+\sqrt{v_{\kappa}}R_0,
\qquad
Y=Y(A).
\]
Consequently the observed marginal \(P_0\) has the everywhere-defined density versions
\[
p(w)=1,
\qquad
g(a\mid w)=1.
\]
Since \(\mathbb E[R_0]=0\) and \(R_0^2=1\), independence gives, for every \((a,w)\in[0,1]^{d+1}\), the conditional-moment versions
\[
\begin{aligned}
m(a,w)
&=\mathbb E_{P_0}[Y\mid A=a,W=w]
=c_{\theta}(a-1/2)=m_0(a,w),\\
r(a,w)
&=\mathbb E_{P_0}[Y^2\mid A=a,W=w]
=m_0(a,w)^2+v_{\kappa}=r_0(a,w).
\end{aligned}
\]
The unit cube \([0,1]^d\) has Lebesgue volume one. Therefore the definitions of \(\theta\), \(\sigma^2\), \(f\), and \(\kappa\) yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
\theta(a)
&=\int_{[0,1]^d}m(a,w)p(w)\,dw
=c_{\theta}(a-1/2),\\
\theta'(a)&=c_{\theta},\\
\sigma^2(a,w)&=r(a,w)-m(a,w)^2=v_{\kappa},\\
f(a)&=\int_{[0,1]^d}g(a\mid w)p(w)\,dw=1,\\
\kappa(a)
&=f(a)\int_{[0,1]^d}\frac{\sigma^2(a,w)p(w)}{g(a\mid w)}\,dw
=v_{\kappa}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The division in the last line is valid because \(g(a\mid w)=1>0\).

We now verify every member condition of \(\texttt{def:model-class}\). The eight restrictions on the fixed constants follow, in their displayed order, from \(\texttt{ass:theta-constant-compatibility}\), \(\texttt{ass:p-lower-compatibility}\), \(\texttt{ass:p-upper-compatibility}\), \(\texttt{ass:g-lower-compatibility}\), \(\texttt{ass:g-upper-compatibility}\), \(\texttt{ass:variance-constant-compatibility}\), \(\texttt{ass:bounded-witness-compatibility}\), and \(\texttt{ass:radius-compatibility}\). The observed-coordinate marginal of \(P_0^{\mathrm F}\) is \(P_0\) by the construction, which verifies \(\texttt{ass:observed-marginal}\), and \(Y=Y(A)\) verifies \(\texttt{ass:consistency}\). For each deterministic \(a\), \(Y(a)\) is a measurable function of \(R_0\), whereas \(A\) is independent of \((W,R_0)\); hence \(Y(a)\perp A\mid W\), proving \(\texttt{ass:exchangeability}\). Also,
\[
\mathbb E_{P_0^{\mathrm F}}[Y(a)\mid W=w]
=c_{\theta}(a-1/2)=\mu^{\mathrm F}(a,w).
\]
This version is jointly measurable in \((a,w)\) and continuous in \(a\), so \(\texttt{ass:potential-mean-continuity}\) holds.

The triangle inequality, \(A\in[0,1]\), and \(\texttt{ass:bounded-witness-compatibility}\) give
\[
|Y|
\le c_{\theta}|A-1/2|+\sqrt{v_{\kappa}}
\le c_{\theta}/2+\sqrt{v_{\kappa}}
\le B,
\]
which proves \(\texttt{ass:outcome-bound}\). By the definition of \(L_0\), the functions \(p=1\), \(g=1\), \(m=m_0\), and \(r=r_0\) have the required \(C^s\) norms at most \(L_0\). Since \(L_0\le L\) by \(\texttt{ass:radius-compatibility}\), the four smoothness conditions \(\texttt{ass:p-smooth}\), \(\texttt{ass:g-smooth}\), \(\texttt{ass:m-smooth}\), and \(\texttt{ass:r-smooth}\) follow.

Because the dose interval and covariate cube both have Lebesgue volume one,
\[
\int_{[0,1]^d}p(w)\,dw=1,
\qquad
\int_0^1g(a\mid w)\,da=1
\quad\text{for every }w\in[0,1]^d.
\]
These equalities prove \(\texttt{ass:p-normalization}\) and \(\texttt{ass:g-normalization}\). Together with \(c_p\le1\le C_p\) and \(c_g\le1\le C_g\), they also verify \(\texttt{ass:p-bounds}\) and \(\texttt{ass:overlap}\). Finally, \(c_{\theta}>0\) and \(\theta'(a)=c_{\theta}\) make \(\theta\) nondecreasing on \([0,1]\). The compatibility inequalities \(c_{\theta}\le C_{\theta}\) and \(v_{\kappa}\le V_{\kappa}\) imply, for every \(a\in J\),
\[
c_{\theta}\le\theta'(a)=c_{\theta}\le C_{\theta},
\qquad
v_{\kappa}\le\kappa(a)=v_{\kappa}\le V_{\kappa}.
\]
Thus \(\texttt{ass:monotone}\), \(\texttt{ass:strict-slope}\), and \(\texttt{ass:variance}\) hold. Every defining condition of \(\mathcal M\) has been verified, so \(P_0\in\mathcal M\), with \(p=g=1\), \(\theta'=c_{\theta}\), and \(\kappa=v_{\kappa}\), as claimed.

%%% FIELD proof-model-inclusion
Nonemptiness follows from \(\texttt{lem:model-nonempty}\), which supplies \(P_0\in\mathcal M\). Now fix \(P\in\mathcal M\). By \(\texttt{def:model-class}\) and \(\texttt{ass:monotone}\), its observed-law curve \(\theta\) is nondecreasing on \([0,1]\). The marginal density of \(A\) is
\[
f(a)=\int_{[0,1]^d}g(a\mid w)p(w)\,dw.
\]
The normalization \(\int_{[0,1]^d}p(w)\,dw=1\), the overlap bound \(g(a\mid w)\ge c_g\), and \(c_g>0\) imply, for every \(a\in[0,1]\),
\[
f(a)
\ge \int_{[0,1]^d}c_g p(w)\,dw
=c_g>0.
\]
In particular, the distribution function of \(A\) is
\[
P(A\le t)=\int_0^t f(a)\,da,
\qquad 0\le t\le1,
\]
and hence is absolutely continuous, and therefore continuous, with an everywhere positive density version. Thus both defining requirements of \(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{WGC}}\) hold, proving
\[
\mathcal M\subseteq\mathcal M_{\mathrm{WGC}}.
\]

For strictness, construct an observed law \(Q\) as follows: \(W\) is uniform on \([0,1]^d\), \(A\) is uniform on \([0,1]\) and independent of \(W\), and \(Y=0\) almost surely. Under \(Q\), the versions
\[
p(w)=1,
\qquad
g(a\mid w)=1,
\qquad
m(a,w)=0
\]
give
\[
\theta(a)=\int_{[0,1]^d}m(a,w)p(w)\,dw=0.
\]
Therefore \(\theta\) is nondecreasing, while the marginal law of \(A\) is continuous with the everywhere positive density \(f=1\). It follows that \(Q\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{WGC}}\). On the other hand, \(\delta\in(0,1/2)\) implies \(1/2\in J=[\delta,1-\delta]\), whereas \(\theta'(1/2)=0<c_{\theta}\) because \(c_{\theta}>0\). Hence \(Q\) violates \(\texttt{ass:strict-slope}\), a member property in \(\texttt{def:model-class}\). This obstruction concerns the observed-law functional \(\theta\), so no choice of full-data extension can repair it. Thus \(Q\notin\mathcal M\), and consequently
\[
\varnothing\ne\mathcal M\subsetneq\mathcal M_{\mathrm{WGC}}.
\]

Finally, the descriptive membership assertion is a direct unpacking of \(\texttt{def:model-class}\): a member observed law admits at least one full-data extension satisfying the stated causal conditions and the jointly measurable continuous conditional-potential-mean version condition, while its observed marginal obeys the global radius-\(L\) \(C^s\) restrictions, the two-sided density restrictions, the strict-slope restriction on \(J\), and the two-sided effective-variance restriction on \(J\). This proves the entire proposition.
