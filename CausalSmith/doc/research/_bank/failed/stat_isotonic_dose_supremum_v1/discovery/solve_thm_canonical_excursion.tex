%%% FIELD proof-canonical-wellposedness
Fix a finite deterministic collection \(x_1,\ldots,x_m\in\mathbb R\).  For each \(x\in\mathbb R\), define the recentered increment path
\[
 \mathbb W_x(t)=\mathbb W(x+t)-\mathbb W(x),\qquad t\in\mathbb R.
\]
The definition of \(X\) in \(\texttt{def:canonical-field}\) gives the pathwise identity
\[
 X(x+t)-X(x)-2xt=\mathbb W_x(t)+t^2. \tag{1}
\]
We first verify the GCM equivariance used with (1).  If \(G\) is the greatest convex minorant of a function \(F\), then
\[
 \widetilde G(t)=G(x+t)-G(x)-2xt
\]
is the greatest convex minorant of
\(
 \widetilde F(t)=F(x+t)-F(x)-2xt
\).
Indeed, \(\widetilde G\) is convex and lies below \(\widetilde F\).  Conversely, translating any convex minorant of \(\widetilde F\) by \(-x\) and adding back the same affine function produces a convex minorant of \(F\), and hence one lying below \(G\).  These two order comparisons prove the assertion.  The identical argument on each of the two half-lines proves equivariance of the constrained one-sided minorants.  Under this transformation, the slope constraint \(2x\) becomes the origin constraint \(0\), and every unrestricted or constrained slope is reduced by \(2x\).  Translation in the energy integral in \(\texttt{def:canonical-field}\) consequently gives, on every path for which the fixed-location energies are defined,
\[
 D(x)=\Phi(\mathbb W_x). \tag{2}
\]

By the independent stationary Gaussian increment property of \(\mathbb W\) in \(\texttt{def:canonical-field}\), \(\mathbb W_x\) has the law of a two-sided standard Brownian motion.  Since \(D(0)=\Phi(\mathbb W)\), equation (2) implies
\[
 D(x)\stackrel{d}{=}D(0). \tag{3}
\]
The fixed-location result \(\texttt{lem:banerjee-wellner-fixed-location-lr}\) states that \(D(0)\) is finite and nonnegative almost surely.  Equation (3) gives the same conclusion for each deterministic \(x_j\).  The intersection of the resulting \(m\) probability-one events still has probability one, proving simultaneous finiteness and nonnegativity of \(D(x_1),\ldots,D(x_m)\).

It remains to prove joint stationarity.  Fix \(r\in\mathbb R\).  For any \(i,j\) and \(s,t\in\mathbb R\), the covariance
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}\!\left\{\mathbb W_{x_i+r}(s),\mathbb W_{x_j+r}(t)\right\}
\]
is the signed length of overlap of the oriented Brownian increment intervals with endpoints \((x_i+r,x_i+r+s)\) and \((x_j+r,x_j+r+t)\).  Translating all four endpoints by \(-r\) leaves that signed overlap unchanged.  The processes are centered Gaussian; therefore
\[
 \bigl(\mathbb W_{x_1+r},\ldots,\mathbb W_{x_m+r}\bigr)
 \stackrel{\mathrm{fdd}}{=}
 \bigl(\mathbb W_{x_1},\ldots,\mathbb W_{x_m}\bigr). \tag{4}
\]
All coordinates have continuous paths, and the Borel sigma-field for the topology of uniform convergence on compact sets is generated by finite collections of evaluation maps.  Thus (4) determines equality of the joint path laws.  Applying the measurable fixed-location functional \(\Phi\) coordinatewise in (2) yields
\[
 \bigl(D(x_1+r),\ldots,D(x_m+r)\bigr)
 \stackrel{d}{=}
 \bigl(D(x_1),\ldots,D(x_m)\bigr). \tag{5}
\]
Only finitely many fixed-location functionals enter this argument.  Thus (1)--(5) do not establish a separable or locally bounded version, a continuum contact-radius estimate, or block independence.

%%% FIELD proof-canonical-excursion
For a deterministic perturbation \(h\), put \(F_h(t)=t^2+h(t)\).  For \(b>0\), the prescribed dilation \(h_b(t)=b^2h(t/b)\) satisfies
\[
 F_{h_b}(t)=b^2F_h(t/b). \tag{1}
\]
Let \(G_h\) be the unrestricted greatest convex minorant of \(F_h\).  The function \(t\mapsto b^2G_h(t/b)\) is convex and lies below the right-hand side of (1).  Conversely, applying the inverse dilation to any convex minorant of \(F_{h_b}\) produces a convex minorant of \(F_h\).  Maximality in both directions therefore gives
\[
 G_{h_b}(t)=b^2G_h(t/b). \tag{2}
\]
The same order-preserving argument on the left and right half-lines gives (2) for the origin-constrained minorants.  Consequently, every corresponding slope is multiplied by \(b\).  Substitution of \(t=bs\) in the defining difference of squared-slope energies in \(\texttt{def:excursion-regime}\) gives
\[
 \Phi(h_b)=b^3\Phi(h), \tag{3}
\]
whenever the energies are defined.

If \(h\) is absolutely continuous, the chain rule gives \(h_b'(t)=b h'(t/b)\) for almost every \(t\).  Since \(h'\in L^2(\mathbb R)\), a change of variables is legitimate and yields
\[
 \frac12\lVert h_b'\rVert_2^2
 =\frac12\int_{\mathbb R}b^2\{h'(t/b)\}^2\,dt
 =b^3\frac12\lVert h'\rVert_2^2. \tag{4}
\]

Fix \(u>0\), and set \(b=u^{1/3}\) and \(\epsilon=u^{-1/2}=b^{-3/2}\).  Brownian self-similarity follows directly by comparing means and covariances of the centered Gaussian processes and gives
\[
 b^{1/2}\mathbb W(\,\cdot\,/b)\stackrel{d}{=}\mathbb W.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 (\epsilon\mathbb W)_b(t)
 =b^2\epsilon\mathbb W(t/b)
 =b^{1/2}\mathbb W(t/b).
\]
Using fixed-location well-posedness from \(\texttt{lem:canonical-wellposedness}\), the equality \(D(0)=\Phi(\mathbb W)\) from \(\texttt{def:canonical-field}\), and (3), we obtain
\[
 D(0)\stackrel{d}{=}\Phi((\epsilon\mathbb W)_b)
 =b^3\Phi(\epsilon\mathbb W)
 =u\Phi(u^{-1/2}\mathbb W).
\]
Division by \(u>0\) therefore proves
\[
 \Pr\{D(0)>u\}=\Pr\{\Phi(u^{-1/2}\mathbb W)>1\}. \tag{5}
\]

We next prove the lower action bound.  Let \(h(0)=0\), let \(h'\in L^2(\mathbb R)\), and write
\[
 y(t)=F_h'(t)=2t+h'(t)
\]
at points of differentiability.  We record the finite-contact calculation underlying the bound.  On a finite interval whose endpoints are contact points, let \(g\) be the unrestricted GCM slope and let \(g^0\) be its origin-constrained counterpart.  If \(G\) is the relevant minorant and \(H=F_h-G\), then \(H\ge0\), the positive Stieltjes measure \(dg\) is carried by the contact set \(\{H=0\}\), and \(H\) vanishes at the contact endpoints.  For every nondecreasing slope competitor \(v\), Stieltjes integration by parts gives
\[
 \int (y-g)(v-g)
 =-\int H\,dv+\int H\,dg
 \le0. \tag{6}
\]
The last integral vanishes by contact support, while the first is nonpositive because \(H\ge0\) and \(dv\) is positive.  Applying the same argument separately on the two half-intervals proves (6) for the constrained slope class; the split-point boundary term is nonpositive because the left slope is capped above by \(0\) and the right slope is floored below by \(0\).  Thus \(g\) and \(g^0\) minimize squared error over, respectively, the unrestricted and origin-constrained convex cones of nondecreasing slopes.

Both cones contain \(0\) and are closed under multiplication by \(2\).  In (6), taking successively \(v=0\) and \(v=2g\), and then making the same two choices for \(g^0\), gives
\[
 \int (y-g)g=0,
 \qquad
 \int (y-g^0)g^0=0. \tag{7}
\]
Expanding squared residuals and using (7) yields the exact finite-contact identity
\[
 \int\{(y-g^0)^2-(y-g)^2\}
 =\int\{g^2-(g^0)^2\}. \tag{8}
\]
The linear slope \(\ell(t)=2t\) is nondecreasing and satisfies the origin split constraint.  Squared-error minimality of \(g^0\), nonnegativity of the unrestricted residual loss, and (8) imply
\[
 0\le \int\{g^2-(g^0)^2\}
 \le \int\{y(t)-\ell(t)\}^2\,dt
 =\int\{h'(t)\}^2\,dt. \tag{9}
\]

For completeness, the passage to the real line does not require a boundary hypothesis.  The Cauchy--Schwarz inequality and \(h(0)=0\) give
\[
 |h(t)|\le |t|^{1/2}\lVert h'\rVert_2,
\]
so \(F_h(t)=t^2+o(t^2)\) as \(|t|\to\infty\).  Hence the global minorants are exhausted by finite intervals with contact endpoints on both sides of the origin.  For every perturbation for which the infinite-domain energy in \(\texttt{def:excursion-regime}\) is well defined, its defining improper energy is the limit of these finite-contact energies.  Applying (9) on the exhausting intervals and using monotone convergence on its nonnegative right-hand side gives
\[
 0\le\Phi(h)\le\lVert h'\rVert_2^2. \tag{10}
\]
In particular, \(\Phi(h)\ge1\) implies
\[
 \frac12\lVert h'\rVert_2^2\ge\frac12.
\]
Taking the infimum in \(\texttt{def:excursion-regime}\) proves
\[
 I_\star\ge\frac12. \tag{11}
\]

For the upper bound, let \(k=4^{1/3}\), let \(a=k/4\), and define the absolutely continuous perturbation by \(h(0)=0\) and
\[
 h'(t)=(k-2t)\mathbf 1\{-a\le t\le k/2\}. \tag{12}
\]
This derivative has compact support and belongs to \(L^2(\mathbb R)\).  The derivative of \(F_h\) is
\[
 y(t)=2t+h'(t)=
 \begin{cases}
 2t,&t<-a,\\
 k,&-a\le t\le k/2,\\
 2t,&t>k/2.
 \end{cases} \tag{13}
\]
Because \(-2a=-k/2<k\) and \(2(k/2)=k\), the function in (13) is nondecreasing.  Thus \(F_h\) is convex and its unrestricted GCM slope is \(y\).  The cap-and-floor construction in \(\texttt{def:canonical-field}\) gives the origin-constrained slope
\[
 g^0(t)=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&-a\le t\le0,\\
 y(t),&t\notin[-a,0].
 \end{cases} \tag{14}
\]
Indeed, on the left of the origin the only portion of the unrestricted slope exceeding \(0\) is the plateau in (13), while on the right the unrestricted slope is already bounded below by \(0\).  Equations (13)--(14) now give
\[
 \Phi(h)=\int_{-a}^{0}k^2\,dt=ak^2=\frac{k^3}{4}=1. \tag{15}
\]
The action of this perturbation is
\[
 \frac12\lVert h'\rVert_2^2
 =\frac12\int_{-a}^{k/2}(k-2t)^2\,dt
 =\frac{(k+2a)^3}{12}
 =\frac{(3k/2)^3}{12}
 =\frac98. \tag{16}
\]
It is therefore admissible in the defining infimum and proves \(I_\star\le9/8\).

The displayed witness is also optimal within the full single-plateau family.  Indeed, for arbitrary \(k,a>0\), the same construction gives \(\Phi(h)=ak^2\).  Under the unit-energy restriction \(ak^2=1\), put \(r=2a/k>0\).  Then \(k^3=2/r\), and the action equals
\[
 \frac{(k+2a)^3}{12}=\frac{(1+r)^3}{6r}. \tag{17}
\]
The derivative of the right-hand side has the sign of \(2r-1\), so it is minimized at \(r=1/2\), where its value is \(9/8\).  Combining (11) and (16) proves
\[
 \frac12\le I_\star\le\frac98.
\]
Neither this deterministic bracket, the within-family optimization, nor the exact scaling identity (5) is a large-deviation principle; none identifies a probability-tail rate, a Palm intensity, or an extreme-value law.

%%% FIELD proof-spatial-clock
Fix \(P\in\mathcal M\) and \(a\in J\).  By \(\texttt{ass:p-normalization}\), \(\int p(w)\,dw=1\), and by \(\texttt{ass:overlap}\),
\[
 c_g\le g(a\mid w)\le C_g
\]
for every \(w\in[0,1]^d\).  Since \(p\ge0\), the definition of \(f\) therefore gives
\[
 c_g=c_g\int p(w)\,dw
 \le f(a)=\int g(a\mid w)p(w)\,dw
 \le C_g\int p(w)\,dw=C_g. \tag{1}
\]
The assumptions \(\texttt{ass:strict-slope}\) and \(\texttt{ass:variance}\) give
\[
 0<c_\theta\le\theta'(a)\le C_\theta,
 \qquad
 0<v_\kappa\le\kappa(a)\le V_\kappa. \tag{2}
\]
Thus every quantity divided by in the definition of \(h_n(a)\) is strictly positive, so \(h_n(a)>0\).  Combining (1)--(2), and taking cube roots only after division by positive quantities, yields
\[
 \left(\frac{4v_\kappa}{nC_gC_\theta^2}\right)^{1/3}
 \le h_n(a)\le
 \left(\frac{4V_\kappa}{nc_gc_\theta^2}\right)^{1/3}. \tag{3}
\]
Multiplication by \(n^{1/3}>0\) proves the asserted uniform bounds on \(n^{1/3}h_n(a)\).

Inverting the positive defining expression gives
\[
 h_n(a)^{-1}=n^{1/3}
 \left\{\frac{f(a)\theta'(a)^2}{4\kappa(a)}\right\}^{1/3}. \tag{4}
\]
Taking reciprocals in (3) bounds the right-hand side of (4) between
\[
 n^{1/3}\left(\frac{c_gc_\theta^2}{4V_\kappa}\right)^{1/3}
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 n^{1/3}\left(\frac{C_gC_\theta^2}{4v_\kappa}\right)^{1/3}. \tag{5}
\]
For \(a_1<a_2\) in \(J\), integration of (5) gives
\[
 n^{1/3}\left(\frac{c_gc_\theta^2}{4V_\kappa}\right)^{1/3}(a_2-a_1)
 \le \Psi_{n,P}(a_2)-\Psi_{n,P}(a_1)
 \le n^{1/3}\left(\frac{C_gC_\theta^2}{4v_\kappa}\right)^{1/3}(a_2-a_1). \tag{6}
\]
Both bounding constants are finite and strictly positive.  Hence \(\Psi_{n,P}\) is strictly increasing and Lipschitz on \(J\), and (6) also makes its inverse Lipschitz on its image.  Thus it is bi-Lipschitz.  Since \(\Psi_{n,P}(\inf J)=0\), the image is an interval of length \(\Psi_{n,P}(\sup J)\).  Integrating (4) and invoking \(\texttt{def:spatial-clock}\) proves
\[
 \Psi_{n,P}(\sup J)
 =n^{1/3}\int_J
 \left\{\frac{f(a)\theta'(a)^2}{4\kappa(a)}\right\}^{1/3}\,da
 =n^{1/3}\mathcal V(P)=T_n(P). \tag{7}
\]
All constants in (3), (5), and (6) depend only on the fixed model constants, so the bounds are uniform over \(P\in\mathcal M\) and \(a\in J\).  The derivation is an exact observed-law coordinate identity and makes no coupling claim for an observed likelihood-ratio statistic.

%%% FIELD proof-conditional-centering
The pointwise bounds proved in \(\texttt{thm:causal-transfer}\) give, for every \(P\in\mathcal M\) and \(a\in J\),
\[
 \left(\frac{c_gc_\theta^2}{4V_\kappa}\right)^{1/3}
 \le
 \left\{\frac{f(a)\theta'(a)^2}{4\kappa(a)}\right\}^{1/3}
 \le
 \left(\frac{C_gC_\theta^2}{4v_\kappa}\right)^{1/3}. \tag{1}
\]
Integration over \(J\), followed by \(\texttt{def:spatial-clock}\), yields
\[
 |J|\left(\frac{c_gc_\theta^2}{4V_\kappa}\right)^{1/3}
 \le\mathcal V(P)\le
 |J|\left(\frac{C_gC_\theta^2}{4v_\kappa}\right)^{1/3}. \tag{2}
\]
Because \(J=[\delta,1-\delta]\) and \(0<\delta<1/2\), one has \(|J|=1-2\delta>0\).  All remaining factors in the lower bound are positive.  Consequently,
\[
 \inf_{P\in\mathcal M}T_n(P)
 \ge n^{1/3}|J|\left(\frac{c_gc_\theta^2}{4V_\kappa}\right)^{1/3}
 \longrightarrow\infty. \tag{3}
\]

We now work under the two conditional premises stated in the theorem.  Let
\[
 b_n=b_{\mathcal E}(T_n(P)),
 \qquad
 \widehat b_n=b_{\mathcal E}(\widehat T_n).
\]
The clock definitions give the exact identity
\[
 \frac{\widehat T_n}{T_n(P)}
 =\frac{\widehat{\mathcal V}_n}{\mathcal V(P)}. \tag{4}
\]
The assumed uniform relative consistency, together with positivity and continuity of the logarithm at \(1\), implies
\[
 \log\frac{\widehat T_n}{T_n(P)}=o_P(1) \tag{5}
\]
uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal M\).  Equations (3)--(4) also show that \(T_n(P)\) diverges uniformly and that \(\widehat T_n\) diverges uniformly in probability.

For \(v>0\),
\[
 \mathcal E'(v)=\beta+\frac{\gamma}{v}\longrightarrow\beta>0. \tag{6}
\]
Thus, with probability tending uniformly to one, both inverse centerings lie in the stipulated eventual interval on which \(\mathcal E\) is strictly increasing.  Since their images \(\log T_n(P)\) and \(\log\widehat T_n\) diverge and \(\mathcal E(v)=\beta v+o(v)\), it follows that \(b_n\to\infty\) uniformly and \(\widehat b_n\to\infty\) uniformly in probability.

On this event, the mean-value theorem supplies a point \(\widetilde b_n\) between \(b_n\) and \(\widehat b_n\) such that
\[
 \mathcal E'(\widetilde b_n)(\widehat b_n-b_n)
 =\mathcal E(\widehat b_n)-\mathcal E(b_n)
 =\log\frac{\widehat T_n}{T_n(P)}. \tag{7}
\]
Both endpoints diverge, so \(\widetilde b_n\to\infty\) uniformly in probability.  By the definition
\(
 a_{\mathcal E}(T)=1/\mathcal E'(b_{\mathcal E}(T))
\), equations (5)--(7) give
\[
 \frac{b_{\mathcal E}(\widehat T_n)-b_{\mathcal E}(T_n(P))}
 {a_{\mathcal E}(T_n(P))}
 =\frac{\mathcal E'(b_n)}{\mathcal E'(\widetilde b_n)}
 \log\frac{\widehat T_n}{T_n(P)}
 =o_P(1), \tag{8}
\]
because the derivative ratio converges uniformly in probability to \(\beta/\beta=1\).  Similarly,
\[
 \frac{a_{\mathcal E}(\widehat T_n)}{a_{\mathcal E}(T_n(P))}
 =\frac{\mathcal E'(b_n)}{\mathcal E'(\widehat b_n)}
 \longrightarrow1 \tag{9}
\]
uniformly.  The future-excursion premise supplies only the contemplated interpretation of these expressions as a center and scale.  Equations (4)--(9) prove conditional plug-in equivalence; they do not establish clock consistency, an excursion law, a critical value, or coverage.

%%% FIELD proof-homogeneous-reduction
Assume that \(f(a)=f_0\), \(\theta'(a)=r_0\), and \(\kappa(a)=\kappa_0\) on \(J\), where all three constants are positive.  Direct substitution into \(\texttt{def:spatial-clock}\) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal V(P)
 &=\int_J\left(\frac{f_0r_0^2}{4\kappa_0}\right)^{1/3}\,da
 =|J|\left(\frac{f_0r_0^2}{4\kappa_0}\right)^{1/3},\\
 T_n(P)
 &=n^{1/3}\mathcal V(P)
 =n^{1/3}|J|\left(\frac{f_0r_0^2}{4\kappa_0}\right)^{1/3}.
 \end{aligned} \tag{1}
\]
The inverse local scale from \(\texttt{thm:causal-transfer}\) is constant in \(a\):
\[
 h_n(a)^{-1}=n^{1/3}
 \left(\frac{f_0r_0^2}{4\kappa_0}\right)^{1/3}. \tag{2}
\]
Integrating (2) from \(\inf J\) to \(a\) shows that \(\Psi_{n,P}\) is affine.  Evaluation at \(\sup J\) gives exactly the image length in (1).

For the fixed-location assertion, apply \(\texttt{lem:canonical-wellposedness}\) with \(m=1\), \(x_1=0\), and common shift equal to an arbitrary \(x\in\mathbb R\).  Its finite-dimensional stationarity conclusion gives
\[
 D(x)\stackrel{d}{=}D(0). \tag{3}
\]
The proof of that lemma obtains (3) by translating the constraint and subtracting the affine tangent, which is precisely translation and affine GCM equivariance.  Equations (1)--(3) are constant-coefficient clock and fixed-location identities only; they imply neither a growing-domain excursion theorem nor statistical calibration.
