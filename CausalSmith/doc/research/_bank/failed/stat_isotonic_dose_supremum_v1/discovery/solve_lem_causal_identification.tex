%%% FIELD prop-exact-dose-nonidentification-statement
The frozen model assumptions do not identify the exact-dose causal curve.  More precisely, there exist compatible fixed constants, an observed law \(P\in\mathcal M\), and two admissible full-data extensions \(P^{\mathrm F}_1\) and \(P^{\mathrm F}_2\) satisfying every membership condition in \(\texttt{def:model-class}\), with the same \((W,A,Y)\)-marginal \(P\), such that, for every deterministic \(a\in[0,1]\),
\[
 \mathbb E_{P^{\mathrm F}_1}[Y(a)]=\theta(a)=a+\frac{1}{10},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_{P^{\mathrm F}_2}[Y(a)]=a\ne\theta(a).
\]
Consequently the exact-dose causal mean is not identified, including on \(J\): consistency at the realized dose, sectionwise conditional exchangeability, strict overlap, smoothness of the observed regression, and continuity of a chosen conditional potential-outcome mean version do not force all admissible full-data extensions of an observed law to agree on \(\mathbb E[Y(a)]\).
%%% FIELD prop-exact-dose-nonidentification-proof
We construct the two full-data extensions and verify every condition equation by equation.  Choose
\[
 d=1,\qquad s=7,\qquad \delta=\frac14,\qquad
 c_{\theta}=C_{\theta}=c_p=C_p=c_g=C_g=1,
 \qquad v_{\kappa}=V_{\kappa}=\frac1{100},\qquad B=2.
\]
The declared symbol ranges give \(d\in\mathbb N\), \(d>0\), \(s=7>3(d+1)=6\), and \(0<\delta<1/2\), hence \(J=[1/4,3/4]\).  The six one-condition compatibility requirements hold because
\[
 c_{\theta}=C_{\theta},\qquad c_p=1=C_p,\qquad
 c_g=1=C_g,\qquad v_{\kappa}=V_{\kappa}.
\]
These equalities verify \(\texttt{ass:theta-constant-compatibility}\), \(\texttt{ass:p-lower-compatibility}\), \(\texttt{ass:p-upper-compatibility}\), \(\texttt{ass:g-lower-compatibility}\), \(\texttt{ass:g-upper-compatibility}\), and \(\texttt{ass:variance-constant-compatibility}\).  Also,
\[
 \frac{c_{\theta}}2+\sqrt{v_{\kappa}}
 =\frac12+\frac1{10}=\frac35<2=B,
\]
which verifies \(\texttt{ass:bounded-witness-compatibility}\).

Consider the candidate observed-law primitives
\[
 p_\star(w)=1,\qquad g_\star(a\mid w)=1,\qquad
 m_\star(a,w)=a+\frac1{10},\qquad
 r_\star(a,w)=\left(a+\frac1{10}\right)^2+\frac1{100}.
\]
They are polynomials on compact cubes and therefore have finite \(C^7\) norms.  The quantity \(L_0\) in the frozen core is also finite because all functions entering its definition are polynomials.  Choose the fixed radius
\[
 L\ge \max\left\{L_0,\lVert p_\star\rVert_{C^7},
 \lVert g_\star\rVert_{C^7},\lVert m_\star\rVert_{C^7},
 \lVert r_\star\rVert_{C^7}\right\}.
\]
This choice verifies \(\texttt{ass:radius-compatibility}\), and it will verify each of the four radius-\(L\) smoothness conditions once the displayed functions are shown to be versions of the observed primitives.

On a probability space let \(W\), \(A\), and \(R\) be mutually independent, let \(W\) and \(A\) each be uniform on \([0,1]\), and impose
\[
 \Pr(R=1)=\Pr(R=-1)=\frac12.
\]
Define
\[
 Y=A+\frac1{10}R+\frac1{10},
\]
and let \(P\) be the law of \((W,A,Y)\).  Since \(0\le Y\le6/5<B\), \(\texttt{ass:outcome-bound}\) holds.  Mutual independence and uniformity provide the everywhere-defined density versions
\[
 p(w)=1,qquad g(a\mid w)=1,qquad
 f(a)=\int_0^1g(a\mid w)p(w)\,dw=1.
\]
Thus
\[
 \int_0^1p(w)\,dw=1,
 \qquad
 \int_0^1g(a\mid w)\,da=1\quad\text{for every }w\in[0,1],
\]
which verifies \(\texttt{ass:p-normalization}\) and \(\texttt{ass:g-normalization}\).  Because all four density-bound constants equal one, the same versions satisfy
\[
 c_p\le p(w)\le C_p,
 \qquad
 c_g\le g(a\mid w)\le C_g
\]
throughout their domains; this proves \(\texttt{ass:p-bounds}\) and \(\texttt{ass:overlap}\).  In particular, \(f(a)=1>0\), so every later division by \(f(a)\) is legitimate.

Direct conditioning, using \(\mathbb E[R]=0\) and \(R^2=1\), gives the everywhere-defined versions
\[
 m(a,w)=\mathbb E_P[Y\mid A=a,W=w]=a+\frac1{10}=m_\star(a,w)
\]
and
\[
 r(a,w)=\mathbb E_P[Y^2\mid A=a,W=w]
 =\left(a+\frac1{10}\right)^2+\frac1{100}=r_\star(a,w).
\]
Together with \(p=p_\star\), \(g=g_\star\), and the choice of \(L\), these identities verify \(\texttt{ass:p-smooth}\), \(\texttt{ass:g-smooth}\), \(\texttt{ass:m-smooth}\), and \(\texttt{ass:r-smooth}\).  By the definition of the observed-law functional \(\theta\) and the normalization of \(p\),
\[
 \theta(a)=\int_0^1m(a,w)p(w)\,dw=a+\frac1{10}
 \quad\text{for every }a\in[0,1].
\]
Hence \(\theta\) is nondecreasing, as required by \(\texttt{ass:monotone}\), and
\[
 \theta'(a)=1,
 \qquad c_{\theta}\le\theta'(a)\le C_{\theta}
 \quad\text{for every }a\in J,
\]
which verifies \(\texttt{ass:strict-slope}\).

The definitions of \(\sigma^2\) and \(e\) now yield
\[
 \sigma^2(a,w)=r(a,w)-m(a,w)^2=\frac1{100},
 \qquad e(a,w)=\frac{g(a\mid w)}{f(a)}=1.
\]
Using the definitions of \(\xi\) and \(\varepsilon\),
\[
 \varepsilon(O)=\frac{Y-m(A,W)}{e(A,W)}=\frac1{10}R.
\]
It follows that the constant conditional-moment version is
\[
 \kappa(a)=\mathbb E_P[\varepsilon(O)^2\mid A=a]
 =\frac1{100}=v_{\kappa}=V_{\kappa}
 \quad\text{for every }a\in J,
\]
which verifies \(\texttt{ass:variance}\) and its required positivity.

We next construct two full-data extensions of this same \(P\).  For every \(a\in[0,1]\), set
\[
 Y_1(a)=a+\frac1{10}R+\frac1{10},
 \qquad
 Y_2(a)=a+\frac1{10}R+\frac1{10}\mathbf 1\{a=A\}.
\]
The first map is Borel measurable in \((a,A,R)\).  The second is also Borel measurable because the diagonal \(\{(a,A):a=A\}\) is closed.  Consequently each sample path is Borel measurable and each coordinate evaluation is a random variable, so both processes are random elements of the evaluation-sigma-field space \(\mathcal Y_B\).  Moreover,
\[
 -\frac1{10}\le Y_j(a)\le\frac65<B
 \quad\text{for all }a\in[0,1]\text{ and }j\in\{1,2\},
\]
so the processes take values in the stipulated range \([-B,B]\).

For \(j\in\{1,2\}\), let \(P_j^{\mathrm F}\) be the law of
\[
 \bigl(W,A,Y,\{Y_j(a):a\in[0,1]\}\bigr).
\]
Its \((W,A,Y)\)-marginal is \(P\) by construction, proving \(\texttt{ass:observed-marginal}\).  Evaluation at the realized dose gives
\[
 Y_1(A)=A+\frac1{10}R+\frac1{10}=Y
\]
and
\[
 Y_2(A)=A+\frac1{10}R+\frac1{10}\mathbf 1\{A=A\}=Y.
\]
Thus each extension satisfies \(\texttt{ass:consistency}\).

Fix a deterministic \(a\in[0,1]\).  The continuous uniform law of \(A\) gives \(\Pr(A=a)=0\); hence
\[
 Y_1(a)=a+\frac1{10}R+\frac1{10}\quad\text{almost surely},
 \qquad
 Y_2(a)=a+\frac1{10}R\quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
Each right-hand side is a measurable function of \(R\), and \(R\) is independent of \((A,W)\).  Therefore, for bounded Borel functions \(h\) and \(q\),
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[h\{Y_j(a)\}q(A)\mid W\right]
 =\mathbb E\!\left[h\{Y_j(a)\}\mid W\right]
  \mathbb E\!\left[q(A)\mid W\right]
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
This conditional factorization proves \(Y_j(a)\perp A\mid W\) for each deterministic \(a\), and hence verifies \(\texttt{ass:exchangeability}\) for both extensions.

Since \(\mathbb E[R]=0\), define
\[
 \mu^{\mathrm F}_1(a,w)=a+\frac1{10},
 \qquad
 \mu^{\mathrm F}_2(a,w)=a.
\]
Both functions are jointly Borel measurable and continuous in \(a\) for every \(w\).  The preceding fixed-\(a\) almost-sure identities and the independence of \(R\) and \(W\) imply
\[
 \mu^{\mathrm F}_j(a,W)
 =\mathbb E_{P_j^{\mathrm F}}[Y_j(a)\mid W]
 \quad\text{almost surely for every fixed }a.
\]
Thus each is a version meeting every clause of \(\texttt{ass:potential-mean-continuity}\).

All observed-law membership conditions of \(\texttt{def:model-class}\) and all full-data extension conditions have now been verified.  Therefore \(P\in\mathcal M\), and \(P_1^{\mathrm F}\) and \(P_2^{\mathrm F}\) are two admissible witnesses for the same observed law.  Finally, for every deterministic \(a\in[0,1]\),
\[
 \mathbb E_{P_1^{\mathrm F}}[Y_1(a)]
 =a+\frac1{10}=\theta(a),
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_{P_2^{\mathrm F}}[Y_2(a)]
 =a\ne a+\frac1{10}=\theta(a).
\]
The modification in \(Y_2\) is supported on the random diagonal \(a=A\).  It is null in every deterministic-dose section, which preserves sectionwise exchangeability and the continuous conditional-mean versions, but consistency evaluates the process exactly on that diagonal.  The two admissible extensions therefore disagree at every deterministic dose, proving exact-dose nonidentification.
