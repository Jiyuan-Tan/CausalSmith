Let \(\Phi(h)\) be the deterministic likelihood-ratio energy at the origin obtained from \(\texttt{def:canonical-field}\) after replacing \(\mathbb W\) by an absolutely continuous function \(h\) such that \(h(0)=0\) and \(h'\in L^2(\mathbb R)\).  For \(b>0\), define
\[
 h_b(t)=b^2h(t/b).
\]
Horizontal dilation by \(b\) and vertical dilation by \(b^2\) commute with taking greatest convex minorants: applying the inverse dilation to any convex minorant of the dilated path gives a convex minorant of the original path, and conversely.  Thus the unrestricted and split-constrained slopes are multiplied by \(b\).  Changing variables \(t=br\) in the likelihood-ratio energy gives
\[
 \Phi(h_b)=b^3\Phi(h).
\]
Because \(h_b'(t)=b h'(t/b)\) almost everywhere, the same change of variables gives
\[
 \frac12\int_{\mathbb R}\{h_b'(t)\}^2\,dt
 =\frac{b^3}{2}\int_{\mathbb R}\{h'(r)\}^2\,dr.
\]
Brownian scaling gives \(b^{1/2}\mathbb W(\,\cdot\,/b)\stackrel{d}=\mathbb W\).  Taking \(b=u^{1/3}\) and \(\varepsilon=u^{-1/2}=b^{-3/2}\), one has
\[
 (\varepsilon\mathbb W)_b(t)=b^2\varepsilon\mathbb W(t/b)
 =b^{1/2}\mathbb W(t/b)\stackrel{d}=\mathbb W(t).
\]
The energy homogeneity therefore yields the exact distributional reduction
\[
 \Pr\{D(0)>u\}
 =\Pr\{\Phi(u^{-1/2}\mathbb W)>1\}.
\]
This equality identifies the small-noise scaling but, by itself, is not a large-deviation principle.

For the deterministic variational value in \(\texttt{def:excursion-regime}\), one has
\[
 \frac12\le I_\star\le\frac98.
\]
To prove the lower bound, put \(y(t)=2t+h'(t)\).  On a finite interval, let \(\mathcal C\) be the closed convex cone of nondecreasing slopes and let \(\mathcal C_0\subset\mathcal C\) impose the split constraint at zero.  If \(G\) is the greatest convex minorant of a primitive of \(y\), \(g=G'\), and \(H\) is that primitive minus \(G\), then \(H\ge0\) and the measure \(dg\) is supported on \(\{H=0\}\).  Integration by parts gives, for every \(v\in\mathcal C\),
\[
 \int (y-g)(v-g)
 =-\int H\,d(v-g)\le0.
\]
Hence \(g\) is the least-squares projection of \(y\) onto \(\mathcal C\).  Applying the same argument on the two half-intervals and capping the left slope above by zero and flooring the right slope below by zero identifies the split-constrained slope with the projection onto \(\mathcal C_0\).  The baseline slope \(r_0(t)=2t\) belongs to \(\mathcal C_0\).  Nestedness and the projection identity thus imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 0\le\Phi(h)
 &=\operatorname{dist}^2(y,\mathcal C_0)
   -\operatorname{dist}^2(y,\mathcal C)\\
 &\le \operatorname{dist}^2(y,\mathcal C_0)
 \le \lVert y-r_0\rVert_2^2
 =\lVert h'\rVert_2^2.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Exhausting \(\mathbb R\) by finite intervals on which the two fits are defined preserves the inequality whenever \(\Phi(h)\) is well defined.  Therefore \(\Phi(h)\ge1\) entails \(\frac12\lVert h'\rVert_2^2\ge1/2\), and taking the infimum proves \(I_\star\ge1/2\).

For the upper bound, let \(k=4^{1/3}\), let \(a=k/4\), fix the additive constant by \(h(0)=0\), and take
\[
 h'(t)=(k-2t)\mathbf 1\{-a\le t\le k/2\}.
\]
Then
\[
 y(t)=2t+h'(t)=
 \begin{cases}
 2t,&t<-a,\\
 k,&-a\le t\le k/2,\\
 2t,&t>k/2.
 \end{cases}
\]
This function is nondecreasing, so its unrestricted projection is itself.  The split-constrained projection agrees with \(y\) off \([-a,0]\) and equals zero on \([-a,0]\).  Indeed, for this candidate \(z\) and any other split-constrained nondecreasing slope \(v\),
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R}(y-z)(v-z)\,dt
 =k\int_{-a}^{0}v(t)\,dt\le0,
\]
because the left split constraint gives \(v(t)\le0\) for \(t<0\).  This is the projection variational inequality.  Consequently,
\[
 \Phi(h)=\int_{-a}^{0}k^2\,dt=ak^2=\frac{k^3}{4}=1.
\]
Direct integration gives
\[
 \frac12\int_{\mathbb R}\{h'(t)\}^2\,dt
 =\frac12\int_{-a}^{k/2}(k-2t)^2\,dt
 =\frac{(k+2a)^3}{12}
 =\frac98.
\]
Thus \(I_\star\le9/8\), and this perturbation is an explicit finite-energy plateau witness.

These calculations neither identify \(I_\star\) with a probability-tail rate nor provide a sharp bounded-level tail ratio.  Even a hypothetical logarithmic equivalent of the form
\[
 -\log\Pr\{D(0)>u\}=\beta u+o(u)
\]
would be insufficient: an \(o(u)\) remainder need not have increments tending to zero under a fixed additive change of \(u\).  A sharp Palm expansion would additionally require minimizer classification modulo translation, transverse fluctuation control, and multiplicity and conditional cluster-shape laws.  Contact truncation may separate distant blocks, but it does not rule out several roots arising from one rare contact configuration.  The exact scaling, action bracket, and plateau witness are therefore the strongest completed conclusions; the large-deviation, anticlustering, marked-Poisson, and continuum-grid steps remain open.
