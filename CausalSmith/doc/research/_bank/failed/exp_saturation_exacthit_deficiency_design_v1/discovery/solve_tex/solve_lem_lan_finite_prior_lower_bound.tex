%%% FIELD lan-finite-prior-statement
Let \(\Theta\) and \(\mathcal D\) be nonempty finite sets and let \(d\geq1\) be an integer.  Define the action simplex by \(\Delta_{\mathcal D}=\{v\in[0,1]^{\mathcal D}:\sum_{a\in\mathcal D}v_a=1\}\), and let \(\varpi=(\varpi_\theta)_{\theta\in\Theta}\) be a probability mass function.  For each positive integer \(C\), let \(\mathsf E_C=(\mathsf X_C,\mathcal F_C,\{P_{C,\theta}:\theta\in\Theta\})\) be an experiment whose laws are all dominated by a reference probability measure \(P_{C,0}\) on \((\mathsf X_C,\mathcal F_C)\), and write \(L_{C,\theta}=dP_{C,\theta}/dP_{C,0}\).  Let the limit experiment be a Gaussian-shift experiment on \(\mathbb R^d\), with canonical observation \(G\), laws \(Q_\theta\) all dominated by a reference law \(Q_0\), and likelihood ratios \(L_\theta(G)=dQ_\theta/dQ_0(G)\).  Suppose that: (i) there are measurable statistics \(T_C\colon\mathsf X_C\to\mathbb R^d\) such that, under \(P_{C,0}\),
\[
 \bigl(T_C,(L_{C,\theta})_{\theta\in\Theta}\bigr)
 \ \rightsquigarrow\ 
 \bigl(G,(L_\theta(G))_{\theta\in\Theta}\bigr),
 \qquad G\sim Q_0;
\]
(ii) every likelihood-ratio sequence is uniformly integrable, in the explicit sense that
\[
 \lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_C
 \mathbb E_{P_{C,0}}\!\left[L_{C,\theta}
 \mathbf 1\{L_{C,\theta}>R\}\right]=0
 \quad\text{for every }\theta\in\Theta;
\]
and (iii) nonnegative losses \(\ell_C(\theta,a)\) and \(\ell(\theta,a)\), indexed by \(\Theta\times\mathcal D\), satisfy
\[
 \max_{\theta\in\Theta,\ a\in\mathcal D}
 |\ell_C(\theta,a)-\ell(\theta,a)|\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 \sup_{C,\theta,a}\ell_C(\theta,a)<\infty.
\]
For a measurable randomized rule \(\delta_C\colon\mathsf X_C\to\Delta_{\mathcal D}\), define
\[
 \mathcal R_{C,\theta}(\delta_C)
 =\mathbb E_{P_{C,\theta}}\!\left[
   \sum_{a\in\mathcal D}\delta_{C,a}\ell_C(\theta,a)
 \right],
 \qquad
 \mathcal B_C(\delta_C)=\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}\varpi_\theta
 \mathcal R_{C,\theta}(\delta_C).
\]
Then every sequence \((\delta_C)\) obeys
\[
 \liminf_{C\to\infty}\mathcal B_C(\delta_C)
 \ \geq\ 
 \mathcal B_\infty^*(\varpi)
 :=\inf_{\substack{\delta\colon\mathbb R^d\to\Delta_{\mathcal D}\\
                    \delta\ \mathrm{measurable}}}
 \sum_{\theta\in\Theta}\varpi_\theta
 \mathbb E_{Q_\theta}\!\left[
   \sum_{a\in\mathcal D}\delta_a(G)\ell(\theta,a)
 \right].
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \liminf_{C\to\infty}\max_{\theta\in\Theta}
 \mathcal R_{C,\theta}(\delta_C)
 \ \geq\ \mathcal B_\infty^*(\varpi).
\]
%%% FIELD lan-finite-prior-proof
Put
\[
 X_C=\bigl(T_C,(L_{C,\theta})_{\theta\in\Theta}\bigr),
 \qquad
 X=\bigl(G,(L_\theta(G))_{\theta\in\Theta}\bigr),
\]
and write \(r=d+|\Theta|\), so that \(X_C,X\in\mathbb R^r\).  Also put
\[
 V_C=(\delta_{C,a})_{a\in\mathcal D}\in\Delta_{\mathcal D},
 \qquad
 b_C=\mathcal B_C(\delta_C).
\]
Because \(\Theta\) and \(\mathcal D\) are finite, hypothesis (iii) gives a finite constant
\[
 B=\max\left\{
       \sup_{C,\theta,a}\ell_C(\theta,a),
       \max_{\theta,a}\ell(\theta,a)
     \right\}<\infty.
 \tag{1}
\]
Thus \(0\leq b_C\leq B\).  Select a subsequence \((C_j)\) such that
\[
 b_{C_j}\longrightarrow\liminf_{C\to\infty}b_C.
 \tag{2}
\]

We first obtain a joint limit without assuming that the rules \(\delta_C\) are functions of \(T_C\).  Let \(J\colon\mathbb R^r\to(-1,1)^r\) act coordinatewise by
\[
 J(x)_s=\frac{2}{\pi}\arctan(x_s),\qquad 1\leq s\leq r.
\]
The space \([-1,1]^r\times\Delta_{\mathcal D}\) is compact and metrizable.  Apply \(\text{lem:weak-compactness-compact-laws}\) to the laws of \((J(X_{C_j}),V_{C_j})\) and pass to a further subsequence to obtain weak convergence to a probability law \(\Lambda^J\) on that compact space.  Hypothesis (i) and the continuous-mapping theorem imply \(J(X_{C_j})\rightsquigarrow J(X)\).  Hence the first marginal of \(\Lambda^J\) is the law of \(J(X)\), which assigns probability one to \((-1,1)^r\).  Since \(J^{-1}\) is continuous on \((-1,1)^r\), the extended continuous-mapping theorem gives, on writing \(\Lambda\) for the image law,
\[
 (X_{C_j},V_{C_j})\rightsquigarrow(X,V)
 \quad\text{under }P_{C_j,0},
 \tag{3}
\]
where \(V\in\Delta_{\mathcal D}\), \(\Lambda\)-almost surely.  Passing to this further subsequence does not change (2).

Fix \(\theta\in\Theta\).  Change of measure, the definition of \(V_C\), and domination by \(P_{C,0}\) yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal R_{C,\theta}(\delta_C)
 &=\mathbb E_{P_{C,\theta}}\!\left[
     \sum_{a\in\mathcal D}V_{C,a}\ell_C(\theta,a)
   \right]\\
 &=\mathbb E_{P_{C,0}}\!\left[
     L_{C,\theta}\sum_{a\in\mathcal D}V_{C,a}\ell_C(\theta,a)
   \right].
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{4}
\]
For \(R<\infty\), the map
\[
 (x,v)\longmapsto
 \bigl((0\vee x_\theta^{L})\wedge R\bigr)
 \sum_{a\in\mathcal D}v_a\ell(\theta,a)
\]
is bounded and continuous on \(\mathbb R^r\times\Delta_{\mathcal D}\), where \(x_\theta^{L}\) denotes the coordinate corresponding to \(L_{C,\theta}\).  All likelihood-ratio coordinates are nonnegative, so this map agrees almost surely with the truncation displayed below.  Therefore (3) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\mathbb E_{P_{C_j,0}}\!\left[
   (L_{C_j,\theta}\wedge R)
   \sum_{a\in\mathcal D}V_{C_j,a}\ell(\theta,a)
 \right]\\
 &\hspace{25mm}\longrightarrow
 \mathbb E_{\Lambda}\!\left[
   (L_\theta(G)\wedge R)
   \sum_{a\in\mathcal D}V_a\ell(\theta,a)
 \right].
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{5}
\]
If
\[
 \epsilon_C=max_{\vartheta\in\Theta,\,a\in\mathcal D}
 |\ell_C(\vartheta,a)-\ell(\vartheta,a)|,
\]
then hypothesis (iii) says \(\epsilon_C\to0\), and replacing \(\ell\) by \(\ell_{C_j}\) on the left side of (5) changes that side by at most
\[
 R\epsilon_{C_j}.
 \tag{6}
\]
Moreover, by (1), the truncation error in (4) is at most
\[
 B\,\mathbb E_{P_{C_j,0}}\!\left[
 L_{C_j,\theta}\mathbf 1\{L_{C_j,\theta}>R\}
 \right].
 \tag{7}
\]
Hypothesis (ii) makes (7) tend to zero uniformly in \(j\) as \(R\to\infty\).  On the limit side, \(L_\theta(G)\) is a nonnegative \(Q_0\)-likelihood ratio and hence
\[
 \mathbb E_{Q_0}[L_\theta(G)]=1.
\]
Consequently, integrability and dominated convergence imply
\[
 B\,\mathbb E_{Q_0}\!\left[
 L_\theta(G)\mathbf 1\{L_\theta(G)>R\}
 \right]\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{8}
\]
Taking \(j\to\infty\) in (5), using (6), and then taking \(R\to\infty\) using (7)--(8), proves
\[
 \mathcal R_{C_j,\theta}(\delta_{C_j})
 \longrightarrow
 \mathbb E_{\Lambda}\!\left[
 L_\theta(G)\sum_{a\in\mathcal D}V_a\ell(\theta,a)
 \right].
 \tag{9}
\]

It remains to show that the right side of (9) is the risk of a rule based only on the Gaussian observation.  For each \(a\in\mathcal D\), define a finite Borel measure on \(\mathbb R^d\) by
\[
 \nu_a(E)=\mathbb E_{\Lambda}\!\left[V_a\mathbf 1\{G\in E\}\right].
\]
Since \(0\leq V_a\leq1\), one has \(\nu_a\ll Q_0\).  Let
\[
 \bar\delta_a=\frac{d\nu_a}{dQ_0}.
\]
The identities \(V_a\geq0\) and \(\sum_aV_a=1\) imply, respectively,
\[
 \bar\delta_a(g)\geq0,
 \qquad
 \sum_{a\in\mathcal D}\bar\delta_a(g)=1
 \quad\text{for }Q_0\text{-almost every }g.
 \tag{10}
\]
After redefining on one \(Q_0\)-null set, \(\bar\delta\colon\mathbb R^d\to\Delta_{\mathcal D}\) is a measurable randomized rule.  The first marginal in (3) has \(L_\theta(G)\) as a measurable function of \(G\).  The Radon--Nikodym identity, followed by the likelihood-ratio identity, therefore gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_{\Lambda}\!\left[
 L_\theta(G)\sum_aV_a\ell(\theta,a)
 \right]
 &=\mathbb E_{Q_0}\!\left[
 L_\theta(G)\sum_a\bar\delta_a(G)\ell(\theta,a)
 \right]\\
 &=\mathbb E_{Q_\theta}\!\left[
 \sum_a\bar\delta_a(G)\ell(\theta,a)
 \right].
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{11}
\]
All terms in (11) are integrable by (1) and \(\mathbb E_{Q_0}L_\theta(G)=1\).

Because \(\Theta\) is finite, equations (2), (9), and (11) may be summed against \(\varpi\).  The definition of the optimal limit Bayes risk then yields
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \liminf_{C\to\infty}\mathcal B_C(\delta_C)
 &=\lim_{j\to\infty}b_{C_j}\\
 &=\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}\varpi_\theta
   \mathbb E_{Q_\theta}\!\left[
     \sum_{a\in\mathcal D}\bar\delta_a(G)\ell(\theta,a)
   \right]\\
 &\geq\mathcal B_\infty^*(\varpi).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{12}
\]
Finally, for every \(C\), nonnegativity of the prior weights and \(\sum_\theta\varpi_\theta=1\) imply
\[
 \max_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathcal R_{C,\theta}(\delta_C)
 \geq\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}\varpi_\theta
       \mathcal R_{C,\theta}(\delta_C)
 =\mathcal B_C(\delta_C).
\]
Taking lower limits and applying (12) proves the claimed maximal-risk inequality.
