Let \(S\) be a compact metric space.  Every family of Borel probability laws on \(S\) is uniformly tight, because \(S\) itself is a compact set having probability one under every such law.  By Prohorov's theorem (\texttt{vanDerVaart1998}, Section 2.4), the family is relatively compact for weak convergence.  The class of probability laws is weakly closed: weak limits preserve nonnegativity and preserve total mass because the constant function \(1\) is bounded and continuous.  Therefore the set of Borel probability laws on \(S\) is weakly compact.
