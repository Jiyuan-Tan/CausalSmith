Write \(d=|A|\), \(\mathbf1=\mathbf1_A\), and \(L(h,a)=\max_kh_k-h_a\).  Then \(L(h+c\mathbf1,a)=L(h,a)\).  For a randomized selector \(\delta=(\delta_a)_a\), define its F{\o}lner average
\[
 \delta_{a,T}(x)=\frac1{2T}\int_{-T}^T\delta_a(x+c\mathbf1)\,dc.
\]
Gaussian translation and loss invariance imply
\[
 R(h,\delta_T)=\frac1{2T}\int_{-T}^TR(h+c\mathbf1,\delta)\,dc
 \leq\sup_{u\in\mathbb R^A}R(u,\delta). \tag{27}
\]
The functions \(\delta_{a,T}\) lie in the unit ball of \(L^2\) on each bounded box.  To obtain a limit without adding a compactness hypothesis, fix a countable orthonormal basis on each box.  Every coefficient sequence is bounded; successive Bolzano--Weierstrass subsequences, diagonalized over bases, boxes, and the finite actions, make every coefficient converge.  The sums of squared limiting coefficients are at most one because this holds for every finite partial sum, so the limits define \(L^2\) functions and convergence is weak by approximation with finite basis spans.  A further diagonal subsequence gives limits \(\bar\delta_a\) on all boxes; testing against negative parts and against \(\sum_a\bar\delta_a-1\) preserves nonnegativity and \(\sum_a\bar\delta_a=1\).  Against a fixed Gaussian density times the bounded-on-compact loss, weak convergence passes the integral on each box, while Gaussian tails control its complement.  Hence (27) gives \(R(h,\bar\delta)\leq\sup_uR(u,\delta)\) for every fixed \(h\), and therefore the same inequality after taking \(\sup_h\).

For fixed \(c_0\), the two intervals in the averages defining \(\delta_T(x+c_0\mathbf1)\) and \(\delta_T(x)\) differ in total length at most \(2|c_0|\), so their difference is bounded by \(|c_0|/T\).  Passing first for rational \(c_0\) and then using continuity of translations in local \(L^2\) yields a version satisfying \(\bar\delta(x+c\mathbf1)=\bar\delta(x)\) for every real \(c\).  Thus invariant selectors attain the same infimum; the reverse inequality follows because they form a subclass.

The matrix \(D_A\) has rank \(d-1\), kernel \(\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf1\}\), and right inverse \(J_A=D_A'(D_AD_A')^{-1}\).  An invariant measurable selector is constant on every fiber of \(D_A\), hence factors measurably through \(D_Ax\).  Linear Gaussian transformation gives
\[
 D_AX\sim N(D_Ah,D_A\Sigma D_A')=N(D_Ah,\Omega_A).
\]
For nonzero \(u\), \(D_A'u\ne0\) and
\[
 u'\Omega_Au=(D_A'u)'\Sigma(D_A'u)>0, \tag{28}
\]
so the quotient covariance is positive definite.  The same argument applies on a fixed quotient ball.

Finally, invariant risks depend only on \(g=D_Ah\), and the balls \(\{g:\|g\|_2\leq M\}\) increase to \(\mathbb R^{d-1}\).  Thus \(G_{A,M}\) is nondecreasing and at most \(G_A\).  Choose \(1/M\)-optimal selectors on successive balls and repeat the diagonal weak-compactness argument on quotient-observation boxes.  The limit has risk at most \(\lim_MG_{A,M}\) at every fixed \(g\), hence on the union of the balls.  Therefore \(G_A\leq\lim_MG_{A,M}\), proving \(G_{A,M}\uparrow G_A\).
