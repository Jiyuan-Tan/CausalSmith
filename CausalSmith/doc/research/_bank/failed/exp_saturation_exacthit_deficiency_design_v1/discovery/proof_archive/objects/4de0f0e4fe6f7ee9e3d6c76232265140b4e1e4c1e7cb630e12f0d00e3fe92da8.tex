Put \(g_z=\Pr(Z=z)\).  The factorization lemma gives \(g_z=q_k/M_k>0\) for \(z\in\mathcal S_k\).  In the saturated observed model a regular conditional-outcome submodel has a score \(s_z(Y(z))\) in each cell, with \(\mathbb E[s_z(Y(z))]=0\); the assignment law is fixed by design and hence has zero score.  Differentiating the exact-slice functional along this submodel gives
\[
 \dot U_k(s)=\frac1{M_k}\sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}
 \mathbb E[(W(z)-\mu_z)s_z(Y(z))]. \tag{2}
\]
On the other hand, for \(\phi_k^B=q_k^{-1}{\mathbf 1}\{Z\in\mathcal S_k\}(W(Z)-\mu_Z)\),
\[
 \mathbb E[\phi_k^B s_Z]
 =\sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}\frac{q_k/M_k}{q_k}
 \mathbb E[(W(z)-\mu_z)s_z(Y(z))]
 =\dot U_k(s). \tag{3}
\]
The coordinate is conditionally centered and bounded by \(q_k^{-1}\), so it belongs to \(L_0^2(Q^B_{P_0,p})\).  It also belongs to the direct sum of the conditional-outcome tangent spaces.  Equations (2)--(3) therefore identify it as the unique tangent-space Riesz representer, hence the canonical gradient.  Its variance is
\[
 \mathbb E[(\phi_k^B)^2]
 =\frac1{q_k^2}\sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}\frac{q_k}{M_k}\operatorname{Var}_{P_0}(W(z))
 =\frac{\tau_k^2}{q_k}. \tag{4}
\]
For \(j\ne k\), disjointness of the slices makes \(\phi_j^B\phi_k^B=0\) pointwise, so the covariance is zero.

For a target cell \(z\in\mathcal S_k\), the laws of large numbers give \(N_z/C\to_Pg_z\).  On \(N_z>0\), ratio expansion yields
\[
 \sqrt C(\overline W_z-\mu_z)
 =\frac1{g_z\sqrt C}\sum_{c=1}^C{\mathbf 1}\{Z_c=z\}\{W_c(z)-\mu_z\}+o_P(1). \tag{5}
\]
Moreover \(\Pr(N_z=0)=(1-g_z)^C\leq e^{-Cg_z}\), so the bounded fallback does not change (5).  Averaging (5) over \(z\in\mathcal S_k\), using \(M_kg_z=q_k\), gives the joint asymptotic linear representation
\[
 \sqrt C\{\widetilde U_C-U(P_0)\}
 =\frac1{\sqrt C}\sum_{c=1}^C(\phi_1^B(O_c),\ldots,\phi_K^B(O_c))'+o_P(1). \tag{6}
\]
The summands in (6) are iid, centered, and bounded.  Expanding their characteristic function at the origin gives, for each fixed \(a\in\mathbb R^K\),
\[
 \left(1-\frac{a'\Sigma_B^{(K)}a}{2C}+O(C^{-3/2})\right)^C
 \longrightarrow \exp\{-a'\Sigma_B^{(K)}a/2\}.
\]
The Cram\'er--Wold argument therefore proves the asserted multivariate normal limit, including possibly degenerate inactive coordinates.

For completeness, on the event that the empirical hit rate \(\widehat q_k=C^{-1}\sum_c{\mathbf 1}\{Z_c\in\mathcal S_k\}\) is positive, form empirical coordinates
\[
 \widehat\phi_{c,k}=\widehat q_k^{-1}{\mathbf 1}\{Z_c\in\mathcal S_k\}
 \{W_c(Z_c)-\overline W_{Z_c}\}.
\]
The prescribed fallback covers the complementary event.  This is the empirical-score construction named by \(\widehat\Sigma_C^{(K)}\).  There are finitely many cells, each cell count diverges in probability, and all outcomes are bounded.  Cellwise laws of large numbers thus imply
\[
 C^{-1}\sum_{c=1}^C\widehat\phi_c\widehat\phi_c'
 \to_P\mathbb E[\phi^B(\phi^B)']=\Sigma_B^{(K)}. \tag{7}
\]
Off-diagonal empirical products are identically zero because the slices are disjoint.  Equations (6)--(7) prove the full claim, and taking the principal subvector and submatrix gives the active restriction.
