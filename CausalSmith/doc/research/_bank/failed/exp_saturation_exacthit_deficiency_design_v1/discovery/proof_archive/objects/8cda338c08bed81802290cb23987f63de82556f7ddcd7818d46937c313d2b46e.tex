Under \(\texttt{ass:cr-exact-slices}\), \(Z_c\in\mathcal S_{L_c}\) almost surely.  Since \(m_1,\ldots,m_K\) are distinct, the slices are disjoint and
\[
L_c=k\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\sum_{j=1}^nZ_{c,j}=m_k. \tag{15}
\]
Thus the deleted label vector is a deterministic measurable function of the retained assignment vectors.  Conversely, the reduced record is obtained from the full record by forgetting that vector.  The two experiments are therefore isomorphic through parameter-free measurable kernels for every fixed label-count vector and every schedule law.  Composing an arbitrary randomized rule on any standard-Borel decision space with either kernel preserves its pointwise risk.  Taking the supremum over \(P\) and infimum over rules proves the displayed minimax equality.  The conclusion applies in particular to finite ranking actions.

The causal target is not defined by this experiment equivalence.  Rather, \(\texttt{ass:cr-label-schedule-independence}\) and \(\texttt{ass:cr-exact-slices}\) give
\[
\mathbb E_P[W(Z_c)\mid L_c=k]
=\frac1{M_k}\sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}\mathbb E_P[W(z)]
=U_k, \tag{16}
\]
where the last equality is \(\texttt{def:exact-slice-welfare}\).  Hence the isomorphic observed experiments concern the same fixed causal exact-slice welfare vector.
