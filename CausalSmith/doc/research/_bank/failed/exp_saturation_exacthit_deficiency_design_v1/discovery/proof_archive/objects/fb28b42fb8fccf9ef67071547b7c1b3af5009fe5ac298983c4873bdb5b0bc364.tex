Fix \(p\in\Delta_K\) and \(0<\gamma<1\).  Because every \(\pi_\ell\) is interior and \(1\leq m_k\leq n-1\),
\[
B_{k\ell}=\binom{n}{m_k}\pi_\ell^{m_k}(1-\pi_\ell)^{n-m_k}>0.
\]
Thus \(q_k=\sum_{\ell=1}^K B_{k\ell}p_\ell>0\), even when \(p\) is on the boundary of the simplex.  By \(\mathrm{lem{:}exact\mbox{-}hit\mbox{-}factorization}\), every \(z\in\mathcal S_k\) has the fixed assignment probability
\[
r_z:=\Pr(Z_c=z)=\frac{q_k}{M_k}>0. \tag{1}
\]
The schedule randomization independence assumptions imply that, conditional on \(Z_c=z\), the observed welfare has mean \(\mu_z\) and variance
\(\sigma_z^2:=\operatorname{Var}_P\{W(z)\}\).  The records are independent and identically distributed across \(c\) by \(\mathrm{ass{:}iid\mbox{-}schedules}\), \(\mathrm{ass{:}bernoulli\mbox{-}label\mbox{-}iid}\), \(\mathrm{ass{:}bernoulli\mbox{-}label\mbox{-}schedule\mbox{-}independence}\), and \(\mathrm{ass{:}bernoulli\mbox{-}units}\).

We first obtain an expansion that is uniform over the schedule law.  Put
\[
\widehat r_z=\frac{N_z}{C},\qquad
A_{C,z}=\frac1C\sum_{c=1}^C\mathbf 1\{Z_c=z\}\{W_c(z)-\mu_z\}.
\]
For each target cell, \(\mathbb E_P A_{C,z}=0\),
\[
\mathbb E_P A_{C,z}^2
=\frac{r_z\sigma_z^2}{C}\leq\frac1C,
\qquad
\mathbb E_P(\widehat r_z-r_z)^2
=\frac{r_z(1-r_z)}C\leq\frac1C, \tag{2}
\]
because \(0\leq W(z)\leq1\).  These bounds do not depend on \(P\).  Since there are only \(\sum_kM_k\) target cells and every \(r_z\) in (1) is fixed and positive, (2), a union bound, and Chebyshev's inequality give, uniformly in \(P\),
\[
\max_z|A_{C,z}|=O_P(C^{-1/2}),qquad
\max_z|\widehat r_z-r_z|=O_P(C^{-1/2}),qquad
\min_z\widehat r_z\geq\frac12\min_zr_z
\quad\text{with probability tending to one}. \tag{3}
\]
On the last event all target cells are occupied, and the definition of the calibrated cell mean yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde U_{C,k}-U_k
&=\frac1{M_k}\sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}\frac{A_{C,z}}{\widehat r_z}\\
&=\frac1C\sum_{c=1}^C
 q_k^{-1}\mathbf 1\{Z_c\in\mathcal S_k\}
 \{W_c(Z_c)-\mu_{Z_c}\}+R_{C,k},\\
R_{C,k}
&=\frac1{M_k}\sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}
A_{C,z}\left(\frac1{\widehat r_z}-\frac1{r_z}\right).
\end{aligned} \tag{4}
\]
Equations (1) and (3) show that \(R_{C,k}=O_P(C^{-1})\), uniformly in \(P\).  The probability excluded in deriving (4) tends to zero uniformly by (3).  Hence, with
\[
\xi_{c,k}(P):=q_k^{-1}\mathbf 1\{Z_c\in\mathcal S_k\}
\{W_c(Z_c)-\mu_{Z_c}\},
\]
we have the uniform asymptotic linearization
\[
\sqrt C\{\widetilde U_C-U(P)\}
=C^{-1/2}\sum_{c=1}^C\xi_c(P)+o_P(1). \tag{5}
\]
This is also the expansion by the canonical gradients \(\phi_k^B\) established in \(\mathrm{thm{:}efficient\mbox{-}exact\mbox{-}hit\mbox{-}bernoulli}\).

For every \(k\), conditioning on the disjoint assignment cells and using (1) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_P\xi_{c,k}(P)&=0,\\
\mathbb E_P\xi_{c,k}(P)^2
&=q_k^{-2}\sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}r_z\sigma_z^2
=\frac1{q_kM_k}\sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}\sigma_z^2
=\frac{\tau_k^2(P)}{q_k}. 
\end{aligned} \tag{6}
\]
For \(j\ne k\), the two slice indicators cannot both equal one, so
\(\mathbb E_P\{\xi_{c,j}(P)\xi_{c,k}(P)\}=0\).  Thus the covariance in (5) is exactly \(\Sigma_B^{(K)}(P)\).

We next prove the uniform Gaussian approximation needed for uniform rank coverage.  Let \((P_C)\) be an arbitrary sequence satisfying
\(\min_k\tau_k^2(P_C)\geq\underline\tau^2\).  Under the bounded outcome domain, the schedule space is the compact metric space
\[
\mathcal Y=[0,1]^{,n\times\mathcal Z_n}.
\]
The probability laws on \(\mathcal Y\) are weakly compact.  Hence every subsequence of \((P_C)\) has a further subsequence, not relabeled, with \(P_C\Rightarrow P_\infty\).  The maps \(Y\mapsto W(z)\) and \(Y\mapsto W(z)^2\) are bounded and continuous.  Therefore \(\mu_z(P_C)\to\mu_z(P_\infty)\), \(\sigma_z^2(P_C)\to\sigma_z^2(P_\infty)\), and, by (6),
\[
\Sigma_B^{(K)}(P_C)\longrightarrow\Sigma_B^{(K)}(P_\infty)=:\Sigma_\infty. \tag{7}
\]
In particular \(\Sigma_\infty\) is positive definite, because its \(k\)-th diagonal entry is at least \(\underline\tau^2/q_k\).

For fixed \(a\in\mathbb R^K\), the scalar \(a'\xi_c(P_C)\) is uniformly bounded by
\(\sum_k|a_k|/q_k\).  Taylor's formula with remainder therefore gives
\[
\mathbb E_{P_C}\exp\!\left\{\frac{i,a'\xi_c(P_C)}{\sqrt C}\right\}
=1-\frac{a'\Sigma_B^{(K)}(P_C)a}{2C}+O(C^{-3/2}), \tag{8}
\]
where the remainder is uniform in \(P_C\).  Independence across clusters, (7), and (8) imply
\[
\mathbb E_{P_C}\exp\!\left\{i,a'C^{-1/2}\sum_{c=1}^C\xi_c(P_C)\right\}
\longrightarrow \exp\{-a'\Sigma_\infty a/2\}. \tag{9}
\]
The right side is the characteristic function of \(N(0,\Sigma_\infty)\); the characteristic-function criterion and (5) consequently give
\[
\sqrt C\{\widetilde U_C-U(P_C)\}\Rightarrow N(0,\Sigma_\infty) \tag{10}
\]
along the selected subsequence.  Because the original sequence \((P_C)\) and its subsequence were arbitrary, this subsequence argument supplies the required uniform triangular-array approximation.

For completeness, unpack the regular branch of the empirical covariance definition.  With
\[
\widehat q_k=C^{-1}\sum_{c=1}^C\mathbf 1\{Z_c\in\mathcal S_k\},
\qquad
\widehat\xi_{c,k}=\widehat q_k^{-1}\mathbf 1\{Z_c\in\mathcal S_k\}
\{W_c(Z_c)-\overline W_{Z_c}\},
\]
it is \(C^{-1}\sum_c\widehat\xi_c\widehat\xi_c'\), with the prescribed bounded value on an undefined cell.  The off-diagonal entries vanish because the slices are disjoint.  For a diagonal entry, the within-cell identity
\[
\sum_{c:Z_c=z}\{W_c(z)-\overline W_z\}^2
=\sum_{c:Z_c=z}\{W_c(z)-\mu_z\}^2
-N_z(\overline W_z-\mu_z)^2 \tag{11}
\]
shows, using (2)--(3), that replacement of \(\overline W_z\) by \(\mu_z\) changes the empirical second moment by \(o_P(1)\), uniformly in \(P\).  Moreover, the summands
\(\mathbf 1\{Z_c=z\}\{W_c(z)-\mu_z\}^2\) are bounded by one, so their empirical averages differ from their expectations by \(o_P(1)\), uniformly by the variance bound \(1/C\).  Finally \(\widehat q_k-q_k=o_P(1)\), again by (2) summed over the slice.  Equations (6) and (11) therefore prove
\[
\widehat\Sigma_C^{(K)}-\Sigma_B^{(K)}(P)\longrightarrow_P0, \tag{12}
\]
uniformly over the displayed class.  In particular, (12) proves the theorem's stated covariance consistency, and along the sequence above it converges to \(\Sigma_\infty\).

Let \(e_{ij}=e_i-e_j\) and define, on the regular branch,
\[
\widehat s_{ij}^2=e_{ij}'\widehat\Sigma_C^{(K)}e_{ij},
\qquad
T_C=\max_{i<j}
\frac{\sqrt C\left|e_{ij}'\{\widetilde U_C-U(P_C)\}\right|}{\widehat s_{ij}}. \tag{13}
\]
Every limiting denominator is nonzero, since
\[
e_{ij}'\Sigma_B^{(K)}(P_C)e_{ij}
=\frac{\tau_i^2(P_C)}{q_i}+\frac{\tau_j^2(P_C)}{q_j}
\geq\underline\tau^2\left(q_i^{-1}+q_j^{-1}\right)>0. \tag{14}
\]
Let \(c_C(\gamma)\) be the conditional \((1-\gamma)\)-quantile used by the max-\(t\) construction: it is the corresponding maximum in (13) with the estimation error replaced by \(G_C\sim N(0,\widehat\Sigma_C^{(K)})\).  From (10)--(14),
\[
T_C\Rightarrow
T_\infty:=\max_{i<j}
\frac{|e_{ij}'G_\infty|}{(e_{ij}'\Sigma_\infty e_{ij})^{1/2}},
\qquad G_\infty\sim N(0,\Sigma_\infty). \tag{15}
\]
The contrast vector of \(G_\infty\) has a positive density on its \((K-1)\)-dimensional contrast space.  The displayed maximum is a norm on that space; every nonempty norm annulus has positive Lebesgue measure.  Consequently the distribution function of \(T_\infty\) is continuous and strictly increasing on \((0,\infty)\).  Continuity of Gaussian laws in their covariance matrices and (12) then imply
\(c_C(\gamma)\to_Pc_\infty(\gamma)\), its unique \((1-\gamma)\)-quantile.  It follows from (15) that
\[
\Pr_{P_C}\{T_C\leq c_C(\gamma)\}\longrightarrow1-\gamma. \tag{16}
\]
If the uniform lower bound claimed in the theorem failed, one could select a violating sequence \((P_C)\); weak compactness and the preceding further-subsequence argument would give (16), a contradiction.  Thus (16) holds in the required uniform liminf sense.

It remains to connect simultaneous contrast coverage to the set-valued causal ranks.  On the event in (16), every interval inverted by \(\mathcal R_{C,k}(\gamma)\) contains its true contrast \(U_j-U_k\).  A policy \(j\) can therefore be certified as strictly above \(k\) only when \(U_j>U_k\), and it can be certified as strictly below \(k\) only when \(U_j<U_k\).  Hence the inverted lower endpoint is at most
\[
r_k^-(U)=1+\sum_{j\ne k}\mathbf 1\{U_j>U_k\},
\]
and its inverted upper endpoint is at least
\[
r_k^+(U)=K-\sum_{j\ne k}\mathbf 1\{U_j<U_k\}.
\]
Therefore \(\operatorname{Rank}_k(U)\subseteq\mathcal R_{C,k}(\gamma)\) for every \(k\) on the same simultaneous event.  This argument uses no separation and hence covers every pattern of partial ties.

Finally, independence across clusters and (1) give, for \(z\in\mathcal S_k\),
\[
\Pr_P(N_z=0)=(1-q_k/M_k)^C\leq\exp(-Cq_k/M_k). \tag{17}
\]
A union bound over all target cells proves that the probability of activating the full-rank empty-cell fallback is at most
\[
\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}M_k\exp(-Cq_k/M_k).
\]
The fallback returns the entire rank set and thus cannot reduce coverage.  Combining (16)--(17) proves the stated simultaneous uniform coverage, covariance consistency, tie robustness, and empty-cell bound.
