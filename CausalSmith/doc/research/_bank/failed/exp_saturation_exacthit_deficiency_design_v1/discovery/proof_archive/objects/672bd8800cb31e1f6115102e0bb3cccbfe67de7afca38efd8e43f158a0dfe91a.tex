Put \(d=|A|-1\), \(g=D_Ah\), and \(J_A=D_A'(D_AD_A')^{-1}\).  The representative \(h^\circ=J_Ag\) gives the quotient loss
\[
\ell_a(g)=\max_{k\in A}(J_Ag)_k-(J_Ag)_a.
\]
On \(K_M=\{g:\|g\|_2\leq M\}\),
\[
0\leq\ell_a(g)\leq L_M:=2\|J_A\|_{2\to\infty}M,\qquad
|\ell_a(g)-\ell_a(g')|\leq2\|J_A\|_{2\to\infty}\|g-g'\|_2. \tag{49}
\]
By \(\texttt{lem:gaussian-quotient-reduction}\), it suffices to certify \(Y\sim N(g,\Omega)\), \(g\in K_M\).

Choose rational points \(g_1,\ldots,g_S\in K_M\) and rational prior weights \(w_s\).  If \(\varphi_\Omega\) is the centered Gaussian density, set
\[
c_a(y)=\sum_{s=1}^Sw_s\ell_a(g_s)\varphi_\Omega(y-g_s). \tag{50}
\]
Tonelli's theorem gives the finite-prior Bayes value exactly as
\[
V(w,\Omega)=\int_{\mathbb R^d}\min_{a\in A}c_a(y)\,dy. \tag{51}
\]
Thus directed lower integration of (51) on any rational box is a valid lower bound; the omitted complement is nonnegative.

For an upper certificate, store a rational action distribution on every rational observation cell and a fixed action outside the box.  If \(\lambda_{\max}(\Omega)\leq\overline\lambda\) throughout the covariance cell and the box contains \([-T,T]^d\), \(T>M\), then
\[
\sup_{g\in K_M}\Pr_{g,\Omega}\{Y\notin[-T,T]^d\}
\leq2d\Phi\!\left(-\frac{T-M}{\sqrt{\overline\lambda}}\right). \tag{52}
\]
Multiplication by \(L_M\) bounds the tail loss.  Directed Gaussian integration bounds each stored action probability.  Cells on which interval costs certify a minimizer are exact up to quadrature; on an ambiguous cell, adding the interval width between the largest and smallest possibly minimizing posterior costs is valid.  Uniform continuity of the finitely many \(c_a\)'s makes the total ambiguous-cell error vanish with the mesh, including exact-tie regions where the true cost difference is zero.

Parameter and covariance interpolation are also explicit.  If \(\lambda_{\min}(\Omega)\geq\underline\lambda>0\), then
\[
\operatorname{KL}\{N(g,\Omega),N(g',\Omega)\}
=\tfrac12(g-g')'\Omega^{-1}(g-g')
\leq\frac{\|g-g'\|_2^2}{2\underline\lambda}. \tag{53}
\]
The log-sum inequality on the likelihood-ratio set, followed by the binary-divergence second-derivative bound, proves Pinsker's inequality \(\|P-Q\|_{\rm TV}^2\leq\operatorname{KL}(P,Q)/2\).  Combining it with (49)--(53) yields the computable parameter modulus
\[
|R(g,\delta)-R(g',\delta)|
\leq2\|J_A\|_{2\to\infty}\|g-g'\|_2
+\frac{L_M}{2\sqrt{\underline\lambda}}\|g-g'\|_2. \tag{54}
\]
For covariances, the exact divergence
\[
\operatorname{KL}\{N(g,\Omega),N(g,\Omega')\}
=\frac12\left[\operatorname{tr}(\Omega'^{-1}\Omega)-d+
\log\frac{\det\Omega'}{\det\Omega}\right] \tag{55}
\]
is interval-evaluated on a cell with eigenvalues bounded below.  Pinsker and \(L_M\) convert its upper enclosure into a covariance-risk modulus.

Termination follows from compactness.  Modulus (54) reduces the parameter ball to a finite grid.  For that grid, selectors are bounded in \(L^2\) under the finite Gaussian-mixture measure.  The orthonormal-basis coefficient diagonalization proved in \(\texttt{lem:gaussian-quotient-reduction}\) gives a weakly convergent subsequence from every selector sequence; the finitely many risk coordinates are continuous linear functionals, so the attainable risk-vector set is compact and convex.  A separating hyperplane between that set and a strictly lower orthant has nonnegative coefficients summing to one, which are finite-prior weights; this directly equates the finite minimax value to the supremum of (51).  Rational prior weights are dense.  Hence a rational prior and represented selector bracket the finite game arbitrarily closely.  Equations (52)--(55) show that sufficiently large truncation boxes and sufficiently fine observation, parameter, and covariance subdivisions preserve any prescribed gap.  Allocating \(\varepsilon\) among parameter interpolation, covariance perturbation, tail, quadrature, ambiguous cells, and rounding proves that exhaustive rational refinement terminates with \(U-L\leq\varepsilon\).  The listed transcript fields suffice to recheck each rational and interval inequality without trusting exploratory numerics.
