We prove the three assertions: certification of the unbounded Gaussian game, certification of the two outer design problems, and uniform transfer to the primitive experiments.

\emph{1. Certification of the unbounded Gaussian game.}
Fix the declared face \(A\), put \(d=|A|\), and first work on one compact rational cell from the statement.  Write
\[
\Sigma=\Sigma(v_A,r_A)=\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_i^2:i\in A),
\qquad \sigma_i^2=v_i/r_i.
\]
Positivity and compactness of this cell give computable constants \(0<\underline\sigma\leq\overline\sigma<\infty\) such that every \(\sigma_i\) lies in \([\underline\sigma,\overline\sigma]\).  Hence all quotient covariance matrices in the cell are uniformly positive definite, as required by \(\texttt{thm:gaussian-finite-certificate}\).

We first obtain a finite cellwise upper bound.  Let \(b\in\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{i\in A}h_i\), put \(d_i=h_b-h_i\geq0\), and use \(\delta_0(X)\in\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{i\in A}X_i\), with deterministic tie breaking.  If \(\delta_0(X)=i\ne b\), then \(X_i-X_b\geq0\).  Since \(X_i-X_b\sim N(-d_i,\sigma_i^2+\sigma_b^2)\), the union bound gives
\[
\mathbb E_h\{h_b-h_{\delta_0(X)}\}
\leq\sum_{i\ne b}d_i\Phi\!\left(-\frac{d_i}{\sqrt{\sigma_i^2+\sigma_b^2}}\right).       \tag{1}
\]
For \(x\geq0\), integration by parts gives \(x\Phi(-x)\leq\varphi(x)\leq(2\pi)^{-1/2}\), and \(\sqrt{\sigma_i^2+\sigma_b^2}\leq\sqrt2\,\overline\sigma\).  Therefore
\[
G_A(v_A,r_A)\leq H_0:=\frac{(d-1)\overline\sigma}{\sqrt\pi}.       \tag{2}
\]

Choose rational \(0<\lambda<1\), then rational \(T>0\), so that, with \(a_\lambda=\sqrt{2/\lambda}\,\overline\sigma\),
\[
\{(1-\lambda)^{-1/2}-1\}H_0<\frac\varepsilon4,
\qquad
\frac{2d^2a_\lambda}{\sqrt{2\pi}}
e^{-T^2/(2a_\lambda^2)}<\frac\varepsilon4.                        \tag{3}
\]
Such rational choices exist because the first expression tends to zero as \(\lambda\downarrow0\), and, for fixed \(\lambda\), the second tends to zero as \(T\to\infty\).  Directed rational interval evaluation verifies the strict inequalities in finite time.

For every nonempty \(S\subseteq A\), choose a rational radius
\[
M_S\geq2\sqrt{1-\lambda}\,T\sqrt{|S|-1}.                          \tag{4}
\]
For a singleton use exact risk zero.  For \(|S|\geq2\), apply \(\texttt{thm:gaussian-finite-certificate}\) on the induced compact positive covariance cell, with a common rational accuracy \(\eta>0\) satisfying
\[
(1-\lambda)^{-1/2}\eta<\frac\varepsilon4.                         \tag{5}
\]
It returns \(L_S\leq G_{S,M_S}(v_S,r_S)\leq U_S\), with \(U_S-L_S\leq\eta\), and a represented selector.  Define
\[
L_A^0=\max_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq A}L_S,
\qquad
U_A^0=(1-\lambda)^{-1/2}\max_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq A}U_S
+\frac{2d^2a_\lambda}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-T^2/(2a_\lambda^2)}.       \tag{6}
\]
The lower inequality in \(\texttt{lem:hierarchical-gaussian-screening}\) yields
\[
L_A^0\leq\max_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq A}G_{S,M_S}(v_S,r_S)
\leq G_A(v_A,r_A).                                                 \tag{7}
\]
The selector constructed in that lemma uses the independent screen with covariance \(\Sigma/\lambda\), retains coordinates within \(T\) of its maximum, and applies the appropriate compact selector to \(\sqrt{1-\lambda}\) times the independent decision observation.  Its risk is at most \(U_A^0\), so
\[
G_A(v_A,r_A)\leq
\sup_{h\in\mathbb R^A}\mathbb E_h\!\left[\max_{i\in A}h_i-h_{\delta(X)}\right]
\leq U_A^0.                                                        \tag{8}
\]
If \(S_\star\) maximizes \(U_S\), then
\[
\max_SU_S=U_{S_\star}\leq L_{S_\star}+\eta\leq L_A^0+\eta.      \tag{9}
\]
Also (2) and (7) give \(L_A^0\leq H_0\).  Equations (3), (5), (6), and (9) imply
\[
U_A^0-L_A^0
\leq\{(1-\lambda)^{-1/2}-1\}H_0+(1-\lambda)^{-1/2}\eta
+\frac{2d^2a_\lambda}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-T^2/(2a_\lambda^2)}
<\frac{3\varepsilon}{4}.                                         \tag{10}
\]
Choose the working precision so that outward rounding enlarges the gap by less than \(\varepsilon/4\).  The rounded values \(L^\infty_{\varepsilon,A}\) and \(U^\infty_{\varepsilon,A}\) then satisfy the first displayed chain and have gap at most \(\varepsilon\).  There are \(2^d-1\) nonempty subsets, so all compact calls and finite rational searches terminate.  Concatenating their receipts with the rational witnesses for (3)--(5), the subset list, the tail enclosure, and the rounding record gives a machine-checkable transcript.  Because the routine ranges over all retained subsets, a configuration with only a proper subset of locally tied coordinates enters that subset's game; no radial restriction on the full vector has been imposed.

\emph{2. Certification of the outer designs.}
Under \(\mathsf B(p)\), \(\texttt{def:hit-map}\) and \(\texttt{lem:exact-hit-factorization}\) give \(q=Bp\).  Every entry of \(B\) is positive, so
\[
(Bp)_k\geq\underline q_k:=\min_{\ell\in\mathcal K}B_{k\ell}>0
\quad(p\in\Delta_K,\ k\in A).                                   \tag{11}
\]
Partition the rational simplex into rational cells.  Linear interval arithmetic gives coordinatewise bounds \(q^-\leq Bp\leq q^+\) on each cell.  By \(\texttt{lem:gaussian-information-order}\), a certified lower bound for \(G_A(v_A,q^+)\) is valid throughout the cell, whereas any feasible rational point gives an upper incumbent.  If \((1-\xi)q\leq q'\leq(1+\xi)q\), with \(0<\xi<1\), the same lemma gives
\[
(1+\xi)^{-1/2}G_A(v_A,q)
\leq G_A(v_A,q')
\leq(1-\xi)^{-1/2}G_A(v_A,q).                                    \tag{12}
\]
The factors tend uniformly to one as \(\xi\downarrow0\), (11) makes all relative cell widths uniformly refinable, and (2) makes the values uniformly finite.  Rational branch-and-bound, with a summable numerical error allocation among calls to Part 1, therefore terminates with an interval of prescribed width, keeps every cell containing a minimizer, and returns a feasible \(\varepsilon\)-optimal rational point.

For exact counts, the feasible rates satisfy
\[
a_i\geq0\quad(i\in A),\qquad\sum_{i\in A}a_i=1.                 \tag{13}
\]
An action-relevant zero coordinate has value \(+\infty\) by \(\texttt{lem:gaussian-information-order}\).  For distinct \(i,j\in A\), the subface inequality in \(\texttt{lem:hierarchical-gaussian-screening}\) and \(\texttt{thm:two-policy-minimax}\) give
\[
G_A(v_A,a)\geq G_{\{i,j\}}(v_{\{i,j\}},a_{\{i,j\}})
=\kappa\sqrt{\frac{v_i}{a_i}+\frac{v_j}{a_j}}
\geq\kappa\sqrt{\frac{v_i}{a_i}}.                               \tag{14}
\]
Let \(\underline v>0\) be a rational lower enclosure for all \(v_i\), and let \(\overline U<\infty\) be a certified upper bound at the uniform allocation.  Choose rational
\[
0<\eta_0<\min\left\{\frac1{2d},
\frac{\kappa^2\underline v}{4(\overline U+1)^2}\right\}.         \tag{15}
\]
If \(a_i<\eta_0\), then (14)--(15) give \(G_A(v_A,a)>2(\overline U+1)>\overline U\).  Thus no minimizer lies there.  The minimizers lie in the compact rational polytope (13) with \(a_i\geq\eta_0\), where the argument based on (12) applies.  This proves the exact-count outer certificate without evaluating a positive-rate formula on the boundary.

\emph{3. Uniform transfer to the primitive experiments.}
Partition the clusters into pilot, screening, and decision pieces with
\[
m_C+N_C^{\mathrm s}+N_C^{\mathrm d}=C,
\quad m_C\to\infty,
\quad m_C/C\to0,
\quad N_C^{\mathrm s}/C\to\lambda,
\quad N_C^{\mathrm d}/C\to1-\lambda.                              \tag{16}
\]
For exact-count randomization, make the analogous split within every active fixed-count stratum.  Assumptions \(\texttt{ass:iid-schedules}\), \(\texttt{ass:bernoulli-label-schedule-independence}\), and \(\texttt{ass:cr-label-schedule-independence}\) give independence of the outcome arrays in the three pieces, conditionally on their prescribed design counts.  The assignment laws are those in \(\texttt{ass:bernoulli-label-iid}\), \(\texttt{ass:bernoulli-units}\), \(\texttt{ass:cr-label-vector}\), and \(\texttt{ass:cr-exact-slices}\); \(\texttt{ass:isolated-clusters}\) makes the observed reward the corresponding within-cluster potential outcome.

For \(z\in\mathcal S_k\), \(\texttt{lem:exact-hit-factorization}\) gives
\[
\rho_z=\Pr(Z_c=z)=\frac{q_k}{M_k}
\geq\frac{\min_\ell B_{k\ell}}{M_k}>0.                           \tag{17}
\]
In the certified exact-count region, the corresponding limiting cell frequency is at least \(\eta_0/M_k\).  There are finitely many target cells.  On the event that every empirical cell frequency is at least half its lower bound, the ratio identity applied cellwise gives, for \(b\in\{\mathrm s,\mathrm d\}\),
\[
\widetilde U_{C,k}^{\,b}-U_k
=\frac1{N_C^b}\sum_{c\in b}
\frac{\mathbf1\{Z_c\in\mathcal S_k\}}{q_k}
\{W_c(Z_c)-\mu_{Z_c}\}+o_P((N_C^b)^{-1/2}),                     \tag{18}
\]
uniformly in the Bernoulli design; under exact counts, \(q_k\) is replaced by the active stratum share.  Indeed, write each cell mean as its empirical centered sum divided by its empirical frequency and replace the reciprocal frequency by its probability reciprocal.  The centered numerator and frequency error are each \(O_P((N_C^b)^{-1/2})\), so their product is \(O_P((N_C^b)^{-1})\); a finite cell sum preserves the remainder.  The empty-cell fallback occurs with exponentially small probability by (17).

The summands in (18) are centered and uniformly bounded.  Their coordinate variances are \(\tau_k^2/q_k\) under Bernoulli assignment and \(\tau_k^2/a_k\) under exact counts, and distinct coordinates have zero product because the slices are disjoint.  For every fixed linear combination, third-order Taylor expansion of its characteristic function and summation of the \(O((N_C^b)^{-3/2})\) remainders give the corresponding centered normal characteristic function.  Since this holds for every linear combination, the vector limits are jointly
\[
\sqrt C\{\widetilde U_C^{\mathrm s}-U\}
\Rightarrow N(0,\Sigma/\lambda),
\qquad
\sqrt C\{\widetilde U_C^{\mathrm d}-U\}
\Rightarrow N(0,\Sigma/(1-\lambda)),                             \tag{19}
\]
with independent limits.  Under a \(C^{-1/2}\)-local displacement \(h\), both limiting means are \(h\).  Hence the screening statistic has covariance \(\Sigma/\lambda\), while
\[
\sqrt{1-\lambda}\,\sqrt C
\{\widetilde U_C^{\mathrm d}-U(P_0)\}                             \tag{20}
\]
has covariance \(\Sigma\) and mean \(\sqrt{1-\lambda}h\).  Since the tied active baseline is a multiple of \(\mathbf1_A\), location invariance removes it; undoing the scale multiplies regret by \((1-\lambda)^{-1/2}\), exactly as in (6).

We now verify the facewise coverage needed to invoke the primitive transfer.  For every retained \(S\subseteq A\) with \(|S|\geq2\), the matrix \(D_S\) has full row rank.  Set
\[
J_S=D_S^{\prime}(D_SD_S^{\prime})^{-1},
\qquad
\mathscr H_{S,M_S}=\{J_Sg:g\in\mathbb R^{|S|-1},\ \lVert g\rVert_2\leq M_S\}.             \tag{21}
\]
The closed Euclidean ball is compact and \(J_S\) is continuous, so \(\mathscr H_{S,M_S}\) is compact.  Moreover \(D_SJ_S=I\), whence
\[
D_S\mathscr H_{S,M_S}=\{g:\lVert g\rVert_2\leq M_S\},
\quad\text{equivalently}\quad
\{[u]:u\in\mathscr H_{S,M_S}\}
=\{[w]\in H_S:\lVert[w]\rVert_S\leq M_S\}.                     \tag{22}
\]
For a singleton set take \(\mathscr H_{S,0}=\{0\}\); its quotient and regret are zero.  On the good screening event, \(\texttt{lem:hierarchical-gaussian-screening}\) gives
\[
\lVert[\sqrt{1-\lambda}h_S]\rVert_S
\leq2\sqrt{1-\lambda}\,T\sqrt{|S|-1}\leq M_S.                  \tag{23}
\]
By (22), some \(h_S^\star\in\mathscr H_{S,M_S}\) has the same quotient coordinate as \(\sqrt{1-\lambda}h_S\).  Their difference lies in \(\ker D_S=\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf1_S\}\).  The Gaussian selector and regret are translation invariant by \(\texttt{lem:gaussian-quotient-reduction}\), so their risks agree.  The repaired \(\texttt{prop:oracle-transfer-bernoulli}\) or \(\texttt{prop:oracle-transfer-cr}\), applied on \(\mathscr H_{S,M_S}\), therefore controls every point of the full compact quotient ball in (23), not merely a chosen compact subset.  Thus it transfers exactly \(G_{S,M_S}\).  This facewise equality is invoked before taking the maximum over retained subsets in (6), and the finite maximum preserves uniform convergence.

For pilot covariance consistency, let \(\rho_0>0\) be the minimum of the finitely many lower bounds in (17) and \(\eta_0/M_k\).  For a piece of size \(N\), exponential Markov applied to a cell count gives constants \(c>0\) such that
\[
\Pr\{N_z^{(N)}\leq N\rho_z/2\}\leq e^{-cN}                       \tag{24}
\]
uniformly over \(\rho_z\geq\rho_0\).  Conditional variances of bounded cell means and second moments are \(O(1/N_z^{(N)})\).  Hence Chebyshev's inequality, (24), and a finite union bound prove uniform covariance consistency and valid outward snapping.  Applying the same bounded-sum argument to the screening piece gives uniform constants \(c_1,c_2>0\) with
\[
\Pr\!\left\{\sqrt C\,|\widetilde U_{C,k}^{\mathrm s}-U_k|>x\right\}
\leq c_1e^{-c_2x^2}+c_1e^{-c_2C},
\qquad 0\leq x\leq\sqrt C.                                      \tag{25}
\]
The pairwise decomposition in \(\texttt{lem:hierarchical-gaussian-screening}\) and (25) give a deterministic remainder tending to zero as \(T\to\infty\) for deleting a best coordinate or retaining a set whose scaled range exceeds \(2T\).  Thus a policy with diverging scaled welfare gap contributes \(o(1)\) to scaled regret.

It remains to justify uniformity over bounded-schedule triangular arrays.  If it failed, the compact schedule space \([0,1]^{n\times\mathcal Z_n}\) and \(\texttt{lem:weak-compactness-compact-laws}\) would give a weakly convergent subsequence of a violating sequence of laws.  Assignment-specific welfare means and variances converge because the relevant coordinates and their squares are bounded continuous functions.  Passing to a further subsequence makes every nonnegative scaled policy gap converge in \([0,\infty]\).  Infinite limiting gaps have vanishing contribution by (25).  On the remaining coordinates, the bounded triangular-array characteristic-function calculation proving (19), pilot consistency, and covariance snapping give joint convergence of pilot, screen, and decision statistics.  The finitely represented selectors have Gaussian-null affine boundaries.  Bounded truncation followed by (25) transfers expected scaled regret to the Gaussian screened selector.  Equations (21)--(23) then place each retained non-singleton face in its full compact quotient game, while the singleton branch is exact; the Gaussian risk bound (8) contradicts the assumed violation.  This proves the asserted uniformity over all retained faces and certified covariance cells.

For \(A=\mathcal K\), (11) supplies positive information for every Bernoulli candidate.  For exact counts, Part 2 puts every optimizer in a compact simplex interior, the domain of the primitive exact-count transfer.  At a vanishing action-relevant share, \(\texttt{lem:gaussian-information-order}\) supplies only the extended value \(+\infty\).  Fixed-scale inferior policies have diverging scaled gaps and vanish by (25), whereas \(C^{-1/2}\)-scale policies remain in a retained compact game.

Finally, at stage \(j\), run Parts 1 and 2 with tolerance \(1/j\), choose the rational split, threshold, full-ball representative sets (21), compact radii, and meshes, and then choose \(C_j\) so that all pilot, split-limit, snapping, screening, and facewise-transfer errors are at most \(1/j\), with all subsample sizes diverging and \(m_C/C\leq1/j\).  Keeping the stage-\(j\) choices for \(C_j\leq C<C_{j+1}\) gives a diagonal sequence.  Equations (6)--(25) transfer the certified noncompact Gaussian risk and both outer designs to the primitive calibrated experiments on exactly the domains in the statement.
