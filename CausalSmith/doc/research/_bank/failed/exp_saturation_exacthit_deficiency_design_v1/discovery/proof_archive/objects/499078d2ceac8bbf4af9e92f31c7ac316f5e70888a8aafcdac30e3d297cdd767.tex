Write \(d=|A|\), \(\mathbf 1=\mathbf 1_A\), and \(L(h,a)=\max_k h_k-h_a\).  The identity \(L(h+c\mathbf 1,a)=L(h,a)\) holds for every \(c\in\mathbb R\).  For a randomized selector \(\delta=(\delta_a)_{a\in A}\), define its Følner average
\[
\delta_{a,T}(x)=\frac1{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}\delta_a(x+c\mathbf 1)\,dc.
\]
If \(X\sim N(h,\Sigma)\), translation of the Gaussian density and invariance of \(L\) give
\[
R(h,\delta_T)=\frac1{2T}\int_{-T}^{T}R(h+c\mathbf 1,\delta)\,dc
\leq \sup_{u\in\mathbb R^A}R(u,\delta). \tag{1}
\]
The functions \(\delta_{a,T}\) lie in the unit ball of \(L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb R^A)\).  On each member of an increasing sequence of bounded boxes, weak sequential compactness of the Hilbert-space unit ball and a diagonal subsequence give limits \(\bar\delta_a\).  Nonnegativity and \(\sum_a\bar\delta_a=1\) are preserved under weak limits.  For each fixed \(u\), Gaussian density times the bounded loss on a compact parameter neighborhood belongs to \(L^2\) on every box, while its complement is controlled by the Gaussian tail; hence the risk is lower semicontinuous along this diagonal limit.  Equation (1) therefore yields
\[
\sup_uR(u,\bar\delta)\leq\sup_uR(u,\delta). \tag{2}
\]
For fixed \(c_0\), the difference between the averages defining \(\delta_T(x+c_0\mathbf 1)\) and \(\delta_T(x)\) is supported on two intervals of total length at most \(2|c_0|\), so its absolute value is at most \(|c_0|/T\).  Passing to the weak limit first for rational \(c_0\), and then using strong continuity of translations in \(L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}\), shows \(\bar\delta(x+c_0\mathbf 1)=\bar\delta(x)\) almost everywhere for every real \(c_0\).  Choosing a measurable version constant on the fibers gives an invariant selector.  Thus (2) proves that restricting to invariant selectors does not increase the minimax value; the reverse inequality is immediate because invariant selectors are a subclass.

By construction, \(D_A\) has rank \(d-1\), kernel \(\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf 1\}\), and a measurable right inverse \(J_A=D_A^{\prime}(D_AD_A^{\prime})^{-1}\).  Hence every invariant measurable function is constant on every fiber of \(D_A\) and factors as \(\tilde\delta(D_Ax)\).  Linear transformation of a Gaussian vector gives
\[
D_AX\sim N(D_Ah,D_A\Sigma D_A^{\prime})=N(D_Ah,\Omega_A).
\]
For nonzero \(u\in\mathbb R^{d-1}\), full row rank and positive definiteness of \(\Sigma\) imply
\[
u^{\prime}\Omega_Au=(D_A^{\prime}u)^{\prime}\Sigma(D_A^{\prime}u)>0,
\]
so the quotient covariance is positive definite.  This also proves the compact-game equivalence.

Finally let \(g=D_Ah\).  The risks of invariant rules depend only on \(g\), and the balls \(\{g:\lVert g\rVert_2\leq M\}\) increase to \(\mathbb R^{d-1}\).  Therefore \(G_{A,M}\) is nondecreasing and bounded above by \(G_A\).  Choose selectors with compact risks within \(1/M\) of \(G_{A,M}\), use the same diagonal weak-compactness argument on quotient observation boxes, and pass to a subsequence along which the values converge.  The limiting selector has risk no larger than \(\lim_MG_{A,M}\) at every fixed \(g\), hence on their union.  Thus \(G_A\leq\lim_MG_{A,M}\), proving \(G_{A,M}\uparrow G_A\).
