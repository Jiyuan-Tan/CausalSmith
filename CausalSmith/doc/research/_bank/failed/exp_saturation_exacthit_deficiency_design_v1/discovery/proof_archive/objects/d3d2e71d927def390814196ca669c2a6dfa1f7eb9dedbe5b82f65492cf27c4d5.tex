Put \(d=|A|-1\), \(g=D_Ah\), and \(J_A=D_A^{\prime}(D_AD_A^{\prime})^{-1}\).  The canonical representative is \(h^\circ=J_Ag\).  The quotient loss
\[
\ell_a(g)=\max_{k\in A}(J_Ag)_k-(J_Ag)_a
\]
is independent of the representative and satisfies on \(K_M=\{g:\lVert g\rVert_2\leq M\}\)
\[
0\leq\ell_a(g)\leq L_M:=2\lVert J_A\rVert_{2\to\infty}M,
\qquad
|\ell_a(g)-\ell_a(g')|\leq2\lVert J_A\rVert_{2\to\infty}\lVert g-g'\rVert_2. \tag{18}
\]
By \(\text{lem:gaussian-quotient-reduction}\), it suffices to certify the experiment \(Y\sim N(g,\Omega)\), \(g\in K_M\).

We first describe exact lower certificates.  Choose a rational grid \(g_1,\ldots,g_S\subset K_M\) and rational prior weights \(w_s\geq0\), \(\sum_sw_s=1\).  For action \(a\), let \(\varphi_\Omega\) denote the centered \(N(0,\Omega)\) density and define the posterior cost density
\[
c_a(y)=\sum_{s=1}^S w_s\ell_a(g_s)\varphi_\Omega(y-g_s). \tag{19}
\]
The Bayes risk of this prior is exactly
\[
V(w,\Omega)=\int_{\mathbb R^d}\min_{a\in A}c_a(y)\,dy. \tag{20}
\]
Indeed, for every randomized selector \(\delta\), Tonelli's theorem applies to the nonnegative bounded integrand and gives Bayes risk \(\int\sum_a\delta_a(y)c_a(y)dy\), minimized pointwise by an action attaining \(\min_ac_a(y)\).  Therefore every outward-downward enclosure of (20) is a valid lower bound for \(G_{A,M}\).  On a rational observation box, directed interval evaluation of (19) and subdivision at action-switching cells encloses the integral.  Omitting the complement is valid for a lower bound because the integrand is nonnegative.

For an upper certificate, store on each rational observation cell a rational action distribution and use a fixed action outside the truncation box.  If \(\overline\lambda\) bounds \(\lambda_{\max}(\Omega)\) on the covariance cell and the box contains \([-T,T]^d\) with \(T>M\), the union bound and the one-dimensional Gaussian tail yield uniformly in \(g\in K_M\)
\[
\Pr_{g,\Omega}\{Y\notin[-T,T]^d\}
\leq2d\,\Phi\!\left(-\frac{T-M}{\sqrt{\overline\lambda}}\right). \tag{21}
\]
Multiplication by \(L_M\) is a certified tail contribution.  On each retained observation cell, interval Gaussian integration gives outward enclosures of the selected-action probability.  If interval posterior costs certify one minimizing action, the cell is marked certified.  Otherwise all possibly minimizing actions are retained and the upper calculation adds the interval width between their largest and smallest posterior costs.  Uniform continuity of the finitely many functions in (19) makes this ambiguous-cell contribution tend to zero as the observation mesh tends to zero, including a region of exact ties, where the true cost difference is zero.

It remains to control parameters and covariances explicitly.  For completeness, if \(A_0=\{dP/dQ\geq1\}\), the log-sum inequality applied to \(A_0,A_0^c\) gives \(\operatorname{KL}(P,Q)\geq d_{\mathrm{bin}}\{P(A_0),Q(A_0)\}\); integrating the second derivative of the binary divergence along the segment between its two arguments gives \(d_{\mathrm{bin}}(u,v)\geq2(u-v)^2\).  Since \(P(A_0)-Q(A_0)=\lVert P-Q\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\), this proves \(\lVert P-Q\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}^2\leq\operatorname{KL}(P,Q)/2\).  For a fixed covariance with \(\lambda_{\min}(\Omega)\geq\underline\lambda>0\), the equal-covariance Gaussian integral gives
\[
\operatorname{KL}\{N(g,\Omega),N(g',\Omega)\}
=\frac12(g-g')^{\prime}\Omega^{-1}(g-g')
\leq\frac{\lVert g-g'\rVert_2^2}{2\underline\lambda}. \tag{22}
\]
Combining (18) and (22), every rule obeys the computable parameter modulus
\[
|R(g,\delta)-R(g',\delta)|
\leq2\lVert J_A\rVert_{2\to\infty}\lVert g-g'\rVert_2
+\frac{L_M}{2\sqrt{\underline\lambda}}\lVert g-g'\rVert_2. \tag{23}
\]
For two covariance matrices the exact same-mean Gaussian divergence is
\[
\operatorname{KL}\{N(g,\Omega),N(g,\Omega')\}
=\frac12\left[\operatorname{tr}(\Omega'^{-1}\Omega)-d
+\log\frac{\det\Omega'}{\det\Omega}\right]. \tag{24}
\]
Every operation in (24), including inverse and determinant, is interval-evaluated on a branch cell whose eigenvalues are bounded below by \(\underline\lambda\).  Pinsker and multiplication by \(L_M\) turn the resulting outward upper enclosure into a covariance-risk modulus.  Thus (23)--(24) cover every point between rational parameter and covariance grid points.

To prove termination, fix a covariance.  Equicontinuity (23) reduces the compact parameter game to a finite parameter grid with an arbitrarily small error.  For that finite grid, the set of attainable risk vectors is convex and compact: bounded selectors are weakly compact in \(L^2\) under the finite Gaussian-mixture measure, and each risk coordinate is a continuous linear functional.  Separating this set from a strictly lower orthant shows that its minimax value equals the supremum over finite prior weights of the Bayes values (20).  Rational weights are dense in the finite simplex.  Hence a rational prior and a represented Bayes selector have lower and upper risks within any prescribed positive gap.  Equations (21)--(23) show that sufficiently large \(T\) and sufficiently fine rational observation and parameter partitions preserve that gap.  Equation (24) and compactness of the covariance cell give a finite subdivision on which the covariance contribution is smaller than any prescribed remainder.  Assigning, for example, one sixth of \(\varepsilon\) to parameter interpolation, covariance perturbation, truncation, quadrature, ambiguous cells, and final outward rounding proves that exhaustive refinement must eventually verify \(U-L\leq\varepsilon\).
