For a selector \(\delta\), write
\[
R_A(\delta,h)=\mathbb E_h[\max_{i\in A}h_i-h_{\delta(X)}]. \tag{56}
\]
Fix nonempty \(S\subseteq A\) and \(M<\infty\).  Every class in the compact \(S\)-game has a representative \(g\) with \(\max_{i\in S}g_i=0\); \(\lVert[g]\rVert_S\leq M\) implies \(-2M\leq g_i\leq0\).  Embed it by
\[
h_i=g_i\ (i\in S),\qquad h_j=-2M-1\ (j\notin S). \tag{57}
\]
Outside actions are dominated.  Diagonal covariance makes \(X_{A\setminus S}\) independent of \(X_S\), with a law independent of \(g\), so those coordinates provide only randomization already allowed to \(S\)-selectors.  Replacing an outside action by a fixed action in \(S\) cannot increase regret.  Hence
\[
G_A(v_A,r_A)\geq G_{S,M}(v_S,r_S). \tag{58}
\]
Letting \(M\to\infty\) and using \(\texttt{lem:gaussian-quotient-reduction}\) proves \(G_S\leq G_A\), and maximizing (58) at the displayed radii proves \(H_{\lambda,T}\leq G_A\).

Write \(X=h+\xi\), independently generate \(E\sim N(0,\Sigma)\), and put
\[
X^{\mathrm s}=X+\sqrt{(1-\lambda)/\lambda}\,E,\qquad
X^{\mathrm d}=X-\sqrt{\lambda/(1-\lambda)}\,E. \tag{59}
\]
Then
\[
X^{\mathrm s}\sim N(h,\Sigma/\lambda),\qquad
X^{\mathrm d}\sim N(h,\Sigma/(1-\lambda)),\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}(X^{\mathrm s},X^{\mathrm d})=0. \tag{60}
\]
Joint Gaussianity makes the two observations independent.  Retain
\[
\widehat S=\{i:X_i^{\mathrm s}\geq\max_jX_j^{\mathrm s}-T\}. \tag{61}
\]
Conditional on \(\widehat S=S\), feed \(\sqrt{1-\lambda}\,X_S^{\mathrm d}\) to the compact selector.  Its covariance is \(\Sigma_{SS}\), its mean is \(\sqrt{1-\lambda}h_S\), and the construction is measurable and location invariant.

Let \(b\) maximize \(h\), set \(d_i=h_b-h_i\), and write \(\xi^{\mathrm s}=X^{\mathrm s}-h\).  Each screening contrast has standard deviation at most \(a_\lambda=\sqrt{2/\lambda}\bar\sigma\).  Since
\[
\Phi(-x)\leq\varphi(x)/x\qquad(x>0), \tag{62}
\]
if \(b\notin\widehat S\), summing over a maximizing screening coordinate gives deletion loss at most
\[
\frac{(d-1)a_\lambda}{\sqrt{2\pi}}
e^{-T^2/(2a_\lambda^2)}. \tag{63}
\]
If retained \(i,j\) have true gap \(D>2T\), then the opposite screening-noise contrast is at least \(D-T\geq D/2\); (62) gives
\[
D\Pr\{i,j\text{ retained}\}
\leq\frac{2a_\lambda}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-T^2/(2a_\lambda^2)}. \tag{64}
\]
The range of a bad retained set is bounded by the sum of its oriented pairwise gaps.  Summing (63)--(64) over fewer than \(d^2\) indices is at most the displayed tail term.

Otherwise the true range in \(S=\widehat S\) is at most \(2T\), and therefore
\[
\lVert[\sqrt{1-\lambda}h_S]\rVert_S
\leq2\sqrt{1-\lambda}T\sqrt{|S|-1}\leq M_S. \tag{65}
\]
Independence means conditioning on the screen does not alter the decision law.  The scaled conditional regret is at most \(G_{S,M_S}+\eta\); undoing the scale multiplies it by \((1-\lambda)^{-1/2}\).  Averaging over the partition by retained subsets costs their maximum, not their sum.  Combining (58), (63)--(65) proves the bound.  The constants are uniform on compact positive-variance cells and over the finite subsets \(S\subseteq A\).  No arm outside the declared \(A\) enters; global adaptation therefore requires \(A=\mathcal K\) and positive information for each candidate.
