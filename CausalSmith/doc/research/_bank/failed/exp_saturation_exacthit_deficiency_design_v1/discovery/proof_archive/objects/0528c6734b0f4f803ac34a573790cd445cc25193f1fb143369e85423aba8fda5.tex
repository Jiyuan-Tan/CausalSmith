Put \(b_C=\mathcal B_C(\delta_C)\).  Assumption (iii) gives a constant \(B<\infty\) such that \(0\leq\ell_C(\theta,a)\leq B\) for every \(C,\theta,a\); increasing \(B\) if necessary also gives \(0\leq\ell(\theta,a)\leq B\).  Hence \(0\leq b_C\leq B\).  Choose a subsequence \((C_j)\) such that
\[
 b_{C_j}\longrightarrow\liminf_{C\to\infty}b_C.
\]

Represent the randomized rule by its action-probability vector
\[
 V_C=(\delta_{C,a})_{a\in\mathcal D}\in\Delta_{\mathcal D}.
\]
By assumption (i), the laws under \(P_{C,0}\) of
\(\bigl(T_C,(L_{C,\theta})_{\theta\in\Theta}\bigr)\) are tight.  The simplex \(\Delta_{\mathcal D}\) is compact because \(\mathcal D\) is finite.  Therefore the joint laws of
\[
 \left(T_{C_j},(L_{C_j,\theta})_{\theta\in\Theta},V_{C_j}\right)
\]
are tight.  Passing to a further subsequence, without changing the displayed limit of \(b_{C_j}\), gives
\[
 \left(T_{C_j},(L_{C_j,\theta})_{\theta\in\Theta},V_{C_j}\right)
 \rightsquigarrow
 \left(G,(L_\theta(G))_{\theta\in\Theta},V\right)
 \tag{1}
\]
under the reference laws.  Let \(\Lambda\) denote the limiting joint law in (1).  Its first two marginals are the marginals fixed by assumption (i), and \(V\in\Delta_{\mathcal D}\) holds \(\Lambda\)-almost surely because the simplex is closed.

We next pass each risk to the limit.  Change of measure and the definition of \(V_C\) give, for every \(\theta\in\Theta\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal R_{C,\theta}(\delta_C)
 &=\mathbb E_{P_{C,\theta}}\!\left[
     \sum_{a\in\mathcal D}V_{C,a}\ell_C(\theta,a)
   \right]\\
 &=\mathbb E_{P_{C,0}}\!\left[
     L_{C,\theta}\sum_{a\in\mathcal D}V_{C,a}
     \ell_C(\theta,a)
   \right].
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{2}
\]
For fixed \(R<\infty\), (1), bounded continuity of
\((x,l,v)\mapsto(l_\theta\wedge R)\sum_a v_a\ell(\theta,a)\), and assumption (iii) imply
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\mathbb E_{P_{C_j,0}}\!\left[
   (L_{C_j,\theta}\wedge R)
   \sum_{a\in\mathcal D}V_{C_j,a}\ell_{C_j}(\theta,a)
 \right]\\
 &\hspace{30mm}\longrightarrow
 \mathbb E_{\Lambda}\!\left[
   (L_\theta(G)\wedge R)
   \sum_{a\in\mathcal D}V_a\ell(\theta,a)
 \right].
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{3}
\]
Indeed, replacing \(\ell_{C_j}\) by \(\ell\) changes the left side by at most
\[
 R\max_{\vartheta\in\Theta,\ a\in\mathcal D}
 |\ell_{C_j}(\vartheta,a)-\ell(\vartheta,a)|,
\]
which tends to zero by assumption (iii).  The truncation error on the finite-experiment side is at most
\[
 B\,\mathbb E_{P_{C_j,0}}\!\left[
 L_{C_j,\theta}\mathbf 1\{L_{C_j,\theta}>R\}
\right],
 \tag{4}
\]
uniformly in \(j\), and (4) tends to zero as \(R\to\infty\) by assumption (ii).  On the limit side the analogous error tends to zero because \(L_\theta(G)\) is a \(Q_0\)-likelihood ratio and hence is nonnegative with
\(\mathbb E_{Q_0}[L_\theta(G)]=1\).  Letting first \(j\to\infty\) in (3) and then \(R\to\infty\), equations (2)--(4) yield
\[
 \mathcal R_{C_j,\theta}(\delta_{C_j})
 \longrightarrow
 \mathbb E_{\Lambda}\!\left[
 L_\theta(G)\sum_{a\in\mathcal D}V_a\ell(\theta,a)
 \right].
 \tag{5}
\]

It remains to turn the possibly enlarged limiting randomization \(V\) into a rule in the Gaussian experiment.  Because \(G\) takes values in the standard Borel space \(\mathbb R^d\), choose measurable versions
\[
 \bar\delta_a(g)=\mathbb E_{\Lambda}[V_a\mid G=g],
 \qquad a\in\mathcal D.
\]
Nonnegativity of the coordinates of \(V\) and \(\sum_aV_a=1\) imply
\(\bar\delta(g)\in\Delta_{\mathcal D}\) for \(Q_0\)-almost every \(g\); redefine it at most on a \(Q_0\)-null set to obtain a measurable randomized rule everywhere.  Since \(L_\theta(G)\) is measurable with respect to \(G\), conditional expectation and then the likelihood-ratio identity give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_{\Lambda}\!\left[
 L_\theta(G)\sum_aV_a\ell(\theta,a)
 \right]
 &=\mathbb E_{Q_0}\!\left[
 L_\theta(G)\sum_a\bar\delta_a(G)\ell(\theta,a)
 \right]\\
 &=\mathbb E_{Q_\theta}\!\left[
 \sum_a\bar\delta_a(G)\ell(\theta,a)
 \right].
 \tag{6}
 \end{aligned}
\]
The absolute integrability needed in (6) follows from the bound \(B\) and
\(\mathbb E_{Q_0}L_\theta(G)=1\).

Because \(\Theta\) is finite, (5)--(6) may be summed against \(\varpi\).  The choice of the subsequence and the definition of the optimal limit Bayes risk then give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \liminf_{C\to\infty}\mathcal B_C(\delta_C)
 &=\lim_{j\to\infty}b_{C_j}\\
 &=\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}\varpi_\theta
   \mathbb E_{Q_\theta}\!\left[
     \sum_{a\in\mathcal D}\bar\delta_a(G)\ell(\theta,a)
   \right]\\
 &\geq \mathcal B_\infty^*(\varpi).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Finally, for every \(C\), a maximum dominates every probability-weighted average:
\[
 \max_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathcal R_{C,\theta}(\delta_C)
 \geq
 \sum_{\theta\in\Theta}\varpi_\theta
 \mathcal R_{C,\theta}(\delta_C)
 =\mathcal B_C(\delta_C).
\]
Taking lower limits and using the Bayes-risk inequality proves the asserted maximal-risk bound.
