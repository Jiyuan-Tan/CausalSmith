Let \(R_{\mathrm{full}}\) and \(R_{\mathrm{hit}}\) denote the two displayed minimax risks.  By \(\texttt{def:regime-experiments}\), the hit record is a measurable image of the full record.  Every hit-data rule therefore lifts by composition to a full-data rule, so
\[
R_{\mathrm{full}}\leq R_{\mathrm{hit}}. \tag{12}
\]
For the converse, for each \(z\in\mathcal Z_n\) let \(F_z\) be the marginal law under \(P\) of the vector \((Y_j(z))_{j=1}^n\in[0,1]^n\).  Any collection \((F_z:z\in\mathcal Z_n)\) is realized by the legal product schedule law \(\bigotimes_zF_z\) on the compact schedule space.  Fix known laws \(F_z^0\) for assignments outside \(\bigcup_k\mathcal S_k\), and restrict nature to product schedule laws with these nontarget marginals and arbitrary target marginals.  This restriction leaves the possible values of \(U\) and all possible target-hit laws unchanged, by \(\texttt{def:exact-slice-welfare}\).

Under this restricted family, conditional on the reduced record, every omitted nontarget outcome vector has the known law \(F_{Z_c}^0\), independently across clusters by \(\texttt{ass:iid-schedules}\), \(\texttt{ass:isolated-clusters}\), and the product coupling.  Given any full-data randomized rule, construct a reduced-data rule by drawing the missing nontarget vectors from those known conditional laws and applying the full rule.  Standard-Borelness ensures that this composition is a measurable Markov kernel.  For every law in the restricted family, the completed record has exactly the full-experiment distribution, so the two rules have identical risk pointwise.  Thus, for every full rule \(\delta\),
\[
\sup_P\mathbb E_P\mathcal L(U,\delta)
\geq \sup_{P\ \mathrm{restricted}}\mathbb E_P\mathcal L(U,\delta)
=\sup_P\mathbb E_P\mathcal L(U,\delta^0)
\geq R_{\mathrm{hit}}. \tag{13}
\]
The middle supremum may be written over all \(P\) because the restricted family exhausts the target marginals and the loss is target-only.  Taking the infimum in (13) and combining with (12) proves the equality for every bounded target-only loss on any standard-Borel decision space.

Finally, \(\texttt{lem:exact-hit-factorization}\) gives, for \(z\in\mathcal S_k\),
\[
\Pr(L_c=\ell\mid Z_c=z)=\frac{p_\ell B_{k\ell}}{q_k}. \tag{14}
\]
This parameter-free kernel is well defined because every \(B_{k\ell}>0\) and hence \(q_k>0\).  A label-observing rule can be reproduced after label deletion by simulating (14), while forgetting the label gives the reverse comparison.  The same two inequalities prove label deletion.  A ranking vector is a finite, hence standard-Borel, action, so the Han--Owusu--Shin ranking action is covered.
