Because only one assignment is observed from each schedule, \(P_0\) and the product coupling \(\bar P_0=\bigotimes_zP_{0,z}\) induce the same one-cluster observed law.  The nontarget marginals are unchanged along the path.  For \(z\in\mathcal S_k\), differentiation of the finite-support exponential tilt at zero gives the conditional score
\[
 h_k\{W(z)-\mu_z\}/\tau_k^2
\]
and
\[
 \left.\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb E_{P_{t,h,z}}W(z)\right|_{t=0}
 =h_k\operatorname{Var}_{P_0}(W(z))/\tau_k^2.
\]
After averaging over \(z\in\mathcal S_k\), the definition of \(\tau_k^2\) gives
\[
 \left.\frac{d}{dt}U_k(\bar P_{t,h})\right|_{t=0}
 =\frac{h_k}{M_k\tau_k^2}\sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}\operatorname{Var}_{P_0}(W(z))=h_k. \tag{12}
\]
Division is valid by active-slice nondegeneracy.  Averaging the conditional score over the design identifies the observed score coordinate as \(\dot\ell_k^B\).  It is centered, bounded, and has covariance
\[
 \mathbb E[\dot\ell_j^B\dot\ell_k^B]
 ={\mathbf 1}\{j=k\}\frac{q_k}{M_k\tau_k^4}
 \sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}\operatorname{Var}_{P_0}(W(z))
 ={\mathbf 1}\{j=k\}\frac{q_k}{\tau_k^2}, \tag{13}
\]
which is \(I_B\).

Since \(W\in[0,1]\), the log normalizer has, uniformly for \(a\) in a fixed neighborhood of zero,
\[
 \Lambda_{z,k}(a)=\frac{a^2}{2}\operatorname{Var}_{P_0}(W(z))+O(|a|^3). \tag{14}
\]
Substitute \(a=C^{-1/2}h_k/\tau_k^2\), sum over clusters, and use the cell-frequency law of large numbers.  The linear terms equal \(h'\Delta_C^B\); equations (13)--(14) make the quadratic terms \(-h'I_Bh/2+o_P(1)\); and the total cubic remainder is \(O(C^{-1/2}\max_k|h_k|^3)\), uniformly on bounded \(h\).  This proves the likelihood expansion.  The summands of \(\Delta_C^B\) are iid, centered, bounded vectors with covariance \(I_B\), so the characteristic-function expansion used in (6) proves \(\Delta_C^B\Rightarrow N(0,I_B)\).

Under \(\bar P_{C,h}\), equation (12) and the bounded third derivative imply
\[
 \sqrt C\{U_k(\bar P_{C,h})-U_k(P_0)\}=h_k+O(C^{-1/2})
\]
uniformly for bounded \(h\).  The direct triangular-array version of (5)--(6) has means shifted by this amount and variances converging to their baseline values.  It follows that
\[
 \sqrt C\{\widetilde U_{C,A}-U_A(P_0)\}\Rightarrow
 (h_k+\sqrt{\tau_k^2/q_k}Z_k)_{k\in A}. \tag{15}
\]

It remains to justify the all-rule lower bound rather than merely the estimator limit.  Fix a finite set \(H_0\subset H_A\), a prior \(\lambda\) on it, and \(M\) containing \(H_0\).  The joint likelihood-ratio vector supplied by the proved LAN expansion converges under the baseline to the likelihood-ratio vector of the Gaussian shift experiment.  Its coordinates have expectation one, and bounded exponential-family scores give uniform integrability on finite \(H_0\).  Hence every sequence of randomized rules has limiting Bayes risk at least the Gaussian Bayes risk for \((H_0,\lambda)\).  The scaled loss converges uniformly on \(H_0\) to \(\max_{k\in A}h_k-h_a\); choosing an inactive, baseline-separated action only adds a nonnegative term diverging at rate \(\sqrt C\).

For clarity, the passage from finite priors to the displayed value is elementary here.  On each compact ball in \(H_A\), Gaussian convolution makes the risk of every measurable randomized rule continuous in \(h\).  Randomized rules form a convex weak-star compact subset of the product of unit balls of \(L^\infty\), and the finite action set makes the risk affine in the rule and in the prior.  Separating the epigraph of attainable risk vectors therefore gives minimax equality; finite-support priors are dense among priors on the compact ball.  Increasing the balls to \(H_A\) yields exactly the definition of \(G_A(\tau_A^2,q_A)\).  Thus
\[
 \lim_{M\to\infty}\liminf_C\sup_{\|h\|\leq M}\sqrt C\,\mathbb E_h[\max_{k\in A}U_k-U_{\delta_C}]
 \geq G_A(\tau_A^2,q_A).
\]
Finally, nuisance deletion shows that adding the full nontarget record cannot lower this worst-case bound, proving the last sentence.
