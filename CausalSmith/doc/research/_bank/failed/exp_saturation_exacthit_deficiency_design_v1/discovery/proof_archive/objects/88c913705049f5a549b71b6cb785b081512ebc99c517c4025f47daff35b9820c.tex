For homogeneity, let \(X=h+\operatorname{diag}(v_k/(sr_k))^{1/2}Z\), put \(\widetilde X=\sqrt sX\) and \(\widetilde h=\sqrt sh\), and use the bijection between selectors \(\delta(x)\) and \(\widetilde\delta(\widetilde x)=\delta(\widetilde x/\sqrt s)\).  The transformed experiment has rates \(r_A\), while
\[
\max_k\widetilde h_k-\widetilde h_a=\sqrt s(\max_kh_k-h_a).
\]
Taking the infimum and supremum proves \(G_A(v_A,sr_A)=s^{-1/2}G_A(v_A,r_A)\).

If \(r'_k\geq r_k>0\), observe the experiment with rates \(r'_A\) and independently add coordinatewise Gaussian noise with variance \(v_k/r_k-v_k/r'_k\).  The result has exactly the rate-\(r_A\) law.  Composing any rate-\(r_A\) rule with this kernel proves \(G_A(v_A,r'_A)\leq G_A(v_A,r_A)\).  Apply this monotonicity to
\[
(1-\xi)r_A\leq r'_A\leq(1+\xi)r_A
\]
and then use homogeneity to obtain the displayed two-sided modulus.

Finiteness follows from the largest-coordinate rule.  If \(i\) is a best arm and \(d_j=h_i-h_j\geq0\), selecting \(j\) requires \(X_j\geq X_i\) for at least one such \(j\), and hence
\[
R(h,\delta_{\max})\leq\sum_{j\ne i}d_j\Phi\!\left(-\frac{d_j}{\sqrt{v_i/r_i+v_j/r_j}}\right)
\leq\kappa\sum_{j\ne i}\sqrt{v_i/r_i+v_j/r_j}<\infty. \tag{3}
\]
For strict positivity, fix \(i\ne j\), set all other coordinates to \(-H\), and give equal prior mass to the two parameter points with \((h_i,h_j)=(d/2,-d/2)\) and \((-d/2,d/2)\).  The other observations have identical distributions at the two points and convey no testing information.  Sending \(H\to\infty\) makes choosing another arm weakly worse than choosing either \(i\) or \(j\).  The likelihood-ratio test is the sign of \(X_i-X_j\), whose error is \(\Phi(-d/a_{ij})\), where
\[
a_{ij}^2=v_i/r_i+v_j/r_j.
\]
The equal-prior Bayes regret is \(d\Phi(-d/a_{ij})\).  Choosing \(d=a_{ij}x\) and maximizing \(x\Phi(-x)\) gives
\[
G_A(v_A,r_A)\geq\kappa a_{ij}>0. \tag{4}
\]

If \(r_i^{(m)}\downarrow0\) while \(r_j^{(m)}\to r_j>0\), (4) tends to \(+\infty\).  If a second coordinate also tends to zero the same conclusion holds.  Thus every positive sequence approaching a rate vector with an action-relevant zero coordinate has liminf value \(+\infty\), and the stipulated lower-semicontinuous boundary value equals \(+\infty\).
