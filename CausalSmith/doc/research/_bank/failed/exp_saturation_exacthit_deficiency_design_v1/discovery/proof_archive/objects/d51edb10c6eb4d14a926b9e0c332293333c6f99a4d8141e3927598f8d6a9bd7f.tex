Fix \(0<\gamma<1\), and abbreviate \(C_k=C_{C,k}\) and \(a_{C,k}=C_k/C\).  Assumption \texttt{ass:cr-all-shares} gives
\[
 a_{C,k}\longrightarrow\alpha_k>0,
 \qquad
 \min_{k\in\mathcal K}a_{C,k}\geq \frac12\min_{k\in\mathcal K}\alpha_k>0
 \tag{1}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(C\).  We first strengthen the pointwise conclusion of \texttt{thm:efficient-exact-hit-cr} to the uniform approximation needed below.

Condition on the fixed-count label vector.  By \texttt{ass:iid-schedules}, \texttt{ass:cr-label-schedule-independence}, and \texttt{ass:cr-exact-slices}, the cluster observations are conditionally independent and, for \(c\) with \(L_c=k\),
\[
 \Pr(Z_c=z\mid L,Y)=M_k^{-1}\mathbf 1\{z\in\mathcal S_k\},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_P\{W_c(z)-\mu_z\}=0.
 \tag{2}
\]
For \(z\in\mathcal S_k\), put
\[
 S_z=\sum_{c:L_c=k}\mathbf 1\{Z_c=z\}\{W_c(z)-\mu_z\}.
\]
On the event that every \(N_z\) is positive, the construction in \texttt{def:calibrated-estimator} gives the exact identity
\[
 \begin{split}
 \sqrt C(\widetilde U_{C,k}-U_k)
 &=\frac{\sqrt C}{M_k}\sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}\frac{S_z}{N_z}\\
 &=\frac1{\sqrt C}\sum_{c=1}^C
       \frac{C}{C_k}\mathbf 1\{L_c=k\}
       \{W_c(Z_c)-\mu_{Z_c}\}+R_{C,k},                                      \tag{3}\\
 R_{C,k}
 &=\frac{\sqrt C}{M_k}\sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}
       \left(\frac1{N_z}-\frac{M_k}{C_k}\right)S_z .                         \tag{4}
 \end{split}
\]
The first equality uses the definition \(U_k=M_k^{-1}\sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}\mu_z\), so the causal target has not been replaced by an observed-law functional.

Conditionally on the labels, \(N_z\) is a marginal \(\operatorname{Binomial}(C_k,M_k^{-1})\) count.  Direct variance calculations give
\[
 \mathbb E\{(N_z-C_k/M_k)^2\mid L\}
 =C_kM_k^{-1}(1-M_k^{-1})\leq C_k/4,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_P(S_z^2\mid L,Z)=N_z\operatorname{Var}_P\{W(z)\}\leq C_k/4.
\]
The pointwise inequality \(\mathbf 1\{|X|>x\}\leq X^2/x^2\), followed by a union bound over the fixed number of cells, therefore shows, uniformly over all bounded-support schedule laws \(P\),
\[
 \max_{z\in\mathcal S_k}|N_z-C_k/M_k|=O_P(\sqrt{C_k}),
 \qquad
 \max_{z\in\mathcal S_k}|S_z|=O_P(\sqrt{C_k}).                               \tag{5}
\]
There are only the fixed number \(M_k\) of cells.  On the event \(N_z\geq C_k/(2M_k)\),
\[
 \left|\frac1{N_z}-\frac{M_k}{C_k}\right|
 \leq \frac{2M_k^2}{C_k^2}|N_z-C_k/M_k|.
\]
Equations (1), (4), and (5) therefore imply
\[
 \sup_{P}\Pr_P\!\left(\max_{k\in\mathcal K}|R_{C,k}|>\varepsilon\right)
 \longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{for every }\varepsilon>0.                                        \tag{6}
\]
Moreover, the probability excluded in deriving (3) is bounded, independently of \(P\), by
\[
 \Pr\{N_z=0\text{ for some }z\in\mathcal S_k, k\in\mathcal K\}
 \leq\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}M_k(1-M_k^{-1})^{C_k}
 \leq\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}M_k e^{-C_k/M_k}\longrightarrow0.                \tag{7}
\]
Thus the prescribed full-rank fallback is asymptotically inactive; when it is active it returns every rank and hence cannot cause undercoverage.

Write the summand in (3) as \(\phi^{CR}_{C,c,k}\).  Equation (2) gives conditional mean zero.  Distinct coordinates have zero product because a cluster has one label, while the conditional diagonal second moments are
\[
 \frac1C\sum_{c=1}^C
 \mathbb E_P\{(\phi^{CR}_{C,c,k})^2\mid L\}
 =\frac{C}{C_k}\frac1{M_k}
   \sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}\operatorname{Var}_P\{W(z)\}
 =\frac{\tau_k^2(P)}{a_{C,k}}.                                                \tag{8}
\]
This is the finite-\(C\) covariance
\[
 \Sigma_C(P)=\operatorname{diag}\!\left(\frac{\tau_k^2(P)}{a_{C,k}}:
 k\in\mathcal K\right),
 \qquad
 \sup_P\|\Sigma_C(P)-\Sigma_{CR}^{(K)}(P)\|\longrightarrow0,               \tag{9}
\]
where the last assertion follows from (1), boundedness \(\tau_k^2(P)\leq1/4\), and the definition of \(\Sigma_{CR}^{(K)}\).

For completeness, the Gaussian approximation in (8) follows from the primitive design conditions rather than from an assumed limit law.  For fixed \(u\in\mathbb R^K\), conditional independence and a third-order Taylor expansion give
\[
 \sum_{c=1}^C\log\mathbb E_P\!\left[
   \exp\!\left\{\frac{i}{\sqrt C}u^{\prime}\phi^{CR}_{C,c}\right\}
   \middle|L\right]
 =-\frac12u^{\prime}\Sigma_C(P)u+o(1).                                      \tag{10}
\]
Indeed, by (1) and \(0\leq W\leq1\), \(\max_c\|\phi^{CR}_{C,c}\|\) is bounded by a constant independent of \(C\) and \(P\).  Hence the sum of the cubic Taylor remainders is \(O(C^{-1/2})\), and the sum of the quadratic errors in replacing \(\log(1+x)\) by \(x\) is \(O(C^{-1})\).  Equations (3), (6), and (10) establish the joint Gaussian approximation uniformly along every sequence of laws for which \(\Sigma_C(P)\) converges.  This also supplies directly the design-based CLT asserted pointwise in \texttt{thm:efficient-exact-hit-cr}.

Next consider studentization.  On the event of defined cells, the empirical-second-moment construction of \(\widehat\Sigma_C^{(K)}\) has zero off-diagonal entries and can be written
\[
 \widehat\Sigma_{C,kk}^{(K)}
 =\frac{C}{C_k^2}\sum_{c:L_c=k}
       \{W_c(Z_c)-\overline W_{Z_c}\}^2.                                    \tag{11}
\]
Replacing \(\overline W_{Z_c}\) in (11) by \(\mu_{Z_c}\) changes the inner average by
\[
 \frac1{C_k}\sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}
 N_z(\overline W_z-\mu_z)^2,                                                  \tag{12}
\]
using the within-cell least-squares identity.  Conditional second-moment bounds and (5) make (12) \(o_P(1)\) uniformly over \(P\).  The oracle average
\[
 \frac1{C_k}\sum_{c:L_c=k}\{W_c(Z_c)-\mu_{Z_c}\}^2
\]
has conditional expectation \(\tau_k^2(P)\) and variance at most \(C_k^{-1}\), because its summands lie in \([0,1]\).  Applying \(\mathbf 1\{|X|>\varepsilon\}\leq X^2/\varepsilon^2\), then using (1) and the finiteness of \(K\), now yields
\[
 \sup_P\Pr_P\{\|\widehat\Sigma_C^{(K)}-\Sigma_C(P)\|>\varepsilon\}
 \longrightarrow0.                                                           \tag{13}
\]
Together with (9), this proves the stated covariance consistency, and in fact proves it uniformly over the displayed nondegenerate class.

We now make the max-\(t\) step explicit.  For every unordered pair \(r=(i,j)\), let \(d_r=e_i-e_j\).  For a positive definite covariance matrix \(V\), define
\[
 T(V,G)=\max_{i<j}\frac{|d_{ij}^{\prime}G|}
                         {(d_{ij}^{\prime}Vd_{ij})^{1/2}},
 \qquad G\sim N(0,V),                                                         \tag{14}
\]
and let \(c_\gamma(V)\) be its \((1-\gamma)\)-quantile.  On the class in the theorem, \texttt{ass:nondegenerate-all-slices} and (1) imply
\[
 d_{ij}^{\prime}\Sigma_{CR}^{(K)}(P)d_{ij}
 =\frac{\tau_i^2(P)}{\alpha_i}+\frac{\tau_j^2(P)}{\alpha_j}
 \geq2\underline\tau^2>0.                                                    \tag{15}
\]
Consequently every denominator in (14) is nonzero.  The law of the maximum in (14) is continuous and strictly increasing on its positive support: the contrast vector is Gaussian with a positive density on its \((K-1)\)-dimensional contrast space, while the boundary of a max-norm ball is a finite union of lower-dimensional hyperplanes.  It follows that
\[
 \Pr\{T(V,G)\leq c_\gamma(V)\}=1-\gamma.                                   \tag{16}
\]
On any compact set of positive definite diagonal \(V\)'s satisfying (15), the map \(V\mapsto c_\gamma(V)\) is continuous.  To see this directly, write \(G=V^{1/2}Z\) with \(Z\sim N(0,I_K)\); pointwise continuity in \(V\), dominated convergence for the associated indicator away from the probability-zero boundary, and (16) give continuity of the distribution functions and their quantiles.

We verify uniform simultaneous difference coverage by contradiction.  Let \(P_C\) be any sequence satisfying \(\min_k\tau_k^2(P_C)\geq\underline\tau^2\).  The schedule space \([0,1]^{n\times\mathcal Z_n}\) is compact; hence its probability laws are weakly compact.  From every subsequence one may therefore extract a further subsequence, still denoted \(P_C\), with \(P_C\Rightarrow P_\star\).  The functions \(W(z)\) and \(W(z)^2\) are bounded and continuous coordinate functions.  Thus \(U_k(P_C)\to U_k(P_\star)\), \(\tau_k^2(P_C)\to\tau_k^2(P_\star)\), and
\[
 \Sigma_C(P_C)\longrightarrow\Sigma_{CR}^{(K)}(P_\star).                    \tag{17}
\]
The characteristic-function calculation (10), the uniform remainder (6), and covariance consistency (13) then imply
\[
 \sqrt C\{\widetilde U_C-U(P_C)\}\Rightarrow
 N\{0,\Sigma_{CR}^{(K)}(P_\star)\},
 \qquad
 \widehat\Sigma_C^{(K)}\to_P\Sigma_{CR}^{(K)}(P_\star).                    \tag{18}
\]
Let \(\widehat c_C=c_\gamma(\widehat\Sigma_C^{(K)})\), with the prescribed fallback on the event where it is undefined.  Equations (14)--(18), plus a \(\delta\)-sandwich at the continuous distribution in (16), give
\[
 \Pr_{P_C}\!\left\{
  |d_{ij}^{\prime}(\widetilde U_C-U(P_C))|
  \leq \widehat c_C
       \left(\frac{d_{ij}^{\prime}\widehat\Sigma_C^{(K)}d_{ij}}{C}\right)^{1/2}
  \text{ for every }i<j
 \right\}
 \longrightarrow1-\gamma.                                                    \tag{19}
\]
Because every allegedly unfavorable sequence has a subsequence satisfying (17)--(19), the usual subsequence contradiction yields
\[
 \liminf_{C\to\infty}\inf_{P:\min_k\tau_k^2(P)\geq\underline\tau^2}
 \Pr_P(E_C)\geq1-\gamma,                                                      \tag{20}
\]
where \(E_C\) is the simultaneous pairwise-coverage event in braces in (19).  This argument uses weak compactness of laws on the compact bounded schedule space; in the binary submodel it reduces to compactness of a finite probability simplex.

It remains to connect (20) to the set-valued causal rank target.  Write the pairwise intervals inverted by \(\mathcal R_{C,k}(\gamma)\) as
\[
 I_{C,jk}=\left[
  \widetilde U_{C,j}-\widetilde U_{C,k}
  \mathbin{-}\widehat c_C\widehat s_{jk}/\sqrt C,
  \widetilde U_{C,j}-\widetilde U_{C,k}
  \mathbin{+}\widehat c_C\widehat s_{jk}/\sqrt C
 \right],
 \quad
 \widehat s_{jk}^2=d_{jk}^{\prime}\widehat\Sigma_C^{(K)}d_{jk}.              \tag{21}
\]
The max-\(t\) inversion in \texttt{def:rank-target-and-set} has endpoints
\[
 \widehat r^-_{C,k}=1+\sum_{j\ne k}\mathbf 1\{\inf I_{C,jk}>0\},
 \qquad
 \widehat r^+_{C,k}=K-\sum_{j\ne k}\mathbf 1\{\sup I_{C,jk}<0\}.           \tag{22}
\]
On \(E_C\), \(U_j-U_k\in I_{C,jk}\) for every pair.  Hence
\[
 \{j:\inf I_{C,jk}>0\}\subseteq\{j:U_j>U_k\},
 \qquad
 \{j:\sup I_{C,jk}<0\}\subseteq\{j:U_j<U_k\}.                              \tag{23}
\]
Using the definitions of \(r_k^-(U)\) and \(r_k^+(U)\), (23) gives
\[
 \widehat r^-_{C,k}\leq r_k^-(U)\leq r_k^+(U)\leq\widehat r^+_{C,k}
 \quad\text{for every }k\in\mathcal K.                                      \tag{24}
\]
Therefore \(E_C\) implies
\(\operatorname{Rank}_k(U)\subseteq\mathcal R_{C,k}(\gamma)\) simultaneously for every \(k\).  Combining this deterministic implication with (20) proves the displayed coverage bound.  No step imposed a positive separation between any two components of \(U\); equalities and sequences approaching any partial-tie configuration are therefore covered uniformly.
