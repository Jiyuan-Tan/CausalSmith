Write \(\Delta_{\mathsf A}=\{(d_a)_{a\in\mathsf A}:d_a\geq0,\ \sum_a d_a=1\}\). This is a compact subset of the finite-dimensional Euclidean space \(\mathbb R^{\mathsf A}\), because \(\mathsf A\) is finite. For the rule \(\delta_r\), define its conditional action-probability vector
\[
 D_r=\bigl(\delta_r(a\mid X_r)\bigr)_{a\in\mathsf A}\in\Delta_{\mathsf A}.
\]
This definition represents every measurable randomized rule; no realization of the auxiliary randomization is needed in the risk because the loss is averaged against its conditional action probabilities.

Let
\[
 b_r=\sum_{\vartheta\in\Theta}\omega_\vartheta
 R_r(\vartheta,\delta_r).
\]
The loss bound \(0\leq\ell_r\leq L\) gives \(0\leq b_r\leq L\). Hence there is a subsequence, denoted \((r_j)\), such that \(b_{r_j}\to\liminf_r b_r\). By the assumed weak convergence, the laws under \(P_{r_j,\vartheta_0}\) of
\[
 Y_{r_j}=\left(T_{r_j},(\Lambda_{r_j,\vartheta})_{\vartheta\in\Theta}\right)
\]
are tight. Since \(D_{r_j}\) takes values in the compact set \(\Delta_{\mathsf A}\), the joint laws of \((Y_{r_j},D_{r_j})\) are tight: for each \(\epsilon>0\), a compact set carrying at least \(1-\epsilon\) of the law of \(Y_{r_j}\), multiplied by \(\Delta_{\mathsf A}\), carries at least the same probability for the joint law. Consequently a further subsequence, not relabeled, has a weak limit
\[
 \left(T_{r_j},(\Lambda_{r_j,\vartheta})_{\vartheta\in\Theta},D_{r_j}\right)
 \Rightarrow
 \left(G,(\Lambda_\vartheta(G))_{\vartheta\in\Theta},D\right)
\]
under the reference laws. Projection onto the first two coordinates and the hypothesized weak limit identify the displayed marginal, while closedness of \(\Delta_{\mathsf A}\) gives \(D\in\Delta_{\mathsf A}\) almost surely.

Set
\[
 e_r=\max_{\vartheta\in\Theta,\ a\in\mathsf A}
 |\ell_r(\vartheta,a)-\ell(\vartheta,a)|,
\]
so that \(e_r\to0\) by the loss-convergence hypothesis. For every \(\vartheta\), the likelihood-ratio identity \(\mathbb E_{P_{r,\vartheta_0}}\Lambda_{r,\vartheta}=1\) and \(D_r\in\Delta_{\mathsf A}\) yield
\[
 \left|
 \mathbb E_{P_{r,\vartheta_0}}\!\left[
  \Lambda_{r,\vartheta}\sum_aD_{r,a}\ell_r(\vartheta,a)
 \right]
 -
 \mathbb E_{P_{r,\vartheta_0}}\!\left[
  \Lambda_{r,\vartheta}\sum_aD_{r,a}\ell(\vartheta,a)
 \right]
 \right|
 \leq e_r.
\]
We next pass to the limit in the second expectation without assuming bounded likelihood ratios. For \(M_0<\infty\), replace a likelihood coordinate \(x\) by the bounded continuous truncation
\[
 x^{(M_0)}=\min\{M_0,\max\{0,x\}\}.
\]
Joint weak convergence and bounded continuity give
\[
 \mathbb E_{P_{r_j,\vartheta_0}}\!\left[
  \Lambda_{r_j,\vartheta}^{(M_0)}
  \sum_aD_{r_j,a}\ell(\vartheta,a)
 \right]
 \longrightarrow
 \mathbb E_{Q_{\vartheta_0}}\!\left[
  \Lambda_\vartheta(G)^{(M_0)}
  \sum_aD_a\ell(\vartheta,a)
 \right].
\]
The absolute difference between either truncated expression and its untruncated counterpart is at most \(L\) times the corresponding likelihood-ratio tail. The assumed uniform integrability makes
\[
 \sup_r\mathbb E_{P_{r,\vartheta_0}}\!\left[
  \Lambda_{r,\vartheta}\mathbf 1\{\Lambda_{r,\vartheta}>M_0\}
 \right]\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{as }M_0\to\infty.
\]
Positive definiteness of \(V\) justifies \(V^{-1}\), and the displayed Gaussian density ratio satisfies \(\mathbb E_{Q_{\vartheta_0}}\Lambda_\vartheta(G)=1\); hence its corresponding tail expectation also tends to zero. First letting \(j\to\infty\) and then \(M_0\to\infty\), and finally using \(e_{r_j}\to0\), proves
\[
 \mathbb E_{P_{r_j,\vartheta_0}}\!\left[
  \Lambda_{r_j,\vartheta}\sum_aD_{r_j,a}\ell_{r_j}(\vartheta,a)
 \right]
 \longrightarrow
 \mathbb E_{Q_{\vartheta_0}}\!\left[
  \Lambda_\vartheta(G)\sum_aD_a\ell(\vartheta,a)
 \right].
\]

It remains to show that the right-hand side is the risk of an admissible rule based only on the Gaussian observation. For each \(a\in\mathsf A\), choose a Borel version
\[
 \eta(a\mid G)=\mathbb E[D_a\mid G].
\]
Conditional expectation preserves nonnegativity and finite sums, so \(\eta(a\mid G)\geq0\) and \(\sum_a\eta(a\mid G)=1\), \(Q_{\vartheta_0}\)-almost surely. On the exceptional null set, set all mass on any fixed element of the nonempty set \(\mathsf A\). This produces a measurable Markov kernel \(\eta\) on \(\mathbb R^d\). Because \(\Lambda_\vartheta(G)\) is measurable with respect to \(G\), the defining property of conditional expectation and then the Gaussian likelihood-ratio identity give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_{Q_{\vartheta_0}}\!\left[
  \Lambda_\vartheta(G)\sum_aD_a\ell(\vartheta,a)
 \right]
 &=\mathbb E_{Q_{\vartheta_0}}\!\left[
  \Lambda_\vartheta(G)\sum_a\eta(a\mid G)\ell(\vartheta,a)
 \right]\\
 &=\mathbb E_{Q_\vartheta}\!\left[
  \sum_a\eta(a\mid G)\ell(\vartheta,a)
 \right]
 =R(\vartheta,\eta).
 \end{aligned}
\]
All Gaussian shifts have the same positive-definite covariance, so \(Q_\vartheta\ll Q_{\vartheta_0}\); thus the modification on the reference-null exceptional set does not alter any of these risks.

For the finite experiments, domination and the definition of \(\Lambda_{r,\vartheta}\) give, exactly,
\[
 R_r(\vartheta,\delta_r)
 =\mathbb E_{P_{r,\vartheta_0}}\!\left[
  \Lambda_{r,\vartheta}\sum_aD_{r,a}\ell_r(\vartheta,a)
 \right].
\]
Since \(\Theta\) is finite, the preceding coordinatewise limits may be summed against \(\omega\). Therefore
\[
 \liminf_r b_r
 =\lim_j b_{r_j}
 =\sum_{\vartheta\in\Theta}\omega_\vartheta R(\vartheta,\eta)
 \geq\inf_{\widetilde\eta}
       \sum_{\vartheta\in\Theta}\omega_\vartheta
       R(\vartheta,\widetilde\eta).
\]
Finally, for every \(r\), nonnegativity of the weights and \(\sum_\vartheta\omega_\vartheta=1\) imply
\[
 \max_{\vartheta\in\Theta}R_r(\vartheta,\delta_r)
 \geq\sum_{\vartheta\in\Theta}\omega_\vartheta
       R_r(\vartheta,\delta_r)=b_r.
\]
Taking lower limits proves both asserted inequalities. Zero prior masses, singleton finite sets, and boundary randomized action probabilities require no separate case, because none of the argument divides by a prior weight or an action probability.
