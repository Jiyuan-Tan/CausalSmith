Let \(H_{c,k}={\mathbf 1}\{Z_c\in\mathcal S_k\}\) and \(N_k=\sum_cH_{c,k}\).  Since \(N_k/C\to_Pq_k>0\), the zero-denominator fallback is negligible.  The ratio identity and a first-order denominator expansion give
\[
 \sqrt C(\widehat U^{\rm raw}_{C,k}-U_k)
 =\frac1{q_k\sqrt C}\sum_{c=1}^CH_{c,k}\{W_c(Z_c)-U_k\}+o_P(1)
 =\frac1{\sqrt C}\sum_{c=1}^C\psi_k(O_c)+o_P(1). \tag{11}
\]
Uniformity of \(Z\) on \(\mathcal S_k\) and the law of total variance yield
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(W(Z)\mid Z\in\mathcal S_k)
 =\frac1{M_k}\sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}\operatorname{Var}_P(W(z))
 +\frac1{M_k}\sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}(\mu_z-U_k)^2
 =\tau_k^2+b_k^2.
\]
Therefore \(\operatorname{Var}(\psi_k)=(\tau_k^2+b_k^2)/q_k=\rho_k^2/q_k\).  The efficient bound is \(\tau_k^2/q_k\), so equality holds exactly when the sum of squares \(b_k^2\) is zero.

For the stated four-cell witness, the four means have average \(U_1=(4/5+4/5+1/5+1/5)/4=1/2\).  A Bernoulli welfare bit with mean either \(4/5\) or \(1/5\) has variance \(4/25\), hence \(\tau_1^2=4/25\).  The between-cell variance is
\[
 b_1^2=\frac14\{4(3/10)^2\}=\frac9{100},
\]
 so \(\rho_1^2=4/25+9/100=1/4\).  Taking all unit outcomes under a given assignment equal to the corresponding bit produces a legal binary schedule law, which verifies the witness and the excess \(9/100\).
