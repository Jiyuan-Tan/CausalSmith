Put \(\sigma_z^2=\operatorname{Var}_{P_0}\{W(z)\}\).  For \(z\in\mathcal S_k\), Lemma \texttt{lem:exact-hit-factorization} gives
\[
r_z:=\Pr_0(Z=z)=\frac{q_k}{M_k}.
\tag{1}
\]
Every entry of \(B\) is positive because \(0<\pi_\ell<1\) and \(1\leq m_k\leq n-1\).  Since \(p\in\Delta_K\), (1) implies \(q_k>0\).  Assumption \texttt{ass:nondegenerate-active-slices} gives \(\tau_k^2\geq\underline\tau^2>0\) for \(k\in A\), so every division below is valid and \(I_B\) is positive definite.

First consider observational equivalence and the path derivative.  A cluster reveals the outcome vector at only its realized assignment.  The law \(\bar P_0=\bigotimes_zP_{0,z}\) has the same marginal \(P_{0,z}\) as \(P_0\) at every assignment \(z\).  The randomization is independent of the schedule by \texttt{ass:bernoulli-label-schedule-independence}, so integrating the kernel \(R_{B,p}\) against either schedule law gives
\[
Q^B_{\bar P_0,p}=Q^B_{P_0,p}.
\tag{2}
\]
For \(z\in\mathcal S_k\), write \(X_z=W(z)-\mu_z\).  Under \(P_{0,z}\), \(|X_z|\leq1\), \(\mathbb E_0X_z=0\), and \(\mathbb E_0X_z^2=\sigma_z^2\).  Hence the log moment-generating function in \texttt{def:least-favourable-path} satisfies
\[
\Lambda_{z,k}(0)=\Lambda'_{z,k}(0)=0,
\qquad
\Lambda''_{z,k}(0)=\sigma_z^2.
\tag{3}
\]
Differentiation is justified by the bounded support.  Indeed, on every fixed compact interval of tilt arguments the first three derivatives of \(\Lambda_{z,k}\) exist and are bounded uniformly over the finitely many target cells.  Therefore
\[
\Lambda_{z,k}(a)=\frac12a^2\sigma_z^2+O(|a|^3),
\qquad
\Lambda'_{z,k}(a)=a\sigma_z^2+O(a^2),
\tag{4}
\]
uniformly over those cells.  Since the tilted mean of \(W(z)\) equals \(\mu_z+\Lambda'_{z,k}(t h_k/\tau_k^2)\), (3) gives
\[
\left.\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb E_{P_{t,h,z}}W(z)\right|_{t=0}
=\frac{h_k\sigma_z^2}{\tau_k^2}.
\tag{5}
\]
Using the definition \(\tau_k^2=M_k^{-1}\sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}\sigma_z^2\) and averaging (5) over \(\mathcal S_k\),
\[
\left.\frac{d}{dt}U_k(\bar P_{t,h})\right|_{t=0}
=\frac{h_k}{M_k\tau_k^2}\sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}\sigma_z^2
=h_k.
\tag{6}
\]
Moreover, (4) shows that, uniformly over every finite set of representatives \(h\),
\[
U_k(\bar P_{C,h})=U_k(P_0)+C^{-1/2}h_k+O(C^{-1}),
\qquad k\in A.
\tag{7}
\]
All assignment marginals outside the active target slices are fixed by the path.

We next derive local asymptotic normality from the primitive randomization.  For one observation, the likelihood ratio is one off the active target slices and, when \(Z=z\in\mathcal S_k\), is
\[
\exp\!\left\{\frac{h_kX_z}{\sqrt C\,\tau_k^2}
-\Lambda_{z,k}\!\left(\frac{h_k}{\sqrt C\,\tau_k^2}\right)\right\}.
\tag{8}
\]
Thus, with \(a_{C,k}=h_k/(\sqrt C\,\tau_k^2)\), the sample log likelihood ratio equals
\[
\sum_{c=1}^C\sum_{k\in A}\mathbf1\{Z_c\in\mathcal S_k\}
\{a_{C,k}(W_c(Z_c)-\mu_{Z_c})-\Lambda_{Z_c,k}(a_{C,k})\}.
\tag{9}
\]
The linear part of (9) is \(h^{\prime}\Delta_C^B\).  By (1), the weak law of large numbers, and the definition of \(\tau_k^2\),
\[
\frac1C\sum_{c=1}^C
\mathbf1\{Z_c\in\mathcal S_k\}\sigma_{Z_c}^2
\longrightarrow_P
\frac{q_k}{M_k}\sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}\sigma_z^2
=q_k\tau_k^2.
\tag{10}
\]
Substituting (4) into the normalizer part of (9), using (10), gives its quadratic term
\[
-\frac12\sum_{k\in A}\frac{h_k^2q_k}{\tau_k^2}+o_P(1)
=-\frac12h^{\prime}I_Bh+o_P(1).
\tag{11}
\]
The sum of the cubic remainders is \(O(C^{-1/2}\max_{k\in A}|h_k|^3)\) for fixed \(h\).  The score coordinates are centered by (3), and disjointness of the slices together with (1) gives
\[
\mathbb E_0(\dot\ell_j^B\dot\ell_k^B)
=\mathbf1\{j=k\}\frac{q_k}{M_k\tau_k^4}
\sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}\sigma_z^2
=\mathbf1\{j=k\}\frac{q_k}{\tau_k^2}.
\tag{12}
\]
They are bounded because \(|X_z|\leq1\) and \(\tau_k^2\geq\underline\tau^2\).  Assumptions \texttt{ass:iid-schedules}, \texttt{ass:bernoulli-label-iid}, \texttt{ass:bernoulli-label-schedule-independence}, and \texttt{ass:bernoulli-units} therefore make the score vectors iid, centered, and bounded.  The scalar Lindeberg condition holds for every linear combination, so the Cram\'{e}r--Wold argument yields
\[
\Delta_C^B\Rightarrow N(0,I_B).
\tag{13}
\]
Equations (9)--(13) prove the displayed LAN expansion, including the score in every common-shift direction \(h+c\mathbf1_A\).

For later use, we verify rather than assume uniform integrability of every likelihood ratio on a finite local parameter set.  Let \(L_C(h)\) denote the likelihood ratio in (9).  Independence across clusters and conditioning on the realized assignment give the exact identity
\[
\mathbb E_0L_C(h)^2
=\left[
1+\sum_{k\in A}\sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}r_z
\left\{\exp\bigl(\Lambda_{z,k}(2a_{C,k})
-2\Lambda_{z,k}(a_{C,k})\bigr)-1\right\}
\right]^C.
\tag{14}
\]
Boundedness of the third derivatives used in (4) implies
\(\left|\Lambda_{z,k}(2a)-2\Lambda_{z,k}(a)\right|\leq C_0a^2\) for all sufficiently small \(|a|\), uniformly over the finitely many cells.  Hence, for every finite \(\Theta\subset\mathbb R^A\),
\[
\sup_{C\geq C_0'}\sup_{h\in\Theta}\mathbb E_0L_C(h)^2<\infty.
\tag{15}
\]
Thus \(\{L_C(h):C\geq1\}\) is uniformly integrable for each \(h\in\Theta\), and jointly so for the finite likelihood-ratio vector.  This is the required likelihood-ratio premise for the finite-prior passage below.

We also record the estimator limit jointly with the central sequence.  On the event that every active assignment cell is observed, whose complement has probability at most \(\sum_{k\in A}\sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}(1-r_z)^C\), ratio expansion of each cell mean gives
\[
\sqrt C\{\overline W_z-\mu_z\}
=\frac{1}{r_z\sqrt C}\sum_{c=1}^C
\mathbf1\{Z_c=z\}\{W_c(z)-\mu_z\}+o_P(1).
\tag{16}
\]
The displayed complement probability tends to zero exponentially, so the prespecified empty-cell fallback contributes \(o_P(1)\).  Averaging (16) and using \(r_z=q_k/M_k\) yields
\[
\sqrt C\{\widetilde U_{C,k}-U_k(P_0)\}
=\frac1{\sqrt C}\sum_{c=1}^C\phi_k^B(O_c)+o_P(1).
\tag{17}
\]
Besides the variance calculation in \texttt{thm:efficient-exact-hit-bernoulli}, direct use of (1) gives
\[
\mathbb E_0(\phi_j^B\phi_k^B)
=\mathbf1\{j=k\}\frac{\tau_k^2}{q_k},
\qquad
\mathbb E_0(\phi_j^B\dot\ell_k^B)=\mathbf1\{j=k\}.
\tag{18}
\]
The bounded-vector central limit argument applied jointly to (17) and \(\Delta_C^B\) therefore gives a centered joint Gaussian limit with covariance blocks \(\Sigma_{B,A}\), \(I_B\), and the identity cross-block.  From (11), (13), (15), and (18), for every bounded continuous \(f\), change of measure gives
\[
\mathbb E_{\bar P_{C,h}}f\!\left(\sqrt C\{\widetilde U_{C,A}-U_A(P_0)\}\right)
=\mathbb E_0\!\left[L_C(h)f\!\left(\sqrt C\{\widetilde U_{C,A}-U_A(P_0)\}\right)\right]
\longrightarrow
\mathbb E f(Z+h),
\tag{19}
\]
where \(Z\sim N(0,\Sigma_{B,A})\).  To see the last equality explicitly, exponential weighting of the joint Gaussian limit by \(\exp\{h^{\prime}\Delta-h^{\prime}I_Bh/2\}\) changes the mean of the first block by \(\operatorname{Cov}(Z,\Delta)h=h\) and leaves its covariance equal to \(\Sigma_{B,A}\).  Uniform integrability in (15) justifies passing the expectation in (19).  This proves the asserted local Gaussian limit without assuming a limit law under the alternatives.

It remains to prove the all-rule local regret bound.  The inactive-action reduction must precede the finite-prior argument.  Let \(u_0=U_k(P_0)\) for \(k\in A\).  If \(A^c\neq\varnothing\), the stated separation and finiteness of \(\mathcal K\) give
\[
g:=u_0-\max_{j\in A^c}U_j(P_0)>0;
\tag{20}
\]
if \(A^c=\varnothing\), the following reduction is vacuous.  Fix \(a_0\in A\), and define \(\rho(a)=a\) for \(a\in A\) and \(\rho(a)=a_0\) otherwise.  For a finite set \(\Theta\subset\{h:\lVert[h]\rVert_A\leq M\}\), (7) is uniform on \(\Theta\), while every inactive welfare is unchanged.  Consequently, for all sufficiently large \(C\), every active welfare exceeds every inactive welfare, simultaneously for \(h\in\Theta\).  Pointwise in the observed data and in any auxiliary randomization of a rule \(\delta_C\),
\[
\max_{k\in A}U_k(\bar P_{C,h})-U_{\delta_C}(\bar P_{C,h})
\geq
\max_{k\in A}U_k(\bar P_{C,h})-U_{\rho(\delta_C)}(\bar P_{C,h}).
\tag{21}
\]
For \(a\in A\), define the scaled active loss
\[
\ell_C(h,a)=\sqrt C\left\{\max_{k\in A}U_k(\bar P_{C,h})-U_a(\bar P_{C,h})\right\}.
\tag{22}
\]
Equation (7) implies, uniformly on the finite set \(\Theta\times A\),
\[
\ell_C(h,a)\longrightarrow
\ell(h,a):=\max_{k\in A}h_k-h_a.
\tag{23}
\]
In particular the losses in (22) are uniformly bounded on \(\Theta\times A\).  This establishes the bounded-loss premise after, rather than before, inactive outputs have been removed.

For completeness, apply the finite-experiment lower-bound argument directly.  Let \(\nu\) be any prior on the finite set \(\Theta\), and represent the active-only randomized rule \(\rho\circ\delta_C\), conditional on the data, by its probability vector \(V_C=(V_{C,a})_{a\in A}\in\Delta_A\).  Along a subsequence attaining its limit inferior Bayes risk, tightness of \(\Delta_C^B\), compactness of \(\Delta_A\), and (11) permit extraction of a joint weak limit
\[
\left(\Delta_C^B,(L_C(h))_{h\in\Theta},V_C\right)
\Rightarrow
\left(\Delta,(L(h))_{h\in\Theta},V\right),
\quad
L(h)=\exp\{h^{\prime}\Delta-h^{\prime}I_Bh/2\},
\tag{24}
\]
where \(\Delta\sim N(0,I_B)\).  By (15), (23), and boundedness of \(V_C\), likelihood-ratio-weighted risks converge along this subsequence.  Set \(X=I_B^{-1}\Delta\), so \(X\sim N(0,\Sigma_{B,A})\) under the reference law, and define the randomized Gaussian rule
\[
\kappa_a(X)=\mathbb E(V_a\mid X),\qquad a\in A.
\tag{25}
\]
Because \(L(h)\) is measurable with respect to \(X\), conditioning in (25) preserves every limiting risk.  Hence
\[
\begin{split}
&\liminf_C\sum_{h\in\Theta}\nu(h)
\mathbb E_{\bar P_{C,h}}\!\left[\ell_C\{h,\rho(\delta_C)\}\right]\\
&\quad\geq
\inf_{\kappa}\sum_{h\in\Theta}\nu(h)
\mathbb E_{X\sim N(h,\Sigma_{B,A})}[\ell\{h,\kappa(X)\}],
\end{split}
\tag{26}
\]
where the infimum is over all measurable randomized Gaussian selectors.  Equation (26) is exactly the proved finite-prior lower-experiment conclusion: no convergence of the original, possibly inactive-valued rule was assumed.  Combining (21) with the fact that maximal risk dominates every prior average proves (26) as a lower bound for the original rule's local maximal regret.

We finally pass from finite priors to the full quotient ball while retaining the unknown common location.  Let \(d=|A|-1\), \(\mathcal H_M=\{u\in\mathbb R^d:\lVert u\rVert_2\leq M\}\), and choose the linear section
\[
S_A=D_A^{\prime}(D_AD_A^{\prime})^{-1},
\qquad D_AS_A=I_d.
\tag{27}
\]
Every representative is \(S_Au+c\mathbf1_A\).  On \(\mathcal H_M\), the loss is independent of \(c\) and is bounded by \(2\lVert S_A\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}M\).  By Lemma \texttt{lem:gaussian-quotient-reduction}, invariant rules use
\[
D_AX\sim N(u,\Omega_A).
\tag{28}
\]
Because \(\Omega_A\) is positive definite, translation of its Gaussian density is continuous in \(L^1\).  Together with the displayed uniform loss bound and linearity of \(h\mapsto\ell(h,a)\), this gives a common modulus of continuity, over all randomized quotient selectors, for their risks as functions of \(u\in\mathcal H_M\).

Let \(\mathcal Q_\eta\) be a finite \(\eta\)-net of \(\mathcal H_M\).  The common modulus implies that the invariant minimax value on \(\mathcal Q_\eta\) converges to \(G_{A,M}(\tau_A^2,q_A)\) as \(\eta\downarrow0\).  On the finite grid, the closure of the attainable risk-vector set is a compact convex subset of a finite-dimensional cube: boundedness gives compactness after closure, and randomization gives convexity.  Separating this set from any strictly lower orthant shows that the finite-grid minimax value equals the supremum, over priors \(\nu\) on \(\mathcal Q_\eta\), of the invariant Bayes value.

It remains to realize that invariant Bayes value by finite priors in the full experiment.  Fix such a quotient prior \(\nu\).  For \(T>0\), average the common shift \(c\) uniformly on \([-T,T]\).  Given any full Gaussian rule \(\delta\), define
\[
\bar\delta_T(a\mid x)=\frac1{2T}\int_{-T}^T
\delta(a\mid x+c\mathbf1_A)\,dc.
\tag{29}
\]
Translation invariance of the loss and the Gaussian family gives the exact identity
\[
\sum_{u}\nu(u)R(S_Au,\bar\delta_T)
=\frac1{2T}\int_{-T}^T\sum_u\nu(u)
R(S_Au+c\mathbf1_A,\delta)\,dc.
\tag{30}
\]
For each fixed \(s\in\mathbb R\), the total variation between the action-probability vectors \(\bar\delta_T(\,\cdot\mid x+s\mathbf1_A)\) and \(\bar\delta_T(\,\cdot\mid x)\) is at most \(2|s|/T\), by cancellation of the overlapping portions of the two intervals in (29).  Taking weak-star limits of the bounded action-probability functions as \(T\to\infty\), first for rational \(s\) and then for all \(s\) by Gaussian \(L^1\)-translation continuity, produces an invariant rule.  Thus the infimum of the right side of (30) converges to the invariant Bayes value as \(T\to\infty\).  For fixed \(T\), the same Gaussian \(L^1\)-translation continuity is uniform over all rules and the finite quotient support, so a sufficiently fine finite grid of shifts has Bayes value arbitrarily close to the integral in (30).  We have therefore constructed a finite set of representatives, including a finite common-shift grid, whose finite-prior Gaussian Bayes value is arbitrarily close to the invariant Bayes value for \(\nu\).

Apply (26) to these finite priors, then let the common-shift mesh vanish, \(T\to\infty\), the quotient-net mesh \(\eta\downarrow0\), and the approximation errors vanish.  Equations (27)--(30) and the quotient reduction yield, for every measurable randomized rule sequence,
\[
\liminf_{C\to\infty}\sup_{\lVert[h]\rVert_A\leq M}
\sqrt C\,\mathbb E_{\bar P_{C,h}}
\left[\max_{k\in A}U_k(\bar P_{C,h})
-U_{\delta_C}(\bar P_{C,h})\right]
\geq G_{A,M}(\tau_A^2,q_A).
\tag{31}
\]
The inactive policies have already been handled pointwise in (21), so no unbounded inactive-action loss enters the finite-prior argument.  Lemma \texttt{lem:gaussian-quotient-reduction} gives \(G_{A,M}\uparrow G_A\) as \(M\to\infty\).

Finally, along this product-coupled path every nontarget marginal is fixed, and conditional on the retained target-hit record the deleted nontarget outcomes and nominal label information have a distribution independent of \(h\).  The reverse-kernel argument in \texttt{thm:nuisance-deletion-bernoulli} therefore applies to each finite local experiment, so a full-record rule cannot evade (31).

As an exact small-instance check, take \(n=4\), \((\pi_1,\pi_2)=(1/4,1/2)\), and the count-one slice.  Then \(M_1=4\), \(B_{11}=27/64\), \(B_{12}=1/4\), and \(q_1=(27/64)p_1+(1/4)p_2\).  Four independent tied welfare bits of mean \(1/2\) give \(\tau_1^2=1/4\), hence information \(q_1/\tau_1^2=4q_1\) and influence variance \(1/(4q_1)\).  For the assignment-heterogeneous model, let all four unit outcomes under a count-one assignment equal a Bernoulli bit whose four cell means are \((4/5,4/5,1/5,1/5)\).  This is a legal bounded within-cluster schedule, and all four cell variances are \(4/25\), hence information \(25q_1/4\) and influence variance \(4/(25q_1)\).  Thus both the tied target-bit submodel and the assignment-heterogeneous model have exactly the \(\tau_1^2/q_1\) covariance and \(q_1/\tau_1^2\) information derived above.  For two active policies, writing \(d=h_1-h_2\) and \(s^2=\tau_1^2/q_1+\tau_2^2/q_2\), the Gaussian rule choosing the larger coordinate has regret \(|d|\Phi(-|d|/s)\); this is also the symmetric two-point Gaussian testing lower bound.  Hence the two-point lower experiment matches the exact-hit pooled Gaussian regret rather than an iid nominal-label variance.
