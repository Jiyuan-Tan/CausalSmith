Put \(g_z=\Pr(Z=z)\).  By \(\texttt{lem:exact-hit-factorization}\), \(g_z=q_k/M_k>0\) for \(z\in\mathcal S_k\).  In the saturated conditional-outcome model, a regular submodel has a centered score \(s_z(Y(z))\) in cell \(z\); the known assignment law has zero score.  Differentiation of the target functional gives
\[
 \dot U_k(s)=\frac1{M_k}\sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}
 \mathbb E_{P_0}\!\left[(W(z)-\mu_z)s_z(Y(z))\right]. \tag{10}
\]
For \(\phi_k^B=q_k^{-1}\mathbf1\{Z\in\mathcal S_k\}(W(Z)-\mu_Z)\),
\[
 \mathbb E_{Q^B_{P_0,p}}(\phi_k^Bs_Z)
 =\sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}\frac{q_k/M_k}{q_k}
 \mathbb E_{P_0}\!\left[(W(z)-\mu_z)s_z(Y(z))\right]
 =\dot U_k(s). \tag{11}
\]
The coordinate is conditionally centered and bounded by \(q_k^{-1}\), hence lies in \(L_0^2\), and it belongs to the direct sum of the conditional tangent spaces.  Equations (10)--(11) make it the unique tangent-space Riesz representer, hence the canonical gradient.  Its covariance is
\[
 \mathbb E(\phi_k^B)^2
 =\frac1{q_k^2}\sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}\frac{q_k}{M_k}
 \operatorname{Var}_{P_0}\{W(z)\}=\frac{\tau_k^2}{q_k}, \tag{12}
\]
and \(\phi_j^B\phi_k^B=0\) pointwise for \(j\ne k\), because the slices are disjoint.

For a target cell \(z\), the law of large numbers gives \(N_z/C\to_Pg_z\).  On \(N_z>0\), the exact ratio identity followed by Slutsky's theorem yields
\[
 \sqrt C(\overline W_z-\mu_z)
 =\frac1{g_z\sqrt C}\sum_{c=1}^C\mathbf1\{Z_c=z\}
   \{W_c(z)-\mu_z\}+o_P(1). \tag{13}
\]
The fallback is negligible because \(\Pr(N_z=0)=(1-g_z)^C\leq e^{-Cg_z}\).  Averaging (13) over each finite slice and using \(M_kg_z=q_k\) gives jointly
\[
 \sqrt C\{\widetilde U_C-U(P_0)\}
 =C^{-1/2}\sum_{c=1}^C(\phi_1^B(O_c),\ldots,\phi_K^B(O_c))'+o_P(1). \tag{14}
\]
The summands are iid, centered, and bounded.  For fixed \(a\in\mathbb R^K\), Taylor expansion of their characteristic function gives
\[
 \left(1-\frac{a'\Sigma_B^{(K)}a}{2C}+O(C^{-3/2})\right)^C
 \longrightarrow e^{-a'\Sigma_B^{(K)}a/2}. \tag{15}
\]
The Cram\'er--Wold device proves the asserted joint normal limit.

On the regular branch define
\[
 \widehat q_k=C^{-1}\sum_c\mathbf1\{Z_c\in\mathcal S_k\},\qquad
 \widehat\phi_{c,k}=\widehat q_k^{-1}\mathbf1\{Z_c\in\mathcal S_k\}
 \{W_c(Z_c)-\overline W_{Z_c}\}.
\]
The prescribed fallback handles undefined cells.  There are finitely many cells, each count diverges in probability, and all observations lie in \([0,1]\).  Cellwise laws of large numbers and the within-cell least-squares identity imply
\[
 C^{-1}\sum_{c=1}^C\widehat\phi_c\widehat\phi_c'\to_P
 \mathbb E\{\phi^B(\phi^B)'\}=\Sigma_B^{(K)}. \tag{16}
\]
Off-diagonal empirical products vanish identically.  Restricting (14)--(16) to \(A\) proves the final assertion.
