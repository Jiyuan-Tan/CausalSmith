We give the construction, its numerical certificate, the outer optimization, and the primitive transfer separately.

\emph{Step 1: a finite certificate for the unbounded Gaussian game.}
Fix a terminal compact covariance cell and let \(d=|A|\).  Uniform positive definiteness and compactness give a computable finite bound \(\bar\sigma\) on every coordinate standard deviation \(\sqrt{v_i/r_i}\).  Selecting the largest Gaussian coordinate has, for a true best arm \(b\) and gaps \(d_i=h_b-h_i\), risk at most
\[
\sum_{i\ne b}d_i\Phi\!\left(-\frac{d_i}{\sqrt{\sigma_i^2+\sigma_b^2}}\right)
\leq \frac{(d-1)\bar\sigma}{\sqrt\pi}=:H_0. \tag{12}
\]
The second inequality is (7) of the hierarchical screening lemma and \(\sqrt{\sigma_i^2+\sigma_b^2}\leq\sqrt2\bar\sigma\).  Consequently \(G_A(v_A,r_A)\leq H_0\) throughout the cell.

Choose a rational \(0<\lambda<1\) so small that
\[
\{(1-\lambda)^{-1/2}-1\}H_0\leq\varepsilon/4, \tag{13}
\]
and then a rational \(T>0\) so large that, with \(a_\lambda=\sqrt{2/\lambda}\bar\sigma\),
\[
\frac{2d^2a_\lambda}{\sqrt{2\pi}}
\exp\!\left\{-\frac{T^2}{2a_\lambda^2}\right\}
\leq\varepsilon/4. \tag{14}
\]
Both searches terminate because the left side of (13) tends to zero with \(\lambda\downarrow0\), and the left side of (14) tends to zero with \(T\to\infty\).  The exponential and square roots are enclosed by directed rational interval arithmetic.

For every nonempty \(S\subseteq A\), choose a rational radius \(M_S\) with a directed-rounding certificate satisfying
\[
M_S\geq2\sqrt{1-\lambda}\,T\sqrt{|S|-1}. \tag{15}
\]
Apply the proved compact Gaussian certificate on the induced covariance cell with accuracy \(\eta>0\), chosen so that
\[
(1-\lambda)^{-1/2}\eta\leq\varepsilon/4. \tag{16}
\]
Let \(L_S\leq G_{S,M_S}\leq U_S\) be its outward bounds, with \(U_S-L_S\leq\eta\), and put
\[
L^{\infty}_{\varepsilon,A}=\max_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq A}L_S,
\qquad
U^{\infty}_{\varepsilon,A}=(1-\lambda)^{-1/2}
\max_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq A}U_S
+\frac{2d^2a_\lambda}{\sqrt{2\pi}}
\exp\!\left\{-\frac{T^2}{2a_\lambda^2}\right\}. \tag{17}
\]
The hierarchical screening lemma gives the middle two inequalities in the theorem and also gives \(L^{\infty}_{\varepsilon,A}\leq G_A\).  Moreover, if \(S_\star\) maximizes \(U_S\), then
\[
\max_SU_S=U_{S_\star}\leq L_{S_\star}+\eta
\leq\max_SL_S+\eta. \tag{18}
\]
Because \(\max_SL_S\leq G_A\leq H_0\), equations (13)--(18) imply
\[
U^{\infty}_{\varepsilon,A}-L^{\infty}_{\varepsilon,A}
\leq\{(1-\lambda)^{-1/2}-1\}H_0
+(1-\lambda)^{-1/2}\eta
+\frac{2d^2a_\lambda}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-T^2/(2a_\lambda^2)}
\leq3\varepsilon/4<\varepsilon. \tag{19}
\]
Thus the calculation terminates.  Its receipt appends \(\lambda,T\), every subset and radius in (15), every compact-certificate checksum, the Gaussian split, the tail enclosure, and the maximizations in (17) to the receipt fields required by the frozen compact-certificate definition.  There are only \(2^d-1\) subsets and only finitely many faces of \(\mathcal K\), so the same construction is simultaneous over all partial-tie faces.  Notice that no bound on the full radial norm of \(h\) was used: if some coordinates diverge downward, (6) in the screening lemma moves the decision to a retained subset.

\emph{Step 2: certified optimization over the two design simplices.}
For Bernoulli labels, the exact-hit factorization and the interior policies give
\[
(Bp)_k\geq\underline q_k:=\min_{\ell\in\mathcal K}B_{k\ell}>0
\quad\text{for every }p\in\Delta_K\text{ and }k\in A. \tag{20}
\]
Thus \((B\Delta_K)_A\) is a compact positive-rate polytope.  Partition the constraints
\[
p_\ell\geq0,\qquad \sum_{\ell=1}^Kp_\ell=1 \tag{21}
\]
into rational simplex cells.  Linear interval evaluation of \(q=Bp\) supplies coordinate bounds \(q^-\leq q\leq q^+\) on each cell.  Gaussian information monotonicity makes a certified value at \(q^+\) a lower bound for that design cell, while evaluation at any feasible rational \(p\) supplies an upper incumbent.  If a cell has relative rate width at most \(\xi<1\), the declared information-order modulus bounds its unresolved multiplicative variation by \((1-\xi)^{-1/2}-(1+\xi)^{-1/2}\).  By (20), this modulus tends uniformly to zero under mesh refinement.  Branches whose lower bound exceeds the incumbent are discarded; refinement terminates when the incumbent minus the smallest surviving lower bound is at most \(\varepsilon\).  The surviving cells contain every optimizer, and the incumbent is the asserted feasible \(p\).

For exact counts the variables and constraints are
\[
a_i\geq0\quad(i\in A),\qquad \sum_{i\in A}a_i=1. \tag{22}
\]
At \(a_i=0\), the established boundary theorem gives \(G_A(v_A,a)=+\infty\).  More quantitatively, the subface inequality from the screening lemma and the proved two-policy value give, for every distinct \(i,j\in A\) with positive rates,
\[
G_A(v_A,a)
\geq G_{\{i,j\}}(v_{\{i,j\}},a_{\{i,j\}})
=\kappa\sqrt{\frac{v_i}{a_i}+\frac{v_j}{a_j}}
\geq\kappa\sqrt{\frac{v_i}{a_i}}. \tag{23}
\]
Let \(\underline v>0\) be the cellwise lower bound on the \(v_i\), and obtain a finite certified upper bound \(\overline U\) at the uniform allocation.  The rational number
\[
\eta_0<\min\left\{\frac1{2d},
\frac{\kappa^2\underline v}{4(\overline U+1)^2}\right\} \tag{24}
\]
is computable.  If any \(a_i<\eta_0\), (23) is larger than \(2(\overline U+1)>\overline U\); hence no minimizer lies there.  The optimizer is therefore contained in the nonempty compact polytope defined by (22) and \(a_i\geq\eta_0\).  The same positive-cell branch-and-bound used after (20), now on this polytope, terminates with the claimed exact-count risk interval, optimizer cells, and feasible allocation.  Equations (23)--(24), rather than a positive-rate evaluation at zero, are the required boundary certificate.

\emph{Step 3: transfer to the calibrated primitive experiments.}
Take an independent covariance pilot with size \(m_C\to\infty\) and \(m_C/C\to0\).  Split the remaining clusters into a screening part with asymptotic fraction \(\lambda\) and a decision part with fraction \(1-\lambda\).  Under complete randomization, make this split separately within every active stratum; the active-interior restriction guarantees that all three stratum counts diverge.  Schedule independence and the respective randomization assumptions make the pilot, screen, and decision outcome arrays independent conditional on their prespecified counts.

Under Bernoulli labels, an assignment \(z\in\mathcal S_k\) has probability
\[
\rho_z=\frac{q_k}{M_k}geq
\frac{\min_\ell B_{k\ell}}{M_k}>0. \tag{25}
\]
Under exact-count assignment with \(a_k\geq\eta_0\), its unconditional asymptotic frequency is at least \(\eta_0/M_k\).  Since \(n\) and \(K\) are fixed, (25) gives a common positive lower bound for every cell used by a retained face.

For completeness, the uniform stochastic step can be derived directly.  If the screening subsample size is \(N\), elementary exponential Markov bounds for a binomial cell count and for averages of independent variables in \([0,1]\) give constants \(c_1,c_2>0\), depending only on the lower bound in (25), \(n\), and \(\lambda\), such that
\[
\Pr\!\left\{\sqrt C\,|\widetilde U^{\rm s}_{C,k}-U_k|>x\right\}
\leq c_1e^{-c_2x^2}+c_1e^{-c_2C},
\qquad 0\leq x\leq\sqrt C. \tag{26}
\]
Indeed, first restrict to the event that every relevant cell count is at least half its expectation; its complement has the second bound in (26).  Conditional on the assignments, convexity of the exponential on \([0,1]\), followed by optimization of the exponential Markov parameter, bounds each cell-mean deviation by \(2e^{-2N_zx^2/C}\).  A union bound over the fixed \(M_k\) cells proves (26).  This derivation also covers the prespecified empty-cell fallback.

Let \(D_{C,i}=\sqrt C\{\max_jU_j-U_i\}\).  Repeating the pairwise decomposition in (8)--(9) with (26) shows that the expected scaled loss due to deleting a best arm or retaining a set of true scaled range exceeding \(2T\) is bounded by a deterministic \(b(T)+o(1)\), uniformly in the schedule law and the design cell, where
\[
b(T)\longrightarrow0\qquad(T\to\infty). \tag{27}
\]
For example, when \(D>2T\), use \(D\leq2(D-T)\) and
\(\sup_{u\geq T}u e^{-c_2u^2}\to0\); the empty-cell contribution is at most \(\sqrt C\,c_1e^{-c_2C}=o(1)\).  In particular, for fixed \(T\), every coordinate with \(D_{C,i}\to\infty\) contributes \(o(1)\) to scaled regret.  This proves (27) without assuming bounded local contrasts.

On the complementary event, the retained scaled welfare vector lies in the subset ball (15).  The decision sample is independent of the screen.  The calibrated ratio expansion is, uniformly over the positive cell probabilities,
\[
\sqrt C\{\widetilde U_{C,k}-U_k\}
=\frac1{\sqrt C}\sum_{c=1}^C
\frac{\mathbf1\{Z_c\in\mathcal S_k\}}{q_k}
\{W_c(Z_c)-\mu_{Z_c}\}+o_P(1) \tag{28}
\]
under Bernoulli labels, with \(q_k\) replaced by the active exact-count share under complete randomization.  To verify uniformity in (28), expand each cell ratio about its deterministic cell probability; (25) bounds every reciprocal, while the cell-count and bounded-outcome second moments make the sum of the finitely many remainders \(o_P(1)\) uniformly.

The summands in (28) are centered and uniformly bounded.  Their covariance is diagonal, because distinct target slices cannot be hit by one cluster, and its \(k\)-th diagonal entry is \(\tau_k^2/q_k\), or \(\tau_k^2/a_k\) under exact counts.  For every fixed vector \(u\), Taylor expansion with a uniform third-order remainder gives
\[
\mathbb E\exp\!\left\{\frac{i}{\sqrt C}u^{\prime}\phi_c\right\}
=1-\frac1{2C}u^{\prime}\Sigma u+O(C^{-3/2}\lVert u\rVert_2^3). \tag{29}
\]
Multiplying (29) over clusters, or over the finitely many fixed-count strata, proves the Gaussian limit uniformly on a compact positive-definite covariance cell.  The same bounded-moment argument gives uniform consistency of the pilot second moments.  Snapping the pilot estimate outward to the rational covariance partition therefore selects the correct cell, or one of its recorded neighboring cells, with probability tending to one.

Conditional on any good retained subset, (28)--(29) and the already proved compact oracle-transfer propositions apply to the corresponding represented compact selector.  There are finitely many subsets and faces, so the largest of their uniform \(o(1)\) terms is still \(o(1)\).  To verify that the same Gaussian upper value, rather than an enlarged primitive tail bound, is obtained uniformly, argue by contradiction.  From any purported violating triangular sequence, compactness of the covariance cell and of \([0,\infty]^d\) gives a subsequence on which every \(D_{C,i}\) converges in the extended half-line and the covariance converges.  Coordinates with infinite limiting gap have \(o(1)\) contribution by (26)--(27).  The remaining coordinates have bounded gaps, so their joint pilot, screen, and decision law converges uniformly by (28)--(29); uniform positive definiteness gives zero Gaussian probability to every finite partition boundary.  Their limiting risk is therefore the risk of exactly the corresponding Gaussian screened selector, which is bounded by (17).  This contradicts the alleged violation.  Hence the primitive limsup is bounded by \(U^{\infty}_{\varepsilon,A}\) uniformly.  Policies with fixed negative welfare gaps have \(D_{C,i}\to\infty\) and are removed by (27); policies with \(C^{-1/2}\)-scale gaps enter the finite-gap Gaussian retained game.  This proves uniformity through partial-tie changes.

For Bernoulli designs, (20) makes all constants uniform over \(p\in\Delta_K\).  For exact-count designs, the transfer is uniform on the certified active-interior polytope from (24).  If an action-relevant share tends to zero, the information-order boundary theorem and (23) give the extended value \(+\infty\), and no positive-rate primitive transfer is claimed there.  For an explicit diagonalization, let \(C_j\geq j^2\) be the least integer exceeding \(C_{j-1}\) at which the uniform pilot, screening, decision, and covariance-snapping errors for tolerance \(1/j\) are all at most \(1/j\), and use the \(1/j\) certificate for \(C_j\leq C<C_{j+1}\).  Existence of every \(C_j\) follows from (26)--(29) and the compact oracle transfers.  Choosing, for example, a pilot size between \(j\) and \(C/j\) at stage \(j\) makes the pilot size diverge and its fraction vanish, while the fixed stagewise screening fraction leaves its count divergent.  This diagonal schedule has certified risk converging to the noncompact Gaussian value; combining it with the terminating outer branch-and-bound proves the final design statement.
