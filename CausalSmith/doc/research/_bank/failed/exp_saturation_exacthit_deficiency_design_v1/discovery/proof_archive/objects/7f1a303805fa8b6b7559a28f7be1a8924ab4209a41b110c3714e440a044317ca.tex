Put \(d=|A|-1\), \(g=D_Ah\), and
\[
J_A=D_A^{\prime}(D_AD_A^{\prime})^{-1}.
\]
The inverse exists because \(D_A\) has full row rank by \(\texttt{def:gaussian-face-game}\).  The canonical representative is \(h^\circ=J_Ag\), and the quotient loss for action \(a\) is
\[
\ell_a(g)=\max_{k\in A}(J_Ag)_k-(J_Ag)_a. \tag{37}
\]
It is independent of the representative.  On \(K_M=\{g:\lVert g\rVert_2\leq M\}\),
\[
0\leq\ell_a(g)\leq L_M:=2\lVert J_A\rVert_{2\to\infty}M,
\quad
|\ell_a(g)-\ell_a(g')|
\leq2\lVert J_A\rVert_{2\to\infty}\lVert g-g'\rVert_2. \tag{38}
\]
By \(\texttt{lem:gaussian-quotient-reduction}\), it suffices to certify \(Y\sim N(g,\Omega)\) on \(K_M\).

Choose a rational parameter grid \(g_1,\ldots,g_S\subset K_M\) and rational prior weights \(w_s\geq0\), \(\sum_sw_s=1\).  If \(\varphi_\Omega\) is the centered Gaussian density, define
\[
c_a(y)=\sum_{s=1}^Sw_s\ell_a(g_s)\varphi_\Omega(y-g_s). \tag{39}
\]
Tonelli's theorem applies because the integrand is nonnegative and bounded.  The Bayes risk is therefore exactly
\[
V(w,\Omega)=\int_{\mathbb R^d}\min_{a\in A}c_a(y)\,dy, \tag{40}
\]
because randomized action probabilities minimize the integrand pointwise.  Thus every outward-downward enclosure of (40) is a valid minimax lower bound.  On a rational observation box, directed interval evaluation of (39), subdivision of cells where the minimizing action is ambiguous, and omission of the nonnegative complement give such an enclosure.

For an upper bound, store a rational action distribution on every observation cell and use a fixed action outside the box.  If \(\overline\lambda\) bounds \(\lambda_{\max}(\Omega)\) on the covariance cell and the box contains \([-T,T]^d\), where \(T>M\), then a coordinate union bound gives, uniformly over \(g\in K_M\),
\[
\Pr_{g,\Omega}\{Y\notin[-T,T]^d\}
\leq2d\Phi\!\left(-\frac{T-M}{\sqrt{\overline\lambda}}\right). \tag{41}
\]
Multiplication by \(L_M\) bounds the omitted loss.  Interval Gaussian integration encloses every selected-action probability on retained cells.  If one posterior action is certified minimizing, record it; otherwise retain all possible minimizers and add the interval width of their posterior costs.  The finitely many functions in (39) are uniformly continuous on the box.  Hence the ambiguous-cell error tends to zero with mesh size, including on exact-tie regions where the true cost difference is zero.

We next give explicit perturbation moduli.  For probability laws \(P,Q\), apply the log-sum inequality to \(\{dP/dQ\geq1\}\) and its complement.  The binary divergence has second derivative at least four along the line between its arguments, so
\[
\lVert P-Q\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}^2\leq\frac12\operatorname{KL}(P,Q). \tag{42}
\]
For equal-covariance Gaussian laws and \(\lambda_{\min}(\Omega)\geq\underline\lambda>0\),
\[
\operatorname{KL}\{N(g,\Omega),N(g',\Omega)\}
=\frac12(g-g')^{\prime}\Omega^{-1}(g-g')
\leq\frac{\lVert g-g'\rVert_2^2}{2\underline\lambda}. \tag{43}
\]
Equations (38), (42), and (43) imply, for every selector,
\[
|R(g,\delta)-R(g',\delta)|
\leq2\lVert J_A\rVert_{2\to\infty}\lVert g-g'\rVert_2
+\frac{L_M}{2\sqrt{\underline\lambda}}\lVert g-g'\rVert_2. \tag{44}
\]
For two positive-definite covariance matrices,
\[
\operatorname{KL}\{N(g,\Omega),N(g,\Omega')\}
=\frac12\left\{\operatorname{tr}(\Omega'^{-1}\Omega)-d
+\log\frac{\det\Omega'}{\det\Omega}\right\}. \tag{45}
\]
Inverse, determinant, trace, and logarithm in (45) admit directed interval enclosures on a rational cell with eigenvalues bounded below.  Combining that enclosure with (42) and \(L_M\) gives a covariance-risk modulus.  Thus (44)--(45) validate every point between parameter and covariance grid points.

It remains to prove termination rather than assume it.  The compact parameter set and (44) reduce the continuous parameter game to a finite parameter grid with arbitrary prescribed error.  Truncate the observation space using (41), and partition its box finely.  Uniform continuity of the Gaussian densities on the product of the compact parameter, observation, and covariance cells makes the induced finite experiment uniformly close in total variation.  The resulting game has finitely many parameter cells, observation cells, and actions.  Its attainable risk vectors form a compact convex polytope.  A separating hyperplane between that polytope and a strictly lower orthant supplies nonnegative weights summing to one; pointwise minimization then gives (40).  This finite-dimensional argument proves equality between its minimax value and maximum finite-prior Bayes value.  Rational weights and rational randomized selectors are dense in the corresponding finite simplices.  Hence exhaustive enumeration eventually finds a rational prior lower bound and a represented selector upper bound within any prescribed positive gap.  Equations (41), (44), and (45) show that sufficiently large truncation and sufficiently fine rational partitions preserve the gap uniformly over the covariance subcell.
