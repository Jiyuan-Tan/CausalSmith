Put \(X_z=W(z)-\mu_z\) and \(\sigma_z^2=\operatorname{Var}_{P_0}\{W(z)\}\), and write \(\mathbb E_0\) for expectation under \(Q^{CR}_{P_0,C_C}\).  Boundedness of the potential outcomes gives \(|X_z|\leq1\).  For \(z\in\mathcal S_k\), differentiation of the finite moment-generating function gives
\[
 \Lambda_{z,k}(0)=\Lambda_{z,k}'(0)=0,
 \qquad \Lambda_{z,k}''(0)=\sigma_z^2.                    \tag{1}
\]
Because there are finitely many assignments and \(|X_z|\leq1\), the first three derivatives of every \(\Lambda_{z,k}\) are uniformly bounded on a fixed neighborhood of zero.  Taylor's formula applied to (1) therefore supplies a finite constant \(K_0\) such that, on that neighborhood,
\[
 \left|\Lambda_{z,k}(a)-\frac12a^2\sigma_z^2\right|
 \leq K_0|a|^3,
 \qquad
 \left|\Lambda_{z,k}'(a)-a\sigma_z^2\right|
 \leq K_0a^2,                                                   \tag{2}
\]
and, after enlarging \(K_0\),
\[
 \Lambda_{z,k}(2a)-2\Lambda_{z,k}(a)\leq K_0a^2.          \tag{3}
\]
These bounds are derived from bounded support, rather than imposed as regularity conditions.  Assumption \(\texttt{ass:nondegenerate-active-slices}\) gives \(\tau_k^2\geq\underline\tau^2>0\) on \(A\), so every division by \(\tau_k^2\) below is valid and the constants in (2)--(3) remain uniform over active coordinates.

We first identify the observed likelihood.  Conditional on a realized assignment array, cluster \(c\) reveals only the marginal outcome vector at \(Z_c\).  The marginal at that assignment is \(P_{0,Z_c}\) under both \(P_0\) and \(\bar P_0=\bigotimes_zP_{0,z}\).  Assumptions \(\texttt{ass:iid-schedules}\), \(\texttt{ass:cr-label-schedule-independence}\), and \(\texttt{ass:cr-exact-slices}\) then imply, after integrating over the same known randomization kernel,
\[
 Q^{CR}_{\bar P_0,C_C}=Q^{CR}_{P_0,C_C}.                  \tag{4}
\]
Fix \(h\in\mathbb R^A\) and set \(t_C=C^{-1/2}\) and
\(a_{C,k}=t_Ch_k/\tau_k^2\).  From \(\texttt{def:least-favourable-path}\), the conditional likelihood-ratio contribution of cluster \(c\), when \(L_c=k\in A\), is
\[
 \exp\!\left\{a_{C,k}X_{Z_c}
              -\Lambda_{Z_c,k}(a_{C,k})\right\};               \tag{5}
\]
clusters with labels outside \(A\) contribute one.  The comparison in (5) is against the right side of (4), so multiplying (5) over clusters is the likelihood ratio displayed in the theorem.

Condition on the complete label vector.  Assumptions \(\texttt{ass:iid-schedules}\) and \(\texttt{ass:cr-label-schedule-independence}\) make the cluster observations independent, and \(\texttt{ass:cr-exact-slices}\) makes \(Z_c\) uniform on \(\mathcal S_k\) in stratum \(k\).  Consequently
\[
 \mathbb E_0[\dot\ell_{c,k}^{CR}\mid L]
 =\mathbf1\{L_c=k\}\frac1{M_k\tau_k^2}
   \sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}\mathbb E_{P_{0,z}}X_z=0,            \tag{6}
\]
so the score is centered in its stratum.  Distinct score coordinates have zero product, while
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \frac1C\sum_{c=1}^C
 \mathbb E_0[(\dot\ell_{c,k}^{CR})^2\mid L]
 &=\frac{C_{C,k}}C\frac1{M_k\tau_k^4}
   \sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}\sigma_z^2\\
 &=\frac{C_{C,k}}C\frac1{\tau_k^2}
 \longrightarrow\frac{\alpha_k}{\tau_k^2},
 \end{aligned}                                                   \tag{7}
\]
where the second equality is the definition of \(\tau_k^2\), and the limit is \(\texttt{ass:cr-shares}\).  Thus the limiting covariance in (7) is
\[
 I_{CR}=\operatorname{diag}(\alpha_k/\tau_k^2:k\in A).           \tag{8}
\]
It is positive definite because every active \(\alpha_k\) is positive and every active \(\tau_k^2\) is at least \(\underline\tau^2\).

Here is a direct triangular-array central-limit calculation.  For fixed \(u\in\mathbb R^A\), put
\(\xi_{C,c}=C^{-1/2}u^{\prime}\dot\ell_{c,A}^{CR}\).  Boundedness and nondegeneracy give \(\max_c|\xi_{C,c}|\leq K_uC^{-1/2}\).  From (6), expansion of the conditional characteristic functions gives
\[
 \mathbb E_0(e^{i\xi_{C,c}}\mid L)
 =1-\frac12\mathbb E_0(\xi_{C,c}^2\mid L)+r_{C,c},
 \qquad
 \sum_{c=1}^C|r_{C,c}|leq
 \frac16\sum_{c=1}^C\mathbb E_0|\xi_{C,c}|^3=O(C^{-1/2}).       \tag{9}
\]
Equations (7)--(9), followed by multiplication of the independent conditional characteristic functions, yield
\[
 \mathbb E_0\!\left[e^{iu^{\prime}\Delta_C^{CR}}\mid L\right]
 \longrightarrow \exp\{-u^{\prime}I_{CR}u/2\}.                 \tag{10}
\]
The conditional expression depends on the label vector only through its fixed counts, so (10) also holds unconditionally.  Since it holds for every \(u\), it proves
\(\Delta_C^{CR}\Rightarrow N(0,I_{CR})\).

We next expand the log likelihood.  By (2) and (5), its linear term is exactly \(h^{\prime}\Delta_C^{CR}\), and its quadratic term is
\[
 -\frac1{2C}\sum_{c:L_c=k\in A}
       \frac{h_k^2\sigma_{Z_c}^2}{\tau_k^4}.                    \tag{11}
\]
Conditional on the labels, the negative of twice (11) has expectation
\[
 \sum_{k\in A}\frac{C_{C,k}}C
       \frac{h_k^2}{M_k\tau_k^4}
       \sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}\sigma_z^2
 =\sum_{k\in A}\frac{C_{C,k}}C\frac{h_k^2}{\tau_k^2}
 \longrightarrow h^{\prime}I_{CR}h.                            \tag{12}
\]
Its conditional variance is \(O(C^{-1})\), because the summands in (11) are independent and uniformly bounded and there are at most \(C\) of them.  Hence (12) and conditional Chebyshev give convergence in probability of (11) to \(-h^{\prime}I_{CR}h/2\).  The sum of the cubic remainders in (2) is bounded by
\[
 K_0\sum_{k\in A}C_{C,k}|a_{C,k}|^3
 \leq \frac{K_0}{\sqrt C}
       \sum_{k\in A}\frac{|h_k|^3}{\underline\tau^6}
 \longrightarrow0.                                              \tag{13}
\]
Combining (10)--(13) proves the asserted LAN expansion.  The same calculation holds jointly for every fixed finite collection of \(h\)'s, since all likelihood ratios are functions of the common central sequence and finitely many remainders tending to zero.

We record now the likelihood-ratio integrability that will be needed before invoking any local minimax result.  Let \(R_C(h)\) denote the likelihood ratio in (5), multiplied over clusters.  From (3), conditional independence, and uniform assignment within a stratum,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_0[R_C(h)^2\mid L]
 &=\prod_{k\in A}\prod_{c:L_c=k}
   \left\{\frac1{M_k}\sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}
   \exp[\Lambda_{z,k}(2a_{C,k})-2\Lambda_{z,k}(a_{C,k})]\right\}\\
 &\leq
 \exp\!\left\{K_0\sum_{k\in A}C_{C,k}a_{C,k}^2\right\}\\
 &\leq
 \exp\!\left\{K_0\sum_{k\in A}h_k^2/\underline\tau^4\right\}<\infty .
 \end{aligned}                                                   \tag{14}
\]
The displayed calculation applies once every \(a_{C,k}\) lies in the neighborhood used in (2)--(3).  The omitted finite set of earlier second moments is finite because bounded support makes every exponential moment finite.  Thus \((R_C(h))_C\) is uniformly integrable for each fixed \(h\); for a finite parameter set the bound is uniform over all of its elements.  This verifies the required likelihood-ratio premise from primitive boundedness, rather than assuming contiguity or a limit theorem.

We next prove the local limit of the calibrated estimator.  The ratio expansion established in \(\texttt{prop:efficient-exact-hit-cr-face}\) is, coordinatewise on \(A\),
\[
 T_{C,k}:=\sqrt C\{\widetilde U_{C,k}-U_k(P_0)\}
 =\frac1{\sqrt C}\sum_{c=1}^C
   \frac{C}{C_{C,k}}\mathbf1\{L_c=k\}X_{Z_c}+o_{P_0}(1).        \tag{15}
\]
The principal term in (15) is
\((C\tau_k^2/C_{C,k})\Delta_{C,k}^{CR}\).  Therefore, jointly with the likelihood ratios for every fixed finite set of local parameters,
\[
 T_C=\Sigma_{CR,A}\Delta_C^{CR}+o_{P_0}(1),
 \qquad
 (T_C,\Delta_C^{CR})\Rightarrow
 (\Sigma_{CR,A}Z,Z),\quad Z\sim N(0,I_{CR}),                    \tag{16}
\]
because \(C\tau_k^2/C_{C,k}\to\tau_k^2/\alpha_k\), and \(\Sigma_{CR,A}=I_{CR}^{-1}\).  For \(s\in\mathbb R^A\), change of measure, (14), and (16) permit passage of the bounded factor \(e^{is^{\prime}T_C}\) through the likelihood ratio:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \lim_C\mathbb E_{Q^{CR}_{\bar P_{C,h},C_C}}e^{is^{\prime}T_C}
 &=\mathbb E\exp\!\left\{is^{\prime}\Sigma_{CR,A}Z
                   +h^{\prime}Z-\frac{1}{2}h^{\prime}I_{CR}h\right\}\\
 &=\exp\!\left\{is^{\prime}h-\frac{1}{2}s^{\prime}\Sigma_{CR,A}s\right\}.
 \end{aligned}                                                   \tag{17}
\]
The last equality is the moment-generating formula for the centered normal vector \(Z\).  Equation (17) is precisely
\[
 \sqrt C\{\widetilde U_{C,A}-U_A(P_0)\}
 \Rightarrow N\{h,\Sigma_{CR,A}\}                              \tag{18}
\]
under \(\bar P_{C,h}\).

For use in the regret argument, we also derive the local target expansion.  Under the tilted marginal at \(z\in\mathcal S_k\),
\[
 \mathbb E_{P_{t,h,z}}W(z)-\mu_z
 =\Lambda_{z,k}'(t h_k/\tau_k^2).
\]
The second inequality in (2) and the definition of \(\tau_k^2\) give, for \(k\in A\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 U_k(\bar P_{t,h})-U_k(P_0)
 &=\frac1{M_k}\sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}
   \Lambda_{z,k}'(t h_k/\tau_k^2)\\
 &=t h_k+O(t^2h_k^2/\underline\tau^4).                          
 \end{aligned}                                                   \tag{19}
\]
Every inactive target slice is a nontilted marginal and therefore has exactly its baseline welfare.  In particular, (19) proves, uniformly over every fixed finite set \(\Theta\subset\mathbb R^A\) and \(a\in A\),
\[
 \ell_C(h,a):=\sqrt C\!\left\{
   \max_{k\in A}U_k(\bar P_{C,h})-U_a(\bar P_{C,h})\right\}
 \longrightarrow
 \ell(h,a):=\max_{k\in A}h_k-h_a.                              \tag{20}
\]
The convergence in (20) is uniform on \(\Theta\times A\), and the left side is uniformly bounded there after deletion of finitely many initial values of \(C\).

We now perform the inactive-action reduction before applying the finite-prior lower-bound lemma.  If \(A\ne\mathcal K\), finiteness of \(\mathcal K\) and the definition \(A=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_kU_k(P_0)\) give the strictly positive baseline gap
\[
 d_0=\min_{b\notin A}\left\{
        \max_{k\in A}U_k(P_0)-U_b(P_0)\right\}>0.               \tag{21}
\]
If \(A=\mathcal K\), set \(d_0=+\infty\).  Fix \(a_0\in A\).  Given a randomized rule \(\delta_C\), form \(\delta_C^A\) by leaving every output in \(A\) unchanged and replacing every output outside \(A\) by \(a_0\).  For a fixed finite \(\Theta\), (19) and (21) show that, for all sufficiently large \(C\), every active welfare exceeds every inactive welfare simultaneously for all \(h\in\Theta\).  Hence, pointwise in the observed data, in the auxiliary randomization of the rule, and in \(h\in\Theta\),
\[
 \max_{k\in A}U_k(\bar P_{C,h})-U_{\delta_C}(\bar P_{C,h})
 \geq
 \max_{k\in A}U_k(\bar P_{C,h})-U_{\delta_C^A}(\bar P_{C,h}).   \tag{22}
\]
Thus no unbounded scaled loss from an inactive output is passed to the finite-prior lemma.  Equations (20) and (22) give an active finite action set, uniform boundedness of its local losses, and uniform actionwise convergence to \(\ell(h,a)\).

For completeness, we next show that finite priors suffice to recover the entire compact quotient Gaussian value, including the common-location nuisance.  Write \(d=|A|\), \(\Sigma=\Sigma_{CR,A}\), \(D=D_A\), and \(\Omega=D\Sigma D^{\prime}\).  Positivity in (8) and full row rank of \(D\) imply \(\Omega\) is positive definite.  Define
\[
 w=\frac{\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf1_A}
         {\mathbf1_A^{\prime}\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf1_A}.
\]
Then \(w^{\prime}\mathbf1_A=1\) and \(D\Sigma w=0\).  The map
\(x\mapsto(Dx,w^{\prime}x)\) is invertible because \(\ker D=\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf1_A\}\).  Let \(J\) be the linear map characterized by
\[
 DJ=I_{d-1},\qquad w^{\prime}J=0.                                \tag{23}
\]
Every \(h\in\mathbb R^A\) then has the unique representation
\(h=Jg+c\mathbf1_A\), where \(g=Dh\) and \(c=w^{\prime}h\).

In the Gaussian limit \(X\sim N(h,\Sigma)\), put \(Y=DX\) and \(S=w^{\prime}X\).  Equations (23) and \(D\Sigma w=0\) give
\[
 Y\sim N(g,\Omega),\qquad
 S\sim N\!\left(c,w^{\prime}\Sigma w\right),
 \qquad Y\ \text{and}\ S\ \text{are independent}.            \tag{24}
\]
The action loss is
\[
 \ell(g,a)=\max_{k\in A}(Jg)_k-(Jg)_a,                          \tag{25}
\]
which depends on \(g\), not on \(c\).  Equations (24)--(25) display explicitly why the common-shift coordinate is a statistical nuisance rather than a fixed known parameter.

Let \(\mathbb B_M=\{g\in\mathbb R^{d-1}:\lVert g\rVert_2\leq M\}\).  For a randomized quotient selector \(\kappa\), write \(R(g,\kappa)\) for its Gaussian risk.  These risks are equicontinuous in \(g\), uniformly over \(\kappa\).  Indeed, if \(f_g\) is the density of \(N(g,\Omega)\), the fundamental theorem of calculus gives
\[
 \int|f_g(y)-f_{g'}(y)|\,dy
 \leq \lVert g-g'\rVert_2
       \mathbb E\lVert\Omega^{-1}Z_\Omega\rVert_2,
 \qquad Z_\Omega\sim N(0,\Omega).                              \tag{26}
\]
The loss in (25) is Lipschitz in \(g\) and bounded on \(\mathbb B_M\).  Combining these facts with (26) produces a modulus \(\omega_M(r)\downarrow0\) such that
\[
 |R(g,\kappa)-R(g',\kappa)|
 \leq\omega_M(\lVert g-g'\rVert_2)                              \tag{27}
\]
for every selector \(\kappa\).

Choose a finite \(r\)-net \(\Gamma_r=\{g_1,\ldots,g_N\}\) of \(\mathbb B_M\), and let
\[
 V_r=\inf_\kappa\max_{1\leq i\leq N}R(g_i,\kappa).
\]
By (27) and the quotient characterization in \(\texttt{lem:gaussian-quotient-reduction}\),
\[
 G_{A,M}(\tau_A^2,\alpha_A)-\omega_M(r)
 \leq V_r\leq G_{A,M}(\tau_A^2,\alpha_A).                       \tag{28}
\]
We give the finite minimax step rather than assuming a least-favorable prior.  Let \(\mathscr R\subset\mathbb R^N\) be the set of risk vectors
\((R(g_i,\kappa))_{i=1}^N\), and let
\(\mathscr C=\overline{\mathscr R+\mathbb R_+^N}\).  The set \(\mathscr C\) is closed and convex because selectors may be randomized.  If \(t<V_r\), then \(t\mathbf1_N\notin\mathscr C\); otherwise a sequence in \(\mathscr R\) would have coordinatewise maximum tending to at most \(t\), contradicting the definition of \(V_r\).  Choose a point \(x^\star\in\mathscr C\) minimizing Euclidean distance to \(t\mathbf1_N\).  Such a point exists: intersect \(\mathscr C\) with a closed ball centered at \(t\mathbf1_N\) and large enough to contain one fixed point of \(\mathscr C\), and minimize the continuous distance on that compact intersection.  Put \(\lambda=x^\star-t\mathbf1_N\ne0\).  For every \(x\in\mathscr C\), convexity puts \(x^\star+s(x-x^\star)\) in \(\mathscr C\) for \(0\leq s\leq1\).  The right derivative at zero of its squared distance from \(t\mathbf1_N\) is nonnegative, and hence \(\lambda^{\prime}(x-x^\star)\geq0\).  It follows that
\[
 \lambda^{\prime}(t\mathbf1_N)
 =\lambda^{\prime}x^\star-\lVert\lambda\rVert_2^2
 <\lambda^{\prime}x^\star
 \leq\inf_{x\in\mathscr C}\lambda^{\prime}x.                  \tag{29}
\]
Upward closure of \(\mathscr C\) forces every coordinate of \(\lambda\) to be nonnegative.  Normalizing gives \(\lambda\in\Delta_N\), and (29) becomes
\[
 \inf_\kappa\sum_{i=1}^N\lambda_iR(g_i,\kappa)>t.              \tag{30}
\]
Thus, by taking \(t\uparrow V_r\), finite priors on finite quotient grids have Bayes values arbitrarily close to \(V_r\).

To retain rather than fix the common location, choose any nonempty finite symmetric grid \(\mathcal C_R\subset\mathbb R\) and form
\[
 \Theta_{r,R}=\{Jg_i+c\mathbf1_A:
                  i=1,\ldots,N,\ c\in\mathcal C_R\},           \tag{31}
\]
with product prior \(\lambda_i/|\mathcal C_R|\).  Every point in (31) satisfies \(\lVert[h]\rVert_A\leq M\).  Under this prior, (24) factorizes the likelihood: \(Y\) contains information only about \(g_i\), while \(S\) contains information only about the independently drawn common shift \(c\).  More explicitly, if \(f_i\) is the density of \(N(g_i,\Omega)\) and \(g_c\) is the density of \(N(c,w^{\prime}\Sigma w)\), then
\[
 \Pr\{i\mid Y=y,S=s\}
 =\frac{\lambda_i f_i(y)\sum_{c\in\mathcal C_R}|\mathcal C_R|^{-1}g_c(s)}
        {\sum_j\lambda_j f_j(y)\sum_{c\in\mathcal C_R}|\mathcal C_R|^{-1}g_c(s)}
 =\frac{\lambda_i f_i(y)}{\sum_j\lambda_j f_j(y)}.
\]
Since (25) is independent of \(c\), conditioning on \(S\) therefore cannot reduce the Bayes risk for choosing an action in \(A\).  Consequently the optimal Bayes risk in the full Gaussian experiment for (31) is exactly the left side of (30).  The grids \(\mathcal C_R\) may be arbitrarily large, so this construction does not treat the common shift as known.

We can now invoke \(\texttt{lem:lan-finite-prior-lower-bound}\), and all of its premises have already been verified.  Specifically, take the finite parameter set (31), the finite action set \(A\), the reference statistic \(I_{CR}^{-1}\Delta_C^{CR}\), and the likelihood-ratio vector \((R_C(h))_{h\in\Theta_{r,R}}\).  Equations (10)--(13) give their joint convergence to the likelihood ratios of \(N(h,\Sigma)\) relative to \(N(0,\Sigma)\); (14) gives coordinatewise uniform integrability; and (20) gives uniformly bounded, uniformly convergent active losses.  Equation (22) was established before this invocation and pointwise dominates the active-only rule's risk.  Therefore, for every original rule sequence \(\delta_C\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\liminf_{C\to\infty}\sup_{\lVert[h]\rVert_A\leq M}
 \sqrt C\,\mathbb E_{Q^{CR}_{\bar P_{C,h},C_C}}
 \left[\max_{k\in A}U_k(\bar P_{C,h})
       -U_{\delta_C}(\bar P_{C,h})\right]\\
 &\hspace{15mm}\geq
 \inf_\kappa\sum_{i=1}^N\lambda_iR(g_i,\kappa)>t.
 \end{aligned}                                                   \tag{32}
\]
Choose \(r\) so that \(\omega_M(r)<\varepsilon/2\), and then choose
\(t>V_r-\varepsilon/2\).  Equations (28) and (32) give the theorem's lower bound minus \(\varepsilon\).  Since \(\varepsilon>0\) is arbitrary, the displayed local minimax bound follows.  The monotone limit
\[
 G_{A,M}(\tau_A^2,\alpha_A)\uparrow
 G_A(\tau_A^2,\alpha_A)
\]
is the final assertion of \(\texttt{lem:gaussian-quotient-reduction}\).  The likelihood calculation above was for the full record.  Because the exact treated counts are distinct, \(L_c\) is recoverable from \(Z_c\); \(\texttt{thm:nuisance-deletion-cr}\) supplies the corresponding all-rule target-only equivalence after deleting the randomized label vector, so no nontarget or label information has been silently credited to the lower experiment.

Finally, construct the target-bit witness by drawing independent bits \(B_z\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\) for active target assignments, setting \(Y_j(z)=B_z\) for every unit \(j\), and setting all remaining schedule coordinates to arbitrary fixed values below \(1/2\).  Then on every active slice \(W(z)=B_z\), so \(\mu_z=1/2\), \(\sigma_z^2=1/4\), and
\[
 \tau_k^2=\frac1{M_k}\sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}\frac14=\frac14.
\]
Substitution into (8) gives information \(\alpha_k/(1/4)=4\alpha_k\).  This is a legal bounded schedule law satisfying the fixed-cluster interference setup; independence of the bits is only an explicit witness and was not used to restrict the general path proved above.
