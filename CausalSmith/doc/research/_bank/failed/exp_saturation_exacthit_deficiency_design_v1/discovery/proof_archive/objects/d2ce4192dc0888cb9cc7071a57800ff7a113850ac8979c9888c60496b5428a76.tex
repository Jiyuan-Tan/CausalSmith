Put \(u_\star=U_k(P_0)\) for \(k\in A\), and, for \(z\in\mathcal S_k\), put
\[
 \sigma_z^2=\operatorname{Var}_{P_{0,z}}\{W(z)\}.
\]
Assumption \(\texttt{ass:nondegenerate-active-slices}\) gives \(\tau_k^2\geq\underline\tau^2>0\) for \(k\in A\), so every division by \(\tau_k^2\) below is valid.  Since every \(\pi_\ell\) is interior and \(1\leq m_k\leq n-1\), every entry \(B_{k\ell}\) is positive.  Because \(p\) belongs to the simplex, \(q_k=\sum_\ell B_{k\ell}p_\ell>0\).  Thus all target assignment cells used below also have positive probability.

\emph{Observed-law equality and target derivative.}
Only the outcome vector belonging to the realized assignment is observed from a cluster.  The laws \(P_0\) and \(\bar P_0=\bigotimes_zP_{0,z}\) have the same marginal \(P_{0,z}\) at every realized \(z\).  The randomization is independent of the schedule by \(\texttt{ass:bernoulli-label-schedule-independence}\), with label and assignment laws given by \(\texttt{ass:bernoulli-label-iid}\) and \(\texttt{ass:bernoulli-units}\).  Hence their images under the kernel in \(\texttt{def:observed-law-kernels}\) agree:
\[
 Q^B_{\bar P_0,p}=Q^B_{P_0,p}.                                      \tag{1}
\]
This argument also uses \(\texttt{ass:isolated-clusters}\): the observed reward in cluster \(c\) is the within-cluster potential outcome indexed by \(Z_c\), with no assignment from another cluster entering it.

For fixed \(z\in\mathcal S_k\), the exponential tilt in \(\texttt{def:least-favourable-path}\) gives
\[
 \mathbb E_{P_{t,h,z}}W(z)
 =\mu_z+\Lambda_{z,k}'(t h_k/\tau_k^2).
\]
The random variable \(W(z)-\mu_z\) lies in \([-1,1]\).  Differentiation under this bounded integral is therefore valid, and \(\Lambda_{z,k}''(0)=\sigma_z^2\).  Consequently
\[
 \left.\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb E_{P_{t,h,z}}W(z)\right|_{t=0}
 =\frac{h_k\sigma_z^2}{\tau_k^2}.                                  \tag{2}
\]
Averaging (2) over the finite slice and using the definition of \(\tau_k^2\) yields
\[
 \left.\frac{d}{dt}U_k(\bar P_{t,h})\right|_{t=0}
 =\frac{h_k}{M_k\tau_k^2}\sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}\sigma_z^2
 =h_k.                                                               \tag{3}
\]
All nontarget marginals are fixed by \(\texttt{def:least-favourable-path}\).  Bounded third derivatives of the finitely many log moment-generating functions also give, uniformly for \(h\) in any fixed compact set,
\[
 U_k(\bar P_{C,h})=u_\star+C^{-1/2}h_k+O(C^{-1}),\quad k\in A.      \tag{4}
\]

\emph{Primitive LAN calculation.}
For \(|a|\) in a fixed neighborhood of zero, Taylor's theorem and boundedness of \(W(z)-\mu_z\) give, uniformly over the finitely many active cells,
\[
 \Lambda_{z,k}(a)=\frac12a^2\sigma_z^2+R_{z,k}(a),
 \qquad |R_{z,k}(a)|\leq K_0|a|^3                                  \tag{5}
\]
for a finite constant \(K_0\).  The one-cluster score in direction \(h\) is \(h^{\prime}\dot\ell_A^B\).  By \(\texttt{lem:exact-hit-factorization}\), \(Z\) has probability \(q_k/M_k\) at each \(z\in\mathcal S_k\).  Therefore every coordinate is centered and bounded, hence belongs to \(L^2_0(Q^B_{P_0,p})\), and disjointness of the slices gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_0(\dot\ell_j^B\dot\ell_k^B)
 &=\mathbf 1\{j=k\}\frac{q_k}{M_k\tau_k^4}
   \sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}\sigma_z^2  \\
 &=\mathbf 1\{j=k\}\frac{q_k}{\tau_k^2}.
 \end{aligned}                                                       \tag{6}
\]
Thus the score covariance is \(I_B\), including in directions having a nonzero common-shift component.

For \(t=C^{-1/2}\), the exact log likelihood ratio is
\[
 \sum_{c=1}^C\sum_{k\in A}\sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}
 \mathbf 1\{Z_c=z\}
 \left[\frac{h_k\{W_c(z)-\mu_z\}}{\sqrt C\,\tau_k^2}
 -\Lambda_{z,k}\!\left(\frac{h_k}{\sqrt C\,\tau_k^2}\right)\right]. \tag{7}
\]
The linear part of (7) is \(h^{\prime}\Delta_C^B\).  The bounded iid cell indicators have empirical frequencies converging to \(q_k/M_k\).  Substitution of (5), the definition of \(\tau_k^2\), and finiteness of the active cells show that the quadratic part of (7) is
\[
 -\frac12\sum_{k\in A}h_k^2\frac{q_k}{M_k\tau_k^4}
       \sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}\sigma_z^2+o_{P_0}(1)
 =-\frac12h^{\prime}I_Bh+o_{P_0}(1).                               \tag{8}
\]
The sum of the cubic remainders in (5) is \(O(C^{-1/2})\), uniformly on every compact set of representatives.  Finally, for every fixed \(u\in\mathbb R^A\), boundedness and (6) give
\[
 \mathbb E_0\exp\{iu^{\prime}\dot\ell_A^B/\sqrt C\}
 =1-\frac{u^{\prime}I_Bu}{2C}+O(C^{-3/2}).                         \tag{9}
\]
Raising (9) to the \(C\)-th power and applying the same calculation to every linear combination proves
\[
 \Delta_C^B\Rightarrow N(0,I_B).                                  \tag{10}
\]
Equations (7)--(10) establish the displayed LAN expansion from the primitive iid randomization, rather than taking asymptotic normality as an assumption.

We record now the likelihood-ratio integrability needed later.  Let \(L_{C,h}\) denote (7) exponentiated.  Conditional on an active target cell, its one-cluster squared density ratio has expectation
\[
 \exp\!\left\{\Lambda_{z,k}(2a)-2\Lambda_{z,k}(a)\right\},
 \qquad a=\frac{h_k}{\sqrt C\,\tau_k^2}.
\]
Equation (5) makes this \(1+O(C^{-1})\), uniformly for \(h\) in a fixed finite set, while a nontarget cell contributes one.  Independence from \(\texttt{ass:iid-schedules}\) and \(\texttt{ass:bernoulli-label-iid}\) therefore gives
\[
 \sup_C\max_{h\in\Theta}\mathbb E_0L_{C,h}^2<\infty              \tag{11}
\]
for every fixed finite \(\Theta\subset\mathbb R^A\).  In particular the finite likelihood-ratio vectors are uniformly integrable.  Equation (11) also implies contiguity directly: for events \(E_C\) with \(P_0(E_C)\to0\), Cauchy--Schwarz gives \(P_{C,h}(E_C)\leq\{\mathbb E_0L_{C,h}^2P_0(E_C)\}^{1/2}\to0\).

\emph{Local law of the calibrated estimator.}
Write \(\rho_z=q_k/M_k\) for \(z\in\mathcal S_k\).  By the factorization lemma, \(\rho_z>0\), and
\[
 \Pr_0(N_z=0)=(1-\rho_z)^C\leq e^{-C\rho_z}.                       \tag{12}
\]
There are finitely many target cells.  The iid law of large numbers and (12), followed by the cellwise ratio identity, give
\[
 \sqrt C\{\overline W_z-\mu_z\}
 =\rho_z^{-1}C^{-1/2}\sum_{c=1}^C
   \mathbf 1\{Z_c=z\}\{W_c(z)-\mu_z\}+o_{P_0}(1).                \tag{13}
\]
The prespecified empty-cell value affects (13) only on the exponentially unlikely event in (12).  Averaging (13) over a slice yields the joint asymptotic linear representation
\[
 \sqrt C\{\widetilde U_{C,A}-U_A(P_0)\}
 =C^{-1/2}\sum_{c=1}^C\phi_A^B(O_c)+o_{P_0}(1).                   \tag{14}
\]
Direct calculation, again using disjoint slices, gives
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}_0(\phi_j^B,\phi_k^B)
 =\mathbf 1\{j=k\}\frac{\tau_k^2}{q_k},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Cov}_0(\phi_j^B,\dot\ell_k^B)=\mathbf 1\{j=k\}. \tag{15}
\]
The same characteristic-function expansion as in (9), now applied jointly to \((\phi_A^B,\dot\ell_A^B)\), proves a centered joint Gaussian limit with covariance blocks \(\Sigma_{B,A}\), \(I_B\), and the identity cross-block in (15).  Weighting its characteristic function by \(L_{C,h}\), using (11) to pass to the limit, exponentially tilts this joint normal law.  The first block consequently acquires mean \(h\) and retains covariance \(\Sigma_{B,A}\).  Contiguity transfers the remainder in (14), proving
\[
 \sqrt C\{\widetilde U_{C,A}-U_A(P_0)\}
 \Rightarrow N\{h,\Sigma_{B,A}\}                                 \tag{16}
\]
under \(\bar P_{C,h}\).  This is also consistent with the full-vector result in \(\texttt{thm:efficient-exact-hit-bernoulli}\), but (12)--(16) explicitly verify the local mean shift used here.

\emph{Inactive-action reduction before the finite-prior argument.}
Let
\[
 \gamma=u_\star-\max_{j\in A^c}U_j(P_0)>0,                         \tag{17}
\]
with the maximum over the empty set omitted.  Fix any finite \(\Theta\subset\{h:\lVert[h]\rVert_A\leq M\}\) and a point \(a_0\in A\).  Given an arbitrary randomized rule \(\delta_C\), define \(\delta_C^A\) by retaining every output in \(A\) and replacing every output in \(A^c\) by \(a_0\).  The inactive marginals are fixed along the path, while (4) is uniform on finite \(\Theta\).  Hence (17) implies that, for all sufficiently large \(C\), every active welfare exceeds every inactive welfare simultaneously for all \(h\in\Theta\).  Pointwise in the data and in \(h\in\Theta\),
\[
 \max_{k\in A}U_k(\bar P_{C,h})-U_{\delta_C}(\bar P_{C,h})
 \geq
 \max_{k\in A}U_k(\bar P_{C,h})-U_{\delta_C^A}(\bar P_{C,h}).     \tag{18}
\]
Thus no limit theorem has yet been invoked when inactive actions are removed.

For \(a\in A\), (4) gives, uniformly on the finite set \(\Theta\),
\[
 \ell_C(h,a):=\sqrt C\left\{\max_{k\in A}U_k(\bar P_{C,h})
                       -U_a(\bar P_{C,h})\right\}
 =\max_{k\in A}h_k-h_a+O(C^{-1/2}).                               \tag{19}
\]
The right side is finite on \(\Theta\), so the active-only losses \(\ell_C\) are uniformly bounded and converge actionwise.  Equations (7)--(11) give joint convergence of the finite likelihood-ratio vector to
\[
 L_h(\Delta)=\exp\{h^{\prime}\Delta-  frac12h^{\prime}I_Bh\},
 \qquad \Delta\sim N(0,I_B),                                     \tag{20}
\]
together with the likelihood-ratio uniform integrability required by \(\texttt{lem:lan-finite-prior-lower-bound}\).  Applying that proved lemma to \(\delta_C^A\), the finite action set \(A\), and any prior \(\nu\) on \(\Theta\), and using (18), shows that the limit inferior of the original maximal scaled risk is at least the optimal \(\nu\)-Bayes risk in the Gaussian experiment
\[
 X\mid h\sim N(h,\Sigma_{B,A}),
 \qquad \ell(h,a)=\max_{k\in A}h_k-h_a.                           \tag{21}
\]
The equivalence of (20) and (21) follows from \(X=I_B^{-1}\Delta\) and \(I_B^{-1}=\Sigma_{B,A}\).

\emph{Finite priors exhaust the compact quotient game.}
We give the promised computable reduction rather than assume a minimax interchange.  Put \(D=D_A\), \(\Sigma=\Sigma_{B,A}\), \(\Omega=D\Sigma D^{\prime}\), and
\[
 J=D^{\prime}(DD^{\prime})^{-1},\qquad DJ=I_{|A|-1}.               \tag{22}
\]
Represent a quotient point by \(Jg\), where \(g\) belongs to the compact ball \(\mathbb B_M=\{g:\lVert g\rVert_2\leq M\}\), and define
\[
 \ell(g,a)=\max_{k\in A}(Jg)_k-(Jg)_a.
\]
This loss is bounded and Lipschitz on \(\mathbb B_M\).  In the quotient experiment \(Y\mid g\sim N(g,\Omega)\), the risks of all randomized selectors have a common modulus of continuity: the loss difference is uniformly Lipschitz, and the \(L^1\) distance between the two Gaussian translate densities tends to zero with \(\lVert g-g'\rVert_2\), independently of the selector.  The latter assertion follows directly by splitting the density integral into a compact set, where translation is uniformly continuous, and a Gaussian tail of arbitrarily small integral.

Let \(\Gamma_\eta\) be a finite \(\eta\)-net of \(\mathbb B_M\), and let \(V_\eta\) be the minimax value with the supremum restricted to \(\Gamma_\eta\).  The common modulus \(\omega(\eta)\downarrow0\) gives
\[
 V_\eta\leq G_{A,M}(\tau_A^2,q_A)
 \leq V_\eta+\omega(\eta).                                      \tag{23}
\]
For completeness, the finite-grid minimax value is a finite-prior Bayes value.  If \(\Gamma_\eta=\{g_1,\ldots,g_s\}\), take the closure \(\mathscr R\subset\mathbb R^s\) of the convex set of attainable risk vectors.  It is compact after restriction to the bounded loss cube.  The set \(\mathscr R\) is disjoint from the open lower orthant \(\{x:x_i<V_\eta\ \text{for all }i\}\).  A separating vector must have nonnegative coordinates because the lower orthant is unbounded downward; normalize those coordinates to a probability vector \(\nu_\eta\).  Letting points of the open orthant increase to \(V_\eta\mathbf 1\) gives
\[
 \inf_\delta\sum_{i=1}^s\nu_{\eta,i}R_\delta(g_i)\geq V_\eta.
\]
The reverse inequality follows because every Bayes risk is bounded by maximal risk.  Thus equality holds.

It remains to realize this quotient prior as a finite prior in the full-location experiment, without treating common welfare as known.  Define
\[
 b=\frac{\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf 1_A}{\mathbf 1_A^{\prime}\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf 1_A}.
\]
Then \(b^{\prime}\mathbf 1_A=1\) and \(D\Sigma b=0\).  For any finite common-shift grid \(\mathcal C_T\) and \(c\in\mathcal C_T\), lift \(g_i\) to
\[
 h_{i,c}=Jg_i+\{c-b^{\prime}Jg_i\}\mathbf 1_A.                    \tag{24}
\]
Equation (24) gives \(Dh_{i,c}=g_i\), \(b^{\prime}h_{i,c}=c\), and \(\lVert[h_{i,c}]\rVert_A\leq M\).  The invertible transform \(X\mapsto(DX,b^{\prime}X)\) has zero Gaussian cross-covariance, because \(D\Sigma b=0\).  Under (24) its two coordinates are therefore independent with laws
\[
 DX\sim N(g_i,\Omega),
 \qquad b^{\prime}X\sim N\!\left(c,(\mathbf 1_A^{\prime}\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf 1_A)^{-1}\right). \tag{25}
\]
Tensor \(\nu_\eta\) with the uniform prior on \(\mathcal C_T\).  The second observation in (25) depends only on the independent grid coordinate \(c\), whereas the loss depends only on \(g_i\).  It supplies no information about \(g_i\), so the full-experiment Bayes risk of this finite prior is exactly the quotient Bayes risk \(V_\eta\).  This holds for every finite shift grid; expanding the grid leaves the equality unchanged and explicitly retains the unrestricted common-location nuisance.

Apply the lower bound obtained from (18)--(21) to this lifted finite prior and then let \(\eta\downarrow0\) in (23).  We obtain
\[
 \liminf_{C\to\infty}\sup_{\lVert[h]\rVert_A\leq M}
 \sqrt C\,\mathbb E_{\bar P_{C,h}}
 \left[\max_{k\in A}U_k(\bar P_{C,h})-U_{\delta_C}(\bar P_{C,h})\right]
 \geq G_{A,M}(\tau_A^2,q_A).                                     \tag{26}
\]
The equality between the all-selector and quotient formulations used in (23), and the monotone convergence \(G_{A,M}\uparrow G_A\), are exactly \(\texttt{lem:gaussian-quotient-reduction}\).

The calculation above already allows the complete record: on the product-coupled path, every nontarget outcome marginal and the conditional label distribution given a realized target assignment are independent of \(h\), by \(\texttt{def:least-favourable-path}\) and \(\texttt{lem:exact-hit-factorization}\).  Hence discarded variables can be simulated conditionally from an \(h\)-free kernel, so any full-record rule induces a randomized hit-record rule with the same risk on this submodel.  This is the pointwise construction underlying \(\texttt{thm:nuisance-deletion-bernoulli}\), and proves the last sentence of the theorem.

\emph{Exact four-node stress test.}
For \(n=4\), \((\pi_1,\pi_2)=(1/4,1/2)\), and \((m_1,m_2)=(1,2)\), direct enumeration gives
\[
 B=\begin{pmatrix}27/64&1/4\\[2pt]27/128&3/8\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad q=Bp.                                                       \tag{27}
\]
If every target-cell welfare is an independent \(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\) bit under the product schedule law, then \(\tau_k^2=1/4\), so (6) gives information \(q_k/\tau_k^2=4q_k\) and calibrated variance \(\tau_k^2/q_k=1/(4q_k)\).  On the count-one slice, the legal assignment-specific Bernoulli means \((4/5,4/5,1/5,1/5)\) instead give
\[
 U_1=1/2,\qquad \tau_1^2=4/25,\qquad
 I_{B,11}=25q_1/4,\qquad (\Sigma_{B,A})_{11}=4/(25q_1).             \tag{28}
\]
Raw pooling about \(U_1\) would have conditional second moment
\[
 \frac14\sum_{z\in\mathcal S_1}
 \left[\operatorname{Var}\{W(z)\}+(\mu_z-U_1)^2\right]
 =\frac4{25}+\frac9{100}=\frac14,
\]
so it loses precisely the assignment-mean component that calibration removes.  Finally, in a two-policy Gaussian face with contrast variance
\(s^2=\tau_1^2/q_1+\tau_2^2/q_2\), the equal prior on contrast means \(d\) and \(-d\) has Bayes regret \(d\Phi(-d/s)\); the sign selector attains the same risk.  Maximizing over \(d\geq0\) gives \(s\max_{x\geq0}x\Phi(-x)\).  Thus the two-point Gaussian lower bound and the pooled local-regret calculation use exactly the information and variance in (27)--(28), so neither the independent-bit nor the assignment-heterogeneous witness refutes the kernel.
