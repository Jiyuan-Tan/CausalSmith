For two arms, \(D_A=(1,-1)\).  By \(\texttt{lem:gaussian-quotient-reduction}\), the experiment reduces to
\[
 Y=X_1-X_2\sim N(d,s^2),\qquad d=h_1-h_2,\qquad
 s^2=\frac{v_1}{r_1}+\frac{v_2}{r_2}. \tag{33}
\]
Choosing arm one iff \(Y\geq0\) has regret \(|d|\Phi(-|d|/s)\), whose supremum is \(\kappa s\).  Conversely, put equal prior mass on the quotient parameters \(d=\pm sx_\star\), where \(x_\star\) maximizes \(x\Phi(-x)\).  Their likelihood-ratio rule is the sign of \(Y\), and their Bayes regret is \(sx_\star\Phi(-x_\star)=\kappa s\).  Maximum risk dominates Bayes risk, proving the exact value and rule.

For exact counts, squared risk is minimized by
\[
 f(\alpha)=\frac{v_1}{\alpha}+\frac{v_2}{1-\alpha},\qquad
 f'(\alpha)=-\frac{v_1}{\alpha^2}+\frac{v_2}{(1-\alpha)^2},\qquad
 f''(\alpha)>0.
\]
The unique stationary point, and hence the global minimizer because \(f\) diverges at both endpoints, is
\(\alpha_1^\star=\sqrt{v_1}/(\sqrt{v_1}+\sqrt{v_2})\).

For Bernoulli labels put \(a=B_{11}\), \(b=B_{12}\), \(c=B_{21}\), \(d_0=B_{22}\), \(A_0=a-b>0\), and \(D_0=d_0-c>0\).  Then
\[
F_v'(t)=-\frac{v_1A_0}{(b+A_0t)^2}+\frac{v_2D_0}{(d_0-D_0t)^2},\qquad
F_v''(t)=\frac{2v_1A_0^2}{(b+A_0t)^3}+\frac{2v_2D_0^2}{(d_0-D_0t)^3}>0. \tag{34}
\]
The signs of \(F_v'(0)\) and \(F_v'(1)\) give exactly the two endpoint inequalities in the statement.  Otherwise the unique root of \(F_v'\) is obtained by taking the positive square root and solving the resulting linear equation, giving the displayed interior formula.  Strict convexity certifies optimality.

For \(n=4\), direct binomial arithmetic gives
\[
B_{11}=27/64,\quad B_{12}=1/4,\quad B_{21}=27/128,\quad B_{22}=3/8.
\]
Substitution with \(v_1=v_2\) gives
\[
t^\star=-80+\frac{288\sqrt{462}}{77},\quad
q_1=-\frac{27}{2}+\frac{9\sqrt{462}}{14},\quad
q_2=\frac{27}{2}-\frac{27\sqrt{462}}{44},
\]
and exact simplification yields
\[
\frac14(q_1^{-1}+q_2^{-1})=\frac{43}{54}+\frac{\sqrt{462}}{27}. \tag{35}
\]
The regret ratio is the square root of (35), and the cluster-count ratio is (35).  Squaring rational endpoints to enclose \(462\), then using monotonicity of the positive arithmetic operations, gives the three outward decimal intervals stated in the theorem.
