Write
\[
\sigma_z^2=\operatorname{Var}_{P_{0,z}}\{W(z)\},
\qquad X_z=W(z)-\mu_z.
\tag{1}
\]
Then \(|X_z|\leq1\), \(\mathbb E_{P_{0,z}}X_z=0\), and, by the definition of \(\tau_k^2\),
\[
\frac1{M_k}\sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}\sigma_z^2=\tau_k^2.
\tag{2}
\]
Assumption \(\texttt{ass:nondegenerate-active-slices}\) gives \(\tau_k^2\geq\underline\tau^2>0\) for \(k\in A\), so every division below is valid.

First we establish observational equivalence at the baseline.  Conditional on a realized pair \((L_c,Z_c)\), only the coordinate \(Y_c(Z_c)\) is observed.  Its marginal law is \(P_{0,Z_c}\) under both \(P_0\) and \(\bar P_0=\bigotimes_zP_{0,z}\).  Assumptions \(\texttt{ass:iid-schedules}\), \(\texttt{ass:cr-label-schedule-independence}\), and \(\texttt{ass:cr-exact-slices}\) make these observed coordinates independent across clusters conditional on the fixed-count label vector, with the same conditional law under the two schedule laws.  Integrating over the label randomization in \(\texttt{ass:cr-label-vector}\) therefore gives
\[
Q^{CR}_{\bar P_0,C_C}=Q^{CR}_{P_0,C_C}.
\tag{3}
\]
Assumption \(\texttt{ass:isolated-clusters}\) ensures that the observed welfare in this calculation is the within-cluster potential outcome \(W_c(Z_c)\), with no assignment from another cluster entering it.

For \(z\in\mathcal S_k\), \(k\in A\), put \(a_{t,k}=t h_k/\tau_k^2\).  Because \(|X_z|\leq1\), differentiation under the finite exponential envelope gives, uniformly over the finitely many active assignment cells and for \(|a|\) in a fixed neighborhood of zero,
\[
\Lambda_{z,k}(a)=\frac12a^2\sigma_z^2+R_{z,k}(a),
\quad |R_{z,k}(a)|\leq K_0|a|^3,
\tag{4}
\]
and
\[
\Lambda'_{z,k}(a)=a\sigma_z^2+R'_{z,k}(a),
\quad |R'_{z,k}(a)|\leq K_1a^2.
\tag{5}
\]
Indeed, the first three derivatives of a log moment-generating function are respectively the tilted mean, variance, and third centered cumulant of \(X_z\); boundedness of \(X_z\) bounds the third derivative uniformly, and Taylor's formula proves (4)--(5).  Under the tilted marginal, \(\mathbb E_{P_{t,h,z}}W(z)=\mu_z+\Lambda'_{z,k}(a_{t,k})\).  Hence (2) and (5) imply
\[
\begin{split}
U_k(\bar P_{t,h})-U_k(P_0)
&=\frac1{M_k}\sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}\Lambda'_{z,k}(a_{t,k})\\
&=\frac{t h_k}{\tau_k^2M_k}\sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}\sigma_z^2+O(t^2h_k^2)
=t h_k+O(t^2h_k^2).
\end{split}
\tag{6}
\]
The implied constant is uniform when \(h\) ranges over a fixed finite set.  Every inactive target marginal is fixed by \(\texttt{def:least-favourable-path}\), so
\[
U_j(\bar P_{t,h})=U_j(P_0),\qquad j\notin A.
\tag{7}
\]

We next derive the likelihood expansion rather than assume it.  Condition on the fixed-count label vector.  For \(t=C^{-1/2}\), the conditional log likelihood ratio following from \(\texttt{def:least-favourable-path}\) is
\[
\sum_{k\in A}\sum_{c:L_c=k}
\left[
\frac{h_k}{\sqrt C\,\tau_k^2}\{W_c(Z_c)-\mu_{Z_c}\}
-\Lambda_{Z_c,k}\!\left(\frac{h_k}{\sqrt C\,\tau_k^2}\right)
\right].
\tag{8}
\]
Its linear part is \(h^{\prime}\Delta_C^{CR}\).  By \(\texttt{ass:cr-exact-slices}\), for every \(k\in A\),
\[
\mathbb E_0\!\left[
\frac1C\sum_{c:L_c=k}\sigma_{Z_c}^2\,\middle|\,L
\right]
=\frac{C_{C,k}}C\,\tau_k^2,
\tag{9}
\]
while the conditional variance of the left side is at most \(C_{C,k}/C^2\), because \(0\leq\sigma_z^2\leq1\).  Assumption \(\texttt{ass:cr-shares}\) and conditional Chebyshev therefore yield
\[
\frac1C\sum_{c:L_c=k}\sigma_{Z_c}^2
\longrightarrow_P\alpha_k\tau_k^2.
\tag{10}
\]
The sum of the cubic remainders in (4) is bounded by
\[
\frac{K_0}{\sqrt C}\sum_{k\in A}\frac{|h_k|^3}{\tau_k^6},
\tag{11}
\]
which tends to zero by active-slice nondegeneracy.  Substituting (4), (10), and (11) into (8) gives
\[
\log\frac{dQ^{CR}_{\bar P_{C,h},C_C}}{dQ^{CR}_{P_0,C_C}}
=h^{\prime}\Delta_C^{CR}
-\frac12\sum_{k\in A}\frac{\alpha_kh_k^2}{\tau_k^2}+o_{P_0}(1)
=h^{\prime}\Delta_C^{CR}-\frac12h^{\prime}I_{CR}h+o_{P_0}(1).
\tag{12}
\]

The score coordinates are centered conditionally on \(L\), because \(Z_c\) is uniform on its label's slice and \(\mathbb E_{P_{0,z}}X_z=0\).  Their conditional covariance satisfies
\[
\frac1C\sum_{c=1}^C
\mathbb E_0\!\left[
\dot\ell_{c,j}^{CR}\dot\ell_{c,k}^{CR}\,\middle|\,L
\right]
=\mathbf1\{j=k\}\frac{C_{C,k}}C
\frac1{M_k\tau_k^4}\sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}\sigma_z^2
\longrightarrow
\mathbf1\{j=k\}\frac{\alpha_k}{\tau_k^2}.
\tag{13}
\]
For any fixed \(b\in\mathbb R^A\), the summands \(b^{\prime}\dot\ell_c^{CR}\) are conditionally independent, centered, and bounded in absolute value by \(\lVert b\rVert_1/\underline\tau^2\).  Expanding their conditional characteristic functions gives, for fixed \(u\),
\[
\mathbb E_0\!\left[
\exp\!\left\{\frac{iu}{\sqrt C}b^{\prime}\dot\ell_c^{CR}\right\}
\,\middle|\,L\right]
=1-\frac{u^2}{2C}\mathbb E_0\!\left[
(b^{\prime}\dot\ell_c^{CR})^2\,\middle|\,L\right]+O(C^{-3/2}),
\tag{14}
\]
uniformly in \(c\).  Multiplication over clusters, followed by (13), makes the product converge to \(\exp\{-u^2b^{\prime}I_{CR}b/2\}\).  Thus every linear combination converges to its stated centered Gaussian law, which proves
\[
\Delta_C^{CR}\Rightarrow N(0,I_{CR}).
\tag{15}
\]
The matrix is positive definite because \(\alpha_k>0\) and \(\tau_k^2>0\) for every \(k\in A\).

We also verify the estimator limit under the local laws.  Let \(\mu_{C,h,z}\) and \(\sigma_{C,h,z}^2\) be the welfare mean and variance in cell \(z\) under \(\bar P_{C,h}\).  Equations (4)--(5) imply \(\mu_{C,h,z}\to\mu_z\), \(\sigma_{C,h,z}^2\to\sigma_z^2\), and
\[
\sqrt C\{U_k(\bar P_{C,h})-U_k(P_0)\}=h_k+O(C^{-1/2}).
\tag{16}
\]
For \(z\in\mathcal S_k\), conditionally on \(L\), \(N_z\) is a cell count with mean \(C_{C,k}/M_k\).  Since \(C_{C,k}/C\to\alpha_k>0\),
\[
N_z=\frac{C_{C,k}}{M_k}+O_P(\sqrt C),
\qquad
\Pr(N_z=0)\leq(1-M_k^{-1})^{C_{C,k}}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{17}
\]
On the nonempty-cell event, put
\[
S_{C,z}=\sum_{c:L_c=k}\mathbf1\{Z_c=z\}
\{W_c(z)-\mu_{C,h,z}\}.
\tag{18}
\]
Boundedness gives \(S_{C,z}=O_P(\sqrt C)\).  Expanding the reciprocal in (17), and then summing over the fixed finite slice, yields the same calibrated ratio expansion as in \(\texttt{prop:efficient-exact-hit-cr-face}\), now under the contiguous local marginal array:
\[
\sqrt C\{\widetilde U_{C,k}-U_k(\bar P_{C,h})\}
=\frac{\sqrt C}{C_{C,k}}
\sum_{c:L_c=k}\{W_c(Z_c)-\mu_{C,h,Z_c}\}+o_P(1).
\tag{19}
\]
The bounded summands in (19) are conditionally independent and centered.  Their limiting coordinate variance is
\[
\frac{C}{C_{C,k}}
\frac1{M_k}\sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}\sigma_{C,h,z}^2
\longrightarrow\frac{\tau_k^2}{\alpha_k},
\tag{20}
\]
and distinct coordinates use disjoint cluster strata.  The characteristic-function calculation in (14), applied to (19), therefore proves the joint centered limit \(N(0,\Sigma_{CR,A})\).  Adding (16) gives
\[
\sqrt C\{\widetilde U_{C,A}-U_A(P_0)\}
\Rightarrow N\{h,\Sigma_{CR,A}\}.
\tag{21}
\]

We now prove the local minimax lower bound.  The reduction of inactive actions is performed before any limit-experiment argument.  Fix an arbitrary finite set \(\Theta\subset\{h:\lVert[h]\rVert_A\leq M\}\), including, when desired, a finite common-shift grid.  Choose \(a_0\in A\), and map every action outside \(A\) to \(a_0\); write \(\delta_C^A\) for the resulting active-only randomized rule.  If \(A^c\ne\varnothing\), finiteness of \(\mathcal K\) and the definition \(A=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_kU_k(P_0)\) give the strictly positive baseline separation
\[
d_0=\max_{k\in A}U_k(P_0)-\max_{j\notin A}U_j(P_0)>0.
\tag{22}
\]
Equations (6)--(7), uniformly on the finite set \(\Theta\), imply that for all sufficiently large \(C\), every active welfare exceeds every inactive welfare.  Consequently, pointwise in the data and for every \(h\in\Theta\),
\[
\max_{k\in A}U_k(\bar P_{C,h})-U_{\delta_C}(\bar P_{C,h})
\geq
\max_{k\in A}U_k(\bar P_{C,h})-U_{\delta_C^A}(\bar P_{C,h}).
\tag{23}
\]
When \(A=\mathcal K\), (23) is equality.  For every \(a\in A\), (6) gives, uniformly over \(h\in\Theta\),
\[
\ell_C(h,a):=\sqrt C\left\{
\max_{k\in A}U_k(\bar P_{C,h})-U_a(\bar P_{C,h})
\right\}
=\max_{k\in A}h_k-h_a+O(C^{-1/2}).
\tag{24}
\]
Thus the active-only losses converge actionwise to \(\ell(h,a)=\max_{k\in A}h_k-h_a\) and are uniformly bounded on the finite parameter-action set \(\Theta\times A\).  This verifies the loss premise before the finite-prior lemma is invoked.

We next verify its likelihood-ratio premise.  The argument leading to (12) holds jointly for every \(h\) in a fixed finite \(\Theta\), so (12) and (15) give joint convergence of the finite likelihood-ratio vector to
\[
L(h)=\exp\{h^{\prime}Z-  frac12h^{\prime}I_{CR}h\},
\qquad Z\sim N(0,I_{CR}),
\tag{25}
\]
the likelihood-ratio vector of the Gaussian shift \(X\sim N(h,\Sigma_{CR,A})\) relative to \(h=0\).  Uniform integrability does not follow merely by assertion.  From the same bounded Taylor argument as (4), there are constants \(u_0,K_2>0\) such that
\[
|\Lambda_{z,k}(u)|\leq K_2u^2,
\qquad |u|\leq u_0, z\in\mathcal S_k, k\in A.
\tag{26}
\]
For the likelihood ratio \(L_C(h)\) in (8), conditional independence and direct integration of each tilted factor give
\[
\begin{split}
\mathbb E_0\{L_C(h)^2\mid L\}
&=\prod_{k\in A}\prod_{c:L_c=k}
\mathbb E_{Z_c}\exp\!\left[
\Lambda_{Z_c,k}(2a_{C,k})-2\Lambda_{Z_c,k}(a_{C,k})
\right]\\
&\leq
\exp\!\left\{6K_2\sum_{k\in A}
\frac{C_{C,k}h_k^2}{C\tau_k^4}\right\}
\leq
\exp\!\left\{6K_2\underline\tau^{-4}\sum_{k\in A}h_k^2\right\}
\end{split}
\tag{27}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(C\).  The right side is finite uniformly over a fixed finite \(\Theta\).  Hence the finite likelihood-ratio vector is uniformly integrable.

Apply the same-round proved node \(\texttt{lem:lan-finite-prior-lower-bound}\) only now, after (23)--(27).  For every probability mass function \(\Pi\) on \(\Theta\), it gives
\[
\begin{split}
&\liminf_{C\to\infty}\sup_{\lVert[h]\rVert_A\leq M}
\sqrt C\,\mathbb E_h\!\left[
\max_{k\in A}U_k(\bar P_{C,h})-U_{\delta_C}(\bar P_{C,h})
\right]\\
&\hspace{2cm}\geq
\inf_{\delta^A}\sum_{h\in\Theta}\Pi(h)
\mathbb E_h^G\!\left[\max_{k\in A}h_k-h_{\delta^A(X)}\right],
\qquad X\sim N(h,\Sigma_{CR,A}),
\end{split}
\tag{28}
\]
where the infimum is over Gaussian randomized selectors taking values in \(A\).  The first inequality uses maximal risk being no smaller than prior-averaged risk and the pointwise domination (23).

For completeness, we show that finite priors in (28) recover the entire compact quotient game, including the unbounded common-location nuisance.  Put \(\Sigma=\Sigma_{CR,A}\), \(D=D_A\), \(d=|A|-1\), and \(\Omega=D\Sigma D^{\prime}\).  Positivity in (20) and full row rank of \(D\) make \(\Omega\) positive definite.  Define
\[
b=\frac{\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf1_A}{\mathbf1_A^{\prime}\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf1_A},
\qquad
J_0=D^{\prime}(DD^{\prime})^{-1},
\qquad
J_\Sigma=J_0-\mathbf1_A b^{\prime}J_0.
\tag{29}
\]
Then \(b^{\prime}\mathbf1_A=1\), \(DJ_\Sigma=I_d\), \(b^{\prime}J_\Sigma=0\), and \(b^{\prime}\Sigma D^{\prime}=0\).  Therefore, for
\[
h=J_\Sigma g+c\mathbf1_A,
\qquad \lVert g\rVert_2\leq M, c\in\mathbb R,
\tag{30}
\]
the invertible transform \(X\mapsto(DX,b^{\prime}X)\) has independent components
\[
DX\sim N(g,\Omega),
\qquad
b^{\prime}X\sim N\!\left(c,
(\mathbf1_A^{\prime}\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf1_A)^{-1}\right).
\tag{31}
\]
The loss in (28) depends on \(g\) and the action but not on \(c\).  Hence for any finite quotient prior \(\nu\) and any finite common-shift grid \(\Xi\), the product prior on \(\{J_\Sigma g+c\mathbf1_A:g\in\operatorname{supp}\nu,c\in\Xi\}\) has Gaussian Bayes risk exactly equal to the Bayes risk of \(DX\sim N(g,\Omega)\) under \(\nu\): the second factor in (31) updates only the independently drawn nuisance \(c\).  Thus every finite shift grid, including an increasing grid, preserves rather than lowers the quotient Bayes risk.

It remains only to approximate the compact quotient ball by finite priors.  For \(K_M=\{g\in\mathbb R^d:\lVert g\rVert_2\leq M\}\), set
\[
\ell_a(g)=\max_{k\in A}(J_\Sigma g)_k-(J_\Sigma g)_a.
\tag{32}
\]
This loss is bounded and Lipschitz on \(K_M\).  If \(R_\delta(g)\) is its risk under \(N(g,\Omega)\), then, uniformly over all randomized selectors,
\[
|R_\delta(g)-R_\delta(g')|
\leq
2\lVert J_\Sigma\rVert_{2\to\infty}\lVert g-g'\rVert_2
+L_M\int_{\mathbb R^d}|\varphi_\Omega(x-g)-\varphi_\Omega(x-g')|\,dx,
\tag{33}
\]
where \(L_M=2M\lVert J_\Sigma\rVert_{2\to\infty}\).  Translation continuity of the integrable Gaussian density makes the right side a modulus \(\omega(\lVert g-g'\rVert_2)\) tending to zero.

Let \(\Gamma_\eta\) be a finite \(\eta\)-net of \(K_M\), and let
\[
V_\eta=\inf_\delta\max_{g\in\Gamma_\eta}R_\delta(g).
\tag{34}
\]
Equation (33) implies
\[
V_\eta\leq G_{A,M}(\tau_A^2,\alpha_A)
\leq V_\eta+\omega(\eta).
\tag{35}
\]
For this finite parameter set, the finite minimax value equals the supremum of finite-prior Bayes values.  Here is a direct verification.  Dominate the finitely many Gaussian laws by their equally weighted mixture.  A randomized selector is an \(A\)-tuple of mixture-square-integrable functions between zero and one that sum to one.  Diagonal extraction of coefficients in a countable orthonormal basis gives a weakly convergent subsequence from every selector sequence; positivity and the unit-sum constraint are preserved by the weak limit.  Each of the finitely many risks is a continuous linear functional because every component density relative to the mixture is bounded by \(|\Gamma_\eta|\).  The attainable finite risk-vector set is therefore compact and convex.  Separating this set from a strictly lower coordinate orthant produces nonnegative separating weights summing to one, and these weights are a prior on \(\Gamma_\eta\).  This proves
\[
V_\eta=sup_{\nu\in\Delta(\Gamma_\eta)}
\inf_\delta\sum_{g\in\Gamma_\eta}\nu(g)R_\delta(g).
\tag{36}
\]
Combining (28), (31), (35), and (36), then letting \(\eta\downarrow0\), proves
\[
\liminf_{C\to\infty}\sup_{\lVert[h]\rVert_A\leq M}
\sqrt C\,\mathbb E_h\!\left[
\max_{k\in A}U_k(\bar P_{C,h})-U_{\delta_C}(\bar P_{C,h})
\right]
\geq G_{A,M}(\tau_A^2,\alpha_A).
\tag{37}
\]
The equivalence with invariant selectors and the monotone limit \(G_{A,M}\uparrow G_A\) are exactly \(\texttt{lem:gaussian-quotient-reduction}\).  Since the preceding likelihood calculation used the full observed law, (37) applies to rules observing the full experiment; \(\texttt{thm:nuisance-deletion-cr}\) separately confirms that deleting the randomized label vector after recording the exact assignment cannot improve or worsen any fixed-\(C\), bounded target-only minimax problem.

Finally, define the target-bit witness by taking, independently for every target assignment \(z\), a bit \(V_z\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\) and setting \(Y_j(z)=V_z\) for every unit \(j\); choose any fixed bounded outcomes on nontarget assignments.  Then \(W(z)=V_z\), so \(\mu_z=1/2\), \(\sigma_z^2=1/4\), and (2) gives \(\tau_k^2=1/4\).  Substitution in (13) gives the exact information coordinate \(\alpha_k/(1/4)=4\alpha_k\).  This is a legal bounded schedule law satisfying active nondegeneracy and supplies the stated witness without restricting the general exponential-tilt path.
