Only one assignment is observed from each schedule.  Consequently \(P_0\) and \(\bar P_0=\bigotimes_zP_{0,z}\) induce the same one-cluster observed law, so \(Q^B_{\bar P_0,p}=Q^B_{P_0,p}\).  The iid schedule, label, schedule-independence, and unit-randomization assumptions make the records independent and give the target-cell probabilities in \(\texttt{lem:exact-hit-factorization}\).

Fix an arbitrary \(h\in\mathbb R^A\), with no restriction on its common-shift component.  For \(z\in\mathcal S_k\), bounded-support exponential tilting gives score
\[
 h_k\{W(z)-\mu_z\}/\tau_k^2
\]
and, by differentiation under a bounded integral,
\[
 \left.\frac d{dt}\mathbb E_{P_{t,h,z}}W(z)\right|_{t=0}
 =\frac{h_k}{\tau_k^2}\operatorname{Var}_{P_0}\{W(z)\}. \tag{36}
\]
Division is valid by \(\tau_k^2\geq\underline\tau^2>0\).  Averaging (36) over \(\mathcal S_k\) gives
\[
 \left.\frac d{dt}U_k(\bar P_{t,h})\right|_{t=0}
 =\frac{h_k}{M_k\tau_k^2}\sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}
 \operatorname{Var}_{P_0}\{W(z)\}=h_k. \tag{37}
\]
The observed score is \(\dot\ell_k^B\).  It is centered and bounded, and disjointness of the slices gives
\[
 \mathbb E_0(\dot\ell_j^B\dot\ell_k^B)
 =\mathbf1\{j=k\}\frac{q_k}{M_k\tau_k^4}
 \sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}\operatorname{Var}_{P_0}\{W(z)\}
 =\mathbf1\{j=k\}\frac{q_k}{\tau_k^2}. \tag{38}
\]
Thus the score covariance is \(I_B\), including the common-shift score \(\sum_k\dot\ell_k^B\).

The early four-node check follows directly from (38).  If the four count-one welfare bits have mean \(1/2\), then \(\tau_1^2=1/4\), information is \(4q_1\), and calibrated influence variance is \(1/(4q_1)\).  For cell means \((4/5,4/5,1/5,1/5)\), every cell variance is \(4/25\), so information is \(25q_1/4\) and influence variance is \(4/(25q_1)\).  In both cases the exact-hit multiplier is \(q_1\).  The empty-cell fallback has finite-sample bias
\[
 \frac1{M_k}\sum_{z\in\mathcal S_k}(1/2-\mu_z)
 \left(1-\frac{q_k}{M_k}\right)^C,
\]
which is exponentially small because \(q_k/M_k>0\); no finite-sample unbiasedness is used.

Because \(0\leq W\leq1\), Taylor's theorem for the log moment-generating function gives, uniformly for \(a\) in a fixed neighborhood of zero,
\[
 \Lambda_{z,k}(a)=\frac{a^2}{2}\operatorname{Var}_{P_0}\{W(z)\}+O(|a|^3). \tag{39}
\]
Substitute \(a=C^{-1/2}h_k/\tau_k^2\), sum over clusters, and use the bounded law of large numbers for assignment-cell frequencies.  The linear term is \(h'\Delta_C^B\), (38)--(39) give \(-h'I_Bh/2+o_P(1)\), and the summed cubic remainder is \(O(C^{-1/2}\max_k|h_k|^3)\) on each compact set of representatives.  The summands of \(\Delta_C^B\) are iid, centered, bounded vectors with covariance \(I_B\), so their characteristic functions yield \(\Delta_C^B\Rightarrow N(0,I_B)\).  This proves the displayed LAN expansion.

Equations (37)--(39), together with the asymptotic linear expansion in \(\texttt{thm:efficient-exact-hit-bernoulli}\), give under \(\bar P_{C,h}\), uniformly on compact representative sets, the following result: multiplying the joint null characteristic function by the displayed likelihood ratio shifts its linear exponent by \(h\) and leaves its quadratic covariance exponent unchanged, so
\[
 \sqrt C\{\widetilde U_{C,A}-U_A(P_0)\}\Rightarrow
 N\{h,\Sigma_{B,A}\}. \tag{40}
\]
For the decision lower bound, take any finite collection of representatives with \(\lVert[h]\rVert_A\leq M\) and any finite prior.  The LAN likelihood ratios and (37) verify the premises of \(\texttt{lem:lan-finite-prior-lower-bound}\); bounded scores give uniform integrability.  Thus the limiting maximal scaled causal regret is at least that prior's Bayes risk in the full Gaussian shift experiment.

To retain the unknown common location, tensor any finite quotient prior with a uniform grid of shifts \(c\mathbf1_A\), \(-T\leq c\leq T\).  Gaussian total-variation continuity makes the mesh error vanish, and the shift grid's boundary-to-volume ratio vanishes as \(T\to\infty\).  The resulting averaged rule is location invariant.  On the compact quotient ball, the loss is bounded and Lipschitz and Gaussian translation is continuous in total variation, so all selector risks have a common modulus; an arbitrarily fine finite quotient grid therefore approximates the supremum risk uniformly over selectors.  On such a finite grid, the orthonormal-basis diagonalization in \(\texttt{lem:gaussian-quotient-reduction}\) makes the attainable finite risk-vector set compact and convex.  Separating it from a strictly lower orthant gives nonnegative separating coefficients summing to one, namely a finite prior, and proves that the finite-grid minimax value is the supremum of its finite-prior Bayes values.  Letting the quotient mesh vanish, then the shift mesh vanish and \(T\to\infty\), and applying the quotient reduction gives \(G_{A,M}(\tau_A^2,q_A)\).  The fixed baseline gap makes every inactive action asymptotically dominated.  The quotient lemma also gives \(G_{A,M}\uparrow G_A\), while \(\texttt{thm:nuisance-deletion-bernoulli}\) transfers the lower bound to full-record rules.
