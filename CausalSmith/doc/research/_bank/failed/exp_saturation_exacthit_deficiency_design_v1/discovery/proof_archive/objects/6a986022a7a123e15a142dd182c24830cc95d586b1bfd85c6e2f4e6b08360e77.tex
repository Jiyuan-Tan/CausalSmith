We prove the three assertions: certification of the unbounded Gaussian game, certification of the two outer design problems, and uniform transfer to the primitive experiments.

\emph{1. Certification of the unbounded Gaussian game.}
Fix the declared face \(A\), put \(d=|A|\), and first work on one compact rational cell from the statement.  Write
\[
 \Sigma=\Sigma(v_A,r_A)=\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_i^2:i\in A),
 \qquad \sigma_i^2=v_i/r_i.
\]
Positivity and compactness of this cell give computable constants \(0<\underline\sigma\leq\overline\sigma<\infty\) such that every \(\sigma_i\) lies in \([\underline\sigma,\overline\sigma]\).  In particular, all quotient covariance matrices belonging to the cell are uniformly positive definite, as required by \(\texttt{thm:gaussian-finite-certificate}\).

We first obtain a finite cellwise upper bound.  Let \(b\in\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{i\in A}h_i\), let \(d_i=h_b-h_i\geq0\), and use the selector \(\delta_0(X)\in\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{i\in A}X_i\), with deterministic tie breaking.  If \(\delta_0(X)=i\ne b\), then \(X_i-X_b\geq0\).  Since \(X_i-X_b\sim N(-d_i,\sigma_i^2+\sigma_b^2)\), the union bound gives
\[
 \mathbb E_h\{h_b-h_{\delta_0(X)}\}
 \leq \sum_{i\ne b}d_i\Phi\!\left(-\frac{d_i}{\sqrt{\sigma_i^2+\sigma_b^2}}\right).
\tag{1}
\]
For \(x\geq0\), integration by parts gives \(x\Phi(-x)\leq\varphi(x)\leq(2\pi)^{-1/2}\).  Also \(\sqrt{\sigma_i^2+\sigma_b^2}\leq\sqrt2\,\overline\sigma\).  Applying these two inequalities term by term in (1) yields
\[
 G_A(v_A,r_A)\leq H_0:=\frac{(d-1)\overline\sigma}{\sqrt\pi}.
\tag{2}
\]
This bound is uniform and computable on the covariance cell.

Choose rational \(0<\lambda<1\) sufficiently small and then rational \(T>0\) sufficiently large that, with \(a_\lambda=\sqrt{2/\lambda}\,\overline\sigma\),
\[
 \{(1-\lambda)^{-1/2}-1\}H_0<\frac{\varepsilon}{4},
 \qquad
 \frac{2d^2a_\lambda}{\sqrt{2\pi}}
       \exp\!\left\{-\frac{T^2}{2a_\lambda^2}\right\}<\frac{\varepsilon}{4}.
\tag{3}
\]
Such rational choices exist by continuity, because the first left side tends to zero as \(\lambda\downarrow0\) and, for fixed \(\lambda\), the second tends to zero as \(T\to\infty\).  Directed rational interval evaluation verifies both strict inequalities in finite time.

For each nonempty \(S\subseteq A\), choose a rational radius satisfying
\[
 M_S\geq2\sqrt{1-\lambda}\,T\sqrt{|S|-1}.
\tag{4}
\]
For a singleton set use its exact risk value zero.  For \(|S|\geq2\), apply \(\texttt{thm:gaussian-finite-certificate}\) on the induced compact positive covariance cell, with a common rational accuracy \(\eta>0\) chosen so that
\[
 (1-\lambda)^{-1/2}\eta<\frac{\varepsilon}{4}.
\tag{5}
\]
It terminates and returns \(L_S\leq G_{S,M_S}(v_S,r_S)\leq U_S\) with \(U_S-L_S\leq\eta\), together with a represented selector.  Define the unrounded endpoints
\[
 L_A^0=\max_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq A}L_S,
 \qquad
 U_A^0=(1-\lambda)^{-1/2}
       \max_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq A}U_S
       +\frac{2d^2a_\lambda}{\sqrt{2\pi}}
        \exp\!\left\{-\frac{T^2}{2a_\lambda^2}\right\}.
\tag{6}
\]
The lower inequality in \(\texttt{lem:hierarchical-gaussian-screening}\) and \(L_S\leq G_{S,M_S}\) imply
\[
 L_A^0\leq\max_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq A}G_{S,M_S}(v_S,r_S)\leq G_A(v_A,r_A).
\tag{7}
\]
The selector constructed in that lemma uses an independent screening observation with covariance \(\Sigma(v_A,r_A)/\lambda\), retains the coordinates within \(T\) of the screening maximum, and applies the corresponding compact selector to the decision observation, after multiplication by \(\sqrt{1-\lambda}\).  Its risk is at most \(U_A^0\); hence
\[
 G_A(v_A,r_A)\leq
 \sup_{h\in\mathbb R^A}\mathbb E_h
 \left[\max_{i\in A}h_i-h_{\delta(X)}\right]
 \leq U_A^0.
\tag{8}
\]
If \(S_\star\) maximizes \(U_S\), then
\[
 \max_SU_S=U_{S_\star}\leq L_{S_\star}+\eta
 \leq\max_SL_S+\eta=L_A^0+\eta.
\tag{9}
\]
Moreover, (2) and (7) give \(L_A^0\leq H_0\).  Subtracting the endpoints in (6), and using (3), (5), and (9), gives
\[
 U_A^0-L_A^0
 \leq\{(1-\lambda)^{-1/2}-1\}H_0
 +(1-\lambda)^{-1/2}\eta
 +\frac{2d^2a_\lambda}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-T^2/(2a_\lambda^2)}
 <\frac{3\varepsilon}{4}.
\tag{10}
\]
Choose the working precision so that outward rounding of both endpoints enlarges the total gap by less than \(\varepsilon/4\).  Calling the rounded endpoints \(L^\infty_{\varepsilon,A}\) and \(U^\infty_{\varepsilon,A}\), (7)--(10) prove the first displayed chain and its gap bound.  There are only \(2^d-1\) nonempty subsets.  Termination of every compact invocation and of the finite rational searches therefore proves termination of this stage.  Concatenating the compact receipts with the rational witnesses for (3)--(5), the tail enclosure, the subset list, and the outward-rounding record gives the stated machine-checkable transcript.  Because the construction ranges over all retained subsets, a configuration with only a proper subset of locally tied coordinates is sent to that subset's certified game; no radial restriction on the full vector \(h\) has been imposed.

\emph{2. Certification of the outer designs.}
Under \(\mathsf B(p)\), \(\texttt{def:hit-map}\) and \(\texttt{lem:exact-hit-factorization}\) give \(q=Bp\).  Since \(0<\pi_\ell<1\) and \(1\leq m_k\leq n-1\), every binomial mass \(B_{k\ell}\) is strictly positive.  Therefore
\[
 (Bp)_k=\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal K}B_{k\ell}p_\ell
 \geq \underline q_k:=\min_{\ell\in\mathcal K}B_{k\ell}>0
 \quad\text{for every }p\in\Delta_K\text{ and }k\in A.
\tag{11}
\]
Partition the rational simplex constraints \(p_\ell\geq0\) and \(\sum_\ell p_\ell=1\) into rational cells.  Linear interval arithmetic on a cell gives coordinatewise bounds \(q^-\leq Bp\leq q^+\).  By the monotonicity in \(\texttt{lem:gaussian-information-order}\), a certified lower bound for \(G_A(v_A,q^+)\) is a lower bound throughout the cell, while evaluation at any feasible rational point gives an upper incumbent.  If the relative width of every rate interval is at most \(\xi<1\), the same lemma gives
\[
 (1+\xi)^{-1/2}G_A(v_A,q)
 \leq G_A(v_A,q')
 \leq(1-\xi)^{-1/2}G_A(v_A,q)
\tag{12}
\]
whenever \((1-\xi)q\leq q'\leq(1+\xi)q\).  The right and left factors in (12) converge uniformly to one as \(\xi\downarrow0\), and (11) makes relative widths uniformly refinable over the whole simplex.  The value is uniformly finite by (2).  Thus rational branch-and-bound, calling the unbounded certificate of Part 1 with a summable allocation of its numerical error budget, terminates with a risk interval of prescribed width, never prunes a cell containing an exact minimizer, records all unpruned minimizer cells, and returns a feasible rational point whose value is within \(\varepsilon\) of the optimum.

For exact counts the feasible rates are
\[
 a_i\geq0\quad(i\in A),\qquad \sum_{i\in A}a_i=1.
\tag{13}
\]
By \(\texttt{lem:gaussian-information-order}\), an action-relevant zero coordinate has value \(+\infty\).  We next exclude a whole neighborhood of this boundary by a finite calculation.  For distinct \(i,j\in A\), the subface inequality in \(\texttt{lem:hierarchical-gaussian-screening}\), followed by \(\texttt{thm:two-policy-minimax}\), gives
\[
 G_A(v_A,a)\geq G_{\{i,j\}}(v_{\{i,j\}},a_{\{i,j\}})
 =\kappa\sqrt{\frac{v_i}{a_i}+\frac{v_j}{a_j}}
 \geq\kappa\sqrt{\frac{v_i}{a_i}}.
\tag{14}
\]
Let \(\underline v>0\) be a rational lower enclosure for all \(v_i\) on the input cell, and let \(\overline U<\infty\) be a certified upper bound obtained at the uniform allocation.  Choose rational
\[
 0<\eta_0<\min\left\{\frac1{2d},
 \frac{\kappa^2\underline v}{4(\overline U+1)^2}\right\}.
\tag{15}
\]
If some \(a_i<\eta_0\), then (14)--(15) give \(G_A(v_A,a)>2(\overline U+1)>\overline U\).  Hence no minimizer lies there, and every minimizer belongs to the compact rational polytope defined by (13) and \(a_i\geq\eta_0\).  On this polytope all rates are uniformly positive, so the Bernoulli branch-and-bound argument based on (12) applies verbatim.  This proves the exact-count outer certificate and also explains why no positive-rate formula is evaluated at a boundary point.

\emph{3. Uniform transfer to the primitive experiments.}
Partition the \(C\) clusters into disjoint pilot, screening, and decision pieces with prespecified sizes
\[
 m_C+N_C^{\mathrm s}+N_C^{\mathrm d}=C,
 \qquad m_C\to\infty,
 \qquad \frac{m_C}{C}\to0,
 \qquad \frac{N_C^{\mathrm s}}C\to\lambda,
 \qquad \frac{N_C^{\mathrm d}}C\to1-\lambda.
\tag{16}
\]
For exact-count randomization, make the analogous split within each active fixed-count stratum, with each of its three counts having the limits dictated by (16).  Assumptions \(\texttt{ass:iid-schedules}\), \(\texttt{ass:bernoulli-label-schedule-independence}\), and \(\texttt{ass:cr-label-schedule-independence}\) imply that the outcome arrays in the three pieces are independent, conditionally on their prespecified design counts.  The two assignment mechanisms are those in \(\texttt{ass:bernoulli-label-iid}\), \(\texttt{ass:bernoulli-units}\), \(\texttt{ass:cr-label-vector}\), and \(\texttt{ass:cr-exact-slices}\); cluster isolation in \(\texttt{ass:isolated-clusters}\) ensures that each observed outcome is the corresponding within-cluster potential outcome.

For \(z\in\mathcal S_k\), the Bernoulli assignment-cell probability is, by \(\texttt{lem:exact-hit-factorization}\),
\[
 \rho_z=\Pr(Z_c=z)=\frac{q_k}{M_k}
 \geq\frac{\min_\ell B_{k\ell}}{M_k}>0.
\tag{17}
\]
In the certified exact-count region the corresponding limiting cell frequency is at least \(\eta_0/M_k\).  Since \(n\) and \(K\) are fixed, there are finitely many target cells.  On the event that every such empirical cell frequency is at least half its lower bound, the ordinary ratio identity, applied separately to every cell, yields for \(b\in\{\mathrm s,\mathrm d\}\)
\[
 \widetilde U_{C,k}^{\,b}-U_k
 =\frac1{N_C^b}\sum_{c\in b}
   \frac{\mathbf1\{Z_c\in\mathcal S_k\}}{q_k}
   \{W_c(Z_c)-\mu_{Z_c}\}
   +o_P((N_C^b)^{-1/2}),
\tag{18}
\]
uniformly in the Bernoulli design; in the exact-count regime \(q_k\) in (18) is replaced by the active stratum share.  To verify (18), write each cell mean as the ratio of its empirical centered outcome sum and empirical frequency, replace the reciprocal empirical frequency by \(\rho_z^{-1}\), and use the uniform positive lower bound in (17).  The product of the \(O_P((N_C^b)^{-1/2})\) centered numerator and the \(O_P((N_C^b)^{-1/2})\) frequency error is \(O_P((N_C^b)^{-1})\); summing over finitely many cells preserves the remainder.  The empty-cell fallback contributes only on an event whose probability is exponentially small by the binomial lower-tail bound.

The summands in (18) are centered by randomization independence and have absolute value bounded uniformly.  Their coordinate variances are \(\tau_k^2/q_k\) under Bernoulli assignment and \(\tau_k^2/a_k\) under exact counts; distinct coordinates have zero product because the target slices are disjoint.  Here is a direct triangular-array calculation of the limit.  For any fixed vector \(u\), apply the third-order Taylor formula to the characteristic function of the \(u\)-weighted summand divided by \(\sqrt{N_C^b}\).  Centering removes its linear term, its quadratic term is minus \(u'\Sigma u/(2N_C^b)\), and uniform boundedness makes the absolute sum of all third-order remainders \(O((N_C^b)^{-1/2})\).  Multiplication of the independent characteristic functions, followed by \(\log(1+x)=x+O(x^2)\), therefore gives the limit \(\exp\{-u'\Sigma u/2\}\).  Conditional on fixed exact-count labels the same calculation is applied to the independent, nonidentically distributed stratum summands; the fixed-share limits identify the same quadratic term.  Since this calculation holds for every \(u\), it gives jointly
\[
 \sqrt C\{\widetilde U_C^{\mathrm s}-U\}
 \Rightarrow N(0,\Sigma/\lambda),
 \qquad
 \sqrt C\{\widetilde U_C^{\mathrm d}-U\}
 \Rightarrow N(0,\Sigma/(1-\lambda)),
\tag{19}
\]
with independent limits.  This is also the split-sample consequence of \(\texttt{thm:efficient-exact-hit-bernoulli}\), \(\texttt{prop:efficient-exact-hit-cr-face}\), \(\texttt{prop:oracle-transfer-bernoulli}\), and \(\texttt{prop:oracle-transfer-cr}\).  Assumption \(\texttt{ass:nondegenerate-active-slices}\) and the positive-rate bounds ensure that all limiting covariance matrices remain in the certified positive cells.

Under a \(C^{-1/2}\)-local welfare displacement \(h\), both statistics in (19) have mean \(h\).  The screening statistic therefore has exactly the limiting covariance \(\Sigma/\lambda\) required in \(\texttt{lem:hierarchical-gaussian-screening}\).  The compact decision selector receives
\[
 \sqrt{1-\lambda}\,\sqrt C
 \{\widetilde U_C^{\mathrm d}-U(P_0)\},
\tag{20}
\]
whose limiting mean is \(\sqrt{1-\lambda}h\) and whose limiting covariance is \(\Sigma\).  The active baseline \(U_A(P_0)\) is a multiple of \(\mathbf1_A\), so translation invariance removes it.  Dividing regret on the scaled parameter by \(\sqrt{1-\lambda}\) produces precisely the factor \((1-\lambda)^{-1/2}\) in (6).  This proves that the primitive normalization matches, rather than merely approximates formally, the Gaussian split construction.

The empirical second moments in the pilot are averages of bounded variables.  Let \(\rho_0>0\) be the minimum of the finitely many Bernoulli cell lower bounds in (17) and the finitely many exact-count cell lower bounds \(\eta_0/M_k\).  For completeness, for a piece of size \(N\), let \(N_z^{(N)}\) be its count in a cell having probability \(\rho_z\geq\rho_0\).  Exponential Markov gives, for a fixed sufficiently small \(s>0\),
\[
 \Pr\{N_z^{(N)}\leq N\rho_z/2\}
 \leq e^{sN\rho_z/2}\{1-\rho_z+\rho_ze^{-s}\}^{N}
 \leq e^{-cN};
\tag{21}
\]
the last inequality follows by continuity uniformly over \(\rho_z\geq\rho_0\).  Conditional on the cell count, convexity of \(e^{sx}\) on the bounded outcome interval bounds each centered summand's moment-generating function by \(e^{c's^2}\); multiplying these bounds and optimizing \(s\) gives \(2e^{-c''N_z^{(N)}x^2}\) for a cell-mean deviation of size \(x\).  Applying these calculations to (17) and its exact-count analogue, and taking a union bound over the finitely many cells, proves uniformly that the pilot covariance estimator converges to the true calibrated covariance and that outward snapping selects a certified cell, or a recorded adjacent cell containing the truth, with probability tending to one.  The same calculation applied to the screening piece gives constants \(c_1,c_2>0\), uniform on the certified cells, such that
\[
 \Pr\!\left\{\sqrt C\,|\widetilde U_{C,k}^{\mathrm s}-U_k|>x\right\}
 \leq c_1e^{-c_2x^2}+c_1e^{-c_2C},
 \qquad 0\leq x\leq\sqrt C.
\tag{22}
\]
The pairwise screening decomposition in \(\texttt{lem:hierarchical-gaussian-screening}\), with (22), supplies a deterministic remainder \(b(T)\downarrow0\) for deleting a best coordinate or retaining coordinates whose scaled range exceeds \(2T\).  In particular, every policy whose scaled welfare gap diverges contributes \(o(1)\) to scaled regret.

It remains to justify the asserted uniformity over bounded-schedule triangular arrays.  Suppose the uniform upper bound failed.  The schedule space \([0,1]^{n\times\mathcal Z_n}\) is compact metric.  By \(\texttt{lem:weak-compactness-compact-laws}\), a violating sequence of schedule laws has a weakly convergent subsequence.  Because every coordinate welfare and its square are bounded continuous functions, all assignment-specific means and variances converge along that subsequence.  Passing to a further subsequence, every nonnegative scaled policy gap converges in \([0,\infty]\).  Coordinates with infinite limiting gap have vanishing scaled contribution by (22).  For the remaining coordinates, the bounded triangular-array characteristic-function calculation proving (19), pilot consistency, and covariance snapping give joint convergence of the pilot, screen, and decision statistics to the corresponding Gaussian objects.  The represented selectors use finitely many rational cells; their boundaries are finite unions of lower-dimensional affine sets and therefore have probability zero under the nondegenerate Gaussian limit.  Bounded truncation followed by the uniform tail estimate (22) consequently transfers expected scaled regret to the Gaussian screened selector.  Its risk is bounded by (8), contradicting the assumed violation.  This subsequence argument proves uniformity simultaneously over the finitely many retained subsets and over every covariance cell in the finite certificate.

Taking \(A=\mathcal K\), (11) supplies positive information for every Bernoulli candidate and hence gives global candidate adaptation.  Under exact counts, Part 2 places every optimizer in a compact simplex interior, which is exactly the domain of the primitive exact-count transfer; at a vanishing action-relevant share, \(\texttt{lem:gaussian-information-order}\) supplies only the extended value \(+\infty\).  Fixed-scale inferior policies have diverging scaled gaps and vanish by (22), whereas \(C^{-1/2}\)-scale policies remain in the appropriate retained compact game.

Finally, at stage \(j\), run Parts 1 and 2 with tolerance \(1/j\), choose the rational split, threshold, compact radii, and meshes as above, and then choose \(C_j\) large enough that the pilot, split-limit, snapping, and screening-transfer errors are all at most \(1/j\), while every subsample size diverges and \(m_C/C\leq1/j\).  Keeping the stage-\(j\) choices for \(C_j\leq C<C_{j+1}\) gives the claimed diagonal sequence.  Equations (6)--(22) then transfer the certified noncompact Gaussian risk and both certified outer designs to the primitive calibrated experiments on exactly the domains stated in the theorem.
