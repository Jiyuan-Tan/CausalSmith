\[
\alpha_n\{3T_{k_n}^\dagger+2\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm A}\}\le 1.
\]
Put \(\delta_n=n^{-3}\).  At every such admissible input, finite-input mode of \(\mathsf E\) terminates and returns rationals \(b_n^-\le b_n^+\), an exact rational worst-case-risk certificate \(U_n\), and a total randomized estimator \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm A}\) under the independent allocation \(q^\dagger\), such that
\[
b_n^-\le B_{n,k_n,\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm A}}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}
\le b_n^+,
\qquad b_n^+-b_n^-\le\delta_n,
\qquad
b_n^-\le R_n(c^\dagger)\le
\max_y\mathbb E\!\left[
\{\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm A}-\tau_{c^\dagger}(y)\}^2

Define
\[
\begin{aligned}
e_n^-&=\max\{e_{\mathrm{loc},n},e_{\mathrm{shell},n},e_{\mathrm{zero},n}\},\\

d_n^-&=n^{4/3}(n^{-1}-U_n),

\end{aligned}

\[
\Lambda_{c^\dagger}-e_{\mathrm{pr},n}

\Lambda_{c^\dagger}+e_n^-,

\[
d_n^-\le D_n\le d_n^+,

|d_n^--\Lambda_{c^\dagger}|\le\eta,

Let \(n\) be in the admissible tail supplied by the unequal-three-arm allocation-tube theorem.  The effective normalized Airy-record lemma proves that the spline record selected at \(k_n\) is produced after finitely many rational interval operations.  The allocation-tube theorem gives
\[
\alpha_n\{3T_{k_n}^\dagger+2\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm A}\}\le1, \tag{1}
\]
so the positivity premise of the corrected finite-input evaluator holds.  Apply that evaluator with \(\delta_n=n^{-3}\).  Its conclusion gives rationals \(b_n^-\le b_n^+\), a rational feasible grid value \(U_n\), and a total estimator \(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm A}\) under \(q^\dagger\), with
\[
\begin{aligned}
b_n^-&\le B_{n,k_n,\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm A}}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}
\le b_n^+,\\
b_n^+-b_n^-&\le n^{-3},\\
b_n^-&\le R_n(c^\dagger)\le
\max_y\mathbb E\!\left[
\{\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm A}-\tau_{c^\dagger}(y)\}^2
\right]\le U_n.
\end{aligned} \tag{2}
\]
Here the lower inequality against \(R_n(c^\dagger)\) is an all-procedure statement: by the corrected permutation-orbit calculation built into the finite-input evaluator, the maximum risk of every outcome-independent design and every randomized labeled-data estimator dominates the Bayes risk \(B_{n,k_n,\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm A}}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}\), and the infimum in the risk-game definition preserves that inequality.  The upper inequality in (2) is witnessed by the displayed total estimator.  The rational-grid primal-error lemma verifies that this finite grid program is the attaining certificate used by the corrected diagonal machine and that its primal error is effective.

We next derive the upper bound on the unrestricted scaled deficit from the three allocation regions; it is not assumed.  Abbreviate
\[
\mathcal B_n(r)=
\mathcal B_{n,k_n,\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm A}}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}(r),
\qquad
G_n(r)=G_{n,k_n,\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm A}}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}(r).
\]
By the exact Airy-tube Bayes-risk definition, the finiteness of \(\mathcal R_{n,3}\), and the fact that multiplication by the positive number \(n^{4/3}\) reverses a minimum after subtraction from \(n^{-1}\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
n^{4/3}\left\{n^{-1}-
B_{n,k_n,\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm A}}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}\right\}
&=n^{4/3}\left\{n^{-1}-\min_{r\in\mathcal R_{n,3}}\mathcal B_n(r)\right\}\\
&=\max_{r\in\mathcal R_{n,3}}G_n(r). 
\end{aligned} \tag{3}
\]
The local positive-count set, the positive-count exterior set, and the zero-count set in conclusion (A) of the allocation-tube theorem are disjoint and cover \(\mathcal R_{n,3}\).  Applying its corresponding bound on each set to (3) therefore gives
\[
n^{4/3}\left\{n^{-1}-
B_{n,k_n,\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm A}}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}\right\}
\le \Lambda_{c^\dagger}+e_n^-,
\quad
e_n^-=\max\{e_{\mathrm{loc},n},e_{\mathrm{shell},n},e_{\mathrm{zero},n}\}.
\tag{4}
\]
Since (2) also gives
\(R_n(c^\dagger)\ge
B_{n,k_n,\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm A}}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}\), equations (3)--(4) imply
\[
D_n=n^{4/3}\{n^{-1}-R_n(c^\dagger)\}
\le\Lambda_{c^\dagger}+e_n^-.
\tag{5}
\]

The finite lower endpoint has only the requested algebraic-enclosure loss.  Indeed, (2) implies
\[
0\le
B_{n,k_n,\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm A}}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}-b_n^-
\le b_n^+-b_n^-\le n^{-3}. \tag{6}
\]
Using (3), (4), and (6),
\[
\begin{aligned}
d_n^+
&=n^{4/3}\left\{n^{-1}-
B_{n,k_n,\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm A}}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}\right\}
+n^{4/3}\left\{
B_{n,k_n,\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm A}}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}-b_n^-\right\}\\
&\le\Lambda_{c^\dagger}+e_n^-+n^{4/3}n^{-3}\\
&=\Lambda_{c^\dagger}+e_n^-+n^{-5/3}\\
&\le\Lambda_{c^\dagger}+e_n^-+n^{-1}.
\end{aligned} \tag{7}
\]
The inequality \(R_n(c^\dagger)\ge b_n^-\) in (2) also gives \(D_n\le d_n^+\).

For the opposite side, let \(U_{\Lambda,n}\) be the rational upper endpoint for \(\Lambda_{c^\dagger}\) in the verified diagonal record.  The evaluator-handle definition gives
\[
\Lambda_{c^\dagger}\le U_{\Lambda,n},
\qquad
\left[n^{4/3}(U_n-n^{-1})+U_{\Lambda,n}\right]_+
\le e_{\mathrm{pr},n}. \tag{8}
\]
For every real \(z\), \(z\le[z]_+\).  Hence (8) yields
\[
n^{4/3}(U_n-n^{-1})+\Lambda_{c^\dagger}
\le e_{\mathrm{pr},n},
\]
or equivalently
\[
d_n^-=n^{4/3}(n^{-1}-U_n)
\ge\Lambda_{c^\dagger}-e_{\mathrm{pr},n}. \tag{9}
\]
Because \(R_n(c^\dagger)\le U_n\) by (2), multiplication by \(-n^{4/3}<0\) and addition of \(n^{1/3}\) give \(d_n^-\le D_n\).  Combining (5), (7), and (9) proves all displayed finite-sample inequalities.

It remains to prove effectiveness across sample sizes.  Conclusion (C) of the allocation-tube theorem supplies a code and correctness certificate for a rational-valued nonincreasing computable \(E\) with \(E(N)\to0\) and
\[
\max\{e_{\mathrm{loc},n},e_{\mathrm{shell},n},
e_{\mathrm{zero},n},e_{\mathrm{pr},n}\}\le E(N)
\qquad(n\ge N). \tag{10}
\]
Thus \(F(N)=E(N)+1/N\) is rational-valued, nonincreasing, computable, and tends to zero.  Given rational \(\eta>0\), enumerate \(N=1,2,\ldots\), replaying the finite tail certificate from conclusion (C), until
\[
F(N)=E(N)+1/N\le\eta. \tag{11}
\]
The certified convergence of \(E\) makes this search terminate; call the first accepted tail index \(N^\dagger(\eta)\).  For \(n\ge N^\dagger(\eta)\), equations (5), (7), (9), and (10) give
\[
\Lambda_{c^\dagger}-E(N)\le d_n^-\le D_n
\le\Lambda_{c^\dagger}+E(N),
\qquad
D_n\le d_n^+\le\Lambda_{c^\dagger}+E(N)+1/N. \tag{12}
\]
The first chain in (12) also gives \(d_n^-\le\Lambda_{c^\dagger}+E(N)\), while the two chains together give \(d_n^+\ge\Lambda_{c^\dagger}-E(N)\).  Therefore (11)--(12) prove the three asserted absolute-error bounds and the enclosing relation \(d_n^-\le D_n\le d_n^+\).

Since \(\tau_{c^\dagger}(y)\in[-1,1]\), its squared error is at most one, while squared-error risk is nonnegative.  Hence \(0\le R_n(c^\dagger)\le1\), proving the stated safe prefix certificate.
