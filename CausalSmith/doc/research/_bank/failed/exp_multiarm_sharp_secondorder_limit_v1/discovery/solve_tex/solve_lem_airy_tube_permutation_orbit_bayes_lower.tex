Put \(p_0=p_0^\dagger\).  From the displayed atomic measures in the frozen definitions,
\[
 \sum_t\lambda_{v,t}^+=\sum_t\lambda_{v,t}^-=3,
 \qquad
 \sum_t\lambda_{d,t}^+=\sum_t\lambda_{d,t}^-=2,
 \qquad
 \sum_t p_{0,t}=1.
\tag{1}
\]
The atoms in \(\lambda_v^-\) are the coordinatewise complements of those in
\(\lambda_v^+\), and the same assertion, with the same coefficients, holds for
\(\lambda_d^-\) and \(\lambda_d^+\).  Since \(b_{\mathbf1-t}=-b_t\), the exact
primitives proposition and the definitions of the signed directions give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_t s_{x,t}^\pm&=0,&
 \sum_t s_{x,t}^\pm b_t&=v,\\
 \sum_t s_{h,t}^\pm&=0,&
 \sum_t s_{h,t}^\pm b_t&=d^\dagger,\\
 \sum_t\gamma_{0,t}&=0,&
 \sum_t\gamma_{0,t}b_t&=b_{111}-b_{000}=\mathbf1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
Here \(\sum_t p_{0,t}b_t=(b_{000}+b_{111})/2=0\) was also used.  Because
\(\theta_t=(c^\dagger)^\top b_t\), while
\[
 (c^\dagger)^\top v=1,
 \qquad (c^\dagger)^\top d^\dagger=0,
 \qquad (c^\dagger)^\top\mathbf1=0,
\tag{3}
\]
multiplication of (2) by \((c^\dagger)^\top\) proves the last column of the
claimed table.  Thus all entries of that table follow from (1)--(3).

We next verify support and positivity, including the boundary of the tube.  On any
sign cell, the perturbation can be rewritten exactly as
\[
 x s_x^{\operatorname{sign}(x)}
 +h s_h^{\operatorname{sign}(h)}+\zeta\gamma_0
 =|x|\lambda_v^{\operatorname{sign}(x)}
  +|h|\lambda_d^{\operatorname{sign}(h)}
  -(3|x|+2|h|)p_0+\zeta\gamma_0.
\tag{4}
\]
Either sign gives the same expression at a zero coordinate.  At a noncentral
type, both \(p_0\) and \(\gamma_0\) vanish, so (4) is nonnegative.  At either
central type, discarding the nonnegative measure terms in (4) yields
\[
 p_{n,x,h,\zeta,t}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}
 \ge \frac12-\alpha_n\left\{
 \frac{3|x|+2|h|}{2}+|\zeta|\right\}
 \ge \frac12\left[1-\alpha_n(3T_k^\dagger+4\varepsilon)\right]\ge0.
\tag{5}
\]
The perturbation in (4) has total mass zero by (2), so its coordinates sum to
one.  Hence (5) proves that \(p_{n,x,h,\zeta}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}\) is a
probability distribution on the entire closed support.  Taking its \(b_t\)- and
\(\theta_t\)-moments and using (2)--(3) gives
\[
 \mathbb E[b_t\mid x,h,\zeta]
 =\alpha_n(xv+hd^\dagger+\zeta\mathbf1),
 \qquad
 \mathbb E[\theta_t\mid x,h,\zeta]=\alpha_nx.
\tag{6}
\]
The exact-primitives proposition separately identifies the realized count target as
\(n^{-1}\sum_tm_t\theta_t\); thus (6) is a conditional-mean identity and does not
replace the finite-population causal target by a surrogate.

Direct multiplication of the two rational matrices in the directional-ledger
definition gives
\[
 \mathsf B^{-1}\mathsf B=I_3.
\tag{7}

For an open-simplex allocation define
\[
 \omega_a=\frac{(c_a^\dagger)^2}{q_aH(q)}.
\tag{8}
\]
All \(\omega_a\) are nonnegative and their sum is one because
\(H(q)=\sum_a(c_a^\dagger)^2/q_a\).  Moreover
\(z_a(q)=\omega_a/c_a^\dagger\).  It follows that
\[
 (c^\dagger)^\top z(q)=\sum_a\omega_a=1,
 \qquad
 \sum_aq_az_a(q)^2
 =\frac{1}{H(q)^2}\sum_a\frac{(c_a^\dagger)^2}{q_a}
 =\frac1{H(q)}.
\tag{9}
\]
The second and third rows of \(\mathsf B^{-1}\), applied to \(z(q)\), give both
the displayed formulas in the definition and the useful convex-coordinate forms
\[
 \beta(q)=-4\omega_2+\frac43\omega_3,
 \qquad
 \gamma(q)=\frac12\omega_1-\frac12\omega_2-\frac12\omega_3
 =\omega_1-\frac12.
\tag{10}
\]
Consequently \(|\beta(q)|\le4\) and \(|\gamma(q)|\le1/2\).  Substitution of
\(q^\dagger=(1/2,1/8,3/8)\) gives
\[
 H(q^\dagger)=4,
 \qquad z(q^\dagger)=v,
 \qquad \beta(q^\dagger)=\gamma(q^\dagger)=0.
\tag{11}
\]
By the definitions of \(J_x(k)\), \(w_k^\dagger\), and
\(\kappa(c^\dagger)=5/8\),
\[
 J_x(k)+\frac58\int_{\mathbb R}|x|w_k^\dagger(x)\,dx
 =\frac{4\int_{\mathbb R}|(\varphi_k^\dagger)'|^2
 +(5/8)\int_{\mathbb R}|x|(\varphi_k^\dagger)^2}
 {\int_{\mathbb R}(\varphi_k^\dagger)^2}.

The Airy-benchmark definition makes \(\Lambda_{c^\dagger}\) the infimum of the

 \mathsf B^{-1}\frac{e_1}{c_1^\dagger}=(1,0,1/2)^{\mathsf T},\qquad

Every proper face omits an arm, so (13) supplies the claimed direction on each

It remains to verify the score and information assertions.  Write
\(w=w_k^\dagger\), \(\rho=\rho_\varepsilon\), and
\(f(x,h,\zeta)=w(x)\rho(h)\rho(\zeta)\).  Define the one-dimensional scores by
\(S_x=w'/w\) and \(S_\rho=\rho'/\rho\) where the respective density is positive,
and set them to zero on its zero set.  These definitions hold almost everywhere,
which is sufficient for all integrals below.  The square of a continuous compactly
supported piecewise-linear function is absolutely continuous and vanishes at the
two exterior endpoints of its support; \(\rho\) has the same properties on
\([-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]\).  Piecewise one-dimensional integration by parts,
with cancellation at the internal breakpoints, therefore gives for every
continuously differentiable test function for which the displayed integrals exist
\[
 \int D_{\beta,\gamma}F\,f
 =-\int F S_{\beta,\gamma}f,
 \qquad
 S_{\beta,\gamma}=S_x+\beta S_\rho(h)+\gamma S_\rho(\zeta),
\tag{14}
\]
and there is no boundary term.  Each component score has mean zero.  Independence
then removes all cross terms, while direct differentiation gives
\[
 \int S_x^2w
 =\frac{4\int| (\varphi_k^\dagger)'|^2}
 {\int(\varphi_k^\dagger)^2}=J_x(k),
 \qquad
 \frac{\rho'(u)}{\rho(u)}=-\frac{4u}{\varepsilon^2-u^2},
\tag{15}
\]
and hence
\[
 \int_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}
 \left(\frac{\rho'}{\rho}\right)^2\rho
 =\frac{15}{\varepsilon^5}
 \int_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}u^2\,du
 =\frac{10}{\varepsilon^2}.
\tag{16}
\]
Combining (10), (14)--(16) proves
\[
 \int S_{\beta,\gamma}^2f
 =J_x(k)+\frac{10}{\varepsilon^2}(\beta^2+\gamma^2)
 \le J_x(k)+\frac{325}{2\varepsilon^2}
 =J_{\max}(k,\varepsilon).
\tag{17}
\]

Conditional on \((x,h,\zeta)\), the multinomial-count draw followed by uniform
labeling is equivalent to independent labeled response types with their common law
\(p_{n,x,h,\zeta}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}\): for a labeled type sequence with counts
\(m\), the multinomial probability times the reciprocal orbit size is
\[
 \left\{\frac{n!}{\prod_tm_t!}\prod_t p_t^{m_t}\right\}
 \left\{\frac{\prod_tm_t!}{n!}\right\}
 =\prod_{i=1}^np_{t_i}.
\tag{18}
\]
Thus, conditional on a fixed assignment with positive counts \(r_a=nq_a\), the
observations are independent Bernoulli variables.  By (6), the success probability
in arm \(a\) is
\[
 p_a=\frac12+\alpha_n\ell_a,
 \qquad
 \ell_a=xv_a+hd_a^\dagger+\zeta.
\tag{19}
\]
In direction \(D_{\beta(q),\gamma(q)}\), equation (7) gives
\(D\ell_a=z_a(q)\).  Differentiating the two Bernoulli log probabilities in
(19), squaring, and averaging gives the one-observation information
\(\alpha_n^2z_a(q)^2/\{1/4-\alpha_n^2\ell_a^2\}\).  Independence and addition
over the \(r_a\) observations prove exactly
\[
 I_{n,q}(x,h,\zeta)
 =\alpha_n^2\sum_{a=1}^3
 \frac{r_az_a(q)^2}{1/4-\alpha_n^2\ell_a^2}.
\tag{20}
\]
On the support,
\[
 |\ell_a|\le \frac{T_k^\dagger}{2}+\frac{3\varepsilon}{4}
 +\varepsilon\le L(k,\varepsilon).
\tag{21}
\]
If \(4\alpha_n^2L(k,\varepsilon)^2\le1/2\), the denominators in (20) are
positive, and (9), (20), and (21) imply
\[
 I_{n,q}(x,h,\zeta)
 \le\frac{4n\alpha_n^2}{1-4\alpha_n^2L(k,\varepsilon)^2}
 \sum_aq_az_a(q)^2
 =\frac{4n\alpha_n^2/H(q)}
 {1-4\alpha_n^2L(k,\varepsilon)^2}.
\tag{22}
\]

Finally, the arrays in the directional ledger, the support radius, and every spline
integral in the verified record are rational.  The maximum defining
\(C(k,\varepsilon)\) enumerates only eight response types, six arm--outcome cells,
and eight sign triples.  Hence \(C(k,\varepsilon)\),
\(J_{\max}(k,\varepsilon)\), \(L(k,\varepsilon)\),
\(\Gamma(k,\varepsilon)\), and \(\mathcal W(k,\varepsilon)\) are computable
rationals.  Every left side in the finite list defining
\(N(k,\varepsilon,\eta)\) is a finite sum of nonnegative rational constants times
negative powers of the integer argument, or is \(2^{-N}\).  It is therefore
nonincreasing and tends to zero.  Comparisons involving fractional powers are
decided by isolating the specified positive algebraic roots.  Sequential search
therefore terminates at a finite least integer, and monotonicity proves that every
accepted inequality remains valid for all \(n\ge N(k,\varepsilon,\eta)\).

%%% FIELD airy-tube-orbit-bayes-proof
Fix \(n,k,\varepsilon\) satisfying the positivity premise.  By the face-robust
tangent-score lemma, every conditional vector
\(p_{n,x,h,\zeta}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}\) is a probability law, and the realized
target associated with a response-count vector is the exact finite-population
contrast \(\tau_{c^\dagger}(m)=n^{-1}\sum_tm_t\theta_t\).  Conditional on the
three tube coordinates, a multinomial count followed by a uniform labeling in its
orbit produces independent labeled response types.  Indeed, if a labeled type
sequence has count vector \(m\), its probability is
\[
 \left\{\frac{n!}{\prod_tm_t!}\prod_t
 (p_{n,x,h,\zeta,t}^{\dagger,\mathrm A})^{m_t}\right\}
 \left\{\frac{\prod_tm_t!}{n!}\right\}
 =\prod_{i=1}^np_{n,x,h,\zeta,t_i}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}.
\tag{1}
\]
After the coordinates are integrated out, the resulting schedule prior is therefore
permutation invariant and, conditional on \(m\), uniform on the response-count
orbit.

Fix an assignment vector \(a_0\) whose allocation count is \(r\), and write
\(x\) for its arm-success count.  Conditional on \(m\), partitioning the unit
labels by assigned arm gives a response-type by arm table \(H\).  The number of
labeled type sequences having a particular feasible table is
\(\prod_a r_a!/\prod_{t,a}H_{t,a}!\), whereas the response-count orbit contains
\(n!/\prod_tm_t!\) sequences.  Summation over the feasible tables consequently
gives
\[
 \Pr_m(X^{\mathrm{obs}}=x\mid A=a_0)
 =\frac{\prod_ar_a!}{n!}
 \sum_{H\in\mathcal H(m,r,x)}
 \prod_t\frac{m_t!}{\prod_aH_{t,a}!}
 =P_m(x\mid r).
\tag{2}
\]
For completeness, a particular labeled outcome vector with count \(x\) has
probability equal to the left side of (2) divided by
\(\prod_a\binom{r_a}{x_a}\): permutations within the assigned-arm blocks act
transitively on such vectors and leave the prior invariant.  This divisor is
independent of \(m\).  It follows that, conditional on \(A=a_0\), the posterior
law of the invariant target depends on the full labeled outcomes only through
\((r,X^{\mathrm{obs}})\), and its likelihood is exactly (2).

Let \(D^{\mathrm A}_{r,x}\) and \(N^{\mathrm A}_{r,x}\) denote the predictive
mass and target-weighted predictive mass in the exact Airy-tube Bayes-risk
definition.  Whenever \(D^{\mathrm A}_{r,x}>0\), the posterior mean is
\[
 \bar\tau_{r,x}=\frac{N^{\mathrm A}_{r,x}}{D^{\mathrm A}_{r,x}}.
\tag{3}
\]
For an arbitrary action \(d\), conditional square completion gives
\[
 \mathbb E\!left[(d-\tau_{c^\dagger}(m))^2\mid r,x\right]
 =\operatorname{Var}\!\left(\tau_{c^\dagger}(m)\mid r,x\right)
 +\left(d-\bar\tau_{r,x}\right)^2.
\tag{4}
\]
If the estimator randomizes after seeing the data, averaging (4) over its Markov
kernel gives the same identity with the last square averaged, because that
randomization is conditionally independent of the schedule.  Thus the posterior
mean (3) minimizes conditional squared error.  Multiplying its posterior variance
by the predictive probabilities and summing yields
\[
 \sum_m\nu_{n,k,\varepsilon}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}(m)
       \tau_{c^\dagger}(m)^2
 -\sum_{x:D^{\mathrm A}_{r,x}>0}
       \frac{(N^{\mathrm A}_{r,x})^2}{D^{\mathrm A}_{r,x}}
 =\mathcal B_{n,k,\varepsilon}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}(r),
\tag{5}
\]
where (2) and the exact Bayes-risk definition justify the equality.  Hence every
possibly randomized labeled-data estimator has conditional Bayes risk at least
the right side of (5) for every fixed assignment vector with count \(r\).

Now draw \(A\sim\mathcal D_n\).  The outcome-independence assumption says that
\(A\) is independent of the fixed schedule before observing outcomes, so
conditioning on \(A=a_0\) does not alter the schedule prior.  Averaging (5) first
over assignment vectors and then grouping vectors by their count gives
\[
 \operatorname{BayesRisk}_{\nu_{n,k,\varepsilon}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}}
       (\mathcal D_n,\widehat\tau)
 \ge\sum_{r\in\mathcal R_{n,3}}\pi(r)
       \mathcal B_{n,k,\varepsilon}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}(r).
\tag{6}
\]
The coefficients \(\pi(r)\) are nonnegative and sum to one.  Therefore, by the
definition of the all-count certificate,
\[
 \sum_r\pi(r)\mathcal B_{n,k,\varepsilon}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}(r)
 \ge\min_{r\in\mathcal R_{n,3}}
       \mathcal B_{n,k,\varepsilon}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}(r)
 =B_{n,k,\varepsilon}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}.
\tag{7}
\]
Equations (6)--(7) hold for arbitrary dependence among the assignments and for
arbitrary randomized estimators, which proves the claim.

%%% FIELD corrected-fixed-input-evaluator-proof
Fix admissible \(n,k,\varepsilon,\delta\) and the supplied verified spline record.
The positivity inequality and the face-robust tangent-score lemma show that the
conditional response vector is a probability law at every point of the compact
three-coordinate support.  The effective normalized Airy-record lemma shows that
\(w_k^\dagger\) is a normalized compactly supported rational piecewise-quadratic
density, and the displayed formula for \(\rho_\varepsilon\) makes each transverse
density normalized and polynomial with rational coefficients on its two sign
intervals.  Thus the construction defines a probability prior.

Fix \(m\in\mathcal M_{n,3}\).  On one rational spline piece and one of the eight
coordinate-sign cells, the integrand defining its prior mass is
\[
 \frac{n!}{\prod_tm_t!}
 w_k^\dagger(x)\rho_\varepsilon(h)\rho_\varepsilon(\zeta)
 \prod_{t\in\{0,1\}^3}
 \left[p_{0,t}^\dagger+\alpha_n
 \left\{xs_{x,t}^{\operatorname{sign}(x)}
 +hs_{h,t}^{\operatorname{sign}(h)}+\zeta\gamma_{0,t}\right\}
 \right]^{m_t}.
\tag{1}
\]
Every endpoint and coefficient in (1), apart from the explicitly represented
\(\alpha_n\), is rational.  Expanding the finite product and integrating each
monomial over its rational rectangular cell therefore gives an element of the
ordered real algebraic field \(\mathbb Q(\alpha_n)\).  There are finitely many
spline pieces and sign cells, so
\[
 \nu_{n,k,\varepsilon}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}(m)
 \in\mathbb Q(\alpha_n)
 \quad\text{for every }m\in\mathcal M_{n,3}.
\tag{2}
\]
The exact-primitives proposition makes the orbit likelihood and the count target
rational.  Closure of the field under finite sums, products, and division by a
positive element then gives
\[
 \mathcal B_{n,k,\varepsilon}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}(r),\quad
 B_{n,k,\varepsilon}^{\dagger,\mathrm A},\quad
 G_{n,k,\varepsilon}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}(r)
 \in\mathbb Q(\alpha_n).
\tag{3}
\]
All enumerations terminate because
\[
 |\mathcal M_{n,3}|<\infty,
 \qquad |\mathcal R_{n,3}|<\infty,
 \qquad \prod_{a=1}^3(r_a+1)<\infty,
 \qquad |\mathcal H(m,r,x^{\mathrm{obs}})|<\infty.
\tag{4}
\]

We spell out the comparison certificate.  Factor the square-free polynomial
\(nX^3-1\) over \(\mathbb Q\), select the factor containing the unique positive
root by its rational isolating interval, and reduce field expressions modulo that
factor.  A reduced numerator is either the zero polynomial, which rational
coefficient comparison decides, or it is nonzero at \(\alpha_n\).  In the latter
case Sturm bisection refines the isolating interval until it contains no root of the
numerator, at which point the numerator has a certified constant sign.  Termination
follows because a finite set of distinct real algebraic roots has pairwise disjoint
rational isolating intervals.  The positive algebraic numbers \(n^{1/6}\) and
\(n^{1/72}\), used only to decide allocation-stratum membership, are handled by
the same root-isolation procedure.  Hence the machine decides every posterior
denominator sign, every stratum-membership test, and every comparison needed for
the finite minima and maxima in (3).  It returns an emptiness certificate for an
empty stratum and otherwise an attaining allocation together with the exact
ordered-field maximum.  Since the target lies in \([-1,1]\), its posterior-mean
risk lies in \([0,1]\); rational bisection therefore terminates with
\[
 b^-\le B_{n,k,\varepsilon}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}\le b^+,
 \qquad b^+-b^-\le\delta.
\tag{5}
\]

The face-robust orbit Bayes lemma applies under the verified positivity condition.
For every admissible design and estimator, maximum risk over schedules dominates
its average under the design-independent tube prior, and that Bayes risk is at
least \(B_{n,k,\varepsilon}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}\).  Taking the infimum over the
same design--estimator class as in the risk-game definition and then using (5)
gives
\[
 R_n(c^\dagger)
 \ge B_{n,k,\varepsilon}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}
 \ge b^-.
\tag{6}
\]

For the upper certificate, under independent assignment with rational allocation
\(q^\dagger=(1/2,1/8,3/8)\), the full labeled observation alphabet
\(\mathcal O_n\) is finite and every probability \(P_y(\omega)\) is rational.
The action grid is finite and rational as well.  The linear program in the evaluator
definition is feasible: placing mass one on any grid action and taking \(U=4\)
satisfies every risk constraint because both action and target lie in
\([-1,1]\).  The risk constraints also force \(U\ge0\), so for minimization one
may add \(U\le4\).  The resulting feasible set is a nonempty compact rational
polytope.  A linear objective has an optimum at a vertex: if an optimizer is not a
vertex, write it as a nontrivial convex combination; linearity makes at least one
component an optimizer, and iteration through successively lower-dimensional faces
ends at a vertex.  Each vertex solves a full-rank subsystem of rational equalities,
so it is rational.  Enumerating all such subsystems, retaining the feasible
solutions, and comparing their \(U\)-coordinates is therefore a terminating exact
rational algorithm.

Let \((z_{\omega j},U)\) be the returned optimizer.  The nonnegativity and
simplex equations make \(z\) a Markov kernel from every observation to the finite
action grid, hence a total randomized estimator with values in \([-1,1]\).  For
each fixed schedule \(y\), its risk is exactly
\[
 \sum_{\omega\in\mathcal O_n}\sum_{j=0}^{2G}
 P_y(\omega)z_{\omega j}
 \{a_j-\tau_{c^\dagger}(y)\}^2\le U
\tag{7}
\]
by the corresponding program constraint.  This independent design and estimator
are feasible choices in the unrestricted risk game, and hence
\[
 R_n(c^\dagger)\le U.
\tag{8}
\]
Equations (2)--(8), together with the exact stratum enumeration above, prove every
finite-input output in the statement.  The argument is pointwise in
\((n,k,\varepsilon,\delta)\) and therefore makes no cross-sample convergence
claim.
