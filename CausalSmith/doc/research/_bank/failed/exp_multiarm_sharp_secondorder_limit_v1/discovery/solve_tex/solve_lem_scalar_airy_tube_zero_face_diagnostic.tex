Fix integers \(n,k\geq 1\) and a rational number \(\varepsilon>0\) such that
\[
\alpha_n(3T_k^\dagger+2\varepsilon)\leq 1.
\tag{1}
\]
We first identify exactly the conditional labeled experiment generated by the legacy prior.  From the definitions
\(s_x^+=\lambda_v^+-3p_0^\dagger\) and
\(s_x^-=3p_0^\dagger-\lambda_v^-\), for every real \(x\),
\[
x s_x^{\operatorname{sign}(x)}
=|x|\lambda_v^{\operatorname{sign}(x)}-3|x|p_0^\dagger;
\tag{2}
\]
the choice of sign at \(x=0\) is immaterial.  The analogous identity following from
\(s_h^+=\lambda_d^+-2p_0^\dagger\) and
\(s_h^-=2p_0^\dagger-\lambda_d^-\) is
\[
h s_h^{\operatorname{sign}(h)}
=|h|\lambda_d^{\operatorname{sign}(h)}-2|h|p_0^\dagger.
\tag{3}
\]
Consequently, the restriction \(\zeta=0\) in
\(\mathrm{def:scalar\text{-}airy\text{-}tube\text{-}legacy}\) has response probabilities
\[
p_{n,x,h,0,t}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}
=p_{0,t}^\dagger+
\alpha_n\left\{|x|\lambda_{v,t}^{\operatorname{sign}(x)}
+|h|\lambda_{d,t}^{\operatorname{sign}(h)}
-(3|x|+2|h|)p_{0,t}^\dagger\right\}.
\tag{4}
\]
This is the two-coordinate fiber in
\(\mathrm{prop:unequal\text{-}three\text{-}arm\text{-}exact\text{-}primitives}\).  Since
\(|x|\leq T_k^\dagger\), \(|h|\leq\varepsilon\), and (1) holds, that proposition proves that (4) is a probability distribution on the eight response types.

Conditional on \((X^\dagger,H^\dagger)=(x,h)\), the construction draws the count vector \(m\) multinomially from (4) and then draws a labeled response-type sequence uniformly in its count orbit.  For a labeled sequence \((t_1,\ldots,t_n)\) with counts \(m=(m_t)_t\), its conditional probability is therefore
\[
\left\{\frac{n!}{\prod_t m_t!}
\prod_t\bigl(p_{n,x,h,0,t}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}\bigr)^{m_t}\right\}
\left\{\frac{\prod_t m_t!}{n!}\right\}
=\prod_{i=1}^n p_{n,x,h,0,t_i}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}.
\tag{5}
\]
Thus the labeled types are conditionally independent and identically distributed with law (4).

Consider the deterministic allocation count \(r=(n,0,0)\).  Every unit is assigned to arm one.  The estimator
\[
\widetilde\tau_n
=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n(2Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}-1)
=\frac{2X_1^{\mathrm{obs}}}{n}-1
\tag{6}
\]
is therefore a function of the observed data.  Define
\[
e(t)=2t_1-1-(c^\dagger)^\top t.
\tag{7}
\]
The finite-population identity in
\(\mathrm{prop:unequal\text{-}three\text{-}arm\text{-}exact\text{-}primitives}\) gives, for every labeled sequence with count vector \(m\),
\[
\widetilde\tau_n-\tau_{c^\dagger}(m)
=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n e(t_i).
\tag{8}
\]
Because \(2t_1-1=2(b_t)_1\), while the same proposition gives
\(\mathbb E[b_t\mid x,h]=\alpha_n(xv+hd^\dagger)\) and
\(\mathbb E[(c^\dagger)^\top t\mid x,h]=\alpha_nx\), we obtain, using
\(v_1=1/2\) and \(d_1^\dagger=0\),
\[
\mathbb E[e(t)\mid x,h]
=2\alpha_n(xv_1+hd_1^\dagger)-\alpha_nx=0.
\tag{9}
\]

In lexicographic order \(000,001,010,011,100,101,110,111\), direct substitution of
\(c^\dagger=(1,-1/4,-3/4)\) into (7) gives
\[
\bigl(e(t)^2\bigr)_t
=\left(1,\frac1{16},\frac9{16},0,0,
\frac9{16},\frac1{16},1\right).
\tag{10}
\]
Since \(p_0^\dagger=(\delta_{000}+\delta_{111})/2\), it follows that
\[
\sum_t p_{0,t}^\dagger e(t)^2=1.
\tag{11}
\]
Using the four response measures displayed in
\(\mathrm{prop:unequal\text{-}three\text{-}arm\text{-}exact\text{-}primitives}\) and (10), for either sign we have
\[
\sum_t\lambda_{v,t}^{\pm}e(t)^2
=1+\frac9{16}+\frac1{16}=\frac{13}{8},
\qquad
\sum_t\lambda_{d,t}^{\pm}e(t)^2
=\frac{23}{16}.
\tag{12}
\]
For completeness, the second equality is, for the positive measure,
\[
\frac34\frac9{16}+\frac34\cdot1
+\frac14\frac1{16}+\frac14\cdot1=\frac{23}{16},
\tag{13}
\]
and the negative measure has the same four squared-error values with the same weights.  Substitution of (11)--(12) into (4) now yields the exact identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E[e(t)^2\mid x,h]
&=1+\alpha_n\left\{
|x|\left(\frac{13}{8}-3\right)
+|h|\left(\frac{23}{16}-2\right)\right\}\\
&=1-\alpha_n\left(\frac{11}{8}|x|+\frac9{16}|h|\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
Combining conditional independence from (5), the zero mean (9), and the target identity (8), we get
\[
\mathbb E\left[
(\widetilde\tau_n-\tau_{c^\dagger}(m))^2\mid x,h\right]
=\frac1n\left\{1-\alpha_n\left(
\frac{11}{8}|x|+\frac9{16}|h|\right)\right\}.
\tag{15}
\]

Under the scalar prior the two coordinates are independent, with densities \(w_k^\dagger\) and \(\rho_\varepsilon\).  The declared formula for the latter gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_{\mathbb R}|h|\rho_\varepsilon(h)\,dh
&=\frac{30}{16\varepsilon}\int_0^\varepsilon
h\left(1-\frac{h^2}{\varepsilon^2}\right)^2\,dh\\
&=\frac{30\varepsilon}{16}\int_0^1u(1-u^2)^2\,du
=\frac5{16}\varepsilon.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
Since \(\alpha_n=n^{-1/3}\), integrating (15) proves that the Bayes risk of the observable estimator (6) equals
\[
\frac1n-n^{-4/3}\left\{
\frac{11}{8}\int_{\mathbb R}|x|w_k^\dagger(x)\,dx
+\frac{45}{256}\varepsilon\right\}.
\tag{17}
\]
For every observation value with positive prior-predictive probability and every action \(a\), conditional square completion gives
\[
\mathbb E[(a-\tau_{c^\dagger}(m))^2\mid X^{\mathrm{obs}}]
=\operatorname{Var}(\tau_{c^\dagger}(m)\mid X^{\mathrm{obs}})
+\left\{a-\mathbb E[\tau_{c^\dagger}(m)\mid X^{\mathrm{obs}}]\right\}^2.
\tag{18}
\]
Hence the posterior mean used in
\(\mathrm{def:exact\text{-}scalar\text{-}airy\text{-}tube\text{-}bayes\text{-}risk}\) minimizes conditional risk; zero-probability observation values contribute nothing.  Because (6) is an admissible observed-data action, (17)--(18) imply
\[
\mathcal B_{n,k,\varepsilon}^{\dagger,\mathrm A,0}(n,0,0)
\leq \frac1n-n^{-4/3}\left\{
\frac{11}{8}\int_{\mathbb R}|x|w_k^\dagger(x)\,dx
+\frac{45}{256}\varepsilon\right\}.
\tag{19}
\]
Applying the definition of
\(G_{n,k,\varepsilon}^{\dagger,\mathrm A,0}\) to (19) proves
\[
G_{n,k,\varepsilon}^{\dagger,\mathrm A,0}(n,0,0)
\geq
\frac{11}{8}\int_{\mathbb R}|x|w_k^\dagger(x)\,dx
+\frac{45}{256}\varepsilon.
\tag{20}
\]

It remains to evaluate the limiting longitudinal moment.  Put
\(\kappa=\kappa(c^\dagger)=5/8\),
\(\Phi=\phi_{c^\dagger}\),
\(A=\int_{\mathbb R}|\Phi'|^2\), and
\(B=\int_{\mathbb R}|x|\Phi^2\).  The scaling
\(f(x)=\kappa^{1/6}g(\kappa^{1/3}x)\) preserves the \(L^2\) norm and gives
\[
4\int|f'|^2+\kappa\int|x|f^2
=\kappa^{2/3}\left\{4\int|g'|^2+\int|x|g^2\right\}.
\tag{21}
\]
Thus the definitions of \(C_A\), \(\Phi\), and \(\Lambda_{c^\dagger}\) imply
\[
4A+\kappa B=C_A\kappa^{2/3}=\Lambda_{c^\dagger}.
\tag{22}
\]
For \(s>0\), let \(\Phi_s(x)=s^{1/2}\Phi(sx)\).  It also has unit \(L^2\) norm, and its energy is
\[
E(s)=4s^2A+\kappa s^{-1}B.
\tag{23}
\]
Because \(\Phi\) is the ground-state minimizer in
\(\mathrm{def:airy\text{-}benchmark}\), \(s=1\) minimizes (23).  Differentiation at this interior point gives
\[
0=E'(1)=8A-\kappa B.
\tag{24}
\]
Solving (22)--(24) and using \(\kappa=5/8\) gives
\[
B=\frac{2\Lambda_{c^\dagger}}{3\kappa}
=\frac{16}{15}\Lambda_{c^\dagger}.
\tag{25}
\]

By \(\mathrm{lem:effective\text{-}normalized\text{-}airy\text{-}record}\),
\(\varphi_k^\dagger\to\Phi\) in weighted \(H^1\).  In particular,
\(Z_k:=\int(\varphi_k^\dagger)^2\to1\).  Moreover,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|\int |x|\{(\varphi_k^\dagger)^2-\Phi^2\}\,dx\right|
&\leq
\left(\int |x||\varphi_k^\dagger-\Phi|^2\,dx\right)^{1/2}\\
&\quad\times
\left(\int |x||\varphi_k^\dagger+\Phi|^2\,dx\right)^{1/2}
\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{26}
\]
The second factor is bounded because
\(|\varphi_k^\dagger+\Phi|^2\leq
2|\varphi_k^\dagger-\Phi|^2+8|\Phi|^2\), weighted convergence holds, and \(B<\infty\).  Since
\(w_k^\dagger=(\varphi_k^\dagger)^2/Z_k\), equations (25)--(26) prove
\[
\int_{\mathbb R}|x|w_k^\dagger(x)\,dx
\longrightarrow \frac{16}{15}\Lambda_{c^\dagger}.
\tag{27}
\]

Now let \(k_n\to\infty\) and let the positive rational sequence \(\varepsilon_n\) satisfy the admissibility condition in the statement.  The transverse term in (20) is nonnegative, so (20) and (27) yield
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
G_{n,k_n,\varepsilon_n}^{\dagger,\mathrm A,0}(n,0,0)
\geq \frac{11}{8}\frac{16}{15}\Lambda_{c^\dagger}
=\frac{22}{15}\Lambda_{c^\dagger}.
\tag{28}
\]
The definition of \(C_A\) and the proved value \(\kappa(c^\dagger)=5/8>0\) give
\(\Lambda_{c^\dagger}>0\); hence the last member of (28) is strictly larger than \(\Lambda_{c^\dagger}\).

Finally, \((n,0,0)\in\mathcal R_{n,3}\) has zero coordinates.  If a sequence
\(e_{\mathrm{zero},n}\to0\) satisfied the asserted upper bound for every allocation count with a zero coordinate, applying it to \((n,0,0)\) would make the left side of (28) at most \(\Lambda_{c^\dagger}\).  This contradicts (28), and proves the final assertion.
