%%% FIELD legacy-scalar-definition-construction
The scalar Airy tube is the restriction of \(\mathrm{def:explicit\text{-}three\text{-}arm\text{-}airy\text{-}tube}\) obtained by fixing \(\zeta=0\) before mixing.  Thus it draws \(X^\dagger\) with density \(w_k^\dagger\), draws \(H^\dagger\) with density \(\rho_\varepsilon\), uses \(p_{n,x,h,0}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}\), then draws the response-count vector and a uniform labeling.  Its schedule prior is denoted \(\nu_{n,k,\varepsilon}^{\dagger,\mathrm A,0}\).  This definition is retained only to state the proved zero-face diagnostic for the superseded scalar architecture.
%%% FIELD legacy-scalar-risk-definition-construction
Using \(\nu_{n,k,\varepsilon}^{\dagger,\mathrm A,0}\) in place of \(\nu_{n,k,\varepsilon}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}\) in \(\mathrm{def:exact\text{-}airy\text{-}tube\text{-}bayes\text{-}risk}\), define the fixed-count posterior-mean risk \(\mathcal B_{n,k,\varepsilon}^{\dagger,\mathrm A,0}(r)\), and put
\[
B_{n,k,\varepsilon}^{\dagger,\mathrm A,0}
=\min_{r\in\mathcal R_{n,3}}
\mathcal B_{n,k,\varepsilon}^{\dagger,\mathrm A,0}(r),
\qquad
G_{n,k,\varepsilon}^{\dagger,\mathrm A,0}(r)
=n^{4/3}\left\{n^{-1}-
\mathcal B_{n,k,\varepsilon}^{\dagger,\mathrm A,0}(r)\right\}.
\]
%%% FIELD scalar-zero-face-diagnostic-statement
For every pair of integers \(n,k\geq1\) and every rational \(\varepsilon>0\) satisfying
\[
\alpha_n(3T_k^\dagger+2\varepsilon)\leq1,
\]
the legacy scalar Airy-tube deficit at the allocation count \((n,0,0)\) obeys
\[
G_{n,k,\varepsilon}^{\dagger,\mathrm A,0}(n,0,0)
\geq
\frac{11}{8}\int_{\mathbb R}|x|w_k^\dagger(x)\,dx
+\frac{45}{256}\varepsilon.
\]
Consequently, for every sequence \(k_n\to\infty\) and every sequence of positive rational numbers \(\varepsilon_n\) satisfying
\(\alpha_n(3T_{k_n}^\dagger+2\varepsilon_n)\leq1\),
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
G_{n,k_n,\varepsilon_n}^{\dagger,\mathrm A,0}(n,0,0)
\geq\frac{22}{15}\Lambda_{c^\dagger}
>\Lambda_{c^\dagger}.
\]
In particular, there is no sequence \(e_{\mathrm{zero},n}\to0\) for which
\[
G_{n,k_n,\varepsilon_n}^{\dagger,\mathrm A,0}(r)
\leq\Lambda_{c^\dagger}+e_{\mathrm{zero},n}
\]
holds for every allocation count \(r\in\mathcal R_{n,3}\) having at least one zero coordinate.  Thus the legacy scalar prior cannot furnish the zero-face part of a sharp all-count converse.
%%% FIELD scalar-zero-face-diagnostic-proof
Fix \(n,k,\varepsilon\) satisfying the displayed admissibility condition.  Conditional on the scalar-tube coordinates \((x,h)\), the construction in \(\mathrm{def:scalar\text{-}airy\text{-}tube\text{-}legacy}\) first draws a count vector \(m\) from the multinomial law with cell probabilities \(p_{n,x,h,0}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}\) and then draws a labeled response-type sequence uniformly in the orbit having counts \(m\).  If a labeled sequence \((t_1,\ldots,t_n)\) has counts \(m=(m_t)_t\), its conditional probability is
\[
\left\{\frac{n!}{\prod_t m_t!}
       \prod_t\bigl(p_{n,x,h,0,t}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}\bigr)^{m_t}\right\}
\left\{\frac{\prod_t m_t!}{n!}\right\}
=\prod_{i=1}^n p_{n,x,h,0,t_i}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}.
\tag{1}
\]
Thus, conditional on \((x,h)\), the labeled types are independent and identically distributed with that cell law.  By the scalar-legacy definition, this law is exactly the two-coordinate law in \(\mathrm{prop:unequal\text{-}three\text{-}arm\text{-}exact\text{-}primitives}\).  The proposition and
\(\alpha_n(3T_k^\dagger+2\varepsilon)\leq1\) therefore ensure that all eight probabilities are nonnegative and sum to one.

For the deterministic outcome-independent allocation having count vector \((n,0,0)\), all units are assigned to arm one, so the observed data reveal \(t_{i,1}\).  Define the observable estimator and its unit-level error by
\[
\widetilde\tau_n=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n(2Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}-1),
\qquad
e(t)=2t_1-1-(c^\dagger)^\top t.
\tag{2}
\]
The exact finite-population target identity in
\(\mathrm{prop:unequal\text{-}three\text{-}arm\text{-}exact\text{-}primitives}\) gives
\[
\widetilde\tau_n-\tau_{c^\dagger}(m)
=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n e(t_i).
\tag{3}
\]
The same proposition gives \(v_1=1/2\), \(d_1^\dagger=0\),
\(\mathbb E[b_t\mid x,h]=\alpha_n(xv+hd^\dagger)\), and
\(\mathbb E[(c^\dagger)^\top t\mid x,h]=\alpha_nx\).  Since
\(2t_1-1=2(b_t)_1\), it follows that
\[
\mathbb E[e(t)\mid x,h]
=2\alpha_n(xv_1+hd_1^\dagger)-\alpha_nx=0.
\tag{4}
\]

For the lexicographically ordered types
\(000,001,010,011,100,101,110,111\), direct substitution of
\(c^\dagger=(1,-1/4,-3/4)\) into (2) gives
\[
\bigl(e(t)^2\bigr)_t
=\left(1,\frac1{16},\frac9{16},0,0,
       \frac9{16},\frac1{16},1\right).
\tag{5}
\]
Because \(p_0^\dagger=(\delta_{000}+\delta_{111})/2\), equation (5) yields
\(\sum_t p_{0,t}^\dagger e(t)^2=1\).  Substitution into the four explicitly declared response measures gives, for either sign,
\[
\sum_t\lambda_{v,t}^{\pm}e(t)^2
=1+\frac9{16}+\frac1{16}=\frac{13}{8},
\qquad
\sum_t\lambda_{d,t}^{\pm}e(t)^2
=\frac{23}{16}.
\tag{6}
\]
The longitudinal measures have mass three and the transverse measures have mass two.  The scalar fiber is
\[
p_{n,x,h,0,t}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}
=p_{0,t}^\dagger+\alpha_n\left\{
|x|\lambda_{v,t}^{\operatorname{sign}(x)}
+|h|\lambda_{d,t}^{\operatorname{sign}(h)}
-(3|x|+2|h|)p_{0,t}^\dagger\right\}.
\tag{7}
\]
Combining (5)--(7) proves the exact conditional second-moment identity
\[
\mathbb E[e(t)^2\mid x,h]
=1-\alpha_n\left(\frac{11}{8}|x|+
                         \frac9{16}|h|\right).
\tag{8}
\]

By the conditional independence in (1), the zero conditional mean in (4), and (3),
\[
\mathbb E\!\left[
(\widetilde\tau_n-\tau_{c^\dagger}(m))^2\mid x,h\right]
=\frac1n\left\{1-\alpha_n\left(
\frac{11}{8}|x|+\frac9{16}|h|\right)\right\}.
\tag{9}
\]
The coordinates are independent under the legacy prior, with respective densities
\(w_k^\dagger\) and \(\rho_\varepsilon\).  From the declared polynomial formula for the latter,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_{\mathbb R}|h|\rho_\varepsilon(h)\,dh
&=\frac{30}{16\varepsilon}\int_0^\varepsilon
h\left(1-\frac{h^2}{\varepsilon^2}\right)^2dh\\
&=\frac{30\varepsilon}{16}\int_0^1u(1-u^2)^2du
=\frac5{16}\varepsilon.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
Since \(\alpha_n=n^{-1/3}\), averaging (9) and applying (10) shows that the Bayes risk of the particular estimator \(\widetilde\tau_n\) is
\[
\frac1n-n^{-4/3}\left\{
\frac{11}{8}\int_{\mathbb R}|x|w_k^\dagger(x)\,dx
+\frac{45}{256}\varepsilon\right\}.
\tag{11}
\]
Under squared error, for every observation value with positive prior-predictive probability, completing the square shows that the conditional posterior mean uniquely minimizes conditional risk; observation values of zero prior-predictive probability contribute zero.  Hence the exact posterior-mean risk
\(\mathcal B_{n,k,\varepsilon}^{\dagger,\mathrm A,0}(n,0,0)\) in
\(\mathrm{def:exact\text{-}scalar\text{-}airy\text{-}tube\text{-}bayes\text{-}risk}\) is at most (11).  The definition
\[
G_{n,k,\varepsilon}^{\dagger,\mathrm A,0}(r)
=n^{4/3}\left\{n^{-1}-
\mathcal B_{n,k,\varepsilon}^{\dagger,\mathrm A,0}(r)\right\}
\tag{12}
\]
then proves the first asserted inequality, including the positive transverse term.

It remains to calculate the limit of the longitudinal term.  Put
\(\Phi=\phi_{c^\dagger}\),
\(A=\int_{\mathbb R}|\Phi'(x)|^2dx\), and
\(B=\int_{\mathbb R}|x|\Phi(x)^2dx\).  Both integrals are finite by
\(\mathrm{def:airy\text{-}benchmark}\).  For \(s>0\), the function
\(\Phi_s(x)=s^{1/2}\Phi(sx)\) has unit \(L^2\) norm, and a change of variables gives its Airy energy as
\[
E(s)=4s^2A+\kappa(c^\dagger)s^{-1}B.
\tag{13}
\]
The ground-state variational property in
\(\mathrm{def:airy\text{-}benchmark}\) makes \(s=1\) a minimum of the differentiable function (13).  Therefore
\[
0=E'(1)=8A-\kappa(c^\dagger)B.
\tag{14}
\]
Together with
\(\Lambda_{c^\dagger}=4A+\kappa(c^\dagger)B\), equation (14) yields
\[
B=\frac{2\Lambda_{c^\dagger}}
        {3\kappa(c^\dagger)}
=\frac{16}{15}\Lambda_{c^\dagger},
\tag{15}
\]
where the last equality uses
\(\kappa(c^\dagger)=5/8\) from
\(\mathrm{prop:unequal\text{-}three\text{-}arm\text{-}exact\text{-}primitives}\).

By \(\mathrm{lem:effective\text{-}normalized\text{-}airy\text{-}record}\),
\(\varphi_k^\dagger\to\Phi\) in weighted \(H^1\).  In particular,
\(\int(\varphi_k^\dagger)^2\to1\), and Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_{\mathbb R}|x|
\left|(\varphi_k^\dagger)^2-\Phi^2\right|dx
&\leq
\left(\int_{\mathbb R}|x|
|\varphi_k^\dagger-\Phi|^2dx\right)^{1/2}\\
&\quad\times
\left(\int_{\mathbb R}|x|
|\varphi_k^\dagger+\Phi|^2dx\right)^{1/2}
\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
The second factor is bounded because weighted \(L^2\) convergence and the finiteness of \(B\) bound it.  Since
\(w_k^\dagger=(\varphi_k^\dagger)^2/
\int(\varphi_k^\dagger)^2\), equations (15)--(16) imply
\[
\int_{\mathbb R}|x|w_k^\dagger(x)\,dx
\longrightarrow\frac{16}{15}\Lambda_{c^\dagger}.
\tag{17}
\]
For any admissible sequences in the statement, the transverse summand in the first inequality is nonnegative.  Taking the lower limit and using (17) gives
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
G_{n,k_n,\varepsilon_n}^{\dagger,\mathrm A,0}(n,0,0)
\geq\frac{11}{8}\frac{16}{15}\Lambda_{c^\dagger}
=\frac{22}{15}\Lambda_{c^\dagger}.
\tag{18}
\]
The benchmark definition and \(\kappa(c^\dagger)=5/8>0\) give
\(\Lambda_{c^\dagger}>0\), so the last member of (18) is strictly larger than \(\Lambda_{c^\dagger}\).  Finally, \((n,0,0)\) has zero coordinates.  If the asserted uniform sequence \(e_{\mathrm{zero},n}\to0\) existed, its bound at \((n,0,0)\) would imply a lower limit no greater than \(\Lambda_{c^\dagger}\), contradicting (18).  This proves the final assertion.
