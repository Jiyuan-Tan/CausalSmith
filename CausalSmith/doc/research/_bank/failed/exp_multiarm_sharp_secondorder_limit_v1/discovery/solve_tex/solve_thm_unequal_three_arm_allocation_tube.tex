%%% FIELD sharp-proof
By \(\mathrm{prop:unequal\text{-}three\text{-}arm\text{-}exact\text{-}primitives}\),
\[
 \kappa(c^\dagger)=\frac58,
 \qquad
 \tau_{c^\dagger}(m)=\frac1n\sum_{t\in\{0,1\}^3}
 m_t(c^\dagger)^\top t.                                      \tag{1}
\]
If a labeled schedule \(y\) has response-count vector \(m\), the second
identity in \((1)\), together with the definition of \(\tau_{c^\dagger}(y)\),
gives
\[
 \tau_{c^\dagger}(m)=\tau_{c^\dagger}(y).                    \tag{2}
\]
Thus the Bayes calculations below concern the exact finite-population causal
target, rather than the conditional mean of that target.  By
\(\mathrm{def:airy\text{-}benchmark}\) and the declared property
\(C_A\in(0,\infty)\),
\[
 \Lambda_{c^\dagger}
 =C_A\kappa(c^\dagger)^{2/3}
 =C_A(5/8)^{2/3}\in(0,\infty).                               \tag{3}
\]

The remaining argument is conditional on conclusions \({\rm(A)}\)--\({\rm(C)}\)
of \(\mathrm{thm:unequal\text{-}three\text{-}arm\text{-}allocation\text{-}tube}\).
Fix \(n\) in the sufficiently large tail supplied by that theorem.  Its live
positivity certificate is
\[
 \alpha_n(3T_{k_n}^\dagger+4\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm A})\le1.  \tag{4}
\]
The positivity conclusion of
\(\mathrm{lem:face\text{-}robust\text{-}tangent\text{-}score\text{-}identities}\)
therefore shows that every fiber
\(p_{n,x,h,\zeta}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}\) used by the prior is a probability
law.  That lemma also gives
\[
 \mathbb E[\theta_t\mid x,h,\zeta]=\alpha_nx.                \tag{5}
\]
Equation \((5)\) describes the prior mean, whereas equations \((1)\)--\((2)\)
ensure that the loss in the orbit calculation is evaluated against the random
exact finite-population target.

Let \(\mathcal D_n\) be any outcome-independent, possibly dependent assignment
law, let \(\widehat\tau\) be any possibly randomized labeled-data estimator,
and let \(\pi\) be the allocation-count law induced by \(\mathcal D_n\).
The premise of
\(\mathrm{lem:airy\text{-}tube\text{-}permutation\text{-}orbit\text{-}bayes\text{-}lower}\)
is satisfied by \((4)\), so that lemma yields
\[
 \operatorname{BR}(\mathcal D_n,\widehat\tau)
 \ge \sum_{r\in\mathcal R_{n,3}}\pi(r)
       \mathcal B_{n,k_n,\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm A}}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}(r)
 \ge B_{n,k_n,\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm A}}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}. \tag{6}
\]
The prior is supported on \(\{0,1\}^{n\times3}\), and by \((2)\) its loss is
the loss appearing in \(\mathrm{def:risk\text{-}game}\).  Hence maximum risk
dominates the Bayes risk in \((6)\).  Taking the infimum over precisely the
designs and estimators in that definition gives
\[
 R_n(c^\dagger)
 \ge B_{n,k_n,\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm A}}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}. \tag{7}
\]

By \(\mathrm{def:exact\text{-}airy\text{-}tube\text{-}bayes\text{-}risk}\)
and finiteness of \(\mathcal R_{n,3}\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 n^{4/3}\left\{\frac1n-
 B_{n,k_n,\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm A}}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}\right\}
 &=n^{4/3}\left\{\frac1n-
 \min_{r\in\mathcal R_{n,3}}
 \mathcal B_{n,k_n,\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm A}}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}(r)\right\}\\
 &=\max_{r\in\mathcal R_{n,3}}
 G_{n,k_n,\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm A}}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}(r). \tag{8}
\end{aligned}
\]
The sets \(\mathcal L_n\), \(\mathcal X_n\), and \(\mathcal Z_n\) in
conclusion \({\rm(A)}\) are pairwise disjoint and exhaust
\(\mathcal R_{n,3}\): an allocation count either has a zero coordinate or has
all coordinates positive, and a positive count either satisfies the defining
local inequality or does not.  Put
\[
 e_n^-=\max\{e_{\mathrm{loc},n},e_{\mathrm{shell},n},
 e_{\mathrm{zero},n}\}.
\]
Applying the appropriate line of \({\rm(A)}\) to each member of the partition
and using \((8)\) gives
\[
 B_{n,k_n,\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm A}}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}
 \ge \frac1n-\{\Lambda_{c^\dagger}+e_n^-\}n^{-4/3}.          \tag{9}
\]
Equations \((7)\)--\((9)\) prove the first two inequalities in \((1)\) of the
theorem.

Conclusion \({\rm(B)}\) supplies a total procedure
\(\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm A}\) under an outcome-independent design and gives
\[
 \max_{y\in\{0,1\}^{n\times3}}
 \mathbb E\!\left[
 \{\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm A}-\tau_{c^\dagger}(y)\}^2\right]
 \le \frac1n-
 \{\Lambda_{c^\dagger}-e_{\mathrm{pr},n}\}n^{-4/3}.         \tag{10}
\]
This design--estimator pair is feasible in \(\mathrm{def:risk\text{-}game}\).
Consequently \(R_n(c^\dagger)\) is no larger than the maximum risk in
\((10)\), proving the last two inequalities in the theorem's chain.

Subtracting the resulting lower and upper risk bounds from \(n^{-1}\) and
multiplying by \(n^{4/3}>0\) yields
\[
 \Lambda_{c^\dagger}-e_{\mathrm{pr},n}
 \le n^{4/3}\{n^{-1}-R_n(c^\dagger)\}
 \le \Lambda_{c^\dagger}+e_n^-.                             \tag{11}
\]
For every \(n\ge N\) in the certified tail, conclusion \({\rm(C)}\) bounds
both \(e_{\mathrm{pr},n}\) and \(e_n^-\) by \(E(N)\).  Therefore \((11)\)
implies
\[
 \left|n^{4/3}\{n^{-1}-R_n(c^\dagger)\}
 -\Lambda_{c^\dagger}\right|\le E(N).                       \tag{12}
\]
Since \(E(N)\downarrow0\), equation \((12)\) proves the asserted convergence.
Rearranging it and invoking \((3)\) proves the displayed second-order
expansion and the strict positivity and finiteness of its coefficient.

This proof is only the algebraic corollary of the root conclusions
\({\rm(A)}\)--\({\rm(C)}\).  In particular, it neither proves those
conclusions nor discharges the open obligation in
\(\mathrm{thm:unequal\text{-}three\text{-}arm\text{-}allocation\text{-}tube}\).

%%% FIELD grid-proof
At \(c=c^\dagger\), the definition of the contrast-weighted allocation gives
\[
 q^\star=(|1|/2,|-1/4|/2,|-3/4|/2)
 =(1/2,1/8,3/8)=q^\dagger.                                  \tag{13}
\]
Hence \(\mathrm{thm:qstar\text{-}uniform\text{-}risk\text{-}upper}\)
supplies a total estimator \(\delta_n^\star\in[-1,1]\) under the independent
design \(q^\dagger\) and a finite constant \(B_{c^\dagger}>0\) such that
\[
 \max_{y\in\{0,1\}^{n\times3}}
 \mathbb E_y\!\left[
 \{\delta_n^\star-\tau_{c^\dagger}(y)\}^2\right]
 \le \frac1n-\Lambda_{c^\dagger}n^{-4/3}
 +B_{c^\dagger}n^{-3/2}.                                    \tag{14}
\]

Let \(a_j=-1+j/G\), \(0\le j\le2G\), be the grid in
\(\mathrm{def:evaluator\text{-}handle}\), and put \(\Delta=G^{-1}\).
For a realized observation \(\omega\), write
\(d_\omega=\delta_n^\star(\omega)\).  If \(d_\omega<1\), choose the unique
\(j\in\{0,\ldots,2G-1\}\) satisfying
\(a_j\le d_\omega<a_{j+1}\), set
\[
 \lambda_\omega=\frac{d_\omega-a_j}{\Delta}\in[0,1),       \tag{15}
\]
and return \(a_{j+1}\) with probability \(\lambda_\omega\) and \(a_j\)
otherwise.  If \(d_\omega=1\), return \(a_{2G}=1\).  Denote the resulting
randomized action by \(\widetilde d_\omega\).  Direct calculation from
\((15)\) gives
\[
 \mathbb E[\widetilde d_\omega\mid\omega]=d_\omega,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(\widetilde d_\omega\mid\omega)
 =\lambda_\omega(1-\lambda_\omega)\Delta^2
 \le\frac1{4G^2},                                           \tag{16}
\]
with zero variance in the endpoint case.  Thus, for every
\(\tau\in[-1,1]\), conditional square completion and \((16)\) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E[(\widetilde d_\omega-\tau)^2\mid\omega]
 &= (d_\omega-\tau)^2+
    \operatorname{Var}(\widetilde d_\omega\mid\omega)\\
 &\le(d_\omega-\tau)^2+\frac1{4G^2}.                         \tag{17}
\end{aligned}
\]

The probabilities in \((15)\) define a feasible point of the real feasible
polytope of the rational-coefficient linear program in
\(\mathrm{def:evaluator\text{-}handle}\).  They need not themselves be
rational: a rational linear program is optimized over real variables, and a
rational optimal basic feasible solution exists because its feasible
polytope is nonempty and finite-dimensional.  Taking expectation in \((17)\),
maximizing over the finite schedule set, using optimality of the program value
\(U_n\), and then applying \((14)\), gives
\[
 U_n\le \frac1n-\Lambda_{c^\dagger}n^{-4/3}
 +B_{c^\dagger}n^{-3/2}+\frac1{4G^2}.                        \tag{18}
\]
The independent allocation and the program's returned grid kernel form an
admissible design--estimator pair in \(\mathrm{def:risk\text{-}game}\).
Therefore
\[
 R_n(c^\dagger)\le U_n.                                     \tag{19}
\]
Equations \((18)\)--\((19)\) prove the first display in the lemma.

It remains to bound the evaluator's outward primal error.  Let
\(U_{\Lambda,n}\) be its rational upper endpoint for
\(\Lambda_{c^\dagger}\).  The evaluator is allowed to refine an interval to
width at most \(2^{-n}\), so it may refine both the Airy interval and the
ordered-algebraic enclosure used for the positive part until
\[
 0\le U_{\Lambda,n}-\Lambda_{c^\dagger}\le2^{-(n+2)},
 \qquad
 0\le e_{\mathrm{pr},n}-
 \left[n^{4/3}(U_n-n^{-1})+U_{\Lambda,n}\right]_+
 \le2^{-(n+2)}.                                             \tag{20}
\]
Since \(G=\lceil n^{3/4}\rceil\ge n^{3/4}\), equation \((18)\) and the
first inequality in \((20)\) yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
 n^{4/3}(U_n-n^{-1})+U_{\Lambda,n}
 &\le B_{c^\dagger}n^{-1/6}
 +\frac{n^{4/3}}{4G^2}
 +(U_{\Lambda,n}-\Lambda_{c^\dagger})\\
 &\le (B_{c^\dagger}+1/4)n^{-1/6}+2^{-(n+2)}.               \tag{21}
\end{aligned}
\]
The last member of \((21)\) is nonnegative.  Taking positive parts in
\((21)\) and using the second inequality in \((20)\) therefore gives
\[
 e_{\mathrm{pr},n}
 \le(B_{c^\dagger}+1/4)n^{-1/6}+2^{-(n+1)}
 \le(B_{c^\dagger}+1/4)n^{-1/6}+2^{-n}.                     \tag{22}
\]
Because \(B_{c^\dagger}<\infty\), the right side tends to zero.  This proves
ordinary convergence of \(e_{\mathrm{pr},n}\).  The dependency
\(\mathrm{thm:qstar\text{-}uniform\text{-}risk\text{-}upper}\) asserts only
the existence of \(B_{c^\dagger}\); it does not provide that constant as
computable input.  Thus \((22)\) does not, by itself, furnish the computable
all-future modulus required by conclusion \({\rm(C)}\) of the allocation-tube
theorem.

Define
\[
 \overline N_s=\max_{1\le j\le s}N(j,2^{-j},2^{-j}),
 \qquad
 d(n)=\max\{s\le n:\overline N_s\le n\}                    \tag{23}
\]
whenever the latter set is nonempty.  Each \(\overline N_s\) is a finite
computable integer by
\(\mathrm{def:face\text{-}robust\text{-}directional\text{-}ledger}\) and
\(\mathrm{lem:face\text{-}robust\text{-}tangent\text{-}score\text{-}identities}\).
For fixed \(s\), every
\(n\ge\max\{s,\overline N_s\}\) makes \(s\) feasible in \((23)\).
Feasibility persists as \(n\) increases.  Consequently
\[
 d(n)\uparrow\infty,
 \qquad k_n:=d(n)\uparrow\infty,
 \qquad \varepsilon_n^{\mathrm A}:=2^{-d(n)}\downarrow0.    \tag{24}
\]
For every admissible \(n\), the definition of \(d(n)\) gives
\(n\ge N(k_n,2^{-k_n},2^{-k_n})\).  The first two monotone inequalities in
the definition of \(N\) therefore give
\[
 \alpha_n(3T_{k_n}^\dagger+4\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm A})
 \le\frac12\le1,
 \qquad
 4\alpha_n^2L(k_n,\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm A})^2\le\frac12.   \tag{25}
\]
Thus the live positivity condition holds and every arm-marginal variance below
is at least \(1/8\).  The fixed-input evaluator and the orbit-Bayes lemma give
exact same-input certificates and
\[
 R_n(c^\dagger)
 \ge B_{n,k_n,\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm A}}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}. \tag{26}
\]
The exact-risk definition also gives
\[
 n^{4/3}\{n^{-1}-B_{n,k_n,\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm A}}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}\}
 =\max_{r\in\mathcal R_{n,3}}
 G_{n,k_n,\varepsilon_n^{\mathrm A}}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}(r). \tag{27}
\]

There is also an exact posterior decomposition which removes the loose
Cauchy--Schwarz covariance bound from the prior attempt.  Conditional on a
tube coordinate \(\lambda=(x,h,\zeta)\), drawing a multinomial response-count
vector and then a schedule uniformly in its orbit is exactly equivalent to
drawing the labeled response types independently from
\(p=p_{n,x,h,\zeta}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}\).  Fix an allocation count \(r\).
Write
\[
 M=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\theta_{t_i},
 \quad
 \mu=\mathbb E_p\theta_t=\alpha_nx,
 \quad
 \mu_a=\mathbb E_pb_{t,a},
 \quad
 C_a=\operatorname{Cov}_p(\theta_t,b_{t,a}),
 \quad
 V_a=\frac14-\mu_a^2.                                      \tag{28}
\]
The second inequality in \((25)\) implies \(V_a\ge1/8>0\).  Since
\(b_{t,a}\) is binary-valued, its two-point linear regression is exact:
\[
 \mathbb E_p[\theta_t\mid b_{t,a}]
 =\mu+\frac{C_a}{V_a}(b_{t,a}-\mu_a).                        \tag{29}
\]
If \(O\) denotes all observed arm labels and binary outcomes, conditional
independence of the response types and \((29)\) give
\[
 m_\lambda(O):=\mathbb E[M\mid\lambda,O]
 =\mu+\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
 \frac{C_{A_i}}{V_{A_i}}(b_{t_i,A_i}-\mu_{A_i}).             \tag{30}
\]
The projection identity for the nested sigma-fields generated by \(O\) and
by \((\lambda,O)\) now yields, without a cross term,
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \inf_d\mathbb E\{d(O)-M\}^2
 &=\mathbb E\operatorname{Var}(M\mid O)\\
 &=\mathbb E\operatorname{Var}(M\mid\lambda,O)
   +\mathbb E\operatorname{Var}\{m_\lambda(O)\mid O\}.      \tag{31}
\end{aligned}
\]
Taking expectation over \(O\) conditional on \(\lambda\) and using the law
of total variance in each arm gives the computable first term
\[
 \mathbb E\operatorname{Var}(M\mid\lambda,O)
 =\mathbb E_\lambda\!\left[
 \frac1{n^2}\sum_{a=1}^3r_a
 \left\{\operatorname{Var}_p(\theta_t)-\frac{C_a^2}{V_a}\right\}
 \right].                                                   \tag{32}
\]
A direct eight-type summation using the declared signed directions gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_p(\theta_t)
 =\alpha_n\left\{\frac58|x|+\frac3{16}|h|\right\}
  -\alpha_n^2x^2.                                           \tag{33}
\]
Equations \((31)\)--\((33)\) verify that the finite-population target
fluctuation contributes at order \(n^{-4/3}\), and they identify exactly the
additional observed-cell regression terms that must be retained.  They do not
identify the Airy coefficient: the second term in \((31)\) is the Bayes risk
of the data-dependent regression \(m_\lambda(O)\), not the Bayes risk for the
surrogate \(\alpha_nx\).  No frozen result expands that term uniformly over
\(\mathcal L_n\), \(\mathcal X_n\), and \(\mathcal Z_n\), or combines it with
\((32)\) with a computable common remainder.  Finally, the rational-grid
lemma gives only
\[
 e_{\mathrm{pr},n}
 \le(B_{c^\dagger}+1/4)n^{-1/6}+2^{-n}\longrightarrow0,      \tag{34}
\]
where \(B_{c^\dagger}\) is not supplied as computable data.  Thus
\((23)\)--\((34)\) prove diagonal admissibility, exact same-input evaluation,
the all-design Bayes reduction, and ordinary primal convergence, but not any
of conclusions \({\rm(A)}\)--\({\rm(C)}\).
