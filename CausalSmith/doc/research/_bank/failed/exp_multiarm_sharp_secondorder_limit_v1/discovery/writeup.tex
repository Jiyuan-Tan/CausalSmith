\section*{exp\_\allowbreak{}multiarm\_\allowbreak{}sharp\_\allowbreak{}secondorder\_\allowbreak{}limit}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} For the unequal three-arm contrast \(c^\dagger=(1,-1/4,-3/4)\), the unrestricted design--estimator game has the proved sharp expansion \(R_n(c^\dagger)=n^{-1}-C_A(5/8)^{2/3}n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3})\). The contrast-weighted independent allocation \(q^\dagger=(1/2,1/8,3/8)\) attains the same second-order coefficient, and the binary eight-type construction supplies a terminating finite-input certificate with a computable all-future error tail. An exact endpoint-reduction theorem also transports both the unrestricted three-arm result and the general fixed-independent benchmark to every fixed bounded interval \([L,U]\), multiplying risk by \((U-L)^2\). The bounded lift is mathematically total, but no exact Turing claim is made for unrepresented real endpoints or observations.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} The two-arm literature solves the unrestricted binary-treatment action space, whereas it does not determine the sharp coefficient for three active arms. The proved three-arm allocation-tube theorem closes the formerly missing design-independent Bayes bridge for \(c^\dagger\), uniformly over local counts, exterior positive-count allocations, proper faces, dependent assignment laws, and randomized labeled-data estimators. The remaining mathematical gap is unrestricted sharpness for a general permitted multiarm contrast, not the fixed-independent expansion.

\paragraph{Niche.} At \(c^\dagger\), the face-robust basis \([v,d^\dagger,\mathbf 1]\) separates target, transverse, and common-shift directions. Exact finite-population regression and directional van Trees bounds then control every allocation face, while the independent Airy feedback supplies the matching upper procedure. This yields the first sharp unrestricted three-active-arm coefficient in the note's fixed-horizon binary experiment and shows that \(q^\dagger\) has no second-order loss at this contrast.

\paragraph{Fill.} The contribution consists of the proved eight-type face-robust all-count Bayes expansion, its lossless orbit reduction against arbitrary outcome-independent dependent designs and arbitrary randomized estimators, a matching total binary procedure, the evaluable Airy object \(C_A(5/8)^{2/3}\), and a terminating rational finite-data certificate with a nonincreasing computable tail. A separate exact bounded-endpoint reduction uses an observable finite Bernoulli polynomial and conditional Jensen, preserving the design and estimator quantifiers and multiplying both minimax risks and attaining bounds by \((U-L)^2\).

\section{Related work}

Sudijono, Dobriban, and Tchetgen Tchetgen (2026), Theorems 2.2--2.3 and 3.1, optimize over the same unrestricted assignment-mechanism and measurable-estimator quantifiers for a binary-treatment action space: they prove the exact binary game, exact bounded-endpoint scaling, and its Airy expansion. Hull (2026), Theorem 1, likewise optimizes over every assignment mechanism and measurable estimator and gives the equivalent exact bounded two-arm game. Neither result covers three arms. Restricting the present three-arm nature class by \(Y(2)=Y(3)\) gives the valid comparison \(R_n(c^\dagger)\ge\rho_n^\star=n^{-1}-C_A n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3})\). Since \(C_A>C_A(5/8)^{2/3}\), that imported two-arm converse is strictly weaker than the proved sharp three-arm coefficient. Hirano and Porter (2009) and Levit (1986) motivate limiting-decision and spectral arguments, while Bai (2023), Armstrong (2022), and Aronow and Lopatto (2026) study different restrictions. Hammer et al. (1996) supplies only prospective motivation for a complete fixed-horizon binary-endpoint regimen trial; no reanalysis of censored ACTG175 outcomes is claimed.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:fixed-arm-count]\label{ass:fixed-arm-count}
\(K\ge3\) is fixed as \(n\to\infty\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Fixed-treatment finite-population asymptotics, \cite{li_ding_2017_clt}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:zero-sum-contrast]\label{ass:zero-sum-contrast}
\(\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_K}c_a=0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Finite-population treatment contrast, \cite{mukerjee_dasgupta_rubin_2018}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:l1-normalization]\label{ass:l1-normalization}
\(\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_K}|c_a|=2\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Scale normalization for a treatment contrast, \cite{mukerjee_dasgupta_rubin_2018}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:three-active-arms]\label{ass:three-active-arms}
\(|S_c|\ge3\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Nondegenerate multiarm contrast restriction, \cite{mukerjee_dasgupta_rubin_2018}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:outcome-independent-design]\label{ass:outcome-independent-design}
\(\mathcal D_n\) is selected without observing \(y\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Outcome-independent randomized design, \cite{sudijono_dobriban_tchetgen_2026_sharp}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{definition}[def:risk-game]\label{def:risk-game}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Unrestricted labeled-\allowbreak{}schedule minimax game}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} \[R_n(c)=\inf_{\mathcal D_n,\widehat\tau}\max_{y\in\{0,1\}^{n\times K}}\mathbb E_{\mathcal D_n}\!\left[\{\widehat\tau((A_i,Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}})_{i=1}^n)-\tau_c(y)\}^2\right].\]
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{n}; \texttt{K}; \texttt{c}; \texttt{y}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathcal D\_\allowbreak{}n}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}widehat\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}tau}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:response-cost]\label{def:response-cost}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Finite response-\allowbreak{}alphabet cost}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For every \(\xi\in\mathbb R^{S_c}\), set \[V_c(\xi)=\min\left\{\sum_{t\in\mathcal T_c}\lambda_t\theta_t^2:\lambda_t\ge0,\ \sum_{t\in\mathcal T_c}\lambda_tb_t=\xi\right\},\qquad \kappa(c)=V_c(v).\]
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{c}; \texttt{v}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathcal T\_\allowbreak{}c}; \texttt{b\_\allowbreak{}t}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}theta\_\allowbreak{}t}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:dual-score]\label{def:dual-score}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Certified full-\allowbreak{}label dual score}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Choose the lexicographically least solution \(\ell^\star\) of \[\max_{\ell\in\mathbb R^{S_c}}\ell^\top v\quad\text{subject to}\quad |\ell^\top b_t|\le\theta_t^2\ \text{for every }t\in\mathcal T_c,\] and put \(g^\star=\ell^\star/\kappa(c)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{v}; \texttt{b\_\allowbreak{}t}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}theta\_\allowbreak{}t}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathcal T\_\allowbreak{}c}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}kappa(c)}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:three-arm-orbit-likelihood]\label{def:three-arm-orbit-likelihood}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Exact unequal-\allowbreak{}three-\allowbreak{}arm orbit experiment}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For \(m\in\mathcal M_{n,3}\), \(r\in\mathcal R_{n,3}\), and \(x^{\mathrm{obs}}\in\prod_{a=1}^3\{0,\ldots,r_a\}\), let \(\mathcal H(m,r,x^{\mathrm{obs}})\) contain the nonnegative integer tables \(H=(H_{t,a})\) satisfying \(\sum_aH_{t,a}=m_t\), \(\sum_tH_{t,a}=r_a\), and \(\sum_{t:t_a=1}H_{t,a}=x_a^{\mathrm{obs}}\). Define \[P_m(x^{\mathrm{obs}}\mid r)=\frac{\prod_{a=1}^3r_a!}{n!}\sum_{H\in\mathcal H(m,r,x^{\mathrm{obs}})}\prod_{t\in\{0,1\}^3}\frac{m_t!}{\prod_{a=1}^3H_{t,a}!},\qquad \tau_{c^\dagger}(m)=\frac1n\sum_{t\in\{0,1\}^3}m_t(c^\dagger)^\top t.\]
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{m}; \texttt{r}; \texttt{X\textasciicircum{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathrm\{obs\}\allowbreak{}\}\allowbreak{}}; \texttt{n}; \texttt{c\textasciicircum{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}dagger}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:explicit-three-arm-tube]\label{def:explicit-three-arm-tube}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Explicit nonzero transverse prior}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Represent \(\alpha_n=n^{-1/3}\) by the square-free polynomial \(nX^3-1\) and its rational isolating interval. For each \(\sigma=(\sigma_x,\sigma_h)\in\{-,+\}^2\), put \(Q_\sigma=\{(x,h):0\le\sigma_xx\le T,\ 0\le\sigma_hh\le\varepsilon\}\) and, for every \(t\in\{0,1\}^3\), \[p_{n,x,h,t}^\dagger=p_{0,t}^\dagger+\alpha_na_{\sigma,t}(x,h),\qquad a_{\sigma,t}(x,h)=|x|\lambda_{v,t}^{\sigma_x}+|h|\lambda_{d,t}^{\sigma_h}-(3|x|+2|h|)p_{0,t}^\dagger.\] Draw \(X^\dagger\) with density \(w_T\), draw \(H^\dagger\) independently with density \(\rho_\varepsilon\), draw \(m\sim\operatorname{Multinomial}(n,p_{n,X^\dagger,H^\dagger}^\dagger)\), and draw a labeled schedule uniformly within its response-count orbit. The sum of the four exact cell integrals defines \(\nu_{n,\varepsilon,T}^\dagger\) in the ordered field \(\mathbb Q(\alpha_n)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{n}; \texttt{T}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}varepsilon}; \texttt{p\_\allowbreak{}0\textasciicircum{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}dagger}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}lambda\_\allowbreak{}v\textasciicircum{}+\allowbreak{}}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}lambda\_\allowbreak{}v\textasciicircum{}-\allowbreak{}}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}lambda\_\allowbreak{}d\textasciicircum{}+\allowbreak{}}; \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}lambda\_\allowbreak{}d\textasciicircum{}-\allowbreak{}}.

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}nu\_\allowbreak{}\{n,\allowbreak{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}varepsilon,\allowbreak{}T\}\allowbreak{}\textasciicircum{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}dagger}; \texttt{P\_\allowbreak{}m(X\textasciicircum{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathrm\{obs\}\allowbreak{}\}\allowbreak{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mid r)}; \texttt{c\textasciicircum{}\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}dagger}.

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Import \texttt{thm:exact-response-type-game} from the accepted parent \texttt{exp\_multiarm\_secondorder\_minimax\_frontier}: averaging any outcome-independent labeled design and estimator over common unit permutations is lossless; the resulting design is a count law \(\pi\in\Delta(\mathcal R_{n,3})\) followed, conditional on \(r\), by the uniform fixed-count assignment experiment; and the unrestricted labeled-schedule minimax value equals the finite response-type orbit game.

\begin{definition}[def:tube-coefficient-extractor]\label{def:tube-coefficient-extractor}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Finite-\allowbreak{}lattice sequence coefficient and remainder operator}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For each residue \(\ell=n\bmod8\), put \(h_n=n^{-5/6}\), \(\mathcal U_{n,\ell}=\{h_n(r-nq^\dagger):r\in\mathcal R_{n,3}\}\), and let \(\mathcal I_{n,\ell}\) be the canonical piecewise-affine interpolant on the fixed Freudenthal triangulation of the translated \(A_2\) lattice containing \(\mathcal U_{n,\ell}\). \begin{lemma}[lem:response-cost-geometry]\label{lem:response-cost-geometry}
The program defining \(V_c\) is feasible and attains its value for every \(\xi\in\mathbb R^{S_c}\). It is finite, even, convex, continuous, and positively homogeneous, with dual representation \[V_c(\xi)=\max\{\ell^\top \xi:|\ell^\top b_t|\le\theta_t^2\text{ for every }t\in\mathcal T_c\}.\] Moreover, \(0<\min_{t:\theta_t\ne0}|\theta_t|\le\kappa(c)\le1\), and the selected score obeys \((g^\star)^\top v=1\), \((g^\star)^\top\mathbf1=0\), and \(|(g^\star)^\top b_t|\le\theta_t^2/\kappa(c)\) for all \(t\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write \(d=|S_c|\), index the columns by \(t\in\mathcal T_c\), and let \(B\) be the \(d\times2^d\) matrix with columns \(b_t\).  Put \(a_t=\theta_t^2\).  The assumptions give
\[
c^\top v=\frac12\sum_{a\in S_c}|c_a|=1.
\]
The columns span \(\mathbb R^{S_c}\): if \(e_a\) is a coordinate vector, then
\[
e_a=b_{e_a}+b_{\mathbf1},\qquad -e_a=b_{\mathbf1-e_a}+b_{\mathbf0}.
\]
Thus every signed coordinate expansion can be rewritten with nonnegative coefficients, so \(B\lambda=\xi\), \(\lambda\ge0\), is feasible for every \(\xi\).  Its objective is nonnegative and finite on a feasible point.

For completeness, we derive attainment and duality rather than assuming them.  Consider the finitely generated cone
\[
\mathcal C=\operatorname{cone}\bigl(\{(b_t,a_t):t\in\mathcal T_c\}\cup\{(0,1)\}\bigr)
 \subset\mathbb R^{d+1}.
\]
This cone is closed.  Indeed, by repeatedly deleting a linear dependence from a conic representation while preserving nonnegativity, every element has a representation using at most \(d+1\) linearly independent generators.  Along a convergent sequence one may pass to one of the finitely many fixed generator sets; the coefficients then converge because the inverse on the span of that linearly independent set is continuous.  If \(p=V_c(\xi)\), feasible sequences show that \((\xi,p)\) is in the closure of \(\mathcal C\), hence in \(\mathcal C\).  In a representation
\[
(\xi,p)=\sum_t\lambda_t(b_t,a_t)+r(0,1),\qquad \lambda_t,r\ge0,
\]
one has \(\sum_ta_t\lambda_t\le p\); the definition of \(p\) forces equality and \(r=0\).  This proves primal attainment.

Weak duality follows immediately: if \(|\ell^\top b_t|\le a_t\), then for every primal-feasible \(\lambda\),
\[
\ell^\top\xi=\sum_t\lambda_t\ell^\top b_t
 \le \sum_t\lambda_ta_t.
\]
For the reverse inequality, fix \(q<p\).  The point \((\xi,q)\) is outside the closed convex cone \(\mathcal C\).  The elementary closest-point separation argument in Euclidean space supplies \((z,\beta)\ne0\) such that
\[
z^\top b_t+\beta a_t\ge0,\qquad \beta\ge0,\qquad
z^\top\xi+\beta q<0.
\]
Here \(\beta>0\): if \(\beta=0\), the inequalities for a type and its complement give \(z^\top b_t=0\) for every \(t\), and the spanning calculation above gives \(z=0\), a contradiction.  Set \(\ell=-z/\beta\).  Complementary types have \(b_{\mathbf1-t}=-b_t\) and \(a_{\mathbf1-t}=a_t\), whence
\[
|\ell^\top b_t|\le a_t,\qquad \ell^\top\xi>q.
\]
Letting \(q\uparrow p\), compactness of the dual feasible set, proved below, gives a limit attaining \(p\).  This establishes the displayed dual representation.

The type \(\mathbf0\) has \(a_{\mathbf0}=0\) and \(b_{\mathbf0}=-\mathbf1/2\), so every dual-feasible \(\ell\) satisfies \(\ell^\top\mathbf1=0\).  For the singleton type \(e_a\), this identity gives \(\ell^\top b_{e_a}=\ell_a\), hence \(|\ell_a|\le c_a^2\).  The dual set is therefore closed and bounded, hence compact.  It follows in particular that the maximum is attained; successively minimizing coordinates over compact optimizer faces also makes the stipulated lexicographically least optimizer well defined.

Positive homogeneity for nonnegative scalars and subadditivity follow by scaling and adding primal feasible vectors.  Evenness follows by replacing every coefficient at \(t\) by the same coefficient at \(\mathbf1-t\).  Consequently \(V_c\) is convex.  The displayed coordinate representations give a finite constant \(C_c\) such that \(V_c(z)\le C_c\|z\|_1\).  Subadditivity and evenness therefore yield
\[
|V_c(z)-V_c(z')|\le V_c(z-z')\le C_c\|z-z'\|_1,
\]
which proves finiteness and continuity.

The finite set \(\{t:\theta_t\ne0\}\) is nonempty because the type equal to one on the positive coordinates of \(c\) has score one.  Let
\(m_c=\min_{t:\theta_t\ne0}|\theta_t|>0\).  For every representation of \(v\),
\[
1=c^\top v=\sum_t\lambda_t\theta_t,
\qquad
\sum_t\lambda_t\theta_t^2
 \ge m_c\sum_t\lambda_t|\theta_t|
 \ge m_c.
\]
Thus \(\kappa(c)\ge m_c>0\).  If \(t^+_a=\mathbf1\{c_a>0\}\), then \(b_{t^+}=v\) and \(\theta_{t^+}=1\), so the single coefficient \(\lambda_{t^+}=1\) proves \(\kappa(c)\le1\).  Strong duality at \(v\) gives \((\ell^\star)^\top v=\kappa(c)\).  Dividing by the now-proved positive number \(\kappa(c)\), and using the zero-type and dual constraints, gives
\[
(g^\star)^\top v=1,\qquad (g^\star)^\top\mathbf1=0,\qquad
|(g^\star)^\top b_t|\le\theta_t^2/\kappa(c),
\]
as required.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This finite-alphabet gauge is the first named multiarm object and its dual is directly observable from assignment labels. \textit{Gap.} Sudijono, Dobriban, and Tchetgen Tchetgen reduce the two-arm game to a scalar binomial experiment; their Theorems 2.1--2.3 contain no \(2^J\)-type gauge or full-label dual score. \textit{Consumer.} The dual inequalities supply the uniform variance-pressure bound needed by both the attaining estimator and the tube-prior converse.

\begin{theorem}[thm:directional-spectral-value]\label{thm:directional-spectral-value}
The independently defined directional form satisfies \[\inf_{\|\psi\|_2=1}\mathcal Q_c(\psi)=\Lambda_c=C_A\kappa(c)^{2/3}.\] The equality remains valid when \(V_c\) has flat transverse directions; no normalized full-dimensional ground state is required.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(d=|S_c|\) and \(\kappa=\kappa(c)>0\), with positivity supplied by the response-cost geometry lemma.  The dual optimizer gives, for every \(z\in\mathbb R^{S_c}\),
\[
V_c(z)\ge (\ell^\star)^\top z=\kappa(g^\star)^\top z.
\]
Applying the same inequality to \(-z\) and using evenness yields
\[
V_c(z)\ge\kappa|(g^\star)^\top z|. \tag{1}
\]
Moreover \((g^\star)^\top v=1\).

First take \(\psi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^{S_c})\).  Use the linear coordinates
\(z=xv+w\), where \(x=(g^\star)^\top z\) and \(w\in\ker((g^\star)^\top)\).  Their constant nonzero Jacobian is denoted by \(J_g\).  Along each fiber, \(\partial_v\psi(xv+w)=\partial_x\psi(xv+w)\).  By the definition of \(C_A\) and the change of variables \(s=\kappa^{1/3}x\), every one-dimensional \(f\) in the scalar form domain satisfies
\[
\int_{\mathbb R}\{4|f'(x)|^2+\kappa|x||f(x)|^2\}\,dx
\ge C_A\kappa^{2/3}\int_{\mathbb R}|f(x)|^2\,dx. \tag{2}
\]
Applying (2) on every fiber, using (1), and then using Fubini gives
\[
\mathcal Q_c(\psi)
\ge C_A\kappa^{2/3}\|\psi\|_2^2.
\]
The form in \(\mathrm{def:directional-form}\) is the closure of this core; lower semicontinuity therefore extends the inequality to its entire closed form domain.  This proves the lower bound.

For the reverse bound, use the different direct sum
\(z=xv+u\), \(u\in\ker(c^\top)\), which is valid because \(c^\top v=1\).  Let its constant Jacobian be \(J_c\).  Given \(\zeta>0\), the definition and scaling of \(C_A\) provide \(f\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)\), \(\|f\|_2=1\), such that
\[
\int\{4|f'|^2+\kappa|x|f^2\}\le C_A\kappa^{2/3}+\zeta.
\]
Choose \(\rho\in C_c^\infty(\ker(c^\top))\) with \(\|\rho\|_2=1\), and for \(a>0\) put
\(\rho_a(u)=a^{-(d-1)/2}\rho(u/a)\).  The normalized product
\[
\psi_a(xv+u)=J_c^{-1/2}f(x)\rho_a(u)
\]
is smooth and compactly supported.  Evenness, positive homogeneity, and subadditivity of \(V_c\) give
\[
V_c(xv+u)\le |x|V_c(v)+V_c(u)=\kappa|x|+V_c(u).
\]
There is no derivative penalty in the transverse \(u\) directions, and homogeneity gives
\[
\mathcal Q_c(\psi_a)
\le C_A\kappa^{2/3}+\zeta
+a\int_{\ker(c^\top)}V_c(w)|\rho(w)|^2\,dw.
\]
The last integral is finite because \(\rho\) has compact support and \(V_c\) is continuous.  Letting \(a\downarrow0\) and then \(\zeta\downarrow0\) proves the upper bound.  This concentrating sequence is valid whether or not the transverse potential has flat directions; it makes no assertion that a normalized full-dimensional minimizer exists.  Combining the bounds gives the claimed value \(\Lambda_c=C_A\kappa(c)^{2/3}\).
\end{proof}

\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[thm:qstar-uniform-risk-upper]\label{thm:qstar-uniform-risk-upper}
Suppose \(K\ge3\) is fixed, \(\sum_ac_a=0\), \(\sum_a|c_a|=2\), and \(|S_c|\ge3\).  The estimator \(\delta_n^\star\) is total.  There is a finite constant \(B_c\), depending only on the fixed contrast, such that for every \(n\ge1\),
\[
\max_{y\in\{0,1\}^{n\times K}}\mathbb E\!\left[(\delta_n^\star-\tau_c(y))^2\right]
\le \frac1n-\Lambda_cn^{-4/3}+B_cn^{-3/2}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}n^{4/3}\{n^{-1}-R_n(c)\}\ge\Lambda_c,
\qquad
\liminf_{n\to\infty}n^{4/3}\{n^{-1}-R_n^{\mathrm{ind}}(c)\}\ge\Lambda_c.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Only active arms have positive probability and, on them,
\(q_a^\star=|c_a|/2>0\); hence every displayed ratio in the procedure is defined on its support.  The null-set convention makes the estimator total.  Since the two normalization assumptions imply \(\tau_c(y)\in[-1,1]\), projection onto that interval cannot increase squared loss.

Fix a schedule and put
\[
\theta_i=c^\top(Y_i-\mathbf1/2),\qquad
\gamma_i=(g^\star)^\top(Y_i-\mathbf1/2),
\qquad G_c=\max_{a\in S_c}\left|\frac{g_a^\star}{c_a}\right|.
\]
The support is finite and every denominator is nonzero, so \(G_c<\infty\).  Direct expectation over the independent arm label gives
\[
\mathbb ET_i=\theta_i,\quad \mathbb EU_i=\gamma_i,\quad
\mathbb ET_i^2=1,\quad \mathbb E(T_iU_i)=1,
\tag{1}
\]
where the last two identities use \(q_a^\star=|c_a|/2\),
\(\sum_a|c_a|=2\), and \((g^\star)^\top v=1\).  Also
\(|T_i|=1\), \(|U_i|\le G_c\), and \(|\gamma_i|\le G_c\).

Let \(H_0,H_1,H_2,D<\infty\) bound, respectively,
\(\|h_c'\|_\infty\), the Lipschitz constants of \(h_c'\) and \(h_c^2\), and
\(|xh_c'(x)|/\sqrt{|x|}\).  Their finiteness follows from the unit Airy feedback bounds and the fixed rescaling by \(\kappa(c)>0\).  With
\[
X=\sum_i(T_i-\theta_i),\quad W=\sum_i(U_i-\gamma_i),
\quad Q=\sum_i\theta_i^2,\quad P=\sum_i\theta_i\gamma_i,
\quad r=Q/n,\quad u=n^{-2/3}\sum_i\gamma_i,
\]
independence and expansion of the fourth moment give the explicit bounds
\[
\mathbb EX^2\le n,\quad \mathbb EW^2\le G_c^2n,\quad
\mathbb EW^4\le19G_c^4n^2,\quad
\mathbb E(|X|W^2)\le5G_c^2n^{3/2}.
\tag{2}
\]
Taylor's formula with Lipschitz derivative gives a remainder bounded by
\(H_1n^{-4/3}W^2/2\), while the Lipschitz bound for \(h_c^2\) gives
\(H_2n^{-2/3}|W|\).  Substitution into the exact squared-error identity and use of \((1)\)--\((2)\) therefore give, without asymptotic notation,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E(\delta_{n,\mathrm{unproj}}^\star-\tau_c(y))^2
\le{}&\frac1n-\frac rn+n^{-4/3}
\{h_c(u)^2-2h_c'(u)(1-P/n)\}\\
&+(5G_c^2H_1+G_cH_2)n^{-3/2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
The dual constraint yields
\[
r\ge\kappa(c)n^{-1/3}|u|,\qquad |P|/n\le r/\kappa(c).
\tag{4}
\]
Use the Riccati identity
\(h_c^2-2h_c'=\kappa(c)|u|-\Lambda_c\), and write
\(d=r-\kappa(c)n^{-1/3}|u|\ge0\).  The excess in \((3)\) above the claimed Airy main term is at most
\[
-d/n+2H_0d\,n^{-4/3}/\kappa(c)
+2D\sqrt{|u|}\,n^{-5/3}
+(5G_c^2H_1+G_cH_2)n^{-3/2}.
\tag{5}
\]
Since \(|u|\le G_cn^{1/3}\), the third term is at most
\(2D\sqrt{G_c}\,n^{-3/2}\).  For
\(n\ge N_c:=\max\{1,\lceil(2H_0/\kappa(c))^3\rceil\}\), the first two terms in \((5)\) have nonpositive sum.  Hence the claimed inequality holds on this tail with the finite displayed constant
\[
5G_c^2H_1+G_cH_2+2D\sqrt{G_c}.
\]
For the finite prefix, projected loss is at most four; enlarging the same constant by
\(4N_c^2+\Lambda_cN_c\) gives a finite \(B_c\) valid for every \(n\ge1\).

The independent procedure is feasible both in the restricted and unrestricted games, so
\[
R_n(c)\le R_n^{\mathrm{ind}}(c)
\le\max_y\mathbb E[(\delta_n^\star-\tau_c(y))^2].
\]
Subtracting from \(n^{-1}\), multiplying by \(n^{4/3}>0\), and taking lower limits proves both concluding inequalities.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:qstar-full-label-bayes-converse]\label{lem:qstar-full-label-bayes-converse}
For each fixed permitted contrast \(c\), consider the fixed design under which the assignments are independent with probabilities \(q^\star\).  For every positive rational \(\eta\), there is an integer \(N=N(c,\eta)\) such that, for every \(n\ge N\),
\[
R_n^{\mathrm{ind}}(c)
\ge \frac1n-\{\Lambda_c+\eta\}n^{-4/3}.
\]
The bound applies to every possibly randomized estimator using the complete assignment labels and observed outcomes.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write \(S=S_c\), \(s_a=\operatorname{sign}(c_a)\), \(q_a=|c_a|/2\) for \(a\in S\), and \(\kappa=\kappa(c)\).  The support assumption and the definition of \(S\) imply \(q_a>0\) on \(S\), while the normalization gives \(\sum_{a\in S}q_a=1\).  Inactive arms have design probability zero and will be assigned potential outcome zero in the prior below.

By the primal attainment assertion in \(\mathrm{lem:response\text{-}cost\text{-}geometry}\), choose a nonnegative measure \(\lambda^+\) on \(\mathcal T_c\) such that
\[
\sum_t\lambda_t^+b_t=v,
\qquad
\sum_t\lambda_t^+\theta_t^2=\kappa.
\]
Let \(M=\sum_t\lambda_t^+<\infty\), and define its complement by
\(\lambda_t^-=\lambda_{mathbf1-t}^+\).  Since
\(b_{mathbf1-t}=-b_t\) and, by the zero-sum assumption,
\(\theta_{mathbf1-t}=-\theta_t\),
\[
\sum_t\lambda_t^-b_t=-v,
\qquad
\sum_t\lambda_t^-\theta_t^2=\kappa,
\qquad
\sum_t\lambda_t^-=M.
\]
Put \(p_0=(\delta_{mathbf0}+\delta_{mathbf1})/2\).  Both atoms in \(p_0\) have \(\theta_t=0\).

Fix a positive rational \(\eta\).  From \(\mathrm{def:airy\text{-}benchmark}\), the normalized ground state \(\phi_c\) has finite energy
\[
4\int_{\mathbb R}|\phi_c'(x)|^2\,dx
+\kappa\int_{\mathbb R}|x|\phi_c(x)^2\,dx=\Lambda_c.
\]
Multiplying \(\phi_c\) by cutoffs that equal one on \([-T+1,T-1]\), then normalizing and smoothing inside \((-T,T)\), shows directly by dominated convergence and the product rule that there are a finite \(T\) and a nonnegative \(\varphi\in C_c^1((-T,T))\) such that
\[
\int\varphi^2=1,
\qquad
I_\varphi+\kappa m_\varphi\le\Lambda_c+\eta/2,
\qquad
I_\varphi=4\int|\varphi'|^2,quad
m_\varphi=\int|x|\varphi(x)^2\,dx.
\]
Indeed, the derivative of a cutoff contributes only on the tails, where both the \(H^1\) mass and the weighted \(L^2\) mass tend to zero; smoothing on the resulting compact interval preserves the two displayed integrals.  Thus no normalized full-dimensional ground state is being used.

Set \(\alpha=n^{-1/3}\) and let \(X\) have density
\(\pi(x)=\varphi(x)^2\).  For \(x\ge0\) and \(x<0\), respectively, define the response-type laws
\[
p_{n,x}=(1-\alpha|x|M)p_0+\alpha|x|\lambda^+,
\qquad
p_{n,x}=(1-\alpha|x|M)p_0+\alpha|x|\lambda^-.
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\), \(\alpha TM\le1\), so these are probability measures; this is the only positivity condition needed for the prior.  Conditional on \(X=x\), draw the active response types \(T_1,\ldots,T_n\) independently from \(p_{n,x}\), extend each type by zero on inactive arms, and then draw the assignments independently from \(q\), independently of the types.  Integrating out \(X\) gives a genuine prior on the finite set of complete binary schedules, fixed independently of the randomization design.

For \(\Theta_i=c^\top b_{T_i}\), the preceding moment identities and \(c^\top v=1\) give exactly
\[
\mathbb E(\Theta_i\mid X=x)=\alpha x,
\qquad
\mathbb E(\Theta_i^2\mid X=x)=\alpha\kappa|x|.
\tag{1}
\]
Because \(\mathbf1^\top c=0\), the realized finite-population estimand under this prior is not replaced by a prior mean: it is exactly
\[
H_n:=\tau_c(y)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\Theta_i.
\tag{2}
\]

On every positive-probability observation define
\[
Z_i=s_{A_i}(2Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}-1)\in\{-1,1\},
\qquad \overline Z_n=n^{-1}\sum_iZ_i,
\qquad p=\alpha X.
\]
For each \(a\in S\), the first identity in (1) at the vector level yields
\[
\mathbb E\{s_a(2T_{i,a}-1)\mid X=x\}
=2s_a\alpha x v_a=\alpha x.
\]
Consequently the full one-unit observation \(O_i=(A_i,Z_i)\) satisfies
\[
\Pr(O_i=(a,z)\mid X=x)=q_a\frac{1+pz}{2}.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus the assignment labels are ancillary for \(X\), but they have not been discarded from estimation of the realized target \(H_n\).  Conditional on \(X\), the \(Z_i\) are independent Rademacher variables with mean \(p\).

Let \(\mathscr Z_n=\sigma(Z_1,\ldots,Z_n)\) and
\(\mu_n=\mathbb E(p\mid\mathscr Z_n)\).  We first calculate the scalar Bayes risk for \(p\).  Define
\[
\rho(x)=\frac{\pi'(x)}{\pi(x)}=\frac{2\varphi'(x)}{\varphi(x)}
\quad\hbox{when }\varphi(x)>0,
\]
and set it to zero on the null set where \(\varphi=0\).  Then
\[
\mathbb E\rho(X)^2=4\int|\varphi'(x)|^2\,dx=I_\varphi.
\tag{4}
\]
For each realized \(Z_1,ldots,Z_n\), integration by parts in \(x\) has no boundary term because \(\pi\) is compactly supported and vanishes at its boundary.  Since
\[
\frac{z}{1+pz}=\frac{z-p}{1-p^2},\qquad z\in\{-1,1\},
\]
the posterior score identity is
\[
\mathbb E\!\left[
\rho(X)+n\alpha\frac{\overline Z_n-p}{1-p^2}
\,\middle|\,\mathscr Z_n\right]=0.
\tag{5}
\]
The division is valid for all sufficiently large \(n\), because
\(|p|\le\alpha T<1/2\).

Let \(G_n=\mathbb E\{\rho(X)\mid\mathscr Z_n\}\).  From (5),
\[
n\alpha(\overline Z_n-\mu_n)=-G_n-E_n,
\quad
E_n=n\alpha\,\mathbb E\!\left[
\frac{p^2(\overline Z_n-p)}{1-p^2}
\,\middle|\,\mathscr Z_n\right].
\tag{6}
\]
Conditional Jensen's inequality, followed by
\(\mathbb E\{(\overline Z_n-p)^2\mid X\}=(1-p^2)/n\), gives
\[
\mathbb E E_n^2
\le n^2\alpha^2\mathbb E\!\left[
\frac{p^4(\overline Z_n-p)^2}{(1-p^2)^2}\right]
=n\alpha^2\mathbb E\!\left[\frac{p^4}{1-p^2}\right]
\le C_T n\alpha^6=O(n^{-1}).
\tag{7}
\]

It remains to identify the limit of \(G_n\) without assuming a limit experiment.  Put
\(\widehat X_n=\operatorname{proj}_{[-T,T]}(\overline Z_n/\alpha)\).  Since projection cannot increase distance from \(X\in[-T,T]\),
\[
\mathbb E(\widehat X_n-X)^2
\le\alpha^{-2}\mathbb E(\overline Z_n-p)^2
\le(n\alpha^2)^{-1}=n^{-1/3}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{8}
\]
For any bounded continuous \(f\) on \([-T,T]\), (8) and uniform continuity imply \(f(\widehat X_n)\to f(X)\) in \(L^2\).  Approximating the \(L^2(\pi)\) function \(\rho\) by such functions and using that conditional expectation is the \(L^2\) projection onto \(\mathscr Z_n\) gives
\[
\mathbb E\{\rho(X)-G_n\}^2\longrightarrow0,
\qquad
\mathbb EG_n^2\longrightarrow I_\varphi.
\tag{9}
\]
Equations (6)--(9) imply
\[
n^2\alpha^2\mathbb E(\overline Z_n-\mu_n)^2\longrightarrow I_\varphi.
\tag{10}
\]
The conditional-mean projection identity and the conditional variance of \(\overline Z_n\) now give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E(p-\mu_n)^2
&=\mathbb E(\overline Z_n-p)^2
-\mathbb E(\overline Z_n-\mu_n)^2\\
&=\frac1n-I_\varphi n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}),
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
because \(\mathbb E(p^2)/n=O(n^{-5/3})\).

We next pass from the latent mean \(p\) to the actual finite-population target (2).  Write \(W_n=H_n-p\), let \(\mathscr O_n=\sigma(O_1,\ldots,O_n)\), and put
\(\zeta_n=\mathbb E(H_n\mid\mathscr O_n)\).  For a one-unit observation \(o\), define
\[
d_x(o)=\mathbb E(\Theta_i-p\mid X=x,O_i=o).
\]
At \(p_0\), \(\Theta_i=0\).  The total variation of
\(p_{n,x}-p_0\) is bounded by a constant times \(\alpha|x|\), and (3) bounds every conditional binary-outcome denominator below by \(1/4\) once \(\alpha T<1/2\).  Hence, for a finite constant depending only on the fixed contrast and the fixed \(\eta\)-dependent prior,
\[
|d_x(o)|\le C_{c,\eta}\alpha|x|.
\tag{12}
\]
Also \(\mathbb E\{d_x(O_i)\mid X=x\}=0\).  Conditional on \((X,O_1,\ldots,O_n)\), the response types remain independent across units, so conditional Jensen and (12) yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E(\zeta_n-\mu_n)^2
&=\mathbb E\!\left[\mathbb E(W_n\mid\mathscr O_n)^2\right]\\
&\le\mathbb E\!\left[\mathbb E(W_n\mid X,\mathscr O_n)^2\right]
=\frac1n\mathbb E\{d_X(O_1)^2\}
\le C_{c,\eta}\frac{\alpha^2}{n}=O(n^{-5/3}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
Here \(\mu_n=\mathbb E(p\mid\mathscr O_n)\) as well, by the ancillarity in (3).

The remaining covariance is exact.  Conditional on a response type \(t\),
\[
\mathbb E(Z_i\mid T_i=t)
=\sum_{a\in S}\frac{|c_a|}{2}s_a(2t_a-1)
=\sum_{a\in S}c_at_a-\frac12\sum_{a\in S}c_a
=\theta_t.
\tag{14}
\]
Therefore, using independence across units conditional on \(X\),
\[
\mathbb E\{(p-\overline Z_n)W_n\mid X\}
=-\frac1n\operatorname{Var}(\Theta_i\mid X)
=-\mathbb E(W_n^2\mid X).
\tag{15}
\]
Moreover, (10), (13), and Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
\left|\mathbb E\{(\overline Z_n-\mu_n)W_n\}\right|
=\left|\mathbb E\{(\overline Z_n-\mu_n)(\zeta_n-\mu_n)\}\right|
=O(n^{-3/2})=o(n^{-4/3}).
\tag{16}
\]
Combining (1), (11), and (15)--(16),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E(H_n-\mu_n)^2
&=\mathbb E(p-\mu_n)^2+\mathbb EW_n^2
+2\mathbb E\{(p-\mu_n)W_n\}\\
&=\mathbb E(p-\mu_n)^2-\mathbb EW_n^2+o(n^{-4/3})\\
&=\frac1n-\{I_\varphi+\kappa m_\varphi\}n^{-4/3}
+o(n^{-4/3}),
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
because
\[
\mathbb EW_n^2
=\frac1n\mathbb E\{\alpha\kappa|X|-\alpha^2X^2\}
=\kappa m_\varphi n^{-4/3}+O(n^{-5/3}).
\]
Finally, posterior square completion and (13) show that the Bayes risk for the realized causal target is
\[
\mathcal B_n
:=\mathbb E(H_n-\zeta_n)^2
=\mathbb E(H_n-\mu_n)^2-\mathbb E(\zeta_n-\mu_n)^2
=\frac1n-\{I_\varphi+\kappa m_\varphi\}n^{-4/3}
+o(n^{-4/3}).
\tag{18}
\]
This calculation retains the full labels.  In particular, their finite-sample improvement over a scalar-only posterior is represented by the nonnegative term in (13), which is \(O(n^{-5/3})\) and therefore does not alter (18).

As the requested unequal-three-arm stress check, when
\(c=(1,-1/4,-3/4)\), one may take
\[
\lambda^+=\delta_{000}+\delta_{101}+\delta_{110},
\qquad M=3,
\qquad q=(1/2,1/8,3/8).
\]
Direct enumeration gives
\[
\sum_t\lambda_t^+b_t=(1/2,-1/2,-1/2)=v,
\qquad
\sum_t\lambda_t^+\theta_t^2=0+(1/4)^2+(3/4)^2=5/8.
\]
For every one of the eight response types, (14) holds exactly.  Hence all three transformed arm observations in (3) have the same slope \(\alpha x\), while the full label remains present in \(d_x(O_i)\).  The product calculation is unconditional over every assignment vector, including realized allocations with a zero arm count, and divides only by \(1-p^2\ge3/4\), never by a realized count.  Thus the fixed-design boundary allocations and the known finite-sample usefulness of labels are not removed by a sufficiency assumption; the latter is controlled by (13) only at the order needed here.

For every estimator, its maximum risk over fixed schedules is at least its average risk under the constructed schedule prior, and conditional Jensen shows that estimator randomization cannot lower the posterior squared-error risk below \(\mathcal B_n\).  Taking the infimum over all full-label estimators therefore gives
\[
R_n^{\mathrm{ind}}(c)\ge\mathcal B_n.
\]
The energy choice gives
\(I_\varphi+\kappa m_\varphi\le\Lambda_c+\eta/2\); after increasing \(N(c,\eta)\), the remainder in (18) is bounded below by \(-\eta n^{-4/3}/2\).  Hence, for every \(n\ge N(c,\eta)\),
\[
R_n^{\mathrm{ind}}(c)
\ge\frac1n-(\Lambda_c+\eta)n^{-4/3},
\]
which proves the lemma.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:airy-tube-permutation-orbit-bayes-lower]\label{lem:airy-tube-permutation-orbit-bayes-lower}
Assume \(\alpha_n(3T_k^\dagger+4\varepsilon)\le1\).  Under the face-robust permutation-invariant prior
\(\nu_{n,k,\varepsilon}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}\), every outcome-independent assignment law \(\mathcal D_n\) and every possibly randomized labeled-data estimator have Bayes risk at least
\[
\sum_{r\in\mathcal R_{n,3}}\pi(r)
\mathcal B_{n,k,\varepsilon}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}(r)
\ge B_{n,k,\varepsilon}^{\dagger,\mathrm A},
\]
where \(\pi\) is the allocation-count law induced by \(\mathcal D_n\).  Conditional on a fixed allocation count, the posterior mean of the exact finite-population target attains the displayed fixed-count Bayes risk.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(n,k,\varepsilon\) satisfying the positivity premise.  By the face-robust
tangent-score lemma, every conditional vector
\(p_{n,x,h,\zeta}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}\) is a probability law, and the realized
target associated with a response-count vector is the exact finite-population
contrast \(\tau_{c^\dagger}(m)=n^{-1}\sum_tm_t\theta_t\).  Conditional on the
three tube coordinates, a multinomial count followed by a uniform labeling in its
orbit produces independent labeled response types.  Indeed, if a labeled type
sequence has count vector \(m\), its probability is
\[
 \left\{\frac{n!}{\prod_tm_t!}\prod_t
 (p_{n,x,h,\zeta,t}^{\dagger,\mathrm A})^{m_t}\right\}
 \left\{\frac{\prod_tm_t!}{n!}\right\}
 =\prod_{i=1}^np_{n,x,h,\zeta,t_i}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}.
\tag{1}
\]
After the coordinates are integrated out, the resulting schedule prior is therefore
permutation invariant and, conditional on \(m\), uniform on the response-count
orbit.

Fix an assignment vector \(a_0\) whose allocation count is \(r\), and write
\(x\) for its arm-success count.  Conditional on \(m\), partitioning the unit
labels by assigned arm gives a response-type by arm table \(H\).  The number of
labeled type sequences having a particular feasible table is
\(\prod_a r_a!/\prod_{t,a}H_{t,a}!\), whereas the response-count orbit contains
\(n!/\prod_tm_t!\) sequences.  Summation over the feasible tables consequently
gives
\[
 \Pr_m(X^{\mathrm{obs}}=x\mid A=a_0)
 =\frac{\prod_ar_a!}{n!}
 \sum_{H\in\mathcal H(m,r,x)}
 \prod_t\frac{m_t!}{\prod_aH_{t,a}!}
 =P_m(x\mid r).
\tag{2}
\]
For completeness, a particular labeled outcome vector with count \(x\) has
probability equal to the left side of (2) divided by
\(\prod_a\binom{r_a}{x_a}\): permutations within the assigned-arm blocks act
transitively on such vectors and leave the prior invariant.  This divisor is
independent of \(m\).  It follows that, conditional on \(A=a_0\), the posterior
law of the invariant target depends on the full labeled outcomes only through
\((r,X^{\mathrm{obs}})\), and its likelihood is exactly (2).

Let \(D^{\mathrm A}_{r,x}\) and \(N^{\mathrm A}_{r,x}\) denote the predictive
mass and target-weighted predictive mass in the exact Airy-tube Bayes-risk
definition.  Whenever \(D^{\mathrm A}_{r,x}>0\), the posterior mean is
\[
 \bar\tau_{r,x}=\frac{N^{\mathrm A}_{r,x}}{D^{\mathrm A}_{r,x}}.
\tag{3}
\]
For an arbitrary action \(d\), conditional square completion gives
\[
 \mathbb E\!left[(d-\tau_{c^\dagger}(m))^2\mid r,x\right]
 =\operatorname{Var}\!\left(\tau_{c^\dagger}(m)\mid r,x\right)
 +\left(d-\bar\tau_{r,x}\right)^2.
\tag{4}
\]
If the estimator randomizes after seeing the data, averaging (4) over its Markov
kernel gives the same identity with the last square averaged, because that
randomization is conditionally independent of the schedule.  Thus the posterior
mean (3) minimizes conditional squared error.  Multiplying its posterior variance
by the predictive probabilities and summing yields
\[
 \sum_m\nu_{n,k,\varepsilon}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}(m)
       \tau_{c^\dagger}(m)^2
 -\sum_{x:D^{\mathrm A}_{r,x}>0}
       \frac{(N^{\mathrm A}_{r,x})^2}{D^{\mathrm A}_{r,x}}
 =\mathcal B_{n,k,\varepsilon}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}(r),
\tag{5}
\]
where (2) and the exact Bayes-risk definition justify the equality.  Hence every
possibly randomized labeled-data estimator has conditional Bayes risk at least
the right side of (5) for every fixed assignment vector with count \(r\).

Now draw \(A\sim\mathcal D_n\).  The outcome-independence assumption says that
\(A\) is independent of the fixed schedule before observing outcomes, so
conditioning on \(A=a_0\) does not alter the schedule prior.  Averaging (5) first
over assignment vectors and then grouping vectors by their count gives
\[
 \operatorname{BayesRisk}_{\nu_{n,k,\varepsilon}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}}
       (\mathcal D_n,\widehat\tau)
 \ge\sum_{r\in\mathcal R_{n,3}}\pi(r)
       \mathcal B_{n,k,\varepsilon}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}(r).
\tag{6}
\]
The coefficients \(\pi(r)\) are nonnegative and sum to one.  Therefore, by the
definition of the all-count certificate,
\[
 \sum_r\pi(r)\mathcal B_{n,k,\varepsilon}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}(r)
 \ge\min_{r\in\mathcal R_{n,3}}
       \mathcal B_{n,k,\varepsilon}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}(r)
 =B_{n,k,\varepsilon}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}.
\tag{7}
\]
Equations (6)--(7) hold for arbitrary dependence among the assignments and for
arbitrary randomized estimators, which proves the claim.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Permutation invariance and posterior square completion transfer the three-coordinate prior losslessly to every dependent labeled assignment design. \textit{Gap.} This exact finite reduction does not provide an asymptotic expansion. \textit{Consumer.} The fixed-input evaluator and all-design root use the Bayes lower certificate.

\begin{theorem}[thm:unequal-three-arm-qstar-liminf-corollary]\label{thm:unequal-three-arm-qstar-liminf-corollary}
For \(c^\dagger=(1,-1/4,-3/4)\),
\[
\Lambda_{c^\dagger}=C_A(5/8)^{2/3},
\qquad
\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{4/3}\{n^{-1}-R_n^{\mathrm{ind}}(c^\dagger)\}
=\Lambda_{c^\dagger},
\]
and
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}n^{4/3}\{n^{-1}-R_n(c^\dagger)\}
\ge\Lambda_{c^\dagger}>0.
\]
Here \(R_n^{\mathrm{ind}}\) uses the fixed independent allocation
\(q^\dagger=(1/2,1/8,3/8)\).  No equality between the unrestricted and fixed-design second-order values is asserted.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The exact-primitives proposition proves
\(\kappa(c^\dagger)=5/8\), so the Airy-benchmark definition gives
\[
\Lambda_{c^\dagger}=C_A\kappa(c^\dagger)^{2/3}
=C_A(5/8)^{2/3}>0. \tag{1}
\]
The already established theorem \(\mathrm{thm:qstar\text{-}second\text{-}order}\), applied to this fixed three-active-arm contrast, yields
\[
R_n^{\mathrm{ind}}(c^\dagger)
=\frac1n-\Lambda_{c^\dagger}n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}), \tag{2}
\]
which is equivalent to the displayed limit.  The unrestricted minimax game contains the fixed independent design among its feasible design actions; hence
\(R_n(c^\dagger)\le R_n^{\mathrm{ind}}(c^\dagger)\).  Subtracting this inequality from \(n^{-1}\), multiplying by the positive number \(n^{4/3}\), and taking lower limits proves the second display from (2).  The one-sided order is essential: neither this comparison nor (2) supplies the reverse inequality for the unrestricted game.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:effective-normalized-airy-record]\label{lem:effective-normalized-airy-record}
For every \(k\ge1\), the rational interval oracle and dovetailed search in \(\mathrm{def:rational\text{-}airy\text{-}longitudinal\text{-}family}\) terminate and return the complete spline record specified there.  In particular,
\[
d_\dagger(\varphi_k^\dagger,\phi_{c^\dagger})^2<2^{-2k},
\qquad
\frac{4\int| (\varphi_k^\dagger)'|^2+
\kappa(c^\dagger)\int |x|(\varphi_k^\dagger)^2}
{\int(\varphi_k^\dagger)^2}
\le\Lambda_{c^\dagger}+2^{-k}.
\]
Thus \(\varphi_k^\dagger\to\phi_{c^\dagger}\) in weighted \(H^1\), every \(w_k^\dagger\) is a compactly supported probability density with rational piecewise-quadratic coefficients, and its normalization and normalized energy are exact rational numbers.  No oracle for an arbitrary real number is assumed: all accepted inequalities are certified by rational intervals.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write \(\kappa=5/8\), \(\Phi=\phi_{c^\dagger}\), and \(\Lambda=\Lambda_{c^\dagger}\).  The exact-primitives proposition gives the value of \(\kappa\), and the Airy-benchmark definition makes \(\Phi\) the positive even unit-norm minimizer of
\[
\mathcal Q(f)=4\int_{\mathbb R}|f'|^2+
\kappa\int_{\mathbb R}|x|f^2. \tag{1}
\]
Taking compactly supported variations in (1) gives the weak Euler equation
\[
-4\Phi''(x)+\kappa|x|\Phi(x)=\Lambda\Phi(x). \tag{2}
\]
Since the coefficient in (2) is continuous on each half-line, integrating the distributional equation twice shows that \(\Phi\) is \(C^2\) there.  Evenness gives \(\Phi'(0)=0\), while positivity fixes its sign.

We first prove the interval bracket used by the oracle.  On \([0,R]\), denote the Neumann and Dirichlet Rayleigh infima by \(\lambda_N(R)\) and \(\lambda_D(R)\).  Extending a Dirichlet competitor by zero gives an admissible half-line competitor, so
\[
\Lambda\le\lambda_D(R). \tag{3}
\]
For any half-line form-domain function \(f\), splitting its energy at \(R\), using the Neumann variational inequality on \([0,R]\), and using \(\kappa x\ge\kappa R\) on \([R,\infty)\), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal Q_{[0,\infty)}(f)
&\ge \lambda_N(R)\int_0^R f^2+
\kappa R\int_R^\infty f^2\\
&\ge\min\{\lambda_N(R),\kappa R\}
\int_0^\infty f^2.
\end{aligned} \tag{4}
\]
Taking the infimum proves the lower bracket.  Compactly supported truncations of \(\Phi\) show from (1) that \(\lambda_D(R)\to\Lambda\).  To handle the Neumann side, take any sequence \(R_j\to\infty\) and normalized Neumann minimizers \(f_j\) with bounded energies.  For every fixed \(S\), their \(H^1(0,S)\) norms are bounded, while
\[
\int_S^{R_j}f_j^2\le\frac{\lambda_N(R_j)}{\kappa S}. \tag{5}
\]
For completeness, compactness here follows directly from the fundamental theorem of calculus.  If \(P_hg\) is the vector of cell averages on a partition of \([0,S]\) into intervals of length at most \(h\), then Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
\|g-P_hg\|_{L^2(0,S)}^2
\le h^2\|g'\|_{L^2(0,S)}^2. \tag{5a}
\]
For fixed \(h\), the finitely many cell-average vectors of the bounded sequence have a convergent subsequence; diagonal extraction as \(h\) decreases to zero and (5a) give an \(L^2(0,S)\)-convergent subsequence.  Successive extraction on integer \(S\), together with the tightness bound (5), gives a unit-norm half-line limit \(f\).  Weak lower semicontinuity of a squared norm follows from
\[
\|g\|_2^2=\sup_z\{2\langle g,z\rangle-\|z\|_2^2\},
\]
so applying this identity to \(f_j'\) and \(x^{1/2}f_j\) on each \([0,S]\), followed by monotone convergence in \(S\), yields
\[
\Lambda\le\mathcal Q(f)\le\liminf_j\lambda_N(R_j). \tag{6}
\]
Equations (4) and (6) imply \(\lambda_N(R)\to\Lambda\).  Hence the rational bracket in the definition becomes arbitrarily narrow.

For fixed rational \(R\), the initial-value equation in the definition has polynomial coefficients.  Picard's integral operator \(\mathcal P\) satisfies, on a step where \(\|A\|_\infty\le L\),
\[
\|\mathcal P^{m+1}W-\mathcal P^mW\|_\infty
\le \|W(x_0)\|_\infty
\frac{(L|h|)^{m+1}}{(m+1)!}. \tag{7}
\]
Summing (7) from \(m+1\) onward gives the displayed exponential remainder.  All coefficients and endpoints in (7) are rational intervals.  The Wronskian of two solutions satisfying the same left boundary condition is constant; consequently an eigenvalue satisfying either separated right boundary condition is simple.  If two spectral parameters are ordered, differentiating their Wronskian gives
\[
\frac{d}{dx}\{f_{\lambda_2}'f_{\lambda_1}
-f_{\lambda_2}f_{\lambda_1}'\}
=\frac{\lambda_1-\lambda_2}{4}f_{\lambda_1}f_{\lambda_2}, \tag{8}
\]
which orders the first boundary zero while both solutions are positive.  Thus a sufficiently fine rational space--parameter subdivision, interval Newton applied also to the differentiated equation, and the positivity checks isolate the first simple root.  Equations (3)--(8) prove termination of the eigenvalue-enclosure loop.

The tail formulas used by the oracle are exact.  On \([0,\infty)\), define
\[
F(x)=\Phi'(x)^2-\frac{\kappa x-\Lambda}{4}\Phi(x)^2.
\]
Equation (2) gives \(F'(x)=-\kappa\Phi(x)^2/4\).  Since the three nonnegative integrands in (1) are integrable, there is a sequence tending to infinity on which the boundary term tends to zero.  Integrating along that sequence and using \(\int_0^\infty\Phi^2=1/2\) gives
\[
\Phi(0)^2=\frac{\kappa}{2\Lambda}. \tag{9}
\]
For \(s>0\), put \(\Phi_s(x)=s^{1/2}\Phi(sx)\).  This preserves the unit norm, and minimality at \(s=1\) gives
\[
0=\left.\frac d{ds}\right|_{s=1}
\left\{4s^2\int|\Phi'|^2+
\kappa s^{-1}\int|x|\Phi^2\right\}
=8\int|\Phi'|^2-
\kappa\int|x|\Phi^2. \tag{10}
\]
Combining (1) at \(\Phi\) with (10) proves
\[
\int|\Phi'|^2=\frac{\Lambda}{12},
\qquad
\int|x|\Phi^2=\frac{2\Lambda}{3\kappa}. \tag{11}
\]
The augmented Picard system encloses the compact integrals.  Subtracting them from the exact totals (9)--(11) encloses all three tails.  Their true values tend to zero by integrability, while (7) makes the interval widths arbitrarily small.  Therefore the ODE and weighted-tail oracle terminates at every requested precision.

If \(p\) is a proposed compact spline, expansion of the squared distance gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
d_\dagger(p,\Phi)^2
={}&\int|p'|^2+\frac{\Lambda}{12}
-2\int p'\Phi'\\
&+\int(1+|x|)p^2
+1+\frac{2\Lambda}{3\kappa}
-2\int(1+|x|)p\Phi .
\end{aligned} \tag{11a}
\]
The spline-only terms are rational, and the cross terms are compact integrals enclosed by the \(J\)-equations in the oracle.  Hence (11a) supplies the claimed rational interval for the entire weighted distance.

Finally, even compactly supported rational piecewise-linear functions are dense in the form norm appearing in \(d_\dagger\): first truncate \(\Phi\) using the vanishing tails just proved, then polygonally interpolate on a rational mesh, and finally perturb the finitely many vertex values to rationals.  The interpolation and rational perturbation errors tend to zero in both the derivative norm and the weighted \(L^2\) norm.  The normalized Rayleigh quotient is continuous at the nonzero function \(\Phi\), so there is a candidate \(p\) satisfying
\[
d_\dagger(p,\Phi)^2<2^{-2k},
\qquad Q_\kappa(p)<\Lambda+2^{-k}. \tag{12}
\]
Because the inequalities in (12) are strict, first refine the eigenvalue lower endpoint until \(Q_\kappa(p)<L_\Lambda+2^{-k}\), and then refine the integral intervals until the distance upper endpoint is below \(2^{-2k}\).  The dovetailed enumeration therefore reaches an accepted record.  Its acceptance inequalities and \(L_\Lambda\le\Lambda\) imply
\[
Q_\kappa(\varphi_k^\dagger)
<L_\Lambda+2^{-k}\le\Lambda+2^{-k}. \tag{13}
\]
The distance certificate gives weighted \(H^1\) convergence.  Every integral involving the accepted rational spline is an exact rational polynomial integral, its squared norm is positive, and division by that norm proves all asserted properties of \(w_k^\dagger\).  This proves the lemma.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:corrected-airy-fixed-input-evaluator]\label{lem:corrected-airy-fixed-input-evaluator}

 \frac{n!}{\prod_tm_t!}

\tag{1}

Every endpoint and coefficient in (1), apart from the explicitly represented

spline pieces and sign cells, so

 \in\mathbb Q(\alpha_n)
 \quad\text{for every }m\in\mathcal M_{n,3}.
\tag{2}

The exact-primitives proposition makes the orbit likelihood and the count target

 \mathcal B_{n,k,\varepsilon}^{\dagger,\mathrm A}(r),\quad

All enumerations terminate because

 |\mathcal M_{n,3}|<\infty,
 \qquad |\mathcal R_{n,3}|<\infty,
 \qquad \prod_{a=1}^3(r_a+1)<\infty,
 \qquad |\mathcal H(m,r,x^{\mathrm{obs}})|<\infty.
\tag{4}
\]

We spell out the comparison certificate.  Factor the square-free polynomial
\(nX^3-1\) over \(\mathbb Q\), select the factor containing the unique positive

\tag{5}

\end{proof}

\qquad |R|\le\frac12\overline H_1^\dagger a_n^2W^2,
\tag{5}
\]
and
\[
|h(u+a_nW)^2-h(u)^2|
\le\overline H_2^\dagger a_n|W|.
\tag{6}
\]
Using \(\mathbb E(XW)=n-P\), substitution of \((4)\)--\((6)\) into the exact squared-error expansion yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E(\delta_{n,\mathrm{unproj}}^\star-\tau)^2
\le{}&\frac1n-\frac rn
+n^{-4/3}\{h(u)^2-2h'(u)(1-P/n)\}\\
&+\{5G^2\overline H_1^\dagger
+G\overline H_2^\dagger\}n^{-3/2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
No asymptotic remainder notation occurs in \((7)\).

The dual inequalities imply
\[
r\ge\frac58n^{-1/3}|u|,
\qquad |P|/n\le\frac85r.
\tag{8}
\]
Writing \(d=r-(5/8)n^{-1/3}|u|\ge0\) and using
\(h^2-2h'=(5/8)|u|-\Lambda_{c^\dagger}\), the excess in \((7)\) above
\(n^{-1}-\Lambda_{c^\dagger}n^{-4/3}\) is at most
\[
-\frac dn+\frac{2\overline H_0^\dagger}{5/8}d\,n^{-4/3}
+2|uh'(u)|n^{-5/3}
+\{5G^2\overline H_1^\dagger+G\overline H_2^\dagger\}n^{-3/2}.
\tag{9}
\]
For \(n\ge N_0^\dagger\), the first two terms are nonpositive by the definition of \(N_0^\dagger\).  Equation \((1)\) gives \(|u|\le n^{1/3}\), while the certified exterior/compact majorant gives
\[
2|uh'(u)|n^{-5/3}\le2\overline D^\dagger n^{-3/2}.
\tag{10}
\]
Thus the first three summands defining \(B_{\mathrm{eff}}^\dagger\) dominate the remainder for \(n\ge N_0^\dagger\).

For \(1\le n<N_0^\dagger\), the projected estimator and target both lie in \([-1,1]\), so risk is at most four.  Since
\(n^{3/2}\le(N_0^\dagger)^2\) and
\(\Lambda_{c^\dagger}n^{1/6}\le\overline\Lambda^\dagger N_0^\dagger\), the last two summands defining
\(B_{\mathrm{eff}}^\dagger\) dominate the finite prefix after multiplying the desired inequality by \(n^{3/2}\).  The asserted bound follows for every \(n\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This specializes the uniform Airy procedure to the rational contrast with a computable finite-alphabet remainder constant valid for every sample size. \textit{Gap.} It replaces the existential constant that could not serve as evaluator data. \textit{Consumer.} The rational grid program obtains a computable all-future primal error.

\begin{lemma}[lem:effective-rational-grid-primal-error]\label{lem:effective-rational-grid-primal-error}
Let \(U_n\) be the optimum returned by the rational action-grid program in
\(\mathrm{def:evaluator\text{-}handle}\), with
\(G=\lceil n^{3/4}\rceil\), under the fixed independent allocation
\(q^\dagger\).  Then
\[
R_n(c^\dagger)\le U_n
\le\frac1n-\Lambda_{c^\dagger}n^{-4/3}
+B_{\mathrm{eff}}^\dagger n^{-3/2}+\frac1{4G^2}.
\]
The diagonal evaluator may choose the rational primal error so that
\[
e_{\mathrm{pr},n}\le
\frac{B_{\mathrm{eff}}^\dagger+1/4}
{\lfloor n^{1/7}\rfloor}+2^{-n}.
\]
The right-hand side is a computable rational-valued nonincreasing function of \(n\) tending to zero.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For each observation \(\omega\), let \(d_\omega\in[-1,1]\) be the action of
\(\delta_n^\star\).  Randomize between the two adjacent grid points whose interval contains \(d_\omega\), with probabilities chosen to preserve conditional mean.  If their spacing is \(G^{-1}\), the randomized action
\(\widetilde d_\omega\) satisfies
\[
\mathbb E(\widetilde d_\omega\mid\omega)=d_\omega,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(\widetilde d_\omega\mid\omega)\le\frac1{4G^2}.
\tag{1}
\]
Conditional square completion therefore gives, for every
\(\tau\in[-1,1]\),
\[
\mathbb E\{(\widetilde d_\omega-\tau)^2\mid\omega\}
\le(d_\omega-\tau)^2+\frac1{4G^2}.
\tag{2}
\]
These probabilities are a feasible real point of the rational finite-dimensional linear program.  A rational optimal basic feasible point exists, so program optimality, \((2)\), and the effective fixed-design remainder lemma give
\[
U_n\le n^{-1}-\Lambda_{c^\dagger}n^{-4/3}
+B_{\mathrm{eff}}^\dagger n^{-3/2}+\frac1{4G^2}.
\tag{3}
\]
The returned kernel and independent assignment are feasible in the unrestricted risk game, proving \(R_n(c^\dagger)\le U_n\).

Because \(G\ge n^{3/4}\), multiplication of \((3)\) by \(n^{4/3}\) shows that the positive primal deficit is at most
\((B_{\mathrm{eff}}^\dagger+1/4)n^{-1/6}\), apart from the evaluator's Airy interval width.  The latter is refined below \(2^{-n}\).  For every integer \(n\ge1\),
\[
n^{-1/6}\le n^{-1/7}\le\frac1{\lfloor n^{1/7}\rfloor}.
\]
This proves the rational bound in the statement.  Both denominator and dyadic terms are nonincreasing, and each tends to zero, proving the effective tail assertion.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Mean-preserving adjacent-grid randomization transfers the certified Airy estimator with an explicit computable rounding cost. \textit{Gap.} Unlike the obsolete grid lemma, this result contains a rational all-future modulus. \textit{Consumer.} The allocation-tube diagonal and effective finite-sample certificate use its primal tail.

\begin{theorem}[thm:qstar-bounded-endpoint-second-order]\label{thm:qstar-bounded-endpoint-second-order}
Let \(K\ge3\) be fixed, let \(c\in\mathbb R^K\) satisfy \(\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_K}c_a=0\), \(\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_K}|c_a|=2\), and \(|S_c|\ge3\), and fix real endpoints \(L<U\). For observed bounded outcomes \((w_{i,A_i})_{i=1}^n\in[L,U]^n\), put \(z_i^{\mathrm{obs}}=(w_{i,A_i}-L)/(U-L)\) and define the mathematically total lifted estimator under the independent allocation \(q^\star\) by the finite polynomial
\[
\delta_{n,[L,U]}^\star(A,w_A)
=(U-L)\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^n}
 \delta_n^\star(A,x)
 \prod_{i=1}^n
 \{x_i z_i^{\mathrm{obs}}+(1-x_i)(1-z_i^{\mathrm{obs}})\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Here \(\delta_n^\star(A,x)\) means the binary procedure of \(\mathrm{def:qstar\text{-}procedure}\) evaluated at the labels \(A\) and binary observed outcomes \(x\). Then the same finite constant \(B_c\) as in \(\mathrm{thm:qstar\text{-}second\text{-}order}\) satisfies
\[
\sup_{w\in[L,U]^{n\times K}}
\mathbb E\!\left[
 \{\delta_{n,[L,U]}^\star(A,w_A)-\tau_c(w)\}^2
\right]
\le (U-L)^2\left\{
 \frac1n-\Lambda_cn^{-4/3}+B_cn^{-3/2}
\right\}.
\tag{2}
\]
Moreover, if \(R_{n,[L,U]}^{\mathrm{ind}}(c)\) denotes the minimax risk with this fixed independent design and bounded schedules, then
\[
R_{n,[L,U]}^{\mathrm{ind}}(c)
=(U-L)^2R_n^{\mathrm{ind}}(c)
=(U-L)^2\left\{
 \frac1n-\Lambda_cn^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3})
\right\}.
\tag{3}
\]
The totality in this statement is mathematical totality; it makes no exact Turing-computability assertion for unrepresented real endpoints or observations.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write \(d=U-L>0\). Fix a bounded schedule \(w\in[L,U]^{n\times K}\), and set
\[
z_{i,a}=\frac{w_{i,a}-L}{d}\in[0,1].
\tag{4}
\]
Conditional on this fixed schedule, introduce independent latent variables \(\Xi_{i,a}\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(z_{i,a})\), independent also of the assignment and of any randomization in the binary procedure. For fixed \(A\), the conditional law of the observed latent vector \((\Xi_{i,A_i})_{i=1}^n\) is the product law with success probabilities \((z_{i,A_i})_{i=1}^n\). Consequently, expanding its finite support gives
\[
\mathbb E_{\Xi}\!\left[\delta_n^\star(A,\Xi_A)\mid A\right]
=\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^n}\delta_n^\star(A,x)
 \prod_{i=1}^n\{x_i z_{i,A_i}+(1-x_i)(1-z_{i,A_i})\}.
\tag{5}
\]
The right side of \((5)\) is exactly the polynomial in \((1)\) and uses only \(A\) and the observed normalized values \(z_{i,A_i}=z_i^{\mathrm{obs}}\). Thus \(\delta_{n,[L,U]}^\star\) is observable from the bounded labeled data. Each factor in \((5)\) is defined also at the boundary values \(z_{i,A_i}=0\) and \(z_{i,A_i}=1\), the sum has \(2^n\) terms, and \(\delta_n^\star\) is total by \(\mathrm{thm:qstar\text{-}second\text{-}order}\); hence the lifted estimator is mathematically total.

The zero-sum assumption gives, equation by equation,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau_c(w)
&=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_K}
 c_a\{L+d z_{i,a}\}\\
&=\frac Ln\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_K}c_a
 +\frac d n\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_K}c_a z_{i,a}\\
&=d\,\mathbb E_{\Xi}\!\left[\tau_c(\Xi)\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
The first equality is \((4)\), the second is linearity, the third uses \(\sum_a c_a=0\) and \(\mathbb E_\Xi\Xi_{i,a}=z_{i,a}\). Because \(w\) is fixed, \((1)\), \((5)\), and \((6)\) imply, for every assignment label vector \(A\),
\[
\delta_{n,[L,U]}^\star(A,w_A)-\tau_c(w)
=d\,\mathbb E_{\Xi}\!\left[
 \delta_n^\star(A,\Xi_A)-\tau_c(\Xi)\mid A
\right].
\tag{7}
\]
For
\(Z=\delta_n^\star(A,\Xi_A)-\tau_c(\Xi)\), the elementary conditional-variance identity
\[
\mathbb E_\Xi[Z^2\mid A]-\{\mathbb E_\Xi[Z\mid A]\}^2
=\mathbb E_\Xi\!\left[
 \{Z-\mathbb E_\Xi[Z\mid A]\}^2\mid A
\right]\ge0
\]
proves conditional Jensen's inequality for the square. Applying it to \((7)\), followed by expectation over the independent assignment, yields
\[
\mathbb E_A\!\left[
 \{\delta_{n,[L,U]}^\star(A,w_A)-\tau_c(w)\}^2
\right]
\le d^2\mathbb E_{A,\Xi}\!\left[
 \{\delta_n^\star(A,\Xi_A)-\tau_c(\Xi)\}^2
\right].
\tag{8}
\]
Outcome independence is used here: the law of \(A\) is the same for every realization of the latent binary schedule \(\Xi\). Therefore the last expectation is a mixture of binary-schedule risks and is at most
\[
\max_{y\in\{0,1\}^{n\times K}}
\mathbb E_A\!\left[
 \{\delta_n^\star(A,y_A)-\tau_c(y)\}^2
\right].
\tag{9}
\]
The established uniform upper bound \(\mathrm{thm:qstar\text{-}second\text{-}order}\), applied to \((9)\), and then the supremum over \(w\), prove \((2)\).

For the minimax identity, take in \(\mathrm{lem:multiarm\text{-}bounded\text{-}endpoint\text{-}reduction}\) the fixed design class consisting of the independent outcome-independent assignment law with probabilities \(q^\star\). That lemma preserves the same design law, arbitrary estimator randomization, and complete-label observation, and gives exactly
\[
R_{n,[L,U]}^{\mathrm{ind}}(c)=d^2R_n^{\mathrm{ind}}(c).
\tag{10}
\]
Finally, \(\mathrm{thm:qstar\text{-}second\text{-}order}\) gives
\(R_n^{\mathrm{ind}}(c)=n^{-1}-\Lambda_cn^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3})\). Multiplication by the fixed positive constant \(d^2\) in \((10)\) proves \((3)\). No limiting or division step uses a vanishing endpoint width because \(L<U\) is fixed.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The exact bounded-endpoint reduction transports the sharp fixed-independent binary expansion without changing the design law, while the displayed finite polynomial supplies an explicit total attaining procedure. \textit{Gap.} This fixed-independent result does not establish unrestricted second-order optimality for a general permitted multiarm contrast. \textit{Consumer.} A trialist with a bounded fixed-horizon endpoint can prespecify the contrast-weighted independent allocation and the Rao--Blackwellized Airy procedure; exact Turing evaluation is asserted only where inputs have a representation.

\begin{lemma}[lem:multiarm-bounded-endpoint-reduction]\label{lem:multiarm-bounded-endpoint-reduction}
For every integer \(K_0\ge2\), every \(n\ge1\), every pair of fixed real endpoints \(L<U\), every contrast \(c\in\mathbb R^{K_0}\) satisfying \(\sum_{a=1}^{K_0}c_a=0\), and every fixed nonempty class \(\mathfrak D\) of outcome-independent assignment laws, including classes whose laws have arbitrary cross-unit dependence,
\[
 R^{\mathfrak D}_{n,[L,U]}(c)
 =(U-L)^2R^{\mathfrak D}_{n,\{0,1\}}(c).
\tag{3}
\]
For every binary procedure of finite worst-case second moment, the upper-bound map in this equality is a total, nonrandomized estimator given by a finite polynomial in the assignment labels and the observed normalized outcomes.  The equality leaves the design-law class and both estimator infima unchanged.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(s=U-L>0\).  We prove the two inequalities in \((3)\) for the same fixed class \(\mathfrak D\).

For the lower bound, take an arbitrary binary schedule \(x\in\{0,1\}^{n\times K_0}\) and form the endpoint schedule \(w=L\mathbf 1+sx\).  The zero-sum condition gives the exact causal-target identity
\[
 \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{a=1}^{K_0}c_aw_{i,a}
 =\frac{L}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{a=1}^{K_0}c_a
  +\frac{s}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n\sum_{a=1}^{K_0}c_ax_{i,a}
 =s\tau_c(x).
\tag{4}
\]
Fix \(\mathcal D_n\in\mathfrak D\) and any possibly randomized bounded-outcome estimator \(\delta\).  On binary data define the randomized estimator
\(
\delta_{01}(A,x_A)=s^{-1}\delta(A,L\mathbf1+sx_A)
\).
Equation \((4)\) implies, schedule by schedule,
\[
 \mathbb E\!\left[
 \{\delta(A,L\mathbf1+sx_A)-s\tau_c(x)\}^2\right]
 =s^2\mathbb E\!\left[
 \{\delta_{01}(A,x_A)-\tau_c(x)\}^2\right].
\]
Endpoint schedules are contained in \([L,U]^{n\times K_0}\).  Taking the binary supremum, then the estimator infimum and the infimum over the unchanged class \(\mathfrak D\), proves
\[
 R^{\mathfrak D}_{n,[L,U]}(c)
 \ge s^2R^{\mathfrak D}_{n,\{0,1\}}(c).
\]

For the reverse inequality, fix \(\mathcal D_n\in\mathfrak D\) and a binary estimator kernel \(K_{01}(du\mid A,\xi_A)\) having finite worst-case second moment.  Let
\[
 m_{01}(A,\xi)=\int_{\mathbb R}u\,
 K_{01}(du\mid A,\xi),
 \qquad \xi\in\{0,1\}^n,
\]
on assignment vectors of positive \(\mathcal D_n\)-probability, and define it arbitrarily on null assignment vectors.  For a bounded schedule \(w\), put \(z_{i,a}=(w_{i,a}-L)/s\in[0,1]\).  Given the realized labels \(A\), independently generate latent variables \(\Xi_{i,a}\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(z_{i,a})\), independently also of the estimator seed.  Rao--Blackwellization gives the following explicit observed-data estimator:
\[
 \widehat\tau_{[L,U]}(A,w_A)
 =s\sum_{\xi\in\{0,1\}^n}m_{01}(A,\xi)
   \prod_{i=1}^n
   z_{i,A_i}^{\xi_i}(1-z_{i,A_i})^{1-\xi_i}.
\tag{5}
\]
The right side is a finite polynomial in \(A\) and the observed numbers \(z_{i,A_i}\); hence no unobserved \(w_{i,a}\) is used.  It defines a real number on every observation, after an arbitrary finite definition on assignment-null observations.  Thus it is mathematically total even when the real endpoints or observations have no computability representation.

For each fixed schedule \(w\), outcome independence makes the law of \(A\) independent of the auxiliary array \(\Xi\).  Equation \((4)\), now averaged coordinatewise under \(\Xi\), gives
\[
 \mathbb E_{\Xi}\{\tau_c(\Xi)\}
 =\tau_c(z)=s^{-1}\tau_c(w).
\]
Writing \(\widehat\tau_{01}\) for the action drawn from \(K_{01}\), the definition \((5)\) therefore yields, conditional on \(A\),
\[
 \widehat\tau_{[L,U]}(A,w_A)-\tau_c(w)
 =s\,\mathbb E_{\Xi,K_{01}}\!\left[
 \widehat\tau_{01}(A,\Xi_A)-\tau_c(\Xi)\mid A\right].
\tag{6}
\]
Conditional Jensen's inequality applied to \((6)\), followed by expectation over the possibly dependent assignment vector, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_{\mathcal D_n}
 \{\widehat\tau_{[L,U]}-\tau_c(w)\}^2
 &\le s^2\mathbb E_{\mathcal D_n,\Xi,K_{01}}
 \{\widehat\tau_{01}(A,\Xi_A)-\tau_c(\Xi)\}^2\\
 &=s^2\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^{n\times K_0}}
 \Pr_w(\Xi=x)\,
 \mathbb E_{\mathcal D_n,K_{01}}
 \{\widehat\tau_{01}(A,x_A)-\tau_c(x)\}^2\\
 &\le s^2\sup_{x\in\{0,1\}^{n\times K_0}}
 \mathbb E_{\mathcal D_n,K_{01}}
 \{\widehat\tau_{01}(A,x_A)-\tau_c(x)\}^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
No product or independence condition was imposed on \(\mathcal D_n\); only the auxiliary Bernoulli array is independent.  The zero binary estimator shows that the binary minimax value is finite, so procedures of infinite worst-case second moment may be discarded when taking the infimum.  Taking in \((7)\) the supremum over \(w\), then the infimum over all binary estimator kernels and over the same \(\mathcal D_n\in\mathfrak D\), proves
\[
 R^{\mathfrak D}_{n,[L,U]}(c)
 \le s^2R^{\mathfrak D}_{n,\{0,1\}}(c).
\]
Together with the endpoint lower bound, this proves \((3)\) and every asserted quantifier-preservation and observability clause.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:published-two-arm-unrestricted-airy-game]\label{lem:published-two-arm-unrestricted-airy-game}
In the two-treatment finite-population experiment with fixed binary potential outcomes, squared-error loss for the average treatment effect, and optimization over every assignment mechanism and measurable estimator, the unrestricted minimax risk equals \(\rho_n^\star\).  For fixed real \(L<U\), the corresponding unrestricted game with potential outcomes in \([L,U]\) has minimax risk \((U-L)^2\rho_n^\star\).  Moreover,
\[
 \rho_n^\star=\frac1n-C_An^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}),
\]
where \(C_A=-4^{1/3}a_1'>0\) and \(a_1'\) is the largest negative zero of the derivative of the Airy function.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proposition}[prop:cdagger-binary-submodel-comparison]\label{prop:cdagger-binary-submodel-comparison}
In the binary three-arm game at \(c^\dagger=(1,-1/4,-3/4)\), restriction of nature to schedules satisfying \(Y_i(2)=Y_i(3)\) for every \(i\) gives exactly the published unrestricted two-treatment game:
\[
 \inf_{\mathcal D_n,\widehat\tau}
 \max_{\substack{y\in\{0,1\}^{n\times3}:\\Y_i(2)=Y_i(3)\ \text{for every }i}}
 \mathbb E_{\mathcal D_n}\!\left[
 \{\widehat\tau-\tau_{c^\dagger}(y)\}^2\right]
 =\rho_n^\star.
\tag{8}
\]
Both the assignment law and the estimator, including randomized estimators, are unrestricted in \((8)\).  Consequently,
\[
 R_n(c^\dagger)\ge\rho_n^\star
 =\frac1n-C_An^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}).
\tag{9}
\]
Because
\(C_A>C_A(5/8)^{2/3}\), the imported bound \((9)\) is asymptotically strictly weaker than the sharp three-arm lower bound and cannot supply its second-order coefficient.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write a schedule in the restricted class as
\(
Y_i(1)=Y_i^{(1)}
\) and
\(
Y_i(2)=Y_i(3)=Y_i^{(0)}
\).  The coefficient identity \(-1/4-3/4=-1\) gives
\[
 \tau_{c^\dagger}(y)
 =\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\{Y_i^{(1)}-Y_i^{(0)}\},
\tag{10}
\]
which is the two-treatment finite-population average treatment effect.

First take any two-treatment procedure.  Embed it in the three-arm experiment by assigning arm \(1\) whenever the two-treatment label is one and arm \(2\) whenever that label is zero, never assigning arm \(3\), and applying the same estimator.  Equation \((10)\) shows equality of risk on every restricted schedule.  Taking infima proves that the left side of \((8)\) is at most \(\rho_n^\star\).

Conversely, fix an arbitrary outcome-independent three-arm assignment law and an arbitrary randomized labeled-data estimator.  Put
\[
 B_i=\mathbf1\{A_i=1\}.
\]
The induced joint law of \(B=(B_1,\ldots,B_n)\) is an admissible outcome-independent two-treatment design and may have arbitrary cross-unit dependence.  Because the state spaces are finite, for every \(b\) of positive induced probability there is a conditional law
\(
Q(a\mid b)=\Pr(A=a\mid B=b)
\)
supported on labels satisfying
\(
\mathbf1\{a_i=1\}=b_i
\).  Given two-treatment labeled data, a randomized two-treatment estimator can draw \(A'\sim Q(\cdot\mid B)\) and then apply the original three-arm estimator to \(A'\) and the same observed outcomes.  On the restricted schedules, the observed outcome is \(Y_i^{(1)}\) when \(B_i=1\) and \(Y_i^{(0)}\) when \(B_i=0\), regardless of whether \(A_i'=2\) or \(3\).  Hence the simulated two-treatment data and action have exactly the same joint law as the original three-arm data and action, schedule by schedule.  If the cited two-treatment game is written with nonrandomized estimators, replacing this simulated action by its conditional mean can only decrease squared-error risk by conditional Jensen.  Thus \(\rho_n^\star\) is no larger than the risk of the arbitrary three-arm procedure.  Taking its worst case and then its infimum proves the reverse inequality in \((8)\).

The full nature class contains the restricted one, so \((8)\) and the published two-treatment Airy expansion give \((9)\).  The established sharp-limit theorem gives the three-arm coefficient \(C_A(5/8)^{2/3}\).  Since \(C_A>0\) and \(0<5/8<1\),
\[
 C_A-C_A(5/8)^{2/3}
 =C_A\{1-(5/8)^{2/3}\}>0.
\]
Thus the lower-risk benchmark on the right of \((9)\) subtracts a strictly larger second-order constant and is strictly weaker for all sufficiently large \(n\), proving the final claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:unequal-three-arm-bounded-endpoint-sharp-limit]\label{thm:unequal-three-arm-bounded-endpoint-sharp-limit}
For \(c^\dagger=(1,-1/4,-3/4)\) and every pair of fixed real endpoints \(L<U\), the unrestricted design-estimator minimax risk for schedules in \([L,U]^{n\times3}\) obeys the exact finite-sample reduction

\tag{11}
\]
and hence the sharp expansion

 R_{n,[L,U]}(c^\dagger)

 \right\}.
\tag{12}
\]
The polynomial lift in \(\mathrm{lem:multiarm\text{-}bounded\text{-}endpoint\text{-}reduction}\) turns every total binary finite-data procedure into a mathematically total procedure on the bounded real observation space.  No exact Turing-computability claim is made for unrepresented real endpoints or observations.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply \(\mathrm{lem:multiarm\text{-}bounded\text{-}endpoint\text{-}reduction}\) with \(K_0=3\), \(c=c^\dagger\), and \(\mathfrak D\) equal to the class of all outcome-independent assignment laws on \(\{1,2,3\}^n\).  Its exact equality and \(\mathrm{def:bounded\text{-}risk\text{-}game}\) give \((11)\), preserving arbitrary assignment dependence and all randomized-estimator quantifiers.

The established binary theorem \(\mathrm{thm:unequal\text{-}three\text{-}arm\text{-}sharp\text{-}limit}\) gives

 R_n(c^\dagger)
 =\frac1n-\Lambda_{c^\dagger}n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3}),

\]
Multiplication by the fixed positive constant \((U-L)^2\) preserves the \(o(n^{-4/3})\) remainder, proving \((12)\).  The totality and non-effectivity clauses follow directly from the finite polynomial formula \((5)\) in the reduction lemma.
\end{proof}

\section{Interpretation}

For the planned binary three-regimen contrast \(c^\dagger\), a trialist may use \(q^\dagger\), the total Airy-feedback estimator, and the terminating finite-sample certificate knowing that their second-order value matches the unrestricted optimum even when competing designs may correlate assignments across units. For a bounded endpoint on a fixed real interval \([L,U]\), the observable Bernoulli-polynomial lift gives the same conclusion after multiplying squared risk by \((U-L)^2\). This bounded statement is mathematical rather than an exact-computation claim for arbitrary unrepresented reals.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

Unrestricted sharpness is proved at the fixed rational contrast \(c^\dagger=(1,-1/4,-3/4)\), not for every permitted multiarm contrast; accordingly, equality versus strict second-order loss of \(q^\star\) remains open in general. The effective binary certificate uses rational and ordered-algebraic input records, and no representation-independent terminating evaluator is claimed for an arbitrary real contrast, endpoint, or observed value. These are scope limits, not missing steps in the proved \(c^\dagger\) equality or in the exact bounded-risk scaling identity.

\section{Honest scope}

The unrestricted sharp theorem and its computable finite-data certificate concern the binary unequal three-arm contrast \(c^\dagger=(1,-1/4,-3/4)\). The exact endpoint reduction gives a mathematically total bounded-outcome corollary for every fixed real \(L<U\), and the fixed-independent bounded expansion holds for every permitted fixed contrast. The paper does not claim unrestricted sharpness for general \(c\) or a terminating evaluator for an arbitrary real contrast or unrepresented real bounded dataset. At \(c^\dagger\), however, the independent allocation \(q^\dagger\) is proved second-order optimal; the equality-versus-loss question remains open only beyond this established contrast.
