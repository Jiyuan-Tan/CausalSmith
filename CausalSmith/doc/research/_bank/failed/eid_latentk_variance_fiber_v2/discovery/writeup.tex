\section*{eid\_\allowbreak{}latentk\_\allowbreak{}variance\_\allowbreak{}fiber}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v2\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} Arbitrary Gaussian error variances identify only a Markov-equivalence class, one common variance identifies the DAG, and a supplied partial-homoscedastic partition refines the class. This project studies the missing observational regime in which the equality partition is not supplied: a faithful rational Gaussian forest covariance is compatible with a root tuple when its exact regression residuals use at most \(K\) numerical values. The supplied-order dynamic program now has the refined binomial state bound \(\sum_{i=0}^{\min(q_\pi,K)}\binom{q_\pi}{i}(K-i+1)\), preserving exact decision, uniqueness, and two-witness recovery and giving both the earlier base-two bound and a \(q_\pi^{O(K)}\) regime. The bounded-component algorithm costs \(d^K\operatorname{poly}(N,K)\). For designated vertices, the population total-effect fiber is empty with the structural fiber; conditional on nonemptiness it is \(\{1\}\) on the diagonal, is \(\{0\}\) across components, and within a component is a subset of \(\{0,\Sigma_{yx}/\Sigma_{xx}\}\). Each within-component value is present exactly when the corresponding side of the first path edge admits a feasible root, and both algorithms recover witnesses. The unrestricted \(K\)-frontier, uniqueness classification, and matching recurrence-width lower bound remain open. A covariance-region projection propagates sampling uncertainty to orientations and effects but is not claimed to be computationally efficient over the covariance region.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Published equal-variance and partial-homoscedastic theory either fixes one common level or supplies the equality partition. Generic minimum-label hardness does not survive the algebraic and arithmetic restrictions of Gaussian residual signatures.

\paragraph{Niche.} The exact latent-\(K\) covariance fiber is a pointwise structural-identification object lying between fully identified equal-error models and unrestricted Markov equivalence, with a parameter whose algorithmic status is unknown.

\paragraph{Fill.} The project gives a root-union reduction, an exact rational tree-signature realization theorem, symbolic root loci, positive algorithms at bounded component size and bounded recurrence width, and an exact tree-effect theorem. The supplied-order algorithm uses \(\sum_{i=0}^{\min(q_\pi,K)}\binom{q_\pi}{i}(K-i+1)\) states per cut; the effect theorem reduces a within-component effect fiber to two root-side feasibility queries and recovers a witness for each attainable value. The unrestricted parameterized classification and matching lower bounds remain explicit open questions whose negative branch must certify edge residuals, positive definiteness, faithfulness, rationality, and bit length before using any Gaussian gadget.

\section{Related work}

Peters and Buehlmann (2014) establish full identification when all innovation variances are equal, the \(K=1\) endpoint. Chen, Drton, and Wang (2019) exploit the same common-variance ordering to build scalable recovery algorithms. Wu and Drton (2023) characterize Gaussian DAG equivalence when the variance partition is supplied and therefore motivate the latent-partition step without occupying it. Boege, Kubjas, Misra, and Solus (2026) provide rational parameter recovery, algebraic Markov descriptions, and generic faithfulness for fixed vertex colorings; the inverse theorem here instead couples every alternative root of one covariance and certifies its piecewise diagonal and off-diagonal tree-product matrix by an arbitrary-root SEM calculation. Fellows, Guo, and Kanj (2010) show that several unrestricted minimum-label graph problems are W[2]-hard, but their label systems are not Gaussian-realizable. Marx (2010) and Lampis (2025) supply decomposition and fine-grained lower-bound techniques for CSP and Set Cover interfaces. Yato and Seta (2003) and Creignou et al. (2013) show why another-solution recovery needs solution-preserving reductions and a canonical neighborhood. Strieder and Drton (2023) and Wang, Kolar, and Drton (2026) motivate projecting structural uncertainty to total effects; here a forest-specific path calculation first identifies the population effect fiber as at most two values and the covariance-region proposition then supplies simultaneous coverage, without an efficient optimization claim for the projection over \(M\in\mathcal C_n\).

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:gaussian-law]\label{ass:gaussian-law}
\(P=\mathcal N_p(0,\Sigma)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Centered linear Gaussian observational law, \cite{PetersBuhlmann2014}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:forest-faithfulness]\label{ass:forest-faithfulness}
\(P\) is faithful to the undirected forest \(T\), equivalently to every collider-free rooting assembled from the directed components \(H_{j,r}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Gaussian causal faithfulness restricted to a forest, \cite{BoegeKubjasMisraSolus2026}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:iid-sample]\label{ass:iid-sample}
\(X^{(1)},\ldots,X^{(n)}\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Independent Gaussian sampling, \cite{StriederDrton2023}).
\end{assumption}

\section{Main results}

\begin{theorem}[thm:root-union]\label{thm:root-union}
For every input in \(\mathfrak M_{p,K}^{\mathrm{forest}}\), mapping a root tuple \(\rho\) to the forest DAG \(H_\rho\) is a bijection between \(\mathcal F_K(\Sigma)\) and the DAGs in the Markov-equivalence class of \(T\) whose Gaussian regression innovations take at most \(K\) distinct variances.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix an input in \(\mathfrak M_{p,K}^{\mathrm{forest}}\).  By \(\text{ass:gaussian-law}\), \(P\) is the centered nonsingular Gaussian law with covariance \(\Sigma\), and by \(\text{ass:forest-faithfulness}\), \(P\) is Markov and faithful to \(T\) and to each collider-free rooting of \(T\).

First characterize the relevant DAGs without using the variance labels.  On a connected component with \(m\) vertices, every orientation is acyclic because the skeleton is a tree.  An orientation having no unshielded collider has indegree at most one at every vertex: two incoming edges would have nonadjacent tails because a forest contains no triangle.  The sum of the indegrees is the number of edges, namely \(m-1\).  Hence exactly one vertex has indegree zero and every other vertex has indegree one.  Calling the unique indegree-zero vertex \(r\), induction on graph distance from \(r\) shows that every edge points away from \(r\), so the orientation is \(H_{j,r}\).  Conversely, \(H_{j,r}\) has one indegree-zero vertex and all other indegrees equal to one, and hence has no collider.  This also covers an isolated vertex.

We verify the Markov-equivalence step directly.  In an away-from-root orientation, the unique path between two vertices has the form of two directed arms leaving their lowest common ancestor and therefore contains no collider.  Conditioning blocks that path exactly when the conditioning set contains an internal path vertex.  Thus every \(H_\rho\) has precisely the separation model of the undirected forest \(T\), so all such rootings are Markov equivalent.  Conversely, adjacency is determined by a DAG's separation model: adjacent vertices cannot be d-separated by a set excluding the endpoints because their one-edge path stays active, whereas for nonadjacent vertices one of the two vertices is a nondescendant of the other and the latter's parent set d-separates them by the local directed Markov argument.  Hence a DAG equivalent to a rooting has skeleton \(T\).  If it contained \(u\to v\leftarrow w\), the forest forces \(u\) and \(w\) to be nonadjacent and their unique path would be blocked without conditioning; in every rooting that path has no collider and is active without conditioning, a contradiction.  It therefore has no collider and the preceding indegree count makes it \(H_\rho\) for a unique root tuple.  Injectivity is explicit because the root is the unique indegree-zero vertex in each component.

It remains to identify the innovation labels.  Fix \(\rho=(r_1,\ldots,r_c)\).  The Gaussian density factorizes according to \(H_\rho\), because faithfulness includes the Markov property.  Every nonroot vertex \(v\) has a unique parent \(u\), while a root has no parent.  Gaussian conditioning therefore gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(X_{r_j})=\Sigma_{r_jr_j}=a_{r_j},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(X_v\mid X_u)
 =\Sigma_{vv}-\frac{\Sigma_{vu}^{2}}{\Sigma_{uu}}
 =b_{v\mid u}.
\]
All denominators are positive because \(\Sigma\succ0\).  After the triangular change of variables from \(X\) to the root variables and regression residuals, the factorized Gaussian density is a product of their one-dimensional Gaussian densities.  Hence these quantities are precisely the mutually independent regression-innovation variances of the DAG.  By \(\text{def:root-signature}\), their set on component \(j\) is \(S_j(r_j)\), and their set on the whole forest is
\[
 L(\rho)=\bigcup_{j=1}^{c}S_j(r_j).
\]
Consequently the DAG has at most \(K\) distinct innovation variances if and only if \(|L(\rho)|\le K\), which is equivalent to \(\rho\in\mathcal F_K(\Sigma)\).  Combining this equivalence with the root--DAG bijection proves the stated bijection.  Notice that the causal fiber is not defined by an externally supplied equality partition: equality is tested on the observable rational residuals in \(\text{def:root-signature}\), and the argument above proves that these residuals equal the causal innovation variances.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This converts an observational causal-structure fiber into an exact minimum-union problem without assuming its equality partition. \textit{Gap.} WuDrton2023 fixes the partition and compares whole models; this theorem is pointwise in one covariance and existentially selects its canonical equality classes. \textit{Consumer.} SEMgraph can use the bijection to report every orientation compatible with at most \(K\) exact innovation levels.

\begin{theorem}[thm:rational-tree-realization]\label{thm:rational-tree-realization}
Fix a component \(T_j\). Positive rational arrays \((\widetilde a_v,\widetilde b_{v\mid u})\) are the vertex variances and both directed-edge regression residuals of a rational positive-definite Gaussian covariance faithful to \(T_j\) if and only if, for every edge \(uv\), there is \(h_{uv}\in(0,1)\cap\mathbb Q\) with \(\widetilde b_{v\mid u}=\widetilde a_vh_{uv}\), \(\widetilde b_{u\mid v}=\widetilde a_uh_{uv}\), and \(\widetilde a_u\widetilde a_v(1-h_{uv})\) a nonzero square in \(\mathbb Q\). Choose a signed rational square root \(c_{uv}\ne0\) on every edge and define the matrix piecewise by \[M_{xx}=\widetilde a_x,\qquad M_{xy}=\frac{\prod_{uv\in\operatorname{path}(x,y)}c_{uv}}{\prod_{z\in\operatorname{intpath}(x,y)}\widetilde a_z}\quad(x\ne y).\] For every root \(r\in V(T_j)\), the linear SEM oriented away from \(r\), with edge coefficient \(c_{uv}/\widetilde a_u\) for \(u\to v\), root innovation variance \(\widetilde a_r\), and nonroot innovation variance \(\widetilde a_vh_{uv}>0\) for \(u\to v\), has covariance exactly \(M\). Consequently \(M\) is positive definite and faithful to \(T_j\), and on every edge \(uv\) it satisfies \(M_{vv}-M_{vu}^{2}/M_{uu}=\widetilde a_vh_{uv}=\widetilde b_{v\mid u}\) and \(M_{uu}-M_{uv}^{2}/M_{vv}=\widetilde a_uh_{uv}=\widetilde b_{u\mid v}\). Conversely, every rational positive-definite covariance faithful to \(T_j\) yields these conditions with \(c_{uv}=M_{uv}\), obeys the same piecewise tree-product formula, and has nonzero edge covariances. Each path entry uses at most \(|V(T_j)|-1\) rational products and divisions, so all entries have bit length polynomial in the slot representation. As a nonunit-diagonal check, for the path \(1-2-3\), take \((\widetilde a_1,\widetilde a_2,\widetilde a_3)=(3,7/4,23/16)\), \((c_{12},c_{23})=(3/2,7/8)\), and \((h_{12},h_{23})=(4/7,16/23)\). The formula gives \[M=\begin{pmatrix}3&3/2&3/4\\3/2&7/4&7/8\\3/4&7/8&23/16\end{pmatrix},\] whose leading principal minors are \(3,3,3\); its directed residuals are \((\widetilde b_{2\mid1},\widetilde b_{1\mid2})=(1,12/7)\) and \((\widetilde b_{3\mid2},\widetilde b_{2\mid3})=(1,28/23)\), and its nonzero edge covariances make the Gaussian path faithful.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write \(a_v=\widetilde a_v\).  We first prove sufficiency.  Suppose that for each edge \(uv\) there is \(h_{uv}\in(0,1)\cap\mathbb Q\) satisfying the two displayed residual identities in the statement and that
\[
 a_u a_v(1-h_{uv})=c_{uv}^{,2}
\]
for a chosen \(c_{uv}\in\mathbb Q\setminus\{0\}\).  Fix an arbitrary root \(r\), orient the tree away from \(r\), and, for every directed edge \(u\to v\), put
\[
 \beta_{vu}=\frac{c_{uv}}{a_u},\qquad
 \omega_r=a_r,qquad
 \omega_v=a_vh_{uv}.
\]
All innovation variances are strictly positive because every \(a_v>0\) and every \(h_{uv}\in(0,1)\).  Let the innovations be independent centered Gaussians and define recursively
\[
 X_r=\varepsilon_r,
 \qquad
 X_v=\beta_{vu}X_u+\varepsilon_v.
\]
Induction away from the root gives \(\operatorname{Var}(X_r)=a_r\).  If \(\operatorname{Var}(X_u)=a_u\), independence of \(X_u\) and \(\varepsilon_v\) gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(X_v)
 =\frac{c_{uv}^{,2}}{a_u^2}a_u+a_vh_{uv}
 =a_v(1-h_{uv})+a_vh_{uv}=a_v,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Cov}(X_u,X_v)=c_{uv}.
\]
Thus every vertex and edge covariance has the prescribed value.

For arbitrary distinct \(x,y\), let \(z\) be their lowest common ancestor in this rooting.  Repeated substitution along the two directed paths from \(z\), using independence of innovations in disjoint descendant branches, yields
\[
 M_{xy}
 =a_z
 \left(\prod_{e\in\operatorname{path}(z,x)}\frac{c_e}{a_{\operatorname{tail}(e)}}\right)
 \left(\prod_{e\in\operatorname{path}(z,y)}\frac{c_e}{a_{\operatorname{tail}(e)}}\right)
 =\frac{\prod_{uv\in\operatorname{path}(x,y)}c_{uv}}
 {\prod_{w\in\operatorname{intpath}(x,y)}a_w}.
\]
The last identity also applies when \(z=x\) or \(z=y\), with empty products equal to one.  It is independent of the chosen root, so the covariance is exactly the piecewise matrix \(M\) in the statement for every root.

In a topological order the structural system has the form \(X=(I-B)^{-1}\varepsilon\), where \(I-B\) is triangular with unit diagonal.  Hence
\[
 M=(I-B)^{-1}\operatorname{diag}(\omega_v)(I-B)^{-\mathsf T}\succ0.
\]
This proves positive definiteness rather than assuming it.  The Gaussian precision matrix is obtained by expanding
\[
 \sum_v\frac{(X_v-\beta_{v,\operatorname{pa}(v)}X_{\operatorname{pa}(v)})^2}{\omega_v},
\]
with the root term interpreted as \(X_r^2/\omega_r\).  Its off-diagonal entry on a parent--child edge is \(-\beta_{vu}/\omega_v\ne0\), and its off-diagonal entries on nonedges vanish.  Therefore its concentration graph is exactly \(T_j\).  To verify faithfulness, condition on any vertex set \(C\).  The conditional precision on the remaining vertices is the corresponding principal submatrix and hence is a forest with the same nonzero entries on every surviving edge.  If \(C\) separates \(x\) and \(y\), the conditional precision is block diagonal, so the conditional covariance vanishes.  If it does not separate them, the unique surviving path from \(x\) to \(y\) has only nonzero edge entries; successive leaf elimination, or equivalently the cofactor expansion along that unique path, expresses the conditional covariance numerator as the product of those nonzero edge entries times positive principal minors of the branches.  It is therefore nonzero.  Thus conditional independence holds exactly for forest separation, proving faithfulness.

On an edge \(uv\), the already established diagonal and edge entries give, in both directions,
\[
 M_{vv}-\frac{M_{vu}^{2}}{M_{uu}}
 =a_v-\frac{c_{uv}^{,2}}{a_u}
 =a_vh_{uv}=\widetilde b_{v\mid u},
\qquad
 M_{uu}-\frac{M_{uv}^{2}}{M_{vv}}
 =a_uh_{uv}=\widetilde b_{u\mid v}.
\]
All entries are rational by the path formula.

For necessity, suppose instead that a rational positive-definite Gaussian covariance \(M\) faithful to \(T_j\) realizes the specified vertex variances and directed-edge residuals.  For each edge define
\[
 c_{uv}=M_{uv},
 \qquad
 h_{uv}=1-\frac{M_{uv}^{2}}{M_{uu}M_{vv}}.
\]
Positive definiteness of the \(2\times2\) principal minor gives \(h_{uv}>0\), and faithfulness gives \(M_{uv}\ne0\), whence \(h_{uv}<1\).  Rationality of \(M\) gives \(h_{uv}\in\mathbb Q\), and direct substitution gives
\[
 \widetilde b_{v\mid u}=\widetilde a_vh_{uv},
 \qquad
 \widetilde b_{u\mid v}=\widetilde a_uh_{uv},
 \qquad
 \widetilde a_u\widetilde a_v(1-h_{uv})=M_{uv}^2=c_{uv}^{,2}\ne0.
\]
The Gaussian Markov property on the tree implies that whenever an internal vertex \(z\) separates \(x\) and \(y\),
\[
 M_{xy}=\frac{M_{xz}M_{zy}}{M_{zz}}.
\]
Applying this identity successively along the unique \(x\)--\(y\) path proves the same path-product formula.  This establishes all necessary conditions and the converse formula.

If the input rationals have maximum numerator-and-denominator bit length \(B\), a path entry uses at most \(|V(T_j)|-1\) edge numerators and at most \(|V(T_j)|-2\) internal-vertex divisions.  Before cancellation its numerator and denominator therefore have bit length \(O(|V(T_j)|B)\); reduction cannot increase that length.  The construction consequently has polynomial bit complexity.

Finally, in the stated three-vertex example,
\[
 M_{13}=\frac{(3/2)(7/8)}{7/4}=\frac34,
\]
and the leading principal minors are
\[
 3,\qquad 3\cdot\frac74-\left(\frac32\right)^2=3,
 \qquad \det(M)=3.
\]
The four directed residual computations are
\[
 \frac74-\frac{(3/2)^2}{3}=1,
 \qquad
 3-\frac{(3/2)^2}{7/4}=\frac{12}{7},
 \qquad
 \frac{23}{16}-\frac{(7/8)^2}{7/4}=1,
 \qquad
 \frac74-\frac{(7/8)^2}{23/16}=\frac{28}{23}.
\]
They agree with the stated \(h_{12}=4/7\) and \(h_{23}=16/23\).  Both edge covariances are nonzero, so the faithfulness argument above applies and completes the check.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The piecewise construction and arbitrary-root SEM identity isolate exactly which abstract label gadgets compile into faithful rational Gaussian tree signatures. \textit{Gap.} BoegeKubjasMisraSolus2026 gives rational parameter recovery and fixed-color model ideals but not inverse simultaneous realization across alternative roots or bit-complexity control. \textit{Consumer.} A hardness reduction for \(\textsc{K-Fiber}\) may invoke this certificate only after checking its edge residuals, positive definiteness, faithfulness, rationality, and bit length; algorithms can exploit the same forced identities.

\begin{lemma}[lem:root-locus]\label{lem:root-locus}
For every component \(T_j\) and dictionary \(D\), \(R_j(D)=\{r\in V(T_j):S_j(r)\subseteq D\}\). Moreover, \(Q_j(D)\) is empty or a connected subtree, and \(R_j(D)\) is computable in time polynomial in \(N\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(T_j\), a dictionary \(D\), and a candidate root \(r\).  For an edge \(uv\), deleting the edge gives the two sides \(T_{u;uv}\) and \(T_{v;uv}\).  In the orientation away from \(r\), the edge is oriented \(u\to v\) exactly when \(r\in T_{u;uv}\).  Consequently, if \(b_{v\mid u}\notin D\), the forbidden orientation \(u\to v\) is avoided exactly when
\[
 r\in T_{v;uv}.
\]
Imposing this condition for every ordered edge pair whose directed residual is absent from \(D\) gives exactly
\[
 r\in Q_j(D)
 =\bigcap_{(u,v):\,uv\in E(T_j),\ b_{v\mid u}\notin D}T_{v;uv}.
\]
The convention for an empty intersection correctly imposes no edge restriction.  By \(\text{def:root-signature}\), after these edge conditions hold, \(S_j(r)\subseteq D\) is equivalent to the remaining root condition \(a_r\in D\).  Thus
\[
 \{r:S_j(r)\subseteq D\}
 =Q_j(D)\cap\{r:a_r\in D\}=R_j(D).
\]

Every half-tree \(T_{v;uv}\) is connected and geodesically convex: it contains the unique path between any two of its vertices.  An intersection of geodesically convex subsets of a tree is geodesically convex.  Hence \(Q_j(D)\) is either empty or connected, and, when nonempty, is a subtree.

For computation, first compare every rational \(b_{v\mid u}\) with the rational dictionary entries using exact cross multiplication.  Initialize a Boolean mark on all vertices and, for each missing directed residual, intersect the marks with the corresponding side of the deleted edge by a tree traversal.  Finally retain marked vertices whose rational \(a_v\) occurs in \(D\).  There are at most \(2|E(T_j)|\) directed residual tests, every traversal is linear in \(|V(T_j)|\), and all rational comparisons use bit length bounded by the input encoding length \(N\).  The total number of bit operations is therefore polynomial in \(N\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The locus is the main symbolic invariant separating Gaussian root signatures from arbitrary multiple-choice label systems. \textit{Gap.} Neither fixed-partition CPDAG closure nor generic minimum-label complexity supplies a subtree-intersection description of roots supported by a numerical dictionary. \textit{Consumer.} The locus gives polynomial dictionary verification and the branching certificates used by exact fiber solvers.

\begin{theorem}[thm:ordered-dp]\label{thm:ordered-dp}
Let
\[
 \mathsf S_{\mathrm{DP}}(q,K)
 =\sum_{i=0}^{\min(q,K)}\binom qi(K-i+1).
\]
Given \((T,\Sigma,K,\pi)\), the live-label state in \(\text{def:ordered-state}\) decides \(\textsc{K-Fiber}\), decides \(\textsc{Unique-K-Fiber}\), and solves \(\textsc{Two-K-Fiber}\) exactly with at most \(\mathsf S_{\mathrm{DP}}(q_\pi,K)\) retained states per cut and \(\mathsf S_{\mathrm{DP}}(q_\pi,K)\operatorname{poly}(N)\) bit operations. In particular, this is at most \((K+1)2^{q_\pi}\operatorname{poly}(N)\), and for \(q_\pi\ge 2\) it is \(q_\pi^{O(K)}\operatorname{poly}(N)\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix an input \((T,\Sigma,K,\pi)\), and put \(q_\pi=\max_{0\leq \ell\leq c}|B_\ell|\).  For a choice of roots in the first \(\ell\) components of the order, define
\[
D_\ell=\bigcup_{h=1}^{\ell}S_{\pi(h)}(r_{\pi(h)}),
\qquad
A_\ell=D_\ell\cap B_\ell,
\qquad
C_\ell=D_\ell\setminus B_\ell,
\qquad
b_\ell=|C_\ell|.
\]
Thus \((A_\ell,b_\ell)\) is exactly the state of \(\texttt{def:ordered-state}\), and
\[
|D_\ell|=|A_\ell|+b_\ell. \tag{1}
\]
All signatures used below are well defined and positive.  Positive definiteness of \(\Sigma\) gives \(a_u=\Sigma_{uu}>0\) for every \(u\in V\).  For each edge \(uv\), positivity of the corresponding two-by-two principal minor gives
\[
\Sigma_{uu}\Sigma_{vv}-\Sigma_{uv}^{2}>0,
\qquad
b_{v\mid u}=\frac{\Sigma_{uu}\Sigma_{vv}-\Sigma_{uv}^{2}}{\Sigma_{uu}}>0.
\]
Thus every division defining a residual label has a nonzero denominator; rationality of \(\Sigma\) makes every label rational and exactly comparable.

We first prove the transition formula and its sufficiency.  If \(\lambda\in C_\ell\), then \(\lambda\) occurs in a processed component.  If it occurred in any unprocessed component as well, then, by the definition of \(U_j\),
\[
\lambda\in
\left(\bigcup_{h\leq\ell}U_{\pi(h)}\right)
\cap
\left(\bigcup_{h>\ell}U_{\pi(h)}\right)
=B_\ell,
\]
contrary to \(\lambda\in D_\ell\setminus B_\ell\).  Hence
\[
C_\ell\cap U_{\pi(h)}=\varnothing
\quad\text{for every }h>\ell. \tag{2}
\]
Choose \(r\in V(T_{\pi(\ell+1)})\), and write
\[
R=S_{\pi(\ell+1)}(r),
\qquad
W=A_\ell\cup R,
\qquad
D_{\ell+1}=D_\ell\cup R.
\]
By (2), \(C_\ell\cap R=\varnothing\), and therefore
\[
D_{\ell+1}=C_\ell\,\dot\cup\,W. \tag{3}
\]
Moreover, (2) says that every member of \(C_\ell\) is absent from all components strictly after \(\ell+1\), so no such member belongs to \(B_{\ell+1}\).  Intersecting (3) with \(B_{\ell+1}\), and then taking the complement of \(B_{\ell+1}\) in (3), gives respectively
\[
D_{\ell+1}\cap B_{\ell+1}=W\cap B_{\ell+1} \tag{4}
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
|D_{\ell+1}\setminus B_{\ell+1}|
&=|C_\ell|+|W\setminus B_{\ell+1}|\\
&=b_\ell+|W\setminus B_{\ell+1}|. \tag{5}
\end{aligned}
\]
Equations (4) and (5) are precisely the transition specified in \(\texttt{def:ordered-state}\).

They also prove right congruence.  Suppose two root prefixes at cut \(\ell\) have the same pair \((A_\ell,b_\ell)\).  For any common choice of the next root, the right sides of (4) and (5) depend only on that pair and the chosen signature \(R\), so the two extended prefixes have the same next state.  Induction over any fixed suffix-root sequence shows that they have the same state at every subsequent cut.  At the final cut the unprocessed union is empty, whence \(B_c=\varnothing\); consequently their common final state is
\[
(A_c,b_c)=(\varnothing,|D_c|). \tag{6}
\]
Thus two prefixes in the same state admit exactly the same feasible suffix-root sequences.  Notice that (2) is the step that makes the identities of the retired labels in \(C_\ell\) irrelevant; only their number \(b_\ell\) remains relevant.

Initialize the dynamic program at \((A_0,b_0)=(\varnothing,0)\), since both \(D_0\) and \(B_0\) are empty.  Retain a generated state only when
\[
|A_\ell|+b_\ell\leq K. \tag{7}
\]
By (1), condition (7) is exactly \(|D_\ell|\leq K\).  The dictionaries \(D_\ell\) can only grow with \(\ell\), so pruning a state that violates (7) cannot discard a feasible completion.  Conversely, every tuple \(\rho\) with \(|L(\rho)|\leq K\) has \(|D_\ell|\leq K\) at every prefix and, by (4)--(5), follows its exact states without being pruned.  In view of (6), the accepting terminal histories are therefore exactly
\[
\{\rho:|L(\rho)|\leq K\}=\mathcal F_K(\Sigma). \tag{8}
\]
This proves exact feasibility for \(\textsc{K-Fiber}\); by \(\texttt{thm:root-union}\), these tuples correspond bijectively to the claimed causal forest orientations.

For exact multiplicity information, associate to each retained state a number
\[
\nu_\ell(A,b)=
\min\!\left\{2,\#\{\text{root prefixes inducing }(A,b)\}\right\}
\]
and retain that many distinct representative backpointer chains.  Initially \(\nu_0(\varnothing,0)=1\).  At a transition, sum over all predecessor-state and next-root pairs that produce the new state, truncating the sum at two.  Distinct such pairs give distinct extended root prefixes, and
\[
\min\!\left\{2,\sum_t n_t\right\}
=
\min\!\left\{2,\sum_t\min\{2,n_t\}\right\} \tag{9}
\]
for all nonnegative integers \(n_t\).  Hence induction using (9) proves that every stored \(\nu_\ell\) is the exact prefix count truncated at two.  Right congruence proves additionally that, when a state has more than two representatives, any two of them have the same set of feasible suffixes as all the others; retaining only two therefore preserves two-witness recovery, not merely the truncated count.  At cut \(c\), sum \(\nu_c(\varnothing,b)\) over \(0\leq b\leq K\), again truncating at two.  By (8), the result is \(0\), \(1\), or \(2\) according as \(|\mathcal F_K(\Sigma)|\) is zero, one, or at least two.  In the last case the two stored terminal backpointer chains recover two distinct feasible tuples.  This decides \(\textsc{Unique-K-Fiber}\) and solves \(\textsc{Two-K-Fiber}\) exactly.

It remains to establish the stated state and time bounds.  At a fixed cut let \(m=|B_\ell|\).  If \(|A_\ell|=i\), then (7) forces
\[
0\leq i\leq\min(m,K),
\qquad
0\leq b_\ell\leq K-i.
\]
There are \(\binom mi\) choices of \(A_\ell\) and \(K-i+1\) possible values of \(b_\ell\).  Therefore the number of retained states is at most
\[
\sum_{i=0}^{\min(m,K)}\binom mi(K-i+1). \tag{10}
\]
Since \(m\leq q_\pi\), any injection of \(B_\ell\) into a set of cardinality \(q_\pi\) injects every pair counted in (10), preserving \(i\) and \(b_\ell\), into a pair counted by
\[
\mathsf S_{\mathrm{DP}}(q_\pi,K)
=\sum_{i=0}^{\min(q_\pi,K)}\binom{q_\pi}{i}(K-i+1). \tag{11}
\]
This proves the per-cut bound.

The root signatures, the sets \(U_j\), and the cuts \(B_\ell\) are computable by exact rational arithmetic.  There are polynomially many input-derived labels, their numerators and denominators have bit length polynomial in \(N\), and exact equality and finite-set operations on them take \(\operatorname{poly}(N)\) bit operations.  From each retained state there are at most \(|V(T_{\pi(\ell+1)})|\leq p\) outgoing root choices.  The \(c\leq p\) cuts, these outgoing choices, the set operations, the truncated-count operations, and the backpointer operations contribute only a polynomial factor in \(N\).  Together with (11), the total number of bit operations is therefore
\[
\mathsf S_{\mathrm{DP}}(q_\pi,K)\operatorname{poly}(N). \tag{12}
\]
Finally,
\[
\mathsf S_{\mathrm{DP}}(q_\pi,K)
\leq (K+1)\sum_{i=0}^{q_\pi}\binom{q_\pi}{i}
=(K+1)2^{q_\pi}. \tag{13}
\]
If \(q_\pi\geq2\), then \(\binom{q_\pi}{i}\leq q_\pi^i\), and hence
\[
\mathsf S_{\mathrm{DP}}(q_\pi,K)
\leq (K+1)\sum_{i=0}^{K}q_\pi^i
\leq (K+1)^2q_\pi^K. \tag{14}
\]
For \(K=0\) the last quantity is one.  For \(K\geq1\), \(K+1\leq2^K\leq q_\pi^K\), so the right side of (14) is at most \(q_\pi^{3K}\).  Thus (12) is also \(q_\pi^{O(K)}\operatorname{poly}(N)\), completing every asserted bound and algorithmic guarantee.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The exact right congruence simultaneously gives feasibility, uniqueness, and two witnesses with a state count that adapts to both recurrence width and label budget. \textit{Gap.} Graph-decomposition interfaces do not account for exact residual labels that retire permanently, so this state separately records the live subset and the completed-label count. \textit{Consumer.} A supplied component order yields exact orientation and two-sided effect-witness recovery within the refined binomial state bound times \(\operatorname{poly}(N)\).

\begin{theorem}[thm:bounded-choice]\label{thm:bounded-choice}
If \(d=\max_j|V(T_j)|\), all three problems in \(\text{def:k-fiber-problems}\) are solvable in \(d^K\operatorname{poly}(N,K)\) time by branching on the root signatures of a component with empty locus and deduplicating terminal root tuples.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We give the algorithm and prove its search invariant.  For a rational dictionary \(D\), call a component satisfied when \(R_j(D)\ne\varnothing\).  By \(\text{lem:root-locus}\), this is equivalent to the existence of a root \(r\) with \(S_j(r)\subseteq D\), and it is testable in polynomial time.

Start with \(D=\varnothing\).  At a search node, discard the node if \(|D|>K\).  If every component is satisfied, choose one root from each \(R_j(D)\); the resulting tuple \(\rho\) obeys
\[
 L(\rho)=\bigcup_jS_j(r_j)\subseteq D,
\]
so it is a valid witness.  Otherwise choose any component \(j\) with \(R_j(D)=\varnothing\), and create one child for each of its at most \(d\) roots, replacing \(D\) by
\[
 D'=D\cup S_j(r).
\]
Because no signature of this component is contained in \(D\), every child satisfies \(|D'|>|D|\).  Therefore every surviving root-to-leaf path has at most \(K\) branching steps.

For completeness, fix any feasible tuple \(\rho^*\) and write \(L^*=L(\rho^*)\), where \(|L^*|\le K\).  The invariant \(D\subseteq L^*\) holds initially.  Whenever the algorithm branches on component \(j\), follow the child indexed by \(r_j^*\).  Since \(S_j(r_j^*)\subseteq L^*\), that child preserves \(D'\subseteq L^*\) and is never discarded.  Its dictionary size increases strictly, so after at most \(K\) such steps the followed path reaches a satisfied node and returns a witness.  Thus the search returns a witness if and only if the fiber is nonempty.

The search tree has branching factor at most \(d\) and depth at most \(K\).  For \(d\ge2\) it has fewer than \(2d^K\) nodes; for \(d=1\) it has at most \(K+1\) nodes.  Each node uses polynomially many calls to \(\text{lem:root-locus}\), so decision and one-witness recovery take \(d^K\operatorname{poly}(N,K)\) time.

To determine uniqueness and recover two witnesses, first run the search.  If it finds no tuple, certify zero witnesses.  Otherwise let the returned tuple be \(\rho^0\).  For every component \(j\) and every root \(r\ne r_j^0\), run the same search with component \(j\) fixed to \(r\), initial dictionary \(D=S_j(r)\), and only the other components subject to the satisfaction test and branching.  The same invariant proves that this constrained search succeeds exactly when a feasible tuple having \(j\)-th root \(r\) exists.  Any tuple distinct from \(\rho^0\) differs at some component and is therefore found by at least one constrained search.  Conversely every constrained output is feasible and differs from \(\rho^0\).  Stop at the first such output to return two witnesses; if every constrained search fails, \(\rho^0\) is unique.  There are at most \(cd\le p^2\) constrained searches.  Their extra factor is polynomial in \(N\), and each still has depth at most \(K\) and branching factor at most \(d\).  Exact root tuples are compared coordinatewise, so numerical label renamings do not create duplicate witnesses.  This proves all three claims within \(d^K\operatorname{poly}(N,K)\) time.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This proves a nontrivial FPT boundary and rules out hardness gadgets built only from bounded-size components. \textit{Gap.} Generic minimum-label hardness allows unrestricted choice systems and does not respect the bounded root domain induced by a forest component. \textit{Consumer.} Sparse applications with uniformly small chain components obtain exact root and ambiguity recovery even if the unrestricted \(K\)-frontier is hard.

\begin{lemma}[lem:two-witness-transfer]\label{lem:two-witness-transfer}
Using \(t>\max U_0\), append the independent covariance block \(\begin{psmallmatrix}4t&2t\\2t&4t\end{psmallmatrix}\). Its two roots have the identical signature \(\{4t,3t\}\), disjoint from \(U_0\). Thus the original \(\textsc{K-Fiber}\) instance is nonempty if and only if the augmented instance has two distinct witnesses at budget \(K+2\); appending the block last preserves \(q_\pi\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The new block has eigenvalues \(2t\) and \(6t\), so it is positive definite.  Its covariance is rational, its sole edge covariance \(2t\) is nonzero, and hence its centered Gaussian law is faithful to the two-vertex tree.  If either endpoint is chosen as root, the root variance is \(4t\), while the child's regression residual variance is
\[
 4t-\frac{(2t)^2}{4t}=3t.
\]
Thus both possible roots have exactly the signature \(\{4t,3t\}\).

Every label in \(U_0\) is positive and is smaller than \(t\), whereas \(3t>t\) and \(4t>t\).  Hence \(\{3t,4t\}\cap U_0=\varnothing\).  Block independence makes the augmented covariance block diagonal and the augmented forest the disjoint union of the original forest and this edge.  The Gaussian law is faithful to that forest: variables in different components are independent, and within each component the original faithfulness and the nonzero new edge give exactly the forest separations.

Let \(\rho^+\) consist of an original tuple \(\rho\) and one of the two new roots.  Disjointness gives the exact equality
\[
 |L(\rho^+)|=|L(\rho)|+2.
\]
It follows that
\[
 \mathcal F_{K+2}(\Sigma^+)
 =\mathcal F_K(\Sigma)\times\{\text{first new root},\text{second new root}\}.
\]
Therefore the original fiber is nonempty if and only if the augmented fiber contains two distinct witnesses, and either original witness explicitly yields the two augmented witnesses.  This is a statement about root tuples, not about names assigned to equal numerical labels.

Append the new component last in the supplied order.  At every old cut, the possible-label set added to the unprocessed side is \(\{3t,4t\}\), which is disjoint from every possible label on the processed original side; hence the crossing set remains the old \(B_\ell\).  At the cut just before the new block the crossing set is \(U_0\cap\{3t,4t\}=\varnothing\), and after the block it is also empty.  Taking maxima proves that \(q_\pi\) is preserved.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is a query-correct Gaussian transfer from decision to actual two-witness recovery rather than a raw dictionary analogy. \textit{Gap.} YatoSeta2003 requires solution-preserving reductions; the equal-signature faithful block supplies that missing property for this direction only. \textit{Consumer.} A lower bound or solver for feasibility immediately yields a certified consequence for two-witness ambiguity.

\begin{proposition}[prop:k-one-sanity]\label{prop:k-one-sanity}
If \(\rho\in\mathcal F_1(\Sigma)\), then \(\mathcal F_1(\Sigma)=\{\rho\}\). Hence the latent-budget fiber reduces at \(K=1\) to the equal-error-variance uniqueness conclusion of PetersBuhlmann2014.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let \(\rho\in\mathcal F_1(\Sigma)\).  Every component signature is nonempty, so there is a positive number \(\lambda\) such that every regression innovation under \(H_\rho\) has variance \(\lambda\).  Positivity follows from \(\Sigma\succ0\).  In each nontrivial rooted component, faithfulness makes every edge regression coefficient nonzero.  Thus, along an edge \(u\to v\), the SEM recursion gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(X_v)
 =\beta_{vu}^2\operatorname{Var}(X_u)+\lambda>\lambda,
\]
whereas every component root has marginal variance exactly \(\lambda\).  Consequently the root chosen by \(\rho\) is the unique vertex of marginal variance \(\lambda\) in its component; isolated components have only one possible root anyway.

Suppose \(\rho'\in\mathcal F_1(\Sigma)\), and let its common positive innovation variance be \(\mu\).  In either DAG the structural matrix is triangular with unit diagonal, so the covariance determinant is the product of the innovation variances.  Hence
\[
 \det(\Sigma)=\lambda^p=\mu^p.
\]
Since \(\lambda,\mu>0\), this implies \(\lambda=\mu\).  Every root under \(\rho'\) must therefore have marginal variance \(\lambda\), and the uniqueness just proved forces it to equal the root under \(\rho\) in every component.  Thus \(\rho'=\rho\), so \(\mathcal F_1(\Sigma)=\{\rho\}\).  Through the bijection of \(\text{thm:root-union}\), this is exactly the all-equal-error unique-orientation endpoint identified by the equal-error-variance result cited in the statement.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The reduction is the required endpoint check for the new fiber definition. \textit{Gap.} PetersBuhlmann2014 proves the general equal-variance identification result; this proposition verifies that the new combinatorial target has exactly that endpoint. \textit{Consumer.} Users selecting \(K=1\) recover the familiar unique-orientation interpretation rather than a different estimand.

\begin{proposition}[prop:projection-coverage]\label{prop:projection-coverage}
Under \(X^{(1)},\ldots,X^{(n)}\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\mathcal N_p(0,\Sigma)\) and \(c_\alpha^2\ge p(p+1)/(n\alpha)\), the construction in \(\text{def:projection}\) satisfies \(\mathbb P_\Sigma\{\Theta_K(\Sigma;x,y)\subseteq\widehat\Theta_{K,n}\}\ge1-\alpha\). The same event simultaneously retains every orientation in \(\mathcal F_K(\Sigma)\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let
\[
 Z^{(i)}=\Sigma^{-1/2}X^{(i)},
 \qquad
 G=\Sigma^{-1/2}\widehat\Sigma\Sigma^{-1/2}
 =\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n}Z^{(i)}Z^{(i)\mathsf T}.
\]
By \(\text{ass:gaussian-law}\) and \(\text{ass:iid-sample}\), the vectors \(Z^{(i)}\) are independent \(\mathcal N_p(0,I)\).  Thus \(\mathbb E G=I\).  A diagonal entry \((Z_k^{(i)})^2\) has variance \(2\), and an off-diagonal entry \(Z_k^{(i)}Z_l^{(i)}\), \(k\ne l\), has variance \(1\).  Independence across observations and summation over all ordered matrix entries give
\[
 \mathbb E\|G-I\|_{\mathrm F}^{2}
 =\frac1n\{2p+p(p-1)\}
 =\frac{p(p+1)}{n}.
\]
Because \(\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\|A\|_{\mathrm F}\), Markov's inequality and the assumed radius yield
\[
 \mathbb P_\Sigma\{\|G-I\|_{\mathrm{op}}>c_\alpha\}
 \le\frac{p(p+1)}{nc_\alpha^2}\le\alpha.
\]
On the complementary event, the spectral order is
\[
 (1-c_\alpha)I\preceq G\preceq(1+c_\alpha)I.
\]
Congruence by \(\Sigma^{1/2}\) preserves Loewner order, including when \(1-c_\alpha\le0\), and gives
\[
 (1-c_\alpha)\Sigma\preceq\widehat\Sigma
 \preceq(1+c_\alpha)\Sigma.
\]
Therefore \(\Sigma\in\mathcal C_n\) with probability at least \(1-\alpha\).

Now work on this event.  If \(\rho\in\mathcal F_K(\Sigma)\), then its exact signatures computed at \(M=\Sigma\) use at most \(K\) values by the definition of \(\mathcal F_K(\Sigma)\).  Since \(M=\Sigma\in\mathcal C_n\), \(\text{def:projection}\) retains the pair \((\Sigma,\rho)\).  It therefore retains every orientation in the fiber simultaneously, using the structural correspondence proved by \(\text{thm:root-union}\), and its effect projection contains \(\theta_\rho(\Sigma;x,y)\).  Taking all \(\rho\in\mathcal F_K(\Sigma)\) yields
\[
 \Theta_K(\Sigma;x,y)\subseteq\widehat\Theta_{K,n}.
\]
The event on which this deterministic inclusion holds has probability at least \(1-\alpha\), which proves both assertions.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The proposition supplies honest structure-aware uncertainty without applying exact equality directly to the sample covariance. \textit{Gap.} StriederDrton2023 treats the all-equal model and WangKolarDrton2026 projects ordering confidence sets; neither projects through an unknown at-most-\(K\) equality fiber. \textit{Consumer.} SEMgraph can return a conservative orientation and total-effect sensitivity set rather than a selected-graph interval.

\begin{theorem}[thm:tree-effect-fiber]\label{thm:tree-effect-fiber}
For every \((T,\Sigma,P,K)\in\mathfrak M_{p,K}^{\mathrm{forest}}\) and \(x,y\in V\), the following conclusions hold. If \(\mathcal F_K(\Sigma)=\varnothing\), then \(\Theta_K(\Sigma;x,y)=\varnothing\). If \(x=y\), every \(\rho\in\mathcal F_K(\Sigma)\) satisfies \(\theta_\rho(\Sigma;x,x)=1\). If \(x\) and \(y\) lie in different components of \(T\), every \(\rho\in\mathcal F_K(\Sigma)\) satisfies \(\theta_\rho(\Sigma;x,y)=0\). Suppose instead that distinct \(x,y\) lie in component \(T_j\), let \(v\) be the neighbor of \(x\) on the unique \(x\)--\(y\) path, and let \(T_{x;xv}\) denote the vertex set of the component containing \(x\) after deleting the edge \(xv\). Then, for every \(\rho\in\mathcal F_K(\Sigma)\),
\[
 \theta_\rho(\Sigma;x,y)=
 \begin{cases}
 \Sigma_{yx}/\Sigma_{xx},&r_j\in T_{x;xv},\\
 0,&r_j\notin T_{x;xv}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Moreover \(\Sigma_{yx}/\Sigma_{xx}\ne0\), so
\[
 \Theta_K(\Sigma;x,y)\subseteq
 \left\{0,\frac{\Sigma_{yx}}{\Sigma_{xx}}\right\},
\]
with the nonzero value present exactly when a feasible tuple has \(r_j\in T_{x;xv}\), and zero present exactly when a feasible tuple has \(r_j\notin T_{x;xv}\). For a supplied component order, each restricted feasibility condition and a corresponding root-tuple witness are computable in
\[
 \left\{\sum_{i=0}^{\min(q_\pi,K)}
 \binom{q_\pi}{i}(K-i+1)\right\}\operatorname{poly}(N)
\]
time; they are also computable in \(d^K\operatorname{poly}(N,K)\) time when \(d=\max_h|V(T_h)|\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The definition of the effect fiber gives
\[
 \Theta_K(\Sigma;x,y)
 =\{\theta_\rho(\Sigma;x,y):\rho\in\mathcal F_K(\Sigma)\}.
\]
Therefore \(\mathcal F_K(\Sigma)=\varnothing\) immediately implies \(\Theta_K(\Sigma;x,y)=\varnothing\). We now fix \(\rho\in\mathcal F_K(\Sigma)\). By \(\text{thm:root-union}\), \(H_\rho\) is the corresponding collider-free forest DAG, and by the definition of \(B_\rho(\Sigma)\), a nonroot vertex \(z\) with its unique parent \(u\) has coefficient
\[
 [B_\rho(\Sigma)]_{zu}
 =\frac{\operatorname{Cov}_P(X_z,X_u)}{\operatorname{Var}_P(X_u)}
 =\frac{\Sigma_{zu}}{\Sigma_{uu}}.
\]
The denominator is strictly positive because \(\Sigma\in\mathbb S_{++}^{p}\).

Since \(H_\rho\) is acyclic, a topological ordering makes \(B_\rho(\Sigma)\) strictly triangular. Hence \(B_\rho(\Sigma)^p=0\) and direct multiplication gives the finite geometric identity
\[
 (I-B_\rho(\Sigma))^{-1}
 =I+B_\rho(\Sigma)+\cdots+B_\rho(\Sigma)^{p-1}.
\]
For \(k\geq1\), the \((y,x)\) entry of \(B_\rho(\Sigma)^k\) is the sum, over directed walks of length \(k\) from \(x\) to \(y\), of the products of their edge coefficients. A directed acyclic graph has no directed walk with a repeated vertex, and the underlying forest has at most one undirected path between two vertices. Thus \(\theta_\rho(\Sigma;x,y)\) is one on the diagonal, is zero when \(x\) and \(y\) are in different components, and for distinct vertices in one component is the product along the unique \(x\)--\(y\) path if that path is directed from \(x\) to \(y\), and zero otherwise. This proves the first three cases, including \(\theta_\rho(\Sigma;x,x)=1\).

Now let distinct \(x,y\) lie in \(T_j\), and write their unique path as
\[
 w_0=x,w_1=v,w_2,\ldots,w_m=y.
\]
The orientation \(H_{j,r_j}\) points away from \(r_j\). After deleting \(xv\), the path from \(r_j\) to \(y\) crosses that edge from \(x\) to \(v\) if and only if \(r_j\in T_{x;xv}\). In that event every edge on the displayed path points from \(w_t\) to \(w_{t+1}\). If \(r_j\notin T_{x;xv}\), its first edge points from \(v\) to \(x\), so the unique path is not directed from \(x\) to \(y\). The path-sum calculation consequently yields
\[
 \theta_\rho(\Sigma;x,y)
 =\begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\prod_{t=0}^{m-1}
 \frac{\Sigma_{w_{t+1}w_t}}{\Sigma_{w_tw_t}},
 &r_j\in T_{x;xv},\\[3mm]
 0,&r_j\notin T_{x;xv}.
 \end{cases}
\]

We derive the asserted simplification of the product from the Gaussian forest law. Faithfulness in \(\text{ass:forest-faithfulness}\) includes the global Markov implication for \(T\). For \(1\leq t<m\), the vertex \(w_t\) separates \(x\) from \(w_{t+1}\) in the forest; hence
\[
 X_x\mathbin{\perp\!\!\!\perp}X_{w_{t+1}}\mid X_{w_t}.
\]
For completeness, the scalar Gaussian covariance identity used here follows directly as follows. Put
\[
 R_x=X_x-\frac{\Sigma_{xw_t}}{\Sigma_{w_tw_t}}X_{w_t},
 \qquad
 R_{t+1}=X_{w_{t+1}}
 -\frac{\Sigma_{w_{t+1}w_t}}{\Sigma_{w_tw_t}}X_{w_t}.
\]
The centered vector \((R_x,R_{t+1},X_{w_t})\) is Gaussian, and each residual has zero covariance with \(X_{w_t}\). Its Gaussian density therefore factors between \((R_x,R_{t+1})\) and \(X_{w_t}\). Thus its conditional covariance is the unconditional residual covariance,
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(X_x,X_{w_{t+1}}\mid X_{w_t})
 =\Sigma_{xw_{t+1}}
 -\frac{\Sigma_{xw_t}\Sigma_{w_tw_{t+1}}}{\Sigma_{w_tw_t}}.
\]
Conditional independence makes the left side zero, so
\[
 \Sigma_{xw_{t+1}}
 =\Sigma_{xw_t}
 \frac{\Sigma_{w_tw_{t+1}}}{\Sigma_{w_tw_t}}.
\]
Starting from
\[
 \frac{\Sigma_{xw_1}}{\Sigma_{xx}}
 =\frac{\Sigma_{w_1w_0}}{\Sigma_{w_0w_0}},
\]
and applying the recurrence for \(t=1,\ldots,m-1\) proves by induction that
\[
 \frac{\Sigma_{yx}}{\Sigma_{xx}}
 =\prod_{t=0}^{m-1}
 \frac{\Sigma_{w_{t+1}w_t}}{\Sigma_{w_tw_t}}.
\]
Substitution into the path-product display proves the claimed formula.

The empty set does not separate distinct connected vertices \(x\) and \(y\) in \(T\). Faithfulness therefore implies that \(X_x\) and \(X_y\) are not marginally independent. Their bivariate Gaussian marginal is independent exactly when its off-diagonal covariance is zero, as is seen by factorizing its Gaussian density. Hence \(\Sigma_{yx}\ne0\); positive definiteness also gives \(\Sigma_{xx}>0\). The two displayed candidate effects are consequently distinct. Taking the image over all feasible \(\rho\) now proves both directions of each membership assertion: a tuple on the indicated root side supplies the corresponding value, and the pointwise formula shows that no tuple on the opposite side can supply that value.

It remains to prove the algorithmic assertions. In the ordered dynamic program, when component \(T_j\) is processed, restrict its transition list to roots in \(T_{x;xv}\), or to roots in its complement, respectively. All other transitions are unchanged. The transition invariant and right-congruence proof in \(\text{thm:ordered-dp}\) applies verbatim to this subcollection, and restriction cannot increase any cut's state set. Thus each side-feasibility test, together with a retained backpointer witness when it succeeds, costs
\[
 \left\{\sum_{i=0}^{\min(q_\pi,K)}
 \binom{q_\pi}{i}(K-i+1)\right\}\operatorname{poly}(N).
\]
The width is still bounded by the supplied \(q_\pi\), since removing roots can only remove possible labels.

For the bounded-component algorithm of \(\text{thm:bounded-choice}\), fix an allowed root \(r\) of \(T_j\), initialize its dictionary search at \(D=S_j(r)\), and apply the same empty-locus branching search to the remaining components. If \(|D|>K\), discard this branch. Otherwise its dictionary can strictly increase at most \(K-|D|\) times, so the search has depth at most \(K\), branching factor at most \(d\), and cost \(d^K\operatorname{poly}(N,K)\). The invariant from \(\text{thm:bounded-choice}\) proves that it succeeds exactly when a feasible tuple has the fixed root \(r\). Running it for the at most \(d\) roots on the requested side changes only the polynomial factor, and the first successful run returns the required witness. This proves both restricted-root algorithms and completes the theorem.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This theorem converts structural root ambiguity into an exact two-value population total-effect sensitivity analysis. \textit{Gap.} General covariance-region projection does not by itself expose the forest path geometry or the exact root-side membership tests. \textit{Consumer.} SEMgraph can return zero, the covariance ratio, or both, and attach a feasible root-tuple witness to each reported effect.

\section{Interpretation}

The causal target remains the feasible orientation fiber rather than an observed regression coefficient. The tree-effect theorem proves their relationship: for distinct vertices in one component, a feasible root on the \(x\)-side of the first \(x\)--\(y\) path edge directs the entire path from \(x\) to \(y\), and its directed coefficient product telescopes to the observed covariance ratio \(\Sigma_{yx}/\Sigma_{xx}\); a feasible root on the other side yields no directed path and effect zero. Faithfulness makes the ratio nonzero, so the two memberships are distinguished exactly. The refined-binomial-bound ordered dynamic program and the bounded-component search test both root-side restrictions and return witnesses. The confidence projection has a different role: it contains the resulting population effect fiber with the stated probability by ranging over covariances in \(\mathcal C_n\), but no efficient method for optimizing that range is asserted.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

A common-center star encoding of Set Cover has a universal two-label center-root witness, while varying centers creates complementary residual labels. Moreover, every edge satisfies \(a_u b_{v\mid u}=a_v b_{u\mid v}\), and rational covariance requires a rational-square edge reconstruction. The first reduction test therefore enumerates one three-choice selector and one compatibility component, including all internal roots; any unintended witness or nonconstant auxiliary-label charge rejects that template. The existing twin-star family also rules out raw representative compression at empty width-one cuts, but it does not rule out a different symbolic FPT algorithm.

\section{Honest scope}

The root-union, root-locus, bounded-choice, refined-binomial-bound ordered-DP, piecewise rational-tree-realization, two-witness-transfer, \(K=1\), tree-effect-fiber, and projection-coverage results are proved in this note. The ordered dynamic program uses \(\sum_{i=0}^{\min(q_\pi,K)}\binom{q_\pi}{i}(K-i+1)\) states per cut and retains exact multiplicity and two witnesses. The tree-effect theorem proves that a within-component population effect fiber has at most the two values zero and \(\Sigma_{yx}/\Sigma_{xx}\), characterizes their presence by root-side feasibility, and recovers witnesses in both positive algorithmic regimes. The headline unrestricted \(K\)-classification, the arbitrary-algorithm \(q_\pi\) lower bound, and the unrestricted uniqueness transfer remain open-ended questions. Every hardness gadget must still verify the realization theorem's hypotheses and enumerate unintended roots and auxiliary labels. The confidence projection claims simultaneous coverage, not efficient optimization over \(M\in\mathcal C_n\), likelihood-ratio calibration, unknown-mean adjustment, or robustness to approximate equality.
