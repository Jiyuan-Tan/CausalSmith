%%% FIELD proof-root-union
Fix an input in \(\mathfrak M_{p,K}^{\mathrm{forest}}\).  By \(\text{ass:gaussian-law}\), \(P\) is the centered nonsingular Gaussian law with covariance \(\Sigma\), and by \(\text{ass:forest-faithfulness}\), \(P\) is Markov and faithful to \(T\) and to each collider-free rooting of \(T\).

First characterize the relevant DAGs without using the variance labels.  On a connected component with \(m\) vertices, every orientation is acyclic because the skeleton is a tree.  An orientation having no unshielded collider has indegree at most one at every vertex: two incoming edges would have nonadjacent tails because a forest contains no triangle.  The sum of the indegrees is the number of edges, namely \(m-1\).  Hence exactly one vertex has indegree zero and every other vertex has indegree one.  Calling the unique indegree-zero vertex \(r\), induction on graph distance from \(r\) shows that every edge points away from \(r\), so the orientation is \(H_{j,r}\).  Conversely, \(H_{j,r}\) has one indegree-zero vertex and all other indegrees equal to one, and hence has no collider.  This also covers an isolated vertex.

We verify the Markov-equivalence step directly.  In an away-from-root orientation, the unique path between two vertices has the form of two directed arms leaving their lowest common ancestor and therefore contains no collider.  Conditioning blocks that path exactly when the conditioning set contains an internal path vertex.  Thus every \(H_\rho\) has precisely the separation model of the undirected forest \(T\), so all such rootings are Markov equivalent.  Conversely, adjacency is determined by a DAG's separation model: adjacent vertices cannot be d-separated by a set excluding the endpoints because their one-edge path stays active, whereas for nonadjacent vertices one of the two vertices is a nondescendant of the other and the latter's parent set d-separates them by the local directed Markov argument.  Hence a DAG equivalent to a rooting has skeleton \(T\).  If it contained \(u\to v\leftarrow w\), the forest forces \(u\) and \(w\) to be nonadjacent and their unique path would be blocked without conditioning; in every rooting that path has no collider and is active without conditioning, a contradiction.  It therefore has no collider and the preceding indegree count makes it \(H_\rho\) for a unique root tuple.  Injectivity is explicit because the root is the unique indegree-zero vertex in each component.

It remains to identify the innovation labels.  Fix \(\rho=(r_1,\ldots,r_c)\).  The Gaussian density factorizes according to \(H_\rho\), because faithfulness includes the Markov property.  Every nonroot vertex \(v\) has a unique parent \(u\), while a root has no parent.  Gaussian conditioning therefore gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(X_{r_j})=\Sigma_{r_jr_j}=a_{r_j},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(X_v\mid X_u)
 =\Sigma_{vv}-\frac{\Sigma_{vu}^{2}}{\Sigma_{uu}}
 =b_{v\mid u}.
\]
All denominators are positive because \(\Sigma\succ0\).  After the triangular change of variables from \(X\) to the root variables and regression residuals, the factorized Gaussian density is a product of their one-dimensional Gaussian densities.  Hence these quantities are precisely the mutually independent regression-innovation variances of the DAG.  By \(\text{def:root-signature}\), their set on component \(j\) is \(S_j(r_j)\), and their set on the whole forest is
\[
 L(\rho)=\bigcup_{j=1}^{c}S_j(r_j).
\]
Consequently the DAG has at most \(K\) distinct innovation variances if and only if \(|L(\rho)|\le K\), which is equivalent to \(\rho\in\mathcal F_K(\Sigma)\).  Combining this equivalence with the root--DAG bijection proves the stated bijection.  Notice that the causal fiber is not defined by an externally supplied equality partition: equality is tested on the observable rational residuals in \(\text{def:root-signature}\), and the argument above proves that these residuals equal the causal innovation variances.

%%% FIELD proof-rational-tree-realization
Write \(a_v=\widetilde a_v\).  We first prove sufficiency.  Suppose that for each edge \(uv\) there is \(h_{uv}\in(0,1)\cap\mathbb Q\) satisfying the two displayed residual identities in the statement and that
\[
 a_u a_v(1-h_{uv})=c_{uv}^{,2}
\]
for a chosen \(c_{uv}\in\mathbb Q\setminus\{0\}\).  Fix an arbitrary root \(r\), orient the tree away from \(r\), and, for every directed edge \(u\to v\), put
\[
 \beta_{vu}=\frac{c_{uv}}{a_u},\qquad
 \omega_r=a_r,qquad
 \omega_v=a_vh_{uv}.
\]
All innovation variances are strictly positive because every \(a_v>0\) and every \(h_{uv}\in(0,1)\).  Let the innovations be independent centered Gaussians and define recursively
\[
 X_r=\varepsilon_r,
 \qquad
 X_v=\beta_{vu}X_u+\varepsilon_v.
\]
Induction away from the root gives \(\operatorname{Var}(X_r)=a_r\).  If \(\operatorname{Var}(X_u)=a_u\), independence of \(X_u\) and \(\varepsilon_v\) gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(X_v)
 =\frac{c_{uv}^{,2}}{a_u^2}a_u+a_vh_{uv}
 =a_v(1-h_{uv})+a_vh_{uv}=a_v,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Cov}(X_u,X_v)=c_{uv}.
\]
Thus every vertex and edge covariance has the prescribed value.

For arbitrary distinct \(x,y\), let \(z\) be their lowest common ancestor in this rooting.  Repeated substitution along the two directed paths from \(z\), using independence of innovations in disjoint descendant branches, yields
\[
 M_{xy}
 =a_z
 \left(\prod_{e\in\operatorname{path}(z,x)}\frac{c_e}{a_{\operatorname{tail}(e)}}\right)
 \left(\prod_{e\in\operatorname{path}(z,y)}\frac{c_e}{a_{\operatorname{tail}(e)}}\right)
 =\frac{\prod_{uv\in\operatorname{path}(x,y)}c_{uv}}
 {\prod_{w\in\operatorname{intpath}(x,y)}a_w}.
\]
The last identity also applies when \(z=x\) or \(z=y\), with empty products equal to one.  It is independent of the chosen root, so the covariance is exactly the piecewise matrix \(M\) in the statement for every root.

In a topological order the structural system has the form \(X=(I-B)^{-1}\varepsilon\), where \(I-B\) is triangular with unit diagonal.  Hence
\[
 M=(I-B)^{-1}\operatorname{diag}(\omega_v)(I-B)^{-\mathsf T}\succ0.
\]
This proves positive definiteness rather than assuming it.  The Gaussian precision matrix is obtained by expanding
\[
 \sum_v\frac{(X_v-\beta_{v,\operatorname{pa}(v)}X_{\operatorname{pa}(v)})^2}{\omega_v},
\]
with the root term interpreted as \(X_r^2/\omega_r\).  Its off-diagonal entry on a parent--child edge is \(-\beta_{vu}/\omega_v\ne0\), and its off-diagonal entries on nonedges vanish.  Therefore its concentration graph is exactly \(T_j\).  To verify faithfulness, condition on any vertex set \(C\).  The conditional precision on the remaining vertices is the corresponding principal submatrix and hence is a forest with the same nonzero entries on every surviving edge.  If \(C\) separates \(x\) and \(y\), the conditional precision is block diagonal, so the conditional covariance vanishes.  If it does not separate them, the unique surviving path from \(x\) to \(y\) has only nonzero edge entries; successive leaf elimination, or equivalently the cofactor expansion along that unique path, expresses the conditional covariance numerator as the product of those nonzero edge entries times positive principal minors of the branches.  It is therefore nonzero.  Thus conditional independence holds exactly for forest separation, proving faithfulness.

On an edge \(uv\), the already established diagonal and edge entries give, in both directions,
\[
 M_{vv}-\frac{M_{vu}^{2}}{M_{uu}}
 =a_v-\frac{c_{uv}^{,2}}{a_u}
 =a_vh_{uv}=\widetilde b_{v\mid u},
\qquad
 M_{uu}-\frac{M_{uv}^{2}}{M_{vv}}
 =a_uh_{uv}=\widetilde b_{u\mid v}.
\]
All entries are rational by the path formula.

For necessity, suppose instead that a rational positive-definite Gaussian covariance \(M\) faithful to \(T_j\) realizes the specified vertex variances and directed-edge residuals.  For each edge define
\[
 c_{uv}=M_{uv},
 \qquad
 h_{uv}=1-\frac{M_{uv}^{2}}{M_{uu}M_{vv}}.
\]
Positive definiteness of the \(2\times2\) principal minor gives \(h_{uv}>0\), and faithfulness gives \(M_{uv}\ne0\), whence \(h_{uv}<1\).  Rationality of \(M\) gives \(h_{uv}\in\mathbb Q\), and direct substitution gives
\[
 \widetilde b_{v\mid u}=\widetilde a_vh_{uv},
 \qquad
 \widetilde b_{u\mid v}=\widetilde a_uh_{uv},
 \qquad
 \widetilde a_u\widetilde a_v(1-h_{uv})=M_{uv}^2=c_{uv}^{,2}\ne0.
\]
The Gaussian Markov property on the tree implies that whenever an internal vertex \(z\) separates \(x\) and \(y\),
\[
 M_{xy}=\frac{M_{xz}M_{zy}}{M_{zz}}.
\]
Applying this identity successively along the unique \(x\)--\(y\) path proves the same path-product formula.  This establishes all necessary conditions and the converse formula.

If the input rationals have maximum numerator-and-denominator bit length \(B\), a path entry uses at most \(|V(T_j)|-1\) edge numerators and at most \(|V(T_j)|-2\) internal-vertex divisions.  Before cancellation its numerator and denominator therefore have bit length \(O(|V(T_j)|B)\); reduction cannot increase that length.  The construction consequently has polynomial bit complexity.

Finally, in the stated three-vertex example,
\[
 M_{13}=\frac{(3/2)(7/8)}{7/4}=\frac34,
\]
and the leading principal minors are
\[
 3,\qquad 3\cdot\frac74-\left(\frac32\right)^2=3,
 \qquad \det(M)=3.
\]
The four directed residual computations are
\[
 \frac74-\frac{(3/2)^2}{3}=1,
 \qquad
 3-\frac{(3/2)^2}{7/4}=\frac{12}{7},
 \qquad
 \frac{23}{16}-\frac{(7/8)^2}{7/4}=1,
 \qquad
 \frac74-\frac{(7/8)^2}{23/16}=\frac{28}{23}.
\]
They agree with the stated \(h_{12}=4/7\) and \(h_{23}=16/23\).  Both edge covariances are nonzero, so the faithfulness argument above applies and completes the check.

%%% FIELD proof-root-locus
Fix \(T_j\), a dictionary \(D\), and a candidate root \(r\).  For an edge \(uv\), deleting the edge gives the two sides \(T_{u;uv}\) and \(T_{v;uv}\).  In the orientation away from \(r\), the edge is oriented \(u\to v\) exactly when \(r\in T_{u;uv}\).  Consequently, if \(b_{v\mid u}\notin D\), the forbidden orientation \(u\to v\) is avoided exactly when
\[
 r\in T_{v;uv}.
\]
Imposing this condition for every ordered edge pair whose directed residual is absent from \(D\) gives exactly
\[
 r\in Q_j(D)
 =\bigcap_{(u,v):\,uv\in E(T_j),\ b_{v\mid u}\notin D}T_{v;uv}.
\]
The convention for an empty intersection correctly imposes no edge restriction.  By \(\text{def:root-signature}\), after these edge conditions hold, \(S_j(r)\subseteq D\) is equivalent to the remaining root condition \(a_r\in D\).  Thus
\[
 \{r:S_j(r)\subseteq D\}
 =Q_j(D)\cap\{r:a_r\in D\}=R_j(D).
\]

Every half-tree \(T_{v;uv}\) is connected and geodesically convex: it contains the unique path between any two of its vertices.  An intersection of geodesically convex subsets of a tree is geodesically convex.  Hence \(Q_j(D)\) is either empty or connected, and, when nonempty, is a subtree.

For computation, first compare every rational \(b_{v\mid u}\) with the rational dictionary entries using exact cross multiplication.  Initialize a Boolean mark on all vertices and, for each missing directed residual, intersect the marks with the corresponding side of the deleted edge by a tree traversal.  Finally retain marked vertices whose rational \(a_v\) occurs in \(D\).  There are at most \(2|E(T_j)|\) directed residual tests, every traversal is linear in \(|V(T_j)|\), and all rational comparisons use bit length bounded by the input encoding length \(N\).  The total number of bit operations is therefore polynomial in \(N\).

%%% FIELD proof-ordered-dp
For \(0\le\ell\le c\), let
\[
 D_\ell=\bigcup_{h\le\ell}S_{\pi(h)}(r_{\pi(h)})
\]
be the label set of a chosen prefix.  Define \(A_\ell=D_\ell\cap B_\ell\) and \(b_\ell=|D_\ell\setminus B_\ell|\), as in \(\text{def:ordered-state}\).  A label in \(D_\ell\setminus B_\ell\) occurs in a processed component but in no unprocessed component: if it occurred on both sides, it would belong to \(B_\ell\).  Thus such a completed label can never occur in \(S_{\pi(\ell+1)}(r)\).

Put \(S=S_{\pi(\ell+1)}(r)\) and \(W=A_\ell\cup S\).  The preceding disjointness gives
\[
 D_{\ell+1}=(D_\ell\setminus B_\ell)\,\dot\cup\,W.
\]
Moreover, every member of \(D_\ell\setminus B_\ell\) remains absent from all later components and hence is absent from \(B_{\ell+1}\).  It follows that
\[
 D_{\ell+1}\cap B_{\ell+1}=W\cap B_{\ell+1}=A_{\ell+1},
\]
and, with disjoint unions,
\[
 |D_{\ell+1}\setminus B_{\ell+1}|
 =b_\ell+|W\setminus B_{\ell+1}|=b_{\ell+1}.
\]
Therefore the transition in \(\text{def:ordered-state}\) computes the exact state of every extended prefix.

The same identities prove right congruence.  Two prefixes with the same pair \((A_\ell,b_\ell)\) have the same completed-label count; their completed labels cannot occur in any suffix; and their only actual labels visible to a suffix are the common set \(A_\ell\).  Hence every fixed sequence of suffix roots produces the same subsequent states and the same final union cardinality from either prefix.  No unrecorded numerical identity can affect a suffix, because all labels that can recur across the cut are, by definition, in \(B_\ell\).

At cut zero the sole state is \((\varnothing,0)\).  Process all roots of the next component by the displayed transition, merging equal states and discarding a state whenever
\[
 b_\ell+|A_\ell|=|D_\ell|>K.
\]
At the final cut \(B_c=\varnothing\), so \(A_c=\varnothing\) and \(b_c=|D_c|\).  Thus a final state is retained exactly when its root tuple lies in \(\mathcal F_K(\Sigma)\).  The bijection in \(\text{thm:root-union}\) then gives the asserted causal interpretation.

To track multiplicity, attach to every state at most two distinct prefix root tuples.  Every root tuple has a unique sequence of transitions, so distinct stored prefixes are never two paths for the same tuple.  If a state receives more than two prefixes, any two suffice: by right congruence every feasible suffix extends both, producing two distinct full tuples.  Conversely, whenever two feasible full tuples exist, either their prefixes occupy different states until a later merge, in which case the merge retains two distinct backpointers, or they remain in different final transition histories; in both cases two final backpointers survive.  Hence zero, one, or at least two witnesses is determined exactly, and two retained full backpointers solve \(\textsc{Two-K-Fiber}\) while the count truncated at two decides \(\textsc{Unique-K-Fiber}\).

For a fixed cut, \(A_\ell\) is a subset of \(B_\ell\), giving at most \(2^{|B_\ell|}\le2^{q_\pi}\) choices, and \(b_\ell\in\{0,\ldots,K\}\), giving at most
\[
 (K+1)2^{q_\pi}
\]
states.  Root signatures are computed once by exact rational arithmetic.  From each state there are at most \(|V(T_{\pi(\ell+1)})|\le p\) transitions, and indexed finite-set operations, rational-label comparisons, and two backpointer updates take \(\operatorname{poly}(N)\) bit operations.  Multiplication by the \(c\le p\) cuts and by the roots is absorbed into \(\operatorname{poly}(N)\).  The total is therefore \((K+1)2^{q_\pi}\operatorname{poly}(N)\), as claimed.

%%% FIELD proof-bounded-choice
We give the algorithm and prove its search invariant.  For a rational dictionary \(D\), call a component satisfied when \(R_j(D)\ne\varnothing\).  By \(\text{lem:root-locus}\), this is equivalent to the existence of a root \(r\) with \(S_j(r)\subseteq D\), and it is testable in polynomial time.

Start with \(D=\varnothing\).  At a search node, discard the node if \(|D|>K\).  If every component is satisfied, choose one root from each \(R_j(D)\); the resulting tuple \(\rho\) obeys
\[
 L(\rho)=\bigcup_jS_j(r_j)\subseteq D,
\]
so it is a valid witness.  Otherwise choose any component \(j\) with \(R_j(D)=\varnothing\), and create one child for each of its at most \(d\) roots, replacing \(D\) by
\[
 D'=D\cup S_j(r).
\]
Because no signature of this component is contained in \(D\), every child satisfies \(|D'|>|D|\).  Therefore every surviving root-to-leaf path has at most \(K\) branching steps.

For completeness, fix any feasible tuple \(\rho^*\) and write \(L^*=L(\rho^*)\), where \(|L^*|\le K\).  The invariant \(D\subseteq L^*\) holds initially.  Whenever the algorithm branches on component \(j\), follow the child indexed by \(r_j^*\).  Since \(S_j(r_j^*)\subseteq L^*\), that child preserves \(D'\subseteq L^*\) and is never discarded.  Its dictionary size increases strictly, so after at most \(K\) such steps the followed path reaches a satisfied node and returns a witness.  Thus the search returns a witness if and only if the fiber is nonempty.

The search tree has branching factor at most \(d\) and depth at most \(K\).  For \(d\ge2\) it has fewer than \(2d^K\) nodes; for \(d=1\) it has at most \(K+1\) nodes.  Each node uses polynomially many calls to \(\text{lem:root-locus}\), so decision and one-witness recovery take \(d^K\operatorname{poly}(N,K)\) time.

To determine uniqueness and recover two witnesses, first run the search.  If it finds no tuple, certify zero witnesses.  Otherwise let the returned tuple be \(\rho^0\).  For every component \(j\) and every root \(r\ne r_j^0\), run the same search with component \(j\) fixed to \(r\), initial dictionary \(D=S_j(r)\), and only the other components subject to the satisfaction test and branching.  The same invariant proves that this constrained search succeeds exactly when a feasible tuple having \(j\)-th root \(r\) exists.  Any tuple distinct from \(\rho^0\) differs at some component and is therefore found by at least one constrained search.  Conversely every constrained output is feasible and differs from \(\rho^0\).  Stop at the first such output to return two witnesses; if every constrained search fails, \(\rho^0\) is unique.  There are at most \(cd\le p^2\) constrained searches.  Their extra factor is polynomial in \(N\), and each still has depth at most \(K\) and branching factor at most \(d\).  Exact root tuples are compared coordinatewise, so numerical label renamings do not create duplicate witnesses.  This proves all three claims within \(d^K\operatorname{poly}(N,K)\) time.

The new block has eigenvalues \(2t\) and \(6t\), so it is positive definite.  Its covariance is rational, its sole edge covariance \(2t\) is nonzero, and hence its centered Gaussian law is faithful to the two-vertex tree.  If either endpoint is chosen as root, the root variance is \(4t\), while the child's regression residual variance is
\[
 4t-\frac{(2t)^2}{4t}=3t.
\]
Thus both possible roots have exactly the signature \(\{4t,3t\}\).

Every label in \(U_0\) is positive and is smaller than \(t\), whereas \(3t>t\) and \(4t>t\).  Hence \(\{3t,4t\}\cap U_0=\varnothing\).  Block independence makes the augmented covariance block diagonal and the augmented forest the disjoint union of the original forest and this edge.  The Gaussian law is faithful to that forest: variables in different components are independent, and within each component the original faithfulness and the nonzero new edge give exactly the forest separations.

Let \(\rho^+\) consist of an original tuple \(\rho\) and one of the two new roots.  Disjointness gives the exact equality
\[
 |L(\rho^+)|=|L(\rho)|+2.
\]
It follows that
\[
 \mathcal F_{K+2}(\Sigma^+)
 =\mathcal F_K(\Sigma)\times\{\text{first new root},\text{second new root}\}.
\]
Therefore the original fiber is nonempty if and only if the augmented fiber contains two distinct witnesses, and either original witness explicitly yields the two augmented witnesses.  This is a statement about root tuples, not about names assigned to equal numerical labels.

Append the new component last in the supplied order.  At every old cut, the possible-label set added to the unprocessed side is \(\{3t,4t\}\), which is disjoint from every possible label on the processed original side; hence the crossing set remains the old \(B_\ell\).  At the cut just before the new block the crossing set is \(U_0\cap\{3t,4t\}=\varnothing\), and after the block it is also empty.  Taking maxima proves that \(q_\pi\) is preserved.

%%% FIELD proof-k-one-sanity
Let \(\rho\in\mathcal F_1(\Sigma)\).  Every component signature is nonempty, so there is a positive number \(\lambda\) such that every regression innovation under \(H_\rho\) has variance \(\lambda\).  Positivity follows from \(\Sigma\succ0\).  In each nontrivial rooted component, faithfulness makes every edge regression coefficient nonzero.  Thus, along an edge \(u\to v\), the SEM recursion gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(X_v)
 =\beta_{vu}^2\operatorname{Var}(X_u)+\lambda>\lambda,
\]
whereas every component root has marginal variance exactly \(\lambda\).  Consequently the root chosen by \(\rho\) is the unique vertex of marginal variance \(\lambda\) in its component; isolated components have only one possible root anyway.

Suppose \(\rho'\in\mathcal F_1(\Sigma)\), and let its common positive innovation variance be \(\mu\).  In either DAG the structural matrix is triangular with unit diagonal, so the covariance determinant is the product of the innovation variances.  Hence
\[
 \det(\Sigma)=\lambda^p=\mu^p.
\]
Since \(\lambda,\mu>0\), this implies \(\lambda=\mu\).  Every root under \(\rho'\) must therefore have marginal variance \(\lambda\), and the uniqueness just proved forces it to equal the root under \(\rho\) in every component.  Thus \(\rho'=\rho\), so \(\mathcal F_1(\Sigma)=\{\rho\}\).  Through the bijection of \(\text{thm:root-union}\), this is exactly the all-equal-error unique-orientation endpoint identified by the equal-error-variance result cited in the statement.

%%% FIELD proof-projection-coverage
Let
\[
 Z^{(i)}=\Sigma^{-1/2}X^{(i)},
 \qquad
 G=\Sigma^{-1/2}\widehat\Sigma\Sigma^{-1/2}
 =\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n}Z^{(i)}Z^{(i)\mathsf T}.
\]
By \(\text{ass:gaussian-law}\) and \(\text{ass:iid-sample}\), the vectors \(Z^{(i)}\) are independent \(\mathcal N_p(0,I)\).  Thus \(\mathbb E G=I\).  A diagonal entry \((Z_k^{(i)})^2\) has variance \(2\), and an off-diagonal entry \(Z_k^{(i)}Z_l^{(i)}\), \(k\ne l\), has variance \(1\).  Independence across observations and summation over all ordered matrix entries give
\[
 \mathbb E\|G-I\|_{\mathrm F}^{2}
 =\frac1n\{2p+p(p-1)\}
 =\frac{p(p+1)}{n}.
\]
Because \(\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\|A\|_{\mathrm F}\), Markov's inequality and the assumed radius yield
\[
 \mathbb P_\Sigma\{\|G-I\|_{\mathrm{op}}>c_\alpha\}
 \le\frac{p(p+1)}{nc_\alpha^2}\le\alpha.
\]
On the complementary event, the spectral order is
\[
 (1-c_\alpha)I\preceq G\preceq(1+c_\alpha)I.
\]
Congruence by \(\Sigma^{1/2}\) preserves Loewner order, including when \(1-c_\alpha\le0\), and gives
\[
 (1-c_\alpha)\Sigma\preceq\widehat\Sigma
 \preceq(1+c_\alpha)\Sigma.
\]
Therefore \(\Sigma\in\mathcal C_n\) with probability at least \(1-\alpha\).

Now work on this event.  If \(\rho\in\mathcal F_K(\Sigma)\), then its exact signatures computed at \(M=\Sigma\) use at most \(K\) values by the definition of \(\mathcal F_K(\Sigma)\).  Since \(M=\Sigma\in\mathcal C_n\), \(\text{def:projection}\) retains the pair \((\Sigma,\rho)\).  It therefore retains every orientation in the fiber simultaneously, using the structural correspondence proved by \(\text{thm:root-union}\), and its effect projection contains \(\theta_\rho(\Sigma;x,y)\).  Taking all \(\rho\in\mathcal F_K(\Sigma)\) yields
\[
 \Theta_K(\Sigma;x,y)\subseteq\widehat\Theta_{K,n}.
\]
The event on which this deterministic inclusion holds has probability at least \(1-\alpha\), which proves both assertions.

%%% FIELD partial-k-frontier
The closed results establish an exact dictionary-feasibility test through \(R_j(D)\), an explicit decision-and-witness algorithm with running time \(d^K\operatorname{poly}(N,K)\), and a complete inverse-realization criterion for any proposed rational tree gadget.  Thus fixed-parameter tractability is proved whenever component size \(d\) is bounded as a function of \(K\).  What remains unresolved is whether the root-locus geometry yields an \(f(K)\operatorname{poly}(N)\) algorithm for unbounded \(d\), or instead admits a faithful rational parameterized-hard family with transformed budget linear in the source parameter.

Uniqueness and two-witness recovery are solved in \(d^K\operatorname{poly}(N,K)\) time by forced-root searches, and in \((K+1)2^{q_\pi}\operatorname{poly}(N)\) time by the ordered dynamic program.  The independent two-vertex block proves the exact solution-set identity \(\mathcal F_{K+2}(\Sigma^+)=\mathcal F_K(\Sigma)\times\{1,2\}\) while preserving \(q_\pi\).  A classification for unrestricted component size still depends on the unresolved feasibility frontier and on a parameter-preserving way to isolate exactly one canonical root tuple.
