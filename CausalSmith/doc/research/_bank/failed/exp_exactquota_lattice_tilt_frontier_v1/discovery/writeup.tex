\section*{exp\_\allowbreak{}exactquota\_\allowbreak{}lattice\_\allowbreak{}tilt\_\allowbreak{}frontier}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} Within the strict finite-population class of deterministic binary outcomes, equal cluster size, common interior exact quotas, exact first-stage arm totals, and uniform within-cluster quota subsets, the response-table inversion covers the complete causal welfare weak order with probability at least \(1-\alpha\). Uniform coverage of any deterministic table-measurable order rule is exactly equivalent to a finite family of population-indexed inequalities. Grouping compatible populations by their realized order defines a computable rational threshold \(\delta_{\mathrm{ord}}\ge\delta_\star>0\); for \(0<\alpha<\delta_{\mathrm{ord}}\), every uniformly valid rule contained in the baseline equals it at every compatible table. Whether the finite inequalities and lattice-consonance constraints permit a deterministic strict contained refinement for \(\alpha\ge\delta_{\mathrm{ord}}\) remains open; randomized reporting is not covered.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Mogstad, Romano, Shaikh, and Wilhelm construct simultaneous rank sets from valid simultaneous confidence regions for pairwise differences, while Rigdon and Hudgens invert exact tests for scalar interference effects. Bazylik, Mogstad, Romano, Shaikh, and Wilhelm obtain finite-sample rank sets by exploiting a multinomial conditional law. These results do not provide the exact finite family of necessary-and-sufficient coverage inequalities for deterministic weak-order reports under fixed saturation-arm totals and within-cluster exact quotas, nor the induced order-specific atom frontier.

\paragraph{Niche.} For the strict class considered here—fixed deterministic binary potential outcomes, binary stratified interference, equal cluster size, common distinct interior quota policies, exact first-stage arm totals, and uniform exact-quota subsets—the response alphabet and every compatible arm-outcome table are finite. This permits exact response-count compression and exact-rational optimization over populations inducing the same complete weak order; no transfer to a larger outcome, cluster-size, or assignment class is claimed.

\paragraph{Fill.} The core constructs a max-discrepancy schedule-level randomization \(p\)-value, inverts it over compatible response counts, and obtains exact simultaneous weak-order coverage. It further proves that uniform validity of a deterministic table rule is equivalent to one explicit finite inequality for every response-count population. Maximizing each observed-table atom over populations with the same order defines \(\lambda_H(o)\) and the computable threshold \(\delta_{\mathrm{ord}}\), below which every uniformly valid contained rule must equal the baseline. The remaining closure problem is restricted to \(\alpha\ge\delta_{\mathrm{ord}}\).

\section{Related work}

Mogstad, Romano, Shaikh, and Wilhelm motivate simultaneous rank uncertainty and show how valid simultaneous regions for pairwise differences transfer to rank coverage; their generic published construction is asymptotic and does not yield the finite deterministic-rule inequalities used here. Rigdon and Hudgens invert exact randomization tests over compatible binary potential-outcome schedules for scalar direct and spillover effects, but not the joint weak order of quota-policy welfare. Bazylik, Mogstad, Romano, Shaikh, and Wilhelm obtain finite-sample rank confidence sets for multinomial counts through a pairwise conditional binomial identity unavailable for heterogeneous exact-quota response kernels. Athey, Eckles, and Imbens use artificial conditional experiments for certain interference nulls, whereas weak average welfare ordering does not impute focal outcomes. Liu, Shaikh, and Zhong study finite-sample saturation tests for partially sharp restrictions and asymptotic tests for weak averages. Jiang, Imai, and Malani derive joint asymptotic covariance and power calculations for two-stage designs; Aronow, Green, and Lee sharply bound unidentified randomization variance; and Han, Owusu, and Shin study empirical saturation-ranking regret and a surrogate design criterion. None of these comparisons is claimed to imply the exact population-indexed iff characterization or the order-specific atom threshold proved here, and the present result is confined to the strict binary, equal-size, common-quota design class.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:fixed-potential-outcomes]\label{ass:fixed-potential-outcomes}
The schedule \(Y\) is fixed, and probability is taken only over \(A\sim\pi_{\exp}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (finite-population randomization framework, \cite{RigdonHudgens2015}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:stratified-interference]\label{ass:stratified-interference}
For every \(c\in\mathcal C\), \(i\in\mathcal I\), and \(r\in\mathcal R\), the potential outcome under a within-cluster assignment depends on that assignment only through own treatment \(z\) and the exact quota label \(r\), and equals \(Y_{ci}(z,r)\in\{0,1\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (binary stratified interference, \cite{RigdonHudgens2015}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:exact-first-stage]\label{ass:exact-first-stage}
The first stage assigns exactly \(m_r\) clusters to each \(r\in\mathcal R\), uniformly over all labeled partitions with these totals.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (two-stage complete randomization with fixed saturation-arm totals, \cite{JiangImaiMalani2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:exact-second-stage]\label{ass:exact-second-stage}
Conditional on the first-stage labels, the treated set in cluster \(c\) is uniform over the \(k_{R_c}\)-subsets of \(\mathcal I\), independently across clusters.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (within-cluster complete randomization at assigned saturation, \cite{JiangImaiMalani2023}).
\end{assumption}

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:exact-table-law]\label{lem:exact-table-law}
For every fixed candidate population with response counts \(N\), the response-type/arm/outcome table generated by \(A\sim\pi_{\exp}\) has support \(\mathcal H(N)\) and mass \(\mathsf P_N(h)\). In particular, \(\sum_{h\in\mathcal H(N)}\mathsf P_N(h)=1\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a deterministic population whose type counts are \(N\), and, for a proposed table \(h\), put
\[
h_{tr\boldsymbol\cdot}=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X_n}h_{trx}.
\]
First consider one cluster of type \(t\) assigned to arm \(r\).  If \(j_{ab}\) of the \(N_r^{ab}\) units of response pair \((a,b)\) are treated, exact second-stage randomization gives
\[
 \Pr\{(j_{ab})_{a,b}\mid t,r\}
 =\binom{n}{k_r}^{-1}\prod_{a,b\in\{0,1\}}\binom{N_r^{ab}}{j_{ab}}
 \quad\text{when}\quad \sum_{a,b}j_{ab}=k_r.
\]
The number of observed successes in that cluster is
\[
 \sum_{a,b}\{(N_r^{ab}-j_{ab})a+j_{ab}b\}
 =N_r^{10}+N_r^{11}-j_{10}+j_{01}.
\]
Consequently the definition of the within-cluster kernel gives
\(Pr\{X_c^{\mathrm{obs}}=x\mid t_c=t,R_c=r\}=Q_{tr}(x)\).  In particular, \(Q_{tr}\) is a probability mass function on \(\mathcal X_n\), and its positive support is exactly the set of means attainable by a \(k_r\)-subset.

There are \(C!/\prod_r m_r!\) first-stage labeled partitions.  For fixed type-by-arm margins \((h_{tr\boldsymbol\cdot})_{t,r}\), the number of such partitions is \(\prod_t N_t!/\prod_{t,r}h_{tr\boldsymbol\cdot}!\).  Thus exact first-stage randomization gives
\[
 \Pr\{(h_{tr\boldsymbol\cdot})_{t,r}\}
 =\frac{\prod_tN_t!\prod_rm_r!}
 {C!\prod_{t,r}h_{tr\boldsymbol\cdot}!}.
\]
Conditional on that first-stage table, exact second-stage randomization is independent across clusters.  Within each type--arm cell its outcome counts are multinomial, whence
\[
 \Pr\{(h_{trx})_x\mid h_{tr\boldsymbol\cdot}\}
 =\frac{h_{tr\boldsymbol\cdot}!}{\prod_xh_{trx}!}
   \prod_xQ_{tr}(x)^{h_{trx}}.
\]
Multiplying the first-stage probability by these conditional probabilities over \((t,r)\) cancels every \(h_{tr\boldsymbol\cdot}!\) and yields
\[
 \Pr_N\{h\}
 =\frac{\prod_tN_t!\prod_rm_r!}{C!\prod_{t,r,x}h_{trx}!}
   \prod_{t,r,x}Q_{tr}(x)^{h_{trx}}
 =\mathsf P_N(h).
\]
The first-stage margins force \(\sum_{r,x}h_{trx}=N_t\) and \(\sum_{t,x}h_{trx}=m_r\); an outcome cell with \(Q_{tr}(x)=0\) cannot occur.  Hence the support is contained in \(\mathcal H(N)\).  Conversely, every \(h\in\mathcal H(N)\) has a feasible first-stage margin and all factors raised to positive powers have positive \(Q_{tr}(x)\), so the displayed construction assigns it positive probability.  Thus the support equals \(\mathcal H(N)\).  Summing the probabilities of this finite exhaustive partition gives \(\sum_{h\in\mathcal H(N)}\mathsf P_N(h)=1\), including degenerate kernels.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the exact finite-population law that replaces an unidentified Gaussian covariance approximation. \textit{Gap.} Rigdon and Hudgens enumerate labeled assignments, while the available two-stage asymptotic theory records moments; neither states this multi-policy response-table quotient for order inversion. \textit{Consumer.} The table mass is the calibration engine for a reproducible exact-order module in the RCT2 saturation-design workflow.

\begin{lemma}[lem:true-counts-compatible]\label{lem:true-counts-compatible}
For every realization of \(A\), the true count vector \(N^\star\) belongs to \(\mathcal N(H^{\mathrm{obs}})\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a realization \(A=(R,Z)\in\Omega\), and define the integer array
\[
 G_{trx}=\bigl|\{c\in\mathcal C:t_c^\star=t,\ R_c=r,\ X_c^{\mathrm{obs}}=x\}\bigr|.
\]
By the definition of the observed table,
\[
 \sum_tG_{trx}=H_{rx}^{\mathrm{obs}}.
\]
Because each cluster appears in exactly one arm--outcome cell,
\[
 \sum_{r,x}G_{trx}=\bigl|\{c:t_c^\star=t\}\bigr|=N_t^\star.
\]
It remains to verify the support restriction in the compatible-count fiber.  For a cluster counted by \(G_{trx}\), let
\[
 j_{ab}=\bigl|\{i\in\mathcal I:Z_{ci}=1,
 (Y_{ci}(0,r),Y_{ci}(1,r))=(a,b)\}\bigr|.
\]
Membership of \(A\) in \(\Omega\) gives \(0\le j_{ab}\le N_{c,r}^{ab}\) and \(\sum_{a,b}j_{ab}=k_r\).  Stratified interference and the definition of \(X_c^{\mathrm{obs}}\) give
\[
 x=\frac{N_{c,r}^{10}+N_{c,r}^{11}-j_{10}+j_{01}}{n}.
\]
The summand indexed by this \((j_{ab})\) in the formula for \(Q_{t_c^\star r}(x)\) is strictly positive.  Hence \(G_{trx}>0\) implies \(Q_{tr}(x)>0\).  The three displayed properties say exactly that \(G\) witnesses \(N^\star\in\mathcal N(H^{\mathrm{obs}})\).  This conclusion is pointwise in the assignment realization and therefore also holds almost surely under \(\pi_{\exp}\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Coverage requires the realized data never to delete the true deterministic schedule fiber. \textit{Gap.} The compatibility step is absent from generic rank inference and is more structured than scalar missing-outcome completion because first-stage totals and quota kernels must be respected jointly. \textit{Consumer.} This lemma permits analysts to coarsen to cluster means without sacrificing validity for the declared welfare target.

\begin{lemma}[lem:schedule-pvalue-valid]\label{lem:schedule-pvalue-valid}
For every fixed candidate count vector \(N\) and every \(q\in[0,1]\), \[\Pr_{A\sim\pi_{\exp},N}\{p_N(H^{\mathrm{obs}})\le q\}\le q.\] This inequality remains valid when the randomization distribution of \(S_N\) is discrete or degenerate.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(N\).  By the exact table law, the full response-type/arm/outcome table \(h\) has mass \(\mathsf P_N\), and its collapse \(H=(\sum_th_{trx})_{r,x}\) has the same law as \(H^{\mathrm{obs}}\) under any fixed labeled schedule with counts \(N\).  The same table also witnesses \(N\in\mathcal N(H)\), so \(p_N(H)\) is defined almost surely.

Let \(W=S_N(H^{\mathrm{obs}})\).  Write its distinct support values as \(w_1<\cdots<w_J\), put \(a_j=\Pr_N\{W=w_j\}>0\), and set
\[
 p_j=\Pr_N\{W\ge w_j\}=\sum_{\ell=j}^J a_\ell.
\]
The definition of the schedule-level \(p\)-value and the exact table law imply \(p_N(H^{\mathrm{obs}})=p_j\) whenever \(W=w_j\).  Since \(p_1>p_2>\cdots>p_J>0\), the set \(\{j:p_j\le q\}\) is either empty or a suffix \(\{j_0,\ldots,J\}\).  In the nonempty case,
\[
 \Pr_N\{p_N(H^{\mathrm{obs}})\le q\}
 =\sum_{j=j_0}^J a_j=p_{j_0}\le q;
\]
in the empty case the probability is zero.  This proves super-uniformity for every \(q\in[0,1]\).  When \(W\) is degenerate, \(J=1\) and \(p_N(H^{\mathrm{obs}})=1\), so the same argument covers the zero-variance case without a continuity or tie-breaking assumption.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Right-tail super-uniformity is the finite-sample step that handles lattice ties and zero-variance faces without regularity conditions. \textit{Gap.} Current saturation randomization tests obtain exactness only after sharpening individual-level nulls; this lemma instead conditions on a complete candidate schedule count and later takes a composite supremum. \textit{Consumer.} The result supplies exact calibration when the number of clusters is too small for the covariance-based procedures in existing saturation calculators.

\begin{theorem}[thm:exact-weak-order-coverage]\label{thm:exact-weak-order-coverage}
Uniformly over every deterministic binary schedule \(Y\) satisfying the stated design and stratified-interference conditions, \[\Pr_{A\sim\pi_{\exp}}\left\{\mathcal O(N^\star)\in\widehat{\mathfrak O}_{\alpha}(H^{\mathrm{obs}})\right\}\ge 1-\alpha.\] The covered object is the complete weak order, so the assertion simultaneously covers all policy ranks, every partial tie pattern, and every zero-variance face. No pairwise allocation of \(\alpha\) and no cross-arm covariance estimator enters the construction. As an immediate reporting corollary, for each \(r\in\mathcal R\), take the union of the integer positions occupied by \(r\) over the retained weak orders, letting a tied block contribute every position that it spans. With the same probability, these rank sets simultaneously contain every policy's true tied-rank interval.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a deterministic binary schedule \(Y\) satisfying Assumptions \texttt{ass:fixed-potential-outcomes}, \texttt{ass:stratified-interference}, \texttt{ass:exact-first-stage}, and \texttt{ass:exact-second-stage}.  We first verify that the ordered object being covered is causal rather than merely a feature of the observed table.  For each cluster \(c\in\mathcal C\) and quota policy \(r\in\mathcal R\), define the design-average potential cluster outcome
\[
 W_{cr}:=
 \mathbb E_{A\sim\pi_{\exp}}\!\left[
   \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}Y_{ci}(Z_{ci},r)
   \mathrel{\Big|} R_c=r
 \right].
\]
The conditional expectation is well defined: \(m_r\geq 1\) by the declared space of \(m\), and exact uniform first-stage assignment gives
\[
 \Pr_{A\sim\pi_{\exp}}(R_c=r)=\frac{m_r}{C}>0.
\]
Under exact second-stage assignment, conditional on \(R_c=r\), every \(k_r\)-subset of \(\mathcal I\) has probability \(\binom n{k_r}^{-1}\).  Hence, for every \(i\in\mathcal I\),
\[
 \Pr(Z_{ci}=1\mid R_c=r)
 =\frac{\binom{n-1}{k_r-1}}{\binom n{k_r}}
 =\frac{k_r}{n},
 \qquad
 \Pr(Z_{ci}=0\mid R_c=r)=1-\frac{k_r}{n}.
\]
Fixed potential outcomes and stratified interference allow the realized summand under policy \(r\) to be written as \(Y_{ci}(Z_{ci},r)\).  Linearity of conditional expectation, which does not require the indicators \((Z_{ci}:i\in\mathcal I)\) to be independent within a cluster, therefore yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
 W_{cr}
 &=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}
   \left\{
    \left(1-\frac{k_r}{n}\right)Y_{ci}(0,r)
    +\frac{k_r}{n}Y_{ci}(1,r)
   \right\}\\
 &=\frac{(n-k_r)(N_{c,r}^{10}+N_{c,r}^{11})
          +k_r(N_{c,r}^{01}+N_{c,r}^{11})}{n^2}\\
 &=u_{t_c^\star r}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The second equality follows from Definition \texttt{def:response-type}: exactly \(N_{c,r}^{10}+N_{c,r}^{11}\) units have \(Y_{ci}(0,r)=1\), and exactly \(N_{c,r}^{01}+N_{c,r}^{11}\) units have \(Y_{ci}(1,r)=1\).  The last equality is the declared formula for \(u\).  Because \(1\leq k_r\leq n-1\), both own-treatment states have positive second-stage probability; no extrapolation outside the randomization support enters this causal policy welfare.

Average over the fixed clusters and group clusters with the same response type.  The definition of \(N^\star\) and the definition of \(U\) give, for every \(r\in\mathcal R\),
\[
 \frac{1}{C}\sum_{c\in\mathcal C}W_{cr}
 =\frac{1}{C}\sum_{t\in\mathcal T}N_t^\star u_{tr}
 =U_r(N^\star).
\]
Thus \(U_r(N^\star)\) is the fixed finite-population causal welfare under quota policy \(r\), and \(\mathcal O(N^\star)\) is its complete weak order.  In contrast, \(\widehat U(H^{\mathrm{obs}})\) is only the realized randomization statistic used inside \(S_{N^\star}\); the proof does not equate it with \(U(N^\star)\).

We next prove coverage.  Lemma \texttt{lem:true-counts-compatible} gives, for every realization of \(A\),
\[
 N^\star\in\mathcal N(H^{\mathrm{obs}}).
\]
Consequently, Definition \texttt{def:order-confidence-set} implies the event inclusion
\[
 \left\{p_{N^\star}(H^{\mathrm{obs}})>\alpha\right\}
 \subseteq
 \left\{\mathcal O(N^\star)
 \in\widehat{\mathfrak O}_{\alpha}(H^{\mathrm{obs}})\right\}.
\]
Taking complements and applying Lemma \texttt{lem:schedule-pvalue-valid} with the fixed candidate count vector \(N=N^\star\) and \(q=\alpha\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\Pr_{A\sim\pi_{\exp}}\!\left\{
   \mathcal O(N^\star)\notin
   \widehat{\mathfrak O}_{\alpha}(H^{\mathrm{obs}})
 \right\}\\
 &\qquad\leq
 \Pr_{A\sim\pi_{\exp}}\!\left\{
   p_{N^\star}(H^{\mathrm{obs}})\leq\alpha
 \right\}
 \leq\alpha.
\end{aligned}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
 \Pr_{A\sim\pi_{\exp}}\!\left\{
   \mathcal O(N^\star)\in
   \widehat{\mathfrak O}_{\alpha}(H^{\mathrm{obs}})
 \right\}\geq 1-\alpha.
\]
The argument holds for each admissible fixed schedule \(Y\), while the right-hand side does not depend on \(Y\); taking the infimum over all such schedules proves the asserted uniformity.  Super-uniformity in Lemma \texttt{lem:schedule-pvalue-valid} explicitly includes discrete and degenerate laws, so ties and zero-variance faces require no separate case.  The construction uses one max-discrepancy randomization tail probability; it neither divides \(\alpha\) among pairs nor estimates a cross-arm covariance matrix.  This is an exact finite-sample argument: no rank, variance-positivity, boundary-regularity, or limiting condition is invoked.

Finally, consider the reporting corollary.  Write any retained complete weak order \(\mathcal O\) as descending nonempty tied blocks \((B_1,\ldots,B_J)\).  If \(r\in B_j\), define the positions occupied by \(r\) under that order as
\[
 \rho_{\mathcal O}(r)
 :=\left\{
  1+\sum_{\ell<j}|B_\ell|,\ldots,
  \sum_{\ell\leq j}|B_\ell|
 \right\},
\]
and define the reported rank set
\[
 \widehat\rho_r
 :=\bigcup_{\mathcal O\in
   \widehat{\mathfrak O}_{\alpha}(H^{\mathrm{obs}})}
   \rho_{\mathcal O}(r).
\]
On the single event that \(\mathcal O(N^\star)\) is retained, for every \(r\in\mathcal R\) simultaneously,
\[
 \rho_{\mathcal O(N^\star)}(r)\subseteq\widehat\rho_r,
\]
because the term indexed by \(\mathcal O(N^\star)\) is one member of the defining union.  The event has probability at least \(1-\alpha\) by the result just proved, establishing simultaneous containment of all true tied-rank intervals.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This theorem supplies the finite-sample simultaneous coverage guarantee for the causal weak order under the exact two-stage design. \textit{Gap.} Existing generic rank methods require a simultaneous difference region, while nearby exact interference procedures target scalar or more sharply restricted nulls rather than the complete weak order of average quota-policy welfare. \textit{Consumer.} RCT2 and the Baird--Bohren--McIntosh--Özler design calculator can report complete and tied policy orderings that remain compatible at level \(1-\alpha\).

\begin{theorem}[thm:response-table-compression]\label{thm:response-table-compression}
The confidence set \(\widehat{\mathfrak O}_{\alpha}(H^{\mathrm{obs}})\) is exactly equal to the result of labeled sharp-schedule inversion based on the coarsened cluster means and discrepancy \(S_N\). Cross-policy alignment of individual response labels does not change compatibility, welfare, the discrepancy, or any table probability. For a finite-instance reference algorithm, set \(d=K(n+1)|\mathcal T|\), \(B_N=\binom{C+|\mathcal T|-1}{|\mathcal T|-1}\), and \(B_H=\binom{C+d-1}{d-1}\). Precomputing the kernels and streaming over all candidate counts and all nonnegative \(d\)-cell response tables gives the explicit upper bound \(O(K|\mathcal T|n^4+(d+K^2)B_NB_H)\) exact-rational arithmetic operations, before bit-complexity costs; legality and compatibility constraints only reduce this enumeration. Here \(|\mathcal T|=\binom{n+3}{3}^{K}\), so the bound is intentionally exponential. By contrast, direct labeled-schedule enumeration ranges over as many as \(2^{2KnC}\) binary potential-outcome schedules and, for each schedule, at most \(K^C2^{nC}\) assignment points. The response-table bound is a finite quotient reference, not a polynomial-time guarantee.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Consider first any labeled deterministic binary schedule and coarsen each labeled cluster to its tuple of policy-specific \(2\)-by-\(2\) response counts.  This produces a type vector and hence counts \(N\).  For any realized assignment, define
\[
 h_{trx}=\bigl|\{c:t_c=t,\ R_c=r,\ X_c^{\mathrm{obs}}=x\}\bigr|.
\]
Exactly as in the compatibility proof, \(h\in\mathcal H(N)\), its collapse is \(H^{\mathrm{obs}}\), and \(N\in\mathcal N(H^{\mathrm{obs}})\).  The exact table law shows that the labeled schedule induces mass \(\mathsf P_N(h)\).  Moreover, the quota welfare \(u_{tr}\), the population welfare \(U(N)\), and the discrepancy \(S_N\) depend on the labeled schedule only through these policy-specific response counts.  Thus every weak order and every acceptance decision produced by labeled sharp-schedule inversion also occurs in the response-count inversion.

Conversely, fix \(N\in\mathcal N(H)\) and let \(G\) witness compatibility.  Label the \(C\) clusters and assign exactly \(N_t\) labels to every type \(t\); within each observed cell \((r,x)\), assign exactly \(G_{trx}\) of its cluster labels to type \(t\).  For every type and every policy, partition the \(n\) unit labels into four sets of sizes \((N_r^{ab})_{a,b}\) and put the corresponding binary pair \((a,b)\) on those unit labels.  These partitions may be selected independently across policies because stratified interference imposes no cross-policy restriction on a unit's potential outcomes.

If \(G_{trx}>0\), compatibility gives \(Q_{tr}(x)>0\).  By the explicit hypergeometric formula for \(Q_{tr}(x)\), there is therefore an integer vector \((j_{ab})\) with \(0\le j_{ab}\le N_r^{ab}\), \(\sum_{a,b}j_{ab}=k_r\), and
\[
 x=\frac{N_r^{10}+N_r^{11}-j_{10}+j_{01}}{n}.
\]
For a cluster assigned to cell \((t,r,x)\), choose its treated set to contain \(j_{ab}\) labels from response cell \((a,b)\).  The resulting assignment belongs to \(\Omega\) because the row totals of \(H\) are \(m_r\), and it realizes the table \(H\).  Hence every compatible count population has a labeled sharp-schedule representative.  Its causal welfare, discrepancy, and exact randomization mass are respectively \(U(N)\), \(S_N\), and \(\mathsf P_N\).  This proves the reverse inclusion and therefore exact equality of the two inversions.  It also proves that changing cross-policy alignment while retaining all policy-specific response counts cannot change compatibility, welfare, discrepancy, or table probability.

It remains to verify the stated finite enumeration bound.  For one policy, a response table is a weak composition of \(n\) into four cells, so there are \(\binom{n+3}{3}\) possibilities.  The policy-specific tables can be chosen independently, giving
\[
 |\mathcal T|=\binom{n+3}{3}^{K}.
\]
Enumerating the at most order \(n^3\) feasible four-cell treatment-count vectors for every \((t,r)\), and accumulating their possible outcome values, is bounded by \(O(K|\mathcal T|n^4)\) exact-rational operations.  There are exactly
\[
 B_N=\binom{C+|\mathcal T|-1}{|\mathcal T|-1}
\]
nonnegative candidate count vectors of total \(C\).  A full response table has
\(d=K(n+1)|\mathcal T|\) cells, and the number of nonnegative \(d\)-cell arrays of total \(C\) is
\[
 B_H=\binom{C+d-1}{d-1}.
\]
Precompute the factorials through \(C!\) and the powers \(Q_{tr}(x)^j\) for \(0\le j\le C\).  Their cost is absorbed by the enumeration bound below because \(B_NB_H\ge C\).  For each pair consisting of a candidate count vector and such an array, all legal-margin and support checks, the factorial mass, and its collapsed arm--outcome table then take \(O(d)\) arithmetic operations; welfare differences and the max discrepancy take an additional \(O(K^2)\).  Streaming these arrays, retaining only legal ones and summing masses whose discrepancy exceeds the observed threshold, therefore costs at most
\[
 O\!\left(K|\mathcal T|n^4+(d+K^2)B_NB_H\right)
\]
exact-rational arithmetic operations, apart from bit complexity.  Streaming also detects compatibility by whether a legal array collapses to the observed \(H\); all constraints can only reduce the enumeration.

Finally, a labeled binary schedule specifies \(2KnC\) binary potential outcomes and hence has at most \(2^{2KnC}\) possibilities.  Ignoring the exact arm-total restriction gives at most \(K^C\) first-stage labelings, and bounding every collection of quota subsets by arbitrary binary treatment vectors gives at most \(2^{nC}\) second-stage assignments per labeling.  These are the asserted direct-enumeration upper bounds and confirm that the quotient algorithm is finite but not claimed to be polynomial time.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Without this quotient the exact confidence set is only a conceptual union over exponentially many labeled missing potential outcomes. \textit{Gap.} Rigdon and Hudgens' scalar algorithm samples labeled sharp schedules, and Bazylik et al.'s exact rank computation uses a multinomial binomial-conditioning identity unavailable under two-stage exact quotas. \textit{Consumer.} The theorem gives software authors a finite, auditable reference algorithm and a ground truth against which pruning or branch-and-bound implementations can be checked.

\begin{openendedquestion}[oeq:consonant-order-closure]\label{oeq:consonant-order-closure}
Fix \(\alpha\in[\delta_{\mathrm{ord}},1)\) when this range is nonempty. Do the exact finite coverage inequalities from \(\texttt{lem:deterministic-coverage-iff}\), together with lattice-consonance constraints, admit a deterministic exact procedure \(\widehat{\mathfrak O}^{\mathrm{cl}}_{\alpha}\) that has the same uniform simultaneous coverage as \(\widehat{\mathfrak O}_{\alpha}\), is contained in it for every observed table, is strictly contained for at least one nondegenerate compatible table, and remains computable solely through finitely many compatible response-table maximizations? Randomized reporting rules are outside this question.
\end{openendedquestion}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Below \(\delta_{\mathrm{ord}}\) the frontier theorem rules out a deterministic strict contained refinement; at or above that threshold, a logically coherent exact refinement is the central open power problem. \textit{Gap.} Mogstad et al. use stepwise directional testing, but no checked result supplies consonant closure for composite exact-quota schedule fibers with fixed arm totals. \textit{Consumer.} A successful refinement would shorten simultaneous policy-rank reports without weakening the exact design guarantee used by small-cluster saturation trials.

\begin{theorem}[thm:small-alpha-atom-obstruction]\label{thm:small-alpha-atom-obstruction}
Use \(L_N(H)\) and \(\delta_\star\) from \(\texttt{lem:global-compatible-atom-bound}\).  Fix \(0<\alpha<\delta_\star\).  Let \(\mathfrak C_\alpha\) be any deterministic map from arm-outcome tables to sets of complete weak orders such that, for every fixed binary schedule satisfying the design and stratified-interference assumptions,
\[
 \Pr_{A\sim\pi_{\exp}}\{\mathcal O(N^\star)\in
       \mathfrak C_\alpha(H^{\mathrm{obs}})\}\ge1-\alpha.
\]
If \(\mathfrak C_\alpha(H)\subseteq\widehat{\mathfrak O}_\alpha(H)\) for every compatible \(H\), then in fact
\[
 \mathfrak C_\alpha(H)=\widehat{\mathfrak O}_\alpha(H)
 \qquad\text{for every compatible }H.
\]
More sharply, if \((H,N)\in\mathfrak J\) and \(\mathcal O(N)\notin\mathfrak C_\alpha(H)\), there is a deterministic schedule realizing \(N\) for which the noncoverage probability is at least
\[
 L_N(H)\ge\delta_\star>\alpha.
\]
Thus no family indexed by all \(\alpha\in(0,1)\) can both preserve uniform coverage at every level and be a strict contained refinement at some compatible table at every level.  This obstruction does not assert impossibility of a consonant strict refinement for \(\alpha\ge\delta_\star\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By Lemma \(\texttt{lem:global-compatible-atom-bound}\) and Theorem \(\texttt{thm:deterministic-order-atom-frontier}\), the finite atom thresholds satisfy
\[
0<\delta_\star\le \delta_{\mathrm{ord}}\le 1.
\]
Hence the assumed inequality \(0<\alpha<\delta_\star\) implies
\[
\alpha<\delta_{\mathrm{ord}}.
\]
Fix a compatible arm--outcome table \(H\), meaning \(\mathcal N(H)\ne\varnothing\), and put
\[
\mathfrak O_H=\{\mathcal O(N):N\in\mathcal N(H)\}.
\]
The rule \(\mathfrak C_\alpha\) is deterministic and table-measurable and, by hypothesis, is uniformly design-valid over all deterministic binary schedules in the stated class. Therefore Theorem \(\texttt{thm:deterministic-order-atom-frontier}\), applied using \(\alpha<\delta_{\mathrm{ord}}\), yields both
\[
\mathfrak O_H\subseteq\mathfrak C_\alpha(H)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\widehat{\mathfrak O}_\alpha(H)=\mathfrak O_H.
\]
The assumed containment \(\mathfrak C_\alpha(H)\subseteq\widehat{\mathfrak O}_\alpha(H)\) consequently gives
\[
\widehat{\mathfrak O}_\alpha(H)
=\mathfrak O_H
\subseteq\mathfrak C_\alpha(H)
\subseteq\widehat{\mathfrak O}_\alpha(H),
\]
and thus
\[
\mathfrak C_\alpha(H)=\widehat{\mathfrak O}_\alpha(H).
\]
Because \(H\) was arbitrary, this equality holds at every compatible table. The baseline rule used here is itself uniformly valid by Theorem \(\texttt{thm:exact-weak-order-coverage}\).

For the sharper witness, fix \((H,N)\in\mathfrak J\) and suppose
\[
\mathcal O(N)\notin\mathfrak C_\alpha(H).
\]
The realizability part of Lemma \(\texttt{lem:deterministic-coverage-iff}\), together with the response-type construction \(\texttt{def:response-type}\) under the fixed-schedule and stratified-interference assumptions, supplies a deterministic binary schedule in the stated class whose response-count vector satisfies \(N^\star=N\). Under the exact first- and second-stage randomization assumptions, the exact identity in that lemma gives its design noncoverage probability as
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\Pr_{A\sim\pi_{\exp}}\!\left\{
\mathcal O(N^\star)\notin
\mathfrak C_\alpha(H^{\mathrm{obs}})\right\}\\
&\qquad=
\sum_{\substack{H':(H',N)\in\mathfrak J}}
L_N(H')\,
\mathbf 1\!\left\{
\mathcal O(N)\notin\mathfrak C_\alpha(H')
\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Every summand is nonnegative. The summand indexed by the fixed \(H\) is present because \((H,N)\in\mathfrak J\), and its indicator is one by the supposed omission. It follows that
\[
\Pr_{A\sim\pi_{\exp}}\!\left\{
\mathcal O(N^\star)\notin
\mathfrak C_\alpha(H^{\mathrm{obs}})\right\}
\ge L_N(H).
\]
Lemma \(\texttt{lem:global-compatible-atom-bound}\) gives \(L_N(H)\ge\delta_\star\), while the present level satisfies \(\delta_\star>\alpha\). Therefore
\[
\Pr_{A\sim\pi_{\exp}}\!\left\{
\mathcal O(N^\star)\notin
\mathfrak C_\alpha(H^{\mathrm{obs}})\right\}
\ge L_N(H)\ge\delta_\star>\alpha.
\]
This is the asserted schedule-specific atom obstruction and contradicts uniform \((1-\alpha)\)-coverage whenever such an omission occurs.

Finally, suppose that a family indexed by every \(\alpha\in(0,1)\) were uniformly valid, were contained in \(\widehat{\mathfrak O}_\alpha\) at every compatible table, and were strictly contained at some compatible table at every level. Since \(\delta_\star>0\), choose \(\alpha_0\in(0,\delta_\star)\). The equality already proved at level \(\alpha_0\) rules out strict containment at every compatible table, a contradiction. The argument uses no claim about \(\alpha\ge\delta_\star\), so the stated upper-level limitation is preserved.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem is the low-threshold corollary of the exact deterministic coverage characterization and the stronger order-specific atom frontier. \textit{Gap.} Deterministic consonant strict refinement remains open for \(\alpha\ge\delta_{\mathrm{ord}}\), and randomized reporting is outside the characterization. \textit{Consumer.} Software designers can avoid closure searches below the computable order-atom threshold and focus on the unresolved boundary and upper-level regime.

\begin{lemma}[lem:global-compatible-atom-bound]\label{lem:global-compatible-atom-bound}
For \(h\in\mathcal H(N)\), define its coarsening by
\[
 \Gamma(h)=\left(\sum_{t\in\mathcal T}h_{trx}\right)_{r\in\mathcal R,\,x\in\mathcal X_n}.
\]
Let
\[
 \mathfrak J=\left\{(H,N):
 H\in\mathbb Z_+^{\mathcal R\times\mathcal X_n},\ 
 \sum_{x\in\mathcal X_n}H_{rx}=m_r\ \text{for every }r,\ 
 \mathcal N(H)\ne\varnothing,\ N\in\mathcal N(H)\right\},
\]
and, for \((H,N)\in\mathfrak J\), set
\[
 L_N(H)=\sum_{\substack{h\in\mathcal H(N)\\ \Gamma(h)=H}}\mathsf P_N(h),
 \qquad
 \delta_\star=\min_{(H,N)\in\mathfrak J}L_N(H).
\]
Then \(\mathfrak J\) is finite and nonempty, every \(L_N(H)\) is a strictly positive rational number equal to the design probability of observing \(H\) under a realizing population with count vector \(N\), and
\[
 0<\delta_\star\le1.
\]
Moreover, for every \((H,N)\in\mathfrak J\),
\[
 p_N(H)\ge L_N(H)\ge\delta_\star.
\]
Consequently, for every \(0<\alpha<\delta_\star\) and every compatible \(H\),
\[
 \widehat{\mathfrak O}_{\alpha}(H)
 =\{\mathcal O(N):N\in\mathcal N(H)\}.
\]
The number \(\delta_\star\) is computable by finite exact-rational enumeration of the displayed joint set.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(t^0\in\mathcal T\) be the all-zero response type: for every \(r\), take \(N_r^{00}=n\) and \(N_r^{ab}=0\) for \((a,b)\ne(0,0)\).  The exact kernel formula gives \(Q_{t^0r}(0)=1\).  Define \(N^0_{t^0}=C\), with all other components zero, and define \(H^0_{r0}=m_r\), with all other cells zero.  The fine table having \(h^0_{t^0r0}=m_r\) and all other cells zero witnesses \(N^0\in\mathcal N(H^0)\).  Thus \(\mathfrak J\ne\varnothing\).  Both the arm-outcome table space and the count-vector space are finite because their entries are nonnegative integers with fixed total \(C\), so \(\mathfrak J\) is finite.

Fix \((H,N)\in\mathfrak J\).  By compatibility there is a nonnegative integer array \(G\) satisfying
\[
 \sum_tG_{trx}=H_{rx},\qquad
 \sum_{r,x}G_{trx}=N_t,
 \qquad
 G_{trx}=0\ \text{if }Q_{tr}(x)=0.
\]
Because \(H\) has arm totals \(m_r\),
\[
 \sum_{t,x}G_{trx}=\sum_xH_{rx}=m_r,
\]
and hence \(G\in\mathcal H(N)\) and \(\Gamma(G)=H\).  Whenever \(G_{trx}>0\), compatibility gives \(Q_{tr}(x)>0\).  For cells with \(G_{trx}=0\), the factor \(Q_{tr}(x)^0\) is defined as one, including when \(Q_{tr}(x)=0\).  All factorials in the exact table mass are positive, so
\[
 \mathsf P_N(G)
 =\frac{\prod_tN_t!\prod_rm_r!}{C!\prod_{t,r,x}G_{trx}!}
   \prod_{t,r,x}Q_{tr}(x)^{G_{trx}}>0.
\]
Therefore \(L_N(H)\ge\mathsf P_N(G)>0\).

The count vector \(N\) is realized by an actual deterministic schedule.  Indeed, for each \(t\in\mathcal T\) and \(r\in\mathcal R\), partition \(\mathcal I\) into four sets of cardinalities \(N_r^{ab}\) and assign the pair \((Y_i(0,r),Y_i(1,r))=(a,b)\) on the corresponding set.  Such a partition exists because \(\sum_{a,b}N_r^{ab}=n\); it may be selected independently for different \(r\) because the stratified-interference schedule imposes no cross-policy equality.  Making exactly \(N_t\) clusters of each type \(t\) gives a binary fixed schedule with response-count vector \(N\).

By \(\texttt{lem:exact-table-law}\), the fine response-type/arm/outcome table has mass \(\mathsf P_N\) on \(\mathcal H(N)\).  The observed arm-outcome table is its coarsening \(\Gamma(h)\), so disjoint summation gives
\[
 \Pr_{A\sim\pi_{\exp},N}\{H^{\mathrm{obs}}=H\}
 =\sum_{\substack{h\in\mathcal H(N)\\\Gamma(h)=H}}
   \mathsf P_N(h)
 =L_N(H).
\]
The kernel probabilities are ratios of integers, so each mass and each finite sum is rational.  Since \(L_N(H)\) is also a probability, it lies in \((0,1]\).  A minimum over the nonempty finite set \(\mathfrak J\) is attained and remains strictly positive, proving \(0<\delta_\star\le1\).

Finally, if \(\Gamma(h)=H\), then the statistic in the right tail defining \(p_N(H)\) satisfies
\[
 S_N(\Gamma(h))=S_N(H).
\]
Every such fine table is therefore included in that weak right tail, whence
\[
 p_N(H)
 \ge\sum_{\substack{h\in\mathcal H(N)\\\Gamma(h)=H}}
       \mathsf P_N(h)
 =L_N(H)\ge\delta_\star.
\]
If \(0<\alpha<\delta_\star\), every \(N\in\mathcal N(H)\) passes the strict rule \(p_N(H)>\alpha\).  The reverse inclusion is built into the definition of \(\widehat{\mathfrak O}_\alpha\), so
\[
 \widehat{\mathfrak O}_{\alpha}(H)
 =\{\mathcal O(N):N\in\mathcal N(H)\}.
\]
All index sets and summands in the definition of \(\delta_\star\) are finite and rational, which also proves the asserted computability.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} A minimum over the finite joint compatibility set turns the qualitative discreteness of the experiment into a strictly positive, computable global atom threshold. \textit{Gap.} A table-specific atom does not support a uniform impossibility statement; the minimum must quantify jointly over every compatible observed table and every candidate response-count population. \textit{Consumer.} Exact-order implementations can enumerate \(L_N(H)\) and report the threshold below which no valid contained shortening is possible.

\begin{lemma}[lem:deterministic-coverage-iff]\label{lem:deterministic-coverage-iff}
Let \(\mathfrak C_\alpha\) be a deterministic map from arm-outcome tables with row totals \(m\) to sets of complete weak orders on \(\mathcal R\). The following are equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For every deterministic binary schedule \(Y\) satisfying the fixed-potential-outcome and stratified-interference conditions,
\[
 \Pr_{A\sim\pi_{\exp}}\!\left\{\mathcal O(N^\star)\in
 \mathfrak C_\alpha(H^{\mathrm{obs}})\right\}\ge 1-\alpha.
\]
\item For every candidate response-count vector \(N\),
\[
 \sum_{\substack{H:(H,N)\in\mathfrak J}}
 L_N(H)\,
 \mathbf 1\!\left\{\mathcal O(N)\notin\mathfrak C_\alpha(H)\right\}
 \le \alpha.
\]
\end{enumerate}
Indeed, for every schedule whose response-count vector is \(N\), the displayed sum is exactly its design noncoverage probability. Thus the equivalence is an exact finite characterization, not merely a necessary atomwise bound.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a candidate count vector \(N\). We first verify that it is realized by a schedule in the stated class. For each response type \(t\in\mathcal T\) and each policy \(r\in\mathcal R\), the defining identity
\[
 \sum_{a,b\in\{0,1\}}N_r^{ab}=n
\]
permits a partition of the labeled unit set \(\mathcal I\) into sets \(I_{t,r}^{ab}\) with \(\lvert I_{t,r}^{ab}\rvert=N_r^{ab}\). On a cluster of type \(t\), set
\[
 \bigl(Y_i(0,r),Y_i(1,r)\bigr)=(a,b)
 \quad\text{for }i\in I_{t,r}^{ab}.
\]
The partitions may be selected separately for distinct \(r\), because the stratified-interference assumption indexes the fixed binary potential outcomes by \((z,r)\) and imposes no cross-policy equality. Assign this schedule to exactly \(N_t\) labeled clusters for every \(t\). Since \(\sum_tN_t=C\), this constructs a deterministic binary schedule with \(N^\star=N\).

Now take any deterministic binary schedule having response-count vector \(N\). Under the exact first-stage allocation and the conditionally independent exact second-stage allocations, Lemma \(\texttt{lem:exact-table-law}\) gives the fine response-type/arm/outcome table mass \(\mathsf P_N(h)\) on \(\mathcal H(N)\). Its coarsening is the observed table, so, by disjoint finite summation and Lemma \(\texttt{lem:global-compatible-atom-bound}\),
\[
 \Pr_{A\sim\pi_{\exp}}\{H^{\mathrm{obs}}=H\}
 =\sum_{\substack{h\in\mathcal H(N)\\
                    \Gamma(h)=H}}\mathsf P_N(h)
 =L_N(H)
\]
whenever \((H,N)\in\mathfrak J\). Conversely, every observed table with positive probability is the coarsening of its realized fine table, and that fine table witnesses \(N\in\mathcal N(H)\); hence the support is exactly the set of \(H\) appearing in this sum.

The rule is deterministic and table-measurable, while the true order is the fixed order \(\mathcal O(N)\). Therefore the law of total probability over the finite observed-table support gives the identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\Pr_{A\sim\pi_{\exp}}\!\left\{
 \mathcal O(N^\star)\notin
 \mathfrak C_\alpha(H^{\mathrm{obs}})\right\}\\
 &\qquad=
 \sum_{\substack{H:(H,N)\in\mathfrak J}}
 L_N(H)\,
 \mathbf 1\!\left\{\mathcal O(N)\notin
 \mathfrak C_\alpha(H)\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
If uniform coverage holds, apply this equality to the realizing schedule constructed above for each \(N\); its noncoverage probability is at most \(\alpha\), proving all the finite inequalities. Conversely, suppose all the finite inequalities hold. Every schedule in the stated class induces some count vector \(N^\star=N\), and the same identity bounds its noncoverage probability by \(\alpha\). This proves uniform coverage and the equivalence.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The exact finite family of population-indexed inequalities is necessary and sufficient for uniform coverage of any deterministic table-measurable order rule. \textit{Gap.} The indicator identity uses deterministic reporting; randomized confidence rules would require an additional reporting kernel and different inequalities. \textit{Consumer.} The order-atom frontier uses the individual nonnegative summands of this exact characterization.

\begin{theorem}[thm:deterministic-order-atom-frontier]\label{thm:deterministic-order-atom-frontier}
For every compatible arm-outcome table \(H\), define its realized-order set and its order-specific largest atom by
\[
 \mathfrak O_H=\{\mathcal O(N):N\in\mathcal N(H)\},
 \qquad
 \lambda_H(o)=
 \max_{\substack{N\in\mathcal N(H)\\ \mathcal O(N)=o}}L_N(H),
 \quad o\in\mathfrak O_H,
\]
and define
\[
 \delta_{\mathrm{ord}}
 =\min_{\substack{H:\mathcal N(H)\ne\varnothing\\
                   o\in\mathfrak O_H}}
 \lambda_H(o).
\]
These quantities are computable by finite exact-rational enumeration and satisfy
\[
 0<\delta_\star\le\delta_{\mathrm{ord}}\le1.
\]
If a deterministic table-measurable rule \(\mathfrak C_\alpha\), taking values in sets of complete weak orders on \(\mathcal R\), satisfies
\[
 \Pr_{A\sim\pi_{\exp}}\!\left\{\mathcal O(N^\star)\in
 \mathfrak C_\alpha(H^{\mathrm{obs}})\right\}\ge1-\alpha
\]
for every deterministic binary schedule \(Y\) in the stated class, then, for every compatible \(H\) and every \(o\in\mathfrak O_H\),
\[
 \lambda_H(o)>\alpha
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 o\in\mathfrak C_\alpha(H).
\]
Conversely, if \(\lambda_H(o)\le\alpha\), there exists a uniformly valid deterministic table-measurable rule that omits \(o\) at \(H\). Hence the displayed implication is pointwise sharp among deterministic table rules, although the witnessing rule is not asserted to be contained in the baseline or lattice-consonant.
Consequently, when \(0<\alpha<\delta_{\mathrm{ord}}\), every such rule contains \(\mathfrak O_H\) at every compatible \(H\), and
\[
 \widehat{\mathfrak O}_\alpha(H)=\mathfrak O_H.
\]
Thus any uniformly valid deterministic rule satisfying \(\mathfrak C_\alpha(H)\subseteq\widehat{\mathfrak O}_\alpha(H)\) for every compatible \(H\) must equal the baseline set everywhere. The baseline itself has uniform coverage by \(\texttt{thm:exact-weak-order-coverage}\). No assertion is made about randomized reporting rules or about strict consonant refinement when \(\alpha\ge\delta_{\mathrm{ord}}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By Lemma \(\texttt{lem:global-compatible-atom-bound}\), the joint compatibility set \(\mathfrak J\) is finite and nonempty, and every \(L_N(H)\) on that set is a positive rational number in \((0,1]\). For a compatible \(H\), the fiber \(\mathcal N(H)\) is nonempty and finite. Hence \(\mathfrak O_H\) is nonempty and finite. For each \(o\in\mathfrak O_H\), the set over which \(\lambda_H(o)\) is maximized is nonempty and finite, so the maximum is attained and is a positive rational number. The type-welfare formula makes every \(u_{tr}\), and hence every \(U_r(N)\), rational, so \(\mathcal O(N)\) is obtained by exact rational comparisons. The set of compatible pairs \((H,o)\) is itself a nonempty finite image of \(\mathfrak J\); therefore the minimum defining \(\delta_{\mathrm{ord}}\) is attained, positive, rational, and computable by finite exact-rational enumeration.

For every \((H,N)\in\mathfrak J\), the global atom lemma gives \(L_N(H)\ge\delta_\star\). A maximum of such values over populations inducing a fixed \((H,o)\) is therefore at least \(\delta_\star\), and taking the minimum over \((H,o)\) preserves that inequality. Since every \(L_N(H)\le1\), their maxima and subsequent minimum are at most one. Thus
\[
 0<\delta_\star\le\delta_{\mathrm{ord}}\le1.
\]

Let \(\mathfrak C_\alpha\) have the stated uniform coverage. Fix compatible \(H\) and \(o\in\mathfrak O_H\). Choose a maximizer \(N_{H,o}\) such that
\[
 N_{H,o}\in\mathcal N(H),\qquad
 \mathcal O(N_{H,o})=o,\qquad
 L_{N_{H,o}}(H)=\lambda_H(o).
\]
Lemma \(\texttt{lem:deterministic-coverage-iff}\) applied to \(N_{H,o}\) yields
\[
 \sum_{\substack{H':(H',N_{H,o})\in\mathfrak J}}
 L_{N_{H,o}}(H')
 \mathbf 1\!\left\{o\notin\mathfrak C_\alpha(H')\right\}
 \le\alpha.
\]
All summands are nonnegative. If \(o\notin\mathfrak C_\alpha(H)\), the \(H\)-summand equals \(L_{N_{H,o}}(H)=\lambda_H(o)\). Hence \(\lambda_H(o)\le\alpha\). The contrapositive proves
\[
 \lambda_H(o)>\alpha\Longrightarrow
 o\in\mathfrak C_\alpha(H).
\]

For pointwise sharpness, suppose \(\lambda_H(o)\le\alpha\), and let \(\mathfrak W\) be the finite set of all complete weak orders on \(\mathcal R\). Define the deterministic rule
\[
 \mathfrak C^{H,o}_\alpha(H')=
 \begin{cases}
  \mathfrak W\setminus\{o\},&H'=H,\\
  \mathfrak W,&H'\ne H.
 \end{cases}
\]
For any candidate \(N\), the finite noncoverage sum from Lemma \(\texttt{lem:deterministic-coverage-iff}\) is zero unless \(\mathcal O(N)=o\) and \((H,N)\in\mathfrak J\). In the remaining case it is exactly
\[
 L_N(H)\le
 \max_{\substack{\widetilde N\in\mathcal N(H)\\
                  \mathcal O(\widetilde N)=o}}
 L_{\widetilde N}(H)
 =\lambda_H(o)\le\alpha.
\]
The iff characterization therefore proves uniform coverage of \(\mathfrak C^{H,o}_\alpha\), while its definition gives \(o\notin\mathfrak C^{H,o}_\alpha(H)\). This witness establishes pointwise sharpness only in the unrestricted class of deterministic table rules; it need not be contained in \(\widehat{\mathfrak O}_\alpha\) or consonant on the ordered-partition lattice.

Suppose now that \(0<\alpha<\delta_{\mathrm{ord}}\). Then \(\lambda_H(o)\ge\delta_{\mathrm{ord}}>\alpha\) for every compatible \((H,o)\), so every uniformly valid deterministic rule contains all of \(\mathfrak O_H\).

It remains to identify the baseline set at this enlarged threshold. Its definition immediately gives
\[
 \widehat{\mathfrak O}_\alpha(H)\subseteq\mathfrak O_H.
\]
For the reverse inclusion, fix \(o\in\mathfrak O_H\) and again use \(N_{H,o}\). The global compatible-atom bound and the maximizing identity give
\[
 p_{N_{H,o}}(H)
 \ge L_{N_{H,o}}(H)
 =\lambda_H(o)
 \ge\delta_{\mathrm{ord}}
 >\alpha.
\]
Thus the definition of the inverted set retains \(\mathcal O(N_{H,o})=o\), proving
\[
 \widehat{\mathfrak O}_\alpha(H)=\mathfrak O_H.
\]
If additionally \(\mathfrak C_\alpha(H)\subseteq\widehat{\mathfrak O}_\alpha(H)\), the two inclusions force equality. Theorem \(\texttt{thm:exact-weak-order-coverage}\) supplies uniform coverage of the baseline itself. The proof used the indicator representation of a deterministic rule and the strict inequality \(\alpha<\delta_{\mathrm{ord}}\); it supplies neither a randomized-rule conclusion nor a conclusion at or above the boundary.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The order-specific maximum atom yields the exact pointwise inclusion certificate needed to sharpen the global low-level obstruction. \textit{Gap.} The certificate does not decide feasibility under lattice-consonance constraints at or above \(\delta_{\mathrm{ord}}\) and does not apply to randomized reporting. \textit{Consumer.} The closure search can be restricted to \(\alpha\ge\delta_{\mathrm{ord}}\), with \(\lambda_H(o)\) serving as a computable mandatory-inclusion diagnostic.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The finite reference algorithm has arithmetic bound \(O(K|\mathcal T|n^4+(d+K^2)B_NB_H)\), where \(d=K(n+1)|\mathcal T|\), \(B_N=\binom{C+|\mathcal T|-1}{|\mathcal T|-1}\), and \(B_H=\binom{C+d-1}{d-1}\); bit complexity is additional. This is an auditable exponential reference, not a polynomial-time implementation. The exact \(C=8\), \(m_2=6\) endpoint-envelope check enumerated all \(526\) feasible welfare-count vectors and attained probability \(37/1306368\) at counts \((1,0,0,0,6,0,0,0,1)\); it checks inherited arithmetic but is not used for coverage. The atom result concerns deterministic table-measurable rules in the strict binary, equal-size, common-quota class. It forces equality with the baseline only for \(0<\alpha<\delta_{\mathrm{ord}}\); it neither constructs nor excludes a lattice-consonant strict refinement when \(\alpha\ge\delta_{\mathrm{ord}}\), and it does not analyze randomized reporting. Exact-rational enumeration of \(\delta_{\mathrm{ord}}\) can itself be expensive.

\section{Honest scope}

The settled inferential results are exact simultaneous coverage of the complete finite-population causal welfare weak order, finite response-table compression, the necessary-and-sufficient finite coverage inequalities for deterministic table rules, and the order-atom obstruction. They hold only for the stated class with deterministic binary schedules, equal cluster size, common distinct interior exact quotas, exact first-stage arm totals, and uniform quota subsets; no superclass transfer is asserted. The obstruction is sharp at the level of unrestricted deterministic table rules: a realized order \(o\) must be included at \(H\) whenever \(\lambda_H(o)>\alpha\), while for \(\lambda_H(o)\le\alpha\) an otherwise unrestricted uniformly valid deterministic rule can omit it. Every uniformly valid contained rule equals the baseline for \(0<\alpha<\delta_{\mathrm{ord}}\). The pointwise witness need not be baseline-contained or lattice-consonant, so whether the exact inequalities together with lattice-consonance constraints admit a deterministic strict contained refinement for any \(\alpha\ge\delta_{\mathrm{ord}}\) remains an acknowledged open question. Randomized reporting, shortness optimality, polynomial complexity, a released calculator, and impossibility at or above the boundary are not claimed. Partial ties remain tied blocks of a complete weak order rather than being broken arbitrarily.
