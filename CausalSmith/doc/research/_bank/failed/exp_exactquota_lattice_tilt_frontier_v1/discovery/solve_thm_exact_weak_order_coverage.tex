%%% FIELD proof-exact-weak-order-coverage
Fix a deterministic binary schedule \(Y\) satisfying Assumptions \texttt{ass:fixed-potential-outcomes}, \texttt{ass:stratified-interference}, \texttt{ass:exact-first-stage}, and \texttt{ass:exact-second-stage}.  We first verify that the ordered object being covered is causal rather than merely a feature of the observed table.  For each cluster \(c\in\mathcal C\) and quota policy \(r\in\mathcal R\), define the design-average potential cluster outcome
\[
 W_{cr}:=
 \mathbb E_{A\sim\pi_{\exp}}\!\left[
   \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}Y_{ci}(Z_{ci},r)
   \mathrel{\Big|} R_c=r
 \right].
\]
The conditional expectation is well defined: \(m_r\geq 1\) by the declared space of \(m\), and exact uniform first-stage assignment gives
\[
 \Pr_{A\sim\pi_{\exp}}(R_c=r)=\frac{m_r}{C}>0.
\]
Under exact second-stage assignment, conditional on \(R_c=r\), every \(k_r\)-subset of \(\mathcal I\) has probability \(\binom n{k_r}^{-1}\).  Hence, for every \(i\in\mathcal I\),
\[
 \Pr(Z_{ci}=1\mid R_c=r)
 =\frac{\binom{n-1}{k_r-1}}{\binom n{k_r}}
 =\frac{k_r}{n},
 \qquad
 \Pr(Z_{ci}=0\mid R_c=r)=1-\frac{k_r}{n}.
\]
Fixed potential outcomes and stratified interference allow the realized summand under policy \(r\) to be written as \(Y_{ci}(Z_{ci},r)\).  Linearity of conditional expectation, which does not require the indicators \((Z_{ci}:i\in\mathcal I)\) to be independent within a cluster, therefore yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
 W_{cr}
 &=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}
   \left\{
    \left(1-\frac{k_r}{n}\right)Y_{ci}(0,r)
    +\frac{k_r}{n}Y_{ci}(1,r)
   \right\}\\
 &=\frac{(n-k_r)(N_{c,r}^{10}+N_{c,r}^{11})
          +k_r(N_{c,r}^{01}+N_{c,r}^{11})}{n^2}\\
 &=u_{t_c^\star r}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The second equality follows from Definition \texttt{def:response-type}: exactly \(N_{c,r}^{10}+N_{c,r}^{11}\) units have \(Y_{ci}(0,r)=1\), and exactly \(N_{c,r}^{01}+N_{c,r}^{11}\) units have \(Y_{ci}(1,r)=1\).  The last equality is the declared formula for \(u\).  Because \(1\leq k_r\leq n-1\), both own-treatment states have positive second-stage probability; no extrapolation outside the randomization support enters this causal policy welfare.

Average over the fixed clusters and group clusters with the same response type.  The definition of \(N^\star\) and the definition of \(U\) give, for every \(r\in\mathcal R\),
\[
 \frac{1}{C}\sum_{c\in\mathcal C}W_{cr}
 =\frac{1}{C}\sum_{t\in\mathcal T}N_t^\star u_{tr}
 =U_r(N^\star).
\]
Thus \(U_r(N^\star)\) is the fixed finite-population causal welfare under quota policy \(r\), and \(\mathcal O(N^\star)\) is its complete weak order.  In contrast, \(\widehat U(H^{\mathrm{obs}})\) is only the realized randomization statistic used inside \(S_{N^\star}\); the proof does not equate it with \(U(N^\star)\).

We next prove coverage.  Lemma \texttt{lem:true-counts-compatible} gives, for every realization of \(A\),
\[
 N^\star\in\mathcal N(H^{\mathrm{obs}}).
\]
Consequently, Definition \texttt{def:order-confidence-set} implies the event inclusion
\[
 \left\{p_{N^\star}(H^{\mathrm{obs}})>\alpha\right\}
 \subseteq
 \left\{\mathcal O(N^\star)
 \in\widehat{\mathfrak O}_{\alpha}(H^{\mathrm{obs}})\right\}.
\]
Taking complements and applying Lemma \texttt{lem:schedule-pvalue-valid} with the fixed candidate count vector \(N=N^\star\) and \(q=\alpha\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\Pr_{A\sim\pi_{\exp}}\!\left\{
   \mathcal O(N^\star)\notin
   \widehat{\mathfrak O}_{\alpha}(H^{\mathrm{obs}})
 \right\}\\
 &\qquad\leq
 \Pr_{A\sim\pi_{\exp}}\!\left\{
   p_{N^\star}(H^{\mathrm{obs}})\leq\alpha
 \right\}
 \leq\alpha.
\end{aligned}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
 \Pr_{A\sim\pi_{\exp}}\!\left\{
   \mathcal O(N^\star)\in
   \widehat{\mathfrak O}_{\alpha}(H^{\mathrm{obs}})
 \right\}\geq 1-\alpha.
\]
The argument holds for each admissible fixed schedule \(Y\), while the right-hand side does not depend on \(Y\); taking the infimum over all such schedules proves the asserted uniformity.  Super-uniformity in Lemma \texttt{lem:schedule-pvalue-valid} explicitly includes discrete and degenerate laws, so ties and zero-variance faces require no separate case.  The construction uses one max-discrepancy randomization tail probability; it neither divides \(\alpha\) among pairs nor estimates a cross-arm covariance matrix.  This is an exact finite-sample argument: no rank, variance-positivity, boundary-regularity, or limiting condition is invoked.

Finally, consider the reporting corollary.  Write any retained complete weak order \(\mathcal O\) as descending nonempty tied blocks \((B_1,\ldots,B_J)\).  If \(r\in B_j\), define the positions occupied by \(r\) under that order as
\[
 \rho_{\mathcal O}(r)
 :=\left\{
  1+\sum_{\ell<j}|B_\ell|,\ldots,
  \sum_{\ell\leq j}|B_\ell|
 \right\},
\]
and define the reported rank set
\[
 \widehat\rho_r
 :=\bigcup_{\mathcal O\in
   \widehat{\mathfrak O}_{\alpha}(H^{\mathrm{obs}})}
   \rho_{\mathcal O}(r).
\]
On the single event that \(\mathcal O(N^\star)\) is retained, for every \(r\in\mathcal R\) simultaneously,
\[
 \rho_{\mathcal O(N^\star)}(r)\subseteq\widehat\rho_r,
\]
because the term indexed by \(\mathcal O(N^\star)\) is one member of the defining union.  The event has probability at least \(1-\alpha\) by the result just proved, establishing simultaneous containment of all true tied-rank intervals.
