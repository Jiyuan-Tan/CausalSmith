 \end{aligned} \tag{38}
\]
On \(A\), the first factor \(\phi-1\) is nonpositive and the likelihood-ratio difference is nonnegative; off \(A\), the two signs are reversed.  The last term is nonpositive by feasibility.  Moreover, for every \(u\ge c\),
\(P_u(A)=\Phi(a-u)\le\Phi(a-c)=\alpha\), so \(\mathbf1_A\) is feasible for the entire opposite set.  Since every rule feasible for the entire set is feasible at \(u=c\), inequality (38) proves optimality and
\[
 \beta^{\star}_{\mathcal G_+}(\alpha)=P_0(A)
 =\Phi(c+\Phi^{-1}(\alpha)). \tag{39}
\]
Strict monotonicity in (37) also shows that this optimizer is unique up to null sets.

Now take \(\mathcal A_{\pm}=(-\infty,-c]\cup[c,\infty)\).  Given any feasible \(\phi\), symmetrize it as
\(\psi(x)=\{\phi(x)+\phi(-x)\}/2\).  Then \(E_0\psi=E_0\phi\), and
\[
 E_c\psi=E_{-c}\psi=\frac{E_c\phi+E_{-c}\phi}{2}\le\alpha. \tag{40}
\]
Let \(Q=(P_c+P_{-c})/2\).  Its density satisfies
\[
 \frac{p_0(x)}{dQ/dx}
 =\frac{e^{c^2/2}}{\cosh(cx)}, \tag{41}
\]
which is strictly decreasing in \(|x|\).  The function
\(t\mapsto Q([-t,t])=\Phi(t-c)-\Phi(-t-c)\) is continuous and strictly increasing from zero to one.  Hence there is a unique \(t_{c,\alpha}>0\) for which this probability equals \(\alpha\).

Apply the same signed-integrand calculation as in (38), now with the density ratio in (41), the measure \(Q\), and the central interval \(A_{\pm}=[-t_{c,\alpha},t_{c,\alpha}]\).  It proves that every \(\psi\) with \(E_Q\psi\le\alpha\) satisfies
\(E_0\psi\le P_0(A_{\pm})\).  The indicator of \(A_{\pm}\) has probability \(\alpha\) under both \(P_c\) and \(P_{-c}\).  For \(u\ge0\),
\[
 \frac{d}{du}P_u(A_{\pm})
 =-\varphi(t_{c,\alpha}-u)+\varphi(t_{c,\alpha}+u)\le0, \tag{42}
\]
where \(\varphi\) is the standard-normal density; the inequality follows because
\(|t_{c,\alpha}+u|\ge|t_{c,\alpha}-u|\).  Symmetry gives the same monotonicity in \(|u|\), so the indicator is feasible for every \(|u|\ge c\).  Therefore
\[
 \beta^{\star}_{\mathcal G_{\pm}}(\alpha)
 =P_0(A_{\pm})=2\Phi(t_{c,\alpha})-1. \tag{43}
\]

The feasible set for \(\mathcal G_{\pm}\) is contained in that for \(\mathcal G_+\).  Equality of the two optima would force the unique one-sided optimizer \(\mathbf1_{(-\infty,a]}\) from (39) to satisfy the additional constraint under \(P_{-c}\).  It does not, because
\[
 P_{-c}(( -\infty,a])=\Phi(a+c)
 =\Phi(\Phi^{-1}(\alpha)+2c)>\alpha. \tag{44}
\]
Thus the inequality in the theorem is strict.  Both geometries have nearest-opposite Euclidean distance \(c\), yet their exact optimal honest certification probabilities differ.  The corrected handle records its Gaussian tangent experiment in information-whitened coordinates, so its Euclidean distance is, by construction, the limiting information distance.  Thus \(\lim\delta_n=c\) retains the nearest distance but not the number or arrangement of incident opposite-classification regions.  The scalar cannot determine a universal envelope or a common attaining rule.  Supplying \(\mathcal G\), as in the corrected handle, is necessary and gives the explicit computable optimization stated above.
