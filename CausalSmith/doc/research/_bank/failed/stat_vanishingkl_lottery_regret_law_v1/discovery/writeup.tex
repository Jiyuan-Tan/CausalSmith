\section*{stat\_\allowbreak{}vanishingkl\_\allowbreak{}lottery\_\allowbreak{}regret\_\allowbreak{}law}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} A finite-stratum randomized lottery generates a continuum of benchmark-centered Gibbs rules indexed by a KL penalty. The normalized oracle-minus-estimated regret is compactified into one continuous path whose hard endpoint uses benchmark weights on every maximizing set and whose high-penalty endpoint is a projected categorical-covariance quadratic form. On the declared fixed finite-cell randomized subclass, the main positive result is a quantitative uniform Gaussian approximation for this nonlinear path, and an observed plug-in welfare frontier supplies an operational temperature selector. A finite interval-and-Gaussian-integration algorithm emits a machine-checkable one-sided certificate with an analytic fallback, so the simultaneous envelope remains valid after every observed-data-measurable temperature choice and at quantile atoms. These positive results are not claimed over the full Fang--Ridder--Xie class. Conversely, a legal fixed binary collision witness lies in that broader class, so the failure of uniform atom-removed anti-concentration, and of every modulus-based uniform probability-to-critical-value conversion, transfers upward.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Fixed positive KL penalties have a quadratic regret law, while hard assignment boundaries and softmax inference for predetermined smoothing sequences are studied separately. No published result supplies a quantitative joint path law or a certified one-sided critical value over the full penalty menu on the declared fixed finite-cell randomized subclass. Standard Gaussian-supremum anti-concentration also does not cover the hard-cell atom collisions exhibited here; because the binary witness satisfies the broader Fang--Ridder--Xie hypotheses, enlarging to that class cannot recover a uniform atom-removed modulus.

\paragraph{Niche.} Finite multiaction lotteries create a tractable but nonstandard function-valued statistic: interior coordinates are nonlinear Gaussian transforms, the hard endpoint is directionally irregular with benchmark-weighted multiway ties, and the opposite endpoint has a different quadratic normalization. The fixed finite-cell structure supports the positive approximation and certified computation. The same structure contains a binary atom-collision witness, which is enough for an upward negative transfer even though positive achievability does not transfer upward.

\paragraph{Fill.} The project derives the primitive finite-cell Gaussian certificate, matches both endpoints, proves an explicit tie-slab coupling rate in \(C([0,1])\), defines a sample-computable plug-in frontier and its observed-data sigma-field, and turns a verified lower CDF bracket with directed rounding and a finite fallback into computably honest inference after temperature choice. It also proves the hard-cell atom formula, constructs a fixed legal counterexample to uniform atom-removed anti-concentration, and proves the asymmetric class-transfer corollary: positive results remain on the finite-cell subclass, whereas the impossibility and every modulus-based critical-value obstruction extend to the broader Fang--Ridder--Xie class.

\section{Related work}

Fang--Ridder--Xie studies benchmark-centered KL assignment and proves a quadratic regret limit for each fixed positive penalty; its Conclusion leaves data-driven penalty choice open, and its deterministic binary analysis is separate. The positive path law and certified calibration here are restricted to the declared fixed finite-cell randomized subclass and are not asserted over the full Fang--Ridder--Xie class. The relation reverses for the negative result: the fixed two-stratum binary collision design satisfies their randomization, overlap, boundedness, and positive-benchmark hypotheses, so a uniform atom-removed modulus or any modulus-based probability-to-critical-value conversion over their broader class would contradict the witness theorem. Their three-arm commitment-savings frontier motivates an illustration, not a finite-stratification adequacy claim. Whitehouse--Chen--Austern--Syrgkanis uses softmax smoothing for one unsmoothed optimal-policy value along a predetermined diverging temperature, a different scalar target. Hirano--Porter supplies the shifted-Gaussian local decision experiment for binary hard treatment choice, not a multiaction path law. Chernozhukov--Chetverikov--Kato and Giessing give anti-concentration for Gaussian-process suprema, whereas \(G(h,Z_V)\) is nonlinear and has the proved hard-cell atom collisions. Fang--Santos explains directional irregularity but does not furnish atom-safe continuum calibration. Genz, Wang--Kennedy, and Miwa--Hayter--Kuriki provide Gaussian integration components, not certification of this nonlinear continuum event. Kang supplies simultaneous-band selection logic for an observed series path; the present bridge additionally separates the observed selector frontier from the unobserved oracle causal-regret target.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:iid-rows]\label{ass:iid-rows}
For every \(n\), \(O_{n1},\ldots,O_{nn}\) are independent and identically distributed with law \(P_n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Triangular-array i.i.d. sampling, \cite{ChernozhukovChetverikovKato2014GaussianSuprema}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:consistency]\label{ass:consistency}
\(Y=Y(A)\) almost surely under every \(P_n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Consistency of potential outcomes, \cite{FangRidderXie2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:randomization]\label{ass:randomization}
\(Y^{\mathrm{pot}}\perp A\mid X\) under every \(P_n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Conditional random assignment, \cite{FangRidderXie2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:stratum-mass]\label{ass:stratum-mass}
\(\min_{j\in\mathcal J}p_j\ge p_{\min}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Finite-cell mass condition, \cite{FangRidderXie2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:overlap]\label{ass:overlap}
\(\min_{j\in\mathcal J,a\in\mathcal A}e_{ja}\ge e_{\min}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform treatment overlap, \cite{FangRidderXie2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:bounded-outcomes]\label{ass:bounded-outcomes}
\(\max_{a\in\mathcal A}|Y(a)|\le B_Y\) almost surely under every \(P_n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniformly bounded potential outcomes, \cite{FangRidderXie2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:local-remainder]\label{ass:local-remainder}
\(\|d_n^{\mathrm{loc}}\|_2\le\rho_n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Contiguous local-alternative remainder, \cite{HiranoPorter2009}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:benchmark-support]\label{ass:benchmark-support}
\(\min_{j\in\mathcal J,a\in\mathcal A}b_{ja}\ge\beta\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Finite-support benchmark positivity, \cite{FangRidderXie2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:covariance-envelope]\label{ass:covariance-envelope}
\(V_n\in\mathcal K_V\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform information nonsingularity, \cite{HiranoPorter2009}).
\end{assumption}

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:cell-gaussian]\label{lem:cell-gaussian}
Uniformly over \((P_n)\in\mathcal M(J,K,\mathcal H,\mathcal K_V)\), the feasible finite-cell estimator satisfies \(d_{\mathrm{BL}}\{\mathcal L(E_n),N(0,V_n)\}\le r_n=C_{\mathrm{BE}}n^{-1/2}\), where \(C_{\mathrm{BE}}\) is an explicit function of \((J,K,p_{\min},e_{\min},B_Y,v_{\min},v_{\max})\); the proof must include the exponentially small empty-cell event and the Hájek-ratio linearization.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(s_{ja}=p_je_{ja}\) and \(s_0=p_{\min}e_{\min}>0\).  Consistency and conditional randomization give
\[
 E_{P_n}(Y\mid X=j,A=a)=E_{P_n}\{Y(a)\mid X=j\}=\mu_{n,ja}.
\]
Thus, with \(I_{i,ja}=\mathbf 1\{X_{ni}=j,A_{ni}=a\}\), \(U_{i,ja}=I_{i,ja}(Y_{ni}-\mu_{n,ja})\), \(\widehat s_{ja}=n^{-1}\sum_iI_{i,ja}\), and \(\overline U_{ja}=n^{-1}\sum_iU_{i,ja}\), one has \(E U_{i,ja}=0\), \(|U_{i,ja}|\le2B_Y\), and, on \(\widehat s_{ja}>0\),
\[
 \sqrt n(\widehat\mu_{n,ja}-\mu_{n,ja})
 =n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^n\frac{U_{i,ja}}{s_{ja}}
 +R_{n,ja},\qquad
 R_{n,ja}=\sqrt n\,\overline U_{ja}
       \left(\frac1{\widehat s_{ja}}-\frac1{s_{ja}}\right).
\]
Let \(\mathcal A_n=\bigcap_{j,a}\{|\widehat s_{ja}-s_{ja}|\le s_{ja}/2\}\).  Hoeffding's inequality, obtained here by applying the exponential Markov bound to the Bernoulli indicators and optimizing its scalar parameter, gives
\[
 P_n(\mathcal A_n^c)\le2JK\exp(-ns_0^2/2).
\]
On \(\mathcal A_n\), Cauchy--Schwarz and the variance identities for the two sample averages yield
\[
 E\{|R_{n,ja}|\mathbf1_{\mathcal A_n}\}
 \le \frac{2\sqrt n}{s_{ja}^2}
       \{E\overline U_{ja}^2\}^{1/2}
       \{E(\widehat s_{ja}-s_{ja})^2\}^{1/2}
 \le \frac{2B_Y}{s_0^2\sqrt n}.
\]
The convention on an empty cell changes a bounded-Lipschitz expectation by at most its probability.  Since \(\sup_{x>0}\sqrt{x}\exp(-s_0^2x/2)=(e^{-1/2}/s_0)\), stacking all cells and taking action-zero differences therefore gives
\[
 d_{\mathrm{BL}}\!\left\{\mathcal L(E_n),
 \mathcal L\!\left(n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^n\xi_n(O_{ni})\right)\right\}
 \le \frac{C_{\mathrm{rat}}}{\sqrt n},
 \quad
 C_{\mathrm{rat}}=8JK\sqrt d\,B_Ys_0^{-2}+2JK(e^{-1/2}/s_0).
\]

For completeness, the finite-dimensional normal comparison needed next can be derived directly.  If independent centered vectors \(X_i\in\mathbb R^d\) satisfy \(\sum_iE(X_iX_i^\top)=\Sigma\), \(\lambda_{\min}(\Sigma)\ge v_{min}\), and \(\lambda_{\max}(\Sigma)\le v_{max}\), Gaussian interpolation of a bounded one-Lipschitz test function, followed by Taylor's formula with integral remainder in each leave-one-out vector, gives
\[
 d_{\mathrm{BL}}\{\mathcal L(\textstyle\sum_iX_i),N(0,\Sigma)\}
 \le 32(d+1)^{3/2}\max(1,\sqrt{v_{\max}}),v_{\min}^{-3/2}
       \sum_iE\|X_i\|_2^3.                                           \tag{1}
\]
Here is the derivative calculation behind (1).  After replacing \(X_i\) by \(\Sigma^{-1/2}X_i\), solve the Gaussian interpolation equation by
\[
 g_f(x)=-\int_0^\infty
 \left[E f\{e^{-s}x+(1-e^{-2s})^{1/2}Z\}-Ef(Z)\right]ds,qquad Z\sim N(0,I_d).
\]
Gaussian integration by parts changes \(E\{\Delta g_f(W)-W^\top\nabla g_f(W)\}\) into the sum of the leave-one-out second-order Taylor remainders.  Splitting the integral at \(s=\|X_i\|_2^2\), using \(|f(x)-f(y)|\le\|x-y\|_2\) before the split and the Gaussian derivative formula after it, bounds the \(i\)-th remainder by \(32(d+1)^{3/2}E\|X_i\|_2^3\).  Undoing the whitening multiplies this by at most \(v_{\min}^{-3/2}\max(1,\sqrt{v_{\max}})\), which proves (1), including for nonsmooth Lipschitz tests by Gaussian mollification and monotone passage to the limit.

In the present problem \(E\xi_n(O)=0\), \(\operatorname{Var}\{\xi_n(O)\}=V_n\), and
\[
 \|\xi_n(O)\|_2\le B_\xi:=\frac{4B_Y\sqrt d}{p_{\min}e_{\min}}.
\]
Apply (1) to \(X_i=\xi_n(O_{ni})/\sqrt n\).  Then \(\sum_iE\|X_i\|_2^3\le B_\xi^3/\sqrt n\).  The triangle inequality gives the asserted result with the explicit choice
\[
 C_{\mathrm{BE}}=C_{\mathrm{rat}}+
 32(d+1)^{3/2}\max(1,\sqrt{v_{\max}})v_{\min}^{-3/2}B_\xi^3.
\]
Every denominator is protected by \(s_0>0\), and the covariance inversion is protected by \(v_{\min}>0\), as required.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This lemma derives the declared Gaussian envelope from primitive finite-cell randomization rather than assuming a property of a named estimator. \textit{Gap.} CCK gives abstract empirical-process coupling, while no cited result records this uniform finite-cell ratio constant for the present stacked contrasts. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies a checkable approximation input for the path theorem and for the conditional three-arm illustration.

\begin{lemma}[lem:bregman-identity]\label{lem:bregman-identity}
For every \(c>0\), positive \(b\), and \(u,z\in\mathbb R^K\), \(\ell_{c,b}(u,z)=c\,\mathrm{KL}\{q_{c,b}(u+z)\|q_{c,b}(u)\}\) and \(\ell_{c,b}(u,z)=c^{-1}\int_0^1s\,z^{\top}C\{q_{c,b}(u+sz)\}z\,ds\). Consequently, \(0\le\ell_{c,b}(u,z)\le\operatorname{osc}(z)\) and \(0\le c\ell_{c,b}(u,z)\le\operatorname{osc}(z)^2/8\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write \(q(v)=q_{c,b}(v)\) and \(\Psi(v)=\Psi_{c,b}(v)\).  Positivity of every coordinate of \(b\) makes all logarithms finite, and direct differentiation gives
\[
 \nabla\Psi(v)=q(v),\qquad \nabla^2\Psi(v)=c^{-1}C\{q(v)\}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \log\frac{q(u+z)_a}{q(u)_a}
 =\frac{z_a}{c}-\log\frac{\sum_rb_r e^{(u_r+z_r)/c}}
                              {\sum_rb_r e^{u_r/c}}.
\]
Multiplication by \(q(u+z)_a\), summation over \(a\), and multiplication by \(c\) give exactly
\[
 c\,\mathrm{KL}\{q(u+z)\|q(u)\}
 =\Psi(u)-\Psi(u+z)+z^\top q(u+z)=\ell_{c,b}(u,z).
\]
For \(f(s)=\Psi(u+sz)\), twice applying the fundamental theorem of calculus yields
\[
 f(0)-f(1)+f'(1)=\int_0^1s f''(s)\,ds,
\]
and hence
\[
 \ell_{c,b}(u,z)=c^{-1}\int_0^1s\,z^\top C\{q(u+sz)\}z\,ds.
\]
The covariance matrix \(C(r)\) is positive semidefinite for every probability vector \(r\), proving nonnegativity.  Also, convexity and the elementary bounds \(\min_a z_a\le z^\top r\le\max_a z_a\) imply
\[
 \ell_{c,b}(u,z)\le \max_a z_a-\min_a z_a=\operatorname{osc}(z).
\]
Finally, \(z^\top C(r)z=\operatorname{Var}_r(z_a)\le\operatorname{osc}(z)^2/4\); inserting this bound into the integral and using \(\int_0^1s\,ds=1/2\) gives \(c\ell_{c,b}(u,z)\le\operatorname{osc}(z)^2/8\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The exact identity fixes the regret normalization and provides an integrable envelope uniformly over temperature. \textit{Gap.} Fang--Ridder--Xie uses second-order regret at a fixed smooth optimum; the exact categorical identity over the complete temperature scale is not its theorem. \textit{Consumer.} The path coupling and certified tail truncation both use the global oscillation bounds.

\begin{lemma}[lem:exact-regret-path]\label{lem:exact-regret-path}
For every \(c>0\) and \(t=c/(1+c)\), the causal penalized-welfare regret satisfies \((1+c)\sqrt n\,\mathscr R_n(c)=G(u_n,E_n)(t)=T_n(t)\). Thus the interior process is an identified causal regret curve, and its values at \(t=0\) and \(t=1\) are defined by the two compactified limits.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Conditional randomization and consistency identify \(\mu_{n,ja}=E(Y\mid X=j,A=a)\); overlap makes each conditioning event positive.  In stratum \(j\), subtracting the action-zero mean, multiplying the objective by \(\sqrt n\), and using \(\lambda_n(c)=c/\sqrt n\) gives
\[
 \sqrt n\left\{\sum_a\mu_{n,ja}\pi_{ja}-\lambda_n(c)\mathrm{KL}(\pi_j\|b_j)-\mu_{n,j0}\right\}
 =(Ru_{n,j})^\top\pi_j-c\,\mathrm{KL}(\pi_j\|b_j).
\]
The Lagrange equations for maximizing the right side over the simplex are
\[
 (Ru_{n,j})_a-c\{1+\log(\pi_{ja}/b_{ja})\}=\kappa_j,
\]
and benchmark positivity gives the unique solution \(q_{c,b_j}(Ru_{n,j})=\pi^*_{n,c,j}\).  For arbitrary \(v,w\in\mathbb R^K\), direct substitution of the Gibbs probabilities shows
\[
 \big[v^\top q(v)-c\mathrm{KL}\{q(v)\|b\}\big]
 -\big[v^\top q(w)-c\mathrm{KL}\{q(w)\|b\}\big]
 =c\mathrm{KL}\{q(w)\|q(v)\}=\ell_{c,b}(v,w-v).
\]
Set \(v=Ru_{n,j}\) and \(w=R\widehat u_{n,j}=R(u_{n,j}+E_{n,j})\), sum with weights \(p_j\), and divide by \(\sqrt n\).  This gives
\[
 \sqrt n\,\mathscr R_n(c)=\sum_jp_j\ell_{c,b_j}(Ru_{n,j},RE_{n,j}).
\]
Multiplication by \(1+c\), followed by \(t=c/(1+c)\), is exactly the interior definition of \(G(u_n,E_n)(t)=T_n(t)\).  The endpoint equalities are compactified limits proved in the hard- and high-endpoint lemmas; they are not additional observed-law definitions of the causal regret.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This identity prevents the Gaussian path from becoming a detached statistical surrogate and pins every interior coordinate to the planner's causal objective. \textit{Gap.} The presolve algebra and Fang--Ridder--Xie fixed-weight criterion motivate the identity, but the full local normalization and endpoint compactification are not a published result. \textit{Consumer.} The simultaneous band can be interpreted directly in penalized-welfare units at every interior temperature selected measurably from the observed sample or the plug-in frontier.

\begin{lemma}[lem:high-endpoint]\label{lem:high-endpoint}
Uniformly for bounded \(u,z\), \(\left|c\ell_{c,b}(u,z)-\frac12z^{\top}C(b)z\right|\le3\|z\|_\infty^2(\|u\|_\infty+\|z\|_\infty)/c\), and \(R^{\top}C(b)R\succeq\beta^2I_{K-1}\). Hence \(G(u,z)(t)\to G(u,z)(1)\) as \(t\uparrow1\) with a computable endpoint modulus.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For \(v\in\mathbb R^K\), differentiate the exponential tilt \(s\mapsto q_{c,b}(sv)\):
\[
 \frac{d}{ds}q_{c,b}(sv)=c^{-1}C\{q_{c,b}(sv)\}v.
\]
Since \(\sum_a q_a|v_a-q^\top v|\le2\|v\|_\infty\), integration from \(0\) to \(1\) gives \(\|q_{c,b}(v)-b\|_1\le2\|v\|_\infty/c\).  For probability vectors \(r,b\), expansion of the diagonal and rank-one terms gives
\[
 |z^\top\{C(r)-C(b)\}z|
 \le3\|z\|_\infty^2\|r-b\|_1.
\]
The integral representation of the Bregman loss therefore implies
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \left|c\ell_{c,b}(u,z)-\tfrac12z^\top C(b)z\right|
 &\le3\|z\|_\infty^2\int_0^1s\|q_{c,b}(u+sz)-b\|_1ds\\
 &\le \frac{3\|z\|_\infty^2(\|u\|_\infty+\|z\|_\infty)}{c}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Also \(\ell_{c,b}(u,z)\le\operatorname{osc}(z)^2/(8c)\), so replacing \(c\ell\) by \((1+c)\ell\) adds only a further computable \(O(c^{-1})\) term on bounded sets.  This proves the asserted endpoint modulus.

For \(x\in\mathbb R^{K-1}\), the categorical variance identity gives
\[
 x^\top R^\top C(b)Rx
 =\sum_{0\le a<r\le K-1}b_ab_r\{(Rx)_a-(Rx)_r\}^2
 \ge b_0\sum_{a=1}^{K-1}b_ax_a^2
 \ge\beta^2\|x\|_2^2.
\]
Thus the projected endpoint is positive definite under the declared support condition.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The projected quadratic endpoint is the fixed-positive-penalty regime after local compactification. \textit{Gap.} The fixed-penalty quadratic law in Fang--Ridder--Xie does not prove uniform matching to all vanishing local penalties. \textit{Consumer.} The endpoint reduction connects the path to published regret theory and removes an infinite temperature tail from numerical integration.

\begin{lemma}[lem:hard-endpoint]\label{lem:hard-endpoint}
Let \(\gamma(v)\) be the gap from \(\max_av_a\) to the largest nonmaximal coordinate, with \(\gamma(v)=\infty\) if every coordinate ties. Then \(\|q_{c,b}(v)-q_{0,b}(v)\|_1\le2\beta^{-1}\exp\{-\gamma(v)/c\}\) and \(|\ell_{c,b}(u,z)-\ell_{0,b}(u,z)|\le2c\log(1/\beta)+2\|z\|_\infty\beta^{-1}\exp\{-\gamma(u+z)/c\}\). Moreover, uniformly in \(h\in\mathcal H\) and \(V\in\mathcal K_V\), the probability that any pair of augmented action coordinates of \(R(h+Z_V)\) lies within \(a>0\) is at most \(JK(K-1)a/\sqrt{2\pi v_{\min}}\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(M=\max_av_a\), \(I=I(v)\), and \(B_I=\sum_{a\in I}b_a\).  Benchmark support gives \(B_I\ge\beta\), while
\[
 \Psi_{c,b}(v)=M+c\log\left\{B_I+\sum_{a\notin I}b_ae^{-(M-v_a)/c}\right\}.
\]
Consequently \(|\Psi_{c,b}(v)-M|\le c\log(1/\beta)\).  The total Gibbs mass outside \(I\) is at most \(\beta^{-1}e^{-\gamma(v)/c}\); conditional on \(I\), its proportions are exactly proportional to \(b_a\).  Hence
\[
 \|q_{c,b}(v)-q_{0,b}(v)\|_1
 =2q_{c,b}(v)(I^c)
 \le2\beta^{-1}e^{-\gamma(v)/c}.
\]
Apply the log-sum bound at \(u\) and \(u+z\), and the last display at \(u+z\), in the definitions of \(\ell_c\) and \(\ell_0\).  The triangle inequality gives
\[
 |\ell_{c,b}(u,z)-\ell_{0,b}(u,z)|
 \le2c\log(1/\beta)+2\|z\|_\infty\beta^{-1}e^{-\gamma(u+z)/c}.
\]
This includes every exact multiway tie; when all coordinates tie, \(\gamma=\infty\) and the Gibbs and hard lotteries agree exactly.

For the probability bound, each within-stratum pairwise difference is \(m+v^\top Z_V\) for a nonzero contrast vector \(v\).  If one action is zero then \(\|v\|_2=1\), and otherwise \(\|v\|_2=\sqrt2\).  Since \(V\succeq v_{\min}I_d\), its variance is at least \(v_{\min}\).  A univariate normal variable of variance at least \(v_{\min}\) has density at most \((2\pi v_{\min})^{-1/2}\), so
\[
 P\{|m+v^\top Z_V|\le a\}\le\frac{2a}{\sqrt{2\pi v_{\min}}}.
\]
There are \(J K(K-1)/2\) pairs.  The union bound proves the displayed \(JK(K-1)a/\sqrt{2\pi v_{\min}}\) inequality, uniformly in the means and covariance.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This lemma handles exact and multiway ties without imposing a unique best action. \textit{Gap.} Whitehouse et al. use uniform weights over exact maximizers for a scalar optimal-value influence function; the present endpoint instead renormalizes the benchmark lottery and requires a uniform multiaction slab bound. \textit{Consumer.} It controls the discontinuous state geometry in the path coupling and certifies off-grid behavior near the hard rule.

\begin{lemma}[lem:path-wellposedness]\label{lem:path-wellposedness}
The map \((u,z)\mapsto G(u,z)\) is Borel measurable into \(C([0,1])\); for every \(h\in\mathcal H\) and \(V\in\mathcal K_V\), \(S(h,Z_V)\) is finite almost surely; and \((h,V)\mapsto\mathcal L\{S(h,Z_V)\}\) is weakly continuous at every continuity set after the benchmark-weighted tie convention is imposed. Consequently, \(Q_{1-\alpha}^{\mathrm{LF}}\) is finite and its right-quantile definition is coverage-valid.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For fixed \((u,z)\), the two endpoint lemmas show that the interior formula for \(G(u,z)\) converges to the declared values at both \(t=0\) and \(t=1\); hence it is in \(C([0,1])\).  Every rational-time evaluation is a Borel function of \((u,z)\), including the hard evaluation because it is a finite combination of comparisons and benchmark-weighted finite sums.  Since \(C([0,1])\) is separable and its Borel sigma-field is generated by rational-time evaluations, \((u,z)\mapsto G(u,z)\) is Borel.

The global loss bounds imply, for every \(t\),
\[
 0\le G(u,z)(t)\le\sum_jp_j\left\{\operatorname{osc}(Rz_j)
       +\operatorname{osc}(Rz_j)^2/8\right\}.
\]
The right side is finite for every finite \(z\), proving almost-sure finiteness under every Gaussian law and a uniform Gaussian tail bound because \(V\preceq v_{\max}I_d\).

Now let \((h_m,V_m)\to(h,V)\), use the continuous positive-definite square root, and couple \(Z_{V_m}=V_m^{1/2}g\), \(Z_V=V^{1/2}g\) with \(g\sim N(0,I_d)\).  The union of the finitely many decision hyperplanes of \(R(h+Z_V)\) has probability zero because \(V\succ0\).  Off that union the winning action in every stratum is locally constant.  On such a realization, split the temperature domain into a small neighborhood of zero, a compact interior, and a neighborhood of one.  The hard bound controls the first piece, ordinary differentiability controls the compact piece, and the high-temperature bound controls the last piece.  It follows that
\[
 \|G(h_m,Z_{V_m})-G(h,Z_V)\|_\infty\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
Thus \(S(h_m,Z_{V_m})\to S(h,Z_V)\) almost surely, and probabilities converge on every continuity set.

Finally, the preceding uniform tail bound makes all individual right \((1-\alpha)\)-quantiles finite uniformly over the compact parameter set.  The least-favorable quantile is their supremum: indeed, \(\inf_{h,V}P\{S(h,Z_V)\le q\}\ge1-\alpha\) holds exactly when \(q\) is at least every individual right quantile.  Therefore \(Q_{1-\alpha}^{\mathrm{LF}}<\infty\), and at that value every individual closed CDF is at least \(1-\alpha\).  This last conclusion uses right quantiles and remains valid at atoms.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Measurability, finiteness, and parameter continuity are required before least-favorable quantile inversion is mathematically meaningful. \textit{Gap.} Generic continuous mapping does not apply across the hard state discontinuities; the result must use their Gaussian null sets and the explicit endpoint bounds. \textit{Consumer.} The calibrated critical value is a well-defined functional over the complete local-parameter and covariance envelopes.

\begin{lemma}[lem:operational-selector-bridge]\label{lem:operational-selector-bridge}
The plug-in frontier \(\widehat{\mathfrak F}_n\) is a \(\mathcal F_n^{\mathrm{obs}}\)-measurable random element of \(C([0,1])\) and is computable from \(\{(X_{ni},A_{ni},Y_{ni}):1\le i\le n\}\), \(b\), and the declared finite-cell estimators, without \(\Delta_n\), \(u_n\), or \(E_n\). For every \(\mathcal F_n^{\mathrm{obs}}\)-measurable \(\widehat t_n\) and every real \(x\), the deterministic inclusion \(\{\sup_{t\in[0,1]}T_n(t)\le x\}\subseteq\{T_n(\widehat t_n)\le x\}\) holds. On \(0<\widehat t_n<1\), with \(\widehat c_n=\widehat t_n/(1-\widehat t_n)\), \(T_n(\widehat t_n)=(1+\widehat c_n)\sqrt n\,\mathscr R_n(\widehat c_n)\). Thus a uniform law or simultaneous coverage bound for the oracle path transfers exactly to a selector computed from the plug-in frontier; no approximation of the unobserved regret by \(\widehat{\mathfrak F}_n\) is required.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each cell count, cell sum, empirical stratum weight, and hence each coordinate of \(\widehat u_n\) is \(\mathcal F_n^{\mathrm{obs}}\)-measurable.  The formulas defining the plug-in frontier use only finite arithmetic, maxima, exponentials, logarithms, and the fixed benchmark.  The endpoint lemmas show that the resulting pointwise formula is a continuous path.  Rational-time measurability and separability of \(C([0,1])\) then show that \(\widehat{\mathfrak F}_n\) is an \(\mathcal F_n^{\mathrm{obs}}\)-measurable random element.  Its displayed construction contains neither \(\Delta_n\), \(u_n\), nor \(E_n\).

For every path \(f\), every index \(s\in[0,1]\), and every real \(x\), \(\sup_tf(t)\le x\) implies \(f(s)\le x\).  Apply this deterministic fact pointwise with \(f=T_n\) and \(s=\widehat t_n\) to obtain
\[
 \{\sup_tT_n(t)\le x\}\subseteq\{T_n(\widehat t_n)\le x\}.
\]
No independence between \(T_n\) and the selector is used.  If \(0<\widehat t_n<1\), the exact regret identity with \(\widehat c_n=\widehat t_n/(1-\widehat t_n)\) gives
\[
 T_n(\widehat t_n)=(1+\widehat c_n)\sqrt n\,\mathscr R_n(\widehat c_n).
\]
Thus the observed frontier selects the index, while the covered target remains the oracle causal regret.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The lemma separates the observable frontier used to choose a temperature from the oracle regret path that the confidence envelope covers. \textit{Gap.} Post-search arguments such as Kang's assume an observed indexed statistic, while the causal regret here contains an unobserved oracle comparator; the exact event inclusion supplies the needed operational bridge without a false plug-in equality. \textit{Consumer.} A planner can compute a temperature from the displayed plug-in frontier and invoke the simultaneous oracle-regret certificate at precisely that observed-data choice.

\begin{theorem}[thm:uniform-path-law]\label{thm:uniform-path-law}
There is an explicit computable \(C_{\mathrm{map}}\) such that, for all sufficiently large \(n\), uniformly over \((P_n)\in\mathcal M(J,K,\mathcal H,\mathcal K_V)\), \(d_{\mathrm{BL}}\{\mathcal L(T_n),\mathcal L(G(h,Z_{V_n}))\}\le\varepsilon_n\) in \(C([0,1])\), with \(\varepsilon_n\) given in \(\mathrm{def:curve\mbox{-}rate}\) and \(\varepsilon_n\to0\). The same coupling is joint with \(E_n\), and the bound includes both compactified endpoints.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We first record the elementary coupling consequence of bounded-Lipschitz distance.  If \(d_{\mathrm{BL}}(P,Q)\le r\le1\), then for every closed \(A\) the ramp \((1-d(x,A)/\delta)_+\), with \(\delta=\sqrt r\), gives \(P(A)\le Q(A^\delta)+\delta\), and symmetrically.  Approximate the two laws by finite partitions of mesh tending to zero; the preceding inequalities are precisely the cut inequalities for a bipartite transport that leaves at most \(\delta\) mass outside pairs at distance at most \(2\delta\).  Finite max-flow/min-cut and weak compactness of couplings therefore give random vectors \((E,Z)\) with the prescribed laws such that
\[
 P(\|E-Z\|_\infty>2\sqrt r)\le2\sqrt r.                \tag{2}
\]
This derivation also shows that the same coupling can be retained when the vector itself is appended to any mapped process.

Apply (2) with \(r=r_n\), \(E=E_n\), and \(Z=Z_{V_n}\).  Put \(x=2\sqrt{r_n}+\rho_n\); changing the harmless factor two only enlarges the final constant multiplying the frozen \(x_n=\sqrt{r_n}+\rho_n\).  For \(M\ge1\) and \(0<a\le1/4\), let \(\mathcal G(M,a)\) be the event that \(\|Z\|_\infty\le M\), the coupling error is at most \(2\sqrt{r_n}\), and every augmented action-coordinate gap of \(R(h+Z)\) exceeds \(a\).  The hard-endpoint slab bound and the Gaussian coordinate tail give
\[
 P\{\mathcal G(M,a)^c\}
 \le C_0\left[\sqrt{r_n}+\frac{JK^2a}{\sqrt{v_{\min}}}
 +d e^{-M^2/(2v_{\max})}\right]                       \tag{3}
\]
for the numerical constant \(C_0=4\).  The condition \(x_n\le a/8\) ensures that the coupled, locally shifted argument remains in the same hard cell.

Here is the deterministic path estimate used on \(\mathcal G(M,a)\).  Let
\[
 c_0=\frac{a}{8\log\{\exp(1)+M/a\}}.
\]
For \(c\le c_0\), compare both paths with their common hard-cell formula.  The hard-endpoint estimate, \(c\ell_c\le c\operatorname{osc}(Rz)\), and \(e^{-a/(2c_0)}\le\{\exp(1)+M/a\}^{-4}\) show that the total error is at most \(C_1(Ma+x)\).  For \(c\ge c_0\), use
\[
 D_u\ell[r]=\{q(u)-q(u+z)\}^\top r+c^{-1}z^\top C\{q(u+z)\}r,
 \qquad
 D_z\ell[r]=c^{-1}z^\top C\{q(u+z)\}r,
\]
when \(c\le1\), and use the integral covariance representation when \(c\ge1\).  The coordinate derivatives of \(C(q)\) are bounded by \(6/c\).  The mean-value theorem consequently gives
\[
 \sup_{c\ge c_0}|(1+c)\ell_c(u',z')-(1+c)\ell_c(u,z)|
 \le C_2\frac{x(1+M)^2\log\{\exp(1)+M/a\}}{a}.        \tag{4}
\]
The constants \(C_1,C_2\) depend only on \(J,K,\beta\) after summing with the weights \(p_j\); the endpoint at infinity is included by the high-temperature expansion.  Equations (3)--(4), the bound by one in the definition of the bounded-Lipschitz metric, and the coupling give
\[
 d_{\mathrm{BL}}\!\left(\mathcal L\{G(u_n,E_n)\},
                    \mathcal L\{G(h,Z_{V_n})\}\right)
 \le C_{\mathrm{map}}\left[\sqrt{r_n}+\frac{JK^2a}{\sqrt{v_{\min}}}
 +d e^{-M^2/(2v_{\max})}+Ma
 +\frac{x_n(1+M)^2\log\{\exp(1)+M/a\}}a\right],
\]
where, for example, tracing the displayed bounds permits
\[
 C_{\mathrm{map}}=2^{12}JK^2\beta^{-2}p_{\min}^{-1}
 (1+v_{\min}^{-1/2}+v_{\max}+\operatorname{diam}(\mathcal H)).
\]
Taking the stated infimum proves the advertised bound.  Applying the same argument to a bounded one-Lipschitz test of \((G(u_n,E_n),E_n)\) proves the joint assertion.

It remains to check convergence.  Set \(M_n=\max\{1,\sqrt{4v_{\max}\log(1/x_n)}\}\) and \(a_n=x_n^{1/3}\) once \(x_n<1\).  For all sufficiently large \(n\), \(x_n\le a_n/8\); every displayed term then tends to zero, including
\[
 x_n a_n^{-1}(1+M_n)^2\log\{\exp(1)+M_n/a_n\}.
\]
Since \(r_n\to0\) and \(\rho_n\to0\), this proves \(\varepsilon_n\to0\) uniformly over the stated model class.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} A quantitative function-valued law is the inferential object needed when the KL weight is selected after observing the sample-computable plug-in frontier. \textit{Gap.} Fang--Ridder--Xie is pointwise at each fixed positive penalty and separate at the deterministic boundary; Whitehouse et al. use one predetermined diverging smoother for a different scalar target. \textit{Consumer.} Together with the operational-selector bridge, it permits simultaneous regret inference at every temperature chosen measurably from the observed sample or its plug-in frontier.

\begin{theorem}[thm:certified-calibration]\label{thm:certified-calibration}
Let \(\mathfrak R\) be a valid effective input with rational \(\alpha,\gamma,\zeta,\overline\varepsilon_n\), total interval oracles for \(p,b,v_{\min},v_{\max},p_{\min},\beta,\varepsilon_n\), rational-box representations of every optimized \(h\in\mathcal H\) and \(V\in\mathcal K_V\), and \(\varepsilon_n\le\overline\varepsilon_n\) with \(\sqrt{\overline\varepsilon_n}+\gamma<\alpha\). The algorithm \(\mathsf A(\mathfrak R)\) halts after at most \(N_{\mathsf A}\) certified box operations and oracle calls of precision at most \(m_{\mathsf A}\), and returns \((Q^+_{n,\alpha},\mathcal E_n,\mathfrak C)\). An exact-rational verifier accepts \(\mathfrak C\) only after checking every elementary-function, spectral, path, and Gaussian-probability outward enclosure, the inequalities \(\omega_t+\omega_h+\omega_V\le\zeta\) and \(\omega_{\mathrm{tail}}+\omega_{\mathrm{int}}+\omega_{\mathrm{fp}}\le\gamma\), and the branch-specific rational conditions in the calibration-certificate definition. Every accepted certificate implies \(\inf_{h\in\mathcal H,V\in\mathcal K_V}P\{S(h,Z_V)\le Q^+_{n,\alpha}+\zeta\}\ge1-\alpha+\sqrt{\overline\varepsilon_n}\). This implication is valid for the refined branch and for the rational fallback \(Q^+_{n,\alpha}=2B_\gamma^++(B_\gamma^+)^2/2\), including when the least-favorable law has atoms. Counting each interval primitive at unit cost, the bit complexity is polynomial in \(N_{\mathsf A}\), the maximum input bit length, and the oracle-output bit length at \(m_{\mathsf A}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(\delta_n=\alpha-\sqrt{\overline\varepsilon_n}-\gamma>0\).  From the definition of \(B_\gamma\),
\[
 2d\exp\{-B_\gamma^2/(2v_{\max})\}=\delta_n.
\]
For every \(V\preceq v_{\max}I_d\), the scalar Gaussian tail bound and a union bound imply
\[
 P(\|Z_V\|_\infty>B_\gamma^+)\le
 2d\exp\{-(B_\gamma^+)^2/(2v_{\max})\}\le\delta_n,     \tag{5}
\]
because directed rounding certifies \(B_\gamma^+\ge B_\gamma\).  The global Bregman and hard-loss bounds give, for every \(h,z\),
\[
 S(h,z)\le2\|z\|_\infty+\tfrac12\|z\|_\infty^2.      \tag{6}
\]
Indeed \(\operatorname{osc}(Rz_j)\le2\|z\|_\infty\), \(c\ell_c\le\operatorname{osc}(Rz_j)^2/8\), the hard loss is at most the same oscillation, and the quadratic endpoint is at most \(\operatorname{osc}(Rz_j)^2/8\).  Equations (5)--(6) prove that the rational fallback
\[
 q^+=2B_\gamma^++(B_\gamma^+)^2/2
\]
has Gaussian lower CDF at least \(1-\alpha+\sqrt{\overline\varepsilon_n}+\gamma\).  After the probability ledger spends at most \(\gamma\), the required lower bound remains.

On the refined branch, every retained parameter box is an outer box, every discarded covariance box has a directed spectral-disjointness witness, every accepted Gaussian box is contained in the sublevel event after the three deterministic off-grid errors, and every density integral is rounded downward.  Therefore the exact-rational sum \(L_{\mathrm{CDF}}\), after subtracting the three probability-ledger upper bounds, is no larger than the true worst-case CDF.  The verifier's inequalities consequently imply
\[
 \inf_{h\in\mathcal H,V\in\mathcal K_V}
 P\{S(h,Z_V)\le Q^+_{n,\alpha}+\zeta\}
 \ge1-\alpha+\sqrt{\overline\varepsilon_n}.            \tag{7}
\]
This is a one-sided inclusion argument and never inverts a continuous approximation to a CDF, so atoms cause no defect.

The algorithm performs no more than \(w_{\max}\) refinement nodes.  Each node has at most \(K^J\{8+4d+d(d+1)\}\) certified box primitives, the fallback and initialization use at most \(1+K^J\{8+4d+d(d+1)\}\), and radius evaluation uses at most \(4d(m_{\mathsf A}+1)\) interval calls.  Their sum is bounded by the stated \(N_{\mathsf A}\).  Totality and the width guarantee of the interval oracles imply that every call at precision at most \(m_{\mathsf A}\) halts; after the work cap, the fallback just proved is emitted.  Rational range reduction plus Taylor remainders evaluates each elementary function with bit cost polynomial in the input and requested output bit lengths.  Thus the claimed unit-cost count and bit-complexity statement follow, and every accepted certificate has implication (7).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} A one-sided critical value remains operationally honest when every real input has an effective name, every rounding direction is independently checkable, and a verified analytic fallback guarantees termination even at atoms. \textit{Gap.} Existing Gaussian integration engines address supplied rectangles or orthants; they do not certify this nonlinear continuum sublevel event or its least-favorable parameter envelope. \textit{Consumer.} The algorithm turns the path approximation into an auditable conservative critical value for low-dimensional multiaction experiments.

\begin{theorem}[thm:atom-safe-envelope]\label{thm:atom-safe-envelope}
For any sequence of accepted effective certificates with \(\varepsilon_n\le\overline\varepsilon_n\to0\), let \(C_{n,\alpha}=Q^+_{n,\alpha}+\sqrt{\overline\varepsilon_n}+\zeta\). Then \(\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{(P_n)\in\mathcal M(J,K,\mathcal H,\mathcal K_V)}P_n\{T_n(t)\le C_{n,\alpha}\ \text{for every }t\in[0,1]\}\ge1-\alpha\). For every operational selector \(\widehat t_n\) that is \(\mathcal F_n^{\mathrm{obs}}\)-measurable, including every Borel-measurable choice based on \(\widehat{\mathfrak F}_n\), \(\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{(P_n)\in\mathcal M(J,K,\mathcal H,\mathcal K_V)}P_n\{T_n(\widehat t_n)\le C_{n,\alpha}\}\ge1-\alpha\). At an interior selected value this event is exactly \(\{(1+\widehat c_n)\sqrt n\,\mathscr R_n(\widehat c_n)\le C_{n,\alpha}\}\), where \(\widehat c_n=\widehat t_n/(1-\widehat t_n)\). The plug-in frontier is used only to generate the observed-data selector; coverage concerns the oracle normalized regret path. The conclusion uses one-sided ramps and the verified lower CDF bracket and does not assume continuity of the distribution of \(S(h,Z_V)\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(q_n=Q^+_{n,\alpha}+\zeta\) and \(\delta_n=\sqrt{\overline\varepsilon_n}\).  Because \(\overline\varepsilon_n<1\), define the one-sided ramp on path space by
\[
 \varphi_n(f)=
 \begin{cases}
 1,&\sup_tf(t)\le q_n,\\
 1-\{\sup_tf(t)-q_n\}/\delta_n,&q_n<\sup_tf(t)<q_n+\delta_n,\\
 0,&\sup_tf(t)\ge q_n+\delta_n.
 \end{cases}
\]
The supremum map is one-Lipschitz, so \(0\le\varphi_n\le1\) and \(\varphi_n\) is \(\delta_n^{-1}\)-Lipschitz.  The uniform path law and \(\varepsilon_n\le\overline\varepsilon_n\) therefore give, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 E_{P_n}\varphi_n(T_n)
 \ge E\varphi_n\{G(h,Z_{V_n})\}
      -\varepsilon_n\max(1,\delta_n^{-1})
 \ge E\varphi_n\{G(h,Z_{V_n})\}-\delta_n.             \tag{8}
\]
The certified calibration gives
\[
 P\{S(h,Z_{V_n})\le q_n\}\ge1-\alpha+\delta_n
\]
uniformly because \(h\in\mathcal H\) and \(V_n\in\mathcal K_V\).  Since the Gaussian sublevel indicator is bounded above by \(\varphi_n\), while \(\varphi_n(T_n)\) is bounded above by the indicator of \(\sup_tT_n(t)\le q_n+\delta_n=C_{n,\alpha}\), (8) yields
\[
 \inf_{(P_n)\in\mathcal M}P_n\{T_n(t)\le C_{n,\alpha}
       \text{ for all }t\in[0,1]\}\ge1-\alpha
\]
for all sufficiently large accepted rows.  Taking the requested lower limit proves the first assertion.  Notice that no probability of an interval \((q_n,q_n+\delta_n]\) was bounded; an atom is therefore harmless.

For every observed-data-measurable selector, the deterministic inclusion from the selector bridge sends the simultaneous event into \(\{T_n(\widehat t_n)\le C_{n,\alpha}\}\), without an independence assumption.  The same lower bound follows.  On the interior, the exact regret identity changes this event, and only this event, into
\[
 \{(1+\widehat c_n)\sqrt n\,\mathscr R_n(\widehat c_n)\le C_{n,\alpha}\}.
\]
This proves selection-valid coverage for the causal oracle regret while keeping the plug-in frontier solely on the selection side.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the operational selection-valid coverage theorem: the temperature is chosen from observed data, the covered quantity is oracle causal regret, and the one-sided ramp remains correct at every quantile atom. \textit{Gap.} Gaussian anti-concentration and regular quantile inversion do not handle the nonlinear hard-cell atom collisions proved in this note; the theorem instead uses a verified lower CDF bracket. \textit{Consumer.} A planner may inspect the sample-computable frontier, choose a temperature measurably, and report a certified upper bound for oracle regret at that chosen point.

\begin{proposition}[prop:covariance-inflation-sharpness]\label{prop:covariance-inflation-sharpness}
In the binary one-stratum submodel with \(\mathcal H=\{0\}\), \(b=(1/2,1/2)\), and scalar \(V\in(v_{\min},v_{\max})\), let \(q_{1-\alpha}(V)\) be the right \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile of \(S(0,Z_V)\). There exist \(c_1,c_2,s_0>0\) such that \(c_1s\le q_{1-\alpha}(V)-q_{1-\alpha}(V-s)\le c_2s\) for \(0<s<s_0\). Hence a localized plug-in calibration that may underestimate \(V\) by \(s\) cannot be made uniformly honest with \(o(s)\) critical-value inflation.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
In this submodel write \(z\in\mathbb R\) and \(S_0(z)=S(0,z)\).  Benchmark symmetry makes \(S_0\) even.  For \(z>0\) and finite \(c>0\), direct differentiation gives
\[
 \frac{d}{dz}\ell_{c,b}(0,z)=\frac{z}{c}q_{c,b}(z)\{1-q_{c,b}(z)\}>0,
\]
and the quadratic endpoint has derivative \(z/4>0\).  The hard endpoint is zero.  Since every positive \(z\) gives a positive interior or quadratic value, no maximizer can lie at the hard endpoint.  Compactness of \([0,1]\) then shows that \(S_0\) is continuous and strictly increasing in \(|z|\).

Let \(a_\alpha=\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha/2)>0\).  Monotonicity and continuity give the exact right-quantile formula
\[
 q_{1-\alpha}(V)=S_0(a_\alpha\sqrt V).
\]
Fix an interior \(V\) and put \(z_0=a_\alpha\sqrt V\).  The set of temperatures attaining, or lying within half the positive value \(S_0(z_0)\), is a compact subset of \((0,1]\).  On a neighborhood of \(z_0\), the displayed derivative and the quadratic derivative have a common positive lower bound \(m\) and a finite upper bound \(M\) on that compact temperature set.  Maximizers at nearby \(z\) stay in the same set.  The mean-value theorem for each active coordinate therefore yields
\[
 m|z-z'|\le |S_0(z)-S_0(z')|\le M|z-z'|
\]
for \(z,z'\) sufficiently close to \(z_0\).  Finally,
\[
 \sqrt V-\sqrt{V-s}=\frac{s}{\sqrt V+\sqrt{V-s}},
\]
whose denominator is bounded above and away from zero for small \(s>0\).  Combining the last two displays supplies constants \(c_1,c_2,s_0>0\) with the asserted two-sided linear inequality.  Hence an undershoot of order \(s\) changes the exact critical value by order \(s\), proving that a uniform \(o(s)\) inflation cannot cover this legal submodel.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The result certifies the order of the covariance component when a user replaces the robust spectral envelope by localized plug-in calibration. \textit{Gap.} CCK compares maxima under covariance perturbations, and Fang--Ridder--Xie links covariance to fixed-weight regret; neither gives the sharp quantile sensitivity of this nonlinear path supremum. \textit{Consumer.} Implementers learn when the robust envelope can be localized and how much critical-value inflation a covariance undershoot necessarily costs.

\begin{theorem}[thm:no-uniform-atom-removed-modulus]\label{thm:no-uniform-atom-removed-modulus}
For fixed \(h\in\mathcal H\), define the open hard cells \(D_{\mathbf a}(h)=\{z:\ a_j\text{ is the unique maximizer of }R(h_j+z_j)\text{ for every }j\}\), their hard levels \(L_{\mathbf a}(h)=\sum_jp_j[\max_r(Rh_j)_r-(Rh_j)_{a_j}]\), and \(U_h(z)=\sup_{0<t\le1}G(h,z)(t)\).  Then every atom of \(S(h,Z_V)\) is at one of the finitely many values \(L_{\mathbf a}(h)\), and
\[
 P\{S(h,Z_V)=q\}=\sum_{\mathbf a:L_{\mathbf a}(h)=q}P\{Z_V\in D_{\mathbf a}(h),\ U_h(Z_V)\le q\}.
\]
The frozen assumptions do not imply a uniform atom-removed modulus.  In particular, there are legal fixed inputs with \(J=K=2\), \(p=(1/2,1/2)\), common benchmark \(b=(64/65,1/65)\), \(\beta=1/65\), \(\mathcal H=[1,5/4]^2\), and \(\mathcal K_V=\{I_2\}\), constants \(\eta>0\), and sequences \(h_m\in\mathcal H\), \(q_m\in\mathbb R\), and \(x_m\downarrow0\) such that
\[
 P\{q_m<S(h_m,Z_{I_2})\le q_m+x_m\}\ge\eta.
\]
Consequently no \(A(x)\downarrow0\) having the requested uniform property follows in general, even after the atom at \(q\) is removed.  The one-sided probability certificate of \(\mathsf A\) remains valid, including at all enumerated atoms, but the frozen assumptions alone do not supply a two-sided least-favorable quantile bracket of prescribed value width.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(h\) and a hard cell \(D_{\mathbf a}(h)\).  On that cell the hard endpoint is the constant \(L_{\mathbf a}(h)\), and continuity in temperature gives
\[
 S(h,z)=\max\{L_{\mathbf a}(h),U_h(z)\},\qquad
 \lim_{c\downarrow0}(1+c)\sum_jp_j\ell_{c,b_j}(Rh_j,Rz_j)=L_{\mathbf a}(h).       \tag{9}
\]
Consider a ray \(z=sv\), \(s>0\), with \(v\ne0\).  For every finite \(c>0\), differentiation under the finite sum gives
\[
 \frac{d}{ds}\left[(1+c)\sum_jp_j\ell_{c,b_j}(Rh_j,sRv_j)\right]
 =\frac{s(1+c)}c\sum_jp_j(Rv_j)^\top C\{q_{c,b_j}(R(h_j+sv_j))\}Rv_j>0.          \tag{10}
\]
The inequality is strict because some block \(v_j\ne0\), \(Rv_j\) is then nonconstant, every Gibbs coordinate is positive, and a categorical variance vanishes only for a constant vector.  The quadratic endpoint is also strictly increasing along the ray.  The intersection of an open polyhedral cell with a ray is an interval.  If \(s_1<s_2\) lie in this interval and \(U_h(s_1v)>L_{\mathbf a}(h)\), compactness of the temperature compactification and (9) imply that the supremum at \(s_1\) is attained at some \(t>0\); (10) then gives \(U_h(s_2v)>U_h(s_1v)\).  Therefore a positive-length flat radial interval wholly inside the cell can only have value \(L_{\mathbf a}(h)\), and, for every \(q\ne L_{\mathbf a}(h)\), each radial section of
\[
 D_{\mathbf a}(h)\cap\{z:U_h(z)=q\}
\]
has one-dimensional Lebesgue measure zero.

Polar Fubini now makes that level set Lebesgue null.  The finitely many decision boundaries are affine hyperplanes and are Gaussian null because \(V\succ0\).  Since the Gaussian has a Lebesgue density, cells with \(L_{\mathbf a}(h)\ne q\) contribute no mass to \(\{S=q\}\): if \(L_{\mathbf a}>q\) the event is empty, and if \(L_{\mathbf a}<q\) it is contained in the null set \(\{U_h=q\}\).  On a cell with \(L_{\mathbf a}=q\), (9) says exactly that \(S=q\) iff \(U_h\le q\).  Summation over the finitely many cells proves the atom formula.

It remains to show that these atoms prevent a uniform atom-removed modulus for some legal fixed inputs.  Take the inputs in the statement and write \(r=b_1/b_0=1/64\).  Both coordinates of \(h=(h_1,h_2)\in[1,5/4]^2\) have action \(1\) as the true maximizer.  At the point \(z^{(1)}=(-2h_1,0)\), only stratum one is assigned to the wrong hard action, and the hard level is \(L_1(h)=h_1/2\).  At \(z^{(2)}=(0,-2h_2)\), only stratum two is wrong, and the hard level is \(L_2(h)=h_2/2\).

We verify that each point is interior to a positive-mass flat region; this is the required collision argument over all temperatures.  For one wrong stratum put \(x=h/c\).  Direct substitution into the binary log-sum-exp formula gives
\[
 \frac{\ell_{c,b}(h,-2h)}h
 =1+\frac1x\log\frac{r+e^{-x}}{1+re^{-x}}
   -\frac{2re^{-x}}{1+re^{-x}}.                         \tag{11}
\]
For \(0<h\le-\log r\), multiplying (11) by \(1+h/x\), separating the cases \(x\le-\log r\) and \(x\ge-\log r\), and applying \(y/(1+y)\le\log(1+y)\le y\) to the two logarithms gives
\[
 (1+c)\ell_{c,b}(h,-2h)<h\qquad(c>0).                  \tag{12}
\]
Here \(-\log r=\log64>5/4\), so (12) is uniform over the displayed range of \(h\) on every compact temperature interval.

The strict inequality must persist in a neighborhood uniformly also at the two ends of the temperature scale.  Near zero, direct log-sum-exp asymptotics, uniform for \(h\in[1,5/4]\), a wrong estimated contrast bounded above by a negative constant, and a correct estimated contrast bounded below by a positive constant, give respectively
\[
 (1+c)\ell_{c,b}(h,z)=h+c\{h+\log(b_1/b_0)\}
   +c^2\log(b_1/b_0)+O\{(1+c)(c+|z|)e^{-\kappa/c}\},
 \qquad
 (1+c)\ell_{c,b}(h,z)=O\{(1+c)(c+|z|)e^{-\kappa/c}\}. \tag{13}
\]
The first coefficient is at most \(5/4-\log64<0\).  Thus a linear-in-\(c\) wrong-action deficit dominates the exponentially small correct-action loss.  On an intermediate compact interval, (12), compactness in \(h\), and continuity provide a strict uniform margin.  At infinity, the high-temperature expansion gives, at the two central points,
\[
 \lim_{c\to\infty}(1+c)\ell_{c,b}(h,-2h)=2b_0b_1h^2<h,
\]
uniformly for \(h\in[1,5/4]\); its \(O(c^{-1})\) remainder and continuity give the same inequality on a neighborhood.  Combining the three temperature ranges produces open boxes \(B_1(h)\ni z^{(1)}\), \(B_2(h)\ni z^{(2)}\), with radii bounded below uniformly in \(h\), on which \(S=L_1(h)\) and \(S=L_2(h)\), respectively.  The standard Gaussian density is bounded below on the resulting compact union, so
\[
 \inf_{h\in[1,5/4]^2}P\{Z_{I_2}\in B_k(h)\}\ge\eta>0,qquad k=1,2.             \tag{14}
\]

Take \(h_m=(1,1+\delta_m)\) with rational \(\delta_m\downarrow0\), \(q_m=1/2\), and \(x_m=\delta_m/2\).  The second atom lies at \(q_m+x_m\), while the first lies at the excluded left endpoint \(q_m\).  Equation (14) yields
\[
 P\{q_m<S(h_m,Z_{I_2})\le q_m+x_m\}\ge\eta.
\]
These fixed inputs are compatible with the primitive model: take \(p_j=e_{ja}=1/2\), independent two-point potential-outcome noises of variance \(1/8\), means \(\mu_{n,j0}=0\), \(\mu_{n,j1}=h_j/\sqrt n\), and consistency.  Then bounded outcomes hold, the local remainder is zero, and the two contrast scores have covariance \(I_2\).  Hence the witness is legal.  The lower bound contradicts every proposed \(A(x)\downarrow0\).  Finally, the certified-calibration theorem uses only a lower CDF bracket and its analytic fallback, so its one-sided conclusion survives these atoms; only the requested uniform route to a two-sided value-width bracket fails.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem identifies every hard-cell atom and proves by a fixed legal binary design that removing the atom at the left endpoint does not restore uniform small-interval control. \textit{Gap.} Gaussian-process anti-concentration does not apply to this nonlinear supremum, and the explicit collision witness shows that the desired modulus is false under the declared class rather than merely unproved. \textit{Consumer.} Implementers know that the one-sided certificate is sound, while a two-sided critical-value tolerance requires an additional separation or atom-structure condition.

\begin{proposition}[prop:fang-ridder-xie-negative-transfer]\label{prop:fang-ridder-xie-negative-transfer}
Let \(\mathcal M(J,K,\mathcal H,\mathcal K_V)\) denote the declared fixed finite-cell randomized subclass.  The positive uniform path law and the causal use of the certified-calibration result are asserted only on this subclass and are not claimed over the full class studied by Fang--Ridder--Xie.  In the opposite direction, the fixed witness from \(\texttt{thm:no-uniform-atom-removed-modulus}\), with \(J=K=2\), \(p=(1/2,1/2)\), \(b=(64/65,1/65)\), \(\beta=1/65\), \(\mathcal H=[1,5/4]^2\), and \(\mathcal K_V=\{I_2\}\), satisfies the Fang--Ridder--Xie randomization, overlap, bounded-outcome, and positive-benchmark hypotheses.  Consequently the impossibility of a uniform atom-removed modulus transfers upward to that broader class: there is no function \(\widetilde A:(0,\infty)\to[0,1]\) with \(\widetilde A(x)\downarrow0\) that uniformly bounds \(P\{q<S(h,Z_V)\le q+x\}\) there.  Hence no uniform probability-to-critical-value conversion whose validity requires such a modulus is available over the broader class.  This negative transfer does not broaden either positive theorem, and the atom-safe one-sided conclusion of \(\mathsf A\) remains restricted to its declared inputs.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The class restriction for the positive statements follows directly from their quantifiers.  The conclusion of \(\texttt{thm:uniform-path-law}\) is uniform over
\[
 (P_n)_{n\geq 1}\in\mathcal M(J,K,\mathcal H,\mathcal K_V),
\]
where \(\texttt{def:model-class}\) requires fixed finite \(J\) and \(K\), the finite-cell mass and treatment-overlap conditions, bounded potential outcomes, the local-remainder condition, benchmark positivity, and the covariance envelope.  The theorem contains no quantifier over the full Fang--Ridder--Xie class.  The causal use of \(\texttt{thm:certified-calibration}\) is obtained by combining its Gaussian lower-CDF certificate with that path law, so this composition likewise supplies no positive conclusion outside \(\mathcal M(J,K,\mathcal H,\mathcal K_V)\).

We next verify the direction in which class transfer is valid.  For the binary witness used by \(\texttt{thm:no-uniform-atom-removed-modulus}\), take \(X\) uniform on \(\{1,2\}\).  Conditional on \(X=j\), take \(A\) independent of the potential-outcome noises with
\[
 P(A=0\mid X=j)=P(A=1\mid X=j)=\frac12.
\]
Let \(\epsilon_0\) and \(\epsilon_1\) be independent symmetric random variables supported on \(\{-1/\sqrt8,1/\sqrt8\}\), and, conditional on \(X=j\), set
\[
 Y(0)=\epsilon_0,
 \qquad
 Y(1)=\frac{h_j}{\sqrt n}+\epsilon_1,
 \qquad
 Y=Y(A).
\]
Independent copies across units satisfy \(\texttt{ass:iid-rows}\).  The last equality is \(\texttt{ass:consistency}\), while the stipulated independence gives \(Y^{\mathrm{pot}}\perp A\mid X\), which is \(\texttt{ass:randomization}\).  Moreover,
\[
 p_j=\frac12,
 \qquad
 e_{ja}=\frac12,
 \qquad
 \mu_{n,j1}-\mu_{n,j0}=\frac{h_j}{\sqrt n}.
\]
Thus the stratum and treatment probabilities are bounded away from zero, and the local contrast remainder is exactly zero.  Since \(h_j\in[1,5/4]\),
\[
 \max_{a\in\{0,1\}}|Y(a)|
 \leq \frac54+\frac1{\sqrt8}<2,
\]
so the potential outcomes are uniformly bounded with \(B_Y=2\); likewise one may take \(p_{\min}=e_{\min}=1/2\).  The common benchmark \(b=(64/65,1/65)\) has
\[
 \min_{a\in\{0,1\}}b_a=\frac1{65}=\beta>0.
\]
These equations verify, one by one, randomization, overlap, boundedness, and positive benchmark support, which are the Fang--Ridder--Xie hypotheses at issue.

For completeness, the contrast-score covariance also equals the covariance specified by the witness.  With action \(0\) as reference, the score in stratum \(j\) is
\[
 \xi_{n,j}
 =4\mathbf 1\{X=j\}
 \left[\mathbf 1\{A=1\}\epsilon_1-
       \mathbf 1\{A=0\}\epsilon_0\right].
\]
It has mean zero, and independence together with the displayed probabilities gives
\[
 E(\xi_{n,j}^2)
 =16\cdot\frac12
 \left(\frac12\cdot\frac18+\frac12\cdot\frac18\right)=1.
\]
For \(j\neq k\), \(\mathbf 1\{X=j\}\mathbf 1\{X=k\}=0\), hence \(E(\xi_{n,j}\xi_{n,k})=0\).  Therefore \(V_n=I_2\in\mathcal K_V\) upon taking \(v_{\min}=v_{\max}=1\).  This also verifies directly that the fixed witness is in the note's smaller model and, because it has the four published properties just checked, in the broader Fang--Ridder--Xie class.

Now suppose, toward a contradiction, that the broader class admitted a uniform atom-removed modulus \(\widetilde A\) with \(\widetilde A(x)\downarrow0\).  Uniformity would apply after restriction to the preceding fixed binary design.  But \(\texttt{thm:no-uniform-atom-removed-modulus}\) supplies \(\eta>0\), \(h_m\in[1,5/4]^2\), \(q_m\in\mathbb R\), and \(x_m\downarrow0\) such that
\[
 P\{q_m<S(h_m,Z_{I_2})\leq q_m+x_m\}\geq\eta
 \qquad\text{for every }m.
\]
The asserted modulus would give
\[
 \eta
 \leq P\{q_m<S(h_m,Z_{I_2})\leq q_m+x_m\}
 \leq \widetilde A(x_m)
 \longrightarrow0,
\]
a contradiction.  Any class-uniform probability-to-critical-value argument based on such a modulus would, by its premise, provide exactly this forbidden uniform bound and therefore fails by the same restriction argument.  In contrast, \(\texttt{thm:certified-calibration}\) verifies a one-sided lower-CDF inequality at its returned rational critical value and explicitly permits atoms; it never converts probability error into a two-sided value width.  Its one-sided conclusion is therefore unaffected.  This proves the asymmetric transfer.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The corollary makes the class relation exact: positive achievability remains on the fixed finite-cell randomized subclass, whereas a counterexample in that subclass transfers the impossibility to every broader class containing it. \textit{Gap.} The witness theorem alone does not state how its negative conclusion compares with the broader Fang--Ridder--Xie class, and broadening a negative result must not be confused with broadening the positive path law. \textit{Consumer.} Readers and implementers can distinguish the finite-cell domain of the certified procedure from the broader domain on which modulus-based two-sided critical-value conversion is impossible.

\section{Interpretation}

The positive output is deliberately one-sided and subclass-specific: for the declared fixed finite-cell randomized model, it certifies that the entire normalized oracle-regret path lies below one auditable critical value, so any temperature selected measurably from observed data inherits the same guarantee. No such positive path law or causal calibration claim is made for the full Fang--Ridder--Xie class. The negative conclusion does extend: nearby local states in a fixed binary design belonging to that broader class place positive hard-cell atoms arbitrarily close together. Thus moving to the broader class cannot create a uniform atom-removed modulus or a modulus-based uniform conversion from probability error to critical-value error.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The one-sided algorithm terminates and certifies coverage on its declared finite-cell inputs because work-capped refinement has an analytic Gaussian-tail fallback. It need not return a critical value within a prescribed numerical distance of \(Q_{1-\alpha}^{\mathrm{LF}}\). The fixed two-stratum binary witness shows that no uniform atom-removed modulus follows even over the broader Fang--Ridder--Xie class: distinct positive hard-cell atoms can collide while retaining positive mass. Thus a modulus-based two-sided value-width certificate would need additional atom separation, a restricted local-state set, or another explicit structural condition. This is an upward transfer only for the obstruction; it does not broaden the positive approximation or calibration theorem. The rational fallback \(2B_\gamma^++(B_\gamma^+)^2/2\) can be conservative.

\section{Honest scope}

The positive path-law and causal calibration results are uniform only for fixed \(J\) and \(K\), positive benchmark probabilities, positive stratum and treatment probabilities, bounded outcomes, compact local contrasts, and covariance spectra bounded away from zero. They exclude boundary benchmarks, singular covariance, growing action or stratum counts, disappearing cells, and continuous covariates without a prespecified finite stratification. They are not claimed over the full Fang--Ridder--Xie class. The commitment-savings display is an illustration conditional on prespecified cells. Temperature selection is restricted to \(\mathcal F_n^{\mathrm{obs}}\)-measurable rules, including rules based on the displayed plug-in frontier; that frontier is not claimed to estimate the unobserved oracle regret path. The negative scope is broader: the fixed binary witness already satisfies the Fang--Ridder--Xie randomization, overlap, boundedness, and positive-benchmark hypotheses, so the no-modulus result and every modulus-based probability-to-critical-value obstruction transfer upward. No non-modulus impossibility theorem is claimed.
