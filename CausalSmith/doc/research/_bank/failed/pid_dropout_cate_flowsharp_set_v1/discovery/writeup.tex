\section*{pid\_\allowbreak{}dropout\_\allowbreak{}cate\_\allowbreak{}flowsharp\_\allowbreak{}set}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} Informative dropout leaves an unknown residual restricted-survival contribution along the predeclared prognostic score. The greatest feasible monotone \(L\)-Lipschitz residual below a capacity \(u\) is the explicit envelope \(\Gamma_Lu\), which gives the exact pointwise projection of the law-sharp compact CATE-curve set. Joint curve coupling is nontrivial: the maintained set is strictly smaller than the Wang endpoint-selection box exactly when some arm-profile dropout capacity is nonzero. Support values separate into certified path-flow obstacle programs, and eroded grids have a uniform Hausdorff remainder even at zero-capacity contacts. For inference, a direct positive-part stability argument turns an externally certified nuisance band into an observable clipped compact convex whole-set superset with directed excess \(8r_n+2(Lh_n+Mh_n^\beta)\), without face differentiation or a direction net. Combining the density band with a finite-multinomial profile-mass region gives conservative simultaneous intervals for every eligible leaf and arbitrary post-selection validity. Efficient local-mass \((np_{\min,n})^{-1/2}\) calibration remains open because the declared score assumptions contain no score-to-statistic expansion or multiplier coupling.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Wang et al. prove covariatewise outer endpoint validity under their treatment-exchangeability, overlap, and consistency conditions, but do not characterize the jointly attainable curve image, its exact contraction under score-shape restrictions, or an estimated-obstacle confidence set that remains valid when capacity contacts change. Generic support-function and directional-bootstrap results do not supply the missing score-to-statistic expansion for the present obstacle program.

\paragraph{Niche.} The missing object is simultaneously causal and functional: a law-sharp compact set of whole CATE curves, an exact published-class benchmark modulo \(P_X\)-null sets, a finite flow certificate, and an observable confidence superset whose validity does not depend on stable active faces.

\paragraph{Fill.} Completion witnesses establish exact population identification in the monotone-Lipschitz class and transfer converse attainability from the continuous witness subclass to the full Wang-compatible class. The envelope, strictness, support-flow, grid-Hausdorff, and stability results characterize and compute the population set. On the externally certified band-valid subclass, the robust union of enlarged gridded sets proves whole-set coverage and shrinking directed excess; a finite-multinomial baseline-law region yields valid but conservative simultaneous leaf projections. Only the sharper derivative-aware local-mass multiplier rate is left unresolved.

\section{Related work}

Wang et al. (2025, Assumption 3.1, Theorem 5.1, equations (8)--(9), and Appendix D.2) provide treatment-exchangeability, overlap, consistency, and conservative arm and CATE endpoint results. This note first states their compatible-law class explicitly, proves their endpoint-selection box is the full measurable image modulo \(P_X\)-null sets by transferring completion witnesses from a smaller continuous subclass, and then shows that its continuous slice is \(\mathcal B(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\). The maintained monotone-Lipschitz model is a distinct sensitivity class: its greatest-obstacle envelope gives exact pointwise projections, and its joint curve image is strictly smaller than the Wang box if and only if at least one capacity is nonzero. Beresteanu, Molchanov, and Molinari inform the support-function view of set identification; Kim informs primal--dual certification; and Chiang et al. inform finite linear encodings of shape restrictions. None supplies the informative-dropout completion or the exact obstacle envelope proved here. Firpo, Galvao, and Parker, Fang and Santos, and Semenova motivate derivative-aware inference for optimized maps, but their results do not create a score-to-statistic expansion for an estimated infinite-dimensional obstacle with changing contacts. The present whole-set theorem instead uses a direct Hausdorff contraction and an external nuisance band, so it needs neither face differentiation nor a direction net. Wei et al. motivate simultaneous subgroup inference for a supplied family of point-identified effects; here density-band and finite-multinomial uncertainty are propagated through a partially identified curve set to give conservative simultaneous and post-selection leaf intervals. The efficient local-mass multiplier refinement remains a separate open problem.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:iid-sampling]\label{ass:iid-sampling}
\(O_1,\ldots,O_n\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}P_{\mathrm{obs}}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Independent trial sampling, \cite{WangFrauenSchweisthalSchroderFeuerriegel2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:consistency]\label{ass:consistency}
\(P\{(T,C)=(T(A),C(A))\}=1\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Potential-outcome consistency, \cite{WangFrauenSchweisthalSchroderFeuerriegel2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:randomization]\label{ass:randomization}
\(A\perp\!\!\!\perp (T(0),T(1),C(0),C(1))\mid X\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This maintained full-design condition is satisfied by trials that draw treatment from the known allocation rule using baseline \(X\) before either potential event or potential censoring time is realized. Complete, block, and stratified randomization based only on \(X\) are concrete admissible designs.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:treatment-overlap]\label{ass:treatment-overlap}
\(\pi_a(x)\ge\varepsilon\quad\forall(a,x)\in\mathcal A\times\mathcal X\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Treatment overlap, \cite{WangFrauenSchweisthalSchroderFeuerriegel2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:bounded-event-time]\label{ass:bounded-event-time}
\(P\{0\le T(a)\le t_{\max}\}=1\quad\forall a\in\mathcal A\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Bounded survival horizon, \cite{WangFrauenSchweisthalSchroderFeuerriegel2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:censoring-overlap]\label{ass:censoring-overlap}
\(\inf_{a\in\mathcal A,\,z\in\mathcal Z}\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{r\sim P_{\mathrm{obs}}(R\mid A=a,Z=z)}P_{\mathrm{obs}}(\Delta=0\mid A=a,Z=z,R=r)\ge\kappa\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uncensored overlap, \cite{WangFrauenSchweisthalSchroderFeuerriegel2025}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:profile-mass]\label{ass:profile-mass}
\(\min_{z\in\mathcal Z}P_{\mathrm{obs}}(Z=z)\ge\omega\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Finite-stratum positivity, \cite{WeiPetersenvanderLaanEtAl2023}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:score-density]\label{ass:score-density}
\(0<c_p\le\inf_{z\in\mathcal Z,\,r\in[0,1]}p_z(r)\le\sup_{z\in\mathcal Z,\,r\in[0,1]}p_z(r)\le C_p<\infty\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This explicit regularity condition requires the prespecified score to retain observations throughout its normalized range within every genomic profile. It can be checked before outcome analysis by profile-specific score histograms and density bands; profiles with sparse boundary support are coarsened or the analysis is restricted to a prespecified common-support interval.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:holder-observed-law]\label{ass:holder-observed-law}
\(\max_{a\in\mathcal A,\,z\in\mathcal Z}\{\|b_{a,z}\|_{\mathcal H^\beta},\|u_{a,z}\|_{\mathcal H^\beta}\}\vee\max_{z\in\mathcal Z}\|p_z\|_{\mathcal H^\beta}\le M\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This explicit regularity condition is plausible for a continuously scaled prognostic score when conditional follow-up, dropout capacity, and score density have no abrupt clinical threshold. The exponent \(\beta\in(0,1]\) is treated as known and fixed before inference, chosen from prespecified smoothness classes supported by external or pilot-only diagnostics; sensitivity is reported over a small prespecified set of candidate exponents.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:residual-continuity]\label{ass:residual-continuity}
\(s_{a,z}\in C([0,1])\quad\forall a\in\mathcal A,\ z\in\mathcal Z\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This mild continuity-only benchmark rules out jumps in mean hidden restricted-survival time along a continuously scaled predeclared prognosis score, without imposing a direction or a finite slope bound. It is assessed by varying score binning in an external near-complete-follow-up cohort and is used only to define the continuous-function comparator class.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:residual-monotonicity]\label{ass:residual-monotonicity}
With \(R\) oriented before analysis so that larger scores indicate weakly longer event-free prognosis, \(s_{a,z}(r)\le s_{a,z}(r')\quad\forall a\in\mathcal A,\ z\in\mathcal Z,\ 0\le r\le r'\le1\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This identifying restriction says that, within a genomic profile and treatment arm, patients with a better predeclared prognosis cannot have a smaller mean amount of event-free time hidden by dropout. Its orientation is fixed without using outcomes. A validation and sensitivity protocol compares monotone trends of completed-minus-observed restricted survival in an external near-complete-follow-up cohort, then reports the identified set both with this sign restriction and with monotonicity removed.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:residual-lipschitz]\label{ass:residual-lipschitz}
\(|s_{a,z}(r)-s_{a,z}(r')|\le L|r-r'|\quad\forall a\in\mathcal A,\ z\in\mathcal Z,\ r,r'\in[0,1]\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This identifying sensitivity restriction caps how quickly hidden event-free time can change along the prespecified score. Clinicians can elicit a maximum change \(d\) in hidden restricted-survival time over a score increment \(\delta_r\), set \(L=d/\delta_r\), compare that calibration with an external near-complete-follow-up cohort, and report a curve of conclusions over a prespecified grid of \(L\), including the residual-continuity-only benchmark.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:vc-leaves]\label{ass:vc-leaves}
\(\operatorname{VC}(\mathcal G)\le V_c\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Finite VC set class, \cite{vanDerVaartWellner1996}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:external-band-prerequisite]\label{ass:external-band-prerequisite}
\(\operatorname{Cert}_n(P;r_n)=1\Longrightarrow P\{\eta(P)\in\widehat{\mathcal B}_{\eta,n},\ \operatorname{diam}_\infty(\widehat{\mathcal B}_{\eta,n})\le2r_n\}\ge1-\alpha/4-o(1)\quad\forall P\in\mathfrak P\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This is an external high-level band prerequisite, not a consequence of the randomized-design model or the stated Hölder restriction. It is operational when an independent calibration sample, a pilot-only procedure, or a separately proved honest-band method certifies the displayed property; all inferential claims are restricted to the certified subclass.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:external-band-radius-vanishes]\label{ass:external-band-radius-vanishes}
\(r_n\to0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Vanishing of the abstract external band radius is imposed only on the band-valid triangular-array subclass. It is plausible when the prespecified score is one-dimensional and an external or pilot-only band construction is available, but it is not asserted to follow from the maintained law class.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:score-envelope]\label{ass:score-envelope}
\(|\psi(O)|\le F_\psi(O)\quad\forall\psi\in\Psi_n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Measurable empirical-process envelope, \cite{vanDerVaartWellner1996}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:score-moment]\label{ass:score-moment}
\(\sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_n^{\mathrm{band}}(r_n)}E_P[F_\psi(O)^{2+\delta_\psi}]\le C_\psi\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform envelope moment condition, \cite{vanDerVaartWellner1996}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:score-entropy]\label{ass:score-entropy}
\(\sup_{P\in\mathfrak P_n^{\mathrm{band}}(r_n)}\log N(t\|F_\psi\|_{P,2},\Psi_n,L^2(P))\le\log|\mathcal D_n|+C_\psi V_c\log(C_\psi/t)\quad\forall t\in(0,1)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (VC-type uniform entropy bound, \cite{vanDerVaartWellner1996}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:eligible-mass-rate]\label{ass:eligible-mass-rate}
\((V_c\log n+\log|\mathcal D_n|+\log(1/\alpha))/(np_{\min,n})\to0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (VC minimum-mass condition, \cite{vanDerVaartWellner1996}).

\left\{\gamma\in[0,1]^{|\mathcal Z|}:\sum_z\gamma_z=1,
\ \sum_z|\gamma_z-\widehat\gamma_z|\leq\varepsilon_{Z,n}\right\}
\]
and the preliminary baseline-law region
\[
\widehat{\mathcal P}^{\,0}_{X,n}
=\left\{Q:Q(\{z\}\times dr)=\gamma_zp'_z(r)\,dr
\text{ for some }(b',u',p')\in\widehat{\mathcal E}_n,
\ \gamma\in\widehat\Gamma_n\right\}.
\]
Set \(\widehat{\mathcal P}_{X,n}=\widehat{\mathcal P}^{\,0}_{X,n}\) when this set is nonempty, and otherwise use the observable fallback singleton containing \(Q^0(\{z\}\times dr)=|\mathcal Z|^{-1}dr\).

Let
\[
\mathcal K_{\mathsf R,M,L,\beta}
=\left\{g\in\mathbb C_{\mathcal Z}:\|g\|_\infty\leq\mathsf R,
\ |g_z(r)-g_z(r')|
\leq2M|r-r'|^\beta+2L|r-r'|\ \forall z,r,r'\right\}.
\]
For \((b',u',p')\in\widehat{\mathcal E}_n\), let \(\mathcal T_{h_n}(b',u')\) denote the gridded-curve construction evaluated at \((b',u')\).  Define
\[
\widehat H_n=
\overline{\operatorname{co}}\!\left(
\bigcup_{(b',u',p')\in\widehat{\mathcal E}_n}
\left\{g+e:g\in\mathcal T_{h_n}(b',u'),\ e\in\mathbb C_{\mathcal Z},\
\|e\|_\infty\leq e_{h_n}\right\}\right)

\]
The band-robust whole-set confidence set is \(\widehat{\mathcal T}_{n,h_n}^{+}=\widehat H_n\) when \(\widehat H_n\neq\varnothing\), and is the fallback singleton \(\{0\}\) otherwise.  The construction is a sample-indexed set-valued object; all coverage claims below are understood in outer probability, avoiding an undeclared measurable-maximum claim for the uncountable band union. Its ingredients use only the observed sample, the externally supplied nuisance band, and predeclared constants; it does not differentiate or hold fixed an obstacle contact face.

\begin{lemma}[lem:completion-witness]\label{lem:completion-witness}
For every family \(f=(f_{a,z})\) with \(f_{a,z}\in\mathcal S_{a,z}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\), the constructed law \(P^f\) belongs to \(\mathcal F(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\), has residual contribution \(s_{a,z}^{P^f}=f_{a,z}\), and preserves the observed censoring law. On \(\{u_{a,z}>0\}\), attainability follows from \(E[(T(a)-\widetilde T)\Delta\mid a,z,r]=q_{a,z}(r)u_{a,z}(r)=f_{a,z}(r)\); on \(\{u_{a,z}=0\}\), feasibility forces \(f_{a,z}=0\), and the tie convention keeps the completion legal.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a feasible family \(f=(f_{a,z})\).  By the obstacle-set definition,
\[
0\leq f_{a,z}(r)\leq u_{a,z}(r)\qquad(a\in\mathcal A,\ z\in\mathcal Z,\ r\in[0,1]).
\]
Consequently the completion probability in the armwise completion definition is well defined and satisfies
\[
q_{a,z}(r)=\frac{f_{a,z}(r)}{u_{a,z}(r)}\in[0,1]
\quad\hbox{if }u_{a,z}(r)>0.
\]
If \(u_{a,z}(r)=0\), the same obstacle inequality forces \(f_{a,z}(r)=0\), and the stipulated value \(q_{a,z}(r)=0\) again gives
\[
q_{a,z}(r)u_{a,z}(r)=f_{a,z}(r).                                      \qquad\text{(1)}
\]
The piecewise ratio is Borel measurable because \(f_{a,z}\) and \(u_{a,z}\) are continuous and \(\{u_{a,z}>0\}\) is open.

We next verify the observation map event by event.  Write \((\widetilde T_a,\Delta_a)\) for the arm-\(a\) draw in the construction.  When \(\Delta_a=0\), the construction gives
\[
T(a)=\widetilde T_a\leq t_{\max}<t_{\max}+1=C(a),
\]
so
\[
\min\{T(a),C(a)\}=\widetilde T_a,
\qquad \mathbf 1\{C(a)\leq T(a)\}=0=\Delta_a.                         \qquad\text{(2)}
\]
When \(\Delta_a=1\), it gives \(C(a)=\widetilde T_a\) and \(T(a)\in\{\widetilde T_a,t_{\max}\}\).  Since \(0\leq\widetilde T_a\leq t_{\max}\),
\[
\min\{T(a),C(a)\}=\widetilde T_a,
\qquad \mathbf 1\{C(a)\leq T(a)\}=1=\Delta_a.                         \qquad\text{(3)}
\]
The weak inequality in (3) is precisely where the stipulated tie-as-dropout convention is used.  Equations (2)--(3), the observed marginal law of \(X\) used by the completion, the observed conditional kernel of \((\widetilde T_a,\Delta_a)\) given \(A=a,X\), and the protocol probabilities \(\pi_a(X)\) show that the observation map under \(P^f\) has law \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\).  In particular, the law of the observed censoring record \((\widetilde T,\Delta)\), conditionally and marginally, is preserved.

The Bernoulli completion coin is conditionally independent of \((\widetilde T_a,\Delta_a)\) given \(X=(z,r)\), and its success probability is \(q_{a,z}(r)\).  Treatment is drawn only after the two potential arm records and independently of them given \(X\).  Therefore, using randomization and the dropout-capacity definition,
\[
\begin{aligned}
&E_{P^f}[(T(a)-\widetilde T)\Delta\mid A=a,Z=z,R=r]\\
&\quad=q_{a,z}(r)
E_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}}[(t_{\max}-\widetilde T)\Delta
 \mid A=a,Z=z,R=r]\\
&\quad=q_{a,z}(r)u_{a,z}(r)
=f_{a,z}(r),                                                            \qquad\text{(4)}
\end{aligned}
\]
where the final equality includes both the positive-capacity and zero-capacity cases by (1).  Thus the residual-contribution definition gives \(s^{P^f}_{a,z}=f_{a,z}\).

It remains to check membership rather than merely observational equivalence.  Defining realized \((T,C)=(T(A),C(A))\) gives consistency.  Drawing \(A\) independently of all potential arm records conditional on \(X\) gives the stated randomization property.  Equations (2)--(3) and the support of the observed record give \(0\leq T(a)\leq t_{\max}\).  All treatment-overlap, censoring-overlap, profile-mass, score-density, and observed-law H\"older conditions are inherited from the unchanged observed law.  Finally, (4) identifies the residual functions with \(f_{a,z}\), which are nonnegative, nondecreasing, and \(L\)-Lipschitz by feasibility.  These are exactly the membership conditions in the compatible-law definition.  Hence \(P^f\in\mathcal F(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\), with the claimed residual contribution and preserved observed censoring law.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the attainability step that turns valid inequalities into law sharpness, including zero-capacity contacts. \textit{Gap.} Wang et al. prove endpoint validity in Theorem 5.1 and Appendix D.2 but do not construct one full-data completion attaining an entire shape-coupled curve. \textit{Consumer.} It lets trial analysts interpret every reported curve in the set as arising from an observationally equivalent informative-dropout law.

\begin{theorem}[thm:law-sharp-curve-set]\label{thm:law-sharp-curve-set}
The identified set of profile-specific CATE curves is exactly \(\{\tau^P:P\in\mathcal F(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\}=\mathcal T(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\). The set \(\mathcal T(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\) is nonempty, compact, and convex in \(\mathbb C_{\mathcal Z}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Define the causal identified set independently of the observed-law functional by
\[
\Theta_I(P_{\mathrm{obs}})
:=\{\tau^P:P\in\mathcal F(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\}
\subset\mathbb C_{\mathcal Z}.
\tag{1}
\]
Fix \(P\in\mathcal F(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\).  On \(\{A=a\}\), consistency and the observation definitions imply
\[
T(a)=\widetilde T+(T(a)-\widetilde T)\Delta.
\tag{2}
\]
If \(\Delta=0\), then \(C(a)>T(a)\) and \(\widetilde T=T(a)\); if \(\Delta=1\), (2) is tautological.  Conditional randomization and treatment overlap give
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_P[T(a)\mid Z=z,R=r]
&=E_P[T(a)\mid A=a,Z=z,R=r]\\
&=b_{a,z}(r)+s^P_{a,z}(r).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
The second equality uses (2), compatibility, and the definitions of \(b\) and \(s\).  Subtracting arms yields
\[
\tau_z^P(r)
=b_{1,z}(r)-b_{0,z}(r)+s^P_{1,z}(r)-s^P_{0,z}(r).
\tag{4}
\]
Bounded event time gives
\[
0\leq(T(a)-\widetilde T)\Delta
\leq(t_{\max}-\widetilde T)\Delta.
\tag{5}
\]
Taking conditional expectations gives
\[
0\leq s^P_{a,z}(r)\leq u_{a,z}(r)
\tag{6}
\]
for the conditional score law almost every \(r\).  The model restrictions make \(s^P_{a,z}\) \(L\)-Lipschitz and \(u_{a,z}\) H\"older continuous.  Profile mass makes the arm-profile conditional law well defined; Bayes' rule, treatment overlap, and the density bound give
\[
\frac{dP_{\mathrm{obs}}(R\in dr\mid A=a,Z=z)}{dr}
=\frac{\pi_a(z,r)p_z(r)}
{P_{\mathrm{obs}}(A=a\mid Z=z)}
\geq\varepsilon c_p>0.
\tag{7}
\]
The denominator is positive and at most one.  A pointwise violation of (6) would persist on a relative interval by continuity, contradicting (7) and almost-sure validity.  Thus (6) holds everywhere.  Residual monotonicity and residual Lipschitz continuity yield
\[
s^P_{a,z}\in\mathcal S_{a,z}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})
\qquad\text{for every }a,z.
\tag{8}
\]
Equations (4) and (8) prove
\[
\Theta_I(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\subseteq\mathcal T(P_{\mathrm{obs}}).
\tag{9}
\]

Conversely, take
\[
g=(b_{1,z}-b_{0,z}+f_{1,z}-f_{0,z})_{z\in\mathcal Z}
\in\mathcal T(P_{\mathrm{obs}}).
\]
The established completion-witness lemma supplies one \(P^f\in\mathcal F(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\) satisfying \(s^{P^f}_{a,z}=f_{a,z}\) simultaneously.  Equation (4) gives \(\tau^{P^f}=g\), so
\[
\mathcal T(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\subseteq\Theta_I(P_{\mathrm{obs}}).
\tag{10}
\]
This proves equality and law sharpness.

For geometry, the zero function belongs to every obstacle set.  Its interval, monotonicity, and Lipschitz inequalities are preserved by convex combinations and uniform limits, so each obstacle set is nonempty, convex, and closed.  Every member obeys
\[
\|f\|_\infty\leq\|u_{a,z}\|_\infty,\qquad
|f(r)-f(r')|\leq L|r-r'|.
\tag{11}
\]
For completeness, take any sequence in one obstacle set, extract a diagonal subsequence converging on a countable dense set, and choose, for each \(\epsilon>0\), a finite dense-set net of mesh below \(\epsilon/(3L)\).  Convergence at its finitely many points and (11) imply, for sufficiently large \(k,\ell\),
\[
\sup_{r\in[0,1]}|f_k(r)-f_\ell(r)|
\leq2L\frac{\epsilon}{3L}+\frac{\epsilon}{3}
=\epsilon.
\tag{12}
\]
The subsequence is uniformly Cauchy, has a continuous uniform limit, and closedness puts that limit in the obstacle set.  Thus the obstacle set is compact in the metric sup-norm topology.

There are \(2|\mathcal Z|\) obstacle factors, so their product is nonempty, compact, and convex.  The affine map
\[
(f_{a,z})_{a,z}\longmapsto
(b_{1,z}-b_{0,z}+f_{1,z}-f_{0,z})_{z\in\mathcal Z}
\tag{13}
\]
is continuous, because the image distance is at most twice the largest coordinatewise input distance.  Its image is \(\mathcal T(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\).  Continuous affine images preserve nonemptiness, compactness, and convexity, completing the proof.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem replaces unrelated pointwise intervals by the joint law-sharp function-valued causal object. \textit{Gap.} Beresteanu, Molchanov, and Molinari characterize random-set expectations abstractly, while Wang et al. give only covariatewise survival-CATE endpoints; neither identifies this dropout obstacle image. \textit{Consumer.} The sharp compact set is the target needed for honest sign statements along a prognostic score and across genomic profiles.

\begin{theorem}[thm:support-flow-certificate]\label{thm:support-flow-certificate}
For every \(v\in\mathbb B_*\), \(h_{\mathcal T}(v)=\sum_z\int(b_{1,z}-b_{0,z})\,dv_z+\sum_z\{V_{1,z}(v_z;u)+V_{0,z}(-v_z;u)\}\). For the grid problem, writing \(w_j=\int\phi_{j,h}\,dv_z\) and \(\bar u_j=[u_{a,z}(r_j)-Mh^\beta]_+\), strong duality gives \(\sup_{x:0\le x_j\le\bar u_j,\,0\le x_{j+1}-x_j\le Lh}\sum_jw_jx_j=\min_{d_h=(\lambda^+,\lambda^-,g,d)\in\mathcal D_h(w,\bar u)}\{\sum_j\bar u_j\lambda_j^+ +Lh\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}g_j\}\). The primal and dual are a path min-cost transshipment pair, and their equality is a finite certificate for each support direction.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(v\in\mathbb B_*\).  All the integrals below are finite: each
\(v_z\) has finite total variation, while the functions \(b_{a,z}\) and
every member of \(\mathcal S_{a,z}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\) are continuous on
the compact interval \([0,1]\).  By the definitions of the sharp curve
set and its support function,
\[
\begin{aligned}
h_{\mathcal T}(v)
&=\sup_{\substack{f_{a,z}\in\mathcal S_{a,z}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\\
                   a\in\{0,1\},\ z\in\mathcal Z}}
 \sum_{z\in\mathcal Z}\int
 \{b_{1,z}-b_{0,z}+f_{1,z}-f_{0,z}\}\,dv_z \\
&=\sum_{z\in\mathcal Z}\int(b_{1,z}-b_{0,z})\,dv_z
  +\sup_{(f_{a,z})}\sum_{z\in\mathcal Z}
       \left\{\int f_{1,z}\,dv_z+\int f_{0,z}\,d(-v_z)\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The feasible family is a finite Cartesian product of nonempty sets, because
the zero function belongs to every obstacle set.  Consequently the last
supremum is the sum of the coordinatewise suprema: the inequality from left
to right is immediate, and the reverse inequality follows by choosing, for
each of the finitely many coordinates, a feasible function within
\(\epsilon/(2|\mathcal Z|)\) of its supremum and then sending
\(\epsilon\downarrow0\).  The definition of \(V_{a,z}\) therefore gives
\[
h_{\mathcal T}(v)
=\sum_z\int(b_{1,z}-b_{0,z})\,dv_z
 +\sum_z\{V_{1,z}(v_z;u)+V_{0,z}(-v_z;u)\}.
\]
This proves the continuous support decomposition.

Fix now one arm-profile pair \((a,z)\), put
\(w_j=\int\phi_{j,h}\,dv_z\), and put
\(\bar u_j=[u_{a,z}(r_j)-Mh^\beta]_+\).  Linearity of integration and the
definition of \(\Phi_h\) yield
\[
 \int\Phi_hx\,dv_z=\sum_{j=0}^m w_jx_j.
\]
Thus the grid support problem is the finite linear program
\[
 p^*=\max_x\left\{\sum_{j=0}^m w_jx_j:
 0\le x_j\le\bar u_j,\quad
 0\le x_{j+1}-x_j\le Lh\ (0\le j<m)\right\}.
\]
Its feasible polytope is nonempty, since it contains \(x=0\), and compact,
since \(0\le x_j\le\bar u_j<\infty\).  Hence it has a maximizer \(x^*\).

For completeness, we derive the specialized dual equality.  Write the four
families of inequalities as
\[
 x_j-\bar u_j\le0,\qquad -x_j\le0,\qquad
 x_{j+1}-x_j-Lh\le0,\qquad x_j-x_{j+1}\le0.
\]
At \(x^*\), the vector \(w=(w_0,\ldots,w_m)\) is a nonnegative linear
combination of the normals of the active inequalities.  Indeed, if it were
not, finite-dimensional separation from the finitely generated cone of
active normals would give a vector \(y\) whose inner product with every
active normal is nonpositive but with \(w^Ty>0\).  Every inactive inequality
has strictly positive slack, so \(x^*+ty\) would remain feasible for all
sufficiently small \(t>0\), contradicting
\(w^T(x^*+ty)>w^Tx^*\).  Denote the resulting nonnegative coefficients by
\(\lambda_j^+,\lambda_j^-,g_j,d_j\), in the order displayed above, and
extend the arc variables by
\(g_{-1}=g_m=d_{-1}=d_m=0\).  Equating the coefficient of \(x_j\) gives
\[
 \lambda_j^+-\lambda_j^-+g_{j-1}-g_j+d_j-d_{j-1}=w_j,
 \qquad j=0,\ldots,m.
\]
Hence \(d_h=(\lambda^+,\lambda^-,g,d)\) belongs to
\(\mathcal D_h(w,\bar u)\).  Because a multiplier can be positive only for
an active constraint,
\[
\begin{aligned}
p^*=w^Tx^*
 &=\sum_{j=0}^m\bar u_j\lambda_j^+
   +Lh\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}g_j.
\end{aligned}
\]
Conversely, for every primal-feasible \(x\) and every
\(d_h\in\mathcal D_h(w,\bar u)\), the balance equations imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_jw_jx_j
&=\sum_j(\lambda_j^+-\lambda_j^-)x_j
 +\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}g_j(x_{j+1}-x_j)
 +\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}d_j(x_j-x_{j+1})\\
&\le \sum_j\bar u_j\lambda_j^+
       +Lh\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}g_j,
\end{aligned}
\]
using respectively \(x_j\le\bar u_j\), \(x_j\ge0\),
\(x_{j+1}-x_j\le Lh\), and \(x_{j+1}-x_j\ge0\).  This is weak duality;
the multipliers constructed at \(x^*\) attain equality, proving the displayed
minimum formula.

The same equations are node-balance equations on the path
\(0,1,\ldots,m\): \(g_j\) and \(d_j\) are the two oriented arc flows, while
\(\lambda_j^+\) and \(\lambda_j^-\) are nonnegative node transactions.
The only nonzero arc capacity cost is \(Lh\sum_jg_j\), and the upper-obstacle
transactions cost \(\sum_j\bar u_j\lambda_j^+\).  A feasible primal vector
and feasible dual flow with the common displayed objective are therefore a
finite, directly checkable min-cost-transshipment certificate.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} A directionwise support identity and primal-dual flow certificate make the infinite-dimensional set auditable rather than merely definitional. \textit{Gap.} Kim's Theorem 1 uses transport duality for fixed-marginal DTE bounds, and Chiang et al.'s Theorem 1 projects a sieve confidence region by generic linear programs; neither yields the dropout-capacity path dual. \textit{Consumer.} Software can return a primal curve and a dual certificate for every clinical leaf or plotted support direction.

\begin{theorem}[thm:grid-hausdorff]\label{thm:grid-hausdorff}
For every mesh \(h=1/m\), \(\mathcal T_h(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\subseteq\mathcal T(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\) and \(d_H^{\|\cdot\|_\infty}(\mathcal T_h(P_{\mathrm{obs}}),\mathcal T(P_{\mathrm{obs}}))\le2(Lh+Mh^\beta)\). The bound is uniform over \(P\in\mathfrak P\), including laws for which \(u_{a,z}\) has intervals or isolated points of zeros.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Set \(\delta_h=Mh^\beta\).  We first verify the inner inclusion one
arm-profile pair at a time.  Let \(\Phi_hx\in\mathcal S^h_{a,z}\).  On each
interval \([r_j,r_{j+1}]\), its slope is
\((x_{j+1}-x_j)/h\in[0,L]\).  Hence \(\Phi_hx\) is nonnegative,
nondecreasing, and \(L\)-Lipschitz.  If
\(r\in[r_j,r_{j+1}]\) and \(k\in\{j,j+1\}\), the H\"older condition on
\(u_{a,z}\) gives
\[
 u_{a,z}(r)\ge u_{a,z}(r_k)-M|r-r_k|^\beta
             \ge u_{a,z}(r_k)-\delta_h.
\]
Because \(u_{a,z}(r)\ge0\), this implies
\[
 x_k\le [u_{a,z}(r_k)-\delta_h]_+\le u_{a,z}(r).
\]
The interpolant is a convex combination of \(x_j\) and \(x_{j+1}\), so
\(\Phi_hx(r)\le u_{a,z}(r)\).  All defining obstacle inequalities therefore
hold, proving
\(\mathcal S^h_{a,z}\subseteq\mathcal S_{a,z}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\).
Taking the finite product over \((a,z)\) and then applying the affine map in
the definition of the curve sets proves
\[
 \mathcal T_h(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\subseteq\mathcal T(P_{\mathrm{obs}}).
\]

For the reverse approximation, fix
\(f\in\mathcal S_{a,z}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\) and define the nodal vector
\[
 x_j=[f(r_j)-\delta_h]_+,
 \qquad j=0,\ldots,m.
\]
Since \(f(r_j)\le u_{a,z}(r_j)\),
\(x_j\le[u_{a,z}(r_j)-\delta_h]_+\).  The map
\(q(t)=[t-\delta_h]_+\) is nondecreasing and one-Lipschitz.  The monotonicity
and Lipschitz restrictions on \(f\) therefore imply
\[
 0\le x_{j+1}-x_j
 \le f(r_{j+1})-f(r_j)
 \le Lh.
\]
Thus \(\Phi_hx\in\mathcal S^h_{a,z}\).  Moreover,
\(|f(r_j)-x_j|\le\delta_h\) at every node.  Writing
\(r=(1-t)r_j+tr_{j+1}\), \(0\le t\le1\), and using the
\(L\)-Lipschitz property twice gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|f(r)-\{(1-t)f(r_j)+tf(r_{j+1})\}\right|
&\le(1-t)Lth+tL(1-t)h\\
&=2t(1-t)Lh\le Lh.
\end{aligned}
\]
The nodal clipping contributes at most \(\delta_h\), whence
\[
 \|f-\Phi_hx\|_\infty\le Lh+Mh^\beta.
\]

Now take any
\(g=(b_{1,z}-b_{0,z}+f_{1,z}-f_{0,z})_z\in\mathcal T(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\).
Apply the preceding construction separately to every \(f_{a,z}\), obtaining
\(f^h_{a,z}\in\mathcal S^h_{a,z}\), and define
\(g^h=(b_{1,z}-b_{0,z}+f^h_{1,z}-f^h_{0,z})_z\).  Then
\(g^h\in\mathcal T_h(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\) and
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|g-g^h\|_\infty
&\le\max_z\left(
 \|f_{1,z}-f^h_{1,z}\|_\infty+
 \|f_{0,z}-f^h_{0,z}\|_\infty\right)\\
&\le2(Lh+Mh^\beta).
\end{aligned}
\]
The inner inclusion makes the directed distance from
\(\mathcal T_h(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\) to \(\mathcal T(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\)
zero, and the last display bounds the opposite directed distance.  Their
maximum is the asserted Hausdorff bound.  Every inequality used only the
common constants \(L,M,\beta\), so the bound is uniform over
\(P\in\mathfrak P\).  The proof never divides by \(u_{a,z}\) and the clipping
map is valid when its argument is zero; hence intervals and isolated points
on which \(u_{a,z}=0\) require no separate positivity condition.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Two-sided Hausdorff control converts a numerical grid into a certified approximation to the entire sharp set. \textit{Gap.} Chiang et al. control sieve approximation inside confidence inequalities, but do not prove two-sided sup-norm Hausdorff approximation for a changing obstacle capacity. \textit{Consumer.} An analyst can choose \(h\) to guarantee that computational error is smaller than a prespecified clinical effect margin.

\begin{lemma}[lem:continuous-box-completion]\label{lem:continuous-box-completion}
For every \(g\in\mathcal B(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\), the constructed law \(P^g_{\mathrm{cont}}\) belongs to \(\mathcal F_{\mathrm{cont}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\), preserves the observed law, and satisfies \(\tau^{P^g_{\mathrm{cont}}}=g\). In particular, \(f^g_{1,z}=[g_z-b_{1,z}+b_{0,z}]_+\) and \(f^g_{0,z}=[-g_z+b_{1,z}-b_{0,z}]_+\) are continuous, obey \(0\le f^g_{a,z}\le u_{a,z}\), and the ratio completion is legal on both \(\{u_{a,z}>0\}\) and \(\{u_{a,z}=0\}\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(g\in\mathcal B(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\) and put
\[
d_z(r):=g_z(r)-b_{1,z}(r)+b_{0,z}(r).
\]
The pointwise-image definition is equivalent to
\[
-u_{0,z}(r)\leq d_z(r)\leq u_{1,z}(r)\qquad(z\in\mathcal Z,\ r\in[0,1]). \qquad\text{(1)}
\]
The proposed residuals are the positive and negative parts
\[
f^g_{1,z}=d_z^+,
\qquad f^g_{0,z}=(-d_z)^+.
\]
Because \(g_z,b_{1,z},b_{0,z}\) are continuous and the positive-part map is continuous, both residuals are continuous.  From (1), considering separately \(d_z\geq0\) and \(d_z<0\),
\[
0\leq f^g_{1,z}(r)\leq u_{1,z}(r),
\qquad
0\leq f^g_{0,z}(r)\leq u_{0,z}(r).                                    \qquad\text{(2)}
\]
Also,
\[
f^g_{1,z}(r)-f^g_{0,z}(r)=d_z(r).                                      \qquad\text{(3)}
\]

Equation (2) shows that \(q^g_{a,z}=f^g_{a,z}/u_{a,z}\) is a probability on \(\{u_{a,z}>0\}\).  On \(\{u_{a,z}=0\}\), (2) forces \(f^g_{a,z}=0\), so the stipulated value \(q^g_{a,z}=0\) is legal and, on both regions,
\[
q^g_{a,z}(r)u_{a,z}(r)=f^g_{a,z}(r).                                   \qquad\text{(4)}
\]

We verify the induced observation rather than assuming it.  For an armwise sampled record \((\widetilde T_a,\Delta_a)\), if \(\Delta_a=0\), then \(T(a)=\widetilde T_a<C(a)=t_{\max}+1\); if \(\Delta_a=1\), then \(C(a)=\widetilde T_a\leq T(a)\).  Therefore in either case
\[
\min\{T(a),C(a)\}=\widetilde T_a,
\qquad \mathbf 1\{C(a)\leq T(a)\}=\Delta_a.                          \qquad\text{(5)}
\]
Together with the observed marginal of \(X\), the observed conditional arm kernels, and the protocol treatment probabilities, (5) proves that the observed law under \(P^g_{\mathrm{cont}}\) is \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\).  The construction draws treatment independently of both potential arm records given \(X\), gives consistency after setting \((T,C)=(T(A),C(A))\), and keeps \(T(a)\) in \([0,t_{\max}]\).

The same conditional calculation, now using (4), yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
s^{P^g_{\mathrm{cont}}}_{a,z}(r)
&=E[(T(a)-\widetilde T)\Delta\mid A=a,Z=z,R=r]\\
&=q^g_{a,z}(r)
E_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}}[(t_{\max}-\widetilde T)\Delta
 \mid A=a,Z=z,R=r]\\
&=q^g_{a,z}(r)u_{a,z}(r)
=f^g_{a,z}(r).                                                         \qquad\text{(6)}
\end{aligned}
\]
These residuals are continuous by (2), and no monotonicity or finite Lipschitz condition is required in the residual-continuity-only class.  All remaining overlap and observed-law regularity properties are inherited from \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\).  Hence \(P^g_{\mathrm{cont}}\in\mathcal F_{\mathrm{cont}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\).

Finally, randomization, consistency, and the armwise decomposition of event time give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau_z^{P^g_{\mathrm{cont}}}(r)
&=b_{1,z}(r)-b_{0,z}(r)
 +f^g_{1,z}(r)-f^g_{0,z}(r)\\
&=b_{1,z}(r)-b_{0,z}(r)+d_z(r)
=g_z(r),                                                               \qquad\text{(7)}
\end{aligned}
\]
where (3) gives the second equality.  Thus one law attains the entire curve \(g\), including every zero-capacity contact.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This separate attainability result prevents the residual-continuity benchmark from borrowing membership in the monotone-Lipschitz law class. \textit{Gap.} Wang et al. establish validity of covariatewise endpoints but do not construct a single residual-continuous full-data law for every curve in the continuous endpoint image. \textit{Consumer.} It certifies the exact benchmark against which the contraction produced by the clinically calibrated shape restrictions is measured.

\begin{theorem}[thm:wang-frontier]\label{thm:wang-frontier}
For the separately defined residual-continuity-only compatible-law class, \(\{\tau^P:P\in\mathcal F_{\mathrm{cont}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\}=\mathcal B(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\subset\mathbb C_{\mathcal Z}\). Its coordinate interval is \([b_{1,z}(r)-b_{0,z}(r)-u_{0,z}(r),b_{1,z}(r)-b_{0,z}(r)+u_{1,z}(r)]\), the continuous-curve image of the conservative endpoint formula in Wang et al., Theorem 5.1 and equations (8)--(9). For the maintained monotone-Lipschitz class, \(\mathcal T(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\subseteq\mathcal B(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\). A sufficient observed criterion for strict inclusion is the existence of \(z,r_1<r_2\) such that \(u_{1,z}(r_1)+u_{0,z}(r_2)>2L(r_2-r_1)\), because the two endpoint choices permitted by \(\mathcal B(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\) violate the \(2L\)-Lipschitz restriction on \(f_{1,z}-f_{0,z}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let
\[
\Theta_{I,\mathrm{cont}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})
:=\{\tau^P:P\in\mathcal F_{\mathrm{cont}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\}.
\tag{1}
\]
For \(P\in\mathcal F_{\mathrm{cont}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\), the consistency identity and bounded-event-time inequality give
\[
0\leq s^P_{a,z}(r)\leq u_{a,z}(r)
\tag{2}
\]
for the conditional score law almost every \(r\).  Both functions are continuous in this class.  Profile mass, Bayes' rule, treatment overlap, and the density bound give
\[
\frac{dP_{\mathrm{obs}}(R\in dr\mid A=a,Z=z)}{dr}
=\frac{\pi_a(z,r)p_z(r)}
{P_{\mathrm{obs}}(A=a\mid Z=z)}
\geq\varepsilon c_p>0.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus (2) holds everywhere.  Conditional randomization and consistency give
\[
b_{1,z}(r)-b_{0,z}(r)-u_{0,z}(r)
\leq\tau_z^P(r)\leq
b_{1,z}(r)-b_{0,z}(r)+u_{1,z}(r).
\tag{4}
\]
The curve is continuous because the bases are H\"older and the residuals are continuous, so
\[
\Theta_{I,\mathrm{cont}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})
\subseteq\mathcal B(P_{\mathrm{obs}}).
\tag{5}
\]
The established continuous-box completion lemma attains every \(g\in\mathcal B(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\) by one \(P^g_{\mathrm{cont}}\in\mathcal F_{\mathrm{cont}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\), proving
\[
\Theta_{I,\mathrm{cont}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})
=\mathcal B(P_{\mathrm{obs}}).
\tag{6}
\]
The continuous upper and lower endpoint curves belong to the box, and their convex mixtures fill every coordinate interval in (4).

The definitions also give
\[
\begin{aligned}
b_{a,z}(r)+u_{a,z}(r)
&=E_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}}
[\widetilde T+(t_{\max}-\widetilde T)\Delta
\mid A=a,Z=z,R=r]\\
&=E_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}}
[\widetilde T\mathbf 1\{\Delta=0\}
+t_{\max}\mathbf 1\{\Delta=1\}
\mid A=a,Z=z,R=r].
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
The cited Wang endpoint lemma identifies \(b_{a,z}\) as the lower arm endpoint, (7) as the upper arm endpoint, and forms CATE endpoints by subtracting opposite arm endpoints.  These differences are
\[
b_{1,z}-(b_{0,z}+u_{0,z})=b_{1,z}-b_{0,z}-u_{0,z},
\qquad
(b_{1,z}+u_{1,z})-b_{0,z}=b_{1,z}-b_{0,z}+u_{1,z}.
\tag{8}
\]

For every \(g\in\mathcal T(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\), its residual difference is \(f_{1,z}-f_{0,z}\) with \(0\leq f_{a,z}\leq u_{a,z}\).  Hence
\[
\mathcal T(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\subseteq\mathcal B(P_{\mathrm{obs}}).
\tag{9}
\]
Suppose \(r_1<r_2\) and
\[
u_{1,z}(r_1)+u_{0,z}(r_2)>2L(r_2-r_1).
\tag{10}
\]
Let \(\lambda\) be zero on \([0,r_1]\), linear from zero to one on \([r_1,r_2]\), and one on \([r_2,1]\), and define
\[
d_z(r)=\{1-\lambda(r)\}u_{1,z}(r)-\lambda(r)u_{0,z}(r).
\tag{11}
\]
Set the other profile residual differences to zero and add them to the base curves.  Equation (11) is a pointwise convex combination of \(u_{1,z}\) and \(-u_{0,z}\), so this continuous curve lies in \(\mathcal B(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\).  But
\[
d_z(r_1)=u_{1,z}(r_1),\qquad
d_z(r_2)=-u_{0,z}(r_2),
\]
and (10) implies
\[
|d_z(r_2)-d_z(r_1)|>2L(r_2-r_1).
\tag{12}
\]
If the curve belonged to \(\mathcal T(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\), then \(d_z=f_{1,z}-f_{0,z}\) for two \(L\)-Lipschitz functions, giving
\[
|d_z(r_2)-d_z(r_1)|
\leq|f_{1,z}(r_2)-f_{1,z}(r_1)|
+|f_{0,z}(r_2)-f_{0,z}(r_1)|
\leq2L(r_2-r_1),
\]
contrary to (12).  This proves the stated sufficient certificate.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The result gives a valid continuous-function sanity reduction and an observable sufficient certificate that curve coupling strictly improves on pointwise bounds. \textit{Gap.} Wang et al.'s endpoint theorem permits independent adversarial completions at each score value and supplies no observed sufficient criterion certifying that joint residual shape strictly contracts its product region. \textit{Consumer.} The sufficient condition identifies profiles and score pairs for which defending the residual-shape sensitivity model can change a treatment-benefit conclusion.

\begin{lemma}[lem:direction-net-hausdorff]\label{lem:direction-net-hausdorff}
For nonempty compact convex \(K_1,K_2\subset\mathbb C_{\mathcal Z,h_n}\) contained in the closed radius-\(\mathsf R\) ball, \(d_H(K_1,K_2)\le\max_{v\in\mathcal D_n}|h_{K_1}(v)-h_{K_2}(v)|+2\mathsf R\delta_n\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(E=\mathbb C_{\mathcal Z,h_n}\) with its inherited norm, and write
\(B_E=\{x\in E:\|x\|\le1\}\).  We first establish directly the support
representation of Hausdorff distance needed here.  If
\(d=d_H(K_1,K_2)\), then
\(K_1\subseteq K_2+dB_E\) and \(K_2\subseteq K_1+dB_E\).  Therefore, for
every \(v\in E^*\),
\[
 |h_{K_1}(v)-h_{K_2}(v)|\le d\|v\|_*.
\]
Conversely, put
\[
 q=\sup_{\|v\|_*\le1}|h_{K_1}(v)-h_{K_2}(v)|.
\]
If some \(x\in K_1\) were outside the closed convex set \(K_2+qB_E\),
finite-dimensional separation would give a nonzero \(v\in E^*\) such that
\[
 v(x)>h_{K_2+qB_E}(v)=h_{K_2}(v)+q\|v\|_*.
\]
After division by \(\|v\|_*\), this contradicts the definition of \(q\).
Thus \(K_1\subseteq K_2+qB_E\); interchanging the sets gives the reverse
inclusion.  It follows that
\[
 d_H(K_1,K_2)=
 \sup_{\|v\|_*\le1}|h_{K_1}(v)-h_{K_2}(v)|.
\]

For any set \(K\) contained in the closed radius-\(\mathsf R\) ball and any
\(v,d\in E^*\),
\[
 |h_K(v)-h_K(d)|
 \le\sup_{x\in K}|(v-d)(x)|
 \le\mathsf R\|v-d\|_*.
\]
Given \(v\) in the dual unit ball, the definition of the
\(\delta_n\)-net supplies \(d\in\mathcal D_n\) with
\(\|v-d\|_*\le\delta_n\).  Applying the preceding Lipschitz bound to both
sets yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
|h_{K_1}(v)-h_{K_2}(v)|
&\le |h_{K_1}(d)-h_{K_2}(d)|
   +|h_{K_1}(v)-h_{K_1}(d)|
   +|h_{K_2}(v)-h_{K_2}(d)|\\
&\le \max_{e\in\mathcal D_n}|h_{K_1}(e)-h_{K_2}(e)|
   +2\mathsf R\delta_n.
\end{aligned}
\]
Taking the supremum over the dual unit ball and using the established
support representation proves the claim.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The finite-net inequality converts support calibration on finitely many directions into Hausdorff control with an explicit geometric remainder. \textit{Gap.} Generic support-function identities take a supremum over the full dual ball; the gridded finite-dimensional reduction records the exact net remainder needed for the changing-contact rate. \textit{Consumer.} It makes the empirical-process prerequisite finite in the direction coordinate and exposes its \(\log|\mathcal D_n|\) cost.

Under the causal conditions of Wang et al., their conservative post-dropout formula has arm-specific lower endpoint \(E_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}}[\widetilde T\mid A=a,Z=z,R=r]\) and upper endpoint \(E_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}}[\widetilde T\mathbf 1\{\Delta=0\}+t_{\max}\mathbf 1\{\Delta=1\}\mid A=a,Z=z,R=r]\); the CATE lower endpoint subtracts the control-arm upper endpoint from the treated-arm lower endpoint, and the CATE upper endpoint subtracts the control-arm lower endpoint from the treated-arm upper endpoint.

\begin{theorem}[thm:obstacle-curve-stability]\label{thm:obstacle-curve-stability}
Fix an arm and profile.  If the obstacle-set construction is evaluated at two nonnegative continuous capacities \(u_{a,z}\) and \(u'_{a,z}\), then
\[
d_H^{\|\cdot\|_\infty}\!\left(\mathcal S_{a,z}(u),\mathcal S_{a,z}(u')\right)
\leq \|u_{a,z}-u'_{a,z}\|_\infty.
\]
Consequently, for two base-capacity pairs \((b,u)\) and \((b',u')\), with
\[
\rho_b=\max_{a,z}\|b_{a,z}-b'_{a,z}\|_\infty,
\qquad
\rho_u=\max_{a,z}\|u_{a,z}-u'_{a,z}\|_\infty,
\]
the corresponding sharp-curve constructions obey
\[
d_H^{\|\cdot\|_\infty}\!\left(\mathcal T(b,u),\mathcal T(b',u')\right)
\leq 2\rho_b+2\rho_u.
\]
Both inequalities hold without any restriction on the zero-capacity contact sets.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(\rho=\|u_{a,z}-u'_{a,z}\|_\infty\), and fix \(f\in\mathcal S_{a,z}(u)\).  Define
\[
(C_\rho f)(r):=[f(r)-\rho]_+.
\tag{1}
\]
The map \(x\mapsto[x-\rho]_+\) is nondecreasing and one-Lipschitz.  Since \(f\) is nonnegative, nondecreasing, and \(L\)-Lipschitz by the obstacle-set definition, (1) is also nonnegative and nondecreasing and satisfies
\[
|(C_\rho f)(r)-(C_\rho f)(r')|
\leq |f(r)-f(r')|
\leq L|r-r'|.
\tag{2}
\]
Moreover, \(f\leq u_{a,z}\leq u'_{a,z}+\rho\) and \(u'_{a,z}\geq0\), whence
\[
0\leq C_\rho f\leq u'_{a,z}.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus \(C_\rho f\in\mathcal S_{a,z}(u')\).  Pointwise,
\[
0\leq f(r)-[f(r)-\rho]_+=\min\{f(r),\rho\}\leq\rho,
\tag{4}
\]
so every element of \(\mathcal S_{a,z}(u)\) is within \(\rho\) of \(\mathcal S_{a,z}(u')\).  Interchanging \(u_{a,z}\) and \(u'_{a,z}\) proves the first Hausdorff inequality.  Notice that (1)--(4) remain valid when either capacity vanishes on an interval or at an isolated point.

For the curve claim, fix
\[
g_z=b_{1,z}-b_{0,z}+f_{1,z}-f_{0,z}\in\mathcal T(b,u).
\]
Apply (1) armwise with \(\rho_u\), writing \(f'_{a,z}=C_{\rho_u}f_{a,z}\).  Then \(f'_{a,z}\in\mathcal S_{a,z}(u')\), and
\[
g'_z=b'_{1,z}-b'_{0,z}+f'_{1,z}-f'_{0,z}\in\mathcal T(b',u').
\]
The triangle inequality and (4) give
\[
\|g-g'\|_\infty
\leq \max_z\bigl(\|b_{1,z}-b'_{1,z}\|_\infty
+\|b_{0,z}-b'_{0,z}\|_\infty
+\|f_{1,z}-f'_{1,z}\|_\infty
+\|f_{0,z}-f'_{0,z}\|_\infty\bigr)
\leq2\rho_b+2\rho_u.
\tag{5}
\]
The same construction with the primed and unprimed pairs exchanged gives the reverse directed bound, proving the second Hausdorff inequality.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This contraction argument is the contact-uniform modulus needed to propagate an external nuisance band through the obstacle image. \textit{Gap.} Directional differentiation can change discontinuously when zero-capacity contacts appear, but the positive-part contraction controls the entire feasible set without differentiating a face. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies the deterministic \(8r_n\) robust-union excess term in the band-robust confidence theorem.

\begin{lemma}[lem:greatest-obstacle-envelope]\label{lem:greatest-obstacle-envelope}
For every nonnegative continuous capacity \(u_{a,z}\), the envelope \(\Gamma_Lu_{a,z}\) is the greatest element of \(\mathcal S_{a,z}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\): it is nonnegative, nondecreasing, \(L\)-Lipschitz, bounded above by \(u_{a,z}\), and every \(f\in\mathcal S_{a,z}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\) satisfies
\[
f(r)\leq(\Gamma_Lu_{a,z})(r)\qquad\text{for every }r\in[0,1].
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(a,z\), abbreviate \(u=u_{a,z}\), and put
\[
G(r):=(\Gamma_Lu)(r)=\inf_{t\in[0,1]}\{u(t)+L(r-t)_+\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Every term under the infimum is nonnegative, so \(G\geq0\).  Taking \(t=r\) gives
\[
G(r)\leq u(r).
\tag{2}
\]
If \(r\leq r'\), then \((r-t)_+\leq(r'-t)_+\) for every \(t\), hence \(G(r)\leq G(r')\).  Also
\[
0\leq(r'-t)_+-(r-t)_+\leq r'-r.
\tag{3}
\]
For every \(\varepsilon>0\), choose \(t_\varepsilon\) whose value in (1) is at most \(G(r)+\varepsilon\).  Then
\[
G(r')
\leq u(t_\varepsilon)+L(r'-t_\varepsilon)_+
\leq G(r)+\varepsilon+L(r'-r).
\]
Letting \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\), and combining this inequality with monotonicity, proves
\[
0\leq G(r')-G(r)\leq L(r'-r).
\tag{4}
\]
Thus \(G\) is nonnegative, nondecreasing, \(L\)-Lipschitz, and below \(u\), so \(G\in\mathcal S_{a,z}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\).

Let \(f\in\mathcal S_{a,z}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\).  For fixed \(r,t\), if \(t\leq r\), the Lipschitz restriction and \(f(t)\leq u(t)\) give
\[
f(r)\leq f(t)+L(r-t)\leq u(t)+L(r-t)_+.
\]
If \(t>r\), monotonicity and the obstacle inequality give
\[
f(r)\leq f(t)\leq u(t)=u(t)+L(r-t)_+.
\]
Taking the infimum over \(t\) proves \(f(r)\leq G(r)\).  Since \(r\) was arbitrary, \(G\) is the greatest obstacle element.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The explicit envelope converts the obstacle constraint into a computable greatest feasible residual and is the key to exact pointwise projection. \textit{Gap.} The obstacle definition alone does not expose its greatest element or show that the pointwise residual maximum can be attained by one globally feasible function. \textit{Consumer.} The exact pointwise projection theorem uses this lemma for both arm endpoints.

\begin{theorem}[thm:exact-pointwise-obstacle-projection]\label{thm:exact-pointwise-obstacle-projection}
For every \(z\in\mathcal Z\) and \(r\in[0,1]\), the exact coordinate projection is
\[
\{g_z(r):g\in\mathcal T(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\}
=\left[
b_{1,z}(r)-b_{0,z}(r)-(\Gamma_Lu_{0,z})(r),
b_{1,z}(r)-b_{0,z}(r)+(\Gamma_Lu_{1,z})(r)
\right].
\]
Thus the displayed interval is sharp, not merely outer.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(z,r\) and write \(c_z=b_{1,z}-b_{0,z}\).  If \(g\in\mathcal T(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\), then
\[
g_z(r)=c_z(r)+f_{1,z}(r)-f_{0,z}(r)
\tag{1}
\]
for obstacle functions \(f_{a,z}\).  The greatest-obstacle lemma and nonnegativity give
\[
0\leq f_{a,z}(r)\leq(\Gamma_Lu_{a,z})(r).
\tag{2}
\]
Equations (1)--(2) prove the outer inclusion in the displayed interval.

Conversely, the zero function is feasible, and the greatest-obstacle lemma makes \(\Gamma_Lu_{a,z}\) feasible.  The lower endpoint is attained by \(f_{0,z}=\Gamma_Lu_{0,z}\), \(f_{1,z}=0\); the upper endpoint is attained by \(f_{1,z}=\Gamma_Lu_{1,z}\), \(f_{0,z}=0\).  Set unused arm-profile functions to zero.  Every defining obstacle inequality is preserved by convex combinations, so convex mixtures of these two feasible families attain every scalar between the endpoints.  This proves equality.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This theorem gives the exact scalar projection of the joint curve set while preserving the distinction between a projection and the whole identified set. \textit{Gap.} Pointwise capacity endpoints ignore monotone-Lipschitz feasibility; the greatest-obstacle envelope supplies the attainable corrected endpoints. \textit{Consumer.} Analysts can read exact scorewise sign bounds from the jointly sharp curve set.

\begin{theorem}[thm:exact-obstacle-strictness]\label{thm:exact-obstacle-strictness}
For the nonnegative continuous capacities in the frozen setup,
\[
\mathcal T(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\subsetneq\mathcal B(P_{\mathrm{obs}})
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\max_{a\in\mathcal A,\,z\in\mathcal Z}\|u_{a,z}\|_\infty>0.
\]
If the maximum is zero, both sets are the singleton base curve.  If it is positive, a continuous box selection has local slope exceeding \(L\), whereas every residual difference generated by two monotone \(L\)-Lipschitz obstacles is itself \(L\)-Lipschitz.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every \(g\in\mathcal T(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\) has
\[
g_z=b_{1,z}-b_{0,z}+d_z,\qquad
d_z=f_{1,z}-f_{0,z},\qquad
0\leq f_{a,z}\leq u_{a,z}.
\tag{1}
\]
Thus \(-u_{0,z}\leq d_z\leq u_{1,z}\), proving
\[
\mathcal T(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\subseteq\mathcal B(P_{\mathrm{obs}}).
\tag{2}
\]
For \(r<r'\), obstacle monotonicity and Lipschitz continuity imply
\[
0\leq f_{a,z}(r')-f_{a,z}(r)\leq L(r'-r),\qquad a\in\{0,1\}.
\tag{3}
\]
The difference of the two increments in (3) lies in \([-L(r'-r),L(r'-r)]\), whence
\[
|d_z(r')-d_z(r)|\leq L|r'-r|.
\tag{4}
\]

Suppose the maximum capacity norm is positive.  Nonnegativity yields \(a_0,z_0,r_0\) with \(u_{a_0,z_0}(r_0)>0\).  Continuity supplies \(c>0\) and a nondegenerate relative interval \(J\subset[0,1]\) on which \(u_{a_0,z_0}\geq c\).  Choose \(x<y<w\) in the relative interior of \(J\) so that
\[
y-x<\frac{c}{2L}.
\tag{5}
\]
Let \(\psi\) be a continuous triangular tent supported on \([x,w]\), linear from \(\psi(x)=0\) to \(\psi(y)=c/2\), linear back to \(\psi(w)=0\), and zero elsewhere.  Then
\[
0\leq\psi\leq u_{a_0,z_0}.
\tag{6}
\]
Define
\[
d_{z_0}=
\begin{cases}
\psi,&a_0=1,\\
-\psi,&a_0=0,
\end{cases}
\qquad
d_z=0\ (z\neq z_0),\qquad
g_z=b_{1,z}-b_{0,z}+d_z.
\tag{7}
\]
Equations (6)--(7) imply \(-u_{0,z}\leq d_z\leq u_{1,z}\), so \(g\in\mathcal B(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\).  But
\[
|d_{z_0}(y)-d_{z_0}(x)|=\frac c2>L(y-x),
\tag{8}
\]
contradicting (4) for every member of \(\mathcal T(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\).  Inclusion (2) is therefore strict.

Conversely, if every capacity norm is zero, nonnegativity gives \(u_{a,z}=0\) for every arm and profile.  The obstacle and box inequalities then force every residual to be zero, so
\[
\mathcal T(P_{\mathrm{obs}})
=\mathcal B(P_{\mathrm{obs}})
=\{(b_{1,z}-b_{0,z})_{z\in\mathcal Z}\}.
\]
This proves the equivalence.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The equivalence is the exact observable frontier for whether scorewise shape restrictions contract the continuous Wang endpoint box. \textit{Gap.} The former score-pair condition was only sufficient and used a loose two-(L) difference bound; monotone arm increments sharpen the residual difference to one-(L) Lipschitz continuity. \textit{Consumer.} It determines exactly when the maintained curve set adds identifying content beyond continuous endpoint selection.

\begin{lemma}[lem:wang-measurable-box-completion]\label{lem:wang-measurable-box-completion}
For every measurable endpoint selection \(g\in\mathcal B_W(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\), there is one full-data law \(P^g\in\mathcal F_W(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\) such that
\[
\tau^{P^g}=g\qquad P_X\text{-almost surely}.
\]
The completion preserves the entire observed law and remains legal on every zero-capacity contact set.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let \(g\in\mathcal B_W(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\), and choose a jointly measurable representative \(\bar g\) of this element of \(L^0(P_X)\).  Write
\[
c_z(r)=b_{1,z}(r)-b_{0,z}(r).
\]
By the definition of \(\mathcal B_W(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\), the measurable set
\[
N=\{(z,r):\bar g_z(r)<c_z(r)-u_{0,z}(r)\text{ or }\bar g_z(r)>c_z(r)+u_{1,z}(r)\}
\]
has \(P_X(N)=0\).  Replace \(\bar g\) on \(N\) by \(c\), which does not change its \(L^0(P_X)\) equivalence class, and denote the resulting measurable representative again by \(g\).  Thus the box inequalities hold at every point for this representative.  Define
\[
f^g_{1,z}(r)=[g_z(r)-c_z(r)]_+,
\qquad
f^g_{0,z}(r)=[c_z(r)-g_z(r)]_+.
\tag{1}
\]
For every \((z,r)\), elementary positive-part algebra and the two box inequalities give
\[
0\le f^g_{a,z}(r)\le u_{a,z}(r),
\qquad
f^g_{1,z}(r)-f^g_{0,z}(r)=g_z(r)-c_z(r).
\tag{2}
\]
In particular, if \(u_{a,z}(r)=0\), then (2) forces \(f^g_{a,z}(r)=0\).  Hence the measurable function
\[
q^g_{a,z}(r)=
\begin{cases}
f^g_{a,z}(r)/u_{a,z}(r),&u_{a,z}(r)>0,\\
0,&u_{a,z}(r)=0
\end{cases}
\tag{3}
\]
is well defined and takes values in \([0,1]\); division is used only on the positive-capacity set.

We now use the armwise completion technique.  The identically zero family belongs to every obstacle set \(\mathcal S_{a,z}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\): it is continuous, nonnegative, nondecreasing, \(L\)-Lipschitz, and bounded above by the nonnegative capacity \(u_{a,z}\).  Therefore the established completion-witness lemma and the completion-law definition provide a law \(P^0\in\mathcal F(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\).  Under that law, for each arm define
\[
\widetilde T_a^0=\min\{T^0(a),C^0(a)\},
\qquad
\Delta_a^0=\mathbf 1\{C^0(a)\le T^0(a)\}.
\tag{4}
\]
Conditional on \(X\), the law of \((\widetilde T_a^0,\Delta_a^0)\) is the observed conditional law of \((\widetilde T,\Delta)\) given \(A=a,X\).  Moreover, the zero-completion construction gives
\[
C^0(a)=t_{\max}+1,quad T^0(a)=\widetilde T_a^0
\quad\text{on }\{\Delta_a^0=0\},
\]
and
\[
C^0(a)=T^0(a)=\widetilde T_a^0
\quad\text{on }\{\Delta_a^0=1\}.
\tag{5}
\]

On a product extension of \(P^0\), draw \(U_0,U_1\) independently from the uniform law on \([0,1]\), independently of all variables under \(P^0\), and put
\[
T^g(a)=
\begin{cases}
t_{\max},&\Delta_a^0=1\text{ and }U_a\le q^g_{a,Z}(R),\\
\widetilde T_a^0,&\text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\qquad
C^g(a)=C^0(a).
\tag{6}
\]
Finally, retain the baseline law and draw \(A\) conditionally on \(X\) with the protocol probabilities \(\pi_a(X)\), independently of the armwise potential vector and the uniforms; set \((T,C)=(T^g(A),C^g(A))\).  Call the resulting full-data law \(P^g\).

We first verify preservation of the observed law.  On \(\{\Delta_a^0=0\}\), equations (5)--(6) give \(T^g(a)=\widetilde T_a^0<C^g(a)=t_{\max}+1\), so the observed pair remains \((\widetilde T_a^0,0)\).  On \(\{\Delta_a^0=1\}\), one has \(C^g(a)=\widetilde T_a^0\) and either \(T^g(a)=\widetilde T_a^0\) or \(T^g(a)=t_{\max}\ge\widetilde T_a^0\).  The stipulated tie convention therefore gives the same observed pair \((\widetilde T_a^0,1)\).  Thus, arm by arm and conditional on \(X\),
\[
\left(\min\{T^g(a),C^g(a)\},
\mathbf 1\{C^g(a)\le T^g(a)\}\right)
=(\widetilde T_a^0,\Delta_a^0),
\tag{7}
\]
and the protocol treatment kernel then implies that the entire law of \((Z,R,A,\widetilde T,\Delta)\) under \(P^g\) is \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\).  Equations (5)--(6) and boundedness of the observed follow-up give \(0\le T^g(a)\le t_{\max}\).

The conditional randomization assumption for \(P^0\) makes \(A\) independent of its full armwise potential vector given \(X\).  Since the new uniforms are also conditionally independent of \(A\), (6) is a measurable armwise transformation and hence
\[
T^g(a)\perp\!\!\!\perp A\mid X.
\tag{8}
\]
Consistency holds by the assigned-arm definition of \((T,C)\), and treatment overlap is preserved because the kernel remains \(\pi_a(X)\).  Censoring overlap is preserved by (7).  The essential-overlap restriction supplies, for each arm, an observed conditional version bounded below by \(\kappa\) off a conditional-null set.  Treatment overlap makes the union of those exceptional sets \(P_X\)-null.  On that null union, redefine the conditional version to put probability one on an uncensored record.  Such a null-set redefinition leaves all defining conditional-integral identities, and hence \(P^g\), unchanged, while satisfying the pointwise overlap convention in the definition of \(\mathcal F_W(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\).  Consequently, by that definition,
\[
P^g\in\mathcal F_W(P_{\mathrm{obs}}).
\tag{9}
\]
No continuity, monotonicity, or Lipschitz property of \(f^g\) has been invoked in this membership argument.

It remains to compute the causal curve.  Conditional independence of \(U_a\), equations (3), (6), and observed-law preservation yield, for \(P_X\)-almost every \((z,r)\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
&E_{P^g}\!\left[(T^g(a)-\widetilde T)\Delta
\mid A=a,Z=z,R=r\right]\\
&\qquad=q^g_{a,z}(r)
E_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}}\!\left[(t_{\max}-\widetilde T)\Delta
\mid A=a,Z=z,R=r\right]\\
&\qquad=q^g_{a,z}(r)u_{a,z}(r)
=f^g_{a,z}(r).
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
The last equality follows by division on \(\{u_{a,z}>0\}\) and is \(0=0\) on \(\{u_{a,z}=0\}\), by (2).  On the assigned arm,
\[
T^g(a)=\widetilde T+(T^g(a)-\widetilde T)\Delta,
\]
so (8), the observed-base definition, and (10) give
\[
E_{P^g}[T^g(a)\mid Z=z,R=r]
=b_{a,z}(r)+f^g_{a,z}(r)
\quad P_X\text{-almost surely}.
\tag{11}
\]
Subtracting (11) for the two arms and using (1)--(2) and the CATE-curve definition proves
\[
\tau_z^{P^g}(r)
=c_z(r)+f^g_{1,z}(r)-f^g_{0,z}(r)
=g_z(r)
\quad P_X\text{-almost surely}.
\tag{12}
\]
Thus one law attains the entire measurable selection simultaneously, including on all zero-capacity contact sets, and the claim follows.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The measurable extension of the armwise completion proves converse attainability for the full Wang-compatible image, including zero-capacity contacts. \textit{Gap.} The frozen continuous completion proves attainability only in the continuous slice and therefore cannot alone establish the full measurable image modulo null sets. \textit{Consumer.} The Wang-compatible box-sharpness theorem uses this completion for its reverse inclusion.

\begin{theorem}[thm:wang-compatible-box-sharpness]\label{thm:wang-compatible-box-sharpness}
For the Wang-compatible class,
\[
\{\tau^P:P\in\mathcal F_W(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\}
=\mathcal B_W(P_{\mathrm{obs}})
\quad\text{in }L^0(P_X).
\]
The residual-continuity compatible class is a smaller witness subclass,
\[
\mathcal F_{\mathrm{cont}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})
\subseteq\mathcal F_W(P_{\mathrm{obs}}),
\]
and, under profile mass, score density, and observed-law continuity,
\[
\mathcal B_W(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\cap\mathbb C_{\mathcal Z}
=\mathcal B(P_{\mathrm{obs}}).
\]
Every member of this continuous slice is attained by a law in \(\mathcal F_{\mathrm{cont}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The observable map sends a full-data law \(P\) to the law of
\[
O=(Z,R,A,\min\{T(A),C(A)\},\mathbf 1\{C(A)\le T(A)\})
\in\mathcal Z\times[0,1]\times\{0,1\}\times[0,t_{\max}]\times\{0,1\}.
\]
For a fixed image \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\), the Wang identified set is
\[
\Theta_{I,W}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})
:=\{\tau^P:P\in\mathcal F_W(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\}
\subseteq L^0(P_X).
\]
This definition fixes the causal target; the endpoint box is a separate functional of the observed law.  We prove their equality by two inclusions and then identify the continuous slice.

First fix \(P\in\mathcal F_W(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\), an arm \(a\in\{0,1\}\), and a covariate value \(x=(z,r)\) in a common full-\(P_X\)-measure set on which the relevant regular conditional expectations are defined.  Treatment exchangeability in the definition of the Wang-compatible class, together with treatment overlap, gives
\[
E_P[T(a)\mid X=x]
=E_P[T(a)\mid A=a,X=x].
\tag{1}
\]
Consistency and the definitions of \(\widetilde T\) and \(\Delta\) imply on \(\{A=a\}\) that
\[
T(a)=\widetilde T+(T(a)-\widetilde T)\Delta.
\tag{2}
\]
Indeed, when \(\Delta=0\), one has \(C>T(a)\) and \(\widetilde T=T(a)\); when \(\Delta=1\), (2) is algebraic.  On the latter event, \(C\le T(a)\), hence \(\widetilde T=C\le T(a)\).  The bounded-event-time restriction therefore yields
\[
0\le (T(a)-\widetilde T)\Delta
\le (t_{\max}-\widetilde T)\Delta.
\tag{3}
\]
Taking conditional expectations in (2)--(3), using (1), using compatibility to replace the assigned-arm observed conditional law by \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\), and then applying the observed-base and dropout-capacity definitions gives
\[
b_{a,z}(r)
\le E_P[T(a)\mid Z=z,R=r]
\le b_{a,z}(r)+u_{a,z}(r)
\quad P_X\text{-almost surely}.
\tag{4}
\]
The cited Wang endpoint lemma confirms that the two expressions in (4) are exactly the published conservative lower and upper arm endpoints: its upper endpoint equals
\[
E_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}}
[\widetilde T\mathbf 1\{\Delta=0\}+t_{\max}\mathbf 1\{\Delta=1\}
\mid A=a,Z=z,R=r]
=b_{a,z}(r)+u_{a,z}(r).
\tag{5}
\]
By linearity of conditional expectation, the CATE definition, and (4), subtracting the control upper endpoint from the treatment lower endpoint and conversely gives
\[
b_{1,z}(r)-b_{0,z}(r)-u_{0,z}(r)
\le\tau_z^P(r)\le
b_{1,z}(r)-b_{0,z}(r)+u_{1,z}(r)
\quad P_X\text{-almost surely}.
\tag{6}
\]
Thus
\[
\{\tau^P:P\in\mathcal F_W(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\}
\subseteq\mathcal B_W(P_{\mathrm{obs}})
\quad\text{in }L^0(P_X).
\tag{7}
\]
Conversely, the measurable-box-completion lemma constructs, for every \(g\in\mathcal B_W(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\), a single law \(P^g\in\mathcal F_W(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\) satisfying \(\tau^{P^g}=g\) \(P_X\)-almost surely.  Therefore the reverse inclusion holds, and (7) is an equality.  This proves law sharpness, not merely an outer bound.

We next compare the continuous witness class.  If \(P\in\mathcal F_{\mathrm{cont}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\), the continuity-model and compatibility definitions give consistency, bounded event-time support, treatment overlap, censoring overlap, and the same observed law.  Its conditional randomization restriction
\[
A\perp\!\!\!\perp(T(0),T(1),C(0),C(1))\mid X
\]
implies the weaker Wang restriction \(T(a)\perp\!\!\!\perp A\mid X\) for each arm.  Uniform essential censoring overlap permits a regular-conditional version bounded below by \(\kappa\) on a full-\(P_X\)-measure set; defining that version by any strictly positive uncensored kernel on the null complement gives the pointwise convention without changing the law.  The Wang class imposes no residual continuity, monotonicity, finite-Lipschitz, or independent-censoring restriction.  Hence every restriction defining the Wang-compatible class holds and
\[
\mathcal F_{\mathrm{cont}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})
\subseteq\mathcal F_W(P_{\mathrm{obs}}).
\tag{8}
\]

Finally, identify \(\mathbb C_{\mathcal Z}\) with its image in \(L^0(P_X)\).  This embedding is injective under profile mass and the score-density lower bound.  Indeed, if two continuous curve families differ at some \((z,r_0)\), continuity gives a nonempty relative interval \(I\subseteq[0,1]\) on which they differ, whereas
\[
P_X(Z=z,R\in I)
=P_{\mathrm{obs}}(Z=z)\int_I p_z(t)\,dt
\ge\omega c_p\operatorname{Leb}(I)>0,
\tag{9}
\]
contradicting equality in \(L^0(P_X)\).

If \(g\in\mathcal B(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\), its defining inequalities hold for every \((z,r)\), and hence almost surely; therefore
\[
\mathcal B(P_{\mathrm{obs}})
\subseteq\mathcal B_W(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\cap\mathbb C_{\mathcal Z}.
\tag{10}
\]
For the reverse inclusion, let \(g\in\mathbb C_{\mathcal Z}\) satisfy the Wang box inequalities \(P_X\)-almost surely.  The observed-law H\"older condition implies that every \(b_{a,z}\) and \(u_{a,z}\) is continuous.  Suppose, for example, that the upper inequality failed at \((z,r_0)\).  Then the continuous function
\[
r\longmapsto g_z(r)-b_{1,z}(r)+b_{0,z}(r)-u_{1,z}(r)
\]
would be strictly positive on some nonempty relative interval \(I\) containing \(r_0\).  Equation (9) would make this violation set have positive \(P_X\)-probability, contradicting almost-sure membership in the Wang box.  Applying the same argument to the continuous lower-gap function rules out a lower-bound failure.  Thus both inequalities hold at every \((z,r)\), which is exactly membership in \(\mathcal B(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\).  Together with (10),
\[
\mathcal B_W(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\cap\mathbb C_{\mathcal Z}
=\mathcal B(P_{\mathrm{obs}}).
\tag{11}
\]
The established continuous-box-completion lemma supplies, for every member \(g\) of the right side of (11), a law \(P^g_{\mathrm{cont}}\in\mathcal F_{\mathrm{cont}}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\) with \(\tau^{P^g_{\mathrm{cont}}}=g\).  Hence every member of the continuous slice is attained by the stated smaller witness class, completing the proof.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This theorem corrects the comparator class: the full Wang-compatible law class has the measurable endpoint-selection image, and the continuous class is only an attainability witness for its continuous slice. \textit{Gap.} A continuous subclass cannot by itself identify the image of the broader published class; the outer endpoint argument and measurable completion close both inclusions at the correct (L\textasciicircum{}0(P\_X)) level. \textit{Consumer.} It supports an exact estimand-level comparison between the maintained curve set and Wang's published-class endpoint region.

\begin{theorem}[thm:band-robust-whole-set-and-conservative-leaves]\label{thm:band-robust-whole-set-and-conservative-leaves}
Let \(\widehat{\mathcal T}_{n,h_n}^{+}\), \(\widehat{\mathcal P}_{X,n}\), and \(\{\widehat I_G:G\in\mathcal G\}\) be the band-robust constructions above, and define
\[
E_{\mathcal T,n}=8r_n+2(Lh_n+Mh_n^\beta),
\qquad
\varepsilon_{Z,n}=2\sqrt{\frac{|\mathcal Z|}{n\alpha}},
\qquad
\Delta_{X,n}=2(r_n+\varepsilon_{Z,n}).
\]
Uniformly over \(P\in\mathfrak P_n^{\mathrm{band}}(r_n)\), with outer probability at least \(1-\alpha/2-o(1)\), all of the following statements hold simultaneously:
\[
\mathcal T(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal T}_{n,h_n}^{+},
\qquad
P_X\in\widehat{\mathcal P}_{X,n},
\]
\[
\sup_{\widehat g\in\widehat{\mathcal T}_{n,h_n}^{+}}
\inf_{g\in\mathcal T(P_{\mathrm{obs}})}\|\widehat g-g\|_\infty
\leq E_{\mathcal T,n},
\qquad
\sup_{Q\in\widehat{\mathcal P}_{X,n}}\|Q-P_X\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\leq\Delta_{X,n},
\]
and, writing \(\widehat I_G=[\widehat{\underline\theta}_G,\widehat{\overline\theta}_G]\), for every \(G\in\mathcal G_n\),
\[
[\underline\theta_G,\overline\theta_G]\subseteq\widehat I_G,
\qquad
0\leq
\max\{\underline\theta_G-\widehat{\underline\theta}_G,
\widehat{\overline\theta}_G-\overline\theta_G\}
\leq E_{\mathcal T,n}+\frac{4\mathsf R\Delta_{X,n}}{p_{\min,n}}.
\]
Thus the whole-set directed excess is \(O(r_n)\) on this event under the declared analysis mesh and tends to zero because \(r_n\to0\).  The conservative simultaneous leaf excess tends to zero along any sequence satisfying \((r_n+n^{-1/2})/p_{\min,n}\to0\).  For every data-measurable \(\widehat G\in\mathcal G_n\), the same event implies \([\underline\theta_{\widehat G},\overline\theta_{\widehat G}]\subseteq\widehat I_{\widehat G}\), without conditioning on its selection rule.

The score-envelope, score-moment, and score-entropy assumptions alone do not imply validity of a derivative-aware common multiplier critical value or the sharper local-mass leaf rate \((np_{\min,n})^{-1/2}\): they admit a zero score class whose multiplier process is identically zero while an eligible empirical leaf mass has nonzero sampling error.  Obtaining that sharper result requires an additional, explicitly declared uniform score-to-statistic expansion and a conditional multiplier approximation.  Hence changing contacts do not obstruct the proved band-robust whole-set result, whereas efficient local-mass multiplier calibration is not identified by the present premises.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write \(\eta^\circ=(b^\circ,u^\circ,p^\circ)\) for the true observed-law nuisance, and let
\[
\mathcal E_{B,n}=
\left\{\eta^\circ\in\widehat{\mathcal B}_{\eta,n},\
\operatorname{diam}_\infty(\widehat{\mathcal B}_{\eta,n})\leq2r_n\right\}.
\tag{1}
\]
The external-band prerequisite and the definition of the band-valid subclass give
\[
\inf_{P\in\mathfrak P_n^{\mathrm{band}}(r_n)}P(\mathcal E_{B,n})
\geq1-\alpha/4-o(1).
\tag{2}
\]
On (1), the score-density and observed-law H\"older assumptions put \(\eta^\circ\) in the admissible portion \(\widehat{\mathcal E}_n\) of the band.

We first prove whole-set coverage.  Put \(e_{h_n}=2(Lh_n+Mh_n^\beta)\) and \(\mathbb B_\infty=\{e\in\mathbb C_{\mathcal Z}:\|e\|_\infty\leq1\}\).  The uniform grid-Hausdorff theorem, evaluated at the true nuisance, gives
\[
\mathcal T(P_{\mathrm{obs}})
\subseteq\mathcal T_{h_n}(b^\circ,u^\circ)+e_{h_n}\mathbb B_\infty.
\tag{3}
\]
Every \(g\in\mathcal T(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\) satisfies \(\|g\|_\infty\leq t_{\max}=\mathsf R\): by law sharpness it is the CATE curve of a compatible law, and bounded event times put both conditional arm means in \([0,t_{\max}]\).  Also, the observed-law H\"older restriction and the residual Lipschitz restriction imply
\[
|g_z(r)-g_z(r')|
\leq2M|r-r'|^\beta+2L|r-r'|.
\tag{4}
\]
Thus \(\mathcal T(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\subseteq\mathcal K_{\mathsf R,M,L,\beta}\).  On \(\mathcal E_{B,n}\), (3), (4), and \(\eta^\circ\in\widehat{\mathcal E}_n\) show
\[
\mathcal T(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\subseteq\widehat H_n
=\widehat{\mathcal T}_{n,h_n}^{+}.
\tag{5}
\]
In particular the fallback clause is inactive.

For clarity, the set in (5) is compact even though the displayed expansion before clipping uses a sup-norm ball.  Indeed, \(\mathcal K_{\mathsf R,M,L,\beta}\) is closed, uniformly bounded, and has a common modulus of continuity.  To verify compactness without invoking an undeclared theorem, take any sequence in this class, extract a diagonal subsequence converging on a countable dense subset of \([0,1]\) in each of the finitely many profiles, and use the common modulus to make that subsequence uniformly Cauchy on a finite score net.  Its uniform limit is continuous and retains the defining inequalities.  Hence \(\mathcal K_{\mathsf R,M,L,\beta}\) is sequentially compact in the sup norm and therefore compact.  The common-modulus inequalities also show that this class is convex.  The set \(\widehat H_n\) is the intersection of it with a closed convex set, so it is compact and convex; the fallback singleton has the same properties.

We next obtain a contact-set-free excess bound.  Fix \((b',u',p')\in\widehat{\mathcal E}_n\).  On \(\mathcal E_{B,n}\), band diameter gives
\[
\max_{a,z}\|b'_{a,z}-b^\circ_{a,z}\|_\infty\leq2r_n,
\qquad
\max_{a,z}\|u'_{a,z}-u^\circ_{a,z}\|_\infty\leq2r_n.
\tag{6}
\]
The obstacle-stability theorem therefore yields
\[
d_H\{\mathcal T(b',u'),\mathcal T(b^\circ,u^\circ)\}\leq8r_n.
\tag{7}
\]
The gridded residual interpolants built from \(u'\) lie in the continuous obstacle for \(u'\).  In fact, on a cell \([r_j,r_{j+1}]\), each endpoint satisfies
\[
x_k\leq[u'_{a,z}(r_k)-Mh_n^\beta]_+\leq u'_{a,z}(r)
\quad(k=j,j+1),
\tag{8}
\]
because \(|u'_{a,z}(r_k)-u'_{a,z}(r)|\leq Mh_n^\beta\) and feasibility of a nonnegative \(x_k\) forces the positive-part upper bound to be nonnegative.  Linear interpolation preserves (8); its slopes lie in \([0,L]\).  Thus
\[
\mathcal T_{h_n}(b',u')\subseteq\mathcal T(b',u').
\tag{9}
\]
Equations (7)--(9) imply that every element of every expanded set inside the union defining \(\widehat H_n\) is within \(8r_n+e_{h_n}\) of \(\mathcal T(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\).  The true set is closed and convex by the law-sharpness theorem.  Distance to a convex set is convex: for finitely many \(y_k\), choose \(t_k\) in the true set arbitrarily close to each distance and observe that \(\sum_k\lambda_kt_k\) remains in the true set.  Distance is also one-Lipschitz, so passage to the closed convex hull preserves the same bound.  Intersecting with \(\mathcal K_{\mathsf R,M,L,\beta}\) cannot increase the supremum.  Therefore, on \(\mathcal E_{B,n}\),
\[
\sup_{\widehat g\in\widehat{\mathcal T}_{n,h_n}^{+}}
\inf_{g\in\mathcal T(P_{\mathrm{obs}})}\|\widehat g-g\|_\infty
\leq8r_n+2(Lh_n+Mh_n^\beta)=E_{\mathcal T,n}.
\tag{10}
\]
No contact-set, active-face, or differentiability condition entered (6)--(10).

We now construct baseline-law coverage.  Let \(\gamma_{0,z}=P_X(Z=z)\) and retain \(\widehat\gamma_z\) from the definition.  Independent sampling gives
\[
E_P\|\widehat\gamma-\gamma_0\|_2^2
=\sum_z\operatorname{Var}_P(\widehat\gamma_z)
=\frac{1-\sum_z\gamma_{0,z}^2}{n}
\leq\frac1n.
\tag{11}
\]
Since \(\|x\|_1\leq\sqrt{|\mathcal Z|}\|x\|_2\), and since \(E[Y]\geq tP(Y>t)\) for every nonnegative \(Y\), equation (11) gives
\[
P\!\left\{\|\widehat\gamma-\gamma_0\|_1>
2\sqrt{\frac{|\mathcal Z|}{n\alpha}}\right\}
\leq
P\!\left\{\|\widehat\gamma-\gamma_0\|_2^2>\frac4{n\alpha}\right\}
\leq\frac\alpha4.
\tag{12}
\]
Call the complementary event \(\mathcal E_{Z,n}\).  On \(\mathcal E_{B,n}\cap\mathcal E_{Z,n}\), the true pair \((\gamma_0,p_0)\) is admitted by the preliminary baseline-law region, hence
\[
P_X\in\widehat{\mathcal P}_{X,n}.
\tag{13}
\]

Define the total-variation norm by
\[
\|Q-P_X\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
=\sup_{|\varphi|\leq1}\left|\int\varphi\,d(Q-P_X)\right|.
\tag{14}
\]
Every \(Q\in\widehat{\mathcal P}_{X,n}\) on the joint event has the form \(Q(\{z\}\times dr)=\gamma_zp'_z(r)\,dr\).  The triangle inequality inside the two probability simplices, band diameter, and the normalization of each conditional density give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|Q-P_X\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
&\leq\sum_z|\gamma_z-\gamma_{0,z}|\int_0^1p'_z(r)\,dr
+\sum_z\gamma_{0,z}\int_0^1|p'_z(r)-p^\circ_z(r)|\,dr\\
&\leq2\varepsilon_{Z,n}+2r_n
=\Delta_{X,n}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]
Here the factor two in the first term follows because both \(\gamma\) and \(\gamma_0\) lie in the \(\varepsilon_{Z,n}\)-ball about \(\widehat\gamma\).

Combining (2) and (12) by the union bound proves that (5), (10), (13), and (15) hold simultaneously with probability at least \(1-\alpha/2-o(1)\), uniformly over the certified subclass.

It remains to verify the leaf assertions.  Fix \(G\in\mathcal G_n\).  By eligibility, \(P_X(G)\geq p_{\min,n}>0\).  Equations (5) and (13) show that every \(g\in\mathcal T(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\), paired with \(P_X\), is feasible for the interval program because \(P_X(G)\geq p_{\min,n}/2\).  Taking the infimum and supremum therefore gives
\[
[\underline\theta_G,\overline\theta_G]\subseteq\widehat I_G.
\tag{16}
\]

For the excess bound, take any feasible \((\widehat g,Q)\).  By (10), for every \(\epsilon>0\) there is \(g^0\in\mathcal T(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\) with \(\|\widehat g-g^0\|_\infty\leq E_{\mathcal T,n}+\epsilon\).  Put \(D=Q(G)\), \(D_0=P_X(G)\), and \(N_Q=\int_Gg^0\,dQ\), \(N_0=\int_Gg^0\,dP_X\).  Since \(D\geq p_{\min,n}/2\), \(D_0\geq p_{\min,n}\), and \(\|g^0\|_\infty\leq\mathsf R\), (14)--(15) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
|\ell_G(\widehat g;Q)-\ell_G(g^0;P_X)|
&\leq\|\widehat g-g^0\|_\infty
+\frac{|N_Q-N_0|}{D}
+\frac{|N_0|\,|D-D_0|}{DD_0}\\
&\leq E_{\mathcal T,n}+\epsilon
+\frac{2\mathsf R\Delta_{X,n}}{D}\\
&\leq E_{\mathcal T,n}+\epsilon
+\frac{4\mathsf R\Delta_{X,n}}{p_{\min,n}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
Letting \(\epsilon\downarrow0\), then taking the infimum and supremum over feasible pairs in (17), proves the simultaneous endpoint-excess inequality.  Because (16) holds for every eligible leaf on one event, substituting any data-measurable \(\widehat G\in\mathcal G_n\) proves the post-selection assertion without conditioning.

The analysis-mesh definition gives \(Lh_n+Mh_n^\beta=O(r_n)\).  Hence (10) is \(O(r_n)\) and tends to zero under \(r_n\to0\).  Since \(|\mathcal Z|\) and \(\alpha\) are fixed, \(\varepsilon_{Z,n}=O(n^{-1/2})\); (17) therefore shrinks under the stated condition \((r_n+n^{-1/2})/p_{\min,n}\to0\).  This is deliberately conservative relative to the desired local-mass term.

Finally, we prove that the remaining derivative-aware claim is not a consequence of the current score premises.  Consider the following allowed instance, taking admissible constants with \(M\geq1\) and \(c_p\leq1\leq C_p\).  Let \(\mathcal Z=\{z_0\}\), let \(R\) be uniform on \([0,1]\), draw \(A\) independently with probability \(1/2\) in each arm, and set \(T(0)=T(1)=0\) and \(C(0)=C(1)=t_{\max}+1\).  Then \(p_{z_0}=1\), \(b_{a,z_0}=u_{a,z_0}=0\), treatment overlap holds because \(\varepsilon<1/2\), uncensored overlap holds with probability one, and all residual and observed-law smoothness conditions hold.  Use the predeclared leaf \(G=\{(z_0,r):r\leq1/2\}\), with \(p_{\min,n}\leq1/2\); its singleton class has finite VC dimension.  An oracle singleton nuisance band satisfies the external band prerequisites.  For every index in \(\mathcal D_n\times\mathcal G_n\), set \(\psi\equiv0\) and take \(F_\psi\equiv0\).  The class is centered, its \((2+\delta_\psi)\)-moment is zero, and its covering number is one, so the score-envelope, score-moment, and score-entropy assumptions all hold.  Nevertheless, for every odd \(n\),
\[
\widehat P_n(G)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf1\{R_i\leq1/2\}\neq\frac12=P_X(G)
\quad\text{for every sample},
\tag{18}
\]
whereas every multiplier process formed from the declared zero scores, and hence every one-sided or two-sided multiplier quantile of that process, is identically zero.  Thus those score premises alone cannot calibrate even the nonzero leaf-mass error in (18), much less an optimized endpoint through a changing critical cone.  An explicit uniform score-to-statistic expansion with a controlled remainder and a conditional multiplier approximation are logically additional premises.  This counterexample establishes the claimed obstruction while (1)--(17) close the contact-stable whole-set and conservative leaf parts.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The result closes the changing-contact whole-set problem by a direct robust-band construction and supplies valid simultaneous post-selection leaf intervals without pretending that the undeclared score expansion has been proved. \textit{Gap.} Existing directional-delta and support-function methods do not themselves connect the frozen score class to these optimized statistics. The direct stability construction bypasses that missing link for whole-set coverage, while an explicit zero-score witness proves why efficient local-mass multiplier calibration remains unavailable from the current premises. \textit{Consumer.} Trial analysts obtain an outer-probability-valid, contact-stable confidence superset and conservative simultaneous genomic-leaf sign intervals; a sharper ADJUVANT analysis must additionally certify the efficient score expansion and multiplier approximation.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The completion uses the convention \(\Delta=\mathbf 1\{C\le T\}\), so assigning \(T=C\) to the non-maximal branch remains recorded as dropout. If a data system codes ties as observed events, exact attainment at zero residual may fail and the sharp object can become a closure; that alternative convention must be audited before application.

\section{Honest scope}

Population claims are exact but class-specific. The maintained monotone-Lipschitz compatible-law class has the law-sharp curve set and exact \(\Gamma_Lu\) pointwise projection; the full Wang-compatible class has the measurable endpoint-selection box modulo \(P_X\)-null sets, with \(\mathcal B(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\) as its continuous slice. The finite-\(L\) and monotonicity restrictions are novel identifying sensitivities, while score density and known-\(\beta\) Hölder regularity are explicit regularity conditions. No nuisance band is derived from those conditions: inference is uniform only over the externally certified subclass. On the band event, the proved whole-set superset covers and has directed excess at most \(8r_n+2(Lh_n+Mh_n^\beta)\), uniformly through changing contacts. Conservative leaf intervals additionally use a finite-multinomial profile-mass region; their displayed excess shrinks under \((r_n+n^{-1/2})/p_{\min,n}\to0\), which is stronger than the desired local-mass regime. The current envelope, moment, and entropy assumptions do not imply a statistic expansion or conditional multiplier approximation, so no efficient \((np_{\min,n})^{-1/2}\) leaf claim is made. Post-selection validity is deterministic on the simultaneous event. An ADJUVANT sign reanalysis still requires individual trial records, a genuinely predeclared score, eligible leaf mass, and a defensible sensitivity value \(L\); absent those inputs, no empirical subgroup sign is asserted.
