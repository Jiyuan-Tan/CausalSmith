%%% FIELD proof-support-flow
Fix \(v\in\mathbb B_*\).  All the integrals below are finite: each
\(v_z\) has finite total variation, while the functions \(b_{a,z}\) and
every member of \(\mathcal S_{a,z}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\) are continuous on
the compact interval \([0,1]\).  By the definitions of the sharp curve
set and its support function,
\[
\begin{aligned}
h_{\mathcal T}(v)
&=\sup_{\substack{f_{a,z}\in\mathcal S_{a,z}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\\
                   a\in\{0,1\},\ z\in\mathcal Z}}
 \sum_{z\in\mathcal Z}\int
 \{b_{1,z}-b_{0,z}+f_{1,z}-f_{0,z}\}\,dv_z \\
&=\sum_{z\in\mathcal Z}\int(b_{1,z}-b_{0,z})\,dv_z
  +\sup_{(f_{a,z})}\sum_{z\in\mathcal Z}
       \left\{\int f_{1,z}\,dv_z+\int f_{0,z}\,d(-v_z)\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The feasible family is a finite Cartesian product of nonempty sets, because
the zero function belongs to every obstacle set.  Consequently the last
supremum is the sum of the coordinatewise suprema: the inequality from left
to right is immediate, and the reverse inequality follows by choosing, for
each of the finitely many coordinates, a feasible function within
\(\epsilon/(2|\mathcal Z|)\) of its supremum and then sending
\(\epsilon\downarrow0\).  The definition of \(V_{a,z}\) therefore gives
\[
h_{\mathcal T}(v)
=\sum_z\int(b_{1,z}-b_{0,z})\,dv_z
 +\sum_z\{V_{1,z}(v_z;u)+V_{0,z}(-v_z;u)\}.
\]
This proves the continuous support decomposition.

Fix now one arm-profile pair \((a,z)\), put
\(w_j=\int\phi_{j,h}\,dv_z\), and put
\(\bar u_j=[u_{a,z}(r_j)-Mh^\beta]_+\).  Linearity of integration and the
definition of \(\Phi_h\) yield
\[
 \int\Phi_hx\,dv_z=\sum_{j=0}^m w_jx_j.
\]
Thus the grid support problem is the finite linear program
\[
 p^*=\max_x\left\{\sum_{j=0}^m w_jx_j:
 0\le x_j\le\bar u_j,\quad
 0\le x_{j+1}-x_j\le Lh\ (0\le j<m)\right\}.
\]
Its feasible polytope is nonempty, since it contains \(x=0\), and compact,
since \(0\le x_j\le\bar u_j<\infty\).  Hence it has a maximizer \(x^*\).

For completeness, we derive the specialized dual equality.  Write the four
families of inequalities as
\[
 x_j-\bar u_j\le0,\qquad -x_j\le0,\qquad
 x_{j+1}-x_j-Lh\le0,\qquad x_j-x_{j+1}\le0.
\]
At \(x^*\), the vector \(w=(w_0,\ldots,w_m)\) is a nonnegative linear
combination of the normals of the active inequalities.  Indeed, if it were
not, finite-dimensional separation from the finitely generated cone of
active normals would give a vector \(y\) whose inner product with every
active normal is nonpositive but with \(w^Ty>0\).  Every inactive inequality
has strictly positive slack, so \(x^*+ty\) would remain feasible for all
sufficiently small \(t>0\), contradicting
\(w^T(x^*+ty)>w^Tx^*\).  Denote the resulting nonnegative coefficients by
\(\lambda_j^+,\lambda_j^-,g_j,d_j\), in the order displayed above, and
extend the arc variables by
\(g_{-1}=g_m=d_{-1}=d_m=0\).  Equating the coefficient of \(x_j\) gives
\[
 \lambda_j^+-\lambda_j^-+g_{j-1}-g_j+d_j-d_{j-1}=w_j,
 \qquad j=0,\ldots,m.
\]
Hence \(d_h=(\lambda^+,\lambda^-,g,d)\) belongs to
\(\mathcal D_h(w,\bar u)\).  Because a multiplier can be positive only for
an active constraint,
\[
\begin{aligned}
p^*=w^Tx^*
 &=\sum_{j=0}^m\bar u_j\lambda_j^+
   +Lh\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}g_j.
\end{aligned}
\]
Conversely, for every primal-feasible \(x\) and every
\(d_h\in\mathcal D_h(w,\bar u)\), the balance equations imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_jw_jx_j
&=\sum_j(\lambda_j^+-\lambda_j^-)x_j
 +\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}g_j(x_{j+1}-x_j)
 +\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}d_j(x_j-x_{j+1})\\
&\le \sum_j\bar u_j\lambda_j^+
       +Lh\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}g_j,
\end{aligned}
\]
using respectively \(x_j\le\bar u_j\), \(x_j\ge0\),
\(x_{j+1}-x_j\le Lh\), and \(x_{j+1}-x_j\ge0\).  This is weak duality;
the multipliers constructed at \(x^*\) attain equality, proving the displayed
minimum formula.

The same equations are node-balance equations on the path
\(0,1,\ldots,m\): \(g_j\) and \(d_j\) are the two oriented arc flows, while
\(\lambda_j^+\) and \(\lambda_j^-\) are nonnegative node transactions.
The only nonzero arc capacity cost is \(Lh\sum_jg_j\), and the upper-obstacle
transactions cost \(\sum_j\bar u_j\lambda_j^+\).  A feasible primal vector
and feasible dual flow with the common displayed objective are therefore a
finite, directly checkable min-cost-transshipment certificate.

%%% FIELD proof-grid-hausdorff
Set \(\delta_h=Mh^\beta\).  We first verify the inner inclusion one
arm-profile pair at a time.  Let \(\Phi_hx\in\mathcal S^h_{a,z}\).  On each
interval \([r_j,r_{j+1}]\), its slope is
\((x_{j+1}-x_j)/h\in[0,L]\).  Hence \(\Phi_hx\) is nonnegative,
nondecreasing, and \(L\)-Lipschitz.  If
\(r\in[r_j,r_{j+1}]\) and \(k\in\{j,j+1\}\), the H\"older condition on
\(u_{a,z}\) gives
\[
 u_{a,z}(r)\ge u_{a,z}(r_k)-M|r-r_k|^\beta
             \ge u_{a,z}(r_k)-\delta_h.
\]
Because \(u_{a,z}(r)\ge0\), this implies
\[
 x_k\le [u_{a,z}(r_k)-\delta_h]_+\le u_{a,z}(r).
\]
The interpolant is a convex combination of \(x_j\) and \(x_{j+1}\), so
\(\Phi_hx(r)\le u_{a,z}(r)\).  All defining obstacle inequalities therefore
hold, proving
\(\mathcal S^h_{a,z}\subseteq\mathcal S_{a,z}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\).
Taking the finite product over \((a,z)\) and then applying the affine map in
the definition of the curve sets proves
\[
 \mathcal T_h(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\subseteq\mathcal T(P_{\mathrm{obs}}).
\]

For the reverse approximation, fix
\(f\in\mathcal S_{a,z}(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\) and define the nodal vector
\[
 x_j=[f(r_j)-\delta_h]_+,
 \qquad j=0,\ldots,m.
\]
Since \(f(r_j)\le u_{a,z}(r_j)\),
\(x_j\le[u_{a,z}(r_j)-\delta_h]_+\).  The map
\(q(t)=[t-\delta_h]_+\) is nondecreasing and one-Lipschitz.  The monotonicity
and Lipschitz restrictions on \(f\) therefore imply
\[
 0\le x_{j+1}-x_j
 \le f(r_{j+1})-f(r_j)
 \le Lh.
\]
Thus \(\Phi_hx\in\mathcal S^h_{a,z}\).  Moreover,
\(|f(r_j)-x_j|\le\delta_h\) at every node.  Writing
\(r=(1-t)r_j+tr_{j+1}\), \(0\le t\le1\), and using the
\(L\)-Lipschitz property twice gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|f(r)-\{(1-t)f(r_j)+tf(r_{j+1})\}\right|
&\le(1-t)Lth+tL(1-t)h\\
&=2t(1-t)Lh\le Lh.
\end{aligned}
\]
The nodal clipping contributes at most \(\delta_h\), whence
\[
 \|f-\Phi_hx\|_\infty\le Lh+Mh^\beta.
\]

Now take any
\(g=(b_{1,z}-b_{0,z}+f_{1,z}-f_{0,z})_z\in\mathcal T(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\).
Apply the preceding construction separately to every \(f_{a,z}\), obtaining
\(f^h_{a,z}\in\mathcal S^h_{a,z}\), and define
\(g^h=(b_{1,z}-b_{0,z}+f^h_{1,z}-f^h_{0,z})_z\).  Then
\(g^h\in\mathcal T_h(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\) and
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|g-g^h\|_\infty
&\le\max_z\left(
 \|f_{1,z}-f^h_{1,z}\|_\infty+
 \|f_{0,z}-f^h_{0,z}\|_\infty\right)\\
&\le2(Lh+Mh^\beta).
\end{aligned}
\]
The inner inclusion makes the directed distance from
\(\mathcal T_h(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\) to \(\mathcal T(P_{\mathrm{obs}})\)
zero, and the last display bounds the opposite directed distance.  Their
maximum is the asserted Hausdorff bound.  Every inequality used only the
common constants \(L,M,\beta\), so the bound is uniform over
\(P\in\mathfrak P\).  The proof never divides by \(u_{a,z}\) and the clipping
map is valid when its argument is zero; hence intervals and isolated points
on which \(u_{a,z}=0\) require no separate positivity condition.

%%% FIELD proof-direction-net
Let \(E=\mathbb C_{\mathcal Z,h_n}\) with its inherited norm, and write
\(B_E=\{x\in E:\|x\|\le1\}\).  We first establish directly the support
representation of Hausdorff distance needed here.  If
\(d=d_H(K_1,K_2)\), then
\(K_1\subseteq K_2+dB_E\) and \(K_2\subseteq K_1+dB_E\).  Therefore, for
every \(v\in E^*\),
\[
 |h_{K_1}(v)-h_{K_2}(v)|\le d\|v\|_*.
\]
Conversely, put
\[
 q=\sup_{\|v\|_*\le1}|h_{K_1}(v)-h_{K_2}(v)|.
\]
If some \(x\in K_1\) were outside the closed convex set \(K_2+qB_E\),
finite-dimensional separation would give a nonzero \(v\in E^*\) such that
\[
 v(x)>h_{K_2+qB_E}(v)=h_{K_2}(v)+q\|v\|_*.
\]
After division by \(\|v\|_*\), this contradicts the definition of \(q\).
Thus \(K_1\subseteq K_2+qB_E\); interchanging the sets gives the reverse
inclusion.  It follows that
\[
 d_H(K_1,K_2)=
 \sup_{\|v\|_*\le1}|h_{K_1}(v)-h_{K_2}(v)|.
\]

For any set \(K\) contained in the closed radius-\(\mathsf R\) ball and any
\(v,d\in E^*\),
\[
 |h_K(v)-h_K(d)|
 \le\sup_{x\in K}|(v-d)(x)|
 \le\mathsf R\|v-d\|_*.
\]
Given \(v\) in the dual unit ball, the definition of the
\(\delta_n\)-net supplies \(d\in\mathcal D_n\) with
\(\|v-d\|_*\le\delta_n\).  Applying the preceding Lipschitz bound to both
sets yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
|h_{K_1}(v)-h_{K_2}(v)|
&\le |h_{K_1}(d)-h_{K_2}(d)|
   +|h_{K_1}(v)-h_{K_1}(d)|
   +|h_{K_2}(v)-h_{K_2}(d)|\\
&\le \max_{e\in\mathcal D_n}|h_{K_1}(e)-h_{K_2}(e)|
   +2\mathsf R\delta_n.
\end{aligned}
\]
Taking the supremum over the dual unit ball and using the established
support representation proves the claim.

%%% FIELD open-partial-inference
The changing-contact geometry itself admits the following uniform bound.
For two nonnegative capacities \(u,u'\), set
\(\rho=\|u-u'\|_\infty\).  If \(f\in\mathcal S(u)\), define
\(C_\rho f=(f-\rho)_+\).  The positive-part map is nondecreasing and
one-Lipschitz, so \(C_\rho f\) remains nonnegative, nondecreasing, and
\(L\)-Lipschitz.  Since \(f\le u\le u'+\rho\), one also has
\(C_\rho f\le u'\), and
\(\|f-C_\rho f\|_\infty\le\rho\).  Interchanging \(u,u'\) proves
\[
 d_H\{\mathcal S(u),\mathcal S(u')\}\le\|u-u'\|_\infty,
\]
with no condition on either zero-capacity contact set.  Consequently, if
\(\rho_b=\max_{a,z}\|b_{a,z}-b'_{a,z}\|_\infty\) and
\(\rho_u=\max_{a,z}\|u_{a,z}-u'_{a,z}\|_\infty\), applying
\(C_{\rho_u}\) separately in the two arms gives
\[
 d_H\{\mathcal T(b,u),\mathcal T(b',u')\}
 \le2\rho_b+2\rho_u.
\]
On the event in the external-band prerequisite, every admissible nuisance
element in the band is within \(2r_n\) of the true nuisance.  Hence the
closure of the union of its admissible obstacle images is within
\(8r_n\) of the true curve set.  Expanding each gridded image by
\(2(Lh_n+Mh_n^\beta)\), as justified by the grid theorem, gives an observable
robust-band whole-set superset that contains the true curve set on that event
and whose directed excess is at most
\(8r_n+2(Lh_n+Mh_n^\beta)\), before any optional direction-net remainder.
Thus changing and even newly appearing contact sets do not obstruct the
whole-set rate.

What remains unproved is the simultaneous multiplier and leaf projection
part.  The current premises bound only the envelope, moment, and covering
numbers of \(\Psi_n\); no declared equation connects an element of
\(\Psi_n\) to the estimation error of a support value, a leaf numerator, a
leaf mass, or an endpoint optimizer, and no premise asserts a uniform
linearization remainder or conditional multiplier coupling.  This is a
logical, not merely technical, omission.  For example, take the score class
to consist of the zero function for every index in
\(\mathcal D_n\times\mathcal G_n\).  It is centered, has zero envelope
moment, and has covering number one, so all three stated score conditions
hold.  Nevertheless, for an eligible leaf \(G\) with \(P_X(G)=1/2\) and an
odd sample size \(n\), the empirical leaf mass cannot equal \(1/2\), whereas
the multiplier supremum of the zero score class and its critical value are
identically zero.  Hence the declared score conditions alone do not control
even the leaf-mass error, much less the optimized leaf endpoints.  A complete
positive theorem must first define the support and leaf statistics and prove
a band-subclass-uniform score expansion for them, including the optimizer's
changing critical cone, and must then establish conditional multiplier
quantile validity for that expansion.  Neither required link is a consequence
of the frozen premises.
