Write \(\mu_a^R\) for the regression of arm \(a\) under a law \(R\in\{P,Q\}\).  We prove the two assertions separately.

For the first assertion, equality of the trajectory laws implies equality of the marginal laws of every observed context.  Hence
\[
P_X=Q_X=:\nu.
\]
For \(R\in\{P,Q\}\), let
\[
p_{t,a}^R(x)=\mathbb P_{R,\mathsf D}(A_t=a\mid X_t=x)
\]
be any measurable version of the marginal assignment probability.  The pair \((X_t,A_t)\) is an observed coordinate of \(O_{1:T}\).  Therefore equality of the trajectory laws gives, for every measurable \(C\subseteq\mathcal X\),
\[
\int_C p_{t,a}^P(x)\,\nu(dx)
=\mathbb P_{P,\mathsf D}(X_t\in C,A_t=a)
=\mathbb P_{Q,\mathsf D}(X_t\in C,A_t=a)
=\int_C p_{t,a}^Q(x)\,\nu(dx).
\]
Applying this identity to the positive and negative sets of \(p_{t,a}^P-p_{t,a}^Q\) shows that
\[
p_{t,a}^P=p_{t,a}^Q\qquad \nu\text{-almost everywhere}.
\]
Denote the common version by \(p_{t,a}\), and put \(s_a(x)=\sum_{t=1}^T p_{t,a}(x)\).  By the definition of exposure,
\[
s_a=s_a^{P,\mathsf D}\qquad \nu\text{-almost everywhere}.
\]

The bounded-mean assumption and the Gaussian reward assumption imply \(\mathbb E_{R,\mathsf D}|Y_t|<\infty\).  Thus, for a measurable \(C\subseteq\mathcal X\), conditional expectation under the Gaussian reward model yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{t=1}^T
\mathbb E_{R,\mathsf D}
 \bigl[\mathbf 1\{X_t\in C,A_t=a\}Y_t\bigr]
&=\sum_{t=1}^T
\mathbb E_{R,\mathsf D}
 \bigl[\mathbf 1\{X_t\in C,A_t=a\}\mu_a^R(X_t)\bigr]\\
&=\int_C \mu_a^R(x)s_a(x)\,\nu(dx).
\end{aligned}
\]
Indeed, the first equality uses
\(\mathbb E_{R,\mathsf D}[Y_t\mid H_{t-1},X_t,A_t]
=\mu_{A_t}^R(X_t)\), which follows from the conditional mean-zero Gaussian innovation in the reward assumption; the second equality uses the definition of \(p_{t,a}\).  The random variable inside each expectation is an observed measurable function of the trajectory.  Equality of the trajectory laws consequently gives
\[
\int_C s_a(x)\{\mu_a^P(x)-\mu_a^Q(x)\}\,\nu(dx)=0
\qquad\text{for every measurable }C\subseteq\mathcal X.
\]
Taking \(C\) successively to be the positive and negative sets of the integrable function
\(s_a(\mu_a^P-\mu_a^Q)\) proves
\[
s_a(x)\{\mu_a^P(x)-\mu_a^Q(x)\}=0
\qquad \nu\text{-almost everywhere}.
\]
The assumed strict positivity of \(s_a^{P,\mathsf D}=s_a\), for each \(a\in\mathcal A\), permits division pointwise off a common \(\nu\)-null set and gives
\[
\mu_a^P(x)=\mu_a^Q(x)
\quad\text{for both }a\in\mathcal A,
\qquad \nu\text{-almost everywhere}.
\]
It follows that \(\tau_P=\tau_Q\) \(\nu\)-almost everywhere.  The benchmark is the same under the two laws by the fixed-benchmark assumption, so the targeting-gain definition gives
\[
\theta(P)
=\int\{(\tau_P(x))_+-\pi_0(x)\tau_P(x)\}\,\nu(dx)
=\int\{(\tau_Q(x))_+-\pi_0(x)\tau_Q(x)\}\,\nu(dx)
=\theta(Q).
\]
This proves the positive-exposure direction without identifying either regression by definition.

We next verify every property of the announced converse witnesses.  Let \(P\) and \(Q\) have the common uniform context distribution and the arm means specified in the statement.  Since the declared domains give \(0<c_X\le 1\le C_X\), the uniform density satisfies the context-density bounds.  Give both laws conditionally independent \(\mathcal N(0,1)\) innovations at scale \(\sigma\), as specified; they then satisfy the Gaussian reward assumption.

The elementary inequalities
\[
|u_+^\beta-v_+^\beta|\le |u-v|^\beta
\quad(0<\beta\le1),
\qquad
\bigl|\|x-x_0\|_\infty-\|x'-x_0\|_\infty\bigr|
\le\|x-x'\|_\infty
\]
imply
\[
|\psi_{x_0,r_0}(x)-\psi_{x_0,r_0}(x')|
\le r_0^{-\beta}\|x-x'\|_\infty^\beta
\le r_0^{-\beta}\|x-x'\|_2^\beta.
\]
For completeness, the first displayed inequality follows by assuming \(u_+\ge v_+\), writing \(u_+=v_++(u_+-v_+)\), and using subadditivity
\((r+s)^\beta\le r^\beta+s^\beta\) for nonnegative \(r,s\), which follows from concavity of \(z\mapsto z^\beta\) when \(0<\beta\le1\).  Hence the perturbed arm under \(Q\) has Hölder seminorm at most \(\eta r_0^{-\beta}\le L\); all other announced mean functions are constant and therefore also obey the Hölder condition.

Because \(0\le\psi_{x_0,r_0}\le1\) and \(\Delta_\dagger+\eta\le2M\), every announced mean lies in \([-M,M]\).  Under either witness, the absolute treatment contrast is at least \(\Delta_\dagger\).  If \(\alpha>0\), then \(\Delta_\dagger\ge C_0^{-1/\alpha}\).  Consequently, for every \(u\ge0\), the event
\(\{0<|\tau_R(X)|\le u\}\) is empty when \(u<\Delta_\dagger\), whereas when \(u\ge\Delta_\dagger\),
\[
\mathbb P_R\{0<|\tau_R(X)|\le u\}
\le1\le C_0u^\alpha,
\qquad R\in\{P,Q\}.
\]
If \(\alpha=0\), the gap--margin-compatibility assumption reduces to \(C_0\ge1\), so the same probability is at most \(1\le C_0u^0\) for \(u>0\), while at \(u=0\) the event is empty.  Thus the local margin condition holds throughout its required range \(0\le u\le\delta_0\).  The nontrivial-margin-regime assumption supplies \(\alpha\beta\le d\).  We have therefore proved, rather than assumed, that
\[
P,Q\in\mathcal P_{\beta,\alpha}^{\mathrm{HM}}.
\]

It remains to establish observational equivalence and target separation.  The two laws have the same context kernel at every round.  Conditional on any common observed history and current context, they also use the same assignment kernel because \(\mathsf D\) is one fixed law-independent design.  If the selected arm is \(1-a\), its mean is \(M\) under both laws.  If the selected arm is \(a\) and \(x\notin B\), then
\(\psi_{x_0,r_0}(x)=0\), so again the two conditional reward distributions coincide.  Finally, when \(x\in B\), the design assigns arm \(a\) with probability zero by hypothesis.  Thus the conditional observed-data kernel at round \(t\) is the same under \(P\) and \(Q\).  Starting from the common law at the first round and iterating this equality for \(t=1,\ldots,T\) proves by finite induction that
\[
\mathbb P_{P,\mathsf D}^{T}=\mathbb P_{Q,\mathsf D}^{T}.
\]

Set
\[
g_{\pi}(z)=z_+-\pi z,
\qquad
w_a(x)=a\pi_0(x)+(1-a)\{1-\pi_0(x)\}.
\]
If \(a=1\), then
\(\tau_P=-\Delta_\dagger\),
\(\tau_Q=-\Delta_\dagger-\eta\psi_{x_0,r_0}\), and direct substitution gives
\[
g_{\pi_0(x)}(\tau_Q(x))-g_{\pi_0(x)}(\tau_P(x))
=\eta\psi_{x_0,r_0}(x)\pi_0(x).
\]
If \(a=0\), then
\(\tau_P=\Delta_\dagger\),
\(\tau_Q=\Delta_\dagger+\eta\psi_{x_0,r_0}\), and
\[
g_{\pi_0(x)}(\tau_Q(x))-g_{\pi_0(x)}(\tau_P(x))
=\eta\psi_{x_0,r_0}(x)\{1-\pi_0(x)\}.
\]
Combining the two cases with the targeting-gain definition yields
\[
\theta(Q)-\theta(P)
=\eta\,\mathbb E_{X\sim\operatorname{Unif}(\mathcal X)}
 \left[\psi_{x_0,r_0}(X)
 \{a\pi_0(X)+(1-a)(1-\pi_0(X))\}\right].
\]
The integrand is nonnegative because \(0\le\psi_{x_0,r_0},\pi_0\le1\), and its expectation is strictly positive by the stated benchmark-relevance hypothesis.  Since \(\eta>0\), the last display is positive, and therefore its value equals \(|\theta(P)-\theta(Q)|\), proving the claimed strict separation.
