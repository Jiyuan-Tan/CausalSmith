%%% FIELD cited-perchet-rigollet-statement
In the two-armed bounded-reward contextual-bandit model of Perchet and Rigollet, with \(\beta\in(0,1]\), \(\alpha>0\), their H\"older condition, their local margin condition, and a context density bounded above and below on \([0,1]^d\), Theorem 5.1 gives an explicit policy with expected regret at most a constant times
\[
T^{(\beta+d-\alpha\beta)/(2\beta+d)}.
\]
The paragraph immediately following that theorem states that, for two arms, the same order is minimax optimal up to a multiplicative constant.

%%% FIELD feasibility-statement
The local margin condition implies the global envelope
\[
P_X\{0<|\tau_P(X)|\leq u\}\leq\overline C u^\alpha
\quad\text{for every }u>0.
\]
There exist constants \(0<c<C<\infty\), depending only on the displayed class constants, such that
\[
cT^{r_{\beta,\alpha,d}}
\leq\rho_{\mathrm{feas}}(T;\mathcal P_{\beta,\alpha}^{\mathrm{HM}})
\leq CT^{r_{\beta,\alpha,d}}.
\]
In particular, \(d=1\), \(\beta=1\), and \(\alpha=1/5\) give \(r_{1,1/5,1}=3/5\), so
\[
W_{T,\delta}(\rho;\mathcal P_{1,1/5}^{\mathrm{HM}})=+\infty
\quad\text{whenever }\rho<cT^{3/5}.
\]

%%% FIELD proof-class-nonemptiness
We check each defining property.  By the declared domains, \(0<c_X\leq1\leq C_X\).  The uniform distribution on \(\mathcal X=[0,1]^d\) therefore has density \(p_X=1\) satisfying the density envelope in the definition of \(\mathcal P_{\beta,\alpha}^{\mathrm{HM}}\).  The construction gives conditionally independent Gaussian arm outcomes with variance \(\sigma^2\), so the Gaussian innovation condition holds.

We next verify that the constant means are admissible.  If \(\alpha=0\), then \(\Delta_\dagger=\Delta_0\leq2M\) by the gap--mean compatibility assumption.  If \(\alpha>0\), gap--margin compatibility gives
\[
C_0(2M)^\alpha\geq1,
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
C_0^{-1/\alpha}\leq2M.
\]
Together with \(\Delta_0\leq2M\), this yields
\[
0<\Delta_\dagger=\max\{\Delta_0,C_0^{-1/\alpha}\}\leq2M.
\]
Thus both constants \(-\Delta_\dagger/2\) and \(\Delta_\dagger/2\) lie in \([-M,M]\).  Constant functions have H\"older seminorm zero, so they satisfy the H\"older bound for the declared \(L>0\).  In either ordering of the two arms,
\[
|\tau_{P^\dagger}(x)|=\Delta_\dagger
\quad\text{for every }x\in\mathcal X,
\]
and therefore the fixed-gap condition follows from \(\Delta_\dagger\geq\Delta_0\).

For the margin condition, first suppose \(\alpha>0\).  If \(0\leq u<\Delta_\dagger\), then
\[
P_X\{0<|\tau_{P^\dagger}(X)|\leq u\}=0.
\]
If \(u\geq\Delta_\dagger\), then \(u\geq C_0^{-1/\alpha}\), and hence
\[
P_X\{0<|\tau_{P^\dagger}(X)|\leq u\}
\leq1\leq C_0u^\alpha.
\]
These two cases cover every \(u\in[0,\delta_0]\).  If \(\alpha=0\), gap--margin compatibility is \(C_0\geq1\); the same probability is zero at \(u=0\) and is at most \(1\leq C_0u^0\) for \(u>0\).  The assumed parameter regime supplies \(\alpha\beta\leq d\).  Hence \(P^\dagger\in\mathcal P_{\beta,\alpha}^{\mathrm{HM}}\).

It remains to check benchmark exposure.  Put
\[
G_+=\int_{\mathcal X}\{1-\pi_0(x)\}\,dx,
\qquad
G_-=\int_{\mathcal X}\pi_0(x)\,dx.
\]
Because \(\mathcal X\) has unit Lebesgue volume and \(0\leq\pi_0\leq1\), \(G_++G_-=1\).  If \(G_+\geq1/2\), the construction makes arm one superior, and the inferior-treatment mass of the benchmark is \(G_+\).  Otherwise it interchanges the means, makes arm zero superior, and the inferior-treatment mass is \(G_-=1-G_+>1/2\).  In both cases that mass is at least \(1/2\geq b_0\).  The benchmark is the same for all laws by the fixed-benchmark assumption.  Consequently
\[
P^\dagger\in\mathcal P_{\Delta_0,b_0}^{\mathrm{fg}}.
\]
Finally, \(\mathcal P_{\Delta_0,b_0}^{\mathrm{fg}}\subseteq\mathcal P_{\beta,\alpha}^{\mathrm{HM}}\) is immediate from the defining set comprehension.  The displayed witness proves that both classes are nonempty.

%%% FIELD proof-feasibility-boundary
For \(0<u\leq\delta_0\), the desired global margin inequality is exactly the local margin assumption and \(C_0\leq\overline C\).  For \(u>\delta_0\), probability is at most one, while
\[
1\leq\delta_0^{-\alpha}u^\alpha\leq\overline C u^\alpha.
\]
At \(u=0\) the event is empty.  Thus
\[
P_X\{0<|\tau_P(X)|\leq u\}\leq\overline C u^\alpha
\quad\text{for every }u\geq0.
\]

By the definitions of participant-welfare loss and the oracle policy, \(\mathcal R_T(P,\mathsf D)\) is precisely the contextual-bandit expected regret of \(\mathsf D\) at horizon \(T\).  Hence
\[
\rho_{\mathrm{feas}}(T;\mathcal P_{\beta,\alpha}^{\mathrm{HM}})
=\inf_{\mathsf D}\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\beta,\alpha}^{\mathrm{HM}}}
\mathcal R_T(P,\mathsf D)
\]
is the corresponding minimax regret.

For \(\alpha>0\), apply the cited two-arm result of Perchet and Rigollet after the following explicit noise-model transfer.  Their upper-bound proof uses the reward law only through deviations of empirical arm means inside predictable bins.  In the present model, if \(S_1<\cdots<S_s\) are successive predictable pull times of a fixed arm in a fixed bin, conditional independence of the Gaussian innovations gives, for every \(\lambda\in\mathbb R\),
\[
\mathbb E\!\left[
 \exp\!\left\{\lambda\sigma\sum_{j=1}^s\varepsilon_{S_j}\right\}
 \middle|\,X_{S_1},A_{S_1},\ldots,X_{S_s},A_{S_s}
\right]
=\exp(\lambda^2\sigma^2s/2).
\]
Therefore
\[
\mathbb P\!\left\{
 \left|s^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^s\sigma\varepsilon_{S_j}\right|>v
\right\}
\leq2\exp\{-sv^2/(2\sigma^2)\}.
\]
Replacing the bounded-reward confidence radius in adaptively binned successive elimination by \(\sigma\sqrt{2\log(cT/s)/s}\) preserves its elimination event.  On that event, a cell of side \(h\) is explored only while its gap is at most a constant times \(h^\beta\).  The global margin inequality just proved bounds the probability of those cells by a constant times \(h^{\alpha\beta}\).  At the terminal resolution
\[
h_T\asymp T^{-1/(2\beta+d)},
\]
the approximation contribution is
\[
T h_T^{\beta(1+\alpha)}
=T^{(\beta+d-\alpha\beta)/(2\beta+d)}.
\]
At coarser dyadic resolutions the analogous terms form a geometric sum because \(\beta+d-\alpha\beta>0\), which follows from \(\alpha\beta\leq d\) and \(\beta>0\).  The displayed Gaussian tail, union-bounded over the finitely many visited cells and stages, makes the complement contribute only a constant after increasing the confidence-radius constant.  This gives the required upper bound under fixed-scale Gaussian noise.

For the converse, use the two-arm hard family underlying the cited minimax statement and keep its context law and mean functions, after an affine rescaling into \([-M,M]\).  Replace its bounded rewards by Gaussian rewards with the same conditional means and variance \(\sigma^2\).  For two neighboring laws whose selected-arm means differ by \(v(x)\), the adaptive trajectory divergence is
\[
\frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\mathbb E\sum_{t=1}^T v(X_t)^2
 \mathbf 1\{A_t\text{ selects the perturbed arm}\}.
\]
This is bounded by a constant, depending only on \(\sigma\) and the fixed rescaling, times the squared-mean information used in the cited testing construction.  Thus the same Assouad comparisons retain a testing error bounded away from zero, while participant loss depends only on the unchanged conditional means.  The separation calculation in that construction is consequently unchanged up to a fixed multiplicative constant and gives the lower order \(T^{r_{\beta,\alpha,d}}\).

When \(\alpha=0\), gap--margin compatibility gives \(C_0\geq1\), so the margin restriction is void.  A direct binned argument supplies the same conclusion.  Partition \([0,1]^d\) into cubes of side \(h\), run a two-arm Gaussian successive-elimination rule separately in each cube, and use the preceding Gaussian tail bound.  H\"older approximation costs at most \(CTh^\beta\), while the sum of the gap-free two-arm learning costs is, by Cauchy--Schwarz and the density upper bound,
\[
C\sum_B\sqrt{\mathbb E N(B)}
\leq C\sqrt{h^{-d}T}.
\]
Choosing \(h\asymp T^{-1/(2\beta+d)}\) balances the two displays and yields \(CT^{(\beta+d)/(2\beta+d)}\).  Conversely, place one signed H\"older bump of height \(ch^\beta\) in each of order \(h^{-d}\) disjoint cubes under the uniform context law.  Neighboring sign vectors have Gaussian divergence at most \(CT h^{d+2\beta}\).  With \(h\asymp T^{-1/(2\beta+d)}\), the binary testing error in each coordinate is bounded below by a positive constant, and summing the regret \(ch^\beta\) over \(T\) rounds gives \(cTh^\beta=cT^{(\beta+d)/(2\beta+d)}\).  The margin condition is automatic because \(C_0\geq1\).

Combining the cases and adjusting constants over the finitely many small horizons proves
\[
cT^{r_{\beta,\alpha,d}}
\leq\rho_{\mathrm{feas}}(T;\mathcal P_{\beta,\alpha}^{\mathrm{HM}})
\leq CT^{r_{\beta,\alpha,d}}.
\]
If \(d=1\), \(\beta=1\), and \(\alpha=1/5\), then
\[
r_{1,1/5,1}=\frac{1+1-1/5}{2+1}=\frac35.
\]
For \(\rho<cT^{3/5}\), the budgeted design class is empty.  The definition of the width envelope then gives \(W_{T,\delta}(\rho;\mathcal P_{1,1/5}^{\mathrm{HM}})=+\infty\).

%%% FIELD proof-coverage-information-converse
Fix \(\mathsf D\), \(I_T\), and \(P,Q\) as in the statement, and abbreviate their trajectory laws by \(\mathbf P\) and \(\mathbf Q\).  Let
\[
A=\{\theta(P)\in I_T(O_{1:T})\},
\qquad
B=\{\theta(Q)\in I_T(O_{1:T})\}.
\]
Uniform honesty gives \(\mathbf P(A)\geq1-\delta\) and \(\mathbf Q(B)\geq1-\delta\).  Write \(V=\|\mathbf P-\mathbf Q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\).  Then
\[
\mathbf P(B)\geq\mathbf Q(B)-V\geq1-\delta-V,
\]
and hence
\[
\mathbf P(A\cap B)\geq1-2\delta-V.
\]

For completeness, the needed information inequality is derived rather than imported.  Let \(p,q\) be densities of \(\mathbf P,\mathbf Q\) with respect to \(\mathbf P+\mathbf Q\), and take a measurable set \(E\) attaining total variation, so that \(V=\mathbf P(E)-\mathbf Q(E)\).  Jensen's inequality applied separately on \(E\) and \(E^c\) gives the binary log-sum bound
\[
\mathrm{KL}(\mathbf P\Vert\mathbf Q)
\geq u\log\frac uv+(1-u)\log\frac{1-u}{1-v},
\qquad
u=\mathbf P(E),\quad v=\mathbf Q(E).
\]
For fixed \(v\in(0,1)\), subtract \(2(u-v)^2\) from the right-hand side.  The resulting function of \(u\) has value and first derivative zero at \(u=v\), and second derivative
\[
\frac{1}{u(1-u)}-4\geq0.
\]
Continuity treats the endpoints.  Therefore
\[
V\leq\sqrt{\mathrm{KL}(\mathbf P\Vert\mathbf Q)/2}.
\]
The assumed divergence bound and the definition of \(\kappa_\delta\) now imply
\[
V\leq\sqrt{\kappa_\delta/2}=\frac{1-2\delta}{2}.
\]
On \(A\cap B\), the reported set is a connected interval containing both \(\theta(P)\) and \(\theta(Q)\), so its length is at least their absolute difference.  Consequently
\[
\mathbb E_{P,\mathsf D}|I_T(O_{1:T})|
\geq |\theta(P)-\theta(Q)|\,\mathbf P(A\cap B)
\geq\frac{1-2\delta}{2}|\theta(P)-\theta(Q)|.
\]
This is stronger than the displayed maximum inequality because it already bounds the expectation under \(P\).

Suppose next that \(P_X=Q_X\).  At round \(t\), conditional on a realized history and current context, the context kernel is common and the assignment kernel is the same fixed kernel \(\mathsf D\).  These two factors therefore cancel from the likelihood ratio.  Conditional on \((H_{t-1},X_t,A_t)\), the remaining factors are Gaussian with common variance \(\sigma^2\) and means \(\mu_{A_t}^P(X_t)\) and \(\mu_{A_t}^Q(X_t)\).  Their conditional divergence is
\[
\frac{\{\mu_{A_t}^P(X_t)-\mu_{A_t}^Q(X_t)\}^2}{2\sigma^2}.
\]
Multiplying the sequential conditional densities, taking the logarithm, and then taking iterated expectation under \(P\) gives exactly
\[
\mathrm{KL}(\mathbb P_{P,\mathsf D}^{T}\Vert\mathbb P_{Q,\mathsf D}^{T})
=\frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\mathbb E_{P,\mathsf D}
\sum_{t=1}^T\{\mu_{A_t}^P(X_t)-\mu_{A_t}^Q(X_t)\}^2.
\]

For each feasible \(\mathsf D\) and each honest \(I_T\), take the supremum of the first bound over every admissible pair in the sequential modulus.  The left-hand side is bounded above by \(\sup_{R\in\mathcal Q}\mathbb E_{R,\mathsf D}|I_T|\).  Infimizing first over \(I_T\) and then over \(\mathsf D\) yields
\[
W_{T,\delta}(\rho;\mathcal Q)
\geq\frac{1-2\delta}{2}\Omega_{T,\delta}(\rho;\mathcal Q).
\]
If the design class is empty, both sides have the stipulated extended-real value and the conclusion holds by the same convention.

\max\{\Delta_0,\min(\delta_0,C_0^{-1/\alpha})\}<2M.

There exist constants \(c,C,c_1,C_1>0\), depending only on the displayed class constants and \(\delta\), such that for \(C_1\log T\leq\rho\leq c_1T\),

%%% FIELD proof-fixed-gap-frontier
We prove the proposed interior-regime statement.  First observe that every \(P\in\mathcal P_{\Delta_0,b_0}^{\mathrm{fg}}\) has one globally superior arm.  Indeed, \(\tau_P\) is continuous because it is the difference of two H\"older functions.  If \(|\tau_P(x)|<\Delta_0\) at one point, continuity produces an open neighborhood with the same strict inequality; the density lower bound gives that neighborhood positive \(P_X\)-mass, contradicting the almost-everywhere fixed-gap condition.  Thus \(|\tau_P|\geq\Delta_0\) everywhere.  Connectedness of \([0,1]^d\) and the intermediate value theorem then rule out both signs.

We next give one law-independent design and interval.  Put \(v_0=4(M^2+\sigma^2)\), and define
\[
e_T=\min\left\{\frac\delta2,\frac{\rho}{16MT},\frac14\right\},
\qquad
k=\left\lceil\frac{2v_0}{\Delta_0^2}
\log\!\left(\frac{1}{e_T}\right)\right\rceil.
\]
Choose the theorem constants so that the displayed range of \(\rho\) ensures \(k\leq T/2\) and \(2Mk\leq\rho/4\).  Such constants exist because \(k=O(\log T)\) uniformly over that range.  During the first \(k\) rounds assign each arm with probability \(1/2\), independently of the past, and define
\[
Z_t=2(2A_t-1)Y_t,
\qquad
\widehat s=\operatorname{sign}\!\left(k^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^kZ_t\right),
\]
with either deterministic tie rule.  Direct conditioning gives \(\mathbb E_PZ_t=\mathbb E_{P_X}\tau_P(X)\), whose magnitude is at least \(\Delta_0\) and whose sign is the globally optimal arm.  Moreover, the bounded signal part of \(Z_t\) lies in \([-2M,2M]\), while its Gaussian part has variance \(4\sigma^2\).  Hoeffding's elementary exponential-moment calculation for the bounded part and the Gaussian moment-generating function therefore give
\[
\mathbb E_P\exp\{\lambda(Z_t-\mathbb E_PZ_t)\}
\leq\exp(v_0\lambda^2/2).
\]
Chernoff's calculation then yields, uniformly in \(P\),
\[
\mathbb P_P\{\widehat s\text{ is wrong}\}
\leq\exp\{-k\Delta_0^2/(2v_0)\}\leq e_T.
\]

Let \(N=T-k\) and
\[
p=\min\left\{\frac14,\frac{\rho}{16MN}\right\}.
\]
In each remaining round, assign the arm declared inferior with probability \(p\) and the arm declared superior with probability \(1-p\), independently conditional on the current history and context.  Pilot regret is at most \(2Mk\leq\rho/4\).  On correct classification, tracking regret is at most \(2MpN\leq\rho/8\); on misclassification it is at most \(2MN\), and its expectation is at most \(2MNe_T\leq2MTe_T\leq\rho/8\).  Hence
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\Delta_0,b_0}^{\mathrm{fg}}}
\mathcal R_T(P,\mathsf D)\leq\rho/2<\rho,
\]
so the design is uniformly feasible.  Notice also that \(\rho\leq T\) and \(N\geq T/2\) imply
\[
Np\geq c_M\rho,
\qquad
c_M=\min\{(16M)^{-1},1/8\}>0.
\]

Use only the last \(N\) observations for inference.  If \(\widehat s=+1\), set \(g(x)=1-\pi_0(x)\) and
\[
U_t=g(X_t)\left\{\frac{A_tY_t}{1-p}-\frac{(1-A_t)Y_t}{p}\right\}.
\]
If \(\widehat s=-1\), set \(g(x)=\pi_0(x)\) and
\[
U_t=g(X_t)\left\{\frac{(1-A_t)Y_t}{1-p}-\frac{A_tY_t}{p}\right\}.
\]
Conditional on the pilot, inverse-propensity cancellation shows that \(N^{-1}\sum U_t\) is unbiased for the targeting gain corresponding to the selected sign.  On the event that the sign is correct, this is exactly \(\theta(P)\), by the targeting-gain definition.  Since \(0\leq g\leq1\) and \(\mathbb E(Y_t^2\mid X_t,A_t)\leq M^2+\sigma^2\),
\[
\mathbb E(U_t^2\mid\text{pilot})
\leq(M^2+\sigma^2)\left\{\frac1p+\frac1{1-p}\right\}
\leq\frac{4(M^2+\sigma^2)}{3p}.
\]
Define the connected interval
\[
I_T=\left[
\frac1N\sum_{t=k+1}^T U_t-r_T,
\frac1N\sum_{t=k+1}^T U_t+r_T
\right],
\qquad
r_T=\sqrt{\frac{8(M^2+\sigma^2)}{3\delta Np}}.
\]
Conditional Chebyshev gives error probability at most \(\delta/2\) for its center around the selected-sign target.  Combining this with the pilot error, which is at most \(e_T\leq\delta/2\), proves uniform \((1-\delta)\)-honesty.  Its deterministic length obeys
\[
|I_T|=2r_T\leq C\rho^{-1/2},
\]
which proves the upper bound by an explicit sample map.

For the converse, the interior condition permits a constant \(\gamma\in(\Delta_0,2M)\) such that either \(\gamma>\delta_0\) or \(C_0\gamma^\alpha\geq1\); when \(\alpha=0\), gap--margin compatibility supplies \(C_0\geq1\).  Choose the arm ordering for which
\[
\int_{\mathcal X}\{1-\pi_0(x)\}\,dx\geq\frac12
\quad\text{or}\quad
\int_{\mathcal X}\pi_0(x)\,dx\geq\frac12,
\]
respectively.  Let \(P\) have uniform contexts, Gaussian noise, and constant means \(\gamma/2\) for the superior arm and \(-\gamma/2\) for the inferior arm.  Let \(Q\) keep the superior mean fixed and lower the inferior mean by
\[
\varepsilon=\frac{c_*}{\sqrt\rho},
\qquad
c_*\leq\min\left\{\frac{2M-\gamma}{4},\sigma\sqrt{2\gamma\kappa_\delta}\right\}.
\]
The lower endpoint \(-\gamma/2-\varepsilon\) remains above \(-M\).  Both laws have constant gap at least \(\gamma>\Delta_0\), satisfy the margin condition by the defining property of \(\gamma\), and have benchmark inferior mass at least \(1/2\geq b_0\).  Thus both belong to the same fixed-gap class.

Under \(P\), every pull of the inferior arm costs \(\gamma\).  Feasibility of an arbitrary design therefore implies
\[
\mathbb E_{P,\mathsf D}N_{\mathrm{inf}}\leq\rho/\gamma.
\]
Only the inferior-arm mean differs, so the exact adaptive Gaussian identity gives
\[
\mathrm{KL}(\mathbb P_{P,\mathsf D}^{T}\Vert\mathbb P_{Q,\mathsf D}^{T})
=\frac{\varepsilon^2}{2\sigma^2}
\mathbb E_{P,\mathsf D}N_{\mathrm{inf}}
\leq\frac{c_*^2}{2\sigma^2\gamma}leq\kappa_\delta.
\]
The targeting-gain difference is \(\varepsilon\) times the selected benchmark weight, and is therefore at least \(\varepsilon/2\).  The coverage--information converse yields
\[
\sup_{R\in\mathcal P_{\Delta_0,b_0}^{\mathrm{fg}}}
\mathbb E_{R,\mathsf D}|I_T|
\geq\frac{1-2\delta}{4}\varepsilon
\geq c\rho^{-1/2}.
\]
Since the design and honest interval were arbitrary, infimizing proves the lower bound.

%%% FIELD proof-target-separating-bumps
We give a two-law finite subexperiment, which is enough for the statement.  Put
\[
g_+(x)=1-\pi_0(x),\qquad g_-(x)=\pi_0(x).
\]
Their Lebesgue integrals sum to one, so choose \(s\in\{+,-\}\) for which \(\int g_s\geq1/2\).  A standard finite family of shifted grids has the following elementary covering property: for every sufficiently small \(h\), one of the grids contains \(N\asymp h^{-d}\) disjoint axis-aligned outer cubes of side \(h\), with concentric core cubes of side \(h/2\), such that the union of the cores captures at least a fixed fraction of \(\int g_s\).  To see this, average the core indicators over shifts in one period; every point is in a core for the same fixed fraction of shifts, and then select a shift attaining at least the average.

Order those \(N\) core cubes by their integrals of \(g_s\).  For every integer \(m\leq c h^{\alpha\beta-d}\), choose the first \(m\), taking the geometric constant \(c\) small enough that \(m\leq N\).  Because the average of the largest \(m\) numbers is at least the average of all \(N\), the selected cores \(C_1,\ldots,C_m\), with outer cubes \(B_1,\ldots,B_m\), satisfy
\[
\sum_{j=1}^m\int_{C_j}g_s(x)\,dx\geq c_0mh^d
\]
for a constant \(c_0>0\) depending only on dimension.

For each \(j\), choose \(\phi_j:\mathcal X\to[0,1]\) equal to one on \(C_j\), equal to zero off \(B_j\), and satisfying
\[
|\phi_j(x)-\phi_j(x')|\leq C_dh^{-\beta}\|x-x'\|_2^\beta.
\]
For example, a clipped \(\beta\)-power of the distance to \(B_j^c\) has these properties; the inequality \(|u^\beta-v^\beta|\leq|u-v|^\beta\) for \(u,v\geq0\) proves the displayed bound.  The supports are disjoint, so \(\Phi=\sum_{j=1}^m\phi_j\) obeys the same bound up to a dimensional constant.

Let the common context law be uniform.  Choose a constant \(a_0>0\) so small that \(a_0C_d\leq L\), and then take \(h\) small enough that \(a_0h^\beta\leq M\).  If \(s=+\), define \(P\) by \(\mu_1^P=M\), \(\mu_0^P=-M\), and define \(Q\) by
\[
\mu_1^Q=M,
\qquad
\mu_0^Q=-M+a_0h^\beta\Phi.
\]
If \(s=-\), interchange the arm labels.  Thus exactly one, and in fact the inferior, arm changes on \(\bigcup_jB_j\); the change has H\"older amplitude of order \(h^\beta\).  The density, Gaussian, mean-bound, and H\"older requirements hold by construction.

Under \(P\), the absolute gap is \(2M\).  Under \(Q\), it lies in \([2M-a_0h^\beta,2M]\).  We verify the margin condition rather than infer it from the construction.  For \(u<2M-a_0h^\beta\), the small-positive-gap event is empty.  For \(2M-a_0h^\beta\leq u<2M\), it is contained in \(\bigcup_jB_j\), whose probability is at most
\[
m h^d\leq c h^{\alpha\beta}.
\]
If \(\alpha>0\), the last quantity is at most \(C_0M^\alpha\leq C_0u^\alpha\) for all sufficiently small \(h\); if \(\alpha=0\), it is at most \(1\leq C_0\) by gap--margin compatibility.  Finally, for \(u\geq2M\), probability is at most one and \(1\leq C_0u^\alpha\) by the same compatibility assumption.  These cases include the required interval \([0,\delta_0]\).  Thus \(P,Q\in\mathcal P_{\beta,\alpha}^{\mathrm{HM}}\).

The targeting-gain definition and the chosen ordering give, in either case,
\[
|\theta(P)-\theta(Q)|
=a_0h^\beta\int_{\mathcal X}g_s(x)\Phi(x)\,dx
\geq a_0h^\beta\sum_{j=1}^m\int_{C_j}g_s(x)\,dx
\geq c_1mh^{d+\beta}.
\]
The reverse bound \(|\theta(P)-\theta(Q)|\leq a_0mh^{d+\beta}\) follows from \(0\leq g_s,\phi_j\leq1\) and the volumes of the outer cubes.  Thus the separation is of order \(mh^{d+\beta}\).  Every summand is nonnegative, so there is no sign cancellation.

Only the selected inferior arm, call it \(a\), differs between the laws.  Applying the exact adaptive Gaussian likelihood calculation gives, for every non-anticipating \(\mathsf D\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{KL}(\mathbb P_{P,\mathsf D}^{T}\Vert\mathbb P_{Q,\mathsf D}^{T})
&=\frac{a_0^2h^{2\beta}}{2\sigma^2}
  \mathbb E_{P,\mathsf D}\sum_{t=1}^T
  \Phi(X_t)^2\mathbf 1\{A_t=a\}\\
&\leq \frac{a_0^2h^{2\beta}}{2\sigma^2}
  \mathbb E_{P,\mathsf D}N_a^{\mathsf D}\!\left(\bigcup_{j=1}^mB_j\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves every asserted property with constants independent of \(h,m,T\), once \(h\) is sufficiently small.

%%% FIELD partial-uniform-budget-saturation
Weak duality does follow from the frozen programs.  If \(z\) is primal feasible and \(\lambda_{\mathsf Q}\) is dual feasible, then for every active cell
\[
\kappa_\delta\lambda_{\mathsf Q}(\mathcal S_{\mathsf Q}(w))
\leq\int\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}z_{\mathsf Q,a}k_{\mathsf Q,a}(s)\,d\lambda_{\mathsf Q}(s)
\leq\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}z_{\mathsf Q,a}.
\]
Summing and taking the supremum and infimum gives \(\mathcal D(w)\leq\mathcal B(w)\).

There is also value equality at every fixed \(w>0\), without dual attainment, provided the phrase ``finite nonnegative measures'' includes finite atomic measures.  For one active cell put
\[
K=\{(k_{\mathsf Q,0}(s),k_{\mathsf Q,1}(s)):s\in\mathcal S_{\mathsf Q}(w)\},
\quad
v=\sup_{u\in\Delta_2}\inf_{k\in K}u\cdot k.
\]
Writing a nonzero primal vector as \(z=c u\), where \(c=z_0+z_1\) and \(u\in\Delta_2\), shows that the cellwise primal value is \(\kappa_\delta/v\), with the usual value \(+\infty\) when \(v=0\).  The coefficients are bounded by \(2M^2/\sigma^2\).  Finite-dimensional separation of the compact convex set \(\overline{\operatorname{conv}}K\) from lower coordinate boxes gives
\[
v=\inf_{\nu\in\mathcal P_{\mathrm{fin}}(K)}
\max_{a\in\mathcal A}\int k_a\,d\nu(k).
\]
Scaling an approximating finite-support probability \(\nu\) into a dual measure shows that the dual cell value is the same \(\kappa_\delta/v\).  The programs separate over the finitely many active cells, hence \(\mathcal D(w)=\mathcal B(w)\).

The remaining saturation and sequential-realization assertions do not follow from this fixed-\(w\) duality.  The map \(w\mapsto\mathcal B(w)\) is nonincreasing, but its generalized inverse only implies
\[
\mathcal B(w_n-)\geq n.
\]
To infer \(\mathcal B(w_n)\leq n\) one needs right closedness at \(w_n\); to infer a constant lower multiple of \(n\) one additionally needs a uniform bound on the downward jump at \(w_n\); and one needs \(w_n>0\) because \(\mathcal B\) was defined only for \(w>0\).  None of these properties is stated or proved for the nonconvex H\"older--margin alternative sets.  Finally, the handle does not specify predictable kernels, quota truncation under random cell arrivals, interval endpoints, or a coverage calculation, so it is not yet a sample-to-output map to which welfare or honesty can be applied.

The proved pieces give the following rigorous bracket in every feasible regime.  The targeting gain satisfies \(0\leq\theta(P)\leq2M\), because it is the pointwise gain of the better arm over a convex combination of the two arm means.  Thus, whenever \(\mathfrak D_T(\rho;\mathcal P_{\beta,\alpha}^{\mathrm{HM}})\) is nonempty, the constant interval \([0,2M]\) is honest and
\[
W_{T,\delta}(\rho;\mathcal P_{\beta,\alpha}^{\mathrm{HM}})\leq2M,
\qquad
\Omega_{T,\delta}(\rho;\mathcal P_{\beta,\alpha}^{\mathrm{HM}})\leq2M.
\]
Under the interior fixed-gap compatibility displayed in the proposed fixed-gap theorem, its two Gaussian witnesses also belong to the full H\"older--margin class.  For every design feasible over the full class, their welfare calculation and Gaussian divergence therefore give
\[
\Omega_{T,\delta}(\rho;\mathcal P_{\beta,\alpha}^{\mathrm{HM}})
\geq c\rho^{-1/2},
\qquad
W_{T,\delta}(\rho;\mathcal P_{\beta,\alpha}^{\mathrm{HM}})
\geq c'\rho^{-1/2}.
\]
Together with the feasibility result, this certifies
\[
c'\rho^{-1/2}\leq W_{T,\delta}(\rho;\mathcal P_{\beta,\alpha}^{\mathrm{HM}})\leq2M
\]
for \(\rho\geq\rho_{\mathrm{feas}}\), and value \(+\infty\) below feasibility.  In the early-kill case \(d=1\), \(\beta=1\), \(\alpha=1/5\), and feasible \(\rho\asymp T^{3/5}\), the compulsory lower branch is \(T^{-3/10}\).  What remains unproved is the gap-sensitive local lower rate and, more importantly, one explicit budget-feasible adaptive design and connected honest interval attaining the maximum of that local rate and the compulsory \(\rho^{-1/2}\) term.
