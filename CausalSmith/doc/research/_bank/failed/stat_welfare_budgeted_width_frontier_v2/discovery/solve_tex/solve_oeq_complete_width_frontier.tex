%%% FIELD def-local-alternatives
For a baseline law \(P\in\mathcal P_{\beta,\alpha}^{\mathrm{HM}}\), a cell \(\mathsf Q\in\mathscr Q\), and \(w>0\), define the complete coverage-separating local alternative set
\[
\mathcal S_{P,\mathsf Q}(w)
=\left\{P'\in\mathcal P_{\beta,\alpha}^{\mathrm{HM}}:
P'_X=P_X,\quad
\mu_a^{P'}(x)=\mu_a^P(x)\ \text{for every }x\in\mathcal X\setminus\mathsf Q\text{ and }a\in\mathcal A,\quad
|\theta(P')-\theta(P)|>\frac{2w}{1-2\delta}
\right\}.
\]
The active baseline--cell index set is
\[
\mathscr A(w)=\left\{(P,\mathsf Q)\in\mathcal P_{\beta,\alpha}^{\mathrm{HM}}\times\mathscr Q:
\mathcal S_{P,\mathsf Q}(w)\ne\varnothing\right\}.
\]
Thus every law with the same context marginal, the prescribed off-cell agreement, and coverage-calibrated target separation is included; no optimal-arm sign switch is required.
%%% FIELD def-local-kl
For \((P,\mathsf Q)\in\mathscr A(w)\), \(P'\in\mathcal S_{P,\mathsf Q}(w)\), \(a\in\mathcal A\), and \(x\in\mathsf Q\), define the pointwise Gaussian information coefficient
\[
k_a(P,P';x)=\frac{\{\mu_a^P(x)-\mu_a^{P'}(x)\}^2}{2\sigma^2}.
\]
An arm-specific local occupation profile is a family \((\nu_{P,\mathsf Q,a})_{a\in\mathcal A}\) of finite nonnegative Borel measures supported on \(\mathsf Q\).  Its allocated information against \(P'\) is the exact integral
\[
\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\int_{\mathsf Q}k_a(P,P';x)\,d\nu_{P,\mathsf Q,a}(x)
=\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\int_{\mathsf Q}
\frac{\{\mu_a^P(x)-\mu_a^{P'}(x)\}^2}{2\sigma^2}
\,d\nu_{P,\mathsf Q,a}(x).
\]
%%% FIELD def-cellwise-region
For each \((P,\mathsf Q)\in\mathscr A(w)\), define
\[
\mathcal R_{P,\mathsf Q}(w)=\left\{
(\nu_{P,\mathsf Q,a})_{a\in\mathcal A}:
\begin{array}{l}
\nu_{P,\mathsf Q,a}\text{ is a finite nonnegative Borel measure supported on }\mathsf Q\text{ for every }a,\\[2pt]
\displaystyle
\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\int_{\mathsf Q}
k_a(P,P';x)\,d\nu_{P,\mathsf Q,a}(x)\ge\kappa_\delta
\quad\text{for every }P'\in\mathcal S_{P,\mathsf Q}(w)
\end{array}
\right\}.
\]
%%% FIELD def-allocation-primal
For \(w>0\), define the worst-baseline occupation-measure primal value
\[
\mathcal B_{T,\rho}(w)
=\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\beta,\alpha}^{\mathrm{HM}}}
\inf_{(\nu_{P,\mathsf Q,a})}
\left\{
\sum_{\mathsf Q:(P,\mathsf Q)\in\mathscr A(w)}
\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\nu_{P,\mathsf Q,a}(\mathsf Q)
\right\},
\]
where the inner infimum is over profiles satisfying all three conditions
\[
(\nu_{P,\mathsf Q,a})_{a\in\mathcal A}\in\mathcal R_{P,\mathsf Q}(w)
\quad\text{for every }\mathsf Q\text{ with }(P,\mathsf Q)\in\mathscr A(w),
\]
\[
\sum_{\mathsf Q:(P,\mathsf Q)\in\mathscr A(w)}
\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\nu_{P,\mathsf Q,a}(E)
\le T P_X(E)
\quad\text{for every Borel }E\subseteq\mathcal X,
\]
and
\[
\sum_{\mathsf Q:(P,\mathsf Q)\in\mathscr A(w)}
\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}
\int_{\mathsf Q}|\tau_P(x)|\mathbf 1\{a\ne\pi_P^\star(x)\}
\,d\nu_{P,\mathsf Q,a}(x)
\le\rho.
\]
The infimum of an empty feasible set is \(+\infty\).  The measure domination is the total-horizon and stochastic context-arrival constraint; in particular it implies that every feasible objective is at most \(T\).
%%% FIELD def-allocation-dual
For \(w>0\), define the baselinewise Lagrange lower value
\[
\begin{split}
\mathcal D_{T,\rho}(w)
=\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\beta,\alpha}^{\mathrm{HM}}}
\sup_{\lambda,\zeta_P,\xi_P}
\Bigg\{&\kappa_\delta
\sum_{\mathsf Q:(P,\mathsf Q)\in\mathscr A(w)}
\lambda_{P,\mathsf Q}(\mathcal S_{P,\mathsf Q}(w))\\
&-T\int_{\mathcal X}\zeta_P(x)\,dP_X(x)-\rho\xi_P\Bigg\}.
\end{split}
\]
Here each \(\lambda_{P,\mathsf Q}\) is a finite nonnegative finitely supported measure on \(\mathcal S_{P,\mathsf Q}(w)\), \(\zeta_P:\mathcal X\to[0,\infty)\) is Borel measurable and \(P_X\)-integrable, and \(\xi_P\ge0\).  The supremum is subject to the single pointwise arm--context constraint
\[
\int_{\mathcal S_{P,\mathsf Q}(w)}
k_a(P,P';x)\,d\lambda_{P,\mathsf Q}(P')
\le
1+\zeta_P(x)+\xi_P|\tau_P(x)|\mathbf 1\{a\ne\pi_P^\star(x)\}
\]
for every active \((P,\mathsf Q)\), every \(a\in\mathcal A\), and \(P_X\)-almost every \(x\in\mathsf Q\).  The zero weights and zero prices are feasible.  This definition asserts neither equality with the primal value nor attainment of either supremum.
%%% FIELD def-implicit-width
Define the budget- and horizon-dependent implicit occupation width by
\[
w_{T,\rho}=\inf\left\{w>0:\mathcal B_{T,\rho}(w)\le T\right\},
\]
with the infimum taken in the extended nonnegative reals.  No power law, boundary closedness, or saturation identity is included in this definition.
%%% FIELD def-handle
The handle \(\mathsf H_{T,\rho}\) denotes the following unresolved realization program.  For levels \(w>w_{T,\rho}\) at which \(\mathcal B_{T,\rho}(w)\le T\), use approximately feasible baseline-indexed occupation profiles and, when useful, feasible dual prices from \(\mathcal D_{T,\rho}(w)\); then seek one law-independent predictable assignment rule whose expected occupations simultaneously realize the relevant profiles subject to random context arrivals and welfare cost, together with one \(P\)-independent Borel map from the observed trajectory to a connected honest interval.  The handle names this program only: it is not itself an assignment kernel, an estimator, or an interval procedure, and it assumes neither primal--dual equality nor attainment.
%%% FIELD oeq-uniform-proposed
For the baseline-indexed occupation-measure programs \(\mathcal B_{T,\rho}(w)\) and \(\mathcal D_{T,\rho}(w)\), determine whether there is a constant \(c>0\), independent of \(T\) and \(\rho\), for which the generalized inverse satisfies the boundary and no-overshoot relation
\[
\mathcal B_{T,\rho}(w_{T,\rho})\le T
\quad\text{and}\quad
\mathcal B_{T,\rho}(w_{T,\rho})\ge cT
\]
whenever \(w_{T,\rho}>0\), or characterize exactly the discontinuities and inactive baseline--cell pairs that prevent such saturation.  Determine separately whether strong duality holds at the relevant levels; neither strong duality nor primal or dual attainment may be assumed.  Finally, prove or disprove that the baseline-indexed occupation profiles can be jointly realized, up to universal constants, by one predictable law-independent design and one connected \(1-\delta\) honest interval with participant-welfare loss at most \(\rho\), expected width of order \(w_{T,\rho}\), and stochastic context-arrival capacities respected.
%%% FIELD oeq-complete-proposed
Determine from the primitive H\"older--margin geometry an explicit piecewise function of \((T,\rho,\beta,\alpha,d,\delta)\) and prove that both \(W_{T,\delta}(\rho;\mathcal P_{\beta,\alpha}^{\mathrm{HM}})\) and \(\Omega_{T,\delta}(\rho;\mathcal P_{\beta,\alpha}^{\mathrm{HM}})\) are equivalent to it up to constants throughout the feasible regime.  The derivation must evaluate the corrected baseline-indexed occupation relaxation, locate every budget branch and flattening threshold, and prove that one law-independent predictable design and one connected honest interval attain the upper side.  It must recover the proved \(\rho^{-1/2}\) fixed-gap edge, recover the fixed-design offline honest-width order when \(\rho\asymp T\), and settle the Gaussian early-kill case \(d=1\), \(\beta=1\), \(\alpha=1/5\), and \(\rho\asymp T^{3/5}\).  No rate, saturation property, strong-duality identity, or realization claim is assumed.
%%% FIELD lem-weak-statement
For every \(T\in\mathbb N\), \(0<\rho\le T\), and \(w>0\), the corrected occupation-measure Lagrange value obeys
\[
\mathcal D_{T,\rho}(w)\le\mathcal B_{T,\rho}(w).
\]
This weak-duality inequality requires neither strong duality nor attainment.
%%% FIELD lem-weak-proof
Fix a baseline \(P\in\mathcal P_{\beta,\alpha}^{\mathrm{HM}}\), a primal-feasible occupation family \(\nu=(\nu_{P,\mathsf Q,a})\), and dual-feasible \((\lambda,\zeta_P,\xi_P)\).  Write
\[
N_P=\sum_{\mathsf Q:(P,\mathsf Q)\in\mathscr A(w)}
\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\nu_{P,\mathsf Q,a}(\mathsf Q)
\]
and let \(C_P\) denote the welfare integral in the third constraint of \(\mathcal B_{T,\rho}(w)\).  The information constraint defining \(\mathcal R_{P,\mathsf Q}(w)\), integrated against the finite nonnegative measure \(\lambda_{P,\mathsf Q}\), gives
\[
\kappa_\delta\lambda_{P,\mathsf Q}(\mathcal S_{P,\mathsf Q}(w))
\le
\int_{\mathcal S_{P,\mathsf Q}(w)}
\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\int_{\mathsf Q}k_a(P,P';x)
\,d\nu_{P,\mathsf Q,a}(x)\,d\lambda_{P,\mathsf Q}(P').
\]
Because \(\lambda_{P,\mathsf Q}\) has finite support and every integrand is nonnegative, interchange of the finite sum and the nonnegative integrals is valid.  Summing over active cells and applying the pointwise dual constraint yields
\[
\begin{split}
\kappa_\delta\sum_{\mathsf Q}
\lambda_{P,\mathsf Q}(\mathcal S_{P,\mathsf Q}(w))
&\le N_P
+\sum_{\mathsf Q,a}\int_{\mathsf Q}\zeta_P(x)
\,d\nu_{P,\mathsf Q,a}(x)+\xi_PC_P.
\end{split}
\]
The context-arrival domination in the primal and nonnegativity of \(\zeta_P\) imply, first for nonnegative simple functions and then by monotone convergence,
\[
\sum_{\mathsf Q,a}\int_{\mathsf Q}\zeta_P(x)
\,d\nu_{P,\mathsf Q,a}(x)
\le T\int_{\mathcal X}\zeta_P(x)\,dP_X(x).
\]
The welfare constraint gives \(C_P\le\rho\), and \(\xi_P\ge0\).  Rearrangement therefore gives
\[
\kappa_\delta\sum_{\mathsf Q}
\lambda_{P,\mathsf Q}(\mathcal S_{P,\mathsf Q}(w))
-T\int\zeta_P\,dP_X-\rho\xi_P\le N_P.
\]
Taking the supremum over dual-feasible variables, then the infimum over primal-feasible occupations, proves the baselinewise inequality; it remains valid when the primal feasible set is empty because its value is \(+\infty\).  Taking the supremum over \(P\) on both sides gives \(\mathcal D_{T,\rho}(w)\le\mathcal B_{T,\rho}(w)\), as claimed.
%%% FIELD lem-relaxation-statement
For every \(w>0\), if \(\mathsf D\in\mathfrak D_T(\rho;\mathcal P_{\beta,\alpha}^{\mathrm{HM}})\) and \(I_T\in\mathfrak C_{T,\delta}(\mathsf D;\mathcal P_{\beta,\alpha}^{\mathrm{HM}})\) satisfy
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\beta,\alpha}^{\mathrm{HM}}}
\mathbb E_{P,\mathsf D}|I_T(O_{1:T})|\le w,
\]
then the design-induced baseline-indexed occupation measures are feasible in \(\mathcal B_{T,\rho}(w)\), and hence \(\mathcal B_{T,\rho}(w)\le T\).  Consequently,
\[
\Omega_{T,\delta}(\rho;\mathcal P_{\beta,\alpha}^{\mathrm{HM}})
\ge\frac{2w_{T,\rho}}{1-2\delta}
\quad\text{and}\quad
W_{T,\delta}(\rho;\mathcal P_{\beta,\alpha}^{\mathrm{HM}})\ge w_{T,\rho},
\]
with the extended-real conventions in the definitions.  These are necessary relaxation bounds only; they assert no sequential realization of a baseline-specific feasible profile.
%%% FIELD lem-relaxation-proof
Fix a baseline \(P\), a cell \(\mathsf Q\), an arm \(a\), and a non-anticipating design \(\mathsf D\).  For every Borel \(E\subseteq\mathcal X\), define the design-induced finite occupation measure
\[
\nu^{\mathsf D}_{P,\mathsf Q,a}(E)
=\mathbb E_{P,\mathsf D}\sum_{t=1}^T
\mathbf 1\{X_t\in E\cap\mathsf Q,A_t=a\}.
\]
It is nonnegative, finite, and supported on \(\mathsf Q\).  Because exactly one arm is selected each round and the contexts have common marginal \(P_X\), for every Borel \(E\),
\[
\sum_{\mathsf Q:(P,\mathsf Q)\in\mathscr A(w)}
\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\nu^{\mathsf D}_{P,\mathsf Q,a}(E)
\le\mathbb E_{P,\mathsf D}\sum_{t=1}^T\mathbf 1\{X_t\in E\}
=T P_X(E).
\]
By the welfare-loss identity and nonnegativity of its summands,
\[
\sum_{\mathsf Q:(P,\mathsf Q)\in\mathscr A(w)}
\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\int_{\mathsf Q}
|\tau_P(x)|\mathbf 1\{a\ne\pi_P^\star(x)\}
\,d\nu^{\mathsf D}_{P,\mathsf Q,a}(x)
\le\mathcal R_T(P,\mathsf D)\le\rho,
\]
where the last inequality is the uniform budget condition defining \(\mathfrak D_T(\rho;\mathcal P_{\beta,\alpha}^{\mathrm{HM}})\).

Now take \(P'\in\mathcal S_{P,\mathsf Q}(w)\).  The laws have the same context marginal and their arm means agree off \(\mathsf Q\).  Therefore the exact adaptive Gaussian likelihood decomposition in the already-established coverage--information converse and the definition of the occupation measure give
\[
\mathrm{KL}(\mathbb P_{P,\mathsf D}^{T}\Vert\mathbb P_{P',\mathsf D}^{T})
=\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\int_{\mathsf Q}
k_a(P,P';x)\,d\nu^{\mathsf D}_{P,\mathsf Q,a}(x).
\]
Suppose the interval in the statement has worst-law expected length at most \(w\).  If the last display were at most \(\kappa_\delta\), the coverage--information converse would imply
\[
\max\left\{\mathbb E_{P,\mathsf D}|I_T|,
\mathbb E_{P',\mathsf D}|I_T|\right\}
\ge\frac{1-2\delta}{2}|\theta(P)-\theta(P')|>w,
\]
contradicting the assumed worst-law bound.  Hence the displayed information is strictly larger than \(\kappa_\delta\) for every local alternative.  The induced occupations belong to every required \(\mathcal R_{P,\mathsf Q}(w)\), obey the capacity and welfare constraints above, and have total mass at most \(T\).  Taking the inner infimum and then the supremum over baselines proves \(\mathcal B_{T,\rho}(w)\le T\).

It remains to derive the two numerical lower bounds.  If \(0<w_1<w_2\), then \(\mathcal S_{P,\mathsf Q}(w_2)\subseteq\mathcal S_{P,\mathsf Q}(w_1)\); restricting a feasible profile to the cells active at \(w_2\) shows that \(\mathcal B_{T,\rho}(w)\) is nonincreasing.  Also, the benchmark arm mean is a convex combination of \(\mu_0(x)\) and \(\mu_1(x)\), so the bounded-mean condition gives
\[
0\le\theta(P)\le2M.
\]
Thus \(\mathcal S_{P,\mathsf Q}(w)=\varnothing\) whenever \(w\ge M(1-2\delta)\), and consequently \(0\le w_{T,\rho}\le M(1-2\delta)<\infty\).

For any \(0<w<w_{T,\rho}\), monotonicity and the generalized-inverse definition give \(\mathcal B_{T,\rho}(w)>T\).  Every profile satisfying the context domination has total mass at most \(T\); hence for every uniformly budget-feasible design its induced profile must fail at least one information constraint.  There are therefore \(P\), an active \(\mathsf Q\), and \(P'\in\mathcal S_{P,\mathsf Q}(w)\) such that
\[
\mathrm{KL}(\mathbb P_{P,\mathsf D}^{T}\Vert\mathbb P_{P',\mathsf D}^{T})<\kappa_\delta,
\qquad
|\theta(P)-\theta(P')|>\frac{2w}{1-2\delta}.
\]
Taking the supremum over admissible pairs, the infimum over budget-feasible designs, and then the supremum over \(w<w_{T,\rho}\) gives
\[
\Omega_{T,\delta}(\rho;\mathcal P_{\beta,\alpha}^{\mathrm{HM}})
\ge\frac{2w_{T,\rho}}{1-2\delta}.
\]
If the design class is empty, the same inequality holds by the stipulated extended value \(+\infty\).  Finally, the already-established coverage--information converse yields
\[
W_{T,\delta}(\rho;\mathcal P_{\beta,\alpha}^{\mathrm{HM}})
\ge\frac{1-2\delta}{2}\Omega_{T,\delta}(\rho;\mathcal P_{\beta,\alpha}^{\mathrm{HM}})
\ge w_{T,\rho}.
\]
No step selects a baseline-independent occupation profile or constructs an interval, so the conclusion is only the necessary side of the relaxation.
%%% FIELD uniform-partial
The corrected programs admit two unconditional facts.  First, weak duality holds:
\[
\mathcal D_{T,\rho}(w)\le\mathcal B_{T,\rho}(w).
\]
Second, every budget-feasible design and honest interval whose worst-law expected length is at most \(w\) induces baseline-indexed occupation measures feasible for \(\mathcal B_{T,\rho}(w)\).  Hence
\[
\Omega_{T,\delta}(\rho;\mathcal P_{\beta,\alpha}^{\mathrm{HM}})
\ge\frac{2w_{T,\rho}}{1-2\delta},
\qquad
W_{T,\delta}(\rho;\mathcal P_{\beta,\alpha}^{\mathrm{HM}})\ge w_{T,\rho}.
\]
The remaining assertions are not consequences of weak duality: one still needs boundary closedness and no-overshoot control for \(w\mapsto\mathcal B_{T,\rho}(w)\), any desired strong-duality theorem, and a coupling of the baseline-specific profiles into one predictable law-independent design and one connected honest interval.
%%% FIELD complete-partial
Below the feasibility boundary, \(W_{T,\delta}(\rho;\mathcal P_{\beta,\alpha}^{\mathrm{HM}})=+\infty\).  In every feasible regime the constant interval \([0,2M]\) is honest, while the corrected occupation relaxation and the coverage converse give
\[
w_{T,\rho}
\le W_{T,\delta}(\rho;\mathcal P_{\beta,\alpha}^{\mathrm{HM}})
\le2M,
\qquad
\Omega_{T,\delta}(\rho;\mathcal P_{\beta,\alpha}^{\mathrm{HM}})
\ge\frac{2w_{T,\rho}}{1-2\delta}.
\]
Under the stated interior fixed-gap compatibility and for \(C_1\log T\le\rho\le c_1T\), monotonicity from the fixed-gap subclass to the full class and the proved fixed-gap theorem additionally give the compulsory lower branch
\[
W_{T,\delta}(\rho;\mathcal P_{\beta,\alpha}^{\mathrm{HM}})\ge c\rho^{-1/2}.
\]
Thus the certified lower envelope is the maximum of the implicit occupation obstruction and the fixed-gap obstruction, whereas only the constant upper bound is currently available over the full class.  In the early-kill specialization \(d=1\), \(\beta=1\), \(\alpha=1/5\), and \(\rho\asymp T^{3/5}\), feasibility is exactly at order \(T^{3/5}\) and the compulsory fixed-gap lower term is \(T^{-3/10}\).  Evaluating \(w_{T,\rho}\), locating the H\"older-local branches, and attaining them by one design and interval remain open.

Weak duality and the necessity of the corrected occupation relaxation are proved, including \(W_{T,\delta}\ge w_{T,\rho}\) and \(\Omega_{T,\delta}\ge2w_{T,\rho}/(1-2\delta)\).  What remains is boundary saturation, any stronger duality statement, and joint sequential realization.
