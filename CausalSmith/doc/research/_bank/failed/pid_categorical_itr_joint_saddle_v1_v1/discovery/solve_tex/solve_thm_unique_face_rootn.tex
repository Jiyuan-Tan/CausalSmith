%%% FIELD unique-face-rootn-proof
Fix a stratum \(x\) and a law \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\in
\mathfrak P_{\kappa,\varepsilon}\).  All vector norms are Euclidean and all
matrix norms are induced operator norms.  The fixed-support multinomial
tangent space is
\[
 \mathcal T_x(p_x)
 =\left\{h:
   \sum_{a\in\mathcal A,\,y\in\{0,1\}}h_{zay}=0
       \text{ for every }z\in\mathcal Z,\quad
   h_{zay}=0\text{ whenever }p_{xzay}=0
  \right\}.
 \tag{1}
\]
Neighborhoods and derivatives below are relative to the fixed-support face,
and hence all directions belong to \(\mathcal T_x(p_x)\).  The convention in
the corrected definition of \(\omega_x(p_x)\) sets its value to zero when this
tangent space is \(\{0\}\).

We first establish stability of the response-basis roster.  Put
\(r_B=\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde B)\), and let \(\mathfrak B\) be the
finite collection of \(r_B\)-element subsets \(\beta\subseteq\mathcal R\)
such that \(\widetilde B_\beta\) is nonsingular.  For
\(\beta\in\mathfrak B\), define
\[
 w_\beta(p_x)=\widetilde B_\beta^{-1}\widetilde p_x.
\]
The last coordinate of \(\widetilde p_x\) is the constant one.  Consequently,
for a fixed matrix \(D_\beta\),
\[
 w_\beta(p_x+h)-w_\beta(p_x)=D_\beta h.
 \tag{2}
\]
Because \(\mathfrak B\) is finite,
\[
 L_B:=\max_{\beta\in\mathfrak B}\|D_\beta\|<\infty.
 \tag{3}
\]
Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}response\mbox{-}fiber\mbox{-}compatibility\mbox{-}derived}\)
implies that \(\mathcal W_x(p_x)\) is nonempty.  It contains a basic feasible
point: choose a feasible \(w\) having support of minimum cardinality.  If the
columns of \(\widetilde B\) indexed by that support were dependent, a nonzero
null vector on the support would have both signs, because the last row of
\(\widetilde B\) is \(\mathbf 1^\top\).  Moving in one of its two signed
directions until a positive coordinate first becomes zero preserves the
equalities and nonnegativity and strictly reduces support, a contradiction.
Thus those columns are independent.  Extending them to \(r_B\) independent
columns, if needed, gives \(\beta\in\mathfrak B\) for which
\(w_\beta(p_x)\geq0\).  Hence
\[
 \mathcal B_x(p_x)\neq\varnothing.
 \tag{4}
\]

Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}response\mbox{-}basis\mbox{-}roster\mbox{-}margin}\)
and the definition of \(\zeta_x(p_x)\) state that every coordinate of every
nonsingular basic solution is separated from zero:
\[
 |w_{\beta,j}(p_x)|\geq\kappa>0
 \quad(\beta\in\mathfrak B,\ j=1,\ldots,r_B).
 \tag{5}
\]
Set
\[
 \rho_B=\frac{\kappa}{2\max\{L_B,1\}}>0.
 \tag{6}
\]
Equations (2), (5), and (6) show that every basic coordinate keeps its sign
when \(h\in\mathcal T_x(p_x)\), \(p_x+h\) remains in the same
fixed-support simplex face, and \(\|h\|<\rho_B\).  Therefore
\[
 \mathcal B_x(p_x+h)=\mathcal B_x(p_x).
 \tag{7}
\]
The radius is uniform over the asserted class: the finite matrices
\(D_\beta\) depend only on the fixed alphabets and response matrix, whereas
the lower bound \(\kappa\) is common to the class.

On the roster in (7), every regret row is affine in \(p_x\).  Indeed,
Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}reduced\mbox{-}saddle\mbox{-}lp}\) and (2) give
\[
 G_{(b,\beta),a}(p_x+h)
 =G_{(b,\beta),a}(p_x)
   +(e_b-e_a)^\top C_\beta D_\beta h.
 \tag{8}
\]
Write
\[
 \mathcal I=\mathcal I_x(p_x),\qquad
 \mathcal J=\mathcal J_x(p_x),\qquad
 H=H_x(p_x),\qquad u=u_x(p_x),
\]
and temporarily retain these active sets.  Let \(H_h\) be the matrix obtained
from Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}active\mbox{-}system}\) by inserting
\(G_{\mathcal I,\mathcal J}(p_x+h)\) while holding \(\mathcal I\) and
\(\mathcal J\) fixed.  With \(\Delta_h=H_h-H\), finiteness and (8) give a
constant \(L_H<\infty\), uniform over the class, such that
\[
 \|\Delta_h\|\leq L_H\|h\|.
 \tag{9}
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}active\mbox{-}system\mbox{-}rank}\) gives
\(\sigma_{\min}(H)\geq\kappa>0\).  Whenever
\(L_H\|h\|\leq\kappa/2\), for every conformable vector \(z\),
\[
 \|H_hz\|\geq\|Hz\|-\|\Delta_hz\|
 \geq(\kappa-L_H\|h\|)\|z\|
 \geq\frac{\kappa}{2}\|z\|.
\]
Thus
\[
 H_h^{-1}\text{ exists},\qquad \|H_h^{-1}\|\leq2\kappa^{-1}.
 \tag{10}
\]
The active-system right-hand side in Definition
\(\mathrm{def{:}active\mbox{-}system}\) is constant while the active sets are
fixed.  Define the candidate solution
\[
 u_h=H_h^{-1}g_x(p_x).
 \tag{11}
\]
The resolvent identity yields
\[
 u_h-u=-H_h^{-1}\Delta_hu,
 \qquad
 \|u_h-u\|\leq2\kappa^{-1}L_H\|u\|\,\|h\|.
 \tag{12}
\]
The vector \(u\) is uniformly bounded.  Its \(q\)- and \(\lambda\)-blocks
are probability vectors.  Since \(Cw\in[0,1]^K\) for every response law,
every entry of \(G_x\) lies in \([-1,1]\), so \(|v_x(p_x)|\leq1\).
The active stationarity equation gives
\(\eta_x=G_{\cdot,a}(p_x)^\top\lambda\in[-1,1]\) for any
\(a\in\mathcal J\).  All dimensions range over finite sets.  Hence the
right side of (12) has a class-uniform coefficient.

We now verify that (11) remains the complete saddle solution.  Assumption
\(\mathrm{ass{:}active\mbox{-}weight\mbox{-}margin}\), followed by (12) and
a uniform reduction of the neighborhood, gives
\[
 q_{h,a}\geq\frac\kappa2\quad(a\in\mathcal J),
 \qquad
 \lambda_{h,s}\geq\frac\kappa2\quad(s\in\mathcal I).
 \tag{13}
\]
Assumptions \(\mathrm{ass{:}primal\mbox{-}slack\mbox{-}margin}\) and
\(\mathrm{ass{:}reduced\mbox{-}cost\mbox{-}margin}\), together with (8) and
(12), similarly imply
\[
 v_h-G_{s,\cdot}(p_x+h)q_h\geq\frac\kappa2
       \quad(s\notin\mathcal I)
 \tag{14}
\]
and
\[
 G_{\cdot,a}(p_x+h)^\top\lambda_h-\eta_h\geq\frac\kappa2
       \quad(a\notin\mathcal J).
 \tag{15}
\]
Here \(q_h\) and \(\lambda_h\) are extended by zero off \(\mathcal J\) and
\(\mathcal I\).  The four block equations in
\(H_hu_h=g_x(p_x)\) are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 G_{\mathcal I,\mathcal J}(p_x+h)q_{h,\mathcal J}
     &=v_h\mathbf 1_{\mathcal I},\\
 G_{\mathcal I,\mathcal J}(p_x+h)^\top\lambda_{h,\mathcal I}
     &=\eta_h\mathbf 1_{\mathcal J},\\
 \mathbf 1^\top q_h&=1,\\
 \mathbf 1^\top\lambda_h&=1.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{16}
\]
Equations (13)--(16) are primal feasibility, dual feasibility, and
complementary slackness.  They also prove equality of the primal and dual
values directly:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 v_h
 &=\sum_s\lambda_{h,s}v_h
  =\sum_s\lambda_{h,s}G_{s,\cdot}(p_x+h)q_h\\
 &=q_h^\top G_x(p_x+h)^\top\lambda_h
  =\eta_h.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{17}
\]
For any primal-feasible \((q',t')\), (15)--(17) give the weak-duality chain
\[
 t'\geq\sum_s\lambda_{h,s}G_{s,\cdot}(p_x+h)q'
 =(q')^\top G_x(p_x+h)^\top\lambda_h
 \geq\eta_h\mathbf 1^\top q'=v_h.
 \tag{18}
\]
Thus \((q_h,v_h)\) is optimal.

It is the unique primal optimum.  Equality in (18) for another optimum would,
by positivity of \(\lambda_{h,s}\) on \(\mathcal I\), force all rows in
\(\mathcal I\) to bind; by the strict dual slacks in (15), it would force
\(q'_a=0\) off \(\mathcal J\).  Keeping the candidate dual block fixed, the
vector \((q'_{\mathcal J},t',\lambda_{h,\mathcal I},\eta_h)\) would solve
(16), and nonsingularity in (10) forces \((q',t')=(q_h,v_h)\).  Conversely,
(14) forces every dual optimum to vanish off \(\mathcal I\), and positivity
of \(q_{h,a}\) on \(\mathcal J\) forces stationarity equality there.  Keeping
the candidate primal block fixed again gives (16), so (10) forces uniqueness
of the dual block.  Therefore
\[
 \mathcal I_x(p_x+h)=\mathcal I,
 \qquad
 \mathcal J_x(p_x+h)=\mathcal J.
 \tag{19}
\]
The neighborhood is uniform: there are finitely many rosters and active-set
pairs; (8) contains only finitely many fixed derivative matrices; (10) is
controlled by the common \(\kappa\); (13)--(15) start from the four common
\(\kappa\)-margins; and \(u\) is uniformly bounded.  Together, (7) and (19)
prove the claimed local constancy and unique active-system representation.

On this neighborhood, \(E_x(p_x+h)=E_x(p_x)=:E\),
\(g_x(p_x+h)=g_x(p_x)=:g\), and \(H_x\) is affine by (8).  Hence
\(\Delta_h=DH_x(p_x)[h]\).  The identities \(H_hu_h=Hu=g\) give
\[
 u_h-u=-H^{-1}\Delta_hu_h
\]
and therefore
\[
 u_h-u+H^{-1}\Delta_hu=-H^{-1}\Delta_h(u_h-u).
\]
Using (9), (10), and (12), there is a class-uniform \(C<\infty\) such that
\[
 \left\|u_h-u+H^{-1}DH_x(p_x)[h]u\right\|
 \leq C\|h\|^2.
 \tag{20}
\]
Since \(T_x(p_x)=Eu_x(p_x)\), (20), applied to every tangential sequence,
proves Hadamard differentiability with
\[
 DT_x(p_x)[h]
 =-E_x(p_x)H_x(p_x)^{-1}DH_x(p_x)[h]u_x(p_x)
 =J_x(p_x)[h].
 \tag{21}
\]
It also proves the asserted structural distinction: \(g_x\) is constant on
the face, whereas the observable table enters the left-hand KKT matrix
through \(G_{\mathcal I_x,\mathcal J_x}(p_x)\).  Thus the derivative contains
the moving-coefficient term \(-E H^{-1}(DH)u\), not merely a perturbation of a
right-hand side.

We next prove the facet-normal claims.  Work in one fixed-support cell on
which the roster, \(\mathcal I\), and \(\mathcal J\) are fixed.  For
\(s=(b,\beta)\), set
\[
 m_\beta(p_x)=C_\beta\widetilde B_\beta^{-1}\widetilde p_x.
\]
Then
\[
 G_{s,a}(p_x)=m_{\beta,b}(p_x)-m_{\beta,a}(p_x),
 \qquad G_{s,b}(p_x)=0,
 \tag{22}
\]
and \(a_{x,s}(p_x)=(G_{s,\cdot}(p_x)^\top,-1)^\top\) is affine.  For a
tangent direction \(h\), if
\[
 (I-\bar a_{x,s}\bar a_{x,s}^\top)Da_{x,s}(p_x)[h]=0,
\]
then \(Da_{x,s}(p_x)[h]=c,a_{x,s}(p_x)\) for some \(c\in\mathbb R\).
The derivative has last coordinate zero, while \(a_{x,s}\) has last
coordinate \(-1\), so \(c=0\).  Hence
\[
 (I-\bar a_{x,s}\bar a_{x,s}^\top)Da_{x,s}(p_x)[h]=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 Da_{x,s}(p_x)[h]=0.
 \tag{23}
\]
On the fixed cell, the tangent space and the affine derivatives in (22) are
constant.  By the corrected definition of \(\omega_x(p_x)\), equation (23)
shows that \(\omega_x(p_x)=0\) exactly when every active normal has zero
derivative in every tangent direction.  Either this holds, in which case all
active normals are locally constant, which is stronger than constancy up to
positive scale, or it fails everywhere in the cell.  In the second case the
zero locus is empty, hence is the proper algebraic subset cut out by the
polynomial \(1\).  This proves the stated dichotomy, including a
zero-dimensional cell under the stated convention.

We finally connect normal motion to the impossibility of a fixed-left-hand
epigraph description.  First, every row in \(\mathcal I\) is nonredundant
after redundant inequalities are removed.  Suppose instead that such a row
\(s\), whose unique optimal dual weight satisfies \(\lambda_s>0\), were
implied on \(\mathbf 1^\top q=1\) by the other scenario inequalities and
\(q\geq0\).  Let \(d=(\mathbf 1,0)\), and let \(\mathcal K_s\) be the cone
generated by
\[
 \{a_{x,r}:r\neq s\}
 \cup\{-(e_a,0):a\in\mathcal A\}
 \cup\{-d\}.
\]
This finitely generated cone is closed.  To see this, repeatedly remove a
linear dependence from the positive-coefficient generators of any conic
representation until the used generators are independent.  Along a
convergent sequence, a subsequence uses one fixed independent subfamily; the
inverse coefficient map on its span is continuous, so the limiting
coefficients remain nonnegative.

If \(a_{x,s}\notin\mathcal K_s\), choose a closest point
\(k_0\in\mathcal K_s\) and put \(y=a_{x,s}-k_0\).  Existence follows by
restricting a minimizing sequence to a bounded ball and using closedness.
For each \(k\in\mathcal K_s\), differentiating at zero the squared distance
to \(k_0+t(k-k_0)\) gives \(y^\top(k-k_0)\leq0\).  Taking \(k=0\) and
\(k=2k_0\) gives \(y^\top k_0=0\), and therefore
\[
 y^\top k\leq0\quad(k\in\mathcal K_s),
 \qquad y^\top a_{x,s}=\|y\|^2>0.
 \tag{24}
\]
Write \(y=(\bar q,\bar t)\).  The generator inequalities in (24) imply
\(\bar q\geq0\), \(\mathbf 1^\top\bar q\geq0\), and
\(a_{x,r}^\top y\leq0\) for \(r\neq s\).  If
\(\mathbf 1^\top\bar q=0\), then \(\bar q=0\); the scenario inequalities
give \(\bar t\geq0\), contradicting
\(a_{x,s}^\top y=-\bar t>0\).  Thus
\(\mathbf 1^\top\bar q>0\).  Dividing by this positive number produces a
simplex point satisfying every row except \(s\) and violating \(s\), contrary
to redundancy.  Hence \(a_{x,s}\in\mathcal K_s\), so there exist
\(\rho_r\geq0\), \(\gamma_a\geq0\), and \(\nu\leq0\) such that
\[
 a_{x,s}
 =\sum_{r\neq s}\rho_r a_{x,r}
  -\sum_a\gamma_a(e_a,0)+\nu(\mathbf 1,0).
 \tag{25}
\]
Evaluate (25) at the optimum.  Its left side is zero, while every term on the
right is nonpositive.  Consequently every term with a positive multiplier is
tight and \(\nu=0\).  Comparing last coordinates gives
\(\sum_{r\neq s}\rho_r=1\).  If
\(c=G_x(p_x)^\top\lambda-\eta_x\mathbf 1\geq0\) denotes the dual slack,
replace
\[
 \lambda_s\text{ by }0,
 \qquad
 \lambda_r\text{ by }\lambda_r+\lambda_s\rho_r\ (r\neq s),
 \qquad
 c\text{ by }c+\lambda_s\gamma.
 \tag{26}
\]
Equation (25), \(\nu=0\), and \(\sum_r\rho_r=1\) show that (26) preserves
stationarity, nonnegativity, total dual mass, and the dual objective, but it
changes the dual solution.  This contradicts Assumption
\(\mathrm{ass{:}unique\mbox{-}primal\mbox{-}dual\mbox{-}face}\).  Thus every
active row is nonredundant.

The epigraph has nonempty relative interior in
\(\mathbf 1^\top q=1\): take \(q_a>0\) for every \(a\), then take \(t\)
strictly larger than every one of the finitely many row values.  A
nonredundant row therefore cuts a relative facet.  Indeed, deletion of that
row admits a point violating it; the segment from that point to the displayed
strict relative-interior point first meets the deleted row where all retained
inequalities are strict, producing a relatively open subset of its
hyperplane.

Suppose that at a law with \(\omega_x(p_x)>0\), a relative neighborhood had
an irredundant epigraph description in the original \((q,t)\)-coordinates
with a fixed left-hand matrix and parameter dependence only in the
right-hand side.  On the affine hull \(\mathbf 1^\top q=1\), two inequalities
defining the same relative facet have positively proportional linear parts
modulo a multiple of \((\mathbf 1,0)\).  There are finitely many rows in the
fixed description.  The active facets vary continuously by (22); after
shrinking the neighborhood, finiteness and irredundancy force each active
facet to match one fixed row.  Normalize that row's nonzero \(t\)-coefficient
to \(-1\).  For a constant vector \(d_s\), equality of the relative facets
then implies
\[
 a_{x,s}(p')=(d_s^\top,-1)^\top+c_s(p')(\mathbf 1,0).
 \tag{27}
\]
The scale is constant because the last coordinate of \(a_{x,s}\) is always
\(-1\).  By (22), its \(b\)-th coordinate is always zero, so the \(b\)-th
coordinate in (27) gives \(c_s(p')=-d_{s,b}\), also constant.  Every active
normal would thus be locally constant, contradicting
\(\omega_x(p_x)>0\) and (23).  The fixed-left-hand representation is
therefore impossible.

It remains to prove the uniform stochastic conclusions.  Define
\[
 \widehat\pi_{xz}=\frac{N_{xz}}n,
 \qquad
 \psi_{x,z,a,y}(O_i)
 =\frac{\mathbf 1\{X_i=x,Z_i=z\}}{\pi_{xz}}
   \left(\mathbf 1\{A_i=a,Y_i=y\}-p_{xzay}\right).
 \tag{28}
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}stratum\mbox{-}instrument\mbox{-}support}\)
states \(\pi_{xz}\geq\pi_{\min}>0\), so the division is valid.  Conditioning
on \((X,Z)\) gives
\[
 E_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}}\psi_x(O_i)=0,
 \qquad
 \|\psi_x(O_i)\|\leq C_\psi\pi_{\min}^{-1}
 \tag{29}
\]
for a constant \(C_\psi\) determined by the fixed alphabet.  Put
\[
 S_{xzay,n}=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
 \mathbf 1\{X_i=x,Z_i=z\}
 \left(\mathbf 1\{A_i=a,Y_i=y\}-p_{xzay}\right).
\]
On \(N_{xz}>0\), direct algebra gives
\[
 \widehat p_{xzay}-p_{xzay}
 =\frac{S_{xzay,n}}{\widehat\pi_{xz}}
 =\frac{S_{xzay,n}}{\pi_{xz}}
  +\frac{S_{xzay,n}(\pi_{xz}-\widehat\pi_{xz})}
         {\pi_{xz}\widehat\pi_{xz}}.
 \tag{30}
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}iid\mbox{-}sampling}\) makes each centered sample
average in (30) have variance at most \(n^{-1}\).  The elementary inequality
\(\mathbf 1\{|V|>t\}\leq V^2/t^2\), followed by expectations, therefore
shows uniformly that
\[
 S_{xzay,n}=O_P(n^{-1/2}),
 \qquad
 \widehat\pi_{xz}-\pi_{xz}=O_P(n^{-1/2}).
 \tag{31}
\]
The same inequality and the support margin imply
\[
 \sup_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}}
 P_{\mathrm{obs}}\!\left(\widehat\pi_{xz}<\frac{\pi_{xz}}2\right)
 \leq\frac{4}{n\pi_{\min}^2}\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 P_{\mathrm{obs}}(N_{xz}=0)
 =(1-\pi_{xz})^n\leq e^{-n\pi_{\min}}.
 \tag{32}
\]
Off the first exceptional event in (32), the remainder in (30) is bounded in
absolute value by
\[
 2\pi_{\min}^{-2}|S_{xzay,n}|
       |\widehat\pi_{xz}-\pi_{xz}|.
\]
The product is uniformly \(O_P(n^{-1})\): on the intersection where both
root-\(n\) magnitudes in (31) are at most \(M\), its root-\(n\) magnitude is
at most \(M^2/\sqrt n\), while the complement can be made uniformly small by
first choosing \(M\) large.  There are finitely many cells.  Accounting also
for the second exceptional event in (32), on which the stipulated default
definition of \(\widehat p_x\) is used, proves
\[
 \widehat p_x-p_x
 =\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\psi_x(O_i)+r_{x,n},
 \qquad
 \sup_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}\in\mathfrak P_{\kappa,\varepsilon}}
 P_{\mathrm{obs}}\!\left(\sqrt n\|r_{x,n}\|>\delta\right)
 \longrightarrow0
 \tag{33}
\]
for every \(\delta>0\).  The same argument gives
\(\widehat p_x-p_x=O_P(n^{-1/2})\) uniformly.

True zero cells have zero empirical counts almost surely.  Every positive
cell is at least \(\varepsilon>0\) by Assumption
\(\mathrm{ass{:}fixed\mbox{-}cell\mbox{-}support}\).  Thus (6), (19),
(32), and (33) imply that \(\widehat p_x\) belongs to the same compatible
fixed-support active cell as \(p_x\) with probability tending to one
uniformly.  On its complement, interpret \(T_x(\widehat p_x)\) by any fixed
measurable extension into \(\Delta_K\times[-1,1]\); this makes the random
vector total and changes no conclusion because that event has uniformly
vanishing probability.  Apply (20) with
\(h=\widehat p_x-p_x\), use (33), and use the uniform boundedness of \(J_x\)
that follows from (9), \(\|H^{-1}\|\leq\kappa^{-1}\), and bounded \(u\).
Then
\[
 \sqrt n\{T_x(\widehat p_x)-T_x(p_x)\}
 =\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^nJ_x(p_x)\psi_x(O_i)+o_P(1)
 \tag{34}
\]
uniformly over \(\mathfrak P_{\kappa,\varepsilon}\).  Indeed, the linear
image of \(r_{x,n}\) is uniformly \(o_P(1)\), and the quadratic remainder is
bounded by a constant times
\(\sqrt n\|\widehat p_x-p_x\|^2=o_P(1)\) uniformly.  This is the first
asserted stochastic display.

For the Gaussian approximation, let
\[
 \xi_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}}(O_i)=J_x(p_x)\psi_x(O_i).
\]
By (29) it has mean zero.  It ranges over the fixed finite alphabet
\(\mathcal X\times\mathcal Z\times\mathcal A\times\{0,1\}\), and the
uniform bounds on \(J_x\) and \(\psi_x\) make it uniformly bounded.  Its
covariance is
\[
 E\{\xi_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}}(O_i)\xi_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}}(O_i)^\top\}
 =J_x(p_x)\Sigma_x(p_x)J_x(p_x)^\top.
\]
The already established Lemma
\(\mathrm{lem{:}finite\mbox{-}alphabet\mbox{-}uniform\mbox{-}clt}\), which
allows singular covariance matrices, yields
\[
 \sup_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}\in\mathfrak P_{\kappa,\varepsilon}}
 d_{\mathrm{BL}}\!\left(
 \mathcal L_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}}\!\left(
  \frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^nJ_x(p_x)\psi_x(O_i)\right),
 N\!\left(0,J_x(p_x)\Sigma_x(p_x)J_x(p_x)^\top\right)
 \right)\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{35}
\]
For any two random vectors \(V,W\) on the same sample and any
\(\delta>0\), every bounded one-Lipschitz function gives
\[
 |Ef(V)-Ef(W)|\leq\delta+2P(\|V-W\|>\delta).
\]
Apply this inequality to (34), then combine it with (35) and the triangle
inequality for \(d_{\mathrm{BL}}\).  The resulting bound tends to zero
uniformly, proving the asserted uniform Gaussian law.

%%% FIELD deployed-certificate-construction
For every cell, write
\(I_{xzay,n}(\alpha)=[\ell_{xzay,n}(\alpha),u_{xzay,n}(\alpha)]\).  For a
fixed stratum \(x\), define the interval-compatible response-law polytope
\[
 \mathcal F_{x,n}(\alpha)
 =\left\{w\in\mathbb R_+^{|\mathcal R|}:
 \mathbf 1^\top w=1,\quad
 \ell_{xzay,n}(\alpha)\leq(Bw)_{(z,a,y)}
 \leq u_{xzay,n}(\alpha)
 \text{ for every }z,a,y\right\}.
\]
For a deployed \(\widetilde q_x\in\Delta_K\) and comparator
\(b\in\mathcal A\), put
\(c_b=C^\top(e_b-\widetilde q_x)\) and, when
\(\mathcal F_{x,n}(\alpha)\neq\varnothing\), define the primal maximization
problem
\[
 S_b=\max_{w\in\mathcal F_{x,n}(\alpha)}c_b^\top w,
 \qquad
 \overline R_{x,n}(\widetilde q_x;\alpha)=\max_{b\in\mathcal A}S_b.
\]
If \(\mathcal F_{x,n}(\alpha)=\varnothing\), set
\(\overline R_{x,n}(\widetilde q_x;\alpha)=1\), the vacuous binary-outcome
regret bound.  On the simultaneous confidence event this fallback is never
used.  A numerical lower certificate for \(S_b\) consists of a verified
primal-feasible \(\bar w_b\in\mathcal F_{x,n}(\alpha)\), with
\(L_b=c_b^\top\bar w_b\).  A numerical upper certificate consists of
verified vectors \(r_b^+,r_b^-\geq0\) and \(\nu_b\in\mathbb R\) satisfying
\[
 B^\top(r_b^+-r_b^-)+\nu_b\mathbf 1\geq c_b,
\]
with
\[
 U_b=u_{x,n}(\alpha)^\top r_b^+
       -\ell_{x,n}(\alpha)^\top r_b^-+\nu_b,
\]
where the endpoint vectors are ordered by \((z,a,y)\).  The displayed
feasibility inequalities must be checked, for example by exact rational or
outward-rounded interval arithmetic; a solver status or a reported internal
gap alone is not a certificate.  For every certified pair, direct weak
duality gives
\[
 L_b\leq S_b\leq U_b.
\]
Thus, with \(L=\max_bL_b\) and \(U=\max_bU_b\), one has
\(L\leq\overline R_{x,n}(\widetilde q_x;\alpha)\leq U\).  For any declared
tolerance \(\gamma\geq0\), only after verifying \(U-L\leq\gamma\) may an
implementation report \(U\) as the conservative regret certificate and
\(U-L\) as its certified numerical gap.
