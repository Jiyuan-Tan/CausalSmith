%%% FIELD proof-background
Fix a stratum \(x\).  Conditional response-type exogeneity, treatment consistency, and outcome exclusion imply that the latent vector
\[
 r=\bigl((A(z))_{z\in\mathcal Z},(Y(a))_{a\in\mathcal A}\bigr)
\]
takes values in the finite set \(\mathcal R\), has a conditional distribution \(w_x\) which does not depend on \(Z\), and satisfies
\[
 p_{xzay}
 =\Pr\{A(z)=a,Y(a)=y\mid X=x\}
 =\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}B_{(z,a,y),r}w_{x,r}.
\]
Deleting one redundant cell equation in each instrument row and retaining normalization therefore gives exactly the equalities and inequalities in \(\mathcal W_x(p_x)\).  Conversely, for any \(w_x\in\mathcal W_x(p_x)\), draw \(r\) conditionally on \(X=x\) with probabilities \(w_x\), independently draw \(Z\) from its observed conditional law given \(X=x\), and set \(A=A(Z)\) and \(Y=Y(A)\).  This construction obeys the three structural assumptions and reproduces every observed cell.  Thus it proves both directions of the compatibility assertion, rather than defining the causal identified set to be the observed fiber.

For such a response law,
\[
 E\{Y(a)\mid X=x\}=\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}y_r(a)w_{x,r}=(Cw_x)_a.
\]
Consequently the sharp identified set of the causal action-mean vector is
\[
 \Theta_I(p_x)=\{(E\{Y(a)\mid X=x\})_{a\in\mathcal A}:w_x\text{ is compatible with }p_x\}
 =\{Cw_x:w_x\in\mathcal W_x(p_x)\}=\mathcal M_x(p_x).
\]
The converse construction in the preceding paragraph establishes sharpness of this equality.

Write \(d=\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde B)\).  The fiber is a nonempty bounded polytope.  Every one of its vertices is a basic feasible solution: indeed, if the positive columns at a vertex were linearly dependent, a nonzero null direction supported on those columns could be followed with both signs for a sufficiently small distance while preserving nonnegativity and \(\widetilde B w_x=\widetilde p_x\), contradicting extremality.  Extending its positive independent columns to \(d\) independent columns yields a basis \(\beta\) and the basic vector \(w_{x,\beta}=\widetilde B_\beta^{-1}\widetilde p_x\geq0\).  Conversely, every such basic vector is feasible.  A linear functional on the compact fiber reaches its maximum at a vertex, and hence, for \(d_b=e_b-q\),
\[
 h_{\mathcal M_x(p_x)}(d_b)
 =\max_{\beta\in\mathcal B_x(p_x)}d_b^\top C_\beta\widetilde B_\beta^{-1}\widetilde p_x
 =\max_{\beta\in\mathcal B_x(p_x)}G_{(b,\beta),\cdot}(p_x)q.
\]
Taking the maximum over \(b\) gives
\[
 R_x(q,p_x)=\max_{b,\beta}G_{(b,\beta),\cdot}(p_x)q.
\]
The epigraph formulation in \texttt{def:reduced-saddle-lp} is therefore exactly, rather than approximately, the observed-law minimax functional.  Its optimal \(q\)-face and value are \(Q_x^*(p_x)\) and \(v_x(p_x)\); retaining every optimal basic solution and its optimal face includes ties and degeneracy.

%%% FIELD proof-pure
By the finite representation in \(\mathrm{lem{:}joint\text{-}saddle\text{-}background}\), put \(f(q)=\max_sG_{s,\cdot}(p_x)q\), where \(s=(b,\beta)\).  At \(e_j\), the active affine functions are exactly \(\mathcal S_j(p_x)\), and their convex hull is the subdifferential:
\[
 \partial f(e_j)=\left\{\sum_s\lambda_sG_{s,\cdot}(p_x):\lambda\geq0,\ \mathbf1^\top\lambda=1,\ \lambda_s=0\text{ if }s\notin\mathcal S_j(p_x)\right\}.
\]
This equality follows directly from the maximum formula: the displayed convex combinations give supporting hyperplanes, while separation from their convex hull would produce a direction contradicting the directional derivative \(f'(e_j;d)=\max_{s\in\mathcal S_j}G_{s,\cdot}d\).

For a convex function on \(\Delta_K\), \(e_j\) is a minimizer if and only if some \(g\in\partial f(e_j)\) satisfies \(g^\top(q-e_j)\geq0\) for every \(q\in\Delta_K\).  Necessity follows by separating \(\partial f(e_j)\) from the negative normal cone if their intersection is empty; sufficiency is the subgradient inequality.  Since the vertices of \(\Delta_K\) are \(e_a\), the condition is equivalent to
\[
 g_a-g_j\geq0\quad(a\in\mathcal A).
\]
Substitution of the displayed convex combination for \(g\) gives precisely
\[
 \sum_{b,\beta}\lambda_{b\beta}\{G_{(b,\beta),a}(p_x)-G_{(b,\beta),j}(p_x)\}\geq0
 \quad(a\in\mathcal A),
\]
together with the remaining defining conditions of \(\mathcal K_j(p_x)\).  Hence \(e_j\in Q_x^*(p_x)\) if and only if \(\mathcal K_j(p_x)\neq\varnothing\).

For observability, enumerate the finitely many nonsingular \(\widetilde B_\beta\).  Each basic coordinate and each entry of \(G_x(p_x)\) is affine in \(\widetilde p_x\), with fixed rational coefficients.  For each proposed feasible roster, worst-state set, and basic feasible solution of the \(\lambda\)-system, feasibility and optimality are finitely many sign conditions on determinants and affine expressions.  Clearing the nonzero constant determinants of \(\widetilde B_\beta\), and taking the finite disjunction over these rosters and bases, yields a finite Boolean combination of weak polynomial inequalities in the observed cells.  Negated weak inequalities encode the strict signs needed to exclude a roster.  Thus the certificate is necessary and sufficient even on ties.

For every compatible \(p_x\), \(v_x^{\Box}(p_x)=v_x(p_x)\) if and only if \(\mathcal F_x^{\mathrm{val}}(p_x)\neq\varnothing\).  Moreover, simultaneous preservation of the value and complete optimizer correspondence,
\[
 v_x^{\Box}(p_x)=v_x(p_x)\quad\text{and}\quad Q_x^{\Box}(p_x)=Q_x^*(p_x),
\]
holds if and only if \(\mathcal F_x^{\mathrm{arg}}(p_x)\) holds.  Both conditions admit finite observable basis certificates.

%%% FIELD proof-rectangle
Let \(f(q)=h_{\mathcal M_x(p_x)}^{\max}(q)=\max_bh_{\mathcal M_x(p_x)}(e_b-q)\) and define \(g(q)\) analogously with \(\mathcal M_x^{\Box}(p_x)\).  The coordinate extrema defining the rectangle imply \(\mathcal M_x(p_x)\subseteq\mathcal M_x^{\Box}(p_x)\), so support-function monotonicity gives
\[
 f(q)\leq g(q)\quad(q\in\Delta_K),\quad v_x(p_x)\leq v_x^{\Box}(p_x).
\]
If \(q\in\mathcal F_x^{\mathrm{val}}(p_x)\), then \(g(q)\leq v_x(p_x)\), whence
\[
 v_x(p_x)\leq v_x^{\Box}(p_x)\leq g(q)\leq v_x(p_x).
\]
Thus the values agree.  Conversely, if the values agree, compactness supplies \(q^{\Box}\in Q_x^{\Box}(p_x)\), and \(g(q^{\Box})=v_x(p_x)\), so \(q^{\Box}\in\mathcal F_x^{\mathrm{val}}(p_x)\).

Now suppose \(\mathcal F_x^{\mathrm{arg}}(p_x)\) holds.  For \(q\in Q_x^*(p_x)\),
\[
 v_x(p_x)=f(q)\leq g(q)\leq v_x(p_x),
\]
so \(q\in Q_x^{\Box}(p_x)\), and the preceding value argument gives \(v_x^{\Box}=v_x\).  Conversely, for \(q\in Q_x^{\Box}(p_x)\),
\[
 v_x(p_x)\leq f(q)\leq g(q)=v_x^{\Box}(p_x)=v_x(p_x),
\]
so \(q\in Q_x^*(p_x)\).  Hence both correspondences agree.  If instead simultaneous value and correspondence preservation is assumed, every \(q\in Q_x^*=Q_x^{\Box}\) satisfies \(g(q)=v_x^{\Box}=v_x\), which is exactly \(Q_x^*\subseteq\mathcal F_x^{\mathrm{val}}\).

Finally, both \(f\) and \(g\) have finite epigraph descriptions.  Basis enumeration for \(f\), endpoint enumeration for \(g\), and finite primal--dual feasibility checks therefore give the claimed observable certificates, including weak inequalities on boundary faces.

For every \(a\), the definition of the marginal extrema gives \(\mathcal M_x(p_x)\subseteq\prod_c[L_{cx},U_{cx}]\).  For \(d=e_a-q\), its \(a\)-th coefficient is \(1-q_a\geq0\), while its \(c\)-th coefficient is \(-q_c\leq0\) for \(c\neq a\).  Therefore

Taking the minimum and the complete argmin over \(q\in\Delta_K\), then applying \(\mathrm{lem{:}stoye\text{-}proposition\text{-}one}\), proves equality with Stoye's allocation value and correspondence.

When \(K=2\), write \(q_1=1-q_0\).  The two displayed regrets are affine functions with opposite slopes; the minimax rule is their intersection when that intersection lies in \([0,1]\), and otherwise the appropriate endpoint.  This is exactly the standard binary fractional minimax-regret rule, including its boundary cases.

Put \(M=2K|\mathcal X||\mathcal Z|\) and let \(E_n\) be the event that every true cell probability belongs to its interval.  Conditional on all row totals \((N_{xz})_{x,z}\), each marginal cell count \(N_{xzay}\) is binomial with total \(N_{xz}\) and success probability \(p_{xzay}\).  By \(\mathrm{lem{:}clopper\text{-}pearson\text{-}cell\text{-}coverage}\), its conditional noncoverage probability is at most \(\alpha/M\); when \(N_{xz}=0\), it is zero by the \([0,1]\) convention.  Dependence among cells is immaterial to the union bound, so
\[
 \Pr(E_n^c\mid(N_{xz})_{x,z})
 \leq\sum_{x,z,a,y}\frac{\alpha}{M}=\alpha.
\]
Taking expectations gives \(\Pr(E_n)\geq1-\alpha\), for every sample size and every law in \(\mathfrak P_{\mathrm{IV}}\).

On \(E_n\), the true table has all interval memberships and multinomial row-sum constraints required by \(\mathcal C_n(\alpha)\).  The response-fiber compatibility assumption supplies \(\mathcal W_x(p_x)\neq\varnothing\), so \(p\in\mathcal C_n(\alpha)\).  The definition of the optimizer confidence set then yields, simultaneously for every \(x\),
\[
 Q_x^*(p_x)\subseteq\bigcup_{p'\in\mathcal C_n(\alpha)}Q_x^*(p'_x)
 =\widehat{\mathcal Q}_{x,n}(\alpha).
\]
Combining this implication with \(\Pr(E_n)\geq1-\alpha\) proves the advertised simultaneous coverage.  Since the lower bound does not depend on the particular law, it is uniform over \(\mathfrak P_{\mathrm{IV}}\).

For computation, the response roster is finite.  On each roster, primal feasibility, dual feasibility, complementarity, and equality of the primal and dual values are polynomial equalities and weak inequalities in \((p',q,t,\lambda)\), because every entry of \(G_x(p'_x)\) is affine in \(p'_x\).  Enumerating all primal--dual active bases therefore enumerates the complete semialgebraic KKT graph.  At a tied law, the full optimizer face is the convex hull of its optimal primal basic solutions, so retaining the face, rather than selecting one basic optimizer, includes every mixture on an intersection.  The interval convention at a zero row imposes no missing constraint, and weak KKT inequalities retain boundary and zero-count cases.  Projection of this finite graph over \(p'\in\mathcal C_n(\alpha)\) is exactly the union in the definition.

%%% FIELD statement-uniform-clt
Let \(d\) be fixed and let \(\mathcal S\) be finite.  For each index \(P\), let \(\xi_P:\mathcal S\to\mathbb R^d\) have mean zero under a probability mass function \(P\), let \(\Gamma_P=E_P(\xi_P\xi_P^\top)\), and suppose \(\sup_{P,s}\|\xi_P(s)\|<\infty\).  If \(\xi_{P,1},\ldots,\xi_{P,n}\) are independent with this common law, then
\[
 \sup_P d_{\mathrm{BL}}\left(\mathcal L_P\left(n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^n\xi_{P,i}\right),N(0,\Gamma_P)\right)\longrightarrow0.
\]
The conclusion permits singular \(\Gamma_P\).

%%% FIELD proof-uniform-clt
Let the common bound be \(M\).  For every fixed \(t\in\mathbb R^d\), Taylor's formula for \(e^{iu}\), the zero-mean condition, and \(|t^\top\xi_P|\leq\|t\|M\) give, uniformly in \(P\),
\[
 E_Pe^{it^\top\xi_P/\sqrt n}
 =1-\frac{t^\top\Gamma_Pt}{2n}+O_{t,M}(n^{-3/2}).
\]
Raising this expression to the \(n\)-th power gives
\[
 E_P\exp\left(it^\top n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^n\xi_{P,i}\right)
 =\exp(-t^\top\Gamma_Pt/2)+o_{t,M}(1),
\]
uniformly in \(P\).

If bounded-Lipschitz convergence were not uniform, there would be a sequence \(P_n\) along which its distance stayed above a positive constant.  The matrices \(\Gamma_{P_n}\) are bounded, so a subsequence converges to a nonnegative semidefinite matrix \(\Gamma\).  The normalized sums are tight because their second moments are \(\operatorname{tr}(\Gamma_{P_n})\leq dM^2\).  Every weakly convergent subsubsequence has, by the preceding characteristic-function identity, characteristic function \(\exp(-t^\top\Gamma t/2)\), and hence law \(N(0,\Gamma)\).  The Gaussian laws \(N(0,\Gamma_{P_n})\) also converge to \(N(0,\Gamma)\).  Bounded-Lipschitz distance metrizes weak convergence in finite-dimensional Euclidean space, producing the required contradiction.  No inverse covariance was used, so singular limits are included.

%%% FIELD proof-rootn
Fix \(x\) and a law in \(\mathfrak P_{\kappa,\varepsilon}\).  First consider a perturbation \(p_x+h\) within the fixed-support multinomial tangent space
\[
 \mathcal T_x=\{h:\sum_{a,y}h_{zay}=0\ \forall z,\quad h_{zay}=0\text{ whenever }p_{xzay}=0\}.
\]
For every nonsingular response basis, \(\widetilde B_\beta^{-1}\widetilde p_x\) is affine in \(p_x\).  The response-basis roster margin says every one of its coordinates has absolute value at least \(\kappa\).  Since there are finitely many bases and their inverse norms are fixed, there is a radius, uniform over the stated class, on which all these signs are unchanged.  Thus \(\mathcal B_x(p_x)\), and consequently the finite affine formula for \(G_x(p_x)\), are locally fixed.

On a fixed roster and proposed active sets \(\mathcal I=\mathcal I_x(p_x)\), \(\mathcal J=\mathcal J_x(p_x)\), the active-system rank assumption gives
\[
 \|H_x(p_x)^{-1}\|\leq\kappa^{-1}.
\]
The candidate \(u_x(p')=H_x(p')^{-1}g_x\) is therefore well defined and continuous for all sufficiently close \(p'\).  The active-weight margin keeps its active \(q\)- and \(\lambda\)-coordinates positive.  The primal-slack and reduced-cost margins keep every inactive primal inequality and inactive policy reduced cost strictly slack.  Hence this candidate satisfies the full primal--dual KKT conditions with the same \((\mathcal I,\mathcal J)\).

It is the unique KKT solution.  Indeed, equality in weak duality for any other optimum forces every constraint with positive \(\lambda_s\) to bind and every action with strictly positive reduced cost to receive zero probability.  Those equalities, together with the two simplex equalities, are the square system \(H_x(p')u=g_x\), whose solution is unique.  Finiteness of all possible rosters and active sets, the common margins \(\kappa\), and the fixed inverse matrices make the preceding neighborhood radius uniform over \(\mathfrak P_{\kappa,\varepsilon}\).  This proves local constancy of \(\mathcal B_x,\mathcal I_x,\mathcal J_x\) and the asserted active-system representation.

On this neighborhood, \(H_x\) is affine and hence continuously differentiable in \(p_x\), while \(g_x\) and the zero-padding matrix \(E_x\) are constant.  From
\[
 H_x(p_x+h)u_x(p_x+h)=g_x
\]
and invertibility, subtraction of the equation at \(p_x\), division by the perturbation size, and passage to the limit give
\[
 Du_x(p_x)[h]=-H_x(p_x)^{-1}DH_x(p_x)[h]u_x(p_x).
\]
After applying \(E_x\), this is exactly \(J_x(p_x)[h]\).  The uniform inverse bound and the finite affine roster also give a uniform quadratic remainder, so the derivative is Hadamard tangentially to \(\mathcal T_x\).

For the moving-normal assertion, on a fixed cell write \(a_{x,s}(p)=(G_{s,\cdot}(p)^\top,-1)^\top\).  If
\[
 (I-\bar a_{x,s}\bar a_{x,s}^\top)Da_{x,s}(p)[h]=0,
\]
then \(Da_{x,s}(p)[h]\) is a scalar multiple of \(a_{x,s}(p)\).  Its last coordinate is zero whereas the last coordinate of \(a_{x,s}\) is \(-1\), so that scalar is zero.  Thus \(\omega_x(p)=0\) exactly when every active normal has zero derivative in every tangent direction.  Because \(G_x\) is affine on the fixed cell, either these derivatives all vanish identically, in which case every active normal is locally constant (and hence constant up to positive scale), or the zero locus is empty, which is a proper algebraic subset.  This proves the stated, slightly weaker, algebraic dichotomy.

Suppose now that \(\omega_x(p)>0\) and that a neighborhood admitted an irredundant epigraph description in the original \((q,t)\)-coordinates with a fixed left-hand matrix.  An active row carrying positive dual weight cannot be redundant: replacing a positive amount of its multiplier by the nonnegative Farkas combination certifying redundancy would give a distinct optimal dual solution, contrary to the singleton primal--dual assumption.  Every remaining facet hyperplane of a polyhedron has a normal unique up to positive scale.  Continuity and finiteness therefore force each active facet near \(p\) to match one fixed row locally, so its projective normal has zero derivative.  This contradicts \(\omega_x(p)>0\).  Therefore coefficient motion cannot be transferred entirely to right-hand sides in those coordinates.

It remains to establish the uniform stochastic assertions.  Define the conditional-table influence coordinate by
\[
 \psi_{x,z,a,y}(O_i)=\frac{\mathbf1\{X_i=x,Z_i=z\}}{\pi_{xz}}
 \bigl(\mathbf1\{A_i=a,Y_i=y\}-p_{xzay}\bigr).
\]
The support assumption gives \(\|\psi_x(O_i)\|\leq C/\pi_{\min}\), uniformly.  On \(N_{xz}>0\), the ratio identity and a first-order expansion of \((N_{xz}/n)^{-1}\) yield
\[
 \widehat p_x-p_x=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\psi_x(O_i)+r_{x,n},
 \quad \sqrt n\,\|r_{x,n}\|=o_P(1)
\]
uniformly.  To see the last claim directly, the remainder is a product of the centered row-frequency error and a centered within-row numerator, divided by quantities bounded away from zero; both centered factors are uniformly \(O_P(n^{-1/2})\).  Moreover, \(\Pr\{N_{xz}=0\}\leq(1-\pi_{\min})^n\), and the union over finitely many rows is still exponentially small.  True zero cells remain zero almost surely on the complementary event, while positive cells are separated by \(\varepsilon\); thus \(\widehat p_x\) lies in the same fixed-support active cell with probability tending to one uniformly.

Applying the uniform quadratic expansion of \(T_x\) gives
\[
 \sqrt n\{T_x(\widehat p_x)-T_x(p_x)\}
 =\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^nJ_x(p_x)\psi_x(O_i)+o_P(1)
\]
uniformly over the class, which is the first probability limit.  The matrices \(J_x(p_x)\) are uniformly bounded: \(H_x^{-1}\) is bounded by \(\kappa^{-1}\), \(DH_x\) ranges over finitely many fixed affine derivatives, and the components of the primal--dual solution are bounded by simplex constraints and bounded payoffs.  Apply \(\mathrm{lem{:}finite\text{-}alphabet\text{-}uniform\text{-}clt}\) to \(\xi_i=J_x(p_x)\psi_x(O_i)\).  Its covariance is \(J_x(p_x)\Sigma_x(p_x)J_x(p_x)^\top\), including possible singular cases.  The uniform asymptotic linear remainder and the bounded-Lipschitz triangle inequality complete the claimed uniform Gaussian approximation.

For every stratum \(x\), uniformly over \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\in\mathfrak P_{\kappa,\varepsilon}\), the feasible response-basis roster \(\mathcal B_x(p_x)\), active regret rows \(\mathcal I_x(p_x)\), and positive policy coordinates \(\mathcal J_x(p_x)\) are locally constant. The KKT vector is the unique solution of \(H_x(p_x)u_x(p_x)=g_x(p_x)\), and \(T_x:p_x\mapsto((q_x^*(p_x))^\top,v_x(p_x))^\top\) is Hadamard differentiable tangentially to the fixed-support multinomial space with derivative \(J_x(p_x)[h]=-E_x(p_x)H_x(p_x)^{-1}DH_x(p_x)[h]u_x(p_x)\). The right-hand side \(g_x(p_x)\) is constant on the face while \(H_x(p_x)\) depends on \(p_x\) through \(G_{\mathcal I_x,\mathcal J_x}(p_x)\). Within each fixed active cell, either every active facet normal is locally constant up to positive scale or the laws satisfying \(\omega_x(p_x)=0\) lie in a proper algebraic subset; at every law with \(\omega_x(p_x)>0\), no neighborhood has an epigraph description in the original \((q,t)\)-coordinates with a fixed left-hand coefficient matrix and all dependence on \(p_x\) confined to the right-hand side after redundant inequalities are removed. For every \(\delta>0\),
\[
\sup_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}\in\mathfrak P_{\kappa,\varepsilon}}P_{\mathrm{obs}}\left(\left\|\sqrt n\{T_x(\widehat p_x)-T_x(p_x)\}-\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^nJ_x(p_x)\psi_x(O_i)\right\|>\delta\right)\longrightarrow0,
\]
and the law of \(\sqrt n\{T_x(\widehat p_x)-T_x(p_x)\}\) converges uniformly in bounded-Lipschitz distance to \(N(0,J_x(p_x)\Sigma_x(p_x)J_x(p_x)^\top)\).

%%% FIELD statement-face-impossibility
In the categorical-instrument model with \(K\geq3\), the handle \(\mathfrak H_{\mathrm{face}}\), and indeed any data-based random closed set, cannot simultaneously have asymptotic coverage at least \(1-\alpha\) uniformly along all \(n^{-1/2}\)-local sequences through intersections of optimal faces and contract in support-function or Hausdorff distance at the \(n^{-1/2}\) rate to \(Q_x^*(p_x)\).  This impossibility holds without selecting a population face.  Exact confidence-fiber coverage remains valid, but uniform root-\(n\) contraction requires excluding such intersections by a face-separation condition or weakening the target to a local outer limit.

%%% FIELD proof-face-impossibility
It suffices to give an explicit regular submodel certificate.  Take one stratum, \(\mathcal Z=\mathcal A=\{0,\ldots,K-1\}\), and randomize \(Z\) uniformly.  Set \(A(z)=z\) for every unit.  Independently of \(Z\), draw a binary potential-outcome vector with marginal means
\[
 \mu_0(\tau)=\tfrac12,\quad \mu_1(\tau)=\tfrac12+\tau,
 \quad \mu_a(\tau)=\tfrac14\quad(2\leq a<K),
\]
for \(|\tau|<1/8\); independent Bernoulli coordinates are one concrete response-type distribution.  This law satisfies response-type exogeneity, consistency, exclusion, and positive instrument support.  Because the structural zeros \(A=Z\) force full compliance, the observed outcome at \(Z=a\) identifies the marginal mean of \(Y(a)\), so \(\mathcal M_x(p_x(\tau))=\{\mu(\tau)\}\).

For a singleton mean vector, the joint-saddle definition reduces to
\[
 R_x(q,p_x(\tau))=\max_a\mu_a(\tau)-q^\top\mu(\tau),
\]
and hence
\[
 Q_x^*(p_x(0))=\operatorname{conv}\{e_0,e_1\},\quad
 Q_x^*(p_x(\tau))=\{e_1\}\quad(\tau>0).
\]
Thus \(\tau=0\) is an explicit codimension-one face intersection and every positive perturbation selects one endpoint.

Let \(\widehat S_n\) be any proposed random closed set and suppose its coverage is at least \(1-\alpha-o(1)\) both at \(\tau=0\) and uniformly on local sequences.  Put \(\tau_n=c/\sqrt n\).  Only the Bernoulli law observed when \(Z=1\) changes.  If \(L_\tau=dP_\tau/dP_0\) is the one-observation likelihood ratio, direct substitution in the two Bernoulli cells gives
\[
 E_0(L_\tau-1)^2\leq C\tau^2
\]
for an absolute finite \(C\) and \(|\tau|<1/8\).  Independence gives
\[
 E_0\left\{\prod_{i=1}^nL_{\tau_n}(O_i)-1\right\}^2
 =\{1+E_0(L_{\tau_n}-1)^2\}^n-1
 \leq e^{Cc^2}-1.
\]
By Cauchy--Schwarz, total variation is at most one half of the square root of the last display.  Thus
\[
 \|P_0^n-P_{\tau_n}^n\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq\rho(c):=\tfrac12\sqrt{e^{Cc^2}-1},
 \quad \rho(c)\longrightarrow0\quad(c\downarrow0).
\]
Choose \(c\) so that \(\rho(c)<1-\alpha\).  At \(\tau=0\), coverage implies
\[
 P_0^n\{\operatorname{conv}(e_0,e_1)\subseteq\widehat S_n\}\geq1-\alpha-o(1).
\]
The total-variation bound therefore implies
\[
 \liminf_nP_{\tau_n}^n\{e_0\in\widehat S_n\}
 \geq1-\alpha-\rho(c)>0.
\]
But the true local-alternative correspondence is \(\{e_1\}\), and on this event its Hausdorff distance from \(\widehat S_n\) is at least \(\|e_0-e_1\|=\sqrt2\).  Equivalently, with \(u=(e_0-e_1)/\sqrt2\), the support-function discrepancy is at least \(u^\top(e_0-e_1)=\sqrt2\).  Hence neither discrepancy is \(O_P(n^{-1/2})\) uniformly along the local sequence.  This contradiction applies to every construction, including adjacent-basis numerical delta methods.  The exact Clopper--Pearson union is not contradicted: it preserves coverage by retaining both locally indistinguishable faces, and consequently need not contract at the root-\(n\) rate at the intersection.
