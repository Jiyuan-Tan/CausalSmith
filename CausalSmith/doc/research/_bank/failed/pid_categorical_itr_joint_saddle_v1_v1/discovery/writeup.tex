\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} Randomized minimax regret under partial identification is not new: Montiel Olea, Qiu, and Stoye already analyze randomized statistical rules in a finite-sample Gaussian-likelihood decision problem.  For the binary-outcome, finite-stratum specialization of Song et al.'s model M1, this paper instead identifies the full categorical-instrument joint action-mean polytope and its complete population minimax correspondence.  The exact three-action witness has joint rule \(q^\dagger=(0,1/4,3/4)\) and value \(3/16\), whereas rectangularization selects \((0,0,1)\) with value \(1/4\); an exact rational matrix certifies all ten projected vertices, and uniform-response-type perturbations retain a value gap of at least \(1/32\) with full observed support.  On separated nondegenerate unique faces, the moving-response-basis and moving-coefficient plug-in map has a uniform root-\(n\) Gaussian law.  At a displayed codimension-one face intersection, class-uniform honest contraction fails at every vanishing rate absent separation, while the Bonferroni Clopper--Pearson confidence-fiber union retains exact correspondence containment.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Montiel Olea, Qiu, and Stoye already supply a direct finite-sample Gaussian decision theory for randomized minimax-regret rules under partial identification, including multiplicity and least-randomizing refinements.  Song et al.'s model M1 supplies response-law compatibility restrictions for discrete instruments and outcomes.  Neither antecedent gives the sharp population minimax correspondence obtained by projecting a categorical-instrument response fiber, the derivative of the resulting plug-in optimizer when both feasible bases and regret coefficients move with the observable multinomial table, or exact confidence-fiber containment of that correspondence.

\paragraph{Niche.} The paper studies the binary-outcome, finite-covariate-stratum specialization of Song et al.'s model M1 and links its sharp joint response-type polytope to a finite randomized saddle program.  Its contribution is the categorical-IV population correspondence, moving-basis fixed-law inference on separated nondegenerate unique faces, and exact set-valued containment across face intersections.  It is not a claim of originating randomized minimax regret under partial identification and does not subsume Montiel Olea--Qiu--Stoye's finite-sample Gaussian admissibility, profiled-regret, or least-randomizing theory.

\paragraph{Fill.} The exact response-fiber construction and feasible-basis program enumerate every population-optimal randomized mixture and expose observable pure-rule certificates.  The positive inference result gives an explicit moving-coefficient derivative and a uniform Gaussian policy-and-value law on separated nondegenerate unique faces; the negative result gives one internal codimension-one submodel on which uniform honest contraction at any vanishing rate is impossible without separation.  The exact Bonferroni confidence-fiber union supplies the noncontracting validity fallback.  An exact rational matrix certifies all ten vertices of the three-action witness, and a full-support perturbation supplies positive cell, treatment-support, variance, value-gap, and optimizer-separation margins.

  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathfrak H\_\allowbreak{}\{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathrm\{face\}\allowbreak{}\}\allowbreak{}} --- \texttt{method handle}; \texttt{adjacent-\allowbreak{}basis directional-\allowbreak{}inference handle}
\end{itemize}

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:iid-sampling]\label{ass:iid-sampling}
\(O_1,\ldots,O_n\) are independent and identically distributed according to \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Independent and identically distributed sampling, \cite{vanDerVaart1998}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:instrument-randomization]\label{ass:instrument-randomization}
\(Z\perp\!\!\perp (A(z):z\in\mathcal Z,\,Y(a):a\in\mathcal A)\mid X\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Conditional response-type exogeneity, \cite{SongEtAl2026CategoricalIV}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:treatment-consistency]\label{ass:treatment-consistency}
\(A=A(Z)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Treatment consistency, \cite{SongEtAl2026CategoricalIV}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:outcome-exclusion]\label{ass:outcome-exclusion}
\(Y=Y(A)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Exclusion through treatment response, \cite{SongEtAl2026CategoricalIV}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:stratum-instrument-support]\label{ass:stratum-instrument-support}
\(\pi_{xz}\geq\pi_{\min}\) for every \(x\in\mathcal X\) and \(z\in\mathcal Z\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Finite-cell overlap, \cite{HruzaEtAl2026CategoricalPIL}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:response-fiber-compatibility]\label{ass:response-fiber-compatibility}
\(\mathcal W_x(p_x)\neq\varnothing\) for every \(x\in\mathcal X\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Categorical instrumental-variable compatibility, \cite{SongEtAl2026CategoricalIV}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:fixed-cell-support]\label{ass:fixed-cell-support}
\(p_{xzay}\in\{0\}\cup[\varepsilon,1]\) for every \(x,z,a,y\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Fixed-support multinomial boundary separation, \cite{KlattMunkZemel2022RandomLP}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:response-basis-roster-margin]\label{ass:response-basis-roster-margin}
\(\zeta_x(p_x)\geq\kappa\) for every \(x\in\mathcal X\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform nondegeneracy of all basic solutions, \cite{BonnansShapiro2000Perturbation}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:unique-primal-dual-face]\label{ass:unique-primal-dual-face}
The reduced saddle program has the singleton primal--dual KKT solution \(\{u_x(p_x)\}\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Unique primal--dual solution, \cite{BonnansShapiro2000Perturbation}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:active-system-rank]\label{ass:active-system-rank}
\(\sigma_{\min}(H_x(p_x))\geq\kappa\) for every \(x\in\mathcal X\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform active-basis nonsingularity, \cite{BonnansShapiro2000Perturbation}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:primal-slack-margin]\label{ass:primal-slack-margin}
\(v_x(p_x)-G_{s,\cdot}(p_x)q_x^*(p_x)\geq\kappa\) for every \(s\notin\mathcal I_x(p_x)\) and every \(x\in\mathcal X\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform strict primal slackness, \cite{BonnansShapiro2000Perturbation}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:reduced-cost-margin]\label{ass:reduced-cost-margin}
\(G_{\cdot,a}(p_x)^\top\lambda-\eta_x\geq\kappa\) for every \(a\notin\mathcal J_x(p_x)\) and every \(x\in\mathcal X\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform strict complementarity margin, \cite{BonnansShapiro2000Perturbation}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:active-weight-margin]\label{ass:active-weight-margin}
\(\min\{q_{x,a}^*(p_x):a\in\mathcal J_x(p_x)\}\wedge\min\{\lambda_s:s\in\mathcal I_x(p_x)\}\geq\kappa\) for every \(x\in\mathcal X\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform strict complementarity of active variables, \cite{BonnansShapiro2000Perturbation}).
\end{assumption}

\section{Main results}

\begin{theorem}[thm:unique-face-rootn]\label{thm:unique-face-rootn}
For every stratum \(x\), uniformly over \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\in\mathfrak P_{\kappa,\varepsilon}\), the feasible response-basis roster \(\mathcal B_x(p_x)\), active regret rows \(\mathcal I_x(p_x)\), and positive policy coordinates \(\mathcal J_x(p_x)\) are locally constant. The KKT vector is the unique solution of \(H_x(p_x)u_x(p_x)=g_x(p_x)\), and \(T_x:p_x\mapsto((q_x^*(p_x))^\top,v_x(p_x))^\top\) is Hadamard differentiable tangentially to the fixed-support multinomial space with derivative \(J_x(p_x)[h]=-E_x(p_x)H_x(p_x)^{-1}DH_x(p_x)[h]u_x(p_x)\). The right-hand side \(g_x(p_x)\) is constant on the face while \(H_x(p_x)\) depends on \(p_x\) through \(G_{\mathcal I_x,\mathcal J_x}(p_x)\). Within each fixed active cell, either every active facet normal is locally constant up to positive scale or the laws satisfying \(\omega_x(p_x)=0\) lie in a proper algebraic subset; at every law with \(\omega_x(p_x)>0\), no neighborhood has an epigraph description in the original \((q,t)\)-coordinates with a fixed left-hand coefficient matrix and all dependence on \(p_x\) confined to the right-hand side after redundant inequalities are removed. For every \(\delta>0\), \[\sup_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}\in\mathfrak P_{\kappa,\varepsilon}}P_{\mathrm{obs}}\left(\left\|\sqrt n\{T_x(\widehat p_x)-T_x(p_x)\}-\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^nJ_x(p_x)\psi_x(O_i)\right\|>\delta\right)\longrightarrow0,\] and the law of \(\sqrt n\{T_x(\widehat p_x)-T_x(p_x)\}\) converges uniformly in bounded-Lipschitz distance to \(N(0,J_x(p_x)\Sigma_x(p_x)J_x(p_x)^\top)\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a stratum \(x\) and a law \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\in\mathfrak P_{\kappa,\varepsilon}\).  All norms below are Euclidean or their induced operator norms.  The fixed-support multinomial tangent space at \(p_x\) is
\[
 \mathcal T_x
 =\left\{h:\sum_{a\in\mathcal A,\,y\in\{0,1\}}h_{zay}=0
       \text{ for every }z,\quad
       h_{zay}=0\text{ whenever }p_{xzay}=0\right\}.
 \tag{1}
\]
Every derivative below is taken tangentially to \(\mathcal T_x\), the direction space of the fixed-support face of \(\prod_{z\in\mathcal Z}\Delta_{2K-1}\).  If this direction space is \(\{0\}\), all local differentiability and facet-motion conclusions are immediate, so suppose when needed that it contains a unit vector.  We prove, in order, response-basis stability, saddle-face stability, differentiability, the facet-normal obstruction, and the two stochastic conclusions.

Let \(r_B=\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde B)\), let \(\mathfrak B\) be the finite collection of \(r_B\)-element sets \(\beta\subseteq\mathcal R\) for which \(\widetilde B_\beta\) is nonsingular, and define
\[
 w_\beta(p_x)=\widetilde B_\beta^{-1}\widetilde p_x.
\]
Because the final, normalization coordinate of \(\widetilde p_x\) is constant, there is for each \(\beta\in\mathfrak B\) a fixed matrix \(D_\beta\) such that
\[
 w_\beta(p_x+h)-w_\beta(p_x)=D_\beta h.
 \tag{2}
\]
Finiteness gives
\[
 L_B:=\max_{\beta\in\mathfrak B}\|D_\beta\|<\infty.
 \tag{3}
\]
The compatibility condition in Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}unique\mbox{-}face\mbox{-}class}\), inherited from Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}response\mbox{-}fiber\mbox{-}compatibility}\), ensures that \(\mathcal W_x(p_x)\) is nonempty.  It has a basic feasible point.  Indeed, choose a feasible \(w\) with support of minimum cardinality.  If the columns of \(\widetilde B\) on that support were linearly dependent, a nonzero vector in their null space would have both signs because the last row of \(\widetilde B\) is \(\mathbf 1^\top\).  Moving from \(w\) along one of the two signed directions until a positive coordinate first reached zero would preserve both equalities and nonnegativity while strictly reducing the support, a contradiction.  Thus the support columns are independent.  Extending them, if necessary, to \(r_B\) independent columns yields a nonsingular \(\widetilde B_\beta\) whose associated basic solution is nonnegative.  Therefore
\[
 \mathcal B_x(p_x)\neq\varnothing.
 \tag{4}
\]

Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}response\mbox{-}basis\mbox{-}roster\mbox{-}margin}\) and the definition of \(\zeta_x(p_x)\) give, for every \(\beta\in\mathfrak B\) and every basic coordinate \(j\),
\[
 |w_{\beta,j}(p_x)|\geq\kappa>0.
 \tag{5}
\]
The strict positivity of \(\kappa\) justifies setting
\[
 \rho_B:=\frac{\kappa}{2\max\{L_B,1\}}>0.
 \tag{6}
\]
Equations (2)--(6) show that every coordinate of every basic solution retains its sign whenever \(h\in\mathcal T_x\), \(p_x+h\) remains in the fixed-support product simplex, and \(\|h\|<\rho_B\).  Hence
\[
 \mathcal B_x(p_x+h)=\mathcal B_x(p_x).
 \tag{7}
\]
In particular, the perturbed response fiber remains nonempty.  The radius in (6) is uniform over the asserted class because \(K\), \(\mathcal Z\), \(\mathcal R\), and the matrices \(D_\beta\) are fixed and finite, while the lower bound \(\kappa\) is common.

On the roster in (7), each regret row is affine in \(p_x\).  Specifically, Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}reduced\mbox{-}saddle\mbox{-}lp}\) and (2) give
\[
 G_{(b,\beta),a}(p_x+h)
 =G_{(b,\beta),a}(p_x)
  +(e_b-e_a)^\top C_\beta D_\beta h.
 \tag{8}
\]
Thus the row derivatives and the row norms are bounded by constants determined only by the finite collection of fixed matrices.  Write
\[
 \mathcal I=\mathcal I_x(p_x),\qquad
 \mathcal J=\mathcal J_x(p_x),\qquad
 H=H_x(p_x),\qquad u=u_x(p_x),
\]
and, while keeping this roster and these two active sets fixed, put
\[
 H_h=H_x(p_x+h),\qquad \Delta_h=H_h-H.
\]
Equation (8) supplies a uniform finite constant \(L_H\) such that
\[
 \|\Delta_h\|\leq L_H\|h\|.
 \tag{9}
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}active\mbox{-}system\mbox{-}rank}\) states that \(\sigma_{\min}(H)\geq\kappa>0\).  If \(L_H\|h\|\leq\kappa/2\), then, for every vector \(z\),
\[
 \|H_hz\|\geq\|Hz\|-\|\Delta_hz\|
 \geq(\kappa-L_H\|h\|)\|z\|
 \geq\frac{\kappa}{2}\|z\|.
\]
Consequently
\[
 H_h^{-1}\text{ exists},\qquad \|H_h^{-1}\|\leq2\kappa^{-1}.
 \tag{10}
\]
Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}active\mbox{-}system}\) makes the active right-hand side constant, so define the candidate
\[
 u_h:=H_h^{-1}g_x(p_x).
 \tag{11}
\]
The resolvent identity, (9), and (10) yield
\[
 u_h-u=-H_h^{-1}\Delta_hu,\qquad
 \|u_h-u\|\leq2\kappa^{-1}L_H\|u\|\,\|h\|.
 \tag{12}
\]
The vector \(u\) is uniformly bounded on \(\mathfrak P_{\kappa,\varepsilon}\).  Its \(q\)- and \(\lambda\)-blocks are probability vectors.  Every feasible basic response law produces action means in \([0,1]\), so every entry of \(G_x\) is in \([-1,1]\), whence \(|v_x(p_x)|\leq1\).  Since \(\mathcal J\neq\varnothing\), active stationarity gives \(\eta_x=G_{\cdot,a}(p_x)^\top\lambda\in[-1,1]\) for any \(a\in\mathcal J\).  Finally, all relevant dimensions range over finite sets.

We next verify that (11), rather than merely a solution of selected equations, remains the complete saddle solution.  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}active\mbox{-}weight\mbox{-}margin}\) and (12), after shrinking the neighborhood uniformly if necessary, give
\[
 q_{h,a}\geq\kappa/2\quad(a\in\mathcal J),\qquad
 \lambda_{h,s}\geq\kappa/2\quad(s\in\mathcal I).
 \tag{13}
\]
Assumptions \(\mathrm{ass{:}primal\mbox{-}slack\mbox{-}margin}\) and \(\mathrm{ass{:}reduced\mbox{-}cost\mbox{-}margin}\), together with the affine bound (8) and the solution bound (12), similarly imply
\[
 v_h-G_{s,\cdot}(p_x+h)q_h\geq\kappa/2
       \quad(s\notin\mathcal I)
 \tag{14}
\]
and
\[
 G_{\cdot,a}(p_x+h)^\top\lambda_h-\eta_h\geq\kappa/2
       \quad(a\notin\mathcal J).
 \tag{15}
\]
The block equations \(H_hu_h=g_x(p_x)\) are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 G_{\mathcal I,\mathcal J}(p_x+h)q_{h,\mathcal J}
       &=v_h\mathbf 1_{\mathcal I},\\
 G_{\mathcal I,\mathcal J}(p_x+h)^\top\lambda_{h,\mathcal I}
       &=\eta_h\mathbf 1_{\mathcal J},\\
 \mathbf 1^\top q_h&=1,\\
 \mathbf 1^\top\lambda_h&=1.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{16}
\]
Extend \(q_h\) and \(\lambda_h\) by zero outside \(\mathcal J\) and \(\mathcal I\), respectively.  Equations (13)--(16) are primal feasibility, dual feasibility, and complementary slackness for the finite saddle program.  Directly, without invoking strong duality,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 v_h
 &=\sum_s\lambda_{h,s}v_h
 =\sum_s\lambda_{h,s}G_{s,\cdot}(p_x+h)q_h\\
 &=q_h^\top G_x(p_x+h)^\top\lambda_h
 =\eta_h.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{17}
\]
For any other primal-feasible \((q',t')\), (15)--(17) give weak duality equation by equation:
\[
 t'\geq\sum_s\lambda_{h,s}G_{s,\cdot}(p_x+h)q'
 =(q')^\top G_x(p_x+h)^\top\lambda_h
 \geq\eta_h\mathbf 1^\top q'=v_h.
 \tag{18}
\]
Thus \((q_h,v_h)\) is optimal.

It is the unique primal optimum.  If another primal optimum \((q',t')\) achieved equality in (18), positivity of every \(\lambda_{h,s}\) for \(s\in\mathcal I\) in (13) would force all rows in \(\mathcal I\) to bind, and strict positivity of the dual slack for every \(a\notin\mathcal J\) in (15) would force \(q'_a=0\) there.  Keeping the candidate dual block fixed, the vector \((q'_{\mathcal J},t',\lambda_{h,\mathcal I},\eta_h)\) would then solve (16).  Nonsingularity in (10) forces \((q',t')=(q_h,v_h)\).  Conversely, (14) and complementary slackness force every dual optimum to put zero mass outside \(\mathcal I\), while positivity of \(q_{h,a}\) on \(\mathcal J\) forces stationarity equality there.  Keeping the candidate primal block fixed again yields system (16), so (10) forces uniqueness of the dual block.  We have proved
\[
 \mathcal I_x(p_x+h)=\mathcal I,\qquad
 \mathcal J_x(p_x+h)=\mathcal J.
 \tag{19}
\]
The neighborhood can be chosen uniformly over \(\mathfrak P_{\kappa,\varepsilon}\): there are finitely many rosters and active-set pairs, (8) involves finitely many fixed derivative matrices, (10) is controlled by the common \(\kappa\), (13)--(15) start from four quantities bounded below by that same \(\kappa\), and \(u\) is uniformly bounded.  Together with (7), this establishes the claimed uniform local constancy and the unique active-system representation.

On this common neighborhood, \(E_x(p_x+h)=E_x(p_x)=:E\), \(g_x(p_x+h)=g_x(p_x)=:g\), and \(H_x\) is affine.  Thus \(\Delta_h=DH_x(p_x)[h]\).  From \(H_hu_h=g=Hu\),
\[
 u_h-u=-H^{-1}\Delta_hu_h,
\]
so
\[
 u_h-u+H^{-1}\Delta_hu
 =-H^{-1}\Delta_h(u_h-u).
\]
Equations (9), (10), and (12) therefore yield a class-uniform constant \(C<\infty\) such that
\[
 \left\|u_h-u+H^{-1}DH_x(p_x)[h]u\right\|
 \leq C\|h\|^2.
 \tag{20}
\]
Because \(T_x(p_x)=Eu_x(p_x)\), (20) proves Hadamard differentiability along every admissible sequence in \(\mathcal T_x\), with derivative
\[
 DT_x(p_x)[h]
 =-E_x(p_x)H_x(p_x)^{-1}DH_x(p_x)[h]u_x(p_x)
 =J_x(p_x)[h].
 \tag{21}
\]
The same display proves that \(g_x\) is constant on the face and that all local parameter dependence in the active equations occurs through \(G_{\mathcal I_x,\mathcal J_x}(p_x)\) inside \(H_x(p_x)\).

We now establish the facet-normal assertions.  Work on a fixed-support cell on which the response roster and the two active sets are fixed.  For \(s=(b,\beta)\), put
\[
 m_\beta(p_x)=C_\beta\widetilde B_\beta^{-1}\widetilde p_x.
\]
Then
\[
 G_{s,a}(p_x)=m_{\beta,b}(p_x)-m_{\beta,a}(p_x),
 \qquad G_{s,b}(p_x)=0,
 \tag{22}
\]
and \(a_{x,s}(p_x)=(G_{s,\cdot}(p_x)^\top,-1)^\top\) is affine.  If
\[
 (I-\bar a_{x,s}\bar a_{x,s}^\top)Da_{x,s}(p_x)[h]=0,
\]
then \(Da_{x,s}(p_x)[h]=c\,a_{x,s}(p_x)\) for some scalar \(c\).  The last coordinate of the derivative is zero while the last coordinate of \(a_{x,s}\) is \(-1\), so \(c=0\).  Hence
\[
 (I-\bar a_{x,s}\bar a_{x,s}^\top)Da_{x,s}(p_x)[h]=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 Da_{x,s}(p_x)[h]=0.
 \tag{23}
\]
By the definition of \(\omega_x(p_x)\), with directions taken in the tangent space (1), \(\omega_x(p_x)=0\) precisely when every active normal has zero derivative in every tangent direction.  The derivatives in (22) are constant on the cell.  Therefore either every active normal is locally constant, which is stronger than constancy up to positive scale, or \(\omega_x\) is strictly positive throughout the cell.  In the latter case its zero set in that cell is empty, hence is the proper algebraic set cut out by \(1=0\).  This proves the asserted dichotomy.

It remains to connect nonzero normal motion to the claimed impossibility of a fixed-left-hand-side epigraph.  First, every active scenario row is nonredundant after redundant inequalities are removed.  Suppose instead that an active row \(s\), whose unique dual weight satisfies \(\lambda_s>0\), were implied by the other scenario inequalities, the inequalities \(-q_a\leq0\), and the affine constraint \(\mathbf 1^\top q=1\).

We derive the needed multiplier certificate.  Let \(d=(\mathbf 1,0)\) and let \(\mathcal K_s\) be the cone generated by
\[
 \{a_{x,r}:r\ne s\}\cup
 \{-(e_a,0):a\in\mathcal A\}\cup\{-d\}.
\]
This finitely generated cone is closed.  Indeed, any one of its elements has a nonnegative representation using a linearly independent subfamily of generators: if its positive-coefficient generators are dependent, choose a nonzero dependence, reverse its sign if necessary so that it has a positive coordinate, and subtract the largest nonnegative multiple that leaves every coefficient nonnegative; this removes at least one positive coefficient and preserves the represented vector.  For a convergent sequence in \(\mathcal K_s\), finiteness permits a subsequence whose representations use one fixed independent subfamily.  The coefficient map on that subfamily has a continuous inverse on its span, so the coefficients converge to nonnegative limits and the vector limit remains in \(\mathcal K_s\).

If \(a_{x,s}\notin\mathcal K_s\), let \(k_0\) minimize its Euclidean distance to the nonempty closed convex cone \(\mathcal K_s\), and set \(y=a_{x,s}-k_0\).  Such a minimizer exists because \(0\in\mathcal K_s\), so a minimizing sequence can be restricted to the compact ball of radius \(2\|a_{x,s}\|\), and closedness retains its limit.  For every \(k\in\mathcal K_s\), differentiating at zero the squared distance from \(a_{x,s}\) to \(k_0+t(k-k_0)\) gives
\[
 y^\top(k-k_0)\leq0.
\]
Taking \(k=0\) and \(k=2k_0\) yields \(y^\top k_0=0\), and hence
\[
 y^\top k\leq0\quad(k\in\mathcal K_s),
 \qquad
 y^\top a_{x,s}=\|y\|^2>0.
\]
Write \(y=(\bar q,\bar t)\).  Applying these inequalities to the generators shows \(\bar q_a\geq0\), \(\mathbf 1^\top\bar q\geq0\), and \(a_{x,r}^\top y\leq0\) for every \(r\ne s\).  If \(\mathbf 1^\top\bar q=0\), nonnegativity gives \(\bar q=0\), while the scenario inequalities give \(\bar t\geq0\); then \(a_{x,s}^\top y=-\bar t\leq0\), a contradiction.  Therefore \(\mathbf 1^\top\bar q>0\).  Dividing \(y\) by this positive number gives a point satisfying \(\mathbf 1^\top q=1\), every nonnegativity constraint, and every retained scenario inequality, but violating row \(s\), contradicting redundancy.  Thus \(a_{x,s}\in\mathcal K_s\).  By the definition of that cone, there are numbers \(\rho_r\geq0\), \(\gamma_a\geq0\), and \(\nu\leq0\) such that
\[
 a_{x,s}
 =\sum_{r\ne s}\rho_r a_{x,r}
   -\sum_a\gamma_a(e_a,0)+\nu(\mathbf 1,0).
 \tag{24}
\]
The sign restriction \(\nu\leq0\) is the nonnegative coefficient condition for the generator \(-d\).  Evaluating (24) at the optimum, where row \(s\) is tight, writes zero as a sum of nonpositive terms.  Thus every retained inequality with a positive multiplier is tight and \(\nu=0\).  Comparing last coordinates gives \(\sum_{r\ne s}\rho_r=1\).  Let
\[
 c=G_x(p_x)^\top\lambda-\eta_x\mathbf 1\geq0
\]
be the vector of dual slacks.  Replace
\[
 \lambda_s\text{ by }0,\qquad
 \lambda_r\text{ by }\lambda_r+\lambda_s\rho_r\ (r\ne s),
 \qquad c\text{ by }c+\lambda_s\gamma.
 \tag{25}
\]
Equation (24), \(\nu=0\), and \(\sum_r\rho_r=1\) show directly that (25) preserves stationarity, dual feasibility, total dual mass one, and the dual objective, but it changes the dual solution because \(\lambda_s>0\).  This contradicts Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}unique\mbox{-}primal\mbox{-}dual\mbox{-}face}\).  Thus every row in \(\mathcal I\) is nonredundant.

The epigraph has nonempty relative interior in the affine hull \(\mathbf 1^\top q=1\): choose \(q_a>0\) for every \(a\) and then take \(t\) strictly larger than all finitely many values \(G_{s,\cdot}(p_x)q\).  A nonredundant row in a finite description of such a polyhedron cuts a relative facet.  Indeed, after deleting that row there is a point satisfying every retained inequality but violating the deleted one.  The segment from this point to the displayed strict relative-interior point first reaches the deleted row at a point where every retained inequality is strict, giving a relatively open subset of the row's hyperplane.  Hence every row in \(\mathcal I\) defines a facet relative to \(\mathbf 1^\top q=1\).

Suppose, contrary to the theorem, that a neighborhood of a law with \(\omega_x(p_x)>0\) admitted an irredundant epigraph description in the original \((q,t)\)-coordinates with a fixed left-hand coefficient matrix and with all dependence on the law confined to the right-hand side.  Two inequalities defining the same relative facet have positively proportional linear parts modulo a multiple of \((\mathbf 1,0)\): their restrictions to
\[
 \{(d_q,d_t):\mathbf 1^\top d_q=0\}
\]
are nonzero linear functionals having the same codimension-one kernel and the same feasible side.  The fixed description has finitely many rows, while every active facet varies continuously by (22), so after shrinking the neighborhood each active facet matches one fixed row.  Normalize that row's nonzero \(t\)-coefficient to \(-1\).  Equality of the relative facets then gives
\[
 a_{x,s}(p')=(d_s^\top,-1)^\top+c_s(p')(\mathbf 1,0)
 \tag{26}
\]
throughout the smaller neighborhood.  The scale in (26) is constant because the last coordinate of \(a_{x,s}\) is always \(-1\).  Moreover, (22) gives \(G_{s,b}(p')=0\) at every \(p'\); the \(b\)-th coordinate in (26) therefore yields \(c_s(p')=-d_{s,b}\), which is constant.  Every active \(a_{x,s}(p')\) would consequently be locally constant.  This contradicts \(\omega_x(p_x)>0\) by (23), proving the moving-left-hand-coefficient assertion.

We finish with the stochastic conclusions.  Define
\[
 \widehat\pi_{xz}=\frac{N_{xz}}n,
 \qquad
 \psi_{x,z,a,y}(O_i)
 =\frac{\mathbf 1\{X_i=x,Z_i=z\}}{\pi_{xz}}
  \left(\mathbf 1\{A_i=a,Y_i=y\}-p_{xzay}\right).
 \tag{27}
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}stratum\mbox{-}instrument\mbox{-}support}\) states \(\pi_{xz}\geq\pi_{\min}>0\), so every division in (27) is valid.  Direct conditioning on \((X,Z)\) gives
\[
 E_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}}\psi_x(O_i)=0,
 \qquad
 \|\psi_x(O_i)\|\leq C_\psi\pi_{\min}^{-1}
 \tag{28}
\]
for a finite constant \(C_\psi\) depending only on the fixed alphabet.  Put
\[
 S_{xzay,n}=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
 \mathbf 1\{X_i=x,Z_i=z\}
 \left(\mathbf 1\{A_i=a,Y_i=y\}-p_{xzay}\right).
\]
On \(N_{xz}>0\), the following ratio identity is exact:
\[
 \widehat p_{xzay}-p_{xzay}
 =\frac{S_{xzay,n}}{\widehat\pi_{xz}}
 =\frac{S_{xzay,n}}{\pi_{xz}}
  +\frac{S_{xzay,n}(\pi_{xz}-\widehat\pi_{xz})}
         {\pi_{xz}\widehat\pi_{xz}}.
 \tag{29}
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}iid\mbox{-}sampling}\) makes both centered sample averages in the numerator of the remainder have variance at most \(n^{-1}\).  Chebyshev's inequality therefore gives, uniformly in \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\),
\[
 S_{xzay,n}=O_P(n^{-1/2}),\qquad
 \widehat\pi_{xz}-\pi_{xz}=O_P(n^{-1/2}).
 \tag{30}
\]
The same variance calculation and the support margin give
\[
 \sup_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}}
 P_{\mathrm{obs}}\!\left(\widehat\pi_{xz}<\frac{\pi_{xz}}2\right)
 \leq\frac{4}{n\pi_{\min}^2}\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 P_{\mathrm{obs}}(N_{xz}=0)
 =(1-\pi_{xz})^n\leq e^{-n\pi_{\min}}.
 \tag{31}
\]
On the complement of the first exceptional event in (31), the absolute remainder in (29) is bounded by
\[
 2\pi_{\min}^{-2}|S_{xzay,n}|\,
       |\widehat\pi_{xz}-\pi_{xz}|.
\]
The product of the two uniform \(O_P(n^{-1/2})\) variables in (30) is uniformly \(O_P(n^{-1})\): for any \(M>0\), intersect the two events on which their root-\(n\) magnitudes are at most \(M\), and on that intersection the root-\(n\) magnitude of the product is at most \(M^2/\sqrt n\).  Since there are finitely many cells, (27)--(31) prove
\[
 \widehat p_x-p_x
 =\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\psi_x(O_i)+r_{x,n},
 \qquad
 \sup_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}\in\mathfrak P_{\kappa,\varepsilon}}
 P_{\mathrm{obs}}\!\left(\sqrt n\|r_{x,n}\|>\delta\right)
 \longrightarrow0
 \tag{32}
\]
for every \(\delta>0\).  The same calculations give \(\widehat p_x-p_x=O_P(n^{-1/2})\) uniformly.

True zero cells have zero empirical counts almost surely.  Every positive population cell is at least \(\varepsilon\) by Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}fixed\mbox{-}cell\mbox{-}support}\).  Thus (6), (19), (31), and (32) imply that \(\widehat p_x\) belongs to the same compatible fixed-support active cell as \(p_x\) with probability tending to one uniformly.  To make \(T_x(\widehat p_x)\) a total random vector on the exceptional event where the local unique-face map is unavailable, fix any deterministic value in \(\Delta_K\times[-1,1]\); this measurable completion is a proof-level convention and cannot affect a conclusion after multiplication by an event whose probability tends uniformly to zero.  Applying the uniform quadratic remainder (20) with \(h=\widehat p_x-p_x\), then using (32), gives
\[
 \sqrt n\{T_x(\widehat p_x)-T_x(p_x)\}
 =\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^nJ_x(p_x)\psi_x(O_i)+o_P(1)
 \tag{33}
\]
uniformly over \(\mathfrak P_{\kappa,\varepsilon}\).  Indeed, the linear image of \(r_{x,n}\) is uniformly \(o_P(1)\), and the quadratic remainder obeys
\[
 \sqrt n\,\|\widehat p_x-p_x\|^2=o_P(1)
\]
uniformly.  Equation (33) is the first asserted limit.

Finally, (10), the finite collection of derivatives in (8), and the uniform bound on \(u_x\) imply through (21) that
\[
 \sup_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}\in\mathfrak P_{\kappa,\varepsilon}}
 \|J_x(p_x)\|<\infty.
 \tag{34}
\]
The vector
\[
 \xi_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}}(O_i)=J_x(p_x)\psi_x(O_i)
\]
has mean zero by (28), ranges over the fixed finite alphabet \(\mathcal X\times\mathcal Z\times\mathcal A\times\{0,1\}\), and is uniformly bounded by (28) and (34).  Its covariance is
\[
 E\{\xi_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}}(O_i)\xi_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}}(O_i)^\top\}
 =J_x(p_x)\Sigma_x(p_x)J_x(p_x)^\top.
\]
The already established Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}finite\mbox{-}alphabet\mbox{-}uniform\mbox{-}clt}\), which permits singular covariance matrices, therefore yields
\[
 \sup_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}\in\mathfrak P_{\kappa,\varepsilon}}
 d_{\mathrm{BL}}\!\left(
 \mathcal L_{P_{\mathrm{obs}}}\!\left(
  \frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^nJ_x(p_x)\psi_x(O_i)\right),
 N\!\left(0,J_x(p_x)\Sigma_x(p_x)J_x(p_x)^\top\right)
 \right)\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{35}
\]
For any \(\delta>0\), coupling the two vectors in (33) on their common sample bounds their bounded-Lipschitz distance by \(\delta+2P(\|o_P(1)\|>\delta)\).  This bound tends to zero uniformly.  Combining (33), (35), and the triangle inequality proves the asserted uniform Gaussian approximation.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:pure-rule-iff]\label{thm:pure-rule-iff}
For every compatible \(p_x\) and action \(j\in\mathcal A\), \(e_j\in Q_x^*(p_x)\) if and only if \(\mathcal K_j(p_x)\neq\varnothing\). Enumerating the response-fiber bases that generate \(G_x(p_x)\) turns this condition into a finite Boolean combination of weak polynomial inequalities in the observable cells \(p_{xzay}\); this is an observable necessary-and-sufficient certificate, not merely a sufficient dominance check.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By the finite representation in \(\mathrm{lem{:}joint\text{-}saddle\text{-}background}\), put \(f(q)=\max_sG_{s,\cdot}(p_x)q\), where \(s=(b,\beta)\).  At \(e_j\), the active affine functions are exactly \(\mathcal S_j(p_x)\), and their convex hull is the subdifferential:
\[
 \partial f(e_j)=\left\{\sum_s\lambda_sG_{s,\cdot}(p_x):\lambda\geq0,\ \mathbf1^\top\lambda=1,\ \lambda_s=0\text{ if }s\notin\mathcal S_j(p_x)\right\}.
\]
This equality follows directly from the maximum formula: the displayed convex combinations give supporting hyperplanes, while separation from their convex hull would produce a direction contradicting the directional derivative \(f'(e_j;d)=\max_{s\in\mathcal S_j}G_{s,\cdot}d\).

For a convex function on \(\Delta_K\), \(e_j\) is a minimizer if and only if some \(g\in\partial f(e_j)\) satisfies \(g^\top(q-e_j)\geq0\) for every \(q\in\Delta_K\).  Necessity follows by separating \(\partial f(e_j)\) from the negative normal cone if their intersection is empty; sufficiency is the subgradient inequality.  Since the vertices of \(\Delta_K\) are \(e_a\), the condition is equivalent to
\[
 g_a-g_j\geq0\quad(a\in\mathcal A).
\]
Substitution of the displayed convex combination for \(g\) gives precisely
\[
 \sum_{b,\beta}\lambda_{b\beta}\{G_{(b,\beta),a}(p_x)-G_{(b,\beta),j}(p_x)\}\geq0
 \quad(a\in\mathcal A),
\]
together with the remaining defining conditions of \(\mathcal K_j(p_x)\).  Hence \(e_j\in Q_x^*(p_x)\) if and only if \(\mathcal K_j(p_x)\neq\varnothing\).

For observability, enumerate the finitely many nonsingular \(\widetilde B_\beta\).  Each basic coordinate and each entry of \(G_x(p_x)\) is affine in \(\widetilde p_x\), with fixed rational coefficients.  For each proposed feasible roster, worst-state set, and basic feasible solution of the \(\lambda\)-system, feasibility and optimality are finitely many sign conditions on determinants and affine expressions.  Clearing the nonzero constant determinants of \(\widetilde B_\beta\), and taking the finite disjunction over these rosters and bases, yields a finite Boolean combination of weak polynomial inequalities in the observed cells.  Negated weak inequalities encode the strict signs needed to exclude a roster.  Thus the certificate is necessary and sufficient even on ties.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The certificate identifies precisely when randomization has no minimax value despite three or more actions. \textit{Gap.} Stoye2007ManyTreatments gives an explicit spike-state solution, but no observable categorical-instrument criterion for pure optimality over a restricted joint mean polytope. \textit{Consumer.} Applied analysts can screen every stratum for a certified deterministic action before invoking a mixed-policy solver.

\begin{proposition}[thm:rectangle-frontier]\label{thm:rectangle-frontier}
For every compatible \(p_x\), \(v_x^{\Box}(p_x)=v_x(p_x)\) if and only if \(\mathcal F_x^{\mathrm{val}}(p_x)\neq\varnothing\).  Moreover, simultaneous preservation of the value and complete optimizer correspondence,
\[
 v_x^{\Box}(p_x)=v_x(p_x)\quad\text{and}\quad Q_x^{\Box}(p_x)=Q_x^*(p_x),
\]
holds if and only if \(\mathcal F_x^{\mathrm{arg}}(p_x)\) holds.  Both conditions admit finite observable basis certificates.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix a stratum \(x\) and a compatible table \(p_x\).  By Assumption~\texttt{ass:response-fiber-compatibility} and Definitions~\texttt{def:response-fiber} and \texttt{def:action-mean-polytope}, \(\mathcal W_x(p_x)\) is a nonempty compact polytope and its linear image \(\mathcal M_x(p_x)\) is a nonempty compact polytope.  Consequently, every coordinate minimum \(L_{ax}\) and maximum \(U_{ax}\) in Definition~\texttt{def:rectangle} is attained.  Every \(\mu\in\mathcal M_x(p_x)\) therefore satisfies \(L_{ax}\leq\mu_a\leq U_{ax}\) for all \(a\in\mathcal A\), and hence
\[
 \mathcal M_x(p_x)\subseteq\mathcal M_x^{\Box}(p_x).
\tag{1}
\]
This is the set-inclusion step.

Define the two observed-law objective functions
\[
 f(q):=\max_{b\in\mathcal A}h_{\mathcal M_x(p_x)}(e_b-q),
 \qquad
 g(q):=\max_{b\in\mathcal A}h_{\mathcal M_x^{\Box}(p_x)}(e_b-q),
 \qquad q\in\Delta_K.
\tag{2}
\]
By Definitions~\texttt{def:joint-saddle} and \texttt{def:rectangle},
\[
 v_x(p_x)=\min_{q\in\Delta_K}f(q),\quad
 Q_x^*(p_x)=\operatorname*{argmin}_{q\in\Delta_K}f(q),
\quad
 v_x^{\Box}(p_x)=\min_{q\in\Delta_K}g(q),\quad
 Q_x^{\Box}(p_x)=\operatorname*{argmin}_{q\in\Delta_K}g(q).
\tag{3}
\]
Thus \(f\), \(g\), their minima, and their argmin sets are observed-law decision functionals.  Their causal content comes from the sharp response-function projection in Lemma~\texttt{lem:joint-saddle-background}; it is not introduced by (2).

Support functions are monotone under set inclusion: indeed, if \(S\subseteq T\), then for every \(d\),
\[
 h_S(d)=\max_{s\in S}d^\top s\leq\max_{t\in T}d^\top t=h_T(d).
\tag{4}
\]
Applying (4) to (1), first for each \(e_b-q\) and then taking the maximum over \(b\), gives
\[
 f(q)\leq g(q)\quad\text{for every }q\in\Delta_K,
 \qquad
 v_x(p_x)\leq v_x^{\Box}(p_x).
\tag{5}
\]
The functions in (2) are continuous because they are maxima of finitely many affine functions by Lemma~\texttt{lem:joint-saddle-background} and the rectangle calculation below.  Since \(\Delta_K\) is nonempty and compact, all four minimum and argmin objects in (3) are attained and the two argmin sets are nonempty.

We first prove the value certificate.  If \(q_0\in\mathcal F_x^{\mathrm{val}}(p_x)\), Definition~\texttt{def:rectangle-frontier} gives \(g(q_0)\leq v_x(p_x)\).  Combining this inequality with (3) and (5) yields
\[
 v_x(p_x)leq v_x^{\Box}(p_x)leq g(q_0)leq v_x(p_x),
\tag{6}
\]
so \(v_x^{\Box}(p_x)=v_x(p_x)\).  Conversely, suppose that the two values are equal and choose \(q^{\Box}\in Q_x^{\Box}(p_x)\), whose existence was just established.  Equations (3) and the assumed equality give
\[
 g(q^{\Box})=v_x^{\Box}(p_x)=v_x(p_x).
\tag{7}
\]
Definition~\texttt{def:rectangle-frontier} then implies \(q^{\Box}\in\mathcal F_x^{\mathrm{val}}(p_x)\), so that set is nonempty.  This proves the first equivalence.

We next prove the simultaneous value-and-argmin certificate.  Suppose \(\mathcal F_x^{\mathrm{arg}}(p_x)\) holds.  By its definition, every \(q^*\in Q_x^*(p_x)\) belongs to \(\mathcal F_x^{\mathrm{val}}(p_x)\).  Hence
\[
 v_x(p_x)=f(q^*)\leq g(q^*)\leq v_x(p_x).
\tag{8}
\]
It follows from (8) and the value equivalence already proved that \(v_x^{\Box}(p_x)=v_x(p_x)\) and \(q^*\in Q_x^{\Box}(p_x)\).  Therefore
\[
 Q_x^*(p_x)\subseteq Q_x^{\Box}(p_x).
\tag{9}
\]
For the reverse inclusion, take \(q^{\Box}\in Q_x^{\Box}(p_x)\).  Equations (3), (5), and equality of the values imply
\[
 v_x(p_x)\leq f(q^{\Box})\leq g(q^{\Box})
 =v_x^{\Box}(p_x)=v_x(p_x).
\tag{10}
\]
Thus \(f(q^{\Box})=v_x(p_x)\), so \(q^{\Box}\in Q_x^*(p_x)\).  Together with (9), this proves equality of the complete optimizer correspondences.

Conversely, assume simultaneous equality of the values and optimizer correspondences.  For every \(q^*\in Q_x^*(p_x)=Q_x^{\Box}(p_x)\),
\[
 g(q^*)=v_x^{\Box}(p_x)=v_x(p_x).
\tag{11}
\]
Equation (11) places every such \(q^*\) in \(\mathcal F_x^{\mathrm{val}}(p_x)\).  Hence \(Q_x^*(p_x)\subseteq\mathcal F_x^{\mathrm{val}}(p_x)\), which is precisely \(\mathcal F_x^{\mathrm{arg}}(p_x)\) by Definition~\texttt{def:rectangle-frontier}.  This proves the second equivalence, including both directions at tied optimal faces.

It remains to expose the finite observable certificates.  For each feasible basis \(\beta\in\mathcal B_x(p_x)\), put
\[
 m_{x,\beta}:=C_\beta\widetilde B_\beta^{-1}\widetilde p_x.
\tag{12}
\]
The feasible-basis representation in Lemma~\texttt{lem:joint-saddle-background} and Definition~\texttt{def:reduced-saddle-lp} gives
\[
 f(q)=\max_{b\in\mathcal A,\,\beta\in\mathcal B_x(p_x)}
 G_{(b,\beta),\cdot}(p_x)q,
\quad
 L_{ax}=\min_{\beta\in\mathcal B_x(p_x)}e_a^\top m_{x,\beta},
\quad
 U_{ax}=\max_{\beta\in\mathcal B_x(p_x)}e_a^\top m_{x,\beta}.
\tag{13}
\]
The last two identities follow by applying the same vertex enumeration to the linear coordinate objectives \(\mu\mapsto\mu_a\).  All lists in (12)--(13) are finite, and every coefficient is a function of the observable table \(p_x\).

For \(q\in\Delta_K\), the coefficient of \(\mu_b\) in \((e_b-q)^\top\mu\) is \(1-q_b\geq0\), while the coefficient of \(\mu_a\) for \(a\neq b\) is \(-q_a\leq0\).  Coordinatewise maximization over the rectangle therefore gives the explicit affine functions
\[
 \gamma_b(q):=(1-q_b)U_{bx}-\sum_{a\neq b}q_aL_{ax},
 \qquad
 g(q)=\max_{b\in\mathcal A}\gamma_b(q).
\tag{14}
\]
Thus \(v_x(p_x)\) is obtained from the finite reduced saddle linear program, and value preservation is certified by feasibility of the finite linear system
\[
 q\geq0,\qquad \mathbf 1^\top q=1,\qquad
 \gamma_b(q)\leq v_x(p_x)\quad(b\in\mathcal A).
\tag{15}
\]
Indeed, (15) is exactly the membership condition \(q\in\mathcal F_x^{\mathrm{val}}(p_x)\).

Finally define the observable joint-optimal face
\[
 \mathcal P_x^*(p_x):=left\{q\in\Delta_K:
 G_{(c,\beta),\cdot}(p_x)q\leq v_x(p_x)
 \text{ for every }c\in\mathcal A,
 \ \beta\in\mathcal B_x(p_x)\right\}.
\tag{16}
\]
Equations (3) and (13) show that \(\mathcal P_x^*(p_x)=Q_x^*(p_x)\).  Consequently, optimizer preservation is certified by the following \(K\) finite linear-program inequalities:
\[
 \max_{q\in\mathcal P_x^*(p_x)}\gamma_b(q)\leq v_x(p_x)
 \qquad(b\in\mathcal A).
\tag{17}
\]
Condition (17) is equivalent to \(Q_x^*(p_x)\subseteq\mathcal F_x^{\mathrm{val}}(p_x)\), hence to \(\mathcal F_x^{\mathrm{arg}}(p_x)\).  Weak inequalities in (15)--(17) retain all boundary and tie cases.  This completes the finite, observable, operational certificate.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:stoye-spike-reduction]\label{thm:stoye-spike-reduction}
Suppose \(\mathcal M_x(p_x)\) contains every Stoye spike vector \(s_a\). Then \(R_x(q,p_x)=\max_{a\in\mathcal A}\{(1-q_a)U_{ax}-\sum_{c\neq a}q_cL_{cx}\}\) for every \(q\in\Delta_K\), so \(Q_x^*(p_x)\) and \(v_x(p_x)\) equal the allocation correspondence and value in Proposition 1 of Stoye2007ManyTreatments. For \(K=2\), the formula reduces to the standard binary fractional minimax-regret rule.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For every \(a\), the definition of the marginal extrema gives \(\mathcal M_x(p_x)\subseteq\prod_c[L_{cx},U_{cx}]\).  For \(d=e_a-q\), its \(a\)-th coefficient is \(1-q_a\geq0\), while its \(c\)-th coefficient is \(-q_c\leq0\) for \(c\neq a\).  Therefore
\[
 h_{\mathcal M_x(p_x)}(e_a-q)
 \leq (1-q_a)U_{ax}-\sum_{c\neq a}q_cL_{cx}.
\]
The right-hand side is \((e_a-q)^\top s_a\).  Under the stated spike-feasibility premise \(s_a\in\mathcal M_x(p_x)\), the reverse inequality holds by the definition of a support function.  Hence equality holds for every \(a\), and
\[
 R_x(q,p_x)=\max_{a\in\mathcal A}\left\{(1-q_a)U_{ax}-\sum_{c\neq a}q_cL_{cx}\right\}.
\]
Taking the minimum and the complete argmin over \(q\in\Delta_K\), then applying \(\mathrm{lem{:}stoye\text{-}proposition\text{-}one}\), proves equality with Stoye's allocation value and correspondence.

When \(K=2\), write \(q_1=1-q_0\).  The two displayed regrets are affine functions with opposite slopes; the minimax rule is their intersection when that intersection lies in \([0,1]\), and otherwise the appropriate endpoint.  This is exactly the standard binary fractional minimax-regret rule, including its boundary cases.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:cp-correspondence-coverage]\label{thm:cp-correspondence-coverage}
Under independent sampling, for every \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\in\mathfrak P_{\mathrm{IV}}\), \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\{Q_x^*(p_x)\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal Q}_{x,n}(\alpha)\text{ for all }x\in\mathcal X\}\geq1-\alpha\). The active-basis algorithm computes exactly the union in the definition, including zero-count cells and laws on intersections of optimal faces; the coverage statement is finite-sample and uniform over \(\mathfrak P_{\mathrm{IV}}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(M=2K|\mathcal X||\mathcal Z|\) and let \(E_n\) be the event that every true cell probability belongs to its interval.  Conditional on all row totals \((N_{xz})_{x,z}\), each marginal cell count \(N_{xzay}\) is binomial with total \(N_{xz}\) and success probability \(p_{xzay}\).  By \(\mathrm{lem{:}clopper\text{-}pearson\text{-}cell\text{-}coverage}\), its conditional noncoverage probability is at most \(\alpha/M\); when \(N_{xz}=0\), it is zero by the \([0,1]\) convention.  Dependence among cells is immaterial to the union bound, so
\[
 \Pr(E_n^c\mid(N_{xz})_{x,z})
 \leq\sum_{x,z,a,y}\frac{\alpha}{M}=\alpha.
\]
Taking expectations gives \(\Pr(E_n)\geq1-\alpha\), for every sample size and every law in \(\mathfrak P_{\mathrm{IV}}\).

On \(E_n\), the true table has all interval memberships and multinomial row-sum constraints required by \(\mathcal C_n(\alpha)\).  The response-fiber compatibility assumption supplies \(\mathcal W_x(p_x)\neq\varnothing\), so \(p\in\mathcal C_n(\alpha)\).  The definition of the optimizer confidence set then yields, simultaneously for every \(x\),
\[
 Q_x^*(p_x)\subseteq\bigcup_{p'\in\mathcal C_n(\alpha)}Q_x^*(p'_x)
 =\widehat{\mathcal Q}_{x,n}(\alpha).
\]
Combining this implication with \(\Pr(E_n)\geq1-\alpha\) proves the advertised simultaneous coverage.  Since the lower bound does not depend on the particular law, it is uniform over \(\mathfrak P_{\mathrm{IV}}\).

For computation, the response roster is finite.  On each roster, primal feasibility, dual feasibility, complementarity, and equality of the primal and dual values are polynomial equalities and weak inequalities in \((p',q,t,\lambda)\), because every entry of \(G_x(p'_x)\) is affine in \(p'_x\).  Enumerating all primal--dual active bases therefore enumerates the complete semialgebraic KKT graph.  At a tied law, the full optimizer face is the convex hull of its optimal primal basic solutions, so retaining the face, rather than selecting one basic optimizer, includes every mixture on an intersection.  The interval convention at a zero row imposes no missing constraint, and weak KKT inequalities retain boundary and zero-count cases.  Projection of this finite graph over \(p'\in\mathcal C_n(\alpha)\) is exactly the union in the definition.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:deployed-regret-certificate]\label{thm:deployed-regret-certificate}
On the simultaneous event \(p\in\mathcal C_n(\alpha)\), every data-dependent deployed mixture \(\widetilde q_x\in\Delta_K\) satisfies \(R_x(\widetilde q_x,p_x)\leq\overline R_{x,n}(\widetilde q_x;\alpha)\) for every \(x\). Consequently, with probability at least \(1-\alpha\), all deployed-stratum inequalities hold simultaneously, including the reported numerical primal--dual gap.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a realization on which \(p\in\mathcal C_n(\alpha)\), a stratum \(x\), and any measurable or nonmeasurable-in-the-data realized choice \(\widetilde q_x\in\Delta_K\).  The true table is one of the points over which the defining supremum is taken, and hence
\[
 R_x(\widetilde q_x,p_x)
 =\max_{b\in\mathcal A}h_{\mathcal M_x(p_x)}(e_b-\widetilde q_x)
 \leq\sup_{p'\in\mathcal C_n(\alpha)}\max_{b\in\mathcal A}h_{\mathcal M_x(p'_x)}(e_b-\widetilde q_x)
 =\overline R_{x,n}(\widetilde q_x;\alpha).
\]
This pointwise argument allows arbitrary data dependence of the deployed rule.  If a numerical primal--dual pair reports an upper endpoint \(U\) and a verified nonnegative gap \(\gamma\), weak duality gives that the exact supremum is at most \(U+\gamma\); the augmentation required in \texttt{def:deployed-certificate} therefore preserves the displayed inequality.

The event used in the proof of \(\mathrm{thm{:}cp\text{-}correspondence\text{-}coverage}\) has probability at least \(1-\alpha\) and implies \(p\in\mathcal C_n(\alpha)\).  The displayed deterministic inequality then holds for every stratum simultaneously on that event, proving the probability statement.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} Coverage of an optimizer set does not by itself certify the actual policy sent to deployment. \textit{Gap.} ClopperPearson1934Binomial supplies cellwise coverage, but the decision-level robust regret certificate and solver-error accounting are absent from categorical-instrument policy learning. \textit{Consumer.} A clinical or public-policy deployment can report an auditable upper bound on worst-case welfare loss for the mixture actually used.

\begin{lemma}[lem:joint-saddle-background]\label{lem:joint-saddle-background}
For every \(P_{\mathrm{obs}}\in\mathfrak P_{\mathrm{IV}}\) and stratum \(x\), the published response-function compatibility fiber projects to \(\mathcal M_x(p_x)=\{Cw_x:w_x\in\mathcal W_x(p_x)\}\). Composing that polytope with the standard finite-scenario randomized-regret program gives \(R_x(q,p_x)=\max_{b,\beta}G_{(b,\beta),\cdot}(p_x)q\), and feasible-basis enumeration returns \(Q_x^*(p_x)\) and \(v_x(p_x)\), including tied optimal faces.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix a stratum \(x\).  Conditional response-type exogeneity, treatment consistency, and outcome exclusion imply that the latent vector
\[
 r=\bigl((A(z))_{z\in\mathcal Z},(Y(a))_{a\in\mathcal A}\bigr)
\]
takes values in the finite set \(\mathcal R\), has a conditional distribution \(w_x\) which does not depend on \(Z\), and satisfies
\[
 p_{xzay}
 =\Pr\{A(z)=a,Y(a)=y\mid X=x\}
 =\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}B_{(z,a,y),r}w_{x,r}.
\]
Deleting one redundant cell equation in each instrument row and retaining normalization therefore gives exactly the equalities and inequalities in \(\mathcal W_x(p_x)\).  Conversely, for any \(w_x\in\mathcal W_x(p_x)\), draw \(r\) conditionally on \(X=x\) with probabilities \(w_x\), independently draw \(Z\) from its observed conditional law given \(X=x\), and set \(A=A(Z)\) and \(Y=Y(A)\).  This construction obeys the three structural assumptions and reproduces every observed cell.  Thus it proves both directions of the compatibility assertion, rather than defining the causal identified set to be the observed fiber.

For such a response law,
\[
 E\{Y(a)\mid X=x\}=\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}y_r(a)w_{x,r}=(Cw_x)_a.
\]
Consequently the sharp identified set of the causal action-mean vector is
\[
 \Theta_I(p_x)=\{(E\{Y(a)\mid X=x\})_{a\in\mathcal A}:w_x\text{ is compatible with }p_x\}
 =\{Cw_x:w_x\in\mathcal W_x(p_x)\}=\mathcal M_x(p_x).
\]
The converse construction in the preceding paragraph establishes sharpness of this equality.

Write \(d=\operatorname{rank}(\widetilde B)\).  The fiber is a nonempty bounded polytope.  Every one of its vertices is a basic feasible solution: indeed, if the positive columns at a vertex were linearly dependent, a nonzero null direction supported on those columns could be followed with both signs for a sufficiently small distance while preserving nonnegativity and \(\widetilde B w_x=\widetilde p_x\), contradicting extremality.  Extending its positive independent columns to \(d\) independent columns yields a basis \(\beta\) and the basic vector \(w_{x,\beta}=\widetilde B_\beta^{-1}\widetilde p_x\geq0\).  Conversely, every such basic vector is feasible.  A linear functional on the compact fiber reaches its maximum at a vertex, and hence, for \(d_b=e_b-q\),
\[
 h_{\mathcal M_x(p_x)}(d_b)
 =\max_{\beta\in\mathcal B_x(p_x)}d_b^\top C_\beta\widetilde B_\beta^{-1}\widetilde p_x
 =\max_{\beta\in\mathcal B_x(p_x)}G_{(b,\beta),\cdot}(p_x)q.
\]
Taking the maximum over \(b\) gives
\[
 R_x(q,p_x)=\max_{b,\beta}G_{(b,\beta),\cdot}(p_x)q.
\]
The epigraph formulation in \texttt{def:reduced-saddle-lp} is therefore exactly, rather than approximately, the observed-law minimax functional.  Its optimal \(q\)-face and value are \(Q_x^*(p_x)\) and \(v_x(p_x)\); retaining every optimal basic solution and its optimal face includes ties and degeneracy.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The lemma fixes the population optimization notation consumed by the coefficient-varying KKT and inference results. \textit{Gap.} SongEtAl2026CategoricalIV supplies the compatible response-law polytope and MastinJailletChin2014RandomizedRegret supplies the finite randomized-regret linear program, so their composition is treated as background rather than a new theorem-level contribution. \textit{Consumer.} All later certificates, the exact witness, and the moving-coefficient limit theorem use this common finite saddle representation.

\begin{theorem}[thm:face-intersection-impossibility]\label{thm:face-intersection-impossibility}
Existential face-intersection model limit.  For every \(K\geq3\) and every support margin satisfying \(0<\pi_{\min}\leq 1/K\), the maintained binary-outcome finite categorical-IV model contains an admissible instance with \(\mathcal X=\{x\}\), \(\mathcal Z=\mathcal A=\{0,\ldots,K-1\}\), and uniform \(Z\), together with observable laws \(P_\tau\in\mathfrak P_{\mathrm{IV}}\), \(|\tau|<1/8\), for which
\[
 Q_x^*(p_x(0))=\operatorname{conv}\{e_0,e_1\},\qquad
 Q_x^*(p_x(\tau))=\{e_1\}\ \text{if }\tau>0,
 \qquad
 Q_x^*(p_x(\tau))=\{e_0\}\ \text{if }\tau<0.
\]
The equality at \(\tau=0\) is a codimension-one optimal-face intersection relative to the full-compliance Bernoulli-mean submodel.  Let \(\widehat S_n\) be any data-based random closed subset of \(\Delta_K\), and assign infinite distance when it is empty.  If
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}P_0^n\!\left\{Q_x^*(p_x(0))\subseteq\widehat S_n\right\}\geq1-\alpha,
\]
then there are constants \(c>0\) and \(\delta>0\) such that, with \(\tau_n=c/\sqrt n\),
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}P_{\tau_n}^n\!\left\{
 d_{\mathrm H}\!\left(\widehat S_n,Q_x^*(p_x(\tau_n))\right)\geq\sqrt2,
 \quad
 d_{\mathrm{sf}}\!\left(\widehat S_n,Q_x^*(p_x(\tau_n))\right)\geq\sqrt2
 \right\}\geq\delta,
\]
where \(d_{\mathrm H}\) and \(d_{\mathrm{sf}}\) are respectively Hausdorff and support-function distance.  Consequently, no procedure can have both uniform asymptotic containment coverage and uniform consistency, or contraction at any deterministic vanishing rate, over \(\mathfrak P_{\mathrm{IV}}\): the displayed local submodel is already a subclass of \(\mathfrak P_{\mathrm{IV}}\).  This conditional obstruction applies in particular to a random set constructed using \(\mathfrak H_{\mathrm{face}}\) if it satisfies the containment premise.  The finite-sample containment conclusion of Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}cp\text{-}correspondence\text{-}coverage}\) remains valid; this theorem does not assert that its confidence set contracts at a face intersection.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We prove the existential obstruction by constructing the observable local experiment, computing its sharp causal identified set and optimizer correspondence, and then applying an elementary likelihood-ratio comparison.  Thus the argument characterizes one included face intersection; it makes no claim about every intersection.

Fix \(K\geq3\) and \(0<\pi_{\min}\leq1/K\).  Let \(\mathcal X=\{x\}\), let \(\mathcal Z=\mathcal A=\{0,\ldots,K-1\}\), put \(P_\tau(X=x)=1\), and draw \(Z\) uniformly on \(\mathcal Z\).  For \(|\tau|<1/8\), define
\[
 m_0(\tau)=\frac12,\quad
 m_1(\tau)=\frac12+\tau,\quad
 m_a(\tau)=\frac14\quad(2\leq a<K).
\]
Conditional on \(X=x\), draw the coordinates \(Y(a)\), \(a\in\mathcal A\), independently with
\[
 P_\tau\{Y(a)=1\mid X=x\}=m_a(\tau),
\]
independently of \(Z\).  Set \(A(z)=z\) for every \(z\in\mathcal Z\), and then set \(A=A(Z)\) and \(Y=Y(A)\).

We verify each maintained restriction.  Independence of \(Z\) from the complete response type \(((A(z))_{z\in\mathcal Z},(Y(a))_{a\in\mathcal A})\), conditional on \(X\), is exactly Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}instrument\text{-}randomization}\).  The two displayed observation equations are Assumptions \(\mathrm{ass{:}treatment\text{-}consistency}\) and \(\mathrm{ass{:}outcome\text{-}exclusion}\).  Moreover,
\[
 \pi_{xz}=P_\tau(X=x,Z=z)=\frac1K\geq\pi_{\min}>0
\]
for every \(z\), so Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}stratum\text{-}instrument\text{-}support}\) holds.  The product Bernoulli law of the potential-outcome vector, concentrated on the treatment-response map \(a_r(z)=z\), is a nonnegative response-type vector \(w_\tau\) with \(\mathbf1^\top w_\tau=1\).  By construction it reproduces the observed cells, so \(w_\tau\in\mathcal W_x(p_x(\tau))\); hence Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}response\text{-}fiber\text{-}compatibility}\) also holds.  Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}iv\text{-}law\text{-}class}\) therefore gives \(P_\tau\in\mathfrak P_{\mathrm{IV}}\).

The observable map is explicit.  It sends the structural law just constructed to the law of \(O=(X,Z,A,Y)\) with \(X=x\) almost surely, \(P_\tau(Z=z\mid X=x)=1/K\), and
\[
 p_{xzay}(\tau)
 =P_\tau(A=a,Y=y\mid X=x,Z=z)
 =\mathbf1\{a=z\}m_z(\tau)^y\{1-m_z(\tau)\}^{1-y}.
\]
The unobserved causal target in this stratum is the \(K\)-vector
\[
 \theta_x(P_\tau)
 =\bigl(E_\tau\{Y(a)\mid X=x\}\bigr)_{a\in\mathcal A}
 \in[0,1]^K.
\]
Its identified set under the maintained response-type restrictions is
\[
 \Theta_I(P_\tau)
 =\left\{\theta\in[0,1]^K:
       \text{there is }w\in\mathcal W_x(p_x(\tau))\text{ with }\theta=Cw\right\}
 =\mathcal M_x(p_x(\tau)),
\]
where the last equality is Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}action\text{-}mean\text{-}polytope}\).  We now compute this observed-law functional, rather than identifying it with the causal target by definition.

Take any \(w\in\mathcal W_x(p_x(\tau))\).  Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}response\text{-}fiber}\) retains all but one observed-cell equation for each \(z\), as well as \(\mathbf1^\top w=1\).  For a fixed \(z\), both the model-implied cells and the observed cells sum to one.  The deleted equation is consequently the difference of these two unit-sum identities and the retained equations.  Thus all original equations hold: \(Bw=p_x(\tau)\).  Every observed off-diagonal cell with \(a\neq z\) is zero.  Since \(w\geq0\),
\[
 w_r>0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad a_r(z)=z
 \quad\text{for every }z\in\mathcal Z.
\]
Indeed, a positive-weight type with \(a_r(z)\neq z\) would contribute positive mass to a zero off-diagonal cell in \(Bw=p_x(\tau)\), a contradiction.  Hence, for every \(a\in\mathcal A\), the equation for the observed cell \((Z,A,Y)=(a,a,1)\) yields
\[
 (Cw)_a
 =\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}w_r y_r(a)
 =P_\tau(Y=1\mid X=x,Z=a)
 =m_a(\tau).
\]
It follows that every compatible response law has the same action-mean vector.  Conversely, the constructed \(w_\tau\) is compatible and has that vector.  Therefore the identifying bound is sharp and in fact a singleton:
\[
 \Theta_I(P_\tau)=\mathcal M_x(p_x(\tau))=\{m(\tau)\}.
\]

We next solve the observed-law decision problem by simplex extreme-point algebra.  Definitions \(\mathrm{def{:}joint\text{-}saddle}\) and \(\mathrm{def{:}action\text{-}mean\text{-}polytope}\) give, for \(q\in\Delta_K\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 R_x(q,p_x(\tau))
 &=\max_{b\in\mathcal A}(e_b-q)^\top m(\tau)\\
 &=\max_{b\in\mathcal A}m_b(\tau)-q^\top m(\tau).
 \end{aligned}
\]
The first term does not depend on \(q\).  The second term is a convex combination of the coordinates of \(m(\tau)\), and is maximized exactly when \(q\) is supported on the set of maximizing coordinates.  Since \(m_a(\tau)=1/4\) for \(a\geq2\) and \(|\tau|<1/8\), direct comparison gives
\[
 Q_x^*(p_x(0))=\operatorname{conv}\{e_0,e_1\},
\]
and
\[
 Q_x^*(p_x(\tau))=\{e_1\}\quad(\tau>0),
 \qquad
 Q_x^*(p_x(\tau))=\{e_0\}\quad(\tau<0).
\]
For completeness, consider the full-compliance Bernoulli-mean family with \(m\) varying in a sufficiently small open neighborhood of \((1/2,1/2,1/4,\ldots,1/4)\).  In that family the two leading actions tie exactly on the affine hyperplane \(m_0-m_1=0\), whose gradient \(e_0-e_1\) is nonzero.  The map from \(m\) to the displayed observable cell table is injective and affine, because the cell \((z,a,y)=(a,a,1)\) equals \(m_a\).  Hence the tie set is codimension one relative to this observable full-compliance submodel.  This establishes the promised explicit optimal-face intersection.

Let \(\widehat S_n\) be any data-based random closed subset of \(\Delta_K\).  A nonempty realization is compact because \(\Delta_K\) is compact.  For nonempty compact \(S,T\subseteq\Delta_K\), let \(d_{\mathrm H}(S,T)\) be Hausdorff distance and define
\[
 h_S(u)=\sup_{s\in S}u^\top s,
 \qquad
 d_{\mathrm{sf}}(S,T)
 =\sup_{\lVert u\rVert_2\leq1}|h_S(u)-h_T(u)|.
\]
Assign both distances the value \(+\infty\) if \(\widehat S_n\) is empty.  Suppose the containment premise holds at \(P_0\), and write
\[
 E_n=\left\{\operatorname{conv}\{e_0,e_1\}\subseteq\widehat S_n\right\}.
\]
Then
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}P_0^n(E_n)\geq1-\alpha.
\]

Fix \(c>0\), to be chosen below, and set \(\tau_n=c/\sqrt n\).  Only the two cells \((Z,A,Y)=(1,1,1)\) and \((1,1,0)\) vary between \(P_{\tau_n}\) and \(P_0\), by amounts \(\tau_n/K\) and \(-\tau_n/K\) in the unconditional law.  Thus this is an \(n^{-1/2}\)-local observable sequence through \(P_0\).  The one-observation likelihood ratio \(L_\tau=dP_\tau/dP_0\) is
\[
 L_\tau=
 \begin{cases}
 1+2\tau,&(Z,A,Y)=(1,1,1),\\
 1-2\tau,&(Z,A,Y)=(1,1,0),\\
 1,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
At \(\tau=0\), each exceptional cell has probability \(1/(2K)\).  Direct calculation therefore gives
\[
 E_0(L_\tau-1)^2
 =\frac{1}{2K}(2\tau)^2+\frac{1}{2K}(-2\tau)^2
 =\frac{4\tau^2}{K}.
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}iid\text{-}sampling}\) makes the product likelihood ratio \(L_{\tau_n}^{(n)}=\prod_{i=1}^nL_{\tau_n}(O_i)\).  Because \(E_0L_{\tau_n}=1\), independence and \(1+s\leq e^s\) yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_0\!\left(L_{\tau_n}^{(n)}-1\right)^2
 &=E_0\!\left\{(L_{\tau_n}^{(n)})^2\right\}-1\\
 &=\left\{E_0(L_{\tau_n}^2)\right\}^n-1\\
 &=\left(1+\frac{4\tau_n^2}{K}\right)^n-1\\
 &\leq \exp(4c^2/K)-1.
 \end{aligned}
\]
For every event \(E\), the likelihood-ratio identity followed by Cauchy--Schwarz gives the explicit total-variation comparison
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |P_{\tau_n}^n(E)-P_0^n(E)|
 &\leq\frac12E_0|L_{\tau_n}^{(n)}-1|\\
 &\leq\frac12\left[E_0\!\left\{(L_{\tau_n}^{(n)}-1)^2\right\}\right]^{1/2}\\
 &\leq \rho_K(c):=\frac12\sqrt{\exp(4c^2/K)-1}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Since \(\rho_K(c)\to0\) as \(c\downarrow0\) and \(\alpha\in(0,1)\), choose \(c>0\) so that \(\rho_K(c)<1-\alpha\), and put
\[
 \delta=1-\alpha-\rho_K(c)>0.
\]
Applying the comparison to \(E_n\) gives
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}P_{\tau_n}^n(E_n)
 \geq1-\alpha-\rho_K(c)=\delta.
\]

For every sufficiently large \(n\), \(\tau_n\in(0,1/8)\), and the exact optimizer correspondence under \(P_{\tau_n}\) is \(\{e_1\}\).  On \(E_n\), the set \(\widehat S_n\) contains \(e_0\).  Consequently,
\[
 d_{\mathrm H}(\widehat S_n,\{e_1\})
 \geq\lVert e_0-e_1\rVert_2=\sqrt2.
\]
For support-function distance, take \(u=(e_0-e_1)/\sqrt2\), so \(\lVert u\rVert_2=1\).  Again on \(E_n\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 d_{\mathrm{sf}}(\widehat S_n,\{e_1\})
 &\geq h_{\widehat S_n}(u)-h_{\{e_1\}}(u)\\
 &\geq u^\top(e_0-e_1)=\sqrt2.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The same event implies both inequalities, and hence
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}P_{\tau_n}^n\!\left\{
 d_{\mathrm H}(\widehat S_n,Q_x^*(p_x(\tau_n)))\geq\sqrt2,
 \quad
 d_{\mathrm{sf}}(\widehat S_n,Q_x^*(p_x(\tau_n)))\geq\sqrt2
 \right\}\geq\delta.
\]

This fixed-error lower bound rules out every vanishing contraction rate.  Let \(r_n>0\) be deterministic with \(r_n\to0\), and let \(M<\infty\).  For all sufficiently large \(n\), \(Mr_n<\sqrt2\), so, for either distance \(d_n\),
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}P_{\tau_n}^n\{d_n/r_n>M\}\geq\delta.
\]
Thus \(d_n/r_n\) is not tight along this admissible local sequence, precluding uniform \(O_P(r_n)\) contraction even on the displayed local submodel.  Since that submodel is contained in \(\mathfrak P_{\mathrm{IV}}\), it also precludes such a uniform guarantee on the full maintained class.  Likewise, for every fixed \(0<\epsilon<\sqrt2\),
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}P_{\tau_n}^n\{d_n>\epsilon\}\geq\delta,
\]
which precludes uniform consistency; choosing \(r_n=n^{-1/2}\) gives the root-\(n\) special case.  Since \(\widehat S_n\) was arbitrary, the conclusion applies conditionally to any output of Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}face\text{-}handle}\) that satisfies the containment premise.  Finally, the already-established Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}cp\text{-}correspondence\text{-}coverage}\) supplies finite-sample containment for the confidence-fiber union even at face intersections.  The present lower bound is compatible with that result because containment does not imply contraction.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:finite-alphabet-uniform-clt]\label{lem:finite-alphabet-uniform-clt}
Let \(d\) be fixed and let \(\mathcal S\) be finite.  For each index \(P\), let \(\xi_P:\mathcal S\to\mathbb R^d\) have mean zero under a probability mass function \(P\), let \(\Gamma_P=E_P(\xi_P\xi_P^\top)\), and suppose \(\sup_{P,s}\|\xi_P(s)\|<\infty\).  If \(\xi_{P,1},\ldots,\xi_{P,n}\) are independent with this common law, then
\[
 \sup_P d_{\mathrm{BL}}\left(\mathcal L_P\left(n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^n\xi_{P,i}\right),N(0,\Gamma_P)\right)\longrightarrow0.
\]
The conclusion permits singular \(\Gamma_P\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let the common bound be \(M\).  For every fixed \(t\in\mathbb R^d\), Taylor's formula for \(e^{iu}\), the zero-mean condition, and \(|t^\top\xi_P|\leq\|t\|M\) give, uniformly in \(P\),
\[
 E_Pe^{it^\top\xi_P/\sqrt n}
 =1-\frac{t^\top\Gamma_Pt}{2n}+O_{t,M}(n^{-3/2}).
\]
Raising this expression to the \(n\)-th power gives
\[
 E_P\exp\left(it^\top n^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^n\xi_{P,i}\right)
 =\exp(-t^\top\Gamma_Pt/2)+o_{t,M}(1),
\]
uniformly in \(P\).

If bounded-Lipschitz convergence were not uniform, there would be a sequence \(P_n\) along which its distance stayed above a positive constant.  The matrices \(\Gamma_{P_n}\) are bounded, so a subsequence converges to a nonnegative semidefinite matrix \(\Gamma\).  The normalized sums are tight because their second moments are \(\operatorname{tr}(\Gamma_{P_n})\leq dM^2\).  Every weakly convergent subsubsequence has, by the preceding characteristic-function identity, characteristic function \(\exp(-t^\top\Gamma t/2)\), and hence law \(N(0,\Gamma)\).  The Gaussian laws \(N(0,\Gamma_{P_n})\) also converge to \(N(0,\Gamma)\).  Bounded-Lipschitz distance metrizes weak convergence in finite-dimensional Euclidean space, producing the required contradiction.  No inverse covariance was used, so singular limits are included.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:full-support-perturbed-witness]\label{prop:full-support-perturbed-witness}
For the three-action witness, let \(u\) be the uniform distribution on all \(3^2 2^3=72\) response types in \(\mathcal R\).  For \(0<\delta<1\), set \(w_\delta=(1-\delta)w^\dagger+\delta u\), let \(X=x\) almost surely, let \(Z\mid X=x\) be uniform on \(\{0,1\}\) and independent of the response type, and impose \(A=A(Z)\) and \(Y=Y(A)\).  The induced conditional table satisfies \(p_{\delta,zay}=(1-\delta)p^\dagger_{zay}+\delta/6\).  The resulting law \(P_\delta\) has \(P_\delta(X=x)=1\), \(P_\delta(X=x,Z=z)=1/2\), \(p_{\delta,zay}\geq\delta/6\), \(P_\delta(X=x,Z=z,A=a,Y=y)\geq\delta/12\), \(P_\delta(A=a\mid X=x,Z=z)\geq\delta/3\), \(P_\delta(A=a\mid X=x)\geq\delta/3\), and
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_{P_\delta}(Y\mid X=x,Z=z,A=a)
 \geq \frac{\delta}{3}\left(1-\frac{\delta}{3}\right)
\]
for every \(z,a,y\).  If \(\pi_{\min}\leq1/2\), then \(P_\delta\in\mathfrak P_{\mathrm{IV}}\).  Moreover, there exist \(\delta_0\in(0,1)\) and disjoint relative neighborhoods \(U_J,U_{\Box}\subset\Delta_3\) of \(q^\dagger=(0,1/4,3/4)\) and \((0,0,1)\), respectively, such that, for every \(0<\delta<\delta_0\),
\[
 v_x^{\Box}(p_\delta)-v_x(p_\delta)\geq\frac1{32},
 \qquad
 Q_x^*(p_\delta)\subset U_J,
 \qquad
 Q_x^{\Box}(p_\delta)\subset U_{\Box}.
\]
No assertion is made that the exact unperturbed optimizers or the exact \(1/16\) value gap persist for \(\delta>0\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
There are \(3^2\) deterministic maps from two instrument values to three actions and \(2^3\) binary potential-outcome vectors, so \(|\mathcal R|=72\).  Under the uniform law \(u\), for each fixed \(z,a,y\), exactly one third of treatment maps have \(A(z)=a\), and independently exactly one half of outcome vectors have \(Y(a)=y\).  Thus
\[
 (Bu)_{zay}=\frac13\frac12=\frac16.
\]
Linearity of the observation map and the equality \(Bw^\dagger=p^\dagger\), proved in Theorem \texttt{thm:three-action-witness}, yield
\[
 p_{\delta,zay}=(Bw_\delta)_{zay}
 =(1-\delta)p^\dagger_{zay}+\frac{\delta}{6}.
\]
The distribution \(w_\delta\) is strictly positive on all response types, is nonnegative, and sums to one.  Drawing this response type independently of the uniform instrument verifies conditional response-type exogeneity.  The imposed equations \(A=A(Z)\) and \(Y=Y(A)\) verify treatment consistency and exclusion.  Finally, \(w_\delta\in\mathcal W_x(p_\delta)\), so the response fiber is nonempty.  Since \(X=x\) almost surely and \(Z\mid X=x\) is uniform,
\[
 P_\delta(X=x)=1,
 \qquad
 P_\delta(X=x,Z=z)=\frac12.
\]
Thus the support assumption also holds whenever \(\pi_{\min}\leq1/2\), proving \(P_\delta\in\mathfrak P_{\mathrm{IV}}\) under that explicit condition.

The conditional-cell formula immediately gives
\[
 p_{\delta,zay}\geq\frac\delta6,
 \qquad
 P_\delta(X=x,Z=z,A=a,Y=y)
 =\frac12p_{\delta,zay}\geq\frac\delta{12}.
\]
Summing over \(y\) gives
\[
 P_\delta(A=a\mid X=x,Z=z)\geq\frac\delta3.
\]
Averaging the last inequality over the two instrument values gives the same lower bound conditional only on \(X=x\).

At the unperturbed table, each treatment marginal \(\sum_y p^\dagger_{zay}\) belongs to \(\{1/4,1/2\}\).  Consequently, for every \(z,a\),
\[
 d_{za,\delta}:=P_\delta(A=a\mid X=x,Z=z)
 \leq(1-\delta)\frac12+\frac\delta3
 =\frac12-\frac\delta6
 \leq\frac12.
\]
Both conditional outcome cells have numerator at least \(\delta/6\), so for \(y=0,1\),
\[
 P_\delta(Y=y\mid X=x,Z=z,A=a)
 =\frac{p_{\delta,zay}}{d_{za,\delta}}
 \geq\frac{\delta/6}{1/2}
 =\frac\delta3.
\]
Division is legitimate because \(d_{za,\delta}\geq\delta/3>0\).  Writing \(s=P_\delta(Y=1\mid X=x,Z=z,A=a)\), we have \(s\in[\delta/3,1-\delta/3]\).  The concave quadratic \(s(1-s)\) takes its minimum over this interval at an endpoint, and hence
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_{P_\delta}(Y\mid X=x,Z=z,A=a)
 =s(1-s)
 \geq\frac\delta3\left(1-\frac\delta3\right).
\]

It remains to prove stability of the two decision problems without claiming constancy of their optimizers.  Put
\[
 \Phi_\delta(q)=R_x(q,p_\delta)
 =\max_{b\in\{0,1,2\}}\max_{w\in\mathcal W_x(p_\delta)}(e_b-q)^\top Cw.
\]
We first prove that \(\Phi_\delta\to\Phi_0\) uniformly on \(\Delta_3\) as \(\delta\downarrow0\).  Let \(\delta_m\downarrow0\) and \(q_m\to q\).  Choose a maximizing pair \((b_m,w_m)\) for \(\Phi_{\delta_m}(q_m)\); existence follows because the comparator set is finite and each nonempty fiber is a closed subset of the compact response simplex.  Along a subsequence, \(b_m=b\) is fixed and \(w_m\to w\).  Passing to the limit in the fiber equalities gives
\[
 w\geq0,\qquad \mathbf1^\top w=1,\qquad \widetilde Bw=\widetilde p^\dagger,
\]
so \(w\in\mathcal W_x(p^\dagger)\).  Therefore
\[
 \limsup_m\Phi_{\delta_m}(q_m)
 =(e_b-q)^\top Cw
 \leq\Phi_0(q).
\]
For the reverse inequality, choose \((b_0,w_0)\) attaining \(\Phi_0(q)\).  Linearity gives
\[
 \bar w_m=(1-\delta_m)w_0+\delta_m u
 \in\mathcal W_x(p_{\delta_m}),
\]
and hence
\[
 \liminf_m\Phi_{\delta_m}(q_m)
 \geq\lim_m(e_{b_0}-q_m)^\top C\bar w_m
 =(e_{b_0}-q)^\top Cw_0
 =\Phi_0(q).
\]
Thus \(\Phi_{\delta_m}(q_m)\to\Phi_0(q)\) for every such pair of sequences.  If convergence were not uniform in \(q\), compactness of \(\Delta_3\) would provide a sequence \(q_m\) with a convergent subsequence contradicting this joint sequential limit and continuity of \(\Phi_0\).  Hence
\[
 \sup_{q\in\Delta_3}|\Phi_\delta(q)-\Phi_0(q)|\longrightarrow0.
\]

The same compactness and mixture argument, applied to the objectives \(e_a^\top Cw\) and \(-e_a^\top Cw\), shows for each action \(a\) that
\[
 L_a(p_\delta)\longrightarrow L_a(p^\dagger),
 \qquad
 U_a(p_\delta)\longrightarrow U_a(p^\dagger).
\]
For a rectangular identified set, the comparator-\(b\) support value has the explicit form
\[
 h_{\mathcal M^{\Box}(p_\delta)}(e_b-q)
 =(1-q_b)U_b(p_\delta)-\sum_{a\ne b}q_aL_a(p_\delta).
\]
Define the rectangle objective by
\[
 \Phi_\delta^{\Box}(q)
 =\max_{b\in\{0,1,2\}}h_{\mathcal M^{\Box}(p_\delta)}(e_b-q).
\]
The endpoint convergence, finiteness of the comparator set, and compactness of \(\Delta_3\) therefore imply uniform convergence of \(\Phi_\delta^{\Box}\) to \(\Phi_0^{\Box}\).

Uniform convergence of objectives gives convergence of their minima, because
\[
 |v_x(p_\delta)-v_x(p^\dagger)|
 \leq\sup_{q\in\Delta_3}|\Phi_\delta(q)-\Phi_0(q)|\longrightarrow0,
\]
and the identical inequality holds for \(v_x^{\Box}\).  Theorem \texttt{thm:three-action-witness} gives
\[
 v_x^{\Box}(p^\dagger)-v_x(p^\dagger)
 =\frac14-\frac3{16}=\frac1{16}.
\]
Therefore there exists \(\delta_1>0\) such that
\[
 v_x^{\Box}(p_\delta)-v_x(p_\delta)\geq\frac1{32}
\]
whenever \(0<\delta<\delta_1\).

Finally, uniform objective convergence and uniqueness at \(\delta=0\) imply upper hemicontinuity of each argmin correspondence at zero.  To see this directly, let \(U\) be a relative neighborhood of the unique minimizer \(q_0\) of either limiting objective.  If optimizers failed eventually to lie in \(U\), there would be \(\delta_m\downarrow0\) and minimizers \(q_m\notin U\).  A subsequence converges in \(\Delta_3\) to some \(\bar q\notin U\).  Uniform convergence and optimality give, for every \(q\in\Delta_3\),
\[
 \Phi_0(\bar q)
 =\lim_m\Phi_{\delta_m}(q_m)
 \leq\lim_m\Phi_{\delta_m}(q)
 =\Phi_0(q),
\]
with the same argument for the rectangle objective.  Thus \(\bar q=q_0\), a contradiction.

Let
\[
 d=\left\|q^\dagger-(0,0,1)\right\|_2=\sqrt{\frac18},
\]
and take the relative open balls
\[
 U_J=\{q\in\Delta_3:\|q-q^\dagger\|_2<d/3\},
 \qquad
 U_{\Box}=\{q\in\Delta_3:\|q-(0,0,1)\|_2<d/3\}.
\]
They are disjoint by the triangle inequality.  Applying the preceding upper-hemicontinuity argument to the two unique unperturbed optimizers gives \(\delta_2>0\) such that both asserted optimizer inclusions hold for \(0<\delta<\delta_2\).  Taking \(\delta_0=\min\{1/2,\delta_1,\delta_2\}\) proves the proposition.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This interior perturbation proves that the joint-versus-rectangle failure is not an artifact of structural-zero observed cells, absent treatment arms, or degenerate conditional outcomes. \textit{Gap.} The exact boundary witness alone has zero cells.  The proposition supplies explicit positive cell, treatment-support, and outcome-variance margins and a perturbation-stable decision gap. \textit{Consumer.} The full-support family is a robust regression target for the released multicategory implementation and for response-basis enumerators near changing rosters.

\section{Interpretation}

The exact witness shows why joint partial-identification geometry matters for categorical treatment choice: the feasible inequality \(\mu_0\leq\mu_2\) changes both the optimal randomized policy and its sharp worst-case regret relative to coordinatewise rectangularization.  A single exact rational matrix simultaneously verifies compatibility and projection for all ten vertices, including the two laws questioned in review.  The uniform-response-type perturbation shows that the failure persists with positive observed cells, positive treatment support, and nondegenerate conditional outcomes, although only a conservative \(1/32\) value gap and disjoint optimizer neighborhoods are asserted away from the exact law.  The decision-theoretic act of randomized minimax regret under partial identification has a direct antecedent in Montiel Olea--Qiu--Stoye; what is specific here is the categorical-IV joint-polytope correspondence and its moving-polytope fixed-law inference.  Separated unique faces admit ordinary root-\(n\) Gaussian analysis, whereas exact confidence-fiber containment remains the appropriate validity claim at face intersections.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The Gaussian policy-and-regret law is restricted to the observable nondegenerate unique-face class with fixed-support, response-basis, rank, slack, reduced-cost, and active-weight margins.  It is a fixed-law plug-in result for a categorical-instrument moving polytope, not a replacement for Montiel Olea--Qiu--Stoye's finite-sample Gaussian admissibility, profiled-regret, or least-randomizing analysis.  The face-intersection theorem is existential and internal to the maintained model: one displayed codimension-one local submodel rules out the specified class-uniform honest contraction at every vanishing rate absent separation.  The Bonferroni confidence-fiber union remains valid but may be conservative and noncontracting.  For the perturbed witness, only a gap of at least \(1/32\) and disjoint optimizer neighborhoods are guaranteed; the exact \(1/16\) gap and exact optimizer formulas are asserted only at \(\delta=0\).

\section{Honest scope}

The maintained causal model is the binary-outcome, finite-stratum specialization of Song et al.'s model M1, with finite randomized instrument support, categorical treatment, response-type exogeneity, consistency, exclusion, positive stratum--instrument support, and response-fiber compatibility.  Response-function projection and finite randomized-regret programming are background ingredients.  Montiel Olea--Qiu--Stoye already analyze randomized minimax-regret statistical decisions under partial identification in a finite-sample Gaussian likelihood; this paper claims neither priority for that decision criterion nor coverage of their admissibility, profiled-regret, or least-randomizing theory.  The delivered objects are the sharp categorical-IV population optimizer correspondence, its moving-basis fixed-law limit law on separated nondegenerate unique faces, and exact confidence-fiber correspondence containment.  Without separation, only an internal existential face-intersection obstruction is claimed.  The exact three-action counterexample is supplemented by full-support perturbations, but its exact optimizer identities and exact \(1/16\) gap are not claimed to persist after perturbation.
