\section*{exp\_\allowbreak{}block\_\allowbreak{}adaptive\_\allowbreak{}ot\_\allowbreak{}variance\_\allowbreak{}envelope}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} Under predictable integer quotas followed by uniform complete randomization within each growing block, past-only AIPW adjustment produces a design-based martingale for the fixed finite-population contrast.  Its predictable quadratic variation lies in a nodewise-exact, pathwise rectangular multi-marginal transport envelope; two-arm sorting and a fixed-\(K\) transportation program compute the endpoints.  Observable assigned-arm residual margins estimate the envelope uniformly at rate \(q_n^{-\kappa}\) over the common moment class and at rate \(q_n^{-1/2}\) over the history-uniform bounded-residual subclass, with neither rate accumulating a block-count factor.  Under the separately imposed uniform predictable-QV stabilization condition, a martingale CLT and the estimated upper endpoint yield conservative Wald coverage.  A globally consistent nonadaptive equal-quota subclass has \(V_n=U_n\) and proves that multiplying the upper endpoint by any fixed \(1-\rho<1\) causes strict asymptotic undercoverage.  Exactness is not claimed for one adaptive completion that simultaneously realizes independently selected optimizers at different history nodes.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Li and Zhao's adaptive design-based AIPW theory permits broader history-measurable block assignment laws and supplies martingale inference under its own covariance normalization, but it does not characterize the sharp residual-coupling range created by predictable fixed-count complete-block randomization.  Static sharp-variance and transport analyses do not supply a history-uniform observable endpoint theorem, conservative adaptive Wald coverage from that endpoint, or the present fixed-fraction no-discount certificate within one admissible finite-population design class.

\paragraph{Niche.} Predictable integer quotas followed by uniform complete-block randomization turn each pre-block conditional variance into a finite-population coupling problem while retaining assignment as the only source of randomness.  Common deterministic overlap, residual-moment, minimum-count, leverage, QV-growth, and QV-stabilization conditions support uniform inference.  The distinction between nodewise rectangular optimization and one globally consistent adaptive-history completion remains explicit throughout.

\paragraph{Fill.} The paper gives the nodewise-exact predictable transport envelope, exact two-arm sorting and fixed-\(K\) LP computation, history-uniform observable endpoint bounds on both the general moment class and the bounded-residual subclass, and conservative martingale Wald inference from the observable upper endpoint.  It also constructs a globally consistent nonadaptive equal-quota subclass on which \(V_n=U_n\), endpoint estimation is consistent, and every fixed multiplicative discount \(1-\rho\) has a strict Gaussian undercoverage limit.  The result does not promote rectangular nodewise attainability into simultaneous attainability by one adaptive-history completion.

\section{Related work}

Li and Zhao (2026), Section 5, Definition 2, allow a broader history-measurable joint block assignment law with positive unit-level marginals, and their adaptive finite-population AIPW results use their own covariance and normalization conditions.  The predictable integer quotas and uniform complete-block draw here are an explicit restriction motivated by quota-controlled batched experiments, not a condition attributed to Li and Zhao.  Gao, Ge, and Qian (2025), equation (1) and Theorem 1, formulate a static multi-arm contrast variance as a quadratic multi-marginal transport problem; Aronow, Green, and Lee (2014), Lemma 1, Proposition 1, and Corollary 1, give the two-arm sorting and sharp-inference prototype; Yu, Zhu, and Liu (2025) aggregate two-arm rearrangement bounds over nonadaptive strata; and Mikhaeil and Green (2024) identify regression residuals as the relevant coupling object for adjusted nonadaptive two-arm estimators.  Those static results are used as attributed validation benchmarks, not as proofs of adaptive history-uniform endpoint estimation or inference.  Ober-Reynolds (2023) studies directional endpoint limits from independent empirical processes.  Saco (2026), Assumption 5.11, uses a different high-probability predictable-QV lower condition, while Theorem 5.14 studentizes by realized quadratic variation.  Harshaw, Middleton, and Savje (2026) optimize admissible quadratic bounds under arbitrary designs and distinguish admissibility from marginal sharpness.  The present equal-quota bounded witness adds a design-based no-discount certificate for the rectangular upper endpoint without asserting cross-history simultaneous optimizer compatibility.  Newby et al. (2025) provide the four-arm mental-health consumer, but their participant-level contextual bandit does not implement fixed block quotas; applying the theorem requires a predictably batched complete-block redesign.

  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Nodewise upper conditional-variance contribution \(U_{nb}^{h}=w_{nb}^2\{\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}c_a^2(S_{nba}^{h})^2/m_{nba}^{h}-J_{nb}^{h,-}/(n_{nb}-1)\}\), with the variances and predictable counts evaluated at \(h\).
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}rho} --- \texttt{fixed real number}; \((0,1)\); \texttt{upper-\allowbreak{}endpoint reduction}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \texttt{Fixed fractional reduction used for a nodewise rectangular upper contribution or for the observable upper endpoint in the fixed-\allowbreak{}quota necessity theorem.}
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}kappa} --- \texttt{fixed positive real number}; \((1/12,1/6)\); \texttt{uniform plug-\allowbreak{}in exponent}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Endpoint-rate exponent \(\kappa=\frac{2+\delta_0}{6(4+\delta_0)}\).
  \item \texttt{r\_\allowbreak{}n} --- \texttt{deterministic positive sequence}; \texttt{uniform plug-\allowbreak{}in rate}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Endpoint-error envelope \(r_n=q_n^{-\kappa}\), which tends to zero.
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}Phi} --- \texttt{function}; \(\mathbb R\to(0,1)\); \texttt{Gaussian coverage map}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Cumulative distribution function of \(\mathcal N(0,1)\).
  \item \texttt{M} --- \texttt{fixed positive real number}; \((0,\infty)\); \texttt{bounded-\allowbreak{}residual envelope}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} \texttt{Common history-\allowbreak{}uniform absolute residual bound for the optional bounded-\allowbreak{}residual subclass.}
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathfrak D\_\allowbreak{}M} --- \texttt{class of triangular experiment-\allowbreak{}analysis arrays}; \texttt{bounded-\allowbreak{}residual inference subclass}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Optional bounded-residual subclass of \(\mathfrak D\) defined in \(\text{def:bounded-residual-arrays}\).
  \item \texttt{\textbackslash{}\allowbreak{}mathfrak E\_\allowbreak{}M} --- \texttt{class of triangular experiment-\allowbreak{}analysis arrays}; \texttt{bounded-\allowbreak{}residual endpoint class}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} Endpoint-only bounded-residual class defined in \(\text{def:bounded-residual-endpoint-arrays}\), omitting residual-moment, block-leverage, and predictable-quadratic-variation conditions.
  \item \texttt{C\_\allowbreak{}M} --- \texttt{positive real number}; \((0,\infty)\); \texttt{bounded-\allowbreak{}residual endpoint-\allowbreak{}error constant}
  \par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Definition.} A constant depending only on \(K,\varepsilon_0,M\), whose existence is proved by \(\text{thm:bounded-residual-parametric-plugin}\).
\end{itemize}

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:fixed-potential-outcomes]\label{ass:fixed-potential-outcomes}
Conditional on baseline information, the array \(\{Y_{nbi}(a):b\le B_n,\ i\in\mathcal U_{nb},\ a\in\mathcal A\}\) is nonrandom under the assignment law.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Fixed finite-population potential outcomes, \cite{LiZhao2026AdaptiveDesignBased}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:consistency-no-interference]\label{ass:consistency-no-interference}
For every unit, \(Y_{nbi}^{\mathrm{obs}}=Y_{nbi}(Z_{nbi})\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Consistency and no interference, \cite{LiZhao2026AdaptiveDesignBased}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:predictable-counts]\label{ass:predictable-counts}
The fixed-count vector \((m_{nba})_{a\in\mathcal A}\) is \(\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\)-measurable for every \(n\) and \(b\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This is an explicit fixed-count complete-block design restriction of the paper. It is implementable in predictably batched platform experiments that update integer arm quotas after each completed batch and then use uniform complete randomization within the next batch; minimum cell sizes and capacity caps can be enforced directly by the quota rule. Li and Zhao's broader block-adaptive framework is not cited as supplying this restriction.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:complete-block-randomization]\label{ass:complete-block-randomization}
Conditional on \(\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\), \((Z_{nbi})_{i\in\mathcal U_{nb}}\) is uniform over assignments having exactly \(m_{nba}\) units in every arm \(a\in\mathcal A\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Complete randomization within a predictable block, \cite{AronowGreenLee2014SharpVariance}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:predictable-adjustment]\label{ass:predictable-adjustment}
The array \((g_{nbi}(a))_{i\in\mathcal U_{nb},\,a\in\mathcal A}\) is \(\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\)-measurable for every \(n\) and \(b\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Past-only adaptive outcome adjustment, \cite{LiZhao2026AdaptiveDesignBased}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:overlap]\label{ass:overlap}
The predictable arm fractions satisfy \(\varepsilon_0\le p_{nba}\le1-(K-1)\varepsilon_0\) almost surely for every \(n\), \(b\), and \(a\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform executed overlap, \cite{LiZhao2026AdaptiveDesignBased}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:contrast-zero-sum]\label{ass:contrast-zero-sum}
The contrast satisfies \(\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}c_a=0\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Multi-arm treatment contrast, \cite{GaoGeQian2025MOT}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:contrast-normalization]\label{ass:contrast-normalization}
The contrast satisfies \(\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}c_a^2=1\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Euclidean contrast normalization, \cite{GaoGeQian2025MOT}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:growing-arm-counts]\label{ass:growing-arm-counts}
The predictable counts satisfy \(\min_{b\le B_n,\,a\in\mathcal A}m_{nba}\ge q_n\) almost surely for every \(n\), where the deterministic lower-count envelope obeys \(q_n\to\infty\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Growing-stratum sampling condition, \cite{YuZhuLiu2025SharpStratified}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:block-leverage]\label{ass:block-leverage}
The design leverage satisfies \(\max_{b\le B_n}\{w_{nb}^2/n_{nb}\}/s_n^2\le\ell_n\) for every \(n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Negligible maximal block leverage, \cite{LiZhao2026AdaptiveDesignBased}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:residual-moments]\label{ass:residual-moments}
The nuisance-adjusted residual arrays satisfy \(\sup_{n,b,a}\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{\mathcal F_{n,b-1}}n_{nb}^{-1}\sum_{i\in\mathcal U_{nb}}|R_{nbi}(a)|^{4+\delta_0}\le C_R\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Uniform finite-population \((4+\delta_0)\)-moment condition, \cite{YuZhuLiu2025SharpStratified}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:qv-growth]\label{ass:qv-growth}
The predictable quadratic variation satisfies \(V_n\ge v_0s_n^2\) almost surely for every \(n\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This common finite-population condition excludes asymptotically deterministic contrasts while allowing heterogeneous residual couplings; it holds, for example, when each root-to-leaf path contains a fixed share of blocks with residual contrast variance bounded below relative to \(s_n^2\).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:qv-stabilization]\label{ass:qv-stabilization}
For every \(\eta>0\), the predictable quadratic variation stabilizes at its deterministic design expectation uniformly over the common-parameter class: \[\sup_{\mathbb D\in\mathfrak D}\Pr_{\mathbb D}\left(\left|\frac{V_n}{E_{\mathbb D}V_n}-1\right|>\eta\right)\longrightarrow0.\]
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} This is an explicit load-bearing high-level regularity condition for the adaptive martingale limit. It is imposed separately and is not derived from predictable quotas, complete-block randomization, overlap, arm-count growth, leverage, residual moments, or predictable-QV growth.
\end{assumption}

\begin{definition}[def:observed-residual-margins]\label{def:observed-residual-margins}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \(\widehat{\bar R}_{nba},\widehat R_{nbi}^{c}(a),\widehat S_{nba}^2,\widehat\mu_{nba}\)
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For \(\mathcal O_{nba}=\{i\in\mathcal U_{nb}:Z_{nbi}=a\}\) of observed size \(|\mathcal O_{nba}|=m_{nba}\), define
\[
\widehat{\bar R}_{nba}=\frac1{m_{nba}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal O_{nba}}\{Y_{nbi}^{\mathrm{obs}}-g_{nbi}(a)\},\qquad \widehat R_{nbi}^{c}(a)=Y_{nbi}^{\mathrm{obs}}-g_{nbi}(a)-\widehat{\bar R}_{nba},
\]
\[
\widehat\mu_{nba}=\frac1{m_{nba}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal O_{nba}}\delta_{\widehat R_{nbi}^{c}(a)},\qquad \widehat S_{nba}^2=\frac1{m_{nba}-1}\sum_{i\in\mathcal O_{nba}}\{\widehat R_{nbi}^{c}(a)\}^2.
\]
Set \(\widehat\mu_{nba}=\delta_{0_{\mathbb R}}\) and \(\widehat S_{nba}^2=0\) when \(m_{nba}=0\), and set \(\widehat S_{nba}^2=0\) when \(m_{nba}=1\). These total conventions are inactive for the endpoint theorem for all sufficiently large \(n\), because \(\min_{b,a}m_{nba}\ge q_n\) and \(q_n\to\infty\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}consistency-\allowbreak{}no-\allowbreak{}interference}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}predictable-\allowbreak{}adjustment}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}growing-\allowbreak{}arm-\allowbreak{}counts}.
\end{definition}

\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Using only the assigned-arm measures and sample variances from \(\text{def:observed-residual-margins}\), define \[\widehat L_n=\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}w_{nb}^2\left\{\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\frac{c_a^2\widehat S_{nba}^2}{m_{nba}}-\frac{\widehat J_{nb}^{+}}{n_{nb}-1}\right\},\qquad \widehat U_n=\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}w_{nb}^2\left\{\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\frac{c_a^2\widehat S_{nba}^2}{m_{nba}}-\frac{\widehat J_{nb}^{-}}{n_{nb}-1}\right\}.\]
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}qv-\allowbreak{}envelope}; \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}observed-\allowbreak{}residual-\allowbreak{}margins}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:twoarm-sorting]\label{def:twoarm-sorting}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} \texttt{Two-\allowbreak{}arm sorting map}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} For \(K=2\) and \(c=(1,-1)/\sqrt2\), let \(F_{nb,a}^{-1}\) be the quantile function of \(\mu_{nba}\) and compute \[J_{nb}^{-}=\frac12\int_0^1\{F_{nb,1}^{-1}(u)-F_{nb,2}^{-1}(u)\}^2\,du,\qquad J_{nb}^{+}=\frac12\int_0^1\{F_{nb,1}^{-1}(u)-F_{nb,2}^{-1}(1-u)\}^2\,du.\]
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}transport-\allowbreak{}values}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:fixed-k-lp]\label{def:fixed-k-lp}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} Fixed-\(K\) empirical transportation program
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Feed the observable measures \(\widehat\mu_{nba}=m_{nba}^{-1}\sum_{i_a\in\mathcal O_{nba}}\delta_{\widehat R_{nbi_a}^{c}(a)}\) from \(\text{def:observed-residual-margins}\) into the finite program. When every \(m_{nba}>0\), optimize over variables \(\pi_{i_1\ldots i_K}\ge0\), indexed by \((i_1,\ldots,i_K)\in\prod_{a=1}^K\mathcal O_{nba}\), the objective \[\sum_{i_1\in\mathcal O_{nb1}}\cdots\sum_{i_K\in\mathcal O_{nbK}}\pi_{i_1\ldots i_K}\left\{\sum_{a=1}^Kc_a\widehat R_{nbi_a}^{c}(a)\right\}^2\] subject, for every \(a\in\mathcal A\) and every \(i_a\in\mathcal O_{nba}\), to the exact marginal constraints \[\sum_{(i_{a'})_{a'\ne a}\in\prod_{a'\ne a}\mathcal O_{nba'}}\pi_{i_1\ldots i_K}=\frac1{m_{nba}}.\] The minimum is \(\widehat J_{nb}^{-}\) and the maximum is \(\widehat J_{nb}^{+}\). If an arm sample is empty, represent its convention \(\widehat\mu_{nba}=\delta_{0_{\mathbb R}}\) by one artificial zero atom of mass one and solve the same marginal-coupling program; the admissible class never invokes this branch.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}transport-\allowbreak{}values}; \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}observed-\allowbreak{}residual-\allowbreak{}margins}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[def:pmone-witness]\label{def:pmone-witness}
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Defined object.} Worked validation: the \(\{-1,-1,1,1\}\) coupling witness
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Construction.} Take one block of size \(n_{nb}=4\), \(K=2\), the unnormalized contrast \(c=(1,-1)\), and both centered marginal lists equal to \(\{-1,-1,1,1\}\). Direct substitution into the two-arm sorting costs gives \[J_{nb}^{-}=\frac14\{(-1+1)^2+(-1+1)^2+(1-1)^2+(1-1)^2\}=0\] under equal-rank pairing and \[J_{nb}^{+}=\frac14\{(-1-1)^2+(-1-1)^2+(1+1)^2+(1+1)^2\}=4\] under reverse-rank pairing. Substitution into \(\text{def:qv-envelope}\) therefore gives \(U_n-L_n=w_{nb}^2(J_{nb}^{+}-J_{nb}^{-})/(n_{nb}-1)=4w_{nb}^2/3>0\) for this single block. Under the normalized contrast \(c=(1,-1)/\sqrt2\), both costs and the width are divided by two. This is a direct arithmetic validation of sorting direction and endpoint signs, not a theorem contribution.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Inputs.} \texttt{def:\allowbreak{}twoarm-\allowbreak{}sorting}.
\end{definition}

No residual-moment, block-leverage, or predictable-quadratic-variation condition is included in \(\mathfrak E_M\). By construction, \(\mathfrak D_M\subseteq\mathfrak E_M\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Member properties.} \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}fixed-\allowbreak{}potential-\allowbreak{}outcomes}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}consistency-\allowbreak{}no-\allowbreak{}interference}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}predictable-\allowbreak{}counts}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}complete-\allowbreak{}block-\allowbreak{}randomization}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}predictable-\allowbreak{}adjustment}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}overlap}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}contrast-\allowbreak{}zero-\allowbreak{}sum}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}contrast-\allowbreak{}normalization}; \texttt{ass:\allowbreak{}growing-\allowbreak{}arm-\allowbreak{}counts}.
\end{definition}

\section{Main results}

\begin{theorem}[thm:exact-predictable-ot-envelope]\label{thm:exact-predictable-ot-envelope}
For every array in \(\mathfrak D\), \((M_{nj},\mathcal F_{n,j})_{j\le B_n}\) is a martingale and \[E(D_{nb}^2\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})=w_{nb}^2\left\{\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\frac{c_a^2S_{nba}^2}{m_{nba}}-\frac1{n_{nb}-1}\int\left(\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}c_ax_a\right)^2d\gamma_{nb}^{\mathrm{true}}\right\},\] where \(\gamma_{nb}^{\mathrm{true}}\in\Gamma_{nb}\) is the joint centered residual coupling in block \(b\). At every \(h\in\mathcal H_{n,b-1}\), the conditional variance range over \(\Gamma_{nb}^{h}\) has endpoints obtained from \(J_{nb}^{h,+}\) and \(J_{nb}^{h,-}\), and both endpoints are attained within that node's convexified coupling set. Evaluating these nodewise endpoints along the realized pre-block histories yields the predictable pathwise containment \(V_n\in[L_n,U_n]\). For any fixed root-to-leaf path, independent selection of feasible couplings at its visited nodes attains both summed endpoints in the rectangular relaxation \(\mathfrak R_n(\boldsymbol\mu_n)\). Thus the interval is exact only nodewise and path by path inside that rectangular convexified relaxation. This theorem does not assert that one globally consistent fixed-potential-outcome adaptive-history completion realizes independently selected optimizers across different nodes. When \(B_n=1\) and \(g_{nbi}(a)=0\), direct substitution gives the static quadratic MOT variance formula of Gao, Ge, and Qian. When \(B_n\ge2\) and counts respond to earlier outcomes, \([L_n,U_n]\) is a sum of pre-block-measurable random endpoints rather than one static MOT functional, and the claim does not condition on \(\mathcal F_{n,B_n}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(n\) and \(b\), condition on \(\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\), and abbreviate
\[
N=n_{nb},\qquad m_a=m_{nba},\qquad p_a=m_a/N,
\qquad R_i(a)=R_{nbi}(a),\qquad x_i(a)=R_i(a)-\bar R_{nba}.
\tag{1}
\]
The divisions are valid because \(1\le m_a\le N-1\), hence \(N\ge2\). By \(\mathrm{ass:fixed\text{-}potential\text{-}outcomes}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:predictable\text{-}adjustment}\), the residual lists in (1) are fixed under this conditioning; by \(\mathrm{ass:predictable\text{-}counts}\), so are the counts.

We first verify unconditional square integrability, rather than infer it merely from conditional finite support. Put \(p=4+\delta_0>4\). The moment condition \(\mathrm{ass:residual\text{-}moments}\) and the power-mean inequality give, almost surely at every pre-block history,
\[
\frac1N\sum_i|R_i(a)|
\le\left\{\frac1N\sum_i|R_i(a)|^p\right\}^{1/p}
\le C_R^{1/p}.
\tag{2}
\]
For every realized assignment,
\[
\left|\frac1{m_a}\sum_i\mathbf1\{Z_{nbi}=a\}R_i(a)-\bar R_{nba}\right|
\le\left(\frac N{m_a}+1\right)C_R^{1/p}
\le(N+1)C_R^{1/p}.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus \(\mathrm{def:block\text{-}martingale}\) implies the deterministic almost-sure bound
\[
|D_{nb}|\le w_{nb}\|c\|_1(N+1)C_R^{1/p}<\infty.
\tag{4}
\]
For fixed \(n,b\), the right side is finite, so \(E(D_{nb}^2)<\infty\). This is the required unconditional square integrability.

Let \(I_i^a=\mathbf1\{Z_{nbi}=a\}\). Complete-block randomization gives
\[
E(I_i^a\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})=\frac{m_a}{N}=p_a.
\tag{5}
\]
Consequently, (5) and \(\mathrm{def:block\text{-}martingale}\) give
\[
E(D_{nb}\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
=w_{nb}\sum_ac_a\left\{\frac1{m_a}\sum_i p_aR_i(a)-\bar R_{nba}\right\}=0.
\tag{6}
\]
The increment \(D_{nb}\) is measurable after block \(b\) is assigned. Hence (4)--(6) prove that \((M_{nj},\mathcal F_{n,j})_{j\le B_n}\) is a square-integrable martingale with differences \(D_{nb}\).

For distinct unit labels \(i\ne k\), the same complete-randomization law gives
\[
E(I_i^aI_k^a\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
=\frac{m_a(m_a-1)}{N(N-1)},
\qquad
E(I_i^aI_k^{a'}\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
=\frac{m_am_{a'}}{N(N-1)}\quad(a\ne a'),
\tag{7}
\]
whereas \(I_i^aI_i^{a'}=0\) for \(a\ne a'\). Since \(\sum_i x_i(a)=0\), expansion of the diagonal and distinct-label terms in (7) yields
\[
\operatorname{Var}\!\left(\frac1{m_a}\sum_iI_i^aR_i(a)\middle|\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\right)
=\left(\frac1{m_a}-\frac1N\right)S_{nba}^2.
\tag{8}
\]
For \(a\ne a'\), use
\(\sum_{i\ne k}x_i(a)x_k(a')=-\sum_i x_i(a)x_i(a')\) in (7) to obtain
\[
\operatorname{Cov}\!\left(\frac1{m_a}\sum_iI_i^aR_i(a),
\frac1{m_{a'}}\sum_iI_i^{a'}R_i(a')\middle|\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\right)
=-\frac{S_{nb,aa'}}N,
\tag{9}
\]
where
\[
S_{nb,aa'}=\frac1{N-1}\sum_i x_i(a)x_i(a').
\]
Combining (8)--(9) and expanding the contrast square gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{E(D_{nb}^2\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})}{w_{nb}^2}
&=\sum_a\frac{c_a^2S_{nba}^2}{m_a}
-\frac1N\left\{\sum_ac_a^2S_{nba}^2+2\sum_{a<a'}c_ac_{a'}S_{nb,aa'}\right\}\\
&=\sum_a\frac{c_a^2S_{nba}^2}{m_a}
-\frac1{N(N-1)}\sum_i\left\{\sum_ac_ax_i(a)\right\}^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
Define the common-label centered law
\[
\gamma_{nb}^{\mathrm{true}}
=\frac1N\sum_{i\in\mathcal U_{nb}}
\delta_{(x_i(1),\ldots,x_i(K))}.
\tag{11}
\]
Its arm-\(a\) marginal is \(\mu_{nba}\), so \(\gamma_{nb}^{\mathrm{true}}\in\Gamma_{nb}\). Substitution of (11) into (10) proves
\[
E(D_{nb}^2\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
=w_{nb}^2\left\{\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\frac{c_a^2S_{nba}^2}{m_{nba}}
-\frac1{n_{nb}-1}\int\left(\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}c_ax_a\right)^2d\gamma_{nb}^{\mathrm{true}}\right\}.
\tag{12}
\]

Fix \(h\in\mathcal H_{n,b-1}\). Each margin \(\mu_{nba}^h\) has finite support. The product measure shows that \(\Gamma_{nb}^h\) is nonempty. In its finite coordinate representation, the marginal equalities and nonnegativity constraints make it closed, while all coordinates lie in \([0,1]\), so it is compact. The quadratic cost is a continuous linear functional of the coupling coordinates. It therefore attains its minimum \(J_{nb}^{h,-}\) and maximum \(J_{nb}^{h,+}\); convexity of the transportation polytope and linearity show that the full image is the interval between these extrema. Because the cost coefficient in (12) is \(-w_{nb}^2/(n_{nb}-1)<0\), the lower conditional-variance endpoint uses \(J_{nb}^{h,+}\), the upper endpoint uses \(J_{nb}^{h,-}\), and each is attained in \(\Gamma_{nb}^h\).

Both endpoints are nonnegative. Indeed, set \(p_{nba}^h=m_{nba}^h/n_{nb}\). Positivity of the nodewise counts and \(\sum_ap_{nba}^h=1\) allow Cauchy--Schwarz to give, for every \(x\in\mathbb R^K\),
\[
\left(\sum_ac_ax_a\right)^2
=\left(\sum_a\frac{c_ax_a}{\sqrt{p_{nba}^h}}\sqrt{p_{nba}^h}\right)^2
\le\sum_a\frac{c_a^2x_a^2}{p_{nba}^h}.
\tag{13}
\]
The definition of the centered empirical marginal gives
\[
\int x_a^2d\mu_{nba}^h(x_a)
=\frac{n_{nb}-1}{n_{nb}}(S_{nba}^h)^2.
\tag{14}
\]
Therefore every \(\gamma\in\Gamma_{nb}^h\) satisfies
\[
\frac1{n_{nb}-1}\int\left(\sum_ac_ax_a\right)^2d\gamma
\le\sum_a\frac{c_a^2(S_{nba}^h)^2}{m_{nba}^h},
\tag{15}
\]
which proves the assertion.

By \(\mathrm{def:history\text{-}tree\text{-}rectangular\text{-}relaxation}\), the coupling coordinate at each history node may be chosen independently. On a fixed root-to-leaf path, the Minkowski sum of the visited nodewise variance intervals is the interval whose endpoints are the sums of the respective nodewise endpoints. Choosing an optimizer at each visited node attains both summed endpoints within \(\mathfrak R_n(\boldsymbol\mu_n)\). At every actually visited node, (11) is feasible. Summing (12) over the visited blocks and applying \(\mathrm{def:qv\text{-}envelope}\) gives the predictable pathwise containment
\[
V_n=\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}E(D_{nb}^2\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})\in[L_n,U_n].
\tag{16}
\]
Every summand and endpoint in (16) is measurable before its block is randomized. Thus no step conditions on \(\mathcal F_{n,B_n}\), and the argument establishes exactness only nodewise and path by path in the Cartesian convexified relaxation. It does not construct a single globally consistent fixed-potential-outcome completion realizing independently selected optimizers at different history nodes.

The smallest adaptive check makes this filtration point explicit. Take two arms, \(c=(1,-1)/\sqrt2\), zero adjustment, and a first block with common arm list \((-1,1)\) and quota \((1,1)\). Its two assignments give \(D_{n1}=\sqrt2w_{n1}\) and \(D_{n1}=-\sqrt2w_{n1}\). In the second block use common list \((-1,0,1)\), choose quota \((1,2)\) after the positive first-block value, and quota \((2,1)\) after the negative value. With \(d=3w_{n2}/(2\sqrt2)\), the three equiprobable second-block values are \((-d,0,d)\) after the first history and \((d,0,-d)\) after the second. Hence all six assignment sequences have conditional second-block mean zero and
\[
E(D_{n1}D_{n2})
=E\{D_{n1}E(D_{n2}\mid\mathcal F_{n,1})\}=0.
\tag{17}
\]
Finally, when \(B_n=1\) and \(g_{nbi}(a)=0\), the residual lists equal the fixed potential-outcome lists. Direct substitution in (12), followed by optimization over their centered joint coupling, gives the stated static quadratic multi-marginal-transport variance formula.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This identifies the adaptive martingale normalization and its nodewise-exact rectangular range; the proof now obtains unconditional square integrability directly from the common residual-moment bound before using conditional complete-randomization identities. \textit{Gap.} Existing adaptive covariance formulas do not characterize the predictable fixed-count complete-block residual-coupling range, and static transport formulas do not provide the history-tree rectangular scope. \textit{Consumer.} A predictably batched multi-arm trial can compute a narrow nodewise rectangular variance envelope for each prespecified causal contrast without conditioning on its realized terminal path.

\begin{proposition}[prop:sorting-and-fixed-k-lp]\label{prop:sorting-and-fixed-k-lp}
The constructions in \(\text{def:twoarm-sorting}\) equal \(J_{nb}^{-}\) and \(J_{nb}^{+}\) for \(K=2\). For each fixed \(K\), feeding the observable assigned-arm measures \(\widehat\mu_{nba}=m_{nba}^{-1}\sum_{i\in\mathcal O_{nba}}\delta_{\widehat R_{nbi}^{c}(a)}\) into the finite primal programs in \(\text{def:fixed-k-lp}\) attains \(\widehat J_{nb}^{-}\) and \(\widehat J_{nb}^{+}\), and their Kantorovich duals have the same values. These are exact convexified empirical endpoints. At any single history node, every rational optimal plan is realized by a deterministic fixed-potential-outcome block completion after finite replication of that node's armwise lists, so its LP endpoints are asymptotically nodewise completion-attainable as block sizes grow. No same-size integrality assertion for \(K>2\), and no cross-history assertion that one \(\Xi_n\) realizes independently chosen nodewise plans, is made.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For \(K=2\) and \(c=(1,-1)/\sqrt2\), the identity
\[
\frac12(x-y)^2=\frac12x^2+\frac12y^2-xy
\tag{1}
\]
shows that the two marginal square terms do not depend on the coupling. The cited rearrangement result \(\mathrm{lem:twoarm\text{-}rearrangement\text{-}extrema}\) says that equal-rank pairing maximizes \(E(XY)\), whereas reverse-rank pairing minimizes it. By (1), equal-rank pairing therefore minimizes the quadratic cost and reverse-rank pairing maximizes it. This proves the two formulas in \(\mathrm{def:twoarm\text{-}sorting}\).

For general fixed \(K\), label every atom of every observed empirical margin, even when two labels carry the same numerical residual. A coupling of the labeled empirical measures assigns a mass \(\pi_{i_1\ldots i_K}\ge0\) to each tuple
\((i_1,\ldots,i_K)\in\prod_{a=1}^K\mathcal O_{nba}\). Its \(a\)-th marginal is \(\widehat\mu_{nba}\) if and only if
\[
\sum_{(i_{a'})_{a'\ne a}\in\prod_{a'\ne a}\mathcal O_{nba'}}
\pi_{i_1\ldots i_K}=\frac1{m_{nba}}
\tag{2}
\]
for every \(a\) and every \(i_a\in\mathcal O_{nba}\). Thus the feasible arrays in \(\mathrm{def:fixed\text{-}k\text{-}lp}\) are exactly all couplings of the empirical margins, and the displayed LP objective is exactly
\[
\int\left(\sum_{a=1}^Kc_ax_a\right)^2d\gamma.
\]
The feasible set is nonempty because it contains the product coupling, and it is a compact polytope because (2), nonnegativity, and total mass one define a closed bounded subset of a finite-dimensional space. The linear objective consequently attains its minimum and maximum, which are respectively \(\widehat J_{nb}^{-}\) and \(\widehat J_{nb}^{+}\) by \(\mathrm{def:transport\text{-}values}\). The cited finite multi-marginal Kantorovich duality result \(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}mot\text{-}kantorovich\text{-}duality}\) equates the minimum with its finite dual. Applying the same result to the negative cost and multiplying both primal and dual values by \(-1\) proves the corresponding equality for the maximum. Hence both programs give exact convexified empirical endpoints.

It remains to prove the replication assertion, which is specific to this proposition. The constraints in (2) have rational coefficients and rational right-hand sides. A linear functional on a nonempty polytope has an optimal face containing an extreme point; every extreme point of this rational polytope is rational because its coordinates solve a full-rank subsystem of the active rational equalities. Thus a rational optimal plan exists. More generally, let \(\pi\) be any rational optimal plan. Put \(N=\sum_am_{nba}=n_{nb}\), and choose an integer \(L\) divisible by \(N\), by every \(m_{nba}\), and by every denominator occurring among the finitely many values \(\pi_{i_1\ldots i_K}\). Create
\[
L\pi_{i_1\ldots i_K}
\]
replicated units of joint type \((i_1,\ldots,i_K)\). These multiplicities are nonnegative integers and sum to \(L\). By (2), the arm-\(a\) label \(i_a\) occurs exactly
\[
\sum_{(i_{a'})_{a'\ne a}}L\pi_{i_1\ldots i_K}
=\frac{L}{m_{nba}}
\tag{3}
\]
times. Therefore, within arm \(a\), every label is replicated by the integer factor \(L/m_{nba}\), and the empirical joint law on the replicated common unit labels is exactly \(\pi\). Set the replicated complete-randomization quota to \(m_{nba}'=(L/N)m_{nba}\); these are positive integers, preserve the original arm fractions, and sum to \(L\). Assigning to each replicated unit the corresponding centered armwise residual values, and choosing fixed potential outcomes and nodewise predictable fitted values whose difference equals those residuals, realizes the plan at that single history node. Repeating the construction at integer multiples of \(L\) gives nodewise completion attainability along growing replicated block sizes. The construction neither retains the original unreplicated block size nor enforces compatibility with any other history node, exactly as the proposition states.

As the smallest arithmetic stress test, take both centered lists to be \(\{-1,-1,1,1\}\). For the unnormalized contrast \((1,-1)\), equal-rank pairing makes the squared contrast identically zero, while reverse-rank pairing makes it identically four. Hence the two costs are \(0\) and \(4\), and for a block of size four the associated envelope width is
\[
\frac{4w_{nb}^2}{4-1}>0.
\]
For the normalized contrast \((1,-1)/\sqrt2\), both costs and this width are divided by two. This verifies both the endpoint ordering and nontrivial width.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The result turns the abstract envelope into sorting for two arms and a finite auditable computation for multiple arms. \textit{Gap.} AronowGreenLee2014SharpVariance gives two-arm rearrangement, while GaoGeQian2025MOT gives static MOT computation; neither states the finite-population replication boundary needed for adaptive design-based sharpness. \textit{Consumer.} A four-arm complete-block redesign of the NewbyEtAl2025MentalHealth minitrials can compute each endpoint using a fixed-\(K\) transportation program.

\begin{theorem}[thm:history-uniform-plugin]\label{thm:history-uniform-plugin}
For every arm and block, form the observable assigned-arm measure \(\widehat\mu_{nba}=m_{nba}^{-1}\sum_{i\in\mathcal O_{nba}}\delta_{Y_{nbi}^{\mathrm{obs}}-g_{nbi}(a)-\widehat{\bar R}_{nba}}\), centered by the observed assigned-arm residual mean \(\widehat{\bar R}_{nba}\), and feed exactly these measures into \(\widehat J_{nb}^{-}\) and \(\widehat J_{nb}^{+}\) and the plug-in envelope. There is a constant \(C_{\mathrm{ep}}<\infty\), depending only on \(K,\varepsilon_0,\delta_0,C_R\), such that for every \(n\), \[\sup_{\mathbb D\in\mathfrak D}E_{\mathbb D}\left[1\wedge\frac{|\widehat L_n-L_n|+|\widehat U_n-U_n|}{s_n^2}\right]\le C_{\mathrm{ep}}r_n.\] Consequently, for every \(\eta>0\), \[\sup_{\mathbb D\in\mathfrak D}\Pr_{\mathbb D}\left(\frac{|\widehat L_n-L_n|+|\widehat U_n-U_n|}{s_n^2}>\eta\right)\le\frac{C_{\mathrm{ep}}r_n}{1\wedge\eta}\longrightarrow0.\] The same bound, without the factor two implicit in the displayed sum, holds for the normalized Hausdorff distance between \([\widehat L_n,\widehat U_n]\) and \([L_n,U_n]\). Probability and expectation are over the original sequential randomization. The inequalities hold uniformly over pre-block histories generated by arrays in the common-parameter class, use observable assigned-arm centering throughout, and do not condition on \(\mathcal F_{n,B_n}\) or freeze the realized final counts.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(r=4+\delta_0\), and fix an arbitrary pre-block history \(\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\). Membership in the common-parameter class specified by \(\text{def:admissible-arrays}\), together with \(\text{ass:predictable-adjustment}\), makes the potential outcomes, count vector, fitting values, and residual lists in block \(b\) fixed at this conditioning event. For one arm \(a\), abbreviate
\[
X_i=R_{nbi}(a)-\bar R_{nba},\qquad N=n_{nb},\qquad m=m_{nba}.
\]
Jensen's inequality and \(\text{ass:residual-moments}\) give
\[
\frac1N\sum_i|X_i|^r
\le 2^{r-1}\left\{\frac1N\sum_i|R_{nbi}(a)|^r+|\bar R_{nba}|^r\right\}
\le 2^r C_R.
\tag{1}
\]
By \(\text{ass:consistency-no-interference}\), the residual observed on a label assigned to arm \(a\) is \(R_{nbi}(a)\). By \(\text{ass:complete-block-randomization}\), the arm-\(a\) labels form a simple random sample without replacement of size \(m\) from the \(N\) labels.

Define the population-centered sample and population measures
\[
\nu=\frac1m\sum_{i:Z_{nbi}=a}\delta_{X_i},
\qquad
\mu=\frac1N\sum_i\delta_{X_i}=\mu_{nba}.
\]
For \(T>0\), clip both measures to \([-T,T]\), partition that interval into \(H\) cells of length at most \(2T/H\), and project each cell to one representative. If \(P_h\) and \(\widehat P_h\) are the population and sample masses of cell \(h\), the first- and second-order inclusion probabilities under simple random sampling give
\[
\operatorname{Var}(\widehat P_h\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
=\frac{N-m}{m(N-1)}P_h(1-P_h)\le\frac1{4m}.
\]
Therefore, by Cauchy--Schwarz,
\[
E\left(\sum_{h=1}^H|\widehat P_h-P_h|\middle|\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\right)
\le\frac{H}{2\sqrt m}.
\tag{2}
\]
Match projected mass inside each cell and move unmatched clipped mass across a distance at most \(2T\). The within-cell squared cost is at most \(4T^2/H^2\), while the unmatched squared cost is at most a universal constant times \(T^2\sum_h|\widehat P_h-P_h|\). Moreover, (1) and
\(X_i^2\mathbf 1\{|X_i|>T\}\le |X_i|^rT^{-(r-2)}\) bound the population clipping cost by \(2^rC_RT^{-(r-2)}\); its conditional sample expectation is the same by the first-order inclusion probability. Applying the triangle inequality for \(W_2\), and absorbing the numerical factors into \(C=C(r,C_R)\), yields
\[
E\{W_2^2(\nu,\mu)\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\}
\le C\left(\frac{T^2}{H^2}+\frac{T^2H}{\sqrt m}+T^{-(r-2)}\right).
\tag{3}
\]
Choose \(H=\lceil m^{1/6}\rceil\) and \(T=m^{1/(3r)}\). Each term on the right of (3) is at most a constant times \(m^{-\beta}\), where
\[
\beta=\frac{r-2}{3r}.
\tag{4}
\]

The observable measure \(\widehat\mu_{nba}\) is the translate of \(\nu\) by minus the sample mean \(\bar X_S=m^{-1}\sum_{i:Z_{nbi}=a}X_i\). Since \(N^{-1}\sum_iX_i=0\), the exact simple-random-sampling covariance identity gives
\[
E(\bar X_S^2\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
=\left(\frac1m-\frac1N\right)S_{nba}^2\le\frac{C}{m},
\tag{5}
\]
where the last inequality follows from (1). Translation by a scalar admits a coupling of squared cost equal to the squared scalar. The triangle inequality for \(W_2\), (3)--(5), and \(0<\beta<1\) imply
\[
E\{W_2^2(\widehat\mu_{nba},\mu_{nba})\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\}
\le C m_{nba}^{-\beta}.
\tag{6}
\]

For the diagonal variance term, write
\[
\overline{X^2}_S=\frac1m\sum_{i:Z_{nbi}=a}X_i^2,
\qquad
\overline{X^2}=\frac1N\sum_iX_i^2.
\]
The same sampling covariance identity, now applied to the finite list \((X_i^2)_i\), and (1) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
E\{|\overline{X^2}_S-\overline{X^2}|\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\}
&\le\left\{\left(\frac1m-\frac1N\right)
\frac1{N-1}\sum_i(X_i^2-\overline{X^2})^2\right\}^{1/2}\\
&\le C m^{-1/2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
For \(m,N\ge2\),
\[
\widehat S_{nba}^2=\frac{m}{m-1}(\overline{X^2}_S-\bar X_S^2),
\qquad
S_{nba}^2=\frac{N}{N-1}\overline{X^2}.
\]
Equations (5)--(7), \(|m/(m-1)-1|\le2/m\), \(|N/(N-1)-1|\le2/N\), and the second-moment consequence of (1) therefore show
\[
E\{|\widehat S_{nba}^2-S_{nba}^2|\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\}
\le C m_{nba}^{-1/2}.
\tag{8}
\]

It remains to control the multi-marginal values. For every arm, choose an optimal quadratic coupling between \(\mu_{nba}\) and \(\widehat\mu_{nba}\). These couplings exist because both measures have finite support. Starting from any \(\gamma\in\Gamma_{nb}\), disintegrate the pairwise couplings and draw the empirical coordinates conditionally on their corresponding population coordinates. If \(X\) has law \(\gamma\) and \(Y\) is the resulting vector, then \(Y\) has the empirical armwise marginals and
\(E(X_a-Y_a)^2=W_2^2(\mu_{nba},\widehat\mu_{nba})\). By \(\text{ass:contrast-normalization}\) and Cauchy--Schwarz,
\[
\begin{aligned}
|E(c^\top X)^2-E(c^\top Y)^2|
&\le\{E(c^\top(X-Y))^2\}^{1/2}
\{E(c^\top(X+Y))^2\}^{1/2}\\
&\le\left\{\sum_aW_2^2(\mu_{nba},\widehat\mu_{nba})\right\}^{1/2}
\left\{2\sum_a(EX_a^2+EY_a^2)\right\}^{1/2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
Apply (9) first to an optimizer for the population coupling polytope and then, with the roles reversed, to an optimizer for the empirical polytope. This bounds both differences of infima and both differences of suprema. Conditional Cauchy--Schwarz, (1), (6), and the corresponding expected second-moment bound for the empirical margins give, for either sign,
\[
E\{|\widehat J_{nb}^{\pm}-J_{nb}^{\pm}|\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\}
\le C\left(\min_a m_{nba}\right)^{-\beta/2}.
\tag{10}
\]
No uniqueness of an optimizer is required.

Let \(t_{nb}=w_{nb}^2/n_{nb}\), so \(\sum_bt_{nb}=s_n^2\). By \(\text{ass:overlap}\), \(m_{nba}^{-1}\le(\varepsilon_0n_{nb})^{-1}\), and \((n_{nb}-1)^{-1}\le2n_{nb}^{-1}\) when \(n_{nb}\ge2\). Combining (8), (10), \(\text{def:qv-envelope}\), and \(\text{def:plugin-envelope}\), then summing the absolute block errors and applying the tower property under the original sequential assignment law, gives whenever every arm count is at least two,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sup_{\mathbb D\in\mathfrak D}
E_{\mathbb D}\frac{|\widehat L_n-L_n|+|\widehat U_n-U_n|}{s_n^2}
&\le\frac{C}{s_n^2}\sum_bt_{nb}
\left(q_n^{-1/2}+q_n^{-\beta/2}\right)\\
&\le Cq_n^{-\kappa},
\qquad
\kappa=\frac\beta2=\frac{2+\delta_0}{6(4+\delta_0)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
The conditional constants are uniform over every possible pre-block history because all primitive envelopes are common over \(\mathfrak D\). Thus (11) uses neither a union bound over histories nor a union bound over blocks; the tower property integrates each realized pre-block history rather than conditioning on the terminal path.

If some arm count is below two, \(\min_{b,a}m_{nba}\ge q_n\) implies \(q_n<2\). The truncated loss is at most one, while \(1\le2^\kappa q_n^{-\kappa}\). Enlarging the constant by \(2^\kappa\) therefore covers every \(n\), including the total-convention cases. Hence
\[
\sup_{\mathbb D\in\mathfrak D}E_{\mathbb D}\left[1\wedge
\frac{|\widehat L_n-L_n|+|\widehat U_n-U_n|}{s_n^2}\right]
\le C_{\mathrm{ep}}q_n^{-\kappa}=C_{\mathrm{ep}}r_n,
\tag{12}
\]
where \(C_{\mathrm{ep}}\) depends only on \(K,\varepsilon_0,\delta_0,C_R\).

For every nonnegative \(X\) and every \(\eta>0\),
\(\mathbf1\{X>\eta\}\le(1\wedge X)/(1\wedge\eta)\). Applying this inequality to the normalized summed endpoint error in (12) gives the asserted probability bound. The Hausdorff distance between two real intervals is
\(\max\{|\widehat L_n-L_n|,|\widehat U_n-U_n|\}\), which is no larger than their summed endpoint error, proving its stated bound with the same constant. Finally, \(q_n\to\infty\) and \(\kappa>0\) imply \(r_n\to0\). Every expectation and probability above is taken under the original sequential randomization law, with conditioning only on \(\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\), never on \(\mathcal F_{n,B_n}\).
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The explicit \(q_n^{-\kappa}\) rate remains the bridge from oracle partial identification to uniform adaptive inference over the general \(4+\delta_0\) moment class. \textit{Gap.} Static iid empirical-margin rates do not directly transfer to predictable finite-population triangular arrays, and the bounded-residual elbow does not replace this moment-only guarantee. \textit{Consumer.} A batched multi-arm trial may consistently estimate the endpoint whenever its minimum predictable arm count diverges under the stated moment envelope; endpoint estimation itself requires neither a logarithmic block-count condition nor block leverage.

\begin{theorem}[thm:conservative-wald]\label{thm:conservative-wald}
For every \(\alpha\in(0,1)\), restrict attention to arrays in the displayed common-parameter class \(\mathfrak D\) for which \(\mathrm{ass:qv\text{-}stabilization}\) additionally holds uniformly. This is an explicit load-bearing high-level regularity condition and is not derived from predictable quotas, complete-block randomization, overlap, growing arm counts, leverage, residual moments, or QV growth. On that restricted class, \[\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{\mathbb D\in\mathfrak D}\Pr_{\mathbb D}\{\tau_n\in\mathrm{CI}_{n,1-\alpha}\}\ge1-\alpha.\] More precisely, the common leverage envelope \(\ell_n\to0\), the common residual-moment envelope \((\delta_0,C_R)\), the finite-population nondegeneracy condition \(V_n\ge v_0s_n^2\), together with this additional uniform predictable-QV stabilization, imply \((\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n)/\sqrt{V_n}\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1)\) uniformly. The nodewise rectangular envelope gives \(V_n\le U_n\) pathwise, and the explicit bound \(C_{\mathrm{ep}}r_n=o(1)\) permits \(U_n\) to be replaced by \(\widehat U_n\). No conditioning on the terminal adaptive path is required.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Work on the subclass in the statement, so in addition to membership in \(\mathfrak D\), the uniform predictable-quadratic-variation stabilization condition holds.  Put
\[
p=4+\delta _0,\qquad t_{nb}=\frac{w_{nb}^{2}}{n_{nb}},\qquad
s_n^2=\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}t_{nb},\qquad \sigma_n^2=E_{\mathbb D}V_n.
\tag{1}
\]
We first verify that the centered causal estimation error is the martingale sum in \(\text{def:block-martingale}\).  By consistency, on \(\{Z_{nbi}=a\}\) one has \(Y_{nbi}^{\mathrm{obs}}-g_{nbi}(a)=R_{nbi}(a)\).  Since \(p_{nba}=m_{nba}/n_{nb}\), \(Y_{nbi}(a)=g_{nbi}(a)+R_{nbi}(a)\), and \(w_{nb}=n_{nb}/N_n\), subtraction of the fixed finite-population contrast from \(\text{def:aipw-estimator}\) gives, block by block,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n
&=\frac1{N_n}\sum_{b,i,a}c_a
 \left\{\frac{\mathbf 1\{Z_{nbi}=a\}}{p_{nba}}R_{nbi}(a)-R_{nbi}(a)\right\}\\
&=\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}w_{nb}\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}c_a
 \left\{\frac1{m_{nba}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal U_{nb}}
 \mathbf 1\{Z_{nbi}=a\}R_{nbi}(a)-\bar R_{nba}\right\}\\
&=\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}D_{nb}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
This proves the relationship between the causal target and the observed-data estimator rather than defining the former by the latter.  Conditional on \(\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\), predictable adjustment and predictable counts make every residual list and every quota fixed.  Complete-block randomization makes the units assigned to arm \(a\) a simple random sample of size \(m_{nba}\); its sample mean has conditional expectation \(\bar R_{nba}\).  Hence
\[
E_{\mathbb D}(D_{nb}\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})=0.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus \((M_{nj},\mathcal F_{n,j})_{j\le B_n}\) is a martingale, consistently with \(\text{thm:exact-predictable-ot-envelope}\).

We next derive conditional Lindeberg from the primitive moment and leverage conditions.  Let \(x_1,\ldots,x_N\) be deterministic, let \(\sum_i x_i=0\), and let \(S\) be a uniform size-\(m\) subset.  Write \(A_r=\sum_i|x_i|^r\) and \(\pi_r=(m)_r/(N)_r\), with \(\pi_r=0\) for \(r>m\).  Expanding according to equality patterns among four sampled indices gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
E\left(\sum_{i\in S}x_i\right)^4
={}&\pi_1A_4-4\pi_2A_4+3\pi_2(A_2^2-A_4)\\
&+6\pi_3(2A_4-A_2^2)+\pi_4(3A_2^2-6A_4).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
Indeed, the identities used in this enumeration are
\[
\sum_{i\ne j}x_i^3x_j=-A_4,
\qquad
\sum_{i\ne j}x_i^2x_j^2=A_2^2-A_4,
\tag{5}
\]
\[
\sum_{\substack{i,j,k\\\mathrm{all\ distinct}}}x_i^2x_jx_k
=2A_4-A_2^2,
\qquad
\sum_{\substack{i,j,k,l\\\mathrm{all\ distinct}}}x_ix_jx_kx_l
=3A_2^2-6A_4;
\tag{6}
\]
each follows by expanding a power of \(\sum_i x_i=0\) and collecting equality patterns.  Since \(A_2^2\le NA_4\), \(\pi_r\le(m/N)^r\), and \(1\le m\le N\), taking absolute values in (4) yields a universal constant \(C_4<\infty\) such that
\[
E\left|\frac1m\sum_{i\in S}x_i\right|^4
\le \frac{C_4}{m^2}\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N|x_i|^4.
\tag{7}
\]

Apply (7), conditional on \(\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\), to
\(x_i=R_{nbi}(a)-\bar R_{nba}\).  Jensen's inequality, the power-mean inequality, and \(p=4+\delta_0\) give
\[
\frac1{n_{nb}}\sum_i|R_{nbi}(a)-\bar R_{nba}|^4
\le16\left\{\frac1{n_{nb}}\sum_i|R_{nbi}(a)|^p\right\}^{4/p}
\le16C_R^{4/p}
\tag{8}
\]
almost surely by \(\mathrm{ass:residual\text{-}moments}\).  Define
\[
X_{nba}=\frac1{m_{nba}}\sum_i\mathbf1\{Z_{nbi}=a\}
\{R_{nbi}(a)-\bar R_{nba}\}.
\]
Contrast normalization and Cauchy--Schwarz imply
\[
\left|\sum_ac_aX_{nba}\right|^4
\le\left(\sum_aX_{nba}^2\right)^2
\le K\sum_aX_{nba}^4.
\tag{9}
\]
Combining (7)--(9) with overlap, \(m_{nba}\ge\varepsilon_0n_{nb}\), gives a constant \(C_D<\infty\), depending only on \(K,\varepsilon_0,\delta_0,C_R\), for which
\[
E_{\mathbb D}(D_{nb}^4\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
\le C_D\frac{w_{nb}^4}{n_{nb}^2}=C_Dt_{nb}^2
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\tag{10}
\]
In particular, the increments are square-integrable.  Predictable-QV growth implies
\[
\sigma_n^2=E_{\mathbb D}V_n\ge v_0s_n^2>0.
\tag{11}
\]
For every \(\epsilon>0\), the deterministic inequality
\(x^2\mathbf1\{|x|>y\}\le x^4/y^2\), followed by (10), (11), and block leverage, gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\frac1{\sigma_n^2}\sum_bE_{\mathbb D}\!\left[D_{nb}^2
 \mathbf1\{|D_{nb}|>\epsilon\sigma_n\}\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\right]\\
&\quad\le\frac{C_D}{\epsilon^2\sigma_n^4}\sum_bt_{nb}^2
\le\frac{C_D}{\epsilon^2v_0^2}
 \frac{\max_bt_{nb}}{s_n^2}
\le\frac{C_D}{\epsilon^2v_0^2}\ell_n
\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
Thus the conditional Lindeberg condition is derived, not assumed.  The additional uniform predictable-QV stabilization condition is exactly
\[
\frac{V_n}{\sigma_n^2}\longrightarrow1
\quad\text{in probability, uniformly over the restricted class}.
\tag{13}
\]
Equations (3), (11)--(13), and \(\text{lem:martingale-clt-predictable-variation}\) now yield
\[
\frac{\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n}{\sigma_n}
=\frac{\sum_bD_{nb}}{\sigma_n}\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1).
\tag{14}
\]
Combining (13) and (14) gives
\[
T_n:=\frac{\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n}{\sqrt{V_n}}
=\frac{(\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n)/\sigma_n}{\sqrt{V_n/\sigma_n^2}}
\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1).
\tag{15}
\]
For completeness, write \(X_n=(\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n)/\sigma_n\) and \(A_n=V_n/\sigma_n^2\).  Convergence in (14) makes \((X_n)\) tight, while (13) implies \(A_n^{-1/2}-1\to0\) in probability.  For any \(C>0\) and \(\eta>0\),
\[
\Pr\{|X_n(A_n^{-1/2}-1)|>\eta\}
\le \Pr\{|X_n|>C\}+\Pr\{|A_n^{-1/2}-1|>\eta/C\}.
\]
Letting first \(n\to\infty\) and then \(C\to\infty\) proves \(T_n-X_n\to0\) in probability.  For every \(x\in\mathbb R\) and \(\eta>0\), the event inclusions
\[
\{X_n\le x-\eta\}\setminus\{|T_n-X_n|>\eta\}
\subseteq\{T_n\le x\}
\subseteq\{X_n\le x+\eta\}\cup\{|T_n-X_n|>\eta\}
\]
and continuity of the Gaussian distribution prove (15).  The convergence in (14)--(15) is uniform: if uniformity failed, one could choose at each \(n\) a member with discrepancy bounded away from zero.  The selected rows form a triangular array obeying the same common constants and deterministic envelopes; (10)--(13) still hold along it, contradicting the just-proved convergence.

It remains to compare the observable scale with the true predictable QV.  The already established \(\text{thm:exact-predictable-ot-envelope}\) gives \(V_n\le U_n\) pathwise.  The already established \(\text{thm:history-uniform-plugin}\), together with \(r_n=q_n^{-\kappa}\to0\), gives for every \(\eta>0\)
\[
\sup_{\mathbb D\in\mathfrak D}
\Pr_{\mathbb D}\!\left\{\frac{|\widehat U_n-U_n|}{s_n^2}>\eta\right\}
\le\frac{C_{\mathrm{ep}}r_n}{1\wedge\eta}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{16}
\]
Consequently, for every fixed \(\zeta\in(0,1)\), QV growth and (16) imply the explicit uniform bound
\[
\Pr_{\mathbb D}\!\left\{\frac{\widehat U_n}{V_n}<1-\zeta\right\}
\le
\Pr_{\mathbb D}\!\left\{\frac{|\widehat U_n-U_n|}{s_n^2}>\zeta v_0\right\}
\longrightarrow0,
\tag{17}
\]
because \(U_n/V_n\ge1\) and \(V_n/s_n^2\ge v_0\).

Let \(z=z_{1-\alpha/2}>0\).  By \(\text{def:wald-interval}\), (15), and (17),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr_{\mathbb D}\{\tau_n\in\mathrm{CI}_{n,1-\alpha}\}
&=\Pr_{\mathbb D}\!\left\{|T_n|\le z\sqrt{\widehat U_n/V_n}\right\}\\
&\ge \Pr_{\mathbb D}\{|T_n|\le z\sqrt{1-\zeta}\}
-\Pr_{\mathbb D}\!\left\{\widehat U_n/V_n<1-\zeta\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]
Take the infimum over the restricted common-parameter class and the lower limit in \(n\).  Uniform convergence in (15) and (17) make the right-hand side at least
\(2\Phi(z\sqrt{1-\zeta})-1\).  Finally let \(\zeta\downarrow0\).  Continuity of \(\Phi\) and the definition of \(z_{1-\alpha/2}\) give
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{\mathbb D\in\mathfrak D}
\Pr_{\mathbb D}\{\tau_n\in\mathrm{CI}_{n,1-\alpha}\}
\ge2\Phi(z)-1=1-\alpha.
\tag{19}
\]
Every probability above is under the original sequential assignment law.  In particular, no step conditions on the terminal adaptive history.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem converts the nodewise rectangular upper endpoint into unconditional conservative design-based coverage under a separately imposed uniform predictable-QV stabilization condition. \textit{Gap.} Adaptive inference results using other assignment mechanisms or realized-QV studentizers do not supply this finite-population complete-block transport endpoint or its observable history-uniform error control. \textit{Consumer.} A quota-controlled batched trial obtains valid multi-arm contrast intervals when the displayed primitive design conditions and the separate predictable-QV stabilization requirement hold; no common limit of \(E(V_n)/s_n^2\) is required.

\begin{lemma}[lem:twoarm-rearrangement-extrema]\label{lem:twoarm-rearrangement-extrema}
For two equally weighted finite empirical margins on \(\mathbb R\) and the contrast \(c=(1,-1)/\sqrt2\), the minimum of \(\frac12E(X_1-X_2)^2\) over all couplings is attained by equal-rank pairing and the maximum is attained by reverse-rank pairing; equivalently, the two quantile formulas in \(\mathrm{def:twoarm\text{-}sorting}\) equal \(J_{nb}^{-}\) and \(J_{nb}^{+}\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[lem:finite-mot-kantorovich-duality]\label{lem:finite-mot-kantorovich-duality}
Let \(K\ge2\), let \(c\in\mathbb R^K\), and let \(\nu_1,\ldots,\nu_K\) be finitely supported probability measures on \(\mathbb R\). Write \(\Gamma(\nu_1,\ldots,\nu_K)\) for their coupling set. For the quadratic cost \(q(x_1,\ldots,x_K)=(\sum_{a=1}^Kc_ax_a)^2\),
\[
\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma(\nu_1,\ldots,\nu_K)}\int q\,d\gamma
=\sup_{\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_K:\,\sum_{a=1}^K\phi_a(x_a)\le q(x_1,\ldots,x_K)}
\sum_{a=1}^K\int\phi_a\,d\nu_a.
\]
Equivalently, after labeling the support atoms, the finite coupling LP and its finite Kantorovich dual have the same optimal value.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[thm:bounded-residual-parametric-plugin]\label{thm:bounded-residual-parametric-plugin}
For every fixed \(M>0\), there is a constant \(C_M<\infty\), depending only on \(K,\varepsilon_0,M\), such that for every \(n\),
\[
\sup_{\mathbb D\in\mathfrak E_M}E_{\mathbb D}\left[1\wedge
\frac{|\widehat L_n-L_n|+|\widehat U_n-U_n|}{s_n^2}\right]
\le C_Mq_n^{-1/2}.
\]
Consequently the same bound holds with \(\mathfrak E_M\) replaced by the bounded-residual subclass \(\mathfrak D_M\subseteq\mathfrak D\), and the normalized endpoint error converges to zero uniformly on either class. The bound has no dependence on \(B_n\), contains no logarithmic history factor, and requires no block-leverage condition. It is unconditional under the original sequential randomization and never freezes the terminal adaptive path.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(\mathbb D\in\mathfrak E_M\), a block \(b\), and a pre-block history \(\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\). The class definition makes the count vector, fitting values, potential outcomes, and residual lists fixed at this node. For an arm \(a\), write
\[
X_i=R_{nbi}(a)-\bar R_{nba},\qquad N=n_{nb},\qquad m=m_{nba},
\]
and let \(\mu=N^{-1}\sum_i\delta_{X_i}=\mu_{nba}\) and
\(\nu=m^{-1}\sum_{i:Z_{nbi}=a}\delta_{X_i}\). The bounded-residual condition gives
\[
|X_i|\le |R_{nbi}(a)|+|\bar R_{nba}|\le2M.
\tag{1}
\]
Consistency identifies the sampled residuals, and conditional complete randomization makes the arm-\(a\) labels a simple random sample without replacement of size \(m\).

We first prove the required one-dimensional finite-population empirical-measure bound. Let \(F\) and \(\widehat F\) be the distribution functions of \(\mu\) and \(\nu\). For each \(t\in\mathbb R\), the exact inclusion-probability calculation for the finite list \((\mathbf1\{X_i\le t\})_{i=1}^N\) gives
\[
\operatorname{Var}\{\widehat F(t)\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\}
=\frac{N-m}{m(N-1)}F(t)\{1-F(t)\}\le\frac1{4m}.
\tag{2}
\]
For completeness, for finitely supported probability measures on the line, order all atoms jointly. Across the interval between two consecutive atom locations, every transport plan must move at least the absolute cumulative mass imbalance across that cut. The greedy increasing matching moves exactly that imbalance and no more. Summing interval length times cumulative imbalance proves directly that
\[
W_1(\nu,\mu)=\int_{\mathbb R}|\widehat F(t)-F(t)|\,dt.
\tag{3}
\]
This is a finite matching identity, not an imported empirical-process theorem. By (1), the integrand in (3) vanishes outside \([-2M,2M]\). Conditional Cauchy--Schwarz and (2) therefore give
\[
E\{W_1(\nu,\mu)\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\}
\le\int_{-2M}^{2M}\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(\widehat F(t)\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})}\,dt
\le\frac{2M}{\sqrt m}.
\tag{4}
\]

Let \(\bar X_S=m^{-1}\sum_{i:Z_{nbi}=a}X_i\). Since the population mean of the \(X_i\)'s is zero, the simple-random-sampling covariance identity and (1) imply
\[
E(\bar X_S^2\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
=\left(\frac1m-\frac1N\right)S_{nba}^2
\le\frac{8M^2}{m}.
\tag{5}
\]
The observable margin is exactly
\(\widehat\mu_{nba}=T_{-\bar X_S\,\#}\nu\), because subtracting the observed residual mean sends \(X_i\) to \(X_i-\bar X_S\). Coupling every atom with its translate, then using (4), (5), and the triangle inequality, yields the observable-centering bound
\[
E\{W_1(\widehat\mu_{nba},\mu_{nba})\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\}
\le (2+2\sqrt2)M m_{nba}^{-1/2}.
\tag{6}
\]

The sample-variance term has the same parametric order. Put
\[
A_S=\frac1m\sum_{i:Z_{nbi}=a}X_i^2,
\qquad A=\frac1N\sum_iX_i^2.
\]
Since \(0\le X_i^2\le4M^2\), the sampling covariance identity gives
\[
E(|A_S-A|\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
\le\left\{\left(\frac1m-\frac1N\right)
\frac1{N-1}\sum_i(X_i^2-A)^2\right\}^{1/2}
\le4\sqrt2 M^2m^{-1/2}.
\tag{7}
\]
For \(m,N\ge2\),
\[
\widehat S_{nba}^2=\frac{m}{m-1}(A_S-\bar X_S^2),
\qquad S_{nba}^2=\frac{N}{N-1}A.
\]
Using (5), (7), \(m/(m-1)\le2\), \(A\le4M^2\), and
\(|m/(m-1)-N/(N-1)|\le2/m\), we obtain a numerical constant \(C_0\) such that
\[
E\{|\widehat S_{nba}^2-S_{nba}^2|\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\}
\le C_0M^2m_{nba}^{-1/2}.
\tag{8}
\]

We next prove bounded-support Lipschitz sensitivity of the fixed-\(K\) multi-marginal objective. Let \(\phi(x)=(c^\top x)^2\). If \(x,y\in[-2M,2M]^K\), contrast normalization implies \(\|c\|_1\le\sqrt K\) and \(|c_a|\le1\), so
\[
|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|
=|c^\top(x-y)|\,|c^\top(x+y)|
\le4M\sqrt K\sum_{a=1}^K|x_a-y_a|.
\tag{9}
\]
For each arm, take an optimal \(W_1\) coupling between \(\mu_{nba}\) and \(\widehat\mu_{nba}\). Glue its conditional kernel to any coupling of the population margins. The resulting vector has the empirical margins, and (9) bounds the change in expected cost by
\[
4M\sqrt K\sum_{a=1}^K W_1(\mu_{nba},\widehat\mu_{nba}).
\]
Apply this construction to a population minimizer and maximizer and then reverse the roles of the population and empirical margins. The finite coupling polytopes are compact, so all four optimizers exist. Equation (6) thus gives, for either sign, a constant \(C_1=C_1(K,M)\) such that
\[
E\{|\widehat J_{nb}^{\pm}-J_{nb}^{\pm}|\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\}
\le C_1\left(\min_a m_{nba}\right)^{-1/2}.
\tag{10}
\]

Let \(t_{nb}=w_{nb}^2/n_{nb}\). When every arm count is at least two, overlap, count growth, and \((n_{nb}-1)^{-1}\le2n_{nb}^{-1}\) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
&E\bigl[|\widehat L_{nb}-L_{nb}|+|\widehat U_{nb}-U_{nb}|
\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\bigr]\\
&\qquad\le
C_2w_{nb}^2\left\{
\sum_a\frac{c_a^2M^2m_{nba}^{-1/2}}{m_{nba}}
+\frac{M^2(\min_am_{nba})^{-1/2}}{n_{nb}-1}\right\}\\
&\qquad\le C_3t_{nb}q_n^{-1/2},
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
where \(L_{nb},U_{nb},\widehat L_{nb},\widehat U_{nb}\) denote the corresponding block summands in the two envelope definitions. In the last line we used \(m_{nba}^{-1}\le(\varepsilon_0n_{nb})^{-1}\), \(m_{nba}^{-1/2}\le q_n^{-1/2}\), \(\sum_ac_a^2=1\), and absorbed the fixed \(K\) into \(C_3=C_3(K,\varepsilon_0,M)\).

The bound (11) is valid at every pre-block history with the same constant. Summing it, applying the tower property along the original adaptive filtration, and using \(\sum_bt_{nb}=s_n^2\) gives
\[
\sup_{\mathbb D\in\mathfrak E_M}
E_{\mathbb D}\frac{|\widehat L_n-L_n|+|\widehat U_n-U_n|}{s_n^2}
\le C_Mq_n^{-1/2}.
\tag{12}
\]
No maximum over history nodes, union bound, or conditioning on \(\mathcal F_{n,B_n}\) occurs. Consequently neither \(B_n\) nor a logarithmic history factor appears, and no block-leverage condition has been used. If an arm count is below two, then \(q_n<2\); since the theorem truncates the normalized loss at one, increasing \(C_M\) by a universal factor covers these finitely indexed convention cases. This proves the displayed theorem for every \(n\). Finally, \(\mathfrak D_M\subseteq\mathfrak E_M\) by the two class definitions, so the same bound holds over \(\mathfrak D_M\), and \(q_n\to\infty\) proves uniform convergence on both classes.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem identifies a parametric \(q_n^{-1/2}\) endpoint-rate elbow under a transparent optional history-uniform residual bound. \textit{Gap.} The bounded-iid static benchmark does not establish the adaptive finite-population conditional-sampling result or justify integrating random future quotas by the tower property. \textit{Consumer.} A quota-controlled batched trial with uniformly bounded nuisance-adjusted residuals obtains \(q_n^{-1/2}\) endpoint error with no \(B_n\) or logarithmic history penalty and no block-leverage requirement for this endpoint guarantee.

Let \(\mu_n\) and \(\mu\) be probability measures on \(\mathbb R\) with characteristic functions \(\chi_n\) and \(\chi\). If \(\chi_n(t)\to\chi(t)\) for every \(t\in\mathbb R\), then \(\mu_n\) converges weakly to \(\mu\).

\begin{lemma}[lem:martingale-clt-predictable-variation]\label{lem:martingale-clt-predictable-variation}
Let \((D_{nb})_{b\le B_n}\) be a triangular array of square-integrable martingale differences with respect to the pre-increment histories \(\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\), let \(V_n=\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}E(D_{nb}^2\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})\), and put \(\sigma_n^2=E(V_n)>0\).  If \(V_n/\sigma_n^2\to1\) in probability and, for every \(\epsilon>0\),
\[
\frac1{\sigma_n^2}\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}E\left[D_{nb}^2\mathbf 1\{|D_{nb}|>\epsilon\sigma_n\}\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\right]\to0
\quad\text{in probability},
\]
then \(\sigma_n^{-1}\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}D_{nb}\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1)\).
\end{lemma}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The lemma is proved self-contained by stopping predictable variance and comparing characteristic functions at the correct \(\epsilon\sigma_n\) Lindeberg threshold; Lévy continuity is the only cited logical substrate. \textit{Gap.} The inverse-threshold formula printed in the Li--Zhao supplementary transcription is mismatch provenance only and is not used as a premise. \textit{Consumer.} It converts the primitive QV stabilization and the fourth-moment-derived conditional Lindeberg bound into the Gaussian limit used by both inference results.

\begin{theorem}[thm:fixed-fraction-upper-endpoint-necessity]\label{thm:fixed-fraction-upper-endpoint-necessity}
There exists a nonadaptive fixed-quota subclass of an admissible common-parameter class \(\mathfrak D\) with \(B_n\to\infty\), \(q_n\to\infty\), equal block weights \(w_{nb}=B_n^{-1}\), block sizes \(n_{nb}=Kq_n\), equal quotas \(m_{nba}=q_n\), zero adjustments \(g_{nbi}(a)=0\), and identical bounded potential-outcome lists across arms, such that \(J_{nb}^{-}=0\) in every block and \(V_n=U_n\) deterministically. This subclass satisfies the full overlap, residual-moment, count-growth, block-leverage, predictable-QV growth, predictable-QV stabilization, endpoint-consistency, and martingale-CLT conditions. Consequently, for every \(\alpha\in(0,1)\) and every fixed \(\rho\in(0,1)\),
\[
 \Pr\!\left\{|\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n|\le z_{1-\alpha/2}
 \sqrt{(1-\rho)\widehat U_n}\right\}
 \longrightarrow
 2\Phi\!\left(z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt{1-\rho}\right)-1
 <1-\alpha.
\]
Therefore, for any broader design family containing this subclass, the limsup of its worst-case coverage after replacing \(\widehat U_n\) by \((1-\rho)\widehat U_n\) is at most the displayed strict sub-nominal limit. This is a fixed-quota subclass converse for uniform inference, not a claim that independently selected nodewise transport optimizers are jointly realizable by one completion over an adaptive history tree.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix the core's \(K\ge2\) and its normalized zero-sum contrast \(c\).  We exhibit a single nonadaptive triangular array, hence a subclass.  Let
\[
B_n=n,\qquad q_n=2n,\qquad n_{nb}=Kq_n,\qquad
m_{nba}=q_n,\qquad w_{nb}=B_n^{-1},\qquad g_{nbi}(a)=0.
\tag{1}
\]
In every block use the same list in every arm: for exactly \(Kq_n/2=Kn\) unit labels set \(Y_{nbi}(a)=1\) for every \(a\), and for the remaining \(Kn\) labels set \(Y_{nbi}(a)=-1\) for every \(a\).  Independently across blocks, randomize uniformly over assignments with the quotas in (1).  This is a history-independent instance of predictable complete-block randomization.

The armwise lists have mean zero and finite-population variance
\[
S_n^2=\frac{Kq_n}{Kq_n-1}.
\tag{2}
\]
They are bounded by one, so their \((4+\delta_0)\)-moment average is one.  Also \(p_{nba}=1/K\), which satisfies both sides of overlap for any fixed \(\varepsilon_0\in(0,1/K)\).  Thus the array satisfies the fixed-outcome, consistency, predictable-count, complete-randomization, predictable-adjustment, overlap, contrast, residual-moment, and growing-count requirements with, for example, \(C_R=1\).  Moreover,
\[
s_n^2=\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}\frac{w_{nb}^2}{n_{nb}}
=\frac1{B_nKq_n},
\qquad
\frac{w_{nb}^2/n_{nb}}{s_n^2}=\frac1{B_n}.
\tag{3}
\]
Hence block leverage holds with \(\ell_n=B_n^{-1}\to0\).

At each block, the common unit label couples all arm coordinates as \((X,\ldots,X)\), where \(X\) has the centered common marginal.  By \(\sum_ac_a=0\), this feasible coupling has quadratic cost
\[
\left(\sum_ac_aX\right)^2=X^2\left(\sum_ac_a\right)^2=0.
\tag{4}
\]
The cost is nonnegative for every coupling, so \(J_{nb}^{-}=0\).  The true fixed-potential-outcome coupling is exactly the coupling in (4).  The conditional-variance identity in \(\text{thm:exact-predictable-ot-envelope}\), followed by \(\sum_ac_a^2=1\), therefore gives
\[
E(D_{nb}^2\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
=w_{nb}^2\sum_a\frac{c_a^2S_n^2}{q_n}
=\frac{w_{nb}^2S_n^2}{q_n}.
\tag{5}
\]
The upper endpoint in \(\text{def:qv-envelope}\) uses the same diagonal term and subtracts \(J_{nb}^{-}/(n_{nb}-1)=0\).  Summing (5) consequently proves the exact identities
\[
V_n=U_n=\frac{S_n^2}{B_nq_n},
\qquad
\frac{V_n}{s_n^2}=KS_n^2\ge K.
\tag{6}
\]
Thus predictable-QV growth holds, for example with \(v_0=1\), and predictable-QV stabilization holds identically because \(V_n=E(V_n)\) is deterministic.

Here is the required smallest exact check.  Take \(K=2\), one block, \(q_n=2\), the common list \((-1,-1,1,1)\), and \(c=(1,-1)/\sqrt2\).  Across all six equiprobable allocations of two labels to arm one, its sample mean is \(-1,0,1\) with multiplicities \(1,4,1\), and the arm-two sample mean is its negative.  Therefore \(D_{n1}\) is \(-\sqrt2w_{n1},0,\sqrt2w_{n1}\) with probabilities \(1/6,4/6,1/6\), whence
\[
E(D_{n1})=0,
\qquad
E(D_{n1}^2)=\frac{2w_{n1}^2}{3}
=\frac{w_{n1}^2}{2}\frac43.
\tag{7}
\]
Since \(S_n^2=4/3\), (7) is exactly (5); enumeration therefore exposes neither bias nor a variance-formula failure.

The established history-uniform plug-in theorem applies to this admissible common-parameter array and yields
\[
\frac{|\widehat U_n-U_n|}{s_n^2}\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{in probability}.
\tag{8}
\]
Equation (6) has \(U_n/s_n^2=KS_n^2\ge K\), so (8) implies
\[
\frac{\widehat U_n}{U_n}\longrightarrow1
\quad\text{in probability}.
\tag{9}
\]
This verifies endpoint consistency under the original randomization law.

We also verify the martingale-CLT primitives.  For a uniform size-\(q_n\) subset \(S\) of a centered list \((x_i)_{i=1}^{Kq_n}\), put \(A_r=\sum_i|x_i|^r\) and \(\pi_r=(q_n)_r/(Kq_n)_r\).  Direct expansion by equality patterns gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
E\left(\sum_{i\in S}x_i\right)^4
={}&\pi_1A_4-4\pi_2A_4+3\pi_2(A_2^2-A_4)\\
&+6\pi_3(2A_4-A_2^2)+\pi_4(3A_2^2-6A_4).
\end{aligned}
\]
For the present \(\{-1,1\}\) list, \(A_2=A_4=Kq_n\) and \(\pi_r\le K^{-r}\).  Dividing by \(q_n^4\), then using \(|\sum_ac_ax_a|^4\le K\sum_a|x_a|^4\), gives a constant \(C_K<\infty\), depending only on fixed \(K\), such that
\[
E(D_{nb}^4\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
\le C_Kw_{nb}^4q_n^{-2}.
\tag{10}
\]
This argument does not assume independence among arm sample means.  With \(\sigma_n^2=V_n=S_n^2/(B_nq_n)>0\), conditional Markov inequality and (10) give, for every \(\epsilon>0\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\frac1{\sigma_n^2}\sum_bE\!\left[D_{nb}^2
 \mathbf1\{|D_{nb}|>\epsilon\sigma_n\}\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\right]\\
&\quad\le\frac1{\epsilon^2\sigma_n^4}\sum_bE(D_{nb}^4\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
\le\frac{C_K}{\epsilon^2B_n(S_n^2)^2}\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
The martingale property follows from the exact-envelope theorem, and QV stabilization follows from (6).  Hence \(\text{lem:martingale-clt-predictable-variation}\) and (11) imply
\[
\frac{\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n}{\sqrt{U_n}}
=\frac{\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n}{\sqrt{V_n}}
\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1).
\tag{12}
\]
The causal estimand is \(\tau_n=0\) on this witness because the potential outcomes agree across arms pointwise and \(\sum_ac_a=0\); it remains the fixed finite-population causal contrast, not a transport or regression functional.

Fix \(\rho\in(0,1)\), \(\alpha\in(0,1)\), and write \(z=z_{1-\alpha/2}>0\).  On \(\{|\widehat U_n/U_n-1|\le\eta\}\), where \(0<\eta<1\), the random threshold below is bracketed by the thresholds obtained by replacing \(\widehat U_n/U_n\) by \(1-\eta\) and \(1+\eta\).  Equations (9), (12), and continuity of the Gaussian distribution therefore give, first as \(n\to\infty\) and then as \(\eta\downarrow0\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\Pr\!\left\{|\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n|
\le z\sqrt{(1-\rho)\widehat U_n}\right\}\\
&\quad=\Pr\!\left\{
\left|\frac{\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n}{\sqrt{U_n}}\right|
\le z\sqrt{(1-\rho)\widehat U_n/U_n}\right\}\\
&\quad\longrightarrow2\Phi\!\left(z\sqrt{1-\rho}\right)-1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
Since \(z>0\), \(0<1-\rho<1\), and \(\Phi\) is strictly increasing, the limit in (13) is strictly less than \(2\Phi(z)-1=1-\alpha\).  Finally, if a broader design family contains this subclass, its worst-case coverage, namely the infimum over that family, is at most the coverage of this displayed member for every \(n\).  Taking the upper limit proves the stated fixed-fraction no-discount certificate.  The witness is nonadaptive and globally consistent, so no simultaneous realization of independently selected optimizers on different adaptive-history nodes is used.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} A bounded equal-quota sequence makes the true common-label coupling a zero-cost optimizer, hence \(V_n=U_n\), satisfies both endpoint-rate guarantees and all martingale conditions, and gives the exact strict undercoverage limit after a fixed discount. \textit{Gap.} The certificate closes fixed-fraction necessity on a globally consistent nonadaptive subclass; it makes no claim about simultaneous compatibility of independent nodewise optimizers on an adaptive history tree. \textit{Consumer.} Any broader design family containing the subclass cannot uniformly shorten the transport upper-endpoint standard error by a fixed fraction while retaining nominal coverage.

\section{Interpretation}

The observable interval estimates the range of the adaptive design's predictable quadratic variation that is compatible, node by node, with the armwise residual margins. It is exact in the rectangular convexified sense: at a fixed history node, and therefore along a fixed path when nodewise couplings are selected independently, both endpoints are attainable. This is not a theorem about one fixed potential-outcome completion simultaneously realizing choices on incompatible branches. The upper endpoint becomes a conservative standard error only under the separately stated predictable-QV stabilization condition, while the fixed-quota witness shows that a uniform procedure cannot discount that endpoint by a fixed fraction.

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The rectangular relaxation permits a separate convexified residual coupling at every possible history node.  The exact-envelope theorem proves endpoint attainment at a single node and along any fixed path inside that rectangular product, but it does not prove that one fixed potential-outcome array and one predictable fitting rule realize independently chosen optimizers across all nodes of an adaptive history tree.  The fixed-fraction necessity theorem does not need that stronger compatibility: its equal-quota witness is nonadaptive, uses one globally consistent bounded potential-outcome array, and makes the true coupling attain the upper endpoint in every block.  Accordingly, the no-discount conclusion is certified for every broader family containing that subclass, while global adaptive-history completion sharpness remains outside the claim.

\section{Honest scope}

The contributions are design-based and concern the fixed finite-population contrast \(\tau_n\): a predictable complete-block transport envelope, sorting and finite-program computation, history-uniform observable endpoint rates, conservative martingale Wald coverage under an explicit additional predictable-QV stabilization condition, and a fixed-quota subclass certificate against any fixed discount of the upper endpoint.  Predictable quotas followed by uniform complete-block randomization are a paper-specific design restriction rather than a feature attributed to the broader Li--Zhao model.  The general moment-class endpoint rate is \(q_n^{-\kappa}\), while the history-uniform bounded-residual subclass has the parametric rate \(q_n^{-1/2}\); neither statement conditions on the terminal adaptive path.  The QV lower bound and uniform QV stabilization are explicit high-level assumptions for inference, not consequences of overlap, count growth, leverage, or residual moments.  The nonadaptive equal-quota witness is globally consistent and proves strict undercoverage after any fixed multiplicative endpoint reduction.  No claim is made that one adaptive fixed-potential-outcome completion simultaneously attains independently selected nodewise optimizers, and the published Newby et al. contextual-bandit allocation would require a predictably batched complete-block redesign before these results apply.
