We construct the subclass for the fixed \(K\ge2\) and the normalized
zero-sum contrast in the core.  Let
\[
B_n=n,\qquad q_n=2n,\qquad n_{nb}=Kq_n,\qquad
m_{nba}=q_n,\qquad w_{nb}=B_n^{-1},\qquad g_{nbi}(a)=0.
\tag{1}
\]
In every arm and block use the same deterministic list: exactly
\(Kq_n/2=Kn\) unit labels have \(Y_{nbi}(a)=1\), the remaining \(Kn\)
labels have \(Y_{nbi}(a)=-1\), and the value is identical across arms for
each label.  Independently across blocks, randomize uniformly over
assignments realizing the fixed quotas in (1).  This is a history-independent,
hence predictable, special case of complete-block randomization.

The lists have mean zero and finite-population variance
\[
S_n^2=\frac{Kq_n}{Kq_n-1}.
\tag{2}
\]
They are bounded by one, their \((4+\delta_0)\)-moment average equals one,
and \(p_{nba}=1/K\).  Thus any fixed \(\varepsilon_0<1/K\), any fixed
\(\delta_0>0\), and \(C_R=1\) satisfy overlap and the residual-moment
condition.  Count growth holds with the displayed \(q_n\).  Moreover,
\[
s_n^2=\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}\frac{w_{nb}^2}{n_{nb}}
=\frac1{B_nKq_n},
\qquad
\frac{w_{nb}^2/n_{nb}}{s_n^2}=\frac1{B_n}.
\tag{3}
\]
Hence block leverage holds with \(\ell_n=B_n^{-1}\to0\).  Fixed potential
outcomes, consistency, predictable quotas, predictable adjustment, and the
two contrast conditions hold by construction and by the frozen properties
of \(c\).

At each block, couple all arm coordinates by the common unit label.  Since
the arm lists are identical and \(\sum_ac_a=0\), this feasible coupling has
cost
\[
\left(\sum_ac_aX\right)^2=X^2\left(\sum_ac_a\right)^2=0.
\tag{4}
\]
The cost is nonnegative, so \(J_{nb}^{-}=0\).  The true common-label
coupling is precisely the coupling in (4).  The exact conditional-variance
identity therefore gives
\[
E(D_{nb}^2\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
=w_{nb}^2\sum_a\frac{c_a^2S_n^2}{q_n}
=\frac{w_{nb}^2S_n^2}{q_n},
\tag{5}
\]
where \(\sum_ac_a^2=1\) was used.  Consequently,
\[
V_n=U_n=\frac{S_n^2}{B_nq_n},
\qquad
\frac{V_n}{s_n^2}=KS_n^2\ge K
\tag{6}
\]
deterministically.  Thus predictable-QV growth holds, for example with
\(v_0=1\), and predictable-QV stabilization holds identically because
\(V_n=E(V_n)\).

The smallest two-arm instance checks the variance identity exactly.  Take
one block, \(q_n=2\), the common list \((-1,-1,1,1)\), and
\(c=(1,-1)/\sqrt2\).  Across all six equiprobable assignments of two labels
to arm one, its sample mean is \(-1,0,1\) with multiplicities \(1,4,1\),
while the arm-two mean is its negative.  Hence \(D_{n1}\) is
\(-\sqrt2w_{n1},0,\sqrt2w_{n1}\) with probabilities
\(1/6,4/6,1/6\), so
\[
E(D_{n1})=0,
\qquad E(D_{n1}^2)=\frac{2w_{n1}^2}{3}
=\frac{w_{n1}^2}{2}\frac{4}{3}.
\tag{7}
\]
This equals (5), since \(S_n^2=4/3\), and the average over all six
assignments is zero.  Thus the exact finite check
exposes no failure of the witness formula.

The established history-uniform plug-in theorem applies to this admissible
array and yields
\[
\frac{|\widehat U_n-U_n|}{s_n^2}\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{in probability}.
\tag{8}
\]
Because (6) gives \(U_n/s_n^2=KS_n^2\ge K\), it follows that
\[
\frac{\widehat U_n}{U_n}\longrightarrow1
\quad\text{in probability}.
\tag{9}
\]
Thus endpoint consistency is obtained under the original sequential
randomization, without fixing a terminal history.

For completeness, we verify the martingale-CLT primitives directly.  If
\(S\) is a size-\(q_n\) simple random sample from the centered list and
\(A_r=\sum_i|x_i|^r\), expansion by equality patterns gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
E\left(\sum_{i\in S}x_i\right)^4
={}&\pi_1A_4-4\pi_2A_4+3\pi_2(A_2^2-A_4)\\
&+6\pi_3(2A_4-A_2^2)+\pi_4(3A_2^2-6A_4),
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
where \(\pi_r=(q_n)_r/(Kq_n)_r\).  Since \(|x_i|=1\),
\(A_2=A_4=Kq_n\), and \(\pi_r\le K^{-r}\), (10) is bounded in absolute
value by a constant depending only on \(K\) times \(q_n^2\).  Dividing by
\(q_n^4\), then using
\(|\sum_ac_ax_a|^4\le K\sum_a|x_a|^4\), gives a constant
\(C_K<\infty\) such that
\[
E(D_{nb}^4\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
\le C_Kw_{nb}^4q_n^{-2}.
\tag{11}
\]
No independence among the arm sample means is used in this inequality.
Here \(\sigma_n^2=V_n=S_n^2/(B_nq_n)>0\).  Conditional Markov inequality
and (11) yield, for every \(\epsilon>0\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\frac1{\sigma_n^2}\sum_bE\!\left[D_{nb}^2
\mathbf1\{|D_{nb}|>\epsilon\sigma_n\}
\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\right]\\
&\qquad\le\frac1{\epsilon^2\sigma_n^4}
\sum_bE(D_{nb}^4\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
\le\frac{C_K}{\epsilon^2B_n(S_n^2)^2}\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
The square-integrable martingale property follows from the exact-envelope
theorem, stabilization is deterministic by (6), and (12) is conditional
Lindeberg at the required \(\epsilon\sigma_n\) threshold.  The proved
martingale CLT therefore gives
\[
\frac{\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n}{\sqrt{U_n}}
=\frac{\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n}{\sqrt{V_n}}
\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1).
\tag{13}
\]
The causal estimand equals zero on this witness because the potential
outcomes agree across arms pointwise and \(\sum_ac_a=0\); it remains the
same fixed finite-population causal contrast \(\tau_n\), not a regression
or optimization functional.

Fix \(\rho\in(0,1)\).  For each \(\eta\in(0,1)\), on
\(\{|\widehat U_n/U_n-1|\le\eta\}\), the random threshold in the event
below lies between the corresponding deterministic thresholds obtained by
replacing \(\widehat U_n/U_n\) with \(1-\eta\) and \(1+\eta\).  Equation
(9) makes the complementary event negligible, and (13) gives convergence
at both deterministic thresholds.  Letting first \(n\to\infty\) and then
\(\eta\downarrow0\), continuity of the standard normal distribution yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\Pr\!\left\{|\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n|
\le z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt{(1-\rho)\widehat U_n}\right\}\\
&\quad=\Pr\!\left\{
\left|\frac{\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n}{\sqrt{U_n}}\right|
\le z_{1-\alpha/2}
\sqrt{(1-\rho)\widehat U_n/U_n}\right\}\\
&\quad\longrightarrow
2\Phi\!\left(z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt{1-\rho}\right)-1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
\]
Because \(\alpha\in(0,1)\), \(z_{1-\alpha/2}>0\); because
\(\rho\in(0,1)\) and \(\Phi\) is strictly increasing, the limit in (14)
is strictly below
\(2\Phi(z_{1-\alpha/2})-1=1-\alpha\).  If a broader design family contains
this subclass, its worst-case coverage is no larger than the coverage of
the displayed member at every \(n\).  Taking the limsup proves the final
claim.  The witness is nonadaptive and globally consistent, so the
certificate uses no assertion that independent nodewise optimizers can be
realized simultaneously over an adaptive history tree.
