Write \(t_{nb}=w_{nb}^2/n_{nb}\), so \(s_n^2=\sum_bt_{nb}\). We first derive the conditional Lindeberg condition from the primitive moment and leverage assumptions. The required moment calculation is elementary. Let \(x_1,\ldots,x_N\) be real numbers with \(\sum_i x_i=0\), and let \(S\) be a uniformly chosen subset of size \(m\), where \(1\le m\le N\). Put \(A_r=\sum_i|x_i|^r\) and \(\pi_r=(m)_r/(N)_r\), with \(\pi_r=0\) when \(r>m\). Expanding the fourth power according to the equality pattern among four indices, and using
\[
\sum_{i\ne j}x_i^3x_j=-\sum_i x_i^4,
\quad
\sum_{i\ne j}x_i^2x_j^2=\left(\sum_i x_i^2\right)^2-\sum_i x_i^4,
\]
\[
\sum_{i,j,k\ {
m distinct}}x_i^2x_jx_k
=2\sum_i x_i^4-\left(\sum_i x_i^2\right)^2,
\quad
\sum_{i,j,k,l\ {
m distinct}}x_ix_jx_kx_l
=3\left(\sum_i x_i^2\right)^2-6\sum_i x_i^4,
\]
gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
E\left(\sum_{i\in S}x_i\right)^4
={}&\pi_1A_4-4\pi_2A_4
+3\pi_2(A_2^2-A_4)\\
&+6\pi_3(2A_4-A_2^2)
+\pi_4(3A_2^2-6A_4).
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
Here \(A_2=\sum_i x_i^2\), since its terms are nonnegative. Because \(A_2^2\le NA_4\), \(\pi_r\le(m/N)^r\), and \(m\le N\), taking absolute values term by term in (12) yields a universal finite constant \(C_4\) such that
\[
E\left|\frac1m\sum_{i\in S}x_i\right|^4
\le\frac{C_4}{m^2}\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N|x_i|^4.
\tag{13}
\]

Apply (13), conditional on \(\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\), to
\(x_i=R_{nbi}(a)-\bar R_{nba}\) and the arm-\(a\) simple random sample supplied by complete-block randomization. The moment assumption and Jensen's inequality imply
\[
\frac1{n_{nb}}\sum_i|R_{nbi}(a)-\bar R_{nba}|^4
\le16\left\{\frac1{n_{nb}}\sum_i|R_{nbi}(a)|^{4+\delta_0}\right\}^{4/(4+\delta_0)}
\le16C_R^{4/(4+\delta_0)}.
\tag{14}
\]
If \(X_{nba}=m_{nba}^{-1}\sum_iI_i^a\{R_{nbi}(a)-\bar R_{nba}\}\), contrast normalization gives
\[
\left|\sum_ac_aX_{nba}\right|^4
\le\left(\sum_aX_{nba}^2\right)^2
\le K\sum_aX_{nba}^4.
\]
Equations (13)--(14), overlap \(m_{nba}\ge\varepsilon_0n_{nb}\), and fixed \(K\) therefore give a constant \(C_D<\infty\), depending only on \(K,\varepsilon_0,\delta_0,C_R\), for which
\[
E(D_{nb}^4\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
\le C_D\frac{w_{nb}^4}{n_{nb}^2}=C_Dt_{nb}^2
\quad\text{almost surely.}
\tag{15}
\]

Let \(\sigma_n^2=E(V_n)\). Predictable-QV growth gives
\[
\sigma_n^2=E(V_n)\ge v_0s_n^2>0.
\tag{16}
\]
For every \(\epsilon>0\), conditional Markov's inequality, (15), (16), and block leverage yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\frac1{\sigma_n^2}\sum_b
E\left[D_{nb}^2\mathbf1\{|D_{nb}|>\epsilon\sigma_n\}
\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\right]\\
&\qquad\le\frac1{\epsilon^2\sigma_n^4}\sum_bE(D_{nb}^4\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
\le\frac{C_D}{\epsilon^2v_0^2(s_n^2)^2}\sum_bt_{nb}^2\\
&\qquad\le\frac{C_D}{\epsilon^2v_0^2}
\frac{\max_bt_{nb}}{s_n^2}
\le\frac{C_D}{\epsilon^2v_0^2}\ell_n\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{17}
\]
Thus the conditional Lindeberg condition is derived rather than assumed. By \(\mathrm{ass:qv\text{-}stabilization}\), \(V_n/\sigma_n^2\to1\) in probability uniformly over the restricted class. The martingale property was proved in \(\mathrm{thm:exact\text{-}predictable\text{-}ot\text{-}envelope}\). Hence \(\mathrm{lem:martingale\text{-}clt\text{-}predictable\text{-}variation}\), applied with (16)--(17), gives
\[
\frac{\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n}{\sigma_n}
=\frac{\sum_bD_{nb}}{\sigma_n}\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1).
\tag{18}
\]
The equality uses \(\mathrm{def:aipw\text{-}estimator}\), consistency, \(p_{nba}=m_{nba}/n_{nb}\), and \(w_{nb}=n_{nb}/N_n\), which reduce its centered block contribution to \(D_{nb}\). Combining (18) with QV stabilization gives
\[
T_n:=\frac{\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n}{\sqrt{V_n}}
=\frac{(\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n)/\sigma_n}{\sqrt{V_n/\sigma_n^2}}
\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1).
\tag{19}
\]
All convergence statements in (18)--(19) are uniform. Indeed, if uniform convergence failed, one could choose a sequence of arrays whose discrepancy stayed bounded away from zero; (15)--(17) and uniform QV stabilization would hold along that selected triangular sequence, and the cited martingale theorem and Slutsky's identity in (19) would give the claimed convergence, a contradiction.

It remains to justify the conservative random scaling. The exact envelope gives \(V_n\le U_n\) pathwise. The already established history-uniform plug-in theorem and \(r_n=q_n^{-\kappa}\to0\) give, for every \(\eta>0\),
\[
\sup_{\mathbb D\in\mathfrak D}
\Pr_{\mathbb D}\left(\frac{|\widehat U_n-U_n|}{s_n^2}>\eta\right)\longrightarrow0.
\tag{20}
\]
Since \(V_n\ge v_0s_n^2\), (20) implies
\[
\frac{\widehat U_n}{V_n}
\ge\frac{U_n}{V_n}-\frac{|\widehat U_n-U_n|}{V_n}
\ge1-o_{\Pr}(1)
\tag{21}
\]
uniformly. Fix \(0<\zeta<1\), and put \(z=z_{1-\alpha/2}>0\). By \(\mathrm{def:wald\text{-}interval}\), (21), and then (19),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr\{\tau_n\in\mathrm{CI}_{n,1-\alpha}\}
&=\Pr\left\{|T_n|\le z\sqrt{\widehat U_n/V_n}\right\}\\
&\ge\Pr\{|T_n|\le z\sqrt{1-\zeta}\}
-\Pr\{\widehat U_n/V_n<1-\zeta\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
\]
Taking the uniform lower limit gives at least
\(2\Phi(z\sqrt{1-\zeta})-1\). Letting \(\zeta\downarrow0\) and using continuity of \(\Phi\) yields \(2\Phi(z)-1=1-\alpha\), proving the asserted uniform conservative coverage. Every probability here is under the original sequential assignment law; terminal-path conditioning is nowhere used.
