We construct the subclass explicitly. Fix \(K\ge2\), a zero-sum contrast satisfying \(\sum_ac_a^2=1\), and set
\[
q_n=2n,\qquad B_n=n^3,
\qquad n_{nb}=Kq_n,qquad m_{nba}=q_n,qquad w_{nb}=B_n^{-1}
\tag{1}
\]
for every \(b\le B_n\) and \(a\in\mathcal A\). In each block label exactly \(Kq_n/2\) units by \(x_i=-1\) and the remaining \(Kq_n/2\) units by \(x_i=1\), and define
\[
Y_{nbi}(a)=x_i\quad\text{for every }a,
\qquad g_{nbi}(a)=0.
\tag{2}
\]
The parity in (2) is valid because \(q_n=2n\). Use the nonadaptive complete-block design that is uniform over assignments with the quotas in (1). This gives fixed potential outcomes, consistency, predictable counts, complete-block randomization, and predictable zero adjustments. The arm fraction is \(1/K\), so overlap holds, for example, with the common constant \(\varepsilon_0=1/(2K)\). The residuals equal \(x_i\) and obey \(|R_{nbi}(a)|=1\), so the residual-moment condition holds with any fixed \(\delta_0>0\) and \(C_R=1\). The count lower bound is exactly \(q_n\to\infty\).

Every arm has the same centered residual marginal
\[
\mu_{nba}=\tfrac12\delta_{-1}+\tfrac12\delta_1,
\qquad S_{nba}^2=\frac{Kq_n}{Kq_n-1}=:S_n^2.
\tag{3}
\]
Coupling all arm coordinates identically, \(x_1=\cdots=x_K\), is feasible and has quadratic contrast cost
\[
\left(\sum_ac_ax_a\right)^2=x_1^2\left(\sum_ac_a\right)^2=0.
\]
The cost is nonnegative, hence \(J_{nb}^{-}=0\). The common unit labels in (2) induce exactly this zero-cost coupling, so the conditional-variance identity in \(\mathrm{thm:exact\text{-}predictable\text{-}ot\text{-}envelope}\) gives, block by block,
\[
E(D_{nb}^2\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
=\frac1{B_n^2}\sum_a\frac{c_a^2S_n^2}{q_n}
=\frac{S_n^2}{B_n^2q_n}=U_{nb}.
\tag{4}
\]
Therefore
\[
V_n=U_n=\frac{S_n^2}{B_nq_n}
\quad\text{deterministically}.
\tag{5}
\]
In particular, the predictable quadratic variation stabilizes exactly at its expectation. Moreover,
\[
s_n^2=\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}\frac{B_n^{-2}}{Kq_n}
=\frac1{B_nKq_n},
\qquad
\frac{V_n}{s_n^2}=KS_n^2\ge K,
\tag{6}
\]
so predictable-QV growth holds with, for example, \(v_0=1\). If \(t_{nb}=w_{nb}^2/n_{nb}\), then
\[
\max_{b\le B_n}\frac{t_{nb}}{s_n^2}=\frac1{B_n}=n^{-3}\longrightarrow0,
\tag{7}
\]
which verifies block leverage with \(\ell_n=B_n^{-1}\). Thus the construction is a nonadaptive fixed-quota subclass of one common-parameter admissible class.

We verify the martingale central limit theorem rather than assume it. Since every armwise sample mean lies in \([-1,1]\), contrast normalization and Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
|D_{nb}|\le B_n^{-1}\sum_a|c_a|le\frac{\sqrt K}{B_n}.
\tag{8}
\]
With \(\sigma_n^2=E(V_n)=V_n\), equations (1), (3), and (5) imply
\[
\max_b\frac{|D_{nb}|}{\sigma_n}
\le\sqrt{\frac{Kq_n}{B_nS_n^2}}
\le\sqrt{\frac{Kq_n}{B_n}}
=\frac{\sqrt{2K}}{n}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{9}
\]
Hence, for every \(\epsilon>0\), the conditional Lindeberg sum in \(\mathrm{lem:martingale\text{-}clt\text{-}predictable\text{-}variation}\) is identically zero for all sufficiently large \(n\); (5) supplies its predictable-variation condition. Since \(\tau_n=0\) by \(\sum_ac_a=0\), that lemma and the AIPW decomposition yield
\[
T_n:=\frac{\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n}{\sqrt{V_n}}
\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1).
\tag{10}
\]

The already proved history-uniform plug-in theorem applies to this admissible subclass. Because \(V_n=U_n\ge Ks_n^2\), it gives
\[
\frac{|\widehat U_n-U_n|}{V_n}
\le\frac1K\frac{|\widehat U_n-U_n|}{s_n^2}
\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{in probability}.
\tag{11}
\]
Thus \(\widehat U_n/V_n\to1\) in probability. Combining (10)--(11), the random-threshold form of Slutsky's argument and continuity of the standard normal law give, for every fixed \(\rho\in(0,1)\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr\!\left\{|\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n|
\le z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt{(1-\rho)\widehat U_n}\right\}
&=\Pr\!\left\{|T_n|
\le z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt{(1-\rho)\widehat U_n/V_n}\right\}\\
&\longrightarrow
2\Phi\!\left(z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt{1-\rho}\right)-1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
Because \(z_{1-\alpha/2}>0\), \(0<\sqrt{1-\rho}<1\), and \(\Phi\) is strictly increasing, the limit in (12) is strictly smaller than
\(2\Phi(z_{1-\alpha/2})-1=1-\alpha\).

For the required smallest exact check, take \(K=2\), \(q_n=2\), one block, \(c=(1,-1)/\sqrt2\), and the common list \((-1,-1,1,1)\). Among the \(\binom42=6\) assignments of arm one, the value of \(D_{n1}\) is \(-\sqrt2\) once, \(0\) four times, and \(\sqrt2\) once. Its exact variance is therefore \(2/3\), while (3)--(4) give \(S_n^2/q_n=(4/3)/2=2/3\) and \(J_{n1}^{-}=0\). Thus the asserted equality \(V_n=U_n\) survives exhaustive enumeration of the smallest nontrivial block.

Finally, if a broader design family contains this subclass, its rowwise worst-case coverage is an infimum over a set containing the constructed row and is therefore no larger than the probability in (12). Taking the limsup proves the advertised upper bound. The construction is nonadaptive and globally fixed, so this certificate uses no simultaneous selection of optimizers across distinct adaptive history nodes.
