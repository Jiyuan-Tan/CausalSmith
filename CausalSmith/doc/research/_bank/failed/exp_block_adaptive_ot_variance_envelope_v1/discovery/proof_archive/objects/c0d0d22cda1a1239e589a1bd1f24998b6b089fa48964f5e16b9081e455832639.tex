Fix an array \(\mathbb D\in\mathfrak D\), and take probability and expectation under its original sequential randomization law. By \(\text{def:aipw-estimator}\), \(\text{ass:consistency-no-interference}\), \(p_{nba}=m_{nba}/n_{nb}\), and the definition of the fixed causal estimand, direct subtraction gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n
&=\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}w_{nb}\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}c_a
\left\{\frac1{m_{nba}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal U_{nb}}
\mathbf 1\{Z_{nbi}=a\}R_{nbi}(a)-\bar R_{nba}\right\}\\
&=\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}D_{nb}=M_{nB_n}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
The martingale conclusion of \(\text{thm:exact-predictable-ot-envelope}\) shows that \((D_{nb})_{b\le B_n}\) is a square-integrable martingale-difference array. Thus cross-block covariances vanish by iterated expectation without conditioning on the terminal adaptive history.

We derive conditional Lindeberg from the primitive moment and leverage conditions. Conditional on \(\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\), put
\[
x_i(a)=R_{nbi}(a)-\bar R_{nba},
\qquad
X_a=\frac1{m_{nba}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal U_{nb}}
\mathbf 1\{Z_{nbi}=a\}x_i(a).
\]
Then \(D_{nb}=w_{nb}\sum_ac_aX_a\). The inequality \(|u-v|^4\le8(|u|^4+|v|^4)\), Jensen's inequality, the power-mean inequality, and \(\text{ass:residual-moments}\) imply, almost surely for every history,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac1{n_{nb}}\sum_i|x_i(a)|^4
&\le 8\frac1{n_{nb}}\sum_i|R_{nbi}(a)|^4+8|\bar R_{nba}|^4\\
&\le16\frac1{n_{nb}}\sum_i|R_{nbi}(a)|^4\\
&\le16\left\{\frac1{n_{nb}}\sum_i|R_{nbi}(a)|^{4+\delta_0}\right\}^{4/(4+\delta_0)}
\le16C_R^{4/(4+\delta_0)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]

We next establish the finite-population fourth-moment bound used below. Let \(x_1,\ldots,x_N\) be a centered fixed list, let \(S\) be a uniform subset of size \(m\), define \(A_r=\sum_{i=1}^Nx_i^r\), and let \(\pi_r=(m)_r/(N)_r\), with \(\pi_r=0\) for \(r>m\). Expansion according to the index-multiplicity patterns \(4\), \(3+1\), \(2+2\), \(2+1+1\), and \(1+1+1+1\), using \(\sum_ix_i=0\), gives
\[
E\left(\sum_{i\in S}x_i\right)^4
=\{\pi_1-7\pi_2+12\pi_3-6\pi_4\}A_4
+3\{\pi_2-2\pi_3+\pi_4\}A_2^2.
\tag{3}
\]
Indeed, the five contributions are respectively
\[
\pi_1A_4,\quad -4\pi_2A_4,\quad
3\pi_2(A_2^2-A_4),\quad
6\pi_3(2A_4-A_2^2),\quad
\pi_4(3A_2^2-6A_4),
\]
whose sum is (3). Since \(0\le\pi_r\le1\), \(A_2^2\le NA_4\), \(m\ge\varepsilon_0N\), and \(m\ge1\), division of (3) by \(m^4\) yields
\[
E\left(\frac1m\sum_{i\in S}x_i\right)^4
\le\left(\frac{26}{\varepsilon_0}+\frac{12}{\varepsilon_0^2}\right)
\frac1{m^2}\frac{A_4}{N}.
\tag{4}
\]
Under \(\text{ass:complete-block-randomization}\), each marginal arm sample is such a uniform subset. The divisions are valid because \(\text{ass:overlap}\) gives \(m_{nba}\ge\varepsilon_0n_{nb}>0\). Combining (2)--(4), \(\text{ass:contrast-normalization}\), and
\[
\left(\sum_ac_aX_a\right)^4
\le\left(\sum_aX_a^2\right)^2
\le K\sum_aX_a^4
\]
produces a finite constant \(C_0\), depending only on \(K,\varepsilon_0,\delta_0,C_R\), such that
\[
E(D_{nb}^4\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
\le C_0\left(\frac{w_{nb}^2}{n_{nb}}\right)^2
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\tag{5}
\]

Define the deterministic, array-specific scale
\[
\sigma_{n,\mathbb D}^2=E_{\mathbb D}V_n.
\]
Martingale orthogonality and (1) give \(\sigma_{n,\mathbb D}^2=\operatorname{Var}_{\mathbb D}(M_{nB_n})\), while \(\text{ass:qv-growth}\) gives
\[
\sigma_{n,\mathbb D}^2\ge v_0s_n^2>0.
\tag{6}
\]
Writing \(t_{nb}=w_{nb}^2/n_{nb}\), equations (5)--(6), \(\sum_bt_{nb}=s_n^2\), and \(\text{ass:block-leverage}\) yield
\[
\sup_{\mathbb D\in\mathfrak D}
\frac{\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}E_{\mathbb D}D_{nb}^4}{\sigma_{n,\mathbb D}^4}
\le\frac{C_0}{v_0^2}\frac{\sum_bt_{nb}^2}{s_n^4}
\le\frac{C_0}{v_0^2}\ell_n\longrightarrow0.
\tag{7}
\]
For \(\epsilon>0\), set
\[
\Lambda_{n,\mathbb D}(\epsilon)
=\frac1{\sigma_{n,\mathbb D}^2}\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}
E_{\mathbb D}\!\left[D_{nb}^2\mathbf1\{|D_{nb}|>\epsilon\sigma_{n,\mathbb D}\}
\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\right].
\]
The inequality \(u^2\mathbf1\{|u|>x\}\le u^4/x^2\), (7), and Markov's inequality imply
\[
\sup_{\mathbb D\in\mathfrak D}E_{\mathbb D}\Lambda_{n,\mathbb D}(\epsilon)
\le\frac1{\epsilon^2}
\sup_{\mathbb D\in\mathfrak D}
\frac{\sum_bE_{\mathbb D}D_{nb}^4}{\sigma_{n,\mathbb D}^4}
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{8}
\]
Thus the conditional Lindeberg condition, with exactly the threshold \(\epsilon\sigma_{n,\mathbb D}\), holds uniformly in probability.

The separate condition \(\text{ass:qv-stabilization}\) is now used. It is not derived from the quota, overlap, moment, leverage, count-growth, or QV-growth assumptions. It gives
\[
\frac{V_n}{\sigma_{n,\mathbb D}^2}
=\frac{V_n}{E_{\mathbb D}V_n}\longrightarrow1
\quad\text{in probability, uniformly over }\mathbb D\in\mathfrak D.
\tag{9}
\]
Equations (8)--(9) discharge the hypotheses of \(\text{lem:martingale-clt-predictable-variation}\). Consequently,
\[
\frac{\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n}{\sigma_{n,\mathbb D}}
\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1)
\quad\text{uniformly over }\mathfrak D.
\tag{10}
\]
To justify uniformity, if (10) failed in bounded-Lipschitz distance, there would be a subsequence and choices \(\mathbb D_n\in\mathfrak D\) retaining a fixed positive discrepancy. Taking row \(n\) from \(\mathbb D_n\) forms a diagonal martingale triangular array satisfying (8)--(9), so the cited lemma would force Gaussian convergence, a contradiction.

Equation (10) gives uniform tightness of \((\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n)/\sigma_{n,\mathbb D}\), and (9) gives \(\sqrt{V_n}/\sigma_{n,\mathbb D}\to1\) uniformly in probability. The uniform Slutsky argument therefore gives
\[
T_{n,\mathbb D}:=\frac{\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n}{\sqrt{V_n}}
\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1)
\quad\text{uniformly over }\mathfrak D.
\tag{11}
\]
Explicitly, for a bounded Lipschitz test function, first restrict to the event on which the numerator in (10) is bounded and the variance ratio in (9) lies in a fixed neighborhood of one, apply the Lipschitz bound there, and then let the two exceptional probabilities vanish.

It remains to prove conservative studentization. The pathwise containment in \(\text{thm:exact-predictable-ot-envelope}\) gives \(V_n\le U_n\). Since \(r_n=q_n^{-\kappa}\to0\), \(\text{thm:history-uniform-plugin}\) and \(V_n\ge v_0s_n^2\) imply, for every fixed \(\eta\in(0,1)\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sup_{\mathbb D\in\mathfrak D}
\Pr_{\mathbb D}\{ |\widehat U_n-U_n|>\eta V_n\}
&\le\sup_{\mathbb D\in\mathfrak D}
\Pr_{\mathbb D}\left\{\frac{|\widehat U_n-U_n|}{s_n^2}>\eta v_0\right\}\\
&\le\frac{C_{\mathrm{ep}}r_n}{1\wedge(\eta v_0)}\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
On the complementary event,
\[
\widehat U_n\ge U_n-\eta V_n\ge(1-\eta)V_n.
\tag{13}
\]
Let \(A_{n,\mathbb D}(\eta)=\{|\widehat U_n-U_n|\le\eta V_n\}\). By \(\text{def:wald-interval}\) and (13),
\[
\Pr_{\mathbb D}\{\tau_n\in\mathrm{CI}_{n,1-\alpha}\}
\ge\Pr_{\mathbb D}\{|T_{n,\mathbb D}|\le z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt{1-\eta}\}
-\Pr_{\mathbb D}\{A_{n,\mathbb D}(\eta)^c\}.
\]
Equations (11)--(12) and continuity of \(\Phi\) give
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{\mathbb D\in\mathfrak D}
\Pr_{\mathbb D}\{\tau_n\in\mathrm{CI}_{n,1-\alpha}\}
\ge2\Phi\!\left(z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt{1-\eta}\right)-1.
\tag{14}
\]
Letting \(\eta\downarrow0\) proves the lower bound \(1-\alpha\). The deterministic normalization \(E_{\mathbb D}V_n\) may depend on the array; no common limit for \(E_{\mathbb D}V_n/s_n^2\) was used, and every coverage probability is unconditional under the original adaptive design.
