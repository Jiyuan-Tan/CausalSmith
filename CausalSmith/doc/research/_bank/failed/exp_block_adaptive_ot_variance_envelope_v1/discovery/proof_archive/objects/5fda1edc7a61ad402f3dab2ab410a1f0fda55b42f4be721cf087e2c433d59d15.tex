Fix \(n\) and suppose that \(\mathcal C_n(\boldsymbol\mu_n)\ne\varnothing\).  The count restrictions give \(n_{nb}\ge2\) at every node.  Moreover, \(w_{nb}>0\), \(n_{nb}>0\), and \(B_n\ge1\), so the definition \(s_n^2=\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}w_{nb}^2/n_{nb}\) gives \(s_n^2>0\).  Thus every division below is well defined.

For a node \((n,b,h)\), define the cost and its best deterministic-matching value by
\[
 f_{nb}^{h}(\gamma)=\int\left(\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}c_ax_a\right)^2d\gamma,
 \qquad
 \iota_{nb}^{h}=\inf_{\gamma\in\mathcal I_{nb}^{h}}f_{nb}^{h}(\gamma),
\]
and put
\[
 \kappa_{nb}=\frac{w_{nb}^2}{s_n^2(n_{nb}-1)},\qquad
 d_{nb}^{h}=\kappa_{nb}\bigl(\iota_{nb}^{h}-J_{nb}^{h,-}\bigr).
\]
For \(\Xi_n\in\mathcal C_n(\boldsymbol\mu_n)\), also put
\[
 e_{nb}^{\Xi,h}=\kappa_{nb}
 \bigl(f_{nb}^{h}(\gamma_{nb}^{\Xi,h})-\iota_{nb}^{h}\bigr).
\]
By Definition~\texttt{def:history-tree-rectangular-relaxation},
\(J_{nb}^{h,-}=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{nb}^{h}}f_{nb}^{h}(\gamma)\).  By the definition of \(\mathcal I_{nb}^{h}\), every deterministic same-size unit-label matching is a coupling of the prescribed margins, and hence
\(\mathcal I_{nb}^{h}\subseteq\Gamma_{nb}^{h}\).  By Definition~\texttt{def:completion-class}, the common labels of any \(\Xi_n\in\mathcal C_n(\boldsymbol\mu_n)\) induce just such a matching, so
\(\gamma_{nb}^{\Xi,h}\in\mathcal I_{nb}^{h}\).  Consequently,
\[
 J_{nb}^{h,-}\le \iota_{nb}^{h}
 \le f_{nb}^{h}(\gamma_{nb}^{\Xi,h}),
\]
and every term is finite: for any \(\gamma\in\Gamma_{nb}^{h}\), the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality and the prescribed \(\mathcal P_2(\mathbb R)\) margins give
\[
 f_{nb}^{h}(\gamma)
 \le K\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}c_a^2\int x_a^2d\mu_{nba}^{h}(x_a)<\infty. 
\]
Since \(\kappa_{nb}>0\), this proves
\[
 d_{nb}^{h}\ge0,
 \qquad
 e_{nb}^{\Xi,h}\ge0. \tag{1}
\]

The diagonal finite-population variance terms in \(U_{nb}^{h}\) and \(Q_{nb}^{\Xi,h}\) are identical.  Subtracting the declared formula for \(Q_{nb}^{\Xi,h}\) from the formula for \(U_{nb}^{h}\) in Definition~\texttt{def:history-tree-rectangular-relaxation} therefore gives, without an inequality,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \frac{U_{nb}^{h}-Q_{nb}^{\Xi,h}}{s_n^2}
 &=\frac{w_{nb}^2}{s_n^2(n_{nb}-1)}
 \left\{f_{nb}^{h}(\gamma_{nb}^{\Xi,h})-J_{nb}^{h,-}\right\}\\
 &=d_{nb}^{h}+e_{nb}^{\Xi,h}. \tag{2}
 \end{aligned}
\]
For a path \(\varpi\in\mathcal P_n\), define
\[
 d_n(\varpi)=\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}d_{nb}^{h_b(\varpi)},
 \qquad
 e_n^{\Xi}(\varpi)=\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}e_{nb}^{\Xi,h_b(\varpi)}.
\]
The definitions of \(A_n\) and \(C_n\) in the theorem statement and identity (2) yield
\[
 A_n=\sup_{\varpi\in\mathcal P_n}d_n(\varpi),
 \qquad
 C_n=\inf_{\Xi_n\in\mathcal C_n(\boldsymbol\mu_n)}
       \sup_{\varpi\in\mathcal P_n}e_n^{\Xi}(\varpi), \tag{3}
\]
and Definition~\texttt{def:completion-gap} yields
\[
 \Delta_n^{\mathrm{tree}}(\boldsymbol\mu_n)
 =\inf_{\Xi_n\in\mathcal C_n(\boldsymbol\mu_n)}
   \sup_{\varpi\in\mathcal P_n}
   \left\{d_n(\varpi)+e_n^{\Xi}(\varpi)\right\}. \tag{4}
\]
All the displayed values are finite for fixed \(n\): the margins have finite support and finite second moments, the path set is finite, and the completion class in (4) is nonempty.

By (1), for every \(\Xi_n\),
\[
 \sup_{\varpi}\{d_n(\varpi)+e_n^{\Xi}(\varpi)\}
 \ge \sup_{\varpi}d_n(\varpi)=A_n.
\]
Taking the infimum over \(\Xi_n\) proves
\(\Delta_n^{\mathrm{tree}}\ge A_n\).  Similarly, (1) gives
\[
 \sup_{\varpi}\{d_n(\varpi)+e_n^{\Xi}(\varpi)\}
 \ge \sup_{\varpi}e_n^{\Xi}(\varpi),
\]
and (3)--(4) give \(\Delta_n^{\mathrm{tree}}\ge C_n\).  Hence
\[
 \Delta_n^{\mathrm{tree}}(\boldsymbol\mu_n)\ge\max\{A_n,C_n\}. \tag{5}
\]
Conversely, let \(\epsilon>0\).  By finiteness of \(C_n\) and the definition of the infimum in (3), there is a completion \(\Xi_n^{\epsilon}\) such that
\[
 \sup_{\varpi}e_n^{\Xi^{\epsilon}}(\varpi)\le C_n+\epsilon.
\]
For this completion, the elementary inequality \(\sup_{\varpi}(r_{\varpi}+t_{\varpi})\le\sup_{\varpi}r_{\varpi}+\sup_{\varpi}t_{\varpi}\) and (3) imply
\[
 \sup_{\varpi}\{d_n(\varpi)+e_n^{\Xi^{\epsilon}}(\varpi)\}
 \le A_n+C_n+\epsilon.
\]
Using this completion in (4) and then letting \(\epsilon\downarrow0\) proves
\[
 \Delta_n^{\mathrm{tree}}(\boldsymbol\mu_n)\le A_n+C_n. \tag{6}
\]
Equations (5)--(6) establish the first claimed display.  Since all three quantities are nonnegative, (5) shows that \(\Delta_n^{\mathrm{tree}}\to0\) implies both \(A_n\to0\) and \(C_n\to0\), while (6) proves the converse.

Definition~\texttt{def:completion-gap} identifies the nodewise diagnostic as
\[
 \Delta_n^{\mathrm{node}}(\boldsymbol\mu_n)
 =\sup_{b\le B_n,\,h\in\mathcal H_{n,b-1}}d_{nb}^{h}. \tag{7}
\]
Every history node has a root-to-leaf extension.  Given \((b,h)\), choose such a path \(\varpi\) with \(h_b(\varpi)=h\).  Nonnegativity in (1) then gives
\[
 A_n\ge d_n(\varpi)\ge d_{nb}^{h}.
\]
Taking the supremum over nodes and using (7) proves
\(\Delta_n^{\mathrm{node}}\le A_n\).  In the other direction, every path visits exactly one node in each of its \(B_n\) blocks, so (7) gives
\[
 d_n(\varpi)\le B_n\Delta_n^{\mathrm{node}}(\boldsymbol\mu_n)
\]
for every \(\varpi\).  Taking the path supremum proves
\(A_n\le B_n\Delta_n^{\mathrm{node}}\).  This also supplies the promised computable side: each \(\mathcal I_{nb}^{h}\) is a finite matching set and \(\mathcal P_n\) is finite, so \(A_n\) is obtained by finitely many cost evaluations, finite minima, path sums, and a finite maximum.  In contrast, (3) shows exactly what \(C_n\) measures: after the best matching is chosen separately at every node, \(C_n\) is the smallest worst-path excess forced by requiring all nodewise couplings to arise from one ex ante completion with common labels, fixed potential outcomes, and one predictable fitting rule.

It remains to verify the obstruction.  Suppose, under the standing nonemptiness condition, that
\(\mu_{nba}^{h}=\delta_{0_{\mathbb R}}\) for every \((b,h,a)\).  If \(\gamma\in\Gamma_{nb}^{h}\), then each coordinate is zero \(\gamma\)-almost surely.  Since \(K\) is finite, all coordinates are simultaneously zero \(\gamma\)-almost surely, and therefore
\[
 \Gamma_{nb}^{h}=\{\delta_{(0,\ldots,0)}\},
 \qquad f_{nb}^{h}(\gamma)=0. \tag{8}
\]
The matching set is nonempty and is contained in this singleton.  Also, every completion-induced coupling belongs to the same singleton.  Hence \(J_{nb}^{h,-}=\iota_{nb}^{h}=0\) and every \(d_{nb}^{h}\) and \(e_{nb}^{\Xi,h}\) is zero.  Equations (3), (4), and (7) now give
\[
 A_n=C_n=\Delta_n^{\mathrm{tree}}(\boldsymbol\mu_n)
 =\Delta_n^{\mathrm{node}}(\boldsymbol\mu_n)=0. \tag{9}
\]

Because \(\mu_{nba}^{h}\) is the empirical law of the centered residuals, equality to \(\delta_{0_{\mathbb R}}\) means
\(R_{nbi}^{h}(a)-\bar R_{nba}^{h}=0\) for every unit.  Thus every nodewise finite-population variance \((S_{nba}^{h})^2\) is zero.  Combining this fact with (8) in the displayed definition of \(Q_{nb}^{\Xi,h}\) gives
\[
 Q_{nb}^{\Xi,h}=w_{nb}^2\left\{0-\frac0{n_{nb}-1}\right\}=0. \tag{10}
\]
The quantity \(Q_{nb}^{\Xi,h}\) is, by definition, the conditional-variance contribution at node \(h\).  Therefore, on every root-to-leaf assignment path, including the realized one,
\[
 V_n=\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}Q_{nb}^{\Xi,h_b(\varpi)}=0. \tag{11}
\]
This conclusion is pathwise and uses no conditioning on the final adaptive history.

The zero-margin case is nonvacuous.  Take the prespecified predictable fitting rule to be identically zero at every history node, take \(Y_{nbi}(a)=0\) for every \((n,b,i,a)\), and use any legal predictable positive fixed-count allocation rule.  These choices are made ex ante, satisfy Definition~\texttt{def:completion-class}, and induce precisely the margins in (8).  Nevertheless, \(v_0>0\) and \(s_n^2>0\), whereas (11) gives \(V_n=0\).  Hence Assumption~\texttt{ass:qv-growth}, which requires \(V_n\ge v_0s_n^2\), fails for every completion of this zero-margin system.  Thus a vanishing global compatibility gap alone cannot furnish an admissible nondegenerate near-attaining completion sequence.  In particular, it cannot by itself imply a fixed-fraction undercoverage assertion whose witness must remain in the admissible nondegenerate class.
