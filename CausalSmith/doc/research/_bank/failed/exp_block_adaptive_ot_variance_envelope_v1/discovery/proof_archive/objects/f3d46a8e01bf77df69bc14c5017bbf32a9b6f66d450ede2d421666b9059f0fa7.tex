Fix \(n\) with \(B_n=2\).  We first prove the assertions without specializing the block sizes.  Conditional on \(\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\), the count vector is fixed by \(\mathrm{ass{:}predictable\text{-}counts}\), the adjustment array is fixed by \(\mathrm{ass{:}predictable\text{-}adjustment}\), and the potential-outcome array is fixed by the design-based definition of \(Y_{nbi}(a)\).  Hence every \(R_{nbi}(a)\) and \(\bar R_{nba}\) is fixed under this conditioning.  The positivity built into the declared range \(m_{nba}\in\{1,\ldots,n_{nb}-1\}\) makes all sample-mean divisions below legitimate.

Write \(I_{nbi}(a)=\mathbf 1\{Z_{nbi}=a\}\).  Under \(\mathrm{ass{:}complete\text{-}block\text{-}randomization}\), the number of assignments in the conditional support is
\[
 \frac{n_{nb}!}{\prod_{d\in\mathcal A}m_{nbd}!},
\]
whereas the number assigning a fixed unit \(i\) to arm \(a\) is
\[
 \frac{(n_{nb}-1)!}{(m_{nba}-1)!\prod_{d\ne a}m_{nbd}!}.
\]
Their ratio gives the conditional first-order inclusion probability
\[
 \Pr\{Z_{nbi}=a\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\}=\frac{m_{nba}}{n_{nb}}=p_{nba}.
\]
Linearity over the finite conditional assignment support therefore yields, arm by arm,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E\!\left[
   \frac1{m_{nba}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal U_{nb}}I_{nbi}(a)R_{nbi}(a)
   \mathrel{\Big|}\mathcal F_{n,b-1}
 \right]
 &=\frac1{m_{nba}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal U_{nb}}
   \frac{m_{nba}}{n_{nb}}R_{nbi}(a)\\
 &=\frac1{n_{nb}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal U_{nb}}R_{nbi}(a)
 =\bar R_{nba}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Substitution in \(\mathrm{def{:}block\text{-}martingale}\) proves
\[
 E(D_{nb}\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
 =w_{nb}\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}c_a(\bar R_{nba}-\bar R_{nba})=0,
 \qquad b=1,2.
\]
All variables are integrable because, for fixed \(n\), they take finitely many finite real values.  The tower identity now gives
\[
 E(D_{n1})=E\{E(D_{n1}\mid\mathcal F_{n,0})\}=0.
\]
Moreover, \(D_{n1}\) is \(\mathcal F_{n,1}\)-measurable: it is a function of the fixed first-block arrays and the first-block assignment, all of which are known after block one.  Consequently, the pull-out and tower identities for conditional expectation give
\[
 E(D_{n1}D_{n2})
 =E\!\left[D_{n1}E(D_{n2}\mid\mathcal F_{n,1})\right]=0.
\]
Thus no cross-block covariance survives adaptivity.  Finally, setting \(B_n=2\) in the defining formula for predictable quadratic variation in \(\mathrm{def{:}block\text{-}martingale}\) gives, pathwise,
\[
 V_n=E(D_{n1}^2\mid\mathcal F_{n,0})
     +E(D_{n2}^2\mid\mathcal F_{n,1}).
\]
Since both increments have mean zero, expansion of the square and the preceding cross-moment calculation also show that the unconditional design variance is
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(D_{n1}+D_{n2})
 =E(D_{n1}^2)+E(D_{n2}^2)
 =E\!\left[E(D_{n1}^2\mid\mathcal F_{n,0})
             +E(D_{n2}^2\mid\mathcal F_{n,1})\right]
 =E(V_n).
\]

Here is the promised exhaustive four-unit check.  Take two arms, four units in each block, first-block counts \((2,2)\), and contrast \(c=(c_1,c_2)\).  Index each first-block assignment by the arm-one subset
\[
 \mathcal S=\{12,13,14,23,24,34\},
\]
each member having probability \(1/6\); its complement receives arm two.  Put \(x_i=R_{n1i}(1)\), \(y_i=R_{n1i}(2)\), \(\bar x=4^{-1}\sum_i x_i\), and \(\bar y=4^{-1}\sum_i y_i\).  On history \(S\in\mathcal S\), the first score is exactly
\[
 d_1(S)=w_{n1}\left[
 c_1\left\{\frac12\sum_{i\in S}x_i-\bar x\right\}
 +c_2\left\{\frac12\sum_{i\notin S}y_i-\bar y\right\}
 \right].
\]
The six assignments form the three complementary pairs \((12,34)\), \((13,24)\), and \((14,23)\).  For each pair, direct subtraction from the four-unit means gives \(d_1(S^c)=-d_1(S)\), so
\[
 \frac16\sum_{S\in\mathcal S}d_1(S)=0,
 \qquad
 q_1:=\operatorname{Var}(D_{n1}\mid\mathcal F_{n,0})
 =\frac16\sum_{S\in\mathcal S}d_1(S)^2.
\]

The quota switch can genuinely depend on outcomes.  For example, choose the fixed first unit so that \(Y_{n1,1}(1)=1\) and \(Y_{n1,1}(2)=-1\), and after block one set
\[
 k(S):=m_{n2,1}
 =\begin{cases}
 1,&Y_{n1,1}^{\mathrm{obs}}>0,\\
 3,&Y_{n1,1}^{\mathrm{obs}}<0,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad m_{n2,2}=4-k(S).
\]
This rule is \(\mathcal F_{n,1}\)-measurable and selects \((1,3)\) on the three histories containing unit one in \(S\), and \((3,1)\) on the other three.  Given a first history \(S\), allow the predictable second-block adjustment, and hence the residuals, to depend on that history; write \(u_i(S)=R_{n2i}(1)\), \(v_i(S)=R_{n2i}(2)\), with corresponding four-unit means \(\bar u(S)\) and \(\bar v(S)\).  The four possible arm-one sets are
\[
 \mathcal T_1=\{1,2,3,4\},
 \qquad
 \mathcal T_3=\{123,124,134,234\},
\]
where the notation lists singleton or three-element subsets.  Each \(T\in\mathcal T_{k(S)}\) has conditional probability \(1/4\), and its score is
\[
 d_2(T;S)=w_{n2}\left[
 c_1\left\{\frac1{k(S)}\sum_{i\in T}u_i(S)-\bar u(S)\right\}
 +c_2\left\{\frac1{4-k(S)}\sum_{i\notin T}v_i(S)-\bar v(S)\right\}
 \right].
\]
Across either list \(\mathcal T_k\), each unit occurs in the arm-one subset in the fraction \(k/4\) of the four assignments and in its complement in the fraction \((4-k)/4\).  Therefore, for every one of the six first-block histories,
\[
 \frac14\sum_{T\in\mathcal T_{k(S)}}d_2(T;S)=0,
 \qquad
 q_2(S):=\operatorname{Var}(D_{n2}\mid\mathcal F_{n,1}=S)
 =\frac14\sum_{T\in\mathcal T_{k(S)}}d_2(T;S)^2.
\]
This enumerates all \(6\times4=24\) adaptive assignment paths.  In particular, the complete cross-block calculation is
\[
 \frac16\sum_{S\in\mathcal S}d_1(S)
 \left\{\frac14\sum_{T\in\mathcal T_{k(S)}}d_2(T;S)\right\}=0.
\]
For every first-block history \(S\), the realized predictable quadratic variation is the sum of the two conditional variances,
\[
 V_n(S)=q_1+q_2(S),
\]
and averaging over the six histories gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(D_{n1}+D_{n2})
 =q_1+\frac16\sum_{S\in\mathcal S}q_2(S)=E(V_n).
\]
The calculation conditions only on the prior history \(\mathcal F_{n,0}\) or \(\mathcal F_{n,1}\), never on the realized final adaptive path.
