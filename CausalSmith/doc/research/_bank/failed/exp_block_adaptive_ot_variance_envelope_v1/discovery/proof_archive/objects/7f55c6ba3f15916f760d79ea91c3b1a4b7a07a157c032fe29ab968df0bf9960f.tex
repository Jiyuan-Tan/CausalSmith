We construct the subclass for the fixed \(K\ge2\) and the normalized zero-sum contrast in the core. Let
\[
B_n=n,qquad q_n=2n,qquad n_{nb}=Kq_n,qquad
m_{nba}=q_n,qquad w_{nb}=B_n^{-1},qquad g_{nbi}(a)=0.
\tag{1}
\]
In every arm and block use the same deterministic list: exactly \(Kq_n/2=Kn\) unit labels have \(Y_{nbi}(a)=1\), the remaining \(Kn\) labels have \(Y_{nbi}(a)=-1\), and the value is identical across arms for each label. Independently across blocks, randomize uniformly over assignments realizing the fixed quotas in (1). This is a nonadaptive special case of predictable complete-block randomization.

The lists have mean zero and finite-population variance
\[
S_n^2=\frac{Kq_n}{Kq_n-1}.
\tag{2}
\]
They are bounded by one, their \((4+\delta_0)\)-moment average equals one, and \(p_{nba}=1/K\). Thus any fixed \(\varepsilon_0<1/K\), any fixed \(\delta_0>0\), and \(C_R=1\) satisfy overlap and the residual-moment condition. Count growth holds with the displayed \(q_n\). Moreover,
\[
s_n^2=\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}\frac{w_{nb}^2}{n_{nb}}
=\frac1{B_nKq_n},
\qquad
\frac{w_{nb}^2/n_{nb}}{s_n^2}=\frac1{B_n}.
\tag{3}
\]
Hence block leverage holds with \(\ell_n=B_n^{-1}\to0\). Fixed potential outcomes, consistency, predictable quotas, predictable adjustment, and the two contrast conditions hold by construction and by the frozen properties of \(c\).

At each block, couple all arm coordinates by the common unit label. Since the arm lists are identical and \(\sum_ac_a=0\), this feasible coupling has cost
\[
\left(\sum_ac_aX\right)^2=X^2\left(\sum_ac_a\right)^2=0.
\tag{4}
\]
The cost is nonnegative, so \(J_{nb}^{-}=0\). The true common-label coupling is precisely the coupling in (4). The exact conditional-variance identity therefore gives
\[
E(D_{nb}^2\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
=w_{nb}^2\sum_a\frac{c_a^2S_n^2}{q_n}
=\frac{w_{nb}^2S_n^2}{q_n},
\tag{5}
\]
where \(\sum_ac_a^2=1\) was used. Consequently,
\[
V_n=U_n=\frac{S_n^2}{B_nq_n},
\qquad
\frac{V_n}{s_n^2}=KS_n^2\ge K
\tag{6}
\]
deterministically. Thus predictable-QV growth holds, for example with \(v_0=1\), and predictable-QV stabilization holds identically because \(V_n=E(V_n)\).

Both endpoint plug-in guarantees apply. The general moment-class theorem gives normalized truncated endpoint error at most \(C_{\mathrm{ep}}q_n^{-\kappa}\). Since this witness has the history-uniform residual bound \(M=1\), \(\mathrm{thm:bounded\text{-}residual\text{-}parametric\text{-}plugin}\) also gives the sharper bound \(C_1q_n^{-1/2}\), with no factor depending on \(B_n\). Either bound, together with (6), implies
\[
\frac{\widehat U_n}{U_n}\longrightarrow1
\quad\text{in probability}.
\tag{7}
\]

For completeness, we verify the martingale CLT primitives directly. Conditional on the past, each arm sample mean is a simple-random-sample mean from the bounded centered list. Expanding the fourth power of a size-\(q_n\) sample sum according to equality patterns among four sampled labels, the inclusion probabilities \((q_n)_r/(Kq_n)_r\), \(r=1,\ldots,4\), and centering show that its fourth moment is bounded by a fixed multiple of \(q_n^2\). Division by \(q_n^4\), followed by \(|\sum_ac_ax_a|^4\le K\sum_a|x_a|^4\), therefore gives a constant \(C_K<\infty\) such that
\[
E(D_{nb}^4\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})\le C_Kw_{nb}^4q_n^{-2}.
\tag{8}
\]
Here \(\sigma_n^2=V_n=S_n^2/(B_nq_n)>0\). Conditional Markov's inequality and (8) yield, for every \(\epsilon>0\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\frac1{\sigma_n^2}\sum_bE\left[D_{nb}^2
\mathbf1\{|D_{nb}|>\epsilon\sigma_n\}\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\right]\\
&\qquad\le\frac1{\epsilon^2\sigma_n^4}\sum_bE(D_{nb}^4\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
\le\frac{C_K}{\epsilon^2B_n(S_n^2)^2}\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
The square-integrable martingale property follows from the exact-envelope theorem, stabilization is deterministic by (6), and (9) is conditional Lindeberg at the required \(\epsilon\sigma_n\) threshold. The proved martingale CLT therefore gives
\[
\frac{\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n}{\sqrt{U_n}}
=\frac{\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n}{\sqrt{V_n}}
\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1).
\tag{10}
\]
The causal estimand equals zero on this witness because the potential outcomes agree across arms pointwise and \(\sum_ac_a=0\); it remains the same fixed finite-population causal contrast \(\tau_n\), not a regression or optimization functional.

Fix \(\rho\in(0,1)\). For each \(\eta\in(0,1)\), on the event \(|\widehat U_n/U_n-1|\le\eta\), the random threshold in the event below lies between the corresponding deterministic thresholds obtained by replacing \(\widehat U_n/U_n\) with \(1-\eta\) and \(1+\eta\). Equation (7) makes the complementary event negligible, and (10) gives convergence at both deterministic thresholds. Letting first \(n\to\infty\) and then \(\eta\downarrow0\), continuity of the standard normal distribution yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\Pr\!\left\{|\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n|
\le z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt{(1-\rho)\widehat U_n}\right\}\\
&\quad=\Pr\!\left\{
\left|\frac{\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n}{\sqrt{U_n}}\right|
\le z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt{(1-\rho)\widehat U_n/U_n}\right\}\\
&\quad\longrightarrow
2\Phi\!\left(z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt{1-\rho}\right)-1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
Because \(\alpha\in(0,1)\), \(z_{1-\alpha/2}>0\); because \(\rho\in(0,1)\) and \(\Phi\) is strictly increasing, the limit in (11) is strictly below
\(2\Phi(z_{1-\alpha/2})-1=1-\alpha\). If a broader design family contains this subclass, its worst-case coverage is no larger than the coverage of the displayed member at every \(n\). Taking the limsup proves the final claim. The witness is nonadaptive and globally consistent, so this certificate uses no assertion that independent nodewise optimizers can be realized simultaneously over an adaptive history tree.
