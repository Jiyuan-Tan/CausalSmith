Let
\[
t_{nb}=\frac{w_{nb}^2}{n_{nb}},\qquad s_n^2=\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}t_{nb},
\qquad p=4+\delta_0.
\tag{1}
\]
We derive the conditional Lindeberg condition from the primitive residual-moment and leverage assumptions. First consider deterministic real numbers \(x_1,\ldots,x_N\) with \(\sum_i x_i=0\), and let \(S\) be a uniformly chosen subset of size \(m\), where \(1\le m\le N\). Write \(A_r=\sum_i|x_i|^r\) and \(\pi_r=(m)_r/(N)_r\), with \(\pi_r=0\) when \(r>m\). Expansion of the fourth power according to equality patterns among its four indices gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
E\left(\sum_{i\in S}x_i\right)^4
={}&\pi_1A_4-4\pi_2A_4+3\pi_2(A_2^2-A_4)\\
&+6\pi_3(2A_4-A_2^2)+\pi_4(3A_2^2-6A_4).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
Indeed, the four identities used in this enumeration are
\[
\sum_{i\ne j}x_i^3x_j=-A_4,
\qquad
\sum_{i\ne j}x_i^2x_j^2=A_2^2-A_4,
\tag{3}
\]
\[
\sum_{\substack{i,j,k\\\mathrm{all\ distinct}}}x_i^2x_jx_k
=2A_4-A_2^2,
\qquad
\sum_{\substack{i,j,k,l\\\mathrm{all\ distinct}}}x_ix_jx_kx_l
=3A_2^2-6A_4,
\tag{4}
\]
each following by expanding \((\sum_i x_i)^r=0\) and collecting equality patterns. Since \(A_2^2\le NA_4\), \(\pi_r\le(m/N)^r\), and \(m\le N\), taking absolute values term by term in (2) yields a universal finite constant \(C_4\) such that
\[
E\left|\frac1m\sum_{i\in S}x_i\right|^4
\le\frac{C_4}{m^2}\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N|x_i|^4.
\tag{5}
\]

Apply (5), conditionally on \(\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\), to
\(x_i=R_{nbi}(a)-\bar R_{nba}\) and the arm-\(a\) simple random sample supplied by complete-block randomization. The residual-moment condition, Jensen's inequality, and the power-mean inequality imply
\[
\frac1{n_{nb}}\sum_i|R_{nbi}(a)-\bar R_{nba}|^4
\le16\left\{\frac1{n_{nb}}\sum_i|R_{nbi}(a)|^p\right\}^{4/p}
\le16C_R^{4/p}.
\tag{6}
\]
Put
\[
X_{nba}=\frac1{m_{nba}}\sum_i\mathbf1\{Z_{nbi}=a\}
\{R_{nbi}(a)-\bar R_{nba}\}.
\]
Contrast normalization gives
\[
\left|\sum_ac_aX_{nba}\right|^4
\le\left(\sum_aX_{nba}^2\right)^2
\le K\sum_aX_{nba}^4.
\tag{7}
\]
Equations (5)--(7), overlap \(m_{nba}\ge\varepsilon_0n_{nb}\), and fixed \(K\) therefore give a constant \(C_D<\infty\), depending only on \(K,\varepsilon_0,\delta_0,C_R\), such that
\[
E(D_{nb}^4\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
\le C_D\frac{w_{nb}^4}{n_{nb}^2}=C_Dt_{nb}^2
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\tag{8}
\]

Let \(\sigma_n^2=E(V_n)\). Predictable-QV growth gives
\[
\sigma_n^2=E(V_n)\ge v_0s_n^2>0.
\tag{9}
\]
For every \(\epsilon>0\), conditional Markov's inequality, (8), (9), and block leverage imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\frac1{\sigma_n^2}\sum_bE\left[D_{nb}^2\mathbf1\{|D_{nb}|>\epsilon\sigma_n\}
\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\right]\\
&\qquad\le\frac1{\epsilon^2\sigma_n^4}\sum_bE(D_{nb}^4\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
\le\frac{C_D}{\epsilon^2v_0^2(s_n^2)^2}\sum_bt_{nb}^2\\
&\qquad\le\frac{C_D}{\epsilon^2v_0^2}
\frac{\max_bt_{nb}}{s_n^2}
\le\frac{C_D}{\epsilon^2v_0^2}\ell_n\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
Thus conditional Lindeberg is derived, not assumed. The separate stabilization assumption gives \(V_n/\sigma_n^2\to1\) in probability uniformly over the restricted class. The martingale and square-integrability properties follow from \(\mathrm{thm:exact\text{-}predictable\text{-}ot\text{-}envelope}\). Hence \(\mathrm{lem:martingale\text{-}clt\text{-}predictable\text{-}variation}\), applied using (9)--(10), yields
\[
\frac{\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n}{\sigma_n}
=\frac{\sum_bD_{nb}}{\sigma_n}\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1).
\tag{11}
\]
The equality follows by subtracting the finite-population target from \(\mathrm{def:aipw\text{-}estimator}\), then using consistency, \(p_{nba}=m_{nba}/n_{nb}\), and \(w_{nb}=n_{nb}/N_n\) block by block. Combining (11) with QV stabilization gives
\[
T_n:=\frac{\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n}{\sqrt{V_n}}
=\frac{(\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n)/\sigma_n}{\sqrt{V_n/\sigma_n^2}}
\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1).
\tag{12}
\]
The convergence in (11)--(12) is uniform. If it were not, one could choose a sequence of arrays for which the discrepancy stays bounded away from zero. The selected rows form a triangular array obeying the same common bounds; (8)--(10) and uniform QV stabilization then hold along that array, while the proved martingale CLT and the ratio identity in (12) force Gaussian convergence, a contradiction.

It remains to justify the conservative observable scale. The exact envelope gives \(V_n\le U_n\) pathwise. The established history-uniform plug-in theorem and \(r_n=q_n^{-\kappa}\to0\) imply, for every \(\eta>0\),
\[
\sup_{\mathbb D\in\mathfrak D}
\Pr_{\mathbb D}\left(\frac{|\widehat U_n-U_n|}{s_n^2}>\eta\right)
\le\frac{C_{\mathrm{ep}}r_n}{1\wedge\eta}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{13}
\]
Since \(V_n\ge v_0s_n^2\), (13) gives
\[
\frac{\widehat U_n}{V_n}
\ge\frac{U_n}{V_n}-\frac{|\widehat U_n-U_n|}{V_n}
\ge1-o_{\Pr}(1)
\tag{14}
\]
uniformly. Fix \(0<\zeta<1\), and write \(z=z_{1-\alpha/2}>0\). By \(\mathrm{def:wald\text{-}interval}\), (12), and (14),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr\{\tau_n\in\mathrm{CI}_{n,1-\alpha}\}
&=\Pr\left\{|T_n|\le z\sqrt{\widehat U_n/V_n}\right\}\\
&\ge\Pr\{|T_n|\le z\sqrt{1-\zeta}\}
-\Pr\{\widehat U_n/V_n<1-\zeta\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]
Taking the uniform lower limit in (15) gives at least
\(2\Phi(z\sqrt{1-\zeta})-1\). Letting \(\zeta\downarrow0\) and using continuity of \(\Phi\) yields \(2\Phi(z)-1=1-\alpha\). This proves uniform conservative coverage under the original sequential assignment law; terminal-history conditioning is not used.
