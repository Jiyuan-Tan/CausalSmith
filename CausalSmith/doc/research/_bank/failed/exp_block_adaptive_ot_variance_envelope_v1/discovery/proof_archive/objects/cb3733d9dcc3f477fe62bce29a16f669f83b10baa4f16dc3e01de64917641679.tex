Fix \(n\) and a block \(b\), and abbreviate \(N=n_{nb}\), \(m_a=m_{nba}\), \(p_a=m_a/N\), \(R_i(a)=R_{nbi}(a)\), and \(x_i(a)=R_i(a)-\bar R_{nba}\).  By \(\mathrm{ass:fixed\text{-}potential\text{-}outcomes}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:predictable\text{-}adjustment}\), all the \(x_i(a)\)'s are fixed conditional on \(\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\).  By \(\mathrm{ass:predictable\text{-}counts}\), so are the positive integers \(m_a\).  Finally, \(\mathrm{ass:complete\text{-}block\text{-}randomization}\) gives
\[
 E\{\mathbf 1(Z_{nbi}=a)\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\}=\frac{m_a}{N}=p_a.
\]
Consequently the arm sample mean is conditionally unbiased for its finite-population mean, and the definition in \(\mathrm{def:block\text{-}martingale}\) gives
\[
 E(D_{nb}\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
 =w_{nb}\sum_a c_a\left\{m_a^{-1}\sum_i p_aR_i(a)-\bar R_{nba}\right\}=0.
\]
Thus \(M_{nj}=\sum_{b\le j}D_{nb}\) is adapted, integrable, and has zero conditional increments; it is a martingale.

We next calculate its conditional variance.  Complete randomization gives, for distinct units \(i\ne k\),
\[
 E(I_i^aI_k^a\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})=\frac{m_a(m_a-1)}{N(N-1)},
 \qquad
 E(I_i^aI_k^{a'}\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})=\frac{m_am_{a'}}{N(N-1)}\quad(a\ne a'),
\]
where \(I_i^a=\mathbf 1(Z_{nbi}=a)\), while \(I_i^aI_i^{a'}=0\) for \(a\ne a'\).  Expanding the two sample means and using \(\sum_i x_i(a)=0\) therefore yields
\[
 \operatorname{Var}\!\left(m_a^{-1}\sum_iI_i^aR_i(a)\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\right)
 =\left(\frac1{m_a}-\frac1N\right)S_{nba}^2
\]
and, for \(a\ne a'\),
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}\!\left(m_a^{-1}\sum_iI_i^aR_i(a),m_{a'}^{-1}\sum_iI_i^{a'}R_i(a')\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\right)
 =-\frac{S_{nb,aa'}}N,
\]
where
\[
 S_{nb,aa'}=\frac1{N-1}\sum_{i\in\mathcal U_{nb}}x_i(a)x_i(a').
\]
It follows equation by equation that
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E(D_{nb}^2\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})/w_{nb}^2
 &=\sum_a c_a^2\frac{S_{nba}^2}{m_a}
   -\frac1N\left\{\sum_a c_a^2S_{nba}^2+2\sum_{a<a'}c_ac_{a'}S_{nb,aa'}\right\}\\
 &=\sum_a c_a^2\frac{S_{nba}^2}{m_a}
   -\frac1{N(N-1)}\sum_i\left\{\sum_ac_ax_i(a)\right\}^2\\
 &=\sum_a c_a^2\frac{S_{nba}^2}{m_a}
   -\frac1{N-1}\int\left(\sum_ac_ax_a\right)^2d\gamma_{nb}^{\mathrm{true}}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The last equality uses the definition
\(\gamma_{nb}^{\mathrm{true}}=N^{-1}\sum_i\delta_{(x_i(1),\ldots,x_i(K))}\), whose \(a\)-th marginal is exactly \(\mu_{nba}\); hence \(\gamma_{nb}^{\mathrm{true}}\in\Gamma_{nb}\).

At a fixed history node \(h\), every margin has finite support, so \(\Gamma_{nb}^h\) is a nonempty compact finite-dimensional transportation polytope.  The displayed quadratic cost is a linear continuous functional of its coupling variable.  It therefore attains its minimum \(J_{nb}^{h,-}\) and maximum \(J_{nb}^{h,+}\), and convexity shows that its image is the whole interval between them.  Since the cost enters the conditional variance with coefficient \(-w_{nb}^2/(n_{nb}-1)\), the variance range has lower endpoint obtained from \(J_{nb}^{h,+}\) and upper endpoint obtained from \(J_{nb}^{h,-}\).  Nonnegativity also follows directly from
\[
 \left(\sum_ac_ax_a\right)^2
 =\left(\sum_a\frac{c_ax_a}{\sqrt{p_{nba}}}\sqrt{p_{nba}}\right)^2
 \le\sum_a\frac{c_a^2x_a^2}{p_{nba}},
\]
because \(\sum_ap_{nba}=1\) and
\(\int x_a^2d\mu_{nba}=(n_{nb}-1)S_{nba}^2/n_{nb}\).

The product in \(\mathrm{def:history\text{-}tree\text{-}rectangular\text{-}relaxation}\) permits the coupling to be chosen independently at each node.  On any root-to-leaf path, summing the nodewise intervals gives precisely the interval whose endpoints are the corresponding sums, because a Minkowski sum of real intervals is an interval.  On the realized path the true nodewise coupling is feasible at every visited node, so summing the conditional variances gives \(V_n\in[L_n,U_n]\) pathwise.  Every summand is measurable before its block is randomized; no conditioning on \(\mathcal F_{n,B_n}\) has been used.  This proves exactness in \(\mathfrak R_n(\boldsymbol\mu_n)\), while \(\mathrm{def:completion\text{-}class}\) explains why it makes no assertion of simultaneous attainment by one \(\Xi_n\).

For the two-block kill test, the same conditional-centering calculation gives
\[
 E(D_{n1}D_{n2})=E\{D_{n1}E(D_{n2}\mid\mathcal F_{n,1})\}=0
\]
for every first-block assignment prefix, even when the second count vector is an arbitrary \(\mathcal F_{n,1}\)-measurable function of the first-block assignments and outcomes.  Hence no unidentified cross-block covariance survives.  Finally, when \(B_n=1\) and \(g_{nbi}(a)=0\), the residuals are the potential outcomes and direct substitution gives the stated static quadratic multi-marginal transport variance formula.
