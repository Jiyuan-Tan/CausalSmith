Put
\[
X_{nb}=\frac{D_{nb}}{\sigma_n},\qquad
v_{nb}=E(X_{nb}^2\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1}),\qquad
A_{nj}=\sum_{b=1}^{j}v_{nb}.
\tag{1}
\]
These quantities are well defined because \(\sigma_n^2=E(V_n)>0\).  The
martingale-difference property gives
\(E(X_{nb}\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})=0\), and the two hypotheses become
\[
A_{nB_n}=\frac{V_n}{\sigma_n^2}\longrightarrow1
\quad\text{in probability}
\tag{2}
\]
and, for every \(\delta>0\),
\[
\Lambda_n(\delta):=\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}
E\!\left[X_{nb}^2\mathbf 1\{|X_{nb}|>\delta\}
\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\right]
\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{in probability}.
\tag{3}
\]

We first stop the predictable variance.  Because \(v_{nb}\) is
\(\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\)-measurable, so is
\[
I_{nb}=\mathbf 1\{A_{nb}\le2\}.
\]
Define
\[
X'_{nb}=I_{nb}X_{nb},\qquad v'_{nb}=I_{nb}v_{nb},\qquad
A'_{nj}=\sum_{b=1}^{j}v'_{nb},\qquad
S'_{nj}=\sum_{b=1}^{j}X'_{nb}.
\tag{4}
\]
Predictability of \(I_{nb}\) shows that \((X'_{nb})\) is again a
martingale-difference array.  Monotonicity of \(A_{nj}\) implies
\[
0\le A'_{nB_n}\le2.
\tag{5}
\]
On \(\{A_{nB_n}\le2\}\), every \(I_{nb}\) equals one.  Hence (2) gives
\[
\Pr\!\left(S'_{nB_n}\ne\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}X_{nb}\right)
\le\Pr(A_{nB_n}>2)\longrightarrow0,
\qquad A'_{nB_n}\longrightarrow1
\quad\text{in probability}.
\tag{6}
\]
Moreover, if
\[
\Lambda'_n(\delta)=\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}
E\!\left[(X'_{nb})^2\mathbf 1\{|X'_{nb}|>\delta\}
\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\right],
\]
then \(0\le\Lambda'_n(\delta)\le\Lambda_n(\delta)\) and
\(\Lambda'_n(\delta)\le A'_{nB_n}\le2\).  Thus (3), boundedness, and
convergence in probability imply
\[
E\Lambda'_n(\delta)\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{for every fixed }\delta>0.
\tag{7}
\]

The largest stopped conditional variance is negligible.  Indeed,
\[
v'_{nb}\le\delta^2+
E\!\left[(X'_{nb})^2\mathbf 1\{|X'_{nb}|>\delta\}
\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\right],
\]
and therefore
\[
\max_{b\le B_n}v'_{nb}\le\delta^2+\Lambda'_n(\delta).
\tag{8}
\]
For each \(\eta>0\), choose \(\delta^2<\eta/2\) and apply (3) in (8).
This proves \(\max_bv'_{nb}\to0\) in probability.  The maximum is bounded
by two by (5), so it also converges to zero in mean.  Consequently,
\[
E\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}(v'_{nb})^2
\le E\!\left[A'_{nB_n}\max_{b\le B_n}v'_{nb}\right]
\le2E\max_{b\le B_n}v'_{nb}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{9}
\]

We now calculate the characteristic function.  Fix \(t\in\mathbb R\), and
let
\[
r_{nb}=E(e^{itX'_{nb}}\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})-1
+\frac{t^2}{2}v'_{nb}.
\tag{10}
\]
The martingale-difference property removes the linear Taylor term.  For
\(|x|\le\delta\), Taylor's formula with integral remainder gives
\[
|e^{itx}-1-itx+t^2x^2/2|\le |t|^3\delta x^2/6,
\]
whereas for \(|x|>\delta\) the same left side is at most
\[
\left(2\delta^{-2}+|t|\delta^{-1}+t^2/2\right)x^2.
\]
Taking conditional expectations and summing yields
\[
\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}|r_{nb}|
\le \frac{|t|^3\delta}{6}A'_{nB_n}
+\left(2\delta^{-2}+|t|\delta^{-1}+t^2/2\right)
\Lambda'_n(\delta).
\tag{11}
\]
Taking expectations, first letting \(n\to\infty\) by (5) and (7), and then
letting \(\delta\downarrow0\), proves
\[
E\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}|r_{nb}|\longrightarrow0.
\tag{12}
\]

Set
\[
H_{nj}=\exp\!\left(itS'_{nj}+\frac{t^2}{2}A'_{nj}\right),
\qquad H_{n0}=1.
\]
Since \(v'_{nb}\) is predictable, (10) gives
\[
E(H_{nb}\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
=H_{n,b-1}e^{t^2v'_{nb}/2}
\left(1-\frac{t^2}{2}v'_{nb}+r_{nb}\right).
\tag{13}
\]
For \(0\le v\le2\), Taylor's formula on this compact interval supplies a
finite constant \(C_t\) such that
\[
\left|e^{t^2v/2}(1-t^2v/2)-1\right|\le C_tv^2.
\tag{14}
\]
By (5), \(|H_{n,b-1}|\le e^{t^2}\) and
\(e^{t^2v'_{nb}/2}\le e^{t^2}\).  Telescoping the expectation differences
in (13), then applying (9), (12), and (14), gives
\[
|EH_{nB_n}-1|
\le e^{t^2}C_tE\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}(v'_{nb})^2
+e^{2t^2}E\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}|r_{nb}|\longrightarrow0.
\tag{15}
\]
By (5)--(6), the bounded variables \(e^{t^2A'_{nB_n}/2}\) converge in
probability and in mean to \(e^{t^2/2}\).  Thus (15) implies
\[
\left|e^{t^2/2}E(e^{itS'_{nB_n}})-1\right|
\le E\left|e^{t^2/2}-e^{t^2A'_{nB_n}/2}\right|
+|EH_{nB_n}-1|\longrightarrow0.
\tag{16}
\]
Therefore \(E(e^{itS'_{nB_n}})\to e^{-t^2/2}\).  Equation (6) and
boundedness of complex exponentials also give
\[
\left|E\exp\!\left(it\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}X_{nb}\right)
-E(e^{itS'_{nB_n}})\right|\longrightarrow0.
\tag{17}
\]
The pointwise limit in (16)--(17) is the characteristic function of
\(\mathcal N(0,1)\).  The cited continuity criterion
\(\mathrm{lem:levy\text{-}continuity\text{-}criterion}\) therefore gives
\[
\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}X_{nb}\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1).
\]
Substituting \(X_{nb}=D_{nb}/\sigma_n\) proves the lemma.  In particular,
(3) is exactly the hypothesis with the pre-normalization threshold
\(\delta\sigma_n\); no inverse-threshold assertion is used.
