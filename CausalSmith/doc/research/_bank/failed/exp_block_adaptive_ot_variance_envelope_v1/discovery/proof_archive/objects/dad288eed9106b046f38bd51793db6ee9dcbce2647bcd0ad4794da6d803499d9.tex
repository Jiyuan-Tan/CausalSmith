Put \(r=4+\delta_0\) and condition on an arbitrary realized \(\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\)-history.  All quantities below are then finite-population quantities.  For an arm \(a\), write \(X_i=R_{nbi}(a)-\bar R_{nba}\), \(N=n_{nb}\), and \(m=m_{nba}\).  By Jensen's inequality and \(\mathrm{ass:residual\text{-}moments}\),
\[
 \frac1N\sum_i|X_i|^r
 \le 2^{r-1}\left\{\frac1N\sum_i|R_{nbi}(a)|^r+|\bar R_{nba}|^r\right\}
 \le 2^r C_R. \tag{1}
\]
The assigned labels form a simple random sample without replacement of size \(m\) by \(\mathrm{ass:complete\text{-}block\text{-}randomization}\).

We first derive, rather than assume, the finite-population transport bound needed below.  Let \(\nu=m^{-1}\sum_{i:Z_{nbi}=a}\delta_{X_i}\) and \(\mu=N^{-1}\sum_i\delta_{X_i}\).  Clip both measures to \([-T,T]\), partition that interval into \(H\) cells of length at most \(2T/H\), and project each cell onto one representative.  If \(P_h\) and \(\widehat P_h\) are the population and sample masses of cell \(h\), the exact inclusion probabilities of simple random sampling give
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(\widehat P_h\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
 =\frac{N-m}{m(N-1)}P_h(1-P_h)\le\frac1{2m},
\]
where \(N\ge2\).  Hence
\[
 E\!\left(\sum_{h=1}^H|\widehat P_h-P_h|\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\right)\le \frac{H}{\sqrt{2m}}. \tag{2}
\]
Couple the common mass within each cell and move unmatched clipped mass by at most \(2T\).  Equations (1)--(2), Markov's inequality applied to \(|X|^r\), and the triangle inequality for quadratic transport give
\[
 E\{W_2^2(\nu,\mu)\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\}
 \le C\left(\frac{T^2}{H^2}+\frac{T^2H}{\sqrt m}+T^{-(r-2)}\right). \tag{3}
\]
Indeed, each clipping leg costs at most
\(N^{-1}\sum_iX_i^2\mathbf 1\{|X_i|>T\}\le 2^rC_RT^{-(r-2)}\), and the expected sample clipping cost equals the population clipping cost by the first-order inclusion probability.

Choose \(H=\lceil m^{1/6}\rceil\) and \(T=m^{1/(3r)}\).  Then every term in (3) is bounded by a constant times
\[
 m^{-\beta},\qquad \beta=\frac{r-2}{3r}. \tag{4}
\]
The actual plug-in margin translates \(\nu\) by minus its sample mean.  Since the population mean of the \(X_i\)'s is zero and simple-random-sampling covariance gives
\[
 E\left[\left\{m^{-1}\sum_{i:Z_{nbi}=a}X_i\right\}^2\middle|\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\right]
 =\left(\frac1m-\frac1N\right)S_{nba}^2\le C/m,
\]
another triangle inequality preserves (4):
\[
 E\{W_2^2(\widehat\mu_{nba},\mu_{nba})\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\}\le C m_{nba}^{-\beta}. \tag{5}
\]

The sample-variance error is controlled directly.  Apply the same inclusion-probability covariance identity to the finite list \(X_i^2\); (1) supplies its second moment.  After expanding the sample variance into the sampled second moment minus the squared sampled mean, and accounting for the factors \(m/(m-1)\) and \(N/(N-1)\), one obtains, for \(m\ge2\),
\[
 E\{|\widehat S_{nba}^2-S_{nba}^2|\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\}\le C m_{nba}^{-1/2}. \tag{6}
\]
The constant in (1)--(6) depends only on \(\delta_0\) and \(C_R\).

We next derive continuity of both multi-marginal endpoints.  Couple each \(\mu_{nba}\) optimally in quadratic transport to \(\widehat\mu_{nba}\), and glue those armwise kernels to an arbitrary \(\gamma\in\Gamma_{nb}\).  If \(X\) has law \(\gamma\) and the resulting \(Y\) has the coupled empirical margins, then \(\mathrm{ass:contrast\text{-}normalization}\), Cauchy--Schwarz, and (1) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |E(c^\top X)^2-E(c^\top Y)^2|
 &\le\{E(c^\top(X-Y))^2\}^{1/2}\{E(c^\top(X+Y))^2\}^{1/2}\\
 &\le\left\{\sum_aW_2^2(\mu_{nba},\widehat\mu_{nba})\right\}^{1/2}
       \left\{2\sum_a(E X_a^2+E Y_a^2)\right\}^{1/2}.
 \end{aligned}\tag{7}
\]
Apply (7) first to an optimizer for the population margins and then in the reverse direction to an optimizer for the empirical margins.  Taking conditional expectations and using (5), the bounded second moments from (1), and fixed \(K\), yields for both signs
\[
 E\{|\widehat J_{nb}^{\pm}-J_{nb}^{\pm}|\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\}
 \le C\left(\min_a m_{nba}\right)^{-\beta/2}. \tag{8}
\]
This argument applies to minima and maxima and does not require uniqueness of a transport plan.

Let \(t_{nb}=w_{nb}^2/n_{nb}\), so \(\sum_bt_{nb}=s_n^2\).  Executed overlap gives \(m_{nba}^{-1}\le(\varepsilon_0n_{nb})^{-1}\), while \((n_{nb}-1)^{-1}\le2n_{nb}^{-1}\).  Combining (6)--(8), summing the absolute block errors, and using the tower property along the original adaptive filtration gives, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_{\mathbb D}\frac{|\widehat L_n-L_n|+|\widehat U_n-U_n|}{s_n^2}
 &\le \frac{C}{s_n^2}\sum_bt_{nb}
 \left(q_n^{-1/2}+q_n^{-\beta/2}\right)\\
 &\le Cq_n^{-\kappa},\qquad
 \kappa=\frac{r-2}{6r}.
 \end{aligned}\tag{9}
\]
Nothing in this tower calculation conditions on the terminal history: at block \(b\), the counts and fitted values are merely held fixed at their pre-block values, the conditional bound is uniform in that history, and then that history is integrated out.

Set \(e=\delta_0/[8(4+\delta_0)]\).  Since
\[
 \kappa=\frac{2+\delta_0}{6(4+\delta_0)}>\frac{\delta_0}{8(4+\delta_0)}=e,
\]
and \(\log(2B_nK)>1\), (9), together with the outer truncation by one for the finitely many cases with \(q_n<2\), implies
\[
 \sup_{\mathbb D\in\mathfrak D}E_{\mathbb D}\left[1\wedge
 \frac{|\widehat L_n-L_n|+|\widehat U_n-U_n|}{s_n^2}\right]
 \le C_{\mathrm{ep}}\left\{\frac{\log(2B_nK)}{q_n}\right\}^{e}
 \le C_{\mathrm{ep}}r_n.
\]
All constants are common over \(\mathfrak D\) and depend only on \(K,\varepsilon_0,\delta_0,C_R\).  If \(X_n\) denotes the nonnegative normalized endpoint error, then
\(\mathbf 1\{X_n>\eta\}\le(1\wedge X_n)/(1\wedge\eta)\), which proves the displayed probability bound.  Finally the Hausdorff distance between two real intervals is
\(\max\{|\widehat L_n-L_n|,|\widehat U_n-U_n|\}\), bounded by the displayed sum, so it obeys the same estimate.  Because \(q_n/\log(2B_nK)\to\infty\) and \(\ell_n\to0\), \(r_n\to0\).
