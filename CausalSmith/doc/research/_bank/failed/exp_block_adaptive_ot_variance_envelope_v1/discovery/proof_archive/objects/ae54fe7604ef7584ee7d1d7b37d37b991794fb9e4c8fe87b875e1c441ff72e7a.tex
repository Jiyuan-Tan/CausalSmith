Put \(X_{nb}=D_{nb}/\sigma_n\), and define the predictable quantities
\[
v_{nb}=E(X_{nb}^2\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1}),\qquad
A_{nj}=\sum_{b=1}^jv_{nb},
\]
\[
\lambda_{nb}(e)=E\{X_{nb}^2\mathbf 1(|X_{nb}|>e)\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\},
\qquad \Lambda_{nj}(e)=\sum_{b=1}^j\lambda_{nb}(e).
\tag{1}
\]
Then \(A_{nB_n}=V_n/\sigma_n^2\to1\) in probability, and the displayed Lindeberg hypothesis says that \(\Lambda_{nB_n}(e)\to0\) in probability for each fixed \(e>0\).

We first turn these two convergences into bounded predictable budgets. There are deterministic sequences \(e_n\downarrow0\) and \(d_n\downarrow0\) such that
\[
\Pr\{|A_{nB_n}-1|>d_n\}+\Pr\{\Lambda_{nB_n}(e_n)>d_n\}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{2}
\]
Indeed, for each integer \(k\ge1\), convergence at the fixed threshold \(1/k\) supplies \(N_k\) such that, for \(n\ge N_k\), both probabilities with \(d_n=e_n=1/k\) are at most \(1/k\). Taking \(N_k\) increasing and using these values on \(N_k\le n<N_{k+1}\) proves (2).

For \(b\le B_n\), set
\[
I_{nb}=\mathbf 1\{A_{nb}\le1+d_n,\ \Lambda_{nb}(e_n)\le d_n\}.
\tag{3}
\]
Because \(v_{nb}\) and \(\lambda_{nb}(e_n)\) are \(\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\)-measurable, \(I_{nb}\) is predictable. Both cumulative processes in (1) are nondecreasing, so the indices for which \(I_{nb}=1\) form an initial segment and
\[
\sum_b I_{nb}v_{nb}\le1+d_n,qquad
\sum_b I_{nb}\lambda_{nb}(e_n)\le d_n.
\tag{4}
\]
Define the stopped, truncated, conditionally recentered differences
\[
Y_{nb}=I_{nb}\left[X_{nb}\mathbf 1\{|X_{nb}|\le e_n\}
-E\{X_{nb}\mathbf 1(|X_{nb}|\le e_n)\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\}\right]
\tag{5}
\]
and \(u_{nb}=E(Y_{nb}^2\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})\), \(B_n^Y=\sum_bu_{nb}\). The variables \(Y_{nb}\) are martingale differences and satisfy \(|Y_{nb}|\le2e_n\). Moreover, (4) gives
\[
0\le B_n^Y\lesum_bI_{nb}v_{nb}\le1+d_n.
\tag{6}
\]

Let
\[
G_n=\{|A_{nB_n}-1|\le d_n,\ \Lambda_{nB_n}(e_n)\le d_n\}.
\]
On \(G_n\), every \(I_{nb}\) equals one. Since \(E(X_{nb}\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})=0\), if
\(r_{nb}=E\{X_{nb}\mathbf 1(|X_{nb}|\le e_n)\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\}\), then
\[
r_{nb}=-E\{X_{nb}\mathbf 1(|X_{nb}|>e_n)\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\},
\qquad r_{nb}^2\le\lambda_{nb}(e_n).
\tag{7}
\]
Consequently, on \(G_n\),
\[
0\le A_{nB_n}-B_n^Y
=\sum_b\{\lambda_{nb}(e_n)+r_{nb}^2\}
\le2d_n,
\tag{8}
\]
and hence \(|B_n^Y-1|\le3d_n\). Equations (2), (6), and (8) imply
\[
B_n^Y\to1\quad\hbox{in probability},qquad E|B_n^Y-1|\longrightarrow0.
\tag{9}
\]
The second conclusion follows because \(0\le B_n^Y\le1+d_n\), so the sequence is uniformly bounded.

The truncation does not alter the normalized sum asymptotically. Put
\[
Q_{nb}=I_{nb}\left[X_{nb}\mathbf 1\{|X_{nb}|>e_n\}
-E\{X_{nb}\mathbf 1(|X_{nb}|>e_n)\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\}\right].
\]
The \(Q_{nb}\)'s are martingale differences, and by (4), orthogonality of martingale differences, and conditional variance bounded by conditional second moment,
\[
E\left(\sum_bQ_{nb}\right)^2
=E\sum_bE(Q_{nb}^2\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
\le E\sum_bI_{nb}\lambda_{nb}(e_n)\le d_n.
\tag{10}
\]
On \(G_n\), \(\sum_b(X_{nb}-Y_{nb})=\sum_bQ_{nb}\). Thus, for every \(\eta>0\), (2), (10), and Chebyshev's inequality yield
\[
\Pr\left\{\left|\sum_bX_{nb}-\sum_bY_{nb}\right|>\eta\right\}
\le\Pr(G_n^c)+d_n\eta^{-2}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{11}
\]

It remains to derive the Gaussian limit for the bounded array in (5). We give the characteristic-function calculation. Write
\(S_{nj}^Y=\sum_{b=1}^jY_{nb}\) and \(B_{nj}^Y=\sum_{b=1}^ju_{nb}\). For fixed \(t\in\mathbb R\), define
\[
H_{nj}=\exp\{itS_{nj}^Y+t^2B_{nj}^Y/2\}.
\tag{12}
\]
Taylor's formula with remainder gives, conditionally on \(\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\),
\[
E(e^{itY_{nb}}\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
=1-\frac{t^2u_{nb}}2+R_{nb},qquad
|R_{nb}|\le\frac{|t|^3}{6}E(|Y_{nb}|^3\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
\le\frac{|t|^3}{3}e_nu_{nb}.
\tag{13}
\]
Because \(|Y_{nb}|\le2e_n\), one also has \(u_{nb}\le4e_n^2\). Expanding \(e^{t^2u_{nb}/2}\) to first order and using (13), there is a finite constant \(C_t\), independent of \(n,b\), such that
\[
\left|e^{t^2u_{nb}/2}E(e^{itY_{nb}}\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})-1\right|
\le C_t(e_nu_{nb}+u_{nb}^2).
\tag{14}
\]
By (6), \(|H_{n,b-1}|\le\exp\{t^2(1+d_n)/2\}\). Taking conditional expectations in (12), telescoping expectations, and applying (6), (14), and
\(\sum_bu_{nb}^2\le4e_n^2\sum_bu_{nb}\), we obtain
\[
|E H_{nB_n}-1|
\le C_t'(e_n+e_n^2)(1+d_n)\longrightarrow0.
\tag{15}
\]
On the other hand, (6) and (9) imply
\[
E\left|e^{-t^2B_n^Y/2}-e^{-t^2/2}\right|\longrightarrow0.
\tag{16}
\]
Since \(e^{itS_{nB_n}^Y}=H_{nB_n}e^{-t^2B_n^Y/2}\), the uniform modulus bound following (12), together with (15)--(16), gives
\[
E e^{itS_{nB_n}^Y}\longrightarrow e^{-t^2/2}
\quad\text{for every }t\in\mathbb R.
\tag{17}
\]
The right side is the characteristic function of \(\mathcal N(0,1)\). The cited \(\text{lem:levy-continuity-criterion}\) applied to (17) yields
\(\sum_bY_{nb}\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1)\). Finally, (11) and the elementary continuity argument
\[
\Pr\{S_n^Y\le x-\eta\}-\Pr\{|S_n^X-S_n^Y|>\eta\}
\le\Pr\{S_n^X\le x\}
\le\Pr\{S_n^Y\le x+\eta\}+\Pr\{|S_n^X-S_n^Y|>\eta\}
\]
at continuity points of the Gaussian distribution show that
\[
\sum_bX_{nb}=\sigma_n^{-1}\sum_bD_{nb}\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1).
\]
This proof uses the Lindeberg threshold \(\epsilon\sigma_n\) exactly as displayed in the statement.
