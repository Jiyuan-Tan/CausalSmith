Fix \(n\) and a block \(b\), and abbreviate \(N=n_{nb}\), \(m_a=m_{nba}\), \(p_a=m_a/N\), \(R_i(a)=R_{nbi}(a)\), and
\[
x_i(a)=R_i(a)-\bar R_{nba}.
\]
By \(\mathrm{ass:fixed\text{-}potential\text{-}outcomes}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:predictable\text{-}adjustment}\), the lists \((x_i(a))_{i\in\mathcal U_{nb}}\) are fixed conditional on \(\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\). By \(\mathrm{ass:predictable\text{-}counts}\), the positive integers \(m_a\) are fixed under the same conditioning. Finally, \(\mathrm{ass:complete\text{-}block\text{-}randomization}\) gives, with \(I_i^a=\mathbf 1\{Z_{nbi}=a\}\),
\[
E(I_i^a\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})=\frac{m_a}{N}=p_a.
\tag{1}
\]
Consequently each arm sample mean is conditionally unbiased for its finite-population mean. The definition in \(\mathrm{def:block\text{-}martingale}\) and (1) therefore give
\[
E(D_{nb}\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
=w_{nb}\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}c_a
\left\{\frac1{m_a}\sum_{i\in\mathcal U_{nb}}p_aR_i(a)-\bar R_{nba}\right\}=0.
\tag{2}
\]
The increment \(D_{nb}\) is measurable after block \(b\) has been randomized, and the residual-moment member property of \(\mathfrak D\) makes it square integrable. Thus \(M_{nj}=\sum_{b\le j}D_{nb}\) is adapted, integrable, and has zero conditional increments by (2); hence \((M_{nj},\mathcal F_{n,j})_{j\le B_n}\) is a martingale.

We next calculate the conditional variance. Complete randomization gives, for distinct units \(i\ne k\),
\[
E(I_i^aI_k^a\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
=\frac{m_a(m_a-1)}{N(N-1)},
\qquad
E(I_i^aI_k^{a'}\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
=\frac{m_am_{a'}}{N(N-1)}\quad(a\ne a'),
\tag{3}
\]
whereas \(I_i^aI_i^{a'}=0\) when \(a\ne a'\). Since \(\sum_i x_i(a)=0\), expansion using (1)--(3) yields
\[
\operatorname{Var}\!\left(\frac1{m_a}\sum_i I_i^aR_i(a)\middle|\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\right)
=\left(\frac1{m_a}-\frac1N\right)S_{nba}^2,
\tag{4}
\]
and, for \(a\ne a'\),
\[
\operatorname{Cov}\!\left(\frac1{m_a}\sum_iI_i^aR_i(a),
\frac1{m_{a'}}\sum_iI_i^{a'}R_i(a')\middle|\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\right)
=-\frac{S_{nb,aa'}}N,
\tag{5}
\]
where
\[
S_{nb,aa'}=\frac1{N-1}\sum_{i\in\mathcal U_{nb}}x_i(a)x_i(a').
\]
Indeed, (4) is the usual expansion into diagonal and distinct-label terms using the first formula in (3), and (5) follows from the second formula in (3) together with
\(\sum_{i\ne k}x_i(a)x_k(a')=-\sum_i x_i(a)x_i(a')\).

Using (4)--(5) and then collecting diagonal and off-diagonal terms gives, equation by equation,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{E(D_{nb}^2\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})}{w_{nb}^2}
&=\sum_a\frac{c_a^2S_{nba}^2}{m_a}
-\frac1N\left\{\sum_a c_a^2S_{nba}^2
+2\sum_{a<a'}c_ac_{a'}S_{nb,aa'}\right\}\\
&=\sum_a\frac{c_a^2S_{nba}^2}{m_a}
-\frac1{N(N-1)}\sum_i\left\{\sum_a c_ax_i(a)\right\}^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
Define the common-label joint centered residual law
\[
\gamma_{nb}^{\mathrm{true}}
=\frac1N\sum_{i\in\mathcal U_{nb}}
\delta_{(x_i(1),\ldots,x_i(K))}.
\]
Its \(a\)-th marginal is exactly \(\mu_{nba}\), so \(\gamma_{nb}^{\mathrm{true}}\in\Gamma_{nb}\). Substitution in (6) proves
\[
E(D_{nb}^2\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
=w_{nb}^2\left\{\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\frac{c_a^2S_{nba}^2}{m_{nba}}
-\frac1{n_{nb}-1}\int\left(\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}c_ax_a\right)^2
d\gamma_{nb}^{\mathrm{true}}\right\}.
\tag{7}
\]

Fix now a history node \(h\in\mathcal H_{n,b-1}\). Each margin \(\mu_{nba}^h\) has finite support. Hence \(\Gamma_{nb}^h\) is a nonempty compact finite-dimensional transportation polytope: nonemptiness follows from the product coupling, closedness from the finitely many marginal equalities, and boundedness from nonnegativity and total mass one. The quadratic cost is a linear continuous functional of the coupling variable. It therefore attains its minimum \(J_{nb}^{h,-}\) and maximum \(J_{nb}^{h,+}\); because the polytope is convex, its image is the whole interval \([J_{nb}^{h,-},J_{nb}^{h,+}]\). The cost enters (7) with coefficient \(-w_{nb}^2/(n_{nb}-1)\). Thus the lower conditional-variance endpoint is obtained from \(J_{nb}^{h,+}\), and the upper endpoint is obtained from \(J_{nb}^{h,-}\), with both attained in the nodewise convexified coupling set.

These endpoints are nonnegative. Indeed, for any \(\gamma\in\Gamma_{nb}^h\), Cauchy--Schwarz, \(p_{nba}^h=m_{nba}^h/n_{nb}\), and \(\sum_ap_{nba}^h=1\) give
\[
\left(\sum_ac_ax_a\right)^2
=\left(\sum_a\frac{c_ax_a}{\sqrt{p_{nba}^h}}\sqrt{p_{nba}^h}\right)^2
\le\sum_a\frac{c_a^2x_a^2}{p_{nba}^h}.
\]
Since
\(\int x_a^2d\mu_{nba}^h=(n_{nb}-1)(S_{nba}^h)^2/n_{nb}\), integration implies
\[
\frac1{n_{nb}-1}\int\left(\sum_ac_ax_a\right)^2d\gamma
\le\sum_a\frac{c_a^2(S_{nba}^h)^2}{m_{nba}^h}.
\tag{8}
\]

By \(\mathrm{def:history\text{-}tree\text{-}rectangular\text{-}relaxation}\), a coupling may be selected independently at every history node. Along any fixed root-to-leaf path, the Minkowski sum of the nodewise real intervals is the interval whose endpoints are the sums of the respective nodewise endpoints. On the realized path, the common-label coupling is feasible at every visited node by (7). Summing (7) over visited blocks and using \(\mathrm{def:qv\text{-}envelope}\) therefore gives the predictable pathwise containment
\[
V_n=\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}E(D_{nb}^2\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})\in[L_n,U_n].
\tag{9}
\]
Every summand and endpoint in (9) is measurable before its own block is randomized. The argument never conditions on \(\mathcal F_{n,B_n}\). The Cartesian-product construction proves exactness in \(\mathfrak R_n(\boldsymbol\mu_n)\), while \(\mathrm{def:completion\text{-}class}\) shows why this says nothing about whether a single globally consistent \(\Xi_n\in\mathcal C_n(\boldsymbol\mu_n)\) realizes independently selected optimizers at all nodes.

The requested two-block kill test is contained in (2). Even if the second-block count vector is an arbitrary \(\mathcal F_{n,1}\)-measurable function of first-block assignments and outcomes,
\[
E(D_{n1}D_{n2})
=E\{D_{n1}E(D_{n2}\mid\mathcal F_{n,1})\}=0.
\tag{10}
\]
Here is an exact enumeration in the smallest convenient adaptive-quota example. Take two arms, \(c=(1,-1)/\sqrt2\), and zero adjustment. In block one use the common two-label residual list \((-1,1)\) and quota \((1,1)\). There are two assignments: according as arm one receives the label \(1\) or \(-1\),
\[
D_{n1}=\sqrt2w_{n1}\quad\text{or}\quad D_{n1}=-\sqrt2w_{n1}.
\]
After the positive value choose the predictable block-two quota \((1,2)\); after the negative value choose \((2,1)\). Give both arms in block two the common residual list \((-1,0,1)\). Put \(d=3w_{n2}/(2\sqrt2)\). Under quota \((1,2)\), the three equiprobable arm-one singleton choices give
\[
D_{n2}\in\{-d,0,d\};
\]
under quota \((2,1)\), the three equiprobable omitted-singleton choices give
\[
D_{n2}\in\{d,0,-d\}.
\]
Thus all six assignment sequences have been enumerated, each history has conditional block-two mean zero, and their cross-product average is
\[
\frac12\left[\sqrt2w_{n1}\frac{-d+0+d}{3}
-\sqrt2w_{n1}\frac{d+0-d}{3}\right]=0.
\tag{11}
\]
Hence no unidentified cross-block covariance survives predictable conditioning. Finally, when \(B_n=1\) and \(g_{nbi}(a)=0\), the residuals equal the potential outcomes, and direct substitution into (7) is exactly the stated static quadratic multi-marginal-transport variance formula.
