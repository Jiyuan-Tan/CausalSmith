Fix \(\mathbb D\in\mathfrak D\), and take probability and expectation under its original sequential randomization law. By \(\mathrm{def:aipw\text{-}estimator}\), consistency, \(p_{nba}=m_{nba}/n_{nb}\), and the fixed causal estimand, direct subtraction gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n
&=\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}w_{nb}\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}c_a
\left\{\frac1{m_{nba}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal U_{nb}}
\mathbf1\{Z_{nbi}=a\}R_{nbi}(a)-\bar R_{nba}\right\}\\
&=\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}D_{nb}=M_{nB_n}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
The martingale conclusion of \(\mathrm{thm:exact\text{-}predictable\text{-}ot\text{-}envelope}\) shows that \((D_{nb})_{b\le B_n}\) is a square-integrable martingale-difference array. In particular, cross-block covariances vanish by iterated expectation without conditioning on the terminal adaptive history.

We derive the conditional Lindeberg condition from the primitive residual-moment and block-leverage assumptions. Conditional on \(\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\), put
\[
x_i(a)=R_{nbi}(a)-\bar R_{nba},
\qquad
X_a=\frac1{m_{nba}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal U_{nb}}
\mathbf1\{Z_{nbi}=a\}x_i(a).
\]
Then \(D_{nb}=w_{nb}\sum_ac_aX_a\). The power-mean inequality, Jensen's inequality, and \(\mathrm{ass:residual\text{-}moments}\) imply
\[
\frac1{n_{nb}}\sum_i|x_i(a)|^4
\le16\frac1{n_{nb}}\sum_i|R_{nbi}(a)|^4
\le16\left\{\frac1{n_{nb}}\sum_i|R_{nbi}(a)|^{4+\delta_0}\right\}^{4/(4+\delta_0)}
\le16C_R^{4/(4+\delta_0)}.
\tag{2}
\]

We now derive the needed fourth-moment sampling bound. Let \(x_1,\ldots,x_N\) be any centered list, let \(S\) be a uniformly chosen subset of size \(m\), define \(A_r=\sum_{i=1}^Nx_i^r\), and let \(\pi_r=(m)_r/(N)_r\), with \(\pi_r=0\) if \(r>m\). Expansion according to the index-multiplicity patterns \(4\), \(3+1\), \(2+2\), \(2+1+1\), and \(1+1+1+1\), using \(\sum_ix_i=0\), gives
\[
E\left(\sum_{i\in S}x_i\right)^4
=\{\pi_1-7\pi_2+12\pi_3-6\pi_4\}A_4
+3\{\pi_2-2\pi_3+\pi_4\}A_2^2.
\tag{3}
\]
For completeness, the five contributions are respectively
\[
\pi_1A_4,\quad -4\pi_2A_4,\quad
3\pi_2(A_2^2-A_4),\quad
6\pi_3(2A_4-A_2^2),\quad
\pi_4(3A_2^2-6A_4).
\]
Since \(0\le\pi_r\le1\), \(A_2^2\le NA_4\), and \(m\ge\varepsilon_0N\), (3) implies
\[
E\left(\frac1m\sum_{i\in S}x_i\right)^4
\le\left(\frac{26}{\varepsilon_0}+\frac{12}{\varepsilon_0^2}\right)
\frac1{m^2}\frac{A_4}{N}.
\tag{4}
\]
Under complete-block randomization, the marginal arm-\(a\) sample is such a uniform subset; overlap gives \(m_{nba}\ge\varepsilon_0n_{nb}\). Combining (2)--(4), \(\sum_ac_a^2=1\), and
\[
\left(\sum_ac_aX_a\right)^4
\le\left(\sum_aX_a^2\right)^2
\le K\sum_aX_a^4
\]
produces a constant \(C_0<\infty\), depending only on \(K,\varepsilon_0,\delta_0,C_R\), such that
\[
E(D_{nb}^4\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
\le C_0\left(\frac{w_{nb}^2}{n_{nb}}\right)^2
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\tag{5}
\]

Define the deterministic, array-specific scale
\[
\sigma_{n,\mathbb D}^2=E_{\mathbb D}V_n.
\]
Martingale orthogonality and (1) give
\(\sigma_{n,\mathbb D}^2=\operatorname{Var}_{\mathbb D}(M_{nB_n})\), while \(\mathrm{ass:qv\text{-}growth}\) gives
\[
\sigma_{n,\mathbb D}^2\ge v_0s_n^2>0.
\tag{6}
\]
Writing \(t_{nb}=w_{nb}^2/n_{nb}\), equations (5)--(6), \(\sum_bt_{nb}=s_n^2\), and \(\mathrm{ass:block\text{-}leverage}\) yield
\[
\sup_{\mathbb D\in\mathfrak D}
\frac{\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}E_{\mathbb D}D_{nb}^4}{\sigma_{n,\mathbb D}^4}
\le\frac{C_0}{v_0^2}\frac{\sum_bt_{nb}^2}{s_n^4}
\le\frac{C_0}{v_0^2}\ell_n\longrightarrow0.
\tag{7}
\]
For \(\epsilon>0\), set
\[
\Lambda_{n,\mathbb D}(\epsilon)
=\frac1{\sigma_{n,\mathbb D}^2}\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}
E_{\mathbb D}\left[D_{nb}^2\mathbf1\{|D_{nb}|>\epsilon\sigma_{n,\mathbb D}\}
\middle|\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\right].
\]
The inequality \(u^2\mathbf1\{|u|>x\}\le u^4/x^2\), (7), and Markov's inequality imply
\[
\sup_{\mathbb D\in\mathfrak D}E_{\mathbb D}\Lambda_{n,\mathbb D}(\epsilon)
\le\frac1{\epsilon^2}
\sup_{\mathbb D\in\mathfrak D}
\frac{\sum_bE_{\mathbb D}D_{nb}^4}{\sigma_{n,\mathbb D}^4}
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{8}
\]
Thus the conditional Lindeberg condition holds uniformly in probability.

The established martingale central limit theorem
\(\mathrm{lem:martingale\text{-}clt\text{-}predictable\text{-}variation}\) applies because (8) supplies its Lindeberg premise and \(\mathrm{ass:qv\text{-}stabilization}\) supplies
\[
\frac{V_n}{\sigma_{n,\mathbb D}^2}
=\frac{V_n}{E_{\mathbb D}V_n}\longrightarrow1
\quad\text{in probability, uniformly over }\mathbb D\in\mathfrak D.
\tag{9}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\frac{\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n}{\sigma_{n,\mathbb D}}
\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1)
\quad\text{uniformly over }\mathfrak D.
\tag{10}
\]
To justify the asserted uniformity, suppose it failed. Then some subsequence and choices \(\mathbb D_n\in\mathfrak D\) would retain a fixed distributional discrepancy. Selecting row \(n\) from \(\mathbb D_n\) forms one diagonal martingale triangular array. The common bounds (8)--(9) hold for this diagonal array, so the cited martingale theorem forces Gaussian convergence, a contradiction.

On the event \(|V_n/\sigma_{n,\mathbb D}^2-1|\le\zeta\), the statistic normalized by \(\sqrt{V_n}\) is sandwiched between deterministic rescalings of the statistic in (10) by \(\sqrt{1-\zeta}\) and \(\sqrt{1+\zeta}\). Equations (9)--(10), continuity of the Gaussian distribution function, and then \(\zeta\downarrow0\) prove
\[
T_{n,\mathbb D}:=\frac{\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n}{\sqrt{V_n}}
\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1)
\quad\text{uniformly over }\mathfrak D.
\tag{11}
\]

It remains to prove conservative studentization. The exact envelope gives \(V_n\le U_n\) pathwise. Since \(r_n=q_n^{-\kappa}\to0\), \(\mathrm{thm:history\text{-}uniform\text{-}plugin}\) and \(V_n\ge v_0s_n^2\) imply, for every fixed \(\eta\in(0,1)\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sup_{\mathbb D\in\mathfrak D}
\Pr_{\mathbb D}\{|\widehat U_n-U_n|>\eta V_n\}
&\le\sup_{\mathbb D\in\mathfrak D}
\Pr_{\mathbb D}\left\{\frac{|\widehat U_n-U_n|}{s_n^2}>\eta v_0\right\}\\
&\le\frac{C_{\mathrm{ep}}r_n}{1\wedge(\eta v_0)}\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]
On the complementary event,
\[
\widehat U_n\ge U_n-\eta V_n\ge(1-\eta)V_n.
\tag{13}
\]
Let \(A_{n,\mathbb D}(\eta)=\{|\widehat U_n-U_n|\le\eta V_n\}\). By \(\mathrm{def:wald\text{-}interval}\) and (13),
\[
\Pr_{\mathbb D}\{\tau_n\in\mathrm{CI}_{n,1-\alpha}\}
\ge\Pr_{\mathbb D}\{|T_{n,\mathbb D}|\le z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt{1-\eta}\}
-\Pr_{\mathbb D}\{A_{n,\mathbb D}(\eta)^c\}.
\]
Equations (11)--(12) and continuity of \(\Phi\) give
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{\mathbb D\in\mathfrak D}
\Pr_{\mathbb D}\{\tau_n\in\mathrm{CI}_{n,1-\alpha}\}
\ge2\Phi\!\left(z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt{1-\eta}\right)-1.
\tag{14}
\]
Letting \(\eta\downarrow0\) proves the lower bound \(1-\alpha\). The deterministic normalization \(E_{\mathbb D}V_n\) may depend on the array, and no common limit for \(E_{\mathbb D}V_n/s_n^2\) was used. All coverage probabilities are unconditional under the original adaptive design.
