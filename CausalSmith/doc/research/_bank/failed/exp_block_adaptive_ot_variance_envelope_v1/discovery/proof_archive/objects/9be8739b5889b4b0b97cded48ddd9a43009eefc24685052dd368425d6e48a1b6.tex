Fix \(n\) and \(b\), condition throughout on \(\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\), and abbreviate \(N=n_{nb}\), \(m_a=m_{nba}\), \(p_a=m_a/N\), \(R_i(a)=R_{nbi}(a)\), and
\[
x_i(a)=R_i(a)-\bar R_{nba}.
\]
The divisions below are valid because \(n_{nb}>0\) and \(1\le m_{nba}\le n_{nb}-1\), so in particular \(N\ge2\). By \(\mathrm{ass:fixed\text{-}potential\text{-}outcomes}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:predictable\text{-}adjustment}\), the lists \((x_i(a))_{i\in\mathcal U_{nb}}\) are fixed under this conditioning. By \(\mathrm{ass:predictable\text{-}counts}\), the counts \((m_a)_{a\in\mathcal A}\) are fixed as well. For \(I_i^a=\mathbf 1\{Z_{nbi}=a\}\), \(\mathrm{ass:complete\text{-}block\text{-}randomization}\) gives
\[
E(I_i^a\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})=\frac{m_a}{N}=p_a.
\tag{1}
\]
Consequently, \(\mathrm{def:block\text{-}martingale}\) and (1) imply
\[
E(D_{nb}\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
=w_{nb}\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}c_a
\left\{\frac1{m_a}\sum_{i\in\mathcal U_{nb}}p_aR_i(a)-\bar R_{nba}\right\}=0.
\tag{2}
\]
The increment is measurable after block \(b\) is assigned. Conditional on the history, its support is finite and every residual is real, so it is square integrable. Thus \((M_{nj},\mathcal F_{n,j})_{j\le B_n}\) is a square-integrable martingale.

For distinct labels \(i\ne k\), complete-block randomization also gives
\[
E(I_i^aI_k^a\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
=\frac{m_a(m_a-1)}{N(N-1)},
\qquad
E(I_i^aI_k^{a'}\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
=\frac{m_am_{a'}}{N(N-1)}\quad(a\ne a'),
\tag{3}
\]
whereas \(I_i^aI_i^{a'}=0\) when \(a\ne a'\). Since \(\sum_i x_i(a)=0\), expansion of the diagonal and distinct-label terms in (3) yields
\[
\operatorname{Var}\!\left(\frac1{m_a}\sum_iI_i^aR_i(a)\middle|\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\right)
=\left(\frac1{m_a}-\frac1N\right)S_{nba}^2.
\tag{4}
\]
For \(a\ne a'\), the identity \(\sum_{i\ne k}x_i(a)x_k(a')=-\sum_i x_i(a)x_i(a')\) similarly gives
\[
\operatorname{Cov}\!\left(\frac1{m_a}\sum_iI_i^aR_i(a),
\frac1{m_{a'}}\sum_iI_i^{a'}R_i(a')\middle|\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\right)
=-\frac{S_{nb,aa'}}N,
\tag{5}
\]
where
\[
S_{nb,aa'}=\frac1{N-1}\sum_{i\in\mathcal U_{nb}}x_i(a)x_i(a').
\]
Combining (4)--(5) and expanding the square gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{E(D_{nb}^2\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})}{w_{nb}^2}
&=\sum_a\frac{c_a^2S_{nba}^2}{m_a}
-\frac1N\left\{\sum_a c_a^2S_{nba}^2+2\sum_{a<a'}c_ac_{a'}S_{nb,aa'}\right\}\\
&=\sum_a\frac{c_a^2S_{nba}^2}{m_a}
-\frac1{N(N-1)}\sum_i\left\{\sum_a c_ax_i(a)\right\}^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
Define the common-label centered law
\[
\gamma_{nb}^{\mathrm{true}}
=\frac1N\sum_{i\in\mathcal U_{nb}}\delta_{(x_i(1),\ldots,x_i(K))}.
\]
Its \(a\)-th marginal is \(\mu_{nba}\), hence \(\gamma_{nb}^{\mathrm{true}}\in\Gamma_{nb}\). Substitution in (6) proves
\[
E(D_{nb}^2\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
=w_{nb}^2\left\{\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\frac{c_a^2S_{nba}^2}{m_{nba}}
-\frac1{n_{nb}-1}\int\left(\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}c_ax_a\right)^2d\gamma_{nb}^{\mathrm{true}}\right\}.
\tag{7}
\]

Fix \(h\in\mathcal H_{n,b-1}\). Every margin \(\mu_{nba}^h\) has finite support. The product measure proves that \(\Gamma_{nb}^h\) is nonempty; its marginal equalities and nonnegativity constraints make it closed; and its coordinates are nonnegative and sum to one, making it bounded. It is therefore a compact finite-dimensional transportation polytope. The quadratic cost is a continuous linear functional of the coupling coordinates, so it attains its minimum \(J_{nb}^{h,-}\) and maximum \(J_{nb}^{h,+}\). Convexity of the polytope and linearity of the functional show that its image is the full interval between those extrema. Because its coefficient in (7) is \(-w_{nb}^2/(n_{nb}-1)<0\), the lower conditional-variance endpoint uses \(J_{nb}^{h,+}\), the upper endpoint uses \(J_{nb}^{h,-}\), and both endpoints are attained within \(\Gamma_{nb}^h\).

These endpoint values are nonnegative. Indeed, put \(p_{nba}^h=m_{nba}^h/n_{nb}\). Positivity of every nodewise count and \(\sum_a p_{nba}^h=1\) allow Cauchy--Schwarz to give, for every \(x\in\mathbb R^K\),
\[
\left(\sum_ac_ax_a\right)^2
=\left(\sum_a\frac{c_ax_a}{\sqrt{p_{nba}^h}}\sqrt{p_{nba}^h}\right)^2
\le\sum_a\frac{c_a^2x_a^2}{p_{nba}^h}.
\]
The marginal definition gives
\(
\int x_a^2d\mu_{nba}^h=(n_{nb}-1)(S_{nba}^h)^2/n_{nb}
\). Hence every \(\gamma\in\Gamma_{nb}^h\) satisfies
\[
\frac1{n_{nb}-1}\int\left(\sum_ac_ax_a\right)^2d\gamma
\le\sum_a\frac{c_a^2(S_{nba}^h)^2}{m_{nba}^h}.
\tag{8}
\]

By \(\mathrm{def:history\text{-}tree\text{-}rectangular\text{-}relaxation}\), the coupling coordinate at each history node may be selected independently. Along any fixed root-to-leaf path, the Minkowski sum of the visited nodewise variance intervals is the interval whose endpoints are the sums of their respective endpoints; choosing a minimizing or maximizing coupling at every visited node attains those summed endpoints in \(\mathfrak R_n(\boldsymbol\mu_n)\). At every actually visited node, the common-label coupling in (7) is feasible. Summing (7) over the visited blocks and applying \(\mathrm{def:qv\text{-}envelope}\) proves the predictable pathwise containment
\[
V_n=\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}E(D_{nb}^2\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})\in[L_n,U_n].
\tag{9}
\]
Each summand and each endpoint in (9) is measurable before its own block is randomized; no step conditions on \(\mathcal F_{n,B_n}\). The construction proves exactness only nodewise and path by path inside the Cartesian convexified relaxation. It does not construct one globally consistent fixed-potential-outcome completion that realizes independently selected coupling coordinates at different history nodes.

The smallest adaptive check makes the filtration point explicit. With two blocks, (2) at the second-block history gives, even if the second count vector is an arbitrary \(\mathcal F_{n,1}\)-measurable function of the first block,
\[
E(D_{n1}D_{n2})=E\{D_{n1}E(D_{n2}\mid\mathcal F_{n,1})\}=0.
\tag{10}
\]
For an exact enumeration, take two arms, \(c=(1,-1)/\sqrt2\), zero adjustment, a first-block list \((-1,1)\), and quota \((1,1)\). Its two assignments give \(D_{n1}=\sqrt2w_{n1}\) and \(D_{n1}=-\sqrt2w_{n1}\). After the positive value choose second-block quota \((1,2)\), and after the negative value choose \((2,1)\); use the list \((-1,0,1)\) in both arms. With \(d=3w_{n2}/(2\sqrt2)\), the three equally likely second-block values are \((-d,0,d)\) after the first history and \((d,0,-d)\) after the second. Thus all six assignment sequences have conditional second-block mean zero, and their cross-product average is
\[
\frac12\left[\sqrt2w_{n1}\frac{-d+0+d}{3}
-\sqrt2w_{n1}\frac{d+0-d}{3}\right]=0.
\tag{11}
\]
Finally, when \(B_n=1\) and \(g_{nbi}(a)=0\), the residual lists are the fixed potential-outcome lists. Equation (7), together with optimization over their joint centered coupling, is exactly the stated static quadratic multi-marginal-transport variance formula.
