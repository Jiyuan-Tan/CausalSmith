Fix \(n\) and \(b\), and abbreviate \(N=n_{nb}\), \(m_a=m_{nba}\), \(p_a=m_a/N\), \(R_i(a)=R_{nbi}(a)\), and
\[
x_i(a)=R_i(a)-\bar R_{nba}.
\]
The divisions below are valid because \(n_{nb}\) is positive and each predictable count satisfies \(1\le m_{nba}\le n_{nb}-1\); in particular \(N\ge2\). By \(\mathrm{ass:fixed\text{-}potential\text{-}outcomes}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:predictable\text{-}adjustment}\), all lists \((x_i(a))_{i\in\mathcal U_{nb}}\) are fixed conditional on \(\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\). By \(\mathrm{ass:predictable\text{-}counts}\), the counts \((m_a)_a\) are fixed under the same conditioning. Finally, \(\mathrm{ass:complete\text{-}block\text{-}randomization}\) gives, for \(I_i^a=\mathbf 1\{Z_{nbi}=a\}\),
\[
E(I_i^a\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})=\frac{m_a}{N}=p_a.
\tag{1}
\]
Consequently, the definition in \(\mathrm{def:block\text{-}martingale}\) and (1) imply
\[
E(D_{nb}\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
=w_{nb}\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}c_a
\left\{\frac1{m_a}\sum_{i\in\mathcal U_{nb}}p_aR_i(a)-\bar R_{nba}\right\}=0.
\tag{2}
\]
The increment is measurable after block \(b\) is assigned. Conditional on the history, the assignment support is finite and every residual is real, so the increment is square integrable. Hence \((M_{nj},\mathcal F_{n,j})_{j\le B_n}\) is a square-integrable martingale.

For distinct labels \(i\ne k\), complete-block randomization also gives
\[
E(I_i^aI_k^a\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
=\frac{m_a(m_a-1)}{N(N-1)},
\qquad
E(I_i^aI_k^{a'}\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
=\frac{m_am_{a'}}{N(N-1)}\quad(a\ne a'),
\tag{3}
\]
whereas \(I_i^aI_i^{a'}=0\) for \(a\ne a'\). Since \(\sum_i x_i(a)=0\), expansion of the diagonal and distinct-label terms in (3) yields
\[
\operatorname{Var}\!\left(\frac1{m_a}\sum_iI_i^aR_i(a)\middle|\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\right)
=\left(\frac1{m_a}-\frac1N\right)S_{nba}^2.
\tag{4}
\]
For \(a\ne a'\), the identity \(\sum_{i\ne k}x_i(a)x_k(a')=-\sum_i x_i(a)x_i(a')\) similarly gives
\[
\operatorname{Cov}\!\left(\frac1{m_a}\sum_iI_i^aR_i(a),
\frac1{m_{a'}}\sum_iI_i^{a'}R_i(a')\middle|\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\right)
=-\frac{S_{nb,aa'}}N,
\tag{5}
\]
where
\[
S_{nb,aa'}=\frac1{N-1}\sum_{i\in\mathcal U_{nb}}x_i(a)x_i(a').
\]
Using (4)--(5), collecting the diagonal and off-diagonal terms, and then using the definition of \(S_{nb,aa'}\), we obtain
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{E(D_{nb}^2\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})}{w_{nb}^2}
&=\sum_a\frac{c_a^2S_{nba}^2}{m_a}
-\frac1N\left\{\sum_a c_a^2S_{nba}^2+2\sum_{a<a'}c_ac_{a'}S_{nb,aa'}\right\}\\
&=\sum_a\frac{c_a^2S_{nba}^2}{m_a}
-\frac1{N(N-1)}\sum_i\left\{\sum_a c_ax_i(a)\right\}^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
Define the common-label centered law
\[
\gamma_{nb}^{\mathrm{true}}
=\frac1N\sum_{i\in\mathcal U_{nb}}\delta_{(x_i(1),\ldots,x_i(K))}.
\]
Its \(a\)-th marginal is \(\mu_{nba}\), so \(\gamma_{nb}^{\mathrm{true}}\in\Gamma_{nb}\). Substitution in (6) proves
\[
E(D_{nb}^2\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
=w_{nb}^2\left\{\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}\frac{c_a^2S_{nba}^2}{m_{nba}}
-\frac1{n_{nb}-1}\int\left(\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}c_ax_a\right)^2d\gamma_{nb}^{\mathrm{true}}\right\}.
\tag{7}
\]

Fix a history node \(h\in\mathcal H_{n,b-1}\). Each \(\mu_{nba}^h\) has finite support. Therefore \(\Gamma_{nb}^h\) is a nonempty compact finite-dimensional transportation polytope: the product measure proves nonemptiness, the marginal equalities and nonnegativity prove closedness, and total mass one proves boundedness. The quadratic cost is a continuous linear functional of the coupling variable. It thus attains its minimum \(J_{nb}^{h,-}\) and maximum \(J_{nb}^{h,+}\); convexity of the polytope implies that the image is the entire interval \([J_{nb}^{h,-},J_{nb}^{h,+}]\). Because the cost in (7) has coefficient \(-w_{nb}^2/(n_{nb}-1)<0\), the lower conditional-variance endpoint uses \(J_{nb}^{h,+}\), the upper endpoint uses \(J_{nb}^{h,-}\), and both are attained within this node's convexified coupling set.

The endpoint values are nonnegative. Indeed, for every \(\gamma\in\Gamma_{nb}^h\), Cauchy--Schwarz, \(p_{nba}^h=m_{nba}^h/n_{nb}>0\), and \(\sum_ap_{nba}^h=1\) give
\[
\left(\sum_ac_ax_a\right)^2
=\left(\sum_a\frac{c_ax_a}{\sqrt{p_{nba}^h}}\sqrt{p_{nba}^h}\right)^2
\le\sum_a\frac{c_a^2x_a^2}{p_{nba}^h}.
\]
Since the marginal definition gives
\(
\int x_a^2d\mu_{nba}^h=(n_{nb}-1)(S_{nba}^h)^2/n_{nb}
\), integration yields
\[
\frac1{n_{nb}-1}\int\left(\sum_ac_ax_a\right)^2d\gamma
\le\sum_a\frac{c_a^2(S_{nba}^h)^2}{m_{nba}^h}.
\tag{8}
\]

By \(\mathrm{def:history\text{-}tree\text{-}rectangular\text{-}relaxation}\), the coupling coordinate at each history node may be selected independently. For any fixed root-to-leaf path, the Minkowski sum of the visited nodewise variance intervals is the interval whose endpoints are the sums of the visited lower and upper endpoints. Thus every endpoint on that path is attained in the rectangular relaxation. At the actually visited node, the common-label coupling used in (7) is feasible. Summing (7) over the visited blocks and invoking \(\mathrm{def:qv\text{-}envelope}\) gives the predictable pathwise containment
\[
V_n=\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}E(D_{nb}^2\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})\in[L_n,U_n].
\tag{9}
\]
Each summand and endpoint in (9) is measurable before its own block is randomized; no step conditions on \(\mathcal F_{n,B_n}\). This proves exactness only nodewise and path by path inside the Cartesian, convexified relaxation. It supplies no construction of one globally consistent fixed-potential-outcome adaptive-history completion realizing independently selected coupling coordinates.

As the smallest adaptive check, take two blocks and apply (2) at the second-block history. Even when the second count vector is an arbitrary \(\mathcal F_{n,1}\)-measurable function of the first block,
\[
E(D_{n1}D_{n2})=E\!\left[D_{n1}E(D_{n2}\mid\mathcal F_{n,1})\right]=0.
\tag{10}
\]
For an exact enumeration, use two arms, \(c=(1,-1)/\sqrt2\), zero adjustment, the block-one list \((-1,1)\), and quota \((1,1)\). The two assignments give \(D_{n1}=\sqrt2w_{n1}\) and \(D_{n1}=-\sqrt2w_{n1}\). After the positive value choose the block-two quota \((1,2)\), and after the negative value choose \((2,1)\); give both arms the block-two list \((-1,0,1)\). With \(d=3w_{n2}/(2\sqrt2)\), the three equally likely second-block values are \((-d,0,d)\) after the first history and \((d,0,-d)\) after the second. All six assignment sequences therefore have conditional second-block mean zero, and their cross-product average is
\[
\frac12\left[\sqrt2w_{n1}\frac{-d+0+d}{3}
-\sqrt2w_{n1}\frac{d+0-d}{3}\right]=0.
\tag{11}
\]
Finally, when \(B_n=1\) and \(g_{nbi}(a)=0\), the residuals equal the fixed potential outcomes, and direct substitution into (7) gives the stated static quadratic multi-marginal-transport variance formula.
