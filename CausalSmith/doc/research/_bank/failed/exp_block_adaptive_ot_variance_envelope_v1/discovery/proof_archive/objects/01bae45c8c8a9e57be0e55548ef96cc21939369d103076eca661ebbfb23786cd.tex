Work on the subclass in the statement, on which \(\text{ass:qv-stabilization}\) holds uniformly in addition to membership in \(\mathfrak D\). Put
\[
p=4+\delta_0,qquad t_{nb}=\frac{w_{nb}^2}{n_{nb}},qquad
s_n^2=\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}t_{nb},qquad \sigma_n^2=E_{\mathbb D}V_n.
\tag{1}
\]
First we connect the observed-data estimator to the fixed causal estimand. By consistency, on \(\{Z_{nbi}=a\}\),
\(Y_{nbi}^{\mathrm{obs}}-g_{nbi}(a)=R_{nbi}(a)\). Using
\(p_{nba}=m_{nba}/n_{nb}\), \(Y_{nbi}(a)=g_{nbi}(a)+R_{nbi}(a)\), and
\(w_{nb}=n_{nb}/N_n\), direct subtraction in \(\text{def:aipw-estimator}\) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n
&=\frac1{N_n}\sum_{b,i,a}c_a\left\{
\frac{\mathbf1\{Z_{nbi}=a\}}{p_{nba}}R_{nbi}(a)-R_{nbi}(a)\right\}\\
&=\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}w_{nb}\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}c_a
\left\{\frac1{m_{nba}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal U_{nb}}
\mathbf1\{Z_{nbi}=a\}R_{nbi}(a)-\bar R_{nba}\right\}\\
&=\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}D_{nb}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]
Thus the equality is derived from the design and consistency, rather than imposed as a definition of the causal target. Conditional on \(\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\), predictable adjustment and predictable counts make every residual list and quota fixed. Complete-block randomization makes the arm-\(a\) labels a simple random sample of size \(m_{nba}\), whose sample mean has expectation \(\bar R_{nba}\). Hence
\[
E(D_{nb}\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})=0.
\tag{3}
\]

We derive the martingale Lindeberg condition from moments and leverage. Let \(x_1,\ldots,x_N\) be deterministic with \(\sum_ix_i=0\), let \(S\) be a uniform size-\(m\) subset, put \(A_r=\sum_i|x_i|^r\), and let \(\pi_r=(m)_r/(N)_r\), with \(\pi_r=0\) for \(r>m\). Expansion according to equality patterns among four sampled indices gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
E\left(\sum_{i\in S}x_i\right)^4
={}&\pi_1A_4-4\pi_2A_4+3\pi_2(A_2^2-A_4)\\
&+6\pi_3(2A_4-A_2^2)+\pi_4(3A_2^2-6A_4).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
Indeed,
\[
\sum_{i\ne j}x_i^3x_j=-A_4,quad
\sum_{i\ne j}x_i^2x_j^2=A_2^2-A_4,
\]
\[
\sum_{i,j,k\ {
m distinct}}x_i^2x_jx_k=2A_4-A_2^2,quad
\sum_{i,j,k,l\ {
m distinct}}x_ix_jx_kx_l=3A_2^2-6A_4,
\tag{5}
\]
and these identities follow by expanding powers of \(\sum_ix_i=0\). Since
\(A_2^2\le NA_4\), \(\pi_r\le(m/N)^r\), and \(m\le N\), taking absolute values in (4) yields a numerical \(C_4<\infty\) such that
\[
E\left|\frac1m\sum_{i\in S}x_i\right|^4
\le\frac{C_4}{m^2}\frac1N\sum_i|x_i|^4.
\tag{6}
\]

Apply (6), conditionally, to \(x_i=R_{nbi}(a)-\bar R_{nba}\). Jensen's inequality, the power-mean inequality, and \(\text{ass:residual-moments}\) imply
\[
\frac1{n_{nb}}\sum_i|R_{nbi}(a)-\bar R_{nba}|^4
\le16\left\{\frac1{n_{nb}}\sum_i|R_{nbi}(a)|^p\right\}^{4/p}
\le16C_R^{4/p}.
\tag{7}
\]
If \(X_{nba}\) denotes the centered arm sample mean, contrast normalization gives
\[
\left|\sum_ac_aX_{nba}\right|^4
\le\left(\sum_aX_{nba}^2\right)^2\le K\sum_aX_{nba}^4.
\tag{8}
\]
Combining (6)--(8) with overlap, \(m_{nba}\ge\varepsilon_0n_{nb}\), gives a constant \(C_D=C_D(K,\varepsilon_0,\delta_0,C_R)\) such that
\[
E(D_{nb}^4\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
\le C_D\frac{w_{nb}^4}{n_{nb}^2}=C_Dt_{nb}^2.
\tag{9}
\]
In particular, the martingale increments are square-integrable. Predictable-QV growth gives
\[
\sigma_n^2=E_{\mathbb D}V_n\ge v_0s_n^2>0.
\tag{10}
\]
For every \(\epsilon>0\), the inequality
\(x^2\mathbf1(|x|>y)\le x^4/y^2\), followed by (9), (10), and block leverage, yields almost surely
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\frac1{\sigma_n^2}\sum_bE\{D_{nb}^2\mathbf1(|D_{nb}|>\epsilon\sigma_n)
\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\}\\
&\quad\le\frac{C_D}{\epsilon^2\sigma_n^4}\sum_bt_{nb}^2
\le\frac{C_D}{\epsilon^2v_0^2}\frac{\max_bt_{nb}}{s_n^2}
\le\frac{C_D}{\epsilon^2v_0^2}\ell_n\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
Thus the conditional Lindeberg condition is derived from primitive design conditions. The additional stabilization assumption is precisely
\[
V_n/\sigma_n^2\longrightarrow1
\quad\text{in probability, uniformly on the restricted class}.
\tag{12}
\]
Equations (3), (10)--(12), and the now self-contained
\(\text{lem:martingale-clt-predictable-variation}\) give
\[
\frac{\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n}{\sigma_n}
=\frac{\sum_bD_{nb}}{\sigma_n}\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1).
\tag{13}
\]
Together with (12), the elementary continuity argument for products gives
\[
T_n:=\frac{\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n}{\sqrt{V_n}}
=\frac{(\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n)/\sigma_n}{\sqrt{V_n/\sigma_n^2}}
\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1).
\tag{14}
\]
For clarity, tightness of the numerator in (13) and convergence of
\((V_n/\sigma_n^2)^{-1/2}\) to one show their product difference tends to zero in probability; event inclusions at \(x\pm\eta\) then prove (14). The convergence is uniform: otherwise a sequence of arrays with a fixed distributional discrepancy would itself form a triangular array obeying the common constants and the uniform versions of (11)--(12), contradicting the proved pointwise limit.

It remains to compare the observable scale with the true predictable QV. The established \(\text{thm:exact-predictable-ot-envelope}\) gives \(V_n\le U_n\) pathwise. The sharpened \(\text{thm:history-uniform-plugin}\), with
\[
r_n=q_n^{-\kappa_\star},\qquad
\kappa_\star=\frac{2+\delta_0}{2(4+\delta_0)}>0,
\tag{15}
\]
gives, for every \(\eta>0\),
\[
\sup_{\mathbb D\in\mathfrak D}\Pr_{\mathbb D}
\left\{\frac{|\widehat U_n-U_n|}{s_n^2}>\eta\right\}
\le\frac{C_{\mathrm{ep}}r_n}{1\wedge\eta}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{16}
\]
Therefore, for every fixed \(\zeta\in(0,1)\), QV growth and (16) imply
\[
\Pr_{\mathbb D}\left\{\frac{\widehat U_n}{V_n}<1-\zeta\right\}
\le\Pr_{\mathbb D}\left\{\frac{|\widehat U_n-U_n|}{s_n^2}>\zeta v_0\right\}
\longrightarrow0
\tag{17}
\]
uniformly, because \(U_n/V_n\ge1\) and \(V_n/s_n^2\ge v_0\).

Let \(z=z_{1-\alpha/2}>0\). By \(\text{def:wald-interval}\), (14), and (17),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr_{\mathbb D}\{\tau_n\in\mathrm{CI}_{n,1-\alpha}\}
&=\Pr_{\mathbb D}\{|T_n|\le z\sqrt{\widehat U_n/V_n}\}\\
&\ge\Pr_{\mathbb D}\{|T_n|\le z\sqrt{1-\zeta}\}
-\Pr_{\mathbb D}\{\widehat U_n/V_n<1-\zeta\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{18}
\]
Take the infimum over the restricted class and then the lower limit in \(n\). Uniform convergence in (14) and (17) makes the right side at least
\(2\Phi(z\sqrt{1-\zeta})-1\). Letting \(\zeta\downarrow0\), continuity of \(\Phi\) and the definition of \(z_{1-\alpha/2}\) give
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{\mathbb D\in\mathfrak D}
\Pr_{\mathbb D}\{\tau_n\in\mathrm{CI}_{n,1-\alpha}\}
\ge2\Phi(z)-1=1-\alpha.
\tag{19}
\]
All probabilities are under the original sequential assignment law; no step conditions on the terminal adaptive history.
