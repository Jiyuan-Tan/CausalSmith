Fix \(n\) and suppose \(\mathcal C_n(\boldsymbol\mu_n)\ne\varnothing\).  By the definitions of \(w_{nb}\) and \(s_n^2\), each \(w_{nb}>0\) and
\[
s_n^2=\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}\frac{w_{nb}^2}{n_{nb}}>0.
\]
The predictable counts satisfy \(1\le m_{nba}^{h}\le n_{nb}-1\), so \(n_{nb}-1>0\).  Therefore the coefficient
\[
d_{nb}:=\frac{w_{nb}^2}{s_n^2(n_{nb}-1)}
\]
is well defined and strictly positive.  For a coupling \(\gamma\) define the nonnegative quadratic cost
\[
\Phi(\gamma):=\int\left(\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}c_ax_a\right)^2d\gamma.
\]
At a node \((b,h)\), set
\[
 r_{nb}^{h}:=d_{nb}\left\{\inf_{\gamma\in\mathcal I_{nb}^{h}}\Phi(\gamma)-J_{nb}^{h,-}\right\}
\]
and, for \(\Xi_n\in\mathcal C_n(\boldsymbol\mu_n)\), set
\[
 t_{nb}^{\Xi,h}:=d_{nb}\left\{\Phi(\gamma_{nb}^{\Xi,h})-\inf_{\gamma\in\mathcal I_{nb}^{h}}\Phi(\gamma)\right\}.
\]

We first establish the signs.  Each \(\mu_{nba}^{h}\) is an empirical law of \(n_{nb}\) centered residuals indexed by \(i\in\mathcal U_{nb}\).  Pairing the atoms with the same index in every arm produces at least one deterministic label-matching coupling, so \(\mathcal I_{nb}^{h}\ne\varnothing\).  By the declared meaning of \(\mathcal I_{nb}^{h}\), every member of this finite matching set is a coupling of the prescribed margins, and hence
\(\mathcal I_{nb}^{h}\subseteq\Gamma_{nb}^{h}\).  Definition \(\text{def:history-tree-rectangular-relaxation}\) gives
\[
J_{nb}^{h,-}=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{nb}^{h}}\Phi(\gamma)
\le \inf_{\gamma\in\mathcal I_{nb}^{h}}\Phi(\gamma),
\]
so \(r_{nb}^{h}\ge0\).  Definition \(\text{def:completion-class}\) says that \(\gamma_{nb}^{\Xi,h}\) is induced by the common unit labels of \(\Xi_n\).  It is therefore one of the deterministic label-matching couplings in \(\mathcal I_{nb}^{h}\), whence
\[
\inf_{\gamma\in\mathcal I_{nb}^{h}}\Phi(\gamma)
\le \Phi(\gamma_{nb}^{\Xi,h})
\]
and \(t_{nb}^{\Xi,h}\ge0\).

The declared formulas for \(U_{nb}^{h}\) and \(Q_{nb}^{\Xi,h}\) have the same diagonal term.  Subtracting them, and then dividing by the positive number \(s_n^2\), gives the exact nodewise identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{U_{nb}^{h}-Q_{nb}^{\Xi,h}}{s_n^2}
&=d_{nb}\left\{\Phi(\gamma_{nb}^{\Xi,h})-J_{nb}^{h,-}\right\}\\
&=r_{nb}^{h}+t_{nb}^{\Xi,h}.
\end{aligned}
\]
For \(\varpi\in\mathcal P_n\), define
\[
R_n(\varpi):=\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}r_{nb}^{h_b(\varpi)},
\qquad
T_n^{\Xi}(\varpi):=\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}t_{nb}^{\Xi,h_b(\varpi)}.
\]
The preceding identity and Definition \(\text{def:completion-gap}\) now yield, without conditioning on a realized terminal path,
\[
\Delta_n^{\mathrm{tree}}(\boldsymbol\mu_n)
=\inf_{\Xi_n\in\mathcal C_n(\boldsymbol\mu_n)}
 \sup_{\varpi\in\mathcal P_n}
 \{R_n(\varpi)+T_n^{\Xi}(\varpi)\}.
\]
By their displayed definitions in the statement,
\[
A_n=\sup_{\varpi\in\mathcal P_n}R_n(\varpi),
\qquad
C_n=\inf_{\Xi_n\in\mathcal C_n(\boldsymbol\mu_n)}
     \sup_{\varpi\in\mathcal P_n}T_n^{\Xi}(\varpi).
\]

Because \(R_n\ge0\) and \(T_n^{\Xi}\ge0\), for every completion \(\Xi_n\),
\[
\sup_{\varpi}\{R_n(\varpi)+T_n^{\Xi}(\varpi)\}
\ge \sup_{\varpi}R_n(\varpi)=A_n
\]
and
\[
\sup_{\varpi}\{R_n(\varpi)+T_n^{\Xi}(\varpi)\}
\ge \sup_{\varpi}T_n^{\Xi}(\varpi).
\]
Taking the infimum over the nonempty completion class in the first and second inequalities gives, respectively,
\[
\Delta_n^{\mathrm{tree}}(\boldsymbol\mu_n)\ge A_n,
\qquad
\Delta_n^{\mathrm{tree}}(\boldsymbol\mu_n)\ge C_n.
\]
For the reverse bound, the elementary supremum inequality gives, for each \(\Xi_n\),
\[
\sup_{\varpi}\{R_n(\varpi)+T_n^{\Xi}(\varpi)\}
\le \sup_{\varpi}R_n(\varpi)+\sup_{\varpi}T_n^{\Xi}(\varpi)
=A_n+\sup_{\varpi}T_n^{\Xi}(\varpi).
\]
Taking the infimum over \(\Xi_n\) yields
\[
\max\{A_n,C_n\}\le
\Delta_n^{\mathrm{tree}}(\boldsymbol\mu_n)
\le A_n+C_n.
\]

By Definition \(\text{def:completion-gap}\),
\[
\Delta_n^{\mathrm{node}}(\boldsymbol\mu_n)
=\sup_{b\le B_n,\,h\in\mathcal H_{n,b-1}}r_{nb}^{h}.
\]
Every node in the universal history tree lies on at least one root-to-leaf path.  Since every \(r_{nb}^{h}\ge0\), if a path \(\varpi\) passes through \(h\) before block \(b\), then
\[
r_{nb}^{h}\le R_n(\varpi)\le A_n.
\]
Taking the supremum over nodes proves
\(\Delta_n^{\mathrm{node}}(\boldsymbol\mu_n)\le A_n\).  Conversely, every root-to-leaf path has exactly \(B_n\) summands and each is at most \(\Delta_n^{\mathrm{node}}(\boldsymbol\mu_n)\), so
\[
R_n(\varpi)\le B_n\Delta_n^{\mathrm{node}}(\boldsymbol\mu_n).
\]
Taking the supremum over \(\varpi\) proves
\[
\Delta_n^{\mathrm{node}}(\boldsymbol\mu_n)
\le A_n\le B_n\Delta_n^{\mathrm{node}}(\boldsymbol\mu_n).
\]

All three quantities in the first sandwich are nonnegative.  Hence \(A_n\to0\) and \(C_n\to0\) imply \(\Delta_n^{\mathrm{tree}}(\boldsymbol\mu_n)\to0\) by the upper bound, while \(\Delta_n^{\mathrm{tree}}(\boldsymbol\mu_n)\to0\) implies both \(A_n\to0\) and \(C_n\to0\) by the two lower bounds.

We make the computability claim explicit.  At a fixed node enumerate the atoms of arm \(a\), with multiplicity, as \(x_{a1},\ldots,x_{a n_{nb}}\).  The value \(J_{nb}^{h,-}\) is the optimum of the finite linear program with variables \(q_{r_1,\ldots,r_K}\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{minimize}_{q}\quad
 &\sum_{r_1=1}^{n_{nb}}\cdots\sum_{r_K=1}^{n_{nb}}
 q_{r_1,\ldots,r_K}
 \left(\sum_{a=1}^{K}c_ax_{a r_a}\right)^2,\\
\operatorname{subject\ to}\quad
 &q_{r_1,\ldots,r_K}\ge0,\\
 &\sum_{(r_j)_{j\ne a}}q_{r_1,\ldots,r_K}=\frac1{n_{nb}}
 \quad\text{for every }a\in\mathcal A\text{ and }r_a\in\{1,\ldots,n_{nb}\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Indeed, every feasible array induces a coupling of the empirical margins, and every such coupling can be lifted to a feasible array by splitting the mass attached to repeated atom values uniformly among their labels.  Thus the program has exactly the feasible costs in \(\Gamma_{nb}^{h}\).  In addition, \(\mathcal P_n\) and every nonempty \(\mathcal I_{nb}^{h}\) are finite by their declared types.  Hence the matching infimum is a finite minimum, each \(r_{nb}^{h}\) is computable from a finite linear program and a finite enumeration, and \(A_n\) is a finite maximum of finite sums.  The exact pathwise equality \(R_n+T_n^{\Xi}\) identifies \(C_n\) as the residual term after imposing one globally compatible completion.

It remains to verify the counterexample and its boundary.  Suppose for every \(n\) that the completion class is nonempty and \(\mu_{nba}^{h}=\delta_{0_{\mathbb R}}\) at every \((b,h,a)\).  The only coupling of these point masses is \(\delta_{(0,\ldots,0)}\); it belongs both to \(\Gamma_{nb}^{h}\) and to \(\mathcal I_{nb}^{h}\), and its cost is zero.  Consequently
\[
J_{nb}^{h,-}=0,
\qquad
r_{nb}^{h}=0,
\qquad
t_{nb}^{\Xi,h}=0
\]
for every node and every completion.  The definitions then give
\[
A_n=C_n=\Delta_n^{\mathrm{tree}}(\boldsymbol\mu_n)
=\Delta_n^{\mathrm{node}}(\boldsymbol\mu_n)=0.
\]
The point-mass margin also implies, unit by unit,
\[
R_{nbi}^{h}(a)-\bar R_{nba}^{h}=0.
\]
At the realized pre-block history this says that every armwise residual is constant across the block.  Since every \(m_{nba}>0\), the sampled residual mean equals the full residual mean for every assignment.  Definition \(\text{def:block-martingale}\) therefore gives \(D_{nb}=0\) in every block, and hence
\[
V_n=\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}E(D_{nb}^2\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})=0.
\]
On the other hand, \(v_0>0\) by its declared type and \(s_n^2>0\), so \(0<v_0s_n^2\).  Thus \(V_n=0\) violates \(\text{ass:qv-growth}\).  This proves that a zero global completion gap does not by itself provide a nondegenerate admissible near-attaining sequence, while making no assertion against a separate fixed-quota subclass converse.
