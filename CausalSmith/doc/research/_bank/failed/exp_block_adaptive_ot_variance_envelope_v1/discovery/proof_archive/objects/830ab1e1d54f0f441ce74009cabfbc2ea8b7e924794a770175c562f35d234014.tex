For \(K=2\) and \(c=(1,-1)/\sqrt2\), the identity
\[
\frac12(x-y)^2=\frac12x^2+\frac12y^2-xy
\tag{1}
\]
shows that the two marginal square terms do not depend on the coupling. The cited rearrangement result \(\mathrm{lem:twoarm\text{-}rearrangement\text{-}extrema}\) says that equal-rank pairing maximizes \(E(XY)\), whereas reverse-rank pairing minimizes it. By (1), equal-rank pairing therefore minimizes the quadratic cost and reverse-rank pairing maximizes it. This proves the two formulas in \(\mathrm{def:twoarm\text{-}sorting}\).

For general fixed \(K\), label every atom of every observed empirical margin, even when two labels carry the same numerical residual. A coupling of the labeled empirical measures assigns a mass \(\pi_{i_1\ldots i_K}\ge0\) to each tuple
\((i_1,\ldots,i_K)\in\prod_{a=1}^K\mathcal O_{nba}\). Its \(a\)-th marginal is \(\widehat\mu_{nba}\) if and only if
\[
\sum_{(i_{a'})_{a'\ne a}\in\prod_{a'\ne a}\mathcal O_{nba'}}
\pi_{i_1\ldots i_K}=\frac1{m_{nba}}
\tag{2}
\]
for every \(a\) and every \(i_a\in\mathcal O_{nba}\). Thus the feasible arrays in \(\mathrm{def:fixed\text{-}k\text{-}lp}\) are exactly all couplings of the empirical margins, and the displayed LP objective is exactly
\[
\int\left(\sum_{a=1}^Kc_ax_a\right)^2d\gamma.
\]
The feasible set is nonempty because it contains the product coupling, and it is a compact polytope because (2), nonnegativity, and total mass one define a closed bounded subset of a finite-dimensional space. The linear objective consequently attains its minimum and maximum, which are respectively \(\widehat J_{nb}^{-}\) and \(\widehat J_{nb}^{+}\) by \(\mathrm{def:transport\text{-}values}\). The cited finite multi-marginal Kantorovich duality result \(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}mot\text{-}kantorovich\text{-}duality}\) equates the minimum with its finite dual. Applying the same result to the negative cost and multiplying both primal and dual values by \(-1\) proves the corresponding equality for the maximum. Hence both programs give exact convexified empirical endpoints.

It remains to prove the replication assertion, which is specific to this proposition. The constraints in (2) have rational coefficients and rational right-hand sides. A linear functional on a nonempty polytope has an optimal face containing an extreme point; every extreme point of this rational polytope is rational because its coordinates solve a full-rank subsystem of the active rational equalities. Thus a rational optimal plan exists. More generally, let \(\pi\) be any rational optimal plan. Put \(N=\sum_am_{nba}=n_{nb}\), and choose an integer \(L\) divisible by \(N\), by every \(m_{nba}\), and by every denominator occurring among the finitely many values \(\pi_{i_1\ldots i_K}\). Create
\[
L\pi_{i_1\ldots i_K}
\]
replicated units of joint type \((i_1,\ldots,i_K)\). These multiplicities are nonnegative integers and sum to \(L\). By (2), the arm-\(a\) label \(i_a\) occurs exactly
\[
\sum_{(i_{a'})_{a'\ne a}}L\pi_{i_1\ldots i_K}
=\frac{L}{m_{nba}}
\tag{3}
\]
times. Therefore, within arm \(a\), every label is replicated by the integer factor \(L/m_{nba}\), and the empirical joint law on the replicated common unit labels is exactly \(\pi\). Set the replicated complete-randomization quota to \(m_{nba}'=(L/N)m_{nba}\); these are positive integers, preserve the original arm fractions, and sum to \(L\). Assigning to each replicated unit the corresponding centered armwise residual values, and choosing fixed potential outcomes and nodewise predictable fitted values whose difference equals those residuals, realizes the plan at that single history node. Repeating the construction at integer multiples of \(L\) gives nodewise completion attainability along growing replicated block sizes. The construction neither retains the original unreplicated block size nor enforces compatibility with any other history node, exactly as the proposition states.

As the smallest arithmetic stress test, take both centered lists to be \(\{-1,-1,1,1\}\). For the unnormalized contrast \((1,-1)\), equal-rank pairing makes the squared contrast identically zero, while reverse-rank pairing makes it identically four. Hence the two costs are \(0\) and \(4\), and for a block of size four the associated envelope width is
\[
\frac{4w_{nb}^2}{4-1}>0.
\]
For the normalized contrast \((1,-1)/\sqrt2\), both costs and this width are divided by two. This verifies both the endpoint ordering and nontrivial width.
