Put
\[
X_{nb}=\frac{D_{nb}}{\sigma_n},\qquad
v_{nb}=E(X_{nb}^2\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1}),\qquad
A_{nj}=\sum_{b=1}^{j}v_{nb},
\]
which is well defined because \(\sigma_n^2=E(V_n)>0\). Then \(E(X_{nb}\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})=0\),
\[
A_{nB_n}=\frac{V_n}{\sigma_n^2}\longrightarrow1
\quad\text{in probability},
\tag{1}
\]
and the assumed conditional Lindeberg condition is, for each \(\delta>0\),
\[
\Lambda_n(\delta):=\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}
E\!\left[X_{nb}^2\mathbf1\{|X_{nb}|>\delta\}\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\right]
\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{in probability}.
\tag{2}
\]

We first make the predictable variance bounded without changing the terminal sum with nonvanishing probability. Define the predictable indicators
\[
I_{nb}=\mathbf1\{A_{nb}\le2\},
\]
which are \(\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\)-measurable because \(v_{nb}\) is, and put
\[
X'_{nb}=I_{nb}X_{nb},\qquad
v'_{nb}=I_{nb}v_{nb},\qquad
A'_{nj}=\sum_{b=1}^{j}v'_{nb},\qquad
S'_{nj}=\sum_{b=1}^{j}X'_{nb}.
\]
The array \((X'_{nb})\) remains a martingale-difference array. If \(A_{nB_n}\le2\), no increment is stopped. Otherwise, immediately before the first crossing of level two the accumulated predictable variance is at most two, and the crossing increment and all later increments are stopped. Hence
\[
0\le A'_{nB_n}\le2.
\tag{3}
\]
On \(\{A_{nB_n}\le2\}\), every \(I_{nb}=1\). Therefore (1) implies
\[
\Pr\!\left(S'_{nB_n}\ne\sum_bX_{nb}\right)
\le\Pr(A_{nB_n}>2)\longrightarrow0,
\qquad
A'_{nB_n}\longrightarrow1
\quad\text{in probability}.
\tag{4}
\]
Moreover, with
\[
\Lambda'_n(\delta)=\sum_bE\!\left[(X'_{nb})^2
\mathbf1\{|X'_{nb}|>\delta\}\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\right],
\]
we have \(0\le\Lambda'_n(\delta)\le\Lambda_n(\delta)\) and \(\Lambda'_n(\delta)\le A'_{nB_n}\le2\). Thus (2), boundedness, and convergence in probability imply
\[
E\Lambda'_n(\delta)\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{for every fixed }\delta>0.
\tag{5}
\]

The largest stopped conditional variance is negligible. Indeed,
\[
v'_{nb}
\le \delta^2+E\!\left[(X'_{nb})^2
\mathbf1\{|X'_{nb}|>\delta\}\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\right],
\]
so
\[
\max_{b\le B_n}v'_{nb}\le\delta^2+\Lambda'_n(\delta).
\tag{6}
\]
For any \(\eta>0\), choose \(\delta^2<\eta/2\) and use (2) in (6); this proves \(\max_bv'_{nb}\to0\) in probability. Since \(0\le\max_bv'_{nb}\le2\), it also converges to zero in mean. Therefore
\[
E\sum_b(v'_{nb})^2
\le E\!\left[A'_{nB_n}\max_bv'_{nb}\right]
\le2E\max_bv'_{nb}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{7}
\]

We now calculate the characteristic function directly. Fix \(t\in\mathbb R\), and define the predictable Taylor remainder
\[
r_{nb}=E(e^{itX'_{nb}}\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})-1+\frac{t^2}{2}v'_{nb}.
\]
The martingale-difference property removes the linear Taylor term. For \(|x|\le\delta\), Taylor's formula gives
\[
|e^{itx}-1-itx+t^2x^2/2|\le |t|^3\delta x^2/6,
\]
while for \(|x|>\delta\), the triangle inequality gives the same left side at most
\[
\left(2\delta^{-2}+|t|\delta^{-1}+t^2/2\right)x^2.
\]
Conditional expectation and summation consequently yield
\[
\sum_b|r_{nb}|
\le \frac{|t|^3\delta}{6}A'_{nB_n}
+\left(2\delta^{-2}+|t|\delta^{-1}+t^2/2\right)\Lambda'_n(\delta).
\tag{8}
\]
Taking expectations, first letting \(n\to\infty\) by (3) and (5), and then letting \(\delta\downarrow0\), proves
\[
E\sum_b|r_{nb}|\longrightarrow0.
\tag{9}
\]

Set
\[
H_{nj}=\exp\!\left(itS'_{nj}+\frac{t^2}{2}A'_{nj}\right),
\qquad H_{n0}=1.
\]
Because \(v'_{nb}\) is \(\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\)-measurable,
\[
E(H_{nb}\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
=H_{n,b-1}e^{t^2v'_{nb}/2}
\left(1-\frac{t^2}{2}v'_{nb}+r_{nb}\right).
\tag{10}
\]
For \(0\le v\le2\), Taylor's formula on this compact interval supplies a finite constant \(C_t\) such that
\[
\left|e^{t^2v/2}(1-t^2v/2)-1\right|\le C_tv^2.
\tag{11}
\]
Also \(|H_{n,b-1}|\le e^{t^2}\) by (3), and \(e^{t^2v'_{nb}/2}\le e^{t^2}\). Summing the expectation differences in (10), and applying (7), (9), and (11), gives
\[
|EH_{nB_n}-1|
\le e^{t^2}C_tE\sum_b(v'_{nb})^2
+e^{2t^2}E\sum_b|r_{nb}|
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{12}
\]
By (3)--(4), the bounded random variables \(e^{t^2A'_{nB_n}/2}\) converge in probability, hence in mean, to \(e^{t^2/2}\). Combining this with (12),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|e^{t^2/2}E(e^{itS'_{nB_n}})-1\right|
&\le E\left|e^{t^2/2}-e^{t^2A'_{nB_n}/2}\right|
+|EH_{nB_n}-1|\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
Thus \(E(e^{itS'_{nB_n}})\to e^{-t^2/2}\). Equation (4) and the boundedness of complex exponentials imply
\[
\left|E\exp\!\left(it\sum_bX_{nb}\right)-E(e^{itS'_{nB_n}})\right|\longrightarrow0.
\tag{14}
\]
The pointwise limit in (13)--(14) is the characteristic function of \(\mathcal N(0,1)\). Applying \(\mathrm{lem:levy\text{-}continuity\text{-}criterion}\) therefore yields
\[
\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}X_{nb}\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1).
\]
Substituting \(X_{nb}=D_{nb}/\sigma_n\) proves the claim. Notice that (2) uses exactly the threshold \(\delta\sigma_n\) before normalization; no inverse-threshold statement is used.
