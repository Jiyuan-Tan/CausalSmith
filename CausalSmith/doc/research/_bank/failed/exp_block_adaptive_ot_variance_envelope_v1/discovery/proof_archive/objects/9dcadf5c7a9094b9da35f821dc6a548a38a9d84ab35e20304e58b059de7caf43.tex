Fix an array \(\mathbb D\in\mathfrak D\), and let all expectations and probabilities below be under its original sequential randomization law.  By \(\text{def:aipw-estimator}\), consistency, and \(p_{nba}=m_{nba}/n_{nb}\), direct subtraction of \(\tau_n\) gives
\[
\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n
=\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}w_{nb}\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}c_a
\left\{\frac1{m_{nba}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal U_{nb}}
\mathbf 1\{Z_{nbi}=a\}R_{nbi}(a)-\bar R_{nba}\right\}
=\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}D_{nb}=M_{nB_n}.
\]
The first equality uses \(Y_{nbi}^{\mathrm{obs}}=Y_{nbi}(Z_{nbi})\) from \(\mathrm{ass:consistency\text{-}no\text{-}interference}\), and the second is \(\text{def:block-martingale}\).  The martingale assertion in \(\text{thm:exact-predictable-ot-envelope}\), proved from predictable counts, predictable adjustment, and conditional complete-block randomization, therefore shows that \((D_{nb})_{b\le B_n}\) is a square-integrable martingale-difference array.  In particular, cross-block covariance terms vanish by iterated expectation; no terminal-history conditioning is involved.

We next derive the Lindeberg condition from the primitive moment and leverage assumptions.  Conditional on \(\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\), put
\[
x_i(a)=R_{nbi}(a)-\bar R_{nba},\qquad
X_a=\frac1{m_{nba}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal U_{nb}}
\mathbf 1\{Z_{nbi}=a\}x_i(a).
\]
Then \(D_{nb}=w_{nb}\sum_a c_aX_a\).  The residual-moment assumption, the power-mean inequality, and Jensen's inequality imply
\[
\frac1{n_{nb}}\sum_i|x_i(a)|^4
\le16\frac1{n_{nb}}\sum_i|R_{nbi}(a)|^4
\le16\left\{\frac1{n_{nb}}\sum_i|R_{nbi}(a)|^{4+\delta_0}\right\}^{4/(4+\delta_0)}
\le16C_R^{4/(4+\delta_0)}.
\tag{1}
\]
Here all sums in (1) are over \(i\in\mathcal U_{nb}\); the last inequality is exactly \(\mathrm{ass:residual\text{-}moments}\).

For completeness, the needed fourth-moment sampling bound follows by finite-population algebra.  For any centered list \(x_1,\ldots,x_N\), let \(S\) be a uniformly chosen subset of size \(m\), set \(A_r=\sum_{i=1}^N x_i^r\), and let \(\pi_r=(m)_r/(N)_r\), with \(\pi_r=0\) when \(r>m\).  Expanding the fourth power and using \(\sum_i x_i=0\) yields
\[
E\left(\sum_{i\in S}x_i\right)^4
=\{\pi_1-7\pi_2+12\pi_3-6\pi_4\}A_4
+3\{\pi_2-2\pi_3+\pi_4\}A_2^2.
\tag{2}
\]
Indeed, the index multiplicity patterns \(4\), \(3+1\), \(2+2\), \(2+1+1\), and \(1+1+1+1\) contribute respectively
\[
\pi_1A_4,\quad -4\pi_2A_4,\quad
3\pi_2(A_2^2-A_4),\quad
6\pi_3(2A_4-A_2^2),\quad
\pi_4(3A_2^2-6A_4),
\]
which proves (2).  Since \(0\le\pi_r\le1\) and \(A_2^2\le NA_4\), if \(m\ge\varepsilon_0N\), then
\[
E\left(\frac1m\sum_{i\in S}x_i\right)^4
\le \left(\frac{26}{\varepsilon_0}+\frac{12}{\varepsilon_0^2}\right)
\frac1{m^2}\frac{A_4}{N}.
\tag{3}
\]
Under \(\mathrm{ass:complete\text{-}block\text{-}randomization}\), the marginal arm-\(a\) sample is precisely such a uniform subset, and \(\mathrm{ass:overlap}\) gives \(m_{nba}\ge\varepsilon_0n_{nb}\).  Combining (1)--(3), \(\sum_ac_a^2=1\) from \(\mathrm{ass:contrast\text{-}normalization}\), and
\[
\left(\sum_ac_aX_a\right)^4
\le\left(\sum_aX_a^2\right)^2
\le K\sum_aX_a^4,
\]
gives a constant \(C_0<\infty\), depending only on \(K,\varepsilon_0,\delta_0,C_R\), such that
\[
E(D_{nb}^4\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1})
\le C_0\left(\frac{w_{nb}^2}{n_{nb}}\right)^2
\quad\text{almost surely.}
\tag{4}
\]

Define the deterministic, array-specific scale
\[
\sigma_{n,\mathbb D}^2=E_{\mathbb D}V_n.
\]
Martingale orthogonality gives \(\sigma_{n,\mathbb D}^2=\operatorname{Var}_{\mathbb D}(M_{nB_n})\), while \(\mathrm{ass:qv\text{-}growth}\) gives
\[
\sigma_{n,\mathbb D}^2\ge v_0s_n^2>0.
\tag{5}
\]
Writing \(t_{nb}=w_{nb}^2/n_{nb}\), (4), (5), \(\sum_bt_{nb}=s_n^2\), and \(\mathrm{ass:block\text{-}leverage}\) give the uniform Lyapunov bound
\[
\sup_{\mathbb D\in\mathfrak D}
\frac{\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}E_{\mathbb D}D_{nb}^4}{\sigma_{n,\mathbb D}^4}
\le\frac{C_0}{v_0^2}
\frac{\sum_bt_{nb}^2}{s_n^4}
\le\frac{C_0}{v_0^2}\ell_n\longrightarrow0.
\tag{6}
\]
For every \(\epsilon>0\), define the conditional Lindeberg quantity
\[
\Lambda_{n,\mathbb D}(\epsilon)
=\frac1{\sigma_{n,\mathbb D}^2}\sum_{b=1}^{B_n}
E_{\mathbb D}\left[D_{nb}^2\mathbf 1\{|D_{nb}|>\epsilon\sigma_{n,\mathbb D}\}
\mid\mathcal F_{n,b-1}\right].
\]
By \(u^2\mathbf 1\{|u|>x\}\le u^4/x^2\), (6), and Markov's inequality,
\[
\sup_{\mathbb D\in\mathfrak D}E_{\mathbb D}\Lambda_{n,\mathbb D}(\epsilon)
\le\frac1{\epsilon^2}
\sup_{\mathbb D\in\mathfrak D}
\frac{\sum_bE_{\mathbb D}D_{nb}^4}{\sigma_{n,\mathbb D}^4}
\longrightarrow0,
\tag{7}
\]
so the conditional Lindeberg condition holds uniformly in probability.

The added classical martingale central limit theorem \(\text{lem:martingale-clt-predictable-variation}\) applies because (7) supplies its Lindeberg premise and \(\mathrm{ass:qv\text{-}stabilization}\) supplies
\[
\frac{V_n}{\sigma_{n,\mathbb D}^2}
=\frac{V_n}{E_{\mathbb D}V_n}\longrightarrow1
\quad\text{in probability, uniformly over }\mathbb D\in\mathfrak D.
\tag{8}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\frac{\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n}{\sigma_{n,\mathbb D}}
=\frac{M_{nB_n}}{\sigma_{n,\mathbb D}}
\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1)
\quad\text{uniformly over }\mathfrak D.
\tag{9}
\]
To justify the uniform conclusion from the cited pointwise theorem, suppose (9) failed.  Then some subsequence and choices \(\mathbb D_n\in\mathfrak D\) would keep a fixed distributional discrepancy from zero.  The diagonal array \((\mathbb D_n)\) still satisfies (7) and (8), with the same constants and envelopes, so the cited theorem gives convergence along that subsequence, a contradiction.  Combining (8) and (9) by Slutsky's argument, justified by the same diagonal-subsequence device, yields
\[
T_{n,\mathbb D}:=\frac{\widehat\tau_n-\tau_n}{\sqrt{V_n}}
\Rightarrow\mathcal N(0,1)
\quad\text{uniformly over }\mathfrak D.
\tag{10}
\]

It remains to establish conservative studentization.  The pathwise containment in \(\mathrm{thm:exact-predictable-ot-envelope}\) gives \(V_n\le U_n\).  Moreover, \(q_n/\log(2B_nK)\to\infty\), \(\ell_n\to0\), and \(\delta_0>0\) imply directly from the definition of \(r_n\) that \(r_n\to0\).  Therefore \(\mathrm{thm:history-uniform-plugin}\), together with \(V_n\ge v_0s_n^2\), gives, for each fixed \(\eta\in(0,1)\),
\[
\sup_{\mathbb D\in\mathfrak D}
\Pr_{\mathbb D}\{|\widehat U_n-U_n|>\eta V_n\}
\le
\sup_{\mathbb D\in\mathfrak D}
\Pr_{\mathbb D}\left\{\frac{|\widehat U_n-U_n|}{s_n^2}>\eta v_0\right\}
\le\frac{C_{\mathrm{ep}}r_n}{1\wedge(\eta v_0)}
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{11}
\]
On the complementary event in (11),
\[
\widehat U_n\ge U_n-\eta V_n\ge(1-\eta)V_n.
\tag{12}
\]
Let \(A_{n,\mathbb D}(\eta)=\{|\widehat U_n-U_n|\le\eta V_n\}\).  By the definition of \(\mathrm{CI}_{n,1-\alpha}\) and (12),
\[
\Pr_{\mathbb D}\{\tau_n\in\mathrm{CI}_{n,1-\alpha}\}
\ge
\Pr_{\mathbb D}\{|T_{n,\mathbb D}|\le z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt{1-\eta}\}
-\Pr_{\mathbb D}\{A_{n,\mathbb D}(\eta)^c\}.
\]
Thus (10), (11), and continuity of the standard normal distribution function \(\Phi\) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{\mathbb D\in\mathfrak D}
\Pr_{\mathbb D}\{\tau_n\in\mathrm{CI}_{n,1-\alpha}\}
&\ge
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{\mathbb D\in\mathfrak D}
\Pr_{\mathbb D}\{|T_{n,\mathbb D}|\le z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt{1-\eta}\}\\
&=2\Phi\!\left(z_{1-\alpha/2}\sqrt{1-\eta}\right)-1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
Letting \(\eta\downarrow0\) in (13) gives \(1-\alpha\), proving the asserted coverage.  The proof uses the deterministic normalization \(E_{\mathbb D}V_n\) separately for each array.  It does not require \(E_{\mathbb D}V_n/s_n^2\) to converge, uniformly or pointwise, to any common scalar, and every probability above is unconditional under the original sequential design.
