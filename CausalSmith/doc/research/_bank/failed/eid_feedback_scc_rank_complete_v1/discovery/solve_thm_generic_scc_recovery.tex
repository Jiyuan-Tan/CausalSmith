Let \(K\) and \(O\) be the two displayed graphs in the corrected statement.  We verify every premise defining \(\mathfrak G_7\).  Each displayed list has eighteen arcs, contains no loop, and contains at most one orientation of each unordered pair.  More explicitly, the three pairs absent from the skeleton of \(K\) are
\[
\{1,2\},\quad \{1,3\},\quad \{2,3\},
\]
whereas the three pairs absent from the skeleton of \(O\) are
\[
\{2,3\},\quad \{3,7\},\quad \{5,7\}.
\]
All other unordered pairs occur exactly once in the respective arc list.  Thus the simple-orientation and noncompleteness conditions hold.  Reading the outgoing arcs row by row gives
\[
\bigl(d_K(1),\ldots,d_K(7)\bigr)
=(4,2,1,2,2,4,3)
=\bigl(d_O(1),\ldots,d_O(7)\bigr).
\tag{1}
\]

We next verify the SCC discrepancy.  In both graphs the vertices \(2,3,4,5\) are mutually reachable along the directed cycle
\[
2\to5\to3\to4\to2.
\tag{2}
\]
In both graphs, \(4\to6\to2\), so vertex \(6\) is mutually reachable with the vertices in (2); likewise \(2\to7\to4\), so vertex \(7\) is mutually reachable with them.  Hence \(\{2,3,4,5,6,7\}\) is strongly connected in both graphs.  No arc of \(K\) enters vertex \(1\), while \(K\) has arcs from \(1\) into the other component.  Therefore
\[
\operatorname{SCC}(K)=\bigl\{\{1\},\{2,3,4,5,6,7\}\bigr\}.
\tag{3}
\]
For \(O\), the arcs \(6\to1\) and \(1\to2\), together with the strong connectivity just established on \(\{2,ldots,7\}\), put vertex \(1\) in that same SCC.  Consequently

\operatorname{SCC}(O)=\bigl\{\{1,2,3,4,5,6,7\}\bigr\}.

Suppose that \(\mathcal F\) satisfies the following finite certificate-separation condition: for every \(K,O\in\mathcal F\) with \(\operatorname{SCC}(K)\ne\operatorname{SCC}(O)\), either \(d_K\ne d_O\), or \(d_K=d_O\) and there exist an ordered pair \((K',O')\in\{(K,O),(O,K)\}\) and a vertex \(i\in V\) such that \(\Delta_i(K',O')>0\).  Then, for every \(G_0\in\mathcal F\), there exists a proper algebraic set \(\mathcal E_{G_0}\subsetneq\mathcal V_{G_0}^\Sigma\) such that
\[
\mathcal R(\Sigma)=\operatorname{SCC}(G_0)
\qquad\text{for every }\Sigma\in\mathcal V_{G_0}^\Sigma\setminus\mathcal E_{G_0}.
\]
The certificate-separation condition is decidable by the maximum-matching computation in the closure--matching theorem and does not assert universal Hall compatibility.

The causal structure target is \(\operatorname{SCC}(G_0)\), whereas \(\mathcal R\) is the observed-covariance functional defined by minimum-dimensional variety compatibility.  We prove their equality under the finite certificate-separation premise of the corrected statement.

First fix \(G,H\in\mathcal F\) with
\[
\operatorname{SCC}(G)\ne\operatorname{SCC}(H).
\tag{10}
\]
If \(d_G\ne d_H\), the unequal-outdegree separation lemma gives
\[
\mathcal V_G^\Sigma\ne\mathcal V_H^\Sigma.
\tag{11}
\]
If \(d_G=d_H\), the certificate-separation premise supplies an ordered choice \((K,O)\in\{(G,H),(H,G)\}\) and \(i\in V\) for which \(\Delta_i(K,O)>0\).  The closure--matching theorem gives a set \(L\in\mathcal L_i(K,O)\) satisfying
\[
|\operatorname{ch}_K(i)\cap L|-|\operatorname{ch}_O(i)\cap L|
=\Delta_i(K,O)>0.
\tag{12}
\]
The incoming-star rank theorem applied to (12) then yields
\[
\mathcal V_K^\Sigma\ne\mathcal V_O^\Sigma,
\]
which is (11) after forgetting the chosen order.  Thus (10) implies (11) for every pair in the candidate family.

We next establish the algebraic facts needed to turn pairwise distinction into a generic selector.  Fix \(G\in\mathcal F\), put \(q_G=|E(G)|\), and let \(A_G=\mathbb A^{q_G+1}\) be the affine parameter space with coordinates given by the allowed edge coefficients and \(\omega\).  Let
\[
U_G:=\{(\Lambda_G,\omega)\in A_G:\det(I_p-\Lambda_G)\ne0\}
\]
and let \(D_G:=U_G\cap\{\omega>0\}\), the domain appearing in the definition of \(\mathcal V_G^\Sigma\).  The set \(D_G\) contains a nonempty Euclidean open neighborhood of \((0,1)\): continuity of the determinant gives such a neighborhood on which \(\det(I_p-\Lambda_G)\ne0\), and positivity gives an open interval about \(1\).  A real polynomial that vanishes on a nonempty Euclidean open set is the zero polynomial, as follows by fixing all but one coordinate and applying the one-variable root bound successively.  Therefore \(D_G\) is Zariski dense in \(A_G\), and hence dense in \(U_G\).

The affine space \(A_G\) is irreducible: two nonempty principal Zariski open sets defined by nonzero polynomials \(f\) and \(g\) intersect because the nonzero polynomial \(fg\) cannot vanish on all of \(\mathbb R^{q_G+1}\).  Every nonempty Zariski open contains a nonempty principal open, so this proves irreducibility.  The nonempty open subset \(U_G\) is therefore irreducible as well.  On \(U_G\), the covariance map
\[
\phi_G(\Lambda_G,\omega)
=\omega(I_p-\Lambda_G)^{-\top}(I_p-\Lambda_G)^{-1}
\tag{13}
\]
is regular, because every inverse entry is an adjugate entry divided by the nonvanishing determinant.  Since regular maps are Zariski continuous and \(D_G\) is dense in \(U_G\),
\[
\overline{\phi_G(D_G)}^{\mathrm{Zar}}
=\overline{\phi_G(U_G)}^{\mathrm{Zar}}
=\mathcal V_G^\Sigma.
\tag{14}
\]
For clarity, the first equality follows directly from continuity: if \(x\in U_G\) and a closed set contains \(\phi_G(D_G)\), its inverse image is closed and contains the dense set \(D_G\), so it contains \(x\) and hence \(\phi_G(x)\).

The closure in (14) is irreducible.  Indeed, if it were the union of two closed sets \(C_1\cup C_2\), then
\[
U_G=\phi_G^{-1}(C_1)\cup\phi_G^{-1}(C_2).
\]
Irreducibility of \(U_G\) would put all of \(U_G\) in one inverse image, say \(\phi_G^{-1}(C_1)\); taking closures in (14) would then give \(\mathcal V_G^\Sigma\subseteq C_1\).  Thus one member already equals the whole closure.  This proves that every \(\mathcal V_G^\Sigma\) is a nonempty irreducible affine algebraic set.

For the fixed true graph \(G_0\), define
\[
\mathcal E_{G_0}:=
\bigcup_{\substack{H\in\mathcal F:\
\operatorname{SCC}(H)\ne\operatorname{SCC}(G_0),\\
\dim\mathcal V_H^\Sigma\le\dim\mathcal V_{G_0}^\Sigma}}
\left(\mathcal V_{G_0}^\Sigma\cap\mathcal V_H^\Sigma\right).
\tag{15}
\]
Every intersection in (15) is a proper closed subset of \(\mathcal V_{G_0}^\Sigma\).  Otherwise \(\mathcal V_{G_0}^\Sigma\subseteq\mathcal V_H^\Sigma\).  Equality is excluded by (11).  Strict inclusion of irreducible affine algebraic sets forces strict increase of dimension directly from the definition of Krull dimension: append \(\mathcal V_H^\Sigma\) to any chain of irreducible closed subsets ending in \(\mathcal V_{G_0}^\Sigma\).  It would follow that
\[
\dim\mathcal V_{G_0}^\Sigma<\dim\mathcal V_H^\Sigma,
\]
contrary to the index restriction in (15).  Since \(\mathcal F\) is finite, (15) is a finite union.  A finite union of proper closed subsets of an irreducible space is proper: repeated application of the defining two-set irreducibility property proves this by induction.  Hence
\[
\mathcal E_{G_0}\subsetneq\mathcal V_{G_0}^\Sigma.
\tag{16}
\]

Finally take \(\Sigma\in\mathcal V_{G_0}^\Sigma\setminus\mathcal E_{G_0}\).  The graph \(G_0\) is feasible in the minimization defining \(G^*(\Sigma)\), so
\[
\dim\mathcal V_{G^*(\Sigma)}^\Sigma
\le \dim\mathcal V_{G_0}^\Sigma.
\tag{17}
\]
If \(\operatorname{SCC}(G^*(\Sigma))\ne\operatorname{SCC}(G_0)\), feasibility of \(G^*(\Sigma)\), together with (17), would put \(\Sigma\) in one of the intersections in (15), contradicting the choice of \(\Sigma\).  Therefore
\[
\mathcal R(\Sigma)
=\operatorname{SCC}(G^*(\Sigma))
=\operatorname{SCC}(G_0).
\]
The fixed order \(\preceq\) only makes \(G^*(\Sigma)\) single valued; the argument proves that every minimum-dimensional compatible minimizer has the correct SCC partition outside (15).
