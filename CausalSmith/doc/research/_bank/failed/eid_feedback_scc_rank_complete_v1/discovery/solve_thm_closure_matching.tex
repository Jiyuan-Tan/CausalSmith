\textbf{Proof.}
Fix the ordered pair \((K,O)\) and \(i\in V\), and abbreviate
\[
 A_K=\operatorname{ch}_K(i),\qquad
 A_O=\operatorname{ch}_O(i),\qquad
 P=A_K\setminus A_O,\qquad Q=A_O\setminus A_K.
\]
The equal-outdegree assumption gives \(|A_K|=|A_O|\), and cancellation of the common children gives, for every \(L\subseteq V\setminus\{i\}\),
\[
 |A_K\cap L|-|A_O\cap L|=|P\cap L|-|Q\cap L|.
 \tag{1}
\]
By the definition of \(D_i(K,O)\), the comparator-parent condition
\[
 \operatorname{pa}_K(A_K\cap L)\cap N_O(i)\subseteq L
\]
holds if and only if \(L\) contains the head of every \(D_i(K,O)\)-arc whose tail lies in \(L\).  Thus \(\mathcal L_i(K,O)\) is exactly the family of successor-closed vertex sets of \(D_i(K,O)\).

Let \(L\in\mathcal L_i(K,O)\), and put \(X=P\cap L\).  Successor closure implies that every vertex of \(Q\) reachable in \(D_i(K,O)\) from \(X\) lies in \(L\).  The definition of \(\Gamma_i(K,O)\) therefore gives
\[
 \operatorname{nb}_{\Gamma_i(K,O)}(X)\subseteq Q\cap L.
\]
Together with \((1)\), this yields
\[
 |A_K\cap L|-|A_O\cap L|
 \le |X|-|\operatorname{nb}_{\Gamma_i(K,O)}(X)|.
 \tag{2}
\]
Conversely, for \(X\subseteq P\), let \(\operatorname{cl}_{D_i}(X)\) be the set of all vertices reachable from \(X\) in \(D_i(K,O)\), including \(X\).  This set is successor closed, so it belongs to \(\mathcal L_i(K,O)\).  Moreover,
\[
 Q\cap\operatorname{cl}_{D_i}(X)
 =\operatorname{nb}_{\Gamma_i(K,O)}(X),
 \qquad
 X\subseteq P\cap\operatorname{cl}_{D_i}(X).
\]
Applying \((1)\) to this closure gives
\[
 |A_K\cap\operatorname{cl}_{D_i}(X)|
 -|A_O\cap\operatorname{cl}_{D_i}(X)|
 \ge |X|-|\operatorname{nb}_{\Gamma_i(K,O)}(X)|.
 \tag{3}
\]
The two opposite inequalities \((2)\) and \((3)\), after maximization, prove the first asserted equality.

It remains to derive the matching formula.  Write \(\Gamma=\Gamma_i(K,O)\), let \(M\) be a maximum matching of size \(\nu=\nu_i(K,O)\), and let \(U\subseteq P\) be the vertices of \(P\) unmatched by \(M\).  For arbitrary \(X\subseteq P\), at most \(|\operatorname{nb}_\Gamma(X)|\) members of \(X\) can be matched, because their matching partners are distinct members of \(\operatorname{nb}_\Gamma(X)\).  Hence
\[
 |X|-|\operatorname{nb}_\Gamma(X)|
 \le |P|-\nu.
 \tag{4}
\]
For the reverse inequality, start at \(U\) and follow alternating paths that use an edge outside \(M\) from \(P\) to \(Q\), and an edge of \(M\) from \(Q\) to \(P\).  Let \(Z_P\) and \(Z_Q\) be the reached vertices in the two parts.  Maximality of \(M\) implies that every member of \(Z_Q\) is matched, since an unmatched member would terminate an augmenting path.  Every neighbor of \(Z_P\) belongs to \(Z_Q\): a nonmatching edge extends the alternating search, while the matching edge incident to a reached vertex of \(P\setminus U\) is the edge by which that vertex was reached.  The reverse inclusion holds by construction, and therefore
\[
 \operatorname{nb}_\Gamma(Z_P)=Z_Q.
\]
The matching edges give a bijection from \(Z_Q\) to \(Z_P\setminus U\).  Consequently
\[
 |Z_P|-|\operatorname{nb}_\Gamma(Z_P)|
 =|Z_P|-|Z_Q|=|U|=|P|-\nu.
 \tag{5}
\]
Equations \((4)\) and \((5)\) establish
\[
 \max_{X\subseteq P}\bigl\{|X|-|\operatorname{nb}_\Gamma(X)|\bigr\}
 =|P|-\nu=\Delta_i(K,O),
\]
where the last equality is the definition of the Hall deficiency.

Finally, \(|P|=|Q|\), again by equal outdegrees.  Thus \(\nu=|P|\) exactly when \(\Gamma_i(K,O)\) has a perfect matching, and a positive certificate exists exactly when \(\Delta_i(K,O)>0\).  A maximum matching and the alternating search above produce a maximizing \(Z_P\); taking \(L=\operatorname{cl}_{D_i}(Z_P)\) produces a maximizing comparator-parent closed set by \((3)\) and the already proved equality of the optima.  Directed reachability, bipartite maximum matching, alternating reachability, and directed closure all require polynomially many elementary graph operations.  Hence the stated certificate is recoverable in polynomial time. \(\square\)

%%% FIELD proof-thm-incoming-star-rank
\textbf{Proof.}
Put
\[
 C=\operatorname{ch}_K(i)\cap L,qquad
 A=\operatorname{pa}_O(i)\cap L,qquad
 B=\operatorname{ch}_O(i)\cap L,qquad
 D=N_O(i)\cap L=A\mathbin{\dot\cup}B.
\]
The disjoint union follows from the simple-orientation assumption.  Equal labeled outdegrees imply \(|E(K)|=|E(O)|=m\).  They also show that \(L\ne V\setminus\{i\}\), because otherwise the strict inequality in the hypothesis would read \(d_K(i)>d_O(i)\).  Choose
\[
 j_0\in V\setminus(L\cup\{i\}).
\]
For an unordered pair \(\{a,b\}\), write \(\Pi_{ab}\) for the corresponding upper-triangular precision coordinate, and define
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 S_E&=\{\Pi_{ab}:\{a,b\}\text{ is a skeleton edge of }O\},\\
 S_-&=\{\Pi_{i\ell}:\ell\in L\},\\
 S_+&=\{\Pi_{\ell\ell}:\ell\in D\}\cup\{\Pi_{j_0j_0}\},\\
 S&=(S_E\setminus S_-)\cup S_+.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{1}
\]
Oriented simplicity gives \(|S_E|=m\).  Exactly the \(|D|\) coordinates in \(S_E\) incident to \(i\) and a member of \(L\) are removed, and \(|D|+1\) distinct diagonal coordinates are added.  Hence \(|S|=m+1\).

Expanding the precision parameterization gives
\[
 \Pi_{ab}=t\left(\mathbf 1\{a=b\}-\lambda_{ab}-\lambda_{ba}
 +\sum_{r\in V}\lambda_{ar}\lambda_{br}\right).
 \tag{2}
\]
For an edge \(i\to j\) of \(K\), differentiation of \((2)\) shows that the row of \(J_K\) indexed by \(\lambda_{ij}\) can be nonzero only in
\[
 \Pi_{ii},\qquad \Pi_{ij},\qquad
 \{\Pi_{i\ell}:\ell\in\operatorname{pa}_K(j)\}.
 \tag{3}
\]
Now take \(j\in C\).  The coordinate \(\Pi_{ii}\) is not in \(S\), and \(\Pi_{ij}\) is either absent from \(S_E\) or removed by \(S_-\), because \(j\in L\).  If a coordinate \(\Pi_{i\ell}\) in the last family of \((3)\) belongs to \(S_E\), then \(\ell\in N_O(i)\).  Comparator-parent closure gives
\[
 \ell\in\operatorname{pa}_K(C)\cap N_O(i)\subseteq L,
\]
so this coordinate too is removed by \(S_-\).  Thus the \(|C|\) edge rows indexed by \(i\to j\), \(j\in C\), vanish identically on the columns \(S\).  Since \(J_K\) has \(m+1\) parameter rows,
\[
 \operatorname{rank}J_K[S]\le m+1-|C|.
 \tag{4}
\]

We next certify the comparator rank.  In graph \(O\), specialize
\[
 t=1,qquad \lambda_{\ell i}=\epsilon\ \ (\ell\in A),
 \qquad \lambda_{ab}=0\ \ \text{for every other edge }a\to b,
 \tag{5}
\]
where \(\epsilon\ne0\) will be sufficiently small.  From \(J_O[S]\), select the following rows and columns:
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c}
 \text{rows}&\text{columns}\\ \hline
 E(O)\setminus\{\text{edges between }i\text{ and }D\}
   &S_E\setminus S_-\\
 \{\ell\to i:\ell\in A\}
   &\{\Pi_{\ell\ell}:\ell\in A\}\\
 \{t\}&\{\Pi_{j_0j_0}\}.
 \end{array}
 \tag{6}
\]
Both lists have length
\[
 (m-|D|)+|A|+1=m+1-|B|.
\]
Order the rows and columns by the three blocks in \((6)\).  Formula \((2)\) shows that the resulting square matrix has the block form
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 M(\epsilon)&0&0\\
 *&2\epsilon I_{|A|}&0\\
 *&*&1
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad M(0)=-I_{m-|D|}.
 \tag{7}
\]
Indeed, an edge row has derivative \(-1\) in its own skeleton coordinate at the zero specialization.  A row in the first block has zero derivative in an \(A\)-diagonal because the only nonzero edge coefficient with tail in \(A\) is the excluded coefficient \(\lambda_{\ell i}\).  The derivative of \(\Pi_{\ell\ell}=t(1+\sum_r\lambda_{\ell r}^2)\) with respect to \(\lambda_{\ell i}\) is \(2\epsilon\).  Finally, \(j_0\notin A\), so the selected edge rows vanish in the \(j_0\)-diagonal column and the \(t\)-row has entry one there.  It follows from \((7)\) that the determinant of this minor is
\[
 \det M(\epsilon)(2\epsilon)^{|A|},
 \tag{8}
\]
where an empty determinant and a zeroth power are interpreted as one.  Continuity and \(M(0)=-I\) make \((8)\) nonzero for every sufficiently small nonzero \(\epsilon\), including the boundary cases \(A=\varnothing\) or \(m=|D|\).  Therefore
\[
\operatorname{rank}J_O[S]\ge m+1-|B|.
\tag{9}
\]
Combining \((4)\), the assumed inequality \(|C|>|B|\), and \((9)\) gives the complete rank chain
\[
 \operatorname{rank}J_K[S]
 \le m+1-|C|
 <m+1-|B|
 \le\operatorname{rank}J_O[S].
\]

The specialization \((5)\) is an incoming star: its coefficient matrix is \(\Lambda_O=\epsilon\mathbf 1_Ae_i^\top\), and hence
\[
 \|\Lambda_O\|_{\mathrm{op}}=|\epsilon|\sqrt{|A|}.
\]
Choosing \(|\epsilon|\sqrt{|A|}<1\) gives the stated strict stability and in particular \(\det(I_p-\Lambda_O)\ne0\).  The minor determinant in \((8)\) is a nonzero polynomial in the edge coefficients and \(t\).  Its nonvanishing locus is Zariski open.  Intersecting that locus with the Euclidean-open stability region, the condition \(t>0\), and the complements of the finitely many coordinate hyperplanes corresponding to present edges gives a nonempty set: sufficiently small generic perturbations of \((5)\) lie in the intersection.  Thus the lower rank bound persists at stable exact-support parameters.

For completeness, we now pass from the rank gap to variety separation rather than defining the latter by the former.  Over a field of characteristic zero, if \(f_1,\ldots,f_q\) are rational functions, then
\[
 \operatorname{trdeg}\,\mathbb R(f_1,\ldots,f_q)
 =\dim_{\mathbb R(\theta)}\operatorname{span}\{df_1,\ldots,df_q\}.
 \tag{10}
\]
To verify \((10)\), choose a transcendence basis among the \(f_s\).  Its formal differentials are independent; every remaining \(f_s\) is algebraic over that basis, and differentiation of its separable minimal polynomial expresses \(df_s\) in their span.  Separability holds because the characteristic is zero.  Applying \((10)\) to the precision coordinates in \(S\) shows that the coordinate projection of the precision-image closure for \(K\) has dimension at most \(m+1-|C|\), whereas the corresponding projection for \(O\) has dimension at least \(m+1-|B|\).  Hence the two precision-image closures are distinct.

Finally, the structural equation and Gaussian error assumptions give, whenever \(I_p-\Lambda_G\) is invertible,
\[
 \Sigma_G=\omega(I_p-\Lambda_G)^{-\top}(I_p-\Lambda_G)^{-1},
 \qquad
 \Pi_G=\Sigma_G^{-1}=t(I_p-\Lambda_G)(I_p-\Lambda_G)^\top.
\]
Matrix inversion is a rational involution on the Zariski-open nonsingular locus.  The nonsingular model images are dense in their closures, so equality of \(\mathcal V_K^\Sigma\) and \(\mathcal V_O^\Sigma\) would imply equality of the corresponding precision-image closures after applying this involution, contradicting the preceding paragraph.  Therefore \(\mathcal V_K^\Sigma\ne\mathcal V_O^\Sigma\). \(\square\)

\textbf{Proof.}
Different labeled outdegree vectors mean that there is an \(i\in V\) for which \(d_K(i)\ne d_O(i)\).  Both graphs are simple and noncomplete by membership in \(\mathfrak G_p\).  The published outdegree-separation lemma therefore gives that \(J_K\) and \(J_O\) represent different precision-coordinate Jacobian matroids.

Let \(\mathcal W_G^\Pi\) denote the Zariski closure of the image of the polynomial precision map \(\psi_G\).  We show that the first conclusion forces \(\mathcal W_K^\Pi\ne\mathcal W_O^\Pi\).  For any coordinate set \(S\), the rank of \(J_G[S]\) over the rational-function field equals the transcendence degree of the precision coordinates indexed by \(S\).  Indeed, after choosing a transcendence basis, its differentials are independent, while differentiation of the separable minimal polynomial of every algebraic remaining coordinate places its differential in their span; characteristic zero supplies separability.  Thus this rank is determined by the polynomial relations, equivalently by the vanishing ideal of \(\mathcal W_G^\Pi\).  Equal precision varieties would have equal vanishing ideals and hence equal Jacobian matroids, contrary to the cited lemma.  Therefore
\[
 \mathcal W_K^\Pi\ne\mathcal W_O^\Pi.
 \tag{1}
\]
On the nonsingular symmetric-matrix locus, inversion \(A\mapsto A^{-1}\) is a rational involution.  By the covariance-variety and precision-map definitions, it takes every nonsingular covariance model point for \(G\) to its precision model point, with \(t=\omega^{-1}>0\), and conversely.  These model points are Zariski dense in the respective closures.  Hence equality \(\mathcal V_K^\Sigma=\mathcal V_O^\Sigma\) would, after inversion on their dense nonsingular loci and Zariski closure, imply \(\mathcal W_K^\Pi=\mathcal W_O^\Pi\), contradicting \((1)\).  Thus \(\mathcal V_K^\Sigma\ne\mathcal V_O^\Sigma\). \(\square\)

%%% FIELD proof-prop-one-tail-sanity
\textbf{Proof.}
Let \(R\) be the directed graph induced by either \(K\) or \(O\) on \(V\setminus\{i\}\).  It is common to the two graphs because every outgoing row other than the row at \(i\) is the same.  Put
\[
 P=\operatorname{ch}_K(i)\setminus\operatorname{ch}_O(i),
 \qquad
 Q=\operatorname{ch}_O(i)\setminus\operatorname{ch}_K(i).
\]
Equal outdegrees give \(|P|=|Q|\).  A perfect matching in \(\Gamma_i(K,O)\) assigns to each \(a\in P\) a distinct \(b\in Q\) that is reachable from \(a\) in \(D_i(K,O)\).  If
\[
 a=x_0\to x_1\to\cdots\to x_r=b
\]
is the supporting \(D_i(K,O)\)-path, the definition of every dependency arc gives the reversed \(K\)-edges
\[
 b=x_r\to x_{r-1}\to\cdots\to x_0=a.
 \tag{1}
\]
The dependency graph has vertex set \(V\setminus\{i\}\), so \((1)\) avoids \(i\).  Every edge of \((1)\) consequently has a tail other than \(i\), belongs to \(R\), and is also an edge of \(O\).  Since \(i\to b\) is an edge of \(O\), the \(K\)-only edge \(i\to a\) can be replaced by the \(O\)-path
\[
 i\to b\to x_{r-1}\to\cdots\to a.
 \tag{2}
\]
Common edges leaving \(i\) require no replacement, and every edge whose tail is not \(i\) already lies in \(R\).  Replacing each \(K\)-only edge in an arbitrary finite \(K\)-walk by its finite path \((2)\) therefore produces an \(O\)-walk with the same endpoints.  Hence
\[
 \operatorname{Rch}(K)\subseteq\operatorname{Rch}(O).
\]
Applying the same argument to the perfect matching in \(\Gamma_i(O,K)\) gives the reverse inclusion.  Thus \(\operatorname{Rch}(K)=\operatorname{Rch}(O)\).  Strongly connected components are precisely the equivalence classes of mutual reachability, so \(\operatorname{SCC}(K)=\operatorname{SCC}(O)\).  The closure-matching theorem additionally identifies the two perfect-matching hypotheses with the absence of a positive comparator-parent certificate in either order. \(\square\)

%%% FIELD proof-prop-outdegree-one-sanity
\textbf{Proof.}
Suppose, toward a contradiction, that every \(\Gamma_i(K,O)\) and every \(\Gamma_i(O,K)\) has a perfect matching.  Since \(K\ne O\), some outgoing row differs.  Choose such an \(i\).  Equal outdegrees and the bound \(d_K=d_O\le1\) imply that there are distinct vertices \(a,b\) with
\[
 \operatorname{ch}_K(i)=\{a\},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{ch}_O(i)=\{b\}.
 \tag{1}
\]
Thus the two parts of \(\Gamma_i(K,O)\) are \(\{a\}\) and \(\{b\}\).  A perfect matching makes \(b\) reachable from \(a\) in \(D_i(K,O)\).  Only \(a\), the sole \(K\)-child of \(i\), can have an outgoing dependency arc.  Since loops are forbidden and \(a\ne b\), the supporting path must be the single arc \(a\to b\).  The dependency-arc definition then gives the edge
\[
 b\to a\quad\text{in }K.
 \tag{2}
\]
The reverse-order perfect matching analogously gives
\[
 a\to b\quad\text{in }O.
 \tag{3}
\]
At vertex \(a\), equation \((3)\) and equal outdegrees imply that \(a\) has a unique \(K\)-child, say \(c\).  Oriented simplicity applied to \((2)\) gives \(c\ne b\), and looplessness gives \(c\ne a\).  Hence
\[
 \operatorname{ch}_K(a)=\{c\},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{ch}_O(a)=\{b\}.
\]
The assumed perfect matching in \(\Gamma_a(K,O)\), by the same one-emitter argument, forces the edge \(b\to c\) in \(K\).  But \((2)\) and the outdegree-one bound say that the unique \(K\)-child of \(b\) is \(a\).  Therefore \(c=a\), contradicting \(c\ne a\).  At least one of the stated local Hall graphs must consequently lack a perfect matching. \(\square\)

%%% FIELD proof-thm-generic-scc-recovery
\textbf{Proof.}
The structural target is \(\operatorname{SCC}(G_0)\); the observed-law functional is the minimum-dimension compatible selector \(\mathcal R\).  We prove their equality away from an explicitly described algebraic exceptional set, using the Hall-compatibility theorem as the conditional premise stated in the theorem.

First fix distinct \(G,H\in\mathcal F\) with \(\operatorname{SCC}(G)\ne\operatorname{SCC}(H)\).  If their labeled outdegree vectors differ, the unequal-outdegree separation lemma gives
\[
 \mathcal V_G^\Sigma\ne\mathcal V_H^\Sigma.
 \tag{1}
\]
If their labeled outdegree vectors agree, Hall compatibility gives an ordered choice \((K,O)\in\{(G,H),(H,G)\}\) and a vertex \(i\) with \(\Delta_i(K,O)>0\).  The closure-matching theorem then supplies \(L\in\mathcal L_i(K,O)\) such that
\[
 |\operatorname{ch}_K(i)\cap L|>
 |\operatorname{ch}_O(i)\cap L|.
\]
The incoming-star rank theorem again yields \((1)\).  Thus every pair in \(\mathcal F\) with different SCC partitions has distinct covariance varieties.

For each \(G\in\mathcal F\), let \(U_G\) be the determinant-nonzero Zariski-open subset of the affine space of its allowed edge coefficients and one scale coordinate.  This set is nonempty, because it contains \((\Lambda_G,\omega)=(0,1)\), and it is irreducible as a nonempty open subset of an affine space.  The restriction \(\omega>0\) is Zariski dense in the scale line, so the covariance variety in its definition is also the Zariski closure of the image of \(U_G\) under the rational covariance map, which is regular on \(U_G\).  If that image closure were the union of two proper closed subsets, their inverse images would cover \(U_G\).  Irreducibility would force one inverse image to equal \(U_G\), and density of the image would then force that closed subset to equal the whole image closure.  Therefore every \(\mathcal V_G^\Sigma\) is irreducible.

For the fixed true graph \(G_0\), define
\[
 \mathcal E_{G_0}
 :=\bigcup_{\substack{H\in\mathcal F:\
       \operatorname{SCC}(H)\ne\operatorname{SCC}(G_0),\\
       \dim\mathcal V_H^\Sigma\le\dim\mathcal V_{G_0}^\Sigma}}
 \left(\mathcal V_{G_0}^\Sigma\cap\mathcal V_H^\Sigma\right).
 \tag{2}
\]
Every intersection in \((2)\) is proper in \(\mathcal V_{G_0}^\Sigma\).  Indeed, if
\(\dim\mathcal V_H^\Sigma<\dim\mathcal V_{G_0}^\Sigma\), containment of the latter in the former is impossible by dimension.  If the dimensions are equal, such containment between irreducible closed varieties would force equality, contrary to \((1)\).  A finite union of proper closed subsets of an irreducible variety is proper: otherwise irreducibility, applied successively to the finite union, would force one member to equal the whole variety.  Finiteness of \(\mathcal F\) therefore makes \(\mathcal E_{G_0}\) in \((2)\) a proper algebraic subset of \(\mathcal V_{G_0}^\Sigma\).

Now take
\[
 \Sigma\in\mathcal V_{G_0}^\Sigma\setminus\mathcal E_{G_0}.
\]
The true graph \(G_0\) is compatible in the minimization defining \(G^*(\Sigma)\).  Consequently
\[
 \dim\mathcal V_{G^*(\Sigma)}^\Sigma
 \le\dim\mathcal V_{G_0}^\Sigma.
\]
If \(G^*(\Sigma)\) had an SCC partition different from that of \(G_0\), compatibility of the selected graph would place \(\Sigma\) in the corresponding intersection in \((2)\), contradicting \(\Sigma\notin\mathcal E_{G_0}\).  Hence
\[
 \mathcal R(\Sigma)=\operatorname{SCC}(G^*(\Sigma))
 =\operatorname{SCC}(G_0).
\]
The deterministic order \(\preceq\) only makes the minimizer single valued; the argument shows that every minimum-dimension compatible minimizer has the same SCC partition outside \(\mathcal E_{G_0}\). \(\square\)

%%% FIELD partial-thm-hall-compatibility
The exact closure--matching identity is proved for every ordered pair and vertex.  Moreover, two-sided perfect matchings imply \(\operatorname{Rch}(K)=\operatorname{Rch}(O)\) when only one outgoing row differs, and distinct equal-degree graphs with maximum outdegree at most one always have a positive Hall deficiency in one order.  For a general supporting dependency path \(a=x_0\to\cdots\to x_r=b\), the reversed \(K\)-path is \(b=x_r\to\cdots\to x_0=a\); every tail \(x_s\) with \(s<r\) after the first reversed edge is a \(K\)-child of the original tail \(i\).  Thus every dependency on a changed edge except possibly the first edge of that reversed path descends along a \(K\)-edge from \(i\); the exceptional first edge has tail \(b\), for which only the changed \(O\)-edge \(i\to b\) is known.

%%% FIELD partial-oeq-dependency-contraction
Dependency cycles whose supporting paths use only edges common to \(K\) and \(O\) admit direct path substitution and preserve the entire reachability relation.  More generally, all noninitial changed-edge dependencies on a supporting reversed \(K\)-path have tails in \(\operatorname{ch}_K(i)\), while the initial dependency may have the comparator-only child \(b\) as its tail.  The one-tail and maximum-outdegree-one propositions close the corresponding special regimes, but no proof presently forces a mixed cycle containing such initial dependencies into a common strongly connected component, and no universal alternative precision separator has been derived.
