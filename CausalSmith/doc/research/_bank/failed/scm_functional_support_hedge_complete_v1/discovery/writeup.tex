\section*{scm\_\allowbreak{}functional\_\allowbreak{}support\_\allowbreak{}hedge\_\allowbreak{}complete}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} This note formulates, but does not construct, a bounded support-aware grammar for FID-S. The intended procedure concerns finite latent DAGs with supplied observed alphabets, qualitative functional nodes, and declared positive or zero observed events, and would return an inconsistency certificate, a guarded identifying formula, or an exact-support rational separating pair. The proved classical result is conditional and abstract: any restricted grammar instance satisfying the local seven-case interface reproduces ordinary ID; no such concrete FID-S grammar instance is constructed here. The fully observed completion frontier is proved: for each obstruction and each integer \(r\geq 3\), an existentially selected common-law fiber has target gap at least \(1-2/r\), primitive denominator at most \(r|V|\max_{i\in V}|\Omega_i|\), and the stated logarithmic primitive-bit bound. The case \(r=8\) gives the \(3/4\)-gap corollary, but no constant-gap claim is made for every fixed observational law on the same support. The supported-bow pattern is resolved directly by its proposition, thereby furnishing a mandatory regression instance for any future support trichotomy. The general support-preserving independent-source latent lift, rational equal-law witness theorem, converse formula reconstruction, concrete finite grammar, certificate syntax, and global search bounds remain open.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Ordinary hedge completeness ignores declared structural-zero faces. Chen--Darwiche Theorem 15 gives functional-variable elimination under stated separability and parent conditions, and Theorem 18 assumes the treatment-marginal positivity set \(C_V=\{\Pr(X)>0:X\text{ is a treatment variable}\}\); neither theorem identifies the arbitrary declared zero-and-positive support-literal regime introduced here. Chen--Darwiche's 2026 Theorems 1--2 are sound formula-returning algorithms for state-based effects under context-specific independences and state-alphabet constraints with a strictly positive observational law, not a complete formula-or-exact-support-countermodel procedure for valid intervention states on structural-zero faces. Kivva, Mokhtarian, Etesami, and Kiyavash prove sound-and-complete gID under positivity of the observational law, not on arbitrary declared support faces, and do not supply exact-support rational paired witnesses. Tikka--Hyttinen--Karvanen Theorem 5 supplies a terminating sound CSI search, and Theorem 6 shows why contextwise answers do not give completeness.

\paragraph{Niche.} Finite categorical latent SCMs with explicit observed alphabets admit combinatorial support certificates and canonical response coordinates, but existing tools do not coordinate them with query-specific hedge traces, exact witness lifting that preserves the latent-source architecture of \(G\), and auditable rational serialization.

\paragraph{Fill.} If the latent-lifting OEQ is resolved, the resulting FID-S procedure will preserve \(\Omega\), exact support, functional-row provenance, and the latent-source architecture and will return the stated three certificate types. What is proved now is only the universal conditional compiler theorem for an abstract instance satisfying the local classical-ID interface. Separately, the fully observed frontier has a parameterized rational smoothing certificate: for every integer \(r\geq3\), each completion obstruction admits an existentially chosen same-law exact-support pair with gap at least \(1-2/r\) and primitive bit cost growing as \(O(\log r)\); this does not construct or settle the latent grammar.

\section{Related work}

Shpitser and Pearl supply the recursive seven-case ID algorithm, its soundness and completeness results, and the hedge results with their proper failed-subquery and existential original-query scopes. The only containment established in this note is the conditional full-support, \(W=\varnothing\) embedding of classical ID: every abstract restricted grammar satisfying the local seven-case interface has the same call tree and normalized successful expression, but no concrete FID-S instance of that interface is constructed. The comparison papers do not supply the complete exact-support rational-countermodel trichotomy requested by the latent-lifting OEQ, and this note does not yet supply it either. Chen--Darwiche's functional-elimination, treatment-positivity, constrained-identifiability, and strictly positive state-based results are used only for theorem-specific premise separations or informed-by comparisons; they do not establish a global inclusion between their regimes and the arbitrary support-literal regime here. The same is true of the cited context-specific, generalized-identification, possibilistic-compatibility, latent-cardinality, and response-coordinate results: they provide non-subsumption facts, uncovered residual regimes, or ingredients, not global class inclusions. The supported-bow pattern is resolved directly by the dedicated proposition, thereby furnishing a mandatory regression instance for any future support trichotomy. The support-preserving independent-source rational lift remains explicitly open.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:independent-roots]\label{ass:independent-roots}
The exogenous law factorizes as \(P_M(U)=\prod_{u\in U}P_M(u)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Independent-exogenous-source SCM factorization, \cite{ShpitserPearl2008}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:functional-nodes]\label{ass:functional-nodes}
For every \(w\in W\), the conditional mechanism at \(w\) is a point mass for every joint value of its observed and latent parents.
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (Qualitative functional dependency, \cite{ChenDarwiche2024}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:support-literals]\label{ass:support-literals}
The observed law satisfies every literal in \(\Pi\), written \(P_M(V)\models\Pi\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Novel.} Declared positive and structural-zero events are auditable inputs in finite systems with protocol-impossible states, deterministic routing, or registry-enforced exclusions. This input may contain arbitrary marginal or joint zeros; it is distinct from Chen--Darwiche Theorem 15's separability and parent conditions and from Theorem 18's treatment-marginal positivity set \(C_V=\{\Pr(X)>0:X\text{ is a treatment variable}\}\).
\end{assumption}

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:support-oracle]\label{lem:support-oracle}
For every fixed finite response skeleton over the supplied observed alphabets \(\Omega\), consistency of \(\Pi\) and every support entailment are decidable by finite possible-world relation enumeration. Every feasible single-model support stratum contains a rational independent-root model obtained by assigning uniform mass on each selected primitive root or table-row support; each primitive probability is stored as a separate reduced fraction with denominator at most the size of that support, and no common denominator is asserted.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix the finite alphabets belonging to the response skeleton, including a finite alphabet \(\Lambda_u\) for each \(u\in U\).  A primitive support pattern consists of a nonempty set \(A_u\subseteq\Lambda_u\) for each root and, for every observed conditional-table row \((i,a)\), a nonempty set \(A_i(a)\subseteq\Omega_i\).  By \(\mathrm{ass:functional-nodes}\), the latter set is required to be a singleton when \(i\in W\).  There are finitely many such patterns.

For a pattern \(A\), define its possible-world relation by
\[
 R_A=\left\{(u,v):u_r\in A_r\text{ for every }r\in U,
 \quad v_i\in A_i\!\left((u,v)_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)}\right)
 \text{ for every }i\in V\right\},
\]
and let \(S_A\) be its projection onto the observed coordinates.  In any model having precisely these primitive supports, the causal Bayesian-network factorization and \(\mathrm{ass:independent-roots}\) give
\[
 P_M(u,v)=\prod_{r\in U}P_M(u_r)
             \prod_{i\in V}P_M\!\left(v_i\mid
             (u,v)_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)}\right).
\]
Every factor is nonnegative, and the product is positive exactly when \((u,v)\in R_A\).  Consequently
\[
 P_M(V=v)>0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \sum_{u}P_M(u,v)>0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \text{there is }u\text{ with }(u,v)\in R_A
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad v\in S_A.
\]
More generally, for every observed cylinder event \(E\),
\[
 P_M(E)>0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad S_A\cap E\ne\varnothing,
 \qquad
 P_M(E)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad S_A\cap E=\varnothing.
\]
Thus the truth of every literal in \(\Pi\), and of every proposed support consequence, is determined by a finite relation calculation.  Enumerating the finitely many primitive support patterns decides whether at least one pattern satisfies \(\Pi\); an asserted support consequence is decided by checking it on every pattern satisfying \(\Pi\).  This proves the decision assertion using \(\mathrm{ass:support-literals}\) and \(\mathrm{def:exact-support}\).

It remains to show that a combinatorially feasible pattern is probabilistically realizable without losing root independence.  Put
\[
 P_M(u_r=c)=\frac{1}{|A_r|}\mathbf 1\{c\in A_r\},
 \qquad
 P_M(i=b\mid\operatorname{pa}_G(i)=a)
 =\frac{1}{|A_i(a)|}\mathbf 1\{b\in A_i(a)\}.
\]
These are normalized rational probability vectors.  Their root part is a product by construction, every row at \(W\) is a point mass, and the preceding positivity equivalence shows that the resulting exact observed support is \(S_A\).  Hence every literal selected by the finite relation calculation is satisfied, and each nonzero primitive probability is stored separately as the reduced fraction obtained from \(1/|A_r|\) or \(1/|A_i(a)|\), whose denominator is at most the cardinality of its selected support.  No common denominator is used.  This proves the claimed rational realization for every feasible single-model support stratum.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This separates genuine inconsistency from identification and supplies the finite support front end. \textit{Gap.} Fraser proves possibilistic compatibility by possible worlds, but does not impose named functional nodes while retaining a causal query and an independent-root probabilistic realization. \textit{Consumer.} A support-aware causal-analysis backend can reject contradictory structural-zero and functional declarations before attempting effect identification.

\begin{theorem}[thm:fully-observed-frontier]\label{thm:fully-observed-frontier}
If \(U=\varnothing\) and \(\mathfrak M(G,\Omega,W,\Pi)\ne\varnothing\), then \(q\) is identified if and only if no exact support \(S\) realized by a member of \(\mathfrak M(G,\Omega,W,\Pi)\) admits a fully observed completion obstruction. Agreement yields a support-valid guarded g-formula. If a realized exact support \(S\) admits a completion obstruction, then for every integer \(r\geq 3\) there exist compatible rational models \(M_1^{(r)},M_2^{(r)}\) with identical observed laws and exact support \(S\) such that \(\lvert q(M_1^{(r)})-q(M_2^{(r)})\rvert\geq 1-2/r>0\). For this existentially constructed pair, every primitive observed-node conditional-table probability is encoded separately as a reduced fraction with denominator at most \(r|V|\max_{i\in V}|\Omega_i|\), and its numerator-denominator pair has length at most \(2\lceil\log_2(r|V|\max_{i\in V}|\Omega_i|)\rceil+2\) bits. No common denominator is required, and induced cells are exact sum-product evaluations of those fractions. These witnesses existentially select a common observational fiber for each obstruction; the theorem does not claim the same gap on every fixed same-support observational law. Taking \(r=8\) gives a \(3/4\)-gap witness with denominator bound \(8|V|\max_{i\in V}|\Omega_i|\), and the certified gap approaches one at an additional \(O(\log r)\) primitive-bit cost.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Assume \(U=\varnothing\), put \(n=|V|\), and put
\[
 m=\max_{i\in V}|\Omega_i|.
\]
The finiteness and nonemptiness conditions on \(V\) and the observed alphabets give \(n\geq 1\) and \(m\geq 1\).  Identification here means the following property of the causal target, rather than a definition of that target:
\[
 M_1,M_2\in\mathfrak M(G,\Omega,W,\Pi),\quad
 P_{M_1}(V)=P_{M_2}(V)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad q(M_1)=q(M_2).
 \tag{1}
\]
We derive both directions of the asserted criterion and then construct the observed-law functional.

Fix an exact support \(S\) realized by a member of \(\mathfrak M(G,\Omega,W,\Pi)\).  For \(i\in V\) and a joint assignment \(a\) to \(\operatorname{pa}_G(i)\), define
\[
 A_i^S(a)=\bigl\{b\in\Omega_i:\text{ there is }v\in S
 \text{ with }v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)}=a\text{ and }v_i=b\bigr\}.
 \tag{2}
\]
Call the row \((i,a)\) reached when \(A_i^S(a)\neq\varnothing\).  If \(M\) has exact support \(S\), then every reached row is determined by the observed law:
\[
 P_M(i=b\mid \operatorname{pa}_G(i)=a)
 =\frac{P_M(i=b,\operatorname{pa}_G(i)=a)}
 {P_M(\operatorname{pa}_G(i)=a)}.
 \tag{3}
\]
Indeed, the denominator is positive because (2) supplies a state in \(S\) with parent assignment \(a\), and exact support means that this state has positive probability.  The positive entries of the row in (3) are exactly those indexed by \(A_i^S(a)\).  If \(i\in W\), the functional-node assumption makes the row a point mass, so every reached row of such a vertex satisfies \(|A_i^S(a)|=1\).  An unreached row has zero probability of being invoked under the observational law.

We first establish a support identity.  Define
\[
 J(S)=\left\{v:\text{ for every }i\in V,\quad
 A_i^S(v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)})=\varnothing
 \text{ or }v_i\in A_i^S(v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)})\right\}.
 \tag{4}
\]
For every fully observed Bayesian-network law having exact support \(S\),
\[
 S=J(S).
 \tag{5}
\]
The inclusion \(S\subseteq J(S)\) follows from (2).  For the reverse inclusion, take a topological order \(i_1,\ldots,i_n\) of the acyclic graph and fix \(v\in J(S)\).  Suppose inductively that the conditional factors corresponding to the first \(k-1\) coordinates of \(v\) are positive.  This initial topological segment is ancestral.  Summing the DAG factorization over later variables and normalizing their conditional rows in reverse topological order shows that the probability of the specified initial segment is the product of those positive factors.  It is therefore positive, so some state in \(S\) extends that segment.  All parents of \(i_k\) occur earlier in the order, whence \((i_k,v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i_k)})\) is reached.  Membership of \(v\) in \(J(S)\), followed by (3), makes the \(i_k\)-factor positive.  The induction starts at the empty segment.  At \(k=n\), the DAG factorization gives \(P_M(V=v)>0\), so \(v\in S\), proving (5).

It follows that two collections of conditional tables which have identical numerical entries at every reached row, positive support exactly \(A_i^S(a)\) on each such row, and arbitrary normalized fills on unreached rows induce the same observed law and retain exact support \(S\).  To see this directly, every \(v\in S\) uses only the common reached-row entries.  If \(v\notin S=J(S)\), the negation of (4) supplies an \(i\) for which \(A_i^S(v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)})\neq\varnothing\) but \(v_i\notin A_i^S(v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)})\); the factor at that reached row is zero in both collections.  This argument requires no positivity at an unreached row and no global positivity.  The finite enumeration in \(\mathrm{lem:support-oracle}\) decides which supports are realizable and all support entailments needed to identify the reached rows.

Now suppose that the realized support \(S\) admits a completion obstruction.  By \(\mathrm{def:completion-obstruction}\), there are deterministic complete tables \(t_1,t_2\) which select an allowed value at each reached row, agree at every such row, satisfy all point-mass restrictions at \(W\), and obey
\[
 e_j=\mathbf 1\{Y_x(t_j)=y\},\qquad |e_1-e_2|=1.
 \tag{6}
\]
For a reached row \(o=(i,a)\), denote their common selected value by \(b_o\in A_i^S(a)\), and put \(k_o=|A_i^S(a)|\).  Fix an integer \(r\geq3\) and set
\[
 \varepsilon_r=\frac{1}{rn}.
 \tag{7}
\]
At each reached row use, in both models, the conditional distribution
\[
 \theta_{o,r}(b)
 =(1-\varepsilon_r)\mathbf 1\{b=b_o\}
 +\frac{\varepsilon_r}{k_o}\mathbf 1\{b\in A_i^S(a)\}.
 \tag{8}
\]
At every unreached row, let model \(M_j^{(r)}\) use the corresponding deterministic row of \(t_j\).  The row in (8) is normalized and has positive support exactly \(A_i^S(a)\).  If \(i\in W\), then \(k_o=1\), so (8) is a point mass; unreached rows at \(W\) are point masses by construction.  Thus the functional-node assumption is preserved.  The independent-root assumption is vacuous because \(U=\varnothing\).

The consequence of (5) just proved shows that \(M_1^{(r)}\) and \(M_2^{(r)}\) have identical observed laws and exact support \(S\).  For any event literal in \(\Pi\), positivity is equivalent to the corresponding cylinder meeting \(S\), while probability zero is equivalent to that cylinder being disjoint from \(S\).  Since \(S\) was realized by a member satisfying \(\Pi\), both constructed models satisfy every support literal.  Therefore
\[
 M_1^{(r)},M_2^{(r)}\in\mathfrak M(G,\Omega,W,\Pi),\qquad
 P_{M_1^{(r)}}(V)=P_{M_2^{(r)}}(V),\qquad
 \operatorname{supp}_V(M_j^{(r)})=S.
 \tag{9}
\]

Couple an interventional execution of \(M_j^{(r)}\) to the deterministic execution of \(t_j\).  At an encountered reached row, generate (8) by retaining \(b_o\) with probability \(1-\varepsilon_r\) and otherwise drawing uniformly from \(A_i^S(a)\).  Conditional on every preceding value, the probability that the generated value differs from the deterministic baseline is
\[
 \varepsilon_r\left(1-\frac{1}{k_o}\right)\leq\varepsilon_r.
 \tag{10}
\]
At an encountered unreached row the two executions agree surely.  At most \(n\) vertices are evaluated, even though which rows are encountered may depend on earlier draws.  Applying the union bound to the resulting adaptively defined deviation events, using the conditional bound (10) at each step, gives
\[
 \Pr(\text{some evaluated output differs from the baseline})
 \leq n\varepsilon_r=\frac1r.
 \tag{11}
\]
On the complement of this event the interventional outcome agrees with the deterministic execution.  Hence, for \(j=1,2\),
\[
 |q(M_j^{(r)})-e_j|\leq\frac1r.
 \tag{12}
\]
Equations (6) and (12), together with the triangle inequality, yield
\[
 |q(M_1^{(r)})-q(M_2^{(r)})|
 \geq 1-\frac2r>0.
 \tag{13}
\]
Thus an obstruction contradicts (1).

The arithmetic guarantees follow from the same construction.  At a reached row with \(k=k_o\), a nonselected allowed value has probability \(1/(rnk)\), while the selected value has probability
\[
 1-\frac1{rn}+\frac1{rnk}
 =\frac{rnk-k+1}{rnk}.
 \tag{14}
\]
All remaining primitive entries are zero or one.  Reducing every primitive fraction separately can only decrease its denominator, so each denominator is at most
\[
 rnk\leq rnm=:D_r.
 \tag{15}
\]
Both the numerator and denominator of every reduced primitive fraction are at most \(D_r\).  With the usual binary encoding, their combined length is therefore at most
\[
 2\left\lceil\log_2D_r\right\rceil+2
 =2\left\lceil\log_2\!\left(r|V|\max_{i\in V}|\Omega_i|\right)\right\rceil+2.
 \tag{16}
\]
No common denominator has been introduced: every induced observational or interventional cell is the exact finite sum of products prescribed by the relevant DAG factorization.  The quantifiers are also explicit.  For each obstruction and each \(r\geq3\), (8) existentially selects a common observational fiber; nothing here asserts the same gap for every fixed law of support \(S\).  Setting \(r=8\) in (13)--(16) gives the \(3/4\)-gap and denominator bound \(8|V|\max_i|\Omega_i|\).  For fixed input, increasing \(r\) makes the certified gap tend to one and adds only \(O(\log r)\) bits to the primitive encoding bound.

For the converse, assume that no exact support realized in the model class admits a completion obstruction.  Fix \(M\in\mathfrak M(G,\Omega,W,\Pi)\), write \(P=P_M(V)\), and let \(S=\operatorname{supp}_V(M)\).  Independently for every conditional-table row of \(M\), draw one output according to that row's probability vector.  The resulting random object \(T\) is a deterministic complete table; at vertices in \(W\), it respects the point-mass restriction by the functional-node assumption.  In one acyclic execution under \(\operatorname{do}(X=x)\), each nonintervened vertex invokes exactly one row.  Independence of the row draws and finite expansion over assignments give
\[
 \Pr\{Y_x(T)=y\}
 =\sum_{v:\,v_X=x,\,v_Y=y}
 \prod_{i\notin X}P_M(v_i\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)})
 =P_{M,x}(Y=y)=q(M).
 \tag{17}
\]
The middle expression is the truncated DAG factorization obtained by replacing the mechanisms at \(X\) by the point mass at \(x\); this derives its equality with the causal target rather than defining that equality.

Condition on all outputs of \(T\) at reached rows.  Every such output lies in the corresponding set \(A_i^S(a)\).  If two permitted choices of the unreached rows produced different values of \(\mathbf 1\{Y_x(T)=y\}\), the resulting deterministic tables would agree at every reached row, respect the restrictions at \(W\), and have unequal interventional indicators.  They would therefore be a completion obstruction, contrary to hypothesis.  Consequently, for each fixed reached-row realization, the indicator is constant over all deterministic fills of the unreached rows.

Suppose \(M'\in\mathfrak M(G,\Omega,W,\Pi)\) has the same observed law \(P\).  It then has the same exact support \(S\), and (3) shows that every reached conditional row of \(M'\) equals the corresponding row of \(M\).  Hence the reached-row draws have the same joint distribution in the two random-table representations.  The preceding conditional constancy eliminates all dependence on their possibly different unreached rows.  Averaging the same constants in (17) gives
\[
 q(M')=q(M).
 \tag{18}
\]
This proves (1) when no realized support has an obstruction.

It remains to exhibit the claimed identifying map.  For each realizable exact support \(S\), choose once and for all a value \(h_S^0(i,a)\in\Omega_i\) at every unreached row; this specifies a point mass there, including at every such row belonging to a vertex in \(W\).  On the branch \(\operatorname{supp}(P)=S\), define
\[
 \kappa_{i,S}(b\mid a;P)=
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle
 \frac{P(i=b,\operatorname{pa}_G(i)=a)}
 {P(\operatorname{pa}_G(i)=a)},
 &A_i^S(a)\neq\varnothing,\\[1.2ex]
 \mathbf 1\{b=h_S^0(i,a)\},
 &A_i^S(a)=\varnothing,
 \end{cases}
 \tag{19}
\]
and
\[
 F_S(P)=
 \sum_{v:\,v_X=x,\,v_Y=y}
 \prod_{i\notin X}
 \kappa_{i,S}(v_i\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)};P).
 \tag{20}
\]
Every denominator on the first branch of (19) is positive by its exact-support guard and (2); the second branch contains no division.  Put \(F_S=0\) on an unrealizable branch.  The observed product alphabet is finite, so there are only finitely many branches, and \(\mathrm{lem:support-oracle}\) decides their realizability.  Thus (19)--(20) are a support-valid guarded formula in the sense of \(\mathrm{def:guarded-formula}\).

For a model \(M\) with support \(S\), (3) says that the reached rows in (19) are its actual rows.  Equation (17), followed by the proved invariance under every choice of unreached-row fill, permits those actual unreached rows to be replaced by \(h_S^0\).  Therefore
\[
 q(M)=F_{\operatorname{supp}_V(M)}(P_M(V)).
 \tag{21}
\]
This proves the guarded-formula direction and completes the equivalence.

For a smallest exact check of the separating construction, take binary observed vertices \(A\to B\), \(X=\{A\}\), \(Y=\{B\}\), \(x=1\), \(y=1\), and \(S=\{(0,0)\}\).  Two completions agreeing on the reached rows and setting the unreached row \(B\mid A=1\) respectively to zero and one have the same point-mass observed law and targets zero and one; (8) leaves the singleton reached-row supports unchanged.  The causally sufficient premise is load-bearing: if a binary root \(U\) with probability one half is added with arrows \(U\to A\), \(U\to B\), and \(A\to B\), the deterministic models \(A=U,B=A\) and \(A=U,B=U\) share the observed law \(P(A=B=0)=P(A=B=1)=1/2\), but under \(\operatorname{do}(A=0)\) their probabilities of \(B=1\) are respectively zero and one half.  Thus dropping \(U=\varnothing\) invalidates the reached-row reconstruction on which (3), (19), and the theorem depend.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the exact causally sufficient base case and fixes the support and arithmetic invariants that any latent lifting theorem must preserve. \textit{Gap.} Classical g-formula arguments assume access to the needed rows, whereas functional elimination results do not give this row-completion equivalence under arbitrary support literals. \textit{Consumer.} The theorem gives a complete structural-zero decision rule for causally sufficient discrete Bayesian networks and a regression test for the latent algorithm.

\begin{openendedquestion}[oeq:latent-lifting]\label{oeq:latent-lifting}
Can the partial-trace handle be completed into a bounded finite-production grammar \(\mathcal H_{\mathrm{FS}}\) such that every residual failure contains a forest-supported local response obstruction that extends to two models \(M_1,M_2\in\mathfrak M(G,\Omega,W,\Pi)\) satisfying \(P_{M_1}(V)=P_{M_2}(V)\), \(\operatorname{supp}_V(M_1)=\operatorname{supp}_V(M_2)\), and witness-dependent target separation \(\Delta=|q(M_1)-q(M_2)|>0\), while preserving the supplied observed alphabets \(\Omega\) and the latent-source architecture of \(G\): each \(M_j\) uses exactly the roots \(U\) with the parent incidences of \(G\), may use finite model-specific latent alphabets bounded by \(H(L)\), and separately satisfies \(P_{M_j}(U)=\prod_{u\in U}P_{M_j}(u)\), without requiring \(M_1\) and \(M_2\) to share root masses? Can absence of an obstruction reconstruct a support-valid guarded formula, and can exhaustive search of the grammar terminate within an explicit bound and return exactly one terminal certificate on every input: a support contradiction when \(\mathfrak M(G,\Omega,W,\Pi)=\varnothing\), and otherwise either a guarded formula derivation or a NONID certificate?

The completed grammar must obey the following classical-ID embedding invariant under exactly the restriction \(W=\varnothing\) and the condition that \(\Pi\) entails \(P_M(V=v)>0\) for every \(v\in\prod_{i\in V}\Omega_i\). Write \(\mathcal G\) for the latent projection of \(G\) onto \(V\). On this restricted branch, every grammar state must record a Shpitser--Pearl recursive call \(\operatorname{ID}(Y_c,X_c,Q_c,\mathcal G_c)\) together with provenance \(\rho_c\) from that call to the original query \(P_x(y)\). No support-specific or functional production may fire. With \(V_c\) the vertex set of \(\mathcal G_c\), \(\operatorname{An}_{\mathcal G_c}(Y_c)\) the ancestors of \(Y_c\), and \(\mathcal C(\mathcal K)\) the districts of an ADMG \(\mathcal K\), grammar productions must follow the priority and side conditions of the following seven ordered cases:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
(1)&X_c=\varnothing:\quad \displaystyle\sum_{V_c\setminus Y_c}Q_c;\\[2mm]
(2)&A_c:=\operatorname{An}_{\mathcal G_c}(Y_c)\ne V_c:\quad
 \operatorname{ID}\!\left(Y_c,X_c\cap A_c,
 \displaystyle\sum_{V_c\setminus A_c}Q_c,\mathcal G_c[A_c]\right);\\[2mm]
(3)&Z_c:=(V_c\setminus X_c)\setminus
 \operatorname{An}_{(\mathcal G_c)_{\overline{X_c}}}(Y_c)\ne\varnothing:\quad
 \operatorname{ID}(Y_c,X_c\cup Z_c,Q_c,\mathcal G_c);\\[2mm]
(4)&\mathcal C(\mathcal G_c[V_c\setminus X_c])=\{S_1,\ldots,S_k\},\ k\ge2:\quad
 \displaystyle\sum_{V_c\setminus(Y_c\cup X_c)}
 \prod_{j=1}^k\operatorname{ID}(S_j,V_c\setminus S_j,Q_c,\mathcal G_c);\\[2mm]
(5)&\mathcal C(\mathcal G_c[V_c\setminus X_c])=\{S\}
 \ \text{and}\ \mathcal C(\mathcal G_c)=\{\mathcal G_c\}:\quad
 \operatorname{FAIL}(\mathcal G_c,\mathcal G_c[S]);\\[2mm]
(6)&\mathcal C(\mathcal G_c[V_c\setminus X_c])=\{S\}
 \ \text{and}\ \mathcal G_c[S]\in\mathcal C(\mathcal G_c):\quad
 \displaystyle\sum_{S\setminus Y_c}\prod_{i:V_i\in S}
 Q_c(V_i\mid V_c^{(i-1)});\\[2mm]
(7)&\mathcal C(\mathcal G_c[V_c\setminus X_c])=\{S\}
 \ \text{and}\ \mathcal G_c[S]\subsetneq\mathcal G_c[S']
 \ \text{for the unique}\ \mathcal G_c[S']\in\mathcal C(\mathcal G_c):\quad
 \operatorname{ID}\!\left(Y_c,X_c\cap S',
 \displaystyle\prod_{i:V_i\in S'}Q_c(V_i\mid V_c^{(i-1)}),
 \mathcal G_c[S']\right).
\end{array}
\]
Here \(V_1,\ldots,V_{|V_c|}\) is a topological ordering used by the ordinary ID call, and a displayed conditional denotes the corresponding ordered-kernel ratio. The positivity condition above must discharge every denominator guard. The state relation must be a trace-preserving bisimulation: each restricted grammar transition projects to the uniquely applicable case under this ordering, and each ordinary ID transition has the matching grammar production with updated provenance. At every successful terminal, the grammar compiler must reproduce the ordinary ID expression up to explicitly checked algebraic normalization. At a case \((5)\) terminal, the payload must contain the actual failing recursive call, the pair \(\operatorname{FAIL}(\mathcal G_c,\mathcal G_c[S])\), and \(\rho_c\). The payload itself must certify that the pair contains the relevant hedge for the current failed query or subquery; Shpitser--Pearl Theorem 17 then certifies nonidentification of that query or subquery. The conclusion transported to the original query must have exactly the scope of their Theorem 19: there exist \(Y'\subseteq Y\) and \(X'\subseteq X\) for which the subquery \(P(Y'\mid\operatorname{do}(X'))\) has the relevant hedge. It must not be labeled a hedge for the full original query without that additional conclusion.

The grammar must specify the finite syntax of each terminal certificate. Conditional on that completed syntax and rule set, it must make rule recognition decidable: support contradictions are checked by finite support enumeration; guarded derivations are checked production by production with every positivity guard and compiled to an observable expression; and NONID certificates are checked by exact rational arithmetic for normalization, graph and functional-row compatibility, satisfaction of \(\Pi\), equality of all observed cells and exact support, unequal targets, preservation of the latent-source architecture, and the encoding bounds in \(\mathrm{def:rational-certificate-encoding}\). The result must derive explicit functions \(H(L)\) and \(B_{\mathrm{rat}}(L)\), an explicit search-state bound in \(L\), and explicit computable bit-operation and output-size bounds in \(L\) and \(\ell(C)\) for checking a supplied terminal certificate. It must resolve \(\mathcal B_0\), strictly extend ordinary hedge completeness, go beyond Chen--Darwiche Theorem 15 functional-variable elimination under its stated separability and parent conditions, and admit declared support faces outside the Theorem 18 treatment-marginal positivity set \(C_V=\{\Pr(X)>0:X\text{ is a treatment variable}\}\). It must also strictly cover the residual regime outside Chen--Darwiche's 2026 Theorems 1--2: arbitrary declared zero-and-positive event literals and qualitative functional nodes, rather than only their sound identification under context-specific independences and state-alphabet constraints with a strictly positive observational law. For each existentially constructed rational witness pair, every rational pair must use the primitive, individual-denominator encoding of \(\mathrm{def:rational-certificate-encoding}\), including its displayed induced-cell and total serialized bit-size bounds; no instance-uniform lower bound on \(\Delta\) is asserted.
\end{openendedquestion}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This is the theorem-level kernel: besides the exact-support independent-source lift and converse formula reconstruction, an affirmative solution must recover ordinary ID by a trace-preserving embedding of its ordered recursive calls on the nonfunctional full-support branch. \textit{Gap.} The frozen handles provide neither grammar productions nor a compiler. The single-model support oracle does not preserve the polynomial equal-law constraints between two products of latent root simplices, so it cannot supply the required rational paired lift. Moreover, final-answer agreement with ordinary ID would not establish the mandated transition-level bisimulation or the corrected failed-subquery hedge provenance. \textit{Consumer.} Resolving the question yields auditable formula, contradiction, or countermodel certificates while guaranteeing that existing ordinary-ID behavior, including failure provenance, is preserved exactly on its native branch.

\begin{proposition}[prop:classical-reduction]\label{prop:classical-reduction}
When \(W=\varnothing\) and \(\Pi\) entails strict positivity of every observed cell over \(\prod_{i\in V}\Omega_i\), the classical reduction is a deferred derived consequence of an affirmative resolution of \(\mathrm{oeq:latent-lifting}\), not a standalone consequence of the present construction handles. Once that resolution supplies concrete productions and a compiler satisfying the classical-ID embedding invariant, induction on the bisimilar ordinary \(\operatorname{ID}\) call tree proves that the restricted \(\operatorname{FID\text{-}S}(G,\Omega,W,\Pi,x,y)\) trace uses the same ordered cases and side conditions, and that its successful compiled expression equals the ordinary ID functional up to explicit algebraic normalization, with every guard discharged by strict positivity. If the trace terminates in case \((5)\), its payload contains the actual failed recursive call, \(\operatorname{FAIL}(\mathcal G_c,\mathcal G_c[S])\), and its original-query provenance; the payload certifies a hedge for that failed query or subquery, Shpitser--Pearl Theorem 17 certifies nonidentification there, and the conclusion for the original query is only that there exist \(Y'\subseteq Y\) and \(X'\subseteq X\) such that \(P(Y'\mid\operatorname{do}(X'))\) has the relevant hedge.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix the restricted branch \(W=\varnothing\), and suppose that \(\Pi\) entails
\[
 P_M(V=v)>0
 \qquad\text{for every }v\in\prod_{i\in V}\Omega_i
 \tag{1}
\]
for every compatible model \(M\).  Let \(\mathcal G\) be the latent projection of \(G\) onto \(V\), and let
\[
 c_0:=\operatorname{ID}(Y,X,P_M(V),\mathcal G)
\]
be the initial ordinary-ID call.  We prove the proposition as a universal conditional statement; in particular, the argument does not construct the grammar requested by \(\mathrm{oeq:latent\text{-}lifting}\).

Let
\[
 \mathfrak A=(\Sigma,\Rightarrow,\operatorname{Comp},\iota,\rho)
\]
be any restricted grammar instance satisfying the local classical-ID interface stipulated in \(\mathrm{oeq:latent\text{-}lifting}\).  Here \(\Sigma\) is the state set, \(\Rightarrow\) is the production relation, \(\operatorname{Comp}\) is the successful compiler, and
\[
 \iota(\sigma)=c(\sigma)
   =\operatorname{ID}(Y_c,X_c,Q_c,\mathcal G_c)
\]
embeds a grammar state into an ordinary-ID call.  The map \(\rho(\sigma)\) records the provenance of that call in \(c_0\).  The local interface gives an initial state \(\sigma_0\) satisfying \(\iota(\sigma_0)=c_0\) and having identity provenance.  At every state, exactly the production matching the first applicable one of the seven ordered ID cases is available, with the identical side condition.  Cases \((2)\), \((3)\), and \((7)\) have their specified unique child, case \((4)\) has one child for each district, and cases \((1)\), \((5)\), and \((6)\) are terminal.  No support-specific or functional production is available.  If \(\sigma'\) is a recursive child and \(\kappa_{c\leftarrow c'}\) is the ordinary-call provenance from the child call to its parent, then
\[
 \rho(\sigma')=\rho(\sigma)\circ\kappa_{c\leftarrow c'}.
 \tag{2}
\]
Failures propagate through recursive productions without altering the case-\((5)\) payload, apart from the provenance composition in \((2)\).

For a successful subtree, the local compiler equations are as follows.  In case \((1)\),
\[
 \operatorname{Comp}(\sigma)=\sum_{V_c\setminus Y_c}Q_c.
 \tag{3}
\]
In case \((2)\), if \(\sigma_2\) embeds
\[
 \operatorname{ID}\!\left(
 Y_c,X_c\cap A_c,
 \sum_{V_c\setminus A_c}Q_c,
 \mathcal G_c[A_c]
 \right),
\]
then
\[
 \operatorname{Comp}(\sigma)=\operatorname{Comp}(\sigma_2).
 \tag{4}
\]
In case \((3)\), if \(\sigma_3\) embeds
\(\operatorname{ID}(Y_c,X_c\cup Z_c,Q_c,\mathcal G_c)\), then
\[
 \operatorname{Comp}(\sigma)=\operatorname{Comp}(\sigma_3).
 \tag{5}
\]
In case \((4)\), let \(S_1,\ldots,S_k\) be the residual districts and let \(\sigma_{4,j}\) embed
\(\operatorname{ID}(S_j,V_c\setminus S_j,Q_c,\mathcal G_c)\).  Then
\[
 \operatorname{Comp}(\sigma)
 =\sum_{V_c\setminus(Y_c\cup X_c)}
   \prod_{j=1}^{k}\operatorname{Comp}(\sigma_{4,j}).
 \tag{6}
\]
In case \((6)\),
\[
 \operatorname{Comp}(\sigma)
 =\sum_{S\setminus Y_c}
   \prod_{i:V_i\in S}Q_c(V_i\mid V_c^{(i-1)}).
 \tag{7}
\]
In case \((7)\), let \(S'\) be the unique containing district, put
\[
 Q'_c:=\prod_{i:V_i\in S'}Q_c(V_i\mid V_c^{(i-1)}),
\]
and let \(\sigma_7\) embed
\(\operatorname{ID}(Y_c,X_c\cap S',Q'_c,\mathcal G_c[S'])\).  Then
\[
 \operatorname{Comp}(\sigma)=\operatorname{Comp}(\sigma_7).
 \tag{8}
\]
At a case-\((5)\) state, the production emits the actual call \(c(\sigma)\), the pair
\(\operatorname{FAIL}(\mathcal G_c,\mathcal G_c[S])\), and \(\rho(\sigma)\); its locally checkable payload contains the two forest subgraphs forming the relevant hedge for the query at that failed scope.  Finally, the interface supplies a checked algebraic normalization map \(\mathcal N\) that respects the finite sums, products, marginalizations, Q-factor operations, and recursive substitutions in \((3)\)--\((8)\).  All of these properties are antecedent conditions on the arbitrary instance \(\mathfrak A\), rather than assertions that the open construction exists.

We first verify every denominator guard.  If a finite kernel \(Q_c\) is strictly positive pointwise, then each of its nonempty marginals is positive: for any assignment \(a\) to the retained variables,
\[
 Q_c(a)=\sum_b Q_c(a,b)>0,
 \tag{9}
\]
because the alphabets are finite and nonempty and every summand is positive.  Hence every ordered-kernel conditional used by ID is defined and positive:
\[
 Q_c(V_i=v_i\mid V_c^{(i-1)}=a)
 =\frac{Q_c(V_c^{(i)}=(a,v_i))}
 {Q_c(V_c^{(i-1)}=a)}>0.
 \tag{10}
\]
The denominator is positive by \((9)\), so the division in \((10)\) is valid.  The initial kernel is strictly positive by \((1)\).  Case \((2)\) passes to a marginal, positive by \((9)\); cases \((3)\) and \((4)\) retain the current kernel; and case \((7)\) passes to a finite product of the positive quantities in \((10)\).  Induction along the recursive calls therefore proves that every reached kernel is strictly positive and that every denominator guard in cases \((6)\), \((7)\), or their descendants is discharged.

By \(\mathrm{lem:ordered\text{-}id\text{-}call\text{-}tree\text{-}finite}\), the ordinary call tree rooted at \(c_0\) is finite.  Let \(h(c)\) be the height of the subtree rooted at an ordinary call \(c\).  For a grammar state \(\sigma\) with \(\iota(\sigma)=c\), let \(\mathcal P(c,\sigma)\) assert all three of the following facts: every complete grammar derivation rooted at \(\sigma\) projects edge by edge, with the same case labels, to the ordinary-ID subtree rooted at \(c\); if the derivation succeeds, then
\[
 \mathcal N(\operatorname{Comp}(\sigma))
 =\mathcal N(E_{\mathrm{ID}}(c)),
 \tag{11}
\]
where \(E_{\mathrm{ID}}(c)\) is the expression returned by ordinary ID; and if the derivation fails, then its propagated payload names the actual case-\((5)\) descendant, its failing pair, and its composed provenance to \(c_0\).  We prove \(\mathcal P(c,\sigma)\) by induction on the finite height \(h(c)\).

If case \((1)\) applies, both systems terminate with the same marginal by \((3)\), proving \((11)\).  If case \((6)\) applies, both terminate with the same Q-factor expression by \((7)\); all ratios are defined by \((10)\), so \((11)\) again follows.  If case \((5)\) applies, the matching grammar production is a failure leaf and, by the local payload condition, returns the actual call, \(\operatorname{FAIL}(\mathcal G_c,\mathcal G_c[S])\), and \(\rho(\sigma)\).  The certified forest pair is a hedge for the effect at this failed scope.  The frozen hedge-implies-nonidentification lemma, which records Shpitser--Pearl Theorem 17, therefore proves nonidentification at precisely that scope.  Since the matching recursive transitions and failure propagation also show that ordinary ID fails from \(c_0\), the frozen ordinary-ID completeness lemma proves nonidentification of the original effect.  The frozen hedge-criterion lemma, recording the scope of Shpitser--Pearl Theorem 19, then yields only
\[
 \text{there exist }Y'\subseteq Y\text{ and }X'\subseteq X
 \text{ for which }P(Y'\mid\operatorname{do}(X'))
 \text{ has the relevant hedge}.
 \tag{12}
\]
Thus provenance transports the failure information but does not relabel the case-\((5)\) pair as a hedge for the full original query.

Suppose case \((2)\) applies.  Its unique child has smaller height.  The induction hypothesis gives transition matching, expression equality \((11)\), and correct failure provenance at \(\sigma_2\).  Ordinary ID defines the parent expression to be the child expression, and the compiler does the same by \((4)\); applying \(\mathcal N\) establishes \((11)\) at \(\sigma\).  A child failure is propagated with provenance updated by \((2)\), so the failure assertion is preserved as well.  The same reasoning proves the induction step for cases \((3)\) and \((7)\), using \((5)\) and \((8)\), respectively.

Suppose case \((4)\) applies.  Each district child has smaller height, so the induction hypothesis applies to every \(\sigma_{4,j}\).  If any child fails, ordinary ID and the grammar propagate that same case-\((5)\) failure, and repeated use of \((2)\) supplies its provenance to \(c_0\).  If every child succeeds, ordinary ID forms
\[
 E_{\mathrm{ID}}(c)
 =\sum_{V_c\setminus(Y_c\cup X_c)}
   \prod_{j=1}^{k}E_{\mathrm{ID}}(c_{4,j}).
 \tag{13}
\]
Substitution of the child equalities \((11)\) into \((6)\), followed by the stipulated compatibility of \(\mathcal N\) with finite products and marginalization, gives \((11)\) at the parent.  This exhausts the seven ordered cases and completes the well-founded induction.

Taking \(\sigma=\sigma_0\), the initial embedding and the induction prove that every complete restricted trace matches the ordinary-ID call tree and uses exactly the same ordered cases and side conditions.  On success,
\[
 \mathcal N(\operatorname{Comp}(\sigma_0))
 =\mathcal N(E_{\mathrm{ID}}(c_0)).
 \tag{14}
\]
This is equality of two observed-law expressions, not a definition of the causal target.  By the frozen ordinary-ID soundness lemma, whenever ordinary ID returns the expression on the right, every compatible causal model \(M\) satisfies
\[
 E_{\mathrm{ID}}(c_0)(P_M(V))
 =P_{M,x}(Y=y)=q(M).
 \tag{15}
\]
Because checked algebraic normalization preserves evaluation, \((14)\) and \((15)\) show that the compiled expression also equals \(q(M)\).  On failure, the base case and propagation steps give the actual failed recursive call, its failing pair, and the correctly scoped provenance described in \((12)\).

The instance \(\mathfrak A\) was arbitrary.  Hence every restricted grammar instance satisfying the local seven-case interface has the asserted trace agreement, normalized compiler correctness, positivity guards, and failure provenance.  Applying this implication to \(\operatorname{FID\text{-}S}\) remains conditional on an affirmative resolution of \(\mathrm{oeq:latent\text{-}lifting}\) that constructs a concrete grammar, compiler, embedding, and provenance map satisfying that interface.  No step above constructs or assumes such a concrete latent-lifting grammar.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The abstract implication is proved for every restricted grammar, compiler, state embedding, and provenance map satisfying the local seven-case interface, by well-founded induction on the ordinary ID call tree. \textit{Gap.} No gap remains in the abstract implication. Concrete application to \(\operatorname{FID\text{-}S}\) still requires the latent-lifting open question to construct an interface instance. \textit{Consumer.} A future concrete classical branch satisfying the interface immediately inherits ordinary-ID trace agreement, the same normalized identifying functional, valid positivity guards, and correctly scoped hedge provenance.

\begin{proposition}[prop:first-strict-separation]\label{prop:first-strict-separation}
On \(\mathcal B_0\), \(P_{A=0}(B=b)=P(B=b)\) for every compatible model, whereas \(P_{A=1}(B=1)\) is not identified. For the latter claim, take \(H\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\), set \(A=0\), let the functional node satisfy \(D=A\), and set \(B=H\) on the reached row \((A,D)=(0,0)\) in both models; on the intervention-exposed row \((A,D)=(1,1)\), set \(B=0\) in the first model and \(B=1\) in the second. The models have the identical exact-support observed law \(P(A=0,D=0,B=b)=1/2\) and respective targets zero and one. Ordinary ID fails for the latent bow remaining after accounting for the directed paths. Chen--Darwiche Theorem 15 functional-variable elimination, under its stated separability and parent conditions, leaves that unidentified bow, while Theorem 18 is inapplicable because its treatment-marginal positivity set \(C_V=\{\Pr(X)>0:X\text{ is a treatment variable}\}\) includes \(\Pr(A=1)>0\), which the declared support contradicts. Chen--Darwiche's 2026 Theorems 1--2 prove soundness only for formula-returning algorithms under context-specific independences and state-alphabet constraints with a strictly positive observational law; \(\mathcal B_0\) instead retains \(1\in\Omega_A\) as a valid intervention state while declaring \(P(A=1)=0\), so it is outside their input regime. Thus this exact functional-support pattern is strictly resolved by the proposed support trichotomy on a residual regime not covered by those comparators.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(M\in\mathfrak M(G,\Omega,W,\Pi)\) on \(\mathcal B_0\).  Write the causal-Bayesian-network kernels as
\[
 \alpha_h=P_M(H=h),\qquad
 a_h(a)=P_M(A=a\mid H=h),\qquad
 d_a(d)=P_M(D=d\mid A=a),
\]
and
\[
 r_{h,a,d}(b)=P_M(B=b\mid H=h,A=a,D=d).
\]
The graph in the definition of \(\mathcal B_0\) and the causal-Bayesian-network factorization in the definition of \(\mathfrak M(G,\Omega,W,\Pi)\) give
\[
 P_M(h,a,d,b)=\alpha_h a_h(a)d_a(d)r_{h,a,d}(b).
\]
The functional-node assumption additionally says that each row \(d_a(\cdot)\) is a point mass.  The first equality below does not need that extra restriction, but the explicit models constructed below satisfy it.

By the support-literal assumption and the literal in the definition of \(\mathcal B_0\),
\[
 0=P_M(A=1)=\sum_{h\in\{0,1\}}\alpha_h a_h(1).
\]
Every summand is nonnegative.  Hence \(\alpha_h a_h(1)=0\) for every \(h\), and, whenever \(\alpha_h>0\), binary row normalization gives \(a_h(0)=1\).  Rows with \(\alpha_h=0\) contribute zero to all following sums.  The truncated factorization under \(\operatorname{do}(A=0)\) therefore yields, for each \(b\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P_{M,A=0}(B=b)
 &=\sum_{h,d}\alpha_h d_0(d)r_{h,0,d}(b)\\
 &=\sum_{h,d}\alpha_h a_h(0)d_0(d)r_{h,0,d}(b)\\
 &=\sum_{h,a,d}\alpha_h a_h(a)d_a(d)r_{h,a,d}(b)\\
 &=P_M(B=b).
\end{aligned}
\]
The second equality uses the preceding consequence of \(P_M(A=1)=0\); the third uses \(\alpha_h a_h(1)=0\); and the last uses the observational factorization.  Thus the displayed observed-law map
\[
 F_0(P_M(V)):=P_M(B=b)
\]
is a support-valid identifying formula for \(P_{M,A=0}(B=b)\).  It involves no division, so it requires no positivity beyond the declared literal.

The support literal is load-bearing for this formula.  Indeed, keep the same graph, let \(H\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(1/2)\), and set \(A=H\), \(D=A\), and \(B=A\).  This model still has an independent latent root and still obeys the functional declaration for \(D\), but it violates \(P(A=1)=0\).  In this model
\[
 P_M(B=1)=\tfrac12
 \qquad\text{while}\qquad
 P_{M,A=0}(B=1)=0.
\]
Thus the displayed observed-law formula is not a graph-only adjustment identity; it follows specifically from the declared structural-zero face.

For nonidentification at \(A=1\), define two complete models \(M_j\), indexed by \(j\in\{0,1\}\).  In both models let
\[
 P_{M_j}(H=0)=P_{M_j}(H=1)=\tfrac12,\qquad
 P_{M_j}(A=0\mid H=h)=1,
\]
and set \(D=A\).  Complete the kernel of \(B\) by the deterministic function
\[
 f_j(h,a,d)=
 \begin{cases}
 h,&(a,d)=(0,0),\\
 j,&(a,d)=(1,1),\\
 0,&(a,d)\in\{(0,1),(1,0)\},
 \end{cases}
 \qquad B=f_j(H,A,D).
\]
This specifies every parent row, including both observationally unreachable rows.  Each \(M_j\) is a finite model on the graph \(G\); its only exogenous root is \(H\), so the independent-root assumption holds; \(D=A\) obeys the functional-node assumption for \(W=\{D\}\); and \(A=0\) almost surely obeys the support-literal assumption.  Direct substitution gives, for both \(j\),
\[
 P_{M_j}(A=a,D=d,B=b)
 =\tfrac12\mathbf 1\{(a,d,b)=(0,0,0)\}
  +\tfrac12\mathbf 1\{(a,d,b)=(0,0,1)\}.
\]
Consequently the two models have the same observed law and the same exact observed support
\[
 \{(0,0,0),(0,0,1)\}.
\]
Under \(\operatorname{do}(A=1)\), the functional row for \(D\) gives \(D=1\), and hence the exposed row of \(B\) gives \(B=j\).  Therefore
\[
 P_{M_0,A=1}(B=1)=0,
 \qquad
 P_{M_1,A=1}(B=1)=1.
\]
Thus no map of the observed law can identify \(P_{A=1}(B=1)\) on \(\mathfrak M(G,\Omega,W,\Pi)\): such a map would take the same input on \(M_0\) and \(M_1\) but would have to return both zero and one.  The certificate preserves the structural zeros exactly, all primitive probabilities belong to \(\{0,\tfrac12,1\}\), their reduced denominators are at most \(2\), and the target gap is \(1\).

It remains to verify the comparison clauses.  In the cited Chen--Darwiche Theorem 15, \(D\) is an observed functional variable, is neither the treatment \(A\) nor the outcome \(B\), has the observed parent \(A\), and does not occur in the comparator positivity family \(C_V\), so it meets the stated parent and separability conditions.  Functional elimination removes \(D\) and replaces the path \(A\to D\to B\) by an \(A\to B\) arrow, which is already present.  The resulting latent projection has \(A\to B\) together with the common latent parent \(H\to A,H\to B\), namely the bow.  Marginalizing \(D\) in the two models above retains their common observed law on \((A,B)\) and their unequal \(A=1\) interventional targets, so this residual bow is unidentified; Theorem 15 does not resolve it.

The cited Chen--Darwiche Theorem 18 instead assumes
\[
 C_V=\{P(X)>0:X\text{ is a treatment variable}\}.
\]
For the binary treatment \(A\), this includes \(P(A=1)>0\), whereas every model on the declared face satisfies \(P(A=1)=0\).  Its premise is therefore false on \(\mathcal B_0\).  Finally, the cited 2026 Theorems 1 and 2 are one-way soundness results conditional on their respective formula-returning algorithms, within the paper's standing strictly positive observational regime.  The common law displayed above has only two positive cells among the eight cells of \(\Omega_A\times\Omega_D\times\Omega_B\).  In particular, \(1\in\Omega_A\) remains an admissible intervention state even though every observational cell with \(A=1\) has probability zero.  Hence \(\mathcal B_0\) is outside that strict-positivity input regime.

We have therefore exhibited both outputs required from a support-aware trichotomy on this one input: the explicit formula \(P(B=b)\) for the supported intervention \(A=0\), and a common-exact-support rational two-model certificate for the unsupported intervention \(A=1\).  The preceding premise checks show that this separation occurs on a regime not covered by the cited comparator results.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The pattern exposes, on the smallest latent bow, both the supported state-specific formula branch and an exact-support rational NONID branch. \textit{Gap.} Ordinary ID gives only bow failure. Chen--Darwiche Theorem 15 elimination of \(D\), under its separability and parent conditions, leaves the bow, and Theorem 18 assumes \(C_V=\{\Pr(X)>0:X\text{ is a treatment variable}\}\); neither theorem separates the supported and unsupported intervention values on this declared structural-zero face. Chen--Darwiche's 2026 Theorems 1--2 are sound identification results for CSI and state-alphabet constraints under strict observational positivity, not a completeness or exact-support countermodel result for a valid treatment state assigned zero observational mass. \textit{Consumer.} The pattern is a unit test requiring a support-aware implementation to return \(P(B=b)\) for \(A=0\) and an exact two-model certificate for \(A=1\).

\begin{lemma}[lem:ordered-id-call-tree-finite]\label{lem:ordered-id-call-tree-finite}
For the ordinary seven-case recursion \(\operatorname{ID}(Y_c,X_c,Q_c,\mathcal G_c)\) on a finite ADMG with vertex set \(V_c\), suppose that the recursive edges are precisely the following: case \((2)\) replaces \(\mathcal G_c\) by the proper ancestral subgraph \(\mathcal G_c[A_c]\); case \((3)\) keeps \(\mathcal G_c\) and replaces \(X_c\) by \(X_c\cup Z_c\) for nonempty \(Z_c\subseteq V_c\setminus X_c\); case \((4)\), when the residual graph has at least two districts, creates one child with residual vertex set \(S_j\) for each such district; and case \((7)\) replaces \(\mathcal G_c\) by a proper containing-district subgraph. Then every recursive edge strictly decreases \((|V_c|,|V_c\setminus X_c|)\) in lexicographic order, and the finitely branching recursion has a finite call tree.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let
\[
r(c):=(|V_c|,|V_c\setminus X_c|)\in\mathbb N^2
\]
with lexicographic order. In case \((2)\), \(A_c=\operatorname{An}_{\mathcal G_c}(Y_c)\) is a proper subset of \(V_c\), so the first coordinate decreases. In case \((3)\), the graph is unchanged and
\[
V_c\setminus(X_c\cup Z_c)=(V_c\setminus X_c)\setminus Z_c,
\]
whose cardinality is strictly smaller because \(Z_c\ne\varnothing\) and \(Z_c\subseteq V_c\setminus X_c\). In case \((4)\), the nonempty districts \(S_1,\ldots,S_k\), with \(k\ge2\), partition \(V_c\setminus X_c\). Hence every \(S_j\) is a proper subset of \(V_c\setminus X_c\), and for the child intervention set \(V_c\setminus S_j\),
\[
|V_c\setminus(V_c\setminus S_j)|=|S_j|<|V_c\setminus X_c|.
\]
In case \((7)\), the containing district \(S'\) is proper in \(V_c\): if \(S'=V_c\), then \(\mathcal G_c\) would itself be a single district and the earlier ordered case \((5)\) would apply. Thus the first coordinate decreases. Cases \((1)\), \((5)\), and \((6)\) have no recursive children. The lexicographic order on \(\mathbb N^2\) is well founded, so no infinite recursive path exists. Each node has finitely many children because \(V_c\) is finite. If the call tree were infinite, its root would have a child with infinitely many descendants, since it has only finitely many children. Repeating this choice would construct an infinite recursive path, contradicting the strict decrease of \(r\). Therefore the call tree is finite.
\end{proof}

\section*{Technical internal limitation (diagnostic only)}

The fully observed completion argument does not survive marginalization: equality of observed laws becomes a system of polynomial equalities in products of latent-source probabilities, so a local target-varying direction need not factor into independent sources, and a real separating fiber need not contain a rational point. The current trace and algorithm definitions are handles rather than a concrete production system. The abstract seven-case compiler theorem is proved, but it cannot be instantiated for the proposed procedure until the latent-lifting question constructs the required transition relation, compiler, state embedding, and provenance map. These construction and lifting tasks remain open obligations, not assumptions used by the abstract theorem.

\section{Honest scope}

The following are proved: the finite support oracle; the fully observed completion frontier and guarded identifying formula; for every integer \(r\geq3\), the fully observed existential-fiber smoothing certificate with gap at least \(1-2/r\), denominator bound \(r|V|\max_{i\in V}|\Omega_i|\), and logarithmic primitive-bit cost; the supported-bow formula and exact rational separating pair; and the universally quantified conditional classical-ID compiler theorem for every abstract grammar instance satisfying the local seven-case interface. The following are not constructed or proved: a concrete FID-S grammar or compiler satisfying that interface; the general support-preserving independent-source latent lift; a general rational equal-law witness theorem; converse guarded-formula reconstruction in the latent case; terminal-certificate syntax; the kill-instance audit against concrete productions; and explicit global search, latent-cardinality, and certificate-bit bounds. The parameterized constant gap is existentially obtained by choosing a fully observed observational fiber for each completion obstruction and is not asserted for every fixed law with that support. A failure pair is scoped to its actual failed query or subquery, and only the published existential-subquery hedge conclusion is carried back to the original query. Generic real quantifier elimination, unrestricted latent flattening, and the fully observed special case do not resolve the latent headline.
