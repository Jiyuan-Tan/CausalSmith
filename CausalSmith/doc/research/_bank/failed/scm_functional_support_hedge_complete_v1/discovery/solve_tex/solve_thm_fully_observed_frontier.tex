%%% FIELD proof-fully-observed-frontier
Assume \(U=\varnothing\), put \(n=|V|\), and put
\[
 m=\max_{i\in V}|\Omega_i|.
\]
The finiteness and nonemptiness conditions on \(V\) and the observed alphabets give \(n\geq 1\) and \(m\geq 1\).  Identification here means the following property of the causal target, rather than a definition of that target:
\[
 M_1,M_2\in\mathfrak M(G,\Omega,W,\Pi),\quad
 P_{M_1}(V)=P_{M_2}(V)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad q(M_1)=q(M_2).
 \tag{1}
\]
We derive both directions of the asserted criterion and then construct the observed-law functional.

Fix an exact support \(S\) realized by a member of \(\mathfrak M(G,\Omega,W,\Pi)\).  For \(i\in V\) and a joint assignment \(a\) to \(\operatorname{pa}_G(i)\), define
\[
 A_i^S(a)=\bigl\{b\in\Omega_i:\text{ there is }v\in S
 \text{ with }v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)}=a\text{ and }v_i=b\bigr\}.
 \tag{2}
\]
Call the row \((i,a)\) reached when \(A_i^S(a)\neq\varnothing\).  If \(M\) has exact support \(S\), then every reached row is determined by the observed law:
\[
 P_M(i=b\mid \operatorname{pa}_G(i)=a)
 =\frac{P_M(i=b,\operatorname{pa}_G(i)=a)}
 {P_M(\operatorname{pa}_G(i)=a)}.
 \tag{3}
\]
Indeed, the denominator is positive because (2) supplies a state in \(S\) with parent assignment \(a\), and exact support means that this state has positive probability.  The positive entries of the row in (3) are exactly those indexed by \(A_i^S(a)\).  If \(i\in W\), the functional-node assumption makes the row a point mass, so every reached row of such a vertex satisfies \(|A_i^S(a)|=1\).  An unreached row has zero probability of being invoked under the observational law.

We first establish a support identity.  Define
\[
 J(S)=\left\{v:\text{ for every }i\in V,\quad
 A_i^S(v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)})=\varnothing
 \text{ or }v_i\in A_i^S(v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)})\right\}.
 \tag{4}
\]
For every fully observed Bayesian-network law having exact support \(S\),
\[
 S=J(S).
 \tag{5}
\]
The inclusion \(S\subseteq J(S)\) follows from (2).  For the reverse inclusion, take a topological order \(i_1,\ldots,i_n\) of the acyclic graph and fix \(v\in J(S)\).  Suppose inductively that the conditional factors corresponding to the first \(k-1\) coordinates of \(v\) are positive.  This initial topological segment is ancestral.  Summing the DAG factorization over later variables and normalizing their conditional rows in reverse topological order shows that the probability of the specified initial segment is the product of those positive factors.  It is therefore positive, so some state in \(S\) extends that segment.  All parents of \(i_k\) occur earlier in the order, whence \((i_k,v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i_k)})\) is reached.  Membership of \(v\) in \(J(S)\), followed by (3), makes the \(i_k\)-factor positive.  The induction starts at the empty segment.  At \(k=n\), the DAG factorization gives \(P_M(V=v)>0\), so \(v\in S\), proving (5).

It follows that two collections of conditional tables which have identical numerical entries at every reached row, positive support exactly \(A_i^S(a)\) on each such row, and arbitrary normalized fills on unreached rows induce the same observed law and retain exact support \(S\).  To see this directly, every \(v\in S\) uses only the common reached-row entries.  If \(v\notin S=J(S)\), the negation of (4) supplies an \(i\) for which \(A_i^S(v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)})\neq\varnothing\) but \(v_i\notin A_i^S(v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)})\); the factor at that reached row is zero in both collections.  This argument requires no positivity at an unreached row and no global positivity.  The finite enumeration in \(\mathrm{lem:support-oracle}\) decides which supports are realizable and all support entailments needed to identify the reached rows.

Now suppose that the realized support \(S\) admits a completion obstruction.  By \(\mathrm{def:completion-obstruction}\), there are deterministic complete tables \(t_1,t_2\) which select an allowed value at each reached row, agree at every such row, satisfy all point-mass restrictions at \(W\), and obey
\[
 e_j=\mathbf 1\{Y_x(t_j)=y\},\qquad |e_1-e_2|=1.
 \tag{6}
\]
For a reached row \(o=(i,a)\), denote their common selected value by \(b_o\in A_i^S(a)\), and put \(k_o=|A_i^S(a)|\).  Fix an integer \(r\geq3\) and set
\[
 \varepsilon_r=\frac{1}{rn}.
 \tag{7}
\]
At each reached row use, in both models, the conditional distribution
\[
 \theta_{o,r}(b)
 =(1-\varepsilon_r)\mathbf 1\{b=b_o\}
 +\frac{\varepsilon_r}{k_o}\mathbf 1\{b\in A_i^S(a)\}.
 \tag{8}
\]
At every unreached row, let model \(M_j^{(r)}\) use the corresponding deterministic row of \(t_j\).  The row in (8) is normalized and has positive support exactly \(A_i^S(a)\).  If \(i\in W\), then \(k_o=1\), so (8) is a point mass; unreached rows at \(W\) are point masses by construction.  Thus the functional-node assumption is preserved.  The independent-root assumption is vacuous because \(U=\varnothing\).

The consequence of (5) just proved shows that \(M_1^{(r)}\) and \(M_2^{(r)}\) have identical observed laws and exact support \(S\).  For any event literal in \(\Pi\), positivity is equivalent to the corresponding cylinder meeting \(S\), while probability zero is equivalent to that cylinder being disjoint from \(S\).  Since \(S\) was realized by a member satisfying \(\Pi\), both constructed models satisfy every support literal.  Therefore
\[
 M_1^{(r)},M_2^{(r)}\in\mathfrak M(G,\Omega,W,\Pi),\qquad
 P_{M_1^{(r)}}(V)=P_{M_2^{(r)}}(V),\qquad
 \operatorname{supp}_V(M_j^{(r)})=S.
 \tag{9}
\]

Couple an interventional execution of \(M_j^{(r)}\) to the deterministic execution of \(t_j\).  At an encountered reached row, generate (8) by retaining \(b_o\) with probability \(1-\varepsilon_r\) and otherwise drawing uniformly from \(A_i^S(a)\).  Conditional on every preceding value, the probability that the generated value differs from the deterministic baseline is
\[
 \varepsilon_r\left(1-\frac{1}{k_o}\right)\leq\varepsilon_r.
 \tag{10}
\]
At an encountered unreached row the two executions agree surely.  At most \(n\) vertices are evaluated, even though which rows are encountered may depend on earlier draws.  Applying the union bound to the resulting adaptively defined deviation events, using the conditional bound (10) at each step, gives
\[
 \Pr(\text{some evaluated output differs from the baseline})
 \leq n\varepsilon_r=\frac1r.
 \tag{11}
\]
On the complement of this event the interventional outcome agrees with the deterministic execution.  Hence, for \(j=1,2\),
\[
 |q(M_j^{(r)})-e_j|\leq\frac1r.
 \tag{12}
\]
Equations (6) and (12), together with the triangle inequality, yield
\[
 |q(M_1^{(r)})-q(M_2^{(r)})|
 \geq 1-\frac2r>0.
 \tag{13}
\]
Thus an obstruction contradicts (1).

The arithmetic guarantees follow from the same construction.  At a reached row with \(k=k_o\), a nonselected allowed value has probability \(1/(rnk)\), while the selected value has probability
\[
 1-\frac1{rn}+\frac1{rnk}
 =\frac{rnk-k+1}{rnk}.
 \tag{14}
\]
All remaining primitive entries are zero or one.  Reducing every primitive fraction separately can only decrease its denominator, so each denominator is at most
\[
 rnk\leq rnm=:D_r.
 \tag{15}
\]
Both the numerator and denominator of every reduced primitive fraction are at most \(D_r\).  With the usual binary encoding, their combined length is therefore at most
\[
 2\left\lceil\log_2D_r\right\rceil+2
 =2\left\lceil\log_2\!\left(r|V|\max_{i\in V}|\Omega_i|\right)\right\rceil+2.
 \tag{16}
\]
No common denominator has been introduced: every induced observational or interventional cell is the exact finite sum of products prescribed by the relevant DAG factorization.  The quantifiers are also explicit.  For each obstruction and each \(r\geq3\), (8) existentially selects a common observational fiber; nothing here asserts the same gap for every fixed law of support \(S\).  Setting \(r=8\) in (13)--(16) gives the \(3/4\)-gap and denominator bound \(8|V|\max_i|\Omega_i|\).  For fixed input, increasing \(r\) makes the certified gap tend to one and adds only \(O(\log r)\) bits to the primitive encoding bound.

For the converse, assume that no exact support realized in the model class admits a completion obstruction.  Fix \(M\in\mathfrak M(G,\Omega,W,\Pi)\), write \(P=P_M(V)\), and let \(S=\operatorname{supp}_V(M)\).  Independently for every conditional-table row of \(M\), draw one output according to that row's probability vector.  The resulting random object \(T\) is a deterministic complete table; at vertices in \(W\), it respects the point-mass restriction by the functional-node assumption.  In one acyclic execution under \(\operatorname{do}(X=x)\), each nonintervened vertex invokes exactly one row.  Independence of the row draws and finite expansion over assignments give
\[
 \Pr\{Y_x(T)=y\}
 =\sum_{v:\,v_X=x,\,v_Y=y}
 \prod_{i\notin X}P_M(v_i\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)})
 =P_{M,x}(Y=y)=q(M).
 \tag{17}
\]
The middle expression is the truncated DAG factorization obtained by replacing the mechanisms at \(X\) by the point mass at \(x\); this derives its equality with the causal target rather than defining that equality.

Condition on all outputs of \(T\) at reached rows.  Every such output lies in the corresponding set \(A_i^S(a)\).  If two permitted choices of the unreached rows produced different values of \(\mathbf 1\{Y_x(T)=y\}\), the resulting deterministic tables would agree at every reached row, respect the restrictions at \(W\), and have unequal interventional indicators.  They would therefore be a completion obstruction, contrary to hypothesis.  Consequently, for each fixed reached-row realization, the indicator is constant over all deterministic fills of the unreached rows.

Suppose \(M'\in\mathfrak M(G,\Omega,W,\Pi)\) has the same observed law \(P\).  It then has the same exact support \(S\), and (3) shows that every reached conditional row of \(M'\) equals the corresponding row of \(M\).  Hence the reached-row draws have the same joint distribution in the two random-table representations.  The preceding conditional constancy eliminates all dependence on their possibly different unreached rows.  Averaging the same constants in (17) gives
\[
 q(M')=q(M).
 \tag{18}
\]
This proves (1) when no realized support has an obstruction.

It remains to exhibit the claimed identifying map.  For each realizable exact support \(S\), choose once and for all a value \(h_S^0(i,a)\in\Omega_i\) at every unreached row; this specifies a point mass there, including at every such row belonging to a vertex in \(W\).  On the branch \(\operatorname{supp}(P)=S\), define
\[
 \kappa_{i,S}(b\mid a;P)=
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle
 \frac{P(i=b,\operatorname{pa}_G(i)=a)}
 {P(\operatorname{pa}_G(i)=a)},
 &A_i^S(a)\neq\varnothing,\\[1.2ex]
 \mathbf 1\{b=h_S^0(i,a)\},
 &A_i^S(a)=\varnothing,
 \end{cases}
 \tag{19}
\]
and
\[
 F_S(P)=
 \sum_{v:\,v_X=x,\,v_Y=y}
 \prod_{i\notin X}
 \kappa_{i,S}(v_i\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)};P).
 \tag{20}
\]
Every denominator on the first branch of (19) is positive by its exact-support guard and (2); the second branch contains no division.  Put \(F_S=0\) on an unrealizable branch.  The observed product alphabet is finite, so there are only finitely many branches, and \(\mathrm{lem:support-oracle}\) decides their realizability.  Thus (19)--(20) are a support-valid guarded formula in the sense of \(\mathrm{def:guarded-formula}\).

For a model \(M\) with support \(S\), (3) says that the reached rows in (19) are its actual rows.  Equation (17), followed by the proved invariance under every choice of unreached-row fill, permits those actual unreached rows to be replaced by \(h_S^0\).  Therefore
\[
 q(M)=F_{\operatorname{supp}_V(M)}(P_M(V)).
 \tag{21}
\]
This proves the guarded-formula direction and completes the equivalence.

For a smallest exact check of the separating construction, take binary observed vertices \(A\to B\), \(X=\{A\}\), \(Y=\{B\}\), \(x=1\), \(y=1\), and \(S=\{(0,0)\}\).  Two completions agreeing on the reached rows and setting the unreached row \(B\mid A=1\) respectively to zero and one have the same point-mass observed law and targets zero and one; (8) leaves the singleton reached-row supports unchanged.  The causally sufficient premise is load-bearing: if a binary root \(U\) with probability one half is added with arrows \(U\to A\), \(U\to B\), and \(A\to B\), the deterministic models \(A=U,B=A\) and \(A=U,B=U\) share the observed law \(P(A=B=0)=P(A=B=1)=1/2\), but under \(\operatorname{do}(A=0)\) their probabilities of \(B=1\) are respectively zero and one half.  Thus dropping \(U=\varnothing\) invalidates the reached-row reconstruction on which (3), (19), and the theorem depend.
