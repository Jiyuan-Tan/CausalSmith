Fix the restricted branch \(W=\varnothing\), and suppose that \(\Pi\) entails
\[
 P_M(V=v)>0
 \qquad\text{for every }v\in\prod_{i\in V}\Omega_i
 \tag{1}
\]
for every compatible model \(M\).  Let \(\mathcal G\) be the latent projection of \(G\) onto \(V\), and let
\[
 c_0:=\operatorname{ID}(Y,X,P_M(V),\mathcal G)
\]
be the initial ordinary-ID call.  We prove the proposition as a universal conditional statement; in particular, the argument does not construct the grammar requested by \(\mathrm{oeq:latent\text{-}lifting}\).

Let
\[
 \mathfrak A=(\Sigma,\Rightarrow,\operatorname{Comp},\iota,\rho)
\]
be any restricted grammar instance satisfying the local classical-ID interface stipulated in \(\mathrm{oeq:latent\text{-}lifting}\).  Here \(\Sigma\) is the state set, \(\Rightarrow\) is the production relation, \(\operatorname{Comp}\) is the successful compiler, and
\[
 \iota(\sigma)=c(\sigma)
   =\operatorname{ID}(Y_c,X_c,Q_c,\mathcal G_c)
\]
embeds a grammar state into an ordinary-ID call.  The map \(\rho(\sigma)\) records the provenance of that call in \(c_0\).  The local interface gives an initial state \(\sigma_0\) satisfying \(\iota(\sigma_0)=c_0\) and having identity provenance.  At every state, exactly the production matching the first applicable one of the seven ordered ID cases is available, with the identical side condition.  Cases \((2)\), \((3)\), and \((7)\) have their specified unique child, case \((4)\) has one child for each district, and cases \((1)\), \((5)\), and \((6)\) are terminal.  No support-specific or functional production is available.  If \(\sigma'\) is a recursive child and \(\kappa_{c\leftarrow c'}\) is the ordinary-call provenance from the child call to its parent, then
\[
 \rho(\sigma')=\rho(\sigma)\circ\kappa_{c\leftarrow c'}.
 \tag{2}
\]
Failures propagate through recursive productions without altering the case-\((5)\) payload, apart from the provenance composition in \((2)\).

For a successful subtree, the local compiler equations are as follows.  In case \((1)\),
\[
 \operatorname{Comp}(\sigma)=\sum_{V_c\setminus Y_c}Q_c.
 \tag{3}
\]
In case \((2)\), if \(\sigma_2\) embeds
\[
 \operatorname{ID}\!\left(
 Y_c,X_c\cap A_c,
 \sum_{V_c\setminus A_c}Q_c,
 \mathcal G_c[A_c]
 \right),
\]
then
\[
 \operatorname{Comp}(\sigma)=\operatorname{Comp}(\sigma_2).
 \tag{4}
\]
In case \((3)\), if \(\sigma_3\) embeds
\(\operatorname{ID}(Y_c,X_c\cup Z_c,Q_c,\mathcal G_c)\), then
\[
 \operatorname{Comp}(\sigma)=\operatorname{Comp}(\sigma_3).
 \tag{5}
\]
In case \((4)\), let \(S_1,\ldots,S_k\) be the residual districts and let \(\sigma_{4,j}\) embed
\(\operatorname{ID}(S_j,V_c\setminus S_j,Q_c,\mathcal G_c)\).  Then
\[
 \operatorname{Comp}(\sigma)
 =\sum_{V_c\setminus(Y_c\cup X_c)}
   \prod_{j=1}^{k}\operatorname{Comp}(\sigma_{4,j}).
 \tag{6}
\]
In case \((6)\),
\[
 \operatorname{Comp}(\sigma)
 =\sum_{S\setminus Y_c}
   \prod_{i:V_i\in S}Q_c(V_i\mid V_c^{(i-1)}).
 \tag{7}
\]
In case \((7)\), let \(S'\) be the unique containing district, put
\[
 Q'_c:=\prod_{i:V_i\in S'}Q_c(V_i\mid V_c^{(i-1)}),
\]
and let \(\sigma_7\) embed
\(\operatorname{ID}(Y_c,X_c\cap S',Q'_c,\mathcal G_c[S'])\).  Then
\[
 \operatorname{Comp}(\sigma)=\operatorname{Comp}(\sigma_7).
 \tag{8}
\]
At a case-\((5)\) state, the production emits the actual call \(c(\sigma)\), the pair
\(\operatorname{FAIL}(\mathcal G_c,\mathcal G_c[S])\), and \(\rho(\sigma)\); its locally checkable payload contains the two forest subgraphs forming the relevant hedge for the query at that failed scope.  Finally, the interface supplies a checked algebraic normalization map \(\mathcal N\) that respects the finite sums, products, marginalizations, Q-factor operations, and recursive substitutions in \((3)\)--\((8)\).  All of these properties are antecedent conditions on the arbitrary instance \(\mathfrak A\), rather than assertions that the open construction exists.

We first verify every denominator guard.  If a finite kernel \(Q_c\) is strictly positive pointwise, then each of its nonempty marginals is positive: for any assignment \(a\) to the retained variables,
\[
 Q_c(a)=\sum_b Q_c(a,b)>0,
 \tag{9}
\]
because the alphabets are finite and nonempty and every summand is positive.  Hence every ordered-kernel conditional used by ID is defined and positive:
\[
 Q_c(V_i=v_i\mid V_c^{(i-1)}=a)
 =\frac{Q_c(V_c^{(i)}=(a,v_i))}
 {Q_c(V_c^{(i-1)}=a)}>0.
 \tag{10}
\]
The denominator is positive by \((9)\), so the division in \((10)\) is valid.  The initial kernel is strictly positive by \((1)\).  Case \((2)\) passes to a marginal, positive by \((9)\); cases \((3)\) and \((4)\) retain the current kernel; and case \((7)\) passes to a finite product of the positive quantities in \((10)\).  Induction along the recursive calls therefore proves that every reached kernel is strictly positive and that every denominator guard in cases \((6)\), \((7)\), or their descendants is discharged.

By \(\mathrm{lem:ordered\text{-}id\text{-}call\text{-}tree\text{-}finite}\), the ordinary call tree rooted at \(c_0\) is finite.  Let \(h(c)\) be the height of the subtree rooted at an ordinary call \(c\).  For a grammar state \(\sigma\) with \(\iota(\sigma)=c\), let \(\mathcal P(c,\sigma)\) assert all three of the following facts: every complete grammar derivation rooted at \(\sigma\) projects edge by edge, with the same case labels, to the ordinary-ID subtree rooted at \(c\); if the derivation succeeds, then
\[
 \mathcal N(\operatorname{Comp}(\sigma))
 =\mathcal N(E_{\mathrm{ID}}(c)),
 \tag{11}
\]
where \(E_{\mathrm{ID}}(c)\) is the expression returned by ordinary ID; and if the derivation fails, then its propagated payload names the actual case-\((5)\) descendant, its failing pair, and its composed provenance to \(c_0\).  We prove \(\mathcal P(c,\sigma)\) by induction on the finite height \(h(c)\).

If case \((1)\) applies, both systems terminate with the same marginal by \((3)\), proving \((11)\).  If case \((6)\) applies, both terminate with the same Q-factor expression by \((7)\); all ratios are defined by \((10)\), so \((11)\) again follows.  If case \((5)\) applies, the matching grammar production is a failure leaf and, by the local payload condition, returns the actual call, \(\operatorname{FAIL}(\mathcal G_c,\mathcal G_c[S])\), and \(\rho(\sigma)\).  The certified forest pair is a hedge for the effect at this failed scope.  The frozen hedge-implies-nonidentification lemma, which records Shpitser--Pearl Theorem 17, therefore proves nonidentification at precisely that scope.  Since the matching recursive transitions and failure propagation also show that ordinary ID fails from \(c_0\), the frozen ordinary-ID completeness lemma proves nonidentification of the original effect.  The frozen hedge-criterion lemma, recording the scope of Shpitser--Pearl Theorem 19, then yields only
\[
 \text{there exist }Y'\subseteq Y\text{ and }X'\subseteq X
 \text{ for which }P(Y'\mid\operatorname{do}(X'))
 \text{ has the relevant hedge}.
 \tag{12}
\]
Thus provenance transports the failure information but does not relabel the case-\((5)\) pair as a hedge for the full original query.

Suppose case \((2)\) applies.  Its unique child has smaller height.  The induction hypothesis gives transition matching, expression equality \((11)\), and correct failure provenance at \(\sigma_2\).  Ordinary ID defines the parent expression to be the child expression, and the compiler does the same by \((4)\); applying \(\mathcal N\) establishes \((11)\) at \(\sigma\).  A child failure is propagated with provenance updated by \((2)\), so the failure assertion is preserved as well.  The same reasoning proves the induction step for cases \((3)\) and \((7)\), using \((5)\) and \((8)\), respectively.

Suppose case \((4)\) applies.  Each district child has smaller height, so the induction hypothesis applies to every \(\sigma_{4,j}\).  If any child fails, ordinary ID and the grammar propagate that same case-\((5)\) failure, and repeated use of \((2)\) supplies its provenance to \(c_0\).  If every child succeeds, ordinary ID forms
\[
 E_{\mathrm{ID}}(c)
 =\sum_{V_c\setminus(Y_c\cup X_c)}
   \prod_{j=1}^{k}E_{\mathrm{ID}}(c_{4,j}).
 \tag{13}
\]
Substitution of the child equalities \((11)\) into \((6)\), followed by the stipulated compatibility of \(\mathcal N\) with finite products and marginalization, gives \((11)\) at the parent.  This exhausts the seven ordered cases and completes the well-founded induction.

Taking \(\sigma=\sigma_0\), the initial embedding and the induction prove that every complete restricted trace matches the ordinary-ID call tree and uses exactly the same ordered cases and side conditions.  On success,
\[
 \mathcal N(\operatorname{Comp}(\sigma_0))
 =\mathcal N(E_{\mathrm{ID}}(c_0)).
 \tag{14}
\]
This is equality of two observed-law expressions, not a definition of the causal target.  By the frozen ordinary-ID soundness lemma, whenever ordinary ID returns the expression on the right, every compatible causal model \(M\) satisfies
\[
 E_{\mathrm{ID}}(c_0)(P_M(V))
 =P_{M,x}(Y=y)=q(M).
 \tag{15}
\]
Because checked algebraic normalization preserves evaluation, \((14)\) and \((15)\) show that the compiled expression also equals \(q(M)\).  On failure, the base case and propagation steps give the actual failed recursive call, its failing pair, and the correctly scoped provenance described in \((12)\).

The instance \(\mathfrak A\) was arbitrary.  Hence every restricted grammar instance satisfying the local seven-case interface has the asserted trace agreement, normalized compiler correctness, positivity guards, and failure provenance.  Applying this implication to \(\operatorname{FID\text{-}S}\) remains conditional on an affirmative resolution of \(\mathrm{oeq:latent\text{-}lifting}\) that constructs a concrete grammar, compiler, embedding, and provenance map satisfying that interface.  No step above constructs or assumes such a concrete latent-lifting grammar.
