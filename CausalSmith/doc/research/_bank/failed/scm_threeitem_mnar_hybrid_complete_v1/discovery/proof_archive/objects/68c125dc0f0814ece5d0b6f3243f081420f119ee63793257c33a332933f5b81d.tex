Fix \(G\in\mathfrak G_3\) for which \(\operatorname{Complete}_3(G)\) has terminal type \(\mathrm{ID}\), and fix \(\epsilon\in(0,1/64)\). Let \(p\in\mathcal M_\epsilon(G)\) and put \(q=C_\#p\). Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}complete3\text{-}iff}\) says that the returned certificate is accepted by \(\operatorname{Check}_3\); Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}certificate\text{-}soundness}\) therefore gives
\[
F_G(q)=p_X.
\]
For each \(w\in\mathcal W\), the fibre \(C^{-1}\{w\}\) is nonempty. Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}observed\text{-}law}\) and Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}fixed\text{-}interior}\) consequently imply
\[
q_w=\sum_{(x,r):C(x,r)=w}p(x,r)\geq\epsilon.
\]
Thus every nonempty observed event \(A\subseteq\mathcal W\) satisfies \(q(A)\geq\epsilon\). By Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}certificate\text{-}denominator\text{-}safety}\), these are exactly the possible divisors in the fixed rational expression \(F_G\).

Let
\[
K_{G,\epsilon}=\{C_\#p:p\in\mathcal M_\epsilon(G)\}.
\]
This set is compact. Indeed, the inequalities \(p(x,r)\geq\epsilon\) define a compact subset of the finite probability simplex. On this subset all conditional probabilities occurring in Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}graph\text{-}factorization}\) have positive marginal denominators and hence are continuous functions of \(p\); the factorization equalities therefore define a closed subset, namely \(\mathcal M_\epsilon(G)\). The linear map \(C_\#\) is continuous, proving compactness of \(K_{G,\epsilon}\).

Because \(F_G\) is a finite rational expression and each of its divisors is at least \(\epsilon\) on \(K_{G,\epsilon}\), there is an open neighborhood of this compact set on which \(F_G\) is twice continuously differentiable. Its second derivative has there a finite uniform operator-norm bound, denoted by \(M_{G,\epsilon}\). Write \(\delta_n=\widehat q-q\), and let \(E_n\) be the event that every empirical divisor required by \(F_G\) is positive. If the certificate uses \(d_G<\infty\) distinct divisor events, then independence in Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}iid\text{-}coarsened\text{-}sampling}\) and the lower bound above give
\[
\sup_{p\in\mathcal M_\epsilon(G)}\Pr_p(E_n^c)
\leq \sum_{A\ {\rm required}}\{1-q(A)\}^n
\leq d_G(1-\epsilon)^n
\leq d_G e^{-n\epsilon}.
\]
Hence the pseudocount branch in Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}plugin\text{-}estimator}\) is used with uniformly vanishing probability.

Put \(D_n=\{\lVert\delta_n\rVert_1\leq\epsilon/2\}\). The exact multinomial second moment is
\[
\mathbb E_q\lVert\widehat q-q\rVert_2^2
=\frac{\operatorname{tr}\{\operatorname{diag}(q)-qq^{\mathsf T}\}}{n}
=\frac{1-\lVert q\rVert_2^2}{n}\leq\frac1n.
\]
Since \(\lVert v\rVert_1^2\leq27\lVert v\rVert_2^2\), the elementary inequality \(\Pr(Y>a)\leq\mathbb E(Y)/a\) for \(Y\geq0\) yields
\[
\sup_{p\in\mathcal M_\epsilon(G)}\Pr_p(D_n^c)
\leq\frac{108}{n\epsilon^2}.
\]
On \(D_n\), every required divisor on the segment \(q+t\delta_n\), \(0\leq t\leq1\), is at least \(\epsilon/2\). Taylor's formula with integral remainder gives
\[
F_G(\widehat q)-F_G(q)=D F_G(q)\delta_n+R_n,
\qquad
R_n=\int_0^1\{D F_G(q+t\delta_n)-D F_G(q)\}\delta_n\,dt,
\]
and therefore
\[
\lVert R_n\rVert_2\leq\frac{M_{G,\epsilon}}2\lVert\delta_n\rVert_2^2
\quad\hbox{on }D_n.
\]
For every \(\eta>0\), the same nonnegative-variable inequality and the preceding moment identity imply
\[
\sup_{p\in\mathcal M_\epsilon(G)}
\Pr_p\{\sqrt n\lVert R_n\rVert_2>\eta,\ D_n\}
\leq\frac{M_{G,\epsilon}}{2\eta\sqrt n}.
\]
Together with the bounds for \(D_n^c\) and \(E_n^c\), this proves \(\sqrt nR_n=o_p(1)\) uniformly over \(\mathcal M_\epsilon(G)\). Since
\[
\sqrt n(\widehat q-q)=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{j=1}^n(e_{W_j}-q),
\]
Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}compiled\text{-}influence}\) now gives
\[
\sqrt n(\widehat p_X-p_X)
=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{j=1}^n\varphi_G(W_j;q)+o_p(1).
\]

Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}finite\text{-}multinomial\text{-}clt}\) gives the multinomial Gaussian limit for every fixed \(q\). Here its characteristic-function calculation is uniform in \(q\). Indeed, for every fixed \(t\in\mathbb R^{27}\), boundedness of \(e_W-q\) and the third-order Taylor formula give
\[
\mathbb E_q\exp\!\left\{\frac{i t^{\mathsf T}(e_W-q)}{\sqrt n}\right\}
=1-\frac{t^{\mathsf T}\Sigma(q)t}{2n}+O(n^{-3/2}),
\]
where the remainder constant depends on \(t\) but not on \(q\). Raising this expression to the \(n\)-th power gives, uniformly for \(q\in K_{G,\epsilon}\),
\[
\exp\{-t^{\mathsf T}\Sigma(q)t/2\}+o(1).
\]
The same calculation after applying the uniformly bounded linear map \(D F_G(q)\) proves
\[
\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{j=1}^n\varphi_G(W_j;q)
\rightsquigarrow N_8(0,V_G(q))
\]
uniformly in bounded-Lipschitz distance. This argument never inverts \(V_G(q)\), so singularity is allowed.

The map
\[
q\longmapsto D F_G(q)\{\operatorname{diag}(q)-qq^{\mathsf T}\}D F_G(q)^{\mathsf T}
\]
is uniformly continuous on a neighborhood of \(K_{G,\epsilon}\). The displayed second-moment identity gives \(\widehat q-q=o_p(1)\) uniformly, and the pseudocount event is negligible. Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}compiled\text{-}influence}\) and the plug-in formula for \(\widehat V_G\) therefore imply
\[
\widehat V_G\longrightarrow_p V_G(q)
\]
uniformly over \(p\in\mathcal M_\epsilon(G)\).

It remains to justify studentization without assuming that the eight-dimensional covariance is nonsingular. Fix \(x\in\mathcal B\), write \(g_x=D F_G(q)(x,\cdot)\), and suppose that \(V_G(q)(x,x)=0\). Since every \(q_w>0\),
\[
0=g_x\Sigma(q)g_x^{\mathsf T}=\operatorname{Var}_q\{g_x(W)\}
\]
forces \(g_x(w)\) to be constant in \(w\). Choose \(y\in\mathcal B\setminus\{x\}\). Keep all response kernels of \(p\) fixed and, for sufficiently small \(|t|\), replace the target law by
\[
p_{X,t}=p_X+t(\mathbf 1_{\{x\}}-\mathbf 1_{\{y\}}).
\]
Strict positivity of \(p\), which follows from Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}fixed\text{-}interior}\), ensures that the resulting factorized law \(p_t\) belongs to \(\mathcal M_+(G)\). If \(q_t=C_\#p_t\), certificate soundness gives \(F_G(q_t)(x)=p_X(x)+t\). Differentiating at zero yields
\[
1=g_x\left.\frac{d q_t}{dt}\right|_{t=0}.
\]
But \(\sum_w d q_t(w)/dt=0\), so a constant \(g_x\) makes the right-hand side zero, a contradiction. Hence \(V_G(q)(x,x)>0\) for every \(q\in K_{G,\epsilon}\) and \(x\in\mathcal B\). Continuity, compactness, and finiteness of \(\mathcal B\) give
\[
\nu_{G,\epsilon}:=\min_{q\in K_{G,\epsilon}}\min_{x\in\mathcal B}V_G(q)(x,x)>0.
\]
Thus the eight coordinatewise studentized statistics converge uniformly to standard normal variables, although their joint covariance may be singular.

Let \(z=z_{1-\alpha/16}\). Each two-sided coordinate error then has limiting probability at most \(2\{1-\Phi(z)\}=\alpha/8\), uniformly in \(p\). Since \(p_X\in\Delta_7\), Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}simultaneous\text{-}region}\) and the union bound give
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{p\in\mathcal M_\epsilon(G)}
\Pr_p\{p_X\in\mathcal C_{n,\alpha}(G)\}
\geq1-8\frac{\alpha}{8}=1-\alpha.
\]
This proves every assertion.
