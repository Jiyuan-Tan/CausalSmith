Fix an \(\mathrm{ID}\) output for \(G\) and \(p\in\mathcal M_\epsilon(G)\).  By Theorem \(\mathrm{thm{:}complete3\text{-}iff}\), the output is checker-accepted and its rational map satisfies
\[
F_G(q)=p_X,\qquad q=C_{\#}p.
\]
For every \(w\in\mathcal W\), the proof of Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}certificate\text{-}denominator\text{-}safety}\), with the stronger bound in Assumption \(\mathrm{ass{:}fixed\text{-}interior}\), gives \(q_w\ge\epsilon\).  Hence every permitted denominator \(q(A)\) is at least \(\epsilon\).

The set
\[
K_{G,\epsilon}=\{C_{\#}p:p\in\mathcal M_\epsilon(G)\}
\]
is compact.  Indeed, it is the continuous image under the linear map \(C_{\#}\) of a closed subset of the finite probability simplex: on the region \(p(x,r)\ge\epsilon\), all conditional probabilities in the graph factorization have positive marginal denominators and are continuous rational functions of \(p\), so the factorization equalities define a closed set.  Since \(F_G\) is a fixed finite rational expression and all of its denominators are at least \(\epsilon\) on this compact set, it is twice continuously differentiable on a neighborhood of \(K_{G,\epsilon}\), with a finite uniform Hessian bound \(M_{G,\epsilon}\).

Let \(E_n\) be the event that every empirical divisor required by \(F_G\) is positive.  If the trace has \(d_G<\infty\) distinct divisor events, then, uniformly over the model,
\[
\Pr_p(E_n^c)
\le\sum_{A\text{ required}}\Pr_p\{\widehat q(A)=0\}
\le d_G(1-\epsilon)^n
\le d_Ge^{-n\epsilon}.
\]
Thus the fixed pseudocount convention is invoked with uniformly vanishing probability.  Put \(\delta_n=\widehat q-q\) and let \(D_n=\{\|\delta_n\|_1\le\epsilon/2\}\).  On \(D_n\), integrating the derivative along the segment \(q+t\delta_n\) gives
\[
F_G(\widehat q)-F_G(q)
=D F_G(q)(\widehat q-q)+R_n,
\qquad
R_n=\int_0^1\{D F_G(q+t\delta_n)-D F_G(q)\}\delta_n\,dt.
\]
The uniform Hessian bound therefore yields
\[
\|R_n\|\le \frac{M_{G,\epsilon}}2\|\widehat q-q\|^2.
\]
For a multinomial empirical vector,
\[
\sup_{q\in K_{G,\epsilon}}E_q\|\widehat q-q\|^2
=\sup_q\frac{\operatorname{tr}\Sigma(q)}{n}
\le\frac1n.
\]
Since \(\|v\|_1^2\le27\|v\|^2\), the preceding display gives
\[
\sup_q\Pr_q(D_n^c)\le\frac{108}{n\epsilon^2}.
\]
On \(D_n\), every required divisor along the entire segment \(q+t\delta_n\) is at least \(\epsilon/2\), so the preceding integral remainder bound applies with a uniform constant.  For every \(\eta>0\), nonnegativity and the second-moment bound give
\[
\sup_q\Pr_q\{\sqrt n\|R_n\|>\eta,D_n\}
\le\frac{M_{G,\epsilon}}{2\eta\sqrt n}.
\]
Together with the bounds for \(D_n^c\) and \(E_n^c\), this proves \(\sqrt nR_n=o_p(1)\) uniformly.  Since
\[
\sqrt n(\widehat q-q)=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{j=1}^n(e_{W_j}-q),
\]
we obtain the asserted uniform asymptotic linear representation with
\[
\varphi_G(w;q)=D F_G(q)(e_w-q).
\]

The finite-multinomial central limit theorem in Lemma \(\mathrm{lem{:}finite\text{-}multinomial\text{-}clt}\) gives the displayed Gaussian limit for each fixed \(p\).  Uniformity over \(K_{G,\epsilon}\) follows by a compactness argument.  More explicitly, for every fixed \(t\in\mathbb R^{27}\), boundedness of \(e_W-q\) gives the characteristic-function expansion
\[
E_q\exp\!\left\{\frac{i t^{\mathsf T}(e_W-q)}{\sqrt n}\right\}
=1-\frac{t^{\mathsf T}\Sigma(q)t}{2n}+O(n^{-3/2}),
\]
where the remainder is uniform in \(q\).  Raising this expression to the \(n\)-th power proves uniform convergence of the characteristic functions to those of \(N_{27}(0,\Sigma(q))\).  Equivalently, any allegedly nonconvergent sequence has a subsequence \(q_n\to q_\infty\), along which the ordinary multivariate central limit theorem gives the required contradiction.  Multiplication by the continuous matrix \(D F_G(q)\) yields
\[
\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{j=1}^n\varphi_G(W_j;q)
\rightsquigarrow N_8(0,V_G(q))
\]
uniformly in this subsequential sense; no inverse of \(V_G(q)\) is used.

The map
\[
q\longmapsto D F_G(q)\{\operatorname{diag}(q)-qq^{\mathsf T}\}D F_G(q)^{\mathsf T}
\]
is uniformly continuous on a neighborhood of \(K_{G,\epsilon}\).  The same second-moment bound gives \(\widehat q-q=o_p(1)\) uniformly, while the pseudocount event is negligible.  Hence \(\widehat V_G\to_p V_G(q)\) uniformly.

Although \(V_G(q)\) can be singular as an eight-dimensional matrix, each coordinate variance is uniformly bounded away from zero.  To see pointwise positivity, let \(g_x=D F_G(q)(x,\cdot)\) and suppose
\[
V_G(q)(x,x)=g_x\Sigma(q)g_x^{\mathsf T}
=\operatorname{Var}_q\{g_x(W)\}=0.
\]
Since every \(q_w>0\), this forces \(g_x(w)\) to equal a constant for all \(w\).  Choose \(y\ne x\), keep all response kernels of \(p\) fixed, and for sufficiently small \(|t|\) replace \(p_X\) by
\[
p_{X,t}=p_X+t(\mathbf1_{\{x\}}-\mathbf1_{\{y\}}).
\]
Strict positivity makes the resulting factorized law \(p_t\) a member of \(\mathcal M_+(G)\).  If \(q_t=C_{\#}p_t\), identification throughout \(\mathcal M_+(G)\) gives \(F_G(q_t)(x)=p_X(x)+t\), and therefore
\[
1=\left.\frac{d}{dt}F_G(q_t)(x)\right|_{t=0}
=g_x q'_0.
\]
But \(\sum_wq'_0(w)=0\), so a constant \(g_x\) would give \(g_xq'_0=0\), a contradiction.  Thus \(V_G(q)(x,x)>0\).  Continuity, compactness of \(K_{G,\epsilon}\), and finiteness of \(\mathcal B\) provide a constant \(\nu_{G,\epsilon}>0\) such that
\[
\min_{q\in K_{G,\epsilon}}\min_{x\in\mathcal B}V_G(q)(x,x)\ge\nu_{G,\epsilon}.
\]
Consequently the coordinatewise studentized statistics converge uniformly to \(N(0,1)\).  With \(z=z_{1-\alpha/16}\), each two-sided coordinate error has limiting probability at most \(\alpha/8\).  Because \(p_X\in\Delta_7\), membership in \(\mathcal C_{n,\alpha}(G)\) is exactly the conjunction of the eight coordinate inequalities.  The union bound therefore gives
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{p\in\mathcal M_\epsilon(G)}
\Pr_p\{p_X\in\mathcal C_{n,\alpha}(G)\}
\ge1-8\frac{\alpha}{8}=1-\alpha.
\]
