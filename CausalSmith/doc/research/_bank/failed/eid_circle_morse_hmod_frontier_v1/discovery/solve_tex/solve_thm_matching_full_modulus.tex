For every fixed nondegenerate bounded class \(\mathcal M_{n,k,m}(c_0,C_0,K,a_0,J,\Delta_0)\) containing the lower family, there exist constants \(0<c\leq C<\infty\) and \(\varepsilon_0>0\) such that
\[
c\varepsilon^{(k+1)/(k+m+1)}
\leq \Omega_{k,m}(\varepsilon)
\leq C\varepsilon^{k/((k+n/2)(m+1))}
\qquad (0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_0).
\]
Here \(d_{\mathrm{rep}}\) is the symmetric aligned discrepancy. For \((n,k,m)=(2,2,1)\), the full-class bracket has lower exponent \(3/4\) and upper exponent \(1/3\); the same bracket holds when the supremum is restricted to \(\mathcal M^{\mathrm{Morse}}_{2,2,1}(c_0,C_0,K,a_0,J,\Delta_0)\). Whenever \(\theta,\widetilde\theta\) in the class satisfy \(D_H(\theta,\widetilde\theta)=0\), their torus encoders are directly identified up to one common node permutation and coordinatewise circle diffeomorphisms. This exact conclusion follows from Proposition \(\mathrm{prop:identification\text{-}hypothesis\text{-}comparison}\), while the strengthened smoothness, conditioning, separation, and attained-order hypotheses are used for the uniform nonzero-error bracket.

%%% FIELD matching-proof
Put
\[
p=\frac{k+1}{k+m+1},
\qquad
q=\frac{k}{(k+n/2)(m+1)}.
\]
By Proposition \(\mathrm{prop:lower\text{-}family\text{-}membership}\), the models used in Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:localized\text{-}lower\text{-}bound}\) belong to the fixed class. That theorem therefore supplies constants \(c>0\) and \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{low}}>0\), depending only on the fixed class constants and on the fixed dimension, such that
\[
\Omega_{k,m}(\varepsilon)
\geq c\varepsilon^p,
\qquad
0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{low}}.
\tag{1}
\]

For the upper bound, fix arbitrary \(\theta,\widetilde\theta\in\mathcal M_{n,k,m}(c_0,C_0,K,a_0,J,\Delta_0)\) satisfying
\[
D_H(\theta,\widetilde\theta)\leq\varepsilon.
\tag{2}
\]
Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:full\text{-}law\text{-}residual\text{-}reduction}\), whose density-overlap and decoder-conditioning hypotheses are included in Definition \(\mathrm{def:model\text{-}class}\), gives a class-uniform constant \(C_{\mathrm{red}}<\infty\) such that
\[
\mathcal E(\theta,\widetilde\theta)
\leq C_{\mathrm{red}}D_H(\theta,\widetilde\theta)
\leq C_{\mathrm{red}}\varepsilon.
\tag{3}
\]
Choose
\[
\varepsilon_0
\leq
\min\{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{low}},C_{\mathrm{red}}^{-1}\}.
\tag{4}
\]
Then \(0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_0\) implies \(\mathcal E(\theta,\widetilde\theta)\leq1\), which is the small-residual premise of Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:scalar\text{-}finite\text{-}type\text{-}weak\text{-}rigidity}\). Applying that lemma to (3) yields
\[
d_{\mathrm{rep}}(\theta,\widetilde\theta)
\leq C_{\mathrm{rig}}\mathcal E(\theta,\widetilde\theta)^q
\leq C_{\mathrm{rig}}C_{\mathrm{red}}^q\varepsilon^q.
\tag{5}
\]
The pair in (2) was arbitrary. Taking the supremum in Definition \(\mathrm{def:inverse\text{-}modulus}\) and enlarging the upper constant if necessary gives
\[
\Omega_{k,m}(\varepsilon)
\leq C\varepsilon^q,
\qquad
0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_0.
\tag{6}
\]
Combining (1) and (6) proves the full-class bracket.

For \((n,k,m)=(2,2,1)\), direct substitution gives
\[
p=\frac{2+1}{2+1+1}=\frac34,
\qquad
q=\frac{2}{(2+2/2)(1+1)}=\frac13.
\tag{7}
\]
Every pair in the primary Morse class is also a pair in \(\mathcal M_{2,2,1}\), so (6) supplies its restricted upper bound. Proposition \(\mathrm{prop:primary\text{-}morse\text{-}audit}\) places the localized lower family in \(\mathcal M^{\mathrm{Morse}}_{2,2,1}\), so (1) supplies the restricted lower bound. This proves the asserted primary bracket.

Finally, suppose \(D_H(\theta,\widetilde\theta)=0\). Proposition \(\mathrm{prop:identification\text{-}hypothesis\text{-}comparison}\) proves directly, from equality of the aligned complete laws and the finite-type isolated-zero conditions, that the transition map has coordinate form after one common node permutation, with every coordinate map a circle diffeomorphism. Hence the two encoders are identified up to the declared action \(\mathfrak S_n\ltimes\mathrm{Diff}(\mathbb T)^n\). This implication is obtained from the direct torus identification proposition; it does not use the invalid converse inference from \(d_{\mathrm{rep}}=0\) to quotient equivalence. The same proposition separates the paired intervention conditions used for zero-law identification from the additional quantitative envelopes used in (3)--(6).

When \((n,k,m)=(2,2,1)\), Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:matching\text{-}full\text{-}modulus}\) gives
\[
c\varepsilon^{3/4}
\leq\Omega_{2,1}(\varepsilon)
\leq C\varepsilon^{1/3}
\]
for all sufficiently small positive \(\varepsilon\), whereas Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:radial\text{-}subclass\text{-}frontier}\) gives the sharp restricted identity \(\Omega_{2,1}^{\mathrm{rad}}(\varepsilon)\asymp\varepsilon^{3/4}\). Whenever \(\theta,\widetilde\theta\) in the primary Morse class satisfy \(D_H(\theta,\widetilde\theta)=0\), Proposition \(\mathrm{prop:identification\text{-}hypothesis\text{-}comparison}\) directly identifies their circular coordinates up to one common node permutation and coordinatewise circle diffeomorphisms, with \(d_{\mathrm{rep}}\) as the symmetric aligned loss. Any contrast that is constant on a nonempty open arc is excluded from this finite-critical-set class.

%%% FIELD primary-proof
Substitute \(n=2\), \(k=2\), and \(m=1\) into Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:matching\text{-}full\text{-}modulus}\). Its two exponents become
\[
\frac{k+1}{k+m+1}=\frac34,
\qquad
\frac{k}{(k+n/2)(m+1)}=\frac13,
\]
and therefore, for sufficiently small positive \(\varepsilon\),
\[
c\varepsilon^{3/4}
\leq\Omega_{2,1}(\varepsilon)
\leq C\varepsilon^{1/3}.
\tag{1}
\]
This is a bracket, not a claim that the full modulus has been shown to be of exact order \(\varepsilon^{3/4}\). Independently, Theorem \(\mathrm{thm:radial\text{-}subclass\text{-}frontier}\) gives
\[
\Omega_{2,1}^{\mathrm{rad}}(\varepsilon)
\asymp
\varepsilon^{(2+1)/(2+1+1)}
=\varepsilon^{3/4},
\tag{2}
\]
which is the two-sided sharp statement on the radial subclass.

If \(D_H(\theta,\widetilde\theta)=0\), Proposition \(\mathrm{prop:identification\text{-}hypothesis\text{-}comparison}\) applies directly and shows that the coordinates differ by one common node permutation and coordinatewise circle diffeomorphisms. Proposition \(\mathrm{prop:symmetric\text{-}quotient\text{-}pseudometric}\) then gives vanishing \(d_{\mathrm{rep}}\) for such equivalent encoders. The logical direction used here is quotient equivalence implying zero discrepancy; no converse property of \(d_{\mathrm{rep}}\) is invoked.

It remains to verify the plateau boundary. Suppose that a contrast \(r_{\theta i}\) is constant on a nonempty open arc \(I\subset\mathbb T\). Differentiability gives
\[
r_{\theta i}'(t)=0
\qquad (t\in I).
\]
By Definition of the critical set, \(I\subseteq Z_{\theta i}\). A nonempty open arc contains infinitely many points, whereas Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:critical\text{-}cardinality}\) requires \(|Z_{\theta i}|\leq J<\infty\). This contradiction proves that plateaus are outside the class covered by (1) and (2).
