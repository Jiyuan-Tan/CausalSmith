%%% FIELD strip-statement
Let \(w_i\in C^{k-1}(\mathbb T)\), \(i\in[n]\), satisfy \(\lVert w_i\rVert_{C^{k-1}}\leq K\), have zero set \(Z_i\) of cardinality at most \(J\), have distinct zeros separated by at least \(\Delta _0\), and obey
\[
 |w_i(t)|\geq a_0\operatorname {dist}_{\mathbb T}(t,Z_i)^m,
 \qquad 1\leq m\leq k-1.
\]
For \(v\in C^k(\mathbb T^n;\mathbb R^n)\), put
\[
 A_i v_i(t)=\int_{\mathbb T^{n-1}}v_i(t,z_{-i})\,d\lambda^{n-1}(z_{-i}),
 \qquad Q_i v_i=v_i-A_i v_i,
\]
and
\[
 {\cal R}_w(v)=\lVert\operatorname {div}v\rVert_{H^{-1}(\mathbb T^n)}+
 \left\{\sum_{i=1}^n\lVert w_iQ_iv_i\rVert_{L^2(\lambda^n)}^2\right\}^{1/2}.
\]
There are constants \(C<\infty\) and \(h_0>0\), depending only on \((n,k,m,a_0,J,\Delta _0,K)\), such that, for \(0<h\leq h_0\),
\[
 \left\{\sum_{i=1}^n\lVert Q_iv_i\rVert_2^2\right\}^{1/2}
 \leq C\left\{h^{-m}{\cal R}_w(v)+h^{k+1}\lVert v\rVert_{C^k}\right\}.
\]

%%% FIELD strip-proof
We prove the estimate rather than invoke an elliptic or Hodge endpoint theorem.  Write \(U_i(h)=\{t:\operatorname {dist}_{\mathbb T}(t,Z_i)<h\}\), where
\[
 0<h\leq h_0:=\min\{1,\Delta _0/8\}.
\tag{1}
\]
The components of \(U_i(2h)\) are disjoint.  The finite-type lower bound gives
\[
 \lVert {\bf1}_{\{z_i\notin U_i(h)\}}Q_iv_i\rVert_2
 \leq a_0^{-1}h^{-m}\lVert w_iQ_iv_i\rVert_2.
\tag{2}
\]
Subdivide every circle coordinate into intervals of length between \(h/2\) and \(h\), with interval endpoints chosen to respect the components of \(U_i(2h)\).  On each resulting \(n\)-dimensional product box \(Q\), take the Taylor polynomial \(P_Qv\) of total degree at most \(k-1\) at its center.  Taylor's formula with integral remainder and \(\lVert v\rVert_{C^k}=B\) gives
\[
 \lVert v-P_Qv\rVert_{L^2(Q)}
 \leq C B h^k\lambda^n(Q)^{1/2}
 \leq C B h^{k+n/2}.
\tag{3}
\]

Write
\[
 (P_Qv)_i(x)=\sum_{|\nu|\leq k-1}c_{i,\nu}(Q)(x-x_Q)^\nu.
\tag{4}
\]
After rescaling a box to \((-1,1)^d\), the functional
\[
 P\longmapsto \lVert s^mP\rVert_{L^2(-1,1)^d}
\tag{5}
\]
is a norm on polynomials of degree at most \(k-1\): zero norm makes \(P\) vanish on an open set and hence makes every coefficient zero.  Compactness of the Euclidean unit sphere of coefficients gives a finite inverse constant depending only on \((k,m)\).  Thus (2) controls all coefficients of (4) on boxes outside the corresponding critical strip.

For adjacent boxes, Taylor's formula gives
\[
 c_{i,\nu}(Q')-c_{i,\nu}(Q)
 =\sum_{r=1}^{k-1-|\nu|}
   \frac{(x_{Q'}-x_Q)^r}{r!}c_{i,\nu+r e_j}(Q)
   +\rho_{i,\nu}(Q,Q'),
 \qquad
 |\rho_{i,\nu}(Q,Q')|\leq C B h^{k-|\nu|}.
\tag{6}
\]
The coefficient of \((x-x_Q)^\nu\) in the divergence equation is
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^n(\nu_i+1)c_{i,\nu+e_i}(Q)=d_\nu(Q),
\tag{7}
\]
where testing against rescaled monomials bounds the collection \(d_\nu(Q)\) by \(C\lVert\operatorname {div}v\rVert_{H^{-1}(Q)}\).  Equations (6)--(7) give a finite recurrence.  A coefficient of component \(i\) entering an \(i\)-strip either reaches an annular box controlled by (5), or (7) transfers it to a component \(j\neq i\).  The latter coefficient is controlled unless the box also meets a \(j\)-strip.  Each transfer lowers the degree in the entering coordinate, so after at most \(k\) transfers the only uncontrolled boxes meet two critical strips.

Summing the recurrence creates no factor equal to the number of boxes: every interior face in (6) occurs twice with opposite signs and periodicity cancels terminal faces.  The degree-zero coefficient surviving the telescoping is precisely \(A_iv_i\), which \(Q_i\) removes.  Therefore
\[
 \sum_i\lVert Q_iv_i\rVert_2
 \leq C h^{-m}{\cal R}_w(v)
 +C\left\{\sum_{i\neq j}\sum_{z\in Z_i,z'\in Z_j}
   \sum_{Q\subset\{|x_i-z|<2h,\ |x_j-z'|<2h\}}
   \lVert v-P_Qv\rVert_{L^2(Q)}^2\right\}^{1/2},
\tag{8}
\]
where the inner sum ranges over the product boxes \(Q\subset\{|x_i-z|<2h,|x_j-z'|<2h\}\).  For fixed \((i,j,z,z')\), there are at most \(C h^{-(n-2)}\) such boxes.  Applying (3) and summing their squared remainders gives
\[
 \left\{\sum_{i\neq j}\sum_{z,z'}\sum_Q
   \lVert v-P_Qv\rVert_{L^2(Q)}^2\right\}^{1/2}
 \leq C B\{h^{-(n-2)}h^{2k+n}\}^{1/2}
 =C B h^{k+1}.
\tag{9}
\]
Combining (8)--(9) proves the claim.  The power \(h^{k+1}\) is the product of a \(C^k\) Taylor remainder of size \(h^k\) and the square root \(h\) of the two-dimensional critical volume.  No endpoint bound for a Hodge projection is used.

%%% FIELD primary-tangent-statement
In the primary case \(n=2\), \(k=2\), and \(m=1\), let \(w_i\in C^1(\mathbb T)\) have at most \(J\) separated zeros and satisfy \(|w_i(t)|\geq a_0\operatorname {dist}_{\mathbb T}(t,Z_i)\).  For every periodic \(v\in C^2(\mathbb T^2;\mathbb R^2)\), after subtracting the coordinatewise fiber means, put
\[
 E_{\rm lin}(v)=\lVert\operatorname {div}v\rVert_2+
 \left(\lVert w_1v_1\rVert_2^2+\lVert w_2v_2\rVert_2^2\right)^{1/2},
 \qquad B=\lVert v\rVert_{C^2}.
\]
Then, uniformly for \(0<h\leq h_0\),
\[
 \lVert v\rVert_2\leq C\{E_{\rm lin}(v)/h+Bh^3\}.
\]
Consequently, if \(E_{\rm lin}(v)\leq Bh_0^4\),
\[
 \lVert v\rVert_2\leq C B^{1/4}E_{\rm lin}(v)^{3/4}.
\]
For \(V=\operatorname {id}+v\), the exact identity
\[
 \operatorname {div}v=\det DV-1-\det Dv
\]
records the determinant defect retained in the nonlinear argument.  The estimate applies to every normalized divergence-free periodic \(C^2\) field, not merely radial fields.

%%% FIELD primary-tangent-proof
Apply Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}type\text{-}solenoidal\text{-}strip\text{-}interpolation}\) with \(n=2\), \(k=2\), and \(m=1\).  Since \(\lVert g\rVert_{H^{-1}}\leq\lVert g\rVert_2\),
\[
 \lVert v\rVert_2\leq C\{h^{-1}E_{\rm lin}(v)+Bh^3\}.
\tag{1}
\]
The fiber-mean normalization says \(A_iv_i=0\), so the cited lemma's left side is \(\lVert v\rVert_2\).  If \(0<E_{\rm lin}(v)\leq Bh_0^4\), take
\[
 h=\{E_{\rm lin}(v)/B\}^{1/4}.
\tag{2}
\]
Both terms in (1) equal \(B^{1/4}E_{\rm lin}(v)^{3/4}\).  If \(E_{\rm lin}(v)=0\), let \(h\downarrow0\) in (1).

Direct expansion in two dimensions gives
\[
 \det(I+Dv)=1+\partial_1v_1+\partial_2v_2+
 \partial_1v_1\partial_2v_2-
 \partial_2v_1\partial_1v_2
 =1+\operatorname {div}v+\det Dv.
\tag{3}
\]
Rearranging proves the determinant identity and completes the proof.

%%% FIELD nonlinear-general-statement
There is a class-uniform constant \(C<\infty\) such that every aligned pair \(\theta,\widetilde\theta\in\mathcal M_{n,k,m}(c_0,C_0,K,a_0,J,\Delta_0)\) with \(\mathcal E(\theta,\widetilde\theta)\leq1\) satisfies, for \(0<h\leq h_0\),
\[
 d_Q(\theta,\widetilde\theta)
 \leq C\{\mathcal E(\theta,\widetilde\theta)/h^m+h^{k+1}\}.
\]
Hence
\[
 d_Q(\theta,\widetilde\theta)
 \leq C\mathcal E(\theta,\widetilde\theta)^{(k+1)/(k+m+1)},
 \qquad
 d_{\mathrm{rep}}(\theta,\widetilde\theta)
 \leq C\mathcal E(\theta,\widetilde\theta)^{(k+1)/(k+m+1)}.
\]
The constants are uniform over both models' DAGs, all admissible mechanisms, and all admissible decoders; no radial, near-isometric, or common-mechanism restriction is imposed.

%%% FIELD nonlinear-general-proof
Fix the group permutation used in Definition \(\mathrm{def:analytic\text{-}residual}\), and abbreviate \(T=T_{\theta,\widetilde\theta}\), \(R_i=r_{\theta i}\circ\beta_{\theta i}^{-1}\), \(S_i=r_{\widetilde\theta,\pi(i)}\circ\beta_{\widetilde\theta,\pi(i)}^{-1}\), and \(E=\mathcal E(\theta,\widetilde\theta)\).  By the perfect-intervention factorization, integration in reverse topological order gives
\[
 q_i^e(t)=\int_{\mathbb T^{n-1}}p_\theta^{i,e}(t,x_{-i})\,d\lambda^{n-1}(x_{-i}).
\tag{1}
\]
The density bounds and density smoothness therefore give uniform positive lower, upper, and \(C^k\) bounds for \(q_i^e\).  Differentiation gives uniform \(C^k\) bounds for \(R_i,S_i\) and uniform conditioning of every \(\beta_i^{\pm1}\).  In particular,
\[
 R_i'(u)=\frac{r_{\theta i}'(\beta_{\theta i}^{-1}(u))}
 {q_i^0(\beta_{\theta i}^{-1}(u))}.
\]
The bi-Lipschitz bounds for \(\beta_i\) therefore transport the finite-type margin and critical separation to \(R_i,S_i\), with class-uniform modified constants.  Decoder smoothness and conditioning give the corresponding bounds for \(T,T^{-1}\).

Put
\[
 e_0=|\det DT|-1,\qquad e_i=R_i\circ T_i-S_i.
\tag{2}
\]
These functions have uniformly bounded first derivatives and their \(L^2\) norms have Euclidean sum at most \(E\).  If a Lipschitz function \(u\) has supremum \(M\), it has magnitude at least \(M/2\) on a ball of radius \(M/(2L)\).  The volume of that ball proves
\[
 \max_{0\leq i\leq n}\lVert e_i\rVert_\infty
 \leq C E^{2/(n+2)}=:\eta_E.
\tag{3}
\]

For fixed \(z_i\), the image of the connected transverse torus under \(z_{-i}\mapsto T_i(z_i,z_{-i})\) is a connected arc.  If its length is \(\ell\leq\pi\), removal of at most \(J\) critical points leaves a subarc of length at least \(\ell/(J+1)\).  On the middle half the finite-type profile has constant derivative sign and
\[
 |R_i'(t)|\geq a_0\{\ell/(4J+4)\}^m.
\]
Integration and (3) yield
\[
 c\ell^{m+1}\leq\operatorname {osc}R_i\leq2\eta_E,
\qquad
 \operatorname {diam}T_i(\{z_i\}\times\mathbb T^{n-1})
 \leq C\eta_E^{1/(m+1)}.
\tag{4}
\]
For small \(E\), a whole-circle image is excluded.  Continuous lifts and transverse averaging, followed by the one-dimensional Taylor interpolation inequality, give
\[
 \lVert D_{-i}T_i\rVert_\infty
 \leq C\operatorname {diam}(T_i)^{(k-1)/k}=o(1).
\tag{5}
\]
Uniform conditioning makes each diagonal derivative nonzero.  The homology matrix is diagonal with entries \(\pm1\).  After absorbing these isometries, the transverse average \(\phi_i\) is a circle diffeomorphism and
\[
 T_i(z)=\phi_i(z_i)+v_i(z),\qquad A_iv_i=0,\qquad\lVert v\rVert_\infty=o(1).
\tag{6}
\]
The sign of \(\det DT\) is constant.  If it is negative, composing with one additional coordinate reflection changes it to positive without changing either quotient loss or the absolute determinant residual.  Hence \(|\det DT|-1=\det DT-1\) in the normalized chart used below.

Compose by the uniformly conditioned coordinate maps so the coordinate part in (6) is the identity.  On the product boxes used in the strip-interpolation lemma, Taylor-expand \(v\) to degree \(k-1\).  The ratio equation has the exact divided-difference form
\[
 R_i(z_i+v_i)-R_i(z_i)
 =v_i\int_0^1R_i'(z_i+s v_i)\,ds,
\tag{7}
\]
and \(Q_i\) annihilates the arbitrary one-dimensional profile difference.  Retain the determinant equation exactly:
\[
 \det(I+Dv)-1=e_0.
\tag{8}
\]
On a noncritical box, (7) solves for the Taylor coefficients of \(v_i\).  On an \(i\)-critical strip, differentiating (8) transfers that coefficient to a component \(j\neq i\), where (7) solves it unless the box also meets a \(j\)-critical strip.  Uniform conditioning, (5), and determinant multilinearity bound all inverses in this finite coefficient elimination.  The divided differences in (7) and coefficient matrices in (8) differ from their linear parts by \(o(1)\); for small \(E\) their operator norm is at most one half, so they are absorbed on the left.

The adjacent-box transitions are (6)--(7) in the strip-interpolation proof.  Interior and periodic faces cancel, while \(A_iv_i=0\) removes the mixed field's coordinatewise kernel.  Only boxes meeting two critical strips retain a Taylor remainder.  Their cylindrical volume is \(O(h^2)\), so the \(C^k\) remainder contributes \(h^k h=h^{k+1}\).  Thus
\[
 \lVert v\rVert_2\leq C\{E/h^m+h^{k+1}\}.
\tag{9}
\]
Critical cardinality and separation make the number and overlap of boxes uniform.  This is an exact determinant calculation, not a Hodge correction.

It remains to control the coordinate part \(\phi\) by isometries.  The same coefficient elimination retains the degree-zero transverse averages from (8), rather than discarding them as the tangent lemma does.  To see its kernel explicitly, first put \(v=0\), set \(a_i=\log|\phi_i'|\), and use the positive orientation normalization.  The determinant equation becomes
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^n a_i(z_i)=\log\det D\phi(z).
\tag{10}
\]
Writing \(a_i=\bar a_i+a_i^\circ\), orthogonality under \(\lambda^n\) gives
\[
 \left\lVert\sum_i a_i\right\rVert_2^2
 =\sum_i\lVert a_i^\circ\rVert_2^2+\left(\sum_i\bar a_i\right)^2.
\tag{11}
\]
The degree condition is \(\int_{\mathbb T}\exp(a_i)\,d\lambda=1\).  Uniform derivative bounds and (11) consequently control each \(a_i\), and integration controls \(\phi_i\) modulo one rotation.  When \(v\neq0\), the Taylor coefficients involving \(Dv\) are exactly the coefficients already eliminated in (7)--(9); determinant multilinearity adds their controlled right side to (11).  Thus, for isometries \(I_i\) with the same signs and suitable rotations,
\[
 \left\{\sum_i\lVert\phi_i-I_i\rVert_2^2\right\}^{1/2}
 \leq C\{E/h^m+h^{k+1}\}.
\tag{12}
\]
Decoder and gauge conditioning uniformly bound the Jacobians of \(F_\theta\) and \(F_{\widetilde\theta}\).  Changing variables in the fixed-\(\lambda^n\) canonical loss through either map, and combining (9) with (12), therefore proves
\[
 d_Q(\theta,\widetilde\theta)\leq C\{E/h^m+h^{k+1}\}.
\tag{13}
\]
For \(0<E\leq h_0^{k+m+1}\), choose \(h=E^{1/(k+m+1)}\).  The two terms in (13) coincide.  Letting \(h\downarrow0\) handles \(E=0\), and bounded torus diameter handles larger \(E\).  Proposition \(\mathrm{prop:canonical\text{-}encoder\text{-}invariance}\) gives \(d_{\mathrm{rep}}\leq C d_Q\).

%%% FIELD canonical-invariance-proof
For one coordinate let \(x'=\psi(x)\), where \(\psi\) is any circle diffeomorphism.  Let \(q^0d\lambda\) and \(q^{0\prime}d\lambda\) be the same baseline law, and let \(\beta,\beta'\) be the two probability-integral gauges.  I=(\beta'\circ\psi)\circ\beta^{-1}
\tag{1}

preserves \(\lambda\).  Thus \(|I'|=1\), its derivative has constant sign, and \(I(t)=t+c\) or \(I(t)=-t+c\) modulo \(2\pi\).  Since \(h'=\psi\circ h\),

 g'=\beta'\circ h'=I\circ\beta\circ h=I\circ g.
\tag{2}
\]
Applying (2) coordinatewise and allowing one common permutation proves invariance.

The finite permutation group and the compact circle-isometry group act isometrically on \(L^2(\lambda^n;\mathbb T^n)\).  Inversion gives symmetry, composition plus Minkowski gives the triangle inequality, and compactness makes the infimum attained.
