Put \(s=s_{\mathrm{obs}}=k-1\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:smoothness\text{-}order}\) gives \(s\geq 1\).  All densities below are with respect to normalized Haar measure \(\lambda^n\) on the compact support \(\mathbb T^n\).  For \(a\in\mathcal A\), write
\[
 u_a(y)=\frac{dP_\theta^a}{d\lambda^n}(y).
\]
By the pushforward definition of \(P_\theta^a\), the change-of-variables identity is
\[
 u_a(y)=p_\theta^a\!\left(f^{-1}(y)\right)
          \left|\det Df^{-1}(y)\right|.
\tag{1}
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:density\text{-}bounds}\) bounds the first factor in (1) between \(c_0\) and \(C_0\).  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:decoder\text{-}conditioning}\) puts every singular value of \(Df\) in \([K^{-1},K]\), so every singular value of \(Df^{-1}\) is in the same interval and
\[
 b:=c_0K^{-n}\leq u_a(y)\leq C_0K^n=:B.
\tag{2}
\]
The product and chain rules applied to (1), using Assumptions \(\mathrm{ass:density\text{-}smoothness}\) and \(\mathrm{ass:decoder\text{-}smoothness}\), give a class-uniform constant \(C_1<\infty\) such that
\[
 \max_{a\in\mathcal A}\lVert u_a\rVert_{C^s(\mathbb T^n)}\leq C_1.
\tag{3}
\]
Only \(s=k-1\) derivatives are used: the determinant in (1) contains first derivatives of \(f^{-1}\), and its derivatives through order \(k-1\) use derivatives of \(f^{-1}\) through order \(k\).

For each environment \(a\), let \(Y_{a1},\ldots,Y_{aN}\) denote the independent observations supplied by Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:iid\text{-}environment\text{-}sampling}\).  For \(\ell\in\mathbb Z^n\), define
\[
 c_{a\ell}=\int_{\mathbb T^n}u_a(y)e^{-\mathrm i\ell^\top y}\,d\lambda^n(y),
 \qquad
 \widehat c_{a\ell}=\frac1N\sum_{r=1}^N e^{-\mathrm i\ell^\top Y_{ar}},
\]
and let \(u_{a,L}\) be the Fourier projection onto \(\{\ell:\lVert\ell\rVert_\infty\leq L\}\).  Repeated integration by parts on the torus gives
\[
 (\mathrm i\ell_j)^s c_{a\ell}
 =\int_{\mathbb T^n}\partial_j^s u_a(y)e^{-\mathrm i\ell^\top y}\,d\lambda^n(y).
\]
Orthogonality of the characters, followed by the Hilbert-space projection inequality, therefore yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \lVert u_a-u_{a,L}\rVert_2^2
 &=\sum_{\lVert\ell\rVert_\infty>L}|c_{a\ell}|^2\\
 &\leq L^{-2s}\sum_{j=1}^n
       \sum_{\ell\in\mathbb Z^n}|\ell_j|^{2s}|c_{a\ell}|^2\\
 &\leq L^{-2s}\sum_{j=1}^n\lVert\partial_j^s u_a\rVert_2^2
 \leq C_2L^{-2s},
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
where (3) justifies the final bound and the boundary terms vanish by periodicity.

Under Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:iid\text{-}environment\text{-}sampling}\), for every \(a,\ell\),
\[
 \mathbb E_\theta\widehat c_{a\ell}=c_{a\ell},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_\theta|\widehat c_{a\ell}-c_{a\ell}|^2
 =\frac1N\operatorname{Var}_\theta(e^{-\mathrm i\ell^\top Y_{a1}})
 \leq\frac1N.
\tag{5}
\]
Expanding the finite trigonometric sum and using character orthogonality shows that the real part in Definition \(\mathrm{def:certified\text{-}spectral\text{-}estimator}\) cannot increase its \(L^2\) error relative to the real density.  Consequently,
\[
 \mathbb E_\theta\lVert s_L^a-u_{a,L}\rVert_2^2
 \leq\sum_{\lVert\ell\rVert_\infty\leq L}
       \mathbb E_\theta|\widehat c_{a\ell}-c_{a\ell}|^2
 \leq\frac{(2L+1)^n}{N}.
\tag{6}
\]
Because \(|\mathcal A|=1+2n\) is fixed, (4), (6), Markov's inequality, and a union bound imply, uniformly in \(\theta\),
\[
 \max_{a\in\mathcal A}\lVert s_L^a-u_a\rVert_2
 =O_{\mathbb P}\!\left(L^{-s}+\sqrt{L^n/N}\right).
\tag{7}
\]
More explicitly, after division by the quantity in parentheses, the supremum over \(\theta\) of the probability of exceeding \(M\) is bounded by \(C_3/M^2\).  With the cutoff in Definition \(\mathrm{def:certified\text{-}spectral\text{-}estimator}\),
\[
 L_N\asymp N^{1/(2s+n)},
 \qquad
 L_N^{-s}\asymp\sqrt{L_N^n/N}
 \asymp N^{-s/(2s+n)}=\rho_N.
\tag{8}
\]
Thus (7) is a uniform \(O_{\mathbb P}(\rho_N)\) assertion without a logarithmic loss.

We next check that positivity correction and normalization preserve this rate.  Let \(\eta_N=N^{-1}\), \(g_L^a=\max\{\eta_N,s_L^a\}\), and \(I_a=\int g_L^a,d\lambda^n\).  Since \(s\geq1\) and \(n\geq2\), one has \(s/(2s+n)<1\), whence
\[
 \eta_N=o(\rho_N).
\tag{9}
\]
For all sufficiently large \(N\), (2) gives \(u_a\geq b>\eta_N\).  Pointwise projection onto \([\eta_N,\infty)\) then gives
\[
 |g_L^a-u_a|\leq|s_L^a-u_a|,
 \qquad
 |I_a-1|\leq\lVert g_L^a-u_a\rVert_1
 \leq\lVert g_L^a-u_a\rVert_2.
\tag{10}
\]
On the event that the last norm is at most \(1/2\), \(I_a\geq1/2\).  From the exact definition
\(
 \widehat p_L^a=\eta_N+(1-\eta_N)g_L^a/I_a
\)
and (2), (9), and (10),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \lVert\widehat p_L^a-u_a\rVert_2
 &\leq \eta_N\lVert1-u_a\rVert_2
 +(1-\eta_N)\left\lVert\frac{g_L^a}{I_a}-u_a\right\rVert_2\\
 &\leq C_4\{\eta_N+\lVert g_L^a-u_a\rVert_2\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
The complement of this event has uniformly vanishing probability by (7)--(8).  Hence (11) and the explicit \(C_3/M^2\) tail following (7) prove
\[
 \max_{a\in\mathcal A}\lVert\widehat p_L^a-u_a\rVert_2
 =O_{\mathbb P}(\rho_N)
\quad\text{uniformly in }\theta.
\tag{12}
\]
The lower overlap bound (2) permits division in the Hellinger identity:
\[
\begin{aligned}
 H^2(P_\theta^a,\widehat p_L^a d\lambda^n)
 &=\frac12\int
 \frac{(u_a-\widehat p_L^a)^2}
 {(\sqrt{u_a}+\sqrt{\widehat p_L^a})^2}\,d\lambda^n\\
 &\leq\frac1{2b}\lVert u_a-\widehat p_L^a\rVert_2^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]
Writing \(\mathbb P_\theta=(P_\theta^a:a\in\mathcal A)\) and
\[
 \Delta(\mathbb P_\theta,\widehat{\mathbb P}_L)
 =\left\{\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}
 H^2(P_\theta^a,\widehat p_L^a d\lambda^n)\right\}^{1/2},
\]
equations (12)--(13) give
\[
 \Delta(\mathbb P_\theta,\widehat{\mathbb P}_L)
 =O_{\mathbb P}(\rho_N)
\quad\text{uniformly in }\theta.
\tag{14}
\]

For completeness, the certified nets required by Definition \(\mathrm{def:certified\text{-}spectral\text{-}estimator}\) exist under the displayed assumptions.  Indeed, (3)--(4) show that, for any \(\delta>0\), one common finite Fourier cutoff makes every tail at most \(\delta\).  At that cutoff the coefficient vectors occupy a bounded subset of a finite-dimensional Euclidean space, which has a finite grid.  Choosing one model from each occupied grid cell gives a finite \(L^2\)-net with centers in the model class.  Applying this simultaneously to the finite family \(\mathcal A\), and using (2) and the analogue of (13) for two class densities, gives a finite complete-family Hellinger net.  Closing it under the finite group \(\mathfrak S_n\) preserves finiteness and permits any optimizing common node permutation to be absorbed into the chosen representative.

Choose accordingly \(\vartheta_N\in\mathfrak M_N\) whose labels have been relabeled so that
\[
 \left\{\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}H^2(P_\theta^a,P_{\vartheta_N}^a)\right\}^{1/2}
 \leq\delta_N.
\tag{15}
\]
The Hellinger distance is the \(L^2\) distance of square-root densities multiplied by \(2^{-1/2}\); the ordinary \(L^2\) triangle inequality followed by the Euclidean triangle inequality over \(a\in\mathcal A\) therefore gives a triangle inequality for \(\Delta\).  The minimum-distance inequality in Definition \(\mathrm{def:certified\text{-}spectral\text{-}estimator}\), (15), and admissibility of the identity permutation in Definition \(\mathrm{def:full\text{-}law\text{-}distance}\) now give the deterministic oracle inequality
\[
\begin{aligned}
 D_H(\theta,\widehat\theta_N)
 &\leq\Delta(\mathbb P_\theta,\widehat{\mathbb P}_L)
      +\Delta(\widehat{\mathbb P}_L,\mathbb P_{\widehat\theta_N})\\
 &\leq\Delta(\mathbb P_\theta,\widehat{\mathbb P}_L)
      +\Delta(\widehat{\mathbb P}_L,\mathbb P_{\vartheta_N})+\delta_N\\
 &\leq2\Delta(\mathbb P_\theta,\widehat{\mathbb P}_L)+2\delta_N.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
Since \(\delta_N=o(\rho_N)\), (14) and (16) yield a constant \(C_5\), independent of \(\theta,N,M\), such that
\[
 \limsup_{N\to\infty}\sup_{\theta\in\mathcal M_{n,k,m}}
 \Pr_\theta\{D_H(\theta,\widehat\theta_N)>M\rho_N\}
 \leq \frac{C_5}{M^2}
\]
after an immaterial fixed rescaling of \(M\).  Letting \(M\to\infty\) proves the asserted uniform limit and hence the stated uniform \(O_{\mathbb P}(\rho_N)\) law-recovery rate.

Let
\[
 \mathcal S_N(\theta)=
 \left\{\Delta(\mathbb P_\theta,\widehat{\mathbb P}_L)
 \leq c_\alpha\rho_N\right\},
\]
where \(\Delta\) is the fixed-label Euclidean sum of the Hellinger distances over the finite environment set \(\mathcal A\).  The calibration in Definition \(\mathrm{def:quotient\text{-}confidence\text{-}set}\), under Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:iid\text{-}environment\text{-}sampling}\), states
\[
 \inf_{\theta\in\mathcal M_{n,k,m}}
 \Pr_\theta\{\mathcal S_N(\theta)\}
 \geq1-\alpha-o(1).
\tag{1}
\]
The deterministic minimum-distance comparison proved in Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:certified\text{-}spectral\text{-}law\text{-}recovery}\) is
\[
 D_H(\theta,\widehat\theta_N)
 \leq2\Delta(\mathbb P_\theta,\widehat{\mathbb P}_L)+2\delta_N.
\tag{2}
\]
Indeed, (2) uses a \(\delta_N\)-net representative, the defining \(\delta_N\)-approximate minimum-distance inequality, and two applications of the finite-family Hellinger triangle inequality; closure of the net under common node permutations converts the net approximation to the fixed labeling used by the objective.

On \(\mathcal S_N(\theta)\), equations (1)--(2) imply
\[
 D_H(\theta,\widehat\theta_N)
 \leq2c_\alpha\rho_N+2\delta_N.
\tag{3}
\]
The true model \(\theta\) is a member of \(\mathcal M_{n,k,m}(c_0,C_0,K,a_0,J,\Delta_0)\).  Therefore (3) makes \(\theta\) an admissible representing model in Definition \(\mathrm{def:quotient\text{-}confidence\text{-}set}\), and that definition places its encoder class \([h_\theta]\) in \(\mathcal C_N(\alpha)\).  Thus
\[
 \Pr_\theta\{[h_\theta]\in\mathcal C_N(\alpha)\}
 \geq\Pr_\theta\{\mathcal S_N(\theta)\}.
\]
Taking the infimum over \(\theta\) and applying (1) proves the claimed uniform coverage.  This argument uses the quotient only to report the set element; it does not infer quotient equivalence from the vanishing of \(d_{\mathrm{rep}}\).

The coverage assertion is exactly Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:corrected\text{-}confidence\text{-}coverage}\), whose sampling premise is Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:iid\text{-}environment\text{-}sampling}\).  It remains to bound the displayed representing-model diameter.  Put
\[
 r_N=2c_\alpha\rho_N+2\delta_N,
 \qquad
 q=\frac{k}{(k+n/2)(m+1)}.
\tag{1}
\]

We first derive the required triangle inequality directly from Definition \(\mathrm{def:full\text{-}law\text{-}distance}\).  For three models \(\vartheta_1,\vartheta_2,\vartheta_3\), let \(\pi\) and \(\sigma\) minimize the finite permutation criteria for the first--second and second--third pairs.  Hellinger distance obeys the triangle inequality because it is \(2^{-1/2}\) times the \(L^2(\lambda^n)\) distance between square-root densities.  Hence
\[
 H(P_{\vartheta_1}^{\mathrm{obs}},P_{\vartheta_3}^{\mathrm{obs}})
 \leq H(P_{\vartheta_1}^{\mathrm{obs}},P_{\vartheta_2}^{\mathrm{obs}})
     +H(P_{\vartheta_2}^{\mathrm{obs}},P_{\vartheta_3}^{\mathrm{obs}})
\tag{2}
\]
and, for every \(i\in[n]\) and \(e\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
 H(P_{\vartheta_1}^{i,e},P_{\vartheta_3}^{\sigma(\pi(i)),e})
 \leq H(P_{\vartheta_1}^{i,e},P_{\vartheta_2}^{\pi(i),e})
     +H(P_{\vartheta_2}^{\pi(i),e},P_{\vartheta_3}^{\sigma(\pi(i)),e}).
\tag{3}
\]
Apply the Euclidean Minkowski inequality to the observational coordinate and the \(2n\) intervention coordinates in (2)--(3), and reindex the second intervention sum by \(j=\pi(i)\).  Since \(\sigma\circ\pi\in\mathfrak S_n\) is admissible for the first--third criterion,
\[
 D_H(\vartheta_1,\vartheta_3)
 \leq D_H(\vartheta_1,\vartheta_2)+D_H(\vartheta_2,\vartheta_3).
\tag{4}
\]
Replacing a permutation by its inverse and reindexing the same finite sum also gives
\[
 D_H(\vartheta_1,\vartheta_2)=D_H(\vartheta_2,\vartheta_1).
\tag{5}
\]

Fix any two representing models \(\vartheta,\vartheta'\) occurring in the diameter in the theorem statement.  Their defining constraints and (4)--(5) imply
\[
\begin{aligned}
 D_H(\vartheta,\vartheta')
 &\leq D_H(\vartheta,\widehat\theta_N)
       +D_H(\widehat\theta_N,\vartheta')\\
 &=D_H(\vartheta,\widehat\theta_N)
       +D_H(\vartheta',\widehat\theta_N)\\
 &\leq2r_N=4c_\alpha\rho_N+4\delta_N.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
Because \(s_{\mathrm{obs}}=k-1\geq1\), the definition
\(
 \rho_N=N^{-s_{\mathrm{obs}}/(2s_{\mathrm{obs}}+n)}
\)
implies \(\rho_N\to0\).  The condition \(\delta_N=o(\rho_N)\) therefore makes the last line of (6) uniformly \(O(\rho_N)\).

Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:full\text{-}law\text{-}residual\text{-}reduction}\), applied using the optimizing common group permutation, supplies a class-uniform \(C_{\mathrm{red}}<\infty\) such that
\[
 \mathcal E(\vartheta,\vartheta')
 \leq C_{\mathrm{red}}D_H(\vartheta,\vartheta')
 \leq2C_{\mathrm{red}}r_N.
\tag{7}
\]
The right side tends to zero, so it is at most one for every sufficiently large \(N\), uniformly over all representing pairs.  Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:scalar\text{-}finite\text{-}type\text{-}weak\text{-}rigidity}\) is then applicable to the same aligned pair and gives, with a class-uniform \(C_{\mathrm{rig}}<\infty\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 d_{\mathrm{rep}}(\vartheta,\vartheta')
 &\leq C_{\mathrm{rig}}\mathcal E(\vartheta,\vartheta')^q\\
 &\leq C_{\mathrm{rig}}(2C_{\mathrm{red}}r_N)^q
 =O\!\left(\rho_N^{k/((k+n/2)(m+1))}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
Every constant in (8) is independent of the chosen pair and of the realized center \(\widehat\theta_N\).  Taking the supremum over all representing pairs therefore preserves (8).  The resulting bound is deterministic for all sufficiently large \(N\), and in particular is
\[
 O_{\mathbb P}\!\left\{\rho_N^{k/((k+n/2)(m+1))}\right\}.
\]

Finally, if \((n,k,m)=(2,2,1)\), then
\[
 q=\frac{2}{(2+2/2)(1+1)}=\frac13,
 \qquad
 s_{\mathrm{obs}}=1,
 \qquad
 \rho_N=N^{-1/(2+2)}=N^{-1/4}.
\]
Thus \(\rho_N^q=N^{-1/12}\), which proves the stated primary specialization.
