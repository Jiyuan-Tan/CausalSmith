%%% FIELD theorem-statement
Under independent sampling and for certified tolerance \(\delta_N=o(\rho_N)\), the positive Fourier minimum-distance estimator satisfies
\[
D_H(\theta,\widehat\theta_N)=O_{\mathbb P}(\rho_N)
\quad\text{and}\quad
d_{\mathrm{rep}}(\theta,\widehat\theta_N)
=O_{\mathbb P}\!\left\{\rho_N^{k/((k+n/2)(m+1))}\right\},
\]
uniformly over \(\mathcal M_{n,k,m}(c_0,C_0,K,a_0,J,\Delta_0)\). In the primary case \(n=2\), \(k=2\), and \(m=1\), the representation rate is \(N^{-1/12}\).
%%% FIELD theorem-proof
Put
\[
q=\frac{k}{(k+n/2)(m+1)}.
\tag{1}
\]
By Assumptions \(\mathrm{ass:dimension}\), \(\mathrm{ass:smoothness\text{-}order}\), and \(\mathrm{ass:critical\text{-}order}\), the restrictions built into \(\mathrm{def:model\text{-}class}\) give \(n\geq2\), \(k\geq2\), and \(m\geq1\). Hence \(q>0\). They also give \(s_{\mathrm{obs}}=k-1>0\).

First note explicitly why the corrected spectral scale has no logarithmic loss. The definitions
\[
L_N\asymp N^{1/(2s_{\mathrm{obs}}+n)}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\rho_N=N^{-s_{\mathrm{obs}}/(2s_{\mathrm{obs}}+n)}
\tag{2}
\]
imply
\[
L_N^{-s_{\mathrm{obs}}}
=O\!\left(N^{-s_{\mathrm{obs}}/(2s_{\mathrm{obs}}+n)}\right)
=O(\rho_N)
\tag{3}
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{L_N^n/N}
&=O\!\left(
N^{\frac{n}{2(2s_{\mathrm{obs}}+n)}-\frac12}
\right)\\
&=O\!\left(N^{-s_{\mathrm{obs}}/(2s_{\mathrm{obs}}+n)}\right)
=O(\rho_N).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]
Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:certified\text{-}spectral\text{-}law\text{-}recovery}\) applies under Assumptions \(\mathrm{ass:density\text{-}smoothness}\), \(\mathrm{ass:decoder\text{-}smoothness}\), and \(\mathrm{ass:iid\text{-}environment\text{-}sampling}\), the certified construction \(\mathrm{def:certified\text{-}spectral\text{-}estimator}\), and \(\delta_N=o(\rho_N)\). Its uniform Fourier expectation and second-moment bound, followed by Markov's inequality over the fixed finite environment family, uses exactly the bias--fluctuation balance (3)--(4). Therefore
\[
\lim_{M\to\infty}\limsup_{N\to\infty}
\sup_{\theta\in\mathcal M_{n,k,m}(c_0,C_0,K,a_0,J,\Delta_0)}
\Pr_\theta\!\left\{D_H(\theta,\widehat\theta_N)>M\rho_N\right\}=0.
\tag{5}
\]
This is the first asserted uniform stochastic order.

By \(\mathrm{def:certified\text{-}spectral\text{-}estimator}\), both \(\theta\) and \(\widehat\theta_N\) belong to the same bounded model class. Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:full\text{-}law\text{-}residual\text{-}reduction}\), with the common group permutation that optimizes the complete-family discrepancy and with the gauges in \(\mathrm{def:analytic\text{-}residual}\), supplies a class-uniform constant \(C_R<\infty\) such that
\[
\mathcal E(\theta,\widehat\theta_N)
\leq C_R D_H(\theta,\widehat\theta_N).
\tag{6}
\]
On the event \(D_H(\theta,\widehat\theta_N)\leq C_R^{-1}\), equation (6) gives \(\mathcal E(\theta,\widehat\theta_N)\leq1\). Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:scalar\text{-}finite\text{-}type\text{-}weak\text{-}rigidity}\), applied to this same aligned pair, then gives a class-uniform constant \(C_W<\infty\) for which
\[
\begin{aligned}
d_{\mathrm{rep}}(\theta,\widehat\theta_N)
&\leq C_W\mathcal E(\theta,\widehat\theta_N)^q\\
&\leq C_W C_R^qD_H(\theta,\widehat\theta_N)^q.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
This step uses only the one-way bound asserted by the weak-rigidity lemma; no converse characterization of the zero set of \(d_{\mathrm{rep}}\) is used.

Let \(C_*=C_WC_R^q\). Since \(s_{\mathrm{obs}}>0\), equation (2) gives \(\rho_N\to0\). Fix \(M>0\). For every sufficiently large \(N\), \(M\rho_N\leq C_R^{-1}\), and (7) yields the event inclusion
\[
\left\{d_{\mathrm{rep}}(\theta,\widehat\theta_N)>C_*M^q\rho_N^q\right\}
\subseteq
\left\{D_H(\theta,\widehat\theta_N)>M\rho_N\right\}.
\tag{8}
\]
Taking the supremum over the model class, then \(\limsup_{N\to\infty}\), and finally \(M\to\infty\), equations (5) and (8) imply
\[
\lim_{B\to\infty}\limsup_{N\to\infty}
\sup_{\theta\in\mathcal M_{n,k,m}(c_0,C_0,K,a_0,J,\Delta_0)}
\Pr_\theta\!\left\{
d_{\mathrm{rep}}(\theta,\widehat\theta_N)>B\rho_N^q
\right\}=0,
\tag{9}
\]
because \(B=C_*M^q\to\infty\) as \(M\to\infty\). This is precisely
\[
d_{\mathrm{rep}}(\theta,\widehat\theta_N)
=O_{\mathbb P}\!\left\{\rho_N^{k/((k+n/2)(m+1))}\right\}
\]
uniformly over the class.

Finally, in the primary case \((n,k,m)=(2,2,1)\), one has \(s_{\mathrm{obs}}=1\), and hence
\[
\rho_N=N^{-1/(2+2)}=N^{-1/4},
\qquad
q=\frac{2}{(2+2/2)(1+1)}=\frac13.
\tag{10}
\]
Therefore
\[
\rho_N^q=N^{-1/12},
\]
which proves the stated primary-case representation rate.
