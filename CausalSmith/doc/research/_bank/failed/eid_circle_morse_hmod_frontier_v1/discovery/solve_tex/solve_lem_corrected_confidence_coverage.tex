For two labeled complete law families \(\mathbb Q=(Q^a:a\in\mathcal A)\) and \(\mathbb R=(R^a:a\in\mathcal A)\), define the fixed-label product Hellinger distance
\[
 \Delta(\mathbb Q,\mathbb R)^2
 =H^2(Q^{\mathrm{obs}},R^{\mathrm{obs}})
  +\sum_{i\in[n]}\sum_{e=0}^1 H^2(Q^{i,e},R^{i,e}).
\tag{1}
\]
If \(\pi\in\mathfrak S_n\), let \(\pi\mathbb Q\) leave the observational component fixed and put \((\pi\mathbb Q)^{i,e}=Q^{\pi^{-1}(i),e}\).  The well-typed empirical complete-law orbit distance appearing in Definition \(\mathrm{def:quotient\text{-}confidence\text{-}set}\) is
\[
 \widehat D_H(\theta,\widehat{\mathbb P}_L)
 :=\min_{\pi\in\mathfrak S_n}
       \Delta(\pi\mathbb P_\theta,\widehat{\mathbb P}_L).
\tag{2}
\]
The minimum in (2) is attained because \(\mathfrak S_n\) is finite.  Moreover, by the definition of Hellinger distance,
\[
 H(Q,R)=2^{-1/2}\lVert \sqrt{dQ/d\mu}-\sqrt{dR/d\mu}\rVert_{L^2(\mu)}
\]
for any common dominating probability measure \(\mu\).  Minkowski's inequality first in each \(L^2(\mu)\) coordinate and then in their finite Euclidean direct sum therefore gives
\[
 \Delta(\mathbb Q,\mathbb R)
 \leq \Delta(\mathbb Q,\mathbb S)+\Delta(\mathbb S,\mathbb R).
\tag{3}
\]
Thus no unproved metric property is being used below.

We next prove the deterministic comparison that makes the calibration permutation-compatible.  Fix \(\theta\in\mathcal M_{n,k,m}(c_0,C_0,K,a_0,J,\Delta_0)\), and choose \(\pi_*\) attaining (2).  Common relabeling of the nodes preserves every defining condition of the model class, by Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:complete\text{-}grouping}\).  The \(\delta_N\)-net property and relabeling closure stipulated in Definition \(\mathrm{def:certified\text{-}spectral\text{-}estimator}\) consequently give a certified \(\vartheta_N\in\mathfrak M_N\) such that
\[
 \Delta(\pi_*\mathbb P_\theta,\mathbb P_{\vartheta_N})\leq\delta_N.
\tag{4}
\]
Indeed, the orbit net first gives a model within \(\delta_N\) after some common permutation, and closure of \(\mathfrak M_N\) under that permutation turns this into the fixed-label inequality (4).

Apply the defining \(\delta_N\)-approximate minimum-distance inequality to the candidate \(\vartheta_N\).  Equations (2)--(4) yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \Delta(\mathbb P_{\widehat\theta_N},\widehat{\mathbb P}_L)
 &\leq \Delta(\mathbb P_{\vartheta_N},\widehat{\mathbb P}_L)+\delta_N\\
 &\leq \Delta(\pi_*\mathbb P_\theta,\widehat{\mathbb P}_L)
       +\Delta(\pi_*\mathbb P_\theta,\mathbb P_{\vartheta_N})+\delta_N\\
 &\leq \widehat D_H(\theta,\widehat{\mathbb P}_L)+2\delta_N.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]
The permutation \(\pi_*\) is also an admissible alignment in Definition \(\mathrm{def:full\text{-}law\text{-}distance}\).  A further application of (3), followed by (5), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 D_H(\theta,\widehat\theta_N)
 &\leq \Delta(\pi_*\mathbb P_\theta,\mathbb P_{\widehat\theta_N})\\
 &\leq \Delta(\pi_*\mathbb P_\theta,\widehat{\mathbb P}_L)
       +\Delta(\widehat{\mathbb P}_L,\mathbb P_{\widehat\theta_N})\\
 &\leq 2\widehat D_H(\theta,\widehat{\mathbb P}_L)+2\delta_N.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
This also shows that minimizing the fixed-label criterion over the relabeling-closed sieve has the same orbit-invariant consequence as minimizing the empirical orbit criterion.

Under Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:iid\text{-}environment\text{-}sampling}\), Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:certified\text{-}spectral\text{-}law\text{-}recovery}\) supplies the uniform law-recovery rate used in the calibration.  By the calibration clause in Definition \(\mathrm{def:quotient\text{-}confidence\text{-}set}\), for fixed \(k,m\) and \(\alpha\in(0,1)\),
\[
 \inf_{\theta\in\mathcal M_{n,k,m}}
 \Pr_\theta\!\left\{
   \widehat D_H(\theta,\widehat{\mathbb P}_L)
   \leq c_\alpha\rho_N
 \right\}
 \geq 1-\alpha-o(1).
\tag{7}
\]
On the event in (7), inequality (6) implies
\[
 D_H(\theta,\widehat\theta_N)
 \leq 2c_\alpha\rho_N+2\delta_N.
\tag{8}
\]
The true \(\theta\) belongs to the displayed model class, so (8) makes \(\theta\) an admissible representing model in the defining set for \(\mathcal C_N(\alpha)\).  Hence
\[
 \left\{
   \widehat D_H(\theta,\widehat{\mathbb P}_L)\leq c_\alpha\rho_N
 \right\}
 \subseteq
 \left\{[h_\theta]\in\mathcal C_N(\alpha)\right\}.
\tag{9}
\]
Taking probabilities in (9), then the infimum over \(\theta\), and finally using (7), proves
\[
 \inf_{\theta\in\mathcal M_{n,k,m}}
 \Pr_\theta\{[h_\theta]\in\mathcal C_N(\alpha)\}
 \geq1-\alpha-o(1).
\]
The argument uses the quotient only through common node relabeling and the reported encoder class; it does not infer equivalence of two encoders from vanishing of an auxiliary discrepancy.
