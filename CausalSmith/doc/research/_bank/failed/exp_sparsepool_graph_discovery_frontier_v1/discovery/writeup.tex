\section*{exp\_\allowbreak{}sparsepool\_\allowbreak{}graph\_\allowbreak{}discovery\_\allowbreak{}frontier}

\noindent\textit{Specialization:} v1\par

\paragraph{TL;DR.} Repeated exact-budget assignments turn unknown additive interference into \(n\) sparse regressions sharing one constrained design. The paper proves noiseless exact identification through a structured sparse singular value, uniform excitation by random fixed-cardinality pools, and exact support recovery under beta-min separation and rowwise conditionally sub-Gaussian noise. For exact mathematical optimizer outputs, the Dantzig construction also gives simultaneous edge and nonedge decisions, while partially penalized Lasso gives a second exact-support upper bound. An adaptive information converse yields a two-sided minimax pool-count bracket and matches the upper bound up to universal constants when \(\sigma^2/\delta^2\ge s+1\). The all-regime sharp frontier, the low-noise sparse-exploration converse, and a certified polynomial-time finite-precision procedure for producing the optimizer outputs remain open.

\section{Project justification}

\paragraph{Gap.} Unknown-network causal papers either bypass uniform graph recovery or learn neighborhoods while optimizing regret, passive wireless graph-recovery papers use traffic-generated activations and binary feedback, and classical sparse-recovery papers use Gaussian, independently sparsified, or Boolean measurements. None supplies a matching all-regime recovery law and certified finite-precision decoder for signed full-vector responses under a designed exact treatment total.

\paragraph{Niche.} Full-vector network experiments reuse each exact-cardinality assignment across all outcome rows. The constraint creates an affine all-ones null direction, while sparse contrasts remain excitable because \(2s+1<n\); all directed rows can therefore be decoded in parallel.

\paragraph{Fill.} The paper supplies exact injectivity and constant-row-weight excitation results, simultaneous Dantzig edge and nonedge certificates, an exact partially penalized-Lasso alternative that removes the Dantzig sparsity multiplier from the noise term, and adaptive information lower bounds. These components yield an honest nonasymptotic bracket and isolate, rather than claim to solve, the sparse-exploration and finite-precision computational gaps.

\section{Related work}

YuAiroldiBorgsChayes2022 establishes the heterogeneous additive network-effects model and shows how total effects can sometimes be estimated without measuring the graph; the present estimand is the directed support itself. AgarwalAgarwalMasoeroWhitehouse2024 and GleichLaberVolfovsky2026 couple sparse neighborhood learning with policy or experimental choice, but do not give uniform signed support certificates under exact-cardinality assignments. YangDraperNowak2016 proves matching observation laws for passive CSMA/CA graph recovery from traffic activations and binary ACK/NACK feedback; those observations neither permit a chosen exact-\(B\) assignment nor reveal signed additive full-vector responses. Wainwright2009 and WangWainwrightRamchandran2010 provide support-recovery information templates for Gaussian and independently sparsified measurements, while AldridgeJohnsonScarlett2016 and LotfiVidyasagar2020 study different Boolean or binary sensing constraints. ZhangCheng2017 treats simultaneous inference in nonadaptive high-dimensional regression. Against these comparators, the present contribution is an exact-budget recovery bracket with two explicit upper witnesses and an adaptive lower witness; no cited result closes its sparse-exploration or finite-precision gap.

\section{Assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[ass:additive-interference]\label{ass:additive-interference}
For every \(i\in\mathcal U\) and \(z\in\mathcal Z_B\), \(\mu_i(z)=\alpha_i+z^{\mathsf T}\theta_i\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (heterogeneous additive network effects, \cite{YuAiroldiBorgsChayes2022}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:directed-row-sparsity]\label{ass:directed-row-sparsity}
For every \(i\in\mathcal U\), \(\lvert S_i\rvert\le s\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (unit-specific sparse interference neighborhoods, \cite{AgarwalAgarwalMasoeroWhitehouse2024}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:beta-min]\label{ass:beta-min}
For every \(i\ne j\) with \(A_{ij}=1\), \(\lvert\Gamma_{ij}\rvert\ge\delta\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (beta-min support separation, \cite{Wainwright2009}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[ass:noise-subgaussian]\label{ass:noise-subgaussian}
For each fixed \(i\in\mathcal U\), every admissible \(d\), every \(t\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\), and every real \(\lambda\), \(\mathbb E_{P,d}[\exp\{\lambda\varepsilon_{t,i}\}\mid\mathcal F_{t-1},Z_{t\cdot}]\le\exp(\lambda^2\sigma^2/2)\).
\par\smallskip\noindent\emph{Standard} (scalar predictable-design conditionally sub-Gaussian martingale innovation, \cite{AbbasiYadkoriPalSzepesvari2011}).
\end{assumption}

\section{Main results}

\begin{lemma}[lem:exact-budget-covariance]\label{lem:exact-budget-covariance}
If \(Z_{t\cdot}\) is uniform on \(\mathcal Z_B\), then \(\operatorname{Cov}(Z_{t\cdot})=p(1-p)\frac{n}{n-1}(I_n-n^{-1}\mathbf 1_n\mathbf 1_n^{\mathsf T})\). Consequently, for every vector \(v\) with \(\lVert v\rVert_0\le k<n\), \(v^{\mathsf T}\operatorname{Cov}(Z_{t\cdot})v\ge p(1-p)\frac{n-k}{n-1}\lVert v\rVert_2^2\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write \(z=Z_{t\cdot}\).  By uniformity on \(\mathcal Z_B\), permutation symmetry gives
\[
 \mathbb E z_j=p,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(z_j)=p(1-p),
 \qquad j\in\mathcal U.
\]
For distinct \(j,k\), the fixed-cardinality constraint and direct counting give
\[
 \mathbb E(z_jz_k)
 =\frac{B(B-1)}{n(n-1)},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Cov}(z_j,z_k)
 =\frac{B(B-1)}{n(n-1)}-\frac{B^2}{n^2}
 =-\frac{p(1-p)}{n-1}.
\]
Thus the diagonal and off-diagonal entries of the covariance matrix are respectively \(p(1-p)\) and \(-p(1-p)/(n-1)\), whence
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(z)
 =p(1-p)\frac{n}{n-1}
   \left(I_n-\frac1n\mathbf 1_n\mathbf 1_n^{\mathsf T}\right).
\]
Now let \(K=\{j:v_j\ne0\}\), so that \(|K|\le k<n\).  Cauchy--Schwarz on \(K\) yields
\[
 (\mathbf 1_n^{\mathsf T}v)^2
 =\left(\sum_{j\in K}v_j\right)^2
 \le |K|\sum_{j\in K}v_j^2
 \le k\lVert v\rVert_2^2.
\]
Substitution into the covariance formula therefore gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 v^{\mathsf T}\operatorname{Cov}(z)v
 &=p(1-p)\frac{n}{n-1}
   \left(\lVert v\rVert_2^2-\frac{(\mathbf 1_n^{\mathsf T}v)^2}{n}\right)\\
 &\ge p(1-p)\frac{n-k}{n-1}\lVert v\rVert_2^2,
 \end{aligned}
\]
which proves both assertions.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This isolates the sole exact-budget null direction and proves that it cannot contain a sufficiently sparse contrast. \textit{Gap.} Binary compressed-sensing analyses allow other row laws; this lemma gives the exact projector and sparse eigenvalue for complete randomization with fixed treatment count. \textit{Consumer.} It supplies the population curvature used to design repeated fixed-budget graph-measurement experiments.

\begin{theorem}[thm:exact-budget-identification]\label{thm:exact-budget-identification}
Fix a legal matrix \(Z\). For every \(i\in\mathcal U\), the noiseless map \((\alpha_i,u)\mapsto\alpha_i\mathbf 1_m+Zu\) is injective on \(\mathbb R\times\mathcal T_i(s)\) if and only if \(\kappa_s(Z)>0\). Hence all signed spillover rows and \(A\) are exactly identified from the full outcome matrix if and only if this single structured sparse singular-value condition holds.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write \(F_{i,Z}:\mathbb R\times\mathcal T_i(s)\to\mathbb R^m\) for the observable noiseless row map
\[
F_{i,Z}(a,u)=a\mathbf 1_m+Zu.
\]
We prove the asserted equivalence with the universal quantifier read jointly, namely
\[
\bigl[\text{for every }i\in\mathcal U,\ F_{i,Z}\text{ is injective}\bigr]
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \kappa_s(Z)>0. \tag{1}
\]

First suppose \(\kappa_s(Z)>0\), fix \(i\in\mathcal U\), and suppose that
\[
F_{i,Z}(a,u)=F_{i,Z}(a',u')
\qquad
\text{for }(a,u),(a',u')\in\mathbb R\times\mathcal T_i(s).
\]
Set \(b=a-a'\) and \(v=u-u'\).  By the definition of \(\mathcal T_i(s)\),
\[
\operatorname{supp}(v_{-i})
 \subseteq \operatorname{supp}(u_{-i})\cup\operatorname{supp}(u'_{-i}),
\qquad
\lVert v_{-i}\rVert_0\le 2s. \tag{2}
\]
Equality of the two observed vectors gives
\[
b\mathbf 1_m+Zv=0. \tag{3}
\]
Because \(H_m\mathbf 1_m=0\) and \(X=H_mZ\) by the centered-design definition, applying \(H_m\) to (3) yields
\[
Xv=0. \tag{4}
\]
If \(v\ne0\), then (2) makes \(v\) feasible in the infimum defining \(\kappa_s(Z)\), so
\[
0=\frac{\lVert Xv\rVert_2}{\sqrt m\lVert v\rVert_2}
 \ge \kappa_s(Z)>0,
\]
a contradiction.  Thus \(v=0\).  Equation (3) then gives \(b\mathbf 1_m=0\), and \(m\ge1\) implies \(b=0\).  Hence \(u=u'\) and \(a=a'\).  Since \(i\) was arbitrary, every \(F_{i,Z}\) is injective.

For the converse, suppose \(\kappa_s(Z)=0\).  We first justify that the zero infimum supplies an exact sparse null vector.  For \(i\in\mathcal U\) and \(J\subseteq\mathcal U\setminus\{i\}\) with \(\lvert J\rvert\le2s\), let
\[
E_{i,J}=\{v\in\mathbb R^n:\operatorname{supp}(v_{-i})\subseteq J\}.
\]
The admissible vectors in the definition of \(\kappa_s(Z)\) form the finite union of the subspaces \(E_{i,J}\).  On each nonempty compact unit sphere
\(E_{i,J}\cap\{v:\lVert v\rVert_2=1\}\), the continuous function \(v\mapsto\lVert Xv\rVert_2/\sqrt m\) attains its minimum.  There are finitely many pairs \((i,J)\).  Consequently the infimum defining \(\kappa_s(Z)\) is attained after normalizing \(v\), and \(\kappa_s(Z)=0\) yields some \(i_0\in\mathcal U\) and \(v\ne0\) such that
\[
\lVert v_{-i_0}\rVert_0\le2s,
\qquad Xv=0. \tag{5}
\]

To obtain not only coefficient aliasing but also graph aliasing, select a row index \(r\) as follows.  If \(v_{-i_0}\ne0\), take \(r=i_0\).  If \(v_{-i_0}=0\), then \(v\) is supported on \(\{i_0\}\); choose any \(r\ne i_0\), which is possible because \(n\ge8\).  In both cases,
\[
1\le\lVert v_{-r}\rVert_0\le2s, \tag{6}
\]
where the upper bound in the second case uses \(s\ge1\).  Partition \(\operatorname{supp}(v_{-r})\) into disjoint sets \(J_0,J_1\) with \(\lvert J_0\rvert\le s\) and \(\lvert J_1\rvert\le s\), which is possible by (6).  Define \(u,u'\in\mathbb R^n\) coordinatewise by
\[
u_r=v_r,
\quad u_j=v_j\ \text{for }j\in J_0,
\quad u_j=0\ \text{otherwise},
\]
and
\[
u'_r=0,
\quad u'_j=-v_j\ \text{for }j\in J_1,
\quad u'_j=0\ \text{otherwise}.
\]
Then
\[
u-u'=v,
\qquad u,u'\in\mathcal T_r(s), \tag{7}
\]
and (6) ensures that the off-diagonal supports of \(u\) and \(u'\) are different.

By (5) and \(X=H_mZ\), \(H_mZv=0\).  Directly from
\(H_m=I_m-m^{-1}\mathbf 1_m\mathbf 1_m^{\mathsf T}\), its kernel is
\(\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf 1_m\}\): indeed, \(H_mw=0\) implies
\(w=m^{-1}(\mathbf 1_m^{\mathsf T}w)\mathbf 1_m\).  Therefore there is \(c\in\mathbb R\) such that
\[
Zv=c\mathbf 1_m. \tag{8}
\]
Using (7)--(8), we obtain the observational alias
\[
F_{r,Z}(-c,u)-F_{r,Z}(0,u')
=-c\mathbf 1_m+Z(u-u')
=-c\mathbf 1_m+Zv
=0. \tag{9}
\]
The two parameter pairs in (9) are distinct because \(v=u-u'\ne0\).  Hence \(F_{r,Z}\) is not injective, proving the reverse implication in (1).

It remains to connect (1), which concerns an observed-law map, to the causal support target rather than identifying the two by definition.  Under additive interference, the noiseless observed column for outcome row \(i\) is
\[
M_{\cdot i}=\alpha_i\mathbf 1_m+Z\theta_i=F_{i,Z}(\alpha_i,\theta_i). \tag{10}
\]
Directed row sparsity gives \(\theta_i\in\mathcal T_i(s)\), because its off-diagonal support is \(S_i\) and \(\lvert S_i\rvert\le s\).  When \(\kappa_s(Z)>0\), (1) makes the inverse \(F_{i,Z}^{-1}\) single-valued on the range containing \(M_{\cdot i}\).  Thus an explicit noiseless recovery functional is
\[
\widetilde\theta_i(M;Z)
=\operatorname{pr}_2\!\left(F_{i,Z}^{-1}(M_{\cdot i})\right),
\qquad
\mathcal R_Z(M)_{ij}
=\mathbf 1\{j\ne i,\ (\widetilde\theta_i(M;Z))_j\ne0\}. \tag{11}
\]
Equations (10)--(11), rather than a definition of the causal target, prove
\[
\widetilde\theta_i(M;Z)=\theta_i,
\qquad
\mathcal R_Z(M)_{ij}
=\mathbf 1\{i\ne j,\Gamma_{ij}\ne0\}=A_{ij}. \tag{12}
\]
Hence all signed rows and their directed support are exactly identified.

Finally, if \(\kappa_s(Z)=0\), use the two alternatives in (9) as row \(r\) of two additive-interference systems and take identical admissible parameters in every other row.  Both systems satisfy directed row sparsity by (7), and (9) makes their entire noiseless outcome matrices equal.  Nevertheless, their signed row coefficients differ, and their off-diagonal supports differ by (6)--(7), so their matrices \(A\) differ.  No functional of the observed noiseless matrix can therefore recover all signed rows or \(A\) uniformly on the stated model class.  This proves the claimed necessity and completes the identification equivalence.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The result gives a necessary-and-sufficient identification boundary rather than treating a restricted eigenvalue as an unexplained sufficient condition. \textit{Gap.} Wainwright2009 and WangWainwrightRamchandran2010 study support recovery under Gaussian or independently sparsified measurements; neither characterizes the affine exact-budget slice shared by all network rows. \textit{Consumer.} An experimenter can audit a proposed pool matrix before deployment and know whether any sparse signed graph is observationally aliased.

\begin{theorem}[thm:random-pool-excitation]\label{thm:random-pool-excitation}
Assume in addition that \(n\ge9\). There exist universal constants \(c,C>0\) such that, for \(Z\sim\Pi_{m,B}\), if \(m\ge Cq^{-1}\{(2s+1)\log(en/(2s+1))+\log(2/\rho)\}\), then \(\Pr\{\kappa_s(Z)^2\ge cq,\ \phi_s(Z)^2\ge cq\}\ge1-\rho\). The assignment matrix itself is generated in randomized time polynomial in \(n\) and \(m\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply the balanced-pool uniform concentration lemma.  A vector admitted in the definition of \(\kappa_s(Z)\) has at most \(2s+1\) nonzero coordinates, so its first conclusion gives
\[
 \kappa_s(Z)^2\ge c_0q.
\]
Under \(n\ge9\), every vector admitted in the definition of \(\phi_s(Z)\) satisfies the factor-three cone condition in the lemma, and hence
\[
 \phi_s(Z)^2\ge c_0q.
\]
Both inequalities hold on the same event of probability at least \(1-\rho\); renaming the universal constants proves the probability assertion.  By the definition of \(\Pi_{m,B}\), one row is generated by a uniform random permutation of \(\mathcal U\) followed by retaining its first \(B\) entries.  A standard shuffle uses \(O(n)\) elementary randomized operations, so all \(m\) rows are generated in \(O(mn)\) operations.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} This turns population excitation into one shared finite pool family that separates every admissible row model and supports stable convex decoding. \textit{Gap.} AldridgeJohnsonScarlett2016 fixes column weights for Boolean group tests, while LotfiVidyasagar2020 studies binary sensing matrices without the exact row-cardinality and centered-null geometry required here. \textit{Consumer.} The theorem provides a deployable fixed-budget exploration batch for platforms that observe every unit outcome after each assignment.

\begin{theorem}[thm:simultaneous-certificates]\label{thm:simultaneous-certificates}
There are universal choices of \(C_{\mathrm{cert}}\) and \(C>0\) such that, if \(Z\sim\Pi_{m,B}\), \(m\ge Cq^{-1}\{(2s+1)\log(en/(2s+1))+(\sigma^2/\delta^2)(s+1)\log(2n^2/\eta)+\log(2/\rho)\}\), and \(P\in\mathcal M_s\), then the displayed sample-size condition implies \(r_m<\delta/2\). Set \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{comp}}=(\delta-2r_m)/8\) and \(R_m=r_m+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{comp}}\), and for every row take any certified finite-tolerance output in the corrected Dantzig-decoder definition. Its valid primal--dual gap bound and strong convexity give \(\lVert\widehat\theta_i-\theta_i^{\mathrm D}\rVert_2\le\varepsilon_{\mathrm{comp}}\), hence the same coordinatewise bound. With probability at least \(1-\rho-\eta\), simultaneously for every \(i\in\mathcal U\), \(\lVert\theta_i^{\mathrm D}-\theta_i\rVert_\infty\le r_m\) and therefore \(\lVert\widehat\theta_i-\theta_i\rVert_\infty\le R_m\). The numerical margin is \(\delta-2R_m=3(\delta-2r_m)/4>0\), so simultaneously for all \(i\ne j\), \(C_{ij}=\mathsf{edge}\) if \(A_{ij}=1\) and \(C_{ij}=\mathsf{nonedge}\) if \(A_{ij}=0\). Thus \(\widehat A=A\), no certificate is undecided, and every certified finite-tolerance output preserves every inclusion and exclusion certificate. The network-wide probability uses the scalar predictable-design innovation condition for each fixed outcome row at error level \(\eta/n\), followed by a union bound over \(i\in\mathcal U\); no restriction on contemporaneous dependence across outcome rows is imposed. No dimension-only real-arithmetic or bit-complexity bound for finding the certified output is asserted.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(T_i=\{i\}\cup S_i\).  Directed row sparsity gives \(|T_i|\le s+1\), and additive interference gives
\[
 H_mY_i=X\theta_i+H_m\varepsilon_i,
 \qquad \varepsilon_i=(\varepsilon_{1,i},\ldots,\varepsilon_{m,i})^{\mathsf T}.
 \tag{20}
\]
Apply the balanced-pool concentration lemma with failure probability \(\rho\).  On its event \(E_Z\), simultaneously for every \(i\),
\[
 \frac{\lVert Xv\rVert_2^2}{m}\ge c_0q\lVert v\rVert_2^2
 \quad\text{if}\quad
 \lVert v_{T_i^c}\rVert_1\le\lVert v_{T_i}\rVert_1,
 \qquad
 \max_j\lVert Xe_j\rVert_2^2\le2mq.
 \tag{21}
\]
This invokes the factor-one cone conclusion and therefore remains valid at \(n=8,s=1\).

Use the nonadaptive implementation that draws the independent pool rows before observing outcomes.  Conditional on the complete assignment matrix, the weights in each score are deterministic and the rowwise predictable sub-Gaussian bound may be iterated over time.  Since \(X^{\mathsf T}H_m=X^{\mathsf T}\), for fixed \(i,j\),
\[
 \Pr_{P,d}\!\left(
  |e_j^{\mathsf T}X^{\mathsf T}H_m\varepsilon_i|>\lambda_m\mid Z
 \right)
 \le2\exp\!\left(-\frac{\lambda_m^2}
 {2\sigma^2\lVert Xe_j\rVert_2^2}\right)
 \le\frac{\eta}{n^2}.
 \tag{22}
\]
When \(\sigma=0\), the score is zero almost surely and (22) is read by continuity.  A union bound over all \(n^2\) row-coordinate pairs gives an event \(E_\varepsilon\) of conditional probability at least \(1-\eta\) on which
\[
 \max_{i\in\mathcal U}\lVert X^{\mathsf T}H_m\varepsilon_i\rVert_\infty
 \le\lambda_m.
 \tag{23}
\]
Only scalar bounds for fixed outcome rows were used; contemporaneous dependence between different rows is immaterial to the union bound.

On \(E_Z\cap E_\varepsilon\), (20) and (23) imply \(\theta_i\in\mathcal D_i\).  The feasible set is nonempty for every data realization because any least-squares solution satisfies the normal equations, and the minimum defining \(v_i\) is attained by closedness and coercivity of the \(\ell_1\) objective.  Hence \(\theta_i^{\mathrm D}\) is well defined.  Put \(\Delta_i=\theta_i^{\mathrm D}-\theta_i\).  Minimality of its \(\ell_1\) norm and the support of \(\theta_i\) give
\[
 \lVert(\Delta_i)_{T_i^c}\rVert_1
 \le\lVert(\Delta_i)_{T_i}\rVert_1.
 \tag{24}
\]
Feasibility of both vectors gives
\[
 \lVert X^{\mathsf T}X\Delta_i\rVert_\infty\le2\lambda_m.
 \tag{25}
\]
Using (21), (24), Cauchy--Schwarz, and (25), in that order,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 c_0mq\lVert\Delta_i\rVert_2^2
 &\le\lVert X\Delta_i\rVert_2^2
 =\Delta_i^{\mathsf T}X^{\mathsf T}X\Delta_i\\
 &\le2\lambda_m\lVert\Delta_i\rVert_1
 \le4\lambda_m\sqrt{s+1}\lVert\Delta_i\rVert_2.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{26}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \lVert\theta_i^{\mathrm D}-\theta_i\rVert_\infty
 \le C_7\sigma\sqrt{\frac{(s+1)\log(2n^2/\eta)}{mq}}.
 \tag{27}
\]
Choose \(C_{\mathrm{cert}}\ge C_7\).  The noise-to-signal term in the sample-size hypothesis then implies
\[
 r_m^2=C_{\mathrm{cert}}^2\sigma^2
 \frac{(s+1)\log(2n^2/\eta)}{mq}<\frac{\delta^2}{4},
 \tag{28}
\]
after one universal enlargement of \(C\).

For a certified feasible \(\widehat\theta_i\), weak duality bounds its objective excess over the unique minimizer by \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{comp}}^2/2\).  The variational inequality at the constrained minimizer and one-strong convexity of \(u\mapsto\lVert u\rVert_2^2/2\) imply
\[
 \frac12\lVert\widehat\theta_i-\theta_i^{\mathrm D}\rVert_2^2
 \le\frac{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{comp}}^2}{2}.
 \tag{29}
\]
Thus its Euclidean and coordinatewise errors are at most \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{comp}}\).  Combining (27)--(29),
\[
 \lVert\widehat\theta_i-\theta_i\rVert_\infty\le R_m,
 \qquad
 \delta-2R_m=\frac34(\delta-2r_m)>0.
 \tag{30}
\]
If \(A_{ij}=0\), then \(|(\widehat\theta_i)_j|\le R_m<\delta-R_m\), so the certificate is \(\mathsf{nonedge}\).  If \(A_{ij}=1\), beta-min and (30) give
\[
 |(\widehat\theta_i)_j|\ge|\Gamma_{ij}|-R_m
 \ge\delta-R_m>R_m,
\]
so it is \(\mathsf{edge}\).  Therefore no certificate is undecided and \(\widehat A=A\).  Finally, \(\Pr(E_Z\cap E_\varepsilon)\ge1-\rho-\eta\), proving the proposed statement.  The proof uses only the validity of the supplied optimization certificate and deliberately makes no complexity claim for finding it.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem converts rowwise scalar concentration into simultaneous positive and negative graph decisions, and it propagates a checkable primal--dual numerical gap without claiming an unproved solver running time. \textit{Gap.} Existing simultaneous-inference and unknown-network regret results do not supply finite-sample graph-wide inclusion and exclusion certificates under exact-budget pooling with arbitrary contemporaneous cross-row dependence. \textit{Consumer.} The certificates determine which directed spillovers can safely enter downstream exposure analysis once a valid finite-tolerance optimization certificate has been obtained.

\begin{proposition}[prop:singleton-reduction]\label{prop:singleton-reduction}
When \(B=1\) and \(Z\) consists of \(r\) repetitions of every singleton assignment, \(m=nr\) and \(m^{-1}X^{\mathsf T}X=n^{-1}(I_n-n^{-1}\mathbf 1_n\mathbf 1_n^{\mathsf T})\). Thus \(\kappa_s(Z)^2\ge n^{-1}(1-(2s+1)/n)\), recovering the classical one-unit-at-a-time identification benchmark.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let \(e_j\in\{0,1\}^n\) denote the \(j\)-th coordinate vector.  By the hypothesis on \(Z\), each \(e_j\), \(j\in\mathcal U\), occurs as a row of \(Z\) exactly \(r\) times.  Every such row belongs to \(\mathcal Z_1\) by Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}exact\mbox{-}budget\mbox{-}actions}\), and counting the rows gives
\[
m=\sum_{j=1}^n r=nr.
\]
The same repetition property yields
\[
Z^{\mathsf T}Z=\sum_{j=1}^n r e_je_j^{\mathsf T}=rI_n,
\qquad
Z^{\mathsf T}\mathbf 1_m=\sum_{j=1}^n r e_j=r\mathbf 1_n.
\]
By Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}centered\mbox{-}design}\), \(X=H_mZ\), where \(H_m\) is symmetric and idempotent.  Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
X^{\mathsf T}X
&=Z^{\mathsf T}H_m^{\mathsf T}H_mZ
=Z^{\mathsf T}H_mZ\\
&=Z^{\mathsf T}Z-\frac{1}{m}Z^{\mathsf T}\mathbf 1_m\mathbf 1_m^{\mathsf T}Z\\
&=rI_n-\frac{r^2}{nr}\mathbf 1_n\mathbf 1_n^{\mathsf T}
=r\left(I_n-\frac{1}{n}\mathbf 1_n\mathbf 1_n^{\mathsf T}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
Dividing by \(m=nr\) proves
\[
\frac{1}{m}X^{\mathsf T}X
=\frac{1}{n}\left(I_n-\frac{1}{n}\mathbf 1_n\mathbf 1_n^{\mathsf T}\right).
\]

It remains to verify the structured sparse singular-value bound.  Fix \(i\in\mathcal U\) and a nonzero \(v\in\mathbb R^n\) satisfying \(\lVert v_{-i}\rVert_0\le 2s\), as in Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}structured\mbox{-}singular\mbox{-}value}\).  With \(K=\{j:v_j\ne0\}\), this restriction implies \(\lvert K\rvert\le 2s+1\).  Because \(v\ne0\), the set \(K\) is nonempty, and the elementary sum-of-squares identity
\[
\lvert K\rvert\sum_{j\in K}v_j^2-\left(\sum_{j\in K}v_j\right)^2
=\sum_{\substack{j,\ell\in K\\j<\ell}}(v_j-v_\ell)^2
\ge0
\]
shows that \(\left(\sum_{j\in K}v_j\right)^2\le\lvert K\rvert\sum_{j\in K}v_j^2\).  Hence the displayed Gram identity gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\lVert Xv\rVert_2^2}{m\lVert v\rVert_2^2}
&=\frac{1}{n}\left(1-\frac{(\mathbf 1_n^{\mathsf T}v)^2}{n\lVert v\rVert_2^2}\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{n}\left(1-\frac{\left(\sum_{j\in K}v_j\right)^2}{n\sum_{j\in K}v_j^2}\right)\\
&\ge \frac{1}{n}\left(1-\frac{\lvert K\rvert}{n}\right)\\
&\ge \frac{1}{n}\left(1-\frac{2s+1}{n}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
The denominators are nonzero because \(n\ge8\) and \(v\ne0\).  Moreover, the stipulated range \(s\le\lfloor n/8\rfloor\) implies \(2s+1<n\), so the final lower bound is positive.  Since the inequality holds for every admissible \(i\) and \(v\), taking first the infimum over \(v\) and then the minimum over \(i\) in Definition \(\mathrm{def{:}structured\mbox{-}singular\mbox{-}value}\), and using nonnegativity of the norm ratio, yields
\[
\kappa_s(Z)^2\ge \frac{1}{n}\left(1-\frac{2s+1}{n}\right).
\]
Thus repeated singleton assignments have precisely the stated centered coordinate-design geometry and its associated one-unit-at-a-time identification bound.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The reduction checks the fixed-budget geometry at its most restrictive boundary. \textit{Gap.} The proposition is a required sanity reduction, not a novelty claim; it exposes how the general criterion collapses to repeated coordinate measurements. \textit{Consumer.} It gives a transparent baseline against which pooled designs can be compared in small-budget experiments.

\begin{theorem}[thm:adaptive-information-lower-bound]\label{thm:adaptive-information-lower-bound}
There exists a universal constant \(c>0\) such that, on the Gaussian-noise subclass of \(\mathcal M_s\), every admissible adaptive exact-budget design and decoder \(d=((\pi_t)_{t=1}^m,g)\) satisfying \(\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_s}\Pr_{P,d}(g(\mathscr H_m)\ne A)\le\eta\) obeys \(m\ge c\,\sigma^2(\log(n-1)+\log(1/\eta))/(q\delta^2)\). In the noiseless degree-one subclass, every admissible \(d\) that identifies \(A\) uniformly also obeys \(m\ge\max\{\lceil\log_2(n+2)\rceil,\lceil1/q\rceil\}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We prove the noisy and noiseless assertions separately.

First suppose \(\sigma>0\); when \(\sigma=0\), the first displayed lower bound has zero right-hand side and is immediate from \(m\ge0\).  Work in the subclass in which the innovations are independent \(N(0,\sigma^2)\) random variables over rounds and outcome coordinates.  This is a subclass of \(\mathcal M_s\): Gaussian innovations satisfy the conditional moment-generating-function inequality in the noise assumption, and every response surface constructed below satisfies additive interference, has incoming degree at most one, and has every nonzero spillover equal in magnitude to \(\delta\).

Define, for \(j\in\mathcal U\) and \(z\in\mathcal Z_B\),
\[
 a_j(z)=\begin{cases}z_j,&p\le1/2,\\1-z_j,&p>1/2.\end{cases}
\]
By the exact-budget condition and the definitions of \(p\) and \(q\),
\[
 \sum_{j\in\mathcal U}a_j(z)=nq. \tag{1}
\]
Let \(P_0\) have \(\alpha=\tau=0\), \(\Gamma=0\), and the Gaussian innovations just specified.  Thus \(A(P_0)\) is empty and all conditional response means are zero.  For each \(j\), choose a fixed \(i(j)\ne j\).  Define \(P_j\) to agree with \(P_0\) except that
\[
 \mu_{i(j)}(z)=\delta a_j(z). \tag{2}
\]
If \(p\le1/2\), equation (2) is realized by \(\Gamma_{i(j),j}=\delta\) and zero baseline.  If \(p>1/2\), it is realized by \(\alpha_{i(j)}=\delta\) and \(\Gamma_{i(j),j}=-\delta\).  Hence \(P_j\in\mathcal M_s\) and its graph contains exactly the edge \((i(j),j)\).

Write \(Q_j=\Pr_{P_j,d}\).  Under either environment, the joint density of a finite history factors into the same predictable assignment factors \(\pi_t(z_t\mid\mathcal F_{t-1})\) and environment-specific Gaussian outcome factors.  In the likelihood ratio \(dQ_0/dQ_j\), every assignment factor therefore cancels pointwise.  For two Gaussian vectors with common covariance \(\sigma^2I_n\) and mean difference \(u\), direct integration of the log likelihood ratio gives divergence \(\lVert u\rVert_2^2/(2\sigma^2)\).  Iterated expectation over the sequential factorization consequently gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_0,Q_j)
 =\frac{\delta^2}{2\sigma^2}
   \mathbb E_{P_0,d}\sum_{t=1}^m a_j(Z_{t\cdot}). \tag{3}
\]
This calculation remains valid for adaptive kernels because the conditional assignment rule, as a function of the realized history, is part of \(d\) and is the same under the two environments.

Summing (3) over \(j\) and using (1) shows that some \(j_*\) satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_0,Q_{j_*})\le \frac{mq\delta^2}{2\sigma^2}. \tag{4}
\]
Let \(E_0=\{g(\mathscr H_m)=A(P_0)\}\).  Uniform graph error at most \(\eta\) implies
\[
 Q_0(E_0)\ge1-\eta,
 \qquad Q_{j_*}(E_0)\le\eta. \tag{5}
\]
Applying the elementary log-sum inequality separately on \(E_0\) and \(E_0^c\) gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_0,Q_{j_*})
 \ge \operatorname{kl}(Q_0(E_0),Q_{j_*}(E_0))
 \ge (1-2\eta)\log\!\left(\frac{1-\eta}{\eta}\right). \tag{6}
\]
For completeness, the first inequality follows by writing each restricted density as its mass times a conditional density and discarding the two nonnegative conditional divergences.  The second follows because binary divergence is increasing in its first argument and decreasing in its second argument on the region in (5).  Since \(\eta\le1/4\), \(1-2\eta\ge1/2\) and
\(\log((1-\eta)/\eta)\ge\log(3/(4\eta))\ge\frac12\log(1/\eta)\).  Equations (4)--(6) therefore prove
\[
 m\ge c_1\frac{\sigma^2\log(1/\eta)}{q\delta^2} \tag{7}
\]
for a universal \(c_1>0\).

We next obtain the \(\log(n-1)\) term without assuming a nonadaptive design.  Put \(a=\lfloor n/2\rfloor\), and choose disjoint \(I,J\subseteq\mathcal U\) with \(|I|=|J|=a\).  For every bijection \(h:I\to J\), define \(P_h\) by
\[
 \mu_i(z)=\delta a_{h(i)}(z)\quad(i\in I),
 \qquad \mu_i(z)=0\quad(i\notin I). \tag{8}
\]
The same baseline-and-spillover construction used after (2) shows that \(P_h\in\mathcal M_s\), and its graph is the directed matching \(\{(i,h(i)):i\in I\}\).  The graphs are distinct as \(h\) varies.  For every legal action,
\[
 \lVert\mu^{(h)}(z)-\mu^{(0)}(z)\rVert_2^2
 =\delta^2\sum_{j\in J}a_j(z)
 \le \delta^2nq, \tag{9}
\]
because a subset contains at most all \(B=nq\) treated units when \(p\le1/2\), and at most all \(n-B=nq\) untreated units when \(p>1/2\).  The sequential likelihood calculation above, now with expectation under \(P_h\), gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(\Pr_{P_h,d},Q_0)
 \le\frac{mnq\delta^2}{2\sigma^2}. \tag{10}
\]

Let \(H\) be uniform on the \(M=a!\) bijections, conditionally generate the history from \(P_H\), and let \(\overline Q=M^{-1}\sum_h\Pr_{P_h,d}\).  Expanding the logarithms in the definitions of divergence and mutual information yields the exact identity
\[
 \frac1M\sum_h\operatorname{KL}(\Pr_{P_h,d},Q_0)
 =I(H;\mathscr H_m)+\operatorname{KL}(\overline Q,Q_0). \tag{11}
\]
Thus (10) upper-bounds \(I(H;\mathscr H_m)\).  Conversely, decode \(H\) from the output graph whenever that graph is one of the matching graphs, using an arbitrary value otherwise.  Its error probability is at most \(\eta\).  If \(E\) is its error indicator, then conditioning first on \(E\) gives
\[
 H(H\mid\mathscr H_m)
 \le H(E)+\Pr(E=1)\log(M-1)
 \le\log2+\eta\log M. \tag{12}
\]
The first inequality follows because \(H\) is determined on \(E=0\) and has at most \(M-1\) possible values on \(E=1\).  Hence
\[
 I(H;\mathscr H_m)\ge(1-\eta)\log M-\log2\ge\frac12\log M, \tag{13}
\]
where \(a\ge4\), so \(M=a!\ge24\), and \(\eta\le1/4\).  At least \(\lfloor a/2\rfloor\) factors in \(a!\) are at least \(a/2\), whence
\[
 \log(a!)\ge\lfloor a/2\rfloor\log(a/2)\ge c_2n\log(n-1) \tag{14}
\]
for a universal \(c_2>0\).  To verify the final uniform constant, for \(n\ge16\) use \(a\ge n/3\), \(\lfloor a/2\rfloor\ge a/3\), and compare \(\log(n/6)\) with \(\log(n-1)\); the finitely many integers \(8\le n<16\) have \(a!\ge24\), so decreasing \(c_2\) covers them.  Combining (10), (11), (13), and (14) proves
\[
 m\ge c_3\frac{\sigma^2\log(n-1)}{q\delta^2}. \tag{15}
\]
The maximum of the right-hand sides of (7) and (15) is at least half their sum.  After decreasing a universal constant, this proves the Gaussian-noise assertion.

It remains to prove the noiseless combinatorial assertion.  Here ``identifies uniformly'' means probability-one correctness for every environment in the degree-one noiseless subclass.  Realize every predictable kernel on the finite action set \(\mathcal Z_B\) by inverse transform from a common sequence of independent uniform seeds, and include those seeds in the recorded internal randomization.  Consider the finite family consisting of the empty environment and all one-edge alternatives used in the next paragraph.  Each environment has a probability-one correctness event in the common seed space; their finite intersection has probability one.  Fix a seed in this intersection.  Along the empty environment's all-zero outcome history, the adaptive rule then generates a deterministic legal matrix \(Z\).

Every column of \(Z\) is nonconstant.  Indeed, if column \(j\) is identically zero, choose \(i\ne j\) and set \(\Gamma_{ij}=\delta\); this one-edge alternative has zero response along the empty path.  If column \(j\) is identically one, set \(\alpha_i=-\delta\) and \(\Gamma_{ij}=\delta\); this alternative also has zero response along that path.  Distinct columns must also be distinct: if columns \(j\) and \(k\) agree, take recipient \(k\), set \(\Gamma_{kj}=\delta\) and \(\tau_k=-\delta\), and obtain
\[
 \mu_k(Z_{t\cdot})=\delta Z_{tj}-\delta Z_{tk}=0
 \quad(t=1,\ldots,m). \tag{16}
\]
Every alternative in this finite family has one spillover edge of magnitude \(\delta\).  Because its outcomes agree recursively with the empty environment, the common seed produces the same assignments, complete history, and terminal output.  Correctness for both distinct graphs is impossible, proving the claimed nonconstancy and pairwise distinctness.

The \(n\) column words and the all-zero and all-one words are therefore pairwise distinct.  Thus
\[
 2^m\ge n+2,
 \qquad m\ge\lceil\log_2(n+2)\rceil. \tag{17}
\]
Nonconstancy also makes every column contain at least one one and at least one zero.  Counting entries first by columns and then by rows gives
\[
 mB\ge n,
 \qquad m(n-B)\ge n. \tag{18}
\]
Since \(q=\min\{B/n,(n-B)/n\}\) and \(m\) is an integer, (18) implies \(m\ge\lceil1/q\rceil\).  Combining this with (17) proves the noiseless degree-one assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:balanced-pool-uniform-concentration]\label{lem:balanced-pool-uniform-concentration}
There are universal constants \(c_0,C_0>0\) with the following property.  Let \(Z\sim\Pi_{m,B}\).  If
\[
 m\ge C_0q^{-1}\left\{(2s+1)\log\!\left(\frac{en}{2s+1}\right)+\log\!\left(\frac2\rho\right)\right\},
\]
then, with probability at least \(1-\rho\), all three conclusions below hold:
\[
 \frac{\lVert Xv\rVert_2^2}{m}\ge c_0q\lVert v\rVert_2^2
 \quad\text{whenever}\quad
 \lVert v\rVert_0\le2s+1,
\]
\[
 \frac{\lVert Xv\rVert_2^2}{m}\ge c_0q\lVert v\rVert_2^2
 \quad\text{whenever there is }S\subseteq\mathcal U, |S|\le s+1,
 \text{ with }\lVert v_{S^c}\rVert_1\le\lVert v_S\rVert_1,
\]
and
\[
 \max_{j\in\mathcal U}\lVert Xe_j\rVert_2^2\le2mq.
\]
If \(n\ge9\), the second conclusion also holds, after decreasing \(c_0\) universally, under the larger cone condition \(\lVert v_{S^c}\rVert_1\le3\lVert v_S\rVert_1\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(h=\min\{B,n-B\}=nq\).  For one row, put \(\xi=Z_{t\cdot}-p\mathbf 1_n\).  If \(B\le n/2\), then
\[
 \xi^{\mathsf T}u=\sum_{j\in R}(u_j-\bar u),
 \qquad \bar u=n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^n u_j,
 \tag{1}
\]
where \(R\) is a uniform \(h\)-subset.  If \(B>n/2\), the same identity holds with a minus sign and \(R\) the untreated \(h\)-subset.  Thus it is enough to analyze the right side of (1).

We first repair the moment calculation by an exchangeable-pair recursion.  Let \(a\in\mathbb R^n\) satisfy \(\sum_j a_j=0\), let \(W=\sum_{j\in R}a_j\), choose \(I\) uniformly from \(R\) and \(J\) uniformly from \(R^c\), and put
\[
 R'=(R\setminus\{I\})\cup\{J\},
 \qquad W'=W-a_I+a_J,
 \qquad \lambda_0=\frac{n}{h(n-h)}.
 \tag{2}
\]
The pair \((W,W')\) is exchangeable, and direct conditional averaging gives
\[
 \mathbb E(W'-W\mid R)
 =-\left(\frac1h+\frac1{n-h}\right)W
 =-\lambda_0W.
 \tag{3}
\]
For every integer \(\ell\ge1\), exchangeability and (3) imply the exact identity
\[
 \mathbb EW^{2\ell}
 =\frac1{2\lambda_0}\,
 \mathbb E\!\left[(W'-W)
       (W'^{\,2\ell-1}-W^{2\ell-1})\right].
 \tag{4}
\]
Indeed, exchangeability changes the sign of
\((W'-W)\{W'^{\,2\ell-1}+W^{2\ell-1}\}\), so its expectation is zero, while conditioning
\((W'-W)W^{2\ell-1}\) on \(R\) gives
\(-\lambda_0\mathbb EW^{2\ell}\).

The conditional squared increment has the deterministic bound
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E\!\left[(W'-W)^2\mid R\right]
 &\le
 2h^{-1}\sum_{i\in R}a_i^2+
 2(n-h)^{-1}\sum_{j\notin R}a_j^2\\
 &\le2\lambda_0\lVert a\rVert_2^2.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{5}
\]
Using
\[
 |(x-y)(x^{2\ell-1}-y^{2\ell-1})|
 \le(2\ell-1)(x-y)^2
       (|x|^{2\ell-2}+|y|^{2\ell-2})
\]
in (4), then exchangeability and (5), yields the recursion
\[
 \mathbb E|W|^{2\ell}
 \le2(2\ell-1)\lVert a\rVert_2^2
       \mathbb E|W|^{2\ell-2}.
 \tag{6}
\]
The exact covariance of simple random sampling gives
\[
 \mathbb EW^2
 =\frac{h(n-h)}{n(n-1)}\lVert a\rVert_2^2
 \le2q\lVert a\rVert_2^2.
\]
Iteration of (6), and Cauchy--Schwarz between the two adjacent even moments for odd orders, therefore prove, for every integer \(r\ge2\),
\[
 \mathbb E|W|^r
 \le q(C_1r)^{r/2}\lVert a\rVert_2^r.
 \tag{7}
\]
Thus the relevant \(2\ell\)-th moment is \(q(C\ell)^\ell\), not the invalid
\((2\ell)!\) bound left after exponential-series division.

Since \(a=u-\bar u\mathbf 1_n\) satisfies \(\lVert a\rVert_2\le\lVert u\rVert_2\), (7) implies the Bernstein moment condition.  More explicitly, with \(V=W^2-\mathbb EW^2\), there are numerical \(v_0,c_0'>0\) such that, for every \(\ell\ge2\),
\[
 \mathbb E|V|^\ell
 \le \frac{\ell!}{2}(v_0q\lVert u\rVert_2^4)
       (c_0'\lVert u\rVert_2^2)^{\ell-2}.
 \tag{8}
\]
The same bound (7), applied to both signs of the exponential parameter and using \(\mathbb EW=0\), gives the analogous Bernstein moment-generating-function bound for \(W\), with variance proxy \(Cq\lVert u\rVert_2^2\) and scale \(C\lVert u\rVert_2\).  Independence of the rows under \(\Pi_{m,B}\), the exponential Markov inequality, and (8) therefore yield, for every \(x>0\),
\[
 \Pr\!\left\{
  \left|\frac1m\sum_{t=1}^m(\xi_t^{\mathsf T}u)^2
        -\mathbb E(\xi^{\mathsf T}u)^2\right|
  >C_2\lVert u\rVert_2^2
       \left(\sqrt{\frac{qx}{m}}+\frac{x}{m}\right)
 \right\}\le2e^{-x},
 \tag{9}
\]
and
\[
 \Pr\!\left\{
  |\bar\xi^{\mathsf T}u|>
  C_2\lVert u\rVert_2
       \left(\sqrt{\frac{qx}{m}}+\frac{x}{m}\right)
 \right\}\le2e^{-x},
 \qquad \bar\xi=m^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^m\xi_t.
 \tag{10}
\]
Thus (9), unlike the discarded argument, follows from a genuine Bernstein moment bound for the quadratic variable.

Let
\[
 M=m^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^m\xi_t\xi_t^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad \Sigma=\mathbb E(\xi\xi^{\mathsf T}).
\]
For each support of size at most \(r\), choose a \(1/4\)-net of its Euclidean unit sphere with at most \(9^r\) points.  Padding smaller supports to size \(r\) shows that the number of choices is at most \((en/r)^r\).  Applying (9)--(10) to all net points and using
\[
 \lVert D\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le2\max_{u\ \text{ in the net}}|u^{\mathsf T}Du|,
 \qquad
 \lVert b\rVert_2
 \le2\max_{u\ \text{ in the net}}|b^{\mathsf T}u|
 \tag{11}
\]
on each support gives, with probability at least \(1-4e^{-x}\),
\[
 d_r:=\sup_{\substack{\lVert u\rVert_2=1\\\lVert u\rVert_0\le r}}
 |u^{\mathsf T}(M-\Sigma)u|
 \le C_3\left(\sqrt{\frac{qL_r}{m}}+\frac{L_r}{m}\right),
 \tag{12}
\]
\[
 b_r:=\sup_{\substack{\lVert u\rVert_2=1\\\lVert u\rVert_0\le r}}
 |\bar\xi^{\mathsf T}u|
 \le C_3\left(\sqrt{\frac{qL_r}{m}}+\frac{L_r}{m}\right),
 \qquad L_r=r\log(en/r)+x.
 \tag{13}
\]

We shall also use the following deterministic consequence of (12)--(13).  If \(D\) is symmetric, decreasing rearrangement of the coordinates of \(v\) into blocks of size \(\lfloor r/2\rfloor\), followed by polarization on every pair of blocks, gives
\[
 |v^{\mathsf T}Dv|
 \le C_4d_r\left(\lVert v\rVert_2+\frac{\lVert v\rVert_1}{\sqrt r}\right)^2.
 \tag{14}
\]
The same blocking without polarization gives
\[
 |b^{\mathsf T}v|
 \le C_4b_r\left(\lVert v\rVert_2+\frac{\lVert v\rVert_1}{\sqrt r}\right).
 \tag{15}
\]
Indeed, monotone ordering bounds the sum of the Euclidean norms of all blocks after the first by \(2\lVert v\rVert_1/\sqrt r\); summing the resulting within-block and cross-block products proves (14)--(15).

Take \(r=\min\{n,\lceil C_5(s+1)\rceil\}\), with \(C_5\) a sufficiently large numerical constant, and set \(x=\log(4/\rho)\).  Since \(s\le n/8\),
\[
 L_r\le C_6\left\{(2s+1)\log\!\left(\frac{en}{2s+1}\right)
                       +\log\!\left(\frac2\rho\right)\right\}.
 \tag{16}
\]
The assumed lower bound on \(m\) makes the right sides of (12)--(13), respectively, at most an arbitrarily small universal multiple of \(q\) and of \(\sqrt q\).

It remains to compare with the population.  By the exact-budget covariance lemma,
\[
 \Sigma=p(1-p)\frac{n}{n-1}
 \left(I_n-\frac1n\mathbf 1_n\mathbf 1_n^{\mathsf T}\right).
 \tag{17}
\]
Because \(p(1-p)=q(1-q)\ge q/2\), the sparse consequence of that lemma and \(2s+1<n\) give
\[
 v^{\mathsf T}\Sigma v\ge c_1q\lVert v\rVert_2^2
 \quad\text{if }\lVert v\rVert_0\le2s+1.
 \tag{18}
\]
For a cone vector, write \(k=|S|\), \(N=n-k\), \(u=\lVert v_S\rVert_1\), and \(w=\lVert v_{S^c}\rVert_1\).  If \(w\le a_0u\) and \(N>a_0k\), Cauchy--Schwarz separately on the two blocks and maximization over \(0\le w/u\le a_0\) give
\[
 \frac{(\mathbf 1_n^{\mathsf T}v)^2}{n\lVert v\rVert_2^2}
 \le \frac{(1+a_0)^2kN}{n(N+a_0^2k)}.
 \tag{19}
\]
For \(a_0=1\), \(k\le s+1\) and \(s\le n/8\) make (19) at most \(3/4\), including \(n=8,s=1\).  Hence (17) gives
\[
 v^{\mathsf T}\Sigma v\ge(q/8)\lVert v\rVert_2^2.
 \tag{20}
\]
For \(a_0=3\), the additional condition \(n\ge9\) gives \(k/n\le2/9\) and \(N>3k\); the right side of (19) is then at most \(224/225\).  Thus
\[
 v^{\mathsf T}\Sigma v\ge(q/450)\lVert v\rVert_2^2.
 \tag{21}
\]
The respective cone conditions also give \(\lVert v\rVert_1\le(1+a_0)\sqrt{s+1}\lVert v\rVert_2\).  Equations (12)--(16), with their universal constants chosen small relative to (18), (20), and (21), therefore transfer all three population lower bounds to the sample matrix because
\[
 \frac1mX^{\mathsf T}X=M-\bar\xi\bar\xi^{\mathsf T}.
 \tag{22}
\]
Finally, \(e_j^{\mathsf T}\Sigma e_j=p(1-p)\le q\).  Equation (12) for one-sparse vectors and (22) imply
\[
 m^{-1}\lVert Xe_j\rVert_2^2\le2q
\]
simultaneously for all \(j\), after a final universal enlargement of \(C_0\).  This proves every asserted conclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[prop:fixed-design-rank-obstruction]\label{prop:fixed-design-rank-obstruction}
For every exact-budget assignment matrix \(Z\), \(\kappa_s(Z)>0\) implies \(m\ge2s+2\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Since \(H_m\) is an orthogonal projector of rank \(m-1\), the centered design satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(X)=\operatorname{rank}(H_mZ)\le m-1.
\]
Suppose toward a contradiction that \(m\le2s+1\).  Fix \(i\in\mathcal U\) and choose a coordinate set \(T\subseteq\mathcal U\) of size \(m\) containing \(i\); this is possible because \(m\le2s+1<n\).  The \(m\)-column matrix \(X_T\) has rank at most \(m-1\), so there is a nonzero vector \(v\) supported on \(T\) with \(Xv=0\).  Moreover \(\lVert v_{-i}\rVert_0\le m-1\le2s\).  This vector is feasible in the infimum defining \(\kappa_s(Z)\), and it makes that infimum zero, contradicting \(\kappa_s(Z)>0\).  Hence \(m\ge2s+2\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[lem:partially-penalized-lasso-upper]\label{lem:partially-penalized-lasso-upper}
There is a universal constant \(C>0\) such that, if
\[
 m\ge Cq^{-1}\left\{(s+1)\log\!\left(\frac{8n^3}{\eta}\right)
 +\frac{\sigma^2}{\delta^2}\log\!\left(\frac{8n^2}{\eta}\right)\right\},
\]
then independent uniform exact-budget pools together with the rowwise partially penalized Lasso, leaving coordinate \(i\) unpenalized and using a sufficiently small universal penalty \(c_{\mathrm L}q\delta\), define an admissible exact decoder satisfying
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M_s}\Pr_{P,d}(\widehat A\ne A)\le\eta.
\]
The decoder uses the minimum-Euclidean-norm minimizer off the unique-solution event and declares \(A_{ij}=1\) exactly when the penalized coordinate \(j\ne i\) of the selected minimizer is nonzero.  This is a statistical exact-minimizer statement and makes no finite-precision operation or bit-complexity claim.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(P\in\mathcal M_s\).  The nonadaptive design used here is the admissible sequential design
\[
 \pi_t(\cdot\mid\mathcal F_{t-1})=\operatorname{Unif}(\mathcal Z_B),
 \qquad t=1,\ldots,m, \tag{19}
\]
implemented with independent fresh pool randomizations.  Thus its stacked assignment matrix has law \(\Pi_{m,B}\).  For row \(i\), put \(T=T_i=\{i\}\cup S_i\), \(k=|T|\le s+1\), and define \(w\in\mathbb R^T\) by \(w_i=0\) and \(w_j=\operatorname{sign}(\Gamma_{ij})\) for \(j\in S_i\).  The partially penalized Lasso minimizes
\[
 L_i(b)=\frac1{2m}\lVert H_mY_i-Xb\rVert_2^2
       +\lambda_{\mathrm L}\lVert b_{-i}\rVert_1,
 \qquad \lambda_{\mathrm L}=c_{\mathrm L}q\delta, \tag{20}
\]
where the positive universal constant \(c_{\mathrm L}\) will be chosen below.  A minimizer exists.  Indeed, along a minimizing sequence the penalty bounds \(\lVert b_{-i}\rVert_1\); if \(Xe_i\ne0\), the squared-loss bound then also bounds \(b_i\), while if \(Xe_i=0\), coordinate \(b_i\) may be set to zero without changing the objective.  A convergent subsequence and continuity give a minimizer.  The minimizer set is nonempty, closed, and convex, so it has a unique minimum-Euclidean-norm point.  One direct measurability argument is to add \(r^{-1}\lVert b\rVert_2^2\) to (20): the resulting strongly convex coercive objective has a unique minimizer that is continuous in the finite-dimensional data, and as \(r\to\infty\) these minimizers converge to the minimum-norm minimizer of (20).  Indeed, comparison with any minimum-norm minimizer bounds their norms, every cluster point minimizes (20), and the norm comparison selects the unique minimum-norm point.  The selected minimizer is therefore a pointwise limit of Borel maps, so the resulting terminal graph decoder is \(\mathcal F_m\)-measurable.

We first derive the exact-budget population algebra.  By the exact-budget covariance lemma,
\[
 \Sigma:=\operatorname{Cov}(Z_{t\cdot})
 =a\left(I_n-\frac1n\mathbf1_n\mathbf1_n^{\mathsf T}\right),
 \qquad a=p(1-p)\frac n{n-1}. \tag{21}
\]
For any \(T\) of size \(k<n\), direct multiplication verifies
\[
 \Sigma_{TT}^{-1}=a^{-1}\left(I_k+\frac{\mathbf1_k\mathbf1_k^{\mathsf T}}{n-k}\right). \tag{22}
\]
Since \(\Sigma_{jT}=-(a/n)\mathbf1_k^{\mathsf T}\) for \(j\notin T\),
\[
 \left|\Sigma_{jT}\Sigma_{TT}^{-1}w\right|
 =\frac{|\sum_{\ell\in S_i}w_\ell|}{n-k}
 \le\frac{s}{n-s-1}\le\frac16. \tag{23}
\]
The last inequality uses \(s\le n/8\), including \(n=8,s=1\).

Let \(G_T=m^{-1}X_T^{\mathsf T}X_T\).  Apply the balanced-pool uniform-concentration lemma with failure probability \(\eta/8\).  The sample-size hypothesis of the present lemma dominates that lemma's requirement after increasing the universal constant.  Hence, on an event \(E_0\) of probability at least \(1-\eta/8\), simultaneously for all relevant \(T\),
\[
 \lambda_{\min}(G_T)\ge c_0q,
 \qquad \max_{j\in\mathcal U}\lVert Xe_j\rVert_2^2\le2mq. \tag{24}
\]

We next prove rather than assume the support-specific empirical strict-dual bounds.  Fix \(T_0\subsetneq\mathcal U\), \(|T_0|=k_0\le s+1\), \(j\notin T_0\), and \(c\in[-1,1]^{T_0}\), and set
\[
 h=X_{T_0}(X_{T_0}^{\mathsf T}X_{T_0})^{-1}c. \tag{25}
\]
On the corresponding part of (24), this is well defined.  Since \(h\perp\mathbf1_m\), \(h^{\mathsf T}X_j=h^{\mathsf T}Z_j\).  Conditional on the raw columns \(Z_{\cdot T_0}\), independence of the pool rows gives independent Bernoulli coordinates with
\[
 r_t:=\mathbb E(Z_{tj}\mid Z_{tT_0})
 =\frac{B-\sum_{\ell\in T_0}Z_{t\ell}}{n-k_0}.
\]
It follows from \(h^{\mathsf T}Z_{T_0}=c^{\mathsf T}\) that
\[
 \mathbb E(h^{\mathsf T}X_j\mid Z_{\cdot T_0})
 =-\frac{\mathbf1_{k_0}^{\mathsf T}c}{n-k_0}. \tag{26}
\]
The conditional Bernoulli variances are at most \(2q\).  If \(p\le1/2\), then \(r_t\le B/(n-k_0)\le2q\); if \(p>1/2\), apply the same bound to \(1-r_t\) using \(n-B\).  Here \(k_0\le s+1\le n/4\).  Equation (24) and \(\lVert c\rVert_2\le\sqrt{k_0}\) give
\[
 \lVert h\rVert_2^2\le\frac{k_0}{c_0mq},
 \qquad
 \lVert h\rVert_\infty\le\frac{k_0}{c_0mq}. \tag{27}
\]
For the second bound, use \(\lVert X_{tT_0}\rVert_2\le\sqrt{k_0}\) and
\(\lVert(X_{T_0}^{\mathsf T}X_{T_0})^{-1}c\rVert_2\le\sqrt{k_0}/(c_0mq)\).

For clarity, the required conditional Bernstein bound follows elementarily here.  If \(V\) is centered and \(|V|\le M\), expansion of the exponential and \(k!\ge2\,3^{k-2}\) for \(k\ge2\) show
\[
 \mathbb E e^{\lambda V}
 \le\exp\!\left\{\frac{\lambda^2\mathbb E(V^2)}{2(1-|\lambda|M/3)}\right\}
 \quad\text{for }|\lambda|M<3.
\]
Apply this inequality conditionally to the independent variables
\(h_t(Z_{tj}-r_t)\), multiply their moment-generating functions, and optimize in \(\lambda\).  Using (27) and the variance bound gives, for every fixed \(0<u\le1\),
\[
 \Pr\!\left(
  \left|h^{\mathsf T}X_j+\frac{\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}c}{n-k_0}\right|>u
  \middle|Z_{\cdot T_0}\right)
 \le2\exp\!\left(-\frac{c_1mqu^2}{k_0}\right) \tag{28}
\]
on the event in (24), for a universal \(c_1>0\).

Use (28) with \(T_0=T_i\), \(c=w\), \(u=1/6\), and all \(i\) and \(j\notin T_i\).  Equations (23), (26), and a union bound give
\[
 \max_i\max_{j\notin T_i}
 \left|m^{-1}X_j^{\mathsf T}X_{T_i}G_{T_i}^{-1}w\right|
 \le\frac13 \tag{29}
\]
outside an assignment event of probability at most \(\eta/8\).  To bound the active inverse, fix \(\ell\in T_i\), apply (28) with \(T_0=T_i\setminus\{\ell\}\), \(c=w_{T_0}\), and \(u=1/12\), and use the scalar Schur-complement formula.  Its numerator is at most
\[
 |w_\ell|+\frac{|\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}w_{T_0}|}{n-k+1}+\frac1{12}<2,
\]
whereas its denominator is the Schur complement of \(G_{T_0}\) in \(G_T\), which is at least \(\lambda_{\min}(G_T)\ge c_0q\).  A union bound over at most \(n(s+1)\) pairs yields
\[
 \max_i\lVert G_{T_i}^{-1}w\rVert_\infty\le\frac{C_2}{q} \tag{30}
\]
outside another event of probability at most \(\eta/8\).  The requirement
\(mq\ge C(s+1)\log(8n^3/\eta)\) makes both union bounds valid.

We now justify all noise concentration conditional on the complete design.  Under (19), future pool randomizations are independent of the present outcomes and of \(\mathcal F_t\).  Consequently, conditioning additionally on \(Z_{t+1},\ldots,Z_m\) does not change the conditional moment-generating-function bound for \(\varepsilon_{t,i}\).  Backward iteration of the rowwise sub-Gaussian assumption therefore gives, for every vector \(c(Z)\) that is deterministic after conditioning on \(Z\),
\[
 \mathbb E_{P,d}\!\left[\exp\!\left\{\lambda\sum_{t=1}^m c_t(Z)\varepsilon_{t,i}\right\}\middle|Z\right]
 \le\exp\!\left\{\frac{\lambda^2\sigma^2}{2}\sum_{t=1}^mc_t(Z)^2\right\}. \tag{31}
\]
This is the precise point at which nonadaptivity of the upper-bound design is used; no independence across outcome rows is used.

On the assignment event (24), (29), and (30), define the restricted candidate
\[
 \widetilde b_T=\theta_{i,T}
 +G_T^{-1}\left(m^{-1}X_T^{\mathsf T}H_m\varepsilon_i
                   -\lambda_{\mathrm L}w\right),
 \qquad \widetilde b_{T^c}=0. \tag{32}
\]
Because \(X=H_mX\), equation (31) shows that the noise part of coordinate \(\ell\in T\) in (32) is conditionally sub-Gaussian with variance proxy
\[
 \frac{\sigma^2}{m}e_\ell^{\mathsf T}G_T^{-1}e_\ell
 \le\frac{\sigma^2}{c_0mq}. \tag{33}
\]
A union bound over fewer than \(n(s+1)\) coordinates and the term
\(mq\delta^2/\sigma^2\ge C\log(8n^2/\eta)\) make their absolute values at most \(\delta/4\), except on an event of probability at most \(\eta/4\); when \(\sigma=0\), this conclusion is deterministic.  Choose \(c_{\mathrm L}\le(8C_2)^{-1}\).  Equations (30)--(33) then imply, for every \(\ell\in S_i\),
\[
 |\widetilde b_\ell-\theta_{i,\ell}|
 \le\frac\delta4+\lambda_{\mathrm L}\frac{C_2}{q}
 \le\frac{3\delta}{8}<\delta. \tag{34}
\]
By beta-min, every such coordinate has the same nonzero sign as \(\Gamma_{i\ell}\).

Let \(P_T=X_T(X_T^{\mathsf T}X_T)^{-1}X_T^{\mathsf T}\).  For \(j\notin T\), the inactive KKT score at (32) is
\[
 m^{-1}X_j^{\mathsf T}(I-P_T)H_m\varepsilon_i
 +\lambda_{\mathrm L}m^{-1}X_j^{\mathsf T}X_TG_T^{-1}w. \tag{35}
\]
By (24) and (31), the first term is conditionally sub-Gaussian with variance proxy at most \(2\sigma^2q/m\).  The second term has absolute value at most \(\lambda_{\mathrm L}/3\) by (29).  The noise-to-signal sample-size term and a union bound over fewer than \(n^2\) scores make the first term at most \(\lambda_{\mathrm L}/3\) simultaneously, outside an event of probability at most \(\eta/4\).  Hence all inactive dual coordinates have absolute value strictly below one.

On \(T\), equation (32) is exactly the KKT equation for (20) with subgradient \(w\); its coordinate \(i\) is zero because that coordinate is unpenalized.  Equation (34) verifies the active signs, while (35) verifies strict dual feasibility.  Thus convexity proves that \(\widetilde b\) is a minimizer.  It is the unique minimizer: any two minimizers have the same fitted value by strict convexity of the squared loss in \(Xb\); strict dual feasibility forces every other minimizer to vanish on \(T^c\); and positive definiteness of \(G_T\) then forces equality on \(T\).

The three assignment failure probabilities and two noise failure probabilities sum to at most \(7\eta/8<\eta\).  Therefore all rows are recovered simultaneously, so the decoder obtained from the minimum-norm minimizers satisfies \(\widehat A=A\).  The argument is uniform over \(P\in\mathcal M_s\), and the union bounds never use contemporaneous independence across outcome rows.  This proves the displayed error guarantee.  The proof selects exact minimizers only and establishes no finite-precision operation or bit-complexity bound, exactly as stated.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[thm:sharp-pool-complexity-bracket]\label{thm:sharp-pool-complexity-bracket}
Define
\[
 U_{\mathrm D}=Cq^{-1}\left\{(2s+1)\log\!\left(\frac{en}{2s+1}\right)
 +\frac{\sigma^2}{\delta^2}(s+1)\log\!\left(\frac{4n^2}{\eta}\right)
 +\log\!\left(\frac4\eta\right)\right\}
\]
and
\[
 U_{\mathrm L}=Cq^{-1}\left\{(s+1)\log\!\left(\frac{8n^3}{\eta}\right)
 +\frac{\sigma^2}{\delta^2}\log\!\left(\frac{8n^2}{\eta}\right)\right\},
\]
where \(C>0\) is universal.  Then the presently proved nonasymptotic answer to the sharp-pool-complexity question is the two-sided bracket
\[
 c\,\frac{\sigma^2\{\log(n-1)+\log(1/\eta)\}}{q\delta^2}
 \le m^*(n,B,s,\delta,\sigma,\eta)
 \le \left\lceil\min\{U_{\mathrm D},U_{\mathrm L}\}\right\rceil.
\]
The \(U_{\mathrm D}\) witness is the simultaneous Dantzig certificate with a mathematically valid finite-tolerance optimization certificate; the \(U_{\mathrm L}\) witness is exact rowwise partially penalized Lasso.  Thus the factor \(s+1\) in the Dantzig noise-to-signal term is not intrinsic to exact convex support recovery, but the proved Lasso alternative pays \((s+1)\log(8n^3/\eta)\) for simultaneous empirical strict dual feasibility.  No dimension-only finite-precision complexity guarantee is proved for either optimization output.  Separately, probability-one identification on the noiseless degree-one subclass requires
\[
 m\ge\max\{\lceil\log_2(n+2)\rceil,\lceil1/q\rceil\},
\]
and every fixed design with \(\kappa_s(Z)>0\) requires \(m\ge2s+2\).  The gap between the displayed lower bound and the better of the two upper bounds remains unresolved outside regimes in which their orders coincide.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For the Dantzig upper bound, apply the simultaneous-certificate theorem with its rowwise statistical error parameter equal to \(\eta/2\) and its pool-construction failure parameter equal to \(\eta/2\).  The theorem's sample-size requirement then becomes
\[
 m\ge Cq^{-1}\left\{(2s+1)\log\!\left(\frac{en}{2s+1}\right)
 +\frac{\sigma^2}{\delta^2}(s+1)\log\!\left(\frac{4n^2}{\eta}\right)
 +\log\!\left(\frac4\eta\right)\right\}=U_{\mathrm D} \tag{36}
\]
after one universal enlargement of \(C\), and its success probability is at least \(1-\eta\).

We verify that this gives an admissible mathematical witness without adding a computational claim.  For every data set, the Dantzig set \(\mathcal D_i\) is nonempty: \(X^{\mathsf T}H_mY_i\) lies in the range of \(X^{\mathsf T}\), which equals the range of \(X^{\mathsf T}X\), so some \(u\) makes \(X^{\mathsf T}(H_mY_i-Xu)=0\).  The set
\[
 Q_i=\{u\in\mathcal D_i:\lVert u\rVert_1\le v_i\}
\]
is therefore a nonempty compact polyhedron, and the strictly convex objective \(\lVert u\rVert_2^2/2\) has the unique minimizer \(\theta_i^{\mathrm D}\).  Write \(Q_i=\{u:a_r^{\mathsf T}u\le b_r,\ r=1,\ldots,N\}\), using the finitely many half-space representations of the infinity- and one-norm constraints.  At the minimizer, \(-\theta_i^{\mathrm D}\) belongs to the cone generated by the active outward normals.  Indeed, otherwise a separating vector would have nonpositive inner product with every active normal and negative inner product with \(\theta_i^{\mathrm D}\); a sufficiently short step in that direction would remain feasible and strictly decrease the objective, a contradiction.  Hence there are nonnegative active multipliers satisfying stationarity and complementary slackness.  Their Lagrangian infimum is a dual lower bound equal to \(\lVert\theta_i^{\mathrm D}\rVert_2^2/2\), so choosing \(\widehat\theta_i=\theta_i^{\mathrm D}\) supplies a valid certificate with zero primal--dual gap and thus meets every positive tolerance in the corrected Dantzig-decoder definition.

This selector is measurable.  One may partition the finite-dimensional data space by the finitely many active constraint sets and signs in the polyhedral description; on each cell the unique quadratic minimizer is given by matrix products and Moore--Penrose inverses, all Borel functions, and a fixed ordering resolves cell boundaries.  Thus the resulting graph output is an \(\mathcal F_m\)-measurable decoder.  Taking \(m=\lceil U_{\mathrm D}\rceil\) in the definition of \(m^*\) proves \(m^*\le\lceil U_{\mathrm D}\rceil\), with no dimension-only real-arithmetic or bit-complexity assertion.

The partially penalized-Lasso lemma proved in this round supplies another admissible decoder with graph error at most \(\eta\) whenever \(m\ge U_{\mathrm L}\), after the same harmless universal enlargement of \(C\).  It therefore proves \(m^*\le\lceil U_{\mathrm L}\rceil\).  Choose the first decoder when \(U_{\mathrm D}\le U_{\mathrm L}\) and the second otherwise.  This gives the explicit upper map
\[
 m^*\le\left\lceil\min\{U_{\mathrm D},U_{\mathrm L}\}\right\rceil. \tag{37}
\]
In particular, inspection of \(U_{\mathrm L}\) proves the stated comparison: its noise-to-signal term does not carry \(s+1\), while its separate empirical strict-dual-feasibility term does.

For the converse, the adaptive information lower-bound theorem proved in this round applies to every recovery-valid admissible design-decoder pair on the Gaussian subclass of \(\mathcal M_s\).  Taking the infimum over such pairs in the definition of \(m^*\) yields
\[
 m^*\ge c\,\frac{\sigma^2\{\log(n-1)+\log(1/\eta)\}}{q\delta^2}. \tag{38}
\]
Its noiseless conclusion gives the separately displayed degree-one obstruction, and the fixed-design rank-obstruction proposition gives \(m\ge2s+2\) whenever \(\kappa_s(Z)>0\).  Equations (37)--(38) and these two established obstructions prove every asserted inequality and qualifier.  None of them gives an adaptive recovery lower bound matching the upper sparse-exploration terms in every regime, and neither exact optimizer is accompanied by a dimension-only finite-precision complexity analysis.  The final sentence of the theorem therefore records the genuine remaining gap rather than identifying the bracket with a solved sharp frontier.
\end{proof}
\par\smallskip\noindent \textit{Justification.} The theorem gives two explicit recovery-valid upper maps and the adaptive information lower map while retaining the result as a standalone partial bracket. \textit{Gap.} The adaptive converse does not match the sparse-exploration term of the better upper witness in every regime, and neither exact optimizer has a certified finite-precision complexity theorem. \textit{Consumer.} An experimentation platform can choose the shorter proved pilot for its sparsity and signal-to-noise regime without treating an unresolved sharp frontier as established.

\section{Honest scope}

The exact injectivity result, random-pool excitation result, statistical support-recovery upper bounds, and adaptive information lower bound are proved. The Dantzig analysis gives simultaneous edge and nonedge decisions for exact mathematical optimizer outputs and for finite-tolerance outputs when the specified valid primal--dual gap bound is supplied; partially penalized Lasso gives an alternative exact-optimizer recovery result. Neither analysis proves a certified polynomial-time finite-precision procedure for producing those outputs, a dimension-only real-arithmetic bound, or a bit-complexity bound for unencoded real outcomes. The Dantzig upper bound retains a factor \(s+1\) in its noise term, whereas the Lasso alternative removes that factor there but pays an \(s+1\) sparse-exploration cost for simultaneous empirical strict dual feasibility. Which sparse costs are intrinsic, and the sharp minimax pool count outside the matched noisy regime, remain open. The simultaneous Dantzig decision result uses only rowwise scalar martingale-innovation control and permits arbitrary contemporaneous dependence across units. The paper identifies directed additive support; it does not cover nonlinear exposure mappings, latent outcomes, or misspecified additivity. The \(B=1\) reduction must reproduce repeated singleton measurement exactly.
