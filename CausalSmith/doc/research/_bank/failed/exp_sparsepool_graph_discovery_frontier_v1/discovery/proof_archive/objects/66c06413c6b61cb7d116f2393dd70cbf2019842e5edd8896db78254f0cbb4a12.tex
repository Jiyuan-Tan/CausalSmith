For the Dantzig upper bound, apply the simultaneous-certificate theorem with its rowwise statistical error parameter equal to \(\eta/2\) and its pool-construction failure parameter equal to \(\eta/2\).  The theorem's sample-size requirement then becomes
\[
 m\ge Cq^{-1}\left\{(2s+1)\log\!\left(\frac{en}{2s+1}\right)
 +\frac{\sigma^2}{\delta^2}(s+1)\log\!\left(\frac{4n^2}{\eta}\right)
 +\log\!\left(\frac4\eta\right)\right\}=U_{\mathrm D} \tag{36}
\]
after one universal enlargement of \(C\), and its success probability is at least \(1-\eta\).

We verify that this gives an admissible mathematical witness without adding a computational claim.  For every data set, the Dantzig set \(\mathcal D_i\) is nonempty: \(X^{\mathsf T}H_mY_i\) lies in the range of \(X^{\mathsf T}\), which equals the range of \(X^{\mathsf T}X\), so some \(u\) makes \(X^{\mathsf T}(H_mY_i-Xu)=0\).  The set
\[
 Q_i=\{u\in\mathcal D_i:\lVert u\rVert_1\le v_i\}
\]
is therefore a nonempty compact polyhedron, and the strictly convex objective \(\lVert u\rVert_2^2/2\) has the unique minimizer \(\theta_i^{\mathrm D}\).  Write \(Q_i=\{u:a_r^{\mathsf T}u\le b_r,\ r=1,\ldots,N\}\), using the finitely many half-space representations of the infinity- and one-norm constraints.  At the minimizer, \(-\theta_i^{\mathrm D}\) belongs to the cone generated by the active outward normals.  Indeed, otherwise a separating vector would have nonpositive inner product with every active normal and negative inner product with \(\theta_i^{\mathrm D}\); a sufficiently short step in that direction would remain feasible and strictly decrease the objective, a contradiction.  Hence there are nonnegative active multipliers satisfying stationarity and complementary slackness.  Their Lagrangian infimum is a dual lower bound equal to \(\lVert\theta_i^{\mathrm D}\rVert_2^2/2\), so choosing \(\widehat\theta_i=\theta_i^{\mathrm D}\) supplies a valid certificate with zero primal--dual gap and thus meets every positive tolerance in the corrected Dantzig-decoder definition.

This selector is measurable.  One may partition the finite-dimensional data space by the finitely many active constraint sets and signs in the polyhedral description; on each cell the unique quadratic minimizer is given by matrix products and Moore--Penrose inverses, all Borel functions, and a fixed ordering resolves cell boundaries.  Thus the resulting graph output is an \(\mathcal F_m\)-measurable decoder.  Taking \(m=\lceil U_{\mathrm D}\rceil\) in the definition of \(m^*\) proves \(m^*\le\lceil U_{\mathrm D}\rceil\), with no dimension-only real-arithmetic or bit-complexity assertion.

The partially penalized-Lasso lemma proved in this round supplies another admissible decoder with graph error at most \(\eta\) whenever \(m\ge U_{\mathrm L}\), after the same harmless universal enlargement of \(C\).  It therefore proves \(m^*\le\lceil U_{\mathrm L}\rceil\).  Choose the first decoder when \(U_{\mathrm D}\le U_{\mathrm L}\) and the second otherwise.  This gives the explicit upper map
\[
 m^*\le\left\lceil\min\{U_{\mathrm D},U_{\mathrm L}\}\right\rceil. \tag{37}
\]
In particular, inspection of \(U_{\mathrm L}\) proves the stated comparison: its noise-to-signal term does not carry \(s+1\), while its separate empirical strict-dual-feasibility term does.

For the converse, the adaptive information lower-bound theorem proved in this round applies to every recovery-valid admissible design-decoder pair on the Gaussian subclass of \(\mathcal M_s\).  Taking the infimum over such pairs in the definition of \(m^*\) yields
\[
 m^*\ge c\,\frac{\sigma^2\{\log(n-1)+\log(1/\eta)\}}{q\delta^2}. \tag{38}
\]
Its noiseless conclusion gives the separately displayed degree-one obstruction, and the fixed-design rank-obstruction proposition gives \(m\ge2s+2\) whenever \(\kappa_s(Z)>0\).  Equations (37)--(38) and these two established obstructions prove every asserted inequality and qualifier.  None of them gives an adaptive recovery lower bound matching the upper sparse-exploration terms in every regime, and neither exact optimizer is accompanied by a dimension-only finite-precision complexity analysis.  The final sentence of the theorem therefore records the genuine remaining gap rather than identifying the bracket with a solved sharp frontier.
