Put \(h=\min\{B,n-B\}=nq\).  For one pool row write
\[
 \xi=Z_{t\cdot}-p\mathbf 1_n.
\]
If \(B\le n/2\), let \(R\) be the treated set; if \(B>n/2\), let \(R\) be the untreated set.  Then \(R\) is a uniform \(h\)-subset of \(\mathcal U\), and, for every \(u\in\mathbb R^n\),
\[
 \xi^{\mathsf T}u=\epsilon_R\sum_{j\in R}(u_j-\bar u),
 \qquad
 \bar u=n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^n u_j,
 \qquad
 \epsilon_R\in\{-1,1\}.
 \tag{1}
\]
Thus the two budget regimes have the same absolute moments.

We first establish the required rowwise moments.  Let \(a\in\mathbb R^n\) satisfy \(\sum_j a_j=0\), set \(W=\sum_{j\in R}a_j\), choose \(I\) uniformly from \(R\) and \(J\) uniformly from \(R^c\), and define
\[
 R'=(R\setminus\{I\})\cup\{J\},
 \qquad W'=W-a_I+a_J,
 \qquad \lambda_0=\frac{n}{h(n-h)}.
 \tag{2}
\]
The pair \((W,W')\) is exchangeable.  Conditional averaging and \(\sum_j a_j=0\) give
\[
 \mathbb E(W'-W\mid R)
 =-\left(\frac1h+\frac1{n-h}\right)W
 =-\lambda_0W.
 \tag{3}
\]
For every integer \(\ell\ge1\), exchangeability and (3) imply
\[
 \mathbb EW^{2\ell}
 =\frac1{2\lambda_0}\mathbb E\!\left[(W'-W)
       (W'^{\,2\ell-1}-W^{2\ell-1})\right].
 \tag{4}
\]
Indeed, after interchanging \(W\) and \(W'\), the expectation of
\((W'-W)(W'^{\,2\ell-1}+W^{2\ell-1})\) changes sign and hence is zero, whereas conditioning \((W'-W)W^{2\ell-1}\) on \(R\) gives \(-\lambda_0\mathbb EW^{2\ell}\).

The conditional squared increment satisfies
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E\{(W'-W)^2\mid R\}
 &\le \frac2h\sum_{i\in R}a_i^2
      +\frac2{n-h}\sum_{j\notin R}a_j^2\\
 &\le2\lambda_0\lVert a\rVert_2^2.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{5}
\]
For real \(x,y\),
\[
 |(x-y)(x^{2\ell-1}-y^{2\ell-1})|
 \le(2\ell-1)(x-y)^2
       (|x|^{2\ell-2}+|y|^{2\ell-2}).
\]
Using this inequality in (4), then exchangeability and (5), yields
\[
 \mathbb E|W|^{2\ell}
 \le2(2\ell-1)\lVert a\rVert_2^2
       \mathbb E|W|^{2\ell-2}.
 \tag{6}
\]
The exact covariance in \(\mathrm{lem{:}exact\text{-}budget\text{-}covariance}\), applied to the zero-sum vector \(a\), gives
\[
 \mathbb EW^2=\frac{h(n-h)}{n(n-1)}\lVert a\rVert_2^2
 \le2q\lVert a\rVert_2^2.
\]
Iterating (6), and applying Cauchy--Schwarz between adjacent even moments for odd orders, proves that a numerical \(C_1\) satisfies
\[
 \mathbb E|W|^r\le q(C_1r)^{r/2}\lVert a\rVert_2^r,
 \qquad r\ge2.
 \tag{7}
\]

For (1), \(a=u-\bar u\mathbf 1_n\) and \(\lVert a\rVert_2\le\lVert u\rVert_2\).  If \(V=W^2-\mathbb EW^2\), (7) and \(|x-y|^\ell\le2^{\ell-1}(|x|^\ell+|y|^\ell)\) give numerical constants \(v_0,c_0'>0\) such that
\[
 \mathbb E|V|^\ell
 \le\frac{\ell!}{2}
      \{v_0q\lVert u\rVert_2^4\}
      \{c_0'\lVert u\rVert_2^2\}^{\ell-2},
 \qquad \ell\ge2.
 \tag{8}
\]
Expanding the exponential series and using (7) similarly gives, for sufficiently small \(|\lambda|\lVert u\rVert_2\), a centered moment-generating-function bound with variance proxy \(Cq\lVert u\rVert_2^2\) and scale \(C\lVert u\rVert_2\).  Applying exponential Markov bounds to the independent rows under \(\Pi_{m,B}\), first to \(V_t\) and then to \(W_t\), therefore gives, for every \(x>0\),
\[
 \Pr\!\left\{
  \left|\frac1m\sum_{t=1}^m(\xi_t^{\mathsf T}u)^2
        -\mathbb E(\xi^{\mathsf T}u)^2\right|
  >C_2\lVert u\rVert_2^2
       \left(\sqrt{\frac{qx}{m}}+\frac{x}{m}\right)
 \right\}\le2e^{-x},
 \tag{9}
\]
and
\[
 \Pr\!\left\{
  |\bar\xi^{\mathsf T}u|>
  C_2\lVert u\rVert_2
       \left(\sqrt{\frac{qx}{m}}+\frac{x}{m}\right)
 \right\}\le2e^{-x},
 \qquad \bar\xi=m^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^m\xi_t.
 \tag{10}
\]

Set
\[
 M=m^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^m\xi_t\xi_t^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad \Sigma=\mathbb E(\xi\xi^{\mathsf T}).
\]
For every support of cardinality at most \(r\), take a \(1/4\)-net of its Euclidean unit sphere having at most \(9^r\) points.  There are at most \((en/r)^r\) padded supports.  On each support, the elementary net inequalities
\[
 \lVert D\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le2\max_{u\text{ in the net}}|u^{\mathsf T}Du|,
 \qquad
 \lVert b\rVert_2
 \le2\max_{u\text{ in the net}}|b^{\mathsf T}u|
 \tag{11}
\]
follow by approximating a maximizing unit vector.  A union bound in (9)--(10) consequently gives, with probability at least \(1-4e^{-x}\),
\[
 d_r:=\sup_{\substack{\lVert u\rVert_2=1\\\lVert u\rVert_0\le r}}
 |u^{\mathsf T}(M-\Sigma)u|
 \le C_3\left(\sqrt{\frac{qL_r}{m}}+\frac{L_r}{m}\right),
 \tag{12}
\]
\[
 b_r:=\sup_{\substack{\lVert u\rVert_2=1\\\lVert u\rVert_0\le r}}
 |\bar\xi^{\mathsf T}u|
 \le C_3\left(\sqrt{\frac{qL_r}{m}}+\frac{L_r}{m}\right),
 \qquad L_r=r\log(en/r)+x.
 \tag{13}
\]

We record the deterministic extension from sparse vectors to cones.  Partition the coordinates of an arbitrary \(v\), in decreasing order of absolute value, into blocks of size \(\lfloor r/2\rfloor\).  The sum of the Euclidean norms of the blocks after the first is at most \(2\lVert v\rVert_1/\sqrt r\).  Polarization on every union of two blocks, whose cardinality is at most \(r\), and summation give
\[
 |v^{\mathsf T}Dv|
 \le C_4d_r\left(\lVert v\rVert_2+\frac{\lVert v\rVert_1}{\sqrt r}\right)^2.
 \tag{14}
\]
The same blocking without polarization gives
\[
 |b^{\mathsf T}v|
 \le C_4b_r\left(\lVert v\rVert_2+\frac{\lVert v\rVert_1}{\sqrt r}\right).
 \tag{15}
\]

Take \(r=\min\{n,\lceil C_5(s+1)\rceil\}\), where \(C_5\) is a sufficiently large numerical constant, and take \(x=\log(4/\rho)\).  Since \(s\le n/8\),
\[
 L_r\le C_6\left\{(2s+1)\log\!\left(\frac{en}{2s+1}\right)
                  +\log\!\left(\frac2\rho\right)\right\}.
 \tag{16}
\]
The assumed sample-size lower bound makes \(d_r\) a sufficiently small universal multiple of \(q\), and makes \(b_r^2\) a sufficiently small universal multiple of \(q\).

It remains to establish the population margins.  The exact-budget covariance lemma gives
\[
 \Sigma=p(1-p)\frac n{n-1}
 \left(I_n-\frac1n\mathbf1_n\mathbf1_n^{\mathsf T}\right).
 \tag{17}
\]
Because \(p(1-p)=q(1-q)\ge q/2\), and because \(s\le n/8\) implies \(2s+1<n\) with a universal margin, the sparse consequence of that lemma gives
\[
 v^{\mathsf T}\Sigma v\ge c_1q\lVert v\rVert_2^2
 \quad\text{when}\quad \lVert v\rVert_0\le2s+1.
 \tag{18}
\]
For a cone vector, put \(k=|S|\), \(N=n-k\), \(u_0=\lVert v_S\rVert_1\), and \(w_0=\lVert v_{S^c}\rVert_1\).  If \(w_0\le a_0u_0\) and \(N>a_0k\), Cauchy--Schwarz on the two blocks gives, after maximizing over \(0\le w_0/u_0\le a_0\),
\[
 \frac{(\mathbf1_n^{\mathsf T}v)^2}{n\lVert v\rVert_2^2}
 \le \frac{(1+a_0)^2kN}{n(N+a_0^2k)}.
 \tag{19}
\]
For \(a_0=1\), \(k\le s+1\le n/4\), so the right side is at most \(3/4\), including \(n=8,s=1\).  Equations (17) and (19) then imply
\[
 v^{\mathsf T}\Sigma v\ge(q/8)\lVert v\rVert_2^2.
 \tag{20}
\]
For \(a_0=3\) and \(n\ge9\), one has \(k/n\le2/9\) and \(N>3k\); the right side of (19) is at most \(224/225\).  Hence
\[
 v^{\mathsf T}\Sigma v\ge(q/450)\lVert v\rVert_2^2.
 \tag{21}
\]
In the respective cones, \(\lVert v\rVert_1\le(1+a_0)\sqrt{s+1}\lVert v\rVert_2\).  Equations (12)--(16), with the universal sample-size constant enlarged relative to the margins in (18), (20), and (21), transfer all three lower bounds to the sample Gram matrix because
\[
 \frac1mX^{\mathsf T}X=M-\bar\xi\bar\xi^{\mathsf T}.
 \tag{22}
\]
Indeed, for sparse vectors use (12)--(13) directly, and for cone vectors use (14)--(15); in each case the two error terms are strictly smaller than half the corresponding population margin.  Finally, \(e_j^{\mathsf T}\Sigma e_j=p(1-p)\le q\).  The one-sparse case of (12) and (22), noting that the rank-one term is nonnegative, gives \(m^{-1}\lVert Xe_j\rVert_2^2\le2q\) simultaneously for all \(j\), after one final universal enlargement of \(C_0\).  The event has probability at least \(1-\rho\), proving all three conclusions.
