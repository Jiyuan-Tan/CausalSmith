Let \(S_i^+=\{i\}\cup S_i\).  Directed row sparsity gives \(|S_i^+|\le s+1\), and additive interference gives, in vector form,
\[
 H_mY_i=X\theta_i+H_m\varepsilon_i,
 \qquad
 \varepsilon_i=(\varepsilon_{1,i},\ldots,\varepsilon_{m,i})^{\mathsf T}.                       \tag{13}
\]
Apply the balanced-pool concentration lemma with failure probability \(\rho\), increasing the universal sample-size constant if necessary.  On its design event \(E_Z\),
\[
 \frac{\lVert Xv\rVert_2^2}{m}\ge c_0q\lVert v\rVert_2^2
 \quad\text{for every }v\text{ satisfying }
 \lVert v_{(S_i^+)^c}\rVert_1\le\lVert v_{S_i^+}\rVert_1,             \tag{14}
\]
simultaneously in \(i\), and \(\max_j\lVert Xe_j\rVert_2^2\le2mq\).  Notice that (14) holds also at \(n=8,s=1\); it uses the factor-one cone conclusion, not the false factor-three boundary claim.

Because \(Z\) is drawn before the outcomes and independently of their innovations, conditioning on the complete assignment matrix preserves the scalar conditional sub-Gaussian bound.  Iterating that conditional moment bound over \(t\), and using \(X^{\mathsf T}H_m=X^{\mathsf T}\), gives on \(E_Z\), for every fixed \(i,j\),
\[
 \Pr_{P,d}\!\left(\left|e_j^{\mathsf T}X^{\mathsf T}H_m\varepsilon_i\right|>\lambda_m\mid Z\right)
 \le2\exp\!\left(-\frac{\lambda_m^2}{2\sigma^2\lVert Xe_j\rVert_2^2}\right)
 \le\frac{\eta}{n^2}.                                                \tag{15}
\]
When \(\sigma=0\), the left side is zero and (15) is understood by continuity.  A union bound over all \(n^2\) row-coordinate pairs therefore defines an event \(E_\varepsilon\) with conditional probability at least \(1-\eta\) on which
\[
 \max_{i\in\mathcal U}\lVert X^{\mathsf T}H_m\varepsilon_i\rVert_\infty\le\lambda_m.          \tag{16}
\]
No joint moment assumption across the outcome coordinates was used: (15) was proved one fixed row at a time, and only the union bound combined rows.

On \(E_Z\cap E_\varepsilon\), equations (13) and (16) show that the true \(\theta_i\) belongs to \(\mathcal D_i\).  The feasible set is nonempty even off this event, because a least-squares solution makes the normal-equation residual zero.  Hence the linear program defining \(v_i\) attains its finite minimum.  Since \(\theta_i^{\mathrm D}\) minimizes the \(\ell_1\) norm over \(\mathcal D_i\), with \(\Delta_i=\theta_i^{\mathrm D}-\theta_i\) we have
\[
 \lVert\theta_i+\Delta_i\rVert_1\le\lVert\theta_i\rVert_1
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \lVert(\Delta_i)_{(S_i^+)^c}\rVert_1\le\lVert(\Delta_i)_{S_i^+}\rVert_1.                  \tag{17}
\]
Both vectors are Dantzig feasible, so
\[
 \lVert X^{\mathsf T}X\Delta_i\rVert_\infty\le2\lambda_m.           \tag{18}
\]
Using (17), Cauchy--Schwarz, (18), and then (14), in that order, gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 c_0mq\lVert\Delta_i\rVert_2^2
 &\le\lVert X\Delta_i\rVert_2^2
 =\Delta_i^{\mathsf T}X^{\mathsf T}X\Delta_i\\
 &\le2\lambda_m\lVert\Delta_i\rVert_1
 \le4\lambda_m\sqrt{s+1}\lVert\Delta_i\rVert_2.
 \end{aligned}                                                        \tag{19}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \lVert\theta_i^{\mathrm D}-\theta_i\rVert_\infty
 \le\lVert\Delta_i\rVert_2
 \le C_7\sigma\sqrt{\frac{(s+1)\log(2n^2/\eta)}{mq}}.                \tag{20}
\]
Choose the universal tuning constant \(C_{\mathrm{cert}}\ge C_7\).  Equation (20) is then bounded by \(r_m\), simultaneously for all rows.  The noise-to-signal part of the displayed sample-size assumption gives
\[
 r_m^2=C_{\mathrm{cert}}^2\sigma^2
 \frac{(s+1)\log(2n^2/\eta)}{mq}<\frac{\delta^2}{4}                    \tag{21}
\]
after one universal enlargement of \(C\), and hence \(r_m<\delta/2\).

The second optimization has a unique minimizer because its objective is one-strongly convex on a nonempty closed convex feasible set.  If a feasible primal point and dual certificate have gap at most \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{comp}}^2/2\), weak duality bounds its objective excess by the same number; the variational inequality at the minimizer and one-strong convexity then give
\[
 \frac12\lVert\widehat\theta_i-\theta_i^{\mathrm D}\rVert_2^2
 \le \frac{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{comp}}^2}{2}.                         \tag{22}
\]
Thus the Euclidean and coordinatewise errors are at most \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{comp}}\).  Introducing epigraph variables for the absolute values writes both optimization problems using \(O(m+n)\) linear inequalities and \(O(n)\) variables.  A logarithmic-barrier path can be followed by Newton systems of dimension \(O(m+n)\): the Newton decrement squares after a fixed damped entry phase, so \(O(\sqrt{m+n}\log(1/\varepsilon_{\mathrm{comp}}))\) Newton systems suffice for the stated gap.  Gaussian elimination uses a polynomial number of real-arithmetic operations per system.  This supplies the asserted Blum--Shub--Smale polynomial operation bound.

Combining (20) and (22) gives
\[
 \lVert\widehat\theta_i-\theta_i\rVert_\infty\le r_m+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{comp}}=R_m.         \tag{23}
\]
With \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{comp}}=(\delta-2r_m)/8\), direct algebra gives
\[
 \delta-2R_m=\frac34(\delta-2r_m)>0.                                  \tag{24}
\]
If \(A_{ij}=0\), then (23) yields \(|(\widehat\theta_i)_j|\le R_m<\delta-R_m\), so the certificate is \(\mathsf{nonedge}\).  If \(A_{ij}=1\), beta-min and (23) yield
\[
 |(\widehat\theta_i)_j|\ge|\Gamma_{ij}|-R_m\ge\delta-R_m>R_m,
\]
so the certificate is \(\mathsf{edge}\).  Hence no certificate is undecided and \(\widehat A=A\).  Finally,
\[
 \Pr(E_Z\cap E_\varepsilon)\ge1-\rho-\eta,
\]
which proves every probability and simultaneity assertion in the theorem.
