For row \(i\), let \(T_i=\{i\}\cup S_i\) and \(\varepsilon_i=(\varepsilon_{1,i},\ldots,\varepsilon_{m,i})^{\mathsf T}\).  Directed row sparsity gives \(|T_i|\le s+1\).  Additive interference and \(H_m\mathbf1_m=0\) give
\[
 H_mY_i=X\theta_i+H_m\varepsilon_i.
 \tag{23}
\]
Apply \(\mathrm{lem{:}balanced\text{-}pool\text{-}uniform\text{-}concentration}\) with failure probability \(\rho\).  On its event \(E_Z\), simultaneously for every row support,
\[
 \frac{\lVert Xv\rVert_2^2}{m}\ge c_0q\lVert v\rVert_2^2
 \quad\text{if}\quad
 \lVert v_{T_i^c}\rVert_1\le\lVert v_{T_i}\rVert_1,
 \qquad
 \max_j\lVert Xe_j\rVert_2^2\le2mq.
 \tag{24}
\]
This uses the factor-one cone conclusion directly and therefore includes the boundary \(n=8,s=1\); it does not invoke the random-pool excitation theorem, whose factor-three conclusion assumes \(n\ge9\).

The nonadaptive implementation is the admissible design with constant kernels
\[
 \pi_t(\cdot\mid\mathcal F_{t-1})=\operatorname{Unif}(\mathcal Z_B),
 \qquad t=1,\ldots,m.
 \tag{25}
\]
Under the induced sequential law, future pool randomizations have the same uniform distribution after every realized history.  Backward integration of these constant kernels shows that, conditional on the complete assignment matrix \(Z\), each innovation retains the conditional moment-generating-function bound from the noise assumption given its preceding history and current assignment.  Iterating that bound over \(t\), now with the entries of \(Xe_j\) deterministic, gives
\[
 \mathbb E_{P,d}\!\left[\exp\!\left\{\lambda e_j^{\mathsf T}X^{\mathsf T}\varepsilon_i\right\}\middle|Z\right]
 \le \exp\!\left\{\frac{\lambda^2\sigma^2\lVert Xe_j\rVert_2^2}{2}\right\}.
 \tag{26}
\]
This is precisely where the constancy of the kernels in (25), and hence independence of future pool randomizations from current outcomes, is used.  Because \(X^{\mathsf T}H_m=X^{\mathsf T}\), (26) and (24) imply, for fixed \(i,j\),
\[
 \Pr_{P,d}\!\left(
  |e_j^{\mathsf T}X^{\mathsf T}H_m\varepsilon_i|>\lambda_m\mid Z
 \right)
 \le2\exp\!\left(-\frac{\lambda_m^2}
 {2\sigma^2\lVert Xe_j\rVert_2^2}\right)
 \le\frac{\eta}{n^2}.
 \tag{27}
\]
When \(\sigma=0\), the conditional moment-generating-function assumption for both signs forces every innovation to be zero almost surely, so (27) is read with a zero score.  A union bound over all \(n^2\) row-coordinate pairs gives an event \(E_\varepsilon\), having conditional probability at least \(1-\eta\) on \(E_Z\), on which
\[
 \max_{i\in\mathcal U}\lVert X^{\mathsf T}H_m\varepsilon_i\rVert_\infty
 \le\lambda_m.
 \tag{28}
\]
Only the scalar condition for each fixed outcome row was used.  No independence between contemporaneous outcome coordinates is needed for the union bound.

On \(E_Z\cap E_\varepsilon\), (23) and (28) show that \(\theta_i\in\mathcal D_i\).  The set \(\mathcal D_i\) is nonempty for every realization: a least-squares minimizer of \(\lVert H_mY_i-Xu\rVert_2^2\) satisfies the normal equations and hence lies in \(\mathcal D_i\).  The minimum defining \(v_i\) is attained because a minimizing sequence may be restricted to a closed bounded \(\ell_1\)-sublevel set.  Therefore the compact convex set \(\{u\in\mathcal D_i:\lVert u\rVert_1\le v_i\}\) is nonempty, and strict convexity gives the stated unique \(\theta_i^{\mathrm D}\).

Put \(\Delta_i=\theta_i^{\mathrm D}-\theta_i\).  Since \(\theta_i\) is supported on \(T_i\), minimality of \(\lVert\theta_i^{\mathrm D}\rVert_1\) gives
\[
 \lVert(\Delta_i)_{T_i^c}\rVert_1
 \le\lVert(\Delta_i)_{T_i}\rVert_1.
 \tag{29}
\]
Feasibility of both vectors gives
\[
 \lVert X^{\mathsf T}X\Delta_i\rVert_\infty\le2\lambda_m.
 \tag{30}
\]
Using (24), (29), H\"older's inequality, and then Cauchy--Schwarz,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 c_0mq\lVert\Delta_i\rVert_2^2
 &\le\lVert X\Delta_i\rVert_2^2
 =\Delta_i^{\mathsf T}X^{\mathsf T}X\Delta_i\\
 &\le2\lambda_m\lVert\Delta_i\rVert_1
 \le4\lambda_m\sqrt{s+1}\lVert\Delta_i\rVert_2.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{31}
\]
If \(\Delta_i=0\) the desired bound is immediate; otherwise division by its positive Euclidean norm gives
\[
 \lVert\theta_i^{\mathrm D}-\theta_i\rVert_\infty
 \le C_7\sigma\sqrt{\frac{(s+1)\log(2n^2/\eta)}{mq}}.
 \tag{32}
\]
Choose \(C_{\mathrm{cert}}\ge C_7\).  Enlarging the universal sample-size constant makes the noise-to-signal term imply
\[
 r_m<\delta/2.
 \tag{33}
\]

Let \(Q_i=\{u\in\mathcal D_i:\lVert u\rVert_1\le v_i\}\) and \(f(u)=\lVert u\rVert_2^2/2\).  At the minimizer \(\theta_i^{\mathrm D}\), convexity of \(Q_i\) gives the variational inequality \(\langle\theta_i^{\mathrm D},u-\theta_i^{\mathrm D}\rangle\ge0\) for every \(u\in Q_i\).  Hence
\[
 f(u)-f(\theta_i^{\mathrm D})
 \ge\frac12\lVert u-\theta_i^{\mathrm D}\rVert_2^2.
 \tag{34}
\]
For a certified feasible \(\widehat\theta_i\), weak duality and the stipulated primal--dual gap show that the left side of (34) is at most \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{comp}}^2/2\).  Therefore
\[
 \lVert\widehat\theta_i-\theta_i^{\mathrm D}\rVert_2
 \le\varepsilon_{\mathrm{comp}},
 \tag{35}
\]
and the same inequality holds coordinatewise.  Combining (32)--(35),
\[
 \lVert\widehat\theta_i-\theta_i\rVert_\infty\le R_m,
 \qquad
 \delta-2R_m=\frac34(\delta-2r_m)>0.
 \tag{36}
\]
If \(A_{ij}=0\), then \(|(\widehat\theta_i)_j|\le R_m<\delta-R_m\), so \(C_{ij}=\mathsf{nonedge}\).  If \(A_{ij}=1\), beta-min and (36) give
\[
 |(\widehat\theta_i)_j|
 \ge|\Gamma_{ij}|-R_m
 \ge\delta-R_m>R_m,
\]
so \(C_{ij}=\mathsf{edge}\).  Thus no certificate is undecided and \(\widehat A=A\).  Finally,
\(\Pr_{P,d}(E_Z\cap E_\varepsilon)\ge1-\rho-\eta\).  The argument uses only the validity of the supplied optimization certificate.  No dimension-only real-arithmetic or bit-complexity guarantee is asserted.
