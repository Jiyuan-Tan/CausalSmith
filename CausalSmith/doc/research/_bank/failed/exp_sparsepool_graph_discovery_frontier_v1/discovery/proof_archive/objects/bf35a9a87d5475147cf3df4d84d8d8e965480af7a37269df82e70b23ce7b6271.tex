If \(\sigma=0\), the Gaussian lower bound has a zero right side and is immediate.  Suppose henceforth that \(\sigma>0\).  We work inside the subclass with innovations independent over rounds and outcome coordinates and distributed as \(N(0,\sigma^2)\).  All direct effects are zero unless specified below.  Every constructed response surface is additive, every row has at most one spillover, and every nonzero spillover has magnitude exactly \(\delta\); hence this is a subclass of \(\mathcal M_s\) by the four defining assumptions and \(s\ge1\).

Define the rare-side indicator
\[
 a_j(z)=
 \begin{cases}
 z_j,&p\le1/2,\\
 1-z_j,&p>1/2.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{37}
\]
For every \(z\in\mathcal Z_B\), \(\sum_{j=1}^na_j(z)=nq\).  Let \(P_0\) have zero response means and empty graph.  For every \(j\), choose \(i(j)\ne j\) and let \(P_j\) have the sole nonzero mean coordinate
\[
 \mu_{i(j)}(z)=\delta a_j(z).
 \tag{38}
\]
If \(p\le1/2\), (38) is obtained with \(\Gamma_{i(j),j}=\delta\) and zero baseline.  If \(p>1/2\), it is obtained with \(\alpha_{i(j)}=\delta\) and \(\Gamma_{i(j),j}=-\delta\).  Thus \(P_j\) has exactly one directed edge.

Fix an admissible \(d\).  The likelihood-ratio chain rule has no assignment contribution: conditional on any realized past, the predictable kernel \(\pi_t\) is the same function under the two environments.  Conditional on the history and action, two Gaussian full-outcome kernels here have common covariance \(\sigma^2I_n\), so their divergence is the squared Euclidean mean difference divided by \(2\sigma^2\).  Consequently,
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(\Pr_{P_0,d},\Pr_{P_j,d})
 =\frac{\delta^2}{2\sigma^2}
   \mathbb E_{P_0,d}\sum_{t=1}^m a_j(Z_{t\cdot}).
 \tag{39}
\]
Summing (39) over \(j\), using the exact identity \(\sum_j a_j(Z_{t\cdot})=nq\) pathwise, shows that some \(j_*\) satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(\Pr_{P_0,d},\Pr_{P_{j_*},d})
 \le\frac{mq\delta^2}{2\sigma^2}.
 \tag{40}
\]
Let \(E_0=\{g(\mathscr H_m)=A(P_0)\}\).  Uniform error at most \(\eta\) implies
\[
 \Pr_{P_0,d}(E_0)\ge1-\eta,
 \qquad
 \Pr_{P_{j_*},d}(E_0)\le\eta.
\]
Applying the log-sum inequality to the partition \(\{E_0,E_0^c\}\), and using monotonicity of binary relative entropy on this rectangle, gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(\Pr_{P_0,d},\Pr_{P_{j_*},d})
 \ge(1-2\eta)\log\!\left(\frac{1-\eta}{\eta}\right)
 \ge c_1\log(1/\eta),
 \tag{41}
\]
where the last bound uses \(0<\eta\le1/4\).  Equations (40)--(41) prove
\[
 m\ge c_2\frac{\sigma^2\log(1/\eta)}{q\delta^2}.
 \tag{42}
\]

For the \(\log(n-1)\) term, put \(a=\lfloor n/2\rfloor\), choose disjoint sets \(I,J\subseteq\mathcal U\) with \(|I|=|J|=a\), and, for every bijection \(h:I\to J\), define
\[
 \mu_i^{(h)}(z)=\delta a_{h(i)}(z)\quad(i\in I),
 \qquad
 \mu_i^{(h)}(z)=0\quad(i\notin I).
 \tag{43}
\]
The associated graph is the matching \(\{(i,h(i)):i\in I\}\), and every row has degree one.  For every legal action,
\[
 \lVert\mu^{(h)}(z)-\mu^{(0)}(z)\rVert_2^2
 =\delta^2\sum_{j\in J}a_j(z)
 \le\delta^2nq.
\]
The adaptive likelihood-ratio calculation, now oriented from \(P_h\) to \(P_0\), therefore yields
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(\Pr_{P_h,d},\Pr_{P_0,d})
 \le\frac{mnq\delta^2}{2\sigma^2}.
 \tag{44}
\]
Let \(H\) be uniform over the \(M=a!\) bijections and let \(\overline P=M^{-1}\sum_h\Pr_{P_h,d}\).  Expanding the logarithms in the definitions of relative entropy and mutual information gives the exact identity
\[
 \frac1M\sum_h\operatorname{KL}(\Pr_{P_h,d},\Pr_{P_0,d})
 =I(H;\mathscr H_m)+\operatorname{KL}(\overline P,\Pr_{P_0,d}).
 \tag{45}
\]
Thus (44) upper-bounds \(I(H;\mathscr H_m)\).  From the decoded graph define an estimate of \(h\), assigning an arbitrary bijection when the output is not one of the matching graphs.  Its error probability is at most the graph error.  If \(E\) denotes its error indicator, the entropy chain rule gives
\[
 H(H\mid\mathscr H_m)
 \le H(E)+\Pr(E=1)\log(M-1)
 \le\log2+\eta\log M.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 I(H;\mathscr H_m)\ge(1-\eta)\log M-\log2\ge c_3\log M,
 \tag{46}
\]
because \(a\ge4\), \(M\ge24\), and \(\eta\le1/4\).  The final at least \(a/2\) factors in \(a!\) are each at least \(a/2\), so
\[
 \log(a!)\ge\frac a2\log\!\left(\frac a2\right)
 \ge c_4n\log(n-1),
 \qquad n\ge8,
 \tag{47}
\]
for a universal \(c_4>0\).  The last comparison is immediate for \(n\ge16\) from \(a\ge n/2-1\), and the finitely many integers \(8\le n<16\) have a positive minimum ratio.  Combining (44)--(47) gives
\[
 m\ge c_5\frac{\sigma^2\log(n-1)}{q\delta^2}.
 \tag{48}
\]
The maximum of (42) and (48) is at least half their sum, which proves the first assertion after changing the universal constant.

It remains to prove the noiseless combinatorial assertion.  Here uniform identification means probability-one correctness for every environment in the degree-one noiseless subclass.  Because every action space is finite, realize each predictable kernel by an independent uniform random seed and an inverse-distribution map.  Use the same seed sequence for the empty environment and for the finite family of all one-edge alternatives described below.  The intersection of their probability-one correctness events has probability one; fix a seed in this intersection.  Under the empty environment, the all-zero outcome history produces a deterministic legal matrix \(Z\).

Every column of \(Z\) must be nonconstant.  If column \(j\) is identically zero, the alternative \(\Gamma_{ij}=\delta\), for any \(i\ne j\), is invisible along the all-zero path.  If column \(j\) is identically one, the alternative \(\alpha_i=-\delta\), \(\Gamma_{ij}=\delta\) is invisible.  Under the common seed, induction over rounds shows that an invisible alternative has the same outcomes, takes the same actions, and reaches the same terminal output as the empty graph, contradicting correctness for both.

Distinct columns must also be distinct.  If columns \(j\) and \(k\) agree, take recipient \(k\), set \(\Gamma_{kj}=\delta\) and \(\tau_k=-\delta\), and set all other coefficients to zero.  Then
\[
 \mu_k(Z_{t\cdot})=\delta Z_{tj}-\delta Z_{tk}=0
 \quad\text{for every }t.
\]
This is again a degree-one graph indistinguishable from the empty graph along the fixed seed, a contradiction.  Hence the \(n\) column words, the all-zero word, and the all-one word are pairwise distinct.  Therefore
\[
 2^m\ge n+2,
 \qquad m\ge\lceil\log_2(n+2)\rceil.
 \tag{49}
\]
Nonconstancy also says that every column contains both a one and a zero.  Counting entries by rows gives
\[
 mB\ge n,
 \qquad m(n-B)\ge n.
\]
Since \(q=\min\{B/n,1-B/n\}\),
\[
 m\ge\max\!\left\{\left\lceil\frac nB\right\rceil,
                    \left\lceil\frac n{n-B}\right\rceil\right\}
 =\lceil1/q\rceil.
 \tag{50}
\]
Equations (49)--(50) prove the noiseless degree-one lower bound.
