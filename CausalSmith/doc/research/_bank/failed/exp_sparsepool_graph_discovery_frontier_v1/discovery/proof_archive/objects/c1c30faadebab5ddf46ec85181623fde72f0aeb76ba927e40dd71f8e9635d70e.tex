Write \(F_{i,Z}:\mathbb R\times\mathcal T_i(s)\to\mathbb R^m\) for the observable noiseless row map
\[
F_{i,Z}(a,u)=a\mathbf 1_m+Zu.
\]
We prove the asserted equivalence with the universal quantifier read jointly, namely
\[
\bigl[\text{for every }i\in\mathcal U,\ F_{i,Z}\text{ is injective}\bigr]
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \kappa_s(Z)>0. \tag{1}
\]

First suppose \(\kappa_s(Z)>0\), fix \(i\in\mathcal U\), and suppose that
\[
F_{i,Z}(a,u)=F_{i,Z}(a',u')
\qquad
\text{for }(a,u),(a',u')\in\mathbb R\times\mathcal T_i(s).
\]
Set \(b=a-a'\) and \(v=u-u'\).  By the definition of \(\mathcal T_i(s)\),
\[
\operatorname{supp}(v_{-i})
 \subseteq \operatorname{supp}(u_{-i})\cup\operatorname{supp}(u'_{-i}),
\qquad
\lVert v_{-i}\rVert_0\le 2s. \tag{2}
\]
Equality of the two observed vectors gives
\[
b\mathbf 1_m+Zv=0. \tag{3}
\]
Because \(H_m\mathbf 1_m=0\) and \(X=H_mZ\) by the centered-design definition, applying \(H_m\) to (3) yields
\[
Xv=0. \tag{4}
\]
If \(v\ne0\), then (2) makes \(v\) feasible in the infimum defining \(\kappa_s(Z)\), so
\[
0=\frac{\lVert Xv\rVert_2}{\sqrt m\lVert v\rVert_2}
 \ge \kappa_s(Z)>0,
\]
a contradiction.  Thus \(v=0\).  Equation (3) then gives \(b\mathbf 1_m=0\), and \(m\ge1\) implies \(b=0\).  Hence \(u=u'\) and \(a=a'\).  Since \(i\) was arbitrary, every \(F_{i,Z}\) is injective.

For the converse, suppose \(\kappa_s(Z)=0\).  We first justify that the zero infimum supplies an exact sparse null vector.  For \(i\in\mathcal U\) and \(J\subseteq\mathcal U\setminus\{i\}\) with \(\lvert J\rvert\le2s\), let
\[
E_{i,J}=\{v\in\mathbb R^n:\operatorname{supp}(v_{-i})\subseteq J\}.
\]
The admissible vectors in the definition of \(\kappa_s(Z)\) form the finite union of the subspaces \(E_{i,J}\).  On each nonempty compact unit sphere
\(E_{i,J}\cap\{v:\lVert v\rVert_2=1\}\), the continuous function \(v\mapsto\lVert Xv\rVert_2/\sqrt m\) attains its minimum.  There are finitely many pairs \((i,J)\).  Consequently the infimum defining \(\kappa_s(Z)\) is attained after normalizing \(v\), and \(\kappa_s(Z)=0\) yields some \(i_0\in\mathcal U\) and \(v\ne0\) such that
\[
\lVert v_{-i_0}\rVert_0\le2s,
\qquad Xv=0. \tag{5}
\]

To obtain not only coefficient aliasing but also graph aliasing, select a row index \(r\) as follows.  If \(v_{-i_0}\ne0\), take \(r=i_0\).  If \(v_{-i_0}=0\), then \(v\) is supported on \(\{i_0\}\); choose any \(r\ne i_0\), which is possible because \(n\ge8\).  In both cases,
\[
1\le\lVert v_{-r}\rVert_0\le2s, \tag{6}
\]
where the upper bound in the second case uses \(s\ge1\).  Partition \(\operatorname{supp}(v_{-r})\) into disjoint sets \(J_0,J_1\) with \(\lvert J_0\rvert\le s\) and \(\lvert J_1\rvert\le s\), which is possible by (6).  Define \(u,u'\in\mathbb R^n\) coordinatewise by
\[
u_r=v_r,
\quad u_j=v_j\ \text{for }j\in J_0,
\quad u_j=0\ \text{otherwise},
\]
and
\[
u'_r=0,
\quad u'_j=-v_j\ \text{for }j\in J_1,
\quad u'_j=0\ \text{otherwise}.
\]
Then
\[
u-u'=v,
\qquad u,u'\in\mathcal T_r(s), \tag{7}
\]
and (6) ensures that the off-diagonal supports of \(u\) and \(u'\) are different.

By (5) and \(X=H_mZ\), \(H_mZv=0\).  Directly from
\(H_m=I_m-m^{-1}\mathbf 1_m\mathbf 1_m^{\mathsf T}\), its kernel is
\(\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf 1_m\}\): indeed, \(H_mw=0\) implies
\(w=m^{-1}(\mathbf 1_m^{\mathsf T}w)\mathbf 1_m\).  Therefore there is \(c\in\mathbb R\) such that
\[
Zv=c\mathbf 1_m. \tag{8}
\]
Using (7)--(8), we obtain the observational alias
\[
F_{r,Z}(-c,u)-F_{r,Z}(0,u')
=-c\mathbf 1_m+Z(u-u')
=-c\mathbf 1_m+Zv
=0. \tag{9}
\]
The two parameter pairs in (9) are distinct because \(v=u-u'\ne0\).  Hence \(F_{r,Z}\) is not injective, proving the reverse implication in (1).

It remains to connect (1), which concerns an observed-law map, to the causal support target rather than identifying the two by definition.  Under additive interference, the noiseless observed column for outcome row \(i\) is
\[
M_{\cdot i}=\alpha_i\mathbf 1_m+Z\theta_i=F_{i,Z}(\alpha_i,\theta_i). \tag{10}
\]
Directed row sparsity gives \(\theta_i\in\mathcal T_i(s)\), because its off-diagonal support is \(S_i\) and \(\lvert S_i\rvert\le s\).  When \(\kappa_s(Z)>0\), (1) makes the inverse \(F_{i,Z}^{-1}\) single-valued on the range containing \(M_{\cdot i}\).  Thus an explicit noiseless recovery functional is
\[
\widetilde\theta_i(M;Z)
=\operatorname{pr}_2\!\left(F_{i,Z}^{-1}(M_{\cdot i})\right),
\qquad
\mathcal R_Z(M)_{ij}
=\mathbf 1\{j\ne i,\ (\widetilde\theta_i(M;Z))_j\ne0\}. \tag{11}
\]
Equations (10)--(11), rather than a definition of the causal target, prove
\[
\widetilde\theta_i(M;Z)=\theta_i,
\qquad
\mathcal R_Z(M)_{ij}
=\mathbf 1\{i\ne j,\Gamma_{ij}\ne0\}=A_{ij}. \tag{12}
\]
Hence all signed rows and their directed support are exactly identified.

Finally, if \(\kappa_s(Z)=0\), use the two alternatives in (9) as row \(r\) of two additive-interference systems and take identical admissible parameters in every other row.  Both systems satisfy directed row sparsity by (7), and (9) makes their entire noiseless outcome matrices equal.  Nevertheless, their signed row coefficients differ, and their off-diagonal supports differ by (6)--(7), so their matrices \(A\) differ.  No functional of the observed noiseless matrix can therefore recover all signed rows or \(A\) uniformly on the stated model class.  This proves the claimed necessity and completes the identification equivalence.
