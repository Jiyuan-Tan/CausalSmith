Put \(\xi_t=Z_{t\cdot}-p\mathbf 1_n\) and \(\Sigma=\mathbb E(\xi_t\xi_t^{\mathsf T})\).  The rows are independent by the definition of \(\Pi_{m,B}\), and the exact covariance lemma gives
\[
 \Sigma=p(1-p)\frac{n}{n-1}
 \left(I_n-\frac1n\mathbf 1_n\mathbf 1_n^{\mathsf T}\right).                                      \tag{1}
\]
We first record the scalar concentration calculation used below.  For a deterministic \(u\in\mathbb R^n\), set \(W=\xi_t^{\mathsf T}u\).  Because exactly \(B\) coordinates are selected,
\[
 W=\sum_{j\in R}(u_j-\bar u),
 \qquad
 \bar u=n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^n u_j,
\]
where \(R\) is a uniform \(B\)-subset.  If \(p>1/2\), the same expression with the complement of \(R\) changes only the sign and replaces \(p\) by \(q=1-p\).  Expanding an even power, grouping terms by their distinct indices, and using
\[
 \Pr\{j_1,\ldots,j_a\in R\}=\frac{(B)_a}{(n)_a}\le p^a
 \quad (j_1,\ldots,j_a\text{ distinct})
\]
in the case \(p\le1/2\), followed by \(2|xy|\le x^2+y^2\) to pair the centered factors, gives, for every integer \(\ell\ge2\),
\[
 \mathbb E|W|^{2\ell}
 \le (2\ell)!\,C_1^{\ell-1}q\lVert u\rVert_2^{2\ell}.                                      \tag{2}
\]
The complement argument gives the same bound when \(p>1/2\).  For completeness, the pairing step is obtained by assigning each occurrence in a monomial to its first equal-index partner; an unpaired occurrence is summed using \(\sum_j(u_j-\bar u)=0\), and each remaining pair contributes at most \(\sum_j(u_j-\bar u)^2\le\lVert u\rVert_2^2\).  There are at most \((2\ell)!\) assignments, and the inclusion probability of the first nonempty index block is at most \(q\).  This proves (2) with a numerical \(C_1\).

Applying (2) to \(W^2-\mathbb EW^2\) and expanding the exponential series yields numerical constants \(c_1,C_2>0\) such that, for \(0<a\le1\),
\[
 \Pr\left\{\left|\frac1m\sum_{t=1}^mW_t^2-\mathbb EW^2\right|>a q\lVert u\rVert_2^2\right\}
 \le2\exp(-c_1a^2mq),                                                \tag{3}
\]
and the same moment calculation applied to \(m^{-1}\sum_tW_t\) gives
\[
 \Pr\left\{\left|\frac1m\sum_{t=1}^mW_t\right|>a\sqrt q\lVert u\rVert_2\right\}
 \le2\exp(-c_1a^2mq).                                                \tag{4}
\]
Indeed, after division by the displayed thresholds, the term of order \(\ell\) in either exponential series is bounded by a geometric series once the exponential parameter is a sufficiently small universal multiple of \(m\); Markov's inequality then gives (3)--(4).

Let \(r\le n\).  For every support of size \(r\), take a \(1/4\)-net of its Euclidean unit sphere with at most \(9^r\) points.  There are at most \((en/r)^r\) such supports.  A union bound in (3)--(4), followed by the standard net identities
\[
 \lVert D\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
 \le2\max_{u\text{ in the net}}|u^{\mathsf T}Du|,
 \qquad
 \lVert b\rVert_2
 \le2\max_{u\text{ in the net}}|b^{\mathsf T}u|,
\]
shows that, when
\[
 mq\ge C_3\{r\log(en/r)+\log(2/\rho)\},                              \tag{5}
\]
simultaneously for all \(r\)-sparse unit vectors \(u\),
\[
 \left|u^{\mathsf T}\left(\frac1m\sum_{t=1}^m\xi_t\xi_t^{\mathsf T}-\Sigma\right)u\right|
 \le a q,
 \qquad
 \left|u^{\mathsf T}\bar\xi\right|\le a\sqrt q,                    \tag{6}
\]
where \(\bar\xi=m^{-1}\sum_t\xi_t\), and \(a>0\) may be any fixed sufficiently small numerical constant by changing \(C_3\).

We next pass from sparse vectors to cones.  The following deterministic blocking calculation is used.  If \(D\) is symmetric and
\[
 d_r=\sup_{\lVert u\rVert_2=1,\ \lVert u\rVert_0\le r}|u^{\mathsf T}Du|,
\]
then ordering the coordinates of \(v\) by magnitude, splitting them into blocks of size \(\lfloor r/2\rfloor\), and using polarization on every pair of blocks gives
\[
 |v^{\mathsf T}Dv|
 \le C_4d_r\left(\lVert v\rVert_2+\frac{\lVert v\rVert_1}{\sqrt r}\right)^2.                 \tag{7}
\]
The identical block decomposition gives, for a vector \(b\),
\[
 |b^{\mathsf T}v|
 \le C_4\sup_{\lVert u\rVert_2=1,\ \lVert u\rVert_0\le r}|b^{\mathsf T}u|
 \left(\lVert v\rVert_2+\frac{\lVert v\rVert_1}{\sqrt r}\right).                            \tag{8}
\]
To check (7), polarization bounds a cross-block term by twice \(d_r\) times the product of the two block norms, while monotone ordering gives the sum of all block norms after the first at most \(\lVert v\rVert_1/\sqrt{\lfloor r/2\rfloor}\); summing the products proves (7).  Equation (8) is the same argument without polarization.

Choose \(r=\min\{n,\lceil C_5(s+1)\rceil\}\), where \(C_5\) is a sufficiently large universal constant.  If \(r=n\), (6) already controls every vector.  Otherwise, (7)--(8) and (6) control every unit vector satisfying \(\lVert v\rVert_1\le4\sqrt{s+1}\).  The entropy in (5) obeys
\[
 r\log(en/r)\le C_6(2s+1)\log\!\left(\frac{en}{2s+1}\right),          \tag{9}
\]
including the case \(r=n\), so the asserted sample-size condition suffices.

It remains to give the population lower bounds.  The exact covariance lemma and \(2s+1<n\) imply, for \((2s+1)\)-sparse \(v\),
\[
 v^{\mathsf T}\Sigma v
 \ge p(1-p)\frac{n-(2s+1)}{n-1}\lVert v\rVert_2^2
 \ge c_2q\lVert v\rVert_2^2.                                        \tag{10}
\]
For a cone vector let \(k=|S|\), \(N=n-k\), \(u=\lVert v_S\rVert_1\), and \(w=\lVert v_{S^c}\rVert_1\).  If \(w\le a_0u\) and \(N>a_0k\), minimizing the squared Euclidean norm within each block and then maximizing \((u+w)^2\) makes \(w=a_0u\), and hence
\[
 \frac{(\mathbf 1_n^{\mathsf T}v)^2}{n\lVert v\rVert_2^2}
 \le \frac{(1+a_0)^2kN}{n(N+a_0^2k)}.                                \tag{11}
\]
For \(a_0=1\), the assumption \(s\le n/8\) gives \(k\le s+1\le n/4\), and the right-hand side is at most \(3/4\).  Thus (1) gives \(v^{\mathsf T}\Sigma v\ge(q/8)\lVert v\rVert_2^2\).  For \(a_0=3\), \(n\ge9\) and \(s\le n/8\) give \(N>3k\); the right-hand side of (11) is at most \(224/225\), with the extremal admissible ratio \(k/n=2/9\).  Hence \(v^{\mathsf T}\Sigma v\ge(q/450)\lVert v\rVert_2^2\).  Both cone conditions imply \(\lVert v\rVert_1\le(1+a_0)\sqrt{s+1}\lVert v\rVert_2\), so (7)--(9), with \(a\) chosen smaller than half of these population constants, transfer (10)--(11) to \(m^{-1}X^{\mathsf T}X\), because
\[
 \frac1mX^{\mathsf T}X
 =\frac1m\sum_{t=1}^m\xi_t\xi_t^{\mathsf T}-\bar\xi\bar\xi^{\mathsf T}.                     \tag{12}
\]
Finally, (1) has \(e_j^{\mathsf T}\Sigma e_j=p(1-p)\le q\).  Taking the constants in (6) small enough and using (12) yields \(m^{-1}\lVert Xe_j\rVert_2^2\le2q\) for every \(j\).  This proves all asserted simultaneous conclusions.
