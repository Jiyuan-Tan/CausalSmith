Fix \(P\in\mathcal M_s\).  For row \(i\), let \(T=T_i=\{i\}\cup S_i\), \(k=|T|\le s+1\), and define \(w\in\mathbb R^T\) by \(w_i=0\) and \(w_j=\operatorname{sign}(\Gamma_{ij})\) for \(j\in S_i\).  The estimator minimizes
\[
 \frac1{2m}\lVert H_mY_i-Xb\rVert_2^2
 +\lambda_{\mathrm L}\lVert b_{-i}\rVert_1,
 \qquad \lambda_{\mathrm L}=c_{\mathrm L}q\delta,
 \tag{31}
\]
with \(c_{\mathrm L}>0\) chosen below.  A minimizer exists because the objective is a continuous convex quadratic plus a polyhedral coercive penalty modulo its flat null directions; among all minimizers the Euclidean norm has a unique minimizer, which supplies a measurable tie-break.  On the success event constructed below the minimizer of (31) itself is unique.

We first derive the exact-budget population algebra required by the directive.  Write
\[
 a=p(1-p)\frac n{n-1},
 \qquad \Sigma=a\left(I_n-\frac1n\mathbf1_n\mathbf1_n^{\mathsf T}\right).
\]
For every \(T\) of size \(k<n\), the Sherman--Morrison identity gives
\[
 \Sigma_{TT}^{-1}=a^{-1}\left(I_k+\frac{\mathbf1_k\mathbf1_k^{\mathsf T}}{n-k}\right).
 \tag{32}
\]
For \(j\notin T\), \(\Sigma_{jT}=-(a/n)\mathbf1_k^{\mathsf T}\); hence
\[
 \left|\Sigma_{jT}\Sigma_{TT}^{-1}w\right|
 =\frac{|\sum_{\ell\in S_i}w_\ell|}{n-k}
 \le\frac{s}{n-s-1}\le\frac16.
 \tag{33}
\]
The last inequality uses \(s\le n/8\), including the boundary \(n=8,s=1\).  Formula (33) is stronger than the sign-specific irrepresentability requirement.  Zhao and Yu, \emph{Journal of Machine Learning Research} 7 (2006), equation (2), record the stronger row-\(\ell_1\) version needed uniformly over all sign vectors.  We need only the \(n\) realized sign vectors and prove their empirical bounds below.  The primal--dual-witness architecture is the one isolated in Wainwright, Technical Report 709, Proposition 1 and conditions (14)--(15), but every specialization to the present dependent-coordinate design is derived here.  Lounici, \emph{Electronic Journal of Statistics} 2 (2008), Theorems 1--2, obtains sup-norm and thresholded-sign guarantees from a mutual-coherence condition; we do not invoke those theorems because empirically verifying their sign-uniform coherence condition would impose a different, stronger scaling.

Let \(G_T=m^{-1}X_T^{\mathsf T}X_T\).  Apply the balanced-pool concentration lemma with failure probability \(\eta/8\).  Its sparse lower bound and column upper bound give an event \(E_0\), of probability at least \(1-\eta/8\), on which
\[
 \lambda_{\min}(G_T)\ge c_0q
 \quad\text{for all the displayed row supports and their subsets},
 \qquad \max_j\lVert Xe_j\rVert_2^2\le2mq.
 \tag{34}
\]

We next prove the support-specific empirical bounds.  Let \(T_0\subsetneq\mathcal U\), \(|T_0|=k_0\le s+1\), let \(j\notin T_0\), and let \(c\in[-1,1]^{T_0}\).  On the part of \(E_0\) determined by \(X_{T_0}\), put
\[
 h=X_{T_0}(X_{T_0}^{\mathsf T}X_{T_0})^{-1}c.
\]
Because \(h\perp\mathbf1_m\), \(h^{\mathsf T}X_j=h^{\mathsf T}Z_j\).  Conditional on the raw assignment columns \(Z_{\cdot T_0}\), the variables \(Z_{tj}\) are independent over \(t\), with
\[
 \mathbb E(Z_{tj}\mid Z_{tT_0})
 =\frac{B-\sum_{\ell\in T_0}Z_{t\ell}}{n-k_0}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \mathbb E(h^{\mathsf T}X_j\mid Z_{\cdot T_0})
 =-\frac{\mathbf1_{k_0}^{\mathsf T}c}{n-k_0}.
 \tag{35}
\]
Moreover the conditional Bernoulli variances are at most \(2q\): if \(p\le1/2\), their success probabilities are at most \(B/(n-k_0)\le2q\), while if \(p>1/2\), their failure probabilities are at most \((n-B)/(n-k_0)\le2q\); here \(k_0\le s+1\le n/4\).  Equation (34) gives
\[
 \lVert h\rVert_2^2\le\frac{k_0}{c_0mq},
 \qquad
 \lVert h\rVert_\infty\le\frac{k_0}{c_0mq}.
 \tag{36}
\]
The second inequality uses \(\lVert (X_{tT_0})\rVert_2\le\sqrt{k_0}\).  Conditional Bernstein applied to (35)--(36) yields, for each fixed \(0<u\le1\),
\[
 \Pr\!\left(
  \left|h^{\mathsf T}X_j+\frac{\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}c}{n-k_0}\right|>u
  \middle|Z_{\cdot T_0}\right)
 \le2\exp\!\left(-\frac{c_1mqu^2}{k_0}\right).
 \tag{37}
\]

Use (37) first with \(T_0=T\), \(c=w\), and every \(j\notin T\).  Since \(|\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}w|/(n-k)\le1/6\), a union bound gives
\[
 \max_{i}\max_{j\notin T_i}
 \left|m^{-1}X_j^{\mathsf T}X_{T_i}G_{T_i}^{-1}w\right|
 \le\frac13
 \tag{38}
\]
except on an event of probability at most \(\eta/8\).  Use (37) again with \(T_0=T\setminus\{\ell\}\) and \(c=w_{T_0}\).  The Schur-complement formula and \(\lambda_{\min}(G_T)\ge c_0q\) show, simultaneously for the at most \(n(s+1)\) relevant pairs,
\[
 \lVert G_T^{-1}w\rVert_\infty\le\frac{C_5}{q},
 \tag{39}
\]
outside another event of probability at most \(\eta/8\).  Indeed, the numerator in the scalar Schur formula is bounded by
\[
 |w_\ell|+\frac{|\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}w_{T_0}|}{n-k+1}+\frac1{12}<2,
\]
whereas its denominator is the Schur complement of \(G_{T_0}\) in \(G_T\), which is at least \(\lambda_{\min}(G_T)\).  The assumed term \(mq\ge C(s+1)\log(8n^3/\eta)\) makes both union bounds valid.  This proves (38)--(39), rather than assuming empirical irrepresentability.

Condition now on the design event (34), (38), and (39).  Since the pools are nonadaptive, every noise score below is a deterministic linear transform of the rowwise martingale innovations.  The candidate restricted solution is
\[
 \widetilde b_T=\theta_{i,T}
 +G_T^{-1}\left(m^{-1}X_T^{\mathsf T}H_m\varepsilon_i
                    -\lambda_{\mathrm L}w\right),
 \qquad \widetilde b_{T^c}=0.
 \tag{40}
\]
For \(\ell\in S_i\), the noise coordinate in (40) is conditionally sub-Gaussian with variance proxy at most
\[
 \frac{\sigma^2}{m}e_\ell^{\mathsf T}G_T^{-1}e_\ell
 \le\frac{\sigma^2}{c_0mq}.
 \tag{41}
\]
A union bound over at most \(n(s+1)\) coordinates, the noise-to-signal sample condition, (39), and a choice \(c_{\mathrm L}\le(8C_5)^{-1}\) imply
\[
 \max_{i}\max_{\ell\in S_i}|\widetilde b_\ell-\theta_{i,\ell}|
 \le\frac\delta4+\lambda_{\mathrm L}\frac{C_5}{q}
 \le\frac{3\delta}{8}<\delta.
 \tag{42}
\]
Thus every spillover coordinate in \(T\) has its correct nonzero sign.

Let \(P_T=X_T(X_T^{\mathsf T}X_T)^{-1}X_T^{\mathsf T}\).  For \(j\notin T\), the inactive KKT score at (40) equals
\[
 m^{-1}X_j^{\mathsf T}(I-P_T)H_m\varepsilon_i
 +\lambda_{\mathrm L}m^{-1}X_j^{\mathsf T}X_TG_T^{-1}w.
 \tag{43}
\]
The first term is conditionally sub-Gaussian with variance proxy at most \(2\sigma^2q/m\), by (34), and the second has absolute value at most \(\lambda_{\mathrm L}/3\), by (38).  The same noise-to-signal condition and a union bound over fewer than \(n^2\) scores therefore make the first term at most \(\lambda_{\mathrm L}/3\) simultaneously, except on an event of probability at most \(\eta/4\).  Hence every inactive dual coordinate is strictly smaller than one in absolute value.

For the coordinates in \(T\), (40) makes the KKT equations hold with subgradient \(w\); in particular, the unpenalized coordinate \(i\) has subgradient zero.  Equation (42) supplies the required active signs, and (43) supplies strict dual feasibility.  Convexity now proves optimality.  It also proves uniqueness: any second optimum must have the same fitted values, strict dual feasibility forces its coordinates in \(T^c\) to vanish, and positive definiteness of \(G_T\) then forces equality on \(T\).  Thus the decoder recovers every \(S_i\), and hence \(A\), on the intersection of the displayed events.  Their failure probabilities sum to at most \(\eta\), uniformly in \(P\), which proves the lemma.

The calculation also identifies the obstruction to the more ambitious scaling.  In (37), take \(c=w\): conditionally, the empirical inactive dual fluctuation has variance of order \(q\lVert h\rVert_2^2\), which can be of order \(s/m\).  Controlling its maximum over order \(n^2\) realized inactive coordinates by a constant margin therefore requires the displayed \(mq\gtrsim s\log(n/\eta)\) bound in this proof.  Population formula (33) alone does not remove that empirical maximum.
