Fix \(P\in\mathcal M_s\).  For row \(i\), put \(T=T_i=\{i\}\cup S_i\), \(k=|T|\le s+1\), and define \(w\in\mathbb R^T\) by \(w_i=0\) and \(w_j=\operatorname{sign}(\Gamma_{ij})\) for \(j\in S_i\).  The estimator minimizes
\[
 \frac1{2m}\lVert H_mY_i-Xb\rVert_2^2
 +\lambda_{\mathrm L}\lVert b_{-i}\rVert_1,
 \qquad \lambda_{\mathrm L}=c_{\mathrm L}q\delta,
 \tag{51}
\]
where the numerical \(c_{\mathrm L}>0\) is chosen below.  A minimizer exists: after introducing positive and negative parts for the penalized coordinates, (51) is a finite-dimensional convex quadratic program whose lower level sets have compact images in the fitted-value and penalized-coordinate variables; the unpenalized null directions may be fixed by minimum Euclidean norm.  The minimum-Euclidean-norm point of the closed convex minimizer set is unique.

We first derive the exact-budget population algebra.  Write
\[
 a_0=p(1-p)\frac n{n-1},
 \qquad
 \Sigma=a_0\left(I_n-\frac1n\mathbf1_n\mathbf1_n^{\mathsf T}\right).
\]
For every \(T\) of size \(k<n\), direct multiplication gives
\[
 \Sigma_{TT}^{-1}=a_0^{-1}
 \left(I_k+\frac{\mathbf1_k\mathbf1_k^{\mathsf T}}{n-k}\right).
 \tag{52}
\]
For \(j\notin T\), \(\Sigma_{jT}=-(a_0/n)\mathbf1_k^{\mathsf T}\); hence
\[
 \left|\Sigma_{jT}\Sigma_{TT}^{-1}w\right|
 =\frac{|\sum_{\ell\in S_i}w_\ell|}{n-k}
 \le\frac{s}{n-s-1}\le\frac16.
 \tag{53}
\]
The final inequality follows from \(s\le n/8\), including \(n=8,s=1\).

Let \(G_T=m^{-1}X_T^{\mathsf T}X_T\).  Apply the balanced-pool concentration lemma with failure probability \(\eta/8\).  The assumed bound on \(m\) dominates its requirement.  Hence there is an event \(E_0\), of probability at least \(1-\eta/8\), on which
\[
 \lambda_{\min}(G_T)\ge c_0q
 \quad\text{for every displayed row support and its subsets},
 \qquad
 \max_j\lVert Xe_j\rVert_2^2\le2mq.
 \tag{54}
\]

We next prove, rather than assume, the support-specific empirical dual bounds.  Fix \(T_0\subsetneq\mathcal U\), \(k_0=|T_0|\le s+1\), \(j\notin T_0\), and \(c\in[-1,1]^{T_0}\).  Conditional on the columns \(Z_{\cdot T_0}\), and on the local event \(\lambda_{\min}(G_{T_0})\ge c_0q\), set
\[
 h=X_{T_0}(X_{T_0}^{\mathsf T}X_{T_0})^{-1}c.
\]
Because \(h\perp\mathbf1_m\), \(h^{\mathsf T}X_j=h^{\mathsf T}Z_j\).  Independence of pool rows and uniform sampling without replacement within each row give
\[
 \mathbb E(Z_{tj}\mid Z_{tT_0})
 =\frac{B-\sum_{\ell\in T_0}Z_{t\ell}}{n-k_0},
\]
and therefore, using \(h^{\mathsf T}Z_\ell=h^{\mathsf T}X_\ell=c_\ell\),
\[
 \mathbb E(h^{\mathsf T}X_j\mid Z_{\cdot T_0})
 =-\frac{\mathbf1_{k_0}^{\mathsf T}c}{n-k_0}.
 \tag{55}
\]
The conditional Bernoulli variances are at most \(2q\).  Indeed, if \(p\le1/2\), the conditional success probability is at most \(B/(n-k_0)\le2q\); if \(p>1/2\), the conditional failure probability is at most \((n-B)/(n-k_0)\le2q\).  Here \(k_0\le s+1\le n/4\).  Equation (54), restricted to this local support, gives
\[
 \lVert h\rVert_2^2\le\frac{k_0}{c_0mq},
 \qquad
 \lVert h\rVert_\infty\le\frac{k_0}{c_0mq}.
 \tag{56}
\]
The second bound follows from \(\lVert X_{tT_0}\rVert_2\le\sqrt{k_0}\).  Expanding the conditional exponential moments of the independent centered Bernoulli summands, using their total variance at most \(2q\lVert h\rVert_2^2\) and maximum magnitude at most \(\lVert h\rVert_\infty\), gives, for \(0<u\le1\),
\[
 \Pr\!\left(
  \left|h^{\mathsf T}X_j+\frac{\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}c}{n-k_0}\right|>u
  \middle|Z_{\cdot T_0}\right)
 \le2\exp\!\left(-\frac{c_1mqu^2}{k_0}\right).
 \tag{57}
\]
The conditioning in (57) is only on \(Z_{\cdot T_0}\); the global event \(E_0\) is intersected afterward, so no unobserved-column conditioning is introduced.

Use (57) with \(T_0=T\), \(c=w\), \(u=1/6\), and each \(j\notin T\).  Equation (53) and a union bound over all rows and inactive coordinates give
\[
 \max_i\max_{j\notin T_i}
 \left|m^{-1}X_j^{\mathsf T}X_{T_i}G_{T_i}^{-1}w\right|
 \le\frac13
 \tag{58}
\]
outside an event of probability at most \(\eta/8\).  Apply (57) again with \(T_0=T\setminus\{\ell\}\), \(c=w_{T_0}\), and \(u=1/12\).  If \(|T|=1\), then \(w=0\) and the next bound is automatic.  Otherwise, the scalar Schur-complement formula gives
\[
 (G_T^{-1}w)_\ell
 =\frac{w_\ell-G_{\ell T_0}G_{T_0}^{-1}w_{T_0}}
 {G_{\ell\ell}-G_{\ell T_0}G_{T_0}^{-1}G_{T_0\ell}}.
\]
The numerator has absolute value less than \(2\) by (57), because
\(|\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}w_{T_0}|/(n-k+1)\le s/(n-s)\).  The denominator is at least \(c_0q\): its reciprocal is a diagonal entry of \(G_T^{-1}\), which is at most \((c_0q)^{-1}\) on (54).  A union bound over at most \(n(s+1)\) pairs yields
\[
 \max_i\lVert G_{T_i}^{-1}w\rVert_\infty\le\frac{C_5}{q}
 \tag{59}
\]
outside another event of probability at most \(\eta/8\).  The exploration term \(mq\ge C(s+1)\log(8n^3/\eta)\) makes both union bounds valid.

Condition now on the intersection of (54), (58), and (59).  Use the constant uniform kernels (25).  As proved by backward integration in (26), conditioning on the complete \(Z\) preserves the rowwise innovation moment-generating-function inequality because all future pool randomizations are independent of current outcomes.  Define the restricted candidate
\[
 \widetilde b_T=\theta_{i,T}
 +G_T^{-1}\left(m^{-1}X_T^{\mathsf T}H_m\varepsilon_i
                    -\lambda_{\mathrm L}w\right),
 \qquad \widetilde b_{T^c}=0.
 \tag{60}
\]
For \(\ell\in S_i\), the noise coordinate in (60) is conditionally sub-Gaussian with variance proxy
\[
 \frac{\sigma^2}{m}e_\ell^{\mathsf T}G_T^{-1}e_\ell
 \le\frac{\sigma^2}{c_0mq}.
 \tag{61}
\]
A union bound over at most \(n(s+1)\) coordinates, the noise-to-signal sample condition, (59), and the choice \(c_{\mathrm L}\le(8C_5)^{-1}\) imply, except on an event of probability at most \(\eta/4\),
\[
 \max_i\max_{\ell\in S_i}|\widetilde b_\ell-\theta_{i,\ell}|
 \le\frac\delta4+\lambda_{\mathrm L}\frac{C_5}{q}
 \le\frac{3\delta}{8}<\delta.
 \tag{62}
\]
Thus every true spillover coordinate has its correct nonzero sign.

Let \(P_T=X_T(X_T^{\mathsf T}X_T)^{-1}X_T^{\mathsf T}\).  For \(j\notin T\), the inactive KKT score at (60) is
\[
 m^{-1}X_j^{\mathsf T}(I-P_T)H_m\varepsilon_i
 +\lambda_{\mathrm L}m^{-1}X_j^{\mathsf T}X_TG_T^{-1}w.
 \tag{63}
\]
The first term is conditionally sub-Gaussian with variance proxy at most \(2\sigma^2q/m\), by (54), while the second has absolute value at most \(\lambda_{\mathrm L}/3\), by (58).  The noise-to-signal condition and a union bound over fewer than \(n^2\) scores make the first term at most \(\lambda_{\mathrm L}/3\) simultaneously, except on an event of probability at most \(\eta/4\).  Hence every inactive dual coordinate has absolute value at most \(2/3<1\).

Equation (60) makes the active KKT equations hold with subgradient \(w\), including zero subgradient on the unpenalized coordinate \(i\).  Equation (62) gives the active signs and (63) gives strict inactive dual feasibility.  The subgradient inequality therefore proves that \(\widetilde b\) minimizes (51).  If \(b'\) is another minimizer, equality in the convexity argument forces \(Xb'=X\widetilde b\); strict inactive dual feasibility forces \(b'_{T^c}=0\), and positive definiteness of \(G_T\) then forces \(b'_T=\widetilde b_T\).  Thus the minimizer is unique on the success event and recovers every \(S_i\).

Off that event, the stipulated minimum-Euclidean-norm minimizer gives a measurable decoder: for each of the finitely many assignment matrices \(Z\), the KKT systems for (51) have finitely many sign and active-set patterns, and on each pattern the minimum-norm solution is given by fixed linear equations together with finitely many linear inequalities in \(H_mY_i\).  These pieces are Borel, and the unique minimum-norm tie-break agrees on their overlaps.  Thus the row solutions and the terminal support map are Borel measurable.  Summing the five displayed failure bounds gives probability at most \(\eta\), uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal M_s\), so the resulting nonadaptive pair is admissible and satisfies
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M_s}\Pr_{P,d}(\widehat A\ne A)\le\eta.
\]
This is an exact-minimizer statistical statement and supplies no finite-precision operation or bit-complexity guarantee.
