%%% FIELD dube-general-statement
Let \(k_{\mathrm{alg}}\geq 1\).  If an ideal over a field in \(k_{\mathrm{alg}}\) indeterminates is generated by polynomials of total degree at most three, then, for every admissible monomial order, every element of its reduced Gr\"obner basis has total degree at most
\[
 \left\lfloor
 2\left(\frac{15}{2}\right)^{2^{k_{\mathrm{alg}}-1}}
 \right\rfloor.
\]
%%% FIELD graph-cutoff-construction
For an acyclic mixed graph \(G=(V_n,D,B)\), put \(m=n(n+1)/2\) and define the graph-sensitive elimination cutoff
\[
 \mathfrak B(G)=
 \left\lfloor
 2\left(\frac{15}{2}\right)^{2^{m+|D|-1}}
 \right\rfloor.
\]
%%% FIELD stopping-schedule-proposal
Given an acyclic mixed graph \(G=(V_n,D,B)\), compute \(\mathfrak B(G)\), run \(\operatorname{DBI}(G,\mathfrak B(G))\) once through its complete internal weighted-degree loop, disable finite wall-clock timeouts, and retain the exact identifying formulas or the complete exact bounded-search certificate.  On the directed-indegree-two input domain, the graph-independent alternative replaces \(\mathfrak B(G)\) by \(B_2(n)=U_2(n)\).

%%% FIELD graph-sensitive-bound-statement
Let \(G=(V_n,D,B)\) be an acyclic mixed graph for which \(\phi_G\) is generically birational onto its image.  For every structural coordinate \(q=\lambda_{uv}\in\theta_G\), there are \(a_q,b_q\in\mathbb Q[s]\) such that
\[
 \tau_G(a_q)\neq0,
 \qquad
 q a_q(s)-b_q(s)\in I_G,
 \qquad
 \deg\{q a_q-b_q\}\leq\mathfrak B(G).
\]
After all structural coordinates have been identified, every supported disturbance coordinate \(q=\omega_{ij}\in\theta_G\) has the recursive certificate
\[
 q-\bigl[(I_n-\Lambda)^{\mathsf T}\Sigma(I_n-\Lambda)\bigr]_{ij}\in I_G
\]
of total degree at most three and denominator one.  Consequently \(ID_G\leq\mathfrak B(G)\).
%%% FIELD graph-sensitive-bound-proof
Write \(m=n(n+1)/2\), \(e=|D|\), and let \(L\) be the set of the \(e\) structural coordinates \(\lambda_{uv}\).  Put
\[
 R=\mathbb Q[L,s],
 \qquad
 T=R[\omega_{ij}:i=j\text{ or }i\leftrightarrow j\in B],
 \qquad
 H=(I_n-\Lambda)^{\mathsf T}\Sigma(I_n-\Lambda).
 \tag{1}
\]
Thus \(R\) has exactly \(m+e\) indeterminates.  Let
\[
 \mathcal M=\lbrace(i,j):1\leq i<j\leq n,\ i\leftrightarrow j\notin B\rbrace,
 \qquad
 \mathcal E=\lbrace(i,j):1\leq i\leq j\leq n,\ i=j\text{ or }i\leftrightarrow j\in B\rbrace.
\]
By \(\mathrm{lem:cubic\mbox{-}graph\mbox{-}ideal\mbox{-}presentation}\) and the support restriction \(\mathrm{ass:omega\mbox{-}support}\),
\[
 I_G=
 \langle H_{ij}:(i,j)\in\mathcal M\rangle_T
 +\langle H_{ij}-\omega_{ij}:(i,j)\in\mathcal E\rangle_T.
 \tag{2}
\]

Define the \(R\)-algebra homomorphism \(\pi:T\to R\) by
\[
 \pi(\omega_{ij})=H_{ij}\quad ((i,j)\in\mathcal E),
 \qquad
 \pi|_R=\operatorname{id}_R.
\]
Successively viewing \(T\) as a univariate polynomial ring in each supported \(\omega_{ij}\) and dividing by the monic polynomial \(\omega_{ij}-H_{ij}\) proves
\[
 \ker(\pi)=\langle H_{ij}-\omega_{ij}:(i,j)\in\mathcal E\rangle_T.
 \tag{3}
\]
If \(f\in I_G\cap R\), then (2), followed by \(\pi\), gives
\[
 f=\pi(f)\in\langle H_{ij}:(i,j)\in\mathcal M\rangle_R.
\]
The reverse inclusion follows directly from (2).  Hence the structural elimination ideal is
\[
 J_G:=I_G\cap R=langle H_{ij}:(i,j)\in\mathcal M\rangle_R.
 \tag{4}
\]
Every entry of \(H\) has total degree at most three in \((L,s)\), since it is an entry of the product of a matrix linear in \(L\), a matrix linear in \(s\), and another matrix linear in \(L\).  Thus (4) is a degree-three presentation in \(m+e\) variables.

Fix a structural coordinate \(q\in L\), and choose the lexicographic order in which the coordinates in \(L\setminus\{q\}\) precede \(q\), which precedes every covariance coordinate.  By \(\mathrm{lem:dube\mbox{-}general\mbox{-}cubic\mbox{-}degree\mbox{-}bound}\), every member of the reduced Gr\"obner basis \(\mathcal B_q\) of \(J_G\) has degree at most
\[
 \mathfrak B(G)=
 \left\lfloor2\left(\frac{15}{2}\right)^{2^{m+e-1}}\right\rfloor.
 \tag{5}
\]
Set
\[
 K=J_G\cap\mathbb Q[s],
 \quad
 J_q=J_G\cap\mathbb Q[q,s],
 \quad
 \mathcal H_q=\mathcal B_q\cap\mathbb Q[q,s],
 \quad
 \mathcal H_0=\mathcal B_q\cap\mathbb Q[s].
 \tag{6}
\]
We verify the elimination fact used below.  If \(f\in J_q\), some \(g\in\mathcal B_q\) has leading monomial dividing the leading monomial of \(f\).  That leading monomial contains no coordinate in \(L\setminus\{q\}\).  Under the chosen lexicographic order, any term of \(g\) containing such a coordinate would exceed every term containing none, so \(g\in\mathbb Q[q,s]\).  Therefore \(\mathcal H_q\) is a Gr\"obner basis of \(J_q\).  Applying the same argument after eliminating \(q\) shows that \(\mathcal H_0\) is a Gr\"obner basis of \(K\).

The ideal \(J_G\) is the kernel of the restriction of \(\tau_G\) to \(R\), by the definition \(I_G=\ker(\tau_G)\) and (4).  It is therefore prime, and
\[
 A:=\mathbb Q[s]/K
\]
is a domain embedded by covariance substitution in \(\mathbb Q[\theta_G]\).  Generic birationality of \(\phi_G\) says that the function field generated by the covariance coordinates equals \(\mathbb Q(\theta_G)\).  In particular, the image of \(q\) lies in \(\operatorname{Frac}(A)\).  Consequently there are \(a,b\in\mathbb Q[s]\) with
\[
 a\notin K,
 \qquad
 qa-b\in J_q.
 \tag{7}
\]
Replace \(a\) and \(b\) by their normal forms \(a'\) and \(b'\) modulo \(\mathcal H_0\).  Since \(a-a'\in K\subseteq J_G\) and \(b-b'\in K\subseteq J_G\), the ideal property gives
\[
 f':=qa'-b'\in J_q.
 \tag{8}
\]
Moreover \(a'\neq0\), because \(a\notin K\), and \(a'\) is reduced with respect to \(\mathcal H_0\).

Reduce the nonzero polynomial \(f'\) to zero by \(\mathcal H_q\).  Its leading monomial contains \(q\), because \(q\) precedes all covariance variables and \(a'\neq0\).  No leading monomial from \(\mathcal H_0\) divides a monomial of \(qa'\), since that would make the corresponding monomial of \(a'\) reducible.  Hence some \(h_q\in\mathcal H_q\setminus\mathcal H_0\) has a leading monomial containing exactly one power of \(q\) and dividing a monomial of \(qa'\).  No term of \(h_q\) can contain a higher power of \(q\), because such a term would precede its leading monomial.  Thus
\[
 h_q=q a_q(s)-b_q(s)
 \tag{9}
\]
for some nonzero \(a_q,b_q\in\mathbb Q[s]\), after changing a sign if necessary.  If \(a_q\in K\), then the leading monomial of \(a_q\) is divisible by the leading monomial of an element of \(\mathcal H_0\).  The corresponding monomial of \(q a_q\) is a monomial of \(h_q\), contradicting reducedness of \(\mathcal B_q\).  Therefore
\[
 a_q\notin K,
 \qquad
 \tau_G(a_q)\neq0.
 \tag{10}
\]
Equations (5), (9), and (10) prove the claimed direct structural certificate.

It remains to identify the supported disturbance coordinates without increasing the largest recursive degree.  Once all coordinates in \(L\) have entered the identified set, equation (2) supplies, for every \((i,j)\in\mathcal E\),
\[
 \omega_{ij}-H_{ij}\in I_G.
 \tag{11}
\]
This is a recursive certificate with denominator one, which is nonzero everywhere, and its total degree is at most three by the degree calculation following (4).  Finally, \(m+e\geq1\), so (5) gives \(\mathfrak B(G)\geq15>3\).  Ordering the structural certificates (9) first and the disturbance certificates (11) afterward therefore gives
\[
 ID_G\leq\max\{\mathfrak B(G),3\}=\mathfrak B(G),
\]
by \(\mathrm{def:recursive\mbox{-}certificate}\).
%%% FIELD graph-sensitive-schedule-statement
Let \(G\) be any acyclic mixed graph on \(n\) vertices.  One exact call to \(\operatorname{DBI}(G,\mathfrak B(G))\), with no wall-clock timeout, terminates and returns ``yes'' with minimum-degree rational formulas if and only if \(\phi_G\) is generically birational onto its image.  Its arithmetic complexity is
\[
 O\!\left(
 \mathfrak B(G)n^5
 \{3\mathfrak B(G)n\}^{4\alpha_{\mathrm{alg}}\mathfrak B(G)^2\log(3\mathfrak B(G)n)}
 \right).
\]
A ``no'' answer is accompanied by a complete exact bounded-search certificate.
%%% FIELD graph-sensitive-schedule-proof
Because \(\mathfrak B(G)\geq15\), it is an admissible cutoff for \(\mathrm{lem:dbi\mbox{-}fixed\mbox{-}cutoff\mbox{-}guarantee}\).  If \(\phi_G\) is generically birational onto its image, \(\mathrm{thm:graph\mbox{-}sensitive\mbox{-}elimination\mbox{-}bound}\) gives
\[
 ID_G\leq\mathfrak B(G),
 \tag{1}
\]
so the exact algorithm returns ``yes'' and its adaptive internal ordering returns formulas at the least successful recursive degree.  If \(\phi_G\) is not generically birational onto its image, the fixed-cutoff guarantee returns ``no''.  This proves the equivalence.

The cutoff \(\mathfrak B(G)\) is finite.  At a fixed finite cutoff the algorithm has finitely many monomials, recursive states, and exact row reductions; removing the wall-clock timeout therefore yields termination.  Substituting \(d=\mathfrak B(G)\) in the complexity bound of \(\mathrm{lem:dbi\mbox{-}fixed\mbox{-}cutoff\mbox{-}guarantee}\) gives the displayed arithmetic bound.

For a ``no'' output, retain the terminal exact rank-intersection transcript.  If \(S\) is its terminal identified set, then \(S\subsetneq\theta_G\), and for every \(q\in\theta_G\setminus S\) it records
\[
 \dim A_{\mathfrak B(G)}(S)
 +\dim\bigl(qC_{\mathfrak B(G)}(S)\bigr)
 =\dim\bigl(A_{\mathfrak B(G)}(S)+qC_{\mathfrak B(G)}(S)\bigr).
 \tag{2}
\]
Thus all relevant intersections are zero and \(S\) is a proper fixed point of the degree closure.  By \(\mathrm{thm:closure\mbox{-}characterization}\), (2) certifies \(ID_G>\mathfrak B(G)\).  In view of (1), this is impossible under generic birationality.  Hence the finite rational transcript is a complete nonidentifiability certificate, not a timeout status.

%%% FIELD effective-proof
Fix \(G\in\mathcal G_{2}^{\mathrm{rat}}(n)\).  By \(\mathrm{def:graph\mbox{-}class}\), its directed part is acyclic, its maximum directed indegree is at most two, and \(\phi_G\) is generically birational onto its image.  Therefore \(\mathrm{thm:graph\mbox{-}sensitive\mbox{-}elimination\mbox{-}bound}\) supplies the stated direct structural certificates, the subsequent degree-three disturbance certificates, and
\[
 ID_G\leq\mathfrak B(G).
 \tag{1}
\]

It remains to compare the graph-sensitive cutoff to a function of \(n\) alone.  If \(n=1\), acyclicity forces \(|D|=0\), so
\[
 \mathfrak B(G)
 =\left\lfloor2\left(\frac{15}{2}\right)^{2^{1-1}}\right\rfloor
 =15
 =U_2(1).
 \tag{2}
\]
Suppose \(n\geq2\).  A finite directed acyclic graph has a vertex of indegree zero: otherwise, repeatedly choosing a parent must revisit a vertex and create a directed cycle.  Removing such a vertex preserves acyclicity, so induction on the number of vertices produces a topological ordering \(v_1,\ldots,v_n\).  Every parent of \(v_j\) belongs to \(\{v_1,\ldots,v_{j-1}\}\).  The first vertex therefore has indegree zero, the second has indegree at most one, and each later vertex has indegree at most two by \(\mathrm{ass:indegree\mbox{-}two}\).  Summing indegrees yields
\[
 |D|=\sum_{j=1}^n|\operatorname{pa}_G(v_j)|
 \leq0+1+2(n-2)=2n-3.
 \tag{3}
\]
The map \(x\mapsto\left\lfloor2(15/2)^{2^x}\right\rfloor\) is increasing.  With \(m=n(n+1)/2\), (3) gives
\[
 \mathfrak B(G)
 =\left\lfloor2\left(\frac{15}{2}\right)^{2^{m+|D|-1}}\right\rfloor
 \leq
 \left\lfloor2\left(\frac{15}{2}\right)^{2^{m+2n-4}}\right\rfloor
 =U_2(n).
 \tag{4}
\]
Combining (1), (2), and (4) proves \(ID_G\leq U_2(n)\) and all of the more precise certificate claims.  Finally, \(\mathcal G_{2}^{\mathrm{rat}}(n)\) is a finite set of mixed graphs on \(V_n\), so maximizing the proved inequality over its members and using the definition of \(D_2(n)\) gives
\[
 D_2(n)=\max_{G\in\mathcal G_{2}^{\mathrm{rat}}(n)}ID_G\leq U_2(n).
\]
No lower-bound or sharpness assertion is used.

One exact call to \(DBI\) at degree \(B_2(n)\), with no wall-clock timeout, terminates and returns ``yes'' with minimum-degree rational formulas if and only if \(G\in\mathcal G_{2}^{\mathrm{rat}}(n)\).  Its arithmetic complexity is
\[
 O\!\left(B_2(n)n^5\{3B_2(n)n\}^{4\alpha_{\mathrm{alg}}B_2(n)^2\log(3B_2(n)n)}\right).
\]
A ``no'' answer is accompanied by a complete exact bounded-search certificate rather than a timeout status.  This schedule is unconditional, although no sharpness claim is made for \(B_2(n)\).
%%% FIELD total-proof
The graph-sensitive schedule is already total by \(\mathrm{thm:graph\mbox{-}sensitive\mbox{-}total\mbox{-}stopping\mbox{-}schedule}\), using the actual cutoff \(\mathfrak B(G)\).  We now verify the advertised graph-independent corollary.  By \(\mathrm{thm:effective\mbox{-}elimination\mbox{-}upper\mbox{-}bound}\), every \(G\in\mathcal G_{2}^{\mathrm{rat}}(n)\) satisfies
\[
 ID_G\leq U_2(n)=B_2(n).
 \tag{1}
\]
The replacement definition of \(U_2\) gives \(B_2(n)\geq15\), hence \(B_2(n)\geq2\), for every positive integer \(n\).  Apply \(\mathrm{lem:dbi\mbox{-}fixed\mbox{-}cutoff\mbox{-}guarantee}\) with \(d=B_2(n)\).  If \(G\in\mathcal G_{2}^{\mathrm{rat}}(n)\), (1) makes the call return ``yes'', and the adaptive internal search returns formulas at the least degree at which an identifying order succeeds.  Conversely, on the stipulated acyclic directed-indegree-two input domain, failure of membership in \(\mathcal G_{2}^{\mathrm{rat}}(n)\) is precisely failure of generic birationality, and the same fixed-cutoff guarantee returns ``no''.  Thus ``yes'' is equivalent to membership in \(\mathcal G_{2}^{\mathrm{rat}}(n)\).

At the fixed finite cutoff \(B_2(n)\), the weighted-degree loop contains finitely many monomials, recursive states, and exact row reductions.  Removing the wall-clock timeout therefore leaves a terminating computation.  Substitution of \(d=B_2(n)\) into the fixed-cutoff arithmetic bound gives
\[
 O\!\left(B_2(n)n^5\{3B_2(n)n\}^{4\alpha_{\mathrm{alg}}B_2(n)^2\log(3B_2(n)n)}\right).
 \tag{2}
\]

For a ``no'' output, retain the final exact rank-intersection transcript.  If \(S\) is the terminal identified set, then for every \(q\in\theta_G\setminus S\) it records
\[
 \dim A_{B_2(n)}(S)
 +\dim\bigl(qC_{B_2(n)}(S)\bigr)
 =\dim\bigl(A_{B_2(n)}(S)+qC_{B_2(n)}(S)\bigr).
 \tag{3}
\]
Equation (3) states that every relevant intersection is zero.  The terminal set is therefore a proper fixed point of \(\operatorname{Cl}_{B_2(n)}(G)\), and \(\mathrm{thm:closure\mbox{-}characterization}\) certifies
\[
 ID_G>B_2(n).
 \tag{4}
\]
In conjunction with (1), (4) is impossible for a member of \(\mathcal G_{2}^{\mathrm{rat}}(n)\).  Thus the transcript is a complete finite exact nonmembership certificate on the stated input domain.  Neither the graph-sensitive schedule nor its uniform corollary uses the unresolved value of \(ID_{K_6}\) or the bounded-versus-unbounded status of \(D_2\), and no sharpness claim is made for either cutoff.
