%%% FIELD proof-rational-chart-inference
Put \(m=n(n+1)/2\), write \(\widehat s=\operatorname{vech}(\widehat\Sigma)\), and abbreviate the nonempty chart in the statement by
\[
 \mathcal C=\mathcal C_G(\delta_{\mathrm{chart}},\kappa_{\mathrm{chart}},K_{\mathrm{chart}},\nu_{\mathrm{chart}}).
\]
Throughout the proof, uniform stochastic order is meant over this fixed chart: for example, \(R_N=o_{\mathbb P}^{\mathcal C}(1)\) means
\[
 \sup_{\Sigma\in\mathcal C}\Pr_{\Sigma}\{\lVert R_N\rVert>\epsilon\}\longrightarrow0
 \quad\text{for every }\epsilon>0.
\]

We first establish the distribution and the uniform moment bounds used below.  Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:linear\mbox{-}sem}\) and the inverse appearing in the definition of \(\Sigma\) give
\[
 X=(I_n-\Lambda^{\mathsf T})^{-1}\varepsilon
   =(I_n-\Lambda)^{-\mathsf T}\varepsilon.
\]
Assumption \(\mathrm{ass:gaussian\mbox{-}noise}\) and the defining covariance formula therefore imply
\[
 X\sim\mathcal N(0,\Sigma).
\tag{1}
\]
By \(\mathrm{def:regular\mbox{-}chart}\), every \(\Sigma\in\mathcal C\) satisfies
\[
 \kappa_{\mathrm{chart}}I_n\preceq\Sigma\preceq K_{\mathrm{chart}}I_n.
\tag{2}
\]
The set of symmetric matrices satisfying (2) is compact.  The denominator margin in the same definition excludes every pole of the fixed rational map \(F_G\).  On that pole-free set, \(F_G\), \(J_F\), and the Gaussian second-moment matrix \(V_F\) are continuous functions of \(s=\operatorname{vech}(\Sigma)\); the last assertion follows because Gaussian fourth moments are polynomial functions of the entries of \(\Sigma\).  Hence all four weak inequalities in \(\mathrm{def:regular\mbox{-}chart}\) define a closed subset of the compact set in (2), and \(\mathcal C\) is compact.

If \(Z\sim\mathcal N(0,I_n)\), then (1) permits the representation \(X\overset{d}=\Sigma^{1/2}Z\).  Thus, for every fixed positive integer \(k\),
\[
 \sup_{\Sigma\in\mathcal C}\mathbb E_{\Sigma}\lVert X\rVert^k
 \le K_{\mathrm{chart}}^{k/2}\mathbb E\lVert Z\rVert^k<\infty.
\tag{3}
\]
This records explicitly the moment domination used in every subsequent uniform probability bound.

Define
\[
 Y_i=\operatorname{vech}(X_iX_i^{\mathsf T}-\Sigma).
\]
By \(\mathrm{ass:iid\mbox{-}sampling}\), the vectors \(Y_i\) are independent and identically distributed, have mean zero by (1), and have uniformly bounded moments of every fixed order by (3).  The definition of \(\widehat\Sigma\) gives the exact identity
\[
 \widehat s-s
 =\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N Y_i
  -\operatorname{vech}(\bar X\bar X^{\mathsf T}).
\tag{4}
\]
For each coordinate, Chebyshev's inequality and (3) give, uniformly over \(\mathcal C\),
\[
 \frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N Y_i=O_{\mathbb P}^{\mathcal C}(N^{-1/2}),
 \qquad
 \bar X=O_{\mathbb P}^{\mathcal C}(N^{-1/2}).
\]
Because \(m\) and \(n\) are fixed, (4) consequently yields
\[
 \widehat s-s=O_{\mathbb P}^{\mathcal C}(N^{-1/2}),
 \qquad
 \sqrt N\operatorname{vech}(\bar X\bar X^{\mathsf T})
 =O_{\mathbb P}^{\mathcal C}(N^{-1/2}).
\tag{5}
\]

We next exclude empirical poles and obtain the asymptotic linear expansion.  Compactness of \(\operatorname{vech}(\mathcal C)\), continuity of the finitely many polynomials \(Q_{F,j}\), and the chart inequality
\[
 \min_{1\le j\le r}|Q_{F,j}(s)|\ge\delta_{\mathrm{chart}}>0
\]
give a single \(\eta>0\) such that, for every \(s\in\operatorname{vech}(\mathcal C)\),
\[
 \lVert t-s\rVert\le\eta
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \min_{1\le j\le r}|Q_{F,j}(t)|\ge\delta_{\mathrm{chart}}/2.
\tag{6}
\]
The closed \(\eta\)-tube in (6) may be chosen bounded; it is compact and contains no pole.  Since \(F_G\) is a fixed finite-dimensional rational map, its first and second derivatives are uniformly bounded on this tube.  Taylor's formula with second-order remainder, followed by (4)--(6), gives on \(\{\lVert\widehat s-s\rVert\le\eta\}\)
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sqrt N\{F_G(\widehat s)-F_G(s)\}
 &=J_F(s)\sqrt N(\widehat s-s)
   +O_{\mathbb P}^{\mathcal C}\!\left(\sqrt N\lVert\widehat s-s\rVert^2\right)\\
 &=\frac1{\sqrt N}\sum_{i=1}^N J_F(s)Y_i
   -J_F(s)\sqrt N\operatorname{vech}(\bar X\bar X^{\mathsf T})
   +o_{\mathbb P}^{\mathcal C}(1)\\
 &=\frac1{\sqrt N}\sum_{i=1}^N\operatorname{IF}_F(X_i)
   +o_{\mathbb P}^{\mathcal C}(1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]
The first equality uses the uniform second-derivative bound; its remainder is \(o_{\mathbb P}^{\mathcal C}(1)\) by (5).  The second equality uses (4), and the last uses (5) and the definition of \(\operatorname{IF}_F\).  Also by (5), the complement of the event used in (7) has probability tending to zero uniformly.  On that event (6) shows that the pole convention in \(\mathrm{def:plug\mbox{-}in\mbox{-}estimator}\) is not invoked.  Thus (7) holds for the total estimator \(\widehat\theta_G\).

We now prove the required uniform central limit assertion rather than assuming a pointwise central limit theorem.  Write
\[
 \psi_{\Sigma}(X)=\operatorname{IF}_F(X)
 =J_F(s)\operatorname{vech}(XX^{\mathsf T}-\Sigma),
 \qquad
 Z_{\Sigma}=V_F(\Sigma)^{-1/2}\psi_{\Sigma}(X).
\]
The inverse exists because \(\mathrm{def:regular\mbox{-}chart}\) imposes
\(\lambda_{\min}(V_F)\ge\nu_{\mathrm{chart}}>0\).  That lower bound, the uniform bound on \(J_F\), and (3) imply
\[
 \mathbb E_{\Sigma}Z_{\Sigma}=0,
 \qquad
 \mathbb E_{\Sigma}Z_{\Sigma}Z_{\Sigma}^{\mathsf T}=I_r,
 \qquad
 \sup_{\Sigma\in\mathcal C}\mathbb E_{\Sigma}\lVert Z_{\Sigma}\rVert^3<\infty.
\tag{8}
\]
The middle equality is exactly the definition of \(V_F\).

For a bounded function \(h:\mathbb R^r\to\mathbb R\) with Lipschitz constant at most one, set
\[
 h_{\epsilon}(x)=\mathbb E\{h(x+\epsilon G_0)\},
 \qquad G_0\sim\mathcal N(0,I_r).
\]
Gaussian convolution, subtraction of \(h(x)\) before differentiation, and integration by parts give constants depending only on fixed \(r\) such that
\[
 \lVert h-h_{\epsilon}\rVert_{\infty}\le C_r\epsilon,
 \qquad
 \sup_x\lVert D^3h_{\epsilon}(x)\rVert\le C_r\epsilon^{-2}.
\tag{9}
\]
Let \(G_1,\ldots,G_N\) be independent \(\mathcal N(0,I_r)\) vectors.  Replace the summands \(Z_{\Sigma,i}/\sqrt N\) one at a time by \(G_i/\sqrt N\).  Conditional third-order Taylor expansion of \(h_{\epsilon}\) about the sum of the other terms has the same constant, linear, and quadratic expected terms for the two increments by (8).  Its integral remainder, (8), (9), and the finite third Gaussian moment give
\[
 \sup_{\Sigma\in\mathcal C}
 \left|
 \mathbb E_{\Sigma}h_{\epsilon}\!\left(N^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^N Z_{\Sigma,i}\right)
 -\mathbb Eh_{\epsilon}\!\left(N^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^N G_i\right)
 \right|
 \le C_rN^{-1/2}\epsilon^{-2}.
\tag{10}
\]
Because \(N^{-1/2}\sum_iG_i\sim\mathcal N(0,I_r)\), taking \(\epsilon=N^{-1/6}\) in (9)--(10) proves
\[
 \sup_{\Sigma\in\mathcal C}
 d_{\mathrm{BL}}\!\left(
  \mathcal L_{\Sigma}\!\left[V_F(\Sigma)^{-1/2}N^{-1/2}
    \sum_{i=1}^N\operatorname{IF}_F(X_i)\right],
  \mathcal N(0,I_r)
 \right)\longrightarrow0.
\tag{11}
\]
Continuity of \(V_F\) on the compact chart gives
\(\sup_{\Sigma\in\mathcal C}\lambda_{\max}(V_F)<\infty\).  Hence multiplication by \(V_F^{1/2}\) in (11) has a uniformly bounded Lipschitz norm, and (11) is equivalently the uniform, covariance-matched assertion
\[
 N^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^N\operatorname{IF}_F(X_i)
 \rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,V_F).
\tag{12}
\]
Equations (7) and (12) prove the displayed asymptotic linear expansion and Gaussian limit in the theorem.

It remains to prove consistency of the plug-in covariance.  On the event in (6), define
\[
 \widehat\psi_i
 =J_F(\widehat s)\operatorname{vech}(X_iX_i^{\mathsf T}-\widehat\Sigma),
 \qquad
 \psi_i=J_F(s)Y_i.
\]
For every entry of \(\psi_i\psi_i^{\mathsf T}\), Chebyshev's inequality and (3) with \(k=8\) give the uniform weak law
\[
 \frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N\psi_i\psi_i^{\mathsf T}-V_F
 =o_{\mathbb P}^{\mathcal C}(1).
\tag{13}
\]
The mean-value bound for \(J_F\) on the tube, (5), and the exact identity
\[
 \widehat\psi_i-\psi_i
 =\{J_F(\widehat s)-J_F(s)\}Y_i
  -J_F(\widehat s)(\widehat s-s)
\]
show that
\[
 \frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N\lVert\widehat\psi_i-\psi_i\rVert^2
 =o_{\mathbb P}^{\mathcal C}(1),
\tag{14}
\]
because \(N^{-1}\sum_i\lVert Y_i\rVert^2=O_{\mathbb P}^{\mathcal C}(1)\).  Applying Cauchy--Schwarz to
\(\widehat\psi_i\widehat\psi_i^{\mathsf T}-\psi_i\psi_i^{\mathsf T}\), and using (13)--(14), yields
\[
 \widehat V_F-V_F=o_{\mathbb P}^{\mathcal C}(1).
\tag{15}
\]
The complement of the tube has uniformly vanishing probability, so defining \(\widehat V_F=0\) at a pole does not alter (15).

The two exceptional-event assertions follow from the preceding bounds.  Equations (5)--(6) imply
\[
 \sup_{\Sigma\in\mathcal C}
 \Pr_{\Sigma}\!\left\{\min_{1\le j\le r}|Q_{F,j}(\widehat s)|=0\right\}
 \le
 \sup_{\Sigma\in\mathcal C}\Pr_{\Sigma}\{\lVert\widehat s-s\rVert>\eta\}
 \longrightarrow0.
\tag{16}
\]
For symmetric matrices, the variational characterization of the least eigenvalue directly gives
\[
 \lambda_{\min}(\widehat V_F)
 \ge\lambda_{\min}(V_F)-\lVert\widehat V_F-V_F\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}.
\]
Consequently, (15) and the chart bound \(\lambda_{\min}(V_F)\ge\nu_{\mathrm{chart}}\) imply
\[
 \sup_{\Sigma\in\mathcal C}\Pr_{\Sigma}\{\widehat V_F\text{ is singular}\}
 \le
 \sup_{\Sigma\in\mathcal C}
 \Pr_{\Sigma}\{\lVert\widehat V_F-V_F\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\ge\nu_{\mathrm{chart}}/2\}
 \longrightarrow0.
\tag{17}
\]

On the complement of the events in (16)--(17), (7), (11), and (15) imply
\[
 N\{\widehat\theta_G-F_G(s)\}^{\mathsf T}\widehat V_F^{-1}
 \{\widehat\theta_G-F_G(s)\}
 \rightsquigarrow\chi_r^2
\tag{18}
\]
uniformly over \(\mathcal C\).  Indeed, on the event
\(\lVert\widehat V_F-V_F\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}<\nu_{\mathrm{chart}}/2\), both inverse norms are uniformly bounded, and
\[
 \widehat V_F^{-1}-V_F^{-1}
 =\widehat V_F^{-1}(V_F-\widehat V_F)V_F^{-1}.
\]
Thus (15) makes the studentization error \(o_{\mathbb P}^{\mathcal C}(1)\), while (11) makes the leading quadratic form converge uniformly to the squared norm of a standard \(r\)-variate Gaussian vector.  The distribution function of \(\chi_r^2\) is continuous at \(\chi^2_{r,1-\gamma}\).  On either exceptional event, \(\mathrm{def:simultaneous\mbox{-}wald\mbox{-}set}\) sets the confidence set equal to \(\mathbb R^r\); the total probability of these events tends to zero uniformly by (16)--(17).  It follows from (18) that
\[
 \inf_{\Sigma\in\mathcal C}
 \Pr_{\Sigma}\{F_G(s)\in\mathcal W_{G,N}(1-\gamma)\}
 \longrightarrow1-\gamma.
\tag{19}
\]

Finally, the target of (7)--(19) is initially the observed-law functional \(F_G(s)\), not the structural vector by definition.  We therefore verify their relationship.  When the exact call in \(\mathrm{thm:total\mbox{-}stopping\mbox{-}schedule}\) returns ``yes'', its retained exact formula for the coordinate \(q\) represented by the \(j\)-th quotient has a certificate
\[
 qQ_{F,j}(s)-P_{F,j}(s)\in I_G.
\tag{20}
\]
By the definition \(I_G=\ker(\tau_G)\), applying \(\tau_G\) to (20) and evaluating at the model covariance gives
\[
 qQ_{F,j}(s)-P_{F,j}(s)=0.
\]
The population denominator is nonzero, because membership in \(\mathcal C\) gives
\(|Q_{F,j}(s)|\ge\delta_{\mathrm{chart}}>0\).  Division is therefore valid and yields
\[
 q=\frac{P_{F,j}(s)}{Q_{F,j}(s)}.
\tag{21}
\]
Applying (21) to all \(r=|\theta_G|\) returned coordinates proves
\(\theta_G=F_G(\operatorname{vech}\Sigma)\) on the successfully identified graph model and this regular chart.  Hence (19) supplies simultaneous Wald inference for the structural vector in precisely that regime.  The proof uses only first-order linearization and makes no efficiency assertion.
