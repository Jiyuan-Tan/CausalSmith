Put \(R=\mathbb Q[\theta_G]\).  By acyclicity, \((I_n-\Lambda)^{-1}\) is a finite path sum, so \(\phi_G\) is polynomial, \(\tau_G\) takes values in the integral domain \(R\), and all the spans below are finite dimensional.

Fix \(S\subseteq\theta_G\), \(q\in\theta_G\setminus S\), and \(d\geq1\).  The span definition implies
\[
 A_d(S)=\{\tau_G(b):b\in\mathbb Q[S,s],\ \deg b\leq d\},\qquad
 C_d(S)=\{\tau_G(a):a\in\mathbb Q[S,s],\ \deg a\leq d-1\}.
\]
Suppose first that \(0\neq h\in A_d(S)\cap qC_d(S)\).  There are \(a,b\) with the displayed degree bounds such that
\[
 h=\tau_G(b)=q\tau_G(a)=\tau_G(qa).
\]
Consequently \(qa-b\in\ker(\tau_G)=I_G\), \(\deg(qa-b)\leq d\), and \(\tau_G(a)\neq0\) because \(h=q\tau_G(a)\neq0\).  Thus \(qa-b\) is a degree-\(d\) recursive certificate.  Conversely, if \(qa-b\in I_G\) is such a certificate, then
\[
 0\neq q\tau_G(a)=\tau_G(b)\in A_d(S)\cap qC_d(S);
\]
nonvanishing follows from \(q\neq0\), \(\tau_G(a)\neq0\), and the fact that \(R\) is a domain.  Hence
\[
 q\text{ is certifiable after }S\text{ at degree }d
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 A_d(S)\cap qC_d(S)\neq\{0\}. \tag{1}
\]

If \(S\subseteq T\), then \(A_d(S)\subseteq A_d(T)\) and \(C_d(S)\subseteq C_d(T)\).  A witness to the right side of (1) for \(S\) is therefore also a witness for \(T\); availability is monotone.

Assume that an identifying order \(q_1,\ldots,q_p\), where \(p=|\theta_G|\), has certificates of degree at most \(d\).  Let \(T=\operatorname{Cl}_d(G)\), the fixed point produced by the simultaneous rounds.  Inductively suppose \(q_1,\ldots,q_{j-1}\in T\).  The certificate for \(q_j\) and (1) make \(q_j\) available over \(\{q_1,\ldots,q_{j-1}\}\); monotonicity makes it available over \(T\).  Since \(T\) is fixed, \(q_j\in T\).  Induction gives \(T=\theta_G\).

Conversely, suppose \(\operatorname{Cl}_d(G)=\theta_G\).  In the closure construction, order first all coordinates in \(S_1\setminus S_0\), then all coordinates in \(S_2\setminus S_1\), and so forth, with an arbitrary order inside a round.  Every coordinate in \(S_{t+1}\setminus S_t\) has, by (1), a degree-\(d\) certificate using only \(S_t\) and \(s\).  All of \(S_t\) precedes it in the resulting linear order, so these certificates form an identifying order of maximum degree at most \(d\).  This proves
\[
 ID_G\leq d\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\operatorname{Cl}_d(G)=\theta_G.
\]

Every strict round adds at least one coordinate and no coordinate is removed.  There are therefore at most \(|\theta_G|\) strict rounds.  Finally, if the fixed point is proper and a recursive order of degree at most \(d\) existed, the preceding induction would put every coordinate of that order in the fixed point, a contradiction.  Because \(\theta_G\) includes every structural coefficient and every permitted disturbance entry, this obstruction covers orders that place disturbance coordinates first.

Write \(m=n(n+1)/2\), \(\widehat s=\operatorname{vech}(\widehat\Sigma)\), and
\[

The linear model with centered Gaussian disturbances gives \(X_i\sim\mathcal N(0,\Sigma)\).  From the definition of the sample covariance,
\[
 \widehat s-s=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N Y_i-\operatorname{vech}(\bar X\bar X^{\mathsf T}). \tag{11}

Let \(\mathcal C_G\) abbreviate the nonempty chart in the statement.  The eigenvalue restrictions put it inside the compact set \(\{\Sigma:\kappa_{\mathrm{chart}}I_n\preceq\Sigma\preceq K_{\mathrm{chart}}I_n\}\).  The denominator and nondegeneracy restrictions are closed because polynomials, rational derivatives away from their poles, Gaussian fourth moments, and eigenvalues are continuous.  \sup_{\Sigma\in\mathcal C_G}\mathbb E_\Sigma\|X\|^k
 \leq K_{\mathrm{chart}}^{k/2}\mathbb E\|Z\|^k<\infty. \tag{12}

In particular, Chebyshev's inequality applied coordinatewise in (11) and to \(\bar X\) yields, uniformly on the chart,
\[
 \|\widehat s-s\|=O_{\mathbb P}(N^{-1/2}),\qquad
 \sqrt N\|\bar X\bar X^{\mathsf T}\|=O_{\mathbb P}(N^{-1/2}). \tag{13}

By compactness, continuity of the finitely many \(Q_{F,j}\), and the margin \(\min_j|Q_{F,j}(s)|\geq\delta_{\mathrm{chart}}\), there is an \(\eta>0\), independent of \(\Sigma\in\mathcal C_G\), such that

 \|t-s\|\leq\eta\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \min_j|Q_{F,j}(t)|\geq\delta_{\mathrm{chart}}/2. \tag{14}

On the compact tube in (14), all first and second derivatives of the fixed rational map \(F_G\) are bounded by a common finite constant.  Taylor's formula, (13), and (14) therefore give uniformly
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sqrt N\{F_G(\widehat s)-F_G(s)\}
 &=J_F(s)\sqrt N(\widehat s-s)+O_{\mathbb P}\!\left(\sqrt N\|\widehat s-s\|^2\right)\\
 &=\frac1{\sqrt N}\sum_{i=1}^N J_F(s)Y_i+o_{\mathbb P}(1)\\
 &=\frac1{\sqrt N}\sum_{i=1}^N\operatorname{IF}_F(X_i)+o_{\mathbb P}(1). \tag{15}
 \end{aligned}
\]
The complement of the tube has probability tending to zero uniformly by (13), so the pole convention does not alter (15).

We verify the asserted uniform Gaussian limit directly.  Put \(Z_{\Sigma}=V_F(\Sigma)^{-1/2}\operatorname{IF}_F(X)\).  The chart condition gives \(\|V_F^{-1/2}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\nu_{\mathrm{chart}}^{-1/2}\); boundedness of \(J_F\) and (12) give

 \mathbb E_\Sigma Z_\Sigma=0,qquad

For fixed \(t\in\mathbb R^r\), the inequality
\[
 \left|e^{ix}-1-ix+\frac{x^2}{2}\right|\leq\frac{|x|^3}{6}

and (16) imply, uniformly in \(\Sigma\),

 \mathbb E_\Sigma e^{it^{\mathsf T}Z_\Sigma/\sqrt N}
 =1-\frac{\|t\|^2}{2N}+O(N^{-3/2}).

Raising this characteristic function to the \(N\)-th power gives \(\exp(-\|t\|^2/2)+o(1)\), uniformly.  Tightness follows from the identity covariance in (16), so every parameter sequence has the standard normal limit.  Undoing the standardization proves the uniform interpretation of
\[
 N^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^N\operatorname{IF}_F(X_i)\rightsquigarrow\mathcal N(0,V_F). \tag{17}

On the tube, define the plug-in influence vectors by

 \widehat V_F\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}V_F \tag{18}

uniformly on the chart.

 \lambda_{\min}(\widehat V_F)\geq
 \lambda_{\min}(V_F)-\|\widehat V_F-V_F\|_{\mathrm{op}}
 \geq\nu_{\mathrm{chart}}/2>0.
\]
Equation (18) therefore proves both exceptional-event assertions.

 N\{\widehat\theta_G-F_G(s)\}^{\mathsf T}\widehat V_F^{-1}

uniformly.  The \(\chi_r^2\) distribution is continuous at \(\chi^2_{r,1-\gamma}\), while on either exceptional event the confidence set is \(\mathbb R^r\).  Since those events have uniformly vanishing probability, the infimal coverage converges to \(1-\gamma\).
