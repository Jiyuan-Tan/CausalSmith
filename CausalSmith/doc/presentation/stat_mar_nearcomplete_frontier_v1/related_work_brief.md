The paper belongs to the literature on population average treatment effects (ATEs), randomized experiments, and inference with missing outcomes. The potential-outcome formulation follows the experimental tradition of \citet{Neyman1990,Rubin1974} and its modern treatment in \citet{ImbensRubin2015}; missing at random (MAR) follows \citet{Rubin1976}, with systematic accounts in \citet{Little2019,Tsiatis2006}. Inverse-probability weighting traces to \citet{Horvitz1952}, while propensity-score identification and efficiency are developed by \citet{Rosenbaum1983,Hahn1998,Hirano2003}. Augmentation, double robustness, targeted estimation, and orthogonal estimation with cross-fitting provide the principal estimation lineage \citep{Robins1994,Bang2005,vanDerLaan2006,Chernozhukov2018}; \citet{Wooldridge2002} connects weighting to selection and attrition in econometric models. These foundations distinguish identification, regular efficiency, and uniform statistical difficulty. The present contribution characterizes the latter for a superpopulation ATE under balanced randomization and unrestricted finite-cell MAR. See the original [MAR formulation](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/63.3.581), [augmented estimation framework](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1994.10476818), and [orthogonal estimation framework](https://doi.org/10.1111/ectj.12097).

Surrogates enter through two established strands. \citet{Prentice1989} formalizes conditions for a surrogate to support treatment comparisons for the primary endpoint. Under surrogacy and comparability across experimental and observational samples, \citet{Athey2026} identifies the long-term ATE through the surrogate index; \citet{Chen2023} characterizes efficiency bounds for related data-combination models. A complementary strand uses surrogate measurements to recover information when primary outcomes are observed on a subset: \citet{Chen2003,Chen2008} develop validation-sample and auxiliary-data efficiency methods, and \citet{Cheng2021,Chakrabortty2024,Hou2025} develop semisupervised treatment-effect estimation. The closest identification and efficiency comparison is \citet[Lemma 1.1 under Assumptions 1--3; Theorem 2.1; Sections 3--4]{KallusMao2025}: they identify the original ATE, derive its efficient influence function and semiparametric variance bound, and construct estimators attaining that bound under their nuisance-convergence conditions. Their causal assumptions are stated through potential outcomes; the present finite-cell observed-data identification calculation supplies the paper's own target mapping, and its constant-surrogate randomized converse submodel supports the lower-bound comparison. Relevant sources are the [surrogate-index article](https://doi.org/10.1093/restud/rdaf087), [long-term efficiency analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.14405), [partially annotated treatment-and-response analysis](https://jmlr.org/papers/v26/23-1587.html), and [surrogate-assisted MAR article](https://doi.org/10.1093/jrsssb/qkae099).

Uniform complexity has a separate methodological history. \citet{Robins1997} demonstrates the importance of selection probabilities for uniform inference in high-dimensional missing-data experiments; higher-order influence functions and mixture comparisons subsequently yield minimax analyses under explicit regularity restrictions \citep{Robins2008,Robins2009,Robins2017}. Recent work characterizes optimality with access to black-box nuisance estimates \citep{Balakrishnan2026,Jin2025}, and combines such information with additional smoothness to obtain doubly robust asymptotic linearity \citep{Bonvini2024}; \citet{Kennedy2024} reviews this broader framework. For partially labeled causal data, \citet[Theorem 2.2, equation (2.4)]{ZhangChakraborttyBradic2025} establishes convergence at the effective-sample rate \((Na_N)^{-1/2}\), where \(N\) is the total sample size and \(a_N\) is their decaying joint labeling-propensity scale, together with standardized asymptotic normality under the theorem's nuisance-rate conditions. Their Theorems 4.1--4.2 establish bias-reduced inference under specified model-correctness, approximation-error, and sparsity conditions; Theorem 4.1 permits variance of order \(\|\alpha^*\|_2^2+\gamma_N^{-1}\), where these quantities describe regression signal and joint treatment-labeling probability. These results supply structured asymptotic benchmarks for the present unrestricted cellwise analysis. See the [high-dimensional uniform-inference foundation](https://doi.org/10.1002/(SICI)1097-0258(19970215)16:3%3C285::AID-SIM535%3E3.0.CO;2-%23), [recent black-box optimality result](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v291/jin25a.html), and [version-specific decaying-MAR theorems](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.12789v3).

The nearest dimension-dependent benchmark is \citet[Theorems 1--2 and Appendix C.5]{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024}, including their June 2026 revision. For complete records with binary treatment and outcome, \(d\) covariate categories, sample size \(n\), and treatment propensity bounded between a fixed \(\epsilon\) and \(1-\epsilon\), their general estimators have MSE bounded by a constant times \(n^{-1}+d^2/n^2\). Their minimax lower bound is \(n^{-1}+[d/(n\log n)]^2\) in the regime \(d\lesssim n\log n\), with constants depending on \(\epsilon\). Polynomial correction supplies a lineage for closing such logarithmic gaps: \citet[Sections III--IV]{JiaoVenkatHanWeissman2015} combines unbiased polynomial estimation in nonsmooth regions with corrected plug-in estimation in smooth regions, while \citet[Proposition 3 and Appendix B.2]{WuYang2016} supplies moment-matching and Poisson-mixture comparison tools; \citet{Wu2020} surveys the underlying approximation and interpolation methods. The working paper \citet{CausalSmith2026} reports a matching fixed-overlap discrete-ATE rate for \(n\ge N_\epsilon\) and \(d\le\rho_\epsilon n\log n\), and a dimension-free parametric endpoint at balanced randomization. In a separate independent annotation experiment, \citet{CausalSmith2026Annotation} reports risk \(\asymp_\epsilon\min\{1,n^{-1}+d^2/[(n+m)^2\log^2(en)]\}\) for \(n\) complete and \(m\) additional treatment-covariate records, uniformly for \(n\ge1,m\ge0,d\ge2\). These working-paper results provide the appropriate credit for fixed-overlap rates, hybrid polynomial architectures, and complete-arrival behavior. See the [discrete-covariate comparison](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.00118v3), [polynomial-method survey](https://doi.org/10.1561/0100000095), [fixed-overlap working paper](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22882661), and [annotation working paper](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22882683).

Honest interval length connects the contribution to a further established strand. Modulus-based estimation and expected-length bounds are developed by \citet{Donoho1994,Low1997} and \citet[Theorem 1 and Section 5]{CaiLow2004}; testing reductions follow the standard framework in \citet[Section 2.2]{Tsybakov2009}. \citet{Armstrong2020} constructs bias-aware honest intervals in nonparametric regression, and \citet{Armstrong2021} derives finite-sample optimal ATE procedures conditional on treatment and covariates under smoothness or shape restrictions and Gaussian errors with known variance, with asymptotic feasible extensions. For randomized trials with MAR outcomes, \citet[Theorem 2, Conditions 2--3]{DiazVanDerLaan2017} proves root-\(n\) asymptotic normality of a drift-corrected targeted estimator under Donsker and nuisance-limit rate conditions, with the required double-robust correctness condition. Against these benchmarks, the present paper establishes uniform all-procedure MSE of order \(n^{-1}+(1-q)^2\min\{1,[d/(n\log(e+n))]^2\}\) and fixed-coverage honest connected-interval worst-law expected length of its square root, for the known arrival floor \(q\in[1/2,1]\), binary surrogate and outcome, and at most \(d\) baseline cells. Its distinctive converse uses exactly normalized randomized-MAR priors and the complete observed record, preserving the factor \(1-q\) in target separation. The contribution is the joint arrival-and-dimension characterization, with identification, polynomial architecture, and the complete-arrival endpoint retaining their established credits. See the [expected-length theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0503662), [optimal ATE inference comparison](https://doi.org/10.3982/ECTA16907), and [missing-outcome trial inference theorem](https://arxiv.org/html/1704.01538).
