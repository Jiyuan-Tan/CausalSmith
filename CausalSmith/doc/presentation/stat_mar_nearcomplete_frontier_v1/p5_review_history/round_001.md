# Referee review

**Recommendation:** major_revision
**Overall score:** 6.9/10 — The verified joint arrival-and-dimension characterization is a credible theoretical contribution, but the manuscript needs substantial editorial restructuring and sharper quantitative positioning to meet a leading journal's presentation standard.

The paper establishes matching minimax squared-error and fixed-coverage honest expected-length orders for a population ATE under balanced Bernoulli randomization, binary measurements, unrestricted finite-cell MAR, and a known arrival floor between one half and one. Its distinctive technical contribution is the normalized complete-record prior comparison preserving the arrival-deficit factor. The principal claims closely follow the verified statements, while excessive repetition, cumbersome definitions, and several scope descriptions require revision.

## Strengths
- The bounds characterize sample size, baseline dimension, and the arrival floor jointly, with universal constants for squared risk and constants depending only on fixed noncoverage for interval length.
- The converse compares mixtures of the complete observed record and constructs admissible normalized causal laws.
- The identification target, sampling experiment, null-cell conventions, and uniform coverage criterion are stated explicitly.
- The dedicated early related-work section engages relevant results and preserves credit for identification, polynomial methods, and the complete-arrival endpoint.
- The limitations section candidly describes the exhibited tuning, interval constants, computational evaluation, and extension questions.

## Findings
- **[major·structure] Global exposition** — The statistical argument is obscured by a lengthy coordinate-by-coordinate setup, repeated explanations after formal objects, exhaustive forward-reference lists, and repeated elementary bookkeeping in the appendices. The central decomposition explaining the missing-membership correction appears most clearly after the long upper-risk proof. This organization makes the contribution substantially harder to assess than its mathematical content requires.
  - *Fix:* Organize the main exposition around the experiment, target, criteria, benchmark, and two headline comparisons; then explain the arrived-plus-missing-mass decomposition before detailing the estimator. Shorten the presentation-level cellwise and estimator-class definitions to their actual inputs, removing their lists of downstream assumptions and results. Consolidate repeated explanations and range/measurability arguments, and place artifact-specific supremum conventions in the verification discussion while retaining the verified conclusions and their conditions.
- **[minor·citation] Related work** — The closest working-paper comparisons remain qualitative: the fixed-overlap paper is described as attaining the 'logarithmically improved dimension rate' within its stated range, while the annotation paper's actual minimax rate is omitted. These descriptions make the incremental statistical contribution difficult to compare quantitatively despite the otherwise careful positioning.
  - *Fix:* State the fixed-overlap benchmark and its sample-size/alphabet-size regime explicitly. State the annotation benchmark min{1, n^{-1}+d²/[(n+m)² log²(en)]}, together with its fixed-overlap constant dependence and parameter range. A compact comparison table can show the experiment, known quantities, rate, and conditions alongside the present result. Preserve accurate source citations and locators.
- **[minor·prose] Introduction; Normalized priors and the dimension lower bound** — The narrative presents the paired-label and filler construction as the construction delivering the converse throughout its stated regime. For example, 'The dimension component of the lower bound rests on two priors' is immediately explained entirely through interpolation weights and paired labels. The supplied proof obtains the prescribed tolerance from that construction beyond a tolerance-dependent threshold and uses one-label point priors below that threshold.
  - *Fix:* State the two branches explicitly in the converse overview: paired-label priors deliver the large-sample comparison, and calibrated one-label point priors complete the all-sample existence result. Describe the theorem's priors existentially when discussing the full parameter range, while retaining the paired construction's distinctive role.
- **[minor·prose] Minimax estimation and honest inference** — The sentence 'It is therefore a theoretical procedure attaining the minimax order for fixed α, with no claim that the exhibited constants give informative intervals at ordinary sample sizes' violates the affirmative contribution framing contract outside the limitations section.
  - *Fix:* Replace the negative clause with the concrete delivered behavior: 'The construction attains the minimax expected-length order for fixed α; with the exhibited constant, its interval equals [-1,1] whenever log(e+n)<128.' Keep practical-development discussion in the limitations section. Likewise, express the polynomial threshold as an exhibited theoretical tuning choice without the contrast to numerical recommendations.
- **[minor·prose] Verification note** — The sentence 'The theorem-local verification disclosures retain the precise scope of each result, including any externally cited conclusions whose source proofs were not formalized' refers to theorem-local disclosures that are absent from the supplied manuscript. The current verification contract records empty external-dependency lists for every mapped object, so this boilerplate gives an unclear account of the actual verification boundary.
  - *Fix:* Replace this sentence with an accurate account of the current artifact: the mapped results are checked under their stated statistical hypotheses, the two identified presentation-level definitions have their disclosed status, and bibliographic comparisons are supported by the cited publications. Describe theorem-local published-input disclosures when applicable to an actual result.
- **[nit·prose] Setup and statistical criteria** — The first tuple use in 'The observed experiment retains (X,A,S,R,RY)' precedes the environments defining these coordinates and lacks the required first-use plain-word gloss.
  - *Fix:* Introduce the tuple with a short coordinate gloss: X is baseline category, A treatment, S the realized surrogate, R arrival status, and RY the observed outcome mark.
