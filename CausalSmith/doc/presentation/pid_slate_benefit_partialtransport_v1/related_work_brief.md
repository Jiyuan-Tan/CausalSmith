The paper sits at the intersection of potential-outcome causal inference, instrumental variables, principal stratification, sample selection, and partial identification. The potential-outcome and ignorability foundations come from \citet{Rubin1974}, \citet{Holland1986}, and \citet{RosenbaumRubin1983}, with structural causal notation and counterfactual interpretation systematized by \citet{Pearl2009}. The local-average-treatment-effect framework of \citet{ImbensAngrist1994} and \citet{AngristImbensRubin1996} supplies the binary instrument, compliance strata, exclusion, and monotonicity logic used to recover complier quantities from observed treatment receipt. \citet{BalkePearl1997} is the canonical sharp-bounds reference for imperfect compliance, while the broader partial-identification tradition of \citet{Manski1990,Manski1997,ManskiPepper2000,Manski2003} frames the identified set as a primitive target and motivates exact characterization rather than point-identifying restrictions.

Selection is handled through the principal-stratification lineage of \citet{FrangakisRubin2002} and the sample-selection lineage of \citet{Heckman1979} and \citet{Lee2009}. \citet{Lee2009} gives sharp trimming bounds for average treatment effects under monotone sample selection, and \citet{Semenova2025} extends this logic to covariate-specific selection directions with sharp generalized Lee bounds and support-function inference. \citet{KennedyHarrisKeele2019} introduce survivor-complier effects for settings with treatment-induced selection and noncompliance, establishing the principal-stratum target used here. \citet{ChenFlores2015} bound mean wage effects for always-employed Job Corps compliers under sample selection and noncompliance; \citet{BlancoFloresFloresLagunes2013} and \citet{ChenFloresFloresLagunes2018} provide additional Job Corps bounds for average and quantile treatment effects. \citet{DongHeiler2026} develop sharp bounds and inference for intensive-margin treatment effects in sample-selection models with treatment endogeneity under covariate-specific weak sample-selection monotonicity. The present paper uses the same structural direction idea to identify selected-complier outcome capacities and then couples those unequal capacities to characterize a same-unit ordinal benefit probability among survivor compliers.

The ordinal-benefit component connects to complete-marginal benefit bounds and transport. \citet{Makarov1982}, \citet{FrankNelsenSchweizer1987}, \citet{WilliamsonDowns1990}, \citet{FanPark2010}, and \citet{ZhangRichardson2024} study sharp distributional bounds for sums and treatment-effect distributions from fixed marginals; \citet{Villani2009} and \citet{CaffarelliMcCann2010} provide the optimal-transport and partial-transport background for mass-constrained couplings. For ordinal outcomes, \citet{AgrestiKateri2017}, \citet{HuangEtAl2017}, and \citet{FayEtAl2018} motivate probabilistic and rank-type benefit summaries. \citet{LuDingDasgupta2018} derive sharp bounds for causal estimands on ordinal outcomes; their Proposition 2, equation (6), gives the fixed-complete-marginal strict-benefit interval with lower endpoint \(\max\{0,\max_t(F_0(t)-F_1(t))\}\) and upper endpoint \(\min_t\{F_0(t-1)+1-F_1(t)\}\). The present threshold formulas recover that benchmark on the no-selection, equal-mass face, while the main construction treats unequal survivor-complier submeasures induced jointly by noncompliance and treatment-dependent selection.

Recent frontier work on probabilities of benefit and causation supplies the closest comparison class. \citet{TianPearl2000} derive identification and bounds for probabilities of causation. \citet{SachsEtAl2023} and \citet{DuarteEtAl2023} develop automated symbolic and discrete causal-bound algorithms. \citet{GabrielSachsJensen2024} give sharp symbolic nonparametric bounds for benefit, harm, and relative treatment effects with ordinal outcomes in observational and imperfect randomized studies; their Biometrika results include confounded-setting bounds and response-function IV bounds for finite ordinal levels. \citet{deAguasEtAl2025} define the probability of tiered benefit for ordered categories and thresholded continuous outcomes, show that strong monotonicity still leaves partial identification for \(K\ge3\), and provide sharp bounds with stabilized one-step inference. \citet{PossebomRiva2025} derive sharp probability-of-causation bounds for always-observed units under exogenous treatment and monotone sample selection, with refinements under monotone treatment response and stochastic dominance. \citet{ChenLi2026} point-identify principal generalized causal effects, including probabilistic-index and win-ratio estimands, under treatment unconfoundedness, monotonicity, and mean principal ignorability. This paper’s identified object is instead the survivor-complier same-unit strict-benefit probability under a conditional IV design, with endpoint-attaining full latent IV and selection laws.

The inferential results build on the standard asymptotic theory of \citet{vanDerVaart1998}, partial-identification inference in \citet{ImbensManski2004}, \citet{ChernozhukovHongTamer2007}, \citet{RomanoShaikh2010}, \citet{Tamer2010}, and \citet{Molinari2020}, and nonsmooth inference tools from \citet{ChernozhukovLeeRosen2013}, \citet{FangSantos2019}, and \citet{HongLi2018}. Computational and conditional-linear-program antecedents include \citet{Russell2021}, \citet{LevisEtAl2025}, \citet{BenMichael2025}, and \citet{KhandamiryanSemenova2026}. \citet{Noack2026} studies sensitivity of LATE estimates to monotonicity violations, and \citet{LeeLiu2024} develops Lee bounds for continuous and multivalued treatments. Relative to this inference lineage, the paper verifies a fixed-law Hadamard directional limit for the projected-capacity, threshold-cut, screened-support endpoint map and supplies a deterministic guard with uniform whole-interval containment under overlap and an aggregate survivor-mass lower bound; the face-aware multiplier construction is recorded as a diagnostic frontier for future calibrated inference.
