# Referee review

**Recommendation:** major_revision
**Overall score:** 6.2/10 — The verified sharp-identification and constructive transport results are substantial, but the manuscript needs materially clearer operational definitions, sharper competitor positioning, and repair of an inference open question that is presently indistinguishable from an already proved guarantee.

The paper characterizes sharp bounds on a survivor-complier ordinal benefit probability by reducing an IV model with treatment-induced selection to cellwise exact-mass partial transport. It delivers closed-form threshold endpoints, full-law endpoint witnesses, sparse algorithms, a fixed-law directional limit, and a conservative finite-sample guard. These are potentially publishable theoretical contributions, but the current exposition obscures implementability and novelty, while several inference and scope statements require correction.

## Strengths
- The sharp cellwise projection and aggregate identified interval are stated with precise structural conditions and are supported by endpoint-attaining full latent laws.
- The branch-free treatment of unequal capacity totals, zero-gap cells, and zero-survivor cells is conceptually useful and more general than a complete-marginal ordinal coupling calculation.
- The threshold-flow construction connects identification, computation, and sharpness, with verified sparse-support and operation-count guarantees.
- The paper distinguishes the fixed-law directional limit from the conservative uniform guard and places genuinely undeveloped calibrated inference in a dedicated limitations section.
- The three-level law provides a compatible observed and latent example with an exactly computed nondegenerate interval.

## Findings
- **[major·citation] Related work** — The novelty comparison with the closest papers is too cursory. In particular, GabrielSachsJensen2024 is described only as using restrictions that “differ from the survivor-complier IV structure,” despite its finite-ordinal response-function IV bounds, while PossebomRiva2025 is omitted and DongHeiler2026 is not compared in terms of target, identified inputs, sharpness construction, and inference. The reader therefore cannot determine which part of the result is new relative to existing IV, selection, and ordinal-benefit bounds.
  - *Fix:* Add a focused comparison, preferably a table, covering target stratum, instrument and selection assumptions, observed identifying inputs, equal versus unequal marginal mass, sharpness witnesses, and inference for GabrielSachsJensen2024, DongHeiler2026, PossebomRiva2025, deAguasEtAl2025, and LuDingDasgupta2018. State precisely that the new object is the exact-mass coupling of selected-complier submeasures under the conditional IV-selection model.
- **[major·statement] Setup and assumptions** — The central observable inputs are not operationally defined where introduced. The definition says that ℓ_i(x) and h_j(x) are “obtained from the corresponding conditional instrument contrasts,” but the actual contrasts appear only deep in the proof. As written, a reader cannot construct the identified set from an observed law using the main text alone.
  - *Fix:* Display the two conditional-probability contrast formulas explicitly in the observable-capacities definition, including the conditioning events, treatment values, selected-outcome cells, and zero-denominator convention. Then derive q_0, q_1, Δq, and m from those displayed quantities.
- **[major·statement] Computation and witness** — The threshold-flow algorithm is underspecified at exactly the point needed for endpoint attainment. The directions “construct a maximum subflow” and “allocate the residual mass on the complete bipartite graph” do not state the ordering, pointer updates, or residual-graph invariant ensuring that completion adds no unwanted benefit or nonbenefit mass. The section also promises that a numerical endpoint is accompanied by a witness allocation, but it never displays the lower- and upper-attaining coupling matrices or traces for the three-level example.
  - *Fix:* Give executable pseudocode for both ordered greedy passes and residual completion, state the invariant showing that residual edges lie in the complementary graph, and display explicit 3-by-3 lower- and upper-attaining couplings for the numerical witness. Include enough detail to reproduce the operation count and endpoint values without consulting the proof.
- **[major·statement] Limitations and future work** — The displayed “Uniform face multiplier calibration” question asks for liminf uniform coverage of at least 1−α, including changing survivor support and threshold ties. The preceding deterministic-guard theorem already proves a stronger every-sample uniform containment statement under weaker conditions. Although the surrounding prose gestures toward first-order calibration, the displayed target neither defines a distinct multiplier-calibrated interval nor requires exactness, reduced conservatism, or another property that remains open.
  - *Fix:* Define a separate multiplier-calibrated candidate interval and state the unresolved objective precisely—for example asymptotically exact coverage, a first-order critical value, or a demonstrably smaller radius than the deterministic guard. Explicitly distinguish this target from the already established conservative interval and avoid reusing the same confidence-set notation for both.
- **[major·structure] Estimation and guarded inference** — The purported confidence-set algorithm includes the oracle quantity δ_n=max_E|P̂_n(E)−P_obs(E)|, while its event collection is initially defined circularly as “all guard-event indices.” The final interval does not use δ_n, so an unobservable proof device is mixed into the operational procedure and the cardinality entering b_{n,α} is not readily computable from the first definition.
  - *Fix:* Enumerate the guard events explicitly before defining the interval, give the resulting formula for their cardinality, and move δ_n to the proof as an analytical device. Present the implementable inputs to the reported interval separately from the multiplier diagnostics and concentration argument.
- **[major·structure] global** — The reader-facing argument is burdened by formalization scaffolding: individual potential variables receive separate definition environments, several aggregate quantities are defined more than once, and implementation-specific cost and empirical-process objects interrupt the economic argument. This makes the novel identification logic difficult to locate despite the correctness of the formal statements.
  - *Fix:* Consolidate the potential-outcome notation into one setup definition, retain only the assumptions and objects needed for the main results in the body, and move duplicate aggregate definitions, cost-model bookkeeping, and formal support objects to a technical appendix. Add a short roadmap from observed cell probabilities to capacities, transport cuts, aggregate endpoints, and inference.
- **[minor·prose] abstract** — The sentence “a deterministic guarded confidence interval contains the full sharp identified interval uniformly under fixed instrument overlap and a uniform positive lower bound on aggregate survivor-complier mass” omits other theorem conditions, notably i.i.d. sampling, fixed finite support, the structural law class, and the stated screening sequence.
  - *Fix:* Qualify the abstract claim as applying to i.i.d. samples from the fixed finite-slate structural class with the prescribed screening rate, common overlap bound, and aggregate-mass lower bound.
- **[minor·statement] Estimation and guarded inference** — The uniform law class is written as a set of latent laws P that “satisfies” an independent-sampling condition, although sampling is a property of an experiment comprising a sampling law and observation sequence. This becomes especially unclear when the uniform theorem later introduces λ-indexed systems, sampling laws, observations, and multipliers separately.
  - *Fix:* Define the uniform class over complete statistical experiments or indexed tuples containing the structural system, observed law, sampling law, and observation sequence. State which support, K, overlap constant, and mass bound are common across the family.
- **[minor·statement] Limitations and future work** — The direction-margin environment imposes only |Δq(x)|≥κ_σ, yet the following prose calls it a “strong sign-separation condition” that stays away from zero. Without κ_σ>0, the displayed condition can be vacuous.
  - *Fix:* Add κ_σ>0 to the formal assumption and propagate that condition to the associated open-question declaration, or weaken the surrounding prose so it does not claim positive sign separation.
- **[minor·statement] Setup and assumptions** — The slate-benefit system definition writes the observed element as O=(X,Z,D,S,SY), whereas the observed-law definition uses an explicit missing-value variable Ỹ. The product SY cannot distinguish an unselected record from a selected zero outcome on a support containing zero.
  - *Fix:* Use O=(X,Z,D,S,Ỹ) consistently and define Ỹ with its missing marker in the consolidated system definition.
- **[minor·prose] Related work** — The sentence describing capacities “where the relevant marginal masses need not be complete probability distributions” uses contribution-by-negation outside the limitations section. Similar contrastive wording appears in the claim that the formulas describe latent laws “rather than only cellwise transport arrays.”
  - *Fix:* Use affirmative descriptions such as “the relevant margins are selected-complier subprobability capacities with potentially unequal totals” and “the formulas are realized by feasible full latent laws.”
- **[minor·structure] Appendix** — The sequence of an unnumbered “Appendices” heading, a numbered section titled “Appendix,” and a later separate “Proofs of the main results” section creates redundant and uninformative hierarchy.
  - *Fix:* Remove the generic duplicate headings and assign descriptive appendix section titles, with the proof appendix integrated into the same hierarchy.

## Questions for authors
- Is the covariate-specific direction d(x) intended to be substantively prespecified, or is the maintained model the union over direction maps, with the observed capacity gap selecting the direction whenever it is nonzero?
- Which precise property should distinguish the proposed multiplier-calibrated interval from the already proved deterministic guard: asymptotically exact coverage, shorter radius, local optimality, or valid critical-value estimation?
- Can the authors provide an explicit comparison showing when their exact-mass survivor-complier interval is tighter, wider, or simply differently targeted than the finite-ordinal response-function IV bounds in GabrielSachsJensen2024?
