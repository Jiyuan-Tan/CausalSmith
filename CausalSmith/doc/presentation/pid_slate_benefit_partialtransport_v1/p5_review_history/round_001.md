# Referee review

**Recommendation:** major_revision
**Overall score:** 6.2/10 — The paper delivers a credible and potentially valuable sharp-identification result, but central definitions, verification disclosures, competitor comparisons, algorithmic reproducibility, and the practical informativeness of the inference procedure require substantial revision.

The paper characterizes sharp bounds for a survivor-complier ordinal benefit probability by reducing the IV-selection model to cellwise exact-mass partial transport, derives threshold formulas, constructs endpoint-attaining latent laws, and supplies sparse computation and guarded inference. The verified results support the core mathematical claims, including zero-gap and zero-mass cells without a direction margin. Publication requires a more self-contained observable-capacity definition, accurate verification-scope disclosure, result-level positioning, reproducible algorithms, and evidence that the guarded interval is informative at relevant sample sizes.

## Strengths
- The sharp cellwise projection and full-law endpoint-attainment results establish a genuine identified-set characterization rather than marginal optimization detached from the latent IV model.
- The branch-free formulation handles positive-survivor zero-gap cells, zero-mass cells, and simultaneous active threshold cuts under the stated assumptions.
- The threshold formulas and sparse-flow theorem provide an appealing computational structure, including support bounds for extremal couplings.
- The fixed-law directional limit and finite-sample whole-interval containment theorem have clearly distinguished scopes.
- The limitations section accurately presents calibrated face-aware multiplier inference, continuous outcomes, sensitivity analysis, and concordance restrictions as future directions.

## Findings
- **[major·prose] abstract and Technical derivations and verification note** — The title-page disclosure states that “the displayed formal statements and proofs are Lean-verified” and that the appendix records “the exact scope, toolchain, and commit.” The appendix supplies neither the commit nor a version-pinned toolchain, and the verification contract classifies several displayed definitions as presentation-synthesized rather than matched Lean declarations. The current wording therefore overstates statement-level verification and reproducibility.
  - *Fix:* Replace the blanket claim with a precise distinction between matched Lean declarations and synthesized expository definitions. Add the verified commit 8f179a26a19156d89598be0173baaccea8453549, exact Lean and dependency versions, build commands, artifact paths, and a compact statement-to-declaration table; retain the theorem-local disclosure for the Lu–Ding–Dasgupta input.
- **[major·statement] Setup and assumptions** — The central definition says that ℓ_i(x) and h_j(x) are “obtained from the corresponding conditional instrument contrasts” without displaying those contrasts. Because every identification, computation, and inference result depends on these quantities, a reader cannot reconstruct the estimand map from the formal definition itself.
  - *Fix:* Display the exact two conditional-probability differences defining each capacity, specify the missing-outcome convention and the zero-denominator convention, and state explicitly how unsupported covariate cells are handled. Use these formulas consistently in the population and empirical definitions.
- **[major·other] Estimation and guarded inference** — The displayed constants suggest that the finite-sample guard may be practically vacuous. For example, with K=3, |X|=1, ε_Z=0.25, α=0.05, and m_star=0.25, the formula gives an uncapped guard of approximately 425,700/sqrt(n), even before adding the screening term; the reported interval is therefore [0,1] until sample sizes on the order of 10^11. The theorem remains valid, but the manuscript has not established the inferential procedure's usefulness.
  - *Fix:* Add numerical guard calculations and Monte Carlo evidence covering active-cut ties, zero capacity gaps, small survivor cells, and varying overlap. Report coverage, width, cap-at-one frequency, screening behavior, and comparison with the unguarded plug-in interval. If realistic designs produce nearly universal [0,1] intervals, either develop a sharper valid guard or position the result explicitly as a theoretical finite-sample certificate.
- **[major·citation] Related work** — The dedicated section is broad but relies mainly on citation clusters. It does not establish result-level distinctions from the closest work on survivor-complier effects, selected-complier bounds, response-function IV bounds for ordinal benefit, and always-observed probability-of-causation bounds. Possebom and Riva, identified in the supplied literature context as a close selection-and-benefit comparison, are absent.
  - *Fix:* Add a result-level comparison, preferably a table, covering target stratum, assignment mechanism, selection restriction, observed inputs, equal versus unequal marginal mass, sharpness construction, and inference guarantee for Kennedy–Harris–Keele, Chen–Flores, Dong–Heiler, Gabriel–Sachs–Jensen, Possebom–Riva, de Aguas et al., and Lu–Ding–Dasgupta. Cite exact theorem, proposition, equation, or section locations supporting each comparison.
- **[major·other] Computation and witness** — The reader-facing algorithm says to “construct a maximum subflow” greedily but does not provide complete pseudocode specifying traversal order, pointer updates, tie handling, residual completion, or output encoding. The long proof contains fragments of these details, yet the advertised O(|X|K) routine and sparse witnesses are difficult to reproduce independently.
  - *Fix:* Provide standalone pseudocode for both endpoint passes and residual completion, release an executable implementation with tests against generic linear-program solutions, and display the lower and upper sparse allocation traces or coupling matrices for the three-level witness.
- **[major·structure] global** — The exposition introduces multiple redundant or forward-referencing environments. In particular, the “Slate-benefit partial-transport system” uses ℓ, h, m, M, and Γ_x^* before their formal definitions; “Threshold-cut capacity aggregates” repeats quantities already defined; and several elementary objects receive separate formal environments that interrupt the identification argument. This obscures the paper's substantive theorem sequence.
  - *Fix:* Reorganize the main text into a compact setup ordered as observed data, assumptions, explicit capacity contrasts, target, transport set, and endpoint theorem. Remove duplicate synthesized environments, move implementation bookkeeping and elementary homogeneity facts to the appendix, and retain only definitions needed to state the main results.
- **[major·structure] Estimation and guarded inference** — The “Guarded confidence set” construction mixes three distinct objects: an implementable deterministic interval, the unobservable analytical deviation δ_n involving P_obs, and an optional multiplier diagnostic. The reported interval depends on b_{n,α} and g_{n,α}, while δ_n enters only the proof and the multiplier envelope supplies no calibration to the confidence set. Presenting all three as algorithm steps makes the operational procedure unclear.
  - *Fix:* State the implementable confidence interval as a self-contained algorithm using observable inputs and deterministic constants. Move δ_n to a concentration lemma in the proof appendix, and place the near-active-face multiplier diagnostic in a separate optional diagnostic subsection or the future-work section with an explicit statement that it does not determine the reported interval.
- **[minor·statement] Setup and assumptions** — The “Slate-benefit partial-transport system” writes the observed element as O=(X,Z,D,S,SY), whereas the formal observed-data definition uses (X,Z,D,S,tilde Y) with a missing-value symbol off selection. With outcome level zero in the support, SY can conflate a genuine zero outcome with an unobserved outcome.
  - *Fix:* Use the missing-value representation (X,Z,D,S,tilde Y) throughout and reserve products such as SY only for contexts where their coding is explicitly defined and lossless.
- **[minor·statement] Limitations and future work** — The direction-margin assumption states |Δq(x)|≥κ_sigma but never states κ_sigma>0, while the surrounding prose calls it a “strong sign-separation condition” that keeps the gap away from zero. For κ_sigma≤0 the displayed assumption supplies no such separation.
  - *Fix:* Add κ_sigma>0 to the formal assumption and its supporting declaration, or revise the prose so that it does not describe the current condition as separation from zero.
- **[minor·statement] Estimation and guarded inference** — The empirical event-frequency definition displays 1/n for an unrestricted sample size n, although the empirical cell-probability definition separately totalizes n=0. Under ordinary mathematical conventions the former expression is undefined at n=0.
  - *Fix:* Restrict the event-frequency and multiplier definitions to n≥1 or add the same explicit n=0 branch used for the empirical cell probability; standardize sample indexing between O_0,… and O_1,… throughout.

## Questions for authors
- For empirically plausible values of K, |X|, overlap, m_star, and n, how often is the deterministic guard strictly below one?
- Will the public artifact include executable threshold-flow code and a version-pinned command that reproduces the witness, theorem checks, and operation-count results?
- In applications, is the covariate-specific selection direction treated as substantively prespecified, inferred from the capacity-gap sign when nonzero, or eliminated entirely by the branch-free representation?
