The paper belongs to the literature on estimating population average treatment effects under consistency, conditional treatment exchangeability, and overlap. Its experiment combines \(n\), the number of complete records, with \(m\), the number of auxiliary treatment–covariate records, over \(d\), the covariate alphabet size, with a public overlap floor \(\epsilon\). The potential-outcome formulation builds on \citep{SplawaNeyman1990,Rubin1974}, while propensity-score adjustment follows \citep{Rosenbaum1983}; standard treatments appear in \citep{Imbens2004,Imbens2015,Hernan2020}. Inverse probability weighting has its sampling-theoretic antecedent in \citep{Horvitz1952}. Semiparametric efficiency, augmentation, and double robustness connect the problem to \citep{Hahn1998,Hirano2003,Robins1994,Bang2005}. Cross-fitting and orthogonal scores extend this lineage to estimated nuisance functions \citep{Chernozhukov2018,Kennedy2022}. The present contribution characterizes finite-sample minimax precision over unrestricted cellwise outcome means and unknown covariate masses and propensities, jointly across the overlap floor and both information budgets. The foundational adjustment and modern estimation frameworks are documented in [Rosenbaum and Rubin](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/70.1.41), [Hirano et al.](https://doi.org/10.1111/1468-0262.00442), and [Chernozhukov et al.](https://doi.org/10.1111/ectj.12097).

Weak overlap has long motivated separate analyses of identification, precision, and inference. \citet{Khan2010} connect irregular identification in inverse-weighted models to relative support and tail conditions. \citet{Crump2009} study precision through overlap-based population selection, and \citet{DAmour2021} characterize restrictions that strict overlap imposes on high-dimensional covariate imbalance. Rothe's analysis explains how small treatment–covariate counts undermine conventional interval approximations and develops robust alternatives \citep[Sections 3--4 of the 2015 discussion-paper version]{Rothe2017LimitedOverlap}. \citet{ArmstrongKolesar2021ATEInference} obtain finite-sample optimal estimators and intervals conditional on the realized design under outcome-regression smoothness or shape restrictions, with exact optimality for Gaussian errors of known variance. \citet{MaWang2020RobustIPW} analyze Gaussian and non-Gaussian IPW limits and correct trimming bias; \citet{Dorn2025} develops thresholded doubly robust Wald inference under weak-overlap and nuisance-estimation conditions. These strands place the paper's uniform squared-error frontier within the broader study of precision as treatment arms become rare. Relevant primary accounts are [Khan and Tamer](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.3982/ECTA7372), [Rothe's preprint](https://docs.iza.org/dp8758.pdf), [Armstrong and Kolesár](https://www.princeton.edu/~mkolesar/papers/cate.pdf), [Ma and Wang](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.11397), and [Dorn](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13273).

The polynomial construction follows the literature on estimating nonlinear functionals of discrete distributions. \citet{Paninski2003} connects functional-estimation bias to polynomial approximation; \citet{Valiant2011} establishes sublinear sample complexity for entropy and support-size estimation. \citet{JiaoVenkatHanWeissman2015Functionals} combines unbiased polynomial estimation in nonsmooth regions with corrected estimation in smooth regions, while \citet{WuYang2016Entropy} links approximation-based upper bounds to moment-matching lower bounds. Higher-order functional estimation provides a complementary lineage \citep{Robins2008}; structure-agnostic optimality and hybrid smoothness models clarify the role of available nuisance information \citep{Balakrishnan2023,Jin2025a,Jin2025b,Bonvini2024}. The closest reciprocal-polynomial antecedent is \citet[Section 3.3, Theorems 5--6]{MaZhuJiaoWainwright2022OPE}. For \(k\) bandit actions, supplied target weights, bounded rewards with upper bound \(r_{\max}\), and a global behavior-probability floor \(\nu\), their Poisson-model Chebyshev upper bound adds \(r_{\max}^2\exp\{-c\sqrt{n\nu\log k}\}\) to the worst-case known-behavior oracle risk, assuming \(\sum_a\pi_t(a)^2\le k^{-\gamma}\) for some \(\gamma>0\). Their fixed-size lower bound adds \(r_{\max}^2\exp\{-200\sqrt{n\nu\log k}\}\) when \(c/(n\log k)\le\nu<(\log k)/n\) and \(\nu\le1/(2k)\). This supplies a precise methodological comparison: the present ATE experiment estimates arbitrary unknown population weights under cell-conditional overlap and unequal observation channels. Primary sources include [Jiao et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.6956), [Wu and Yang](https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.0381), [Ma et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2101.07781), and the [recent structure-agnostic extension](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.17341).

The nearest unrestricted discrete-ATE analysis is \citet[model (3), Theorems 1--2]{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2026Discrete}. For one potential-outcome mean, their plug-in MSE is bounded by \((1-\epsilon)^2d^2/(\epsilon^2n^2)+C/(\epsilon n)\); the signed ATE inherits the same order. For fixed overlap and \(d\lesssim n\log n\), their minimax lower bound is \(n^{-1}+d^2/(n^2\log^2 n)\), with floor-dependent constants. The credited fixed-interior comparator establishes the matching supervised rate for sufficiently large \(n\) and \(d\le\rho_\epsilon n\log n\) \citep{InternalDiscreteATEPolynomialUpperMatch}; the fresh search identifies its public working-paper version. The annotation parent establishes \(\min\{1,n^{-1}+d^2/[(n+m)^2\log^2(\exp(1)n)]\}\) with fixed-floor comparison constants \citep[Theorem 4]{CausalSmith2026Annotation}. Against these benchmarks, the present contribution establishes universal-constant comparison with \(\min\{1,(n\epsilon)^{-1}+[d/((n+m)\epsilon\log(\exp(1)+n\epsilon))]^2\}\), together with original-record attainment, a normalized same-class lower bound, and exact resource phases. Separate internal comparators concern optimal policy value under vanishing overlap and ATE estimation near randomized assignment \citep{InternalVanishingOverlapOptimalValue,InternalNearRandomATEFrontier}. The direct comparisons are anchored by [Zeng et al.'s June 2026 revision](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.00118v3), the [fixed-interior working paper](https://causalsmith.org/papers/stat_discrete_ate_minimax_loggap_polynomial_upper_match/), and the [annotation parent](https://causalsmith.org/papers/stat_semisupervised_discrete_ate_annotation_frontier_v1/).

Auxiliary-data methods provide the paper's annotation context. General semi-supervised inference develops gains from additional covariates \citep{Chakrabortty2018,Zhang2019}. For treatment effects, \citet[Theorem 2.1 and Corollary 2.1]{ChakraborttyDai2024Semisupervised} derives asymptotic expansions under overlap, moment, and nuisance-error conditions; with correctly specified nuisances, vanishing nuisance errors, and an \(o(n^{-1/2})\) product remainder, these expansions yield label-scale asymptotic normality. Decaying-label analyses accommodate selection bias and diminishing labeling probabilities \citep[Theorems 3.2 and 3.7]{ZhangChakraborttyBradic2023DecayingOverlap}, with later model-robust causal developments in \citep{Zhang2025}. Predictive features, surrogate outcomes, and partial treatment annotation motivate related efficiency results \citep{Cheng2021,Kallus2025,Hou2025}; \citet{Kato2025} studies efficiency with auxiliary covariates in one- and two-sample settings. The immediate consumer is \citet[Theorems 4.2 and 5.1 of the arXiv manuscript]{NwankwoGoldkindZhou2026Annotations}: they optimize feasible batch annotation probabilities through asymptotic variance and establish asymptotic normality under their adaptive design and nuisance conditions. Their allocation theorem receives direct credit. The present passive binary finite-alphabet benchmark complements that work by quantifying the marginal-information gains and rare-outcome-label cost relevant to prospective annotation planning. Primary accounts are [Chakrabortty and Dai](https://arxiv.org/html/2201.00468v3), [decaying-label inference](https://academic.oup.com/imaiai/article/12/3/2066/7225146), [surrogate efficiency](https://academic.oup.com/jrsssb/article/87/2/480/7829031), [Kato's revised manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08303), and [Nwankwo et al.'s manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.10605) and [conference publication](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v300/nwankwo26a.html).
