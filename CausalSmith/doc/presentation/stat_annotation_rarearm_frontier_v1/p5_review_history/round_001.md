# Referee review

**Recommendation:** major_revision
**Overall score:** 7.2/10 — The verified uniform frontier is a substantial theoretical contribution with largely faithful claims and fair positioning, but the manuscript needs substantial consolidation and clearer explanation of its statistical mechanism.

The paper characterizes minimax squared-error risk for a population-weighted treatment contrast using independent complete and auxiliary records, uniformly across sample budgets, alphabet size, and the overlap floor. Its principal advance is the overlap-uniform comparison, supported by original-record attainment, matching lower bounds, exact resource criteria, and a fixed-parameter exact-marginal limit. The central claims faithfully represent the supplied verification contract, while publication readiness requires a more focused structure and stronger conceptual exposition.

## Strengths
- The numerical comparison constants are uniform over all four experiment indices, making diminishing overlap an explicit part of the precision characterization.
- The results distinguish complete-record outcome information from marginal information supplied by both sampling channels.
- The lower bound covers measurable randomized rules, and the attaining estimator is defined on the original fixed-size records.
- The exact-marginal convergence statement preserves its fixed-n, fixed-d, fixed-overlap quantification.
- The related-work section engages the closest supervised and auxiliary-record results through their actual rates and conditions.
- The causal interpretation preserves consistency and conditional exchangeability, and the limitations section candidly explains the proof-oriented tuning.

## Findings
- **[major·structure] global** — The manuscript repeatedly restates the same risk decomposition, benchmark interpretation, and resource criteria across the introduction, main results, discussion, and appendices. Elementary definitions and extensive presentations of standard tools further obscure the new statistical construction. The complete attaining algorithm also precedes the main theorem, while the subsequent estimator section begins with the stale transition, "We first define the clipping operation used by every prefix estimate," although clipping and the estimator have already been defined.
  - *Fix:* Organize the main text around the experiment, frontier, conceptual estimator explanation, and resource consequences. Place the frontier before the detailed algorithm, consolidate repeated benchmark and phase discussions, and collect the full construction and technical details in clearly referenced appendices. Replace stale transitions with references to the existing definitions and shorten standard-tool exposition while retaining precise statements and citations.
- **[minor·prose] Minimax precision with two information budgets; An attaining estimator** — The text names the two sources of difficulty but gives limited intuition for the distinctive dependence on overlap and for why the logarithmic scale uses rare-arm outcome information rather than the total record budget. Readers encounter the full tuning machinery before receiving a concise account of the weighting correction and approximation tradeoff.
  - *Fix:* Add a short conceptual explanation connecting success probabilities to population-weighted arm means, then explain how sparse arm masses generate reciprocal correction and how the existing degree calibration balances approximation error against outcome noise. Explicitly connect that explanation to the overlap factor and the outcome-based logarithm, preserving the stated calibration conditions.
- **[minor·prose] An attaining estimator** — The sentence "the attaining construction divides the many-cell term by \(\ell^2\) ... while retaining the same rare-arm outcome term" can suggest an established comparison between the risks of the two estimators. The displayed results compare upper guarantees; the baseline theorem supplies an upper bound.
  - *Fix:* Write that the attaining estimator's upper guarantee replaces the baseline guarantee's many-cell term by a term smaller by the squared logarithmic factor, up to numerical constants, while preserving the inverse rare-arm information term. Keep the comparison explicitly about the displayed worst-case guarantees.
- **[minor·prose] Consistency and the value of auxiliary records** — The resource criteria are accurately stated, but their implications remain mostly symbolic and repetitive. The two qualitative cases for strict improvement provide less guidance than concrete experiment sequences would, particularly when overlap diminishes and the supervised risk already vanishes.
  - *Fix:* Add a compact set of worked sequence examples using the existing criteria: supervised rare-label order, auxiliary improvement between two vanishing risks, and auxiliary expansion from saturation to consistency. Specify the complete-record, auxiliary, alphabet, and overlap sequences and identify the relevant conditions. Present these as illustrations of order characterizations.
- **[minor·citation] Related work; Experiment, target, and causal interpretation** — Several citations supporting specific technical comparisons lack locators. The fixed-interior result is introduced through a raw theorem identifier in ordinary prose, and the semi-supervised and decaying-label comparisons omit the theorem locators supplied in the related-work context. Standard causal identification and the exact-marginal inverse-weighting construction also receive limited foundational attribution.
  - *Fix:* Put the fixed-interior theorem locator in the external citation's optional argument and identify its public working paper in readable prose. Add the relevant theorem and corollary locators for the semi-supervised and decaying-label comparisons. Add concise foundational attribution for propensity adjustment, causal identification, and inverse probability weighting at their substantive uses.
- **[nit·prose] Consistency and the value of auxiliary records** — Allowed experiment sequences are indexed from zero, while the resource-phase definition subsequently describes them using indices beginning at one. The asymptotic conclusions are unaffected, but the notation unnecessarily changes the stated sequence domain.
  - *Fix:* Use zero-based indexing consistently in the sequence and phase definitions and their surrounding prose.

## Questions for authors
- Which concrete econometric sampling settings best motivate independent auxiliary treatment–covariate records from the same population, and which resource regimes are most relevant in those settings?
