## escalate — out of causalsmith scope (bank/causalsmith)
- [major·statement·source_change] (Discussion, limitations, and open questions) The sharp-length question is internally incompatible with the object it proposes to calibrate. The manuscript explicitly explains that the radius is unweighted, omits retained-mean and atom-mass uncertainty, and has its natural local-regression scale h_n^β at zero threshold; nevertheless the remark requires its length and 2W_{n,B_n} to match r_n for every regime, including δ_n=0 through the q=0 case. At zero and regular/critical thresholds, the missing atom weight generally makes this component radius much larger than the complete clamp frontier, so the displayed matching limits cannot describe the defined interval. The requirement of one constant c across all phase limits is also unsupported and generally implausible when the relative regular and local contributions vary with the phase limit.
- [major·other·simulation] (Discussion, limitations, and open questions) The paper proposes a nonstandard split-sample estimator, total-Gram stabilization rule, fallback branch, and honest interval, yet supplies only a one-cell exponent calculation. The manuscript itself identifies fallback frequency, realized coverage, and expected length as potentially sensitive to constants and moderate sample size. Without numerical evidence, readers cannot assess whether the proposed procedure is usable in the regimes for which it is advocated.
- [minor·statement·rewrite] (Main statistical results) The displayed minimax theorem writes the estimator risk as an expectation E_P even though the estimator is a function of an n-observation sample and the verified criterion uses the product experiment. As written, the measure on that expectation has the wrong sample space.
- [minor·statement·rewrite] (Main statistical results) The zero-threshold bullet says stabilized risk and length are “both asymptotic to n^{-1/2},” while the verified conclusion is order comparability (AsympSeq/\asymp), not ratio convergence with constant one. Ordinary mathematical usage makes “asymptotic to” stronger than the delivered result.
