\section{Appendix: proofs and verification note}
\label{sec:appendix-proofs-verification}

This appendix collects the auxiliary statements that support the main risk, honest-length, causal-bridge, and continuity-only conclusions. The statements are organized by mathematical role: the clamp pushforward and support facts, the bandwidth and local-design algebra, the stabilized estimator and interval bounds, the phase calculations, the population moment identities underlying total-Gram stabilization, and the uniqueness statement for the continuity-only regression extension.

The first two propositions record the measure-theoretic behavior of the exact lower-threshold clamp. They connect the deterministic policy in \cref{obj:def:clamp-policy} to the mixed law that appears in the observed clamp functional in \cref{obj:def:clamp-functional}.

\begin{propositionv}{prop:pushforward-setup}[Clamp pushforward decomposition]
Let \(J\) be a positive integer, let \(P\in\mathcal M\) satisfy \cref{obj:def:model-class}, and let \(d_\delta(a)=\max\{a,\delta\}\) as in \cref{obj:def:clamp-policy}. Suppose the constants satisfy:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Smoothness and thinning.)} \(\beta>0\), \(\kappa\geq 0\), \(L>0\), and \(0<c_-\leq \kappa+1\leq c_+\).
\item \textbf{(Stratum mass.)} \(0<p_{\min}\leq 1/J\).
\item \textbf{(Threshold and level.)} \(0<\bar\delta<1\) and \(0<\alpha<1/2\).
\item \textbf{(Evaluation point.)} \(x\in\mathcal X\) and \(0\leq\delta\leq\bar\delta\).
\end{itemize}
Define the lower-tail atom mass
\[
q_x(\delta)=\int_{[0,\delta]}\pi_x(a)\,da .
\]
Then the conditional pushforward law of the clamped treatment satisfies
\[
P\{d_\delta(A)\in da\mid X=x\}
=
P\{A\in da,\ A\in(\delta,1]\mid X=x\}
+
q_x(\delta)\,\delta_\delta(da),
\]
and the atom mass obeys
\[
\frac{c_-\,\delta^{\kappa+1}}{\kappa+1}
\leq
q_x(\delta)
\leq
\frac{c_+\,\delta^{\kappa+1}}{\kappa+1}.
\]
\end{propositionv}

\Cref{obj:prop:pushforward-setup} supplies the exact mixed-measure decomposition induced by the clamp. The continuous part retains the natural treatment values above the threshold, and the lower tail becomes a point mass at the threshold whose order is \(\delta^{\kappa+1}\) under \cref{obj:ass:polynomial-thinning}. This atom-size calculation is the source of the weighting in \cref{obj:def:frontier} and \cref{obj:def:continuity-frontier}.

\begin{propositionv}{prop:policy-support}[Policy support preservation]
Let \(P\), \(\beta,\kappa,L,c_-,c_+,p_{\min},\bar\delta,\alpha\), \(x\), and \(\delta\) satisfy:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Model.)} \(P\in\mathcal M\) satisfies \cref{obj:def:model-class} with constants \(\beta,\kappa,L,c_-,c_+,p_{\min}\).
\item \textbf{(Regime constants.)} \(J\ge 1\), \(0<\beta\), \(0\le \kappa\), \(0<L\), \(0<c_-\le \kappa+1\le c_+\), \(0<p_{\min}\le 1/J\), \(0<\bar\delta<1\), and \(0<\alpha<1/2\).
\item \textbf{(Stratum and threshold.)} \(x\in\mathcal X\) and \(0\le \delta\le \bar\delta\).
\end{itemize}
Then, for conditional-treatment almost every \(a\) in stratum \(x\), the clamped value \(d_\delta(a)=\max\{a,\delta\}\) from \cref{obj:def:clamp-policy} is a support point of the conditional natural-treatment law given \(X=x\): every open neighborhood \(U\) of \(d_\delta(a)\) has positive conditional-treatment measure in stratum \(x\),
\[
0<\Pr_P(A\in U\mid X=x).
\]
\end{propositionv}

\Cref{obj:prop:policy-support} records the support property used by the causal bridge in \cref{obj:prop:causal-bridge}. The polynomial lower envelope gives positive conditional treatment measure in every neighborhood of the clamp value, so the structural response at the clamped treatment is evaluated on the support generated by the observed design.

\Cref{obj:lem:bandwidth-balance,obj:lem:local-gram-coercivity,obj:lem:good-gram-weights,obj:lem:weight-energy-balance} establish the bandwidth normalization and the realized local-polynomial algebra used by the total-Gram estimator. These statements translate the thinning envelope into local sample information and deterministic weight identities.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:shifted-moment-coercivity}[Uniform shifted coercivity]
Assume:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Degree.)} Fix a polynomial degree \(\ell\in\mathbb N\).
\item \textbf{(Exponent.)} Fix a thinning exponent \(\kappa\in\mathbb R\).
\item \textbf{(Nonnegativity.)} The exponent satisfies \(0\le \kappa\), as in \cref{obj:ass:polynomial-thinning}.
\item \textbf{(Reference matrix.)} Let \(M_{\ell,\kappa,\rho}\) denote the normalized population reference moment matrix in \cref{obj:def:local-design-notation}.
\end{itemize}
Then there exists a constant \(c=c(\ell,\kappa)>0\) such that, for every shift \(\rho\ge 0\) and every vector \(z=(z_0,\ldots,z_\ell)\in\mathbb R^{\ell+1}\),
\[
c\sum_{j=0}^{\ell} z_j^2
\le
z^\top M_{\ell,\kappa,\rho}z .
\]
\end{lemmav}

\Cref{obj:lem:bandwidth-balance} turns the infimum definition of \(h_n\) into an eventual equality at the information boundary. This equality is the normalization used in the risk upper bound and in the expected weight-energy calculation.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:lambda-star-positive}[Positive Gram constant]
In the local-design notation of \cref{obj:def:local-design-notation}, suppose:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Degree.)} The polynomial degree is \(\ell\in\mathbb N\).
\item \textbf{(Thinning exponent.)} The exponent satisfies \(\kappa\ge 0\), as in \cref{obj:ass:polynomial-thinning}.
\item \textbf{(Envelope constants.)} The envelope constants satisfy \(c_->0\) and \(c_+>0\).
\end{itemize}
Then the uniform population Gram lower-bound constant
\[
\lambda_\star(\ell,\kappa,c_-,c_+)
=
\frac{c_-}{c_+}\inf_{\rho\ge 0}\lambda_{\min}(M_{\ell,\kappa,\rho})
\]
is strictly positive:
\[
\lambda_\star(\ell,\kappa,c_-,c_+)>0.
\]
\end{lemmav}

\Cref{obj:lem:local-gram-coercivity} gives the population conditioning bound for the local monomial design. The lower bound is scaled by the local window mass, so it remains compatible with the global polynomial-thinning envelope in the lower-endpoint window.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:power-window-integral-lower}[Power window integral lower bound]
Using the thinning-exponent notation of \cref{obj:ass:polynomial-thinning}, let \(\delta\) be a threshold, \(h\) a window width, and \(\kappa\) an exponent. Assume:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Threshold.)} \(0\le \delta\).
\item \textbf{(Window width.)} \(0<h\).
\item \textbf{(Exponent.)} \(0\le \kappa\).
\end{itemize}
Then
\[
\frac{h}{2}\left(\frac{\delta+h}{2}\right)^{\kappa}
\le
\int_{\delta}^{\delta+h} a^{\kappa}\,da .
\]
\end{lemmav}

\Cref{obj:lem:good-gram-weights} is the deterministic local-polynomial fact used on the good-design event. Moment reproduction gives the intercept interpretation at the threshold, while the absolute-weight and squared-weight bounds control the Hölder bias and stochastic radius in \cref{obj:def:honest-interval}.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:local-window-mass}[Local window mass identity]
Let \(P\), an observed-data law, belong to the model class \(\mathcal M\) of \cref{obj:def:model-class}, with conditional treatment densities, stratum masses, and polynomial thinning as in \cref{obj:ass:conditional-density-law,obj:ass:stratum-mass,obj:ass:polynomial-thinning} for constants \((\beta,\kappa,L,c_-,c_+,p_{\min})\).  Fix:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Stratum.)} A stratum \(x\in\mathcal X\).
\item \textbf{(Threshold and width.)} A threshold \(\delta\in\mathbb R\) and a window width \(h\in\mathbb R\) with \(h>0\).
\item \textbf{(Window support.)} \([\delta,\delta+h]\subseteq[0,1]\).
\end{itemize}
Then the population mass of the local window in stratum \(x\) is
\[
\int \mathbf 1\{X=x,\ 0\le (A-\delta)/h\le 1\}\,dP
=
p_x\int_{\delta}^{\delta+h}\pi_x(a)\,da.
\]
\end{lemmav}

\Cref{obj:lem:weight-energy-balance} integrates the squared-weight scale over the random design. Under the balance equation, the average stochastic radius has the same \(h^\beta\) order as the deterministic local-polynomial bias term.

The following two lemmas are the finite-sample ingredients for the stabilized procedures. They combine bounded-outcome concentration, deterministic fallback branches, and the local-design controls above.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:local-gram-entry-integral}[Local Gram integral identity]
Fix a law \(P\in\mathcal M\) with declared constants \((\beta,\kappa,L,c_-,c_+,p_{\min})\), as in \cref{obj:def:model-class,obj:ass:conditional-density-law,obj:ass:stratum-mass,obj:ass:polynomial-thinning}.  Let \(\ell\in\mathbb N\), \(x\in\mathcal X\), and \(\delta,h\in\mathbb R\).  Assume:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Constant signs.)} \(\kappa\ge 0\), \(c_+\ge 0\), and \(p_{\min}>0\).
\item \textbf{(Window.)} \(h>0\) and \([\delta,\delta+h]\subseteq[0,1]\).
\item \textbf{(Entries.)} \(j,k\in\{0,\ldots,\ell\}\).
\end{itemize}
For \(u_\delta(a)=(a-\delta)/h\), write \(v_\ell(u)=(1,u,\ldots,u^\ell)\) as in \cref{obj:def:local-design-notation}, and define
\[
\phi_j(O)=\mathbf 1\{0\le u_\delta(A)\le 1\}\,v_\ell(u_\delta(A))_j .
\]
Then
\[
\int \mathbf 1\{X=x,\ 0\le u_\delta(A)\le 1\}\,\phi_j(O)\phi_k(O)\,dP(O)
=
p_x\int_{\delta}^{\delta+h}
\pi_x(a)\,
v_\ell(u_\delta(a))_j\,
v_\ell(u_\delta(a))_k\,da .
\]
\end{lemmav}

\Cref{obj:lem:stabilized-interval-honesty} gives the finite-sample coverage statement for the interval in \cref{obj:def:honest-interval}. The interval combines split-block concentration with a bias-aware local radius; on singular local designs, the bounded fallback radius covers the whole possible threshold-mean contribution in the affected stratum.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:scaled-window-coercivity}[Scaled window coercivity]
Let \(\ell\in\mathbb N\), and let \(\lambda_\star=\lambda_\star(\ell,\kappa,c_-,c_+)\) be the uniform population Gram lower-bound constant from \cref{obj:def:local-design-notation}, formed with the lower and upper thinning constants in \cref{obj:ass:polynomial-thinning}. Suppose that
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Shape constants.)} \(\kappa\ge 0\), \(c_->0\), and \(c_+>0\).
\item \textbf{(Window.)} \(\delta\ge 0\) and \(h>0\).
\item \textbf{(Coefficient vector.)} \(v=(v_0,\ldots,v_\ell)\in\mathbb R^{\ell+1}\).
\end{itemize}
Then
\[
\lambda_\star c_+
\left(\int_{\delta}^{\delta+h} a^\kappa\,\mathrm da\right)
\sum_{j=0}^{\ell} v_j^2
\le
c_-
\int_{\delta}^{\delta+h}
\left\{
\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}
v_j\left(\frac{a-\delta}{h}\right)^j
\right\}^2
a^\kappa\,\mathrm da .
\]
\end{lemmav}

\Cref{obj:lem:stabilized-risk-upper} supplies the estimator side of the upper bound in \cref{obj:thm:minimax-risk}. The retained-course and atom-mass empirical terms contribute the \(n^{-1/2}\) scale, while \cref{obj:lem:weight-energy-balance} aligns the threshold-regression stochastic scale with the Hölder bias scale after multiplication by the collapsed mass.

The phase analysis rests on a separate bandwidth comparison. The result below identifies the edge scale where the boundary form of the local design transitions to the interior form.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:intercept-weight-formula}[Intercept weight formula]
Fix the local-design notation of \cref{obj:def:local-design-notation} and the split-block construction of \cref{obj:def:total-gram-estimator}.  Let \(B_n=(I_0,I_1,I_2)\) be a deterministic three-way split with pairwise disjoint blocks, each of cardinality at least \(\lfloor n/4\rfloor\).  Fix a realized sample \(z=(O_1,\ldots,O_n)\), a stratum \(x\in\mathcal X\), a degree \(\ell\), constants \(\kappa,c_-,c_+\), a threshold \(\delta\), a window width \(h\), and an index \(i\).

Assume:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Good design.)} The good-design event \(\Omega_x\) holds for \(B_n,z,x,\ell,\kappa,c_-,c_+,\delta,h\).
\item \textbf{(Regression block.)} The index satisfies \(i\in I_2\).
\item \textbf{(Stratum match.)} The realized stratum satisfies \(X_i=x\).
\item \textbf{(Local window.)} With \(U_i=(A_i-\delta)/h\), one has \(U_i\in[0,1]\).
\end{itemize}

Then the exact local-polynomial intercept weight satisfies
\[
w_{ix}=e_0^\top G_x^{-1}v_\ell(U_i).
\]
\end{lemmav}

\Cref{obj:lem:bandwidth-phases} is the algebraic input behind the phase diagram in \cref{obj:thm:phase-diagram}. It shows that the bandwidth is comparable to the boundary scale near the edge and to the usual thinned interior bandwidth when the threshold is asymptotically beyond that edge.

The total-Gram probability bound depends on population coercivity and elementary local-window identities. \Cref{obj:lem:shifted-moment-coercivity,obj:lem:lambda-star-positive,obj:lem:power-window-integral-lower,obj:lem:local-window-mass,obj:lem:local-gram-entry-integral,obj:lem:scaled-window-coercivity} isolate these deterministic and integral facts before they are assembled in \cref{obj:lem:total-gram}.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:good-gram-reproduction}[Good Gram reproduction]
Fix \(J,n,\ell\in\mathbb N\), a realized sample \(z=(O_1,\ldots,O_n)\), a stratum \(x\in\mathcal X\), constants \(\kappa,c_-,c_+,\delta,h\in\mathbb R\), and deterministic split blocks \(B_n=(I_0,I_1,I_2)\) as in \cref{obj:def:total-gram-estimator}.  Use the local-design notation \(U_i\), \(N_x\), \(G_x\), \(\lambda_\star\), \(\Omega_x\), and \(w_{ix}\) from \cref{obj:def:local-design-notation}.

Assume that \(\lambda_\star>0\) and that the good-design event \(\Omega_x\) holds for \(B_n,z,x,\ell,\kappa,c_-,c_+,\delta,h\).  Then the exact local-polynomial intercept weights reproduce every monomial coordinate at the threshold: for every \(j=0,\ldots,\ell\),
\[
\sum_{i\in I_2} w_{ix} U_i^j
=
\begin{cases}
1, & j=0,\\
0, & j\ne 0.
\end{cases}
\]
\end{lemmav}

\Cref{obj:lem:shifted-moment-coercivity} gives a uniform lower eigenvalue bound for the shifted reference moment matrices. The bound is uniform in the shift parameter, which is what permits thresholds ranging from the boundary region to fixed positive interior points.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:intercept-weight-pointwise}[Pointwise intercept-weight bound]
Fix integers \(J,n,\ell\), a split \(B_n=(I_0,I_1,I_2)\), a realized sample \(z=(O_1,\ldots,O_n)\), a stratum \(x\in\mathcal X\), constants \(\kappa,c_-,c_+,\delta,h\in\mathbb R\), and an index \(i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\).  With \(N_x\), \(\lambda_\star\), \(\Omega_x\), and \(w_{ix}\) as in \cref{obj:def:local-design-notation}, assume:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Split.)} The split \(B_n\) has three pairwise disjoint deterministic blocks \(I_0,I_1,I_2\), each of cardinality at least \(\lfloor n/4\rfloor\), as in \cref{obj:def:total-gram-estimator}.
\item \textbf{(Positive population Gram constant.)} \(\lambda_\star>0\).
\item \textbf{(Good design.)} The good-design event \(\Omega_x\) holds at the realized sample \(z\).
\end{itemize}
Then the exact local-polynomial intercept weight at index \(i\) satisfies
\[
|w_{ix}|
\le
\left(\frac{\lambda_\star N_x}{2}\right)^{-1}\sqrt{\ell+1}.
\]
\end{lemmav}

\Cref{obj:lem:lambda-star-positive} verifies the positivity of the Gram constant used in \cref{obj:def:local-design-notation}. This makes the good-design event meaningful uniformly over the polynomial-thinning envelope.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:bandwidth-balance}[Eventual bandwidth balance]
Let \(h_n\) denote the information-balance bandwidth from \cref{obj:def:bandwidth} formed with \(\delta_n,\beta,\kappa,\bar\delta\). Suppose that:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Hölder exponent.)} The exponent \(\beta\) appearing in \cref{obj:ass:holder-regression} satisfies \(\beta>0\).
\item \textbf{(Polynomial-thinning exponent.)} The exponent \(\kappa\) appearing in \cref{obj:ass:polynomial-thinning} satisfies \(\kappa\geq0\).
\item \textbf{(Threshold cap.)} \(0<\bar\delta<1\).
\item \textbf{(Threshold sequence.)} \((\delta_n)_{n\geq1}\) is a deterministic real sequence satisfying \(\delta_n\in[0,\bar\delta]\) for every \(n\).
\end{itemize}
Then, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
0<h_n,\qquad
h_n\leq 1-\bar\delta,\qquad
n h_n^{2\beta+1}(\delta_n+h_n)^\kappa=1.
\]
\end{lemmav}

\Cref{obj:lem:power-window-integral-lower} gives a simple lower bound on local design mass under the power envelope. It is used to compare the realized local count with \(h(\delta+h)^\kappa\).

\begin{lemmav}{lem:local-gram-coercivity}[Local Gram coercivity]
Fix an observed law \(P\in\mathcal M\) as in \cref{obj:def:model-class}, with conditional-density, stratum-mass, and polynomial-thinning components as in \cref{obj:ass:conditional-density-law,obj:ass:stratum-mass,obj:ass:polynomial-thinning}.  Fix constants \(\beta,\kappa,L,c_-,c_+\), and \(p_{\min}\), and a degree \(\ell\in\mathbb N\).  Suppose that
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Design constants.)} \(\kappa\ge0\), \(c_->0\), \(c_+>0\), and \(p_{\min}>0\).
\item \textbf{(Local window.)} \(x\in\mathcal X\), \(\delta\ge0\), \(h>0\), and \(\delta+h\le1\).
\end{itemize}
For an observation \(O=(X,A,Y)\), set
\[
p_x^{\mathrm{loc}}(\delta,h)
=
\mathbb E_P\!\left[
\mathbf 1\!\left\{X=x,\ 0\le \frac{A-\delta}{h}\le 1\right\}
\right],
\]
and, for \(j=0,\ldots,\ell\), define the local monomial feature
\[
\phi_j(O)
=
\mathbf 1\!\left\{0\le \frac{A-\delta}{h}\le 1\right\}
\left(\frac{A-\delta}{h}\right)^j .
\]
Then, with \(\lambda_\star=\lambda_\star(\ell,\kappa,c_-,c_+)\) denoting the uniform population Gram lower-bound constant from \cref{obj:def:local-design-notation}, every \(v=(v_0,\ldots,v_\ell)\in\mathbb R^{\ell+1}\) satisfies
\[
\lambda_\star\,p_x^{\mathrm{loc}}(\delta,h)\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}v_j^2
\le
\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}
v_jv_k\,
\mathbb E_P\!\left[
\mathbf 1\!\left\{X=x,\ 0\le \frac{A-\delta}{h}\le 1\right\}
\phi_j(O)\phi_k(O)
\right].
\]
\end{lemmav}

\Cref{obj:lem:local-window-mass} expresses the local-window probability through the stratum mass and conditional density. It is the population counterpart of the count \(N_x\) in \cref{obj:def:local-design-notation}.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:good-gram-weights}[Good Gram weights]
Fix \(J,n,\ell\in\mathbb N\), a split \(B_n=(I_0,I_1,I_2)\) with pairwise disjoint blocks satisfying \(|I_r|\ge \lfloor n/4\rfloor\) for \(r=0,1,2\), an observed sample \(z=(O_1,\ldots,O_n)\), and a stratum \(x\in\mathcal X\).  Let \(\kappa,c_-,c_+,\delta,h\in\mathbb R\).  Suppose:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Positive bandwidth.)} \(h>0\).
\item \textbf{(Positive population Gram constant.)} The constant \(\lambda_\star\) from \cref{obj:def:local-design-notation} satisfies \(\lambda_\star>0\).
\item \textbf{(Good local design.)} The good-design event \(\Omega_x\) from \cref{obj:def:local-design-notation} holds for \(B_n,z,x,\ell,\kappa,c_-,c_+,\delta,h\).
\end{itemize}
Then the exact local-polynomial intercept weights \(w_{ix}\) and rescaled coordinates \(U_i\) from \cref{obj:def:local-design-notation} satisfy, for every \(j=0,\ldots,\ell\),
\[
\sum_{i\in I_2} w_{ix} U_i^j=\mathbf 1\{j=0\}.
\]
They also obey the two deterministic weight bounds
\[
\sum_{i\in I_2}|w_{ix}|
\le
\frac{2\sqrt{\ell+1}}{\lambda_\star},
\]
and, with \(N_x\) denoting the local count in \cref{obj:def:local-design-notation},
\[
\sum_{i\in I_2}w_{ix}^2
\le
\frac{4(\ell+1)}{\lambda_\star^2 N_x}.
\]
\end{lemmav}

\Cref{obj:lem:local-gram-entry-integral} identifies each population Gram entry with a one-dimensional integral over the local treatment window. Combined with the density envelope, it connects the empirical Gram matrix in \cref{obj:def:local-design-notation} to the shifted reference matrices.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:weight-energy-balance}[Balanced weight energy]
Let \(J,n,\ell\in\mathbb N\), let \(P\) be an observed-data law on \(J\) strata, let \(B_n=(I_0,I_1,I_2)\) be a deterministic split, and fix a stratum \(x\in\mathcal X\).  Let \(\beta,\kappa,L,c_-,c_+,p_{\min},\delta,h\in\mathbb R\), and define the observed product sampling law by
\[
P^{\otimes n}=\bigotimes_{j=1}^n P .
\]
Suppose that:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Model and sampling.)} \(P\) belongs to the observed model class \(\mathcal M\) in \cref{obj:def:model-class} with fixed constants \(\beta,\kappa,L,c_-,c_+,p_{\min}\), including the polynomial-thinning, stratum-mass, and Hölder-regression components in \cref{obj:ass:polynomial-thinning,obj:ass:stratum-mass,obj:ass:holder-regression}; the observed sample is independently drawn from \(P\) as in \cref{obj:ass:iid-sampling}; and \(B_n\) is a three-way split as in \cref{obj:def:total-gram-estimator}.
\item \textbf{(Numerical conditions.)} \(n\ge 8\), \(\beta>0\), \(\kappa\ge0\), \(c_->0\), \(c_+\ge0\), \(p_{\min}>0\), and \(\delta\ge0\).
\item \textbf{(Bandwidth and design.)} \(h>0\), \(\delta+h\le1\), and the uniform population Gram lower-bound constant \(\lambda_\star=\lambda_\star(\ell,\kappa,c_-,c_+)\) from \cref{obj:def:local-design-notation} satisfies \(\lambda_\star>0\).
\item \textbf{(Balance.)} The bandwidth is information-balanced:
\[
n h^{2\beta+1}(\delta+h)^\kappa=1.
\]
\end{itemize}
For the exact local-polynomial intercept weights \(w_{ix}\) from \cref{obj:def:local-design-notation},
\[
\int
\left(\sum_{i\in I_2} w_{ix}(z)^2\right)^{1/2}
\,dP^{\otimes n}(z)
\le
\left(
\frac{336\{4(\ell+1)/\lambda_\star^2\}}
{p_{\min}c_-/(2\cdot 2^\kappa)}
\right)^{1/2}
h^\beta .
\]
\end{lemmav}

\Cref{obj:lem:scaled-window-coercivity} converts the normalized shifted-moment lower bound into the physical treatment scale. This is the population inequality used before applying concentration to the realized Gram matrix.

\Cref{obj:lem:intercept-weight-formula,obj:lem:good-gram-reproduction,obj:lem:intercept-weight-pointwise} record pointwise properties of the exact intercept weights on the good-design event. They are deterministic consequences of the total Gram matrix and the affine reproduction equations.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:stabilized-interval-honesty}[Stabilized interval honesty]
Fix integers \(J\) and \(n\), deterministic split blocks \(B=(I_0,I_1,I_2)\), constants \(\beta,\kappa,L,c_-,c_+,p_{\min},\bar\delta,\alpha\), and a threshold \(\delta\).  Let \(h_n\) be the information-balance bandwidth from \cref{obj:def:bandwidth} evaluated at \((n,\delta,\beta,\kappa,\bar\delta)\), and let \(\mathrm{CI}_n\) be the stabilized interval from \cref{obj:def:honest-interval} formed with split \(B\), bandwidth \(h_n\), and threshold \(\delta\).  Suppose that:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Split blocks.)} \(B=(I_0,I_1,I_2)\) consists of three deterministic, pairwise-disjoint subsets of \(\{1,\ldots,n\}\) with
\[
|I_0|\ge \lfloor n/4\rfloor,\qquad
|I_1|\ge \lfloor n/4\rfloor,\qquad
|I_2|\ge \lfloor n/4\rfloor .
\]
\item \textbf{(Regime constants.)} The declared constants satisfy
\[
J\ge 1,\quad
\beta>0,\quad
\kappa\ge 0,\quad
L>0,\quad
0<c_-\le \kappa+1\le c_+,\quad
0<p_{\min}\le 1/J,\quad
0<\bar\delta<1,\quad
0<\alpha<1/2 .
\]
\item \textbf{(Sample size.)} \(n\ge 4\).
\item \textbf{(Threshold range.)} \(\delta\in[0,\bar\delta]\).
\item \textbf{(Interior bandwidth.)} \(0<h_n\) and \(\delta+h_n\le 1\).
\end{itemize}
Then \(\mathrm{CI}_n\) is an observed-sample measurable interval, and for every observed-data law \(P\) in the clamp model \(\mathcal M\) of \cref{obj:def:model-class},
\[
1-\alpha
\le
P^{\otimes n}\!\left\{
\theta_\delta(P)\in \mathrm{CI}_n(O_1,\ldots,O_n)
\right\},
\]
where \(\theta_\delta(P)\) is the unsmoothed observed-data clamp target from \cref{obj:def:clamp-functional}.
\end{lemmav}

\Cref{obj:lem:intercept-weight-formula} gives the explicit intercept-weight representation for observations in the local stratum-window. It is the algebraic formula behind the threshold regression term in \cref{obj:def:total-gram-estimator}.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:stabilized-risk-upper}[Stabilized risk upper bound]
Assume that the declared constants satisfy
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Strata.)} \(J\geq 1\).
\item \textbf{(Smoothness and thinning.)} \(\beta>0\) and \(\kappa\geq 0\).
\item \textbf{(Model radii.)} \(L>0\), \(c_->0\), \(c_-\leq \kappa+1\leq c_+\), and \(p_{\min}>0\) with \(p_{\min}\leq 1/J\).
\item \textbf{(Threshold and calibration.)} \(0<\bar\delta<1\) and \(0<\alpha<1/2\).
\end{itemize}
Then there is a constant \(C_{\mathrm{up}}>0\), depending on these declared constants, such that the following holds.  For every deterministic threshold sequence \((\delta_n)_{n\geq 1}\) with \(0\leq \delta_n\leq \bar\delta\) for every \(n\), and for every deterministic split sequence \(B_{\bullet}=(B_n)_{n\geq 1}\) in which each \(B_n=(I_{0,n},I_{1,n},I_{2,n})\) consists of pairwise disjoint subsets of \(\{1,\ldots,n\}\) with
\[
|I_{0,n}|\geq \lfloor n/4\rfloor,\qquad
|I_{1,n}|\geq \lfloor n/4\rfloor,\qquad
|I_{2,n}|\geq \lfloor n/4\rfloor,
\]
there is \(N<\infty\) such that, for every \(n\geq N\),
\[
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M}
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}
\left|\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{TG}}_{n,B_n}-\theta_{\delta_n}(P)\right|
\leq
C_{\mathrm{up}}\left(n^{-1/2}+\delta_n^{\kappa+1}h_n^\beta\right),
\]
where \(\mathcal M\) is the model class in \cref{obj:def:model-class}, \(\theta_{\delta_n}(P)\) is the clamp target in \cref{obj:def:clamp-functional}, \(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{TG}}_{n,B_n}\) is the total-Gram estimator in \cref{obj:def:total-gram-estimator}, \(h_n\) is the information-balance bandwidth for \(\delta_n\) in \cref{obj:def:bandwidth}, and the rate is the frontier in \cref{obj:def:frontier}.
\end{lemmav}

\Cref{obj:lem:good-gram-reproduction} states the monomial reproduction property in the same notation used by the estimator. This property turns local-polynomial weighting into an estimate of the regression value at the threshold.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:bandwidth-phases}[Bandwidth phase bounds]
There exist constants \(c_{\mathrm{bal}},C_{\mathrm{bal}},c_{\mathrm{far}},C_{\mathrm{far}}\) with
\[
0<c_{\mathrm{bal}}\le C_{\mathrm{bal}},
\qquad
0<c_{\mathrm{far}}\le C_{\mathrm{far}},
\]
such that the following assertions hold.  Let
\[
\delta_{\mathrm{edge},n}
=
n^{-1/(2\beta+\kappa+1)} .
\]
For every \(J\), every observed law \(P\), every \(L,c_-,c_+,p_{\min},\alpha\), and every deterministic sequence \((\delta_n)_{n\ge1}\), suppose that:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Regime constants.)} \(J\ge1\), \(\beta>0\), \(\kappa\ge0\), \(L>0\), \(c_->0\), \(c_-\le \kappa+1\le c_+\), \(p_{\min}>0\), \(p_{\min}\le 1/J\), \(0<\bar\delta<1\), and \(0<\alpha<1/2\).
\item \textbf{(Thinning.)} The law \(P\) satisfies the polynomial thinning condition in \cref{obj:ass:polynomial-thinning} with exponent \(\kappa\) and constants \(c_-,c_+\).
\item \textbf{(Thresholds.)} \(\delta_n\in[0,\bar\delta]\) for every \(n\).
\end{itemize}
Let \(h_n\) be the information-balance bandwidth from \cref{obj:def:bandwidth} for \((n,\delta_n,\beta,\kappa,\bar\delta)\).  Then, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
c_{\mathrm{bal}}
\le
n h_n^{2\beta+1}(\delta_n+h_n)^\kappa
\le
C_{\mathrm{bal}},
\]
and
\[
c_{\mathrm{bal}}h_n^{-2\beta}
\le
n h_n(\delta_n+h_n)^\kappa
\le
C_{\mathrm{bal}}h_n^{-2\beta}.
\]
If \(\delta_n/\delta_{\mathrm{edge},n}\to\infty\), then, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
c_{\mathrm{far}}\bigl(n\delta_n^\kappa\bigr)^{-1/(2\beta+1)}
\le
h_n
\le
C_{\mathrm{far}}\bigl(n\delta_n^\kappa\bigr)^{-1/(2\beta+1)} .
\]

Moreover, for every \(D>0\), there exist constants \(c_{\mathrm{near}},C_{\mathrm{near}}\), depending on \(D\), with
\[
0<c_{\mathrm{near}}\le C_{\mathrm{near}},
\]
such that, under the same regime-constant, thinning, and threshold conditions, if
\[
|\delta_n|\le D|\delta_{\mathrm{edge},n}|
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\), then, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
c_{\mathrm{near}}n^{-1/(2\beta+\kappa+1)}
\le
h_n
\le
C_{\mathrm{near}}n^{-1/(2\beta+\kappa+1)} .
\]
\end{lemmav}

\Cref{obj:lem:intercept-weight-pointwise} gives the pointwise weight control implied by Gram coercivity. Together with \cref{obj:lem:good-gram-weights}, it bounds both individual leverage and aggregate variance.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:total-gram}[Total Gram stabilization]
Let \(J\) be a positive integer and let the declared constants satisfy
\[
\beta>0,\qquad \kappa\ge 0,\qquad L>0,\qquad
0<c_-\le \kappa+1\le c_+,\qquad
0<p_{\min}\le 1/J,\qquad
0<\bar\delta<1,\qquad 0<\alpha<1/2 .
\]
Then there are constants \(C,c>0\), depending only on these declared constants, such that the uniform population Gram lower-bound constant \(\lambda_\star\) in \cref{obj:def:local-design-notation}, evaluated at \((\ell,\kappa,c_-,c_+)\) with \(\ell=\lceil\beta\rceil-1\), satisfies \(\lambda_\star>0\), and the following hold.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Model and sampling.)} \(P\in\mathcal M\) as in \cref{obj:def:model-class}, and \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\) are sampled according to \cref{obj:ass:iid-sampling}.
\item \textbf{(Split and threshold.)} \(I_0,I_1,I_2\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}\) are deterministic, pairwise-disjoint split blocks with \(|I_b|\ge \lfloor n/4\rfloor\) for \(b=0,1,2\), and \(\delta\in[0,\bar\delta]\).
\item \textbf{(Bandwidth and stratum.)} \(h_n\) is the information-balance bandwidth from \cref{obj:def:bandwidth} at \((n,\delta,\beta,\kappa,\bar\delta)\), and \(x\in\mathcal X\).
\end{itemize}

For the good-design event \(\Omega_x\) in \cref{obj:def:local-design-notation},
\[
P^{\otimes n}(\Omega_x^c)
\le
C\exp\{-c\,n\,h_n(\delta+h_n)^\kappa\}.
\]
On \(\Omega_x\), the exact local-polynomial intercept weights \(w_{ix}\) in \cref{obj:def:local-design-notation} satisfy, for every \(j=0,\ldots,\ell\),
\[
\sum_{i\in I_2} w_{ix} U_i^j
=
\begin{cases}
1, & j=0,\\
0, & j\ge 1,
\end{cases}
\]
and also obey
\[
\sum_{i\in I_2}|w_{ix}|\le C,
\qquad
\sum_{i\in I_2}w_{ix}^2\le \frac{C}{N_x}.
\]
\end{lemmav}

\Cref{obj:lem:total-gram} assembles the local-window identities, shifted coercivity, and concentration into the good-design probability bound used by the stabilized procedures. The exponential bound makes the singular-design fallback negligible on the scales used in \cref{obj:thm:minimax-risk,obj:thm:honest-length}, while the on-event identities preserve the usual local-polynomial intercept behavior.

The last auxiliary lemma for the appendix supports the continuity-only causal and observed targets. It establishes pointwise uniqueness of the continuous conditional-regression extension on the threshold envelope.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:continuity-regression-extension-unique}[Unique continuous regression extension]
Let \(J\) be a positive integer, let \(\kappa,c_-,c_+,p_{\min},\bar\delta\in\mathbb R\), and let \(P\in\mathcal M_{\mathrm{cont}}\) be a continuity-only observed clamp model with these constants, as in \cref{obj:def:continuity-model-class}. Suppose that
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Design constants.)} The constants satisfy
\[
0\leq \kappa,\qquad
0<c_-,\qquad
c_-\leq \kappa+1\leq c_+,\qquad
0<p_{\min}\leq \frac1J,\qquad
0<\bar\delta<1 .
\]
\item \textbf{(Continuous versions.)} For \(r=1,2\), \(\mu_r:\mathcal X\times\mathbb R\to\mathbb R\) satisfies that \(a\mapsto \mu_r(x,a)\) is continuous on \([0,\bar\delta]\) for every \(x\in\mathcal X\).
\item \textbf{(Regression version property.)} For \(r=1,2\),
\[
\mathbb E_P[Y\mid X,A]=\mu_r(X,A)
\quad\text{\(P\)-almost surely.}
\]
\end{itemize}
Then, for every \(x\in\mathcal X\),
\[
\mu_1(x,a)=\mu_2(x,a)
\qquad\text{for every }a\in[0,\bar\delta].
\]
\end{lemmav}

\Cref{obj:lem:continuity-regression-extension-unique} justifies the pointwise notation for the continuity-only regression extension used in \cref{obj:def:continuity-clamp-functional}. The positive treatment-density envelope gives enough support throughout \([0,\bar\delta]\) for almost-everywhere equality of continuous versions to determine the extension at every threshold value.
