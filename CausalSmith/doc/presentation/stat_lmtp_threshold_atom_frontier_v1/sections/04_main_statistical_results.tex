\section{Main statistical results}
\label{sec:main-statistical-results}

\Cref{sec:estimation-intervals} constructed the split-sample total-Gram estimator and its bias-aware interval for the observed clamp target in \cref{obj:def:clamp-functional}. We now state the corresponding performance guarantees. The product sampling law in \cref{obj:synth_16} and the interval-length convention in \cref{obj:synth_17} put point estimators, confidence intervals, and minimax benchmarks on the same finite-sample scale.

The scalar calibration at the end of the section uses a concrete Bernoulli pair. We record it before the main bounds so that the same notation can be used in the rate check.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_10}[One-cell Bernoulli regressions]
In the one-stratum calibration with \(\beta=\kappa=1\) and \(\pi(a)=2a\), define the null Bernoulli regression by \(m_0(a)=1/2\). For a threshold \(\delta_n\) and bandwidth \(h_n>0\), define the localized alternative by \(m_{1,\delta_n,h_n}(a)=1/2+h_n\,\tau_{\delta_n,h_n}(a)\), where \(\tau_{\delta,h}(a)=\max\{0,1-|a-\delta|/h\}\).
\end{definitionv}

The triangular alternative in \cref{obj:synth_10} is centered at the moving threshold and has height proportional to the bandwidth. It gives a transparent version of the same local perturbation that drives the lower-bound component of the general theory.

The minimax benchmarks optimize over all observed-sample procedures. The first criterion measures worst-case absolute accuracy for estimating the unsmoothed clamp target, and the second measures worst-case expected length among intervals with uniform coverage.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_3}[Observed minimax risk and honest length]
For the observed clamp target \(\theta_{\delta_n}(P)\) over \(\mathcal M\), the minimax absolute-error risk is
\[
  R_n^\star=\inf_{\widehat\theta}\sup_{P\in\mathcal M}
  \mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}\!\left|\widehat\theta(O_1,\ldots,O_n)-\theta_{\delta_n}(P)\right|,
\]
where the infimum ranges over observed-sample measurable estimators taking values in \([0,1]\). The minimax honest expected length is
\[
  L_n^\star=\inf_{C_n:\,\inf_{P\in\mathcal M}P^{\otimes n}\{\theta_{\delta_n}(P)\in C_n\}\ge 1-\alpha}
  \sup_{P\in\mathcal M}\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}[\operatorname{len}(C_n)],
\]
where \(C_n\) ranges over observed-sample measurable interval procedures with endpoints in \([0,1]\).
\end{definitionv}

Thus \leanref{sym:R_n_star}{\ensuremath{R_n^\star}} is the best possible worst-case absolute accuracy for the observed clamp target, while \leanref{sym:L_n_star}{\ensuremath{L_n^\star}} is the best possible worst-case length under uniform honesty. These criteria match the honesty framework used in nonparametric confidence intervals \citep{Low1997NonparametricConfidenceIntervals,ArmstrongKolesar2018OptimalInference}.

The main risk result gives matching upper and lower bounds at the rate in \cref{obj:def:frontier}. The upper bound is attained by the stabilized total-Gram estimator from \cref{obj:def:total-gram-estimator}; the lower bound is witnessed by both a global mean shift and a localized threshold perturbation.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:minimax-risk}[Minimax clamp frontier]
Fix \(J,\beta,\kappa,L,c_-,c_+,p_{\min},\bar\delta,\alpha\) satisfying the regime conditions:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Regime.)} \(J\geq 1\), \(\beta>0\), \(\kappa\geq0\), \(L>0\), \(c_->0\), \(c_-\leq \kappa+1\leq c_+\), \(p_{\min}>0\), \(p_{\min}\leq 1/J\), \(\bar\delta\in(0,1)\), and \(\alpha\in(0,1/2)\).
\end{itemize}
Then there exist constants \(0<c<C\) and an amplitude \(a\in(0,1/4]\), depending only on the displayed regime constants, such that, for every deterministic threshold sequence \((\delta_n)_{n\geq1}\) with \(\delta_n\in[0,\bar\delta]\) for every \(n\) and every deterministic sequence \(B_n=(I_{0,n},I_{1,n},I_{2,n})\) of three pairwise-disjoint sample blocks with \(|I_{j,n}|\geq \lfloor n/4\rfloor\) for \(j=0,1,2\), for all sufficiently large \(n\), with \(h_n\) the information-balance bandwidth from \cref{obj:def:bandwidth} and
\[
r_n=n^{-1/2}+\delta_n^{\kappa+1}h_n^\beta
\]
as in \cref{obj:def:frontier}, the observed minimax absolute-error risk \(R_n^\star\) over the clamp model \(\mathcal M\) in \cref{obj:def:model-class} and the worst-case i.i.d. risk over \(\mathcal M\) of the stabilized estimator using \(B_n\) satisfy
\[
c r_n
\leq
R_n^\star
\leq
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M} \mathbb E_P\!\left[
\left|\widehat\theta_{n,B_n}^{\mathrm{TG}}-\theta_{\delta_n}(P)\right|
\right]
\leq
C r_n .
\]
Moreover, for all sufficiently large \(n\), the lower bound is witnessed inside the same clamp model by:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Global shift.)} Laws \(P_0,P_1\in\mathcal M\) satisfying \cref{obj:ass:iid-sampling}, with the same \((X,A)\)-design distribution and the same \(p_x\) and \(\pi_x\) functions, having Bernoulli outcomes, and obeying
\[
\mu_x^{P_1}(a)=\mu_x^{P_0}(a)+n^{-1/2}
\quad\text{for every }x\text{ and }a\in[0,1],
\]
with well-posed product chi-squared divergence,
\[
P_1^{\otimes n}\ll P_0^{\otimes n},
\qquad
\int \left(\frac{dP_1^{\otimes n}}{dP_0^{\otimes n}}-1\right)^2\,dP_0^{\otimes n}<\infty,
\]
and
\[
c n^{-1/2}
\leq
\left|\theta_{\delta_n}(P_1)-\theta_{\delta_n}(P_0)\right|,
\qquad
\chi^2\!\left(P_1^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n}\right)\leq C .
\]
\item \textbf{(Localized perturbation.)} Laws \(Q_0,Q_1\in\mathcal M\) satisfying \cref{obj:ass:iid-sampling}, with the same \((X,A)\)-design distribution and the same \(p_x\) and \(\pi_x\) functions, having Bernoulli outcomes, and admitting a continuous bump \(b:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R\) satisfying \(b(0)=1\), \(b(u)\geq0\) for every \(u\), \(b(u)=0\) for \(u\notin[-1,1]\), and \(|b(u)|\leq1\) for \(u\in[-1,1]\), with regression shift
\[
\mu_x^{Q_1}(t)-\mu_x^{Q_0}(t)
=
a\,h_n^\beta b\!\left(\frac{t-\delta_n}{h_n}\right)
\]
for every \(x\) and \(t\in[0,1]\). The product chi-squared divergence is well posed,
\[
Q_1^{\otimes n}\ll Q_0^{\otimes n},
\qquad
\int \left(\frac{dQ_1^{\otimes n}}{dQ_0^{\otimes n}}-1\right)^2\,dQ_0^{\otimes n}<\infty,
\]
and
\[
c\,\delta_n^{\kappa+1}h_n^\beta
\leq
\left|\theta_{\delta_n}(Q_1)-\theta_{\delta_n}(Q_0)\right|,
\qquad
\chi^2\!\left(Q_1^{\otimes n},Q_0^{\otimes n}\right)\leq C .
\]
\end{itemize}
\end{theoremv}

The two lower-bound constructions in \cref{obj:thm:minimax-risk} isolate the two terms in \(r_n\). A global Bernoulli shift produces the parametric contribution, while a Hölder-sized bump at the threshold produces the atom-weighted local contribution. The estimator in \cref{obj:def:total-gram-estimator} attains this combined scale, so the observed clamp problem has the same order from the attainable and unavoidable sides.

The corresponding interval result uses the same rate scale. Its statement combines finite-sample coverage for the constructed interval with a minimax lower bound for every honest observed-sample interval.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:honest-length}[Honest length frontier]
There are constants \(c,C\in(0,\infty)\), with \(c<C\), for which the following holds.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Regime constants.)} The number of strata \(J\) is a positive integer, \(\beta>0\), \(\kappa\geq0\), \(L>0\), \(c_->0\), \(c_-\leq \kappa+1\leq c_+\), \(p_{\min}>0\), \(p_{\min}\leq 1/J\), \(\bar\delta\in(0,1)\), and \(\alpha\in(0,1/2)\).
\item \textbf{(Threshold path.)} The deterministic thresholds satisfy \(0\leq \delta_n\leq \bar\delta\) for every \(n\).
\item \textbf{(Sample splits.)} For every \(n\), \(B_n=(I_{0,n},I_{1,n},I_{2,n})\) is a deterministic three-way split of the sample indices with pairwise disjoint blocks and \(|I_{j,n}|\geq \lfloor n/4\rfloor\) for \(j=0,1,2\).
\end{itemize}

For all sufficiently large \(n\), set \(h_n\) to be the information-balance bandwidth for \(\delta_n\) from \cref{obj:def:bandwidth}, and set
\[
r_n=n^{-1/2}+\delta_n^{\kappa+1}h_n^\beta .
\]
Then the stabilized interval \(\mathrm{CI}_n\) has worst-case coverage
\[
\inf\Bigl\{
P^{\otimes n}\{\theta_{\delta_n}(P)\in \mathrm{CI}_n(O_1,\ldots,O_n)\}:
P\in\mathcal M
\Bigr\}\geq 1-\alpha,
\]
and its worst-case expected length is comparable to \(r_n\):
\[
c\,r_n
\leq
\sup\Bigl\{
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}\bigl[\operatorname{len}(\mathrm{CI}_n)\bigr]:
P\in\mathcal M
\Bigr\}
\leq
C\,r_n .
\]
Moreover, the minimax honest expected length satisfies
\[
c\,r_n
\leq
L_n^\star,
\]
where \(L_n^\star\) is the infimum, over observed-sample confidence procedures with uniform coverage at least \(1-\alpha\) over \(\mathcal M\), of their worst-case expected length. Equivalently, every observed-sample confidence procedure \(C_n\) with
\[
\inf\Bigl\{
P^{\otimes n}\{\theta_{\delta_n}(P)\in C_n(O_1,\ldots,O_n)\}:
P\in\mathcal M
\Bigr\}\geq 1-\alpha
\]
has worst-case expected length at least \(c\,r_n\):
\[
c\,r_n
\leq
\sup\Bigl\{
\mathbb E_{P^{\otimes n}}\bigl[\operatorname{len}(C_n)\bigr]:
P\in\mathcal M
\Bigr\}.
\]
\end{theoremv}

The interval in \cref{obj:thm:honest-length} is calibrated to the same two sources of difficulty as the estimator. The retained-course and atom-mass terms are controlled by bounded-outcome concentration, while the threshold regression term carries the local Hölder bias and the realized Gram behavior encoded in \cref{obj:def:honest-interval}. The lower bound shows that uniform honesty over \(\mathcal M\) requires expected length of order \(r_n\), in line with modulus-based accounts of optimal confidence sets \citep{Low1997NonparametricConfidenceIntervals,ArmstrongKolesar2020}.

The two theorems above leave the rate in the compact form \(r_n\). The next result expands that expression into the threshold regimes induced by the information-balance bandwidth. Two deterministic scales organize those regimes, the risk-transition scale \leanref{sym:delta_crit}{\ensuremath{\delta_{\mathrm{crit},n}}} and the bandwidth-transition scale \leanref{sym:delta_edge}{\ensuremath{\delta_{\mathrm{edge},n}}},
\[
\delta_{\mathrm{crit},n}
=
n^{-1/\{2(\beta\kappa+2\beta+\kappa+1)\}},
\qquad
\delta_{\mathrm{edge},n}
=
n^{-1/(2\beta+\kappa+1)} ,
\]
and they are restated inside \cref{obj:thm:phase-diagram}. \Cref{tab:phase-summary} collects the regimes, separating the geometry of the treatment support at the threshold from the resulting risk order.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:phase-diagram}[Phase boundary regimes]
Let \(J\) be a positive integer and let
\[
\beta>0,\qquad
\kappa\geq 0,\qquad
L>0,\qquad
0<c_-\leq \kappa+1\leq c_+,\qquad
0<p_{\min}\leq 1/J,\qquad
0<\bar\delta<1,\qquad
0<\alpha<1/2 .
\]
Define the phase and edge scales by
\[
\delta_{\mathrm{crit},n}
=
n^{-1/\{2(\beta\kappa+2\beta+\kappa+1)\}},
\qquad
\delta_{\mathrm{edge},n}
=
n^{-1/(2\beta+\kappa+1)} .
\]
Then
\[
\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{crit},n}}{\delta_{\mathrm{edge},n}}\to\infty .
\]
Moreover, for every deterministic threshold sequence \((\delta_n)_{n\geq 1}\) with \(\delta_n\in[0,\bar\delta]\) for every \(n\), let \(h_n\) be the information-balance bandwidth in \cref{obj:def:bandwidth}, and set
\[
a_n=\delta_n^{\kappa+1}h_n^\beta,
\qquad
r_n=n^{-1/2}+\delta_n^{\kappa+1}h_n^\beta
\]
as in \cref{obj:def:frontier}. The following conclusions hold:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Regular thresholds.} If \(\delta_n/\delta_{\mathrm{crit},n}\to 0\), then \(r_n\asymp n^{-1/2}\).
\item \textbf{Critical thresholds.} For every \(c_0>0\), if \(\delta_n/\delta_{\mathrm{crit},n}\to c_0\), then \(a_n\asymp n^{-1/2}\) and \(r_n\asymp n^{-1/2}\).
\item \textbf{Vanishing atom-dominated thresholds.} If \(\delta_n/\delta_{\mathrm{crit},n}\to\infty\) and \(\delta_n\to0\), then
\[
r_n\asymp
\delta_n^{\kappa+1}\bigl(n\delta_n^\kappa\bigr)^{-\beta/(2\beta+1)} .
\]
\item \textbf{Fixed thresholds.} For every \(\delta_0\in(0,\bar\delta]\), if \(\delta_n\to\delta_0\), then
\[
r_n\asymp n^{-\beta/(2\beta+1)} .
\]
\item \textbf{Zero threshold identities.} For every \(n\) and every \(P\in\mathcal M\) satisfying \cref{obj:def:model-class},
\[
q_x(0)=0\quad\text{for every }x,\qquad
\theta_0(P)=\int Y\,dP,\qquad
r_n\big|_{\delta_n=0}=n^{-1/2},
\]
where the last identity uses the bandwidth \(h_n\) at threshold \(0\).
\item \textbf{Zero threshold estimator.} For every \(n\), every \(P\in\mathcal M\) satisfying \cref{obj:def:model-class}, and every admissible three-way split \(B=(I_0,I_1,I_2)\) as in \cref{obj:def:total-gram-estimator}, the total-Gram estimator in \cref{obj:def:total-gram-estimator}, formed at threshold \(0\) with bandwidth \(h_n\), agrees under the product sampling law generated by \(P\) with
\[
z\mapsto
\operatorname{clamp}_{[0,1]}\!\left(\frac{1}{|I_0|}\sum_{i\in I_0}Y_i\right).
\]
\item \textbf{Zero threshold stabilized guarantees.} For every deterministic sequence \(B_\bullet=(B_n)_{n\geq1}\) of admissible three-way split blocks, the stabilized zero-threshold procedure has coverage at least \(1-\alpha\) for all sufficiently large \(n\). Its stabilized worst-case risk at threshold \(0\) and its stabilized worst-case expected length at threshold \(0\) with noncoverage level \(\alpha\) are both asymptotic to \(n^{-1/2}\).
\end{itemize}
\end{theoremv}

\Cref{obj:thm:phase-diagram} identifies \(\delta_{\mathrm{crit},n}\) as the boundary at which the local atom contribution reaches the parametric scale, and \(\delta_{\mathrm{edge},n}\) as the smaller edge scale for the bandwidth algebra. Regular and critical threshold paths retain root-\(n\) order. Vanishing thresholds above the critical scale inherit a degenerate-design nonparametric rate of the kind studied by \citet{Gaiffas2005DegenerateDesign}: the estimation point rides toward the thinned endpoint \(a=0\), so the local design mass degenerates at the polynomial rate implied by \cref{obj:ass:polynomial-thinning}. A fixed positive threshold is an interior-design geometry. There the target point \(\delta_0\) is interior to the treatment support and the density at that point is bounded away from zero, so the exponent \(\beta/(2\beta+1)\) is the ordinary positive-density pointwise Hölder rate \citep{Stone1982,Donoho1991} multiplied by an atom weight \(\delta_0^{\kappa+1}\) bounded away from zero. The estimator still uses a one-sided window, because the clamp evaluates the regression at \(\delta_0\) from above, but that is a property of the procedure rather than of the design geometry. At the exact zero threshold, the clamp target reduces to the observed mean and the total-Gram estimator reduces to the clipped split-sample outcome mean.

The section closes with a scalar calibration. It specializes the localized perturbation to one stratum, linear thinning, and Lipschitz smoothness, giving an explicit exponent check for the critical scale.

\begin{propositionv}{prop:one-cell-calibration}[One-cell calibration]
Let \(\bar\delta\in(0,1)\), and let \((\delta_n)_{n\geq1}\) be a deterministic threshold sequence satisfying
\[
0\leq \delta_n\leq \bar\delta
\qquad\text{for every }n .
\]
Assume that \(\delta_n\gg n^{-1/4}\), in the sense that
\[
\frac{\delta_n}{n^{-1/4}}\longrightarrow\infty .
\]
In the one-stratum model with \(\beta=\kappa=1\) and \(\pi(a)=2a\), let \(h_n\) be the information-balance bandwidth from \cref{obj:def:bandwidth} evaluated at \((\delta_n,1,1,\bar\delta)\). For the concrete localized Bernoulli alternatives, define
\[
K_n
=
n\int_0^1
\operatorname{KL}\!\left(
\operatorname{Bernoulli}(m_{1,\delta_n,h_n}(a)),
\operatorname{Bernoulli}(m_0(a))
\right)\,2a\,da
\]
and
\[
\Delta_n
=
\int_{\delta_n}^1
\bigl(m_{1,\delta_n,h_n}(a)-m_0(a)\bigr)\,2a\,da
+
\left(\int_0^{\delta_n}2a\,da\right)
\bigl(m_{1,\delta_n,h_n}(\delta_n)-m_0(\delta_n)\bigr).
\]
Then
\[
h_n\asymp (n\delta_n)^{-1/3},
\qquad
K_n\asymp n\delta_n h_n^3,
\qquad
\Delta_n\asymp \delta_n^2h_n.
\]
Moreover,
\[
\delta_n\asymp n^{-1/10}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\Delta_n\asymp n^{-1/2}.
\]
\end{propositionv}

In \cref{obj:prop:one-cell-calibration}, \(K_n\) is the product Kullback--Leibler scale for the localized Bernoulli alternative, while \(\Delta_n\) is the induced clamp-target separation. The balance \(h_n\asymp(n\delta_n)^{-1/3}\) keeps the localized alternative statistically contiguous at constant information scale, and the identity \(\delta_n\asymp n^{-1/10}\) reproduces the critical exponent from \cref{obj:thm:phase-diagram} when \(\beta=\kappa=1\).
