\section{Causal interpretation through full-data lifts}
\label{sec:causal-interpretation}

The observed-data results in \cref{obj:thm:minimax-risk,obj:thm:honest-length} concern the clamp functional \(\theta_\delta(P)\) under the law of \((X,A,Y)\). This section supplies a full-data causal interpretation for the same target.  One notational convention applies throughout this section and the continuity-only section.  Every estimator and interval used in this paper is a measurable function of the observed sample, so its risk, coverage probability, and expected length are the same whether computed under the \(n\)-fold product law of the observed margin or under the \(n\)-fold product law of the full-data package.  We therefore state all such quantities under the observed-margin product law \(P^{\otimes n}\), and use the full-data product law only where the statement genuinely concerns full-data objects. The structural full-data overlay augments the observed experiment with a full-data law \leanref{sym:P_full}{\ensuremath{P^{\mathrm F}}}, a member-specific latent-response space \leanref{sym:mathcal_U}{\ensuremath{\mathcal U}}, a latent response variable \leanref{sym:U}{\ensuremath{U}}, structural response maps \leanref{sym:g_x}{\ensuremath{g_x}}, and a potential-outcome process \leanref{sym:Y_a}{\ensuremath{\{Y(a):a\in[0,1]\}}}. Within that overlay, the lower-threshold policy changes the realized treatment through the same clamp map introduced in \cref{obj:def:clamp-policy}; the statistical analysis remains based on the observed sample.

The analysis rests on three full-data restrictions and one response-mean definition. The first two restrictions are standard causal conditions for a latent-response representation, and the third records the continuity required on the threshold interval used by the clamp policy.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:consistency}[Structural consistency]
Under \(P^{\mathrm F}\), the following conditions hold:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Latent response.)} The latent response variable \(U\) takes values in \(\mathcal U\).
\item \textbf{(Structural maps.)} For every \(x\in\mathcal X\), the map
\[
g_x:[0,1]\times\mathcal U\to[0,1]
\]
is jointly Borel measurable.
\item \textbf{(Potential and observed outcomes.)} There is a single \(P^{\mathrm F}\)-null set outside which, for every \(a\in[0,1]\),
\[
Y(a)=g_X(a,U),
\qquad
Y=g_X(A,U).
\]
\end{itemize}
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:consistency} is the structural consistency condition: a common latent response generates all potential outcomes and the observed outcome at the realized treatment, in the simultaneous NPSEM-style formulation used for modified treatment policies \citep{DiazWilliamsHoffmanSchenck2023}. The common null set makes the equality simultaneous over doses, which is the form needed when the clamp map evaluates the response at \(d_\delta(A)\).

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:exchangeability}[Conditional exchangeability]
Under \(P^{\mathrm F}\), the treatment \(A\) and latent response variable \(U\) are conditionally independent given \(X\).
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:exchangeability} is the latent-response randomization condition \citep{DiazWilliamsHoffmanSchenck2023}. Within each stratum, it lets the conditional treatment density weight the same latent-response distribution that determines the structural mean.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_1}[Full-data response mean]
For a full-data law package \(P^{\mathrm F}\) with latent-response carrier \(\mathcal U\), latent variable \(U\), structural maps \(g_x:[0,1]\times\mathcal U\to[0,1]\), and observed stratum \(X\), the full-data response mean in stratum \(x\in\mathcal X\) is
\[
  m_x^{\mathrm F}(a)=\mathbb E_{P^{\mathrm F}}\{g_x(a,U)\mid X=x\},\qquad a\in[0,1].
\]
When the law package is in the full-data continuity model, the map \(a\mapsto m_x^{\mathrm F}(a)\) is continuous on \([0,\bar\delta]\) for every \(x\in\mathcal X\).
\end{definitionv}

The full-data response mean \leanref{sym:m_x_F}{\ensuremath{m_x^{\mathrm F}(a)}} is the fiberwise structural analogue of the observed regression extension. It isolates the average structural response at dose \(a\) among units in stratum \(x\), which is the quantity the bridge result compares with \(\mu_x^P\) on the threshold interval.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:full-data-response-continuity}[Full-data response continuity]
Under the full-data law \(P^{\mathrm F}\), for every \(x\in\mathcal X\), the full-data response mean \(a\mapsto m_x^{\mathrm F}(a)\) is continuous on \([0,\bar\delta]\).
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:full-data-response-continuity} is specific to this analysis. It places the continuity requirement directly on the structural response mean over the range where clamping can create a threshold evaluation, matching the interval on which the observed regression extension is used.

These conditions define the full-data causal class and the decision criteria used below.

\begin{definitionv}{def:full-data-model-class}[Full-data causal class \(\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}\)]
\(\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}\) is the collection of full-data law packages \(P^{\mathrm F}\) satisfying the following memberwise structure relative to \cref{obj:def:model-class,obj:ass:consistency,obj:ass:exchangeability,obj:ass:full-data-response-continuity}.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Latent response space.)} Each member carries its own standard Borel latent-response space \(\mathcal U\) and an \(\mathcal U\)-valued random variable \(U\).
\item \textbf{(Structural response maps.)} Each member carries jointly Borel maps
\[
g_x:[0,1]\times\mathcal U\to[0,1],
\qquad x\in\mathcal X,
\]
and a jointly measurable potential-outcome process
\[
\{Y(a):a\in[0,1]\}.
\]
\item \textbf{(Full-data law.)} Each member carries a probability law for
\[
(X,A,Y,U,\{Y(a):a\in[0,1]\}).
\]
\item \textbf{(Observed margin.)} The \((X,A,Y)\)-margin is some \(P\in\mathcal M\).
\item \textbf{(Causal member conditions.)} Within the member's own carrier, the simultaneous latent-response consistency, latent randomization, and fiberwise structural-mean continuity on \([0,\bar\delta]\) conditions hold.
\end{itemize}
The class is formed member by member, with membership determined on each member's own standard Borel carrier.
\end{definitionv}

The full-data class \leanref{sym:mathcal_M_F}{\ensuremath{\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}}} keeps the observed law inside \(\mathcal M\) while allowing each member to carry its own standard Borel latent-response space. This memberwise formulation is the natural setting for comparing causal and observed decision problems without imposing a common latent carrier across all laws.

\begin{definitionv}{def:causal-clamp-mean}[Causal clamp mean \(\psi_\delta(P^{\mathrm F})\)]
For a full-data law \(P^{\mathrm F}\) as in \cref{obj:def:full-data-model-class}, the clamp policy \(d_\delta\) from \cref{obj:def:clamp-policy}, and \(0\leq\delta\leq\bar\delta\), define the causal clamp mean by
\[
\psi_\delta(P^{\mathrm F})
=
\mathbb E_{P^{\mathrm F}}\!\left[Y(d_\delta(A))\right].
\]
\end{definitionv}

The causal clamp mean \leanref{sym:psi_delta}{\ensuremath{\psi_\delta(P^{\mathrm F})}} is the mean outcome under the same lower-threshold modified treatment policy used to define the observed clamp functional in \cref{obj:def:clamp-functional}. Thus the causal estimand changes the interpretation of the response while preserving the policy rule and threshold range.

\begin{definitionv}{def:causal-frontier-criteria}[Causal frontier criteria \(R_{n,\mathrm F}^\star\) and \(L_{n,\mathrm F}^\star\)]
For the causal clamp mean in \cref{obj:def:causal-clamp-mean}, define the causal minimax absolute-error risk over estimators measurable with respect to the observed sample \(O_1,\ldots,O_n\) by
\[
 R_{n,\mathrm F}^\star
 =
 \inf_{\widehat\psi}\sup_{P^{\mathrm F}\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}}
 \mathbb E_{(P^{\mathrm F})^{\otimes n}}
 \left|\widehat\psi-\psi_{\delta_n}(P^{\mathrm F})\right|.
\]
For intervals \(C_n\) measurable with respect to the same observed sample, define the causal minimax honest expected length by
\[
 L_{n,\mathrm F}^\star
 =
 \inf_{C_n:\,\inf_{P^{\mathrm F}\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}}
 (P^{\mathrm F})^{\otimes n}
 \left\{\psi_{\delta_n}(P^{\mathrm F})\in C_n\right\}\geq1-\alpha}
 \sup_{P^{\mathrm F}\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}}
 \mathbb E_{(P^{\mathrm F})^{\otimes n}}
 \left[\operatorname{len}(C_n)\right].
\]
\end{definitionv}

The causal minimax risk \leanref{sym:R_n_F_star}{\ensuremath{R_{n,\mathrm F}^\star}} and causal honest-length criterion \leanref{sym:L_n_F_star}{\ensuremath{L_{n,\mathrm F}^\star}} compare observed-sample estimators and intervals under the full-data class through their induced observed-sample behavior. The procedures therefore use the same data as in the observed experiment while the target is the causal clamp mean.

The next proposition gives the identification step. It connects the structural mean under the full-data law to the observed regression extension and then identifies the causal clamp mean with the observed clamp target.

\begin{propositionv}{prop:causal-bridge}[Causal bridge identity]
Let \(P^{\mathrm F}\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}\) satisfy \cref{obj:def:full-data-model-class}, let \(P\) be its observed \((X,A,Y)\)-margin, and let \(d_\delta(a)=\max\{a,\delta\}\) be the clamp map in \cref{obj:def:clamp-policy}.  Suppose the frontier constants satisfy
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Strata.)} \(J\geq 1\).
\item \textbf{(Smoothness and thinning.)} \(\beta>0\), \(\kappa\geq 0\), and \(L>0\).
\item \textbf{(Density and mass constants.)} \(c_->0\), \(c_-\leq \kappa+1\leq c_+\), \(p_{\min}>0\), and \(p_{\min}\leq 1/J\).
\item \textbf{(Threshold and coverage levels.)} \(0<\bar\delta<1\) and \(0<\alpha<1/2\).
\end{itemize}
For \(x\in\mathcal X\) and \(a\in[0,1]\), define the full-data response mean
\[
m_x^{\mathrm F}(a)
=
P^{\mathrm F}(X=x)^{-1}
\int_{\{X=x\}} g_x(a,U)\,dP^{\mathrm F}.
\]
Then, for every \(x\in\mathcal X\) and every Borel set \(B\subseteq[0,1]\),
\[
P(X=x)^{-1}
\int_{\{X=x,\ A\in B\}} Y\,dP^{\mathrm F}
=
\int_B m_x^{\mathrm F}(a)\pi_x(a)\,da.
\]
Moreover, for every \(x\in\mathcal X\) and every \(a\in[0,\bar\delta]\),
\[
m_x^{\mathrm F}(a)=\mu_x^P(a).
\]
Consequently, for every \(\delta\in[0,\bar\delta]\), the following identities hold:
\[
d_\delta(a)\in \operatorname{supp}\{\pi_x(a)\,da\text{ on }[0,1]\}
\quad
\text{for each }x\in\mathcal X\text{ and for conditional-treatment-a.e. }a,
\]
\[
Y(d_\delta(A))
=
Y\mathbf 1\{\delta<A\}+Y(\delta)\mathbf 1\{A\leq\delta\}
\quad P^{\mathrm F}\text{-a.s.},
\]
and, with
\[
\psi_\delta(P^{\mathrm F})=\int Y(d_\delta(A))\,dP^{\mathrm F},
\qquad
\nu_\delta(P)=\int Y\mathbf 1\{\delta<A\}\,dP,
\qquad
q_x(\delta)=\int_0^\delta \pi_x(a)\,da,
\]
the causal clamp mean equals the observed clamp target
\[
\psi_\delta(P^{\mathrm F})
=
\nu_\delta(P)+\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}p_xq_x(\delta)\mu_x^P(\delta)
=
\theta_\delta(P).
\]
\end{propositionv}

\Cref{obj:prop:causal-bridge} is the fiberwise bridge. Conditional exchangeability converts observed outcomes within a stratum and treatment set into an integral of the structural response mean against the treatment density; continuity and the observed regression extension then identify that mean on the threshold interval. The final identity shows that the causal lower-threshold policy mean is exactly the clamp functional analyzed in the observed experiment.

A second structural fact records that the full-data class covers the observed model class exactly at the margin. The construction uses regular conditional distributions on standard Borel spaces, a standard measurable-disintegration tool \citep[Theorem 6.3]{Kallenberg2002Foundations}.

\begin{propositionv}{prop:observed-margin-surjectivity}[Observed margin surjectivity]*
Fix \(J\in\mathbb N\) and constants \(\beta,\kappa,L,c_-,c_+,p_{\min},\bar\delta,\alpha\in\mathbb R\). Assume:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Regime constants.)} \(0<J\), \(0<\beta\), \(0\leq \kappa\), \(0<L\), \(0<c_-\), \(c_-\leq \kappa+1\), \(\kappa+1\leq c_+\), \(0<p_{\min}\), \(p_{\min}\leq 1/J\), \(0<\bar\delta<1\), and \(0<\alpha<1/2\).
\item \textbf{(Observed model.)} \(P\in\mathcal M\) means that \(P\) satisfies \cref{obj:def:model-class} with constants \((\beta,\kappa,L,c_-,c_+,p_{\min})\).
\item \textbf{(Full-data model.)} \(P^{\mathrm F}\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}\) means that \(P^{\mathrm F}\) satisfies \cref{obj:def:full-data-model-class} with constants \((\beta,\kappa,L,c_-,c_+,p_{\min},\bar\delta)\).
\end{itemize}
Then, for every \(P\in\mathcal M\), there exists \(P^{\mathrm F}\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}\) whose observed \((X,A,Y)\)-margin is exactly \(P\). Hence the observed-margin map from \(\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}\) onto \(\mathcal M\) is surjective.

Moreover, for every \(n\in\mathbb N\) and every \(\delta\in[0,\bar\delta]\), the causal frontier criteria in \cref{obj:def:causal-frontier-criteria} satisfy
\[
R_{n,\mathrm F}^{\star}(\delta)=R_n^\star(\delta)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
L_{n,\mathrm F}^{\star}(\delta,\alpha)=L_n^\star(\delta,\alpha).
\]
Here \(R_n^\star(\delta)\) is the infimum, over observed-measurable estimators with values in \([0,1]\), of the worst-case absolute-error risk over \(P\in\mathcal M\) for the observed clamp target at threshold \(\delta\), and \(L_n^\star(\delta,\alpha)\) is the infimum, over uniformly honest observed-measurable confidence procedures at level \(1-\alpha\), of the worst-case expected interval length over \(P\in\mathcal M\).
\end{propositionv}

\Cref{obj:prop:observed-margin-surjectivity} establishes the decision-theoretic equivalence needed for causal transport. Every observed law in \(\mathcal M\) has a full-data representative in \(\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}\), and the risk and honest-length criteria agree at each fixed threshold. Thus lower bounds and attainable procedures for the observed clamp target can be read as statements about the causal clamp mean on the declared full-data class.

One technical convention remains for comparing expected lengths. The next definition fixes the order-preserving way real bounds are read inside the extended nonnegative reals.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_11}[Real-to-extended embedding]
Under the notation and hypotheses of the objects introduced earlier in the paper, a real number \(t\in\mathbb R\) is embedded into the extended nonnegative reals by
\[
t\longmapsto \max\{t,0\}\in[0,\infty]\;.
\]
The image is finite and equals the positive part of \(t\), so real expected-length bounds are compared with extended-real expected lengths through this embedding.
\end{definitionv}

The embedding in \cref{obj:synth_11} lets the real constants in the rate bounds be compared with honest expected lengths when those lengths are represented in the extended nonnegative-real order. This is the scale used in the final causal length statement.

The main causal conclusion now follows by combining the bridge identity, observed-margin surjectivity, and the observed-data minimax and honest-interval results.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:causal-frontier-lift}[Causal frontier lift]
Let \(J\in\mathbb N\), and let \(\beta,\kappa,L,c_-,c_+,p_{\min},\bar\delta,\alpha\in\mathbb R\). Suppose that
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Regime constants.)} The constants satisfy
\[
0<J,\qquad
\beta>0,\qquad
\kappa\geq0,\qquad
L>0,\qquad
c_->0,\qquad
c_-\leq \kappa+1\leq c_+,
\]
\[
p_{\min}>0,\qquad
p_{\min}\leq \frac1J,\qquad
0<\bar\delta<1,\qquad
0<\alpha<\frac12 .
\]
\item \textbf{(Threshold sequence.)} The deterministic thresholds \((\delta_n)_{n\in\mathbb N}\) satisfy
\[
0\leq \delta_n\leq \bar\delta
\]
for every \(n\in\mathbb N\).
\item \textbf{(Split blocks.)} For each \(n\in\mathbb N\), \(B_n=(I_{0,n},I_{1,n},I_{2,n})\) is a deterministic three-way split with pairwise disjoint blocks and
\[
\left\lfloor \frac n4\right\rfloor\leq |I_{0,n}|,\qquad
\left\lfloor \frac n4\right\rfloor\leq |I_{1,n}|,\qquad
\left\lfloor \frac n4\right\rfloor\leq |I_{2,n}|.
\]
\end{itemize}
Let \(h_n\), the information-balance bandwidth for \(\delta_n\), be as in \cref{obj:def:bandwidth}, and let
\[
r_n=n^{-1/2}+\delta_n^{\kappa+1}h_n^\beta
\]
be the frontier rate from \cref{obj:def:frontier}. Then there exist constants \(c,C\in\mathbb R\), with \(0<c<C\), depending only on these regime constants, such that, for every threshold sequence and every deterministic split-block sequence satisfying the preceding conditions, all sufficiently large \(n\) satisfy the following conclusions.

The total-Gram estimator \(\widehat\theta_n\) in \cref{obj:def:total-gram-estimator}, formed with split \(B_n\), order \(\ell=\lceil\beta\rceil-1\), bandwidth \(h_n\), and threshold \(\delta_n\), is observed-sample measurable. The corresponding bias-aware interval \(\mathrm{CI}_n\) in \cref{obj:def:honest-interval}, formed with the same split, order, bandwidth, and threshold, is an observed-sample measurable interval.

For the causal minimax criteria \(R_{n,\mathrm F}^\star\) and \(L_{n,\mathrm F}^\star\) in \cref{obj:def:causal-frontier-criteria},
\[
c r_n\leq R_{n,\mathrm F}^\star
\leq
\sup_{P^{\mathrm F}\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}}
\mathbb E_{(P^{\mathrm F})^{\otimes n}}
\left|\widehat\theta_n-\psi_{\delta_n}(P^{\mathrm F})\right|
\leq C r_n,
\]
\[
\inf_{P^{\mathrm F}\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}}
(P^{\mathrm F})^{\otimes n}\{\psi_{\delta_n}(P^{\mathrm F})\in \mathrm{CI}_n\}
\geq 1-\alpha,
\]
\[
\sup_{P^{\mathrm F}\in\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}}
\mathbb E_{(P^{\mathrm F})^{\otimes n}}\operatorname{len}(\mathrm{CI}_n)
\leq C r_n.
\]
Moreover, in the extended nonnegative-real order, after embedding the nonnegative real bounds into \(\mathbb R_{\geq0}\cup\{\infty\}\),
\[
c r_n\leq L_{n,\mathrm F}^\star\leq C r_n .
\]
\end{theoremv}

\Cref{obj:thm:causal-frontier-lift} transports the observed minimax and honest-length frontier to the causal clamp mean on \(\mathcal M^{\mathrm F}\). The same total-Gram estimator and the same bias-aware interval remain observed-sample procedures, and their guarantees hold uniformly over the full-data class because \cref{obj:prop:causal-bridge} aligns the target while \cref{obj:prop:observed-margin-surjectivity} aligns the induced decision criteria. The rate is therefore the same \(r_n\) characterized in \cref{obj:def:frontier,obj:thm:phase-diagram}: root-\(n\) and boundary-driven regimes carry over with the causal interpretation supplied by the full-data lift.
