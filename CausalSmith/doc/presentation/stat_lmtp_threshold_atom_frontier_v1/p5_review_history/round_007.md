# Referee review

**Recommendation:** major_revision
**Overall score:** 6.1/10 — The paper delivers a potentially important and carefully verified rate characterization, but the manuscript requires substantial repair to its exact-modulus proposal, verification metadata, organization, and evidence for finite-sample performance.

The paper characterizes minimax absolute risk and uniformly honest expected length for a deterministic lower-threshold clamp under polynomial treatment-density thinning, and transports the result to a precisely defined causal class. The principal frontier and phase diagram are novel and potentially useful, while the continuity-only comparison clarifies the role of quantitative smoothness. The verified central results support the main contribution, but the exact-modulus discussion is presently miscalibrated to the full target, and extensive duplication and stale reproducibility information prevent publication in the current form.

## Strengths
- The paper isolates the atom-regression product created by the deterministic clamp and derives a clear two-component minimax frontier.
- The matching risk and honest-length results, including separate global-shift and localized perturbation experiments, form a coherent theoretical contribution.
- The phase diagram distinguishes endpoint, moving-interior, fixed-threshold, and zero-threshold geometries in an economically interpretable way.
- The causal claims are scoped carefully to the declared full-data classes and are supported by target and decision-criterion equivalences.
- The related-work section engages the closest degenerate-design and honest-inference antecedents with explicit rate comparisons.

## Findings
- **[major·structure] Discussion, limitations, and open questions** — The proposed exact-modulus interval is not calibrated to the full clamp target even though the text says that it provides “a candidate honest interval” and “a fully specified sample map.” Its radius is the supremum of unweighted stratumwise regression radii; it omits the retained-mean radius, atom-mass uncertainty, atom weights, and aggregation across strata. It also assigns radius zero when the affine constraints are infeasible. The zero-threshold regime exposes the mismatch especially clearly: the target is an ordinary mean with rate n^{-1/2}, whereas the proposed radius continues to solve a local-regression problem whose natural scale is h_n^β. Consequently, the sharp-calibration property posed for regimes including phase ratio q=0 is not a credible property of the displayed interval.
  - *Fix:* Either remove the exact-modulus interval and sharp-calibration remark, or redefine the modulus for the complete clamp functional. A retained version should incorporate the regular empirical components, atom weights and their uncertainty, stratum aggregation, and a valid singular-design fallback; the sharp-constant question must then be reformulated for that corrected object.
- **[major·prose] Proofs and verification note** — The reproducibility record is stale. The manuscript states that the checks were run at commit 9e7bb3e56f80e1f7f02213898a320461d7886359, while the authoritative verification contract is for commit 6f11a039165a75921bbb6499f3d9bf40f3f404f8. The claims about the archived source, manifest, absence of added axioms, and reproduced checks therefore do not presently identify the artifact that supports this submission.
  - *Fix:* Regenerate the verification note, crosswalk, audit receipts, and archive information from commit 6f11a039165a75921bbb6499f3d9bf40f3f404f8, or regenerate the verification contract from the exact archived manuscript commit. Ensure that every toolchain, no-sorry, and axiom-audit claim refers to that single immutable state.
- **[major·structure] Setup and assumptions** — The manuscript says “Each object is defined once,” but repeatedly gives full second definitions of central objects. The environment titled “Observed continuity class” defines both the Hölder class and the continuity class before the canonical model definitions; later environments likewise duplicate the clamp target, estimator, and interval. This makes it difficult to know which formulation is authoritative and substantially obscures the statistical argument.
  - *Fix:* Retain one canonical environment for each model, target, estimator, interval, and criterion. Replace presentation-synthesized duplicates with one- or two-sentence orientation text and cross-references. In particular, move the continuity class to the continuity-only section and remove the duplicate definition of the Hölder class from that environment.
- **[major·other] Discussion, limitations, and open questions** — The paper proposes a fairly elaborate split-sample estimator and stabilization rule but supplies no numerical evidence about fallback frequency, coverage, or expected length. This matters because the publication case rests on a usable honest procedure, while the proved rate bounds deliberately suppress constants and the manuscript itself identifies moderate-sample behavior as unresolved.
  - *Fix:* Add a reproducible simulation study spanning regular, critical, atom-dominated, fixed-positive, and near-zero thresholds. Report fallback probabilities, bias, absolute risk, coverage, and length, with comparisons to the unstabilized local-polynomial construction and appropriate regular-policy benchmarks where the estimands are comparable.
- **[minor·statement] Main statistical results** — The minimax-risk theorem writes the sample-dependent risk as E_P[|hatθ−θ|], although the experiment and verified result use the n-fold product law. This is a conspicuous ambiguity in the central theorem.
  - *Fix:* Replace that expectation by E_{P^{⊗n}} and use the same product-law notation consistently in the theorem, proof, and surrounding discussion.
- **[minor·statement] Main statistical results** — The zero-threshold item says the stabilized risk and length are “both asymptotic to n^{-1/2},” while the verified conclusion is order equivalence (AsympSeq), not convergence of their ratios to one. In standard asymptotic terminology, “asymptotic to” overstates the result.
  - *Fix:* Write “are both of order n^{-1/2}” or display the relation with asymp notation.
- **[minor·statement] Main statistical results** — The displayed minimax-risk definitions omit the estimator range restriction used by the verified criteria and by the proof, which restrict estimators to [0,1]. Clipping makes the two formulations equivalent for this target, but that equivalence is currently implicit.
  - *Fix:* State the [0,1]-valued restriction in every risk criterion, or add a short projection argument establishing that unrestricted estimators yield the same minimax value.
- **[minor·prose] Related work** — The sentence “The present paper does not resolve that question” violates the affirmative contribution-framing contract outside a labeled limitations or open-questions section.
  - *Fix:* Write affirmatively that the reported frontier is the known-class benchmark with β, L, and κ fixed, and move the unresolved adaptation question to the labeled Open questions paragraph.
- **[minor·citation] Proofs of the main results** — The proof writes “By citep{Kallenberg2002Foundations}, Theorem 6.3,” separating the locator from the citation command and producing awkward or malformed citation prose at the exact use of the external dependency.
  - *Fix:* Use the external-source form “By citet[Theorem 6.3]{Kallenberg2002Foundations}” and preserve the same locator at every direct invocation.
- **[minor·prose] Causal interpretation through full-data lifts** — The manuscript alternates between expectations under the full-data product law and under the product law of the observed margin. These are equivalent for observed-sample procedures, but the notation changes between the Hölder and continuity-only sections and burdens the reader.
  - *Fix:* Choose one convention—preferably the observed-margin product law—and state once that it equals the expectation induced by independent full-data draws for every observed-sample-measurable procedure.
- **[nit·prose] abstract** — The abstract's third sentence carries endpoint geometry, fixed-threshold geometry, continuity-only behavior, and the zero-threshold identity in one long construction, making the contribution unusually difficult to parse.
  - *Fix:* Split the sentence into two or three shorter sentences separating the Hölder geometry from the continuity-only comparison.

## Questions for authors
- Is the exact-modulus object intended to optimize uncertainty for each threshold regression alone, or for the complete clamp functional? The current interval and sharp-calibration question conflate those two tasks.
- Which immutable repository commit and archive are intended to be the evidentiary artifact for this submission?
- Can the authors provide finite-sample evidence that the total-Gram fallback is rare and that the advertised interval has useful length at sample sizes relevant to continuous-treatment applications?
