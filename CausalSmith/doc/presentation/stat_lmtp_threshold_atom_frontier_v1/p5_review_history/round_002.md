# Referee review

**Recommendation:** major_revision
**Overall score:** 6.2/10 — The verified minimax frontier is a credible and potentially publishable contribution, but substantial problems in positioning, verification-scope wording, notation, duplication, and the sharp-constant discussion prevent the manuscript from earning publication as written.

The paper characterizes minimax absolute risk and uniformly honest expected length for an exact continuous-treatment threshold clamp under finite strata, bounded outcomes, Hölder regression, and a global polynomial density envelope, and it derives a complementary continuity-only frontier. The matched rates, phase diagram, total-Gram stabilization, and exact transport to the declared causal class form a coherent core contribution. The manuscript nevertheless requires major revision because it does not adequately distinguish this contribution from the closest recent LMTP and support-violation literature, contains stale or inconsistent descriptions of the formal parameterization and verification scope, and devotes extensive space to a sharp-constant candidate whose radius does not match the target decomposition it is said to represent.

## Strengths
- The central observed-data contribution is precise: minimax absolute risk and minimax honest expected length have the matched frontier n^{-1/2} plus the atom-weighted boundary-regression term.
- The phase diagram usefully separates the bandwidth edge from the risk transition and covers regular, critical, vanishing atom-dominated, fixed-positive, and zero-threshold regimes.
- The estimator and interval are totalized on empty or singular local designs, addressing a real random-design issue that is often suppressed in asymptotic presentations.
- The continuity-only result identifies an explicit threshold-atom frontier and elbow rather than invoking generic nonadaptation arguments alone.
- The causal claims are generally confined to a clearly declared full-data class, and the manuscript correctly presents the causal decision problem as an exact transport of the observed experiment.

## Findings
- **[major·citation] Related work** — The dedicated section cites broad LMTP, dose-response, degenerate-design, and honest-inference lineages but does not engage several closest contemporary comparators described in the available literature context. In particular, it does not compare the exact-clamp result with Koo et al.'s support-violation decomposition, the 2026 natural-history LMTP results, recent conditional or functional MTP work, or the modulus-based literature of Donoho–Liu and Cai–Low. The section mostly categorizes prior work instead of comparing estimands, overlap conditions, rates, and inferential guarantees.
  - *Fix:* Add a focused comparison subsection or table stating, for each closest paper, its estimand, support or smoothness conditions, inferential result, and the precise distinction supplied here: an exact deterministic clamp with a moving Dirac component under a global power envelope. Explicitly characterize the relationship to support-restricted stochastic targets and Lipschitz extrapolation methods without attributing conclusions about their estimators that this paper does not establish.
- **[major·prose] Discussion, limitations, and open questions** — The sharp-constant candidate is not aligned with the target whose length it is supposed to calibrate. The proposed radius is the supremum over strata of an unweighted local-regression modulus, whereas the clamp target combines a retained mean, estimated atom masses, and a sum of atom-weighted regression terms. The later sentence claiming that the target-level question uses “an interval whose radius accounts for the retained mean, atom masses, and atom-weighted threshold regressions” does not describe the displayed candidate. A single constant shared across every regime limit is also asserted as the natural question without explaining why regime-specific constants should coincide.
  - *Fix:* Either remove the explicit candidate and state a concise open problem about constructing the full target-level modulus, or redefine the radius to include the retained-mean, atom-mass, atom-weight, and cross-stratum components and allow the limiting constant to depend on the regime parameter unless a reason for universality is supplied. Keep the zero-totalization issue explicitly separated from any honesty or optimality claim.
- **[major·prose] Proofs and verification note** — The sentence “Lean machine-checks each delivered theorem, proposition, lemma, definition, and algorithm declaration under its displayed assumptions and definitions” overstates the supplied verification record. Several displayed definitions are marked presentation-synthesized and have no associated Lean declaration or statement audit, even when they restate checked underlying objects. The author footnote similarly refers collectively to “displayed formal statements and proofs” as Lean-verified.
  - *Fix:* State exactly which named declarations are machine-checked, distinguish presentation-synthesized restatements from checked declarations, and explain that proof-faithfulness audits are separate from kernel checking. Retain the theorem-local disclosures for the conditional-distribution and Hoeffding inputs.
- **[major·prose] Setup and assumptions** — The manuscript treats the local-polynomial degree as an independently fixed oracle input: “The procedures are class-indexed in the usual oracle sense: J, β, ℓ, L, ... are ... available.” The current extracted declarations instead use ℓ = ceil(β) - 1 through ellOf beta, and the causal frontier theorem eventually states that choice explicitly. The Hölder assumption and model definition therefore leave readers uncertain whether ℓ is a free class parameter or a function of β.
  - *Fix:* Define ℓ = ceil(β) - 1 once before the Hölder condition, use that convention throughout all model and procedure definitions, and remove ℓ from the list of independently known oracle constants. If independent choice is intended, a corresponding formal result would be required.
- **[major·statement] Main statistical results** — The displayed minimax theorem writes the concrete sample risk as an expectation under P, even though the estimator is a function of n observations and the verified criterion uses the product sample law. This conflicts with the consistent use of P^{⊗n} elsewhere and makes the central displayed inequality formally ambiguous.
  - *Fix:* Replace the expectation in the upper-bound chain of the minimax theorem by expectation under P^{⊗n}, and check every observed and full-data risk display for the same sampling-law convention.
- **[major·structure] global** — The paper repeatedly defines the same objects in parallel environments: the model classes, clamp target, estimator, interval, and split-indexed versions are each presented more than once. The appendix then reproduces a very large collection of low-level algebraic and measure-theoretic lemmas before supplying another main-proof section. This obscures the economic and statistical argument and makes the paper read like an artifact dump rather than a journal article.
  - *Fix:* Retain one reader-facing definition for each object, move split-indexed notation into short conventions, and transfer low-level verification lemmas and full proof transcripts to an online supplement. The main paper should present the target decomposition, estimator, core rate arguments, phase diagram, causal bridge, and continuity comparison in a single narrative.
- **[minor·statement] Main statistical results** — The zero-threshold bullet says the stabilized risk and expected length are “both asymptotic to n^{-1/2},” while the verified declaration establishes two-sided order equivalence rather than convergence of the ratio to one. The proof itself concludes with asymptotic comparability.
  - *Fix:* Replace “asymptotic to” by “of order n^{-1/2}” or display the asymptotic-comparability symbol.
- **[minor·statement] Main statistical results** — The reader-facing definition of minimax risk says the infimum ranges over all observed-sample measurable estimators, whereas the extracted formal criterion restricts estimators to values in [0,1]. Clipping should make the two criteria equal for this target, but that equivalence is not stated at the definition.
  - *Fix:* Either restrict the displayed infimum to [0,1]-valued estimators, matching the formal criterion, or add a short clipping observation establishing equality with the unrestricted criterion.
- **[minor·prose] Setup and assumptions** — The sentence “adaptive estimation of smoothness or thinning parameters is treated as a separate limitation” places a non-coverage statement outside the explicitly titled limitations portion, contrary to the manuscript's affirmative contribution-framing contract.
  - *Fix:* Remove this sentence from the setup and keep the non-coverage statement in the Limitations paragraph. In the setup, describe the procedures affirmatively as oracle procedures indexed by the listed class constants.
- **[minor·prose] Causal interpretation through full-data lifts** — The causal lift is technically exact but its substantive content is underexplained. Because the full-data class is constructed to be onto the observed model, the equality of decision criteria is primarily an experiment-equivalence result rather than an independently new causal frontier. Calling it the “third contribution” without emphasizing this role risks giving it disproportionate novelty weight.
  - *Fix:* Describe the lift affirmatively as a structural interpretation and exact transport theorem for the declared class, and explain why simultaneous consistency, conditional exchangeability, and response-mean continuity are the exact bridge conditions. Reserve the primary novelty claim for the observed threshold-atom frontier.
- **[nit·prose] abstract** — The thinning exponent κ appears in the density envelope and rate before receiving a plain-language first-use gloss.
  - *Fix:* Write, for example, “with thinning exponent κ” at its first appearance.

## Questions for authors
- Is the polynomial degree intended to be fixed as ceil(β) - 1 everywhere, or do the authors intend a theorem uniform over an independently chosen degree?
- Is the sharp-constant section intended to propose a full clamp-target modulus or merely a local-regression building block? The current formulas implement the latter while the prose describes the former.
- Can the authors explain which recent support-violation or natural-value-policy result is the closest substantive comparator and state explicitly how its target and assumptions differ from the exact clamp experiment?
- Do the authors have finite-sample evidence showing how often the Gram fallback is used and how conservative the proposed honest interval is across the edge and critical regimes?
