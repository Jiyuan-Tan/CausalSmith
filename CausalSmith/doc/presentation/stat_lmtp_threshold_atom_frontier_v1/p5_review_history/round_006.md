# Referee review

**Recommendation:** major_revision
**Overall score:** 7/10 — The paper delivers a technically strong and apparently novel matched frontier, but publication requires substantial improvements in organization, reproducibility, finite-sample evidence, and compliance with the stated prose and cross-reference conventions.

The paper characterizes minimax absolute risk and uniformly honest expected length for a deterministic continuous-treatment clamp whose pushforward distribution contains a threshold atom. Its strongest contributions are the matched Hölder frontier, the threshold phase diagram, the total-Gram stabilization, and the exact transport of the observed decision problem to a carefully defined causal class; the continuity-only frontier is also useful. The verified results support these central claims, but the manuscript is substantially longer and more repetitive than necessary, and the inferential proposal needs accessible reproducibility materials and finite-sample evaluation before it is ready for publication.

## Strengths
- The paper identifies a clear statistical mechanism: the clamp target combines a regular retained mean with an atom-weighted boundary-regression value.
- The matched risk and honest-length frontier is stated with explicit threshold, smoothness, thinning, and random-design conditions.
- The phase diagram distinguishes the bandwidth edge from the risk transition and includes fixed-positive and zero-threshold regimes.
- The total-Gram construction handles empty and singular realized local designs explicitly, a useful contribution for honest random-design inference.
- The causal claims are appropriately derived through an exact target and decision-problem equivalence on a declared full-data class rather than presented as a separate unrestricted frontier.
- The continuity-only theorem isolates the value of a quantitative Hölder modulus and gives an explicit threshold-atom elbow.

## Findings
- **[major·structure] global** — The manuscript repeatedly presents the same objects in canonical and synthesized forms, including the model classes, target, estimator, interval, risk criteria, and split-indexed aliases. The main line is consequently buried beneath formalization-oriented restatements and many theorem parameters that play no substantive role in the particular result.
  - *Fix:* Retain one canonical definition for each object, introduce a single standing regime-and-sampling condition, and move duplicate aliases, low-level coercivity and integration lemmas, and full declaration-style statements to an online supplement. Keep concise proof sketches and the principal theorems in the paper, with a short map to the verification supplement.
- **[major·prose] appendix** — The proofs contain pervasive manual directional cross-references such as “Step 1,” “the preceding item,” “the preceding display,” and “the first step.” These violate the manuscript's exclusive cleveref contract and become fragile when the long proofs are edited.
  - *Fix:* Give referenced proof steps and equations stable non-object labels and use only \cref or \Cref, or rewrite the argument so each local inference is self-contained without a directional reference. Audit the entire source for manual references, including references to numbered items and equations.
- **[major·other] verification note** — The reproducibility information currently supplies a commit hash, Lean version, and architecture, while publication-grade verification also requires an accessible artifact and executable instructions that connect the manuscript anchors to the checked declarations.
  - *Fix:* Archive the exact source publicly, provide a persistent URL or DOI, document checkout and build commands from a clean environment, list all nonstandard dependencies, and include a machine-readable anchor-to-declaration manifest. Verify that the archived commit reproduces the reported checks.
- **[major·other] main statistical results** — The finite-sample case for the proposed estimator and intervals currently rests on asymptotic theory and a one-cell analytic calibration. The paper's practical claims center on singular-design stabilization and honest coverage, both of which can behave materially differently before the eventual regime.
  - *Fix:* Add a focused simulation study spanning regular, critical, atom-dominated, fixed-threshold, and near-zero regimes. Report risk, coverage, expected length, frequency of Gram fallback, sensitivity to thinning and smoothness, and comparisons with unstabilized local polynomial and regular LMTP-style benchmarks where their targets and conditions permit a fair comparison.
- **[minor·prose] abstract** — The sentence “the lower tail collapses to an atom whose mean must be learned from a shrinking one-sided neighborhood” is broader than the delivered procedures: local learning describes the Hölder construction with a positive atom, whereas the continuity-only procedure uses a bounded fallback and the zero-threshold atom vanishes.
  - *Fix:* Write affirmatively and conditionally, for example: “In the Hölder model, a positive lower-tail atom weights a threshold regression value estimated from a one-sided neighborhood; the continuity-only analysis instead controls that contribution through its mass and the bounded outcome range.”
- **[minor·prose] setup and assumptions** — The explanation of the power envelope conflates its roles: “The lower bound supplies the local window mass needed for Gram coercivity and for the least-favorable localized perturbation, while the upper bound controls the same window mass and the lower-tail atom in upper-bound calculations.” The verified arguments use the lower envelope for local-information and atom lower bounds, the upper envelope for upper-risk quantities, and a specifically chosen canonical density in the lower experiments.
  - *Fix:* Separate the roles explicitly: identify which envelope side controls local counts, Gram coercivity, atom upper and lower bounds, and likelihood divergence, and state that the lower experiments use a canonical density admitted by the envelope.
- **[minor·citation] related work** — The positioning gives useful rate comparisons with Gaïffas and discusses Low and Armstrong–Kolesár, but the closest adaptation and modulus antecedents receive limited engagement. In particular, Gaïffas's adaptive degenerate-design result and the Donoho–Liu and Cai–Low modulus literature are directly relevant to the paper's oracle assumptions and sharp-constant program.
  - *Fix:* Add a concise comparison stating the adaptive degenerate-design rate and logarithmic payment, explain how atom weighting changes that question, and relate the honest-length lower bound and open modulus problem to the classical within-class and between-class modulus frameworks.
- **[minor·prose] main statistical results** — The discussion calls the fixed-positive-threshold result “the usual Hölder point-estimation scale for the boundary regression contribution.” At a fixed positive threshold the target point is interior to the treatment support, although the proposed estimator uses a one-sided window, so “boundary regression” can mislead readers about the relevant design geometry.
  - *Fix:* Distinguish the endpoint-thinning regime from one-sided estimation at a fixed interior threshold, and explain that the fixed-threshold exponent is the ordinary positive-density pointwise Hölder rate multiplied by an atom weight bounded away from zero.
- **[minor·structure] main statistical results** — The phase table uses the critical and edge scales before displaying their formulas in the following theorem, which weakens the table's intended stand-alone orientation.
  - *Fix:* Display both scale definitions immediately before the table or include them in the caption, and add a column distinguishing treatment-support geometry from the resulting risk-dominance regime.

## Questions for authors
- Where will the exact Lean source and reproducible build environment be permanently archived?
- How frequently does the total-Gram fallback occur at sample sizes relevant to the intended applications across the stated threshold regimes?
- Is the primary intended audience theoretical econometricians studying nonregular policy functionals, or practitioners using modified-treatment-policy software, and how will the presentation and numerical evidence be calibrated to that audience?
