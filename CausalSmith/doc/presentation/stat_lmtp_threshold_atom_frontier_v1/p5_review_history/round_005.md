# Referee review

**Recommendation:** major_revision
**Overall score:** 6.4/10 — The paper delivers a technically strong and carefully verified minimax characterization, but its publication case is weakened by an incoherent sharp-constant formulation, understated oracle requirements, incomplete competitor engagement, and excessive repetition.

The paper characterizes minimax absolute risk and uniformly honest expected length for a deterministic lower-threshold clamp with continuous treatment, including a phase diagram, a stabilized local-polynomial procedure, an exact causal transport result for the declared full-data class, and a continuity-only frontier. These are substantive theoretical contributions, and the principal prose claims generally track the verified statements. Major revision is needed to sharpen the practical and literature positioning, streamline the presentation, disclose the procedure's oracle inputs prominently, and replace the current local-modulus sharp-constant discussion with a target-aligned formulation.

## Strengths
- The matched risk and honest-length frontier is a clear contribution for a policy-induced atom whose weight and regression difficulty move jointly with the threshold.
- The phase diagram distinguishes the bandwidth edge from the minimax-risk transition and includes useful fixed- and zero-threshold benchmarks.
- The total-Gram construction is fully defined on empty or singular realized designs, addressing a genuine gap that is often hidden in local-polynomial descriptions.
- The causal claims are appropriately confined to a declared full-data class and transported through exact target and decision-problem equalities rather than presented as an independently novel causal frontier.
- The continuity-only result cleanly quantifies the value of a Hölder modulus through the comparison between the atom-mass and smoothed atom terms.
- The verification contract supports the formal statements and records the published conditional-distribution and concentration inputs at their points of use.

## Findings
- **[major·structure] Discussion, limitations, and open questions** — The proposed sharp-constant normalization is not aligned with the full clamp target. The quantity W_{n,B_n} is an unweighted local threshold-regression radius, whereas the target contains a retained-mean component and atom-weighted, cross-stratum regression components. The mismatch is especially visible at δ_n=0: the verified phase theorem gives θ_0(P)=E_P[Y] and r_n=n^{-1/2}, while W_{n,B_n} continues to measure the difficulty of a local boundary-regression problem that has zero target weight. Consequently, the three universal matching limits involving 2W_{n,B_n} lack a coherent target-level basis, as the surrounding prose itself partly acknowledges.
  - *Fix:* Either recast W_{n,B_n} explicitly as a modulus for the local-regression subproblem and delete the proposed universal equivalence with L_n^star, or formulate a target-level modulus that includes retained-mean uncertainty, empirical atom-mass uncertainty, atom weights, and cross-stratum aggregation. Treat zero, regular, critical, atom-dominated, and fixed-threshold regimes separately unless a common normalization is established.
- **[major·prose] abstract** — The abstract presents the estimator and honest interval as procedures that attain the frontier without stating that they are class-indexed oracle procedures requiring the fixed smoothness, thinning, envelope, and related constants. That qualification appears much later in the setup and limitations, although it materially determines what the inference result delivers.
  - *Fix:* State affirmatively in the abstract and first contribution paragraph that, for fixed known class constants and deterministic thresholds and splits, the proposed procedure attains the stated risk and honest-length bounds. Preserve the later discussion of adaptation as a limitations item.
- **[major·citation] Related work** — The section identifies broad literatures but does not compare the paper systematically with several closest natural-value-policy and adaptive nonparametric results available to the authors. The sentence "this paper therefore studies minimax risk and uniformly honest expected length for the unsmoothed clamp functional rather than regular inference for a stochastic, support-restricted, extrapolated, or effect-modifier target" also groups technically different competitors under an overly broad characterization. The treatment of recent LMTP survival, mediation, simultaneous-inference, heterogeneous-nudge, functional-treatment, survey, and degenerate-design adaptation work is incomplete.
  - *Fix:* Add a concise comparison organized by estimand, post-policy law, support condition, smoothness/design assumptions, inference criterion, and rate. Engage the closest results individually, including the recent LMTP extensions and Gaïffas's adaptive procedure, and replace the umbrella characterization of all neighboring targets as regular inference with source-specific descriptions of what each paper proves.
- **[major·structure] global** — The manuscript is substantially longer and more repetitive than needed for its statistical contribution. The model classes, target, estimator, and interval are each stated canonically and then restated in presentation-synthesized environments; regime conditions recur in nearly every theorem and lemma; and the appendix reproduces lengthy proof narratives whose code-oriented bookkeeping obscures the mathematical ideas.
  - *Fix:* Retain one canonical definition for each object, replace duplicate synthesized environments with short notation sentences, centralize the standing regime assumptions, and move the detailed verification-oriented proof transcripts to a supplementary artifact. Keep in the paper concise proofs or proof sketches emphasizing the statistical reductions, lower experiments, design concentration, and causal transport.
- **[major·other] Main statistical results** — The paper proposes a highly conservative sample-split interval with oracle bias constants, Gram cutoffs, clipping, and singular-design fallbacks, but supplies no finite-sample evidence about coverage, expected length, fallback frequency, or the numerical size of its constants. The one-cell calculation illustrates exponents but does not evaluate the proposed estimator or interval.
  - *Fix:* Add a Monte Carlo study spanning the edge, critical, atom-dominated, fixed-threshold, and zero-threshold regimes. Report uniform-coverage stress tests, expected length, point risk, Gram-fallback frequency, and sensitivity to L, κ, and the envelope constants; include transparent comparisons with natural local-polynomial and retained-mean baselines.
- **[minor·other] Proofs and verification note** — The commit hash and Lean version are useful, but the manuscript gives no stable repository or archive location, dependency lock information, or exact command that reproduces the kernel checks. A commit identifier alone is not an actionable reproducibility record without access to the corresponding source.
  - *Fix:* Provide a persistent repository or archival DOI, identify the relevant Lean project and lock files, and give the exact build or verification command plus the expected successful output.
- **[minor·prose] Introduction** — The sentence "That simple operation produces a statistical problem with both regular and nonregular components" uses technical regularity terminology beyond what the stated results establish. The paper proves rate decompositions and later says that "regular thresholds" is solely a rate-regime label; it does not state a pathwise-differentiability, LAN, or asymptotic-linearity classification.
  - *Fix:* Replace this sentence with the proved distinction, such as: "The operation produces a root-n bounded-mean component and an atom-weighted boundary-regression component." Use "regular" elsewhere only with the paper's explicit rate-regime meaning.
- **[minor·prose] Related work** — Several passages frame the contribution through excluded alternatives outside a limitations section, including "rather than regular inference for a stochastic, support-restricted, extrapolated, or effect-modifier target." Similar negative phrasing appears in the phase discussion through "without asserting" and "rather than convergence of the ratio to one." This conflicts with the required affirmative contribution framing.
  - *Fix:* Describe the delivered scope directly: state that the estimand is the unsmoothed deterministic clamp with a moving Dirac component, and state that the relevant asymptotic notation means two-sided rate comparability. Move genuine non-coverage statements to the limitations paragraph.
- **[minor·prose] global** — The manuscript repeatedly uses reader-visible \leanref commands for formal experiments and symbols, although the cross-reference contract requires cleveref exclusively for reader-facing cross-references.
  - *Fix:* Render ordinary symbols as plain mathematics and replace references to numbered manuscript objects with \cref or \Cref using their exact labels. Remove reader-facing \leanref links unless they are mechanically generated verification disclosures covered by an explicit exemption.
- **[nit·statement] Setup and assumptions** — The term "three-way split" conventionally suggests an exhaustive partition, but the formal object requires only three pairwise-disjoint subsets of sufficient size and allows unused observations. Some theorem prose calls it a "split of the sample indices," which may reinforce the partition interpretation.
  - *Fix:* Use "three deterministic disjoint blocks" consistently, or explicitly state that remaining observations may be unused.

## Questions for authors
- Which empirical settings motivate thresholds near the critical and edge scales, and what ranges of the oracle class constants are plausible there?
- How can the proposed W_{n,B_n} normalization represent target uncertainty at δ_n=0 when the clamp target has zero atom weight but W_{n,B_n} remains a local-regression radius?
- Will the Lean sources and a one-command reproduction environment be deposited in a persistent public archive?
