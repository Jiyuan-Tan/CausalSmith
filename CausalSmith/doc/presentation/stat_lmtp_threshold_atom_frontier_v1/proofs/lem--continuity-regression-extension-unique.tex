\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:continuity-regression-extension-unique}]
Fix \(x\in\mathcal X\).  The two regression-version assumptions imply
\[
\mu_1(X,A)=\mu_2(X,A)
\quad P\text{-a.s.}
\]% lean: cont_regression_extension_unique
Define functions on the real line by
\[
f_r(a)=
\begin{cases}
\mu_r(x,a),&a\in[0,\bar\delta],\\
0,&a\notin[0,\bar\delta],
\end{cases}
\qquad r=1,2.
\]% lean: cont_regression_extension_unique
The continuity hypotheses make these functions measurable.  Set
\[
B=\{a\in\mathbb R:f_1(a)\neq f_2(a)\}.
\]% lean: cont_regression_extension_unique
Then \(B\) is measurable and
\[
B\subset[0,\bar\delta]\subset[0,1].
\]% lean: cont_regression_extension_unique
The almost-sure equality of the two versions gives
\[
P\{X=x,\ A\in B\}=0.
\]% lean: cont_regression_extension_unique
The conditional-density identity in \cref{obj:ass:conditional-density-law} applies to this measurable subset of \([0,1]\), so
\[
0=P\{X=x,\ A\in B\}
=
p_x\int_B\pi_x(a)\,da.
\]% lean: CondDensityLaw
The stratum-mass condition in \cref{obj:ass:stratum-mass}, together with \(p_{\min}>0\), gives \(p_x>0\).  Hence
\[
\int_B\pi_x(a)\,da=0.
\]% lean: StratumMass

It remains to justify the zero-integral argument on \(B\).  The conditional-density measurability in \cref{obj:ass:conditional-density-law} gives the needed measurability on the restricted set.  Since \(B\subset[0,1]\), the set \(B\) has finite Lebesgue measure.  For Lebesgue-a.e. \(a\in B\), the nonnegativity in \cref{obj:ass:conditional-density-law}, the upper envelope in \cref{obj:ass:polynomial-thinning}, and the design inequalities \(0\leq\kappa\) and \(0\leq c_+\) give
\[
|\pi_x(a)|=\pi_x(a)
\leq c_+a^\kappa
\leq c_+,
\qquad 0\leq a\leq1 .
\]% lean: cont_regression_extension_unique
Thus \(a\mapsto\pi_x(a)\) is integrable on \(B\).  The nonnegativity part of \cref{obj:ass:conditional-density-law} and the zero integral therefore yield
\[
\pi_x(a)=0
\quad\text{for Lebesgue-a.e. }a\in B.
\]% lean: setIntegral_eq_zero_iff_of_nonneg_ae
The point \(a=0\) has Lebesgue measure zero.  For Lebesgue-a.e. \(a\in B\) with \(a\neq0\), the inclusion \(B\subset[0,1]\) gives \(a>0\).  The lower envelope in \cref{obj:ass:polynomial-thinning} and \(c_->0\) then give
\[
\pi_x(a)\geq c_-a^\kappa>0
\quad\text{for Lebesgue-a.e. }a\in B\setminus\{0\}.
\]% lean: PolynomialThinning
Combining this positivity with \(\pi_x=0\) Lebesgue-a.e. on \(B\) gives
\[
\operatorname{Leb}(B)=0.
\]% lean: cont_regression_extension_unique
Consequently \(f_1=f_2\) Lebesgue-a.e. on \(\mathbb R\), and the definitions of \(f_1\) and \(f_2\) give
\[
\mu_1(x,a)=\mu_2(x,a)
\quad\text{for Lebesgue-a.e. }a\in[0,\bar\delta].
\]% lean: cont_regression_extension_unique

Finally, both functions \(a\mapsto\mu_1(x,a)\) and \(a\mapsto\mu_2(x,a)\) are continuous on \([0,\bar\delta]\).  If they differed at some point of \([0,\bar\delta]\), continuity would make them differ on a nonempty relatively open interval in \([0,\bar\delta]\), which has positive Lebesgue measure, contradicting the almost-everywhere equality on \([0,\bar\delta]\).  Hence
\[
\mu_1(x,a)=\mu_2(x,a)
\qquad\text{for every }a\in[0,\bar\delta].
\]% lean: volume.eqOn_Icc_of_ae_eq
Since \(x\) was arbitrary, the conclusion holds in every stratum.
\end{proof}
