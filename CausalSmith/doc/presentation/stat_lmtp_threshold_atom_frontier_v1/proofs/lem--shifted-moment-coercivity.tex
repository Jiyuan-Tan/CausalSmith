\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:shifted-moment-coercivity}]
For \(z=(z_0,\ldots,z_\ell)\), set
\[
P_z(u)=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}z_i u^i
\]
and
\[
E_\kappa(z)
=
\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}
\frac{z_i z_j}{i+j+\kappa+1}.
\]
Since \(i+j+\kappa>-1\), expansion of the square and the identity
\(\int_0^1 u^{i+j+\kappa}\,du=(i+j+\kappa+1)^{-1}\) give
\[
E_\kappa(z)
=
\int_0^1 P_z(u)^2u^\kappa\,du .
\] % lean: powerPolynomialEnergy_eq_integral

If \(z\ne0\), then \(P_z\) is a nonzero polynomial. Its zero set in \((0,1)\) is finite, so choose \(u_0\in(0,1)\) with \(P_z(u_0)\ne0\). At this point
\(P_z(u_0)^2u_0^\kappa>0\), while the integrand is continuous and nonnegative on \([0,1]\). Hence
\[
E_\kappa(z)>0
\qquad (z\ne0).
\] % lean: powerPolynomialEnergy_pos

The map \(z\mapsto E_\kappa(z)\) is continuous, because it is a finite sum of quadratic coordinate terms. Let
\[
S=\{z\in\mathbb R^{\ell+1}:\|z\|_\infty=1\}.
\]
The set \(S\) is compact and nonempty, so \(E_\kappa\) attains its minimum on \(S\), say at \(z^\circ\in S\). Since \(z^\circ\ne0\), strict positivity of \(E_\kappa\) at nonzero vectors gives \(E_\kappa(z^\circ)>0\). Define
\[
c=\frac{E_\kappa(z^\circ)}{\ell+1}.
\]
Then \(c>0\). For \(z=0\), the desired bound against \(E_\kappa(z)\) is immediate. For \(z\ne0\), put
\[
r=\|z\|_\infty,
\qquad
v=r^{-1}z .
\]
Then \(v\in S\), \(E_\kappa(z)=r^2E_\kappa(v)\), and
\[
\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}z_j^2\le(\ell+1)r^2.
\]
Therefore
\[
c\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}z_j^2
\le
E_\kappa(z^\circ)r^2
\le
E_\kappa(v)r^2
=
E_\kappa(z).
\] % lean: powerPolynomialEnergy_coercive

It remains to compare this unshifted energy with the normalized shifted moment matrix. Fix \(\rho\ge0\), and define
\[
D_\rho=\int_0^1(\rho+u)^\kappa\,du,
\qquad
N_\rho(z)=\int_0^1P_z(u)^2(\rho+u)^\kappa\,du .
\]
By the definition of \(M_{\ell,\kappa,\rho}\),
\[
z^\top M_{\ell,\kappa,\rho}z
=
\frac{N_\rho(z)}{D_\rho}.
\] % lean: matrixQuadratic_refMomentMatrix_eq

The denominator is positive. Indeed, \((\rho+u)^\kappa\) is continuous and nonnegative on \([0,1]\), and it is strictly positive near \(u=1\). With
\[
R_\rho=(\rho+1)^\kappa,
\]
monotonicity of \(t\mapsto t^\kappa\) on \([0,\infty)\) gives
\[
D_\rho\le R_\rho .
\] % lean: uniform_shifted_power_moment_coercivity

For \(u\in[0,1]\),
\[
u(\rho+1)\le \rho+u,
\]
and hence
\[
R_\rho u^\kappa
=
\{u(\rho+1)\}^\kappa
\le
(\rho+u)^\kappa .
\]
Multiplying by \(P_z(u)^2\) and integrating yields
\[
R_\rho E_\kappa(z)
\le
N_\rho(z).
\] % lean: uniform_shifted_power_moment_coercivity

Since \(E_\kappa(z)\ge0\), the inequalities \(D_\rho\le R_\rho\) and
\(R_\rho E_\kappa(z)\le N_\rho(z)\) imply
\[
E_\kappa(z)\le \frac{N_\rho(z)}{D_\rho}.
\] % lean: uniform_shifted_power_moment_coercivity

Combining the coercive bound for \(E_\kappa\) with the displayed expression for
\(z^\top M_{\ell,\kappa,\rho}z\) gives, for every \(\rho\ge0\),
\[
c\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}z_j^2
\le
z^\top M_{\ell,\kappa,\rho}z .
\] % lean: uniform_shifted_power_moment_coercivity
\end{proof}
