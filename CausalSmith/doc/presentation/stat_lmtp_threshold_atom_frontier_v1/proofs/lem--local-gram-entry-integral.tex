\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:local-gram-entry-integral}]
Write
\[
T=[\delta,\delta+h],
\qquad
u_\delta(a)=\frac{a-\delta}{h}.
\]
Since \(h>0\), for every real \(a\),
\[
0\le u_\delta(a)\le1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
a\in T .
\] % lean: scaledDose_mem_Icc_iff

Define the bounded measurable function
\[
f(a)
=
\mathbf 1\{0\le u_\delta(a)\le1\}\,
v_\ell(u_\delta(a))_j\,v_\ell(u_\delta(a))_k .
\]
For \(u\in[0,1]\), the entries \(v_\ell(u)_j\) and \(v_\ell(u)_k\) are powers of
\(u\), so their absolute values are at most one. Hence
\[
|f(a)|\le1
\qquad\text{for every }a .
\] % lean: localWindowGramEntry_integral_eq

The signs \(\kappa\ge0\) and \(c_+\ge0\), together with the density envelope on
\([0,1]\), give the pointwise domination used for integrability: for
Lebesgue-a.e. \(a\in[0,1]\),
\[
0\le \pi_x(a)\le c_+a^\kappa\le c_+ .
\]
The last inequality follows from \(0\le a\le1\) and \(\kappa\ge0\), and it is
preserved after multiplication by \(c_+\ge0\). Hence
\[
\int_{[0,1]}\pi_x(a)\,da\le c_+<\infty .
\]
Consequently, if \(b\) is measurable and \(|b(a)|\le1\), then
\[
\int_{[0,1]} |b(a)|\pi_x(a)\,da
\le
\int_{[0,1]}\pi_x(a)\,da
<\infty,
\]
so bounded measurable multiples are integrable for the conditional treatment
measure in stratum \(x\). The hypothesis \(p_{\min}>0\) is carried with the
model hypotheses used below for the stratum identity. % lean: localWindowGramEntry_integral_eq

The stratum-restricted treatment law therefore has the following integral form:
for every bounded measurable \(g:[0,1]\to\mathbb R\),
\[
\int \mathbf 1\{X=x\}g(A)\,dP
=
p_x\int_{[0,1]}g(a)\pi_x(a)\,da .
\]
To see this, apply the conditional-density condition in
\(\cref{obj:ass:conditional-density-law}\) first to indicators
\(g=\mathbf 1_B\), where \(B\subseteq[0,1]\) is Borel:
\[
\int \mathbf 1\{X=x\}\mathbf 1_B(A)\,dP
=
P(X=x,A\in B)
=
p_x\int_B\pi_x(a)\,da .
\]
Linearity gives the identity for nonnegative simple functions, monotone
convergence gives it for nonnegative measurable functions, and decomposition
into positive and negative parts gives it for bounded measurable \(g\); the
integrability just established makes both sides finite. % lean: stratumTreatment_integral_eq

Applying this stratum identity to the bounded measurable function \(f\) gives
\[
\int \mathbf 1\{X=x\}f(A)\,dP
=
p_x\int_{[0,1]}f(a)\pi_x(a)\,da .
\] % lean: conditionalTreatment_integral_eq_density

By the definition of \(\phi_j\) and \(\phi_k\),
\[
\mathbf 1\{X=x,\ 0\le u_\delta(A)\le1\}\phi_j(O)\phi_k(O)
=
\mathbf 1\{X=x\}f(A).
\] % lean: localWindowGramEntry_integral_eq

Finally, since \(T\subseteq[0,1]\) and \(f(a)=0\) off \(T\), while
\(f(a)=v_\ell(u_\delta(a))_jv_\ell(u_\delta(a))_k\) on \(T\),
\[
\int_{[0,1]}f(a)\pi_x(a)\,da
=
\int_{\delta}^{\delta+h}
\pi_x(a)\,
v_\ell(u_\delta(a))_j\,
v_\ell(u_\delta(a))_k\,da .
\] % lean: localWindowGramEntry_integral_eq

Combining the displayed stratum decomposition with the preceding indicator rewriting yields
\[
\int \mathbf 1\{X=x,\ 0\le u_\delta(A)\le 1\}\,\phi_j(O)\phi_k(O)\,dP(O)
=
p_x\int_{\delta}^{\delta+h}
\pi_x(a)\,
v_\ell(u_\delta(a))_j\,
v_\ell(u_\delta(a))_k\,da .
\] % lean: localWindowGramEntry_integral_eq
\end{proof}
