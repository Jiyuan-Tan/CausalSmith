\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:bandwidth-phases}]
Set
\[
p=2\beta+1,
\qquad
c_{\mathrm{bal}}=C_{\mathrm{bal}}=1,
\qquad
C_{\mathrm{far}}=1,
\]
and choose
\[
c_{\mathrm{far}}=\min\{1,2^{-\kappa/p}\}.
\]
At this initial choice of witnesses, the quotient \(-\kappa/p\) is interpreted by the total real-division convention: it equals \(0\) when \(p=0\) and agrees with ordinary division when \(p\ne0\). Thus \(c_{\mathrm{far}}\) is a real constant for every value of \(\beta\). Since the base \(2\) is positive, \(2^{-\kappa/p}>0\), and therefore
\[
0<c_{\mathrm{far}}\le1=C_{\mathrm{far}}.
\]
Together with \(c_{\mathrm{bal}}=C_{\mathrm{bal}}=1\), this gives
\[
0<c_{\mathrm{bal}}\le C_{\mathrm{bal}},
\qquad
0<c_{\mathrm{far}}\le C_{\mathrm{far}}.
\]
% lean: bandwidth_phases

Fix \(J,P,L,c_-,c_+,p_{\min},\alpha\) and a deterministic threshold sequence satisfying the hypotheses. The regime assumptions give \(\beta>0\), \(\kappa\ge0\), and \(0<\bar\delta<1\), hence \(p=2\beta+1>0\). Let \(h_n\) be the information-balance bandwidth from \cref{obj:def:bandwidth}; thus
\[
h_n=\inf\{h\in(0,1-\bar\delta]:
n h^{2\beta+1}(\delta_n+h)^\kappa\ge1\}
\]
when the displayed set is nonempty, with fallback value \(h_n=1-\bar\delta\) when that set is empty. By \cref{obj:lem:bandwidth-balance}, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
0<h_n,
\qquad
h_n\le1-\bar\delta,
\qquad
n h_n^{p}(\delta_n+h_n)^\kappa=1 .
\]
% lean: infoBandwidth_eventually_balance
The first balance assertion follows immediately:
\[
1
\le
n h_n^{2\beta+1}(\delta_n+h_n)^\kappa
\le
1 .
\]
% lean: bandwidth_phases
Since \(h_n>0\),
\[
h_n^{2\beta+1}=h_n^{2\beta}h_n,
\qquad
h_n^{-2\beta}=(h_n^{2\beta})^{-1}.
\]
Dividing the balance identity by \(h_n^{2\beta}\) gives
\[
n h_n(\delta_n+h_n)^\kappa=h_n^{-2\beta},
\]
and therefore
\[
h_n^{-2\beta}
\le
n h_n(\delta_n+h_n)^\kappa
\le
h_n^{-2\beta}.
\]
% lean: bandwidth_phases

Now assume
\[
\frac{\delta_n}{\delta_{\mathrm{edge},n}}\to\infty,
\qquad
\delta_{\mathrm{edge},n}=n^{-1/(2\beta+\kappa+1)}=n^{-1/(p+\kappa)}.
\]
On a sufficiently large eventual set, the balance identity holds, \(n>0\), and the convergence gives \(1\le\delta_n/\delta_{\mathrm{edge},n}\). Since \(\delta_{\mathrm{edge},n}>0\) on this set,
\[
\delta_{\mathrm{edge},n}\le\delta_n,
\qquad
\delta_n>0.
\]
% lean: bandwidth_phases
For those \(n\), \(\delta_n^\kappa\le(\delta_n+h_n)^\kappa\), so the balance identity implies
\[
h_n^p(n\delta_n^\kappa)\le1.
\]
Taking \(p\)-th roots gives
\[
h_n\le (n\delta_n^\kappa)^{-1/p} .
\]
% lean: balance_root_le_interior_scale
The comparison \(\delta_{\mathrm{edge},n}\le\delta_n\) gives
\[
1=n\delta_{\mathrm{edge},n}^{p+\kappa}
\le n\delta_n^{p+\kappa}
=\delta_n^p(n\delta_n^\kappa),
\]
and hence
\[
(n\delta_n^\kappa)^{-1/p}\le\delta_n .
\]
% lean: interior_scale_le_threshold_of_edge_le
Consequently \(h_n\le\delta_n\), and therefore
\[
\delta_n+h_n\le2\delta_n .
\]
Using the balance identity again,
\[
1
=
n h_n^p(\delta_n+h_n)^\kappa
\le
n h_n^p(2\delta_n)^\kappa
=
h_n^p\,2^\kappa(n\delta_n^\kappa).
\]
Thus
\[
2^{-\kappa/p}(n\delta_n^\kappa)^{-1/p}\le h_n .
\]
% lean: interior_scale_le_balance_root
Under \(p>0\) and \(\kappa\ge0\), \(2^{-\kappa/p}\le1\), so the chosen constant satisfies \(c_{\mathrm{far}}=2^{-\kappa/p}\). With \(C_{\mathrm{far}}=1\), the far-threshold comparison follows:
\[
c_{\mathrm{far}}(n\delta_n^\kappa)^{-1/(2\beta+1)}
\le
h_n
\le
C_{\mathrm{far}}(n\delta_n^\kappa)^{-1/(2\beta+1)} .
\]
% lean: bandwidth_phases

Finally fix \(D>0\). Set
\[
C_{\mathrm{near}}=1,
\qquad
c_{\mathrm{near}}=\min\{1,(D+1)^{-\kappa/p}\},
\]
again using the same total real-division convention for the displayed quotient. Since \(D+1>0\), the positive-base property of real powers gives \((D+1)^{-\kappa/p}>0\), and therefore
\[
0<c_{\mathrm{near}}\le1=C_{\mathrm{near}}.
\]
Fix again \(J,P,L,c_-,c_+,p_{\min},\alpha\) and a deterministic threshold sequence satisfying the hypotheses, and let \(h_n\) be the corresponding bandwidth from \cref{obj:def:bandwidth}. The regime assumptions again give \(p>0\) and \(\kappa\ge0\). Suppose that eventually
\[
|\delta_n|\le D|\delta_{\mathrm{edge},n}|.
\]
On the eventual set where this bound and \cref{obj:lem:bandwidth-balance} both hold, \(n>0\), \(\delta_n\ge0\), and \(\delta_{\mathrm{edge},n}>0\), so
\[
\delta_n\le D n^{-1/(p+\kappa)}.
\]
% lean: bandwidth_phases
The balance identity and \(h_n^\kappa\le(\delta_n+h_n)^\kappa\) give
\[
n h_n^{p+\kappa}\le1,
\]
hence
\[
h_n\le n^{-1/(p+\kappa)}.
\]
% lean: balance_root_le_edge_scale
Together with the threshold bound, this gives
\[
\delta_n+h_n\le(D+1)n^{-1/(p+\kappa)}.
\]
Therefore
\[
1
=
n h_n^p(\delta_n+h_n)^\kappa
\le
n h_n^p (D+1)^\kappa\bigl(n^{-1/(p+\kappa)}\bigr)^\kappa .
\]
Since \(n\bigl(n^{-1/(p+\kappa)}\bigr)^{p+\kappa}=1\), the displayed inequality \(1\le n h_n^p (D+1)^\kappa (n^{-1/(p+\kappa)})^\kappa\) rearranges to
\[
(D+1)^{-\kappa/p}n^{-1/(p+\kappa)}\le h_n .
\]
% lean: edge_scale_le_balance_root
Under \(p>0\), \(\kappa\ge0\), and \(D>0\), \((D+1)^{-\kappa/p}\le1\), so the chosen constant satisfies \(c_{\mathrm{near}}=(D+1)^{-\kappa/p}\). Since \(p+\kappa=2\beta+\kappa+1\), we obtain, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
c_{\mathrm{near}}n^{-1/(2\beta+\kappa+1)}
\le
h_n
\le
C_{\mathrm{near}}n^{-1/(2\beta+\kappa+1)} .
\]
% lean: bandwidth_phases
\end{proof}
