This paper sits at the intersection of causal estimands that depend on the natural value of treatment, continuous-treatment identification, and nonparametric minimax inference. The causal framework begins with the potential-outcome and structural traditions of Robins (1986), Pearl (2009), and the longitudinal semiparametric treatment in van der Laan and Robins (2003), while Rosenbaum and Rubin (1983), Imbens (2000), Hirano and Imbens (2005), and Imai and van Dyk (2004) supply the canonical propensity-score and generalized-propensity-score background for nonbinary and continuous treatments. Díaz and van der Laan (2012), Haneuse and Rotnitzky (2013), and Young, Hernán, and Robins (2014) established the modern lineage of stochastic and modified interventions: their targets use the observed or natural treatment value to define feasible intervention effects under support conditions that are aligned with the realized treatment law. Díaz, Williams, Hoffman, and Schenck (2023) then developed longitudinal modified treatment policies (LMTPs), including identification by sequential regression, efficient influence-function theory for the regular setting, and efficient or sequentially doubly robust estimators.

Within the LMTP literature, Williams and Díaz (2023) document the `lmtp` implementation that accepts user-specified modified policies, and Hoffman et al. (2024) provide the applied tutorial that explicitly includes threshold interventions. Díaz, Hoffman, and Hejazi (2024) extend LMTP estimation to right-censored competing-risk survival outcomes with locally efficient estimators retaining root-\(n\) behavior under their regularity conditions. Gilbert et al. (2024) extend the LMTP framework to mediation with efficient, asymptotically normal cross-fitted sequential-regression estimators, and Shahu et al. (2026) develop simultaneous inference for effects on rates of change. The 2026 frontier broadens natural-value policies further: Díaz et al. (2026) allow LMTPs to depend on the natural treatment history and obtain targeted-learning estimators with root-\(n\) inference under standard doubly robust nuisance-rate conditions; Koo et al. (2026) retain MTP targets under positivity violations by decomposing the mean into a point-identified in-support component and a Lipschitz-bounded extrapolation component; Pal and Huling (2026) define conditional nudge and conditional MTP effects for heterogeneous small shifts; Jiang et al. (2026) formulate modified functional treatment policies for curve-valued treatments. Sarvet and Stensrud (2026) survey this natural-treatment-value literature and place LMTPs within the broader family of interventions depending on the factual treatment process.

The directly adjacent continuous-treatment and threshold-response literature supplies regular estimands and fast-rate comparators. Kennedy, Ma, McHugh, and Small (2017) study doubly robust nonparametric estimation of continuous-treatment dose-response curves by two-stage local regression. Bonvini and Kennedy (2026) analyze global and pointwise dose-response estimation, proving mean-square upper bounds for empirical-risk-minimization, doubly robust local learning, and higher-order influence-function estimators under smoothness and positive-density conditions. Donovan, Hudgens, and Gilbert (2019) develop nonparametric inference for immune-response threshold-risk curves, and van der Laan, Zhang, and Gilbert (2023) give an efficient targeted estimator and simultaneous confidence bands for a covariate-adjusted threshold-response function interpretable as a support-restricted stochastic intervention. McCoy et al. (2026) use stochastic shift interventions and cross-validated targeted learning to identify effect modifiers with valid confidence intervals for their stochastic-shift effect-modifier target. These papers characterize regular stochastic, region-average, dose-response, or effect-modifier targets; the present threshold-atom frontier characterizes the exact deterministic clamp functional consumed by the LMTP policy workflow, where the pushforward law contains a moving Dirac component and the local information is governed by polynomial generalized-propensity thinning.

The statistical rate and interval theory is anchored in Stone’s (1982) minimax rates, Fan and Gijbels’s (1996) local-polynomial regression theory, Tsybakov’s (2009) nonparametric minimax toolkit, and Korostelev and Tsybakov’s (1993) asymptotic minimax framework. Gaïffas (2005) is the closest degenerate-design antecedent: for pointwise regression with design density regularly varying at the target point and regression smoothness \(s\), the minimax rate is \(n^{-s/(1+2s+\beta)}\) up to slowly varying factors; Gaïffas (2007) gives a Lepski-type adaptive local-polynomial estimator with the corresponding logarithmic adaptation payment. Honest inference is tied to Donoho and Liu’s (1991) modulus-of-continuity geometry, Low’s (1997) lower bound for expected length under uniform coverage and his nonadaptation corollary, Cai and Low’s (2004) between-class modulus theory for adaptive confidence intervals, Robins and van der Vaart’s (2006) adaptive confidence sets, and Armstrong and Kolesár’s (2018, 2020) optimal and bias-aware intervals for fixed-design nonparametric regression. The present paper uses that lineage to establish, under a fixed finite-stratum Hölder model and the measurable envelope \(c_-a^\kappa \le \pi_x(a)\le c_+a^\kappa\), the matched minimax absolute-risk and uniformly honest expected-length frontier \(n^{-1/2}+\delta_n^{\kappa+1}h_n^\beta\) with \(n h_n^{2\beta+1}(\delta_n+h_n)^\kappa\asymp1\), together with the regular, critical, atom-dominated, and fixed-threshold regimes for the unsmoothed clamp target.
