# Referee review

**Recommendation:** major_revision
**Overall score:** 6.4/10 — The paper contains a potentially important generic-identification result, but central notation, decoder specification, open-frontier claims, and the stale verification record require substantial repair before the contribution can be evaluated and used reliably.

The paper establishes generic Gaussian-MMD separation of likelihood-ratio laws and exact population recovery of a nonlinear causal representation from one unknown-target perfect intervention per scalar latent coordinate in a positive smooth compact-cube regime. It also provides simultaneous confidence edges conditional on a uniform first-stage ratio-error contract and clearly labels generated-rank estimation as a future statistical problem. The verified results are substantial, but the manuscript currently obscures their scope through an inconsistent intervention index, an incompletely specified population decoder, overstatements surrounding the open statistical frontier, and an obsolete verification record.

## Strengths
- The open-dense cover-separation theorem is a novel and meaningful route from environment likelihood ratios to ancestral structure in arbitrary finite dimension.
- The exact-decoder theorem connects ratio-law separation, conditional ranks, target alignment, and conditional-independence pruning in a logically coherent population identification result.
- The paper states the strong compact-support, smoothness, positivity, derivative-sign, and intervention-design conditions prominently rather than hiding them.
- The finite-sample theorem cleanly separates the assumed first-stage ratio accuracy from the explicit evaluation-fold MMD concentration argument.
- The dedicated related-work section identifies the closest unknown-target nonlinear-representation comparator and generally presents the one-intervention contribution as applying on an overlapping, smaller model class.

## Findings
- **[major·statement] Setup and ratio observables** — The reader-facing definition of the central edge contrast is internally inconsistent about intervention indexing. Earlier, P^e denotes environment label e and environment e targets π(e), but the displayed definition of F^{can}_{ab} integrates against P^{π(a)}. Used inside F^{can}_{π^{-1}(j),π^{-1}(i)}, this appears to apply the permutation twice, whereas the formal construction compares environment a with environment 0.
  - *Fix:* Separate environment-indexed and latent-target-indexed laws explicitly. Write the contrast using P^a_{θ,π}, or introduce an unambiguous target-indexed law such as P^{do(a)}_θ, and then audit every occurrence of F^{can}, P^e, and p^a so that the displayed definition visibly matches the formal environment-label comparison.
- **[major·structure] Population decoding** — The decoder is presented as a total algorithm on arbitrary observed laws, yet its instructions require choosing a topological order of H_D and identifying a unique inclusion-minimal admissible set. For arbitrary law families H_D can be cyclic and the minimal set need not be unique. Calling the construction a “constructive population decoder” that “computes” exact Radon–Nikodym derivatives, conditional-law versions, and conditional independences also risks suggesting computational implementability beyond the proved population-oracle result.
  - *Fix:* Either restrict the displayed decoder's domain to law families for which acyclicity and uniqueness hold, or state the deterministic fallback conventions used by the formal total definition. Describe it consistently as a population identification functional with oracle access to the environment laws, and distinguish its output from the finite-sample procedure, which currently estimates only the discrepancy graph.
- **[major·structure] Generic ratio separation** — The sentence “The last standing condition in this section is the usual faithfulness condition” makes faithfulness appear to be a premise of the generic-separation theorem. The verified genericity and exact-decoding results use causal minimality, while faithfulness is a distinct stronger condition relevant to the explicit witness and the overlap comparison with prior work.
  - *Fix:* Relocate faithfulness to the witness or comparison subsection and state the assumption map affirmatively: the core genericity and decoder theorems operate on the causal-minimal stratum, while the faithful subclass supplies the stated overlap corollary and witness comparison.
- **[major·prose] Proofs and verification** — The verification record is stale. It identifies repository state 5cf69768, while the authoritative current contract identifies commit 43ec493aed5f5f0e25ce0257177d2fc4709049a2. It also reports 67 formal environments and 53 declaration-naming environments, whereas the supplied current contract enumerates 64 objects, comprising 50 declaration-naming objects and 14 presentation-level objects.
  - *Fix:* Regenerate the verification paragraph and crosswalk from the current contract, replace the commit hash and all object/audit counts, and ensure that the archived artifact and manuscript refer to the same immutable revision.
- **[major·prose] Related work / Limitations and future work** — The manuscript gives the open generated-rank remark more downstream force than it supports. The sentence “records the corresponding rate target for translating this conditional edge guarantee into a sample-complexity statement for named first-stage estimators” suggests a delivered bridge to named estimators, although the verified object is an open question conditional on an abstract C1 first-stage rate. Likewise, saying the target “connects ... to rank-coordinate uncertainty in implementations such as ROPES” can be read as asserting model compatibility of the existing ROPES benchmark, which the stated scope does not establish.
  - *Fix:* Describe the object as an open conditional-rank estimation problem under an abstract first-stage contract. In the limitations section, state that ROPES could consume such a result after adopting a positive common-support intervention generator and a ratio estimator satisfying the required contract, and distinguish that prospective use from the existing benchmark.
- **[minor·citation] Related work** — The closest score-based and finite-sample competitors are cited more than compared. The paper does not give the intervention counts and transformation restrictions that distinguish the one-hard-intervention linear-identification and two-hard-intervention general-identification results, nor does it report the available sample-complexity orders for the cited linear CRL work.
  - *Fix:* Add a compact, source-checked comparison stating each closest result's intervention design, known or unknown target information, mixing/latent restrictions, recovered equivalence class, and any advertised rate or sample-complexity bound, with theorem or page locators.
- **[minor·prose] Sample-split confidence edges / Related work** — The prose contract is violated outside the limitations section by sentences such as “it neither constructs such an estimator nor calibrates a_N” and “their full Euclidean latent space ... is not contained in the compact-cube C2 class.” These explain scope through absent deliverables or noncoverage.
  - *Fix:* Rewrite the first passage affirmatively, for example: “The theorem takes a supplied simultaneous L1 radius as input and provides an explicit evaluation-fold concentration radius.” Move the support-class noncoverage comparison to the limitations section or replace it with an affirmative description of the two overlapping model domains.
- **[minor·structure] Introduction / Related work** — The roadmap uses manual and incorrect section numbers: “Section 2 introduces the compact-cube latent model” although Section 2 is Related work. The same paragraph manually enumerates Sections 2–6 instead of using cleveref. External references such as “Their Theorem 3.2” and “their Theorem 3.4” also lack the required citation-command locators.
  - *Fix:* Add structural labels and replace every internal roadmap reference with the corresponding cleveref command. Replace external theorem prose with forms such as citet[Theorem 3.2]{...} and citet[Theorem 3.4]{...}, preserving accurate locators.
- **[minor·structure] global** — The manuscript's conceptual contribution is buried under a large number of bookkeeping definitions and proof-infrastructure objects, including observed-world packaging, totalization conventions, fiber products, and score-range retractions. This makes it difficult for an econometrics reader to see which assumptions feed genericity, exact decoding, representation uniqueness, and finite-sample edge inference.
  - *Fix:* Add a compact assumption-to-result table and a short decoder roadmap in the main text. Move proof-only totalizations and measure-theoretic infrastructure to a technical or online appendix while retaining the definitions needed to state the main results precisely.
- **[nit·prose] Limitations and future work** — The symbol for the minimum environment sample size, underline N, first appears inside the generated-rank frontier before receiving its plain-language identification in the following paragraph.
  - *Fix:* Define underline N immediately before its first displayed use, preferably in the bounded-regime definition or at the start of the frontier remark.

## Questions for authors
- In the definition of F^{can}_{ab}, is P^{π(a)} intended to denote observed environment a or a latent intervention indexed by target π(a)?
- Is the population decoder intended to be defined only on model-compatible law families, or should the manuscript reproduce the formal construction's fallback behavior for cyclic ratio graphs and nonunique pruning sets?
- Will the public verification artifact and crosswalk be archived at commit 43ec493aed5f5f0e25ce0257177d2fc4709049a2 and synchronized with the revised manuscript counts?
