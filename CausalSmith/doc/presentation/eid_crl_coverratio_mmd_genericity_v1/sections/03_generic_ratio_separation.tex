\section{Generic ratio separation}\label{sec:generic-separation}

The separation sets in \cref{obj:def:edge-separated-set,obj:def:cover-separated-set} turn observable ratio laws into graph information. This section supplies the two ingredients behind that step. First, explicit three-node mechanisms show both a strict direct-edge discrepancy and an exact cancellation. Second, the genericity result shows that, within any nonempty fixed-sign stratum, the separating behavior is open and dense.

We begin with two concrete mechanisms on the graph \(1\to2\), with node \(3\) isolated. The first mechanism is the separating endpoint used to certify that the relevant analytic contrast can be nonzero.

\begin{definitionv}{def:sparse-witness}[Sparse witness \(\theta^{\mathrm{sp}}\)]
On the graph \(1\to2\) with node \(3\) isolated, define \(\theta^{\mathrm{sp}}\) by
\[
h(z)=2z-1,
\]
\[
q_i(z)=\frac{4\exp(4z)}{\exp(4)-1},
\]
\[
p_2(y\mid x)=1+0.1h(x)h(y),
\]
and
\[
p_1=p_3=1.
\]
For a sign vector with a negative prescribed sign in coordinate \(i\), the corresponding coordinate is reflected by the map \(z\mapsto1-z\).
\end{definitionv}

The affine dependence in \cref{obj:def:sparse-witness} creates a direct response of the ratio coordinate at node \(2\) to interventions at node \(1\). Reflections preserve the fixed-sign convention from \cref{obj:ass:fixed-own-derivative-sign}, so the same construction serves every prescribed sign pattern.

The next mechanism has the same graph and the same intervention tilts, but it places the parent dependence in a form that exactly balances the ratio law across the relevant environments.

\begin{definitionv}{def:cancellation-witness}[Cancellation witness $\theta^{\mathrm{can}}$]
On the graph \(1\to2\) with node \(3\) isolated, define \(\theta^{\mathrm{can}}\) by
\[
q_i(z)=\frac{4\exp(4z)}{\exp(4)-1},
\qquad
p_1=1,
\qquad
p_3=1,
\]
\[
h(y)=2y-1,
\qquad
H(x)=\frac{\exp(4x)-1}{\exp(4)-1}-x,
\qquad
p_2(y\mid x)=1+0.1H(x)h(y).
\]
For any other sign vector, \(\theta^{\mathrm{can}}\) is obtained from this mechanism by coordinate reflections.
\end{definitionv}

The contrast between \cref{obj:def:sparse-witness,obj:def:cancellation-witness} is useful because the ratio-law condition can hold strictly at one regular point and vanish at another regular point. Thus the generic result below is a topological statement about typical mechanisms in the stratum, with the cancellation example marking the boundary behavior that analytic perturbations must move away from.

For the witness calculation and the generic statement, it is helpful to name the observed law system induced by a mechanism and a shared coordinate map.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_28}[Coherence of observed laws with an observed world]
Let \(W\) be an observed world generated by a mechanism \(\theta=((p_i,q_i))_{i\in[n]}\) on \(G\), with shared mixing map \(f:[0,1]^n\to\mathcal X\), inverse \(f^{-1}\), and target permutation \(\pi\). We say that observed laws \((P^0,\ldots,P^n)\) are \(\mathrm{coherent\ with}\) \(W\) when \(P^0=f_{\#}p^0\) and, for each interventional environment label \(e\in[n]\), \(P^e=f_{\#}p^{\pi(e)}\), where
\[
p^0(v)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}p_i(v_i\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)})
\]
and
\[
p^a(v)=q_a(v_a)\prod_{\ell\ne a}p_\ell(v_\ell\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(\ell)})
\qquad(a\in[n]).
\]
Equivalently, under \(P^0\) and \(P^e\), the unmixed variable \(V=f^{-1}(X)\) has densities \(p^0\) and \(p^{\pi(e)}\), respectively, on \([0,1]^n\).
\end{definitionv}

\Cref{obj:synth_28} records the population object available to the ratio analysis: the same observation map pushes forward the observational law and each single-target intervention law. In the explicit witness world, the shared map and target permutation are identities, so the latent and observed densities coincide.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_27}[Sparse-witness environment laws]
Let \(W^{\mathrm{sp}}\) be the observed world generated by the sparse witness mechanism \(\theta^{\mathrm{sp}}\) with identity mixing and identity target permutation on the three-node DAG \(1\to2\) with node \(3\) isolated. For \(e\in\{0,1,2,3\}\), \(P^e_{\mathrm{sp}}\) denotes the environment-\(e\) law in this world. Thus \(P^0_{\mathrm{sp}}\) is the observational law with density
\[
p^0_{\mathrm{sp}}(v)
=
p^{\mathrm{sp}}_1(v_1)\,
p^{\mathrm{sp}}_2(v_2\mid v_1)\,
p^{\mathrm{sp}}_3(v_3),
\]
and, for \(e\in\{1,2,3\}\), \(P^e_{\mathrm{sp}}\) is the intervention law at target \(e\), with density
\[
p^e_{\mathrm{sp}}(v)
=
q^{\mathrm{sp}}_e(v_e)
\prod_{\ell\ne e}
p^{\mathrm{sp}}_\ell(v_\ell\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(\ell)}).
\]
Because the mixing map is the identity, these latent densities are also the corresponding observed laws.
\end{definitionv}

The graph-theoretic and probabilistic conventions used by the generic result are standard. We state them here to make the separation theorem self-contained.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_24}[d-separation in a DAG]
Let \(G\) be a DAG on \([n]\), and let \(A,B,C\subseteq[n]\) be pairwise disjoint. A path in the underlying undirected graph of \(G\) is \(C\)-active when every non-collider on the path is outside \(C\), and every collider on the path belongs to \(C\) or has a directed path in \(G\) to some vertex of \(C\). The sets \(A\) and \(B\) are d-separated by \(C\) in \(G\) when there is no \(C\)-active path in the underlying undirected graph of \(G\) with one endpoint in \(A\) and the other endpoint in \(B\).
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{synth_10}[Faithfulness to \(G\)]
An observational law \(p^0\) on \(V=(V_1,\ldots,V_n)\) is faithful to the directed acyclic graph \(G\) when, for all disjoint subsets \(A,B,C\subseteq[n]\),
\[
V_A\mathbin{\perp\!\!\!\perp}_{p^0} V_B \mid V_C
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
A \text{ and } B \text{ are d-separated by } C \text{ in } G .
\]
Equivalently, the conditional independence relations of \(p^0\) are exactly the d-separation relations encoded by \(G\).
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{synth_23}[Ancestral order in a DAG]
For a DAG \(G\) on the finite labeled vertex set \([n]\) and vertices \(j,i\in[n]\), write
\[
j<_G i
\]
when \(j\) is a strict ancestor of \(i\) in \(G\), equivalently when there exists a directed path in \(G\) of positive length from \(j\) to \(i\).
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{synth_4}[Ancestral cover relation]
For a DAG \(G\) on the finite labeled vertex set \([n]\), define \(j\lessdot_G i\) to be the cover relation in the ancestor order of \(G\):
\[
j\lessdot_G i
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
j <_G i
\;\;\text{and}\;\;
\{k\in[n]: j <_G k <_G i\}=\varnothing .
\]
\end{definitionv}

The cover relation in \cref{obj:synth_4} is the transitive-reduction analogue for the ancestor order. It selects the ancestral comparisons whose ratio-law discrepancies form the population input for the decoder in the next section.

We also record the ratio-law notation used in the witness certificate and in the open-dense theorem.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_1}[Latent state space]
For \(n\in\mathbb N\), the latent state \(V=(V_1,\ldots,V_n)\) ranges over the real coordinate space
\[
\mathbb R^{[n]}=\{v:[n]\to\mathbb R\}.
\]
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{synth_20}[Observational, intervention, and ratio laws]
For a mechanism \(\theta=((p_i,q_i))_{i=1}^n\in\Theta_{G,s}\), let \(\mathcal L(\theta)\) denote the observational law on the observed support \(\mathcal X\) generated by
\[
p^0_\theta(v)=\prod_{\ell=1}^n p_\ell(v_\ell\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(\ell)})
\]
and the shared mixing map \(X=f(V)\). For \(i\in[n]\), let \(\operatorname{do}(i)\theta\) denote the perfect-intervention law obtained from \(\theta\) by replacing the \(i\)-th observational conditional mechanism \(p_i(v_i\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)})\) with the parent-independent intervention density \(q_i(v_i)\), so its latent density is
\[
p^i_\theta(v)=q_i(v_i)\prod_{\ell\ne i}p_\ell(v_\ell\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(\ell)}),
\]
again pushed forward by \(f\) to \(\mathcal X\). Given a target permutation \(\pi\), write \(P^0_{\theta,\pi}=\mathcal L(\theta)\) and, for \(e\in[n]\), \(P^e_{\theta,\pi}=\operatorname{do}(\pi(e))\theta\). For \(i\in[n]\), let
\[
R_i^{\theta,\pi}=\frac{dP^i_{\theta,\pi}}{dP^0_{\theta,\pi}}
\]
be the observed density ratio for environment label \(i\). For \(e\in\{0,\ldots,n\}\), define
\[
\mathcal L_e^{\theta,\pi,i}
  = (R_i^{\theta,\pi})_{\#}P^e_{\theta,\pi},
\]
the probability law on \(\mathbb R\) obtained by pushing the canonical environment-\(e\) observed law forward through the ratio coordinate \(R_i^{\theta,\pi}\).
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{synth_3}[Intervention distribution function]
For a mechanism \(\theta\) on a DAG \(G\) over \([n]\), with parent-independent intervention density \(q_i\) at coordinate \(i\), define
\[
Q_i(v)=\int_0^v q_i(u)\,du
\]
for \(i\in[n]\) and \(v\in\mathbb R\).
\end{definitionv}

One further condition is recorded here for use by the explicit witness and by the comparison with prior work: the usual faithfulness condition, linking conditional independences in the observational density to d-separation in the graph. It is a strictly stronger requirement than the causal minimality used by \cref{obj:thm:generic-cover-separation} and \cref{obj:thm:exact-ratio-decoder}, which quantify over the causal-minimal stratum; faithfulness enters only where it is cited by name.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:faithfulness}[Faithful observational law]
The observational latent density \(p^0\) is faithful to the DAG \(G\).
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:faithfulness} is the standard causal faithfulness condition \citep{SpirtesGlymourScheines2000}. It rules the observational conditional independences to be exactly those encoded by d-separation, so graphical ancestry and the statistical independences used by causal minimality are aligned.

The explicit witnesses now certify the two endpoint behaviors needed for the perturbation argument.

\begin{theoremv}{prop:sparse-witness-certificate}[Sparse witness certificate]
For every sign vector \(s\in\{-1,1\}^3\), let \(\theta^{\mathrm{sp}}\) be the construction in \cref{obj:def:sparse-witness} and let \(\theta^{\mathrm{can}}\) be the construction in \cref{obj:def:cancellation-witness}, both on the three-node DAG \(1\to2\) with node \(3\) isolated. Let \(W^{\mathrm{sp}}\) and \(W^{\mathrm{can}}\) be the corresponding observed worlds with identity mixing and identity target permutation, so that environment \(0\) has the observational law, environment \(e\) has the intervention law at target \(e\), and
\[
R_i(v)=\frac{q_i(v_i)}{p_i(v_i\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)})}.
\]
Then:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Sparse witness regularity.)} The mechanism \(\theta^{\mathrm{sp}}\) satisfies \cref{obj:ass:positive-normalized-smooth-mechanisms}, \cref{obj:ass:fixed-own-derivative-sign} with sign vector \(s\), and \cref{obj:ass:faithfulness}. Moreover, every observational conditional density \(p_i^{\mathrm{sp}}\) is \(C^\infty\) on \([0,1]^3\), and every intervention density \(q_i^{\mathrm{sp}}\) is \(C^\infty\) on \([0,1]\).
\item \textbf{(Cancellation witness regularity.)} The mechanism \(\theta^{\mathrm{can}}\) satisfies \cref{obj:ass:positive-normalized-smooth-mechanisms}, \cref{obj:ass:fixed-own-derivative-sign} with sign vector \(s\), and \cref{obj:ass:faithfulness}. Moreover, every observational conditional density \(p_i^{\mathrm{can}}\) is \(C^\infty\) on \([0,1]^3\), and every intervention density \(q_i^{\mathrm{can}}\) is \(C^\infty\) on \([0,1]\).
\item \textbf{(Sparse witness separation.)} The sparse witness has
\[
\frac{3}{10000}
<
\int R_2(x)^2\,dP^0_{\mathrm{sp}}(x)
-
\int R_2(x)^2\,dP^1_{\mathrm{sp}}(x),
\]
and its Gaussian-kernel population discrepancy satisfies
\[
\frac{5}{100000000}<D_{12}(W^{\mathrm{sp}}).
\]
\item \textbf{(Cancellation witness equality.)} For the cancellation witness, the law of \(R_2\) under the observational environment equals the law of \(R_2\) under environment \(1\), and
\[
D_{12}(W^{\mathrm{can}})=0.
\]
\end{itemize}
\end{theoremv}

\Cref{obj:prop:sparse-witness-certificate} gives a regular separating point and a regular cancelling point inside the same elementary graph geometry. The strict lower bounds for the sparse witness show that the direct-edge second-moment contrast and the Gaussian-kernel discrepancy can both detect the edge \(1\to2\). The cancellation equality shows that equality of ratio laws can occur at structured mechanisms satisfying the same smoothness, sign, and faithfulness requirements, which is exactly the situation addressed by a generic rather than universal separation theorem.

The main population result converts that endpoint calculation into a statement for every nonempty fixed-sign stratum. Its topology is the relative product \(C^2\) topology built into \cref{obj:def:model-stratum}.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:generic-cover-separation}[Generic cover separation]
Let \(n\) be the ambient number of scalar latent variables. Assume:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Finite labeled DAG.)} \(G\) is a finite labeled DAG on \([n]\).
\item \textbf{(Sign stratum.)} \(s\) is a sign vector with coordinates in \(\{-1,1\}\), and the stratum \(\Theta_{G,s}\) of \cref{obj:def:model-stratum} is nonempty.
\item \textbf{(Target permutation.)} \(\pi\) is a permutation of \([n]\).
\end{itemize}
Let \(\mathcal V_{G,s,\pi}\) be the direct-edge separation set of \cref{obj:def:edge-separated-set}, and let \(\mathcal U_{G,s,\pi}\) be the ancestral-cover separation set of \cref{obj:def:cover-separated-set} formed with the Gaussian feature map \(\phi(a)=k(a,\cdot)\) for \(k(a,b)=\exp\{-(a-b)^2\}\). Then \(\mathcal V_{G,s,\pi}\) is open and dense in \(\Theta_{G,s}\), and
\[
\mathcal V_{G,s,\pi}\subseteq \mathcal U_{G,s,\pi}.
\]
Moreover, \(\mathcal U_{G,s,\pi}\) is open and dense in \(\Theta_{G,s}\), and its complement in \(\Theta_{G,s}\) is meagre, closed, and nowhere dense. If \(G\) has no directed edges, then
\[
\mathcal V_{G,s,\pi}=\Theta_{G,s}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\mathcal U_{G,s,\pi}=\Theta_{G,s}.
\]
\end{theoremv}

\Cref{obj:thm:generic-cover-separation} establishes that ratio-law separation is typical in every nonempty stratum with fixed own-coordinate derivative signs. Openness says that a separated mechanism remains separated under sufficiently small perturbations of the normalized mechanisms. Density says that every admissible mechanism can be approximated by mechanisms whose direct-edge contrasts are nonzero and whose ancestral-cover discrepancies are positive. The meagreness and nowhere-density clauses give the corresponding topological description of the exceptional set.

The intuition is that the witness in \cref{obj:prop:sparse-witness-certificate} provides a local direction in which an edge-specific ratio contrast becomes nonzero. Along normalized analytic perturbation paths, a contrast that is nonzero at one endpoint can vanish only on a thin set of perturbation values unless it is identically zero. Applying this reasoning across the finitely many relevant edges and covers yields the open-dense separation class used by the population decoder in the next section.
