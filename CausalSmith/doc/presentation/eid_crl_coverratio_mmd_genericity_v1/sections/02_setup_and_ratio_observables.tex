\section{Setup and ratio observables}\label{sec:setup}

Throughout the paper, \(n\) is the ambient number of scalar latent variables, \([n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}\) is the labeled vertex set, and \(G\) is a finite labeled DAG. Vectors are written componentwise, with latent coordinates \leanref{sym:V}{\ensuremath{V=(V_1,\ldots,V_n)}} taking values in the compact cube \([0,1]^n\). Generic constants may change from line to line, norms are taken in the space indicated by their subscripts, and all index ranges are over \([n]\) unless stated otherwise. The sign vector \leanref{sym:s}{\ensuremath{s}} records the prescribed own-coordinate derivative directions.

The structural model is specified by observational conditional densities \leanref{sym:p_i}{\ensuremath{p_i}}, parent-independent intervention densities \leanref{sym:q_i}{\ensuremath{q_i}}, a shared observation map \(f\), and observed environment laws \(P^0,\ldots,P^n\) on the observed support \leanref{sym:\mathcal X}{\ensuremath{\mathcal X}}. The first group of conditions fixes the smooth positive latent model and the common invertible observation system.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:positive-normalized-smooth-mechanisms}[Positive smooth mechanisms]
For every \(i\in[n]\), the observational mechanism \(p_i\) and the intervention density \(q_i\) are strictly positive, normalized \(C^3\) densities on their respective closed cubes.
\end{assumptionv}

The smooth full-support compact-cube density condition in \cref{obj:ass:positive-normalized-smooth-mechanisms} places the observational and intervention mechanisms on a common regular support for the ratio construction in this paper. Positivity makes likelihood ratios well-defined on the latent cube, while normalization preserves the interpretation of each carrier as a conditional or marginal density.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:shared-diffeomorphic-mixing}[Shared diffeomorphic mixing]
The same map \(f\) is a \(C^2\) diffeomorphism from \([0,1]^n\) onto \(\mathcal X\) in every environment.
\end{assumptionv}

The shared diffeomorphic mixing condition in \cref{obj:ass:shared-diffeomorphic-mixing} is the standard invertible-observation restriction for this setting \citep{vonKugelgenEtAl2023UnknownInterventions}. It ensures that every environment is observed through the same smooth coordinate change, so differences across \(P^0,\ldots,P^n\) come from the latent intervention laws rather than from environment-specific measurement maps.

We next make the graph notation and latent product law explicit.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_9}[Parent set \(\operatorname{pa}_G(i)\)]
For a finite labeled directed acyclic graph \(G\) on \([n]\) and a vertex \(i\in[n]\), \(\operatorname{pa}_G(i)\) denotes the set of parents of \(i\) in \(G\):
\[
\operatorname{pa}_G(i)=\{j\in[n]: j\to i \text{ is an edge of }G\}.
\]
For \(A\subseteq[n]\), write \(V_A=(V_j)_{j\in A}\).
\end{definitionv}

The notation in \cref{obj:synth_9} is used throughout to express local Markov factorization through parent coordinates. In particular, \(p_i\) depends on its own coordinate and the parent set \leanref{sym:\operatorname{pa}_G(i)}{\ensuremath{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)}}.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_2}[Observational product density]
For a finite dimension \(n\), a labeled DAG \(G\) on \([n]\), a mechanism \(\theta\) with observational conditional-density carriers \(p_i\), and a latent state \(v=(v_1,\ldots,v_n)\in\mathbb R^n\), define
\[
p^0(v)=\prod_{i=1}^n p_i\bigl(v_i\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)}\bigr).
\]
\end{definitionv}

The product density \leanref{sym:p^0}{\ensuremath{p^0}} in \cref{obj:synth_2} is the observational latent law associated with \(G\) and the local mechanisms. The target-\(i\) intervention law \leanref{sym:p^i}{\ensuremath{p^i}} replaces the \(i\)th observational mechanism by \(q_i\), and this replacement is formalized in the observed laws below.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:one-perfect-intervention-per-node}[Perfect intervention laws]
For the mechanism \(\theta\), the shared mixing map \(f\), the observed laws \(P^0,\ldots,P^n\), the target permutation \(\pi\), and the supplied likelihood ratios \(R_1,\ldots,R_n\), the following conditions hold:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Observational pushforward.)} The observational law satisfies
\[
P^0
=
f_\#\bigl(p^0(v)\,dv\bigr),
\qquad
p^0(v)=\prod_{i=1}^n p_i(v_i\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)}),
\]
with \(v\in[0,1]^n\).
\item \textbf{(Single-target pushforwards.)} For every environment \(e\in[n]\),
\[
P^e
=
f_\#\bigl(p^{\pi(e)}(v)\,dv\bigr),
\qquad
p^{\pi(e)}(v)
=
q_{\pi(e)}(v_{\pi(e)})
\prod_{\ell\ne \pi(e)}
p_\ell(v_\ell\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(\ell)}).
\]
\item \textbf{(Radon--Nikodym ratios.)} For every \(e\in[n]\),
\[
R_e(X)=\frac{dP^e}{dP^0}(X)
\quad\text{\(P^0\)-almost surely.}
\]
\item \textbf{(Latent ratio formula.)} For every \(e\in[n]\) and every \(v\in[0,1]^n\),
\[
R_e(f(v))
=
\frac{q_{\pi(e)}(v_{\pi(e)})}
{p_{\pi(e)}(v_{\pi(e)}\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(\pi(e))})}.
\]
\end{itemize}
\end{assumptionv}

The unknown-target stochastic perfect-intervention coverage condition in \cref{obj:ass:one-perfect-intervention-per-node} is standard in this literature \citep{vonKugelgenEtAl2023UnknownInterventions}. It ties each nonobservational environment to exactly one latent target through the permutation \(\pi\), and it supplies the likelihood-ratio representation that turns observed laws into one-dimensional ratio coordinates. When \(X=f(V)\), the ratio for an intervention targeting \(i\) depends on \(V_i\) and its parents through the displayed quotient.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:fixed-own-derivative-sign}[Fixed derivative sign]
For every \(i\in[n]\),
\[
s_i\,\partial_{v_i}\log\{q_i(v_i)/p_i(v_i\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)})\}>0
\]
throughout the closed cube.
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:fixed-own-derivative-sign} orients each ratio in its own coordinate through a compact-support fixed-sign derivative condition. It records that the log-ratio changes monotonically in the intervened coordinate after applying the prescribed sign \(s_i\), which later supports the rank-based decoding step.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:causal-minimality}[Causal minimality]
For every edge \(j\to i\) of \(G\), the observational law \(p^0\) satisfies
\[
V_i\not\!\perp\!\!\!\perp V_j
\mid
V_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)\setminus\{j\}} .
\]
\end{assumptionv}

The local edge form of causal minimality in \cref{obj:ass:causal-minimality} is the standard positivity-compatible requirement that each displayed parent contributes to the observational conditional law \citep{SpirtesGlymourScheines2000}. It anchors the graph \(G\) to conditional distributions under \(p^0\), so the formal stratum below consists of mechanisms whose graph edges are statistically active.

\begin{definitionv}{def:model-stratum}[Model stratum \(\Theta_{G,s}\)]
\[
\Theta_{G,s}
=
\left\{
((p_i,q_i))_{i=1}^{n}:
\cref{obj:ass:positive-normalized-smooth-mechanisms,obj:ass:causal-minimality,obj:ass:fixed-own-derivative-sign}
\text{ hold for }G\text{ and }s
\right\}.
\]
The set \(\Theta_{G,s}\) is equipped with the relative product \(C^2\) topology.
\end{definitionv}

The model stratum \leanref{sym:\Theta_{G,s}}{\ensuremath{\Theta_{G,s}}} collects the latent mechanisms satisfying the positivity, minimality, and signed-ratio restrictions for the fixed graph and sign vector. The relative product \(C^2\) topology is the topology used later for generic separation statements.

The remaining definitions describe the observed data object and the two population separation classes used by the ratio-decoding argument.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_19}[Observed world data]
For a mechanism \(\theta\) on the labeled DAG \(G\) with ambient vertex set \([n]\), an observed world is the following data:
\[
\bigl(f,\mathrm{unmix},\pi,(P^e)_{e=0}^n,(R_i)_{i\in[n]}\bigr).
\]
Here \(f\) and \(\mathrm{unmix}\) are maps from the latent state space \(\mathbb R^{[n]}\) to itself, \(\pi\) is a permutation of \([n]\), \(P^e\) is a measure on \(\mathbb R^{[n]}\) for each environment \(e\in\{0,\ldots,n\}\), and \(R_i:\mathbb R^{[n]}\to\mathbb R\) is a real-valued ratio carrier for each target \(i\in[n]\).
\end{definitionv}

\Cref{obj:synth_19} packages the objects that are available to population procedures: common coordinates, environment laws, target labels, and ratio carriers. In the canonical observed world used below, the mixing and unmixing maps are identities, so the same definitions can be evaluated directly on latent laws.

\begin{definitionv}{def:edge-separated-set}[Edge separation set]
For a target-label permutation \(\pi\), define
\[
\mathcal V_{G,s,\pi}
=
\left\{
\theta\in\Theta_{G,s}:
F^{\mathrm{can}}_{\pi^{-1}(j),\pi^{-1}(i)}(\theta;\pi)\ne0
\text{ for every edge }j\to i\text{ of }G
\right\}.
\]
Here \(F^{\mathrm{can}}_{ab}(\theta;\pi)\) is the second-moment contrast in the canonical observed world generated by \(\theta\) and \(\pi\):
\[
F^{\mathrm{can}}_{ab}(\theta;\pi)
=
\int
\left(
\frac{q_{\pi(b)}(v_{\pi(b)})}
     {p_{\pi(b)}(v_{\pi(b)}\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(\pi(b))})}
\right)^2
\,dP^{a}(v)
-
\int
\left(
\frac{q_{\pi(b)}(v_{\pi(b)})}
     {p_{\pi(b)}(v_{\pi(b)}\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(\pi(b))})}
\right)^2
\,dP^0(v).
\]
\end{definitionv}

The set \leanref{sym:\mathcal V_{G,s,\pi}}{\ensuremath{\mathcal V_{G,s,\pi}}} records mechanisms for which every direct edge has a nonzero canonical second-moment contrast. The contrast \leanref{sym:F_{ji}}{\ensuremath{F_{ji}}} compares the squared ratio coordinate under the observational law and under another intervention environment after the target relabeling \(\pi\).

\begin{definitionv}{synth_13}[Population MMD discrepancy]
For a complete real Hilbert space \(H\), let \(U\) be a feature map \(r\mapsto \Phi(r)\in H\) with \(\|\Phi(r)\|_H=1\) and
\[
\langle \Phi(a),\Phi(b)\rangle_H=k(a,b)=\exp(-(a-b)^2).
\]
For an observed world \(W\) with environment laws \(P^0,\ldots,P^n\) and ratio coordinates \(R_1,\ldots,R_n\), and for \(j,i\in[n]\), define the population MMD discrepancy \(D_{ji}(W)\) by
\[
D_{ji}(W)
=
\left\|
\int \Phi(r)\,d\bigl((R_i)_\# P^0\bigr)(r)
-
\int \Phi(r)\,d\bigl((R_i)_\# P^j\bigr)(r)
\right\|_H .
\]
When the observed world is fixed by the context we abbreviate \(D_{ji}(W)\) to \(D_{ji}\).
\end{definitionv}

The discrepancy \leanref{sym:D_{ji}}{\ensuremath{D_{ji}}} in \cref{obj:synth_13} is the maximum mean discrepancy between the one-dimensional laws of ratio coordinate \(i\) under environments \(0\) and \(j\), using the bounded Gaussian kernel and its Hilbert-space feature map. This embeds the ratio-law comparison in the standard kernel two-sample framework \citep{GrettonEtAl2012KernelTwoSample,Sriperumbudur2010,Sriperumbudur2011}.

\begin{definitionv}{def:cover-separated-set}[Cover-separated mechanisms]
Let \(H\) be a complete real Hilbert space, and let \(U\) be a feature map \(\phi:\mathbb R\to H\) satisfying
\[
\|\phi(r)\|_H=1,
\qquad
\langle \phi(a),\phi(b)\rangle_H=k(a,b)
\]
for all \(r,a,b\in\mathbb R\).  For a target permutation \(\pi\), let \(W_{\theta,\pi}^{\mathrm{can}}\) be the canonical observed world generated by \(\theta\) and \(\pi\): the mixing and unmixing maps are the identity, the target permutation is \(\pi\), the observational environment has law induced by \(\theta\)'s observational law, intervention environment \(e\) has law induced by the intervention targeting \(\pi(e)\), and its ratio carrier is
\[
R_e(v)=
\frac{q_{\pi(e)}(v_{\pi(e)})}
{p_{\pi(e)}(v_{\pi(e)}\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(\pi(e))})}.
\]
The cover-separated set is
\[
\mathcal U_{G,s,\pi}
=
\left\{
\theta\in\Theta_{G,s}:
D_{ji}\!\left(W_{\theta,\pi}^{\mathrm{can}}\right)>0
\ \text{for every } j,i\in[n]\text{ with }\pi(j)\lessdot_G\pi(i)
\right\},
\]
where
\[
D_{ji}\!\left(W_{\theta,\pi}^{\mathrm{can}}\right)
=
\left\|
\int \phi(r)\,d\mathsf P^0_{\theta,\pi,i}(r)
-
\int \phi(r)\,d\mathsf P^j_{\theta,\pi,i}(r)
\right\|_H,
\]
\(\mathsf P^0_{\theta,\pi,i}\) is the law of ratio \(i\) under the observational environment of \(W_{\theta,\pi}^{\mathrm{can}}\), and \(\mathsf P^j_{\theta,\pi,i}\) is the law of ratio \(i\) under intervention environment \(j\) of \(W_{\theta,\pi}^{\mathrm{can}}\).
\end{definitionv}

The cover relation \leanref{sym:j\lessdot_G i}{\ensuremath{j\lessdot_G i}} selects ancestral covers in the latent DAG, and \cref{obj:def:cover-separated-set} requires a positive ratio-law discrepancy on every such transported cover after applying \(\pi\). Thus \leanref{sym:\mathcal U_{G,s,\pi}}{\ensuremath{\mathcal U_{G,s,\pi}}} is the population class on which the observable discrepancy graph contains the cover information needed by the decoder developed in the next section.
