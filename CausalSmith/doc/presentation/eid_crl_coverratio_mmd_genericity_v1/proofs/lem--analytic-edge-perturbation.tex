\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:analytic-edge-perturbation}]
Work after the stated relabeling, so the target permutation is the identity.  Let
\(\theta^\star\) denote the edge-specific sparse endpoint used in
\cref{obj:def:affine-path}, and write the affine extension for real \(t\) as
\[
p_\ell^t=(1-t)p_\ell+t p_\ell^\star,\qquad
q_\ell^t=(1-t)q_\ell+t q_\ell^\star .
\]

\emph{Step 1: the positive analytic parameter set.}
What the rest of the argument uses is an open convex set \(O\supseteq[0,1]\) on which every path mechanism is positive and normalized, together with real analyticity on \(O\) of the parameter integral displayed in Step 2.  We exhibit one such set.  Strict positivity of \(\theta\in\Theta_{G,s}\), strict positivity of the sparse endpoint, continuity on the compact cubes, and finiteness of the node set give a number \(m>0\) such that every \(p_\ell,p_\ell^\star,q_\ell,q_\ell^\star\) is at least \(m\) on its domain; any positive lower bound serves equally well below.  Define
\[
O_p=\left\{t:\ \frac m2<(1-t)p_\ell(v)+t p_\ell^\star(v)
\text{ for all }\ell\text{ and all }v\in[0,1]^n\right\},
\]
\[
O_q=\left\{t:\ \frac m2<(1-t)q_\ell(z)+t q_\ell^\star(z)
\text{ for all }\ell\text{ and all }z\in[0,1]\right\},
\qquad O=O_p\cap O_q .
\]
For fixed \(\ell\) and cube point, the two displayed inequalities define open half-lines in the parameter \(t\).  Taking the infimum over the compact sets \([n]\times[0,1]^n\) and \([n]\times[0,1]\) gives continuous lower-envelope functions, so \(O_p\) and \(O_q\) are open; as intersections of affine strict-superlevel conditions, they are convex.  If \(0\le t\le1\), then for every admissible argument
\[
(1-t)m+tm=m
\le (1-t)p_\ell+t p_\ell^\star,
\qquad
(1-t)m+tm=m
\le (1-t)q_\ell+t q_\ell^\star,
\]
and hence \([0,1]\subseteq O\).  On \(O\), all \(p_\ell^t\) and \(q_\ell^t\) are positive.  Normalization and \(C^3\)-smoothness are affine identities: for every real \(t\), every \(v\in[0,1]^n\), and every \(\ell\),
\[
\int_0^1 p_\ell^t(z\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(\ell)})\,dz
=(1-t)+t=1,\qquad
\int_0^1 q_\ell^t(z)\,dz=(1-t)+t=1,
\]
and the derivatives up to order three are the same affine combinations of the endpoint derivatives.  Moreover, for every \(t\in O\) and \(v\in[0,1]^n\),
\[
\frac m2\le p_i^t(v_i\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)}).
\]

We now make the analytic-under-the-integral step explicit.  Put
\[
\mathsf D(t,v)=(1-t)p_i(v_i\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)})
+t p_i^\star(v_i\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)})
\]
and
\[
\mathsf P(t,v)
=(q_i^t(v_i))^2\bigl(q_j^t(v_j)-p_j^t(v_j\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(j)})\bigr)
\prod_{\ell\ne i,j}p_\ell^t(v_\ell\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(\ell)}),
\]
with the empty product equal to \(1\).  Since the product has finitely many factors and every factor is affine in \(t\), there are measurable coefficient functions \(\mathsf a_0,\ldots,\mathsf a_{n+3}:[0,1]^n\to\mathbb R\), continuous on the cube after choosing the standard continuous representatives on the ambient cube, such that
\[
\mathsf P(t,v)=\sum_{r=0}^{n+3}\mathsf a_r(v)t^r,
\qquad
\sup_{v\in[0,1]^n}|\mathsf a_r(v)|<\infty
\quad(0\le r\le n+3).
\]
Fix \(t_0\in O\).  The denominator satisfies \(\mathsf D(t_0,v)\ge m/2\) on the compact latent cube, while
\[
\mathsf D(t,v)=\mathsf D(t_0,v)+(t-t_0)
\{p_i^\star(v_i\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)})-p_i(v_i\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)})\}.
\]
By compactness, choose \(\rho_{t_0}>0\) so that for \(|t-t_0|<\rho_{t_0}\),
\[
\left|
(t-t_0)\frac{p_i^\star(v_i\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)})
-p_i(v_i\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)})}{\mathsf D(t_0,v)}
\right|\le \frac12
\quad\text{for all }v\in[0,1]^n.
\]
Then
\[
\frac1{\mathsf D(t,v)}
=\frac1{\mathsf D(t_0,v)}
\sum_{r=0}^\infty
\left[-(t-t_0)\frac{p_i^\star(v_i\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)})
-p_i(v_i\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)})}{\mathsf D(t_0,v)}\right]^r
\]
converges uniformly in \(v\).  Multiplying by the polynomial \(\mathsf P(t,v)\), the coefficient bounds and the finite volume of \([0,1]^n\) give a summable dominating majorant on a smaller neighborhood of \(t_0\).  Termwise integration therefore gives a power series for
\[
t\longmapsto \int_{[0,1]^n}\frac{\mathsf P(t,v)}{\mathsf D(t,v)}\,dv
\]
near \(t_0\).  Thus the parameter integral is real analytic on a neighborhood of every point of \(O\). % lean: affinePathContrastIntegral_analyticOnNhd

\emph{Step 2: the integral identity.}
Since \(j\to i\) is an edge in a DAG, \(j\ne i\).  For each \(t\in O\), the construction of \(O\) supplies positive normalized smooth mechanisms.  In the identity-target canonical observed world generated by \(\theta^t\), the latent observational density, target-\(j\) density, and target-\(i\) ratio are
\[
p^{0,t}(v)=\prod_{\ell=1}^n p_\ell^t(v_\ell\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(\ell)}),
\qquad
p^{j,t}(v)=q_j^t(v_j)\prod_{\ell\ne j}p_\ell^t(v_\ell\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(\ell)}),
\]
and
\[
R_i^t(v)=\frac{q_i^t(v_i)}
{p_i^t(v_i\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)})}.
\]
The lower bound on \(p_i^t\) makes the ratio continuous and integrable on the compact latent cube.  Therefore, with the empty product interpreted as \(1\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
F_{ji}(\theta^t)
&=\int_{[0,1]^n}(R_i^t(v))^2\{p^{j,t}(v)-p^{0,t}(v)\}\,dv\\
&=\int_{[0,1]^n}
\frac{(q_i^t(v_i))^2
\bigl(q_j^t(v_j)-p_j^t(v_j\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(j)})\bigr)
\prod_{\ell\ne i,j}p_\ell^t(v_\ell\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(\ell)})}
{p_i^t(v_i\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i)})}\,dv .
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves the displayed identity for every \(t\in O\).  The right-hand side is the analytic parameter integral constructed above, so the same is true of \(t\mapsto F_{ji}(\theta^t)\). % lean: canonical_secondMomentContrast_eq_integral

\emph{Step 3: the endpoint is nonzero.}
At \(t=1\), the path is the embedded sparse endpoint.  Write
\[
h(z)=2z-1,
\qquad
\mathfrak q(z)=\frac{4\exp(4z)}{\exp(4)-1},
\qquad
\rho_\ell(z)=\operatorname{reflect}(s_\ell,z),
\]
where \(\rho_\ell\) is the coordinate reflection appearing in \cref{obj:def:affine-path}.  At the sparse endpoint,
\[
p_i^1(v)=1+\frac{1}{10}h(\rho_j(v_j))h(\rho_i(v_i)),
\qquad
p_j^1(v)=1,
\qquad
q_\ell^1(z)=\mathfrak q(\rho_\ell(z)),
\]
and \(p_\ell^1(v)=1\) for \(\ell\ne i\).  Since the edge \(j\to i\) gives \(j\ne i\), the two selected coordinates form a two-element set.  The integrand in the identity from Step 2 is therefore
\[
\Phi(v)=
\frac{\mathfrak q(\rho_i(v_i))^2\bigl(\mathfrak q(\rho_j(v_j))-1\bigr)}
{1+\frac{1}{10}h(\rho_j(v_j))h(\rho_i(v_i))},
\]
which depends only on \((v_j,v_i)\); the finite product over \(\ell\ne i,j\) is \(1\), with the empty-product convention covering the case of no unused coordinates.  Product integration on \([0,1]^n\) then gives
\[
F_{ji}(\theta^1)
=\int_0^1\!\int_0^1
\frac{\mathfrak q(\rho_i(y))^2\bigl(\mathfrak q(\rho_j(x))-1\bigr)}
{1+\frac{1}{10}h(\rho_j(x))h(\rho_i(y))}\,dy\,dx .
\]
Each \(\rho_\ell\) is either the identity map or \(z\mapsto1-z\), hence preserves Lebesgue measure on \([0,1]\).  Define, for \(x\in[0,1]\),
\[
\mathcal A(x)=\int_0^1
\frac{\mathfrak q(y)^2}
{1+\frac{1}{10}h(x)h(y)}\,dy .
\]
Changing variables first in \(y\) and then in \(x\) gives
\[
F_{ji}(\theta^1)
=\int_0^1 \bigl(\mathfrak q(x)-1\bigr)\mathcal A(x)\,dx .
\]

It remains to establish the scalar gap for this last integral.  Introduce the auxiliary primitive
\[
H(x)=\frac{\exp(4x)-1}{\exp(4)-1}-x,
\]
and, for \(a\in[-1/10,1/10]\), set
\[
\omega_a(y)=\frac{\mathfrak q(y)^2}{(1+a h(y))^2},
\qquad y\in[0,1].
\]
The identities \(H(0)=H(1)=0\) and \(H'(x)=\mathfrak q(x)-1\) imply, by integration by parts,
\[
\int_0^1 \bigl(1-\mathfrak q(x)\bigr)\mathcal A(x)\,dx
=\int_0^1 H(x)\mathcal A'(x)\,dx .
\]
For \(x\in[0,1]\), differentiating the defining integral for \(\mathcal A\) under the integral sign gives
\[
\mathcal A'(x)
=-\frac15\int_0^1 h(y)\omega_{h(x)/10}(y)\,dy .
\]
The differentiation is justified because \(|h(y)|\le1\) and \(h(x)/10\in[-1/10,1/10]\), so the denominator is bounded away from zero uniformly on the square.

For every \(a\in[-1/10,1/10]\) and \(y\in[0,1]\), the same bounds give \(1+a h(y)\in[9/10,11/10]\).  Together with \(13<\exp(4)<55\), this yields
\[
\omega_a(y)>\left(\frac{4}{729}\right)\left(\frac{100}{121}\right),
\qquad
8-\frac{4a}{1+a h(y)}\ge \frac{68}{9}.
\]
Since
\[
\omega_a'(y)
=\omega_a(y)\left(8-\frac{4a}{1+a h(y)}\right),
\]
we have the uniform derivative bound
\[
\left(\frac{68}{9}\right)\left(\frac{4}{729}\right)\left(\frac{100}{121}\right)
\le \omega_a'(y).
\]
Consequently, for \(0\le z\le y\le1\),
\[
\left(\frac{68}{9}\right)\left(\frac{4}{729}\right)\left(\frac{100}{121}\right)(y-z)
\le \omega_a(y)-\omega_a(z).
\]
Applying this secant estimate on the intervals from \(1/2\) to \(y\) and from \(y\) to \(1/2\) gives, for every \(y\in[0,1]\),
\[
\left(\frac{68}{9}\right)\left(\frac{4}{729}\right)\left(\frac{100}{121}\right)2\left(y-\frac12\right)^2
\le h(y)\bigl(\omega_a(y)-\omega_a(1/2)\bigr).
\]
Because \(\int_0^1 h(y)\,dy=0\) and \(\int_0^1 2(y-1/2)^2\,dy=1/6\), integration gives
\[
\frac16\left(\frac{68}{9}\right)\left(\frac{4}{729}\right)\left(\frac{100}{121}\right)
\le
\int_0^1 h(y)\omega_a(y)\,dy .
\]
Hence, for \(x\in[0,1]\),
\[
\mathcal A'(x)
\le
-\frac1{30}\left(\frac{68}{9}\right)\left(\frac{4}{729}\right)\left(\frac{100}{121}\right).
\]
The exponential distribution function in \(H\) lies in \([0,1]\), and convexity of the exponential on \([0,4]\) gives \((\exp(4x)-1)/(\exp(4)-1)\le x\); therefore \(-1\le H(x)\le0\) on \([0,1]\).  Multiplying the last derivative bound by the nonpositive function \(H\) and integrating yields
\[
\int_0^1 \bigl(1-\mathfrak q(x)\bigr)\mathcal A(x)\,dx
\ge
\frac1{30}\left(\frac{68}{9}\right)\left(\frac{4}{729}\right)\left(\frac{100}{121}\right)
\int_0^1 -H(x)\,dx .
\]
A direct integration of the exponential density gives
\[
\int_0^1 x\mathfrak q(x)\,dx
=\frac{1}{1-\exp(-4)}-\frac14,
\]
so integration by parts gives
\[
\int_0^1 -H(x)\,dx
=\frac{1}{1-\exp(-4)}-\frac34
>\frac{29}{108}.
\]
Combining the rational factors,
\[
\frac1{30}\left(\frac{68}{9}\right)\left(\frac{4}{729}\right)\left(\frac{100}{121}\right)\left(\frac{29}{108}\right)
=\frac{19720}{64304361}
>\frac{3}{10000},
\]
and hence
\[
\frac{3}{10000}
<
\int_0^1 \bigl(1-\mathfrak q(x)\bigr)\mathcal A(x)\,dx .
\]
Thus
\[
F_{ji}(\theta^1)
=-\int_0^1 \bigl(1-\mathfrak q(x)\bigr)\mathcal A(x)\,dx
< -\frac{3}{10000},
\]
and in particular
\[
F_{ji}(\theta^1)\ne0 .
\] % lean: affinePathContrast_one_ne_zero

\emph{Step 4: isolated zeros.}
Let \(t_0\in O\) and suppose \(F_{ji}(\theta^{t_0})=0\).  The set \(O\) is convex, hence preconnected, and \(1\in O\) with \(F_{ji}(\theta^1)\ne0\).  For a real analytic function on a preconnected set, the local dichotomy at \(t_0\) is: either the function vanishes on a neighborhood of \(t_0\), or it is nonzero on a punctured neighborhood of \(t_0\).  The first alternative would force the function to vanish throughout \(O\), contradicting the nonzero value at \(1\).  Thus there is \(\varepsilon>0\) such that
\[
u\in(t_0-\varepsilon,t_0+\varepsilon),\quad u\ne t_0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
F_{ji}(\theta^u)\ne0 .
\] % lean: analyticOnNhd_isolated_zeros_of_nonzero

\emph{Step 5: small stratum-preserving nonzero perturbations.}
Let \(N\) be any neighborhood of \(\theta\) in the topology of \cref{obj:def:model-stratum}; in particular this covers every relative \(C^2\) neighborhood in the statement.  The affine path satisfies
\[
\|p_\ell^t-p_\ell\|_{C^2}=t\|p_\ell^\star-p_\ell\|_{C^2},
\qquad
\|q_\ell^t-q_\ell\|_{C^2}=t\|q_\ell^\star-q_\ell\|_{C^2},
\]
so \(\theta^t\to\theta\) in the topology of \cref{obj:def:model-stratum}.  Hence there is \(\delta_N>0\) such that \(0<t<\delta_N\) implies \(\theta^t\in N\).

The conditions defining \(\Theta_{G,s}\) persist on a sufficiently short initial segment.  Positivity, normalization, and smoothness are handled first.  For every \(t\in[0,1]\), convexity of the positive endpoint densities gives positivity of every \(p_\ell^t\) and \(q_\ell^t\), and the affine identities
\[
\int_0^1 p_\ell^t(z\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(\ell)})\,dz=(1-t)+t=1,
\qquad
\int_0^1 q_\ell^t(z)\,dz=(1-t)+t=1
\]
show normalization; \(C^3\)-smoothness follows by applying the same affine combination to derivatives up to order three.

For the fixed-sign part, define for \(\ell\in[n]\), \(t\in\mathbb R\), and \(v\in[0,1]^n\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Sigma_\ell(t,v)
=s_\ell\Bigg(
&\frac{(1-t)\partial_z q_\ell(v_\ell)+t\partial_z q_\ell^\star(v_\ell)}
{(1-t)q_\ell(v_\ell)+tq_\ell^\star(v_\ell)} \\
&-\frac{(1-t)\partial_{v_\ell}p_\ell(v)+t\partial_{v_\ell}p_\ell^\star(v)}
{(1-t)p_\ell(v)+tp_\ell^\star(v)}
\Bigg),
\end{aligned}
\]
where \(\partial_z\) is the one-dimensional derivative on \([0,1]\) and \(\partial_{v_\ell}\) is the own-coordinate derivative on the latent cube.  Here \(p_\ell(v)\) abbreviates \(p_\ell(v_\ell\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(\ell)})\) regarded as a function on the whole latent cube, and likewise \(p_\ell^\star(v)\).  At \(t=0\), this is exactly the fixed own-coordinate log-ratio derivative required in \cref{obj:ass:fixed-own-derivative-sign}.  The numerator and denominator terms in \(\Sigma_\ell\) are continuous in \((t,v)\), and the denominators are bounded away from zero at \(t=0\).  Since \([0,1]^n\) is compact and there are finitely many \(\ell\), the strict inequalities \(\Sigma_\ell(0,v)>0\) persist uniformly for all \(v\in[0,1]^n\) and all sufficiently small \(t\ge0\).

It remains to record the local witness used for causal minimality.  For a positive normalized smooth mechanism \(\vartheta\), let \(\mathcal F_\vartheta\) be the compact product factorization on \(\prod_{r\in[n]}[0,1]\) with local factor
\[
\varphi_{\vartheta,r}(\xi)=p_{r,\vartheta}(\xi_r\mid \xi_{\operatorname{pa}_G(r)}),
\qquad \xi\in[0,1]^n.
\]
For an edge \(j_0\to i_0\), a base point \(\xi\in[0,1]^n\), child values \(y_0,y_1\in[0,1]\), and parent values \(x_0,x_1\in[0,1]\), define the local determinant
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L_{i_0j_0}^{\vartheta}(\xi;y_0,y_1,x_0,x_1)
&=\varphi_{\vartheta,i_0}(\xi[i_0:=y_0,j_0:=x_0])
  \varphi_{\vartheta,i_0}(\xi[i_0:=y_1,j_0:=x_1])\\
&\quad-\varphi_{\vartheta,i_0}(\xi[i_0:=y_0,j_0:=x_1])
  \varphi_{\vartheta,i_0}(\xi[i_0:=y_1,j_0:=x_0]),
\end{aligned}
\]
where \(\xi[i_0:=y,j_0:=x]\) is obtained from \(\xi\) by replacing the \(i_0\)-coordinate by \(y\) and the \(j_0\)-coordinate by \(x\).  In a positive compact product factorization,
\[
V_{i_0}\perp\!\!\!\perp V_{j_0}
\mid V_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i_0)\setminus\{j_0\}}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathcal L_{i_0j_0}^{\vartheta}(\xi;y_0,y_1,x_0,x_1)=0
\]
for all such \(\xi,y_0,y_1,x_0,x_1\).  Indeed, after conditioning on \(V_{\operatorname{pa}_G(i_0)\setminus\{j_0\}}\), positivity lets one cancel the remaining factors, and conditional independence is exactly the rank-one condition that all two-by-two determinants of the child factor as a function of \((v_{i_0},v_{j_0})\) vanish.  The compact-coordinate statement is the same as the coordinate conditional-independence statement in \cref{obj:ass:causal-minimality}, because both use the same cube-supported observational density.

Since \(\theta\in\Theta_{G,s}\), for every edge \(j_0\to i_0\) there is a witness
\[
(\xi_{i_0j_0},y_{i_0j_0}^0,y_{i_0j_0}^1,x_{i_0j_0}^0,x_{i_0j_0}^1)
\]
with
\[
\mathcal L_{i_0j_0}^{\theta}
(\xi_{i_0j_0};y_{i_0j_0}^0,y_{i_0j_0}^1,
 x_{i_0j_0}^0,x_{i_0j_0}^1)\ne0 .
\]
For each fixed witness, the map \((z_1,z_2,z_3,z_4)\mapsto z_1z_2-z_3z_4\) is continuous, so the witness remains nonzero when the corresponding local factors are uniformly close to those of \(\theta\).  Taking the minimum over the finite edge set gives \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{fac}}>0\) such that every positive compact factorization satisfying
\[
\max_{r\in[n]}\sup_{\xi\in[0,1]^n}
\bigl|\varphi_{\vartheta,r}(\xi)-\varphi_{\theta,r}(\xi)\bigr|<\varepsilon_{\mathrm{fac}}
\]
has the same nonzero witnesses and hence satisfies causal minimality.  If the edge set is empty, the condition is vacuous.

Along the affine path,
\[
\max_{r\in[n]}\sup_{\xi\in[0,1]^n}
\bigl|p_r^t(\xi)-p_r(\xi)\bigr|
\le
 t\max_{r\in[n]}\sup_{\xi\in[0,1]^n}
\bigl|p_r^\star(\xi)-p_r(\xi)\bigr|,
\]
so the factorization neighborhood above contains \(\theta^t\) for all sufficiently small \(t\ge0\).  Combining the closed-segment positive normalized smoothness with the local fixed-sign and causal-minimality neighborhoods, there is \(\delta_s>0\) such that \(0\le t\le1\) and \(t<\delta_s\) imply \(\theta^t\in\Theta_{G,s}\). % lean: affinePathExtension_eventually_modelStratum

It remains to choose \(t\) avoiding the zero set.  If \(F_{ji}(\theta)\ne0\), analyticity gives continuity at \(0\), so \(F_{ji}(\theta^u)\ne0\) for all sufficiently small \(u\).  If \(F_{ji}(\theta)=0\), the isolated-zero conclusion at \(0\in O\) gives the same conclusion for all sufficiently small nonzero \(u\).  Hence there is \(\delta_z>0\) such that
\[
0<|u|<\delta_z
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
F_{ji}(\theta^u)\ne0 .
\]
Set
\[
r=\min\{1,\delta_s,\delta_N,\delta_z\},\qquad t=\frac r2 .
\]
Then \(t\in(0,1)\), \(\theta^t\in N\), \(\theta^t\in\Theta_{G,s}\), and \(F_{ji}(\theta^t)\ne0\). % lean: affinePath_exists_small_stratum_nonzero
\end{proof}
