\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:thm:exact-ratio-decoder}]
Put \(W=\mathsf{world}(\theta)\), let \(\mathcal P=(P^0,\ldots,P^n)\), and write
\[
H_D=\{j\to i:j\ne i\text{ and }0<D_{ji}\}.
\]
Throughout the proof the superscript \(0\) is reserved for the observational environment, while for \(i\in[n]\) the notation \(P^i\) denotes the single-target intervention environment whose target is \(\pi(i)\); hence \(R_i=dP^i/dP^0\) always compares intervention environment \(i\) with the observational law. The assumptions in \cref{obj:ass:positive-normalized-smooth-mechanisms,obj:ass:shared-diffeomorphic-mixing,obj:ass:one-perfect-intervention-per-node} make each \(P^e\) a probability law. They also give the coherence of \(\mathcal P\) with the observed world \(W\), in the following concrete sense. If \(\mathcal R_i^W\) denotes the likelihood-ratio function specified by the world \(W\) for environment \(i\), and if \(R_i\) denotes the observed-law likelihood ratio selected from \((P^0,
P^i)\), then
\[
\mathcal R_i^W(x)=R_i(x)
\qquad\text{for }P^0\text{-almost every }x.
\]
The same coherence statement records that selected rank coordinates are functions of the common law family whenever the two worlds have the same observed laws. More precisely, if \(W'\) is compatible with \(W\), so that both worlds have the same environment-law family \(\mathcal P\), then for every order \(\tau\), every \(i\), and every point \(x\) in the observed support of \(W\),
\[
C_i^{W'}\!\left(L_i^{W'}(x)\mid L_{B_i^\tau}^{W'}(x)\right)
=
C_i^{W}\!\left(L_i^{W}(x)\mid L_{B_i^\tau}^{W}(x)\right),
\]
where each side is the selected rank coordinate computed from the same observed law family. Separately, the pointwise ratio formula in \cref{obj:ass:one-perfect-intervention-per-node}, together with the continuous observed-law representative of the likelihood ratio, gives, for every \(i\) and every \(v\in[0,1]^n\),
\begin{equation}
L_i(f(v))
=
\log R_i(f(v))
=
\log
\frac{q_{\pi(i)}(v_{\pi(i)})}
{p_{\pi(i)}(v_{\pi(i)}\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(\pi(i))})}.
\label{eq:exact-ratio-decoder-log-ratio}
\end{equation}
If \(n=0\), all indexed conclusions below are over empty finite sets and the same displayed identities are vacuous; the argument is otherwise unchanged. % lean: observedWorldLawCoherent_of_assumptions

The cover-separation premise is stated for the canonical identity-mixing world. For an ancestral cover \(a\lessdot_G b\), \cref{obj:def:cover-separated-set} gives
\[
0<
D_{\pi^{-1}(a),\pi^{-1}(b)}(W_{\theta,\pi}^{\mathrm{can}}).
\]
The transfer from the canonical world to the supplied world is needed only on ancestral covers. Applied to the displayed cover, it rewrites the positive canonical population discrepancy as the corresponding supplied-world observed-law discrepancy, using \cref{obj:ass:one-perfect-intervention-per-node} for the environment pushforwards, \cref{obj:ass:shared-diffeomorphic-mixing} for the inverse on observed support, and the almost-sure ratio coherence established above. Hence
\begin{equation}
0<
D_{\pi^{-1}(a),\pi^{-1}(b)}(W)
\qquad(a\lessdot_G b).
\label{eq:exact-ratio-decoder-cover-positive}
\end{equation}
Thus every transported ancestral cover appears as an edge of \(H_D\). % lean: canonical_coverDiscrepancy_to_supplied

Conversely, \cref{obj:lem:ratio-nonancestor-zero} applies under \cref{obj:ass:positive-normalized-smooth-mechanisms,obj:ass:shared-diffeomorphic-mixing,obj:ass:one-perfect-intervention-per-node}. Hence, whenever \(j\ne i\) and \(\pi(j)\) is not an ancestor of \(\pi(i)\) in \(G\), the Gaussian-kernel discrepancy of ratio \(i\) between the observational environment and intervention environment \(j\) satisfies
\[
D_{ji}=0.
\]
The lemma gives this conclusion by marginalizing over the complement of the ancestral set \(\operatorname{an}_G(\pi(i))\cup\{\pi(i)\}\): the observational and intervention-\(j\) marginal laws agree on the coordinates on which \(R_i\) depends, so the two ratio pushforward laws, and therefore their Gaussian mean embeddings, coincide. % lean: ratio_nonancestor_zero
We now pass from these discrepancy statements to the graph identity. Let \(G^\pi\) denote the environment-label graph
\[
j\to i\text{ in }G^\pi
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\pi(j)\to\pi(i)\text{ in }G.
\]
If \(j\to i\) is an edge of \(H_D\), then \(D_{ji}>0\). The zero-discrepancy conclusion above shows that \(\pi(j)\) must be an ancestor of \(\pi(i)\) in \(G\); hence every edge of \(H_D\) is contained in the transitive closure of \(G^\pi\). Therefore
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{TC}(H_D)\subseteq\operatorname{TC}(G^\pi).
\label{eq:exact-ratio-decoder-tc-forward}
\end{equation}
For the reverse inclusion, take any ancestral relation \(\pi(j)\leadsto_G\pi(i)\). In the finite ancestral order, choose a saturated chain
\[
\pi(j)=a_0\lessdot_G a_1\lessdot_G\cdots\lessdot_G a_m=\pi(i),
\]
where each step is an ancestral cover. By \cref{eq:exact-ratio-decoder-cover-positive}, each transported pair
\[
\pi^{-1}(a_{r-1})\to\pi^{-1}(a_r),
\qquad r=1,\ldots,m,
\]
is an edge of \(H_D\). Thus \(j\) reaches \(i\) in \(H_D\), and
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{TC}(G^\pi)\subseteq\operatorname{TC}(H_D).
\label{eq:exact-ratio-decoder-tc-reverse}
\end{equation}
Combining \cref{eq:exact-ratio-decoder-tc-forward,eq:exact-ratio-decoder-tc-reverse} gives
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{TC}(H_D)=\operatorname{TC}(G^\pi).
\label{eq:exact-ratio-decoder-tc}
\end{equation}
The graph reconstruction statement also supplies the decoder's selected order as a topological ordering of \(H_D\). If \(\tau\) is any topological ordering of \(H_D\) and \(a\in\operatorname{pa}_G(\pi(i))\), then \(\pi^{-1}(a)\) reaches \(i\) in \(H_D\) by \cref{eq:exact-ratio-decoder-tc}; applying the topological-order inequalities along the path gives
\begin{equation}
\pi^{-1}\!\bigl(\operatorname{pa}_G(\pi(i))\bigr)
\subseteq
B_i^\tau:=\{j:\tau(j)<\tau(i)\}.
\label{eq:exact-ratio-decoder-predecessors}
\end{equation}
% lean: ratioGraph_reconstruction_order_and_predecessors

Fix such an order \(\tau\). By \cref{eq:exact-ratio-decoder-tc}, \(\tau\) also respects every transported latent edge. For \(i\in[n]\), set
\[
A_i^\tau=\{\pi(i)\}\cup\pi(B_i^\tau).
\]
Let
\[
\mathcal Z_i^\tau=\prod_{b\in B_i^\tau}[0,1],
\]
with the usual one-point product when \(B_i^\tau=\varnothing\). Embed \(\chi\in\mathcal Z_i^\tau\) into the full latent cube by the zero-filled map
\[
E_i^\tau(\chi)_a=
\begin{cases}
\chi_b, & a=\pi(b)\text{ for some }b\in B_i^\tau,\\
0, & a\notin\pi(B_i^\tau).
\end{cases}
\]
Define the predecessor score map and its range by
\[
\Lambda_i^\tau:\mathcal Z_i^\tau\to\mathbb R^{B_i^\tau},
\qquad
\Lambda_i^\tau(\chi)_b
=
\log
\frac{q_{\pi(b)}\!\left(E_i^\tau(\chi)_{\pi(b)}\right)}
{p_{\pi(b)}\!\left(E_i^\tau(\chi)_{\pi(b)}\mid
E_i^\tau(\chi)_{\operatorname{pa}_G(\pi(b))}\right)},
\qquad
\mathcal R_i^\tau=\Lambda_i^\tau(\mathcal Z_i^\tau).
\]
If \(v\in[0,1]^n\), then \(\chi_b=v_{\pi(b)}\) gives
\[
\Lambda_i^\tau(\chi)_b=L_b(f(v)),
\qquad b\in B_i^\tau.
\]
Indeed, every parent of \(\pi(b)\) has environment label preceding \(b\), hence belongs to \(B_i^\tau\); on the own coordinate and on those parent coordinates the zero-filled point \(E_i^\tau(\chi)\) agrees with \(v\), and the local dependence of \(p_{\pi(b)}\) removes all other coordinates. By \cref{obj:lem:predecessor-score-homeomorphism}, applied to the present order \(\tau\),
\begin{equation}
\Lambda_i^\tau:\mathcal Z_i^\tau\longrightarrow\mathcal R_i^\tau
\quad\text{is a homeomorphism onto its image.}
\label{eq:exact-ratio-decoder-predecessor-homeomorphism}
\end{equation}
In particular, \(\mathcal R_i^\tau\) is compact and the inverse \((\Lambda_i^\tau)^{-1}:\mathcal R_i^\tau\to\mathcal Z_i^\tau\) is continuous. % lean: predecessorScoreHomeomorph

Now work under the target-\(\pi(i)\) environment. For \(\zeta\in[0,1]^{[n]\setminus A_i^\tau}\), let \(u^{v,\tau}(\zeta)\in[0,1]^n\) be the filled-in latent point defined by
\[
u^{v,\tau}(\zeta)_a=
\begin{cases}
v_a, & a\in A_i^\tau,\\
\zeta_a, & a\notin A_i^\tau.
\end{cases}
\]
The marginalization identity in \cref{obj:lem:equation-ten-intervention-marginal} gives, for \(v\in[0,1]^n\),
\begin{equation}
\int_{[0,1]^{[n]\setminus A_i^\tau}}
q_{\pi(i)}\!\left(u^{v,\tau}(\zeta)_{\pi(i)}\right)
\prod_{\ell\ne\pi(i)}
p_\ell\!\left(u^{v,\tau}(\zeta)_\ell\mid
u^{v,\tau}(\zeta)_{\operatorname{pa}_G(\ell)}\right)\,d\zeta
=
q_{\pi(i)}(v_{\pi(i)})
\prod_{\ell\in A_i^\tau\setminus\{\pi(i)\}}
p_\ell(v_\ell\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(\ell)}).
\label{eq:exact-ratio-decoder-marginal}
\end{equation}
The fiber version in \cref{obj:lem:equation-ten-fiber-product} gives the same factorization after replacing \(v_{\pi(i)}\) by an arbitrary scalar \(w\). These identities are the density input for \cref{obj:lem:exact-ratio-decoder-continuous-rank-clauses}. Applying that result to the order \(\tau\), the law-selected conditional CDF \(C_i(t\mid L_{B_i^\tau})\) used by \(\mathscr D\) is a continuous conditional-CDF version on the observed conditional-ratio support, and there is a continuous version \(C\) such that, for all \(t\in\mathbb R\) and \(v\in[0,1]^n\),
\begin{equation}
C\!\left(t\mid L_{B_i^\tau}(f(v))\right)
=
C_{\theta,\pi(i)}^{\mathrm{str}}(t\mid v),
\label{eq:exact-ratio-decoder-continuous-version}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
C_{\theta,\pi(i)}^{\mathrm{str}}(t\mid v)
=
\int_0^1
\mathbf 1\!\left\{
\log\frac{q_{\pi(i)}(w)}
{p_{\pi(i)}(w\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(\pi(i))})}
\le t
\right\}
q_{\pi(i)}(w)\,dw.
\label{eq:exact-ratio-decoder-structural-cdf}
\end{equation}
Here \(p_{\pi(i)}(w\mid v_{\operatorname{pa}_G(\pi(i))})\) is the value \(p_{\pi(i)}(v^{(w)})\) of the mechanism at the point \(v^{(w)}\) obtained from \(v\) by replacing coordinate \(\pi(i)\) with \(w\); the two agree because a mechanism depends only on its own coordinate and its parents.
The same cited result states that every continuous conditional-CDF version agrees with the selected version on the observed conditional-ratio support and that the selected version itself satisfies the structural identity at every mixed latent point:
\begin{equation}
C_i\!\left(t\mid L_{B_i^\tau}(f(v))\right)
=
C_{\theta,\pi(i)}^{\mathrm{str}}(t\mid v),
\qquad v\in[0,1]^n.
\label{eq:exact-ratio-decoder-selected-cdf}
\end{equation}
% lean: exactRatioDecoder_continuousRankClauses

Evaluating \cref{eq:exact-ratio-decoder-selected-cdf} at \(t=L_i(f(v))\) and using the strict monotonicity from \cref{obj:ass:fixed-own-derivative-sign} gives
\begin{equation}
U_i(f(v))
=
\begin{cases}
Q_{\pi(i)}(v_{\pi(i)}), & s_{\pi(i)}=1,\\
1-Q_{\pi(i)}(v_{\pi(i)}), & s_{\pi(i)}=-1.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:exact-ratio-decoder-rank-coordinate}
\end{equation}
The right-hand side is a componentwise continuous, strictly monotone transform of the latent coordinate. % lean: exactRatioDecoder_continuousRankClauses

We next prove the pruning characterization used by the decoder. Let
\[
P_i^\pi=\pi^{-1}\!\bigl(\operatorname{pa}_G(\pi(i))\bigr),
\qquad
S_i^\tau=B_i^\tau.
\]
For every \(A\) with \(A\subseteq S_i^\tau\), the target assertion is
\begin{equation}
U_i\perp\!\!\!\perp U_{S_i^\tau\setminus A}\mid U_A
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
P_i^\pi\subseteq A.
\label{eq:exact-ratio-decoder-pruning-claim}
\end{equation}
The pruning characterization is supplied by the rank conditional-independence characterization applied at the present order. Its hypotheses are exactly the positivity, causal minimality, shared mixing, one-intervention, sign, and rank-identity clauses already established, together with the subset bookkeeping \(A\subseteq S_i^\tau=B_i^\tau\). It yields
\begin{equation}
U_i\perp\!\!\!\perp U_{B_i^\tau\setminus A}\mid U_A
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
P_i^\pi\subseteq A.
\label{eq:exact-ratio-decoder-pruning-iff}
\end{equation}
% lean: exactRankCondIndepCharacterization

\Cref{eq:exact-ratio-decoder-pruning-iff} says exactly that the admissible sets are the supersets of \(P_i^\pi\). Indeed, by \cref{obj:def:population-decoder}, a set \(A\subseteq B_i^\tau\) is admissible precisely when the left side of \cref{eq:exact-ratio-decoder-pruning-iff} holds. Thus \(P_i^\pi\) is admissible by \cref{eq:exact-ratio-decoder-predecessors}, and if \(A\) is any admissible set then \(P_i^\pi\subseteq A\). Conversely every superset of \(P_i^\pi\) contained in \(B_i^\tau\) is admissible. Hence \(P_i^\pi\) is the unique inclusion-minimal admissible parent set for \(i\), for every topological ordering \(\tau\) of \(H_D\). Applying this extraction to the selected order in \cref{obj:def:population-decoder}, the parent-pruned graph has edge relation
\[
a\to i
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
a\in P_i^\pi
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\pi(a)\in\operatorname{pa}_G(\pi(i)),
\]
which is exactly
\begin{equation}
G_{\mathscr D}^\pi=G^\pi.
\label{eq:exact-ratio-decoder-selected-graph}
\end{equation}
% lean: selectedParentDAG_eq_permutedGraph_of_exactPruning

It remains to prove representation uniqueness. Let \(G'\), \(\theta'\), and \(W'\) satisfy the same regularity, causal-minimality, sign, mixing, and one-intervention assumptions, and suppose \(W'\) has the same observed law family as \(W\). Write \(\widetilde f\) and \(\widetilde\pi\) for the mixing map and target permutation of \(W'\); these are in general different from \(f\) and \(\pi\). Let \(\upsilon'\) be the canonical topological ordering of \(G'\), and define the environment-label order
\[
\tau'(e)=\upsilon'(\widetilde\pi(e)),
\qquad e\in[n].
\]
The common-order lemma for equal observed law families gives that \(\tau'\) is a topological ordering of the common observable ratio graph. Since \cref{eq:exact-ratio-decoder-tc} holds for \(W\), the same \(\tau'\) also respects the transported graph of \(W\); by construction through \(\upsilon'\), it respects the transported graph of \(W'\). % lean: competitorTopologicalOrder_is_common_of_law_eq

For any coordinate label \(a\), define the signed intervention distribution functions
\[
S_a(u)=
\begin{cases}
Q_a(u), & s_a=1,\\
1-Q_a(u), & s_a=-1,
\end{cases}
\qquad
S'_a(u)=
\begin{cases}
Q'_a(u), & s_a=1,\\
1-Q'_a(u), & s_a=-1.
\end{cases}
\]
Here \(Q'_a\) is the intervention distribution function for coordinate \(a\) of \(\theta'\), and both signed functions are indexed by the same ambient sign vector \(s\). Using the continuous-rank construction at this common order gives, for \(v,z\in[0,1]^n\),
\begin{equation}
U_e(f(v))=S_{\pi(e)}(v_{\pi(e)}),
\qquad
U_e(\widetilde f(z))=S'_{\widetilde\pi(e)}(z_{\widetilde\pi(e)}).
\label{eq:exact-ratio-decoder-common-ranks}
\end{equation} Applying \cref{eq:exact-ratio-decoder-pruning-iff} to \(W\) and to \(W'\) at the same order shows that both representations have the same unique minimal admissible parent sets. Therefore, with
\[
\sigma=\widetilde\pi\circ\pi^{-1},
\qquad
\sigma(\pi(e))=\widetilde\pi(e),
\]
where \(\pi\) and \(\widetilde\pi\) are the target permutations of \(W\) and \(W'\), respectively, the map \(\sigma\) applies the inverse target permutation of \(W\) first and then the target permutation of \(W'\), and carries \(G\)-labels to \(G'\)-labels, one has, for every \(j,i\),
\begin{equation}
\sigma(j)\to\sigma(i)\text{ in }G'
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
j\to i\text{ in }G.
\label{eq:exact-ratio-decoder-aligned-edges}
\end{equation}
Equality of the observational laws gives equality of observed supports,
\begin{equation}
f([0,1]^n)=\widetilde f([0,1]^n).
\label{eq:exact-ratio-decoder-common-support}
\end{equation}
It remains to assemble the common ranks, aligned edges, and common support into componentwise \(C^2\) equivalence. We use the following implication, proved in the formal development: if two worlds have the common rank identities in \cref{eq:exact-ratio-decoder-common-ranks}, equal observed supports, and the edge alignment in \cref{eq:exact-ratio-decoder-aligned-edges}, then there are interval maps \(h_i,\bar h_i:[0,1]\to[0,1]\), one pair for each latent coordinate \(i\), such that
\[
h_i\in C^2([0,1]),
\qquad
\bar h_i\in C^2([0,1]),
\qquad
\bar h_i\circ h_i=\operatorname{id}_{[0,1]},
\qquad
h_i\circ\bar h_i=\operatorname{id}_{[0,1]},
\]
and, for every observed point \(x\in f([0,1]^n)=\widetilde f([0,1]^n)\),
\begin{equation}
\widetilde f^{-1}(x)_{\sigma(i)}=h_i\bigl(f^{-1}(x)_i\bigr).
\label{eq:exact-ratio-decoder-componentwise-assembly-target}
\end{equation}
The smoothness of these charts follows by composing the one-coordinate face embedding \(z\mapsto 0_n^{i\leftarrow z}\), the \(C^2\) mixing map, the inverse mixing map on the common observed support, and the coordinate projection; the inverse charts are obtained by interchanging the two worlds. Together with \cref{eq:exact-ratio-decoder-aligned-edges}, this is exactly the componentwise \(C^2\) equivalence of \(W\) and \(W'\) up to the simultaneous relabeling \(\sigma=\widetilde\pi\circ\pi^{-1}\). % lean: componentwiseC2Equivalent_of_common_rank_and_aligned_edges

When \(n=2\), every edge is an ancestral cover. Therefore \cref{eq:exact-ratio-decoder-cover-positive} gives, for each edge \(j\to i\) of \(G\),
\begin{equation}
0<D_{\pi^{-1}(j),\pi^{-1}(i)}.
\label{eq:exact-ratio-decoder-bivariate}
\end{equation}
% lean: bivariate_edge_discrepancy_pos_of_coverSeparated

The established graph equality \cref{eq:exact-ratio-decoder-selected-graph}, the order and predecessor containment \cref{eq:exact-ratio-decoder-tc,eq:exact-ratio-decoder-predecessors}, the rank identity \cref{eq:exact-ratio-decoder-rank-coordinate}, the uniqueness statement above, and the bivariate positivity \cref{eq:exact-ratio-decoder-bivariate} supply the packaged comparison clauses: within the positive compact-cube \(C^3\) mechanism class with shared \(C^2\) mixing, the decoder identifies the latent representation and structure from one perfect intervention per node, supplies the continuous law-separation witness used by \citet[Theorem~3.2]{vonKugelgenEtAl2023UnknownInterventions} when \(n=2\), and recovers the graph, topological order, and intervention alignment relative to \citet[Theorem~4.2]{WendongEtAl2023CauCA} and \citet[Corollary~{D}.1]{YaoEtAl2025InvariancePrinciple}. Collecting \cref{eq:exact-ratio-decoder-tc,eq:exact-ratio-decoder-selected-cdf,eq:exact-ratio-decoder-rank-coordinate,eq:exact-ratio-decoder-predecessors,eq:exact-ratio-decoder-pruning-iff,eq:exact-ratio-decoder-selected-graph}, the coherence of \(\mathcal P\) with \(W\), the componentwise equivalence statement, \cref{eq:exact-ratio-decoder-bivariate}, and these comparison clauses gives every assertion of \cref{obj:thm:exact-ratio-decoder}. % lean: exactRatioDecoderComparisonClauses_of_mainConclusions
\end{proof}
