Causal representation learning joins graphical causal inference, latent-variable identification, and distributional testing. The graphical side traces to structural causal models and diagrammatic identification \citep{Pearl1995,Pearl2009,SpirtesGlymourScheines2000,Peters2017,Hunermund2025}, with faithfulness and genericity supplied by the Bayesian-network tradition \citep{Meek1995,BoekenForreMooij2024TypicalFaithfulness}. The representation side descends from independent component analysis \citep{Hyvarinen2001} and identifiable nonlinear ICA, where auxiliary variables, time contrast, and conditional priors recover latent coordinates up to controlled componentwise ambiguities \citep{Hyvarinen2016,Hyvarinen2019,Khemakhem2020,Hyvarinen2023}. The broad causal-representation program is framed by \citet{Scholkopf2021}: high-dimensional observations are treated as mixtures of lower-dimensional causal variables, and identification requires inductive structure supplied by time, interventions, environments, or mechanism invariance.

Interventional causal discovery supplies the methodological language for using multiple environments. Known-target perfect interventions refine Markov equivalence classes \citep{Hauser2012}, general interventions admit their own interventional equivalence classes \citep{Yang2018}, and unknown soft targets can be handled by invariance-based equivalence-class characterizations \citep{JaberEtAl2020SoftUnknownTargets,Dai2025,Zhang2026}. In latent settings, mixture-oracle and independent-noise approaches give constructive graph recovery under measurement or linear-latent structure \citep{Kivva2021,Xie2024,JiangAragam2023UnknownInterventions}. Kernel mean embeddings and MMD provide the paper's observable law-comparison primitive: characteristic kernels make equality of embeddings equivalent to equality of laws \citep{Smola2007,Sriperumbudur2010,Sriperumbudur2011}, while two-sample MMD concentration and adaptive aggregation give the testing backbone \citep{Gretton2007,GrettonEtAl2012KernelTwoSample,SchrabEtAl2023MMDAgg}.

The closest causal-representation comparators are the recent intervention-based identifiability results. \citet{vonKugelgenEtAl2023UnknownInterventions} prove that for two latent variables, an observational law plus one unknown-target perfect stochastic intervention per node identifies the graph and representation under a continuous ratio-law witness condition; in arbitrary dimension, their theorem uses a paired design with at least two distinct perfect interventions per node, and their Section 7 states the one-intervention arbitrary-dimensional extension as a conjectural direction. \citet{WendongEtAl2023CauCA} prove CauCA identification with one target-aligned perfect stochastic intervention per node when the DAG and intervention targets are supplied; \citet{YaoEtAl2025InvariancePrinciple} likewise work with target order information in their single-node intervention corollary. \citet{BuchholzEtAl2023LinearCausalRepresentations} identify linear Gaussian latent SCMs under arbitrary nonlinear mixing and unknown single-node targets by exploiting quadratic precision-geometry signatures. \citet{AhujaEtAl2023InterventionalCRL} identify intervened coordinates from one point-mass perfect intervention per latent through polynomial support geometry, up to affine ambiguities. \citet{VariciEtAl2025ScoreBasedCRL} use score variation: one stochastic hard intervention per node identifies linear transformations, while two stochastic hard interventions per node identify general transformations; \citet{Varici2024} is the AISTATS precursor for two uncoupled hard interventions, and \citet{Varici2024b,Bing2024} extend linear-mixing analyses to unknown multi-node interventions. \citet{NgEtAl2025GeneralEnvironments} recover latent DAGs and variables under nonparametric mixing from derivative-rich general environments with additive-Gaussian or heteroscedastic latent restrictions.

This paper sits at the intersection of those strands by using observed likelihood-ratio laws rather than scores, supports, precision quadrics, or supplied target metadata. In its positive smooth compact-cube, causal-minimal scalar perfect-intervention regime with shared \(C^2\) diffeomorphic mixing, it establishes an open-dense Gaussian-MMD cover-separation condition for every finite DAG and fixed derivative-sign stratum. The resulting ratio graph recovers the ancestral order; conditional ranks align targets and recover componentwise coordinates; causal minimality and strict positivity make parent pruning exact. On the overlapping compact faithful regime, this yields the arbitrary-dimensional one-perfect-intervention-per-node comparison to \citet{vonKugelgenEtAl2023UnknownInterventions}; relative to \citet{WendongEtAl2023CauCA,YaoEtAl2025InvariancePrinciple}, the graph, order, and target alignment are recovered from the same ratio law.

The statistical layer connects the population decoder to standard nonparametric smoothing and generated-regressor theory. Local polynomial and conditional-density/CDF methods provide the oracle conditional-rank template \citep{FanGijbels1996,Fan1996,Xie2023UniformConditionalCDF}, and generated-covariate expansions quantify the effect of first-stage estimated arguments \citep{Pagan1984,MammenRotheSchienle2012GeneratedCovariates,Lee2018PartialMeanGeneratedRegressors}. \citet{AcarturkEtAl2024SampleComplexityCRL} give finite-sample guarantees for linear score-based CRL: \(O((\log(1/\delta))^4)\) samples suffice for graph recovery up to ancestors and \(O(((1/\epsilon)\log(1/\delta))^4)\) samples suffice for \(\epsilon\)-close latent-variable recovery in their linear, soft-intervention setting. \citet{LeeJinAragam2026FewEnvironments} prove finite-sample recovery with logarithmically many unknown multi-node interventions under high-dimensional linear factor mixing. The present finite-sample result supplies familywise confidence edges for the ratio-MMD graph conditional on a simultaneous first-stage ratio-error event, while the generated-ratio conditional-CDF rank expansion is posed as the statistical frontier for coordinate error bars. Recent broadening work on general environments, multimodal or spatiotemporal observations, benchmarks, and robotics applications \citep{Jin2024,Zhang2024,Ahuja2024,Sun2024,Wang2024,Chen2025,ROPES2025} underscores the same central theme: identifiable causal representations require explicit, auditable assumptions tying environmental variation to latent causal structure.
