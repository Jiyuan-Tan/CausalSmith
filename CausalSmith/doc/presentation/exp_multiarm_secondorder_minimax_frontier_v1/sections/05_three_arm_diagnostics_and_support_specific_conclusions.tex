\section{Three-arm diagnostics and support-specific conclusions}\label{sec:threearm}

The finite certificates of \cref{obj:thm:k3-grid-certificate-sandwich} give a computable way to audit three-arm risks. We first use them to separate the full labeled experiment from a natural scalar signed-score reduction. For the diagnostic contrast \(c^\dagger=(1,-1/2,-1/2)\), the scalar statistic records the sign-adjusted observed outcome, which retains the one-dimensional target direction and nothing else. The full experiment keeps a richer summary of the assignment and the outcomes: the rule exhibited below reads the number of units sent to the positively weighted arm, the signed outcome total within that arm, and the signed outcome total within the negatively weighted group. That summary already suffices, under the same independent allocation rule, to attain a strictly smaller worst-case risk than any rule based on the scalar score. The comparison is therefore between the scalar score and this richer assignment--outcome summary, which the exhibited rule reads while aggregating the two negatively weighted arms.

\begin{propositionv}{prop:k3-scalar-score-not-minimax-preserving}[Scalar score separation]
For \(K=3\), let \(c^\dagger=(1,-1/2,-1/2)\), and let \(\mathcal D^\star\) be the independent design assigning each unit with probabilities \(q^\star=(1/2,1/4,1/4)\).  For any \(n\geq 1\), any schedule \(z\in\mathcal T_3^n\), and any unit \(i\), define
\[
Z_i=\operatorname{sign}(c^\dagger_{A_i})(2Y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}-1),
\qquad
\mu_i=t_{i,1}-\frac{t_{i,2}+t_{i,3}}{2}.
\]
Then the following statements hold.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Score moments.)}  For every \(n\geq 1\), every \(z\in\mathcal T_3^n\), and every unit \(i\),
\[
\mathbb E_{\mathcal D^\star}(Z_i\mid z)=\mu_i,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_{\mathcal D^\star}(Z_i\mid z)=1-\mu_i^2 .
\]
\item \textbf{(Average-score risk.)}  For every \(n\geq 1\) and every \(z\in\mathcal T_3^n\),
\[
\mathbb E_{\mathcal D^\star}\!\left[
\left\{\frac{1}{n}\sum_i Z_i-\tau_{c^\dagger}(z)\right\}^2
\,\middle|\, z\right]
=
\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{n^2}\sum_i \mu_i^2 .
\]
\item \textbf{(Scalar minimax value.)}  For the scalar experiment at \(n=3\) that retains \((Z_1,Z_2,Z_3)\) and lets each \(\mu_i\) range over the five feasible values, its minimax value is
\[
V_3^Z=1-\frac{\sqrt 3}{2}.
\]
\item \textbf{(Full-data certificate.)}  There exists a clipped full-data estimator using the observed arm labels and outcomes under \(\mathcal D^\star\) whose worst-case risk over \(z\in\mathcal T_3^3\) is
\[
\frac{511653}{4000000}.
\]
Moreover,
\[
\frac{511653}{4000000}
<
\frac{18213}{136000}
<
1-\frac{\sqrt 3}{2}.
\]
\item \textbf{(Scalar moment envelope.)}  For every \(n\), every real \(M\), and every feasible three-arm scalar total \(M=|\sum_i\mu_i|\), the minimum attainable value of \(\sum_i\mu_i^2\) among scalar mean vectors with \(|\sum_i\mu_i|=M\) is
\[
\begin{cases}
M/2, & M\leq n/2,\\
(3M-n)/2, & M>n/2.
\end{cases}
\]
\end{itemize}
Thus the scalar signed-score experiment has exact value \(V_3^Z\) at \(n=3\), while the full observed arm-label-and-outcome experiment under the same independent \(q^\star\) design attains the smaller certified worst-case risk displayed above.
\end{propositionv}

The diagnostic has two consequences for the rest of the section. First, scalar moment calculations remain useful for locating difficult schedules, because the displayed envelope identifies how the average-score risk depends on the feasible means. Second, the minimax audit is conducted in the full labeled experiment: \cref{obj:prop:k3-scalar-score-not-minimax-preserving} gives an explicit \(n=3\) comparison in which arm labels strictly lower the worst-case risk.

We now package the certificate objects used to compare rational finite programs and real contrasts. The cluster terminology names the primal weights, barycenter estimator, and dual response-count prior that move together in the finite orbit calculation.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_18}[Exact rational grid-certificate cluster]
Fix a rational nonzero zero-sum contrast \(c\in\mathbb Q^K\) and integers \(n,M\ge1\). An \emph{exact rational grid-certificate cluster} at \((n,M,c)\) consists of an exact rational primal-dual grid certificate for the grid-action program of \cref{obj:def:rational-contrast-grid-lp}, in the sense of \cref{obj:synth_12} --- rational primal weights \((\pi^{n,M,c},w^{n,M,c})\) and the rational epigraph value \(\Lambda_{n,M}(c)\) --- together with the rational nonnegative response-count multiplier
\[
\nu_{n,M,c}\in\Delta(\mathcal M_{n,K})
\]
read off its dual solution. Its derived objects are the barycenter estimator \(\delta_{n,M,c}\) and the upper risk certificate \(U_{n,M}(c)\), both built from \(w^{n,M,c}\), and the posterior-mean lower Bayes-risk certificate \(B_{n,c}(\nu_{n,M,c})\), built from \(\nu_{n,M,c}\).
\end{definitionv}

For real contrasts, the transfer argument uses the same half-\(\ell_1\) metric as \cref{obj:thm:contrast-risk-continuity}. We record the notation before stating the support-three-and-larger result.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_11}[Contrast distance]
For two real contrasts $c_1,c_2$ on $\mathcal A_K$, define
\[
\operatorname{dist}(c_1,c_2)=\eta(c_1,c_2)=\frac{L_{c_1-c_2}}2=\frac12\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_K}|c_1(a)-c_2(a)|.
\]
Thus $\operatorname{dist}(c,c^{(n)})=\eta(c,c^{(n)})$.
\end{definitionv}

The next result collects the resolved second-order scale and the finite-certificate approximations for contrasts with at least three active arms. It uses the improvement \(d_n(c)=C_0(c)/n-\rho_n(c)\) and the normalized quantity \(x_n(c)=n^{4/3}d_n(c)\), matching the rate established in \cref{obj:thm:universal-second-order-rate} and the lower comparison in \cref{obj:thm:coarse-two-arm-minimax-lower}.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:second-order-rate-and-certificate-frontier}[Second-order certificate frontier]
Fix \(K\in\mathbb N\) and a contrast \(c\) on \(\mathcal A_K\) as in \cref{obj:def:labeled-schedule-game}.  Suppose that
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Arm count.)} \(2\le K\).
\item \textbf{(Active support.)} With \(S_c=\{a\in\mathcal A_K:c_a\ne0\}\), one has \(|S_c|\ge3\).
\end{itemize}
Define
\[
x_n(c)=n^{4/3}d_n(c),
\qquad
d_n(c)=\frac{C_0(c)}{n}-\rho_n(c).
\]
Then
\[
0<\liminf_{n\to\infty}x_n(c)
\le
\limsup_{n\to\infty}x_n(c)
\le
43C_0(c).
\]
Moreover, \(x_n(c)\) has at least one subsequential limit, and its subsequential-limit set
\[
\{a\in\mathbb R:\text{there is a strictly increasing }\phi:\mathbb N\to\mathbb N
\text{ with }x_{\phi(n)}(c)\to a\}
\]
is compact.

For every positive sequence \(a_n\), every \(\beta\in\mathbb R\), and every \(C>0\), if
\[
\frac{a_{\lfloor tn\rfloor}}{a_n}\to t^\beta
\quad\text{for every }t>0
\]
and
\[
a_n d_n(c)\to C,
\]
then \(\beta=4/3\).  In addition,
\[
\begin{gathered}
\exists\, a_n>0,\ \exists\, C>0
\text{ such that }
\frac{a_{\lfloor tn\rfloor}}{a_n}\to t^{4/3}
\text{ for every }t>0
\text{ and }a_n d_n(c)\to C
\\
\Longleftrightarrow
\\
x_n(c)>0\text{ eventually and }
\frac{x_{\lfloor tn\rfloor}(c)}{x_n(c)}\to1
\text{ for every }t>0 .
\end{gathered}
\]

For every rational contrast \(q\) on \(\mathcal A_K\) whose real-valued contrast is \(c\), there exist sequences \(G_n\), \(P_n\), and \(L_n\) such that \(G_n\) is an exact rational grid-certificate cluster for \(q\), and
\[
G_n-x_n(c)\to0,\qquad
P_n-G_n\to0,\qquad
L_n-G_n\to0.
\]

There also exist rational contrasts \(c^{(n)}\), certificate values \(B_n,U_n\), and endpoints \(R_n^-,R_n^+\) such that, for every arm \(a\),
\[
c_a^{(n)}\to c_a,
\]
and
\[
n^{5/6}\operatorname{dist}(c,c^{(n)})\to0.
\]
For every \(n>0\), there are an exact rational primal-dual certificate, a grid barycenter estimator, and a rational prior for \(c^{(n)}\) such that
\[
B_n=\text{the lower certificate from the prior},
\qquad
U_n=\text{the upper certificate from the grid barycenter},
\]
\[
R_n^-=
\left(\max\left\{0,\sqrt{B_n}-\operatorname{dist}(c,c^{(n)})\right\}\right)^2,
\qquad
R_n^+=
\left(\sqrt{U_n}+\operatorname{dist}(c,c^{(n)})\right)^2,
\]
and
\[
R_n^-\le \rho_n(c)\le R_n^+.
\]
The transferred normalized endpoint improvements are asymptotically coincident with \(x_n(c)\):
\[
n^{4/3}\left(\frac{C_0(c)}{n}-R_n^-\right)-x_n(c)\to0,
\qquad
n^{4/3}\left(\frac{C_0(c)}{n}-R_n^+\right)-x_n(c)\to0.
\]

Finally, for the three-arm contrast \(c^\dagger=(1,-1/2,-1/2)\), there exists an estimator rule \(\widehat\tau\) in the \(K=3,n=3\) labeled game such that
\[
\sup_{z\in\mathcal T_3^3}
R_3(\mathcal D^\star,\widehat\tau;z)
<
V_3^Z,
\]
where \(\mathcal D^\star\) is the product design using the \(q^\star\) allocation rule for \(c^\dagger\) and \(V_3^Z\) is the three-observation scalar sign-score minimax value.
\end{theoremv}

The theorem gives the \(n^{-4/3}\) scale an operational interpretation. The bounded positive liminf and finite limsup locate the improvement between two constants on that scale, while the regular-variation clause says that any convergent power normalization must use exponent \(4/3\). The finite-program clauses connect the asymptotic quantity to exactly solvable rational programs in principle: rational clusters approximate the normalized improvement, and rational approximants transfer the same bracketing statement to real contrasts through the distance defined in the setup. The three-arm scalar diagnostic is stated separately as a finite \(n=3\) illustration and is then used as the running example for the bracket construction.

It remains to isolate the support-two case. When the contrast has exactly one positive and one negative active arm, the multi-arm problem embeds the two-arm labeled experiment by assigning mass only to those active arms and rescaling the target by the half-range \(h_c\).

\begin{definitionv}{synth_17}[Sign-pair embedding and lift]
Assume \(|S_c|=2\), and write \(S_c=\{a_+,a_-\}\) with \(c_{a_+}=h_c=L_c/2\) and \(c_{a_-}=-h_c\). The sign-schedule embedding \(\iota_c:\mathcal T_2^n\to\mathcal T_K^n\) sends a two-arm schedule \(s=((s_{i,+},s_{i,-}))_{i=1}^n\) to the \(K\)-arm schedule \(z=\iota_c(s)\) with \(z_{i,a_+}=s_{i,+}\), \(z_{i,a_-}=s_{i,-}\), and fixed zero coordinates on arms outside \(\{a_+,a_-\}\). The sign-pair lift of a two-arm labeled procedure \(p_2=(\mathcal D_2,\widehat\tau_2)\) is the \(K\)-arm procedure that maps the two labels \((+, -)\) to \((a_+,a_-)\), assigns no mass to other arms, and reports \(h_c\widehat\tau_2\) after translating the observed active-arm data back to two-arm notation. Under this embedding, \(\tau_c(\iota_c(s))=h_c\tau_{(1,-1)}(s)\), and pushed-forward priors on \(\mathcal T_2^n\) become priors on \(\mathcal T_K^n\).
\end{definitionv}

The concluding theorem combines the support-two exact value, the order-attaining shrinkage rule, and the certificate statements used for finite audits. In applications with several randomized arms, such as multi-arm binary endpoint analyses in the style of ACTG 175 \citep{hammer_et_al_1996_actg175}, these clauses identify which support geometry determines the minimax comparison.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:attainment-and-k3-certified-converse}[Two-arm value transfer and finite brackets]
Fix \(K\ge 2\) and a nonzero zero-sum contrast \(c\) on \(\mathcal A_K\). Here \(\rho_n(c)\) is the labeled-schedule minimax risk of \cref{obj:def:labeled-schedule-game} and \(G_n(c)\) the finite orbit-game value of \cref{obj:def:finite-orbit-game}.
The following statements hold.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Two-active-arm value and saddle transfer.)} For every \(n\ge 1\), if \(|S_c|=2\), then
\[
\rho_n(c)=C_0(c)\rho_{n,2}.
\]
Moreover, there exist a two-arm orbit procedure \(q_2\), a prior \(\nu_2\) on \(\mathcal M_{n,2}\), a two-arm labeled procedure \(p_2\), a prior \(\mathrm{prior}_2\) on \(\mathcal T_2^n\), a \(K\)-arm labeled procedure \(p_K\), and a prior \(\mathrm{prior}_K\) on \(\mathcal T_K^n\) such that
\[
R^{\mathrm{orb}}_{(1,-1)}(q_2;m)\le G_n((1,-1))
\quad\text{for every }m\in\mathcal M_{n,2},
\]
and, for every two-arm orbit procedure \(q'_2\),
\[
G_n((1,-1))
\le
\mathbb E_{\nu_2}\!\left[R^{\mathrm{orb}}_{(1,-1)}(q'_2;m)\right].
\]
The labeled procedure \(p_2\) is the invariant labeled procedure induced by \(q_2\), and the labeled prior satisfies
\[
\mathrm{prior}_2(z)=
\frac{\nu_2(\operatorname{counts}(z))}
     {|\{z'\in\mathcal T_2^n:\operatorname{counts}(z')=\operatorname{counts}(z)\}|}.
\]
The \(K\)-arm procedure \(p_K\) is the sign-pair lift of \(p_2\) along \(c\), and \(\mathrm{prior}_K\) is the pushforward of \(\mathrm{prior}_2\) under the sign-schedule embedding. These objects satisfy
\[
R_n(p_2;z)\le \rho_{n,2}
\quad\text{for every }z\in\mathcal T_2^n,
\]
\[
\rho_{n,2}\le
\mathbb E_{\mathrm{prior}_2}\!\left[R_n(p'_2;z)\right]
\quad\text{for every two-arm labeled procedure }p'_2,
\]
\[
R_n(p_K;z)\le \rho_n(c)
\quad\text{for every }z\in\mathcal T_K^n,
\]
and
\[
\rho_n(c)\le
\mathbb E_{\mathrm{prior}_K}\!\left[R_n(p'_K;z)\right]
\quad\text{for every \(K\)-arm labeled procedure }p'_K.
\]

\item \textbf{(Second-order scale.)} The normalized improvement \(d_n(c)=C_0(c)/n-\rho_n(c)\) satisfies
\[
C_0(c)\kappa_c
\le
\liminf_{n\to\infty} n^{4/3}d_n(c)
\le
\limsup_{n\to\infty} n^{4/3}d_n(c)
\le
43C_0(c).
\]

\item \textbf{(Shrinkage attainment.)} There is an integer \(N\) such that, for every \(n\ge N\), the explicit clipped-shrinkage procedure satisfies
\[
\sup_{z\in\mathcal T_K^n}
R_n(\mathcal D^\star,\widehat\tau_n^{\mathrm{sh}};z)
\le
C_0(c)\left\{n^{-1}-\kappa_c n^{-4/3}\right\}.
\]

\item \textbf{(Real-contrast transfer certificates.)} For every rational nonzero zero-sum contrast \(q\), every \(n\ge 1\), and every \(M\ge 1\), there exist real numbers \(B,U,R^-,R^+\) satisfying the real-contrast transfer-certificate conditions of \cref{obj:thm:real-contrast-grid-certificate-transfer} for \((K,n,M,c,q)\). There also exist rational contrasts \(c^{(n)}\) and real sequences \(B_n,U_n,R_n^-,R_n^+\) such that
\[
c^{(n)}_a\to c_a
\quad\text{for every }a\in\mathcal A_K,
\]
\[
n^{5/6}\,\eta(c,c^{(n)})\to 0,
\]
the tuple \((B_n,U_n,R_n^-,R_n^+)\) satisfies the real-contrast transfer-certificate conditions of \cref{obj:thm:real-contrast-grid-certificate-transfer} for \((K,n,n,c,c^{(n)})\) whenever \(n\ge 1\), and
\[
n^{4/3}(R_n^+-R_n^-)\to 0.
\]
If a rational contrast \(q\) represents \(c\) exactly, then for every \(n\ge 1\) there exist real numbers \(B,U,R^-,R^+\) satisfying the same transfer-certificate conditions for \((K,n,n,c,q)\) and
\[
U-B\le \frac{C_0(c)}{4n^2}.
\]

\item \textbf{(Three-arm rational certificates.)} For every \(n\ge 1\) and \(M\ge 1\), there exist a three-arm grid allocation certificate \(\pi\), a grid weight \(w\), a rational epigraph value \(u\), a rational response-count prior \(\nu\), and a barycenter estimator \(\delta\) satisfying the exact primal-dual certificate and barycenter conditions of \cref{obj:thm:k3-grid-certificate-sandwich} for \(c^\dagger\). These objects satisfy
\[
B_n(\nu)\le \rho_n^\dagger\le U_{n,M}
\le B_n(\nu)+\frac{1}{4M^2}.
\]
\end{itemize}
\end{theoremv}

The support-two clause is an exact reduction: the sign-pair embedding in \cref{obj:synth_17} transports both the upper procedure and the least favorable prior, so the \(K\)-arm value is the two-arm value scaled by \(C_0(c)\). For larger supports, the shrinkage and certificate clauses give a common conclusion on the same second-order scale. The explicit procedure attains a \(C_0(c)\{n^{-1}-\kappa_c n^{-4/3}\}\) bound eventually, while the rational and real-certificate brackets provide finite lower and upper checks around the unrestricted minimax risk. The specialized three-arm certificate closes the loop with \cref{obj:prop:k3-scalar-score-not-minimax-preserving}: the same orbit-program machinery that audits \(\rho_n^\dagger\) also records where the scalar signed-score reduction separates from the full observed-data experiment.
