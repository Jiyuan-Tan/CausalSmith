\section{Finite-program certificates for rational and real contrasts}\label{sec:programs}

The preceding section identifies \(n^{4/3}\) as the scale on which the second-order improvement can have a nondegenerate limit. This section represents that scale by finite linear programs. The programs are finite and all their data are exactly rational, so a bracket at a given \((n,K)\) gives an exact rational certificate for that finite orbit game. The number of response-count rows is \(\binom{n+2^K-1}{2^K-1}\), which grows quickly in both arguments. A finite bracket at a chosen \((n,K)\) compares a proposed asymptotic design against the exact finite orbit game and identifies which response-count states drive the lower bound. The construction replaces the continuum of estimator values by a finite rational grid, solves a primal linear program over allocation-count orbits and grid actions, and reads a matching lower bound from the dual response-count multipliers. The first version is the three-arm diagnostic contrast \(c^\dagger\); the general version covers rational contrasts and then transfers to real contrasts by continuity.

We begin with the three-arm program because it is the smallest setting in which the multi-arm response-type geometry is visible. The grid \leanref{sym:\Gamma_M}{\ensuremath{\Gamma_M}} records candidate estimator values, the variable \leanref{sym:w_{r,x,g}}{\ensuremath{w_{r,x,g}}} couples an allocation count and observation with a grid action \leanref{sym:g}{\ensuremath{g}}, and the epigraph variable \leanref{sym:u}{\ensuremath{u}} bounds the risk uniformly over response-count vectors.

\begin{definitionv}{def:k3-rational-lp}[Three-arm LP value $\Lambda_{n,M}^\dagger$]
For \(K=3\), \(c^\dagger=(1,-1/2,-1/2)\), and
\[
\Gamma_M=\{-1+j/M:0\le j\le 2M\},
\]
the three-arm rational grid-action LP value \(\Lambda_{n,M}^\dagger\) is the minimum of \(u\) over rational variables \(\pi_r\ge 0\) and \(w_{r,x,g}\ge 0\) satisfying
\[
\sum_{r\in\mathcal R_{n,3}}\pi_r=1,
\]
\[
\sum_{g\in\Gamma_M}w_{r,x,g}=\pi_r
\quad
\text{for every } r\in\mathcal R_{n,3}
\text{ and } x\in\prod_{a\in\mathcal A_3}\{0,\ldots,r_a\},
\]
and
\[
\sum_{r,x,g}P_m(x\mid r)w_{r,x,g}\{g-\tau_{c^\dagger}(m)\}^2\le u
\quad
\text{for every } m\in\mathcal M_{n,3}.
\]
\end{definitionv}

The same construction applies to any rational nonzero zero-sum contrast. Its only additional bookkeeping is the contrast-dependent target range: the grid \leanref{sym:\Gamma_{M,c}}{\ensuremath{\Gamma_{M,c}}} spans \([-L_c/2,L_c/2]\), so each feasible barycenter remains in the admissible range for the orbit game of \cref{obj:def:finite-orbit-game}. The resulting value \leanref{sym:\Lambda_{n,M}(c)}{\ensuremath{\Lambda_{n,M}(c)}} is the rational finite program used throughout the rest of the section.

\begin{definitionv}{def:rational-contrast-grid-lp}[Rational grid-action value \(\Lambda_{n,M}(c)\)]
For \(K\geq 2\), \(n,M\geq 1\), and a rational nonzero zero-sum contrast \(c\in\mathbb Q^K\), define the rational contrast grid-action value \(\Lambda_{n,M}(c)\) as the optimal value of the following finite linear program. Let
\[
h_c=L_c/2,
\qquad
\Gamma_{M,c}=\{-h_c+jh_c/M:j=0,\ldots,2M\}.
\]
The program minimizes \(u\) over variables \(\pi_r\geq0\) and \(w_{r,x,g}\geq0\), subject to
\[
\sum_{r\in\mathcal R_{n,K}}\pi_r=1,
\]
\[
\sum_{g\in\Gamma_{M,c}}w_{r,x,g}=\pi_r
\quad\text{for every }(r,x),
\]
and
\[
\sum_{r\in\mathcal R_{n,K}}\sum_x\sum_{g\in\Gamma_{M,c}}
P_m(x\mid r)w_{r,x,g}\{g-\tau_c(m)\}^2\leq u
\quad\text{for every }m\in\mathcal M_{n,K}.
\]
\end{definitionv}

For later lower bounds, the dual variables are interpreted as a response-count prior. The next definition fixes the objective convention used to turn dual feasibility into a numerical certificate.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_8}[Dual objective for the grid linear program]
For the grid-action linear program with primal constraints written in standard form \(A\xi\le b\) and objective \(u\), let \(y\) denote the nonnegative vector of dual row multipliers satisfying the stationarity equations for the variables \((u,\pi,w)\). Define
\[
\operatorname{obj}_{\mathrm{dual}}(y)=-\sum_i y_i b_i.
\]
In the grid program, the restriction of \(y\) to the response-count risk rows is the response-count multiplier \(\nu\), and dual feasibility gives a lower certificate for the corresponding primal epigraph value.
\end{definitionv}

The three-arm diagnostic program has an exact rational certificate at every sample size and grid resolution. The statement also records the mesh size \(M=n^2\), which is fine enough for the grid error to vanish at the \(n^{4/3}\) scale.

\begin{propositionv}{prop:k3-lp-certificate}[Three-arm LP certificate]
For the three-arm diagnostic contrast \(c^\dagger=(1,-1/2,-1/2)\) and every pair of positive integers \(n\) and \(M\), let \(\rho_n^\dagger\) be the unrestricted three-arm minimax risk and let \(\Lambda_{n,M}^\dagger\) be the three-arm rational grid-action LP value from \cref{obj:def:k3-rational-lp}. Then
\[
\rho_n^\dagger
\le
\Lambda_{n,M}^\dagger
\le
\rho_n^\dagger+\frac{2}{M}+\frac{1}{M^2},
\qquad
0\le \Lambda_{n,M}^\dagger\le 1 .
\]
At the resolution \(M=n^2\), there exist a rational allocation mass \(\pi\) on allocation-count vectors, a joint design-action mass \(w\), a rational epigraph value \(u\), and rational response-count multipliers \(\nu\) on count vectors \(m:\mathcal T_3\to\{0,\ldots,n\}\) with \(\sum_{t\in\mathcal T_3}m_t=n\), such that there are a rational grid weight \(w^{\mathbb Q}\) and dual multipliers \(y\) satisfying
\[
w=w^{\mathbb Q}\ \text{after real embedding},\qquad
(\pi,w^{\mathbb Q},u)\ \text{is primal feasible},\qquad
y\ \text{is dual feasible},\qquad
\nu=y_{\mathrm{risk}},\qquad
u=\operatorname{obj}_{\mathrm{dual}}(y),
\]
and
\[
u=\Lambda_{n,n^2}^\dagger .
\]
Moreover, every binary response type \(t\in\mathcal T_3\) is represented by some response-count vector \(m\) with total population size \(n\) and \(m_t>0\), and the grid-discretization error at the certificate scale satisfies
\[
\lim_{k\to\infty} k^{4/3}\left(\frac{2}{k^2}+\frac{1}{k^4}\right)=0 .
\]
\end{propositionv}

\Cref{obj:prop:k3-lp-certificate} supplies a concrete finite certificate for the three-arm contrast. Its primal side gives a randomized invariant procedure whose risk is bounded by the grid value; its dual side gives response-count multipliers that certify a matching lower value up to the displayed mesh error. The representation clause records that every response type occurs in at least one feasible response-count vector, so the finite state space of the program covers the whole type space; which types carry positive dual mass is decided by the solution, not by this clause.

The lower endpoint in the general certificate is a Bayes risk computed at a fixed allocation count. The formula below is the posterior-variance form of that Bayes risk, expressed entirely with the orbit likelihood from \cref{obj:def:orbit-likelihood}.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_7}[Orbit Bayes risk at an allocation count]
For a response-count prior \(\nu\in\Delta(\mathcal M_{n,K})\), a contrast \(c\), and an allocation count \(r\in\mathcal R_{n,K}\), define
\[
\operatorname{BayesRisk}_{n,c}(r,\nu)
=\inf_{\delta_r}\sum_{m\in\mathcal M_{n,K}}\nu_m\sum_xP_m(x\mid r)\{\delta_r(x)-\tau_c(m)\}^2,
\]
where \(x\in\prod_{a\in\mathcal A_K}\{0,\ldots,r_a\}\) and \(\delta_r(x)\in[-L_c/2,L_c/2]\). Equivalently, with
\[
D_{r,x,c}(\nu)=\sum_m\nu_mP_m(x\mid r),
\qquad
N_{r,x,c}(\nu)=\sum_m\nu_mP_m(x\mid r)\tau_c(m),
\]
\[
\operatorname{BayesRisk}_{n,c}(r,\nu)
=\sum_m\nu_m\tau_c(m)^2-\sum_{x:D_{r,x,c}(\nu)>0}\frac{N_{r,x,c}(\nu)^2}{D_{r,x,c}(\nu)}.
\]
\end{definitionv}

The rational certificate theorem combines the primal grid solution, its barycenter estimator, and the dual Bayes-risk endpoint. The statement also gives the orbit counts, making the computational size of the certificate explicit.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:rational-contrast-grid-certificate-sandwich}[Rational grid certificate sandwich]
Fix natural numbers \(K,n,M\), a rational nonzero zero-sum contrast \(c\in\mathbb Q^K\), and suppose \(n\geq 1\) and \(M\geq 1\); here \(\rho_n(c)\) is the labeled-schedule minimax risk of \cref{obj:def:labeled-schedule-game}.  In the rational grid-action program of \cref{obj:def:rational-contrast-grid-lp}, there exist a rational allocation certificate \(\pi^{n,M,c}\), a joint design-action weight \(w^{n,M,c}\), a rational epigraph value \(u\), a rational response-count multiplier \(\nu_{n,M,c}:\mathcal M_{n,K}\to\mathbb Q\), and an invariant estimator \(\delta_{n,M,c}\) on allocation and observation counts such that:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Exact rational certificate.)}  There are rational grid weights \(w^{\mathbb Q}\) and rational dual multipliers \(y\) such that \(w^{n,M,c}\) is the real embedding of \(w^{\mathbb Q}\), the tuple \((\pi^{n,M,c},w^{\mathbb Q},u)\) is primal feasible for the grid program, \(y\) is dual feasible, \(\nu_{n,M,c}\) is the risk component of \(y\), and \(u\) is the dual objective value of \(y\).
\item \textbf{(Barycenter estimator.)}  For every allocation count \(r\in\mathcal R_{n,K}\) and compatible observation \(x\),
\[
\delta_{n,M,c}(r,x)
=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle
\sum_{g\in\Gamma_{M,c}} g\,w_{r,x,g}^{n,M,c}/\pi_r^{n,M,c},
& \pi_r^{n,M,c}>0,\\[0.7em]
0,& \pi_r^{n,M,c}=0.
\end{cases}
\]
\item \textbf{(Optimal value.)}  The real value of \(u\) is the grid LP value:
\[
u=\Lambda_{n,M}(c).
\]
\item \textbf{(Certificate sandwich.)}  With
\[
B_{n,c}(\nu_{n,M,c})
=
\inf\Bigl\{v\in\mathbb R:\exists r\in\mathcal R_{n,K}\ \text{with}\ 
v=\operatorname{BayesRisk}_{n,c}(r,\nu_{n,M,c})\Bigr\},
\]
and with \(U_{n,M}(c)\) denoting the unrestricted labeled-schedule risk of \(\delta_{n,M,c}\) under \(\pi^{n,M,c}\), the certificates satisfy
\[
B_{n,c}(\nu_{n,M,c})
\leq
\rho_n(c)
\leq
U_{n,M}(c)
\leq
\Lambda_{n,M}(c)
\leq
B_{n,c}(\nu_{n,M,c})+\frac{C_0(c)}{4M^2}.
\]
\item \textbf{(Orbit counts.)}  The finite sets in the certificate have cardinalities
\[
|\mathcal M_{n,K}|=\binom{n+2^K-1}{2^K-1},
\qquad
|\mathcal R_{n,K}|=\binom{n+K-1}{K-1},
\]
and
\[
\sum_{r\in\mathcal R_{n,K}}|\{x:x\text{ is compatible with }r\}|
=
\binom{n+2K-1}{2K-1}.
\]
\item \textbf{(Mesh-rate scaling.)}  For every positive sequence \(a_k\) and every integer sequence \(M_k\geq1\), if
\[
\frac{a_k}{M_k^2}\longrightarrow 0,
\]
then
\[
a_k\,\frac{C_0(c)}{4M_k^2}\longrightarrow 0.
\]
\item \textbf{(Asymptotic certificates.)}  For every positive normalizing sequence \(a_k\) and every grid-resolution sequence \(M_k\geq1\) satisfying \(a_k/M_k^2\to0\), the rational certificates at mesh \(M_k\) admit lower and upper endpoints \(B_{k}\) and \(U_{k}\) with
\[
B_k\leq \rho_k(c)\leq U_k,
\qquad
a_k\{U_k-B_k\}\longrightarrow 0.
\]
Consequently, whenever \(a_k d_k(c)\) has a finite limit \(\ell\), the scaled lower and upper certificate improvements both converge to that limit:
\[
a_k\left\{\frac{C_0(c)}{k}-U_k\right\}\longrightarrow \ell,
\qquad
a_k\left\{\frac{C_0(c)}{k}-B_k\right\}\longrightarrow \ell.
\]
At the second-order scale \(a_k=k^{4/3}\), the specialization \(M_k=k\) satisfies the mesh condition and yields
\[
k^{4/3}\{U_k-B_k\}\longrightarrow 0.
\]
\end{itemize}
\end{theoremv}

\Cref{obj:thm:rational-contrast-grid-certificate-sandwich} turns each rational contrast into two computable endpoints. The upper endpoint is obtained by replacing the randomized grid action with its conditional barycenter, which preserves the relevant risk inequality by convexity. The lower endpoint is the best allocation-count Bayes risk under the dual response-count multiplier. Strong duality for the finite rational program then leaves only the grid mesh gap, and that gap is \(o(k^{-4/3})\) when \(M_k=k\).

The transfer from rational to real contrasts uses a direct Lipschitz comparison for the square root of the minimax risk. The distance is the half-\(\ell_1\) contrast distance, so the comparison is expressed in the same scale as the target interval.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:contrast-risk-continuity}[Contrast risk continuity]
Let \(K\) and \(n\) be integers, and let \(c,c'\in\mathbb R^K\) be nonzero zero-sum treatment contrasts for the labeled-schedule game in \cref{obj:def:labeled-schedule-game}. Suppose that
\[
K\ge 2
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
n\ge 1 .
\]
Define the contrast distance
\[
\eta(c,c')
=
\frac12\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_K}|c_a-c'_a| .
\]
Then the unrestricted labeled-schedule minimax risk satisfies
\[
\left|\sqrt{\rho_n(c)}-\sqrt{\rho_n(c')}\right|
\le
\eta(c,c') .
\]
\end{theoremv}

The square-root form of \cref{obj:thm:contrast-risk-continuity} matches the geometry of squared-error risk: changing the contrast shifts every target by at most \(\eta(c,c')\), and the minimax risk changes accordingly in root-risk units. This gives a stable way to carry finite rational certificates to nearby real contrasts.

It remains to name the exact rational primal-dual certificate as a reusable object. The definition records the feasibility, dual stationarity, and zero-gap conditions that are invoked in the transfer theorem.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_12}[Exact rational primal-dual grid certificate]
Fix rational nonzero zero-sum $c\in\mathbb Q^K$ and integers $n,M\ge1$. An exact rational primal-dual grid certificate for the program defining $\Lambda_{n,M}(c)$ consists of rational values $(\pi_r)_{r\in\mathcal R_{n,K}}$, rational values $(w_{r,x,g})$, a rational epigraph value $u$, and rational dual multipliers for the linear constraints, such that $\pi_r\ge0$, $w_{r,x,g}\ge0$, $\sum_r\pi_r=1$, $\sum_{g\in\Gamma_{M,c}}w_{r,x,g}=\pi_r$ for every $(r,x)$, and
\[
\sum_{r\in\mathcal R_{n,K}}\sum_x\sum_{g\in\Gamma_{M,c}}P_m(x\mid r)w_{r,x,g}\{g-\tau_c(m)\}^2\le u
\quad\text{for every }m\in\mathcal M_{n,K}.
\]
The dual multipliers are rational, nonnegative on inequality rows, satisfy the stationarity equations for the same rational linear program, and have dual objective equal to the primal objective $u$. The common value is $\Lambda_{n,M}(c)$.
\end{definitionv}

The real-contrast result chooses rational approximants close enough that the continuity loss is dominated by the second-order scale. For an already rational contrast, the same statement specializes to a bracket with the \(n^{-2}\) mesh width.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:real-contrast-grid-certificate-transfer}[Real contrast certificate transfer]
Fix \(K\ge 2\) and a nonzero zero-sum real contrast \(c\) on \(\mathcal A_K\).  For real contrasts \(c_1,c_2\), write
\[
\eta(c_1,c_2)=\frac12\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_K}|c_1(a)-c_2(a)|,
\qquad
C_0(c_1)=\frac{L_{c_1}^2}{4}.
\]
A tuple \((B,U,R^-,R^+)\) is a transferred certificate for \((n,M,c,q)\), where \(q\in\mathbb Q^K\setminus\{0\}\) is zero-sum, if there exist an invariant allocation-count distribution \(\pi\), a rational grid-action weight \(w\), a rational epigraph value \(u\), a rational response-count prior \(\nu\), an invariant barycenter rule \(\delta\), a projected upper procedure, and a schedule prior such that the exact rational primal-dual grid certificate and barycenter identities hold for \(q,\pi,w,u,\nu,\delta\), and
\[
B=B_{n,q}(\nu),\qquad
U=U_{n,M}(q;\pi,\delta),\qquad
u=\Lambda_{n,M}(q),
\]
\[
B\le \rho_n(q)\le U\le u
\le B+\frac{C_0(q)}{4M^2},
\]
\[
R^-=\left\{\max\bigl(0,\sqrt B-\eta(c,q)\bigr)\right\}^2,
\qquad
R^+=\left(\sqrt U+\eta(c,q)\right)^2,
\]
\[
R^-\le \rho_n(c)\le R^+,
\]
and
\[
R^+-R^-\le
\frac{5C_0(q)}{4M^2}
+4\sqrt{C_0(q)}\,\eta(c,q)\,n^{-1/2}
+3\eta(c,q)^2.
\]
The projected upper procedure certifies worst-case risk at most \(R^+\), and the schedule prior certifies Bayes risk at least \(R^-\).

Then the following hold.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Finite-grid transfer.)} For every rational zero-sum contrast \(q\in\mathbb Q^K\setminus\{0\}\) and every pair of integers \(n,M\ge 1\), there exist \(B,U,R^-,R^+\in\mathbb R\) forming a transferred certificate for \((n,M,c,q)\).

\item \textbf{(Real-contrast approximation.)} There exist rational zero-sum contrasts \(c^{(n)}\in\mathbb Q^K\setminus\{0\}\) and real sequences \(B_n,U_n,R_n^-,R_n^+\) such that, for every arm \(a\in\mathcal A_K\),
\[
c^{(n)}(a)\longrightarrow c(a),
\]
\[
n^{5/6}\eta(c,c^{(n)})\longrightarrow 0,
\]
for every \(n\ge 1\), \((B_n,U_n,R_n^-,R_n^+)\) is a transferred certificate for \((n,n,c,c^{(n)})\), and
\[
n^{4/3}\bigl(R_n^+-R_n^-\bigr)\longrightarrow 0.
\]

\item \textbf{(Exact rational case.)} If a rational zero-sum contrast \(q\in\mathbb Q^K\setminus\{0\}\) embeds coefficientwise as \(c\), then for every \(n\ge 1\) there exist \(B,U,R^-,R^+\in\mathbb R\) forming a transferred certificate for \((n,n,c,q)\) and satisfying
\[
U-B\le \frac{C_0(c)}{4n^2}.
\]
\end{itemize}
\end{theoremv}

\Cref{obj:thm:real-contrast-grid-certificate-transfer} gives the certificate construction for every real contrast. The rational program supplies the inner bracket for the approximating contrast \(q\), while \cref{obj:thm:contrast-risk-continuity} expands the bracket just enough to cover the target contrast \(c\). The displayed bound separates the mesh contribution, the contrast-approximation contribution, and the quadratic approximation term, so choosing \(M=n\) and \(n^{5/6}\eta(c,c^{(n)})\to0\) produces a bracket whose width is negligible at the \(n^{4/3}\) scale.

We close the section by recording the specialized three-arm sandwich. It packages the diagnostic program of \cref{obj:def:k3-rational-lp} in the same lower-upper form as the general rational theorem and gives the certificate comparison used in the three-arm analysis that follows.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:k3-grid-certificate-sandwich}[Three-arm grid sandwich]
For integers \(n\ge 1\) and \(M\ge 1\), in the three-arm rational grid program of \cref{obj:def:k3-rational-lp}, there exist a rational allocation certificate \(\pi^{n,M}\), a joint design--action weight \(w^{n,M}\), a rational epigraph value \(u\), a rational response-count multiplier \(\nu_{n,M}\), and an estimator \(\delta_{n,M}\) such that:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Exact certificate.)} The tuple \((\pi^{n,M},w^{n,M},u,\nu_{n,M})\) is represented by a rational grid weight whose real embedding is \(w^{n,M}\), a primal feasible point of the rational grid LP, and a dual feasible point whose risk component is \(\nu_{n,M}\) and whose dual objective is \(u\).
\item \textbf{(Barycenter rule.)} For every allocation count \(r\in\mathcal R_{n,3}\) and compatible observation \(x\), the estimator is
\[
\delta_{n,M}(r,x)
=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle
\sum_{g\in\Gamma_M} g\,\frac{w_{r,x,g}^{n,M}}{\pi_r^{n,M}},
& \pi_r^{n,M}>0,\\[1ex]
0, & \pi_r^{n,M}=0.
\end{cases}
\]
\item \textbf{(Grid value.)} The real value of \(u\) is \(\Lambda_{n,M}^\dagger\).
\end{itemize}
Define
\[
B_n(\nu_{n,M})
=
\inf_{r\in\mathcal R_{n,3}}
\operatorname{BayesRisk}_{n,c^\dagger}(r,\nu_{n,M})
\]
and
\[
U_{n,M}
=
\sup_{m\in\mathcal M_{n,3}}
\sum_{r\in\mathcal R_{n,3}}\pi_r^{n,M}
\sum_x
P_m(x\mid r)
\{\delta_{n,M}(r,x)-\tau_{c^\dagger}(m)\}^2 .
\]
Then
\[
B_n(\nu_{n,M})
\le
\rho_n^\dagger
\le
U_{n,M}
\le
\Lambda_{n,M}^\dagger
\le
B_n(\nu_{n,M})+\frac{1}{4M^2}.
\]
Moreover, for every positive normalizing sequence \(a_k\) and every grid-resolution sequence \(M_k\) with \(M_k\ge 1\) for all \(k\), if
\[
\frac{a_k}{M_k^2}\longrightarrow 0,
\]
then
\[
a_k\,\frac{1}{4M_k^2}\longrightarrow 0.
\]
Finally, for every positive normalizing sequence \(a_k\), every grid-resolution sequence \(M_k\), every pair of real sequences \(L_k,U_k\), and every real number \(C\), if for every \(k\ge 1\),
\[
M_k\ge 1,\qquad
L_k\le \rho_k^\dagger\le U_k,\qquad
U_k-L_k\le \frac{1}{4M_k^2},
\]
and if
\[
\frac{a_k}{M_k^2}\longrightarrow 0,
\qquad
a_k d_k(c^\dagger)\longrightarrow C,
\]
then
\[
a_k\left\{\frac{C_0(c^\dagger)}{k}-L_k\right\}\longrightarrow C
\quad\text{and}\quad
a_k\left\{\frac{C_0(c^\dagger)}{k}-U_k\right\}\longrightarrow C .
\]
\end{theoremv}

\Cref{obj:thm:k3-grid-certificate-sandwich} specializes the rational programs to \(c^\dagger\), where the mesh gap is \(1/(4M^2)\). Thus any independently computed bracket with that width inherits the same second-order limit for the scaled improvement. The next section uses this certificate form to diagnose the three-arm geometry and to compare support-specific conclusions at the \(n^{4/3}\) scale.
