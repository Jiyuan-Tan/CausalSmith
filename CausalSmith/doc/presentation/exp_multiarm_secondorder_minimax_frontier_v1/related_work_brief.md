Randomization-based causal inference begins with Fisher's experimental design principles and Neyman's finite-population potential-outcome formulation, later developed into the modern potential-outcomes framework by Rubin and synthesized in standard treatments and surveys \citep{fisher_1935,neyman_1990,rubin_1974,rubin_1978,holland_1986,imbens_rubin_2015,imbens_wooldridge_2009,athey_imbens_2017,ding_2024,bai_2024}. Within this tradition, the target is a finite-population causal contrast and the design supplies the randomness. Work on regression adjustment, factorial and multi-treatment experiments, rerandomization, and complex assignments gives the main inferential context: \citet{freedman_2008} analyzed finite-population regression adjustment with several treatments, \citet{lin_2013} gave asymptotic efficiency guarantees for interacted adjustment, \citet{dasgupta_2015} formulated causal inference for \(2^K\) factorial designs through potential outcomes, and \citet{morgan_2012,li_2018} developed rerandomization theory for treatment-control experiments. \citet{mukerjee_dasgupta_rubin_2018} connect Neymanian causal inference with finite-population sampling for multiple-treatment contrasts, unequal replications, randomization restrictions, and variance estimation; the present paper instead studies an unrestricted design-estimator minimax value for a fixed binary response-type game.

A second canonical strand is finite-population sampling and decision-theoretic minimax analysis. The Horvitz--Thompson estimator and design-based sampling theory \citep{horvitz_1952,hansen_1953,sarndal_1992} provide the inverse-probability and finite-population language, while \citet{gabler_1990} and \citet{aronow_lopatto_2026_minimax_unbiased} provide close minimax sampling antecedents. Aronow and Lopatto study design-unbiased estimation of a bounded finite-population total: for inclusion probabilities \(\pi_i>0\) and ranges \(r_i=(u_i-\ell_i)/2\), their sharp worst-case squared-error lower bound is \(\sum_i r_i^2(1-\pi_i)/\pi_i\), attained by the midpoint-differenced Horvitz--Thompson estimator under pairwise-independent inclusion indicators on positive-range coordinates; with an expected-sample-size budget, independent sampling with \(\pi_i^\star=\min\{1,\gamma r_i\}\) solves the joint design problem. The present causal game uses mutually exclusive treatment assignment and permits biased estimators, while its first-order allocation \(q_a^\star\propto |c_a|\) echoes the range-weighted sampling logic.

The methodological lineage also includes optimum design, Bayesian information inequalities, and second-order minimax theory. Classical optimum-design work from \citet{smith_1918,kiefer_1959,kiefer_1974,atkinson_2007} frames design choice as an optimization problem, while Wald's decision theory and Le Cam's asymptotic experiment program supply minimax and comparison tools \citep{wald_1950,lecam_1986,ibragimov_1981}. The internal lower-bound argument is closest in spirit to Bayesian Cramér--Rao and van Trees methods \citep{rao_1945,van_trees_1968,gill_1995}. The second-order frontier is informed by constrained and asymptotic minimax examples \citep{pinsker_1980,levit_1981_second_order,bickel_1981,levit_1986_schrodinger,feldman_1991,johnstone_1992}: those works show how nonstandard boundary geometry and differential or spectral problems can determine refined risk expansions, while the present contribution identifies the \(n^{-4/3}\) order for the fixed-\(K\) binary causal game and leaves the sharp limiting operator as an explicit frontier question.

The nearest randomized-experiment competitors are recent minimax design papers. \citet{kallus_2018,kallus_2020_optimality} study minimax variance for two-arm experiments under structured conditional-mean classes, mixed-strategy optimal designs, and design-based inference constraints; under permutation symmetry, complete randomization is optimal for the variance criterion, and the estimator class is fixed around sample-average or Horvitz--Thompson-type forms. \citet{bai_2023_why} proves finite-sample minimax optimality under permutation invariance for randomization and linear estimation schemes. \citet{harshaw_2024} constructs Gram--Schmidt walk designs that trade robustness and covariate balance with finite-sample MSE guarantees; \citet{basse_2023} gives minimax designs for temporal experiments with habituation; \citet{derezinski_2019} studies minimax experimental design for least-squares regression under worst-case responses; and \citet{hu_2024} studies minimax-regret sample selection for randomized trials with heterogeneous welfare. Network-interference work \citep{eckles_2014,kandiros_2024,kandiros_2026} extends design-based minimax questions to exposure mappings and conflict graphs; in particular, \citet{kandiros_2026} characterizes minimax rates for network causal estimands through local and global connectivity properties of the conflict graph. These results supply adjacent minimax-design methodology, while the present paper gives the exact finite orbit game on \(2^K\) binary response types under no interference and unrestricted estimators.

The closest scalar frontier is \citet{sudijono_dobriban_tchetgen_2026_sharp}. For two binary treatment arms and unrestricted design-estimator pairs, they reduce the full finite-population game to a scalar experiment with parameters \((p_+,p_-,r_0)\), observation \(X_2=p_+ + \operatorname{Binomial}(r_0,1/2)\), and target \((p_+-p_-)/n\); they prove \(\rho_n=n^{-1}-C_A n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3})\), with \(C_A=-4^{1/3}a_1'=1.617\ldots\), and show attainment by Bernoulli randomization with a nonlinear shrinkage estimator tied to a least-favorable prior. \citet{hull_2026_bounded} studies the two-arm bounded-potential-outcome problem, showing optimality of independent random assignment and a midpoint-centered affine rule among affine estimators, then extending optimality to arbitrary estimators with a generally nonlinear rule. \citet{rosenman_hunter_2026_shrinkage} studies sequential multi-arm adaptive allocation with Gaussian heteroscedastic treatment-effect estimates and Stein-like shrinkage loss. The present paper positions \citet{sudijono_dobriban_tchetgen_2026_sharp} as sharp two-arm calibration, proves its own order converse through an internal complete-schedule Bayesian information argument, extends the unrestricted binary game to every fixed \(K\ge3\), supplies exact-rational finite-program and continuity-transfer certificates, and uses the ACTG 175 trial \citep{hammer_et_al_1996_actg175} as a four-regimen binary-endpoint consumer for prespecified regimen-versus-average contrasts.
