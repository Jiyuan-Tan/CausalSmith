Potential outcomes and randomized assignment provide the causal framework \citep{Rubin1974}; propensity scores and inverse-probability weighting provide the linked-data benchmark \citep{HorvitzThompson1952,RosenbaumRubin1983,Hahn1998,HiranoImbensRidder2003}. Partial-identification analysis asks which treatment effects remain compatible with observed data and maintained assumptions \citep{Manski1990,HorowitzManski1995,Tamer2010}. The present paper studies a randomized trial in which the population law \(H\) of assignment probabilities is known, while each treatment–outcome row retains only a deterministic score label. Its target is the average treatment effect (ATE) under bounded outcomes and uniform overlap.

Data-combination research characterizes what separate sources reveal about an unobserved joint distribution \citep{DOrazio2006,RidderMoffitt2007,Conti2017}. The rearrangement inequality \citep{HardyLittlewoodPolya1934,CambanisSimonsStout1976} is central here: \citet[Theorem 3.2 and eq. (3.1)]{FanShermanShum2014} derive sharp potential-outcome mean and ATE bounds for **fixed** combined-data marginals ([article](https://doi.org/10.3982/ECTA10601)). The present paper credits that interval and optimizes its attained width across every released outcome law compatible with \(H\) and the label rule. Its resulting certificate has a common Bernoulli outcome law attaining the maximum, including for atomic score laws and disconnected label cells. Related sharp-set methods include subdistribution extension \citep{Torgovitsky2019} and the conditional optimal transport characterization of general incomplete-data moment models by \citet[Section 3]{FanParkPassShi2025}; the latter obtains convex identified sets and transport-computable support functions for affine moment models ([revised paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.16098)). The score-based randomization and outer optimization over released trial laws specify the present paper’s additional object.

Statistical matching and record-linkage studies supply a second comparison. \citet{MarellaPfeffermann2019} analyze matching under informative sampling using a conditional-independence sample-likelihood model, extended to nonresponse in \citet{Marella2023}; \citet{Wortman2018} study treatment-effect estimation when probabilistic record links contain errors. \citet{Komarova2015,Komarova2018} connect the quality of individual links to identification and disclosure risk, with partial identification under a bound on disclosure risk ([published analysis](https://doi.org/10.3982/QE568)). The present experiment instead fixes the released object as a deterministic function of the assignment score and characterizes its causal information exactly. Its universal point-identification condition says that the linked label determines the score almost surely.

Subclassification \citep{Cochran1968,RosenbaumRubin1984} and scalar quantization \citep{GrayNeuhoff1998,GrafLuschgy2000,LemaireMontesPages2020} motivate finite label design. With person-level information retained, \citet{WangZhangRichardsonZhou2021,Wang2026} construct robust subclassification weights; \citet{KalavasisMehrotraZampetakis2024} obtain confidence-interval size of order \(\varepsilon+n^{-1/2}\) for data-dependent coarse inverse weighting under their score-error, outcome-smoothness, and extreme-score conditions ([full paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01658)). \citet{LeeWeidner2026} derive treatment-effect bounds by limited pooling across linked covariate observations, including challenging overlap regimes ([article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jeconom.2026.106254)). For deleted score linkage, the present certificate yields inverse-\(K\) optimal ambiguity under a positive density lower bound and a high-resolution companding constant under a continuous, strictly positive score density. Its honest-length results sit alongside interval-inference methods \citep{ImbensManski2004,Stoye2009,ChernozhukovHongTamer2007,KaidoMolinariStoye2019,FangSantos2019,FanPark2012}: the known-\(H\) length has order \(K^{-1}+n^{-1/2}\), and an independent score log adds an \(m^{-1/2}\) sampling direction. Recent combined experimental and observational data bounds for distributional effects \citep{Sakaguchi2025} further place this work within the active data-fusion literature.
