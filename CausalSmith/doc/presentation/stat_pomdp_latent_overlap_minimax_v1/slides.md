# Evaluating Policies With Hidden States

Weighting recent action histories lets us evaluate a policy’s long-run reward; how much history we need depends on how far the policy can shift hidden-state occupancy.

---

## Motivation

- We observe one trajectory of length \(T\) under a known behavior policy \(b\).
- Each date records observed state \(X_t\), binary action \(A_t\), and reward \(Y_t\).
- A known target policy \(e\) chooses actions differently.
- We want its long-run average reward, including how earlier actions change today’s hidden state.

Changing today’s treatment can change tomorrow’s condition. How can we evaluate that accumulated effect from a partially observed record?

---

## Research question

Current-action importance weighting replaces today’s behavior action probabilities with target probabilities.

It still averages rewards over states generated by earlier behavior actions.

- With the full Markov state observed, stationary distribution corrections can support parametric estimation: Mehrabi and Wager (2025).
- With hidden state, Hu and Wager (2023) weight recent action histories. Longer histories reduce bias but increase variance.

Does bounded target-to-behavior stationary occupancy restore parametric accuracy when the state is hidden?

---

## Key idea

Let \(d_b\) and \(d_e\) be the behavior and target stationary distributions of the full state \(s\), including its hidden component.

Restrict how much the target can increase occupancy:

\[
d_e(s)\le C\,d_b(s),
\qquad s\in\mathcal S .
\]

Here \(\mathcal S\) is the full state space and \(C\ge1\) is the occupancy bound. Its distance from equality is:

\[
q_C:=\frac{C-1}{C}.
\]

The radius \(q_C\) bounds the state imbalance inherited from behavior actions.

**Our answer:** with finite states, binary actions, stationary data, known observed-state randomization, bounded rewards and action ratios, and geometric forgetting, calibrated history weighting attains matching minimax bounds.

Fixed \(C>1\) still allows slower than parametric estimation. Parametric accuracy emerges when the radius is sufficiently small.

---

## Example

The observed state is constant. Hidden state contains depth \(J_t\in\{0,\ldots,Q\}\), with \(Q\ge1\), and sign \(U_t\in\{-1,+1\}\).

**Depth transitions:** below \(Q\), advance to \(J_t+1\) with probability \(\alpha\), and reset to zero with probability \(1-\alpha\). At \(Q\), reset with probability one. Here \(0<\alpha<1\).

- An advance preserves sign under action one; action zero redraws sign fairly.
- A reset draws a fresh sign with mean \(\varepsilon_C=\min\{(C-1)/2,1/2\}\).

\[
b(A_t=1)=\frac{1}{L},\qquad b(A_t=0)=1-\frac{1}{L},
\qquad
e(A_t=1)=1,\qquad e(A_t=0)=0.
\]

Here \(L>1\) controls action overlap. The target always preserves sign; behavior can erase it.

Rewards depend on sign only at terminal depth \(Q\). Earlier actions therefore affect today’s reward even though the observed state never changes.

---

## Model

The full Markov state is \(S_t=(X_t,H_t)\), with hidden component \(H_t\). A time-invariant kernel \(K\) generates reward and next state from current full state and action.

- Known policies \(b,e\) depend only on \(X_t\).
- **Sequential ignorability:** given \(X_t\), behavior randomization additionally depends on neither hidden state nor past history.
- **Stationary start:** \(S_0\sim d_b\).
- Rewards satisfy \(|Y_t|\le1\); both state spaces are finite and actions are binary.

Our stationary target value is:

\[
\theta(K,e)
:=
\sum_{s=(x,h)\in\mathcal S} d_e(s)g_e(s)
=
\sum_{s=(x,h)\in\mathcal S} d_e(s)
\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}
e(a\mid x)\,E_K[Y_t\mid S_t=s,A_t=a].
\]

Here \(\mathcal A=\{0,1\}\). The function \(g_e(s)\) averages current reward over target actions at state \(s\). The outer average uses the full-state distribution produced by running the target in the long run.

---

## Assumptions

**Action overlap** controls observable importance weights:

\[
e(a\mid x)\le L b(a\mid x).
\]

Here \(L=\exp(\zeta)\), with \(\zeta>0\). Every target action has behavior support; larger \(L\) permits larger weights.

**Geometric mixing** makes both policies forget their starting states:

\[
\|\nu P_p-\nu'P_p\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\le
\alpha\|\nu-\nu'\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\]

For \(p=b,e\), \(P_p\) is the full-state transition under policy \(p\). Total variation measures the difference between distributions \(\nu,\nu'\).

Here \(\alpha=\exp(-1/t_0)\), with \(t_0>0\); larger \(t_0\) means slower forgetting.

Together with stationary overlap \(d_e(s)\le C d_b(s)\) and the model conditions, these define \(\mathcal M_T(t_0,\zeta,C)\).

In the example, every state shares a reset component of probability \(1-\alpha\). That component forgets both depth and sign, giving contraction by at most \(\alpha\).

---

## Main result

Let \(R_T(t_0,\zeta,C)\) be the smallest worst-case mean squared error attainable from observed data and known policies over \(\mathcal M_T(t_0,\zeta,C)\).

\[
\beta=\frac{2}{2+t_0\zeta},
\qquad
\mathfrak r_T(t_0,\zeta,C)
=
T^{-1}+T^{-\beta}q_C^{2(1-\beta)} .
\]

The exponent \(\beta\) combines mixing and action overlap. The risk surface \(\mathfrak r_T\) adds ordinary sampling error to the cost of repairing hidden-state imbalance.

@informal thm:uniform-overlap-frontier: For fixed \(t_0,\zeta>0\), all \(C\ge1\), and sufficiently large \(T\), observable minimax risk over \(\mathcal M_T(t_0,\zeta,C)\) is comparable to \(\mathfrak r_T\); radius-calibrated histories attain its upper bound.

\[
c_{\mathrm{lo}}\mathfrak r_T(t_0,\zeta,C)
\le
R_T(t_0,\zeta,C)
\le
c_{\mathrm{hi}}\mathfrak r_T(t_0,\zeta,C),
\]

The positive constants and sample-size threshold depend only on \(t_0,\zeta\), uniformly over \(C\) and all finite state counts.

The estimator uses supplied values of \(t_0,\zeta,C\). Every observable estimator faces the lower bound.

---

## Risk regimes

Three readings of the same surface:

- **Fixed \(C>1\):** mean squared error is comparable to \(T^{-\beta}\), retaining the Hu–Wager hidden-state exponent.
- **Equality, \(C=1\):** \(d_e=d_b\), and current-action weighting attains the \(T^{-1}\) scale.
- **Shrinking radius:** the transition occurs at \(q_C\asymp T^{-1/2}\). If \(Tq_C^2\) remains bounded, current-action weighting attains parametric accuracy.

In the example, fixed \(C>1\) permits a fixed positive reset-sign bias. Increasing terminal depth then sustains the slower hidden-state rate while respecting stationary overlap.

## Partial histories

Define the observed action ratio \(\rho_t=e(A_t\mid X_t)/b(A_t\mid X_t)\) on behavior-supported actions.

Choose depth \(k\): \(k\) past actions plus the current action.

\[
Z_t(k)
:=
Y_t\prod_{j=t-k}^{t}\rho_j,
\]

The score \(Z_t(k)\) reweights reward \(Y_t\) by target-to-behavior action ratios throughout the window.

\[
\widehat\theta_k
:=
\operatorname{clip}_{[-1,1]}
\left\{
\frac{1}{T-k}\sum_{t=k}^{T-1} Z_t(k)
\right\}.
\]

The estimator averages the complete windows, then clips to the reward range \([-1,1]\).

In the example, only all-one action windows receive positive weight. Weighting them reproduces the target’s sign-preserving action sequence.

---

## Remaining bias

Sequential ignorability makes the weight product replace behavior actions with target actions throughout the window.

Its population mean is \(\sum_s d_b(s)(P_e^k g_e)(s)\): start under \(d_b\), run \(k\) target transitions, then average reward under the target action rule.

- Stationary overlap means \(d_b\) contains at least a \(1/C\) share of \(d_e\). Their total variation distance is therefore at most \(q_C\).
- Each target transition contracts this mismatch by \(\alpha\).
- Remaining reward bias is controlled on the scale \(q_C\alpha^k\).

At depth zero, the state distribution remains \(d_b\).

In the example, longer weighted histories preserve more sign memory. Resets make sign information predating the window progressively less consequential.

---

## Bias–variance bound

Action ratios have conditional mean one and are bounded by \(L\). Multiplying them makes the variance grow on the scale \(L^k/T\).

Overlapping windows do not add another factor of \(k\): nonshared ratios average out, leaving geometric covariance bounds. For separated windows, behavior mixing controls the dependence.

For \(M\in\mathcal M_T(t_0,\zeta,C)\) and \(k\le T/2\):

\[
\mathbb E_M\!\left[\{\widehat\theta_k-\theta(K,e)\}^2\right]
\le
4q_C^2\alpha^{2k}
+\frac{2}{T}\left\{
L^{k+1}\left(1+\frac{4}{L-1}\right)
+\frac{4}{1-\alpha}
\right\},
\]

The expectation averages over the behavior trajectory.

- Squared bias decreases with history length.
- Action-weighting variance increases with history length.

A smaller occupancy radius means less bias needs repair.

---

## History depth

Balance squared bias \(q_C^2\alpha^{2k}\) against variance \(L^k/T\):

\[
\kappa_T^{\mathrm{rad}}(C):=
\begin{cases}
0, & Tq_C^2\le 1,\\[3pt]
\displaystyle
\min\!\left\{
\left\lfloor\frac T2\right\rfloor,
\left\lfloor\frac{\log(Tq_C^2)}{2\log(1/\alpha)+\log L}\right\rfloor
\right\},
& Tq_C^2>1.
\end{cases}
\]

The chosen depth \(\kappa_T^{\mathrm{rad}}(C)\) uses trajectory length and supplied mixing, action-overlap, and stationary-overlap bounds.

- When \(Tq_C^2\le1\), use depth zero: occupancy bias is already small enough.
- Otherwise, history grows logarithmically with \(Tq_C^2\).

The denominator combines two mixing exponents from squared bias with one action-overlap exponent from variance. This balance produces \(\beta\) and the upper risk bound.

Could another observable estimator avoid this tradeoff?

---

## Stationary occupancy

Return to the depth-and-sign example. Actions change sign memory, while **depth transitions are identical under both policies**.

Only an advance from \(j-1\) reaches depth \(j>0\), so stationary masses satisfy \(w_j=\alpha w_{j-1}\). The forced reset at \(Q\) closes the chain; normalizing this finite geometric sequence gives:

\[
w_j\frac{1+u\varepsilon_C L^{-j}}{2},
\qquad
w_j=
\frac{(1-\alpha)\alpha^j}{1-\alpha^{Q+1}},
\qquad
j\in\{0,\ldots,Q\},\ u\in\{-1,+1\}.
\]

The first expression is behavior’s stationary probability of depth \(j\) and sign \(u\).

Both policies have depth mass \(w_j\). Behavior’s sign mean is \(\varepsilon_C L^{-j}\); the target preserves \(\varepsilon_C\).

Their full-state laws satisfy:

\[
d_e(s)\le (1+\varepsilon_C)d_b(s),
\qquad
1+\varepsilon_C\le C.
\]

Increasing terminal depth preserves the occupancy restriction.

---

## Reward alternatives

The alternatives \(v=+1\) and \(v=-1\) share dynamics and policies. They reverse only the reward response.

Conditional on the current hidden state, the probability of reward \(y\in\{-1,+1\}\) is:

\[
\frac{1+yvc_0U_t\mathbf 1\{J_t=Q\}}{2}
\]

Here \(c_0=(1-\alpha)/4\) is the reward amplitude. The indicator turns on the sign response only at depth \(Q\); all other rewards are fair coins.

Under the target, terminal sign has mean \(\varepsilon_C\). Thus:

\[
\theta(K,e)
=
v\,c_0(t_0)\,\varepsilon_C\,w_Q .
\]

The notation \(c_0(t_0)\) emphasizes that the amplitude depends on the mixing scale.

The target-value gap is twice amplitude times reset-sign bias times terminal occupancy. Its square is comparable to \(\varepsilon_C^2\alpha^{2Q}\).

---

## Observed sign memory

Let \(\mathcal F_{t-1}^{\mathrm{obs}}\) be the past action-and-reward record, and \(o\) one realized record.

Define \(q_t^v(o)=\Pr_v(J_t=Q\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}^{\mathrm{obs}}=o)\), the conditional terminal probability.

With \(\ell_t=\min\{Q,t\}\), surviving sign memory is recorded by:

\[
a_t^{(Q)}
:=
L^{-(Q-\ell_t)}
\prod_{j=t-\ell_t}^{t-1}\mathbf 1\{A_j=1\},
\]

After \(Q\) dates, this is the indicator that the previous \(Q\) actions were all one. Earlier, the power of \(L\) accounts for pre-sample actions.

A run ending at \(Q\) has encountered only fair rewards since its reset. Those rewards cannot select its current sign.

The exact observed reward mean is:

\[
\mathbb E_v[Y_t\mid \mathcal F_{t-1}^{\mathrm{obs}}=o]
=
v\,c_0\,\varepsilon_C\,a_t^{(Q)}(o)\,q_t^v(o),
\]

The signal requires both surviving sign memory and a chance of terminal depth.

---

## Reward updating

Why can favorable reward histories not concentrate the conditional probability at \(Q\)?

- Ending at \(Q\) requires \(Q\) consecutive advances. They contribute \(\alpha^Q\), and their preceding rewards are fair.
- An informative reward occurs at \(Q\) and is followed by a forced reset. Its information cannot carry forward along that same advance run.
- Conditioning still divides by the likelihood of observed rewards across **all** hidden paths.

At date \(i\), a reward’s likelihood relative to a fair coin is at least \(1-c_0q_i^v\).

Consequently, for \(t\ge Q\), the conditional terminal probability obeys
\(q_t^v\le\alpha^Q\prod_{i=t-Q}^{t-1}(1-c_0q_i^v)^{-1}\), evaluated along the observed history.

Only the latest \(Q\) reward updates enter. Before \(Q\) dates, the stationary geometric depth tail supplies the missing advance probabilities.

---

## Uniform filtering

The reward-update bound can be used twice.

1. Start with \(q_i^v\le1\). Even the most favorable reward sequence gives \(q_t^v\le(\alpha/(1-c_0))^Q\).
2. Write \(\eta_0=\alpha/(1-c_0)\). Our amplitude choice \(c_0=(1-\alpha)/4\) ensures \(\eta_0<1\).
3. Insert the first bound back into the update: each likelihood denominator is now at least \(1-c_0\eta_0^Q\).

The cumulative inflation is at most \((1-c_0\eta_0^Q)^{-Q}\). The exponentially small correction \(\eta_0^Q\) outweighs the \(Q\) possible updates, so this inflation is bounded uniformly over depth.

Therefore, for every observed history:

\[
q_t^v(o)\le K_0\,\alpha^Q .
\]

Here \(K_0\) depends only on \(t_0\).

Terminal probability stays on its rarity scale even for reward sequences that favor terminal depth.

---

## Information limit

Hidden terminal probability enters the observed reward mean **before** information squares it.

Reward probabilities stay between \((1-c_0)/2\) and \((1+c_0)/2\). Thus information is quadratic in the conditional mean difference, giving the squared terminal factor \(\alpha^{2Q}\).

The surviving-memory factor supplies another restriction: under behavior, an all-one history of length \(Q\) occurs with probability \(L^{-Q}\). Its indicator remains unchanged when squared. Initial incomplete windows have no larger second moment.

Let \(\mathbb P_{Q,+1}^{\mathrm{obs},T}\) and \(\mathbb P_{Q,-1}^{\mathrm{obs},T}\) be the two observed-trajectory laws. Their Kullback–Leibler divergence measures distinguishability:

\[
\operatorname{KL}\!\left(\mathbb P_{Q,+1}^{\mathrm{obs},T}\,\middle\|\,\mathbb P_{Q,-1}^{\mathrm{obs},T}\right)
\le
\frac{4c_0^2K_0^2}{1-c_0^2}\,
T\,\varepsilon_C^2\,\alpha^{2Q}L^{-Q}.
\]

Across \(T\) dates, reset bias and hidden terminal probability are squared; the frequency of informative action histories enters once.

---

## Matching lower bound

When \(Tq_C^2>1\), choose terminal depth \(Q\) so that \(T\varepsilon_C^2\alpha^{2Q}L^{-Q}\) stays bounded.

The two alternatives remain statistically hard to distinguish, while their squared target-value separation is comparable to \(\varepsilon_C^2\alpha^{2Q}\).

The reset bias tracks the occupancy radius:

\[
\frac{q_C}{2}\le \varepsilon_C \le q_C
\]

- Increasing depth reduces squared target separation through \(\alpha^{2Q}\).
- Observable information falls faster, through \(\alpha^{2Q}L^{-Q}\).

Balancing these effects leaves squared separation on the scale \(T^{-\beta}q_C^{2(1-\beta)}\), with the same \(\beta\) as the estimator’s balance. Hard-to-distinguish values force that order of worst-case error.

Ordinary reward uncertainty also forces a \(T^{-1}\) floor, including when the radius is small. Together these give the matching risk surface.

## Open questions

- Choosing history depth when the stationary-overlap radius is unknown.
- Confidence intervals for stationary target values.
- Policy-learning regret built on value estimation.
- Risk with a fixed hidden-state size: our matching alternatives increase terminal depth, and therefore state count, with \(T\).

---

## Takeaways

- History weighting changes the transitions that generated today’s state. Mixing limits how much unreweighted history still matters.
- Action overlap determines the variance cost of longer histories; stationary overlap determines how much initial imbalance needs repair.
- Fixed \(C>1\) retains the hidden-state rate. At \(q_C\lesssim T^{-1/2}\), parametric accuracy is possible. The matching construction keeps terminal depth rare even after conditioning on rewards.
