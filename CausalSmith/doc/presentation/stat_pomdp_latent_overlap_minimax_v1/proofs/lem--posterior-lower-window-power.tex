\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:posterior-lower-window-power}]
We prove the slightly stronger assertion with the prefix length left variable.  For a prefix
\(o\) of length \(n\), write
\[
\Lambda_d^{(n)}(o)
=
\prod_{\substack{0\le i<n\\ n-d\le i}}
\left(1-c_0(t_0)\pi_v(o_{\le i})\right),
\]
with the convention that an empty product is \(1\).

1. If \(d=0\), then the condition \(n\le i<n\) selects no indices, so
\[
\Lambda_0^{(n)}(o)=1=(1-c_0(t_0)b)^0 .
\]
This proves the base case. % lean: CausalSmith.Stat.PomdpLatentOverlapMinimax.pow_one_sub_le_signedDepthPosteriorLowerWindow

2. Assume the claim has been proved for \(d\).  Consider a prefix \(o\) of length \(n\) with
\(d+1\le n\).  If \(n=0\), this inequality is impossible, so there is no case to prove.  Thus write
\(n=k+1\).  Let \(o^{-}\) be the length-\(k\) prefix obtained by dropping the last observed pair:
\[
o^{-}(i)=o(i),\qquad 0\le i<k .
\]
For every \(0\le i<k\), the first \(i\) observations of \(o^{-}\) are exactly the first \(i\)
observations of \(o\):
\[
(o^{-})_{\le i}=o_{\le i}.
\]
Hence the strict-prefix posterior hypothesis for \(o\) gives
\[
\pi_v\bigl((o^{-})_{\le i}\bigr)=\pi_v(o_{\le i})\le b,
\qquad 0\le i<k .
\]
Since \(d+1\le k+1\), we have \(d\le k\), so the induction hypothesis applied to \(o^{-}\) yields
\[
(1-c_0(t_0)b)^d\le \Lambda_d^{(k)}(o^{-}).
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.PomdpLatentOverlapMinimax.observedPrefixTake_castSucc

3. The final-window product for length \(k+1\) splits into the length-\(k\) final-window product
for \(o^{-}\) and the last factor:
\[
\Lambda_{d+1}^{(k+1)}(o)
=
\Lambda_d^{(k)}(o^{-})
\left(1-c_0(t_0)\pi_v(o_{\le k})\right).
\]
Indeed, the threshold satisfies
\[
(k+1)-(d+1)=k-d,
\]
so the eligible indices before the last one are precisely the eligible indices in the length-\(k\)
window, and the remaining eligible index is \(i=k\). % lean: CausalSmith.Stat.PomdpLatentOverlapMinimax.signedDepthPosteriorLowerWindow_succ

4. The posterior hypothesis at the last strict prefix gives \(\pi_v(o_{\le k})\le b\).  Because
\(0\le c_0(t_0)\), multiplying preserves the inequality,
\[
c_0(t_0)\pi_v(o_{\le k})\le c_0(t_0)b,
\]
and subtracting from \(1\) reverses the side on which the smaller product appears:
\[
1-c_0(t_0)b\le 1-c_0(t_0)\pi_v(o_{\le k}).
\]
Together with the assumed nonnegativity \(0\le 1-c_0(t_0)b\), we have
\[
0\le (1-c_0(t_0)b)^d .
\]
Combining this nonnegativity, the induction bound, and the last-factor bound gives
\[
(1-c_0(t_0)b)^{d+1}
=
(1-c_0(t_0)b)^d(1-c_0(t_0)b)
\le
\Lambda_d^{(k)}(o^{-})
\left(1-c_0(t_0)\pi_v(o_{\le k})\right)
=
\Lambda_{d+1}^{(k+1)}(o).
\]
This completes the induction, and hence the desired bound for the original prefix of length \(k\). % lean: CausalSmith.Stat.PomdpLatentOverlapMinimax.pow_one_sub_le_signedDepthPosteriorLowerWindow
\end{proof}
