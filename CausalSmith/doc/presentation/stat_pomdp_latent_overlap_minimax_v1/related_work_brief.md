Off-policy evaluation for dynamic treatment and reinforcement-learning policies sits at the intersection of longitudinal causal inference, Markov decision processes, and partial observation. The causal lineage begins with the g-formula and time-varying treatment framework of \citet{Robins1986}, propensity-score balancing in \citet{Rosenbaum1983}, marginal structural models and inverse-probability weighting in \citet{Robins2000}, and dynamic treatment regimes in \citet{Murphy2003}. The stochastic-control side is anchored by finite-state MDP theory \citep{Puterman1994}, finite-horizon POMDP control \citep{Smallwood1973}, the AI formulation of POMDP planning \citep{Kaelbling1998}, and reinforcement-learning foundations \citep{Sutton2018}. This paper uses that shared language to study the stationary mean outcome of a known target policy from one behavior trajectory when the Markov state has an unobserved component.

Within fully observed or effectively observed MDPs, the main methodological lineage replaces trajectory-wise importance products by value functions, stationary density ratios, and doubly robust structure. \citet{Precup2000} developed off-policy eligibility traces, while \citet{Jiang2016} and \citet{Thomas2016} extended doubly robust off-policy value estimation to sequential decisions. For long-run and infinite-horizon settings, \citet{Liu2018} introduced stationary state-visitation density-ratio estimation to address horizon growth, \citet{Nachum2019} proposed DualDICE for discounted stationary distribution corrections, \citet{Uehara2020} unified minimax learning of marginalized weights and value functions, and \citet{KallusUehara2020} derived semiparametric efficiency theory for fully observed MDP OPE with doubly robust estimators based on \(q\)-functions and marginalized density ratios. \citet{LiaoKlasnjaMurphy2021} gives inference for long-term average outcomes in mobile health, and \citet{Liao2022} establishes semiparametric efficiency and finite-sample regret guarantees for batch policy learning in average-reward MDPs. These papers supply the fully observed benchmark: under observable state and suitable overlap, stationary-ratio and doubly robust methods support root-\(T\) estimation and valid inference.

The closest partially observed baseline is \citet{HuWager2023}. In finite-state POMDPs under sequential ignorability, known behavior and target policies, bounded one-step policy overlap, and geometric mixing, they introduce partial-history importance weighting (PHIW). Their analysis gives a polynomial MSE rate whose exponent is governed by the mixing time and log policy-overlap; in the binary-action bounded-reward specialization considered here, the corresponding exponent is \(\beta=2/(2+t_0\zeta)\), and their minimax lower bound shows that partial observation carries a genuine statistical penalty relative to fully observed MDPs. \citet{TennenholtzShalitMannor2020} studies batch OPE in POMDPs through a decoupled POMDP model with structural recovery conditions. \citet{Bennett2021}, \citet{Miao2022}, and \citet{Shi2022} develop proximal or bridge-function approaches for partially observed and confounded POMDPs, with identification and estimation based on proxy variables, conditional moment equations, and ill-posed inverse problems. \citet{Uehara2023} introduces future-dependent value functions and obtains PAC-style guarantees under sufficient proxy information and Bellman completeness, while \citet{ZhangJiang2024} shows that the future-dependent framework needs coverage controls such as outcome and belief coverage to keep the relevant quantities polynomial. \citet{Zhang2025} extends the frontier to history-dependent target policies and separates model-free hardness from model-based tractability in large-observation POMDPs, and \citet{Kuang2025} studies confounded POMDP OPE with methods aimed at reducing order dependence.

The present result occupies the Hu--Wager branch rather than the proxy-recovery branch. It keeps the single stationary trajectory, known memoryless policies, bounded rewards, finite but cardinality-unrestricted latent state, and no proxy-informativeness or emission-rank assumptions. Its additional structural condition is latent stationary overlap, \(d_e(s)\le C d_b(s)\), imposed on the unobserved joint state. \citet{MehrabiWager2025} is the closest overlap comparator in the fully observed MDP setting: their truncated doubly robust estimator recovers root-\(T\) behavior when the observable stationary density ratio is square-integrable and has slower tail-dependent rates when that ratio has heavier tails, with minimax optimality under mixing classes. The present paper characterizes what that style of occupancy assurance buys under hidden state. For fixed latent overlap radius \(C>1\), it establishes the same hidden-state exponent as Hu--Wager, while the all-radius surface \(T^{-1}+T^{-\beta}q_C^{2(1-\beta)}\) identifies the parametric boundary layer \(q_C\asymp T^{-1/2}\). Thus bounded latent occupancy is a quantitative radius condition for PHIW depth selection; observable stationary-ratio methods become root-\(T\) comparators when the relevant ratio is available in the observed state.

The mobile-health motivation follows the average-reward and dynamic-regime literature. \citet{NahumShani2018} frames just-in-time adaptive interventions as policies adapting treatment to evolving context, and \citet{LiaoKlasnjaMurphy2021} uses HeartSteps to motivate long-run policy evaluation. \citet{LuckettEtAl2020} develops V-learning for indefinite-horizon mobile-health treatment regimes and applies it to type 1 diabetes management; \citet{MaahsEtAl2012} provides the outpatient glucose-excursion data context used in that line of work. Hu and Wager’s glucose application uses a simulator calibrated to this literature. The finite-state insulin adaptation in the present paper serves as a controlled demonstration of the theorem’s scope: under a discretized model with certified contraction and latent stationary overlap, immediate action weighting remains biased, and the minimax analysis explains when longer observable histories are the statistically justified response.
