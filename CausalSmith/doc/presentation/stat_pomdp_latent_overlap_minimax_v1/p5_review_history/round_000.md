# Referee review

**Recommendation:** major_revision
**Overall score:** 6.4/10 — The paper delivers a potentially important, formally verified minimax frontier, but several material presentation inconsistencies and an overly burdensome manuscript architecture currently prevent reliable journal-level reading and evaluation.

The submission characterizes the cardinality-uniform minimax risk for stationary off-policy evaluation in finite POMDPs under action overlap, contraction, and latent stationary overlap, deriving a continuous all-radius frontier and a matching radius-calibrated PHIW estimator. The verified results support the central mathematical contribution, including the fixed-radius exponent, the parametric endpoint, and the signed-depth converse with growing latent dimension. The paper is promising, but major revision is needed to correct indexing and estimator-calibration inconsistencies, accurately document verification, and reorganize the presentation around the master theorem.

## Strengths
- The all-radius surface gives a precise and interesting account of how latent stationary overlap interacts with partial observation.
- The upper and lower bounds are cardinality-uniform, and the manuscript clearly discloses that the converse uses a latent alphabet growing as order log T.
- The signed-depth construction and radius-explicit KL calculation provide a concrete mechanism explaining the hidden-state difficulty.
- The related-work section identifies Hu--Wager as the closest partially observed benchmark and distinguishes the paper from proxy- and bridge-based identification approaches.
- The limitations sections appropriately identify empirical untestability of latent overlap, unknown-radius calibration, nonstationary starts, and the fixed-hidden-alphabet question.

## Findings
- **[major·statement] Setup and estimands** — The time conventions are internally inconsistent. The full trajectory is defined with states indexed from 0 through T and action-reward pairs from 0 through T-1, while the Markov-kernel and sequential-ignorability assumptions use t=1,...,T and invoke X_{t+1}; the observable path later alternates among (X_t,A_t,Y_t)_{t=1}^T and (X_t,A_t,Y_{t+1})_{t=0}^{T-1}. As written, some displayed variables fall outside the trajectory just defined.
  - *Fix:* Choose one convention throughout. For example, define states as S_1,...,S_{T+1} and observations as (X_t,A_t,Y_t) for t=1,...,T, then rewrite every assumption, score, filtration, and proof index accordingly; alternatively use the verified zero-based convention everywhere and provide one explicit translation once.
- **[major·prose] Introduction** — The insulin paragraph gives an exact calibration that contradicts the defined estimator: “the implied radius-calibrated depth is κ_T^{cal}(720)=⌈log{T(719/720)^2}/log(L/α^2)⌉_+.” The verified depth κ_T^{rad} uses a floor, an explicit T/2 cap, and a separate zero-depth branch; κ_T^{cal} is otherwise undefined.
  - *Fix:* Replace the formula by the exact definition of κ_T^{rad}(720), including the branch, floor, and cap. If an approximation is desired, state only that using the sufficient bound C=720 yields a depth differing from the fixed-radius balanced depth by a bounded rounding adjustment for large T.
- **[major·prose] global** — The author footnote promises that statement mappings, verification scope, toolchain, commit, and conditional inputs are “recorded in the verification appendix,” but the manuscript contains only a short generic verification note. It provides no commit, toolchain, object-level mapping, or identification of presentation-synthesized definitions, and it claims grouping by appendix subsection although the appendix is not organized that way.
  - *Fix:* Add a genuine verification appendix or supplementary table listing the exact artifact commit, Lean/toolchain version, each anchored object and declaration, proof status, presentation-only objects, and any external dependency. Alternatively, narrow the author footnote to the information actually supplied and give a stable public artifact reference.
- **[major·structure] Main results and appendix** — The exposition is organized against its logical hierarchy. The uniform frontier is described as the master theorem and the fixed, unit, and shrinking results as slices, yet it appears after those results, and the proof of the shrinking theorem invokes the later uniform theorem. The appendix then reproduces extremely long low-level formal proofs, obscuring the statistical ideas. The opening sentence of “Estimator-specific risk” compounds this by forward-referencing nearly every theorem and lemma in the paper.
  - *Fix:* State and explain the uniform frontier first, present the endpoint, fixed-radius, and shrinking-radius results explicitly as corollaries, and order proofs accordingly. Replace the giant dependency sentence with a direct definition. Move machine-oriented proof renderings to an online supplement and retain concise mathematical proof sketches emphasizing the bias-variance balance and two-point construction.
- **[minor·prose] Main results** — The repeated term “radius-adaptive” can suggest adaptation to an unknown radius, although the estimator explicitly takes C as a tuning input and the limitations state that data-driven selection remains open.
  - *Fix:* Use “radius-calibrated” or “oracle radius-calibrated” consistently, and state at the first estimator mention that C is treated as known or externally supplied. Reserve “adaptive” for a procedure that selects depth without knowing C.
- **[minor·prose] A finite insulin-policy adaptation** — The conclusion “Consequently, the adaptive observable-history estimator ... is the relevant estimator for this finite threshold-policy example” is stronger than the demonstrated facts. A nonzero stationary bias for immediate weighting does not establish that the proposed calibrated PHIW estimator improves finite-sample risk for this particular grid, nor that the conservative sufficient bound C=720 gives an effective practical depth.
  - *Fix:* State affirmatively what the example establishes: it lies in the theorem's finite class and exhibits nonzero immediate-weighting bias. Characterize PHIW as the theorem-supported class-level estimator, or add a direct finite-sample comparison if model-specific practical superiority is intended.
- **[minor·citation] Related work** — Several quantitative competitor claims lack pinpoint support, especially the description of Mehrabi--Wager's parametric regime and the claim that the Hu--Wager lower-bound stationary ratio grows exponentially with depth. The comparison also uses the vague phrase “the same type of density-ratio bound” despite meaningful differences between pointwise bounded overlap and square-integrability or tail conditions.
  - *Fix:* Add theorem, assumption, equation, section, or page locators for these claims and sharpen the comparison of overlap norms, observability, estimator inputs, state-dimension regimes, and inferential targets.
- **[minor·statement] Setup and estimands** — The minimax-risk display places the fixed condition “1≤T” inside the supremum over M even though T is already the external horizon parameter and model-class membership carries positive horizon. This makes the optimization domain look variable or conditional in a confusing way.
  - *Fix:* Define R_T only for T≥1 and write the supremum simply over M in the stated model class; explain separately how the T=0 formal carrier, if retained in the artifact, is excluded from the statistical analysis.

## Questions for authors
- Is C intended to be known exactly, a known conservative upper bound, or merely an existential property of the data-generating process?
- Will the declaration map, toolchain, and exact verified commit be made available as a stable public supplement at submission?
- Can the authors formulate the main statistical argument independently of the rendered Lean proof trace, with the latter archived as verification material?
