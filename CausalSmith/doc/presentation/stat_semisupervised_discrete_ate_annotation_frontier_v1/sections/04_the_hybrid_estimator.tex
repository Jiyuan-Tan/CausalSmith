\section{The hybrid estimator}\label{sec:hybrid-estimator}

This section gives an algorithmic reading of the estimator used in \cref{obj:def:mixed-estimator-handle}. The construction separates three statistical roles that are intertwined in the target functional: the labeled records supply outcome marks, the combined treatment--covariate records estimate marginal cell frequencies, and the calibration chooses when a cell is treated by polynomial extrapolation rather than by direct inverse-count weighting.

The first step is deterministic sample splitting. The labeled sample is divided into an outcome block of size \(M_0\), a pilot contribution of size \(n_p\), and a factorial contribution of size \(n_f\). The auxiliary sample is divided into pilot and factorial contributions of sizes \(m_p\) and \(m_f\). Pooling the corresponding labeled and auxiliary treatment--covariate records gives pilot and factorial pools of sizes \(M_p\) and \(M_f\). This split makes the outcome counts independent of the treatment--covariate counts used to choose and evaluate the weights, matching the independence structure required by the risk analysis.

The finite calibration in \cref{obj:def:mixed-estimator-handle} then fixes the polynomial degree \(L\), the light-cell scale \(B\), and the pilot threshold \(k_0\). The predicate \(\mathsf C_\epsilon(n,m)\) is a computable list of inequalities involving these quantities, the dyadic overlap certificate \(\bar\epsilon\), and the prefix intensities \(u,t_p,t\). When the predicate holds, the selected scales keep the polynomial coefficients and Poisson tails within the finite-sample ranges used by the upper-bound argument. When it fails, the estimator is defined by the zero rule, which keeps the procedure total for every admissible \(n,m,d\).

The estimator uses Poisson-prefix derandomization to express an independent-Poisson statistic as a deterministic function of the fixed samples. For each triple of prefix lengths \((r_0,r_p,r_f)\), the rule keeps the first \(r_0\) observations in the outcome block, the first \(r_p\) observations in the pilot pool, and the first \(r_f\) observations in the factorial pool, then averages the resulting clipped statistic against the three Poisson probabilities. This finite sum is the conditional expectation of the randomized prefix statistic, so the implemented estimator remains an ordinary measurable statistic of the observed data.

For each covariate cell \(x\), the statistic forms three counts. The pilot count \(J_x\) decides the regime. The outcome counts \(S_{1x}\) and \(S_{0x}\) count labeled successes in the two treatment arms within the outcome prefix. The factorial counts \(K_{1x}\) and \(K_{0x}\) count treatment--covariate observations in the factorial prefix. Thus outcome information enters only through the success counts, while the larger annotated and unannotated treatment--covariate pool enters through \(J_x\) and \(K_{ax}\).

Cells with \(J_x\le k_0\) use the light-cell polynomial weight. The Chebyshev component \(T_L\) defines \(H_L\), its continuation \(E_L\), and the correction polynomial \(G_L\), giving the weight polynomial \(W_{a,L}\) in \cref{obj:def:mixed-estimator-handle}. Its factorial lift \(\widehat W_{a,L,x}\) replaces monomials in the unknown cell probabilities by falling-factorial count statistics. This applies the polynomial-approximation strategy used in sharp discrete functional estimation \citep{JiaoVenkatHanWeissman2015,WuYang2016} to the inverse propensity-type weights needed for the average treatment effect, with the rare-cell organization motivated by sample-amplification and unseen-species perspectives \citep{ValiantValiant2017,OrlitskySureshWu2016}.

Cells with \(J_x>k_0\) use the heavy-cell inverse-count rule. In those cells, the factorial prefix supplies enough treatment--covariate mass for the ratios \((K_{0x}+K_{1x}+1)/(K_{ax}+1)\) to act as stabilized inverse-count weights. The added ones keep the weights finite at every realized count configuration, while the pilot threshold routes cells with limited marginal information to the polynomial branch.

Finally, \cref{obj:def:mixed-estimator-handle} sums the cell contributions and applies \(\operatorname{clip}_{[-1,1]}\). The clipping matches the natural range of the average treatment effect functional and controls exceptional prefix realizations without changing the structure of the light- and heavy-cell rules. The resulting statistic, \leanref{sym:\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{mix}}_{n,m,d}}{\ensuremath{\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{mix}}_{n,m,d}}}, is therefore a fully specified finite-sample estimator built from deterministic splitting, calibrated polynomial extrapolation, inverse-count stabilization, and bounded aggregation.

\Cref{obj:thm:uniform-mixed-upper} is the performance guarantee attached to this construction. Under the overlap model and product-sample experiment stated earlier, the calibrated estimator attains the rate in \cref{obj:def:frontier-rate-handle} uniformly over the model class. The two pieces of the rate correspond to the two resources used by the algorithm: labeled outcomes determine the \(1/n\) component, while treatment--covariate counts from both samples determine the marginal-frequency component through \(n+m\).

\paragraph{Proof roadmap.}
Poissonization first makes the outcome, pilot, and factorial counts independent. The light-cell argument uses Chebyshev approximation and unbiased factorial moments; the heavy-cell argument uses the exact stabilized inverse-count mean and moment bounds. Pilot tails control routing errors, and summing the cellwise bias and variance bounds yields the Poissonized risk. Prefix conditioning then transfers that bound to the fixed samples. For the converse, moment-matched common-marginal priors keep the auxiliary table indistinguishable while separating the ATE, and a separate binary submodel supplies the \(1/n\) label floor. The dependency map at the start of \cref{sec:appendix-upper} links these ideas to the detailed certificates.
