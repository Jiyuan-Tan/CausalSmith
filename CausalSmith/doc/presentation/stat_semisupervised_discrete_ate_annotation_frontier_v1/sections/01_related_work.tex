\section{Related work}\label{sec:related-work}

This paper studies a \leanref{S-1}{two-sample semi-supervised discrete causal experiment}: a complete outcome-labeled sample of size \leanref{sym:n}{\ensuremath{n}} is augmented by an outcome-unlabeled treatment-covariate sample of size \leanref{sym:m}{\ensuremath{m}} on a finite covariate alphabet of size \leanref{sym:d}{\ensuremath{d}}. In that experiment, the target is the average treatment effect under the usual potential-outcomes interpretation, while the statistical difficulty is governed by how outcome labels and treatment-covariate cell information are split across the two samples. The contribution is therefore most closely connected to five literatures: identification in potential-outcomes models, semiparametric efficiency for average treatment effects with missing or observed outcomes, high-dimensional causal inference under structure, semi-supervised treatment-effect estimation, and minimax estimation of non-smooth discrete functionals.

The identification framework follows the potential-outcomes tradition of \citet{Rubin1974} and the propensity-score formulation of \citet{RosenbaumRubin1983}; see also \citet{ImbensRubin2015} for a systematic account. In that tradition, consistency and conditional exchangeability connect observed outcome regressions to the causal estimand. The present analysis specializes this framework to a finite covariate space and asks how accurately the resulting discrete average treatment effect can be estimated when additional observations reveal treatment and covariates but omit the outcome.

A large semiparametric literature characterizes regular estimation of average treatment effects under missingness, inverse weighting, augmentation, and doubly robust structure. Classical efficiency calculations include \citet{RobinsRotnitzkyZhao1994}, \citet{Hahn1998}, and \citet{HiranoImbensRidder2003}, with augmented and robust estimation developed further by \citet{BangRobins2005}. Modern high-dimensional versions combine orthogonal scores with flexible first-stage learning, as in \citet{Farrell2015}, \citet{AtheyImbensWager2018}, and \citet{ChernozhukovChetverikovDemirerDufloHansenNeweyRobins2018}; \citet{Kennedy2016} gives a broad review of semiparametric causal estimation. Those results identify regular-asymptotic regimes in which nuisance estimation error can be controlled through smoothness, sparsity, sample splitting, or orthogonality. Here the object is the finite-alphabet minimax risk in an unrestricted discrete model, where rare treatment-covariate cells create a non-smooth estimation problem.

Work on high-dimensional and structured semi-supervised inference provides a complementary perspective. \citet{ZhangBrownCai2019}, \citet{ChakraborttyCai2018}, and \citet{DengNingZhaoZhang2024} develop semi-supervised methods for means and regression targets in high-dimensional settings. Related robustness and prediction-assisted ideas appear in \citet{AngelopoulosBatesFannjiangJordanZrnic2023}. These papers show how auxiliary samples and predictive structure can improve estimation in rich covariate spaces. The present setting uses the finite alphabet itself as the primitive statistical object and characterizes the risk uniformly over the unrestricted cell-level causal model.

A related auxiliary-data literature studies how unlabeled samples, missing-outcome structure, transfer information, regression targets, quantile targets, and surrogate outcomes improve estimation. \citet{ChengAnanthakrishnanCai2021}, \citet[Sections 2--4]{HouMukherjeeCai2025}, \citet[Sections 3--5]{Kato2025}, \citet{ChakraborttyDai2024}, \citet{AnniwaerZhang2025}, and \citet{XuWittenShojaie2025} develop semi-supervised, transfer-style, or regular-asymptotic causal estimators, while \citet{ChakraborttyLuCaiLi2019}, \citet{ChakraborttyDaiCarroll2022}, and \citet{KallusMao2025} analyze missing-outcome, quantile, and surrogate-outcome settings. Hou, Mukherjee, and Cai establish root-\(n\) inference and efficiency for partially annotated treatment and response under structured nuisance estimation; Kato studies a two-sample unlabeled-covariate design through generalized Riesz regression and characterizes its efficiency bound. The present paper instead takes an unrestricted finite-cell model and asks for the global finite-sample minimax risk, including rare-cell regimes where regular asymptotics need not apply.

The discrete nature of the model also connects the analysis to minimax estimation of non-smooth functionals of distributions. For entropy, support-size, and related functionals, \citet{JiaoVenkatHanWeissman2015} and \citet{WuYang2016} show that polynomial approximation helps determine sharp rates, while \citet{ValiantValiant2017} and \citet{OrlitskySureshWu2016} develop sample-amplification and unseen-species perspectives on rare cells. The average treatment effect in the annotation experiment has an analogous cell-wise difficulty: estimating outcome means requires labeled observations, while estimating the treatment-covariate weights benefits from the larger treatment-covariate sample. The resulting minimax rate reflects that interaction between labeled outcome information and auxiliary marginal information.

The most direct benchmark is \citet[Theorems 1--2 and the discussion following Theorem 2]{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024}, who study the same unrestricted binary-outcome, discrete-covariate overlap class with only complete records. Their plug-in upper bound holds for all \(d\), whereas their minimax lower bound is stated for \(d\lesssim n\log n\); the two differ by a logarithmic factor in the alphabet-dependent term. The present paper closes that gap at the supervised endpoint and extends the problem to separate outcome-labeled and outcome-unlabeled samples. \Cref{tab:closest-comparisons} summarizes the sampling design, structural regime, target, and guarantee for the nearest comparisons.
