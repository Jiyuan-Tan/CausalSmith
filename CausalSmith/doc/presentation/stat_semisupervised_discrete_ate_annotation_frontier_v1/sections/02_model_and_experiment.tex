\section{Model and experiment}\label{sec:model}

Throughout the paper, \(x\) indexes a finite covariate cell, \(a\in\{0,1\}\) indexes a treatment arm, and sums over cells run over the known alphabet \([d]=\{1,\ldots,d\}\). Generic constants may depend on the overlap level \(\epsilon\) when this dependence is indicated, while sample-size dependence is displayed explicitly. Norms and asymptotic notation are used in their standard finite-dimensional sense, and all probability laws below live on finite measurable spaces unless stated otherwise.

The statistical object is an observed record with a finite covariate, a binary treatment, and a binary outcome. Writing \leanref{sym:X}{\ensuremath{X}} for the covariate, \leanref{sym:A}{\ensuremath{A}} for treatment, and \leanref{sym:Y}{\ensuremath{Y}} for the observed outcome, the complete record takes values in the following finite space.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_11}[Complete observed-record space]
For each natural number \(d\), define the complete observed-record space by
\[
\operatorname{Obs}(d)\coloneqq [d]\times\{0,1\}\times\{0,1\}.
\]
An element of \(\operatorname{Obs}(d)\) is a triple \((X,A,Y)\), where \(X\in[d]\) is the finite-alphabet covariate, \(A\in\{0,1\}\) is the binary treatment indicator, and \(Y\in\{0,1\}\) is the binary observed outcome.
\end{definitionv}

Let \leanref{sym:P}{\ensuremath{P}} denote a probability law on this observed record space. For a cell \(x\), the covariate mass \leanref{sym:p_x}{\ensuremath{p_x}} records how much population probability lies in that cell.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_16}[Covariate cell mass]
For an observed-data law \(P\) on \([d]\times\{0,1\}^2\) and a covariate value \(x\in[d]\), define the covariate cell mass
\[
p_x
=
\sum_{a\in\{0,1\}}\sum_{y\in\{0,1\}}
P(X=x,A=a,Y=y).
\]
\end{definitionv}

We also write \leanref{sym:s_{ax}}{\ensuremath{s_{ax}}} for the treatment-covariate mass in arm \(a\). The treatment propensity \leanref{sym:e_x}{\ensuremath{e_x}} is the conditional treatment probability in the cell, with the finite-space convention made explicit next.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_10}[Cell treatment propensity]
For an observed-data law \(P\) on \([d]\times\{0,1\}^2\) and a covariate cell \(x\in[d]\), define the treatment propensity \(e_x\) by
\[
e_x
:=
\frac{\sum_{y\in\{0,1\}} P(X=x,A=1,Y=y)}
{\sum_{a\in\{0,1\}}\sum_{y\in\{0,1\}} P(X=x,A=a,Y=y)}.
\]
The ratio is interpreted with the usual totalized real division convention, so it is \(0\) when the denominator is \(0\).
\end{definitionv}

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:overlap}[Occupied-cell overlap]
For every covariate cell \(x\in[d]\) with cell mass \(p_x>0\), the propensity score satisfies
\[
\epsilon \le e_x \le 1-\epsilon .
\]
\end{assumptionv}

The occupied-cell overlap condition in \cref{obj:ass:overlap} is the standard strong-overlap restriction on cells that occur with positive probability \citep{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024}. It keeps both treatment arms represented wherever the covariate distribution places mass and therefore gives the outcome regressions in the ATE functional a common observed-data interpretation.

The experiment combines complete outcome-labeled records with additional treatment-covariate records. We first record the sampling law for the labeled observations and then define the marginal law governing the auxiliary observations.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:labeled-iid}[Labeled sampling]
The labeled sample is distributed as
\[
\mathcal L_n \sim P^{\otimes n}.
\]
\end{assumptionv}

The labeled sampling condition in \cref{obj:ass:labeled-iid} is the standard independent and identically distributed sampling condition for complete records \citep{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024}. These observations supply the outcome information used to learn the arm-specific regressions.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_14}[Treatment-covariate marginal]
For any \(d\) and any observed-data law \(P\) on \([d]\times\{0,1\}^2\), the treatment-covariate marginal \(P_{XA}\) is the probability mass function on \([d]\times\{0,1\}\) obtained by pushing \(P\) forward under the projection \((x,a,y)\mapsto (x,a)\):
\[
P_{XA}
=
P\circ\bigl((x,a,y)\mapsto (x,a)\bigr)^{-1}.
\]
\end{definitionv}

The marginal \leanref{sym:P_{XA}}{\ensuremath{P_{XA}}} records the \(2d\) treatment-covariate cell probabilities while integrating out the outcome. The auxiliary sample \leanref{sym:\mathcal U_m}{\ensuremath{\mathcal U_m}} is drawn from this same population marginal.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:auxiliary-iid}[Auxiliary sampling]
The auxiliary sample is distributed as
\[
\mathcal U_m \sim P_{XA}^{\otimes m}.
\]
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:auxiliary-iid} is the standard same-population outcome-unlabeled sampling condition \citep{ChakraborttyDai2024}. It lets the auxiliary records refine information about the treatment-covariate distribution relevant for weighting cell-level outcome contrasts.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:sample-independence}[Sample independence]
The labeled and auxiliary samples are independent:
\[
\mathcal L_n \perp\!\!\!\perp \mathcal U_m .
\]
\end{assumptionv}

The independent two-sample design in \cref{obj:ass:sample-independence} is standard in semisupervised treatment-effect settings with separate labeled and outcome-unlabeled samples \citep{ChakraborttyDai2024}. Under this design, the product experiment separates outcome information from additional marginal information.

The causal interpretation uses a finite potential-outcome overlay on the same observed-data law. Let \leanref{sym:Y(a)}{\ensuremath{Y(a)}} denote the binary potential outcome under arm \(a\); the following assumptions connect the observed-data functional to the causal average treatment effect in the usual potential-outcome framework \citep{Rubin1974,ImbensRubin2015}.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:consistency}[Potential-outcome consistency]
The observed binary outcome equals the potential outcome under the realized treatment:
\[
Y = Y(A)
\]
almost surely.
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:consistency} is the standard consistency condition \citep{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024}. It identifies the observed outcome within each treatment arm with the corresponding potential outcome.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:conditional-exchangeability}[Conditional exchangeability]
The potential outcomes are conditionally independent of treatment given the finite covariate:
\[
(Y(0),Y(1)) \perp\!\!\!\perp A \mid X .
\]
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:conditional-exchangeability} is the standard conditional exchangeability condition \citep{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024}. Together with \cref{obj:ass:consistency,obj:ass:overlap}, it gives the observed conditional means their usual causal meaning within each finite covariate cell \citep{RosenbaumRubin1983,ImbensRubin2015}.

More explicitly, let \(Q\) be a full-data law for
\((X,A,Y(0),Y(1))\), and let \(P\) be its observed-data pushforward under
\[
(x,a,y_0,y_1)\longmapsto (x,a,y_a).
\]
Consistency and conditional exchangeability imply, for every occupied cell \(x\) and arm \(a\),
\[
\mathbb E_Q\{Y(a)\mid X=x\}
=
\mathbb E_Q\{Y(a)\mid X=x,A=a\}
=
\mathbb E_P\{Y\mid X=x,A=a\}.
\]
Overlap makes the last conditional mean identified in every occupied cell. Therefore
\[
\mathbb E_Q\{Y(1)-Y(0)\}
=
\sum_{x=1}^d p_x
\left[
\mathbb E_P\{Y\mid X=x,A=1\}
-
\mathbb E_P\{Y\mid X=x,A=0\}
\right],
\]
which is exactly the observed-data functional defined below. Thus the minimax analysis is over observed laws \(P\), while the causal interpretation is supplied by any compatible full-data law \(Q\) satisfying these assumptions.

We now collect the finite-space probability notation, the model class, and the target functional used in the risk analysis.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_13}[Probability laws on a measurable space]
For a measurable space \(\mathcal Z\), let \(\mathsf{Prob}(\mathcal Z)\) denote the set of all probability measures on \(\mathcal Z\). For the finite spaces used below, including \([d]\times\{0,1\}^2\) and \([d]\times\{0,1\}\), the measurable structure is the full power set.
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{def:model-class}[Model class \(\mathcal M_{d,\epsilon}\)]
For an overlap constant \(\epsilon\) and alphabet size \(d\), define
\[
\mathcal M_{d,\epsilon}
:=
\Bigl\{
P\in\mathsf{Prob}\bigl([d]\times\{0,1\}^2\bigr):
d\ge2,\ 0<\epsilon<1/2,\ \text{\(P\) satisfies \cref{obj:ass:overlap}}
\Bigr\}.
\]
\end{definitionv}

Thus \leanref{sym:\mathcal M_{d,\epsilon}}{\ensuremath{\mathcal M_{d,\epsilon}}} is the class of observed-data laws on the finite alphabet satisfying occupied-cell overlap at level \(\epsilon\). All suprema in the main risk statements range over this unrestricted cell-level model.

For each arm and cell, the outcome regression \leanref{sym:mu_{ax}}{\ensuremath{\mu_{ax}}} is the conditional success probability computed from the observed law.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_17}[Outcome regression]
For a law \(P\) on \([d]\times\{0,1\}^2\), a treatment arm \(a\in\{0,1\}\), and a covariate cell \(x\in[d]\), define
\[
\mu_{ax}(P)
:=
\frac{P(X=x,A=a,Y=1)}
{\sum_{y\in\{0,1\}} P(X=x,A=a,Y=y)} .
\]
The quotient is interpreted as total real division, so \(\mu_{ax}(P)=0\) when the treatment--covariate cell mass \(\sum_{y\in\{0,1\}}P(X=x,A=a,Y=y)\) is zero. In particular, \(\mu_{1x}(P)\) and \(\mu_{0x}(P)\) are obtained by taking \(a=1\) and \(a=0\), respectively.
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{def:ate-functional}[Average treatment effect functional]
For a finite alphabet size \(d\) and an observed-data law \(P\) on \([d]\times\{0,1\}^2\), define the average treatment effect functional by
\[
\tau(P):=\sum_{x=1}^{d}p_x(P)\bigl(\mu_{1x}(P)-\mu_{0x}(P)\bigr).
\]
Here \(p_x(P)\) is the covariate cell mass at \(x\), and \(\mu_{ax}(P)\) is the binary outcome regression in treatment arm \(a\) and covariate cell \(x\), totalized by division in the arm cell.
\end{definitionv}

The functional \leanref{sym:\tau(P)}{\ensuremath{\tau(P)}} averages the within-cell treated-control contrast using the population covariate distribution. Under the potential-outcome conditions above, it is the finite-alphabet causal average treatment effect.

Finally, the annotation experiment and minimax risk formalize the statistical problem studied in the rest of the paper.

\begin{definitionv}{def:annotation-experiment}[Annotation experiment \(\mathcal E_{n,m,d,\epsilon}\)]
Define the annotation experiment by
\[
\mathcal E_{n,m,d,\epsilon}
:=
\Bigl\{
P^{\otimes n}\otimes P_{XA}^{\otimes m}:
P\in\mathcal M_{d,\epsilon},\ n\ge1,\ m\ge0,\ d\ge2
\Bigr\}.
\]
The product law corresponds to \(n\) independent complete outcome-labeled records and \(m\) independent additional outcome-unlabeled treatment-covariate records, with independence between the two samples.
\end{definitionv}

\Cref{obj:def:annotation-experiment} defines \leanref{sym:\mathcal E_{n,m,d,\epsilon}}{\ensuremath{\mathcal E_{n,m,d,\epsilon}}}, the experiment in which outcome labels and treatment-covariate information enter through separate independent samples. This formulation makes the amount of outcome information and the amount of marginal information explicit.

The realized data live on the corresponding product sample space.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_18}[Joint sample space]
For natural numbers \(n,m,d\), the joint sample space is
\[
\mathrm{Sample}(n,m,d)
=
\bigl([n]\to [d]\times\{0,1\}\times\{0,1\}\bigr)
\times
\bigl([m]\to [d]\times\{0,1\}\bigr).
\]
Equivalently, an element consists of \(n\) complete records \((X,A,Y)\) and \(m\) outcome-unlabeled records \((X,A)\).
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{def:minimax-risk}[Minimax risk \(R_\epsilon(n,m,d)\)]
Define the minimax mean-squared risk by
\[
R_\epsilon(n,m,d)
:=
\inf_{T\in\mathcal T_{n,m,d}}
\sup_{P\in\mathcal M_{d,\epsilon}}
E_{P^{\otimes n}\otimes P_{XA}^{\otimes m}}
\!\left[\bigl(T-\tau(P)\bigr)^2\right],
\]
where \(\mathcal T_{n,m,d}\) is the class of estimators measurable with respect to the labeled sample and auxiliary sample.
\end{definitionv}

The risk \leanref{sym:R_\epsilon(n,m,d)}{\ensuremath{R_\epsilon(n,m,d)}} measures the best possible uniform mean-squared accuracy for estimating the ATE functional in \cref{obj:def:ate-functional} under the experiment in \cref{obj:def:annotation-experiment}. The estimator class \leanref{sym:\mathcal T_{n,m,d}}{\ensuremath{\mathcal T_{n,m,d}}} contains all measurable procedures based on the two samples, so the minimax risk isolates the statistical difficulty created by finite cells, overlap, and the split between outcome-labeled and outcome-unlabeled information.
