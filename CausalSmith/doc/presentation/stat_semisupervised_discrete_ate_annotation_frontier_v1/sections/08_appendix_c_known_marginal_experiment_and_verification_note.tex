\section{Known-marginal experiment and verification note}\label{sec:appendix-known}

The benchmark in \cref{obj:def:known-marginal-risk,obj:thm:known-marginal-boundary,obj:thm:known-marginal-limit} uses the same labeled experiment as the annotation problem, but supplies the treatment--covariate marginal as exact side information. This appendix records the corresponding experiment explicitly so that the comparison with the auxiliary-sample experiment in \cref{obj:def:annotation-experiment} has a fixed statistical object.

\begin{definitionv}{def:known-marginal-experiment}[Known-marginal experiment \(\mathcal E^{\mathrm{known}}_{n,d,\epsilon}\)]
For \(n\ge1\) and \(d\ge2\), the known-marginal experiment is
\[
\mathcal E^{\mathrm{known}}_{n,d,\epsilon}
:=
\{(P^{\otimes n},P_{XA}):P\in\mathcal M_{d,\epsilon}\}.
\]
The first coordinate generates \(\mathcal L_n\), and the second coordinate is the exact \(2d\)-cell table observed as deterministic side information.
\end{definitionv}

The deterministic table \leanref{sym:P_{XA}}{\ensuremath{P_{XA}}} contains the full joint distribution of treatment and covariates under the same observed-data law \(P\) used for the labeled sample. Thus the benchmark isolates the contribution of outcome labels after the treatment--covariate frequencies are known exactly. This is the population analogue of having unlimited annotation data for \((X,A)\), and it is the object compared with finite-\(m\) annotation in \cref{obj:thm:known-marginal-limit}.

The risk \leanref{sym:R_\epsilon^{\mathrm{known}}(n,d)}{\ensuremath{R_\epsilon^{\mathrm{known}}(n,d)}} in \cref{obj:def:known-marginal-risk} takes the infimum over \leanref{sym:\mathcal T^{\mathrm{known}}_{n,d}}{\ensuremath{\mathcal T^{\mathrm{known}}_{n,d}}}, the estimators measurable with respect to the labeled sample and this exact table. The boundary result in \cref{obj:thm:known-marginal-boundary} therefore describes the benchmark rate for the known-marginal problem, while \cref{obj:thm:known-marginal-limit} identifies the annotation regimes in which the finite auxiliary sample attains the same limiting benchmark. The construction is aligned with the finite-alphabet causal model in \cref{obj:def:model-class} and the average treatment effect functional in \cref{obj:def:ate-functional}; the overlap and sampling conditions remain those stated in \cref{obj:ass:overlap,obj:ass:labeled-iid}.

The next statements record the finite side-information comparison underlying that benchmark. The finite-cover statement is the compactness input that turns exact side information into finitely many stable decision rules; the causal specialization then yields convergence of empirical side samples to the exact-side benchmark; and the measurable comparison formalizes exact side information as at least as informative as an empirical side sample.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:finite-side-cover-approximate-decision}[Finite side cover]
Let \(\Theta\) be a nonempty compact topological space, let \(\mathsf Z\) be a finite set, let \(\mathsf C\) be a finite nonempty set, and let \(l\le u\). Suppose the following conditions hold.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Laws.)} For each \(\theta\in\Theta\), \(\Lambda_\theta\) is a probability vector on \(\mathsf Z\) and \(\Xi_\theta\) is a probability vector on \(\mathsf C\).
\item \textbf{(Target.)} For each \(\theta\in\Theta\), \(\upsilon_\theta\in[l,u]\).
\item \textbf{(Continuity.)} For every \(z\in\mathsf Z\) and \(c\in\mathsf C\), the maps \(\theta\mapsto\Lambda_\theta(z)\), \(\theta\mapsto\Xi_\theta(c)\), and \(\theta\mapsto\upsilon_\theta\) are continuous.
\end{itemize}
For probability vectors \(\nu,\nu'\) on \(\mathsf C\), set
\[
D_\infty(\nu,\nu')=\max_{c\in\mathsf C}|\nu(c)-\nu'(c)|.
\]
Define
\[
\mathfrak R_\star
=
\inf_{\psi}\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}
\sum_{z\in\mathsf Z}\Lambda_\theta(z)
\bigl(\psi(z,\Xi_\theta)-\upsilon_\theta\bigr)^2,
\]
where \(\psi\) ranges over all maps
\[
\psi:\mathsf Z\times\mathsf{Prob}(\mathsf C)\to[l,u].
\]
Then for every \(\delta>0\), there exist an integer \(k\ge1\), centers
\(\theta^\circ_1,\ldots,\theta^\circ_k\in\Theta\), decisions
\(\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_k:\mathsf Z\to[l,u]\), radii
\(\operatorname{rad}_1,\ldots,\operatorname{rad}_k\), and a margin \(\eta>0\) such that
\[
\eta<\operatorname{rad}_j
\]
for every \(j\), every \(\theta\in\Theta\) admits some \(j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\) with
\[
D_\infty(\Xi_\theta,\Xi_{\theta^\circ_j})<\operatorname{rad}_j-\eta,
\]
and, for every \(j\in\{1,\ldots,k\}\) and every \(\theta\in\Theta\),
\[
D_\infty(\Xi_\theta,\Xi_{\theta^\circ_j})<\operatorname{rad}_j
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\sum_{z\in\mathsf Z}\Lambda_\theta(z)
\bigl(\beta_j(z)-\upsilon_\theta\bigr)^2
\le
\mathfrak R_\star+\delta.
\]
\end{lemmav}

\Cref{obj:lem:finite-side-cover-approximate-decision} supplies the finite selection step used in the side-information limit. Compactness and continuity allow the exact-side decision problem to be represented, up to any tolerance \(\delta\), by finitely many neighborhoods of side laws. The positive margin \(\eta\) gives room for an empirical side table to select one of these neighborhoods uniformly once the auxiliary sample is large.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:finite-side-information-convergence}[Finite side-information convergence]
Let the overlap parameter \(\epsilon\) satisfy \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), let \(n\ge0\), and let \(d\ge2\).  Let
\[
\Theta_{\epsilon,d}
=
\Bigl\{(\pi,\varrho,\omega):
\pi\in\mathsf{Prob}([d]),\
\varrho_x\in[\epsilon,1-\epsilon],\
\omega_{\alpha x}\in[0,1]\ \text{for }x\in[d],\ \alpha\in\{0,1\}
\Bigr\}.
\]
For \(\theta=(\pi,\varrho,\omega)\in\Theta_{\epsilon,d}\), set
\[
P_\theta(x,\alpha,y)
=
\pi_x\varrho_x^\alpha(1-\varrho_x)^{1-\alpha}
\omega_{\alpha x}^y(1-\omega_{\alpha x})^{1-y},
\]
let \(\lambda_\theta=P_\theta^{\otimes n}\) be the labeled-sample law on \(([d]\times\{0,1\}^2)^n\), define
\[
\xi_\theta(x,\alpha)=\sum_{y\in\{0,1\}}P_\theta(x,\alpha,y),
\qquad
\upsilon_\theta=\sum_{x\in[d]}\pi_x(\omega_{1x}-\omega_{0x}).
\]
For each \(m\ge0\), define
\[
\mathfrak R_m
=
\inf_{\varphi:([d]\times\{0,1\}^2)^n\times([d]\times\{0,1\})^m\to[-1,1]}
\sup_{\theta\in\Theta_{\epsilon,d}}
\int
\bigl(\varphi(Z,U)-\upsilon_\theta\bigr)^2\,
d\bigl(\lambda_\theta\otimes\xi_\theta^{\otimes m}\bigr)(Z,U),
\]
and define
\[
\mathfrak R_\star
=
\inf_{\psi:([d]\times\{0,1\}^2)^n\times\mathsf{Prob}([d]\times\{0,1\})\to[-1,1]}
\sup_{\theta\in\Theta_{\epsilon,d}}
\int
\bigl(\psi(Z,\xi_\theta)-\upsilon_\theta\bigr)^2\,
d\lambda_\theta(Z).
\]
Then
\[
\lim_{m\to\infty}\mathfrak R_m=\mathfrak R_\star .
\]
\end{lemmav}

\Cref{obj:lem:finite-side-information-convergence} states the population-side-information approximation directly: as the number of auxiliary treatment--covariate records grows, the finite-sample minimax risk converges to the risk obtained when the treatment--covariate marginal is observed exactly. This supplies the finite-dimensional continuity step behind the known-marginal limit in \cref{obj:thm:known-marginal-limit} and keeps the benchmark comparison on the same labeled-sample scale as the annotation experiment.

\paragraph{Finite-cover selection input.}
The finite-cover mechanism in \cref{obj:lem:finite-side-cover-approximate-decision} applies to the discrete causal parametrization because the parameter set is compact and its labeled probabilities, side-law probabilities, and target are continuous functions of the cell probabilities. Concentration of the empirical side table then selects a valid neighborhood with probability tending uniformly to one, while \cref{obj:lem:finite-side-measurable-comparison} gives the complementary comparison from an empirical side sample to its exact probability vector.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:finite-side-measurable-comparison}[Empirical side comparison]
Let \(\Theta\) be a nonempty parameter set, let \(\mathcal Z\) and \(\mathcal C\) be nonempty finite sets, and let \(\beta_-\le \beta_+\) be real numbers. Suppose that, for each \(\theta\in\Theta\), the following objects are given:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Observed side law.)} A probability vector \(\lambda_\theta\) on \(\mathcal Z\).
\item \textbf{(Empirical side law.)} A probability vector \(\xi_\theta\) on \(\mathcal C\).
\item \textbf{(Target.)} A number \(\upsilon_\theta\in[\beta_-,\beta_+]\).
\end{itemize}
For each integer \(m\ge0\), define
\[
\mathfrak R_m
=
\inf_{\varphi:\mathcal Z\times\mathcal C^m\to[\beta_-,\beta_+]}
\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}
\sum_{z\in\mathcal Z}\lambda_\theta(z)
\sum_{v\in\mathcal C^m}
\left(\prod_{j=1}^m \xi_\theta(v_j)\right)
\bigl(\varphi(z,v)-\upsilon_\theta\bigr)^2 .
\]
Define also
\[
\widetilde{\mathfrak R}_\star
=
\inf_{\psi}
\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}
\sum_{z\in\mathcal Z}\lambda_\theta(z)
\bigl(\psi(z,\xi_\theta)-\upsilon_\theta\bigr)^2 ,
\]
where the infimum ranges over all maps
\[
\psi:\mathcal Z\times\mathbb R^{\mathcal C}\to[\beta_-,\beta_+]
\]
such that, for each \(z\in\mathcal Z\), the section \(w\mapsto\psi(z,w)\) is Borel measurable. Then, for every integer \(m\ge0\),
\[
\widetilde{\mathfrak R}_\star\le \mathfrak R_m .
\]
\end{lemmav}

\Cref{obj:lem:finite-side-measurable-comparison} gives the corresponding monotone comparison for finite spaces: replacing an empirical side sample by its exact probability vector weakly lowers the minimax risk. In the known-marginal application, this comparison expresses the benchmark as the risk associated with observing the treatment--covariate law itself, rather than a random finite sample from that law.

A complementary asymptotic comparison spells out when auxiliary annotation improves the rate in \cref{obj:def:frontier-rate-handle} relative to the labeled-only case. It expresses the improvement entirely through the alphabet growth and the effective treatment--covariate sample size.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:annotation-frontier-strict-improvement}[Strict frontier improvement]
Let \((m_n)_{n\ge 1}\) and \((d_n)_{n\ge 1}\) be sequences of nonnegative integers such that \(d_n\ge 2\) for every \(n\ge 1\). For each \(n\ge 1\), set \(N_n=n+m_n\) and \(\ell_n=\log(en)\), and let \(r_\epsilon(n,m,d)\) be the annotation-frontier rate in \cref{obj:def:frontier-rate-handle}. Then, as \(n\to\infty\),
\[
r_\epsilon(n,m_n,d_n)
=
o\!\left(r_\epsilon(n,0,d_n)\right)
\]
if and only if
\[
\frac{d_n}{\sqrt n\,\ell_n}\to\infty
\]
and
\[
\frac{d_n/(N_n\ell_n)}
{\min\{1,d_n/(n\ell_n)\}}
\to 0.
\]
\end{lemmav}

\Cref{obj:lem:annotation-frontier-strict-improvement} identifies the regimes in which the annotation rate is asymptotically smaller than the labeled-only rate. The first condition places the alphabet beyond the labeled-only parametric scale, so treatment--covariate information can affect the leading rate. The second condition requires the combined treatment--covariate sample size to reduce the alphabet-dependent term relative to its labeled-only analogue. Together these conditions give an exact rate-level criterion for when auxiliary annotation changes the asymptotic order delivered by \cref{obj:thm:sharp-annotation-frontier}.
