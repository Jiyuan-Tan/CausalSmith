Causal estimation of average treatment effects is rooted in the potential-outcomes and randomization framework of \citet{Neyman1923,Rubin1974}, with observational identification organized around adjustment and propensity scores in \citet{RosenbaumRubin1983} and the modern textbook synthesis of \citet{ImbensRubin2015}. Semiparametric efficiency theory for ATEs and missing-data functionals was developed through \citet{RobinsRotnitzkyZhao1994,Hahn1998,HiranoImbensRidder2003,BangRobins2005}; these papers characterize influence-function based estimation, inverse weighting, and doubly robust correction under regular asymptotic regimes. The present paper studies the same identified ATE functional in a finite-alphabet observational model with fixed overlap, binary treatment and outcome, and random outcome annotation, and asks for the finite-sample minimax mean-squared error when treatment-covariate information and outcome information arrive at different sample sizes.

High-dimensional causal-inference methods extend this semiparametric lineage through structural regularity. \citet{BelloniChernozhukovHansen2014} introduced post-double-selection inference for treatment effects under approximate sparsity, \citet{Farrell2015} developed uniformly valid doubly robust ATE inference after model selection with possibly more covariates than observations, \citet{AtheyImbensWager2018} combined balancing weights with regularized outcome adjustment for high-dimensional linear models, and \citet{ChernozhukovChetverikovDemirerDufloHansenNeweyRobins2018} formalized orthogonal-score and cross-fitting methods for root-\(n\) inference with machine-learned nuisances. The closest discrete-covariate benchmark is \citet{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024}: for supervised samples with high-dimensional discrete covariates, common regression, weighting, and doubly robust estimators have MSE order \(d^2/n^2+1/n\), the minimax lower bound has order \(d^2/(n^2\log^2 n)+1/n\), and additional structure such as effect homogeneity or known covariate distribution yields rates of order \(d/n^2+1/n\). The annotation-frontier result keeps the unrestricted discrete nuisance class and replaces the single supervised information budget by separate outcome-bearing and treatment-covariate budgets.

Semi-supervised and missing-outcome work supplies the empirical motivation and regular-asymptotic competitors. \citet{ZhangBrownCai2019} developed assumption-lean semi-supervised mean inference; \citet{ChakraborttyCai2018} gave efficient adaptive semi-supervised linear regression; \citet{DengNingZhaoZhang2024} characterized optimal and safe high-dimensional semi-supervised regression estimation; and \citet{AngelopoulosBatesFannjiangJordanZrnic2023} introduced prediction-powered inference for valid estimation with machine predictions and a labeled calibration sample. For treatment effects, \citet{ChengAnanthakrishnanCai2021} studied EHR ATE estimation with a small chart-reviewed outcome sample and surrogate features observed broadly, giving a robust imputation-based estimator that is doubly robust for consistency and locally semiparametrically efficient under an ideal semi-supervised model; their inflammatory-bowel-disease study is the motivating chart-review design. \citet{ChakraborttyDai2024} develops semi-supervised ATE and quantile-treatment-effect estimators that are root-\(n\) consistent and asymptotically normal when the propensity model is correctly specified and semiparametrically efficient when all nuisance functions are correctly specified. \citet{KallusMao2025} quantifies efficiency gains from surrogate outcomes with limited target-outcome data, \citet{HouMukherjeeCai2025} treats partially annotated treatment and response with efficient influence functions and a semi-supervised multiple-machine-learning estimator, and \citet{Kato2025} derives efficiency bounds and generalized-Riesz estimators for one-sample and two-sample semi-supervised treatment-effect designs. The present theorem gives a baseline-only discrete minimax benchmark for the same chart-review allocation question under unrestricted cellwise propensities and outcome regressions.

The minimax proof strategy connects to the non-smooth functional-estimation literature for large discrete distributions. \citet{ValiantValiant2017} developed sample-amplification estimators for unseen distributional structure; \citet{JiaoVenkatHanWeissman2015} gave a general minimax program for discrete-distribution functionals using polynomial approximation in non-smooth regions; \citet{WuYang2016} established sharp entropy rates via best polynomial approximation and moment-matching lower bounds; and \citet{OrlitskySureshWu2016} obtained optimal unseen-species prediction. The annotation-frontier theorem adapts this approximation-and-indistinguishability lineage to a causal functional whose outcome marks appear in \(n\) labeled records while cell masses and propensities are informed by \(n+m\) treatment-covariate records. The resulting rate separates the label-gated \(n^{-1}\) floor from the pooled treatment-mass approximation term \(d^2/\{(n+m)^2\log^2(en)\}\), covering the supervised endpoint, the finite-auxiliary transition, and the known-\(P_{XA}\) benchmark in a single experiment.
