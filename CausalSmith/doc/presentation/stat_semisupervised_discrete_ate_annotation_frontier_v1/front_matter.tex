\begin{abstract}
This paper characterizes the minimax value of outcome annotation in a finite-alphabet causal experiment. A sample of \(n\) complete records \((X,A,Y)\) is augmented by \(m\) additional records \((X,A)\) from the same population, where \(X\) takes \(d\) known values, \(A\) and \(Y\) are binary, and occupied covariate cells satisfy overlap at level \(\epsilon\). For the average treatment effect under consistency and conditional exchangeability, the minimax mean-squared risk \(R_\epsilon(n,m,d)\) satisfies, uniformly over the unrestricted cellwise model,
\[
R_\epsilon(n,m,d)\asymp_\epsilon
\min\left\{1,\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{(n+m)^2\log^2(en)}\right\}.
\]
The rate separates outcome information from treatment--covariate marginal information: the \(n^{-1}\) term is the labeled-outcome contribution, while the alphabet-dependent term is governed by the combined \(n+m\) treatment--covariate records. The upper bound is attained by a fully specified hybrid estimator that uses polynomial correction in light cells and stabilized inverse counts in heavy cells. The lower bound uses common-marginal priors whose induced treatment--covariate tables have the same distribution across hypotheses, so the auxiliary sample is aligned with the marginal-information component. The characterization yields exact consistency, parametric-rate, strict-improvement, binary-alphabet, inverse-count-baseline, and known-marginal-limit consequences for the annotation experiment.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Chart-reviewed outcomes are often the scarce resource in observational causal studies, while baseline covariates and treatment assignments are available for many more units. This paper studies the resulting allocation problem in a finite-alphabet causal model. There are \(n\) outcome-labeled records \((X,A,Y)\) and \(m\) additional outcome-unlabeled records \((X,A)\), drawn independently from the same population. The covariate \(X\) takes values in a known alphabet of size \(d\), treatment and outcome are binary, and every occupied covariate cell satisfies overlap at level \(\epsilon\). Under the usual potential-outcome consistency and conditional-exchangeability conditions, the target is the finite-alphabet average treatment effect \(\tau(P)\) in \cref{obj:def:ate-functional}.

The main result gives the finite-sample minimax frontier for this annotation experiment. For every \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), \cref{obj:thm:sharp-annotation-frontier} proves that the minimax mean-squared risk in \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk} obeys
\[
R_\epsilon(n,m,d)\asymp_\epsilon
\min\left\{1,\frac1n+\frac{d^2}{(n+m)^2\log^2(en)}\right\}
\]
for all \(n\ge1\), \(m\ge0\), and \(d\ge2\). The rate has two resources. Outcome marks enter through the \(n^{-1}\) term. Treatment--covariate frequencies enter through the second term, where the relevant sample size is the pooled marginal-information size \(n+m\). Thus the theorem gives a quantitative rule for how additional treatment--covariate records change the attainable accuracy of average-treatment-effect estimation in the unrestricted finite-cell model.

The upper bound is constructive. The estimator in \cref{obj:def:mixed-estimator-handle} splits the labeled sample into outcome, pilot, and factorial pools, splits the auxiliary sample into pilot and factorial pools, and then combines the treatment--covariate records for marginal estimation. Cells with small pilot counts use a Chebyshev polynomial correction and a factorial-moment lift, following the polynomial-approximation ideas used in sharp discrete functional estimation \citep{JiaoVenkatHanWeissman2015,WuYang2016} and the rare-cell perspective developed in sample amplification and unseen-species prediction \citep{ValiantValiant2017,OrlitskySureshWu2016}. Cells with larger pilot counts use stabilized inverse-count weights. \Cref{obj:thm:uniform-mixed-upper} shows that this total, finitely calibrated estimator attains the frontier rate uniformly over the model class.

The matching lower bound uses a common-marginal construction. \Cref{obj:thm:common-marginal-converse} supplies priors whose induced random treatment--covariate marginal \(P_{XA}\), the full \(2d\)-cell table, has the same distribution across the two hypotheses, while the outcome regressions shift the target. This aligns the converse with the two-sample annotation design: auxiliary observations reveal the same random marginal object under both branches, and the remaining testing difficulty produces the label floor and the rare-cell approximation term. A separate parametric label floor in \cref{obj:lem:parametric-label-floor} supplies the \(n^{-1}\) component.

The frontier connects several familiar regimes. At \(m=0\), the rate becomes \(\min\{1,n^{-1}+d^2/(n^2\log^2(en))\}\). This refines the bracket of \citet[Theorems 1--2 and the discussion following Theorem 2]{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024}: their plug-in upper bound \(d^2/n^2+1/n\) holds over the unrestricted binary-outcome, discrete-covariate overlap class, while their minimax lower bound \(d^2/\{n^2\log^2 n\}+1/n\) is stated for \(d\lesssim n\log n\); they leave closing the logarithmic gap by a polynomial estimator as future work. The present result supplies that construction and, with auxiliary records, replaces the marginal-information sample size \(n\) by \(n+m\). With fixed \(n\) and \(d\), \cref{obj:thm:known-marginal-limit} shows that \(R_\epsilon(n,m,d)\) converges as \(m\to\infty\) to the known-marginal risk in \cref{obj:def:known-marginal-risk}, whose order is \(n^{-1}\) by \cref{obj:thm:known-marginal-boundary}. For sequences, \cref{obj:thm:sharp-annotation-frontier} states that consistency is equivalent to \(d_n/\{(n+m_n)\log(en)\}\to0\), and that parametric risk is equivalent to \(d_n=O((n+m_n)\log(en)/\sqrt n)\). The same theorem gives the exact criterion under which auxiliary treatment--covariate records improve the asymptotic order relative to the labeled-only endpoint.

The paper contributes to causal estimation under potential outcomes \citep{Rubin1974,RosenbaumRubin1983,ImbensRubin2015}, semiparametric and doubly robust average-treatment-effect estimation \citep{RobinsRotnitzkyZhao1994,Hahn1998,HiranoImbensRidder2003,BangRobins2005,Farrell2015,AtheyImbensWager2018,ChernozhukovChetverikovDemirerDufloHansenNeweyRobins2018,Kennedy2016}, and auxiliary-data methods for causal and distributional estimation, including semi-supervised treatment-effect, surrogate-outcome, missing-outcome, regression, and quantile settings \citep{ChengAnanthakrishnanCai2021,ChakraborttyLuCaiLi2019,ChakraborttyDai2024,ChakraborttyDaiCarroll2022,KallusMao2025,AnniwaerZhang2025,XuWittenShojaie2025}. Those literatures characterize identification, regular asymptotics, efficiency, and gains from structure or auxiliary measurements. The present analysis gives a finite-alphabet minimax benchmark for the binary-treatment, binary-outcome chart-review design under independent random outcome labeling, occupied-cell overlap, and unrestricted cellwise propensities and outcome regressions.
