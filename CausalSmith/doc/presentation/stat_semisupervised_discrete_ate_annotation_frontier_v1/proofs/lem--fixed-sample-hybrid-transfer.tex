\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:fixed-sample-hybrid-transfer}]
Take \(C=384\).  Fix \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), \(n\ge1\), \(m\ge0\), \(d\ge2\), and \(P\in\mathcal M_{d,\epsilon}\).  The estimator is measurable because it is either the constant zero map or, on the calibrated branch of \cref{obj:def:mixed-estimator-handle}, a finite linear combination of measurable prefix-count statistics.  It remains to prove the displayed risk bound. % lean: CausalSmith.Stat.SemisupervisedDiscreteAteAnnotationFrontier.measurable_mixedEstimator

1. Suppose first that \(\mathsf C_\epsilon(n,m)\) holds.  Put
\[
M_0=\lfloor n/3\rfloor,\qquad
M_p=n_p+m_p,\qquad
M_f=n_f+m_f,
\]
and
\[
u=\frac{M_0}{8},\qquad t_p=\frac{M_p}{8},\qquad t=\frac{M_f}{8}.
\]
Let \(\mathbb P_3\) be the three-pool product law whose complete-record coordinate has law \(P\) on
\([d]\times\{0,1\}\times\{0,1\}\), while the two shared coordinates have law \(P_{XA}\) on
\([d]\times\{0,1\}\).  Let \(\boldsymbol\lambda=(u,t_p,t)\) and let the capacity vector be
\(\boldsymbol M=(M_0,M_p,M_f)\).  For a three-prefix array
\[
\mathfrak s=(\mathfrak s_0,\mathfrak s_p,\mathfrak s_f),
\]
define
\[
\Psi_{n,m,d,\epsilon}(\mathfrak s)
=
Z^{\mathrm{mix}}_{n,m,\epsilon,u,t}
\bigl(\operatorname{hist}(\mathfrak s_0),
      \operatorname{hist}(\mathfrak s_p),
      \operatorname{hist}(\mathfrak s_f)\bigr),
\]
where the first histogram records complete outcome-labeled observations and the last two record treatment-covariate observations.  This prefix statistic is measurable and satisfies
\[
-1\le \Psi_{n,m,d,\epsilon}(\mathfrak s)\le 1
\quad\text{for every }\mathfrak s,
\qquad
-1\le \tau(P)\le 1,
\]
the first bound coming from clipping in \cref{obj:def:mixed-estimator-handle} and the second from \cref{obj:def:ate-functional} together with occupied-cell overlap in \cref{obj:def:model-class}. % lean: CausalSmith.Stat.SemisupervisedDiscreteAteAnnotationFrontier.finitePrefixHybridStatistic_mem_Icc; CausalSmith.Stat.SemisupervisedDiscreteAteAnnotationFrontier.ateFunctional_mem_Icc_neg_one_one

2. For fixed pools
\[
\mathfrak p=(\mathfrak p_0,\mathfrak p_p,\mathfrak p_f)
\in
\bigl([d]\times\{0,1\}^2\bigr)^{M_0}
\times
\bigl([d]\times\{0,1\}\bigr)^{M_p}
\times
\bigl([d]\times\{0,1\}\bigr)^{M_f},
\]
define the Rao--Blackwellized prefix statistic by
\[
\operatorname{RB}(\mathfrak p)
=
\sum_{r_0,r_p,r_f\ge0}
\Pr\{\operatorname{Poi}(u)=r_0\}
\Pr\{\operatorname{Poi}(t_p)=r_p\}
\Pr\{\operatorname{Poi}(t)=r_f\}
\,\Psi^\circ(\mathfrak p;r_0,r_p,r_f),
\]
where
\[
\Psi^\circ(\mathfrak p;r_0,r_p,r_f)
=
\begin{cases}
\Psi_{n,m,d,\epsilon}
\bigl(\mathfrak p_0[1{:}r_0],
      \mathfrak p_p[1{:}r_p],
      \mathfrak p_f[1{:}r_f]\bigr),
& r_0\le M_0,\ r_p\le M_p,\ r_f\le M_f,\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\]
Under \(\mathsf C_\epsilon(n,m)\), expanding the finite sums in \cref{obj:def:mixed-estimator-handle} gives the pointwise identity
\[
\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{mix}}_{n,m,d}(\mathcal L_n,\mathcal U_m)
=
\operatorname{RB}\bigl(\mathfrak P(\mathcal L_n,\mathcal U_m)\bigr),
\]
where \(\mathfrak P(\mathcal L_n,\mathcal U_m)\) is the deterministic three-pool split formed from the first \(M_0\) complete labeled records, the next \(n_p\) labeled records projected to \((X,A)\) together with the first \(m_p\) auxiliary records, and the remaining \(n_f\) projected labeled records together with the remaining \(m_f\) auxiliary records.  By \cref{obj:def:annotation-experiment}, this split has law equal to the fixed-pool law with coordinate law \(\mathbb P_3\) and capacities \(\boldsymbol M\).  Hence
\[
\mathbb E\!\left[
\left\{\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{mix}}_{n,m,d}-\tau(P)\right\}^2
\right]
=
\int
\bigl\{\operatorname{RB}(\mathfrak p)-\tau(P)\bigr\}^2
\,d\mathbb P_{\mathrm{fix}}(\mathfrak p).
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.SemisupervisedDiscreteAteAnnotationFrontier.prefixRaoBlackwellStatistic_hybridFixedPools; CausalSmith.Stat.SemisupervisedDiscreteAteAnnotationFrontier.hybridFixedPools_map_annotationLaw

3. The finite-pool-to-Poisson comparison used here is the following.  For any prefix statistic with values in an interval \([a,b]\), any target and overflow value in \([a,b]\), and independent Poisson requested lengths with means \(\boldsymbol\lambda\),
\[
\int
\bigl\{\operatorname{RB}(\mathfrak p)-\theta\bigr\}^2
\,d\mathbb P_{\mathrm{fix}}(\mathfrak p)
\le
\int
\bigl\{\Psi(\mathfrak s)-\theta\bigr\}^2
\,d\mathbb P_{\mathrm{Pois}}(\mathfrak s)
+
(b-a)^2
\sum_{\iota\in\{0,p,f\}}
\Pr\{\operatorname{Poi}(\lambda_\iota)>M_\iota\}.
\]
Indeed, \(\operatorname{RB}\) is the conditional expectation of the capped randomized-prefix statistic given the fixed pools, so Jensen's inequality bounds its squared risk by the capped randomized-prefix risk.  Off the overflow event the capped and uncapped prefixes agree; on overflow both the capped value and the target lie in \([a,b]\), so the extra squared loss is bounded by \((b-a)^2\) times the overflow probability.  Applying this with \(a=-1\), \(b=1\), \(\theta=\tau(P)\), overflow value \(0\), and \(\Psi=\Psi_{n,m,d,\epsilon}\) gives
\[
\int
\bigl\{\operatorname{RB}(\mathfrak p)-\tau(P)\bigr\}^2
\,d\mathbb P_{\mathrm{fix}}(\mathfrak p)
\le
\int
\bigl\{\Psi_{n,m,d,\epsilon}(\mathfrak s)-\tau(P)\bigr\}^2
\,d\mathbb P_{\mathrm{Pois}}(\mathfrak s)
+
4\bigl[
\pi_0+\pi_p+\pi_f
\bigr],
\]
where
\[
\pi_0=\Pr\{\operatorname{Poi}(u)>M_0\},\qquad
\pi_p=\Pr\{\operatorname{Poi}(t_p)>M_p\},\qquad
\pi_f=\Pr\{\operatorname{Poi}(t)>M_f\}.
\]
The image of the independent Poisson-prefix law under the three histograms is exactly
\(\mathbb P^{\mathrm{Pois}}_{P,u,t_p,t}\), and therefore
\[
\int
\bigl\{\Psi_{n,m,d,\epsilon}(\mathfrak s)-\tau(P)\bigr\}^2
\,d\mathbb P_{\mathrm{Pois}}(\mathfrak s)
=
\mathcal R^{\mathrm{Pois}}_{\mathrm{hyb}}(P;n,m,\epsilon)
\]
on the calibrated branch. % lean: Causalean.Stat.FiniteRaoBlackwell.IndependentPoissonPrefix.FiniteFamily.finiteAlphabet_threePoolSharedLaw_risk_le; CausalSmith.Stat.SemisupervisedDiscreteAteAnnotationFrontier.hybridPrefixCounts_map_eq_hybridPoissonCountLaw

4. It remains to bound the three overflow probabilities.  The calibration predicate gives \(24\le n\).  From the definitions of the integer blocks,
\[
n\le4M_0,\qquad n\le4M_p,\qquad n\le4M_f,
\]
and all three capacities are positive.  For any positive integer \(M_\bullet\) satisfying \(n\le4M_\bullet\),
\[
\Pr\{\operatorname{Poi}(M_\bullet/8)>M_\bullet\}
\le
\frac{8}{M_\bullet}
\le
\frac{32}{n}.
\]
To prove the first inequality, let \(X_\bullet\sim\operatorname{Poi}(M_\bullet/8)\).  The overflow event is contained in the self-normalized Bernstein event at parameter \(M_\bullet/4\), because
\[
\sqrt{2\cdot\frac{M_\bullet}{8}\cdot\frac{M_\bullet}{4}}
=\frac{M_\bullet}{4}
\]
and \(X_\bullet>M_\bullet\) implies
\[
X_\bullet-\frac{M_\bullet}{8}
>\frac{7M_\bullet}{8}
>\frac{M_\bullet}{4}+2\cdot\frac{M_\bullet}{4}.
\]
The Poisson Bernstein bound for this larger event gives
\[
\Pr\{\operatorname{Poi}(M_\bullet/8)>M_\bullet\}
\le
\exp(-M_\bullet/4)
=
\frac1{\exp(M_\bullet/4)}
\le
\frac{4}{M_\bullet}
\le
\frac{8}{M_\bullet},
\]
where \(1+M_\bullet/4\le \exp(M_\bullet/4)\) gives the penultimate inequality.
Consequently
\[
\pi_0+\pi_p+\pi_f\le \frac{96}{n},
\]
and the preceding display yields
\[
\mathbb E\!\left[
\left\{\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{mix}}_{n,m,d}-\tau(P)\right\}^2
\right]
\le
\mathcal R^{\mathrm{Pois}}_{\mathrm{hyb}}(P;n,m,\epsilon)
+\frac{384}{n}.
\]
Since \(\mathcal R^{\mathrm{Pois}}_{\mathrm{hyb}}(P;n,m,\epsilon)\ge0\), this is at most
\[
2\,\mathcal R^{\mathrm{Pois}}_{\mathrm{hyb}}(P;n,m,\epsilon)
+\frac{384}{n}.
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.SemisupervisedDiscreteAteAnnotationFrontier.poisson_eighth_tail; CausalSmith.Stat.SemisupervisedDiscreteAteAnnotationFrontier.fixed_sample_hybrid_transfer

5. If \(\mathsf C_\epsilon(n,m)\) fails, then \cref{obj:def:mixed-estimator-handle} sets
\(\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{mix}}_{n,m,d}=0\), while the definition of the hybrid Poisson risk gives
\[
\mathcal R^{\mathrm{Pois}}_{\mathrm{hyb}}(P;n,m,\epsilon)=\tau(P)^2.
\]
Because the annotation law is a probability measure,
\[
\mathbb E\!\left[
\left\{\widehat\tau^{\mathrm{mix}}_{n,m,d}-\tau(P)\right\}^2
\right]
=
\tau(P)^2
\le
2\tau(P)^2+\frac{384}{n}
=
2\,\mathcal R^{\mathrm{Pois}}_{\mathrm{hyb}}(P;n,m,\epsilon)+\frac{384}{n},
\]
using \(n\ge1\).  The two cases prove the asserted bound with the universal constant \(C=384\). % lean: CausalSmith.Stat.SemisupervisedDiscreteAteAnnotationFrontier.fixed_sample_hybrid_transfer
\end{proof}
