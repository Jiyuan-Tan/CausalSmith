\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:poisson-inverse-arm-mean-formula}]
1.  Put \(\bar a:=1-a\) and write
\[
\Lambda_a:=u q_{ax},\qquad
\Theta_a:=t s_{ax},\qquad
\Theta_{\bar a}:=t s_{\bar a x}.
\]
The cell probabilities are nonnegative, so \(s_{ax}\ge0\), \(s_{\bar a x}\ge0\), and \(q_{ax}\ge0\). Since \(u,t>0\), the nonnegative rates above are the real values of the corresponding Poisson intensities. Also,
\[
s_{ax}+s_{\bar a x}=p_x,
\]
because the two treatment arms partition the covariate cell \(x\). % lean: CausalSmith.Stat.SemisupervisedDiscreteAteAnnotationFrontier.poisson_inverse_arm_mean_formula

2.  Define
\[
\Omega_a:=
\mathbb E\!\left[
1+\frac{K_{\bar a x}}{K_{ax}+1}
\right],
\qquad
K_{ax}\sim\operatorname{Poi}(\Theta_a),\quad
K_{\bar a x}\sim\operatorname{Poi}(\Theta_{\bar a}),
\]
with the two counts independent. The product-Poisson identity used for this expectation is
\[
\Theta_a\Omega_a
=
\Theta_a+\Theta_{\bar a}\bigl(1-e^{-\Theta_a}\bigr).
\]
Indeed, for \(r(k):=(k+1)^{-1}\),
\[
\Omega_a
=
1+
\int v\,r(k)\,
d\bigl(\operatorname{Poi}(\Theta_a)\otimes\operatorname{Poi}(\Theta_{\bar a})\bigr)(k,v).
\]
Fubini factorizes the cross term as
\[
\int v\,r(k)\,d(\operatorname{Poi}(\Theta_a)\otimes\operatorname{Poi}(\Theta_{\bar a}))
=
\left(\int r(k)\,d\operatorname{Poi}(\Theta_a)(k)\right)
\left(\int v\,d\operatorname{Poi}(\Theta_{\bar a})(v)\right).
\]
The Poisson first moment gives
\[
\int v\,d\operatorname{Poi}(\Theta_{\bar a})(v)=\Theta_{\bar a},
\]
and the shifted reciprocal identity gives
\[
\Theta_a\int \frac{1}{k+1}\,d\operatorname{Poi}(\Theta_a)(k)
=
1-e^{-\Theta_a}.
\]
Multiplying the displayed formula for \(\Omega_a\) by \(\Theta_a\) yields the claimed identity. % lean: CausalSmith.Stat.SemisupervisedDiscreteAteAnnotationFrontier.poisson_inverse_weight_mean_mul_rate

3.  Substituting \(\Theta_a=t s_{ax}\), \(\Theta_{\bar a}=t s_{\bar a x}\), and \(s_{\bar a x}=p_x-s_{ax}\), the preceding display becomes
\[
(t s_{ax})\Omega_a
=
t s_{ax}
+
t(p_x-s_{ax})\bigl(1-e^{-t s_{ax}}\bigr).
\]
Because \(t>0\), cancellation of \(t\) gives
\[
s_{ax}\Omega_a
=
s_{ax}
+
(p_x-s_{ax})\bigl(1-e^{-t s_{ax}}\bigr).
\]
Multiplying by \(\mu_{ax}\) and rearranging,
\[
(s_{ax}\mu_{ax})\Omega_a
=
p_x\mu_{ax}
-
(p_x-s_{ax})\mu_{ax}e^{-t s_{ax}}.
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.SemisupervisedDiscreteAteAnnotationFrontier.poisson_inverse_arm_expectation_scalar

4.  Finally,
\[
q_{ax}=s_{ax}\mu_{ax}.
\]
This is immediate from \(\mu_{ax}=q_{ax}/s_{ax}\) when \(s_{ax}\ne0\). When \(s_{ax}=0\), the bound \(0\le q_{ax}\le s_{ax}\), obtained by summing the nonnegative outcome masses in the arm cell, gives \(q_{ax}=0\), and the same identity holds under the totalized division convention. Hence, since the normalized outcome count has mean \(q_{ax}\),
\[
q_{ax}\,
\mathbb E\!\left[1+\frac{K_{\bar a x}}{K_{ax}+1}\right]
=
(s_{ax}\mu_{ax})\Omega_a
=
p_x\mu_{ax}
-
(p_x-s_{ax})\mu_{ax}e^{-t s_{ax}},
\]
as claimed. % lean: CausalSmith.Stat.SemisupervisedDiscreteAteAnnotationFrontier.markedMass_eq_armMass_mul_outcomeMean
\end{proof}
