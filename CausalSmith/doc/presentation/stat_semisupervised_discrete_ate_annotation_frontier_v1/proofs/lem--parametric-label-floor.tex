\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:parametric-label-floor}]
Set \(c=1/100\). Fix \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), \(n\ge1\), \(m\ge0\), and \(d\ge2\). Define
\[
\eta_n:=\frac{2}{5\sqrt n}.
\]
Then \(\eta_n\ge0\), and
\[
\eta_n^2=\frac{4}{25n}.
\]
Consider two laws \(P_{\mathrm{null}}\) and \(P_{\mathrm{pert}}\) in \(\mathcal M_{d,\epsilon}\) with uniform covariate distribution on \([d]\), propensity \(e_x=1/2\) in every cell, control regression \(1/2\) in every cell, and treated regression respectively \(1/2\) and \(1/2+\eta_n\). Since \(n\ge1\), \(\eta_n\le2/5\), hence \(1/2+\eta_n\le1\). The overlap condition follows from \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), so both laws belong to \(\mathcal M_{d,\epsilon}\). % lean: CausalSmith.Stat.SemisupervisedDiscreteAteAnnotationFrontier.parametric_label_floor

For the labeled samples, the parametric total-variation bound applies once
\[
n\cdot \frac{(1/2)\eta_n^2}{(1/2)(1-1/2)}\le \log 2 .
\]
Using the displayed value of \(\eta_n^2\), the left side is \(8/25\), and \(8/25\le \log 2\). Therefore
\[
\operatorname{TV}\!\left(P_{\mathrm{null}}^{\otimes n},
P_{\mathrm{pert}}^{\otimes n}\right)\le \frac12 .
\]
% lean: Causalean.Estimation.MinimaxATE.Parametric.tvDist_productLaw_le_half

The auxiliary treatment-covariate marginals of the two laws are equal:
\[
(P_{\mathrm{null}})_{XA}=(P_{\mathrm{pert}})_{XA}.
\]
Indeed, both laws have the same uniform covariate distribution and the same propensity \(e_x=1/2\) in every cell; summing over the binary outcome coordinate gives the same mass for each \((x,a)\). Hence their \(m\)-fold auxiliary product laws are identical. The product total-variation inequality gives
\[
\operatorname{TV}\!\left(
P_{\mathrm{null}}^{\otimes n}\otimes (P_{\mathrm{null}})_{XA}^{\otimes m},
P_{\mathrm{pert}}^{\otimes n}\otimes (P_{\mathrm{pert}})_{XA}^{\otimes m}
\right)
\le
\operatorname{TV}\!\left(P_{\mathrm{null}}^{\otimes n},P_{\mathrm{pert}}^{\otimes n}\right)+0
\le \frac12 .
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.SemisupervisedDiscreteAteAnnotationFrontier.parametricFloor_auxMarginal_eq

The two average treatment effects are
\[
\tau(P_{\mathrm{null}})=0,
\qquad
\tau(P_{\mathrm{pert}})=\eta_n.
\]
The first identity follows because the two outcome regressions coincide in every cell, and the second because every cell has treated-minus-control contrast \(\eta_n\) and the cell masses sum to one. Thus, with \(s_n:=\eta_n/2\),
\[
2s_n=\eta_n
\le
\bigl|\tau(P_{\mathrm{null}})-\tau(P_{\mathrm{pert}})\bigr|.
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.SemisupervisedDiscreteAteAnnotationFrontier.parametricFloorPertLaw_ate

We now record the two-point risk consequence used for \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk}. Let \(T\in\mathcal T_{n,m,d}\) be any estimator, and let
\[
\mathsf A_0:=\{z:s_n\le |T(z)-\tau(P_{\mathrm{null}})|\},
\qquad
\mathsf A_1:=\{z:s_n\le |T(z)-\tau(P_{\mathrm{pert}})|\}.
\]
The testing bound at separation \(2s_n\), together with the preceding total-variation bound, yields
\[
\max\Bigl\{
\Pr_{P_{\mathrm{null}}^{\otimes n}\otimes(P_{\mathrm{null}})_{XA}^{\otimes m}}(\mathsf A_0),
\Pr_{P_{\mathrm{pert}}^{\otimes n}\otimes(P_{\mathrm{pert}})_{XA}^{\otimes m}}(\mathsf A_1)
\Bigr\}
\ge \frac14 .
\]
Moreover, by the elementary inequality
\[
s_n^2\,\Pr\{s_n\le |T-\theta|\}
\le
\mathbb E[(T-\theta)^2],
\]
applied under each of the two laws, the larger of the two mean-squared risks is at least \(s_n^2/4\). Taking the supremum over \(P\in\mathcal M_{d,\epsilon}\) and then the infimum over \(T\in\mathcal T_{n,m,d}\) gives
\[
\frac{s_n^2}{4}\le R_\epsilon(n,m,d).
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.SemisupervisedDiscreteAteAnnotationFrontier.annotationMinimaxRisk_ge_two_model_tv

Finally,
\[
\frac{s_n^2}{4}
=
\frac{(\eta_n/2)^2}{4}
=
\frac{1}{100\,n}.
\]
Consequently \(R_\epsilon(n,m,d)\ge 1/(100n)\), which proves the claim with the universal constant \(c=1/100\). % lean: CausalSmith.Stat.SemisupervisedDiscreteAteAnnotationFrontier.parametric_label_floor
\end{proof}
