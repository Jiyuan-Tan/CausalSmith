\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:poisson-prefix-count-law}]
Let \(\Omega_{\mathrm{obs}}=[d]\times\{0,1\}^2\) and
\(\Omega_{\mathrm{aux}}=[d]\times\{0,1\}\).  For a finite prefix
\(\xi=(z_1,\ldots,z_r)\) in a finite space \(\Omega\), write
\[
H_\xi(z)=\#\{i:z_i=z\},\qquad z\in\Omega .
\]
The elementary Poisson splitting identity says that if \(R\sim\operatorname{Poi}(\lambda)\)
and, conditionally on \(R\), \(z_1,\ldots,z_R\) are i.i.d. with law \(\nu\) on
\(\Omega\), then
\[
(H_\xi(z))_{z\in\Omega}\sim \bigotimes_{z\in\Omega}\operatorname{Poi}(\lambda\nu\{z\}).
\]
Indeed, for any nonnegative integers \((r_z)_{z\in\Omega}\), with
\(r_\bullet=\sum_z r_z\),
\[
\Pr\{H_\xi(z)=r_z\ \forall z\}
=e^{-\lambda}\frac{\lambda^{r_\bullet}}{r_\bullet!}
\frac{r_\bullet!}{\prod_z r_z!}\prod_z \nu\{z\}^{r_z}
=\prod_z e^{-\lambda\nu\{z\}}\frac{(\lambda\nu\{z\})^{r_z}}{r_z!}.
\]
Applied to the outcome-marked prefix with intensity \(u\) and the treatment--covariate
prefix with intensity \(t\), and using the independence of the two prefixes, this gives
\[
(H^{\mathrm{obs}}_z)_{z\in\Omega_{\mathrm{obs}}}
\sim
\bigotimes_{z\in\Omega_{\mathrm{obs}}}\operatorname{Poi}(uP\{z\}),
\qquad
(H^{\mathrm{aux}}_w)_{w\in\Omega_{\mathrm{aux}}}
\sim
\bigotimes_{w\in\Omega_{\mathrm{aux}}}\operatorname{Poi}(tP_{XA}\{w\}),
\]
with the two histogram arrays independent.  Mapping the first histogram to
\[
E(H^{\mathrm{obs}})(x,a)=H^{\mathrm{obs}}(x,a,1)
\]
selects one coordinate for each \((x,a)\).  Since the selected observed coordinates are
distinct, and the auxiliary histogram is independent of the observed histogram, the
array
\[
\bigl(E(H^{\mathrm{obs}})(x,a),H^{\mathrm{aux}}(x,a)\bigr)_{x\in[d],\,a\in\{0,1\}}
\]
has law
\[
\bigotimes_{x\in[d]}\bigotimes_{a\in\{0,1\}}
\left\{\operatorname{Poi}\bigl(uP\{(x,a,1)\}\bigr)
\otimes
\operatorname{Poi}\bigl(tP_{XA}\{(x,a)\}\bigr)\right\}.
\]
This array is exactly \((S_{ax},K_{ax})_{x,a}\).  In the degenerate case
\([d]=\varnothing\), the same display is the empty product measure on the one-point
array space. % lean: finitePrefixCounts_map_independentPoissonPrefixLaw

It remains to identify the two singleton masses in the displayed product.  By the
definition of \(q_{ax}\),
\[
P\{(x,a,1)\}=P(x,a,1)=q_{ax}.
\]
Hence the first Poisson intensity is \(u q_{ax}\). % lean: finitePrefixCounts_map_eq_poissonCountLaw

For the auxiliary marginal, \(P_{XA}\) is the pushforward of \(P\) under
\((x,a,y)\mapsto(x,a)\).  Therefore
\[
P_{XA}\{(x,a)\}
=\sum_{y\in\{0,1\}}P(x,a,y)
=P(x,a,0)+P(x,a,1)
=s_{ax}.
\]
Thus the second Poisson intensity is \(t s_{ax}\).  Since \(u,t\ge0\), these products
are the corresponding nonnegative Poisson parameters, and substituting the two
identities into the preceding product law yields
\[
\bigotimes_{x\in[d]}\bigotimes_{a\in\{0,1\}}
\left\{\operatorname{Poi}(u q_{ax})\otimes
\operatorname{Poi}(t s_{ax})\right\}.
\]
The product representation is equivalent to mutual independence of all coordinates
and to the stated marginal laws for every \(x\) and \(a\). % lean: finitePrefixCounts_map_eq_poissonCountLaw
\end{proof}
