Modern optimal-treatment-value analysis combines causal identification, statistical decision theory, and individualized treatment-rule methodology. Under consistency, conditional exchangeability, and positivity, the g-formula tradition of \cite{Robins1986}, the propensity-score formulation of \cite{Rosenbaum1983}, and the synthesis in \cite{Hernan2020} identify the value of a treatment rule from observed regressions. \cite{Manski2004} formulated treatment choice as a statistical decision problem evaluated by expected welfare, while \cite{Murphy2003} developed optimal dynamic treatment regimes in the potential-outcome model. \cite{Hirano2009} supplied local asymptotic optimality theory for statistical treatment rules, and \cite{Kitagawa2018} established matching \(n^{-1/2}\)-order worst-case welfare-regret bounds for empirical welfare maximization over policy classes of controlled complexity. \cite{Qian2011} connected regression estimation to individualized-rule performance through finite-sample oracle bounds, and \cite{Athey2021} developed efficient observational policy learning with asymptotic regret guarantees. These works principally evaluate or learn a rule; the present paper estimates the scalar population value obtained by maximizing over all binary-treatment choices separately within each categorical covariate cell.

The maximization makes that scalar value nonregular at treatment-effect ties. \cite{Chakraborty2010} analyzed the nonregular parameters generated by backward optimization in dynamic regimes, and \cite{Hirano2012} showed more generally that nondifferentiability rules out locally asymptotically unbiased or regular estimation under their stated criteria. In the directly relevant semiparametric literature, \cite{vanDerLaan2015} derived the efficient influence curve and targeted estimators for the mean under an optimal dynamic rule at laws satisfying its pathwise-differentiability and remainder conditions. \cite{LuedtkeVanderLaan2016OptimalValue} then characterized pathwise differentiability of the optimal value and constructed online root-\(n\) inference that remains applicable under specified forms of nonuniqueness. The applied \texttt{tmle3mopttx} workflow of \cite{Malenica2022} implements this targeted-learning estimand for categorical treatments. Recent smoothing approaches extend the inferential frontier: \cite{Chen2023} obtains valid inference for finite-horizon optimal dynamic policies through a diverging-temperature softmax approximation; \cite{WhitehouseChenAusternSyrgkanis2025} establishes root-\(n\) asymptotic linearity for smoothed maxima under explicit positivity, nuisance-rate, and near-tie regularity conditions; and \cite{Wei2025} characterizes efficient off-policy inference in Markov decision processes and supplies procedures calibrated to unique and nonunique optima. The present result addresses the complementary finite-sample minimax problem over an unrestricted growing categorical alphabet, including arbitrary exact ties, and characterizes its risk uniformly rather than through a fixed-law limit distribution.

Its statistical construction belongs to the large-alphabet theory of nonsmooth functional estimation. The decision-theoretic foundations in \cite{LeCam1986,Tsybakov2009}, together with the constructive-approximation tools surveyed by \cite{DeVore1993}, underlie the combination of Poissonization, polynomial approximation, and minimax testing. \cite{CaiLow2011L1} developed an approximation--moment dual lower-bound method for the absolute-value cusp and obtained a sharp minimax analysis of an average absolute Gaussian mean. \cite{Valiant2017} showed that entropy and other symmetric distribution properties can exhibit an effective logarithmic enlargement of sample size. \cite{JiaoVenkatHanWeissman2015Functionals} systematized the corresponding estimator architecture: classify cells through an independent pilot, approximate the nonsmooth component polynomially, and lift polynomial terms with unbiased factorial moments. \cite{Wu2016} obtained the entropy risk
\[
  \left\{\frac{d}{n\log d}\right\}^{2}+\frac{\log^{2}d}{n}
\]
above the \(d/\log d\) consistency threshold by pairing best polynomial approximation with moment-matched lower bounds. \cite{HanJiaoWeissman2018Local} subsequently developed local moment matching and minimax-optimal reconstruction for symmetric functionals. The present Jackson--factorial construction adapts this lineage to grouped four-coordinate observational cells whose arm-specific outcome means contain unknown denominators.

The closest large-alphabet comparisons clarify the new frontier. For two unknown \(d\)-point distributions, \cite{JiaoHanWeissman2018L1} constructs minimax-rate estimators of \(L_1\) distance and proves the logarithmic effective-sample-size phenomenon; its normalized two-sample experiment yields the \(d/\{n\log(en)\}\) squared-risk obstruction used by the present exact equal-propensity embedding. For causal adjustment with unrestricted high-dimensional categorical covariates, \cite{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024Discrete} proves that standard regression, weighting, and doubly robust estimators of linear treatment-specific means have mean-squared error bounded by \(d^{2}/n^{2}+1/n\), alongside a minimax lower bound of order \(d^{2}/\{n^{2}\log^{2}n\}+1/n\); under effect homogeneity or known covariate distribution it gives the faster order \(d/n^{2}+1/n\). The present target replaces that signed linear aggregation by the cellwise maximum and establishes the matched all-regime frontier
\[
  \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}\asymp_{\epsilon}
  \min\!\left\{1,\frac{d}{n\log(ed)}\right\}.
\]
Thus one explicit estimator and a matching all-measurable-estimator converse characterize optimal-value estimation with unknown unequal propensities, arbitrary cell masses, null cells, boundary outcome means, and treatment-effect ties; the frontier yields uniform consistency exactly when \(d=o\{n\log(en)\}\) and parametric mean-squared error exactly along bounded-alphabet sequences.
