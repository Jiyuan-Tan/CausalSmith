\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:dense-amplitude-range}]
Write
\[
L_d=\log(ed),\qquad
K_d^{\mathrm{lb}}=2\lceil 4L_d\rceil,\qquad
\lambda_{n,d}=\frac{2n}{d},
\qquad
a_{n,d}=\sqrt{\frac{K_d^{\mathrm{lb}}}{64\lambda_{n,d}}},
\]
as in \cref{obj:def:dense-moment-matching-construction}.  Since \(2\le d\), we have \(0<d\) and hence, as real numbers,
\[
\log d\le d-1.
\]
Moreover
\[
L_d=\log(ed)=1+\log d,
\]
so
\[
L_d\le d.
\]
Also \(ed>1\), and therefore
\[
0<L_d.
\]
It follows that
\[
0<\lceil 4L_d\rceil,
\qquad
0<K_d^{\mathrm{lb}},
\]
and, using \(L_d\le d\),
\[
\lceil 4L_d\rceil\le 4d,
\qquad
K_d^{\mathrm{lb}}=2\lceil 4L_d\rceil\le 8d .
\]
% lean: denseAmplitude_range

The assumption \(d^2<n\) gives \(0<n\), so
\[
0<\lambda_{n,d}=\frac{2n}{d}.
\]
Consequently
\[
0<
\frac{K_d^{\mathrm{lb}}}{64\lambda_{n,d}},
\]
and hence
\[
0<a_{n,d}.
\]
% lean: denseAmplitude_range

It remains to bound the square-root argument by \(1/4\).  Since
\[
K_d^{\mathrm{lb}}\le 8d
\]
and \(d^2<n\), we have
\[
K_d^{\mathrm{lb}}d\le 8d^2<8n\le 32n .
\]
Dividing by the positive quantity \(32n\) gives
\[
\frac{K_d^{\mathrm{lb}}d}{32n}\le 1.
\]
Equivalently, because \(\lambda_{n,d}=2n/d\),
\[
\frac{K_d^{\mathrm{lb}}}{64\lambda_{n,d}}
=
\frac{K_d^{\mathrm{lb}}d}{128n}
\le
\frac14
=
\left(\frac12\right)^2 .
\]
Taking square roots yields
\[
a_{n,d}
=
\sqrt{\frac{K_d^{\mathrm{lb}}}{64\lambda_{n,d}}}
\le
\frac12 .
\]
Combining this with \(0<a_{n,d}\) proves the claim.
% lean: denseAmplitude_range
\end{proof}
