\section{Lower bounds and the matched rate}

The upper bound in \cref{obj:thm:jackson-factorial-upper} is matched by a converse built from a normalized two-sample \(L_1\) problem. The reduction uses an equal-propensity slice of the observed model: two probability vectors are encoded into the four treatment--outcome atoms so that the optimal-regression value equals one half plus one quarter of their \(L_1\) distance. This makes a classical nonsmooth-distribution obstruction available inside the treatment-value experiment.

\begin{definitionv}{def:l1-embedding}[\(L_1\) embedding \((\mathbf P,\mathbf Q)\)]
For \((\mathbf P,\mathbf Q)\in\Delta_d^2\), define the observed atom masses
\[
q_{11,x}=q_{00,x}=\frac{P_x}{4},
\qquad
q_{10,x}=q_{01,x}=\frac{Q_x}{4},
\qquad x\in[d].
\]
Complete the observed law by taking the potential outcomes to be conditionally independent Bernoulli variables, setting the treatment propensity equal to \(1/2\), and imposing consistency.
\end{definitionv}

The construction in \cref{obj:def:l1-embedding} puts both arms at propensity \(1/2\), so it lies within every fixed-overlap class with overlap constant below one half. Its purpose is to make the absolute difference between the two simplex inputs appear as the cellwise gain from choosing the better arm.

To state the source minimax problem and its realized observed-data embedding, we record the fixed-sample \(L_1\) risk and the corresponding full-data law.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_7}[Fixed-sample two-sample \(L_1\) minimax risk]
Let \((Z_i,W_i)_{i=1}^n\) be independent paired observations with \(Z_i\sim\mathbf P\), \(W_i\sim\mathbf Q\), and \((\mathbf P,\mathbf Q)\in\Delta_d^2\). The normalized two-sample \(L_1\) minimax mean-squared error is \[ \mathfrak R^{L_1}_{n,d}:=\inf_{\widehat D}\sup_{(\mathbf P,\mathbf Q)\in\Delta_d^2}E_{\mathbf P,\mathbf Q}\left[\left\{\widehat D((Z_i,W_i)_{i=1}^n)-\|\mathbf P-\mathbf Q\|_1\right\}^2\right], \] where the infimum is over all measurable real-valued functions of the paired sample.
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{synth_6}[Equal-propensity normalized \(L_1\) embedding]
For \((\mathbf P,\mathbf Q)\in\Delta_d^2\), \(\mathrm{L1Emb}(\mathbf P,\mathbf Q)\) is the full-data law on \((X,A,Y,Y(0),Y(1))\) with covariate mass \(p_x=(P_x+Q_x)/2\), treatment propensity \(\pi_x=1/2\), outcome regressions \(\mu_{1x}=P_x/(P_x+Q_x)\) and \(\mu_{0x}=Q_x/(P_x+Q_x)\) on cells with \(P_x+Q_x>0\), conditionally independent Bernoulli potential outcomes with these means, treatment drawn independently of the potential outcomes given \(X\), and observed outcome \(Y=Y(A)\). Equivalently, its observed margin has cell masses \(q_{11,x}=q_{00,x}=P_x/4\) and \(q_{10,x}=q_{01,x}=Q_x/4\), with a consistency-compatible completion.
\end{definitionv}

\Cref{obj:synth_7} fixes the comparison risk for estimating normalized \(L_1\) distance from paired samples. \Cref{obj:synth_6} then realizes the same pair inside the causal completion framework used by \cref{obj:def:model-class}, while preserving the observed atom identities displayed in \cref{obj:def:l1-embedding}.

\begin{propositionv}{prop:equal-propensity-l1-reduction}[Equal-propensity L1 reduction]
Let \(n,d\in\mathbb N\) and let \(\epsilon\) be the overlap parameter. Suppose that:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Sampling.)} The sample law \(\mu_n\) is the \(n\)-fold product law generated by an observed law \(\mathbb P_0\), as in \cref{obj:ass:iid-sampling}.
\item \textbf{(Alphabet size.)} \(2\le d\).
\item \textbf{(Overlap range.)} \(0<\epsilon<1/2\).
\end{itemize}
Then, for every pair \((\mathbf P,\mathbf Q)\in\Delta_d^2\), let
\[
\mathbb P=\operatorname{Obs}\{\mathrm{L1Emb}(\mathbf P,\mathbf Q)\},
\]
where \(\operatorname{Obs}\) is the observed margin from \cref{obj:def:observed-margin} and \(\mathrm{L1Emb}\) is the embedding in \cref{obj:def:l1-embedding}. The law \(\mathbb P\) belongs to \(\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}\) in the sense of \cref{obj:def:observed-model-class}, and, for every \(x\in[d]\),
\[
p_x=\frac{P_x+Q_x}{2}.
\]
Moreover, whenever \(p_x>0\),
\[
\pi_x=\frac12,
\]
and the observed-law optimal-regression value from \cref{obj:def:observed-optimal-value} satisfies
\[
\Psi(\mathbb P)=\frac12+\frac14\sum_{x\in[d]} |P_x-Q_x|.
\]
Finally, the explicit fixed-\(n\) product Markov kernel obtained by applying the single-observation \(L_1\) embedding kernel independently across the \(n\) coordinates transforms the law of \(n\) paired draws from \((\mathbf P,\mathbf Q)\) exactly into the \(n\)-fold product law \(\mathbb P^{\otimes n}\). Consequently, for the fixed-sample minimax squared risk in \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk},
\[
\frac{1}{16}\,\mathfrak R^{L_1}_{n,d}\le \mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon},
\]
where \(\mathfrak R^{L_1}_{n,d}\) denotes the corresponding normalized two-sample \(L_1\) minimax mean-squared risk.
\end{propositionv}

\Cref{obj:prop:equal-propensity-l1-reduction} transfers the two-sample \(L_1\) lower bound into the observed optimal-value problem through an exact product Markov kernel. The factor \(1/16\) is the square of the one-quarter scaling between \(L_1\) distance and the value shift.

The full converse combines this embedded \(L_1\) slice with the dense moment-matching construction used for large alphabets. The moment-matching ingredient follows the best polynomial approximation of \(|t|\) developed by \citet{CaiLow2011L1} and the Poissonized \(L_1\) lower-bound method of \citet{JiaoHanWeissman2018L1}; the fixed-sample statement below incorporates the regime and padding steps into the observed-law risk.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:all-estimator-lower}[Global minimax lower bound]*
Suppose the observed samples satisfy the independent product sampling law in \cref{obj:ass:iid-sampling}. For every overlap constant \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), there exists a constant \(c_\epsilon>0\) such that, for every \(n\geq 1\) and \(d\geq 2\), the fixed-sample minimax squared risk \(\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}\) in \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk} satisfies
\[
c_\epsilon \min\left\{1,\frac{d}{n\log(ed)}\right\}
\leq
\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}.
\]
\end{theoremv}

\Cref{obj:thm:all-estimator-lower} establishes an information-theoretic benchmark for every measurable estimator over the full observed-law class. The scale is bounded by a constant in the saturated region and otherwise decreases as \(d/\{n\log(ed)\}\), reflecting the same nonsmooth absolute-value difficulty that drives large-alphabet \(L_1\) estimation.

Combining the lower bound with the constructive estimator gives the exact finite-sample rate order. The statement uses the logarithmic alphabet scale \(L_d=\log(ed)\) and the all-data Rao--Blackwellized estimator from \cref{obj:def:jackson-factorial-estimator}.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:matched-minimax-frontier}[Matched minimax frontier]*
There are universal tuning constants \(H_0>0\), \(0<\kappa<1\), and an integer cutoff \(D_0>2\) for the Jackson factorial estimator such that the following holds.  For every fixed overlap constant \(\epsilon\in(0,1/2)\), there are real constants \(c_\epsilon\) and \(C_\epsilon\) with
\[
0<c_\epsilon\leq C_\epsilon .
\]
For every \(n\geq 1\) and \(d\geq 2\), with \(L_d=\log(ed)\) and with \(\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}\) denoting the fixed-sample observed minimax squared risk in \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk},
\[
c_\epsilon \min\left\{1,\frac{d}{nL_d}\right\}
\leq
\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
\leq
\sup_{\mathbb P\in\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}}
E_{\mathbb P^{\otimes n}}\!\left[
  \left(\widehat V_{n,d}^{\mathrm{JF}}-\Psi(\mathbb P)\right)^2
\right]
\leq
C_\epsilon \min\left\{1,\frac{d}{nL_d}\right\}.
\]
Moreover, the fixed-sample minimax squared risk over the full-data causal completion class \(\mathcal P_{d,\epsilon}\) is exactly \(\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}\) for the same \(n\), \(d\), and \(\epsilon\).
\end{theoremv}

\Cref{obj:thm:matched-minimax-frontier} characterizes the minimax mean-squared risk order for the observed optimal-regression value and gives an explicit estimator attaining it. The final clause carries the same characterization to the causal completion class, using the identification bridge in \cref{obj:prop:causal-optimal-value-corollary}.

The finite-sample characterization has direct asymptotic consequences for sequences of growing alphabets. Writing the alphabet size as a function of the sample size, the next result converts the matched bound into consistency and parametric-rate criteria.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:consistency-and-parametric-boundaries}[Consistency and parametric boundaries]*
For every overlap constant \(\epsilon\) with \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), and for every alphabet-size sequence \(d_\bullet=(d_n)_{n\ge 1}\) satisfying \(d_n\ge 2\) for all \(n\), the following equivalences hold:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Observed consistency.)} The fixed-sample minimax squared risk \(\mathfrak R_{n,d_n,\epsilon}\) of \cref{obj:def:minimax-risk} satisfies
\[
\mathfrak R_{n,d_n,\epsilon}\longrightarrow 0
\]
if and only if
\[
d_n=o\{n\log(en)\}.
\]
\item \textbf{(Causal consistency.)} The causal minimax squared risk over \(\mathcal P_{d_n,\epsilon}\) satisfies
\[
\mathfrak R^{\mathrm{causal}}_{n,d_n,\epsilon}\longrightarrow 0
\]
if and only if
\[
d_n=o\{n\log(en)\}.
\]
\item \textbf{(Observed parametric boundary.)} The observed minimax risk sequence has the parametric \(n^{-1}\) rate, meaning that there exist constants \(c,C\) with \(0<c\le C\) such that, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
\frac{c}{n}\le \mathfrak R_{n,d_n,\epsilon}\le \frac{C}{n},
\]
if and only if the alphabet-size sequence is uniformly bounded asymptotically, meaning \(d_n=O(1)\).
\item \textbf{(Causal parametric boundary.)} The causal minimax risk sequence has the parametric \(n^{-1}\) rate, meaning that there exist constants \(c,C\) with \(0<c\le C\) such that, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
\frac{c}{n}\le \mathfrak R^{\mathrm{causal}}_{n,d_n,\epsilon}\le \frac{C}{n},
\]
if and only if \(d_n=O(1)\).
\end{itemize}
\end{theoremv}

\Cref{obj:thm:consistency-and-parametric-boundaries} states the operational growth rules implied by the finite-sample rate. Uniform consistency holds exactly along sequences whose alphabet grows more slowly than \(n\log(en)\), while the \(n^{-1}\) risk scale is tied exactly to asymptotically bounded alphabets. The observed-law and causal-completion formulations share these criteria because their minimax risks coincide under the conditions of \cref{obj:thm:matched-minimax-frontier}.
