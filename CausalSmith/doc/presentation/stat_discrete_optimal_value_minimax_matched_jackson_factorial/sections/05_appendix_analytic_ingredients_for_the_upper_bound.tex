\section{Analytic ingredients for the upper bound}

The upper bound in \cref{obj:thm:jackson-factorial-upper} rests on two local analytic devices. The first is a Jackson polynomial certificate for the globally defined cell contribution \(\bar f_\epsilon\) from \cref{obj:def:global-extension}, localized to the pilot rectangle \(Q_x\) constructed in \cref{obj:def:jackson-factorial-estimator}. The second is a centered Poisson factorial lift that turns the local polynomial into a cell statistic whose bias and variance remain controlled at the cell mass scale.

We begin with the approximation component. The construction uses the four outcome-arm coordinate set \(\mathcal J\) and smooths a bounded continuous function on the normalized cube by the tensor product of the order-four Jackson kernel in \cref{obj:def:jackson-kernel}. The following coefficient envelope records the degree and one-norm control needed when the smoothed polynomial is later evaluated by factorial moments.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:tensor-jackson-coefficient-envelope}[Tensor Jackson coefficient envelope]
With the Jackson kernel notation of \cref{obj:def:jackson-kernel}, assume:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Order.)} \(K\ge 1\) is an integer.
\item \textbf{(Function.)} \(f\) is continuous on the normalized cube \([-1,1]^{\mathcal J}\), where \(\mathcal J\) is the four arm-outcome coordinate set.
\item \textbf{(Envelope.)} \(B\ge 0\) and \(\lvert f(z)\rvert\le B\) for every \(z\in[-1,1]^{\mathcal J}\).
\end{itemize}
Then there exists a polynomial \(\mathcal R\) in the four normalized coordinates such that, for every angle vector \(\varphi:\mathcal J\to\mathbb R\),
\[
\mathcal R(\cos\varphi)
=
\int_{[-\pi,\pi]^{\mathcal J}}
f\{\cos(\varphi-\tau)\}
\prod_{\jmath\in\mathcal J}J_K(\tau_\jmath)\,d\tau .
\]
Here cosine is applied coordinatewise. Moreover, every multi-index \(\beta\in\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal R)\) satisfies
\[
\beta_\omega\le 2(K-1)
\qquad\text{for every }\omega\in\mathcal J,
\]
the total degree of \(\mathcal R\) is at most \(8(K-1)\), and its coefficient one-norm satisfies
\[
\sum_{\beta\in\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal R)}
\lvert \mathcal R_\beta\rvert
\le 2^{40K+20}B .
\]
\end{lemmav}

The envelope in \cref{obj:lem:tensor-jackson-coefficient-envelope} is deliberately expressed in normalized coordinates. After an affine change of variables from the pilot rectangle to \([-1,1]^{\mathcal J}\), it gives a physical polynomial \(P_{x,K}\) while preserving a centered expansion around \(b_x\), the rectangle center from \cref{obj:def:jackson-kernel}.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:jackson-tensor-extraction}[Jackson tensor extraction]
Using the overlap-parameter notation of \cref{obj:ass:fixed-overlap}, let \(\epsilon\in\mathbb R\), let \(K\) be an integer, and let
\(Q_x=\prod_{\jmath\in\mathcal J}[\ell_{\jmath,x},u_{\jmath,x}]\) be a four-coordinate rectangle with
\[
b_{\jmath,x}=\frac{\ell_{\jmath,x}+u_{\jmath,x}}{2},
\qquad
r_{\jmath,x}=\frac{u_{\jmath,x}-\ell_{\jmath,x}}{2}.
\]
Assume:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Degree.)} \(K\ge 2\).
\item \textbf{(Lower endpoints.)} \(\ell_{\jmath,x}\ge 0\) for every \(\jmath\in\mathcal J\).
\item \textbf{(Overlap parameter.)} \(\epsilon>0\).
\item \textbf{(Radii.)} \(r_{\jmath,x}>0\) for every \(\jmath\in\mathcal J\).
\end{itemize}
Then there exist a polynomial \(P_{x,K}\) in the four physical coordinates and a polynomial
\(\mathcal R_{x,K}\) in the four normalized coordinates such that:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Physical degree.)} Every coordinate degree of \(P_{x,K}\) is at most \(2(K-1)\).
\item \textbf{(Normalized degree.)} Every \(\beta\in\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal R_{x,K})\) satisfies
\(\beta_\omega\le 2(K-1)\) for every coordinate \(\omega\in\mathcal J\).
\item \textbf{(Centered evaluation.)} For every four-coordinate vector \(v\),
\[
P_{x,K}(v)-\bar f_\epsilon(b_x)
=
\mathcal R_{x,K}\left(\left\{\frac{v_\jmath-b_{\jmath,x}}{r_{\jmath,x}}\right\}_{\jmath\in\mathcal J}\right).
\]
Here \(\bar f_\epsilon\) is the globally defined cellwise maximum contribution from
\cref{obj:def:global-extension}.
\item \textbf{(Jackson convolution.)} For every angle vector \(\vartheta:\mathcal J\to\mathbb R\),
\[
P_{x,K}\left(\left\{b_{\jmath,x}+r_{\jmath,x}\cos(\vartheta_\jmath)\right\}_{\jmath\in\mathcal J}\right)
\]
equals the order-\(K\) tensor Jackson convolution from \cref{obj:def:jackson-kernel} of
\(\bar f_\epsilon\) precomposed with the affine parametrization of \(Q_x\), evaluated at
\(\vartheta\).
\item \textbf{(Coefficient envelope.)}
\[
\sum_{\beta\in\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal R_{x,K})}
\left|(\mathcal R_{x,K})_\beta\right|
\le
2^{40K+20}(1+\epsilon^{-1})\sum_{\jmath\in\mathcal J}r_{\jmath,x}.
\]
\end{itemize}
\end{lemmav}

The extraction step supplies the polynomial used by the estimator, and its centered form is the one matched to the factorial lift below. The dependence on \(\sum_{\jmath}r_{\jmath,x}\) is the local scale that later becomes the Poisson noise scale after the pilot rectangle is chosen from the data.

For reference, we also isolate the tensor convolution operator used in these statements. This notation matches the product-kernel approximation scheme standard in Jackson-type polynomial approximation \citep{DeVore1993}.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_20}[Tensor Jackson convolution operator $\mathcal J_K$]
For a positive integer $K$, let $J_K(t)=c_K\{\sin(Kt/2)/\sin(t/2)\}^4$ on $[-\pi,\pi]$, with the removable values filled in continuously and with $c_K$ chosen so that $\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}J_K(t)\,dt=1$. For a finite coordinate dimension $D$ and a function $f:\mathbb R^D\to\mathbb R$, the order-$K$ tensor Jackson convolution is the operator $\mathcal J_K$ defined, for $\theta\in\mathbb R^D$, by \[ (\mathcal J_K f)(\cos\theta) := \int_{[-\pi,\pi]^D} f\{\cos(\theta+t)\}\prod_{i=1}^D J_K(t_i)\,dt, \] where cosine and addition are coordinatewise. Equivalently, the product kernel $\prod_{i=1}^D J_K(t_i)$ is normalized to have integral one over $[-\pi,\pi]^D$.
\end{definitionv}

The approximation error is controlled through a weighted modulus along angular perturbations. The square-root boundary term in the main certificate comes from converting angular increments back to physical coordinates on a rectangle.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:jackson-convolution-modulus}[Tensor Jackson modulus]
Let \(D\) be a finite coordinate dimension, let \(K\) be a positive integer, and let \(f:\mathbb R^D\to\mathbb R\). Let \(L\ge0\), let \(a_i\ge0\) and \(b_i\ge0\) for each \(i\in[D]\), and fix an angle vector \(\varphi\in\mathbb R^D\). Suppose:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Kernel.)} The order-\(K\) tensor Jackson convolution is formed from the order-four Jackson kernel in \cref{obj:def:jackson-kernel}.
\item \textbf{(Continuity.)} The function \(f\) is continuous on the normalized cube \([-1,1]^D\).
\item \textbf{(Weighted increment modulus.)} For every shift \(\tau\in\mathbb R^D\),
\[
\left|f\{\cos(\varphi-\tau)\}-f(\cos\varphi)\right|
\le
L\sum_{i=1}^D\left(a_i|\tau_i|+b_i\tau_i^2\right),
\]
where cosine is applied coordinatewise.
\end{itemize}
Then
\[
\left|(\mathcal J_K f)(\cos\varphi)-f(\cos\varphi)\right|
\le
L\sum_{i=1}^D\left(\frac{32a_i}{K}+\frac{64b_i}{K^2}\right).
\]
\end{lemmav}

Combining the coefficient envelope with the angular modulus gives the single polynomial certificate used in the upper-bound proof. It applies uniformly to nonnegative rectangles with positive radii, including the pilot-local rectangles that may meet coordinate faces or the vertex of the overlap cone.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:simultaneous-jackson-certificate}[Simultaneous Jackson certificate]
There is a universal constant \(A>0\) such that, for every overlap parameter \(\epsilon\) with \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), there is a constant \(C_\epsilon>0\) with the following property. Let:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Degree.)} \(K\ge 2\).
\item \textbf{(Rectangle.)} \(Q_x=\prod_{\jmath\in\mathcal J}[\ell_{\jmath,x},u_{\jmath,x}]\) have \(\ell_{\jmath,x}\le u_{\jmath,x}\), \(\ell_{\jmath,x}\ge 0\), and positive radii
\[
r_{\jmath,x}=\frac{u_{\jmath,x}-\ell_{\jmath,x}}{2}>0
\]
for every \(\jmath\in\mathcal J\).
\end{itemize}
Let \(P_{x,K}\) be the tensor Jackson polynomial associated with \(\epsilon\), \(K\), and \(Q_x\) in \cref{obj:def:jackson-kernel}. Then the same polynomial satisfies all of the following conclusions:
\[
\deg_{\jmath}(P_{x,K})\le 2(K-1)
\qquad\text{for every }\jmath\in\mathcal J .
\]
For every \(v\in Q_x\),
\[
\left|P_{x,K}(v)-\bar f_\epsilon(v)\right|
\le
C_\epsilon
\sum_{\jmath\in\mathcal J}
\left\{
\frac{\sqrt{(v_{\jmath}-\ell_{\jmath,x})(u_{\jmath,x}-v_{\jmath})}}{K}
+
\frac{r_{\jmath,x}}{K^2}
\right\}.
\]
There are a finite multi-index set \(\mathcal A_x\) and coefficients \(b_{x,\alpha}\) such that, for every \(v\),
\[
P_{x,K}(v)-\bar f_\epsilon(b_x)
=
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal A_x}
b_{x,\alpha}
\prod_{\jmath\in\mathcal J}
\left(\frac{v_{\jmath}-b_{\jmath,x}}{r_{\jmath,x}}\right)^{\alpha_{\jmath}},
\]
with
\[
\alpha_{\jmath}\le 2(K-1)
\qquad\text{for every }\alpha\in\mathcal A_x\text{ and every }\jmath\in\mathcal J,
\]
and
\[
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal A_x}|b_{x,\alpha}|
\le
A^K(1+\epsilon^{-1})
\sum_{\jmath\in\mathcal J} r_{\jmath,x}.
\]
\end{lemmav}

\Cref{obj:lem:simultaneous-jackson-certificate} is the analytic bridge between approximation and estimation. The pointwise bound controls the local bias of \(P_{x,K}\) on \(Q_x\), while the centered coefficient expansion controls the moments of the factorial estimator after replacing monomials by centered Poisson factorial polynomials.

We next record the pilot calculations that make the random rectangles usable. The good-pilot event is self-normalized: its radius is computed from the observed pilot count itself, so the event has the same scale in small and large cells.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:canonical-pilot-bad-moment}[Bad pilot moment bound]
Fix the four-coordinate set \(\mathcal J=\{0,1\}\times\{0,1\}\). Let \(q=(q_\jmath)_{\jmath\in\mathcal J}\) have nonnegative coordinates, let \(m>0\), let \(L\ge 1\), and let \(t\in\{1,2,4\}\). Suppose the pilot counts \((N'_{\jmath})_{\jmath\in\mathcal J}\) are independent with
\[
N'_{\jmath}\sim \operatorname{Pois}(m q_\jmath),
\qquad \jmath\in\mathcal J .
\]
With \(H_0=1024\), define the normalized aggregate pilot score
\[
\Gamma(N')
=
\sum_{\jmath\in\mathcal J}
\left[
\left|{N'_{\jmath}\over m}-q_\jmath\right|
+
H_0\left\{\sqrt{{N'_{\jmath}\over m}{L\over m}}+{L\over m}\right\}
\right],
\]
and define the self-normalized bad-pilot event
\[
\mathsf G^c
=
\left\{
\exists\,\jmath\in\mathcal J:
\left|{N'_{\jmath}\over m}-q_\jmath\right|
>
{H_0\over 4}
\left\{\sqrt{{N'_{\jmath}\over m}{L\over m}}+{L\over m}\right\}
\right\}.
\]
Then, with \(C_t=10^{80(t+1)}\),
\[
E\!\left[\Gamma(N')^t\mathbf 1_{\mathsf G^c}\right]
\le
C_t e^{-20L}
\left\{
\sqrt{{\left(\sum_{\jmath\in\mathcal J}q_\jmath\right)L\over m}}
+
{L\over m}
\right\}^{t}.
\]
\end{lemmav}

The exponential factor in \cref{obj:lem:canonical-pilot-bad-moment} is paired with the logarithmic choice \(L_d=\log(ed)\) from \cref{obj:def:jackson-factorial-estimator}. This pairing makes the contribution from bad pilot rectangles summable across cells at the rate used in \cref{obj:thm:jackson-factorial-upper}.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_12}[Good-pilot event]
Fix a cell \(x\), four-cell mass vector \(q_x=(q_{\jmath,x})_{\jmath\in\mathcal J}\), Poisson scale \(m>0\), alphabet logarithm \(L_d=\log(ed)\), pilot-radius constant \(H_0\), and pilot counts \((N'_{\jmath,x})_{\jmath\in\mathcal J}\). Put \(c_{\jmath,x}=N'_{\jmath,x}/m\) and \(h_{\jmath,x}=H_0\{\sqrt{c_{\jmath,x}L_d/m}+L_d/m\}\). The coordinatewise good-pilot event used for the pilot rectangle is \[\mathsf G_x:=\bigcap_{\jmath\in\mathcal J}\left\{\left|\frac{N'_{\jmath,x}}{m}-q_{\jmath,x}\right|\le \frac{h_{\jmath,x}}{4}\right\}.\]
\end{definitionv}

On \(\mathsf G_x\), the pilot rectangle radius is comparable to the local Poisson scale. The next lemma expresses that comparison in the aggregate form needed by the coefficient envelope.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:good-pilot-radius-sum}[Good pilot radius bound]
Fix \(m>0\), an integer \(d\ge 1\), pilot counts \(N'_{\jmath,x}\) for \(\jmath\) in the four-coordinate set \(\mathcal J\), and coordinate intensities \(q_\jmath\ge 0\).  Let \(L_d=\log(ed)\), set \(p_x=\sum_{\jmath\in\mathcal J}q_\jmath\), and let \(Q_x=\prod_{\jmath\in\mathcal J}[\ell_{\jmath,x},u_{\jmath,x}]\) be the canonical pilot rectangle of \cref{obj:def:jackson-factorial-estimator}, with radii
\[
r_{\jmath,x}=\frac{u_{\jmath,x}-\ell_{\jmath,x}}{2}.
\]
Assume that the good-pilot event
\[
\mathsf G_x=\bigcap_{\jmath\in\mathcal J}
\left\{\left|c_{\jmath,x}-q_\jmath\right|\le \frac{h_{\jmath,x}}{4}\right\},
\qquad c_{\jmath,x}=\frac{N'_{\jmath,x}}{m},
\]
holds, where \(h_{\jmath,x}\) is the canonical pilot half-width in \cref{obj:def:jackson-factorial-estimator}.  Then
\[
\sum_{\jmath\in\mathcal J} r_{\jmath,x}
\le
2\cdot 10^6
\left\{
\sqrt{\frac{p_x L_d}{m}}+\frac{L_d}{m}
\right\}.
\]
\end{lemmav}

The radius bound turns the abstract coefficient control into a stochastic bound indexed by \(p_x\), the cell mass. The following normalized envelope is the version applied directly to the centered polynomial expansion inside the estimator.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:jackson-normalized-coefficient-envelope}[Normalized coefficient envelope]
Let \(\epsilon>0\) be the overlap parameter from \cref{obj:ass:fixed-overlap}, let \(K\ge2\) be an integer, and let \(Q_x=\prod_{\jmath\in\mathcal J}[\ell_{\jmath,x},u_{\jmath,x}]\) be a rectangle over the four outcome-arm coordinate set \(\mathcal J\). Suppose that:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Ordered endpoints.)} \(\ell_{\jmath,x}\le u_{\jmath,x}\) for every \(\jmath\in\mathcal J\).
\item \textbf{(Nonnegative lower endpoints.)} \(\ell_{\jmath,x}\ge0\) for every \(\jmath\in\mathcal J\).
\item \textbf{(Positive radii.)} With
\[
b_{\jmath,x}=\frac{\ell_{\jmath,x}+u_{\jmath,x}}{2},
\qquad
r_{\jmath,x}=\frac{u_{\jmath,x}-\ell_{\jmath,x}}{2},
\]
one has \(r_{\jmath,x}>0\) for every \(\jmath\in\mathcal J\).
\end{itemize}
Let \(b_x=(b_{\jmath,x})_{\jmath\in\mathcal J}\), let \(r_x=(r_{\jmath,x})_{\jmath\in\mathcal J}\), and let \(P_{x,K}\) be the Jackson tensor polynomial attached to \(Q_x\) by \cref{obj:def:jackson-kernel}. Define
\[
\mathcal R_{x,K}(z)
=
P_{x,K}\bigl((b_{\jmath,x}+r_{\jmath,x}z_\jmath)_{\jmath\in\mathcal J}\bigr)
-\bar f_\epsilon(b_x)
=
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal A_x} b_{x,\alpha}\prod_{\jmath\in\mathcal J} z_\jmath^{\alpha_\jmath},
\]
where \(\bar f_\epsilon\) is the global cell extension from \cref{obj:def:global-extension} and \(\mathcal A_x=\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal R_{x,K})\). Then
\[
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal A_x}|b_{x,\alpha}|
\le
(2^{60})^{K}(1+\epsilon^{-1})\sum_{\jmath\in\mathcal J}r_{\jmath,x}.
\]
Moreover, every \(\alpha\in\mathcal A_x\) satisfies
\[
\alpha_\jmath\le 2(K-1)
\]
for every \(\jmath\in\mathcal J\).
\end{lemmav}

The centered form in \cref{obj:lem:jackson-normalized-coefficient-envelope} aligns with the polynomial \(U_h(N;z)\) from \cref{obj:def:jackson-factorial-estimator}. This is the same unbiased polynomial-lifting principle used for nonsmooth functional estimation under Poisson sampling \citep{JiaoVenkatHanWeissman2015Functionals}.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_5}[Pilot-failure contributions]
Fix a cell \(x\), a four-cell mass vector \(q_x\), Poisson scale \(m>0\), and Jackson tuning. Let \(\mathbb P_{m,q_x}^{\mathrm{pil,eval}}\) denote the product-Poisson law of the pilot and evaluation count vectors, and let \(\mathsf G_x\) be the coordinatewise good-pilot event used for the pilot rectangle in the Jackson--factorial construction. With \(T_x^{\mathrm{JF}}\) denoting the clipped cell statistic built from the pilot and evaluation counts, define \[ \mathrm{PF}_{1,x}:=\int_{\mathsf G_x^c}\left|T_x^{\mathrm{JF}}-\bar f_\epsilon(q_x)\right|\,d\mathbb P_{m,q_x}^{\mathrm{pil,eval}}, \qquad \mathrm{PF}_{2,x}:=\int_{\mathsf G_x^c}\left(T_x^{\mathrm{JF}}-\bar f_\epsilon(q_x)\right)^2\,d\mathbb P_{m,q_x}^{\mathrm{pil,eval}}. \]
\end{definitionv}

The quantities in \cref{obj:synth_5} separate the contribution of pilot failures from the conditional bias and variance calculations on \(\mathsf G_x\). The final lemma assembles the distributional identities, the conditional factorial moments, and the resulting cellwise controls.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:centered-factorial-pilot-control}[Centered factorial pilot control]
Assume the product experiment satisfies the i.i.d. sampling law in \cref{obj:ass:iid-sampling}.  There is a universal Jackson tuning with self-normalized Poisson pilot-radius constant, polynomial-degree constant \(\kappa=1/100000\), and cutoff \(D_0=3\).  For every overlap parameter \(\epsilon\in(0,1/2)\), there is a constant \(C_\epsilon>0\) such that the following assertions hold for every \(n,d\), every observed law \(\mathbb P\) on \([d]\times\{0,1\}^2\), and every positive sample size \(n\), whenever \(\mathbb P\in\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}\) as in \cref{obj:def:observed-model-class}.  Set \(m=n/8\), let \(q_x=(q_{00,x},q_{01,x},q_{10,x},q_{11,x})\) be the four-coordinate cell vector, and let \(p_x\) be the marginal mass of cell \(x\).

In the uncapped marked experiment generated by
\[
M\sim \operatorname{Pois}(n/4)
\]
together with i.i.d. observations from \(\mathbb P\) and independent fair pilot/evaluation marks, the count \(M\) has law \(\operatorname{Pois}(n/4)\).  The induced pilot/evaluation table has law equal to the product, over cells \(x\in[d]\) and coordinates \(\jmath\in\mathcal J\), of two independent Poisson count arrays with coordinate means \(m q_{\jmath,x}\).  For each fixed cell \(x\), the marginal law of the pilot and evaluation counts in that cell is therefore
\[
\bigotimes_{\jmath\in\mathcal J}\operatorname{Pois}(m q_{\jmath,x})
\otimes
\bigotimes_{\jmath\in\mathcal J}\operatorname{Pois}(m q_{\jmath,x}).
\]

For every cell \(x\), every fixed pilot table, every coordinate \(\jmath\in\mathcal J\), every \(z\in\mathbb R\), and every \(h,t\in\mathbb N\), conditional expectation over the evaluation counts satisfies
\[
\mathbb E\!\left[U_h(N_{\jmath,x};z)\mid N'\right]
=
(q_{\jmath,x}-z)^h
\]
and
\[
\mathbb E\!\left[U_h(N_{\jmath,x};z)U_t(N_{\jmath,x};z)\mid N'\right]
=
\sum_{\ell=0}^{\min\{h,t\}}
\binom{h}{\ell}\binom{t}{\ell}\ell!
\left(\frac{q_{\jmath,x}}{m}\right)^\ell
(q_{\jmath,x}-z)^{h+t-2\ell}.
\]

With the same tuning, the clipped Jackson-factorial cell statistic \(T_x^{\mathrm{JF}}\) obeys, uniformly over all cells \(x\in[d]\),
\[
\left|
\mathbb E T_x^{\mathrm{JF}}-\bar f_\epsilon(q_x)
\right|
\le
C_\epsilon
\left\{
\sqrt{\frac{p_x}{mL_d}}+\frac{1}{mL_d}
\right\},
\]
\[
\operatorname{Var}(T_x^{\mathrm{JF}})
\le
C_\epsilon d^{1/16}
\left\{
\frac{p_x L_d}{m}+\frac{L_d^2}{m^2}
\right\},
\]
and the pilot-failure contributions satisfy
\[
\mathrm{PF}_{1,x}
\le
C_\epsilon
\left\{
\sqrt{\frac{p_x}{mL_d}}+\frac{1}{mL_d}
\right\},
\qquad
\mathrm{PF}_{2,x}
\le
C_\epsilon d^{1/16}
\left\{
\frac{p_x L_d}{m}+\frac{L_d^2}{m^2}
\right\}.
\]
\end{lemmav}

\Cref{obj:lem:centered-factorial-pilot-control} supplies the cellwise estimates used to prove the upper bound. The first displayed identities identify the Poissonized marked experiment with independent pilot and evaluation arrays. The factorial identities then center the polynomial at the pilot rectangle center, and the final bounds control bias, variance, and pilot-failure terms at the same local scale.
