\section{Discussion and limitations}

The matched characterization in \cref{obj:thm:matched-minimax-frontier} has two complementary interpretations. In growing alphabets, the optimal-treatment value behaves like a nonsmooth large-alphabet functional: many cellwise maxima must be learned at once, and the logarithmic factor reflects the polynomial-approximation structure familiar from \(L_1\)-type discrete-distribution estimation \citep{CaiLow2011L1,JiaoHanWeissman2018L1}. In bounded alphabets, the same scalar value returns to the ordinary \(n^{-1}\) mean-squared risk scale recorded in \cref{obj:thm:consistency-and-parametric-boundaries}. Exact ties are part of this characterization because the target takes a maximum within each cell; the rate accounts for the local absolute-value behavior generated by those ties.

The fixed-overlap formulation also clarifies the role of treatment imbalance. The lower-bound embedding in \cref{obj:prop:equal-propensity-l1-reduction} uses the equal-propensity slice \(\pi_x=1/2\), so the nonsmooth difficulty is already present in the balanced experiment. The estimator in \cref{obj:def:jackson-factorial-estimator} works over the full fixed-overlap class \(\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}\), where propensities may vary by cell inside the interval imposed by \cref{obj:ass:fixed-overlap}. Null cells enter through the table representation in \cref{obj:def:observed-model-class}; they contribute zero mass to \(\Psi(\mathbb P)\) and are handled by the globally defined cell contribution in \cref{obj:def:global-extension}.

The following comparison records how the present matched-rate statement relates to the earlier bounded-alphabet and predecessor bracket on the same observed-law minimax criterion.

\begin{propositionv}{prop:parent-reduction}[Parent minimax reduction]*
There exist tuning constants \(H_0>0\), \(\kappa\in(0,1)\), and an integer cutoff \(D_0>2\), used by the Jackson factorial estimator in \cref{obj:def:jackson-factorial-estimator}, such that:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Matched frontier.)} For every overlap parameter \(\epsilon\in(0,1/2)\), there are constants \(c\) and \(C\) with \(0<c\leq C\) such that, for every \(n\geq1\) and \(d\geq2\),
\[
c\min\left\{1,\frac{\sqrt d}{n}+\frac{d}{n\log(en)}\right\}
\leq
\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
\leq
C\frac{d}{n}.
\]
\item \textbf{(Bounded alphabets.)} For every \(n\), every \(2\leq d<D_0\), every overlap parameter \(\epsilon\), and every observed sample, the Jackson factorial estimator in \cref{obj:def:jackson-factorial-estimator} equals the empirical-ratio estimator in \cref{obj:def:empirical-ratio-estimator}:
\[
\widehat V_{n,d}^{\mathrm{JF}}=\widehat V_{n,d}^{\mathrm{ER}}.
\]
\item \textbf{(Bounded-alphabet sequences.)} For every overlap parameter \(\epsilon\in(0,1/2)\) and every sequence \(d_n\) with \(d_n\geq2\) for all \(n\) and \(d_n\leq d_{\max}\) for some finite \(d_{\max}\), there are constants \(c\) and \(C\) with \(0<c\leq C\) such that, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
\frac{c}{n}
\leq
\mathfrak R_{n,d_n,\epsilon}
\leq
\frac{C}{n}.
\]
\end{itemize}
\end{propositionv}

\Cref{obj:prop:parent-reduction} places the present estimator on the same model class and scalar target as the predecessor comparison. Its bounded-alphabet clause also identifies the small-\(d\) branch of \cref{obj:def:jackson-factorial-estimator}: below the cutoff \(D_0\), the all-data estimator coincides with the empirical-ratio estimator of \cref{obj:def:empirical-ratio-estimator}. The bounded-sequence clause matches the parametric boundary in \cref{obj:thm:consistency-and-parametric-boundaries} and makes the transition from fixed finite alphabets to growing alphabets explicit.

A limitation of the finite-sample rate theorem is that it gives comparison constants rather than an exact asymptotic constant. The matched order in \cref{obj:thm:matched-minimax-frontier} makes the natural normalization unambiguous on nonsaturated sequences, and the next definition names the analytic route for that sharper question.

\begin{definitionv}{def:constant-handle}[Constant route \(\mathscr H_\epsilon^{\mathrm{const}}\)]
The route \(\mathscr H_\epsilon^{\mathrm{const}}\) rescales the pilot-local four-cell rectangles by their Poisson noise geometry, optimizes the tensor-polynomial bias--variance functional for \(\bar f_\epsilon\), and dualizes the approximation constraint into moment-matched observed laws in \(\mathcal D_{d,\epsilon}^{\mathrm{obs}}\) for the target \(\Psi(\mathbb P)\).
\end{definitionv}

The route \leanref{sym:\(\mathscr H_\epsilon^{\mathrm{const}}\)}{$\mathscr H_\epsilon^{\mathrm{const}}$} keeps the two ingredients of the matched rate together: the upper-bound side through local polynomial bias--variance optimization, and the lower-bound side through moment-matched observed laws. It is therefore the appropriate object for asking whether comparison bounds can be sharpened to a fixed-overlap efficiency constant.

\begin{remarkv}{oeq:sharp-overlap-constant}[Sharp overlap constant]
A natural next question is whether, along each nonsaturated sequence with \(d\to\infty\) and
\[
\frac{d}{n\log(ed)}\to0,
\]
the normalized risk
\[
\frac{n\log(ed)}{d}\mathfrak R_{n,d,\epsilon}
\]
converges to a finite positive limit, and whether an estimator attaining that limit can be derived through \(\mathscr H_\epsilon^{\mathrm{const}}\).
\end{remarkv}

\Cref{obj:oeq:sharp-overlap-constant} formulates the future-work problem left by the rate characterization. An exact constant along nonsaturated sequences would calibrate the polynomial degree and quantify, at the minimax level, the efficiency cost associated with unknown observational treatment imbalance under fixed overlap.
