The paper belongs to the literature on precision in randomized experiments, grounded in Neyman’s potential-outcome analysis, Fisher’s design principles, and Rubin’s causal framework \citep{Neyman1990,Fisher1935,Rubin1974,Imbens2015}. Horvitz–Thompson estimation connects assignment probabilities to unbiased causal estimation \citep{Horvitz1952}, while semiparametric and experimental-design efficiency theory characterizes attainable asymptotic variance \citep{Hahn1998,Armstrong2022}. Matching and stratification provide constructive routes toward these benchmarks: multivariate matching precedes randomization in \citet{Greevy2004}; \citet{Bai2022b} characterizes optimal matched-pair designs within half-probability stratified schemes; and \citet{Bai2022a,Bai2023} establish inference and efficiency results for matched pairs and highly stratified experiments. Pilot-based stratification trees optimize asymptotic variance within a specified tree class \citep{TabordMeehan2023}, and finely stratified survey experiments combine representative sampling with balanced assignment \citep{Cytrynbaum2026}. Reviews of experimental practice and econometric analysis provide the broader context \citep{Bruhn2009,Bai2024}. The present contribution quantifies finite-sample, worst-case excess variance under a specified smooth interaction class as covariate dimension grows.

A second strand treats balance as an explicit design objective. Rerandomization restricts assignments according to prespecified covariate criteria, with established asymptotic theory and extensions to diverging covariate dimension \citep{Morgan2012,Li2018,Wang2022}; covariate-adaptive randomization has its own inference theory \citep{Bugni2018}. Optimization and decision-theoretic approaches formalize tradeoffs between balance, randomization, and outcome assumptions \citep{Bertsimas2015,Kasy2016}. In particular, \citet{Kallus2018,Kallus2020} formulate worst-case conditional variance over outcome-function classes and study randomized minimax strategies. Regression adjustment supplies a complementary route to precision, including interacted regression, sparse adjustment, growing-dimensional adjustment, and adjustment adapted to stratification \citep{Lin2013,Bloniarz2016,Lei2021,Cytrynbaum2024}. The Gram–Schmidt walk links assignment covariance to ridge-type variance guarantees \citep{HarshawSavjeSpielmanZhang2024GSW}, with online extensions, general assignment probabilities, and limit theory for randomized pivot order \citep{Arbour2022,Rao2025,Chatterjee2025}. A particularly relevant recent extension is \citet[Lemma 4.2 and Proposition 5.1]{Chen2026}: for its normalized nonlinear feature Gram matrix \(K\), its assignment sign vector \(Z\), and its robustness parameter \(\phi\in(0,1)\), the design satisfies \(\operatorname{Cov}(Z)\preceq\{\phi I+(1-\phi)K\}^{-1}\), yielding a fixed-schedule ridge approximation bound for actual HT variance. The present paper establishes a matching population minimax rate for the specified pooled Sobolev interaction class over all fair original-sample designs.

The structured function class connects functional ANOVA and nonparametric modeling to discrepancy and numerical integration. Orthogonal decompositions and sensitivity analysis distinguish interaction order from ambient dimension \citep{Hoeffding1948,Sobol1993}; additive modeling and nonparametric rate theory explain the statistical value of low-dimensional components \citep{Stone1982,Stone1985,Hastie1986}. Effective dimension, weighted integration spaces, and finite-order weights similarly organize high-dimensional QMC guarantees \citep{Caflisch1997,Sloan1998,Sloan2004}, within the broader theory of scrambled quadrature and digital nets \citep{Owen1997,Dick2010}. Reproducing kernels, Sobolev spaces, and empirical-process theory supply standard analytic foundations \citep{Aronszajn1950,Adams2003,Gine2016}. The construction draws on vector balancing, constructive discrepancy minimization, and marginal-preserving randomized rounding \citep{Matousek1999,Banaszczyk1998,Bansal2019,LovettMeka2015EdgeWalk,Bansal2024IteratedRandomizedRounding}. Its lower-bound argument uses the absolute-supremum Rademacher contraction inequality \citep[Definition 2 and Theorem 12(4)]{BartlettMendelson2002Complexities}; its nonperiodic norm transfer uses normalized Hilbert interpolation and weighted \(L^2\) interpolation \citep[Theorems 2.2(i) and 3.1, with the corrigendum]{ChandlerWildeHewettMoiola2015Interpolation}. These references support the methodological and analytic ingredients; the matched capacity statement concerns their application to fair experimental signing with a fixed-function supremum outside sampling expectation.

The closest econometric comparison is \citet{Cytrynbaum2026Limits}. Proposition 2.3, equation (2.4), expresses actual HT excess variance as prognostic signing risk. For correctly specified order-two structure, Theorem B.8(ii), supported by Lemma B.9, gives a GSW upper bound of order \((d^2\log n/n)^{s\wedge1}\), where \(d\) is covariate dimension, \(n\) is sample size, and \(s\) is component smoothness; the tuning parameter and density bounds are fixed. Theorem 4.9 supplies the all-design fixed-dimensional lower exponent, specializing to \(n^{-s}\) for a single bivariate component. One-frequency saturation is also established territory: applying \citet[Theorem 1.2(2a)]{KoganNandyHuang2024Extremal} to independent bounded, centered, variance-one cosine coordinates gives a smallest eigenvalue limit \((1-\sqrt q)^2\) for the pair-feature Gram matrix normalized by the interaction count, when \(q\), the limiting sample-to-interaction ratio, lies in \((0,1)\). This application supplies smooth one-frequency saturation. The present paper establishes the full frequency-rich subcritical comparison
\(R_s(n,d)\asymp\min\{1,(B/n)^s\}\), where \(R_s(n,d)\) is minimax scaled excess risk and \(B=\binom d2\) is the number of coordinate pairs, uniformly over integer dimensions and \(0<s\leq1\). It characterizes the endpoint and logarithmic windows, constructs one smoothness-independent fair design, and proves an independent-prior converse against every admissible design. For fixed smoothness, the resulting vanishing frontier is \(d=o(\sqrt n)\). Cytrynbaum’s Section 6 calibrations, including the rural-Pakistan intervention of \citet{Augsburg2022}, and the official implementation \citep{GSWDesignImplementation} provide concrete consumers for this theoretical precision benchmark.

Recent discrepancy-based integration results sharpen the methodological comparison. \citet[Theorem 1.1 and Section 5]{BansalJiang2024QMC} obtain mean-squared integration error \((\log n)^{O(d)}\sigma_{\mathrm{SO}}(f)^2/n^2\), where \(\sigma_{\mathrm{SO}}(f)\) is smoothed-out variation, by partitioning \(n^2\) iid starting points into size-\(n\) subsets and using random shifts. \citet[Theorem 3.3, Section 3 and Appendix A.2]{ChenJiangKirk2025HighDimensionalQMC} obtain, for functions with interactions of order at most two, the more explicit bound \(C d^2[\log(dn)]^3\sigma_{\mathrm{SO}}(f)^2/n^2\), also from a quadratic starting pool. Building on online Haar thinning \citep{Dwivedi2019}, \citet[Theorem 1.2 and footnote 4]{EzeunalaJhaJiang2026QMCII} reduce the input to \((1+\varepsilon)n\) points for fixed \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\), produce a size-\(n\) subset with high probability, and state expected squared error \(C_{d,\varepsilon}(\log n)^d\sigma_{\mathrm{SO}}(f)^2/n^2\); their constants retain dimension dependence. Kernel thinning provides another RKHS-based sample-compression approach \citep{Dwivedi2024}. These guarantees concern integration on retained subsets, whereas the present capacity concerns fair assignment of every original observation at its original HT weight. A separate minimax frontier optimizes both design and estimator for binary finite-population outcomes: \citet{SudijonoDobribanTchetgen2026Minimax} obtain risk \(n^{-1}-C n^{-4/3}+o(n^{-4/3})\) using Bernoulli assignment and nonlinear shrinkage. The present result fixes actual HT estimation and supplies the attainable all-interaction excess-variance benchmark under uniform sampling and the exact pooled nonperiodic bivariate specification.
