The paper belongs to the literature on heterogeneous causal effects under observational identification. Potential outcomes, consistency, conditional exchangeability, and propensity-score adjustment provide its foundational framework \citep{Rubin1974,Rosenbaum1983}; the broader econometric and causal-inference context is developed by \citet{Imbens2009} and \citet{Hernan2020}. [Identification sources](https://academic.oup.com/biomet/article-abstract/70/1/41/240879), [econometric survey](https://pubs.aeaweb.org/doi/10.1257/jel.47.1.5), [textbook](https://miguelhernan.org/whatifbook). Residualization and efficient semiparametric estimation supply the methodological foundation for orthogonal and doubly robust learning \citep{Robinson1988,Robins1994,Hahn1998,Chernozhukov2018}. This lineage includes the R-learner, doubly robust CATE regression, and general orthogonal statistical learning \citep{Nie2021,Kennedy2023,Foster2023}, alongside kernel and series approaches to conditional-effect inference \citep{Abrevaya2015,Fan2022,Semenova2021}. [R-learner](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.04912), [doubly robust CATE regression](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.14497), [orthogonal learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.09036). A complementary algorithmic strand runs from random forests through causal trees, causal forests, generalized random forests, and metalearners \citep{Breiman2001,Athey2016,WagerAthey2018,Athey2019,Kunzel2019}. The `causal_forest` reference describes the binary-treatment, unconfounded conditional-effect estimand and provides pointwise variance output \citep[][Description and Examples]{GRFDocumentation2026}. The present result supplies private-release accuracy and uncertainty benchmarks for that point-CATE question under the stated statistical class. [Causal forest documentation](https://grf-labs.github.io/grf/reference/causal_forest.html).

The minimax analysis connects classical nonparametric estimation \citep{Stone1982,Tsybakov2009} with higher-order influence functions and quadratic estimating equations for rough nuisance functions \citep{Robins2008,Robins2009,Robins2017}. [Higher-order influence functions](https://arxiv.org/abs/0805.3040), [higher-order estimating equations](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.02174). \citet{Gao2020} establish minimax results for heterogeneous effects in a fixed-design experiment with treatment-assignment geometry entering the estimation problem. [Fixed-design analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.06471). The closest nonprivate comparison is \citet[][Theorems 1--2 and Remarks 2 and 11]{KennedyBalakrishnanRobinsWasserman2024}. Their pointwise absolute-risk lower bound specializes to \(n^{-1/4}\) for one covariate, Lipschitz treatment effects, and propensity and control-regression Hölder orders \(1/10\): the average nuisance smoothness is \(1/10\), below the relevant threshold \(1/6\). Their matching upper theorem uses a higher-order local-polynomial estimator under covariate-distribution approximation, nuisance-estimation, basis, and matrix regularity conditions. Their lower-bound model accommodates real-valued outcomes and an upper density bound. The present paper establishes a pure-private frontier on its specified binary experiment with an unknown measurable density bounded above and below, using actual cell occupancies for attainment. [Minimax CATE source](https://arxiv.org/html/2203.00837v4). A recent alternative is \citet[][Corollary 4]{Kim2026}: under its RKHS subspace model and Sobolev conditions \(\gamma>\beta>d/2\), where \(\gamma\) and \(\beta\) are contrast and nuisance smoothness orders and \(d\) is covariate dimension, its squared pointwise error is \(\widetilde O((n\kappa)^{-(2\gamma-d)/(2\gamma)})\), with \(\kappa\) the overlap floor. This provides a contrast-adaptive comparison in a smoother nuisance regime. [Kernel-ridge frontier](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.18958v2).

The privacy framework follows sensitivity-calibrated noise and postprocessing \citep{Dwork2006,Dwork2014}, statistical decision theory for private estimation \citep{Wasserman2010,Cai2021}, and private testing reductions based on dataset couplings \citep[][Section 2]{AcharyaSunZhang2021}. Local privacy has its own information-contraction and minimax theory \citep{Duchi2018}. [Sensitivity calibration](https://people.csail.mit.edu/asmith/PS/sensitivity-tcc-final.pdf), [central-private minimax estimation](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.04495), [private testing](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v132/acharya21a.html), [local-private estimation](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.02390). The pair construction also belongs to the U-statistic lineage \citep{Hoeffding1948}. \citet[][Theorem 2]{ChaudhuriLohPandeySarkar2024} stabilize bounded U-statistics through local Hájek projections. Their high-probability error bound combines sampling fluctuation with terms of orders \(k\xi/(m\epsilon)\), \(Ck^2/(m^2\epsilon^2)\), and \(Ck^3/(m^3\epsilon^3)\), where \(k\) is kernel order, \(\xi\) controls local projection dispersion, \(C\) bounds the kernel range, and \(m\) is the confidence-wrapper sample size. The present count-weighted covariance-to-variance construction supplies an occupancy-sensitive route for a localized causal ratio. [Private U-statistics](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.04945v1). Distributed U-statistic inference and bootstrap methods extend this methodological strand \citep{Liu2026}; federated regression and adaptive density estimation establish pointwise and global privacy frontiers for Besov classes under distributed privacy constraints \citep[][Corollary 2]{Cai2024} \citep[][Theorems 2.1--2.2]{CaiChakrabortyVuursteen2025Adaptation}. [Distributed inference](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/sta4.70170), [federated regression](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.06755v1), [adaptive density estimation](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.14337v1).

Private causal learning supplies the immediate application-specific comparisons. \citet{NiuEtAl2022} construct private single-stage and multistage heterogeneous-effect learners, using sample splitting and composition to combine private nuisance and second-stage algorithms, and evaluate their accuracy empirically. [Private causal metalearners](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v177/niu22a.html). \citet[][Theorem 1]{SchroderMelnychukFeuerriegel2025} establish approximate-private heterogeneous-effect releases using influence-function sensitivity: their Gaussian noise scale is \(5\gamma_{\mathrm{IF}}\sqrt{2\log n\log(2/\delta)}/(n\epsilon)\), where \(\gamma_{\mathrm{IF}}\) measures empirical influence-function sensitivity, \(n\) is second-stage sample size, and \(\delta\) is the approximate-privacy parameter. Their subsequent results address orthogonality and RKHS function release. [Private heterogeneous-effect learners](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.03486v1). For weighted average effects, \citet[][Theorem 3.2]{Ohnishi2024} report mean squared error \(O(n^{-1}+(n\epsilon)^{-2})\) under their scoring-rule, overlap, and sieve regularity conditions with fixed privacy allocation; \citet[][Theorem 3.3]{Guha2025} establish pure-private sample-and-aggregate releases of binary-outcome weighted effects and variance information. [Covariate balancing](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.14789v1), [binary weighted effects](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.14766). \citet[][Theorems 1--2]{Lebeda2025} provide Gaussian-private model-agnostic ATE inference under consistency and nuisance-rate conditions, while \citet{Yuan2025} develop a separate matching-based ATE framework bearing the name PrivATE. \citet[][Theorem 3]{AsiaeeAryan2026} analyze causal-workload synthetic data through an error decomposition covering sampling, privacy, approximation, and calibration. These studies situate the present finite-sample pure-private point-CATE frontier among private learner, average-effect, and synthetic-data approaches. [Model-agnostic inference](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.19589v1), [matching-based PrivATE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.14557), [causal-workload synthetic data](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08122v2).

The interval result connects to optimal recovery and honest nonparametric inference, where coverage and expected length are distinct decision criteria \citep{Donoho1994,Low1997,Cai2004}. \citet{ArmstrongKolesar2018,ArmstrongKolesar2020} develop optimal and bias-aware confidence intervals over regression-function classes. [Optimal recovery](https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1176325367), [honest adaptive intervals](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0503662), [optimal regression inference](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.06028), [bias-aware intervals](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.01200). Private inference includes smooth-sensitivity and asymptotically efficient procedures \citep{Nissim2007,Smith2011,AvellaMedina2021}, finite-sample Gaussian-mean intervals \citep{Karwa2018}, and nonparametric median intervals \citep{DrechslerGlobusHarrisMcMillanSarathySmith2022}. [Gaussian-mean intervals](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITCS.2018.44), [median intervals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10333). The causal PrivATE method of \citet[][Theorem 4.6 and Appendix B.2.3]{SchroderHartensteinFeuerriegel2025} establishes asymptotic ATE coverage with a privacy-variance correction under overlap, boundedness, and nuisance product-rate conditions. \citet[][Theorem 3.3 and Corollary 3.4]{ShohamEtAl2025} establish asymptotic subsampling-based private coverage under estimator consistency, continuous limiting distributions, and subsample-size conditions. [ATE intervals](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.21641v1), [black-box private intervals](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.01303v1). For the stated binary, fixed-overlap, rough-nuisance experiment, the present paper characterizes both minimax absolute risk and maximal expected length of uniformly honest 90% connected intervals by the numerical benchmark \(r(n,\epsilon)=\min\{1,\max[n^{-1/4},(n^2\epsilon)^{-1/7},(n\epsilon)^{-1/2}]\}\), where \(n\) is sample size and \(\epsilon\) is the pure central privacy budget. A total density-free release attains this scale, and an interval-specific simultaneous-containment argument supplies the matching length converse throughout the unknown measurable bounded-density class.
