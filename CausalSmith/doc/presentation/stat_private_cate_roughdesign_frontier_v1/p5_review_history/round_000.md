# Referee review

**Recommendation:** major_revision
**Overall score:** 6.5/10 — The verified private estimation and honest-length frontiers constitute a substantive theoretical contribution, but scope inconsistencies, delayed estimand definitions, and insufficient interpretation of the construction's numerical guarantees require substantial revision.

The paper characterizes minimax pointwise absolute risk and honest connected-interval length for a precisely specified binary causal model under pure replacement privacy. Its occupancy-weighted pair construction and matching private testing comparisons deliver the advertised rate orders under the supplied verification contract. The main statistical claims are faithful to those results, but the manuscript needs clearer model organization, closer quantitative positioning, and a calibrated account of its practical implications.

## Strengths
- Matching upper and lower orders cover every admissible sample size and privacy budget within the specified experiment.
- Shared occupancy weights support attainment uniformly over unknown measurable densities bounded above and away from zero, including admissible boundary arm means.
- The honest-length converse addresses coverage and connectedness directly, giving substantive support beyond the estimation-risk comparison.
- The manuscript carefully distinguishes pointwise identification, dataset-wide privacy, and class-wide sampling guarantees.
- Related work appears early and distinguishes pure from approximate privacy and pointwise effects from average effects.
- Ordinary contribution prose generally follows the affirmative framing contract, and internal references consistently use cleveref.

## Findings
- **[major·prose] Author footnote and Verification note** — The manuscript states, "The theorem statements and proofs are machine-checked in Lean 4," and the author footnote says that "each displayed proof renders a Lean proof." These public formalization claims conflict with the supplied honest-scope charter, which expressly excludes claiming a Lean formalization. The author footnote also promises toolchain and commit information in the verification appendix, but the appendix supplies neither.
  - *Fix:* Align the public account with the charter by removing the broad formalization claims and the unsupported appendix-metadata promise. Retain the required theorem-local Formalization scope disclosures, with their exact published-input locators, and distinguish those verification-scope disclosures from the paper's statistical contribution.
- **[major·structure] Observation model, identification, and decision criteria** — The main model and decision criteria use the treated regression, contrast, and point target before giving their defining equations. The sentence "The selected treated outcome regression completes the observational contrast" names the objects, but their mathematical relationship first appears in the appendix's point-identification lemma. Readers therefore encounter contrast smoothness, class membership, risk, and coverage before receiving a complete definition of the estimand.
  - *Fix:* Immediately after the selected density and regression definition, specify the selected treated regression and display the equations defining the observational contrast, evaluation location, and point target. Explain there that conditional expressions denote selected versions; then use the point-identification lemma for the identification conclusion.
- **[major·prose] A private release based on within-cell pairs; Discussion and limitations** — The discussion says, "Public tuning makes that guide concrete," but the numerical scale of the supplied interval is insufficiently explained. In the local branch, the stated envelope and tuning imply that the squared radius is at least 5 times 2^20 times n^(-1/2). Consequently, for every admissible budget and every sample size between 2 and 2^40, the specified interval returns the entire target range on every realization; the fallback branch also returns that range. This materially affects how readers should interpret the release-planning discussion.
  - *Fix:* Add a numerical interpretation of the existing envelope, including this conservative sample-size range of deterministic full-range intervals. Describe the planning conclusions as asymptotic order comparisons, and place the practical consequences of the conservative constants in the limitations subsection. Preserve the verified attainment and coverage statements.
- **[minor·prose] Introduction and Related work** — The Kennedy–Balakrishnan–Robins–Wasserman comparison carefully discusses model and attainment conditions but omits its specialized rate. Readers are left to infer whether the statistical term is inherited from an established rough-nuisance benchmark and how the two privacy terms extend that benchmark. The choice of nuisance order 1/10 also receives little substantive motivation.
  - *Fix:* State that their cited pointwise lower-bound rate specializes to n^(-1/4) under the corresponding dimension and smoothness indices, and explain the rough-nuisance threshold motivating this example. Preserve the distinction between their broader lower-bound experiment and the present binary bounded-positive-density class. Identify the occupancy-based attainment and the two budget-dependent scales as the additional contributions established here.
- **[minor·structure] Observation model, identification, and decision criteria** — The privacy assumption appears amid statistical-class restrictions before its kernel, output space, and adjacency notation are introduced. Conversely, the causal-law class definition appears inside the private-releases subsection. Separate coordinate definitions and repeated explanations further interrupt the model's logical progression.
  - *Fix:* Collect causal coordinates, selected regressions, statistical restrictions, and the causal-law class in the model subsections. Introduce adjacency, output spaces, and randomized kernels before the privacy assumption, then present the private procedure class and decision criteria. Consolidate the coordinate explanations while retaining their anchored objects.
- **[minor·structure] Global** — The same benchmark, tuning branches, privacy domain, and sampling-randomization convention are repeatedly restated in introductions, algorithms, theorem discussions, and appendices. These repetitions obscure the central bias–occupancy–privacy argument and make the manuscript substantially longer than its conceptual structure requires.
  - *Fix:* Keep complete conditions in the anchored formal statements, but shorten surrounding prose through exact cleveref references. Organize the narrative around the common occupancy scale, the four envelope terms, the three rate scales, and the interval-specific containment argument. Consolidate repeated explanations of published inputs while preserving citations at their uses and all required scope footnotes.
- **[minor·prose] A private release based on within-cell pairs; Population pair moments and occupancy** — The symbol b denotes both the conservative square-root bias allowance and the distinct cellwise bound 3h plus 24 times the cell width to the power 1/5. The appendix acknowledges the change, but the interval algorithm refers generically to "the bias envelope b," leaving its intended meaning unnecessarily ambiguous.
  - *Fix:* Use distinct names for the cellwise bias certificate and the conservative allowance incorporated into the public envelope, and state their relationship once. Apply the distinction consistently in the explanatory prose and algorithm inputs.

## Questions for authors
- What motivates the particular nuisance order 1/10 as the paper's principal econometric example, beyond its usefulness for exhibiting the rough-nuisance regime?
- How should an empirical reader use the sample-size and budget guide given the deterministic full-range behavior of the certified interval over the sample-size range identified above?
