\section{Main results: law stability and estimation}

The preceding section reduced the proxy causal model to the observable summary \(S(P)\) and the quotient law \(\nu_P\) of latent-class average treatment effects. This section gives the population stability statement that makes the quotient law a regular law-level target, then turns the modulus into two estimators: a nearest-summary repair estimator defined on the compact summary closure and a finite structured-lattice estimator evaluated from the empirical summary with supplied \(k,d_x,d_z,L,\pi_0,\sigma_0\). The constants below depend only on those fixed dimensions and conditioning constants.

\begin{definitionv}{def:concentration-constant}[Simultaneous-concentration constant \(C_0\)]
Fix a simultaneous-concentration constant
\[
C_0\ge 1.
\]
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{def:summary-radius}[Summary radius \(r_{n,\alpha}\)]
For \(n\ge 1\) and \(0<\alpha<1/2\), the simultaneous concentration radius is
\[
r_{n,\alpha}
:=
C_0L\sqrt{\frac{\log(C_0/\alpha)}{n}}.
\]
\end{definitionv}

The constant \(C_0\) calibrates a uniform finite-sample concentration inequality for all five coordinates of \(\widehat S_n\). The radius \(r_{n,\alpha}\) is the corresponding \(d_S\)-radius at confidence level \(1-\alpha\), with the model envelope \(L\) scaling the observable moment fluctuations.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_27}[Ambient summary space]
For fixed proxy dimensions \(d_x\) and \(d_z\), the ambient five-block observable-summary space is
\[
\mathscr S_{\mathrm{all}}
:=
(\mathbb R^{d_z\times d_x})^4\times\mathbb R^{d_x}.
\]
A point \(s\in\mathscr S_{\mathrm{all}}\) is written \(s=(s_1,s_2,s_3,s_4,s_5)\), with four matrix blocks and one target-proxy mean block, and the summary metric is
\[
d_S(s,s'):=\sum_{j=1}^{4}\|s_j-s'_j\|_{\mathrm{op}}+\|s_5-s'_5\|_2 .
\]
The admissible image \(\mathscr S\) and its closure \(\mathcal K=\overline{\mathscr S}\) are subsets of \(\mathscr S_{\mathrm{all}}\), and the repair selector \(\Pi\) is a total Borel map from \(\mathscr S_{\mathrm{all}}\) to \(\mathscr S_{\mathrm{all}}\).
\end{definitionv}

The ambient space in \cref{obj:synth_27} lets the empirical summary be evaluated for every sample, while the feasible population summaries remain restricted to the compact closure \(\mathcal K\) from \cref{obj:prop:summary-closure-compact}. The repair construction projects an empirical summary back to that population domain before applying the quotient-law functional.

\begin{algorithmv}{def:summary-space-repair}[Summary-space repair map]
Fix \(k,d_x,d_z,n\in\mathbb N\) and \(L,\pi_0,\sigma_0\in\mathbb R\). Let
\[
L_\tau=\frac{4L\sqrt{d_z}}{\sigma_0},
\qquad
\mathcal K=\overline{\mathscr S},
\]
where \(\mathscr S\) is the admissible image in the five-block summary space. A summary-space repair datum consists of \(k>0\), \(L_\tau\ge 0\), and maps
\[
\overline F:\mathcal K\to \mathcal P_k([-L_\tau,L_\tau]),
\qquad
\Pi:\mathscr S_{\mathrm{all}}\to \mathscr S_{\mathrm{all}},
\]
with the following certified properties:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Extension.)} The map \(\overline F\) is continuous and, for every full-data law \(P\) in the uniformly conditioned model class, sends its observable summary \(S(P)\in\mathcal K\) to the quotient latent-effect law \(\nu_P\).
\item \textbf{(Nearest selector.)} The map \(\Pi\) is Borel measurable and, whenever \(\mathcal K\ne\varnothing\),
\[
\Pi(s)\in\mathcal K,
\qquad
d_S(\Pi(s),s)\le d_S(q,s)\quad\text{for every }q\in\mathcal K .
\]
\item \textbf{(Fallback.)} Whenever \(\mathcal K=\varnothing\), the selector satisfies
\[
\Pi(s)=0
\]
in the ambient five-block summary space for every \(s\).
\item \textbf{(Repair measurability.)} The sample-to-law map defined below is Borel measurable.
\end{itemize}
For an observed sample \(\omega:\{0,\ldots,n-1\}\to O_{d_x,d_z}\) with empirical five-block summary \(\widehat S_n(\omega)\), the repaired estimator is the total map
\[
\widehat\nu_n(\omega)=
\begin{cases}
\delta_0, & \mathcal K=\varnothing,\\
\overline F\bigl(\Pi(\widehat S_n(\omega))\bigr), & \mathcal K\ne\varnothing .
\end{cases}
\]
\end{algorithmv}

The estimator in \cref{obj:def:summary-space-repair} is the repaired-summary route: it expresses the quotient-law estimator as a continuous extension evaluated at the nearest feasible summary. This directly links observable-summary uncertainty to \(W_1\) uncertainty for the law.

The next construction replaces nearest-summary projection with a finite exhaustive lattice over the spectral factors that determine the quotient law.

\begin{definitionv}{def:structured-lattice-constant}[Structured-lattice constant \(C_{\mathrm{lat}}\)]
The structured-lattice constant is
\[
C_{\mathrm{lat}}
=
C_{\mathrm{lat}}(k,d_x,d_z,L,\pi_0,\sigma_0),
\]
the explicit prescribed constant given by the maximum of the threshold-stability constants and the grid-approximation constants displayed in the structured-lattice construction.
\end{definitionv}

\begin{algorithmv}{def:structured-lattice-program}[Structured lattice program]
For integers \(k,d_x,d_z,n\), constants \(L,\pi_0,\sigma_0\), and
\[
L_\tau:=4L\sqrt{d_z}/\sigma_0,
\]
a structured-lattice estimator \(A\) at radius \(L_\tau\) is prescribed when, writing \(N_A\) for the candidate count recorded in \(A\), there exist candidates
\[
\vartheta_i=(V_i,R_i,p_i,\tau_i),\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,N_A\},
\]
and, for every sample \(\omega=(O_1,\ldots,O_n)\) of records in \(O_{d_x,d_z}\), a selected index
\[
i_\omega\in\{1,\ldots,N_A\},
\]
such that:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Well-formed exhaustive list.)} Each listed candidate \(\vartheta_i\) is well formed for the parameters \((n,L,\pi_0,\sigma_0)\). Conversely, every well-formed structured lattice point \(\vartheta=(V,R,p,\tau)\) at radius \(L_\tau\) appears at some listed index \(i\) with
\[
V_i=V,\qquad R_i=R,\qquad p_i=p,\qquad \tau_i=\tau .
\]
\item \textbf{(No duplicates.)} If two listed candidates agree in all four displayed coordinates,
\[
V_i=V_j,\qquad R_i=R_j,\qquad p_i=p_j,\qquad \tau_i=\tau_j,
\]
then \(i=j\).
\item \textbf{(Lexicographic order.)} The index order agrees exactly with the lexicographic order on structured lattice points:
\[
i\le j
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\vartheta_i\le_{\mathrm{lex}}\vartheta_j .
\]
\item \textbf{(First minimizer.)} For the empirical summary \(s=\widehat S_n(\omega)\), the selected index minimizes the criterion \(J_n\) computed with threshold \(\pi_0\sigma_0^2/2\):
\[
J_n(\vartheta_{i_\omega};\widehat S_n(\omega))
\le
J_n(\vartheta_i;\widehat S_n(\omega))
\qquad\text{for every }i\in\{1,\ldots,N_A\}.
\]
Ties are resolved by the first lexicographic index:
\[
J_n(\vartheta_{i_\omega};\widehat S_n(\omega))
=
J_n(\vartheta_i;\widehat S_n(\omega))
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
i_\omega\le i .
\]
\item \textbf{(Returned law.)} On every sample \(\omega\), the estimator map recorded in \(A\) returns the atomic effect law attached to the selected candidate:
\[
\widehat\lambda_n(\omega)=\lambda_{\vartheta_{i_\omega}} .
\]
\end{itemize}
\end{algorithmv}

The lattice program in \cref{obj:def:structured-lattice-program} searches over discretized signal bases, conditioning matrices, latent masses, and effect locations. Its criterion matches the empirical compressed operator, target-proxy mean, and anchor equation, so the returned law is tied to the same observable structure as the population quotient law.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:uniform-summary-concentration}[Uniform summary concentration]
Let \(k,d_x,d_z\in\mathbb N\), and let \(\pi_0,\sigma_0\in\mathbb R\). Suppose that
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Dimensions.)} \(2\le k\), \(k\le d_x\), and \(k\le d_z\).
\item \textbf{(Positivity calibration.)} \(0<\pi_0\) and
\[
\pi_0\le \frac{1}{2k}.
\]
\item \textbf{(Rank-margin calibration.)} \(0<\sigma_0\) and \(\sigma_0\le 1\).
\end{itemize}
Then there exists a simultaneous-concentration constant \(C_0\in\mathbb R\) with \(1\le C_0\) such that the following holds. For every \(L\in\mathbb R\) and \(n\in\mathbb N\) with \(1\le L\) and \(1\le n\), every full-data probability law \(P\) on records \((U,T,X,Z,Y(0),Y(1),Y)\in \{1,\ldots,k\}\times\{0,1\}\times\mathbb R^{d_x}\times\mathbb R^{d_z}\times\mathbb R^3\) in the uniformly conditioned proxy causal model class \(\mathcal M\) with radius \(L\), latent-arm positivity margin \(\pi_0\), and proxy-rank singular-value margin \(\sigma_0\), and every tail probability \(\eta\) satisfying \(0<\eta<1/2\),
\[
Q_P^{(n)}\left\{d_S(\widehat S_n,S(P))>
C_0L\sqrt{\frac{\log(C_0/\eta)}{n}}\right\}\le \eta .
\]
Moreover, for each treatment arm \(t\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
Q_P^{(n)}\{N_{t,n}=0\}
=
\left(1-P_O\{O:T=t\}\right)^n
\le
(1-k\pi_0)^n .
\]
\end{lemmav}

\Cref{obj:lem:uniform-summary-concentration} is the sampling input for the law estimators. It gives a single high-probability radius for the empirical five-block summary and also bounds the probability of an empty treatment arm under the total empirical-summary convention.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:gap-free-positive-measure-modulus}[Gap-free modulus extension]
Fix natural numbers \(k,d_x,d_z\) and real constants \(L,\pi_0,\sigma_0\).  Assume:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Latent dimension.)} \(2\le k\), \(k\le d_x\), and \(k\le d_z\).
\item \textbf{(Bound and margins.)} \(1\le L\), \(0<\pi_0\le (2k)^{-1}\), and \(0<\sigma_0\le 1\).
\end{itemize}
Let \(L_\tau=4L\sqrt{d_z}/\sigma_0\), and let \(\mathcal K=\overline{\mathscr S}\) be the summary closure from \cref{obj:def:summary-closure}.  For two summaries
\[
s=(M_0,M_1,N_0,N_1,m_X),
\qquad
q=(M'_0,M'_1,N'_0,N'_1,m'_X),
\]
write
\[
d_S(s,q)
=
\|M_0-M'_0\|+\|M_1-M'_1\|
+\|N_0-N'_0\|+\|N_1-N'_1\|
+\Bigl(\sum_i (m_X(i)-m'_X(i))^2\Bigr)^{1/2}.
\]
There exists a constant \(C_{\mathrm{mod}}>0\) such that, for every pair of full-data laws \(P,Q\in\mathcal M\),
\[
W_1(\nu_P,\nu_Q)\le C_{\mathrm{mod}}\,d_S(S(P),S(Q)).
\]
Moreover, there is a map
\[
\overline F:\mathcal K\to \mathcal P_k([-L_\tau,L_\tau])
\]
such that \(\overline F\) is continuous and Borel measurable,
\[
W_1(\overline F(q),\overline F(q'))
\le C_{\mathrm{mod}}\,d_S(q,q')
\qquad\text{for all }q,q'\in\mathcal K,
\]
and
\[
\overline F(S(Q))=\nu_Q
\qquad\text{for every }Q\in\mathcal M.
\]
This map is unique among continuous maps \(G:\mathcal K\to\mathcal P_k([-L_\tau,L_\tau])\) that obey
\[
W_1(G(q),G(q'))
\le C_{\mathrm{mod}}\,d_S(q,q')
\qquad\text{for all }q,q'\in\mathcal K
\]
and satisfy \(G(S(Q))=\nu_Q\) for every \(Q\in\mathcal M\): every such \(G\) satisfies \(G(q)=\overline F(q)\) for all \(q\in\mathcal K\).
\end{theoremv}

\Cref{obj:thm:gap-free-positive-measure-modulus} is the population result behind the statistical theory. It establishes a Lipschitz \(W_1\) modulus from observable summaries to quotient effect laws over the uniformly conditioned model class, and it extends the summary-to-law map continuously from the admissible image to the compact closure. The result is stated for the law that aggregates coincident effect values, so the same bound covers homogeneous, partially colliding, and separated latent-effect configurations.

The intuition is spectral but the conclusion is metric. The proxy moments determine a compressed effect operator through the VMW moment identity \citep{VirkMazaheriWu2026}; perturbation control for finite-dimensional operators \citep{BauerFike1960,Kato1995} gives stable spectral moments; and the quotient law is compared in \(W_1\), a metric that transports aggregate probability mass between effect values. The continuous extension \(\overline F\) then makes feasible-summary projection a valid route from empirical summaries to law estimates.

\begin{propositionv}{prop:summary-repair-total-borel}[Total Borel repair]
Let \(k,d_x,d_z,n\in\mathbb N\) and \(L,\pi_0,\sigma_0,\alpha,C_0\in\mathbb R\).  Suppose that:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Dimensions.)} \(2\le k\), \(k\le d_x\), and \(k\le d_z\).
\item \textbf{(Model radii.)} \(1\le L\), \(0<\pi_0\le 1/(2k)\), and \(0<\sigma_0\le 1\).
\item \textbf{(Sample size.)} \(1\le n\).
\item \textbf{(Confidence parameters.)} The miscoverage level satisfies \(0<\alpha<1/2\), and the simultaneous-concentration constant satisfies \(1\le C_0\).
\item \textbf{(Empirical summary.)} The empirical summary map \(\widehat S_n\) of \cref{obj:def:empirical-summary} is measurable.
\end{itemize}
Then there exists repair data \(R\) of the form specified in \cref{obj:def:summary-space-repair} such that the estimator \(\widehat\nu_n\) induced by \(R\) is Borel measurable.  Moreover, writing \(\mathcal K\) for the \(d_S\)-closure of the admissible summary image, if \(\mathcal K=\varnothing\), then for every sample \(\omega\),
\[
\widehat\nu_n(\omega)=\delta_0.
\]
For every sample \(\omega\), the sharp summary-inversion confidence set \(\mathcal C_{n,\alpha}(\omega)\) associated with \(R\), with singleton value \(\{\delta_0\}\) when \(\mathcal K=\varnothing\) and otherwise consisting of the laws \(\nu=\overline F(q)\) with \(q\in\mathcal K\) and \(d_S(q,\Pi(\widehat S_n(\omega)))\le 2r_{n,\alpha}\), is nonempty.
\end{propositionv}

\Cref{obj:prop:summary-repair-total-borel} supplies the measurable total version of the nearest-summary construction. It also records nonemptiness of the exact repaired-image summary-inversion confidence set for every sample, preparing the confidence-set construction in the next section.

The structured-lattice estimator uses the same observable ingredients and replaces projection over the compact summary closure by a finite lattice search. The work count below fixes the computational scale certified here: a fixed-dimensional unit-cost exact-real benchmark, with arithmetic, comparison, singular-value, polar-decomposition, and fixed-degree root-isolation operations counted as unit primitives.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_16}[Structured-lattice work count]
For the explicit structured-lattice estimator \(\widehat\lambda_n\), let \(\operatorname{Work}_n\) denote the fixed-dimensional exact-real operation count for forming \(\widehat S_n\), enumerating the lexicographically ordered candidate lattice, evaluating the criterion \(J_n\), and returning the first minimizer \(\widehat\vartheta_n\). In the fixed-dimensional unit-cost exact-real model, \(\operatorname{Work}_n\) counts arithmetic, comparison, singular-value, and fixed-degree root-isolation operations and satisfies
\[
\operatorname{Work}_n\le C\left\{n+n^{(d_xk+k^2+2k-1)/2}\right\},
\]
for a finite constant \(C=C(k,d_x,d_z,L,\pi_0,\sigma_0)\).
\end{definitionv}

\begin{propositionv}{prop:polynomial-net-law-estimator}[Polynomial lattice estimator]
Fix integers \(k,d_x,d_z\) and constants \(L,\pi_0,\sigma_0\).  Suppose that
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Dimension.)} \(2\le k\), \(k\le d_x\), and \(k\le d_z\).
\item \textbf{(Radius and margins.)} \(1\le L\), \(0<\pi_0\le (2k)^{-1}\), and \(0<\sigma_0\le 1\).
\end{itemize}
Put \(L_\tau:=4L\sqrt{d_z}/\sigma_0\) and \(s_0:=\pi_0\sigma_0^2\).  Define
\[
\begin{aligned}
c_V&:=4\sqrt{d_xk},
&
K_D&:=\frac{4\sqrt{k}LL_\tau}{\sigma_0},
&
A_D&:=\frac{8}{3s_0}+\frac{32L}{s_0^2},\\
K_f&:=\frac{16\sqrt{d_x}\,kL^2}{\sigma_0^2},
&
c_D&:=2K_Dc_V+\frac{4k\sqrt{k}LL_\tau}{\sigma_0^2}
+\frac{2\sqrt{k}L}{\sigma_0}+\frac{kL_\tau}{\sigma_0},\\
c_m&:=2\sqrt{k}Lc_V+k+kL,
&
c_b&:=k+2\sqrt{k}Lc_V,
&
c_{\mathrm{grid}}&:=c_D+c_m+c_b,\\
B_{\mathrm{lat}}&:=2(A_D+1)+c_{\mathrm{grid}},
&
C_{\mathrm{lat}}&:=\max\!\left\{\frac{8L_\tau}{s_0},
(L_\tau+K_D+LK_f)B_{\mathrm{lat}}\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Then \(C_{\mathrm{lat}}>0\), and there is a finite constant
\(C=C(k,d_x,d_z,L,\pi_0,\sigma_0)>0\) such that, for every \(n\ge1\), there is a total Borel summary rule whose empirical-summary estimator \(\widehat\lambda_n\) is the lexicographically first minimizer over the prescribed structured lattice with effect radius \(L_\tau\), atom floor \(\pi_0\), and threshold \(s_0/2\).  For every sample, every distinct represented atom of \(\widehat\lambda_n\) has aggregate mass at least \(\pi_0\).

For every \(P\in\mathcal M\) in \cref{obj:def:model-class}, the stacked observable proxy moment at \(S(P)\), and at every summary \(s\) with
\[
d_S(s,S(P))<\frac{\pi_0\sigma_0^2}{2},
\]
has exactly its first \(k\) singular values at least \(\pi_0\sigma_0^2/2\) and all remaining singular values below \(\pi_0\sigma_0^2/2\).  The deterministic loss bound
\[
W_1(\widehat\lambda_n(\omega),\nu_P)
\le
C_{\mathrm{lat}}\left\{
d_S(\widehat S_n(\omega),S(P))+\frac{1}{\sqrt n}
\right\}
\]
holds for every sample \(\omega\), where \(\widehat S_n\) is the empirical summary in \cref{obj:def:empirical-summary}.  The candidate list and exhaustive-search operation count satisfy
\[
|\mathcal A_n|
\le C\, n^{(d_xk+k^2+2k-1)/2},
\qquad
\operatorname{Work}_n
\le
C\left(n+n^{(d_xk+k^2+2k-1)/2}\right).
\]
Finally, for every \(0<\eta<1/2\), every \(P\in\mathcal M\), and the observed product experiment \(Q_P^{(n)}\),
\[
Q_P^{(n)}\!\left\{
W_1(\widehat\lambda_n,\nu_P)>
C\sqrt{\frac{\log(C/\eta)}{n}}
\right\}
\le \eta .
\]
\end{propositionv}

\Cref{obj:prop:polynomial-net-law-estimator} establishes the empirical structured-lattice law estimator used for the algorithmic confidence set and cluster report. Its deterministic inequality converts summary error and lattice mesh width into \(W_1\) error, while its high-probability conclusion follows by combining that inequality with the summary concentration in \cref{obj:lem:uniform-summary-concentration}. The candidate bound records polynomial growth in \(n\) for fixed \(k,d_x,d_z,L,\pi_0,\sigma_0\) in the exact-real operation model stated above.

The role of the lattice is to preserve the stabilized spectral structure at empirical summaries near the model image. The singular-value threshold keeps the compressed operator in the uniformly conditioned rank-\(k\) regime; the grids over \(V\), \(R\), \(p\), and \(\tau\) approximate the population spectral representation; and lexicographic tie-breaking turns the exhaustive search into a total Borel rule.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:collision-uniform-root-n}[Collision-uniform root-n rate]
Fix integers \(k,d_x,d_z\) and constants \(L,\pi_0,\sigma_0\).  Suppose that
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Dimensions.)} \(2\le k\), \(k\le d_x\), and \(k\le d_z\).
\item \textbf{(Uniform bounds.)} \(1\le L\), \(0<\pi_0\le 1/(2k)\), and \(0<\sigma_0\le 1\).
\end{itemize}
Then there is a constant \(C=C(k,d_x,d_z,L,\pi_0,\sigma_0)>0\) such that, for every \(n\ge 1\), there exist a structured-lattice estimator \(\widehat\lambda_n\) taking values in \(\mathcal P_k([-L_\tau,L_\tau])\), with
\[
L_\tau=4L\sqrt{d_z}/\sigma_0,
\]
and a nearest-summary repair estimator \(\widehat\nu_n=\overline F(\Pi(\widehat S_n))\) from \cref{obj:def:summary-space-repair}, such that \(\widehat\lambda_n\) is the first lexicographic minimizer over a finite exhaustive structured lattice of well-formed candidates \(\vartheta\) for the criterion
\[
J_n(\vartheta;\widehat S_n),
\]
with tie-breaking by the lattice order and output \(\lambda_\vartheta\).  Both estimators are Borel sample maps.  For every \(\eta\) satisfying \(0<\eta<1/2\), every \(P\in\mathcal M\), and \(Q_P^{(n)}\)-sample,
\[
Q_P^{(n)}\!\left\{
\max\!\left\{
W_1(\widehat\lambda_n,\nu_P),
W_1(\widehat\nu_n,\nu_P)
\right\}
>
C\sqrt{\frac{\log(C/\eta)}{n}}
\right\}\le \eta .
\]
Moreover, for each treatment arm \(t\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
Q_P^{(n)}\{N_{t,n}=0\}\le (1-k\pi_0)^n .
\]
\end{theoremv}

\Cref{obj:thm:collision-uniform-root-n} is the law-estimation conclusion. Under the fixed dimension, envelope, positivity, and rank-margin conditions defining the uniformly conditioned class, both the nearest-summary repair estimator and the structured-lattice estimator achieve the same root-\(n\) \(W_1\) tail rate uniformly over \(\mathcal M\). The statement also carries forward the armwise empty-sample bound, ensuring that the total empirical-summary convention is accompanied by an explicit probability control.

The theorem separates the regular law target from label-sensitive quantities. At the level of \(\nu_P\), coincident latent-class mean effects are represented by aggregate mass at a shared support point, and the modulus in \cref{obj:thm:gap-free-positive-measure-modulus} transfers root-\(n\) summary concentration directly to \(W_1\) law error. The next section uses the structured-lattice estimator as the center of exact-real finite-constraint confidence reporting and then treats ordered weights on the gap-local stratum, where effect separation determines the relevant local scale.
