The paper sits at the intersection of potential-outcomes causal inference, proxy identification under unmeasured confounding, and finite-mixture estimation. The causal estimand language is anchored in the potential-outcomes tradition of \cite{Neyman1990,Rubin1974,Holland1986,ImbensRubin2015}, with observational-study adjustment and structural causal modeling supplied by \cite{RosenbaumRubin1983,Pearl2009}. The moment-estimation side traces to Pearson’s method of moments and Hansen’s generalized method of moments \cite{Pearson1894,Hansen1982}, while the lower-bound and local-experiment language follows Le Cam’s decision-theoretic framework \cite{LeCam1986} and the functional-estimation duality perspective of \cite{PolyanskiyWu2019}.

The proxy-causal lineage begins with effect restoration from proxy measurements in \cite{KurokiPearl2014} and the two-proxy, rank-based nonparametric identification result of \cite{MiaoGengTchetgen2018}: with at least two conditionally independent proxies satisfying a rank condition, causal effects are identified even when the proxy measurement mechanism itself remains latent. Proximal causal inference develops this into bridge-function identification and estimation; \cite{TchetgenTchetgen2024} formulates the proximal g-formula, \cite{ShiMiaoNelsonTchetgen2020} gives multiply robust double-negative-control adjustment for categorical unmeasured confounding, and \cite{CuiPuShiMiaoTchetgen2024} derives semiparametric efficiency theory and locally efficient proximal estimators for average treatment effects. Recent extensions include multiple-treatment identification \cite{MiaoHuOgburnZhou2023}, proximal individualized treatment regimes \cite{QiMiaoZhang2024}, regression-based implementations \cite{LiuParkLiTchetgen2024}, right-censored time-to-event proximal regression \cite{LiLindermanShiTchetgen2024}, efficient moment-restriction estimation \cite{AiShan2025}, and categorical-confounder mixture recovery by tensor methods \cite{SahaBatesShah2026}. The PROTECT stage-III NSCLC study of \cite{VanAmsterdamEtAl2022} is the applied consumer in this line: it estimates individual treatment effects from observational cancer data using proxies for unobserved confounding.

The latent-mixture and spectral-method lineage supplies the identification geometry. Classical mixture identifiability and mixture geometry are represented by \cite{Teicher1963,Lindsay1995}, with latent-product and repeated-measurement identification developed in \cite{AllmanMatiasRhodes2009,BonhommeJochmansRobin2016}. Econometric measurement-error and latent-type identification enter through \cite{Hu2008,HuSchennach2008,KasaharaShimotsu2009}. Spectral and tensor methods for latent-variable learning, including singular-value conditioning and moment decompositions, are represented by \cite{HsuKakadeZhang2009,Anandkumar2014,SidiropoulosBro2000}, while the perturbation analysis uses eigenvalue localization, spectral projectors, and subspace stability from \cite{BauerFike1960,Kato1995,DavisKahan1970,StewartSun1990}. In the immediate causal-mixture frontier, \cite{MazaheriSquiresUhler2025} identifies finite latent-effect mixtures through synthetic counterfactual moments and a hierarchy of causal mixture identifiability. \cite{VirkMazaheriWu2026} then constructs an exact compressed observable operator in the same proxy model: its eigenvalues are latent treatment effects, its lifted left eigenvectors recover anchor-normalized feature matrices and mixture weights, and its finite-sample theorem gives high-probability first-order recovery for separated effects, features, and simplex-projected weights under rank, boundedness, positivity, and spectral-separation conditions.

The statistical frontier on collisions and Wasserstein losses comes from finite-mixture singularity theory and optimal transport inference. \cite{Nguyen2013} uses Wasserstein metrics to relate convergence of mixing measures to divergence on mixture distributions; \cite{Chen1995,HoNguyenWeak2016,HeinrichKahn2018,HoNguyen2016} characterize how component collisions and weak identifiability change parameter rates in ordinary mixtures, with \cite{HeinrichKahn2018} giving local minimax rate \(n^{-1/(4(m-m_0)+2)}\) around an \(m_0\)-component mixing law when fitting \(m\) components under strong identifiability. \cite{WuYang2020} projects noisy moments onto a feasible moment space and attains adaptive optimal rates for Gaussian mixtures, while \cite{DossWuYangZhou2023} proves high-dimensional Gaussian location-mixture rates \(\Theta((d/n)^{1/4}+n^{-1/(4k-2)})\). \cite{ManoleHo2022} refines Wasserstein-based MLE rates by using stronger losses that track heterogeneous component convergence, and \cite{NguyenLeRinaldoHo2026} gives separation-dependent finite-Gaussian-mixture geometry. For inference, \cite{DeoRandrianarisoa2023} constructs adaptive honest confidence sets whose diameters are measured in Wasserstein distance, \cite{RobinsVanDerVaart2006} supplies the general adaptive-confidence framework, and \cite{BingBuneaNilesWeed2025} proves minimax lower bounds and data-driven confidence intervals for the Wasserstein distance between topic-model mixing measures; \cite{NilesWeedRigollet2022} provides a complementary minimax theory for Wasserstein-distance estimation under low-dimensional transport structure.

Against these strands, the present paper treats the quotient latent-effect law in the fixed-\(k\) Virk--Mazaheri--Wu proxy model as the regular object. It establishes a gap-free one-Wasserstein \(W_1\) modulus from observable summaries to the positive quotient law through simultaneous eigenvalue merges and splits, derives collision-uniform root-\(n\) estimation and honest root-\(n\)-diameter confidence sets, and supplies a computable cluster-adaptive report. For labeled effect-ordered weights, it proves the clipped inverse-gap rate \(\min\{1,(\sqrt n\,\delta)^{-1}\}\) and matches both the quotient-law and labeled-weight regimes by local lower bounds using explicit factorization-preserving two-point witnesses in the observed proxy experiment. The explicit unequal-weight \(k=2\) collision witness verifies that the collision phenomenon is compatible with full proxy rank and strict positivity, thereby placing the comparison directly inside the proxy-causal model rather than in an ordinary mixture surrogate.
