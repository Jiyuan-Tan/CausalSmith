# Referee review

**Recommendation:** major_revision
**Overall score:** 6.4/10 — The paper delivers a technically significant and unusually well-scoped collision-uniform inference result, but stale verification metadata, an oracle-contaminated algorithm definition, foundational representation ambiguities, and severe organizational redundancy prevent publication in its current form.

The paper establishes a Lipschitz observable-summary-to-quotient-law map under fixed latent cardinality and uniform positivity and proxy-rank margins, then derives root-n estimation, honest Wasserstein confidence sets, cluster reporting, and inverse-gap ordered-weight rates. The principal theorem claims and their conditions are largely faithful to the verified statements, and the explicit two-class lower bounds give convincing sharpness evidence on the stated specialization. The contribution is potentially publishable, but the manuscript requires substantial restructuring and correction of its reproducibility record.

## Strengths
- The quotient latent-effect law is a natural and important target for effect collisions, and the gap-free Lipschitz modulus is a substantive contribution.
- The abstract and introduction generally state the fixed-k, fixed-dimensional, uniformly conditioned scope accurately.
- The paper cleanly distinguishes collision-uniform law estimation from gap-dependent effect-ordered mass recovery.
- The upper bounds, confidence statements, and lower bounds are carefully qualified, with same-class minimax optimality claimed only on the displayed two-class specialization.
- The related-work table engages several close finite-mixture competitors through their experiments, targets, assumptions, and rates rather than citation alone.
- The theorem-local disclosures accurately identify the two results that depend on published VMW inputs.

## Findings
- **[major·prose] Proofs and verification note** — The reproducibility record is stale. The manuscript says that the checked declarations correspond to commit “e8453d0e3f6a7dd3f8fe166f2ba0c12c0dd50678,” whereas the authoritative current verification contract is for commit “89624484c86e86e2c4e2348f09f234a6aced1cf9.” It also says that the appendix preserves “% lean:” audit markers, but no such markers appear in the supplied proof text.
  - *Fix:* Regenerate the verification appendix from the current manifest, replace the commit and dependency metadata with the current audited values, rerun the displayed build command at that commit, and either restore the claimed audit markers or delete the sentence claiming their presence.
- **[major·structure] Confidence sets, cluster reports, and ordered weights** — The formal algorithm is presented as data-computable while its opening sentence says, “The cluster report is computed from \(\widehat S_n\), \(S(P)\), ...,” and its numbered construction includes \(E_P\), \(K_C(P)\), and \(\Delta_C(P)\). These are unknown population quantities. Later prose correctly calls them theorem-side validation objects, but that clarification conflicts with the algorithm as written.
  - *Fix:* Split the material into two environments: define the reported components, support intervals, and mass intervals using only the sample, supplied constants, lattice center, and outer confidence set; then define \(E_P\), \(K_C(P)\), and \(\Delta_C(P)\) separately as population validation quantities used by the validity theorem.
- **[major·structure] Setup and assumptions** — The setup violates defined-before-use discipline and obscures the model's economic logic. Thresholded inverses, transport plans, endpoint feasibility, candidate counts, quotient topology, and lattice operators appear before the observed record, model assumptions, summary, and estimand are formally defined. The section also duplicates the empirical summary, summary radius, KL divergence, matrix action, and representation discussion.
  - *Fix:* Rebuild the section in dependency order: records and laws; causal and proxy assumptions; latent parameters; population moments and summary; quotient target and Wasserstein loss; model class and gap stratum; empirical summary. Move lattice, confidence, transport-program, and exact-real implementation objects to the sections where they are first used, and eliminate duplicate definitions.
- **[major·statement] Setup and assumptions** — The raw-coordinate, valid-law, and quotient-law levels remain misleadingly notated. The environment “Atomic law coordinates” denotes an unrestricted real coordinate container by \(\mathcal P_{\le k}\), even though its weights need not be nonnegative or sum to one and its locations need not lie in the displayed interval. Earlier, \(W_1\) is defined for “represented atomic laws” without first stating validity. This makes probability-law and confidence-set statements harder to type-check conceptually, despite the later explanatory paragraph.
  - *Fix:* Give the unrestricted coordinate record a neutral symbol, define the valid labelled probability-law subtype explicitly, define its induced measure and \(W_1\), and only then introduce the quotient by measure equality. Reserve \(\mathcal P_{\le k}\) for valid probability laws and state explicitly which estimator and confidence-set objects live in the quotient space.
- **[major·prose] Main results: law stability and estimation** — The manuscript repeatedly calls the quotient law a “regular law-level target” and, in the discussion, “the regular object.” In econometrics, regularity commonly invokes pathwise differentiability, regular estimators, LAN, or stable asymptotic distributions. The verified results establish Lipschitz stability, root-n tail bounds, and a two-class minimax rate, but do not establish those stronger notions of regularity.
  - *Fix:* Replace “regular” throughout with the exact delivered properties, such as “Lipschitz-stable over the fixed-margin class” or “uniformly root-n estimable in \(W_1\).” If a technical regularity claim is intended, state and prove the relevant definition and theorem.
- **[minor·prose] Related work** — The sentence calling inverse-gap ordered-weight behavior “the direct analogue of the mixture exponents” is stronger than the comparison establishes. The cited ordinary-mixture results concern different experiments and polynomial collision singularities, while the present result is a separation-dependent weight bound in a proxy-moment experiment.
  - *Fix:* Describe the relationship as a qualitative analogue—both exhibit collision sensitivity for label-dependent targets—while explicitly preserving the different experiments, losses, and rate geometries.
- **[minor·citation] Related work** — The positioning omits a substantive discussion of the foundational proxy-restoration result of Kuroki and Pearl and gives only a brief citation-level treatment of the nearby causal-mixture work of Mazaheri, Squires, and Uhler. The latter is especially relevant for distinguishing the present observable experiment and quotient-law target from adjacent latent-effect mixture identification.
  - *Fix:* Add a concise paragraph comparing the assumptions, observables, identified target, and collision treatment in those papers with the present fixed-margin VMW specialization; verify and provide precise locators for any detailed claims.
- **[minor·prose] Discussion, extensions, and open questions** — The paper states the strong exact conditional-independence, known-cardinality, supplied-envelope, joint latent-arm positivity, and proxy-rank conditions accurately, but offers limited substantive guidance about their plausibility, calibration, or observable implications. The brief application paragraph appropriately avoids claiming an application mapping, yet readers receive little help assessing when the theorem's model class is credible.
  - *Fix:* Add a clearly titled Limitations subsection explaining the economic meaning of each uniform margin, which quantities are supplied rather than estimated, how conservative calibration changes the radii, and what evidence would be needed to map an applied proxy design into the model class.
- **[minor·other] Confidence sets, cluster reports, and ordered weights** — The cluster report and structured-lattice estimator are central deliverables, but the manuscript provides no numerical example showing their finite-sample behavior or the practical effect of the \(4\rho_{n,\alpha}\) merge threshold. The witness tables are deterministic reading guides rather than evaluations of the procedure.
  - *Fix:* Add a reproducible two-class simulation spanning collision, near-collision, and separated regimes, reporting Wasserstein error, confidence-set coverage and diameter, component merges, mass-interval widths, candidate counts, and runtime.
- **[nit·structure] Spectral repair and finite-net benchmark** — The “Constructive repair handle” combines empirical spectral repair with an unrelated lower-bound score-cancellation path in item 4, making the object appear to be a single algorithm when it bundles two logically separate constructions.
  - *Fix:* Move the score-cancellation material to the lower-bound section and retain only the spectral-moment and positive-law repair components in the repair handle.

## Questions for authors
- Is the cluster endpoint construction intended as an executable exact-real algorithm, or only as a finite constrained representation whose solution complexity remains open?
- How do the authors envision practitioners supplying defensible values of \(k\), \(L\), \(\pi_0\), and \(\sigma_0\) without compromising the advertised coverage guarantee?
- Will the final archive include the exact current verification manifest, dependency lock information, and a successful build receipt tied to the manuscript's displayed commit?
