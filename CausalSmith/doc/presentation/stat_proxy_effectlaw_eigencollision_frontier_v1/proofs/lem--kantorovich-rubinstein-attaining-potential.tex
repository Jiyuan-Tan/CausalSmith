\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:kantorovich-rubinstein-attaining-potential}]
Write
\[
\nu=\sum_i p_i\delta_{x_i},
\qquad
\xi=\sum_j q_j\delta_{y_j},
\]
with \(p_i,q_j\ge0\), \(\sum_i p_i=\sum_jq_j=1\).  Define
\[
F_\nu(t):=\sum_i p_i{\bf 1}\{x_i\le t\},
\qquad
F_\xi(t):=\sum_j q_j{\bf 1}\{y_j\le t\},
\qquad
\Delta(t):=F_\nu(t)-F_\xi(t),
\]
and let
\[
s(t):=
\begin{cases}
1,&\Delta(t)>0,\\
-1,&\Delta(t)<0,\\
0,&\Delta(t)=0.
\end{cases}
\]
The selected potential is
\[
\varphi_{\nu,\xi}(x):=\int_0^x s(t)\,dt .
\]
Since \(|s(t)|\le1\), for all \(x,y\in\mathbb R\),
\[
|\varphi_{\nu,\xi}(x)-\varphi_{\nu,\xi}(y)|
=
\left|\int_y^x s(t)\,dt\right|
\le |x-y|,
\]
so \(\varphi_{\nu,\xi}\) is one-Lipschitz.  Also
\[
\varphi_{\nu,\xi}(0)=\int_0^0 s(t)\,dt=0 .
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Substrate.CollisionSafeSpectralLaw.AtomicLaw.krPotential_zero

We next record the finite one-dimensional transport identity used by the potential.  For any finite transport plan \(\gamma=(\gamma_{ij})\) from \(\nu\) to \(\xi\), put
\[
C_\gamma(t)
:=
\sum_{i,j}\gamma_{ij}
\bigl(
{\bf 1}\{x_i\le t<y_j\}
-
{\bf 1}\{y_j\le t<x_i\}
\bigr).
\]
Using the two marginal constraints of the plan gives
\[
\Delta(t)=C_\gamma(t)
\qquad\text{for every }t\in\mathbb R .
\]
Hence, with
\[
E_\gamma(t):=
\sum_{i,j}\gamma_{ij}
{\bf 1}\{\min(x_i,y_j)\le t<\max(x_i,y_j)\},
\]
we have
\[
|\Delta(t)|\le E_\gamma(t).
\]
Integrating and using
\[
\int_{\mathbb R}{\bf 1}\{\min(x_i,y_j)\le t<\max(x_i,y_j)\}\,dt
=
|x_i-y_j|
\]
yields
\[
\int_{\mathbb R}|\Delta(t)|\,dt
\le
\sum_{i,j}\gamma_{ij}|x_i-y_j|.
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Substrate.CollisionSafeSpectralLaw.AtomicLaw.integral_abs_cdfGap_le_transportCost

Conversely, the finite transport polytope is compact and the quadratic cost
\[
\sum_{i,j}\gamma_{ij}(x_i-y_j)^2
\]
therefore has a minimizer.  If such a minimizer sent positive mass across some cut in both directions, shifting
\(\varepsilon=\min\{\gamma_{ij},\gamma_{kl}\}\) from the crossing pairs \((i,j),(k,l)\) to \((i,l),(k,j)\) would preserve the two marginals and decrease the quadratic cost by
\[
2\varepsilon(x_k-x_i)(y_j-y_l)>0,
\]
a contradiction.  Thus the minimizer has no counterflow across any cut, and for that plan \(|C_\gamma(t)|=E_\gamma(t)\) for every \(t\).  Consequently
\[
\sum_{i,j}\gamma_{ij}|x_i-y_j|
=
\int_{\mathbb R}E_\gamma(t)\,dt
=
\int_{\mathbb R}|\Delta(t)|\,dt .
\]
Together with the preceding inequality and \cref{obj:def:wasserstein-distance}, this gives
\[
W_1(\nu,\xi)=\int_{\mathbb R}|\Delta(t)|\,dt .
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Substrate.CollisionSafeSpectralLaw.AtomicLaw.w1_eq_integral_abs_cdfGap

It remains to evaluate the potential.  For an atom \(a\), set
\[
\chi_a(t):=
{\bf 1}\{0\le t<a\}-{\bf 1}\{a\le t<0\}.
\]
Then
\[
\varphi_{\nu,\xi}(a)=\int_{\mathbb R}s(t)\chi_a(t)\,dt .
\]
Interchanging the finite sums with the integral gives
\[
\int \varphi_{\nu,\xi}\,d\nu-\int \varphi_{\nu,\xi}\,d\xi
=
\int_{\mathbb R}s(t)
\left\{
\sum_i p_i\chi_{x_i}(t)-\sum_j q_j\chi_{y_j}(t)
\right\}\,dt .
\]
For \(t\ge0\), the expression in braces equals
\((1-F_\nu(t))-(1-F_\xi(t))=-\Delta(t)\); for \(t<0\), it equals
\(-F_\nu(t)+F_\xi(t)=-\Delta(t)\).  Therefore
\[
\int \varphi_{\nu,\xi}\,d\nu-\int \varphi_{\nu,\xi}\,d\xi
=
-\int_{\mathbb R}s(t)\Delta(t)\,dt
=
-\int_{\mathbb R}|\Delta(t)|\,dt .
\]
Taking absolute values and using the preceding display for \(W_1\) gives
\[
W_1(\nu,\xi)
=
\left|
  \int \varphi_{\nu,\xi}\,d\nu
  -
  \int \varphi_{\nu,\xi}\,d\xi
\right|.
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Substrate.CollisionSafeSpectralLaw.AtomicLaw.krPotential_attains

Finally, suppose \(\epsilon\in\mathbb R\) and every normalized one-Lipschitz test function satisfies the displayed bound.  Applying that bound to the just-constructed \(\varphi_{\nu,\xi}\), which is one-Lipschitz and satisfies \(\varphi_{\nu,\xi}(0)=0\), gives
\[
W_1(\nu,\xi)
=
\left|
  \int \varphi_{\nu,\xi}\,d\nu
  -
  \int \varphi_{\nu,\xi}\,d\xi
\right|
\le \epsilon .
\]
% lean: CausalSmith.Stat.ProxyEffectlawEigencollisionFrontier.krPotential_attains_with_normalization
\end{proof}
