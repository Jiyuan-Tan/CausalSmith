\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:proxy-coordinate-envelopes}]
Since \(2\le k\le d_x\), the first target-proxy coordinate is available; write it as coordinate \(1\). We use the following coordinate bound: for any real \(d_z\times d_x\) matrix \(A\),
\[
|A_{j i}|\le \|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\qquad
(1\le j\le d_z,\ 1\le i\le d_x).
\]
Indeed, with \(e_i\) the \(i\)th Euclidean basis vector,
\[
|A_{j i}|
=
|(Ae_i)_j|
\le
\|Ae_i\|_2
\le
\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|e_i\|_2
=
\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}} .
\] % lean: abs_matrix_entry_le_matrixCLM_norm

On the probability-one event from \cref{obj:ass:bounded-x},
\[
\|X\|_2\le L .
\]
Hence, for every target-proxy coordinate \(i\),
\[
|X_i|\le \|X\|_2\le L .
\] % lean: proxy_coordinate_bounds_of_model

On the probability-one event where \cref{obj:ass:anchor,obj:ass:bounded-proxy-product} both hold,
\[
X_1=1,
\qquad
\|ZX^\top\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le L .
\]
For every reference-proxy coordinate \(j\), the \((j,1)\) entry of \(ZX^\top\) is \(Z_jX_1=Z_j\), so the coordinate bound gives
\[
|Z_j|
=
|(ZX^\top)_{j1}|
\le
\|ZX^\top\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le L .
\] % lean: proxy_coordinate_bounds_of_model

On the probability-one event where \cref{obj:ass:anchor,obj:ass:bounded-outcome-proxy-product} both hold,
\[
X_1=1,
\qquad
\|YZX^\top\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le L .
\]
For every reference-proxy coordinate \(j\), the \((j,1)\) entry of \(YZX^\top\) is \(YZ_jX_1=YZ_j\). Therefore
\[
|YZ_j|
=
|(YZX^\top)_{j1}|
\le
\|YZX^\top\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le L .
\] % lean: proxy_coordinate_bounds_of_model

The three displayed coordinatewise bounds hold on probability-one events. Since the coordinate sets are finite, these are exactly
\[
\Pr_P\{\,|X_i|\le L\text{ for every }1\le i\le d_x\,\}=1,
\]
\[
\Pr_P\{\,|Z_j|\le L\text{ for every }1\le j\le d_z\,\}=1,
\]
and
\[
\Pr_P\{\,|YZ_j|\le L\text{ for every }1\le j\le d_z\,\}=1 .
\] % lean: proxy_coordinate_bounds_of_model
\end{proof}
