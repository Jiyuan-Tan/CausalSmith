\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{obj:lem:path-local-weight-experiment}]
The bound \(|h|\le 1/100\) gives \(-1/100\le h\le 1/100\), hence also \(h\in[-1,1]\).  On this range the latent masses
\[
\frac25+h,\qquad \frac35-h
\]
belong to \([0,1]\).  The treatment Bernoulli probabilities are obtained by dividing
\[
\frac4{25}+\frac35h,\qquad \frac9{25}-\frac35h
\]
by the corresponding latent masses, and the same bounds on \(h\) make each numerator nonnegative and no larger than its denominator.  The target-proxy probabilities
\[
\frac15,\qquad \frac{12/25-h/5}{3/5-h}
\]
also lie in \([0,1]\).  For the reference-proxy probabilities, \(|h|\le1/100\) gives positive denominators and
\[
0\le
\frac{225h+28}{20(15h+4)}
\le 1,
\qquad
0\le
\frac{35h+9}{10(5h+3)}
\le 1,
\]
while the remaining displayed values \(3/4\) and \(7/10\) already lie in \([0,1]\).  Finally \(1/4\) and \(1/2\pm g/2\) are Bernoulli probabilities because \(0\le g\le 1/4\).  Thus all finite product weights defining \(P_{g,h}\) in \cref{obj:synth_14} are nonnegative, and summing those product weights over
\[
u\in\{1,2\},\qquad t,x,z,y_0,y_1\in\{0,1\}
\]
leaves the two latent masses, whose sum is one.  Hence \(P_{g,h}\) is a probability law.  The same argument applies at \(h=0\), and \cref{obj:synth_14} gives
\[
P_{g,0}=P^{\mathrm{wit}}_{g/2}.
\] % lean: pathLaw_isProbabilityMeasure pathLaw_zero

By \cref{obj:lem:path-gap-stratum-membership}, the hypotheses \(0<g\le 1/4\) and \(|h|\le 1/100\) imply
\[
P_{g,h}\in\mathcal M(g)
\]
with \(L=2\), \(\pi_0=1/10\), and \(\sigma_0=1/10\).  The local-radius hypothesis supplies
\[
0<c_{\mathrm{loc}}<1 .
\] % lean: path_gapStratum path_localWeightExperiment

It remains to verify the local observed-neighborhood inequality.  Let
\[
\mathsf{Cell}_{\mathrm{vis}}:=\{0,1\}^4 .
\]
For \((\bar t,\bar x,\bar z,\bar y)\in\mathsf{Cell}_{\mathrm{vis}}\), define
\[
\operatorname{vis}_{g,h}(\bar t,\bar x,\bar z,\bar y)
=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle
\sum_{u=1}^{2}\sum_{\bar y_0\in\{0,1\}}
P_{g,h}(U=u,T=1,X_2=\bar x,Z_2=\bar z,Y(0)=\bar y_0,Y(1)=\bar y),
& \bar t=1,\\[0.9em]
\displaystyle
\sum_{u=1}^{2}\sum_{\bar y_1\in\{0,1\}}
P_{g,h}(U=u,T=0,X_2=\bar x,Z_2=\bar z,Y(0)=\bar y,Y(1)=\bar y_1),
& \bar t=0 .
\end{cases}
\]
Let \(\mathsf{Vis}_{g,h}\) be the atomic law on \(\mathsf{Cell}_{\mathrm{vis}}\) with these singleton masses.  The map
\[
\Psi_{\mathrm{vis}}(\bar t,\bar x,\bar z,\bar y)
=
\bigl(\bar t,(1,\bar x)^\top,(1,\bar z)^\top,\bar y\bigr)
\]
pushes \(\mathsf{Vis}_{g,h}\) to the observed margin:
\[
(\mathsf{Vis}_{g,h})\circ\Psi_{\mathrm{vis}}^{-1}=(P_{g,h})_O .
\]
Therefore data processing gives
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left((P_{g,h})_O\,\Vert\,(P_{g,0})_O\right)
\le
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(\mathsf{Vis}_{g,h}\,\Vert\,\mathsf{Vis}_{g,0}\right).
\] % lean: pathVisibleLaw pathVisibleLaw_singleton pathVisibleLaw_map InformationTheory.klDiv_map_le pathLaw_observed_kl_bound

At \(h=0\), direct expansion of the sixteen visible cells gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
\bar t=0,\;(\bar x,\bar z) & \bar y=0 & \bar y=1\\ \hline
(0,0) & 279/2500 & 93/2500\\
(0,1) & 171/2500 & 57/2500\\
(1,0) & 171/2500 & 57/2500\\
(1,1) & 279/2500 & 93/2500
\end{array}
\qquad
\begin{array}{c|cc}
\bar t=1,\;(\bar x,\bar z) & \bar y=0 & \bar y=1\\ \hline
(0,0) & (253+163g)/5000 & (253-163g)/5000\\
(0,1) & (247-23g)/5000 & (247+23g)/5000\\
(1,0) & (29-16g)/625 & (29+16g)/625\\
(1,1) & (71-64g)/625 & (71+64g)/625 .
\end{array}
\]
Since \(0\le g\le 1/4\), every entry in these two tables is at least \(1/1000\).  Hence
\[
\mathsf{Vis}_{g,0}\{(\bar t,\bar x,\bar z,\bar y)\}\ge \frac1{1000}
\qquad
\text{for every }(\bar t,\bar x,\bar z,\bar y)\in\mathsf{Cell}_{\mathrm{vis}},
\]
and \(\mathsf{Vis}_{g,h}\ll\mathsf{Vis}_{g,0}\). % lean: pathVisibleMass_base_floor pathVisibleLaw_base_floor pathVisibleLaw_absolutelyContinuous

For each visible cell, the same finite expansion gives the exact displacement
\[
\operatorname{vis}_{g,h}(\bar t,\bar x,\bar z,\bar y)
-
\operatorname{vis}_{g,0}(\bar t,\bar x,\bar z,\bar y)
=
\begin{cases}
(1-2\bar y)\,\beta_{\mathrm{vis}}(\bar x,\bar z)\,g h,
& \bar t=1,\\
0,& \bar t=0,
\end{cases}
\]
where
\[
\beta_{\mathrm{vis}}(0,0)=\frac3{25},\qquad
\beta_{\mathrm{vis}}(0,1)=\frac9{25},\qquad
\beta_{\mathrm{vis}}(1,0)=\frac3{100},\qquad
\beta_{\mathrm{vis}}(1,1)=\frac9{100}.
\]
All four coefficients are at most one, and \(g\ge0\), so
\[
\left|
\mathsf{Vis}_{g,h}\{(\bar t,\bar x,\bar z,\bar y)\}
-
\mathsf{Vis}_{g,0}\{(\bar t,\bar x,\bar z,\bar y)\}
\right|
\le g|h|
\]
for every visible cell. % lean: pathVisibleMass_diff_formula pathVisibleMass_diff_bound pathVisibleLaw_singleton

For finite probability laws \(\mathfrak p\ll\mathfrak q\) with positive base mass on every cell,
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathfrak p\Vert\mathfrak q)
\le
\chi^2(\mathfrak p,\mathfrak q),
\qquad
\chi^2(\mathfrak p,\mathfrak q)
=
\sum_{\upsilon\in\mathsf{Cell}_{\mathrm{vis}}}
\frac{(\mathfrak p\{\upsilon\}-\mathfrak q\{\upsilon\})^2}{\mathfrak q\{\upsilon\}},
\]
using \(\log r\le r-1\) for the first inequality.  Applying this with \(\mathfrak p=\mathsf{Vis}_{g,h}\) and \(\mathfrak q=\mathsf{Vis}_{g,0}\), the preceding floor and displacement bound yield, cell by cell,
\[
\frac{
\left(\mathsf{Vis}_{g,h}\{\upsilon\}-\mathsf{Vis}_{g,0}\{\upsilon\}\right)^2
}{
\mathsf{Vis}_{g,0}\{\upsilon\}
}
\le
1000\,g^2h^2 .
\]
There are \(16\) visible cells, and therefore
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(\mathsf{Vis}_{g,h}\,\Vert\,\mathsf{Vis}_{g,0}\right)
\le
16000\,g^2h^2 .
\] % lean: klDiv_le_chiSqDiv pathVisibleLaw_chiSqDiv_bound

Combining data processing with the last display and the assumed calibration gives
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left((P_{g,h})_O\,\Vert\,(P_{g,0})_O\right)
\le
16000\,g^2h^2
\le
\frac{c_{\mathrm{loc}}}{n}.
\]
Using \(P_{g,0}=P^{\mathrm{wit}}_{g/2}\), this is exactly the neighborhood condition in \cref{obj:ass:local-weight-neighborhood}.  Together with \(P_{g,h}\in\mathcal M(g)\), it proves
\[
P_{g,h}\in\mathcal L_{n,g}^p
\]
as defined in \cref{obj:def:local-weight-experiment}. % lean: pathLaw_observed_kl_bound ENNReal.ofReal_le_ofReal path_localWeightExperiment
\end{proof}
