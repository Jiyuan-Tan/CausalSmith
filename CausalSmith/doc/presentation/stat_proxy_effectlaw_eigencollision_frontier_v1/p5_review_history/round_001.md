# Referee review

**Recommendation:** major_revision
**Overall score:** 5.3/10 — The verified results deliver a potentially important collision-robust inference theory, but the manuscript requires substantial reorganization, sharper verification and computational disclosures, and more quantitative positioning before it is publishable.

The paper establishes a Lipschitz observable-summary-to-quotient-law map under fixed known dimensions and uniform positivity and rank margins, then derives root-n Wasserstein estimation, honest confidence sets, cluster reporting, and inverse-gap ordered-weight rates. The verification contract supports the central theorem statements and the explicit two-class lower bounds, and the prose generally respects their fixed-margin scope. The present exposition, however, obscures the contribution through duplicated and prematurely introduced definitions, conflates raw coordinates with valid and quotient probability laws, and overstates the scope of the verification and computational records.

## Strengths
- The quotient latent-effect law is a well-motivated estimand for effect collisions and gives a coherent distinction between aggregate law inference and effect-ordered weight recovery.
- The central upper bounds retain the exact fixed-k, bounded-envelope, latent-arm positivity, and proxy-rank conditions recorded in the verified statements.
- The paper supplies both collision-uniform law results and matching two-class lower bounds, including an explicit unequal-mass collision witness inside the conditioned proxy model.
- The confidence-set and cluster-report results translate Wasserstein uncertainty into interpretable aggregate support and mass statements.
- The dedicated related-work section covers proxy causal identification, spectral latent-variable recovery, mixture singularities, and Wasserstein inference.

## Findings
- **[major·prose] global** — The author note states that "The displayed formal statements and proofs are Lean-verified" and that the verification appendix records "the exact scope, toolchain, and commit." The supplied record distinguishes directly matched declarations from presentation-synthesized definitions, while the manuscript appendix supplies neither the commit identifier nor the Lean, dependency, and toolchain versions. The current wording therefore exceeds the documented verification scope and promises reproducibility metadata absent from the paper.
  - *Fix:* Narrow the verification claim to the theorem, proposition, and lemma declarations actually covered by the verification manifest; identify presentation-synthesized definitions as expository renderings; and add the exact commit, Lean version, dependency versions, build command, and a stable statement-to-source-file manifest.
- **[major·statement] Setup and assumptions** — The three representation levels are not typed clearly enough. "Atomic law coordinates" assigns the notation for at-most-k probability laws to an unconstrained pair of real vectors, even though weights may be negative, need not sum to one, and locations need not lie in the displayed interval. The subsequent Wasserstein, atom-floor, estimator, and confidence-set definitions then reuse this notation as though it denoted valid probability laws. This makes the mathematical domains of several reader-facing definitions ambiguous despite the verified results operating on valid laws and their quotient.
  - *Fix:* Introduce separate notation for the raw coordinate container, the valid labelled probability-law subspace, and the measure-equivalence quotient. Reserve the probability-law notation and W1 definition for valid laws, and revise every estimator and confidence-set codomain consistently.
- **[major·structure] Setup and assumptions** — The section introduces thresholded inverses, transport plans, VMW Assumption 4, endpoint feasibility, candidate counts, matrix actions, and lattice objectives before defining the observed record, model assumptions, observable summary, or quotient target. It also gives duplicate definitions of the empirical summary and later duplicate definitions of Kullback--Leibler divergence. The resulting order prevents readers from reconstructing the dependency chain claimed in the opening prose.
  - *Fix:* Rebuild the section in dependency order: observed and full-data records; causal and proxy assumptions; latent parameters; population moments and quotient law; model class and gap stratum; empirical summary; then spectral, lattice, confidence, and lower-bound objects in their respective later sections. Delete duplicate definitions and consolidate repeated operator and radius notation.
- **[major·structure] Confidence sets, cluster reports, and ordered weights** — The algorithm states that "The cluster report is computed from \(\widehat S_n\), \(S(P)\), ...," while the surrounding prose later calls the report data-facing and assigns \(S(P)\), \(K_C(P)\), and \(\Delta_C(P)\) to theorem-side validation. As written, the algorithm mixes observable output with unknown population quantities and therefore obscures which object a practitioner can calculate.
  - *Fix:* Split the environment into a sample-only reporting algorithm whose output uses the estimated law, supplied constants, components, support intervals, and constrained mass extrema, followed by separate validation definitions for the concentration event, true-support association, and external gap.
- **[major·citation] Related work** — The section cites the closest papers but rarely compares actual rates, losses, and conditioning regimes. In particular, it omits the explicit collision rates for overfitted ordinary mixtures, gives no quantitative account of the separated VMW recovery theorem, and does not explain precisely why root-n quotient-law recovery here is compatible with slower Wasserstein mixing-measure rates in ordinary mixture experiments.
  - *Fix:* Add a compact comparison table or focused subsection listing each closest result's experiment, target, loss, separation or identifiability conditions, and rate. Explain that this paper observes a uniformly conditioned proxy-moment operator and targets its quotient effect law, whereas ordinary mixture papers observe draws from a mixture distribution and face a different local inverse geometry.
- **[minor·prose] Confidence sets, cluster reports, and ordered weights** — The term "computable Wasserstein confidence set" is stronger than the delivered computational characterization. The verified statements give a finite constrained transport representation and polynomial candidate growth in a fixed-dimensional unit-cost exact-real model; they do not provide ordinary bit complexity for the endpoint programs or a practical implementation.
  - *Fix:* Use "finitely represented exact-real confidence set" in the main text, state the exact-real operation model and its unit-cost primitives at first use, and place bit-complexity and numerical implementation questions explicitly in the Open questions subsection.
- **[minor·prose] Introduction** — The sentence "The report pools estimated atoms at the association radius" misstates the displayed construction, which joins estimated support points at distance at most \(4\rho_{n,\alpha}\), while \(\rho_{n,\alpha}\) controls association to candidate or true support.
  - *Fix:* State that empirical atoms are connected at threshold \(4\rho_{n,\alpha}\) and that support association and interval expansion use radius \(\rho_{n,\alpha}\).
- **[minor·citation] Confidence sets, cluster reports, and ordered weights** — The sentence attributing "confidence sets obtained by inverting sample restrictions" to Robins and van der Vaart is imprecise relative to the paper's own literature map, which assigns GMM-style restriction inversion to Hansen and adaptive-confidence theory to Robins and van der Vaart.
  - *Fix:* Cite Hansen for moment-restriction inversion and cite Robins and van der Vaart specifically for adaptation and honesty; add precise locators if a more specific construction is intended.
- **[minor·prose] Setup and assumptions** — The paper alternates between one-based \(\sigma_k\) and zero-based \(\sigma_{k-1}\) singular-value indexing. Local explanations prevent a formal contradiction, but the switch is unnecessarily difficult for an econometrics reader and makes threshold statements appear inconsistent.
  - *Fix:* Use one-based singular-value indexing throughout the manuscript and confine the zero-based Lean-index translation to the verification appendix.
- **[minor·structure] global** — The manuscript offers no compact visual or worked example connecting a two-class collision, the quotient law, the Wasserstein confidence set, and the resulting component report. Given the volume of notation, the explicit witness already developed in the lower-bound section is an underused expository asset.
  - *Fix:* Add one schematic figure or worked two-class table showing separated atoms approaching collision, aggregation of their masses in the quotient law, and the cluster report before and after the merge threshold. Derive it from the existing witness without extending the theorem claims.
- **[nit·prose] Introduction** — Two consecutive sentences begin "The verification appendix records," producing an avoidable repetition and separating the two aspects of the same disclosure.
  - *Fix:* Combine them into one sentence identifying the publication-level inputs and the verified internal derivations.

## Questions for authors
- Is the intended computational contribution an exact-real finite representation, or do the authors intend to claim an implementable finite-precision procedure with controlled bit complexity?
- Can the public verification artifact expose the exact commit, toolchain lockfile, build command, and mapping from every reader-facing verified result to its declaration?
- Is the cluster report intended to be a wholly sample-computable output, with \(K_C(P)\) and \(\Delta_C(P)\) used exclusively for coverage analysis?
