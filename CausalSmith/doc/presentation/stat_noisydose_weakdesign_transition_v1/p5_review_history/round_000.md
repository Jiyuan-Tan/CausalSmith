# Referee review

**Recommendation:** major_revision
**Overall score:** 7.2/10 — The verified uniform frontier is a substantial theoretical contribution, but publication requires clearer quantitative positioning against compact-support regression and a more focused presentation.

The paper establishes matching minimax absolute-error and honest connected-interval-length orders for causal evaluation under polynomial dose-density envelopes and known Gaussian measurement error. Its uniform characterization joins direct estimation, Fourier inversion, and compact-support localization, supported by an observable construction and observed-law lower bounds. The principal results faithfully represent the verification contract, with one confidence-level overstatement; the main revision needs concern positioning and exposition.

## Strengths
- The characterization is uniform over the known error scale, includes error-free observation, and controls both transition neighborhoods.
- Achievability and the converse concern the same bounded structural class and the complete observed experiment.
- The manuscript carefully distinguishes finite-sample coverage from sufficiently-large-sample risk and length guarantees.
- The dedicated related-work section preserves important differences in experiments, losses, smoothness conditions, and operator assumptions.

## Findings
- **[major·prose] Related work** — The compact-support regression comparison leaves the most consequential quantitative contrast implicit. Jiang–Ma–Carroll is described through its conditions and 'usual spline bias and variance orders,' although the supplied comparison gives algebraic spline error bounds. Readers need those bounds to assess the contribution of the present logarithmic frontier.
  - *Fix:* Display the cited supremum-error bound O_p{(nh_b)^(-1/2)+h_b^q} and pointwise mean squared error bound O{h_b^(2q)+(nh_b)^(-1)}, preserving their source smoothness, operator, and nonsingularity conditions. Explain how those premises compare with the present measurable density envelopes and Hölder mean class, and identify the uniform noise-scale and same-class minimax characterization as the delivered advance. Preserve the distinction between the two experiments.
- **[major·structure] Global** — The exposition repeatedly restates assumptions, packet guarantees, rate branches, and coverage quantifiers across the main text and appendices. The lengthy witness statement reproduces much of the packet theorem, while several appendix subsections summarize conclusions that appear again in the deferred proofs. This repetition obscures the statistical argument for an econometrics readership.
  - *Fix:* Organize the main text around the model, frontier, explicit estimator and certificate, and observed-law comparison. Move the detailed packet construction and witness tuning into the technical appendix, retain the exact formal statements and verification disclosures, and replace repeated summaries with precise cross-references. Add one concise explanation connecting localization bias, inverse-weight variability, and moment cancellation to the three scales.
- **[minor·prose] Related work** — The sentence 'For fixed smoothness and design-decay exponents in the declared ranges and a fixed confidence level exceeding one half, \cref{obj:thm:uniform-frontier} gives matching orders' suggests a result for arbitrary fixed confidence levels above one half. The verified characterization fixes noncoverage at 1/10.
  - *Fix:* Replace the confidence-level phrase with 'at the fixed 90% confidence level.' Keep the characterization attached to the verified value of the noncoverage probability.
- **[minor·prose] Observable estimation and honest intervals** — The advertised procedure selects weights and calibrates intervals using A_q, but the main construction introduces this score by reference to an appendix lemma. Its formula, and the formula for B_q, are central to understanding both selection and coverage.
  - *Fix:* Display B_q = 64 I_(kappa+beta)(q)/I_kappa(q) and A_q = B_q + (12/b_q)sqrt(V_q/n) + 2sqrt(3/n) before dictionary selection, explicitly for n >= 3. Explain that the score and selected weight are deterministic functions of the public parameters, and retain \cref{obj:lem:public-error-certificate} for the guarantee.
- **[minor·statement] Observable estimation and honest intervals** — The numbered total-estimator procedure performs score minimization before its final empty-block branch. For the zero-sample experiment, the displayed dictionary bandwidth bound involves n^(-2), and the certificate formula involves division by n. The verified estimator branches on block nonemptiness before using the selected entry.
  - *Fix:* Immediately after forming the blocks, specify that an empty block returns the midpoint estimate and full target-range interval. Execute dictionary construction, score minimization, and empirical calculations in the nonempty-block branch, where n >= 3.
- **[minor·prose] Related work** — The sentence 'Polynomial continuation yields an upper rate governed by a logarithm divided by an iterated logarithm' is ambiguous about the direction of the estimation-error rate. The supplied Meister comparison gives a vanishing mean integrated squared error order with the iterated logarithm in the numerator.
  - *Fix:* State the density mean integrated squared error order explicitly as {(log log n)/(log n)}^(2s), with s identified as the source Sobolev smoothness and the cited theorem conditions retained. Distinguish this loss from the present pointwise absolute-error order.
- **[minor·citation] Setup and assumptions; Observed-law lower bounds** — Several assumption motivations cite Huang–Zhang without source locators. In particular, 'The binary-potential-outcome condition is standard for bounded lower-bound witnesses \citep{HuangZhang2023}' leaves the support for that attribution unclear. The supplied description of that source concerns causal deconvolution estimation and its expansion.
  - *Fix:* Check the assumption-level attributions and supply exact source locators where the cited paper supports them. Describe the numerical bounds as choices defining the present class. For the Bernoulli witness construction, either supply a source that supports the lower-bound attribution or explain its role directly as the explicit subclass constructed here.

## Questions for authors
- Which specific operator and regularity conditions account for the algebraic spline bounds in the Jiang–Ma–Carroll comparison, and how do those conditions relate to the present class?
- For representative public parameters and sample sizes, how informative are the certified intervals relative to the full target range?
