# Referee review

**Recommendation:** major_revision
**Overall score:** 6.8/10 — The verified uniform frontier is a substantial theoretical contribution, but conflicting auxiliary estimator specifications and extensive duplication materially weaken the manuscript's clarity and reproducibility.

The paper establishes matching orders for pointwise causal estimation and honest connected-interval length under bounded outcomes, polynomial design envelopes, and known Gaussian measurement error. Its principal contribution is the uniform characterization joining direct, Fourier-inversion, and compact-support regimes within one statistical class. The principal claims generally preserve the verified conditions and quantifiers, and the closest literature receives substantive treatment. Publication requires a coherent estimator specification and substantial editorial consolidation.

## Strengths
- The characterization is uniform over the maintained noise range, includes error-free observation, and supplies comparisons throughout both transition neighborhoods.
- Estimation and honest interval length are characterized over the same structural class, with finite-sample coverage distinguished carefully from large-sample rate guarantees.
- The converse evaluates complete observed samples and constructs legal bounded structural alternatives.
- The dedicated early related-work section discusses competing rates, losses, and operator conditions rather than relying on citation lists.
- The abstract and principal interpretations generally preserve envelope calibration, public parameter requirements, and the distinct ranges of the noncoverage probability.

## Findings
- **[major·statement] Observable estimation and honest intervals** — The auxiliary split-sample definition conflicts with the general estimator and the certificate attributed to it. The text says, "For n≥3, it certifies the expected absolute error of the weight-specific split-sample estimate" and directs readers to the definition displaying projection onto [1/8,7/8]. That definition also states, "The fixed normalization in this construction is" p_min c_lo = 1/16. The verified certificate concerns the estimator projected onto [0,1], with general public K. The narrower projection excludes permitted endpoint means, and the normalization introduces an additional restriction absent from the main construction.
  - *Fix:* Make the auxiliary split-sample definition display the general [0,1]-projected estimator with b_q(K)=p_min c_lo I_kappa(q), and direct the certificate discussion to that estimator. Remove the narrower projection or present it explicitly as a separate construction for a correspondingly restricted response class. Express the factor-16 denominator as a conditional reference normalization, consistent with the score definition.
- **[major·structure] global** — The manuscript repeatedly reproduces its model, witnesses, estimator quantities, frontier, and dependency narrative. Several formal environments end with cross-reference lists spanning nearly the entire paper. The setup contains two lengthy witness constructions before identification, while the lower-bound packet definition interrupts the observable-estimator section. These choices obscure the statistical argument and make the main text disproportionately difficult to navigate.
  - *Fix:* Organize the main text around the model and target, identification, frontier, estimator and interval, and converse. Move detailed witness predicates and packet formulas to the converse appendix. Consolidate overlapping witness descriptions, replace exhaustive cross-reference lists with references to immediate dependencies, and state each estimator quantity once. Retain the existing anchored labels and required theorem-local verification disclosures.
- **[minor·statement] Setup and assumptions — Structural witnesses and observed-law comparisons** — The synthesized witness definition defines P_s as the law of a five-coordinate tuple (X,A,Z,schedule,Y), whereas the structural model has six coordinates and retains U. The subsequent construction predicate correctly retains U. The surrounding transitions also describe definitions as forthcoming after they have appeared: "We first define the divergence" precedes the witness definition, and "For later use, we collect ... into a single construction predicate" follows that predicate.
  - *Fix:* Include U in the displayed structural tuple and make the witness descriptions use one consistent coordinate order. Rewrite the transitions to follow the actual order of divergence, seed representation, and construction predicate. Distinguish the predicate's representation conditions from the model-membership guarantees supplied by the witness theorem.
- **[minor·prose] Related work** — The sentence "The measurable polynomial envelopes and Hölder mean restriction used here do not impose that increasing-dimensional operator condition uniformly over the model class" uses contribution-by-negation outside the permitted limitations section. Its substantive comparison is useful, but its framing violates the prose contract.
  - *Fix:* Frame the comparison affirmatively: the present characterization is uniform over measurable densities between the stated envelopes and Hölder conditional means, whereas the cited spline rates are established under their increasing-dimensional operator and nonsingularity conditions. Preserve the distinction between the experiments, losses, and uniformity quantifiers.
- **[minor·prose] Related work — Compact-support regression comparison** — The Jiang–Ma–Carroll rate display introduces h_b and q without identifying them locally. In particular, q later denotes this paper's latent localization weight, so the source smoothness parameter is easy to confuse with the manuscript's notation.
  - *Fix:* Before the display, identify h_b as the source spline spacing and q as its mean-smoothness order. State explicitly that these are source-local symbols, or use a clearly distinguished notation while preserving the cited theorem's meaning and locators.
- **[minor·statement] Observable estimation and honest intervals — Total estimator** — The operational description branches before dictionary evaluation and selects the public-score minimizer before using outcomes. The numbered algorithm instead accepts the dictionary as an input, computes empirical moments for every entry, and selects the minimizer afterward. Its unconditional selection step also obscures the immediate small-sample fallback described in the prose.
  - *Fix:* Reorder the algorithm to form the blocks, return the fallback when a block is empty, construct and score the dictionary otherwise, select the first minimizer, and compute empirical moments for that selected entry. This presents the same verified estimator with a single unambiguous evaluation order.

## Questions for authors
- What substantive exposure or assignment mechanisms motivate the global polynomial envelopes around an interior target dose, and how would practitioners specify their public constants?
- For representative calibrated parameters and sample sizes, how informative are the explicit score-based intervals, and when does selection move between the Fourier and polynomial families?
