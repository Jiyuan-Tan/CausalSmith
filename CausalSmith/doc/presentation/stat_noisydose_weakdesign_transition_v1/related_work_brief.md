The paper belongs to the literature on causal dose-response estimation under conditional exchangeability, rooted in the potential-outcome framework and propensity-score adjustment \citep{Rubin1974,Rosenbaum1983,Imbens2015}. Generalized propensity scores and propensity functions extend this framework to continuous and general treatment regimes \citep{Hirano2004,Imai2004}; [Hirano and Imbens](https://doi.org/10.1002/0470090456.ch7) provide the canonical continuous-treatment formulation. Subsequent work combines adjustment with nonparametric localization: \citet{Kennedy2017} construct a doubly robust pseudo-outcome and smooth it over treatment, while \citet{Colangelo2026} establish asymptotic normality for cross-fitted kernel estimators under positivity, smoothness, and nuisance-rate conditions ([Kennedy et al.](https://doi.org/10.1111/rssb.12212); [Colangelo and Lee](https://doi.org/10.1080/07350015.2025.2505487)). The higher-order analysis of \citet[Theorem 3.1 and Section 3.4, arXiv version 2]{Bonvini2026} yields the conditional pointwise mean squared error bound \(O\{h^{2\alpha}+k^{-4s/d}+(nh)^{-1}\}\) in its stated equal-covariate-smoothness specialization, with negligible density remainder, suitable projection dimension \(k\), and treatment-density smoothness at least \(\alpha\). Here \(\alpha\) is treatment-regression smoothness, \(s\) is covariate smoothness, and \(d\) is covariate dimension. For \(s\ge d/4\), suitable tuning attains \(n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}\) ([source manuscript](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.11825)). The present paper characterizes evaluation at a prespecified latent dose in two observed strata, with polynomially weak support and a known Gaussian measurement channel.

The closest causal measurement-error antecedent is \citet{HuangZhang2023}. Under their positivity and smoothness conditions, they identify the average dose-response function through stabilized weights, estimate those weights using local generalized empirical likelihood, and construct a deconvolution estimator. Their supersmooth expansion, in \citet[Theorem 4.4, arXiv version 2]{HuangZhang2023}, has bias terms of orders \(h^2+h_0^2\) and stochastic remainder \(O_p\{N^{-1/2}/\min(e(h),e(h_0))\}\), where \(h,h_0\) are bandwidths, \(N\) is sample size, and \(e(b)=b^{1/2}\exp(-b^{-r}/\gamma)\) for supersmooth error exponent \(r\) and error-tail constant \(\gamma\). Its normal-limit conclusion includes an additional variance lower-bound condition. Their NHANES-I dietary-fat application supplies a concrete setting in which validated weak support and measurement precision would make the present frontier informative ([theory and application](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.04642)). Recent approaches also include the latent-treatment variational framework of \citet{Gao2024} and identification under the structured Gaussian conditional model of \citet{Pollanen2024} ([Gao et al.](https://openreview.net/forum?id=S46Knicu56); [Pöllänen and Marttinen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10614)). Classical repeated-measurement methods address channel recovery through additional observations \citep{Schennach2004a,Schennach2004b,Delaigle2008}. These strands place the present result within causal estimation using contaminated treatments; its specific contribution is matched absolute-risk and honest connected-interval-length orders over the same measurable weak-design class, uniformly across known noise scales.

The inverse-estimation lineage begins with kernel density deconvolution and the distinction between ordinary-smooth and supersmooth errors \citep{Carroll1988,Stefanski1990,Fan1991,Fan1992}, synthesized by \citet{Meister2009} and the inverse-problem survey of \citet{Cavalier2008}. For regression, \citet[Sections 3--4]{FanTruong1993} establish matching upper and lower rates under their positive-design and regression/design regularity conditions: with source smoothness order \(k\) and supersmooth exponent \(r\), the pointwise error scale is \((\log n)^{-k/r}\), specializing to \((\log n)^{-k/2}\) for Gaussian errors ([source manuscript](https://archive.ymsc.tsinghua.edu.cn/pacm_download/468/10828-Jianqing_Fan_37.pdf)). The direct weak-support benchmark is \citet[Definition 2 and Theorem 1]{Gaiffas2005}: Gaussian-response regression with local smoothness modulus proportional to \(h^s\) and design density proportional to distance-to-target raised to \(v\) has absolute-risk order \(n^{-s/(2s+v+1)}\). Its local approximation class retains degree-one polynomials at \(s=1\) ([source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0410354)). Setting \(s=\beta\), the present mean-smoothness exponent, and \(v=\kappa\), the present design-zero exponent, aligns the zero-noise powers. The supplied accepted causal artifact \citep{CausalSmithDoseResponse} provides a separate interior-dose mean squared error lower floor \(n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)}\), with the stated oracle-regime exponent alignment when \(s\ge d/4\). These are comparisons of exponents across their respective experiments and losses; the present zero-noise absolute-risk and honest-length conclusions arise from its own same-class frontier.

Known compact support supplies a second inversion mechanism. \citet[Theorems 1--2]{Meister2007} establish matching integrated squared density-error orders \(\{\log\log n/\log n\}^{2s}\) for fixed compact support and source Sobolev smoothness \(s\), under the specified error class ([source manuscript](https://www.f08.uni-stuttgart.de/.content/media/downloads/Mathematik/mathematische_berichte/2005/2005-007.pdf)). A particularly important regression comparison is \citet[Theorem 2, conditions (C2)--(C4) and (D1)--(D5)]{JiangMaCarroll2026}: their compact-support spline estimator satisfies supremum error \(O_p\{(nh_b)^{-1/2}+h_b^q\}\) and pointwise mean squared error \(O\{h_b^{2q}+(nh_b)^{-1}\}\), where \(h_b\) is spline spacing and \(q\) is source smoothness. These conclusions include bounded-operator premises and a score derivative whose singular values are bounded and bounded away from zero ([theorem and conditions](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.00793)). The present analysis combines positive latent localization with observable polynomial inverse-heat weights over arbitrary measurable density envelopes. Gaussian inversion by differentiation and unbiased normal-mean function estimation provide earlier analytic antecedents \citep{Masry1992,Stefanski1989}; moment methods for Gaussian mixtures and smoothing-divergence comparisons provide related tools \citep{Wu2020,ChenNilesWeed2021}. The packet construction draws on filtered-Jacobi localization \citep[Theorem 2.4]{PetrushevXu2005} and local kernel Lipschitz control \citep[Theorem 2.2]{https://people.math.sc.edu/pencho/Publications/kpx-06-28-06-web.pdf}, with Jacobi normalization, explicit representations, and gamma ratios anchored in the cited DLMF identities ([localization](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0508581); [Lipschitz estimate](https://people.math.sc.edu/pencho/Publications/kpx-06-28-06-web.pdf)). Durot and Mukherjee’s vanishing-noise minimax analysis concerns monotone shuffled and unlinked regression, providing a recent comparison for noise-dependent inverse rates in a different observation structure \citep{DurotMukherjee2026} ([published article](https://doi.org/10.3150/25-BEJ1952)).

The uncertainty analysis connects minimax estimation with honest inference, following the foundational work of \citet{Li1989}, \citet{Donoho1994}, and \citet{Low1997}, and the standard decision-theoretic treatment in \citet{Tsybakov2009}. \citet{ArmstrongKolesar2020} calibrate direct-regression intervals using critical values that incorporate worst-case smoothing bias ([source](https://doi.org/10.3982/QE1199)). \citet{KatoSasaki2019} establish asymptotically valid multiplier-bootstrap bands for errors-in-variables regression under ordinary-smooth error, positive design, and their regularity and auxiliary-sample conditions ([source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1702.03377)). For error-free causal curves, \citet{Takatsu2025} develop debiased local-linear pointwise intervals and uniform bands, and \citet{Doss2024} develop a doubly robust test of a constant treatment-effect curve ([Takatsu and Westling](https://doi.org/10.1093/jrsssb/qkae041); [Doss et al.](https://doi.org/10.1214/24-AOS2405)). Related support-sensitive causal targets include stochastic shift interventions \citep{Diaz2012} and continuous-exposure incremental effects \citep{Schindl2026}; the latter study exponential tilting and derive lower bounds with effective sample size \(n/\delta\), where \(\delta\) is the tilt magnitude ([source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.11967)). Functional smoothness also governs convolution-functional rates \citep{Butucea2009}. The supplied internal comparators concern a known-Gaussian true-side regression-discontinuity endpoint, a circular unknown-variance noisy-dose claim, and an integrated smooth shift-value functional \citep{CausalSmithTrueSideFrontier,CausalSmithCircularUnknownVariance,CausalSmithSmoothShiftValue}. Against these distinct targets and channels, the present paper establishes finite-sample 0.9 coverage and matching worst-law expected connected-interval length for evaluation at the weak-support latent dose, using a public expected-error certificate and an observed marked-Gaussian converse.
