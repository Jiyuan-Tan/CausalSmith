# Revision routing plan (major_revision)

## fix by hand in the authored sources (prose/structure rewrite)
- [major·structure·rewrite] (Global organization; Minimax precision and attainable estimation; Testing bounds and minimax proofs) The manuscript repeatedly defines and proves essentially the same central objects and conclusions. The upper rule, attaining rule, and precision pair repeat the estimator; the observed-record frontier and explicit frontier repeat the risk and consistency characterization; the block-testing theorem and nonlocal-testing theorem repeat the canonical-design comparison. The deferred proofs then reproduce substantial portions of the frontier argument. This obscures the contribution and makes the paper much longer than its mathematical content requires.
- [minor·prose·rewrite] (Introduction; Minimax precision and attainable estimation) The central lower-bound mechanism is introduced mainly through technical names: 'Translation bounds, all-order Walsh energy control, and hidden-allocation contraction yield the testing comparison.' Readers receive considerably less explanation of why a source's association is revealed with probability 1-(1-q)^d, why repeated recipient responses matter, and how unknown fixed baselines preserve ambiguity despite noiseless outcomes. These points explain the substantive novelty of the complete-record converse.
- [minor·prose·rewrite] (Population, causal target, and observation design; Complete-record mixture notation) The fixed-schedule experiment definition sets P_{theta,q}=Law(O,xi), whereas subsequent mixture notation treats fixed-schedule laws as laws of O and then appends an independent seed. The distinction between a record law and its seeded extension is therefore inconsistent across the exposition. This is a notation problem surrounding the verified results, rather than a challenge to their conclusions.
- [minor·prose·rewrite] (Minimax precision and attainable estimation; Edge collection and the supplied-graph benchmark) The attaining rule uses d to evaluate its envelope and choose its branch, but the observation-design exposition explicitly identifies q as known without equally clearly explaining the estimator's access to an admissible degree bound. A retained graph reveals sampled degrees, so readers implementing the planning calculation need to understand what value of d the guarantee assumes.

- [minor·citation·citation_research] (Related work) The discussion gives useful comparisons with SNIPE and incomplete-network methods, but the estimator lineage jumps directly to SNIPE. Inverse-inclusion correction also belongs to the Horvitz–Thompson tradition, and additive interference estimation has a broader linear-unbiased and representer-based lineage. Engaging these connections would make the attribution of the observable procedure and the distinction between estimator construction and the new minimax converse more precise.
