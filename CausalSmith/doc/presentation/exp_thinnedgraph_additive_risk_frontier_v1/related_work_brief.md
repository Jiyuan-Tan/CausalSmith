The paper belongs to the design-based literature on causal inference under interference, combining finite-population potential outcomes with precision analysis under a specified randomization mechanism \citep{Neyman1990,Rubin1974,Imbens2015}. Inverse-inclusion weighting follows the sampling-theoretic lineage of \citet{Horvitz1952}. Foundational interference work established how treatment assignments to other units enter causal comparisons: \citet{Sobel2006} examines housing-mobility experiments, \citet{Rosenbaum2007} develops randomization-based inference under interference, and \citet{Hudgens2008} defines direct, indirect, total, and overall effects under partial interference. \citet{TchetgenTchetgen2012} extends this framework through weighting and finite-sample inference. For general interference, \citet{Manski2013} organizes identification through restrictions on treatment response, while \citet{Aronow2017} connects exposure mappings, experimental designs, and inverse-probability estimators. The structural social-effects literature supplies complementary identification foundations \citep{Manski1993,Bramoulle2009}; its network formation and measurement issues are surveyed by \citet{Advani2018}. \citet{BhadraSchweinberger2026Survey} provides current field-wide context. Within these strands, the present paper studies the all-treated-versus-all-control population contrast under heterogeneous additive responses and a calibrated retained-edge observation channel.

Two methodological approaches improve precision by controlling either assignment dependence or response complexity. Graph cluster randomization increases the probability of relevant neighborhood exposures \citep{Ugander2013,Ugander2023}, while correlated assignments can reduce interference bias under suitable response restrictions \citep{Eckles2017}. Randomized saturation designs permit separate treatment and spillover comparisons under partial interference \citep{Baird2018}; spatially decaying interference motivates rate-optimal cluster choices \citep{Leung2022b}. A complementary asymptotic literature establishes inference under network sparsity, decaying dependence, or random-graph structure \citep{Leung2020,Leung2022a,Li2022}. \citet{Savje2021} studies assignment-marginalized own-treatment effects under limited unknown interference, and \citet{Savje2024} separates exposure-based estimands from assumptions that exposures exhaust the interference structure. Recent extensions cover regression-based analysis \citep{Gao2025}, Bernoulli-design inference under uniformly bounded neighbourhood interference \citep{Lu2026}, dense deterministic graph limits \citep{Park2026}, and neighborhood-excluded cross-fitting \citep{Yu2026}. Randomization testing supplies another complementary strand \citep{Athey2018}. These results emphasize the joint roles of estimand, response restrictions, and observation design; the present analysis fixes one half-Bernoulli experiment and characterizes worst-case squared precision over its complete observed record.

The closest estimator lineage exploits additive or low-order response structure. \citet{Sussman2017} develops linear-unbiased estimation theory under neighborhood interference; \citet{KungSussman2022AdditiveExposure} studies additive exposure models; and \citet{HarshawSavjeWang2022GeneralDesignFramework} formulates causal effects as linear functionals on potential-outcome spaces and derives representer-based estimators. The exposure-reweighted linear estimator of \citet{Harshaw2023} provides unbiased estimation and sparse-graph asymptotic inference in bipartite experiments. SNIPE constructs an unbiased total-treatment-effect estimator using known neighborhoods \citep[Sections 3--5]{CortezRodriguezEichhornYu2023SNIPE}. Its published general variance bound implies order \(D^3/n\) for interaction order one, half-Bernoulli assignment, unit coefficient mass, population size \(n\), and common augmented degree bound \(D\); its separate minimax construction establishes an \(\Omega((n\pi^\beta)^{-1})\) lower bound when \(\pi^\beta<0.16\), where \(\pi\) is treatment probability and \(\beta\) is interaction order \citep[Theorems 1--2]{CortezRodriguezEichhornYu2023SNIPE}. The supplied known-graph research note is the disclosed source of the sharper bounded-additive degree benchmark \citep{CausalSmithKnownGraph2026}. The conflict-graph design provides spectral variance bounds for arbitrary neighborhood responses \citep{Kandiros2024}; the subsequent minimax theory optimizes both designs and estimators with the network supplied. Under a second-moment envelope, its random-regular-graph global-effect comparison ranges from order \(k/n\) to \(k^2/n\), with high probability for graph degree \(k=o(\sqrt n)\) \citep[Corollary 5.4]{KandirosHarshawSavje2026DesignBasedMinimax}. These comparisons retain their respective response classes and assignment choices. Regression adjustment \citep{Chin2019} and variance-improving covariate-adjusted SNIPE \citep[Theorems 1--4]{WangLi2025CovariateAdjustedSNIPE} extend estimation within specified network experiments.

Incomplete-network methods already encompass correction, latent-network inference, sensitivity analysis, and richer experimental information. \citet[Section 3; Theorems 4--7]{LiSussmanKolaczyk2021NetworkNoise} inverts exposure-misclassification matrices and establishes consistency and asymptotic normality for corrected four-level exposure means. Its stated regimes include vanishing treatment probability, growing expected treated counts, mean degree proportional to inverse treatment probability, and specific false-positive and false-negative scaling; network replicates support estimation of error rates. This correction lineage connects to method-of-moments inference for noisy-network subgraph densities \citep{Chang2022}. Missing-link mixture models \citep{Hardy2019}, unobserved-network sensitivity analysis \citep{Egami2021}, validation-based spillover correction \citep{Chao2025}, and Bayesian inference from latent-network proxies \citep{Weinstein2025} provide additional approaches. \citet[Sections 4--5]{Weinstein2026} bounds exposure-effect bias through induced exposure probabilities and constructs an estimator that is unbiased when at least one supplied candidate network correctly specifies interference. UNITE establishes unbiased polynomial-response estimation from neighborhood supersets; its variance bound gives order \(1/n\) with fixed interaction order, treatment probability, and true and supplied-supergraph degrees \citep[Theorems 1, 4, and 8; Appendix B.1]{ShankarEtAl2024UNITE}. Model-based partial-network inference averages over plausible graph completions \citep{WilkinsReevesEtAl2024PartialNetworks}; the final single-network theorem requires graph-estimation error to be negligible relative to the dependence-adjusted score scale \citep[Assumption 3.1 and Theorem 3.2]{WilkinsReevesEtAl2026SingleNetwork}. Graph-free additive estimation also benefits from an observed population baseline \citep[Corollary 3]{YuAiroldiBorgsChayes2022}, while staggered and clustered rollout designs obtain identifying information from repeated observations \citep{CortezRodriguez2022,CortezRodriguez2024}. The present contribution characterizes bounded-additive total-effect precision when independently retained edges, assignments, and contemporaneous noiseless outcomes jointly constitute the experiment’s information.

The immediate application is the surrogate-network platform workflow of \citet{JiangDengWangWang2024ApproximateNetworks}. Under bounded outcomes and their influence restrictions, the pseudoinverse estimator has asymptotic variance \(O(d_{\mathcal G}^2/[n\pi(1-\pi)])\), where \(d_{\mathcal G}\) is surrogate-network degree; constant outcomes on a regular surrogate graph yield the matching estimator-specific variance order \citep[Assumptions 1--2 and Theorems 1--2]{JiangDengWangWang2024ApproximateNetworks}. Their additive-model relative-bias bound is \(O(\delta)\) under a weighted omitted-influence condition, with \(\delta\) measuring that omission \citep[Assumption 3 and Lemma 2]{JiangDengWangWang2024ApproximateNetworks}. The present paper’s comparison is an unrestricted minimax statement: for off-diagonal in- and out-degree bound \(d\), known edge-retention probability \(q>0\), bounded coefficient mass, and compulsory observed own-treatment coordinates, the squared-risk order is \(\min\{1,d^2/[n(1-(1-q)^d)]\}\), equivalently \(\min\{1,d^2/n+d/(nq)\}\). It couples a total observable attaining rule with a converse that retains outcome-driven graph information, using the testing-to-estimation lineage summarized in \citet[Chapter 2]{Tsybakov2009}. Full retention recovers the credited known-graph degree order, and zero retention has constant worst-case risk. The platform implication is prospective calibrated edge collection: Jiang et al.’s historical 28-day interaction filter supplies the application context, while independent thinning specifies the sampling mechanism analyzed here.
