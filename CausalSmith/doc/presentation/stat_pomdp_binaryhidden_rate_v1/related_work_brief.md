Stationary off-policy evaluation connects causal inference for sequential interventions with the statistical analysis of controlled Markov processes. The intervention interpretation follows the longitudinal framework of \citet{Robins1986}, dynamic treatment regimes \citep{Murphy2003}, and the synthesis in \citet{Hernan2020}. Its weighting and efficiency foundations include augmented inverse-probability estimation \citep{Robins1994} and semiparametric propensity-score theory \citep{Hahn1998,Hirano2003}. Markov decision processes supply the long-run policy-value framework \citep{Puterman1994}, while partially observable control and hidden Markov models supply the latent-state representation \citep{Smallwood1973,Kaelbling1998,Rabiner1989}. Within this framework, the present paper characterizes the minimax mean-squared error for stationary causal value from one logged trajectory with binary observed and hidden states, known policies randomized through the observed state, bounded action likelihood ratios, and contracting behavior and target kernels. The broader off-policy literature and its statistical distinctions are surveyed by \citet{Uehara2026}.

The methodological lineage runs from sequential importance weighting \citep{Precup2000} and doubly robust trajectory evaluation \citep{Jiang2016} to marginalized density ratios, Bellman equations, and minimax estimation \citep{Liu2018,Uehara2020,Kallus2020}. For fully observed processes, \citet{Liao2021} studies long-term average outcomes, including mobile-health applications, and \citet{Liao2022} develops average-reward policy learning. For a normalized discounted estimand, \citet[Theorem 12]{KallusUehara2022} establishes efficient root-\(T\) estimation from a stationary trajectory, where \(T\) is the trajectory length, under mixing, bounded occupancy ratios, nuisance consistency, and an appropriate nuisance-product rate. Weak stationary overlap changes this benchmark: \citet[Theorems 3.9--3.10]{Mehrabi2024} obtains, in addition to nuisance-product error, average-reward mean-squared-error terms of order \(T^{-2\delta/(1+\delta)}\), \((\log T)/T\), and \(T^{-1}\) as the occupancy-ratio tail index \(\delta\) lies below, at, or above one; root-\(T\) asymptotic normality requires the stronger-overlap regime and accompanying nuisance conditions. These results establish the fully observed long-run lineage against which the latent-state experiment can be positioned. [Published single-trajectory efficiency result](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/opre.2021.2249); [weak-overlap analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08201).

A complementary strand addresses logging mechanisms influenced by hidden variables through observable proxies. Proxy identification \citep{Miao2018}, proximal causal inference \citep{TchetgenTchetgen2024}, and its semiparametric theory \citep{Cui2024} underpin this approach. \citet[Sections 4--5]{TennenholtzShalitMannor2020} develops finite-horizon identification using observable proxy matrices. \citet[Section 4, Assumptions 2--4, and Theorems 2--4]{NairJiang2021} extends the spectral construction to histories and futures, requiring relevant observable moment matrices to have rank equal to latent-state cardinality and using projected pseudoinverses. \citet[Section 4 and Theorems 7--8]{ShiUeharaHuangJiang2022} develops discounted-value bridge estimation from independent observed transition/proxy tuples; its root-\(n\) efficiency result, where \(n\) is the number of independent samples, uses existing unique bridges, boundedness and consistency conditions, and a value-residual/weight-error product of order \(o(n^{-1/2})\). \citet[Theorems 3--4]{BennettKallus2023} obtains cross-fitted root-\(n\) asymptotic normality from independent finite-horizon trajectories under proximal identification and nuisance-product conditions; its efficiency characterization adds local operator invertibility and stability. For long-run confounded evaluation, \citet{Bennett2021} uses an identified latent model and an independent-confounder structure. Model-based bridge learning is developed by \citet{Hong2024}, and \citet[abstract]{Venkatesh2026} introduces cross-fitted estimation with an oracle-comparator error decomposition. In tabular finite-horizon confounded POMDPs, \citet[abstract]{Kuang2025} reports value-error bounds of order \(H^{3/2}/\sqrt n\), where \(H\) is the planning horizon, under rank conditions, improving to \(H/\sqrt n\) with additional concentrability. These guarantees describe proxy-information and sampling conditions explicitly, providing a precise comparison with observed-state randomization and the seven-moment stationary experiment. [Minimax bridge study](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/shi22f.html); [proximal reinforcement learning](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/opre.2021.0781); [horizon-explicit tabular bounds](https://proceedings.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/46a675d435a6c020aef3356d6c463b10-Abstract-Conference.html).

The finite-moment construction also connects to realization theory and predictive representations \citep{Ho1966,Carlyle1971,Littman2001}. Spectral hidden-Markov learning controls observable-operator estimation through rank and singular-value conditions \citep{HsuKakadeZhang2012}; generic latent-model identification is studied by \citet{Allman2009,Schoenhuth2011}, and \citet{Gassiat2016} establishes identification under full-rank transitions and linearly independent emissions. \citet{Ohta2021} studies effective realization under deterministic observations, while \citet{AbrahamGassiatNaulet2023} quantifies parameter-learning difficulty near degenerate two-state hidden-Markov configurations. Functional stability has a complementary lineage in modulus-based minimax analysis \citep{Donoho1991}, contraction theory \citep{Seneta1981}, and two-point testing \citep[Chapter 2]{Tsybakov2009}. Recent observable-value approaches sharpen the comparison: \citet[Theorem 3]{Uehara2023} gives discounted-value error of order \(n^{-1/2}\), with explicit instrument-strength and distribution-shift factors, under realization, Bellman completeness, latent-error identifiability, and coverage conditions. \citet[Theorem 7]{Zhang2024} obtains a finite-horizon bound proportional to \(H^2/\sqrt n\), up to class-complexity and coverage factors, under outcome and belief coverage. \citet[Sections 3--4]{Zhang2025} studies hardness for history-dependent policies and positive model-based results under revealing assumptions; \citet[Sections 4--5]{Zhu2026} develops discounted analysis through belief-space covering and stability. The present value modulus characterizes a scalar stationary functional uniformly across emission-rank degeneracies through a fixed recurrence. [Future-dependent value analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.13081); [coverage refinement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14703); [history-dependent policy analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.01134).

The closest stationary-trajectory frontier is \citet[Theorem 2.1, Corollary 2.2, and Theorem 3.1]{HuWager2023}. Under sequential ignorability, bounded one-step policy ratios, reward-moment bounds, and contraction, partial-history importance weighting balances exponentially decreasing truncation bias against exponentially increasing weighting variance, yielding matched mean-squared-error exponent \(\beta=2/(2+t_0\zeta)\), where \(t_0\) is the mixing scale and \(\zeta\) is the logarithmic policy-ratio bound. Its minimax class permits hidden cardinality to increase with sample size. The public manuscript \citet[Theorem 1 and the signed-depth lower-bound construction]{CausalSmithLatentOverlap2026} adds the stationary occupancy bound \(C\) and establishes the cardinality-uniform rate \(T^{-1}+T^{-\beta}q_C^{2(1-\beta)}\), where \(q_C=(C-1)/C\); its hard family has \(2(Q+1)\) hidden states with depth \(Q\asymp\log T\). The present paper characterizes the fixed four-joint-state experiment: seven action-weighted intervention moments, using one through seven action weights, support a uniform stationary-value modulus and a finite stable-grid estimator with mean-squared error of order \(T^{-1}\), matched by an all-procedure four-state converse. This characterization covers arbitrary joint reward/next-state dependence, singular emissions, vanishing behavior cells, and every subclass satisfying a bounded stationary occupancy ratio. It places fixed latent dimension alongside the growing-depth constructions as the decisive distinction between the two minimax experiments.
