Potential-outcome treatment choice begins with \citet{Rubin1974}; \citet{RosenbaumRubin1983} and \citet{HernanRobins2020} supply the exchangeability and overlap framework used to identify cellwise arm means from observational records. \citet{Hahn1998}, \citet{Robins1994}, and \citet{Hirano2003} develop influential efficiency and weighting tools for average treatment effects. The decision-theoretic strand treats the policy itself as a statistical choice: \citet{Manski2004} bounds the worst-case regret of treatment rules using covariates, \citet{Dehejia2005} frames program evaluation as a decision problem, \citet{Hirano2009} studies asymptotically optimal treatment rules, and \citet{Stoye2009} derives exact finite-sample minimax-regret rules for two-treatment choices. The present estimand is the *population oracle-value curve* indexed by treatment capacity, with risk measured by expected squared supremum error.

For allocation under a budget, \citet{BhattacharyaDupas2012} study welfare-maximizing assignment, derive asymptotic inference for the welfare of a sample allocation rule, and apply the method to bed-net subsidies. \citet{KitagawaTetenov2018} establish a minimax-optimal \(n^{-1/2}\) uniform welfare-regret rate for empirical welfare maximization when propensities are known; \citet{AtheyWager2021} develop observational policy learning for constrained policy classes. Recent constrained-policy work addresses complementary decisions: \citet{Pellatt2023} gives nonasymptotic cost bounds and welfare-regret oracle inequalities for budgeted stochastic rules, \citet{Sun2026} analyzes welfare and feasibility when policy costs are estimated, and \citet{Sakaguchi2025} gives optimal \(n^{-1/2}\) worst-case welfare-regret rates for constrained dynamic treatment rules. \citet{Kitagawa2023} characterizes consistency of surrogate losses under constrained policy classes; \citet{Athey2025} illustrates causal targeting under a treatment-capacity comparison.

The closest value-process comparison is \citet[Section 4 and Theorem 4.1.1]{FengHongNekipelov2026}, which obtains functional delta-method asymptotics for a binary constrained optimal-allocation value process under regular level-set and empirical-process conditions. \citet{FirpoGalvaoParker2023} develops directional-differentiability-based uniform inference for functionals of optimized value functions. \citet{LuedtkeVanDerLaan2016} characterizes pathwise differentiability of the unrestricted optimal value and constructs root-\(n\) confidence intervals under its stated conditions. For budget paths, \citet{SverdrupWuAtheyWager2025} characterizes multi-arm threshold allocations and establishes pointwise large-sample inference for estimated Qini values; \citet{Karlsson2025} studies Qini-curve estimation with clustered interference. Under a growing *known* discrete alphabet, fixed overlap, and possible exact ties, the present paper targets a finite-sample, all-procedure risk characterization for the entire binary-treatment budget-value curve.

The large-alphabet method follows a separate lineage. \citet{Wu2016} and \citet{JiaoVenkatHanWeissman2015} use polynomial approximation and unbiased count statistics to attain sharp rates for discrete functionals. \citet{CaiLow2011} develops moment-matching lower bounds for a nonsmooth absolute-value functional. \citet{JiaoHanWeissman2018} proves effective \(n\log n\) sample-size enlargement for estimating discrete \(L_1\) distance, including known-reference lower bounds in its stated large-alphabet regime. The accepted unrestricted discrete causal-value study \citet{CausalSmithUnrestricted2026} reports squared minimax risk of order \(\min\{1,d/[n\log(ed)]\}\) for the scalar oracle value. At full coverage that scalar is one coordinate of the present curve; the proposed comparison is simultaneous precision across budgets, with a half-population lower-bound family whose unrestricted value is fixed at \(1/2\). Standard process and asymptotic references are \citet{vanDerVaart1998} and \citet{vanDerVaart1996}.
