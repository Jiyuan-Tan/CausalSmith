The paper belongs to the literature on statistical treatment choice and estimation of welfare under optimal assignment. Potential-outcome identification and randomized assignment provide its causal foundation \citep{Rubin1974,Hernan2020}; efficient treatment-effect estimation supplies the adjoining semiparametric framework \citep{Hirano2003,Chernozhukov2018}. \citet{Manski2004} evaluates treatment rules through population welfare and finite-sample decision performance, while \citet{Kitagawa2018} develops empirical welfare maximization over candidate policy classes and \citet{Athey2021} extends policy learning to observational data using doubly robust scores. Individualized treatment rules and outcome-weighted learning provide a complementary methodological strand \citep{Qian2011,Zhao2012}, surveyed by \citet{Li2023}. The present estimand is the first-best population mean outcome over all deterministic binary assignments of the covariate cells. It coincides with the unrestricted, risk-neutral binary welfare benchmark in \citet[Section 2, equations (3)--(7)]{Cerulli2026OptimalPolicy}. The statistical model specifies known fair assignment, binary outcomes, public uniform probabilities for \(d\), the number of strata, and arbitrary arm-by-cell means in \([1/4,3/4]\). This gives a prospective confidential-reporting benchmark for first-best welfare. The policytree implementation supplies an additional computational connection through welfare maximization over trees \citep{Sverdrup2020}; its oracle coincides with the cellwise benchmark when its policy class represents every binary cell assignment.

The methodological core is nonsmooth functional estimation through polynomial approximation and moment matching. \citet{Lepski1999} studies regression-norm estimation, and \citet[Theorem 1 and Sections 3--5]{CaiLow2011Nonsmooth} establishes sharp results for the average absolute value of Gaussian means. With independent unit-variance Gaussian observations and a fixed coordinate bound \(M>0\), their minimax MSE is asymptotic to \(\beta_*^2M^2(\log\log d/\log d)^2\), where \(\beta_*\) is the Bernstein approximation constant. Their construction combines absolute-value approximation with unbiased Hermite moments, and their converse uses moment-matched priors. \citet{Jiao2015} and \citet{Wu2016} develop the corresponding approximation-and-unbiased-moment program for discrete distribution functionals. For distance estimation, \citet[Theorems 2--4]{JiaoHanWeissman2018L1} obtains reference-dependent minimax bounds for the scalar \(L_1\) distance from raw samples, including worst-reference MSE of order \(S/(n\log n)\) under their stated large-alphabet comparability conditions, with \(S\) the alphabet size and \(n\) the sample size. This lineage motivates approximation of absolute treatment contrasts, with private transcript moments replacing raw count or Gaussian moments. Distinct-person moment products also connect to classical U-statistics \citep{Hoeffding1948}, and pilot calibration uses bounded-summand concentration \citep[Theorem 2]{Hoeffding1963Bounded}. The inference component belongs to the honest-confidence literature: \citet{Low1997} bounds expected length under uniform coverage, \citet{CaiLow2004AdaptationCI} develops sharp modulus-based length theory for linear functionals, and \citet{ArmstrongKolesar2020HonestCI} constructs bias-aware nonparametric intervals. Here connected-interval length is analyzed directly for the nonsmooth welfare target.

Local confidentiality extends randomized response \citep{Warner1965} within the differential-privacy framework \citep{Dwork2006,Dwork2014,Wasserman2010}. Mechanism optimization and statistical converses are developed by \citet{Kairouz2016} and \citet{DuchiJordanWainwright2018Minimax}; distribution estimation under vector loss is treated by \citet{Ye2019}. Scalar local geometry, divergence contraction, and regular private efficiency provide further foundations \citep{RohdeSteinberger2020Geometry,Duchi2024,AsoodehZhang2024Contraction,Steinberger2024Efficiency}. Write \(t=n\varepsilon^2\) for the effective private resource, where \(n\) is the number of independent participants and \(\varepsilon\) is the pure local privacy budget. The closest discrete-functional comparison is \citet[Theorems 2.4--2.7]{ButuceaIssartel2021Functionals}: for positive power sums with exponent \(\gamma>1\), their sequential estimator has MSE bounded by a constant depending on \(\gamma\) times \(t^{-((\gamma-1)\wedge1)}\); for \(\gamma\ge2\), matching converses establish order \(t^{-1}\). Their exponent-one functional is the constant sum of probabilities. The present absolute signed-contrast functional has its kink at a treatment tie. Interaction can materially change other functional problems: over bounded Besov density classes with integrability index at least two, \citet[Theorems 3.2--3.3 and 4.1--4.2]{ButuceaRohdeSteinberger2023Quadratic} studies the integrated squared density. With \(s\) the smoothness index and \(a>1\) their mechanism parameter, their nonparametric MSE upper bounds are \((\log t)^{4a+1}t^{-8s/(4s+3)}\) for noninteraction and \((\log t)^{2a+1}t^{-4s/(2s+1)}\) for sequential interaction; their converses match the resource powers up to logarithmic factors. The parametric upper regimes begin at \(s>3/4\) and \(s>1/2\), respectively. In causal privacy, \citet[Lemma 4 and Theorem 7]{OhnishiAwan2025Randomized} obtains a matching \(O(t^{-1})\) known-propensity ATE estimator by privatizing the bounded inverse-probability score, together with asymptotic normal intervals. These results supply precise comparisons for positive power sums, smooth quadratic functionals, and bounded causal means. \citet[Section 7]{KentBerrettYu2026UserLevel} treats multiple observations per user and identifies distribution-functional estimation as an extension. Recent semiparametric and density-estimation work also uses local total-variation privacy \citep[Definition 1]{Kormos2026}; \citet{Butucea2026} obtains pointwise Sobolev density rates under that relaxed privacy definition. The paper’s frontier is evaluated for pure multiplicative local privacy on each original record.

The active optimal-value inference literature provides especially close estimand comparisons. \citet{LuedtkeVanDerLaan2016OptimalValue} characterizes pathwise differentiability and develops online one-step inference under conditions accommodating possibly nonunique optimal strategies. Subagging and smoothed index-rule inference offer additional approaches to nonregular value inference \citep{Shi2020,Wu2021}. \citet[Theorem 3.3]{WhitehouseChenAusternSyrgkanis2026Softmax} proves root-\(n\) asymptotic linearity for optimal policy value using orthogonal softmax scores. Its conditions include bounded outcomes and nuisance functions, controlled gap density with a positive exponent, a sufficiently fast-growing smoothing parameter \(\beta_n\), outcome-regression error \(o_p(n^{-1/4}\beta_n^{-1/2})\), and nuisance-product error \(o_p(n^{-1/2})\), where \(\beta_n\) controls smoothing. \citet[Theorem 1; Assumptions 1, 2(b)--(c), and 3]{Chen2025} establishes root-\(n\) asymptotic normality for welfare gain, the population average positive treatment effect, under first-stage linearization, regular zero-set geometry, and regression sup-norm error \(o_p(n^{-1/4})\). Their indicator-weighted value functionals receive separate boundary-integral analysis. \citet[Sections 3.2 and 4]{Feng2026} derives Hadamard differentiability of sorting and optimal-allocation value maps under regular-level-set and support conditions, then develops delta-method and debiased inference. The newly published \citet[Theorems 2.2 and 3.1; Assumptions 1--8]{Xu2026} obtains studentized root-\(n\) normality and a minimum asymptotic variance within its class of robust asymptotically linear unbiased estimators. Its conditions include overlap, consistent nuisances with product error \(o_p(n^{-1/2})\), a polynomial margin bound away from exact ties, calibrated smoothing, and the stated conditional-variance assumption; its supplement extends the efficiency calculation to heteroscedasticity. These papers establish population-value inference under their respective asymptotic conditions. The present contribution evaluates finite-resource minimax precision and global connected-interval honesty over arbitrary growing categorical heterogeneity, including exact ties, using confidential original-record transcripts.

Shared randomness and sketching are substantive comparisons because the welfare functional has an exact paired-uniform total-variation representation. \citet{Indyk2006} develops stable-projection norm sketches, while \citet{AcharyaCanonneFreitagTyagi2019TestWithoutTrust} and its expanded journal treatment \citep{Acharya2021} establish optimal locally private testing protocols. For identity testing on \(S\) symbols at separation \(\eta\), their public-coin sample complexity has order \(S/(\varepsilon^2\eta^2)\), compared with \(S^{3/2}/(\varepsilon^2\eta^2)\) for private coins in the high-privacy regime. Scalar distance estimation receives a distinct comparison from \citet[Theorems 4.6, 4.10, and 4.13]{CormodeTing2025FederatedShift}: their empirical-frequency sketches give multiplicative \(L_1\) approximation using \(\widetilde O(\eta^{-3})\) space and constant update time, and client noise supplies approximate item-level local privacy. Private policy optimization is represented by the September 2026 \citet[Theorems 1--2]{Yang2026}. Under central approximate privacy, Lipschitz and Hölder-smooth surrogate conditions, their SGD surrogate excess-risk bound is \(O(n^{-1/2}+\sqrt{p\log(1/\delta_{\mathrm{DP}})}/(n\varepsilon))\), where \(p\) is feature dimension and \(\delta_{\mathrm{DP}}\) is the approximate-privacy parameter; a Tsybakov noise condition converts this into a value-regret bound with exponent \((\zeta+1)/(\zeta+2)\), where \(\zeta\) is their noise exponent. Against these testing, empirical-sketch, and policy-regret benchmarks, the paper establishes an all-channel scalar estimation frontier. With \(L=\log(ed)\), the logarithmic dimension factor, its MSE order is \(d^2/(tL)\) for \(t\ge d^2L\) and \(\min\{1,[\log(e+d^2L/t)/L]^2\}\) below that boundary. Explicit noninteractive original-record protocols attain this order, and converses cover one-message sequential interaction and arbitrary independent shared public seeds. A separate connectedness argument establishes square-root honest-length order at finite global coverage \(0.90\). The exact experiment bridge yields the same complete estimation and honesty frontier on the paired-uniform TV family, and model inclusion transfers its lower bounds to TV estimation on the full simplex.
