The paper joins the instrumental-variable literature on complier effects with the literature on transporting causal effects across populations. The identification framework originates in \citet{Imbens1994,Angrist1996}, while \citet{Frangakis2002} formalize principal strata; \citet{Imbens2014} surveys their econometric interpretation. Covariate adjustment, weighting, and semiparametric estimation develop through \citet{Abadie2003,Tan2006,Frolich2007,Ogburn2015}, with a recent synthesis in \citet{Levis2024}. [The latter review](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09332) organizes the structural assumptions behind conditional Wald identification and efficient estimation. External validity of instrument-specific effects is treated directly by \citet{Angrist2013} and reviewed by \citet{Mogstad2018a}; \citet{Mogstad2018b} develop extrapolation using restrictions on treatment-response functions. [Angrist and Fernández-Val](https://www.nber.org/papers/w16566) construct effects for new subpopulations from covariate-specific LATEs under conditional effect restrictions. Causal transport more generally rests on selection diagrams and do-calculus \citep{Pearl2014}, trial-participation weighting \citep{Cole2010,Stuart2011}, and potential-outcome identification and estimation \citep{Lesko2017,Dahabreh2019}; [\citet{Degtiar2023}](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-statistics-042522-103837) survey these strands. Adjacent cross-population methods target compliance-adjusted population average effects \citep{Ottoboni2020}, policy value under selection uncertainty \citep{HattTschernutterFeuerriegel2022Policy}, and predictive coverage under covariate shift \citep{TibshiraniBarberCandesRamdas2019CovariateShift}; their respective targets help locate the present transported complier-effect confidence problem.

The closest causal comparators establish identification and regular inference for transported complier or principal-stratum effects. \citet[Theorem 3.1]{ChenHuang2025Generalizing} identify transported CACE through a ratio of target-averaged source encouragement contrasts. After aligning encouragement potential outcomes with receipt-mediated outcomes under exclusion and monotonicity, their Assumption 2 corresponds to complier-weighted outcome transport; the present pointwise compliance-share equality implies their averaged Assumption 4. Their Theorem 4.2 gives asymptotic normality at the square-root total-sample rate with known assignment and selection probabilities and finite weighted second moments; Theorem 4.3 permits correctly specified logistic selection estimation with known assignment probability and additional moment and rank conditions. [Their results](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00149) provide the regular transported-CACE comparison. \citet[Sections 5 and 7]{RudolphVanDerLaan2017Robust} derive a transported-CACE efficient influence curve, construct a ratio TMLE with influence-function inference, and report Boston-to-Los Angeles Moving to Opportunity transported CACE intervals. [Their observation scheme](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5784860/) includes target instrument and receipt records and transports a common conditional outcome model. \citet[Theorem 6]{Clark2024} obtain efficient, square-root-rate asymptotic normality for transported principal effects under principal ignorability and principal-stratum transport assumptions, independent nuisance training, positivity, bounded regressions, influence-function consistency, and three specified nuisance-error products of order smaller than the inverse square root of sample size; their asymptotics also fix a positive limiting source-to-target sample-size ratio. [These conditions](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.04419) distinguish regular principal-effect inference from the present uniform weak-strength, covariate-only-target experiment.

The estimation architecture belongs to semiparametric influence-function theory \citep{Bickel1993,VanderVaart1998}, augmented estimation \citep{Robins1994}, targeted learning \citep{VanderLaan2006}, and orthogonal scores with cross-fitting \citep{Chernozhukov2018}. Rough nonlinear functionals connect this lineage to classical integral-functional estimation \citep{Bickel1988,Birge1995} and higher-order U-statistic corrections \citep{Hoeffding1948,Robins2008,RobinsLiMukherjeeTchetgenVanDerVaart2017HigherOrder}. [Robins et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6453538/) develop projection-based higher-order estimating equations, including optimal rates below the square-root regime in missing-response models. Empirical higher-order constructions and numerical stability are developed further by \citet{Liu2026,Liu2023}; [\citet{Balakrishnan2026}](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04116) characterize the information available in structure-agnostic nuisance-error neighborhoods. The closest transport-specific rate comparison is \citet[Theorems 6 and 8]{ZengKennedyBodnarNaimi2025Efficient}. Their minimax lower bound has root-mean-square scale \(n^{-4s/(4s+d)}\) for \(s<d/4\), where \(s\) is average outcome-regression and inverse selection-treatment-weight smoothness and \(d\) is covariate dimension, in a restricted design class with constant density–participation–treatment product. At \(s=1/8\) and \(d=1\), this scale is \(n^{-1/3}\). Their transported quadratic estimator has conditional variance of order \(n^{-1}+k n^{-2}\), where \(k\) is projection dimension, and a bias bound containing projection errors and a third-order Gram-matrix estimation remainder. [Those bounds](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.00092) support the smooth-functional comparison under their stated approximation, positivity, and remainder conditions. The present construction establishes the corresponding observable cubic score bound for its seven marked-density coordinates and fully learned nuisance geometry.

Inference with a small compliance denominator follows the ratio-confidence and identification-robust traditions of \citet{Anderson1949,Fieller1954}, weak-instrument asymptotics \citep{Staiger1997,Stock2000}, and conditional inference \citep{Andrews2016}; \citet{Andrews2019} provide a modern survey. \citet{Dufour1997} characterize confidence-set restrictions for unbounded, locally almost unidentified parameters, placing the parameter domain explicitly at the center of weak-identification analysis. [The present bounded effect domain](https://doi.org/10.2307/2171740) instead permits a constant-length weak-strength branch. For the same one-population LATE estimand, \citet[Theorem 3.2]{Ma2026IdentificationRobust} establish uniform asymptotic size for an orthogonalized Anderson–Rubin test under Assumptions 3.1–3.2, including conditional IV validity, overlap, weak-or-strong positive first stages, sparse nuisance models, and their estimation-rate and growth conditions. [Test inversion](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.09756) consequently provides identification-robust asymptotic confidence sets within that regime. \citet[Section 3 and Theorem 1]{SmuclerLanniMasip2025Score} characterize score-set geometry and show that, at a fixed law with nonzero denominator, bounded outcomes, overlap, independent nuisance training, consistency, and their product-rate conditions, score-set endpoints agree with doubly robust Wald endpoints up to \(O_P(n^{-1})\), with probability tending to one. [Their Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.10449) also describes the nonnormal ratio limit under first-stage strength of order \(n^{-1/2}\). The present affine-score inversion uses an explicit finite-sample mean-square calibration over the declared rough class.

The expected-length criterion connects the contribution to optimal recovery, honest nonparametric confidence intervals, and between-class modulus theory \citep{Donoho1994,Low1997,CaiLow2004Adaptation}; [\citet{CaiLow2004Adaptation}](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0503662) give expected-length lower bounds and adaptive interval constructions for linear functionals. Mixture comparisons for nonlinear functionals with multiple infinite-dimensional nuisances are developed by \citet{VanDerVaartTchetgenRobinsLi2009Mixtures}, with general testing-to-estimation tools summarized by \citet[Chapter 2]{Tsybakov2009}. [The corrected 2009 reference](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5884174/) is *Semiparametric minimax rates*; the separately named higher-order influence-function work is \citet{Robins2008}. Causal legality of the present converse also connects to the observable restrictions studied by \citet{Balke1997,Kitagawa2015}, particularly nonnegative principal-stratum outcome masses. The supplied accepted-bank comparator \citep{CausalSmith2026TransportedLATEStrengthFrontier} establishes the square-root score benchmark and attainment in supplied, finite-cell, and regular-cell weight geometries. [That benchmark](https://causalsmith.org/papers/stat_transported_late_strength_frontier_v1/) provides the immediate internal comparison. In the present equal-size experiment, with scalar covariates, fixed Hölder exponent \(1/8\), positive bounded source and target densities, overlapping unknown instrument propensity, bounded outcomes, and the two pointwise transport equalities, the paper establishes the sharp honest expected-length frontier \(\min\{1,n^{-1/3}/a\}\), where \(a\) is the transported first-stage evaluation floor. A single observable interval sequence attains this order without using the floor, and a nuisance-varying two-sample continuum with a no-defiers full-data realization supplies the matching converse. This gives an iid family-unit, scalar-score precision benchmark for the transported CACE question illustrated by Moving to Opportunity.
