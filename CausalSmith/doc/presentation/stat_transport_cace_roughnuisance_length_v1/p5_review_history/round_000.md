# Referee review

**Recommendation:** major_revision
**Overall score:** 6.8/10 — The verified frontier and causally admissible converse constitute a credible theoretical contribution, but inconsistent perturbation notation, incomplete primitive definitions, and substantial presentation overhead require revision.

The paper establishes the sharp order of globally honest expected confidence-interval length for transported complier effects in a scalar, equal-size two-sample model with fixed roughness. Its observable cubic construction and no-defiers lower continuum connect established functional-estimation and score-inversion methods to a useful precision criterion. The principal prose claims accurately preserve the verified coverage, strength, and sample-size conditions. Publication nevertheless requires a more self-contained model presentation, consistent lower-family notation, and clearer exposition of the methodological contribution.

## Strengths
- The matching bounds characterize both the constant-order weak-strength branch and the inverse-strength branch uniformly over the stated evaluation floors.
- One observable procedure achieves global finite-sample coverage and law-specific expected-length control across all strength slices.
- The converse covers arbitrary measurable confidence sets and retains a compatible monotone instrumental-variable potential-outcome realization.
- The early related-work section engages close competitors' actual rates, observation schemes, and nuisance conditions.
- The manuscript carefully distinguishes global coverage from strength-specific precision and presents unequal allocation as an open question.

## Findings
- **[major·statement] Lower-bound construction and proof** — The coupled tiled perturbation definition writes u_lambda as tau times the tiled sum, whereas the legal-mixture construction treats u_lambda as the covariate perturbation and separately sets v_lambda,tau = tau u_lambda. The extracted declarations distinguish an unscaled tiled perturbation from its tau-scaled coupled perturbation. The manuscript therefore leaves two incompatible readings of the inputs to the lower family.
  - *Fix:* Define the unscaled u_lambda explicitly, then identify the coupled perturbation as v_lambda,tau = tau u_lambda. State explicitly which object the existing coupled-perturbation definition denotes, and use those names consistently throughout the construction and surrounding prose while preserving the verified continuum statement.
- **[major·structure] Model, transport restrictions and inference criterion** — The text announces that 'The following presentation definition fixes these primitives,' but the promised primitive definition is absent. Potential receipts receive their binary domain only implicitly, the full-data record is introduced later without its components, and the relationship between the full-data law, assignment law, and observed one-record laws remains implicit. This obstructs a self-contained reading of the law-level model.
  - *Fix:* Insert a compact primitive setup specifying population coding, the full-data record, binary potential receipts, potential-outcome domains, the complier indicator, and how the source assignment and observed laws and target covariate law are induced. Explicitly define the target conditional effect and specify joint graph measurability for confidence-set procedures. Describe the existing verified setup without adding restrictions.
- **[major·structure] Main results and an attaining interval** — The main result is buried behind repeated block definitions, indexing conversions, coordinate conventions, and histogram domain conventions. The coordinate-marks definition ends with a nearly manuscript-wide cross-reference list. These details obscure the central methodological question: how the cubic correction accommodates learned rough geometry at the required estimation scale.
  - *Fix:* State the frontier immediately after a concise model and inference-criterion summary. Retain the marked-density representation and an estimator overview in the main text, and move exhaustive indexing and arithmetic conventions to a technical appendix while preserving their anchors. Replace the blanket cross-reference list with local references, and add a short rate-balance explanation connecting pilot accuracy, correction resolutions, remainder control, and the final mean-square scale.
- **[minor·citation] Related work** — The close contemporary comparisons are detailed, but the paper's two organizing principles—affine-moment inversion under weak identification and optimal expected length under uniform honesty—receive little engagement with their foundational literatures. This leaves the econometric and decision-theoretic positioning incomplete.
  - *Fix:* Add concise comparisons to Anderson–Rubin and Fieller inference, bounded-domain weak-identification analysis, and honest expected-length or modulus theory. Explain which established principles the paper specializes and how the rough transported functional and causal lower family deliver the present characterization. Verify appropriate locators for the added references.
- **[minor·prose] Discussion and open questions** — The discussion repeats the rate characterization but gives little interpretation of the particular smoothness exponent or the explicit calibration constant. The distinction between the estimator threshold n_0 = 256 and sample sizes yielding useful calibrated precision deserves explanation.
  - *Fix:* Motivate the choice beta = 1/8 as a concrete rough regime and explain the methodological role of cubic correction. Discuss how the displayed calibration radius depends on the supplied envelopes, radius, and coverage level, and distinguish the construction threshold from the sample-size scale at which that radius becomes small relative to the bounded effect domain.
- **[minor·structure] Comparator definitions and verification scope** — The finite-cell subclass combines continuity on the connected interval with constancy on every cell of a finite interval partition. For an ordinary partition covering the domain, continuity forces adjacent cell constants to agree, so this comparator reduces to globally constant geometry. Its relationship to the earlier nonuniform cell-based benchmark is consequently unclear. The auxiliary comparators also occupy substantial space without an associated comparison conclusion.
  - *Fix:* Explain the continuous finite-cell subclass's resulting scope and distinguish it from the external discrete or piecewise-constant experiment. Condense the comparator apparatus to objects used in a concrete comparison, or explain their precise illustrative purpose.
- **[minor·prose] Introduction** — The roadmap refers readers to related work, discussion, and the appendices through manually named destinations rather than cleveref structural references, contrary to the cross-reference contract.
  - *Fix:* Add structural labels to the referenced sections and appendices and use \cref or \Cref for each reader-facing destination. Keep object references attached to their exact existing labels.
- **[nit·prose] Introduction** — The sentence 'The results have the scope specified by their stated assumptions and conclusions' is vacuous and interrupts an otherwise substantive contribution discussion.
  - *Fix:* Delete the sentence or replace it with a concise affirmative description of the scalar, equal-size, fixed-roughness domain.

## Questions for authors
- What methodological consideration motivates fixing the Hölder exponent at 1/8, and which part of the construction should readers regard as the principal advance over the cited quadratic transport estimator?
- For representative regularity envelopes and coverage levels, what sample-size scale makes the stated deterministic calibration informative relative to the effect domain?
- What concrete comparison is the auxiliary finite-cell and continuous-witness apparatus intended to establish or illustrate?
