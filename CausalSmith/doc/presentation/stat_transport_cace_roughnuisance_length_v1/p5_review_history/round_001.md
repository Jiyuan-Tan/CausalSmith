# Referee review

**Recommendation:** major_revision
**Overall score:** 7/10 — The verified frontier and causally admissible converse constitute a credible, focused theoretical contribution, while substantial reorganization and sharper interpretation are needed for publication.

The paper establishes a sharp honest expected-length frontier for transported complier effects in an equal-size, scalar-covariate model with fixed rough smoothness. Its contribution combines an observable cubic estimator, finite-sample score calibration, and a matching continuum of causally admissible lower experiments. The principal claims faithfully reflect the verification contract, but the manuscript needs substantial restructuring and several targeted corrections to its exposition and literature comparisons.

## Strengths
- The distinction between global coverage and precision evaluated on a strength slice is explicit and consistently maintained.
- A single interval sequence attains the frontier across all evaluation floors and has a law-specific expected-length guarantee.
- The lower construction preserves monotonicity, compatible potential outcomes, and both transport restrictions, including throughout the weak-strength branch.
- The dedicated early related-work section engages competitors' observation schemes, rates, and nuisance conditions.
- The unequal-sample extension is clearly presented as a defined decision problem and an open question.

## Findings
- **[major·structure] Main results and an attaining interval; appendices** — The organization obscures the central contribution. The exact frontier statement appears deep inside the appendix, while the subsection titled 'Identification from primitive variables' begins with an extended sequence of block, histogram, and estimator definitions before reaching identification. Repeated conventions and the exhaustive cross-reference list in the coordinate-mark definition further interrupt the argument. Several transitions describe objects already presented as forthcoming. The main-results claim that exact statements and proofs appear in the upper and lower appendices also overlooks the separate 'Proofs of the main results' appendix.
  - *Fix:* Place the sharp frontier statement and precise score-interval construction in the main-results section. Organize the technical material in dependency order: identification, coordinate and sampling conventions, estimator, mean-square bound, upper-bound argument, lower construction, and lower-bound argument. Consolidate repeated indexing explanations into one notation block, replace the exhaustive reference list with targeted references, repair the displaced transitions, and update the roadmap to include the actual proof locations. Preserve all theorem conditions and anchored labels.
- **[minor·citation] Related work** — The description of Smucler, Lanni, and Masip says their endpoints agree 'up to endpoint errors of order 1/n,' whereas the supplied comparison specifies stochastic O_P(n^{-1}) endpoint errors with probability tending to one. The Clark comparison also omits the positive limiting source-to-target sample-size ratio identified in the related-work brief. These qualifications matter when contrasting their asymptotic guarantees with this paper's finite-sample guarantee.
  - *Fix:* State the stochastic endpoint order and probability qualification explicitly. Add the positive limiting sample-size-ratio condition to the Clark comparison, retaining the fixed-law, positive-first-stage and nuisance-rate qualifications and the existing theorem locators.
- **[minor·prose] Discussion and open questions; Uniform mean-square control** — The computable calibration constant is central to the delivered interval, but its numerical implications remain abstract. The discussion advises readers to account for C_0 without showing the magnitude of the displayed calibration under any admissible parameter choices. Readers therefore have little basis for interpreting finite-sample attainment or selecting the required envelope and smoothness inputs.
  - *Fix:* Add a compact numerical illustration evaluating the existing calibration formula for representative admissible density envelopes, Hölder radii, coverage levels, and sample sizes. Report the score tolerance relative to the bounded effect domain, distinguish the theoretical envelope from realized interval length, and explain how an analyst supplies the fixed regularity bounds. This can use the established formulas directly.
- **[minor·prose] Related work; Instrument validity and complier transport** — The causal positioning would benefit from an explicit mapping to the closest identification antecedent. The phrases 'Specific to this analysis' and 'Also specific to this analysis' introduce transport restrictions whose relationship to Chen and Huang is left implicit. The supplied brief identifies the outcome restriction with their Assumption 2 after aligning potential outcomes, and identifies the pointwise share restriction as implying their averaged Assumption 4.
  - *Fix:* Explain this correspondence with the appropriate assumption locators. Describe the pointwise compliance-share equality as the chosen transport condition for the present model, and locate the contribution in the rough-class estimation and honest expected-length characterization. Add a concise substantive interpretation of when conditional complier prevalence can reasonably remain stable across populations.
- **[minor·prose] Discussion and open questions; Auxiliary comparators** — Ordinary prose outside the permitted limitations and open-question subsections uses contribution-by-negation. Examples include 'rather than an adaptation claim,' 'It does not promise a numerically short interval at n=256,' and 'We use it only as a syntax-level comparison object and claim no separate rate conclusion for it here.'
  - *Fix:* Use affirmative scope statements: the fixed exponent specifies the analyzed regularity class; the threshold ensures that the block and resolution construction is defined; numerical precision depends on the calibration constants; and the continuous finite-cell subclass provides a globally constant geometry specialization. Move any genuine non-coverage discussion into 'Limitations and future work.'
- **[minor·prose] Primitive variables and the observed experiment; Why the cubic correction reaches the rough scale** — Some symbols precede their formal definitions without the required first-use gloss. The first occurrence of P_n^F is 'a primitive full-data record under P_n^F,' and C_mse first appears in the main-text mean-square display without being named as the calibration constant.
  - *Fix:* Write 'under the full-data law P_n^F' at its first occurrence. Introduce the transported-contrast estimator and its uniform mean-square envelope constant immediately before the main-text risk display.
- **[minor·prose] abstract** — The abstract states the minimax order uniformly across strength floors without identifying the sample-size range of the verified two-sided frontier. The contract establishes that comparison for n at least 256, while coverage of the proposed sequence holds for every positive sample size.
  - *Fix:* Attach 'for n at least 256' to the frontier claim and retain the separate all-positive-sample-size coverage scope when describing the interval sequence.

## Questions for authors
- How should an applied analyst specify the density envelopes and Hölder radius used by the finite-sample calibration?
- Which substantive transport setting motivates equality of conditional complier shares across the source and target populations?
