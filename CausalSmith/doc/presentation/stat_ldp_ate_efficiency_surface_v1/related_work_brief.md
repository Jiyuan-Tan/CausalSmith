The paper connects randomized-experiment inference with statistical efficiency under local differential privacy. Its average treatment effect is the difference between two binary potential-outcome means, within the framework developed by \citet{Neyman1990,Rubin1974} and synthesized by \citet{Imbens2015}. The known-assignment inverse-probability comparator follows the weighting principle of \citet{Horvitz1952}. The econometric efficiency lineage includes \citet[Theorem 1]{Hahn1998}, who derives treatment-effect efficiency bounds under conditional ignorability, and \citet[pp. 1161–1189]{Hirano2003}, who constructs efficient weighting estimators using an estimated propensity score. Here randomization supplies identification, while joint privatization of treatment and outcome determines the available information. Randomized response originates with \citet{Warner1965}; the formal privacy framework and sensitivity-calibrated noise originate with \citet{Dwork2006} and are surveyed by \citet{Dwork2014}. \citet{Kasiviswanathan2011} establishes the connection between local private learning and statistical queries, and \citet{Wasserman2010} develops a statistical treatment of privacy-preserving data release. These strands place the contribution within established causal and private-inference theory.

The statistical antecedents characterize how privacy changes estimation difficulty. For sample size \(n\) and privacy budget \(\varepsilon\), \citet[Section 3.2.1]{DuchiJordanWainwright2018} obtains the bounded scalar-mean squared-error rate \(\min\{1,(n\varepsilon^2)^{-1}\}\), up to numerical constants in the high-privacy regime \(0<\varepsilon\le1\), alongside general contraction inequalities and bounds for other estimation problems. \citet[pp. 2646–2670]{Rohde2020} characterizes functional-estimation rates through total-variation moduli and establishes binary-release rate attainment for linear functionals over convex models. \citet[Sections 3–4]{Duchi2024} develops instance-specific local minimax bounds and an \(L_1\)-score measure of complexity, again with numerical-constant comparisons. A complementary route uses information in the privatized experiment: under a bound \(I_0\), the upper bound on directional input-score second moments, \citet[Proposition 2]{BarnesChenOzgur2020} proves \(\operatorname{tr}(I_{\mathrm{out}})\le I_0(e^\varepsilon-1)^2\), where \(I_{\mathrm{out}}\) is the output Fisher-information matrix. Their further score-tail inequalities and van Trees arguments yield order-optimal lower bounds for Gaussian, discrete-distribution, and sparse Bernoulli models. \citet{Duchi2019} extends rate lower bounds to arbitrarily interactive protocols through communication complexity, while \citet{Joseph2019} establishes distinctions between sequential and repeated-user interaction. The present paper identifies an exact pointwise, fixed-privacy contrast constant within the once-per-subject sequential class.

The asymptotic argument builds on local experiments and nuisance-score projection \citep[Chapter 6]{LeCam2000}, \citep[Chapters 7–8 and 25]{VanDerVaart1998}, \citep{Bickel1993}, together with the Bayesian information inequality of \citet{VanTrees2001} and its statistical applications in \citet[pp. 59–79]{Gill1995}. The closest asymptotic predecessor is \citet[Theorems 3.3 and 3.5]{Steinberger2024}: for models differentiable in quadratic mean and countably generated input and output sigma-fields, he develops sequential private local asymptotic mixed normality and convolution theory, with the stated information-limit conditions. In the scalar case, his optimal asymptotic variance is the reciprocal of supremal marginal-channel Fisher information, and Section 4 constructs a private preliminary estimate followed by an asymptotically efficient procedure. Remark 3.6 identifies optimization of scalar functionals of inverse information in multiparameter models as a further direction. \citet[pp. 118–126]{KalininSteinberger2025} obtains an exact solution for a unit-variance Gaussian mean when \(\varepsilon\le1\): randomized response to the sign of the centered observation, implemented through a private two-stage procedure. \citet{Gaboardi2019} supplies Gaussian-mean confidence intervals and matching rate bounds. For fixed interior binary arm means, known assignment bounded away from zero and one, and fixed positive privacy, the present paper characterizes the nuisance-profiled ATE variance over arbitrary measurable sequential output spaces and constructs an attaining private-pilot affine estimator with Wald inference across support changes.

Channel optimization also has a substantial methodological lineage. Comparison by randomization follows \citet[pp. 265–272]{Blackwell1953}; contrast-specific optimal design follows \citet[Sections 2–4]{Elfving1952} and \citet[Chapter 2]{Pukelsheim2006}, with saddle-point theory represented by \citet{Sion1958}. For finite private inputs, \citet[Theorems 2 and 4]{KairouzOhViswanath2016} establishes staircase optimality for its sublinear utility class, a finite linear program, and an optimizer with output cardinality at most the input cardinality. Profiling the nuisance parameter here produces a minimum of linear pattern objectives, leading to a different support geometry. Related optimization includes discrete-distribution mechanisms \citep{Ye2018}, minimum-variance PrivUnit randomizers within the specified class of vector-mean protocols \citep{Asi2022}, and numerical workload-specific optimization of unbiased linear-query mechanisms \citep[Sections 3–4]{McKenna2020}. \citet{Chen2025} optimizes stratified sampling allocations under specified additive privacy mechanisms, while \citet[Sections 3–5]{RaabEtAl2025} surveys and compares descriptive-statistic releases. Particularly relevant frontier work is \citet[Sections 2–4, Theorems 2–3]{Amorino2025}, which develops extremal-mechanism factorization, continuous staircase optimization, and the scalar high-privacy information equivalent \(\varepsilon^2(\mathbb E|s|)^2/4\), with \(s\) denoting the input likelihood score. \citet[Section 3]{Amorino2026} studies symmetric and asymmetric staircase information across privacy regimes. For two known finite-alphabet distributions, \citet[Corollary 3.2 and Section 3.2]{GhaziNasserCalmonIssa2026} characterizes optimal \(f\)-divergence mechanisms by likelihood-ratio sorting, contiguous partitioning, and randomized response, with a cubic-time dynamic program. Against this lineage, the paper establishes a finite profiled-contrast saddle and certified open regions of exact five-output and three-output stationary attainment at common assignment and privacy settings.

The immediate causal comparator is \citet[Sections 3.1 and 3.3, Lemma 4, Theorem 7, and Appendix A.5]{OhnishiAwan2025}. For potential outcomes in \([0,1]\), their high-privacy minimax lower bound is a constant multiple of \(\min\{1,(n\varepsilon^2)^{-1}\}\); their custom known-assignment Laplace sample mean attains the corresponding privacy rate and has a central limit theorem and plug-in Wald interval. Writing \(p\) for the known treatment probability, \(\mu_0,\mu_1\) for the arm means, and \(\tau=\mu_1-\mu_0\) for the ATE, its binary-outcome variance specializes to \(\mu_1/p+\mu_0/(1-p)-\tau^2+2/\{\varepsilon^2p^2(1-p)^2\}\). This published release and sample mean define the interval-efficiency benchmark. The wider private-causal literature includes causal-direction procedures \citep{Kusner2016}, observational inverse-probability weighting \citep{Lee2019}, privacy-sensitive identification \citep{Komarova2020}, covariate balancing \citep{Ohnishi2024}, and model-agnostic treatment-effect estimation with private intervals \citep{Lebeda2025}. \citet[Theorem 2 and Corollary 1]{KormosVanderVaart2026} derives efficient influence functions and rate-double-robust inference for specified identifying mechanisms under operator-range, integrability, and nuisance-rate conditions; its efficiency criterion concerns the induced private model. The applied setting comes from the Colombian education-transfer experiment of \citet{BarreraOsorioEtAl2011}. \citet[Section 6]{OhnishiAwan2025} analyzes attendance rates and describes the program's 80-percent attendance requirement. The present applied consumer is a prospectively constructed binary indicator of meeting that threshold, for which the optimized channel and interval procedure provide a design-specific efficiency analysis.
