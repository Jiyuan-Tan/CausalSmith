This paper sits at the intersection of structural causal models, proxy-based adjustment for unmeasured confounding, and transport of causal information across environments. The structural and interventional language follows the SCM tradition of \cite{Pearl2009}, with adjustment and longitudinal causal-functional antecedents in \cite{RosenbaumRubin1983,Robins1986}. The transport side is connected to selection-diagram and data-fusion work: \cite{PearlBareinboim2011} introduced a formal transportability framework, \cite{BareinboimPearl2012,BareinboimPearl2013} gave completeness and an algorithmic transport formula, and \cite{BareinboimPearl2016} framed data fusion as combining heterogeneous observational and experimental regimes. In the present finite latent-shift setting, the transported object is a target-domain interventional law learned from source observations of \((E,W,X,Y)\) and target observations of \(W\), so the contribution refines transportability by using proxy-channel geometry rather than only graphical transport criteria.

The proxy strand begins with measurement-error and proxy restoration results such as \cite{KurokiPearl2014} and the finite proxy-rank identification theorem of \cite{MiaoGengTchetgen2018}, which identifies causal effects with two proxy variables under rank/completeness conditions while allowing the measurement mechanism to remain unidentified. The proximal causal learning literature develops bridge functions and semiparametric estimation: \cite{TchetgenTchetgen2024} surveys the framework and proximal \(g\)-formula, \cite{Shi2020} gives multiply robust double-negative-control adjustment for categorical unmeasured confounding, \cite{Cui2024} develops semiparametric proximal ATE and ATT theory with efficiency bounds and doubly robust locally efficient estimators, and \cite{Ying2023} extends proximal identification and regular asymptotically linear estimation to longitudinal marginal structural mean models. Kernel and minimax implementations in \cite{Mastouri2021,Kallus2021,Liu2025} address bridge estimation in nonlinear or regression-based forms. The current result uses the same proxy-completeness intuition in a finite shared-channel SCM, but its identification frontier is the observable, target-specific span condition \(Q(W)\in\operatorname{col}\{P(W\mid E,x)\}\) under a full-column-rank proxy channel.

The closest causal transport comparator is \cite{IglesiasAlonsoEtAl2025}. Their Assumption 1 requires \(\operatorname{rank} P(W\mid E,x)\ge k_U\) for every treatment value, and their Theorem 1 identifies the target causal law by
\[
q(y\mid\mathrm{do}(x))=P(y\mid E,x)\,P(W\mid E,x)^\dagger Q(W),
\]
with consistency and pointwise asymptotic normality for the reduced estimator under that full-rank regime. Their Appendix B.2 gives a failure construction in which \(P(W\mid U)\) has rank below the latent cardinality and two SCMs agree on observed source and target-proxy laws while producing different causal effects. The present paper preserves the same Expedia-style data pattern and estimand while characterizing the exact deficient-source-rank frontier with an injective proxy channel: source rank below latent cardinality is compatible with identification exactly when the target proxy mixture lies in the observed source proxy span, and off-span cases admit strictly positive full-observed-law countermodels.

Two other frontier papers delimit the algebraic novelty. \cite{ZhangLiMiaoTchetgen2023}, Proposition 2.3, proves that for an outcome bridge equation, a linear functional \(\beta_o=\mathbb E[\phi_o h]\) is identified over a nonunique bridge fiber iff \(\phi_o\in\mathcal N(T_o)^\perp\); Lemma 2.4 further connects root-\(n\) estimability to membership in the adjoint range. The present theorem specializes the null-space-annihilation idea to finite target transport and adds SCM realizability over the full supplied law. \cite{RahiminasabEtAl2026}, Theorem 3, proves point-identification of the target predictor \(\mathbb E[Y\mid X=x,Z=S+1]\) when latent equivalent classes are mixed with sufficient cross-domain rank so that the target operator is a linear combination of distinguishing source-environment operators. That result targets prediction under imperfect, noninjective proxies; the present paper targets an interventional law under an injective shared proxy channel and makes the target proxy distribution itself the audited object. Related partial-identification and finite-SCM bounding work in \cite{Manski1990,Manski2003,Tamer2010,ChernozhukovHongTamer2007,BeresteanuMolinari2008,GhassamiShpitserTchetgen2023,DuarteEtAl2024} supplies the broader set-identification background for off-span fibers and sharp-bounds computation.

The inference component belongs to the weak-identification and inverse-problem tradition. \cite{Hansen1982} anchors the GMM moment framework, while \cite{AndersonRubin1949,StockWrightYogo2002,Moreira2003,Kleibergen2005,DufourTaamouti2005,AndrewsCheng2012,AndrewsCheng2014,AndrewsGuggenberger2019} develop robust tests, projection confidence sets, and uniform inference under weak or partial identification. In nonparametric inverse problems, \cite{NeweyPowell2003,CarrascoFlorensRenault2007,Santos2011,ChernozhukovNeweySantos2023,BennettEtAl2023} characterize estimation and inference for functions or linear functionals when inverse nuisance components are weakly identified. The paper’s finite-sample projection set follows this inversion-and-projection lineage: it gives honest two-sample multinomial coverage for the target causal probability across arbitrary ranks and balancing weights, while the regular Wald comparison is reserved for singular-value-separated submodels. Recent proxy and latent-shift work including \cite{Alabdulmohsin2023,TsaiEtAl2024,Walker2023,Sloman2025,Guo2025,Ringlein2025,Vollmer2026,Guo2026} shows the active frontier around proxy informativeness, latent-shift adaptation, and bridge-versus-factor recovery; \cite{Ursu2018} supplies the Expedia ranking application context in which target-specific proxy transport can be audited hotel by hotel.
