The paper studies recovery of the treated causal response, \(\mu_1(x)=\mathbb E[Y(1)\mid X=x]\), within the potential-outcome framework developed by \citet{rubin1974} and the propensity-score identification theory of \citet{rosenbaum1983}; \citet{imbens2015} provides the standard textbook treatment. Conditional exchangeability and consistency identify this response through treated outcome regression, making its estimation a building block for causal adjustment. The surrounding semiparametric literature connects inverse-probability weighting \citep{horvitz1952}, augmented estimating equations \citep{robins1994}, efficiency bounds \citep{hahn1998}, and efficient estimation using estimated propensities \citep{hirano2003}; \citet{kennedy2022} reviews these connections. Weak overlap introduces a separate difficulty: \citet{khan2010} characterize irregular identification associated with inverse weighting, and \citet{damour2021} explain restrictions imposed by strict overlap in high-dimensional observational studies. Responses include optimal subsample selection \citep{crump2009}, overlap-weighted average effects \citep{li2018}, robust confidence intervals \citep{rothe2017}, trimming-bias correction and resampling \citep{MaWang2020}, stable-law inference \citep{HeilerKazak2021}, and finite-sample optimal estimation of average effects under smoothness restrictions \citep{armstrong2021}. The present contribution characterizes recovery of the entire response function under a quantitative global propensity-tail condition.

Its estimation strategy belongs to the local-polynomial and partition-series tradition. \citet{Stone1982} establishes the regular-design global regression benchmark: for function smoothness \(\beta\) and covariate dimension \(d\), the usual supremum rate is \((\log n/n)^{\beta/(2\beta+d)}\). \citet{fan1996} develops local-polynomial approximation and boundary treatment, while \citet{newey1997}, \citet{belloni2015}, and \citet{chen2015} provide convergence and uniform approximation theory for series regression. \citet{CattaneoFarrellFeng2020} develops bias, integrated mean-square error, and pointwise and uniform inference for partitioning-based estimators under its design and basis conditions; \citet{CalonicoCattaneoFarrell2018} studies coverage-error optimality of local-polynomial intervals. Adaptive bandwidth selection has a canonical foundation in \citet{lepski1997}, and \citet{tsybakov2009} supplies standard minimax testing tools. A particularly close pointwise antecedent is \citet[Assumption M and Theorem 1, pp. 3--4]{Gaiffas2005DegenerateRates}: in univariate Gaussian regression with locally symmetric design density regularly varying with index \(b>-1\), and a local polynomial-approximation modulus regularly varying with smoothness index \(s>0\), rooted pointwise \(L^p\) minimax risk is of order \(\sigma^{2s/(1+2s+b)}n^{-s/(1+2s+b)}\ell_{\omega,\nu}(n^{-1})\), where \(\sigma\) is the noise standard deviation and \(\ell_{\omega,\nu}\) is slowly varying. Its approximation class depends on sample size. \citet[Abstract]{Gaiffas2005AdaptiveDegenerateDesign} obtains joint pointwise adaptation to design and smoothness at \((\log n/n)^{s/(1+2s+b)}\ell(\log n/n)\) and establishes the necessity of the logarithmic adaptation price in that experiment.

Uniform recovery under inhomogeneous designs supplies another comparison strand. \citet[Assumption D, equation (2.1), and Theorems 1--2]{gaiffas2009} constructs a design- and smoothness-adaptive estimator for univariate Gaussian regression with continuous design density and finitely many zeros having specified local power behavior. Its spatial normalization is \(r_n(x)=Lh_n(x)^s\), where \(L\) is the smoothness radius and \(h_n(x)\) balances \(Lh_n(x)^s\) against \(\sigma\{\log n/[nP_X([x-h_n(x),x+h_n(x)])]\}^{1/2}\); the upper theorem controls expected loss of the spatially normalized supremum. \citet[Sections 1 and 3]{schmidthieber2024} establishes local optimality of least squares over univariate 1-Lipschitz functions under a local doubling condition, with uniform probability bounds normalized by the solution \(t_n(x)\) of \(t_n(x)^2P_X([x-t_n(x),x+t_n(x)])=\log n/n\). These are comparisons under spatially structured designs and spatially varying loss normalizations. Covariate-shift theory also connects local information to recovery: \citet[Section 1.1]{pathak2022} uses integrated reciprocal source-ball probabilities to characterize Hölder-regression minimax risk under a target distribution. \citet[Theorems 1--3]{MouDingWainwrightBartlett2023} derives nonasymptotic local minimax mean-square lower bounds for linear functionals over convex symmetric regression classes through a modulus of continuity, and analyzes kernel estimators attaining the associated instance-dependent scale under the stated RKHS, noise, and effective-dimension conditions. Its functional-loss criterion complements the pointwise and spatial-supremum criteria studied here.

The immediate theorem-level comparator is \citet[Assumptions 1--3 and Theorem 1(ii), p. 12]{Dorn2026GlobalOverlap}. For a fixed source family, his Gaussian uniform-design model retains common conditional-moment, variance, global-tail, and Hölder-seminorm restrictions, together with Assumption 3’s local propensity anti-concentration condition. With response smoothness \(\beta\), dimension \(d\), and global-tail exponent \(\gamma>1\), Theorem 1(ii) establishes uniform-in-family convergence in probability in \(L^\infty(P)\) at \(n^{-\beta/(2\beta+d\gamma/(\gamma-1))}\), as a pure power of sample size. The theorem also attributes computation without knowledge of the overlap exponent to its estimator. Dorn explicitly identifies robustness to degenerate local treated geometry as a future direction \citep[Assumption 3 discussion, p. 7; Section 5, p. 28]{Dorn2026GlobalOverlap}. The present paper establishes the same polynomial order for pointwise minimax risk and expected spatial-supremum minimax risk under measurable propensities satisfying the global tail bound. Its common upper class has covariate density bounded below, bounded treated conditional means, conditional sub-Gaussian residuals, and known Hölder smoothness \(\beta>1\). Equal total weighting across a fixed polynomial-unisolvent microcell template supplies deterministic conditioning; treatment-count selection adapts to the unknown overlap exponent uniformly over fixed compact ranges. The ordered treated-mass profile makes the maximal variance series summable at the stated scale, explaining the logarithm-free supremum bound. A bounded Bernoulli subclass supplies the matching pointwise converse, and the Gaussian transfer retains Dorn’s remaining source restrictions while removing Assumption 3.

The causal-learning frontier distinguishes response recovery from estimation of a smooth treatment contrast. Residualization originates in \citet{robinson1988} and supports orthogonal nuisance adjustment \citep{chernozhukov2018}, the R-learner \citep{nie2021}, and doubly robust local-polynomial estimation \citep{kennedy2023}; causal forests and metalearners provide complementary implementation frameworks \citep{wager2018,kunzel2019}, while \citet{gao2020} studies minimax heterogeneous-effect recovery as a function of covariate geometry. More precisely, \citet[Theorems 1--2 and Remark 11]{KennedyBalakrishnanRobinsWasserman2024} studies pointwise CATE mean absolute error under strict overlap. Write \(u\) for CATE smoothness and \(s=(a+b)/2\) for average propensity and control-regression smoothness. Its lower benchmark is \(n^{-1/(1+d/(2u)+d/(4s))}\) when \(s<(d/4)/(1+d/(2u))\), and \(n^{-u/(2u+d)}\) otherwise; its local-polynomial R-learner attains these rates under the stated approximation, eigenvalue, boundedness, and covariate-distribution estimation conditions. The present theorem characterizes treatment-specific response recovery under global overlap tails. Related applications include observational policy learning \citep{AtheyWager2021} and pessimistic policy learning, whose regret bounds depend on optimal-policy overlap and policy-class complexity with known behavior policies \citep[Abstract]{JinRenYangWang2022}. Recent work develops orthogonal overlap-weighted risks for longitudinal effects \citep{hess2026} and Bayesian response extrapolation for population average effects \citep{massmann2026}. Thresholded AIPW analysis \citep{Dorn2025TStatistics} and the April 2026 revision of bias-corrected doubly robust estimation \citep{MaSantAnnaSasakiUra2023} provide downstream settings in which response-regression guarantees can enter nuisance-rate conditions.
