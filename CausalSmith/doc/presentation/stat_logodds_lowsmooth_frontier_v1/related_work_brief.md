Inference for a homogeneous conditional causal odds ratio connects classical stratified association analysis with modern semiparametric causal inference. \citet{Mantel1959} established a canonical framework for combining stratum-specific odds-ratio information. Potential-outcome identification and propensity-score adjustment provide the causal framework \citep{Rubin1974,Rosenbaum1983}, while the distinction between confounding and collapsibility explains why a conditional odds contrast has its own interpretation \citep{Greenland1999}. The present paper targets one homogeneous conditional log-odds coefficient under consistency and conditional exchangeability, with a public uniform covariate distribution and prescribed Hölder restrictions on the propensity and prognosis logits. Its treatment of a scalar parameter with infinite-dimensional nuisances belongs to the semiparametric tradition of \citet{Robinson1988,Bickel1993,vanderVaart1998}. Missing-data and causal inference theory supplies the influence-function and nuisance-adjustment foundations \citep{Robins1994,vanderLaan2003,Tsiatis2006}; doubly robust estimation and modern cross-fitting develop these foundations into practical estimation procedures \citep{Bang2005,Chernozhukov2018}. \citet{Kennedy2024} surveys this methodological lineage.

The closest estimand-specific antecedents concern semiparametric odds-ratio and logistic partially linear models. \citet{TchetgenTchetgen2010} constructs doubly robust odds-ratio estimators using alternative baseline conditional-density models, and \citet{TchetgenTchetgen2013} specializes this approach to a closed-form adjusted odds-ratio estimator for binary exposure. \citet[Propositions 1--3]{Tan2019LogisticDR} develops logistic partially linear scores with consistency under either of two nuisance-model specifications and efficiency comparisons near a zero coefficient. \citet[arXiv version 1, Assumptions REG and ML1, Theorem 2]{LiuZhangZhou2020LogisticDML} establishes asymptotic normality of the scalar coefficient under its regularity conditions, uniform consistency of the prognostic-logit and reverse-exposure nuisance fits, and an \(o_P(n^{-1/4})\) sum of their \(L^2\) errors. Generalized-response treatment-effect methods extend orthogonal estimation beyond conditional mean differences, complementing the heterogeneous-effect approaches of \citet{Nie2021,Dandl2024}. In particular, \citet[Sections 2.1.1 and 3.3, equation (5), Proposition 1]{GaoHastie2025DINA} explicitly targets binary conditional log-odds contrasts. For a correctly specified finite-dimensional effect basis, bounded covariates and nuisance functions, a bounded parameter region, and a uniformly nonsingular population score derivative, its coefficient error is \(\widetilde O(c_n^2+n^{-1/2})\), where \(c_n\) is the common \(L^2\) nuisance-error scale tending to zero. The constant basis gives a homogeneous effect; efficiency follows under a correctly specified likelihood and \(c_n=o(n^{-1/4})\). \citet[Appendix A]{VansteelandtDukes2022AssumptionLean} provides a complementary framework separating generalized-linear regression-summary definitions from influence-function inference.

The paper's projected product coordinates draw on the theory of quadratic and nonlinear functional estimation. Hoeffding's decomposition supplies the basic U-statistic machinery \citep{Hoeffding1948}, while the classical quadratic-functional literature establishes smoothness-dependent minimax rates and regularity elbows \citep{Bickel1988,Donoho1990,Laurent1996,Gine2008}. Higher-order influence functions and estimating equations carry this architecture into semiparametric problems \citep{Robins2008,RobinsEtAlHigherOrderInfluenceFunctions,Robins2017}; \citet{LiuMukherjeeRobinsTchetgen2021Adaptive} develops adaptive functional estimation. Cross-fitting and deliberate nuisance tuning are further antecedents \citep{Newey2018}. For expected conditional covariance, \citet[version 5, Theorem 1 and Corollary 1]{McGrath2026} shows that, with known design density and double sample splitting, integrated projection products have bias of order \(k^{-s/d}\) and variance bounded by a constant times \(n^{-1}+k/n^2\), where \(k\) is the smaller projection rank, \(s\) is the sum of the two nuisance smoothness exponents, and \(d\) is covariate dimension. Appropriate tuning attains the covariance minimax rate throughout the Hölder regimes. \citet[arXiv version 3, Assumption 4 and Theorems 2--3]{McCleanBalakrishnanKennedyWasserman2024DCDR} obtains expected absolute error \(n^{-2s/(2s+d)}\) for \(s\le d/2\), and efficient root-\(n\) inference for \(s>d/2\), under bounded conditional moments, a compact design with density bounded above and below, and a known density whose smoothness is at least the larger nuisance exponent. Its nonregular normal approximation uses additional boundedness and continuity conditions and bandwidth \(n^{-(2+\epsilon)/(2s+d)}\), with \(0<\epsilon<4s/d\). These results establish the covariance-estimation lineage for the present numerator coordinate.

Recent work sharpens both lower-bound methodology and higher-order inference for nonlinear targets. \citet[Theorem 2.1]{RobinsTchetgenLiVanderVaart2009Minimax} supplies partition-based two-mixture affinity bounds. Structure-agnostic analysis characterizes what nuisance-error information alone permits \citep{Balakrishnan2026}, and hybrid smoothness restrictions yield further ATE improvements \citep{Bonvini2024}. \citet[Appendix B]{JinMackeySyrgkanis2025HardNormal} constructs binary identity-link alternatives retaining a common scalar coefficient and establishes lower bounds involving quadratic treatment-nuisance error and mixed nuisance-error terms. For an unrestricted average conditional log-odds difference, \citet[version 2, Theorem 7.7]{JinSyrgkanis2025GeneralLinearLower} proves an expected-absolute-error lower bound of order \(u_n^2+u_nv_n+n^{-1/2}\), where \(u_n\) and \(v_n\) are the logit-regression and treatment-Riesz-representer \(L^2\) neighborhood radii. Its conditions include interior arm risks and propensity, nondegenerate covariate support, and treated-risk curvature on a set of positive covariate probability. For implicit treatment moments, \citet[Section 3.2, Theorems 1 and 3--4]{ZhangLiuZhang2026GeneralTreatmentHOIF} gives, under its boundedness, smoothness, basis, invertibility, and local inverse-slope conditions, an error bound comprising \((\sqrt{k}/n\vee n^{-1/2})\log n\), a product of projection residual norms, and a feasible-estimation term equal to two nuisance-error norms times the operator-norm Gram-matrix error; its normal approximation requires additional conditions controlling that last term. The 2026 black-box partial-linear frontier also separates approximation from stochastic learning error. \citet[Sections 2.4--2.5]{Gu2026} obtains matching one-learner coefficient rates \(n^{-1/2}+a_\mu a_\pi+s^2\), where \(a_\mu,a_\pi\) are approximation budgets and \(s\) is a stochastic complexity radius. \citet[Section 3, Theorem 4.1 and Corollary 4.3]{Hu2026} extends this budget analysis to two learners: for bounded identity-link laws with uniform design and residual treatment variance bounded away from zero, its finite-sample MSE lower bound is proportional to
\(1\wedge\{n^{-1}+[a_\mu a_\pi+\min(a_\pi s_\mu+s_\pi^2,a_\mu s_\pi+s_\mu^2)]^2\}\).
The matching comparison holds for sufficiently large samples along vanishing-budget sequences. These results provide supporting inference and testing machinery in their respective statistical classes; the present comparison experiment uses continuous homogeneous logistic laws with prescribed native-logit smoothness.

The decision criterion connects to the honest-confidence-interval literature: uniform coverage constrains attainable expected length \citep{Low1997}, and \citet[Theorem 1]{CaiLow2004ConfidenceAdaptation} formalizes ambient coverage with subclass length evaluation through moduli in Gaussian experiments. \citet{Armstrong2018} develops optimal inference over regression-function classes. Within the present binary experiment, the contribution characterizes the sharp globally honest expected-length profile
\[
n^{-\min\{1/2,\,2(\alpha+\beta)/(2\alpha+2\beta+1)\}}
+r\,n^{-4\beta/(4\beta+1)},
\]
where \(\alpha,\beta\) are the public propensity- and prognosis-logit Hölder exponents and \(r\) is the effect evaluation radius. The characterization covers \(0<\beta<1/4\), \(\beta<\alpha\le1\), and every radius in \([0,1/2]\), with pure-power matching bounds, one radius-independent interval honest at every finite sample size, and original-record converses whose every prior draw retains one homogeneous effect. This advances the credited known-fair and fixed-\((0.3,0.1)\) support problems \citep{PriorFairRadius,PriorFixedAnisotropic} to the complete public-exponent domain; the credited rough-design proposal instead concerns fixed exponents and an unknown bounded density \citep{PriorRoughDesign}. These internal proposals supply research provenance and inherited supporting constructions. The prospective software connection is concrete: the author-maintained \texttt{npOddsRatio::spOR} function defaults to a constant effect formula and returns normal-theory endpoints using \(1.96\) times its estimated standard error \citep[function \texttt{spOR}, default argument and interval-return code]{VanDerLaanSpOR}. The theorem supplies a theoretical precision benchmark for this scalar conditional-odds target, including the root-\(n\) null regime when \(\alpha+\beta\ge1/2\) and the effect-sensitive precision required across evaluation radii.
