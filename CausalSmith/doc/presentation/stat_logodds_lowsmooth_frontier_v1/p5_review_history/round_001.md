# Referee review

**Recommendation:** major_revision
**Overall score:** 7.2/10 — The verified characterization is a substantial theoretical contribution with strong claim fidelity, but the organization and extensive duplication require substantial revision for journal readers.

The paper characterizes minimax expected interval length for a homogeneous conditional log-odds coefficient under a known uniform scalar design and public nuisance smoothness exponents. Its effect-dependent profile, simultaneous attainment across radii, and continuous homogeneous-model alternatives form a coherent and potentially important contribution. The principal statistical claims faithfully reflect the supplied verification contract. Publication requires a clearer presentation that foregrounds the statistical result and explains the converse while organizing the computational and formal details separately.

## Strengths
- The coverage class and length-evaluation subclasses are clearly distinguished, making the null and shrinking-radius comparisons meaningful.
- The verified result combines matching bounds, finite-sample honesty, absolute comparison constants, and one radius-independent attaining procedure.
- The continuous calibrated alternatives preserve homogeneity and native-logit smoothness for every prior draw, directly addressing the specified statistical model.
- The dedicated early related-work section engages close competitors through their targets, rates, and conditions.
- The arithmetic guarantees carefully retain valid-input conditions and permit implementation-dependent rational outputs.

## Findings
- **[major·structure] Sharp expected length across effect radii; global** — The central theorem is buried beneath extensive definitions of numerical representations, arithmetic engines, calibration maps, and lower-bound priors. The paper then repeats substantially the same comparison in three long theorem statements and repeatedly reproduces model conditions, rate formulas, and dependency lists. This organization obscures the contribution and makes the manuscript read as a declaration inventory.
  - *Fix:* Present the model, length objective, observable identity, and one canonical statistical theorem consecutively. Move the detailed arithmetic contracts and calibrated-prior definitions to their corresponding appendices. Consolidate repeated synthesis statements and replace long dependency inventories with focused references or a compact traceability table, preserving exact conditions and valid anchors.
- **[minor·prose] Constructing an honest interval; Original-record lower bounds and sharp comparison** — The main exposition explains the upper construction much more effectively than the converse. Exact singleton matching and latent-sign components are mentioned, but readers receive little explanation of why these constructions generate the two different terms in the profile. This leaves a central source of novelty difficult to assess before entering lengthy appendices.
  - *Fix:* Add a concise converse overview in the main text. Explain that the mixed family separates effects at the product-amplitude scale, the fair family separates them at the effect-times-squared-amplitude scale, and the respective original-record Hellinger bounds determine their resolutions. Describe why shared endpoint signs require component factorization after conditioning on covariates. Cite the existing verified statements without reproducing their proofs.
- **[minor·prose] Model and honest interval criterion** — The preliminary specification 'An interval output belongs to \(\mathcal C=\{[l,u]:-1/2\le l\le u\le1/2\}\)' restricts the comparison class to closed intervals. Subsequently, \cref{obj:def:honest-procedures} describes nonempty connected Borel intervals, and the supplied formal comparison class permits different endpoint-inclusion choices. The manuscript therefore gives inconsistent descriptions of the competitors covered by the universal converse.
  - *Fix:* Define the comparison output space using all admissible nonempty connected effect intervals, including endpoint-inclusion indicators and their measurable structure. State separately that the constructed attaining procedure always returns a closed interval with rational endpoints.
- **[minor·prose] Rational realization and the upper bound** — The sentence 'These are mathematical termination and enclosure guarantees for the stated computational model; they do not assert a practical running-time benchmark' explains scope through an absent deliverable outside the explicitly titled limitations subsection.
  - *Fix:* Replace it with an affirmative description: 'The guarantees establish termination and endpoint enclosure under the stated rational computation model, with public bounds on primitive iteration counts.' Keep discussion of practical benchmarking in the limitations subsection.
- **[minor·prose] Constructing an honest interval** — The sentence 'Detailed numerical admissibility is deferred to Appendix B' manually names and numbers an internal target, contrary to the exclusive cleveref requirement. It is also inaccurate about placement because extensive numerical admissibility definitions already precede the main theorem.
  - *Fix:* After reorganizing the numerical material, give the rational-realization appendix a structural label and refer to it exclusively through \cref or \Cref. Update the sentence to describe the material's actual location.
- **[minor·other] Verification note** — The reproduction instruction 'run the project Lean build for that module tree' leaves the executable command, toolchain, dependency setup, and retrievable source location unspecified. The revision identifier and internal paths provide useful traceability, but a publication reader needs a concrete route from those identifiers to the artifact.
  - *Fix:* Supply a stable source or archive link at the stated revision, the exact build command and working directory, and the toolchain and dependency manifest locations. Identify where readers obtain the crosswalk, snippets, bibliography, and macro files needed to reproduce the manuscript and its verification.
- **[nit·statement] Original-record lower bounds and sharp comparison** — The phrase 'using the identically false sign vector' in \cref{obj:lem:original-record-mixtures} introduces Boolean implementation terminology into a presentation where sign vectors take values in \(\{-1,1\}\).
  - *Fix:* Use 'the constant sign vector with every entry equal to \(-1\)' and retain the explanation that the deterministic comparator is independent of the supplied signs.
