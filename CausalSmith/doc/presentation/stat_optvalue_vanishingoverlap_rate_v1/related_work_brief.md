The paper belongs to the literature on causal identification, individualized treatment, and statistical decision making. Potential outcomes and adjustment under conditional exchangeability provide its causal foundation \citep{Rubin1974,Rosenbaum1983,Hernan2020}; semiparametric efficiency, augmented weighting, and estimation of unknown propensities provide the classical estimation background \citep{Robins1994,Hahn1998,Hirano2003}. Murphy’s optimal-regime formulation and Manski’s welfare-based treatment-choice framework establish the population benchmark obtained by assigning the best treatment at each covariate profile \citep{Murphy2003,Manski2004}. Here that benchmark is the unrestricted oracle value, the population mean of the larger conditional outcome mean. With \(n\) observed units, \(d\) categorical cells, and \(\epsilon\) the public overlap floor, \cref{thm:matched-frontier} characterizes its minimax squared risk as \(\min\{1,d/[n\epsilon\log(ed)]\}\), uniformly over unknown cell masses and propensities, Bernoulli outcomes, null cells, and exact ties. This uniform finite-sample characterization complements the fixed-law asymptotic framework summarized by \citet{VanderVaart1998}. Causal identification follows the established adjustment framework; the statistical contribution concerns estimation of the cellwise maximum under jointly growing support and shrinking overlap.

A second strand studies learning a treatment rule and evaluating specified policies. Outcome-weighted learning connects treatment choice to weighted classification \citep[Section 2.2]{Zhao2012}, while margin conditions explain faster excess-risk rates in plug-in classification \citep{Audibert2007}. Empirical welfare maximization and efficient observational policy learning provide regret guarantees over policy classes under their respective overlap and nuisance-estimation conditions \citep{Kitagawa2018,Athey2021}; local asymptotic decision theory supplies another treatment-choice benchmark \citep{Hirano2009}. With bounded rewards and known behavior policies, \citet[Theorem 4.1 and Corollary 4.3]{JinRenYangWang2025Pessimism} obtain pessimistic-policy regret bounded by twice a complexity multiplier times the optimal policy’s empirical uncertainty radius. If the behavior probability of the optimal action is bounded below by \(c_{\mathrm{opt}}>0\), their corollary gives, up to logarithmic factors, \(\sqrt{\mathrm{Ndim}(\Pi)/(nc_{\mathrm{opt}})}\), where \(\mathrm{Ndim}(\Pi)\) is policy-class complexity. Off-policy evaluation has a related minimax lineage \citep{Wang2017}. For bounded-reward multi-armed bandits with known behavior probabilities, \citet[Theorem 1 and equation (15)]{Ma2022} characterize squared risk by a switch estimator’s squared omitted target mass plus its retained importance-weight variance, uniformly in sample size. Their unknown-behavior and public-minimum-probability results further connect nuisance uncertainty to polynomial approximation. These results concern policy regret or a specified policy’s value; the present frontier estimates the population scalar defined by unrestricted optimization.

Optimal-value inference forms the closest estimand-specific asymptotic strand. Targeted learning supplies plug-in and influence-function constructions \citep{VanderLaan2006,VanderLaan2011,VanderLaan2015}. \citet[Theorem 1]{LuedtkeVanderLaan2016OptimalValue} characterize pathwise differentiability through treatment-effect ties and conditional outcome variances, and develop inference under additional convergence and variance conditions. \citet[Assumptions A3--A4 and Theorems 2--3]{Shi2020} obtain studentized root-\(n\) normality through subagging under fixed positivity, sufficiently fast expected policy-value convergence, suitable subsample and aggregation sizes, nondegenerate variance, and nuisance mean-square errors \(o(n^{-1/2})\). \citet[Assumption 2, Theorem 3.3, and Corollary 3.4]{WhitehouseChenAusternSyrgkanis2025} obtain root-\(n\) asymptotic linearity and normality under strong positivity and controlled densities of positive action gaps near zero. Their smoothing parameter \(\beta_n\), the inverse temperature, must satisfy \(\beta_n\gg n^{1/[2(1+\delta)]}\), where \(\delta>0\) is the gap-density exponent; outcome-regression error must be \(o_P(n^{-1/4}\beta_n^{-1/2})\), with an \(o_P(n^{-1/2})\) product of regression and weighting-nuisance errors and additional boundedness and consistency conditions. The density condition permits exact ties. Differentiability-based allocation inference extends this frontier to constrained and multiclass problems \citep{Feng2026}, alongside general orthogonal-score methods \citep{Chernozhukov2018}. The present model instead supplies a uniform risk characterization across the full finite-cell class.

The methodological lineage runs through approximation theory and minimax functional estimation. Classical polynomial approximation and endpoint-sensitive moduli underpin Jackson constructions \citep{Timan1963,Ditzian1987}; optimal recovery and testing methods relate functional separation to statistical difficulty \citep{Donoho1994,Tsybakov2009}. Entropy estimation established the importance of bias and unseen categories \citep{Paninski2003,Valiant2017}. \citet{JiaoVenkatHanWeissman2015Functionals} combine polynomial approximation in nonsmooth regions with unbiased polynomial estimation and bias correction in smooth regions; \citet{Wu2016} connect approximation duality to sharp large-alphabet entropy rates. Composite-prior testing for absolute-value functionals is developed by \citet{Cai2011}. More directly, \citet[Theorems 3--4 and 6]{JiaoHanWeissman2018L1} obtain worst-case \(L_1\)-distance squared-risk order \(d/(n\log n)\) in the stated nonsaturated regimes with controlled logarithmic sample-to-alphabet growth. Local moment matching provides a broader distribution-estimation construction \citep{HanJiaoWeissman2018Local}, and support-size estimation makes approximation–moment duality explicit \citep{WuYang2016Chebyshev,Wu2020}. The present construction adapts this architecture to maxima of unknown observational arm ratios: armwise Jackson sensitivity gives the attaining estimator, while \cref{thm:weighted-separation-and-frontier} supplies weighted separation of order \(\sqrt M/K\), with \(M\) the sparse-intensity endpoint and \(K\) the matched-moment degree, together with common-mean probability priors and a full-likelihood causal reduction.

The nearest discrete-causal comparison is \citet[Theorems 1--2]{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2024Discrete}, using the June 2026 revision. For a linear causal arm mean, their empirical estimator has squared-risk upper bound \((1-\epsilon)^2d^2/(\epsilon^2n^2)+C/(\epsilon n)\); for \(d\lesssim n\log n\), their minimax lower bound is of order \(d^2/(n^2\log^2 n)+1/n\), with overlap-dependent constants. Limited-overlap theory additionally characterizes optimal trimming, irregular inverse weighting, robust IPW inference, and shrinking-propensity finite-population limits \citep{Crump2009,Khan2010,Ma2020,Hong2020}. For linear observational functionals over convex symmetric regression classes, \citet{Mou2023} give nonasymptotic local minimax bounds attained within constants for reproducing-kernel Hilbert spaces. The supplied accepted same-target Jackson–factorial archive establishes \(\min\{1,d/[n\log(ed)]\}\) for fixed \(0<\epsilon<1/2\), with comparison constants depending on that floor \citep{CausalSmithFixedOverlap2026}. The present frontier makes the constants universal across \(0<\epsilon\leq1/2\) and yields the consistency condition \(d/[n\epsilon\log(ed)]\to0\). It therefore supplies an oracle-value precision benchmark for the tlverse workflow, whose maintained chapter explicitly targets the population value of a sample-fitted rule \citep[``Defining the causal effect of an optimal individualized intervention'']{tlverseOptimalTreatment}. Leading-constant calibration remains a distinct research direction recorded in the supplied downgraded archive \citep{CausalSmithSharpConstant2026}.
