\begin{abstract}
This paper studies design-based variance estimation for random group-formation experiments in finite populations. Units are assigned to disjoint groups of a fixed common size \(M\), the group-size parameter, and treatment is randomized across realized groups. The target is the finite-population partition-average marginal effect, defined by averaging composition-indexed potential outcomes over the uniform \(M\)-slice of possible groups. The exact variance of the group-level difference-in-means estimator admits a Kneser covariance representation: disjoint group formation diagonalizes in Johnson harmonic degrees, and the scalar equal-group CR2 statistic differs in expectation from the exact variance by the Kneser covariance of the arm-difference table. In triangular arrays with growing group count \(G_n\), the number of realized groups, stable treatment fraction, bounded potential outcomes, and grouped-unit sampling fraction \(N_n/n\) converging to \(\rho\), the limiting grouped-unit fraction, the exact design variance \(\sigma_n^2\) of the difference-in-means estimator satisfies, after scaling by \(G_n\),
\[
G_n\sigma_n^2=R_n-\rho E_{\tau,1,n}+o(1),
\]
where \(R_n\) is the independent-group leading variance scale and \(E_{\tau,1,n}\) is the degree-one Johnson energy of the arm contrast. Consequently, the equal-group CR2 statistic estimates \(R_n/G_n\), is asymptotically conservative for the exact variance in the stated one-sided probability sense, and is ratio-consistent exactly when the dense correction is negligible relative to the corrected variance scale. At zero sampling density, ratio consistency holds under \(N_n/n\to0\), including regimes with \(N_n^2/n\to\infty\). The paper also identifies the scalar statistic returned by a versioned \texttt{clubSandwich} CR2 calculation, gives an eight-unit witness with exact variance \(1/7\) and expected CR2 value \(2/7\), and proves a positive-density lower bound: for a fixed group size \(M\ge2\), a strictly positive limiting grouped-unit fraction \(\rho>0\), an outcome envelope \(B\ge1\), and a variance floor \(c_\sigma\) below the separated independent-arm limit \(1/[Mp(1-p)]-2\rho/M\), every measurable one-realization variance statistic has nonvanishing worst-case relative error over the bounded schedule class with those parameters.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Random group formation is common in experiments where outcomes depend on who interacts with whom. Classrooms, teams, peer groups, neighborhoods, and online clusters can all be randomized objects rather than fixed sampling units. In such designs, the realized groups are typically disjoint: once a unit is placed in one group, it is removed from the pool available to the others. This finite-population dependence changes the variance target for a group-level treatment contrast, even when treatment is randomized across groups after the partition is formed.

This paper gives a design-based account of that variance target for homogeneous random partitions. The finite population has size \(n\), the common group size is fixed at \(M\), and \(G_n\) disjoint groups are drawn uniformly before a balanced treatment assignment is applied across groups. Potential outcomes are deterministic functions of the treatment arm and the realized group. The estimand is the partition-average marginal effect, obtained by averaging the contrast of group-level potential-outcome tables over the uniform \(M\)-slice of possible groups. This places random group formation inside the finite-population potential-outcome tradition of \citet{Neyman1923}, \citet{Fisher1935}, \citet{Cox1958}, \citet{Rubin1974}, and \citet{Holland1986}.

The central object is the covariance induced by drawing disjoint groups. On the uniform \(M\)-slice, the normalized Kneser disjointness operator averages a group table over all groups disjoint from a given group. The Johnson association scheme diagonalizes this operator degree by degree. \Cref{obj:thm:exact-kneser-identity} uses that diagonalization to express the covariance of two centered group tables as a sum of Johnson harmonic inner products multiplied by finite-\(n\) Kneser eigenvalues. Applied to the arm-difference table, this identity identifies the exact finite-population covariance term generated by group formation.

The exact design variance follows from combining this Kneser covariance with balanced treatment assignment. \Cref{obj:thm:exact-pame-variance} establishes that the group-level difference in means is unbiased for the partition-average marginal effect and that its exact variance decomposes into an independent-group component plus ordered-disjoint covariance terms. The same theorem shows that the expected scalar equal-group CR2 statistic differs from the exact variance by exactly the negative of the Kneser covariance contrast. Thus the discrepancy between the usual cluster calculation and the finite-population randomization variance is determined by the geometry of the full potential group tables.

The asymptotic analysis studies triangular arrays with fixed \(M\), growing group count, stable treated-group fraction \(p_n\to p\in(0,1)\), bounded potential outcomes, nondegenerate scaled exact variance, and grouped-unit sampling fraction \(N_n/n\to\rho\in[0,1]\). Under these conditions, \cref{obj:thm:dense-projection-limit} gives the expansion
\[
G_n\sigma_n^2=R_n-\rho E_{\tau,1,n}+o(1),
\]
where \(R_n\) is the independent-group leading variance scale and \(E_{\tau,1,n}\) is the degree-one Johnson energy of the arm-difference table. The degree-one term has a direct finite-population interpretation: it is the component of the treatment-control group-table contrast carried by marginal unit participation, and disjoint sampling makes that component contribute at first order whenever the limiting grouped-unit fraction is positive.

This expansion yields the CR2 phase frontier. \Cref{obj:thm:cr2-phase-frontier} shows that the scalar equal-group CR2 statistic estimates \(R_n/G_n\) in probability, while the exact variance uses the corrected scale \((R_n-\rho E_{\tau,1,n})/G_n\). The theorem gives a one-sided asymptotic conservativeness statement and characterizes ratio consistency by the relative-energy condition
\[
\frac{\rho E_{\tau,1,n}}{R_n-\rho E_{\tau,1,n}}\to0.
\]
At zero sampling density, \cref{obj:thm:sparse-beyond-birthday} gives ratio consistency under \(N_n/n\to0\), including the benchmark \(N_n=M\lfloor n^{3/4}/M\rfloor\), for which \(N_n^2/n\to\infty\). This connects the dense calculation to the sparse intuition in \citet{FuSamiiWang2026} while identifying the sampling fraction that governs the scalar variance ratio in the present fixed-\(M\) random-partition setting.

The paper also relates the population calculation to applied regression software. In the equal-group design, \cref{obj:prop:clubsandwich-consumer} identifies the treatment-coordinate entry returned by \texttt{clubSandwich::vcovCR} with type \texttt{CR2}, for the stated unweighted intercept-plus-treatment regression and versioned \texttt{sandwich} bread convention, with the scalar statistic analyzed in the design theory. \Cref{obj:prop:eight-unit-witness} then gives a finite eight-unit, four-pair, two-treated-group schedule in which the exact variance is \(1/7\) and the expected CR2 statistic is \(2/7\). The witness makes the dense correction visible in a small balanced design.

Finally, the one-realization result gives an identification-theoretic interpretation of the dense correction. \Cref{obj:prop:rademacher-mixture-separation} constructs same-sign and independent-arm Rademacher schedule priors whose one-realization observed-data mixtures agree while their scaled exact variances converge to separated limits at positive density. \Cref{obj:thm:qv-diagonal-impossibility} converts this comparison into a uniform lower bound over the dense bounded schedule class: for some positive \(\varepsilon\), every measurable statistic of the realized partition, assignment, and observed outcomes has worst-case relative error probability at least one half asymptotically. The obstruction is the degree-one cross-arm component of the potential group tables, which enters the exact variance but is not uniformly recoverable from a single realized grouping and assignment.

The formal statements and internal algebraic derivations are machine-checked in Lean 4; the verification appendix records the precise scope. The paper proceeds as follows. \Cref{sec:related-work} places the results in the design-based, survey-sampling, cluster-robust, and interference literatures. \Cref{sec:setup} defines the random group-formation design, the partition-average marginal effect, the Johnson decomposition, and the scalar CR2 statistic. \Cref{sec:exact-variance} proves the Kneser covariance identity and the finite-sample PAME variance formula. \Cref{sec:dense-sparse} derives the projection limit, the CR2 phase frontier, and the zero-density ratio result. \Cref{sec:software-witness} connects the scalar statistic to the versioned \texttt{clubSandwich} calculation and evaluates the eight-unit witness. \Cref{sec:lower-bound} proves the one-realization obstruction, \cref{sec:discussion} summarizes the scope and extensions, and \cref{sec:appendix} collects the proofs and the verification note.
