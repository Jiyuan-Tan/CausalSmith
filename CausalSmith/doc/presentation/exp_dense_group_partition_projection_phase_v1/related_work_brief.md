The paper belongs to the design-based tradition in which randomization, rather than a superpopulation model, supplies the sampling law. The foundational line runs from Neyman's finite-population potential-outcome formulation and Fisherian randomization logic through Cox's articulation of noninterference, Rubin's potential-outcome framework, and Holland's synthesis of statistics and causal inference \citep{Neyman1923,Fisher1935,Cox1958,Rubin1974,Holland1986}. Its finite-population asymptotic language is anchored in survey sampling and randomization central limit theory, especially \citet{Hajek1960}, \citet{Cochran1977}, \citet{Ohlsson1989}, \citet{Fuller2009}, and the general finite-population CLTs of \citet{LiDing2017}. The Kneser/Johnson covariance identity draws on the algebraic-combinatorial strand represented by association schemes and distance-regular graphs \citep{Delsarte1973,BrouwerCohenNeumaier1989}, with the slice-harmonic basis supplied by \citet{Filmus2016} and the Kneser spectrum recorded in \citet{BrouwerEtAl2018}.

A second strand concerns robust variance estimation for regression and clustered designs. \citet{White1980} initiated heteroskedasticity-consistent covariance estimation, \citet{LiangZeger1986} developed estimating-equation covariance calculations for clustered/longitudinal data, and \citet{CameronMiller2015} and \citet{Abadie2023} give econometric guidance on cluster-robust inference and the design or sampling reasons for clustering. Small-sample leverage corrections trace through \citet{BellMcCaffrey2002}, \citet{ImbensKolesar2016}, and \citet{PustejovskyTipton2018}; the latter develops bias-reduced linearization and Satterthwaite-type testing for fixed-effects models under an independent-cluster working covariance model. \citet{SuDing2021} gives a design-based comparison of individual-level, cluster-average, and cluster-total regression analyses in cluster-randomized experiments, showing how targets and robust standard errors depend on cluster-size weighting and model-assisted structure. The present paper identifies the exact scalar equal-group CR2 quantity returned by the versioned \texttt{clubSandwich} and \texttt{sandwich} implementation pathway \citep{ClubSandwich2026,Sandwich2026,Zeileis2004,Zeileis2006,ZeileisKollGraham2020}, then characterizes its dense random-partition probability limit and ratio-consistency frontier.

The interference literature supplies the causal estimand and observation-channel context. \citet{Sobel2006}, \citet{HudgensHalloran2008}, \citet{TchetgenTchetgen2012}, and \citet{Manski2013} formalize causal effects under interference, partial interference, and social interactions. \citet{LiuHudgens2014} establishes large-sample randomization inference under partial interference; \citet{AronowSamii2017} develops exposure-mapping estimators with variance estimators and asymptotic normality under known arbitrary interference and local dependence. Network and spatial experiment methods further develop exposure-based and design-based analysis: \citet{Ugander2013} and \citet{Eckles2017} use graph cluster randomization to reduce bias from network interference, \citet{Athey2018} constructs exact p-values for non-sharp network-interference nulls by an artificial-experiment device, \citet{Wang2025} defines and estimates spatial average marginalized effects under unknown interference, \citet{Samii2023} implements related spatial-inference tools, and \citet{Gao2025} gives regression-based network-experiment estimators and standard-error theory with design-based properties.

The closest group-formation and variance-estimation frontier is more specialized. \citet{Li2019} develops randomization inference for peer effects with arbitrary peer counts and peer types; \citet{BasseDingFellerToulis2024} provides exact finite-sample permutation tests for peer effects in group-formation experiments; \citet{Xu2021} extends randomization inference to composite group-formation experiments with a second intervention and spillovers; and \citet{PuelzBasseFellerToulis2022} uses bicliques to make broad interference nulls sharp for conditional randomization tests. \citet{FuSamiiWang2026} is the theorem-level comparator: for group interaction experiments with fixed or randomly formed groups and fixed or interference potential outcomes, its Section 3 and Theorems 1--2 establish asymptotic normality and CR2 ratio consistency under boundedness, its Assumption 1, and the sparse whole-tuple condition \(N_n^2/n\to0\). The present paper works in the homogeneous fixed-\(M\) random-partition subcase and establishes exact Kneser variance identities, the expansion \(G_n\sigma_n^2=R_n-\rho E_{\tau,1,n}+o(1)\), and the CR2 phase frontier; at \(\rho=0\) the ratio result holds under the weaker sampling-fraction condition \(N_n/n\to0\), including regimes such as \(N_n\asymp n^{3/4}\).

The variance-nonidentification strand explains the lower-bound side. \citet{AronowGreenLee2014} gives sharp conservative bounds for the unidentifiable variance term in ordinary complete randomization, while \citet{BasseAiroldi2018} shows nonconcentration phenomena for design-based inference under arbitrary and network interference. \citet{HarshawMiddletonSavje2026} formulates conservative variance estimation under interference and complex designs as an optimization problem over estimable quadratic upper bounds, and \citet{ParkWager2026} proposes a Neyman-jackknife framework for conservative design-based variance estimation under interference. The present lower bound uses equal observed-data Rademacher mixtures with separated dense PAME variance limits, yielding a positive-density nonextension theorem for one-realization exact-variance ratio consistency over the full bounded composition-interference class while operating on an additive no-interference subclass.
