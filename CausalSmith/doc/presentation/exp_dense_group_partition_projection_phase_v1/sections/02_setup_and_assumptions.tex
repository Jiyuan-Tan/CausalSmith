\section{Setup and assumptions}\label{sec:setup}

Throughout, \(n\) indexes the finite population size and \(M\) is the fixed common group size. Row-dependent quantities carry an \(n\) subscript, all limits are along the triangular array, and generic finite constants may change from line to line. Expectations, inner products, and norms below are finite-population objects taken over the uniform slice of possible groups.

Three row-level quantities recur in the conditions below and are fixed once here. The realized group count is \(G_n\); the number of grouped units is
\[
N_n=MG_n,
\]
the total number of population units that end up inside a realized group; and \(\sigma_n^2=\operatorname{Var}(\widehat\tau_n)\) is the exact design variance of the group-level difference-in-means estimator of \cref{obj:def:pame-estimator}, taken over the joint law of the realized groups and the treatment assignment. The asymptotic analysis rests on the number of realized groups and on the treatment, sampling, boundedness, and variance-scale restrictions imposed on the triangular array.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:group-count-growth}[Group-count growth]
The number of realized groups diverges:
\[
G_n \to \infty .
\]
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:group-count-growth} is the growing number of randomized groups condition, a standard route for design-based asymptotics with many randomized clusters \citep{FuSamiiWang2026}. It is the dimension along which the random partition supplies repeated group-level observations.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_11}[Row treatment fraction]
For a triangular schedule array with row group count \(G_n\) and treated-group count \(G_{1n}\), where \(0<G_{1n}<G_n\), the row treatment fraction is
\[
p_n=\frac{G_{1n}}{G_n}.
\]
\end{definitionv}

The number of treated groups \leanref{sym:G_{1n}}{\ensuremath{G_{1n}}} determines the row treatment fraction in \cref{obj:synth_11}; the number of control groups \leanref{sym:G_{0n}}{\ensuremath{G_{0n}}} is the complementary count used in the difference-in-means estimator below.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:stable-treatment-fraction}[Stable treatment fraction]
The treated-group fraction \(p_n\) converges to an interior limit:
\[
p_n \to p \in (0,1).
\]
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:stable-treatment-fraction} is the stable complete-randomization arm fractions condition \citep{LiDing2017}. The interior limiting treated-group fraction \leanref{sym:p}{\ensuremath{p}} keeps both treatment arms asymptotically represented in the randomized group assignment.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:sampling-fraction}[Sampling fraction limit]
The grouped-unit fraction \(N_n/n\) converges:
\[
\frac{N_n}{n} \to \rho \in [0,1].
\]
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:sampling-fraction} is the finite-population sampling-fraction limit condition \citep{Ohlsson1989}. It records the limiting density of grouped units in the population, allowing the same notation to cover sparse and dense grouping regimes.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:bounded-potential-outcomes}[Bounded potential outcomes]
There is a finite uniform outcome envelope \(B\) such that
\[
\sup_{n,i,z,A}\left|Y_{i,n}(z,A)\right| \le B < \infty .
\]
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:bounded-potential-outcomes} is the uniformly bounded composition potential outcomes condition \citep{FuSamiiWang2026}. The deterministic potential outcome \leanref{sym:Y_{i,n}(z,A)}{\ensuremath{Y_{i,n}(z,A)}} may depend on the unit, arm, and realized group, while the uniform envelope \leanref{sym:B}{\ensuremath{B}} supplies a common finite bound across rows.

\begin{assumptionv}{ass:scaled-variance-nondegeneracy}[Scaled variance nondegeneracy]
For a feasible triangular schedule array with common group size \(M\ge 2\), positive group counts \(G_n\), treated-group counts satisfying \(0<G_{1n}<G_n\), grouped size \(MG_n\le n\), and deterministic potential-outcome schedules, there is a scaled-variance lower-bound constant \(c_\sigma\) such that \(c_\sigma>0\) and
\[
c_\sigma \le \liminf_{n\to\infty} G_n\sigma_n^2 .
\]
\end{assumptionv}

\Cref{obj:ass:scaled-variance-nondegeneracy} is the nondegenerate scaled design variance condition \citep{LiDing2017}. The scaled-variance lower-bound constant \leanref{sym:c_\sigma}{\ensuremath{c_\sigma}} fixes the normalization used by the asymptotic variance comparisons.

We next define the finite-population design and the function spaces on which its variance geometry is expressed.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_2}[Treatment-arm set]
The treatment-arm set is the two-point set
\[
\mathcal Z=\{0,1\}.
\]
\end{definitionv}

The treatment-arm set \leanref{sym:\mathcal Z}{\ensuremath{\mathcal Z}} is used throughout for the two arms of the group-level potential-outcome tables.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_1}[Feasible schedule array]
A feasible triangular schedule array with common group size \(M\) consists of the following data:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Group size.)} A natural number \(M\) satisfying \(2\le M\).
\item \textbf{(Row sizes.)} For each row \(r\in\mathbb N\), a population size \(n_r\in\mathbb N\), a number of groups \(G_r\in\mathbb N\), and a number of treated groups \(G_{1r}\in\mathbb N\).
\item \textbf{(Feasibility.)} For every row \(r\),
\[
0<G_r,\qquad M G_r\le n_r,\qquad 0<G_{1r},\qquad G_{1r}<G_r,\qquad G_{1r}\le G_r .
\]
\item \textbf{(Potential-outcome schedule.)} For every row \(r\), a deterministic potential-outcome schedule
\[
Y_r:\Omega_{n_r,M}\to \{i\in \{0,\ldots,n_r-1\}: i\in A\}\to \mathcal Z\to \mathbb R,
\]
where \(A\in\Omega_{n_r,M}\) is the realized group argument of \(Y_r\).
\end{itemize}
\end{definitionv}

\Cref{obj:synth_1} collects the rowwise feasibility restrictions used in the design: the group size is common, each row has positive groups and a nontrivial treated count, and the potential-outcome schedule is deterministic.

\begin{definitionv}{def:random-partition-design}[Random partition design \((\mathbf A_n,\mathbf Z_n)\)]
Let \(\mathcal U_n\) be the finite population of \(n\) labeled units, and let
\[
\Omega_{n,M}=\{A\subseteq \mathcal U_n: |A|=M\}.
\]
The group tuple \(\mathbf A_n=(A_{1n},\ldots,A_{G_n n})\) is drawn uniformly from all ordered \(G_n\)-tuples of pairwise-disjoint elements of \(\Omega_{n,M}\). Independently, the assignment vector \(\mathbf Z_n\) is drawn uniformly from all binary vectors with exactly \(G_{1n}\) treated groups.
\end{definitionv}

\Cref{obj:def:random-partition-design} defines the homogeneous random group-formation design. The ordered group tuple \leanref{sym:\mathbf A_n}{\ensuremath{\mathbf A_n}} carves \(G_n\) pairwise-disjoint groups of common size \(M\) out of the finite population \leanref{sym:\mathcal U_n}{\ensuremath{\mathcal U_n}}, and the balanced group-treatment assignment vector \leanref{sym:\mathbf Z_n}{\ensuremath{\mathbf Z_n}} is drawn independently conditional on the realized groups. The feasibility condition \(N_n=MG_n\le n\) allows the grouped units to occupy any fraction of the population. The realized groups therefore form a disjoint packing of \(\mathcal U_n\): they are pairwise disjoint and each has size \(M\), and they cover the whole population exactly when \(N_n=n\). The general case, in which \(n-N_n\) units lie outside every realized group and contribute no observation, is what gives the sampling fraction \(N_n/n\) its range and is where the sparse and intermediate-density regimes studied below live. The design's name, \emph{random partition}, refers throughout to this packing mechanism; the saturated case \(N_n=n\) is the special case in which it is literally a partition.

\begin{definitionv}{def:group-table}[Group-level arm table \(h_{z,n}\)]
For each treatment arm \(z\in\mathcal Z\) and each group \(A\in\Omega_{n,M}\), define the group-level arm table by
\[
h_{z,n}(A)=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i\in A}Y_{i,n}(z,A).
\]
\end{definitionv}

The group-level arm table \leanref{sym:h_{z,n}}{\ensuremath{h_{z,n}}} converts unit-level composition potential outcomes into the group mean observed when group \(A\) receives arm \(z\).

\begin{definitionv}{synth_3}[Uniform slice averaging]
For integers \(M\le n\), let
\[
\Omega_{n,M}:=\{A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}: |A|=M\}.
\]
The \(M\)-slice design is the complete-randomization design on \(\Omega_{n,M}\), equivalently the uniform distribution over all \(M\)-element subsets of the \(n\)-unit population. For any real-valued table \(f:\Omega_{n,M}\to\mathbb R\), write
\[
\mathbb E_n f
:=
\frac{1}{|\Omega_{n,M}|}\sum_{A\in\Omega_{n,M}} f(A).
\]
\end{definitionv}

The uniform \(M\)-slice \leanref{sym:\Omega_{n,M}}{\ensuremath{\Omega_{n,M}}} is the population of possible groups, and the averaging operator \leanref{sym:\mathbb E_n}{\ensuremath{\mathbb E_n}} turns any group table into its finite-population slice mean.

\begin{definitionv}{def:pame-estimator}[PAME and estimator \(\tau_n,\widehat\tau_n\)]
Define the finite-population partition-average marginal effect by
\[
\tau_n=\mathbb E_n\!\left[h_{1,n}-h_{0,n}\right].
\]
The difference-in-means estimator across treated and control groups is
\[
\widehat\tau_n
=
\frac{1}{G_{1n}}\sum_g Z_{ng}h_{1,n}(A_{ng})
-
\frac{1}{G_{0n}}\sum_g (1-Z_{ng})h_{0,n}(A_{ng}).
\]
\end{definitionv}

\Cref{obj:def:pame-estimator} defines the finite-population partition-average marginal effect and its realized group-level difference-in-means estimator. The estimand averages the contrast of the two arm tables over the full slice, while the estimator uses the treatment labels and realized groups produced by \cref{obj:def:random-partition-design}.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_12}[Uniform-slice \(L^2\) space]
For integers \(n\) and \(M\) with \(2M\le n\), let \(\mathcal U_n=\{1,\ldots,n\}\) and
\[
\Omega_{n,M}=\{A\subseteq\mathcal U_n: |A|=M\}
\]
be the uniform \(M\)-slice. The space \(L^2(\Omega_{n,M})\) is the real vector space of functions \(f:\Omega_{n,M}\to\mathbb R\), equipped with uniform-slice expectation
\[
\mathbb E_n f=\binom nM^{-1}\sum_{A\in\Omega_{n,M}} f(A),
\]
inner product
\[
\langle f,g\rangle_n=\mathbb E_n[fg],
\]
and induced norm \(\|f\|_n^2=\langle f,f\rangle_n\).
\end{definitionv}

The inner product \leanref{sym:\langle\cdot}{\ensuremath{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_n}} and induced norm \leanref{sym:\|\cdot\|_n}{\ensuremath{\|\cdot\|_n}} in \cref{obj:synth_12} provide the Hilbert-space notation used to decompose group tables by Johnson degree.

\begin{definitionv}{def:johnson-decomposition}[Johnson orthogonal decomposition]
For integers \(n,M\) with \(2M\le n\), let
\[
\Omega_{n,M}:=\{A\subseteq \{1,\ldots,n\}: |A|=M\}
\]
be the uniform \(M\)-slice, let \(\mathbb E_n\) denote uniform averaging on \(\Omega_{n,M}\), and let
\[
\langle f,g\rangle_n:=\mathbb E_n[fg]
\]
be the uniform-slice inner product. For each \(k=0,\ldots,M\), let \(\mathcal H_{n,k}\) be the canonical degree-\(k\) Johnson harmonic subspace and let \(\Pi_{n,k}\) be the orthogonal projection onto \(\mathcal H_{n,k}\): \(\Pi_{n,k}\) is linear, has range \(\mathcal H_{n,k}\), fixes every element of \(\mathcal H_{n,k}\), and satisfies
\[
\langle f-\Pi_{n,k}f,g\rangle_n=0
\qquad
\text{for every }g\in\mathcal H_{n,k}.
\]
For every \(f\in L^2(\Omega_{n,M})\), define its Johnson components by
\[
f_k:=\Pi_{n,k}f,\qquad k=0,\ldots,M .
\]
These components satisfy the centered decomposition
\[
\sum_{k=1}^M \Pi_{n,k}f(A)=f(A)-\mathbb E_n f
\qquad\text{for every }A\in\Omega_{n,M},
\]
and distinct degrees are orthogonal:
\[
\langle \Pi_{n,k}f,\Pi_{n,\ell}g\rangle_n=0
\qquad\text{whenever }k\ne \ell .
\]
\end{definitionv}

The Johnson harmonic subspace \leanref{sym:\mathcal H_{n,k}}{\ensuremath{\mathcal H_{n,k}}} and projection \leanref{sym:\Pi_{n,k}}{\ensuremath{\Pi_{n,k}}} organize a centered group table into orthogonal degrees. This is the representation-theoretic coordinate system for the exact variance identities below, following the Johnson and Kneser association-scheme perspective \citep{Filmus2016,BrouwerEtAl2018}.

Two derived slice quantities enter the next two definitions. For each arm \(z\), the centered arm table is
\[
h_{z,n}^{\circ}=h_{z,n}-\mathbb E_n h_{z,n},
\]
the group-level arm table of \cref{obj:def:group-table} with its slice mean removed, as recorded in \cref{obj:synth_5}; and the uniform-slice arm variance is its energy,
\[
V_{z,n}=\|h_{z,n}^{\circ}\|_n^2=\mathbb E_n\bigl[(h_{z,n}-\mathbb E_n h_{z,n})^2\bigr],
\]
the variance of the arm-\(z\) group table under a single uniform draw from the slice. These are the only two objects needed to read the covariance and correction definitions that follow.

\begin{definitionv}{def:kneser-covariance}[Kneser covariance \(C_{ab,n}\)]
For a function \(f\) on \(\Omega_{n,M}\), define the normalized Kneser disjointness operator by
\[
(\mathsf K_n f)(A)
=
\binom{n-M}{M}^{-1}
\sum_{C:C\cap A=\varnothing} f(C).
\]
For arm tables \(h_{a,n}\) and \(h_{b,n}\), define the ordered-disjoint covariance by
\[
C_{ab,n}
=
\left\langle h_{a,n}^{\circ},\mathsf K_n h_{b,n}^{\circ}\right\rangle_n .
\]
\end{definitionv}

\Cref{obj:def:kneser-covariance} records the covariance generated by sampling disjoint groups. The normalized disjointness operator \leanref{sym:\mathsf K_n}{\ensuremath{\mathsf K_n}} averages over groups disjoint from a given group, while the centered arm table \leanref{sym:h_{z,n}^{\circ}}{\ensuremath{h_{z,n}^{\circ}}} and ordered-disjoint covariance \leanref{sym:C_{ab,n}}{\ensuremath{C_{ab,n}}} isolate the finite-population dependence induced by the partition.

\begin{definitionv}{def:dense-correction}[Dense correction terms \(E_{\tau,1,n},R_n\)]
Define the degree-one Johnson energy of the arm-difference table by
\[
E_{\tau,1,n}
=
\left\|\Pi_{n,1}(h_{1,n}-h_{0,n})\right\|_n^2 .
\]
Define the independent-group leading variance scale by
\[
R_n
=
\frac{V_{1,n}}{p_n}
+
\frac{V_{0,n}}{1-p_n}.
\]
\end{definitionv}

The degree-one energy \leanref{sym:E_{\tau,1,n}}{\ensuremath{E_{\tau,1,n}}} is the part of the arm-difference table carried by the linear Johnson component. The uniform-slice arm variance \leanref{sym:V_{z,n}}{\ensuremath{V_{z,n}}} enters \cref{obj:def:dense-correction} through the independent-group leading variance scale.

\begin{definitionv}{def:cr2-statistic}[CR2 statistic \(\widehat V_{\mathrm{CR2},n}\)]
For each arm \(z\), let \(s_{z,n}^2\) be the within-arm sample variance of the observed group means, computed with denominator \(G_{zn}-1\). Define the scalar equal-group CR2 variance statistic by
\[
\widehat V_{\mathrm{CR2},n}
=
\frac{s_{1,n}^2}{G_{1n}}
+
\frac{s_{0,n}^2}{G_{0n}}.
\]
\end{definitionv}

The within-arm sample variance \leanref{sym:s_{z,n}^2}{\ensuremath{s_{z,n}^2}} in \cref{obj:def:cr2-statistic} is computed from observed group means within each assigned arm. The scalar CR2 statistic \leanref{sym:\widehat V_{\mathrm{CR2},n}}{\ensuremath{\widehat V_{\mathrm{CR2},n}}} is therefore the equal-group cluster-robust variance quantity analyzed in the later software identity and asymptotic results.

The class defined next is named for the dense regime that motivates it, and its parameter \(\rho\) ranges over the whole unit interval: arrays with \(\rho=0\) belong to it, and the results below treat \(\rho=0\) and \(\rho>0\) as the two phases of one statement.

\begin{definitionv}{def:dense-schedule-class}[Dense schedule class]
The class \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{dense}}\) consists of triangular arrays \((Y_{i,n},G_n,G_{1n})_{n\ge 1}\), with fixed common group size \(M\), for which:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Feasible rows.)} The common group size satisfies \(M\ge 2\).  In each row, \(G_n>0\), \(N_n=M G_n\le n\), \(0<G_{1n}<G_n\), and \(G_{1n}\le G_n\).  The potential-outcome schedule assigns a real value \(Y_{i,n}(z,A)\) for every group \(A\in\Omega_{n,M}\), every unit \(i\in A\), and every arm \(z\in\mathcal Z\).
\item \textbf{(Dense growth.)} The group count satisfies \cref{obj:ass:group-count-growth}, so \(G_n\to\infty\).
\item \textbf{(Treatment fraction.)} The treated-group fraction satisfies \cref{obj:ass:stable-treatment-fraction}: for an interior limiting treated-group fraction \(p\), \(0<p<1\) and \(p_n\to p\).
\item \textbf{(Sampling fraction.)} The grouped-unit sampling fraction satisfies \cref{obj:ass:sampling-fraction}: for a limiting grouped-unit sampling fraction \(\rho\), \(0\le\rho\le 1\) and \(N_n/n\to\rho\).
\item \textbf{(Uniform envelope.)} The potential outcomes satisfy \cref{obj:ass:bounded-potential-outcomes}: for a uniform envelope \(B>0\),
\[
\sup_{n,A\in\Omega_{n,M},\,i\in A,\,z\in\mathcal Z}\lvert Y_{i,n}(z,A)\rvert\le B .
\]
\item \textbf{(Scaled variance.)} The exact design variance satisfies \cref{obj:ass:scaled-variance-nondegeneracy}: for a scaled-variance lower-bound constant \(c_\sigma>0\),
\[
c_\sigma\le \liminf_{n\to\infty} G_n\sigma_n^2 .
\]
\end{itemize}
Equivalently,
\[
\mathcal C_{\mathrm{dense}}
=
\left\{
(Y_{i,n},G_n,G_{1n})_{n\ge 1}:
G_n\to\infty,\ 
p_n\to p,\ 
N_n/n\to\rho,\ 
\sup_{n,A\in\Omega_{n,M},\,i\in A,\,z\in\mathcal Z}\lvert Y_{i,n}(z,A)\rvert\le B,\ 
\liminf_{n\to\infty}G_n\sigma_n^2\ge c_\sigma
\right\},
\]
with the row feasibility, interiority, closed-interval, envelope, and variance-floor conditions listed above.
\end{definitionv}

The dense schedule class \leanref{sym:\mathcal C_{\mathrm{dense}}}{\ensuremath{\mathcal C_{\mathrm{dense}}}} packages the conditions in \cref{obj:ass:group-count-growth,obj:ass:stable-treatment-fraction,obj:ass:sampling-fraction,obj:ass:bounded-potential-outcomes,obj:ass:scaled-variance-nondegeneracy}. It is the common domain for the dense asymptotic statements, combining feasible rows, a stable treatment split, a limiting grouped-unit fraction, bounded potential outcomes, and a positive scaled design-variance floor.
