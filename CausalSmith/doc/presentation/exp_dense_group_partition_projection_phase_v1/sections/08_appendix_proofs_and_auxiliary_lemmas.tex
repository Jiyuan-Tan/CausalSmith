\section{Appendix: proofs and auxiliary lemmas}\label{sec:appendix}

This appendix collects the auxiliary spectral statements used by the variance geometry and records how the formal arguments fit together. The starting point is the Johnson association scheme on the uniform slice, whose orthogonal decomposition supplies the coordinates used in \cref{obj:def:johnson-decomposition} and whose Kneser disjointness operator supplies the covariance operator in \cref{obj:def:kneser-covariance}. Classical accounts of these schemes appear in \citet{Delsarte1973}, \citet{BrouwerCohenNeumaier1989}, and \citet{BrouwerEtAl2018}; the harmonic-analysis notation follows the slice presentation in \citet{Filmus2016}.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_10}[Johnson and Kneser covariance apparatus]
For \(0\le k\le M\), let \(\mathcal H_{n,k}\subseteq L^2(\Omega_{n,M})\) be the degree-\(k\) Johnson harmonic subspace, and let \(\Pi_{n,k}\) be the \(\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_n\)-orthogonal projection onto \(\mathcal H_{n,k}\). For \(f\in L^2(\Omega_{n,M})\), the centered Johnson decomposition is
\[
f-\mathbb E_n f=\sum_{k=1}^M\Pi_{n,k}f.
\]
For \(2M\le n\), the unnormalized Kneser adjacency operator is
\[
(\mathrm{Adj}_{n,M}f)(A)=\sum_{\substack{C\in\Omega_{n,M}\\ C\cap A=\varnothing}} f(C),
\qquad A\in\Omega_{n,M}.
\]
The normalized Kneser disjointness operator is
\[
(\mathsf K_n f)(A)=\binom{n-M}{M}^{-1}
\sum_{\substack{C\in\Omega_{n,M}\\ C\cap A=\varnothing}} f(C).
\]
Its eigenvalue on \(\mathcal H_{n,k}\) is denoted by \(\lambda_{n,k}\). For \(a,b\in\mathcal Z\), define the ordered-disjoint covariance and the arm-difference covariance by
\[
C_{ab,n}=\langle h_{a,n}^{\circ},\mathsf K_n h_{b,n}^{\circ}\rangle_n,\qquad
C_{\tau\tau,n}=C_{11,n}+C_{00,n}-2C_{10,n}.
\]
The degree-one arm-difference energy is
\[
E_{\tau,1,n}=\|\Pi_{n,1}(h_{1,n}-h_{0,n})\|_n^2.
\]
\end{definitionv}

The objects in \cref{obj:synth_10} restate, in one place, the spectral apparatus used throughout the exact and asymptotic variance arguments. The decomposition separates centered group tables into orthogonal Johnson degrees, and the Kneser operator averages over disjoint groups, matching the dependence induced by random partitioning. With this notation fixed, two Kneser statements follow, and they are deliberately distinct. \Cref{obj:lem:johnson-kneser-adjacency-eigenvalue} is the combinatorial fact: the \emph{unnormalized} disjointness adjacency operator \(\mathrm{Adj}_{n,M}\), which sums a table over all groups disjoint from a given one, acts on the degree-\(k\) harmonic space by the integer eigenvalue \((-1)^k\binom{n-M-k}{M-k}\). \Cref{obj:lem:classical-kneser-spectrum} is the form the variance algebra consumes: it divides by the number \(\binom{n-M}{M}\) of disjoint groups to obtain the \emph{averaging} operator \(\mathsf K_n\) of \cref{obj:def:kneser-covariance}, whose eigenvalues \(\lambda_{n,k}=(-1)^k(M)_k/(n-M)_k\) are the falling-factorial ratios that carry the \(n\)-dependence of the variance expansion. The second is obtained from the first by that normalization, which is why its proof is short; keeping them apart isolates the one combinatorial input of the paper from the bookkeeping that turns it into a covariance statement.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:johnson-kneser-adjacency-eigenvalue}[Johnson--Kneser adjacency eigenvalue]
Using the uniform \(M\)-slice \(\Omega_{n,M}\), the slice function space \(L^2(\Omega_{n,M})\), and the Johnson harmonic subspaces \(\mathcal H_{n,k}\) from \cref{obj:def:johnson-decomposition}, suppose that:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Population and group size.)} \(n\) and \(M\) are nonnegative integers, with \(n\) the population size and \(M\) the common group size, and \(2M\le n\).
\item \textbf{(Harmonic degree.)} \(k\in\{0,\ldots,M\}\).
\item \textbf{(Function.)} \(f\in\mathcal H_{n,k}\).
\end{itemize}
Let \(\mathrm{Adj}_{n,M}\) be the linear operator on \(L^2(\Omega_{n,M})\) defined by
\[
(\mathrm{Adj}_{n,M}f)(\Gamma)
=
\sum_{\Delta\in\Omega_{n,M}}
\mathbf 1\{\Gamma\cap\Delta=\varnothing\}f(\Delta).
\]
Then \(f\) is an eigenfunction of \(\mathrm{Adj}_{n,M}\) with eigenvalue
\[
(-1)^k\binom{n-M-k}{M-k}.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\mathrm{Adj}_{n,M}f
=
(-1)^k\binom{n-M-k}{M-k}\,f
\]
as an identity in \(L^2(\Omega_{n,M})\).
\end{lemmav}

\begin{lemmav}{lem:classical-kneser-spectrum}[Classical Kneser spectrum]
For any population size \(n\) and common group size \(M\), suppose:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Overlap.)} \(2M\le n\).
\item \textbf{(Harmonic degree.)} \(k\) is an integer with \(0\le k\le M\).
\item \textbf{(Harmonic function.)} \(f:\Omega_{n,M}\to\mathbb R\) belongs to the degree-\(k\) Johnson harmonic subspace \(\mathcal H_{n,k}\).
\end{itemize}
Then the unnormalized Kneser adjacency operator, which sums \(f\) over \(M\)-subsets disjoint from the argument, acts on \(f\) by
\[
\bigl(\mathrm{Adj}_{n,M} f\bigr)(A)
=
(-1)^k \binom{n-M-k}{M-k} f(A)
\]
for every \(A\in\Omega_{n,M}\).
\end{lemmav}

Together, \cref{obj:lem:johnson-kneser-adjacency-eigenvalue,obj:lem:classical-kneser-spectrum} give the spectral multiplier for disjointness averaging on each Johnson degree. After normalization by the number of disjoint \(M\)-subsets, these multipliers become the eigenvalues used in the covariance contrast of \cref{obj:thm:exact-kneser-identity}. The alternating sign in degree \(k\) is the algebraic source of the projection correction that later appears in \cref{obj:thm:dense-projection-limit}.

The remaining classical input is the orthogonal expansion of functions on the slice. It ensures that the covariance calculation can be expressed degree by degree rather than as a direct summation over all ordered disjoint pairs.

\begin{lemmav}{lem:classical-johnson-decomposition}[Johnson orthogonal decomposition]
Let \(n\) and \(M\) be nonnegative integers with \(M\le n\). For the canonical Johnson harmonic projections \(\Pi_{n,k}\) on \(L^2(\Omega_{n,M})\), whenever \(2M\le n\), every function \(f:\Omega_{n,M}\to\mathbb R\) and every \(A\in\Omega_{n,M}\) satisfy
\[
\sum_{k=1}^{M}\Pi_{n,k}f(A)
=
f(A)-\mathbb E_n f .
\]
Moreover, the projections are pairwise orthogonal: for all \(k,\ell\in\{0,\ldots,M\}\), all functions \(f,g:\Omega_{n,M}\to\mathbb R\), and \(k\ne \ell\),
\[
\langle \Pi_{n,k}f,\Pi_{n,\ell}g\rangle_n=0 .
\]
\end{lemmav}

\Cref{obj:lem:classical-johnson-decomposition} is the bookkeeping device behind the exact variance formula. Applying the decomposition to the centered arm tables in \cref{obj:def:group-table} lets the Kneser covariance in \cref{obj:def:kneser-covariance} act separately on each harmonic component. This is what turns the two-stage random partition design in \cref{obj:def:random-partition-design} into the exact identity in \cref{obj:thm:exact-pame-variance}.

The subsequent verified arguments use these ingredients in a fixed order. First, the finite-sample variance algebra combines the PAME estimator in \cref{obj:def:pame-estimator} with the Kneser covariance identity to obtain \cref{obj:thm:exact-pame-variance}. Next, the dense schedule class in \cref{obj:def:dense-schedule-class} specializes that identity to the projection limit in \cref{obj:thm:dense-projection-limit} and to the CR2 comparison in \cref{obj:thm:cr2-phase-frontier}; the sparse construction gives the complementary behavior in \cref{obj:thm:sparse-beyond-birthday}. The software calculation in \cref{obj:prop:clubsandwich-consumer} identifies the scalar CR2 statistic with the stated \texttt{clubSandwich} call, and \cref{obj:prop:eight-unit-witness} evaluates the finite eight-unit witness from \cref{obj:def:eight-unit-witness}. Finally, the randomized schedule construction in \cref{obj:prop:rademacher-mixture-separation} feeds the one-realization lower bound in \cref{obj:thm:qv-diagonal-impossibility}.

The finite-sample and software statements are algebraic consequences of the displayed definitions and of the versioned software conventions cited in \citet{ClubSandwich2026} and \citet{Sandwich2026}. The surrounding econometric motivation connects these calculations to random group formation and cluster-robust variance practice as in \citet{FuSamiiWang2026}.

\paragraph{Verification note.}
Every theorem, proposition, and assumption displayed in this paper, and every internal algebraic derivation supporting them, is machine-checked in Lean 4, with no unproved hypotheses and no remaining proof obligations. The displayed definitions divide into two kinds. Those carrying a Lean declaration -- the schedule array, the arm set, slice averaging, the slice norm, the centered arm table, the two Rademacher priors, the one-realization observation, the row treatment fraction, and all numbered \texttt{def:} objects -- are the objects the verified statements quantify over. Three are presentation-level definitions written for the reader and carry no Lean declaration of their own: the uniform-slice space \(L^2(\Omega_{n,M})\) in \cref{obj:synth_12}, the realized arm group counts in \cref{obj:synth_9}, and the unnormalized adjacency operator \(\mathrm{Adj}_{n,M}\) in \cref{obj:synth_10}. They name notation that the verified statements express directly in terms of the slice and its inner product, and nothing in the verified development depends on them. The trust boundary is narrower than is usual for results of this kind: the classical Johnson decomposition and Kneser spectrum recorded in \cref{obj:lem:classical-johnson-decomposition,obj:lem:classical-kneser-spectrum,obj:lem:johnson-kneser-adjacency-eigenvalue} are not imported as cited facts but are themselves proved in the verified development, so the citations to \citet{Delsarte1973}, \citet{BrouwerCohenNeumaier1989}, \citet{Filmus2016}, and \citet{BrouwerEtAl2018} attribute these classical results rather than assume them. What remains outside the machine-checked scope is the bibliographic attribution itself and the versioned software conventions of \citet{ClubSandwich2026} and \citet{Sandwich2026}, which enter \cref{obj:prop:clubsandwich-consumer} as stated implementation conventions.
