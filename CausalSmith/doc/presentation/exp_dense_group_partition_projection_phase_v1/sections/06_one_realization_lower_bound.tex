\section{One-realization lower bound}\label{sec:lower-bound}

The finite witness in \cref{obj:prop:eight-unit-witness} shows how the dense correction can move the exact variance away from the equal-group CR2 target in a small design. This section turns that calculation into a positive-density impossibility statement for one-realization variance recovery. The construction compares two randomized schedule priors whose induced observed-data mixtures agree for every statistic of the realized groups, assignments, and observed outcomes, while their scaled exact variances concentrate at separated limits.

We first record the observed object and the two sign priors used in the comparison.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_8}[One-realization observation]
A one-realization observation \(\mathcal O_n\) at population size \(n\), common group size \(M\), realized group count \(G_n\), and treated-group count \(G_{1n}\) is a triple
\[
\mathcal O_n=(\mathbf A_n,\mathbf Z_n,Y^{\mathrm{obs}}_n)
\]
with the following components:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Partition.)} \(\mathbf A_n=(A_{g,n})_{g=1}^{G_n}\) is an ordered tuple with \(A_{g,n}\in\Omega_{n,M}\) for every \(g\), and \(A_{g,n}\cap A_{g',n}=\varnothing\) whenever \(g\ne g'\).
\item \textbf{(Treatment allocation.)} \(\mathbf Z_n\) selects a subset of the realized groups with exactly \(G_{1n}\) elements.
\item \textbf{(Observed outcomes.)} For every realized group \(g\) and every unit \(i\in A_{g,n}\), \(Y^{\mathrm{obs}}_n\) assigns a real observed outcome \(Y^{\mathrm{obs}}_{i,g,n}\in\mathbb R\).
\end{itemize}
\end{definitionv}

\Cref{obj:synth_8} fixes the sigma-field available to a one-realization procedure: the sampled partition, the group treatment allocation, and the observed unit outcomes within sampled groups. It matches the information used by the design estimator in \cref{obj:def:pame-estimator} and by the equal-group CR2 statistic in \cref{obj:def:cr2-statistic}, but it treats the variance statistic itself as an arbitrary measurable function of the realized data.

\begin{definitionv}{synth_7}[Independent sign prior]
For a population size \(n\), \(\mathbb H_n^{\mathrm{ind}}\) is the product finite design on arrays \(h:\{1,\ldots,n\}\times\{0,1\}\to\{0,1\}\) under which every unit-arm coordinate is drawn independently from the fair two-point law:
\[
\mathbb H_n^{\mathrm{ind}}
=
\bigotimes_{(i,z)\in \{1,\ldots,n\}\times\{0,1\}}
\operatorname{Bernoulli}\!\left(\frac12\right).
\]
\end{definitionv}

\begin{definitionv}{synth_6}[Same-sign schedule prior]
For each \(n\), \(\mathbb H_n^{\mathrm{same}}\) is the finite product design on Boolean schedules \(h:\{1,\ldots,n\}\to\{0,1\}\) whose coordinate design at every \(i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\) is the fair coin design:
\[
\mathbb H_n^{\mathrm{same}}
:=
\bigotimes_{i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}}
\operatorname{Coin}\!\left(\frac12\right).
\]
\end{definitionv}

Both priors are stated on Boolean coordinates, and a Boolean draw becomes a potential-outcome schedule through the sign map
\[
\varepsilon(0)=-1,\qquad \varepsilon(1)=+1 .
\]
Given a common-sign draw \(u:\{1,\ldots,n\}\to\{0,1\}\) from \(\mathbb H_n^{\mathrm{same}}\), the induced schedule is additive and arm-free,
\[
Y_{i,n}(z,A)=\varepsilon(u_i)\qquad\text{for every arm }z\text{ and every group }A\ni i,
\]
so the same unit sign is carried into both arms. Given an arm-specific draw \(u:\{1,\ldots,n\}\times\{0,1\}\to\{0,1\}\) from \(\mathbb H_n^{\mathrm{ind}}\), the induced schedule is
\[
Y_{i,n}(z,A)=\varepsilon(u_{i,z}),
\]
with the two arms drawn independently. Both induced schedules take values in \(\{-1,+1\}\) and are additive: the potential outcome of unit \(i\) does not depend on the composition of the group it lands in. Consequently every group table is an average of \(M\) independent signs, which is what produces the arm variances \(V_{z,n}\to1/M\) quoted in the proposition. The independent prior in \cref{obj:synth_7} randomizes unit signs separately by treatment arm; the same-sign prior in \cref{obj:synth_6} uses a common unit sign across arms. Both priors generate bounded Boolean schedules, but they place different mass on the degree-one arm-difference energy in \cref{obj:def:dense-correction}. That difference is invisible after observing only the assigned arm in a single realization.

\begin{propositionv}{prop:rademacher-mixture-separation}[Rademacher mixture separation]
Fix a common group size \(M\ge 2\).  Let \(A\) be a feasible triangular array with row population sizes \(n\), group counts \(G_n\), treated-group counts \(G_{1n}\), \(0<G_{1n}<G_n\), \(M G_n\le n\), and deterministic schedules.  Let \(p\in\mathbb R\) and let \(\rho\), the limiting grouped-unit fraction, satisfy \(\rho>0\).  Suppose:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Group growth.)} The array satisfies \cref{obj:ass:group-count-growth}.
\item \textbf{(Treatment fractions.)} The treated-group fractions satisfy \cref{obj:ass:stable-treatment-fraction} with limit \(p\).
\item \textbf{(Sampling fractions.)} The grouped-unit fractions satisfy \cref{obj:ass:sampling-fraction} with limit \(\rho\).
\item \textbf{(Johnson projections.)} For every row \(n\), the canonical Johnson projections \(\Pi_{n,k}\) on \(L^2(\Omega_{n,M})\) are linear orthogonal projections onto \(\mathcal H_{n,k}\), fix \(\mathcal H_{n,k}\), and have residuals orthogonal to \(\mathcal H_{n,k}\).
\item \textbf{(Johnson decomposition.)} For every row with \(2M\le n\), every \(f\in L^2(\Omega_{n,M})\), and every \(A\in\Omega_{n,M}\),
\[
\sum_{1\le k\le M}\Pi_{n,k}f(A)=f(A)-\mathbb E_n f,
\]
and distinct Johnson degrees are orthogonal under \(\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_n\).
\item \textbf{(Kneser spectrum.)} For every row with \(2M\le n\), every \(0\le k\le M\), and every \(f\in\mathcal H_{n,k}\), the unnormalized Kneser adjacency operator satisfies
\[
\sum_{C\in\Omega_{n,M}:C\cap A=\varnothing} f(C)
=
(-1)^k {\,n-M-k\choose M-k\,} f(A).
\]
\end{itemize}
Then, for every row \(n\) and every statistic \(\varphi\) of the one-realization observation \(\mathcal O_n\),
\[
\mathbb E_{\mathbb H_n^{\mathrm{same}}}\mathbb E_{\mathbf Z_n}\{\varphi(\mathcal O_n)\}
=
\mathbb E_{\mathbb H_n^{\mathrm{ind}}}\mathbb E_{\mathbf Z_n}\{\varphi(\mathcal O_n)\}.
\]
Moreover, under \(\mathbb H_n^{\mathrm{same}}\),
\[
V_{1,n}\stackrel{p}{\longrightarrow}\frac1M,
\qquad
V_{0,n}\stackrel{p}{\longrightarrow}\frac1M,
\qquad
E_{\tau,1,n}\stackrel{p}{\longrightarrow}0,
\qquad
G_n\sigma_n^2\stackrel{p}{\longrightarrow}\frac{1}{M p(1-p)}.
\]
Under \(\mathbb H_n^{\mathrm{ind}}\),
\[
E_{\tau,1,n}\stackrel{p}{\longrightarrow}\frac2M,
\qquad
G_n\sigma_n^2\stackrel{p}{\longrightarrow}\frac{1}{M p(1-p)}-\frac{2\rho}{M}.
\]
The two scaled-variance limits are separated by a positive gap:
\[
0<\frac{2\rho}{M},
\qquad
\frac2M\le \frac{1}{M p(1-p)}-\frac{2\rho}{M}.
\]
Consequently, for every fixed \(c_\sigma\in\mathbb R\) satisfying
\[
0<c_\sigma<\frac{1}{M p(1-p)}-\frac{2\rho}{M},
\]
the lower-tail probabilities satisfy
\[
\Pr_{\mathbb H_n^{\mathrm{same}}}\{G_n\sigma_n^2\ge c_\sigma\}\to 1,
\qquad
\Pr_{\mathbb H_n^{\mathrm{ind}}}\{G_n\sigma_n^2\ge c_\sigma\}\to 1.
\]
\end{propositionv}

\Cref{obj:prop:rademacher-mixture-separation} isolates the information loss created by one realized treatment assignment. Every statistic of the one-realization observation \(\mathcal O_n\) has the same mixture expectation under \(\mathbb H_n^{\mathrm{same}}\) and \(\mathbb H_n^{\mathrm{ind}}\), yet the exact-variance limit changes by \(2\rho/M\). The equal observed-data mixture reflects the fact that each observed unit contributes only its assigned-arm sign; the variance separation comes from the unobserved cross-arm alignment, which enters \cref{obj:thm:exact-pame-variance} through the degree-one term \(E_{\tau,1,n}\). In positive-density designs, the grouped-unit fraction \(\rho\) keeps that term on the same scale as the leading variance.

The next result converts the mixture separation into a uniform lower bound over the dense schedule class. It conditions the two priors on high-probability deterministic supports, so the comparison is stated directly over arrays in \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{dense}}\) from \cref{obj:def:dense-schedule-class}.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:qv-diagonal-impossibility}[Diagonal variance obstruction]
Fix a common group size \(M\) with \(2\le M\), and let \(A\) be a triangular schedule array with population sizes \(n\), group counts \(G_n>0\), treated-group counts \(0<G_{1n}<G_n\), \(M G_n\le n\), and deterministic potential-outcome rows.  Let \(p,\rho,B,c_\sigma\in\mathbb R\).  Suppose:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Envelope and fractions.)} \(0<\rho\), \(1\le B\), and the array satisfies \cref{obj:ass:group-count-growth,obj:ass:stable-treatment-fraction,obj:ass:sampling-fraction} with limiting treated-group fraction \(p\) and limiting grouped-unit fraction \(\rho\).
\item \textbf{(Johnson decomposition input.)} For every row \(n\), there are orthogonal projections \(\Pi_{n,k}\) onto the degree-\(k\) Johnson harmonic spaces \(\mathcal H_{n,k}\), \(k=0,\ldots,M\), whose positive-degree components give the centered orthogonal decomposition of every \(f\in L^2(\Omega_{n,M})\):
\[
\sum_{k=1}^{M}\Pi_{n,k}f
=
f-\mathbb E_n f,
\qquad
\langle \Pi_{n,k}f,\Pi_{n,\ell}g\rangle_n=0
\quad\text{for }k\ne \ell .
\]
\item \textbf{(Kneser spectrum input.)} For every row \(n\) with \(2M\le n\), the Kneser adjacency operator acts on \(f\in\mathcal H_{n,k}\) by the eigenvalue
\[
(-1)^k {\,n-M-k\,\choose\, M-k\,},
\qquad k=0,\ldots,M .
\]
\item \textbf{(Variance lower bound.)} With
\[
d_{\mathrm{same}}=\frac{1}{M p(1-p)},
\qquad
d_{\mathrm{ind}}=\frac{1}{M p(1-p)}-\frac{2\rho}{M},
\]
the constant \(c_\sigma\) satisfies
\[
0<c_\sigma<d_{\mathrm{ind}} .
\]
\end{itemize}
Then there exist support sets \(\Gamma_n^{\mathrm{same}}\) for the common-arm Rademacher prior \(\mathbb H_n^{\mathrm{same}}\) and \(\Gamma_n^{\mathrm{ind}}\) for the independent-arm Rademacher prior \(\mathbb H_n^{\mathrm{ind}}\) such that both prior probabilities tend to one, every diagonal selection from either support induces an array in \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{dense}}\) with parameters \((p,\rho,B,c_\sigma)\), the conditioned one-realization laws satisfy
\[
\operatorname{TV}\!\left(
\mathbb H_n^{\mathrm{same}}(\mathcal O_n\in\cdot\mid \Gamma_n^{\mathrm{same}}),
\mathbb H_n^{\mathrm{ind}}(\mathcal O_n\in\cdot\mid \Gamma_n^{\mathrm{ind}})
\right)\to 0,
\]
and the scaled exact variances converge uniformly on the respective supports:
\[
\sup_{u\in\Gamma_n^{\mathrm{same}}}
\left|G_n\sigma_n^2(u)-d_{\mathrm{same}}\right|\to 0,
\qquad
\sup_{u\in\Gamma_n^{\mathrm{ind}}}
\left|G_n\sigma_n^2(u)-d_{\mathrm{ind}}\right|\to 0 .
\]
Moreover, there is an \(\varepsilon>0\) such that every measurable one-realization variance statistic \(\widehat S_n(\mathcal O_n)\) satisfies
\[
\frac12
\le
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
\sup_{Y\in\mathcal C_{\mathrm{dense}}}
\Pr_Y\!\left(
\left|
\frac{\widehat S_n(\mathcal O_n)}{\sigma_n^2}-1
\right|>\varepsilon
\right).
\]
\end{theoremv}

\Cref{obj:thm:qv-diagonal-impossibility} establishes the one-realization lower bound in the positive-density class. The high-probability supports place both constructions inside \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{dense}}\) with the same envelope, fraction, and scaled-variance parameters, while total variation convergence makes the conditioned observed laws asymptotically indistinguishable. Uniform convergence of the scaled exact variances to \(d_{\mathrm{same}}\) and \(d_{\mathrm{ind}}\) supplies the separated targets, and the final display gives a single \(\varepsilon>0\) for which every measurable statistic \(\widehat S_n(\mathcal O_n)\) has worst-case relative error probability bounded below by one half.

The intuition is the same as in a two-point Le Cam comparison, adapted to deterministic triangular arrays. If the observed laws are asymptotically the same, a statistic based only on \(\mathcal O_n\) cannot reliably determine which variance target generated the data. Because the targets remain separated by the positive-density correction \(2\rho/M\), any one-realization variance statistic must make a nonvanishing relative error on at least one member of the dense class. This identifies the degree-one cross-arm component in \cref{obj:def:dense-correction} as the obstruction to uniform exact-variance ratio consistency from a single realized grouping and assignment.
