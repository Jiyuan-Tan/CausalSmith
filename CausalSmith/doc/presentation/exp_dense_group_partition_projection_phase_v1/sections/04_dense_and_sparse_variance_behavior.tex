\section{Dense and sparse variance behavior}\label{sec:dense-sparse}

The exact identities in \cref{obj:thm:exact-kneser-identity,obj:thm:exact-pame-variance} reduce the large-population question to the behavior of Johnson energies under random grouping. In dense arrays, where a positive fraction of the finite population may be grouped, the first Johnson degree carries the leading correction to the independent-group variance scale. The higher degrees remain present in the finite-sample identity, but their aggregate contribution is uniformly of smaller order under the bounded-outcome and fixed-group-size conditions.

A word on how to read the statements below. Each theorem carries its Johnson-projection, orthogonal-decomposition, and Kneser-spectrum clauses explicitly, because the verified statements quantify over the projections rather than fixing them. Those three clauses are not extra economic assumptions: they hold automatically for the canonical Johnson projections on the uniform \(M\)-slice whenever \(2M\le n\), by \cref{obj:lem:classical-johnson-decomposition,obj:lem:classical-kneser-spectrum}, which are proved in \cref{sec:appendix}. The substantive conditions are only the four array conditions of \cref{sec:setup}: growing group count, stable treatment fraction, converging sampling fraction, and bounded potential outcomes. A reader may take the projection clauses as given and read each theorem as a statement about arrays satisfying those four conditions alone.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:dense-projection-limit}[Dense projection limit]
Fix a common group size \(M\ge 2\) and real numbers \(p\), \(\rho\), and \(B\).  There is a constant \(C>0\) such that, for every feasible triangular schedule array with common group size \(M\) and every rowwise choice of Johnson projections \(\Pi_{n,k}\) on \(L^2(\Omega_{n,M})\), the following conditions imply the displayed limits below:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Group growth.)} The array satisfies \cref{obj:ass:group-count-growth}.
\item \textbf{(Treatment fractions.)} The array satisfies \cref{obj:ass:stable-treatment-fraction} with limiting treated-group fraction \(p\).
\item \textbf{(Sampling fractions.)} The array satisfies \cref{obj:ass:sampling-fraction} with limiting grouped-unit fraction \(\rho\).
\item \textbf{(Bounded outcomes.)} The array satisfies \cref{obj:ass:bounded-potential-outcomes} with uniform outcome envelope \(B\).
\item \textbf{(Johnson decomposition.)} In every row with \(2M\le n\), the projections give the centered orthogonal Johnson decomposition
\[
\sum_{k=1}^{M}\Pi_{n,k}f(A)=f(A)-\mathbb E_n f
\]
for every \(f\) and every \(A\in\Omega_{n,M}\), and distinct Johnson degrees are orthogonal under \(\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_n\).
\item \textbf{(Kneser spectrum.)} In every row with \(2M\le n\), every \(f\in\mathcal H_{n,k}\) is an eigenfunction of the unnormalized Kneser adjacency operator with eigenvalue
\[
(-1)^k {\,n-M-k\choose M-k\,}.
\]
\end{itemize}
Then
\[
G_n\sigma_n^2-\bigl(R_n-\rho E_{\tau,1,n}\bigr)\to 0,
\]
\[
G_nC_{\tau\tau,n}+\rho E_{\tau,1,n}\to 0,
\]
and
\[
\frac{C_{11,n}}{p_n}+\frac{C_{00,n}}{1-p_n}\to 0.
\]
Moreover, for every row \(n\), the pooled contribution of Johnson degrees \(k\ge 2\),
\[
G_n\sum_{k=2}^{M}\lambda_{n,k}
\left\|\Pi_{n,k}\bigl(h_{1,n}-h_{0,n}\bigr)\right\|_{n}^{2},
\]
has absolute value at most \(C/n\).
\end{theoremv}

The theorem identifies the dense correction as the product of the grouped-unit sampling limit and the degree-one contrast energy \leanref{sym:E_{\tau,1,n}}{\ensuremath{E_{\tau,1,n}}}. Intuitively, degree one records the part of the arm-difference table that is visible through the marginal participation of individual population members in sampled groups. Sampling without replacement induces competition for those members across groups, and the limit in \cref{obj:thm:dense-projection-limit} converts that competition into the correction \(-\rho E_{\tau,1,n}\) to the scaled exact variance. The final bound gives the corresponding uniform remainder statement for Johnson degrees at least two, so the first-order dense comparison is governed by the degree-one projection.

This projection expansion feeds directly into the behavior of the equal-group CR2 statistic. The statistic \leanref{sym:\widehat V_{\mathrm{CR2},n}}{\ensuremath{\widehat V_{\mathrm{CR2},n}}} is the usual scalar cluster-robust variance estimator for the difference in group means in this balanced design, matching the equal-group CR2 form used in finite-sample cluster-robust inference \citep{PustejovskyTipton2018}. The next result states its probability limit and the exact criterion under which the conservative dense correction vanishes relative to the target variance.

\begin{theoremv}{thm:cr2-phase-frontier}[CR2 phase frontier]
Let \(M\ge 2\), and let \(A\) be a triangular array of feasible random-group experiment rows with population sizes \(n\), group counts \(G_n>0\), treated-group counts \(0<G_{1n}<G_n\), grouped-unit counts \(N_n=MG_n\le n\), and deterministic potential-outcome schedules.  Fix constants \(p,\rho,B,c_\sigma\in\mathbb R\).  Suppose that:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Dense array.)} The array \(A\) belongs to the dense bounded schedule class \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{dense}}\) in \cref{obj:def:dense-schedule-class}, with parameters \(p,\rho,B,c_\sigma\).
\item \textbf{(Johnson projections.)} For every row \(n\), the spaces \(L^2(\Omega_{n,M})\) are equipped with linear projections \(\Pi_{n,k}\), \(0\le k\le M\), whose ranges are the degree-\(k\) Johnson harmonic subspaces \(\mathcal H_{n,k}\), which fix functions in their ranges and have residuals orthogonal to \(\mathcal H_{n,k}\) under \(\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_n\).
\item \textbf{(Orthogonal decomposition.)} For every row with \(2M\le n\), every \(f\in L^2(\Omega_{n,M})\), and every \(A\in\Omega_{n,M}\),
\[
\sum_{k=1}^{M}\Pi_{n,k}f(A)=f(A)-\mathbb E_n f,
\]
and distinct Johnson components are orthogonal:
\[
\langle \Pi_{n,k}f,\Pi_{n,\ell}g\rangle_n=0
\qquad\text{whenever } k\ne \ell .
\]
\item \textbf{(Kneser spectrum.)} For every row with \(2M\le n\), every \(0\le k\le M\), and every \(f\in\mathcal H_{n,k}\), the unnormalized Kneser adjacency operator satisfies
\[
\sum_{C\cap A=\varnothing} f(C)
=
(-1)^k {\,n-M-k\choose M-k\,} f(A).
\]
\end{itemize}
Then, as \(n\to\infty\),
\[
G_n\widehat V_{\mathrm{CR2},n}-R_n \xrightarrow{p}0
\]
and
\[
G_n\bigl(\widehat V_{\mathrm{CR2},n}-\sigma_n^2\bigr)
-\rho E_{\tau,1,n}
\xrightarrow{p}0 .
\]
Moreover, for every \(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
\Pr\!\left\{
\widehat V_{\mathrm{CR2},n}/\sigma_n^2<1-\varepsilon
\right\}\longrightarrow 0 ,
\]
and the ratio \(\widehat V_{\mathrm{CR2},n}/\sigma_n^2\) converges in probability to \(1\) if and only if
\[
\frac{\rho E_{\tau,1,n}}{R_n-\rho E_{\tau,1,n}}\longrightarrow 0 .
\]
\end{theoremv}

\Cref{obj:thm:cr2-phase-frontier} says that CR2 estimates the independent-group scale \(R_n/G_n\) in probability, while the exact design variance subtracts the dense projection correction. Consequently, the statistic is asymptotically conservative for the exact variance in the one-sided probability sense stated in the theorem. Its ratio consistency is characterized by a single relative-energy condition: the dense correction \(\rho E_{\tau,1,n}\) must be negligible compared with the corrected scaled variance \(R_n-\rho E_{\tau,1,n}\). This gives a diagnostic interpretation to the Johnson decomposition in \cref{obj:def:johnson-decomposition}: degree-one heterogeneity in the arm contrast is precisely the component that determines whether the usual CR2 standard error is sharp in dense random-group designs.

The sparse case follows by setting the grouped-unit sampling fraction to zero. In that regime, the same theorem conditions allow many realized groups while making the first-order finite-pool correction vanish at the scale relevant for the variance ratio. The final result records this implication and isolates the benchmark count sequence in which \(N_n/n\to0\) even though \(N_n^2/n\to\infty\).

\begin{theoremv}{thm:sparse-beyond-birthday}[Sparse birthday consistency]
Fix an integer \(M\ge 2\).  Then the following statements hold.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(Sparse dense arrays.)} For every feasible triangular schedule array with common group size \(M\), if the array belongs to \(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{dense}}\) in \cref{obj:def:dense-schedule-class} with limiting grouped-unit fraction \(\rho=0\), then
\[
\frac{\widehat V_{\mathrm{CR2},n}}{\sigma_n^2}\xrightarrow{p}1 .
\]
\item \textbf{(Birthday counts.)} With
\[
N_n^{\mathrm{bd}}=M\Big\lfloor \frac{n^{3/4}}{M}\Big\rfloor ,
\]
the benchmark counts satisfy \(N_n^{\mathrm{bd}}\le n\) for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
\frac{N_n^{\mathrm{bd}}}{n}\to 0,
\qquad
\frac{(N_n^{\mathrm{bd}})^2}{n}\to\infty .
\]
\item \textbf{(Birthday-aligned arrays.)} For every feasible triangular schedule array with common group size \(M\), if its grouped-unit count satisfies
\[
N_n=M\Big\lfloor \frac{n^{3/4}}{M}\Big\rfloor
\]
row by row and the array satisfies \cref{obj:ass:group-count-growth,obj:ass:stable-treatment-fraction,obj:ass:bounded-potential-outcomes,obj:ass:scaled-variance-nondegeneracy} for some \(p\), \(B\), and \(c_\sigma\), then
\[
\frac{\widehat V_{\mathrm{CR2},n}}{\sigma_n^2}\xrightarrow{p}1 .
\]
\end{itemize}
\end{theoremv}

The sparse result separates the variance question from whole-design collision heuristics. What matters for the scalar PAME variance is the grouped-unit fraction \(N_n/n\), and \cref{obj:thm:sparse-beyond-birthday} establishes ratio consistency whenever that fraction converges to zero within the stated dense schedule class. The explicit sequence \(N_n=M\lfloor n^{3/4}/M\rfloor\) gives a concrete regime with vanishing sampling fraction and diverging \(N_n^2/n\). Thus CR2 remains ratio-consistent for the exact design variance in a range where the experiment still uses many overlapping opportunities in the underlying finite population.
