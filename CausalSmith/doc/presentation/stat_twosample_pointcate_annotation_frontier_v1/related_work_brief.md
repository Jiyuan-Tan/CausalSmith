The paper belongs to the literature on identifying and estimating heterogeneous causal effects from observational data. The potential-outcome framework of [\citet{Rubin1974}](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0037350) and the propensity-score analysis of [\citet{Rosenbaum1983}](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/70.1.41) supply its identification foundations; [\citet{Imbens2015}](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9781139025751) and [\citet[Chapters 3--4]{Hernan2020}](https://www.hsph.harvard.edu/miguel-hernan/wp-content/uploads/sites/1268/2024/04/hernanrobins_WhatIf_26apr24.pdf) provide standard treatments of conditional exchangeability and overlap. Semiparametric average-effect efficiency was developed by [\citet{Hahn1998}](https://doi.org/10.2307/2998560) and [\citet{Hirano2003}](https://doi.org/10.1111/1468-0262.00442), while augmented estimating equations and double robustness connect causal inference to missing-data theory through [\citet{Robins1994}](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1994.10476818) and [\citet{Bang2005}](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1541-0420.2005.00377.x). Residualization and orthogonal estimation trace through [\citet{Robinson1988}](https://doi.org/10.2307/1912705) to [\citet{Chernozhukov2018}](https://doi.org/10.1111/ectj.12097). The present contribution combines these identification principles with nonparametric point estimation: local polynomial reproduction, smoothness-dependent approximation, and minimax testing have canonical treatments in [\citet{Fan1996}](https://doi.org/10.1201/9780203748725) and [\citet[Chapter 2]{Tsybakov2009}](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-0-387-79052-7), alongside the broader optimal-rate theory of [\citet{Stone1982}](https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1176345969).

Several methodological strands address conditional average treatment effects. Honest recursive partitioning and causal forests support heterogeneous-effect estimation and inference under their respective sampling, overlap, and tree-regularity conditions [\citep{Athey2016}](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.01132), [\citep{Wager2018}](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.2017.1319839); generalized random forests extend local estimation to conditional moment restrictions [\citep{Athey2019}](https://doi.org/10.1214/18-AOS1709). Metalearners organize alternative ways of combining arm-specific regressions [\citep{Kunzel2019}](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1804597116). The R-learner of [\citet{Nie2021}](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/asaa076) achieves quasi-oracle prediction guarantees under its RKHS complexity, overlap, and first-stage accuracy conditions. [\citet{Kennedy2023}](https://doi.org/10.1214/23-EJS2157) develops doubly robust regression guarantees that separate the second-stage oracle error from errors involving products of propensity and outcome-regression estimation errors; [\citet{Foster2023}](https://doi.org/10.1214/23-AOS2258) provides a general nonasymptotic theory of orthogonal statistical learning. Debiased series and local regression methods yield asymptotic inference for conditional causal functions under approximation, bandwidth, and nuisance-rate restrictions [\citep{Semenova2021}](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.06240), [\citep{Fan2022}](https://doi.org/10.1080/07350015.2020.1811102). Relevant uncertainty-calibration results include pointwise and uniform series theory in [\citet[Theorem 4.3]{BelloniChernozhukovChetverikovKato2015}](https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.0442), uniform CATE-band coverage through Gaussian-process approximation in [\citet[Theorem 2]{LeeOkuiWhang2017}](https://arxiv.org/html/1601.02801), and bias-aware interval coverage under Gaussian-approximation and standard-error conditions in [\citet[Theorem 2.2]{ArmstrongKolesar2020SimpleHonest}](https://arxiv.org/html/1606.01200). These results establish complementary prediction and inference guarantees within their specified models.

The closest supervised minimax comparator is [\citet[Section 3, Theorem 1]{KennedyBalakrishnanRobinsWasserman2024}](https://arxiv.org/html/2203.00837v4). For covariate dimension \(d\), propensity, control-mean, and contrast Hölder orders \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\), and average nuisance smoothness \(s=(\alpha+\beta)/2\), their bounded-density model has a pointwise expected absolute-error lower bound of order \(n^{-1/(1+d/(2\gamma)+d/(4s))}\) below the cutoff \(s=(d/4)/(1+d/(2\gamma))\), and \(n^{-1/(2+d/\gamma)}\) at or above it, where \(n\) is the labeled sample size. Their Theorem 2 supplies matching attainment under its overlap, projection-matrix, learner-smoothness, boundedness, and density-estimation conditions. Equation (14) supplies the localized second-order projection architecture relevant to the control-mean parametrization when \(\gamma\ge\beta\). The present supervised numerical branches agree with these exponents after substituting \(s=(\alpha+\beta)/2\); its original uniform-design converse establishes the lower bound for the stipulated primitive class. Earlier heterogeneous-effect minimax analysis includes the fixed-design and assignment configurations of [\citet{Gao2020}](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.06471). The projection correction also belongs to the higher-order influence-function lineage of [\citet{Robins2008}](https://doi.org/10.1214/193940307000000527) and [\citet{Robins2017}](https://doi.org/10.1214/16-AOS1515), with variance decomposition rooted in [\citet{Hoeffding1948}](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177730196) and mixture-distance lower-bound methods in [\citet[Theorem 2.1]{Robins2009}](https://doi.org/10.1214/09-EJS479). Recent higher-order debiasing for implicitly defined treatment functionals extends this methodological strand to general treatment models [\citep{Zhang2026}](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01706v2).

Outcome-free records connect the paper to auxiliary-data efficiency and semi-supervised estimation. Canonical contributions establish efficiency theory for GMM with auxiliary observations [\citep{Chen2008}](https://doi.org/10.1214/009053607000000947), efficient semi-supervised linear regression [\citep{Chakrabortty2018}](https://doi.org/10.1214/17-AOS1594), and general mean-estimation theory [\citep{Zhang2019}](https://doi.org/10.1214/18-AOS1756). For treatment effects, [\citet[Sections 2.1, 2.5--2.6, and 4]{ChengAnanthakrishnanCai2021}](https://arxiv.org/html/1804.00195) develops surrogate-assisted ATE estimation with a vanishing labeled fraction, consistency when either its initial propensity or baseline outcome model is correct, and local efficiency in an ideal experiment with known unlabeled-data distribution. [\citet[Theorem 2.1, Corollary 2.1, and Sections 2.5 and 3]{ChakraborttyDai2022}](https://arxiv.org/html/2201.00468v3) derives nuisance-dependent expansions and root-\(n\) inference for ATE and QTE. Its outcome-misspecification-robust normal approximation requires the stated remainder conditions; Corollary 2.1 includes inverse-propensity error \(o(n^{-1/2})\) and a vanishing labeled fraction. [\citet[Theorems 4.2 and 4.4 and Corollary 4.5]{Kato2026}](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08303v2) characterizes regular-estimator ATE efficiency with auxiliary covariates and provides an attaining cross-fitted estimator under nuisance consistency and product-rate conditions. In the same-law specialization, its leading variance separates conditional outcome noise divided by the labeled count from effect-heterogeneity variance divided by the total count. [\citet{Kallus2025}](https://doi.org/10.1093/jrsssb/qkae099) characterizes surrogate-assisted efficiency under treatment and outcome-observation assumptions, including regimes with scarce outcomes. [\citet{Hou2025}](https://jmlr.org/papers/v26/23-1587.html) treats jointly scarce gold-standard treatment and response labels with abundant surrogate measurements, obtaining efficient and robust ATE procedures under its smoothness or sparse-model conditions. For fixed-kernel functionals, [\citet[Theorem 1 and Section 7]{KimWassermanBalakrishnanNeykov2025}](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.18921v2) establishes semi-supervised U-statistic normal approximation under finite variance, positive residual first-projection variance, and mean-square-consistent assistance, and develops methods across bivariate degeneracy regimes. The supplied internal discrete-ATE record, \citet{stat_semisupervised_discrete_ate_annotation_frontier_v1}, contributes an unequal-channel positioning analogue through factorial-moment estimation with a growing finite alphabet and mean squared loss.

Recent frontier work distinguishes several information structures and targets. Prediction-powered inference uses a supplied predictor to combine labeled residual information with auxiliary prediction information [\citep{Angelopoulos2023}](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adi6000). For conditional functionals, [\citet[Theorem 1 and equation (13)]{SuiZhouZhouDai2026}](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05575v2) gives a high-probability error bound separating predictor-residual noise, auxiliary-prediction noise, weight estimation, and regularization bias. Its stated Sobolev-RKHS specialization, with Sobolev order \(u>d/2\), auxiliary count \(n^r\) satisfying \(r\ge1\) and \(r>d/u\), and its remaining regularity and nondegeneracy conditions, yields order \((\log n/n)^{1/2-d/(4u)}\). Under the bounded ambient-RKHS model, overlap, and contrast-subspace conditions of [\citet[Sections 2.3 and 6, Corollary 4]{Kim2026StructureAdaptiveCATE}](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.18958v2), a Sobolev contrast of order \(t>d/2\) has pointwise squared-error order \((n\kappa)^{-(2t-d)/(2t)}\), up to logarithmic factors, where \(\kappa\) is the overlap parameter. [\citet{Kim2025}](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11331v4) supplies target-population mean-squared-error guarantees and adaptive kernel selection under its covariate-shift, overlap, and RKHS structure. [\citet[Theorems 5.1--5.2]{PengZhouLu2026}](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.13945v1) establishes consistency and root-\(n\) normality for a treatment-rule index under a monotone-index contrast, kernel undersmoothing, curvature and moment conditions, and the specified propensity or doubly robust nuisance-model assumptions. Within the present known-uniform binary-response benchmark, public Hölder orders \(\alpha,\beta\in(0,1]\), \(\gamma\in[1,3]\), public radius \(L\ge10\), propensity range \([1/4,3/4]\), and arm-mean range \([1/8,7/8]\), the contribution is the sharp point-CATE absolute-error minimax risk
\[
R(n,m)\asymp
\max\left\{
n^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)},
[n(n+m)]^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d+\gamma d/(\alpha+\beta))}
\right\},
\]
where \(R(n,m)\) denotes minimax risk and \(m\) denotes the independent outcome-free treatment-record count. A total rectangular projection estimator and an original-record mixture converse establish this frontier uniformly over sample imbalance. When \(\alpha+\beta<\gamma d/(2\gamma+d)\), oracle-order precision requires total treatment-record counts of order \(n^{\gamma d/[(\alpha+\beta)(2\gamma+d)]}\), and strict risk-order improvement occurs exactly when \(m/n\to\infty\). The clinical application is an acquisition benchmark for true response labels and routine treatment/covariate records: [\citet[Introduction and Methods]{Ananthakrishnan2016}](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4772891/) motivates matching individual patients to effective agents, while Cheng et al.'s ATE analysis supplies the chart-review and electronic-record setting.
