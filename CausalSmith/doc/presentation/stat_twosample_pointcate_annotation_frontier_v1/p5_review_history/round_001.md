# Referee review

**Recommendation:** major_revision
**Overall score:** 7.2/10 — The verified unequal-sample minimax characterization is a substantial theoretical contribution, but disrupted exposition and extensive duplication require significant editorial revision.

The paper establishes sharp pointwise CATE absolute-error rates for independent labeled and outcome-free treatment records under a precisely specified uniform-design Hölder model. Its matching estimator and original-record lower bound deliver meaningful acquisition thresholds, and the comparisons with supervised and semi-supervised results generally respect their respective assumptions. Taking the verified statements as established, the principal concerns are organization, presentation of numerical conventions, and clarity about acquisition bookkeeping and the supplied-propensity decision class.

## Strengths
- The characterization covers every admissible sample imbalance and distinguishes strict improvement from attainment of the supplied-propensity order.
- The estimator is explicitly defined on every dataset, with public tuning, guarded inversion, clipping, and verified uniform attainment.
- The original-record lower bound establishes the converse within the stipulated known-uniform class.
- The early related-work section engages comparator rates, conditions, targets, and auxiliary information structures with unusually careful scope boundaries.
- The abstract and main interpretations generally preserve the verified parameter restrictions and distinguish exact numerical rate identities from risk comparisons up to constants.

## Findings
- **[major·structure] Estimation with outcome-free records** — The estimator exposition contains displaced lower-bound material. The sentence “The associated projection is specified next” introduces the constructed arm means rather than the projection. Later, “we also record the constructed arm means” introduces the rectangular upper-bound theorem, followed by “These arm means share the same response-frame perturbation.” These broken transitions obscure the construction's dependencies and refer readers to lower-bound objects before their introduction.
  - *Fix:* Move \cref{obj:synth_2} and its explanatory paragraphs beside \cref{obj:def:marked-handle} in the lower-bound appendix. Place \cref{obj:def:projection} immediately after the fine-space description, and place \cref{obj:thm:rectangular-upper} immediately after the estimator and tuning discussion. Rewrite the surrounding transitions to identify the actual following object.
- **[major·structure] global** — Extensive repetition weakens the manuscript's hierarchy. The rate receives several names and a separate rate-and-decision pair; the acquisition proposition repeats the minimax sandwich, all rate branches, and the supervised benchmark already stated elsewhere. The appendices repeatedly summarize these same conclusions, while elementary calculations receive lengthy sequences of named constants. This makes the substantive unequal-channel contribution unnecessarily difficult to follow.
  - *Fix:* Use one primary rate notation, organize the main exposition around the estimator guarantee, minimax characterization, and acquisition consequences, and replace repeated conclusions with exact cleveref references. Consolidate standing hypotheses while preserving every condition. Present the appendix arguments in conventional mathematical prose and retain detailed formal bookkeeping in a clearly identified technical supplement.
- **[minor·prose] Introduction; Discussion and limitations** — The acquisition interpretation says that “acquiring another response label increases both the response information and the treatment-record count.” This describes adding a new labeled record with the auxiliary count fixed. Chart review of a record already counted in the routine collection instead changes its record type while preserving the total count. The manuscript's clinical wording leaves these operations insufficiently distinguished.
  - *Fix:* State explicitly that adding a new independent labeled record changes the counts from (n,m) to (n+1,m), whereas labeling an existing auxiliary record changes them to (n+1,m−1) at fixed total count. Describe the frontier as a benchmark for the resulting sample composition under the stated sampling assumptions, and distinguish these operations when discussing acquisition costs.
- **[minor·statement] Sharp precision and sample requirements** — The acquisition proposition includes “At n=0, both the numerical benchmark and r_*(n,0) equal zero.” The contract establishes this numerical extension under a totalized power convention, while the statistical guarantees concern labeled counts of at least two. Its placement inside the acquisition result invites readers to attach a statistical precision interpretation to the zero value.
  - *Fix:* Present the acquisition and supervised comparisons on their statistical domain n≥2. Move the zero-base numerical extension to a technical note that explicitly identifies its arithmetic convention and its role in the formal representation.
- **[minor·statement] Setup and assumptions** — The supplied-propensity risk ranges over “Borel real-valued decisions” receiving an entire propensity function, but the manuscript supplies no measurable structure on that function input. The later description uses measurability of the dataset section for each fixed supplied function. Readers therefore encounter two potentially different interpretations of admissibility.
  - *Fix:* Specify the verified admissibility convention explicitly in \cref{obj:def:oracle-risk}: identify measurability in the dataset and randomizer for each fixed supplied Borel propensity, or state the function-space measurable structure if joint measurability is intended. Use the same convention throughout the supplied-propensity discussion.
- **[minor·prose] Setup and assumptions** — The setup initially fixes L≥1, whereas the abstract, introduction, and statistical guarantees use L≥10. The class can be defined on a broader radius domain, but the distinction is currently implicit. Likewise, auxiliary counts and sequences alternate between natural-number notation and explicitly nonnegative notation despite the central role of zero auxiliary records.
  - *Fix:* Either adopt L≥10 as the standing analysis domain or expressly distinguish the broader class-definition domain from the guarantee domain. Define the natural-number convention and consistently write auxiliary counts and sequences as nonnegative integers.
- **[minor·prose] Verification note** — The sentence “The formal results are machine-verified in Lean 4 within the precise scope specified by the theorem-local verification disclosures” refers to disclosures absent from the supplied manuscript. The note also provides no artifact location or immutable revision through which a reader can locate the checked statements.
  - *Fix:* Make the verification note self-contained, replace the reference to absent disclosures with an accurate description of the checked scope, and add an artifact link, immutable revision, and theorem-to-file mapping. Preserve the distinction between the checked numerical supervised comparison and the cited source's statistical guarantees.
