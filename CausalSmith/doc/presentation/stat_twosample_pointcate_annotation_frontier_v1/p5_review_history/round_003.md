# Referee review

**Recommendation:** major_revision
**Overall score:** 7.2/10 — The verified unequal-sample minimax characterization delivers a substantial theoretical contribution, while extensive repetition and several presentation inconsistencies require substantial editorial revision for journal publication.

Under known uniform design, fixed public overlap and Hölder regularity, the paper characterizes pointwise CATE absolute-error minimax risk using labeled and outcome-free treatment records. Its attaining estimator and original-record converse establish the product-of-counts regime, supplied-propensity saturation, and acquisition thresholds uniformly over sample imbalance. The statistical contribution is persuasive and generally represented faithfully, but the manuscript needs a more economical organization and consistent parameter and notation conventions.

## Strengths
- The sharp characterization retains both information counts and distinguishes strict improvement from attainment of the supplied-propensity order.
- The original-record lower bound establishes the converse within the specified known-uniform class, with its occupancy condition and complementary supplied-propensity argument clearly distinguished.
- The estimator specifies observable moments, independent roles, public tuning, an eigenvalue guard, and clipping, supporting uniform finite-sample attainment.
- The early related-work section engages the closest supervised comparator's rates and conditions and fairly distinguishes marginal, surrogate-assisted, and supplied-predictor results.
- The numerical acquisition example clearly separates equality of rate formulas from minimax comparisons with parameter-dependent constants.

## Findings
- **[major·structure] Global organization, especially Estimation with outcome-free records and the appendices** — Repetition obscures the statistical argument. The manuscript repeatedly defines the same rate and decision through r_up, r_*, the sharp decision, and the frontier handle; repeats the role split inside the moments algorithm; and restates the minimax sandwich and branches in the acquisition proposition. Lengthy elementary constant bookkeeping and power manipulations dominate the appendices, making the central two-scale estimator and original-record information argument difficult to follow.
  - *Fix:* Organize the main exposition around one rate definition, one estimator construction, the minimax theorem, and the acquisition consequences. Reference the existing role algorithm from the moments algorithm, replace repeated explanatory passages with short cross-references, and move the frontier handle and representation-specific conventions to the verification material. Condense the written proofs around their statistical identities and bounds, retaining every condition, gate, and exact formal label.
- **[minor·prose] Setup and assumptions** — The sentence 'Fix public parameters ... and a finite Hölder radius L≥10' contradicts the radius domain L>1/2 in the abstract, primitive-class definition, verified guarantees, and worked example. As a standing setup restriction, it leaves readers uncertain about the scope of subsequent claims.
  - *Fix:* Replace L≥10 with finite L>1/2 and use that domain consistently throughout the manuscript.
- **[minor·prose] Global notation** — The manuscript explicitly defines N_0 to include zero but subsequently uses N for auxiliary counts and polynomial multi-indices without defining whether N includes zero. Zero is essential both to the advertised m=0 experiment and to the constant polynomial coordinate, so the distinction affects how readers interpret displayed statements.
  - *Fix:* State explicitly that N includes zero, or consistently use N_0 for counts and multi-indices and N_{≥1} for positive dimensions and resolutions. Apply the chosen convention throughout the formal-environment presentation.
- **[minor·prose] Estimation with outcome-free records** — Immediately after the localized cellwise basis definition, the prose says 'these coordinates are orthonormal ... Write q=binom(d+2,2) for their number.' The preceding coordinates are the fine basis, whose stated dimension is k=qJ^d. The subsequent instruction to 'Choose a cellwise orthonormal polynomial basis' also restarts a construction that has already been explicitly fixed.
  - *Fix:* Identify r as the coarse orthonormal basis with q coordinates and b_J as the prescribed fine basis with k=qJ^d coordinates. Replace the second partition-and-choice paragraph with a reference to the existing localized cellwise basis definition.
- **[minor·structure] Sharp precision and sample requirements** — The supplied-propensity theorem describes an 'explicit degree-two smoother' through its pseudo-outcomes and bandwidth, while its aggregation formula appears much later in the proof. The phrase can suggest empirical local least squares, whereas the verified construction uses a known-design polynomial evaluation average.
  - *Fix:* Display the clipped average using w_h(X_i)r_0^T r(X_i) and the stated pseudo-outcome beside the supplied-propensity theorem, or introduce it immediately beforehand. Preserve the stated bandwidth and localization extension to h≤1.
- **[minor·prose] Introduction and Sharp precision and sample requirements** — Structural references such as 'proved in the acquisition appendix' and 'The appendix supplies the approximation, sampling, and inversion arguments' provide reader-facing destinations without cleveref references. This makes navigation less precise and falls short of the cross-reference contract.
  - *Fix:* Add ordinary structural labels to the relevant sections and appendices and replace these destination references with \cref or sentence-initial \Cref commands. Retain the existing correctly formed object references.
