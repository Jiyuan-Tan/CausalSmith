# Referee review

**Recommendation:** major_revision
**Overall score:** 7.2/10 — The verified unequal-sample minimax characterization is a substantial theoretical contribution, but inconsistent target scope and a disrupted estimator exposition require substantial editorial repair.

The paper establishes a sharp pointwise absolute-error minimax rate for conditional treatment effects at the cube’s center under a known uniform design, fixed overlap, binary responses, and public Hölder regularity. Its attaining rectangular estimator and original-record lower bound yield informative acquisition and saturation thresholds. The contribution merits serious consideration, but the manuscript needs coordinated repairs to its target specification, construction sequence, verification description, and presentation.

## Strengths
- The characterization retains both sample sizes uniformly and separates strict improvement from attainment of the supplied-propensity order.
- The verified original-record converse establishes the lower bound within the stipulated known-uniform class.
- The estimator is explicitly defined through independent roles, empirical moments, guarded inversion, clipping, and public tuning.
- The acquisition discussion carefully distinguishes exact numerical rate conditions from minimax guarantees up to parameter-dependent constants.
- The dedicated early related-work section engages the closest supervised benchmark and preserves its source-model boundaries.

## Findings
- **[major·prose] Setup and assumptions; abstract; introduction** — The setup states, “The evaluation profile x_0∈(0,1)^d is fixed publicly throughout the analysis,” suggesting an arbitrary interior profile. The verified target and the displayed estimator use x_0=(1/2,…,1/2). Later definitions reset the profile to the center, leaving the scope of the risk definitions and headline guarantee inconsistent.
  - *Fix:* Specify x_0=(1/2,…,1/2) when introducing the target and maintain that specification throughout. Frame the abstract’s contribution explicitly as estimation at the cube’s center, and align the discussion of constant dependence with this fixed profile.
- **[major·structure] Estimation with outcome-free records** — The construction sequence contains conspicuously misplaced material. The paragraph introducing the coarse polynomial basis is followed by the lower-bound response-mean definition; the next sentence says “these coordinates are orthonormal,” apparently referring to those response means. The actual coarse-basis definition appears much later. Likewise, prose announcing that testing response means are “recorded next” precedes the rectangular upper-bound theorem, followed by another displaced perturbation explanation.
  - *Fix:* Place the scaled coarse-basis definition immediately after its introductory paragraph. Move the response-amplitude definition and its explanations into the lower-bound appendix, after the frame and bump are introduced. Place the rectangular upper-bound theorem beside its four-term interpretation and remove or relocate the orphaned transitions.
- **[minor·prose] Verification note** — The sentence “its source proof remains a literature dependency, as identified by the theorem-local disclosure” gives a stale account of the absolute-loss fuzzy-testing lemma. The current verification contract records a proved, sorry-free declaration, a faithful proof audit, and an empty external-dependency list for that lemma.
  - *Fix:* Describe the absolute-loss testing lemma as proved in the formal artifact, with the published source retained as bibliographic attribution. Update the verification summary and any associated disclosure to reflect the current proved dependency structure.
- **[minor·citation] Related work** — The discussion of Kim’s structure-adaptive CATE analysis emphasizes integrated squared-error and model-selection guarantees. The supplied literature context also identifies a directly relevant pointwise squared-error result, making the comparison incomplete for this paper’s pointwise contribution.
  - *Fix:* Add the pointwise result with the existing source citation and its Sections 2.3 and 6, Corollary 4 locator. State its bounded ambient-RKHS, overlap, and contrast-subspace conditions and its Sobolev pointwise squared-error order, then distinguish its loss and information structure from the present Hölder absolute-error experiment.
- **[minor·statement] Sharp precision and sample requirements** — The supplied-propensity theorem uses h=n^{-1/(2γ+d)}, which can exceed 1/2 for admissible small sample sizes. The localization measure was introduced for 0<h≤1/2, while the supplied-propensity proof explicitly extends the same formulas to 0<h≤1. The theorem’s notation therefore exceeds its previously declared domain without an accompanying explanation.
  - *Fix:* State beside the supplied-propensity estimator that its localization formulas use the extension to 0<h≤1, whose centered cube remains inside the design support. Preserve the rectangular estimator’s stated restriction 0<h≤1/2.
- **[minor·statement] Setup and assumptions; estimation construction; appendices** — The manuscript initially defines multi-indices in N_0^d but later indexes polynomial bases by N^d without defining whether N includes zero. The intended constant and lower-degree coordinates require nonnegative indices; the separate introduction of N_0 makes the switch particularly confusing.
  - *Fix:* Use N_0^d consistently for polynomial multi-indices and state the natural-number convention explicitly. Express sample-count domains consistently as n≥2 and m≥0.
- **[minor·statement] Acquisition consequences and supervised comparison** — The published-benchmark lemma labels the condition 1≤γ as “Propensity smoothness.” Throughout the model, γ is the causal-contrast smoothness order and α is the propensity smoothness order.
  - *Fix:* Change the condition label to “Effect smoothness” or “Contrast smoothness” and align the corresponding descriptive metadata with that terminology.
- **[minor·structure] Global; sharp precision and sample requirements; appendices** — Repeated definitions of the same rate, decision, regimes, and sample requirements substantially dilute the main contribution. The acquisition proposition repeats the minimax sandwich and supervised benchmark, including a zero-sample numerical convention outside the statistical domain n≥2. Long expansions of standard normalization and constant arithmetic further interrupt the argument.
  - *Fix:* Give the rate and decision one canonical definition, keep the main acquisition proposition focused on its new consequences, and use cross-references for repeated regimes and benchmarks. Relocate the zero-sample algebraic convention to a compact technical note. Condense standard proof steps while retaining all conditions and the substantive approximation, rectangular-sampling, and component-counting arguments.
