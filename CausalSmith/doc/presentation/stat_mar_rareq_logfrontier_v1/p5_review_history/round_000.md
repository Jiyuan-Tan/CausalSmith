# Referee review

**Recommendation:** major_revision
**Overall score:** 6.5/10 — The verified results constitute a substantial theoretical contribution, but unresolved priority attribution, disordered exposition, and several misleading descriptions prevent publication in the present form.

The paper establishes minimax precision comparisons for randomized experiments with categorical covariates and selectively observed binary outcomes, including rare-arrival interval bounds, a large-alphabet ascertainment-deficit comparison, and a constructive observational extension. Its central abstract and introduction claims largely preserve the verified regimes, quantifiers, and constant dependencies. Publication requires a clearer account of novelty, substantial consolidation of the exposition, and corrections to the descriptions of auxiliary-stream independence and mathematical notation.

## Strengths
- The constructive observational upper bound closes the logarithmic comparison with the cited fixed-positivity lower benchmark.
- The rare-arrival lower comparison retains full observed memberships and covers parameter-independent randomized estimators.
- The connected-interval result covers every fixed positive coverage level and explicitly incorporates endpoint randomization.
- The large-alphabet deficit comparison yields a precise parametric-risk criterion uniform over alphabet sizes.
- The principal prose carefully distinguishes universal rare-arrival comparisons, fixed-floor comparisons, and the all-arrival upper envelope.
- The dedicated early related-work section engages the closest competitors' rates and assumptions, and theorem-local disclosures preserve the external lower-bound dependency.

## Findings
- **[major·citation] Introduction; Related work; Limitations and open questions** — The manuscript leaves theorem-level priority relative to an unnamed earlier announcement unresolved, while presenting the constructive rate characterization as a principal contribution. The related-work brief identifies Kennedy's 2019 discrete-causal-model announcement, but the manuscript supplies neither that citation nor an identifiable comparison. The sentence beginning 'A separate unresolved question concerns theorem-level priority relative to the earlier announcement on discrete causal models' gives readers insufficient information to assess novelty.
  - *Fix:* Identify and cite the announcement in the early related-work discussion using its abstract locator. Conduct a source-supported comparison of the announced model, positivity regime, target, and rate with the present results. State the established contribution relative to the available evidence, and retain any specifically unresolved comparison in the limitations section.
- **[major·structure] Experiment, identification, and risk; Point estimation across ascertainment regimes; A constructive estimator; appendices** — The exposition repeatedly interrupts its statistical argument with duplicated definitions and certificates. Several setup paragraphs announce a condition different from the environment immediately following them: for example, 'Treatment assignment is governed by the following independence condition' precedes arrival MAR. The entire estimator is defined before the dedicated construction section and then explained again, while the same envelope appears in several long theorem statements. The arrived-count definition also contains an exhaustive cross-reference list spanning almost the entire paper.
  - *Fix:* Organize the setup into records, sampling and assignment, arrival conditions, identification, and decision criteria, with each introductory paragraph adjacent to its intended object. Give one canonical estimator presentation and one canonical envelope formula; use short references for specializations and consequences. Replace the exhaustive reference list with the few definitions needed locally, and consolidate repeated decision-theoretic explanations and numerical bookkeeping.
- **[major·prose] Risk certificates for the constructive estimator — Membership weights and branch control** — The sentence 'The membership weight ... is estimated from the membership stream, independently of the pilot and estimation streams in the auxiliary construction' attributes unconditional independence to the capped finite-sample construction. Independent Poisson streams belong to the uncapped comparison experiment used in the risk analysis; the finite-prefix construction and its overflow event require the transfer argument. The opening description of 'independent streams' repeats this conflation.
  - *Fix:* Attribute stream independence explicitly to the uncapped Poisson comparison experiment. Describe the reported finite-sample estimator through its capped prefix, assignments, and conditional averaging, and explain that the prefix-transfer bound connects it to the independent-stream analysis.
- **[minor·structure] Discussion and extensions; Limitations and open questions** — A limitations subsection and a subsequent standalone limitations section repeat overlapping material and both declare the label sec:discussion-limitations-and-open-questions. The duplicate label makes the intended cross-reference destination ambiguous.
  - *Fix:* Retain one limitations and open-questions section, merge the useful content into it, and declare its structural label exactly once. Update the discussion transition to reference that unique destination.
- **[minor·prose] Point estimation across ascertainment regimes; A constructive estimator; Risk certificates for the constructive estimator** — Several cleveref commands resolve to the wrong mathematical objects. The effective-size tuning references include the cell-alphabet definition. The risk-certificate prose cites the membership-count and weight definitions for H_j and G_j, the arrived-count definition for W_j, the weight definition for the selected statistic, and the arrived-one count for the elementary ratio. These errors obstruct following the construction despite the correct use of cleveref syntax.
  - *Fix:* Correct the targets: use \cref{obj:synth_14,obj:synth_15} for the factorial and selected statistics, \cref{obj:synth_11} for membership weights, and \cref{obj:synth_12} for the elementary ratio. Cite the local effective-size equation and the actual logarithmic and degree definitions for tuning. Check every prose reference against its target's title and content.
- **[minor·statement] Experiment, identification, and risk — Canonical observed sample law** — The canonical-law definition assigns the symbol \mathbf O_n to a probability measure, whereas the risk definitions and estimator subsequently use the same symbol for the random record tuple and then take its law. This creates a persistent type ambiguity in otherwise central notation.
  - *Fix:* Use distinct symbols for the sample tuple and its product measure. Preserve the canonical-law definition's product-distribution content, and update surrounding notation so that estimator arguments are tuples and integration measures are their laws.
- **[minor·prose] Abstract; Experiment, identification, and risk; Honest intervals under rare outcome arrival** — The estimator and interval envelope require the arrival-floor value as an input, but the manuscript emphasizes unknown cell arrival probabilities without clearly stating how the floor itself is supplied. The model class fixes q, and the reported deterministic guarantee is calibrated to that fixed value.
  - *Fix:* State early that q is a supplied positive lower bound used to index and tune the procedure, while individual occupied-cell arrival probabilities are unknown. Explain that prospective application requires a justified floor and that the stated coverage guarantee applies when this supplied bound satisfies the model condition.
- **[minor·prose] A constructive estimator; Honest intervals under rare outcome arrival; Limitations and open questions** — The computational and finite-sample significance of the construction needs clearer interpretation. The polynomial branch begins at log(e+nq) >= 128, corresponding to an effective sample size of approximately e^128. Exact averaging enumerates stream assignments, and the observational estimator adds an average over 2^n synthetic assignments. The limitations section acknowledges computational evaluation, but the main construction's 'precise computational interpretation' leaves these scales implicit.
  - *Fix:* Describe the procedure in the main text as an explicit mathematical attainment construction and state the polynomial-activation threshold in sample-size terms. In the limitations section, explain the cost of the displayed exact averages and distinguish the order comparison from the numerical usefulness of the explicit risk envelope and interval widths. Preserve the existing theorem guarantees.

## Questions for authors
- What source-supported comparison establishes the novelty of the discrete observational construction relative to Kennedy's 2019 announcement?
- How would a prospective trial analysis supply a justified arrival-floor value for tuning and coverage calibration?
- Can exact conditional averaging be evaluated more efficiently than the displayed enumeration, and what sample sizes yield informative numerical envelope intervals?
