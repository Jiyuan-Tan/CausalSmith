# Referee review

**Recommendation:** major_revision
**Overall score:** 6.8/10 — The verified results constitute a substantial theoretical contribution with carefully preserved scope, while extensive repetition, limited statistical intuition, and incomplete positioning impede publication in the present form.

The paper establishes constructive risk bounds for randomized treatment-effect estimation with categorical covariates and selectively observed binary outcomes, with matching comparisons under specified arrival regimes. Its honest-interval characterization, large-alphabet ascertainment-deficit result, and target-preserving observational reduction provide substantive additional contributions. The principal prose claims faithfully preserve the verified conditions and constant dependencies. A substantial revision should make the statistical mechanisms and contribution easier to assess and streamline the exposition.

## Strengths
- The rare-arrival comparison characterizes both effective sample size and category complexity with universal constants.
- The lower bounds retain observed memberships and cover parameter-independent randomized procedures.
- The interval result covers every fixed positive coverage level and preserves joint sampling-and-endpoint randomization.
- The large-alphabet result yields a precise near-complete ascertainment criterion with the correct uniform-over-alphabets quantifier.
- The synthetic-assignment transformation supplies an explicit observational estimator and clearly explains the direction of the experiment comparison.
- The manuscript carefully distinguishes universal upper envelopes, matched regimes, citation-dependent conclusions, and practical evaluation.

## Findings
- **[major·structure] Global organization** — The exposition repeatedly presents the same estimator, counts, and full risk envelope. The point-estimation section develops the entire construction before the dedicated constructive-estimator section repeats it; several appendix certificates repeat the same branch formulas again. The arrived-count definition also contains an enormous cross-reference list extending across nearly the entire paper. This repetition obscures the hierarchy of substantive results.
  - *Fix:* Present the model and identification concisely, define the estimator and envelope once, and state the regime comparisons using those definitions. Collect straightforward specializations as corollaries or in the comparison table. Place detailed certificates and total-domain conventions in a technical appendix, and replace the arrived-count cross-reference list with references to its immediate ingredients.
- **[major·prose] Introduction; A constructive estimator; Lower bounds with full observed memberships** — The manuscript gives detailed formulas and repeated rate interpretations, but the central statistical mechanisms receive comparatively little explanation. Readers need a clearer account of how unknown heterogeneous ascertainment creates the category term, how the polynomial statistic improves its logarithmic scale, and how the activated experiment makes the rare-arrival restriction relevant.
  - *Fix:* Add a short statistical roadmap connecting the identified weighted cell means to scarce-arrival cell bias, the elementary aggregate bias scale, and the polynomial bias scale already displayed in the certificate. Explain how moment matching preserves observation-law similarity despite full memberships, and how activation probability qH with H proportional to the squared logarithmic scale motivates the stated rare-arrival domain. Use these explanations to introduce the construction and converse before their detailed formulas.
- **[major·citation] Related work** — The comparisons with discrete observational estimation, surrogate efficiency, and decaying labelling are detailed and useful. Positioning against regression-functional bias correction, higher-order influence-function methods, and weak-overlap inference remains underdeveloped, although these literatures directly concern unknown nuisance functions, attainable precision, and positivity.
  - *Fix:* Research and add focused comparisons with the relevant regression-functional and higher-order influence-function results, and with weak-overlap inference. Identify their model restrictions, losses or inference criteria, and nuisance assumptions, then explain the contribution of arbitrary categorical frequencies and heterogeneous unknown arrival probabilities here. Add a foundational MAR identification reference at the identification discussion.
- **[minor·prose] Experiment, identification, and risk** — The canonical observed-sample definition assigns the symbol \(\mathbf O_n\) to a probability law, while the immediately following paragraph assigns it to a random tuple. Explaining this switch leaves the same symbol with incompatible types throughout the presentation.
  - *Fix:* Reserve \(\mathbf O_n\) for the random tuple and introduce a separate symbol for its canonical product law. Update the presentation definition and subsequent notation consistently while preserving the verified product-law identity.
- **[minor·prose] Related work; A constructive estimator** — Outside the limitations section, the sentence “The abstract does not state the resulting rates, the precise weakening of positivity, or an attaining estimator” frames the priority comparison through absent information. The construction discussion similarly ends its scale explanation with “rather than a claim of practical numerical efficiency.” These passages conflict with the affirmative prose contract.
  - *Fix:* Describe the conference abstract affirmatively as establishing earlier consideration of shrinking positivity, and keep the unresolved theorem-level comparison in the existing limitations section. Describe the estimator affirmatively as an explicit mathematical attainment construction with the stated activation threshold; retain computational evaluation questions in the limitations section.
- **[minor·statement] Point estimation across ascertainment regimes** — The titles “Unrestricted fixed-positivity minimax frontier” and “Unrestricted fixed-overlap phase boundaries” use terminology commonly associated with treatment propensities for results whose parameter q is an outcome-arrival floor. The statements explain this distinction correctly, but the titles blur the paper's separation of the randomized and observational experiments.
  - *Fix:* Rename these displayed results using “fixed arrival floor,” reserving treatment-positivity terminology for the observational margin. Preserve their existing hypotheses, conclusions, and exact cross-reference labels.

## Questions for authors
- Which existing regression-functional bias-correction results provide the closest comparison to the constructive logarithmic improvement, and which assumptions distinguish their guarantees?
- Can a concise prospective example explain how an arrival floor and baseline alphabet would be specified before analysis while preserving the stated uniform guarantees?
