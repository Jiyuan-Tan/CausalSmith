The paper belongs to the literature on average treatment effects, missing outcomes, and statistical precision under randomization. The potential-outcome formulation follows \citet{Neyman1990,Rubin1974}, with identification and experimental design treated systematically by \citet{Imbens2015}. Inverse-probability estimation traces to \citet{Horvitz1952}, while propensity-score adjustment and efficient observational ATE estimation were developed by \citet{Rosenbaum1983,Hahn1998,Hirano2003}. Rubin’s missing-at-random framework and subsequent augmented estimating equations supply the identification and estimation foundations \citep{Rubin1976,Robins1994,Bang2005}; \citet{Little2019,Tsiatis2006,Bickel1993,VanderVaart1998} provide standard treatments. Orthogonal scores and cross-fitting extend this framework to flexible nuisance estimation under product-rate conditions \citep{Chernozhukov2018,Kennedy2022}. The present analysis characterizes precision uniformly over growing finite baseline alphabets and unknown heterogeneous outcome-arrival probabilities. Its arrival floor plays the statistical role of an observation-positivity parameter, connecting it to the literature on limited overlap, irregular estimation, trimming, and robust inference \citep{Crump2009,Khan2010,Rothe2017,Ma2020}. Recent work by \citet{Dorn2025} establishes asymptotic normality and Wald inference for thresholded doubly robust estimators under weak-overlap and nuisance-rate conditions. These strands establish how observation probabilities and nuisance complexity affect attainable precision. Sources: [MAR estimating equations](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/01621459.1994.10476818), [double/debiased machine learning](https://academic.oup.com/ectj/article/21/1/C1/5056401), [weak-overlap inference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13273v2).

An immediate surrogate also connects the paper to clinical-trial and semi-supervised inference. \citet{Prentice1989,Fleming1996} articulate the requirements and interpretive risks of substituting surrogate endpoints for clinical outcomes. Under statistical surrogacy and cross-sample comparability, \citet{Athey2026} identify long-term treatment effects through a surrogate index and develop efficient estimators. Here, the immediate surrogate enters the conditioning set for outcome-arrival MAR. \citet{MarschnerBecker2001} analyze interim long-term binary treatment differences using early measurements, demonstrating precision gains theoretically, by simulation, and in a cholesterol-trial illustration; this supplies prospective motivation for the fixed-horizon precision benchmark. \citet{VanLancker2020} further develop interim decisions using short-term endpoints and prognostic covariates. Semi-supervised ATE and mean estimation provide another methodological lineage \citep{Cheng2021,Zhang2022,Chakrabortty2024,Hou2025}. The closest surrogate-assisted comparator, \citet[Theorems 2.1–2.2 and 4.1–4.2]{KallusMao2025}, derives semiparametric efficiency bounds under unconfoundedness and MAR and quantifies gains from surrogate information. In its dominating-unlabelled regime, Theorem 4.2 establishes asymptotic normality at the square-root labelled-sample scale under the stated nuisance-product conditions, with the efficiency analysis expressed through density ratios and labelled-population treatment overlap. Sources: [surrogate-index identification](https://academic.oup.com/restud/article/93/4/2284/8268796), [Marschner–Becker](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/interim-monitoring-of-clinical-trials-based-on-long-term-binary-e/), [Kallus–Mao, Sections 2 and 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.12408).

The nearest rate comparison is \citet[Assumption 2, Theorems 1–2, Section 3.2, and Appendix C.5]{ZengBalakrishnanHanKennedy2026}. For binary outcomes and discrete observational covariates with \(d\), the number of categories, their strict positivity margin \(0<\epsilon<1/2\) bounds occupied-cell propensities between \(\epsilon\) and \(1-\epsilon\). Theorem 1 bounds the treated regression-mean plug-in MSE by \((1-\epsilon)^2d^2/(\epsilon^2n^2)+C/(\epsilon n)\), where \(n\) is sample size and \(C\) is universal. For \(d\lesssim n\log n\), Theorem 2 gives the lower order \(n^{-1}+[d/(n\log n)]^2\), with constants depending on fixed \(\epsilon\); Section 3.2 identifies polynomial approximation as a route to closing the logarithmic gap. The present paper establishes a matched frontier for randomized MAR arrival, uniformly in \(q\), the known arrival floor, under \(q\log^2(e+nq)\le1/64\). Its fixed-positivity observational comparison uses an explicit fair-coin experiment map. Decaying-arrival asymptotics are also developed by \citet[Theorems 2.2 and 4.1]{ZhangChakraborttyBradic2025}. Writing \(m\) for their total sample size and \(a_m=\{E[\gamma_m(X)^{-1}]\}^{-1}\) for their reciprocal-mean observation scale, Theorem 2.2 obtains error order \((ma_m)^{-1/2}\) and standardized asymptotic normality when \(ma_m\to\infty\), both nuisances are correctly specified, residual-variance and moment conditions hold, and the nuisance product satisfies their equation (2.3). Theorem 4.1 develops bias-reduced inference under additional structural, sparsity, and approximation conditions. Kennedy’s earlier announcement explicitly discusses a discrete causal model with shrinking positivity \citep[abstract 300411]{Kennedy2019DiscreteAbstract}; it receives priority attribution, with the exact theorem-level overlap retained as an open priority audit. Sources: [Zeng et al., v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.00118v3), [decaying MAR, v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.12789v3), [Kennedy’s 2019 abstract](https://ww2.amstat.org/meetings/jsm/2019/onlineprogram/AbstractDetails.cfm?abstractid=300411).

The constructive and converse arguments follow the approximation-theoretic lineage of discrete functional estimation. Classical count extrapolation \citep{Good1956} and the analysis of large-alphabet plug-in bias \citep{Paninski2003} precede the polynomial estimators of \citet{JiaoVenkatHanWeissman2015,Wu2016}, which establish logarithmic effective-sample-size enlargement for their respective discrete functionals. \citet{WuYang2019} develops a Chebyshev estimator and moment-matching lower bounds for support-size estimation. Reciprocal approximation is a classical ingredient \citep[p. 33]{Meinardus1967}; \citet[Theorem 3.4]{https://doi.org/10.1007/s11075-026-02364-1} supplies an explicit best-uniform-absolute-error formula and credits this earlier source. The present construction adapts these tools to independently estimated full-membership weights and marked factorial moments, while its activation converse preserves the original cell memberships and fair randomized arms. This complements the higher-order influence-function and semiparametric minimax literature \citep{RobinsLiTchetgenVanderVaart2008,Robins2009}, and minimax bias correction for regression functionals \citep{Hirshberg2021}. Fuzzy-hypothesis comparisons fit the general minimax framework surveyed by \citet{Tsybakov2009}. Sources: [discrete-functional estimation](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.6956), [Chebyshev and moment matching](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.01227), [reciprocal approximation, Theorem 3.4](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11075-026-02364-1).

The interval result connects minimax estimation to honest uncertainty quantification. \citet{Donoho1994} relates functional estimation to optimal recovery, while \citet{Cai2004} develops expected-length lower bounds and adaptation theory for honest intervals. \citet{ArmstrongKolesar2020} constructs honest nonparametric-regression intervals using critical values that account for worst-case bias. In the present randomized binary-outcome experiment, a deterministic MSE envelope yields honest connected intervals, and a finite family of activated, mutually moment-matched priors yields the corresponding lower bound for every parameter-independent randomized endpoint kernel. Under the rare-arrival condition, the paper establishes MSE order \(r=\min\{1,(nq)^{-1}+[d/\{nq\log(e+nq)\}]^2\}\), the minimax squared-error rate, and maximal expected-length order \(\sqrt r\) at each fixed positive coverage level. Its additional point-risk results characterize fixed \(0<q<1/2\) with \(q\)-dependent constants and the large-alphabet order \(n^{-1}+(1-q)^2\) for \(d\ge1024n^2\). They identify \(1-q_n=O(n^{-1/2})\) as the necessary and sufficient parametric window uniformly over alphabet growth. A recent complementary frontier concerns complete-outcome finite-population experiments: \citet{Sudijono2026} optimizes jointly over designs and estimators for binary sample-average treatment effects, obtaining leading minimax risk \(n^{-1}\) and a second-order improvement through Bernoulli randomization and nonlinear shrinkage. Sources: [honest-interval adaptation](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0503662), [bias-aware intervals](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.3982/QE1199), [randomized-experiment minimax theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13822).
