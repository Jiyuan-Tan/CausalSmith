The paper belongs to the continuity-based regression discontinuity literature originating with \citet{Thistlethwaite1960} and developed within the potential-outcome framework of \citet{Rubin1974}. \citet{Hahn2001} formalize identification and estimation of cutoff treatment effects; the fuzzy-design interpretation also draws on the local-average-treatment-effect framework of \citet{Imbens1994}. Standard accounts of this field are \citet{Imbens2008,Lee2010,CattaneoTitiunik2022Survey}. Estimation at a cutoff connects RD to local polynomial boundary estimation \citep{Fan1996,Cheng1997}, nonparametric minimax theory \citep{Stone1980,Tsybakov2009}, and bandwidth selection \citep{Imbens2012}. Robust bias correction and coverage-error optimization develop asymptotic inference for direct local polynomial regression \citep{Calonico2014,Calonico2022}. A complementary lineage studies estimation and interval length under explicit smoothness restrictions: optimal recovery \citep{Donoho1994}, uniform-coverage constraints \citep{Low1997,Kamat2018}, optimal and bias-aware regression inference \citep{ArmstrongKolesar2018OptimalInference,ArmstrongKolesar2020SimpleHonest}, and optimized RD weights \citep{Imbens2019}. Boundary minimax results in Gaussian white-noise models \citep{Gao2018,Tuvaandorj2020} further locate the endpoint decision problem. These strands motivate treating absolute estimation risk and honest expected interval length as distinct criteria on a stated smoothness class. The present paper carries these criteria into a latent-score inverse experiment with recorded true-side assignment. The inverse-problem lineage begins with deconvolution density estimators and their optimal convergence rates \citep{Carroll1988,Stefanski1990,Fan1991}, and extends to regression ratios formed from deconvolved outcome-weighted and unweighted densities \citep{Fan1993,Delaigle2009}. \citet{Meister2009} and \citet{Schennach2016} provide broader statistical and econometric context. Compact latent support is consequential: \citet[Theorem 1(a), fixed-support case]{Meister2007CompactSupport} obtains mean integrated squared error of order \((\log n/\log\log n)^{-2s}\), where \(n\) is sample size and \(s\) is Fourier–Sobolev smoothness, for densities on a known fixed interval and an error characteristic function known and nonzero on a neighborhood of zero. Its lower-bound theorem is formulated over a joint signal-and-error class. Discrete-transform methods \citep{HallQiu2005DiscreteTransform}, Gaussian denoised moments \citep{WuYang2020DenoisedMoments}, and minimax estimation of convolution-model linear functionals \citep{Butucea2009,Pensky2017} supply additional methodological antecedents. The present construction uses finite inverse-heat polynomial weights for positive endpoint averages, followed by observable arm ratios. Its analysis links endpoint concentration, a factorial derivative bound, exact Gaussian covariance, and Bernoulli-marked likelihood comparisons to the causal cutoff contrast. Classical Legendre and probabilists' Hermite identities have standard provenance in \citet[Chapter 18, Tables 18.3.1 and 18.6.1, equations 18.14.1 and 18.12.16]{NISTDLMF}. [Compact-support theorem](https://www.f08.uni-stuttgart.de/.content/media/downloads/Mathematik/mathematische_berichte/2005/2005-007.pdf); [Gaussian moment methods](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.07237); [classical polynomial identities](https://dlmf.nist.gov/18).

The closest causal antecedents observe treatment together with a mismeasured score. \citet[Assumptions 1Y, 2C, and 7; Proposition 4(b)]{PeiShen2017DevilTails} identify the continuous-score sharp RD contrast under centered Gaussian error jointly independent of the latent score and observed outcome, with a density positive in a neighborhood of the cutoff. Their treatment-side convention is accommodated by reflecting the score and proxy. Potential-mean continuity is an explicit maintained condition here and appears in Dong's identification assumptions. \citet[Chapter 2, Assumptions 8, 9, 11, and 13; Theorem 6; printed pp. 49, 59, and 61]{Dong2018Thesis} develops observed-treatment deconvolution local-linear inference: under local twice-continuously-differentiable density and one-sided regression conditions, transform-integrability and kernel restrictions, a fixed known supersmooth error law, and a Lyapunov condition, the bias-centered and variance-normalized estimator is asymptotically standard normal. For Gaussian error, the specified bandwidth has order \((\log n)^{-1/2}\). This is the nearest established inference comparison for the latent cutoff estimand. \citet[Sections 3.1–3.2 and 4.1, Assumptions 3–5 and Theorems 1–2]{DaveziesLeBarbanchon2017ContinuousError} establish identification and sieve consistency for fuzzy-design LATE using nondifferential error and an auxiliary treated sample measuring the score discrepancy, with positive treatment probability across latent-score values. Related corrections accommodate group-specific mismeasurement \citep{Bartalotti2021}; \citet[Section 2.2, Proposition 1]{Carril2022} studies identification under piecewise-linear outcome structure and group-specific symmetric errors. These comparisons identify the respective assignment record, auxiliary information, smoothness conditions, and causal target needed for theorem-level positioning. Boundary deconvolution and vanishing noise supply two further comparisons. \citet{Zhang2000} and \citet[abstract and Sections 4–5 excerpts]{ZhangKarunamuni2009Boundary} study boundary density or density-derivative estimation with known independent additive error, using asymptotic mean squared error for ordinary-smooth and supersmooth errors. The verified comparison concerns that target, experiment, and loss. \citet{LeeHall2013BoundarySieve} establishes boundary-aware sieve consistency for mixture distributions. Deconvolution and errors-in-variables inference also includes maximal-deviation and bootstrap methods \citep{Bissantz2007,Delaigle2015} and asymptotically valid uniform bands with auxiliary information about the error law \citep{KatoSasaki2018DeconvolutionBands,KatoSasaki2019EIVBands}. For changing noise scales, \citet{Delaigle2008ShrinkingNoise} studies density kernels under shrinking measurement error. A current frontier is \citet[arXiv version, Section 2, Theorems 2.4–2.6]{DurotMukherjee2026VanishingNoise}. On a distribution class with bounded \((a+2)\)-moment, \(a>0\), their Gaussian Wasserstein-one minimax lower bound has order \(\sigma_n(\log n)^{-1/2}+n^{-1/2}\), where \(\sigma_n\) is the noise standard deviation. Their upper bounds match the large-noise regime \(\sigma_n\ge n^{-1/2+\eta}\), for fixed \(\eta>0\), and the regime \(\sigma_n\le n^{-1/2}\); the intervening regime has separately stated logarithmic bounds. This positions the present uniform noise-scale frontier alongside distribution-recovery results while retaining its endpoint functional and honest-length criterion. Assignment using the noisy score defines another important causal experiment. \citet[Section 2]{DongKolesar2023IgnoreMeasurementError} gives an observed-score causal interpretation when the score correctly classifies treatment and the relevant conditional potential means satisfy the stated continuity conditions. More recently, \citet[Assumptions 1–4, Theorem 2 and Corollary 2]{Eckles2025} uses treatment assignment by the noisy score as a source of randomization. With a known measurement channel, conditional exogeneity, bounded outcomes, positive conditional outcome variance, controlled weight sequences, and a declared treatment-effect heterogeneity class, it establishes asymptotic normality and asymptotically valid bias-aware intervals for weighted latent-population treatment effects; its optimization balances variance and latent-population imbalance. In the present experiment, administrative assignment reveals the true side of the latent cutoff. On the stated bounded latent range, bounded-density, and full-domain Hölder potential-mean benchmark, the paper characterizes matched minimax absolute risk and \(90\%\)-honest connected-interval expected length uniformly over every sample size \(n\ge2\) and known Gaussian noise standard deviation \(\sigma\in[0,1]\), with constants independent of \(\sigma\). For fixed Hölder exponent \(\beta\in(0,1]\), the frontier connects the direct order \(n^{-\beta/(2\beta+1)}\), the intermediate order \(\sigma^\beta/[1+\log(n\sigma^{2\beta+1})]^{3\beta/2}\) on its specified branch, and the fixed-positive-noise order \([\log\log(en)/\log(en)]^{2\beta}\). Observable polynomial ratios and a public Chebyshev interval attain the frontier, while legal full marked-likelihood alternatives establish all-procedure converses. Dong's Chapter 2, Section 2.6 Medicaid/CHIP analysis supplies a prospective application setting augmented by administrative true-assignment linkage, known Gaussian calibration, and a known bounded latent range, with the benchmark's global density, outcome-interior, and smoothness conditions explicitly maintained.
