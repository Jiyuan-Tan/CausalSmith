# Referee review

**Recommendation:** major_revision
**Overall score:** 7.1/10 — The verified uniform frontier is a substantial theoretical contribution, while incomplete main-text definitions, deferred tuning specifications, and several presentation and positioning issues require revision.

The paper establishes matching minimax orders for latent-cutoff estimation and honest connected-interval length under a precisely specified Gaussian measurement-error benchmark. Its simultaneous treatment of sample size and noise scale, including interfaces and shrinking-noise sequences, provides a coherent contribution to endpoint inverse problems and regression discontinuity. The principal contribution claims faithfully represent the verified results, and the larger-class discussion correctly presents transferred lower bounds. Publication requires a more self-contained statistical setup, a fully specified main-text procedure, and tighter exposition and literature comparisons.

## Strengths
- The matched estimation and honest-length frontier holds uniformly over sample sizes and known noise scales at each fixed Hölder exponent.
- The observable construction combines positive endpoint averaging, Gaussian polynomial inversion, stabilized ratios, and deterministic tuning with finite-sample coverage.
- The lower bounds concern the complete observed experiment and include independently randomized procedures.
- The benchmark clearly retains global density bounds and outcome-mean Hölder restrictions while allowing the density to vary without a Hölder restriction.
- The dedicated early related-work section distinguishes assignment mechanisms, causal targets, information structures, and decision criteria.
- The prospective application and larger-class converse generally preserve the exact scope of the benchmark results.

## Findings
- **[major·structure] Experiment, assumptions, and decision criteria** — The statistical setup introduces the observation map, P_obs, the randomized experiment, and the minimax criteria through descriptive paragraphs, but their complete mathematical definitions arrive substantially later. In particular, the benchmark observation equations first appear together in the appendix's alternative-law construction, and the minimax objective formulas appear after the main frontier theorem.
  - *Fix:* Define D=1{X≥0}, Y=(1−D)Y^0+DY^1, W=X+σε, the observed-law pushforward, θ(P), and the sample-and-seed product law in the latent-and-observed-experiments subsection. Display both minimax objectives in the decision-criteria subsection, with their measurable decision classes and coverage requirement, before presenting any theorem using them. Choose a consistent coordinate order for the observed triple.
- **[major·structure] Observable estimation and finite-sample inference** — The construction promises definitions that are deferred to proofs. The sentence 'We define them before introducing their sample counterparts' is followed by prose rather than formulas for A_{d,L}(P) and B_{d,L}(P). Likewise, public selection takes L_max as an input, while its required value is specified only deep in the proof of the uniform-frontier theorem. Readers therefore encounter the advertised attaining procedure before its tuning rule is complete.
  - *Fix:* Display the reflected-arm integrals defining A_{d,L}(P) and B_{d,L}(P) immediately before the sample averages. Specify L_max=ceil(n^{1/(4β+2)}) immediately before or within the public-selection algorithm, and make explicit that this cap defines the selected procedures used in the frontier theorem.
- **[minor·prose] Related work** — The comparisons carefully describe targets and conditions, but largely omit the quantitative results needed to assess the contribution against the nearest statistical antecedents. The compact-support discussion gives Meister's loss without its rate, the Dong comparison gives asymptotic normality without the Gaussian bandwidth order, and the vanishing-noise comparison describes regimes without reporting their bounds.
  - *Fix:* Add a compact comparison paragraph or table using the supplied source information: Meister's fixed-support MISE order (log n/log log n)^(−2s), with s identified as Fourier–Sobolev smoothness; Dong's Gaussian bandwidth order (log n)^(−1/2), attached to the bias-centered normality result; and Durot–Mukherjee's Gaussian Wasserstein-one lower bound and matching-regime qualifications. Explain the distinct targets and losses before interpreting the numerical orders, and retain exact source locators.
- **[minor·prose] Rate comparison, branch formulas, and verification scope — Verification scope** — The sentence 'Classical polynomial identities appear as explicit dependencies in the relevant statements; theorem-local verification notes identify any cited conclusions whose source proofs remain external' leaves the trust boundary ambiguous. The supplied verification contract records proved Legendre and Hermite declarations and empty external-dependency lists for the current objects.
  - *Fix:* Update this paragraph to state affirmatively that the classical Legendre and Hermite identities used here are established by the verified declarations. Describe theorem-to-theorem dependencies separately from external source assumptions, and align any discussion of external proof scope with the current dependency lists.
- **[minor·citation] Lower bounds for the observed experiment — Full observed-law comparison and decision transfer** — The full marked likelihood lemma introduces 'the cancellation polynomial g_m of \cref{obj:lem:classical-legendre-facts}'. That target contains classical Legendre identities; the cancellation polynomial is defined in the legal-cancellation lemma. The explanatory correction following the proof leaves the incorrect reference in the statement.
  - *Fix:* Replace that reference with \cref{obj:lem:legal-cancellation}. Retain references to \cref{obj:lem:classical-legendre-facts} where the intended attribution is to the underlying polynomial identities.
- **[minor·structure] Global** — Repeated restatements obscure the hierarchy of results. The direct-reduction theorem largely specializes the frontier; the precision handle inventories already defined objects; and the uniform-precision proposition repeats the main comparison and branch equations. Extensive explanatory passages then repeat these same conclusions across appendices. Extended-real conversion details also interrupt the statistical narrative.
  - *Fix:* Keep one principal frontier statement and present the direct specialization concisely. Consolidate repeated summaries and branch discussions, and move the precision inventory and extended-real bookkeeping into a compact verification appendix. Preserve every mathematical condition and exact interface convention while shortening the surrounding exposition.
- **[minor·citation] Related work; A prospective eligibility-effect benchmark** — Dong's dissertation is documented through a bibliographic footnote rather than the required external citation commands, and the Medicaid/CHIP subsection introduces its application setting without attributing it to Dong's Chapter 2, Section 2.6. This weakens provenance for the closest inference comparison and the proposed consumer.
  - *Fix:* Use a bibliography entry and \citet or \citep with Chapter 2, the relevant assumptions and Theorem 6, and the supplied printed-page locators. Cite Chapter 2, Section 2.6 when introducing Medicaid/CHIP, then identify administrative true-side linkage, calibrated Gaussian error, bounded latent range, and global regularity as maintained conditions of the proposed benchmark.
- **[minor·other] Observable estimation and finite-sample inference; A prospective eligibility-effect benchmark** — The explicit certificate uses substantial numerical constants and a radius-one-half fallback. Its finite-sample informativeness is consequently material to the prospective design interpretation, yet readers receive no numerical account of selected degrees, radii, or sample sizes at which positive-index candidates improve on the fallback. Rate optimality establishes asymptotic order, whereas those quantities determine the operational precision of this particular certificate.
  - *Fix:* Provide reproducible deterministic calculations of L_star and E_beta(n,σ) on a stated grid of smoothness, sample size, and noise values, including the transition from fallback to positive-index selection. Use numerically stable polynomial evaluation and integration, document precision checks, and interpret these calculations as illustrations of the established certificate.

## Questions for authors
- At economically relevant sample sizes and calibrated noise levels, when does the specified public certificate select a positive index and yield an interval shorter than the fallback?
- Which administrative and validation evidence would support the true-side assignment record and global benchmark restrictions in the prospective Medicaid/CHIP design?
