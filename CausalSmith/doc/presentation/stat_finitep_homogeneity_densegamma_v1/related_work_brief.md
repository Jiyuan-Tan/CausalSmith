The paper belongs to the literature on heterogeneous causal effects under unconfoundedness. Potential-outcome identification and propensity-score adjustment provide its foundation \citep{Rubin1974,Rosenbaum1983,Imbens2015,Hernan2020}; inverse-probability weighting and semiparametric efficiency supply the principal score constructions \citep{Horvitz1952,Robins1994,Hahn1998,Hirano2003}. Orthogonalization connects this tradition to modern nuisance learning \citep{Robinson1988,Chernozhukov2018}. Within this framework, heterogeneous-effect estimation has developed through regularization, honest recursive partitioning, causal forests, generalized forests, metalearners, and residualized or doubly robust learners \citep{Imai2013,Athey2016,Wager2018,Athey2019,Kunzel2019,Nie2021,Kennedy2023}. Projection-based inference and simultaneous inference for causal functions provide complementary summaries \citep{Semenova2021,Chernozhukov2025}. This paper studies a population feature of the original conditional mean effect: its \(L_2\) deviation from an unknown constant. Its contribution is a matched testing frontier under known uniform design, fixed overlap, independently specified primitive smoothness, and a public armwise conditional moment envelope.

The statistical lineage combines nonparametric specification testing, quadratic-functional inference, and robust estimation. Integrated conditional moments, kernel specification tests, and generalized likelihood-ratio methods supply established approaches to detecting departures from functional restrictions \citep{Bierens1990,Zheng1996,Fan2001}; Hoeffding projections provide the decomposition underlying quadratic statistics \citep{Hoeffding1948}. Gaussian minimax testing supplies canonical separation theory \citep{Ingster2003,Tsybakov2009}. In particular, \citet[Section 6.1]{IngsterSapatinas2009GoodnessOfFit} obtain the Sobolev separation rate \(n^{-2\sigma/(4\sigma+d)}\), where \(\sigma\) is smoothness and \(d\) is covariate dimension, under \(\sigma>d/4\). The present paper supplies its own treatment of the effect-smoothness endpoint one quarter. \citet{CommingesDalalyan2013QuadraticNull} develop minimax testing for composite regression nulls defined by quadratic functionals. Robust means, robust U-statistics, and rank-based regression tests contribute distinct tools for managing outcome tails \citep{Bubeck2013,Devroye2016,JolyLugosi2016RobustU,Feng2016,Lugosi2019}. Higher-order estimating equations connect projection corrections to causal nuisance products \citep{RobinsEtAl2017HigherOrder}. For CATE estimation, \citet[Theorem 1 and Section 4]{KennedyBalakrishnanRobinsWasserman2024MinimaxCATE} establish a pointwise lower-bound phase between exponents \(1/\{1+d/(2\gamma)+d/(4s)\}\) and \(1/(2+d/\gamma)\), where \(\gamma\) is effect smoothness and \(s\) is average propensity/baseline smoothness, and give attainment under stated density and nuisance conditions. Together with \citet{Gao2020}, this work supplies an estimation benchmark; the present analysis derives separation from quadratic null cancellation and full-record testing converses.

Direct homogeneity testing has a substantial econometric history. \citet[author manuscript, Section 3.4, Theorems 3.3--3.4]{CrumpHotzImbensMitnik2008Homogeneity} construct a standardized sieve Wald test with an asymptotic normal null distribution and consistency against smooth local alternatives satisfying their sieve-dependent separation condition. \citet[author manuscript, Section 4.2, equation (4.2), Theorem 6]{SantAnna2021DurationHomogeneity} obtain bootstrap size, fixed-alternative consistency, and nontrivial \(n^{-1/2}\) local power for duration-effect homogeneity under their censoring, integrability, and series conditions. \citet[Section 3]{DaiStern2022Rank} develop finite-stratum rank U-statistics with asymptotic calibration; their rank restrictions correspond to equality of mean contrasts under the stated location-shift conditions. \citet[author manuscript, Sections 2--3, Theorems 1--2]{DaiShenStern2023Observational} extend this approach through propensity weighting and asymptotic projection calculations. Randomization and permutation methods address individual-effect variation or common distributional shifts under their respective assignment and outcome restrictions \citep{Ding2016,Ding2019,Chung2021}, while qualitative interaction tests assess changes in treatment preference \citep{Gail1985}. Variance-based summaries also have an established lineage: \citet{Levy2021} develop targeted estimation of treatment-effect variance, and \citet[Theorems 6--7]{SanchezBecerra2023} establish efficiency for the full variance under additional learning conditions and uniform asymptotic coverage for a fold-specific proxy variance under known experimental assignment and fourth-moment conditions. For score-defined groups, \citet[Theorem 3]{Imai2025} obtain asymptotic homogeneity-test calibration, and \citet{Li2023} construct uniform confidence bands for sorted group effects.

The closest current mean-effect tests use orthogonal scores and asymptotic calibration. \citet[Assumptions 1--5, Theorems 3.1--3.3 and 4.1]{LuSong2026OrthogonalICM} test heterogeneity across a specified covariate subvector using an orthogonal marked empirical process. Their smooth compact-support and series-growth conditions yield a Gaussian null limit, fixed-alternative consistency, shifted limits for fixed-shape \(n^{-1/2}\) local alternatives, and valid multiplier-bootstrap critical values. \citet[Assumptions 3.3--3.4, Propositions 3.1--3.2]{LapentaStrittmatterVergara2026Omnibus} obtain asymptotic bootstrap size and fixed-alternative consistency for integrated conditional moments with bounded outcomes and \(o_P(n^{-1/4})\) propensity and arm-regression errors in \(L_2\). \citet[Assumptions 3--4, Theorem 1]{Yu2026VarianceHTE} test zero CATE variance through a split-sample statistic: fourth outcome moments, nondegeneracy, \(o_P(n^{-1/4})\) \(L_2\) errors for propensity, arm means, and CATE, and a root-\(n\) average-effect estimator support asymptotic normality, limiting size control, and fixed-alternative consistency. \citet[Theorem 1, Corollaries 1--2]{DhawanGuoShah2026Directional} develop a directional hunt-and-test procedure with uniform asymptotic size under their regularity conditions, including a standardized \(2+\delta\) moment condition. Their power guarantee requires alignment of the learned direction with the population score and divergence of the stated signal–alignment product. These results give precise asymptotic comparators for the present paper's finite-sample, whole-null guarantees and uniform \(L_2\)-separation criterion.

Adjacent developments clarify the range of heterogeneity questions. \citet[arXiv version 4, Theorems 1--3]{DukesStensrudBrioschiHudson2026Homogeneity}, now published in JASA, develop quantitative and qualitative policy-relevant tests with Gaussian-process calibration under their nuisance and function-class conditions. \citet[Theorems 3.1--3.3]{JainLuedtke2026Distributional} study kernel-smoothed conditional distributional contrasts, obtaining root-\(n\) Hilbert-space inference and testing under conditions that include a propensity convergence exponent plus the smaller arm-embedding exponent exceeding one half. Predictive comparisons assess gains from treatment-effect flexibility \citep{Watson2020,Williamson2023,AshouriHenderson2026Predictive}; Ashouri–Henderson use nested cross-validation to compare a flexible learner with an HTE-constrained learner. Regression-discontinuity tests pursue heterogeneity under a separate identification design \citep{Song2026}. Official forest calibration fits held-out best-linear coefficients and interprets a significantly positive differential-prediction coefficient as evidence of heterogeneity \citep[Description]{GRFCalibrationDocumentation}. Against this background, the present paper establishes the sharp original-mean homogeneity frontier for every public moment exponent between one and two, independent propensity and baseline Hölder exponents between zero and one, and effect Hölder exponent between one quarter and one. Its observable tests have finite-sample size control over the entire composite null, matched nonempty-alternative power, all-original-record randomized-test converses, and a supplied-propensity comparison. The internal bibliography records historical construction provenance separately, while the accepted discrete-adjustment entry supplies an ATE-estimation comparison \citep{CausalSmith2026a}.
