# Referee review

**Recommendation:** major_revision
**Overall score:** 6.8/10 — The verified separation frontiers constitute a substantial theoretical contribution, but the manuscript needs substantial editorial restructuring, clearer explanation of its distinctive mechanisms, and several targeted presentation repairs.

The paper establishes matching minimax separation rates for conditional mean-effect homogeneity under finite conditional moments, independently specified nuisance smoothness, and a known uniform design. It also constructs calibrated tests, establishes bounded–binary benchmark equivalence, and characterizes the value of supplying the true propensity. The principal abstract and introductory claims faithfully preserve the verified parameter domains, power conditions, and capped-radius interpretation. Publication requires a more focused exposition that makes the statistical contribution accessible without repeated formal bookkeeping.

## Strengths
- Matching upper and lower bounds characterize the interaction of outcome moments, effect smoothness, and separate propensity and baseline smoothness.
- Finite-sample calibration covers the entire composite null, and power claims consistently retain the nonempty-separation condition.
- The supplied-propensity comparison distinguishes broader-class attainment from the preservation boundary on the original shared class.
- Exact bounded–binary minimax equivalence provides a particularly clean benchmark for the conditional mean-effect target.
- The early related-work section engages several close competitors' assumptions and guarantees, including existing uniform asymptotic inference.
- The contribution is framed affirmatively and generally avoids overstating either causal identification or literature priority.

## Findings
- **[major·structure] Global organization and main results** — Repeated definitions and overlapping theorem packages obscure the hierarchy of contributions. The distance, model supremum, experiment, parameter domains, calibration guarantees, witness normalization, and threshold conditions recur extensively. The heavy-tail comparison combines the primary frontier with detailed prior constructions and auxiliary topological conclusions, making the central result difficult to extract.
  - *Fix:* Reorganize the main text around the original-record frontier, the bounded comparison, the calibrated construction, and the supplied-propensity comparison. Define common notation once. Move lengthy witness packages, explicit constant inventories, and overlapping full statements to the appendices, preserving their anchors and every hypothesis. Give concise main-text summaries with exact cleveref references to the complete statements.
- **[major·prose] Separation rates and the cost of outcome tails; A calibrated test from the original records** — The two mechanisms are named, but the distinctive rough-confounding mechanism receives insufficient statistical explanation. Readers encounter the exponent and a lengthy tuning algorithm before learning why the projection correction, nuisance-product bias, and resolution-specific clipping jointly produce that exponent. The prose describing each algorithm step largely restates its formula.
  - *Fix:* Add a short explanatory derivation using the existing verified mean and covariance bounds. Explain the nuisance-product cancellation, the roles of coarse and fine resolution, the reason clipping varies across correction levels, and the lower-bound information concealed by the coupled coefficient fields. Explicitly distinguish the rate boundary F(p)=1 from the algorithm's representation-selection condition. Keep the exact constants and detailed calculations in the appendices.
- **[minor·citation] Related work** — The variance-target discussion concentrates on Yu while leaving the earlier treatment-effect variance literature identified in the supplied background unexamined. Because the paper's squared distance equals treatment-effect variance, targeted variance estimation and uniform inference for variance-related targets are especially relevant to its positioning.
  - *Fix:* Consult and discuss Levy and Sanchez-Becerra with precise source locators. Compare their population targets, assignment information, moment conditions, nuisance-learning requirements, and uniformity guarantees with this paper's minimax separation criterion. Preserve the distinction between inference for a full population variance and inference for a fold-specific proxy.
- **[minor·structure] Setup and assumptions** — Formal objects repeatedly appear under subsections concerning a different experiment. The bounded-outcome and signed-binary assumptions interrupt causal interpretation; the causal-completion definition appears inside the original-record testing subsection; and the original-model testing-risk and critical-radius definitions appear after the matched-benchmark heading. This placement makes support restrictions look like part of the causal interpretation and obscures which experiment each definition belongs to.
  - *Fix:* Place the causal-completion definition with causal interpretation. Complete the original experiment, null, risk, and radius before introducing benchmarks. Move both outcome-support assumptions into the benchmark subsection immediately before their associated model definitions.
- **[minor·statement] Heavy-tail critical-radius comparison; Sharp rough full-record lower bound** — Several references resolve to objects that omit the content attributed to them. The heavy-tail comparison says the exponent gap equals a phase-difference expression in \cref{obj:def:sharp-frontier-scales}, but that definition contains no such expression. The rough lower-bound statement attributes the selected bounded priors to \cref{obj:def:converse-handle}, whereas their selection is supplied by \cref{obj:synth_1}. Surrounding text also announces a definition immediately before a lemma.
  - *Fix:* Give the phase-difference display an exact structural or equation label and reference it with cleveref, or include the expression at the stated location. Point bounded-prior selection references to \cref{obj:synth_1}, and place that definition before its use. Rewrite the adjacent transitions to match the actual environment kind and order.
- **[minor·statement] Sharp rough full-record lower bound** — The statement uses the symbols A and B for 'activity budgets' attributed to the component bound and then displays deterministic bounds. In the component bound, those symbols denote augmentation-dependent conditional budgets whose expectations are bounded. The extracted rough-lower declaration instead bounds the deterministic activityBudgetA and activityBudgetB quantities. The shared notation leaves the level of the assertion ambiguous.
  - *Fix:* Introduce distinct notation for the deterministic activity bounds and the conditional random budgets. State the displayed numerical bounds for the deterministic quantities, and separately state that the expectations of the conditional budgets are bounded by them. Apply that distinction consistently in the surrounding prose.
- **[minor·statement] Original-record attainable separation rate** — The compact-uniformity display ends with '\qquad\ne\le E(v)'. In LaTeX, '\ne' denotes inequality; it does not introduce the positive lower-bound variable e. The rendered condition therefore fails to express the verified assertion.
  - *Fix:* Replace the malformed fragment with '\qquad e\le E(v)', retaining the preceding condition e>0.
- **[minor·prose] Discussion and extensions** — The discussion describes the public thresholds as giving the procedures an 'operational interpretation', but provides no numerical evaluation of the explicit constants or thresholds. The supplied formulas contain substantial conservative multipliers, so readers cannot assess the sample-size scale of the guaranteed power domain.
  - *Fix:* Add a small numerical table evaluating the existing upper multipliers and public thresholds for representative admissible tuples, using logarithms where necessary. Distinguish guaranteed thresholds from any observed performance and describe the test's operational scope in light of those calculations.
- **[minor·prose] Abstract; Discussion and extensions** — The phrase 'the exact boundary at which learning assignment probabilities preserves separation order' can suggest a guarantee for procedures using an estimated propensity function. The verified comparison concerns minimax radii in the unknown- and supplied-propensity experiments, and the attaining original-record procedure uses projection corrections directly.
  - *Fix:* Describe the boundary as the region where the original-record experiment attains the supplied-propensity separation order. Reserve claims about accuracy requirements for estimated propensity functions for the explicitly labelled future-work discussion.

## Questions for authors
- Which elements of the rough-confounding construction should readers regard as the principal methodological innovation relative to existing higher-order causal methods?
- How large are the explicit guaranteed-power thresholds for representative tuples in the two regimes and on their equality boundary?
- Can the main text present one consistent notation and indexing convention, with formal bookkeeping translations confined to the appendices?
