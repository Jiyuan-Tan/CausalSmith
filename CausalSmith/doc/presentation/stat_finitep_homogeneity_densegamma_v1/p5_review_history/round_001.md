# Referee review

**Recommendation:** major_revision
**Overall score:** 7.2/10 — The verified frontiers constitute a substantial theoretical contribution, while extensive repetition, limited explanation of the rate mechanisms, and insufficient interpretation of the explicit calibration constants require substantial revision.

The paper establishes matching minimax separation rates for conditional mean-effect homogeneity under fixed overlap, uniform one-dimensional design, public smoothness exponents, and conditional moments of order between one and two. Its bounded-outcome equivalence and supplied-propensity comparison provide informative benchmarks, and the principal prose accurately preserves the verified power conditions and parameter domains. Publication requires a more focused exposition, clearer statistical intuition, and concrete interpretation of the finite-sample construction.

## Strengths
- Matching upper and lower bounds characterize both the outcome-moment mechanism and the propensity–baseline interaction mechanism.
- The explicit tests accommodate the unknown common effect and provide uniform finite-sample calibration with carefully stated nonempty-power conditions.
- The supplied-propensity analysis distinguishes broader-class attainment from comparison on the original shared class.
- Exact bounded–signed-binary risk equivalence and complete-record lower witnesses strengthen the interpretation of the outcome comparison.
- The dedicated early related-work section engages competitors' assumptions, calibration criteria, and alternative-power guarantees.

## Findings
- **[major·structure] Global organization; setup; main results; appendices** — Repeated definitions, theorem premises, constants, and interpretations overwhelm the central contribution. The model distance, parameter domains, testing risks, and saturation conditions are restated throughout; the two supplied-propensity frontier statements reproduce large overlapping packages. The main comparison theorem combines the rate result with detailed witness properties, positivity assertions, topology, and threshold bookkeeping.
  - *Fix:* Organize the main exposition around the population target, the two rate mechanisms, the attaining test, and the two experiment comparisons. State shared notation once, move complete witness and constant packages to the appendices, and retain concise summaries with exact cleveref references in the main text. Preserve all anchored claims, quantifiers, and power gates while consolidating surrounding prose.
- **[major·prose] Separation rates and the cost of outcome tails; A calibrated test from the original records** — The main text describes E_0 as an approximation–moment balance and E_4 as a nuisance interaction, but the connection between the statistical construction and their denominators remains largely confined to the attainment proof. In particular, readers receive little explanation of the asymmetric roles of propensity and baseline smoothness when p<2, or why correction-specific clipping supports the rough-confounding exponent.
  - *Fix:* Add a short heuristic derivation using the existing bias and covariance bounds. Explain how coarse approximation, fine nuisance-product bias, and clipped quadratic variability produce the two balances; explain the role of multiresolution clipping and distinguish its computational branch condition from the statistical phase condition. Include one numerical parameter example in each regime and an explanation of the baseline-oriented model parameterization.
- **[major·prose] Discussion and extensions; Explicit attainment constants and separation** — The sentence 'These thresholds give the rate statements an operational interpretation' emphasizes applicability without showing the scale of the supplied constants. The public thresholds can be extremely large: even the displayed supplied-propensity threshold at p=2 and effect smoothness one is approximately seven trillion observations. Readers need to distinguish the numerical sufficient guarantees from the minimax rate comparison.
  - *Fix:* Evaluate the existing multipliers and sufficient thresholds for a small representative table covering moment order two, a lower moment order, and both statistical regimes. Describe these as conservative sufficient bounds and distinguish the nominal rate separation from the directly computed calibrated separation. Calibrate the operational discussion to these numerical values; any stronger practical-performance claim should be supported by implementation and simulation evidence.
- **[minor·prose] Abstract; Introduction; Discussion and extensions** — The abstract's phrase 'the exact boundary at which learning assignment probabilities preserves separation order' can suggest a guarantee for a particular propensity estimator or plug-in learning procedure. The verified comparison characterizes the minimax radii of the unknown- and supplied-propensity experiments, and the attaining original-record rule uses projection corrections.
  - *Fix:* Describe the boundary as the exact region where the unknown-propensity experiment attains the supplied-propensity separation order on the original class. Use this wording consistently, and reserve estimator-specific accuracy requirements for the explicitly labelled future-work discussion.
- **[minor·citation] Related work** — The variance-target positioning gives detailed attention to Yu's recent test. The established treatment-effect-variance literature, especially Levy and Sánchez-Becerra, provides additional close comparisons for the zero-variance target and the known-assignment benchmark.
  - *Fix:* Add a concise paragraph comparing the paper's full population variance separation criterion with targeted variance estimation and Sánchez-Becerra's full-variance efficiency and fold-specific proxy coverage results. Verify the precise targets, assignment information, moment conditions, and learning assumptions, and provide theorem-level locators.
- **[minor·citation] Setup and assumptions; Causal interpretation; Supplied-propensity proofs and comparison algebra** — Several citations supporting technical statements lack the required source locators, including the repeated Kennedy–Balakrishnan–Robins–Wasserman citations attached to primitive restrictions, the causal-identification citations, and the inverse-weighting citation. Repeated attribution of individual numerical normalizations also obscures which restrictions are stipulated here and which formulation is inherited.
  - *Fix:* Consolidate the assumption-motivation citations and distinguish the paper's fixed numerical envelopes from the cited modeling precedent. Add accurate section, theorem, equation, or page locators to technical source claims, preserving the existing precise locators elsewhere.
- **[minor·statement] Bias, covariance, profiling, and attainment — Calibration and population decomposition** — The original score mean handle uses T_0 for the separately supplied main cutoff while also indexing increment cutoffs T_j from zero. It additionally switches the block scores from one-based to zero-based notation. The subsequent translation paragraph explains the intended correspondence, but the displayed notation still assigns overlapping names to distinct inputs.
  - *Fix:* Use distinct names for the main cutoff and the increment array, or adopt the one-based increment convention throughout the paper. Keep one block-index convention in the reader-facing exposition and record the formal indexing correspondence once. Preserve the underlying score definitions and bounds.

## Questions for authors
- How do the directly computed calibrated separations compare numerically with the nominal upper-rate bounds at representative sample sizes and public tuples?
- What statistical interpretation best explains the asymmetric propensity and baseline smoothness dependence under moments below order two, given the baseline-oriented model parameterization?
- Which construction ingredients are essential to attaining the rough-confounding exponent, and which serve primarily to make calibration constants explicit?
