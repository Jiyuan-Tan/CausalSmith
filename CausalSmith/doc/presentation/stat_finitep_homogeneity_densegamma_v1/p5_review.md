# Referee review

**Recommendation:** major_revision
**Overall score:** 7/10 — The verified separation frontiers offer a substantial theoretical contribution with largely faithful scope, while the placement of principal results and extensive repetition require substantial editorial revision.

The paper characterizes minimax separation for conditional mean-effect homogeneity under conditional moment and independent smoothness restrictions, constructs calibrated tests, and establishes matched outcome-envelope and supplied-propensity comparisons. Taking the verification contract as authoritative, the central conclusions are strong and the prose generally preserves their conditions, including saturation and nonempty-power gates. Publication requires a clearer main-results structure, considerable compression, and more focused explanation of the methodological and econometric significance.

## Strengths
- Matching upper and lower separation bounds characterize two mechanisms and their exact equality boundary across the stipulated parameter domain.
- The original-record construction explicitly handles clipping, nuisance-product corrections, independent evaluation blocks, and the unknown common effect.
- The outcome-envelope comparison preserves primitive smoothness and includes exact bounded–signed-binary minimax equivalence.
- The supplied-propensity analysis distinguishes broader-class attainment from the comparison on the original shared class.
- The manuscript consistently preserves finite-sample power gates and candidly reports the scale of its conservative sufficient thresholds.
- The dedicated early related-work section engages competitors' outcome conditions, nuisance-learning requirements, calibration criteria, and alternative classes.

## Findings
- **[major·structure] Separation rates and the cost of outcome tails; Supplied propensity scores and assignment information** — The principal frontier statements appear near the end of the supplied-propensity appendix, although the main text repeatedly announces an immediately forthcoming result. For example, 'The next result gives matching bounds for the broader-class critical radius' is followed by interpretation rather than the theorem. The same problem affects the heavy-tail comparison, the shared-class oracle frontier, and the preservation boundary. Readers must traverse extensive construction details and appendices to locate the formal answers to the paper's main questions.
  - *Fix:* Move the existing heavy-tail comparison environment into the separation-rates section, and move the broader-class frontier, shared-class frontier, and preservation-boundary environments into their corresponding main-text subsections. Preserve their anchors, hypotheses, constants, and power gates. Place interpretation immediately after each statement and retain supporting calculations and proofs in the appendices.
- **[major·structure] global** — Repeated definitions and repeated explanatory summaries obscure the logical progression. The heterogeneity distance, model suprema, public domains, experiment, and rejection expectation are reintroduced across numerous environments and their surrounding paragraphs. The calibration and mean handles also restate much of the already specified algorithm, while several paragraphs repeat the same size, power-gate, and capping interpretations.
  - *Fix:* Establish a single standing-notation passage and use cross-references in surrounding prose. Remove duplicate formula displays immediately preceding environments that contain the same formulas, consolidate repeated interpretations, and streamline the appendix introductions. Retain the complete verified statements where needed, while giving each surrounding paragraph a distinct explanatory purpose.
- **[minor·prose] Related work; Supplied propensity scores and assignment information; Discussion and extensions** — Several ordinary-prose passages violate the affirmative-scope contract. These include 'it does not replace the variance by a fold-specific proxy or assume a learned first stage,' 'Requirements for a particular estimated-propensity procedure are outside this experiment comparison,' and the statement that practical calibration requires implementation and simulation 'beyond the present analysis.' The last two identify genuine extension or non-coverage content outside the expressly titled limitations subsection.
  - *Fix:* Describe the delivered target affirmatively, for example: 'The criterion assesses finite-sample testing risk for the full population variance over the stipulated primitive classes.' Describe the assignment comparison as comparing experiments receiving records alone or records together with the true propensity. Move the estimated-propensity and practical-calibration extension statements into 'Limitations and future work.'
- **[minor·prose] Separation rates and the cost of outcome tails** — The explanation that 'propensity regularity enters twice through treatment weighting and projection correction, whereas the baseline appears once and is amplified by the tail factor' assigns a precise mechanism to individual denominator terms without displaying the scalar balance that supports that interpretation. The verified statements establish the exponent and covariance bounds; readers need a clearer bridge between those bounds and this explanation.
  - *Fix:* Replace the term-by-term verbal attribution with a short tuning argument showing the interaction bias K^{-S}, the relevant coarse-rank factor, and the correction covariance contribution after clipping allocation. Explain how their balance produces the displayed denominator, using the existing bounds and preserving the distinction between representation selection and the statistical phase boundary.
- **[minor·citation] Related work** — The higher-order CATE paragraph identifies the methodological connection and different loss criterion, but its comparison remains qualitative despite the cited work's explicit minimax phase transition. This is particularly relevant to the paper's independent propensity and baseline exponents and its quadratic-testing mechanism.
  - *Fix:* Add a concise, source-scoped comparison reporting the cited pointwise estimation exponents, their smoothness threshold, and the stated attainment conditions. Explain how the present global testing frontier relates to that estimation benchmark while preserving the distinct targets and model restrictions. Verify the theorem and section locators for the version cited.
- **[minor·prose] Introduction; Discussion and extensions** — The scientific interpretation centers on the standard deviation of the conditional effect, but the economic reason to care about the two comparisons remains abstract. The introduction gives extensive technical detail before developing a concrete use of a scalar effect modifier or explaining when supplied assignment information corresponds to an economically meaningful design.
  - *Fix:* Add one concise motivating example of homogeneity across a scalar covariate under the stipulated design. Explain how the target measures variation in conditional mean effects and how records-only versus supplied-propensity experiments inform observational and known-assignment settings. Keep the public-exponent and known-design conditions explicit.
- **[nit·prose] Bias, covariance, profiling, and attainment** — The sentence 'The same calculation applies under the zero-based convention after translating the block indices' introduces an unexplained alternative convention after the manuscript has consistently adopted one-based blocks. Terms such as 'retained,' 'handle,' and 'ledger' also recur where standard descriptions would communicate the mathematical role more directly.
  - *Fix:* Delete the alternative-indexing sentence and use the established block convention throughout. In running prose, prefer 'score decomposition,' 'bias bound,' and 'covariance bound' where these describe the object accurately, retaining formal environment identifiers and notation.

## Questions for authors
- Which aspect of the rough-confounding construction is the principal methodological advance beyond existing projection-based causal estimation: the multiresolution clipping allocation, the testing-specific cancellation, or their combination?
- How informative is the directly evaluable calibrated separation, compared with the coarse sufficient multiplier, at economically relevant sample sizes?
- Can the main exposition organize the original-record frontier, outcome-envelope comparison, and supplied-propensity comparison around three clearly stated results?
