%%% FIELD proof-prop-anisotropic-spectrum-bias
Write \(\Phi=\Phi_{J_y,J_z}\), \(G=G_{R,J_y,J_z,n}\), \(H=H^{\mathrm{form}}_{J_y,J_z,n}\), \(B=B_{J_y,J_z,n}\), and \(K=K_{J_y,J_z,n}\).  All inner products and norms without a displayed measure are in \(L^2(dy\,dR_n)\).  Constants below depend only on \(c,C,L,s_y,s_z,d\), the compact supports, and the fixed boundary-wavelet system, and not on \(n,J_y,J_z,\rho\), or the member of \(\mathcal M_{n,d}(s_y,s_z,\rho)\).

First consider the inverse-elliptic form.  If \(f(\cdot,z)\) has zero outcome integral, set
\[
 H_f(y,z)=\int_0^y f(v,z)\,dv,
 \qquad
 a(f,g)=\int_{\mathcal Z_d}\int_{\mathcal Y}
 f(y,z)\{\mathcal G_{n,z}g(\cdot,z)\}(y)\,dy\,dR_n(z).
\]
The weak equation in the definition of \(\mathcal G_{n,z}\), its Neumann boundary conditions, and \(H_f(1,z)=0\) imply
\[
 \partial_y\mathcal G_{n,z}f(y,z)=-\frac{H_f(y,z)}{\bar q_n(y,z)}. \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, integrating \(-\partial_y(\bar q_n\partial_y\mathcal G_{n,z}f)=f\) from \(0\) to \(y\) and using the boundary condition at zero gives (1), while the zero integral gives the boundary condition at one.  Thus, by \(c\leq\bar q_n\leq C\), which follows from \(\mathrm{ass{:}conditional\text{-}density\text{-}bounds}\),
\[
 a(f,f)=\int\frac{|H_f(y,z)|^2}{\bar q_n(y,z)}\,dy\,dR_n(z)
 \asymp \|f\|_{L^2(dR_n;H_y^{-1})}^2. \tag{2}
\]
The same weak identity gives
\[
 a(f,g)=\int \bar q_n
 \partial_y\mathcal G_nf\,\partial_y\mathcal G_ng\,dy\,dR_n=a(g,f), \tag{3}
\]
so the inverse is self-adjoint on the zero-outcome-mass subspace.  This is the inverse-elliptic curvature appearing in \(\mathrm{lem{:}cosine\text{-}second\text{-}variation}\).

We record explicitly the wavelet estimate used in (2).  For a finite outcome-wavelet sum \(f=\sum_{j,k}c_{j,k}\psi^y_{j,k}\), compact support, the vanishing constant moment, and boundary regularity strictly greater than one give
\[
 \left\|\int_0^{\,\cdot}f(v)\,dv\right\|_2^2
 \asymp\sum_{j,k}2^{-2j}c_{j,k}^2. \tag{4}
\]
To see the constants directly, rescaling gives \(\|\int_0^{\,\cdot}\psi^y_{j,k}\|_2\asymp2^{-j}\); at each scale the antiderivatives have a uniformly bounded overlap number.  Between distinct scales, subtracting the constant moment and applying one integration by parts gives a geometric factor \(2^{-|j-j'|/2}\) after the factors \(2^{-j}\) and \(2^{-j'}\) are removed.  The Schur row and column sums of that cross-scale Gram matrix are therefore bounded.  Applying the same estimate to its biorthogonal boundary system gives the reverse inequality.  The finitely many boundary functions change only the two constants.  These facts use exactly the compact support and regularity in \(\mathrm{ass{:}wavelet\text{-}regularity}\).

For \(g=x^{\top}\Phi\), apply (2)--(4) at each \(z\).  The covariate-density bounds in \(\mathrm{ass{:}covariate\text{-}density\text{-}bounds}\), or \(r_n\equiv1\) when \(d=0\), and Lebesgue orthonormality of the covariate wavelets yield
\[
 c_H\sum_{j_y,k_y,\gamma}2^{-2j_y}x_{j_y,k_y,\gamma}^2
 \leq x^{\top}Hx
 \leq C_H\sum_{j_y,k_y,\gamma}2^{-2j_y}x_{j_y,k_y,\gamma}^2. \tag{5}
\]
This proves the first displayed assertion of the proposition.

We next pass from the bilinear-form coordinates to raw feature moments.  Since \(R_n\) depends only on \(z\), outcome orthogonality in \(\mathrm{def{:}anisotropic\text{-}projection}\) gives
\[
 G=I_y\otimes G_z,
 \qquad c_GI\preceq G\preceq C_GI, \tag{6}
\]
where (6) follows from \(c\leq r_n\leq C\) for \(d\geq1\), and \(G=I\) for \(d=0\).  In particular, \(G^{-1}\) does not mix outcome indices and commutes with the diagonal matrix \(W=\operatorname{diag}(2^{-2j_y})\).  Since \(B=G^{-1}HG^{-1}\), (5)--(6) imply
\[
 c_BW\preceq B\preceq C_BW. \tag{7}
\]

For \(f=x^{\top}\Phi\), the covariance variational identity is
\[
 x^{\top}\Gamma_{a,J_y,J_z,n}x
 =\inf_{v\in\mathbb R}\int(f-v)^2q_{a,n}r_n\,dy\,dz. \tag{8}
\]
Every retained outcome feature is orthogonal to the constant, so \(\int f(y,z)\,dy=0\) for every \(z\).  Consequently
\[
 \int(f-v)^2\,dy\,dz=\|x\|_2^2+v^2.
\]
Using both density lower bounds in (8) when \(d\geq1\), and only the conditional-density bound when \(d=0\), gives a fixed lower bound times \(\|x\|_2^2\).  Taking \(v=0\) and using the upper bounds gives the corresponding upper bound.  Summing the two arm covariances proves
\[
 c_\Gamma I\preceq\Gamma_{J_y,J_z,n}\preceq C_\Gamma I. \tag{9}
\]

The nonzero eigenvalues of \(K=\Gamma^{1/2}B\Gamma^{1/2}\) equal those of \(B^{1/2}\Gamma B^{1/2}\): these two matrices are respectively \(XX^{\top}\) and \(X^{\top}X\) for \(X=\Gamma^{1/2}B^{1/2}\).  Moreover, if \(A_1\preceq A_2\), then their decreasingly ordered eigenvalues obey \(\lambda_k(A_1)\leq\lambda_k(A_2)\).  Indeed,
\[
 \lambda_k(A)=\min_{\substack{V\subset\mathbb R^D\\\dim V=D-k+1}}
 \max_{0\neq x\in V}\frac{x^{\top}Ax}{x^{\top}x},
\]
as follows directly by expanding \(x\) in an orthonormal eigenbasis, and the displayed quotient is monotone under Loewner order.  Applying this identity first to \(c_\Gamma B\preceq B^{1/2}\Gamma B^{1/2}\preceq C_\Gamma B\) and then to (7) shows, after arranging \(w_1\geq\cdots\geq w_D\) from the diagonal entries of \(W\), that
\[
 \underline c\,w_k\leq\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}\leq\overline C\,w_k,
 \qquad 1\leq k\leq D_{J_y,J_z}. \tag{10}
\]
Taking superlevel sets in (10) gives exactly the two counting inequalities in the statement.  At outcome level \(j_y\) there are \(\asymp2^{j_y}\) outcome indices and \(\asymp2^{dJ_z}\) retained covariate indices.  Hence, throughout the stated nonempty threshold range,
\[
 N_{J_y,J_z,n}(u)
 \asymp 2^{dJ_z}
 \sum_{j_0\leq j\leq\min\{J_y,\,\frac12\log_2(u^{-1})+O(1)\}}2^j
 \asymp\min\{D_{J_y,J_z},2^{dJ_z}u^{-1/2}\}. \tag{11}
\]
Inverting (10)--(11), with the fixed boundary block absorbed into the constants, gives
\[
 \nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}\asymp\min\{1,(2^{dJ_z}/k)^2\}. \tag{12}
\]
It follows that, for \(d\geq1\),
\[
 \|K\|_{\mathrm{op}}\asymp1,
 \qquad
 \|K\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
 \asymp 2^{dJ_z}+2^{4dJ_z}\sum_{k>2^{dJ_z}}k^{-4}
 \asymp2^{dJ_z}, \tag{13}
\]
and division of the operator bound by the square root of the Hilbert--Schmidt bound proves the asserted \(2^{-dJ_z/2}\) ratio.  When \(d=0\), the covariate multiplicity is one and \(\sum_{j\geq j_0}2^j2^{-4j}\) is finite and has a fixed positive first block.  Thus the Hilbert--Schmidt norm is bounded above and away from zero as \(J_y\to\infty\).

It remains to audit both full-minus-projected energy gaps, including the boundary cross term.  Let \(P_y\) be the unweighted outcome orthogonal projector through \(J_y\), applied separately at each \(z\), and let \(P_z^R\) be the \(L^2(dR_n)\)-orthogonal covariate projector through \(J_z\), applied separately at each \(y\).  The tensor Gram formula in \(\mathrm{def{:}anisotropic\text{-}projection}\) gives
\[
 \Pi_{J_y,J_z}=P_yP_z^R=P_z^RP_y. \tag{14}
\]
Define
\[
 p=P_yP_z^R\Delta_n,
 \qquad r_z=P_y(I-P_z^R)\Delta_n,
 \qquad r_y=(I-P_y)\Delta_n. \tag{15}
\]
Then \(P_y\Delta_n=p+r_z\) and \(\Delta_n=p+r_z+r_y\).  Since \(\Delta_n\) has zero outcome integral and both projectors preserve that property, all three terms lie in the domain of the bilinear form \(a\).

We now derive the projector estimates needed below from the primitive assumptions.  At resolution \(J_z\), write the covariate scaling-space Gram matrix as
\[
 (G_z)_{kl}=\langle\varphi^z_{J_z,k},r_n\varphi^z_{J_z,l}\rangle.
\]
Compact support makes this matrix banded in the finite-neighbor lattice metric, while \(\mathrm{ass{:}covariate\text{-}density\text{-}bounds}\) gives \(cI\preceq G_z\preceq CI\).  With \(\alpha_0=2/(c+C)\) and \(R_z^0=I-\alpha_0G_z\),
\[
 \|R_z^0\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\vartheta:=\frac{C-c}{C+c}<1,
 \qquad G_z^{-1}=\alpha_0\sum_{h=0}^{\infty}(R_z^0)^h. \tag{16}
\]
If the lattice distance from \(k\) to \(l\) exceeds the bandwidth times \(h\), then \(((R_z^0)^h)_{kl}=0\).  Therefore (16) implies
\[
 |(G_z^{-1})_{kl}|\leq C_1\vartheta^{c_1\operatorname{dist}(k,l)}. \tag{17}
\]
The number of lattice sites at distance \(h\) is polynomial in \(h\), so (17) has uniformly bounded row and column sums.  Combining those sums with the finite overlap and the scaling \(\|\varphi^z_{J_z,k}\|_\infty\lesssim2^{dJ_z/2}\) proves that \(P_z^R\) is uniformly bounded on \(L^\infty\).  To make the Jackson step explicit, let \(Q_{J_z}f\) be the local boundary-wavelet scaling approximant.  Wavelet regularity strictly greater than \(s_z\) gives
\[
 \|f-Q_{J_z}f\|_\infty+\|f-Q_{J_z}f\|_{L^2(dR_n)}
 \lesssim2^{-s_zJ_z}\|f\|_{\mathcal C^{s_z}}. \tag{18}
\]
Because \(P_z^R Q_{J_z}f=Q_{J_z}f\),
\[
 (I-P_z^R)f=(I-P_z^R)(f-Q_{J_z}f). \tag{19}
\]
The \(L^\infty\) stability just proved, orthogonal contraction in \(L^2(dR_n)\), and (18)--(19) give the first line below.  The inverse inequality on the scaling space,
\(\|v\|_{\mathcal C^{s_z}}\lesssim2^{s_zJ_z}\|v\|_\infty\), applied to \(P_z^R(f-Q_{J_z}f)\), gives the second line.  Thus
\[
 \begin{split}
 \|(I-P_z^R)f\|_{L^2(dR_n)}+\|(I-P_z^R)f\|_\infty
 &\lesssim2^{-s_zJ_z}\|f\|_{\mathcal C^{s_z}},\\
 \|P_z^Rf\|_{\mathcal C^{s_z}}&\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal C^{s_z}}.
 \end{split} \tag{20}
\]
The same estimates are valid for functions valued in \(\mathcal C^{s_y}(\mathcal Y)\), because (17), finite overlap, (18), and the inverse inequality all pass through the Banach norm.  For the unweighted outcome projector, the identical calculation, now with identity Gram matrix, and the regularity margin in \(\mathrm{ass{:}wavelet\text{-}regularity}\) give
\[
 \begin{split}
 \|(I-P_y)f\|_2&\lesssim2^{-s_yJ_y}\|f\|_{\mathcal C^{s_y}},\\
 \|(I-P_y)f\|_{H_y^{-1}}&\lesssim2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}\|f\|_{\mathcal C^{s_y}},\\
 \|(I-P_y)v\|_2&\lesssim2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}\|v\|_{\mathcal C^{s_y+1}}.
 \end{split} \tag{21}
\]
The second line follows by applying the coefficient calculation (4) to the omitted levels; the first and third are the corresponding Jackson sums.  Thus no unweighted-versus-weighted projection is interchanged in (20)--(21).

By \(\mathrm{ass{:}anisotropic\text{-}conditional\text{-}smoothness}\), \(\Delta_n=q_{1,n}-q_{0,n}\) has a uniformly bounded anisotropic H\"older norm.  Equations (20)--(21), orthogonal contraction, and (15) give
\[
 \|r_y\|_2\lesssim2^{-s_yJ_y},
 \qquad \|r_y\|_{L^2(dR_n;H_y^{-1})}\lesssim2^{-(s_y+1)J_y},
 \qquad \|r_z\|_2\lesssim2^{-s_zJ_z}. \tag{22}
\]
More importantly, (17) makes \(P_z^R\) bounded on \(L^\infty(\mathcal Z_d;\mathcal C^{s_y}(\mathcal Y))\).  It commutes with outcome derivatives and outcome difference quotients, while \(P_y\) is uniformly bounded on \(\mathcal C^{s_y}(\mathcal Y)\).  Hence the exact definition in (15) gives
\[
 \sup_z\|r_z(\cdot,z)\|_{\mathcal C^{s_y}}
 \leq \|P_y\|_{\mathcal C^{s_y}\to\mathcal C^{s_y}}
 \|I-P_z^R\|_{L^\infty(\mathcal C^{s_y})\to L^\infty(\mathcal C^{s_y})}
 \sup_z\|\Delta_n(\cdot,z)\|_{\mathcal C^{s_y}}.
\]
The last factor is at most \(2L\) by the conditional-smoothness assumption, so the required uniform outcome regularity is
\[
 \sup_{z\in\mathcal Z_d}\|r_z(\cdot,z)\|_{\mathcal C^{s_y}(\mathcal Y)}
 \leq C_r,
 \qquad \int_0^1r_z(y,z)\,dy=0. \tag{23}
\]
The same argument gives the corresponding bound for \(p\).

For \(f=p\) or \(f=r_z\), equation (1) applies.  Integration maps \(\mathcal C^{s_y}\) into \(\mathcal C^{s_y+1}\); the map \(q\mapsto q^{-1}\) is bounded on the subset \(q\geq c\) of \(\mathcal C^{s_y}\); and multiplication is continuous in that H\"older class.  The conditional smoothness assumption, (23), and the preceding bound for \(p\) therefore give
\[
 \sup_z\|\mathcal G_{n,z}f(\cdot,z)\|_{\mathcal C^{s_y+1}}
 \lesssim1,
 \qquad f\in\{p,r_z\}. \tag{24}
\]
The arbitrary additive constant is fixed by the zero-mean normalization in the definition of \(\mathcal G_{n,z}\), and its magnitude is bounded by the derivative bound, so it does not spoil (24).  Applying the third Jackson estimate in (21) to (24) proves, in particular,
\[
 \|(I-P_y)\mathcal G_nr_z\|_2
 \lesssim2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}. \tag{25}
\]
This is the boundary-residual estimate required for the retained--omitted outcome cross term.  The same estimate holds with \(r_z\) replaced by \(p\).

For the covariate cross term, (20), anisotropic conditional smoothness, positivity of \(\bar q_n\), and formula (1) also show that \(P_y\mathcal G_np\) has uniformly bounded \(\mathcal C^{s_z}\)-norm in \(z\).  Consequently
\[
 \|(I-P_z^R)P_y\mathcal G_np\|_2\lesssim2^{-s_zJ_z}. \tag{26}
\]

We finally use only self-adjointness (3), projection orthogonality, and Cauchy--Schwarz.  Since \(r_y\) is orthogonal to the range of \(P_y\), while \(r_z\) lies in that range,
\[
 \begin{split}
 a(p,r_y)&=\langle(I-P_y)\mathcal G_np,r_y\rangle_2,\\
 a(r_z,r_y)&=\langle(I-P_y)\mathcal G_nr_z,r_y\rangle_2.
 \end{split} \tag{27}
\]
The second equality in (27), together with (22) and (25), explicitly gives
\[
 |a(r_z,r_y)|
 \leq\|(I-P_y)\mathcal G_nr_z\|_2\|r_y\|_2
 \lesssim2^{-(2s_y+1)J_y}. \tag{28}
\]
The first equality gives the same bound for \(a(p,r_y)\).  Likewise, \(p\) lies in the range of \(P_z^R\), \(r_z\) is orthogonal to that range, and \(r_z=P_yr_z\), so
\[
 a(p,r_z)=\langle(I-P_z^R)P_y\mathcal G_np,r_z\rangle_2,
 \qquad |a(p,r_z)|\lesssim2^{-2s_zJ_z}. \tag{29}
\]
Finally, (2) and (22) imply
\[
 a(r_y,r_y)\lesssim2^{-2(s_y+1)J_y},
 \qquad a(r_z,r_z)\lesssim\|r_z\|_2^2\lesssim2^{-2s_zJ_z}. \tag{30}
\]

By the definitions of the two sequential full-minus-projected energy gaps and the exact decomposition (15),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 B^y_{J_y,n}
 &=a(\Delta_n,\Delta_n)-a(P_y\Delta_n,P_y\Delta_n)\\
 &=2a(p,r_y)+2a(r_z,r_y)+a(r_y,r_y),
 \end{aligned} \tag{31}
\]
and
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 B^z_{J_z,n}
 &=a(P_y\Delta_n,P_y\Delta_n)-a(p,p)\\
 &=2a(p,r_z)+a(r_z,r_z).
 \end{aligned} \tag{32}
\]
Equations (28)--(32) prove
\[
 |B^y_{J_y,n}|\lesssim2^{-(2s_y+1)J_y},
 \qquad |B^z_{J_z,n}|\lesssim2^{-2s_zJ_z}. \tag{33}
\]
The exact identities (31)--(32) also show that these quantities compare the true discrepancy energy with sequential projected energies.  They therefore are not biases or centering terms of \(\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}\).  This completes every assertion uniformly over the declared model class.
