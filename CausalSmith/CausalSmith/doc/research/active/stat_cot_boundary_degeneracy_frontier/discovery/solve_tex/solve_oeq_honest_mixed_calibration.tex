Fix \(d\ge 1\) and a triangular sequence of laws in \(\mathcal M_{n,d}(s_y,s_z,\rho)\) satisfying the local score-approximation premises of \(\mathrm{thm{:}mixed\text{-}spectral\text{-}transition}\).  Choose \(C_B\) at least as large as the uniform constant in \(\mathrm{thm{:}uniform\text{-}second\text{-}order\text{-}expansion}\), and define
\[
E_{R,n}=C_B\left[
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}\{2^{-(s_y+1)J_y}+2^{-s_zJ_z}\}
+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{2+a_y}
+\rho^{2a_y/(2+3/s_y+d/s_z)}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^2
\right].
\]
Because \(\rho_n\le\rho\), the uniform remainder conclusion of that theorem supplies a deterministic sequence
\[
r_n^\circ=o\!\left\{\rho n^{-1/2}+A_{J_y,J_z,n}/n\right\}
\quad\text{such that}\quad
\|\mathbb R_{J_y,J_z,n}\|_2\le E_{R,n}+r_n^\circ.
\]
Let
\[
Z_n^\circ=\sum_{e=1}^2\sum_k\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}
\{(\xi_{e,k}+\eta_{k,n})^2-(1+\eta_{k,n}^2)\},
\]
and let \(q_{n,1-\alpha/2}^\circ\) be the lower \((1-\alpha/2)\)-quantile of \(|Z_n^\circ|\).  Write
\[
d_n^\circ=d_{\mathrm{BL}}\!\left(
\mathcal L\!\left[\frac{n(\Theta^{\mathrm{or}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n)}{S_n}\right],
\mathcal L\!\left[\frac{Z_n^\circ}{S_n}\right]
\right).
\]
The established transition theorem gives \(d_n^\circ\to0\).  Hence one may choose a deterministic \(\delta_n\downarrow0\) with \(d_n^\circ/\delta_n\to0\).  Define the infeasible interval
\[
\widetilde{\mathcal I}_n^\circ=
\left[\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-h_n^\circ,
\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}+h_n^\circ\right],
\qquad
h_n^\circ=\frac{q_{n,1-\alpha/2}^\circ+\delta_nS_n}{n}
+\sqrt{\frac{2}{\alpha}}\,(E_{R,n}+r_n^\circ).
\]
To verify coverage, apply the definition of bounded--Lipschitz distance to the function that is one on
\(\{|x|\le q_{n,1-\alpha/2}^\circ/S_n\}\), decreases linearly to zero across a band of width \(\delta_n\), and is zero outside that band.  This gives
\[
P\!\left(
\left|n(\Theta^{\mathrm{or}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n)\right|
\le q_{n,1-\alpha/2}^\circ+\delta_nS_n
\right)
\ge 1-\frac{\alpha}{2}-\frac{2d_n^\circ}{\delta_n}.
\]
Moreover, the elementary inequality
\(\mathbf 1\{|X|>t\}\le X^2/t^2\) and the displayed \(L^2\) remainder bound imply
\[
P\!\left(
|\mathbb R_{J_y,J_z,n}|>\sqrt{2/\alpha}\,(E_{R,n}+r_n^\circ)
\right)\le\frac{\alpha}{2}.
\]
Since the definitions of \(\Theta^{\mathrm{or}}_{J_y,J_z,n}\) and \(\mathbb R_{J_y,J_z,n}\) give the exact identity
\[
\widehat\theta^{\mathrm{an}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n
=\Theta^{\mathrm{or}}_{J_y,J_z,n}-\theta_n
+\mathbb R_{J_y,J_z,n},
\]
the union bound proves
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}P\{\theta_n\in\widetilde{\mathcal I}_n^\circ\}\ge1-\alpha.
\]
For a standard Gaussian coordinate, oddness gives \(E\xi_{e,k}=E\xi_{e,k}^3=0\), while two integrations by parts in its density give \(E\xi_{e,k}^2=1\) and \(E\xi_{e,k}^4=3\), hence \(E(\xi_{e,k}^2-1)^2=2\).  Independence of the coordinates therefore gives
\[
E Z_n^\circ=0,\qquad
E\{(Z_n^\circ)^2\}
=A_{J_y,J_z,n}^2+8\sum_k\nu_{k,J_y,J_z,n}^2\eta_{k,n}^2.
\]
The score-approximation step in \(\mathrm{thm{:}mixed\text{-}spectral\text{-}transition}\) identifies the second summand as \(nV_{L,n}+o(S_n^2)\).  Thus
\[
E\{(Z_n^\circ)^2\}=A_{J_y,J_z,n}^2+nV_{L,n}+o(S_n^2)=O(S_n^2).
\]
Applying \(\mathbf 1\{|Z_n^\circ|>t\}\le (Z_n^\circ)^2/t^2\) shows
\(q_{n,1-\alpha/2}^\circ=O_\alpha(S_n)\).  Because \(V_{L,n}\lesssim\rho_n^2\le\rho^2\), it follows that
\[
h_n^\circ
=O_\alpha\!\left\{
E_{R,n}+\frac{A_{J_y,J_z,n}}n+\frac{\rho}{\sqrt n}
\right\}.
\]
At \((J_y^*,J_z^*)\), \(\mathrm{prop{:}anisotropic\text{-}spectrum\text{-}bias}\) and \(\mathrm{def{:}anisotropic\text{-}tuning}\) give
\[
\frac{A_{J_y^*,J_z^*,n}}n
\asymp\frac{2^{dJ_z^*/2}}n
\asymp n^{-2s_z/(2s_z+d)}
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{d/(2s_z+d)},
\]
and each of the first two projection terms in \(E_{R,n}\) is of this same order.  Thus \(h_n^\circ\) has exactly the requested positive-dimensional order, with no centering by \(B^y_{J_y,n}+B^z_{J_z,n}\).

This result does not yield the requested observable interval.  Indeed, consider the allowed parameters \(d=1\), \(s_y=1/10\), and \(s_z=1\).  Then
\[
\beta=\frac1{11},\qquad a_y=\frac1{10},\qquad
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}=\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/13},
\]
\[
2^{J_z^*}\asymp(n\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n})^{2/3},qquad
2^{J_y^*}\asymp(n\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n})^{20/33},qquad
D_{J_y^*,J_z^*}\asymp(n\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n})^{14/11}.
\]
The uniform conditional-equality law belongs to the class and satisfies the transition theorem's score-approximation premise here, because its discrepancy is zero and
\[
\frac{\sqrt n\{2^{-(s_y+1)J_y^*}+2^{-s_zJ_z^*}\}}
{A_{J_y^*,J_z^*,n}}
\asymp n^{-1/2}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{-1}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Consequently
\[
\frac{D_{J_y^*,J_z^*}\log n}{m}
\asymp n^{25/143}(\log n)^{157/143}\longrightarrow\infty,
\]
so the sufficient covariance condition in \(\mathrm{lem{:}observable\text{-}pilot\text{-}control}\) is unavailable.  Even if the population covariance were supplied, converting the frozen operator-norm Hessian bound to the relative Hilbert--Schmidt bound needed for spectral-quantile replacement gives only
\[
\frac{\sqrt{D_{J_y^*,J_z^*}}\,
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{a_y}}
{2^{dJ_z^*/2}}
\asymp
(n\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n})^{10/33}
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pil},n}^{1/10}
=n^{389/1430}(\log n)^{133/4290}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Thus the available bounds do not imply relative Hilbert--Schmidt consistency of the observed spectrum at the corrected resolutions.  They also provide neither a uniform anti-concentration inequality for the mixed triangular Gaussian quadratic form nor a common-multiplier coupling that estimates its local shifts while preserving linear--quadratic covariance.

There is one observable no-covariate subcase.  Under the uniform \(d=0\) equality law, put
\[
H_0=2\int_0^1\mathbb B(t)^2dt
=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{\pi^2k^2}\xi_{1,k}^2
\]
and let \(q^0_{1-\alpha}\) be its lower \((1-\alpha)\)-quantile.  The series is finite almost surely because its nonnegative partial sums have bounded expectations,
\[
E H_0=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{\pi^2k^2}=\frac13.
\]
Its distribution is continuous: the first summand has an absolutely continuous law and is independent of the remaining series, so their convolution has no atoms.  Therefore the established null convergence and the continuity-set form of weak convergence give
\[
\mathcal I_n^0=
\left[\widehat\theta^0_{J_y^0,n}-\frac{q^0_{1-\alpha}}n,
\widehat\theta^0_{J_y^0,n}+\frac{q^0_{1-\alpha}}n\right],
\qquad
P\{0\in\mathcal I_n^0\}
=P\{n\widehat\theta^0_{J_y^0,n}\le q^0_{1-\alpha}\}
\longrightarrow1-\alpha.
\]
Thus the exact uniform-null submodel has an observable interval of half-width \(q^0_{1-\alpha}/n\).  However, \(\mathrm{thm{:}no\text{-}covariate\text{-}recovery}\) supplies no single observable coupling or anti-concentration bound uniform over general equality laws, local alternatives, and fixed unequal laws.  Hence the frozen results certify the displayed positive-dimensional infeasible interval and the exact no-covariate uniform-null interval, but do not answer the requested observable mixed-calibration question.
