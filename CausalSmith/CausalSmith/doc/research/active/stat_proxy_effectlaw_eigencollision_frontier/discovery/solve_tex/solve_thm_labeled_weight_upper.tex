Fix \(g\in(0,1/4]\) and \(P\in\mathcal M(g)\).  By \(\mathrm{def:gap\text{-}stratum}\), \(P\in\mathcal M\).  Put
\[
 Z_P:=W_1(\widehat\nu_n,\nu_P).
\]
By \(\mathrm{def:quotient\text{-}law}\), \(\mathrm{def:summary\text{-}space\text{-}repair}\), and \(\mathrm{prop:summary\text{-}repair\text{-}total\text{-}borel}\), both arguments of this Wasserstein distance are probability measures supported on \([-L_{\tau},L_{\tau}]\), the second estimator is a total Borel map, and therefore
\[
 0\le Z_P\le 2L_{\tau}.                                                    \tag{1}
\]
Choose the constant \(A\ge2\), depending only on \(k,d_x,d_z,L,\pi_0,\sigma_0\), large enough that the conclusion of \(\mathrm{thm:collision\text{-}uniform\text{-}root\text{-}n}\) holds with \(A\) in place of its displayed constant.  For
\[
 x\ge x_0:=A\sqrt{\frac{\log(4A)}{n}},
 \qquad
 \eta_x:=A\exp\!\left(-\frac{nx^2}{A^2}\right),
\]
we have \(0<\eta_x\le1/4<1/2\) and
\[
 A\sqrt{\frac{\log(A/\eta_x)}{n}}=x.
\]
Applying \(\mathrm{thm:collision\text{-}uniform\text{-}root\text{-}n}\) at level \(\eta_x\), and observing that the event for \(Z_P\) is contained in the event for the maximum in that theorem, gives
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M}Q_P^{(n)}\{Z_P>x\}
 \le A\exp\!\left(-\frac{nx^2}{A^2}\right),
 \qquad x\ge x_0.                                                          \tag{2}
\]
If \(x_0<2L_{\tau}\), the layer-cake identity, Tonelli's theorem for the nonnegative integrand, (1), and (2) yield
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M}E_{Q_P^{(n)}}[Z_P]
 &\le x_0+\int_{x_0}^{\infty}
 A\exp\!\left(-\frac{nx^2}{A^2}\right)\,dx\\
 &=\frac{A\sqrt{\log(4A)}}{\sqrt n}
   +\frac{A^2}{\sqrt n}\int_{\sqrt{\log(4A)}}^\infty e^{-y^2}\,dy
 \le \frac{C_1}{\sqrt n}.                                                  \tag{3}
\end{aligned}
\]
If \(x_0\ge2L_{\tau}\), (1) instead gives \(E[Z_P]\le2L_{\tau}\le x_0\), so (3) remains valid after increasing \(C_1\).  Thus (3) holds uniformly over \(P\in\mathcal M\), with \(C_1\) depending only on the displayed fixed class parameters.

We now convert transport error to ordered-label error.  By \(\mathrm{ass:distinct\text{-}effects}\), which is part of \(\mathrm{def:gap\text{-}stratum}\), write
\[
 \nu_P=\sum_{i=1}^k p_i\delta_{\tau_i},
 \qquad
 \tau_1<\cdots<\tau_k,
 \qquad
 p^{\uparrow}(P)=(p_1,\ldots,p_k).
\]
Because there are exactly \(k\) distinct effects among \(k\) latent classes, each \(p_i\) is one latent-class mass.  The two inequalities in \(\mathrm{ass:latent\text{-}arm\text{-}positivity}\), one for each \(t\in\{0,1\}\), imply
\[
 p_i=P(U=i_P)=\sum_{t=0}^1P(U=i_P,T=t)\ge2\pi_0=:m_0,                       \tag{4}
\]
where \(i_P\) is the latent class whose effect is \(\tau_i\).  Put \(\delta:=\delta(P)\).  The gap-window condition \(\mathrm{ass:gap\text{-}window}\) gives
\[
 0<\frac g2\le\delta\le2g.                                                  \tag{5}
\]
Consider the event
\[
 \mathcal A:=\left\{Z_P<\frac{m_0\delta}{4}\right\}.                       \tag{6}
\]
For every \(i\), on \(\mathcal A\) the support of \(\widehat\nu_n\) intersects
\[
 G_i:=\{x:|x-\tau_i|<\delta/4\}.                                             \tag{7}
\]
Indeed, if it did not, then under every coupling of \(\nu_P\) and \(\widehat\nu_n\), all of the mass \(p_i\ge m_0\) at \(\tau_i\) would travel at least \(\delta/4\).  The definition of \(W_1\) would then give \(Z_P\ge m_0\delta/4\), contradicting (6).  The sets \(G_1,\ldots,G_k\) are pairwise disjoint by the definition of \(\delta(P)\).  Since \(\widehat\nu_n\in\mathcal P_k([-L_{\tau},L_{\tau}])\) has at most \(k\) distinct support points, (7) forces it to have exactly one point \(\widehat\tau_i\) in each \(G_i\) and no other support point.  Hence, on \(\mathcal A\),
\[
 \widehat\nu_n=\sum_{i=1}^k\widehat p_i\delta_{\widehat\tau_i},
 \qquad
 \widehat\tau_1<\cdots<\widehat\tau_k,
 \qquad
 \widehat p_n^{\uparrow}=(\widehat p_1,\ldots,\widehat p_k),                \tag{8}
\]
where the last equality is exactly \(\mathrm{def:labeled\text{-}weight\text{-}estimator}\).

Let \((\gamma_{ij})_{1\le i,j\le k}\) be any coupling matrix between the two discrete laws in (8).  Its row sums are \(p_i\) and its column sums are \(\widehat p_j\).  Consequently,
\[
 \sum_{i\ne j}\gamma_{ij}
 =1-\sum_i\gamma_{ii}
 \ge1-\sum_i\min\{p_i,\widehat p_i\}
 =\frac12\sum_i|p_i-\widehat p_i|.                                         \tag{9}
\]
For \(i\ne j\), (7) and the definition of \(\delta\) give
\[
 |\tau_i-\widehat\tau_j|
 \ge |\tau_i-\tau_j|-|\tau_j-\widehat\tau_j|
 >\delta-\frac\delta4
 \ge\frac\delta2.                                                          \tag{10}
\]
Thus every such coupling has transport cost at least \(\delta\lVert p-\widehat p\rVert_1/4\), by (9)--(10).  Taking the infimum over couplings in the definition of \(W_1\) proves that, on \(\mathcal A\),
\[
 \lVert\widehat p_n^{\uparrow}-p^{\uparrow}(P)\rVert_1
 \le\frac{4Z_P}{\delta}.                                                    \tag{11}
\]
On \(\mathcal A^c\), the diameter of the simplex under \(\ell_1\) loss is two, while (6) implies
\[
 \mathbf1_{\mathcal A^c}\le\frac{4Z_P}{m_0\delta}.                         \tag{12}
\]
Equations (3), (11), and (12), together with nonnegativity, now give
\[
\begin{aligned}
 E_{Q_P^{(n)}}\!\left[
 \lVert\widehat p_n^{\uparrow}-p^{\uparrow}(P)\rVert_1\right]
 &\le \frac4\delta E[Z_P]
      +2Q_P^{(n)}(\mathcal A^c)\\
 &\le\left(4+\frac8{m_0}\right)\frac{C_1}{\sqrt n\,\delta}
 \le\frac{C_2}{\sqrt n\,g},                                               \tag{13}
\end{aligned}
\]
where the last inequality uses (5), and \(C_2\) depends only on \(k,d_x,d_z,L,\pi_0,\sigma_0\).  Independently, the simplex diameter always gives the left-hand side of (13) at most two.  Therefore, with \(C:=\max\{2,C_2\}\),
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal M(g)}E_{Q_P^{(n)}}\!\left[
 \lVert\widehat p_n^{\uparrow}-p^{\uparrow}(P)\rVert_1\right]
 \le C\min\left\{1,\frac1{\sqrt n\,g}\right\}.
\]
All constants are independent of \(g\in(0,1/4]\).  The only inverse-gap step is the transport-to-label inequality (11); the quotient-law risk (3) remains gap-free.

For \(g\in(0,1/4]\), define
\[
 r_{n,g}:=\min\left\{1,\frac1{\sqrt n\,g}\right\},
 \qquad
 R_{n,g}:=\inf_{\widetilde p_n}\sup_{P\in\mathcal M(g)}
 E_{Q_P^{(n)}}\!\left[
 \lVert\widetilde p_n-p^{\uparrow}(P)\rVert_1\right].                       \tag{14}
\]
Let \(R_{n,g}^{\mathrm{loc}}\) denote the same minimax expression with \(\mathcal M(g)\) replaced by \(\mathcal L_{n,g}^{p}\), and with the same decision class of measurable maps into \(\Delta^{k-1}\).  By \(\mathrm{def:local\text{-}weight\text{-}experiment}\),
\[
 \mathcal L_{n,g}^{p}\subseteq\mathcal M(g).                                \tag{15}
\]
For every fixed estimator, monotonicity of the supremum under (15), followed by taking the infimum over that common decision class, gives \(R_{n,g}\ge R_{n,g}^{\mathrm{loc}}\).  The labeled-weight part of \(\mathrm{thm:matching\text{-}local\text{-}lower\text{-}bounds}\) therefore yields a constant \(c_->0\), independent of \(n\) and \(g\), such that
\[
 R_{n,g}\ge R_{n,g}^{\mathrm{loc}}\ge c_-r_{n,g}.                           \tag{16}
\]

By \(\mathrm{def:labeled\text{-}weight\text{-}estimator}\), \(\widehat p_n^{\uparrow}\) is a total Borel map into \(\Delta^{k-1}\), so it is an admissible choice in the infimum in (14).  Applying \(\mathrm{thm:labeled\text{-}weight\text{-}upper}\) to that choice gives a finite constant \(C_+\), also independent of \(n\) and \(g\), for which
\[
 R_{n,g}
 \le\sup_{P\in\mathcal M(g)}E_{Q_P^{(n)}}\!\left[
 \lVert\widehat p_n^{\uparrow}-p^{\uparrow}(P)\rVert_1\right]
 \le C_+r_{n,g}.                                                             \tag{17}
\]
Taking \(c:=c_-/2\) and \(C:=C_++c_-+1\) ensures \(0<c<C<\infty\).  Equations (16)--(17) then imply
\[
 c\min\left\{1,\frac1{\sqrt n\,g}\right\}
 \le\inf_{\widetilde p_n}\sup_{P\in\mathcal M(g)}
 E_{Q_P^{(n)}}\!\left[
 \lVert\widetilde p_n-p^{\uparrow}(P)\rVert_1\right]
 \le C\min\left\{1,\frac1{\sqrt n\,g}\right\},
\]
uniformly for \(g\in(0,1/4]\), as claimed.
