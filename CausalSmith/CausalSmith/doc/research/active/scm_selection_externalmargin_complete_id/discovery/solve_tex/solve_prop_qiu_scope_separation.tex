Let \(C_Q\), \(D\), and \(R\) denote, respectively, the covariate block, binary treatment, and outcome in \(\mathrm{lem{:}qiu\text{-}external\text{-}ate\text{-}characterization\).  For the comparison of input signatures, relabel
\[
T_0=C_Q,\qquad X=\{D\},\qquad Y=\{R\},\qquad
V=C_Q\mathbin{\dot\cup}\{D,R\}.
\tag{1}
\]
By \(\mathrm{def{:}observed\text{-}data\text{-}map}\), the present observation map then has the two entries
\[
\operatorname{Obs}_{T_0}(M)
=\bigl(P_M(V\mid S=1),P_M(T_0)\bigr)
=\bigl(P_M(C_Q,D,R\mid S=1),P_M(C_Q)\bigr).
\tag{2}
\]
The cited Qiu characterization states that its inputs are a selected observational law on covariates, treatment, and outcome, together with an external unbiased covariate margin.  Thus (2) proves equality of the data-slot pattern after relabeling.  This comparison of signatures asserts no equality, inclusion, or nonempty intersection of the underlying model classes.

We next prove the asserted estimand map.  Suppose that \(D\) is binary and that \(R\) has a finite numeric state space.  By the definitions of \(\theta_{\mathrm{ATE}}\) and \(\psi_{x,y}\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\theta_{\mathrm{ATE}}(M)
&=E_M[R\mid\operatorname{do}(D=1)]
  -E_M[R\mid\operatorname{do}(D=0)]\\
&=\sum_{r\in\mathcal X_R}r\,
  P_M(R=r\mid\operatorname{do}(D=1))
  -\sum_{r\in\mathcal X_R}r\,
  P_M(R=r\mid\operatorname{do}(D=0))\\
&=\sum_{r\in\mathcal X_R}r\,\psi_{1,r}(M)
  -\sum_{r\in\mathcal X_R}r\,\psi_{0,r}(M).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]
All sums in (3) are finite by \(\mathrm{def{:}model\text{-}class}\), through its finite-state membership condition, so no integrability or limiting premise is needed.  Equation (3), together with the coordinate indexing in \(\mathrm{def{:}full\text{-}kernel\text{-}collection}\), is an explicit finite linear map from \(\boldsymbol\psi(M)\) to \(\theta_{\mathrm{ATE}}(M)\).

The converse estimand map fails even within a strictly positive finite Markovian DAG subclass.  Consider the DAG \(D\to R\) with \(S\) isolated, set \(X=\{D\}\), \(Y=\{R\}\), and take \(T_0=\varnothing\).  Let \(U_D\) and \(U_S\) be independent Bernoulli\((1/2)\) variables, and let \(U_R\), independent of both, be uniform on \(\{1,\ldots,12\}\).  Define two SCMs by
\[
D=U_D,\qquad S=U_S,
\qquad
R_1=\mathbf 1\{U_R\le 3+6D\},
\qquad
R_2=\mathbf 1\{U_R\le 4+6D\}.
\tag{4}
\]
Each model is recursive and finite.  Mutual independence of its exogenous variables makes it Markovian, hence semi-Markovian, and each structural function uses only a displayed parent in the stated DAG.  Moreover, \(P_{M_j}(S=1)=1/2>0\).  Since \(S\) is independent of \((D,R_j)\), conditioning on \(S=1\) leaves the substantive law unchanged, and direct evaluation of (4) gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
& (D,R_j)=(0,1)&(0,0)&(1,1)&(1,0)\\ \hline
j=1&1/8&3/8&3/8&1/8\\
j=2&1/6&1/3&5/12&1/12.
\end{array}
\tag{5}
\]
Thus every selected substantive cell is positive, with common lower bound \(1/12\); because \(S\) itself has both values with probability \(1/2\), every joint cell of \((D,R_j,S)\) is also positive.  In particular, this witness uses no boundary distribution.

Under \(\operatorname{do}(D=d)\), the structural equation for \(D\) is replaced by the constant \(d\), while the equation for \(R_j\) in (4) is unchanged.  Consequently,
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc|c}
&P_{M_j}(R=1\mid\operatorname{do}(D=0))
&P_{M_j}(R=1\mid\operatorname{do}(D=1))
&\theta_{\mathrm{ATE}}(M_j)\\ \hline
j=1&3/12&9/12&6/12\\
j=2&4/12&10/12&6/12.
\end{array}
\tag{6}
\]
Both scalar effects equal \(1/2\), whereas
\[
\psi_{0,1}(M_1)=\frac14\ne\frac13=\psi_{0,1}(M_2).
\tag{7}
\]
Equations (6)--(7) prove that the linear map in (3) is not injective even on the claimed strictly positive finite Markovian DAG subclass.  Hence the scalar ATE alone does not determine the complete interventional table.  This pair is used only to disprove the reverse estimand map; no equality of its two observed-data laws is asserted or needed.

It remains to verify the two scope separations.  By \(\mathrm{lem{:}qiu\text{-}external\text{-}ate\text{-}characterization}\), Qiu et al.'s theorem assumes unconfoundedness and treatment overlap and imposes its pairwise distinguishability condition on specified classes of propensity scores, covariate--potential-outcome laws, and selection laws.  Its literal conclusion identifies the single scalar \(\theta_{\mathrm{ATE}}\), not every cell \(\psi_{x,y}\) for an arbitrary semi-Markovian \(\mathcal G\).  Equation (7) additionally proves that the scalar conclusion alone cannot be inverted into the complete-table conclusion.  Therefore the cited Qiu theorem does not subsume the present arbitrary-cell graphical decision problem.

Conversely, \(\mathrm{def{:}model\text{-}class}\) restricts the present class to finite-state recursive semi-Markovian SCMs and imposes exactly its listed graph-compatibility, positive-selection, and selected-cell positivity conditions.  The Qiu characterization states that its published setup uses continuous covariate--outcome laws with densities for exposition, includes continuous probability-family examples, and indexes its criterion by separately specified probability classes.  Those continuous examples are outside the present finite-state class.  Moreover, neither a graph \(\mathcal G\) nor the membership conditions of \(\mathrm{def{:}model\text{-}class}\) select those probability classes or imply Qiu et al.'s pairwise distinguishability condition.  Thus the present graph class does not subsume the cited setup.  Together, (2), (3), (7), and these two class separations establish exactly the claimed input-shape and estimand-map analogy, the failure of the reverse estimand map, and non-subsumption in both directions, without asserting a literal model-class intersection.
