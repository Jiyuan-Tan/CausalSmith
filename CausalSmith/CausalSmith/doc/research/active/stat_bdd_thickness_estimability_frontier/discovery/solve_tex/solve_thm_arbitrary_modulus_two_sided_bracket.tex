The distance map \(z\mapsto d(z,x_j)\) is one-Lipschitz.  Hence
\(\psi_{j,h}(z)=(1-d(z,x_j)/h)_+\) is \(h^{-1}\)-Lipschitz, and each
perturbed side regression in \(\mathrm{def:fano\text{-}tent\text{-}family}\)
is \(L/4\)-Lipschitz.  Its absolute trace is at most \(Lh/4\le L\), since
\(h\le h_0<1\).  The perturbation changes neither the score law nor any
local-mass or packing property.  The conditional sub-Gaussian-error,
sharp-assignment, and consistency properties follow directly from the
construction.  Therefore
\(\mathrm{lem:circular\text{-}witness\text{-}membership}\) and the class
definitions put every pervasive perturbation in
\(\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}\) and both isolated perturbations in
\(\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}\).

For \(h\le h_0/6<1/6\), Euclidean chord length and arclength on \(S^1\)
are comparable by fixed numerical constants.  Maximality of the
\(3h\)-separated set implies that arcs of chord radius \(3h\) cover
\(S^1\), while arcs of chord radius \(3h/2\) about its points are
disjoint.  Comparing their total arclength with \(2\pi\) gives
\[
 c h^{-1}\le |\Xi_h^{\mathrm{perv}}|\le C h^{-1}. \tag{7}
\]

Put \(a=Lh/4\).  Conditional on \(X=z\), the relevant Gaussian
coordinates under alternatives \(j\) and \(k\) have common variance
\(\sigma^2\), and their mean difference has absolute value
\(a|\psi_{j,h}(z)-\psi_{k,h}(z)|\).  Direct integration of the normal
likelihood ratio gives
\[
 \operatorname{KL}\{\mathcal N(\mu,\sigma^2),\mathcal N(\nu,\sigma^2)\}
 =\frac{(\mu-\nu)^2}{2\sigma^2}. \tag{8}
\]
The integrand vanishes outside \(B(x_j,h)\cup B(x_k,h)\).  The local-mass
calculations in
\(\mathrm{lem:circular\text{-}witness\text{-}membership}\) give
\[
 \int_{B(x_j,h)}f_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}(z)\,dz\le Ch^{\kappa},
 \qquad
 \int_{B(x_{\star},h)}f_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}(z)\,dz\le Ch^{\kappa}.
\]
Since \(0\le\psi_{j,h}\le1\), conditioning on \(X\) in (8), and summing
the two potential-outcome coordinates when the superpopulation
experiment is used, gives in either family
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(\mathbb P_{\kappa,j,h},\mathbb P_{\kappa,k,h})
 \le \frac{Ca^2h^{\kappa}}{\sigma^2}
 \le \frac{CL^2h^{\kappa+2}}{\sigma^2}. \tag{9}
\]
For the displayed product measures, likelihood ratios factor, so direct
integration of the log likelihood ratio makes relative entropy additive.
Thus
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(\mathbb P_{\kappa,j,h}^{\otimes n},
 \mathbb P_{\kappa,k,h}^{\otimes n})
 \le \frac{CnL^2h^{\kappa+2}}{\sigma^2}. \tag{10}
\]
At an active center, \(\psi_{j,h}(x_j)=1\).  The trace formulas in
\(\mathrm{def:fano\text{-}tent\text{-}family}\) yield
\[
 \theta_{1,P}(x_j)=\frac{Lh}{4},\qquad
 \theta_{0,P}(x_j)=-\frac{Lh}{4},\qquad
 \tau_P(x_j)=\frac{Lh}{2}.
\]
Together with (7) and (10), these are all the asserted properties.

For a law \(P\), define the finite one-sided score measure
\[
 \mu_{t,P}(E)=P_X(E\cap\mathcal A_t).
\]
If \(P\) belongs to either displayed class, \(x\in\mathcal B\), and
\(r>0\), put \(s=\min\{h_0,r/2\}\).  The polynomial lower-mass property
inherited from the corresponding class definition gives
\[
 \mu_{t,P}\{z:d(z,x)<r\}
 \ge q_{t,P}(x,s)
 \ge c_ms^{\kappa}>0. \tag{23}
\]
Thus every boundary point belongs to \(\mathcal X_{t,P}\).  The
model-class Lipschitz property makes the distinguished representative
continuous there, so the trace definition gives
\[
 \theta_{t,P}(x)=g_{t,P}(x),
 \qquad t\in\{0,1\},\quad x\in\mathcal B. \tag{24}
\]
Because \(\mathcal A_0=\mathbb R^2\setminus\mathcal A_1\), sharp
assignment makes \(X\in\mathcal A_t\) equivalent to \(T=t\).  Thus all
side averages below have conditional observed-outcome mean
\(g_{t,P}(X)\).  Consistency supplies the causal interpretation of the
resulting trace contrast; the risk calculations concern its identified
observed-law functional.

We first prove the lower bounds by experiments contained in the stated
classes.  Begin with any member of the nonempty class under
consideration, retain its score law and geometry, take independent
\(Z_0,Z_1\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\), and set \(Y(s)=\sigma Z_s\) for
\(s\in\{0,1\}\).  Call the resulting law \(Q_0\).  Its side regressions
and traces vanish.  All design conditions are unchanged, its traces obey
the envelope, and its observed residual is conditionally
\(\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)\), so \(Q_0\) remains in the same class.

For the isolated class let
\[
 h=a_0n^{-1/(\kappa+2)},
\]
where \(a_0>0\) is fixed below.  For all sufficiently large \(n\),
\(h\le h_0/3\).  The isolated thin-site property and the definitions of
\(\mathsf J_P\) and \(a_P\) provide \(x_h\in\mathcal B\) and
\(t_h\in\{0,1\}\) such that
\[
 q_{t_h,P}(x_h,h)=a_P(x_h,h)\le C_mh^{\kappa}. \tag{25}
\]
Set
\[
 \psi_h(z)=\left(1-\frac{3d(z,x_h)}h\right)_+,
 \qquad \eta_h=\frac{Lh}{12}.
\]
If \(t_h=1\), add \(\eta_h\psi_h(X)\) to \(Y(1)\); if \(t_h=0\),
subtract it from \(Y(0)\); leave the other potential outcome unchanged.
The function \(\eta_h\psi_h\) is \(L/4\)-Lipschitz, its absolute value
is at most \(Lh_0/12<L\), and the score law and conditional Gaussian
error law are unchanged.  Hence the perturbed law \(Q_1\) belongs to
\(\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}\).  By (24),
\[
 \lVert\tau_{Q_1}-\tau_{Q_0}\rVert_{\infty}\ge\eta_h. \tag{26}
\]
The two observed laws have the same score marginal.  Conditional on an
assigned-side score \(z\), their Gaussian means differ by
\(\eta_h\psi_h(z)\).  Direct evaluation of the Gaussian likelihood ratio
and (25) give
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_1,Q_0)
 &=\frac{\eta_h^2}{2\sigma^2}
   \int\psi_h(z)^2\,d\mu_{t_h,P}(z)\\
 &\le\frac{C_mL^2}{288\sigma^2}h^{\kappa+2}.
 \end{aligned} \tag{27}
\]
Relative entropy is additive under independent sampling, so
\[
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_1^{\otimes n},Q_0^{\otimes n})
 \le C a_0^{\kappa+2}.
\]
Choose \(a_0\) so the last bound is at most \(1/8\).  The two-law clause
of \(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}information\text{-}testing}\), with the
supremum metric and \(2\delta=\eta_h\), yields
\[
 R_n(\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}})
 \ge c n^{-1/(\kappa+2)}
 =c r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}. \tag{28}
\]

For the pervasive class let
\[
 h=a_0\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(\kappa+2)}.
\]
For large \(n\), \(h\le h_0/3\).  The pervasive thin-site property
supplies an \(h\)-separated set \(x_1,\ldots,x_M\) such that
\[
 M\ge c_Jh^{-1},
 \qquad a_P(x_j,h)\le C_mh^{\kappa}\quad(1\le j\le M). \tag{29}
\]
Its finiteness follows from global boundary entropy.  At each \(x_j\),
choose a side attaining the minimum in \(a_P(x_j,h)\), and perturb that
side by a radius-\(h/3\), height-\(Lh/12\) tent, upward on side one and
downward on side zero.  The membership argument above gives laws
\(Q_1,\ldots,Q_M\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}\).  At \(x_j\),
the target under \(Q_j\) differs from the zero target by \(Lh/12\), while
every other tent vanishes there because the centers are
\(h\)-separated.  Thus the target curves are pairwise separated by at
least \(Lh/12\) in supremum norm.  The calculation in (27) gives
\[
 \max_{1\le j\le M}
 \operatorname{KL}(Q_j^{\otimes n},Q_0^{\otimes n})
 \le Cnh^{\kappa+2}
 =Ca_0^{\kappa+2}\log n. \tag{30}
\]
Equation (29) implies \(\log M\ge c_{\overline\kappa}\log n\)
eventually, uniformly over \(2<\kappa\le\overline\kappa\).  Choose
\(a_0\) smaller if necessary so that (30) is at most \(a\log M\), with
\(a<1/8\) as in
\(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}information\text{-}testing}\).  Its
multiple-law clause gives
\[
 R_n(\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}})
 \ge c\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(\kappa+2)}
 =c r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}. \tag{31}
\]
The exact-Gaussian circular families in
\(\mathrm{lem:fano\text{-}tent\text{-}validity}\) provide explicit
experiments with the same information budgets.

We next give data-driven upper-bound witnesses for the infimum defining
\(R_n\).  Let \(s_n\) denote either \(r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}\) or
\(r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}\).  By
\(\mathrm{def:dyadic\text{-}grid}\), for all sufficiently large \(n\)
there is \(b=b_n\in\mathcal H_n\) such that
\[
 s_n\le b<2s_n,
 \qquad b/2\in\mathcal H_n,
 \qquad b\le h_0/2. \tag{32}
\]
Choose a deterministic maximal \(b/2\)-separated set
\(z_1,\ldots,z_M\) in \(\mathcal B\).  It is a finite \(b/2\)-net, and
global boundary entropy gives \(M\le2C_{\mathcal B}b^{-1}\).  Let
\(j(x)\) be the least index satisfying \(d(x,z_{j(x)})\le b/2\), and
define
\[
 K_{t,j}=\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf1\{X_i\in\mathcal A_t,
 d(X_i,z_j)\le b\},
\]
\[
 \overline g_{t,j}
 =\frac{\sum_{i=1}^nY_i\mathbf1\{X_i\in\mathcal A_t,
 d(X_i,z_j)\le b\}}{K_{t,j}\vee1},
\]
and
\[
 \widetilde\tau_{n,b}(x)
 =\Pi_{[-2L,2L]}\{\overline g_{1,j(x)}-
 \overline g_{0,j(x)}\}. \tag{33}
\]
This is one estimator measurable in the sample and known geometry.  Since
the trace envelope gives \(\tau_P(x)\in[-2L,2L]\), projection cannot
increase pointwise error.  On \(K_{t,j(x)}>0\), every included score is
within \(3b/2\) of \(x\), so (24) and Lipschitz smoothness imply
\[
 \left|\mathbb E_P(\overline g_{t,j(x)}\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n)
       -\theta_{t,P}(x)\right|
 \le\frac{3Lb}{2}. \tag{34}
\]

We use the following elementary count bound.  If
\(N\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,q)\), Markov's inequality applied to
\(2^{-N}\), followed by \(1-y\le e^{-y}\), gives
\[
 \Pr(N\le nq/2)
 \le2^{nq/2}(1-q/2)^n
 \le e^{-nq/8}. \tag{35}
\]

For the pervasive rate, take \(s_n=r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}\).  The
polynomial lower-mass property, (35), and a union bound over two sides and
the net give an event \(H_b\) on which every
\(K_{t,j}\ge nc_mb^{\kappa}/2\) and
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}}P(H_b^c)
 \le Cb^{-1}e^{-cnb^{\kappa}}. \tag{36}
\]
For fixed scores, every nonempty set defining \(\overline g_{t,j}\) is a
disk pattern in
\(\mathrm{lem:disk\text{-}pattern\text{-}sub\text{-}gaussian\text{-}control}\).
Since
\[
 |\mathcal H_n|\le C\log(en),
 \qquad \sum_{r=0}^3\binom nr\le C(1+n^3),
\]
that lemma bounds the conditional expected maximum normalized residual
sum by \(C\sqrt{\log(en)}\).  Hence, on \(H_b\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P\left[
 \max_{t,j}\left|
 \overline g_{t,j}-\mathbb E_P(\overline g_{t,j}\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n)
 \right|\ \middle|\ X_1,\ldots,X_n\right]
 \le C\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(en)}{nc_mb^{\kappa}}}. \tag{37}
\]
Because \(nb^{\kappa+2}\asymp\log n\), the right side is at most
\(C_{\overline\kappa}b\).  On \(H_b^c\), clipping bounds the loss by
\(4L\).  Equations (34), (36), and (37), with the exponentially small
remainder absorbed into \(b\), give
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}}
 \mathbb E_P\lVert\widetilde\tau_{n,b}-\tau_P\rVert_{\infty}
 \le C_{\overline\kappa}b
 \le C_{\overline\kappa}r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}. \tag{38}
\]

For the isolated rate take \(s_n=r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}\), so
\(nb^{\kappa}\asymp b^{-2}\).  Fix a side, choose \(D\ge C_m\), set
\(u_\ell=\min\{1,D2^\ell b^{\kappa}\}\), and put a net center in group
zero when \(q_{t,P}(z_j,b)\le u_0\), and in group \(\ell\ge1\) when
\[
 u_{\ell-1}<q_{t,P}(z_j,b)\le u_\ell.
\]
Only the first index with \(u_\ell=1\) can be terminal.  Every center in
group \(\ell\) satisfies
\[
 a_P(z_j,b/2)\le q_{t,P}(z_j,b/2)
 \le q_{t,P}(z_j,b)\le u_\ell.
\]
The centers are \(b/2\)-separated.  Applying the isolated upper profile
at radius \(b/2\), including the terminal group, gives
\[
 M_\ell\le C_{\kappa}
 \{1+2^{\ell/(\kappa-2)}\}. \tag{39}
\]
In group zero, polynomial lower mass makes the mean count at least
\(c_mnb^{\kappa}\ge cb^{-2}\); in every nonempty group \(\ell\ge1\), it
is at least \(D2^{\ell-1}nb^{\kappa}\ge c2^\ell b^{-2}\).  Equations
(35) and (39), summed over groups and both sides, give an event \(H_b\)
on which every group-\(\ell\) count is at least \(c2^\ell b^{-2}\) and
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}}P(H_b^c)
 &\le C_{\kappa}\sum_{\ell\ge0}
 \{1+2^{\ell/(\kappa-2)}\}e^{-c2^\ell/b^2}\\
 &\le C_{\kappa}e^{-c_{\kappa}/b^2}.
 \end{aligned} \tag{40}
\]
The last bound follows because, for sufficiently small \(b\), the ratio
of consecutive summands is uniformly smaller than one.

Conditionally on the scores, independent sampling and
\(\mathrm{ass:gaussian\text{-}errors}\) make each nonempty residual
average centered sub-Gaussian with proxy \(\sigma^2/K_{t,j}\).  On
\(H_b\), the two-sided sub-Gaussian tail bound and a union bound within
groups yield, for \(z>0\),
\[
 \Pr_P\left\{
 \max_{t,j}\left|\overline g_{t,j}-
 \mathbb E_P(\overline g_{t,j}\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n)\right|>zb
 \ \middle|\ X_1,\ldots,X_n\right\}
 \le C_{\kappa}\sum_{\ell\ge0}
 \{1+2^{\ell/(\kappa-2)}\}e^{-cz^22^\ell}. \tag{41}
\]
For all sufficiently large fixed \(z\), the series is bounded by
\(C_{\kappa}e^{-c_{\kappa}z^2}\).  Integrating this
tail and using the trivial bound one below that threshold makes the
conditional expected stochastic maximum at most
\(C_{\kappa}b\).  Equations (34), (40), and the \(4L\)
clipped-loss bound on \(H_b^c\) now imply
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}}
 \mathbb E_P\lVert\widetilde\tau_{n,b}-\tau_P\rVert_{\infty}
 \le C_{\kappa}b
 \le C_{\kappa}r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}. \tag{42}
\]

The estimator (33) is an admissible witness for the common infimum in
\(\mathrm{def:expected\text{-}sup\text{-}risk}\).  Thus (38) and (42)
prove the upper bounds, while (28) and (31) prove the matching lower
bounds.  The circular nonemptiness specifications are supplied by
\(\mathrm{lem:circular\text{-}witness\text{-}membership}\).  Finally,
\[
 \frac{r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}}{r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}}
 =(\log n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
The two classes have the same scalar lower modulus \(c_mh^{\kappa}\), so
that modulus alone cannot determine the expected-sup frontier;
thin-site multiplicity separates the two risks.

The partition \(\mathcal A_0=\mathbb R^2\setminus\mathcal A_1\) and sharp
assignment imply \(X_i\in\mathcal A_t\) if and only if \(T_i=t\).
Inspection of \(\mathrm{def:empirical\text{-}counts}\),
\(\mathrm{def:local\text{-}constant\text{-}side\text{-}fit}\),
\(\mathrm{def:admissible\text{-}bandwidths}\),
\(\mathrm{def:count\text{-}adaptive\text{-}bandwidth}\), and
\(\mathrm{def:mass\text{-}adaptive\text{-}estimator}\) shows that
\(\widehat\tau_n\) is measurable in \(\mathcal D_n\), the known
geometry, \(L\), \(\sigma\), and \(n\), and uses neither \(\kappa\) nor
the score density.

Fix \(2<\kappa\le\overline\kappa\), and put
\[
 r=\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(\kappa+2)}.
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\), the dyadic grid contains \(h_*\) with
\[
 r\le h_*<2r. \tag{43}
\]
Take a maximal \(h_*/2\)-separated boundary net.  It has at most
\(2C_{\mathcal B}h_*^{-1}\) points by global boundary entropy.  If a net
point \(z_j\) is within \(h_*/2\) of \(x\), then
\(B(z_j,h_*/2)\subseteq B(x,h_*)\).  Polynomial lower mass gives
\[
 \mathbb E_PN_{t,n}(z_j,h_*/2)
 \ge nc_m(h_*/2)^{\kappa}. \tag{44}
\]
For \(N\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,q)\), Markov's inequality applied to
\(2^{-N}\) gives
\[
 \Pr(N\le nq/2)\le2^{nq/2}(1-q/2)^n\le e^{-nq/8}.
\]
Applying this inequality to all center-side counts and using ball
inclusion yields an event \(G_n\) on which, simultaneously in
\(x\in\mathcal B\) and \(t\in\{0,1\}\),
\[
 N_{t,n}(x,h_*)\ge c nc_mh_*^{\kappa},
\]
and
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}}P(G_n^c)
 \le Ch_*^{-1}e^{-cnc_mh_*^{\kappa}}. \tag{45}
\]
Uniformly over \(2<\kappa\le\overline\kappa\),
\[
 \frac{nh_*^{\kappa}}{\log(e+n/h_*)}
 \ge c\left(\frac n{\log n}\right)^{2/(\overline\kappa+2)}
 \longrightarrow\infty. \tag{46}
\]
Thus \(h_*\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_{t,n}(x)\) everywhere on \(G_n\), for
all sufficiently large \(n\).  The minimizing property of the selected
bandwidth, (43), and \(nr^{\kappa+2}=\log n\) imply
\[
 L\widehat h_{t,n}(x)+
 \sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(e+n/\widehat h_{t,n}(x))}
 {N_{t,n}(x,\widehat h_{t,n}(x))}}
 \le Cr. \tag{47}
\]

Condition on the scores.  If \(x\in\mathcal B\) and
\(s=\min\{h_0,r_0/2\}\) for any \(r_0>0\), the one-sided restricted
score measure satisfies
\[
 P_X\{X\in\mathcal A_t:d(X,x)<r_0\}
 \ge q_{t,P}(x,s)\ge c_ms^\kappa>0.
\]
Thus every boundary point belongs to both side supports.  Lipschitz
continuity and the trace definition give
\(\theta_{t,P}(x)=g_{t,P}(x)\).  If the selected count \(N\) is nonzero,
Lipschitz smoothness gives
\[
 \left|N^{-1}\sum_{i\in S_{t,x,\widehat h}}g_{t,P}(X_i)
 -\theta_{t,P}(x)\right|
 \le L\widehat h_{t,n}(x). \tag{48}
\]
Let
\[
 Z_n=\max_{S\in\mathscr S_n}
 \frac{|\sum_{i\in S}\{Y_i-g_{T_i,P}(X_i)\}|}
 {\sigma\sqrt{|S|}}.
\]
By
\(\mathrm{lem:disk\text{-}pattern\text{-}sub\text{-}gaussian\text{-}control}\)
and \(|\mathcal H_n|\le2+\log_2n\),
\[
 \mathbb E_P(Z_n\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n)\le C\sqrt{\log n}. \tag{49}
\]
Every selected nonempty set belongs to \(\mathscr S_n\).  Since
\(h\le h_0<1\) implies \(\log(e+n/h)\ge\log n\), equations (47)--(49),
followed by the triangle inequality over the sides, show that the
conditional expected supremum loss is at most \(Cr\) on \(G_n\).

On \(G_n^c\), a selected set comes from either an admissible bandwidth or
the fallback \(h_0\).  The trace envelope and Lipschitz smoothness bound
the conditional mean of every nonempty fit in absolute value by
\(L(1+h_0)<2L\), while an empty fit is zero.  Equation (49), with
\(|S|^{-1/2}\le1\), gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P\left[\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}
 |\widehat\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|\ \middle|\ X_1,\ldots,X_n\right]
 \le C\{L+\sigma\sqrt{\log n}\}. \tag{50}
\]
Using (45)--(50), and the common divergence in (46),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}}
 \mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}|\widehat\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 &\le Cr+C\{L+\sigma\sqrt{\log n}\}h_*^{-1}
 e^{-cnc_mh_*^{\kappa}}\\
 &\le Cr.
 \end{aligned} \tag{51}
\]
The constants are uniform in \(2<\kappa\le\overline\kappa\): dyadic
factors are bounded by powers of two with exponent at most
\(\overline\kappa\), and (46) gives a common diverging lower bound for the
exponential argument.

The pervasive lower construction in
\(\mathrm{thm:thickness\text{-}entropy\text{-}separation}\), explicitly
certified by \(\mathrm{lem:fano\text{-}tent\text{-}validity}\), applies at
scale \(a_0r\).  Its packing logarithm is bounded below by a fixed
multiple of \(\log n\), uniformly over the exponent range, whereas the
product divergence is at most
\[
 Ca_0^{\kappa+2}\log n\le Ca_0^4\log n
\]
for \(0<a_0<1\).  A fixed sufficiently small \(a_0\) and
\(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}information\text{-}testing}\) yield
\[
 R_n(\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}})\ge cr. \tag{52}
\]

It remains to verify the fallback probability.  Take a maximal
\(h_0/2\)-separated boundary net; it has at most
\(2C_{\mathcal B}/h_0\) points.  At every center and side, the expected
radius-\(h_0/2\) count is at least \(nc_m(h_0/2)^{\kappa}\), which
uniformly in the exponent range is eventually at least
\(16\log(e+n/h_0)\).  The binomial inequality derived above, a union
bound, and ball inclusion show that, outside an event of probability at
most
\[
 C\exp\{-cnc_mh_0^{\kappa}\}, \tag{53}
\]
one has \(N_{t,n}(x,h_0)\ge8\log(e+n/h_0)\) for every \(x,t\).  Thus
\(h_0\) is admissible everywhere and no fallback occurs.  Enlarging
\(C\), (53) implies the claimed
\(Cn^3\exp\{-cnc_mh_0^{\kappa}\}\) bound.  Equation (50) and the sharper
event estimate (45) show that the fallback contribution is already
included in (51).  Equations (51)--(53) prove all conclusions.

Fix \(P\in\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-})\), and define
\(\mu_{t,P}(E)=P_X(E\cap\mathcal A_t)\).  If \(x\in\mathcal B\) and
\(r>0\), put \(s=\min\{h_0,r/2\}\).  Positivity of \(m\) and the
lower-mass member property give
\[
 \mu_{t,P}\{z:d(z,x)<r\}
 \ge q_{t,P}(x,s)
 \ge m(s)>0. \tag{54}
\]
Thus \(x\in\mathcal X_{t,P}\) on both sides.  The Lipschitz member
property makes the specified representative continuous at \(x\), so
\[
 \theta_{t,P}(x)=g_{t,P}(x). \tag{55}
\]
The assignment partition and sharp assignment make
\(X_i\in\mathcal A_t\) equivalent to \(T_i=t\).  Hence, conditionally on
the scores, each observed side average has mean equal to the
corresponding average of \(g_{t,P}(X_i)\).  The trace envelope gives
\(\tau_P(x)\in[-2L,2L]\).  Projection onto this interval cannot enlarge
pointwise error, and the clipped loss is at most \(4L\).

We first prove the upper bound.  Fix \(h\) such that
\(h,2h\in\mathcal H_n\) and
\[
 nm(h)\ge A\log(e+n/h). \tag{56}
\]
Let \(z_1,\ldots,z_K\) be a maximal \(h\)-separated subset of
\(\mathcal B\).  It is an \(h\)-net, and global boundary entropy gives
\(K\le C_{\mathcal B}h^{-1}\).  By independent sampling, for each
\(j,t\),
\[
 N_{t,n}(z_j,h)\sim\operatorname{Bin}\{n,q_{t,P}(z_j,h)\},
 \qquad q_{t,P}(z_j,h)\ge m(h). \tag{57}
\]
For \(N\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,q)\), Markov's inequality applied to
\(2^{-N}\), followed by \(1-y\le e^{-y}\), gives
\[
 \Pr\{N\le nq/2\}
 \le2^{nq/2}(1-q/2)^n
 \le e^{-nq/8}. \tag{58}
\]
Let \(H_h\) be the event that
\(N_{t,n}(z_j,h)\ge nm(h)/2\) for every \(j,t\).  Equations (57)--(58)
and the union bound imply
\[
 \Pr_P(H_h^c)\le2C_{\mathcal B}h^{-1}e^{-nm(h)/8}. \tag{59}
\]
For any \(x\in\mathcal B\), choose \(z_j\) with \(d(x,z_j)\le h\).
Since \(B(z_j,h)\subseteq B(x,2h)\), on \(H_h\),
\[
 \inf_{x\in\mathcal B,\,t\in\{0,1\}}N_{t,n}(x,2h)
 \ge\frac{nm(h)}2. \tag{60}
\]
Choose the fixed constant \(A\) large enough that (56) makes the
right-hand side of (60) at least \(8\log(e+n/(2h))\).  Then
\(2h\in\widehat{\mathcal H}_{t,n}(x)\) for every \(x,t\) on \(H_h\).

Condition on \(X_1,\ldots,X_n\).  For any nonempty averaging set indexed
by \((x,b,t)\), (55) and Lipschitz smoothness give
\[
 \left|
 \frac1{N_{t,n}(x,b)}
 \sum_{i:X_i\in\mathcal A_t,\ d(X_i,x)\le b}g_{t,P}(X_i)
 -\theta_{t,P}(x)
 \right|\le Lb. \tag{61}
\]
Let \(Z_n\) be the maximum normalized error over the nonempty disk
patterns in
\(\mathrm{lem:disk\text{-}pattern\text{-}sub\text{-}gaussian\text{-}control}\),
with its zero convention.  That lemma gives
\[
 \mathbb E_P(Z_n\mid X_1,\ldots,X_n)
 \le\sqrt{2\log\left\{4|\mathcal H_n|
 \sum_{j=0}^{3}\binom nj\right\}}+1
 \le C\sqrt{\log(e+n)}. \tag{62}
\]
On \(H_h\), every selected bandwidth is admissible and its averaging set
is nonempty.  Conditional on the scores, it is fixed because it depends
only on counts.  For every candidate bandwidth \(b\le h_0<1\),
\(\log(e+n/b)\ge\log(e+n)\).  Equations (61)--(62) therefore bound the
conditional expected side error by a constant times the selected
criterion in
\(\mathrm{def:count\text{-}adaptive\text{-}bandwidth}\).  Its minimizing
property and the admissible comparison bandwidth \(2h\), together with
(60), imply
\[
 \mathbb E_P\left[
 \sup_{x\in\mathcal B}|\widehat\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|
 \ \middle|\ X_1,\ldots,X_n
 \right]
 \le C\left\{Lh+
 \sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(e+n/h)}{nm(h)}}\right\}
 \quad\text{on }H_h. \tag{63}
\]
The two side errors and fixed factors from \(2h\) and (60) are absorbed
into \(C\).

Projection does not enlarge error, and on \(H_h^c\) its supremum is at
most \(4L\).  Taking expectations in (63) and using (59) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\sup_{P\in\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-})}
 \mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}
 |\overline\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|\\
 &\qquad\le C\left\{Lh+
 \sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(e+n/h)}{nm(h)}}\right\}
 +CLh^{-1}e^{-nm(h)/8}.
 \end{aligned} \tag{64}
\]
Increasing \(A\) so \(A/8\ge2\), (56), \(h\le h_0<1\), and \(n\ge1\)
imply
\[
 h^{-1}e^{-nm(h)/8}
 \le h^{-1}(e+n/h)^{-A/8}
 \le h.
\]
Thus the last term in (64) is absorbed into \(CLh\).  Independently of
(56), the clipped loss is at most \(4L\).  Taking the infimum over
qualifying dyadic pairs, with the stated empty-set convention, proves the
upper display.  The explicit estimator definitions and the fixed
projection use neither \(m\) nor \(\mathsf J_{-}\).

We next prove the lower display inside the same class.  Choose
\(P^\circ\) in the assumed nonempty class and retain its score marginal
and geometry.  On an extension carrying independent
\(Z_0,Z_1\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\), define \(P_0\) by
\(Y(t)=\sigma Z_t\).  Every score-dependent member property is
unchanged, while the side regressions and traces are zero.  Therefore
\[
 P_0\in\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-}). \tag{65}
\]
Let \(M=\mathsf J_{-}(h,u)\ge1\).  The lower-profile member property and
the definition of \(\mathsf J_{P_0}\) supply \(h\)-separated points
\(x_1,\ldots,x_M\) such that
\[
 \min_{t\in\{0,1\}}q_{t,P_0}(x_j,h)\le u.
\]
Choose \(t_j\) attaining the minimum, and set
\[
 \eta_h=\frac{Lh}{12},
 \qquad \psi_{j,h}(z)=\left(1-\frac{3d(z,x_j)}h\right)_+. \tag{66}
\]
Retaining the score law and Gaussian errors, define \(P_j\) by shifting
only the potential outcome on side \(t_j\): add
\(\eta_h\psi_{j,h}(X)\) if \(t_j=1\), and subtract it if \(t_j=0\).
Since \(\psi_{j,h}\) is \(3/h\)-Lipschitz, the perturbation is
\(L/4\)-Lipschitz.  Also \(h\le h_0/3<1\) gives \(\eta_h<L\).  All
design properties are unchanged and the residual about the shifted
observed conditional mean remains \(\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)\).  Hence
\[
 P_j\in\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-}),
 \qquad 1\le j\le M. \tag{67}
\]
Equation (54) puts each center in both side supports.  The sign convention
makes the causal contrast at \(x_j\) increase by \(\eta_h\), regardless
of \(t_j\).  For \(k\ne j\), \(d(x_j,x_k)\ge h\), while
\(\psi_{k,h}\) is supported on \(B(x_k,h/3)\).  Consequently
\[
 \|\tau_{P_j}-\tau_{P_k}\|_\infty\ge\eta_h,
 \qquad 0\le j<k\le M. \tag{68}
\]
If a conditional normal mean changes from zero to \(\mu\), direct
substitution into the Gaussian densities gives
\[
 \mathbb E_{Y\sim\mathcal N(\mu,\sigma^2)}
 \log\frac{\phi_\sigma(Y-\mu)}{\phi_\sigma(Y)}
 =\frac{\mu^2}{2\sigma^2}. \tag{69}
\]
The perturbation is present only when \(X\in\mathcal A_{t_j}\), and the
tent is supported in \(B(x_j,h/3)\subseteq B(x_j,h)\).  Thus independent
sampling, (69), and \(q_{t_j,P_0}(x_j,h)\le u\) yield
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{KL}(P_j^{\otimes n},P_0^{\otimes n})
 &=\frac{n\eta_h^2}{2\sigma^2}
 \int_{\mathcal A_{t_j}}\psi_{j,h}(z)^2\,dP_X(z)\\
 &\le\frac{nL^2h^2u}{288\sigma^2}.
 \end{aligned} \tag{70}
\]
If \(M\ge1\) and \(nL^2h^2u/\sigma^2\le a\), retain one alternative
and decrease \(a\), if needed, so that (70) is at most \(1/8\).  The
two-law clause of
\(\mathrm{lem:finite\text{-}information\text{-}testing}\), with
\(2\delta=\eta_h\), gives risk at least a numerical multiple of \(Lh\).
If \(M\ge2\) and
\(nL^2h^2u/\sigma^2\le a\log(M+1)\), use
\[
 \frac{\log(M+1)}{\log M}\le\frac{\log3}{\log2}
\]
and choose \(a\) smaller by this fixed factor.  Then the average
divergence in (70) is below the multiple-law threshold times \(\log M\).
Equations (68) and the multiple-law clause give
\[
 R_n\{\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-})\}\ge cLh. \tag{71}
\]
This proves both alternatives in the lower display.

For a sequence of nonempty classes, suppose there are \(h_n\downarrow0\),
with \(h_n,2h_n\in\mathcal H_n\), such that
\[
 \frac{nm_n(h_n)}{\log(e+n/h_n)}\longrightarrow\infty. \tag{72}
\]
The eligibility condition (56) then holds eventually, while
\(Lh_n\to0\) and the square-root term in (64) tends to zero.  The upper
display proves uniform consistency.  Conversely, let \(F_n\) be the set
of radii satisfying either information condition in the lower display.
If \(\sup F_n\not\to0\), then along a subsequence one can choose feasible
\(h_n\) bounded below by a positive constant, taking a value above half
the supremum when it is not attained.  Equation (71) then bounds minimax
risk away from zero.  Thus uniform consistency requires
\(\sup F_n\to0\).

For the fixed exponential modulus,
\(\mathrm{prop:exponential\text{-}horn\text{-}consistency}\) gives
\[
 m_\alpha(h)\asymp h^{\alpha+1}e^{-h^{-\alpha}}.
\]
Set \(s_n=\{(\log n)/2\}^{-1/\alpha}\).  Then
\[
 \frac{nm_\alpha(s_n)}{\log(e+n/s_n)}
 \asymp\frac{n^{1/2}s_n^{\alpha+1}}{\log n}
 \longrightarrow\infty. \tag{73}
\]
Because \(s_n\downarrow0\) and \(ns_n\to\infty\), for all large \(n\)
it can be rounded upward to \(h_n\in\mathcal H_n\) so that
\(s_n\le h_n<2s_n\) and \(2h_n\in\mathcal H_n\).  Monotonicity of
\(m_\alpha\) and
\(\log(e+n/h_n)\le\log(e+n/s_n)\) preserve (73).

For polynomial profiles, put \(u=C_mh^\kappa\).  If \(M\asymp1\), the
first information condition holds at
\(h\asymp n^{-1/(\kappa+2)}\).  If \(M\asymp h^{-1}\), then
\(\log(M+1)\asymp\log(1/h)\), and the second condition holds at
\[
 h\asymp(\log n/n)^{1/(\kappa+2)},
 \qquad nh^{\kappa+2}\asymp\log(1/h).
\]
These lower rates match the corresponding achievability conclusions of
\(\mathrm{thm:thickness\text{-}entropy\text{-}separation}\).

Finally, the generic displays can differ when the declared lower packing
profile is slack.  For the circular pervasive witness of
\(\mathrm{lem:circular\text{-}witness\text{-}membership}\), take
\(m(h)=c_mh^\kappa\) and declare \(\mathsf J_{-}(h,u)=0\) for
\(u<C_mh^\kappa\) and \(\mathsf J_{-}(h,u)=1\) for
\(u\ge C_mh^\kappa\).  This legal declaration exposes only \(M=1\) in
(71), while the upper proof covers a global net of order \(h^{-1}\).
Hence the arbitrary-profile result is exactly the claimed two-sided
bracket, with equality of rates asserted only in the polynomial isolated
and pervasive regimes already proved.

%%% FIELD proof-global-honest-band-and-width-obstruction
The partition and sharp-assignment conditions imply
\(X_i\in\mathcal A_t\) if and only if \(T_i=t\).  Condition on the
scores, so every selected bandwidth is fixed.  Each member of the
triangular union belongs to a polynomial base class.  For any boundary
point \(x\), side \(t\), and radius \(r_0>0\), put
\(s=\min\{h_0,r_0/2\}\).  Positive polynomial lower mass gives
\[
 P_X\{X\in\mathcal A_t:d(X,x)<r_0\}
 \ge q_{t,P}(x,s)\ge c_ms^\kappa>0.
\]
Thus \(x\) belongs to each side support; Lipschitz continuity and the
trace definition imply \(\theta_{t,P}(x)=g_{t,P}(x)\).  Consequently,
whenever the selected count \(N\) is positive,
\[
 \left|N^{-1}\sum_{i\in S}g_{t,P}(X_i)-\theta_{t,P}(x)\right|
 \le L\widehat h_{t,n}(x). \tag{81}
\]
If \(N=0\), the side fit is zero and the bounded-trace member property
bounds its error by \(L\).

By
\(\mathrm{lem:disk\text{-}pattern\text{-}sub\text{-}gaussian\text{-}control}\),
at most
\[
 M_n=2|\mathcal H_n|\sum_{j=0}^3\binom nj
\]
nonempty averaging sets can occur.  Its conditional sub-Gaussian tail
bound at \(z=\sqrt{2\log(2M_n/\gamma)}\) implies that, conditionally on
the scores, with probability at least \(1-\gamma\), every nonempty
candidate set satisfies
\[
 \left|N^{-1}\sum_{i\in S}\{Y_i-g_{T_i,P}(X_i)\}\right|
 \le\sigma\sqrt{\frac{2\log(2M_n/\gamma)}N}. \tag{82}
\]
Equations (81)--(82), together with the zero-count bound, show that each
side error is at most \(B_{t,n}(x)\), simultaneously over \(x\) and
\(t\).  The triangle inequality proves conditional, and hence
unconditional, simultaneous coverage.  All counts and fits are
measurable, the grids are finite, and the bandwidth rule has a
deterministic tie break, so the band is data measurable.  Since the same
argument applies to every member of every component of the nonempty
triangular union, taking the infimum over
\(P\in\mathcal P_n(\Lambda_n)\) preserves the lower coverage bound
\(1-\gamma\).

For the pervasive width assertion, put
\(r=(\log n/n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}\) and choose
\(h_*\in\mathcal H_n\) with \(r\le h_*<2r\).  A maximal
\(h_*/2\)-separated boundary net has at most
\(2C_{\mathcal B}h_*^{-1}\) points.  Every radius-\(h_*/2\) center-side
count is binomial with mean at least \(nc_m(h_*/2)^\kappa\).  For
\(N\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,q)\), Markov's inequality applied to
\(2^{-N}\) gives \(\Pr(N\le nq/2)\le e^{-nq/8}\).  A union bound and
ball inclusion therefore give an event \(G_n\) such that
\[
 \inf_{x,t}N_{t,n}(x,h_*)\ge cn c_mh_*^\kappa,
 \qquad
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_\kappa^{\mathrm{perv}}}P(G_n^c)
 \le Ch_*^{-1}e^{-cnc_mh_*^\kappa}. \tag{83}
\]
Since \(nh_*^\kappa/\log(e+n/h_*)\to\infty\), the comparison bandwidth
\(h_*\) is admissible everywhere on \(G_n\) for all sufficiently large
\(n\).  The minimizing property of \(\widehat h_{t,n}(x)\), together
with \(nr^{\kappa+2}=\log n\), yields
\[
 L\widehat h_{t,n}(x)+
 \sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(e+n/\widehat h_{t,n}(x))}
 {N_{t,n}(x,\widehat h_{t,n}(x))}}
 \le Cr \quad\text{on }G_n. \tag{84}
\]
Also \(\log(2M_n/\gamma)\le C\log n\), while
\(\log(e+n/b)\ge\log n\) for \(b\le h_0<1\).  Hence (84) implies
\(B_{t,n}(x)\le Cr\) simultaneously on \(G_n\).  On \(G_n^c\), each
radius is at most \(L+\sigma\sqrt{C\log n}\): if \(N=0\) this is its
definition, and if \(N>0\) it follows from \(N\ge1\) and
\(\widehat h_{t,n}(x)\le h_0<1\).  The exceptional contribution in
(83) is \(o(r)\), because \(nh_*^\kappa\) grows polynomially in \(n\).
Since the constructed interval has
\[
 W_n(x)=B_{0,n}(x)+B_{1,n}(x),
\]
the claimed uniform expected-sup width follows.

For the obstruction, fix \(x\in\mathcal B\) and let \(A_j\) be the
event that \(\mathcal C_n(x)\) contains \(\tau_{P_j}(x)\).  Honesty gives
\[
 P_j(A_j)\ge1-\gamma-\epsilon_n,
 \qquad j=0,1.
\]
By the definition of total variation for the full observed product
experiments,
\[
 P_0(A_1)\ge P_1(A_1)-
 \operatorname{TV}(P_0^{\otimes n},P_1^{\otimes n}).
\]
Therefore the union bound gives
\[
 P_0(A_0\cap A_1)
 \ge1-2\gamma-2\epsilon_n-
 \operatorname{TV}(P_0^{\otimes n},P_1^{\otimes n}). \tag{85}
\]
On \(A_0\cap A_1\), the interval contains both target values, so its
half-width is at least half their separation.  Multiplying this
implication by the positive part of (85) and taking \(P_0\)-expectations
proves
\[
 \mathbb E_{P_0}W_n(x)
 \ge\frac{|\tau_{P_1}(x)-\tau_{P_0}(x)|}{2}
 \left[1-2\gamma-2\epsilon_n-
 \operatorname{TV}(P_0^{\otimes n},P_1^{\otimes n})\right]_+.
\]
This uses total variation of the full observed experiments.  A score
marginal is only a measurable projection and cannot supply the required
upper bound on the full distance without an additional same-class
outcome construction.  Likewise, the global calibration has
\(\log M_n\asymp\log n\), whereas
\(\log\{eK_P(x,h)/\gamma\}\) may be bounded at an isolated thin site.
The proof therefore establishes precisely the stated global band and
full-law obstruction, but neither disclaimed sharper conclusion.

%%% FIELD proof-lower-profile-only-class
Define \(m\) and \(\mathsf J_{-}\) as in the theorem.  The map
\(m(h)=c_mh^\kappa\) is nondecreasing and strictly positive on
\((0,h_0]\).  The constants are those certified by
\(\mathrm{lem:circular\text{-}witness\text{-}membership}\).  That lemma
supplies a member for which the polynomial lower-mass inequality holds,
while the definition of \(q_{t,P}\) gives \(q_{t,P}(x,h)\le1\).  Hence
\(c_mh^\kappa\le1\) for \(0<h\le h_0\), so
\(m:(0,h_0]\to[0,1]\) is well typed.  The displayed indicator defining
\(\mathsf J_{-}\) takes values in \(\mathbb N_0\) and is nondecreasing in
\(u\).

First take \(P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}\).  By
\(\mathrm{def:pervasive\text{-}thinning\text{-}class}\) and
\(\mathrm{def:base\text{-}thickness\text{-}class}\), this law has the
sharp-assignment, consistency, boundary-entropy, Lipschitz-regression,
bounded-trace, and conditional sub-Gaussian residual properties required
by \(\mathrm{def:modulus\text{-}profile\text{-}class}\).  The polynomial
lower-mass member property gives
\[
 q_{t,P}(x,h)\ge c_mh^\kappa=m(h)
 \quad
 (x\in\mathcal B,\ t\in\{0,1\},\ 0<h\le h_0). \tag{86}
\]
For fixed \(h\), the thin-site set in the definition of
\(\mathsf J_P(h,u)\) is nondecreasing in \(u\); consequently its maximal
\(h\)-packing cardinality is nondecreasing in \(u\).  If
\(u<C_mh^\kappa\), then
\(\mathsf J_{-}(h,u)=0\le\mathsf J_P(h,u)\).  If
\(u\ge C_mh^\kappa\), the pervasive thin-site member property and this
monotonicity imply
\[
 \mathsf J_P(h,u)
 \ge\mathsf J_P(h,C_mh^\kappa)
 \ge c_Jh^{-1}>0. \tag{87}
\]
Because \(\mathsf J_P(h,u)\in\mathbb N_0\), (87) entails
\(\mathsf J_P(h,u)\ge1=\mathsf J_{-}(h,u)\).  Thus (86)--(87) and
\(\mathrm{def:modulus\text{-}profile\text{-}class}\) yield
\[
 \mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}
 \subseteq\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-}). \tag{88}
\]

Now take \(P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}\).  The base-class
definition again gives (86) and all common member properties.  The
isolated thin-site member property gives
\[
 \mathsf J_P(h,C_mh^\kappa)\ge1. \tag{89}
\]
The same monotonicity in \(u\), together with
\(\mathsf J_{-}(h,u)=0\) below the threshold, verifies the declared lower
profile and proves
\[
 \mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}
 \subseteq\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-}). \tag{90}
\]
Equations (88) and (90) prove the asserted union inclusion.  Moreover,
\(\mathrm{lem:circular\text{-}witness\text{-}membership}\) supplies one
member of each subclass.  The containing class is therefore nonempty,
as required by \(\mathrm{def:expected\text{-}sup\text{-}risk}\) and
\(\mathrm{thm:arbitrary\text{-}modulus\text{-}two\text{-}sided\text{-}bracket}\).

We next use monotonicity of minimax risk.  If nonempty classes satisfy
\(\mathcal Q_1\subseteq\mathcal Q_2\), then every estimator
\(\widetilde\tau_n\) obeys
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal Q_2}
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}
 |\widetilde\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|\right]
 \ge
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal Q_1}
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}
 |\widetilde\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|\right].
\]
Taking the infimum over the identical collection of sample-measurable
estimators in \(\mathrm{def:expected\text{-}sup\text{-}risk}\) preserves
the inequality.  Apply it to (88), and then invoke the pervasive lower
bound in \(\mathrm{thm:thickness\text{-}entropy\text{-}separation}\).
For all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 R_n\{\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-})\}
 \ge R_n(\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}})
 \ge c\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(\kappa+2)}
 =c\,r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}. \tag{91}
\]

For the reverse inequality, put
\[
 s_n=\left(\frac{\log n}{n}\right)^{1/(\kappa+2)}.
\]
Then \(s_n\downarrow0\) and \(ns_n/h_0\to\infty\).  Let
\(j_n=\lfloor\log_2(h_0/s_n)\rfloor\) and
\(h_n=2^{-j_n}h_0\).  For all sufficiently large \(n\),
\(1\le j_n\le\lceil\log_2 n\rceil\), and
\(\mathrm{def:dyadic\text{-}grid}\) therefore gives
\[
 s_n\le h_n<2s_n,
 \qquad h_n,2h_n\in\mathcal H_n. \tag{92}
\]
Since \(h_n\ge s_n\), the polynomial form of \(m\) and
\(\log(e+n/h_n)\le C\log n\) imply
\[
 \frac{nm(h_n)}{\log(e+n/h_n)}
 \ge c\frac{ns_n^\kappa}{\log n}
 =c\left(\frac n{\log n}\right)^{2/(\kappa+2)}
 \longrightarrow\infty. \tag{93}
\]
In particular, the count-qualification inequality from
\(\mathrm{thm:arbitrary\text{-}modulus\text{-}two\text{-}sided\text{-}bracket}\)
holds eventually.  Because \(ns_n^{\kappa+2}=\log n\), the upper half of
that theorem, evaluated at the admissible pair \(h_n,2h_n\), yields for
its explicit clipped count-adaptive estimator \(\overline\tau_n\)
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-})}
 \mathbb E_P\!\left[\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}
 |\overline\tau_n(x)-\tau_P(x)|\right]
 &\le C\left\{Lh_n+
 \sigma\sqrt{\frac{\log(e+n/h_n)}{nm(h_n)}}\right\}\\
 &\le C\left\{s_n+
 \sqrt{\frac{\log n}{ns_n^\kappa}}\right\}
 =Cs_n.
 \end{aligned} \tag{94}
\]
The estimator \(\overline\tau_n\) is a measurable function of
\(\mathcal D_n\) and the known assignment geometry by the construction in
that theorem, so it is admissible in the infimum defining \(R_n\).
Consequently
\[
 R_n\{\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-})\}
 \le Cs_n
 =C\,r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}. \tag{95}
\]
Together, (91) and (95) establish the matched pervasive frontier for the
displayed two-argument class.

Finally, the two defined polynomial rates satisfy
\[
 \frac{r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}}
 {r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{iso}}}
 =\frac{(\log n/n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}}{n^{-1/(\kappa+2)}}
 =(\log n)^{1/(\kappa+2)}\longrightarrow\infty. \tag{96}
\]
Thus the declaration
\(\mathsf J_{-}(h,u)=\mathbf1\{u\ge C_mh^\kappa\}\), which guarantees
only one thin site at the threshold, does not imply the isolated rate:
by (88) it still admits the pervasive subclass, and by (91)--(95) its
full class has the pervasive rate.  The upper thin-site restriction in
\(\mathrm{def:isolated\text{-}thinning\text{-}class}\), absent from
\(\mathcal P(m,\mathsf J_{-})\), excludes precisely this counterexample.
Hence a lower profile alone cannot express the desired
isolated-versus-pervasive distinction; an upper packing restriction or
an intrinsic two-sided profile is required.  This proves the asserted
negative answer.

%%% FIELD statement-honest-adaptive-bands-partial
Suppose \(\mathcal A_0=\mathbb R^2\setminus\mathcal A_1\).  The explicit
band \(\mathcal C_n\) constructed in
\(\mathrm{thm:global\text{-}honest\text{-}band\text{-}and\text{-}width\text{-}obstruction}\)
is data measurable and, for every declared nonempty triangular union,
\[
 \inf_{P\in\mathcal P_n(\Lambda_n)}
 P\{\tau_P(x)\in\mathcal C_n(x)\text{ for every }x\in\mathcal B\}
 \ge1-\gamma.
\]
For every fixed \(\kappa\in(2,\overline\kappa]\), there is a constant
\(0<C<\infty\), independent of \(n\), such that, for all sufficiently
large \(n\), it also satisfies
\[
 \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}}
 \mathbb E_P\sup_{x\in\mathcal B}W_n(x)
 \le C r_{n,\kappa}^{\mathrm{perv}}.
\]
Moreover, for any \(\epsilon_n\ge0\), every band with simultaneous
coverage at least \(1-\gamma-\epsilon_n\) over a class containing
\(P_0,P_1\) obeys, for every \(x\in\mathcal B\),
\[
 \mathbb E_{P_0}W_n(x)\ge
 \frac{|\tau_{P_1}(x)-\tau_{P_0}(x)|}{2}
 \left[1-2\gamma-2\epsilon_n-
 \operatorname{TV}(P_0^{\otimes n},P_1^{\otimes n})\right]_+.
\]
These conclusions give a finite-sample globally calibrated band and a
general full-observed-law width obstruction.  They make no assertion of
a uniform local-oracle ratio involving \(r_{n,P}^{\mathrm{loc}}\), nor of
an exact adaptation criterion based only on \(P_X^{\otimes n}\).
