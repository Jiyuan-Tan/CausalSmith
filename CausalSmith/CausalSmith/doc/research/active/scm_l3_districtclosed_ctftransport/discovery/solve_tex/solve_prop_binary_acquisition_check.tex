%%% FIELD prop-binary-acquisition-check-proof
Write the coordinates of a law on the ordered response-profile set in
\(\mathrm{def:binary\text{-}check}\) as
\[
q=(q_{00},q_{01},q_{10},q_{11})^{\mathsf T}.
\]
By the definitions of \(A_D\), \(m_D\), and \(\mathcal K_D(m_D)\), membership
\(q\in\mathcal K_D(m_D)\) is equivalent to nonnegativity together with
\[
q_{00}+q_{01}+q_{10}+q_{11}=1,
\qquad q_{10}+q_{11}=\frac12,
\qquad q_{01}+q_{11}=\frac12.
\tag{1}
\]
Set \(p=q_{11}\).  The last two equations in \((1)\) give
\(q_{10}=q_{01}=1/2-p\), and substitution in the first gives \(q_{00}=p\).
Thus every feasible law has the unique form
\[
q(p)=\left(p,\frac12-p,\frac12-p,p\right)^{\mathsf T}.
\tag{2}
\]
All four coordinates in \((2)\) are nonnegative exactly when
\(0\leq p\leq1/2\); conversely, for each such \(p\), equation \((2)\)
satisfies \((1)\).  This proves the claimed parameterization.

The evidence row specified in \(\mathrm{def:binary\text{-}check}\) satisfies
\[
d_D^{\mathsf T}q(p)
=(1,1,1,1)q(p)=1.
\tag{3}
\]
Hence the evidence probability is strictly positive throughout the face, so
every conditional value below is defined; this is precisely the supplied
certificate \(\varepsilon_D=1\) in the
\(\mathsf{CheckedLocalArms}_D\) record.  Using the two numerator rows from
that record and \((2)\)--\((3)\),
\[
Q_\rho(q(p))
=\frac{(0,1,1,0)q(p)}{(1,1,1,1)q(p)}
=\left(\frac12-p\right)+\left(\frac12-p\right)
=1-2p,
\tag{4}
\]
and
\[
Q_\sigma(q(p))
=\frac{(0,1,0,1)q(p)}{(1,1,1,1)q(p)}
=\left(\frac12-p\right)+p
=\frac12.
\tag{5}
\]

By \(\mathrm{def:binary\text{-}check}\),
\(r_D^\dagger=(0,0,0,1)\), and therefore
\[
(r_D^\dagger)^{\mathsf T}q(p)=q_{11}=p.
\tag{6}
\]
Equations \((2)\) and \((6)\) show both that \(I_D=[0,1/2]\) and that the
row-augmented fiber at a prescribed \(p\in I_D\) is the singleton
\(\{q(p)\}\).  Since
\[
g_{\rho,\sigma,D}^{\mathsf T}q(p)
=(0,0,-1,1)q(p)
=-\left(\frac12-p\right)+p
=2p-\frac12,
\tag{7}
\]
the minimum in \(\mathrm{def:parametric\text{-}menu\text{-}frontier}\) is
\[
\Gamma_{\rho\prec\sigma}(p;m_D)=2p-\frac12.
\tag{8}
\]
Consequently, using \(p\in[0,1/2]\),
\[
\mathcal J_{\rho\prec\sigma}^+
=\left\{p\in[0,1/2]:2p-\frac12>0\right\}
=\left(\frac14,\frac12\right].
\tag{9}
\]
This is also the explicit one-piece specialization of
\(\mathrm{thm:one\text{-}cell\text{-}sign\text{-}regime\text{-}frontier}\).

For completeness, let \(\widetilde A_D\) be \(A_D\) with
\(r_D^\dagger\) appended and let
\(\widetilde m_D(p)=(1,1/2,1/2,p)^{\mathsf T}\).  With the stated augmented
row order, put
\[
\eta=(0,-1,0,2)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Direct coordinate multiplication gives
\[
\widetilde A_D^{\mathsf T}\eta
=\begin{pmatrix}0\\0\\-1\\1\end{pmatrix}
=g_{\rho,\sigma,D},
\qquad
\widetilde m_D(p)^{\mathsf T}\eta
=-\frac12+2p.
\tag{10}
\]
Thus \(\eta\) satisfies the row-augmented dual inequality with equality.
Its objective in \((10)\) equals the primal value in \((7)\), so it is an
optimal dual certificate.  At the supplied value \(p=3/8\), that value is
\[
2\left(\frac38\right)-\frac12=\frac14>0.
\tag{11}
\]

At the unique sign boundary \(p=1/4\), equation \((2)\) gives the response
law
\[
q(1/4)=(1/4,1/4,1/4,1/4).
\tag{12}
\]
The endpoint laws are
\[
q(0)=(0,1/2,1/2,0),
\qquad
q(1/2)=(1/2,0,0,1/2).
\tag{13}
\]
Each law in \((12)\)--\((13)\) is a probability law on the four deterministic
binary response maps \((Y_0,Y_1)\) in the declared carrier \(\Omega_D\), so
it uses no enlarged endogenous alphabet.  More explicitly, take a latent
response block with the displayed law and the binary structural equation
\(Y=Y_{T_{\mathrm{tr}}}\), retain the specified binary mechanisms of the
nonfocal districts (including the distinct target and source mechanisms for
\(Z\)), and use the same response-block law in the target and source.  This
last equality is required because
\(D=\{Y\}\) and \(D\cap\Delta_s=\varnothing\) when
\(\Delta_s=\{Z\}\).  The base equalities are preserved by \((1)\).
Equivalently, the supplied binary cells are either focal-district local or
independent of the focal profile, so the focal projection clause of
\(\mathrm{thm:one\text{-}district\text{-}fiber\text{-}projection}\) lifts
these face laws to original-alphabet focal-district-saturated SCM families
while leaving every nonfocal district fixed.  Equations \((4)\)--\((5)\)
then show that \(q(0)\) makes \(\rho\) strictly optimal and \(q(1/2)\)
makes \(\sigma\) strictly optimal.

It remains to verify the possibly-optimal sets.  With no experiment acquired,
\(\mathcal K_{\varnothing}=\mathcal K_D(m_D)\) by
\(\mathrm{def:regime\text{-}block\text{-}acquisition}\).  At \(q(0)\),
equations \((4)\)--\((5)\) give
\(Q_\rho=1>1/2=Q_\sigma\), so \(\rho\in\operatorname{PO}(\varnothing)\).
At \(q(1/2)\), they give
\(Q_\sigma=1/2>0=Q_\rho\), so
\(\sigma\in\operatorname{PO}(\varnothing)\).  Because
\(\mathcal R_{\mathrm{arm}}=\{\rho,\sigma\}\),
\[
\operatorname{PO}(\varnothing)=\{\rho,\sigma\}.
\tag{14}
\]
Acquiring \(e^\dagger\) imposes
\((r_D^\dagger)^{\mathsf T}q=3/8\); the complementary output-cell equality
is redundant with normalization.  Equations \((2)\) and \((6)\) therefore
give
\[
\mathcal K_{\{e^\dagger\}}=\{q(3/8)\}.
\tag{15}
\]
At this sole feasible law, equations \((4)\)--\((5)\) yield
\[
Q_\rho(q(3/8))=\frac14
<\frac12=Q_\sigma(q(3/8)).
\tag{16}
\]
Thus only \(\sigma\) is possibly optimal after acquisition.  Finally,
\(\mathcal U=\{e^\dagger\}\) and
\(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{full}}=\operatorname{PO}(\mathcal U)\) by definition,
so \((15)\)--\((16)\) imply
\[
\operatorname{PO}(\{e^\dagger\})
=\mathcal P_{\mathrm{full}}
=\{\sigma\},
\]
as claimed.
