Fix an integer \(p\geq 3\) and \((a,b)\in\mathbb R^2\) with \(a\neq0\) and \(b\neq0\).  Let \(M^{\mathrm{ch}}\) and \(M^{\mathrm{fk}}\) be the paired SCMs in the construction \(\text{def:chain-fork-witness}\), and write \(U=(U_1,\ldots,U_{p-3})\).  When \(p=3\), this is the empty tuple and \(I_{p-3}=I_0\) is the empty covariance block.  We verify the observational law, every condition defining \(\mathcal M_p^{\mathrm{df}}\), and the confidence lower bound.

In either environment of \(M^{\mathrm{ch}}\), the equations in \(\text{def:chain-fork-witness}\) give
\[
 X=\varepsilon_X,\qquad
 Y=a\varepsilon_X+\varepsilon_Y,
 \qquad
 W=ab\varepsilon_X+b\varepsilon_Y+\varepsilon_W.
\]
The innovations are mutually independent, centered, and have variance one.  Therefore bilinearity of covariance gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Var}(X)&=1,
 &\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)&=a,
 &\operatorname{Var}(Y)&=a^2+1,\\
 \operatorname{Cov}(X,W)&=ab,
 &\operatorname{Cov}(Y,W)&=b(a^2+1),
 &\operatorname{Var}(W)&=b^2(a^2+1)+1.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the covariance of \((X,Y,W)\) is \(\Sigma^{\mathrm{cf}}(a,b)\).  More explicitly, if
\[
 L=\begin{pmatrix}
 1&0&0\\
 a&1&0\\
 ab&b&1
 \end{pmatrix},
\]
then direct multiplication yields
\[
 \Sigma^{\mathrm{cf}}(a,b)=LL^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Because \(L\) is triangular with unit diagonal, it is invertible.  Hence, for every nonzero \(q\in\mathbb R^3\),
\[
 q^{\mathsf T}\Sigma^{\mathrm{cf}}(a,b)q
 =\lVert L^{\mathsf T}q\rVert_2^2>0,
 \qquad
 \det\Sigma^{\mathrm{cf}}(a,b)=(\det L)^2=1.
\]
Consequently \(\Sigma^{\mathrm{cf}}(a,b)\) is positive definite.  Each \(U_j\) is, by \(\text{def:chain-fork-witness}\), standard Gaussian and independent of \((X,Y,W)\) and of every other \(U_k\).  The full covariance in either environment of the chain model is therefore
\[
 \widetilde\Sigma_p(a,b)
 :=\Sigma^{\mathrm{cf}}(a,b)\oplus I_{p-3},
\]
which is positive definite.

For \(M^{\mathrm{fk}}\), set \(c=a/(a^2+1)\), which is well defined because \(a^2+1>0\).  In either environment, \(Y=\eta_Y\), \(X=cY+\eta_X\), and \(W=bY+\eta_W\), while
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(\eta_Y)=a^2+1,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(\eta_X)=(a^2+1)^{-1},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(\eta_W)=1.
\]
Independence of the innovations implies
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Var}(Y)&=a^2+1,
 &\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)&=c(a^2+1)=a,\\
 \operatorname{Var}(X)&=c^2(a^2+1)+(a^2+1)^{-1}=1,
 &\operatorname{Cov}(Y,W)&=b(a^2+1),\\
 \operatorname{Cov}(X,W)&=b\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)=ab,
 &\operatorname{Var}(W)&=b^2(a^2+1)+1.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the fork has the same \((X,Y,W)\) covariance \(\Sigma^{\mathrm{cf}}(a,b)\).  Its appended variables are again independent standard Gaussians, so its full covariance is also \(\widetilde\Sigma_p(a,b)\).  In both constructions every observed variable is a linear transformation of mutually independent centered Gaussian innovations, as stipulated in \(\text{def:chain-fork-witness}\) and \(\text{ass:independent-gaussian-innovations}\).  Hence every environment law of either model is
\[
 \mathcal N\!\left(0,\Sigma^{\mathrm{cf}}(a,b)\oplus I_{p-3}\right),
\]
and the two models induce the asserted identical law pair.

We next verify membership in the class of \(\text{def:boundary-model-class}\).  The chain graph has exactly the arrows \(X\to Y\to W\), the fork graph has exactly the arrows \(Y\to X\) and \(Y\to W\), and every \(U_j\) is isolated.  The orders specified in \(\text{def:chain-fork-witness}\) are therefore topological, so all baseline and environment graphs are acyclic.  The displayed equations have the form required by \(\text{ass:linear-structural-equations}\).  Within either paired model, the baseline graph and coefficient matrix equal the graph and coefficient matrix in each environment.  Thus
\[
 \operatorname{Pa}_{G_e}(v)=\operatorname{Pa}_{G^*}(v)
\]
for every \(e\in\mathbb E\) and \(v\in\mathbb V\), proving \(\text{ass:baseline-parent-containment}\).  Because \(\mathcal I_1=\mathcal I_2=\varnothing\) and all environment mechanisms equal their baseline mechanisms, \(\text{ass:modular-interventions}\) holds.  The innovation laws in the construction are independent centered Gaussians, establishing \(\text{ass:independent-gaussian-innovations}\); their variance vectors agree across the two environments within each model, establishing \(\text{ass:invariant-innovation-variances}\).  Finally, all chain innovation variances equal one, and all fork innovation variances belong to
\[
 \left\{(a^2+1)^{-1},a^2+1,1\right\}\subset(0,\infty),
\]
so \(\text{ass:positive-innovation-variances}\) holds.

It remains to check \(\text{ass:diff-faithfulness}\) for every ordered pair of distinct vertices and every eligible conditioning set.  In either paired model, \(B_1=B_2\).  The construction of \(D(M)\) in \(\text{def:difference-dag}\) therefore gives
\[
 E_D(M)=\varnothing.
\]
Fix distinct \(A,B\in\mathbb V\) and \(S\subseteq\mathbb V\setminus\{A,B\}\).  Because the difference DAG has no edge, no path can contain an edge of \(D(M)\), and no vertex can have an ancestor with an incoming edge in \(D(M)\).  Hence no path is diff-relevant by \(\text{def:diff-relevant-path}\).  Each of the three alternatives in \(\text{def:conditionally-diff-relevant-path}\) likewise requires an incoming edge in \(D(M)\), so no path is conditionally diff-relevant.  The universal blocking condition in \(\text{def:diff-separation}\) is consequently satisfied, and
\[
 A\perp_{\mathrm{diff},M}B\mid S.
\]

For the regression side of diff-faithfulness, the two covariance matrices within the paired model both equal the positive-definite matrix \(\widetilde\Sigma_p(a,b)\).  We make explicit why every denominator in the regression functional is positive.  If \(S\neq\varnothing\), the principal submatrix \(K=\widetilde\Sigma_p(a,b)_{SS}\) is positive definite: extending any nonzero vector on \(S\) by zeros gives a nonzero vector in \(\mathbb R^p\).  Thus \(K^{-1}\) exists.  In coordinates \((B,S)\), define
\[
 v=\begin{pmatrix}1\\-K^{-1}\widetilde\Sigma_p(a,b)_{SB}\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The relevant principal submatrix is positive definite and \(v\neq0\), whence
\[
 \widetilde\Sigma_{p,BB\mathbin{\cdot}S}(a,b)
 =v^{\mathsf T}
   \widetilde\Sigma_p(a,b)_{\{B\}\cup S,\{B\}\cup S}v
 >0.
\]
If \(S=\varnothing\), the same conclusion is \(\widetilde\Sigma_{p,BB}(a,b)>0\).  Division in \(r_e(A,B\mid S)\) is therefore valid.  Equality of the two environment covariance matrices now implies
\[
 r_1(A,B\mid S)=r_2(A,B\mid S).
\]
Thus both sides of the equivalence in \(\text{ass:diff-faithfulness}\) are true.  Since \((A,B,S)\) was arbitrary, diff-faithfulness holds for every required triple, including conditioning sets containing isolated vertices.  We have verified every member condition in \(\text{def:boundary-model-class}\), and hence
\[
 M^{\mathrm{ch}},M^{\mathrm{fk}}\in\mathcal M_p^{\mathrm{df}}.
\]

We already showed that the difference DAG of each model is empty.  Moreover, the two models have the same covariance coordinate, and \(\mathcal L(s)\) in \(\text{def:exact-ledger}\) is a deterministic function of that coordinate.  Their complete equality and sepset ledgers therefore coincide.  Within either model, equality of its two environment covariances makes every cross-environment regression difference zero, so every equality query in this complete ledger is answered affirmatively.  Diff-faithfulness was just established, so \(\text{lem:ldiffpc-population-soundness}\) applies: the output skeleton is the true difference-DAG skeleton and every output orientation is a true difference-DAG orientation.  The true skeleton has no edge; consequently the population LDiffPC output is empty for both models.

In \(M^{\mathrm{ch}}\), the coefficient on \(X\to Y\) is \(a\) in each environment.  In \(M^{\mathrm{fk}}\), that arrow is absent and its coefficient is zero in each environment.  The definitions of \(\alpha_e\) and \(\tau\) therefore yield
\[
 \tau(M^{\mathrm{ch}})=(a,a,0),
 \qquad
 \tau(M^{\mathrm{fk}})=(0,0,0).
\]
Their Euclidean separation is
\[
 \left\lVert\tau(M^{\mathrm{ch}})-\tau(M^{\mathrm{fk}})\right\rVert_2
 =\sqrt{a^2+a^2}=\sqrt2\lvert a\rvert.
\]

Finally, fix any admissible sample-size pair and let \(N=n_1+n_2\).  By \(\text{ass:independent-gaussian-sampling}\) and the equality of both environment laws under the two models, the complete sample has the same distribution under \(M^{\mathrm{ch}}\) and \(M^{\mathrm{fk}}\); call this distribution \(Q_N\).  Set
\[
 E_{\mathrm{ch}}=\{(a,a,0)\in D_N\},
 \qquad
 E_{\mathrm{fk}}=\{(0,0,0)\in D_N\}.
\]
Coverage at each of the two models gives
\[
 Q_N(E_{\mathrm{ch}})\geq1-\gamma,
 \qquad
 Q_N(E_{\mathrm{fk}})\geq1-\gamma.
\]
The union bound, using \(0<\gamma<1/2\), gives
\[
 Q_N(E_{\mathrm{ch}}\cap E_{\mathrm{fk}})
 \geq1-Q_N(E_{\mathrm{ch}}^c)-Q_N(E_{\mathrm{fk}}^c)
 \geq1-2\gamma>0.
\]
On \(E_{\mathrm{ch}}\cap E_{\mathrm{fk}}\), the closed set \(D_N\) contains both target points, so the definition of Euclidean diameter implies
\[
 \operatorname{diam}(D_N)
 \geq\left\lVert(a,a,0)-(0,0,0)\right\rVert_2
 =\sqrt2\lvert a\rvert.
\]
It follows that
\[
 \Pr\!\left\{\operatorname{diam}(D_N)\geq\sqrt2\lvert a\rvert\right\}
 \geq1-2\gamma.
\]
The common sample law makes this probability the same under either witness, and the argument holds for every admissible \(N\), completing the proof.
